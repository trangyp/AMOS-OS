## 1. Bioelectric Membrane Potential Dynamics

BEI models cognitive and structural nodes as cells endowed with steady-state resting membrane potentials ($V_{\text{mem}}$) generated by selective ion fluxes:

$$\frac{\partial V_i}{\partial t} = \frac{1}{C_m} \left( \sum_{\text{ion} \in \{\text{Na}^+, \text{K}^+, \text{Cl}^-, \text{Ca}^{2+}\}} I_{\text{ion}}(V_i, c_i) + I_{\text{pump}} \right) + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} G_{ij}(V_i, V_j) (V_j - V_i)$$

Where:
* $C_m$ is the membrane capacitance.
* $G_{ij}$ is the gap junctional conductance between adjacent nodes $i$ and $j$.
* $I_{\text{ion}} = g_{\text{ion}} (V_i - E_{\text{ion}})$ follows Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz driving forces.

## 2. Syncytial Gap-Junctional Network & Morphogenetic Memory

Unlike synaptic transmission (which is directional, rapid, and pulse-based), gap junctions form a continuous conductive syncytium that supports long-range, steady-state spatial gradients:

### 2.1 Voltage-Gated Gap Junction Permeance
$$G_{ij}(V_i, V_j) = G_{\text{min}} + \frac{G_{\text{max}} - G_{\text{min}}}{1 + \exp\left(-\frac{|V_i - V_j| - V_0}{\kappa_{\text{gap}}}\right)}$$

### 2.2 Target Morphology Encoding
The global anatomical shape and invariant structural connections of the AMOS vault are stored as a distributed bioelectric attractor $\mathbf{V}^* = \{V_1^*, \ldots, V_N^*\}$. When damage occurs (e.g., deleted MOC, orphaned note, corrupted schema), the local voltage pattern deviates:

$$\Delta V_i(t) = V_i(t) - V_i^*$$

The gradient $\nabla(\Delta V_i)$ serves as an error vector, guiding automated graph repair agents to reconstitute missing connections and restore original topological closure.
