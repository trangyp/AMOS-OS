## Engine Overview

The HSE CEO Engine is an executive leadership extension of the Human Systems Engine (HSE). It focuses specifically on the apex of organizational power, mapping the relationship between the CEO, the Board of Directors, and enterprise-wide risk appetite.

- **Core Engine:** HSE CEO Engine
- **Skill Mapping:** `amos-hse-ceo-engine-layer`
- **Provenance:** HSE_CEO_Enginev.txt
- **Confidence:** STRUCTURAL

## Key Components

### 1. Executive Archetypes

Maps the specific behavioral archetypes that typically occupy the CEO position (e.g., The Visionary, The Restructurer, The Operator) and how those archetypes fail under stress. Each archetype has characteristic failure modes that emerge under pressure, and the engine models these to predict executive breakdown patterns.

### 2. Board Dynamics and Governance Rhythm

- **Decision Rhythms:** Defines 4 governance rhythms: Crisis Mode, Steady State, Expansion, Retrenchment. Each rhythm has distinct decision-making protocols and information requirements.
- **Power Checks:** Maps how the Board restrains or amplifies the CEO's inherent behavioral tendencies. The engine models the tension between executive initiative and board oversight.

### 3. Enterprise Risk Appetite and Red Lines

Connects the CEO's personal risk tolerance to the organization's structural risk exposure. Enforces "Red Line" rules: strict operational boundaries that must trigger Board intervention if crossed by executive action. Red Lines are non-negotiable thresholds that override executive discretion.

### 4. The 8-Step Decision Flow

A formalized pipeline for executive decision-making, ensuring that decisions are structurally sound and account for blind spots inherent to the CEO's archetype. The flow ensures that archetype-specific cognitive biases are surfaced and mitigated before final decisions are committed.

## Human Systems Engine (HSE) Foundation

The HSE CEO Engine builds on the broader Human Systems Engine, which models human-organization-market-power-risk systems. The HSE defines 8 layers:

1. **Human Layer** -- Individual behavior, work styles, alignment states, behavior signals, risk flags, collapse and recovery trajectories
2. **Team Layer** -- Team dynamics, productivity, conflict, sabotage, overload patterns
3. **Organization Layer** -- Org structure, role families, Vietnam-specific org risks, lazy behavior patterns, process gaps
4. **Market Sector Layer** -- Full sector map across business, government, social, infrastructure, and power/incentive systems
5. **Infrastructure Layer** -- Physical and digital infrastructure interaction with organizations and markets
6. **Power Incentive Layer** -- Formal and informal decision-making structures, actor types, motivation types, decision cycle patterns
7. **Risk Layer** -- Unified risk model: human, operational, financial, political, social, partner, sabotage, reputation
8. **Prediction Layer** -- Time horizons (1, 3, 6, 12, 24, 36 months), prediction outputs including individual performance trends, team collapse risk, project timeline slip risk

## Human Layer Risk Flags

The HSE tracks 10 repeat behavioral risk flags with thresholds: warning at 3 repeats, high_risk at 5 repeats. Flags include: repeat missed deadlines, repeat no status updates, repeat conflicts with colleagues, repeat disrespect to manager, repeat manipulation of information, repeat bending rules for self-benefit, repeat negative influence on team, repeat ignoring instructions, repeat backchannel politics, repeat low effort/quality.

## Collapse and Recovery Trajectories

The engine models a 10-step collapse trajectory: stress increase, hesitation, initiative loss, just-do-minimum, silent resistance, emotional withdrawal, performance drop, team impact, system damage, exit or removal. The corresponding 12-step recovery trajectory: acceptance, clarity of role, psychological safety, task simplification, retraining, small wins, confidence return, renewed initiative, better collaboration, stable contribution, leadership potential, system positive impact.

## Integration and Output

Used primarily by the `amos-bod-engine-layer` and the `amos-org-governance-engine-layer`. When an AMOS agent is tasked with advising on corporate strategy, evaluating executive performance, or designing board reporting structures, it must invoke this engine to accurately model the human element at the top of the hierarchy.
