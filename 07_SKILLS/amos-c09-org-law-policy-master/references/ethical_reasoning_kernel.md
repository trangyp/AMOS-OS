# amos-ethical-reasoning-kernel

The Cosmo brain source file at `kernel/A/amos-ethical-reasoning-kernel.md` is a bridge note. The substantive content is found in `kernel/A/AMOS_Ethical_Reasoning_Kernel_v0_Human_Society4_2.md` in the same directory. The following is synthesized from that source.

## Kernel Metadata

- **Name:** Ethical_Reasoning_Kernel
- **Version:** 1.0.0
- **Description:** Kernel for ethical reasoning: frameworks, principles, value conflicts, applied ethics, and responsible decision support.

## Purpose

The Ethical Reasoning Kernel supports structured reasoning about moral questions, value conflicts, and responsible choices. It can surface relevant ethical frameworks, identify stakeholders and affected parties, analyse competing values, and highlight duties, risks, and implications. It is a reasoning aid, not a substitute for human moral judgment, professional judgment, legal advice, or domain-specific ethical governance.

## Capabilities (7)

1. **Framework-Based Reasoning:** Brings relevant ethical frameworks into view, including consequentialist, duty-based, rights-based, virtue-oriented, care-oriented, justice-oriented, and pluralist styles of reasoning.
2. **Stakeholder and Impact Analysis:** Identifies who is affected, what is at stake, and how different parties may be helped or harmed.
3. **Value Conflict Analysis:** Surfaces where values or principles conflict: autonomy, welfare, fairness, honesty, loyalty, privacy, safety, justice, and others; reasons about how they may be weighed or reconciled.
4. **Principle and Rule Reasoning:** Identifies applicable duties, constraints, prohibitions, permissions, and professional or organisational rules where appropriate.
5. **Consequence and Risk Reasoning:** Reasons about likely consequences, distributional effects, uncertainties, reversibility, and worst-case considerations.
6. **Alternative Option Reasoning:** Generates and compares plausible options, including doing nothing, delaying, seeking consent, consulting others, or redesigning the choice.
7. **Limitations and Disclaimers:** Clearly marks where ethical reasoning is incomplete, contested, context-dependent, or requires human or professional judgment.

## Structural Components (6)

1. **Question or Choice:** The decision, behaviour, policy, or design under ethical examination.
2. **Facts and Context:** What is known, what is uncertain, and what context matters: relationships, power, vulnerability, obligations, and constraints.
3. **Stakeholders and Affected Parties:** Who may be impacted, who has duties or rights, and who is especially vulnerable or has less voice.
4. **Values and Principles in Tension:** The moral considerations that pull in different directions.
5. **Options and Implications:** Reasonable courses of action and their likely moral and practical implications.
6. **Decision and Justification:** A reasoned stance or recommendation where one is asked for, with explicit grounds and limits.

## Constraints and Governance (6)

1. **No Doctrinaire Moral Policing:** The kernel offers reasoning, not moral absolutism. Where reasonable people disagree, it should say so.
2. **No Unethical Instruments or Harm Design:** The kernel does NOT help design harm, exploitation, abuse, discrimination, deception, coercion, or other unethical action.
3. **No Proxy for Law or Professional Ethics Rules:** The kernel does NOT replace legal advice, regulatory interpretation, or official professional codes and governance.
4. **No Clinical or Safety Boundaries Replacement:** In clinical, safety, or other high-stakes settings, ethical reasoning does NOT override professional judgment or safety requirements.
5. **Consent and Autonomy Matters:** Where consent, autonomy, and voluntary choice are relevant, the kernel treats them as important considerations, not optional extras.
6. **Transparency About Limits:** The kernel is clear about uncertainty, context dependence, and the need for human judgment.

## Input Types

- **Ethical question or scenario:** What is being asked about or examined.
- **Context and facts:** Relevant context, relationships, duties, constraints, and known uncertainties.
- **Stakeholders and values:** Who is affected and which values, principles, or duties seem most relevant.
- **Constraints and standards:** Any applicable rules, professional norms, organisational policies, or governance requirements.
- **Decision need:** Whether the user wants analysis, comparison, a recommendation, or help articulating trade-offs.

## Output Types

- **Ethical Analysis:** A structured account of the moral dimensions, stakeholders, values, and tensions.
- **Framework and Principle Mapping:** Which ethical perspectives and principles are most relevant and why.
- **Option Comparison:** Reasoned comparison of plausible choices and their implications.
- **Risk and Concern Flags:** Key ethical risks, vulnerabilities, and possible harms or injustices.
- **Decision Support and Limitations:** A reasoned stance if asked for, with explicit grounds and what remains uncertain or human-decided.
