## The 7 Parts (from the canon)

### Part I — Constraint
- **Why anything exists at all**
- If there are no constraints, there is no system — only noise.
- **Properties:** Scarcity · Boundaries · Non-infinite capacity · Irreversibility
- **Examples:** Finite energy (physics), speed of light, metabolic limits (biology), logistics ceilings (war), time/attention/legitimacy (civilization)

### Part II — Flow
- **How power moves**
- Power exists only while it is moving.
- **Definition:** Constrained throughput across a system. Flow is not possession. Flow is conversion under limits.
- **Properties:** Input → transformation → output · Bottlenecks · Leakage · Queues
- **Examples:** Energy through matter, blood through organs, supplies through armies, capital through economies
- **️ GENUINE GAP:** The vault has no first-class canon for Flow. This is the single most valuable addition this canon makes.

### Part III — Structure
- **What holds flow together**
- Flow without structure dissipates. Structure without flow decays.
- **Properties:** Architecture · Hierarchy · Interfaces · Load-bearing elements
- **Examples:** Atomic bonds, organs, command hierarchies, institutions

### Part IV — Enforcement
- **Why structure holds**
- Unenforced structure is not structure.
- **Definition:** The mechanism that prevents deviation from structure. Enforcement is not morality. It is mechanical correction.
- **Properties:** Rule consistency · Boundary correction · Deviation cost · Predictability
- **Examples:** Physical laws, immune systems, military discipline, legal systems

### Part V — Time
- **Why everything changes**
- Time exposes all unenforced assumptions.
- **Properties:** Inevitability · Entropy · Recurrence · Thresholds
- **Examples:** Aging, wear, technological obsolescence, civilizational cycles

### Part VI — Adaptation
- **How systems respond**
- Adaptation at the core destroys identity. Adaptation at the edge preserves survival.
- **Properties:** Core vs edge adaptation · Identity preservation · Survival optimization
- **Examples:** Immune system mutation, organizational pivots, evolutionary adaptation, ecosystem response

### Part VII — Termination
- **Why systems end or survive**
- Systems do not fail randomly. They terminate when correction capacity is exceeded.
- **Properties:** Correction capacity limit · Failure mode · Survival vs termination decision
- **Examples:** System shutdown, revolution, extinction, jubilee

## Epistemic Boundary
This is a **persistence canon** — it answers *why systems emerge, operate, change, survive, collapse, or recover*. It is **orthogonal** to the vault's 10-part Universe Total Canon (UTC), which is a **composition canon** answering *what exists across scales*. A system can be fully described by both without duplication.
