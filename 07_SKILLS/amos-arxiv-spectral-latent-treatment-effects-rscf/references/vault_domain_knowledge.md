# AI Sweet Spots: Differential Cognitive Effects of Generative AI

## Background
MIT neuroscience evidence shows LLMs induce **20-30% cognitive debt** in neurotypical users (reduced neural connectivity, impaired recall, diminished ownership). But this assumes cognitive homogeneity.

## AI Sweet Spot Model
Maps **optimal AI involvement levels** across 6 cognitive populations against two variables:
- **Cognitive Effectiveness** (quality, recall, ownership, neural engagement)
- **Cognitive Workload** (effort, strain, time, physiological markers)


## Sweet Spot Findings

| Population | Optimal AI % | Key Finding |
|------------|-------------|-------------|
| Neurotypical | 20-35% | Cognitive debt kicks in above 35% |
| ADHD | 40-70% | Often outperforms neurotypical baseline |
| Autism Spectrum | ~50% | Systematic thinking strengths benefit from structured scaffolding |
| Dyslexic | 10-20% | Immediate gains from minimal support |
| Twice-Exceptional (2e) | Dual peaks (25%, 70%) | Mixed ability profiles, domain-specific optimization |
| Elderly (65+) | 35-60% | Prosthetic-level assistance compensates for working memory decline |
| Cultural overlay | Shifts curve | Indigenous/collectivist contexts show independent effectiveness shifts |

## Populations Studied
N=54 neurotypical, N=633 ADHD, N=240 autism, N=200 dyslexic, N=50 twice-exceptional, N=400,000+ elderly

## Methods
- Databases: PubMed, PsycINFO, IEEE Xplore, arXiv (2020-2025)
- Statistical: Polynomial regression, Cohen's d, 95% CIs, I² heterogeneity
- Variables: 11 AI involvement levels × 2 dependent variables
