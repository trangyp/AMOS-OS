## 4. Canonical Laws of the Quantum Stack

### Law AQS-01: Classical-Control Feasibility Invariant
No fault-tolerant quantum architecture claim is admissible without bounding classical decoding latency:
$$t_{\text{decoding}} \le t_{\text{syndrome\_cadence}} \quad (\text{Palsberg et al., arXiv:2601.20247})$$
If classical syndrome decoding cannot keep pace with physical error generation, FTQC fails regardless of theoretical code distance ($d$).

### Law AQS-02: QML Advantage Audit Requirement
Any claim of Quantum Machine Learning (QML) advantage over classical algorithms must be audited against:
1. Matched classical baseline (state-of-the-art classical neural networks and kernel methods);
2. Total energy, runtime, and I/O cost (including data-loading and quantum state preparation);
3. Spectral rank collapse and angle redundancy checks (`arXiv:2605.24324`).
Claims failing these criteria must be classified as `COMPETING` or `UNKNOWN/GAP`.

### Law AQS-03: Distributed FT Communication Bounds
Distributed quantum operations across linked nodes must explicitly specify ebit fidelity, link noise ($\epsilon_{\text{link}}$), and communication overhead ($\text{Stack et al., Nature Communications 2026}$).
