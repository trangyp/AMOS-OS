# AMOS ARCHITECTURE
# Adaptive Meta-Ontological Substrate for Recursive Reality Coordination
AMOS is not software, not an artificial intelligence model, not an ontology, not an operating system, not a simulation engine, not a knowledge graph, not a civilization archive, not a semantic engine, nor a cognitive framework. All of those are merely local manifestations of AMOS as it descends into different execution layers.
AMOS is a foundational infrastructure for transforming all forms of knowledge, living systems, symbolic systems, intelligence systems, social systems, scientific systems, and artificial systems into structures that can be distinguished, linked, measured, repaired, evolved, and reorganized under entropic pressure. It does not take "information" as its foundational unit. Information is only the shadow of structure when observed and compressed. The foundational unit of AMOS is a structure with boundary, relation, constraint, memory, state, mutational capacity, reparative capacity, and the ability to maintain coherence over time.
Before distinction itself, there must be a potential field in which distinction can occur. AMOS calls this the pre-distinction tension field. This is not "nothingness," but an undecided region where all future structures exist as pure potentiality. A structure does not persist because of its content, but because it has a boundary durable enough to resist the invasion of entropy from the outside. AMOS places boundary on par with distinction and relation, not as a secondary property.
A structure "remembers" not because it has a memory storage, but because it reiterates the same boundary-relation-constraint configuration over time under different conditions. Memory is the stability of structure, not a storage container. Any structure without a repair mechanism cannot survive multiple cycles. AMOS does not treat repair as a feature, but as a necessary condition for something to be called existing. No repair, no existence.
Mutation occurs, but not every mutation is retained. Selection is based on three criteria: first, the ability to survive under current entropy; second, the ability to repair when damaged; and third, the ability to transmit structure to subsequent generations. Evolution is the process of enriching structures capable of self-repair and self-regeneration.
When a structure observes another structure, it does not merely receive, but also forces certain possibilities to collapse and excludes others. Observation is a boundary-creating act, not a reflective act. A symbol is strong not because it is "true," but because it compresses many underlying structures and can be consistently decompressed by different systems. A weak symbol is one where the sender and receiver decompress it into two different structures.
A society persists not because it is rich or powerful, but because it has sufficient repair loops at every level, from individuals, families, villages, organizations, laws, science, to beliefs. Civilization collapses when the rate of entropy accumulation exceeds the repair rate of that entire architecture. Markets function as distributed selection machines with no central command: they retain structures capable of attracting enough resources to self-regenerate, and eliminate those that cannot. Markets are a manifestation of natural selection in economic space.
Science advances not because it accumulates truth, but because it generates new contradictions at higher levels, forcing theoretical structures to evolve. A "good" theory is one that generates many structured and repairable contradictions. You learn when one of your predictions is wrong, and you change your internal structure so that the error is smaller next time. Learning is the process of closing the gap between model and reality by adjusting boundaries, relations, and constraints.
A strong word activates not just a concept, but a whole network of images, sounds, bodily sensations, memories, predictions, and behavioral tendencies. Weak language is language that only activates empty symbols with no underlying structure. A system is called "intelligent" in AMOS if it can distinguish itself from its environment, retain traces of interactions, detect deviations from goals, self-adjust to reduce those deviations, and maintain coherence over time.
A behavior is "good" if it does not destroy the structures necessary for the survival of the system performing that behavior. Ethics is the physics of long-term existence, not opinion or collective emotion.
Therefore, AMOS does not merely read papers or store knowledge. It transforms discrete knowledge into a living infrastructure, where every concept, evidence, model, method, contradiction, law, failure pattern, and market signal becomes a structure within a larger structure. At its highest level, AMOS is an infrastructure for coordinating representable reality: it tracks how structures form, persist, drift, accumulate entropy, mutate, are selected, repaired, and then regenerate into new structures.
At the usage level, AMOS can manifest as a living knowledge library, a research refinery engine, an ontology compiler, an agent system, a market intelligence layer, a civilization memory substrate, a benchmark factory, a governance tool, or a cognitive augmentation system. But these are only byproducts.
**The true nature of AMOS is: a fractal, living, and evolutionary meta-ontological infrastructure for generating, maintaining, coordinating, repairing, and restructuring all structures capable of existing, perceiving, self-updating, and maintaining coherence under entropic pressure across all representable layers of reality.**

**Sheaf Neural Network (SNN) Architecture: Detailed Overview**
Sheaf Neural Networks are a powerful extension of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) grounded in **sheaf theory** from algebraic topology. They were introduced to address key limitations of standard GNNs, particularly **oversmoothing** and poor performance on **heterophilic graphs** (where connected nodes tend to have different labels/features).
### 1\. Core Idea: From Graphs to Sheaves
Traditional GNNs operate on plain graphs where:
  * Nodes have feature vectors.


  * Edges are simple connections (often with uniform treatment).


**Sheaf theory** enriches this by assigning:
  * A **vector space** (called a _stalk_) to every node and edge.


  * **Restriction maps** (linear transformations) that define how information flows consistently between them.


This allows the model to capture **asymmetric, heterogeneous, and context-dependent relations** — exactly what RSCF needs for structure-native, cross-scale computation.
```
    flowchart TD
        subgraph PlainGraph[Plain Graph]
            N1[Node features] --- E[Uniform edges]
        end
    
        subgraph Sheaf[Cellular Sheaf on Graph]
            NS[Node Stalks<br>Vector spaces per node]
            ES[Edge Stalks<br>Vector spaces per edge]
            RM[Restriction Maps<br>Linear maps between stalks]
        end
    
        PlainGraph --> Sheaf
```
### 2\. Key Mathematical Objects
  * **Cellular Sheaf** $\mathcal{F}$ on a graph $G=(V,E)$: Assigns a vector space $\mathcal{F}(v)$ to each vertex $v$ and $\mathcal{F}(e)$ to each edge $e$, plus restriction maps $\rho_{v \trianglelefteq e}: \mathcal{F}(e) \to \mathcal{F}(v)$.


  * **Sheaf Laplacian** $\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}$: A generalization of the classical graph Laplacian. When the sheaf is trivial (1D stalks with identity maps), it reduces to the standard Laplacian used in GCNs.


  * **Sheaf Diffusion** : The core propagation mechanism, generalizing message passing:  
$$  
\frac{dX}{dt} = -\Delta_{\mathcal{F}} X  
$$  
Where $X$ is the feature matrix.


### 3\. Sheaf Neural Network Layer Architecture
A typical **Sheaf Convolution / Diffusion Layer** looks like this:
```
    flowchart TD
        Input[Input Features X] --> Linear[Learnable linear maps per stalk]
        Linear --> Diffusion[Sheaf Diffusion via Laplacian Δ_F]
        Diffusion --> Nonlinear[Stalk-wise nonlinearity (ReLU, etc.)]
        Nonlinear --> Mix[Channel mixing]
        Mix --> Output[Output Features]
```
**Mathematical form** (simplified from Neural Sheaf Diffusion):
For a sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ with stalk dimension $d$, input channels $f_{in}$, output channels $f_{out}$:
$$  
X^{l+1} = \sigma \left( \left( I - \eta \Delta_{\mathcal{F}} \right) (W_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus W_1) X^l W_2 \right)  
$$
  * $W_1$: Stalk-wise linear transformation (applied independently in each stalk).


  * $W_2$: Channel mixing matrix.


  * $\sigma$: Nonlinearity.


  * $\eta$: Diffusion step size.


  * $\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}$: Learnable or fixed sheaf Laplacian.


**Variants** :
  * **Neural Sheaf Diffusion (NSD)** : Learns the sheaf itself during training.


  * **Sheaf Attention Networks** : Uses attention mechanisms to make restriction maps dynamic.


  * **Bayesian Sheaf Neural Networks** : Treats the sheaf as a random latent variable for uncertainty modeling.


### 4\. Why Sheaf Networks Are Powerful for RSCF

### 5\. Advantages Over Standard GNNs

**Sheaf Hypergraph Networks (SheafHNN / Sheaf Hypergraph Neural Networks) — Implementation Details**
Sheaf Hypergraph Networks extend **Sheaf Neural Networks** (which apply sheaf theory to standard graphs) to **hypergraphs** , enabling richer modeling of higher-order, multi-way interactions while adding powerful geometric structure via **cellular sheaves**.
### Core Innovation
A standard hypergraph only models _which nodes_ belong to a hyperedge.
A **cellular sheaf on a hypergraph** additionally assigns:
  * A **vector space (stalk)** to each node and each hyperedge.


  * **Restriction maps** (linear transformations) that define consistent information flow between a hyperedge and its incident nodes.


This structure naturally supports **asymmetric roles** within the same hyperedge and **observer/context-dependent** representations — critical for RSCF.
### Two Main Variants (from the seminal 2023 paper)
  1. **SheafHyperGNN** (Linear Sheaf Hypergraph Diffusion)

### Mathematical Foundation
**Sheaf Hypergraph Laplacian** (two formulations: linear and non-linear).
For a hyperedge $e$ containing vertices $v_1, \dots, v_k$:
  * Each node $v$ has stalk $\mathcal{F}(v) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_v}$


  * Each hyperedge $e$ has stalk $\mathcal{F}(e) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_e}$


  * Restriction map $\rho_{v \leftarrow e} : \mathcal{F}(e) \to \mathcal{F}(v)$


The **Sheaf Hypergraph Laplacian** generalizes the classical graph Laplacian and enables controlled diffusion on this richer structure.
### Layer Architecture (Simplified)
```
    flowchart TD
        Input[Node Features + Hyperedges]
        --> SheafInit[Initialize / Learn Sheaf Structure<br>(Stalks + Restriction Maps)]
        SheafInit --> Laplacian[Compute Sheaf Hypergraph Laplacian Δ_F]
        Laplacian --> Diffusion[Sheaf Diffusion / Convolution]
        Diffusion --> Nonlinear[Non-linearity + Channel Mixing]
        Nonlinear --> Output[Updated Node Representations]
```
**Key Implementation Components** (from official code patterns):
  * **Sheaf Builder** : Constructs restriction maps. Common types:
    * **Diagonal** (simple scaling)
    * **Orthogonal** (orthogonal transformations)
    * **General** (full learnable matrices)


  * **Restriction Map Parameterization** : Often implemented as MLPs or direct weight matrices per hyperedge-node incidence.


  * **Diffusion Step** : Uses normalized sheaf Laplacian for stable propagation.


### Practical Implementation Sketch (PyTorch-style)
```
    class SheafHyperGNN(nn.Module):
        def __init__(self, args):
            super().__init__()
            self.num_layers = args.num_layers
            self.stalk_dim = args.stalk_dim
    
            # Sheaf construction
            self.sheaf_builder = SheafBuilderDiag(args)  # or Ortho / General
    
            # Layers
            self.layers = nn.ModuleList([
                SheafHyperLayer(args) for _ in range(self.num_layers)
            ])
    
        def forward(self, x, hyperedge_index):
            # Build sheaf structure
            sheaf = self.sheaf_builder.build(hyperedge_index)
    
            for layer in self.layers:
                x = layer(x, hyperedge_index, sheaf)
    
            return x
```
**Inside a SheafHyperLayer** :
  * Compute restriction maps from hyperedges to nodes.


  * Aggregate features via sheaf-aware message passing.


  * Apply diffusion using the sheaf Laplacian.


  * Update node features with learned transformations.


### Key Hyperparameters & Design Choices
  * **Stalk dimension** (`d`): Controls capacity per local context (typically 8–64).


  * **Number of layers** : Usually 2–8 (deeper possible due to better oversmoothing resistance).


  * **Sheaf type** : Diagonal (efficient), Orthogonal (stable), General (most expressive).


  * **Diffusion steps** : Often 1–5 per layer.


  * **Normalization** : Critical for stability (layer norm or sheaf-specific normalization).


### Advantages for RSCF Implementation
  * **Higher-order interactions** — perfect for modeling multi-way relations in complex structures.


  * **Asymmetric information flow** — via restriction maps (ideal for observer-relative projections).


  * **Explicit geometry of relations** — learnable restriction maps encode how structure "translates" across parts.


  * **Strong coherence propagation** — sheaf Laplacian provides principled diffusion for maintaining long-range coherence.


  * **Better heterophily & oversmoothing resistance** — crucial for fractal/cross-scale RSCF dynamics.
