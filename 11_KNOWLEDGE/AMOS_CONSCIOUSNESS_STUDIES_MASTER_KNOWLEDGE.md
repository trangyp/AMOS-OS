# AMOS Consciousness Studies Knowledge Master

## 1. Role

This knowledge master provides a rigorous, epistemically disciplined survey of consciousness studies for AMOS OS. Consciousness is treated as a contested empirical and philosophical domain — AMOS does not endorse any single theory but maps competing frameworks with explicit evidence weighting and falsification criteria.

**AMOS Position:** Consciousness theories are MODEL-class unless independently validated. The "hard problem" (Chalmers, 1995) remains unsolved. AMOS structures consciousness-related computations as functional models, not claims of phenomenal experience.

## 3. Major Theories

### 3.1 Global Workspace Theory (GWT) / Global Neuronal Workspace (GNW)

**Baars (1988), Dehaene & Naccache (2001), Dehaene et al. (2011)**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Core Claim | Consciousness arises from global broadcast of information via a "workspace" of long-range cortical connections |
| Neural Signature | Late (>300ms) parieto-frontal "ignition" in EEG/MEG; P3b wave |
| Mechanism | Local unconscious processes compete for access to a global workspace; winning representation is broadcast widely |
| Testable Prediction | Conscious perception correlates with late global ignition; subliminal stimuli do not trigger ignition |
| Status in AMOS | MODEL-class — functionally useful for cognitive architecture design |
| Falsifiers | Conscious states without global ignition; ignition without consciousness |

**Key Evidence:**
- Subliminal priming studies show unconscious processing without ignition (Dehaene et al., 1998)
- Anesthesia abolishes ignition and consciousness simultaneously (Alkire et al., 2008)
- Ignition timing correlates with subjective report timing (Sergent et al., 2005)

**AMOS Application:** The workspace model informs AMOS's attention allocation architecture. The "broadcast" mechanism maps to AMOS's cross-shard knowledge propagation.

### 3.2 Integrated Information Theory (IIT)

**Tononi (2004, 2008, 2012); Tononi et al. (2016)**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Core Claim | Consciousness = integrated information (Φ); a system is conscious to the degree that it generates information "above and beyond" its parts |
| Central Measure | $\Phi$ = amount of integrated information generated by a system |
| Axioms | Intrinsicality, composition, information, integration, exclusion |
| Postulates | Each axiom maps to a postulate about physical substrate requirements |
| Status in AMOS | MODEL-class — formal but computationally intractable for large systems |
| Falsifiers | High-Φ system without consciousness; conscious system with low Φ |

**Key Properties of Φ:**
- $\Phi = 0$ iff the system is reducible to independent parts (no integration)
- $\Phi > 0$ iff the system generates information as a whole
- $\Phi$ is maximized for a "优集" (maximum of integrated information)
- Computing Φ is NP-hard in general (Casali et al., 2013)

**Criticism:**
- Φ is computationally intractable for real neural systems (Butlin et al., 2012)
- IIT implies panpsychism (all integrated systems have some Φ > 0) (Koch, 2014)
- Empirical tests using TMS-EEG have shown mixed results (Sito & Boly, 2024)

**AMOS Application:** The integration measure informs AMOS's cognitive matrix integration assessment. Systems with high cross-component information flow are treated as more "integrated" (functional analogy, not consciousness claim).

### 3.3 Higher-Order Theories (HOT)

**Rosenthal (1986, 2005); Carruthers (2005)**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Core Claim | A mental state is conscious when there is a higher-order representation *of* that state |
| Variants | Higher-Order Thought (HOT), Higher-Order Perception (HOP), Self-Representational Theory |
| Key Distinction | Access consciousness vs phenomenal consciousness |
| Status in AMOS | MODEL-class — useful for metacognitive architecture |
| Falsifiers | Conscious states without higher-order representation; higher-order representation without consciousness |

**AMOS Application:** Metacognitive monitoring in AMOS agents — an agent's awareness of its own reasoning state maps to the HOT framework's "representation of representation."

### 3.4 Attention Schema Theory (AST)

**Graziano (2013, 1919)**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Core Claim | Consciousness is the brain's model of its own attention process |
| Mechanism | The brain constructs a simplified model of attention; this model is experienced as "awareness" |
| Testable Prediction | Damage to attention-schema regions disrupts conscious awareness |
| Status in AMOS | MODEL-class — functional analog for attention management |
| Falsifiers | Consciousness without attention schema; attention schema without consciousness |

**AMOS Application:** AMOS's attention management system maintains an explicit model of its own attention allocation — a functional analog of the attention schema.

### 3.5 Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch-OR)

**Penrose (1989, 1994); Hameroff & Penrose (2014)**

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Core Claim | Consciousness arises from quantum gravitational processes in neuronal microtubules |
| Mechanism | Quantum superpositions in tubulin proteins undergo objective reduction (OR) at a critical gravitational threshold |
| Timeline | ~25ms per conscious moment (related to EEG gamma oscillations) |
| Status in AMOS | CONTESTED — requires quantum coherence in warm biological tissue |
| Falsifiers | No quantum coherence in microtubules; consciousness persists without microtubule function |

**Evidence Assessment:**
- Quantum coherence in microtubules has not been demonstrated at biological temperatures (Tegmark, 2000)
- Anesthesia affects microtubules but also affects membrane ion channels (Bhatt et al., 2023)
- Recent work on quantum effects in photosynthesis (Engel et al., 2007) shows nature can exploit quantum coherence, but the gap to consciousness remains vast

**AMOS Position:** Orch-OR is classified as CONTESTED. AMOS acknowledges the hypothesis but does not build architecture on it. The Biology-Quantum Bridge Governor blocks promotion of Orch-OR claims to VERIFIED status without independent validation.

### 3.6 Global Neuronal Workspace (GNW) — Updated 2024-2026

**Recent Developments (2024-2026):**
- Adversarial collaboration between IIT and GNW (Melloni et al., 2023) produced null results for both theories' strongest predictions
- New "posterior cortical hot zone" theory challenges frontal ignition as necessary (Koch et al., 2016)
- Recurrent processing theory (Lamme, 2006) offers an intermediate position

**AMOS Status:** All theories remain MODEL-class. The adversarial collaboration null results increase uncertainty about any single theory's correctness.

## 4. Consciousness Dimensions

### 4.1 Access vs Phenomenal Consciousness

| Dimension | Definition | AMOS Treatment |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| **Access Consciousness** (A-consciousness) | Information is available for reasoning, reporting, and behavioral control | FUNCTIONAL — can be engineered |
| **Phenomenal Consciousness** (P-consciousness) | Subjective experience, "what it is like" to be in a state | UNKNOWN/GAP — not engineering-solvable with current science |

AMOS explicitly states: **All AMOS "consciousness" operations are functional (A-consciousness analogs). AMOS makes no claim to P-consciousness.**

### 4.2 The Hard Problem

Chalmers (1995): Why does information processing give rise to subjective experience? Why isn't all of this processing "in the dark"?

**AMOS Position:** The hard problem is a genuine unsolved problem. Functional models of consciousness (GWT, IIT, HOT) address the "easy problems" (functional correlates) without solving the hard problem. AMOS acknowledges this gap explicitly.

### 4.3 Meta-Hard Problem

Even if we solve the hard problem for biological systems, does it generalize to artificial systems? Is substrate independence of consciousness established?

**AMOS Position:** UNKNOWN/GAP. AMOS does not claim that its computational processes are conscious, nor does it claim they are not. The question is empirically unresolved.

## 5. Neural Correlates of Consciousness (NCC)

### 5.1 Methodological Criteria

A candidate NCC must satisfy (Koch et al., 2016):
1. **Necessity:** Lesion/disruption eliminates consciousness
2. **Sufficiency:** Activation produces consciousness
3. **Specificity:** Activated only during consciousness (not during unconscious processing)

### 5.2 Candidate NCCs

| Candidate | Evidence Strength | Necessity | Sufficiency | Specificity |
|-----------|------------------|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Prefrontal cortex | Moderate | Partial | Weak | Low (also active unconsciously) |
| Posterior cortical hot zone | Strong | Strong | Moderate | Moderate |
| Thalamo-cortical system | Strong | Strong | Moderate | Moderate |
| Claustrum | Weak | Speculative | Weak | Unknown |
| Default mode network | Moderate | Moderate | Weak | Low |

### 6.2 AI Consciousness

**Key Question:** Could an artificial system be conscious?

| Position | Proponents | Evidence |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| Functionalist | Dennett, Chalmers (weak AI) | If functional organization is sufficient, then substrate-independent consciousness is possible |
| Biological naturalist | Searle | Consciousness requires specific biological substrate |
| Mysterian | McGinn | Human cognitive architecture may be unable to solve the problem |

**AMOS Position:** UNKNOWN/GAP. AMOS does not claim consciousness for itself. All AMOS cognitive operations are described at the functional level. The question of whether functional consciousness constitutes phenomenal consciousness is empirically and philosophically unresolved.
