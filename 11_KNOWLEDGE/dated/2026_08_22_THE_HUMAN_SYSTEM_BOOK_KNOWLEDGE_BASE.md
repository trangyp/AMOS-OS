### Polyvagal Hierarchy (ANS Subsystem)

The autonomic nervous system is not a simple sympathetic/parasympathetic dichotomy. The polyvagal theory (Porges) describes a hierarchical three-branch system based on vagal fiber myelination and evolutionary age:

```
Ventral Vagal (Myelinated) → Social Engagement
  Cranial Nerve X fibers from nucleus ambiguus
  Regulates heart, lungs, facial muscles
  Myelinated → fast, nuanced control
  Associated with calm, connection, communication

↕

Sympathetic → Mobilization
  Thoracolumbar chain (T1–L2)
  Accelerates heart, dilates pupils, inhibits digestion
  Mobilizes energy for action
  Associated with fight/flight, active defense

↕

Dorsal Vagal (Unmyelinated) → Freeze/Collapse
  Cranial Nerve X fibers from dorsal motor nucleus
  Bradycardia, digestion shutdown, collapse
  Unmyelinated → slow, primitive response
  Associated with shutdown, dissociation, freeze response
```

**Hierarchy principle:** The system tends to use the most evolutionarily recent (ventral vagal) strategy first; if that fails, it escalates to sympathetic; if that fails, it collapses to dorsal vagal. This is an adaptive hierarchy, not a switch.

**Clinical relevance:** ANS dysregulation can manifest as any point on this hierarchy — chronic sympathetic (anxiety, hypervigilance), chronic dorsal vagal (depression, shutdown, dissociation), or impaired ventral vagal (social difficulties, flat affect).

## Operationalizing 'Distinction' (Making the Equations Less Metaphorical)

Several equations use 'Distinction' as a variable (`Anxiety = Potential / Distinction`, `Boundary_Coherence = Signal_Separation`). Without a measurable definition, these are metaphors. The book implies three distinguishable forms:

| Form | Definition | Neural Substrate | How to Measure |
|------|------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **(a) Self/Other Discrimination** | Ability to distinguish self-generated signals from external/social signals | TPJ, mPFC, precuneus — social brain network | Social cognition tasks, self/other attribution paradigms, fMRI of TPJ activation during self vs other judgments |
| **(b) Signal/Noise Separation** | Ability to separate meaningful signal from background noise in sensory and internal processing | Thalamic gating, sensory cortex filtering, NMDA-dependent sensory gating | P50 suppression, prepulse inhibition (PPI), sensory gating tasks, mismatch negativity (MMN) |
| **(c) Boundary Clarity** | Coherent sense of body self-boundary — where 'me' ends and 'world' begins | Insula-ACC-prefrontal circuits, interoceptive integration | Interoceptive accuracy tasks, body ownership illusions (rubber hand), sense of agency measures |

**Why this matters:** The same word 'Distinction' maps to three different measurable constructs. `Anxiety = Potential / Distinction` most likely refers to (c) boundary clarity — when you can't tell where you end and the world begins, potential overwhelms. Schizophrenia's `Boundary_Coherence failure` maps to (b) signal/noise separation — internal thoughts experienced as external. The mental illness reframe's "at which layer" question maps to which form of distinction has failed.

**Status:** These operationalizations are inferred from the book's anatomical grounding, not explicitly stated. The book gives the anatomy; the measurable constructs are a reasonable extrapolation.
