# TRANG TÁT 2

## Independent Cross-Validation Principle

> **Trang Tát 2** is the source-defined AMOS principle requiring at least two genuinely independent evidentiary paths before a sufficiently consequential claim, belief, or decision is treated as reliable enough for action.

Canonical shorthand:

$$
\boxed{
T2
=
\text{Independent Cross-Validation Before Commitment}
}
$$

Source metaphor:

> **The Second Eye of Truth**

Operationally:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Claim}
\rightarrow
\text{Independent Confirmation}
\rightarrow
\text{Decision Gate}
}
$$

The central load-bearing distinction is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Repeated Observation}
\neq
\text{Independent Confirmation}
}
$$

and more specifically:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Source Count}
\neq
\text{Independent Provenance Roots}
}
$$

______________________________________________________________________

## 1. Canonical Purpose

Trang Tát 2 exists to reduce failure caused by:

- hallucination
- confirmation bias
- correlated error
- shared-source contamination
- circular reasoning
- source laundering
- repeated misinformation
- same-method failure
- same-observer failure
- same-incentive failure
- same-model failure
- epistemic overconfidence

Its canonical role is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Belief}
\not\rightarrow
\text{Action}
}
$$

until an appropriate validation gate is satisfied.

In source form:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Trang Tát 2 is the final barrier before belief becomes action.}
}
$$

______________________________________________________________________

## 2. Core Definition

Let:

$$
C
$$

be a claim.

Let:

$$
S_1,S_2,\dots,S_n
$$

be candidate sources.

A source $S_i$ confirms $C$ when it supplies evidence, observation, argument, or data supporting $C$.

T2 requires that at least two confirming paths possess sufficiently independent origins.

Source-defined condition:

$$
\boxed{
T2(C)=True
\iff
\exists i\neq j:
Confirm(S_i,C)\land Confirm(S_j,C)
}
$$

subject to:

$$
\boxed{
Independent(S_i,S_j)=True
}
$$

______________________________________________________________________

## 3. Independence Is the Critical Variable

The number two is not sufficient by itself.

Two reports derived from the same original source may constitute:

$$
2\text{ reports}
$$

but only:

$$
1\text{ provenance root}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
2\text{ documents}
\not\Rightarrow
T2
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
100\text{ repetitions of one lineage}
\not\Rightarrow
100\text{ confirmations}
}
$$

______________________________________________________________________

## 4. Source Independence

A strong independence assessment should examine whether sources share:

- original data
- author ancestry
- institutional ancestry
- measurement equipment
- methodological assumptions
- training corpus
- codebase
- funding incentives
- political incentives
- social coordination
- data vendors
- copied citations
- model lineage
- communication channels
- systematic biases

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Independence
=
\text{Topology Property}
}
$$

not merely a source-count property.

______________________________________________________________________

## 5. Provenance Topology

Suppose:

$$
S_2\leftarrow S_1
$$

Then:

$$
S_1
$$

and:

$$
S_2
$$

are not independent roots.

Likewise:

$$
R\rightarrow S_1
$$

and:

$$
R\rightarrow S_2
$$

share root $R$.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
Roots(S_1)=Roots(S_2)
\Rightarrow
Independent(S_1,S_2)=False
}
$$

unless independent evidence is added elsewhere.

______________________________________________________________________

## 6. T2 Is Not Re-Checking

Canonical firewall:

```text
RECHECK != T2
```

Two measurements performed:

- by the same person
- using the same machine
- using the same dataset
- using the same methodology
- under the same failure mode

may improve repeatability but do not automatically establish independent corroboration.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
Repeatability
\neq
Independence
}
$$

______________________________________________________________________

## 7. Four Independence Dimensions in the Source

The source identifies four desirable forms of variation.

## 7.1 Method Independence

Different methods may include:

- direct observation
- experiment
- statistical inference
- formal reasoning
- simulation
- theoretical derivation
- archival analysis

Conceptually:

$$
Method(S_1)\neq Method(S_2)
$$

______________________________________________________________________

## 7.2 L/M/H Layer Independence

The source maps confirmation through:

$$
[L,M,H]
$$

where:

- $L$ = foundation
- $M$ = mediator
- $H$ = peak

The purpose is to avoid verifying a claim only from one structural layer.

______________________________________________________________________

## 7.3 Temporal Independence

Confirmations separated in time can reduce some forms of transient correlation.

$$
t_1\neq t_2
$$

with adequate separation.

Time separation alone does not prove source independence.

______________________________________________________________________

## 7.4 Observer Independence

Different:

- people
- laboratories
- institutions
- cultures
- jurisdictions
- civilizations

may provide stronger independence when they do not share relevant error ancestry.

Observer difference alone is insufficient if both observers depend on one original source.

______________________________________________________________________

## 8. Three T2 Layers — L / M / H

The source defines three validation layers.

| Layer              | Canonical Role                  | Source Examples                                                                   | Epistemic Caution                                                             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **L — Foundation** | Direct grounding                | raw data, physical observation, experiment, measurement, archaeology, source code | measurement can still contain bias/error                                      |
| **M — Mediator**   | Analytical connection           | logic, mathematics, model, simulation, peer review, system feedback               | model agreement is not independent observation                                |
| **H — Peak**       | synthesis / principles / values | first principles, philosophy, ethical values, expert intuition                    | cannot substitute for empirical evidence where empirical evidence is required |

______________________________________________________________________

## 9. L — Foundation Validation

The L layer corresponds in the source to relatively direct grounding.

Examples include:

- raw measurements
- repeated physical observation
- laboratory evidence
- original records
- archaeological artifacts
- source code
- instrument output

Canonical representation:

$$
\boxed{
Confirm_L(C)
}
$$

means that $C$ receives support at the foundational evidence layer.

______________________________________________________________________

## 10. L Is Not Automatically Infallible

Direct evidence can still fail through:

- sensor error
- sampling error
- data corruption
- misclassification
- calibration failure
- fraud
- measurement bias
- observer effects
- uncontrolled confounding

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
L\neq AbsoluteTruth
}
$$

______________________________________________________________________

## 11. M — Mediator Validation

The M layer includes:

- logical analysis
- mathematical modeling
- simulation
- peer review
- system feedback
- structured expert interpretation

Represented as:

$$
\boxed{
Confirm_M(C)
}
$$

______________________________________________________________________

## 12. M Does Not Replace L

A model can internally validate coherence without establishing empirical truth.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
ModelAgreement
\neq
EmpiricalConfirmation
}
$$

and:

$$
\boxed{
Confirm_M(C)
\not\Rightarrow
Confirm_L(C)
}
$$

______________________________________________________________________

## 13. H — Peak Validation

The H layer includes source-defined high-level synthesis such as:

- first principles
- philosophy
- ethical commitments
- deeply trained intuition
- strategic synthesis

Represented as:

$$
\boxed{
Confirm_H(C)
}
$$

______________________________________________________________________

## 14. H Epistemic Firewall

For empirical claims:

$$
\boxed{
H
\not\Rightarrow
EmpiricalTruth
}
$$

Intuition, philosophy, or values may affect:

- interpretation
- decision framing
- governance
- moral choice

but cannot by themselves serve as empirical measurement.

This should be interpreted as a **source-defined high-strength validation architecture**, not a universal scientific truth criterion.

## 16. T2 Strength Classes

The source defines four classes.

| Class      | Symbol | Source Definition                                     |
| ---------- | -----: | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Weak T2    | $T2_1$ | two sources in the same layer using different methods |
| Medium T2  | $T2_2$ | confirmation from L and M                             |
| Strong T2  | $T2_3$ | two different layers with at least L or H represented |
| Perfect T2 | $T2^*$ | L, M and H all independently support the claim        |

## 23. Probability Firewall

The expression:

$$
1-\prod_i(1-P_i)
$$

normally represents the probability that **at least one** independent event succeeds when event-success probabilities are $P_i$.

It does not automatically represent:

> probability that a claim is correct because multiple sources confirm it.

For such an interpretation, additional assumptions are required concerning:

- base rates
- sensitivity
- specificity
- false-positive rates
- false-negative rates
- source conditional independence
- likelihood functions
- prior probabilities

Therefore:

```yaml
formula:
  source_status: SOURCE_DEFINED
  universal_bayesian_validity: NOT_ESTABLISHED
  use_as_calibrated_truth_probability: PROHIBITED_WITHOUT_MODEL
```

## 27. Weakest Load-Bearing Premise

This prevents corroboration from laundering weak premises into stronger conclusions.

______________________________________________________________________

## 37. COMPETING

When:

$$

$$

while:

$$

$$

and neither path dominates, T2 does not justify forced convergence.

Instead:

```yaml

```

until discriminating evidence appears.
