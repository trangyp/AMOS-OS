# **Join the Signal Economy Trust Study**
## **Why This Matters**
The world is entering a new era where **signals — neural, biometric, emotional — are the next infrastructure** of life. AR glasses, wearables, and neural interfaces are moving from labs to markets. This transformation will touch health, work, education, and commerce. But these systems will only succeed if they are trusted.
This study invites you — whether you build, fund, govern, or deploy signal-based technologies — to help define the **first global Trust Framework for the Signal Economy.** Your insights will guide how companies design, validate, and govern technologies that read and act on human signals, ensuring they align with safety, transparency, and human wellbeing.
## **What Participation Looks Like**
Participation is simple and voluntary. You will read a brief overview of the project and answer questions about **trust, consent, privacy, governance, and design trade-offs** in neurotechnology and biometric systems. Together, these perspectives form the foundation for a trustworthy future.

# **Background Information**
**The Importance of Trustworthy Signal Technology**
Signal technology — including devices, platforms, and systems that measure, process, and respond to human signals such as neural activity, biometrics, motion, and emotional states — is advancing rapidly. These breakthroughs are unlocking new possibilities for health, communication, productivity, and augmented experience — but they are also raising new questions.
Public excitement about the benefits of these tools is tempered by concerns about safety, data privacy, and non-consensual use. Industry practices that grant companies unlimited access to intimate data — from neural activity to heart rate — amplify those concerns.
Signal technology has the potential to transform daily life, but its success depends on public trust. As it moves from research labs into homes, workplaces, and public spaces, the companies shaping the signal economy must commit to responsible design and clear standards of safety, privacy, and human alignment.
**A Transparent Framework for Evaluating and Promoting Trustworthiness**
The Signal Economy Futures Lab is developing a transparent framework for evaluating the trustworthiness of signal-based technologies and the organisations building them. This framework turns abstract principles — such as safety, consent, and fairness — into concrete, measurable criteria that companies can be assessed against.
The framework is designed to serve three purposes:
**Transparency:** Clear, public standards for evaluating signal technologies and companies.
**Accountability:** Incentives for responsible development, reinforced by ranking and recognition.
**Public Empowerment:** Enabling users, patients, developers, investors, and policymakers to make informed choices about the technologies they adopt and support.
**Your Input**
We are seeking insight from professionals and experts working with signal technologies. Your input will directly shape this evaluation framework and influence how the future signal economy develops.
The framework evaluates companies across four key dimensions: 1) Device and Platform Functionality, 2) Signal Data Practices, 3) Human Autonomy and Consent, and 4) Societal and Ecological Impact.
In the following sections, you will review subcategories and criteria under each dimension and rate how important you believe each is for building trustworthy signal systems. You will also be asked about your general views on signal technology and a small set of demographic questions so that we can represent diverse perspectives in the results.
