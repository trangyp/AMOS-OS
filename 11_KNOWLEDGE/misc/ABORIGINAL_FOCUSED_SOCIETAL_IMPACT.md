# **Theory of Change & Evaluation Framework**
 _(Culturally-Grounded, Non-Western Aligned)_
## **Core Premise (State This Clearly)**
Aboriginal learners do not lack capability or motivation.
What fails is the **fit between dominant education systems and Aboriginal cognition, authority structures, and cultural protocols**.
This project assumes that **when learning environments respect Aboriginal ways of knowing, authority, timing, and dignity, confidence and participation emerge naturally** — without behavioural intervention or assimilation.
* * *
## **Theory of Change (Non-Linear, Relational)**
### **Long-Term Vision**
Aboriginal people are able to learn, teach, and participate in education systems **with confidence, cultural continuity, and self-determination** , while retaining full authority over their knowledge and identity.
* * *
### **Preconditions (What Must Be True)**
  1. Aboriginal knowledge systems are treated as **complete and legitimate** , not supplementary


  2. Elders and custodians hold **real authority** , not advisory roles


  3. Learning is not forced into linear, performance-based structures


  4. Digital systems do not extract, flatten, or rank cultural knowledge


Without these conditions, no intervention will succeed.
* * *
### **Inputs (What the Project Brings)**
  * Community-led co-design processes


  * Aboriginal governance and cultural authority protocols


  * A digital platform designed around **Aboriginal cognitive structures**


  * Time and space for relationship-based engagement


  * Funding that enables patience, not acceleration


* * *
### **Activities (What Happens)**
Activities do **not** follow a fixed sequence. They occur relationally and iteratively.
  * Community listening and yarning sessions


  * Definition of local learning protocols and boundaries


  * Co-design of platform features with Elders and learners


  * Piloting learning spaces that allow:
    * observation
    * silence
    * storytelling
    * gradual participation


  * Ongoing adjustment based on community feedback


* * *
### **Immediate Effects (What Changes F irst)**
  * Learners feel **safe** , not evaluated


  * Silence is recognised as participation


  * Elders’ authority is visible and respected


  * Learners engage without fear of shame or exposure


  * Learning occurs without pressure to perform


These effects are **relational** , not measurable by attendance alone.
* * *
### **Intermediate Outcomes (What Emerges Over Time)**
  * Increased learner confidence **without confidence training**


  * Greater willingness to engage with learning materials


  * Stronger intergenerational knowledge transmission


  * Reduced disengagement and quiet withdrawal


  * Re-legitimisation of Aboriginal knowledge within education contexts


Importantly, outcomes emerge **because the system changes** , not because learners are pushed.
* * *
### **Long-Term Outcomes (What Is Sustained)**
  * Cultural continuity through digital and community-held means


  * Aboriginal people participating in education **on their own terms**


  * Increased epistemic confidence (knowing that one’s way of knowing is valid)


  * A scalable model for culturally safe digital learning design


* * *
## **What This Theory Explicitly Rejects**
This project does **not** assume:
  * Behaviour change is the primary lever


  * Motivation needs to be engineered


  * Outcomes must be standardised


  * Learning must be visible to be real


  * Western assessment defines success


These assumptions are known sources of harm.
* * *
# **Evaluation Framework (Culturally Appropriate)**
## **Evaluation Philosophy**
Evaluation is **not surveillance**.
Evaluation exists to:
  * ensure cultural safety,


  * confirm alignment with community intent,


  * and protect dignity.


It is **formative, narrative, and community-interpreted**.
* * *
## **What Is Evaluated (Domains, Not KPIs)**
### **1\. Cultural Safety & Authority**
**Questions**
  * Do Elders feel their authority is respected?


  * Are cultural boundaries being upheld?


  * Does the platform adapt to community protocols?


**Methods**
  * Elder reflections


  * Governance group feedback


  * Cultural review checkpoints


* * *
### **2\. Epistemic Confidence (Not Performance)**
**Questions**
  * Do learners feel their knowledge is legitimate?


  * Are they more willing to engage?


  * Is shame or withdrawal reduced?


**Methods**
  * Voluntary learner narratives


  * Yarning circles


  * Observed changes in participation patterns (not frequency)


* * *
### **3\. Learning Engagement (Broadly Defined)**
**Questions**
  * Are learners returning voluntarily?


  * Are they observing, listening, revisiting materials?


  * Are learning moments happening outside formal sessions?


**Methods**
  * Platform usage patterns (non-comparative)


  * Facilitator observations


  * Community stories of learning transfer


* * *
### **4\. Cultural Continuity**
**Questions**
  * Is knowledge being passed between generations?


  * Are Elders using the platform as intended?


  * Is the platform strengthening, not replacing, community teaching?


**Methods**
  * Community-led reflection


  * Intergenerational feedback


  * Cultural continuity indicators defined locally


* * *

## **Not**
## **Measured**
  * Individual ranking


  * Comparative achievement


  * Standardised test scores


  * Forced articulation of learning


  * Western notions of “progress velocity”


Excluding these is a **design choice** , not an oversight.

  * Communities decide:
    * what is collected
    * how it is interpreted
    * who can see it


  * No data is used for deficit narratives or external benchmarking


  * Evaluation findings are returned to communities first
