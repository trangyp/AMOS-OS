# **HUMANITY FROM THE ICE AGE TO THE PRESENT**
## **I. THE LAST ICE AGE: HARD CONSTRAINTS ON HUMANITY**
**~115,000 – 11,700 years ago**
 _(Late Pleistocene → Holocene transition)_
### **Global conditions (measured)**
  * Ice sheets:
    * Laurentide (North America): thickness up to **3–4 km**
    * Fennoscandian (Northern Europe)


  * Sea level:
    * **~120–130 meters lower** than present (coral terrace dating, oxygen isotope records)


  * Atmospheric CO₂:
    * **~180–190 ppm** (vs ~420 ppm today)


  * Temperature:
    * Global mean **~6–8°C colder**


  * Climate volatility:
    * Dansgaard–Oeschger events: abrupt warming of **5–8°C within decades**
    * Heinrich events: massive iceberg discharges disrupting ocean circulation


### **What this means factually**
  * Ecosystems unstable on century and even decadal scales


  * Large mammals migrate or collapse


  * Coastal zones periodically disappear


  * Carrying capacity fluctuates sharply


### **Human survival therefore required:**
  * High mobility (archaeological site turnover)


  * Flexible subsistence strategies (mixed hunting–gathering)


  * Cooperative breeding (grandmother hypothesis supported by demography)


  * Long childhood dependency (energetic cost tolerated only socially)


  * High-fidelity intergenerational knowledge transfer


**No large-scale agriculture possible**
**No permanent cities possible**
➡️ These are ecological constraints, not cultural choices.
Humanity at this stage is already **fully Homo sapiens**.
Biologically modern humans predate this entire period by **~180,000 years**.

## **II. ICE AGE HUMANS (BEFORE CIVILISATION)**
### **Homo sapiens status (genetic and fossil consensus)**
  * Species emergence: **~300,000 years ago**
    * Jebel Irhoud (Morocco)
    * Omo Kibish (~195,000 BP)


  * By **70,000–50,000 years ago** :
    * Continuous occupation of:
      * Africa
      * Europe
      * Asia
      * Sahul (Australia–New Guinea)


### **Interbreeding (confirmed by whole-genome sequencing)**
  * Neanderthals:
    * Diverged ~550–765k years ago
    * Admixture events ~60–50k BP


  * Denisovans:
    * Admixture in Southeast Asia and Melanesia


Genetic facts:
  * Non-African populations: **~1–2% Neanderthal DNA**


  * Melanesian populations: up to **~5% Denisovan DNA**


  * Introgressed genes linked to:
    * Immune response (HLA variants)
    * High-altitude adaptation (EPAS1 in Tibetans)


➡️ “Modern humanity” is genetically **composite** , not pure.
This is settled genomics.

## **III. COGNITIVE AND CULTURAL CAPACITY DURING THE ICE AGE**
**Before 40,000 years ago** , evidence shows:
  * Controlled fire: habitual


  * Composite tools: stone + wood + resin


  * Tailored clothing ( needle use by ~40–30k BP)


  * Long-distance trade:
    * Obsidian transport **> 200–300 km**


  * Symbolic behaviour:
    * Ochre processing
    * Personal ornamentation
    * Intentional burials


### **Key sites (dated)**
  * Blombos Cave (South Africa):
    * Engraved ochre: **~75,000 BP**
    * Shell beads: **~100,000 BP**


  * Pinnacle Point:
    * Heat-treated stone tools


  * Levantine burials:
    * Grave goods
    * Repeated burial locations


➡️ Cognitive modernity is **not** a late Upper Paleolithic event.
It is **deeply pre-Ice-Age**.

## **IV. THE LAST GLACIAL MAXIMUM (LGM)**
**~26,000 – 19,000 years ago**
### **Conditions**
  * Ice coverage at maximum extent


  * Desertification of large r egions


  * Collapse of many megafauna populations


### **Human response (demographically inferred)**
  * Global population estimates:
    * Likely **< 5 million** humans worldwide


  * Severe population bottlenecks


  * Cultural regionalization


### **Refuge zones:**
  * Iberian Peninsula


  * Italian Peninsula


  * Balkans


  * Levant


  * Southern China


  * Sub-Saharan Africa


**Key fact**
Humanity survives this bottleneck **without** :
  * Agriculture


  * Writing


  * States


  * Formal institutions


➡️ This directly falsifies the claim that civilisation causes intelligence.

## **V. ICE AGE ENDING: ABRUPT CLIMATE SHOCK**
**~14,700 – 11,700 BP**
### **Sequence**
  * Bølling–Allerød warming:
    * Rapid temperature rise
    * Ecosystem expansion


  * Younger Dryas:
    * Sudden return to near-glacial conditions
    * Cooling within **decades**
    * Duration: **~1,200 years**


### **Implications**
  * Demonstrated climatic unpredictability


  * Human groups experience:
    * Resource collapse
    * Forced re-adaptation
    * Cultural stress


➡️ This period selects for **flexibility** , not optimization.
