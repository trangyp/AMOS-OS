STRONGLY KOSZUL EDGE RINGS
TAKAYUKI HIBI, KAZUNORI MATSUDA, AND HIDEFUMI OHSUGI
Abstract. We classify the ﬁnite connected simple graphs whose edge rings are
strongly Koszul. From the classiﬁcation, it follows that if the edge ring is strongly
Koszul, then its toric ideal possesses a quadratic Gr¨obner basis.
Introduction
Edge rings, edge polytopes as well as toric ideals arising from ﬁnite simple graphs
([7], [8], [9]) are fashionable in the current trend of combinatorics and commutative
algebra. The purpose of the present paper is to classify the ﬁnite connected simple
graphs G for which the edge ring K[G] of G is strongly Koszul (Theorem 2.8). From
the classiﬁcation, it follows that if K[G] is strongly Koszul, then its toric ideal IG
possesses a quadratic Gr¨obner basis (Corollary 2.10). It is unclear whether the toric
ideal of a strongly Koszul toric ring possesses a quadratic Gr¨obner basis.
It seems that the class of strongly Koszul toric rings is rather small. In fact,
the classiﬁcation of strongly Koszul algebras has been achieved for (i) edge rings of
bipartite graphs ([3, Theorem 4.5]), (ii) toric rings arising from ﬁnite distributive
lattices ([3, Theorem 3.2]), and (iii) toric rings of stable set polytopes ([5, Theorem
5.1]). We refer the reader to, e.g., [1] and [2] for the background of Koszul algebras.
1. Strongly Koszul algebra
Let K be a ﬁeld and let m = R+ be the homogeneous maximal ideal of a graded
K-algebra R.
Deﬁnition 1.1 ([3]). A graded K-algebra R is said to be strongly Koszul if m has
a minimal system of generators {u1, . . . , ut} which satisﬁes the following condition:
For all subsequences ui1, . . . , uir of {u1, . . . , ut} (i1 ≤· · · ≤ir) and
for all j = 1, . . . , r −1, (ui1, . . . , uij−1) : uij is generated by a subset
of elements of {u1, . . . , ut}.
A graded K-algebra R is called Koszul if K = R/m has a linear resolution. By the
following proposition ([3, Theorem 1.2]), we can see that a strongly Koszul algebra
is Koszul.
Proposition 1.2. If R is strongly Koszul with respect to the minimal homoge-
neous generators {u1, . . . , ut} of m = R+, then for all subsequences {ui1, . . . , uir}
of {u1, . . . , ut}, R/(ui1, . . . , uir) has a linear resolution.

Let R = K[t1, . . . , tn] be the polynomial ring in n variables over K. Let A be a
homogeneous aﬃne semigroup ring generated by monomials belonging to R. If T
is a nonempty subset of [n] = {1, . . . , n}, then we write RT for the polynomial ring
K[tj : j ∈T] with the restricted variables. A subring of A of the form A ∩RT with
∅̸= T ⊂[n] is called a combinatorial pure subring of A. See [6] for details. The
following is known to be true [6, Corollary 1.6].
Proposition 1.3. Let A be a homogeneous aﬃne semigroup ring generated by mono-
mials belonging to R and let A ∩RT be a combinatorial pure subring of A. If A is
strongly Koszul, then so is A ∩RT .
This fact is very useful. For example, we can determine when the d-th squarefree
Veronese subring Rn,d of polynomial ring K[t1, . . . , tn] is strongly Koszul. Note that
Rn,2 is the edge ring of the complete graph Kn of n vertices (which will be deﬁned
later).
Proposition 1.4. Let 2 ≤d < n be integers. Then the following conditions are
equivalent:
(i) Rn,d is strongly Koszul.
(ii) Either (n, d) = (4, 2) or n = d + 1.
Proof. It is known [3, Example 1.6 (3)] that Rn,2 is strongly Koszul if and only if
n ≤4. If n = d + 1, then Rd+1,d is isomorphic to a polynomial ring. In particular,
it is strongly Koszul.
If (n, d) = (6, 3), then the semigroup ring A generated by {t1titj : 2 ≤i < j ≤6}
over K is a combinatorial pure subring of R6,3. Since A is isomorphic to R5,2, R6,3
is not strongly Koszul. By a similar argument, Rn,d with n ≥d + 3 is not strongly
Koszul. In general, it is known that Rn,d ≃Rn,n−d. If d ≥3 and n = d + 2, then
Rd+2,d ≃Rd+2,2 is not strongly Koszul since d + 2 ≥5.
□
We call a semigroup ring A trivial if, starting with polynomial rings, A is obtained
by repeated applications of Segre products and tensor products. If A is trivial, then
it is strongly Koszul. See [3].
Remark 1.5. We note that R4,2 is a non-trivial strongly Koszul complete intersec-
tion semigroup ring. Indeed, if we assume that R4,2 is trivial, then there exists a
3-poset P such that the Hibi ring (see [4]) RK[P] is isomorphic to R4,2 since all of
the trivial rings can be constructed as Hibi rings. However, there exists no 3-poset
P such that RK[P] is a complete intersection and its embedding dimension is 6.
Hence, R4,2 is not trivial.
2. Strongly Koszul edge rings
Let G be a ﬁnite connected simple graph on the vertex set V (G) = [n] =
{1, 2, . . . , n}. Let E(G) = {e1, . . . , ed} be its edge set. Recall that a ﬁnite graph
is simple if it possesses no loops or multiple edges.
Let K[X] = K[X1, . . . , Xn]
be the polynomial ring in n variables over a ﬁeld K. If e = {i, j} ∈E(G), then
Xe stands for the quadratic monomial XiXj ∈K[X]. The edge ring of G is the
subring K[G] = K[Xe1, . . . , Xed] of K[X]. Let K[Y ] = K[Y1, . . . , Yd] denote the
polynomial ring in d variables over K with each deg Yi = 1 and deﬁne the surjective

The toric ideal IG of G is the kernel of π. It is known [12, Corollary 4.3] that IG

By Proposition 1.3, we have the following fact concerning edge rings.
Corollary 2.1. Let GW be an induced subgraph of a graph G on the vertex set W.
If K[G] is strongly Koszul, then so is K[GW].

We now generalize Proposition 2.2 to an arbitrary graph. The main theorem of
this paper is as follows.
Theorem 2.8. Let G be a connected simple graph and let G1, . . . , Gs be the 2-
connected components of G.
Then K[G] is strongly Koszul if and only if by a
permutation of the indices, G1, . . . , Gs−1 are complete bipartite and Gs is of the
following types:
(i) A complete bipartite.
(ii) An almost bipartite graph such that each 2-connected component of Gs(v, V1, V2)
is complete bipartite where V1 ∪V2 is a bipartition of the vertex set of the
bipartite graph (Gs)[n]\v.
(iii) The complete graph K4.
Proof. (“If”) It is enough to show that K[Gs] is strongly Koszul. If Gs satisﬁes
one of (i) and (ii), then K[Gs] is trivial and hence, in particular, strongly Koszul.
Suppose that Gs satisﬁes (iii). By Proposition 1.4, R4,2 = K[K4] is strongly Koszul.
(“Only if”) Suppose that K[G] is strongly Koszul and has 2-connected components
G1, . . . , Gs. By Lemma 2.5, we may assume that G1, . . . , Gs−1 is complete bipartite.
Suppose that Gs ̸= K4 and Gs is not complete bipartite. If Gs is not almost bipartite,
then K4 is a subgraph of Gs by Lemma 2.7. Then by Lemma 2.6, Gs = K4, a
contradiction. Thus, Gs is almost bipartite. Then by Proposition 2.3, the bipartite
graph Gs(v, V1, V2) satisﬁes K[Gs] ≃K[Gs(v, V1, V2)]. By Proposition 2.2, any 2-
connected component of Gs(v, V1, V2) is complete bipartite. Therefore, Gs satisﬁes
condition (ii), as desired.
□
Since a trivial edge ring is strongly Koszul, such an edge ring satisﬁes one of the
conditions in Theorem 2.8. As stated in Remark 1.5, K[K4] = R4,2 is nontrivial.
Hence we have the following.
Corollary 2.9. Let G be a connected simple graph and let G1, . . . , Gs be the 2-
connected components of G. Then K[G] is trivial if and only if by a permutation of
the indices, G1, . . . , Gs−1 are complete bipartite and Gs is of the following types:
(i) A complete bipartite.

It is known (e.g., [12]) that the toric ideals of both K4 and a complete bipartite

Corollary 2.10. If K[G] is strongly Koszul, then the toric ideal IG of K[G] possesses
