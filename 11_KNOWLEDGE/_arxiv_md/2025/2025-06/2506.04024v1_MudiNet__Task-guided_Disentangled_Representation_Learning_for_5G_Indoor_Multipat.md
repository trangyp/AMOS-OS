MudiNet: Task-guided Disentangled Representation
Learning for 5G Indoor Multipath-assisted
Positioning
Ye Tian, Xueting Xu and Ao Peng*, Member, IEEE,
Abstract—In the fifth-generation communication system (5G),
multipath-assisted positioning (MAP) has emerged as a promising
approach. With the enhancement of signal resolution, multipath
component (MPC) are no longer regarded as noise but rather
as valuable information that can contribute to positioning.
However, existing research often treats reflective surfaces as ideal
reflectors, while being powerless in the face of indistinguishable
multipath caused by diffuse reflectors. This study approaches
diffuse reflectors from the perspective of uncertainty, investigat-
ing the statistical distribution characteristics of indoor diffuse
and specular reflectors. Based on these insights, a task-guided
disentangled representation learning method leveraging multi-
time channel impulse response (CIR) observations is designed
to directly map CIRs to positions, while mitigating the adverse
effects of components that contribute minimally to localization
accuracy (e.g., diffuse multipath).In this semi-supervised learning
framework, a global feature extraction architecture based on
self-attention is proposed to capture location-independent wire-
less environmental information, while an MLP is employed to
extract the time-varying features related to user equipment (UE)
positions. Variational inference based on a latent variable model
(LVM) is applied to separate independent features within the
CIR, with position labels guiding the LVM to express components
more beneficial for localization. Additionally, we provide a
feasibility proof for the separability of diffuse and specular
environmental features in CIRs. Simulation results demonstrate
that the proposed method achieves higher localization accuracy
compared to conventional search-based localization methods,
with enhanced robustness against indistinguishable multipath
from diffuse reflectors.

diffuse reflection, variational inference, semi-supervised
I. INTRODUCTION
Indoor positioning is an essential technology for accurately
locating objects or individuals within buildings where GNSS
signals are weak or unavailable. This intricate challenge is
met with a variety of technologies, including Wi-Fi [1], Blue-
tooth [2], IMU [3], UWB [4], and notably, cellular networks,
which are defined as a paradigm of Integrated Sensing and
Communication (ISAC) [5]. These systems estimate positions
based on such as signal strength, time-of-arrival (TOA), or
angles-of-arrival (AOA). With the advent of fifth-generation
communication system (5G), cellular network positioning,
leveraging wide bandwidth, dense networks, and advanced

In cellular network-based indoor positioning, multipath
component (MPC) of signals - reflections of walls, floors,
and other obstacles - can significantly impact the accuracy
of trilateration or triangulation, which are the most popular
positioning methods. Traditionally viewed as a source of
error, these MPCs complicate the positioning process by
affecting the determination of the line-of-sight (LOS) path.
However, as the resolution of the signal increases, recent
advancements have turned this challenge into an opportunity
through multipath-assisted positioning (MAP) [7]. Positioning
accuracy can be enhanced by exploiting the channel geometric
parameters of resolvable reflected signals. This innovative
approach transforms what was once considered noise into
valuable data, providing more reliable indoor positioning,
especially in complex environments where LOS signals are
insufficient [8]. In certain scenarios, methods based on MPC
can achieve localization with even a single transmitter [9],
[10].
MAP can be broadly categorized into non-geometric and
geometric methods. Geometric methods, such as trilateral
positioning and triangular positioning, utilize mathematical
models to estimate positions based on the geometric parameter
characteristics of MPCs, the positioning accuracy is strictly
dependent on the estimation precision of geometric parameters
such as the TOA and AOA of MPCs. A single antenna can
estimate the TOA [11], [12] of MPCs using peak tracking
methods, while antenna arrays enable the estimation of AOA
of MPCs with a certain level of precision and resolution [13],
[14]. For geometric parameter estimation based on traditional
signal processing methods, the phase information of specular
reflection MPCs is utilized, where an extended Kalman filter
(EKF) is employed to track the geometric parameters of MPCs
in [15]. In [16], a probability hypothesis density (PHD) filter
is employed to track complex MPCs, where the distinction
between specular reflection MPCs and diffuse reflection MPCs
is discussed. The MPCs caused by diffuse reflectors are
treated as signals directly emitted from the diffuse reflectors.
Data-driven approaches, such as using LSTM to mitigate
the impact of NLOS environments on MPC estimation, can
improve the accuracy of geometric parameter estimation for
MPCs [17]. Alternatively, variational inference has also been

Several studies have explored position estimation in com-
plex scenarios. In [26], the impact of complex reflection en-
vironments on CIR observations is qualitatively analyzed, and
the feasibility of extracting complex environmental features
from CIR measurements is discussed. The effects of various
indoor propagation environments, including obstacles such as
concrete walls, on CIR are examined in [27], along with testing
the performance of several classical deep learning methods
and genetic algorithms for position matching. The authors of
[28], [29] point out that the characteristics of diffuse reflectors
cannot be described by any deterministic components. Previ-
ous studies, such as [20], also mention the convergence chal-
lenges of estimated VAs associated with diffuse reflectors in
geometry-based methods. To address this, a Bayesian particle-
based method is proposed in [30] to integrate the ambiguous
VA generated by rough surfaces. Further exploration of diffuse
reflectors’ impact on the geometric estimation of specular
reflection MPCs is discussed in [31]. In [32], diffuse reflection
is modeled by representing scattering points deterministically
as an array of multiple scattering elements, approximating
it as dense specular reflection, though this assumption is
considered idealized. A similar modeling method, using tensor
decomposition to estimate diffuse MPCs within the reflective
surface is presented in [33]. The localization problem in
the presence of dense MPCs caused by low bandwidth is
investigated in [15], where large MIMO antenna arrays are
used to separately estimate the phase parameters of dense
multipath and specular MPCs, which are tightly coupled with
TOA. These dense MPCs are modeled as Gaussian noise with
delay-dependent attenuation. Similarly, in [34], diffuse MPCs
are treated as dense specular MPCs from rough surfaces,
where effective diffuse MPCs were considered specular MPCs
caused by random points on the rough surface. The distribution
of these random points was determined by the surface’s
roughness. This work used a clustering approach to define
diffuse reflectors, aiming to estimate and cluster the effective
VAs of diffuse reflectors. These diffuse reflection mapping
methods all assume or require that MPCs are distinguishable.
This work focuses on a complex multipath environment
where specular reflection and diffuse reflection coexist. Dis-
tinct from the aforementioned research, our work models
diffuse reflectors from the perspective of uncertainty, analyzing
the statistical distribution of power-delay characteristics of
MPCs reflected by diffuse and specular surfaces. Based on
this theoretical foundation, we propose a variational inference-
based semi-supervised deep learning method for filtering lo-
calization information. By using CIR as input, the method
isolates indistinguishable MPCs that do not contribute to lo-
calization, such as diffuse MPCs, achieving both compression
of localization information and improvement in positioning
accuracy.

A. Multipath-assisted Positioning under Specular and Diffuse
Coexist Situation
Consider a typical wide-sense stationary (WSS) wireless
environment with a transmitter (Tx) and a UE receiver. The
signal transmitted from the Tx reaches the UE after being
reflected by various reflectors, so the received signal at the
UE can be represented as a superposition of reflected MPCs
with different time delays. Equation 2 represents the MPC
as an ideal model, each MPC corresponds to a VA. How-
ever, the presence of diffuse reflection introduces additional
randomness to the MPCs. The MPCs caused by scatterers
arises from the diffusion of signal energy in various direc-
tions when electromagnetic waves strike rough scatterers. In
modeling, existing studies often treat all reflectors including
both scatterers (diffuse reflectors) and specular reflectors in the
environment as a unified random distribution throughout the
entire space [35]–[37]. However, in reality, scatterers are fixed
physical entities, such as indoor plants or rough walls. This
study differentiates between diffuse reflectors and specular
reflectors in the spatial model, treating the specular reflection
components as deterministic multipath, while considering the
diffuse reflection components as uncertain, indistinguishable
multipath, as shown in Figure 1. For different types of reflec-
tors, the signal model can be formulated as a combination of
two components: specular component CIRs(t, τ) and diffuse

A. Latent Variable Model of Multi-time CIRs
The latent variable model (LVM) is a type of statistical
model that introduces unobserved latent variables to capture
the underlying structure or complex relationships within the
data. Numerous studies [38]–[40] have demonstrated that
LVMs can facilitate the disentangling of the latent structure
in data.
In general multipath channel environments, the CIR features
can typically be decomposed into two groups: UE position-
related features and environmental features. Previous works
have extensively modeled these two components mathemati-
cally [8], [20], [41], [42]. In these models, UE position-related
features include information such as ranging and angle mea-
surements, while environmental features encompass aspects
such as the distribution and material properties of reflectors.
Moreover, it has been demonstrated in related research that
specular environmental features and UE position-related fea-
tures are independent [18], [19].
From Equation (12), it follows that the CIR components
generated by diffuse scatterers and those generated by spec-
ular reflectors are conditionally independent given the UE’s
position. However, the observation of diffuse scatterers from
a single CIR at a specific time is incomplete. The cutting arc
s(t0, τ) formed by a single focal ellipse struggles to capture
the distribution of diffuse reflectors across the entire region D,
and the observation results are highly dependent on the UE’s
position at that moment. Therefore, as illustrated in the figure,
we aim to use multi-time CIR observations to enhance the
observation of diffuse scatterers, to facilitate the disentangling
of diffuse environmental features from UE position related-
features.
Building upon the analysis of the independence of diffuse
CIR component and specular component CIR, these findings
motivate us to use LVMs to further disentangle the feature
structure of the CIR, thereby mitigating the adverse impact
of diffuse reflectors on positioning. In this study, we define
three sets of latent variables: the environment-related variable
zd associated with diffuse scatterers, the environment-related
variable zs associated with specular reflectors, and the UE
position-related variable zu.

Among the baseline methods, MLP achieves performance
closest to our method. This is primarily because MLP adopts
exactly the same parameter settings as our method but lacks
the environmental variable estimation and separation compo-
nents. Additionally, the difference in training methods plays
a role. Our method incorporates multi-time CIR inputs for
global environmental variable estimation. However, for a pure
localization task, splitting multi-time CIR into single-time
samples aligns better with standard localization frameworks.
This is especially relevant given that our simulation employs
randomly generated trajectories, where single-time samples
prevent the model from incorrectly learning non-existent
global position relationships. Furthermore, we observe that
the Transformer with multi-time CIR inputs yields the worst
results, supporting our argument that global information exists
only in the estimation of environmental variables from multi-
time CIR samples and is unrelated to the UE’s position itself.

Among these baseline methods trained on single-time CIR
samples, despite using the same number of layers, CNN and

information. Specifically, in the delay axis, lower-power fea-

degradation. Meanwhile, CNN is essentially a sparsely con-

spatial structures through 2D convolution kernels. However,
in 1D data processing, this unique advantage is lost.

V. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we model diffuse multipath as an indistin-
guishable multipath component caused by the random distri-
bution of diffuse scatterers in physical space and demonstrate
that indistinguishable multipath is statistically independent
and separable from ideal specular multipath, which positively
contributes to localization. Based on this understanding, we
design a deep learning method based on variational inference
to enhance localization accuracy by separating and suppressing
indistinguishable multipath, particularly improving localiza-
tion robustness under severe indistinguishable multipath con-
ditions.
We conducted simulation experiments in a SISO single-
base-station localization scenario, where dynamic transmission
power adjustment was used to simulate multipath aliasing
under different SNR conditions. Compared to the baseline
methods, as the SNR decreased, our method’s performance im-
provement over the second-best method increased from 0.06m
to 0.49m, while the second-best method’s improvement over
the third-best method increased from 0.05m to 0.26m. This
demonstrates the effectiveness of our approach in enhancing
localization accuracy and robustness under severe multipath
aliasing conditions.
