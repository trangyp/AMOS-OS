Abstract—We present TIP-Search, a deadline-aware inference
scheduling framework for real-time market prediction under un-
certainty. In latency-critical environments such as high-frequency
trading, inference decisions must be made within strict time
budgets despite variable model performance and heterogeneous
data distributions. TIP-Search dynamically selects one model per
task from a pool of pre-trained deep learning models, balanc-
ing inference latency and predictive accuracy. Our approach
leverages offline profiling and domain-aware generalization to
route each task to the fastest eligible model expected to meet
its deadline. Extensive evaluations on three real-world limit
order book (LOB) datasets—FI-2010, Binance BTC/USDT, and
LOBSTER AAPL—demonstrate that TIP-Search achieves up to
8.5% higher accuracy than fixed or random strategies while
satisfying 100% of deadline constraints. We further conduct
comprehensive ablation studies and analyze latency–throughput
tradeoffs across scheduling policies.
Index Terms—Real-time inference, deadline scheduling, het-
erogeneous models, market prediction, accuracy–latency tradeoff,
LOB datasets
I. INTRODUCTION
Modern financial systems increasingly rely on deep learning
models for tasks such as limit order book (LOB) prediction,
algorithmic trading, and price movement forecasting. These
applications often operate under stringent latency constraints,
where decision quality is tightly coupled with response time.
The accuracy–latency tradeoff therefore becomes a central
consideration in real-time deployment.
Deep learning inference, however, is inherently variable in
latency, especially when models differ in size, architecture,
and domain specialization. For instance, a convolutional model
trained on high-frequency data from Binance may offer low-
latency predictions but perform poorly when evaluated on
Nasdaq-based FI-2010 data. Conversely, larger or more robust
models may generalize well across market regimes yet incur
higher computational cost. In high-stakes scenarios where each
millisecond can alter trading outcomes, the need to reconcile
inference accuracy with temporal predictability becomes im-
perative.
Traditional approaches to model deployment in financial
AI pipelines typically assume fixed model allocation, offline
provisioning for worst-case latency, or simplified selection
heuristics. These methods fail to adapt to real-time variability
in both data characteristics and system performance. Market
environments are non-stationary, exhibiting bursty task arrivals
and shifting distributions. In such settings, static selection

strategies often lead to suboptimal performance: either violat-
ing latency guarantees or compromising predictive accuracy
due to model–data mismatch.
This work addresses the problem of online model selec-
tion under deadline constraints by introducing TIP-Search, a
framework for Time-Predictable Inference Scheduling. Rather
than assigning a single model to all incoming tasks, TIP-
Search dynamically selects a model per task from a hetero-
geneous pool, each trained on a distinct real-world financial
dataset. The selection process is guided by offline latency
profiling and estimated generalization performance, enabling
per-task routing to the fastest eligible model with the highest
expected accuracy.
TIP-Search is built on the insight that the inference-
time–accuracy landscape forms a Pareto frontier. When suf-
ficient slack is available, the scheduler favors more accurate
models; under tighter deadlines, it prioritizes those with faster
response time. Crucially, this tradeoff is navigated without
violating task-level timing constraints.
To evaluate the efficacy of TIP-Search, we conduct ex-
periments using three publicly available financial datasets:
FI-2010, Binance BTC/USDT, and LOBSTER AAPL. Each
dataset is used to train one DeepLOB model, forming a hetero-
geneous ensemble. Using realistic task arrival rates and fixed
deadlines, we show that TIP-Search consistently improves
prediction accuracy over fixed-model and random-selection
baselines while maintaining 100% deadline satisfaction.
This paper makes the following contributions. First, we for-
mulate the problem of deadline-constrained model selection in
the context of real-time market prediction. Second, we propose
TIP-Search, a practical and modular scheduling framework
that combines model latency profiling with source-aware accu-
racy estimation. Third, we demonstrate through comprehensive
evaluation that TIP-Search outperforms conventional heuris-

learning under uncertainty. The proposed framework con-

Real-time

market opportunities or suboptimal trades. Consequently, the
deployment of deep learning models in these environments
must address not only the predictive performance of each
model but also its computational latency. The core operational
requirement is the ability to deliver high-quality predictions
within strict and deterministic timing bounds.
Despite significant advances in model design for financial
time series prediction, the integration of these models into real-
time systems remains challenging. Inference latency varies
considerably across different architectures and deployment
configurations due to differences in computational complexity,
parameter size, and hardware contention. Additionally, task
input characteristics—such as the volatility of market dynam-
ics or the structural patterns of LOB events—can influence
inference behavior, leading to heterogeneous response times
even within the same model.
Crucially, financial inputs arrive in an inherently stochastic
manner. Unlike domains with stable periodic input rates,
market data streams are characterized by burstiness and tem-
poral irregularity, resulting from shifts in trader behavior,
economic announcements, or liquidity shocks. This temporal
uncertainty amplifies the difficulty of static resource allocation
or fixed pipeline designs. Furthermore, the assumption that
a single model can generalize across all market conditions
and asset classes is often violated in practice. For instance, a
model trained on historical Nasdaq data may perform poorly
when evaluated on cryptocurrency order books, not due to
architectural flaws but due to domain shift.
Previous attempts to mitigate these issues have largely
focused on two types of strategies. The first class adopts
conservative worst-case latency provisioning, in which all
tasks are routed through the fastest model to guarantee dead-
line compliance. While this approach simplifies scheduling,
it typically sacrifices prediction quality, especially when faster
models are less expressive or trained on less relevant data. The
second class relies on offline model selection, where a fixed
model is assigned to all tasks under the assumption of input
homogeneity. Such approaches ignore runtime variability and
fail to adapt to changes in input source or model availability.
This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a dy-
namic and adaptive framework—TIP-Search—that selects the
optimal model for each task at runtime based on latency
constraints and expected predictive accuracy. The motivation
is rooted in the observation that different models offer distinct
tradeoffs in the latency–accuracy space, and that effective
real-time inference demands a scheduling policy capable of
navigating this tradeoff dynamically. TIP-Search avoids both
the pessimism of worst-case provisioning and the rigidity
of static model assignment by using a lightweight decision
mechanism to select the fastest eligible model that is likely to
yield the most accurate result, given the task’s characteristics
and timing budget.
In summary, the motivation for TIP-Search arises from
the confluence of three key challenges in real-time market
inference: variability in model latency, heterogeneity in model
generalization, and uncertainty in input arrival patterns. These
challenges necessitate a fundamentally different approach to
model scheduling—one that explicitly accounts for both per-
formance and timing constraints while remaining adaptable to
runtime conditions. The TIP-Search framework is designed
to meet this need, providing a practical and theoretically
grounded solution for real-world financial inference systems.

V. TIP-SEARCH ALGORITHM DESIGN
TIP-Search is designed as a deadline-aware inference or-
chestration framework, aimed at ensuring predictable response
under uncertainty. It operates by dynamically selecting a model
from a heterogeneous pool such that the execution satisfies
latency constraints while maximizing expected predictive ac-
curacy.
A. System Architecture
The system processes real-time tasks, each corresponding
to a market snapshot, and performs deadline-constrained infer-
ence using a multi-model dispatch mechanism. The architec-
ture comprises three key components: (1) a deadline monitor
that computes the residual slack for each task; (2) a model
selector that filters and ranks eligible models according to
latency constraints and estimated accuracy; and (3) a model
pool consisting of multiple pre-trained models, each with
distinct latency–accuracy tradeoffs.

task arrives, its deadline is computed based on the arrival
time and a fixed budget. The scheduler evaluates all available
models for latency eligibility and selects the one with the
highest expected accuracy among them.

B. Latency Profiling and Accuracy Estimation
Prior to deployment, all models are profiled to record their
average latency under warm-up conditions. These latency pro-
files Lk are stored for all models Mk ∈M and referenced at
runtime to enforce deadline compliance. For predictive quality,
each model is also associated with an empirical accuracy
estimator ˆAk(ti), which may be derived from offline validation
or adaptive online tracking.
C. Scheduling Logic
Given a task ti arriving at time ai and deadline di, the
scheduler identifies the set E of models for which ai + Lk ≤
di holds. Among the eligible models, it selects M ∗(ti) that
maximizes ˆAk(ti). The scheduling strategy ensures that all
dispatched tasks respect timing constraints.
D. Execution Workflow
At runtime, the inference loop continuously monitors in-
coming tasks. Upon each arrival, the system computes the
remaining time to deadline and invokes the scheduling logic to
determine the model to use. The selected model processes the
task immediately. Since all latency profiles are preloaded and
accuracy estimators are lightweight, the scheduling overhead
is negligible.
E. Integration and Extension
The modularity of TIP-Search allows it to be integrated into
existing inference engines. It is agnostic to model architecture
and supports GPU/CPU inference interchangeably. For exten-
sibility, the accuracy estimation component can incorporate
confidence scores, domain embeddings, or self-supervised
feedback, making it adaptable to future research in learning-
based scheduling.
F. Theoretical Analysis and Feasibility Considerations
To analyze the schedulability of TIP-Search under hard
real-time constraints, we define the notion of task feasibility
with respect to the system’s model pool M. Given a task ti
with arrival time ai and deadline di, TIP-Search guarantees
feasibility if there exists at least one model Mk such that
ai + Lk ≤di. This ensures that task-level schedulability is
preserved provided the model latency profiles {Lk} are known
and stable.
In the worst case, the selection logic incurs O(K) com-
putational overhead per task, where K is the number of
candidate models. This ensures constant-time decision latency
for bounded K, which holds in typical real-time deployments
with pre-enumerated inference configurations.
Moreover, we observe that the scheduling decision reduces
to a constrained maximization over a pre-filtered set, which
can be formulated as a special case of deadline-constrained
utility maximization. While the exact optimality of the sur-
rogate accuracy function ˆAk cannot be guaranteed in non-
stationary settings, TIP-Search is provably deadline-feasible
given static latency bounds.

VII. EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION
This section presents an empirical evaluation of TIP-Search
under deadline-constrained market prediction workloads. The
experiments are designed to assess the framework’s ability to
maintain real-time responsiveness while maximizing predictive
accuracy, as well as to quantify its generalization capacity,
scheduling efficiency, and comparative performance against
representative baselines.
A. Experimental Setup and Datasets
To simulate realistic online inference scenarios, we con-
struct a heterogeneous workload using three distinct financial
limit order book (LOB) datasets. The FI-2010 dataset [3]
comprises ten days of Nasdaq LOB data annotated with five-
class mid-price movements. The Binance BTC/USDT dataset
contains a 2025/05/10 snapshot of raw cryptocurrency trade
events labeled for three-class price direction. The LOBSTER
AAPL dataset provides high-frequency order book data for
Apple Inc. over ten trading days, representing a more stable
equity market.
Each dataset is used to independently train a DeepLOB
model [4], yielding three heterogeneous models in the infer-
ence pool. These models are deployed on a single GPU, and
runtime inference is triggered every 5ms, with a strict 10ms
deadline per task. Each task includes an LOB snapshot, a
ground-truth directional label, and an identifier of the dataset
source. The TIP-Search framework must select a model per
task to maximize predictive performance without violating the
deadline constraint.
B. Evaluation Protocol and Metrics
We evaluate all scheduling policies on a shared stream of
298 tasks, sampled evenly across the three datasets. Each pol-
icy operates in isolation under identical inter-arrival and hard-
ware conditions. Four primary metrics are collected: prediction
accuracy under deadline constraint, deadline satisfaction rate,
average inference latency per model, and total task throughput.
These jointly reflect both effectiveness and efficiency of the
tested strategies.

VIII. ABLATION STUDY AND DISCUSSION
To assess the effectiveness of TIP-Search in real-time infer-

with simplified decision strategies. Each strategy imposes a
constraint on the model selection logic, thereby isolating the
impact of specific scheduling principles.
A. Experimental Conditions
The ablation experiments are structured to examine four
distinct policies under identical workload conditions. The
Fixed strategy consistently employs a single preselected model
for all tasks, regardless of input characteristics. The Random
strategy selects among eligible models with uniform probabil-
ity, without consideration of model quality. The Source-Only
strategy dispatches each task to the model trained on the same
source as the input data. Finally, TIP-Search—our proposed
framework—dynamically selects the model with the highest
expected accuracy from the subset of models that satisfy the
deadline constraint.
Each policy is evaluated on a unified stream of market tasks
drawn from multiple domains. Every task must be executed
within a 10ms timing budget, and all experiments are executed
under identical hardware and scheduling conditions to ensure
comparability.

D. Interpretation and Implications
These ablation results underscore several key findings.
First, model mismatch remains a fundamental challenge: no
single model performs consistently well across all domains.
Second, static strategies—including fixed or source-specific
dispatch—fail to account for generalization variability and
thus underutilize the potential of heterogeneous model pools.
Finally, TIP-Search demonstrates superior adaptability by
leveraging deadline-aware inference and accuracy estimation
to navigate tradeoffs in real time. This robustness is critical
for deployment in latency-sensitive applications such as algo-
rithmic trading and financial forecasting.
The subsequent section discusses broader implications for
system-level integration and outlines potential extensions of
TIP-Search to multi-GPU, edge, or federated environments.
IX. DEPLOYMENT CONSIDERATIONS AND LIMITATIONS
A. Real-Time System Integration
TIP-Search is architected to operate with minimal runtime
overhead, making it amenable to integration with existing real-
time financial inference pipelines. The scheduling mechanism
executes entirely on the CPU, thereby avoiding contention with
GPU-based inference workloads and preserving parallelism. In
deployment environments with multiple resident models, TIP-
Search supports concurrent GPU model sharing by leveraging
preloaded weights in memory, which significantly reduces
switching and loading latencies. Furthermore, the system as-
sumes a warm-start initialization for all models, ensuring that
cold-start latency penalties are eliminated prior to runtime.
In dynamic market conditions, TIP-Search remains deadline-
aware and supports per-task timing adaptation, enabling seam-
less operation under varying load intensities or application-
specific latency constraints.
B. Scalability in Heterogeneous Environments
The proposed scheduling framework exhibits favorable scal-
ability characteristics when managing a moderate number of
candidate models, typically ranging from ten to twenty. This
scale suffices for most real-time inference scenarios in market
prediction, where models are often trained on distinct market
instruments, time frames, or architectural variants. Neverthe-
less, scaling beyond this range may introduce computational
overhead during eligibility filtering and accuracy ranking. In
such cases, performance can be preserved through hierarchical
scheduling mechanisms, lightweight surrogate scoring models,
or pruning strategies that narrow the candidate set based on
early-exit conditions or hardware-specific latency predictors.
C. Limitations and Future Directions
Despite its effectiveness in balancing inference quality and
latency, TIP-Search is subject to several limitations. First, the
system relies on the availability of multiple pre-trained models,
each tailored to a specific domain or data distribution. This re-
quirement introduces additional training and maintenance cost,
particularly in evolving market environments. Second, TIP-
Search currently assumes stationarity in model generalization
profiles over time; however, financial markets are inherently
non-stationary. Without mechanisms for continual learning or
model retraining, TIP-Search may experience degradation in
performance due to regime shifts or concept drift. Finally, the
system estimates model accuracy using static or heuristically
updated metrics, which may be insufficient under adversarial
conditions or in the presence of previously unseen input
distributions.
To address these challenges, future extensions of TIP-
Search could incorporate online adaptation mechanisms, in-
cluding self-supervised performance feedback, domain shift
detection, or ensemble uncertainty quantification. Additionally,
integrating low-cost latency prediction models or confidence-
aware inference strategies may further enhance the system’s
responsiveness in highly dynamic environments.

D. Transferring to Embedded Inference Scenarios
Although this work focuses on financial inference systems,
the TIP-Search framework is well-suited for deployment in
embedded real-time environments. In embedded AI systems,
such as autonomous drones, mobile robots, and sensor-driven
industrial controllers, real-time constraints and heterogeneous
inference paths are common challenges.
TIP-Search’s model-pool-based scheduling policy general-
izes to scenarios where different model configurations corre-
spond to distinct memory footprints, energy consumption pro-
files, or data preprocessing pipelines. For example, a robotic
system may maintain one small CNN for low-latency edge
detection and another transformer-based model for more accu-
rate, but slower, spatial reasoning. TIP-Search can dynamically
choose the inference path based on a task’s urgency and timing
budget.
Moreover, the offline latency profiling strategy aligns with
the static analysis common in embedded systems, while the
lightweight runtime dispatch ensures minimal CPU overhead.
Because embedded systems often operate under constrained
computational budgets, TIP-Search’s O(K) decision complex-
ity and deadline-satisfaction guarantee offer practical benefits.
In future extensions, TIP-Search could integrate with exist-
ing real-time operating systems (RTOS) via inference-aware
task schedulers, or be combined with early-exit networks to
support dynamic voltage and frequency scaling (DVFS) in
power-sensitive deployments.
X. RELEVANCE TO REAL-TIME SYSTEMS RESEARCH
Although TIP-Search is evaluated in the context of financial
market prediction, the methodology and scheduling challenges
it addresses are fundamental to the broader class of real-
time AI systems. The proposed framework deals with online
task arrival, strict latency constraints, heterogeneous execution
paths, and dynamic model selection—elements commonly
encountered in robotics, autonomous vehicles, embedded per-
ception, and other cyber-physical applications.
In particular, the formulation of latency-aware, accuracy-
guided model dispatch closely mirrors decision-making un-
der bounded computational budgets in real-time embedded
systems. Moreover, TIP-Search aligns with the traditional
goals of real-time scheduling: deadline satisfaction, throughput
maximization, and resource-aware dispatch.
By framing the deep learning inference pipeline as a real-
time scheduling problem with accuracy–latency trade-offs,
this work extends the RTSS discourse into the AI-inference
domain. It demonstrates that rigorous real-time techniques are
not only applicable but also beneficial in managing uncertainty
and dynamics in data-driven settings. We believe TIP-Search
opens new directions for integrating RT scheduling theory with
adaptive model-based decision systems.
XI. CONCLUSION
We proposed TIP-Search, a time-predictable inference
scheduling framework that adaptively selects deep models
for real-time financial market prediction. TIP-Search enables
robust accuracy under deadline constraints by leveraging
domain-aware generalization and latency profiling.
Through experiments on three real-world datasets (Binance,
FI-2010, LOBSTER), we showed that TIP-Search outperforms
fixed, random, and source-specific strategies. Our ablation
studies confirm its advantage in handling model diversity and
latency variation.
TIP-Search provides a practical and modular enhancement
to real-time AI systems, especially in latency-sensitive do-
mains such as trading, robotics, and cyber-physical systems.
