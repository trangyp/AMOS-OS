ABSTRACT 
Objective 
To characterize stigma dimensions, social, and related behavioral circumstances in people living 
with HIV (PLWHs) seeking care, using natural language processing methods applied to a large 
collection of electronic health record (EHR) clinical notes from a large integrated health system 
in the southeast United States. 
Methods 
We identified a cohort of PLWHs from the University of Florida (UF) Health Integrated Data 
Repository and performed topic modeling analysis using Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) to 
uncover stigma-related dimensions and related social and behavioral contexts.  Domain experts 
created a seed list of HIV-related stigma keywords, then applied a snowball strategy to 
iteratively review notes for additional terms until saturation was reached.  To identify more 
target topics, we tested three keyword-based filtering strategies.  The detected topics were 
evaluated using three widely used metrics and manually reviewed by specialists.  Word 
frequency analysis was used to highlight the prevalent terms associated with each topic.  In 
addition, we conducted topic variation analysis among subgroups to examine differences across 
age and sex-specific demographics. 
Results 
We identified 9,140 PLWHs at UF Health and collected 2.9 million clinical notes.  Through the 
iterative keyword approach, we generated a list of 91 keywords associated with HIV-related


## Page 3


stigma.  Topic modeling on sentences containing at least one keyword uncovered a wide range of 
topic themes associated with HIV-related stigma, social, and related behaviors circumstances, 
including “Mental Health Concern and Stigma”, “Social Support and Engagement”, “Limited 
Healthcare Access and Severe Illness”, “Missed Appointments and HIV Care Monitoring”, 
“Treatment Refusal and Isolation”, “Intimate Partner Violence and Relationship Concerns”, 
“Fear of Falling and Physical Health Concerns”, “Substance Abuse”, and “Food Insecurity and 
Resource Scarcity”.  Topic variation analysis across sex and age subgroups revealed no 
substantial difference between males and females; however, there were differences were 
observed among different ages. For example, "Fear of Falling and Physical Health Concerns" 
was notably more prevalent among older adults.   
Conclusion 
Extracting and understanding the HIV-related stigma and associated social and behavioral 
circumstances from EHR clinical notes enables scalable, time-efficient assessment and 
overcoming the limitations of traditional questionnaires. Findings from this research provide 
actionable insights to inform patient care and interventions to improve HIV-care outcomes.

1. INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND  
The human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) is recognized as the causative agent of acquired 
immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS), a global health burden.  Based on the World Health 
Organization’s estimates, 91.4 million people have been infected with HIV, and approximately 
44.1 million have died from AIDS or related complications since the beginning of the epidemic 
in the 1980s. As of the end of 2024, an estimated 40.8 million people were living with HIV 
worldwide [1].  In addition to medical complications, comorbidities, and AIDS progression, 
people living with HIV (PLWHs) have historically experienced stigma across diverse global 
contexts, including both internalized stigma, such as disclosure concerns and negative self-
image, and external stigma such as discrimination and social stigma [2]–[4].  The stigma can 
trigger anxiety, depression, and other mental health challenges, undermining treatment adherence 
and care engagement [5]–[7].   Compounding this, social and related behavioral circumstances, 
such as discrimination in clinical settings, limited medical and social support, which further 
discourage HIV-infected people from seeking help, negatively impacting their mental health [8], 
[9].  Together, these intersecting obstacles adversely affect their mental well-being, physical 
health outcomes, and overall quality of life.  Therefore, assessing and addressing stigma, 
alongside these social and behavioral circumstances, are crucial for care management of PLWHs 
and improvement of outcomes, both short and long term.  
The traditional way to measure stigma and social, behavioral determinants of health is through 
surveys and questionnaires [2], [10]–[12], with standardized instruments tailored for PLWHs.  
For instance, the Berger stigma scale [2] utilizes 40 likert-scale items to assess personalized 
stigma, concerns about disclosure, negative self-image, and public attitudes.  Holzemer [12] 
developed and tested an instrument to measure perceived stigma, establish a baseline for tracking


## Page 5


changes in stigma over time, and monitor progress towards stigma reduction.  More recently, 
studies have looked at social media to examine HIV-related discussions and their social, 
behavioral aspects, including stigma [13], [14].  Unfortunately, surveys are hard to scale up for 
large populations, as they are time and resource-consuming, and have a significant burden on the 
responders.  While social media offers a much larger sample size, there is considerable selection 
bias, and it is extremely difficult to link such to health measurements at the individual level. 
There has been an increasing interest in utilizing clinical notes from electronic health records 
(EHR) of healthcare encounters to study stigma, social, and related behavioral circumstances, 
since this information is not typically captured in a structured data field but may be documented 
in clinical notes.  Clinical notes collected during routine care capture rich, physician-documented 
details on patients’ experiences and are considered more accurate and reliable than social media.  
Compared with the traditional survey approaches, EHR notes minimize patient burden by 
avoiding the additional time and effort required to complete questionnaires, removing the need 
for staff training on survey administration and participant recruitment, and reducing issues such 
as low response rates and inconsistent question wording.  Currently, these notes have been 
employed to screen for HIV risk behavior and mental health conditions in PLWHs, but have not 
been leveraged yet for characterizing HIV-related stigma and its dimensions [11], [15].  
Topic modeling is a widely used method to discover hidden topics from large corpora in various 
fields [16]–[18] and can be a fundamental step to categorizing, understanding HIV-related 
stigma and social, behavioral factors from clinical notes.  Previous studies have applied topic 
modeling to clinical notes, investigating COVID-19 effects in community health [19], natural 
history of autoantibodies directed against neutrophil cytoplasmic antigens (ANCA)-associated


## Page 6


vasculitis [20], rapid response in nursing interventions [21], and patient trajectories of 
Alzheimer’s disease and related dementias (ADRD) [22].   
In this work, we conducted a large-scale, time-efficient investigation of stigma leveraging the 
real-world EHR.  We employed the Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) [23], the most widely 
used unsupervised probabilistic topic modeling technique [24], to discover the linguistic themes 
and discourses from PLWHs, focusing on stigma dimensions, social, and related behavioral 
circumstances, and analyzing differences by demographic groups. 
2. MATERIALS AND METHODS 
2.1. Data Sources 
The study used EHR, including clinical notes, from PLWHs who have received care between 
2012 and 2022 at University of Florida (UF) Health, a large academic medical center in Florida, 
with several hospitals and satellite clinics covering a rural-urban continuum for over 2 million 
people.  PLWHs were identified using a computable phenotype algorithm [25].  In brief, the 
algorithm identifies PLWHs if they have at least one HIV diagnosis code and either laboratory 
records suggesting HIV-positive status, prescription or medication dispense records for HIV 
treatment, or multiple HIV-related clinical encounters.  The algorithm has been validated via 
chart review, achieving a 98.9% sensitivity and 97.6% specificity.  
The UF Institutional Review Board (IRB) approved the study protocol under protocol 
IRB202300703, which governs our access to the limited clinical notes dataset. Data were 
obtained from our clinical data warehouse, the UF Integrated Data Repository (IDR), which 
contains fully digitized patient records from diverse healthcare settings, including but not limited


## Page 7


to inpatient, outpatient, and emergency department visits, and extracted from multiple 
information systems such as Epic. All the protected health information was removed during 
quality checks. The clinical notes in our dataset include all narrative free-text documents entered 
by providers into the patient EHR at UF Health, including progress notes, plan of care, 
admission, and discharge summaries. Scanned documents requiring optical character recognition 
(OCR) were excluded. The full list of note types was provided in Supplementary Table 1. 
2.2. Data Preprocessing 
A total of 2.9 million clinical notes were identified from this HIV cohort.  As not all notes 
contain stigma documentations, we experimented with three strategies to refine clinical notes 
used for topic modeling, as shown in Figure 1A.  The first strategy used all clinical notes from 
PLWHs to conduct topical modeling analysis.  The second strategy used clinical notes with at 
least one stigma-related keyword for topic modeling.  The third strategy only used the sentences 
that contain at least one identified keyword.   
To compose the keywords, we asked HIV experts to compile a list of keywords related to stigma 
based on the questions from established HIV stigma measures, such as the Berger stigma scale.  
Based on this keyword list, we used a snowball strategy to iteratively review a small batch of 
randomly sampled notes from the full corpus using a uniform random sampling procedure to 
identify new keywords until no more new keywords could be identified.  To further extend the 
keyword list, we also identified the synonyms of these keywords by calculating cosine similarity 
using the word embeddings derived using GatorTron [26], a clinical large language model 
developed at UF.


## Page 8


We performed common text cleaning and processing of the notes using the NLTK package [27], 
such as removing unreadable characters, numbers, punctuation, and stop words, from the original 
text.  We used lemmatization to transform words back to their base form to minimize the 
vocabulary size.  All text was converted to lowercase. Duplicates and notes with fewer than 50 
characters were removed.   
2.3. Topic Modeling  
LDA is a widely used and well-validated probabilistic topic modeling technique for discovering 
and identifying potential topics within a corpus. LDA assumes that documents are generated 
from a mixture of multiple topics, and the probability of words occurring in a specific topic 
varies among different topics.  A document-term matrix is used to calculate topic distributions 
based on word frequency and co-occurrence. 
In this study, we used the Gensim LDA implementation. [28]  The LDA model was trained with 
hyperparameters ‘alpha’ set to ‘auto’ and ‘beta’ set to ‘symmetric’. The number of ‘passes’ over 
the corpus is equal to 10. The inference parameter ‘iterations’ was fixed at 10 to control 
convergence during model training. To ensure the stability and robustness of the generated 
topics, we conducted 10 iterations through the corpus during the topic modeling analyses with 
different random seeds.  The number of topics (K) is one of the most important hyperparameters 
for LDA analysis.  To determine the optimal number of clusters, we performed an LDA analysis 
using numbers varying from 5 to 30 for each analysis.   
We utilized three evaluation metrics to compare the different numbers of topics, including topic 
coherence [29], topic similarity [30], and topic diversity [31].  Topic coherence measures the 
semantic similarity between high-scoring words in the topic, with higher values indicating better


## Page 9


coherence.  Topic similarity, calculated via the Jaccard index, assesses how similar two clusters 
are based on the words within the topics, with lower values indicating better similarity.  Topic 
diversity is defined as the percentage of unique words across the top words of all topics.  A 
diversity value closer to 0 implies redundant topics, while a value closer to 1 signifies more 
varied topics.  A good number of topics are expected to have high topic coherence, high topic 
diversity, and low topic similarity. To determine the optimal K, we averaged the metric scores 
across the 10 iterations for a given K. The composite score was computed by taking a weighted 
average across the three metrics, assigning equal weights to topic coherence and diversity, and a 
negative weight to topic similarity to penalize redundancy. The K with the highest composite 
score was chosen as the optimal solution.  
We further examined the top three iterations with the highest performance under the selected K. 
Two domain experts independently reviewed the semantics of each topic, merged subtopics with 
overlapping meanings based on proportional contribution, and assigned descriptive labels that 
best represented the content. A similar procedure was applied at the word level.  When 
disagreements arose among the experts, a consensus discussion with clinicians was held to 
reconcile differences.  The overall workflow is shown in Figure 1. The code is publicly available 
at: https://github.com/uf-hobi-informatics-lab/HIV-Stigma-Topic-Modeling.

3.4. Topic Variance among Sex and Age-stratified Groups 
Following Griffiths and Steyvers et al. [32], we used a document-topic matrix to capture the 
probabilities of various topics for all documents.  To investigate potential sex differences, we 
used the ‘Sex’ feature derived from structured EHR, calculated sex-specific topic probabilities 
by averaging the topic distribution across all documents from male and female patients, 
respectively.  As shown in Figure 4a, the heatmap cell color intensity indicates a higher 
proportion of sentences in that topic assigned to each group.  Overall, the average probabilities of 
topics are not remarkably different between the male and female groups. The absolute 
differences in prevalence between male and female patients were generally <0.01 across all 
topics. Both groups shared the same rank ordering of topic prevalence. These findings suggest 
that similar information was documented for the two sex groups.


## Page 20


Figure 4. Topic distribution across sex and age groups. 
Similar to the sex-specific analysis, we stratified the cohort into six age-specific groups ranging 
from 10 to 90 years old.  For each age group, we calculate the average probabilities of topics and 
compare the differences across all age groups.  Figure 4b shows the comparison results.  The 
variations of probability among age groups are remarkably larger than the gender-based groups 
(> 0.01).  Among the age groups, three topics show consistent decline in probability as age 
increases, including “Substance Abuse”, “Intimate Partner Violence and Relationship Concerns”, 
and “Missed Appointments and HIV Care Monitoring”; one topic, “Fear of Falling and Physical 
Health Concerns”, shows a remarkable increase in probability in older age groups.  There are no 
remarkable differences for other topics across age groups, such as “Social Support and 
Engagement”, “Medical History and Screening”, “Food Insecurity and Resource Scarcity”, and 
“Limited Healthcare Access and Severe Illness”.

4. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
In this study, we applied topic modeling to analyze around 2.9 million clinical notes from 
PLWHs receiving care at a large academic medical center in Florida to discover topics of stigma 
dimensions, social, and related behavioral circumstances.  Using an unsupervised topic modeling 
based on LDA, we experimented with three different strategies and discovered related topics 
buried in the large numbers of clinical notes.  The topic analysis using Strategy 3, followed by 
manual review and summarization, generated 11 topics capturing medical, social, behavioral, 
and stigma information.  The following sex and age-specific analysis further examined the topic 
differences among sex and age groups.  This study discovered important topics captured in 
narrative clinical notes for PLWHs to facilitate future studies to perform a deep analysis of 
clinical notes for PLWHs. 
The stigma-related dimensions and related social and behavioral contexts topics identified in our 
study aligned with findings from previous studies.  Topics such as “Food Insecurity and 
Resource Scarcity” and “Healthcare Access Barriers” are important socioeconomic factors 
associated with health outcomes of PLWHs, with studies showing that enhancing food security 
and strengthening social and healthcare support can benefit both mental health and HIV 
outcomes in resource-limited settings [9], [33].  The topic "Mental Health Concerns and Stigma" 
aligns with existing findings that high rates of HIV stigma, depression, and past trauma in 
PLWHs, and about half of these individuals are under treatment [6], [34].  The topic “Substance 
Abuse” captures a higher level of drug dependence and other substance use among PLWHs 
compared to the HIV-uninfected population [35].  Both topics align closely with cohort clinical 
characteristics.  The topic “Physical symptoms and HIV pain management” and “Fear of Falling 
and Physical Health Concerns” often relates to musculoskeletal pain and falling experienced by

PLWHs, which remains common even after patients achieve near-normal immune function [36], 
[37].  The sex- and age-specific analysis using the average probabilities revealed no notable 
differences in topics among sex groups. However, differences were observed in age groups for 
certain topics, aligning with prior studies indicating that the older group tends to have lower 
substance use, fewer social contacts, less visit missingness, and more physical pain [38], [39]. 
Understanding the social, behavioral, and clinical context of HIV stigma is the very first step for 
future studies to examine the details and develop early interventions to improve treatment 
outcomes and quality of life for PLWHs.  There are many strengths in our study.  We targeted 
HIV-related sensitive psychosocial domains that have not previously been characterized from a 
large number of real-world EHR notes by topic modeling. Guided by domain experts, we 
iteratively developed the stigma keyword list and experimented with three different filtering 
strategies systematically. The third strategy identified the sentences using a stigma-related 
keyword list, which was able to identify more topics of interest.  We experimented with different 
numbers of topics and evaluated results using both qualitative (manual review and 
summarization by experts) and quantitative (topic coherence, topic similarity, and topic 
diversity) evaluation metrics.  To better interpret the topics, we performed word frequency 
analysis using a word cloud and examined the topics across different sex and age groups using 
structured EHR information to provide insights into the differences among subgroups of 
PLWHs. Although our study focused on HIV-related stigma, the proposed method can be 
applied to other disease domains (e.g., hepatitis, cancer) and other healthcare institutions with 
minimal effort to customize the disease-specific keywords. 
This study has limitations.  We examined the EHR from a single healthcare institution, as 
privacy protections and the lack of public HIV cohorts with clinical notes limit multi-


## Page 23


institutional access.  We manually summarized topic labels based on each cluster, which may not 
reflect the content outside of these clusters. We also acknowledge that topic modeling is an 
unsupervised method, and by design, it may underrepresent rare themes. Individuals with more 
visits may still contribute more notes to the dataset, which could potentially skew the topic 
distribution. In our future research, we plan to develop natural language processing tools to 
capture more details of these topics and study the role of stigma dimensions, social, and related 
behavioral circumstances in HIV outcomes across multiple healthcare systems. 
 
SUMMARY TABLE 
What was already known on the topic: 
• People living with HIV are known to experience stigma, triggering mental health 
challenges and undermining treatment adherence and health outcomes. 
• Social and related behavioral circumstances discourage HIV-infected people from 
seeking help, negatively impacting their mental health 
What this study added to our knowledge: 
• The first large-scale topic modeling study on the documentation of stigma, social, and 
behavioral factors in clinical narratives for people living with HIV 
• Discovers 11 topics from large-scale clinical narratives to improve the understanding of 
documentation of stigma, social, and health behaviors for people living with HIV 
• Age and sex group analysis discovers differences in the documentation of stigma, social, 
and health behaviors related information.
