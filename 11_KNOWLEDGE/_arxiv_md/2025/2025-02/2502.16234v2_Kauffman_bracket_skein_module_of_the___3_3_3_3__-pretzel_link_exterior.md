As a convention, R is identified with R∅⊆S(M; R), via 1 7→∅.
When Σ is an oriented surface, S(Σ; R) := S(Σ×[0, 1]; R) is a R-algebra,
called the Kauffman bracket skein algebra of Σ. Given links L1, L2, their
product L1L2 is defined by stacking L1 over L2 in the [0, 1] direction.
When ∂M ̸= ∅, a collar neighborhood of ∂M equips S(M; R) with the
structure of a module over S(∂M; R), via stacking.
In the case R = C and q

where X(M) = X(π1(M)) is the SL(2, C)-character variety of π1(M).
In recent years, skein modules have become central objects in quantum
topology.
One important reason is that, in light of (1), skein module is
regarded as a quantization of character variety. More deep studies in this
vein were carried out in [10, 11, 17].
Computational results were seen in
[1,2,4,12–14,18–20] and the references therein. However, skein modules still
appear rather mysterious; there are many open problems about structures
and properties of skein modules, as well as connections to classical invariants.
From now on, let R = Q(q
1
2 ), and abbreviate S(M; R) to S(M).
As a remarkable achievement, Gunningham, Jordan and Safronov [15]
confirmed Witten’s Finiteness Conjecture which asserts that dimR S(M) <
∞for each closed 3-manifold M.
Some generalizations of the finiteness
conjecture to bounded 3-manifolds were proposed by Detcherry [9] in 2021.
Suppose L ⊂S3 is a link with n-components. Let EL denote its exterior, and
let ti ∈S(EL) denote the meridian of the i-th component. Then R[t1, . . . , tn]
is a subring of S(∂EL). The following was stated as [9, Conjecture 3.2]:
Conjecture 1.1. S(EL) is finitely generated as a R[t1, . . . , tn]-module.

left part of Figure 1. Let t1, t2 be the meridians of the two components of

L. For each k ≥1, let xk
1 denote the element of S(EL) given in the right
part of Figure 1. Based on partial computation, we will show
Theorem 1.2. For any finite subset N ⊂S(EL), there exists k0 ∈N such

Consequently, S(EL) is not a finitely generated R[t1, t2]-module.
Thus, L is a counterexample to Conjecture 1.1.
The motivation of this work is that, in 2022 the author [6] found that for
a 4-strand Montesinos knot K, usually X(EK) admits a high-dimensional
component, i.e., one with dimension larger than 1. Similarly, X(EL) admits
a component with dimension larger than 2. To be precise, when χ(t1), χ(t2)
are fixed generic values, χ(x1) can take an arbitrary generic value. From the
viewpoint of (1), there would be something strange if Conjecture 1.1 holds
for L. So we are curious to seek the relations among the xk
1’s.
To study S(EL), we will decompose EL as
EL = U ∪H1 ∪H2 ∪H3 ∪H4 ∪B3,
where U is a handlebody of genus 4, the Hi’s are 2-handles, and B3 is a 3-
ball. Basically, S(EL) ∼= S(U)/Sl, where Sl is the submodule generated by
“sliding relators”. We will find a free basis B for S(U), and show that when
the relations given by Sl are used up, still so many elements of B survive
that S(EL) cannot be finitely generated as a R[t1, t2]-module.
As we will see, “relations among relations” in S(EL) are much richer
than those in the classical counterpart C[X(EL)]. The non-commutativity
and the lack of ring structure of S(EL) cause much complexity. During the
process of overcoming various difficulties, we develop some methodologies
for studying skein modules.
