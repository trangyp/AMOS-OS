Lambda Value-at-Risk under ambiguity and risk sharing
Peng Liu∗
Alexander Schied†
November 4, 2025
Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the Lambda Value-at-Risk (ΛVaR) under ambiguity, where
the ambiguity is represented by a family of probability measures. We establish that for
increasing Lambda functions, the robust (i.e., worst-case) ΛVaR under such an ambiguity
set is equivalent to ΛVaR computed with respect to a capacity, a novel extension in the
literature. This framework unifies and extends both traditional ΛVaR and Choquet quantiles
(Value-at-Risk under ambiguity). We analyze the fundamental properties of this extended
risk measure and establish a novel equivalent representation for ΛVaR under capacities with
monotone Lambda functions in terms of families of downsets. Moreover, explicit formulas
are derived for robust ΛVaR when ambiguity sets are characterized by ϕ-divergence and the
likelihood ratio constraints, respectively.
We further explore the applications in risk sharing among multiple agents. We demon-
strate that the family of risk measures induced by families of downsets is closed under
inf-convolution. In particular, we prove that the inf-convolution of ΛVaR with capacities
and monotone Lambda functions is another ΛVaR under a capacity. The explicit forms
of optimal allocations are also derived. Moreover, we obtain more explicit results for risk
sharing under ambiguity sets characterized by ϕ-divergence and likelihood ratio constraints.
Finally, we explore comonotonic risk-sharing for ΛVaR under ambiguity.
Key-words: Lambda Value-at-Risk under capacities; Value-at-Risk; Choquet quan-
tiles; Risk sharing; Inf-convolution; Model uncertainty; ϕ-divergence; Kullback-Leibler di-
vergence; Likelihood ratio

Introduction
Model uncertainty, also known as ambiguity in decision theory, plays a central role in
decision-making and risk quantification. The Basel regulatory framework (BCBS (2019)) explic-
itly requires that risks need to be assessed under a variety of stressed probability scenarios. This
introduces ambiguity into the evaluation process, as considering multiple probability models re-
flects underlying model uncertainty. A widely used paradigm to address this is the application of
worst-case risk measures across these scenarios, which is a well-established approach in modeling
decisions under ambiguity. It was first axiomatized by Gilboa and Schmeidler (1989) through
the maxmin expected utility framework, and subsequently extended by Hansen and Sargent
(2001) (multiplier preferences), Ghirardato et al. (2004) (the α-maxmin model), Marinacci et
al. (2006) (variational preferences), and Cerreia-Vioglio et al. (2021) (preferences under model
misspecification). Worst-case risk measures under multiple probability distributions have been
extensively studied in the context of risk optimization under uncertainty; see El Ghaoui et al.
(2003), Natarajan et al. (2008), and Zhu and Fukushima (2009) for applications to Value-at-Risk
(VaR) and Expected Shortfall (ES).
Recently, it was shown in Liu et al. (2024) that the worst-case quantiles under a set of
probability measures correspond to the so-called Choquet quantiles, introduced in Chambers
(2007). These quantiles are defined with respect to a capacity–a non-additive prior. Choquet
quantiles belong to the broader framework of decision-making under non-additive probabilities,
as studied in Yaari (1987) (dual utility theory), Quiggin (1982) (rank-dependent expected utility),
and Schmeidler (1989) (Choquet expected utility). The risk sharing with Choquet quantiles was
also studied in Liu et al. (2024).
Inspired by Liu et al. (2024), we investigate the worst-case of Lambda Value-at-Risk (ΛVaR)
under a set of probability measures and study the risk sharing problem for multiple ΛVaR under
model uncertainty. The target is to find the explicit expression of the worst case of ΛVaR similarly
as Choquet quantiles, and the expressions of the inf-convolution and the optimal allocations for
risk sharing under model uncertainty. Differently from the setup in this paper, the robust model
of ΛVaR under a set of distribution functions was established in Han and Liu (2025).
In Section 3, we analyze the fundamental properties of ΛVaR defined with respect to ca-
pacities, denoted by ΛVaRw, where w is a capacity and Λ is a function. It has the following
form:
ΛVaRw(X) = inf{x ∈R : w(X > x) ⩽Λ(x)}.
One essential property is that for increasing Λ, the ΛVaRw is closed under the sup operator, i.e.,
the worst case of ΛVaRw under a set of capacities is a ΛVaRw∗with w∗a new capacity. This

families of downsets. We find that ΛVaRw with monotone Λ functions corresponds to the risk
measures induced by monotone families of downsets, offering an equivalent representation for
ΛVaRw and playing an important role in the study of risk sharing in Section 5.
In Section 4, we consider the robust ΛVaR with increasing Λ under two uncertainty sets:
i) the uncertainty sets induced by ϕ-divergence including the well-known Kullback-Leibler (KL)
divergence and chi-squared divergence as special cases; ii) the uncertainty sets defined by the
likelihood ratio, where the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the underlying probability measure with
respect to a reference probability measure is bounded by two random variables from above and
below respectively. Surprisingly, the robust ΛVaR under the first uncertainty set is equivalent
to a ΛVaR under the reference probability with a transformed Λ function. This conclusion also
holds true for the second uncertainty set if the two random variables are constants. If the two
random variables for the second uncertainty set are not constants, we also derive the robust
models for ΛVaR, and a more explicit expression is derived for the case that the random variable
controlling the lower bound is zero.
Section 5 is devoted to the problem of risk sharing with multiple agents. We find that
the inf-convolution operator is closed for risk measures induced by families of downsets, which
include all ΛVaRw as special cases. This implies that the inf-convolution of ΛVaRw is a risk
measure induced by a family of downsets. We also derive the corresponding optimal allocations
by splitting and distributing the tail events among agents, in a manner similarly to the model
without uncertainty considered in Embrechts et al. (2020), Liu (2025) and Xia and Hu (2025).
Interestingly, we further demonstrate that the inf-convolution of multiple ΛVaRw, when all Λ
functions are monotone in the same direction, results in another ΛVaRw.
This extends the
related results for Choquet quantiles in Liu et al. (2024), and suggests that the inf-convolution
of multiple ΛVaR with increasing Λ functions under ambiguity yields a ΛVaRw with w being
a capacity. In fact, our results are very general, and unify and extend the prior findings: the
results for VaR in Embrechts et al. (2018) and Embrechts et al. (2020) under both homogeneous

sharing problem under the two specific uncertainty sets introduced in Section 4 is also discussed.

1.1
Related literature
The ΛVaR was introduced by Frittelli et al. (2014) as an extension of VaR, enabling the
use of multiple confidence levels through a loss-dependent function Λ.
The choice of the Λ
function is highly flexible, as it can be either increasing or decreasing. The selection of Λ based
on data was explored in Hitaj et al. (2018).
Compared to VaR, one advantage of ΛVaR is
its ability to distinguish tail risk when Λ is decreasing, in a manner similar to the Loss VaR
proposed by Bignozzi et al. (2020). For increasing Λ functions, the ΛVaR can also incorporate
additional elements, such as the judgment of risk managers in the risk management process; see
Bellini and Peri (2022). Furthermore, recent literature confirms several desirable properties of
the ΛVaR. Han et al. (2025) showed that, for an increasing Λ function, the ΛVaR satisfies quasi-
star-shapedness, a property weaker than quasi-convexity that helps mitigate risk concentration.
Additionally, ΛVaR with increasing Λ functions satisfies cash subadditivity, which is particularly
useful for measuring future financial loss under stochastic interest rates; see El Karoui and
Ravanelli (2009) and Han et al. (2025). For properties like monotonicity, locality, and others,
we refer to Bellini and Peri (2022), and for robustness, elicitability, and consistency, see Burzoni
et al. (2017). The characterization of ΛVaR was given in Bellini and Peri (2022) and Chambers
et al. (2025) respectively.
The ΛVaR has been applied to several problems in quantitative risk management recently.
The risk sharing problem for ΛVaR under homogeneous beliefs was studied in Liu (2025) and
Xia and Hu (2025), and under heterogeneous beliefs it was studied in Liu et al. (2025), where
expressions for the inf-convolution and forms of the optimal allocation were derived. Additionally,

the ΛVaR has been applied to optimal reinsurance design with model homogeneity in Balb´as
et al. (2023) and Boonen et al. (2025), as well as to robust portfolio selection and sensitivity
analysis in Han and Liu (2025) and Ince et al. (2022).
Since the introduction of convex and coherent risk measures by Artzner et al. (1999), F¨ollmer
and Schied (2002) and Frittelli and Rosazza Gianin (2005), the risk sharing problem for risk
measures has been studied extensively. The risk sharing for convex risk measures was studied by
Barrieu and El Karoui (2005), Jouini et al. (2008) and Filipovi´c and Svindland (2008), showing
the optimal allocations are comonotonic if the risk measures are law-invariant. Recently, more
focus has been placed on the risk sharing problem for non-convex risk measures, such as quantile-
based risk measures including Value-at-Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall (ES) as special cases
(e.g., Embrechts et al. (2018), Liu et al. (2022) and Weber (2018)) and distortion riskmetrics
including inter-quantile-range as an example (e.g., Lauzier et al. (2023b)). In those papers, it
is shown that the optimal allocation is pairwise countermonotonic, as defined in Lauzier et al.
(2023a). The risk sharing problem with heterogeneous beliefs was studied in Embrechts et al.
(2020), Liu (2020), Liebrich (2024), Liu et al. (2025), Amarante et al. (2015) and Boonen and
Ghossoub (2020). Under ambiguity, the risk sharing for several VaR was investigated in Liu et
al. (2024), deriving the expressions of the inf-convolution and the optimal allocations, and the
comonotonic risk sharing for VaR with uncertainty sets characterized by Wassertein ball was
studied in Liu et al. (2022).

Notation and Definitions
For a given measurable space (Ω, F), let X0 denote the collection of all bounded random
variables. Moreover, let X be a set of random variables containing X0. We do not specify X but
we suppose X has good enough properties to conduct our study such as X = X0 or X1, where
X1 represents the set of all random variables.
For a mapping ρ : X →R, we say that ρ is monotone if X ⩽Y implies ρ(X) ⩽ρ(Y ); and
ρ is cash-additive if ρ(X + c) = ρ(X) + c for X ∈X and c ∈R; ρ is cash-subadditive (resp.
cash-supadditive) if ρ(X + m) ⩽ρ(X) + m (resp. ρ(X + m) ⩾ρ(X) + m) for all X ∈X and
m ⩾0; ρ is quasi-star-shaped if ρ(λX + (1 −λ)t) ⩽max{ρ(X), ρ(t)} for all X ∈X, t ∈R and
λ ∈[0, 1]. We say ρ is a monetary risk measure if it is monotone and cash-additive. We refer to
Chapter 4 of F¨ollmer and Schied (2025) for more details on the properties, characterization and
applications of risk measures.
In the context of capital requirements, cash-subadditivity considers the time value of money
by allowing a non-linear increase in the capital requirement as cash is added to the future financial

convexity, and its theoretic-decision interpretation can be found at Han et al. (2025).

Conclusion
This paper investigates robust models of ΛVaR where the ambiguity sets are composed of
probability measures. We demonstrate that when Λ is increasing, the robust ΛVaR corresponds
to a ΛVaR defined under a capacity, which generally does not constitute a probability measure.
We establish several key properties of this extended risk measure, including monotonicity, cash-
subadditivity, and quasi-star-shapedness. Furthermore, we provide an equivalent representation
of ΛVaRw for monotone Λ using families of downsets.
Our analysis yields broad results in the context of risk sharing, showing that risk measures
induced by families of downsets are closed under inf-convolution.
This implies that the inf-
convolution of ΛVaRw results in another risk measure of the same type. Specifically, we prove
that the class of risk measures of the form ΛVaRw with a monotone function Λ is stable under
inf-convolution. However, whether this property holds for non-monotone Λ remains an open
question and warrants further investigation.
Additionally, we derive risk-sharing results under uncertainty sets defined by ϕ-divergence
and likelihood ratios.
