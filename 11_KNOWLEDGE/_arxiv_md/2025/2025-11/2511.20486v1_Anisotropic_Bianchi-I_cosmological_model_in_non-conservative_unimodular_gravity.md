In this article, we propose an anisotropic Bianchi-I type cosmological model in non-conservative
Unimodular Gravity (NUG). We show that simply using the Bianchi-I type metric does not resolve
a striking characteristic of the field equations in NUG: their underdetermination. This fact led us
to implement extra conditions on the combination (ρ + p) and, consequently, obtain a consistent
background cosmological analysis. In the vacuum case, we obtain an analogy between the Kasner
solutions and the equations in NUG. We also propose a new analysis of a non-homogeneous equation
of state, the combination (ρ + p) = l.
We identify that the cosmological dynamics are strictly
dependent on the value of the constant l. The physically interesting case is at the value l < 0,
which seems to indicate a super-accelerated, ghost-like universe. This case still requires a more
detailed analysis, for example, from a thermodynamic point of view, keeping in mind that (ρ + p)
may be interpreted as enthalpy of the system. For the cases (ρ + p) ∝a−3 and (ρ + p) ∝a−4, we
obtain a description consistent with the anisotropic cosmological model described by GR. In all
cases analyzed, a small value for the anisotropic parameter ΩA (on the order of 10−2) is required in
order to have agreement, for example, with the age of the universe to be approximately 12−14 Gyr,
agreeing with the age of globular clusters. As the universe expands an isotropization is verified,
with the anistropies going to zero asymptotically, similarly with what happens in an anistropic
cosmological model based on GR.
Keywords: Gravitation, Unimodular Gravity, Cosmology, Anisotropic model.
I.
INTRODUCTION
After just over a century of its theoretical development, General Relativity GR [1] remains the subject of considerable
debate in the literature. Despite its success in explaining several phenomena (such as the theoretical prediction of
black holes and gravitational waves, both confirmed by observations), the theory has difficulties in explaining other
phenomena. The Standard Cosmological Scenario, for example, which is based on GR, needs the introduction of a dark
sector which remains without any direct detection. Furthermore, GR predicts the existence of singularities either inside
black holes and in the early universe. These theoretical predictions are a great challenge for the theory. On the other
side, applying GR theory to cosmology, together with the cosmological principle, which proposes spatial homogeneity
and isotropy on large scales of the Universe, leads to a successful theoretical model in describing the Universe as a
whole: the Standard Cosmological Model ΛCDM [2–4]. It simply explains the current phase of accelerated expansion
of the universe and give the best fits observational data. However, as already mentioned, it must introduce two
exotic components of unknown origin into its description of the dark sector: dark matter and dark energy [2, 4].
Regarding the data, recent observations indicate unexpected features in the microwave sky, such as the absence of
variance and correlation at the largest angular scales and the alignment of multipolar moments [5–7], and this seems
to indicate a violation of statistical isotropy at large scales [6, 8]. Indeed, even though observations of the cosmic
microwave background (CMB) [9, 10] is in agreement with ΛCDM model based on the cosmological principle, it seems
to exhibited several anisotropic features on large angular scales, and these are the subject of studies in the literature,
see [11, 12]. This motivates us to explore theoretical models that exhibit a violation of the cosmological principle
while maintaining spatial homogeneity and introducing new forms of anisotropy. Numerous studies have been carried
out in this regard on anisotropic models [13–18]. An example in the context of dark energy can be seen in [19, 20]
and references therein.
On the other hand, the dark energy responsible for the current accelerated expansion of the Universe is driven
(modeled) by the Cosmological Constant (CC) Λ [3, 21, 22] where, at this point, one of the fundamental problems of

Shortly after GR′s theoretical development, several issues were debated, and new alternative theories were created
in the quest for a better understanding of gravitational phenomena. And the author of GR himself, Albert Einstein,
initiated what is now considered one of the simplest alternative theories in by restricting the determinant of the metric
tensor √−g = 1, obtaining a considerable simplification in the search for solutions to the gravitational field equations
[25]. Later, this restriction re-elaborated through the principle of least action in which the condition on the determinant
of the metric tensor would appear as a constraint in the system [26, 27]. This approach was named Unimodular Theory
or simply Unimodular Gravity (UG). One of the main features of this theory is its trace-free field equations. A direct
consequence of this is the absence of information about, for example, geometric quantities such as the Ricci scalar.
Assuming conservation of the energy-momentum tensor as an extra condition in the solution of the complete set of
equations in the cosmological context yields exactly GR + Λ ; however, here Λ is a simple constant of integration
associated with CC. This has sometimes been considered as a possible solution to the CC problem, see [24, 28, 29].
We also highlight that the condition trace-free of the equations makes it sensitive only to the combination (ρ + p)
which, from a thermodynamic point of view, represents the enthalpy [30]. Furthermore, unlike in GR, the Bianchi
Identities (BI) in UG do not lead directly to conservation of the energy-momentum tensor, which makes it possible
to explore it as a non-conservative theory, see [31, 32]. Although this can be somewhat mapped into GR as a Λ(t),
this possibility has been gaining prominence in the literature, especially in the cosmological context, as a discussion
about a dynamic CC Λ (t) in UG [33] or even a possible solution to the tension problem in H0 [34, 35]. However,
when the conservation of the energy-momentum tensor is not separately imposed, an underdetermined system of
equations is obtained and its solution will only be possible if an extra condition is imposed see [30, 32, 33, 36]. In
this case, a parallel to the the GR approach to a dynamic cosmological term can be traced. Given the many open
questions still related to both GR and UG, we would like to propose an analysis of anisotropic Bianchi I-type models
in non-conservative unimodular theory. It is worth highlighting that the term ’non-conservative’ means generalizing
the usual minimal conservation laws, introducing a new connection between geometry and matter fields. It should
be kept in mind that this term does not mean abandoning the general conservation laws in the strict sense. We will
explore the features arising from trace-free equations and non-conservation. Under these conditions, can we achieve
a system that is not underdetermined? How does the universe evolve for specific cases of the combination (ρ + p)?
Can we find a viable model description for our universe? These are some of the questions we would like to discuss in
this paper.

VII.
CONCLUSIONS
In summary, we studied an anisotropic Bianchi I-type cosmological model in non-conservative Unimodular Gravity.
One of the main striking characteristics in NUG is the underdetermination of the complete set of equations that
describe the cosmological dynamics; that is, to solve it, we need to provide extra information to the set of equations.
We saw that studying an anisotropic cosmological model in NUG does not resolve this pathology in its structure. To
circumvent this situation, we propose extra information about the combination (ρ + p).
For the case (ρ + p) = 0, the vacuum case, we obtain a cosmological dynamic very close to that described according
to GR + Λ, as discussed in section (III). We find an analytical solution for the scale factor (44) and asymptotically
the universe experiences a decelerated expansion (t →0+) evolving into an accelerated de Sitter-type expansion
(t →+∞). For values of ΩA = 0.01 and, consequently, ΩU = 0.99 we find the age of the universe to be on the order
of ≈14.0 Gyr. Kasner’s solution is also a solution in NUG, as we demonstrated in subsection (III A). From Kasner’s
conditions for the indices pi, we obtain a relationship between the constants X1, X2, and ΩA that is exactly the same
as the one we defined. This fact demonstrates that the parameter ΩA is connected in the geometry of the Bianchi
I-type metric and the field equations in NUG.
We also carried out a completely new analysis which proved to be quite intriguing; the combination (ρ + p) = l = cte,
in section (IV). This mere consideration leads to a logarithmic term appearing in the Hubble expansion rate (70).
This is typical of a non-barotropic cosmic fluid. We find from the analysis of this case that the cosmological dynamics
in the future are strictly dependent on the sign of the constant l. For l > 0 (Ωl > 0) the universe in the future reaches
a maximum value and recolapses. Therefore, problems with E2 (a) < 0 lead to a loss of credibility of the model. On
the other hand, the case l < 0 (Ωl < 0) demonstrated a more interesting characteristic in the cosmological sense. In
the future, the term that has a logarithmic value becomes positive, and the universe finds itself in a super-accelerated
state, as highlighted in the figure (2). When we recover the isotropy, the characteristics remain for both cases. If we
consider Ωl < 0, the universe evolves from one infinite value to another infinite value, passing through a minimum,
which would be a non-singular and eternal universe. Exploring this solution in other cosmological concepts, such
as the description of gravitational waves and the formation of structures in the universe, should be key to a better
understanding of NUG.
Furthermore, the thermodynamic context can also be explored in an attempt to clarify
enthalpy, since the combination (ρ + p) can be interpreted as the enthalpy density of the system.

For the cases where the combination (ρ + p) ∝a−3 and (ρ + p) ∝a−4 in NUG, we find something very similar
to the description of an anisotropic (isotropic) cosmological model according to GR + Λ. There are two points that
differentiate both approaches; the first is the term ΩU which appears naturally (in all cases analyzed) as a simple
integration constant, and we highlight its important role in cosmological dynamics, similar to CC. Furthermore,
a second point is the combination (ρ + p), which, we emphasize once again, is associated with the enthalpy of the
system, and in both cases the chosen behavior is valid regardless of the fluid considered; that is, we are not assuming
any equation of state of the type pi = ωiρi in choosing the combination (ρ + p).
We would like to point out that the choices for the combination (ρ + p) can also be mapped onto models in GR
with a dynamic cosmological term, which also requires an ansatz to obtain a complete set of equations.
Regarding the anisotropic term in the cosmological equations in NUG, for all cases studied it always appears with
a factor a−6 similar to the case described according to GR. Now, regarding the fractional energy density parameter
ΩA we note, that in order to have an age of the universe compatible with the age of globular cluster [41–43], it must
be around ΩA ≈10−2. However, we emphasize that to ensure the value of ΩA, a more complete statistical analysis
comparing it with cosmological data should be applied.
