Abstract. For d ≥1, we define semibricks and wide subcategories in the d-
extended hearts of bounded t-structures on a triangulated category. We show that
these semibricks are in bijection with finite-length wide subcategories. When the d-
extended heart is the d-extended module category d- mod Λ of a finite-dimensional
algebra Λ over a field, we define left/right-finite semibricks and left/right-finite wide
subcategories in d- mod Λ and show bijections with (d + 1)-term simple-minded
collections, generalising the bijections between 2-term simple-minded collections,
left/right-finite wide subcategories and left/right-finite semibricks in mod Λ. We use
a relation between semibricks and silting complexes to characterise which mutations
of (d + 1)-term silting complexes are again (d + 1)-term.

1. Introduction
Let Λ be a finite-dimensional algebra over a field K. In the representation theory
of finite-dimensional algebras, simple modules play a fundamental role, generating
the category of finite-dimensional modules under extensions. The notion of simple
modules can be generalised to the class of bricks, i.e., modules whose endomorphism
ring is a division algebra. These have been extensively studied in the literature. Gen-
eralising the fact that there are no non-zero morphisms between two non-isomorphic
simple modules, one considers the class of semibricks, i.e., a collection of pairwise
Hom-orthogonal bricks. A classical result of Ringel [28, § 1.2] shows that these are
in bijection with wide subcategories of mod Λ, i.e., full subcategories that are closed
under kernels, cokernels, and extensions.
In the context of τ-tilting theory, Asai [5] introduced a finiteness condition on
semibricks: a semibrick is called left-finite if the smallest torsion class containing
it is functorially finite. He then showed that these left-finite semibricks are in bi-
jection with functorially finite torsion classes in mod Λ.
Under Ringel’s bijection
between semibricks and wide subcategories, he subsequently extended the notion of
left-finiteness to wide subcategories. This allows him to recover the bijection of [23]
between left-finite wide subcategories and functorially finite torsion classes. This was
first shown to be the case for hereditary algebras in [14].
Viewing the simple modules as objects in the derived category Db(mod Λ), one
generalises them to simple-minded collections, which are collections of objects whose
endomorphism algebras are division rings, subject to certain additional conditions.
A simple-minded collection is called 2-term if the cohomology of all its elements
is concentrated in degrees −1, 0. Asai then showed that the left-finite semibricks in
mod Λ are also in bijection with these 2-term simple-minded collections in Db(mod Λ).
These 2-term simple-minded collections were also shown to be in bijection with 2-term
silting objects in [7].
Asai also gave a direct bijection between left-finite semibricks and support τ-tilting
modules in mod Λ using the quotient of a τ-tilting module by its radical over its
endomorphism ring. Support τ-tilting modules were introduced in [2] as a ‘mutation
completion’ of tilting modules and were shown to be in bijection with functorially
finite torsion classes in mod Λ and with 2-term silting objects in Kb(proj Λ). The link
between 2-term silting objects and support τ-tilting modules can also be found in the
work of Derksen and Fei [9]. In [27], functorially finite torsion classes were shown to
be in bijection with complete cotorsion classes in K[−1,0](proj Λ). A summary of these
results is provided in Figure 1. In [12], a further bijection was provided between left-
finite wide subcategories in mod Λ and thick subcategories with enough injectives in
K[−1,0](proj Λ). Several relations between (co) torsion classes, 2-term silting objects,
intermediate t-structures, and intermediate co-t-structures have also been studied [7,
16, 27].
Our goal in this paper is to generalise the above results to a ‘higher’ setting. More
precisely, we want to generalise the above classes in order to get bijections with
arbitrary (d + 1)-term simple-minded collections, d ≥1. This is done by considering

(3) Hom(S, S′) = 0 for any S ̸= S′ ∈S.
