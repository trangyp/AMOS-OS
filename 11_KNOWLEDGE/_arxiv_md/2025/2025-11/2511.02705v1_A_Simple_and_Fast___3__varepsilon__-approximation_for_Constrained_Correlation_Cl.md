A Simple and Fast (3 + ε)-approximation for
Constrained Correlation Clustering
Nate Veldt
Department of Computer Science and Engineering
Texas A&M University
nveldt@tamu.edu
Abstract
In Constrained Correlation Clustering, the goal is to cluster a complete signed graph in a way
that minimizes the number of negative edges inside clusters plus the number of positive edges between
clusters, while respecting hard constraints on how to cluster certain friendly or hostile node pairs. Fischer
et al. [FKKT25a] recently developed a ˜O(n3)-time 16-approximation algorithm for this problem. We
settle an open question posed by these authors by designing an algorithm that is equally fast but brings
the approximation factor down to (3 + ε) for arbitrary constant ε > 0. Although several new algorithmic
steps are needed to obtain our improved approximation, our approach maintains many advantages in
terms of simplicity. In particular, it relies mainly on rounding a (new) covering linear program, which
can be approximated quickly and combinatorially. Furthermore, the rounding step amounts to applying
the very familiar Pivot algorithm to an auxiliary graph. Finally, we develop much simpler algorithms for
instances that involve only friendly or only hostile constraints.

Introduction
Given a complete unweighted signed graph G = (V, E+, E−), Correlation Clustering (CC) seeks to
partition V in a way that minimizes the number of positive edges between clusters (called positive mistakes)
plus negative edges inside clusters (negative mistakes) [BBC04]. The problem has been studied extensively
from the perspective of approximation algorithms, and variants of the problem have been applied to di-
verse applications in social network analysis [VGW18, YSM+24, LDPM17], bioinformatics [BD08, BDY99],
databases [HCLM09], computer vision [KNKY11, YIF12], and more. See the work of Bonchi et al. [BGSG22]
for a recent detailed survey.
This paper focuses on Constrained Correlation Clustering (ConstrainedCC), a variant of CC
in which G is accompanied by a set of friendly edges F and hostile edges H [vZW09, FKKT25a, KKV25].
The goal is to minimize mistakes while ensuring that both endpoints of each friendly edge are in the same
cluster, and both endpoints of each hostile edge are in separate clusters. Our main contribution is a ˜O(n3)-
time (3 + ε)-algorithm for ConstrainedCC, where n = |V |, ε > 0 is an arbitrary constant, and ˜O-notation
hides polylogarithmic factors (i.e., O(polylog(n)) = ˜O(1)). This matches the best approximation factor of
3 [vZW09] up to an arbitrarily small constant, while satisfying desirable properties in terms of speed and
simplicity. In particular, the latter 3-approximation relies on a canonical linear programming relaxation
that takes Ω(n7.11) time to solve, whereas ours is much faster and can be made completely combinatorial.
Our result directly answers an open question of Fischer et al. [FKKT25a], who developed a ˜O(n3)-time
16-approximation for ConstrainedCC, and asked whether it was possible to improve the approximation
factor to 3 with the same runtime. Before discussing our techniques and contributions in detail, we provide
a more in-depth look at prior work on CC and ConstrainedCC.

1.1
Prior work
We first review key algorithmic techniques for standard CC that rely on three different types of linear
programming (LP) relaxations for CC, shown in Figure 1.
Variants of these LPs have also been very
influential in the development of ConstrainedCC algorithms.
Best approximation factors using expensive LPs. Correlation Clustering was introduced by
Bansal et al. [BBC04], who proved NP-hardness and gave a constant-factor approximation. The approxi-
mation was later reduced to 4 by rounding the natural Canonical LP (see Figure 1, left) [CGW05]. The
best factor was then improved to 2.5 [ACN08] and then 2.06 [CMSY15] by rounding the same LP. Recently,
a sequence of breakthrough results using more expensive relaxations led to approximation factors below
2 [CALN22, CALLN23, CCAL+24], culminating in a (1.437 + ε)-approximation. These results can all be
described as rounding schemes for a more expensive Cluster LP relaxation (Figure 1, middle). The Cluster
LP has an exponential number of variables, but prior work has shown how to approximate it in polynomial
time [CCAL+24] or even sublinear time [CCAL+25].
Though significant from a theoretical perspective,
algorithms relying on these LP relaxations exhibit disadvantages in terms of simplicity and speed. The
Canonical LP has Θ(n3) constraints, and takes Ω(n3ω) time to solve (which is Ω(n7.11) using the current
best bound for ω) even using the best theoretical LP solvers. There is some prior work on practical solvers for
the Canonical LP [VGWS19, RVG20, SG22], but these have only scaled to graphs with under two hundred
thousand nodes. Meanwhile, the sublinear time approximation algorithm for the Cluster LP [CCAL+25]
is an exciting breakthrough result, but is complicated and has a runtime that depends exponentially on a
high-degree polynomial in terms of (1/ε), for a small ε > 0. To our knowledge, no one has attempted to
implement any approximate solvers for the Cluster LP.
Fast and simple algorithms using the Charging LP. A significant amount of prior work focuses on
faster and simpler algorithms for CC and its variants, at the expense of worse approximation factors [ACN08,
Vel22, MC23, CALP+24, BXCV24, FKKT25a]. These often rely in some way on the simpler Charging LP
(Figure 1, right) for CC. The best-known example is the celebrated Pivot algorithm [ACN08], which obtains
an expected 3-approximation for CC by repeatedly selecting a random pivot node and clustering it with all
its positive neighbors. Pivot never solves the Charging LP explicitly, but Ailon et al. [ACN08] proved that
the expected cost of this algorithm is within a factor 3 of a feasible solution for the dual of this LP.
The Charging LP is a covering LP, meaning that it has the form minx cT x s.t. Ax ≥b, x ≥0 where A,
b, and c all have nonnegative entries. This special structure comes with many benefits in terms of speed
and simplicity. Fischer et al. [FKKT25a] recently used specialized solvers for covering LPs to quickly find
approximate solutions for the Charging LP. They used these solvers to develop a derandomized version
of Pivot that achieves a (3 + ε)-approximation for CC in ˜O(n3) time. There are even simpler and faster
techniques for finding a binary feasible solution that 3-approximates this LP, by finding a maximal variable-
disjoint set of constraints. This has led to extremely fast and simple constant-factor approximations for
CC and certain weighted variants [Vel22, BV23, BXCV24], which come with simple implementations that
scale easily to graphs with millions of nodes. The Charging LP was also used recently to develop the first

for the Cluster LP to the constrained case would lead to an improved poly-time approximation factor for
ConstrainedCC. However, this approach would exhibit the same disadvantages (with respect to simplicity
and implementation) associated with solving the standard Cluster LP.
Recently, Fischer et al. [FKKT25a] provided a simple approach for converting the input graph G into
a new auxiliary graph ˆG, such that running an α-approximate Pivot algorithm on ˆG produces an O(α)-
approximation for the original ConstrainedCC problem on G.
They then applied their deterministic
(3 + ε)-approximate Pivot algorithm (for unconstrained CC) to ˆG. The overall procedure takes only ˜O(n3)-
time, but has an approximation factor of 16. The authors therefore posed the following question:
Open Question [FKKT25a]. Can we improve the approximation factor of Constrained Correlation
Clustering from 16 to 3 while keeping the running time at ˜O(n3)?
A closely related question is whether we can obtain a 3-approximation algorithm by rounding some type
of covering LP with O(n3) constraints, as prior algorithms for variants of CC that apply this strategy tend
to be particularly simple and easy to implement.
1.2
Our approach and contributions
This paper settles the open question of Fischer [FKKT25a] by presenting a ˜O(ε−3n3)-time (3+ε)-approximation
algorithm for ConstrainedCC. Our algorithm is based on rounding a new covering LP, and therefore en-
joys many benefits in terms of simplicity. Our covering LP generalizes the Charging LP in Figure 1 for
standard CC by incorporating two new types of constraints. The first type ensures that if two edges are
forced to be clustered in the same way because of constraints F ∪H, then their LP variables are equal. The
second special type of constraint lower bounds LP variables for certain triplets of positive edges where every
feasible solution is guaranteed to make at least one mistake. We refer to these as Hostile Edge And Positives
(HEAP) constraints; see Section 3 for a formal definition. We show that enforcing O(n3) such constraints
is sufficient for our analysis.
After approximately solving our covering LP relaxation, we use its output to construct an auxiliary graph
ˆG. We show that applying Pivot to ˆG is sufficient to obtain the (3 + ε)-approximation. Our algorithm can
be made completely combinatorial and deterministic. This approach shares similarities with the work of
Fischer et al. [FKKT25a], but we consider a different type of covering LP, apply a different auxiliary graph
construction, and then provide a more in-depth approximation analysis to achieve the tighter approximation.
As a final contribution, we present substantially simplified algorithms for instances that only involve
friendly constraints (FriendlyCC) or hostile constraints (HostileCC). For the latter, we develop a (3+ε)-
approximation by rounding a simpler covering LP without HEAP constraints. For HostileCC, we do not
even need to solve a covering LP, although one is considered implicitly in the analysis. Instead, we construct
an auxiliary graph based on information from an edge-disjoint set of dangerous triangles (involving two
positive edges and a hostile edge), and then run a randomized Pivot algorithm on it.
This results in a
(randomized) 3-approximate algorithm that runs in O(n3) time; note that there is no dependence on a
constant ε > 0 nor logarithmic factors in the runtime.

5
Conclusion and Discussion
We have presented a (3 + ε)-approximation algorithm for ConstrainedCC with a runtime of ˜O(n3) time,
settling the open question of Fischer et al. [FKKT25a] up to an arbitrarily small constant ε > 0.
The
algorithm is simple in that it applies a familiar pivoting framework and can be made purely combinatorial.
It also relies on rounding a covering LP relaxation, rather than on a variant of the Canonical LP or the
Cluster LP, which are more expensive and complicated to solve or approximate.
Our work also includes simpler algorithms when there are only hostile or only friendly constraints. Each
simplified algorithm includes a key advance needed to generalize the celebrated 3-approximate Pivot algorithm
to a case involving one of the new constraint types. For FriendlyCC, the main advance is to incorporate
superedge constraints into a new covering LP formulation. The rest follows mostly from a straightforward
application of the techniques of van Zuylen and Williamson [vZW09]. For HostileCC, a key step is to
note that the algorithm will make a large number of mistakes at a certain class of edges (ED). This leads
to a tighter analysis than would be obtained by directly applying techniques of van Zuylen and Williamson
(e.g., applying Theorem 3.1 of [vZW09]) as a black box. We remark that our strategy for HostileCC
directly generalizes the analysis for a recent 3-approximation for Cluster Deletion [BXCV24], which can
be viewed as an instance of ConstrainedCC where all negative edges are hostile.
The (3 + ε)-approximation for ConstrainedCC combines the key advances used by algorithms for
FriendlyCC and HostileCC. However, by themselves these advances appear insufficient to produce a
(3 + ε)-approximation for ConstrainedCC. In particular, the full algorithm for ConstrainedCC also
uses the notion of HEAP constraints, to ensure that a key inequality (see Lemma 3.4) holds for certain bad
triangles. Although O(n3) HEAP constraints are sufficient to guarantee the (3 + ε)-approximation, an open
question is whether they are strictly necessary to obtain this approximation, or if a different proof strategy
could avoid them. Finally, it is interesting to note that for HostileCC, we can obtain an O(n3)-time 3-
approximation, where the approximation does not depend on ε > 0, the runtime does not involve logarithmic
factors, and there is no need to explicitly solve an LP relaxation. An interesting question is whether this
could also be achieved for ConstrainedCC, or even just for FriendlyCC, using alternative techniques.
