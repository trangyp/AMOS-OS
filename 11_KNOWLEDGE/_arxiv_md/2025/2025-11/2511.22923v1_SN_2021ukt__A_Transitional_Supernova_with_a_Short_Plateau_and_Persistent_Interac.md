ABSTRACT
We present spectroscopic and photometric observations of supernova (SN) 2021ukt, a peculiar short-
plateau object that was originally identified as a Type IIn SN and later underwent an unprecedented
transition to a Type Ib (possibly Type IIb) SN. The early-time light curves of SN 2021ukt exhibit a
∼25 day plateau. Such a short phase of hydrogen recombination suggests a rather thin H-rich outer
envelope of the progenitor star. The relatively narrow Balmer emission lines in spectra of SN 2021ukt
during the first week indicate the interaction between the expanding ejecta and the immediate circum-
stellar material (CSM). This Hα line is observed throughout its helium-rich ejecta-dominated phase
and nebular phase, suggesting persistent interaction with a radially extended CSM profile. We ex-
plore the synthetic light-curve model among grids of parameters generated by MESA+STELLA. We also
compare the spectrophotometric evolution of SN 2021ukt with several well-sampled supernovae that
exhibit a short plateau and persistent ejecta-CSM interaction. An estimate of the progenitor mass of
SN 2021ukt is made based on the flux ratio between [Ca II] λλ7291, 7324 and [O I] λλ6300, 6364 during
its nebular phase. Our analysis suggests that the progenitor star of SN 2021ukt has a zero-age main-
sequence (ZAMS) mass of MZAMS ≈12 M⊙, a mass of radioactive 56Ni synthesized in the SN ejecta

1. INTRODUCTION
When the core of a massive star (M > 8 M⊙) consists
of iron and is no longer able to undergo nuclear fusion,
it collapses under its own gravity to form a protoneu-
tron star, and the stellar envelope falls inward.
The
protoneutron star rebounds, sending a shock wave out-
ward that triggers an explosion of the stellar envelope,
propelled mostly by neutrino interactions. This process
leads to a core-collapse (CC) supernova (SN). Among all
known CC supernovae (SNe), about 63% are found to be
hydrogen-rich Type II supernovae (hereafter SNe II; e.g.,
Smartt 2009; Smith et al. 2011). The observed diversity
among H-rich CCSNe can be attributed to differences in
the mass of the progenitor star and the mass of the hy-
drogen envelope at the moment of explosion; the latter
can also be influenced by mass loss and binary interac-
tions (Nomoto et al. 1995; Eldridge et al. 2018; Dessart
et al. 2011, and references therein).
Immediately after the initial rise in the light curve,
SNe IIP exhibit a distinctive ∼80–120 day plateau.
SNe IIL are observed with a characteristic linear de-
cline in magnitude.
SNe IIb display spectra with ini-
tially H-dominant to later He-dominant phases in the
first month after the SN explosion (Filippenko et al.
1993), indicating a thinner hydrogen envelope compared
to other Type II subtypes.
SNe IIn exhibit evidence
of interaction between the ejecta and the circumstel-
lar material (CSM) through relatively narrow Balmer
emission lines (Schlegel 1990). Based on pre-explosion
imaging and stellar-evolution models, the likely progen-
itor scenarios are red supergiants (RSGs) with masses
in the range 10–17 M⊙and extended H-rich envelopes
for SNe IIP (Chevalier 1976; Falk & Arnett 1977); yel-
low supergiants (YSGs) with a binary companion for
SNe IIb (Filippenko et al. 1993; Woosley et al. 1994;
Maund et al. 2004); and luminous blue variable stars
undergoing enhanced mass loss for SNe IIn (Humphreys
& Davidson 1994; Gal-Yam & Leonard 2009; Smith et al.
2011). The progenitors of SNe IIL are not yet well de-
termined, but are possibly RSGs or YSGs (see Van Dyk
2017 for review). Among the wide diversity of H-rich
CCSNe, detailed investigations are establishing links be-

tween various subtypes. For example, Anderson et al.
(2014) suggest that a continuum of pre-explosion mass-
loss history and evolution, primarily influenced by the
amount of H retained by the progenitor star, may ac-
count for the transition from the no plateau (SNe IIL)
to short and long plateau SN II subtypes. Historically,
observations of short plateaus (lasting ∼20–70 days af-
ter first light) remain scarce (Hiramatsu et al. 2021).
Some progenitors can lose their outer H and/or
He envelope and explode as H-poor or even H-free
stripped-envelope CCSNe (SESNe); for reviews, see Fil-
ippenko (1997) and Pian & Mazzali (2017).
The
subclasses of SESNe include H-poor SNe IIb, H-free
(or highly H-poor) SNe Ib, and H-free + He-poor/free
SNe Ic (Woosley et al. 1994). The progenitors of SESNe
are likely low-mass stars in binary systems (Nomoto
et al. 1995; Prentice et al. 2018; Modjaz et al. 2019). The
envelope stripping can be caused by both stellar winds
from sufficiently massive stars (Ud-Doula et al. 2009)
and (more frequently) binary interactions (Smith 2014).
Some studies suggest that these envelopes are stripped
in the late stages of stellar mass loss (see, e.g., Ofek et al.
2014; Strotjohann et al. 2021).
Observations of CCSNe have shown signatures of in-
teraction with pre-existing CSM. The relatively narrow
or intermediate-width Hα emission (full width at half-
maximum intensity (FWHM) ≈1000 km s−1) observed
in SNe IIn (e.g., SN 2010jl; Ofek et al. 2014) is found to
be the result of interaction between the ejecta and dense,
optically thick, H-rich CSM (Leonard et al. 2000; Ran-
some et al. 2022). Furthermore, late-time observations
months after the explosion can reveal interaction with
the ejecta and an extended CSM shell (see, e.g., Math-
eson et al. 2000; Andrews & Smith 2018). One peculiar
case of CSM interaction was with SN 2014C, which ini-
tially behaved as a normal SN Ib. After ∼100 days, the
spectrum revealed relatively narrow Hα emission. This
was found to be partly due to delayed interaction with
CSM formed by the material stripped from the star (Mil-
isavljevic et al. 2015).
Here we present optical spectroscopy and photometry
of SN 2021ukt.

display a close resemblance to features of SNe IIn, and
later observations reveal features resembling those of
SNe Ib with evidence of CSM interaction. Meanwhile,

SN 2021ukt (ZTF21abpxquj) was discovered by the
Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF; Bellm et al. 2019; Gra-
ham et al. 2019) on July 31, 2021, 10:34:47 (UTC dates
are used throughout this paper; MJD 59426.44) in the r
band at 16.281 ± 0.031 mag (De 2021). The last nonde-
tection was obtained by the Asteroid Terrestrial-impact

26, 2021, 13:23:31 (MJD 59421.55) in the o band at
18.670 mag.
The five-day gap between the last non-
detection and the first detection makes the time of first

midpoint between the last nondetection and first detec-

first light. Throughout this paper, all phases are given
relative to the estimated date of first light.

3.1. CSM-Dominated Phase
The left panel of Figure 1 shows the CSM-dominated
spectrum of SN 2021ukt (+3 days, SNIFS), which is
characterized by various emission lines superimposed on
a blackbody continuum of ∼10, 000 K (Hinkle 2021).
The strongest emission lines can be identified as the
characteristic H I Balmer series (in blue), which are of-
ten presented in the early spectra of SNe IIn, such as
SN 2010jl (Ofek et al. 2014) and SN 2006gy (Smith et al.
2010).
The emission lines arise from the correspond-
ing ionic species within the ambient CSM photoionized
by the initial shock wave as it runs through and man-
ifest in the spectrum as broad, Lorentzian wings. The
broad wings of the features are most likely due to scat-
tering by free electrons in the unshocked, photoionized
CSM (Chugai 2001; Dessart et al. 2009; Huang & Cheva-
lier 2018).
To measure the width of such line profiles, after sub-
tracting the underlying continuum through an arbitrary
linear interpolation, we fit a Lorentzian function to the
emission profiles. Figure 3 portrays the Hα profile with
a FHWM = 890 ± 40 km s−1 at day +3 and FHWM =
1130 ± 90 km s−1 at day +5.
Weak emission lines include He I λ5876, N IV λ4058,
and N IV λλ7109, 7123. These lines persisted for the
first ∼eight days, where He I λ6678 and λ7065 also
appear. The growing strength of the broad He I λ5876
and Ca II H&K λλ3933, 3968 absorption lines indicates
the evolution to the ejecta-dominated phase.
3.2. Ejecta-Dominated Phase
On day +5, the photosphere has already receded into
the fast-moving ejecta, as evidenced by the emergence
of the broad P Cygni profiles labeled in the middle panel
of Figure 1. The prompt emergence of the helium-rich
layer of the ejecta as indicated by the development of
the P Cygni profile of the He I λ5876 line indicates a
close resemblance to that observed in other SNe Ib and
IIb at similar phases (see Figure 2; also, e.g., Valenti
et al. 2011). The evolution of SN 2021ukt from its CSM-
dominated phase to its ejecta-dominated phase marks an
unprecedented transition from its original SN IIn classi-
fication to an SN Ib (possibly SN IIb).
As illustrated in Figure 1, the P Cygni profile of the
He I λ5876 line is fully developed.
We estimate the
velocity of the outer He-rich layer of the ejecta by fit-
ting a Gaussian distribution using the scipy.optimize
package to the absorption minimum of this feature.
The absorption minimum is measured at a velocity of
∼15, 000 km s−1, consistent with that measured from
the absorption minima of the He I λ6678 and λ7065
lines.
At +33 days, the Kast and FLOYDS spectral
continua are consistent with a blackbody of ∼5500 K,
and the He I λ5876 absorption minimum shifts to ∼
11, 000 km s−1. At +63 days, continuum emission ceases
and the He I λ5876 absorption minimum further shifts
to ∼9000 km s−1. These values are consistent with the
velocities measured by Yesmin et al. (2025).
He I λ5876 exhibits a flat-topped emission profile in
Figure 1 from +11 to +44 days, while the emission peak
of He I λ7065 is relatively narrow.
From +8 to +72
days, an intermediate-width Hα component is superim-
posed on the broad emission of He I λ6678 and resem-
bles a “shark-fin” shape.
At +44 days, the strength
of He I λ6678 weakens relative to the Hα component,
highlighting the persistence of Hα emission. This sug-

the nebular phase.
3.3. Nebular Phase
The right panel of Figure 1 shows the spectral evolu-
tion of SN 2021ukt from +63 days to +180 days. In the
+63 day FLOYDS spectrum, we observe weaker emis-
sion and shallower absorption profiles of lines that previ-
ously dominated the spectrum, such as He I, Ca II H&K,
and Fe II. This coincides with the growing emission of
the Ca II near-infrared (NIR) triplet alongside forbidden
lines [O I] λλ6300, 6364 and [Ca II] λλ7291, 7324. From
+63 to +78 days, the He I λ6678 line weakens, leaving
only Hα emission along with the forbidden lines.
SN 2021ukt was also observed by Keck/LRIS on day
+180, when the SN had entered the nebular phase (see
the right panel of Figure 1 and Figure 4).
The day
+180 spectrum of SN 2021ukt exhibits several charac-
teristic prominent emission lines, including [O I] λλ6300,
6364, [Ca II] λλ 7291, 7324, Ca II NIR triplet, and Hα.
Furthermore, Mg I] λ4571, Na I λ5890, and O I λ9221
are tentatively detected. The emission peaks of [O I]
λλ6300, 6364 and [Ca II] λλ7291, 7324 also exhibit a
∼1500 km s−1 blueshift with respect to the rest frame.
Oxygen is one of the products of a series of hydro-
static burning phases that exhibits prominent features
in nebular-phase spectra of CCSNe. The oxygen content
in the SN ejecta increases with the zero-age sequence-
mass (ZAMS) of the progenitor star. As fuel for cal-
cium production during the explosive burning phase, the
strength of [O I] λλ6300, 6364 and the [Ca II]/[O I] flux
ratio provide sensitive indicators of the ZAMS mass of
the progenitor of CCSNe (Fransson & Chevalier 1989;
Jerkstrand et al. 2012, 2014; Kuncarayakti et al. 2015;
Dessart et al. 2021; Fang et al. 2022).
We estimate the ZAMS mass by measuring the
[Ca II]/[O I] flux ratio (see the caption of Figure 5).
From the +180 day spectrum, we measure [Ca II]/[O I]
= 1.4 ± 0.05. Some SNe with greater oxygen-core masses
(i.e., [Ca II]/[O I] < 1) represent a population of SN
progenitors in the MZAMS range of 20–40 M⊙(e.g.,
SN Ib 2009jf, Valenti et al. 2011; SN Ic 1998bw, Mazzali
et al. 2001; SN Ic 2008ap, Mazzali et al. 2002). Consid-
ering the relatively low oxygen-core mass of SN 2021ukt,
this suggests an upper limit of MZAMS ≲20 M⊙.
Following the methods outlined by Jerkstrand et al.
(2015), we estimate the ZAMS mass of SN 2021ukt by
comparing the [O I] λλ6300, 6364 luminosity normalized
to the 56Co decay power of SNe IIb (see Eq. 1 of Jerk-
strand et al. 2015). Using the methods for measuring
the flux described above, we convert the [O I] flux to lu-
minosity with our distance measurement and assuming
a 56Ni mass of 0.04 M⊙(see Section 4.2.2). Therefore,
we calculate Lnorm(180) = 0.004 ± 0.0001 and suggest
that this value plausibly matches models 12A and 12D
with MZAMS = 12 M⊙(see Figure 15 of Jerkstrand et al.
2015). This ZAMS mass is consistent with our discus-
sion above.
4. PHOTOMETRIC ANALYSIS
4.1. Optical Light Curve
Figure 6 shows the Lick + LCO optical light curves
of SN 2021ukt during the first five months after the first
light.
SN 2021ukt peaked at V ≈14 mag and main-
tained a nearly constant luminosity for a period of ∼25
days past maximum.
During such an optical plateau
phase, a subtle rise in the R light curve begins around
15 days and peaks at 20 days. We then observe a grad-
ual decrease in flux before the light curve declines to the
56Co-powered radioactive tail, which is typical of other
SNe II.
The plateau observed in SN 2021ukt may be at-
tributed to an optically thick, He-rich envelope that un-
dergoes interaction with an H-rich wind.
The ejecta-
CSM interaction can produce excess emission, which
may lead to a higher peak luminosity and extend the
duration of the plateau phase (see Figure 8 of Hiramatsu
et al. 2021; also, see Matsumoto et al. 2025). However,
CSM interaction alone is not enough to be responsible
for extending the plateau.
Additional factors such as
the progenitor radius and explosion energy may also af-
fect the length of the plateau in CCSNe light curves
(Goldberg et al. 2019). Given the limited number of ob-
servations of short-plateau SNe, SN 2021ukt represents a
unique intermediate case within the continuum of SN IIL
and SN IIP light curves (Anderson et al. 2014).

5. DISCUSSION AND COMPARISONS
Figure 8 compares SN 2021ukt with SNe IIb 2013df
and 2020bio.
Similarly to SN 2021ukt, the first spec-
tra of SNe 2013df and 2020bio obtained within ≲5
days after the SN explosion are dominated by H I
emission that subsequently transitions to show strong
He I emission (Morales-Garoffolo et al. 2014; Pellegrino
et al. 2023). SN 2013df revealed evidence of interaction
through the presence of Hα in the late-time spectrum
(Morales-Garoffolo et al. 2014), while the early interac-
tion of SN 2020bio is evidenced by a remarkably blue
early-time light curve (Pellegrino et al. 2023).
This
could suggest that the progenitors of SNe 2013df and
2020bio had been stripped of their outer envelopes prior
to the explosion.
One natural explanation for strip-
ping is offered by binarity, such as SNe 1993J (Filip-
penko et al. 1993; Woosley et al. 1994; Maund et al.
2004; Stancliffe & Eldridge 2009), 2011dh (Benvenuto
et al. 2012; Shivvers et al. 2013), 2013df (Van Dyk et al.
2014), and 2020bio (Pellegrino et al. 2023). Although
SN 2021ukt did not show remarkably blue light curves
in early phases, one cannot rule out the role of binary
mass transfer. A detailed stellar population synthesis
to examine SN 2021ukt as a binary progenitor system is
beyond the scope of this paper.

light curves;
the primary peak corresponds to the
early shock-cooling emission of an extended outer enve-
lope (Soderberg et al. 2012) and the secondary peak cor-

of SNe 2013df and 2020bio have a low MH,env: Morales-

6. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
We have presented our analysis of the optical spec-
troscopy and photometry of SN 2021ukt.
The persis-
tent Hα emission indicates ongoing interaction with ex-
tended, H-rich material that could have been stripped
from the progenitor prior to its explosion.
Our analysis is limited by several aspects, including
poor constraints of the time of first light. Future ob-
servations of similar objects would greatly benefit from
early-time UV observations, which would have helped
constrain the progenitor environment that may influ-
ence the early-time light curve. Models capturing the
complex physics associated with interaction with CSM
or a binary companion may provide a better fit to the
light curve.
We summarize the primary findings of SN 2021ukt:
• The evolution of SN 2021ukt is the first known SN
to spectroscopically transition from an SN IIn to
an interacting SN Ib (possibly SN IIb).
• Measurements of the [O I] flux are consistent with
a lower-mass progenitor, MZAMS ≈12 M⊙. Com-
parisons with SNe IIb also provide an upper limit
of MH,env ≲0.5 M⊙.
• SN 2021ukt is a member of a scarce population of
SNe II having short-plateau light curves, with an
upper limit on the progenitor MNi ≲0.04 M⊙and
MZAMS ≲25 M⊙.
• Given the fainter radioactive tail relative to other
SNe and synthetic models, SN 2021ukt may have a
low MNi, or low-density, high-velocity ejecta with
inefficient trapping, or a combination of both con-
ditions.
• We interpret the lasting H I emission as persis-
tent interaction with an extended CSM, possibly
formed from H-rich material that was stripped
from the progenitor star prior to the explosion.
We emphasize that the progenitor of SN 2021ukt is
unique. Based on our analysis, the progenitor was likely
an analog to an SN IIn with a lower ZAMS mass. The
H-rich envelope that was stripped from the progenitor
remained sufficiently nearby to interact with the ejecta
and produce relatively narrow emission lines.
As the
stripped material was swept up, the outer layers of the
ejecta were revealed and evolved in close resemblance to
those of SNe Ib with evidence of persistent CSM interac-
tion. More modeling is needed to better understand the
properties of the progenitor and its mass-loss history.
