Distributed Primal-Dual Algorithm for Constrained
Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning under Coupled
Policies
Pengcheng Dai, He Wang, Dongming Wang, and Wenwu Yu Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—This paper investigates constrained multi-agent re-
inforcement learning (CMARL), where agents collaboratively
maximize the sum of their local objectives while satisfying
individual safety constraints. Existing studies on CMARL face
two main limitations: (1) most rely on independent policies that
fail to capture the complex interactions in coupled environments;
and (2) agents must obtain true Lagrange multiplier parameters,
which, unlike environmental information (e.g., state-action pairs
and rewards), are learned variables, and directly exchanging
them increases vulnerability to potential network attacks. To
overcome these issues, we propose a framework where agents
adopt coupled policies that depend on both local states and
parameters, as well as those of their κp-hop neighbors, with
κp > 0 denoting the coupling distance. A distributed primal-dual
algorithm is further developed under this framework, wherein
each agent has access only to state-action pairs within its 2κp-hop
neighborhood and to reward information within its κ + 2κp-
hop neighborhood, with κ > 0 representing the truncation
distance. Moreover, agents are not permitted to directly share
their true policy parameters or Lagrange multipliers. Instead,
each agent constructs and maintains local estimates of these
variables for other agents and employs such estimates to execute
its policy. Additionally, these estimates are further updated and
exchanged exclusively through an independent, time-varying net-
works, which enhances the overall system security. We establish
that, with high probability, our algorithm can achieve an ϵ-first-
order stationary convergence with an approximation error of
O(γ
κ+1
κp ) for discount factor γ ∈(0, 1). Finally, simulations
in GridWorld environment are conducted to demonstrate the
effectiveness of the proposed algorithm.
Index Terms—Constrained multi-agent reinforcement learning,
coupled policies, distributed primal-dual algorithm, ϵ-first-order

Solving CRL problems is inherently challenging, as it
involves simultaneous policy optimization and constraint sat-
isfaction, which substantially increases the complexity of both
algorithm design and theoretical analysis. For CRL problem
with single-agent, a widely adopted classical approach is pro-
vided by Lagrangian-based algorithms [17], [18], [19], where
the primal variable is updated through policy gradient ascent,
and the dual variable is adjusted using projected subgradient
descent.
Although a substantial body of research has been devoted
to CRL in single-agent systems, the development of effective
methodologies for multi-agent environments is still relatively
limited. This limitation can be attributed to two primary
challenges. First, the curse of dimensionality becomes more
pronounced in multi-agent systems, as the joint state-action
space grows exponentially with each additional agent, which
significantly complicates policy optimization. Second, achiev-
ing cooperative optimization among multiple agents poses a
significant challenge, as each agent must coordinate its actions
to contribute to the maximization of a global objective while
adhering to its individual safety constraints. Building on these
insights, the first approach to address CMARL was proposed
in [20], which introduces a distributed policy gradient algo-
rithm for policy optimization over a network. However, this
approach assumes that agents have access to the global state
and therefore does not adequately address the curse of state
dimensionality inherent in multi-agent environments. Inspired
by the exponential decay property, whereby the influence of
other agents’ state-action pairs on a given agent diminishes
with distance in multi-agent systems characterized by localized
state-action interactions, [21] proposed a scalable primal-dual
actor-critic algorithm. In this algorithm, each agent does not
require access to the global state and relies solely on state-
action pairs from its κ-hop neighbors. This design effectively
addresses both primary challenges, enabling efficient coordi-
nation among agents while mitigating the exponential growth
of the joint state-action space.
Despite the above advancements, the framework proposed
in [21] still presents significant limitations. Specifically, it
employs an independent policy architecture, wherein each
agent’s decision-making process relies solely on its local state
and policy parameter, without explicitly accounting for inter-

As discussed above, the aforementioned limitations inspire
our investigation of CMARL under coupled policies, where
an agent’s policy depends not only on its own state and
policy parameter but also on the states and policy param-
eters of its κp-hop neighboring agents. In addition, we re-
strict the exchange of private information, such as the true
Lagrange multipliers and actual policy parameters, to local
neighborhoods within an alternative network, with the aim
of enhancing system security. Although this design offers a
broader range of applicable scenarios while providing im-
proved privacy protection, it also brings several fundamental
challenges. (1) Complexity of coupled policy optimization:
Coupled policies make it highly challenging for agents to
collaboratively optimize their objective while simultaneously
ensuring satisfaction of individual constraints. (2) Privacy-
preserving parameter updates: Both policy parameters and
Lagrange multipliers are sensitive agent-specific information,
and designing mechanisms that allow agents to update these
parameters using only local communications remains a non-
trivial problem. (3) Theoretical analysis challenges: The use
of coupled policies, along with limited access to actual pol-
icy parameters and Lagrange multipliers, presents significant
challenges in theoretical analysis. Since existing analytical
frameworks are designed for independent policies, they are
not applicable in the current context. Therefore, it is essential
to develop new analytical techniques.
To address these challenges, the main contributions of this
work are as follows.
1) To protect sensitive agent-specific information, each
agent maintains local estimates of other agents’ policy
parameters and Lagrange multipliers, while exchanging
its true variables only with immediate neighbors over
a time-varying networks. Accordingly, we develop a
distributed update mechanism for these local estimates
based on the push-sum protocol. This design not only
refines each agent’s understanding of others’ parameters
but also promotes the generation of informative samples
that guide coordinated policy improvement across the
system.
2) In the proposed framework, each agent computes an
approximated policy gradient for policy update, which
depends on the objective rewards, the local constraint
rewards, and the estimated Lagrange multipliers of its
(κ + 2κp)-hop neighborhood, as well as the state-action
pairs and the estimated policy parameters of its 2κp-
hop neighbors. Given access to such environmental
information, agents employ a geometric 2-horizon sam-
pling scheme to estimate this gradient and subsequently
update their policy parameters, enabling coordinated
optimization while satisfying local constraints.
3) A rigorous theoretical framework of the proposed al-
gorithm for an ϵ-first-order stationary convergence is
established. First, we derive an upper bound for the
error between the approximated policy gradient and the
actual policy gradient (see Theorem 1). Subsequently,
we prove that each agent’s estimates of other agents’
policy parameters and Lagrange multiplies converge
to their true values (see Theorem 2). Based on these
theoretical results, we further establish that, with high
probability, our algorithm can achieve an ϵ-first-order
stationary convergence with an approximation error of
O(γ
κ+1
κp ) for discount factor γ ∈(0, 1) (see Theorem 3).
To the best of our knowledge, this work is the first study
of CMARL under coupled policy settings with formal
theoretical guarantees.

VII. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we study the CMARL problem under coupled
policies, with a focus on the setting of distributed learning
without global environmental information. To address the
challenges of scalability and collaborative optimization of
coupled policies, we propose a distributed primal-dual algo-
rithm that enables interactions of state-action pairs and reward
signals within defined neighborhoods across the environmental
network. Additionally, the algorithm allows for the exchange
of policy parameters over a separate, time-varying learning
network. We also establish theoretical guarantees, showing
that the proposed method achieves an ϵ-first-order stationary
point with an approximation error of O(γ
κ+1
κp ). Simulation
results in stochastic GridWorld environment further validate
the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm.
In future research, an important direction involves in-
tegrating neural network approximation and deep learning
techniques to tackle high-dimensional state spaces subject to
coupled constraints, which poses substantial theoretical and
algorithmic challenges.
