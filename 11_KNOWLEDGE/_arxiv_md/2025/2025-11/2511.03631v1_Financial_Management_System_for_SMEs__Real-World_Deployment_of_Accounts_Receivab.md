Abstract
Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs), particularly freelancers and
early-stage businesses, face unique financial management challenges due
to limited resources, small customer bases, and constrained data avail-
ability. This paper presents the development and deployment of an in-
tegrated financial prediction system that combines accounts receivable
prediction and cash flow forecasting specifically designed for SME oper-
ational constraints.
Our system addresses the gap between enterprise-
focused financial tools and the needs of freelancers and small businesses.
The solution integrates two key components: a binary classification model
for predicting invoice payment delays, and a multi-module cash flow fore-
casting model that handles incomplete and limited historical data. A pro-
totype system has been implemented and deployed as a web application
with integration into Cluee’s platform, a startup providing financial man-
agement tools for freelancers, demonstrating practical feasibility for real-
world SME financial management.

Introduction
Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs), particularly early-stage businesses,
micro-enterprises, and freelancers face unique challenges in financial manage-
ment that differ significantly from large corporations. With limited customer
bases and smaller operational scales, these businesses are particularly vulnerable
to cash flow disruptions and payment delays [1,2]. A single delayed invoice can

represent a substantial portion of monthly revenue, making accurate financial
prediction not just beneficial but critical for survival.
Traditional financial forecasting tools are designed for large enterprises with
extensive historical data, dedicated finance teams, and complex enterprise sys-
tems.
SMEs operate under fundamentally different constraints: limited his-
torical data, basic invoicing systems, incomplete record-keeping, and minimal
resources for financial analysis [3]. This creates a significant gap between avail-
able tools and actual business needs.
To address this challenge, we developed and deployed a financial manage-
ment system in collaboration with Cluee1, a startup providing self-organization
and smart-budgeting tools for freelancers. Our system combines two compo-
nents: accounts receivable prediction to anticipate payment delays, and cash
flow forecasting to estimate future financial positions.
The system supports
both standalone accounts receivable prediction for individual invoice manage-
ment and integrated cash flow forecasting that incorporates payment delay es-
timates for comprehensive financial planning. The integration of these compo-
nents provides SMEs with actionable insights for financial planning and liquidity
management.
This paper presents the real-world deployment experience of our system,
focusing on practical challenges, architectural decisions, and business impact
rather than purely algorithmic contributions.
We demonstrate how to build
effective financial prediction systems under the realistic constraints faced by
SMEs, including limited data availability, incomplete information, and the need
for understandable, actionable predictions.
Key contributions of this work include:
• a deployed integrated system combining accounts receivable prediction
and cash flow forecasting specifically designed for SME constraints;
• practical solutions for handling limited and incomplete financial data in
real business environments;
• business validation through actual deployment;
• insights and lessons learned from building financial prediction systems for
resource-constrained businesses.
The system has been implemented and deployed as a web application with
integration into Cluee’s platform, demonstrating the practical feasibility of our
approach for real-world SME financial management.

Related Work
Financial prediction for businesses has been extensively studied, but most ex-
isting work focuses on large enterprises with substantial datasets. Research in
accounts receivable prediction typically examines datasets from multinational
companies with hundreds of thousands of invoices [4–6], while cash flow forecast-
ing studies utilize data from power companies, construction projects, or other
large-scale operations [7–9].

Accounts receivable prediction approaches employ machine learning methods
such as gradient boosted trees, random forests, and neural networks on feature-
rich datasets [4,10]. Zeng et al. [5] demonstrated the importance of customer-
based and history-based features using 54 invoice-based features, while Hu [6]
analyzed various prediction aspects using datasets with extensive customer co-
operation histories. These feature requirements far exceed what typical SMEs
can provide.
Cash flow forecasting methods include ARIMA, Prophet [11], and neural
networks (LSTM, GRU) [7, 12]. Cheng et al. proposed adaptive support vec-
tor regression [8] and evolutionary fuzzy decision models [9] for construction
projects. These approaches assume substantial historical data and regular pat-
terns unavailable to SMEs.
Limited research addresses SME-specific constraints. Weytjens et al. [12]
compared machine learning to traditional methods but used relatively large
datasets. The gap between enterprise-focused solutions and SME needs repre-
sents a significant challenge that existing literature does not adequately address.
Our work addresses this gap by integrating accounts receivable prediction
with cash flow forecasting specifically for resource-constrained environments,
focusing on practical deployment rather than purely algorithmic optimization.
3
Problem Definition and Requirements
3.1
SME Constraints
Small and medium enterprises face fundamentally different financial manage-
ment challenges compared to large corporations.
Through our collaboration
with Cluee and interviews with multiple SME stakeholders, we identified sev-
eral constraints that existing financial prediction tools fail to address.
First, SMEs, particularly newer businesses and freelancers, typically main-
tain minimal historical data due to limited operational history and reliance on
basic or ad hoc accounting systems [3]. While large enterprises may have years
of detailed transactional records with comprehensive customer profiles, SMEs
often operate with basic invoicing systems containing only essential informa-
tion: customer ID, invoice amount, issue date, due date, and payment date.
Additional context such as customer demographics, detailed payment terms, or
extensive cooperation history is rarely available.
Second, SMEs experience irregular cash flow patterns that don’t conform to
traditional time series assumptions. A freelancer might have periods of intense
activity followed by gaps, while a small consulting firm might depend on a
handful of large clients with varying payment behaviors. These patterns make
standard forecasting approaches unsuitable.
Third, transparency is important for SME decision-making. Unlike large
corporations with dedicated finance teams, SME owners need to understand and
act on predictions directly. Black-box models that provide predictions without
clear reasoning are insufficient for businesses where owners must make rapid,
high-stakes financial decisions.

3.2
Workflow Needs
Our analysis of SME financial management workflows revealed that effective
prediction systems must support two interconnected decision-making processes:
short-term liquidity management and medium-term financial planning.
For short-term liquidity management, users need to identify which outstand-
ing invoices are likely to be paid late, allowing them to take preemptive actions
such as sending payment reminders, offering early payment discounts, or adjust-
ing their own payment schedules accordingly. This requires accounts receivable
predictions that can operate with limited customer history and provide action-
able insights.
For medium-term financial planning, users need cash flow forecasts that in-
tegrate multiple income sources: project-based work, recurring payments, one-
time invoices, and planned expenses. These forecasts must accommodate in-
complete data and provide actionable insights about future financial positions,
enabling users to plan for various scenarios.
The integration of these two prediction tasks is essential because accounts
receivable directly impacts cash flow forecasting. Late payments affect not only
current liquidity but also the reliability of future cash flow projections. Systems
that treat these problems independently require users to manually synthesize
insights, adding complexity that resource-constrained SMEs are unable to af-
ford.
3.3
System Requirements
Based on our analysis of real SME data patterns, we established several key sys-
tem requirements. The severity of data scarcity in SME environments is evident
from our dataset, where slightly more than 50% of customers received only one
or two invoices throughout the entire cooperation period. This extreme data
limitation means the system must operate effectively with minimal training data
per customer, gracefully handle cases where customers have virtually no histor-
ical cooperation patterns, and accommodate missing or incomplete information
without requiring comprehensive data preprocessing.
The system architecture must be modular to handle different types of finan-
cial data sources. SMEs often have a mix of project-based income (with hourly
rates or flat fees), recurring subscriptions, one-time payments, and various ex-
pense categories. Each data type requires different prediction approaches while
contributing to an integrated cash flow forecast.
Finally, the system must provide actionable outputs rather than purely sta-
tistical predictions. Users need to understand which factors drive predictions
and what actions they can take to improve their financial position. This re-
quirement influenced our choice of relatively simple models over complex deep
learning approaches.
4
Deployed Solution
4.1
System Architecture
Our deployed system consists of two interconnected prediction modules inte-
grated through a unified data processing pipeline. The architecture prioritizes

modularity and transparency to accommodate the diverse data patterns and
understandability requirements identified in SME workflows.
The accounts receivable prediction module operates as a binary classifier,
determining whether invoices will be paid within a grace period (7 days past
due date) or experience significant delays. The cash flow forecasting module
employs a multi-component approach, with separate sub-models for different
income and expense categories that are then aggregated into unified predictions.

forecasting pipeline where payment delay predictions inform future cash flow

4.2
Accounts Receivable Prediction Module
The accounts receivable module addresses the challenge of predicting payment
delays with minimal customer history. Given the constraint that SMEs often
have customers with very limited cooperation history, we developed a feature en-
gineering approach that maximizes information extraction from available data.

incomplete.

4.4
Integration and Deployment
The system operates in two modes to provide coherent financial projections.
For standalone accounts receivable prediction, the system processes individual
invoices to estimate payment delay likelihood. For integrated cash flow fore-
casting, the cash flow module directly calls the accounts receivable module to
obtain payment delay predictions, which then adjust the timing assumptions in
cash flow forecasts, improving the accuracy of liquidity projections and enabling
more reliable financial planning.
The system is implemented as a REST API using Flask web framework
and deployed on Google App Engine. This cloud-based architecture provides
scalability while minimizing infrastructure requirements for the startup partner.
The API design supports integration with existing SME financial management
tools and allows for incremental feature additions as user needs evolve.
The API responses provide detailed prediction components and high-level
summaries suitable for quick decision-making. For cash flow forecasting, the
system delivers decomposed results showing contributions from each of the four
sub-modules (hourly project work, non-recurring invoices, flat-rate projects, and
recurring payments), along with identification of specific invoices that may expe-
rience payment delays. For accounts receivable prediction, the system provides
insights into customer payment patterns, such as whether a customer’s payment
behavior is improving or deteriorating based on recent trends.
This enables client applications to present actionable insights to users about
which income sources drive their financial projections, which specific invoices
and customers require attention, and opportunities to send payment reminders
or take proactive collection actions.
5
Real-World Performance and Impact
5.1
Evaluation Setup and Datasets
Our evaluation focused on demonstrating system effectiveness under realistic
SME constraints. We used three datasets representing different scales of busi-
ness operation: a startup-provided dataset from Cluee (297 invoices, 60 cus-
tomers), and two public datasets filtered to match SME characteristics - IBM
Late Payment Histories [14] (2,466 invoices) and Payment Date Dataset [15]
(12,071 invoices).
The filtering process limited customer cooperation history to maximum 50
invoices per customer, reflecting typical SME customer relationships. We im-
plemented a 7-day grace period for payment delay classification, accounting for
7


## Page 8


payment processing delays commonly experienced in small business transac-
tions.
To address the challenge of limited cash flow data availability, we developed
a synthetic data generator producing realistic project-based work patterns for
1,000 simulated users over one year (422,306 work sessions total). The generator
incorporates variable working hours, multiple concurrent projects, and wage
dynamics typical of freelance environments.
5.2
Accounts Receivable Prediction Performance
The accounts receivable module demonstrated consistent performance across
datasets, with the startup dataset presenting the most challenging scenario due
to limited training data. We evaluated multiple classification algorithms using
5-fold cross-validation, with balanced accuracy [16] as the primary metric to
account for class imbalance across datasets.
Table 1 shows the comparative performance of different classification meth-

achieved consistent performance across all datasets. On the startup dataset, the
0.56 balanced accuracy, while modest, represents meaningful predictive capabil-
ity given the severe data constraints - 50% of customers had only 1-2 invoices.

5.3
Cash Flow Forecasting Results
Cash flow forecasting evaluation revealed the importance of model simplicity
and transparency in resource-constrained environments. Our custom modular
approach consistently outperformed traditional time series methods (ARIMA,
Prophet, SVR) when training data was limited.
For the most challenging scenario - predicting 11 months based on only 1
month of historical data - our approach achieved 11.85% mean absolute percent-

5.4
Deployment Impact and User Experience
The system deployment provided valuable insights into practical SME financial
management needs. The REST API architecture was designed to enable inte-
gration with existing accounting workflows, as demonstrated through our work
with Cluee’s platform.
Initial feedback from the startup team and their user community was posi-
tive, particularly regarding the system’s forecast insights and conservative fore-
casting approach. Users valued understanding the reasoning behind predictions,
and the integration of accounts receivable predictions with cash flow forecasting
provided valuable insights for proactive financial management.
6
Discussion and Future Directions
Our deployment experience highlighted several considerations for building finan-
cial prediction systems in resource-constrained environments. The importance
of prediction insights emerged as a key factor - SME owners appear to need
transparent models they can understand to make informed decisions. Simple
approaches with prediction insights often proved more suitable than complex
methods when data availability is limited and user adoption is critical.
The modular architecture approach showed promise for handling the diverse
nature of SME financial data. Different businesses have varying data availability
9


## Page 10


patterns, and the ability to operate with partial information appears valuable
for practical deployment scenarios.
The system deployment demonstrates the practical value of combining ac-
counts receivable prediction with cash flow forecasting for SME environments.
The system handles the data constraints typical of small businesses while pro-
viding actionable insights that standalone tools cannot offer. While broader
evaluation across diverse SME contexts would provide additional validation,
the initial deployment results indicate promising potential for this integrated
approach.
The system’s ability to operate with minimal historical data addresses a key
barrier for SME financial prediction systems. The modular architecture and
conservative forecasting approach align well with the practical needs and risk
tolerance observed in small business environments.
Future development will focus on expanding system capabilities as more de-
ployment data becomes available. Integration with additional accounting plat-
forms and support for more complex financial scenarios represent natural evo-
lution paths. We also plan to explore ensemble approaches that could combine
the models with more sophisticated techniques as historical data accumulates
over time.
