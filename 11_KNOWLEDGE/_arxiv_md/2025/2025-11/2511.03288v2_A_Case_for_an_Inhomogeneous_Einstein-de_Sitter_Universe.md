We present a local-to-global cosmological framework in which cosmic acceleration emerges from
structure formation in an inhomogeneous Einstein–de Sitter (iEdS) universe, without dark energy.
The model exhibits a quasilinear coasting evolution toward an effective Milne state driven by growing
inhomogeneities. We test the iEdS model with H0 = 72.5 km s−1 Mpc−1 and Ωm,0 = 0.272 using
CMB, BAO, and SN Ia data. The iEdS model fits the data comparably to ΛCDM and resolves the
H0 tension, while yielding a cosmic age t0 ≃13.67 Gyr consistent with globular-cluster estimates.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The flat Lambda Cold Dark Matter (ΛCDM) model
is the prevailing cosmological model, describing a spa-
tially flat universe with dark energy in the form of a cos-
mological constant (Λ) [1]. It shows remarkable concor-
dance with diverse observations across cosmic epochs [2],
including three standard precision probes: cosmic mi-
crowave background (CMB) anisotropies [3–5], baryon
acoustic oscillations (BAOs) identified in galaxy sur-
veys [6–8], and the type Ia supernova (SN Ia) distance
modulus–redshift relation [9–11]. Despite its successes,
tensions between locally measured parameters and those
derived from CMB and BAO observations —most no-
tably in the Hubble constant (H0) [12] and the structure
growth parameter (S8) [13]— as well as other anoma-
lies [14], may indicate the need for further refinement of
this cosmological framework.
The flat ΛCDM model is based on a global-to-local ap-
proach. It assumes large-scale homogeneity and isotropy
(the cosmological principle [15]), which imply a global
scale factor a(t) evolving with a universal (cosmic) time
t according to the Friedmann equations [16, 17]. Local
inhomogeneities (large-scale structure and gravitational
gradients [18]) and motions (peculiar velocities [19]) are
typically treated as perturbations evolving in a uniformly
expanding background, or as potential contributors to
the global Friedmann dynamics through the nonlinear
nature of Einstein’s field equations, an effect known as
cosmological backreaction [20, 21].
It has been pro-
posed that backreaction could mimic a cosmological-
constant–like term in the global expansion [22], though
the prevailing view, despite numerous counterclaims
(e.g. [23]), is that its magnitude is too small to account
for the observed acceleration of the universe.
In this paper, we present a local-to-global framework

showing that the observed cosmic acceleration, well de-
scribed by an apparent Λ > 0 term, arises naturally from
local dynamics in an inhomogeneous Einstein–de Sitter
(hereafter iEdS) universe without invoking dark energy.
In Sec. II, we show that such an iEdS universe follows
a quasilinear coasting evolution, starting from the Ein-
stein–de Sitter state [24] with negligible inhomogeneities
at recombination, then transitioning through an era of ac-
celerated expansion, and finally asymptoting to a Milne
universe [15] with a(t) ∝t coasting evolution. The ac-
celeration arises as a purely general-relativistic effect of
structure formation, which amplifies an effective negative
spatial curvature that emerges naturally without break-
ing spatial flatness. Fitting the iEdS model to the Planck
2018 CMB temperature power spectrum [5], DESI DR2
BAO data [25], and Pantheon+ SNe Ia [26] (Sec. III), we
find it fits the data comparably to ΛCDM while resolv-
ing the Hubble tension. Conclusions are summarized in
Sec. IV.

III.1.
Cosmic Microwave Background
We computed the lensed CMB temperature power
spectrum using the camb Boltzmann code [37] with de-
fault accuracy settings, implementing the iEdS model
as an effective dark-energy cosmology with a time-

A
full multi-parameter CMB fit is computationally pro-
