EXTENSIVE EMBEDDINGS INTO FRA¨ISS´E STRUCTURES AND STATIONARY WEAK
INDEPENDENCE RELATIONS
ALEKSANDRA KWIATKOWSKA, ROB SULLIVAN AND JEROEN WINKEL
Abstract. Let M be a Fra¨ıss´e structure (a countably infinite ultrahomogeneous structure). We call an em-
bedding f : A →M extensive if each automorphism of its image extends to an automorphism of M, where the
extension map respects composition, and we say that M has extensible ω-age if each substructure admits an
extensive embedding into M.
We investigate the relationship between the following two properties: the presence of a stationary weak
independence relation (SWIR) on M, and extensibility of the ω-age of M.
We show that linearly ordered
Fra¨ıss´e structures with a SWIR have extensible ω-age, but also we give examples of Fra¨ıss´e structures where
only one of the two properties holds. Finally, we consider whether a wide range of examples of Fra¨ıss´e structures
have extensible ω-age or a finite SWIR expansion, including all countably infinite ultrahomogeneous oriented
graphs (with one exception).
1. Introduction
Recall that a structure M is ultrahomogeneous if each isomorphism between finitely generated substructures of
M extends to an automorphism of M. We call a countably infinite ultrahomogeneous structure M a Fra¨ıss´e
structure (see [Mac11], [Hod93] for background). In this paper we investigate the following two notions.
• Extensive embeddings. Let M be a Fra¨ıss´e structure. For f : A →M an embedding, we say that
f is extensive if there exists a group embedding θ : Aut(f(A)) →Aut(M) such that g ⊆θ(g) for all
g ∈Aut(f(A)): informally, each automorphism of the image of A extends to an automorphism of M
in a manner that preserves group composition. Define the ω-age of M, written Ageω(M), to be the
class of structures which are embeddable into M (we permit infinite structures). If each A ∈Ageω(M)
admits an extensive embedding into M, we say that Ageω(M) is extensible.
• Stationary weak independence relations (SWIRs). These generalise the well-known notion of
stationary independence relation (SIR) by dropping the assumption of symmetry. A stationary weak
independence relation is a ternary relation |⌣on the set of finitely generated substructures of a Fra¨ıss´e
structure M, written as B |⌣A C for A, B, C ⊆f.g. M, which satisfies certain axioms: automorphism-
invariance, stationarity, existence and monotonicity.
We are concerned with the following three main questions:
Question 1.1. For which Fra¨ıss´e structures M is Ageω(M) extensible?
Question 1.2. What is the relationship between extensibility of Ageω(M) and the presence of a SWIR on M?
Question 1.3. Which Fra¨ıss´e structures have a SWIR? If a Fra¨ıss´e structure does not have a SWIR, does it
have an expansion with one?
We discuss the background and motivation for these questions below.

Kubi´s and Maˇsulovi´c observe in [KM17] that the Katˇetov tower construction appearing in various guises in the
above papers is actually an instance of a more general functorial construction on categories. The key ingredient
that makes the construction work is the following:
Definition 1.4 ([KM17, Definition 2.1]). Let M be a Fra¨ıss´e structure. Recall that Age(M) is the class of
finitely generated structures which are embeddable in M. (We allow the empty structure.) We write A(M),
Aω(M) for the classes of non-empty structures in Age(M), Ageω(M). Consider A(M), Aω(M) as categories,
where in each case the class of morphisms is given by all embeddings between objects.
A Katˇetov functor K for M is a functor K : A(M) →Aω(M) such that there exists a family of embeddings
η = (ηA : A →K(A))A∈A(M) with the following properties:
(i) η is a natural transformation: that is, for each embedding f : A →B in A(M) we have K(f)◦ηA = ηB◦f;
(ii) for each one-point extension ζ : A →B in A(M), there is an embedding f : B →K(A) with f ◦ζ = ηA.
Here, a one-point extension in A(M) is an embedding ζ : A →B in A(M) where there exists e ∈B \ ζ(A) such
that B is generated by ζ(A) ∪{e}.
(In fact, [KM17] gives a more abstract version of the above, allowing more general classes of morphisms.)
A Katˇetov functor can be thought of as giving a functorial amalgam of all one-point extensions of a structure,
for each structure in the age, and this functorial amalgam is enough to carry out a categorical version of the
Katˇetov tower construction:
Fact 1.5 ([KM17, Corollary 3.9, paraphrased]). Let M be a Fra¨ıss´e structure. If M has a Katˇetov functor,
then Ageω(M) is extensible.
We give a very brief sketch of the proof idea.
[KM17, Theorem 2.2] shows that, given a Katˇetov functor
K : A(M) →Aω(M) with an associated natural transformation η = (ηA)A∈A(M), one may extend K, η to a
functor ˆK : Aω(M) →Aω(M) and a natural transformation ˆη = (ˆηA)A∈Aω(M). Subsequently [KM17, Theorem
3.2, Theorem 3.3] then show that for each A ∈Aω(M), applying ˆK ω-many times gives a copy of the Fra¨ıss´e
limit M, and as this construction is functorial, we immediately obtain Fact 1.5.
As a consequence of Fact 1.5, in order to provide positive examples to answer Question 1.1, it suffices to produce
Fra¨ıss´e structures M which have Katˇetov functors. In [KM17] a wide variety of examples of Fra¨ıss´e structures
with Katˇetov functors were already given: those with free amalgamation ([KM17, Theorem 2.12]), the rational
Urysohn space, the countable atomless Boolean algebra, the random poset, the random lattice, the random
semilattice, the random digraph and the random tournament.
Remark 1.6. The reader may wonder if in fact all embeddings are extensive. To see that this is not the case,
let M be the random graph and A a countably infinite anticlique. Let v ∈M. Using the extension property
of M, there is an embedding f : A →M such that the set C = {u ∈f(A) | u ∼v} is infinite/coinfinite in
f(A). As there are continuum-many infinite/coinfinite subsets of f(A) and M \ f(A) is countable, there is an
infinite/coinfinite subset C′ ⊆f(A) such that there is no w ∈M with C′ = {u ∈f(A) | u ∼w}. Then any
automorphism of f(A) sending C to C′ does not extend to an automorphism of M.
Background: negative examples for Question 1.1 and non-universal automorphism groups.
Jaligot asked a more abstract version of Question 1.1:
Question ([Jal07]). Does there exist a Fra¨ıss´e structure M for which Aut(M) is not universal?
Here, the automorphism group Aut(M) of a Fra¨ıss´e structure M is universal if for each A ∈Ageω(M) there is
an abstract group embedding Aut(A) →Aut(M). Note that, a priori, the group embedding need not have any
relation to the structure. It is immediate that extensibility of Ageω(M) implies universality of Aut(M).
Piotr Kowalski observed that for each prime p the Fra¨ıss´e limit of the class of finite fields of characteristic p has
non-universal automorphism group (see [M¨ul16]), but the question remained open for relational classes until
the relatively recent paper [KS20] of Kubi´s and Shelah, which gives examples where universality dramatically
fails. In particular, the end of [KS20, Section 4] gives the example of a Fra¨ıss´e structure MKS consisting of a
red countably infinite set and a blue copy of (Q, <) with a generic bipartite graph structure between them (in
a signature {R, B, <, ∼}). The automorphism group of MKS is torsion-free (it embeds in Aut(Q, <)), but the
class of automorphism groups of structures in Age(MKS) contains all finite symmetric groups.

Background: stationary (weak) independence relations.
A stationary independence relation (SIR), first defined in [TZ13], is a ternary relation |⌣on the set of finitely
generated substructures of a Fra¨ıss´e structure M, written as B |⌣A C for A, B, C ⊆f.g. M, which satisfies the
axioms of automorphism-invariance, stationarity, existence, monotonicity and symmetry (see Definition 2.3).
Examples of Fra¨ıss´e structures with SIRs include those with free amalgamation, the rational Urysohn space, the
random poset and many metrically homogeneous graphs (for the latter see [ABH+17]). SIRs have numerous
applications in the study of Fra¨ıss´e structures (see for example [TZ13], [M¨ul16], [ABH+17], [PS17], [KS19],
[EHK+21]), and were originally used in [TZ13] to determine the normal subgroups of the isometry group of the
(complete) Urysohn space.
As Ageω(M) is extensible for M with free amalgamation, and as free amalgamation gives an example of a
SIR, it is natural to ask whether the presence of a SIR on M is sufficient for extensibility. In [M¨ul16], M¨uller
showed that this is indeed the case: if a Fra¨ıss´e structure M has a SIR, then Ageω(M) is extensible. Her proof
used a Katˇetov tower construction directly, rather than the abstract machinery of Katˇetov functors (the papers
[KM17], [M¨ul16] were more or less contemporaneous).
Li generalised the definition of SIR in [Li19], [Li21] to that of a stationary weak independence relation (SWIR),
and used SWIRs to show the simplicity of certain automorphism groups of Fra¨ıss´e structures with asymmetric
relations by generalising the main theorem of [TZ13].
A SWIR has the same axioms as a SIR minus the
assumption of symmetry. (Another generalisation of SIRs to the asymmetric case was given in [CKT21] and
similarly used to prove simplicity of certain automorphism groups of Fra¨ıss´e structures.)
The original motivation for this paper was to see if the result of [M¨ul16] likewise generalises to the case of
SWIRs: this formed our initial approach to Question 1.2. Given the wide variety of applications of SIRs or
similar notions appearing in the literature, we also regard the presence of a SWIR on a Fra¨ıss´e structure as
having its own inherent interest, leading to Question 1.3. In Section 2, we show that the existence of a SWIR on
a Fra¨ıss´e structure is equivalent to a certain natural notion of canonical amalgamation on its age, which we call a
standard amalgamation operator, and so Question 1.3 can be viewed as asking how much information is required
in an expansion in order to be able to canonically amalgamate elements of the expanded age. A related question
is asked in [KS19, Conjecture 7.1] in the context of ω-categorical structures and with a similar definition of an
independence relation called a CIR (canonical independence relation): Kaplan and Simon conjecture that every
ω-categorical structure has an ω-categorical expansion with a CIR. We discuss the relationship between SWIRs
and CIRs further in Section 2.
Main results and structure of the paper.
In Section 2, we show that a Fra¨ıss´e structure M has a SWIR if and only if its age has a certain notion of
canonical amalgamation, which we call a standard amalgamation operator. In Section 3 we consider three key
examples. Two of them demonstrate that neither direction of implication in Question 1.2 holds:
Theorem 3.1. There is a relational Fra¨ıss´e structure with a Katˇetov functor but without a (local) SWIR, and
there is a relational Fra¨ıss´e structure with a SWIR but without a universal automorphism group (hence its ω-age
is non-extensible and it has no Katˇetov functor).
(Note that the Fra¨ıss´e structure MKS of Kubi´s and Shelah mentioned above also has a SWIR and a non-universal
automorphism group, but we give the first vertex-transitive and primitive examples with these properties.)
The three examples in Section 3 form part of the proof of Theorem 5.1 below. There is a key idea underlying
these examples, as well as the proofs of the main theorems in Section 4 and most of the examples in Section 5:
the definition of a Katˇetov functor does not require that each one-point extension of A occurs uniquely in
K(A), and hence when defining a functorial amalgam K(A) of one-point extensions of A, we may add multiple
copies of each one-point extension labelled with extra information, and use this extra information to guarantee
functoriality – see the discussion at the beginning of Section 3 and also Example 3.5.
In Section 4, we use the equivalence of SWIRs and standard amalgamation operators, together with the ma-
chinery of Katˇetov functors, to give some general positive results for Question 1.1. We first quickly recover the
result of M¨uller ([M¨ul16]): specifically, we show that any Fra¨ıss´e structure with a SIR has a Katˇetov functor
(and hence extensible ω-age). We then show:
Theorem 4.4. Let M be a linearly ordered Fra¨ıss´e structure with strong amalgamation and a local SWIR. Then
M has a Katˇetov functor, and hence its ω-age is extensible.
Theorem 4.11. Let M be a relational Fra¨ıss´e structure with strong amalgamation and a local SIR. Then the
generic tournament expansion of M has a Katˇetov functor, and hence has extensible ω-age.
Using Theorem 4.4, we have for example that the generic ordered graph, the generic ordered rational Urysohn
space, the generic n-linear order and the generic poset with compatible linear order have Katˇetov functors and
hence extensible ω-age – see Example 4.5. In Section 4.5, we give an example of a relational Fra¨ıss´e structure

Proposition 6.1. Let M be the generic ordered graph. Then Ageinf(M) is uniquely extensible.

equal to the generic linear order expansion of a (non-trivial) Fra¨ıss´e structure with free amalgamation; we do
not check the details in the present paper.

2. SWIRs and standard amalgamation operators
2.1. Stationary weak independence relations (SWIRs).
Notation 2.1. In the below, we write AB to mean the substructure generated by A ∪B.
Definition 2.2. Let M be a Fra¨ıss´e structure, and let A, B, B′ ⊆f.g. M. We write B ≡A B′ if there exists
f ∈Aut(M) with f(B) = B′ and f|A = idA, or equivalently if B, B′ have the same quantifier-free type over A
in some enumeration. (The two definitions are equivalent by ultrahomogeneity.) We will be a little flexible in
our notation: if a particular isomorphism is clear from context (for example when there is a given isomorphism
taking B to B′) then we use this isomorphism. For instance, in the below stationarity axiom, when we write
B ≡A B′ ⇒B ≡AC B′, both statements refer to the same isomorphism from B to B′.
Definition 2.3 ([Li21, Definition 3.1.1]). Let M be a Fra¨ıss´e structure. Let |⌣be a ternary relation on the set
of finitely generated substructures of M, written B |⌣A C. We say that |⌣is a stationary weak independence
relation (SWIR) on M if it satisfies the following axioms:
• Invariance (Inv): for g ∈Aut(M) we have B |⌣A C ⇒gB |⌣gA gC;
• Existence (Ex): for all A, B, C ⊆f.g. M,
– there exists B′ ⊆f.g. M with B ≡A B′ such that B′ |⌣A C;
– there exists C′ ⊆f.g. M with C ≡A C′ such that B |⌣A C′;
• Stationarity (Sta): for all A, B, C ⊆f.g. M,
– if B |⌣A C ∧B′ |⌣A C and B ≡A B′, then B ≡AC B′;
– if B |⌣A C ∧B |⌣A C′ and C ≡A C′, then C ≡AB C′;
• Monotonicity (Mon):
– BD |⌣A C ⇒B |⌣A C ∧D |⌣AB C;
– B |⌣A CD ⇒B |⌣A C ∧B |⌣AC D;
We call a ternary relation |⌣defined on the set of non-empty finitely generated substructures of M a local SWIR
if it satisfies the above axioms. We call a SWIR a global SWIR when we particularly wish to emphasise that it
is also defined over the empty set, but the reader should assume SWIRs are global unless specified otherwise.
Note that a global SWIR restricts to a local SWIR. We give several examples in Section 5 of structures with
local SWIRs which do not have a global SWIR. Note that we defined Katˇetov functors in Definition 1.4 to be
local.
Remark 2.4 ([Li21, 3.1.2(iii)]). In fact, left-(Ex) and (Inv) imply right-(Ex). Let A, B, C ⊆f.g. M. By left-(Ex),
there is B′ ⊆f.g. M with B ≡A B′ and B′ |⌣A C. As M is ultrahomogeneous there exists g ∈Aut(M) fixing A
pointwise and sending B′ to B. So by (Inv), we have B |⌣A gC. We have gC ≡A C, showing right-(Ex).
Remark 2.5. In [KS19, Definition 3.10], Kaplan and Simon give a similar yet distinct definition of a canonical
independence relation (CIR) in the more general setting of countable structures.
Using Lemma 2.10, it is
straightforward to see that the relationship between the notions of SWIR and CIR for Fra¨ıss´e structures is as
follows:
global SWIR ⇔(CIR on finitely generated substructures + stationarity over all A ⊆f.g. M).
Definition 2.6 ([TZ13, Definition 2.1]). Let M be a Fra¨ıss´e structure, and let |⌣be a (local) SWIR on M. If
in addition |⌣is symmetric, i.e. for all A, B, C ⊆f.g. M we have that B |⌣A C ⇔C |⌣A B, then we call |⌣a
(local) stationary independence relation (SIR).
Example 2.7. We give examples of Fra¨ıss´e structures with SWIRs. In each example, the SWIR is given by a
certain canonical notion of amalgamation – see the remainder of this section below.
• A relational Fra¨ıss´e structure M with free amalgamation has a SIR: for A, B, C ⊆fin M, define B |⌣A C
if BA, CA are freely amalgamated over A.
• The random poset has a SIR: define B |⌣A C if for b ∈B \ A, c ∈C \ A such that there is no a ∈A
with b < a < c or b > a > c, we have b, c incomparable.
• The rational Urysohn space has a local SIR: define B
|⌣A C if for b ∈B \ A, c ∈C \ A, we have
d(b, c) = min{d(b, a) + d(c, a) | a ∈A}.
• Q has a SWIR: define B |⌣A C if for b ∈B \ A, c ∈C \ A such that there is no a ∈A with b < a < c
or b > a > c, we have b < c.
• The random tournament has a SWIR: define B |⌣A C if for b ∈B \ A, c ∈C \ A we have b →c.

We now prove a number of basic properties of SWIRs. We phrase them for SWIRs, but the same properties
likewise hold for local SWIRs assuming that the base is non-empty.
Lemma 2.8 (Base triviality). Let M be a Fra¨ıss´e structure with SWIR |⌣. Then A |⌣A B and B |⌣A A for
all A, B ⊆f.g. M.
Proof. By (Ex) there is B′ ⊆f.g. M with B ≡A B′ and A |⌣A B′. By ultrahomogeneity of M there is g ∈Aut(M)
with idA ⊆g and gB′ = B, so A |⌣A B by (Inv). The proof of the other statement is similar.
□
Lemma 2.9 ([Li21, 3.1.2(ii)]). Let M be a Fra¨ıss´e structure with SWIR |⌣. Then we have Transitivity (Tr):
B |⌣A C ∧B |⌣AC D ⇒B |⌣A CD;
B |⌣A C ∧D |⌣AB C ⇒BD |⌣A C.
Proof. We show the first statement. Suppose B |⌣A C∧B |⌣AC D. By (Ex) there is B′ ⊆f.g. M with B′ |⌣A CD
and B ≡A B′. By (Mon) we have B′ |⌣A C ∧B′ |⌣AC D. As B ≡A B′ and B |⌣A C ∧B′ |⌣A C, by (Sta) we
have B ≡AC B′. By (Sta) we have B ≡ACD B′ and by (Inv) we have B |⌣A CD.
□

The three examples in this section illustrate a key idea which runs through much of this paper: when construct-

process of attempting it one generally comes up with a proof of non-universality of the automorphism group.

we present here, as each example seems to require some specific technical adjustments to the general technique.

5.9.1. Meet trees. Let L = {<, ∧} with ≤a binary relation symbol and ∧a binary function symbol. We say
that an L-structure A is a meet-tree if:
• (A, <) is a poset;
• for each a ∈A, {b ∈A | b ≤a} is linearly ordered by <;
• for a, b ∈A, the set {c ∈A | c ≤a, b} is non-empty and has greatest element a ∧b.
Let C be the class of finite meet-trees. It is straightforward to see that C is a Fra¨ıss´e class with strong amalgam-

functor – we give a brief proof, leaving some straightforward checking to the reader.
We call the least vertex of a finite meet tree the root. Let A be a finite meet tree, and let B be a meet tree with
A ⊆B. Let a0, a1 ∈A. If a0 < a1 and there is no a ∈A with a0 < a < a1, then we call (a0, a1) a cut of A, and
we say that each vertex v ∈B with a0 < v < a1 lies in the cut (a0, a1). We also consider (−∞, root(A)) to be
a cut of A. For a ∈A, we say that v ∈B branches from A at a if a < v and for each a′ ∈A with a′ > a we
have a′ ∧v = a. For a cut (a0, a1) of A, we say that v ∈B branches from A at the cut (a0, a1) if v ∧a1 lies in
(a0, a1).

• if b, c branch from A at the same point a ∈A, then b ∧c = a.

6. Unique extensibility
We now briefly consider unique extensibility. For M a Fra¨ıss´e structure, we say that the class Ageinf(M) of
infinite structures embeddable in M is uniquely extensible if each A ∈Ageinf(M) admits a uniquely extensive
embedding into M: an embedding f : A →M such that each automorphism of f(A) extends to a unique auto-
morphism of M. Note that by uniqueness, group composition is respected, so a uniquely extensive embedding
f : A →M induces a group embedding Aut(f(A)) →Aut(M).
Henson ([Hen71, Theorem 3.1]) showed that Ageinf(M) is uniquely extensible for M the random graph (also
shown independently by Macpherson and Woodrow in [MW92]), and Bilge ([Bil12]) extended this result to
any transitive Fra¨ıss´e structure M with free amalgamation in a finite relational language (and satisfying the
non-triviality condition that M is not an indiscernible set). For the particular case of the generic Kn-free graph,
see [BJ12]. In [KSW25] the present authors showed via new techniques that Ageinf(M) is uniquely extensible
for M equal to the random poset.
Bilge observed ([Bil12, Proposition 8.33]) that Ageinf((Q, <)) is not uniquely extensible. Indeed, no countably
infinite linear order A admits a uniquely extensive embedding f into (Q, <): if such an f were to exist, then by
uniqueness f(A) must be dense in Q, and one can construct an automorphism of f(A) for which any extension
would be forced to send a rational to an irrational. We show in this section that this failure of unique extensibility
for (Q, <) does not transfer to free superpositions:
Proposition 6.1. Let M be the generic ordered graph. Then Ageinf(M) is uniquely extensible.
The proof is via a simple generalisation of Henson’s original construction, and we only provide a sketch.

7. Questions
For two structures considered in this paper, we found a finite expansion with a SWIR, but the existence of a
SWIR for the structure itself remains open:
Question 7.1. Do the generic 3-hypertournament (3.1) or the generic C4-enlarged tournament (5.4) have a
SWIR?
We were also not able to find a Katˇetov functor for P(3):
Question 7.2. Does the ultrahomogeneous oriented graph P(3) have a Katˇetov functor? (See [Che98] for a
description of this structure.)
The second and third author, together in a project with Shujie Yang, have found a finite SWIR expansion of
P(3) (given by adding a constant to fix a point), and used this to show that Aut(P(3)) is simple.
It would also be interesting to investigate generalisations of Katˇetov functors to continuous logic. In [BY14],
Ben Yaacov constructs the Gurarij space via an analogue of the Katˇetov tower construction, and perhaps similar
results for a range of continuous structures are achievable. In the conclusion of [KM17], the authors mention
that they have successfully obtained such a generalisation, but we could not find any further references in the
literature. We would also be interested in analogues of unique extensibility in this context:
Question 7.3. Does the Gurarij space satisfy a suitable analogue of unique extensibility?
It would also be nice to consider unique extensibility more generally. In [KSW25] the authors considered the
case of the generic poset, unique extensibility for which was obtained via a relatively technical construction –
it seemed that the presence of a SIR did not lead to a straightforward generalisation of the results by Bilge for
free amalgamation structures.
Our final question concerns the two papers [GK11], [HKO11], which define that A ⊆M is symmetrically
embedded in M if each element of Aut(A) extends to Aut(M) (a weaker definition than that of extensive
embedding, as the map Aut(A) →Aut(M) need not be a homomorphism), and which give Ramsey-type results
regarding symmetric indivisibility: they show for several Fra¨ıss´e structures M that for any finite colouring of
M there is a monochromatic symmetrically-embedded copy of M. Also, the lexicographic product is used to
give a quick proof that each countable linear order has a symmetrically-embedded copy in Q ([HKO11, Lemma
2.8]). We ask:
Question 7.4. Can some of the results from [GK11], [HKO11] be unified with the Katˇetov functor framework?
