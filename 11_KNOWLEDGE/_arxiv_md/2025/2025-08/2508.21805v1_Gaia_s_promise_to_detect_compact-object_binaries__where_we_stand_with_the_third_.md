ABSTRACT
With its third data release (DR3), Gaia begins unveiling dormant candidate compact object (CO)
binaries with luminous companions (LC) as predicted by several past theoretical studies. To date, 3
black hole (BH), 21 neutron star (NS), and ≈3200 white dwarf (WD) candidates have been identi-
fied with LCs in detached orbits using astrometry. We adopt an observationally motivated sampling
scheme for the star formation history of the Milky Way, and initial zero-age main-sequence binary
properties, incorporate all relevant binary interaction processes during evolution to obtain a realistic
present-day intrinsic population of CO–LC binaries. We apply Gaia’s selection criteria to identify the
CO–LC binaries detectable using the observational cuts applicable for DR3 as well as its end-of-mission
(EOM). We find that under the DR3 selection cuts, our detectable population includes no BH–LCs,
approximately 10–40 NS–LCs, and around ∼4300 WD–LCs.
In contrast, by EOM, the expected
numbers increase to 30–300 BH–LCs, 1500–5000 NS–LCs, and ∼105–106 WD–LC binaries, primarily
because of the significantly longer baseline of observation.
1. INTRODUCTION
Gaia, with its third data release (DR3), makes a sig-
nificant leap in both the quality of data and the range
of parameters available for each observed source. The
primary objective of the mission is to study the struc-
ture, chemical composition, and star formation history
of the Milky Way (MW). By mapping the distribution
of positions, kinematics, and properties of stellar pop-
ulations, Gaia is expected to provide an unprecedented
map of the MW (Perryman, M. A. C. et al. 2001; Gaia
Collaboration et al. 2016a).
The first two data releases (DR) provided parallax and
proper motion measurements for about a billion sources
(Gaia Collaboration et al. 2016b, 2018). With the re-
lease of early DR3 and, more recently, DR3, there is
an expansion in both the variety of sources and the di-
versity of their observed properties (Gaia Collaboration
et al. 2021, 2023a).
The DR3 catalog, in particular,
features low-resolution BP/RP spectra (De Angeli, F.

end-of-mission (EOM) detectable populations.
These

the modeled populations of detached CO–LC binaries.
Moreover, we expand our populations to include all CO-
LC binaries without restricting to BH-LC binaries only
(e.g., in Chawla et al. 2022, 2024).
We present the intrinsic detached CO-LC populations
expected to be present in the MW. In addition, we
identify the populations that would be detectable by
Gaia’s astrometry by the EOM. Moreover, we present
the populations that are specifically identifiable under
Gaia’s DR3-specific observational constraints.

2. NUMERICAL SETUP
We use COSMIC (Breivik et al. 2020; Breivik et al.
2021), a publicly available rapid binary population syn-
thesis suite, to model highly realistic populations of
BH–LC, NS–LC, and WD–LC binaries in the MW.
COSMIC is based on the stellar evolution fits from SSE
(Hurley et al. 2000) and binary evolution algorithm BSE
(Hurley et al. 2002). The current version of COSMIC in-
corporates various modifications from SSE/BSE for the
stellar evolution models and to treat binary interaction

processes; for a detailed review, we encourage readers to
see Breivik et al. (2020).
Using COSMIC, we model the present-day CO–LC bi-
nary populations by evolving an initial Zero Age Main
Sequence (ZAMS) binary population.
We generate
the ZAMS binary stellar and orbital parameters from
various observationally motivated probability distribu-
tion functions by adopting the ‘independent’ sampling
scheme of COSMIC which assumes no correlations be-
tween stellar parameters (e.g., primary mass, mass ra-
tio) and orbital parameters (e.g., semi-major axis, ec-
centricity) at birth. We sample the primary mass mpri
between 0.08 and 150 M⊙from the initial stellar mass
function given in Kroupa (2001).
We assign the sec-
ondary masses msec such that msec/M⊙≥0.08 and
the mass ratio q ≡msec/mpri distribution is uniform
(Mazeh et al. 1992; Goldberg & Mazeh 1994; Kobulnicky
& Fryer 2007). We assume an initially thermal eccen-
tricity distribution (Jeans 1919; Ambartsumian 1937;
Heggie 1975). The initial semi-major axis (a) is sam-
pled from a log-uniform distribution (Abt 1983) with
an upper bound of 105R⊙and a lower bound set by
Rpri ≤RRL/2 (Dominik et al. 2012), where Rpri and
RRL denote the radius and Roche radius of the primary.
We use a metallicity-dependent star formation rate
based on the final snapshot of the synthetically modeled
MW-like galaxy m12i in the Latte suite of the Feed-
back In Realistic Environments 2 (FIRE-2)1 simulation
suite (Hopkins et al. 2014, 2018; Wetzel et al. 2016).
We further use the ananke framework to assign three-
dimensional Galactic positions for each binary by re-
sampling from the smoothed density distribution of the
star particles (subsection 2.3; Sanderson et al. 2020).
This approach preserves the complex correlations be-
tween age, metallicity (Z), and the spatial distribution
of the star particles in m12i. This simulation does not
account for the potential impact of natal kicks which
may alter the final positions of the star particles that
are used in our study (Wagg et al. 2025). However, we
note that Wagg et al. (2025) focus exclusively on the
population of massive stars which originate in disrupted
binaries. In contrast, we are only concerned with de-
tached CO–LC binaries that survive the natal kicks and
hence should have much lower systemic velocities com-
pared to the systems studied by Wagg et al. (2025). Fi-
nally, we note that BSE restricts the metallicity values
to the range 10−4 ≤Z ≤0.03; therefore, COSMIC as-
signs these limiting values to binaries when the sampled
metallicity falls outside this range.

Various binary interaction processes, for example,
mass transfer (MT), common envelope (CE) evolution,
and tides, play a crucial role in shaping the present-day
properties of the CO–LC binary populations. We use
the adiabatic response of the donor star to determine
whether MT through Roche lobe overflow (RLOF) is
stable or leads to CE (Soberman et al. 1997). We as-
sume critical mass ratios as a function of donor type
from Belczynski et al. (2008). If a binary enters a CE,
we estimate the post-CE state using the “energy formal-
ism” (Livio & Soker 1984; Webbink 1984) which uses
two parameters, α, the efficiency with which the avail-
able energy transfers to the envelope for ejection, and
λ, a measure of the binding energy of the envelope rel-
ative to the gravitation potential energy of the binary
orbit, to predict the final orbital eccentricity and orbital
period. We adopt the standard value α = 1 (Dominik
et al. 2012), whereas λ depends on the present-day evo-
lutionary stage of the star as described in Claeys et al.
(2014).
2.1. Compact-Object Formation: White dwarfs
Three types of WDs can be produced in our models de-
pending on metallicity, ZAMS mass, and binary interac-
tions. After low-mass (0.08 ≲MZAMS/M⊙≲0.8) stars
evolve off the MS, the He core contracts until electron
degeneracy pressure supports further contraction and
form a He WD. More massive stars may also produce He
WDs if they interact and lose their envelopes before He
shell burning. Low-mass stars (0.8 ≤MZAMS/M⊙≤2)
may burn He via the so-called He flash to create a
carbon-oxygen core. Whereas, intermediate-mass stars
(2 ≤MZAMS/M⊙≤8) can create a carbon-oxygen
core via core-He burning. Eventually electron degener-
acy pressure takes over to leave behind a carbon-oxygen
WD. Higher-mass stars (8 ≤MZAMS/M⊙≤10) ignite
both He and carbon in their cores. The ultimate fate
of these stars is determined by the mass of the neon-
oxygen-magnesium core after carbon burning ends. If
the core mass exceeds MCh ≈1.38 M⊙(Chandrasekhar
1931), electron capture initiates collapse to form a low-
mass NS (e.g., Miyaji et al. 1980; Nomoto 1984, 1987).
Otherwise, a ONe WD remains.2

2.2. Compact-Object Formation: Neutron stars and
black holes
Stars with ZAMS mass MZAMS/M⊙≳8 are massive
enough to ignite the elements beyond carbon, ultimately
forming an iron core. At collapse, the final structure of
these stars is composed of an iron core surrounded by
shells of various elements produced as a by-product of
nuclear burning at different stages of a star’s life. When
such a star explodes, it leaves behind a NS or a BH
(Fryer 1999; Fryer & Kalogera 2001; Heger et al. 2003).
We adopt two widely used explosion mechanisms for
NS or BH formation through core-collapse supernovae
(CCSNe), ‘rapid’ and ‘delayed’ (Fryer et al. 2012).
These mechanisms differ in the explosion time after the
core bounce set by the timescales on which neutrino-
driven instabilities are allowed to grow (Colgate &
White 1966; Boccioli et al. 2025). Relevant to this work,
this difference results in a ‘mass-gap’ in the BH mass
function at birth in the rapid prescription which we
adopt to be between 3 and 5 M⊙(Bailyn et al. 1998;
¨Ozel et al. 2010; Farr et al. 2011; Kreidberg et al. 2012).
In contrast, the delayed prescription has no mass gap.
For the NS mass distribution, the delayed prescription
produces heavier NSs compared to rapid due to higher
accretion on the proto-NS before the explosion (Fryer
et al. 2012). We also consider NS formation via electron-
capture SN (ECSN). The key parameter in this process
is the range of He-core mass that no longer forms an
iron core. The collapse is initiated by electron capture
by 24Mg and 20Ne, which drops the electron degeneracy
pressure inside the stellar core. We adopt that He-core
masses between 1.4 −2.5 M⊙may form NSs via ECSN
(Podsiadlowski et al. 2004). NSs may also form if ECSN
is triggered via the so-called accretion induced collapse
(AIC) of an ONeMg WD. In particular, if an ONeMg
WD’s mass exceeds MCh due to accretion via RLOF,
a NS can form (Nomoto & Kondo 1991; Podsiadlowski
et al. 2004).
Due to deviations from spherical symmetry during the
collapse and subsequent explosion, the stellar remnant
may experience a kick at the time of formation (Fryer
& Kalogera 2001; Janka 2013).
For NSs, we adopt
a bimodal Maxwellian distribution with σ = 265 and
20 km s−1 for CCSN and ECSN, respectively (Hobbs
et al. 2005; Podsiadlowski et al. 2004).
For BHs, we
assume a fallback-modulated kick magnitude, where the
initial kicks are sampled from a Maxwellian distribution
with σ = 265 km s−1 (Belczynski et al. 2012; Fryer et al.
2012).
2.3. Synthetic Milky Way population
COSMIC iteratively simulates binaries until the distri-
butions of observable parameters including MCO, MLC,
Porb, and Ecc converges to a high degree of resolution.
We quantify the convergence of the distributions us-
ing a ‘Match’ parameter inspired by the matched fil-
tering technique (Chatziioannou et al. 2017; Breivik
et al. 2020). For this study we have adopted log10(1 −

COSMIC to evolve only a fraction of the MW mass to
generate a population of CO–LC binaries, referred to as
the intrinsic population, which accurately captures the
parameter space of the full CO–LC binary population
in the MW. To estimate the total number of CO–LC
binaries in the MW, we scale the intrinsic CO–LC pop-
ulation by a factor equal to the ratio of the MW mass
(Mm12i as specified by m12i) to the total simulated
mass (Msim including both single and binary stars de-
termined based on the adopted binary fraction) which
created the converged intrinsic CO–LC population. The
number of CO–LC binaries in the MW (NCO–LC,MW)

3. DETECTIBILITY BY Gaia
In this section, we describe the detectability criteria
that we adopted to characterize a binary as resolvable
by Gaia, based on magnitude G and the astrometric
motion of the LC due to its orbit around the CO. We
separately consider detectability of binaries resolvable
by EOM and within the more stringent restrictions of
DR3.
Note that the Gaia selection function used in this
study is approximate, as it compares the sky-projected
angular size with the Gaia pointing error. It is, how-
ever, computationally inexpensive and thus suitable for
our large-scale simulations. More sophisticated Gaia se-
lection functions, which incorporate additional complex-
ities such as the scanning law, crowding, and photomet-
ric systematics, have been developed following the re-
lease of DR3 (El-Badry et al. 2024a; Lam et al. 2025).
While our simplified approach does not capture these ef-
fects in detail, they are unlikely to significantly alter our
population-level conclusions.
Moreover, our predicted
detection rates are consistent with theoretical studies
employing more refined approaches (Nagarajan et al.
2025a; Yamaguchi et al. 2025) as well as with the cur-
rently observed census of CO–LC binaries in the DR3
data (Shahaf et al. 2022, 2024; El-Badry et al. 2024b,c).
3.1. End-of-mission resolvable population
We employ three important observational cuts while
defining the resolvability of the detached CO–LC bina-
ries by Gaia’s astrometry:
1. Porb/yr ≤10,
2. G ≤20,
3. α ≥kσG,
where α is the sky-projected angular size of the LC orbit,
σG is Gaia’s pointing precision as a function of G (Linde-
gren et al. 2018). We create two different sets, k = 1 (3)
for optimistic (pessimistic) case.
These observational
cuts are identical to those adopted in Chawla et al.
(2022) where a more detailed description is given. We
refer to these sets as optimistic and pessimistic ‘Gaia-
resolvable’ CO–LC binaries.
3.2. DR3 selection cuts
Gaia’s NSS catalog includes sources that do not have
optimal single-star solutions. The input source list used
to create the NSS catalog was selected from single-star
solutions with renormalized unit weight error RUWE ≥
1.4 (Belokurov et al. 2020; Penoyre et al. 2020, 2022a,b;
Gandhi et al. 2021; Andrew et al. 2022; Gaia Collabo-
ration et al. 2023b), indicating that these sources might
either be a binary star or have a problematic astro-
metric single star solution.
This input list gets fur-
ther filtered using additional cuts on available param-
eters, ultimately selecting sources with G ≤19. Despite
the stringent selection criteria, the output dataset still
contained spurious binary solutions (Gaia Collaboration
et al. 2023b).
A significant portion of the false posi-
tives in the two-body orbital solutions arose from bina-
ries with orbital periods matching harmonics of Gaia’s
scanning law.
The processing of orbital solutions be-
gins with a period search, which can lead to the detec-
tion of periods where the motion of the photocenter is
due to the scanning law rather than true periodic mo-
tion (Holl, B. et al. 2023b). A majority of these solu-
tions, particularly those with periods of approximately
100 days, were problematic and resulted in incorrect or
unreasonably large mass functions (Gaia Collaboration
et al. 2023b; Halbwachs, Jean-Louis et al. 2023). To fil-
ter out these spurious solutions from the NSS catalog,
the DR3 pipeline adopts additional cuts.
We identify the synthetic CO–LC populations de-
tectable in DR3 by adopting selection cuts in addition
to those adopted for identifying the EOM CO–LC pop-
ulations (subsection 3.1) based on the selection cuts rel-
evant specifically for DR3.

4.1. Intrinsic CO–LC population in the MW
The distributions of the stellar and orbital properties
of the intrinsic CO–LC population provide important
clues about the underlying binary interaction physics.
Various binary interaction processes, for example, MT,
CE evolution, tidal evolution, and SN, collectively shape
the present-day characteristics of the CO–LC popula-
tion. Analyzing the trends observed in the properties
helps to understand how the parameter space of the in-
trinsic population is represented by the Gaia observed
CO–LC population.

5. COMPARISON WITH DETECTED CO–LC
CANDIDATES
Gaia’s NSS catalog has already enabled the discover-
ies of several CO–LC candidates using astrometry, spec-
troscopy, and photometry. In the following sections, we
provide an overview of the detected CO–LC candidates
and compare the properties of the detected CO–LC can-
didates with our modeled populations where applicable.
5.1. Detached BH–LC candidates discovered in DR3
Three detached BH–LC binaries has been identified
using DR3 astrometry and radial velocity followup, ref-
ered to as Gaia BH1, BH2, and BH3 (El-Badry et al.
2022c, 2023; Gaia Collaboration et al. 2024). Gaia BH1
contains a 9.62 M⊙BH with a bright solar-type MS com-
panion with MLC/M⊙= 0.93 and [Fe/H] = −0.2, in an
orbit with Porb/day = 185.6 and Ecc ≈0.45. Theoret-
ical modeling of the galactic orbit indicates a thin disk
origin (El-Badry et al. 2022c). Gaia BH2 consists of an
8.9 M⊙BH orbiting a red giant with MLC/M⊙≈1 in
a fairly wide (Porb/day = 1276), eccentric (Ecc ≈0.52)
orbit. Gaia BH3 is a 32.7 M⊙BH with a metal poor
([Fe/H] = −2.56) companion of mass 0.76 M⊙in a wide
(Porb/day ∼4253) eccentric (Ecc ≈0.7) orbit. Nev-
ertheless, none of the BH–LC binaries produced in our
models survive the DR3 cuts.
Indeed, a lot of past theoretical studies have tried to
explain the Gaia BHs (El-Badry et al. 2022c, 2023; El-
Badry 2024; Rastello et al. 2023; Tanikawa et al. 2023a;
Tanikawa et al. 2025; Di Carlo et al. 2024; Generozov &
Perets 2024; Gilkis & Mazeh 2024; Iorio, Giuliano et al.
2024; Kotko et al. 2024; Kruckow, Matthias U. et al.
2024; Li et al. 2024; Mar´ın Pina, Daniel et al. 2024; Fan-
toccoli et al. 2025). The overwhelming consensus is that
under the standard assumptions of binary populations
synthesis of isolated binaries, it is challenging to form
BH–LC binaries resembling the properties of Gaia BH1
and BH2. In both cases, the general expectation is that
the progenitor binary must go through at least one CE
evolution. However, the typical choice of the efficiency
parameter α ∼1 fails to reproduce the large enough fi-
nal orbits. Instead, α ≈14 is required (El-Badry et al.
2022c). Such high values of α indicates dominant addi-
tional heating mechanisms to eject the envelope and is
not expected (e.g., Ivanova 2018).
Alternatively, such BH–LC binaries could also be pro-
duced inside young star clusters (YSCs, e.g., Porte-
gies Zwart et al. 2010; Shikauchi et al. 2020) and dense
star clusters (Chatterjee et al. 2017; Fantoccoli et al.
2025) via dynamical processes. Recent numerical stud-
ies have shown the potential of YSCs to form Gaia BH1
and BH2-like binaries (Rastello et al. 2023; Tanikawa
et al. 2023a; Di Carlo et al. 2024) indicating that YSCs
with initial mass ∼3 × 102–3 × 104 M⊙are likely most
efficient in their production.
The story for Gaia BH3 is arguably more fascinating.
The Galactic orbit of Gaia BH3 and the chemical com-
position of its LC establishes its association of the ED-2
stream, believed to be created by a disrupted globular
cluster of mass between 2 × 103–4 × 104 M⊙(Balbinot,
E. et al. 2023, 2024; Dodd, Emma et al. 2023). This
would indicate that Gaia BH3 may also have formed
via dynamical processes (e.g., Mar´ın Pina, Daniel et al.
2024). Nevertheless, Iorio, Giuliano et al. (2024) showed

5.2. Detached NS–LC candidates discovered in DR3
El-Badry et al. (2024c) have reported 21 NS–LC can-
didates in the DR3 catalog. For these observed NS–LC
candidates, the MNS distribution ranges between 1.25−
1.9 M⊙, with MS companions with MLC ranging be-
tween 0.7 −1.3 M⊙. They have near-solar metallicities
(−0.5 ≤[Fe/H] ≤0.5) and are within 1.5 kpc. Most of
the NS–LC binaries have Porb between 102 −103 days
and inferred eccentricity higher than 0.4 (Hobbs et al.
2005).
Our models predict roughly 20−40 resolvable NS–LC
binaries in DR3. These have MNS/M⊙∼1.25–1.9. The
Porb is between 102 −103 days with a wide eccentricity
spread 0 −0.98 (Figure 8). About ∼90% of the NS–LC
binaries with NSs formed via CCSN are highly eccen-
tric (Ecc ≥0.8), in contrast, those produced via ECSN
exhibit a more equitable spread in the eccentricities.
Clearly, the modeled NS–LC properties and those of the
observed candidates show a reasonably good agreement.
In particular, we find one NS–LC binary very similar
in properties to Gaia NS1 (El-Badry et al. 2024b) in
our modeled DR3 population. This binary has MNS =
1.9 M⊙, Porb = 1056 days, MLC/M⊙= 0.89 and has
a circular orbit (pink star in Figure 8). The NS pro-
genitor expands during the giant branch and initiates
a CE phase. This shrinks the orbit from 674 days be-
fore the start of CE to 0.5 days and completely erases
the initial eccentricity. Afterwards, at the time of NS
formation, the natal kick expands the orbit to 2.4 days
and makes the orbit eccentric. At a later time, the LC
fills its Roche lobe and initiates mass transfer to the NS.
This further expands the orbit to its present day value
when the binary detaches. This final MT episode again
circularizes the orbit. Furthermore, this model NS–LC
binary has a present-day age of ≈9 Gyr, comparable
to the inferred age of Gaia NS1. Overall, while high-
eccentricity NS–LC binaries seem to be more common,
it is possible to form DR3-detectable NS–LC binaries
with circular and close to circular orbits, via an episode
of MT post NS formation.

6. CONCLUSIONS
In this work, we present a detailed analysis of the
Gaia-detectable detached CO–LC populations. We dis-
cuss the key present-day observable properties of the
intrinsic CO–LC binary populations in the Milky Way
and investigate the same for the subsets that are resolv-
able by Gaia’s astrometry with DR3 and EOM selection
cuts.
• The model intrinsic present-day Milky Way pop-
ulation of CO–LC binaries exhibits a wide spread
in the MCO distribution, spanning from 0.1 M⊙to
45 M⊙. WDs, NSs, and BHs exhibit mass ranges
0.1 ≤MWD/M⊙≤1.4, 1.2 ≤MNS/M⊙≤3, and
3 ≤MBH/M⊙≤45 (Figure 1).
• The MCO distribution (Figure 1) clearly distin-
guishes the populations generated by the two
adopted SN models. The rapid SN prescription
yields a pronounced lower mass-gap (3 −5 M⊙) in
the MCO distribution, whereas the delayed pre-
scription does not exhibit such a gap. Notably, a
small fraction (∼1%) of BHs located within the
mass gap in the rapid model form via AIC of NSs.
• The intrinsic NS–LC binaries with Porb/yr ≤10 in
our models do not contain NSs with MNS/M⊙≥2.
We find that all of these high-mass NSs with short-
period orbits are accreting via RLOF. This indi-
cates that such high mass NSs are unlikely to be
found in detached systems by Gaia. Indeed, no
such candidate NS–LC binaries have been identi-
fied using DR3 yet.
• We find a significant difference in the Ecc distri-
bution for CO–LC binary orbits depending on the
adopted SN prescription (Figure 2). The delayed
model exhibits higher Ecc compared to the rapid
model; ∼70% (30%) of BH–LC binaries with
Porb/yr ≤10 exhibit Ecc ≤0.1 in the rapid
(delayed) model.
• All model CO–LC binaries detectable by Gaia’s
DR3 or EOM go through MT, CE, and tidal evo-
lution, all of which contribute to erasing the initial
orbital Ecc. The final Ecc is set by natal kicks
during CO formation. As a result, devoid of na-
tal kicks for WDs, the detectable WD–LC binaries
produced in our fiducial model exhibit only circu-
lar orbits. In contrast, with large natal kicks, 57%
(18%) of the NS–LC binaries with NSs formed via
CCSN have Ecc > 0.5 (Ecc < 0.1). Due to the
adopted lower natal kicks for ECSN, ∼42% (40%)
of the NS–LC binaries with NSs formed via ECSN
have Ecc > 0.5 (Ecc < 0.1) (see subsection 4.1).
• We find that the relative production of CO–LC
binaries is metallicity dependent: systems with a
BH as the compact object decline in frequency at
higher metallicities due to enhanced stellar-wind
mass loss, whereas the formation rate of WD/NS–
LC binaries remains largely unaffected (see Fig-
ure 4).
• Our models predict that by EOM, Gaia could
identify 102–103 BH–LC, 103–104 NS–LC, and
∼106 WD–LC binaries using astrometry alone.
The respective expected numbers in DR3 from
our model are zero BH–LC, 20–40 NS–LC, and
∼4 × 103 WD–LC binaries (see subsection 4.2).
• The predicted number as well as the orbital prop-
erties of DR3-detectable NS–LC binaries in our
models agree well with the observed NS–LC bina-
ries (see Figure 8).
• The orbital properties, in particular the observed
eccentricities for the WD–LC binaries require
modest 5–15 km s−1 natal kicks during WD for-
mation (see Figure 9).
In Chawla et al. (2022, 2024), we demonstrated the
potential of the Gaia sky survey to detect the BH–LC
binary population using astrometric, photometric, and
spectroscopic observations. This work extends our pre-
vious efforts and complements our earlier findings, fur-
ther establishing our prediction that Gaia is expected
revolutionize the study of CO–LC binaries through its
discoveries (Andrews et al. 2022; El-Badry et al. 2022c,
2023; El-Badry et al. 2024b; El-Badry et al. 2022a; Sha-
haf et al. 2022, 2024; Chakrabarti et al. 2023; Tanikawa
et al. 2023b; Gaia Collaboration et al. 2024). It is heart-
ening that our earlier predictions are roughly consistent
