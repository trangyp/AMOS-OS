Abstract
In this paper we explore the concept of locally band preserving functions, introduced
by Ercan and Wickstead, on Dedekind complete Φ-algebras. Specifically, we show that all
super order differentiable functions are locally band preserving. Furthermore, some foun-
dational results from classical analysis are proved in this setting, such as the Intermediate
Value Theorem, the Extreme Value Theorem, and the Mean Value Theorem. Moreover,
we show that these generalisations can fail for functions that are not locally band pre-
serving. With the goal in mind to further develop the theory of complex differentiation in
Dedekind complete complex Φ-algebras, a complex version of the Mean Value Theorem is
also provided.
Keywords: differentiation, complex vector lattice, locally band preserving, Mean
Value Theorem, Extreme Value Theorem, Intermediate Value Theorem

Introduction
The theory of complex analysis on Banach spaces enjoys a long, well-known,
and fruitful history, which is summarised in the text [Muj86]. This theory
generalises the classical theory of functions of a complex variable by using
norms as an abstraction of the ordinary modulus on the complex plane.
Recently, the papers [RS19,RS25] took an alternative approach to com-
plex analysis in abstract spaces: an order-theoretic approach. Indeed, [RS19]
began this journey by proving an nth root test for the absolute order con-
vergence of series in universally complete complex Φ-algebras (unital f-
algebras). From this result, Cauchy-Hadamard formulas for calculating the
radius of convergence for power series in this setting were obtained.
This order-theoretic complex analysis was furthered in [RS25], where the
authors introduced two notions of differentiability built from the notion of
order convergence in Dedekind complete Φ-algebras: order differentiability
and the much stronger super order differentiability.
Such results enable one to meaningfully investigate analytical properties
of certain spaces which do not have a natural norm, such as C∞
C (X). They
furthermore illustrate how fundamental concepts in vector lattice theory,
such as weak order units and band projections, have purposeful roles to
play in complex analysis. Thirdly, this theory, through analogue, sheds light
on an order-theoretical real analysis counterpart. It is to this real analysis
complement that we devote much of our attention in this paper.
In Sections 2 and 3 we introduce ε-δ reformulations of order continuity,
order differentiability, and super order differentiability, with the goal in mind
to have the results in this paper resemble the classical theory.
Using these reformulations, the objective of Sections 4 and 5 is to prove
analogues of a Boundedness Theorem, an Intermediate Value Theorem, an
Extreme Value Theorem, and a Mean Value Theorem in Dedekind com-
plete real Φ-algebras. It turns out, however, that order continuity on order
intervals is insufficient to prove these analogues.
Therefore, in Section 4
we consider locally band preserving functions, which were introduced by Er-
can and Wickstead in [EW98].
The reader can also infer the similarities
between locally band preserving functions and functions with the so-called
local property, introduced by Avil´es and Zapata in [AZ18], where Φ-algebras
of measurable functions are considered. These locally band preserving func-
tions prove to be a natural class of functions to consider, since it will be
shown that all super order differentiable functions are locally band preserv-

ing. Furthermore, it is also demonstrated that the Boundedness Theorem,
the Intermediate Value Theorem, and the Extreme Value Theorem can fail
for functions that are not locally band preserving.
In Section 5 we prove the analogues of the aforementioned classical the-
orems for order continuous and locally band preserving functions defined on
a closed order interval. Using our Extreme Value Theorem, we then provide
an analogue of Rolle’s Theorem and of the Mean Value Theorem. As a con-
sequence, a super order differentiable function that has derivative identically

illustrate through example that (i) this last result can fail for functions that
are order differentiable but not super order differentiable, and (ii) that an
order differentiable function need not be locally band preserving.
Finally, in order to further develop the theory of complex differentiation in
Dedekind complete complex Φ-algebras, a complex version of the Mean Value
Theorem is proved. Consequently, any complex super order differentiable
function with zero derivative on an order neighbourhood must be constant.

The reader is referred to the standard texts [AB06, LZ71, dP81, Zaa97] for

A Φ-algebra is an f-algebra with a multiplicative unit, which will be
denoted by e. Given a real vector lattice F, the vector space complexification

2.1
Band decompositions from inequalities
Because Dedekind completeness implies the projection property, every band
B in E is a projection band.
This fact means that E = B ⊕Bd, and,
associated with B, there is a band projection P: E →B. The reader may
assume that any notation used to denote a band carries over to its band
projection (replacing the B with P), e.g. the band projections of B, B1, and
Bx will be written as P, P1, and Px respectively.
We record some basic facts about bands, band projections, and weak order
units in Proposition 2.1 below that we will repeatedly use throughout this
paper. The proof of (i) follows from [AB06, Theorem 2.37], and statement (ii)
is a consequence of (i). Statement (iii) follows from [AB06, Theorem 2.44]
and [Zaa97, Theorem 18.13], with (iv) as a consequence.
Statement (v)
follows from [AB06, Theorem 1.45], and a proof of statement (vi) can be
found in [Zaa97, Theorem 32.5]. Since the band generated by x contains the
band generated by r whenever 0 ≤r ≤x, statement (vii) is evident. Since
every band is a projection band in E, statement (viii) follows. Statement (ix)
holds, because the sum of two bands is a band when E has the projection
property by [LZ71, Theorem 30.1(ii)], and the fact that the bands form a
distributive lattice by [LZ71, Theorem 22.6].

2.2
An ε-δ reformulation of order continuity
The notation f : dom(f) →E refers to a function whose domain dom(f)
is a subset of E. We define what it means for such functions to be order
continuous in terms of order convergence, and reformulate the definition to
resemble the classical ε-δ definition.
Notation 2.9. Given a real (or complex) vector lattice E, the notation
A ↘0 means A ⊆Eρ and inf A = 0.
If additionally A is downwards
directed, we write A ↓0.
Definition 2.10. Let E be a real (or complex) vector lattice. A net (xα)α
in E is said to converge in order to x ∈E, written xα →x, if there exists an
E ↘0 such that for every ε ∈E there is an α0 satisfying
α ≥α0 =⇒|xα −x| ≤ε.

This convergence is the only one on E that will be considered in this
paper.
Remark 2.11. In Definition 2.10, the set E can be replaced by the set
of finite infima of its elements, making it downwards directed, but without
changing the infimum.
Definition 2.12. Let E be a real (or complex) vector lattice. A function
f : dom(f) →E is called order continuous at c ∈dom(f) if whenever xα →c
in dom(f), we have f(xα) →f(c). The function f is called order continuous
if it is order continuous at every point in dom(f).
In order to make the similarity with classical theory in this paper explicit,
the above definition is reformulated to resemble the classical ε-δ definition.
Proposition 2.13. Let E be a real (or complex) vector lattice. A function
f : dom(f) →E is order continuous at c ∈dom(f) if and only if for every
∆↓0 there exists an E ↘0 with the property that for all ε ∈E there exists

A := {(x, δ): x ∈dom(f), δ ∈∆, and |x −c| ≤δ}
is directed by (x1, δ1) ≤(x2, δ2) if and only if δ2 ≤δ1.
Define, for any

If x ∈dom(f) with |x −c| ≤δ0, then (x, δ0) ≥(x0, δ0) = α0, and so we have

Order differentiability
We define what it means for a function to be differentiable on subsets of E,

can also be used for real Φ-algebras.
Definition 3.1. Let E be a Dedekind complete real (or complex) Φ-algebra,

unit.

For c ∈E and r ≫0, define

4
Locally band preserving functions
This section is dedicated to the notion of locally band preserving functions.
The main reason being that order continuous functions on order intervals, in
general, do not inherit the properties real-valued functions on real intervals
posses. We start with Example 4.1, where an example is given of an un-
bounded order continuous function on a closed order interval. Example 4.2
shows that the Intermediate Value Theorem and the Extreme Value Theo-
rem can fail for order continuous and order bounded functions defined on a
closed order interval. These results are recovered for locally band preserving,
order continuous functions in Section 5.

Definition 4.3. Let E be a Dedekind complete real (or complex) Φ-algebra,
