Passive nonlinear FIR filters
for data-driven control
Zixing Wanga, Graduate Student Member, IEEE, Fulvio Fornia, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract— We propose a new class of passive nonlin-
ear finite impulse response operators. This class is con-
structed by the action of finite impulse response filters
in a lifted space. This allows for efficient control synthe-
sis through constrained optimization. Closed-loop perfor-
mance is taken into account through least-squares fitting,
based on the theory of virtual reference feedback tuning.
Passivity is established through efficient linear constraints,
based on sampling in the frequency domain. Because of
passivity, this class of operators is particularly suited for
the control of physical systems, such as electromechanical
systems.
Index Terms— Passivity, Data-driven Control, Nonlinear
system identification
I. INTRODUCTION
Passivity is a common property for most physical systems
[1]–[3]. It formalizes the idea that, if a physical system has no
internal sources, its internal stored energy must be bounded
by the energy supplied at its ports [2], [3]. For feedback
systems, the passivity theorem guarantees that the feedback
interconnection of passive systems remains passive [1], [4],
[5]. This makes passivity a crucial compositional property
in the analysis and synthesis of interconnected systems. In
fact, passivity-based control is at the core of the control of
electromechanical systems
[2], [6], [7], from robotics [8]–
[10], to complex networks [11], [12].
In the context of electromechanical systems, passivity-
based control aims to achieve desired objectives by shaping
the closed-loop (potential) energy and modulating its dissi-
pation [13]. This energy perspective is intrinsically robust.
The passivity theorem guarantees system stability even in
the presence of plant uncertainties, as long as passivity is
preserved. However, achieving performance guarantees relies
on ‘the exact knowledge of the energy function of the system’
[2, Chap. 3, Sec. 1.3]. This means that optimizing passivity-
based controllers for performance is inherently challenging.
From an energy-shaping perspective, this translates to the
difficulty of finding the best achievable shape of the potential
energy and the most effective damping-injection strategy.
In this paper, we look into the design of nonlinear passive
operators for passive physical systems, from a data-driven
perspective. We design these as optimal controllers (given

experimental (model-free) data. From an optimization perspec-

Passivity guarantees a well-behaved closed loop even if the
plant is uncertain or the data is sparse or corrupted. If the
data contains valuable information, we derive a control action
that optimizes performance. The cost is that the optimality of
our control action is limited by the requirement of passivity.
We aim to answer the following question. How can one
construct a passive nonlinear controller capable of delivering
specified performance, particularly when the nonlinear passive
plant dynamics are only partially known or entirely unknown?
We tackle this problem by using an expressive controller struc-
ture that can be (i) efficiently trained from data and for which
(ii) we can write efficient passivity constraints. We work with
a specific class of nonlinear finite impulse response (NFIR)
operators. While diverse NFIR filter structures exist in the
literature [14], [15], our design is deliberately constructed with
the goal of deriving efficient passivity constraints. Causality is
built in, and convexity with respect to the operator parameters
enables streamlined tuning through convex optimization. The
expressivity of our NFIR operators, that is, their capacity
to capture general control actions, is analyzed within the
framework of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces. Our NFIR
operators are linear operators in a lifted space. This linear rep-
resentation is instrumental in establishing passivity conditions
with high computational efficiency. This allows us to extend
the work of [16], to derive a set of linear inequalities based
on sampling in the frequency-domain of the linear operator
associated with the NFIR operator.
Given a target closed-loop behavior, we use the plant data
to infer the input-output relation of an ideal controller, taking
advantage of the theory of the nonlinear virtual reference
feedback tuning. We then tune a NFIR controller via least
squares, to optimally approximated the input-output behavior
of the ideal controller. When combined with explicit passivity
constraints, this process leads to a constrained least-squares
problem, which yields a passive nonlinear controller. The
closed loop between passive controller and passive plant is
necessarily stable. The optimality of the controller leads to
performance guarantees, within the limits of (i) data quality,
(ii) passivity constraints, (iii) expressivity of the operator class.

a specialized instance of the work by [25], particularly when
interpreted through reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces. How-
ever, we address two critical limitations: ensuring the causality
of the passive operator and guaranteeing the computational
tractability of the passivity constraints. Other relevant work
such as [26]–[28], enforce passivity by imposing Lagrangian
or Hamiltonian structures, with models learned via kernel
methods or neural networks. In contrast, our approach focuses
on identifying the operator in an input-output framework and
adopts simple least-squares optimization.
Beyond identification, the design of passive controllers
directly from plant data is a growing area of research in
data-driven control [29]–[32]. Although most of the work is
confined to the linear setting, [32] provides a comprehensive
nonlinear design method, where data acquisition and control
design are co-optimized, leading to a nonconvex problem.
In contrast, data acquisition and control optimization remain
separated in our approach, which allows us to remain within
the domain of convex optimization. Furthermore, our specific
choice of NFIR operators leads to more efficient passivity
constraints. [16] shows that constraints based on frequency-
domain sampling are orders of magnitude more efficient than
constraints based on linear matrix inequalities.
This paper is structured as follows. The control goal is
formalized in Section II. Section III reviews linear finite
impulse response filters and introduces and discusses our class
of NFIR operators. Section VII briefly introduces the class of
extended NFIR operators. Passivity is developed in Section IV.
The implementation details are discussed in Section V. Sev-
eral examples, ranging from passive identification to control
synthesis, are presented in Section VI. Conclusions follows.

B. Comparison with the literature
As mentioned in Remark 2, our NFIR operators can be
interpreted as a restricted causal version of the nonlinear
operators proposed in [25]. Passivity is also explored in [25],
using linear matrix inequalities (LMIs) based on the matrices
Mrj in (19b). A significant limitation of [25] lies in the
computational complexity of these constraints, which scales
quadratically with length of the training data, leading to
feasibility challenges for large datasets. Although these issues
are mitigated in [25] through the introduction of suitable input-
output bases. these may preclude the real-time implementation
of the controller. In contrast, the passivity constraints of The-
orem 1 are independent of the training data length, depending
solely on the size of the operator’s finite memory. Furthermore,
the convolution structure of Γ(gj) enables the derivation of
frequency-domain constraints (23b). These are formulated as
linear inequalities, offering significantly higher computational
efficiency compared to linear matrix inequalities, as illustrated
in an example in Section VI-A.
The concept of incrementally passive operators within re-
producing kernel Hilbert spaces has been explored in [24] and
[36]. In [24], operators with incremental gain less than one
are derived using regularized unconstrained optimization, with
passivity then recovered via a scattering transform. [36] further
refines these results, by identifying a ‘memory functional’,
which allows for the derivation of causal operators as in this
paper. A key distinction with our work is that Theorem 1
enforces passivity directly, via constrained optimization. In
addition, our NFIR operators are passive, a weaker property
than incremental passivity. This difference is significant since,
as discussed in [24, Sec. IX.B] and [25, Sec. I], the use of the
scattering transform to derive passive but not incrementally
passive operators is not feasible.
The proof of Theorem 1 leverages the lifting of linear
passive FIR filters into a larger feature space. This involves
the pre- and post-composition with a ‘lifting’ operator and
its adjoint, both carefully designed to ensure the causality
of the resultant nonlinear operator. Although this idea is not
completely new, with early roots in state-space modeling [5,
Prop 2.11 and Chap 4.3], our contribution is in developing this
lifting in the operator-theoretic setting. This extension allows
us to construct a new class of nonlinear input-output operators
from linear FIR filters. We also provides an interpretation of
this lifting through the theory of reproducing kernel Hilbert
spaces.
By combining Theorem 1 with Example 1, we can derive
passive ‘gain-scheduling’ controllers. The idea of enforcing
passivity for gain-scheduling controllers with the goal of
guaranteeing closed-loop stability has been explored in the
early work of Damaren [37] and, more recently, by [38], [39].
As discussed in Sections VI-C and VII, our NFIR operators
exhibit a gain-scheduling property similar to that proposed in
[39]. However, our NFIR operators are not limited to gain-
scheduling, as the selection of the set of lifting functions F
allows for the realization a wider class of nonlinear operators.

VIII. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we introduced the class of NFIR operators. We
showed that these operators can be efficiently designed using
convex optimization and subsequently made passive via con-
strained optimization based on frequency sampling. Enforcing
passivity is a key contribution of this approach, which holds
true irrespective of the quality and quantity of the training
data. We also explored the representational capabilities of our
NFIR operators, providing an analysis through the theory of
reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and validating our findings
with numerical examples.
This work opens up several promising research directions.
The parallel bank of filters at the core of NFIR operators
could be generalized to richer interconnections. Within the
RKHS interpretation of Section III-B, this would correspond
to removing the constraint of Mrj = 0 in Equation (19b) for
all r ̸= j, allowing for coupling between any two branches
of a NFIR operator. However, this generality would lead


## Page 14


14

to higher computational costs during training and requires
further investigation. We are also interested in extending
NFIR operators to the multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO)
setting and to linear infinite impulse response (IIR) subfilters,
both in continuous and and discrete time. These developments
would broaden the applicability of the framework and enable
analog implementations. Finally, the class of externally driven
NFIR operators enables real-time adaptation of the lifting
functions, which could lead to new directions in the design of
adaptive controllers within an operator-theoretic setting. These
intriguing directions are beyond the scope of the current paper
and will be addressed in future work.
