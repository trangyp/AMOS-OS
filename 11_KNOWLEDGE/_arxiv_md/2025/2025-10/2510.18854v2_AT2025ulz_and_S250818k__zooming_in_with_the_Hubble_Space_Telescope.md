ABSTRACT
AT2025ulz is an optical/near-infrared transient discovered during follow-up of the candidate gravi-
tational wave (GW) event S250818k. Its young age (≲1 d), rapid decline and strong color evolution
over the first 48 hr classify it as a potential kilonova candidate. In this work, we present the results
of our observing campaign, carried out with the Gran Telescopio Canarias (GTC) and the Hubble
Space Telescope (HST). Although the early time evolution of AT2025ulz resembles some aspects of
a kilonova, its rapid onset (∼3 hr after the GW trigger) and luminosity (a factor of ∼5 brighter
than AT2017gfo in g-band) are difficult to reproduce. Only a small subset of our kilonova models
matches its multi-color light curve, and the inferred ejecta mass is uncomfortably large given the low
chirp mass (≲0.87 M⊙) of the GW candidate. HST observations place the transient within a nearby
(z =0.08489) spiral galaxy with on-going star-formation and measure a color (F336W −F160W ≈1.4
mag) that is too blue to match with a kilonova. Our data support the classification of AT2025ulz as
a supernova, initially undergoing a shock-cooling phase and later entering its photospheric phase, and
spectroscopically identified via its broad absorption features.

1. INTRODUCTION
The first direct detection of gravitational waves
(GWs) during the first LIGO observing run (O1) was
a watershed moment in modern astrophysics (B. P.
Abbott et al. 2016).
Shortly thereafter, the second
observing run (O2) led to the ground-breaking dis-
covery of GW170817 (B. P. Abbott et al. 2017a,b),
a binary neutron-star (NS) merger detected in GWs
and accompanied by a weak short gamma-ray burst
(GRB 170817A; A. Goldstein et al. 2017; V. Savchenko
et al. 2017), an optical/near-infrared (nIR) kilonova
(AT2017gfo; D. A. Coulter et al. 2017) and a non-
thermal afterglow radiation (E. Troja et al. 2017; G.
Hallinan et al. 2017).
Subsequent LIGO-Virgo-Kagra (LVK) observing runs
(R. Abbott et al. 2023; The LVK Collaboration 2025a)
underscored the challenges of GW follow-up. GW lo-
calizations often extend over hundreds of square degrees
with broad distance posteriors, requiring efficient search
strategies that balance wide-field tiling and galaxy-
targeted pointings informed by three-dimensional proba-
bility maps and galaxy catalogs (N. Gehrels et al. 2016;
L. P. Singer et al. 2016a; P. A. Evans et al. 2016; I.
Arcavi et al. 2017a; M. W. Coughlin et al. 2018; J.-G.
Ducoin et al. 2020; L. Salmon et al. 2020; A. M. Wat-
son et al. 2020; R. L. Becerra et al. 2021; G. D´alya
et al. 2022; D. O. Cook et al. 2023; D. A. Coulter
et al. 2025).
Even as network sensitivities improve,
a significant fraction of events retain large sky areas
and poorly constrained distances (P. Petrov et al. 2022;
B. P. Abbott et al. 2020a), complicating follow-up triage.
Moreover, the local Universe is rich in unrelated tran-
sients: core-collapse supernovae (ccSNe) and Type Ia
supernovae (SNe) dominate the optical transient rate
and can masquerade as young, blue kilonovae if sam-
pling is sparse (Z. Doctor et al. 2017; P. S. Cowperth-
waite & E. Berger 2015). Heterogeneous data streams
(prediscovery limits, early colors, low-resolution spec-
tra, radio/X-ray constraints) are rapidly combined to
identify high-probability kilonova candidates and down-
select interlopers (A. L. Thakur et al. 2020; N. Vieira
et al. 2020; K. Ackley et al. 2020; S. Antier et al. 2020;
K. L. Page et al. 2020; K. Paterson et al. 2021; T. Ahu-
mada et al. 2024; D. A. Coulter et al. 2025). Following
this triage workflow, no event after AT2017gfo was ele-
vated to the status of likely kilonova until AT2025ulz (R.
Stein et al. 2025; X. J. Hall et al. 2025a; B. O’Connor
et al. 2025a) was discovered within the localization of
the low-significance GW candidate S250818k ( The LVK
Collaboration 2025b).
S250818k22 is a sub-threshold GW candidate with
false alarm rate (FAR) of ≈1 per five months. It was
localized within an area of 786 deg2 (90% credible re-
gion) at a distance of 259±74 Mpc ( The LVK Collab-
oration 2025b). Despite its large localization area and
high probability of being terrestrial noise (71%), several
follow-up campaigns were initiated.
Observations were motivated by the fact that since
the start of O4, this event has the highest probability
(≈29%) of being a binary neutron star (BNS) merger
and thus to produce an electromagnetic signal.
AT2025ulz was identified by R. Stein et al. (2025) at
r≈21.3 AB mag within a galaxy, classified as elliptical,
at z ≈0.09, within the 3 σ distance interval set by the
LVK collaboration. Attention to the source intensified
when non-detections in ATLAS imaging constrained the
rise to ≲1.7 days prior to discovery (S. Srivastav et al.
2025), strengthening the case for a very young transient.
Within the next day, independent follow-up observations
consistently measured a rapid decline in flux accompa-
nied by pronounced reddening (M. Busmann et al. 2025;
X. J. Hall et al. 2025b; B. O’Connor et al. 2025b; R. L.
Becerra et al. 2025). Spectroscopic observations deter-
mined the redshift more precisely to be z = 0.0848 and
revealed a smooth featureless spectrum consistent with
a kilonova origin (V. Karambelkar et al. 2025). Unfor-
tunately, over the first four days, no near-infrared con-
straint was sufficiently sensitive to test this hypothesis
(G. Mo et al. 2025; S. Banerjee et al. 2025a; N. Passaleva
et al. 2025).
In this manuscript we report our observations of
AT2025ulz with the Hubble Space Telescope (HST)
which detected the source in the near-infrared and pro-
vided the first evidence of a color inconsistent with a
kilonova interpretation. Moreover, our imaging provides
a crisp view of its host galaxy, revealing a spiral mor-
phology with on-going star-formation. We leverage our
dataset with optical imaging and spectroscopy acquired
with the Gran Telescopio Canarias (GTC). Our dataset
supports the classification of this event as a Type II SN,
unrelated to the GW candidate trigger.

ΛCDM cosmology ( Planck Collaboration et al. 2020)

2.1. Gran Telescopio Canarias
We carried out the observations of AT2025ulz using
the GTC with the upgraded Optical System for Imag-
ing and low-Intermediate-Resolution Integrated Spec-
troscopy (OSIRIS; J. Cepa et al. 2000) under pro-
grams GTCMULTIPLE2A-25AMEX (PI: A. M. Wat-
son), GTCMULTIPLE3A-25AMEX (PI: A. M. Watson)
and GTCMULTIPLE3B-25A (PI: A. J. Castro Tirado).
2.1.1. Imaging Observations
Imaging observations were conducted on August 21
and 24, 2025 using the full set of griz filters. The 1st
epoch was observed with an average airmass of 1.2 and
a seeing of 0.8′′. The 2nd epoch was observed with an
average airmass of 1.4 and a seeing of 0.8′′.
We also
took the r-band acquisition image on August 30 with an
airmass of 1.3 and seeing of 1′′.
The photometry of the source is complicated by the
bright host galaxy.
We employed the saccadic Fast
Fourier Transform (SFFT) algorithm (L. Hu et al. 2022)
to disentangle the transient signal from the host galaxy,
derived from archival grz-band templates of the DESI
Legacy Imaging Surveys (A. Dey et al. 2019)23 for and
i-band template of the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS;
Abdurro’uf et al. 2022)24. Aperture photometry was
performed using Source Extractor (E. Bertin & S.
Arnouts 1996), with results shown in Table A1. Magni-
tudes are calibrated against the Pan-STARRS DR2 cat-
alog (H. Flewelling 2018) and in AB magnitude system
(J. B. Oke & J. E. Gunn 1983).
2.1.2. Spectroscopy
Spectroscopic data were taken with a 0.8′′ long slit
with an average airmass of 1.4 and exposures times of
6 × 300 s and 5 × 600 s on August 21 and 30, 2025,
respectively. The blue region of the spectrum was ob-
served with the R1000B grism, which provides a wave-
length coverage of 3630–7500 ˚A. The red part was cov-
ered with the R1000R grism with a range of 5100–
10000 ˚A. In the second epoch, the slit was rotated by
≈90 degrees to minimize contamination by the galaxy’s
light. As shown in Figure 3a, several emission lines (Hα,
Hβ, [OII], [OIII], [NII], [SII]) and absorption features
(CaH&K, NaID) are identified at a common redshift of
0.08489 ± 0.00003.
A comparison of the two spectra highlights the pres-
ence of a broad absorption feature at ≈6750 ˚A in the
latest spectrum, also identified in the VLT/MUSE (S.

However, its luminosity exceeded that of
AT2017gfo at a similar epoch (∆mr ≈−1.5 mag).
A kilonova classification is not confirmed by observa-
tions at later time (≳4 d). Our HST imaging detects the
source in the nIR filters and in the blue F336W filter at
4.8 d. The observed color F336W −F160W ≈1.4 mag
is substantially different from the color of AT2017gfo
at a similar epoch (F336W −F160W ≈7 mag; M. M.
Kasliwal et al. 2017; E. Troja et al. 2017). Moreover, the
observed flux is seen to rise out to 12.8 d in r-band when
our monitoring ended. The color, rising light curve, and
luminosity are instead naturally explained by a SN ori-
gin.
3.2. Host Galaxy
We performed a two–dimensional morphological anal-
ysis of the host galaxy with GALFIT (C. Y. Peng et al.
2002, 2010), using our HST images in the F606W and
F160W filters. We modeled the galaxy’s surface bright-
ness using a single S´ersic profile, parametrized with an
effective radius Re, index n, axis ratio q = b/a, and
position angle PA. GALFIT convolves the model with
the point spread function (PSF), selected from a grid of
spatially-dependent models (J. Anderson & I. R. King
2006), and compares it to the observed surface bright-
ness. Model performance was evaluated using χ2 mini-
mization and inspection of the residual images, obtained
by subtracting the best fit model from the science im-
ages.
The single component model leaves prominent resid-
uals, indicating a more complex morphology. The best

diagnostics, or from a recent decline in the SFR given
the different tracer timescales (see also X. J. Hall et al.

physical offset of 1.45 ± 0.06 kpc. The host-normalized
offset is ≈0.35.
These values are rather small com-
pared to the population of high-energy transients (B.

inclination of the system and the compression of lengths
along the line of sight, they should be considered as lower
bounds to the true offset.

4. CONCLUSIONS
The sub-threshold GW event S250818k, with its prob-
ability of being a binary NS merger, coupled with the
optical transient AT2025ulz identified within a nearby
and possibly elliptical galaxy (R. Stein et al. 2025), trig-
gered a broad interest for further observations across
multiple facilities.
HST imaging reveals that the transient was located
in a nearly edge-on spiral galaxy, where star-formation
is still on-going. Such environment is consistent with a
broad range of stellar progenitors, from NS mergers to
massive stars.
Our analysis shows that, at early times, some of
the properties of AT2025ulz are reminiscent of a kilo-
nova, at least in the optical band.
However, several
other ones, such as its early peak (≈3 hr) and color
(F336W −F160W ≈1.4 mag at 4.8 d), are not con-
sistent with this interpretation. Early peak emission is
not reproduced by any of our kilonova models, and is
more indicative of SN shock breakout and cooling. The
strength of the supernova cooling emission depends both
on internal shocks and shocks in the immediate circum-
stellar material, but typically is characterized by strong
UV emission with cooling timescales of 1–10 d (J. De La
Rosa et al. 2017; A. J. Bayless et al. 2022; B. M. Sub-
rayan et al. 2025). Ultraviolet emission from kilonova
(P. A. Evans et al. 2017) tends to drop off more quickly
(<1d) than supernova shock cooling (C. L. Fryer et al.
2024). Strong UV after 1 d, as observed with HST, in-
dicates a SN shock cooling event.
After the cooling peak, SN models predict a rise as
the standard SN light curve dominates the emission.
Therefore, a SN naturally explains the late-time (≳5
d) evolution of AT2025ulz. Broad absorption features
in our GTC optical spectrum confirm that the emission
was powered by fast-moving ejecta.
Their identifica-
tion as P-Cygni profiles in the Balmer series indicates
an ejecta composition (H-rich) and velocity (≈10,000–
16,000 km s−1) characteristic of a Type II SN.
