ACYCLIC COMPLEXES
JIANGSHENG HU, WEI REN, XIAOYAN YANG, HANYANG YOU†
Abstract. Let (X, Y) be a balanced pair in an abelian category A. Denote
by KE-ac(X) the chain homotopy category of right X-acyclic complexes with
all items in X, and dually by KE-ac(Y) the chain homotopy category of left
Y-acyclic complexes with all items in Y. We establish realizations of KE-ac(X)
and KE-ac(Y) as homotopy categories of model categories under mild condi-
tions. Consequently, we obtain relative versions of recollements of Krause and
Neeman-Murfet.
We further give applications to Gorenstein projective and
Gorenstein injective modules.
1. Introduction
A model structure on a category C is a triple of three classes of morphisms,
called cofibrations, fibrations, and weak equivalences, satisfying a few axioms; see
[17, 26] for details. When C is an additive category equipped with a model struc-
ture, its homotopy category in the sense of Quillen (i.e., the localization of C with
respect to weak equivalences) is a pretriangulated category in the sense of [3]. If C is
weakly idempotent complete, then the homotopy category of an exact model struc-
ture carries a triangulated structure (see [14, Section 6]). Consequently, a Quillen
equivalence between such model categories yields a triangle equivalence between
their homotopy categories.
The Hovey correspondence [17, 18] of abelian categories is an effective tool for
constructing model structures on abelian categories. It is inspired by the some-
what canonical model structure on a Frobenius category, but with two cotorsion
pairs mimicking the role played by the projectives and the injectives. Furthermore,
Hovey’s correspondence has been extended as the one-one correspondence between
exact model structures and the Hovey triples on weakly idempotent complete exact
categories, by Gillespie [12] (see also ˇSˇtov´ıˇcek [29]).
An important application of model category theory lies in providing systematic
methods for constructing recollements of triangulated categories (see [2, 9, 11, 13,
20]). Recall that the notion of a recollement, introduced by Beilinson, Bernstein,
and Deligne in [1], can be viewed as a form of “short exact sequence” of triangulated
categories, in which the functors involved admit both left and right adjoints. For
example, Becker [2] recovered Krause’s recollement Kac(I) −→K(I) −→D(R)
from [21], and Gillespie [13] recovered Neeman-Murfet’s recollement Kac(P) −→
K(P) −→D(R) from [23] using the theory of abelian model categories. Here, K(I)
(resp. K(P)) denotes the chain homotopy category of all complexes of injective
(resp. projective) modules, Kac(I) (resp. Kac(P)) is the full subcategory of exact
complexes of injective (resp. projective) modules, and D(R) is the derived category
of a ring R.

Recall that a pair (X, Y) of additive subcategories in an abelian category A
is said to be balanced if every object of A admits an X-resolution that remains
acyclic after applying HomA(−, Y ) for all Y ∈Y, and also admits a Y-coresolution
that is acyclic after applying HomA(X, −) for all X ∈X. This condition implies
a balancing phenomenon: the relative right-derived functors of HomA(−, −) can
be computed either via an X-resolution of the first variable, or equivalently via a
Y-coresolution of the second variable. In other words, the Hom functor is right-
balanced by X × Y; see [7, §8.2]. It is straightforward to verify that (P, I) is a
balanced pair. We refer to [5, 7, 8] for more examples of balanced pairs.
Let A be an abelian category equipped with a balanced pair (X, Y). Denote
by K(X) (resp. K(Y)) the chain homotopy category of complexes with all items
in X (resp. Y), and by KE-ac(X) (resp. KE-ac(Y)) the full subcategory of K(X)
(resp. K(Y)) consisting of complexes that are acyclic with respect to the functor
HomA(X, −) (resp. HomA(−, Y)). One then considers the following sequences of
triangulated categories:
KE-ac(X) −→K(X) −→DX (A) and KE-ac(Y) −→K(Y) −→DY(A),
where DX (A) (resp. DY(A)) is the relative derived category in the sense of [5,
Definition 3.1] (see also [7, 22, 30]). We have proved in [19] that the chain homotopy
categories K(X) and K(Y), and the relative derived categories DX (A) and DY(A)
can be realized as homotopy categories of model categories under certain conditions.
This naturally leads us to seek realizations of KE-ac(X) and KE-ac(Y) as homotopy
categories of suitable model categories, thereby obtaining relative versions of the
Krause’s and Neeman-Murfet’s recollements.

2. Preliminaries
Let A be a complete and cocomplete abelian category. A class of objects in A will
be always assumed to be closed under isomorphisms and under finite direct sums.
An exact category is a pair (A, E) where E is a class of “short exact sequences” in A,
i.e. kernel-cokernel pairs (i, p) depicted by A′
i↣A
p↠A′′, satisfying some axioms;
see Quillen’s original definition in [27]. A map such as i is called an admissible
monomorphism while p is called an admissible epimorphism. Recall that an exact
category (A, E) is weakly idempotent complete if every split monomorphism has a
cokernel and every split epimorphism has a kernel; see [12, Definition 2.2] or [4,
Definition 7.2]. We refer to a readable exposition [4] for details on exact categories.
Cotorsion pairs. In analogy to abelian categories, the axioms of exact categories
allow for the usual construction of the Yoneda Ext bifunctor Ext1
E(M, N). It is the
abelian group of equivalence classes of short exact sequences N ↣L ↠M. In
particular, we get that Ext1
E(M, N) = 0 if and only if every short exact sequence
N ↣L ↠M is isomorphic to the split exact sequence N ↣N ⊕M ↠M.
The definition of a cotorsion pair readily generalizes to exact categories; see [12,
Definition 2.1]. Specifically, a pair of classes (F, C) in (A, E) is a cotorsion pair
provided that F = ⊥C and C = F⊥, where the left orthogonal class ⊥C consists of
F such that Ext1
E(F, X) = 0 for all X ∈C, and the right orthogonal class F⊥is
defined similarly. We say the cotorsion pair (F, C) is hereditary if F is closed under
taking kernels of admissible epimorphisms between objects of F, and if C is closed
under taking cokernels of admissible monomorphisms between objects of C.
The cotorsion pair (F, C) is said to be complete if for any object M ∈A, there
exist short exact sequences C ↣F ↠M and M ↣C′ ↠F ′ with F, F ′ ∈F
and C, C′ ∈C. In this case, F ↠M is called a special right F-approximation (or,
special F-precover) of M, and M ↣C′ is called a special left C-approximation (or,
special C-preenvelope) of M.
Approximation and balanced pairs. Let X be a subcategory of the abelian
category A and M an object in A. A morphism f : X →M (resp. f : M →X)
with X ∈X is called a right X-approximation (resp. left X-approximation) of M,
if any morphism from an object in X to M (resp. M to X) factors through f.
The subcategory X is called contravariantly finite (resp. covariantly finite) if each
object in A has a right X-approximation (resp. left X-approximation).

Recall that a complex is right X-acyclic (resp. left Y-acyclic) if it remains acyclic
after applying HomA(X, −) for all X ∈X (resp. HomA(−, Y ) for all Y ∈Y).
A pair (X, Y) of subcategory in an abelian category A is called a balanced pair
if the following conditions are satisfied (see [5, Definition 1.1]):
(1) the subcategory X is contravariantly finite and Y is covariantly finite;
(2) for each object M ∈A, there is a complex · · · →X1 →X0 →M →0 with
each Xi ∈X which is both right X-acyclic and left Y-acyclic;
(3) for each object N ∈A, there is a complex 0 →M →Y0 →Y1 →· · · with
each Yi ∈Y which is both right X-acyclic and left Y-acyclic;
The balanced pair is called admissible if each right X-approximation is an epi-
morphism and each left Y-approximation is a monomorphism. It follows from [5,
Proposition 2.6] that if there exist two complete and hereditary cotorsion pairs
(X, Z) and (Z, Y) in A, then the pair (X, Y) is an admissible balanced pair. In
this case, (X, Z, Y) is called a cotorsion triple. It follows from [8, Theorem 4.4]
that the existence of complete and hereditary cotorsion triple in A is equivalent to
that A has enough projective objects and injective objects.
