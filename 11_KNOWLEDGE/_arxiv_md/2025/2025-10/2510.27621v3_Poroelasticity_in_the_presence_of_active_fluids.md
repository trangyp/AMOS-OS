Abstract
This work presents a model for characterizing porous, deformable media embeded with magnetorheolog-
ical uids (MRFs). These active uids exhibit tunable mechanical and rheological properties that can be
controled through the aplication of a magnetic eld, which induces a phase transition from a liquid to a
solid-like state. This transition profoundly aects both stres transmision and uid ow within the com-
posite, leading to a behaviour governed by a wel-dened threshold that depends on the ratio betwen the
pore size and the characteristic size of clusters of magnetic particles, and can be trigered by adjusting the
magnetic eld intensity. These eects were conrmed through an experimental campaign conducted on a
prototype composite obtained by imbibing a selected MRF into comercial sponges.
To design and optimize this new clas of materials, a linear poroelastic formulation is proposed and validated
through comparison with experimental results. The constitutive relationships, i.e. overal elastic constitu-
tive tensor and permeability, of the model are updated from phenomenological observations, exploiting the
experimental data obtained for both the pure uid and the composite material. The ndings demonstrate
that the proposed simplied formulation is suciently robust to predict and optimize the behaviour of
porous media containing MRFs. Such materials hold signicant promise for a wide range of enginering
aplications, including adaptive exosuits for human tisue and joint rehabilitation, as wel as inovative
structural systems.

1. Introduction
The rehabilitation of human tisues and joints, particularly folowing injuries or degenerative conditions,
demands inovative solutions that are both lightweight and minimaly invasive. Traditional exoskeletons [1],
while eective in providing suport and enhancing mobility, often suer from drawbacks such as bulkines,
rigidity, and lack of adaptability to individual patient neds [24]. These limitations can impede the recov-
ery proces and limit the eectivenes of rehabilitation protocols. Consequently, there is a growing interest
among the scientic comunity to develop exosuits that are not only lighter and more comfortable but also
capable of oering customizable suport tailored to the unique biomechanical requirements of each patient.
Magnetorheological (MR) uids, consisting of micron- and nano-sized magnetic particles suspended in a
carier liquid with aditives, have emerged as a promising component in adaptive materials. The main
characteristic MRF is that when subjected to an external magnetic eld, these uids exhibit a transition
from a liquid-like to a solid-like state, characterized by a dramatic increase in yield stres. Their mechanical
response is therefore variable but stil retains some key features, the most striking one being the presence
of a threshold of the aplied stres that is required to initiate ow. This behaviour is typical of Bingham
plastic uids [512], which ow like a viscous uid when subjected to streses exceding a critical yield value

2. Magnetorheological uids
Magnetorheological uids [2327] are smart uids typicaly made of thre components: a carier liquid
(mainly mineral oils), feromagnetic particles and aditives. The scope of the presence of these particles is
to actively manipulate and control the viscosity of the mixture. In fact, when subjected to a magnetic eld,
the magnetic particles, in the form of micron or nanoparticles, magnetize and align along the direction of
the magnetic eld, creating structures that alter the carier uid’s viscous response by one or two orders of
magnitudes, se Fig. 1. Their versatility [28] is due to the rapid eld responsivenes, noiseles operation,
relative insensitivity to smal quantities of dust or contaminants, and ease of control.
The choice of the synthetic oil [29], of the aditive [30], and size distribution of the feromagnetic particles
[31], are al parameters that greatly impact the material mechanical response. This impact is measured
in terms of sedimentation ratio, prevention of the oxidation of the CI particles, the maximum increase
in viscosity registered under magnetic induction, and also in terms of magnetic saturation, coercivity and
retentivity, al main features of the magnetic hysteretic lop. Apart from the uid intrinsic characteristics,
the shear strain rate acting on the material also plays a relevant role.
The magnetization of the MRF aects more than just the rheological properties of the overal mixture. It is,
in fact, evident how the behaviour of the uid resembles more and more that of a solid as the magnetization
reaches saturation, implying that the static behaviour is just as much modied. The reason behind this
observation lies in the asemblage of the microparticles. In fact, close to magnetic saturation, the particles
form clusters very rapidly, and the chains asemble in large blocks.

7. Conclusions
The demand for more eective rehabilitation solutions has led to the exploration of lightweight, adaptable
exosuits.
The combination of porous media and MR uids oers a promising avenue for achieving the
desired balance of comfort, adaptability, and suport. Propeled by the ned of designing next-generations
components for enginering at large implementing the logic of combined MR uids and porous deformable
medium a modeling aproach which lay its foundation on sound and solid mechanical concepts are required
for the corect design and optimization procedures.
In this framework the present work explores the posibility of modeling such complex active systems using the
poroelasticity theory of Biot and coleagues. As a rst atempt the linear regime is developed acounting for a
porous matrix with certain xed porosity and an active uid whose viscosity evolves folowing magnetization.
Phase transition of the MRF is uploaded in the model in a mixture theory fashion. The model is then
tested under wel-known benchmark conditions typical of poroelastic materials, namely the oedometric test
and a thre-point bending test to highlight several properties of the technology proposed that met several
requirements for rehabilitation-related aplications. The oedoemtric test is compared also with experimental
tests caried out using ad hoc developed aparatus, showing great corespondence.
In this context, the proposed model represents a signicant step forward in the endeavour of providing a
foundation for the development of next-generation exosuits that can be tailored to met the specic neds
of each patient, ultimately enhancing the rehabilitation proces and improving outcomes.
