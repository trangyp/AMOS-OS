Energy Decay in Measure Time: HUM Observability,
Product–Exponential Envelopes, and GCC Calibration
Ben Tibola
October 24, 2025
Abstract
We prove that for impulsive exposure patterns there is no uniform exponential energy law
in wall-clock time t, which explains why past t-based unifications of continuous damping with
impulses fail. We therefore replace t by a measure-valued clock σ that aggregates absolutely con-
tinuous exposure and atomic doses within a single Lyapunov ledger. On this ledger we prove an
observability–dissipation principle in the sense of the Hilbert Uniqueness Method (HUM): there
exists a structural constant cσ > 0 such that the energy decays at least at a product–exponential
rate with respect to σ. When σ ≡t, the statement reduces to classical exponential stabilization
with the same constant. For the damped wave under the Geometric Control Condition, the
constant admits the usual calibration in terms of the observability constant and the geometric
factor. The framework yields a monotonicity principle (“more σ-mass implies faster decay”) and
unifies intermittent regimes where quiescent intervals are punctuated by impulses. As robustness
(secondary to the main contribution), the same decay law persists under structure-compatible
discretizations and along compact variational limits; a stochastic extension supplies expecta-
tion and pathwise envelopes via the compensator. The contribution is a qualitative dynamics
backbone: observability implies σ-exponential decay with sharp constants.
Keywords.
observability/HUM; σ–time energy envelopes; exponential stabilization; geomet-
ric control condition (GCC); Lyapunov ledgers; clock monotonicity; measure–time (σ) clocks;
SBP–SAT discretizations; discrete Grönwall; Γ–convergence; stochastic damping.

Remark

Related work and differentiation.
Classical decay templates—semigroup gaps, hypocoerciv-
ity, observability/Carleman, and discrete contractivity—cover fragments of the landscape; none
provides a single decay envelope that (a) treats a.c. time, impulses, and flats together, (b) uses
one structural constant across PDE and fully discrete schemes, and (c) transfers decay through to
the limit. Our measure–time calculus does all three: Thm. 7.8 (continuum), Thm. 8.28 (discrete,
same cσ), and Thm. 8.10/8.19 (Γ-bridge). This directly serves the analysis–computation interface
in dynamics and differential equations: constants do not drift under discretization, the Γ bridge
keeps the envelope in the limit, and the stochastic extension adds an expectation/pathwise pillar
driven by the compensator. In sum, this is a single-constant four-frameworks program rather than
a one-off result.
Operator–presentation invariance (PiNA).
We use a Operator presentation via Green/trace
pairings: weak differential operators are specified by their Green/trace pairings, so changes of
coordinates or unitary reparametrisations do not alter the bilinear forms that calibrate the HUM
constant and the σ–ledger. Consequently, all envelope statements here are presentation-invariant
(same cσ); see Appendix A (“Pi-Native Analysis”) for the definitions and the formal invariance
theorem.

Scope and limitations of the present Γ–limit
We emphasize that the present work establishes a light Γ–limit for the energy ledgers and the
associated dissipation envelopes, and deliberately does not claim convergence of the fully discrete
flows in the sense of Evolution Variational Inequalities (EVI) or curves of maximal slope. Concretely,
our Γ–limit asserts equi-coercivity, a Γ–liminf inequality, the existence of recovery sequences, and
the persistence of the structural constant cσ in the canonical envelope. This yields stability of energy
levels and dissipation inequalities in the limit. In contrast, EVI convergence requires, in addition,
metrized control of the variational derivatives (metric slopes), chain rules along limiting trajectories,
and convergence of time-interpolants that encode the evolution law itself. These ingredients are
beyond the scope of the present paper.
The choice to pursue the light Γ–limit is motivated by the aims of the manuscript: (i) to
identify cσ as a structural, discretization-independent constant controlling continuum, discrete,
and limiting dissipation, and (ii) to deliver a robust envelope principle that is directly actionable
for numerical design and parameter calibration. These goals are achieved under minimal hypotheses
without imposing the stronger convexity and interpolation structure typically required for EVI. For
a quarantined EVI upgrade under additional structure, see Subsection 2.

Conclusion and outlook
What we established.
We developed a measure–time calculus in which decay is governed by
a single structural constant cσ that is stable under three operations that typically break quan-
titative bounds: moving from a continuum model to fully discrete schemes, passing to the limit
via a compact variational bridge, and working with mixed time geometries (absolutely continuous
segments, atoms, and flats). (Made explicit in Corollary 2.19; continuum master decay in Theo-
rem 7.8; discrete uniform estimate in Theorem 8.28; discrete premise Lemma D.11). The discrete
σ–Grönwall premise together with the SAT sign/scale is the minimal mechanism that keeps the
estimate uniform, and the Γ-bridge transfers it without upgrading to a full EVI theory. Concretely,
for κ > 0 and calibrated cσ, E(t) ≤E(0) exp(−2κcσσ(t)) with the same cσ across levels; see § 10.
A GCC exemplar makes the constants explicit on a benchmark hyperbolic problem.
How this differs from the usual templates.
Semigroup spectral gaps, hypocoercivity, observ-
ability/Carleman, and discrete contractivity arguments each capture a slice of the picture, but none
delivers a single, partition-uniform envelope constant that survives discretization and limits while
simultaneously allowing atoms and flats. The present calculus isolates exactly those ingredients
that make such persistence possible and packages them in a way that can be reused across analyses
and codes.
Assumptions and boundaries.
Results rely on (H1)–(H4): local coercivity/observability, dissi-
pativity on the a.c. part, and non-expansive atomic updates; the Γ transfer is compact and does not
assert gradient-flow/EVI stability; and the stochastic extension is treated under Markov-modulated
damping. These choices keep the machinery light and portable, but leave room for sharper structure
(e.g., optimal constants, rough data, or geometric low-regularity).
Why σ is necessary.
There is no schedule-uniform exponential estimate in physical time at fixed
total impulse mass; the σ-clock measures the dissipation budget (see Theorems 2.22 and 2.24).
