Abstract
Pearl’s do-calculus is a complete axiom system for interventional identification in acyclic
causal models under classical (Boolean) logic. We generalize it to j-stable causal inference inside
a topos of sheaves ShJ(C), where regimes form a site (C, J) and observations/interventions
are sheaves on that site. Our framework is an elaboration of the recently proposed framework
of Topos Causal Models (TCM) Mahadevan [2025a], where causal interventions are defined
as subobjects. We generalize the original setting of TCM using the Lawvere-Tierney topology
on a topos, defined by a modal operator j on the subobject classifier Ω. We introduce
j-do-calculus, where we replace global truth with local truth (Kripke–Joyal semantics) and
formalize causal interventions as structure-preserving morphisms that are stable along J-covers.
j-do-calculus is a sound rule system whose premises and conclusions are formulas of the
internal (intuitionistic) logic of ShJ(C). We define j-stability for conditional independences
and interventional claims as local truth in the internal logic of ShJ(C). We give three
inference rules that strictly generalize Pearl’s insertion/deletion and action/observation
exchange, and we prove soundness in the Kripke–Joyal semantics. We show how these
rules specialize back to classical do-calculus when J is the trivial topology (Boolean case)
and to regime-aware identification when J encodes experimental covers.
This paper develops the theory of j-stable causal inference and a j-do-calculus inside ShJ(C).
Our focus is conceptual: we assume access to the theoretical objects (e.g., stages U, J-covers
of U, and the internal interventional distribution Pint) and study their logical consequences.
A companion paper in preparation provides the algorithmic side – how to estimate the
required entities from data and how to instantiate j-do with standard discovery procedures
(e.g., score-based and constraint-based methods) – which describes experimental results
on how to (i) form data-driven J-covers (via regime/section constructions), (ii) compute
chartwise CIs after graph surgeries, and (iii) glue them to certify the premises of the j-do
rules in practice

Introduction
In this paper, we build on the recently proposed Topos Causal Models (TCMs) [Mahadevan, 2025a] that
formulates causal inference in the category of toposes. TCMs are part of a recent series of papers on
categorical models of causality, including those using symmetric monoidal categories [Fong, 2012, Fritz and
Klingler, 2023, Cho and Jacobs, 2019, Jacobs et al., 2018], as well as simplicial sets and higher-order categories
[Mahadevan, 2023]. Any causal model based on graphs [Pearl, 2009, Forré and Mooij, 2017, Spirtes et al.,
2000] can be translated into a categorical language. Operations on causal models, such as interventions,
can be modeled as functors on the objects of the associated symmetric monoidal category or simplicial set.
Categorical approaches to causality also extend to the potential outcomes counterfactual framework [Imbens
and Rubin, 2015].
TCM is also part of a line of previous research of ours [Mahadevan, 2023, 2025b] termed universal causality
(UC). This notion derives from the concept defined in category theory [Riehl, 2017]: a property is universal if
it can be defined in terms of an initial or final object in a category of diagrams, or in terms of a representable
functor using the Yoneda Lemma. For example, a structural causal model (SCM) [Pearl, 2009] was originally
defined as a unique (deterministic) function mapping a collection of exogenous variables into a collection of
endogenous variables, by “collating" local functions that serve as independent causal mechanisms [Galles
and Pearl, 1988, Parascandolo et al., 2017]. In UC, an SCM is further decomposed into diagrams that reveal its
universal properties, such as categorical product, coproduct, limits and colimits, equalizers and coequalizers
etc. These latter properties can be shown formally to be initial or final objects in a category of diagrams
[Riehl, 2017], or as representable functors through the Yoneda Lemma [MacLane, 1971].
A site (C, J) is a small category C whose objects index regimes (e.g., labs, contexts) and a Grothendieck
topology J that specifies which families {ui →u} “cover” a stage u. The topos ShJ(C) of J-sheaves
behaves like a universe of sets varying over regimes. A formula φ is J-stable at u iff it holds on a cover of
u (Kripke–Joyal semantics). Intuitively: truth is verified locally and then glued. Random variables and
probabilities live internally (as objects/morphisms of ShJ(C)). We write P(Y | Do(X), Z) for an internal
conditional, and X ⊥⊥Y | Z for internal conditional independence. When J is trivial, these reduce to the
classical notions.
Pearl’s do-calculus [Pearl, 2009] is a complete axiom system for interventional identification in acyclic causal
models under classical (Boolean) logic. We generalize it to j-stable causal inference inside a topos of sheaves
ShJ(C), where regimes form a site (C, J) and observations/interventions are sheaves on that site. Our main
idea is to replace global truth with local truth (Kripke–Joyal semantics) and to formalize interventions as
structure-preserving morphisms that are stable along J-covers. This yields a sound rule system (J–do-calculus)
whose premises and conclusions are formulas of the internal (intuitionistic) logic of ShJ(C).
We use a number of informal terms like stages/contexts and regimes/charts. Formally, in a site (C, J) a chart over
an ambient object U is a morphism f : V→U; a J-cover of U is a family {fi : Vi →U} whose generated sieve
lies in J(U). When we say that a statement holds “on a J-cover of U,” we mean it holds chartwise for some
covering sieve of U. We adopt this light touch to keep the exposition readable. We plan to revise this paper
into a more rigorous categorical presentation in future work.
Contributions.
(i) We define j-stability for conditional independences and interventional claims as local truth
in the internal logic of ShJ(C). (ii) We give three inference rules (J1–J3) that mirror Pearl’s insertion/deletion
and action/observation exchange, and we characterize their soundness using the Kripke–Joyal semantics.
(iii) We show how these rules specialize back to classical do-calculus when J is the trivial topology (Boolean
case) and to regime-aware identification when J encodes experimental covers. (iv) We provide a range
of running examples to concretize the abstractions, and a translation table mapping standard graphical
d-separation statements to J-stable internal formulas. Table 1 summarizes the paper.
Scope and companion work.
This paper develops the theory of j-stable causal inference and a j-do-calculus
inside ShJ(C). Our focus is conceptual: we assume access to the theoretical objects (e.g., stages U, J-covers of
U, and the internal interventional distribution Pint) and study their logical consequences. A companion paper
provides the algorithmic side: how to estimate the required entities from data and how to instantiate j-do
with standard discovery procedures (e.g., score-based and constraint-based methods), building on recent
surveys [Zanga and Stella, 2023]. There we show how to (i) form data-driven J-covers (via regime/section
constructions), (ii) compute chartwise CIs after graph surgeries, and (iii) glue them to certify the premises of
the j-do rules in practice.

From Classical Do-Calculus to j-Do-Calculus
In this paper, we describe how to generalize classical do-calculus to j-stable causal inference inside a topos
of sheaves ShJ(C), where regimes form a site (C, J) and observations/interventions are sheaves on that
site. Before going through some fairly elaborate technical preliminaries, we want to give the reader a quick
“glimpse of the new terrain" that we will be touring through. There will be much terminology here that is
not yet defined, but that will be rectified in the following sections. To reiterate, the proposed framework
an elaboration of the recently proposed framework of Topos Causal Models (TCM) Mahadevan [2025a],
where causal interventions are defined as subobjects. We generalize the original setting of TCM using
the Lawvere-Tierney topology on a topos, defined by a modal operator j on the subobject classifier Ω.
We introduce j-do-calculus, where we replace global truth with local truth (Kripke–Joyal semantics) and
formalize causal interventions as structure-preserving morphisms that are stable along J-covers. j-do-calculus
is a sound rule system whose premises and conclusions are formulas of the internal (intuitionistic) logic
of ShJ(C). We define j-stability for conditional independences and interventional claims as local truth in
the internal logic of ShJ(C). We give three inference rules that strictly generalize Pearl’s insertion/deletion
and action/observation exchange, and we prove soundness in the Kripke–Joyal semantics. We show how
these rules specialize back to classical do-calculus when J is the trivial topology (Boolean case) and to
regime-aware identification when J encodes experimental covers. Table 2 is a glossary of the terms that will

2.1
Classical Do-Calculus
We briefly review the notion of a structural causal model (SCM) [Pearl, 2009], and the classical notion of
do-calculus. Succinctly, any SCM M defines a unique function from exogenous variables to endogenous
variables, and do-calculus models interventions as “sub-functions":
Definition 1. [Pearl, 2009] A structural causal model (SCM) is defined as the triple ⟨U, V, F⟩where V = {V1, . . . , Vn}
is a set of endogenous variables, U is a set of exogenous variables, F is a set {f1, . . . , fn} of “local functions"
fi : U ∪(V \ Vi) →Vi whose composition induces a unique function F from U to V.
Definition 2. [Pearl, 2009] Let M = ⟨U, V, F⟩be a causal model defined as an SCM, and X be a subset of variables
in V, and x be a particular realization of X. A submodel Mx = ⟨U, V, Fx⟩of M is the causal model Mx = ⟨U, V, Fx⟩,

Definition 3. Pearl [2009] Let M be an SCM, X be a set of variables in V, and x be a particular realization of X. The
effect of an action do(X = x) on M is given by the submodel Mx.

2.2
j-Do-Calculus: A Birds-Eye View
In moving from classical do-calculus to j-do-calculus, we transition from causal models over graphs to
general categories, specifically toposes. The simplest way to understand this transition is to note that a
category C whose objects are functions f : A →B over sets, and whose arrows are commutative diagrams
between functions f and g, defined as C( f, g) defines a topos [Goldblatt, 2006]. This result, which was shown
in detail for the case of SCMs in [Mahadevan, 2025a], shows that causal inference in SCMs and graphs
is intrinsically topos-theoretic. One can expand this simple result to cover more cases. For example, the
category of graphs G can be defined to consist of two objects v and e, and two non-identity arrows from v
to e. Each graph then is defined as a presheaf SetsGop, a functor that maps the objects v and e to the set of
edges E and vertices V of the actual graph, and that maps the two non-identity arrows between v and e to
the initial and terminal vertex of each edge. More generally, any (small) category C can be converted into
a topos through the Yoneda embedding C →SetsCop, defined as c 7→C(−, c), and called the presheaf. The
category of presheafs forms a topos [MacLane and leke Moerdijk, 1994]. We will also develop a new set of
rules of j-do-calculus, described in Figure 1, which will be explained at length in the remainder of the paper.

Causal Models Over a Topos of Sheaves
The categorical framework underlying Topos Causal Models (TCMs) introduced in [Mahadevan, 2025a]
defines causal inference in a topos by using the property that Yoneda embeddings of presheaves forms a
topos [MacLane and leke Moerdijk, 1994]. To ensure consistent extension into a unique global function,
we build on the theory of sheaves [Mac Lane and Moerdijk, 1992], which ensures local functions can be
“collated" together to yield a unique global function. In our setting, we will construct sheaves from categories
over causal models through the Yoneda embedding よ(x) : C →SetsCop and impose a Grothendieck topology.
TCMs were originally defined over Grothendieck topologies on categories (known as sites), and we will
generalize that formulation here to Lawvere-Tierney topologies.

Grothendieck Topology on Sites
Definition 6. A sieve for any object x in any (small) category C is a subobject of its Yoneda embedding よ(x) = C(−, x).
If S is a sieve on x, and h : y →x is any arrow in category C, then
h∗(S) = {g | cod(g) = y, hg ∈S}
Definition 7. [Mac Lane and Moerdijk, 1992] A Grothendieck topology on a category C is a function J which
assigns to each object x of C a collection J(x) of sieves on x such that
1. the maximum sieve tx = {f|cod(f) = x} is in J(x).
2. If S ∈J(x) then h∗(S) ∈J(y) for any arrow h : y →x.
3. If S ∈J(x) and R is any sieve on x, such that h∗(R) ∈J(y) for all h : y →x, then R ∈J(C).
A J-cover is a covering family whose generated sieve lies in J(U).
Lemma 1 (Families vs. sieves). A family {fi : Vi →U} is J-covering iff its generated sieve ⟨fi⟩lies in J(U). Moreover,

3.2
Lawvere-Tierney Topologies on a Topos
In the original paper defining TCM [Mahadevan, 2025a], the category of sheaves defining CT CM (e.g., sheaves
over a Markov category) was given a Grothendieck topology. A more elegant framework is to use the
Lawvere-Tierney topology on the subobject classifier Ω[MacLane and leke Moerdijk, 1994].
Definition 10 (Lawvere–Tierney causal topology). Let E be an elementary topos with subobject classifier Ωand
distribution monad DistE. A causal topology on E is a Lawvere–Tierney topology j : Ω→Ωsatisfying:
j(⊤) = ⊤,
j(p ∧q) = j(p) ∧j(q),
j(j(p)) = j(p),
where ⊤= true and such that for every stochastic morphism k : A →DistE(B) in the topos causal model, the
comprehension subobject {x | P(x)} ,→A is j-closed whenever P is stable under intervention, i.e. j(χP) = χP.
The original Grothendieck topology formulation in [Mahadevan, 2025a] is a special case of this more general
formulation, which we expand on in greater depth in the remainder of the paper. In particular, we have the
following result shown in [MacLane and leke Moerdijk, 1994].
Theorem 1. [MacLane and leke Moerdijk, 1994] If C is a small category, the Grothendieck topologies J on C correspond
exactly to Lawvere- Tierney topologies on the presheaf topos SetsCop.

Stages and generalized elements.

Charts (“regimes”) and J-covers.

φ is (internally) true at the ambient object U in ShJ(C). In Kripke–Joyal semantics this is equivalent to

⇐⇒
∃S ∈J(U) with ∀f : V →U in S,
V ⊩J φ| f.
Informally: φ holds chartwise on a J-cover of U.

{fi} is a J-cover of U

We review the concept of internal logic that is intrinisc to every topos, including TCM’s, and this logical

6.1

Kripke–Joyal Forcing and Internal Semantics in a TCM
Before explaining how to prove the validity of do-calculus statements in a TCM, we need to provide more
detail on the Kripke-Joyal intuitionistic semantics that constitutes the “semantic engine" in a topos.
Motivation.
In a classical set-based semantics, a statement such as “∀x ∈X, φ(x)” is true if φ(x) holds for
each element x ∈X. In a topos, however, elements are generalized—they are morphisms α : N →X from
a test object N. The Kripke–Joyal forcing relation provides the inductive definition of what it means for a
formula φ to hold at a stage N and under a generalized element α : N →Γ. This endows the internal logic of
a topos with semantics analogous to intuitionistic Kripke models.
Definition (Forcing relation).
For an elementary topos E, the forcing relation
N ⊩E φ[α]
reads “φ holds at stage N under assignment α : N →Γ.” The semantics is defined inductively:
N ⊩(x = y)[α]
iff α∗x = α∗y in E,
N ⊩(φ ∧ψ)[α]
iff N ⊩φ[α] and N ⊩ψ[α],
N ⊩(φ ⇒ψ)[α]
iff for all u : N′ →N, N′ ⊩φ[α◦u] ⇒N′ ⊩ψ[α◦u],
N ⊩(∃x : A) φ(x)[α]
iff there exists an epi e : M →N and a : M →A s.t. M ⊩φ[a, α◦e],
N ⊩(∀x : A) φ(x)[α]
iff for all u : N′ →N, and all a : N′ →A,
N′ ⊩φ[a, α◦u].
Truth is thus monotone: if N ⊩φ[α] and u : N′ →N, then N′ ⊩φ[α◦u]. This matches intuitionistic semantics
where information grows along morphisms.
Interpretation in TCMs.
In a Topos Causal Model, contexts Γ denote joint variable spaces, and a stage
α : N →Γ represents a local assignment or partial observation. A judgment
Γ ⊢φ
is true in E if N ⊩E φ[α] for all α : N →Γ. For instance, the sequent
Γ ⊢P(Y | do(Z), X) = P(Y | X)

and P(Y | X) ◦α coincide in Kl(DistE). Hence equality of arrows is verified stagewise.

Practical upshot.
In j–stable learning, G–invariance lets us (i) aggregate evidence across permuted charts
(variance reduction), (ii) constrain estimators to depend only on orbit features (e.g., treated fraction), and (iii)
define interventions that commute with relabeling. This is especially natural for panel/cluster data and for
regime–wise exchangeability.
Connections to do–Finetti.
The exchangeable j–stable framework recovers the core invariance statements
of do–Finetti [Guo et al., 2023] in the classical setting when j is the trivial topology and C is a one–object site.
Our formulation clarifies how such principles persist when (a) interventions are represented as morphisms
on a site, and (b) causal judgments are taken j–locally via covers.
Related Work.
Closest to our setting is the recent “do–Finetti” line of work [Guo et al., 2023], which
investigates causal identifiability and transport under exchangeability in the classical (Boolean) semantics. Our
j–stable formulation places exchangeability internally to a presheaf topos via a G–invariant Lawvere–Tierney
topology, so that symmetry is preserved chartwise and commutes with interventions. This connects to
de Finetti–type representations and their array analogues (Aldous–Hoover) by interpreting conditional
i.i.d. structure as j–local. It is complementary to invariance-based causal methods such as Invariant Causal
Prediction (ICP) and pooled/transport rules, which correspond to particular choices of covers but do
not supply an internal logic for interventions on orbits. Our treatment also aligns with recent interest
in symmetry/equivariance in causal discovery and learning, but makes the group action explicit at the
site/topology level, yielding orbit-wise effect functionals F(|S|, x) in the fully exchangeable case.

Summary
In this paper, we introduced the concept of j-do-calculus, which extends classical do-calculus to the
recently proposed Topos Causal Model (TCM) [Mahadevan, 2025a]. Pearl’s do-calculus is a complete
axiom system for interventional identification in acyclic causal models under classical (Boolean) logic. We
generalize it to j-stable causal inference inside a topos of sheaves ShJ(C), where regimes form a site (C, J) and
observations/interventions are sheaves on that site. Our framework is an elaboration of the recently proposed
framework of Topos Causal Models (TCM) Mahadevan [2025a], where causal interventions are defined as
subobjects. We generalize the original setting of TCM using the Lawvere-Tierney topology on a topos, defined
by a modal operator j on the subobject classifier Ω. We introduce j-do-calculus, where we replace global truth
with local truth (Kripke–Joyal semantics) and formalize causal interventions as structure-preserving morphisms
that are stable along J-covers. j-do-calculus is a sound rule system whose premises and conclusions are
formulas of the internal (intuitionistic) logic of ShJ(C). We define j-stability for conditional independences
and interventional claims as local truth in the internal logic of ShJ(C). We give three inference rules that
strictly generalize Pearl’s insertion/deletion and action/observation exchange, and we prove soundness in
the Kripke–Joyal semantics. We show how these rules specialize back to classical do-calculus when J is the
trivial topology (Boolean case) and to regime-aware identification when J encodes experimental covers.
We provide several running examples and a translation table mapping standard graphical d-separation
statements to J-stable internal formulas.
