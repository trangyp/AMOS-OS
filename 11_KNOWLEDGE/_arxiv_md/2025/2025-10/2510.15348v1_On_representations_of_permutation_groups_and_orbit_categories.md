Abstract. Given an infinite set Ωand a ring R as well as a group G acting on them, we show
that G and a subgroup H share the same canonical relational structure on Ωif and only if the
restriction functor gives an equivalence from the category of discrete representations of G to that
of H. Moreover, the age of this relational structure satisfies the strong amalgamation property if
and only if there is a canonical isomorphism from the category of finite substructures of Ωand
embeddings to the opposite category of the orbit category of G. As an application, we prove that
finitely generated discrete representations of highly homogeneous groups over the polynomial ring
k[Ω] are Noetherian.
1. Introduction
The study of infinite permutation groups occupies a central position at the intersection of group
theory, combinatorics, and model theory. They are not only closely tied to combinatorial notions
such as homogeneity, oligomorphy, and Ramsey-type properties, but also play a prominent role in
model theory, where the automorphism group of a countable structure encodes substantial infor-
mation about the definability and classification of the structure. For example, the correspondence
between oligomorphic groups and ω-categorical structures, developed by Engeler, Ryll-Nardzewski,
and Svenonius, has been a driving force in connecting infinite group actions to logical classification
problems [10, 27].
In more recent years, representation theory of infinite permutation groups, in particular their
actions on commutative rings emerged as a new complementary area. Applying techniques from
commutative algebra, invariant theory, and algebraic geometry, people have obtained significant
progress along this approach. One of the key achievements of this perspective is the development
of equivariant Noetherianity. Explicitly, let G be a permutation group on an infinite set Ω, and k a
commutative Noetherian ring. While the polynomial ring k[Ω] whose indeterminates are elements
in Ωis far from Noetherian in the classical sense, it often exhibits Noetherian behavior once the
symmetries of an infinite permutation group are taken into account. For instance, Cohen [6] proved
that the poset of G-stable ideals of R satisfies the ascending chain condition; Aschenbrenner and
Hillar [1] showed that the ideal of relations among minors of an infinite generic matrix is finitely
generated up to the natural action of the infinite general linear group. Subsequent works have shown
that a broad class of rings and varieties have this property, which has had fruitful applications in
algebra, geometry, and combinatorics such as conceptual explanation for the stabilization of syzygies
in large algebraic structures, asymptotic behavior in algebraic statistics and tensor problems, etc.
Moreover, this topic has deeply been intertwined with other areas such as representation theory
of combinatorial categories. For a few references, see the works of Draisma [8, 9], Hillar-Sullivant
[13], Laudone-Snowden [18], Van Le-Nagel-Nguyen-R¨omer [19], Li-Peng-Yuan [20], Nagel-R¨omer
[23], Nagpal-Snowden [25], Ramos [26], Sam-Snowden [28, 29], and many more.
In [20] the author and his collaborators proved that k[Ω] satisfies the G-equivariant Noetherianity
if the action of G on Ωis highly homogeneous.
Moreover, for two special cases, the category
of discrete representations of G over k[Ω] is equivalent to that of ˜G over k[Ω], where ˜G is the
automorphism group of the canonical relational structure induced by the action of G. For details,

Let H be another group acting on Ω. We say that G and H share the same orbits on Ωif for
each x ∈Ω, one has G · x = H · x, where G · x is the G-orbit containing x. The following fact is

Lemma 2.2. Suppose that G and H act on Ω, and fix n ⩾1. Then the following are equivalent:
(1) G and H share the same orbits on Ωn;
(2) G and H share the same orbits on Ω(n);
(3) G and H share the same orbits on Ωs for every s ⩽n;
(4) G and H share the same orbits on Ω(s) for every s ⩽n.
Proof. (1) ⇒(2). This is trivial since Ω(n) is a G-subset of Ωn.
(2) ⇒(4). Fix s ⩽n, and let
x = (x1, . . . , xs),
y = (y1, . . . , ys)
be two elements in Ω(s). Since Ωis infinite, we can choose an (n −s)-tuple z whose entries are
distinct and disjoint from {x1, . . . , xs}. Clearly, (x, z) ∈Ω(n).
If x and y lie in the same G-orbit in Ω(s), we can pick g ∈G with g · x = y, and set w = g · z.
Then (x, z) and (y, w) lie in the same G-orbit inside Ω(n). By (2) they therefore lie in the same
H-orbit, so some h ∈H sends (x, z) to (y, w); in particular h · x = y. Thus x and y lie in the
same H-orbit. The conclusion of (4) then follows from symmetry.
(4) ⇒(3). Fix a certain s ⩽n and take an arbitrary
x = (x1, . . . , xs) ∈Ωs.
We need to show G · x = H · x; that is, for any g ∈G, one can find a certain h ∈H such that
g · x = h · x.
Let {xi1, . . . , xiℓ} be the set of distinct values occurring in x (listed in any order) and put
z = (xi1, . . . , xiℓ) ∈Ω(ℓ).
By hypothesis (4), G and H share the same orbits on Ω(ℓ), so G · z = H · z. In particular, one can
find a certain h ∈H such that g · z = h · z. But this happens if and only if g · x = h · x.
(3) ⇒(1). This is trivial.

Recall that the action of G on Ωinduces the canonical relational structure M = (Ω, (Ri)i∈I).
Explicitly, each Ri is an equivalence relation in Ωni such that two tuples x and y in Ωni are
contained in Rni if and only if they lie in the same G-orbit. This is a homogeneous structure,
meaning that embeddings between finite substructures of M extend to elements in G.2 Clearly,
two groups G and H acting on Ωinduce the same canonical relational structure if and only if they
share the same orbits on Ωn for all n ∈N.
We give two immediate consequences of the above lemma.
Corollary 2.4. Suppose that G and H act on Ω. Then the following are equivalent:
(1) G and H induce the same relational structure on Ω;
(2) for any N ∈N, there exists n ⩾N such that G and H share the same orbits on Ωn;
(3) for any N ∈N, there exists n ⩾N such that G and H share the same orbits on Ω(n).
In some situations we can use the growth rates to determine whether two groups induce the same
relational structure.
Corollary 2.5. Suppose that the action of G on Ωis oligomorphic, and let H be a subgroup of G.
Then the following are equivalent:
(1) G and H induce the same relational structure on Ω;
(2) for any N ∈N, there exists n ⩾N such that FG,Ω(n) = FH,Ω(n);
(3) for any N ∈N, there exists n ⩾N such that F ∗
G,Ω(n) = F ∗
H,Ω(n).
Proof. Clearly, (1) implies both (2) and (3). We show that (2) implies (1), and a similar argument
gives the implication (3) ⇒(1).
Given s ∈N, one can find a certain n ⩾s such that FG,Ω(n) = FH,Ω(n). Since the action of
G on Ωis oligomorphic, FG,Ω(n) is finite. Since each G-orbit is a disjoint union of H-orbits, the
equality forces that G and H share the same orbits on Ω(n), and hence the same orbits on Ω(s).
Since s is arbitrary, we conclude that G and H induce the same relational structure on Ω.

We have proved that V is finitely generated if and only if its restriction to D is finitely generated.
Thus it is Noetherian if and only if so is its restriction to D. The conclusion for PSh(C, Jat, R) then
follows from this observation and [23, Theorem 6.15].
Now we turn to Sh(C, Jat, R).
Let W be a subobject of a finitely generated object V in
Sh(C, Jat, R). We need to show that W is finitely generated in Sh(C, Jat, R). Note that we have a
pair of adjoint functors
inc : Sh(C, Jat, R) −→PSh(C, Jat, R),
♯: PSh(C, Jat, R) −→Sh(C, Jat, R)
where inc is the inclusion functor and ♯is the sheafification functor.
Via inc, we can view W
as a subpresheaf of the presheaf V . Furthermore, we can assume that V is finitely generated in
PSh(C, Jat, R). Indeed, since V is a finitely generated sheaf, we have an epimorphism
φ :

n −→V
in Sh(C, Jat, R) such that the sum of multiplicities cn is finite.
Let V ′ be the image of φ in

Then V ′ and V represent the same object in Sh(C, Jat, R) since Sh(C, Jat, R)
is an exact reflective localization of PSh(C, Jat, R). Thus if necessary we can replace V by V ′,

in PSh(C, Jat, R), so W is finitely generated in PSh(C, Jat, R), and hence finitely generated in
Sh(C, Jat, R) by the following well known fact: if a morphism between two sheaves is an epimorphism
in the presheaf category, then it is also an epimorphism in the sheaf category.
