ABSTRACT
Transaction costs and regime shifts are the main reasons why paper portfolios fail in live trading.
We develop FR–LUX (Friction–aware, Regime–conditioned Learning under eXecution costs), a
reinforcement–learning framework that learns after–cost trading policies and remains robust across
volatility–liquidity regimes. FR–LUX integrates three ingredients: (i) a microstructure–consistent
execution model combining proportional and impact costs, directly embedded in the reward; (ii) a
trade–space trust region that constrains changes in inventory flow rather than only logits, yielding
stable, low–turnover updates; and (iii) explicit regime conditioning so the policy specializes to
LL/LH/HL/HH states without fragmenting the data. On a 4 × 5 grid of regimes and cost levels
(0–50 bps) with three seeds per cell, FR–LUX achieves the top average Sharpe across all 20 scenarios
with narrow bootstrap confidence intervals, maintains a flatter cost–performance slope than strong
baselines (vanilla PPO, mean–variance with/without caps, risk–parity), and attains superior risk–return
efficiency for a given turnover budget. Pairwise scenario–level improvements are strictly positive and
remain statistically significant after Romano–Wolf stepdown and HAC–aware Sharpe comparisons.
We provide formal guarantees: existence of an optimal stationary policy under convex frictions; a
monotonic improvement lower bound under a KL trust region with explicit remainder terms; an
upper bound on long–run turnover and an induced inaction band due to proportional costs; a strictly
positive value advantage for regime–conditioned policies when cross–regime actions are separated;
and robustness of realized value to cost misspecification. The methodology is implementable—costs
are calibrated from standard liquidity proxies, scenario–level inference avoids pseudo–replication,
and all figures and tables are reproducible from our artifacts.
Keywords transaction costs; market microstructure; regime switching; reinforcement learning; portfolio optimization;
CVaR / maximum drawdown; turnover; Sharpe ratio; multiple testing; implementability.

Introduction
Problem statement and motivation. Hedging equity exposures with volatility instruments (e.g., SPX options, VIX
futures) is most fragile in precisely the regimes that matter: near option expiry and under strong spot–volatility coupling,
where leverage effects and order-flow feedback magnify errors and costs. In these windows the hedge ratio becomes stiff
and discontinuous, gamma/theta explode, and the PnL is dominated by a small number of adverse moves and liquidity
gaps. Empirically, such regimes concentrate on rebalancing times that coincide with index events, macro releases,
and end-of-day queues; the impact of even small participation can be highly nonlinear when order books are thin
and resiliency is slow. The resulting failures are well documented across microstructure and execution studies—from
stylized impact models and resilience dynamics [51, 65, 57] and empirical tapes [43, 12, 37] to volatility-surface
construction [28, 25, 33, 32], variance-index design [21, 24], and path-dependent volatility dynamics [41, 6]. In
practice, purely black-box hedgers can overfit benign regimes and chase noisy cross-asset signals, while classical

Why now? Recent technical and empirical context. Volatility surfaces and indices have seen renewed emphasis on
arbitrage-free learning and dynamic coherence. On the modeling side, arbitrage-free SSVI calibration and interpolation
are mature [33], with robust multi-slice procedures and hybrids that combine learned priors with no-arbitrage projectors
[56, 64]. Empirically, implied volatility and volatility indices exhibit strong path dependence on spot trajectories and
realized-of-realized structures [41, 6], raising the bar for hedgers that over-rely on static proxies. On the control side,
CBFs have emerged as a principled way to encode safety as constraints in optimization-based controllers [4, 3, 39, 20],
while CVaR offers a coherent tail-risk objective with tractable surrogates [60, 61]. Execution with transient impact
continues to evolve, including signal-adaptive liquidations [55] and multi-agent generalizations [16]. Our framework
unifies these strands into an auditable hedging stack faithful to exchange rules and frictions.
Methodological contributions.
1. Quant-consistent teacher. We integrate an SSVI shell [33, 32] and a Cboe-compliant VIX module [21, 24] to
supervise an interpretable implied-volatility closure. Dupire’s extractor [28] then yields dynamics consistent
with quotes and index construction. The shell explicitly mirrors no-arbitrage surface constraints and exchange
wing-pruning, producing targets that are both fast to evaluate and faithful to practice.
2. Data-driven VIX sensitivity map. A bump-and-invert procedure learns a time-varying κ(Trem) mapping
30D VIX shocks to option-price deltas, with smoothing across maturities (cf. path-dependent co-movements
[41, 6]). This provides a transparent cross-asset linkage between index variation and option exposures.
3. Tail-safe CBF-QP controller. We pose hedging as a small QP with CBF-style safety boxes for inventory,
rate, and CVaR [3, 60]. Three upgrades—dynamic VIX weighting, guarded no-trade bands, and expiry-aware
micro-thresholds—suppress VIX chasing while preserving tracking under impact [65, 57]. The controller
exposes KKT multipliers and active constraints for audit.
4. White-box diagnostics. Constraint-activation, gate decisions, and risk-vs-cost ledgers make failure modes and
safety activations auditable, aligning with model-risk governance demands in high-impact settings [43, 12, 37].
Theoretical contributions (preview). Beyond engineering, the paper includes formal guarantees that raise interpretabil-
ity and credibility:
• Well-posedness of hedging QP (Theorem 1). Under convex impact costs and polyhedral CBF-style safety
sets, the per-step QP admits a unique solution and is Lipschitz in state variables. This follows from strong
convexity and Slater-type feasibility inherited from a strictly feasible no-trade ellipse.
• Safety invariance under stochastic disturbances (Theorem 2). With a discrete-time stochastic CBF adapted
to price dynamics and a CVaR-consistent risk envelope, the post-trade state remains in a forward-invariant safe
set with high probability, quantified via supermartingale concentration [20, 61].
• Gated trading efficiency (Proposition 1). A gate that executes only when quadratic risk drop exceeds a
scaled execution cost yields (i) finite trade counts and (ii) a uniform bound on small-amplitude churn near
expiry, assuming transient-impact regularity [57].
• Dynamic VIX weighting optimality (Proposition 2). For a class of linear-Gaussian spot–VIX co-movements,
the correlation/expiry-aware VIX weight minimizes a tight upper bound on ex ante CVaRα subject to execution
frictions, connecting safety heuristics to risk-sensitive control.
Positioning and novelty. Our work complements arbitrage-free learning of volatility surfaces [33, 56, 64] and path-
dependent volatility modeling [41, 6] by focusing on control: we translate index/surface coherence into actionable,
auditable hedging policies that explicitly trade off risk reduction and cost. Relative to deep hedging pipelines, we
restrict function approximation to an interpretable teacher and leave safety to constraints, not opaque penalties; relative
to classic quadratic trackers, we import modern safety and risk tools (CBF, CVaR) and microstructure fidelity. The
result is a controller that (i) respects exchange rules by design, (ii) explains its interventions via dual certificates, and
(iii) prioritizes tail protection when it matters most.
Scope of this preprint. This arXiv version emphasizes method, theory, and a fully reproducible synthetic environment
that mirrors exchange rules (wing pruning, 30D interpolation) and realistic impact. We intentionally do not present
real-data live backtests; instead, we provide: (i) proofs of Theorems 1–2 and Propositions 1–2; (ii) ablations of
safety components; (iii) stress tests across spot–vol correlation and variance-of-variance; and (iv) artifacts for exact
reproducibility.
Roadmap. Section 3 builds the arbitrage-free teacher (SSVI →VIX →Dupire). Section 6 defines targets, the CBF-QP,
and the three tail-safety upgrades, and proves Theorems 1–2. Section 7 presents synthetic experiments, diagnostics, and
ablations. Section 8 discusses connections to execution, volatility modeling, and safe control.

Related Work
Market microstructure and execution with impact.
Foundational models of information and liquidity provi-
sion—Kyle’s insider market [51], Almgren–Chriss temporary impact [65], and transient supply/demand dynamics
[57]—establish the core cost–risk calculus for execution. Empirical and theoretical syntheses (e.g., Hasbrouck’s
monograph [43] and Bouchaud et al. [12]) document stylized facts, order-book resilience, and impact concavity/decay.
Surveys of limit-order books [37] and Hawkes/order-flow clustering [7] provide a stochastic substrate for modeling
endogenous liquidity and volatility bursts. Our control layer (§6) inherits the classical convex impact cost but augments
it with gate and CBF safety, targeting the regimes—near expiry and strong negative spot–vol correlation—where impact
magnifies hedging errors. Related work on signal-adaptive and transient-impact optimal trading includes [55, 34], and
recent execution reviews [26, 17].
Variance indices, variance swaps, and VIX methodology.
Variance-swap replication and model-free variance date
to [18, 24], connecting integrated variance to a portfolio of OTM options. Cboe’s VIX methodology [21, 22] codifies (i)
OTM aggregation via the Q(K) integrand, (ii) wing pruning using the two-consecutive-zero-bid rule, and (iii) 30-day
constant-maturity interpolation in year-fraction total variance. Empirical uses of model-free implied variance and its
information content are studied in [47]. Our shell reproduces these rules exactly and proves explicit surface–index
coherence bounds that quantify how VIX residuals scale with ATM-shape error and the second-order strike quadrature
error.
Volatility surfaces: construction, no-arbitrage, and dynamics.
Arbitrage-free parameterizations and smile dynam-
ics have been explored from the early implied trees [25] to SSVI and its no-arbitrage conditions [33] and practitioner
guidance [32]. Practical interpolation/extrapolation techniques include Andreasen–Huge’s robust recipes [5] and
Homescu’s survey [46]. For dynamics, Dupire’s local volatility [28] and the Markovian projection principle (Gyöngy)
connect quote surfaces to diffusion coefficients. More recently, path-dependent volatility and term-structure effects have
been clarified in [41, 6], while rough volatility [8] refines short-time behaviors. On the learning side, arbitrage-free or
arbitrage-aware surface generation appears in [56, 64]. Our ASL teacher sits deliberately between closed-form and
black-box: it preserves ATM shape up to second order, stays interpretable, and is anchored by a VIX supervision term
that enforces index-level coherence.
Local-volatility numerics and stability.
Finite-difference accuracy and stability for parabolic operators are standard
[53], and local-vol implementations generally require convexity-preserving interpolation and small-denominator clipping
in ∂KKC. We formalize these folk practices: Theorem 1 bounds the discrete local variance and proves O(∆K2 +∆T 2)
consistency, while Theorem 2 ensures strong convergence of the log–Euler path simulation [35, 49, 45].
Affine variance factors and index proxies.
Affine diffusions for variance (Heston/CIR families) are classical
[44, 27, 30]. We use a CIR-style factor only as an index proxy consistent with variance-swap identities; the mapping to
30D variance is closed-form and Lipschitz (Proposition 1). Theorem 5 quantifies proxy error in terms of (i) projection
error from local volatility to affine variance, (ii) surface–index residuals, and (iii) strike quadrature.
Risk-sensitive control, CVaR, and safe optimization.
CVaR/ES is a coherent tail-risk functional with convenient
convex surrogates [60, 61, 2]; spectral/Kusuoka representations connect it to robust envelopes [50, 63]. In continuous
control, risk-sensitive criteria have a long history (e.g., exponential/quadratic costs), while in modern safe control,
control barrier functions enforce forward invariance via online QPs [3, 4, 20, 38]. Our controller uses CBF-style linear
boxes for inventory/rate/CVaR and a sufficient-descent gate that guarantees one-step risk decrease when trades are
executed (Theorem 12), reconciling risk decline with execution frictions—an aspect under-emphasized in classical
trackers.
Learning-based hedging and robustness.
Deep hedging demonstrates that function approximation can absorb
many modeling frictions [15], but interpretability and distributional robustness remain challenges under regime shifts.
Reinforcement and risk-sensitive variants (e.g., CVaR-aware learning) address tail objectives but often lack white-box
guarantees. Our stance is complementary: restrict learning to an interpretable teacher (anchored by arbitrage and index
rules) and encode safety as convex constraints and gates, yielding auditable policies with explicit invariance and descent
guarantees. This design aligns with model-risk governance and facilitates diagnosis through constraint activations and
gate ledgers (cf. §7.3).
Positioning and novelty.
Relative to arbitrage-free surface construction [33, 5, 56, 64], our contribution is to bridge
surfaces and index construction into a control problem with provable safety and coherence. Relative to classical

Discussion, Limitations, and Societal Impact
What our guarantees do (and do not) cover.
Theorem Groups I–IV provide structural and control guarantees
that are intentionally modular. (i) Group I addresses surface–index coherence and the scaling of VIX residuals with
ATM-shape error and second-order strike quadrature. (ii) Group II establishes positivity/boundedness/consistency for
the discrete Dupire surface and pathwise stability of log–Euler simulation (Theorems 1 and 2), plus an index-proxy
bound for a CIR variance factor (Theorem 5). (iii) Group III turns the κ map into a functional sensitivity with positivity
and near-expiry stability. (iv) Group IV ensures QP feasibility/uniqueness and KKT well-posedness (Theorem 10
and Proposition 5), forward invariance of safety sets (Theorem 11), one-step risk descent under the gate (Theorem 12),
and no chattering with bounded trade rates (Theorem 13 and Proposition 6). These guarantees do not assert optimality
relative to all admissible hedgers, nor do they promise real-market performance without calibration; rather, they aim to
formalize coherence and safety in a white-box stack.
Numerical stability and implementation choices.
Our Dupire extractor uses central differences and convexity
clipping of ∂KKC; this is consistent with practice and retains O(∆K2+∆T 2) accuracy (§4.1), but the constants hidden
in O(·) depend on near-wing sampling and interpolation. In particular, coarse strike grids or aggressive extrapolation
may inflate Cmax in Theorem 1. The log–Euler step converges with strong order 1/2 (Theorem 2); higher-order
schemes (e.g., Milstein) can be used when smoothness permits, but are not required for the guarantees we pursue.
Modeling assumptions and domain shift.
The shell is arbitrage-free and exchange-coherent by construction;
nonetheless, market data introduce microstructure frictions absent from our synthetic world: discrete strikes, crossed
quotes, stale prints, RFQ frictions, and inventory constraints of liquidity providers. Likewise, the CIR proxy is not
a claim that VIX dynamics are one-factor affine; it is only an index mapping that allows closed-form diagnostics
(Proposition 1 and Theorem 5). Real regimes with jumps or roughness may require S(L)V generalizations or multi-factor
terms. Our controller’s safety boxes and gate are agnostic to those changes; the same CBF/QP machinery applies once
prices and index construction remain coherent.
Choice of risk metric.
We use ES97.5 as the tail metric, motivated by coherence and tractable convex surrogates.
Other coherent risk measures (spectral, entropic) could be substituted with minor changes in the QP soft boxes and the
gate’s sufficient-descent logic, provided a convex upper bound is available.
Complexity and deployment.
The per-step compute is dominated by a 2 × 2 QP with box constraints
(closed-form/active-set) and a handful of diagnostics (band/gate checks), making the controller viable at sub-millisecond
scales on a single CPU core in the synthetic world. In production, the latency budget is dominated by market-data
access, order-routing, and risk-system interactions; the QP itself is negligible.
Failure modes and mitigations.
Potential failure modes include: (i) Teacher mis-specification, evidenced by growing
VIX residuals δVIX: mitigation—raise wVIX temporarily or tighten ATM shape scales (sL, sS, sC); (ii) Over-guarding,
where beff
V grows too large and misses risk-reducing trades: mitigation—cap guard multipliers (τtail, τρ, τpred) and
monitor gate vetoes; (iii) Correlation mis-estimation, leading to over- or under-weighting VIX: mitigation—clip |bρ| and
fall back to a floor weff
VIX ≥wmin; (iv) Cooldown over-use, delaying legitimate trades: mitigation—shorten Ncd in calm
regimes or use an adaptive cooldown keyed to realized volatility.
Reproducibility and auditability.
All pieces—SSVI calibration, VIX quadrature, ASL teacher, Dupire differences,
κ estimator, QP/CBF constraints—are parameterized in human-readable YAML and logged per step. We recommend
retaining (i) constraint multipliers for post-trade audit; (ii) gate ledgers for accepted vs. vetoed trades; and (iii) coherence
dashboards for δVIX, quadrature convergence, and ATM-shape stability.
Societal impact: benefits and risks.
By construction, the stack discourages VIX chasing in brittle regimes and
prioritizes tail-risk reduction per unit cost. In principle, this can reduce disorderly hedging and inventory whipsaws that
have amplified historical dislocations [48]. The white-box design aids model-risk governance and regulatory audit by
exposing safety activations and trade vetoes, potentially lowering systemic externalities. Conversely, wide deployment
of similar controllers could lead to synchronization (herding) if common signals dominate; network-based systemic-risk
20


## Page 21


studies warn that correlated behaviors can amplify shocks [1]. Our guards and cooldown induce dwell-times that
mitigate rapid feedback, but broader monitoring (cross-participant diversity, circuit-breakers) remains essential.
Ethics, responsible release, and scope.
This work is a research artifact, not trading advice. We release synthetic
data and code solely to foster reproducibility and stress-testing of structure-aware safety for cross-asset hedging. Any
real-market use requires (i) independent calibration, (ii) pre-trade risk sign-offs, (iii) guardrails for extreme events, and
(iv) continuous monitoring for drift and data quality. We intentionally avoid high-frequency order-book features in the
public artifact to reduce potential for misuse.
Limitations (Summary)
• Teacher/runtime gap: The ASL closure may underfit unusual term-structure kinks; online adaptation is
limited to small parameter moves.
• Local-vol smoothness: Dupire relies on second spatial derivatives; wings remain numerically delicate despite
clipping.
• One-factor proxy: The CIR mapping is a pragmatic index proxy; multi-factor and jump features are out of
scope here.
• Static costs: Impact parameters are stationary in our experiments; real liquidity is state- and time-of-day
dependent.
• Synthetic evidence: We intentionally omit live backtests; generalization to real markets is unproven and left
to future work with proper controls.

Conclusion
We presented a white-box, risk-sensitive hedging stack for SPX–VIX that links market structure to control via provable
coherence and safety. At the market layer, an arbitrage-free SSVI surface and a Cboe-compliant VIX computation
supervise an interpretable ASL teacher; Dupire then supplies dynamics consistent with quotes and index construction.
At the control layer, a two-variable CBF-QP with a sufficient-descent gate, a correlation/expiry-aware dynamic VIX
weight, guarded no-trade bands, and expiry-aware micro-thresholds/cooldown implements tail-safety as constraints
rather than opaque penalties.
On the theory side, we established (i) surface–index coherence with explicit dependence on ATM-shape and second-order
quadrature; (ii) positivity/boundedness/consistency of the discrete Dupire surface and pathwise stability of log–Euler;
(iii) a functional representation for the VIX-to-price sensitivity κ(Trem) together with positivity and near-expiry
robustness; and (iv) feasibility/uniqueness/KKT properties of the QP, forward invariance via CBFs, one-step risk
descent under the gate, and no chattering with bounded trade rates. In a fully reproducible synthetic world mirroring
exchange rules and impact, the teacher VIX matches the surface VIX to within 2.8 × 10−3, and the tail-safe controller
yields statistically significant ES improvements with reduced nuisance turnover.
Outlook.
Three concrete extensions appear most promising: (i) microstructure-consistent option execution for the
VIX leg (RFQ, quote staleness, minimum size, and latency), (ii) beyond local volatility via calibrated stochastic-local
volatility or rough-volatility projections while retaining the coherence pipeline, and (iii) multi-maturity coupling (VIX
term structure & SPX options) to study term-structure hedging with shared safety budgets. Our broader thesis is that
structure-aware safety layers—coherence at the pricing/index interface and invariance at the control interface—offer an
auditable, modular foundation for robust learning-based hedging.

A.4
Proof of Corollary(Calendar coherence and no-butterfly)
Proof. By [33, Thm. 2], the SSVI parameterization satisfies static no-arbitrage (no butterfly) for each fixed T when
the Gatheral–Jacquier inequalities hold: they enforce convexity of k 7→w(k; T) and thus convexity of K 7→C(K, T)
via the Black–Scholes mapping and the Breeden–Litzenberger identity ∂KKC = e−rT Q(ST ≥K) ≥0 [14]. For
calendar coherence, sufficient conditions ensuring that T 7→w(k; T) is non-decreasing for all k are discussed in [33];
in our construction we explicitly enforce monotonicity of the term variance T 7→θ(T) together with smoothness of
ρ(T), ϕ(T) across T (spline regularization and post-fit monotone adjustment). Since w(k; T) is affine in θ(T) with
a positive coefficient (and the remaining dependence is monotone in the calibrated regime), the practical monotone
post-adjustment renders T 7→w(k; T) non-decreasing on the grid, thus preventing calendar arbitrage (cf. [5, 46]).

teacher exactly matches (L, S, C) and the residual is O(k3).
