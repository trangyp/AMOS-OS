Narrative abstract
Boolean logic underpins digital computing: every variable is true or false, and all truths
can be listed in a single global ledger.
Quantum experiments showed that sometimes
such a ledger cannot exist, even if every local piece is consistent. We call this Curved
Boolean Logic (CBL). It extends Boolean logic with local truths that may fail globally,
much as curved geometry extends flat space. This is not only philosophically striking but
computationally useful: it defines new satisfiability problems, proof systems, and solvers
that prune contradictions earlier. Applications range from SAT solvers and verification to
reasoning in AI.
Lay summary
Boolean logic assumes all truths can be written in one global list. Quantum exper-
iments showed this assumption can fail. Curved Boolean Logic generalizes Boolean
logic: local truths can exist but refuse to combine globally. This idea, inspired
by physics but usable on today’s computers, offers new strategies for solving hard
problems like SAT and verification by detecting contradictions earlier.

Introduction
Classical propositional logic assumes that jointly compatible propositions admit a sin-
gle global assignment. This assumption underlies circuits, compilers, SAT solvers, and
verification.
Yet results in quantum foundations (Bell, Kochen–Specker, KCBS) show
that—even when local families are consistent—no global ledger may exist.
Analogy to relativity. Boolean logic corresponds to flat ledgers (special rel-
ativity); CBL introduces curvature: local truths without global extension
(general relativity). Boolean logic is recovered in the flat limit κ = 0.

The Kochen–Specker theorem (1967) first proved global valuations

ducible statistical validation.
Outlook.
Although the core of this work develops CBL as a rigorous formalism for con-
textual inference and counterfactual consistency in quantum and algorithmic settings, its
implications extend much further. The same ε-bounded curvature logic that stabilizes
quantum measurements applies to a wide class of resistant mathematical and computa-
tional problems— from combinatorial optimization and proof complexity to contextual
causal inference, information geometry, and large-language-model compression. By re-
casting logical inconsistency as measurable geometric curvature, CBL provides a unifying
language for reasoning under contextual tension. A detailed survey of these prospective
applications is given in Sec. 30.

Background and Related Work
Contextuality in physics. The Kochen–Specker theorem showed that global truth assign-
ments may be impossible even when all local constraints are consistent [2]. The KCBS
inequality gave a minimal odd-cycle witness of contextuality in a spin-1 system [3]. A
sheaf-theoretic formulation unified these phenomena [4], while graph-theoretic exclusivity
methods connected contextuality to combinatorics [5].
Logics beyond Boolean. Orthomodular/quantum logics were proposed to capture alge-
braic structure beyond classical distributivity [6]; paraconsistent logics tolerate contradic-
tions without collapse to triviality [7]. CBL differs: it preserves local classical reasoning
but can forbid a global valuation; contradictions are not tolerated, they are flagged struc-
turally by curvature.
Constraint satisfaction and CSP dichotomy. Classical CSP theory separates tractable
from NP-hard classes via algebraic/structural criteria [8]. Our contribution is orthogonal:
curvature is a property of *overlaps* rather than clause language; two clause sets with
identical local forms can differ by curvature and hence by pruning power under CBL.
Prior device-independent robustness. Existing analyses treat global noise/imperfections
at the inequality level; see e.g. modern reviews on steering/nonlocality [9]. CBL adds a

Curved Boolean Logic: Semantics
Let V be a finite set of propositional variables. A context system is a finite family C =
{Ci}i∈I of nonempty subsets Ci ⊆V such that each v ∈V appears in at least one Ci.
Intuitively, each C ∈C is a set of jointly testable variables.
Definition 6 (Local and global valuations). A local valuation on context C is a Boolean
assignment ℓC : C →{0, 1}.
A family {ℓC}C∈C is compatible if for all C, C′ ∈C,
ℓC|C∩C′ = ℓC′|C∩C′. A global valuation is g : V →{0, 1} restricting to each ℓC.
Curvature.
We say (V, C) is flat if every compatible family admits a global valuation;
otherwise it is curved. Curvature measures the obstruction to extending local truths to a
global ledger.
5.1
Sheaf semantics
Let Ctx be the poset category of contexts ordered by inclusion. Define a presheaf F with
F(C) = {0, 1}C and, for D ⊆C, restriction maps ρCD : F(C) →F(D) by restriction
of functions. A compatible family is a matching family; a global section is g ∈F(V )
restricting to each ℓC.
Definition 7 (Curvature functional). A curvature functional is any map κ : (V, C) 7→N0
such that κ = 0 iff every compatible family admits a global section, and κ is monotone
under context refinement.
5.2

Following Abramsky et al. [10], consider the nerve of the cover C and the presheaf A
assigning {0, 1}C under XOR. A compatible family is a 0-cocycle; a global section exists
iff its class is trivial in H1(Ctx, A). We may therefore set

Proof Theory
We develop a context-sequent calculus to reason in CBL. Sequents are annotated with
contexts: Γ ⊢C φ means within context C, from assumptions Γ, infer φ.
7.1
Local rules
Within a fixed context C, inference is standard propositional sequent calculus. Weakening,
contraction, cut, and introduction rules for ∧, ∨, ¬ apply to variables in C only.

Overlap rules
Contexts overlap; we allow transport and consistency rules:
Γ ⊢C ψ

These rules enforce that overlaps agree and contradictions on overlaps propagate glob-
ally.
7.3
Soundness
Theorem 2 (Soundness). If Γ ⊢C φ is derivable, then every local valuation ℓC with ℓC |= Γ
satisfies φ. With the overlap and consistency rules (OVL, CONS), derivability is sound
with respect to the sheaf semantics introduced in Sec. 5.
Sketch. Local rules coincide with the classical propositional sequent calculus and are there-
fore sound. For (OVL), if Γ ⊢C ψ and Vars(ψ) ⊆C ∩C′, then compatibility of local valua-
tions ensures ℓC′ agrees on the overlap, hence ψ also holds in C′. For (CONS), if Γ, ψ ⊢C ⊥
and Γ, ¬ψ ⊢C′ ⊥, any global assignment extending both contexts would contradict itself
on C ∪C′.
7.4
Flat-limit conservativity
Theorem 3 (Flat-limit conservativity). If κ(V, C) = 0, then Γ ⊢C φ is derivable in CBL
if and only if the same sequent is derivable in classical propositional logic.
Sketch. When κ(V, C) = 0, every compatible local family admits a global valuation; thus
CBL derivability coincides with Boolean derivability.

CBL-SAT and Complexity
Scope and claims.
We make no blanket claim of asymptotic speedups for arbitrary
SAT. Our contributions are: (i) structural reductions showing how curvature/overlap pa-
rameters control branching and pruning in certain families, (ii) formal bounds connecting
runtime to curvature rank under A1–A5, (iii) empirical evidence on structured bench-
marks. We highlight open problems where tighter worst-case bounds or lower bounds may
hold.

Definition 9 (CBL-SAT). Instance: a context system (V, C) and a set of clauses Φ, each
clause ϕ ∈Φ attached to a context C(ϕ) ∈C. Question: does there exist a compatible
family {ℓC} with ℓC(ϕ) |= ϕ for all ϕ?
8.1
Basic properties
Proposition 1 (NP-membership). CBL-SAT ∈NP. Certificate: local assignments ℓC.
Verification: check local clause satisfaction and overlap equalities in polynomial time.
Proposition 2 (Flat limit). If κ = 0, CBL-SAT reduces to SAT by the global valuation
extending all ℓC.
8.2
Hardness
Theorem 4. CBL-SAT is NP-complete.
Sketch. Membership: by certificate verification. Hardness: reduce SAT to CBL-SAT by
taking a single context C = V . Thus CBL-SAT inherits NP-completeness.
8.3
Tractable subclasses
Theorem 5 (Treewidth). If the context hypergraph has bounded treewidth and each clause
respects a bag, CBL-SAT is solvable in polynomial time by dynamic programming.
Sketch. Maintain tables of partial assignments on bags. Propagate overlaps through in-
troduce/forget/join nodes. Runtime O(|Φ| · 2O(w)) with treewidth w.
8.4
Parameterized complexity
Parameters of interest:
• Maximum overlap size k: fixed k yields polynomial overlap checks.
• Number of curved cores: bounded number allows branching then flattening.
• Curvature rank κ: small κ may yield FPT algorithms.

CBL Operators and Case Studies
Motivation.
CBL is useful when it is simple to deploy, conservative in the flat limit, and
strictly stronger on instances with curved cores. We present two drop-in operators—CBL-
AC and CBL-CONS—and three case studies (SAT, CSP/AC, 3-Colorability).
Each
operator is sound, proof-loggable, and costs essentially nothing on flat instances.

Sketch. With no faces, the overlap transport is trivial; there are no curved cores to trigger
blocking or overlap contradictions. The operators reduce to AC and overlap equalities.
Theorem 7 (Strict dominance on curved cores). On any instance with at least one min-

Theorem 8 (Certificate soundness). Every elimination or cut emitted by CBL-AC/CBL-

Sketch. Branch only on a cover of faces; each face collapses by CBL-AC or is discharged by

overlap width.

Case Studies: SAT, CSP/AC, and 3-Colorability
12.1
SAT (structured families)
We integrate CBL-CONS into a CDCL solver as a proof-logged cut generator. On struc-
tured families (Mermin/Feistel-style wirings, parity gadgets), curved faces trigger early
global cuts and certified UNSAT, reducing node counts by 20–80% on our prototypes.
Flat instances incur near-zero overhead; proof logs verify each cut in exact arithmetic.
12.2
CSPs via CBL-AC (Latin Square / Sudoku)
Contexts are row/column/block scopes; overlaps are shared cells. CBL-AC eliminates val-
ues that participate in curved faces formed by overlapping scopes with parity/uniqueness
constraints. We observe 10–30% additional domain pruning over classical AC before search
on medium grids, with negligible overhead on flat/easy puzzles.
12.3
3-Colorability (odd-cycle cores)
Contexts are neighborhoods; curved faces are odd cycles with overlapping edges. CBL-
CONS detects early infeasibility (3-color) by transporting contradictions around odd cores
and emitting global cuts. On planar triangulations and random graphs with planted odd
cores, we obtain early UNSAT with short certificates; DSATUR/backtracking baselines
do not see the contradiction without deeper search.

Noise Models and Robustness Guarantees
We formalize three noise classes and state stability bounds for a generic CBL witness S
(e.g., a folded face statistic). Proofs and constants appear in Appendix A.
13.1

CBL for Large Language Models and Contextual AI Systems
Motivation.
Modern large language models (LLMs) exhibit context-sensitive reason-
ing: their outputs depend not only on the explicit prompt but also on latent priors and
conversational history.
This behavior parallels the contextual dependence of measure-
ment outcomes in quantum systems. CBL provides a formalism for reasoning about such
dependencies in a controlled, mathematically traceable way.
15.1
Contextual stability in inference chains
An LLM’s response distribution can be viewed as a map
R : (prompt context, latent state) −→token probabilities.
Analogous to experimental observables, we can treat the prompt context as a “measure-
ment setting” and the latent state as a hidden variable. Under CBL, we introduce an
ε-bounded perturbation model: small changes in the prompt or latent embedding must
not change logical entailments beyond an ε margin.
Definition (CBL-stable inference).
An inference chain c1 →c2 →· · · →ck in an
LLM is CBL-stable if, for any perturbation δ in the embedding space with ∥δ∥≤ε, the
resulting distributional change in the inferred logical relation remains bounded:
DKL(R(ci + δ) ∥R(ci)) ≤f(ε),
where f(ε) obeys the same Lipschitz bounds as in Axiom A2. This captures deterministic
robustness without requiring complete retraining.
15.2
Counterfactual consistency for chain-of-thought reasoning
LLMs generate reasoning traces (chains-of-thought) that can loop back on previous tokens.
CBL’s counterfactual semantics allows a consistent treatment of such loops: a reasoning
step may depend on hypothetical alternatives (“what if this token were different”) with-
out producing global contradictions. In CBL notation, the inference graph of a multi-turn
reasoning sequence becomes a folded contextual graph with curvature parameter θ0. Flat
(θ0=0) reasoning corresponds to purely logical deduction; curved (θ0̸=0) reasoning cap-
tures context reweighting, memory decay, or instruction alignment.
15.3
Applications to alignment and interpretability
Alignment.
Under bounded perturbations of prompt or reward signals, CBL yields
quantitative alignment stability metrics. For instance, if a model’s instruction-following
probability shifts by more than ε under paraphrased prompts, it violates the ε-bounded
perturbation assumption and becomes context-unstable.
Such metrics can guide fine-
tuning or reinforcement-learning-with-feedback loops.
Interpretability.
Each folded context in an LLM corresponds to a measurable projec-
tion of the attention map. By treating attention heads as “measurement contexts” and
activations as outcomes, CBL quantifies contextual interference among heads. The dif-
ferential sensitivity dS/dp then measures how much a local attention flip influences the
global output, offering a principled proxy for saliency.

Algorithmic analogy
The connection between CBL-SAT pruning and LLM inference is direct: both perform

CBL extends naturally from quantum contextuality to machine learning

Discussion
CBL coupling and invariance.
Sec. 23.1 argues that the large–L per-edge holonomy in
CBL splits into a geometric O(1/L) tail plus a size-independent topological term equal to a
fixed phase per contextual face, yielding the dimensionless coupling gCBL = |θ0| ρface/(2π).
After a single CHSH calibration, this produces parameter-free predictions across KCBS
and SAT tilings that match our numerics (Fig. 10, Tab. 13). The observed invariance of
the fitted intercepts under gate-law swap, decimation, and ε-probe placement supports
interpreting gCBL as a genuine topological invariant rather than a modelling artefact.
Practical operators.
CBL’s value here is operational: CBL-AC and CBL-CONS are
trivial to add to existing solvers, cost essentially nothing on flat families, and yield strictly
stronger pruning or earlier infeasibility when curved cores are present. The case studies
demonstrate this on SAT, CSP/AC, and 3-Colorability.
Syntax–semantics duality
CBL highlights a clear duality:

• Syntax: context-sequent calculus with overlap rules, capturing derivability.
• Semantics: presheaf and hypergraph models, capturing obstruction to global sec-

Flat-limit theorems ensure they coincide when κ = 0. Curved cases mark divergence:
syntactic derivations exist locally, but semantics forbids a global valuation.
Remark 2 (Categorical semantics). CBL instances can be regarded as presheaves on the
context poset Ctx.
Logic of local sections embeds in the topos of presheaves SetCtxop.
Curvature corresponds to failure of global sections functor to be exact. This connects CBL
to categorical logic and topos-theoretic semantics.
Remark 3 (Analogy to algebraic geometry). Flat instances correspond to Boolean va-
rieties where global solutions exist.
Curved instances resemble inconsistent polynomial
systems: each patch has solutions, but no global solution exists. Curvature plays the role
of obstruction classes, akin to ideals with no common zero. This analogy ties logical cur-
vature to algebraic obstructions.
Philosophical implications
Traditionally, mathematics provides tools for physics. Here, physics (contextuality experi-
ments) revealed incompleteness of Boolean logic. This makes mathematics partly a science
of discovery: structures latent in logic were uncovered by empirical anomalies. CBL thus
blurs lines between mathematics and physics, supporting the view that logics themselves
can be discovered experimentally.

Beyond quantum foundations.

algebras through finite curvature embeddings.
30.3
Logic and Proof Complexity
CBL introduces a geometric measure of contextual reuse. This can be interpreted as a
“curvature complexity” giving lower bounds on resolution or circuit size: each contextual
loop adds quantifiable logical tension that classical proofs must unwind step by step.

Probability, Statistics, and Information Theory
CBL’s noise model (Axiom A2) formalizes adversarial rather than purely stochastic uncer-
tainty. This enables deterministic capacity bounds for correlated or worst-case channels,
and robust Bayesian updates whose posterior drift is bounded by ε even under contextual
dataset shifts. In causal inference, folded CBL graphs extend ordinary DAGs to settings
with latent feedback or self-reference, maintaining identifiability conditions that fail in
standard frameworks.

Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence
