THE AXIOM OF REAL DETERMINACY AND THE AXIOM OF REAL
BLACKWELL DETERMINACY
DAISUKE IKEGAMI AND W. HUGH WOODIN
Abstract. We show that the Axiom of Real Determinacy ADR and the Axiom of Real
Blackwell Determinacy Bl-ADR are equivalent in ZF+DC.
This answers the question
of L¨owe [15, Question 53].
While we do not know whether they are equivalent in
ZF+ACω(R), we show that the theories ZF+ADR and ZF+ACω(R)+Bl-ADR are equicon-
sistent.
1. Introduction
In this paper, we compare the stronger versions of determinacy of Gale-Stewart games
and Blackwell games, i.e., the Axiom of Real Determinacy ADR and the Axiom of Real
Blackwell Determinacy Bl-ADR.
In 1953, Gale and Stewart [5] developed the general theory of infinite games, so-called
Gale-Stewart games, which are two-player zero-sum infinite games with perfect informa-
tion. The theory of Gale-Stewart games has been investigated by many logicians and now
it is one of the main topics in set theory and it has connections with other topics in set
theory as well as model theory and computer science.
In 1928, John von Neumann proved his famous minimax theorem which is about finite
games with imperfect information. Infinite versions of von Neumann’s games were intro-
duced by David Blackwell [2] where he proved the analogue of von Neumann’s theorem
for Gδ sets of reals (i.e., Π
e
0
2 sets of reals). The games he introduced are called Blackwell
games and they were called by him “games with slightly imperfect information” in his
paper [3].
In 1998, Martin [16] proved that in most cases, Blackwell determinacy axioms follow
from the corresponding determinacy axiom. Martin conjectured that they are equivalent,
and many instances of equivalence have been shown (e.g., [17] and Martin’s proof of Π1
1
determinacy presented in [14, Corollary 3.9]).
However, the general question, and in
particular the most intriguing instance, namely whether AD and the axiom of Blackwell
determinacy Bl-AD are equivalent, remains open.
De Kloet, L¨owe, and the first author [7] turned to the other mentioned determinacy
axiom, the stronger ADR and its Blackwell analogue. They introduced the Axiom of Real
Blackwell Determinacy Bl-ADR and then proved that Bl-ADR implies the existence of R#,
and that the consistency of ZF+ACω(R)+Bl-ADR is strictly stronger than that of ZF+AD.

2. Preliminaries
Throughout this paper, we work in ZF+ACω(R), where ACω(R) states that for all ω-
sequences (An | n < ω) of nonempty sets of reals, there is a function f : ω →R such
that for all n < ω, f(n) ∈An. This small fragment of the axiom of choice is necessary
for the definitions of axioms of Blackwell determinacy. Using ACω(R), one can develop
the basics of measure theory. If we need more than ZF+ACω(R) for some definitions and
statements, we explicitly mention the additional axioms. We use standard notations from
set theory and assume that the reader is familiar with forcing, descriptive set theory, and
determinacy of Gale-Stewart games, whose basics can be found in e.g., [8] and [18]. By
reals, we mean elements of the Cantor space and we use 2ω or R to denote the Cantor
space.

2.3. Blackwell games. In this subsection, we introduce Blackwell games, which are infi-
nite games with imperfect information, and compare Blackwell games with Gale-Stewart
games.
We start with the definition of Blackwell games.1 Let X be a set with at least two
elements and assume ACω(Xω). The topology of Xω is given by the product topology
where each coordinate (i.e., X) is seen as the discrete space. For each finite sequence s
of elements in X, [s] denotes the set {x ∈Xω | x ⊇s} and it is a basic open set in the
topological space Xω. In Blackwell games, players choose probabilities on X instead of
elements of X and with those probabilities, one can derive a Borel probability on Xω, i.e.,
a measure assigning probability to each Borel subset of Xω. Player I wins if the probability
of a given payoff set is 1 and player II wins if the probability of the payoff set is 0. Let
us formulate this in detail. We write XEven for the set of all finite sequences of elements
of X of even length and XOdd for the set of all finite sequences of elements of X of odd
length.

γ0 = supn<ω αn. Then γ0 is closed under f. Let N = HODR∪Γγ0. Then by the property
of f, N is an inner model of ZF+Bl-ADR such that γ0 = ΘN. We argue that γ0 = ΘN is of
countable cofinality in N. Since f is ordinal definable, so is the sequence (α | n < ω), and
hence γ0 = supn<ω αn is of countable cofinality in HOD. Since HOD ⊆HODR∪Γγ0 = N,
γ0 = ΘN is of countable cofinality in N, as desired.
□(Theorem 53)
This completes the proof of Theorem 51.
□
8. Questions
We close this paper by raising some open questions:
Question 1. Are AD and Bl-AD equivalent?
Martin conjectured that the answer to Question 1 is ‘Yes’ (Conjecture 1).
Question 2. Are ADR and Bl-ADR equivalent without assuming DC?
As in Theorem 1, ADR and Bl-ADR are equivalent in ZF+DC.
