Helium-3 Enrichment in Gradual Solar Energetic Particle Events: Evidence for Jet-Supplied Seed
Population
Radoslav Buˇc´ık,1 Samuel T. Hart,1 Maher A. Dayeh,1, 2 Mihir I. Desai,1, 2 Glenn M. Mason,3 and
Mark E. Wiedenbeck4
1Southwest Research Institute, San Antonio, TX 78238, USA

ABSTRACT
Enhancements in 3He abundance, a characteristic feature of impulsive solar energetic particle (ISEP)
events, are also frequently observed in gradual SEP (GSEP) events. Understanding the origin of this
enrichment is crucial for identifying the mechanisms behind SEP generation.
We investigate the
origin of 3He enrichment in high-energy (25–50 MeV) solar proton events observed by SOHO, selecting
events that coincide with ≲1 MeV nucleon−1 3He-rich periods detected by ACE during 1997–2021.
The identiﬁed 3He enhancements include cases where material from independent impulsive (3He-rich)
SEP events is mixed with GSEP proton populations. Two high-energy proton events exhibit elemental
composition and solar source characteristics consistent with ISEPs. Extreme-ultraviolet imaging from
SDO and STEREO reveals narrow, jet-like eruptions in the parent active regions of about 60% of the
remaining events. Notably, the highest 3He/4He ratios occur when coronal jets are present, consistent
with fresh, jet-driven injection of suprathermal 3He that is subsequently re-accelerated during the
event. Correspondingly, jet-associated events show fewer pre-event (residual) 3He counts, indicating
that enrichment in these cases does not primarily come from remnant material. We ﬁnd a positive
correlation between 3He/4He and Fe/O, strongest in jet-associated events, consistent with a common
jet-supplied seed population re-accelerated by the CME shock.

1. INTRODUCTION
Suprathermal ions, traveling at speeds several times greater than the bulk solar wind, constitute the seed popula-
tion for acceleration by coronal mass ejection (CME)-driven shocks in gradual solar energetic particle (GSEP) events
(e.g., M. Desai & J. Giacalone 2016). High-resolution abundance measurements from the Advanced Composition Ex-
plorer (ACE) spacecraft, launched in 1997, revealed that these events frequently exhibit modest enhancements in the
abundances of 3He and Fe, signatures associated with ﬂare-related impulsive SEP (ISEP) events. ISEP events are
characterized by extreme enhancements in the 3He/4He ratio, exceeding solar wind abundance by factors up to 104
(e.g., G. M. Mason 2007). In addition, these events show signiﬁcant enrichment in both heavy ions (Ne–Fe), by factors
of 3–10, and ultraheavy ions (mass > 70 AMU), by factors exceeding 100 in what appears to be a mass-per-charge
dependence, though this dependence remains an active debate topic (R. Buˇc´ık et al. 2021a; G. M. Mason et al. 2023;
S. T. Hart et al. 2024; R. Buˇc´ık et al. 2025).
G. M. Mason et al. (1999) examined 12 GSEP events and found that, in eight cases, the 0.5–2.0MeV nucleon−1
3He/4He ratio ranged from 5 to 135 times the average slow solar wind (SSW) value of (4.08 ± 0.25) × 10−4
(G. Gloeckler & J. Geiss 1998). M. I. Desai et al. (2006) analyzed 64 GSEP events observed by NOAA GOES be-
tween November 1997 and January 2005, identifying 29 with distinct and ﬁnite 3He peaks.

0.5–2.0MeV nucleon−1 3He/4He ratio was enhanced by factors of ∼2–150 over the SSW value, with only 23 (36%)
events exceeding a factor of 4. In that work, they cautioned that enhancements by a factor of ∼2 may reﬂect ﬂuctua-

signiﬁcant. A subsequent study by M. I. Desai et al. (2016), covering 46 GSEP events from April 1998 to February
2014 (including 26 from the earlier survey), reported enhancements by factors ∼1.5–194 in 27 events, with 24 events
(52%) showing enhancements above a factor of 4. More recently, R. Buˇc´ık et al. (2023a) reported a GSEP event on
2020 November 24, observed by Solar Orbiter at 0.9 au, with a 3He/4He enhancement of 24, following a prolonged
period of 3He-rich SEPs. Similar enhancements at higher energies (>5 MeV nucleon−1) have also been observed (e.g.,
C. M. S. Cohen et al. 1999; M. E. Wiedenbeck et al. 2000).
Fe/O enhancements are frequently reported in these events (G. M. Mason et al. 1999; M. I. Desai et al. 2006, 2016;
C. M. S. Cohen et al. 1999; A. J. Tylka et al. 2005). While these may arise from magnetic rigidity-dependent transport
eﬀects (e.g., G. M. Mason et al. 2012), due to the lower charge-to-mass ratio of Fe compared to O, such transport biases
do not account for 3He/4He enhancements (e.g., G. M. Mason et al. 1999). Thus, 3He enrichment is a robust indicator
of ﬂare material in GSEP events (e.g., M. I. Desai et al. 2016).
Despite signiﬁcant progress, the mechanisms by which CME-driven shocks access ﬂare-accelerated suprathermal
populations and generate the observed compositional signatures in GSEP events remain poorly understood.
One
proposed scenario involves the re-acceleration of remnant ﬂare suprathermal ions, which are commonly present in
the interplanetary (IP) medium at 1 au (G. M. Mason et al. 1999; A. J. Tylka et al. 2005; M. I. Desai et al. 2006;
R. A. Mewaldt et al. 2012; M. E. Wiedenbeck & G. M. Mason 2014; R. Buˇc´ık et al. 2023a).
An alternative sce-
nario suggests the re-acceleration of suprathermal ions produced by ﬂares in the parent active regions (ARs)
(T. T. von Rosenvinge et al. 2000; H. V. Cane et al. 2003, 2006; Y.-K. Ko et al. 2013). In addition to observational
analysis, coupled ﬂare and CME-driven shock acceleration has also been modeled (e.g., G. Li & G. P. Zank 2005).
The presence of type III radio bursts in GSEP events indicates that electron beams escape along open ﬁeld lines into
IP space, suggesting that suprathermal ions from the same site can also escape (H. V. Cane et al. 2006, 2010). In this
context, the observer’s heliolongitude relative to the source ﬂare has been suggested to inﬂuence the Fe/O ratio in
GSEP events (H. V. Cane et al. 2003; Y.-K. Ko et al. 2013).
In this study, we investigate the origin of 3He enhancements in 3He-enriched GSEP events. We examine ion intensity
time proﬁles for evidence of independent ISEP events. To evaluate the contributions of remnant suprathermal ions
and suprathermals associated with concomitant activity, we analyze integrated 3He counts prior to the GSEP onset,
magnetic connectivity, and the morphology of ﬂare activity at the associated solar source regions.
2. DATA AND EVENT SELECTION
2.1. Initial catalog
We surveyed the live catalog of 3He-rich periods detected by the Ultra-Low Energy Isotope Spectrometer (ULEIS;
G. M. Mason et al. 1998) on ACE and compiled by S. T. Hart et al. (2022), covering the interval from 1997 September
29 to 2021 March 1, with the aim of identifying concurrent GSEP events. The catalog lists periods in the energy
ranges 0.32–0.45MeV nucleon−1 and 0.64–1.28MeV nucleon−1. For our analysis, we focused on the Solar Dynamics
Observatory (SDO) era (post-May 2010), during which 174 3He-rich periods were reported. We selected proton events
observed by Energetic and Relativistic Nuclei and Electron (ERNE; J. Torsti et al. 1995) on the Solar and Heliospheric
Observatory (SOHO) that exhibited suﬃciently high intensities at higher energies. Speciﬁcally, we required that the
ratio of the 25–50 MeV proton intensity peak to the 24-hour pre-event background exceeded a factor of two. Such
events exhibit visually distinguishable enhancements above background. In addition to GSEP events that overlap a
cataloged 3He-rich period, we also included six events for which a cataloged 3He-rich period ended within 24 hours
before the proton-event onset (#566, 604, 641, 682, 700, and 790; S. T. Hart et al. 2022).
We treat this one-day
window as an observational proxy for seed availability along the observer-connected ﬁeld line. Notably, no 3He periods
ended between one and two days before the onset, and periods ending three or more days earlier were excluded.
A total of 41 proton events satisﬁed our selection criteria. Among these, 10 3He periods (#471, 537, 576, 646,
680, 681, 688, 744, 772, 794) showed no low-energy (≲1 MeV nucleon−1) enhancements in 4He (and often O) above
the preceding background and were therefore excluded. Period #688 spanned two closely spaced proton events. We
further excluded six 3He periods (#560, 608, 672, 758, 773, 840) associated with corotating interaction regions (CIRs),
identiﬁed by characteristic signatures in the solar wind plasma and IP magnetic ﬁeld (e.g., enhanced total pressure
during gradual solar wind speed increases; L. F. Burlaga 1995). One additional period (#532) was excluded due to an

4. DISCUSSION
M. I. Desai et al. (2006) reported a correlation of r = 0.66 between 3He/4He and Fe/O based on 29 GSEP events
with ﬁnite 3He peaks. However, excluding two outliers reduced the correlation to r = 0.026, leading the authors to
concluded that Fe/O is independent of 3He/4He in GSEP events. Similar ﬁndings were reported for ISEP events by
G. M. Mason et al. (1986) and D. V. Reames et al. (1994). S. T. Hart et al. (2022) show that 3He-rich time periods
with low Fe/O, indicative of mixed ISEP and GSEP events, also tend to exhibit lower 3He/4He, whereas 3He/4He and
Fe/O in pure ISEP events are uncorrelated. The present observations show that 3He/4He and Fe/O are positively
correlated in GSEP events as suggested in S. T. Hart et al. (2022). The correlation is strongest for GSEP events
associated with concurrent jets, consistent with both ratios increasing as the fraction of jet-supplied seed ions (3He, 4He,
O, Fe) that are subsequently re-accelerated becomes larger. Compared with the broader GSEP set of M. I. Desai et al.
(2006), which exhibited up to two orders of magnitude event-to-event variation in elemental ratios, our focus on 3He-
enriched GSEP events reduces scatter and may help reveal this correlation.
If suprathermal ions are supplied by the parent ﬂare, well-connected events should exhibit higher enrichments.
Consistent with this expectation, we ﬁnd a negative correlation between Fe/O and the absolute longitudinal separation
between the magnetic footpoint and the associated ﬂare. In contrast, 3He/4He shows no correlation. This trend
persists when the most distant event (#14) is omitted, reinforcing that the contrasting behaviors of 3He/4He (no

14, 19, 20) all belong to this jet-associated subset (see Fig. 5, right panel). This complete overlap highlights jets as a
likely co-temporal source of the 3He-rich seed population in GSEP events. The elevated 3He/4He ratio in event #14
may reﬂect contributions from two EUV jets (Table 1) within the integration window. Compared to ISEP events,
those examined here were linked to more energetic ﬂares, so some jets may have been missed due to observational
limitations.
No clear correlation was found between the amount of residual suprathermal 3He and the observed 3He/4He or
Fe/O ratios. Similar results were reported by M. I. Desai et al. (2006), who noted a lack of correlation between 8-day
pre-event suprathermal Fe/O and the Fe/O ratio during GSEP events. In this study, we use a 24-hr integration window
for suprathermal 3He to remain consistent with our event selection. Longer windows can fold in ions from prior GSEP
events. Indeed, M. I. Desai et al. (2006) excluded
30% of events because their 8-day interval overlapped previous
GSEP events. Although the ratios do not track the pre-event counts, the distribution of counts by jet classiﬁcation
(Fig. 9) suggests two regimes: jet events generally have low pre-event 3He, whereas non-jet events often have higher
pre-event 3He, implying diﬀerent relative contributions of contemporaneous jet seeds and residual populations.
Finally, we note a moderate anti-correlation between 3He/4He ratio and CME speed, consistent with a scenario
in which the jet or residual pool supplies the 3He-rich seed population, while the CME-driven shock increasingly
accelerates ambient 4He as the speed rises, thereby reducing the ﬁnal 3He/4He.

5. CONCLUSION
We investigated the origin of 3He enrichment in 23 high-energy (25–50 MeV) proton events. After excluding intervals
contaminated by independent ISEP events, and removing two events with ISEP characteristics, we retained 15 clean
GSEP events in which 3He abundance enhancement is consistent with re-acceleration of suprathermal ﬂare ions. Our
main ﬁndings for these 15 events are:
• EUV imaging shows jets in ∼60% (9/15) of the GSEP sources. The four largest 3He/4He ratios all occur in
jet-associated events, consistent with jets supplying the 3He-rich seed population.
• 3He/4He and Fe/O are positively correlated in the GSEP events, with the correlation driven by jet-associated
sources. This supports a scenario in which jets provide a common seed population that is subsequently re-
accelerated by the CME shock.
• GSEP events with jets generally show lower pre-event 3He counts, whereas events without jets show higher
counts. This pattern suggests that jets can supply 3He locally at the time of the event, while in non-jet cases a
residual suprathermal population from prior activity may contribute. Neither source alone controls the elemental
ratios.
• Well-connected GSEP events tend to show higher Fe/O ratios, whereas a similar trend is not evident in 3He/4He.
Plausibly the large intrinsic variability of the suprathermal helium seed population masks any magnetic connec-
tion dependence.
• Heavy-ion abundances in the 3He-enriched GSEP events are consistent with average GSEP values at compa-
rable energies, suggesting that the heavy-ion component is largely drawn from the ambient suprathermal seed
population and then re-accelerated by the CME shock.
About 40% (9/21) of the apparent 3He enrichments were due to overlap with independent ISEP activity, ﬁve whole
events and brief intervals in four others, showing that a sizeable fraction of 3He-rich GSEP identiﬁcations can be
superpositions rather than re-acceleration. This study provides a framework for further investigation: extending the
live catalog beyond 2021 March 1 and incorporating measurements at diﬀerent heliocentric distances (Parker Solar
Probe, Solar Orbiter) will better constrain when and how 3He enrichment arises in GSEP events.
