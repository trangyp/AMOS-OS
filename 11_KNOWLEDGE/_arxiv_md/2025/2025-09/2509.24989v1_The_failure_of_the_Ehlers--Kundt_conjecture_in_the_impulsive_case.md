in the impulsive case

Abstract
In 1962, Ehlers and Kundt conjectured that plane waves are the only class of complete
Ricci-flat pp-waves, i.e. metrics on R4 of the form
ds2 = 2du dv + dx2 + dy2 + H(x, y, u)du2 .
Recently, Flores and S´anchez gave a proof of the conjecture in the fundamental case of spatially
polynomially bounded profile functions H.
However, impulsive pp-waves, i.e., waves with
concentrated profile functions of the form H(x, y, u) = f(x, y) δ(u) (δ, the Dirac measure)
have been found to be complete. We summarise completeness results for several classes of
impulsive wave spacetimes achieved during the last years and discuss them in the context of
the Ehlers–Kundt conjecture.
Keywords: pp-waves, impulsive gravitational waves, nonlinear distributional geometry, com-
pleteness

Introduction
The class of pp-waves is an important family of exact solutions to the Einstein equations.
Definition 1.1 (pp-wave). A plane-fronted wave with parallel rays is a Lorentzian manifold
(M, g) with M = R4 = {(u, v, x) | u, v ∈R, x = (x1, x2) := (x, y) ∈R2} with metric given by
g = 2 du dv + H(u, x) du2 + δij dxi dxj,
(1.1)
where H : R × R2 →R is a smooth function.
Such metrics first arose in the class studied by Brinkmann [9], who investigated the problem
of when an Einstein metric can be conformally mapped to another Einstein metric, and, slightly
later, in the physics literature in the work of Baldwin–Jeffrey [5]. Unfortunately, these works do
not seem to have attracted great attention at the time and, indeed, it is only after the discovery
of such metrics by Peres [38], H´ely [25] and Kundt [27] (see, also, [8]) that the work of Brinkmann
appears to have been recognised.1 The metrics (1.1) were first referred to as pp-waves in the 1962
paper of Ehlers and Kundt [13].2

Given the form of the metric g, it follows that the scalar curvature of g is identically zero.
Therefore, the metric (1.1) is Ricci flat and satisfies the vacuum Einstein equations if and only if

A special class of pp-metrics are the so-called plane waves for which, for each fixed u ∈R, the
map x 7→H(u, x) is a quadratic form, i.e. H(u, x) = hij(u)xixj where, without loss of generality,
we take the matrix h = (hij) to be symmetric. In this case, the metric (1.1) is Ricci-flat if and
only if the matrix h is trace-free, i.e. hii = 0.
More generally, it can be shown [20, Lemma 3.1] that given any (n+2)-dimensional Lorentzian
metric that admits a parallel null vector field Z with the property that the curvature tensor, viewed
as a linear map from bivectors to bivectors, is non-trivial but zero on restriction to Z⊥∧Z⊥,3 there
exist local coordinates (u, v, x1, . . . , xn) in which the metric is locally of the form (1.1). Imposing
that ∇UR = 0 for all U ∈Z⊥then yields (locally) the plane wave metrics, where the function
H(u, x) is of the form hij(u)xixj.
Remark 1.2. The causal behaviour of plane wave metrics is quite well understood. It was shown
by Penrose [36] that plane wave metrics are not global hyperbolic. This result was refined in [14],4
where it was shown that (Ricci-flat) plane wave metrics are strongly causal, stably causal and
causally continuous, but they are not causally simple (and, hence, not globally hyperbolic).

1.3
The Ehlers–Kundt conjecture
In their 1962 article [13, pp. 97], Ehlers and Kundt proposed the following problem, suggesting
that plane waves play a privileged role among pp-waves:
“Prove the plane waves to be the only g-complete pp waves, no matter which topology one chooses.”
This statement is now referred to as “the Ehlers–Kundt conjecture.” Intuitively speaking, a com-
plete, Ricci-flat pp-wave describes a gravitational field without singularities (i.e. it is geodesically
complete) and without internal matter sources (i.e. it satisfies the vacuum Einstein equations).
By analogy with Maxwell’s equations in vacuum, where the simplest solutions are monochromatic
plane waves, the Ehlers–Kundt conjecture asserts that the gravitational plane waves play the
corresponding role as the most elementary, globally regular, source-free gravitational fields.
In a more precise formulation, noting that only Ricci-flat metrics are considered in [13], the
conjecture can be stated as follows.
Conjecture 1.5 (Ehlers–Kundt). A Ricci-flat pp-wave (R4, g), with metric
g = 2du dv + H(u, x) du2 + dx2 + dy2,
where H : R × R2 →R, is geodesically complete if and only if the function x 7→H(u, x) is
polynomial of degree at most two, for all u ∈R.
Remark 1.6. The geodesic equations for the plane wave metrics can be integrated directly,7 and
show that plane wave the metrics are geodesically complete.
(See [13, Theorem 2-5.9] or [6,
Chapter 13].)
While quadratic behavior of H has been successfully shown to be critical for various geometric
properties of the pp-wave spacetime, e.g. asymptotic flatness and causality (cf. [15]), the Ehlers–
Kundt conjecture remains an open problem (cf. [43, Section 5]).

in [16]. They work in the setting of dynamical systems and, more precisely, assume V = −1

2
Completeness for impulsive waves
2.1
Impulsive gravitational waves
In this section, we turn to impulsive waves or, more precisely, to the impulsive version of the met-
ric (1.2). Generally, impulsive gravitational waves model short but violent pulses of gravitational
or other radiation. They were introduced by Penrose in the late 1960’s and considered in detail
in [37]. Over the decades the models have been widely generalised to include a non-vanishing
cosmological constant as well as gyratonic terms [23, Ch. 20]. They have been found to arise
as ultrarelativistic limits of Kerr–Newman and other static spacetimes (see, e.g., [1, 26, 34, 2]),
which makes them interesting models for quantum scattering in general relativistic spacetimes
(see, e.g., [7, 48]), the wave memory effect [55, 52] and entanglement harvesting (see [22] and
references therein). Moreover, they have found applications in astrophysics [3], and serve as key
examples in mathematical investigations of low-regularity spacetimes. (See [53] and forthcoming
works in synthetic Lorentzian geometry.)
Following [39], the simplest way to introduce impulsive waves in our context is to start with
metric (1.2) and set
H(x, u) = f(x)δ(u) ,

Ricci tensor of the metric does not include u-derivatives of H (see (1.3)), so the field equations do
not restrict its u-behavior.) Therefore, we consider the spacetime M = N × R2

wave profile. This not only settles the natural “impulsive EK” analogue in the negative, but also
underscores the subtle role played by regularity in the geometric and dynamical characterizations
of exact wave spacetimes.
