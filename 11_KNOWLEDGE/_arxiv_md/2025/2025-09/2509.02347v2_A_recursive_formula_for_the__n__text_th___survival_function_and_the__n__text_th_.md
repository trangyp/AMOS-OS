A RECURSIVE FORMULA FOR THE nth SURVIVAL
FUNCTION AND THE nth FIRST PASSAGE TIME
DISTRIBUTION FOR JUMP AND DIFFUSION
PROCESSES. APPLICATIONS TO THE PRICING OF
nth-TO-DEFAULT CDS.
ALESSIO LAPOLLA
Abstract. We derive some rather general, but complicated, formulae
to compute the survival function and the first passage time distribution
of the nth coordinate of a many-body stochastic process in the presence
of a killing barrier. First we will study the case of two coordinates and
then we will generalize the results to three or more coordinates. Even if
the results are difficult to implement, we will provide examples of their
use applying them to a physical system, the single file diffusion, and
to the financial problem of pricing a nth-to-default credit default swap
(nth-CDS).
1. Introduction
The first passage time problem refers to the characterization of statis-
tics concerning the first time a stochastic process interacts with a boundary.
This intuitive definition allowed its application to the most diverse fields like:
finance [4, 7], neuroscience [37], physics [35, 3], psychology [33], biology [24],
etc. . . In the case of a single process (in many dimensions as well) several
studies have been and are being performed investigating a great varieties
of models, on the other hand studies on systems with two or more coordi-
nates are less common. One of the first fully general results involves the
two-dimensional correlated Wiener process [5, 20, 38], even for this model
(arguably the most important and fundamental) the solution is rather com-
plicated. Monte Carlo simulations are a powerful and adaptable tool for
the investigation of first passage time problems. However, they sometimes
suffer of discretization errors [19], therefore the investigation into alternative
calculation methods has some practical applications as well.
Given a probability space (Ω, F, P) with right-continuous complete filtration
Ft that supports a stochastic process Xt, describing N different coordinates,
on the measurable state space (R, Σ); a first passage time problem requires
the existence of several hitting times τi = inf{t > 0|E(Xit, ∂R)} with re-
spect to the filtration, one for each coordinate forming Xt.

the boundary of the measurable space ∂R.

anymore with the others and a dimensionality reduction restricts the state

the single file diffusion, is a classic problem in statistical physics.

4. Conclusion
The main results of the article are theorems 1 and 2. The reader may
wonder what is the point of such inconvenient answers [39], for example
the simulation of the blue line in Fig. 2 is only three times slower then
the implementation of Eq. (37), and the latter is infinitely slower and more
complicated than the clever solution of Locatelli et al. [27] in Eq. (40).
However, we argue that the main contribution of our theorems is that they
highlight the non-Markovian nature of the problem, in fact Eq. (44) shows
explicitly how the history of the process enters the solution via the “integrals
over the paths” in Γ. This path dependence has been leveraged to tackle
the valuation of multi-name credit derivatives, we hope that this way of
thinking about these problems can inspire more accurate pricing method in
the space. We also hope that our results may spur more discoveries on a more
fundamental level. We believe that our work could be used to unveil a richer
phenomenology in the persistence exponents [3, 1] or that it could inspire
some approximate solutions, especially in the large number of interacting
particle systems, perhaps up to the mean-field limit [10], or valuation of
collateralized debt obligations [13, 23]; these large scale applications are
likely beyond any reasonable use of our formulae.
