Abstract.
In this paper, we propose an alternative valuation approach for CAT bonds where
a pricing formula is learned by deep neural networks. Once trained, these networks can be used
to price CAT bonds as a function of inputs that reflect both the current market conditions and
the specific features of the contract. This approach offers two main advantages. First, due to the
expressive power of neural networks, the trained model enables fast and accurate evaluation of CAT
bond prices. Second, because of its fast execution the trained neural network can be easily analyzed
to study its sensitivities w.r.t. changes of the underlying market conditions offering valuable insights
for risk management.
Keywords: CAT-bonds, deep learning, importance sampling, sensitivities
1. Introduction
Hurricane Andrew struck the Gulf Coast of the United States in August 1992, resulting in
insurance-related damages of $15.5B and the failure of at least 8 insurance companies. Several
insurance companies reassessed their risk exposure to the coastal regions across the US, see also
[1], and subsequently, risk exposure to catastrophic events in American coastal regions substantially
decreased, while demand for catastrophe insurance increased. This shortage paved the way for the
creation of catastrophe (CAT) bonds in the mid 1990s. In 1995, the American insurance industry
had an estimated capital holding of $240B and the possibility of single-event losses of over $100B, see
[11]. Insurance firms consequently started issuing CAT bonds to transfer risk off its balance sheet
to the capital markets where institutional investors sought high yields uncorrelated with equity and
fixed income returns, provided they indemnified the issuer if a catastrophe-related or trigger event
occurred. Providing evidence for the resultant success of CAT bonds, according to [7], the total
outstanding market has grown from $1B to $49.1B in June 2024.
When issuing a CAT bond, a trigger event and corresponding payout mechanism are defined in
the bond contract. The catastrophe is defined using peril (catastrophe type) and territory (catas-
trophe location). The trigger event type is usually either indemnity (loss of the sponsor exceeds
a predetermined threshold), industry index (the aggregate estimated industry loss, measured by
an independent third-party index, exceeds a predetermined threshold) or parametric (measurable
characteristic of the defined catastrophe exceeds a predetermined threshold), compare also Table 1
for an overview of the market shares of the different loss types.

The pricing of CAT bonds has been a subject of increasing interest in both financial and actuarial
literature, particularly due to the complexities introduced by low-probability, high-impact trigger
events and the structural features of the bonds themselves. The literature on CAT bond pricing
frequently employs stochastic modeling frameworks. Notably, Baryshnikov et al. introduced in
[40] a framework, in analogy to the pricing of defaultable bonds (compare, e.g., [4]), where a
compound Poisson process is employed to capture the the cumulated random losses. While their
model effectively incorporates the stochastic nature of catastrophic events, it does not yield a
closed-form pricing formula. Nevertheless, the study provides a thorough numerical analysis based
on simulation techniques. A simplified valuation framework is presented in Jarrow ([24]), where
a closed-form solution for CAT bond pricing is derived. The model relies on the initial LIBOR
zero-coupon bond price curve along with two key inputs: the hazard rate of the catastrophic event
and the expected loss severity.
A foundational mathematical treatment was presented by Burnecki and Kukla ([10]), who sub-
sequently refine the methodology from [40], addressing key limitations in the original formulation.
This work is particularly influential in bridging actuarial modeling with applied probability the-
ory, offering a robust and tractable approach for CAT bond pricing. Their enhanced framework is
further applied by H¨ardle and L´opez ([20]) to examine the calibration of CAT bonds for Mexican
earthquakes, demonstrating its practical applicability in real-world catastrophe-linked securities.
Further mathematical refinement was proposed by Nowak and Romaniuk ([32]), who incorporated
interest rate risk and dependence structures into CAT bond valuation.
Their model provide a
generalized catastrophe bond pricing formula, assuming the one-factor stochastic diffusion form of
the risk-free interest rate process, where the interest rate behavior is described by the multi-factor
Cox–Ingersoll–Ross (CIR) model. Additional contributions such as Ma and Ma ([29]) addressed
the impact of multiple risk drivers - loss severity distributions, claim arrival intensities, trigger
thresholds, and stochastic interest rates - on CAT bond valuation. Recent research has focused on
enhancing CAT bond pricing models by incorporating more realistic features of catastrophe risk
and financial market dynamics. Hofer et al. ([21]) developed a risk-neutral pricing framework for
CAT bonds, extending the basic structure of compound Poisson models to account for time-varying
intensities. Burnecki ([9]) formalizes the structure of contingent convertible catastrophe bonds (Co-
coCats), modeling catastrophe losses with a time-inhomogeneous compound Poisson process and
interest rates using the Longstaff model.
Several numerical methods to pricing CAT bonds have been developed over the years with Monte
Carlo methods and numerical PDE solvers remaining the main tools, especially when analytical
tractability is lost due to model complexity. For example, Reshetar ([36]) models payoff structures
contingent on both catastrophic property losses and catastrophic mortality events, using simulation-
based techniques.
Moreover, Siyamah et al. ([37]) utilized Monte Carlo and quasi-Monte Carlo
simulation techniques for CAT bond valuation, highlighting efficiency gains in estimating expected
losses under stochastic triggers.
Our contribution to the literature on the pricing of CAT bonds is to establish a novel deep
learning-based valuation approach where the relationship between model and contract features
and the bond price is learned by a deep feedforward neural network under a compound Poisson
framework, akin to the model introduced in [40]. The input features include both model-specific
parameters, such as the intensity and frequency of catastrophic events, and contract specifications,
including maturity, trigger threshold, and coupon frequency.
In our approach, CAT bond prices are initially computed using Monte Carlo simulation enhanced
with importance sampling to reduce variance and computational efficiency. These simulated prices
are then used to train neural networks to approximate the pricing function. Through an extensive
empirical study, we demonstrate that our neural network-based pricing method achieves high accu-
racy and significantly outperforms state-of-the-art techniques, such as the partial integro-differential

5. Conclusion
In this work, we proposed a novel framework for the valuation of CAT bonds using deep learning
techniques. Building on a doubly stochastic Poisson process framework for modeling catastrophe
events, in Section 3.2 we first developed efficient Monte Carlo simulation methods enhanced by im-
portance sampling to estimate CAT bond prices under various loss distributions. These simulations
provide high-quality training data for fully connected feedforward neural networks that approximate
the CAT bond pricing function, see Section 4.2.
Our empirical results from Section 4.3 demonstrate that neural networks trained on simulated
data can predict CAT bond prices with high accuracy and significantly improved computational
efficiency compared to traditional pricing techniques, including Monte Carlo even in combination
with importance sampling and PIDE solvers. In particular, the neural network models allow for
the almost instant computation of prices several orders of magnitude faster than simulation-based
approaches while maintaining accuracy. Furthermore, with Proposition 3.1 we provided theoretical
justification for the use of neural networks based on the universal approximation theorem and
analyzed their sensitivity to key model inputs, offering practical insights into risk assessment and
model interpretability.
Overall, our approach offers a scalable and accurate alternative to conventional CAT bond pricing
methods, making it especially valuable in real-time risk management and portfolio optimization
contexts.
