Abstract—Sound field estimation methods based on kernel
ridge regression have proven effective, allowing for strict en-
forcement of physical properties, in addition to the inclusion
of prior knowledge such as directionality of the sound field.
These methods have been formulated for single-frequency sound
fields, restricting the types of data and prior knowledge that can
be used. In this paper, the kernel ridge regression approach is
generalized to consider discrete-time sound fields. The proposed
method provides time-domain sound field estimates that can
be computed in closed form, are guaranteed to be physically
realizable, and for which time-domain properties of the sound
fields can be exploited to improve estimation performance.
Exploiting prior information on the time-domain behaviour of
room impulse responses, the estimation performance of the
proposed method is shown to be improved using a time-domain
data weighting, demonstrating the usefulness of the proposed
approach. It is further shown using both simulated and real data
that the time-domain data weighting can be combined with a
directional weighting, exploiting prior knowledge of both spatial
and temporal properties of the room impulse responses. The
theoretical framework of the proposed method enables solving
a broader class of sound field estimation problems using kernel
ridge regression where it would be required to consider the time-
domain response rather than the frequency-domain response of
each frequency separately.
Index Terms—Sound field estimation, moving microphone,
kernel ridge regression, reproducing kernel Hilbert space, regu-
larization

As a consequence of the undersampling, sound field estimation
is an ill-posed inverse problem, requiring prior knowledge to
achieve satisfactory estimation performance.
A sound field produced by a spatially localized source in a
room emitting an impulse is referred to as a room impulse
response (RIR) function, and such a function evaluated in
a single point is referred to as a RIR. RIR functions are
of particular interest due to their importance in sound field
reproduction applications. RIR functions have a common
structure that can be exploited [4]–[8]. Although such sound
fields are mostly assumed to be stationary, the sound field is
slowly time-varying [9]–[12]. The time-variation means that
the quality of previous estimates decreases as time passes,
leading to a need to re-measure, which in turn requires the
sound field estimation process to be practical.
A number of different approaches exist for sound field
estimation, where different types of prior knowledge are
exploited. If there exists a basis in which the sound field
is sparse, the sound field can be reconstructed from fewer
measurements, which is exploited in [13]–[16]. A Bayesian
approach allows for the inclusion of prior knowledge in
the form of a probabilistic model [17], [18]. Data-driven
approaches have become more popular, which rely on prior
knowledge extracted from data [19]–[23]. The sound field can
also be modelled using a set of equivalent sources, with which
prior knowledge in the form of sparsity [24] or an optimal
mass transport regularization [25] can be exploited.
The approach considered in this paper is based on kernel
ridge regression (KRR) [26]. KRR allows for optimization
over a function space constrained to satisfy the Helmholtz
equation, i.e. contains only physically realizable sound fields
[27]. The KRR approach has been shown to be practical
to apply to spatial active noise control [28]–[30], sound
zone control [31] and sound field reproduction [32], in part
due to the estimator being linear as a function of the data,
which leads to practical algorithms when the estimators are
integrated into the control methods. The KRR approach has
been able to include more information about the sound field
through a directional weighting [30], [31], [33], [34]. The
KRR approach has also been shown to be closely related to
Bayesian inference of spherical harmonic wave functions [35]–
[39], as well as Gaussian process regression [40]–[44]. The
strength of the approach lies in the representer theorems [45]–
[50], which characterize the optimal solutions of a large class
of KRR problems, reducing infinite-dimensional optimization
problems to finite-dimensional problems.

[33], [34], [37] consider single-frequency sound fields. This
restricts the set of problems that can be solved to those which
can be formulated for each frequency individually, in addition
to limiting the type of prior knowledge that can be used.
Although a continuous-time formulation was proposed in [51],
in contrast to other kernel-based methods, no closed form
solution is available, making the method costly to use. In
addition, the obtained solution only satisfies the wave equation
approximately by minimizing a physics-informed cost, rather
than guaranteeing that such a constraint is exactly satisfied.
A generalization of the KRR approach is proposed in this
paper, with which discrete-time sound fields in continuous
space are modelled. The proposed method provides estimates
which are guaranteed to satisfy the wave equation with a kernel
that is obtained in closed form. Using the discrete Fourier
transform (DFT), a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS)
modelling discrete-time sound fields is derived. The model
allows for the inclusion of prior knowledge both relating to
the time-domain and frequency-domain properties of the sound
field. This is shown to be useful for the identification of
RIRs, which have a high degree of predictable time-domain
behaviour.
The paper makes a theoretical contribution, by constructing
sound field models and function spaces with which sound
field-related optimization problems beyond those considered
in the paper can be solved with KRR. Compared to previous
methods, the generalization to time-domain sound fields allows
for a broader class of problems to be considered using KRR.
Such problems include those where the sound field at multiple
frequencies must be considered jointly, or where the time-
domain response is of crucial importance.
The structure of the paper is as follows. In section II the
sound field estimation problem is introduced, with a model
of the available data. In section III the sound field model is
introduced, and a RKHS modelling time-domain sound fields
is defined. In section IV the kernel ridge regression problem
is defined and solved, producing a closed form expression for
the optimal sound field estimate. In section V the inclusion
of prior knowledge is considered, introducing a directionally
weighted regularization, and a data-weighting using the time-
domain properties of the RIRs. In section VI the proposed
method is evaluated in a number of experiments. Finally the
paper is concluded in section VII. To facilitate easy use of
the results, code associated with the proposed method is made
available at github.com/sounds-research/aspcol.

VII. CONCLUSION
A sound field estimation method has been presented, with
which time-domain sound fields can be estimated using kernel
ridge regression. The method is a generalization of previous
kernel ridge regression methods, allowing for the inclusion
of prior knowledge relating to time-domain properties of the
sound field. A data weighting has been proposed, which can
improve estimation performance especially in noisy condi-
tions, by considering the time-domain characteristics of the
RIR as prior knowledge. The weighting has been shown to
be effectively modelled by only the propagation delay and
reverberation time, which is information that can be obtained
in practice. In addition, the data weighting has been shown
to be even more effective when combined with a directional
weighting, which exploits spatial characteristics of the sound
field.
