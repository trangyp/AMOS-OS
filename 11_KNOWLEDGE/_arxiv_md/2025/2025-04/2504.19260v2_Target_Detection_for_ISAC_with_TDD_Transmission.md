Abstract—Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) poses
various challenges that arise from the communication-centric
design of cellular networks. One of them is target detection with
time division duplex (TDD) transmission used in current 5G and
future 6G deployments, where the periodic on-off behavior of the
transmitter creates impulsive sidelobes in the radar point spread
function (PSF). These can be mistaken for actual targets by
conventional peak detection techniques, leading to false alarms.
In this work, we first analytically describe the range-Doppler
PSF due to TDD windowing. We then propose a computationally
efficient method that leverages the PSF to distinguish impulsive
sidelobes from valid target peaks. Simulation results and outdoor
drone measurements with an ISAC proof of concept demonstrate
the capability of our algorithm, showing that it can achieve
reliable target detection while limiting false alarms.
I. INTRODUCTION
Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) is expected to
be one of the key new features of future sixth generation
(6G) networks [1], [2]. Ongoing discussions within the 3rd
Generation Partnership Project (3GPP), e. g., a feasibility
study [3] and one on channel modeling [4], indicate that ISAC
is increasingly becoming a reality.
However, there is still a plethora of research challenges to
be addressed. One fundamental question is how to seamlessly
integrate sensing into legacy communications systems that
were originally designed solely for this purpose. One example
is the current prevalence of time division duplex (TDD) with
periodical alternation between downlink (DL) and uplink (UL)
transmission. In base station-based sensing in the DL, this
leads to empty UL symbols acting as time domain windowing
to the sensing signal. As we will show in Section III, the
periodic nature of these acquisition holes creates impulsive
sidelobes along the velocity (Doppler) axis of the periodogram.
These sidelobes cannot be suppressed with conventional win-
dowing (e. g., Chebyshev) and can be wrongly identified as
true targets by traditional peak detection methods, e. g., based
on cell averaging constant false alarm rate (CA-CFAR). This
calls for algorithms that can reliably discriminate valid peaks
from impulsive sidelobes caused by TDD windowing.
To detect target contributions in the channel state infor-
mation (CSI), many variations of the well-known algorithms
CLEAN [5] and Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP) [6] have
been proposed in literature. On a high level, both work by iter-
atively removing strong signal contributions to detect weaker

Recently, the authors of [7] utilized CLEAN for WiFi sensing,

However, those prior art techniques were typically not
employed to decide whether peaks are due to targets or ar-

particular problem introduced by ISAC with TDD transmission
thus necessitates a solution that goes beyond the iterative
detection and removal of peaks and also checks their validity.
To close this gap, we introduce a target detection method
for ISAC with non-uniformly sampled data due to TDD
transmission. Our algorithm uses the analytically derived two-
dimensional (2D) point spread function (PSF) including TDD
effects to account for the time domain windowing and allows
peaks due to impulsive sidelobes to be identified. The main
contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
• We analytically derive the 2D PSF with TDD windowing
and finite sampling in both time and frequency domain.
• Leveraging the 2D PSF and additional signal processing
techniques, we propose an efficient method to distinguish
true peaks from impulsive sidelobes and other artifacts.
• Based on this approach, we perform iterative peak detec-
tion using a CLEAN-type procedure.
• We validate our approach both with Monte Carlo ex-
periments and measurement data obtained from outdoor
drone detection experiments with an ISAC demonstrator.

V. SIMULATION RESULTS
We investigate the capability of our proposal with Monte
Carlo simulations. The monostatic sensing setup according
to the system model in Section II uses the radio frequency
(RF) parameters listed in Table I that are based on our ISAC
PoC [9]. In each of the 10000 trials per noise power point,
|P| ∈{1, 3} targets are placed in the environment with random
ranges rp ∼U(10 m, 100 m) and speeds vp ∼U(−5 m
s , 5 m
s )
relative to the system. Moreover, their complex coefficients αp
are generated according to a Rician distribution with ν = 2
and σ = 1 and include attenuation due to free space path loss.
To evaluate the detection capabilities, we investigate the
missed detection probability PMD and the F1 score as a metric
that also takes false alarms into account. A detected peak
is considered to be a true detection if its 2D range-speed
estimation error lies within the theoretical resolution limit (in
the Euclidean sense) of the ground truth. The probability of
false alarm of the CA-CFAR detector used is 10−6.
We compare our solution to the following baselines:
PER Conventional. The most straightforward baseline is
periodogram processing using the full CSI matrix as described
in IV-A with CA-CFAR detection, i. e., no peak checking.

VII. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we presented an algorithm for target detection
in ISAC that can cope with impulsive sidelobes due to TDD
transmission. We analytically derived the 2D range-Doppler
PSF, which we use to distinguish valid peaks from impulsive
sidelobes. Simulations showed that our approach achieves
good target detection capabilities and improves the F1 score
over the considered baselines by limiting false alarms. Further-
more, we validated our method with drone measurements from
our ISAC PoC. In the future, we plan to validate our approach
with more extensive measurements and address distortions due
to other standard constraints or suboptimal hardware.
