ABSTRACT
Many post-AGB star binaries are observed to have relatively high orbital eccentricities (up to 0.6).
Recently, AC Her was observed to have a polar-aligned circumbinary disk. We perform hydrodynamic
simulations to explore the impact of a polar-aligned disk on the eccentricity of a binary. For a binary
system with central masses of 0.73 M⊙and 1.4 M⊙, we find that a disk with a total mass of 0.1 M⊙
can enhance the binary eccentricity from 0.2 to 0.7 within 5000 years, or from 0.01 to 0.65 within 15000
years. Even if the disk mass is as low as 0.01 M⊙, the binary eccentricity grows within our simulation
time while the system remains stable. These eccentricity variations are associated with the variations
of the inclination between the disk and the binary orbit due to von Zeipel-Kozai-Lidov oscillations.
The oscillations eventually damp and leave the binary eccentricity at a high value. The numerical
results are in good agreement with analytical estimates. In addition, we examine the AC-Her system
and find that the disk mass should be on the order of 10−3 M⊙for the disk to remain polar.
Keywords: Accretion – Stellar accretion disks – Post-asymptotic giant branch stars – Binary stars
1. INTRODUCTION
Many post–asymptotic giant branch (AGB) stars host
a stable Keplerian disk (de Ruyter et al. 2006; Bujarra-
bal et al. 2013; Gallardo Cava et al. 2021).
All the
post-AGB stars with disks are found in binaries and the
disks are all circumbinary disks (Bujarrabal et al. 2013;
Izzard & Jermyn 2018). The orbital periods of the bi-
naries range from tens to thousands of days (Oomen
et al. 2018). For the binaries with periods shorter than
1000 d, it is likely that they have undergone a common
envelope phase (Izzard et al. 2012). During this phase,
the binary transfers orbital energy and angular momen-
tum to the envelope due to tidal dissipation and friction
(Iben & Livio 1993). This interaction causes the enve-
lope to be ejected, forming a circumbinary disk (Kashi
& Soker 2011; Chen et al. 2017; Izzard & Jermyn 2023),
while the binary orbit becomes circularized (Zahn 1977;
Shu & Lubow 1981).
This process is challenged by the discovery of many
post-AGB binaries with high eccentricities, some as
large as 0.4 and a few even up to 0.6 (Oomen et al. 2018).
It has been suggested that such high eccentricity could
be driven by gravitational torques associated with the
accreting gas (Sepinsky et al. 2007; Dermine et al. 2013;
Rafikov 2016; Mu˜noz et al. 2019; Oomen et al. 2020;
D’Orazio & Duffell 2021; Zrake et al. 2021). However,
the models in these works have their limitations, such as
most of them require a massive long-lived circumbinary
disk (Dermine et al. 2013; Rafikov 2016).
Moreover,
all of these works assume a coplanar circumbinary disk.
Many recent works have revealed that the circumbinary
disk could be stable in a high inclination with respect to
the binary if the binary is in a non-circular orbit (Aly
et al. 2015; Martin & Lubow 2017, 2018; Lubow & Mar-
tin 2018; Zanazzi & Lai 2018; Martin & Lubow 2019;
Smallwood et al. 2019; Cuello & Giuppone 2019). In
this work, we explore the impact of a polar-aligned disk
on binary eccentricity, providing a new perspective on
the mechanisms driving high eccentricities.
Observations suggest that misalignments may be com-
mon between a binary orbit and its circumbinary disk
(e.g., Chiang & Murray-Clay 2004; Winn et al. 2004;
Capelo et al. 2012; Kennedy et al. 2012; Brinch et al.
2016; Aly et al. 2018; Aronow et al. 2018; Czekala et al.
2019; Kenworthy et al. 2022). One interesting case is the
AC-Her system, which hosts a polar-aligned circumbi-
nary disk and potentially a polar-aligned planet (Hillen
et al. 2015; Gallardo Cava et al. 2021; Anugu et al. 2023;
Martin et al. 2023). While the binary orbit is well de-
termined, the total disk mass and size remain debated.
These features are crucial since a polar-aligned circumbi-

If the disk is significantly radially extended, its outer re-

model explores the role of the circumbinary disk on the

the AC-Her system. Finally, we draw our conclusions in
Section 5.
2. PROBLEM SETUP
To explore the effects of polar-aligned circumbinary
disks on the binary, we run hydrodynamic simulations
using the smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH; e.g.,
Price 2012) code phantom (Lodato & Price 2010; Price
& Federrath 2010; Price et al. 2018). phantom has been
widely used for simulations of misaligned accretion disks
in binary systems (e.g., Nixon 2012; Nixon et al. 2013;
Facchini et al. 2013; Martin et al. 2014; Fu et al. 2015;
Franchini et al. 2019; Nealon et al. 2020). We set up the
simulation based on the observational results of the AC
Her system (Hillen et al. 2015; Anugu et al. 2023; Mar-
tin et al. 2023). The units we use for mass, time, and
length are M⊙, years, and AU, respectively. The masses
of the post-AGB star and the companion star in the AC
Her system are M1 = 0.73 M⊙and M2 = 1.4 M⊙, re-
spectively. The binary semi-major axis is ab = 2.83 AU,
and the eccentricity is e = 0.206. The inclination of the
circumbinary disk with respect to the binary is observed
to be ibd = 96.◦5 with error bars that allow for a polar-
aligned disk (ibd = 90◦). In our simulations, the binary
stars are treated as two sink particles each with a sink
radius of 0.5 AU. Any gas particle within 0.5 AU of ei-
ther star is directly accreted by the star. The mass and
angular momentum of an accreted particle are added to
the sink particle (Bate et al. 1995). This is a relatively
large sink radius since we focus on the circumbinary disk
and are not simulating the small disks around each star
(see Artymowicz & Lubow 1996; Mu˜noz et al. 2019, 2020
for more details).
We assume that the disk is initially polar-aligned to
the binary, i.e., ibd = 90◦with the disk angular mo-
mentum vector aligned to the binary eccentricity vec-
tor.
The disk initially extends from rin = 9 AU to
rout = 50 AU with a radial density profile following a
power law Σ = Σ0r−p where the index p = 1.5 and Σ0
is defined by the total disk mass. The disk expands in-
wards and outwards over time. The inner parts of the
disc reach a quasi-equilibrium state within a few binary
orbits, while our simulations run for 6000 binary orbits.

For the lower viscosity case (run 10), directly reducing α
in SPH simulations can introduce numerical challenges.
Instead, we reduce the disk scale height H to achieve a
lower effective viscosity in the simulation, as the kine-
matic viscosity is given by ν = αcsH. The binary ec-
centricity, disk inclination relative to the binary, and the
disk mass evolutions are shown in Figure 6.
In Figure 6, for the high-viscosity case (green line,
run 11), the rapid dissipation of the disk prevents the
binary eccentricity from reaching the same maximum
value observed in the standard case (blue line, run 6).
Even so, we might gain insight into how the ZKL os-
cillation ends.
As expected, the binary’s eccentricity
remains essentially constant after the disk loses most of
its mass.
For the low-viscosity case (run 10), the initial mass
loss is much lower. However, the accretion rate under-
goes a transition at a time of about t = 2000 years,
which is due to the disk breaking. When the viscosity
is low, the circumbinary disk inclination varies with ra-
dius due to the strong gravitational torques from the
binary. The communication timescale through the disc
is longer than the precession timescale and the disk
breaks into two disjoint sections with different inclina-
tions. As shown in Figure 7, the disk breaks at a radius
of r ≈20 AU (see e.g. Papaloizou & Pringle 1983; Pa-
paloizou & Lin 1995; Ogilvie 1999; Lodato & Price 2010;
Nixon & King 2012, 2016; Nealon et al. 2016; Young
et al. 2023; Rabago et al. 2024 for discussions on disk
warping and breaking). Following the break, the outer
disk remains almost fixed, while the inner disk under-
goes faster precession, as depicted in the middle panel
of Figure 6 (orange lines). Since the inner disk is closer
to the binary, its effect dominates the binary evolution.
Consequently, the binary’s eccentricity oscillates on a
shorter timescale.

of the disk. Hillen et al. (2015) reported the outer ra-

disk’s angular momentum, the total angular momen-

disk’s stability. Therefore, it is worth examining how
much the total disk’s angular momentum will change

radius of 9 AU.

5. CONCLUSIONS
Motivated by the recent observation of a polar-aligned
disc in AC Her, we have examined the interaction be-
tween a post-AGB star binary and a polar-aligned cir-
cumbinary disk. Our findings indicate that large binary
eccentricities can be driven by massive disks, potentially
explaining some of the high eccentricities observed in
post-AGB binaries. Specifically, a disk with an initial
mass of 0.1 M⊙can efficiently enhance the binary eccen-
tricity, reaching a maximum of 0.7. Even when starting
from a very low initial eccentricity (e.g., e0 = 0.01), it
can still grow, although on a longer timescale. The bi-
nary eccentricity subsequently oscillates along with the
disk inclination relative to the binary due to ZKL oscilla-
tions. These oscillations eventually cease due to viscous
dissipation in the disk, resulting in a relatively large bi-
nary eccentricity that could be any value between the
oscillation minimum and maximum.
We also performed an evolution study for the disk
mass. We found that the normalized mass loss rates are
almost identical regardless of the initial disk mass, in-
dicating that disks with larger masses lose mass more
quickly. High-viscosity disks exhibit a rapid mass loss,
leading to the cessation of oscillations before the eccen-
tricity reaches its peak. In contrast, mass loss occurs
more slowly in low-viscosity disks, but these disks are
prone to disk warping.
However, it is challenging to
determine the exact viscosity threshold below which a
disk will break. Therefore, each case must be analyzed
individually.
We explored the mass limit of the disk in the AC-Her
system. A large disk mass would be unstable for polar
alignment and induce ZKL oscillation. Our simulation,
based on an initial mass of 8.1 × 10−4 M⊙as suggested
by Gallardo Cava et al. (2021), shows that this disk
mass is preferable because the disk inclination relative
to the binary remains stable within the observational
error range proposed by Martin et al. (2023). Further-
more, we found that the total angular momentum only
increases by 2 ∼4 times if the disk extends to 1000 AU
from 50 AU. Despite this change, the disk could still re-
main stable. Thus, we conclude that the total disk mass
should be on the order of 10−3 M⊙and that the disk
could extend out to 1000 AU in the AC-Her system.
