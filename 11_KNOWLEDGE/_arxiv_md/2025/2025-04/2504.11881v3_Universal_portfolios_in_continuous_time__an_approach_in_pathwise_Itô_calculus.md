an approach in pathwise Itˆo calculus
Xiyue Han∗and Alexander Schied∗
First version: April 16, 2025
this version: August 25, 2025
Abstract
We provide a simple and straightforward approach to a continuous-time version of Cover’s
universal portfolio strategies within the model-free context of F¨ollmer’s pathwise Itˆo calculus.
We establish the existence of the universal portfolio strategy and prove that its portfolio value
process is the average of all values of constant rebalanced strategies.
This result relies on a
systematic comparison between two alternative descriptions of self-financing trading strategies
within pathwise Itˆo calculus. We moreover provide a comparison result for the performance and
the realized volatility and variance of constant rebalanced portfolio strategies.

Introduction
In mainstream finance, the price evolution of a risky asset is usually modeled as a stochastic process
defined on some probability space. It is hence subject to model uncertainty. In some situations, how-
ever, it is possible to construct continuous-time strategies on a path-by-path basis and without making
any probabilistic assumptions on the asset price evolution. For instance, such a model-free approach
is possible for constant-proportion portfolio insurance (CPPI), which the second author investigated
while discussing the paper [19] with Rudi Zagst at TU M¨unchen. The results of this investigation were
eventually published in [16]. The present paper is a continuation of the strictly pathwise approach
taken in [16] and some later follow-up work such as [17, 18]. The pathwise, model-free approach to
continuous-time finance rests itself on the seminal work of F¨ollmer [9, 10] and its application to option
pricing by Bick and Willinger [3] and Lyons [13]. Recent work on model-free finance includes, for
instance, Bartl et al. [2], Chiu and Cont [6], Karatzas and Kim [12], and Allan, Cuchiero, Liu, and
Pr¨omel [1].
In Section 2, we carry out a systematic comparison between two possible descriptions of continuous-
time trading strategies in F¨ollmer’s pathwise Itˆo calculus. On the one hand, there are self-financing
trading strategies that describe the number of shares of each asset that are held at time t. On the other
hand, there are portfolio strategies that describe which percentages of the available capital is currently
invested into the various assets. Proposition 2.1, our first result, states that both descriptions are
basically equivalent under a no-short-sales constraint. Examples 2.2 and 2.3 discuss constant rebalanced
portfolio strategies and CPPI, respectively.

Section 3 contains a construction of a continuous-time version of the universal portfolio strategy,
which was introduced by Cover in [7]. Since Cover’s original construction in discrete time is model-
free, it is natural to attempt a similar construction within the model-free framework of pathwise Itˆo
calculus. Earlier approaches to this question were given by Jamshidian [11] in a setup in which prices are
modeled by Itˆo processes and by Cuchiero, Schachermayer, and Wong [8], where a general class of Cover-
type strategies was obtained by averaging over certain functionally-generated portfolios. Subsequently,
Allan, Cuchiero, Liu, and Pr¨omel [1] constructed pathwise versions of Cover’s universal portfolio for sets
of admissible portfolios, which are controlled paths in the rough path sense. Here, we restrict ourselves
to Cover’s original idea and the simple setting of F¨ollmer’s pathwise Itˆo calculus, which avoids the
intricacies of rough paths theory. In this simple framework, we provide a straightforward construction
of the continuous-time universal portfolio strategy by averaging over all constant rebalanced strategies.
Our main result is Theorem 3.1, which establishes the existence of the universal portfolio strategy and
establishes its portfolio value process as the average of all values of constant rebalanced strategies.
In Section 4, we investigate further properties of constant rebalanced portfolio strategies in our
model-free context of pathwise Itˆo calculus. Part (a) of Theorem 4.1 shows that a constant rebalanced
portfolio strategy beats the weighted geometric mean of the underlying assets, a formula that can be
regarded as an Itˆo analogue of the classical inequality between the weighted arithmetic and geometric
means. Theorem 4.1 also provides upper bounds on the realized variance and the realized volatility of
the constant rebalanced portfolio strategy. These bounds are given in terms of the weighted averages
of the realized variance and volatility of the underlying assets, respectively.
For the convenience of the reader, some notation, terminology, and preliminary results on pathwise
Itˆo calculus are collected in Section 5.

The universal portfolio strategy
Universal portfolios were introduced in discrete time by Cover [7]. Jamshidian [11] provided a continuous-
time approach in a setup in which prices are modeled by Itˆo processes. Cuchiero et al. [8] introduced
a general class of Cover-type strategies in continuous time by averaging over classes of functionally
generated portfolios. Subsequently, Allan et al. [1] constructed pathwise versions of Cover’s universal
portfolio for sets of admissible portfolios, which are controlled paths in the rough path sense. Here,
we restrict ourselves to Cover’s original idea and the simple setting of F¨ollmer’s pathwise Itˆo calculus,
which avoids the intricacies of rough paths theory. In this framework, we provide a straightforward
construction of universal portfolios in continuous time that is based on constant rebalanced strategies
and is thus a direct continuous-time analogue of the discrete-time case in [7]. The following theorem
