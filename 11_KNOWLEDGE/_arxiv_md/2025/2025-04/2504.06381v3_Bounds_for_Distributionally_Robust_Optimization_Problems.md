We study distributionally robust optimization (DRO) problems with uncertainty sets consisting of high-
dimensional random vectors that are close in the multivariate Wasserstein distance to a reference random
vector. We give conditions when the images of these sets under scalar-valued aggregation functions are
contained in and contain uncertainty sets of univariate random variables defined via a univariate Wasserstein
distance. This provides lower and upper bounds for the solution to general multivariate DRO problems that
are computationally tractable. Furthermore, we generalize the results to uncertainty sets characterized by
Bregman-Wasserstein divergences, which allows for asymmetric deviations from the reference random vector.
Moreover, for DRO problems with risk measure criterion in the class of signed Choquet integrals, we derive
semi-analytic formulae for the upper and lower bounds and the distribution that attains these bounds.
Key words : distributionally robust optimization, Wasserstein distance, Bregman-Wasserstein divergence,
signed Choquet integral

Early research in DRO focuses on linear programs with ellipsoidal uncertainty sets (Ben-Tal
and Nemirovski (1998, 1999)). Here we work, among others, with uncertainty sets defined by
a Wasserstein distance constraint. The Wasserstein distance is a well studied metric originating
from the field of optimal transport (Villani (1973)). The metric is now widely used outside of
the theory of optimal transport, with important applications in mathematics, probability, and
statistical theory (Panaretos and Zemel (2019), Dobrushin (1970), Munk and Czado (1998)). More
recent works discussing applications of the Wasserstein distance to DRO problems include Gao and
Kleywegt (2023), who focus exclusively on the Wasserstein distance, Blanchet and Murphy (2019),
who consider optimal transport distances associated with lower semi-continuous cost functions,
and Pesenti and Jaimungal (2023), who study portfolio allocation. In our work, we consider both
the Wasserstein distance and an asymmetric generalization of the Wasserstein distance known as
the Bregman-Wasserstein (BW) divergence. The BW divergence was first introduced by Carlier
and Jimenez (2007), its geometry is discussed in Kainth et al. (2025), and BW uncertainty sets are
studied in Guo et al. (2017), Pesenti and Vanduffel (2024), and Pesenti et al. (2024a).
In this work, we focus on the inner optimization problem in (2) and assume that the risk factor
X is multivariate. Specifically, we consider uncertainty sets defined using multivariate Wasserstein
distance and multivariate BW divergence constraints. DRO problems involving the multivariate
Wasserstein distance are challenging because explicit formulas of the Wasserstein distance between
distributions are only known for a few distributions such as the Gaussian (Dowson and Landau
(1982), Gelbrich (1990)). Numerical algorithms, e.g., the Sinkhorn algorithm, can be used in mul-
tivariate settings, but it is well-known that the computational cost significantly increases with the
dimension of the problem (Cuturi (2013)). We contribute to the literature by proposing upper
and lower bounds to the inner optimization problem of (2) that depend only on the univariate
Wasserstein distance, regardless of the dimension of the original problem. Moreover, for specific
cases we provide semi-analytic formulae for these bounds and identify cases when the upper and
lower bounds coincide. Furthermore, we consider uncertainty sets characterized via the BW diver-
gence and derive lower and upper bounds for multivariate DRO problems. For some choices of

We illustrate our lower and upper bounds on risk-aware DRO problems, where the expected value
in (2) is replaced by a law-invariant risk functional ρ (also called risk measure). Risk functionals
are well studied in the literature. One of the earliest and most significant classes of risk functionals
is the class of coherent risk measures, which was first introduced in Artzner et al. (1999). This class
includes the expected value and the popular Expected Shortfall (ES). Over the past two decades,
various generalizations have been proposed, including convex risk measures (F¨ollmer and Schied
(2002)), generalized deviation measures (Rockafellar et al. (2006)), and the characterization of law-
invariant coherent risk measures (Kusuoka (2001)). More recently, Wang et al. (2020) provided an
extensive study of signed Choquet integrals, which are a large class of risk functionals subsuming
the well-known class of distortion risk measures. DRO problems with distortion risk measures are
for example studied in Bernard et al. (2024), Cai et al. (2023), Coache and Jaimungal (2024),
and Moresco et al. (2024). Bernard et al. (2024) focus on Wasserstein distance constraints for
univariate risks, Cai et al. (2023) consider moment based constraints for multivariate risks, and
Coache and Jaimungal (2024) and Moresco et al. (2024) work with robust dynamic risk measures.
In our work, we consider multivariate risk factors and any law-invariant risk functional, and provide
an application of our results to the class of signed Choquet integrals.
The paper is structured as follows. Section 2 introduces the notation, while Section 3 is devoted
to the inner optimization problem in (2) for law-invariant risk functionals when U is a multivariate
Wasserstein uncertainty set and ℓis a Lipschitz continuous function. In Section 4, we generalize
our results to the case when U is a BW uncertainty set. Section 5 contains explicit bounds when
the risk functional is a signed Choquet integral, and Section 6 illustrates numerical examples.

We first consider the class of K-Lipschitz continuous functions. Recall that a function f : Rn →R
is K-Lipschitz (wrt the La norm) if |f(x) −f(y)| ≤K ∥x −y∥a for all x,y ∈Rn. Our first main

tolerance distance of Kε – the product of the Lipschitz constant and the tolerance distance ε of the

important for fairness assessment (Dwork et al. (2012)), as well as ubiquitous in machine learning.

Lipschitz activation functions (e.g., ReLU, SoftPlus, sigmoid) is itself Lipschitz continuous. We
refer to Virmaux and Scaman (2018), Jordan and Dimakis (2020), and Kim et al. (2021) for details
and discussions on Lipschitz constants of neural networks.

Here we consider uncertainty sets defined via a BW divergence, which is a generalization of the
Wasserstein distance. Specifically, we consider uncertainty sets defined as all random vectors that
have a BW divergence of at most ε around a reference random vector.
Key results are in Section 4.2, where we generalize Theorem 1 to BW divergences with separable

Bregman generators that are compositions of a univariate generator and a scalar valued aggregation

Conclusion
We prove that the image of multivariate Wasserstein uncertainty sets under Lipschitz aggregation
functions are contained in and contain Wasserstein uncertainty sets of univariate rvs. This provides
lower and upper bound to risk-aware DRO problems with law-invariant risk functionals. We show
that for special cases of aggregation functions the bounds become equalities. We generalize the
results to uncertainty sets characterized by the Bregman-Wasserstein divergences and illustrate
the bounds on the class of signed Choquet integrals.
