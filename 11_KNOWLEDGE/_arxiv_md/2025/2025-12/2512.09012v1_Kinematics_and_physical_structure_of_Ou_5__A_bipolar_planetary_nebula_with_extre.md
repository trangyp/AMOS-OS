ABSTRACT
We present new deep imaging and high-resolution spectroscopy of the extreme-abundance-discrepancy planetary nebula Ou 5,
together with photoionization modelling aimed at probing its unusual thermal and chemical structure. The nebula exhibits a
nested bipolar morphology, including inner and outer shells, faint outer lobes, and polar knots. Remarkably, all these components
share a dynamical age of order 104 yr. Thermal broadening of the H𝛼line relative to heavier ions implies a hydrogen temperature
3000 K to 6000 K, in contrast to the ∼104 K derived from collisionally excited line diagnostics. This provides independent
support for the presence of at least two distinct temperature/metallicity phases, as previously proposed to explain extreme
abundance discrepancies. Photoionization models with sinusoidally varying metallicity successfully reproduce the observed
nebular spectrum and morphology. A mixture of fluctuations with both extreme and moderate metallicity contrasts is required to
simultaneously fit the O ii and the [O iii] observations. The nebular He ii emission demands a hotter and more luminous central
star than previously inferred, consistent with a ≈0.58 M⊙post-AGB progenitor evolving toward a CO white dwarf. Ou 5 thus
reinforces the link between close-binary nuclei and extreme abundance discrepancies, and provides a valuable benchmark for
understanding how common-envelope ejections give rise to the thermal and abundance inhomogeneities observed in planetary
nebulae.

individual (Ou 5) – stars: AGB and post-AGB
1 INTRODUCTION
Planetary nebulae (PNe) are formed when low- to intermediate-mass
stars eject their envelopes during late stages of evolution. In the
canonical picture, a dense slow wind from the asymptotic giant
branch (AGB) is swept up by a subsequent fast wind, leading to
an ionized shell (Kwok et al. 1978; Kahn & West 1985). While
this framework has been broadly successful, many observed features
remain challenging to explain, including the wide diversity of mor-
phologies and the prevalence of highly collimated outflows (for a
review, see López 2022). Mounting evidence suggests that binary in-
teractions, and in particular common-envelope (CE) evolution, play
a key role in producing these structures (e.g. Nordhaus & Blackman
2006; De Marco 2009; Jones & Boffin 2017).

Ou 5 (IPHASXJ211420.0+434136) is a striking example of a PN
with a post-CE binary central star. It was first identified in the IPHAS
survey (Drew et al. 2005) and independently rediscovered by Nicolas
Outters (Acker et al. 2012). The central binary, confirmed as an
eclipsing system with a 8.74 h orbital period, was studied by Corradi
et al. (2014) and Jones et al. (2022). Ou 5’s main shell shows a
complex bipolar morphology with truncated inner lobes, faint outer
extensions, and filamentary cusps, making it an excellent candidate
for probing the dynamical effects of a CE ejection.
Spectroscopic studies have also revealed that Ou 5 has an extreme
abundance discrepancy factor (ADF), with recombination lines in-
dicating much higher abundances than collisionally excited lines
(Corradi et al. 2015; Wesson et al. 2018). Such high-ADF nebu-
lae are frequently associated with post-CE binaries, but the physical
origin of the discrepancy remains uncertain (see review by García-
Rojas et al. 2019). Ou 5 therefore provides an important test case for
exploring both the morphological imprint of CE evolution and the
thermal/chemical inhomogeneities implied by its spectrum.
In this paper we present new imaging and high-resolution spec-
troscopy of Ou 5, together with photoionization models that explore

2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION
The Observations were carried out over multiple observing runs, as
summarized in Table 1. The data reduction was performed using
standard procedures with IRAF1 (Tody 1986, 1993; Fitzpatrick et al.
2024). The reduction process included bias subtraction, cosmic ray
removal, and wavelength calibration of the two-dimensional spectra.
2.1 Long-slit Spectroscopic Observations
The planetary nebula Ou 5 was observed at the Observatorio As-
tronómico Nacional in San Pedro Mártir, Baja California, Mexico.
Observations were conducted using the Manchester Echelle Spec-
trometer (MES-SPM: Meaburn et al. 2003) mounted on the 2.1 m
telescope in its f/7.5 configuration. The MES-SPM, was operated
in its long-slit echelle mode without a cross-disperser, employing
narrow-band interference filters to isolate individual echelle orders.
Specifically, a 90 E bandwidth filter was used to isolate the 87th
order, covering the H𝛼𝜆6563 and [N ii] 𝜆𝜆6548,84 emission lines,
while a 50 E bandwidth filter covered the 114th order, corresponding
to the [O iii] 𝜆5007 emission line. The detector was a Marconi 2
CCD featuring a 2048×2048 pixels array, with each pixel measuring
13.6 𝜇m on a side. A two-by-two binning was applied during the
observations. The standard slit width was 150 𝜇m, resulting in a pro-
jected slit width 1.9 arcsec, a slit length 5.47 arcmin and a velocity
resolution of 11.8 km s−1 at H𝛼. Additionally, for slit positions b, c
and d a narrower 70 𝜇m wide slit was used, resulting in a projected
slit width 0.95 arcsec and a velocity resolution of 5.9 km s−1 at H𝛼.
The pixel scale along the slit was 0.312 arcsec per pixel. The full-
width half maximum of the seeing was measured as 1.6 ± 0.1 arcsec.
Wavelength calibration was performed using Th-Ar arc lamp spec-
tra, achieving a radial velocity accuracy of approximately ±1 km s−1.
In this study, all presented spectra were corrected to heliocentric
velocity.
The spectroscopic observations covered seven distinct slit posi-
tions. Six consecutive North-South positions (a–f) were stepped
approximately ∼3 arcsec apart across the nebula (position angle
PA = 0◦). An additional east-west-oriented slit position (slit g,
PA = 90◦) was placed centrally across the nebula (see Fig. 1). Each
on-target spectrum was acquired with exposure times of 1800 s. Ta-
ble 1 summarizes these spectroscopic observations.
In addition, MES-SPM was used in its imaging configuration to
obtain two deep 1800 s images of the Ou 5 field employing the H𝛼
𝜆6563 + [N ii] 𝜆𝜆6548,84 and [O iii] 𝜆5007 filters. Fig. 1 shows the
positions of the long slits projected onto the [O iii] image obtained
on August 28, 2017, which reveals the overall morphology of the
nebula.

2.2 Narrow-band imaging
In addition to our longslit spectroscopy, we analyze archival optical
narrow-band imaging observations from the Alhambra Faint Object
Spectrograph and Camera (ALFOSC) of the Nordic Optical Tele-
scope (NOT) in three filters. Images in filters targeting H𝛼𝜆6563
and [O iii] 𝜆5007 from 2013 November 7 were previously described
in Corradi et al. (2014), while images targeting He ii 𝜆4686 from
2016 May 26 are presented here for the first time. The central wave-
lengths (widths) are respectively 6577 E (180 E), 5007 E (30 E), and
4687 E (35 E).
All images were bias-subtracted, astrometrically aligned, and re-
sampled to a common pixel grid. Only one usable exposure (900 s)
was available for [O iii], but we combined two 900 s exposures for
H𝛼and three 1200 s exposures for He ii, with outlier rejection to
eliminate cosmic rays. Fig. 3a shows an RGB image of the bright
core of the nebula that combines the final images in the three filters,
with He ii in red, H𝛼in green, and [O iii] in blue. The H𝛼filter also
admits the [N ii] nebular lines, but their combined brightness is only
6% of H𝛼. Contamination by continuum emission from stars is small
for the [O iii] filter but is more significant for the wider H𝛼filter and
for the He ii filter where the nebula is intrinsically fainter.
To obtain the deepest possible exposure of the faint regions of the
nebula, we co-add the NOT and SPM [O iii] observations. The SPM
image has worse seeing, but this is not important for the more diffuse
outer regions. The result is shown as inverse grayscale in Fig. 3a for
the fainter parts of the image below the 1% [O iii] brightness contour.

3 DISTANCE DETERMINATION
Gaia parallax measurements for the central star of Ou 5 have very
large uncertainties, which means that no usable direct parallax dis-
tance is available (Hernández-Juárez et al. 2024). The most reliable
distance can therefore be obtained by statistical methods based on the
mean trend of surface brightness versus size (e.g., Smith 2015), which
are anchored by reference samples of nebulae with well-determined
distances (Frew et al. 2016). Based on earlier calibrations of the dis-
tance scale, Corradi et al. (2014) found 𝐷= 5.0 ± 1.0 kpc, but we
repeat the calculation here using the most recent calibrations, which
are based on Gaia DR3 parallaxes (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2016,
2023), using the surface brightness of optical recombination lines
(Bucciarelli & Stanghellini 2023) or radio free-free emission (Ali
et al. 2022).
For the nominal angular radius of the nebula, 𝑅, we follow Frew
etal. (2016) by taking the geometricmean ofthe minorand major axes
of the H𝛼isophote contour at 10% of the maximum brightness, which
yields 𝑅= 11.4 ± 1.0 arcsec. We combine this with the observed H𝛼
flux and Balmer decrement from Corradi et al. (2014) to find the
mean extinction-corrected H𝛼surface brightness as 𝑆(H𝛼) = 4.0 ±
2.0 × 10−4 erg s−1 cm−2 sr−1. Taking the average of recent distance
scale calibrations (Frew et al. 2016; Ali et al. 2022; Bucciarelli &
Stanghellini 2023) yields a physical radius 𝑅= 0.22 ± 0.04 pc and
a distance 𝐷= 4.0 ± 0.7 kpc, which is the value that we adopt
throughout this paper. The uncertainty in the derived distance has
roughly equal contributions from observational uncertainties and
from the dispersion of individual nebulae around the mean radius–
brightness trend.
Jones et al. (2022) found a smaller distance of 𝐷= 3.1 ± 0.3 kpc
from simultaneous fitting of the eclipsing binary light curve and
radial velocity curve. Note, however, that this is still marginally con-
sistent with our adopted value. Ou 5 is a post-common-envelope
system and there is some evidence (Frew 2008, Fig. 7.10) that such
nebulae are systematically offset from the mean radius–brightness
trend, which would bring the two methods into closer agreement.
However, preliminary analysis of a much larger sample of post-CE
PNe shows no significant offset from the mean trend for single-star
nebulae (Luna-Niño et al., in preparation).
4 NEBULAR MORPHOLOGY AND KINEMATICS
4.1 Morphological features
From the image presented in Fig. 3a we can extend the analysis of
Corradi et al. (2014, 2015) to arrive at a more complete description
of the different morphological features of the nebula, as illustrated
in Fig. 3b. The bright inner nebula is revealed as having a composite
structure, with an inner equatorial ring in addition to the inner bipolar
lobes. The He ii emission is relatively brighter in the ring, while [O iii]
is brighter in the lobes, with H𝛼showing an intermediate behaviour.
The fainter outer lobes and outer equatorial ring combine to produce
a six-pointed star shape for the 1% isophote of [O iii] (boundary
between colour and greyscale image in Fig. 3a). He ii is not detected
from either the outer lobes or the outer ring. The very faint “fishtail”
structure on the East side (Corradi et al. 2014) is evidence for a third
set of lobes, which we identify as “outermost lobes”. It is notable
that no corresponding structure is seen to the West, but it is possible
that it is too faint to detect, given that the inner and outer lobes also
show mild lateral asymmetries, with the East side being the brighter.
The deep image also shows the morphology of the faint polar knots,
which we detect in our slit spectroscopy. The South knot is relatively
compact, whereas the North knot is much more diffuse. There is an
indication of a faint extension of the outer lobes around the location
of the knots, especially to the South, but this is close to the noise
limit.
Fig. 4 shows profile cuts along the equator and polar axis. The equa-
torial profile (Fig. 4a) shows most clearly the difference between the
emission lines, with He ii peaking at ±4 arcsec and [O iii] peaking at
±7 arcsec. The H𝛼profile is approximately the average of the [O iii]
and He ii profiles. The cuts along the polar axis (Fig. 4b) are much
more similar between the different lines. There is some indication
that [O iii] is more flat-topped than H𝛼and He ii, but contamina-
tion by continuum emission from stars complicates the analysis. To

4.2 Kinematic analysis
Fig. 2 shows the location of the central slit d superimposed on the
SPM [O iii] image, which clearly reveals the bipolar axial symmetry
of Ou5. Several regions of interest are indicated in this figure. Specif-
ically, the blue squares indicate the positions of the polar knots, the
pink rectangles trace the approximate extent of the outer lobes, and
the yellow area highlights the brighter inner lobes. The P-V array
to the right of the image and at the centre of Fig. 2 corresponds to
the bi-dimensional [O iii] spectrum from slit d. The same regions of
interest as in the image are indicated here. Position-velocity images
of all the slits are shown in Fig. 6, which shows that there is very little
variation between the three positions closest to the nebular axis: c, d
and e. We therefore coadd these three slits to improve the signal-to-
noise, with results shown in Fig. 7, with Fig. 8 showing line profiles
extracted from each region of interest.
The systemic velocity of Ou 5 was determined using slit d from the
H𝛼and [O iii] lines. The flux-weighted heliocentric mean velocity
at the projected position of the central star is 𝑉sys = −33 ± 1 km s−1,
which we adopt here as the systemic velocity of the nebular shell.
Note that this systemic velocity differs from the −26 km s−1 center-
of-mass velocity of the binary central star (Jones et al. 2022).
We performed gaussian fits to the different regions of interest along
the axis, with resultant profiles shown in Fig. 8. Two gaussians were
fitted for the core, inner, and outer lobes, with one gaussian fitted for
the polar knots. In general, there is a good agreement between the two
emission lines in the derived velocities of the gaussian components,
although the splitting is consistently larger by about 2 km s−1 in [O iii]
compared with H𝛼.

4.3 Line-of-sight inclination of the nebula axis

the inner lobes is slightly sheared along the slit (Fig. 9). The simplest

tilted from the plane of the sky.

5 PHYSICAL PARAMETERS
In this section we use our spectroscopic data to constrain the physical
conditions of Ou 5. We begin in Section 5.1 by deriving the mean
kinetic temperature of the ionized gas from thermal line broadening,
considering both single-temperature and two-phase interpretations.
We then estimate in Section 5.2 the electron densities and ionized
masses of the principal nebular components from the calibrated H𝛼
surface brightness. Finally, in Section 5.3 we apply the He ii Zanstra
method to determine the effective temperature and luminosity of the
central star.
5.1 Estimate of nebula kinetic temperature
Ou 5 is an example of a class of planetary nebulae with a high abun-
dance discrepancy factor (ADF), in which metal ion abundances de-
rived from recombination lines are systematically higher than those
derived from collisional lines (see García-Rojas et al. 2019 and ref-
erences therein). One potential explanation for these discrepancies is
the coexistence of different “phases” within the ionized nebula with
varying metallicity and physical conditions (Liu et al. 2000). The
presence of warm and cool photoionized phases has been postulated
for Ou 5 (Corradi et al. 2015), with the warm-phase temperature
determined from [O iii] line ratios as 10 150 ± 300 K after correc-
tion for a recombination contribution to the auroral line. For some
other nebulae with high ADF, the Paschen jump or Balmer jump
implies a lower mean temperature for the H recombination spec-
trum than for collisional lines (García-Rojas et al. 2022), but no such
measurements exist for Ou 5. However, an independent method of
estimating the mean temperature of ionized hydrogen in the nebula
is via the excess thermal broadening of hydrogen emission lines, as
compared with lines from heavier ions. Our high resolution spectra
are well-suited to such a measurement.
Several different physical processes contribute to the observed
profile of an emission line 𝐼(𝑣). For lines from ionized nebulae, it is
convenient to divide the processes as follows (see García-Díaz et al.
2008):
1. Instrumental profile, 𝐼ins(𝑣), assumed to be Gaussian with
FWHM given in Table 1.
2. Thermal broadening profile, 𝐼th(𝑣), assumed to be gaussian
with RMS width 𝜎th = (9.1 km s−1)(𝑇4/𝐴)1/2, where 𝑇4 =
𝑇/10 000 K and 𝐴is the atomic mass of the emitting species.
3. Kinematic profile, 𝐼exp(𝑣), due to the systematic expansion of
the nebula.
4. Fine-structure profile, 𝐼fs(𝑣), due to closely spaced atomic sub-
levels.
5. Non-thermal profile, 𝐼nt(𝑣), which is a catch-all term for any
other physical processes that might broaden the lines, such as
turbulence or scattering by dust.

better constrain the two-phase model parameters, it is important to

illustrative rather than exhaustive, and are constrained to reproduce
the observed H𝛼flux of the nebula, with input parameters that are

7 DISCUSSION
In this section we place our results in the broader context of previ-
ous studies of Ou 5 and of high-abundance-discrepancy nebulae in
general. Table 5 summarizes the principal geometrical, kinematic,
and physical parameters derived in this paper and compares them
with those from earlier works. Here we highlight the implications of
our new kinematic analysis for the dynamical history of the nebula,
examine the evidence for a two-phase temperature structure and its
relation to the abundance discrepancy, assess the successes and lim-
itations of the Cloudy photoionization models, and finally consider
Ou 5 in the context of other post-common-envelope and high-ADF
planetary nebulae.
7.1 Implications of our kinematic results
The kinematic analysis of Section 4.2 shows that all of the prin-
cipal components of the nebula (inner shell, outer shell, outermost
shell, and polar knots) share a common kinematic age of ∼104 yr
(Table 3). This might seem to argue against a sequence of distinct
ejection events, since the factor of three difference in radius between
components is not accompanied by a difference in age. However, the
nebula is rather old, so we cannot rule out a sequence of ejections
over a period of ≲103 yr. Indeed, we find weak evidence that the
high-latitude nebular components (outer shell and polar knots) are
slightly older kinematically (1.2 ± 0.2 × 104 yr) than the low-latitude
inner and outermost shells (8.0 ± 0.5 × 103 yr).
Morphologically, the nebula bears considerable resemblance to
nested bipolar nebulae around symbiotic stars, such as Ced 211
around R Aquarii (Solf & Ulrich 1985; Henney & Dyson 1992) and
the Southern Crab (PN G315.4+09.4) around Henize 2-104 (Schwarz
et al. 1989; Corradi et al. 2001). The Southern Crab has a kinematic
age of 4000 ± 1000 yr and the expansion velocities are similar to
Ou 5 at the equator (10 km s−1 to 15 km s−1) but much larger at the
poles (50 km s−1 to 250 km s−1). As with Ou 5 the expansion follows
a Hubble-type law (𝑉∝𝑟) such that the kinematic age of all nebular
components is approximately equal (Corradi et al. 2001; Santander-
García et al. 2008). R Aquarii is much younger, with the outer nebula,
inner bipolar nebula, and helical filament having kinematic ages of
450 ± 25 yr, 270 ± 20 yr, and 285 ± 20 yr, respectively (Santamaría
et al. 2024). This is suggestive of two different outburst events, sep-
arated by 170 ± 25 yr, but such a small time difference will be very
difficult to perceive once the nebula is 10 000 yr old. This provides
a natural explanation for the globally Hubble-like velocity laws seen
in older nebulae.
Ou 5 is not a symbiotic system (white dwarf accreting from a
cool giant), but it has passed through a common-envelope stage (see
below) and the shell ejections may be related to enhanced mass
transfer to the low-mass main-sequence secondary during or just
before this stage (Chamandy et al. 2018; Khouri et al. 2021; López-
Cámara et al. 2022). Similar nested hourglass shells are seen in some
young planetary nebulae such as Hb 12 (Clark et al. 2014; Hsia
et al. 2021), which may be a closer analogue to Ou 5 since it is also
possibly hosts a short-period post-common-envelope binary (Hsia
et al. 2006)10
If this scenario of closely spaced multiple outbursts is true, one
might ask why the outer shells (which were ejected first) are always
expanding faster than the inner shells (which were ejected slightly
later). But this can be simply explained as a selection effect: any shell
that is ejected at a faster velocity than the shells from earlier outbursts
will eventually catch up with them and merge. Therefore, in the long
run, the only shells that survive to be observed as evolved nested
bipolar nebulae are those with the “correct” ordering of expansion
velocities.
Our measured systemic velocity of the nebula, 𝑉sys = −33 km s−1,
is offset from the center-of-mass velocity of the binary, −26 km s−1
(Jones et al. 2022). Although the difference is only ∼7 km s−1, it
is much larger than the velocity precision of our spectroscopy and
may indicate the imprint of asymmetric mass loss during envelope
ejection. The surface brightness of the nebula is also slightly lop-
sided, being 10 to 20% brighter on the east side (see Fig. 4), which
supports this interpretation.
Similarly, we find a small but statistically significant difference
between the inclination of the nebular axis, 𝑖= 85 ± 2◦(Sec. 4.3),
and the binary orbital inclination that is inferred from eclipse fitting,
82±1◦(Jones et al. 2022). This is hard to explain, especially since we
find consistent inclinations for both the inner and outermost shells.

7.2 Evolutionary status of the central star
A robust result of our nebular analysis (section 5.3) is that the ion-
izing central star must have an effective temperature ≳105 K.12
This contradicts the finding of a significantly cooler temperature
(67 ± 5 K) from analysis of the binary light curve and radial ve-
locity variations (Jones et al. 2022). Together with a low derived
luminosity (Table 5), this led those authors to propose that the pri-
mary is a post–red-giant-branch (RGB) star with a He core, rather
than a post–asymptotic-giant-branch (AGB) star with a C/O core,
as is more usual in planetary nebulae. However, we believe that our
higher temperature determination from the He ii Zanstra method is
much more reliable, since it is (1) dependent on the high-energy tail
of the stellar SED, which is a steep function of 𝑇★, and (2) based
on a photon counting argument, and thus insensitive to assumptions
about the nebular density or He abundance.
For these reasons, we strongly disfavor the post-RGB hypothesis
since models of post-RGB evolution (Hall et al. 2013; Istrate et al.
2016) show that the maximum effective temperature is always <
105 K. The dynamical stellar mass 𝑀★= 0.50 ± 0.06 M⊙derived by
Jones et al. (2022) is very close to the threshold for core He burning
of 0.48 M⊙to 0.50 M⊙(e.g., Salaris & Cassisi 2005, p. 148), so is
consistent with either hypothesis. We therefore proceed to analyze
the central star in the light of post-AGB evolution, for which we
employ interpolated tracks from the MESA Isochrones and Stellar
Tracks (MIST) project (Dotter 2016; Choi et al. 2016), which are
calculated using the stellar evolution code Modules for Experiments
in Stellar Astrophysics (MESA: Paxton et al. 2011; Jermyn et al.
2023).

7.3 Two-phase temperature structure and the abundance
discrepancy
Our measurements of thermal line broadening (Sec. 5.1) imply a
mean H𝛼temperature of 𝑇≃3500 K to 6000 K, significantly below
the ∼104 K implied by collisionally excited line (CEL) diagnostics
(cf. Corradi et al. 2015). This robustly confirms that internal tem-
perature fluctuations must be at least a part of the explanation for
the observed abundance discrepancy in this object. In a two-phase
framework, the fraction of H𝛼emission arising in the cool phase
in Ou 5 must be 𝜔> 0.4, which is larger than the typical values
𝜔≲0.1 derived from other nebulae with more moderate abundance
discrepancies (Richer et al. 2022; García-Rojas et al. 2022; Gómez-
Llanos et al. 2024). However, it is comparable to the emission frac-
tion of 𝜔= 0.2 to 0.7 estimated for Hf 2-2 (García-Rojas et al.
2022; Castañeda-Carlos et al. 2025), which is another extreme-ADF
nebula.
Spatially, the cool phase seems more centrally concentrated. The
NOT narrow-band images (Sec. 4.1) show that recombination lines
(e.g., He ii 𝜆4686, H𝛼) are relatively brighter in the inner ring, while
CELs such as [O iii] 𝜆5007 show limb-brightening in the lobes. This
agrees with the slit spectroscopy of Corradi et al. (2015), whose ORL
spatial profiles peak more centrally than the CELs. Such differences
argue for radial gradients in the two-phase parameters: either the
cool fraction 𝜔, the cool-phase temperature 𝑇cool, or both, must vary

7.4 Constraints from Cloudy modeling
The photoionization models of Section 6 all reproduce the global H𝛼
flux and He ii 𝜆4686/H𝛽𝜆4861 ratio, regardless of whether metal-
licity fluctuations are included, but chemically homogeneous models
are incapable of reproducing either the forbidden or permitted metal
lines. Models with sinusoidal abundance fluctuations can fit the ob-
servations better, and by combining moderate fluctuations (𝑍0 ≃6)
with more extreme fluctuations (𝑍0 ≃120), it is possible to simul-
taneously fit a broad range of the observed line ratios, together with
the inferred mean temperature. The derived fraction of the nebular
shell containing extreme fluctuations, 𝑓= 0.60 ± 0.05 is of the same
order as the cool-phase fraction 𝜔derived from the two-phase anal-
ysis above. We have not attempted to directly fit the observed spatial
distribution of different emission lines, which would require more
sophisticated modeling such as in Ercolano et al. (2003); Gómez-
Llanos & Morisset (2020), but the general trends are qualitatively
reproduced by our large-scale fluctuation models, while the small-
scale fluctuation models predict that the optical recombination line
emission is biased towards the outside of the shell, contrary to the
observations.

7.5 Ou 5 in the context of other high-ADF planetary nebulae
The most extreme abundance discrepancies (ADF ≳100) are seen
in localized H-deficient knots inside the planetary nebulae Abell 30
(Wesson et al. 2003), Abell 58 (Wesson et al. 2008), and Abell 46
(Corradi et al. 2015). All but the last of these are “born-again” nebu-
lae, which may be the result of a very late thermal pulse (VLTP) that
mixes the outer layers of the envelope and ejects H-deficient material
inside a pre-existing nebula (Iben et al. 1983). Other examples of this
class are Abell 78 (Fang et al. 2014; Montoro-Molina et al. 2023)
and WR 72 (Gvaramadze et al. 2020), which also contain H-deficient
knots, but which do not have detections of the metal recombination
lines necessary to formally determine an ADF.
Ou 5, with its measured ADF = 56, is unambiguously an extreme-
ADF nebula, yet it belongs to the group of about ten objects with
ADF ≃20 to 80 that form a distinct tier below the ultra-extreme cases
(ADF ≳100). The properties of this group were studied in detail by
Wesson et al. (2018), who found a clear association with binarity: 9
out of the 14 nebulae with measured ADF > 20 host close-binary
nuclei,16 suggesting that Ou 5, like its peers, was ejected during
a common-envelope (CE) phase (Jones 2025). The details of the
physical processes that gave rise to both the nebula ejection and the
formation of the high-metallicity phase are both unknown, although
the empirical correlations suggest these must be linked (Wesson
et al. 2018). One possibility is explosive CE ejection initiated by an
in-spiraling secondary that overflows its Roche lobe. This can cause
a stream of H-rich material to penetrate deep into the AGB core,
where it triggers the explosive destruction of the He-burning shell,
ejecting the envelope together with some He-burning products. This
scenario has mainly been explored in the context of more massive
binaries (Ivanova et al. 2002), but we speculate that a similar process
(section 3.3.4 of Ivanova et al. 2013) may give rise to high-ADF
post-CE nebulae such as Ou 5. However, in order to explain the
observed ionized mass in the Ou 5 nebula (≈0.1 M⊙, sec. 7.4), it
seems necessary that a large part of the AGB envelope had already
been lost prior to this event (see Santander-García et al. 2022 for the
general problem of “missing mass” in planetary nebulae).
An alternative mechanism for producing metallicity inhomo-
geneities is the evaporation of solid bodies such as comets or plan-
ets (Liu 2003, 2006). However, quantitative calculations (Henney &
Stasińska 2010) show that evaporation during the planetary nebula
phase itself is incapable of generating sufficient quantity of gas-phase
metals. If the evaporation began during the previous AGB phase, then
the mechanism becomes more feasible, but that cannot explain the
concentration of high-metallicity towards the inner regions of the
nebula, as observed in Ou 5.

8 CONCLUSIONS
We have presented new imaging and high-resolution spectroscopy of
the extreme-abundance-discrepancy planetary nebula Ou 5, together
with photoionization models that explore its unusual thermal and
chemical structure. Our principal conclusions are as follows:
1. The nebula has a complex, nested bipolar morphology consisting
of inner and outer shells, faint outermost lobes, and polar knots.
Despite their differing scales, all components share a common
dynamical age of order 104 yr, implying that if they were ejected
in discrete events, this must have occurred over only ∼103 yr,
much shorter than their present age.
2. Thermal line broadening implies a mean hydrogen tempera-
ture 𝑇≲6000 K for the entire nebula and 𝑇≈3500 K for the
best-studied region, much lower than the ∼104 K derived from
diagnostic line ratios of collisionally excited lines (CELs). This
provides strong, independent evidence for the coexistence of mul-
tiple temperature/metallicity phases within the ionized gas, as
previously proposed to explain the extreme abundance discrepan-
cies observed in this nebula.
3. Simple cloudy models with sinusoidally varying metallicity are
very successful at reproducing the observed emission line spec-
trum. In order to simultaneously fit the CEL and ORL diagnostics,
a mixture of fluctuations with large amplitude (metallicity contrast
𝑍0 ≈120) and small amplitude (𝑍0 ≈6) is required.
4. The relatively bright He ii emission from the nebula requires a
higher stellar temperature and luminosity than previously claimed,
which rules out the possibility that Ou 5 is a low-mass post-RGB
object. Instead, the primary central star is consistent with post-
AGB evolution toward a CO white dwarf of mass ≈0.58 M⊙.
Ou 5 strengthens the emerging link between high abundance dis-
crepancies and close-binary central stars, since most nebulae with
ADF > 20 host post-CE binaries. It thus provides an important test
case for understanding how the common-envelope interaction shapes
planetary nebulae and for probing the physical origin of the abun-
dance discrepancy problem.
