EchoingPixels: Cross-Modal Adaptive Token Reduction for Efficient
Audio-Visual LLMs
Chao Gong1,2, Depeng Wang2, Zhipeng Wei3, Ya Guo2, Huijia Zhu2, Jingjing Chen1
1 Fudan University
2 Ant Group
3 UC Berkeley
cgong24@m.fudan.edu.cn
Abstract
Audio-Visual Large Language Models (AV-LLMs) face pro-
hibitive computational overhead from massive audio and
video tokens. Token reduction, while extensively explored
for video-only LLMs, is insufficient for the audio-visual do-
main, as these unimodal methods cannot leverage audio-
visual cross-modal synergies. Furthermore, the distinct and
dynamic information densities of audio and video render
static budgets per modality suboptimal. How to perform to-
ken reduction on a joint audio-visual stream thus remains
an unaddressed bottleneck. To fill this gap, we introduce
EchoingPixels, a framework inspired by the coexistence
and interaction of visuals and sound in real-world scenes.
The core of our framework is the Cross-Modal Semantic
Sieve (CS2), a module enabling early audio-visual inter-
action. Instead of compressing modalities independently,
CS2 co-attends to the joint multimodal stream and reduces
tokens from an entire combined pool of audio-visual to-
kens rather than using fixed budgets per modality.
This
single-pool approach allows it to adaptively allocate the
token budget across both modalities and dynamically iden-
tify salient tokens in concert. To ensure this aggressive re-
duction preserves the vital temporal modeling capability,
we co-design a Synchronization-Augmented RoPE (Sync-
RoPE) to maintain critical temporal relationships for the
sparsely selected tokens.
Extensive experiments demon-
strate that EchoingPixels achieves performance compara-
ble to strong baselines using only 5-20% of the original to-
kens, with a 2-3x speedup and memory reduction.

To fill this gap, we argue that token reduction must
be guided by cross-modal interaction. Compressing each
modality in isolation is blind to their rich, synergistic re-
lationships. The potential of cross-modal guidance is clear:
as illustrated in Fig. 1a, explicit audio cues (“your eye”) can
guide the model to treat the character’s eye as the salient
video tokens. Conversely, ambient silence can signal audio
redundancy, guiding the model to allocate its precious to-
ken budget to the visual stream, as shown in Fig. 1b. This
demonstrates that the importance of any single token is rel-
ative and dependent on the other modality. Furthermore,
even without such obvious directive relationships, process-
ing them jointly as a whole helps the model capture the full
spectrum of cross-modal correlations. This insight inspires
our framework, EchoingPixels, which is named for its core
philosophy: it treats audio and video as a real-world scene
where visuals and sound coexist, with one modality contex-
tualizing the other. Instead of naively processing all tokens
(Fig. 2a) or using static, intra-modal compression (Fig. 2b),
EchoingPixels is designed to replicate this real-world fu-
sion. It operates on the principle of Cross-Modal Adaptive
Reduction: the framework learns to dynamically assess the
synergistic joint audio-visual stream, arbitrating and allo-
cating the token budget to information-rich regions across
both modalities in concert, as illustrated in Fig. 2c.
Specifically, our framework, EchoingPixels, implements
this synergistic and adaptive strategy through two co-
designed components. First, we propose the Cross-Modal
Semantic Sieve (CS2) module.
This serves as the core
adaptive mechanism. Instead of compressing each single
modality independently, CS2 enables early, bi-directional
audio-visual interaction. It ingests the entire token stream
(audio, video, text) and holistically scores each audio and
visual token based on its cross-modal importance.
Cru-
cially, instead of using static or pre-set budgets for each
modality, our token selection operates on the entire com-
bined pool of audio-visual tokens. This single-pool mech-
anism is what allows the model to dynamically arbitrate the
total budget between modalities and retain the most criti-
cal tokens, thereby effectively realizing the adaptive strat-
egy shown in Fig. 2c. Second, to ensure that this aggres-
sive, adaptive reduction preserves the temporal relation-
ships vital for audio-visual understanding (e.g., event se-
quence), we co-design a Synchronization-Augmented Ro-
tary Position Embedding (Sync-RoPE). Discarding to-
kens (even adaptively) can disrupt the model’s understand-
ing of temporal sequence and alignment. By improving the
frequency allocation for temporal positions in the original
RoPE [44, 56], Sync-RoPE enhances the model’s ability
to model correct temporal relationships and its understand-
ing of audio-video synchronization, thereby preserving key
temporal relationships in the final sparse, interacted audio-
visual stream.
We validate EchoingPixels on the Qwen2.5-Omni se-
ries [56] across four audio-visual and video-only bench-
marks. Extensive experiments demonstrate that Echoing-
Pixels achieves performance comparable to strong baselines
with only 5-20% of the original tokens. Our contributions
are three-fold:
• We systematically address the neglected token reduction
bottleneck for joint audio-visual streams, identifying the
insufficiency of intra-modal methods in cross-modal syn-
ergies and dynamic information densities.
• We propose EchoingPixels, a novel framework for Cross-
Modal Adaptive Token Reduction, consisting of two com-
ponents: the CS2, which dynamically selects salient to-
kens from the joint audio-visual stream, and Sync-RoPE,
which preserves temporal modeling in the sparse stream.
• Extensive experiments demonstrate that EchoingPix-
els achieves performance comparable to state-of-the-art
baselines with an extremely low token budget (5-20%),
validating our adaptive approach.
2. Related Work
Audio-Visual Large Language Models
The develop-
ment of multimodal models has accelerated recently, with
emerging AV-LLMs also making significant progress. Rep-
resentative closed-source models include OpenAI’s GPT-4o
[25] and Google’s Gemini series [12, 48], which demon-
strate impressive capabilities in understanding and rea-
soning across audio, vision, and text.
In parallel, the
open-source community has made substantial progress. A
prevalent architectural paradigm involves employing a pre-
trained Vision Transformer (ViT) [15, 41] to encode visual
information and a specialized audio encoder [6, 22, 42] for
the audio stream. The resulting audio and visual tokens are
typically concatenated and projected into the input embed-
ding space of an LLM backbone. This approach is exempli-
fied by a growing body of work [7, 8, 10, 17, 38, 54]. Re-
cent efforts like [20] and [46] have focused on long-video
understanding and captioning-enhanced learning, respec-
tively. Models such as [32, 36, 56, 57] further highlight the

rapid progress in this domain. Despite their impressive ca-
pabilities, this paradigm incurs a significant computational
overhead. Scanty compression efforts for these models have
been simplistic, such as pruning based on instruction rele-
vance [68] and audio downsampling [36]. Crucially, audio-
visual models still process the full token stream, or op-

be intra-modal. How to leverage cross-modal synergies to
perform adaptive token reduction on the joint audio-visual
stream remains a key unaddressed problem.
Video Token Compression
Reducing the computational
cost of attention mechanisms in video models is a criti-
cal and well-studied problem. The field has evolved from
early methods for Video Transformers [4, 11, 49] to a di-
verse range of strategies for modern VLLMs. For VLLMs,
one line of work adapts techniques from image compres-
sion, treating each video frame independently to prune spa-
tial redundancy within frames [61, 64]. More recent ap-
proaches explicitly consider the high degree of repetition
over time, focusing on compressing temporal redundancy
between frames [40, 47, 58]. A more advanced research
direction pursues joint spatio-temporal compression. This
is often achieved through unified frameworks that employ
techniques like hierarchical compression [31] or various to-
ken merging and clustering strategies [26, 27, 43, 45] to
holistically reduce visual tokens. In parallel, another cat-
egory of methods leverages linguistic context, using text in-
structions or cross-modal attention scores to guide the reten-
tion of salient visual tokens [5, 37, 47, 55, 65]. Despite their
technical diversity, these methods are inherently insufficient
for AV-LLMs. Designed for the video-only modality, they
cannot leverage the cross-modal synergies involving audio
interaction. Therefore, designing a compression framework
that assesses token importance on the joint audio-visual
stream—rather than just intra-modal streams—remains an
open and critical challenge.
3. Method
As shown in Fig. 3, our framework EchoingPixels is de-
signed to address the core challenge of AV-LLM token re-
duction: how to holistically compress a joint audio-visual
stream that has distinct information densities across modal-
ities. We propose a Cross-Modal Adaptive Reduction strat-
egy, which treats audio and video as a real-world scene
where visuals and sound coexist, with one modality con-
textualizing the other. Our method learns to identify and
preserve the most salient tokens from this fused representa-
tion, adaptively allocating the token budget across modal-
ities. This strategy is realized by two co-designed compo-
nents: (1) the Cross-Modal Semantic Sieve (CS2) to per-
form the joint-stream interaction and adaptive selection; and
(2) the Synchronization-Augmented RoPE (Sync-RoPE) to
ensure temporal integrity in the final sparse stream.
3.1. Cross-Modal Semantic Sieve
3.1.1. Cross-Modal Encoder
The goal of the Cross-Modal Semantic Sieve (CS2) is to
evaluate every token within a holistic, cross-modal context.
This is critical for two reasons. First, traditional methods
that process modalities in isolation are blind to cross-modal
synergies.
Second, standard AV-LLMs rely on a causal
attention decoder for cross-modal fusion. This unidirec-
tional flow prevents early video/audio tokens from being
influenced by later cues, which is suboptimal for a holistic
importance-reduction task.
To overcome this, we propose a cross-modal encoder to
achieve maximum contextual awareness before pruning, re-
quiring each token to see all information before and after its
occurrence. Let Tv ∈RLv×D, Ta ∈RLa×D, Tt ∈RLt×D
be the token sequences for video, audio, and text instruc-
tions. Our processing object is the concatenated sequence
T = Concat(Tv, Ta, Tt). Cross-modal encoding requires
powerful multimodal processing capabilities, which pre-
trained AV-LLMs precisely satisfy, making them ideal for
our context-aware task.
Therefore, we make a trainable
copy of its first N decoder layers to form a lightweight
cross-modal encoder E(·) and further train it for context
awareness. Crucially, we modify the standard causal atten-
tion to a bidirectional attention mechanism. This modifica-
tion is the key that unlocks the joint stream, allowing tokens
from earlier in the sequence (e.g., earlier events in video) to
attend to tokens later (e.g., later events in audio), and vice-
versa. This process creates an integrated representation T ′,
where each token’s representation is fully contextualized by
all other tokens in the audio-visual stream:

3.1.2. Adaptive Token Selection
The next step is to adaptively select information-rich tokens
across both modalities given a total budget, with the budget
for each modality arbitrated by the model itself. This adap-

yet effective per-token “score and selection” strategy.

which is a two-layer MLP. Unlike intra-modal compression
(which would use static or pre-set budgets per modality),
our selection operates on the entire combined pool of au-
dio/video tokens. The scorer holistically projects each au-

• Backward Pass: While the true gradient ∂yi/∂si is zero

“straight-through” the Top-K operation back to the score
si, thus enabling the MLP scorer to learn to assign higher
scores to tokens that are more beneficial for the final task.
3.2. Synchronization-Augmented RoPE
Despite its effectiveness, a key challenge arising from
our adaptive strategy is that the CS2 module (1) uses an
order-agnostic bidirectional attention, and (2) produces a
sparse and irregularly-timed sequence Tcomp. This combina-
tion—especially the sparsity—is the core of our efficiency
gain, but it undermines the standard temporal modeling of
AV-LLMs.

injects relative position information into tokens by applying
a rotation matrix R to queries and keys. This rotation matrix
depends on the position m of the token and the frequency
θ associated with the feature channel. Its mechanism is as
follows:

model must measure the temporal distance S = ta −tv.

distances, allowing the model to distinguish “near” (S ≈0)
from “far”. A high-frequency, oscillating signal is the op-
posite; it loses its stable sense of distance, making it hard to
learn precise alignment across the sparse gaps we create.

Fig. 5, Sync-RoPE re-partitions the embedding dimension
into four parts: [t, h, w, t]. By judiciously assigning low-
frequency channels to temporal positions, this enhances
the model’s ability to capture long-term sequences, while

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental Settings
Models
We evaluate EchoingPixels on two representative
open-source AV-LLMs: Qwen2.5-Omni-3B and Qwen2.5-
Omni-7B [56].
The Qwen2.5-Omni series utilizes a na-
tive dynamic resolution, which converts 28x28 pixel regions
into tokens, dynamically converting frames into sequences
with corresponding lengths. For audio, it converts each sec-
ond of audio into 25 tokens (each representing about 40ms).
Training Data
Our cross-modal encoder is initialized
with a portion of the pre-trained LLM decoder. This re-
tains strong pre-trained capabilities and makes our training
data-efficient. We use a total of 390k samples, compris-
ing 123k LLaVA-Video data re-annotated by Ola [36] from
speech and visual perspectives, 37k AVQA data [59] for
scene sound, and 32k FortisAVQA [39] for music. To en-
sure proficiency in pure video tasks, we also include 200k
data from the original LLaVA-Video [66] along with audio.

parators.

reference. (2) As no dedicated work on audio-visual to-
ken compression exists, we construct a strong IntraModal
baseline by combining state-of-the-art unimodal compres-
sion schemes. For video, we use pixel unshuffle adopted
by leading VLLMs [2, 9, 50, 52, 58, 71]. It concatenates

Benchmarks
We evaluate on four authoritative bench-
marks. For Audio-Visual tasks, we use Daily-Omni [70],
WorldSense [23], and Video-MME [18] (with audio) to as-
sess synergistic audio-visual perception. For Video-Only
tasks, we use Video-MME (without audio) and MLVU-dev
[69] to evaluate visual understanding across short, medium,
and long-term videos. For all benchmarks, we adhere to
a consistent evaluation protocol to guarantee a fair com-
parison. To this end, we run the evaluation for all meth-
ods under identical settings. WorldSense, Video-MME, and
MLVU are evaluated with VLMEvalKit [16], while Daily-
Omni references its open-source code.
Evaluation Metrics
In addition to the metrics of all
benchmarks, we also report an efficiency analysis, pre-
sented in terms of inference latency and CUDA memory.
Implementation Details
We evaluate token budgets of
5%, 10%, and 20% on the 3B model, and extend this to
10% on the 7B model. The CS2 encoder uses N = 4 lay-
ers. This choice is validated in [64] as a robust trade-off be-
tween contextual encoding and efficiency. For Sync-RoPE,
we partition the 64 available frequencies into a [18, 18, 18,
10] split, corresponding to [t, h, w, t]. We fine-tune both the
CS2 module and the decoder with a batch size of 128 and
a learning rate of 2e-5 for 1 epoch. Training is performed
using ms-swift [67]. For both training and evaluation, FPS
is set to 2 to follow the default settings [56]. The maximum
resolution is set to 105369 according to [67], ensuring ≤144
tokens per video frame. The compression module and de-
coder of the IntraModal baseline are trained with same data.

5. Conclusion
In this work, we propose EchoingPixels, a token reduc-
tion framework for efficient Audio-Visual LLMs.
Exist-
ing methods are limited by their inability to capture cross-
modal interactions or adapt to varying information density
across modalities. Our key contribution is the Cross-Modal
Semantic Sieve (CS2), which enables early cross-modal in-
teraction and implements a unified token selection mecha-
nism from a combined audio-visual pool. This allows adap-
tive and dynamic token budgeting across modalities. To
maintain temporal coherence in the sparsely sampled to-
ken sequence, we co-design a novel Sync-RoPE, which pre-
serves critical temporal relationships. Experiments demon-
strate that EchoingPixels retains strong baseline perfor-
mance using only 5–20% of original tokens, while achiev-
ing 2–3× speedup and memory savings.

A.1. Benchmark Details
The Daily-Omni Benchmark is used in Sec 4.2 Main Re-

ing types: (1) AV Event Alignment: Questions to determine

temporal sequence of visual and audio events in the video;
(3) Reasoning: Questions to explain the cause or reason be-

(4) Inference: Questions to speculate on information not

audio and visual information of two or more events in the
video; (6) Context Understanding: Questions to determine
the contextual information surrounding a specific event in
the video.
A.2. Baseline Details
Exclusion of Attention-Score-Guided Methods in Main
Text
In our main manuscript, we exclude attention-score-
guided token reduction methods from the primary compar-
ison due to a fundamental incompatibility with efficient at-
tention mechanisms. These methods typically require ma-
terializing the full attention matrix to compute importance
scores, which negates the efficiency and memory benefits
of efficient attention. Specifically, our experiments using
vanilla attention reveal that, regardless of whether token
reduction is applied, the peak memory usage exceededs
75GB. This is prohibitively high compared to the <15GB
footprint achieved by our EchoingPixels framework using
efficient attention (as reported in Tab. 2 of the main text).
Consequently, we construct our primary IntraModal base-
line by combining state-of-the-art unimodal compression
schemes, consisting of representative practices adopted in
leading MLLMs such as Qwen-VL and InternVL series.
Additional Baselines for Comprehensive Comparison
To ensure a broader and more rigorous evaluation in this
Appendix, we extend our comparative landscape with the
following models:
• FastV [5]: FastV is the most representative attention-
score-guided token compression method.
Despite the
high memory cost mentioned above, we include it here
to provide a complete performance reference. We con-
figure FastV to select the 20% of audio and video tokens
with the highest attention scores after the second decoder
layer.
• State-of-the-art AV-LLMs: We also compare against a
wider range of recently representative AV-LLMs, includ-
ing Unified-IO-2 [38], VideoLLaMA2 [10], Vita-1.5 [19],
and Ola [36].

B.1. Additional Performance Analysis
We present an extended comparison in Tab. 4, incorporating
FastV and other representative AV-LLMs.
The results demonstrate the superiority of EchoingPixels
over compression baselines. Our method significantly out-
performs both IntraModal and FastV baselines in terms of
performance and token budgets.
• vs. IntraModal: On the 7B model, EchoingPixels (with
only 10% budget, 1,014 tokens/min) surpasses the In-

validates that EchoingPixels is a far more robust solution
for efficient AV-LLMs.
Our compressed EchoingPixels even outperforms many
of the uncompressed baseline systems on overlapping
benchmarks, highlighting the effectiveness of our approach
on top of a strong base model.

B.4. Ablation Study on CS2 Encoder Depth
We investigate the impact of the Cross-Modal Encoder
(CS2) depth (N) on the 3B model with a 20% budget. The
depth N is gradually decreased, starting from N = 4 in
[64].
The results in Tab. 7 reveal an interesting capacity
threshold: performance deteriorates significantly at shal-
lower depths (N = 2, 3). We hypothesize that these config-
urations sit in a “capacity trap”: the encoder attempts com-
plex, global cross-modal modeling but lacks the sufficient
depth to do so correctly, resulting in confused representa-
tions. In contrast, performance significantly improves at
N = 4, where the model achieves 99.0% of the full model’s
performance. This confirms that N = 4 provides the neces-
sary minimum capacity to unlock robust cross-modal align-
ment, validating our design choice and aligning with prece-
dents in similar architectures [64].
