Higher differentiation via higher formal groupoids
Christopher L. Rogers ∗
16 December 2025
Abstract
We solve the differentiation problem for Lie ∞-groups. Our approach builds
on a classical version of Cartier duality which canonically identifies the Hopf
algebra of point distributions supported at the identity of a Lie group with
the universal enveloping algebra of its Lie algebra. Hence, for Lie ∞-groups,
we consider simplicial coalgebras of point distributions. To do this properly,
we first develop the homotopy theory of pointed formal ∞-groupoids within
K. Behrend and E. Getzler’s framework for higher geometric stacks.
These
objects are “higher” but not “derived”, which is an important distinction for
the geometric applications in mind. The second part of our construction relies
on a careful analysis of J. Pridham’s variation of the Dold-Kan adjunction for
cosimplicial algebras. Our main result is a differentiation functor at the level of
1-categories from finite-dimensional Lie ∞-groups to finite-type Lie ∞-algebras
that is homotopically well-behaved, coordinate-free, and explicit yet tractable.
In particular, if G• is a simplicial Lie group with Lie algebra g•, we prove that
the differentiation of its classifying space W• G is canonically isomorphic to the
dg Lie algebra of normalized chains N∗(g•).

Introduction
Lie ∞-groups are reduced simplicial manifolds that satisfy a smooth geometric ana-
log of the horn filling conditions for Kan simplicial sets. Classical examples include
the nerve of any Lie group N•G, and, more generally, the classifying space W• G of a
simplicial Lie group G•. In general, Lie ∞-groups can be understood as the “higher”
or “stacky” analogue of Lie groups. More interesting examples were introduced by
A. Henriques in [21] within the context of simplicial Banach manifolds in order to
geometrically model the full Whitehead tower of a Lie group. However, in this pa-
per, we only consider Lie ∞-groups that are level-wise finite-dimensional. This is, of
course, the ideal situation geometrically, and we lose nothing by doing so. Indeed,
by our recent joint work with J. Wolfson [46], all known examples of interest, in-
cluding those considered in [21], are weakly equivalent to such finite-dimensional Lie
∞-groups. Let us also emphasize right away that all of the homotopical machinery
considered in the present paper, including the aforementioned notion of weak equiv-
alence, lives at the 1-categorical level. No ∞-category theory is required, although
our constructions immediately pass to the correct underlying ∞-categories, if one
wishes to do so.
The infinitesimal analogues of finite-dimensional Lie ∞-groups are finite-type Lie
∞-algebras, i.e. degree-wise finite-dimensional, homologically and non-negatively
graded L∞-algebras.
They are the source of the above examples, and the main
result of [46] can be characterized as a finite-dimensional integration procedure, i.e.,
Lie’s 3rd Theorem for Lie ∞-algebras. The focus of this paper is the reverse process:
differentiation.
A long-standing unfulfilled desideratum in “higher geometry” is a lift of the
classical differentiation procedure for Lie groups to the world of Lie ∞-groups which
satisfies the following criteria:
• functoriality at the 1-categorical level, from finite-dimensional Lie ∞-groups
to finite-type Lie ∞-algebras;
• exactness, i.e. maximal homotopical compatibility between the respective
homotopy theories for Lie ∞-groups and Lie ∞-algebras;
• canonical identifications with classical examples, i.e. a natural isomorphism
between the differentiation of W• G of a simplicial Lie group G•, and the dg
Lie algebra of normalized chains on the simplicial Lie algebra of G•;
• tractability, i.e. in the general case, the underlying complex of the differen-
tiation should coincide with the shifted tangent complex of the Lie ∞-group,
and the higher L∞brackets of the differentiation should be computationally
accessible.
Partial results along these lines were given in 2006 by P. ˇSevera [50] (see also [28,
§8.3]) using the language of supermanifolds. But further progress in this direction

1.2
The ∞-categorical/formal moduli point of view
Given the appearance of formal ∞-groups and L∞-algebras in this work, it is not
surprising that a comparison can be made with the theory of formal moduli stacks
and derived deformation theory. From this point of view, the difficulty of the dif-
ferentiation problem for Lie ∞-groups is analogous to the difficulty in extracting
an explicit dg Lie algebra directly from a given derived Schlessinger functor for a
deformation problem.
Recall that the Lurie-Pridham Theorem provides an equivalence dgLie[W −1] ≃
FMP between the ∞-category of unbounded dg Lie algebras over a field of charac-
teristic zero localized with respect to the quasi-isomorphisms, and the ∞-category
of formal moduli problems2. While the technical aspects of our work rely heavily
on the ideas developed by Pridham in his proof [37] of the theorem, the conceptual
aspects are perhaps closer in spirit to the formalism developed by D. Gaitsgory and
N. Rozenblyum in [13].
In addition to these conceptual relationships, one can say something precise. As
we recall in Sec. 2.5.2, it follows from a theorem of K. Behrend and E. Getzler [2]
that ∞-groupoids in Sh(Mfdfd) form a category of fibrant objects for a homotopy
theory. Our Prop. 2.37 implies that the category of pointed Lie ∞-groupoids canon-
ically inherits, via the Yoneda embedding, the structure of an “incomplete category
of fibrant objects”. This, in turn, makes the full subcategory of Lie ∞-groups a rel-
ative category (Lie∞Grpfd, W) with some extra structure. In particular, [47, Prop.
6.7] implies that a morphism in Lie∞Grpfd is a weak equivalence if and only if it is
