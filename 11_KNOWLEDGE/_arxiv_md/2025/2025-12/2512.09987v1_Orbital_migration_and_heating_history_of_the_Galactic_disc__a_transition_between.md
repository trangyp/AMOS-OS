ABSTRACT
Stellar orbits in the Galactic disc evolve from their birth to the current shape through both radial migration and dynamical heating.
The history of their secular evolution is imprinted in the current kinematics and age-metallicity distribution. We construct a
chrono-chemo-dynamical model of the disc, incorporating inside-out growth, metallicity evolution, radial migration, and heating
to fit the observed age-metallicity-kinematics distribution of LAMOST subgiant stars in both the low and high-𝛼disc. By
modelling all distribution parameters with spline fitting, we present the first non-parametric stellar migration and heating history
of the Galaxy. We determine the heating-to-migration ratio, the ratio of the root-mean-square changes in radial/vertical and
azimuthal actions, to be ≈0.075 for radial to azimuthal actions and ≈0.015 for vertical to azimuthal actions, implying a highly
anisotropic diffusion in the action space. Furthermore, we identify a transition in radial migration efficiency coinciding with
the transition moment of the bimodal disc, for which the radial migration was more efficient for the high-𝛼disc than for the
low-𝛼disc. This transition may be attributed to two correlated scenarios: 1) a bar formation epoch accompanied by violent
outward migration, and 2) a drop in the gas mass fraction in the disc when the low-𝛼disc began to form. These findings offer
further constraints on the formation mechanisms of bimodal discs, favouring the downsizing scenario. We also briefly discuss
the connection between our results and recent high-redshift observations. In addition to the secular evolution history, our model
maps the Milky Way ISM metallicity gradient at different lookback times, which we find has only varied a little (in the range of
−0.07 to −0.10 dex/kpc) since disc formation.
Key words: Galaxy: disc – Galaxy: evolution – ISM: abundances – Galaxy: kinematics and dynamics – galaxies: evolution
1 INTRODUCTION
Our Milky Way’s disc formed early in time (∼1−2 Gyr after the Big
Bang) at low metallicity (Belokurov & Kravtsov 2022, 2024; Conroy
et al. 2022; Rix et al. 2022). Since its formation, the orbits of disc
stars have experienced secular evolution (e.g. Sellwood & Binney
2002; Roškar et al. 2008; Schönrich et al. 2010) and merger-induced
heating (e.g. the Splash, Bonaca et al. 2017; Di Matteo et al. 2019;
Gallart et al. 2019; Belokurov et al. 2020). The secular evolution of
orbits is driven by internal processes, such as dynamical interactions
with non-axisymmetric perturbations in the Galaxy (e.g. the bar,
spiral arms, and giant molecular clouds; see Sellwood & Binney
2002; Roškar et al. 2008; Sellwood 2014).
Secular evolution can be decomposed into two main components:
changes in the “size” of orbits and changes in the “shape” of orbits.
The former is usually described by drifts in the angular momentum
or guiding radii of the orbits (Δ𝐿𝑧or Δ𝑅𝑔), termed radial migration;

the latter is described by orbital eccentricity and vertical extent or,
canonically, changes in the radial and vertical actions of the orbits
(Δ𝐽𝑅,𝑧), termed heating (Sellwood & Binney 2002; Schönrich et al.
2010). Radial migration processes were first discussed by Sellwood
& Binney (2002) and Roškar et al. (2008), who illustrated that radial
migration occurs through interaction with the resonances of transient
spiral arms in simulated galaxies. The interaction with different res-
onances will cause varying degrees of heating of stars during migra-
tion. Perfect radial migration (Δ𝐽𝑅/Δ𝐿𝑧= 0) only occurs around the
corotation resonances, while other resonances cause additional heat-
ing during migration (Sellwood & Binney 2002; Minchev & Quillen
2006; Daniel et al. 2019). In addition to transient spiral arms, many
studies have identified other mechanisms that cause or enhance radial
migration in simulated galaxies, such as bar-spiral resonance overlap
(Minchev & Famaey 2010; Minchev et al. 2011; Solway et al. 2012),
outward radial transport by the decelerating bar (Khoperskov et al.
2020; Chiba & Schönrich 2021; Haywood et al. 2024; Zhang et al.
2025), and spatial overlap between the bar and spiral arms at the bar
tips (Marques et al. 2025). Some studies also highlight the influence

Radial migration has a strong impact on the chemical distribu-
tion in galactic discs (Minchev et al. 2013; Kubryk et al. 2013;
Grand et al. 2015). This has been explored intensively with simu-
lated galaxies and also with theoretical modelling (e.g. Schönrich
et al. 2010; Loebman et al. 2016; Schönrich & McMillan 2017).
Most notably, radial migration broadens the metallicity distribution
function of a galaxy at different radii because it mixes stars born at
different radii together (Sellwood & Binney 2002; Schönrich et al.
2010; Loebman et al. 2016). As radial mixing occurs, it gradually
flattens the radial metallicity gradient of the disc and also changes the
vertical metallicity gradient (Schönrich & McMillan 2017; Kawata
et al. 2017). Interestingly, radial migration could also help produce
the 𝛼-bimodality in the Galactic disc by mixing stars born at dif-
ferent radii with different 𝛼-knees (Schönrich et al. 2010; Sharma
et al. 2021b; Zhang et al. 2025). Recent studies of simulations show
the impact of radial migration on biasing the star formation history
measurement of galaxies (Minchev et al. 2025; Bernaldez et al. 2025;
Ratcliffe et al. 2025).
Many studies suggest that radial migration was efficient in the
Milky Way’s past (Frankel et al. 2018, 2020). Radial migration in the
Milky Way is frequently studied via the age-metallicity-𝑅distribution
of stars, as in Frankel et al. (2018, 2020). They revealed that stars in
the low-𝛼disc now in the solar vicinity have, on average, migrated
more than 3 kpc during the past 8 Gyr. Haywood et al. (2024) used
the breaks in the radial metallicity profile in different age bins to
argue that stars born around 8 −10 Gyr ago experienced violent
outward migration due to a bar formation epoch and migrated out to
the outer Lindblad resonance radius of the bar, in agreement with the
bar formation epoch derived by Sanders et al. (2024). Zhang et al.
(2025) also analysed the shape of the age-metallicity distributions
at different radial bins to argue that a significant portion of stars
currently located around the bar corotation radius migrated outwards
due to the corotation resonance dragging of the decelerating bar
from the very inner Galaxy to their present location. The amount
of heat that accompanied this migration has recently been a topic
of intense debate. Frankel et al. (2020) intriguingly revealed that
stars experienced little heating compared to the amount of migration
(Δ𝐽𝑅/Δ𝐿𝑧≈0.1). Hamilton et al. (2024) compared this number
with test particle simulations and transient spiral arm-driven radial
migration therein and found a clear inconsistency between the model
and observation, for which the observed heating is too little. However,
Sellwood & Binney (2025) re-ran the simulation originally presented
in Sellwood & Binney (2002) and found that the heating versus
migration ratio is broadly consistent with that observed, but leaving
no room for extra heating from other sources (such as mergers or
giant molecular clouds). Recently, McCluskey et al. (2025) compared
galaxies in the FIRE-2 cosmological simulation suite with external
galaxies and found consistency, but noted that the Milky Way is about
2 times colder than the external and simulated galaxies.
In this work, we construct a statistical model, similar to that in
Frankel et al. (2020), to fit the age-metallicity-kinematics distribution
in the observation (the goal is similar to the chemodynamical model
in Binney & Vasiliev 2024 but with a different model). The main
improvement is to extend the model to also include the high-𝛼disc,
and simultaneously fit all the model parameters (e.g. radial migration
efficiency and birth metallicity gradient) non-parametrically with
cubic splines so that we can fully explore the signature of radial
migration and heating in both the time and radial domains.

2 DATA
To unveil the orbital history of stars in the Milky Way’s disc, accurate
stellar age, metallicity, and kinematics measurements are required.
We used the stellar parameter and isochrone age measurements of
approximately 320, 000 subgiant stars derived in Xiang et al. (2025),
which is an update of Xiang & Rix (2022). Their subgiant sample
utilises stellar parameter spectroscopic measurements from LAM-
OST DR7 (Zhao et al. 2012; Xiang et al. 2019) and astrometric
measurements from Gaia DR3 (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2022). The
subgiant stars are selected from the effective temperature–absolute
magnitude (𝑇eff −𝑀𝐾) plane, following a series of quality cuts that
remove binary and blue horizontal branch stars (we refer the reader
interested in the details to Xiang & Rix 2022; Xiang et al. 2025). The
median age uncertainty is ∼8%. For the kinematic measurements,
we also adopt the astrometric measurements from Gaia DR3 and ge-
ometric distances from Bailer-Jones et al. (2021). The adopted radial
velocity measurements are mainly from Gaia DR3, but for a small
number of stars for which Gaia radial velocities are unavailable, we
use the radial velocity measured by LAMOST (Xiang et al. 2015).
To further select high-quality disc stars, we apply the following
cuts to the original sample in Xiang et al. (2025). We select stars
with metallicity uncertainty Σ[Fe/H] < 0.1 and age uncertainty (on
a logarithmic scale) Σlog10 𝜏< 0.05. We remove stars with large
kinematics uncertainties by applying the parallax cut 𝜛/Σ𝜛> 5
and the radial velocity uncertainty cut ΣRV < 10 km s−1. We remove
halo contamination by selecting stars with vertical height |𝑧| < 3 kpc,
metallicity [Fe/H] > −1 and ages 𝜏< 13 Gyr, which is consistent
with the spin-up epoch of the Milky Way considering a reasonable
age uncertainty for old disc stars (Miglio et al. 2021; Belokurov
& Kravtsov 2022; Rix et al. 2022; Chandra et al. 2023; Queiroz

3 CHRONO-CHEMO-DYNAMICAL MODEL
In this section, we present the model for the Milky Way’s age-
metallicity-kinematic distribution to decode its orbital history. We
briefly summarise the assumptions of this model here, but for clarity,
we present the detailed reasons and justifications in the correspond-
ing sections. The main underlying assumptions are: 1) stars born at
the same time and radius have approximately the same metallicity
within a tolerance; 2) stars are born on near circular orbits; 3) the
change in stellar orbits is dominated by secular processes, such as
radial migration and dynamical heating. Building on these assump-
tions, we construct a model describing 𝑝([Fe/H], 𝐽𝑅, 𝐽𝑧|𝑅𝑔, 𝜏) to
disentangle radial migration (change in 𝑅𝑔or angular momentum
𝐿𝑧) and dynamical heating (change in 𝐽𝑅and 𝐽𝑧), similar to the
model in Frankel et al. (2020). We will now describe the ingredients
of this model.
3.1 Metallicity evolution
The metallicity of stars, [Fe/H], is usually considered an imprint
of their birthplace, known as chemical tagging (Freeman & Bland-
Hawthorn 2002; Sellwood & Binney 2002). This technique has re-
cently been revived to calculate the birth radius of stars in the Galactic
disc (Minchev et al. 2018; Lu et al. 2024; Ratcliffe et al. 2024a, 2025).
We will adopt the same idea to decode the radial migration history
in the Galactic disc by fitting a metallicity evolution model to the
Milky Way.

results converge, which usually occurs reasonably within 3 iterations.

elled at different guiding radii, while keeping the birth scale length
and metallicity gradient the same across different radius.
3.7 Mock data test
We generate a mock sample to test our fitting routine. The purpose of
this test is to ensure that our model is robust against the underlying
selection functions and the measurement uncertainties of the sample.
The systematic caveats of the model, e.g., model assumptions, are
not tested in this section; they are instead discussed in Section 6, and
tested with a simulated galaxy presented in Appendix A.
We postulate the free parameters in our model and their time de-
pendence and use these as the ground truth to sample the metallicity
and kinematics of the mock stars. First, we sample the ages of the
stars from a uniform distribution between 0 −12 Gyr. Using the
mock ages, we assign the position and velocity of the stars by sam-
pling from the quasi-isothermal distribution function implemented
in AGAMA using the sampling routine in Vasiliev (2019), as-
suming the McMillan (2017) potential. We manually apply mock
selection functions to the mock stars in age, Galactocentric radius,
and angular momentum to mimic the distributions of the sample we
construct in Section 2 in a similar approach as in Zhang & Sanders
(2023). Then, we assign the metallicity to each star according to
a probability distribution 𝑝([Fe/H]|𝜏, 𝐿𝑧) in Eq. (5) without con-
volving the measurement uncertainties. After creating this sample of
mock stars with all the observables {w, 𝜏, [Fe/H]}, we further add
scatters to these observables to mock the measurement uncertainties,
also on the same order of magnitude as the adopted sample. The
mock error ellipse of this scattering is recorded for modelling.

4 BEST FIT MODEL OF THE MILKY WAY
In this section, we present the chrono-chemo-dynamical model of
the Milky Way by applying the model described in section 3 to the
subgiant star sample in section 2 with guiding radius from 6−13 kpc.
The best-fit model is shown in Fig. 3 and Fig. 4, in which the initial
length of the disc scale and the metallicity gradient at birth are
fitted globally for all radii, but the diffusion of angular momentum,
the dispersion of radial and vertical velocity are fitted at different
guiding radii.
4.1 Diffusion of the orbital actions
In the left panel of Fig. 3, we show the best-fit angular momentum
diffusion coefficient (𝜎𝐿𝑧) as a function of stellar age, coloured by
the present-day guiding radius. We observe a smooth increase in
radial migration strength with increasing stellar age for the young
disc (𝜏≲8 Gyr), as expected. However, the model finds a transition
around ∼9 −10 Gyr, where old stars (𝜏≳10 Gyr) in the Milky
Way experienced much stronger radial migration at earlier times.
This transition time matches that of the high-𝛼to low-𝛼disc on the
timescale of this sample (Xiang & Rix 2022). Therefore, the high
radial migration efficiency in the model is required to explain the flat
(or even slightly positive) radial metallicity distribution of the old,
high-𝛼disc (Hayden et al. 2015; Chen et al. 2025), implying that
the Galactic high-𝛼disc is fully radially mixed and no signature of
the initial metallicity gradient remains. We leave the interpretation
of this transition to Section 5.3.
In the low-𝛼, young disc, 𝜎𝐿𝑧also increases with age, meaning
that older stars are more radially mixed. Angular momentum dif-
fusion can flatten the metallicity gradient (Schönrich & McMillan
2017; Kawata et al. 2018), which is consistent with the observed
gradual flattening of the metallicity gradient with increasing stellar
age (e.g. Vickers et al. 2021; Chen et al. 2025). In Fig. 3, curves cor-
responding to larger 𝑅𝑔(orange/yellow) consistently lie below the
curves corresponding the smaller 𝑅𝑔(red/purple). This means that
the radial migration efficiency decreases radially outwards, which is
frequently observed in simulations due to the decrease of bar and
spiral perturbations (Roškar et al. 2008; Bird et al. 2012; Kubryk
et al. 2015, Zh¯ang et al. in prep). In addition to the general radial
decline of the radial migration strength, we also find that 𝜎𝐿𝑧(𝜏)
has similar behaviour in the 𝑅𝑔bins of ≈6.5 −9 kpc, 9 −11 kpc,
and ≥11 kpc. This could be due to different radial migration mech-
anisms that dominate different radiuses (e.g. Minchev & Famaey
2010; Halle et al. 2015; Khoperskov et al. 2020; Haywood et al.
2024; Zhang et al. 2025; Marques et al. 2025), which implies that
the radial migration behaviour in the low-𝛼disc is more structured
than a simple stochastic diffusion with a smoothly varying diffusion
coefficient across different radii. Bar-driven radial migration could
be crucial for understanding this behaviour (Halle et al. 2015; Khop-
erskov et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2025; Cerqui et al. 2025; Marques
et al. 2025). We will discuss this in more detail in Section 5.2.
The best-fit radial and vertical velocity dispersions are shown in
the middle and right panels of Fig. 3. Both velocity dispersions
increase with increasing stellar ages, consistent with the age-velocity
dispersion relation of the Milky Way (Aumer et al. 2016; Sanders
& Das 2018; Sharma et al. 2021a). The radial velocity dispersion
exhibits a clear decline towards the outer radius, which is compatible
with past observations, too. The vertical velocity dispersion also
declines as the radius becomes larger, but this decline is much weaker
than that in the radial velocity dispersion.

early times, reaching a minimum at approximately 8 Gyr, and then
starts to flatten to the present value of -0.07 dex/kpc (Bragança et al.
2019). The variation is much smaller than the ISM metallicity gra-
dient calibrated using other methods directly from the stellar metal-
licity dispersion at fixed age bins (e.g. Minchev et al. 2018; Lu et al.
2024; Ratcliffe et al. 2024b). We claim that this metallicity gradient

4.3 Evolution of the disc scale length at birth
The model for the initial scale length of the disc is presented in the
right panel of Fig. 4, where the model result is shown by the blue
line and blue shaded region. The constraint is tight for the early disc
when the radial migration efficiency is high (𝜎𝐿𝑧≳500 kpc km/s),
enabling the model to learn the initial scale length of the disc from
the shape of the metallicity distribution at the corresponding ages.
The constraints on the late disc become weaker as the total angular
momentum diffusion drops. Our results are broadly consistent with
the initial scale length found in Frankel et al. (2020). We also illustrate
the current disc scale length with the black dashed line, which is
obtained by dynamical modelling of the disc kinematics (Funakoshi
et al. 2025). Funakoshi et al. (2025) used the spectroscopic ages of
red giant stars in Ciucă et al. (2024) to build a dynamical model
of the Milky Way’s disc from present-day kinematics and derived
the current disc scale length from the velocity distributions in the
Galactic disc. The initial scale length obtained is consistently shorter
than the current scale length, as expected, because outward radial
migration should flatten the surface profile of the disc (see Ratcliffe
et al. 2025 for similar results).
The initial scale length of the disc shows a rapid increase for the
Milky Way’s low-𝛼disc, consistent with the inside-out disc formation
scenario (Chiappini et al. 2001). Interestingly, we observe a drop in
the initial scale length at ∼10 Gyr. A similar drop is also observed
in Funakoshi et al. (2025) at the same age for the current scale
length, which was attributed to gas disc shrinkage caused by the Gaia-
Sausage-Enceladus (GS/E) merger (Grand et al. 2018; Funakoshi
et al. 2025) at approximately 10 Gyr ago (Belokurov et al. 2020;
Helmi 2020). Our result reinforces their argument.

the model yields an initial disc scale length longer than the present-
day scale length of the high-𝛼, old disc. The choice of gravitational
potential, as another possible source of systematic uncertainty, has
only a limited effect on the model outputs, given that the Galactic
potential is now well constrained (McMillan 2017; Hunter et al.
2024; Dillamore et al. 2025; see also the recent Dillamore & Sanders
2025). Overall, we consider that the best-fitting model provides a
good description of the observations and is robust against many
potential sources of systematic uncertainty (see further discussion of
model caveats in Section 6).

5.3 Transition phase between the young and old disc
Another interesting feature in the 𝜎𝐿𝑧(𝜏) fitting in Fig. 3 is the
transition of radial migration efficiency around ∼8−10 Gyr ago, for
which stars older than ∼10 Gyr witnessed a higher radial migration
efficiency than younger stars, as shown in the left panel of Fig. 3.
By marginalising over the sample and taking the average of the time
derivative of 𝜎𝐿𝑧over a window with a width of 1.5 Gyr, we find
⟨𝑑𝑅𝑔/𝑑𝑡⟩≈0.49 ± 0.08 kpc/Gyr for 𝜏< 8 Gyr and ⟨𝑑𝑅𝑔/𝑑𝑡⟩≈
0.92 ± 0.11 kpc/Gyr for 𝜏> 10 Gyr. (It is noteworthy that the results
in this paper are effective solutions that fit the present-day state of the
Galactic disc, which does not necessarily describe the evolution of the
Galactic disc, so these numbers should be taken with caution.) There
are two ways to interpret this transition: 1) a high bar deceleration
rate during the bar formation epoch (see Haywood et al. 2024); or
2) a transition of the gas content in the Galaxy (see Zh¯ang et al. in
prep). The GS/E merger (Belokurov et al. 2018; Helmi et al. 2018)
could also induce some disc reshuffling, but the mass ratio of the
Milky Way and the GS/E progenitor is unlikely to produce such a
large effect (but see also Lane & Bovy 2025 for recent updates on
estimated GS/E’s mass).
1) Haywood et al. (2024) demonstrated, using a hydrodynamical
simulation, that there is an episode of outward radial migration
during the onset of bar formation in that galaxy. They argued
that the violent outward migration episode is driven by the early
deceleration of the Galactic bar, where the bar decelerates from a
high initial pattern speed of ≳100 km/s/kpc at the moment that
the quadrupole strength starts to increase to a final pattern speed of
∼40 km/s/kpc when the bar reaches its maximum strength. Many
stars born inside the corotation radius 𝑅ini < 𝑅CR(𝑡) are dragged
by the expansion of the corotation radius, while other stars are less
affected. Stars experiencing this outward migration episode have
their final destiny in a range from the innermost part of the Galaxy
out to the OLR radius. Dating the star formation epoch in the nuclear
stellar disc using Mira variables, Sanders et al. (2024) estimated
the bar formation epoch of the Milky Way to be ∼8 −10 Gyr,
compatible with the time of transition of 𝜎𝐿𝑧as observed in this
work. This implies that the Milky Way bar might experience an
exceptionally strong deceleration episode during the bar formation
epoch, instead of a constant pattern speed during bar formation
that was conventionally adopted in previous models (e.g., Chiba &
Schönrich 2021; Dillamore et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2025).
2) In the Nexus simulation (Tepper-García et al. 2024), it is demon-
strated that the behaviour of radial migration (or orbital diffusion)
varies as a function of the gas mass fraction ( 𝑓gas) of the galaxies. A
galaxy with a high gas fraction shows high efficiency of radial mi-
gration and simultaneously a higher heating-to-migration ratio. This
is because stars in gas-rich galaxies experience stronger density per-
turbations with more clumpiness, mainly supported by the active gas
in the discs. The angular momentum diffusion rate in the most gas-
rich galaxy in the Nexus simulation suite, 𝑓gas = 80%, is ≈2 times
greater than that in its gas-poor counterpart, 𝑓gas = 20% (Zh¯ang et al.
in prep.). Therefore, the transition phase of 𝜎𝐿𝑧in our model could
also represent a transition from a gas-rich to a gas-poor disc when
the low-𝛼disc formed (see also relevant evidence and discussion of
the gas disc shrinking in Funakoshi et al. 2025). Similar correlation
between the radial migration strength and galaxies’ gas fraction is
also seen in galaxies in the TNG50 simulation suite, for which we
present two examples in Appendix A. However, no obvious increase
in the 𝛿𝐽𝑅/𝛿𝐿𝑧ratio is observed in our results, so it is unlikely that
we can explain all results solely by the gas transition. A careful de-

6 FURTHER DISCUSSIONS AND MODEL LIMITATIONS
In this section, we discuss the systematic caveats of the model and
examine the robustness of our results.
6.1 Azimuthal metallicity dispersion

above, we fixed the metallicity dispersion to be 𝜎[Fe/H] = 0.05 dex,

Way (Wenger et al. 2019), and a typical value of the azimuthal metal-

more gas-rich could be different than today.

6.2 Missing of the bar radial transportation
Radial migration history of the Milky Way is modelled in this work
by the 1D angular momentum diffusion equation (Sanders & Binney
2015). It can describe stochastic random scattering of angular mo-
mentum, which is applicable for modelling the dynamical interaction
between stars and transient spiral arms or density clumps. However,
this model suffers from incorporation of bar-driven radial migration
because it assigns an equal probability for a star to migrate inward or
outward, but the Galactic bar might particularly favour the outward
migration. For example, stars trapped by the bar corotation resonance
at an early time will be transported outward by resonance dragging
of the bar deceleration for violent deceleration during bar formation
(Haywood et al. 2024) or gradual deceleration after bar formation
(Khoperskov et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2025). Also, Marques et al.
(2025) illustrated that stars at the tips of the bar would be transported
outward when there are bar-spiral spatial overlaps.
Zhang et al. (2025) demonstrated using a test particle with a decel-
erating bar that the stars transported outward by the expansion of the
bar corotation resonance can create the "V-shaped" age-metallicity
sequences as a signature we observe around the present corotation
radius in the second panel of Fig. 8. This bimodal "V-shaped" age-
metallicity sequence cannot be easily fitted with the angular momen-
tum diffusion kernel adopted in this work; instead, the model can
only describe the unimodal metallicity distribution. Therefore, our
model cannot be used as a perfect description of radial migration
around bar resonances, but it finds an effective 𝜎𝐿𝑧,eff that accounts
for the change in angular momentum driven both by bar and tran-
sient perturbations. Building an accurate statistical description of the
bar-driven radial migration is beyond the scope of this paper, and we
leave it for future work.

In this work, we model the distribution of metallicity conditioned
on the observables that are subject to potential selection bias, e.g. 𝜏
and 𝑅𝑔, when fitting the angular momentum diffusion. However, the
radial migration model that we adopted only describes the changes
in the guiding radii of the stars, not their Galactocentric radius 𝑅.

selection function on top of the guiding radius selection effect, which
means that the orbital sets are not complete in the guiding radii bins.
Incomplete orbital sets would bias the shape of the age-metallicity

One way of solving this selection bias is to use the orbital super-

global model of the radial migration history in the Milky Way’s disc.
In the future, it is also worth including both the radial and vertical
distributions of stars into the radial migration model, not only due to
this selection effect, but also because the vertical metallicity gradient
could provide further constraints on the radial migration history of
the Galaxy (Kawata et al. 2017).
7 CONCLUSIONS
We build a chrono-chemo-dynamical model for the Milky Way’s
discs over 13 Gyr since disc formation, thereby describing
the metallicity and secular evolution history of the Galac-
tic disc. Similarly to Frankel et al. (2020), the model in-
volves inside-out growth, metallicity evolution, radial migra-
tion, and dynamical heating. All parameters in the model Θ =
{𝑅𝑑,0(𝜏), ∇[Fe/H](𝜏), 𝜎𝐿𝑧(𝜏, 𝑅𝑔), 𝜎𝑅(𝜏, 𝑅𝑔), 𝜎𝑧(𝜏, 𝑅𝑔)} are fitted
non-parametrically with a cubic spline to fully explore the parameter
space, allowing us to uncover the evolution history of the disc. The
fitted parameters are presented in Fig. 3and 4. We summarise the
main results below:
1) As shown in Fig. 3, the angular momentum diffusion coefficient
gradually increases with age, and then there is a transition epoch of
radial migration efficiency around 8 −10 Gyr ago, coinciding with
the onset of the low-𝛼thin disc. According to our model, the stars
in the high-𝛼old disc experienced a high radial migration efficiency
with ⟨𝑑𝑅𝑔/𝑑𝑡⟩≈0.92 ± 0.11 kpc/Gyr, which then decreased to
⟨𝑑𝑅𝑔/𝑑𝑡⟩≈0.49 ± 0.09 kpc/Gyr for the low-𝛼young disc. The
high radial migration efficiency for the ancient, high-𝛼disc implies
that the high-𝛼disc is fully radially mixed, which then explains
the flat (or even positive) metallicity gradient there. This transition
from a high to low radial migration efficiency could be caused by
two distinct but interconnected mechanisms: a) the transition from
a gas-rich to a gas-poor disc (Zh¯ang et al. in prep.); b) the onset
of Galactic bar formation with a high initial pattern speed and the
accompanied outward migration episode (Haywood et al. 2024). We
conclude that both mechanisms are possible, and it is likely that
both contributed to this transition.
2) We observe a decline in radial migration efficiency with in-
creasing guiding radius as shown in Fig. 8. Interestingly, we detected
three radial regimes of radial migration in the low-𝛼disc with
different radial migration histories. We interpret this as the effect of
the Galactic bar. a) In the first regime, around the corotation radius,
6.5 < 𝑅𝑔/kpc < 9, radial migration is contributed both by the coro-
tation resonance dragging of the decelerating bar and the interaction
with transient spiral arms, leading to a "V-shaped" age-metallicity
sequence. b) In the second regime, between the corotation and OLR
radius, 9 < 𝑅𝑔/kpc < 11, the age-metallicity signature is consistent
with migration driven by transient spiral arms. c) In the outermost
radii, 𝑅𝑔/kpc ≥11, a sharp decrease in radial migration efficiency
is seen, reflecting the OLR radial migration barrier. These results
emphasise the impact of the Galactic bar on the secular evolution his-
tory of the Galactic disc from the inner Galaxy out to the OLR radius.
3) We map the diffusion in the 3D cylindrical action space. Our
model suggests an approximately constant heating-to-migration ratio
in terms of 𝛿𝐽𝑅,𝑧/𝛿𝐿𝑧across all ages, with 𝛿𝐽𝑅/𝛿𝐿𝑧≈0.075±0.022
and 𝛿𝐽𝑧/𝛿𝐿𝑧≈0.015 ± 0.005. The overall diffusion ratios of the
three actions are highly anisotropic 𝛿𝐽𝑧: 𝛿𝐽𝑅: 𝛿𝐿𝑧≈1 : 4 : 50.
There are no evident radial trends in 𝛿𝐽𝑅,𝑧/𝛿𝐿𝑧, except that the ratios
are exceedingly high for the outermost radii 𝑅𝑔> 11 kpc. This is
consistent with simulation results that the heating-to-migration ratio
becomes higher outside the OLR barrier. Comparing to the Nexus
simulation, the measured low heating-to-migration ratios are con-
sistent with a kinematically cold, gas-poor disc (Zh¯ang et al. in prep.).
4) We also produce a metallicity evolution model with high fidelity.
This metallicity model could be used for weak chemical tagging,
similar to the model in Lu et al. (2024); Ratcliffe et al. (2024a). The
model predicts that the birth radius of the Sun is 𝑅⊙,0 ∼5.7 kpc,
which means that the Sun had to migrate for more than 2 kpc to its
present location. It is difficult to determine the exact path of the radial
migration, but an interesting possibility is that it migrated with the
expansion of the bar corotation radius based on its location in the
age-metallicity plane (see also the discussion in Baba et al. 2024).
Finally, in an interesting connection with high-redshift observa-
tions, our results are in line with and add further evidence to the
bimodal disc formation scenario suggested in Tsukui et al. (2025) by
the structural decomposition of high-redshift galaxies. The transition
of radial migration efficiency and the heating-to-migration ratio all
suggest a potential transition from a gas-rich to a gas-poor disc around
the time of the low-𝛼thin disc formation. These results, combined
with recent studies of stellar birth kinematics (e.g. Yu et al. 2021; van
Donkelaar et al. 2022; Bland-Hawthorn et al. 2025), are compatible
with the scenario in which the high-𝛼thick disc was formed thick
and the thin disc thinner because of the drop in the gas fraction. The
radial extent of the thick disc today is much larger than that at birth
due to the outward radial migration driven by the decelerating bar
during and after bar formation.

APPENDIX A: TEST ON TNG50
A1 Model verification with TNG50
To examine the chemo-chrono-dynamical model we built in this
work, we apply the modelling procedure described in Section 3 to a
galaxy with id=547293 in the TNG50 cosmological simulation suite
(Nelson et al. 2019). We select the disc star particles in this simulation
with a simple age cut to select stars formed after the spin-up of the disc
(age ≈10 Gyr) in this galaxy (Belokurov & Kravtsov 2022), which
has been demonstrated as a satisfactory selection of disc particles by
Zhang et al. (2024c). We also remove stars with age < 2 Gyr. Our
model cannot apply to the stellar population formed after 𝑡lb ≈2 Gyr
because the AGN feedback creates a central hole in the star-forming
gaseous disc, which deviates the radial profile of the star-forming disc
far from the exponential profile, as assumed in our model. For stars
with ages between 2 and 10 Gyr, we scatter the age and metallicity
with uncertainties of Σlog10 𝜏= 0.02, and Σ[Fe/H] = 0.1 dex.
We implement the chemo-chrono-dynamical model described in
Section 3 to the age-metallicity-𝑅𝑔distribution of the disc stellar
particles in this simulated galaxy. The best-fit initial disc scale length
and metallicity gradient are shown in the blue solid lines in the left
and middle panels of Fig. A1, and the best-fit angular momentum
diffusion coefficient in the coloured solid lines in the right panel
of Fig. A1. The ground truth values of these three parameters are
calculated using the recorded position and velocity distribution of
these stars at birth and at the present day. The initial disc scale
lengths are computed by fitting exponential profiles of the birth radii
of the stars, binned according to their ages. Similarly, the initial
radial metallicity gradients at different lookback times are obtained
by fitting the linear model to the [Fe/H] v.s. 𝑅𝑏sequence of stars
with different ages. The ground truth angular momentum diffusion
coefficients are computed as the RMS of the variation of the guiding
radii of stars with various ages that are sitting at different guiding
radii today. The ground truth values of these three parameters are
shown in the dashed lines in Fig. A1.
Comparing the best-fit model as shown by the solid lines with
the shaded regions with the ground truth as shown by the dashed
lines, we find that our model recovers the initial disc scalelength,
birth metallicity gradient, and angular momentum diffusion in this
galaxy with good precision and satisfactory accuracy, especially for
the initial metallicity gradient.
A2 Changing of radial migration strength as a consequence of
gas-rich to gas-poor disc transition
For the observed transition of angular momentum diffusion around
9.5 Gyr (left panel in Fig. 3), one of our proposed interpretations
is a change in the gas fraction of the galaxy. We examined this by
analysing the Nexus hydrodynamical simulation suite (Tepper-García
et al. 2024) and TNG50 cosmological suite (Nelson et al. 2019).
We discuss the Nexus simulation results intensively in Appendix B,
whereas in this section, we discuss the results from the TNG50
simulation suite.
We select Milky Way-mass galaxies in TNG50 with an early form-
ing disc (𝑡disc formation ≳10 Gyr). We calculate the gas mass fraction
of these Milky Way-analogue galaxies by taking the ratio of the gas
mass to total baryon mass within two times the half-mass radius of
the disc, 𝑅< 2𝑅ℎ𝑚. To compare the evolution of gas fraction with
the radial migration, we again compute the RMS of the changes in
the angular momentum of disc stars that are outside the bar/bulge re-
gions. In Fig. A2, we show the results of two galaxies with id=547293
and id=523889, in which the RMS angular momentum variation is
shown in red lines with the axis on the left, and the gas fraction is
shown in the blue lines with the axis on the right. We find that the
evolution of gas fraction in these galaxies shares a similar trend to the
strength of angular momentum variation, or radial migration. This
could be because of the high turbulence of gas when the gas fraction
is high, which can enhance radial migration in the stellar disc (El-
Badry et al. 2016). Confirmed with TNG50 simulations, a transition
of the gas fraction in the Milky Way could be a valid explanation for
the transition of the angular momentum diffusion coefficient around
the low-𝛼disc formation time (see left panel of Fig. 3).

𝛿𝐽𝑅/𝛿𝐿𝑧≈0.25, and 𝛿𝐽𝑧/𝛿𝐿𝑧≈0.06 for the most gas-rich analogue
𝑓gas = 80%. Both radial and vertical heating-to-migration ratio of the
Milky Way’s disc are compatible with the 𝑓gas = 20% galaxy model,
which suggests that the kinematics of the Milky Way’s disc is com-
patible with a gas-poor disc.
