RISK LIMITED ASSET ALLOCATION WITH A BUDGET
THRESHOLD UTILITY FUNCTION AND LEPTOKURTOTIC
DISTRIBUTIONS OF RETURNS
GRAHAM L. GILLER
ABSTRACT. An analytical solution to single-horizon asset allocation for an in-
vestor with a piecewise-linear utility function, called herein the “budget thresh-
old utility,” and exogenous position limits is presented. The resulting functional
form has a surprisingly simple structure and can be readily interpreted as repre-
senting the addition of a simple “risk cost” to otherwise frictionless trading.
1. INTRODUCTION
Beginning with the pioneering work of Markowitz[8], the theory of optimal
investment (and optimal trading) has been the subject of extensive analytical work,
both in academia and within financial institutions. The purpose of this note is
not to provide a comprehensive discussion of the subject, which can be found in
numerous places such as the work of Ziemba and MacLean[13], the recent book
by Paleologo[9], and the author’s own works[6]. Nor is to provide a thoroughly
developed theory of investment in theory or in practice, which can be found in
many places including those cited, but merely to present an interesting1 analytical
result that is of relevance to the general subject. Properly, this might be thought of
as sitting somewhere between fully optimal investment strategy and a completely
na¨ıve investment strategy, as an informed reader will be able to readily identify
structural flaws in some of the major propositions. In fact, a correspondent of
the author went as far as to describe the shape of the utility function discussed
as “irrational.”[10]. Nevertheless, the result presented is fully optimal given the
assumptions presented, connects a gap between risk neutral investment and risk
averse investment, and is of a remarkably simple analytical form.
2. THE BUDGET-THRESHOLD UTILITY FUNCTION
Influenced by the work of Nawrocki and Viole[11], and the author’s personal
experience in proprietary trading at a large investment bank[5], we construct a
piecewise linear utility function that is indifferent to wealth, W, above a pre-defined
threshold, or “budget,” β, and linear in the dis-utility of wealth below that budget.

5. MAXIMUM EXPECTED UTILITY SUBJECT TO AN EXOGENOUS RISK-LIMIT

upper or lower limit to the positions taken that arises naturally from the problem
specification. This is unlike mean-variance optimization in which the quadratic
form guarantees a finite solution for finite arguments. However, this defect can be
regularized away by introducing an exogenous risk limit, L > 0, to the problem.

6. INTERPRETATION OF THE RESULT AND
A PRACTICAL ALGORITHM FOR TRADERS
I believe that, despite the apparent na¨ıvety of the problem proposed, the solu-
tion is, in fact, quite interesting. It is well known that a risk-limited gross-profit
maximizing investor should obtain a position as large as possible in the direction
of the alpha[6]. The introduction of the budget threshold, as specified in this work,
changes such a long-short, or “binary,” trading algorithm to a three state system
that considers long, short, and flat positions only. The term στ(κ) can be thought
of as a “risk cost” that must be exceeded to make risk-taking worthwhile to the
investor.
