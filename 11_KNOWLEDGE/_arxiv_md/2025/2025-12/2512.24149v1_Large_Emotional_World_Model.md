Large Emotional World Model
Changhao Song, Yazhou Zhang†, Hui Gao, Chang Yang and Peng Zhang‡
College of Intelligence and Computing, Tianjin University, Tianjin, China
†yzhou_zhang@tju.edu.cn, ‡pzhang@tju.edu.cn

Abstract
World Models serve as tools for understand-
ing the current state of the world and pre-
dicting its future dynamics, with broad appli-
cation potential across numerous fields. As
a key component of world knowledge, emo-
tion significantly influences human decision-
making. While existing Large Language Mod-
els (LLMs) have shown preliminary capabil-
ity in capturing world knowledge, they primar-
ily focus on modeling physical-world regular-
ities and lack systematic exploration of emo-
tional factors. In this paper, we first demon-
strate the importance of emotion in understand-
ing the world by showing that removing emo-
tionally relevant information degrades reason-
ing performance. Inspired by theory of mind,
we further propose a Large Emotional World
Model (LEWM). Specifically, we construct the
Emotion-Why-How (EWH) dataset, which in-
tegrates emotion into causal relationships and
enables reasoning about why actions occur and
how emotions drive future world states. Based
on this dataset, LEWM explicitly models emo-
tional states alongside visual observations and
actions, allowing the world model to predict
both future states and emotional transitions. Ex-
perimental results show that LEWM more accu-
rately predicts emotion-driven social behaviors
while maintaining comparable performance to
general world models on basic tasks.

Introduction
World Models are designed to understand envi-
ronments and predict their future states (Ha and
Schmidhuber, 2018). They function by construct-
ing implicit representations from world knowledge
and simulating possible evolutions, thereby sup-
porting reasoning and decision-making (Hafner
et al., 2025). Such models have found broad appli-
cations in autonomous driving, robotics, and social
simulation, where they enhance perception, plan-
ning, and interactive capabilities.

Current World Models predominantly emphasize
capturing physical regularities and environmental
dynamics, such as Sora’s ability to generate visu-
ally realistic videos that adhere to basic physical
principles (Brooks et al., 2024). However, real-
world human behavior is often driven by social and
emotional factors that are not fully encapsulated
by physical or logical rules (Park et al., 2023). As
illustrated in Fig 1, an individual experiencing low
mood may resort to impulsive spending to allevi-
ate emotional distress—a behavior that contradicts
rational cost-benefit reasoning and may lead to fi-
nancial waste. This discrepancy between emotion-
driven actions and logic-based predictions reveals
a critical gap in existing world models, limiting
their capacity to fully simulate the complexities of
real-world social systems.
As recent studies have demonstrated, Large Lan-
guage Models (LLMs) contain stable and trace-
able affective circuits that consistently drive emo-
tional expressions across various contexts (Wang
et al., 2025). Moreover, these models encompass
neuron groups that exert causal influence on emo-
tional recognition and generation (Lee et al., 2025).
These findings indicate that affective mechanisms
are not only present but also functionally active
within LLMs. Based on this understanding, we
design an emotion-filtering module as a prelimi-
nary validation experiment and incorporate it into

Guided by theory-of-mind principles, we de-

which embeds emotion into structured causal rela-

only about why actions emerge under particular
emotional conditions, but also how evolving emo-
tional states influence subsequent world changes.
Building on this foundation, we introduce the Large
Emotion World Model (LEWM), a novel architec-
tural framework that extends conventional world
models by explicitly incorporating affective reason-
ing. Built upon EWH, LEWM jointly represents
emotional states, visual observations, and human
actions within a unified modeling paradigm, en-
abling simultaneous prediction of future states and
emotional transitions. In doing so, LEWM strength-
ens affective reasoning capability while promoting
a more coherent integration of emotional knowl-
edge into world understanding.
We conducted an empirical evaluation of the pro-
posed method across a series of emotional and non-
emotional tasks, and compared its performance
with several SoTA general world models.
We
highlight three key findings: (1) Incorporating an
emotion-filtering module causes a marked decline
in subjective tasks (up to 8%↓in Acc), while it
leads to a slight drop (approximately 1%↓) in objec-
tive reasoning and knowledge tasks, indicating that
emotional context acts as a systematic modulator
that shapes model behavior across diverse cognitive
demands; (2) LEWM achieves promising results in
emotion-driven behavior prediction, demonstrating
that incorporating emotional reasoning into world
models can enhance the accuracy of predicting real-
world human actions; (3) LEWM performs compa-
rably to general world models on basic tasks, which
suggests that integrating emotional understanding
does not compromise the fundamental performance
of the model on general tasks.
Our main contributions are as follows:
• We design an emotion-filtering module and
empirically verify that emotional informa-
tion systematically modulates model behavior
across diverse tasks.
• We construct EWH dataset, which encodes
emotion-driven causal relationships through
paired state–action–emotion transitions.
• We propose LEWM, a world modeling frame-
work that explicitly integrates affective rea-
soning, and demonstrate that it improves pre-
diction of emotion-driven human behaviors.

Related Work
World models are computational frameworks that
primarily serving two interconnected purposes:
building implicit internal representations of the en-
vironment and predicting future states. The con-
cept originates from early AI research on mental
models and has been revitalized through model-
based reinforcement learning (MBRL) and neural
architectures that learn latent dynamics (Ha and
Schmidhuber, 2018). A significant evolution oc-
curred with the rise of multimodal generative mod-
els, such as Sora, which demonstrate an emerging
capacity to simulate realistic video sequences and
physical interactions (Brooks et al., 2024). These
models are applied across key domains like au-
tonomous driving (Guan et al., 2024), task plan-
ning(Wu et al., 2023), and social simulacra(Park
et al., 2023). We propose the Large Emotional
World Model (LEWM), which enhances the ac-
curate modeling of emotion-driven human behav-
iors by integrating emotional reasoning, thereby
extending the applicability of world models to the
understanding of complex social dynamics.

Methodology
Building upon the findings from the preliminary
experiment, we further propose EWH dataset and
LEWM to enhance world modeling.
4.1
Emotion-Why-How(EWH) Dataset
Emotion-Why-How(EWH) is a multimodal dataset
designed to capture emotion-driven state transitions
in real-world behavioral scenarios. In contrast to
existing world model datasets that emphasize phys-
ical or logical rules, EWH focuses on scenarios
where Emotion acts as a key mediator shaping
human behavior and subsequent world dynamics,
enabling models to reason about Why actions occur
and How emotions alter future states.
As illustrated in Fig. 3, each state captures both
the visual appearance and acoustic expression of
human subjects in daily-life social contexts, in-
cluding movies, television scenes, and egocentric
recordings. Formally, each EWH sample is repre-
sented as a temporally grounded tuple:
⟨(S0, E0), A, (S1, E1)⟩

6
Conclusion
This study underscores the importance of incor-
porating emotional reasoning into world models
and presents the Large Emotional World Model
(LEWM) as an effective step toward this goal. By
leveraging the Emotion-Why-How (EWH) dataset
together with an emotion-aware modeling frame-
work, LEWM better aligns latent affective repre-
sentations with human emotional understanding.
Experimental results demonstrate that LEWM im-
proves the prediction of emotion-driven social be-
haviors while retaining comparable capability on
general world modeling tasks. These results point
toward a promising direction for developing more
comprehensive and human-aligned world models.
