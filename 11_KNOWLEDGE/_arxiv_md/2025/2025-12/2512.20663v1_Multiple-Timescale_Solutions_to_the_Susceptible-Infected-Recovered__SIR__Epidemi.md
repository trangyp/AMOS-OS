Multiple-Timescale Solutions to the Susceptible-Infected-Recovered (SIR) Epidemic
Model Equations in the Case of High Basic Reproduction Number
Oleg B. Shiryaev∗
Biomedicine Institute, Pirogov Russian National Research Medical University, Ostrovityanov Street 1, Moscow, Russia, 117513
(Dated: December 25, 2025)
A class of multiple-timescale asymptotic solutions to the equations of the susceptible-infected-
recovered (SIR) model is presented for the case of high basic reproduction number, with the inverse
of the latter employed as the expansion parameter. High values of the basic reproduction number, a
coefficient defined as the ratio of the infection and recovery rates built into the SIR model equations,
are associated with escalating epidemics. Combinations of fast and slow timescales in the suggested
multiple-timescale solutions prove adequate to reflect the acknowledged epidemic paradigm, which
is characterized by the concatenation of a sharp outbreak with a subsequent protracted plateau.
Explicit solutions for the numbers of the infected, susceptible, and recovered compartments of the
SIR model are derived via the asymptotic treatment, and the epidemic peak timing and magnitude
are assessed on this basis. The asymptotic results agree seamlessly with numerical simulations based
on the SIR model.
Keywords: susceptible-infected-recovered epidemic model; multiple-timescale method; asymptotics
I.
INTRODUCTION
The objective of the present inquiry is to develop explicit asymptotic solutions to the equations of the susceptible-
infected-recovered (SIR) epidemic model in the case of high basic reproduction number. Originally enacted in [1], SIR
model continues to serve the purpose of laconic description of epidemic processes. In its framework, the population
covered by infectious disease is described as a conglomerate of dynamically evolving compartments of the susceptible,
infected, and permanently immune recovered. Coefficients governing the transitions between the compartments - the
infection and recovery rates - tend to remain steady over considerable periods of time. The ratio between the two
quantities is identified in the context of the SIR model as the basic reproduction number, its high values signaling a
steep rise of the epidemic (in contrast, the epidemic fades if the basic reproduction rate is less than unity). Estimates
of the basic reproduction number vary widely in practice, but consensus converges on the order of several as applied
to the past COVID epidemic [2].
The SIR model equations are routinely solved numerically. Analytical, readily usable solutions for high reproduction
numbers are suggested in what follows. Such solutions should facilitate the application of the SIR and related models
in epidemiological studies and generally clarify significant mathematical aspects of the epidemic dynamics. The SIR
model is reformulated below in terms of time normalized to the inverse of the infection rate, with the resulting
equations involving only one coefficient, a dimensionless small parameter which coincides with the inverse of the
basic reproduction rate. As the next step, solutions to the problem are generated in the form of multiple-timescale
asymptotic series (the technique is exemplified e.g. in [3]; theory pertinent specifically to the type of problem explored
is spelled out in [4]). The asymptotic expressions combine fast and slow terms, the former corresponding to the
epidemic peak and the latter - to a protracted plateau. Thereby, the character of the multiple-timescale solutions
constructed via the asymptotic algorithm evokes the well-known pattern of the COVID epidemic.
It should be noted that multiple upgrades of the SIR model are available. In particular, enhancements are achieved
by adding compartments to the original epidemic model. Reader can find an extensive review on the subject in [5]. At
the same time, the potential for improving prediction accuracy by the incorporation of additional compartments into
the epidemic model is called into question in a number of studies. According to [6], the SIR model outperforms the
more extensive SEIR (susceptible, exposed, infected, recovered) one in dealing with the dataset acquired in Wuhan
under the containment regime. An argument in favor of the SIR model over the SEIR one specifically with regard
to COVID is that, according to prevalent evidence, individuals are capable of spreading the disease over the entire
time of staying infected, including the latency period [7]. As another update of the basic model, SIR simulations
with time-dependent transition rates are carried out [8]. The SIR and related models are also generalized to be
implemented on networks which emerge due to various venues of population mobility [9]. A thorough survey of the
available epidemic models, along with broader coverage of the topic of epidemic-related simulations, are given in [10].

IV.
CONCLUSIONS
Multiple-timescale asymptotic solutions to the equations of the SIR epidemic model are established in the present
study for the case of high basic reproduction number, with the inverse of the latter playing the role of a small
parameter for the expansion. Explicit expressions for the compartments of susceptible, infected, and recovered vs
time are derived.
The classical SIR theory continues to be widely employed in the modeling of the epidemic dynamics. Within the
SIR perspective, the affected population is split into three dynamically interconnected compartments - the susceptible
who are vulnerable to the disease, the currently infected, and the recovered who are assumed to retain immunity. The
basic reproduction number, which may be defined in the context of the SIR model as the ratio of the transmission
and recovery rates (both built into the model), plays the role of a switch between the epidemic scenarios as its values
less than unity imply a muted epidemic regime, while its high magnitudes are indicative of intense unfolding of the
process.
The suggested approximate solutions to the SIR model equations are developed in the form of series with terms

simultaneously depending on the fast and slow variables equal to time normalized to the inverse of either the transmis-
sion or the recovery rate. The duality reflects the fact that the solutions represent a concatenation of a fast-evolving
outbreak which peaks early in the course of the epidemic and a protracted plateau. The solutions describing the sus-
ceptible, infected, and recovered compartments are resolved as compact, directly usable expressions. The asymptotic
results agree thoroughly with the numerical solutions for the SIR model.
The explicit approximate solutions to the SIR model equations are used above to calculate analytically the timing
and height of the epidemic peak vs the initial conditions for the three-compartment system.

the compartments from empirical data, the rates being instrumental in predictive simulations of epidemic processes.
