Abstract. As stars traverse the Milky Way, their orbits evolve through perturbations that alter their orbital radii. These changes arise
from two mechanisms: churning, which modiﬁes angular momentum, and blurring, which induces eccentric orbits without major
angular momentum change. To assess whether churning or blurring dominates the dynamical evolution of Gaia-ESO stars, we reﬁne
Galactic chemical-evolution models by constructing ﬁner grids that span a wider age range. Using a generalised additive model
(GAM), we estimate stellar birth radii beyond the limits of binned metallicity models and compare them with dynamical parameters
derived from Gaia parallaxes and proper motions, and Galpy. Our metallicity-stratiﬁed sample, grouped through hierarchical
clustering of 21 chemical abundances, reveals clear migratory signatures: metal-rich stars formed in the inner disc preferentially
move outwards, while more metal-poor stars formed at larger radii tend to migrate inwards. About 75% of stars show signs of
churning, while the remainder are largely undisturbed or shaped by blurring. These patterns vary among chemical groups, likely
reﬂecting interactions with the Galactic bar and spiral arms.

1. Introduction
The Milky Way (MW) is a barred spiral galaxy, most likely
of SBbc classiﬁcation (Kormendy & Bender, 2019), composed
of several interconnected stellar components, each preserving
distinct clues to its formation history. Its thin and thick discs
dominate the stellar mass in the Solar neighbourhood, with the
thin disc hosting younger, more metal-rich, and kinematically
cooler populations, while the thick disc is older, more metal-
poor, and dynamically hotter (Gilmore & Reid, 1983; Bensby
et al., 2014; Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard, 2016). The central re-
gions are structured around a boxy/peanut-shaped barred bulge,
shaping the stellar orbits in the inner Galaxy (Wegg & Gerhard,
2013; Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard, 2016; Barbuy et al., 2018;
Zoccali & Valenti, 2026). Surrounding these disc components
lies the stellar halo, a diﬀuse and dark-matter- and accretion-
dominated structure with wide metallicity and kinematic distri-
butions (Helmi, 2020).
Although the MW disc is often described in terms of dis-
tinct structural components, stars do not remain ﬁxed at the
radii where they formed. Instead, their angular momenta can
evolve over Gyr timescales through interactions with Galactic
non-axisymmetric features such as spiral arms, the central bar,
and transient perturbations. These processes, collectively known
as radial migration or churning, change a stars guiding radius
by altering its angular momentum while tending to reduce its
orbital eccentricity. This contrasts with blurring, in which stars
oscillate around the same guiding radius through epicyclic mo-
tions, increasing their radial excursions without experiencing
a net change in angular momentum. As a consequence, the
present-day chemo-dynamic distributions of disc stars are the
result of both churning and blurring, producing extensive dy-
namical reshuﬄing that complicates any attempt to infer the
Galaxys evolutionary history from current positions alone (e.g.
Sellwood & Binney, 2002; Schönrich & Binney, 2009; Frankel
et al., 2020).
Quantifying the extent of stellar mixing across the Galaxy
is challenging because not all components preserve informative
chemo-dynamic signatures. The bulge is dominated by complex
bar-driven orbital families, rapid early star formation, and over-

Bonaca et al., 2017). By contrast, the thin disc retains com-

chemical gradients, making it the most suitable environment in
which to quantify radial migration and its contribution to disc
evolution.
In this context, our recent work Dantas et al. (2025) de-
veloped a data-driven framework to estimate stellar birth radii
using chemo-dynamic information from the Gaia-ESO survey
(Gilmore et al., 2022; Randich et al., 2022). By combining the
widely spaced bins of the chemical-enrichment models from
Magrini et al. (2009) with a generalised additive model (GAM),
we directly inferred the birth locations of our thin disc stars and
compared them with their current guiding radii, allowing us to
quantify inward and outward migrators and to characterise the
degree of radial mixing. In this proceedings contribution, we
brieﬂy summarise the main results of Dantas et al. (2025) and
present additional visualisations that further highlight the under-
lying chemo-dynamic trends.
2. Data and methodology
Our analysis is based on the high-resolution Gaia-ESO survey
sample described in Dantas et al. (2023, 2025), comprising 1188
thin disc FGK stars observed with UVES (R ≈47 000) and pro-
cessed via the homogenised iDR6 pipeline (Sacco et al., 2014;
Smiljanic et al., 2014; Hourihane et al., 2023; Worley et al.,
2024). For each star, we adopted atmospheric parameters and
detailed chemical abundances (21 species of 18 elements), com-
plemented by ages from Unidam (Mints & Hekker, 2017, 2018)
via PARSEC isochrone ﬁtting (Bressan et al., 2012), and or-
bital parameters computed with Galpy (Bovy, 2015) using Gaia
EDR3 astrometry (Gaia Collaboration, Brown, et al., 2021) and
the McMillan (2017) potential. Following Boesso and Rocha-
Pinto (2018) and the full description in Dantas et al. (2023), we
applied a hierarchical clustering (HC) analysis to these abun-
dances, grouping stars into chemically similar stellar popula-
tions. The data selection and treatment is fully described in
Dantas et al. (2023).
To estimate the birth radii of these stars, we constructed
a GAM using the radial metallicity proﬁles of Magrini et al.
(2009), which provide Galactocentric radius (R) as a function
of [Fe/H] gradients and formation epoch (t). The GAM extends
these sparsely binned theoretical proﬁles into a smooth, contin-
uous surface R([Fe/H], t), enabling us to infer the most likely
birth radius (Rb) for any star given its metallicity and age. We
used the mgcv package in the r environment to implement the
GAM with smoothing splines, including a two-dimensional in-
teraction term to capture non-linear couplings between [Fe/H]
and formation time. The method is described and discussed in
detail in Dantas et al. (2025).
Each stars Rb was then derived by applying the GAM to
[Fe/H] and its formation epoch, deﬁned as t = 13.8 Gyr −t⋆,
where t⋆is the median stellar age from Unidam. Uncertainties
were evaluated using a bootstrap procedure in which stellar
parameters ([Fe/H], t) were resampled 1000 times following
Dantas et al. (2023). This provides posterior distributions, quan-
tiles, and robust standard errors for Rb. Throughout, ⟨Rb⟩de-
notes the median of each stars bootstrapped ⟨Rb⟩distribution,
and we compare it with the current guiding radius (⟨Rg⟩) to iden-
tify inward-, outward-, and non-migrating populations across the
chemically deﬁned stellar groups.
3. Results, discussion, and ﬁnal remarks
The chemically distinct groups identiﬁed in Dantas et al. (2023)
provide a useful framework for examining how stars of diﬀer-
ent metallicities have contributed to the build-up of the Galactic
thin disc. These groups span a wide range of abundance patterns
and ages, reﬂecting the discs extended formation history and the
diversity of enrichment pathways. By combining their chemical
signatures with the ⟨Rb⟩inferred from the GAM, we can evalu-
ate how strongly each group has been aﬀected by stellar mixing
mechanisms and how these processes vary across the thin discs
metallicity sequence.
The radial redistribution of these populations can be seen by
comparing the distributions of ⟨Rb⟩and ⟨Rg⟩directly, as shown
in Fig. 1. The side-by-side letter-value plots (Hofmann et al.,
2017) show the median values and the spread of ⟨Rb⟩and ⟨Rg⟩
within each chemical group, ordered by decreasing [Fe/H] (from
left to right). Since all stars have been recently observed in the
Solar neighbourhood, their ⟨Rg⟩distributions are broadly simi-
lar to one another. However, in terms of ⟨Rb⟩each group has a
distinct behaviour. Metal-rich stars exhibit smaller ⟨Rb⟩, being
formed closer to the inner Galaxy, showcasing outward migra-
tion. As metallicity decreases, this trend slowly reverts, show-
ing an increasingly more skewed distribution toward larger ⟨Rb⟩.
The metal-poor thin disc group displays the broadest birth-radius
spread, consistent with a larger number of stars migrating in-
ward. Altogether, the ﬁgure highlights the diversity of migration
histories encoded in the chemically resolved thin disc.
The proportions of stars that have migrated outwards, in-
wards, or remained approximately at their birth radii are illus-
trated in Fig. 2. This visualisation highlights clear, coherent
trends across the chemical groups. The super-metal-rich popu-
lation (Group 2) is strongly dominated by outward migrators,
consistent with their origin in the inner disc and subsequent dis-
placement to larger guiding radii (this particular Group was dis-
cussed in detail in Dantas et al., 2023). As metallicity decreases,
the balance shifts: the intermediate groups show a higher frac-
tion of stars whose ⟨Rg⟩are compatible with their inferred ⟨Rb⟩,
while the most metal-poor thin disc groups display increasing
fractions of inward migrators. These contrasting trends reinforce
the well-established link between metallicity and birth environ-
ment, while also showing how radial migration modulates this
relationship across the thin disc.
Interpreting these patterns requires considering the interplay
between chemical evolution, the inside-out growth of the disc,
and the eﬃciency of angular-momentum redistribution. Metal-
rich stars formed in the rapidly enriched inner regions are more
susceptible to outward churning, which transports them into the
Solar neighbourhood. Conversely, the more metal-poor groups
contain older stars that likely formed over a broader radial range
and at epochs when the radial metallicity gradient was steeper.
Their present-day inward shifts are consistent with the combined
action of churning and perhaps (mild) blurring, bringing stars
from a range of formation radii into overlapping regions of the
thin disc. Together, these trends align with the quantitative mix-
ing estimates presented in Dantas et al. (2025), while Fig. 2 com-

In summary, the new visualisations presented here oﬀer
a complementary perspective on the ﬁndings of Dantas et
al. (2025). By emphasising relative fractions and distribution
shapes, rather than absolute counts or model-based diagnostics,
these ﬁgures provide an accessible view of how diﬀerent stellar
populations have contributed to the modern thin disc. They re-
inforce the importance of chemically informed analyses and of
ﬂexible modelling tools such as GAMs in disentangling the discs
complex chemo-dynamic assembly. Continued development of
these approaches, together with larger and more precise spec-
troscopic samples, will further reﬁne our understanding of the
Galaxys long-term evolutionary history.
