Using the Jones Polynomial to Prove Infinite
Families of Knots Satisfy the Cosmetic Surgery
Conjecture
F. M. Brady
December 23, 2025
Abstract
This paper computes the Jones polynomial and the invariants obstruct-
ing cosmetic surgery which are derived from it for two infinite families
of knots, proving they satisfy the Purely Cosmetic Surgery Conjecture.
Both the method of computation and the method for generating families
of knots extend.

Introduction
Let S3
r(K) be the manifold obtained from surgery along K with slope r.
Conjecture 1 (Purely Cosmetic Surgery Conjecture). If K is a knot
and S3
r(K) ∼= S3
r′(K) via an orientation preserving homeomorphism then
either K is the unknot or r = r′.
For a knot K to have cosmetic surgeries, the surgery slopes r and r′
must be ±2, the genus of K must be 2, and the Alexander polynomial of
K must be identically 1 [1]. The knot K must also be prime [17] and its
Jones polynomial VK must satisfy V ′′
K(1) = 0 and V ′′′
K (1) = 0. [9]. The
derivatives of Jones polynomials are examples of finite type invariants; the
higher order derivatives are powerful but previous obstructions were more
complicated relations dependent on surgery slopes [10]. We will derive,
from [10] and the restrictions on the Alexander polynomial from [1], the
following.
Proposition 1. If a knot K has cosmetic surgeries, then V ′′′′
K (1) = 0.
This paper presents a method for computing the Jones polynomial,
and, in particular, the derivatives of the Jones polynomial evaluated at 1,
for infinite families of knots obtained from additions of twists at crossings.
The method enables direct computation of arbitrarily high derivatives
of the Jones polynomial for these infinite families, allowing higher order
derivative restrictions to be computationally useful in general.
Theorem 1. The infinite families of knots generated from the knots 76,
812 and 1058 satisfy the Cosmetic Surgery Conjecture.

Relation to Previous Work
The knots considered here are knots of canonical genus 2. The canonical
genus of a knot is the minimal genus obtained from the Seifert algorithm
over any possible diagram.
While the potential difference between the
genus and the canonical genus of any knot is unbounded [11], considering
knots of a given canonical genus is a useful way of ordering families of
knots.
Explicitly, all knots of canonical genus 2 can be related by t′
2
moves to one of the knots: 51, 62, 63, 75, 76, 77, 812, 814, 815, 923, 925,
938, 939, 941, 1058, 1097, 10101, 10120, 11123, 11148, 11329, 121097, 121202,
or 134233 [16]. A t′
2 move is the operation of changing a single crossing to
three crossings, which corresponds to introducing a full twist to a band
in a Seifert diagram, visualized as a collection of nodes and connecting
bands. See 1.
There is some overlap between canonical genus 2 families of knots and
knots previously shown to satisfy the Cosmetic Surgery Conjecture, but
enumerating these overlaps verifies that the result is novel. For instance,
the knots related to 51 are precisely the genus 2, 5-stranded pretzel knots.
Pretzel knots have already been proven to satisfy the conjecture [15].
All prime alternating knots of genus 2 and signature 0 (hence τ invari-
ant 0) can be obtained by starting with one of six knots, 63, 77, 812, 941,
1058, 121202, and performing t′
2 moves. (Proposition 3.2; [16]) These are
not excluded from consideration entirely, since it is possible to introduce
