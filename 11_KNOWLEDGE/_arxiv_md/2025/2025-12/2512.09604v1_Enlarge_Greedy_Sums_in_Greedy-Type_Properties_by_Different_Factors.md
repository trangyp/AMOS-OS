ABSTRACT. It was previously known that the almost greedy (AG) property essentially
remains the same when we enlarge greedy sums in the classical definition by a factor
λ ⩾1. The present paper shows that if instead, we enlarge greedy sums in a refor-
mulation of the AG property, we obtain a weaker one. However, the new property is
essentially independent of the enlarging factor λ once λ > 1. In contrast, we observe
a continuum of partially greedy-like properties by varying λ ∈[1, ∞). Last but not
least, under a threshold for λ, we characterize the isometric version of the weakened
AG property. Specifically, the characterization holds if and only if λ ∈[1, 2].

2. MAIN RESULTS AND PRELIMINARIES
In this section, we record our main results along with useful preliminaries. We start
with the first result that answers Question 1.7.
Theorem 2.1. Let 1 ⩽λ1 < λ2. There exists an unconditional basis that is λ2-PG but
not λ1-PG, i.e., we have a continuum of PG-type properties.
We remark that Theorem 2.1 differentiates the case of PG bases from the case of
greedy bases, where we obtain the same property regardless of the λ in (1.1) as long as
λ > 1.
We now turn to Question 1.9 by first recalling relevant previous work. Companions
to the PG bases are the so-called reverse partially greedy (RPG) bases introduced by
Dilworth and Khurana [18]. Like the AG property, enlarging greedy sums in the original
definition of RPG bases does not weaken the property. To obtain an analog of Theorem
1.6 for RPG bases, [12, Theorem 1.1] “intervalizes" the property. We, therefore, suspect
that the reformulation asked by Question 1.9 must be an “interval version" of the AG
property. A natural candidate is the reformulation in [7]. Denote the collection of all
finite intervals of N by I, which includes the empty set.

Proposition 2.5. Let 1 < λ1 < λ2. A basis is λ1-AG2 if and only if it is λ2-AG2.
As an intermediate step to prove Theorem 2.4 and Proposition 2.5, we characterize
λ-AG2 bases, using what we called the λ-symmetry for largest coefficients of type 2
(λ-SLC2) (Definition 4.1) and λ-democracy of type 2 (Definition 4.6).
Theorem 2.6. Let λ ⩾1. The following are equivalent:
i) a basis is λ-AG2;
ii) a basis is quasi-greedy and λ-SLC2;
iii) a basis is quasi-greedy and λ-democratic of type 2.
Here is a corollary of Proposition 2.5 and Theorem 2.6:
Corollary 2.7. The following are equivalent:
i) a basis is λ-AG2 for (some) all λ > 1;
ii) a basis is quasi-greedy and λ-SLC2 for (some) all λ > 1;
iii) a basis is quasi-greedy and λ-democratic of type 2 for (some) all λ > 1.
We are also interested in the isometric theory. We show that the equivalence between
“λ-AG2 with constant 1" and “λ-SLC2 with constant 1" holds if and only if λ ⩽2.
Formally,
Theorem 2.8. For every λ ⩾1, we have the implication
λ-AG2 with constant 1 ⇐= λ-SLC2 with constant 1.
For λ ⩽2, the following equivalence holds
λ-AG2 with constant 1 ⇐⇒λ-SLC2 with constant 1.
For every λ > 2, there exists a basis that is λ-AG2 with constant 1 but is not λ-SLC2
with constant 1.

7. CONCLUSION
We have shown that all of the following greedy-type properties: greedy, AG, PG,
and RPG are weakened when we enlarge greedy sums in either their original defini-
tions or their equivalent reformulations that involve intervals (see Theorem 2.4 and [12,
Theorem 1.4]). Out of all these properties, only the PG property gives us a continuum
of PG-type properties as the enlarging factor λ varies in [1, ∞); for the greedy, AG,
and RPG properties, varying λ ∈(1, ∞) makes no difference (see [17, Theorem 3.3],
Theorem 2.1, and Propositions 2.5 and 6.5).
