Abstract
Accurate age verification can protect underage users from
unauthorized access to online platforms and e-commerce
sites that provide age-restricted services. However, accu-
rate age estimation can be confounded by several factors,
including facial makeup that can induce changes to alter per-
ceived identity and age to fool both humans and machines.
In this work, we propose DIFFCLEAN which erases makeup
traces using a text-guided diffusion model to defend against
makeup attacks. DIFFCLEAN improves age estimation (mi-
nor vs. adult accuracy by 5.8%) and face verification (TMR
by 5.1% at FMR=0.01%) compared to images with makeup.
Our method is robust across digitally simulated and real-
world makeup styles, and outperforms multiple baselines in
terms of biometric and perceptual quality. Our codes are
available at https://github.com/Ektagavas/DiffClean.
1. Introduction
Motivation. Our constant need to stay digitally connected
has given rise to a wide range of online services, including so-
cial media platforms, chat rooms, and dating websites. Sev-
eral online services rely on digital age verification, typically
age verification from selfie-styled facial scans (e.g., Yoti,
Ondato, TrustStamp). But there have been reports of minors
bypassing age verification checks, exposing them to cyber-
bullying and online dangers. 12 In such scenarios, precise age
estimation is critical in protecting underage users. Recently,
government agencies and commercial content providers are
actively pushing for online facial age verification for protect-
ing teenagers (US’s Identifying Minors Online, UK’s Online
Safety Act, YouTube) [6, 11] from unauthorized access to
adult-themed websites.

Age estimation is intrinsically challenging due to complex
genetic, environmental, demographic, and subjective factors;
this is compounded when an individual puts on makeup.
Facial makeup significantly alters the perception of facial
features, affecting reliable age prediction [13, 18, 47]. The
perceived age in younger people with makeup is generally
overestimated, while in older people the predicted age is
underestimated [59, 62]. Makeup can be used to bypass
automated age estimation [8], the primary issue addressed
in this work, but we also look at its impact on face recog-
nition [58]. Intuitively, we can design a makeup-invariant
age estimator and face matcher, specifically designed for
minors by using “more data”. However, this immediately
raises privacy concerns: we will need data on minors wear-
ing makeup. The next strategy might be to generate synthetic
makeup images for training new models. But makeup can
vary in terms of intensity, texture, color, region of applica-
tion, and subjective preferences. So, synthetic data may not
capture the full diversity of facial makeup.
Our goal. We propose to defend against makeup attacks
by designing a makeup removal framework, known as DIF-
FCLEAN. It will be deployed as a plug-in module that will
filter the selfie-styled facial input image to erase makeup
traces, which will primarily benefit accurate age estimation,
and secondary tasks like identity verification.
Our approach. We formulate makeup removal as an im-
age translation task, leveraging generative models known
for seamless and controllable digital makeup transfer [9,
29, 35, 40, 50, 55, 64]. Unlike prior works that rely on at-
tribute editing or reference-based adversarial transfer, our
method DIFFCLEAN is a reference-free, text-guided diffu-
sion model that transforms a face image with makeup to a
face image without makeup using a combination of CLIP
loss (for makeup detection), perceptual loss (for fidelity re-
tention), biometric loss (for identity restoration), and age
loss (for age restoration); see Fig. 1. Our method primar-
ily enhances age estimation accuracy by removing makeup

Contributions. Our main contributions are as follows:
1. We propose DIFFCLEAN, a novel reference-free text-
guided diffusion-based makeup removal framework that
digitally erases the traces of makeup present in face im-
ages, and improves age estimation (minor vs. adult ac-
curacy by 5.8%) and face verification (TMR by 5.1% at
FMR=0.01%) compared to images with makeup, while
retaining visual quality (SSIM=0.98, PSNR=35.64).
2. We utilize text-guided CLIP loss for detecting makeup,
perceptual loss for minimizing visual artifacts, and a com-
bination of age and identity losses for restoring the facial
cues affected by makeup. The combined losses ensure
that our model works on both makeup and non-makeup
images without producing artifacts.
3. We empirically demonstrate that DIFFCLEAN works on
both digitally simulated and real makeup images across
variations in pose, illumination, facial accessories, and
makeup styles, while exhibiting demographic fairness.
Our method can potentially assist with digital age verifi-
cation to protect underage users from cyber harms.
Responsible Usage. We recognize that our application
focus is a sensitive topic, particularly since any modeling
or algorithmic errors may adversely impact a vulnerable
demographic (minors/teenagers). We strongly advocate for
the ethical use of DIFFCLEAN only to assist with facial
analytics (not for the purposes of malicious image editing).
2. Related Work
Facial Age Prediction. [33] used statistical face models
generated by applying Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
on a set of face images for age estimation. Texture features
such as Local Binary Pattern (LBP) and its variants have been
used for reliable age estimation [21, 51, 67]. Biologically-
inspired features have been used in [22, 24]. Deep-learning
-based approaches rely on convolutional neural networks
(CNNs) and transformers for improved robustness. A multi-
stream CNN was used to learn high-dimensional structured
information from face patches in [66]. Deep CNN was
used for age estimation in unconstrained settings in [34,
44, 61]. In [42], each age was treated as a rank to apply
ordinal regression problem to age estimation using a deep
CNN. A multi-task learning approach for heterogeneous
attribute estimation was adopted in [25]. Deep Expectation
(DEX) [45] formulated age estimation by converting it into
a classification-regression problem. SSRNet [65] adopted a
compact soft stagewise regression model to perform multi-
class classification. Auxiliary facial cues were used to boost
reliable age estimation in [38, 68]. Deep random forests
were used for age estimation in [52]. FP-age [37] used a
face parsing attention module to incorporate facial semantics
into age estimation. Swin Transformer with attention-based
convolution (ABC) for facial age estimation was proposed
in [53]. In [32], a multi-input transformer-based model,
MiVOLO, predicted age and gender using facial and full
body information.
Age Estimation in Minors. Limited works have focused
on improving age estimation performance in minors. In [5],
a finetuned VGG-based ‘children’ network was used for ap-
parent age estimation of children aged from 0 to 12 years.
[4] trained ResNet50 on the underage facial dataset ‘Vis-
AGe’ to improve the age estimation for minors. The need for
fast, accurate, and robust age estimation and verification has
gained traction recently. NIST Face Analysis Technology
Evaluation (FATE) Age Estimation & Verification reports
that subjects aged between 8 and 12 years tend to be overesti-
mated to be 13 to 16 years old with false positives as high as
0.76 3; higher errors are reported for females than for males,
specifically, in 17-year-olds [26]. The performance of facial
age estimation algorithms can be affected by several factors
like gender, ethnicity, image quality, facial expression, and
makeup [3, 12].
Impact of Cosmetic Makeup on Facial Analytics. [58]
demonstrated the impact of facial makeup on automatic face
recognition. [18] observed that makeup applied to different
facial regions had varying effects on age prediction quan-
tified as Young Index (YI). [17] observed that the Identi-
fication Rate (IDR) decreased with increasing amount of
makeup. [7, 23, 27] focused on making face recognition
more robust to makeup. Authors in [8] observed that facial
cosmetics (both real makeup and synthetically retouched)
affect automated age estimation, causing a mean absolute
error of up to 5.84 years. They further indicated that lip

makeup had the least impact on automated age estimation,

Facial Makeup Transfer and Removal. Approaches
for makeup transfer relied on GANs for style-transfer net-
works [9, 29, 35, 40, 57, 64]. Later, diffusion models were
used [46, 56, 70]. Makeup removal, on the other hand, poses
a more challenging task where the goal is to reveal the face
under makeup without adversely affecting the biometric and
perceptual features. Anti-makeup [36] integrated two adver-
sarial networks, both at pixel and feature levels, to generate
identity-preserving synthetic non-makeup faces. LADN [20]
used local disentanglement with adversarial network for
makeup transfer and removal.
[69] replaced convolution
layers in GAN with resnet blocks to design makeup removal
network. PSGAN++ [39] used pose and expression robust
spatial-aware GAN for detail-preserving makeup transfer
and removal. CLIP2Protect [50] and DiffAM [55] use ad-
versarial makeup transfer for privacy with capability to gen-
erate ‘no makeup’ faces. [46] used domain translation for a
wide array of makeup tasks, including makeup transfer and
removal as a one-for-all diffusion-based framework, but suf-
fers from lack of generalizability. DeBeauty [60] is the only
reference-free approach to the best of our knowledge that
uses adversarial de-makeup flow and relocation deformation
flow to remove makeup.
3. Proposed Method
Our proposed method specifically targets mitigating the im-
pact of makeup on automated facial age estimation and,
additionally, facial identity verification. Defending against
makeup attacks can prevent minors from bypassing age ver-
ification intended for protection against cyber abuse. One
strategy may involve collecting data of minors wearing
makeup for improving the robustness of age estimators; this
poses strong privacy concerns. So, we propose a generative
model DIFFCLEAN, which is designed to mitigate age vari-
ations arising due to makeup. There are two challenges in
designing this network: (1) we need a robust makeup style
generator to synthesize data needed for training the makeup
removal model and thus circumvent data collection; (2) we
need a robust age estimator to serve as an auxiliary classifier
to guide DIFFCLEAN to restore age-specific cues. For the
makeup style generator, we use EleGANt [64] for locally-
editable makeup transfer; see Fig. 2. For the age estimator,
we use SSRNet [65] fine-tuned on UTKFace [71] dataset
that has images from different age groups.

4. Experiments and Analysis
4.1. Datasets
Training/Fine-tuning: We used the UTKFace [71] dataset
to fine-tune SSRNet for age estimation with a training set of
15,364 images and a testing set of 6,701 images. The original
set of 20K images was split into training and testing splits
(70:30) after filtering out images with ages ≥70 yrs. We
adopted a two-step approach for fine-tuning DIFFCLEAN for
makeup removal. First, we used 300 images (200 training,
100 validation) from MT dataset [35] following the guide-
lines in [55]. We further refined the model by additionally
fine-tuning it on 300 images (200 training, 100 validation)
from the UTKFace dataset after applying makeup using El-
eGANt [64] with the combined loss. Thus, our training set
Dtr consisted of a total of 600 images.
Evaluation:
We tested our proposed DIFFCLEAN on
both synthetic makeup (on FFHQ) and real-world makeup
(LADN, BeautyFace, Makeup-Wild) images.
Synthetic makeup data generation: We sampled 2,556 im-
ages from the Flickr-Faces-HQ Dataset (FFHQ) [31] by

4.2. Metrics
We evaluated DIFFCLEAN for (1) age restoration using
Mean Absolute Error (MAE) between ground truth (if avail-
able) and predicted age values (lower is better); Age group
accuracy to assess if the predicted age is correctly grouped
into age bins (higher is better); Minor/Adult Accuracy to
evaluate if the predicted age ≥18 to classify as adult, oth-
erwise, minor (higher is better). We evaluated DIFFCLEAN
for (2) identity verification by reporting the True Match
Rate (TMR) at a False Match Rate (FMR) of 0.01% and
illustrated the ROC curves. Finally, we computed (3) image
quality of generated images using SSIM and PSNR.
4.3. Implementation Details
The SSRNet model was fine-tuned with a batch size of 50
with an Adam optimizer with weight decay of 1e-4, a cosine
annealing scheduler, and a learning rate set to 1e-3 for 200
epochs with early stopping. The input image size is 64×64.
The makeup removal module takes the makeup image, Im,
of size 256×256 as input, and uses 40 DDIM inversion steps
with 6 DDIM sampling steps (total time steps=80), and noise
scale for latent image generation is controlled by cosine beta
scheduler [41]. We used the Adam optimizer with a learning
rate of 4e-3. The model is fine-tuned for 5 epochs. All ex-
periments were carried out on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU.
Hyperparameters in Eqn. 6 were obtained using grid search
on the validation set (see sensitivity analysis in Sec. 5.6). For
testing, we use MiVOLO [32] for age estimation (training
uses SSRNet) and MobileFace [10] and FaceNet [48] for
identity verification ( training uses ArcFace IRSE50 [28]).
4.4. Baselines
We compared DIFFCLEAN with 6 baselines (4 GAN-
based and 2 diffusion-based models): BeautyGAN [35],
LADN [20], PSGAN++ [39] CLIP2Protect [50], Dif-
fAM [55], and MAD [46]. We randomly selected six non-
makeup images as references for baselines following [60].

We present the results of biometric identity verification in

5.3. Image quality evaluation
We compared the images before and after makeup removal
in terms of image quality metrics: SSIM and PSNR, and
reported our findings in Table 2. We compute the Original
performance by comparing the clean FFHQ face images
(without makeup) with images simulated using EleGANt
(with makeup). Our algorithm is at par with DiffAM in
terms of SSIM=0.98 while being second best in terms of
PSNR=35.64, showing high retention of visual fidelity.
5.4. Real-world makeup generalizability
Real-world use cases, such as online age verification only
include a facial image with or without makeup; it does not in-
clude the individual’s actual age label. So, in this experiment,
we applied DIFFCLEAN to real-world makeup images from
the BeautyFace [63], LADN [20], and Makeup-Wild [29]
datasets, and used MiVOLO to predict age before and after
makeup removal. See examples of real-world makeup re-
moval in Figs. 6 and 7. Since we do not have ground truth
age labels, instead, we report both overestimation and under-
estimation error statistics in Table 3. Further, we analyzed
the confidence interval (CI) using a statistically significant
sample size of 3K images from BeautyFace using the stu-
dent’s t-distribution at a 95% confidence level. We obtain CI
= [2.88, 3.09] with a margin of error = 0.10 on the underesti-
mation errors, and CI = [2.75, 2.89] with a margin of error
of 0.07 on the overestimation errors. Results indicate that
DIFFCLEAN generalizes across various real-world makeup
styles, pose, and illumination variations, and can be readily
used in practical settings.

6. Conclusion
In this work, we investigate the inadequacies of automated
facial age estimators in presence of makeup. Makeup can
be used to bypass age verification, exposing minors to cyber
abuse. To address these issues, we designed DIFFCLEAN, a
reference-free diffusion-based makeup removal framework
that can be deployed as a ready-to-plugin pre-processing
module in a real-world application of online age verification
for protecting minors. We combined text-guided CLIP loss
with identity preservation and visual quality retention for
makeup removal while restoring age-relevant features. Eval-
uations show DIFFCLEAN outperforms competing baselines
in terms of age estimation (by 5.8%) and identity verification
(by 5.1%) and is robust across real-world makeup styles.
