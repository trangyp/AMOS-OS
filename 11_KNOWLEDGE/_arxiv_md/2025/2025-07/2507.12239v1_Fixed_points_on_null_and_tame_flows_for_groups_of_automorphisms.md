ALESSANDRO CODENOTTI
Abstract. Using a generalization of the Kechris-Pestov-Todorčević correspondence
due to Nguyen Van Thé we obtain fixed point theorems for null and tame actions of
groups of the form Aut(F), where F is a Fraïssé structure. In particular we show
that if Age(F) is a free joint embedding class, then every null flow Aut(F) ↷X
has a fixed point, while if Age(F) is a free amalgamation class, then every tame flow
Aut(F) ↷X has a fixed point.
1. Introduction
Given a topological group G, which we will always assume to be Hausdorff, a G-flow,
or simply a flow when G is clear from context, is a compact space X with a continuous
action G ↷X. A G-flow is called minimal if G · x = X for every x ∈X, equivalently if
there are no proper, closed, G-invariant subspaces. A G-flow Y is a factor of a G-flow X
if there there is a continuous surjection f : X →Y which is G-equivariant, that is
f(g · x) = g · f(x), for every x ∈X, g ∈G.
A classical result of Ellis shows that every topological group G has a universal minimal
flow, that is a minimal G-flow which has all minimal G-flows as factors, moreover this flow
is unique up to isomorphism [Ell60, Theorem 2] so that we can talk about the universal
minimal flow of G, denoted by M(G). A topological group G is called extremely amenable
if every G-flow has a fixed point, or equivalently if M(G) is a singleton. A celebrated re-
sult, now known as the KPT correspondence, established in [KPT05] by Kechris, Pestov
and Todorčević, characterizes which groups of the form Aut(F), where F is a Fraïssé
structure, are extremely amenable, in terms of a Ramsey property for Age(F). The KPT
correspondence was generalized by Nguyen van Thé [Ngu19, Theorem 1.2], where fixed
points on a specified class of Aut(F)-flows are obtained from an approximate version of
the Ramsey property of Age(F) for a specified family of colourings (see Theorem 3.4 for
a precise statement). We focus on the classes of null and tame Aut(F)-flows, those being
two notion of well-behavedness for flows (defined precisely in Definitions 2.6 and 2.11).
The study of tame dynamical systems was initiated by Köhler [Köh95] (under the name of
regular systems), who obtained a dicothomy for the enveloping semigroups of dynamical
systems consisting of interval maps, based on the Bourgain-Fremlin-Talagrand dichotomy
for pointwise compact sets of Baire class 1 functions [BFT78]. The dynamical version
of this dicothomy has more recently been extended to arbitrary metrizable systems in
[GM04], based on the notion of tameness, that is the nonexistence of continuous function
whose orbit contain a set equivalent to the standard basis of ℓ1 (indeed the roots of the
Bourgain-Fremlin-Talagrand dicothomy trace back to Rosenthal’s groundbreaking char-
acterization of Banach spaces not embedding ℓ1 [Ros78]). The theory of tame dynamical
systems has been greatly developed by various authors over the past few decades, see in
particular [GM18] and the reference therein for a survey. This has produced many equiv-
alent characterization of tameness, the most important for us being the one established
in [KL07] in terms of the nonexistence of infinite independent sets (see Definition 2.11).
The notion of nullness, initially defined in terms of sequence entropy, but later character-
ized in terms of the nonexistence of arbitrarily large (but finite) independence sets (see
Definition 2.6), is a natural strengthening of the notion of tameness. We introduce two
variants of the Ramsey property for the age of a Fraïssé class, closely related to the classes
of flows mentioned above, which we call the null and tame Ramsey properties. We then
show that free joint embedding classes satisfy the approximate null Ramsey property,

2. Definitions and background
2.1. Fraïssé theory. Let L = {Ri}i∈I be a relational language, where Ri has arity ni
for every i. A countable L-structure M is called ultrahomogeneous if whenever A, B ⊆M
are finite substructures and f : A →B is an isomorphism, it is possible to extend f
to an automorphism of M. A countable, ultrahomogeneous structure is called a Fraïssé
structure. Given a Fraïssé structure M we let Age(M) denote the class of finite structures
that embed into M. Using the ultrahomogeneity of M, it is easy to check that Age(M)
satisfies the following properties:
(1) Age(M) contains countably many structures, up to isomorphism.
(2) If A ∈Age(M) and B ⊆A, then B ∈Age(M), this is known as the hereditary
property.
(3) If A, B ∈Age(M), then there exists C ∈Age(M) into which both A and B can
be embedded, this is known as the joint embedding property
(4) If A, B, C ∈Age(M) and f1 : A →B, f2 : A →C are two embeddings, then
there exist D ∈Age(M) and embeddings g1 : B →D and g2 : C →D such that
g1 ◦f1 = g2 ◦f2, this is known as the amalgamation property.
A class of finite structures satisfying the four properties above is called a Fraïssé class
and, by a classical result due to Fraïssé, there is a one-to-one correspondence between
Fraïssé classes and Fraïssé structures. Given a Fraïssé class F, the (unique up to iso-
morphism) Fraïssé structure M with Age(M) = F is called the Fraïssé limit of F. The
Fraïssé limit of F is also characterized as the unique structure M with the following

Definition 2.15. Let G be a topological group. We denote with Mn(G) the universal
minimal null flow of G. This is a minimal null G-flow such that if X is any other minimal

Remark 3.9. We could not find whether the Henson property has been named before
in the literature, so we adopted this name, since it was first shown to hold for the ran-
dom graph and the Kn-free random graphs (also called the Henson graphs) by Henson
[Hen71, Theorem 3.1]. A natural strengthening, which we will call the strong Henson
property would be to require that every automorphism of h(A) extends uniquely to an
automorphism of F (indeed in [Hen71] the strong Henson property is established for the
graphs mentioned above). The strong Henson property has been established for all free
amalgamation classes in the PhD thesis [Bil12] and later expanded to the wider class of
Fraïssé classes with a stationary independence relation in [Mül15]. There exists in the
literature a similar but distinct notion of universal Fraïssé structure F, that is a Fraïssé
structure such that for every A ⊆F, Aut(F) contains a copy of Aut(A). For examples
all Fraïssé structures that admit Katětov functors are universal [KM17].
