A central challenge for understanding unconventional superconduc-
tivity in most strongly correlated electronic materials is their com-
plicated band structure and presence of competing orders. In con-
trast, quasi-two-dimensional organic spin liquids are single-band sys-
tems with superconductivity arising near the bandwidth-tuned Mott
metal-insulator transition in the absence of other orders. Here, we
study chemically substituted κ-organics in which superconducting
ﬂuctuations emerge in the phase coexistence region between the
Mott insulator and the Fermi liquid. Using magnetotransport and

Unconventional superconductivity1 (SC) has been reported in a wide range of strongly
correlated materials, including cuprates2, heavy fermion and organic systems3, iron-based
materials4, magic-angle twisted graphene5, and layered metallic kagome compounds6.
However, its precise physical origin has proved diﬃcult to unravel in most systems because
of their complicated electronic structure, often assuming a multi-band or multi-orbital
character within a unit cell, as well as the presence of many competing energy scales.
Furthermore, in many materials such as cuprates, unconventional SC emerges near an
insulating or magnetically ordered state, but understanding the interplay of the Mott
physics with SC remains a long-standing problem. In this regard, quasi-two-dimensional
(quasi-2D) organic charge-transfer salts7 have attracted considerable attention, precisely
because many of their properties can be understood in terms of the simplest single-band
Hubbard model8–10. In particular, the so-called κ-organics, which can be tuned across the
Mott metal-insulator transition (MIT) by varying the bandwidth7, feature a single half-
ﬁlled band near the Fermi energy and SC with typical transition temperatures Tc ∼10 K.

Remarkably, several materials in this family, those identiﬁed as spin liquids, do not dis-
play any sign of magnetic, charge, or structural orders accompanying the transition, while
clear signatures of SC arise on the metallic side7,8. Thus these materials represent the
simplest model systems to investigate strongly correlated phenomena and the related un-
conventional SC. However, tuning across the Mott transition is usually done by applying
external pressure, which makes many experiments diﬃcult or unfeasible, especially at
cryogenic temperatures.
Recent work11–13 has revealed, though, that most features of the bandwidth-tuned
Mott transition in these materials can be alternatively induced by chemical substitution,
which increases the electronic bandwidth W while remaining at ambient pressure. In
particular, in charge-transfer salts κ-[(BEDT-TTF)1−x(BEDT-STF)x]2Cu2(CN)3 (abbre-
viated κ-STFx), arguably an ideal realization of the single-band Hubbard model on a
half-ﬁlled triangular lattice, partial substitution of S atoms in the organic (BEDT-TTF)
donor molecules by Se results in Se-containing (BEDT-STF) with more extended orbitals
and, hence, a larger W (Fig. 1a and Supplementary Fig. 1). Detailed studies, combining
experiment and theory, have ﬁrmly established a number of issues concerning the normal
phase, including the formation or destruction of a strongly correlated Fermi liquid (FL)
on the metallic side12, and the physical nature of the coexistence region around the Mott
transition13. In addition, the onset of superconducting ﬂuctuations (SCFs) was observed
at Tc ∼2−3 K within the insulator-metal phase coexistence region14, but no bulk SC was
found down to the lowest experimental T ≈1.4 K. Therefore, since dimers of (BEDT-
TTF) and (BEDT-STF) in κ-STFx are arranged in an almost ideal triangular lattice in
the 2D planes (Fig. 1a and Supplementary Fig. 1), making this material a well-known
quantum spin liquid candidate15, the important question about the interplay of uncon-
ventional SC and the Mott MIT in systems with a high degree of geometrical frustration

In this work, we explore the emergence of SC in κ-STFx by extending the temperature
range of measurements by two orders of magnitude, down to T = 0.02 K, and in perpen-
dicular H up to 18 T. Surprisingly, we ﬁnd that the system fails to reach a zero-resistivity
(ρ = 0) state although SCFs are observed below the onset Tc within the insulator-metal
phase coexistence region (Fig. 1b). Our results, however, reveal several features consistent
with granular SC, reﬂecting the presence of superconducting domains embedded in the
metallic percolating cluster (see sketch in Fig. 1b). Using percolation theory18, we extract
the resistivity of the superconducting domains ρsc(T, H) and show that it exhibits quan-
tum criticality associated with a ﬁeld-tuned quantum superconductor-to-metal transition
(QSMT). At high H where SC is suppressed, we observe universal conductance ﬂuctu-
ations (UCFs) in the millimeter-sized samples, consistent with a picture of mesoscopic
phase segregation. Our results thus reveal that failed SC in κ-STFx arises due to the
intrinsic inhomogeneity characteristic of the phase coexistence region, providing a new
perspective on the “anomalous metal”, i.e. failed superconductor behavior reported in
diverse 2D superconducting systems16,17.
Failed superconductivity
The in-plane (bc) resistivity ρ was measured on several single-crystalline κ-STFx samples
with a nominal x = 0.10 −0.16 (see Methods), encompassing the phase coexistence and
FL regions (Fig. 1b). The maximum in ρ(T) (Fig. 1c and Supplementary Figs. 1b-d)
identiﬁes the Brinkman-Rice temperature TBR, above which metallic transport is lost and

quasiparticles are destroyed12. FL behavior ρ = ρ0 + AT 2 is observed at T < TFL for
higher x (Fig. 1c lower inset and Supplementary Fig. 1d). Within the phase coexistence
region, which is the focus of our work, ρ(T) exhibits a drop with decreasing T associated
with the onset of SCFs at Tc (Fig. 1c insets and Supplementary Fig. 1c), as discussed in
detail below. The values of TBR, TFL, and Tc are in good agreement with those from prior
work12–14 (see solid vs open symbols in Fig. 1b).
Measurements down to the lowest T show that, at T < Tc, a large positive magnetore-
sistance (MR) ρ(H) is observed in ﬁelds up to H ∼2.5 T (Fig. 2a, b and Supplementary
Fig. 2a, b), indicating a suppression of SC by H. At higher H, the MR is weak, represent-
ing the normal-state behavior. The ρ(T) curves extracted from the same data (Fig. 2c, d
and Supplementary Fig. 2c, d) show the H-dependence reminiscent of a ﬁeld-tuned su-
perconducting QPT. However, despite the presence of SCFs, the global SC fails to set in:
ρ(T, H = 0) does not drop all the way to zero but rather it saturates (Fig. 2d) or shows an
upturn (Fig. 2c) at T < Tsc, similar to the behavior of granular superconductors16,19,20.
The resistivity data thus suggest that SC is established at T ≈Tsc in superconducting
islands (grains or domains) with negligible coupling to each other within the normal-state
background, and it is the latter that determines ρ(T = 0, H = 0).
Revealing superconductivity: Percolation and quantum criticality
Previous joint theoretical and experimental work has demonstrated the dominance of
percolative eﬀects in the phase coexistence region13. Here we focus on using classical
percolation theory to extract the resistivity of the superconducting domains ρsc(T, H)
from the measured ρ(T, H). For simplicity, we assume that the sample is a mixture of
two types of domains: superconducting, with ρsc, and normal-state, with resistivity ρn.

It is intriguing that ρ(H) in the ﬁeld-revealed normal state (Fig. 2a, b) exhibits ﬂuctua-
tions that are highly reproducible upon sweeping H up and down over the entire measure-
ment range (Supplementary Fig. 8a). Figure 4c, d shows the corresponding conductance
(G = 1/R) ﬂuctuations, ∆G(H), which are random, i.e. uncorrelated beyond a small ﬁeld
scale H1/2 ≲0.5 T (Supplementary Fig. 8b and Supplementary Note 1), and get more
pronounced with decreasing T. The phenomenology of these sample-speciﬁc magnetoﬁn-
gerprints is consistent with UCFs33,34, which are a consequence of quantum interference
of electron waves propagating along diﬀerent paths, a manifestation of quantum coherent
transport. In an applied H, the ﬂux modiﬁes the relative phases of interfering paths via
the Aharonov-Bohm eﬀect, leading to reproducible conductance ﬂuctuations as a function
of H. Such ﬂuctuations in metallic samples have a universal T = 0 root-mean-squared
amplitude ∆Grms ∼e2/h in all dimensions, and independent of sample size and degree
of (weak) disorder. However, when the phase coherence length (i.e. inelastic scattering
length Lin) becomes smaller than the sample size, ∆Grms decreases with increasing T due
to classical self-averaging at scales larger than Lin. In addition, ∆Grms can be reduced
below its T = 0 value due to thermal averaging, i.e. because charge carriers with a dif-
ferent momentum acquire diﬀerent phases along the same path. For κ-STFx, we estimate
(Supplementary Note 1) that phase is thermally randomized over a length LT ∼1000 nm
at the lowest T. On the other hand, Lin ∼100 nm ≪LT, and thus thermal averaging
can be neglected. We ﬁnd that ∆Grms ∼0.1e2/h observed at the lowest T (Fig. 4c, d)
is indeed consistent with classical self-averaging (see Supplementary Note 1 for a more
detailed discussion). The surprising observation of UCFs in macroscopic, millimeter-sized
samples is thus attributed to transport through the metallic, FL regions that surround
insulating domains, i.e., it is consistent with the picture of phase coexistence just above
the percolation threshold for metallic conduction. However, the precise theoretical de-

Discussion
Our ﬁndings in highly frustrated κ-STFx paint a consistent picture of an intrinsically
granular superconductor, which we expect within a phase coexistence region. However, in
contrast to the behavior of pressure-tuned parent compounds in which a superconducting
phase arises on the metallic side7,8, here SC emerges only within a phase coexistence
region and it fails to develop global phase coherence even as T →0. We attribute the
fragility of the superconducting state in κ-STFx to disorder that is enhanced by chemical
substitution35. By taking into account percolative eﬀects, already known to be present
in the phase coexistence region7,13, we have demonstrated that the observed failed super-
conductivity is a result of the phase coexistence of a superconductor, the FL metal, and
the Mott insulator, such that isolated superconducting domains are concentrated in the
inﬁnite metallic cluster that barely percolates in the insulating background (see sketch in
Fig. 1b). It is interesting that a percolative scenario of the interplay of superconductivity
and the intrinsic inhomogeneity has been argued for cuprates36,37, and suggested14 also
for fullerides and other unconventional superconductors exhibiting a dome of Tc.
The percolation analysis has allowed us also to reveal evidence for quantum-critical
behavior of ρsc(T, H), the resistivity of superconducting islands, reﬂecting an underlying
H-tuned QSMT with the critical exponent zν ≈1.3. Assuming that z = 1 due to the
long-range Coulomb interaction between charges21,38, as measured, e.g., in 2D a-MoGe
thin ﬁlms23, we ﬁnd that ν ≈1.3. While there is currently no satisfactory theory for a
ﬁeld-tuned QSMT16, at least in the context of the 2D SIT the exponent ν ⩾1 is believed
to be in the universality class of the T = 0 SIT in a disordered system21, consistent with

with zν > 1 was found also in underdoped La-214 cuprates39,40, and attributed to the
interplay of disorder and quantum phase ﬂuctuations.
At low ﬁelds H < Hc and low temperatures where superconducting correlations are
present, the observed ρxx ̸= 0 with ρyx(H) ≈0 (Supplementary Fig. 7) is reminis-
cent of the behavior found within the vortex liquid regime of some conventional dis-
ordered 2D superconductors16,41 and oxide interfaces42, as well as in cuprates, includ-
ing YBa2Cu3Oy thin ﬁlms43, charge- and spin-stripe ordered La1.875Ba0.125CuO4 (ref. 44),
La1.7Eu0.2Sr0.1CuO4 and La1.48Nd0.4Sr0.12CuO4 (ref. 45), and Bi-2201 from underdoped to
heavily overdoped regions46. While ρyx(H) ≈0 accompanied by ρxx(T →0) = 0 has
been determined30,39,40,45,46 to be a consequence of the slowing down and freezing of the
vortex motion with decreasing T in the presence of disorder, the origin of ρyx(H) ≈0
with ρxx(T →0) ̸= 0, a characteristic of “anomalous metals” or “failed superconduc-
tors”, is still an open question16,47.
In κ-STFx, we have explained ρxx(T →0) ̸= 0
(Fig. 2c, d) by percolative eﬀects, i.e., the presence of isolated superconducting islands,
so that the measured ρyx(H) must then correspond to the Hall resistivity of the nor-
mal background; hence, ρyx(H) ≈0. Therefore, the remaining question is the origin of
the vanishing Hall response in the normal state over a wide range of H. Interestingly,
ρyx(H) ≈0 was observed45 also in the ﬁeld-revealed normal state of La1.7Eu0.2Sr0.1CuO4
and La1.48Nd0.4Sr0.12CuO4 over a huge range of H and T, where it seemed to be cru-
cially related to the presence of short-range charge-order domains or stripes, separated by
hole-poor spin stripes. The similarity to our simpler system, the metal-insulator phase
coexistence region in κ-STFx, suggests that ρyx(H) ≈0 might be a more general prop-
erty of systems with an intrinsic electronic inhomogeneity. Further insight into this issue
might come from experiments on other materials and from other techniques, especially

Methods
Samples. Plate-like single crystals of κ-[(BEDT-TTF)1−x(BEDT-STF)x]2Cu2(CN)3 with
various stoichiometries were prepared by electrochemical oxidation48; here (BEDT-TTF)
and (BEDT-STF) stand for bis-(ethylenedithio)-tetrathiafulvalene and bis-(ethylenedithio)-
diseleniumdithiafulvalene, respectively. Measurements of transport in the bc plane, with
current along the c-axis (the most conductive axis49), were performed on crystals with
x = 0.10 −0.16 at 1.5 K≤T ≤300 K (Fig. 1b, c and Supplementary Fig. 1b-d). De-
tailed magnetotransport measurements down to T = 0.02 K were carried out on samples
with x = 0.11 ± 0.01 and x = 0.12 ± 0.01. After ∼2 years, the absolute value of the
resistance of sample S1 (x = 0.12) changed, so we consider it a diﬀerent sample S1N
(x = 0.12). The dimensions of the S1 (S1N) sample are a × b × c = 0.04 × 0.4 × 0.9 mm3.
The data are shown for the voltage contacts (“V+” and “V–” in the sample image in
Suppl. Fig. 1a) separated by ≈0.44 mm. The dimensions of sample S2 (x = 0.11) are
a × b × c = 0.13 × 0.7 × 1.3 mm3, with a pair of voltage contacts on each side of the
sample, separated by ≈0.37 mm and ≈0.36 mm, respectively. The resistivity data ob-
tained from the two pairs are qualitatively, and nearly quantitatively consistent with each
other; nevertheless, for precision, we denote them as samples S2A and S2B. All contacts
are made using a DOTITE XC-12 carbon paste connected to 25 µm-thick gold wires.
The ac susceptibility measurements were carried out on dozens of κ-STFx samples with a
nominal x = 0.11 and dimensions comparable to those used for transport. The total vol-

amorphous indium pieces with twice the total volume (1.26 mm3) and similar shapes to

Measurements. The longitudinal resistance and Hall eﬀect were measured using the
standard four-probe ac method (typically ∼13 Hz) with the Lakeshore 372 AC resis-
tance bridge or Signal Recovery 7265 lock-in ampliﬁer and SR5113 voltage preampliﬁer.
Depending on the temperature and sample, the excitation current (density) ranged from

est T. These excitation currents were low enough to avoid Joule heating. Magnetic ﬁeld
was applied parallel and antiparallel to the a axis, and the Hall resistance was determined
from the transverse voltage by extracting the component antisymmetric in the magnetic
ﬁeld. To increase the signal-to-noise ratio, an excitation of 10 µA (∼10−2 Acm−2) was
used in the Hall eﬀect measurements. Despite potential heating, this does not aﬀect the
main conclusion that the Hall resistivity ρyx ≈0 and, in particular, it is negligible com-
pared to the longitudinal resistivity. The sheet resistance per layer R□/layer = ρ/d, where
d ∼16 ˚A is the interlayer distance11.
Several diﬀerent cryostats were used for the resistivity measurements, including a 3He

with superconducting magnets up to 9 T and 18 T, respectively.

ﬁlter [35 dB (70 dB) noise suppression at 10 MHz (> 100 MHz)] in series did not aﬀect
the results down to the lowest test T = 0.070 K, indicating that the eﬀects of heating by
the external EM noise are negligible at least down to that temperature. At the same time,
they cannot be completely ruled out at even lower T and, indeed, it is likely because of

(0.05 K ≤T ≤1 K) with a 9 T superconducting magnet and low-pass RC ﬁlters with

In all cryostats, the ﬁelds were swept at constant temperatures
using 0.1 −0.3 T/min sweep rates that were low enough to avoid eddy current heating
of the samples. Zero-ﬁeld ρ(T) curves shown in Fig. 1c and Supplementary Figs. 1b-d

temperature to equilibrate; the wait times were ∼4 −20 minutes depending on the
temperature and the cryostat.
The ac susceptibility was measured in a 3He system (0.3 K ≤T ≤60 K) using the conven-

ﬁeld (1.6 Oe, 200 Hz) was generated by an ac current source (Stanford Research CS 580),

(≤0.5 K/min from ∼5 K to ∼0.3 K) and warming up. The ac susceptometer was
calibrated by measuring the superconducting transition of indium that was cut to have
twice the volume and similar shape as the κ-STFx samples.
