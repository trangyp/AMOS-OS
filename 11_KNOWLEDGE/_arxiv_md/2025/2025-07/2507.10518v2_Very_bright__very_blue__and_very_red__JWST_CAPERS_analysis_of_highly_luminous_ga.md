ABSTRACT
We present JWST/NIRSpec PRISM observations of three luminous (MUV < −20) galaxies at z ∼10
observed with the CAPERS Cycle 3 program. These galaxies exhibit extreme UV slopes compared to
typical galaxies at z = 10. Of the three sources, two of them are a close pair (0.22′′) of blue galaxies
at z = 9.800 ± 0.003 and z = 9.808 ± 0.002 with UV slopes of β = −2.87 ± 0.15 and β = −2.46 ± 0.10
respectively, selected from PRIMER COSMOS NIRCam imaging. We perform spectrophotometric
modeling of the galaxies which suggests extremely young stellar ages and a lack of dust attenuation.
For the bluest galaxy, its UV slope also suggests significant Lyman continuum escape. In contrast, the

1. INTRODUCTION
Understanding the formation and evolution of the first
populations of galaxies in the universe is crucial for
building a complete understanding of galaxy evolution.
Prior to the launch of JWST, galaxies had been detected
out to z ∼10 from both space and ground-based tele-
scopes (e.g., Ellis et al. 2013; McLure et al. 2013; Oesch
et al. 2014, 2018; Bowler et al. 2014, 2015, 2020; Finkel-
stein et al. 2015; McLeod et al. 2015, 2016; Bouwens
et al. 2021, 2022). However, the launch of JWST has
allowed the robust detection of galaxies at z > 10 for
the first time due to the highly sensitive and multi-filter
imaging at λ = 1 −5 µm. This has produced large sam-
ples of galaxy candidates at z > 10 (e.g., Castellano
et al. 2022; Naidu et al. 2022; Donnan et al. 2023b,a;
Adams et al. 2023; Finkelstein et al. 2023; Harikane et al.
2023; Franco et al. 2024).
These samples of galaxies
have been used to measure the evolution of the galaxy
luminosity function (LF) showing a slow, gradual evolu-
tion out to z ≃14 (e.g., Finkelstein et al. 2024; P´erez-
Gonz´alez et al. 2023, 2025; McLeod et al. 2024; Adams
et al. 2024; Donnan et al. 2024; Whitler et al. 2025).
This was in agreement with some pre-JWST observa-
tions (McLeod et al. 2016) but was a slower evolution
than many pre-JWST theoretical models (e.g., Mason
et al. 2015; Tacchella et al. 2018; Yung et al. 2019), par-
ticularly at the bright end (e.g., MUV < −20).
There have been a number of suggested alterations to
the physical properties of galaxies at z ≥10 to explain
this evolution in the UV LF. For example, increases
in the star-formation efficiency (Harikane et al. 2023;
Dekel et al. 2023; Somerville et al. 2025), a lack of dust-
attenuation (Ferrara et al. 2023, 2025b), active galactic
nuclei (AGN) (Pacucci et al. 2022; Hegde et al. 2024),

and/or a more top-heavy initial mass function (Hutter
et al. 2025) have all been suggested.
However, the UV LF at z > 10 (and the galaxy stel-
lar mass function) can be successfully modeled without
any of these changes.
A simple model where stellar
ages decrease with increasing redshift is sufficient (Don-
nan et al. 2025) to explain the abundance of galaxies
through decreasing the mass-to-light ratio at z ≥10.
Similarly, bursty star formation can also lower the mass-
to-light ratio required to reproduce the UV LF (Gelli
et al. 2024; Yung et al. 2024a; Kokorev et al. 2025). Al-
though modeling the UV LF can be achieved with a sim-
ple model, breaking degeneracies between these different
potential explanations and understanding the diversity
of the galaxy population at z ≥10 represents a more sig-
nificant challenge. Detailed spectroscopic observations
of galaxies at z ≥10 are vital to fully understand this
population.
Early JWST NIRSpec observations have successfully
confirmed the redshifts of a number of galaxies at z ≥10
(e.g., Curtis-Lake et al. 2023; Bunker et al. 2023a; Arra-
bal Haro et al. 2023a; Carniani et al. 2024; Castellano
et al. 2024; Napolitano et al. 2024a, 2025; Kokorev et al.
2025; Naidu et al. 2025) with an ever growing number of
NIRSpec observations of highly luminous (MUV < −20)
galaxies at z > 9. These observations have revealed a
diverse range of chemical properties with some galaxies
at z > 9 showing elevated nitrogen and sub-solar car-
bon abundances (e.g., Bunker et al. 2023a; Castellano
et al. 2024) whereas others have weak or non-detected
UV emission lines (e.g., Curtis-Lake et al. 2023; Carni-
ani et al. 2024). There is also evidence of AGN activity
in some of these sources (e.g. Napolitano et al. 2024a;
Taylor et al. 2025).
An important property of high-z galaxies is their rest-
frame UV-continuum slope, β (where fλ ∝λβ). The
slope of the UV stellar continuum is primarily sensi-

tive to the age, metallicity, and dust attenuation of a
galaxy (e.g., Calzetti et al. 1994; Schaerer 2002; Cal-
abr`o et al. 2021) with bluer/steeper slopes observed in
galaxies that are younger, lower metallicity, and have
less dust attenuation.
However, at z > 9, the domi-
nant property that affects the stellar UV slope is dust
attenuation (Tacchella et al. 2022).
The UV slope is
also sensitive to the contribution of nebular continuum
(Cameron et al. 2024; Katz et al. 2024) and therefore
the fraction of Lyman continuum radiation that escapes
a galaxy, with bluer slopes for increasing fesc (Chisholm
et al. 2022; Mascia et al. 2025; Papovich et al. 2025).
The UV slopes of galaxies have been measured with HST
at z = 6−9 showing an average slope of β ≃−2 (Dunlop
et al. 2012; Finkelstein et al. 2012; Bouwens et al. 2014)
aligning with theoretical predictions (Dayal & Ferrara
2012). The UV slope of galaxies has also been observed
to evolve with both redshift and UV magnitude, with β
becoming increasingly blue with increasing redshift and
decreasing UV luminosity (Rogers et al. 2014; Austin
et al. 2024).
JWST has allowed the UV-continuum slope to be
measured at z ≥10 for the first time, showing an in-
crease in steepness to β ≃−2.6 at z ≃12, indicat-
ing a transition in the galaxy population to one which
lacks significant dust attenuation (e.g., Morales et al.
2024; Topping et al. 2023; Cullen et al. 2024; Dottorini
et al. 2024). Alternatively, it has been suggested that
supernova-produced dust grains cause less UV attenu-
ation (McKinney et al. 2025) at fixed AV, due to po-
tentially grayer attenuation laws.
However, the mea-
surement of β for individual galaxies has been difficult
with NIRCam due to the limited number of data points
from broad photometric filters, with these results mainly
describing the average galaxy population at a given red-
shift and UV luminosity. Therefore, it remains unclear
how much scatter there truly is in the UV-continuum
slope at z > 9, with deep NIRSpec observations re-
quired to measure β more precisely for individual galax-
ies. There have been a number of extremely blue UV
slope (e.g., β < −2.4) candidates identified from NIR-
Cam imaging (e.g., Topping et al. 2022). However, it
remains unclear if there are any particularly red galax-
ies (e.g., β > −1.6) at z > 9 which could imply signif-
icant dust attenuation. Spectroscopy is crucial to con-
firm their UV slopes and understand what drives such
extreme (either red or blue) UV slopes only ∼500 Myr
after the Big Bang.
In this paper we present three galaxies observed with
NIRSpec in the CANDELS-Area Prism Epoch of Reion-
ization Survey (CAPERS; GO-6368, PI M. Dickinson)
selected for their extreme UV slopes at z = 10. Two of
these sources have extremely blue UV slopes (β < −2.4)
and the other is extremely red (β > −1.6). We ana-
lyze their spectra to understand the UV continuum and
physical properties of these extreme sources at z ≃10.
This paper is structured as follows. In Section 2 we
introduce the objects analyzed here, and describe the
photometric and spectroscopic data used in this work.
In Section 3 we describe the redshift fitting, spectral en-
ergy distribution (SED) fitting and emission line fitting.
In Section 4 and Section 5 we discuss the nature of the
galaxies analyzed in this work. Finally, in Section 6 we
summarize our conclusions. Throughout we use magni-
tudes in the AB system (Oke 1974; Oke & Gunn 1983), a
Kroupa (2001) initial mass function, and assume a stan-
dard cosmological model with H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1,
Ωm = 0.3 and ΩΛ = 0.7.
2. OBSERVATIONAL DATA
2.1. Selection of targets
CAPERS is a JWST Cycle 3 Treasury Program that
is carrying out NIRSpec multi-object spectroscopy with
the low-resolution (R ≃30 −300) PRISM disperser for
several thousand galaxies out to the highest accessible
redshifts.
CAPERS targets are selected from multi-
band JWST/NIRCam imaging in three of the legacy
CANDELS (Grogin et al. 2011; Koekemoer et al. 2011)
fields. The Cosmic Evolution Survey (COSMOS, Scov-
ille et al. 2007) and Ultra-Deep Survey (UDS, Lawrence
et al. 2007) fields were observed with NIRCam in the
Public Release IMaging for Extragalactic Research sur-
vey (PRIMER; Dunlop et al. in prep.), while the Ex-
tended Groth Strip (EGS) was observed in the Cosmic
Evolution Early Release Science (CEERS; Finkelstein
et al. 2025) program.
A primary goal of CAPERS is to obtain NIRSpec
PRISM spectra of galaxies at z ≳9.
The selection
of these candidates is based on multiple samples with
different selection criteria, in some cases selected from
different photometric catalogs.
A full description of
CAPERS target selection and observations will be pre-
sented in future publications. In this paper, we focus on
three galaxies (two of which form a very close pair) with
spectroscopic redshifts z ≈10 that lie at extremes of the
UV continuum slope distribution: very red, and very
blue. There are a number of z ≳10 galaxies success-
fully confirmed by CAPERS (e.g. Kokorev et al. 2025),
however the galaxies presented in this work have ex-
treme UV slopes compared to the full sample at this
redshift (and indeed more extreme than other published
CAPERS galaxies) and therefore form the focus of this
paper.

2.2. Photometry
All three CAPERS survey fields have JWST/NIRCam
imaging in the F090W, F115W, F150W, F200W,
F277W, F356W, F410M, and F444W filters, as well as
the HST/ACS F606W and F814W filters.
The pho-
tometry for EGS-25297 was measured using the default
catalog used for CAPERS selection. This photometric
technique is similar to that in Finkelstein et al. (2024),
and will be described in full in Finkelstein (in prep.). As
the default catalog does not correctly split the PRIMER-
COSMOS pair, for these sources we measure photometry
following a procedure described in detail in Donnan et al.
(2024); we provide a short description here. Sources are
detected using Source Extractor (Bertin & Arnouts
1996) in the F150W, F200W, and F277W images. The
photometry is then measured using 0.3-arcsec diameter
apertures on images for which the point spread func-
tions (PSFs) have been homogenized to match that of
the F444W filter (which has a PSF full width at half
maximum [FWHM] of 0.145-arcsec). It was then cor-
rected to total from the flux enclosed for the F444W PSF
curve-of-growth. For both systems, we use the NIRCam
photometry in combination with the NIRSpec spectrum
to assist in modelling the SED of the sources as well as
for correcting NIRSpec slit losses.
Although the PRIMER and CEERS surveys contain
some level of MIRI imaging that overlaps with the NIR-
Cam coverage, they do not have coverage for these
sources. The COSMOS pair is covered by F770W from
the COSMOS-Web program (Casey et al. 2023) and
F2100W from COSMOS-3D (Kakiichi et al. 2024), but
the galaxies are undetected in both filters.
2.3. Spectroscopy
CAPERS NIRSpec observations are split into 21
pointings (7 per survey field). Each pointing is observed
using three NIRSpec Multi-Shutter Assembly (MSA)
configurations.
The highest priority sources are ob-
served in all 3 configurations per pointing to maximize
the observing time. This is the case for EGS-25297 with
a total observing time of 17069 s (4.74 hr). However, al-
though COSMOS-300001 and COSMOS-52185 are both
classified as high priority targets, due to their close prox-
imity to each other and the aperture position for the
scheduled NIRSpec observations, the 4.74 hr observing
time was split between the 2 sources. COSMOS-300001
was observed in a single configuration with an observ-
ing time of 5689.67 s (1.58 hr) and COSMOS-52185 was
observed in 2 configurations with an observing time of
11379 s (3.16 hr).

of the JWST Calibration Pipeline (Bushouse et al.

3. DATA ANALYSIS
3.1. Spectroscopic redshifts
All three objects show very strong Lyα breaks near
λobs ≈1.3 µm.
For EGS-25297, the red end of the
spectrum shows very strong emission lines from [O II],
[Ne III], Hδ and blended Hγ+[O III] λ4363. These lines
are weaker for COSMOS-300001 and COSMOS-52185,
and thanks to their slightly lower redshifts Hβ is also
detected. Possible detections of UV rest-frame emission
lines are discussed below.
The measurement of spectroscopic redshifts in CA-
PERS is being undertaken by multiple redshift fitting
codes as well as visual inspection.
In this paper we
provide a redshift measurement using the SED fitting

code bagpipes (Carnall et al. 2018).
Due to the de-
tection of a number of emission lines, we are able to
obtain a more precise redshift than is possible with
only a Lyα break (e.g., Curtis-Lake et al. 2023; Arrabal
Haro et al. 2023b). For COSMOS-300001 we measure
zspec = 9.800 ± 0.003 and for COSMOS-52185 we mea-
sure zspec = 9.808 ± 0.002. For EGS-25297 we measure
zspec = 9.942 ± 0.002.
3.2. SED fitting
To measure a number of physical properties of the
galaxies, we further use the SED fitting code BAG-
PIPES to model the spectra and photometry of each
galaxy simultaneously (a full description of how this
works is available in Carnall et al. 2019).
The SED
modeling with bagpipes accounts for the variable spec-
tral resolution of the NIRSpec PRISM spectra.
We
use Bruzual & Charlot (2003) stellar population models
with the MILES stellar library (Falc´on-Barroso et al.
2011).
We assume a Calzetti et al. (2000) dust-
attenuation law with a uniform prior on the V-band
attenuation over the range AV = 0 −3.
We also as-
sume a log-uniform prior on the stellar mass where
log(M⋆/M⊙) = 5.0−13.0, a log-uniform prior on metal-
licity where Z/Z⊙= 0 −2.5 and a log-uniform prior
on the ionization parameter where −4 < log U < 0.
We assume a Kroupa (2001) initial mass function in
this fitting. The inferred star-formation history (SFH)
of a galaxy through SED fitting is often sensitive to
the assumed parametric form of the SFH. Here we test
a number of different parametric and non-parametric
forms for the SFH when fitting the SED. Firstly, we
adopt a constant SFH prior where we place a log-uniform
prior on the stellar age between 1 Myr and 10 Gyr. Sec-
ondly, we adopt a double power-law (DPL) prior which
parametrizes the SFH with rising and falling power-
laws. Here we impose a log-uniform prior on each slope
(d log(SFR)/d log t) between 0.01 and 1000. Finally, we
adopt a non-parametric prior on the SFH. We assume a
“continuity-bursty” prior as defined by Tacchella et al.
(2022) where we allow the SFH to change over 8 bins
in cosmic time. The first 4 bins are spaced logarithmi-
cally between z = 20 and 100 Myr with the final 4 bins

3.4. Emission lines
In Fig. 2 we highlight the positions of typically strong
rest-frame UV and optical galaxy emission lines as
shown by the gray dashed lines. We initially measure
the continuum of the spectra by adopting the best-fitting
bagpipes model where we mask any emission lines. To
initially assess which lines have been detected we per-
form a direct integration of the continuum-subtracted
spectra at the expected wavelengths of these lines in a
window of 4σR(λ) where σR(λ) is the expected stan-
dard deviation of a Gaussian emission line determined
by the variable spectral resolution of NIRSpec PRISM
which increases from R ≃30 to R ≃300 as a function
of wavelength. For emission lines with a > 3σ signif-
icance from this direct integration, we then determine
the best-fitting model to each emission line. To do this
we use a Levenberg-Marquardt method to fit a Gaus-
sian profile at the position of each emission line. We
estimate the uncertainty of the line fluxes by randomly
perturbing the observed fluxes assuming they follow a
Gaussian distribution with a standard deviation equal
to the flux uncertainty. We repeat this 1000 times to
create a distribution of line flux measurements and take
the standard deviation of this distribution as the un-
certainty on the line flux. The continuum measurement
from the bagpipes model is used in the EW0 measure-
ment.
Although the Hγ λ4341 and [O III] λ4363 emission
lines are partially blended, the spectral resolution of
the PRISM at its reddest wavelengths, in conjunction
with a precise redshift from other emission lines, is
sufficient to enable us to de-blend them in COSMOS-
52185 and EGS-25927. (No significant emission from Hγ
λ4341+[O III] λ4363 is detected for COSMOS-300001.)
We therefore perform a 2-component Gaussian fit to ex-
tract the emission line fluxes for each line separately
where each line shares the same width. In Table 3 we
report the measured line fluxes, rest-frame equivalent
widths (EWs) and their associated uncertainties for a
number of rest-frame UV and optical emission lines.

The transverse separation of the two galaxies is ≃
0.93 kpc and their redshift separation ∆z corresponds
to a velocity difference of ∆v = 220 ± 100 km s−1; both
measurements may be underestimates of the true three
dimensional quantities due to projection effects. The dy-
namical timescale corresponding to an interaction with
this relative velocity and physical separation would be
of the order of ≃4 Myr. If this system will undergo
a merger then its true kinematic state would likely be
more complex than the simple approximation obtained
here from the projected separation and the line-of-sight
velocity. Both galaxies still appear relatively distinct,
although at such a large redshift cosmological dimming
surely inhibits detection of low surface brightness fea-
tures such as tidal debris and bridges. It seems likely
that any merger may be in its early stages, possibly
with just the initial passage having taken place. Hence
this dynamical timescale of ≃4 Myr may be of the
same order of magnitude as the time elapsed since the
initial passage, and it is interesting that this compares
well to the inferred mass-weighted burst stellar ages of
3.53+1.22
−2.52 Myr and 3.36+0.71
−1.25 Myr in Table 2, as well those
ages being fairly co-eval, thereby supporting a scenario
where a merger or interaction may have played a role in
triggering the star formation in this galaxy pair.

4.2. The nature of blue UV slopes
The steepness of the UV slope is defined by both the
stellar and nebular continua.
AGN emission can also
affect the continuum slope, and is generally redder than
the measured β values for these COSMOS galaxies, but
we find no clear indication of AGN activity from the
spectra of these sources.
Younger stellar age, lower
metallicity and lower dust attenuation all steepen the
UV slope of the stellar continuum.
A galaxy with a
purely stellar spectrum with extremely young ages, ex-
tremely low metallicity and a lack of dust attenuation
can have a UV slope of β ≲−3 and detecting this has
been proposed as a method for detecting the presence
of Population III stars (Schaerer 2002). However, star-
forming galaxies typically contain ionized gas which pro-
duces both nebular emission lines as well as continuum
emission. Nebular continuum emission can redden the
UV slope of a galaxy, and if one assumes a maximum
contribution from nebular emission and a standard slope
and cutoff to the high-mass IMF, the bluest allowed UV
slope is β ≃−2.6 with different models suggesting the
minimum is in the range β ≃−2.4 to β ≃−2.6 (Stanway
et al. 2016; Topping et al. 2022; Cullen et al. 2024). In
Fig. 5 we show this minimum allowed UV slope by the
shaded blue region. Therefore, an observed UV slope
steeper than β ≃−2.6 likely indicates the galaxy has
very low or zero dust attenuation as well as a low con-
tribution from nebular continuum, which in turn may
imply a non-negligible Lyman continuum escape frac-
tion.
The observed UV slopes for COSMOS-52815 and
COSMOS-300001 are both significantly steeper/bluer
than is typical for bright galaxies (MUV ≲−20) at
z ≃10 as observed from large photometric samples
(e.g., Morales et al. 2024; Cullen et al. 2024) as well
as the current sample of other spectroscopically con-
firmed galaxies at z > 9 (e.g., Dottorini et al. 2024).
UV slopes this blue have been observed at z ≃10 but
for galaxies ∼1 −2 mag fainter (e.g., Yanagisawa et al.
2024), as well as at lower redshift (Jiang et al. 2020;
Bolamperti et al. 2023).
Both sources are consistent
with little-to-no dust attenuation as evidenced by their
steep UV slopes.
This is also consistent with the re-
sults of SED fitting with bagpipes where we obtain
AV = 0.07+0.05
−0.03 for COSMOS-52185 and AV = 0.02+0.03
−0.01
for COSMOS-300001 (assuming a fixed dust-attenuation
law).
This is consistent with the expectations of re-
cent theoretical models.
Ferrara et al. (2023, 2025b)
suggest that for bright galaxies at z > 10, there is
dust production but then it is ejected or destroyed in
galaxies where sSFR > 30 Gyr−1 causing an observed

4.3. Implications for cosmic reionization
COSMOS-300001 has one of the bluest UV slopes at
z > 9 observed with β = −2.87 ± 0.15 and as shown in
the right-hand panel of Fig. 5, it is the bluest UV slope
observed to date in a bright galaxy (MUV ≲−20) at
z > 9. As we discussed in Section 4.2, this suggests that
COSMOS-300001 cannot have significant dust attenua-
tion. It is also bluer than the limit of β ≃−2.6 for a
galaxy with a full contribution from stellar and nebular
continuum even with no dust attenuation. Therefore,
this suggests a weakened contribution from the nebular
continuum. This is also evidenced by the weaker nebular
emission lines observed in COSMOS-300001 compared
to COSMOS-52185 (e.g., Hβ), although their presence
in 300001 does signpost the presence of ionized gas
(EW0 = 104 ± 32˚A for Hβ is hardly negligible). The
blue UV slope could therefore indicate the presence of
strong outflows which create channels in the ISM to al-
low a significant fraction of ionizing radiation to escape
(Ferrara et al. 2025a).
Studies of low-redshift star forming galaxies have
demonstrated a strong correlation between Lyman con-
tinuum escape fraction and UV spectral slope (Chisholm
et al. 2022).
Applying this same correlation at high
redshift, the very steep UV slopes of the COSMOS
pair galaxies imply significant ionizing escape fractions:
fesc = 0.41 ± 0.20 for COSMOS-300001 and fesc =
0.12 ± 0.04 for COSMOS-52815. Weaker EW(Hβ) for
300001 compared to 52185 would also be qualitatively
consistent with a larger fesc. We also derive fesc by fit-
ting the photometry and spectrum (including emission
lines) using bagpipes where we allow fesc to be a free
parameter following the methodology of Papovich et al.
(2025). With this method, we find lower escape frac-
tions than the values predicted by the β −fesc relation-
ship (consistent with the overall findings of Papovich
et al. 2025), but a similar trend, with larger escape
fractions predicted (or at least allowed) for COSMOS-
300001 (fesc = 0.02+0.14
−0.02) compared to COSMOS-52815
(fesc = 0.01+0.04
−0.01). For COSMOS-300001, the 84% con-
fidence range on fesc extends to ∼3× the correspond-
ing range for COSMOS-52185, and the tail of the prob-
ability distribution extends as high as 30%, which is
very unusual in the sample analyzed by Papovich et al.
(2025). Although this highlights that the absolute value
of fesc is highly method-dependent, the steep UV slope
of COSMOS-300001 does suggest higher fesc than is typ-
ical for galaxies at z > 9.
Given the close presence of two UV luminous galaxies
together during the epoch of reionization, we explore the
possibility of the total ionizing output carving an ionized
bubble. This is of particular interest given the lack of
Lyα detection in either galaxy. Following the method
of Napolitano et al. (2024b), we are able to place strong
upper limits on the 3σ rest-frame equivalent width of
Lyα of < 15 ˚A and < 11 ˚A for COSMOS-300001 and
COSMOS-52185 respectively. Using the measured MUV
and β to infer the rate of ionizing photon escape using
the Chisholm et al. 2022 method, and the stellar age t⋆,
we determine Rion = 0.2±0.1 Mpc following the method
of Napolitano et al. (2024b). This is only ∼10% the
expected size of an ionized bubble that would allow Lyα
to escape (and would be even smaller using fesc derived
from SED fitting), consistent with the lack of a detection
of Lyα in this galaxy (e.g. Lu et al. 2024).
Alternatively, we can use the ratio of the luminosity
of Hβ to the UV luminosity to estimate the ionizing
photon production efficiency, ξion.
Using the relation
from Emami et al. (2020) assuming no dust reddening
and fesc = 0 (chosen given the uncertainty on the exact
value described above) we derive ξion = 25.0 ± 0.1 and
ξion = 25.5 ± 0.1 for COSMOS-300001 and COSMOS-
52185 respectively. These values are consistent with typ-
ical ionizing photon production efficiencies at z ≥6 (e.g.
Simmonds et al. 2024; Begley et al. 2024; Llerena et al.
2025).

5.2. Emission line properties
Given the significant (> 3σ) detection of the electron-
temperature sensitive [O III] λ4363 emission line in
EGS-25297, we can estimate the oxygen abundance via
the “direct-Te” method. As we do not resolve the den-
sity sensitive [O II] λ3727 doublet and do not cover the
[S II] λλ6717, 31 doublet, we employ a forward mod-
eling approach using PyNeb (e.g., Cullen et al. 2025;
Scholte et al. 2025). We construct a model of five free
parameters: electron temperature (Te [O III]), electron
density (ne), the abundance of singly ionized oxygen
(log(O+/H+)), the abundance of doubly ionized oxy-
gen (log(O2+/H+)), and the V -band dust attenuation
(AV ). We then explore the parameter space using the
dynesty nested sampling code (Speagle 2020; Koposov
et al. 2023). For each parameter set we calculate the
emissivities of the observed lines, Hδ, Hγ, Hβ, [O III]
λ4363, [O III] λ4959, and [O III] λ5007, and apply red-
dening using the model AV (assuming a Cardelli et al.
1989 attenuation prescription). We then compare ratios
of these emission lines to the observed (i.e., not dust
corrected) emission line ratios to determine the best fit-
ting model.
Using forward modeling is advantageous
compared the traditional multi-step approach as we are
able to marginalize over electron density, and we can fit
the observed line flux ratios by including the V -band
attenuation as a free parameter. This approach has also
been shown to yield consistent results with the stan-
dard methodology (e.g., Arellano-C´ordova et al. 2025;
Scholte et al. 2025; Stanton et al. 2025). Applying the
more traditional methodology for EGS-25297 does not
change our resulting metallicity.
Our best fitting model yields an electron tempera-
ture of Te [O III] = 17200 ± 1900 K and a total oxygen
abundance of 12 + log(O/H) = 7.70 ± 0.13, which cor-
responds to Z ≃0.1 Z⊙assuming a solar metallicity
of 12 + log(O/H) = 8.69 (Asplund et al. 2021) This is
fully consistent with the recent analysis by Pollock et al.
(2025), who estimated the direct-method oxygen abun-
dances of a sample of z ∼10 galaxies, finding an elec-
tron temperature and oxygen abundance for EGS-25297
of Te = 17000±1200 K and 12+log(O/H) = 7.69±0.08
respectively. Interestingly, our result is also fully con-

5.3. Does EGS-25297 host an AGN?
JWST has revealed a significant population of broad-
line AGN that have been named “Little Red Dots”
(LRD; Matthee et al. 2024). Since this initial discovery,
there have been a number of studies that have selected
LRDs based on photometric colors (Kocevski et al. 2025;
P´erez-Gonz´alez et al. 2024) due to their blue rest-frame
UV emission and red rest-frame optical emission. Re-
cently, it has been demonstrated that a number of LRDs
with confirmed broad-line detections, exhibit similarly
red UV slopes to that of EGS-25297 (Greene et al. 2024).
We therefore discuss the possibility of an AGN in EGS-
25297 and review the available evidence.
Although this galaxy had been primarily selected as
a z > 9 galaxy candidate (e.g., McLeod et al. 2024;
Finkelstein et al. 2024) it had also been selected as an
LRD candidate by Kocevski et al. (2025), based on rest-
frame optical and UV slopes, requiring βUV < −0.37 and
βopt > 0. EGS-25297 met this criteria due to the previ-
ous photometry-based UV and optical slopes (and pho-
tometric redshift zphot = 10.21) of βUV = −1.47 ± 0.15
and βopt = −0.02 ± 0.24. However, now that we have
a NIRSpec spectrum of this galaxy, it is apparent that
the red rest-frame optical slope is partly driven by the
presence of strong emission lines which dominate the
flux observed in the F410M and F444W filters. Using
the NIRSpec spectrum (and spectroscopic redshift) and
masking all detected emission lines, we now measure
a rest-frame optical slope of βopt = −0.65 ± 0.35. As
this removes the impact of emission lines and therefore
purely traces the underlying continuum, we find a sig-
nificantly bluer rest-frame optical slope such that this
object would fail LRD selection based on its continuum
SED alone.
The low spectral resolution of the NIRSpec PRISM
makes it difficult to determine if there are broad com-
ponents to the Balmer emission lines. No strong broad
emission with FWHM > 1500 km s−1 is seen for Hγ
or Hβ, but careful attention is required to decompose
narrow and broad components. New R ≃1000 NIR-
Spec G395M observations (GO 5718, PI: D. Kocevski)
should be able to definitively determine the presence or
absence of broad line emission in this galaxy.
The detection of UV emission lines can also be used
to determine the presence of AGN activity as they are
sensitive to the hardness of ionizing radiation. Because
of this a number of rest-frame UV line ratio diagnostics
have been developed (e.g., Feltre et al. 2016; Nakajima
et al. 2018; Hirschmann et al. 2019). However, due to the
lack of detection of these key emission lines, this method
of determining the presence of an AGN is unavailable.
However given that the 2σ upper limits on the rest-frame
UV emission lines are ≲15 ˚A, this suggests that extreme
emission from these UV lines which would be expected
in the case of an AGN is absent in EGS-25297.
Given the lack of detected UV emission lines, we can in
principle use the rest-frame optical AGN diagnostic dia-
gram from Mazzolari et al. (2024) to test if the measured
line-ratios of [O III] λ4363/Hγ to [Ne III] λ3868/[O II]
λ3727 can confirm/rule-out the presence of an AGN.
However, this diagnostic can only confirm the presence
of an AGN particularly in the case of a strong [O III]
λ4363/Hγ ratio. For EGS-25297, we find that it is con-
sistent with AGN or star-formation by this diagnostic
and therefore we cannot use this to exclude the possi-
bility of an AGN within EGS-25297.

increased stellar mass is confirmed, it would be one of
the most massive galaxies at z > 9, exceeding that of
Gz9p3 (log(M⋆/M⊙) = 9.2; Boyett et al. 2024) and
GHz2 (log(M⋆/M⊙) = 9.1; Castellano et al. 2024).
Assuming the Hβ derived value for SFR10 and the
DPL SFH stellar mass of log(M⋆/M⊙) = 8.95, we obtain
sSFR ≃60 Gyr−1 for EGS-25297 which would suggest
there should be dust ejection according to the Ferrara
et al. (2025b) model. This is clearly inconsistent with
the observed dust-attenuation in this galaxy potentially
suggesting there are galaxies at z > 9 that can achieve
high sSFR without ejecting their dust.
Alternatively,
this inconsistency can be resolved if we assume the
greater stellar mass obtained from the non-parametric
SFH (log(M⋆/M⊙) = 9.6) yielding sSFR ≃12 Gyr−1
which is significantly below the threshold for dust ejec-
tion in the Ferrara et al. (2025b) model (sSFR =
30 Gyr−1).
Given the presence of dust in this galaxy (AV ≃0.92),
this may suggest a requirement for a previous episode of
star-formation in order to assemble sufficient dust by the
time of observation. Typically in galaxies, the majority
of dust assembles through grain growth (Draine 2009)
and this can happen on short timescales (< 1 Myr).
However, this typically only occurs in galaxies where
the gas-phase metallicity is > 0.2 Z⊙(Galliano et al.
2021) and is therefore unlikely to occur in EGS-25297.
Type II supernovae can produce dust in low-metallicity
galaxies and are often assumed to dominate the dust
production in high-z galaxies (e.g., Todini & Ferrara
2001; Le´sniewska & Micha lowski 2019) and can produce
dust on very short timescales. Therefore, it is perhaps
not necessary to require significant older star-formation
to produce an AV ≃0.9 by the time of observation.
Finally, another explanation for the red UV slope
could be the presence of a significantly older stellar pop-
ulation reddening the UV slope instead of dust with
a recent upturn in star-formation driving the observed
emission lines. Sources with such properties have been
observed at z ∼4 −7 (e.g., Strait et al. 2023; Looser
et al. 2024). However, this would require significant star-
formation at z > 20 and the strong equivalent widths of
the observed emission lines require a dominant, young,
actively star-forming stellar population which would still
dominate the UV continuum (and therefore require dust
to create the observed red UV slope) making this sce-
nario also unlikely.
Overall, although the data cannot exclude the possi-
bility of significant past star-formation (either producing
significant dust and/or older stellar populations), the
data is entirely consistent with simply a young, dust-
reddened star-forming galaxy.

JWST/NIRSpec has the ability to not only confirm
the redshift of galaxies with extreme UV slopes at
z > 9, but also determine the physical drivers of these
UV slopes. The complete sample of galaxies at z > 9
observed with CAPERS will provide valuable statistics
on the diversity of UV slopes, and follow-up higher reso-
lution NIRSpec observations as well as those with other
instruments/facilities, can aid our understanding of the
diverse galaxy population at z > 9.
