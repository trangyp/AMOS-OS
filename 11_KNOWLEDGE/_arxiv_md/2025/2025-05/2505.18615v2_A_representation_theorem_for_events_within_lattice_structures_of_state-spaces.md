A representation theorem for events within
lattice structures of state-spaces
Alex A.T. Rathke∗
July 31, 2025
Abstract
For the standard lattice model of information structures, we derive
a reduced poset representation which provides the same informational
content as the complete lattice structure which derives it. Rational agents
can recover the complete lattice of events by means of the reduced poset
alone. We find that both structures provide isomorphic models under
mild conditions.
Keywords: Information structure of agents, lattice model, knowledge, un-
awareness.
JEL Classification: C70, C72, D80, D83.

Introduction
We introduce the lattice model of information structures of agents proposed by
[4, 5, 7, 8, 14]. Let a lattice (S, ⪰) of disjoint state-spaces S ∈S be partially
ordered by the level of "expressiveness" of each space, where S′ ⪰S indicates
that S′ is at least as expressive as S. The order S′ ⪰S means that states ω ∈S′
may describe states in S in a richer or more expressive way [4, 5, 7, 8, 14]. The
set Ω:= S
S(S) defines the union of all states across all state-spaces S ∈S.
For ease of exposition, we assume a full sigma-algebra 2S for every state-space
S ∈S, unless explicitly referred otherwise.

Related Literature and Discussion
The lattice model of information structures is first proposed by [7], for it be-
comes the baseline structure underlying current set-theoretic models of knowl-
edge of agents, e.g. [4, 5, 14, 8]. For each event defined as a pair of upper sets
and base-spaces, see Eq. 1, the knowledge of each agent derives from a gener-
alised operator defined over the collection of all events. It formalises knowledge
as an event itself, therefore providing a model of introspective reasoning and
mutual information [4, 5, 7, 8, 14].
The lattice of events based on the order of expressiveness ⩽derives some
well-defined properties applicable to the modelling of rational knowledge, e.g.
monotonicity, positive introspection, and the property of truth [5, 8, 14]. The
lattice of events also proposes a solution to major inconsistencies found in the
standard state-space model, notably regarding the representation of unaware-
ness of agents [10, 11, 3].
One relevant application of the lattice model refers to the analysis of the
special conditions under which speculative trade occurs. Classical "no-trade"
theorems in economics and finance demonstrate that trade is not feasible when
