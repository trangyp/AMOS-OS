A Connection Between Learning to Reject and
Bhattacharyya Divergences
Alexander Soen1,2[0000−0002−2440−4814]
1 Australian National University, Canberra, Australia

Abstract. Learning to reject provide a learning paradigm which allows
for our models to abstain from making predictions. One way to learn the
rejector is to learn an ideal marginal distribution (w.r.t. the input do-
main) — which characterizes a hypothetical best marginal distribution
— and compares it to the true marginal distribution via a density ratio.
In this paper, we consider learning a joint ideal distribution over both
inputs and labels; and develop a link between rejection and thresholding
different statistical divergences. We further find that when one considers
a variant of the log-loss, the rejector obtained by considering the joint
ideal distribution corresponds to the thresholding of the skewed Bhat-
tacharyya divergence between class-probabilities. This is in contrast to
the marginal case — that is equivalent to a typical characterization of
optimal rejection, Chow’s Rule — which corresponds to a thresholding
of the Kullback-Leibler divergence. In general, we find that rejecting via
a Bhattacharyya divergence is less aggressive than Chow’s Rule.
Keywords: Learning to Reject · Bhattacharyya Divergence · Statistical
Divergence.
