Abstract
The coverage and composition of pretraining data significantly impacts the general-
ization ability of Large Language Models (LLMs). To reduce the carbon footprint
and financial costs of training, some data mixing methods, which applied the
optimized domain weights of a small proxy model to train a larger one, were
proposed. However, these methods did not evolute with the training dynamics.
The existing online data mixing (ODM) method addressed this limitation by ap-
plying the multi-armed bandit algorithm as data sampling strategy. Yet, it did not
consider the intra-domain interactions. In this paper, we develop an actor-critic
based online data mixing (AC-ODM) method, which captures the varying domain
weights by auxiliary actor-critic networks and consider the intra-domain interac-
tions with the reward function. While constructing the dataset to pretrain a large
target LLM, we directly apply the actor, which is trained with a small proxy LLM
as the environment, as the sampling strategy. The transfer of sampling strategy
can not only ensure the efficiency of dynamical data mixing, but also expedite the
convergence of pretraining the target LLM. Numerical results demonstrate that
AC-ODM-410M, which invokes the sampling strategy obtained by a proxy LLM
with 410M parameters, reaching the optimal validation perplexity of ODM 71%
faster, and improves performance on the zero-shot MMLU benchmark by 27.5%
of accuracy, about 2.23 better on pass@1 of HumanEval benchmark.

Introduction
The dataset, which contains data from various domains, is crucial for pretraining large language
models (LLMs), due to its significant impact on effectiveness John and Draper [1975], Du et al. [2022].
In particular, when the pretraining corpus is fixed, the quantity and composition of the selected data
primarily determine the amount of information absorbed, as well as the model’s convergence speed
Lee et al. [2022], Sorscher et al. [2023], Xie et al. [2023b], Albalak et al. [2024]. However, the
domain weights of almost all current state-of-the-art LLMs are determined by heuristics Gao et al.
[2020], raising the question of whether a more performant set of weights could be found.
Data mixing methods try to optimize the weights of the data domains to improve training efficiency
and model performance Xie et al. [2023a], Fan et al. [2024], Albalak et al. [2023], Xia et al. [2024].
There are two main categories: methods that determine the weights apart from model training (offline)
and those that determine the weights during model training (online) Albalak et al. [2024]. The
representative offline method DOREMI Xie et al. [2023a] started the two-stage training mode. Firstly,

the worst-case loss gap is slightly different from obtaining a well-trained model. Following this
mode, the domain reweighting with generalization estimation (DoGE) Fan et al. [2024] optimized
the gradient alignment between each domain and all other domains, instead of optimizing excess
loss in the first stage. However, Xie et al. [2023a] showed that in this two-stage mode, the domain
weights did not transfer well across models due to the inability to adapt to changing dynamics during
pretraining. It required training a new model to determine the best weights for each new architecture
or tokenizer, which reduced the effectiveness and efficiency of the two-stage offline framework.
Follow the principle that the best domain weights to train on are those that adapt to changing dynamics
of training the ultimate target model. Motivated by the goal of maximizing information gain during
model training, the online data mixing (ODM) Albalak et al. [2023] optimized the distribution of data
from various sources using an online mode. To minimize computation, it viewed each data domain as
an arm of a multi-armed bandit (MAB) based on EXP3 Auer et al. [2002] and formulated the reward
for each domain as its loss. The design of rewards and strategies would steer the MAB algorithm
to prioritize learning from data domains with larger losses. Hence, data domains whose semantic
space distributions differ more from that of current LLM’s predictions are prioritized for training.
On the other hand, this reward implicitly assumed that data qualities across domains were consistent
and ignored complex intra-domain interactions. From a data perspective, the data not only assist
in the knowledge construction of the corresponding domain, but also contribute to accelerate the
learning of other domains due to lexical, syntactic, or semantic overlap. By focusing on the domains
with stronger intra-domain interactions in the early stage of pretraining, we could accelerate the
pretraining process.
In summary, this paper proposes an online data mixing method based on the actor-critic (AC)
algorithm, which models the impacts of data domain weights on the LLM pretraining process with
a reinforcement learning (RL) model. Inspired by DoGE, we define the reward as the measure of
the gradient alignment W, which reflects both the contribution to the loss and the intra-domain
interactions. More precisely, by maximizing the expected reward, we prioritize learning datasets
with the strongest commonalities and tasks with the greatest generality, thereby accelerating the
convergence of pretraining. In terms of the solution, we adopt the deep deterministic policy gradient
(DDPG) method. By invoking the AC algorithm, this method is applicable when the state space
and action space are continuous, and allows for independent execution of the actor for data mixing
inference on datasets composed of the same data domains (e.g., The Pile, which consists of 22 data
domains). To pretraining a large target LLM, we can first optimize an online data mixing policy with

1. We improve ODM by considering the intra-domain interactions. Following the idea of

The primary overhead wall-clock time incurred from the computation of W. Although the storage

only during the training of the small proxy LLM, and the pressure due to the computation of W is
minimized.

Experimental Setup
The experimental setup considers dataset selection, model training protocols, baseline configurations
and evaluation criteria. For the AC networks, we include a detailed elaboration on state setting and
reward setting. All experiments were completed under a unified hardware environment, namely: an
Intel(R) Xeon(R) Platinum 8468 CPU and 8 NVIDIA H800 GPUs, each with 80 GB of memory.
LLM training
We utilize The Pile Gao et al. [2020], an open-source language modeling dataset
comprising 825GB of text from 22 diverse sources, including YouTube Subtitles, GitHub, Wikipedia,
and so on. To train our decoder-only Transformer model, we adopt a modified version of the GPT-
NeoX library Black et al. [2022]. Across all experiments, we configure our model following Pythia
Biderman et al. [2023] and train a 1-billion-parameter model. Each GPU processes a micro-batch of 8
sequences, and we employ gradient accumulation across 8 GPUs with the accumulation step being 18,
resulting in an effective batch size of 1152 samples. Specifically, for each batch, we initially conduct
random sampling of 10% of the samples from each domain to capture intra-domain relationships
and to uphold the exploration and exploitation policy of AC network. The sequence length is set
to 1024, and we apply the sequence packing Roberts et al. [2022]. The model is trained in 41,667
steps, corresponding to 50 billion tokens. During the first 833-step warmup phase, instead of using
domain weights provided by AC, we apply domain weights from The Pile with an added Gaussian
noise sampled from N(0, 0.02).
AC training
AC networks and LLM undergo the same number of warmup and main training steps,
with a cosine decay learning rate schedule that starts at 0.01 and decays to 0.001 during the course of
training. During the warmup phase, AC networks are trained using the replaybuffer B. To initialize
the actor, we use the domain weights of the LLM during warmup as labels (those of The Pile with
an added Gaussian noise) and optimize using MSE Loss. For the critic, we initialize the labels as
(1 + γ)rt and optimize with MSE Loss. In the main training phase, we sample 256 tuples from B
per iteration as training samples, and dispatch 32 tuples as a micro-batch to each GPU. We employ
gradient accumulation across 8 GPUs with an accumulation step of 1, resulting in an effective batch
size of 256 samples. The Pythia-1B and AC networks can be found in the Appendix A.
State Setting
We design the representation of states from six perspectives: 1. Number of samples
in each domain in the current step. 2. The current training step. 3. Losses of all domains in the
current training step. 4. Variations of the losses of the current domain from the last step. 5. The L2
norm of weights of the selected layers of the LLM. 6. The norm of the variation of weights of the
selected layers. The selected layers of Pythia-1B model are the Transformer layers with even indexes
along with the first one.
Reward Setting
To balance the efficiency and capability of pretraining the LLM, we carefully
select partial parameters of the LLM to participate in the calculation of rewards. More precisely, for
the Pythia-1B framework, we choose the last full connected layers of the transformers indexed with
12, 14 and 16 (50,331,648 parameters) as the model representation to calculate the rewards. In this
way, we dramatically drop the computational overhead while keeping the representation effective.
Baselines
The original domain weights suggested by The Pile Gao et al. [2020] refer to as The
Pile Weights (TPW) serve as a baseline. Furthermore, the domain weights proposed by ODM and
DoReMi computed with a 50k vocabulary tokenizer Albalak et al. [2023] are also considered.
Evaluation
We perform a comprehensive evaluation to compare the proposed method with baselines.
First, we measure the perplexity on both validation and test sets across all domains of the Pile dataset.
To assess the performance of downstream tasks, we evaluate on 57 diverse multiple-choice tasks from
the MMLU Hendrycks et al. [2021] benchmark, employing 0-shot and 5-shot in-context learning for
each task. Furthermore, we extend our evaluation to code completion using HumanEval Chen et al.
[2021] dataset, thereby verifying model performance in programming tasks.

Improvement of perplexity to baselines
The reward of AC-ODM selects data from domains
bringing forward gain to other domains by emphasizing intra-domain interactions. Therefore, exploit
the data carrying shared information across domains to accelerate the training process. Figure 3
shows the test perplexity of each method on held-out data as well as the average perplexity. AC-

that maximize cross-domain knowledge (i.e., data with the highest contribution to learning other
domains). This further demonstrates the effectiveness of the reward mechanism across different data
distributions. Consequently, AC-ODM achieves the lowest global perplexity throughout the training
process, validating its robustness in multi-domain pretraining scenarios.

Effect of proxy model size
Figure 4 compares the perplexities training of a 1B model with sampling
strategies produced by increasing sizes of proxy models (AC-ODM-70M, AC-ODM-160M, AC-ODM-
410M), along with training the 1B model and the AC networks simultaneously (AC-ODM). The losses
of training the proxy LLM are 2.8 for 70M, 2.65 for 160M, and 2.48 for 410M. The corresponding
validation perplexities are 20.3, 15.5 and 12.1, respectively. We note that both AC-ODM-160M
and AC-ODM-410M surpass AC-ODM, which states the efficacy of applying well-trained sampling
strategies to train the target model. The well-trained sampling strategy is able to select training data
according to the training dynamics, while adjusting the sampling strategy during training (AC-ODM)
cannot fully utilize the sampling strategy since the actor is not converged, especially at the early steps.
AC-ODM-70M performs the worst, which indicates that the model with less parameters may not
gain the generalization ability required, yet make it to serve as a pool environment. AC-ODM-160M
performs very similar to AC-ODM-410M, especially at the early steps. This may due to the limit size
of the target model. We believe the gap between 410M and 160M would enlarge if the size of the
target model increased. We will study if there would be extrapolation relationship between the sizes
of the proxy and target LLMs in the future.

Conclusion
In this work, we propose the actor-critic based online data mixing. By regarding the gradient
alignment score of each domain as its reward, we take into account the intra-domain interactions.

ODM and AC-ODM by 20.7%, 16.4% and 13.1% with 41667 steps. Moreover, it outperforms
all other methods across all 22 domains. Consequently, AC-ODM-410M demonstrates superior
performance in evaluations of downstream tasks. In 0-shot and 5-shot accuracy evaluations of
MMLU, it outperforms ODM by 27.5% and 23.9%, respectively. In the HumanEval benchmark,
it achieves a 2.23 relative improvement over ODM. It shows that well-trained AC networks can

A
A Model Configuration
A.1
LLM Model Configuration
We adopt the sequence length of 1024 and employ a 16-layer Transformers architecture with a hidden
size of 2048 and 16 attention heads. Rotary positional embedding Su et al. [2023] is incorporated. We
leverage FlashAttention Dao et al. [2022], which optimizes memory access and reduces computation
overhead, to improve training efficiency. The model is trained using Adam optimizer Kingma and Ba
[2017]. The learning rate undergoes a linear warm-up for 833 iterations, gradually increasing from a
minimum of 2.5e-5 to a peak of 2.5e-4, followed by a cosine decay back to 2.5e-5. We utilize the
GPT-NeoX-20B tokenizer Black et al. [2022] for text processing.
A.2
AC Networks Configuration
For both the actor and the critic, we employ a fully connected 6-layer neural network with 1024
neurons per hidden layer. Except for the output layer, each layer is followed by layer normalization
and a ReLU activation. In the actor, the output layer is further processed by the softmax activation
function, while in the critic, the output layer is post-processed by the identity activation function.
