Abstract
We theoretically investigate Λ production via a (K−, π−) reaction on a 6Li target, using the
distorted-wave impulse approximation (DWIA) with a Fermi-averaged K−n →π−Λ amplitude. We
calculate Λ production spectra using the Green’s function method for a 5Li nuclear core + Λ system,
employing a Λ folding-model potential based on the 5Li nuclear density, which is constructed
from α-p and 3He-d cluster wave functions. The results show that the calculated spectrum, which
includes Λ bound, resonance, and continuum states, agrees well with the experimental data from the
(K−, π−) reaction at pK−= 790 MeV/c (0◦), where substitutional (0p, 0p−1)Λn and (0s, 0s−1)Λn
configurations dominate in the near-recoilless reactions. A narrow peak corresponds to a high-
lying excited state with spin-parity JP = 1+ at EΛ = 13.8 MeV near the 3He + d + Λ threshold,
arising from interference effects between 5Li(3/2+)⊗(0s1/2)Λ and 5Li(1/2+)⊗(0s1/2)Λ components.
This study offers a valuable framework for extracting essential information on the structure and
production mechanisms of hypernuclear states from experimental data.
Keywords: Hypernuclei, Continuum states, Λ-nucleus potential

I.
INTRODUCTION
Recently, the J-PARC E10 Collaboration [1] demonstrated the absence of a bound state
for the neutron-rich 6
ΛH hypernucleus in a (π−, K+) reaction on a 6Li target [2]. Addition-
ally, the analysis of the continuum spectrum data [3] indicated that the Σ−-5He potential
is repulsive, with a strength of UΣ = +30 MeV. The J-PARC E75 Collaboration [4] has
conducted a search for the Ξ hypernucleus 7
ΞH via a (K−,K+) reaction on a 7Li target to
investigate the properties of the yet unsettled Ξ nuclear potential. A search for 6
ΞH using
the (K−,K+) reaction on a 6Li target has also been proposed at J-PARC.
One of the primary objectives of this study is to elucidate the structure and production
mechanisms of hypernuclei by theoretically analyzing experimental spectra from (K−, π−),
(π+, K+), and (K−, K+) reactions.
As an initial step toward achieving this goal, we
aim to establish an effective method for analyzing Λ production spectra, including both
bound and continuum states, for Λ hypernuclei whose overall characteristics are already
well understood.
The (K−, π−) reaction has played a crucial role in elucidating the structure of Λ hy-
pernuclear excited states by inducing a small momentum transfer of q ≃60–80 MeV/c to
a Λ hyperon [5–7]. This reaction has allowed the observation of prominent peaks corre-
sponding to “substitutional states” in Λ hypernuclei in experimental data on light nuclear
targets [7–11]. The Heidelberg-Saclay-Strasbourg Collaboration [10, 11] has reported that
the 6Li(K−,π−) reaction at pK−= 790 MeV/c and θlab = 0◦reveals two prominent peaks
at EΛ = 3.8 MeV and 13.8 MeV, measured relative to the 5Li(g.s) + Λ threshold, as shown
in Fig. 1. These peaks are identified as states with spin-parity Jp = 1+ in a 6
ΛLi hyper-
nucleus, corresponding to substitutional (0p, 0p−1)Λn and (0s, 0s−1)Λn configurations [11].
This is attributed to the dominance of an angular-momentum transfer of ∆L = 0 in the
near-recoilless (K−, π−) reactions on a 6Li(1+; g.s.) target. In Fig. 1, we also include data
from the proton-coincidence nonmesonic weak decay of 5
ΛHe, obtained from the π−spectrum
in a 5
ΛHe lifetime measurement using the 6Li(K−,π−) reaction at pK−= 800 MeV/c [12].
Notably, the small yield from 6
ΛLi(1+) at 13.8 MeV is comparable to that from 6
ΛLi(g.s.) at
−4.5 MeV. These data suggest a minimal (∼4%) admixture of (α-p)-Λ to (3He-d)-Λ in 6
ΛLi,
whose wave functions exhibit the spatial symmetries of [f] = [411] and [321] in the Young’s
scheme [13], respectively, as discussed by Majling et al. [14].

Many authors [15–17] have theoretically studied spectroscopy of p-shell Λ hypernuclei
in (K−, π−), (π+, K+), and (e, e′K+) reactions, using a distorted-wave impulse approxi-
mation. Shell-model [14, 18] and cluster-model [19–21] descriptions discussed the structure
and production of 6
ΛLi via the 6Li(K−, π−) reaction within a bound-state approximation.
Auerbach and Van Giai [22] studied basic gross features of continuum effects considering
ΛN −1 configurations in the (K−, π−) reaction on 6Li, together with 7Li and 9Be.
In this paper, we theoretically investigate Λ production via the (K−, π−) reaction on a 6Li

Figure 8 shows the contributions from neutron 0p−1 and 0s−1 states to the inclusive π−

For low-lying states with (α-p)-Λ configurations, the cross section of the JB = 2−(exc.)

In contrast, the cross section of the 1−(g.s.) state at EΛ = −4.51 MeV is negligible in the Λ

from the physical axis, as discussed in Sec. IV A. This suggests that the broad peak of the 1+

the crucial role of continuum effects in Λ production, as observed in the π−spectra [23],
indicating that bound-state approximations are insufficient for accurately describing contin-
uum spectra [19–21]. Therefore, a rigorous approach, such as the Green’s function method
[23], is crucial for a precise analysis of Λ production spectra in nuclear (K−, π−) reactions.

B.
Effects of the three-body Y NN force and Σ admixture
The high-lying excited states of 6
ΛLi appear to be promising candidates for investigating
the three-body Y NN force involving ΛNN-ΣNN coupling induced by the ΛN-ΣN inter-
action, rather than the Fujita-Miyazawa-type ΛNN force, which is expected to have only
a minor impact on the energies in free space. In the (3He-d)-Λ configuration, the 3He + Λ
subsystem is coupled with 3H + Σ+ and 3He + Σ0 in 4
ΛHe via the ΛNN-ΣNN interaction
caused by coherent Λ-Σ coupling [25, 58], and the d+Λ subsystem is coupled with {nn}Σ+,
{pn}Σ0, and {pp}Σ−in 3
ΛH [51]. Therefore, the three-body Y NN force is expected to play
a crucial role in determining the fine structure of the excited (3He-d)-Λ states, depending
on the spin-isospin states in 5Li(JC)-Λ/Σ. However, the present analysis cannot resolve the
energy shifts of the fine Λ states, as the experimental spectra were measured with a resolu-
tion of approximately 4 MeV FWHM [10]. To clarify the effects of the Y NN force in this
nucleus, high-resolution measurements at dedicated beamlines are required.
Moreover, Λ-Σ coupling induces a Σ admixture in the wave functions of Λ hypernuclear
states [59]. These Σ components can also be populated through direct processes such as
K−p →π−Σ+ and K−n →π−Σ0, which provide direct access to the Σ admixture in
the nucleus [60]. However, this effect is expected to be negligible in the nuclear (K−, π−)
reaction at pK−= 790 MeV/c, as the Fermi-averaged amplitudes f K−p→π−Σ+ and f K−n→π−Σ0
are much smaller than f K−n→π−Λ.
C.
Comparison with other calculations
Majling et al. [14] discussed that the peak at 13.8 MeV in the 6Li(K−, π−) data indicates
Λ production via (0s, 0s−1)Λn with a narrow width of Γ ≃0.7 ± 1.0 MeV, due to the
small admixture between wave functions with [f] = [411] and [321] in spatial symmetry.
According to Ref. [14], we approximately neglect the couplings between the (α-p)-Λ and
(3He-d)-Λ channels in the CC equation. This assumption is further supported by the small
imaginary part of the optical potential for t + d scattering [13, 34] and the reduction of
events near the peak at 13.8 MeV in the spectrum for proton coincidence via nonmesonic
weak decay of 5
ΛHe [12]. Therefore, we conclude that this approximation is valid in our
calculations.

Auerbach and Van Giai [22] attempted to calculate continuum spectra within a single-
particle Λ description using a ΛN −1 basis for 6Li, 7Li, and 9Be targets, revealing the funda-
mental features of continuum effects in nuclear (K−, π−) reactions. They indicated that the
peak at 3.5 MeV in 6
ΛLi does not correspond to a resonance state but rather to a continuum
state above the 5Li(g.s.) + Λ threshold. However, their calculated spectra fail to accurately
reproduce the experimental data across the entire energy range due to the omission of spin
structure in the 5Li(JC) ⊗Λ configuration. When evaluating the continuum states in the
spectra, it is essential to incorporate the spin structure of 6
ΛLi, as discussed in Sec. IV.
Motoba et al. [19, 20] investigated the structures and production cross sections in the
(K−, π−) reaction on a 6Li target using a microscopic α + p + Λ cluster model.
They
analyzed the spin structure in 6
ΛLi, demonstrating that the σN · σΛ term plays a crucial
role in determining the cross sections. Their findings indicate that the production cross
section for the 2−(exc.) state is larger than that for the 1−(g.s.) state because the spin
S> and S< components are significantly mixed only in the 1−(g.s.) state. The peak at 3.8
MeV has been identified as a broad 1+ resonance, which does not have a simple shell-model
substitutional structure such as (p3/2, p−1
3/2)Λn. Instead, it exhibits a strong admixture of
5Li(3/2−)⊗(0p3/2)Λ and 5Li(3/2−)⊗(0p1/2)Λ within the cluster-model framework. However,
our results suggest that no broad resonance peak exists for the 1+(exc.) state, despite the
significant role of the spin structure in describing the spectrum data.
Ohkura et al. [21] extensively studied the structure and production mechanisms of a high-
lying excited state of 6
ΛLi using a microscopic 3He + d + Λ cluster model. Their calculated
energies and widths of the 1+

fail to describe the continuum Λ states accurately above the 3He + d + Λ threshold.

hypernuclei, it is crucial to compute the Λ production spectra while explicitly incorporating
the spin structure of Λ bound, resonance, and continuum states in 6

D.
Future outlook
It is important to discuss potential improvements in the theoretical framework of our
calculations to achieve our goals. One key aspect of such improvements is the consideration
of self-consistency in describing the structure of light Λ hypernuclei, particularly for highly
excited states near the nuclear core breakup thresholds, at which the α cluster breaks into
3N + N.
This consideration is crucial because the shrinkage effects induced by the Λ
hyperon [47] can enhance the nuclear core density, requiring more accurate microscopic
descriptions of the core states.
The fine structure of high-lying excited states in 6
ΛLi is important for examining the effects
of the three-body Y NN force, which involves ΛNN-ΣNN coupling induced by the ΛN-ΣN
interaction in the Λ production mechanism. To achieve a comprehensive understanding of
such systems, it is also essential to adopt a unified treatment for describing the production
reactions of Λ hypernuclei. The reaction channel (3He-d)-Λ involves rearrangement channels
corresponding to (3He-Λ)-d. Thus, a consistent theoretical approach to these rearrangement-
channel reactions is essential. Moreover, a unified approach to the Λ production reaction in
both the (α-p)-Λ and (3He-d)-Λ channels is required to perform a fully coupled calculation
that accurately describes the reaction dynamics.
VI.
SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION
We have conducted a theoretical investigation of the Λ production spectra via the
(K−, π−) reaction on a 6Li target, using DWIA with a Fermi-averaged K−n →π−Λ ampli-
tude obtained through EOFA. We have calculated the Λ bound, resonance, and continuum
states in the Green’s function method for the 5Li-Λ system by solving the CC equations
with the Λ folding-model potentials based on α-p and 3He-d cluster wave functions. We have
analyzed the shape and magnitude of the spectra by comparing them with experimental
data from the 6Li(K−, π−) reaction. Results are summarized as follows:
(1) The calculated spectrum for the (K−, π−) reaction at pK−= 790 MeV/c (0◦)
agrees well with the experimental data, incorporating substitutional (0p, 0p−1)Λn
and (0s, 0s−1)Λn configurations. It is crucial to consider the spin structure of 6
ΛLi to
accurately evaluate the spectra in the Λ continuum regions.
24


## Page 25


(2) The two-pole structure for the 1+
1 and 1+
2 resonance states emerges near the 3He+d+Λ
threshold, where interference effects between 5Li(3/2+) ⊗(0s1/2)Λ and 5Li(1/2+) ⊗
(0s1/2)Λ modify the Λ production spectrum, leading to a narrower peak at 13.8 MeV
for JP = 1+.
(3) The magnitude of the differential cross sections with the Fermi-averaged K−n →
π−Λ amplitude obtained through EOFA is approximately half that obtained using
the SFA. Nevertheless, the absolute values of these cross sections agrees well with the
experimental data, effectively resolving the discrepancy between the theories and BNL
experimental results.
In conclusion, we have successfully explained the inclusive π−spectra for Λ production in
6
ΛLi via the 6Li(K−, π−) reaction within the DWIA framework using EOFA, along with the
Green’s function method and Λ folding-model potentials. Continuum effects, including nu-
clear spin structure considerations, enable accurate reproduction of the spectral data. This
study provides a valuable framework for extracting essential information on the structure
and production mechanisms of Λ hypernuclei from experimental data. Further microscopic
calculations of Λ-nucleus potentials, based on modern ΛN interactions, are essential for
gaining deeper insights into Y N and Y NN interactions that involve Y N short-range corre-
lations [61]. These aspects will be explored in future studies.
