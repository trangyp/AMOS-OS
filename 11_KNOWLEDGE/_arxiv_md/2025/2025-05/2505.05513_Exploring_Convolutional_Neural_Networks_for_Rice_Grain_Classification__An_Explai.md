Abstract— Rice is a global staple food that plays a key role 
in terms of fostering global trade, economic developments and 
food. China, India, Pakistan, Thailand, Vietnam and Indonesia 
are some of the Asian countries that have been engaged in the 
production of various varieties of rice including short and long 
grains. These varieties are even further categorized into 
basmati, jasmine, kainat saila, ipsala, arborio, and so on, which 
suits all the different culinary styles as well as cultural 
backgrounds. In case of local and international trade, there is 
need to check and maintain the quality of rice grains to satisfy 
the customers and to maintain the reputation of a country. 
Quality check and classification using the manual are very 
tedious and time consuming. It is also very susceptible to errors. 
In this research paper, the automatic framework is a 
convolutional neural network (CNN) framework to classify rice 
varieties, i.e. basmati, jasmine, ipsala, arborio, and karacadag. 
The CNN model was trained and validated, with an impressive 
accuracy rate and an ideal area under each class Receiver 
Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve. The confusion matrix 
analysis indicated that the model was effective in differentiating 
the various rice varieties with few misclassifications. Also, the 
use of explainability methods including LIME (Local 
Interpretable 
Model-agnostic 
Explanations) 
and 
SHAP 
(SHapley Additive exPlanations) helped to understand the 
decision-making process of a model and provided some new 
insights about how particular features of rice grains impacted 
the results of classification. The interpretability fosters trust in 
the model predictions and increases its applicability in the real-
world context. The results demonstrate the enormous potential 
of deep learning methods in agriculture, which opens the path 
to the development of automated classification systems. 
Keywords— Crop Classification, Rice Grain Classification, 
Explainable AI, XAI, CNN, Deep Learning 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Agriculture includes the production of crops and farming 
animals to be used by people and thus giving economies a gain 
all around the world. It also encompasses animal husbandry, 
planting and harvesting [1]. The symbiotic relationship 
between agricultural practices and crop production guarantees 
a stable food supply, farming revenues, and the wellbeing of 
the world population [2]. Accurate and precise analysis of 
crops is key to this system, which allows optimization of 
yields, improvement of sustainability, and decision-making 
about the health of crops and resource distribution. Crop 
analysis enables the agricultural industry to use various 
technologies to come up with detailed analysis of crop health, 
classification, crop yields and effective resource allocation 
[3]. Satellite images, unmanned aerial vehicles, and remote 
sensing are examples of tools that complement crop 
production by collecting vast amounts of information about 
the state of the agricultural sector [4].

This information can be analyzed using machine learning and 
artificial intelligence systems, and it can provide useful 
information on the detection of diseases, nutrient deficiencies, 
and the overall condition of crops [5]. The recent development 
of AI has encouraged agricultural scientists to explore the field 
of deep learning, with a focus on the application of AI to crop 
analysis. Many agricultural activities such as identifying 
problems, monitoring crop health [6], estimating yields [7] 
estimating prices [8] but also mapping yields, optimizing the 
use of pesticides [9], and fertilizers, and applying them, 
benefit greatly through the addition of AI [10]. A major 
phenomenon of crop analysis is crop classification, which 
involves classifying different types of crops. Proper 
categorization of crop type plays an essential role in 
appropriate agricultural management, as it helps to make 
predictions more effectively, make decisions based on data 
and practice a sustainable agriculture [11]. 
Rice, a major staple crop, is also grown in over 100 
countries and is produced in massively in China and Southeast 
Asia. This is a highly important grain, which supports a 
population of about 3.5 billion people across the world, and it 
is the third most produced crop after maize and sugarcane 
[12]. Manual rice grains classification is time-consuming and 
tedious and requires careful inspection of grain size, 
morphology, and color. This is not economical and efficient 
when dealing with large operations. A practical alternative is

automated classification systems, which combine computer 
vision (CV) and deep learning (DL), and in a manner akin to 
a large-scale analysis of large datasets, the systems can 
classify and identify rice varieties based on a predefined 
attribute. It leads to faster and more precise classification, cost 
and time savings, reduced human error, and better 
identification consistency of the rice type [13] . In this paper, 
an automatic system of categorizing different types of rice (as 
shown in Fig.  1) that is, Arborio, Basmati, Jasmine, Ipsala 
and Karacadag, will be proposed using a combination of 
parameters such as grain size, type of grain and other pertinent 
features.  
Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are among the 
most effective deep learning architectures, especially useful in 
the processing and analysis of visual impingements. CNNs 
produce hierarchical features representations of raw images 
and thus yield high accuracy in classifying images such as rice 
grains [14]. In the agricultural sector, CNNs are particularly 
effective in identifying salient features and attributes of 
images of rice grains (including grain size, shape, colour, 
eccentricity, major axis length, and minor axis length) which 
can be used to distinguish between varieties. Significant CNN 
models, such as VGG16 [15], InceptionNet [16], and 
MobileNetV3Small [17]  have been applied to various crop-
analysis tasks with better accuracy. However, the lack of 
transparency of these models as opaque black-box models 
may hinder their usage in rice-grain classification problems, 
where interpretability and accountability are essential in 
making decisions which have a direct impact on product 
quality and supply-chain logistics. 
In this paper, the proposed methodological framework 
combines CNNs with two different explainable AI (XAI) 
approaches: Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations 
(LIME) [18], [19] and Shapley Additive explanations (SHAP) 
[18], [19] to obtain accurate classification of rice-grain 
varieties. To increase transparency we combine Explainable 
Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques, namely LIME (Local 
Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations) and SHAP 
(SHapley Additive exPlanations). These methods provide 
human-understandable explanations of the predictions made 
by the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) by visualizing 
the impact of image features, e.g. grain lines and textures, on 
the model outputs. LIME focuses on super pixels effects of 
individual prediction, while SHAP offers a global view of 
feature relevance that is based on game-theoretic concepts. 
With the combination of the tools, we ensure that the model is 
accurate, interpretable, and reliable, which is critical to be 
used in the agricultural systems. The block diagram of our 
proposed system is (as shown in Fig.  2).  
The novelty of the current study is in the fact that the 
application of explainable AI methods (LIME and SHAP) has 
been incorporated in the integrated manner, to interpret and 
justify the predictions of the model at the granular level. In 
contrast to the previous studies which are interested only in 
the accuracy of the classification, the current study puts more 
emphasis on the transparency of the model and provides the 
visual descriptions of the learned features and the decision-
making procedure concerning every type of rice grain. This 
two-explainability approach does not only support the 
reliability of the model but also provides human-
understandable information on the significance of features, 
thus making the model more useful in terms of agricultural 
quality control and practical application. The main 
contributions of this paper will be as follows:

II. LITERATURE REVIEW 
Recent developments in the field of artificial intelligence 
have seen a broad application of deep learning to various 
industrial applications, including that forecast passenger 
behaviour [20] to predictive maintenance of machinery [21] to 
video analysis [22], [23], [24]. The technology has also been 
successful in various classification problems, such as in cancer 
cell identification [25] and patient categorisation [26]. Deep 
learning approaches are gaining importance in the agrarian 
domain [14]. Crop analysis using deep learning has received 
much investigation, especially focusing on crop classification 
[27] and crop disease prediction [28]. Although there are a 
number of proposed and proven models, classifying rice 
grains is still a major challenge to farmers and quality analysts. 
This research paper introduces an automated system that can 
be used to classify rice varieties efficiently and accurately. The 
next section is a literature review on deep learning methods 
used in rice grain classification. A comparative analysis was 
conducted by Koklu et al. [29] between artificial neural


## Page 3


networks (ANNs), deep neural networks (DNNs) when using 
feature datasets, and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) 
when using image datasets. Performance measures (F1-score, 
precision, accuracy, false positive rate (FPR), false negative 
rate (FNR), sensitivity, specificity) were used to assess it. The 
models were trained using image-based data consisting of 
75,000 images of five varieties of rice grain namely, arborio, 
jasmine, basmati, ipsala and karacadag. The research achieved 
100 mg to CNN, 99.95% to DNN, and 99.87% to ANN, which 
implies that the application will be applicable in the 
classification of various food grains. 
In [30], the author developed a study in which seven 
machine-learning classifiers, including logistic regression 
(LR), decision tree (DT), support vector machine (SVM), 
random forest (RF), multilayer perceptron (MLP), Naïve 
Bayes (NB), and k-nearest neighbors (KNN) are used to 
classify rice varieties. The experimental outcomes were 
encouraging, and the random-forest classification was 99.85 
% accurate and the decision-tree classification was 99.68% 
accurate.  Another author, in [31], suggested that a multiclass 
SVM framework be used to classify three types of rice grain, 
basmati, ponni, and brown grains. The proposed research was 
tested with 90 test images and attained a classification of 
92.22%. The study by Ramadhani et al. [32] focused on the 
optimization of SVM and KNN using genetic algorithms. 
They obtained 92.81% accuracy with SVM-GA and 88.31% 
with KNN-GA. The findings reveal that the combination of 
SVM and genetic algorithms is more effective than other 
algorithms, which may improve sustainable practices of 
cultivating rice and its productivity, in general [33]. Bejerano 
et al. [34] introduced a hybrid model that combines a deep 
convolutional neural network (DCNN) and support vector 
machine (SVM). The suggested framework was trained to be 
able to classify four types of rice, which are damaged, 
discolored, broken, and chalky. SVM was used to produce 
classification and DCNN produced morphological features. 
The grading of rice was tested and graded on a model with an 
accuracy of 98.33% and 98.75% on the training and validation 
data respectively.   
A framework of classification of five types of rice grains 
using ResNet-50 was suggested by Rayudu et al. [35] which 
included basmati, jasmine, arborio, Uppsala, and karacadag. 
The model has been trained and tested on a small dataset 
consisting of 2,500 images (500 each) used. When compared 
to VGG16 and MobileNet, the ResNet-50 demonstrated 
higher results indicating the strong potential of deep learning 
to enhance the efficiency of rice categorization and, therefore, 
quality control, supply-chain management, and consumer 
confidence.  
III. DESCRIPTION & METHODOLOGY OF PROPOSED 
SYSTEM 
The suggested methodology (as shown in Fig.  3) will 
help to improve the classification of rice grains with the help 
of a CNN that is backed up by explainable AI. Once data has 
been acquired, pre-processed, and splitted, the CNN is then 
trained in hierarchical features. LIME is used to get local 
interpretability whereas SHAP is used to get global insights. 
The last model is tested on a test set to provide correct 
unbiased predictions.
 
Fig.  3.  Proposed Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) Framework for Rice Grain Classification 
A. Image Acquisition and Preprocessing: 
A publicly available dataset on Kaggle is the Rice Image 
Dataset [29], which is suggested to be used in the development 
of the system. The proposed dataset will have 75,000 images, 
that is, each rice grain variety will have 15,000 images.  
1. Edge Detection and Image Segmentation: To help with 
classification and analysis, the edge detector was used to 
emphasize the outline of the grains (as shown in Fig.  4). In 
this investigation, Canny edge detector that detects sharp 
variations in intensity and is useful in rice grain boundaries 
was used. Photos were image-to-grayscale, denoised through 
the Gaussian blur algorithm, and the Canny algorithm was 
applied (as shown in Fig.  5). This refines the grain 
morphology and yields better visualization which is a crucial 
preprocessing stage towards the correct recognition of rice 
varieties. After edge detection, image segmentation is used to 
outline separate regions, which can help isolate and analyze 
individual rice grains precisely. The effect of this step of the 
procedure is the enhancement of the classification accuracy 
due to the clear division of grains and background as well as 
grains themselves.

approach. 
 
Fig.  5. Rice grains with detected edges, highlighting the contours and 
characteristics essential for classification and analysis. 
3. Data Normalization and Augmentation:  
 
The non-homogenous pixel values make the model 
convergence in training difficult. To overcome this, any

segmented images undergo a normalization process that maps 
the pixel values in a scalable manner to a limited set of 0-1. 
All the images were augmented by two unique data 
augmentation processes: horizontal flipping and rotation to 
increase the data volume and variety. Images were flipped 
and rotated 90 and 180 and flipped at the probability values 
of -1 to +1. 
 
 
Fig.  6. Segmented rice grain images highlighting individual grain

B. Proposed Model: 
 
In this paper, CNN (as shown in Fig.  7)  is developed 
to categorize 50×50 RGB rice grain images with the 
assistance of Keras Sequential API. It has an opening 
Conv2D filter of 32 (3×3) with ReLU to obtain simple 
spatial elements and is followed by MaxPooling2D to be 
used to down sample. A second Conv2D and another 
pooling layer detect patterns on high level. The feature is 
flattened and then subjected to a 32-unit dense layer of 
ReLU to abstract features. The last SoftMax layer makes 
a prediction of five classes. The model is trained on the 
Adamax optimizer categorical cross-entropy and assessed 
based on accuracy.

IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
The plots, (as shown in Fig.  8), give important 
information on the dynamics of the training of the CNN, both 
the loss and accuracy measures over the training epochs. The 
training loss curve presented in the left hand panel (in red) 
shows a gradual decrease as the training proceeds. This steady 
drop means that this model is successfully absorbing the 
trends of the training data and the loss value is near to a lower 
limit, which means better predictive ability on the training set. 
By contrast, the validation loss, indicated in green, starts to 
exhibit a similar trend to the training loss but it has a 
downward trend which indicates successful generalisation at 
the early stages of training. But with the continued training, 
there is a slight increase in the validation loss, which indicates 
the start of over-fitting where the model will start memorising 
the training data and lose its ability to predict on unseen data. 
The strongly indicated mark with the label best epoch (at 
epoch 10) indicates the most ideal time to switch the model 
before the drop and thus, the conclusion of training should be 
optimally at this epoch in order to retain the generalisation 
skills.

V. CONCLUSIONS 
 
The current study presents and tests a convolutional neural 
network (CNN) to classify one of the varieties of rice, such as 
Arborio, Basmati, Ipsala, Jasmine, and Karacadag. The model 
performed exceptionally well through tough training and 
validation achieving high accuracy and a perfect area under 
ROC curve per class. The confusion matrix revealed that the 
model had success in differentiating the rice varieties, and its 
misclassifications were very limited. The model was also 
found to learn and generalize as the dynamics of the training 
were analyzed using the loss and accuracy plots. It is 
important to note that the overfitting to the end of the training 
is observed, and it is necessary to monitor validation values to 
maintain model strength. Further, the additions of 
explainability tools like LIME and SHAP have provided 
important information on the decision making process of the 
model. These techniques advance our knowledge on how the 
model infers the rice grain properties by visualizing the 
contribution of individual characteristics to classification 
outcomes, thus developing confidence in predicting rice grain. 
The above results highlight the promise of deep-learning 
methods in the field of agriculture with a particular role in 
enhancing the classification of crops.
