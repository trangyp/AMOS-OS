ABSTRACT
In this paper, we carry out multiwavelength and multiview observations of the eruption of an intermediate
prominence originating from the farside of the Sun on 2023 March 12. The southeast footpoint of the promi-
nence is located in active region (AR) 13252. The eruption generates a B7.8 class ﬂare and a partial halo coronal
mass ejection (CME). The prominence takes oﬀat 02:00 UT and accelerates for nearly three hours. Rotation of
the southeast leg of the prominence in the counterclockwise direction is revealed by spectroscopic and imaging
observations. The apex of the prominence changes from a smooth loop to a cusp structure during the rising
motion and the northwest leg displays a drift motion after 04:30 UT, implying a writhing motion. Hence, the
prominence eruption is most likely triggered by ideal kink instability. For the ﬁrst time, we apply the Gradu-
ated Cylindrical Shell (GCS) modeling in three-dimensional reconstruction and tracking of the prominence for
nearly two hours. Both the source region (110◦E, 43◦N) and northwest footpoint (162◦E, 44◦N) are located. The
edge-on and face-on angular widths of the prominence are ∼6◦and ∼86◦, respectively. The axis has a tilt angle
of ∼70◦with the meridian. The heliocentric distance of the prominence leading edge increases from ∼1.26R⊙
to ∼2.27 R⊙. The true speed of the CME increases from ∼610 to ∼849 km s−1.
Keywords: Sun: prominences — Sun: ﬂares — Sun: coronal mass ejections (CMEs)
1. INTRODUCTION
Prominences are cool and dense plasmas hanging up in
the hot solar corona (Tandberg-Hanssen 1995; Mackay et al.
2010; Gibson 2018).
The materials are found to come
from direct injections from the chromosphere (Wang et al.
2019), levitation (Zhao et al. 2017), or coronal conden-
sation as a result of thermal instability (Yoshihisa et al.
2025; Zhou et al. 2025) along polarity inversion lines
(PILs) (Martin 1998; Chen et al. 2014).
After forma-
tion, they are supported by magnetic dips in sheared ar-
cades (Aulanier et al. 2002) or magnetic ﬂux ropes (MFRs;
Zhou et al. 2018). Prominences are rich in dynamics, includ-
ing counter streamings (Zirker et al. 1998; Chen et al. 2014),
magneto-thermal convection (Berger et al.
2011),
rota-
tions (Yan et al. 2014b), tornadoes (Wedemeyer-B¨ohm et al.
2012), small-amplitude oscillations (Okamoto et al. 2007;

The three-dimensional (3D) morphology and evolution
of a prominence or a CME are unclear from single-
view observations.
The separate vantage points of twin
spacecrafts of the Solar TErrestrial RElations Observa-
tory (STEREO; Kaiser et al. 2008) in combination with the
vantage point of Earth allow for precise 3D reconstruc-
tions and continuous tracking of prominences and CMEs.
In the past two decades, several methods and models
have been developed (see Mierla et al. 2010, and refer-
ences therein). The most popular one is tie-pointing (TP)
or triangulation method (Inhester 2006; Bemporad 2009;
Liewer et al. 2009; Joshi & Srivastava 2011; Li et al. 2011;
Panasenco et al. 2011; Thompson 2011; Thompson et al.
2012; Shen et al. 2012; Bi et al. 2013; Howard 2015;
Guo et al. 2019; Sahade et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2025). The
most common feature reconstructed by TP is the leading edge
of a CME and more compact structures (Mierla et al. 2008).
At least two viewpoints are required in this technique. Cor-
rect identiﬁcation of the same feature in these images is very
important. The limitation of this method might be that fea-
tures at the farside of the Sun observed from either viewpoint
could not be reconstructed, since it is impossible to ﬁnd a
correspondence between pixels in the images taken by these
spacecrafts.
To investigate the geometry and kinematics of non-radial
prominence eruptions and CMEs, Zhang (2021) proposed a
revised cone model, which is characterized by four parame-
ters: the length and angular width of the cone, the deﬂection
angles in longitude and latitude directions, respectively. The
tip of the cone is on the solar surface rather than at the Sun
center. Using multiwavelength and multiview observations,
the model is successfully applied to the 3D reconstructions
and tracking of bright fronts of CMEs (Zhang 2022; Dai et al.
2023) and the leading edges of prominences (Zhang et al.
2024b) as far as ∼10 R⊙. Deﬂection, acceleration, and ex-
pansion are perfectly uncovered based on the modeling. The
revised cone model is obviously inappropriate for reconstruc-
tions of huge prominences whose footpoints are remarkably
separated.
Considering that a large number of CMEs are MFRs
in nature (Lepping et al. 1990; Bothmer & Schwenn 1998;
Vourlidas et al. 2013; Palmerio et al. 2018), plenty of mod-
els featuring a MFR have been developed to perform
3D reconstructions of CMEs (e.g., Thernisien et al. 2006;
Wood & Howard 2009; Isavnin 2016; M¨ostl et al. 2018). The
Graduated Cylindrical Shell (GCS; Thernisien et al. 2006,
2009; Thernisien 2011) model is composed of two con-
ical legs and a middle circulus connecting the two legs,
resembling a croissant.
The model has been widely ap-
plied to 3D reconstructions of CMEs observed by white-light
(WL) coronagraphs, such as COR1 and COR2 on board the
STEREO spacecrafts, C2 and C3 of the Large Angle Spec-
troscopic Coronagraph (LASCO; Brueckner et al. 1995) on
board the Solar and Heliospheric Observatory (SOHO) mis-
sion, and Metis (Antonucci et al. 2020) on board the So-
lar Orbiter (SolO; M¨uller et al. 2020). The geometrical and
kinematical evolutions of CMEs are exhaustively investi-
gated (Cheng et al. 2013; Colaninno et al. 2013; Kwon et al.
2014; Andretta et al. 2021; Bemporad et al. 2022; Zhou et al.
2023; Shaik et al. 2024; Teng et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2024d;
Sahade et al. 2025). It should be noted that the GCS is just
a geometrical model without taking into account the mag-
netic ﬁeld, which is diﬀerent from the FRi3D model (Isavnin
2016) and 3DCORE model (M¨ostl et al. 2018).
To explore the evolutions of ﬂux ropes or prominences
originating from ARs, whose footpoints are close to each
other, Zhang et al. (2023) made a slight modiﬁcation to the
GCS model and applied it to the 3D reconstruction of an
eruptive prominence originating from AR 13110 on 2022
September 23. It is found that the prominence experiences
a southward deﬂection by ∼15◦. Meanwhile, the velocity
increases from ∼246 to ∼708 km s−1 during the impulsive
phase of the related M1.7 class ﬂare. The revised GCS model
has the same limitation as the revised cone model.
In this paper, we study a long, intermediate prominence,
with one footpoint locating in active region (AR) 13252 on
the visible side, while the other footpoint being on the far-
side as observed from Earth. Therefore, the two footpoints
are considerably separated. Both the revised cone model and
revised GCS model are unsuitable for modeling. For the ﬁrst
time, we apply the standard GCS model (Thernisien et al.
2006) to the 3D reconstruction and tracking of the promi-
nence assuming a radial propagation without deﬂections.
The paper is organized as follows. We describe multiwave-
length observations in Section 2. Data analysis and results of
reconstructions are presented in Section 3. Discussions and
a brief conclusion are given in Sections 4 and 5, respectively.

close to the eastern limb, generating a weak B7.8 class ﬂare

2012) on board the Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO;
Pesnell et al. 2012) spacecraft, the Solar Ultraviolet Imager

shape of the apex changes from a smooth loop to a cusp

such as magnetic ﬂux emergence (Chen & Shibata 2000) and
torus instability (Kliem & T¨or¨ok 2006), could not be fully

farside of the Sun are common.

5. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we carry out multiwavelength and multiview
observations of the eruption of an intermediate prominence
originating from the farside of the Sun on 2023 March 12.
The southeast footpoint of the prominence is rooted in AR
13252. The eruption generates a B7.8 class ﬂare and a partial
halo CME. The main results are as follows:
1. The prominence lifts oﬀat 02:00 UT and undergoes
continuing acceleration for nearly three hours. Rota-
tion of the southeast leg of the prominence in the coun-
terclockwise direction is revealed by spectroscopic
observations of CHASE/HIS and imaging observa-
tions of ASO-S/SCI UV. The apex of the prominence
changes from a smooth loop to a cusp structure dur-
ing the rising motion and the northwest leg displays a
drift motion after 04:30 UT, indicating a writhing mo-
tion. Hence, the prominence eruption is most likely
triggered by ideal kink instability.
2. For the ﬁrst time, we apply the GCS modeling in 3D re-
construction and tracking of the prominence for nearly
two hours. Both the source region (110◦E, 43◦N) and
northwest footpoint (162◦E, 44◦N) are pinpointed. The
edge-on and face-on angular widths of the prominence
are ∼6◦and ∼86◦, respectively. The axis has a tilt angle
of ∼70◦with the meridian. The heliocentric distance of
the prominence leading edge increases from ∼1.26 R⊙
to ∼2.27 R⊙. According to the direction of propaga-
tion, the true speed of the related CME increases from
∼610 to ∼849 km s−1.
