Abstract
For a Kummer extension defined by the affine equation ym = Qr
i=1(x −αi)λi
over an algebraic extension K of a finite field Fq, where λi ∈Z\{0} for
1 ≤i ≤r, gcd(m, q) = 1, and α1, · · · , αr ∈K are pairwise distinct ele-
ments, we propose a simple and efficient method to find all pure gaps at many
totally ramified places. We introduce a bottom set of pure gaps and indicate that
the set of pure gaps is completely determined by the bottom set. Furthermore,
we demonstrate that a pure gap can be deduced from a known pure gap by easily
verifying only one inequality. Then, in the case where λ1 = λ2 = · · · = λr, we
fully determine an explicit description of the set of pure gaps at many totally ram-
ified places, This includes the scenario in which the set of these places contains
the infinite place. Finally, we apply these results to construct multi-point alge-
braic geometry codes with good parameters. As one of the examples, a presented
code with parameters [74, 60, ≥10] over F25 yields a new record.
Keywords: Kummer extensions, Pure gaps, Algebraic geometry codes
1 Introduction
Since Goppa introduced the algebraic geometry (AG) codes [1], the research on AG
codes has drawn significant attention in the field of coding theory. Goppa’s construc-
tion of AG codes relies on a function field over a finite field. By utilizing a function

field, we hope to construct AG codes with desirable parameters, namely the length,
the dimension, and the minimum distance.
One approach to constructing multi-point AG codes with good parameters is to
utilize pure gaps at several places. Homma and Kim [2] introduced the concept of
pure gaps at two places, which can be used to improve the minimum distance of an
AG code. Then, this result was extended to several places by Carvalho and Torres in
[3]. The study of pure gaps and the construction of AG codes using pure gaps can be
found in [2–16].

algebraic extension of Fq. Consider the function field F = K(x, y)/K(x) which is a

distinct elements. Pure gaps at several totally ramified places over Kummer exten-
sions have been extensively studied in the literature. However, the majority of the
results were based on Kummer extensions defined by (1), where all the multiplicities
λ1, · · · , λr are equal. For example, Castellanos, Masuda and Quoos [9] characterized
the set of pure gaps at two totally ramified places. In [5], Hu and Yang provided an
explicit characterization of pure gaps at several totally ramified places, and they gave
an arithmetic characterization of the set of pure gaps at several places on a quotient of
the Hermition function field in [15]. Bartoli, Quoos and Zini [11] proposed a criterion
for determining pure gaps and presented some families of pure gaps at many totally
ramified places.
Recently, pure gaps over Kummer extensions defined by (1), where not all the
multiplicities λ1, · · · , λr are equal, have been explored. In [6], Bartoli et al. extended
the concept of pure gaps to c-gaps and obtained numerous families of pure gaps at
two totally ramified places. In [10], based on the knowledge of the minimal generating
set of the Weierstrass semigroup, Castellanos, Mendoza and Quoos characterized the
set of pure gaps at two totally ramified places. However, these studies only foucsed on
investigating pure gaps at two ramified places over Kummer extensions.
Explicitly describing and identifying the set of pure gaps at more than two places
is a complex task. In [17] and [8], using maximal elements in generalized Weierstrass
semigroups, Castellanos, Mendoza and Tizziotti provided a way to completely deter-
mine the set of pure gaps at two places and several rational places in an arbitrary
function field, respectively. For Kummer extensions defined by (1), where all the mul-
tiplicities λ1, · · · , λr are equal, they provided an explicit description of the set of
pure gaps at several totally ramified places distinct to the infinite place. However, an
explicit description of the set of pure gaps at several totally ramified places including
the infinite place remains unexplored in the literature.
In this work, we focus on investigating pure gaps at many totally ramified places
over Kummer extensions defined by (1), where not all λ1, · · · , λr are equal. Inspired by
[6, Theorem 3.3], we propose a new characterization for identifying pure gaps at many

totally ramified places. Then we present a straightforward and efficient method to
find all pure gaps. Our approach differs from the methods mentioned in the preceding
literature. Firstly, we introduce a bottom set of pure gaps, which has the ability to
determine the set of pure gaps (see Definition 2.6 and Theorem 3.1). Subsequently,
we propose that the bottom set of pure gaps at s + 1 totally ramified places can be
efficiently deduced from that of pure gaps at s totally ramified places (see Algorithm 2).
Consequently, by leveraging the principle of induction and the bottom set of pure gaps,
we are able to identify all pure gaps at many totally ramified places (see Algorithm
3). Furthermore, we indicate that a pure gap can be deduced from a known pure gap
by easily verifying only one inequality (see Theorem 3.6).
In particular, for Kummer extensions defined by (1), where all the multiplicities
λ1, · · · , λr are equal, we present a novel description of the set of gaps at some totally
ramified place (see Proposition 4.5). With this new characterization, we fully determine
an explicit description of the set of pure gaps at many totally ramified places, including
the scenario where the set of these places contains the infinite place (see Propositons
4.1, 4.2 and 4.3). These extend the results in [8].
Consecutive pure gaps play a crucial role in improving the minimum distance when
constructing multi-point algebraic geometry codes. Based on our methods, we eas-
ily obtain several families of consecutive pure gaps at many totally ramified places
(see Propositons 4.6, 4.8 and 4.10). By applying these consecutive pure gaps, we con-
struct multi-point AG codes with good parameters, which outperform the parameters
provided in MinT’s Tables [18]. A new record-giving [74, 60, ≥10]-code over F25 is
presented as one of the examples (see Example 5.1).
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we briefly review
some notations and preliminary results on function fields, AG codes and pure gaps.
In Section 3, we present a new characterization of pure gaps at many totally ramified
places and propose a simple and efficient method for finding all pure gaps at many
totally ramified places. In Section 4, we determine an explicit description of set of
pure gaps at many totally ramified places from special Kummer extensions and obtain
several families of consecutive pure gaps. In Section 5, we apply our results to construct
multi-point AG codes with good parameters and showcase several examples.
