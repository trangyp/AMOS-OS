Abstract—As end-user device capability increases and demand
for intelligent services at the Internet’s edge rise, distributed
learning has emerged as a key enabling technology for the
intelligent edge. Existing approaches like federated learning
(FL) and decentralized FL (DFL) enable privacy-preserving
distributed learning among clients, while gossip learning (GL)
approaches have emerged to address the potential challenges in
resource-constrained, connectivity-challenged infrastructure-less
environments. However, most distributed learning approaches
assume largely homogeneous data distributions and may not con-
sider or exploit the heterogeneity of clients and their underlying
data distributions. This paper introduces Chisme, a novel fully
decentralized distributed learning algorithm designed to address
the challenges of implementing robust intelligence in network
edge contexts characterized by heterogeneous data distributions,
episodic connectivity, and sparse network infrastructure or lack
thereof. Chisme leverages cosine similarity-based data affinity
heuristics calculated from received model exchanges to inform
how much influence received models have when merging into
the local model. By doing so, it facilitates stronger merging
influence between clients with more similar model learning
progressions, enabling clients to strategically balance between
broader collaboration to build more general knowledge and
more selective collaboration to build specific knowledge. We
evaluate Chisme against contemporary approaches using image
recognition and time-series prediction scenarios while considering
different network connectivity conditions, representative of real-
world distributed intelligent systems. Our experiments demon-
strate that Chisme outperforms state-of-the-art edge intelligence
approaches in almost every case – clients using Chisme exhibit
faster training convergence, lower final loss after training, and
lower performance disparity between clients.
Index Terms—IoT intelligence, gossip learning, distributed
learning, decentralized learning, AI democratization
I. INTRODUCTION
The use of AI/ML has become increasingly ubiquitous
today, enabling numerous applications and services that people
and infrastructure utilize regularly. Many of these applications
and services are accessed through devices at the network
edge, including personal devices and Internet-of-Things (IoT)
systems. However, AI/ML operations have historically been
resource-intensive and typically require large amounts of data
to yield sufficient performance. Network edge environments
typically involve smaller, more resource-constrained devices
with relatively small amounts of sometimes incongruent data,
on networks with varying degrees of connectivity and relia-
bility. These characteristics have presented barriers to hosting
AI/ML operations. As AI/ML processes have become more
efficient and hardware has become more powerful, AI/ML
operations have become increasingly feasible on more acces-
sible and affordable devices closer to the edge, which has
the potential to manifest as a more pervasive and distributed
intelligence landscape [1]. More localized and decentralized
AI/ML is further reinforced by AI/ML democratization, a
broad movement which pushes for AI/ML capabilities to be
more versatile and more available to under-served domains,
while being less restricted to dominating, centralized ser-
vices [2]. Such intelligence at the edge has the potential to
empower many domains, including remote monitoring systems
in agriculture [3], sustainability [4], and disaster response [5]
which often leverage rugged, replaceable devices in remote
or infrastructure-less environments; privacy-preserving health
diagnostic services on personal and wearable devices over
local and personal area networks [6]; and autonomous vehicle
systems [7] that leverage onboard sensors, computing and
communication capability to infer about and actuate on the
physical world.
Many of these applications have turned to distributed learn-
ing as a solution to train models on data collected by and
stored across a network of devices. Federated learning (FL) [8]
has become a foundational paradigm of distributed learning
and and while it has proven beneficial in various applications,
it can be ineffective in the more volatile environments and
scenarios due to its dependence on centralized infrastructure
to facilitate collaboration between the server and clients,
creating vulnerability to server failures, service disruptions,
and network volatility. While many centralized FL, decen-
tralized FL (DFL), and gossip learning (GL) [9] approaches
have been proposed to address these infrastructure limitations,
most of them optimize towards a single solution across all
clients. Thus, while they may serve application scenarios with
homogeneous data distributions – i.e., distributions in which
data samples are broadly similar in nature and characteristics
– they may not adequately serve all clients well or equally for
applications with more heterogeneous data distributions.

II. BACKGROUND & RELATED WORK
Decentralized machine learning approaches have existed for
decades, with many early works focusing on the distribution
of tasks, leveraging multi-agent systems, and parallel process-
ing [10]. The advent and rise of deep learning as a dominant
form of AI/ML presented challenges to distributed and col-
laborative machine learning as meaningful performance from
deep learning models usually required more computing than
what was available on typical edge devices. The alternative
was to require edge devices to transmit large amounts of
encrypted data over the network to be collected and processed
at data centers, making these systems vulnerable to high
communication costs, security risks, and privacy concerns.
As devices became more capable, federated learning
(FL) [8] was proposed as a solution to leverage on-device
training, in which a central server facilitates model training
over data distributed across many clients, where clients’ data
never leave their devices. The standard FL process is as
follows:
• The server disseminates the global model to all clients.
• Each client trains its model copy on local data and
computes gradient or weight updates.
• Each client sends its update back to the server.
• The server aggregates the updates and applies a single
update to the global model.
FL has been quickly adopted as a core enabling paradigm
across many different distributed application domains for its
communication efficiency and privacy preservation features,
including smartphone services that leverage user and sensor
input data to provide intelligent recommendations and analyt-
ics [11], healthcare solutions for privacy-preserving predictive
services and analytics [12], and numerous IoT applications,
ranging from smart homes and cities to unmanned aerial and
terrestrial vehicles [13], [14].
However, FL has inherent limitations. For one, standard FL
is centralized and depends on a server to facilitate the dissem-
ination and aggregation of model updates, with performance
hinging on server availability. Decentralized FL (DFL) [15],
[16] – in which agents within a network act as both the
server and the client – have been proposed almost since FL
itself was proposed. Gossip learning (GL) [9] has also been
offered as an alternative to FL to address the limitations posed
by centralization as a fully decentralized and asynchronous
paradigm in which agents train their local models and send
them asynchronously, merging received models into their own
as they come in. Secondly, standard FL has a single objective
function – to train a model to a single solution for all
clients, adequate for scenarios with more homogeneous data
distributions. However, for scenarios with more heterogeneous
or incongruent data distributions, multiple optimal solutions
exist and can differ from client to client, making a single
global solution inadequate, as illustrated in Figure 1. Even with
DFL and GL, while many approaches focus on communication
efficiency and computing viability, just as with FL, most
approaches primarily target eventual convergence to a single
stationary solution meant to serve all agents.
In discussing these heterogeneously distributed systems, we
refer to the term differentiation, a concept borrowed from
the education domain to describe teaching methodologies that
consider the differences inherent among a set of learners and
provide solutions that are responsive to each learner while
accomplishing learning outcomes for all learners [17]. In
distributed deep learning, the implied existence of multiple
distinct optimal solutions necessitates more differentiated ap-
proaches, i.e., approaches that facilitate collaborative model
training while allowing multiple optimal solutions across
clients. To this end, several approaches have leveraged global
and local model training to yield differentiated solutions,

III. Chisme: COLLABORATIVE HETEROGENEITY-AWARE
INTELLIGENCE VIA SIMILARITY-BASED MODEL
EXCHANGES
We consider a decentralized network of clients N, where
every client x ∈N has a local dataset Dx from its local dis-
tribution Px and is instantiated with the same baseline model
θt=0. We assume that each client x periodically trains its local
model parameters θx on its local dataset Dx and produces
an updated model state ˆθx. We consider heterogeneous data
distribution such that for any clients i, j ⊂N where i ̸= j,
it is possible that their distributions Pi, Pj may be more or
less similar. We assume that each client x is connected to
its neighbors Mx ⊂N by an underlying network topology
that remains static for the duration of the system runtime.
Whether this static topology is defined by an infrastructure-
based network (e.g., the Internet) or an ad-hoc network (e.g.,
peer-to-peer) is not necessarily important for this work, as
we assume that communication cost between client x and
its neighbor y ⊂Mx is constant. We assume that the links
along this topology can be volatile, such that at a given
timestep t, client x may only be able to communicate model
update messages mt
x to a subset of its neighbors Rt
x ⊆Mx.
Given this system model, we propose to address the following
problem — how can we facilitate the collaborative training
of differentiated models θi among clients i ∈N to improve
model training performance on their local data distributions Pi
without relying on centralized infrastructure and using only the
communication of model update messages m between clients?
To answer this, we first re-establish the notion of gossip
learning which we use as a baseline for our work. Then,
we discuss the use of cosine similarity to infer data affinity,
which is the key component of our novel differentiated model
merging. We briefly discuss an intermediary step we took in
applying inferred data affinity to the model aggregation in
DFL. Finally, we present our approach, in which we implement
our data affinity heuristic into a similarity-based model merge
method for gossip learning to enable fully decentralized and
differentiated deep learning over edge networks.

B. Inferring Data Similarity through Cosine Similarity
Given our network of clients N, we know an optimal
solution θopt.

calculated and weight update applied moves the model toward
that optimal solution. If we consider two clients, then for each
client, optimal solutions exist that the clients train their models
towards. Suppose the clients are from congruent or similar data
distributions. In that case, these optimal solutions are likely to
be close, and the clients’ models would be trained towards
those closely aligned optimal solutions. Therefore, given that
identical copies of the baseline model are disseminated to
clients at initialization and that they are being trained towards
closely aligned optimal solutions, these models train in the
same “direction”, i.e., the vectors of their respective updates
from a common baseline will have a low angular distance.

local training towards local optima with similar underlying
data distributions yield a low angular distance between model

Note on Memory & Convergence: Our method has each
client store its local active model parameters θt, its saved
prior model parameters θτ, and the received model parameters
θ∗that are being analyzed and merged during an interaction.
Given a constant number of parameters per model p = |θ|, its
memory usage can be described as 3p, which stays constant
even as the network size increases. When a client receives a
message while its active model is training, the active model
may be locked and unable to be used for merging. In this
case, which we do not explore in this work, a client may
consider storing these messages in a fixed-size β buffer, in
which memory usage would need to be evaluated as (3+β)p.
We consider the memory footprint of M to be trivial given
that it contains integers and |N|2 ≪p. Because Chisme
actuates model aggregation similarly to standard GL [9] and
FL [8], [21], [22] approaches that are known to converge
when collaboration is strong and mimics classical on-device
learning when collaboration is low, we can assume that it will
converge within these bounded convergences. We validate this
convergence empirically in our experiments.

sufficient angular differences between dissimilar clients to
achieve effective clustering. We also include our intermediary
approach, CosSimDFL as described in Section III-C, for
reference.

B. Discussion
We find that in almost every case, Chisme outperforms all
other approaches, with clients’ joint model training converging
faster to a lower loss while achieving lower performance
disparity between clients. We observe its performance ad-
vantages are more pronounced under less favorable network
conditions. In the modified MNIST scenario under favorable
network conditions (Figure 3a), we observe that FLIC does
outperform Chisme in achieving lower loss and performance
disparity. Even then, under less connected and reliable net-
work conditions (Figure 3b), Chisme edges out FLIC in loss
reduction with much lower performance disparity. Also, as
mentioned before, this scenario is an artificially high degree
of incongruence to model extreme heterogeneity; in the more
realistic FEMNIST and real-world Irish Weather Station sce-
narios, Chisme retains its performance advantages. In some
cases (Figure 4), we observe that Chisme initially exhibits
higher performance disparity before that disparity drops lower
than other approaches; we reason that this is because Chisme is
more selective about collaboration at earlier stages, and we can
see analagous behavior from FLIC after its clustering rounds
in which collaboration becomes focused within clusters.
Our results suggest that as clients in Chisme train and
collaborate, our methods push clients to be more selective
about accepting and merging incoming model updates than
other approaches. While we had some concern about this trend
pushing towards non-collaboration as the optimal paradigm,
we observed that clients using Chisme do maintain some
non-trivial level of collaboration throughout system runtime
and still outperforms non-collaborative, on-device training.
Thus, we assert that while many previous approaches to
differentiated deep learning [19]–[21] have included discrete
stages of broad collaboration and selective collaboration (e.g.,
clustering or on-device finetuning) to achieve general and
specific knowledge-building respectively, Chisme exhibits a
smooth transition from broad to selective collaboration. We
also assert that, given our observations its comparatively
low performance disparity, Chisme results in more equitable

V. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we introduced Chisme, a fully decentralized
heterogeneity-aware approach to distributed deep learning
suited for the network edge and IoT environments, using a
cosine similarity-based data affinity heuristic derived from
received updates’ model parameters to adjust how those up-
dates are merged into the local model. Our evaluation of
Chisme indicate that it performs competitively against existing
approaches while providing equitable performance outcomes
for all clients despite heterogeneity in dataset sizes and data
distributions; these advantages are more pronounced in less
favorable network contexts. In improving our approach, we
hypothesize that using a parameterized activation function
on the scaled cosine similarity value – such as a sigmoid
function with tunable shift and steepness – could provide more
versatility in different scenarios. We also seek to understand
and compare Chisme with other fully decentralized approaches
w.r.t. network bandwidth utilization, limited communication
budgets, and model performance in specific application do-
mains such as sensor networks and smart cities. While this
paper only focused on a few application scenarios, we aim
to explore how our and similar methods could prove useful
in training or fine-tuning large, pre-trained deep learning
models over heterogeneous clients and how we may integrate
methods like optimizing communication efficiency and joint
model pruning. We seek to apply Chisme and orient these
explorations to further contribute to the emerging class of
robust and ambient intelligence protocols.
