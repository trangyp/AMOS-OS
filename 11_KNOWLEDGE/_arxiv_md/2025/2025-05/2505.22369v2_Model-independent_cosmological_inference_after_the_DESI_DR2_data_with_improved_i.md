Recently, the baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) measurements from the Dark Energy Spec-
troscopic Instrument (DESI) Collaboration survey have suggested hints of dynamical dark energy,
challenging the standard Λ cold dark matter (ΛCDM) model. In this work, we adopt an improved in-
verse distance ladder approach based on the latest cosmological data to provide a model-independent
perspective, employing a global parametrization based on cosmic age (PAge). Our analysis incor-
porates DESI DR2 BAO measurements, cosmic chronometer (CC) data, and type Ia supernovae
observations from either the DESY5 or PantheonPlus datasets. For the DESY5+DESI DR2+CC
datasets, we obtain H0 = 67.91±2.33 km s−1 Mpc−1. This value is consistent with the Planck 2018
result, while showing 2.0σ tension with the SH0ES measurement. Furthermore, by mapping specific
cosmological models into PAge approximation parameter space (page, η), our model-independent
analysis reveals a notable deviation from the ΛCDM model, as indicated by the DESY5 and DESI
DR2 datasets. Finally, DESY5+DESI DR2+CC datasets provide nearly decisive evidence favor-
ing the PAge model over the standard ΛCDM model. These findings highlight the need for further
investigation into the expansion history to better understand the deviations from the ΛCDM model.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The simplest Λ cold dark matter (ΛCDM) model
has been remarkably consistent with most astronomi-
cal observations across different epochs and scales of
the Universe [1–6].
However, the persistent discrep-
ancy in the value of the Hubble constant H0 inferred
from the early and late Universe, known as the Hub-
ble tension, is challenging the ΛCDM model [7–10].
Among various discrepancies, the most significant is the
over 5σ divergence between the Planck 2018 estimate
of H0 = 67.4 ± 0.5 km s−1 Mpc−1, indirectly inferred
from the cosmic microwave background (CMB) data as-
suming the ΛCDM model [11], and the SH0ES result
of H0 = 73.04 ± 1.04 km s−1 Mpc−1 directly measured
from the type Ia supernovae (SNe) calibrated using the
Cepheid variables [12].
The tension cannot be merely
explained by a statistical fluctuation, even after the re-
analysis of systematic uncertainties [13, 14]. Therefore,
the irreconcilable tension may indicate underlying sys-
tematics or new physics beyond ΛCDM [15–18].
However, no consensus has yet been reached on re-
solving the tension based on current data.
Given this
discrepancy between the early and late Universe, pro-
posed scenarios can broadly be classified into early-time
or late-time solutions.

In early-time solutions, new physics is introduced to re-
duce the sound horizon, rd, thereby increasing H0 while
preserving the baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) mea-
surement of rdH0. One realization involves modifying ex-
pansion history by introducing additional energy compo-
nents around the epoch of matter-radiation equality, such
as early dark energy [19–23] or extra neutrino species [24–
31]. Alternatively, early-time solutions may also involve
modifying the recombination history [32–34] or introduc-
ing primordial magnetic fields [35–37]. However, these
early-time solutions are in tension with other cosmolog-
ical observables, such as the fluctuation amplitude σ81
and the integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect, as discussed in the
no-go arguments of Refs. [40–44].
Late-time solutions
modify the Hubble parameter H(z) in the late universe,
so that it can accommodate the SH0ES H0 result locally
while leaving the CMB anisotropy spectrum unchanged,
such as the deformation of the dark-energy equation of
state [45–53], modified gravity [54–56], and parameter
transitions in the late Universe [57, 58]. Analogous to
early-time solutions, various no-go arguments have also
been raised against the late-time scenarios.
The re-
quirement of consistency between low redshift cosmolog-
ical distance measurements from BAO and SNe imposes
strong constraints on the late-time solutions [59, 60].
Moreover, these solutions cannot reconcile the H0 ten-

In light of the plethora of proposed cosmological mod-
els and corresponding no-go arguments, it is compelling
to explore new and independent measurements of H0.
For instance, techniques such as strong gravitational lens-
ing time delay [66, 67] and gravitational wave standard
sirens [68–70] provide an independent perspective on this
tension. We can also employ other independent calibra-
tors to replace Cepheid variables in the distance ladder,
such as megamasers [71], the tip of the red giant branch
[72], surface brightness fluctuations [73], and Mira vari-
ables [74].
The inverse distance ladder (IDL) calibrates the SNe
via anchored BAO instead of Cepheid variables, provid-
ing a model-insensitive method to infer H0 [75–82]. In
the traditional IDL, the BAO data are anchored using an
rd prior derived from the CMB or big bang nucleosynthe-
sis. Then the data are used to calibrate SNe to give an
absolute distance. Finally, the distance is extrapolated
to z = 0 to infer H0 under a fiducial H(z) model [83–89].
It should be noted that the rd prior introduces depen-
dence on early-Universe physics [90]. The effect of this rd
prior has been systematically evaluated in Refs. [91, 92].
To mitigate such dependence, the rd prior can be re-
placed with other independent measurements. The cos-
mic chronometer (CC) measures H(z) directly, rather
than through integrals of H(z), such as those involved
in lensing distances from strong lensing systems [93–96],
thus offering higher sensitivity to cosmological param-
eters [97–99]. CC data have been widely applied as an
effective replacement for rd in constructing IDL [92, 100–
102].
Furthermore, adopting a fiducial H(z) derived from a
specific cosmological model in IDL will inevitably intro-
duce model dependence. To avoid this, the traditional
approach is to parametrize H(z) via a Taylor expan-
sion in terms of z or y ≡1 −a = z/(1 + z), known
as the cosmography method [78, 87, 92, 103–108]. Al-
ternatively, purely data-driven techniques such as the
Gaussian process (GP) have been employed to recon-
struct H(z) [100, 109–112]. However, both approaches
possess intrinsic limitations.
The Taylor expansion is
problematic at z ∼1 [102, 105, 113, 114]. GP methods
are sensitive to the choice of kernel functions and tend
to yield smaller errors at the cost of strongly correlated
results [115, 116]. To address these limitations, we adopt
a global parametrization based on the cosmic age (PAge)
[117, 118] and its extension, a more accurate parametriza-
tion based on cosmic age (MAPAge) [114, 119], to accu-
rately reproduce late-time cosmological behaviors.

The PAge model can be interpreted as an effective de-
scription of homogeneous late-time scenarios. The stud-
ies [120–122] have employed the PAge model to ana-
lyze gamma-ray bursts, redshift-space distortions, and
quasars. References [102, 114, 123] have derived no-go
theorems against late-time solutions through the compar-
ison between the PAge (denoted as late-time scenarios)
and ΛCDM models.
Recently, the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument
(DESI) Collaboration reported a 3.9σ hint of dynamical
dark energy in the first data release (DR1), which was
further increased to 4.2σ in the second data release (DR2)
[88, 124]. This finding has sparked intense discussions
about possible new physics and systematic uncertainties
[125–165].
This behavior, which deviates from ΛCDM
can be effectively described by the PAge model.
This
motivates us to propose a PAge-improved IDL to yield
fully model-independent cosmological constraints in this
work. Our results will provide model-independent per-
spectives on the H0 tension and the hint of dynamical
dark energy.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In
Sec. II, we introduce the PAge model and cosmological
datasets used in our analysis. We present the cosmolog-
ical constraints and related discussion in Sec. III. The
paper concludes with a summary in Sec. IV.
II.
METHODOLOGY
A.
The PAge and MAPAge parametrization
Within the IDL, the PAge (MAPAge) parametrization
is used to extrapolate H(z) to z = 0, thereby determining
H0 in a cosmological-model-independent manner.
The
PAge (MAPAge) rests on two assumptions:
(i) At z ≫1, the Universe is matter dominated, and
the brief radiation-dominated era is neglected.
(ii) In PAge, the product of the Hubble parameter H
and the cosmic time t, can be approximated by a
quadratic function of t. In MAPAge, this product
can be approximated by a cubic function of t.
These assumptions are independent of any specific cos-
mological model, such as dark energy models and mod-
ified gravity theories [102, 114, 117, 123, 166]. Rather
than parametrizing the dark energy equation of state,
PAge directly parametrizes the cosmic expansion his-
tory, providing a more straightforward probe of that
history than equation of state based approaches such
as w0waCDM. Compared with traditional parametriza-
tions, such as expansions in z or y [105, 107, 167], PAge
(MAPAge) can parametrize H(z) over a wider redshift
range with higher accuracy. As shown in Fig. 1, PAge
(MAPAge) can approximate the target w0waCDM model
up to z ∼103, whereas z and y expansion develop devi-
ation at z > 1 [102, 168]. Moreover, for target models

IV.
CONCLUSION
The tension between the early-Universe and late-
Universe measurements of H0 has become one of the most
significant challenges in modern cosmology.
Recently,
DESI DR1 [88] and DR2 [124] have further sharpened
this tension with addition hints of dynamical dark en-
ergy in 3.9σ and 4.2σ level. In the context of the H0 ten-
sion, numerous theoretical models have been proposed,
although many models face corresponding no-go argu-
ments. Similarly, the hint of dynamical dark energy has
been critically examined through the reanalyzes of the
DESY5 SNe data [129, 131, 202, 203] and the DESI BAO
measurements [204–206].
To provide a model-independent perspective, we em-
ploy the PAge-improved IDL in this work to con-
strain cosmological parameters.
Our work is based
on four datasets,
namely DESY5+DESI DR2+CC,
DESY5+SDSS+CC,
PantheonPlus+DESI
DR2+CC,
and PantheonPlus+SDSS+CC, allowing us to investigate
the respective roles of SNe and BAO data in constraining
cosmological parameters. For DESY5+DESI DR2+CC,
we find H0 = 67.91 ± 2.33 km s−1 Mpc−1, which agrees
with the Planck 2018 result, but exhibits a mild 2.0σ ten-
sion with the SH0ES measurement. The other datasets
also remain consistent with Planck 2018. Compared to
SH0ES, the resulting tensions are 2.1σ, 1.6σ, and 1.7σ
for DESY5+SDSS+CC, PantheonPlus+DESI DR2+CC,
and PantheonPlus+SDSS+CC, respectively. In our re-
sults, the DESY5 data tend to favor a lower value of H0
and a higher value of MB, which correspond to a shift in
the intercept of the magnitude–redshift relation within

Mapping specific cosmological models into the PAge

DESI DR2 hints of dynamical dark energy and deviate

In future work, we aim to explore the data in more
detail by performing a redshift-binned analysis.
Mo-
tivated by the connection between the expansion his-
tory and the dark-energy equation of state, we will fur-
ther investigate the occurrence of unphysical phantom
behaviors [207, 208] using the PAge model.
It should
be noted that the PAge model is essentially a Taylor
expansion, which may suffer from singularities as dis-
cussed in Ref. [105] when reconstructing the equation-
of-state parameter. We are going to calibrate the Tay-
lor expansion with alternatives beyond the early-matter-
dominated Universe used in the PAge model, to avoid the
singularities. These efforts may provide new insights into
the nature of dark energy and further clarify the physical
origin of the H0 tension.
