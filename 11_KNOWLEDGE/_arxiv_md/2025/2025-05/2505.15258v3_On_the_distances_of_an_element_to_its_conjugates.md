ON THE DISTANCES OF AN ELEMENT TO ITS CONJUGATES
JOSNEI NOVACOSKI
Abstract. For a valued field (K, v), with a fixed extension of v to the algebraic
closure K of K, and an element θ ∈K, we are interested in the possible values of
θ −θ′ where θ′ runs through all the K-conjugates of θ. The study of these values
is a classic problem in number theory and ramification theory. However, the classic
results focus on tame, and in particular defectless, extensions. In this paper we
focus on the study of defect extensions. We want to compare the number of such
values to invariants of θ. The main invariant we have in mind is the depth of θ. We
present various examples that show that, in the defect case, none of the equivalent
of the classic results are true. We also discuss the relation between the number of
such values and the number of ramification ideals of the extension (K(θ)/K, v). In
order to do so, we present some results about ramification ideals that have interest
on their own.
1. Introduction
Let K be a field with a fixed algebraic closure K, fix a valuation v on K and set
Γ := vK. For a separable element θ ∈K we are interested in the set
Sθ = {v(θ′ −θ) | θ′ is a K-conjugate of θ and θ ̸= θ′}.
The Krasner’s constant ω(θ) is, by definition, the maximum of Sθ. It follows from
the definition that #Sθ = 1 if and only if
(1)
v(θ −θ′) = ω(θ) for every conjugate θ′ of θ, θ′ ̸= θ.
We want to compare the cardinality of Sθ with natural invariants of θ. The main
number we have in mind is the depth of θ. For θ ∈K we consider the valuation vθ
on K[x] defined as
vθ(f) = v(f(θ)).
The length of the Mac Lane-Vaqui´e (MLV) chains of vθ is said to be the depth of vθ,
denoted by depth(θ) (see Section 3 for more details). This concept is not intrinsically
associated to E := (L/K, v). Different generators of the same extension may have
different depths. This leads to defining the depth of a simple algebraic extension of
valued fields as
depth(E) := min{depth(θ) | L = K(θ)}.
We will say that θ is pure (or that E is pure in θ) if depth(θ) = 1.

2. Notation and terminology
Throughout this paper, we will denote by N the set of all positive and by N0 the
set of all non-negative integers.
We will say that the extension E = (L/K, v) is unibranched if v is the only exten-
sion of v|K to L. For a field L, K ⊆L ⊆K, we will denote by Lh the henselization
of L, i.e., the smallest subfield L′, L ⊆L′ ⊆K, such that (K/L′, v) is unibranched.
A valued field (K, v) is Henselian if K = Kh, or equivalently, if (K/K, v) is uni-
branched.
We will regard (in the natural way) Kv as a subfield of Lv. The defect of E is the
number

We will say that E is defectless if d(E) = 1. Otherwise, we will say that E is a
defect extension. We will call E is an immediate extension if
