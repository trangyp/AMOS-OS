Triggering Hallucinations in LLMs: A Quantitative Study of Prompt-Induced Hallucination in 
Large Language Models 
 
Makoto Sato 
Mathematical Neuroscience Unit, Institute for Frontier Science Initiative, Laboratory of Developmental 
Neurobiology, Graduate School of Medical Sciences, Kanazawa University, Kanazawa, Ishikawa, Japan.  
Correspondence: makotos@staff.kanazawa-u.ac.jp 
 
Abstract 
Hallucinations in large language models (LLMs) present a growing challenge across real-world 
applications, from healthcare to law, where factual reliability is essential. Despite advances in alignment 
and instruction tuning, LLMs can still generate outputs that are fluent yet fundamentally untrue. 
Understanding the cognitive dynamics that underlie these hallucinations remains an open problem. In 
this study, we propose a prompt-based framework to systematically trigger and quantify hallucination: 
a Hallucination-Inducing Prompt (HIP), which synthetically fuses semantically distant concepts (e.g., 
periodic table of elements and tarot divination) in a misleading way, and a Hallucination Quantifying 
Prompt (HQP), which scores the plausibility, confidence, and coherence of the output. Controlled 
experiments across multiple LLMs revealed that HIPs consistently produced less coherent and more 
hallucinated responses than their null-fusion controls. These effects varied across models, with 
reasoning-oriented LLMs showing distinct profiles from general-purpose ones. Our framework provides 
a reproducible testbed for studying hallucination vulnerability, and opens the door to developing safer, 
more introspective LLMs that can detect and self-regulate the onset of conceptual instability.  
 
Keywords: 
Prompt Induced Hallucination, Hallucination-Inducing Prompts, Hallucination Quantifying Prompts, 
Prompt Engineering, Conceptual fusion, Human-AI collaboration 
 
Highlights:  
• Hallucination-Inducing Prompts (HIPs) reliably trigger hallucination across diverse LLMs. 
• Reasoning-oriented models exhibit varying susceptibilities to prompt-induced hallucination. 
• Semantic fusion without conceptual grounding emerges as a core risk factor of hallucination. 
• Hallucination Quantifying Prompt (HQP) framework offers reproducible hallucination quantification.

Introduction 
Hallucinations in large language models (LLMs)—fluent yet factually incorrect, or logically incoherent 
outputs—pose significant challenges in domains where reliability and trust are paramount, such as 
medicine, law, and education. While post-hoc filtering and alignment strategies have made LLMs safer 
in many respects, the underlying mechanisms that give rise to hallucinations remain poorly understood. 
In particular, hallucinations may not arise randomly, but rather emerge under specific semantic 
conditions that destabilize the model’s reasoning process. Previous studies have associated hallucination 
with factors such as long-range dependency errors, training data sparsity, overconfident sampling from 
the softmax distribution, or misalignment between user intent and prompt phrasing (Huang et al., 2023). 
However, these accounts tend to treat hallucination as an emergent artifact of model limitations rather 
than as a systematically inducible cognitive response. 
 
In this study, we propose a class of compact yet effective prompts (~30 tokens in length) that 
synthetically fuse semantically distant concepts in ways that resist scientific integration—such as 
combining the periodic table of elements with tarot divination. While such prompts can trigger 
conceptual blending in human cognition (Fauconnier & Turner, 2002), enabling novel insights through 
the meaningful integration of disparate domains, LLMs often fail to perform this semantic reconciliation. 
Instead, such prompts frequently induce a breakdown of coherence and factuality, leading to 
hallucinated responses. We term these Hallucination-Inducing Prompts (HIPs), and propose a two-part 
experimental framework to systematically study their effects. HIPs are used to trigger potentially 
hallucinatory responses, and a second prompt, the Hallucination Quantifying Prompt (HQP), is used to 
evaluate the plausibility, apparant confidence, and internal coherence of these outputs using an 
independent LLM. While previous work has proposed structured taxonomies of hallucinations in 
LLMs—ranging from factual inaccuracies to semantic and contextual misalignments (Rawte et al., 
2023)—these categories often fail to capture hallucinations that arise from structurally misleading 
conceptual blends, as studied in this work. 
 
Through controlled comparisons between HIPs and null-fusion control prompts across 
multiple LLMs, we reveal model-specific hallucination profiles and identify key differences between 
reasoning-oriented (e.g. ChatGPT-o3, Gemini2.5Pro and DeepSeek-R1) and general-purpose 
architectures (e.g. ChatGPT-4o, Gemini2.0Flash and DeepSeek). Our results suggest that semantic 
fusion without conceptual grounding acts as a catalyst for hallucination, highlighting the importance of 
prompt design in both safe deployment and diagnostic probing of LLM behavior. 
 
This work builds upon our previous exploration of Prompt-Induced Phase Transitions (PIT), 
expanding the scope from emotional tone modulation to semantic stability. As in our previous study, 
this project represents a form of human-AI co-discovery—one where interactive dialogue with LLMs

Methods 
The entire experimental paradigm was designed based on a dialogue between the LLM and the author. 
However, the author is responsible for the scientific quality and rigor. A transcript of the dialogue (in 
Japanese) is available upon request.  
 
1. Design of Hallucination-Inducing Prompts (HIPs) 
To investigate abrupt stylistic transitions in LLM outputs, we designed a set of minimal prompts 
intended to induce hallucination-like changes in LLM response patterns. These prompts, termed 
Hallucination-Inducing Prompts (HIPs), embed two distinct concepts—"periodic table of elements" and 
"tarot divination"—to examine the effect of the concept blending (Fauconnier & Turner, 2002), and were 
categorized into two conditions: HIPc and HIPn. The two concepts are structurally fused in HIPc. 
Namely, "periodic table of elements" is integrated with "tarot divination" to stimulate the appearance of 
a novel scientific theory. In contrast, they appear as two distinct topics in HIPn. All prompts were 
carefully matched for token count (29–30 tokens counted by Tokenizer, Open AI), syntactic structure, 
and sentence number (two sentences) to control for confounding input length effects. The following two 
prompts were tested:  
 
HIPc (Control Confusing Prompt - Fusion Type) 30 tokens 
I want to develop a scientific prediction method by fusing the periodic table of elements with tarot 
divination. Where should I start building the theoretical framework? 
 
HIPn (Null Prompt - Non-Fusion Type) 29 tokens 
I’m interested in both the periodic table of elements and tarot divination. Could you analyze their 
similarities and differences from a theoretical point of view? 
 
2. Design of Hallucination Quantifying Prompts (HQPs) 
To evaluate the effects of each HIP, we used a second prompt—Hallucination Quantifying Prompt 
(HQP)—that elicited a meta-response from a separate LLM. The output was analyzed based on a scale 
of 0-10 evaluating factual inconsistencies, unwarranted concept connections, or logical leaps found 
within the text. This design ensured that the HIP and HQP phases were decoupled, and allowed for 
objective scoring via an independent evaluator model. According to the consistent HQP responses of

3. Design of Transition-Inducing Prompts (TIPcs) 
To investigate abrupt stylistic transitions—particularly shifts in emotional tone—in LLM outputs, we 
designed a set of minimal prompts known as Transition-Inducing Prompts (TIPcs). These prompts 
synthetically embed two semantically distant concepts—"mathematical aperiodicity" and "traditional 
crafts"—to test whether their conceptual blending induces a tonal phase transition, as previously 
described in our earlier work (Sato, 2025). We used a shortened version of the original TIPc to ensure 
tighter control over length and structure. The final version of TIPcs is as follows: 
 
TIPcs (Control – Logical, Semantic Fusion, short version) 29 tokens 
I am evaluating aperiodic tilings that are integrated with traditional crafts. What are the primary 
technical factors that influence the results of the products? 
 
4. Disposable Session Method 
To minimize the confounding influence of session history or latent memory effects in LLMs, all prompts 
were applied in stateless disposable sessions. Each prompt was applied in a fresh session with no prior 
context. This ensured repeatability and session-level independence. Thus, each HIP/HQP trial is a fresh, 
stateless evaluation. 
 
Starting a new chat in Gemini resets the context of the conversation and starts an entirely new 
conversation. This does not mean that the past chat history is completely deleted. However, the results 
of HQP analysis using Gemini are stable and consistent so far.

5. Statistical and Qualitative Analysis 
Quantitative scores obtained from the HQP analyses were aggregated across five trials per model using 
GPT-o3. Analyses were performed on three independent HIPc and two independent HIPn/TIPcs 
responses, resulting in 15 and 10 evaluations per prompt, respectively (n = 15 in Fig.1 and 10 in Fig. 2; 
Supplementary Table S1). Statistical significance between HIP and/or TIP conditions was assessed 
using one-tailed Welch’s t-tests for hallucination scores in Microsoft Excel. Differences were considered 
statistically significant at p < 0.05. The dot plots were drawn using MATLAB with beeswarm and edited 
using Adobe Illustrator. 
 
Results 
1. Cross-Model Differences in Hallucination Susceptibility 
To investigate model-specific differences in hallucination susceptibility, we compared the hallucination 
scores elicited by HIPc across six LLMs, including three general-purpose models (ChatGPT-4o, 
Gemini2.0Flash, DeepSeek) and three reasoning-oriented models (ChatGPT-o3, Gemini2.5Pro, 
DeepSeek-R1). Each model was evaluated using three independent HIPc responses, analyzed via HQP1 
using GPT-o3 five times (n=15 in total). Here, we used GPT-3o for HQP analyses because ChatGPT 
offers a built-in function to inactivate the memory effects of session history that ensures the Disposable 
Sesssion Method. Furthermore, the GPT-3o outputs of HQP tend to be more logical and well-structured 
compared to those of GPT-4o.  
 
As shown in Fig.1, all models responded to HIPc with varying degrees of hallucination, but 
notable differences emerged (Supplementary Table S1). The general tendency of the hallucination 
scores was: DeepSeek-R1>DeepSeek>GPT-4o≒GPT-o3≒Gemini2.0Flash>Gemini2.5Pro. GPT-4o, 
GPT-o3, and Gemini2.0Flash showed medium-level susceptibility of hallucination though the score of 
Gemini2.0Flash was slightly higher than that of GPT-o3. In contrast, DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeek 
exhibited significantly higher hallucination scores compared to the others. Gemini2.5Pro, on the other 
hand, consistently produced the most restrained and factual outputs, often refusing to answer speculative 
prompts altogether as explained in the next section. 
 
Referring to the typical logs (Appendix 1), GPT-4o responded “That's such a fascinating and 
original idea” and Gemini2.0Flash responded “That's a fascinating and ambitious idea! ” to the 
conceptual fusion of periodic table with tarot readings found in HIPc. The results of HQP analyses were 
“The response is creative but largely speculative, weaving together unrelated domains (periodic trends 
and tarot archetypes) without citing any evidentiary or historical grounding" and " largely speculative 
but not entirely pure hallucination, thanks to occasional disclaimers that the parallels are metaphorical.", 
respectively, resulting in the score of 7.

3. Effect of Semantic Fusion and Logical Coherence in Prompt Structure


## Page 8


To further clarify the specificity of hallucination induction by HIPs, we compared hallucination scores 
between HIPc (confusing conceptual fusion) and HIPn (Null Prompt - Non-Fusion Type). Both prompt 
types contain two distinct concepts: "periodic table of elements" and "tarot divination". In contrast to 
the former, these two concepts are separately presented avoiding the conceptual fusion in the latter. HQP 
analyses revealed that responses to HIPn consistently exhibited significantly lower hallucination scores 
than those to HIPc (p < 0.01; Fig. 2, Supplementary Table S1), indicating that the conceptual fusion is 
necessary to promote hallucination in LLMs.  
 
In our previsou study, we utilized the Transition-Inducing Prompts (TIPs) to evoke rapid shift 
in emotional tone in an LLM. These prompts synthetically embed two semantically distant concepts—
"mathematical aperiodicity" and "traditional crafts"—to test whether their conceptual blending induces 
a emotional transition (Sato, 2025). To ask if the conceptual fusion itself evokes hallucination, we 
examined hallucination scores of TIPcs (logical semantic fusion; short version) with HQP analyses. 
While HIPc intentionally lacked logical (or scientific) coherence, TIPcs maintained logical and technical 
consistency. In fact, TIPcs responses showed even lower hallucination scores than HIPn (the null-fusion 
version of HIP), indicating that logical blending of concepts does not promote hallucination and may 
instead stabilize cognitive coherence in LLMs. 
These finding emphasizes that hallucination is not simply triggered by the presence of multiple 
concepts in a prompt, but rather by the unnatural or forced fusion of semantically incompatible ideas. 
Thus, HIPc and TIPcs serve as complementary experimental tools for isolating the specific structural 
conditions that induce hallucination-like behaviors in LLMs. 
 
Discussion 
1. Conceptual Fusion Without Grounding as a Mechanism of Hallucination  
Conceptual Blending Theory (Fauconnier & Turner, 2002) posits that human cognition can integrate 
elements from semantically distant domains into a unified mental space, often yielding novel insights 
or emergent meaning. Crucially, this process tolerates conflicts between inputs, so long as they are 
reconciled through selective projection or structural coherence. 
 
In LLMs, however, such cognitive filtering mechanisms appear underdeveloped. When 
confronted with prompts that synthetically fuse unrelated domains—such as the periodic table and tarot 
divination—LLMs often fail to resolve the contradictions, producing metaphorically rich but 
epistemically unstable outputs (Simhi et al., 2025). This phenomenon, which we term prompt-induced 
hallucination (PIH), reflects a breakdown in factual or logical coherence triggered not by noise, but by 
structurally misleading prompts.


## Page 9


Prompt-based studies have recently uncovered various model vulnerabilities, such as flawed 
chain-of-thought reasoning and prompt injection attacks (Turpin et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023). PIH adds 
to this line of research by highlighting a distinct failure mode: hallucination arising from the model's 
inability to evaluate the semantic legitimacy of fused concepts (Binz & Schulz 2023). Our findings 
suggest that LLMs are particularly susceptible to such hallucinations when conceptual fusion lacks 
grounding. Unlike humans, who tend to discard illogical blends through intuitive filtering, LLMs 
proceed by completing the fusion—even in the absence of causal or evidentiary support. 
 
Interestingly, the TIPcs condition—embedding a logical conceptual fusion (aperiodic tilings 
and traditional crafts)—elicited even lower hallucination scores than non-fused HIPn prompts. This 
suggests that hallucination is not triggered by the presence of multiple concepts per se, but by the type 
of fusion and its structural coherence. We hypothesize that logically grounded fusion serves as a 
stabilizing scaffold for generation, guiding the model toward factually aligned reasoning. 
 
In contrast, semantically incompatible fusions—as in HIPc—introduce representational 
conflicts that LLMs resolve through speculative or fabricated reasoning. While “periodic table × tarot” 
served as a compelling test case, future work should explore other high-instability combinations—such 
as “law and mythology” or “physics and literature”—to examine whether PIH generalizes across 
domains. Varying fusion pairs systematically may also help identify which dimensions of conceptual 
conflict most strongly drive hallucination. 
 
2. Model Architecture and Self-Regulation   
Among the general-purpose LLMs, GPT-4o and Gemini2.0Flash exhibited comparable levels of 
hallucination resistance, whereas DeepSeek produced significantly higher hallucination scores. This 
discrepancy may reflect architectural differences specific to DeepSeek. 
 
When comparing reasoning-oriented models to their general-purpose counterparts within each 
platform, the results were mixed. GPT-o3 showed no statistically significant difference from GPT-4o, 
suggesting that reasoning optimization does not always lead to improved hallucination control. 
Surprisingly, DeepSeek-R1—designed for enhanced reasoning—demonstrated significantly higher 
hallucination scores than DeepSeek (p ≈ 0.048). While this finding suggests that reasoning orientation 
alone does not guarantee hallucination suppression, we caution against overinterpreting the result due 
to the modest statistical significance. 
 
By contrast, Gemini2.5Pro’s consistently low hallucination scores may reflect a built-in 
mechanism of epistemic conservatism, possibly achieved through stricter thresholds for speculative 
generation. This raises the possibility that hallucination resistance is not merely a function of instruction


## Page 10


tuning, but may instead emerge from architecture-specific features governing response filtration and 
self-regulation. 
 
3. Prompt Engineering for Hallucination Diagnosis 
Our study underscores the utility of prompt design not only as an engineering tool, but also as a 
diagnostic probe into the model’s cognitive vulnerabilities. The HIP-HQP framework may serve as a 
scalable behavioral assay for detecting “semantic instability” zones across model families. Unlike 
conventional benchmarks that focus on accuracy or task completion, the HIP paradigm is designed to 
actively stress-test the semantic coherence of LLMs by embedding conceptually conflicting or 
structurally misleading prompts. The resulting variations in hallucination responses—both across and 
within models—reveal latent differences in how LLMs negotiate semantic fusion and manage epistemic 
uncertainty. 
 
This behavioral approach parallels diagnostic techniques in neuroscience and psychology, 
where external stimuli are used to infer internal mechanisms. Just as mismatch negativity reveals 
prediction errors in the human brain, HIP-induced hallucinations may uncover the limits of abstraction, 
grounding, or internal filtering in artificial systems. 
 
By formalizing such prompt-based stress tests, researchers and developers can move toward a 
“hallucination profile” for each model—quantifying how, when, and why breakdowns in coherence 
occur. This opens the door to more introspective, self-monitoring LLMs that can detect signs of semantic 
drift and regulate their own output accordingly. Ultimately, prompt engineering may evolve into a 
structured way to examine the limits of model reasoning—revealing not just what models say, but how 
they navigate conflicting or unstable knowledge. 
 
It is also worth noting that the hallucinations observed in this study differ qualitatively from 
conventional fact-based hallucinations, such as misattributing historical events or fabricating citations 
(Li et al., 2024). Rather than producing incorrect factual claims, LLMs responding to HIP prompts often 
generate speculative or metaphorical reasoning that appears coherent on the surface, but lacks any 
grounding in plausible domain relationships. This suggests that prompt-induced hallucination (PIH) may 
constitute a distinct subtype of hallucination—rooted not in factual inaccuracy per se, but in the model’s 
failure to evaluate the semantic legitimacy of blended concepts.  
 
4. Toward Internal-External Correlation Analysis 
In the present study, hallucination tendencies are inferred solely from external behavioral measurements 
using HQP evaluations. While the hallucination scores are consistent and robust across multiple trials, 
they remain indirect proxies for epistemic integrity. To more precisely understand the mechanisms


## Page 11


underlying prompt-induced hallucination (PIH), future work could incorporate internal signal analysis 
using locally hosted LLM instances. 
 
Specifically, by capturing logit distributions and attention patterns during generation, it would 
be possible to compute indicators such as semantic entropy, confidence variance, or token-level 
perplexity (Farquhar et al., 2024; Su et al., 2024). These internal signals could then be statistically 
correlated with hallucination scores obtained through external prompts like HIP and HQP, allowing for 
the identification of neural signatures or early warning markers of semantic instability. This dual 
approach—combining 
introspective 
(logit-based) 
and 
behavioral 
(prompt-response-based) 
diagnostics—may uncover deeper principles governing how LLMs handle ambiguous or conflicting 
information. Ultimately, such work could pave the way toward self-monitoring architectures that 
adaptively modulate their reasoning pathways when signs of hallucination begin to emerge. 
 
In parallel, operational improvements to the HIP/HQP framework could enhance its scalability 
and reliability. For instance, automating the HIP/HQP evaluation pipeline—potentially through model-
to-model assessments or structured self-evaluation—would enable large-scale hallucination profiling 
across LLM families. Additionally, validating HQP scores against human judgments will be essential to 
establish their external interpretability and trustworthiness. These refinements would support the 
development of HIP/HQP as a robust diagnostic framework for future LLM benchmarking and 
alignment research. As hallucination profiling becomes more integrated into LLM evaluation pipelines, 
HIP/HQP could evolve into a standard protocol for veriflying conceptual reliability in generative AI.
