Abstract
Natural medicines, particularly Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), are gaining global
recognition for their therapeutic potential in addressing human symptoms and diseases.
TCM, with its systematic theories and extensive practical experience, provides abundant
resources for healthcare. However, the effective application of TCM requires precise
syndrome diagnosis, determination of treatment principles, and prescription formulation,
which demand decades of clinical expertise. Despite advancements in TCM-based
decision systems, machine learning, and deep learning research, limitations in data and
single-objective constraints hinder their practical application. In recent years, large
language models (LLMs) have demonstrated potential in complex tasks, but lack
specialization in TCM and face significant challenges, such as too big model scale to
deploy and issues with hallucination. To address these challenges, we introduce Tianyi
with 7.6-billion-parameter LLM, a model scale proper and specifically designed for TCM,
pre-trained and fine-tuned on diverse TCM corpora, including classical texts, expert
treatises, clinical records, and knowledge graphs. Tianyi is designed to assimilate
interconnected and systematic TCM knowledge through a progressive learning manner.
Additionally, we establish TCMEval, a comprehensive evaluation benchmark, to assess
LLMs in TCM examinations, clinical tasks, domain-specific question-answering, and
real-world trials. The extensive evaluations demonstrate the significant potential of
Tianyi as an AI assistant in TCM clinical practice and research, bridging the gap between
TCM knowledge and practical application.

Introduction
The therapeutic potential of natural medicines, as a significant potential for the future
following chemical and biological medicine, in addressing human symptoms and
diseases is increasingly recognized by a growing number of researchers and healthcare
professionals from countries worldwide[1-4]. Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), as a
medicine with the systematic theory and abundant practical experience over natural
medicines (also called, traditional Chinese medicines), provides the infinite resources
and knowledge for humankind[5, 6]. Yet, the delivery of the proper usage of the natural
medicines with the patient conditions plays a critical role in clinical administration of
medicine and reveals the essential ability and skill of TCM doctors. It typically consists of
the TCM syndrome diagnosis, the decision of treatment principle, and the TCM
prescription generation, of which the decision of each step is made through a series of
reasoning process, for instance, the TCM syndrome of a patient is diagnosed based on
the comprehensive analysis patient’s medical history, current symptoms, signs, and
togue and pulse condition. Despite comprehensive training in TCM knowledge, TCM
doctors who achieve high cure rates through the use of traditional Chinese herbs (natural
medicines) often require decades of extensive clinical practice. It reflects the difficulty of
systematically and comprehensively mastering and applying TCM knowledge and
traditional Chinese medicines. For decades, a large amount of efforts have been
attempted to improve the diagnosis accuracy and treatment ability by TCM theory and
traditional Chinese medicines, including the TCM rule-based clinical decision-making
system[7], expert system[8], machine learning and deep learning models[9-14] that
explore the structured clinical records and unstructured TCM resources to develop a
TCM syndrome predictive or TCM prescription generative models. However, the way of
single-objective constraint modeling and the limited available TCM resources of the
previous works lead to the fact of strong biased and incomplete TCM knowledge
training and learning for these models. Most of TCM-related models fail if the clinical
settings of their application shift, remaining a substantial gap between desirable TCM
intelligent models and their actual application in diverse TCM clinical medical scenarios.

for the purpose of TCM specialization, the TCM ability mainly focus on the

scenarios of TCM no matter the diagnosis and treatment or the researches. It becomes to
the priority for TCM to incorporate comprehensive TCM knowledge and resources,
design the proper tasks with in-depth cooperation of TCM clinician, and align the TCM
clinical objectives. Besides, the current limited benchmarks and automated ML metrics in
the evaluations of LLMs in TCM emphasize the necessity for a more thorough evaluation
of LLM-based tools in real-world TCM clinical scenarios.
To address these challenges, we make several contributions. Firstly, we present the
Tianyi, a language model designed and trained with the elegant model-scale (7.6 billion
parameters) and TCM objectives, which is pre-trained and fine-tuned with the diverse
TCM corpora as well as the TCM domain labeled samples, including the TCM classics
(e.g., Treatise on Cold Damage, Essentials from the Golden Cabinet, Emergency Prescriptions for
Urgent Cases), the experience-based treatises by renowned TCM experts or national TCM
masters, the TCM question-answering books, the general textbooks of TCM(e.g., TCM
internal medicine, TCM surgery, TCM emergency medicine, diagnostics of TCM, science
of Chinese Materia Medica, formulary science of TCM), medical discussions on TCM
theory, TCM clinical doctor-patient consultations, TCM research articles, TCM clinical
electronic healthcare records (EHRs), TCM knowledge graph[12], etc. This diverse TCM
corpora allows the model to learn the TCM knowledge and clinical experience from a
comprehensive perspective and in an interconnected manner. Moreover, we design a
series of types of correlated target-specific instructions in-depth for fine-tuning according
to the essential tasks in TCM clinical. These special designed instructions not only
motivate Tianyi to assimilate and connect knowledge from different branches of TCM,
but learn the pros and cons of these knowledge in clinical practices, emerging the
unexpected but valuable abilities. Furthermore, we propose a progressive domain

knowledge and abilities. The TCMEval is established by the independent resources and

generation), TCM domain QA, human evaluation of TCM syndrome and prescription,
TCM clinical trial result prediction. The first three benchmarks evaluate the LLM’s basic
TCM knowledge and the ability for the TCM diagnosis and treatment based on TCM
clinical record. The last two types of trials include a retrospective and a prospective

In this work, we curated the three datasets to develop Tianyi in the stages of pre-training,
instruct fine-tuning, and preference alignment. For pre-training, we built TCMCorpurs, a
diverse and large-scale corpus of unstructured text. It includes the content of typical
TCM free text as much as possible, such as, the National planned textbooks for higher
education in TCM, publications of different TCM schools of thoughts, TCM research
articles. For instruct fine-tuning, we collect TCM clinical EHRs from cooperated TCM
hospital from Beijing Gulou Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, Beijing University
of Traditional Chinese Medicine, Beijing University of Traditional Chinese Medicine
Dongfang Hospital, and its affiliated Dongzhimen and Dongfang hospital with a
wide-range of disease types. All EHRs desensitize the patient personal information. We
also design and convert the TCM articles, TCM knowledge graph, and QA books into the
fine-tuned samples. For preference alignment, we randomly select the samples from
fine-tuning dataset and built the preference samples.
Pre-training dataset for developing TCM foundation LLM.
We curated the TCMCorpus, a large-scale TCM collection that contains the diverse TCM
knowledge and resources for the pre-training of TCM-LLM-Base, which consists of three
types of resources: TCM literatures and textbooks, TCM clinical records, and TCM
research articles, forming 3.4 billion TCM in-domain tokens.
TCM literatures and textbooks consists of two primary categories of typical book
text: the TCM textbooks, and collection of TCM experiences. The TCM textbooks are
specifically designed for undergraduate and graduate students at TCM institutions. They
cover all major fields of TCM, such as Chinese medicine, pharmacology, and
acupuncture, with the aim of providing a systematic and comprehensive knowledge
system of TCM, including the terminology, concepts and explanations, basic principle of
TCM diagnosis and treatment, natural medicine characteristics (e.g., natural properties,
efficacy, and taboo), and different TCM schools of thoughts. Typically written by experts
and professors in the field of TCM, these textbooks undergo strict review to ensure its
scientific validity and authority, which serve as crucial resources for TCM students and
are equally important for TCM LLM. The collection of TCM experiences contains the
publicly available wide-range of TCM classics, the books of medical discussions on TCM
knowledge and theory, and the experience books of renowned TCM experts or national
TCM masters, such as, the Treatise on Cold Damage (Shang Han Lun), Essentials from the
Golden Cabinet (Jin Kui Yao Lue), Emergency Prescriptions for Urgent Cases (Zhou Hou Bei Ji
Fang), Universal Relief Formulary (Pu Ji Ben Shi Fang), Systematic Differentiation of Warm
Diseases (Wen Bing Tiao Bian) for TCM classic, and the Clinical Experience Collection of Du
Huaitang and the Clinical Case Records of Pediatric Master Liu Bichen in Beijing for the latter
two categories of books. The TCM classics hold significant historical and academic value
in the field of TCM, which form the foundation of TCM theory and practice, exerting a
profound influence on the subsequent development of TCM. They are not only crucial
references for TCM education and research but also serve as important guidance in
clinical practice, reflecting the unique theoretical
system and
abundant
clinical
experience of TCM, and continue to offer valuable insights for the development of
modern TCM. The latter two categories of books typically refer to theoretical discussions
and explanations within TCM, focusing on the interpretation, transmission, and clinical

application of the TCM theoretical system. It mainly involves academic exploration of
fundamental TCM theories, diagnostic methods, treatment principles, and other related
aspects. It emphasizes a deep analysis of the core concepts of TCM from a theoretical
perspective, while also providing theoretical basis for clinical practice. Such works are
often the result of doctors who, after systematically studying TCM knowledge, combine
their own clinical experience to further summarize and refine existing TCM knowledge
or specialized knowledge in specific TCM fields, thus forming clinical treatment methods
with their own distinctive features. The collection of TCM experiences, which also covers
various disciplines of TCM as much as possible, contains 29312 books and forms the core
TCM knowledge corpus for pre-training.
TCM clinical records comprise the real-world TCM clinical records that collected in
our previous works[11-13] and the new collections provided by the cooperation TCM
hospitals, forming a corpus with 30K clinical records. In pre-training, we reform the them
with the special designed template to convert structural record into plain text, simulating
the process of TCM doctor browse the clinical records. TCM research articles are the
high-quality resources for LLM to improve its complicated reasoning ability with TCM
knowledge since they both contained the specific TCM knowledge explanation and the
related strict-reviewed TCM clinical records. We collect 2000 TCM articles in the topic of
case study in TCM, and extract the content of each article with data preprocessing
techniques such as optical character recognition and PDF text recognition. Same with the
TCM clinical records, we designed a template to form a plain text of the extracted content
of article for pre-training.
Fine-tuning and Preference alignment dataset for developing Tianyi.
For fine-tuning and aligning, we curated an instruction medical dataset by converting the
TCM clinical records, TCM research articles (2K), TCM examination and QA books
(100K), complete TCM consultation of doctor-patients (1K), and TCM knowledge graph
(20K) according to the special designed instructions (see details in supplemental
materials). For TCM clinical records, there are three types of clinical records: TCM
renowned expert or national master clinical records (10K), clinical specialty records (10K),
other records collected from books and articles (10K). The TCM renowned expert or
national master clinical records are collected from both the cooperation hospitals and
their publications, which contains the patient’s symptoms, tongue and pulse condition,
medical history and allergy history, physical signs, laboratory indicators, TCM syndrome
diagnosis result, treatment principle decision, TCM prescription, the etiological analysis
for syndrome diagnosis and prescription analysis. We form each instruction sample by
these features with instruction template shown accordingly. TCM research articles are
mainly clinical case studies with the applied TCM knowledge, the etiological analysis,

Model Overview
In this subsection, we present TCM-LLM-Base, a pre-trained LLM tailored with
large-scale TCM resources as the base for subsequent TCM medical application, and
Tianyi, a fine-tuned and aligned by the post-training process with the curated instruction
and preference datasets. Firstly, a large scale TCM domain dataset is curated and used to
pre-train the Baichuan2 (7.6 billion parameters) model for learning the TCM domain
knowledge and boosting the performance in TCM field. Then, the instruction and
alignment datasets are exploited sequential to motivate and rectify LLM’s capabilities
according to TCM tasks designed in the content and format instructions.
Progressive pre-training for developing TCM foundation LLM. In this work, we adapt
the Baichuan2 series LLM, the Llama-based decoder-only transformer large language
model, as the base model to perform the TCM domain pre-training process with the
TCMCorpus. The Baichuan2 series models are trained with extreme large multilingual
and general corpus which achieves terabytes of text. We choose Baichuan2 model as our
base model due to its consistent and readily scalability on the vocabulary, the
competitive status in open-sourced LLM in Chinese, emergent capabilities and extensive
knowledge base. For TCM domain pre-training, the Baichuan2 model is trained along

Evaluation Benchmarks
In this work, we evaluate our Tianyi model and all comparison LLMs by two major
categories in TCM: 1) Regular TCM Benchmark Tasks, 2) TCM Real-World Evaluation,
which is called TCMEval. The whole framework of our designed evaluation framework
is demonstrated in Fig. 5.
Regular TCM Benchmark Tasks. We follow the conventional method and metrics that
general LLMs (e.g., the Llama series, GPT series, Qwen series) are assessed by.
Differently from the commonly used benchmarks, we construct 3 types of TCM
benchmarks for evaluating the basic capability on TCM of LLM.

Traditional Chinese Medicine Multiple Choice Exams (TCM-MCE). It is comprised by
different level of TCM exams, i.e., the TCM undergraduate end-of-term exams, the TCM

extract all the multiple-choice questions and its answers from the available books and
materials and randomly shuffle and sample 20% records to form TCM-MCE. We assess
all comparison LLMs’ performance by their accuracy on this benchmark.
Traditional Chinese Medicine Clinical Records Tasks (TCM-CRT). This benchmark
contains various TCM clinical records that we collect during our researches on AI of
TCM. TCM-CRT includes 3 types of classic and important tasks: TCM syndrome
prediction, treatment principle (treatment strategy) decision, and the TCM prescription
generation. These 3 tasks are the fundamental and indispensable components in TCM
daily outpatient clinic from diagnosis to the treatment. For this benchmark, we assess all
comparisons independently on these 3 tasks, the accuracy for TCM syndrome prediction
and treatment principle, and the F1-scores for the TCM prescription generation.

Discussion
In this work, we present Tianyi, a scale-proper large language model for the learning and
application of natural medicines and the theoretical diagnosis-treatment knowledge
based on it (i.e., Traditional Chinese Medicine knowledge system), which is trained and
fine-tuned in a comprehensive manner to simulate the TCM clinician expertise across the
TCM general disciplines and various application scenarios. Evaluated on the TCMEval,
Tianyi emerges the superiority among a wide-range of TCM specialties and tasks.
Besides, the extensive comparison studies with the same model scale indicates its
potential of real-world clinical case assistance diagnosed and treated by TCM for the
junior doctors with limited clinical experience in diagnosis and treatment. Furthermore,
the real-world evaluations on TCM real clinical cases and clinical trial studies
demonstrate the capability on solving the unseen, real-world clinical tasks, indicating
Tianyi’s potential as a top-tier TCM assistant for clinician in their daily works.
The TCM knowledge system, as a holistic medical framework, is designed to
differentiate and diagnose patient symptoms and etiologies, and to treat them using a
variety of natural medicines. This system is inherently complex, challenging to master
thoroughly, and difficult to teach and pass down through generations. It transcends the
simplistic correlation between a patient's symptoms and the corresponding natural
remedies. Instead, it necessitates a comprehensive analysis of the underlying causes,
accurate diagnosis of TCM syndromes, formulation of treatment principles, and the
prescription of TCM formulas composed of natural medicines. This intricate process
requires the integration of knowledge from multiple TCM disciplines, including TCM
Diagnostics, TCM Internal Medicine or Surgery, Pharmacology of Chinese Medicines,
TCM Prescription Studies, and TCM Pharmacology, among others. The sophisticated
application of such diverse medical knowledge is prone to errors, often resulting in
misdiagnosis and inappropriate treatment. Previous AI-assisted systems for TCM have
included rule-based clinical decision-making systems, expert systems, and machine
learning or deep learning models trained on Electronic Health Records (EHRs). However,


## Page 22


these systems are constrained by their limited model scale and the biased, oversimplified
nature of their knowledge bases.
The recent researches of LLM demonstrate its advanced potential in understanding,
interpreting,
and
generating
the
text
effectively
across
diverse
conditions
and
requirements with the minimal specific fine-tuning[35]. However, these advancements
fall short when applied to complex and diverse clinical scenarios, particularly in the
context of TCM. Existing LLMs are not capable of efficiently modeling the vast of TCM
medical knowledge in a scalable manner. Furthermore, the special designed TCM LLMs
of similar scale to Tianyi are mainly focus on the chatbot of TCM consultation purpose,
trained and fine-tuned by using limited TCM resources and instruction samples. This
results
in
the
lack
of
systematic
TCM
knowledge,
factual
inaccuracies,
logic
inconsistencies, and an inability to handle TCM clinical tasks. For instance, the widely
cited Zhongjing-Llama, despite being trained and fine-tuned on a broad range of TCM
corpora and instruction samples for clinical consultation purposes, fail to properly
perform the diverse clinical tasks we designed in this work no matter of the basic ones
and the real-world evaluations[14]. To address these challenges, we introduce Tianyi,
which, to our knowledge, is the first TCM LLM that has been comprehensively trained
and fine-tuned on diverse TCM corpora. It is designed with advancements in model
scalability, outstanding clinical capabilities, and adaptability to various clinical scenarios.
In our viewpoint, for the development of medical LLM, we should not solely focus on
increasing the model scale and/or exploiting medical resources in a superficial manner.
Instead, the specialized and professionally designed strategy should be adopted, tailored
to the diverse medical conditions and considering cost-effectiveness for real-world
medical practice, where the sufficient computational resources are scarce to deploy the
LLMs with very large model scale. While Tianyi demonstrates the promising and
advanced TCM clinical performance with a model scale approximately 10% that of the
comparable LLM, alleviate these challenges and offering the insights for developing
medical LLM assistant for clinicians.
We conduct a comprehensive basic and clinical evaluations for the popular LLMs
and Tianyi within practical clinical scenarios, which includes the Regular TCM
Benchmark Tasks and the TCM Real-World Evaluation. In Regular TCM Benchmark
Tasks, we built three TCM benchmarks: TCM multiple choice exams, TCM domain
question answering, and TCM clinical records tasks. The first two benchmarks are the
factual knowledge evaluations that include general TCM disciplines which cover a wide
array of aspects, assessing the LLM’s proficiency in TCM knowledge. The third
benchmark contains a collection of historical TCM case records, of which each record
consists of three tasks: the TCM syndrome diagnosis, the treatment principle decision,
and the TCM prescription generation. The LLM need to provide three predictions at first.
Then, the average F1-score is calculated based on three tasks’ F1-scores. This task with
increasing difficulty valid the extent of solving the TCM essential tasks by applying the
TCM knowledge in the comprehensive manner. As shown in Table 1, results
demonstrate that our model is not only learnt professional TCM knowledge capacity to
complete the ordinary knowledge problems, but also capable of solving the syndrome
diagnosis, treatment principle decision, and TCM prescription at the same time,


## Page 23


demonstrating its potential of being the clinician’s assistance in the TCM clinical practice.
Additionally, we conduct the TCM clinical study on the prediction of clinical trial
researches, which are the scenarios that all LLMs including Tianyi are unseen before. It
evaluates the extent of LLMs’ ability emergence, and valid if the LLM can induce and
deduce the knowledge they learned, analyze the clinical condition, obey the task
instructions to provide the reliable predictions for these clinical trial researches. All
LLMs predict the results for each case of the clinical trial by converting the original
clinical indexes of symptoms, physical signs, medications into the designed prompt by
zero-shot learning. The results show the promising potential of Tianyi to assist the
researchers in the natural medicines efficacy for different patients and adverse reactions
prediction. Such ability demonstrates the probable of enable LLM prediction ability in
various clinical study tasks if the knowledge and experience of target clinical study can
be designed for LLM learning, enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of clinical
study.
Although our model has demonstrated the promising performance, several
challenges remain. First, as for the proper use of natural medicines, TCM doctors use
observation, listening and smelling, inquiry, and palpation (called, the four diagnostic
methods of TCM) to gather comprehensive information about a patient's condition. The
observation and palpation are the different modalities from text. How to integrated the
multi-modal data into the process of model’s information reasoning is still an open
theme. In the future, studying and integrating multi-modal data and approaches could
enable Tianyi to adopt a more comprehensive way to improve its performance on diverse
TCM
clinical
scenarios
from
the
raw
multi-modal
settings,
enhancing
the
human–computer collaboration. Furthermore, improving Tianyi's complex reasoning
capabilities could significantly enhance its performance and applicability in both clinical
practice and in-depth research. Currently, while Tianyi possesses reasoning abilities,
these were not explicitly designed and remain at a foundational level. There is
considerable potential for Tianyi to advance its deductive capabilities in areas such as the
clinical analysis of TCM syndromes and etiologies, the rationale behind treatment
principles, the selection of natural medicines, and the logical progression of illnesses and
corresponding adjustments in natural medicine prescriptions. Strengthening Tianyi's
reasoning abilities would not only improve its practical integration into clinical
workflows but also maximize its potential to benefit healthcare practices, particularly in
primary care settings.
