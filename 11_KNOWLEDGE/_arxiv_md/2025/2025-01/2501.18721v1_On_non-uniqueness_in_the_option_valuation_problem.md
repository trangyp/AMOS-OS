ON NON-UNIQUENESS IN THE OPTION VALUATION
PROBLEM
EKATERINA A. LADYKOVA, OLGA S. ROZANOVA
Abstract. It is known [1] that the value of a call option in the case of constant
elasticity processes (CEV) with the indicator α exceeding the critical α = 1 is
determined in a non-unique way. We show how, based on an already existing
mathematical theory concerning the correctness of boundary conditions for
degenerate parabolic equations on the semi-axis [0, ∞), this phenomenon can
be explained. Namely, for 1 < α ≤3
2 the non-uniqueness is due to the fact
that the initial data of the call option are outside the T¨acklind class, and for
α > 3
2 it is due to the absence boundary condition for x = ∞.
1. Introduction. The price of an option with the exercise function Φ(x) in
the case where the random process determining the dynamics of the risky asset is
a process with constant elasticity (CEV), see (13), is deﬁned as the solution of the
following boundary value problem with a terminal condition [2]:

The equation (1) is best known in the case of α = 1, when the random process
coincides with geometric Brownian motion, the equation in this case is named after
Black and Scholes, and the formula that gives the solution to the problem (1), (2) is
called the Black-Scholes formula [3]. It is one of the most widely used achievements
of ﬁnancial mathematics (e.g., [2]). The simplicity of this formula is due to the fact
that by replacing the independent variable x = ln y as a ﬁrst step, the equation (1)
is reduced to the heat equation. However, in the process of studying real markets it
became clear that it is necessary to consider a wider class of random processes than
geometric Brownian motion, which is associated with the interest in considering
CEV processes with exponents diﬀerent from one. Formulas for the option price
have long been known. They were derived for all cases of the α exponent by reducing
to the Feller equation, for which the fundamental solution is known [4]. For α < 1
this was done in [5], and for α > 1 — in [6]). The formulas are quite cumbersome
and contain the so-called χ2-distribution, they are included in classical textbooks,
for example [2]. However, 25 years after the publication of the option price formula

7. Conclusion. From the above analysis it follows that the non-uniqueness of
the solution in the problem (5), (3) for α > 1 occurs for the following reasons.
1. For 1 < α ≤3
2 for x = +∞no condition should be imposed, but the solution
is unique only in the class of functions that have sublinear growth at inﬁnity, where
the initial data (3) do not fall.
2. For α > 3
2 an additional condition on the behavior of the solution for x = +∞
is required, otherwise the problem is underdetermined.
Note that in [1] methods for isolating a unique solution are proposed, for example,
through the parity of the “call” and “put” options.
Taking into account the results described above, we see that uniqueness can be
achieved, for example, by requiring that the solution be found using the formula
(9). Note that this automatically imposes the requirement that the solution be
bounded for α > 3
2.

analyze them, and oﬀer a new perspective on the problem.
