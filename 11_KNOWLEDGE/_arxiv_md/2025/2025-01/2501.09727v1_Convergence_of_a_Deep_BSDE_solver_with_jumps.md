CONVERGENCE OF A DEEP BSDE SOLVER WITH JUMPS
ALESSANDRO GNOATTO, KATHARINA OBERPRILLER, AND ATHENA PICARELLI
Abstract. We study the error arising in the numerical approximation of FBSDEs and related
PIDEs by means of a deep learning-based method. Our results focus on decoupled FBSDEs with
jumps and extend the seminal work of [19] analyzing the numerical error of the deep BSDE solver
proposed in [16]. We provide a priori and a posteriori error estimates for the ﬁnite and inﬁnite
activity case.

The seminal work in this stream of literature is [16] coupled with the convergence analysis of
[19]. In these works, formulated in a setting without jumps, the BSDE is discretized forward in
time using an Euler discretization and then a family of ANNs, one ANN for each time step, is
used to parametrize the control process of the BSDE. The parameters of the ANN are chosen as
to minimize the squared expected distance between the terminal condition of the FBSDE and the
Euler discretization of the BSDE at terminal time. Alternatively, [21] base their approach on a
backward procedure based on the dynamic programming principle. We also mention [6] based on
the idea of operator splitting and [28]. Extensions of the approaches above to the case with jumps
have been proposed. The work [18] generalized [6], but considers only jumps of ﬁnite activity. A
ﬁrst generalization of [21] is studied in [10], whose result have been recently reﬁned in [22]. Of
particular relevance for the present paper is [2] which extends [16] to the case of jumps. Further
algorithms are studied and compared in [1].
Concerning the convergence analysis of such algorithms, our main reference is the aforementioned
contribution [19] that considers weakly coupled FBSDEs without jumps and provides the conver-
gence analysis for the approach of [16]. A ﬁrst extension involving jumps is proposed in [30]: weakly
coupled FBSDEs are treated, only jumps of ﬁnite activity are considered and the analysis is limited
to a posteriori estimates. Our analysis of a posteriori estimates (which excludes weak coupling)
is in line with that of [30] but we also consider inﬁnite activity jumps and, more importantly, we
obtain a priori estimates which are missing in [30]. Further contribution on the error analysis of
deep learning based solvers for FBSDEs/PIDEs include [10] and [22] for the deep backward dynamic
programming principle of [21] and [17] which generalized [6].
This paper is organized as follows: in Section 2 we present the problem and illustrate the gen-
eralization of the algorithm of [16] that constitutes our object of analysis. In Section 3 we provide
a self contained derivation of a posteriori estimates that is in line with the results of [30] but is
extended to consider the inﬁnite activity case in Section 3.2. Section 4 provides a priori estimates
both for the ﬁnite and inﬁnite activity case.

2.1. The extension of the deep BSDE solver in [16] to the case with jumps. Using the
stochastic optimal control problem in (2.15) the idea for the algorithm is to discretize the FBSDE
and approximate at each time step the control processes by families of artiﬁcial neural networks.
The goal is to minimize the loss function in (2.15) by ﬁtting the parameters of the neural networks.
While this approach is frequently used in the case without jumps, see e.g. [16], the diﬃculty with
jumps is to develop an algorithm which is also able to deal with a forward process X with inﬁnitely
many jumps of ﬁnite or inﬁnite variation. To overcome this problem the following procedure is used.
In a ﬁrst step an algorithm for the ﬁnite activity case is developed. In a second step the forward
process X is approximated by a jump diﬀusion Xε with ﬁnitely many jumps, which allows to make
use of the already existing algorithm for the ﬁnite activity case. The approximation of the forward
process relies here on results in [4], [11] and [23].
Before presenting the algorithms for the ﬁnite and inﬁnite activity case in details, we introduce the
architecture of the underlying artiﬁcial neural networks we employ.
We use feedforward ANNs with L + 1 ∈N\{1, 2} layers, with each layer consisting of vℓnodes or
neurons, for ℓ= 0, . . . , L. The 0-th layer is called the input layer, while the L-th layer represents
the output layer.
The remaining L −1 layers are termed hidden layers.
For simplicity, we set
vℓ= v, ℓ= 1, . . . , L −1. The dimension of the input layer is equal to d, which equals the dimension
of the forward process X. A feedforward neural network is a function deﬁned via the composition
