Doppler-free Selective Reﬂection spectroscopy of the 6s 2S1/2 - 7p 2P3/2 transition of
Cesium using a nanofabricated vapor cell
Armen Sargsyan,1 Rodolphe Momier,2, ∗and David Sarkisyan1
1Institute for Physical Research, National Academy of Sciences of Armenia, Ashtarak-2, 0204 Armenia
2QUANTUM, Institut f¨ur Physik, Johannes Gutenberg Universit¨at Mainz, Staudingerweg 7, 55128 Mainz, Germany
(Dated: January 22, 2025)
Selective Reﬂection (SR) spectroscopy of the 6s 2S1/2 →7p 2P3/2 electric dipole transition of
Cesium (λ = 456 nm) is performed for the ﬁrst time using a nanometric-thin cell (L = 50−1500 nm).
We succesfully form narrow resonances corresponding to the Fg = 3, 4 →Fe = 2, 3, 4, 5 hyperﬁne
transitions. All transitions are well spectrally resolved and the geometry of the cell allows us to
obtain a strong 30 times narrowing of the Doppler width with a single beam pass.
For lower
thicknesses (L < 400 nm), we observe a red shift of the SR lines which we attribute to atom-surface
interactions, and provide estimates of the C3 interaction coeﬃcient. The SR signal reaches several
percent of the incident radiation up to a saturation intensity of 100 mW/cm2. SR is a convenient
tool for laser spectroscopy of the hyperﬁne structure of atoms as well as for the study of Zeeman
transitions in magnetic ﬁelds. Experimental measurements are in good agreement with theoretical
calculations.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Numerous publications focus on the study of SR of
laser radiation from the boundary between an alkali
metal vapor and dielectric windows in centimeter-long
spectroscopic cells [1–4]. This interest is mainly due to
the sub-Doppler structure of the SR signal, making the
latter highly advantageous for detailed spectral analysis
[4]. The SR theory for a centimeter-long cell with atomic
vapor is given in [5]. In work [6], the SR process was
investigated at high vapor pressures. The results of [7]
show that Rydberg atoms can be used for practical ap-
plications of the SR process.
The use of thin spectroscopic cells (thickness lying in
the 30 −1000 nm range) turns out to be very useful
for SR spectroscopy and its applications [8–10]. In this
case, Doppler broadening is mostly cancelled, and nar-
row (∼50 MHz) SR resonances can be formed, much
narrower than what would be obtained in long cells due
to the Doppler width.
SR has for example been proven useful to perform spec-
troscopy of Zeeman transitions in magnetic ﬁelds. In [11],
SR spectroscopy of Cs D1 line (6s 2S1/2 →6p 2P1/2) was
performed in the 700 – 900 nm range, and a method al-
lowing to measure magnetic ﬁelds in the range 0.1 – 10 kG
was presented. Thanks to the small dimensions of the
cell, micrometric spatial resolution can be achieved. Per-
manent magnets are known to create highly non-uniform
magnetic ﬁelds with a gradient of ∼100 G/mm, which
strongly complicate the use of centimeter-long cells. Us-
ing a nanocell (NC) allows to mitigate the gradient by
placing the cell much closer to the magnet. Moreover,
in a ∼300 nm thick-vapor, the magnetic ﬁeld can be
considered practically uniform.

6s 2S1/2 →7p 2P1/2 transition recently in [17].
However, SR spectroscopy of the 6s 2S1/2 →7p 2P3/2
transition remains quite a challenge for various reasons:
• As it is well knows, the absorption obeys α = σNL,
where N is the vapor density and σ the resonant
absorption cross-section, proportional to the oscil-
lator strength f [18]. For Cs, f is almost two or-
ders of magnitude smaller here than for the transi-
tion to 6p 2P3/2. It therefore becomes necessary to
strongly increase the vapor density to obtain even
1% absorption at L = 200 −300 nm, requiring a
vapor temperature of around 200 ◦C.
• The Doppler width is three times bigger than in the
IR region for the D1 and D2 lines.
• The hyperﬁne splittings between the upper levels
of 7p 2P3/2 are around three times smaller than for
the 6p 2P3/2 state.

• Due to a bigger vapor density and smaller wave-
length, van der Waals interaction is much stronger
at 456 nm than for the D1 and D2 lines (852 nm),
leading to an undesirable broadening and red shift

The use of the SR process with a NC whose thick-

6s 2S1/2 −7p2P3/2 transition for the ﬁrst time. More-

the optical pumping eﬀect [18] is signiﬁcantly reduced
compared to its manifestation in cm-long cells. This re-
duction enables the use of powerful incident radiation to
achieve a strong SR signal, which is particularly advan-
tageous for practical applications [10].

As discussed in the experimental section,
the red shift of the transitions with respect to the theo-

the calculations. The line proﬁles are similar to what is
expected for such thin cells [28]. Speciﬁc eﬀects arising

the model does not accurately reﬂect the inﬂuence of the
incident laser power on line broadening, which is why we
do not provide additional theoretical spectra. The model
also does not reﬂect the inﬂuence of temperature broad-
ening. Here, the atomic density is a free parameter which
only acts on the transition amplitude at the moment.
IV.
CONCLUSION
Doppler-free high-resolution SR spectroscopy of the
6s 2S1/2 →7p 2P3/2 transition of Cesium was performed


## Page 6


6
for the ﬁrst time using a nanocell whose thickness lies
in the 150 - 500 nm range. We succesfully formed nar-
row (less than 30 MHz) resonances corresponding to the
3, 4 →2′, 3′, 4′, 5′ hyperﬁne transitions with a single
beam pass, with a signiﬁcantly better resolution than
what would be obtained in a usual cell (30 times nar-
rower than the one-photon Doppler width).
This is a
direct result of the geometry of the cell: if we consider
a cell of thickness roughly 400 nm, atoms ﬂying perpen-
dicularly to the laser beam propagation direction (beam
diameter ∼1 mm) exhibit an interaction time three or-
ders of magnitude bigger than atoms ﬂying in the laser
beam propagation direction. For these atoms, k · v = 0
and Doppler broadening is almost completely cancelled
out. The other atoms ﬂying in the laser propagation di-
rection have a 1/2 probability of ending up in the ground
state after colliding with a window, thereby dramatically
reducing their interaction time and participation to the
SR and absorption signals [33].
With the use of a second laser, Λ-systems can be
formed and EIT resonances were observed in SR and
transmission spectra of Rb D2 line [34, 35]. We expect
to obtain the same results with the 6s 2S1/2 →7p 2P3/2
transition. It is important to note that since SR makes
up about 5% of the incident radiation, it could be used
to register weak transitions such as the 3 →5′ and
4 →2′ Zeeman magnetically-induced transitions (∆F =
±2) [36]. We also expect that SR spectroscopy of the
6s 2S1/2 →8p 2P3/2 (389 nm) can also be performed.
The recent development of a glass NC [37], easier to man-
ufacture than sapphire-based cells such as one used in
this work, could make this kind of study possible for a
wider range of researchers.
