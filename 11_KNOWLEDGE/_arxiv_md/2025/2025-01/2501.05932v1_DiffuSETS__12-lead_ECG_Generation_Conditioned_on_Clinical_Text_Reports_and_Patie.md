SUMMARY
13
Heart disease remains a significant threat to human health. As a non-invasive diagnostic tool,
14
the electrocardiogram (ECG) is one of the most widely used methods for cardiac screening.
15
However, the scarcity of high-quality ECG data, driven by privacy concerns and limited medical
16
resources, creates a pressing need for effective ECG signal generation. Existing approaches for
17
generating ECG signals typically rely on small training datasets, lack comprehensive evaluation
18
frameworks, and overlook potential applications beyond data augmentation. To address these
19
challenges, we propose DiffuSETS, a novel framework capable of generating ECG signals with
20
high semantic alignment and fidelity. DiffuSETS accepts various modalities of clinical text reports
21
and patient-specific information as inputs, enabling the creation of clinically meaningful ECG
22
signals. Additionally, to address the lack of standardized evaluation in ECG generation, we
23
introduce a comprehensive benchmarking methodology to assess the effectiveness of generative
24
models in this domain. Our model achieve excellent results in tests, proving its superiority in
25
the task of ECG generation. Furthermore, we showcase its potential to mitigate data scarcity
26
while exploring novel applications in cardiology education and medical knowledge discovery,
27
highlighting the broader impact of our work.

Given these challenges, a critical upstream task is the generation of synthetic ECG signals8,10.
42
In the field of ECG signal generation, the main research goal is to generate ECG signal samples
43
with high fidelity and rich diversity. Many studies have adopted the Generative Adversarial Network
44
(GAN) architecture to generate ECG signals11, and others have introduced Ordinary Differential
45
Equation (ODE) systems representing cardiac dynamics to create ECG samples8. Recently,
46
some new studies have incorporated patient-specific cardiac disease information into the ECG
47
generation process to improve the generated outcomes9,12, and some have included the content
48
of patients’ clinical text reports during generation13. However, current research in this field still has
49
certain deficiencies: (1) Dataset limitations. Most of the previous ECG datasets contain a limited
50
variety of samples and have low textual richness in clinical text reports. Therefore, generative
51
models trained on such datasets have limited performance and are unable to generate extremely
52
rare ECG signals that are absent from the training data. Although extremely rare ECG samples
53
remain scarce, the newly released MIMIC-IV-ECG14 and MIMIC-IV-Clinical15 provide a large
54
ECG dataset with ample related information, paving the way for generation models with more
55
possibilities. (2) Difficulty in unifying features across different modalities. Clinical text reports
56
and patient-specific information encompass data from various modalities, each with distinct
57
distributions, making it challenging to integrate all this information into a model. Consequently,
58
many ECG generation models rely solely on high-level label information, which limits the diversity
59
of the generated outputs. (3) Lack of benchmarking methods. In the field of ECG generation,
60
there is still a lack of comprehensive benchmarking methods, making it difficult to assess the
61
relative merits of models.
62
Equally significant is the growing interest in expanding the applications of generative models
63
beyond merely providing data for other AI models. The capabilities of generative methods, such as
64
Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)16, have been demonstrated, and their potential
65
applications in scientific fields like neuroscience are beginning to be explored17. However, the full
66
potential of generative models in cardiology remains largely untapped. This raises a compelling
67
question: what additional contributions can generative models make to the field of cardiology?
68
Answering this question presents a meaningful challenge that warrants exploration.

72
DiffuSETS utilizes the MIMIC-IV-ECG dataset as the training dataset, which features a wide variety
73

74

75

RESULTS
101
DifuSETS overview and Data curation
102
In this paper, we propose DiffuSETS, a diffusion model capable of generating 12-lead ECGs from
103
clinical text reports and patient-specific information. The architecture of DiffuSETS is illustrated
104
in the Figure 1B, involving three modalities: signal space, latent space, and conditional informa-
105
tion space (clinical text reports and patient-specific information). The variational autoencoder
106
facilitates the conversion of vectors between signal space and latent space, while a prompted
107
large language model, serving as the semantic embedding model, extracts embedding vec-
108
tors from clinical text reports to capture semantic information, which are then merged with a
109
embedding vector generated from patient-specific information. Finally, the denoising diffusion
110
process and noise prediction model collaboratively achieve the cross-modal generation from text
111
to electrocardiographic latent variables.
112
We use the MIMIC-IV-ECG dataset14 for training DiffuSETS, which contains 794, 372 12-lead
113
ECG signal records after preprocessing. From this dataset, we extract heart rate features and
114
corresponding clinical text reports. Then, using patient ID as key, we query the MIMIC-IV-
115
CLINICAL dataset patient table to retrieve patient-specific information, such as sex and age.
116
Additionally, we utilize PTB-XL dataset, which contains 299, 712 samples, for external validation
117
and comparison with other baseline models.
118
DiffuSETS can generate high fidelity ECG
119
Our conprehensive evaluation involves experiments and analysis at the signal level, feature level,
120
and diagnostic level. The overall design is shown in Figure 1C. In this framework, we aim to ask
121
three key questions regarding ECG signals generated from clinical text reports and patient-specific
122
information:
123
1. (Signal level) Does the generated ECG signal resemble real ECG signals?
124
2. (Feature level) Are the input features accurately reflected in the generated signals?
125
3. (Diagnostic level) How closely does the generated ECG align with the corresponding clinical
126
text report?
127
These questions guide our evaluation of the effectiveness and relevance of the generated
128
ECG signals in capturing both the clinical context and the underlying physiological characteristics.
129
DiffuSETS acheives remarkable scores in this three level comprehensive evaluation, results are
130
listed in Table 1.

At the signal level, we focus on the fidelity and stability of the generated signals by evaluating
132
various metrics that assess both the distribution similarity and the structural resemblance between
133
real and generated ECG signals. First, we calculate the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) score
134
to measure the distribution similarity between the two sets of signals. Since the FID score
135
only assesses data distribution through macro scope of mean and variance, we also evaluate
136
waveform-level similarity to ensure the quality of the generated ECG signals. For phase deviations
137
may occur between the generated and real signals, traditional Mean Absolute Error (MAE) is
138
not a reliable metric for assessing similarity. Instead, we adopt the brilliantly proposed method18,
139
which calculates precision and recall by counting the ratio of generated ECG signal representation
140
points that fall within the manifold constructed by the real ECG signal representation points, and
141
vice versa (illustrative cases are depicted in Figure 2C). Finally the F1 score is derived to assess
142
the overall performance of accuracy and diversity. We use a pretrained Net1D19 as the encoder
143
to obtain ECG representations in signal level assessment. The results shows that the ECG
144
signals generated by DiffuSETS resemble the real ECG signals in both signal distribution and
145
representation manifold, which proves the fidelity and stability of generated signals.

DiffuSETS has the potential to pass the Turing test
210
We conduct two kinds of cardiologist evaluation test. In the fidelity evaluation, we extract feature
211
information from 50 records in the MIMIC-IV-ECG dataset, using as input to generate 50 ECG
212
signals, and then randomly select another 50 ECG signals from the MIMIC-IV-ECG dataset. We
213
provide these generated data alongside the real data to cardiologists for Turing test assessment.
214
The cardiologists are tasked with determining whether the provided ECGs are generated by a
215
machine. The judgements are recorded in Figure 3A. In the test for semantic alignment, we
216
provide 100 generated ECGs (using different conditions, especially clinical text reports, recorded
217
in MIMIC-IV-ECG). Experts are asked to determine whether our generated results matched all
218
the descriptions in the clinical text reports.
219
In Turing test, despite different cardiologists may have different criteria for distinguishing
220
real and generated ECG, which results in quantity gap of total number of ECG judged to be
221
model-generated, judgments made by the same cardiologist show non-discrimination for real
222
and generated ECG, i.e. the TP(Generated ECG judged as generated) value is close to FP(Real
223
ECG misjudged as generated) value within the same row. This consistency indicates that even
224
experienced cardiologists struggle to differentiate between real and generated ECG signals.
225
Moreover, the significant proportion of false negatives (generated ECG misjudged as real) further
226
underscores the high fidelity of the ECG signals produced by the DiffuSETS model, making them
227
virtually indistinguishable from real ones. For alignment test, according to expert evaluation, the
228
accuracy of semantic alignment in the electrocardiogram generated by DiffuSETS has reached
229
76%. Actually it is a relatively hard task since all reports need to check the consistency before the
230
ECG data can be judged as alignment, there are still more than three-quarter generated ECGs
231
are considered matching to input clinical text reports, which indicates that DiffuSETS model do
232
grasp the diagnostic information between signal and text modalities when expanding the diversity
233
of generated ECG signals.

261

The input clinical text report for this electrocardiogram (Figure 4A) is "1. Atrial fibrillation with
267
PVC(s) or aberrant ventricular conduction. 2. Left anterior fascicular block. 3. Anteroseptal infarct
268
- age undetermined. 4. Left ventricular hypertrophy. 5. Lateral ST-T changes may be due to
269
hypertrophy and/or ischemia. 6. Abnormal ECG.", with a heart rate of 79 beats per minute and
270
the male patient is 96 years old. The patient suffers from multiple concurrent severe arrhythmias,
271
making it difficult to generate. Overall, it is an abnormal ECG. The generated ECG has a heart
272
rate of approximately 80 beats per minute, which is consistent with the input information. The
273
patient’s RR intervals are irregular, and the P waves are absent, which is characteristic of atrial
274
fibrillation. The QRS complex of the 9th heartbeat is significantly different from the others, being
275
wide and malformed, indicating the occurrence of a ventricular premature beat. Moreover, the
276
overall width of the QRS complex is wider, suggesting that the patient’s ventricular conduction is
277
abnormal, specifically diagnosed as left anterior fascicular block. In addition, the patient exhibits
278
changes in the ST segment and T wave, indicating that the patient may have severe symptoms of
279
myocardial ischemia or infarction. Finally, the voltage of the QRS complex is high, suggesting the
280
possibility of left ventricular hypertrophy. This case sufficiently proves that our model is competent
281
to generate ECG under a complex cardiac condition.
282
The other significant limitation in current cardiology education is the scarcity of ECG examples
283
for extremely rare heart diseases, such as Brugada syndrome23 and Long QT syndrome24, both
284
of which are often associated with a high risk of sudden cardiac death. Diagnosing these rare
285
conditions typically relies on memorizing a few waveform patterns outlined in textbooks. Thus, it
286
would be highly beneficial for cardiologists to have access to an ample supply of ECG samples
287
for specific cases. However, the rarity of these diseases poses a challenge for conventional
288
conditional generative learning methods, which require a large number of labeled training samples
289
to model the relationship between conditions and data distributions effectively. To address this
290
issue, we propose a novel framework leveraging DiffuSETS in conjunction with large language
291
models, enabling the generation of ECG signals for rare cardiac conditions (Figure 4B).
292
The proposed framework operates in two stages. First, a large language model is queried to
293

The electrocardiogram (ECG) is an essential tool in cardiological diagnosis, serving as a foun-

359

361

362

363

Motivated by aforementioned concerns, we present DiffuSETS, a novel electrocardiogram

372

Related Works
417
There are many studies currently attempting to address the generation of electrocardiogram
418
(ECG) signals, but these methods have several limitations. Firstly, many models can only generate
419
short-term time series28–31, enabling them to produce only the content of a single heartbeat,
420
rather than long-term ECG recordings. Secondly, they are often trained on small datasets with a
421
limited number of patients32, or they use only a limited set of conditional labels8,33,34. In addition,
422
many of these methods require ECG segmentation as a pre-training step, rather than directly
423
processing noises8,30,31,33. Moreover, many of these methods are capable of generating and
424
classifying for specific patients only34, and lack comprehensive training data and samples aimed
425
at the general population22.
426
In recent studies, some researchers have attempted to apply diffusion models to the generation
427
of ECGs11, treating ECGs as images rather than time series, and their methods were limited to
428
the unconditional generation of single-lead ECGs. Moreover, from a quantitative performance
429
evaluation perspective, these methods have not surpassed those based on GANs for generating
430
ECGs. ME-GAN9 introduces a disease-aware generative adversarial network for multi-view ECG
431
synthesis, focusing on how to appropriately inject cardiac disease information into the generation
432
process and maintain the correct sequence between views. However, their approach does
433
not consider text input, and therefore cannot incorporate information from clinical text reports.
434
Auto-TTE13 proposed a conditional generative model that can produce ECGs from clinical text
435
reports, but they also segmented the ECGs as a preprocessing step. SSSD-ECG12 introduced
436
a conditional generative model of ECGs with a structured state space, encoding labels for 71
437
diseases and incorporating them into the model training as conditions, but it cannot accept clinical
438
text reports in the form of natural language text, thus lacking some of the rich semantic information
439
inherent in disease diagnosis. At the same time, due to the lack of a unified performance
440
evaluation setup, it is often challenging to quickly assess the relative merits of these methods.
