Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) primarily trained on English
texts, often face biases and inaccuracies in Chinese contexts.
Their limitations are pronounced in fields like Traditional
Chinese Medicine (TCM), where cultural and clinical sub-
tleties are vital, further hindered by a lack of domain-specific
data, such as rheumatoid arthritis (RA). To address these is-
sues, this paper introduces Hengqin-RA-v1, the first large lan-
guage model specifically tailored for TCM with a focus on di-
agnosing and treating RA. We also present HQ-GCM-RA-C1,
a comprehensive RA-specific dataset curated from ancient
Chinese medical literature, classical texts, and modern clini-
cal studies. This dataset empowers Hengqin-RA-v1 to deliver
accurate and culturally informed responses, effectively bridg-
ing the gaps left by general-purpose models. Extensive exper-
iments demonstrate that Hengqin-RA-v1 outperforms state-
of-the-art models, even surpassing the diagnostic accuracy of
TCM practitioners in certain cases.
Introduction
Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs),
such as PaLM (Chowdhery et al. 2023), GPT (Achiam et al.
2023), and ChatGLM (Du et al. 2022), have significantly
improved state-of-the-art performance across various NLP
tasks, including text generation(Lin et al. 2023), summa-
rization(Huang et al. 2024a), and question answering (Zhou
et al. 2023). While LLMs excel at addressing common, ev-
eryday questions, they often fall short in highly specialized
fields like medicine, where their responses lack the accu-
racy, reliability, interpretability, and contextual understand-
ing necessary to match those of physicians (Zou and He
2023; Nazi and Peng 2024). Also, medical imaging and its
interaction with LLM need human evaluation (Achiam et al.
2023; Saab et al. 2024). However, such areas are lacked.
LLMs face significant challenges due to inherent biases
in training data, particularly in the context of Chinese cor-
pora (Achiam et al. 2023). For instance, GPT-3 contains less
than 1% Chinese content, and while GPT-4 improves on this
slightly, the shortage of high-quality Chinese data persists.

This shortage stems from political biases in data availabil-
ity within China and the substantial resources required for
collecting and cleaning Chinese datasets. Existing Chinese
LLMs, such as ChatGLM-6B (GLM et al. 2024), Huatuo
(Wang et al. 2023), ChatYuan (Xuanwei Zhang and Zhao
2022), Linly (Li et al. 2024), and Pangu-α (Zeng et al. 2021),
can handle general tasks but face challenges in specialized
domains like rheumatoid arthritis (RA). Especially tradi-
tional Chinese medicine (TCM) for RA, LLMs encounter
two primary challenges: (1) the lack of precise medical clas-
sifications and a scarcity of Chinese medical corpora; (2) no
LLMs that specifically dealing with the field of RA, espe-
cially based on Chinese corpora and TCM data.
To address these issues, we introduce Hengqin-RA-v1, the
first LLM specifically tailored to the TCM subfield of RA.
Alongside this model, we present HQ-GCM-RA-C1, the first
Chinese corpus dataset focused on RA in TCM. This dataset
spans ancient Chinese texts, modern Chinese medical litera-
ture, and contemporary insights, making it a comprehensive
resource for TCM-based rheumatoid studies.
All in all, we make the following contributions:
• We introduce the first TCM LLM, Hengqin-RA-v1,
specifically designed for rheumatoid arthritis diagnosis
and treatment. This is the first Chinese LLM for rheuma-
toid arthritis and the first rooted in TCM principles.
• We propose a Chinese corpus dataset, HQ-GCM-RA-C1,
based on TCM for rheumatoid arthritis. Organized in
prompt form, it includes ancient Chinese medical texts
and exam questions, making it useful for training LLMs
and other NLP models.
• Hengqin-RA-v1 outperforms mainstream LLMs in gen-
erating text on rheumatoid diagnosis and treatment. In
some aspects, it is even more convincing than human ex-
perts.

Pre-trained Models in Medical Domain
Pre-training LLMs for medical applications uses extensive
structured and unstructured medical texts, such as clinical
notes and PubMedBERT (Gu et al. 2021), to adapt general
objectives like masked language modeling and next token
prediction for medical needs (Zhou et al. 2023). Fine-tuning
strategies include Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) (Singhal
et al. 2023) with existing corpora, Instruction Fine-Tuning
(IFT) (Han et al. 2023) with human-curated datasets, and
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) (Toma et al. 2023).
Prompting methods, particularly In-Context Learning (ICL),
streamline task execution through steps like task under-
standing and knowledge reasoning. Chinese medical LLMs
(Wang et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024; Xuanwei Zhang and
Zhao 2022; Li et al. 2024) exemplify these advancements in
Chinese medical LLMs.
So does Hengqin-RA-v1. We use these techniques to re-
design the underlying parameters to align with the Chinese
corpus and its encoding characteristics, which specializes in
diagnosis and treatment of RA. During result generation, ex-
pert feedback is incorporated to continuously refine the an-
swers, enabling Hengqin-RA-v1 to approach greater accu-
racy in subsequent training and testing.
Hengqin-RA-v1
Hengqin-RA-v1 is an advanced iteration of Huatuo2 (Zhang
et al. 2023), developed on LLaMA-7B (Touvron et al. 2023).
It has been fine-tuned using the Chinese Medical Knowledge
Graph (CMeKG)1 and medical instruction data generated by
GPT-3.5 (Achiam et al. 2023), with the goal of improving
question-answering capabilities in the medical domain.
There are three challenges currently encountered in the
vertical field of dedicated TCM diagnosis and treatment: (1)
current data preprocessing technologies cannot extract diag-
nostic and treatment information from raw medical records;
(2) these technologies also overlooks the context and in-
fluence of papers and literature. (3) Existing TCM LLMs
(Zhang et al. 2023; Xuanwei Zhang and Zhao 2022; Li et al.
2024; GLM et al. 2024) overlook the nuances of various
fine-tuning techniques. Relying solely on LoRA (Hu et al.
2021) cannot guarantee retention of the base model’s learned
knowledge. To overcome these challenges, a progressive
training strategy was adopted to improve Hengqin-RA-v1,
as illustrated in Fig. 1.

Our approach to data input differs from other large TCM
models (Zhang et al. 2023; Xuanwei Zhang and Zhao 2022;
GLM et al. 2024). Instead of directly feeding raw data into
the model, we organize the data according to the character-
istics of TCM diagnosis and treatment, creating datasets in
multiple text modes, such as thought chains, multi-text for-
mats, and dialogue sets.
During model training, we prioritize both the correctness
of the knowledge learned and the scale of the training data,
adhering to the scaling law. This approach addresses the lim-
itations of traditional models, which often emphasize data
quantity over quality.
Experimental Results
As shown in 5, the TCM recommendations generated by
Hengqin-RA-v1 demonstrate an ability to summarize patient
symptoms and provide preliminary diagnoses, effectively in-
tegrating modern medical data with TCM theory. For exam-
ple, the model identifies damp-heat symptoms like a ”greasy
yellow and dark” complexion and thick, greasy tongue coat-
ing, linking TCM concepts such as ”heat syndrome” and
”damp-heat” with laboratory markers like CRP and ALT lev-
els. However, it lacks coverage of auscultation and palpa-
tion, omitting key details like odor characteristics and pulse
information. While inspection and questioning provide basic
insights, they fail to capture personalized nuances.
The recommended Chinese medicines, such as ”Bupleu-
rum” and ”Coptis chinensis,” align with damp-heat syn-
drome differentiation but lack clear explanations for dosage
adjustments or compatibility logic, resulting in overly broad
and generalized recommendations. Additionally, the lan-
guage, while fluent, often lacks fine-grained TCM-specific
terminology, reducing its precision.

four diagnostic methods, especially auscultation and palpa-
tion, enhancing syndrome differentiation, and personalizing
treatment recommendations. Better integration of modern
medical data and optimization of language expression to re-
duce generalizations and emphasize professionalism are also
essential. While the model demonstrates a logical frame-

Patient Case: Liang, female, 22 years old, began experiencing unexplained fever and throat pain in September
2023, with a maximum temperature of 40°C. The fever persisted and was followed by joint pain and
submandibular lymph node swelling. After treatment with cephalosporins, her temperature returned to normal,
but intermittent fevers recurred. In November 2023, she was hospitalized and diagnosed with adult-onset Still’s
disease (AOSD) and macrophage activation syndrome (MAS). Initial treatment with methylprednisolone (20
mg/d and 40 mg/d) for anti-inflammatory and antipyretic effects proved ineffective. The dose was gradually
increased to 160 mg/d and combined with hydroxychloroquine and cyclosporine, but results were still
unsatisfactory. After discharge, the patient discontinued Western medications and opted for traditional Chinese
medicine (TCM) treatment.
Past examinations showed elevated liver enzymes in October 2023, with ALT at 111.2 U/L, AST at 141.3 U/L,
and ALP at 106.1 U/L. Stool occult blood was positive at 2+, ASO was 248 U/mL, CRP was 77.6 mg/L, and
autoimmune antibodies were negative. By November 2023, ferritin was significantly elevated at 10,173.08

Conclusion
In this paper, we introduce Hengqin-RA-v1, the first Chinese
large language model for diagnosing and treating rheuma-
toid arthritis using traditional Chinese medicine (TCM).
The model generates personalized, expert-validated treat-
ment plans that surpass the precision and specificity of other
Chinese and English LLMs, such as GPT and Gemini. We
also present HQ-GCM-RA-C1, the first TCM corpus dataset
for rheumatoid arthritis, designed for training LLMs and de-
veloping specialized report generation models. This dataset
complements English medical models, reducing bias and
promoting fairness, while advancing corpus completeness
and equitable representation in Chinese and English LLMs.
Future Work
In the future, we aim to enhance the Hengqin TCM LLMs
by continuously optimizing it in alignment with our expand-
ing dataset. Hengqin-RA-v1 and HQ-GCM-RA-C1 represent
our first-generation LLM and dataset. Building on this foun-
dation, we plan to develop v2, v3, and general-purpose large
models for comprehensive TCM intelligent systems. Addi-
tionally, we will introduce new TCM datasets, such as those
focused on arthritis, and expand HQ-GCM-RA-C1 as data
availability grows.
