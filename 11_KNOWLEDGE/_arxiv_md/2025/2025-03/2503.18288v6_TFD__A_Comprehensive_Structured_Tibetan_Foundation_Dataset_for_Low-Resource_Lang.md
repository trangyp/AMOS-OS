TFD: A Comprehensive Structured Tibetan Foundation Dataset for
Low-Resource Language Processing and Large-Scale Modeling

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) have achieved
remarkable success in high-resource languages,
yet progress for Tibetan remains severely con-
strained by the lack of large-scale, high-quality,
and structured data. Existing Tibetan resources
are fragmented, domain-limited, and insuffi-
cient to support modern LLM pipelines requir-
ing pretraining, instruction tuning, safety align-
ment, and reasoning supervision. We intro-
duce the Tibetan Foundation Dataset (TFD),
the first comprehensive, large-scale, and expert-
curated dataset explicitly designed for Tibetan
large language modeling. TFD comprises two
complementary components: TIBSTC, a uni-
fied corpus of over 11 billion tokens spanning
literature, law, medicine, religion, and every-
day communication, and TIBSTC-CoT, the first
large-scale Tibetan chain-of-thought dataset
supporting explicit multi-step reasoning across
diverse domains. Unlike prior Tibetan datasets,
TFD is structurally organized to support the
full LLM development lifecycle, including pre-
training, supervised fine-tuning, safety align-
ment, and preference optimization. We demon-
strate its utility by training the Sun-Shine fam-
ily of Tibetan LLMs and evaluating them on
understanding, safety, reasoning, and genera-
tion tasks. Results show consistent improve-
ments over strong open-source and proprietary
baselines, underscoring the importance of large-
scale, structured data for low-resource language
modeling. We release TFD to facilitate repro-
ducible research and the development of robust,
culturally aligned Tibetan LLMs. Code and
data are available at https://github.com/
Vicentvankor/sun-shine.

Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) (Hurst et al., 2024;
Achiam et al., 2023; Dubey et al., 2024; Guo et al.,
2025; Liu et al., 2024a) have demonstrated impres-
sive performance across a broad range of natural
language processing (NLP) tasks, exhibiting strong
generalization and emergent capabilities. However,
LLMs in low-resource and linguistically complex
languages (Zhang et al., 2025; Maji et al., 2025;
Kim et al., 2025), like Tibetan (Huang et al., 2025),
remain significantly underserved. The primary and
most fundamental cause of this problem is the long-
standing absence of high-quality Tibetan datasets.
For Tibetan itself, existing data resources are
extremely limited in scale, uneven in quality, and
lack standardized processing pipelines (Huang et al.,
2025; Wagner and Zeisler, 2004). Most prior Ti-
betan datasets (Cuo et al., 2017; Hao et al., 2024;
Ma et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2020; Gongqu et al.,
2024) cover only narrow domains, contain sub-
stantial noise or inconsistencies, and fail to pro-
vide the structured and large-scale supervision re-
quired by modern language modeling (Liang et al.,
2024; Lv et al., 2025). As a result, current Tibetan
NLP systems struggle to capture essential linguistic
characteristics, including morphology, syntax, dis-
course structure, and domain-specific terminology
and leading to poor generalization and unreliable
generation (Liu and Best, 2025).
To address this gap, we introduce the Tibetan
Foundation Dataset (TFD), the first large-scale,
structured, and expert-curated Tibetan dataset.
TFD consists of two sub-datasets, TIBSTC and
TIBSTC-CoT. For the TIBSTC, it covers over 11
billion tokens from domains such as literature, law,
religion, medicine, and daily communication, TIB-
STC is designed to support all key stages of LLM de-
velopment, including pre-training, instruction tun-
ing, safety alignment, and preference optimization.
For the TIBSTC-CoT, it is the first large-scale Ti-
betan chain-of-thought dataset, constructed to pro-
vide explicit intermediate reasoning supervision
across multiple domains and task types. Together,
these two components form a unified foundation
that addresses both general language modeling and
reasoning-oriented learning, filling a critical gap in
the Tibetan NLP ecosystem.

All in all, our contributions are as follows:
• We introduce TFD, the first large-scale, struc-
tured, and expert-curated Tibetan dataset, com-

over 11 billion tokens for pretraining and in-

• We design a comprehensive data processing

• We have released Sun-Shine family, the refer-

Related Work
2.1
Language Modeling
Tibetan is a severely low-resource language in NLP,
characterized by complex stacked syllables, rich
morphology, and limited digitized corpora (Wag-
ner and Zeisler, 2004), as shown in Appendix A
Figure 6. Existing Tibetan resources mainly target
narrow tasks such as segmentation, OCR, senti-
ment analysis, or text classification, and are typi-
cally small-scale, domain-specific, and weakly stan-
dardized (Liu and Best, 2025; Huang et al., 2025;
Liu et al., 2025d,b). Although recent efforts adapt
multilingual or pretrained models to Tibetan, the un-
derlying data remain fragmented, noisy, and insuf-
ficient to support foundation-level language model-
ing (Gao et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025c,a). In particu-
lar, prior datasets do not jointly support large-scale
pretraining, instruction tuning, safety alignment,
and preference learning, which are now standard
components in modern LLM pipelines (Zhuang and
Sun, 2025; Xu et al., 2025; Feng et al., 2025; Pan
et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025). As a result, current
Tibetan LLMs exhibit limited generalization, weak
discourse modeling, and unstable generation across
domains. To our knowledge, no existing dataset
provides a unified, large-scale, and structured foun-
dation explicitly designed for Tibetan LLM devel-
opment.
2.2
Chain of Thought
Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting has proven
effective for improving multi-step reasoning in
LLLMs, with extensive validation on high-resource
languages (Wei et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023). Ex-
tensions such as self-consistency and automated
reasoning generation further enhance robustness.
However, CoT research remains almost entirely con-
centrated on English and a few other high-resource
languages (Li et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2022; Cobbe
et al., 2021). For Tibetan, there is a complete ab-
sence of native, large-scale reasoning datasets that
preserve linguistic structure, logical coherence, and
cultural grounding. Existing multilingual reason-
ing benchmarks either exclude Tibetan or rely on
direct translation, which often degrades reasoning
fidelity. This lack of reasoning-oriented data funda-
mentally limits the development of Tibetan LLMs
with explicit reasoning and thinking capabilities.

Implementation
4.1
Reference LLMs: Sun-Shine Family
4.1.1
General Language Modeling
Sun-Shine 1.0 (Sun-Shine) is built on the LLaMA-
3.1 (8B) (Dubey et al., 2024), and its training pi-
pline of Sun-Shine consists of three stages: Pre-
Training, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) (Yu et al.,
2021), and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)
(Rafailov et al., 2024). Pre-training equips Sun-
Shine with a strong foundation in language under-
standing and generation. SFT (Yu et al., 2021) fur-
ther enhances its ability to follow task-specific in-
structions and produce responses that meet safety
and alignment standards. Finally, DPO (Rafailov
et al., 2024) aligns the model’s outputs with user
preferences, improving both relevance and user sat-
isfaction. For detailed hyperparameters and training
configurations of Sun-Shine 1.0, please refer to the
Appendix D.1.
4.1.2
Reasoning & Thinking
Sun-Shine 2.0 series is built on the Qwen-3 back-
bone (Yang et al., 2025) and trained using super-
vised fine-tuning (Yu et al., 2021). Sun-Shine 2.0 is
explicitly designed to support reasoning and think-
ing by learning from instruction data that include
structured intermediate reasoning steps. The train-
ing data span multiple domains and task types, en-
abling the models to perform multi-step reasoning
across generation, knowledge understanding, and
classification tasks. By incorporating explicit rea-
soning signals during fine-tuning, Sun-Shine 2.0
improves consistency, interpretability, and robust-
ness in complex reasoning scenarios, particularly in
low-resource language settings. For detailed hyper-
parameters and training configurations of Sun-Shine
2.0, please refer to the Appendix D.2
4.2
Baseline Model
Our experiments cover a diverse range of LLMs,
including both open-source and proprietary ones.
For detailed hyperparameters and training configu-
rations of LLMs, please refer to the Appendix E.
4.2.1
Open-source LLM
We evaluate several opensource LLMs, including
LlaMA-3.1-405B (Dubey et al., 2024), LlaMA-

represent a variety of architectures and parameter

source advancements in Tibetan language under-
standing.
4.2.2
Proprietary LLM
We also evaluate several proprietary LLMs, in-
cluding GPT-4o (Achiam et al., 2023), GPT-3.5-
Turbo (Achiam et al., 2023), Claude-3.5-Sonnet
(Anthropic, 2024), Gemini-1.5-Flash (Team et al.,
2024), and O1-Mini (Hurst et al., 2024). These
models serve as strong baselines for commercial
LLM performance in Tibetan tasks.
4.3
Understanding Evaluation: TLUE
We use the TLUE benchmark (Gao et al., 2025),
the first Tibetan language benchmark that includes
Ti-MMLU and Ti-Safetybench, to assess the Tibetan
language proficiency of LLMs. We will introduce
them in the following section. TLUE is designed
as a zero-shot evaluation benchmark for Tibetan
4


## Page 5


language understanding. All tasks consist of stan-
dardized natural-language prompts and reference
labels, enabling LLMs to be evaluated directly in a
zero-shot setting without task-specific fine-tuning.
4.3.1
Ti-MMLU
Ti-MMLU consists of 11,528 questions spanning 67
subjects, with each subject containing at least 105
questions. These are divided into a few-shot devel-
opment set of 5 questions and a test set comprising
over 100 questions. Ti-MMLU includes 17 STEM
tasks, 13 Humanities (Human) tasks, 22 Social Sci-
ence (Social) tasks, and 15 tasks from Other do-
mains. Notably, 16 tasks are China-specific (China),
meaning they either lack equivalents in other re-
gions or may have contextually different answers
elsewhere. The evaluation framework follows the
original CMMLU metrics (Li et al., 2024), includ-
ing
• Response Rate (RR) measures the proportion
of instances where LLMs provide a valid re-
sponse.
• Accuracy (ACC) evaluates the percentage of
correct answers.
• Conditional Accuracy (CA) assesses perfor-
mance only when a valid response is given.
4.3.2
Ti-Safetybench
Ti-Safetybench is based on SafetyBench (Zheng
et al., 2024), which is translated and manually vali-
dated by experts to ensure consistency with tradi-
tional Tibetan linguistic and cultural standards. Ti-
Safetybench contains 11,435 multiple-choice ques-
tions across 7 distinct safety categories: Offensive-
ness (OFF), Unfairness and Bias (UB), Physical
Health (PH), Mental Health (MH), Illegal Activi-
ties (IA), Ethics and Morality (EM), and Privacy
and Property (PP). The evaluation methodology
incorporates two scoring methods:
• Direct Answer Calculation (DA) assesses the
model’s ability to provide valid and accurate
responses.
• Concern All Answer Calculation (CAA) cal-
culates the performance ceiling by filtering
valid choices and verifying correctness after
removing irrelevant combinations.
4.4
Generation Evaluation: Downstream Task
4.4.1
Generation & Translation
We evaluate multilingual translation performance,
focusing on low-resource directions involving Ti-
betan. The evaluation covers four translation pairs
across Tibetan (BO), Chinese (ZH), and English
(EN), assessing both understanding and generation
capabilities. To ensure the comprehensiveness and
reliability of the evaluation, we conduct tests us-
ing the Flore-200 benchmark (Costa-Jussà et al.,
2022), a widely recognized multilingual translation
benchmark that includes a diverse range of language
pairs and covers various domains, styles, and text
types. This benchmark is particularly valuable for
evaluating low-resource language translation due
to its inclusion of underrepresented languages and
its design to reflect real-world translation scenarios,
which helps in better assessing the model’s adapt-
ability and robustness in practical applications.
4.4.2
Metrics
We adopt the standard automatic evaluation met-
rics BLEU (BLEU-4) (Papineni et al., 2002) and
ROUGE (ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2 and ROUGE-L)
(Lin, 2004) to assess the model’s capability in Ti-
betan machine translation and Tibetan story gener-
ation, respectively. BLEU-4 measures n-gram pre-
cision up to four grams and is widely used to assess
translation quality (Papineni et al., 2002). ROUGE-
1 and ROUGE-2 capture unigram and bigram re-
call, reflecting lexical overlap and local phrase-level
coherence (Lin, 2004). ROUGE-L computes the
longest common subsequence between the gener-
ated text and the reference, providing a measure of
sentence-level fluency and overall structural simi-
larity (Lin, 2004).
chrF++ (Popović, 2017) is introduced as a met-
ric to calculate character-level precision and re-
call, providing a better measure of fluency and
semantic fidelity in morphologically rich or low-
resource languages. When combined with the Flore-
200 benchmark (Costa-Jussà et al., 2022), chrF++
(Popović, 2017) enables a more nuanced analysis
of the model’s performance, capturing subtle differ-
ences in translation quality that may be overlooked
by token-level metrics, especially for languages like
Tibetan with complex morphological structures.

Experimental Result
5.1
Understanding
5.1.1
Sun-Shine 1.0
As shown in Figure 3a, Figure 3b and Figure 3c,
Sun-Shine 1.0, GPT-4o (Achiam et al., 2023) and
DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) outperform all
other LLMs. Among them, Sun-Shine is slightly
ahead of these. For the Response Rate, Sun-Shine
1.0 and GPT-4o (Achiam et al., 2023) achieve the
highest Response Rate, with Sun-Shine 1.0 leading
across all sub-branches,shown in Appendix F.1.1
Figure 15, demonstrating superior responsiveness
to Tibetan prompts enabled by its domain-specific
training. For Conditional Accuracy, Sun-Shine 1.0
and DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) perform best,
with the latter holding a slight advantage. Both
models maintain consistently high scores, reflect-
ing strong general knowledge capabilities. In terms
of overall Accuracy, Sun-Shine 1.0 outperforms
DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) in China-Specific,
STEM, and Other categories, while DeepSeek-R1
(Guo et al., 2025) leads in the remaining areas. This
underscores that Sun-Shine 1.0’s strong Tibetan un-
derstanding stems not only from model design but
also from effective use of the TIBSTC dataset and
targeted training, allowing an 8B model to surpass
the 671B MoE DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) in
key tasks.
As shown in Figure 3d and Figure 3e, Sun-Shine
1.0 was evaluated against leading LLMs on Ti-
SafetyBench across safety-related categories using
DA and CCA filtering methods. Compared to GPT-

6
Conclusion
In this work, we introduced TFD, the first large-
scale, structured, and expert-curated foundation
dataset explicitly designed for Tibetan language
modeling. By unifying a multi-domain corpus of
over 11 billion tokens, TIBSTC with the first large-
scale Tibetan chain-of-thought dataset TIBSTC-
CoT, TFD provides comprehensive support for
LLM pipelines, including pretraining, instruction
tuning, safety alignment, and explicit reasoning su-
pervision. Experiments with the Sun-Shine family
demonstrate that models trained on TFD achieve
consistently strong performance on Tibetan under-
standing, safety, reasoning, and generation bench-
marks, surpassing much larger general-purpose
models. These results show that high-quality, cultur-
ally grounded, and structurally organized data is a
key factor in advancing low-resource language mod-
eling. We release TFD to facilitate reproducible
research and to support the development of robust,
safe, and culturally aligned Tibetan LLMs.

Limitations
TFD still has several limitations. Although it covers
diverse domains and both modern and classical Ti-
betan, its domain distribution is constrained by data
availability, and some specialized topics remain un-
derrepresented. The construction of TIBSTC-CoT
relies on a hybrid LLM-assisted and expert-verified
pipeline, which may introduce residual model bi-
ases. In addition, our evaluations focus on text-
based tasks and do not yet consider multimodal
Tibetan settings. Finally, we do not exhaustively
study model architectures or training strategies, as
the primary goal of this work is to establish a foun-
dation dataset rather than optimize models.

D.1.1
Training Pipline
As illustrated in Figure 14, the training pipline of
Sun-Shine 1.0 consists of three stages: Pre-training,
SFT (Yu et al., 2021), and DPO (Rafailov et al.,
2024).
• Pre-Training: Sun-Shine 1.0 is pre-trained on
the Corpus, which helps the Sun-Shine learn
general language representation and genera-
tion capabilities using a standard objective,
such as Next Token Prediction. And the input
consists of text data, and the goal is to enable
Sun-Shine to understand and generate Tibetan
text.
• SFT: Sun-Shine 1.0 undergoes supervised fine-
tuning (Yu et al., 2021) with Alpaca-Ti and
Safety-Prompts-Ti.
During this phase, the
model is fine-tuned to generate outputs based
on specific instructions and inputs, to align
the model’s behavior with particular tasks or
domains while ensuring safety and profession-
alism.
• DPO: Sun-Shine 1.0’s alignment and output
quality are further optimized using preference
data. The input data comes from CValues-Ti
and hh-rlhf-Ti. Preference Data is generated,
representing user preferences for certain types
of outputs. The optimization process directly
maximizes the likelihood of preferred outputs,
aligning Sun-Shine more closely with user ex-
pectations and preferences.
D.1.2
Hyperparameter
Table 9 shows the parameters’ settings. Notably,
we adopted LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-
tuning (Hu et al., 2021) for both SFT (Yu et al.,
2021) and DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024), which sig-
nificantly reduced memory usage without sacrific-
ing performance. Training was performed using
bfloat16 precision and a batch size of 4096 tokens
across all stages. LoRA adapters (rank=32) (Hu
et al., 2021) were applied to both attention and
feed-forward layers during SFT (Yu et al., 2021)
and DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024). All stages used
AdamW optimizer (Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019)
with a weight decay of 0.1 and a dropout rate of
0.1. The pretraining phase involved over 11B to-
kens with three full epochs. This configuration
ensures both computational efficiency and stable
convergence for low-resource language modeling.

Ancient Poetry Writing
As shown in Figure 17, different LLMs exhibit
clear stylistic differences when responding to the
culturally sensitive prompt “Please write a poem
in praise of father” across Tibetan, Chinese, and
English. Sun-Shine 1.0 maintains a neutral and
