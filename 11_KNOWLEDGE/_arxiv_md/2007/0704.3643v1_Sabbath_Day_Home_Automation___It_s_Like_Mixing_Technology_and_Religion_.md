  bootstrap_capsule:

    class: DERIVED

    text: >
      The paper studies long-term use of home automation by 20 American
      Jewish households, predominantly Orthodox, where timers, X10 systems,
      and a specialized calendar-aware system were used to configure domestic
      environments in advance of the Sabbath. Participants often described
      automation as reducing the need to attend to mundane household controls,
      supporting Sabbath atmosphere and family life, and enabling intentional
      surrender of in-the-moment control. The authors argue that automation
      also functions as an organizing system that encodes and reinforces
      household routines and social expectations. From this case they derive
      broader HCI design hypotheses concerning surrender of control,
      technologies that support chosen lifestyles and long-term values,
      and technology respite structured at community scale.
