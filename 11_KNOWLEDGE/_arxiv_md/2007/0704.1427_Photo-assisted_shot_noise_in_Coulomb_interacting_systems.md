# Photo-Assisted Shot Noise in Coulomb Interacting Systems

## AMOS Knowledge Identity

**Node ID:** `ARXIV_0704_1427_V1`

**Source class:** PRIMARY_PAPER

**Epistemic default:** SOURCE_CLAIM

> Important: statements below describe the models, calculations,
> interpretations, and conclusions reported by the authors. They are
> not automatically promoted to independently VERIFIED empirical facts.

---

# 1. PAPER OBJECTIVE

The paper studies electrical-current fluctuations under a voltage containing
both DC and AC components:

\[
V(t)=V_0+V_1\cos(\omega t).
\]

The central observable is photo-assisted shot noise.

The authors contrast the known non-interacting result—where the derivative
of photo-assisted noise exhibits a staircase—with interacting systems.

Two systems are analyzed:

1. fractional quantum Hall edge states;
2. an interacting carbon nanotube receiving electrons from an STM tip.

---

# 2. GLOBAL PHYSICAL QUESTION

## 2.1 Baseline

For non-interacting metallic systems, the derivative of photo-assisted noise
has steps when the DC-induced frequency becomes an integer multiple of the
AC frequency.

## 2.2 Research question

How do Coulomb interactions alter these photo-assisted noise signatures?

## 2.3 Reported answer

The modification depends strongly on the interacting system.

### Fractional quantum Hall regime

The derivative develops singular structures associated with fractional
quasiparticle transport.

### Carbon nanotube

The staircase is smoothed by interaction-dependent tunneling properties,
with finite-length physics changing the result as the relevant frequency
ratio changes.

---

# 3. COMMON PHOTO-ASSISTED STRUCTURE

A central mathematical mechanism in both systems is the time-dependent
tunneling/backscattering amplitude generated by

\[
V(t)=V_0+V_1\cos(\omega t).
\]

The phase-modulated amplitude can be expanded into photon sidebands using
Bessel functions:

\[
e^{i(\omega_1/\omega)\sin(\omega t)}
=
\sum_{p=-\infty}^{+\infty}
J_p\left(\frac{\omega_1}{\omega}\right)e^{ip\omega t}.
\]

Therefore transport processes occur through effective frequency channels

\[
\omega_0+p\omega.
\]

### Interpretation

`p` indexes absorption/emission sidebands associated with the AC drive.

### Important distinction

The definition of \(\omega_0\) and \(\omega_1\) differs between the two
systems because the relevant tunneling charge differs.

---

# 4. SYSTEM A — FRACTIONAL QUANTUM HALL REGIME

## 4.1 Model

The system consists of counter-propagating chiral edge states.

The Hamiltonian is

\[
H=H_0+H_B.
\]

The kinetic contribution describes right- and left-moving chiral
excitations.

The backscattering Hamiltonian transfers quasiparticles between edges.

---

## 4.2 Quasiparticle operator

The edge quasiparticle fields are represented bosonically as

\[
\Psi_{R(L)}
=
\frac{F_{R(L)}}{\sqrt{2\pi a}}
e^{i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{R(L)}}.
\]

### Parameters

- \(F_R,F_L\): Klein factors
- \(a\): short-distance cutoff
- \(\phi_R,\phi_L\): chiral bosonic fields
- \(\nu\): filling factor

The quasiparticle charge is

\[
e^*=\nu e.
\]

This relation is load-bearing for the paper's interpretation of
photo-assisted noise as a fractional-charge probe.

---

# 5. TIME-DEPENDENT BACKSCATTERING

For

\[
V(t)=V_0+V_1\cos(\omega t),
\]

the backscattering amplitude becomes

\[
A(t)
=
\Gamma_0
e^{i\omega_0t+i(\omega_1/\omega)\sin(\omega t)}.
\]

With

\[
\omega_0=\frac{\nu eV_0}{\hbar},
\qquad
\omega_1=\frac{\nu eV_1}{\hbar},
\]

the Bessel expansion gives

\[
A(t)=
\Gamma_0
\sum_{p=-\infty}^{+\infty}
J_p\left(\frac{\omega_1}{\omega}\right)
e^{i(\omega_0+p\omega)t}.
\]

## Equation role

This is the paper's fundamental photo-assisted sideband decomposition for
the FQH system.

---

# 6. BACKSCATTERING CURRENT

The current operator is

\[
I_B(t)
=
i\nu e A(t)\Psi_R^\dagger(t)\Psi_L(t)
-\mathrm{h.c.}
\]

The noise is formulated on the Keldysh contour.

The analysis is performed in the weak-backscattering / Poissonian regime,
retaining the second-order contribution in the tunneling amplitude.

---

# 7. ZERO-FREQUENCY PHOTO-ASSISTED NOISE

The quantity of interest is the double Fourier transform

\[
S(\Omega_1,\Omega_2)
\]

evaluated at

\[
\Omega_1=\Omega_2=0.
\]

At finite temperature, the reported expression is

\[
S(0,0)
=
\frac{(e^*)^2\Gamma_0^2}
{2\pi^2a^2\Gamma(2\nu)}
\left(\frac{a}{v_F}\right)^{2\nu}
\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{2\nu-1}
\sum_{p=-\infty}^{+\infty}
J_p^2\left(\frac{\omega_1}{\omega}\right)
\cosh\left[
\frac{(\omega_0+p\omega)\beta}{2}
\right]
\left|
\Gamma\left(
\nu+i\frac{(\omega_0+p\omega)\beta}{2\pi}
\right)
\right|^2.
\]

where

\[
\beta=\frac{1}{k_BT}.
\]

---

# 8. NON-INTERACTING VALIDATION LIMIT

The authors test

\[
\nu=1.
\]

This corresponds to their non-interacting reference case.

The derivative of the noise exhibits staircase behavior.

Steps occur when

\[
\omega_0=n\omega,
\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

This reproduces the expected Lesovik-Levitov behavior.

### AMOS classification

**Class:** SOURCE_CLAIM with literature consistency check.

It should not be relabeled as a new empirical validation performed by the
paper.

---

# 9. FRACTIONAL CASE

For

\[
\nu=\frac13,
\]

the authors report evenly spaced singularities in the derivative of the
shot noise.

Because

\[
\omega_0=\frac{\nu eV_0}{\hbar},
\]

the voltage positions encode the quasiparticle charge

\[
e^*=\nu e.
\]

## Physical interpretation

The singularities are interpreted as reminiscent of the tunneling
density-of-states singularities associated with Laughlin quasiparticles.

## Proposed measurement implication

Photo-assisted transport may therefore act as a probe of effective
fractional charge.

### Epistemic class

`SOURCE_CLAIM / MODEL-BASED PROPOSAL`

The paper proposes the measurement implication; the provided source does
not itself constitute an experimental demonstration of fractional-charge
extraction by this method.

---

# 10. SYSTEM B — CARBON NANOTUBE

## 10.1 Geometry

An STM tip is positioned near a carbon nanotube connected to leads at both
ends.

Electrons tunnel from the STM into the central nanotube region.

Injected charge excitations subsequently propagate toward the two leads.

---

# 11. NANOTUBE HAMILTONIAN

The total Hamiltonian is

\[
H=H_N+H_{\mathrm{STM}}+H_T.
\]

The nanotube is modeled as a non-chiral Luttinger liquid:

\[
H_N
=
\frac12
\sum_{j\delta}
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}
dx\,
v_{j\delta}(x)
\left[
K_{j\delta}(x)(\partial_x\phi_{j\delta})^2
+
K^{-1}_{j\delta}(x)(\partial_x\theta_{j\delta})^2
\right].
\]

The sectors are

\[
j\delta\in\{c+,c-,s+,s-\}.
\]

They correspond to charge/spin and total/relative modes.

---

# 12. INTERACTION PARAMETERS

The model takes

\[
K_{c-}=K_{s+}=K_{s-}=1.
\]

The interacting total-charge sector is

\[
K_{c+}.
\]

Its position dependence models the nanotube/contact structure.

The velocities satisfy

\[
v_{j\delta}(x)
=
\frac{v_F}{K_{j\delta}(x)}.
\]

For the plotted interacting example,

\[
K_{c+}=0.2.
\]

---

# 13. STM TUNNELING

The tunneling Hamiltonian is

\[
H_T(t)
=
\sum_{r\alpha\sigma}
\Gamma(t)
\Psi_{r\alpha\sigma}^{\dagger}(0,t)c_\sigma(t)
+
\mathrm{h.c.}
\]

Here:

- \(r\): branch index
- \(\alpha\): mode index
- \(\sigma\): spin
- \(c_\sigma\): STM electron field
- \(\Psi_{r\alpha\sigma}\): nanotube electron field

Unlike the fractional Hall case, the tunneling particles here are
electrons.

Therefore

\[
\omega_0=\frac{eV_0}{\hbar},
\qquad
\omega_1=\frac{eV_1}{\hbar}.
\]

This distinction must not be collapsed with the FQH definition.

---

# 14. PHOTO-ASSISTED TUNNEL AMPLITUDE

The tunneling amplitude is

\[
\Gamma(t)
=
\Gamma
\sum_{p=-\infty}^{+\infty}
J_p\left(\frac{\omega_1}{\omega}\right)
e^{i(\omega_0+p\omega)t}.
\]

The same Bessel-sideband architecture appears as in the FQH calculation,
but the charge scale and interacting propagators differ.

---

# 15. CONTACT REFLECTION PARAMETER

The paper defines

\[
b_{c+}
=
\frac{K_{c+}-1}{K_{c+}+1}.
\]

This parameter represents reflection associated with the nanotube contacts
at

\[
x=\pm L/2.
\]

The exponent entering the nanotube noise expression is

\[
\nu
=
\frac18
\sum_{j\delta}
\left(
\frac1{K_{j\delta}}+K_{j\delta}
\right).
\]

Important semantic warning:

The symbol \(\nu\) here appears in a different model context from the FQH
filling factor. The two uses must remain separately scoped.

---

# 16. NANOTUBE PHOTO-ASSISTED NOISE

The paper derives a zero-frequency expression of the form

\[
S(0,0)
=
\frac{4\Gamma^2e^2}{\pi^2a^2}
\sum_p
J_p^2\left(\frac{\omega_1}{\omega}\right)
\int_0^\infty
d\tau\,
\cos[(\omega_0+p\omega)\tau]
\times
\mathcal{G}_{CNT}(\tau),
\]

where \(\mathcal{G}_{CNT}\) contains the interaction-dependent Luttinger
liquid correlations and the repeated contact-reflection contributions.

The full source expression should be retained in the raw-equation layer
rather than replaced by the compact notation above.

---

# 17. NANOTUBE REGIME STRUCTURE

The decisive control parameter is the ratio involving the finite-length
frequency scale and AC frequency:

\[
\frac{\omega_L}{\omega}.
\]

The authors examine three representative values:

\[
0.1,\quad1.2,\quad6.
\]

---

# 18. REGIME A — \(\omega_L/\omega=0.1\)

The wave-packet spatial extension is smaller than the nanotube length.

The interaction effects dominate much of the relevant voltage range.

Instead of a sharp staircase, the derivative is smoothed.

The original non-interacting step locations nevertheless leave visible
signatures through abrupt changes in slope.

## Proposed mechanism

The authors attribute this smoothing to the Coulomb-interaction-modified
tunneling density of states of the nanotube.

### Causal discipline

This attribution should remain:

`SOURCE_MODEL_INTERPRETATION`

rather than being silently promoted to universally established causation.

---

# 19. REGIME B — \(\omega_L/\omega=1.2\)

This is an intermediate regime.

Electron wave packets become comparable in spatial extent to the nanotube
length.

Neither a purely interaction-dominated nor purely finite-size-dominated
description captures the whole behavior.

---

# 20. REGIME C — \(\omega_L/\omega=6\)

Electron wave packets are larger than the nanotube length.

The authors report that finite-size effects dominate over Coulomb
interaction effects.

A stepwise structure characteristic of the non-interacting reference
behavior can again be identified.

# 24. CENTRAL CONCLUSION

The source supports the following paper-level conclusion:

> Photo-assisted shot noise is sensitive to Coulomb interactions, but the
> observable signature depends on the correlated conductor.

For the fractional quantum Hall model, the predicted singularity spacing
contains information about fractional quasiparticle charge.

For the nanotube model, interactions alter the height and shape of the
noise-derivative staircase, while finite-size effects can attenuate those
interaction signatures.
