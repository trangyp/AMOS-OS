  # 3. BOOTSTRAP CAPSULE

  bootstrap_capsule:

    class: DERIVED

text: ">"
      The paper studies hidden-variable network models in which each node
      possesses an unobserved variable g drawn from a distribution rho(g),
      while the probability of an edge between nodes is p(g,g'). Network
      observables depend on the conditional degree distribution, or propagator,
      G0(k,g). The authors approximate this propagator by averaging p(g,g')
      over the neighboring hidden variable, obtaining a binomial leading-order
      form with connection probability pbar(g). They validate this approximation
      analytically and numerically for several models. Applied to protein-protein
      interaction networks, they interpret g through association free-energy
      variation and use a Fermi-type binding probability. The model reproduces
      degree distributions and many measured clustering coefficients across
      several species. Importantly, two parameter sets can generate nearly
      identical human degree distributions while predicting clustering
      coefficients differing by about a factor of two, showing that degree
      distributions alone do not identify the underlying network structure.
