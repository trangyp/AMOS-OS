  # 3. BOOTSTRAP CAPSULE
  # ============================================================

  bootstrap_capsule:

    class: DERIVED

    text: >
      Neronov and Aharonian investigate whether the day-scale variable
      TeV gamma-ray emission reported from M87 can originate close to its
      central supermassive black hole. They argue that the low nuclear
      luminosity can permit multi-TeV photons to escape, estimate that
      near-horizon magnetic fields are modest under their accretion assumptions,
      and find conventional proton channels problematic for reproducing the
      observed TeV spectrum and rapid variability. They instead model
      electron acceleration in rotation-induced vacuum gaps of a Kerr black-hole
      magnetosphere. Electrons reach very high energies in a loss-saturated
      regime and emit curvature/synchrotron and inverse-Compton photons.
      Absorption of the highest-energy primary gamma rays produces secondary
      electron-positron pairs, whose inverse-Compton emission generates an
      electromagnetic cascade that can supply a more isotropic TeV component.
      The authors report that a toy implementation can reproduce major features
      of the HESS spectrum while explicitly cautioning against overinterpreting
      the fit.
