  bootstrap_capsule:

    class: DERIVED

text: ">"
      Viterbo and Hong study Golden space-time trellis coded modulation
      originally optimized for slow fading and ask whether the same codes
      remain effective when a codeword spans multiple independently faded
      blocks. The transmitted sequence consists of Golden-code matrices
      constrained by an outer trellis. For block length N and codeword length
      L, the number of independently faded blocks is B=L/N. Pairwise error
      behavior is governed by products of determinants of block-aggregated
      distance matrices F_l. Because this exact metric is non-additive, the
      authors lower-bound each determinant by a sum of per-symbol
      determinants, creating a tractable block-fading performance surrogate.
      They then analyze shortest simple trellis error events: an event of
      length S intersects either ceil(S/N) or ceil(S/N)+1 blocks, with
      computable multiplicities. The resulting two dominant event classes
      yield a two-term truncated union-bound interpretation of performance.
      Simulations for multiple 4-, 16-, and 64-state GST-TCM designs show
      modest degradation as fading changes from slow to fast, with about
      1 dB loss at FER 10^-3 in the tested cases, supporting the source's
      claim that slow-fading-optimized GST-TCM is robust across the studied
      block-fading conditions.

        encountered. The exact block-fading distance criterion is a product

        inequality yields a tractable lower-bound metric based on sums of
        per-symbol determinants. The authors then show that a shortest trellis
        error event of length S occupies either ceil(S/N) or ceil(S/N)+1

        from higher asymptotic diversity. The reusable principle is that
