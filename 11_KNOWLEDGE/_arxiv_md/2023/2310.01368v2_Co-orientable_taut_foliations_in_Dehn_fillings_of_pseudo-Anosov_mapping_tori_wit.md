1. Introduction
Throughout this paper, all 3-manifolds are connected, orientable and irreducible.
The L-space conjecture ([BGW], [J]) predicts that the following statements are equivalent for a
closed orientable irreducible 3-manifold M:
(1) M is a non-L-space.
(2) π1(M) is left-orderable.
(3) M admits a co-orientable taut foliation.
The implication (3) =⇒(1) is confirmed in [OS1] (see also [Bo], [KazR2]). In the case that M
has positive first Betti number, (2) is proved in [BRW], and (3) is proved in [Ga1]. The L-space
conjecture has been verified when M is a graph manifold ([BC], [Ra], [HRRW]).
There are two common ways for constructing closed orientable 3-manifolds: Dehn surgeries on
knots and links in S3, and Dehn fillings on mapping tori of compact orientable surfaces. Both of
these two methods can produce all closed orientable 3-manifolds ([A], [Lic], [W]). A slope on a knot
manifold is called an NLS or LO or CTF filling slope if the corresponding Dehn filling satisfies (1)
or (2) or (3), respectively, and it is called an L-space filling slope if the corresponding Dehn filling
is an L-space. Similarly, we use the terminologies NLS, LO, CTF, L-space filling multislopes for
link manifolds (see Subsection 2.1 for the convention of multislopes). We also use the terminologies
NLS, LO, CTF, L-space surgery (multi)slopes for knots or links.

Now we review some known results on CTF filling slopes of M. To be convenient, we state the
following two theorems in our setting, although they do not need to restrict φ to be pseudo-Anosov.
The following theorem is proved by Roberts in [Ro1], [Ro2], originally stated in [Ro2, Theorem
4.7] for the case where φ is pseudo-Anosov. For a version that does not require φ to be pseudo-
Anosov, see [DR, Theorem 1.4].
Theorem 1.2 (Roberts). Suppose that Σ has exactly one boundary component.
(a) If φ is right-veering, then M(s) admits a co-orientable taut foliation for any rational slope
s ∈(−∞, 1).
(b) If φ is left-veering, then M(s) admits a co-orientable taut foliation for any rational slope
s ∈(−1, +∞).
(c) If φ is neither right-veering nor left-veering, then M(s) admits a co-orientable taut foliation
for any rational slope s ∈(−∞, +∞).
Moreover, the core curve of the filling solid torus is transverse to the foliation in each case.

Let Fs, Fu denote the stable and unstable foliations of φ. The monodromy φ is said to be co-
orientable if Fs is co-orientable, and φ is said to be co-orientation-preserving (resp. co-orientation-
reversing) if φ is co-orientable and preserves (resp. reverses) the co-orientation on Fs. Here, Fs
can be replaced with Fu (see Remark 2.6). For more information on co-orientable pseudo-Anosov
homeomorphisms, see Subsection 2.4.
For general pseudo-Anosov mapping tori of compact surfaces with more than one boundary
component, co-orientation-preserving is the only known case to have an explicit multi-interval such
that all rational multislopes in it are CTF filling multislopes. The following theorem is implicitly
contained in [Ga3]. For the reader’s convenience, we briefly sketch how it is deduced from Gabai’s
argument. By pushing ∂M slightly inward, we obtain from the suspension foliation of Fs a foliation
that meets each boundary torus in Reeb annuli separated by circles (see [Ga3, Operation 2.3.1]).
Likewise, for each interior singular orbit of the suspension flow of φ, we can choose a solid torus
whose core is precisely this singular orbit and whose boundary torus intersects the suspension
foliation of Fs in the same way, namely in Reeb annuli separated by circles. Removing all such
solid tori from M, the resulting manifold satisfies the hypotheses of [Ga3, Operation 2.4.1], which
ensures that any Dehn filling whose restricted slope on each boundary component is not isotopic
to the core of a Reeb annulus admits a co-orientable taut foliation. Finally, by assigning to every
boundary torus coming from an interior singular orbit the filling slope corresponding to that of the
original solid torus, we obtain the desired result for Dehn fillings of M.
Theorem 1.4 (Gabai). Assume that φ is co-orientable and co-orientation-preserving. Let k denote
the number of boundary components of M. For any multislope (s1, . . . , sk) ∈(Q ∪{∞})k such that
each si is not the degeneracy slope of the corresponding boundary component, M(s1, . . . , sk) admits
a co-orientable taut foliation. Moreover, the core curves of the filling solid tori are transverse to the
foliation.
As an immediate consequence of the above result, for any hyperbolic fibered knot in S3 with
co-orientation-preserving monodromy, the degeneracy slope is the meridian; this conclusion also
follows from [GO, Theorem 5.3].
Unlike the co-orientation-preserving case, many M(s1, . . . , sk) (with each si being other than the
degeneracy slope) admit no co-orientable taut foliation when φ is co-orientation-reversing, since
there are many hyperbolic fibered L-space knots in S3 and lens spaces that have co-orientation-
reversing monodromy (see Proposition 1.9, Examples 1.13∼1.15).

1.2. Applications to Dehn surgeries on knots. In this subsection, we apply Corollary 1.6 to
Dehn surgeries on knots in S3 or spherical manifolds.
At first, we describe some properties of
L-space filling slopes on knot manifolds.
A knot manifold is Floer simple if it has at least two distinct L-space filling slopes (compare with
[RR, Proposition 1.3]), and a knot in a closed 3-manifold is called an L-space knot if its exterior is
Floer simple. We note from [RR, Theorem 1.6] that for any Floer simple knot manifold N, there is
an interval L(N) of slopes such that
{L-space filling slopes of N} = L(N) ∩(Q ∪{∞}),
and in particular, L(N) either consists of all slopes except the homological longitude, or is a closed
interval. We call L(N) the maximal L-space filling interval of N. If L(N) is a closed interval, then
we call its complement the maximal NLS filling interval of N.
A knot in a closed 3-manifold is called a fibered knot if its exterior is a once-punctured surface

left-veering or right-veering. Let T = ∂M, and let d(T) = (p; q) (then δT = p

For a connected, simply connected, possibly non-Hausdorff 1-manifold, the approach to obtain its
maximal Hausdorff quotient is described in [RSS, Section 4], and the resulting space is referred to
as its associated Hausdorff tree in [RSS, Definition 4.6]. As described in [RS, page 186] or [Fe, page
4], this operation also applies to R-order trees.
