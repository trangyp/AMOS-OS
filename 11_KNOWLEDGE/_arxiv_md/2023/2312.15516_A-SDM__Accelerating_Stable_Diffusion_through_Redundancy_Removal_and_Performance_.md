A-SDM: Accelerating Stable Diffusion through Redundancy Removal
and Performance Optimization

Abstract
The Stable Diffusion Model (SDM) is a popular and efficient text-to-image (t2i) generation and image-to-image (i2i) generation
model. Although there have been some attempts to reduce sampling steps, model distillation, and network quantization, these
previous methods generally retain the original network architecture. Billion scale parameters and high computing requirements
make the research of model architecture adjustment scarce. In this work, we first explore the computational redundancy part of the
network, and then prune the redundancy blocks of the model and maintain the network performance through a progressive incuba-
tion strategy. Secondly, in order to maintaining the model performance, we add cross-layer multi-expert conditional convolution
(CLME-Condconv) to the block pruning part to inherit the original convolution parameters. Thirdly, we propose a global-regional
interactive (GRI) attention to speed up the computationally intensive attention part. Finally, we use semantic-aware supervision
(SAS) to align the outputs of the teacher model and student model at the semantic level. Experiments show that this method can
effectively train a lightweight model close to the performance of the original SD model, and effectively improve the model speed
under limited resources. Experiments show that the proposed method can effectively train a light-weight model close to the per-
formance of the original SD model, and effectively improve the model speed under limited resources. After acceleration, the UNet
part of the model is 22% faster and the overall speed is 19% faster.
Keywords: Stable diffision, Distillation, Transformer, Attention
1. Introduction
The Stable Diffusion model (SDMs) is the most popular and
well-known text-to-image (t2i) generation model. Because of
its high-quality generation results, it has been applied to a va-
riety of text-guided visual tasks, such as image-to-image (i2i)
generation for style transformation, video generation, and in-
painting. SDMs are a latent diffusion model (LDM) for text-to-
image task, which improves computational efficiency by per-
forming denoising calculations in the latent space. Although
latent space optimization has been done, due to the need for
iterative calculation of UNet inside SDM, the final calculation
amount is also very large, which brings a huge burden on com-
puting resources, and also brings resistance to the promotion
of SDM on mobile terminals. Fig.1 shows that the computing
resource consumption is mainly in the part of iterating UNet.
To mitigate this problem, a variety of SDM optimization
methods have been proposed, such as reducing denoising steps
by distillation, replacing the teacher model of batch 2 with the
student model of batch 1 by distillation, and reducing computa-
tional effort by merging tokens.
However, there are relatively few researches on the modifi-
cation of UNet network architecture. This paper optimizes the
redundant part of the network architecture according to the oc-
cupy time of each blocks. Fig.2 shows the latency for each part

2. Compression Methed
2.1. UNet architecture
To facilitate the introduction, we define the entire UNet ar-
chitecture into three tiers: block, layer, and unit. The standard
UNet in the stable diffusion model consists of down blocks, up
blocks, and mid block. There is one mid block, three down
blocks and three up blocks, which form a U-shaped network ar-
chitecture. The down blocks are composed of two layers, and
the up blocks are composed of three layers. Each layer contains
a ResNet unit and a Transformer unit. It is worth noting that the
deepest dn block, up block and mid block are special.
2.2. Progressive Incubation Strategy
Figure 3: Macro progressive incubation Process.
Training stable diffusion models usually requires huge com-
putational resources, which makes researchers less involved in
model architecture reconstruction. Here we design a progres-
sive incubation strategy to allow researchers to train the model
with relatively few computational resources. Fig.3 shows our
training ideas through a simplified diagram. First, we train a
compressed model, which is a small model that removes some
layers in each BLOCK of the model. Then we combine the
parts of the compressed model with the parts of the original
model to get the new student model. Since the original model
part of this model is frozen, it is very effective for reducing
the computational stress of subsequent knowledge distillation
training.
Figure 4: Specific stable diffusion progressive incubation process.
Fig.4 shows the distillation process of the UNet model in
the specific stable diffusion. The compressed UNet is obtained
by pruning off the layers marked in the red box in the original
UNet. In the 2nd step of progressive incubation, the part of the
original UNet and the part of the compressed UNet are recom-
bined, and combined UNet is used as a student model for a new
round of distillation. This time we only train the shallow part
of the calculation redundancy, and the rest is frozen.

2.3. Cross-layer Multi-expert Conditional Convolution
Generally, the convolution part pays more attention to de-
tails, while the attention part pays more attention to global in-
formation. For shallow layers with large feature scales, convo-
lution usually plays a key role in detail optimization, while the
attention part is relatively redundant, which has been widely
recognized in the field of detection and segmentation. There-
fore, we will increase the capacity of the convolution part and
improve the calculation of the attention part.
For a compressed UNet model in which shallow compo-
nents are subtracted, the computational capacity is reduced even
though the original performance is maintained through distilla-
tion. Therefore, we adapt cross-layer multi-expert condition
convolution (CLME-Condconv) to increase the shallow layer
capacity of the combined UNet. The 3x3 convolution weights
of each layer of the original up-block3 are collected and com-
bined with multi-expert conditional convolution to assign each
layer of the up-block3 of the student model. Dn-block0 does a
similar operation.

2.5. Semantic-Aware Supervision
We have improved on the loss in the distillation process.
In the critical 2nd-stage distillation process, we apply the con-
ventional denoising loss and the distillation loss at the output
position of the teacher and student models.
To strengthen feature alignment at the semantic level, we
add semantic-aware supervision. Because there are many inter-
nal components of the UNet network, we added mid-block out-
put supervision to strengthen alignment at the semantic level,
which has a better optimization effect for the previous dn-block0.
Besides, we added a frozen VGG network at the end of the
teacher and student model to extract the deep information of the
denoising features. As shown in Fig.6, the output of the teacher
and the output of the student model are both passed through the
same VGG model, and the purpose of this VGG loss is to align
the various levels of features, thereby enhancing the semantic
awareness of the student model.

3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets
We primarily use 0.22M image-text pairs from LAION Aes-
thetics V2, which are significantly fewer than the original train-
ing data used for SDMs( 2,000M pairs).
3.2. Implementation
We adjust the codes in Diffusers. We use a 32 NVIDIA
V100 35G GPU for main retraining. For compute efficiency, we
always opt for 30 denoising steps of the UNet at the inference
phase, unless specified. The classifier-free guidance scale is
set to the default value of 8.0. The latent resolution is set to
512×512.
3.3. Qualitative evaluation.
We have tested on two animation style models, and the ex-
perimental effect is shown in Fig. The optimized model ob-
tained by our method is close to the original model.
3.4. Conclusion
We explore an effective model compression method, opti-
mize the structure of the computational redundancy part of the
stable diffusion model, and achieve a large speed increase while
maintaining the original performance of the original stable dif-
fusion model with limited computing resources. We adopt a
progressive incubation strategy to prune the redundant parts of
the stable diffusion model. At the same time, we expand the
resnet block capacity and optimize the efficiency of the atten-
tion block. In terms of supervision strategy, semantic-aware
supervision is utilized. Experiments show that the proposed
method can effectively train a light-weight model close to the
performance of the original SD model, and effectively improve
the model speed under limited resources. After acceleration,
the UNet part of the model is 22% faster and the overall speed
is 19% faster.
