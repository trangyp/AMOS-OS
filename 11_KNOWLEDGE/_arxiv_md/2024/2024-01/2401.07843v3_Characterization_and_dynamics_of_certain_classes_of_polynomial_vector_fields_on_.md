CHARACTERIZATION AND DYNAMICS OF CERTAIN CLASSES OF
POLYNOMIAL VECTOR FIELDS ON THE TORUS
SUPRIYO JANA
Abstract. In this paper, we classify all polynomial vector ﬁelds in R3 of degree up to
three such that their ﬂow makes the torus
T2 = {(x, y, z) ∈R3 : (x2 + y2 −a2)2 + z2 −1 = 0} with a ∈(1, ∞)
invariant. We also classify cubic Kolmogorov vector ﬁelds on T2 and prove that they
exhibit a rational ﬁrst integral. We study ‘pseudo-type-n’ vector ﬁelds on T2 and show
that any such vector ﬁeld is completely integrable. We prove that the Lie bracket of any
two quadratic vector ﬁelds on T2 is completely integrable. We explicitly ﬁnd all cubic
vector ﬁelds on T2 which achieve the sharp bounds for the number of invariant meridians
and parallels. We present necessary and suﬃcient conditions when invariant meridians
and parallels of cubic vector ﬁelds on T2 are periodic orbits or limit cycles. We discuss
invariant meridians and parallels of pseudo-type-n vector ﬁelds as well. Moreover, we
characterize the singular points of a class of polynomial vector ﬁelds on T2.
1. Introduction
Polynomial vector ﬁelds have been intensely explored since 1900 due to the second part
of Hilbert’s 16th problem [8, 11, 12, 15]. People also studied invariant algebraic curves of
polynomial vector ﬁelds in the plane due to Darboux Integrability Theory [5], which states
that a suﬃcient number of invariant algebraic curves guarantees the existence of a ﬁrst
integral. Llibre and Zhang [23] extended the Darboux Integrability Theory to vector ﬁelds
on any regular algebraic hypersurface in Rn. The ﬁrst integral is also an important part of
the qualitative theory of diﬀerential systems, see for instance [10, 16, 25, 27] and references
therein. We remark that a ﬁrst integral of the associated vector ﬁeld of a diﬀerential system
reduces the dimension of the system by one, making the analysis of the system easier.

Planar Kolmogorov systems were introduced by Kolmogorov [14] to describe the in-
teraction between two species occupying the same ecological habitat. Later, polynomial
Kolmogorov systems were generalized to higher dimensions with the following form:
dxi
dt = xiPi(x1, ..., xn) where Pi ∈R[x1, ..., xn].
These systems may exhibit quite complex dynamical behaviors, see [9] and the references
therein (e.g., references 6, 10, 11, and 12). The associated vector ﬁeld of a Kolmogorov
system is called a Kolmogorov vector ﬁeld.
The algebraic hypersurface given by {f = 0} with f ∈R[x, y, z] is called invariant if
χf = Kf for some K ∈R[x, y, z]. The polynomial K is called the cofactor of χ for the
hypersurface {f = 0}. Moreover, χ is called a vector ﬁeld on {f = 0}. In this case, if any
solution curve of (1.1) intersects the hypersurface {f = 0} then the entire curve lies in
{f = 0}. Polynomial vector ﬁelds on the various algebraic hypersurfaces have been studied
in [3, 7, 17, 20, 21, 22, 26].
Llibre and Medrado [19] studied polynomial vector ﬁelds on the two-dimensional torus
T2 given by
T2 = {(x, y, z) ∈R3 : (x2 + y2 −a2)2 + z2 −1 = 0} where a ∈(1, ∞).
They investigated the sharp upper bounds for the number of invariant meridians, parallels
and discussed limit cycles of quadratic vector ﬁelds on T2. The deﬁnition of invariant
meridians and invariant parallels can be found in Section 3. Note that limit cycles are
periodic orbits that are isolated in the set of all periodic orbits of the vector ﬁeld.
In this paper, we classify arbitrary cubic vector ﬁelds, cubic Kolmogorov vector ﬁelds,
and the Lie bracket of two quadratic vector ﬁelds on T2. We discuss the existence of ﬁrst
integrals and characterize the invariant meridians and parallels of these vector ﬁelds. In
particular, for a cubic vector ﬁeld on T2, we provide necessary and suﬃcient conditions
when invariant meridians are limit cycles and invariant parallels are periodic orbits. We
prove the following in Section 4.
Proposition 1.1. Suppose that χ = (P, Q, R) is a vector ﬁeld on T2 such that P, Q, R are
homogeneous polynomials with deg P = deg Q = n and deg R ≤n. Then R = 0.
Therefore, we deﬁne χ = (P, Q, R) as a pseudo-type-n vector ﬁeld if P, Q are homoge-
neous polynomials of degree n and R = 0. We classify all pseudo-type-n vector ﬁelds on
T2 as well.
In Section 5, we provide a diﬀerent proof of the sharp bound of invariant meridians
for a polynomial vector ﬁeld on T2, see Theorem 5.1. We give a necessary condition for
the existence of singular points of a quadratic vector ﬁeld on T2. Finally, we discuss the
local structure of trajectories on T2 around the isolated singular points of the vector ﬁeld
(Ay, −Ax, 0) where A ∈R[x, y, z].

3. Preliminaries
In this section, we present some basic deﬁnitions and useful results. We recall the notion
of the Lie bracket of two vector ﬁelds.
For polynomial vector ﬁelds on a regular algebraic hypersurface, [17, Theorem 3] states
that a suﬃcient number of invariant algebraic hypersurfaces guarantees the existence of a
rational ﬁrst integral. So, it is essential to study diﬀerent invariant algebraic hypersurfaces
for a given vector ﬁeld. In this paper, we study the invariant planes in R3 containing the
z-axis or orthogonal to the z-axis.
We recall the following deﬁnitions from [19]. The curves obtained by the intersection of
a plane containing the z-axis with T2 are called meridians. Similarly, the curves obtained
by the intersection of a plane orthogonal to the z-axis with T2 are called parallels. More
precisely, meridians can be obtained by {ax + by = 0} ∩T2 where a, b ∈R. So, meridians
always appear in pairs. The plane ax + by = 0 is said to be a meridian plane. Also,
parallels can be obtained by {z −k = 0} ∩T2 with −1 ≤k ≤1. So, if k ̸= ±1 then
parallels appear in pairs. The plane z −k = 0 is said to be a parallel plane.
We say that a meridian obtained by the plane ax + by = 0 is invariant if {ax + by = 0}
is invariant for χ. Similarly, we say that a parallel obtained by the plane z −k = 0 is
invariant if {z −k = 0} is invariant for χ.
