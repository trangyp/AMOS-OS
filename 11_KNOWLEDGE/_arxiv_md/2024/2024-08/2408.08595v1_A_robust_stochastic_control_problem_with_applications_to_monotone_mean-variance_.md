Abstract
This paper studies a robust stochastic control problem with a monotone mean-variance cost
functional and random coeﬃcients. The main technique is to ﬁnd the saddle point through two
backward stochastic diﬀerential equations (BSDEs) with unbounded coeﬃcients. We further show
that the robust stochastic control problem shares the same optimal control and optimal value with
the stochastic control problem with a mean-variance cost functional. The results obtained are then
applied to monotone mean-variance and mean-variance portfolio selection problems and monotone
mean-variance and mean-variance investment-reinsurance problems.
Key words: robust stochastic control, monotone mean-variance, mean-variance, random coeﬃcients,
backward stochastic diﬀerential equations.
MSC-classiﬁcation: 91B16, 93E20, 60H30, 91G1.

Introduction
The mean-variance (MV) preferences, initially introduced by Markowitz [13], stand as one of the
most widely used objectives for portfolio selection problems. Many studies have utilized this criterion
to investigate the portfolio selection problems, see e.g., [21, 22, 6] in continuous-time settings and
[8, 10, 23] in discrete-time settings. However, a major drawback of MV preferences is their lack of
monotonicity. That means a strictly wealthier investor may possess lower utility compared to others,
contradicting the fundamental assumption of economic rationality. To address this signiﬁcant limita-
tion, Maccheroni et al. [12] introduced monotone mean-variance (MMV) preferences and tackled the
MMV model in a single-period setting. They showed that the MMV preference constitutes the smallest
monotone preference functional coinciding with mean–variance on their domain of monotonicity. This
provided an approach to show the optimal strategies to MV problems and MMV problems coincide
by proving the optimal strategies to both MV and MMV preferences lead to a terminal wealth within
the domain of monotonicity of the classical mean–variance functional. Within this framework, Strub
and Li [18] illustrated the optimal strategies and values of MV and MMV are consistent when asset
prices are continuous. Further, in a stochastic factor model, Li, Liang and Pang [9] demonstrated
the equivalence of MV and MMV under certain conditions, including situations involving jumps. Re-
cently, Du and Strub [2] took the trading constraints into account and investigated the equivalence
of MV and MMV portfolio selection problems when asset prices are continuous under general trading
constraints.

On the other hand, to the best of our knowledge, Trybu la and Zawisza [20] ﬁrst considered a
continuous-time stochastic factor model, obtained the optimal strategy and value function for both
the MV and MMV optimization problems in an explicit form, and demonstrated their equivalence
through direct comparison. Following the same direction as [20], Shen and Zou [15] considered a
deterministic coeﬃcients model with cone trading constraints and solved the MMV and the MV
problems successively by means of the HJBI equation approach and veriﬁed that the optimal strategies
to both problems coincide. Then, Hu, Shi and Xu [4] generalized [15] to a diﬀusion model with random
appreciation rate, volatility and deterministic interest rate. Recently, Shi and Xu [17] reach the same
conclusion for the MMV and MV investment-reinsurance problems. In the conclusion of [4], they
pointed out that how to solve the MMV problem when the interest rate is random even without any
constraints constitutes an interesting question for future research.

and the following state dynamics

where all coeﬃcients are random and the coeﬃcient A is even unbounded. The main technique is to
ﬁnd the saddle point through two backward stochastic diﬀerential equations (BSDEs) with unbounded
coeﬃcients. It is worth noting that the coeﬃcients in our setting are all random and one of them
is even unbounded, which makes our BSDEs signiﬁcantly diﬀerent from those in [4] and cannot be
solved by existing results on BSDEs. Relying on BMO martingale techniques, we establish some a
priori estimates and successfully obtain the existence and uniqueness results of these two BSDEs.
We further study the corresponding stochastic control problem with mean-variance cost functional
and achieve that both the robust stochastic control problem and the stochastic control problem with
mean-variance cost functional share the same optimal control and optimal value. Finally we apply our
results to MV an MMV portfolio selection problems. We obtain optimal strategies for both MV and
MMV problems with random coeﬃcients in a complete market and show by direct comparison that
they coincide. To the best of our knowledge, this is the ﬁrst result of obtaining the explicit solution of
the MMV problems with random (and unbounded) interest rate, which partially answer the question
proposed in [4] for random (and unbounded) interest rate and complete market. Upon this basis, we
further investigate MMV and MV investment-reinsurance problems and obtain the same conclusion.
The reminder of this paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we formulate the robust control
problems with random coeﬃcients. In section 3, we establish the existence and uniqueness of solutions
to two BSDEs with unbounded coeﬃcients and obtain the optimal pair of the robust control problem.
In section 4, we solve the control problem with mean-variance cost functional and make a comparison
with the robust control problem. Applications to MMV and MV portfolio selection problems and
MMV and MV investment-reinsurance problems are presented in section 5.
