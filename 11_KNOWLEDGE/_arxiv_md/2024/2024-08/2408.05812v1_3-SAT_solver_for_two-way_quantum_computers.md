3-SAT solver for two-way quantum computers
Jarek Duda
Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science, Jagiellonian University, Krakow, Poland, dudajar@gmail.com
Abstract—While quantum computers assume existence of state
preparation process |0⟩, CPT symmetry of physics says that per-
forming such process in CPT symmetry perspective, e.g. reversing
used EM impulses (V (t) →V (−t)), we should get its symmetric
analog ⟨0|, referred here as state postparation - which should provide
results as postselection, but with higher success rate.
Two-way quantum computers (2WQC) assume having both |0⟩
and ⟨0| pre and postparation. In theory they allow to solve NP
problems, however, basic approach would be more difficult than
Shor algorithm, which is now far from being practical. This
article discusses approach to make practical 2WQC 3-SAT solver,
requiring exponential reduction of error rate, what should be
achievable through linear increase of the numbers of gates.
2WQC also provides additional error correction capabilities,
like more stable Grover algorithm, or mid-circuit enforcement of
syndrome to zero, like proposed equalizer enforcing qubit equality.
Keywords: 3-SAT, NP problems, quantum computers, CPT
symmetry, computational complexity, quantum error correction
I. INTRODUCTION
While CPT symmetry is necessary for Lorentz invariant local
quantum field theories [1], standard one-way quantum computers
(1WQC) assume asymmetric treatment of boundary conditions:
allowing only state preparation at the beginning, and only
measurement at the end.
For QC technologies like silicon quantum dots [2], state
preparation |0⟩is performed with EM impulses - hence using
reversed impulses (V (t) →V (−t)), we should perform state
preparation in perspective of CPT symmetry, what corresponds
to ⟨0|. While pre-measurement is considered in literature [3],
alone it does not seem to bring new capabilities. In contrast,
adding state postparation ⟨0| as reversed preparation brings
essential improvements, like 3-SAT solver we will discuss here.
Therefore, we will focus on 2WQC as 1WQC with added ⟨0|
state postparation operation, which mathematically gives results
as postselection |0⟩⟨0|, but with higher success rate - physically
enforcing the final state exactly as state preparation enforces
the initial state. For example 2WQC allows for faster and more
stable Grover algorithm [4], and generally should allow to solve
PostBQP [5] problems. However, while cloning is claimed being
possible using closed timelike curves [6], for 2WQC no-cloning
theorem remains valid [7].
As summarized in Fig. 1, this article focuses on 3-SAT solver
initially suggested in [8]. Generally it seems more difficult
to realize than Shor algorithm [9], currently being far from
practical. However, 2WQC also brings looking more powerful
error correction capabilities, like proposed equalizer gadget, as
discussed here in theory allowing to overcome imperfections,
and hopefully in future leading to practical realizations.

B. 3-SAT problem
3-SAT [10] (boolean satisfiability problem) is one of the basic
NP complete problems, also very convenient for the discussed
approach. We ask if there exist binary variables x, such all given
alternatives of triples of variables (some can be negated) are
satisfied. For example:
∃x1x2... (x1∨¬x2∨x3)∧(¬x4∨x2∨¬x3)∧(x5∨¬x4∨x2)∧. . . ?
For a chosen instance of 3-SAT problem, let us denote n as
the number of variables: x ∈{0, 1}n, and m as the number of
its alternative clauses - the formula to satisfy is ∀i=1..m Ci(x),
where Ci(x) clause is alternative of some 3 of variables, some
of which can be negated.
Restringing the number of times a variable is used, turns out
for 4 appearances the problem is still NP complete [11], hence
we can assume each variable is used at most 4 times, what allows
to bound depth of the proposed 2WQC solver.
C. Basic 2WQC 3-SAT solver
In Shor algorithm we first prepare superposition of all 2n
inputs, then calculate a classical function (ya mod N to fac-
torize N, using a random y) into additional qubits, which are
measured - what restricts the ensemble to usually a periodic one.
Performing its Fourier transform (QFT) and measuring allows
to obtain this period, which provides hint for the factorization
problem.
The proposed 3-SAT solver is similar, for the classical func-
tion using the verifier of NP problem: for 3-SAT calculating
m alternative clauses. We could postselect them to 1 as ”true”:
ensuring satisfaction of all clauses - measuring input qubits we
should get a solution of this 3-SAT problem.
However, such p1WQC approach would have pessimistically
P(c) ∼2−n success probability of postselection, requiring
exponential number of runs. 2WQC would allow to replace this
|1⟩⟨1| postselection with ⟨1| postparation as CPT analog of state
preparation, in theory increasing success probability to 1.
Such solver requires 4-qubit C-OR gates (reversible), to be
optimized with basic quantum gates in the future.

III. CONCLUSION AND FURTHER WORK
This article proposes construction of 3-SAT solver for 2WQC,
with basic analysis and dedicated error correction mechanisms,
in theory allowing to overcome imperfections, and hopefully
leading to practical realizations.
There are many direction for further work, for example:
• There was used C-OR as 4-qubit gate, which needs reduc-
tion and optimization into basic gates.
• There was used some basic imperfection model, which
should be extended in the future, including e.g. decoher-
ence, and analysis of imperfections of proposed equalizers.
• Practical realizations might require optimization of e.g.
serial-parallel setting and mesh of equalizers.
• While we have focused on 3-SAT, it might be worth
considering other NP complete problems, especially having
in mind polynomial cost of transformations between such
problems.
• Finally such NP solvers should be realized and tested: first
in simulators, and then in hardware.
• We should investigate also possibility of attacks on PSPACE
problems, e.g. adding forall quantifier for some variables of
3-SAT problem.
• Search for practical applications for such NP solvers, de-
pending on a growing number of available qubits.
• Generally, possibility of such quantum NP solvers should
be verified, leading to necessary preparations if positive -
especially in search for some nextgen postquantum cryp-
tography: resistant to imperfect quantum NP solvers.
