Abstract This paper introduces the first axiomatic quantification of the de-
gree of risk aversion for spectral and law-invariant coherent risk measures.
This notion formalizes the idea that some risk measures are “more risk-averse”
than others. To this end, I propose a degree functional on the space of spectral
risk measures that is uniquely defined by two axioms: a normalization on the
space of CVaRs and a linearity axiom. I present two formulas for the degree
functional and discuss several properties and interpretations. Finally, I extend
the definition to law invariant coherent risk measures.

We are interested in assessing the degree of risk aversion of a spectral risk
measure ρ ∈R. While spectral risk measures are widely used (see, e.g., [10,
12,14,15,17,18,28] for recent applications and [13] for an example by the late
Werner R¨omisch), there is currently no principled way to quantify how risk
averse a given spectral risk measure is or to compare different spectral risk
measures in terms of their degree of risk aversion. Beyond theoretical interest,
such a quantification would be of practical use in optimization and finance.
For instance, it could enable consistent calibration of risk preferences across
different modeling paradigms and provides a scalar index of conservatism in
risk-based decision models.
This paper introduces the first axiomatic and quantitative notion of the
degree of risk aversion of spectral risk measures. To this end, we axiomatically
define a degree functional Dp : R →[0, 1] (parametrized by p) on the space
R of spectral risk measures that quantifies their degree of risk aversion. The
degree functional Dp is uniquely defined by a normalization axiom on the space
of conditional value at risk measures and a linearity axiom. Its interpretation
is that higher values of Dp(ρ) correspond to risk measures ρ ∈R that are
“more risk-averse”.
Our degree functional is inspired by the literature on expected utility risk
measures, based on the primal1 theory of rational choice under uncertainty by
von Neumann and Morgenstern [19]. These risk measures take the form ρ(Z) =
E[u(Z)], with u : R →R some utility function. For expected utility, various
quantifications of the degree of risk aversion exist based on the utility function
u. The most notable examples are the measures of absolute and relative risk
aversion by Arrow and Pratt [2,22]. The original Arrow-Pratt measures are
local, in the sense that they specify the risk aversion at a current cost level Z =
z. Some authors have extended Arrow-Pratt measures to global measures of
risk aversion, by, e.g. taking the infimum [6] of the local Arrow-Pratt measures
over all possible values of Z. For other approaches, see [7,8,11,24].
However, for spectral risk measures, based on Yaari’s dual theory of ra-
tional choice under uncertainty, no such quantifications of risk aversion are
known in the literature. The same holds for the popular and more general
class of law invariant coherent risk measures. This paper aims to fill this gap
by axiomatically constructing a global measure of the degree of risk aversion
of spectral risk measures, called the degree functional, and extending it to law
invariant coherent risk measures by means of their Kusuoka representation
[16,27]. In sum, we provide the dual counterpart to global Arrow-Pratt-type
measures of risk aversion.
We derive two representations of the degree functional Dp. The first is in
terms of a p-generalized mean with respect to the Kusuoka representer µρ. This
expression helps interpreting the degree functional Dp for different values of
p. The second representation is in the form of a Stieltjes integral with respect

convex combinations of CVaRs; Axiom 1 tells us how to deal with CVaR, while
Axiom 2 tells us how to deal with convex combinations.

enough that it should be maintained.

5 Conclusion
The degree functional Dp formalizes what it means for a spectral or law in-
variant coherent risk measure to be “more risk-averse” than another. In this
way, it extends the notion of global measures of risk aversion in the spirit of
Arrow and Pratt from the primal world of expected utility to the dual world
of spectral risk measures, and to more general law invariant coherent risk
measures.
Thus, the degree functional Dp offers a unifying language for comparing,
ranking, and calibrating risk measures. This can be helpful in practice, when
deciding which risk measure to use for a particular practical problem. More-
over, it opens the door for formalizing intuitive notions that some operations
(e.g., mixing with another risk measure) make a risk measure “more” or “less”
risk-averse. Indeed, future research might be aimed in this direction. Finally,
future research could further explore the dual relationship between the degree
functional Dp for (dual) spectral risk measures and Arrow-Pratt-type measures
of risk aversion for (primal) expected utility.
