Abstract
We prove a principle of linearized stability for traveling wave solutions to neural field
equations posed on the real line.
Additionally, we provide the existence of a finite
dimensional invariant center manifold close to a traveling wave, this allows to study bi-
furcations of traveling waves. Finally, the spectral properties of the modulated traveling
waves are investigated. Numerical schemes for the computation of modulated traveling
waves are provided. We then apply these results and methods to study a neural field
model in a inhibitory stabilized regime. We showcase Fold, Hopf and Bodgdanov-Takens
bifurcations of traveling pulses. Additionally, we continue the modulated traveling pulses
as function of the time scale ratio of the two neural populations and show numerical
evidences for snaking of modulated traveling pulses.
Keywords:
neural fields, traveling waves, modulated traveling waves, bifurcation
theory, shooting, DAE, freezing method
1. Introduction
Cortical waves have been the subject of active studies as reviewed in [MCRS18].
Some of the latest results concern the stimulus evoked activity in the visual cortex of
awake monkeys [MRCD14] where it was experimentally shown that this cortical activity
is a traveling wave (see Figure 2) thanks to a recent phase-based algorithm for wave
detection. It was suggested in [MCRS18] that the main mechanism for the existence of
this traveling wave is a non zero - distance dependent conduction - delay.
On the other hand, the visual cortex in cats was shown in [OFS+09] to be best mod-
eled with an inhibition stabilized network (ISN) in which a strong recurrent inhibitory
input onto the excitatory cells paradoxically shapes the dynamics [TSSM97]. The dy-
namics of traveling pulses and fronts in this type of network was very nicely studied
in [HE18] as function of the inhibition space / time scales. Notably, the authors found
modulated traveling pulses by direct simulation. They also narrowed the analysis to the
specific exponential connectivity kernels in order to perform numerical continuation of
the waves by relying on the spatial dynamics trick [San02].

Here, we theoretically and numerically investigate the traveling wave dynamics in
ISNs in hope to elucidate key mechanisms in monkey visual cortex dynamics [MRCD14].
We thus do not impose the connectivity kernel function. We do not study the existence of
wave [EM93, Fay18, Fay19] and simply assume that one is provided. At Hopf bifurcations
in the wave frame, modulated traveling waves (MTW) emerge which are the main subject
of this study. We also aim to study how the principle of linearized stability applies to
TW and MTW in the context of neural fields. This was for example conjectured in
[Fay18] although this result is well known in the context of reaction-diffusion models
[SS01b, San02]. We based our analysis on the freezing method [BT04, RM11]. When
used numerically, this method allows us to simulate the long time asymptotic of waves
without being limited by the simulation domain as in [HE18].
The (numerical) study of waves has a long history in the context of neural fields as
reviewed in [Coo05, CGPW14, Bre14], starting with analytical expressions [Ama77] in
which the authors assumed a Heaviside nonlinearity, and spanning the theory as well
as the numerical analysis. The proof of existence of waves is a fascinating albeit very
technical domain of research [EM93] with some of the latest tools reviewed in [Fay19].
The study [RAB+14] is among the first one to perform numerical continuation of sta-
tionary solutions (not waves) of neural fields equations based on a matrix-free approach,
freeing the authors from the use of specific connectivity kernels and space discretization
size. This was further extended in [FSV22] where the numerical bifurcation analysis was
entirely performed on GPU. The computation of (stationary) waves using matrix-free
methods has first been tackled in [Lai14] albeit without the preconditioner discovered
in this paper which greatly speeds up the convergence of the newton algorithm.
The literature on the numerical computation of MTW is more scarce in the context
of neural fields (but see [WCLK10]) and this work is probably the first study in this
area. Their computation as zeros of functionals [Lai14], and not by direct simulations
has been performed, for other models, using a shooting method in [WCLK10, GNS16]
or a trapezoidal scheme in [Uec21, KUY21, LTBK18]. See also [She12] for a software
dedicated to the computation of MTW in the case of PDEs. In the neural fields commu-
nity, MTWs have been computed by direct simulation in [BWE05, FB04, Fol17, KF14]
or by interface dynamics [CL11] but so far, they have not been studied using numerical
bifurcation tools. Compared to PDEs where sparse linear algebra can be used, the linear
operators are dense which imposes the use of matrix free methods and iterative solvers.
These methods are usually impeded by the slow convergence rate of the solvers but we
will see that a simple preconditioner can remediate that.
The plan of the paper is as follows. In a first section 2, we present the neural fields
model. In section 3, we informally review the concepts associated with the dynamics
of waves. In section 4, we state our main results. In section 5, we prove a principle

section 7, we prove a principle of linearized stability for modulated traveling waves. In

4. Notations and main results
In the previous section, we described the type of waves that we want to study. Our
work is not focused on the very delicate question of existence [EM93] of such solutions
(some of the most recent methods are nicely presented in [Fay19]) except some MTW
which can be predicted from a Hopf bifurcation of TW (see theorem 2 below). We are
interested in the dynamical stability of these solutions. Some of these stability results
are very well known [San02] albeit mostly in the diffusive case (but see [RM12]).

8. Numerical methods
Owing to the number of space variables, we aim at performing the computations in
a matrix-free setting freeing us from the limitation on the number of variables. In effect,
for a space discretization of size N, the number of unknowns is 2N + 1 for a TW.
16


## Page 19


Note that pde2path [Uec14] supports the computation of waves through the addition
of constraints to the model formulation. However, the software lacks shooting capabilities
and that is why we use BifurcationKit.jl [Vel20] instead. The implemented methods
require very little modification of this Julia package and will be added to the official
package in the near future.
8.1. Traveling waves
The traveling waves are numerically approximated as zeros of (5) with Neumann
boundary conditions for ∂using a Krylov-Newton [Kel95, RAB+14] algorithm with
preconditioner [SGN13] Pr := (−I +cprec∂, −I/τ +cprec∂) for cprec estimated with direct
simulation. It is very cheap to cache the sparse LU decomposition of Pr even for large
discretizations. Without this preconditioner, the Krylov-Newton algorithm converges
very slowly or does not converge at all. The iterative linear solver used for the Newton
iterations is GMRES [Bar94].
8.2. Stability of traveling waves
The linear stability of a TW (¯v, ¯c) is deduced from the computation of the spectrum
of A/¯c in (7).
This is performed using a shift-invert method [Saa11] based on the
preconditioner Pr in order to extract the right-most eigenvalue. However, we found
that the method sometimes misses eigenvalues with large imaginary parts stemming
from the transport operator ∂, so we validate our results with the slower computation
of the spectrum of etA (in effect the semigroup) for a fixed and small t > 0, see [Tuc20].
In all cases, the iterative eigensolver is based on the Arnoldi algorithm [Saa11].
Another method consists in computing the spectrum of AP based on lemma 5. In the
context of bifurcation theory, this is really advantageous as it removes the 0 eigenvalue
associated with ∂¯v with is nonzero (numerically) although quite small in practice (≈
10−9).
8.3. Numerical bifurcation analysis of traveling waves
To study the dependence of the TW on a parameter p, we use a pseudo-arclength con-
tinuation [Gov00] method which allows us to obtain curves of solutions (¯u(s), ¯c(s), p(s)),
s being the arclength. The wave reference ˆu and the wave speed of the preconditioner
Pr is updated with the previously found wave solution.
The detection of bifurcation points in a continuation run is performed using the
bisection algorithm implemented in [Vel20]. The continuation of codim 1 bifurcation
points (Fold / Hopf) in the wave frame is based on a minimally augmented formulation
of (4) with the diagonal mass matrix M = diag(I2N, 0). Finally, the codimension 2
bifurcations are computed along the Fold/Hopf curve based on theorem 2.
8.4. Direct simulations
The computation of the solutions of the DAE (4) and the ODE (6) is based on
the adaptive and implicit time stepper Rodas4P2 of the Julia package DifferentialE-
quations.jl [RN17] using a Jacobian-free formulation with preconditioner Pr updated at
time steps for which the iterative solver GMRES has longer iterations than a prescribed
number.

All numerical computations were performed in the Julia programming language (ver-

sion 0.2.1). Unless otherwise specified, all computations were made using N = 2048
points on [−L, L] with L = 500. We could have used N ∼106 on a laptop for the
analysis of TPs but the computation of MTPs would have required the use of dedicated
hardware, e.g. GPU and we did not find that increasing N dramatically changed our
numerical observations. Centered finite differences of order 2 were used for the spatial
derivative ∂. The newton tolerance for all computation was set to 1e−9 in supremum
norm.
The numerically stable computation of Floquet exponents using box scheme
(section 8.6) proved to be brittle. We therefore refrain from computing bifurcations
from MTPs using finite differences, and instead check their stability using the shooting
method.
