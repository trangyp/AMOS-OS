Probabilistic Representation for Viscosity Solutions to Double-Obstacle
Quasi-Variational Inequalities
Magnus Perninge∗
September 9, 2024
Abstract
We prove the existence and uniqueness of viscosity solutions to quasi-variational inequalities (QVIs)
with both upper and lower obstacles.
In contrast to most previous works, we allow all involved
coeﬃcients to depend on the state variable and do not assume any type of monotonicity. It is well
known that double obstacle QVIs are related to zero-sum games of impulse control, and our existence
result is derived by considering a sequence of such games. Full generality is obtained by allowing one
player in the game to randomize their control. A by-product of our result is that the corresponding
zero-sum game has a value, which is a direct consequence of viscosity comparison.
Utilizing recent results for backward stochastic diﬀerential equations (BSDEs), we ﬁnd that the
unique viscosity solution to our QVI is related to optimal stopping of BSDEs with constrained jumps
and, in particular, to the corresponding non-linear Snell envelope.
This gives a new probabilistic
representation for double obstacle QVIs.
It should be noted that we consider the min-max version (or equivalently the max-min version);
however, the conditions under which solutions to the min-max and max-min versions coincide remain
unknown and is a topic left for future work.

Related literature When the lower barrier is absent (i.e. when ℓ≡∞), the PDE described in
(1.1) reduces to a standard (single-barrier) QVI. It is well known that, under suitable conditions on the
involved parameters, value functions of impulse control problems are solutions (in viscosity sense) to
standard QVIs when the driving noise process is a Brownian motion (see the seminal work in [4]) and
to so-called quasi-integrovariational inequalities when the driving noise is a general L´evy process [17].
Employing a contraction argument, QVIs featuring general non-local drivers were investigated in [19].
Speciﬁcally, [19] establishes a Feynman-Kac representation of such QVIs by linking their solutions to
systems of reﬂected BSDEs (RBSDEs).
In the case when the impulse costs ℓand χ are non-increasing in time and independent of the state
variable, x, a zero-sum game of impulse control was resolved in [7] by proving that the corresponding
value function is indeed the unique viscosity solution to a double obstacle QVI. The results presented in
[7] extended previous results from [23], where one player was restricted to play a switching control. Since
then, zero-sum games where both players implement switching controls have been considered in a more
general framework in [9] and [10].
An alternative Feynman-Kac representation for solutions to standard QVIs was proposed in [15] (see
also [5]), where the solution to a QVI is related to the minimal solution of a BSDE with constrained
jumps. Following the seminal work in [15], BSDEs with positive jumps were related to fully non-linear
Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman integro-partial diﬀerential equations (HJB-IPDEs) through a Feynman-Kac
representation in [16], while a correspondence between RBSDEs with positive jumps and fully non-linear
variational inequalities was established in [6].
The ensemble of approaches to ﬁnd probabilistic representations of PDEs or to solve stochastic op-
timal control problems that utilize BSDEs with constrained jumps is commonly referred to as control
randomization.
Recently, [22] further extended the scope of control randomization by establishing a
link between the solution to a reﬂected BSDE with constrained jumps and optimal stopping of BSDEs
with constrained jumps. The results in [22] are applicable to the optimal stopping problem described
in (1.6)-(1.8), provided that WY (τ, Xt,x

Outline In the next section, we set the notations and state the assumptions that hold throughout.
In addition, we give some preliminary results that are repeatedly referred in the article. In Section 3, we
formulate the zero-sum game and state our main result (Theorem 3.1). In the following section, we turn
to the upper and lower bounds v and ¯v and show that v (resp. ¯v) is a supersolution (resp. subsolution)
to (1.1).
Together with the comparison principle derived in Appendix A (eﬀectively stating that a
supersolution to (1.1) always dominates a subsolution) this proves existence of a unique viscosity solution
to (1.1), along with a value for the game. Finally, in Section 5 we turn to the probabilistic representation
which, in light of our earlier ﬁndings, is derived in a straightforward manner.
