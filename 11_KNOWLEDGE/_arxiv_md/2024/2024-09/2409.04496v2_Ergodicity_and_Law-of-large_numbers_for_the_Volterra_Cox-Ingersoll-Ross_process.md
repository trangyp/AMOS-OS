ERGODICITY AND LAW-OF-LARGE NUMBERS FOR THE
VOLTERRA COX-INGERSOLL-ROSS PROCESS
MOHAMED BEN ALAYA, MARTIN FRIESEN, AND JONAS KREMER1
Abstract. We study the ergodic properties for the Volterra Cox-Ingersoll-Ross pro-
cess on R+ and its stationary version. Based on a fine asymptotic analysis of the corre-
sponding Volterra Riccati equation combined with the affine transformation formula,
we first show that the finite-dimensional distributions are asymptotically indepen-
dent. Afterwards, we prove a Law of Large Numbers in Lp(Ω) with p ≥2 and show
that the stationary process is ergodic. As an application, we prove the consistency of
the method of moments and study the maximum-likelihood estimation for continuous
and discrete high-frequency observations.

1.3. Application to statistical inference. As an application of the established Law
of Large Numbers, we study the estimation of drift parameters (b, β) based on contin-
uous, discrete high-frequency, and discrete low-frequency observations. The probably
simplest method to estimate the drift parameters (b, β) is based on the method of mo-
ments. Since here explicit computations are crucial to obtain explicit estimators, in
Section 2.4 we focus on the particular case of fractional kernels (1.1). Applying the Law
of Large Numbers (1.10), we derive in Corollary 2.6 the consistency for the method of
moments.
A more delicate estimation method that allows for more efficient estimators is pro-
vided by the maximum-likelihood method. In the absence of memory, results applicable
to classical affine processes have been obtained in [5, 6, 11], while we refer to [31] for
the general theory on statistical inference for Markov diffusion processes. Following
the approach described in [10] for general stochastic Volterra equations, in Section 3
we use the Law of Large Numbers to establish convergence properties for the MLE of
(b, β) first for continuous, and then for discrete high-frequency observations. More pre-
cisely, based on continuous observations we first derive an explicit formula for the MLE
(bbT , bβT ) and then show that it is strongly consistent, and asymptotically normal locally
uniformly in the drift parameters (b, β). Subsequently, we treat the case of discrete
high-frequency observations by numerical discretisation of the continuous MLE and, in
particular, derive bounds on the discretisation error.
Finally, we conclude with additional comments on the estimation of the other model
