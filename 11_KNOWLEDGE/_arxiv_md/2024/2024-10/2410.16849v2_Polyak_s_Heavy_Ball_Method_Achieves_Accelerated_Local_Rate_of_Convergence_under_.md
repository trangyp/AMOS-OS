POLYAK’S HEAVY BALL METHOD ACHIEVES ACCELERATED LOCAL
RATE OF CONVERGENCE UNDER POLYAK-ŁOJASIEWICZ INEQUALITY
SEBASTIAN KASSING AND SIMON WEISSMANN
Abstract. In this work, we analyze the convergence of Polyak’s heavy ball method in both con-
tinuous and discrete time for non-convex C4-objective functions satisfying the Polyak-Łojasiewicz
inequality. Under this weak assumption, we recover the asymptotic convergence rates originally
derived by Polyak in [Pol64] for strongly convex objectives. Our results demonstrate that the
heavy ball method exhibits asymptotic local acceleration on this class of functions. In particular,
in the discrete time setting, we prove local convergence of the iterates to a minimum once the
method enters a suﬀiciently small neighborhood of the set of minima, for a broad range of hy-
perparameters, including aggressive choices for the momentum parameter and the step-size for
which global convergence is known to fail. Instead of the usually employed Lyapunov-type argu-
ments, our approach leverages a new differential geometric perspective of the Polyak-Łojasiewicz
inequality proposed in [RB24].
1. Introduction
In this work, we study the unconstrained minimization problem

leading to improved convergence on ill-conditioned optimization problems. However, the theo-
retical understanding of the advantages of these methods remains incomplete.

In many practical applications, classical convexity assumptions are violated [VBGS17]. For
example, objective functions arising in supervised learning with neural networks often exhibit
complicated, non-isolated sets of critical points; see for instance [Coo21, FGJ20, DK22, Woj23].

a deteriorated rate of convergence for the heavy ball method, both in continuous and in discrete

The derivation of convergence rates typically relies on crafting a suitable Lyapunov function
depending on the class of objective functions under consideration. The proof strategy then
consists of deriving a convergence rate for the Lyapunov function and, in a second step, proving
that this implies a convergence rate for the objective function value. In contrast to this, our
proof exploits a new differential geometric perspective on the PL-inequality; see [Fee20, RB24].
More specifically, if f is C2 and satisfies the PL-inequality (PL), then the set of minima forms
a manifold, and the function grows quadratically with the distance to this manifold. In that
sense, we consider the dynamics of the heavy ball method under a chart that separates the
space into tangential and normal directions. In the normal directions the manifold is strongly
attracting, whereas in the tangential directions there is no restoring force. First, we prove fast
convergence in the normal directions. This implies that the gradient of the objective function
vanishes exponentially fast, which, due to the friction in the dynamical system, then leads to
exponential convergence in the tangential directions as well. Therefore, the distance traveled
by heavy ball can be bounded, which in turn leads to convergence of the dynamical system. A
similar geometric approach has been used in [GW25] to show fast convergence rates of Nesterov’s
accelerated gradient descent in a non-convex setting.

2. Literature review
Early work on second-order gradient systems was carried out by Haraux and Jendoubi in
[HJ98]. They established convergence to equilibrium for dynamical systems of the form
¨xt + g(xt) = ∇f(xt)
where f is analytic and g is Lipschitz and coercive. Their framework includes the heavy ball
ODE as a special case and represents one of the first general convergence results for inertial
optimization dynamics beyond the convex setting. In a related direction, [AGR00] studied the
heavy ball ODE in Hilbert spaces for smooth functions that are either convex or Morse. Therein,
global existence of trajectories was established, weak convergence was obtained under convexity,
and asymptotic convergence to local minima was shown in the Morse case, while examples of
divergent solutions were also constructed. Subsequent works investigated second-order gradient
systems with asymptotically vanishing viscous damping, motivated by fast convergence in con-
vex optimization. In particular, [CEG09a, CEG09b, ACPR18] analyzed long-time behavior and
convergence properties of inertial dynamics with time-dependent damping coeﬀicients. It was
observed that, for a specific choice of vanishing damping, these second-order systems can be
interpreted as continuous-time limits of Nesterov’s accelerated gradient method [SBC16]. A re-
fined class of such models, incorporating Hessian-driven damping, was studied in [APR16], where
well-posedness and fast convergence were established in the convex regime. In [MSFAO25], the
stability of these dynamical systems under stochastic perturbation has been analyzed.
A broad line of research investigates the heavy ball ODE and heavy ball methods under non-
convex regularity assumptions; see e.g. [Sie19, ADR22, ADR23, AGV22, ADLR24]. Convergence
of discrete heavy ball schemes or stochastic variants has been established under various struc-
tural assumptions, including weak quasi-convexity or metric subregularity conditions [DKP20,
WLWH22, GK26, DK24, WKAD25]. Further, [GM09] studied gradient flow, heavy ball dynam-
ics and proximal inertial variants under quasi-convexity in Hilbert spaces, establishing weak
(and in symmetric cases strong) convergence. The work extends the convex theory presented in
[Alv00]. More recent developments have also examined inertial Newton-like systems in Morse
settings [Cas23], which include the heavy ball ODE as a limiting case and show almost-sure avoid-
ance of strict saddle points. A central theme in all these works are geometric assumptions that
lie strictly between strong convexity and general non-convexity. Metric subregularity emerged
originally from variational analysis [CJZ97], and have since been refined in the non-smooth and
semi-algebraic setting through the works of [DI15, DMN14].
To the best of our knowledge, [WLWH22] is the only work that shows an accelerated exponen-
tial rate of convergence for the heavy ball method beyond strongly convex objective functions.
However, the function class covered by Theorem 1 in [WLWH22] is still quite restrictive. In
particular, in addition to assuming the PL-inequality, they assume that, at each iteration, the
Hessian is a diagonal matrix. Moreover, they assume that there exists a global minimum x∗such
that, at each iteration n ∈N, when averaging the Hessian of f on the straight line from x∗to xn
one gets a strictly positive definite matrix. Note that assuming this property on a neighborhood

One of the most prominent assumptions on the objective function was introduced by Polyak
[Pol63] and Łojasiewicz [Łoj63] and is known as the Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) inequality (PL).
Due to its simplicity, generality and applicability for objective functions appearing in machine
learning, see e.g. [KNS16, KSA23, Gar23, Woj23, DK24], the PL-inequality is becoming a popular
assumption for proving fast convergence for optimization algorithms on non-convex objective
functions. It provides a gradient-dominance property ensuring global convergence of gradient-
based methods without convexity (see Definition 1.1). Related Łojasiewicz-type inequalities have
played a central role in non-convex optimization more broadly: the Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz (KL)
framework [ABS13b, ABS13a] provides a unified analysis for a wide class of descent algorithms
including proximal, forward–backward and inexact gradient methods. Important connections
between the quadratic growth condition (QG) and KL inequalities were developed in the works
[BNP+17, AC14].
This KL perspective underlies many results for non-smooth or composite optimization involv-
ing inertial or proximal terms. For instance, the iPiano algorithm [OCBP14] is a non-smooth
forward-backward generalization of the heavy ball method. In [OCBP14], the authors prove
global convergence to critical points under the KL property together with sublinear convergence
of the proximal gradient residual for this algorithm. A similar KL-based convergence analysis has
been developed for non-convex forward–backward splitting in [LFP16]. The same authors used
differential geometric and linearization arguments (similar to the ones used in the present work),
to show local linear convergence of forward-backward splitting methods; see [LFP17]. Moreover,
a local convergence analysis for the heavy ball and iPiano methods under local KL inequalities
have been developed in [Och18], showing local linear convergence to local minima. In the convex
composite setting, global acceleration is classically achieved by Nesterov-type methods, most
prominently the Fast Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm (FISTA) [BT09].
Finally, the role of KL-type and PL-type inequalities in infinite-dimensional settings relevant
for inverse problems has also been explored. In [GRV23], the authors derived a Łojasiewicz
inequality for certain convex, non-smooth quadratic functionals on Hilbert spaces and proved
linear convergence of forward–backward splitting.
