Note on surface-link of trivial components
Akio Kawauchi
Osaka Central Advanced Mathematical Institute, Osaka Metropolitan University
Sugimoto, Sumiyoshi-ku, Osaka 558-8585, Japan
kawauchi@omu..ac.jp
Abstract
As a previous result, it has shown that every sphere-link consisting of trivial com-
ponents is a ribbon sphere-link. In this note, it is shown that for every closed oriented
disconnected surface F with just one non-sphere component, every F-link consisting
of trivial components is a ribbon surface-link.
Further, it is shown that for every
closed oriented disconnected surface F containing at least two non-sphere components,
there exist a pair of a ribbon F-link and a non-ribbon F-link that consist of trivial
components and have meridian-preservingly isomorphic fundamental groups.
Keywords: Ribbon, Non-ribbon, Surface-link.
Mathematics Subject Classiﬁcation 2010: Primary 57Q45; Secondary 57N13
Let F be a (possibly disconnected) closed surface. An F-link in the 4-sphere S4 is the
image of a smooth embedding F →S4. When F is connected, it is also called an F-knot.
An F-link or F-knot for an F is called a surface-link or surface-knot in S4, respectively. If F
consists of some copies of the 2-sphere S2, then it is also called an S2-link. and an S2-knot
for F = S2. A trivial surface-link is a surface-link F which bounds disjoint handlebodies
smoothly embedded in S4. A ribbon surface-link is a surface-link F which is obtained from
a trivial nS2-link O for some n (where nS2 denotes the disjoint union of n copies of S2) by
surgery along an embedded 1-handle system, [6], [7], [8], [9], [11]. It was shown that every
S2-link consisting of trivial components is a ribbon S2-link, [10]. The following result is a
generalization of this result.
Theorem 1. Let F be a closed oriented disconnected surface with at most one non-sphere
component. Then every F-link L in S4 consisting of trivial components is a ribbon F-link
in S4.
Proof of Theorem 1. The case that F consists of only S2-components has been given, [10,
Theorem 1.5]. This proof is done by a similar method. Let F have S2-components and only

In the case that F has at least two non-sphere components, the following result is ob-
tained.
Theorem 2. Let F be any closed oriented disconnected surface with at least two non-sphere
components. Then there exist a pair of a ribbon F-link L and a non-ribbon F-link L′ in S4
that consist of trivial components and have meridian-preservingly isomorphic fundamental
groups.
Proof of Theorem 2. Let k ∪k′ be a non-splitable link in the interior of a 3-ball B such that

In the proof of Theorem 2, the diﬀeomorphism ∂τ of S × S1 coincides with Gluck’s non-
spin diﬀeomorphism of S2 × S1, [2]. The torus-link (M, TM) called a turned torus-link of
a link k ∪k′ in B is an analogy of a turned torus-knot of a knot in B, [1]. There is an
invariant of a surface-knot used to conﬁrm a non-ribbon surface-knot, [5]. This invariant is
easily generalized to an invariant of a surface-link, which can be also applied to conﬁrm that
L′ is a non-ribbon surface-link.
