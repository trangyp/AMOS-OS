Abstract
In this Article we analyze the algebraic properties of the asymptotic
dynamics of ﬁnite-dimensional open quantum systems in the Heisen-
berg picture. In particular, a natural product (Choi-Eﬀros product)
can be deﬁned in the asymptotic regime. Motivated by this structure,
we introduce a new space called the Choi-Eﬀros decoherence-free alge-
bra. Interestingly, this space is both a C*-algebra with respect to the
composition product, and a B*-algebra with respect to the Choi-Eﬀros
product. Moreover, such space admits a direct-sum decomposition reveal-
ing a clear relationship with the attractor subspace of the dynamics. In
particular, the equality between the attractor subspace and the Choi-
Eﬀros decoherence-free algebra is a necessary and suﬃcient condition
for a faithful dynamics. Finally, we show how all the ﬁndings do not
rely on complete positivity but on the much weaker Schwarz property.
1 Introduction
Quantum technologies are evolving so rapidly over the last few years that a
solid understanding of open-system theory, the theoretical framework of such
applications, is crucially needed. In particular, asymptotics of open quantum
systems has attracted a lot of interest by theoretical physicists [1, 63, 64], in
light of possible applications in quantum information processing [80], quantum
reservoir engineering [67, 76], and matrix product states [66].

At the same time, long-time behavior of open quantum systems has drawn
the attention of several mathematicians since the birth of the ﬁeld in the early
seventies [59]. In particular, after the seminal works by Gorini, Kossakowski,
Sudarshan [51], and Lindblad [61] on quantum dynamical semigroups, several
works by Evans [38], Spohn [73], Frigerio and Verri [46, 47] focused on the
existence and the uniqueness of a stationary state of this kind of dynamics.
The more recent papers by Fagnola and Rebolledo [40, 41] can be framed in
this research direction.
Motivated by the work on environmental decoherence by Blanchard and
Olkiewicz [12] (see also [15]), the asymptotic Heisenberg dynamics of open
quantum systems was also studied in terms of its multiplicative properties [16,
36, 42, 44], using the notion of decoherence-free algebra [38, 47].
In particular, a decomposition of the dynamics into reversible and stable
parts was sought [9, 44], following the seminal works by Jacobs [55], DeLeeuw
and Glicksberg [34, 35]. Incidentally, multiplicative properties of the dynamics
also turned out to have interesting connections with quantum error correc-
tion [22, 68]. Also, multiplicative features of more general maps, including
positive ones, were also taken into account in the literature [74].
With few exceptions [16], the majority of works adopting this approach
study Markovian continuous-time evolutions. Moreover, the dynamics is
usually supposed to be faithful.
A spectral approach was instead adopted by Wolf and Perez-Garcia [79],
Albert [1], and the authors [3, 4] in the Schr¨odinger picture and by Bhat et al.
[11] in the Heisenberg picture. This strategy deﬁnes the attractor subspace,
where the asymptotic dynamics takes place, in terms of the peripheral (uni-
modular) eigenvalues of the evolution. Notice that those works tackled the
problem of the asymptotics of open quantum systems without the assumption
of faithfulness or Markovianity. Moreover, Bhat et al. started to relate the
multiplicative and spectral approaches for the asymptotic evolution [10].
In this Article we focus on the asymptotic discrete-time dynamics of a
ﬁnite-dimensional open quantum system in the Heisenberg picture. Before
summarizing the ﬁndings of the present work, let us recall the guiding prin-
ciple behind the multiplicative strategy to the asymptotics addressed by the
mathematical community over the last ﬁfty years.
The seminal papers that introduce the decoherence-free algebra [12, 15]
are based on the observation that a ∗-automorphism on the space of bounded
operators on a Hilbert space is necessarily a unitary conjugation [75]. Since one
expects that the large time-dynamics of an open quantum system is a unitary-
like evolution, in the multiplicative perspective it is reasonable to identify the
asymptotic subspace with the largest subspace on which the dynamics acts as
a ∗-homomorphism, i.e. the decoherence-free algebra. Indeed, for Markovian
evolutions, taken into account in [12, 15], the restriction of the dynamics to the
decoherence-free algebra is a unitary conjugation, related to the Hamiltonian
part of the generator of the evolution [43].

However, it is worthwhile to note that this is not generally true in the
non-Markovian (even faithful) case as a consequence of a residual classical
asymptotic dynamics [3, 4]. This is in line with the fact that a ∗-automorphism
on a C∗-algebra is not necessarily a unitary conjugation.
Moreover, another complication emerges in the non-faithful case: the com-
position product equipping the initial algebra of observables may not be
appropriate in the asymptotic limit [2, 23] and, instead, the Choi-Eﬀros prod-
uct [21] is more suitable. Therefore, we will study the multiplicative properties
of a Heisenberg dynamics at large times with respect to this product.
In particular, we will introduce the Choi-Eﬀros decoherence-free algebra,
see Eq. (29), which generalizes the standard one by replacing the composi-
tion product with the Choi-Eﬀros product deﬁned by Eq. (13). Interestingly,
it turns out that this space is a C∗-algebra with respect to the composi-
tion product, and a B∗-algebra with respect to the Choi-Eﬀros product, see
Theorems 3.6 and 3.9, respectively.
In particular, we will see that the Choi-Eﬀros decoherence-free algebra
admits a direct-sum decomposition (Theorem 3.6), which naturally relates it to
the attractor subspace [Eq. (1)], where the large-time evolution of the system
occurs in the spectral perspective. Therefore, the Choi-Eﬀros decoherence-free
algebra always contains the attractor subspace [condition b) of Theorem 3.5]
and, interestingly, equality characterizes faithful evolutions (Theorem 3.11).
These properties reveal that, according to the algebraic structure of the attrac-
tor subspace (Theorem 2.3), the Choi-Eﬀros product is suitable for the study
of the multiplicative properties of the dynamics in the asymptotic limit. Also,
in the spirit of [10], these ﬁndings further bridge the gap between the spectral
and multiplicative methods to address the asymptotics.
Finally, we will show in Section 5 that all the results obtained in the present
Article exploit a much weaker property than complete positivity, called the
operator Schwarz inequality, see Eq. (12). Roughly speaking, the Schwarz prop-
erty is suﬃcient for the description of the asymptotics of an open quantum
system [47, 79]. Similarly, Schwarz positivity, rather than complete positivity,
turns out to be suﬃcient to obtain a number of monotonicity results [17, 18]
such as the data processing inequality [60], crucial in quantum information the-
ory [62]. Notice also that complete positivity may be replaced by this property
when we address the reversibility problem of quantum channels [56].
The Article is organised as follows. Section 2 recalls some preliminary con-
cepts needed in the following. In Section 3 a new space related to Heisenberg
asymptotics, called the Choi-Eﬀros decoherence-free algebra, is introduced and
its properties, as well as its relation with the attractor subspace, are discussed
in detail. In Subsection 3.1 we analyze the qubit case, while in Section 4 we
show the application of the general results to a couple of examples of qutrit sys-
tems. Section 5 shows that all the ﬁndings, discussed in the previous sections
for Heisenberg evolutions, continue to be valid for the much larger class of
Schwarz maps. After the conclusions (Section 6), we present in Appendix A an

2 Preliminaries
In this section we set up the notation and summarize the main results on the
algebraic properties of the asymptotic dynamics.
First, remember that quantum mechanics can be described in diﬀerent
frameworks. According to the standard Dirac-Schr¨odinger picture, the funda-
mental mathematical objects of the theory are states, described as unit rays
of a Hilbert space [37]. Observables are then introduced as self-adjoint (lin-
ear) operators on such space. One of the more fundamental descriptions is the
algebraic picture of quantum theory [14, 77]. In this case, one starts with the
algebra of observables, and then introduces states as linear functionals over it,
detached from any Hilbert space structure [39]. Other descriptions of quantum
mechanics could be considered, like the Schwinger picture [70], where quantum
mechanics is encoded in the transitions [29–31].
In this Article, we will mainly focus on the algebraic description, which
turns out to be convenient for analyzing the mathematical structure of the
asymptotics in the Heisenberg picture. In this picture, observables are deﬁned
as the self-adjoint elements of a C∗-algebra A, namely a Banach space with a
product ·, an involution ∗and a norm ∥∥satisfying the C∗-identity ∥a∗· a∥=
∥a∥2, a ∈A. We will frequently use the shorthand (A, ·, ∥· ∥, ∗).
One of the main results in the theory of C∗-algebras, which motivated
their deﬁnition, is the Gel’fand-Neumark theorem [49, 50]. It shows that
every C∗-algebra may be faithfully represented as a norm-closed ∗-subalgebra
of bounded operators on some Hilbert space. Throughout this work, we
will mostly consider the C∗-algebra B(H) of bounded operators on a ﬁnite-
dimensional Hilbert space H and its C∗-subalgebras. From now on we will drop
the symbol · in the equations for the composition product between bounded
operators over H. Moreover, in all the ∗-algebras we shall consider, the invo-
lution will be the adjoint in B(H), therefore we will simply denote a B∗- or
C∗-algebra A as (A, ·, ∥· ∥).
Once the observables have been deﬁned, states are introduced as linear
functionals over A, ω : A →C with ω(a∗a) ⩾0, a ∈A, and ∥ω∥= 1.
It is always possible to represent a state as a vector in a proper GNS (or
cyclic) representation πω on a Hilbert space Hω with ω(a) = ⟨Ωω|πω(a)Ωω⟩.
In particular, this representation is unique (up to a unitary equivalence). We
will see in the following sections how this representation can be used to obtain
(up to a ∗-automorphism) the attractor subspace as a suitable GNS Hilbert
space of the Choi-Eﬀros decoherence-free algebra.

2.1 Unital completely positive maps
The present subsection is devoted to the basic properties of the dynamics in
the Heisenberg picture, which is natural for the algebraic picture of quantum
theory, where observables are the primary objects.
In the discrete-time description of the dynamics, the evolution in the
Heisenberg picture of a ﬁnite-dimensional open quantum system is described
by a unital completely positive (UCP) map Φ on B(H), where B(H) is the
algebra of bounded operators on a d-dimensional Hilbert space H. Let ⟨·|·⟩be
the scalar product in H, which we will assume to be conjugate-linear in the
ﬁrst entry. Here B(H) plays the role of the algebra of observables of the sys-
tem undergoing the evolution. For a survey on positive and completely positive
maps in the ﬁnite-dimensional setting see e.g. [28]. If the system observable at
time t = 0 is A, its evolution at time t = n ∈N will be given by the action of
the n-fold composition Φn of the map Φ, i.e. A(n) = Φn(A).
The spectrum spect(Φ) of a UCP map Φ, i.e. the set of its eigenvalues,
satisﬁes the following three properties
a) 1 ∈spect(Φ);
b) λ ∈spect(Φ) ⇒¯λ ∈spect(Φ);
c) spect(Φ) ⊆{λ ∈C | |λ| ⩽1}.

2.3 Decoherence-free algebra
In this section we will recall the deﬁnition and several relevant properties of
the decoherence-free algebra. Inspired by these properties, we will introduce
in Section 3 the Choi-Eﬀros decoherence-free algebra. We will see that this
algebra has many interesting mathematical features, and in particular a clear
relationship with the attractor manifold.
Let us try to motivate the introduction of the decoherence-free algebra. It
is well-known that a unitary UCP map on B(H), which describes the Heisen-
berg dynamics of a closed quantum system, is a ∗-automorphism and, indeed,
the latter condition characterizes this class of maps, see e.g. [75, Proposition
3.1.7]. Thus, since the asymptotic evolution should resemble a closed-system
dynamics, the decoherence-free algebra of a UCP map Φ is deﬁned precisely as
the subset of B(H) where Φ acts as a ∗-homomorphism, namely:
N := {X ∈B(H) , Φn(Y X) = Φn(Y )Φn(X),
Φn(XY ) = Φn(X)Φn(Y ), ∀Y ∈B(H), ∀n ∈N}.
(20)
It is well-known that N is a C∗-subalgebra of B(H), see e.g. [16]. Indeed, N is
the largest subalgebra of B(H) on which Φn|N is a ∗-homomorphism. However,
as already observed in [16, Remark 1], Φ|N (and, consequently, Φn|N ) is not
a ∗-automorphism in general.
The space N can be characterized in a simpler way by using [78, Theorem
5.4].

5 Is complete positivity needed?
The answer is no: In this section we will generalize Theorem 2.3 and
Theorem 2.4 to Schwarz maps, showing that all the previous results are valid
for this larger class of maps. Therefore, complete positivity and, more gener-
ally, k-positivity with k ⩾2 is not used in their full power for characterizing
the asymptotic dynamics of an open quantum system, see [47, Section 1]
and [79, Section V] for an analogous comment in the Schr¨odinger picture of
the evolution.
A positive unital map Φ is called strongly positive [69], 1 1/2-positive [58],
or Schwarz [79], iﬀit satisﬁes the operator Schwarz inequality (12). (Note
that in the literature the unitality assumption is sometimes dropped in the
deﬁnition of Schwarz maps, see e.g. [72]). Similarly, we will call a positive
unital map obeying the ⋆-operator Schwarz inequality (28) a ⋆-Schwarz map.
The transposition map is an example of a positive unital map not obeying
Eq. (12). As underlined by Choi [19, Corollary 2.8], any 2-positive unital map
is a Schwarz map, and, more generally, a 2-positive map satisﬁes a generalized
operator Schwarz inequality, see [17, Corollary 7]. However, 2-positivity is not
necessary for (12) to hold, see [20, 27]. Furthermore, a concept lying between
the operator Schwarz inequality and 2-positivity was recently introduced [17].

Indeed, the result holds for any Schwarz map Φ (see [53, Theorem 2.3] or [48,

Proposition 5.1 Let Φ be a Schwarz map. Then its attractor subspace Attr(Φ)

6 Conclusions
In this Article we have explored the large-time dynamics of ﬁnite-dimensional
open quantum systems in the Heisenberg picture. In particular, following an
algebraic approach, we introduced a new space N⋆, called the Choi-Eﬀros
decoherence-free algebra of a UCP map Φ, obtained from the standard one,
see Eq. (20), by replacing the composition product with the Choi-Eﬀros prod-
uct (13). Such deﬁnition was motivated by Theorem 2.4, which establishes that
the appropriate product in the asymptotic limit is the Choi-Eﬀros one. The
two most important features of this space, which do not hold for the standard
decoherence-free algebra N, are the following:
1. it has a direct-sum decomposition (Theorem 3.6), implying that, in par-
ticular, N⋆⊇Attr(Φ), the attractor subspace of Φ [condition b) of
Theorem 3.5];
2. Attr(Φ) = N⋆is equivalent to the faithfulness of Φ (Theorem 3.11).
In particular, the clear relationship between N⋆and Attr(Φ) sheds light on the
interplay between the spectral and multiplicative approaches to the asymp-
totics. As a consequence of Theorem 3.6, N⋆is a C∗-algebra with respect to
the composition product ·, the operator norm ∥· ∥, and the adjoint ∗of B(H)
and, indeed, by deﬁnition, N⋆is the largest C∗-subalgebra of B(H) on which
(Φn)n∈N acts as a ∗-homomorphism with respect to the Choi-Eﬀros product.
Finally, as shown in Section 5, our results do not depend on the complete
positivity of the open-system Heisenberg dynamics Φ, but only on the operator
Schwarz inequality (12), a quite less strict requirement. Therefore, given the
diﬀerent applications of this property in quantum dynamics, also mentioned in
the Introduction, it would be interesting to understand the physical interpre-
tation of the operator Schwarz inequality, beyond its interesting mathematical
form and its physical implications.
