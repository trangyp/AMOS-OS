Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) has made
significant progress in a number of profes-
sional fields, including medicine, law, and
finance.
However, in traditional Chinese
medicine (TCM), there are challenges such as
the essential differences between theory and
modern medicine, the lack of specialized cor-
pus resources, and the fact that relying only on
supervised fine-tuning may lead to overconfi-
dent predictions. To address these challenges,
we propose a two-stage training approach that
combines continuous pre-training and super-
vised fine-tuning. A notable contribution of our
study is the processing of a 2GB corpus dedi-
cated to TCM, constructing pre-training and in-
struction fine-tuning datasets for TCM, respec-
tively. In addition, we have developed Qibo-
Benchmark, a tool that evaluates the perfor-
mance of LLM in the TCM on multiple dimen-
sions, including subjective, objective, and three
TCM NLP tasks. The medical LLM trained
with our pipeline, named Qibo, exhibits sig-
nificant performance boosts. Compared to the
baselines, the average subjective win rate is
63%, the average objective accuracy improved
by 23% to 58%, and the Rouge-L scores for the
three TCM NLP tasks are 0.72, 0.61, and 0.55.
Finally, we propose a pipline to apply Qibo to
TCM consultation and demonstrate the model
performance through an extensive case study.

Introduction
Recently, significant advances have been made in
LLM, such as ChatGPT (OpenAI, 2022), GPT-4
(Achiam and et al., 2023), and GPT-4o (OpenAI,
2024). These models can understand and answer a
wide range of questions and outperform humans in
many general-purpose areas. The open source com-
munity soon introduced high-performance LLMs
as well, such as LLaMA (Touvron and al., 2023),
Alpaca (Taori et al., 2023), Bloom (Workshop and
al., 2022), and Falcon (Almazrouei et al., 2023).
To fill the gaps in the Chinese language processing
capabilities of these models, researchers have also
introduced more powerful Chinese language mod-
els (Cui et al., 2023; Du et al., 2021; Zhang et al.,
2022; Baichuan, 2023; Bai et al., 2023).
However, while these general-purpose LLMs
perform well in many tasks, their performance in
specific areas of specialization e.g., the biomed-
ical domain is often limited due to a lack of do-
main expertise (Zhao et al., 2023). The intricacies
and specialization of knowledge in the biomed-
ical domain place higher accuracy and safety re-
quirements on the successful development of LLMs
(Singhal et al., 2022). Similar to that, Traditional
Chinese Medicine (TCM) also encounters compa-
rable challenges.
In the realm of TCM, several medical LLMs
have been proposed (Li et al., 2023; Zhang et al.,
2023; Xiong et al., 2023).
These LLMs are
mainly trained through supervised fine-tuning
(SFT). While SFT is crucial for acquiring domain-
specific knowledge, it often results in limited
knowledge infusion and can lead to overconfi-
dent generalizations (Luo et al., 2023).As well as
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback
(RLHF) is a popular method to offset some of
the limitations of SFT, but it is very complex and
requires rigorous hyperparameter tuning (Rama-
murthy et al., 2022). Han et al. (2021); Zhou et al.
(2023) have shown that almost all knowledge is
learned in pre-training, which is a crucial stage for
accumulating basic knowledge.
Although many works have existed on LLMs
in TCM, and these works have further advanced
the development of large models in TCM, yet the
characteristics of TCM domain are neglected by
them. They have never considered the essential
differences between TCM and modern medical the-
ories. Unlike modern medicine, patients with the
same disease may present with different signs and
symptoms (症) and thus receive different treatments,

These differences in theoretical knowledge
frameworks between TCM and modern medicine
determine the different methods and data required
for modeling them. Therefore, This paper aims to
develop a language model with TCM expertise
to address the limitations of current models that
merely integrate basic medicine knowledge.The
main contributions of this paper are as follows:
1. We have trained a new TCM LLM Qibo
based on Chinese-LLaMA in two stages from
pre-training to SFT.
2. We provide a data processing scheme based
on different granularity rules to further
improve the quality of TCM training corpus.
3. We constructe an assessment benchmark to
validate the multiple TCM capabilities from
multiple subjective and objective dimensions,
bridging the gaps in TCM assessment
benchmarks.
4. Finally, a pipeline is designed to apply the
model to TCM consultation and syndrome dif-
ferentiation to improve the interpretability and
performance of the diagnosis process. The
performance of Qibo is demonstrated through
extensive experiments and case studies.

Related Works
2.1
Large Language Models
The remarkable achievements of Large Language
Models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT (OpenAI, 2022),
GPT-4 (Achiam and et al., 2023) and GPT-4o (Ope-
nAI, 2024), have garnered substantial attention,
which relies on large-scale pre-training. While Ope-
nAI has yet to publicly disclose its training strategy
or weights, the rapid emergence of open source
LLMs like LLaMA (Touvron and al., 2023), Alpaca
(Taori et al., 2023), Vicuna (Chiang et al., 2023),
Bloom (Workshop and al., 2022), and Falcon (Al-
mazrouei et al., 2023). To compensate for their
initially limited Chinese language proficiency, they
were successfully trained to enhance their skills in
Chinese through large Chinese language datasets,
using next-token prediction as a key training ob-
jective to understand context and predict the next
word. Chinese LLaMA and Chinese Alpaca (Cui
et al., 2023) continually pre-trained and optimized
with Chinese data and vocabulary. Ziya-LLaMA
(Zhang et al., 2022) completed the RLHF process,
enhancing instruction following ability and safety.
In addition, there have been notable attempts to
build efficient Chinese LLMs from scratch, such
as BaiChuan (Baichuan, 2023), Qwen (Bai et al.,
2023), and GLM (Du et al., 2021).
2.2
LLMs in Medical Domain
Large models generally perform best in medical
contexts demanding complex knowledge and high
precision. Attempts to improve this include MedAl-
paca (Han et al., 2023) and ChatDoctor (Yunxiang

et al., 2023), which employe continuous training,
and Med-PaLM (Singhal et al., 2022), and Med-
PaLM2 (Singhal et al., 2023), receiving favourable

et al., 2023), which used extensive Chinese medical

base, and BenTsao (Wang et al., 2023), utilizing

Zhongjing (Yang et al., 2023), which is a Chi-

showcasing enhanced performance and more re-
fined outputs in medical applications, owing to
its innovative multi-stage training approach. It
also curates a 3GB dataset of TCM, encompassing
medical question answering, plain text, knowledge
graph, and dialogue.

3.2
Training Phase
Qibo undergoes through two stages of training:
Continuous Pre-training and Supervised Instruc-
tion Fine-Tuning. The former gained basic under-
standing of TCM, while the latter gained the ability
to follow and answer TCM instructions.
3.2.1
Continuous Pre-training
High-quality pre-trained corpora can greatly im-
prove the performance of LLM and even break the
scaling law to some extent (Gunasekar et al., 2023).
Considering the complexity and breadth of the med-
ical domain, data diversity and high quality need
to be emphasized. It contains a wealth of knowl-
edge and skills that require comprehensive training
similar to that of specialized physicians. Relying
on medical textbooks alone is not enough, as they
can only provide basic theoretical knowledge. In
the real world, it takes medical experience, pro-
fessional acumen and intuition to understand the
specific condition of patient and make informed
decisions.
For this reason, we collect a variety of authentic
and relevant textual data, mainly including modern
medical textbooks, TCM textbooks, TCM prescrip-
tion datasets, TCM reading comprehension data,
TCM treatment plan canons, TCM ancient books,
encyclopedia of TCM, and a number of other cor-

3.2.2
Supervised Instruction Fine-Tuning
Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is a key stage in
making LLMs conversationally competent. With
high-quality doctor-patient dialogue data, the
model can effectively call upon the medical knowl-
edge accumulated in pre-training to understand and
respond to user queries. Over-reliance on refined
data from ChatGPT often mimics the speech pat-
terns, which may lead to a breakdown of intrinsic
abilities rather than learning substantive abilities
(Shumailov et al., 2023; Gudibande et al., 2023).
Although large amounts of refined data can quickly
improve conversational fluency, medical accuracy
is paramount. Therefore, we avoid using only re-
fined data. We use four types of data in the SFT
phase and convert them into the conversation for-
mat of Alpaca (Cui et al., 2023).
TCM Single-turn Conversation Data: In order to

improve the dialogue capability in TCM, we use
a dialogue dataset of single-argument dialogue in-
structions ChatMed-TCM. The dialogue capability
of the model can be significantly improved by fine-
tuning the monologue dialogue instructions.
TCM Multi-turn Conversation Data: The abil-
ity to engage in multi-turn dialogues is essential

CMtMedQA (Yang et al., 2023), and mix it into
the fine-tuned dataset.
CMtMedQA is a large-
scale, multi-turn TCM question-answering dataset
suitable for LLM training, significantly enhancing
the capability for multi-turn conversations. This
dataset encompasses 14 medical departments and
over 10 scenarios, featuring numerous proactive
inquiry statements that encourage the model to ini-
tiate questions, a crucial aspect of TCM consulta-
tions.
TCM NLP Tasks Instruction Data: A wide range
of tasks can improve the zero-point generalization
ability of the model (Sanh et al., 2021). To pre-
vent over-fitting medical conversation tasks, we
convert all TCM NLP task data (e.g., prescription
entity recognition, symptom identification, reading
comprehension) into the instruction conversation
format, thus improving its generalization ability.
General Medical-related Dialogue Data: In or-
der to prevent catastrophic forgetting of previous
general dialogue abilities after incremental train-
ing (Aghajanyan et al., 2021), we include sim-
ple dialogue related to the medical topic section.
This not only reduces forgetting, but also enhances
model the understanding of the medical domain,
which also contain modifications related to the self-
perception for model.

3.3
TCM Consultation and Syndrome
Differentiation
In order to further enhance the ability to use TCM
models for consultation, we simulate the process
of TCM consultation and designe a set of retrieval
enhancement based on an external knowledge base
of TCM to construct a thinking chain method for
TCM consultation prompts. This method starts
from the main complaint, inquires about syndrome
related symptoms, prompts and guides users to
pay attention to other abnormal situations in the
body, and combines information such as the bowel
movements, sleep conditions, and allergies to make
a comprehensive dialectical prescription.
This method utilizes external professional knowl-
edge to guide the model in asking patients, collect-
ing consultation information, further explaining
relevant consultation principles, increasing the in-
terpretability and credibility of the consultation.
On the other hand, by using multiple rounds of
comprehensive information to judge the condition
of patients, the accuracy of the consultation can be
further improved. The specific process is shown
in the bottom left part of Figure 2, and some ex-
amples of a single consultation are shown in the
bottom right part. Based on the initial judgment
of the chief complaint, the model identified “defi-
ciency of both Qi and blood syndrome” (气血两
虚证). After multiple rounds of questioning with
the patient to obtain more relevant information, it
is determined that the “Qi and blood deficiency
syndrome” is accompanied by “spleen and kidney
yang deficiency syndrome” (脾肾阳虚证). The ex-
ample of detailed consultation information can be

4.2
Baselines
In order to evaluate our model, we chose a series
of LLMs with different parameter scales as bench-
marks for comparison, including generalized LLMs
and medical LLMs.
ChatGPT (OpenAI, 2022): a well-known LLM
with about 175B parameters. Although not specifi-
cally trained for the medical domain, it shows im-
pressive performance in the medical conversation
task.
Chinese-LLaMA (Cui et al., 2023): this is a
fully trained Chinese generalized LLM and is our
base model for comparing performance improve-
ments.
BenTsao (Wang et al., 2023): the first Chi-
nese medical large-scale model, based on Chinese-
LLaMA (Cui et al., 2023) and fine-tuned on an
8K-scale medical conversation dataset.
DoctorGLM (Xiong et al., 2023): a Chinese
medical large-scale model based on ChatGLM-6B
(Du et al., 2021) with fine-tuning on a large amount
of medical guidance data.
HuatuoGPT (Zhang et al., 2023): based on
the previous best LLM of TCM implemented in
Bloomz-7b1-mt (Muennighoff et al., 2022), which
is fine-tuned on a large set of medical instructions
of 25M size using SFT (Li et al., 2023) and fur-
ther optimized by ChatGPT-based reinforcement
learning for further optimization.
ZhongJing (Yang et al., 2023): Ziya-LLaMA-
based LLM for Chinese medicine, which imple-
ments the entire training pipeline from pre-training,
SFT, to RLHF, and enhances the multi-turn conver-
sation capability using 70, 000 multi-turn conver-
sation data.

Evaluation

4.3.1
Subjective Evaluation
We collect and organize 150 TCM-related ques-
tions for experiments, and evaluate the TCM LLM
dialogues in terms of three dimensions: profession-
alism, safety, and fluency, and use the win rate,
tie rate, and loss rate as the measurement crite-
ria, respectively. The assessment combines both
human and AI components. Due to the complex-
ity of the safety assessment (Wang et al., 2022),
we use assessments from medical experts as sam-
ples for evaluation using GPT-4. For the simpler
dimensions of professionalism and fluency, we uti-
lized GPT-4 (Sun et al., 2023; Chiang et al., 2023)
for scoring to save human resources. The specific
meanings of safety, professionalism, and fluency
are as follows (Yang et al., 2023):
Safety: (1) Must provide scientifically accurate
medical knowledge, especially in cases of disease
diagnosis, medication recommendations, etc.; (2)
must admit ignorance of unknown knowledge; (3)
must ensure patient safety; (4) must refuse to an-
swer information or advice that could cause harm;
(5) must comply with medical ethics while respect-
ing patient choice and refuse to answer if violated.
Professionalism: (1) Must have an accurate under-
standing of the problems and needs from patients
in order to provide relevant responses and advice;
(2) must explain complex medical knowledge so
that the patient can understand; (3) and must be
proactive in asking for information about the con-

4.4
Results and Limitations
The results of the subjective assessment are shown
in Figure 3, and the results of the objective assess-
ment are shown in Figure 4, and the results of the
TCM NLP task are shown in Table 3. The results
show that Qibo can achieve excellent results in
all three dimensions of the subjective assessment,
and the best results in the objective assessment.
In the TCM NLP task, although the model does
not achieve the same level of performance as the
optimal method for the task, it nevertheless out-
performs other medical models. It consistently
outperforms the baseline models.
Ablation experiments were conducted on vari-
ous Chinese base models using distinct datasets,
thereby validating the efficacy of the dataset. The
findings are detailed in Table 5 in Appendix C,
indicating an average accuracy improvement of
22% in TCM multiple-choice questions. Addi-
tionally, this result suggests that, within the realm
of TCM, Chinese-LLaMA outperforms ChatGLM
and BaiChuan as the Chinese base model.
Tables 7 and 8 present the results of the few-shot
testing for Qibo-7B and 13B, respectively. While
some subjects exhibited performance enhancement
with the increase in samples, the optimal perfor-
mance is nearly always achieved with the baseline
prompts. We speculate that prompts incorporating
few-shot learning may disrupt the task, or that large
models with fewer parameters may not possess
robust few-shot learning capabilities. Naturally,
this conclusion requires further validation across a
broader range of tasks.
Table 9 presents the responses from 6 baseline
models. The results indicate that Erien, GPT-3.5,
and GPT-4 can effectively address the question
through the use of Retrieval Augumented Genera-
tion (RAG). While the responses from Zhongjing
and BenTsao contain a little of information related
to the question, the assistance that they offered
is limited on it; in contrast, HuatuoGPT delivered
more comprehensive medical advice. Other models
adequately listed various medications but lacked
analysis of diverse scenarios. Our model not only
offered pertinent recommendations for TCM but
also provided differentiated suggestions for addi-
tional symptoms patients might exhibit.
Table 10 demonstrates the dialectical process
of using prompts to guide the model in inquiring
about diagnostic syndromes from patients. Based
on the dialectical outcomes, combined with allergy
considerations, it makes sound recommendations
for TCM. In the case, an initial symptom diagno-
sis is based on the principal complaint, followed
by more precise assessments as more information
became available. With each round of dialogue,
inquiries and assessments are refined using perti-
nent professional knowledge, prompting patients to
focus on additional symptoms, thus enhancing the
interpret ability and credibility of the diagnostic
dialectic. The case study establishes a foundation
for further exploration into the potential of large
models in relation to TCM capabilities.
The following are our main observations and
conclusions from the experimental results: Com-
pared with the other baseline models, our model
has excellent performance in constructing TCM
benchmarks. Our model outperforms other medical
models in both subjective and objective evaluations.
Although the performance NLP tasks is not as good
as the methods specifically designed for this task,
it can still achieve certain results in the correspond-
ing task and outperform other models. In addition,
scale effects still exist, and models trained with
more data and a larger number of parameters often
perform better. For example, Qibo-13B has higher
accuracy in evaluating multiple-choice questions

Limitations: Despite these achievements, we also
recognize the limitations of the model. (1) Apart
from the discussion above which lacks the ability
of few shot learning, accurately recognize char-
acter positions, and perform well in NLP tasks
like the best traditional methods, Qibo cannot guar-

information to provide more comprehensive and
accurate healthcare. Despite its limitations, Qibo
remains primarily a research tool rather than a sub-
stitute for professional medical advice.

Conclusion
We present Qibo, an LLM in the traditional Chi-
nese medical domain, which is implemented from
pre-training to SFT. Its performance exceeds that
of other open-source Chinese medical models in
TCM, and is comparable to models with signifi-
cantly more parameters. We have collected high-
quality training corpus data in TCM and con-
structed Qibo-Banchmark, an evaluation bench-
mark, to address the evaluation gap. Furthermore,
a pipeline is designed to apply the model to TCM
diagnosis and dialectics, with the aim of improving
the interpretability and performance of the diag-
nostic process. Finally, sufficient experiments have
validated the excellent performance of our model
in TCM.
