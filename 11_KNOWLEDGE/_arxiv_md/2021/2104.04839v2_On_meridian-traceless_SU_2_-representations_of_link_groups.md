ON MERIDIAN-TRACELESS SU(2)–REPRESENTATIONS OF

YI XIE AND BOYU ZHANG
Abstract. Suppose L is a link in S3. We show that π1(S3 −L) admits an
irreducible meridian-traceless representation in SU(2) if and only if L is not
the unknot, the Hopf link, or a connected sum of Hopf links. As a corollary,
π1(S3 −L) admits an irreducible representation in SU(2) if and only if L is
neither the unknot nor the Hopf link.
This result generalizes a theorem of
Kronheimer and Mrowka to the case of links.
1. Introduction
The geometric and topological properties of a 3–manifold can be strongly re-
ﬂected by its fundamental group.
One approach to understand the fundamen-
tal group is to study its representations into linear groups such as SU(2) and
SL(2, C). Many topological invariants are deﬁned by considering such represen-
tations, such as the Casson invariant [AM90], the Casson-Lin invariant [Lin92],
and the A-polynomial [CCG+94].
The following is a natural question about the fundamental groups of 3-manifolds
and is a question on Kirby’s problem list [Kir95].
Question 1.1 ([Kir95, 3.105(A)]). Suppose Y is an integer homology 3-sphere with
π1(Y ) ̸= 0, does π1(Y ) always admit an irreducible representation in SU(2)?
Remark 1.2. There exist closed 3-manifolds Y which are not integer homology
spheres such that π1(Y ) admits no irreducible representation in SU(2), see [SZ21,
Theorem 1.6] or the remark below [Kir95, 3.105(A)].
Positive answers to Question 1.1 have been obtained for many diﬀerent families
of integer homology 3-spheres: the Dehn surgeries of non-trivial knots with cer-
tain conditions on the surgery slopes [KM04b,KM04a,Lin16,BS19], the branched
double covers of S3 over knots with determinant 1 [Zen17], the splicing of two
knot complements [Zen18], Stein ﬁllable 3–manifolds [BS18], and toroidal integer
homology 3–spheres [LPCZ21, BS21]. A complete answer to the existence prob-
lem of irreducible SU(2)–representations is obtained for non-hyperbolic geometric
closed 3–manifolds [SZ21]. There are also several related results for 4-manifolds
(see [DLVVW19,Tan19,Dae20,DS20]).
It is also natural to ask about non-closed 3–manifolds. The following question
is on Kirby’s problem list [Kir95, Problem 1.86] and is attributed to Cooper.
Question 1.3 (Cooper). Suppose K is a non-trivial knot in S3, is it true that
π1(S3 −K) always has an irreducible representation in SL(2, C)?

Question 1.3 was positively answered by Kronheimer and Mrowka [KM04b] as
a corollary of their proof for the Property P conjecture. Moreover, in [KM10b],
Kronheimer and Mrowka proved the following stronger result.
Theorem 1.4 ([KM10b, Corollary 7.17]). If K is a non-trivial knot in S3, then
π1(S3 −K) admits an irreducible representation in SU(2) such that the meridian
of K is mapped to a traceless element in SU(2).
From now on, we will call the fundamental groups of knot complements as knot
groups, and call the fundamental groups of link complements as link groups.1 We say
that an SU(2)–representation of a knot group or a link group is meridian-traceless,
if the meridian of every component is mapped to a traceless element in SU(2) (see
Deﬁnition 2.1).
This paper generalizes Theorem 1.4 to the case of links. The main result is the
following theorem.
Theorem 1.5. Suppose L ⊂S3 is a link. Then π1(S3 −L) admits an irreducible
meridian-traceless representation in SU(2) if and only if L is not the unknot, the
Hopf link, or a connected sum of Hopf links.
As a corollary, we obtain the following result about general SU(2)–representations
(without the meridian-traceless condition).
Corollary 1.6. Suppose L ⊂S3 is a link. Then π1(S3 −L) admits an irreducible
representation in SU(2) if and only if L is neither the unknot nor the Hopf link.
Remark 1.7. It is already known that the only links with abelian link groups are
the unknot and the Hopf link (see [Kaw96, Section 6.3]). Corollary 1.6 recovers
this result, and also shows that every non-abelian link group admits an irreducible
SU(2)–representation.
Assuming the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH), Kuperberg proved that
the unknot recognition problem lies in the complexity class coNP [Kup14, Theo-
rem 1.1]. The same result was later proved by Lackenby [Lac16] without assuming
the GRH. Kuperberg’s proof is based on the positive answer to Question 1.3. The
argument was later used by Zentner to prove that the 3-sphere recognition prob-
lem is in coNP (assuming the GRH) [Zen18, Theorem 11.2]. Following the same
argument, we have the following corollary of Theorem 1.5.
Corollary 1.8. Suppose L ⊂S3 is given by a link diagram, and assume the gener-
alized Riemann hypothesis. Then the assertion that L is the unknot, the Hopf link
or a connected sum of Hopf links is in coNP.

L has the minimal possible rank (Propositions 3.5 and 3.7). We will then prove
several topological properties for links with minimal instanton Floer homology us-

2. The main result
Suppose G is a group; a representation ρ : G →SU(2) is called irreducible if the
image of ρ is non-abelian. Otherwise, ρ is called reducible or abelian.
Notice that the set of traceless matrices is a conjugacy class in SU(2). Let M
be a path-connected space, ρ : π1(M) →SU(2) be a representation, and γ be
an oriented closed loop in M without a base point. Then γ deﬁnes an element
[γ] ∈π1(M) up to conjugacy. We say that ρ maps γ to a traceless matrix if ρ([γ])
is traceless.
We introduce the following deﬁnition.
Deﬁnition 2.1. Let L ⊂S3 be a link. A representation ρ : π1(S3 −L) →SU(2) is
called meridian-traceless if it maps all meridians of L to traceless matrices.
The main result of this article is the following theorem.
Theorem 1.5. Suppose L ⊂S3 is a link. Then π1(S3 −L) admits an irreducible
meridian-traceless SU(2)–representation if and only if L is not the unknot, the Hopf
link, or a connected sum of Hopf links.
Theorem 1.5 has the following immediate corollary.
Corollary 1.6. Suppose L ⊂S3 is a link. Then π1(S3 −L) admits an irreducible
SU(2)–representation if and only if L is neither the unknot nor the Hopf link.
Proof of Corollary 1.6 assuming Theorem 1.5. If L is the unknot or the Hopf link,
then π1(S3−L) is abelian, so it does not admit any irreducible SU(2)–representation.

If L is a connected sum of at least two Hopf links, then there exist two compo-

is the free group generated by two elements and hence has an irreducible SU(2)–
representation. Since the embedding of S3−L into S3−K1−K2 induces a surjection
on π1, the group π1(S3 −L) also has an irreducible SU(2)–representation.
If L is not the unknot, the Hopf link, or a connected sum of Hopf links, then by
Theorem 1.5, the fundamental group π1(S3−L) has a meridian-traceless irreducible
SU(2)–representation.

Now we prove Corollary 1.8.
Corollary 1.8. Suppose L ⊂S3 is given by a link diagram, and assume the gen-
eralized Riemann hypothesis (GRH). Then the assertion that L is the unknot, the
Hopf link or a connected sum of Hopf links is in coNP.
Proof. The proof is adapted from [Kup14] with minor changes.
Given a link diagram of L, we obtain the Wirtinger presentation of π1(S3 −L)
whose length l depends polynomially on the number of crossings. If L is not the
unknot, the Hopf link, or a connected sum of Hopf links, then by Theorem 1.5,
there is a meridian-traceless homomorphism
ρC : π1(S3 −L) →SU(2) ⊂SL(2, C)
with non-abelian image.
By the Wirtinger presentation, meridian-traceless representations of π1(S3 −L)
in SL(2, C) are given by solutions to a system of polynomial equations with integer
coeﬃcients. Assuming the GRH, Kuperberg [Kup14, Theorem 3.3] proved that if
a system of polynomial equations with integer coeﬃcients has a solution over C,
then it also has a solution over Z/p for some prime p, such that log p is bounded
from above by a polynomial on the number of equations, the number of variables,
the number of digits of the maximum degree of the equations, and the number of
digits of the maximum absolute value of the coeﬃcients. All these data are bounded
from above by a polynomial of l. As a consequence, there is a meridian-traceless
homomorphism
ρp : π1(S3 −L) →SL(2, Z/p),
where p is a prime number, such that log p is bounded from above by a polynomial
of l. Moreover, one can further require that ρp has a non-abelian image using the
“Rabinowitsch trick” described in [Kup14, Theorem 3.4].

3. Singular Instanton Floer homology
This section proves several general properties of singular instanton Floer homol-
ogy. Suppose L is a link in S3, we use |L| to denote the number of components of
L. Pick a base point p ∈L, the reduced singular instanton Floer homology I♮(L, p)
is deﬁned by Kronheimer and Mrowka in [KM11a]. Roughly speaking, I♮(L, p) is
deﬁned as the Morse homology of the (perturbed) Chern-Simons functional on a
conﬁguration space of orbifold connections. Let mp be a small meridian around
p, let ω be a small arc joining mp and p, let µ(ω) be a meridian of ω, and let µi
(i = 1, · · · , |L|+1) be the meridians of the components of L♮:= L∪mp. We require
µ|L|+1 to be the meridian of mp. The critical set of the unperturbed Chern-Simons
functional is given by
R♮(L, p) := {ρ : π1(S3 −(L♮∪ω)) →SU(2)|
trace ρ(µi) = 0 for all i, ρ(µ(ω)) = −id}/conjugations.
