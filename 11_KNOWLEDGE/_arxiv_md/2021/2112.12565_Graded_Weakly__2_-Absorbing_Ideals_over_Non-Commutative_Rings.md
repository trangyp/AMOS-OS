Graded Weakly 2-Absorbing Ideals over
Non-Commutative Rings
Azzh Saad Alshehry, Jebrel Habeb, Rashid Abu-Dawwas, and Ahmad Alrawabdeh
Abstract. For commutative graded rings, the concept of graded 2-absorbing
(graded weakly 2-absorbing) ideals was introduced and examined by Al-Zoubi,
Abu-Dawwas and C¸eken (Hacettepe Journal of Mathematics and Statistics, 48 (3)
(2019), 724-731) as a generalization of the concept of graded prime (graded weakly
prime) ideals. Up to now, research on these topics mainly concentrated on commu-
tative graded rings. On the other hand, graded prime ideals over non-commutative
graded rings have been introduced and examined by Abu-Dawwas, Bataineh and
Al-Muanger (Vietnam Journal of Mathematics, 46 (3) (2018), 681-692). As a gen-
eralization of graded prime ideals over non-commutative graded rings, the concept
of graded 2-absorbing ideals over non-commutative graded rings has been intro-
duced and investigated by Abu-Dawwas, Shashan and Dagher (WSEAS Transac-
tions on Mathematics, 19 (2020), 232-238). Recently, graded weakly prime ideals
over non-commutative graded rings have been introduced and studied by Alshehry
and Abu-Dawwas (Communications in Algebra, 49 (11) (2021), 4712-4723). In this
article, we introduce and study the concept of graded weakly 2-absorbing ideals
as a generalization of graded weakly prime ideals in a non-commutative graded
ring, and show that many of the results in commutative graded rings also hold in
non-commutative graded rings.

For commutative graded rings, graded 2-absorbing ideals, which are a generaliza-
tion of graded prime ideals, were introduced and investigated in [4]. Recall from [5]
that a proper graded ideal P of a commutative graded ring R is said to be a graded
weakly prime ideal of R if whenever x, y ∈h(R) and 0 ̸= xy ∈P, then either x ∈P
or y ∈P. Also from [4] a proper graded ideal P of a commutative graded ring R is
called a graded 2-absorbing ideal of R if whenever x, y, z ∈h(R) and xyz ∈P, then
either xy ∈P or xz ∈P or yz ∈P. The concept of a graded weakly 2-absorbing
ideal of a commutative graded ring R was introduced in [4]. A proper graded ideal
P of a commutative graded ring R is called a graded weakly 2-absorbing ideal of
R if whenever x, y, z ∈h(R) and 0 ̸= xyz ∈P, then either xy ∈P or xz ∈P or
yz ∈P.
Graded prime ideals over non-commutative graded rings have been introduced
and examined by Abu-Dawwas, Bataineh and Al-Muanger in [1]. A proper graded
ideal P of R is said to be graded prime if whenever I and J are graded ideals of
R such that IJ ⊆P, then I ⊆P or J ⊆P. As a generalization of graded prime
ideals over non-commutative graded rings, the concept of graded 2-absorbing ideals
over non-commutative graded rings has been introduced and investigated by Abu-
Dawwas, Shashan and Dagher in [2]. A proper graded ideal P of R is said to be
graded 2-absorbing if whenever x, y, z ∈h(R) such that xRyRz ⊆P, then xy ∈P
or yz ∈P or xz ∈P. Recently, graded weakly prime ideals over non-commutative
graded rings have been introduced and studied by Alshehry and Abu-Dawwas in
[3]. A proper graded ideal P of R is said to be graded weakly prime if whenever
I and J are graded ideals of R such that 0 ̸= IJ ⊆P, then I ⊆P or J ⊆P.
In this article, we are following [7] to introduce and study the concept of graded
weakly 2-absorbing ideals as a generalization of graded weakly prime ideals in a
non-commutative graded ring, and show that many of the results in commutative
graded rings also hold in non-commutative graded rings.
2. Graded Weakly 2-Absorbing Ideals

Undoubtedly, every graded 2-absorbing ideal of a graded ring R is a graded
weakly 2-absorbing ideal of R. If R is any graded ring, then P = {0} is a graded
weakly 2-absorbing ideal of R by deﬁnition. On the other hand, P = {0} is not
necessary to be a graded 2-absorbing ideal, check the following example:
Example 2.3. Consider R = Z8[i] and G = Z2. Then R is G-graded by R0 = Z8
and R1 = iZ8. Undeniably, P = {0} is not a graded 2-absorbing ideal of R since

Lemma 2.4. Let P be a graded ideal in a graded ring R. Assume that P is a
graded weakly prime ideal of R. If I and J are graded right (left) ideals of R such
that 0 ̸= IJ ⊆P, then I ⊆P or J ⊆P.
Proof. Suppose that I and J are graded right (left) ideals of R such that
0 ̸= IJ ⊆P. Let (I) and (J) be the graded ideals generated by I and J respectively.
Then 0 ̸= (I)(J) ⊆P, whence I ⊆(I) ⊆P or J ⊆(J) ⊆P.
□
Lemma 2.5. Let P be a graded ideal in a graded ring R. If P is a graded weakly
prime ideal of R and 0 ̸= xRyRz ⊆P for x, y, z ∈h(R), then x ∈P or y ∈P or
z ∈P.
Proof. Suppose that x, y, z ∈h(R) such that 0 ̸= xRyRz ⊆P. Then 0 ̸=
(Rx)RyRz ⊆P and it follows from Lemma 2.4 that x ∈Rx ⊆P or 0 ̸= RyRz ⊆P.
By reiterating this, the result follows.
□
Proposition 2.6. Let R be a graded ring and P be a proper graded ideal of R.
If P is a graded weakly prime ideal of R, then P is a graded weakly 2-absorbing ideal
of R.
Proof. Let x, y, z ∈h(R) such that 0 ̸= xRyRz ⊆P. By Lemma 2.5, we have
x ∈P or y ∈P or z ∈P. Accordingly, xy ∈P or yz ∈P or xz ∈P, and the result
holds.
□
Proposition 2.7. If P and K are two distinct graded weakly prime ideals of a
graded ring R, then P T K is a graded weakly 2-absorbing ideal of R.
Proof. If P T K = {0}, then it is clear that P T K is a graded weakly 2-
absorbing ideal of R. Let x1, x2, x3 ∈h(R) such that 0 ̸= x1Rx2Rx3 ⊆P T K.
Then 0 ̸= x1Rx2Rx3 ⊆P and 0 ̸= x1Rx2Rx3 ⊆K. It now follows from Lemma 2.5
that xi ∈P and xj ∈K for some i, j, and then xixj ∈P T K. Thereupon, P T K
is a graded weakly 2-absorbing ideal of R.
□

only if P/K is a graded ideal of R/K.
Proposition 2.10. Let R be a graded ring and P be a graded weakly 2-absorbing
ideal of R. If K is a graded ideal of R with K ⊆P, then P/K is a graded weakly
2-absorbing ideal of R/K.
Proof. Let x+K, y+K, z+K ∈h(R/K) such that 0+K ̸= (x+K)(R/K)(y+

hence (x + K)(y + K) ∈P/K or (y + K)(z + K) ∈P/K or (x + K)(z + K) ∈P/K.
