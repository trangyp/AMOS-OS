Abstract
In this note, we show the fundamental group of the complement of the Borromean rings in S3 has
exactly two representations in PSL(2, C) which are faithful, discrete and send meridians into parabolic
elements. Using this result we are able to show that Borromean rings admit exactly one hyperbolic
structure.
Keywords and phrases : Quandles, Representation Variety, Links.
2010 Mathematics Subject Classification Primary 37F99, 32Q, 32M Secondary 30F40, 20H10, 57M60, 53C.
Introduction
We can say that Ahlfors’ ﬁniteness theorem, see [1, 2], and Sullivan’s ﬁniteness theorem for cusps, see [15],
are the major pillars in Kleinian groups, with deep implications in dynamics, topology and geometry, see
for example [6, 16]. For several years after the publication of this results, mathematicians around the world
worked on new proofs with the aim to generalize this theorems for higher dimensional conformal Kleinian
groups, see [10], despite the known evidence in those years M. Kapovich and L. Potyagalio constructed an
example that shows that this kind of theorems cannot hold in the higher dimensional conformal setting, see
[9]. The construction Kapovich and Potyagalio is based in the existence of a complete hyperbolic structure
on the complement of the Borromean rings, more precisely Kapovich-Potyagalio utilizes a discrete subgroup
of M¨obius transformation whose quotient in H3 is the complement of the Borromean rings in S3. The main
purpose is to construct all the reprsentations of the fundamental group of the complent of the Borromean
ring into Mob( ˆC) which sends meridians into parabolic elements, more precisely in this article we show:
Theorem 0.1 There are exactly two representations, namely ρ1, ρ2, from the complement of the Borromean
rings into PSL(3, C) which are faithful, have a discrete image and sends meridians into parabolic elements.
Moreover, we have ρ1 and ρ2 are complex conjugate each other, i. e. ρ1 = ρ2.
Theorem 0.2 The complement of the Borromean in S3 (as well as its mirror image) has exactly one complete
real hyperbolic structure. Moreover if ρ1 , ρ2 are the respective holonomies of the complete real hyperbolic
structures on the Borromean links and its mirror image, then ρ1 = ρ2.
The paper is organized as follows: Section 1 reviews some well-known facts on Knots and Quandles, see
[8, 14] for detailed information. In Section 2, we compute a representation for the fundamental quandle of
the Borromean rings and as a consequence, we are able to compute all PSL(2, C)-colorations for the rings.
Finally in section 3 we prove the main results of this article. The results exposed here well know, see [17]
for a beautiful sketch and [19] for a detailed proof, and the procedure is not new, as far as we know it was
introduced by Inoue and Kabaya in [7], but here is presented in a systematic manner so it can be used for
future reference. The article is itself contained so we hope this can be read by a wide audience.

1.1.
Knots
In this subsection we review the facts used trough the text about links and knots, there are tons of
excellent books on this subject, we ﬁnd useful and very well writing the following books [3, 11, 14]. A knot is
an embedding of a circle S1 into the 3-sphere, S3, more generally a link is a collection of knots which do not
intersect. A knot is called tame if it is isotopic to a polygonal knot, i. e. a knot in R3 which is the union
of a ﬁnite set of line segments (the endpoints of such segment lines are called the vertices). Similarly, we
will say that a link is tame if it is isotopic to a link where each connected component is a polygonal knot,
trough this article all the links are assumed to be tame. An oriented link is a link where each connected
component has an orientation. For example the trivial knot is given by the following embedding S1 →R3
given by e2πiθ 7→(Cosθ, Senθ, 0).
A useful way to visualize and manipulate links is to project the link onto a plane, more precisely if L ⊂R3
is a polygonal link, P ⊂R3 a plane and p : R3 →P is the orthogonal projection, the projection p is said to
be regular if:
1. The set of points x ∈p(L) whose preimage p−1(x) under the projection contains more than one points
(crossing points) is ﬁnite and the crossing points are double points,
2. no vertex of L is mapped onto a double point.
It is possible to show given a polygonal link we can ﬁnd an isotopic link with a regular projection, see [3]. A
link diagram is the regular projection of a link to the plane with broken lines indicating where one part of
the link under crosses the other part, a connected component of a link diagram will be called an arc inherit
the orientation also it is trivial that a link can be reconstructed from its diagram, see [3].
In order to determine whether two projections come from the same link, Reidemeister introduced three
transformations, called Reidemeister moves, that change the projection but don’t change the knot: the
ﬁrst one adds or removes a link, the second pass or underpass strand of the knot over another and the third
passes a strand over a crossing of another, see the ﬁgure below.
Theorem 1.1 (Reidemaster, see [11]) Two links are equivalent if and only if all their diagrams are
connected by a ﬁnite sequence of Reidemeister moves.
Observe that in order to prove the invariance for some function on knots, one should check its invariance
under Reidemeister moves.

of the basic theory.
