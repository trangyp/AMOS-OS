Let K be a closed subset of Rd. An important variant of the moment problem,
called the K-moment problem, requires that the representing µ is supported on K. If
only a ﬁnite number of moments are given, we have the truncated moment problem.
Moments were applied to the study of functions by the Russian mathematicians
P.L. Chebychev (1874) and A.A. Markov (1884). However, the moment problem
ﬁrst appeared in a famous memoir (1894) of the Dutch mathematician T.J. Stieltjes.
He formulated and solved this problem for the half-line and gave the ﬁrst explicit
example of an indeterminate problem. The cases of the real line and of bounded
intervals were studied only later by H. Hamburger (1920) and F. Hausdorff (1920).
Because of the simplicity of its formulation it is surprising that the moment prob-
lem has deep interactions with many branches of mathematics (functional analysis,
function theory, real algebraic geometry, spectral theory, optimization, numerical
analysis, convex analysis, harmonic analysis and others) and a broad range of ap-
plications. The AMS volume “Moments in Mathematics", edited by H.J. Landau
(1987), and the book “Moments, Positive Polynomials and Their Applications" by
J.P. Lasserre (2015) illustrate this in a convincing manner.

Lecture 1
Integral representations of linear functionals
Abstract:
An integral representation theorem of positive functionals on Choquet’s adapted
spaces is obtained. As applications, Haviland’s theorem is derived and existence
results for moment problems on intervals are developed.
All variants of the moment problem deal with integral representations of certain
linear functionals. We begin with a rather general setup.
Suppose X is a locally compact topological Hausdorff space. The Borel algebra
B(X) is the σ-algebra generated by the open subsets of X.
Deﬁnition 1.1. A Radon measure on X is a measure µ : B(X) →[0,+∞] such that
µ(K) < ∞for each compact subset K of X and
µ(M) = sup{µ(K) : K ⊆M, K compact}
for all M ∈B(X).
(1.1)
Thus, in our terminology Radon measures are always nonnegative!
Closed subsets of Rd are locally compact in the induced topology of Rd.
Further, suppose E is a vector space of continuous real-valued functions on X.
In a very general and modern form, the moment problem is the following problem:
Given a linear functional L : E →R and a closed subset K of X, when does there
exist a Radon measure µ supported on K such that
L(f) =
Z
X f(x)dµ(x)
for f ∈E?
(1.2)
In this case, L is called a K-moment functional or a moment functional if K = X.
When we write equations such as (1.2) we always mean that the function f(x) is
µ-integrable and its µ-integral is L(f).
Let {ej : j ∈J} be a basis of the vector space E. Then, for each real sequence
s = (sj)j∈J, there is unique linear functional Ls : E →R given by Ls(ej) = sj for
j ∈J; this functional is called the Riesz functional associated with s. Clearly, (1.2)
holds for the functional L = Ls if and only if sj =
R ej(x)dµ(x) for all j ∈J. In this
case, the numbers sj are called the generalized moment of µ.

1.1 Positive linear functionals on adapted spaces
The representation theorem proved below is based on the following notion, invented
by G. Choquet, of an adapted space.
Deﬁnition 1.2. A linear subspace E of C(X;R) is called adapted if the following
conditions are satisﬁed:
(i) E = E+ −E+.
(ii) For each x ∈X there exists an fx ∈E+ such that fx(x) > 0.
(iii) For each f ∈E+ there exists a g ∈E+ such that for any ε > 0 there exists a
compact subset Kε of X such that |f(x)| ≤ε|g(x)| for x ∈X\Kε.
If condition (iii) is satisﬁed, we shall say that g dominates f. Roughly speaking,
this means that |f(x)/g(x)| →0 as x →∞.
Example 1.3. Let X be closed subset of Rd.
Then E = Rd[x]⌈X is an adapted subspace of C(X;R). Indeed, condition (i) in
Deﬁnition 1.2 follows from the relation 4p = (p + 1)2 −(p −1)2. (ii) is trivial. If
