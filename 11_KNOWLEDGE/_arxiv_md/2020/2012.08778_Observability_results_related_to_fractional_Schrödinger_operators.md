FABRICIO MACI`A
Abstract. We establish observability inequalities for various problems involv-
ing fractional Schr¨odinger operators (−∆)α/2 + V , α > 0, on a compact Rie-
mannian manifold. Observability from an open set for the corresponding frac-
tional Schr¨odinger evolution equation with α > 1 is proved to hold as soon as
the observation set satisﬁes the Geometric Control Condition; it is also shown
that this condition is necessary when the manifold is the d-dimensional sphere
equipped with the standard metric. This is in stark contrast with the case of
eigenfunctions. We construct potentials on the two-sphere with the property
that there exist two points on the sphere such that eigenfunctions of −∆+ V
are uniformly observable from an arbitrarily small neighborhood of those two
points. This condition is much weaker than the Geometric Control Condition,
which is necessary for uniform observability of eigenfunctions for the free Lapla-
cian on the sphere. The same result also holds for eigenfunctions of (−∆)α/2+V ,
for any α > 0.
1. Introduction
Consider a compact Riemannian manifold (M, g), an open subset ω ⊆M and
the Laplace-Beltrami operator (or simply the Laplacian) ∆on M associated to the
metric g. Since −∆is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on L2(M), it is possible
to deﬁne the fractional Laplacian (−∆)α/2 for α > 0 using the spectral theorem.
In this article we are interested in understanding the validity of various ob-
servability estimates associated to (−∆)α/2 or, more generally, to the fractional

fundamental question in mathematical quantum mechanics. It has been shown
in [23] that this property is related to controllability properties of wave equations

It is known since the seventies that both (O(T, ω)) and (OE(ω)) hold under a
geometric condition that relates the geometry of the observability region ω and
the geodesics of the manifold (M, g); more detailed references will be given after
the statement of our ﬁrst result.
Let us ﬁrst recall the precise deﬁnitions.
The open subset ω ⊆M satisﬁes
the Geometric Control Condition (GCC) provided that every geodesic of (M, g)
intersects ω. Given T > 0, we say that ω satisﬁes GCCT whenever every geodesic
segment of length smaller than T intersects ω. Since M is compact, ω satisﬁes
GCC if and only if it satisﬁes GCCT for some T > 0.

ary, T > 0 and ω ⊆M open. Then the following results hold.
• if 0 < α < 1 and ω ̸= M then (O(T, ω)) does not hold no matter how large
T > 0 is.

• if α > 1 and ω satisfes GCC then (O(T, ω)) holds for every T > 0.
We give a uniﬁed proof of this result in Section 2. Some of the statements in
Theorem 1 hold for time-dependent potentials; Theorem 4 in Section 2 presents

The case α = 1 corresponds to the wave equation and was ﬁrst proved by Rauch
and Taylor in [25]. The necessary condition is related to the existence of highly
localized solutions to the wave equation known as Gaussian beams or wave-packets,
see [24, 21]. Note that the necessary condition for observability is slightly weaker
than GCCT, since geodesics are just required to intersect the larger set ω; the
suﬃciency of this less restrictive condition depends in general of the particular
conﬁguration of geodesics that intersect ω precisely at the boundary ∂ω (the so-
called grazing rays).
When α = 2, problem (1) is the usual Schr¨odinger equation. The suﬃciency of
GCC in this case was established by Lebeau [13], in the more general setting of
manifolds with boundary. We present here a rather direct proof in the boundaryless
setting that remains valid for every α > 1.
Finally, note that when α > 2 (or α > 1 but T large enough so that GCCT
holds) and V = 0 , the suﬃciency of GCC can also be deduced from that of the
case α = 2 from abstract, functional-theoretic arguments, see [22, Theorem 3.5].
In general, GCC is far from being necessary for the observability property when
α = 2. The simplest setting where this kind of behavior takes place is the ﬂat
torus Td = Rd/Zd, where (O(T, ω)) holds for every open set ω ⊆Td. This was
ﬁrst proved by Jaﬀard [11] for the free Laplacian when d = 2, and generalized to
the multidimensional case by Komornik [12]. The addition of a potential does not
change the ﬁnal result: see [7, 6] for results on the two-dimensional case d = 2
and [4] for the general case. The article [1] provides more general results that
encompass the general case α > 1 on the torus Td.
Other situations where observability for the Schr¨odinger equation has been es-
tablished include Zoll manifolds (i.e. manifolds all of whose geodesics are closed)
[17, 19] and the Euclidean disk [3, 2]. On the disk, a necessary and suﬃcient
condition for observability is that the observability region intersects the boundary
of the disk in an open arc. Note that this situation is intermediate between the
torus (for which observability holds unconditionally) and the sphere, for which it
turns out that GCC is necessary. This is the content of the next result.
Theorem 2. Suppose (M, g) = (Sd, can) is the sphere equipped with its canonical
metric. Let ω ⊆Sd be an open set such that M \ ω contains a geodesic. Then, for
every α > 0 the following results hold.
• The observability estimate (O(T, ω)) does not hold for any T > 0;
• If in addition V = 0, then (OE(ω)) fails as well.

The proof of this theorem is given in Section 3, and covers the case of time-

results in [17, 19] for α = 2, and here we follow closely the strategy of the proofs in

It turns out that the conditions under which (OE(ω)) holds change dramatically
when a perturbation V is present.
This was ﬁrst analyzed in [20], where it is
shown that, on the d-dimensional sphere, (OE(ω)) holds provided ω ⊆Sd satisﬁes
a geometric condition involving the potential V (what we call here V -GCC, see
Section 4) that is in general much weaker than GCC. Here we present a proof
for fractional Schr¨odinger operators based on an observability estimate over long
times for the fractional Schr¨odinger evolution, see Theorem 10 in Section 4 which
is of independent interest. This is based on the detailed study of the long-time
dynamics of Schr¨odinger ﬂows presented in [16, 19].
Our last result, valid in two dimensions, gives an explicit construction of a family
of smooth potentials on the sphere such that (OE(ω)) holds when ω is an arbitrarily
small neighborhood of two ﬁxed points on the sphere. This is in stark contrast with
the same situation for the free Laplacian, for which GCC is a necessary condition
for (OE(ω)).

contains p, q.
This result is proved in Section 5. As it will be clear from the proof, the size
of V in any reasonable norm can be arbitrarily small. The result follows from the
analysis of the ﬂow of a certain Hamiltonian vector ﬁeld on the sphere constructed
