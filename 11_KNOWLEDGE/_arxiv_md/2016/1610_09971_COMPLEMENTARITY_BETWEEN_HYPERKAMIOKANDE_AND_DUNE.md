Complementarity Between Hyperkamiokande and DUNE
OSAMU YASUDA a, SHINYA FUKASAWA and MONOJIT GHOSH
Department of Physics, Tokyo Metropolitan University, Hachioji, Tokyo 192-0397, Japan
In this talk we present our results on the sensitivity to the neutrino mass hierarchy, the octant
of the mixing angle and the CP phase in the future long baseline experiments T2HK and
DUNE as well as in the atmospheric neutrino observation at Hyperkamiokande (HK).
1
Introduction
Thanks to the neutrino experiments in the last two decades, the values of the three mixing angles
and the values of the mass squared diﬀerences are now determined in the three ﬂavor mixing
framework to some precision. The unknown quantities at present are: (i) the mass hierarchy or
the sign of ∆m2
31 (NH: normal hierarchy i.e., ∆m2
31 > 0 or IH: inverted hierarchy i.e., ∆m2
31 > 0),
(ii) the octant of θ23 (LO: lower octant i.e., θ23 < 45◦or HO: higher octant i.e., θ23 > 45◦) and
(iii) the CP phase δCP . These unknown quantities are expected to be determined in the future
experiments, such as T2HK2, DUNE3 and the atmospheric neutrino measurement at HK4, etc.
The T2HK experiment is an upgrade of the ongoing T2K experiment which will use a detector
to have a volume almost 25 times larger than the existing T2K detector. HK is the atmospheric
counterpart of the T2HK experiment. On the other hand DUNE is a high statistics beam based
experiment to use high beam power, large detector volume and longer baseline. In this talk we
study for the ﬁrst time the joint sensitivity of the long-baseline experiments T2HK, DUNE and
the atmospheric experiment HK in determining the remaining unknowns in neutrino oscillation
sector.b In this work we study: (i) the sensitivity of the T2HK, HK and DUNE experiments,
(ii) the synergy between the T2HK and HK experiments to resolve the parameter degeneracy in
the neutrino oscillation, (iii) how far the sensitivities in determining hierarchy, octant and CP
can be stretched when all these three powerful experiments are combined together and (iv) the
precision measurements of θ23, δCP and ∆m2
31 of this setup.
2

3
Combined Sensitivity of T2HK, HK and DUNE for determining the unknown
parameters
3.1
Hierarchy
We have calculated the hierarchy χ2 by taking the true hierarchy in the simulated data and
wrong hierarchy in theory. We have marginalized over θ23 from 38◦- 52◦and δCP from −180◦
to +180◦. We have given our results in Fig 1. From the left and middle panels of the upper
row we see that for T2HK the hierarchy sensitivity is poor in the unfavorable parameter space
i.e., 0◦< δCP < 180◦for NH and −180◦< δCP < 0◦for IH. This is due to the presence of
hierarchy-δCP degeneracy. In these plots the width of the bands is due to the variation of θ23
from 40◦to 50◦. If we add the HK data to T2HK then there is an improvement in the unfavorable
regions and we obtain a 5σ hierarchy sensitivity for all the values of true δCP . However for the
combination of T2HK+HK+DUNE, its possible to get a hierarchy sensitivity as high as 15σ for
all the values of δCP for both the hierarchies. In the right panel of Fig. 1, we have shown the
hierarchy sensitivity of HK alone. From that plot we understand that hierarchy sensitivity of
HK is poor if the true hierarchy is IH. This feature can be explained from the bottom row of Fig.
1, where we have plotted the total electron (left panel) and the total muon (right panel) events
as a function of δCP for diﬀerent values of θ23. From the plot we notice that in both the panels

NH-LO is degenerate with IH-HO. Thus because of this degeneracy the hierarchy sensitivity of
HK for IH is poor as compared to NH.
3.2
Octant and CP
To calculate octant sensitivity χ2 we have taken the true octant in the simulated data and the
wrong octant in the theory. In this process we have marginalized over δCP and sign of ∆m2
31
in theory. We have presented our results for octant sensitivity in the upper row of Fig. 2 as a
function of true θ23. From the ﬁgures we see that the octant sensitivity of T2HK, T2HK+HK
and DUNE to exclude wrong octant solution at 5σ C.L is almost same.
However, for the
combined setup i.e., T2HK+HK+DUNE, there is a signiﬁcant amount of improvement in the
octant sensitivity. For the combined setup it is possible to have a 5σ octant sensitivity except
43.5◦< θ23 < 48◦for both the hierarchies.
In the bottom row of Fig. 2, we have presented the CP violation (CPV) discovery χ2 as a
function of true δCP . The CPV discovery potential of an experiment is deﬁned by its capability
to distinguish a true value of δCP other than 0◦and 180◦. In calculating CPV χ2 we have
marginalized over θ23 and sign of ∆m2
31 in theory. We have presented our results for θ23 = 48◦
which is the current best-ﬁt value of this mixing angle as obtained from the global analysis of
the world data. From the plots we see that CP sensitivity of T2HK is poor in the unfavorable
parameter space as mentioned earlier.
But if we add HK data to T2HK, then there is an
improvement in the unfavorable region. This is because the hierarchy sensitivity of HK resolves
the hierarchy-δCP degeneracy. For the combined setup we ﬁnd that a 10σ CP sensitivity is
obtained for δCP = ±90◦. In the lower panel of Fig. 2 we have plotted the fraction of δCP for
which a 5σ CPV can be discovered as a function of θ23. From these ﬁgures wee see that the
sensitivity of T2HK and DUNE is similar for both the hierarchies. From the panels we also note
that for the combined setup i.e., T2HK+HK+DUNE, CPV can be discovered at 5σ for at least
70% values of true δCP irrespective of the true value of θ23.
4
Precision of δCP , θ23 and ∆m2
31
To gauge the precision of δCP in this setup, in the upper panels of Fig. 3 we have given 3σ
C.L contours in the true δCP - test δCP plane. We have given our results for θ23 = 48◦. From
the plots we see that the precision of δCP is poor for T2HK. Apart from the true diagonal
solution, there are also oﬀdiagonal fake solutions. As mentioned earlier, these wrong solutions
appear due to the hierarchy-δCP degeneracy. But if we add HK data, then we see that these
fake solutions disappears. This is again due to the hierarchy sensitivity of HK. We also notice
from the ﬁgures that as we keep adding the data from diﬀerent experiments, the precision of
δCP gets improved. In the middle row we have given the 90% precision plots in the test δCP -
test θ23 plane for diﬀerent true values of δCP and θ23. Here we have presented our results for
normal hierarchy. From the plot we see that for true δCP = −180◦, there are wrong CP solutions
for T2HK around δCP = −30◦. But when HK data is added to it we see that the wrong CP
solutions get vanished. For the combined setup i.e., T2HK+HK+DUNE, we ﬁnd that δCP can
be measured within 20% precision for the true value of δCP = −90◦and θ23 = 46◦. In the
bottom row of Fig. 3, we have plotted the 1, 2 and 3 σ precision plots in θ23 - ∆m2
31 plane.
We show our results only for NH. The results for IH is similar as that of NH. From the plots
we see that the sensitivity of the combined setup is always better the individual set up. For the
combines setup of T2HK+HK+DUNE, it is possible to attain a 0.4% precision in ∆m2
31 and a
1.3% precision for θ23.

for unfavorable region of the CP phase, when we combine it with the atmospheric neutrino
measurement at Hyperkamiokande, we can determine the mass hierarchy at 5 σ C.L. for any
