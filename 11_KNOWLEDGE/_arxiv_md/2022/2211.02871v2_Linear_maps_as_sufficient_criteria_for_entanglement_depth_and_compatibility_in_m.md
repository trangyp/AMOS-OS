Physical transformations are described by linear maps that are completely positive and trace pre-
serving (CPTP). However, maps that are positive (P) but not completely positive (CP) are instru-
mental to derive separability/entanglement criteria. Moreover, the properties of such maps can be
linked to entanglement properties of the states they detect. Here, we extend the results presented
in [Phys. Rev A 93, 042335 (2016)], where suﬃcient separability criteria for bipartite systems were
derived. In particular, we analyze the entanglement depth of an N-qubit system by proposing linear
maps that, when applied to any state, result in a bi-separable state for the 1 : (N −1) partitions, i.e.,
(N −1)-entanglement depth. Furthermore, we derive criteria to detect arbitrary (N −n)-entanglement
depth tailored to states in close vicinity of the completely depolarized state (the normalized identity
matrix). We also provide separability (or 1- entanglement depth) conditions in the symmetric sector,
including for diagonal states. Finally, we suggest how similar map techniques can be used to derive
suﬃcient conditions for a set of expectation values to be compatible with separable states or local-
hidden-variable theories. We dedicate this paper to the memory of the late Andrzej Kossakowski, our
spiritual and intellectual mentor in the ﬁeld of linear maps.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Linear maps are the fundamental tools to describe
transformations in quantum systems. In fact, any phys-
ical transformation corresponds to a completely posi-
tive trace preserving operation (CPTP), that is, a linear
map, Λ, that is positive (P), Λ(ρ) ≥0, on all quantum
states (ρ ≥0), and whose extension to a larger tenso-
rial space leaves all states in the composite space positive
as well ( 1A ⊗ΛB)ρAB ≥0. Such description applies for
closed and open quantum systems irrespectively from
the fact that the quantum system of interest is single
or multipartite.
However, maps that are positive (P)
but not completely positive (CP) are instrumental to de-
rive separability/entanglement criteria, that is, the ab-
sence/presence of shared quantum correlations between
the constituents of multipartite systems. Moreover, the
properties of such maps can be linked to entanglement
properties of the states they detect. Entanglement, the
cornerstone feature of quantum systems, has no classi-
cal analogue, and is a fundamental resource for quantum
information tasks.
Entanglement has been mostly studied and addressed
and exploited in the bipartite scenario.
But even for
this simplest case, checking if a given composite sys-
tem is separable (entangled) is an NP-hard problem[2].
Despite the fact that in low dimensions one can use
semi-deﬁnite programming to numerically determine
the presence of entanglement [3], such method be-
comes unfeasible when the dimensions of the compos-
ite Hilbert space grows, when the number of involved
∗maciej.lewenstein@icfo.es
parties increases, or when the aim, rather than check-
ing the entanglement of a speciﬁc quantum state, is to
is characterize the entanglement properties of families
of states. For all these cases the common approaches in-
clude using separability criteria or constructing entan-
glement witnesses.
To date, most of the known separability criteria are
necessary but not suﬃcient, starting with the prominent
positive partial transpose criterion, proposed by Peres
[4]. In fact transposition T is a paradigmatic example
of a positive map, i.e., a map that maps states onto states.
Transposition is not, however, completely positive, i.e.,
its extensions by identity map to larger tensor spaces do
not correspond to positive maps. Therefore, this condi-
tion becomes a necessary criterion of separability. Such
map however, has proven to be suﬃcient condition of
separability only for two-qubits and one qubit-qutrit by
Horodeckis [5].
For all bipartite systems (i.e.
Alice-
Bob), it has also been proven in (cf.[1]), that any positive
map acting exclusively on a space of Alice’s (Bob’s) op-
erators, transforms a separable state into a positive def-
inite state. Conversely, a state is separable if it remains
positive deﬁnite under the action of all positive maps on
Alice’s (Bob’s) side. Well known positive maps that have
been proposed and applied to determine separability are
the celebrated reduction map [6, 7], the Breuer-Hall map
[8, 9], or the Choi map [10].
Here, we follow the approach used to derive suﬃcient
criteria of separability for bipartite systems (necessary
criteria of entanglement) previously introduced by some
of us in [34]. Now, our ﬁrst aim is to derive suﬃcient cri-
teria to determine the entanglement depth of an ensem-
ble of N-qubit systems. The entanglement depth n of an
ensemble of N-parties expresses the fact that the quan-

MAIN RESULTS
Here we present the main results on the construction
of new suﬃcient criteria for N-qubit systems from the
inversion of linear maps. We will consider families of
such transformations inspired by the reduction and the
Breuer-Hall maps.
A.
Reduction-inspired maps
To start with, we consider the simple reduction-type
family of maps deﬁned as Λα(ρ) = Tr(ρ) 1 + αρ.
1.
Suﬃcient criteria for N −1 - entanglement depth
Now we ﬁx S ′ ⊂S the set of bi-separable states in
the partitions 1 : (N −1), (one qubit subsystem versus
the others N −1). In other words, S ′ is the set of states
with entanglement depth (N −1), so the set does not con-
tain genuine N-qubit entanglement. The task is to ﬁnd
the range of α in which for all states ρ ≥0 we have that
Λα(ρ) is bi-separable for any partition 1 : (N −1). Once
this is done, Λ−1
α (ρ) ≥0 would be a suﬃcient (N −1)-
entanglement depth criterion. Bi-separability of Λα(ρ)
for N qubits follows from the following known result.
Theorem 2. Compare [21, 24]. Let Λα(ρ) = Tr(ρ) 1 + αρ

Λα(ρ) =: σ ∈ΣN−1, i.e. σ is bi-separable in any 1 : (N −1)
partition (i.e. has entanglement depth (N −1))
Proof. The proof follows [34]. It is enough to prove the
theorem for pure states, ρ = |Ψ⟩⟨Ψ|. For N qubits, |Ψ⟩has
maximally Schmidt rank 2, in the partition Alice:All Oth-
ers (any other 1 : (N −1)). Without loosing generality,
we can assume that |Ψ⟩= λ0|0⟩⊗|0⟩N−1 + λ1|1⟩⊗|1⟩N−1. It
is then enough to check the positiveness and separabil-
ity of Λ(|Ψ⟩⟨Ψ|) on a 2⊗2 space spanned by |0⟩, |1⟩, |0⟩N−1,
|1⟩N−1 in both Alice’s and All Others’ spaces. PPT pro-
vides then necessary and suﬃcient condition [5], and we
easily get that indeed −1 ≤α ≤2. The condition α ≥−1
actually follows already from a simpler requirement of
positiveness of Λα(ρ), as it appears in the deﬁnition of
the reduction map [6, 7].
□
The inverse of the reduction map leads to the follow-
ing condition:
Corollary 3. (Suﬃcient N −1-entanglement depth cri-
terion 1) If Λ−1

σ ∈ΣN−1, i.e. σ is bi-separable in any partition 1 : (N −1).
It is interesting to check whether simple criteria are
strong enough to detect some states outside the "separa-
ble ball" around identity for N qubits[24–26] Of course,
we do not expect that all states from the separable ball
are detected. The reason for this weakness is that Bar-
num and Gurvits, and Szarek have estimated the radius
of the separable ball using a stronger result, namely: if
the operator norm of a Hermitian matrix X is bounded
by 1, then
1 + X is separable. In contrast, our simple cri-
teria depend on the spectrum, not on the norm, and are
thereby independent. Nonetheless, we will see in the
next sections that with other choices of Λp’s, we could
derive further stronger suﬃcient (N −1)-depth criteria.

VI.
CONCLUSIONS AND OUTLOOK
We have presented in this paper several families of lin-
ear maps, that have a property that when applied to N-
qubit states, result in states separable with respect a bi-
partition. When convertible, such maps allow deriving
suﬃcient criteria for entanglement depth in N qubit sys-
tems. In particular, in the present work we derive suf-
ﬁcient criteria for arbitrary entanglement depth tailored


## Page 10


10
to states close to the normalized identity matrix and suf-
ﬁcient entanglement criteria for diﬀerent classes of sym-
metric and symmetric-diagonal states. Finally, we pro-
pose how the application of linear maps can be used
to detect states leading to expectation values compati-
ble with separable states and local-hidden-variable the-
ories. Such criteria have rather obvious possible applica-
tions and implications in quantum information science
and quantum many body physics: they can namely be
applied to determine whether and how much entangle-
ment is needed for various speciﬁc protocols for quan-
tum computation or other quantum tasks, similarly as it
was discussed in Ref. [23].
