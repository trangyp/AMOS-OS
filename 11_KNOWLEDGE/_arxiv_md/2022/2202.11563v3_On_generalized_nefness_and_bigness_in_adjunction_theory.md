CAMILLA FELISETTI AND CLAUDIO FONTANARI
Abstract. We investigate eﬀectiveness and ampleness of adjoint di-
visors of the form aL + bKX, where L is a suitably positive line bundle
on a smooth projective variety X and a, b are positive integers.
1. Introduction
Let X be a smooth projective variety of dimension n deﬁned over the
complex ﬁeld C and let KX be its canonical divisor. Adjunction theory is
a classical branch of algebraic geometry investigating positivity properties
of adjoint divisors, namely, divisors of the form aL + bKX, where L is a
suitably positive line bundle on X (for instance, eﬀective, nef, big, or ample)
and a, b are positive integers. In order to give a ﬂavour of the various open
questions in the ﬁeld, we distinguish several cases.
First, ﬁx a = b = 1 and consider the eﬀectiveness of L + KX. This is the
content of the following conjecture, ﬁrst stated by Ambro in [1]:
Conjecture 1.1 (Ambro). If D is an ample divisor on X and D −KX is
nef and big, then H0(X, D) ̸= 0.
In the original statement D was assumed just to be nef, but (as ex-
plained in [11, p. 176]) one can actually reduce to the case where D is
ample. Though Conjecture 1.1 is yet to be proven in full generality, some
partial answer have been provided by Kawamata [11] for surfaces and min-
imal threefolds, by Broustet-H¨oring [5] for non-uniruled threefolds, and by
H¨oring [10] for all threefolds, even allowing Q-factorial canonical singular-
ities.
Now, ﬁx a = 1 and let b grow and consider the eﬀectiveness of L + bKX.
This is the classical problem of Termination of adjunction, ﬁrst addressed
by Castelnuovo and Enriques (see [8]) for algebraic surfaces. More generally,
in [2], the following natural conjecture was formulated:

Conjecture 1.2. Let L be an eﬀective divisor on X. If adjunction termi-
nates in the classical sense for L, i.e. if there exists an integer m0 ≥1 such
that H0(X, L + mKX) = 0 for every integer m ≥m0, then X is uniruled.
Indeed, if adjunction terminates in the classical sense for some eﬀective
divisor L, then X has negative Kodaira dimension (see [2, Proposition
2]) and all varieties with negative Kodaira dimension are expected to be
uniruled (see for instance [18, (11.5)]).
Finally, ﬁx b = 1 and let a grow and consider the ampleness of aL+KX.
This is the content of the following conjecture, stated by Serrano in [20]:
Conjecture 1.3 (Serrano). Let L be a strictly nef line bundle on X. Then
KX + tL is ample for t > n + 1.
Recall that a divisor L is said to be strictly nef if L · C > 0 for each
irreducible curve C on X. Conjecture 1.3 has been veriﬁed for surfaces and
for threefolds with some possible exceptions for Calabi-Yau threefolds (see
[20] and [6]).
Here we collect partial results towards these three directions.
First, we introduce a natural notion of k-big divisors (see Deﬁnition 2.1
and Lemma 2.1: notice the slight diﬀerence from the analogous notion in
[4, p. 5]), in such a way that a nef divisor L is 1-big if Ln−1 · H > 0 for
some ample divisor H, and we investigate its basic properties. We may
generalize Conjecture 1.1 in terms of 1-bigness as follows:
Conjecture 1.4 (Generalized Ambro conjecture). Let D be an ample line
bundle such that D −KX is nef and 1-big. Then H0(X, D) ̸= 0.
We provide the ﬁrst evidence for this generalized conjecture by verifying
it for non-uniruled surfaces (see Proposition 2.4) and for minimal threefolds
(see Proposition 2.5).
Next, we point out that if we add the assumption that X is minimal in
the sense of the Minimal Model Program, i.e. we assume that KX is nef,
then the problem of termination of adjunction becomes more manageable.
Indeed, in this case h0(X, aL + bKX) = χ(X, aL + bKX) for every nef and
big divisor L by Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing theorem.
In dimension up to 5 we obtain the following result:
Proposition 1.5. Let X be minimal of dimension n ≤5 and let L be an
eﬀective ample divisor on X. If n = 5 assume also that (ν(X), χ(X, OX)) ̸=
(1, 0), where ν(X) denotes the numerical dimension of X. Then adjunction
does not terminate in the classical sense for L.
