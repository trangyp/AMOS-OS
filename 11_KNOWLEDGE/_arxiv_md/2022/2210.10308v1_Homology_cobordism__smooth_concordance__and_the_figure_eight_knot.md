SALLY COLLINS
Abstract. The 0-surgeries of two knots K1 and K2 are homology cobordant rel meridians if there
exists a Z-homology cobordism X between them such that the two knot meridians are in the same
homology class in H1(X, Z). In this paper, we give a pair of rationally slice knots which are not
smoothly concordant but whose 0-surgeries are homology cobordant rel meridians. One knot in the
pair is the ﬁgure eight knot, which has concordance order two; all previous examples of such pairs
of knots are inﬁnite order.
1. Introduction
Two oriented knots K1 and K2 in S3 are smoothly concordant, denoted K1 ∼K2, if they cobound
a smooth annulus in S3×[0, 1]. The set of isotopy classes of knots forms a monoid, and that monoid
modulo this smooth concordance relation yields an abelian group with binary operation connected
sum. We call this group the smooth concordance group, denoted C, whose identity element is the
class of smoothly slice knots. There are several variations on C, including the rational concordance
group CQ, where two knots are equivalent if they cobound a smooth annulus in a rational homology
cobordism, and the topological concordance group CTOP, where we relax the conditions on the
annulus the knots cobound to only require that it be locally ﬂat.
We let S3
n(K) denote the 3-manifold that results from performing n-framed surgery on a knot
K in S3. In general, two 3-manifolds Y1 and Y2 are Z-homology cobordant if there exists a smooth,
compact, oriented 4-manifold X such that ∂X = (−Y1) ∪Y2 and H∗(X, Yi; Z) = 0 for i = 1, 2.
If we let Y1 = S3
n(K1) and Y2 = S3
n(K2) for some K1, K2 knots in S3 and some n ∈Z, then the
n-surgeries of K1 and K2 are Z-homology cobordant rel meridians if they are Z-homology cobordant
by cobordism X and the positively oriented meridians of the Ki are representatives of the same
homology class in H1(X; Z).
It is a natural question to ask whether there is a relationship between the homeomorphism type of
S3
n(K) and the knot type of K; in the Kirby problem list [Kir97], Akbulut and Kirby conjectured in
Problem 1.19 and subsequent remarks that two knots with the same 0-surgeries will be concordant
knots. This conjecture inspires the following question:
Question 1.1. If S3
0(K1) and S3
0(K2) are Z-homology cobordant rel meridians, are K1 and K2
smoothly concordant?
In [CFHH13], Cochran-Franklin-Hedden-Horn give a negative answer to this question by pro-
viding a family of topologically slice knots which are not pairwise smoothly concordant, but whose
0-surguries satisfy the hypothesis of 1.1. All knots in this family have inﬁnite order in C, and are
distinguishable as non-concordant in C by Ozsv´ath-Szab´o’s τ invariant [OS04b] and Rasmussen’s s
invariant [Ras03].

In [Yas15], Yasui gives a condition on knots such that their images under the Mazur pattern and
under a second closely related pattern gives a pair of knots with the same 0-surgery which are not
concordant for any orientations.
In this paper, we will expand on this work in the smooth category. We show the answer to
Question 1.1 is “No” even when one knot in the pair is torsion in C. Interestingly, most of the
smooth concordance invariants coming from the knot Floer package vanish for our pair of knots,
and both knots are rationally slice. We summarize our results thusly:
Theorem 1.1. There exist rationally slice knots whose zero surgeries are smoothly Z-homology
cobordant rel meridians, but which are not smoothly concordant, and furthermore have diﬀerent
orders in C.

2. Background: knot and bordered Floer homology
We assume familiarity with the Heegaard Floer homology package of Ozsv´ath-Szab´o [OS04a].
We will provide here an overview of knot Floer homology, deﬁned by Ozsv´ath-Szab´o [OS04b]
and independently by J. Rasmussen [Ras03], and bordered Heegaard Floer homology, deﬁned by
Lipshitz-Ozsv´ath-Thurston [LOT18]. Section 4 will provide the necessary background on involutive
knot Floer homology, deﬁned by Hendricks and Manolescu [HM17]. For a more in-depth consider-
ation of any of these theories, we direct the reader to the papers cited, or to the following helpful
application and survey papers: [Hom17], [LOT12], and [HHL19].
2.1. Knot Floer Homology. Knot Floer homology is a Heegaard Floer theory for null-homologous
knots embedded in 3-manifolds. We will restrict our attention to knots in S3. To such an oriented
knot K, we associate a doubly pointed Heegaard diagram H = (Σ, α, β, w, z), with base points w
and z, where Σ is the genus-g closed, oriented Heegaard surface. This surface is decorated with
α, a g-tuple of α-circles, and β, a g-tuple of β-circles, and these attaching circles satisfy a series
of intersection conditions amongst same sets and between the two sets. Then, the knot Floer com-
plex is the chain complex we build using the intersection data of two Lagrangian submanifolds
Tα = α1 × α2 × · · · × αg and Tβ = β1 × β2 × · · · × βg in the symmetric product of Σg. Intersection
points between the two tori give generators of our complex, and the diﬀerential counts the pseudo-
holomorphic discs with boundary on Tα ∪Tβ that connect these points. The knot Floer complex

2.2. Bordered Floer homology. Bordered Floer homology is a version of Heegaard Floer theory
developed by Lipshitz-Ozsv´ath-Thurston [LOT18] for 3-manifolds with boundary. We equip the
