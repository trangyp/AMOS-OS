EXAMPLES OF HOMOLOGICAL TORSION AND VOLUME GROWTH
ABHIJIT CHAMPANERKAR AND ILYA KOFMAN
Abstract. We provide examples of towers of covers of cusped hyperbolic 3–manifolds
whose exponential homological torsion growth is explicitly computed in terms of volume
growth.
These examples arise from abelian covers of alternating links in the thickened
torus. A corollary is that the spanning tree entropy for each regular planar lattice is given
by the volume of a hyperbolic polyhedron.
1. Introduction
For a manifold M, a tower of covers is a sequence of ﬁnite covers,
· · · →Mn →Mn−1 →· · · →M1 →M0 = M.

subgroups, denoted here by TH1(Mn).

2.1. Biperiodic links. A biperiodic alternating link is a link in R2×I, with I = (−1, 1), with
a diagram on R2 ×{0} which can be isotoped to be invariant under Z2 acting by independent
translations of R2, such that the quotient link diagram is alternating on the torus T 2 × {0}.
Figure 1(a) shows the biperiodic alternating link W, called the square weave, with the
fundamental domain in T 2 × I shown in Figure 1(b). Let Wn be the toroidally alternating
link in T 2 × I, which is the quotient of W by the nZ × nZ action, so that the crossing
number c(Wn) = 4n2. Figure 1(c) shows another biperiodic alternating link, the triaxial
link Q. The fundamental domain for its toroidally alternating quotient link Q1 in T 2 × I is
shown in Figure 1(d). Taking the quotient by nZ × nZ, we get the links Qn in T 2 × I with
c(Qn) = 3n2.
For any link L in T 2 × I, we have T 2 × I −L ∼= S3 −ℓ, where ℓhas a Hopf sublink given
by the cores of the two Heegaard tori. In the case of W1, ℓ= L12n2256 (see [6, Figure 4]).
In the case of Q1, ℓ= L12n2232 (see [7, Figure 12]), whose complement is homeomorphic
to that of the minimally twisted 5–chain link. The complements of both W1 and Q1 are
principal congruence manifolds [2].
More generally, let L be any biperiodic alternating link, with a toroidally alternating
quotient link L1 in the fundamental domain T 2 × I. For n ≥1, taking the quotient by
nZ × nZ as above, we get a sequence of toroidally alternating links Ln in T 2 × I. Let X(Ln)
be the 2–fold cyclic cover of T 2 × I −Ln, which is the cover associated to the kernel of
π1(T 2 × I −Ln) →Z/2Z, with each meridian of Ln mapped to 1, and the two Hopf link
meridians mapped to 0. Thus, {X(Ln)} are ﬁnite covers of T 2 × I −L1, corresponding to
ﬁnite index subgroups of Z2. Moreover, R2 × I −L and, hence, all T 2 × I −Ln and X(Ln)
are often hyperbolic 3–manifolds; see [7]. In particular, W and Q are hyperbolic.
2.2. Torsion and Alexander polynomials. Suppose T 2 ×I −L1 ∼= S3 −ℓ. The Hopf link
meridians acquire an orientation from the action by Z2 on the universal cover of T 2 × {0}.
Following [18], we can deﬁne the Alexander polynomial ∆ℓ(−1, . . . , −1, x, y) via Fox calculus,
with Z[π1(S3 −ℓ)] →Z[x±1, y±1] given by mapping the oriented Hopf link meridians to x, y,
and the other meridians to −1.
Using results in [18] and [8], we now extend equation (2) to these non-cyclic covers.
