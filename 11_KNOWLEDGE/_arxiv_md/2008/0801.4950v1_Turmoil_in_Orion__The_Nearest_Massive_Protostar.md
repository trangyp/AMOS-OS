  # 3. BOOTSTRAP CAPSULE
  # ============================================================

  bootstrap_capsule:

    class:
      DERIVED

    text: >
      Tan reviews three broad pathways for massive-star formation: collapse
      from massive gas-dominated cores, competitive Bondi-Hoyle accretion onto
      protostellar seeds, and protostellar/stellar collisions at extreme stellar
      densities. The Turbulent Core Model is developed as the favored core-based
      framework, with massive approximately pressure-confined turbulent cores
      embedded in high-surface-density protocluster clumps. The model predicts
      core size, accretion rate, collapse time, disk scale, protostellar
      luminosity, ionizing output, and outflow properties as functions of core
      mass and clump surface density. Orion KL is then used as a nearby test
      case. BN is interpreted as a runaway star most likely ejected from the
      Theta1C multiple system; source n is treated as a lower-mass protostar;
      source I, near the Orion Hot Core, is argued to be the dominant massive
      protostar powering the KL region. Source I's core size, inferred central
      mass, luminosity, accretion rate, disk scale, and radio morphology are
      presented as broadly consistent with the Turbulent Core Model, although
      maser kinematics and disk/outflow orientation remain unresolved.
