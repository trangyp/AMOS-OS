  # ============================================================
  # 3. BOOTSTRAP CAPSULE
  # ============================================================

  bootstrap_capsule:

    class:
      DERIVED

    text: >
      Goda, Solli, and Jalali introduce amplified dispersive optical tomography
      as a high-speed OCT architecture in which a broadband interference
      spectrum is mapped directly into a temporal waveform by large fiber
      dispersion and simultaneously amplified using distributed Raman gain
      inside the dispersive medium. Conventional dispersive Fourier-transform
      OCT trades increased dispersion, needed for spectral resolution and
      imaging depth, against increasing optical loss and therefore decreasing
      sensitivity. ADOT attacks this bottleneck locally: the weak sample
      reflection is amplified during the same dispersive propagation that
      performs spectrum-to-time conversion. In the proof-of-principle system,
      equal-dispersion fiber modules are placed in both Michelson arms and the
      sample-arm DCF is pumped at 1470 and 1480 nm. The system demonstrates
      single-shot depth imaging at a 36.6 MHz axial scan rate and approximately
      15 dB sensitivity enhancement. The source further derives the amplified
      dispersive-FT mapping including second- and third-order dispersion and
      shows that future resolution/depth trade-offs depend jointly on source
      bandwidth, dispersion, electrical bandwidth, loss, gain, and pulse
      repetition rate.

      text: >
        Goda, Solli, and Jalali demonstrate amplified dispersive optical
        tomography, an OCT architecture that combines spectrum-to-time mapping
        by dispersive Fourier transformation with distributed Raman
        amplification inside the same dispersive fiber. Conventional
        dispersive-FT OCT benefits from greater dispersion because it improves
        optical spectral resolution and imaging depth, but the additional
        dispersive propagation also increases loss, degrading sensitivity and
        forcing longer integration. ADOT inserts gain directly at this
        bottleneck. In the demonstrated Michelson interferometer, both arms
        contain matched DCF while the sample-arm DCF is pumped at 1470 and
        1480 nm. The returning sample field is amplified during its dispersive
        transformation, increasing spectral-fringe visibility before
        photodetection. The proof-of-concept system achieves single-shot depth
        acquisition at 36.6 MHz and approximately 15 dB sensitivity
        improvement. Its 227 um axial resolution is limited by the 15 nm
        optical bandwidth used, while the calculated spectral resolution and
        depth range are determined jointly by dispersion and digitizer
        performance. The source's higher-order theory shows that third-order
        dispersion must be included for sufficiently broadband operation.
        The deeper reusable architecture is that when the transform that
        creates information resolution also causes signal loss, distributed
        gain can be embedded inside the transform to preserve detectability.
        This does not eliminate engineering trade-offs; it relocates them to
        gain noise, pump power, nonlinearity, gain bandwidth, detector dynamic
        range, and pulse-overlap constraints.
