  # 3. BOOTSTRAP CAPSULE
  # ============================================================

  bootstrap_capsule:

    class:
      DERIVED

    text: >
      Chen and Yan model short double-stranded DNA as a discrete semiflexible
      polymer with local mechanical defects represented either as flexible
      hinges of reduced bending rigidity or as kinks with a preferred bending
      angle. Defects may be fixed at specific sites or thermally excited with a
      free-energy penalty. A site-dependent transfer-matrix method is used to
      calculate force-extension curves, effective persistence lengths,
      end-to-end distance distributions, J-factors, and looping probabilities
      under different boundary conditions. The source predicts that a single
      defect in ~100 nm DNA can produce measurable extension shifts, that
      fixed defects strongly broaden and shorten the end-to-end distance
      distribution, that excited defects disproportionately enhance highly
      bent conformations, and that short-DNA cyclization can increase by many
      orders of magnitude. Defect position and relative separation matter
      strongly for unconstrained looping, while parallel cyclization has
      distinct trace-symmetry behavior. The deepest structural result is that
      different mechanical observables probe different projections of the same
      local defect energy landscape, with sensitivity increasing as the DNA
      configuration demands stronger curvature.
