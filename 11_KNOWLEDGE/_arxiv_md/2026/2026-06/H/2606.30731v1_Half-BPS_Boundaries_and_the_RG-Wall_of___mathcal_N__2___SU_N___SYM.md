Abstract: We identify the 3d theory that realizes the RG-wall interface of 4d N = 2
SU(N) super-Yang-Mills, interpolating between the UV Lagrangian and the IR Seiberg-
Witten effective description.
The same theory also describes the low-energy boundary
condition that corresponds to giving half-BPS Dirichlet boundary condition in the La-
grangian description of 4d N = 2 SU(N) super-Yang-Mills. The theory is a 3d N = 2
SCFT that can be obtained as a massive deformation of the T[SU(N)] theory, which is
the S-duality interface of 4d N = 4 SU(N) SYM. As the main validating tests, we match
half-indices and discuss non-trivial consistency conditions when colliding such interfaces.

Introduction and Summary
Quantum field theories admit natural constructions involving coupled D-dimensional bulk
theories and (D−1)-dimensional theories supported on codimension-one loci. These setups
include interfaces between different theories, codimension-1 defects and boundaries, and
provide a framework in which the dynamics of the D-dimensional and (D −1)-dimensional
degrees of freedom are tightly interrelated.
Interesting interfaces involving strongly coupled (D−1)-dimensional theories can arise
naturally when considering dualities or deformations of the D-dimensional theory. A promi-
nent example is provided by S-duality walls in 4d N = 4 super-Yang-Mills [1–3], realized
as interacting 3d theories, denoted as T[G], that implement the action of S-duality on su-
persymmetric boundary conditions for the theory in the bulk. In this sense, S-duality walls
provide the realization of S-duality as an interface between two equivalent descriptions of
the 4d theory.
A different, naturally occurring, type of interface can be obtained by turning on an
RG-flow on half of spacetime. The resulting interface, which now bridges between a UV
and an IR theory, is denoted as “RG-wall” or “RG-interface” and is the main topic of this
paper.
The concept of RG-walls was first formalized for 2d CFTs [4] and the first example
for a 4d SQFT was provided by Dimofte, Gaiotto and van der Veen in [5] for N = 2
SU(2) gauge theories. In the case of pure SU(2) SYM, the RG-wall is described by a
simple three-dimensional model with four supercharges, consisting of two free chirals and
a background Chern-Simons interaction. This simple theory naturally appears as the RG-
interface between the UV Lagrangian description of N = 2 SU(2) SYM and the low energy
effective description provided by the Seiberg-Witten (SW) solution in the strong-coupling
region of the Coulomb branch [6, 7].
In this work we present the 3d N = 2 theory that describes the RG-wall for pure
N = 2 SU(N) super-Yang-Mills, thus generalizing to higher rank the result for SU(2) of
[5]. The RG-wall theory, that we denote as TWall, can be obtained as a mass deformation
of the S-duality wall of N = 4 SYM, namely T[SU(N)], followed by gauging some Abelian
symmetries. The particular mass deformation of T[SU(N)] was first introduced in [8, 9]
and breaks 3d SUSY from N = 4 to N = 2, while simultaneously breaking the global
symmetry to SU(N) × U(1)N−1. The resulting 3d theory, denoted as G[SU(N)] in [8, 9],

There are various interesting directions that one can follow from here. A first natural
extension of the proposal, as well as an additional check of it, would be to study the
analogous problem from the point of view of the half-four-sphere partition function [26, 27].
Possibly, studying the mass deformation of the S-dual setup of N = 4 SYM [3]. On a more
physical note, it would also be interesting to study implications of the RG-wall on 4d N = 2
theories. For example, it was shown that RG-walls between 2d minimal models [4] encode
the mapping of operators between the two theories. It would be interesting to extend this
analysis to the case of RG-walls of 4d SQFTs. Similarly, we expect the RG-wall to play
a role in the study of consequences on different possible choices of the global form of the
gauge group of 4d N = 2 SYM [28–30], in the Seiberg-Witten theory, see e.g. [31–35].
Another natural direction is to study analogous boundary problems for different N = 2
gauge theories, such as the SQCD and even quiver theories. We also expect this broader
class of RG-walls to be related to other types of walls, such as the braid duality wall of
N = 2 SQCD of [36, 37], duality walls for quiver theories [38], or to the R-flow theories
of [39]. It is also quite intriguing to consider the possibility to study theories with lower
SUSY such as N = 1 SYM and their relation with the domain walls of [40].
A second direction regards the possible geometric construction of the TWall theory. In
their first appearance, the RG-walls for 4d N = 2 SU(2) gauge theories were obtained from
the compactification of the 6d a1 N = (2, 0) theory on particular framed three-manifolds
with boundary [5, 12, 41]. It is then quite natural to expect that the TWall, our proposal
for the RG-wall of SU(N) SYM, can be obtained by a suitable compactification of the 6d
aN−1 N = (2, 0) theory on the same three-manifolds. On more general grounds, it would
be interesting to further explore the landscape of 3d N = 2 theories resulting from generic
compactifications of the 6d aN−1 theory for N > 1, for example by relating the TWall
theory to the construction of [42]. In this context, the 3d N = 2 mirror-like dualities of
[8, 9, 43, 44], as well as the alternative IR-dual Lagrangian description of the TWall theory
that we provide in this work, could facilitate this task and reveal connections with geometry.
The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2 we describe our proposal for the
RG boundary problem in N = 2 SYM, providing a heuristic derivation and discussing the
details of the proposal itself. In Section 3 we tackle interface setups and provide consistency
checks of our proposal for the RG-wall of N = 2 SYM by considering collision of interfaces.
We discuss a possible connection with the Acharya-Vafa domain walls of N = 1 SYM. In
Section 4 we compute the half-indices related to the boundary setups. The perturbative
match of such half-indices between the UV and the IR constitutes the most stringent test
of our results. In Section 5 we provide similar checks in the context of the interfaces. The
appendix A contains a review of the 3d theories of main interest in this paper. We also
discuss novel 3d dualities that have not yet appeared in the literature.

Bridging between the UV and IR of 4d N = 2 SYM
In this section we present the main result of this paper, which is an explicit Lagrangian
for the 3d N = 2 theory describing the RG-wall of 4d N = 2 SU(N) super-Yang-Mills.
This result generalizes to higher rank the SU(2) case proposed by Dimofte-Gaiotto-van der
Veen in [5]. We start by describing half-BPS boundary conditions for N = 2 SYM.
We consider N = 2 SYM on half of flat space R3 × R+ where R3 is parametrized
by x0,1,2 and R+ is parametrized by x3 ≥0, with the boundary sitting at x3 = 0. One
can define half-BPS boundary conditions for 4d N = 2 gauge theories which preserve 3d
N = 2 supersymmetry at the boundary, in the spirit of [2] (see also [5, 23, 38, 45–49]).
On the boundary the SU(2)R R-symmetry breaks to a Cartan U(1)R and the 4d N = 2
vector multiplet reorganize into 3d N = 2 vector and chiral multiplets. A 4d N = 2 vector
multiplet has on-shell bosonic field content (Aµ, ϕ), where we parameterize the complex
scalar in terms of two real fields as: ϕ = X + iY . With a suitable choice of embedding
of the 3d N = 2 superconformal group into the 4d N = 2 one, the 3d N = 2 vector
has bosonic field content (Ai, X), with i = 0, 1, 2, and the chiral has bosonic field content
(A3, Y ) which combine into a complex scalar1. The gaugini similarly split between the two
3d multiplets according to supersymmetry transformations (see [49, 50] for more details).
To preserve half of supersymmetry on the boundary one has to choose consistent b.c. for
the fields sitting in the same 3d supermultiplet. We consider half-BPS boundary conditions
for N = 2 SU(N) SYM which involve Dirichlet boundary conditions for the boundary 3d
N = 2 vector multiplet and Neumann for the chiral multiplet. We denote this b.c. as
Dirichlet-like, or simply as D:

2.1
S-duality of Dirichlet b.c. in N = 4 SYM
Let us consider the maximally supersymmetric setup given by N = 4 SU(N) SYM on
R3 × R+ with half-BPS Dirichlet boundary conditions for the gauge fields.
In N = 4
SYM the coupling τ is exactly marginal and we can relate the theory at weak coupling
Im(τ) >> 1 to strong coupling Im(τ ′) << 1 using S-duality which maps τ →τ ′ = −1/τ. A
problem similar to that posed for N = 2 SYM previously, consists of starting from N = 4

To perform the deformation, we first notice that in the N = 2 language there is a

by weakly gauging U(1)A and turning on a suitable VEV for its complex scalar component.
What is left to understand is how this deformation is projected on the boundary and what
is its effect. To solve this problem we note the following points:
• The 4d U(1)A global symmetry of the bulk remains as a global symmetry also on the
boundary and it is identified with the commutant of the U(1)R 3d N = 2 R-symmetry
subgroup inside the SU(2) × SU(2) N = 4 R-symmetry.
• The U(1)A background N = 2 vector multiplet, involved in the weakly gauging
procedure, decomposes on the boundary into a 3d N = 2 vector and a 3d N = 2
chiral, i.e. a N = 4 vector. In particular, as described at the beginning of Section 2,
the complex scalar m splits on the boundary so that its real part becomes the real
scalar sitting inside the 3d N = 2 vector, while the imaginary part, together with
the orthogonal component of the gauge field, parameterize the complex scalar of the
3d chiral multiplet.
• By giving N boundary conditions to the 4d N = 2 vector, the 3d N = 2 vector also
receives Neumann-like b.c. and thus we are allowed to turn on a VEV for its real
scalar component. This implies that we can turn on a real non-zero value for m on
all R3 × R+, including the boundary, in an half-BPS way.
• Similarly, suitable real VEVs for the scalar in the dynamical 4d N = 2 vector multi-
plet correspond to real masses for the SU(N)H symmetry of T[SU(N)], the one that
is coupled to the bulk trough the boundary gauging. As we do not want to Higgs the
4d gauge group, we do not turn on these VEVs.
All in all, we can conclude that turning on a real value of m in the bulk, it corresponds
to an axial real mass deformation associated to the U(1)A subgroup of the 3d N = 4
R-symmetry, which breaks SUSY to N = 2. The T[SU(N)] theory is then deformed by
this real mass and, in principle, we expect it to flow to a 3d N = 2 interacting SCFT as
it should possess, for example, conserved currents and operators to be able to properly
couple it to the bulk.

The RG-wall as an interface
In this section we consider setup consisting of interfaces in N = 2 SYM. Roughly speaking
there are two situations at hand. We can consider an interface between the UV description
of N = 2 SYM and an IR effective description, which is naturally realized by the TWall
theory. The second possibility instead consists of considering an interface between two
different IR descriptions which are related by low-energy electromagnetic dualities.
In
this section we construct and describe all these setups and discuss non-trivial consistency
conditions arising from the collision of multiple interfaces. In Section 5 we provide clear
computations of these setups from localization and present high-precision checks of these
consistency conditions.
Indeed, one can consider setups where the collision of two RG-walls produces an inter-
face between two identical descriptions of the theory, either between two UV descriptions
or between the same IR effective theory. In these situations, we expect that the interface
becomes “transparent”, which simply means that it must be IR-equivalent to having no
interface at all. Even in these simpler cases, the generic proposal of the TWall theory given
in Section 2, proves to satisfy a number of non-trivial properties as a 3d N = 2 SCFT
encoding all the desired properties.
We will start the discussion with the interface between the UV and IR description, fol-
lowed by the case of two IR effective descriptions and, finally, we comment the transparent
interface between two UV descriptions.

the correct 3d theory implementing the domain wall of pure N = 1 SYM. It would be
extremely interesting to study such a deformation for generic SU(N) gauge group and to
make this analysis more precise, we leave this to future work.

In this appendix we review in more detail properties of the G[SU(N)] theory, starting from

Real mass deformation of the T[SU(N)] theory
The G[SU(N)] theory can be de-
fined as the result of a real mass deformation of the T[SU(N)] theory associated to the
axial subgroup of the R-symmetry U(1)A, which has the effect of breaking the R-symmetry
as SU(2)H × SU(2)C →U(1)R and therefore supersymmetry from N = 4 to N = 2.
We observe that for non-zero value of the axial real mass parameter mA, there is an
interacting vacuum where the flavor SU(N) symmetry is not broken, only if we turn on
non-zero FI parameters satisfying:
Yi+1 −Yi = 2mA
(A.20)
and non-zero CB parameters. That is we turn on a VEV for the σ(n) real scalar component
in the dynamical vector multiplets, that transform in the adjoint representation of the U(n)
gauge node, as:
σ(n) = (N −n)mAI(n)
(A.21)
where I(n) is the n × n identity matrix. Indeed, this deformation does not Higgs the gauge
groups as the VEV is diagonal. From the data of the deformation it is possible to compute
effective masses for the chiral fields and thus the Lagrangian of the resulting theory, as well
as Chern-Simons interactions produced by integrating out massive chiral multiplets. For
example, all the bifundamental hypermultiplets acquire an effective mass:
meff = mA ± mA ,
(A.22)
where the plus sign refers to the chiral which is in the fundamental representation of U(n)
and antifundamental of U(n + 1). Oppositely, the anti-chiral is represented by the minus
sign and is in the antifundamental of U(n) and fundamental of U(n + 1). Consequently,
