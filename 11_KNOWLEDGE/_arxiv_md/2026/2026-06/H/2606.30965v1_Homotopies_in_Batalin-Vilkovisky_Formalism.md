Abstract
We review the notion of homotopy of quantum master actions in geometric Batalin-
Vilkovisky formalism. Then we construct new examples of such homotopies, coming
from renormalization group flow and non-infinitesimal changes of gauge fixing. Fi-
nally, we use the field redefinitions given by these homotopies to construct spans of
quantum master actions with isomorphic effective actions.

Introduction
Batalin-Vilkovisky (BV) formalism was introduced as a tool to handle perturbative gauge
quantum field theories [BV81]. Afterwards, Schwarz and Khudaverdian [Sch93; Khu04]
interpreted it in terms of odd symplectic geometry, and Ševera combined it with Wein-
stein’s idea of the symplectic category [Wei81; Šev04].
In BV formalism, a quantum field theory is given by a half-density ρ on an odd sym-
plectic manifold of BV fields, which is closed with respect to the BV operator ∆. Conse-
quently, one of many insights of homological algebra into BV formalism is a meaningful
definition of homotopy of such theories ρ1, ρ2: namely a half-density φ such ρ2−ρ1 = ∆φ.
In physics terminology, ρ is the exponential of a quantum master action S and a change of
ρ by homotopy corresponds to a canonical transformation in the sense of BV formalism.
The goal of this paper is to explain some of the consequences of this notion of homo-
topy, and to give many examples of such homotopies. These will come from symplectic
flows, the renormalization group flow and homological perturbation theory. Moreover,
we investigate the space of BV transfers in the setting of linear finite-dimensional vec-
tor spaces, where we prove that “strict” transfers onto cohomology are all homotopy
equivalent. Finally, we use these methods to construct a “fiber product” of theories with
isomorphic minimal models.
In this way, we extend the ideas of Hamiltonian flows giving homotopic theories
[Šev04], infinitesimal deformations of Lagrangian relations [Mne09; Mne08; CM09], renor-
malization group techniques of Costello [Cos07; Cos11] and spans of classical BV theories
[JSW24].

In this section, we review BV formalism, emphasizing a geometric perspective. We begin

BV formalism, the classical BV formalsim is the ℏ→0, tree level approximation to the

5.4
The space of special deformation retracts
Let us summarize what we know about the shape of the space of cyclic special deformation
retracts. If we fix V and U, the space of all cyclic SDRs V →U is a principal bundle over
the space of strict SDRs, with the structure group being the dg symplectic group of U.
We note that this group can have non-trivial topology: for example for U = T ∗[−1]Rn
with qU = 0, the group is GLn(R).
As we have seen, for U ∼= H(V, q), the base of this bundle is contractible. For big-
ger U (i.e. smaller non-degenerate isotropes J), this is not the case. For example, for
V = T ∗[−1]Rn, a differential is given by a quadratic form Sfree on Rn, and the space
of non-degenerate isotropic subspaces of dimension k is the complement of the vanishing
locus of a polynomial function on the Grassmanniann Grk(n). For n = 2, the space of
non-degenerate 1-dimensional isotropic subspaces is homeomorphic to S1, R or R ⊔R,
depending on the signature of the quadratic form Sfree [Vor26, Príklad 7].
If V
= T ∗[−1]Rn and Sfree is positive (or negative) definite, the space of non-
degenerate isotropes of fixed dimension is always connected.
Remark 5.8. In the non-symplectic setting, special deformation retracts onto cohomology
are parametrized by a choice of complements to Ker d ⊂V and Im Q ⊂Q. Since such
splittings form an affine space, the space of strict special deformation retracts is always
contractible. In our case, with a symplectic form on V , we require that the complements
are isotropic and symplectic respectively (26), which puts non-linear conditions on the
splittings.
