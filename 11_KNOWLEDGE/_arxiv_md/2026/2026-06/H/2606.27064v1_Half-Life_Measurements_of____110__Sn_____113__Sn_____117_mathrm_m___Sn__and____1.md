Half-Life Measurements of 110Sn, 113Sn, 117mSn, and 123mSn Produced via Photon
Activation of Natural Tin
O. Nusair
a,∗, D. DeVriesa, M. Toro-Gonzalez
aNorthStar Medical Radioisotopes, LLC, Beloit, WI, 53511, USA
Abstract
We report independent determinations of the ground-state half-lives of 110Sn, 113Sn, and the isomeric states 117mSn (Jπ = 11/2−)
and 123mSn (Jπ = 3/2+), produced via photon activation of natural tin using a TT-300HE Rhodotron accelerator. The activated
samples were monitored over several months using a high-purity germanium (HPGe) detector. Time-dependent γ-ray spectra were
analyzed using Gaussian peak fitting for the 280.49 keV, 391.697 keV, 158.56 keV, and 160.34 keV transitions, yielding half-lives
of (4.165±0.025) h for 110Sn, (116.08±0.94) d for 113Sn, (13.95±0.01) d for 117mSn, and (39.95±0.12) min for 123mSn. Agreement
with Nuclear Data Sheets (NDS) recommended values is generally observed for 110Sn, 113Sn, and 123mSn, with deviations consistent
within combined uncertainties when quantified using standardized differences (z-scores). In contrast, 117mSn exhibits a statistically
significant deviation from the evaluated value of (13.76 ± 0.04) d, with a z-score indicating a discrepancy well beyond expected
statistical fluctuations. This result suggests a systematic difference warranting further investigation, with potential implications for
applications relying on precise decay data, including calibration, dosimetry, and astrophysical modeling.
Keywords: photon activation, half-life, tin isotopes, HPGe spectroscopy, exponentially modified Gaussian, nuclear data
1. Introduction
Precise radionuclide half-lives are fundamental to applied
and basic nuclear science. They underpin normalization of ac-
tivation measurements used to constrain astrophysical reaction
rates, detector efficiency calibration, decay corrections in envi-
ronmental and medical assays, and nuclear data. In this work,
we revisit four tin nuclides:
110Sn (astrophysical p-process
benchmarks), 113Sn (a calibration standard), 117mSn (nuclear
medicine dosimetry), and 123mSn (nuclear data). All were pro-
duced via photon activation of natural tin and monitored by
long-term HPGe spectrometry, providing an independent cross-
check of evaluated decay data.

1.2. 113Sn: Calibration and Standards
The ground state of 113Sn decays by electron capture to 113In,
emitting well-characterized γ rays widely used for HPGe spec-
trometer calibration and tracer studies. Its half-life has been
measured repeatedly over decades. Early systematic programs
at Oak Ridge National Laboratory employed NaI(Tl), propor-
tional counters, and ionization chambers, establishing values
near ∼115 d Emery (1972). Independent γ-spectrometric mea-
surements with radiochemically purified samples corroborated
this range Lagoutine et al. (1972). Large inter-comparison ef-
forts coordinated by the International Atomic Energy Agency
(IAEA) remeasured numerous γ emitters (including 113Sn)
with calibrated 4π ionization chambers, yielding high-precision
decay constants Houtermans et al. (1980).
Subsequent re-
examinations at national standards laboratories incorporated
improved detector stability, impurity analyses, and reference-
source corrections Hoppes and Schima (1982); Unterweger
et al. (1992). These studies support the currently recommended
value T1/2 = (115.09±0.03) d in the Nuclear Data Sheets (NDS)

1.3. 117mSn: Nuclear Medicine and Dosimetry
The isomeric state 117mSn (Jπ = 11/2−) combines a mo-
noenergetic 159 keV photon suitable for Single-Photon Emis-
sion Computed Tomography (SPECT) imaging with 127–155
keV conversion electrons for localized therapy. Its decay data
are comprehensively evaluated in the NDS for A = 117 Bla-
chot (2002), and its medical applications have been explored in
modern radiopharmaceutical reviews Boschi et al. (2019). Its
∼14-day half-life enables distribution and radiopharmaceutical
synthesis, including SnCl2 and DOTA-based chelates for bone
metastasis treatment.
Traditional production routes employ
neutron or proton reactions on tin and antimony, often yield-
ing low specific activity or complex target chemistry Maslov
et al. (2011); Stevenson et al. (2015). Photonuclear production
via 118Sn(γ,n)117mSn using modern electron accelerators offers
a clean and scalable alternative Utsunomiya et al. (2011). Accu-
rate half-life data for 117mSn are critical because the NDS rec-
ommends T1/2 = (13.76 ± 0.04) d Blachot (2002), and even
small deviations from this value can propagate into decay-
correction protocols, patient dose planning, and quantitative
imaging. Such discrepancies directly affect absorbed dose esti-
mates and therapeutic efficacy, making high-precision measure-
ments essential for reliable nuclear medicine applications.
1.4. 123mSn: Nuclear Data Evaluations
The isomeric state 123mSn (Jπ = 3/2+) has a recommended
half-life of T1/2 = (40.06 ± 0.02) min according to the NDS
evaluation for A = 123 Chen (2021). This isomer plays a role in
nuclear data libraries because its decay characteristics are used
in activation analysis, decay heat calculations, and modeling of
photonuclear reaction chains. Even small deviations from the
evaluated half-life can propagate into decay-correction factors
and impact the accuracy of cross-section measurements and ap-
plied nuclear science calculations.
2. Experimental Method
2.1. Activation
A 0.6186 g high-purity natural tin target, composed of 99.3 %
Sn with a 0.7 % Cu impurity fraction, was irradiated down-
stream of a 6 mm-thick tantalum bremsstrahlung converter. The
irradiation was performed using a TT-300HE rhodotron deliv-
ering 40 MeV electrons at a beam current of 1 µA, with a nom-
inal beam spot diameter of 20 mm. The converter consists of
Ta plates, with water channels flowing between the plates as
well as in front and back of the first and last plates. The ir-
radiation geometry and particle transport are illustrated in Fig-
ure 1, where bremsstrahlung photons (yellow tracks) and elec-
trons (red tracks) are shown interacting with the converter and
target.
The normalized photon flux distribution at the target location
is shown in Figure 2. This total energy-integrated photon flux
map corresponds to a bremsstrahlung photon spectrum with
40 MeV end-point energy. To obtain the absolute flux, the color
scale must be multiplied by the electron rate for 1 µA beam cur-
rent (6.24 × 1012 e−/s). The black square in Figure 2 indicates
the tin target position. The irradiation time was 10 s to mini-
mize the buildup of potential interferences and dead-time dur-
ing the counting of short-lived nuclides such as 123mSn, while
producing sufficient 113Sn and 117mSn activities for long-term
counting.
In addition, a second irradiation was performed as part of
a broader test to measure cross sections at lower beam energy.
For this, a tin target (0.9526 g) was placed behind a single 3 mm
tungsten plate (non-cooled) and irradiated under identical con-
ditions of beam current (1 µA) and irradiation time (20 s), but
with an electron end-point energy of 36 MeV. After activation,
the target was moved for extended counting with 180 s live-time
intervals over more than one day to enable a precise determina-
tion of the 110Sn half-life. Only the 110Sn data originate from
this lower-energy irradiation; all other isotopes (113Sn, 117mSn,
and 123mSn) were measured from the first irradiation using the
6 mm Ta converter at 40 MeV.
2.2. γ-Spectral Processing and Calibration
All spectra were parsed to extract run start times, live and real
time, and channel-wise counts. For each spectrum, candidate
peaks corresponding to the selected γ lines (Ei) were located
and fitted.
Energy, Resolution and Efficiency Calibrations. To ensure ac-
curate identification of γ-ray lines and assess detector perfor-
mance, energy, resolution, and efficiency calibrations were per-
formed using standard reference peaks from multiple calibra-
tion sources.

4. Discussion
This work provides independent verification of evaluated
half-lives through photon activation of natural tin and long-term
HPGe monitoring. In several cases, these measurements repre-
sent the only determinations obtained via a photo-nuclear pro-
duction route for the isotope or its isomeric state, offering a
unique complement to the established dataset.
Generally speaking, the NDS methodology consolidates re-
sults from a wide range of experimental techniques, includ-
ing charged-particle reactions such as (α, 2n), (p, t), and (d, p),
Coulomb excitation, photo-nuclear processes, and neutron-
capture routes.
For example, the adopted half-life of 110Sn
(4.154 h) reflects a weighted average of measurements from
(α, 2nγ) reaction on 108Cd, (p, t) reaction on 112Sn, Coulomb
excitation studies, and other routes, with uncertainties primar-
ily statistical. Similarly, 113Sn values originate from activation
via (α, nγ) reaction on 110Cd, (p, nγ) reaction on 113In, (p, t) re-
action on 115Sn, and (n, γ) reaction on 112Sn, complemented by
decay studies following ε capture. For 117mSn, the NDS recom-
mendation (13.76 d) aggregates results from β−decay chains of
117In, internal transition studies, and multiple charged-particle
reactions, such as (α, nγ) reaction on 114Cd, and (d, p) reaction
on 116Sn, and neutron radiative capture on 116Sn, while 123mSn
values are based on decay monitoring after (d, p) and (n, γ) re-
actions on 122Sn.
Our approach differs in two key aspects: (i) the use of photon
activation, which enables simultaneous production of multiple
isotopes under identical conditions and minimizes contamina-

Overall, these results underscore the importance of indepen-
dent verification using alternative production mechanisms and
stringent uncertainty criteria. Periodic reevaluation of medi-
cally relevant isotopes remains essential to maintain confidence
in nuclear data libraries and their downstream applications.
5. Conclusions
Two short photon activations of natural tin were performed
to produce measurable activities of 110Sn, 113Sn, 117mSn, and
123mSn suitable for short- and long-term HPGe monitoring. The
primary irradiation used a 6 mm Ta converter and a 40 MeV
electron beam, while a secondary irradiation at 36 MeV with a
single 3 mm W plate was carried out as part of a broader photo-
nuclear reaction cross-section test. Extended counting intervals
(180 s) were applied only to the second irradiation to enable a
precise determination of the 110Sn half-life; all other isotopes
were analyzed from the first irradiation.
Based on isotope-specific peak models (single Gaussian,
triple Gaussian, and Gaussian with tail) and iterative decay cor-
rection, the following half-lives were determined:
• 110Sn: T1/2 = (4.165 ± 0.025) h,
• 113Sn: T1/2 = (116.08 ± 0.94) d,
• 117mSn: T1/2 = (13.95 ± 0.01) d,
• 123mSn: T1/2 = (39.95 ± 0.12) min.
These results agree with NDS recommendations within about
1% (z-score ≲1), except for 117mSn, which shows a larger de-
viation. The measured 123mSn half-life also differs from the
evaluated value, underscoring the need to periodically reassess
decay data. Independent determinations based on distinct acti-
vation and counting strategies strengthen confidence in adopted
decay parameters for tracer and calibration applications while
highlighting the importance of maintaining accuracy for dosi-
metric and analytical consistency.
