HYPERELLIPTIC ATKIN–LEHNER QUOTIENTS OF SHIMURA
CURVES
ERAN ASSAF AND SACHI HASHIMOTO
Abstract. We work towards completely classifying all hyperelliptic Atkin–Lehner quotients
of Shimura curves X0(D, N)/W with level N coprime to D and W ≤W0(D, N), extending,
on the one hand, a result of Ogg that provided such a classification for the trivial quotients
(the case W = 1), and on the other hand, results of Furumoto and Hasegawa that provided
such a classification for modular curves (the case D = 1). As a byproduct of our methods,
building on the works of Guo and Yang, we also obtain models for some quotients of genus
at most two, answering some questions of Padurariu and Saia.
1. Introduction
1.1. Background and motivation. Let D, N be positive integers, such that µ(D) = 1 (i.e.
D is squarefree with an even number of prime factors) and (D, N) = 1. Let B = BD be the
quaternion algebra of discriminant D over Q, and let O ⊆B be an Eichler order of level
N. The Shimura curves X0(D, N) classify abelian surfaces with (potential) quaternionic
multiplication by O and the study of their low degree points has a long history, beginning
with [Ogg83], who showed they only have rational points for D = 1.
A basic invariant of a curve X is its gonality γ(X) – the least degree of a map from the
curve to P1. For example, the gonality 2 curves are the (geometrically) hyperelliptic curves.
We say a curve is subhyperelliptic if its gonality is at most 2. In [Ogg74], Ogg classified which
of the curves X0(D, N) are hyperelliptic. Following this line of research, Padurariu and Saia,
in [PS25a], classified the trigonal (γ(X) = 3) ones and began to classify bielliptic such curves,
using this classification to determine which ones have infinitely many quadratic points.
The curve X0(D, N) admits a natural action by the associated Atkin–Lehner group
W0(D, N) = NB×(O)/O×, leading to another natural source for low degree points, namely
the quotient curves X0(D, N)/W for subgroups W ≤W0(D, N). Study of the rational points
on these curves began in [PS23], where the authors find rational points when X0(D, N)
is hyperelliptic. However, the broader question of classifying the hyperelliptic quotients
X0(D, N)/W remains open when D > 1. In this work, we take the first steps towards
answering this question,
Question 1. For which values of D and N, and which subgroups W ≤W0(D, N) is the
curve X0(D, N)/W geometrically hyperelliptic?
The case D = 1, where the curve X0(1, N) ≃X0(N) is isomorphic to the classical modular
curve, classifying cyclic N-isogenies between elliptic curves, has been treated in a series of
papers by Hasegawa and Hashimoto [HH96], Hasegawa [Has97], and Furumoto and Hasegawa
[FH99]. They reach a full classification by applying a variety of methods, some of which
directly use the q-expansions of classical modular forms and explicit equations embedding
the curves in projective space.

Interestingly, the case D > 1 has proved greater resistance to such methods. Although
methods for producing q-expansions of quaternionic modular forms around CM points have
been described in the literature [VW14, Nel15], in practice, a robust rigorous version has not
been implemented, and so one cannot obtain results that are provably correct.
There have been numerous attempts to produce explicit equations for Shimura curves with
D > 1, see [Tak77, Elk98, GR04, GR06, Yan18, PS25b], constructing models for curves of
genus ≤2, and [Mol12, GY17], producing equations for hyperelliptic curves. However, since
the methods of [Mol12] and [GY17] require knowledge of the hyperelliptic map, one cannot
use them directly to detect hyperellipticity. Moreover, none of these methods have been
robustly implemented.
This paper generalizes the methods of [HH96, Has97, FH99] for D > 1, attempting to
classify as many curves as possible without resorting to explicit equations. As some of the
methods require explicit equations for some genus 0 quotients, we have also implemented a
version of the method described in [GY17], using Borcherds products and Schofer’s formula to
construct equations for these quotients. These models, in turn, answer some open questions
raised in [PS25b].
1.2. Results. We begin by reducing the question to a finite problem.
Theorem 1.2.1. There are finitely many D, N, W such that X0(D, N)/W is hyperelliptic.
We prove this using point counts over finite fields. There is an upper bound on the number
of points of a hyperelliptic curve coming from the fact that it possesses a degree 2 map
X →P1. On the other hand, we have a lower bound from supersingular points. These bounds
cross for large enough (D, N), reducing the computation to 2342 pairs of (D, N).
The main result of our paper is the following more explicit description of the set of
(geometrically) hyperelliptic curves.
Theorem 1.2.2. There exist sets Sh and Su with #Sh = 3423 and #Su = 4207, and a set S
with Sh ⊆S ⊆Su such that X = X0(D, N)/W is hyperelliptic if and only if (D, N, W) ∈S.
The sets Sh and Su describe a set of curves that are proved to be hyperelliptic, and a
candidate set of curves containing all hyperelliptic curves, respectively, are given explicitly,
and can be found on our GitHub repository [AH26] at https://github.com/assaferan/
ShimuraCurveALQuotients/blob/v1.2/data/curves_after_UpdateCurves8.dat.

1.3. Overall strategy. The broad strategy is to first reduce to a finite list of curves, then
filter the list of candidates using a variety of techniques.
The covering structure of the Shimura curves X0(D, N)/W plays an important role in
determining and propagating the subhyperellipticity. An important basic application of the
covering is closure principles, which are used throughout. The following two principles are
logically equivalent, but will be applied in different settings.
Proposition 1.3.1 (Closure principles).
(1) (“Downward closure”) If X is subhyperelliptic then any covered curve X ↠Y is also
subhyperelliptic.
(2) (“Upward closure”) If Y is not subhyperelliptic then any cover X ↠Y is not subhy-
perelliptic.
Proof. This follows from γ(Y ) ≤γ(X), see [Poo07, Prop. A.1.(vii)].
□
The star curves X∗
0(D, N) := X0(D, N)/W0(D, N) are minimal with respect to the covering
structure. If a star curve is not subhyperelliptic, by upward closure, no cover is. We apply
this in the following way. To reduce to a finite problem, we first reduce the list of star curves
to the list of potentially subhyperelliptic star curves. By upward closure, we can then consider
only the covers of the subhyperelliptic star curves. The finiteness of the subhyperelliptic star
curve candidates also shows finiteness of all candidates X0(D, N)/W.
After reducing to a finite list of star curves we apply a variety of methods to determine if
a star curve is hyperelliptic or not. Then we generate all covers of the list subhyperelliptic
and potentially hyperelliptic star curves. We use various methods to determine if curves in
this bigger list are hyperelliptic or not. At the end of the process, we are left with a list of
subhyperelliptic curves, and a list of remaining candidates which are potentially hyperelliptic
but whose status remains unknown.
Since X0(N) ≃X0(1, N) we take advantage of the opportunity to verify the results of our
code against the results and tables in [HH96, Has97, FH99]. In particular, in this paper we
include the case D = 1 everywhere.
1.4. Overview of paper. In Section 2 we prove the reduction to a finite list of pairs (D, N),
which proves Theorem 1.2.1. Next, Section 3 recalls background material about optimal
embeddings and fixed points of Atkin–Lehner involutions in order to derive a genus formula for
the curves X0(D, N)/W. In this section we also introduce results deriving from a proposition
of Ogg which filter out non-hyperelliptic curves by counting fixed points of involutions. In
Section 4 we use trace formulas to count points over finite fields. We also introduce the extra
modular non-Atkin–Lehner involutions that appear when 4 or 9 divide the level N. However,
we defer the trace formulas for these other operators to Appendix A. Section 5 discusses
several tools for determining hyperellipticity that leverage the covering structure of the curves
X0(D, N)/W. In Section 6 we discuss several techniques for forming isomorphisms between
these curves and in Section 7 we discuss the application of Weil polynomials.
We discuss the results of applying all of these tests to the candidate curves in Section 8.
We also discuss potential methods for future study and limitations of the current method.
Finally in Section 9 we discuss an implementation of the methods in [GY17] to compute
models of low genus Shimura curves, and use this to answer some questions of [PS25b].

9.1. Equations of hyperelliptic Shimura curves. The method of [GY17] can be used
to address some of these ambiguities. Their idea is to use the exceptional isomorphism to
O(2, 1), and regard the Shimura curve as a Shimura variety for the orthogonal group, therefore
amenable to construction of weakly holomorphic modular forms with prescribed divisors.
Using Schofer’s formula [Sch09], one can then evaluate these functions at CM points. When
a curve exhibits a double cover to P1, one can construct forms supported on the ramification
divisors and by solving linear and quadratic equations arising from evaluation at CM points
and then reconstruct equations for the curve.
9.2. Current limitations. We have implemented the described algorithm, but ran into
two difficulties. The first is a theoretical gap – the evaluation of Schofer’s formula at non-
fundamental CM discriminants when the weight of the Borcherds form is not 0 needs some
modification. However, the correction term does not appear in the literature.
The second is in the implementation aspect. The proof relies on computing spaces of
weakly holomorphic modular forms of level roughly DN by spanning them with eta quotients.
Determining the possible eta quotients is done via integer programming. For large values of D
and N, determining the possible eta quotients and narrowing this to a basis of eta quotients
of sufficient precision around both 0 and ∞requires a massive investment of resources.
