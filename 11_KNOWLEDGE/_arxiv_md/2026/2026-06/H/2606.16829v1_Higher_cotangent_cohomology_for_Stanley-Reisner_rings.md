HIGHER COTANGENT COHOMOLOGY FOR
STANLEY-REISNER RINGS
NATHAN ILTEN, FRANCESCO MEAZZINI, AND ANDREA PETRACCI
Abstract. Inspired by work of Altmann and Christophersen, we study the
graded pieces of the cotangent cohomology T i
SK, i ≥3 of the Stanley-Reisner
ring SK associated to a simplicial complex K. We prove a localization formula
allowing one to reduce to the case of negative weights. Our results give a com-
plete description of T 3 and T 4 in terms of the topology of K whenever K is a
flag complex. As an application, we give a sufficient criterion for the vanishing
of T 3 for simplicial spheres, classify two-spheres that have vanishing T 3, and
show that the boundary complex of the dual associahedron has vanishing T 3.
Our results make use of the arborescent resolutions considered by Hancharuk,
Laurent-Gengoux, and Strobl. We give an alternative and self-contained treat-
ment of these resolutions that may be of independent interest.

1. Introduction
1.1. Background. Let S be a finitely generated (commutative) algebra over a field
k of characteristic zero. The cotangent cohomology modules T i
S/k for i ∈Z≥0 in-
troduced in [And74] are finitely generated S-modules that encode aspects of the
deformation theory of Spec S: T 0
S/k is just the set of k-linear derivations from S
to itself, T 1
S/k is the space of isomorphism classes of first order deformations of
Spec S over k, T 2
S/k contains the obstructions to lifting infinitesimal deformations
of Spec S, and the higher cotangent cohomology T i
S/k for i > 2 is relevant for de-
formations of complete intersections in Spec S, see e.g. [BC91, CI17]. Likewise, the
higher cotangent cohomology modules T i
S/k are important in derived deformation
theory, see e.g. [PV21, §6]. For an example geometric application in our setting,
see Corollary 9.9 in §9.5.
While the modules T i
S/k for i = 0, 1, 2 have relatively concrete descriptions, they
can still be challenging to explicitly compute in practice. For the higher cotangent
cohomology modules the situation is even more challenging: in characteristic zero
they can be described via Harrison cohomology [Lod98, Proposition 4.5.13], but
this is not particularly amenable to explicit computations. It is thus an interesting
problem to give more explicit descriptions of the T i
S/k for special classes of rings S.
Along these lines, Altmann and Christophersen [AC04] studied the cotangent
cohomology modules T 1
S/k and T 2
S/k when S is a Stanley-Reisner ring, that is, a
quotient of a polynomial ring by a square-free1 monomial ideal. Recall that to any
simplicial complex K on vertex set V, there is an associated Stanley-Reisner ideal
IK ⊆k[xv | v ∈V], see §3. The ideal IK is precisely the square-free monomial ideal
generated by products of variables whose corresponding vertices do not belong to
a common face, and all square-free monomial ideals arise in this way. Hence for a

1.5. Method. The cotangent cohomology modules T i
S/k may be defined as follows.
Let P• be a Koszul-Tate resolution of S, that is, a semifree commutative differential
(non-negatively) graded algebra (cdga) that is quasi-isomorphic to S, see §2.1. Then
T i
S/k is the ith cohomology of the complex Der•(P•, S) of graded derivations.
The first challenge is finding an explicit Koszul-Tate resolution P• of S. Viewing
S as a quotient of a polynomial ring R, Hancharuk, Laurent-Gengoux, and Strobl
[HLGS24] show how to construct such P• by starting with a free resolution of S as an
R-module only making finitely many choices along the way. They call the resulting
resolution an arborescent resolution of S. In §2.2, we give an alternative inductive
construction of arborescent resolutions that avoids some of the technicalities of
[HLGS24].
When S is the quotient of a polynomial ring R by a monomial ideal I, S has a
canonical free resolution as an R-module, the so-called Taylor resolution (see §2.4).
In fact, the Taylor resolution has the structure of a cdga, but is not semifree. The
results of [HLGS24, §4.2] imply that there is a canonical arborescent resolution of
S in this case, see §2.3 for a self-contained treatment. We make this resolution as
explicit as we can in §2.5. In §2.6, we use this resolution to define explicit complexes
W c
• of finite-dimensional vector spaces whose ith homology is dual to the weight c
graded piece of T i
