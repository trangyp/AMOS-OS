SHUHO KANDA
Abstract. We prove that every simply connected nilpotent Lie group endowed with a left-
invariant nilpotent complex structure is biholomorphic to Cn. Moreover, we construct such a
biholomorphism explicitly by polynomial maps in exponential coordinates. As a consequence,
every lattice in such a Lie group admits a free, properly discontinuous and cocompact action on
Cn by holomorphic polynomial automorphisms. We interpret this consequence as a holomorphic
analogue of polynomial crystallographic actions introduced by Dekimpe, Igodt, and Lee.

1. Introduction
1.1. A motivating example and the questions. The main objects studied in this paper are
simply connected nilpotent Lie groups G endowed with left-invariant complex structures J. The
simplest non-abelian example appears in the Kodaira–Thurston manifold.
Let H3 denote the Heisenberg group (see Section 5). The Kodaira–Thurston manifold X is
obtained by equipping G = H3(R) × R with a left-invariant complex structure J and taking the
quotient by the lattice Γ = H3(Z) × Z:
X = (Γ\G, J).

other hand, the same manifold can also be constructed as the quotient of C2 by the following affine,

A weaker, and perhaps more natural, lattice version of this question is the following:
Question 1.3 (⇔Question 4.6). Let G be a simply connected nilpotent Lie group of real dimension
2n, and let Γ ⊂G be a lattice.
Does Γ admit an action on Cn by holomorphic polynomial
automorphisms which is free, properly discontinuous and cocompact?
If (G, J) satisfies the property stated in Question 1.2, then the desired action in Question 1.3
is obtained by setting the action of Γ on Cn by γ · z := Φ ◦Lγ ◦Φ−1(z). Conversely, one may
also wonder whether an action of Γ as in Question 1.3 should come from a picture like the one in
Question 1.2.
We also note that, in the special case where G is a complex nilpotent Lie group and J is induced
by its complex structure, the property stated in Question 1.2 holds in an elementary way. Indeed,
the exponential map identifies G biholomorphically with Cn, and left translations are polynomial
in the corresponding coordinates by the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula.
1.2. Background for the two questions. Question 1.1 was explicitly formulated as a conjecture
by Hasegawa [Has10]. To the best of the author’s knowledge, there has been little progress on this
question. For solvable Lie groups, the analogous statement is false: as in the case of Inoue surfaces,
the universal cover may be biholomorphic to C × H rather than to C2. In this broader solvable
setting, however, it remains open whether the universal cover is always Stein. Even in the nilpotent
case, the Steinness of (G, J) is still unknown in general. The results of this paper answer Question
1.1 affirmatively for a certain class of left-invariant complex structures J.
Question 1.2 has a deep background going back to Milnor’s 1977 paper [Mil77]. The relevant
class of groups in this context is the class of polycyclic-by-finite groups, which contains the lattices
in nilpotent Lie groups considered here. This background is explained in detail, for example, in
[Dek96], [DI97b], and [Dek00]: we briefly recall it here.
In 1977, Milnor asked whether every torsion-free polycyclic-by-finite group Γ occurs as the
fundamental group of a compact complete affinely flat manifold. Equivalently, this asks whether Γ
admits a free, properly discontinuous, and cocompact affine action on Rm, where m is the Hirsch
length of Γ. Such an action of Γ is called an affine crystallographic action [FG83], [GS94]. This
question remained open for a long time, until Y. Benoist found counterexamples among nilpotent
groups [Ben92], [Ben95]. Later, Burde and Grunewald found simpler counterexamples [BG95],
[Bur96].
Inspired by this problem, Dekimpe, Igodt, and Lee introduced the notion of a polynomial crys-
tallographic action in [DIL96]. This is a polynomial analogue of an affine crystallographic action,
obtained by replacing affine actions with polynomial actions: see Definition 4.2. When Γ is nilpo-
tent, it is a lattice in the nilpotent Lie group GΓ, called the real Malcev completion of Γ, which is
diffeomorphic to Rm through exponential coordinates. Thus the action of Γ on GΓ by left transla-
tions gives, via the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula, a polynomial action on Rm. Dekimpe and
Igodt proved in [DI97a], [DI97b] that this existence result extends to polycyclic-by-finite groups.
Subsequent work has studied, among other things, bounds on the degrees of such polynomial
actions.
In this paper, we restrict our attention to nilpotent groups and consider a holomorphic analogue
of these notions. Here, Ph(Cn) denotes the group of holomorphic polynomial automorphisms of
Cn.
Definition 1.4 (= Definition 4.3). Let Γ be a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group with
h(Γ) = 2n. A holomorphic polynomial crystallographic action of Γ is a homomorphism
ρ : Γ →Ph(Cn)

Question 1.3 asks whether every torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group Γ with even

its Lie algebra. A left-invariant complex structure J on G is equivalent to an integrable complex
structure, also denoted by J, on g. We recall the notion of a nilpotent complex structure.
Definition 1.5 (= Definition 2.3). A complex structure J on a nilpotent Lie algebra g is called
nilpotent if there exists a complex basis {Zi}n

Nilpotent complex structures were introduced in [CFGU97] in the study of compact nilmanifolds.
In low dimensions, this condition is satisfied by many complex structures on nilpotent Lie algebras.
See Remark 2.4 for further details.
The main theorem of this paper is the following.
Theorem 1.6 (= Theorem 3.1). Let G be a simply connected nilpotent Lie group of dimension
2n with a left-invariant nilpotent complex structure J. Then there exists a biholomorphic map
Φ: (G, J) →Cn.
Furthermore, Φ and Φ−1 can be taken to be polynomial in the exponential
coordinates on G.
We now reinterpret this consequence from the point of view of the lattice Γ, rather than the
Lie group G.
Let Γ be a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group with h(Γ) = 2n.
By
Malcev’s theorem, there exists a uniquely determined simply connected nilpotent Lie group GΓ
in which Γ embeds as a lattice. We say that Γ is of nilpotent-complex type if GΓ admits a left-
invariant nilpotent complex structure. As an immediate consequence of Theorem 1.6, we obtain
the following:
Theorem 1.7 (= Theorem 4.5). Let Γ be a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group with
h(Γ) = 2n. If Γ is of nilpotent-complex type, then Γ admits a holomorphic polynomial crystallo-
graphic action.
At present, no counterexample to Question 1.3 seems to be known. Thus it is completely unclear
whether the converse of the preceding theorem should hold. As a possible necessary condition for
the existence of a holomorphic polynomial crystallographic action, one might at least expect that
the Malcev completion GΓ admits a left-invariant complex structure, but even this is not known.
On the other hand, under an additional boundedness assumption, this expectation does hold.
More precisely, in [BD02], Benoist and Dekimpe proved a uniqueness theorem for polynomial crys-
tallographic actions of bounded degree, that is, for polynomial crystallographic actions whose de-
grees are uniformly bounded. Using this result, we show that if Γ admits a holomorphic polynomial
crystallographic action of bounded degree, then its Malcev completion GΓ admits a left-invariant
complex structure.
Theorem 1.8 (= Theorem 4.8). Let Γ be a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group with
h(Γ) = 2n. If Γ admits a holomorphic polynomial crystallographic action of bounded degree, then
GΓ admits a left-invariant complex structure J such that (GΓ, J) ≃Cn.

4. Holomorphic polynomial crystallographic action
The preceding sections are concerned with a simply connected nilpotent Lie group G endowed
with a left-invariant nilpotent complex structure J. In this section we record a consequence for
lattices in G and place it in the context of polynomial crystallographic actions in the sense of
Dekimpe, Igodt and Lee. The point of view is the following: we fix a lattice Γ ⊂G and ask
whether Γ can be realized as the fundamental group of a compact quotient of Cn by holomorphic
polynomial automorphisms.
We first recall the group-theoretic language used below. Let Γ be a finitely generated nilpotent
group. Its Hirsch length h(Γ) is the number of infinite cyclic factors in a polycyclic series for Γ,
equivalently, if

4.1. Real and holomorphic polynomial crystallographic action. Let P(Rm) be the group
of polynomial diffeomorphisms of Rm, namely polynomial maps whose inverse is again polynomial.
Definition 4.2. Let Γ be a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group with h(Γ) = m. A real
polynomial crystallographic action of Γ is a homomorphism
ρ : Γ →P(Rm)
such that the induced action of Γ on Rm is free, properly discontinuous and cocompact.
This is the polynomial analogue of an affine crystallographic action, and was introduced and
studied by Dekimpe, Igodt, and Lee in [DIL96]. In this paper, we formulate a holomorphic ana-
logue. Let Ph(Cn) be the group of holomorphic polynomial automorphisms of Cn, namely holo-
morphic polynomial maps whose inverse is again polynomial.
Definition 4.3. Let Γ be a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group with h(Γ) = 2n. A
holomorphic polynomial crystallographic action of Γ is a homomorphism
ρ : Γ →Ph(Cn)
such that the induced action of Γ on Cn is free, properly discontinuous and cocompact.
Every holomorphic polynomial crystallographic action is, after identifying Cn with R2n, a real
polynomial crystallographic action.
4.2. A lattice consequence of the main theorem. We now formulate the consequence of the
main theorem in terms of the group Γ.
Definition 4.4. Let Γ be a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group with h(Γ) = 2n. We say
that Γ is of nilpotent-complex type if its Malcev completion GΓ admits a left-invariant nilpotent
complex structure.
This condition is intrinsic to Γ, since GΓ is determined by Γ.
Theorem 4.5. Let Γ be a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group with h(Γ) = 2n. If Γ is
of nilpotent-complex type, then Γ admits a holomorphic polynomial crystallographic action.
Proof. Choose a left-invariant nilpotent complex structure J on GΓ. By Theorem 3.1, there exists
a biholomorphism
Φ : (GΓ, J)
∼
−−→Cn
which is polynomial with respect to the exponential coordinates on GΓ.
The left translations on (GΓ, J) are holomorphic. Moreover, since GΓ is nilpotent, the Baker–
Campbell–Hausdorff formula shows that the left translations are polynomial in exponential coor-
dinates. Therefore, for every g ∈GΓ, we have
Φ ◦Lg ◦Φ−1 ∈Ph(Cn).
Restricting this action to the lattice Γ ⊂GΓ, we obtain a holomorphic polynomial crystallographic
action Γ →Ph(Cn).
□
Thus the main result of this paper gives a positive answer to the holomorphic polynomial
crystallographic problem for the class of torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent groups of nilpotent-
complex type.
It is then natural to ask whether there exists a torsion-free finitely generated
nilpotent group Γ with h(Γ) = 2n which does not admit a holomorphic polynomial crystallographic
action. At present, no such example seems to be known.
Question 4.6. Does every torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group Γ with h(Γ) = 2n admit
a holomorphic polynomial crystallographic action?
Suppose that Γ admits a holomorphic polynomial crystallographic action. Then it is natural
to expect that GΓ admits a left-invariant complex structure J, and even that (GΓ, J) ≃Cn. If,
however, one assumes more strongly that Γ admits a holomorphic polynomial crystallographic
action of bounded degree, then the remarkable uniqueness theorem due to Benoist and Dekimpe
imposes the expected restriction on Γ.
Here, a (holomorphic) polynomial crystallographic action of Γ is said to be of bounded degree
if the degrees of the polynomial transformations are bounded uniformly. Although the following

torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group is polycyclic-by-finite.
Theorem 4.7 ([BD02], Theorem 1). Let Γ be a polycyclic-by-finite group, and let
ρ1, ρ2 : Γ →P(Rm)
be two polynomial crystallographic actions of bounded degree. Then ρ1 and ρ2 are polynomially
conjugate. In other words, there exists Ψ ∈P(Rm) such that
Ψ ◦ρ1(γ) = ρ2(γ) ◦Ψ
for all γ ∈Γ.
Using this theorem, we obtain the following:
Theorem 4.8. Let Γ be a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group with h(Γ) = 2n. If Γ
admits a holomorphic polynomial crystallographic action of bounded degree, then GΓ admits a left-
invariant complex structure J such that (GΓ, J) ≃Cn.
Proof. Let ρ : Γ →Ph(Cn) be a holomorphic polynomial crystallographic action of bounded
degree. After identifying Cn with R2n, we regard ρ as a real polynomial crystallographic action
of Γ on R2n.
On the other hand, since Γ is a lattice in its Malcev completion GΓ, the left
action of Γ on GΓ is properly discontinuous and cocompact. Moreover, after choosing exponential
coordinates κ: GΓ →R2n, the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula shows that the left translations
are polynomial maps.
Since GΓ is nilpotent, their degrees are uniformly bounded.
Thus the
canonical left action
λ : Γ →P(R2n),
γ 7→κ ◦Lγ ◦κ−1
is also a polynomial crystallographic action of bounded degree. Applying Theorem 4.7 to the two
actions λ and ρ, we obtain a polynomial diffeomorphism Ψ ∈P(R2n) such that
Ψ ◦(κ ◦Lγ ◦κ−1) ◦Ψ−1 = ρ(γ)
for every γ ∈Γ.
Let J be the pull-back of the standard complex structure on R2n ≃Cn by
F := Ψ ◦κ. Then F is a biholomorphism
F : (GΓ, J)
∼
−−→Cn.
Moreover, Lγ is a biholomorphism of (GΓ, J) for every γ ∈Γ. It remains to see that J is left-
invariant under all of GΓ, not only under Γ.
In exponential coordinates on GΓ, the tensor
((Lg)∗J −J)x ∈Hom(TxGΓ, TxGΓ)
has coefficients which are polynomial functions of the point x ∈GΓ and of the translating element
g ∈GΓ. Hence the condition
(Lg)∗J = J
on GΓ
is equivalent to the vanishing of finitely many polynomial functions of g.
For every γ ∈Γ, the map Lγ is biholomorphic with respect to J, and hence these polynomial
equations are satisfied by all γ ∈Γ. Since Γ is Zariski dense in GΓ, the same equations hold for all
g ∈GΓ. Therefore every left translation Lg preserves J, and so J is left-invariant.
□
Therefore, if one wants to find a group Γ giving a negative answer to Question 4.6, then a
first promising candidate is a group whose Malcev completion admits no left-invariant complex
structure. Such examples already exist among 6-dimensional nilpotent Lie groups.
Theorem 4.8 would be particularly useful if the bounded-degree assumption could be verified
in the cases of interest. However, this seems to be a difficult problem. For instance, Dekimpe
proved in [Dek02] that polynomial crystallographic actions of polycyclic-by-finite groups on R2 are
of bounded degree, but such a general boundedness result is not known in higher dimensions. In
particular, the theorem does not currently apply to the 6-dimensional candidates mentioned above
unless one can independently prove the bounded-degree property.
