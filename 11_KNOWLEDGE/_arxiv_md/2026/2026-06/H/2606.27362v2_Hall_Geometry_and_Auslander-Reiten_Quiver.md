Our point in this note was that quiver moduli stacks contain more than just
counting. At Hall correspondence, it already knows about the structure of extensions
on Repk(Q). Once we recognize it, we can pass to AR theory. Thus, the passage
from quiver moduli to the motivic Hall algebra and Hall geometry, and then to AR
theory, can be thought of as a journey from enumerative geometry to homological
representation theory. We chose AR theory as a specific interest; we can generalize
this bridge to other homological interests.

Appendix A. AR Triangles and Happel’s Theorem for Triangulated
Categories
This appendix is presented for intellectual consumption about AR triangles in
Db(Mod-kQ).
There have been attempts to understand the derived Hall algebra [11, 15, 16].
But our motivation and purpose differ from those of derived Hall algebras.
The
passage from geometric information to our correspondence of stacks leads to the
structure of the abelian category Repk(Q) and thus the Auslander-Reiten theory as
discussed above. Similar to Auslander-Reiten (AR) sequences in Mod-kQ, we study
the Auslander-Reiten triangles in Db(Mod-kQ). AR sequences in Mod-kQ are almost
split sequences Ext1(A, τA)
0 →τA →E →A →0
(19)
where τ is the AR translate, A and τA are indecomposables, and A is a non-projective
module. For A2, we have an AR sequence
0 →S2 →P1 →S1 →0
(20)
In Db(Mod-kQ), an AR triangle is
τA →E →A →τA[1]

triangles is non-trivial. For this, we find a triangulated category which is equivalent
to Db(Mod-kQ) and study the AR triangles in that category. We know that Mod-kQ
is a hereditary algebra and hence has finite global dimension (in fact, one), but in
general, for any finite-dimensional algebra of finite global dimension, we have the
following theorem due to Happel [17], also see [18].
