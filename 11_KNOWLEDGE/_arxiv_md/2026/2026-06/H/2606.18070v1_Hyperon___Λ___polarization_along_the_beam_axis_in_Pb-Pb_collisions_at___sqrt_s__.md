Hyperon (Λ) polarization along the beam axis in Pb–Pb collisions at
√sNN = 5.36 TeV
ALICE Collaboration*
Abstract
The measurement of hyperon (Λ and Λ) polarization along the beam axis in Pb–Pb collisions at
√sNN = 5.36 TeV using the ALICE detector is presented. The polarization, arising from vortic-
ity components induced by anisotropic flow, is studied relative to the second- and third-order event
planes. The measured polarization exhibits clear azimuthal sine modulations, providing the first ob-
servation of polarization along the beam direction measured relative to the third-order event plane at
the Large Hadron Collider. The values of the polarization measured with respect to the second-order
event plane relative to the second harmonic event plane are consistent with previous measurements
at √sNN = 5.02 TeV and show improved statistical precision owing to the larger data sample. Com-
parisons with hydrodynamic calculations indicate that the measured polarization is sensitive to the
bulk viscosity and the vortical structure of the quark–gluon plasma, offering new constraints on its
transport properties. In particular, the polarization measured relative to the third-order event plane is
expected to provide an additional and independent input to constrain both bulk and shear viscosities
of the quark–gluon plasma.

Introduction
In relativistic heavy-ion collisions, a strongly-interacting medium known as the quark–gluon plasma
(QGP) [1–3] is formed. This medium exhibits collective behavior resembling that of a nearly perfect
fluid and is characterized by nontrivial velocity and vorticity fields [4, 5]. These vortical structures can
lead to the spin polarization of produced particles through spin-vorticity coupling [6, 7]. The observation
of global polarization of Λ hyperons at the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) [8, 9] and the Large
Hadron Collider (LHC) [10] provided compelling evidence for the creation and evolution of the vortical
fluid. The magnitude of the global polarization decreases with increasing collision energy, reflecting the
reduced effect of baryon stopping at midrapidity [11].
In addition to the global polarization arising from the system’s orbital angular momentum, local vorticity
fields may also emerge due to anisotropic flow [12–15]. The hydrodynamic-evolution model [13, 16],
transport model [17, 18], and blast-wave model [15, 19, 20] predict that the strong anisotropic flow
developed in non-central collisions generates vorticity components along the beam axis, giving rise to
a quadrupole and higher-order structure of the polarization in the transverse plane. The longitudinal
polarization refers to the component of the polarization along the beam direction, which is adopted
throughout this paper. The longitudinal polarization exhibits a modulation pattern similar to that of the
collective flow, as both originate from the same underlying anisotropy of the medium.
Previous measurements by the STAR [20, 21] and ALICE [22] Collaborations in heavy-ion collisions
revealed a non-zero longitudinal polarization with a positive sine-modulating component, opposite in
sign to the prediction from thermal-vorticity-based models [18]. Recent theoretical developments incor-
porating shear-induced effects qualitatively reproduce the observed behavior and make predictions for
centrality, which characterizes the degree of overlap between the two colliding nuclei, and transverse
momentum (pT) dependences of the polarization signal [16, 20] for different parametrization of bulk and
shear viscosities.
The measurement of longitudinal polarization measured relative to the high-order event planes is there-
fore of particular interest, as it can reveal how the underlying vorticity and transport properties of the
QGP couple to different flow harmonics. Because the polarization is sensitive to both the initial-state
geometry and the final-state collective dynamics [23], it provides valuable constraints on the space–time
evolution of the QGP. In particular, recent model calculations indicate that the longitudinal polarization
at LHC energies is more sensitive to the bulk viscosity of the QGP than at RHIC energies [16], thus
offering a new handle on transport properties of the medium.
In this manuscript, the measurement of the longitudinal polarization of Λ and Λ hyperons with respect
to the second- and third-order event planes in Pb–Pb collisions at √sNN = 5.36 TeV with the ALICE
detector at the LHC is presented. Section 2 describes the experimental setup. In Sec. 3, the analysis
procedure is detailed. The systematic uncertainties in the measurement are described in Sec. 4. Section 5
presents results of the polarization measurement with model comparison. Conclusions are drawn in
Sec. 6.
2
Experimental setup
The data sample used in this analysis was collected with the ALICE detector during the 2023 heavy-ion
data-taking period at the LHC. Details of the ALICE apparatus during the LHC Run 3 are described in
Ref. [24]. The following detectors are used in the analysis: the Fast Interaction Trigger (FIT) [25], which
was newly installed for Run 3, as well as the Inner Tracking System (ITS) [26] and the Time Projection
Chamber (TPC) [27], both upgraded for the LHC Run 3.
The FIT detector is composed of three subsystems, which are positioned on both sides of the interaction
point in the forward-rapidity region. The FT0 consists of two arrays (FT0A and FT0C) of fast Cherenkov

The FT0 detector is used to measure the minimum-bias interaction rate with the coincident signals of

primary-vertex position along the beam direction is required to be within 10 cm from the nominal in-

same bunch crossing.
The TPC, which surrounds the ITS, is a large cylindrical detector equipped with a Gas Electron Multi-
plier (GEM)-based readout system, covering the longitudinal and radial dimension of |z| < 250 cm and
85 < r < 247 cm, respectively. The TPC reconstructs charged-particle tracks together with the ITS and
identifies particle species based on the specific energy loss (dE/dx) of charged particles in the TPC gas.

Data analysis
The Λ (Λ) hyperons are reconstructed via the Λ →pπ−(Λ →¯pπ+) decay channel. The decay products
are identified using the nσTPC variable, defined as the difference between the measured ionization energy
loss in the TPC and the expected value from the Bethe–Bloch parameterization for pions and protons,
divided by the corresponding resolution. A selection of |nσTPC| < 5 is applied. In addition, decay
products are required to cross at least 50 pad rows in the TPC to ensure good momentum resolution,
and to satisfy pT > 0.2 (0.5) GeV/c and |η| < 0.8 for the pions (protons) to guarantee a uniform detector
acceptance. Selected hyperons are further required to satisfy the topological selection described in Tab. 1
to enhance the signal significance with respect to the combinatorial background.

Systematic uncertainties
The systematic uncertainties of longitudinal hyperon polarization are estimated by varying the analy-
sis selection criteria, event-plane determination, and fitting procedure, as well as feed-down effects for
hyperons. Each systematic variation is performed solely while other selections remain unchanged. A
Barlow test [33] is performed for each systematic variation, requiring that the standard deviation of the
Barlow factor distribution does not exceed 1.3. Most of the relative uncertainties are large only in central
collisions, where the polarization is close to zero.
The same analysis is conducted with a different primary-vertex selection of |zvtx| < 8 cm with respect
to the default selection of |zvtx| < 10 cm to evaluate the systematic uncertainty from the primary-vertex
selection. The systematic deviation from the variation of the selection is found to be negligible compared
to the statistical fluctuations, and therefore no uncertainty is assigned. The systematic uncertainty from
the event-plane detector is evaluated by replacing the FT0C with TPC tracks at forward and backward
rapidity. The systematic uncertainty from the event-plane detector is found to be negligible.
The systematic uncertainty on the hyperon selection is evaluated by varying the topological requirements
for kinematic variables of reconstructed hyperons. For each selection, the variations are defined by ap-
plying both stricter and looser criteria with respect to the default values listed in Tab. 1. The resulting
systematic deviation from these variations yields the systematic uncertainties of 1–8% and 2–10% for the
second- and third-order polarization, respectively, from peripheral to central collisions. The systematic
uncertainty on the selection for decay products from hyperons is calculated by varying requirements for
the minimum number of crossed TPC pad rows and the measured dE/dx deviation. Systematic uncertain-
ties are found to be 1–12% and 1–20% for the second- and third-order polarization, respectively, from
peripheral to central collisions.
Systematic uncertainties associated with the signal extraction are estimated by changing the background
modeling from second- to third-order polynomial function. The systematic deviation is found to be neg-
ligible from the variation of the background function. The systematic uncertainties associated with the
feed-down contribution to Λ polarization are conservatively estimated to be 10%, based on the stud-
ies [34, 35] for second- and third-order polarizations.

The model calculations are based on a hybrid framework combining initial conditions, (3+1)D viscous
hydrodynamic evolution, particlization, and hadronic transport [16]. Within this framework, the polariza-
tion is evaluated at the hadronization hypersurface assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium. The re-
sulting spin polarization arises from multiple contributions related to fluid gradients, including vorticity,
shear, acceleration, and expansion effects. Therefore, the observed polarization reflects the interplay and
partial cancellation of these different contributions, rather than a pure manifestation of vorticity alone. In
this context, the relative importance of these contributions is strongly influenced by the transport proper-
ties of the medium. The experimental polarization can be explained with the QGP bulk viscosity (ζ/s)
implementation. With the absence of the QGP bulk viscosity, the calculated longitudinal polarization is
opposite to the experimental data. Different bulk-viscosity options are implemented via phenomenologi-
cal temperature-dependent parametrizations that feature a peak around the critical temperature, reflecting
the expected enhancement of bulk viscosity near the QCD phase transition [36–38].
The bulk viscosity plays a particularly important role in the longitudinal polarization at LHC energies.
While its effect is relatively modest at lower collision energies such as those at RHIC, it becomes signif-
icant at LHC energies, where the inclusion of bulk viscosity leads to a sign change of the longitudinal
polarization. This behavior arises because bulk viscosity modifies the space–time evolution of the sys-
tem and, consequently, the velocity gradients entering the polarization mechanism. Although it does not
directly generate polarization, it changes the relative balance among different contributions, leading to a
reorganization of their interplay. As a result, the inclusion of bulk viscosity can qualitatively alter the po-
larization signal at LHC energies, bringing the calculations into agreement with the observed trend. This
sensitivity indicates that longitudinal polarization provides constraints not only on the vortical structure

Conclusions
High-precision measurements of longitudinal hyperon (Λ and Λ) polarization in Pb–Pb collisions at
√sNN = 5.36 TeV are presented. The longitudinal hyperon polarization relative to the second-order
event plane is observed to increase toward peripheral collisions and to show a mild dependence on trans-
verse momentum, in qualitative agreement with earlier measurements at √sNN = 5.02 TeV. Benefiting
from the large Run 3 data sample, the present results feature substantially reduced statistical uncertain-
ties, allowing for more detailed and stringent comparisons with theoretical models. Comparisons with
recent viscous hydrodynamic calculations indicate that the measured polarization is sensitive to the trans-
port properties of the QGP. In particular, the inclusion of the bulk viscosity is found to be important to
reproduce the sign of the observed longitudinal hyperon polarization relative to the second-order event
plane, underscoring the role of dissipative effects in the development of the local spin polarization in the
medium.
In addition, the measurement of the longitudinal hyperon polarization relative to the third-order event
plane is performed for the first time at the LHC. These observables provide an independent and com-
plementary handle on the vortical structure of the system, which is largely influenced by event-by-event
initial-state fluctuations and higher-order anisotropic flow. The third-order component therefore extends
the sensitivity of longitudinal polarization measurements beyond the dominant elliptic geometry.
Recent theoretical studies motivate the combined investigation of multiple harmonic components of lon-
gitudinal polarization, as such observables can receive contributions from different mechanisms, includ-
ing thermal vorticity and shear-induced effects, depending on the viscous properties of the medium and
the initial-state geometry. In this context, higher-order polarization measurements offer additional infor-
mation that is complementary to that obtained from traditional flow observables. Therefore, the present
measurement provides an important opportunity to constrain the vortical response and transport proper-
ties of the QGP through the study of higher-order polarization structures.
