Abstract. For every countable compact space K, we determine the exact
Borel complexity of the isometry class of the Banach space C(K). As a byprod-
uct, we also determine the precise Borel complexity of the homeomorphism
class of a fixed countable compact space K, improving earlier results of Cen-
zer and Mauldin. The above results provide concrete and natural examples of
sets with arbitrarily high, still exactly determined, Borel complexity.
Moreover, we find a new characterization of those real L1-preduals that
are isometric to C(K) for some zero-dimensional compact space K and we
determine the precise Borel complexity of C(2N).

Introduction
The classification of various classes of separable Banach spaces is a natural prob-
lem in functional analysis. A systematic framework for studying and comparing
such classifications is provided by descriptive set theory, which enables one to assess
their relative complexity via the notion of Borel complexity. Within this framework,
one can meaningfully distinguish between “simpler” and “more complex” classifica-
tion problems. The main aim of this work is, given an infinite countable compact
space K, to determine the exact complexity of the isometry class of the real Banach
space C(K).

Before presenting our results, we note that apart from the Banach space theory,
this work is closely related to the research program in invariant descriptive set
theory (IDST), which provides a unified framework for comparing classification
problems across mathematics and revealing connections between them. We refer
e.g. to the monograph [17], where the appropriate context may be found. Both
classical and invariant DST in Banach space theory have been intensively studied in
recent years, leading to a range of interesting results and applications. These include
(a) the study of universality problems, initiated by the work of Bourgain [4] and
subsequently developed through numerous contributions, including, for instance,
the recent work [1]; (b) the analysis of the complexity of various classification
problems via Borel reducibility, an approach rooted in Bossard’s coding of separable
Banach spaces [3] and further advanced, for example, in the recent work [11]; and
(c) the analysis of the complexity of various classes of Banach spaces, for which we
refer, e.g., to the survey [19] as well as to more recent contributions such as [21, 28].
A standard approach – introduced by Bossard in [2, 3] – is to code separa-
ble Banach spaces by elements of the standard Borel space SB(X) of closed lin-
ear subspaces of X, where X is a universal separable Banach space (for instance
X = C(2N)). In this work, we use the codings P∞and B introduced in [13], which
appears more natural in our context; see Remark II.2 for a detailed discussion of
this choice. Let us recall the coding B, for its variant P∞we refer to the introduc-
tion to Part II, where more details may be found.
Definition. Let V be the countable Q-linear vector space of elements of c00 having
rational entries, and define B to be the set of all functions µ ∈[0, ∞)V which can be
extended (uniquely) to a norm on c00. For µ ∈B we denote by Xµ the completion
of (V, µ).
It is easy to check that then for any infinite-dimensional separable Banach space
X there exists µ ∈B such that X is linearly isometric to Xµ and that B ⊆[0, ∞)V
is a Gδ set and therefore B is Polish space when endowed with the product topology
inherited from [0, ∞)V . We refer to the introduction to Part II for more details
including further references.
Given an infinite-dimensional separable Banach space X we denote
⟨X⟩≡:= {µ ∈B; X is linearly isometric to Xµ}
and we say that the set ⟨X⟩≡is the isometry class of X. In [14] the authors proved
that ℓ2 is the only space with closed isometry class, that ⟨Lp([0, 1])⟩≡is Gδ-complete
(hence, Gδ and not Fσ) whenever p ∈[1, ∞)\{2}, while the classes of ℓp and c0 are
Fσδ-complete (hence, Fσδ and not Gδσ). Other closely related papers dealing with
similar problems using the coding B are e.g. [12] (characterizing Banach spaces with
Gδ isometry class), [33] (devoted to the classification of the Daugavet and several
related diameter two properties), or [35] (devoted to the study of complexities in
the context of Lipschitz-free spaces).

By the Mazurkiewicz-Sierpiński theorem, for every infinite countable compact

the isometry classes of C(K) spaces with K infinite countable compacta or K being

As a byproduct of our method of proof, we obtain the following additional results.
Answering [14, Question 6] in the negative, we show that the class of Banach spaces
with a summable Szlenk index is Σ0
ω-hard (see Corollary II.51). Furthermore, we
establish a precise correspondence between topological and isometric complexity:
given any uncountable compact metric space X, the class of compact subsets of X
that are homeomorphic to [0, ωβ+n], respectively to [0, ωβ+n · k] with k ≥2, has
exactly the same Borel complexity as the isometry class of C(ωβ+n), respectively
of C(ωβ+n · k) (see Theorem II.6). In contrast, for the Cantor set 2N, the isometry
class of C(2N) is different from the homeomorphism class of 2N. Indeed, it follows
from known results that the latter is always a Gδ set, regardless of the ambient
space X; see Remark II.23.
The highlight of our result is that it provides concrete and natural examples of
classes whose complexities are arbitrarily high, still exactly determined, in the Borel
hierarchy. Such examples are rather scarce in analysis and topology. For instance,
in the classical monograph by Kechris [26, p. 189] the author asks for natural sets
which are, at least, from the 5th Borel class but not in any lower Borel class.
For the homeomorphism classes of compact sets, examples have been known.
Indeed, Cenzer and Mauldin [9, 10] (see also [26, p. 182]) proved that given β = 0
or β being a countable limit ordinal and n ∈N ∪{0}, the set of compact spaces
with Cantor-Bendixson derivative less than β + n is Σ0
β+2n-complete. Moreover, it
follows essentially from their proofs (see Remark II.26 for further details) that the
set of those compact subsets of 2N which are homeomorphic to [0, ωβ+n] is Π0

on the Borel complexity by 1 (by proving that the set is, in fact, Π0
β+2n+1-hard);

an upper bound (that is, we show that the set is, in fact, Π0
β+2n+1-complete); c)
we provide an analogous result also for [0, ωβ+n · k] with k ≥2.

Part I. Characterization of spaces isometric to C(K) for K countable
The main results of this part are Theorem B together with a new isometric
characterization of C(K) spaces, where K is either the Cantor set or a countable
infinite compact set. Our motivation comes from the fact that, when studying upper
bounds on the Borel complexity of the isometry class of a Banach space, one must
first find a suitable isometric characterization of that space. In many cases, this
requires introducing a new characterization designed specifically for this purpose.
In [14, Theorem 4.13] the authors obtained isometric characterization of the Banach
space c0, which suggests pursuing analogous ideas in our setting. A careful analysis
of the proof of [14, Theorem 4.13] reveals a gap in the part of the argument where
a condition ensuring that X∗is isometric to ℓ1 is established. We address this issue
by providing an argument that is useful in our setting, and by supplying details that
fill the gap in the proof of [14, Theorem 4.13]. This is the content of the very short
Section I.1. The next step is to exploit Szlenk derivatives similarly as it was done in
[14, Theorem 4.13]. Accordingly, in Section I.2 we compute the Szlenk derivatives
for the unit ball in C(K) spaces with K being a scattered compact space (not even
necessarily metrizable) and, as a consequence, we obtain isometric characterization
of C(ωα · k) spaces among separable C(K) spaces, see Theorem I.6. However, when
trying to characterize the space c, we observed that the Szlenk derivative alone is
insufficient to distinguish it from other L1-preduals such as e.g. the space c ⊕∞c0.
After a non-negligible effort we obtained Theorem I.12, which implies Theorem B
and provides a characterization of C(K) spaces with K zero-dimensional within the
class of L1-preduals. Notably, our method applies even in the nonseparable setting.
This constitutes the central and technically most demanding part of Part I, and is
developed in Section I.3. As a byproduct of our proofs we also obtain an isometric
characterization of C(2N). The isometric characterization of C(ωα ·k) spaces is then
obtained as a combination of Theorem I.6 with Theorem I.12, see Corollary I.13.

we use below. Given a compact space K, we identify the dual of the Banach space
C(K) with the space M(K) of all regular signed Borel measures on K endowed
with the variation norm. Unless otherwise stated, the ball BM(K) is endowed with
the weak∗topology given by this duality. For µ ∈M(K), its variation is denoted
