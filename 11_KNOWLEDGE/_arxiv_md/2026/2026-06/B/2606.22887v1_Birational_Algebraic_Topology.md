Introduction to Birational Algebraic Topology
1.1
The aims of birational motivic homotopy theory
Birational geometry studies birational morphisms of schemes. Denoted X 99K Y , a
birational morphism from X to Y stands for a span X
i←−- U
j,−→Y of dense open
immersions (i.e., both i and j have homeomorphic images that are dense in their codomain
and induce isomorphisms i∗OX ≃OU ≃j∗OY of structure sheaves). One of the classical
questions in birational geometry is the rationality problem: How to determine whether a
given variety is birational to An? However clean the statement may seem, the rationality
problem has no universal solution.
The issue is that birational morphisms are not morphisms in any sufficiently well-behaved
1-category that suitably enlarges the category of (smooth) schemes.
Moreover, the
rationality question is a rigid one, in the sense that it is really about isomorphism classes.
Problems of this type often require one to consider the ‘possible’ (2, 1)-category of such
spans, which extends the 1-category of (smooth) schemes in a suitable manner.

One of the best techniques for obtaining negative answers to such problems is to construct
invariants that induce isomorphisms on such spans.
However, an appropriate, well-
behaved invariant must satisfy the properties of a good cohomology theory, for which
even 2-categories are insufficient. One shall rather build a category that has all higher
categorical colimits. The ‘minimal’ such category, which contains all smooth schemes and
is a natural home for such spans, is precisely the point of the birational homotopy category.
This is the localization of PΣ(SmS) (see [Lur09, Definition 5.5.8.8] for the notation) at
the set of dense open immersions, denoted Hb(S) := LdensePΣ(SmS).
Frankly, if one really wants cohomology theories of sufficiently good nature, a priori, it is
not clear why our birational category should enforce them. Therefore, it is suggestive to
start with a good cohomological extension rather than PΣ(SmS). One of the best and most
popular such unstable candidates is, no doubt, the A1 homotopy category, constructed
by Morel and Voevodsky [MV99] (recall that this is defined as the A1-localization of the
Nisnevich ∞-topos of SmS). So the correct candidate for birational homotopy shall rather
be HbA1(S) := LdenseHA1(S).
In fact, historically, this was the first construction of the ‘birational motivic homotopy
category’ and appeared in [Pel14] for a completely different reason: to study a possible
slice filtration of the unstable motivic homotopy category. This unstable theory has a
significant impact on motivic slice computations [see BE21]. (We will carry out a similar
computation in another work [Mai26d]).
With a different goal in mind, Choudhury and Roy [CR24] considered both constructions
over a field k and showed in Theorem 3.4 of loc.cit. that they coincide. That is, homotopy
invariance and Nisnevich locality are automatic in Hb(k). This result also appeared in
a work of Cisinski and Kahn [CK23, Corollary 2.3]. However, both contain a technical
oversight: they rely on the unrestricted ∞-category of presheaves P(Smk) rather than
PΣ(Smk), so that their Hb(k) (which evaluates to LdenseP(Smk) in our setting) contains
objects that fail to be local with respect to disjoint covers. The first section of this
paper addresses this gap by providing a rigorously corrected version of this strategy while
generalizing it to all base schemes. Thus, we show that for any qcqs scheme S, there is
an equality of ∞-categories HbA1(S) = Hb(S). (The strategy we develop for automatic
Nisnevich locality differs from that of [CR24]. In another work [Mai26c, Theorem 2.3.6],
I provide a corrected version of their Corollary 3.7 using the necessary framework of
PΣ(SmS), carried out uniformly over all qcqs base schemes.)
Now, obviously, the A1 homotopy category is not the most general cohomologically rich
construction, simply because not all cohomologies are A1-invariant. There are a few
more on the list that do a little better. Among many such candidates, another unstable
cohomological category is the logarithmic motivic homotopy category of [BPOsr22]. In
§2.3 of this paper, we will see that even with the logarithmic setup, one does not get
anything new: the ‘strict logarithmic birational motivic’ homotopy category is equivalent
to the ordinary ‘birational motivic homotopy category’.
We may therefore unambiguously denote the birational motivic homotopy category by
Hb(S). It will therefore follow that computations in HbA1(S) are significantly simpler than
their A1-homotopical counterparts.

Constructing the Birational Motivic Homotopy Category
Let S be a Qcqs scheme. By SmS we mean the full subcategory of SchS spanned by
schemes over S with finitely presented and smooth structure maps. By an S-space we mean
a presheaf of spaces on SmS. The ∞-category of S-spaces, denoted P(S), is defined as
the ∞-category of presheaves of spaces on SmS, i.e., P(S) := P(SmS) = Fun(Smop
S , Spc).
Since SmS is (essentially) small, by [Lur09, Remark 5.5.3.7] the ∞-category P(S) is
presentable and, in fact, an ∞-topos [see Lur09, Definition 6.1.0.4]. When the base
scheme S is fixed, we refer to S-spaces simply as ’spaces’ and implicitly identify any
smooth scheme X with its representable presheaf hS(X).
We say that an open immersion [see Sta26, Definition 01HE] j : U ,→X is a dense open
immersion if the underlying topological space j(U) is dense in X. We say that a presheaf
X ∈P(S) is dense local if it is local with respect to the class
B0(S) := {j : U ,→X ∈SmS | j is a dense open immersion}
of dense open immersions in SmS. We denote the full subcategory of P(S) consisting of
dense local spaces by LdenseP(S). Moreover, since B0(S) is (essentially) small, it follows
from [Lur09, Proposition 5.5.4.15] that LdenseP(S) is an accessible localization of P(SmS).
Let us denote the associated saturated class and the corresponding localization functor by
Dense(S) and Ldense, respectively.
It is well known that the Yoneda embedding h : SmS →P(SmS) does not, in general,
preserve coproducts.
Consequently, the ∞-category P(S) :=P(SmS) contains many
(geometrically) unidentified homotopy types. As a result, the dense local homotopy
category LdenseP(S) is too naive to support meaningful birational geometry. To avoid
introducing new homotopy types for existing coproducts, one should replace P(SmS) with
the category PΣ(SmS) [Lur09, Definition 5.5.8.8]. This is the full subcategory of P(SmS)
consisting of presheaves that send finite coproducts in SmS to products in Spc.
Remark 2.0.1. Since SmS is an extensive (∞-)category (i.e., has disjoint coproducts),
the category PΣ(S) := PΣ(SmS) is equivalent to the ∞-category P⊔(SmS) of sheaves of
spaces on SmS with the disjoint cover topology (i.e., {Uα →X} is a cover iff ⊔Uα →X
is an isomorphism) [BH21, Lemma 2.4]. Thus, the localization functor LΣ : P(SmS) →
PΣ(SmS) is left exact and accessible, making PΣ(SmS) an ∞-topos. Clearly, the Nisnevich
topology is finer than the ⊔-topology and hence Pnis(S) ⊂PΣ(S).
With this understanding, we are now ready to define the correct version of the birational
homotopy category:
Definition 2.0.2. A presheaf X ∈P(S) is said to be birational local if it belongs to
PΣ(S) and is dense local. The full subcategory of P(S) consisting of birational local
spaces is called the Birational Homotopy category, denoted Hb(S). Since B0(S) is
an essentially small set, we note that Hb(S) ⊂P(S) is an accessible localization. The
associated saturated class is denoted bir(S), and a morphism in it is called a Birational
equivalence.
