1. Introduction
Cosiotropic A-branes were introduced by Kapustin–Orlov [14] to enlarge the Fukaya category so as to ac-
commodate Kontsevich’s homological mirror symmetry conjecture [15]. Among them, space-filling coisotropic
A-branes are distinguished by the holomorphic symplectic structure ΩX on the ambient manifold X induced
by their curvature, and play a central role of the brane quantization proposal of Gukov–Witten [13]. In
their framework, one considers a distinguished space-filling A-brane Bcc 1 on a complexification (X, ΩX)
of a K¨ahler manifold (M, ωM). They propose that the endomorphism algebra HomA(Bcc, Bcc) provides a
holomorphic deformation quantization of (X, ΩX), while for a Lagrangian A-brane B supported on M, the
morphism space HomA(B, Bcc) gives the geometric quantization of (M, ωM). These two structures are linked
by a folklore action “DQ ↷GQ”, implemented by the composition map
HomA(Bcc, Bcc) ⊗HomA(B, Bcc) →HomA(B, Bcc).

3. A space-filling A-brane on X and its endomorphism algebra
The goal of this section is to construct a space-filling coisotropic A-brane of (X, ω) starting from a semi-
affine one, and to present a mathematical model for its endomorphism algebra.
Subsection 3.1 introduces a semi-affine coisotropic A-brane Bcc,0 of (X0, ω|X0), which already serves as a
prototypical example within this class of objects. A key observation is that the monodromy of the complex
structure I0 on X0, induced by Bcc,0, agrees with that of the semi-flat mirror of X. This suggests that I0 can
be corrected to a global complex structure on X. Subsection 3.2 promotes this correction to the level of the
A-brane, thereby producing a space-filling coisotropic A-brane Bcc of (X, ω).
Associated with Bcc,0 is a non-formal deformation quantization constructed in [8], which may be viewed
as a mathematical realization of the endomorphism algebra of Bcc,0. In Subsection 3.3, we incorporate the
corresponding corrections into this deformation quantization, and propose that the resulting non-commutative
algebra should be viewed as the endomorphism algebra of Bcc, in accordance with our mirror theorem. In
Subsection 3.4, we discuss how this noncommutative algebra may be understood as a specialization of a
deformation quantization.
