BIHOLOMORPHISM TYPE OF LEFT-INVARIANT COMPLEX
STRUCTURES ON NILPOTENT LIE GROUPS
KEIZO HASEGAWA, SÖNKE ROLLENSKE, LORENZO SILLARI, ADRIANO TOMASSINI
AND KONSTANTIN WEHLER
Abstract. In this note we prove a conjecture by Hasegawa stating that a simply
connected, nilpotent Lie group of dimension 2n endowed with a left-invariant
complex structure is biholomorphic to Cn.
1. Introduction
The uniformisation theorem is a cornerstone of the theory of complex manifolds.
It characterises the topological, algebraic-geometric and differential-geometric prop-
erties of a Riemann surface in terms of its universal cover being isomorphic to P1, C
or H. The problem of characterising manifolds with a fixed type of universal cover
or determining properties of the universal cover of a certain type of manifold has
continued to see interest ever since, and gained traction especially in Kähler and
algebraic geometry over the last decades, see for instance [Kol95, CDS14, GKT18].
In the case of non-Kähler manifolds the picture is, as usual, less systematic and
focuses on the homogeneous case. Here, the most prominent conjecture has been
proposed by Hasegawa [Has09, Has10].
Conjecture 1.1 — Let G be a simply connected, nilpotent Lie group of dimension
2n and let J be a left-invariant complex structure on G. Then the complex manifold
(G, J) is biholomorphic to Cn.
We prove the conjecture by explicitly constructing a biholomorphism, which turns
out to be polynomial in exponential coordinates.
Theorem 1.2 — Let G be a simply connected, nilpotent Lie group of dimension 2n
and let J be a left-invariant complex structure on G. Then the complex manifold
(G, J) is biholomorphic to Cn by a polynomial map.
In particular, the universal
cover of a complex nilmanifold of dimension n is biholomorphic to Cn.
This theorem was already known in several special cases: Nakamura addressed
the case of complex nilpotent Lie groups [Nak75], while the surface case was impli-
citly contained in Kodaira’s work [Kod66], and explicitly in the work of Oeljeklaus–
Richthofer [OR84].

Kodaira–Thurston manifold [Thu76].
In this context, so-called nilpotent complex structures have been introduced in
[CFGU97], and recently Kanda [Kan26] gave an explicit constructive proof of The-
orem 1.2 in this special case. In terms of the universal cover, nilpotent complex
structures can be characterised by (G, J) being an iterated holomorphic principal
bundle for the additive group C, so that this case can also be abstractly deduced
from the Oka–Grauert principle [Gra58].
In Example 4.1 we explicitly compute a polynomial biholomorphism from a 6-
dimensional Lie group with a non-nilpotent complex structure to C3, exhibiting the
generality of our result.
The key ingredient for the general case, beyond the classical work of Snow [Sno85,
Sno86], is a theorem by Bialynicki-Birula and Rosenlicht [BBR62] stating that an
injective polynomial self-map of Rn is already surjective. Surjectivity of injective
algebraic self-maps was first established by Newman for polynomials in R2 [New60],
and by Bialynicki-Birula and Rosenlicht for polynomial self-maps of Rn and for
regular self-maps of complex algebraic varieties [BBR62]. The complex case was also
obtained independently by Ax, Grothendieck and Borel [Ax69, Gro66, Bor69].
As a direct consequence of the biholomorphism constructed in the proof of The-
orem 1.2 being polynomial, the applications obtained by Kanda [Kan26] under the
nilpotency assumption on J extend to arbitrary left-invariant complex structures. A
further application to the moduli of complex nilmanifolds is developed in the thesis
of Wehler [Weh26].
One should note that the analogue of Theorem 1.2 fails if the Lie group G is no
longer nilpotent, but merely solvable. This can already be observed for the class
of two-dimensional complex solvmanifolds, known as Inoue surfaces (cf. [Has05]),
whose universal cover is biholomorphic to C × H. Nonetheless, Hasegawa [Has10]
conjectured that, for G a simply connected, unimodular, solvable Lie group with a
left-invariant complex structure J, the complex manifold (G, J) is always Stein.

2. Preliminaries and notation
Let G be a simply connected, nilpotent Lie group of dimension 2n with associated
Lie algebra g. A left-invariant almost complex structure on G is an endomorphism
J : g →g with J2 = −id, extended to all of TG by left translations. The almost
complex structure J yields a decomposition of the complexified Lie algebra
gC = g1,0 ⊕g0,1
into the ±i-eigenspaces of its complex-linear extension, and J is integrable if and
only if g1,0 is a complex Lie subalgebra of gC, that is, if [g1,0, g1,0] ⊆g1,0. From
now on we will always assume that J is integrable, in which case J defines a left-
invariant complex structure on G, and the pair (G, J) is a complex manifold of
complex dimension n.
In [Sno86], Snow constructed a map Φ: (G, J) →Cn, which is used crucially in
the proof of Theorem 1.2. We will briefly recall his construction. Since G is simply

dimension 2n, the map dΦe is an isomorphism, and Φ is a local biholomorphism.
3. Proof of Theorem 1.2
We will show that the map Φ: (G, J) 7→Cn is bijective and polynomial in expo-

if G is nilpotent. Indeed, two elements in (G, J) have the same image under Φ if
and only if they differ by an element of G ∩G0,1, but since the exponential map is
bijective we have G ∩G0,1 = exp(g ∩g0,1) = exp(0).

4. Concluding Remarks
If the complex structure J is nilpotent in the sense of Cordero–Fernández–Gray–
Ugarte [CFGU97], then there exists a basis Z1, . . . , Zn of g1,0 compatible with the
ascending central series of g. In this case, it follows directly from the argument in the
previous section that the degree of the polynomials given by the map Φ is bounded
by the nilpotency index of the Lie algebra g. This is explicitly demonstrated by the
examples given in [Kan26, Section 5]. However, as the following example shows, this
bound no longer holds if the complex structure is non-nilpotent.

polynomial for every left-invariant complex structure, including those that are non-
nilpotent. In real dimension 6 there are, up to isomorphism, exactly two nilpotent
Lie algebras carrying non-nilpotent complex structures [COUV16]; we treat one of
