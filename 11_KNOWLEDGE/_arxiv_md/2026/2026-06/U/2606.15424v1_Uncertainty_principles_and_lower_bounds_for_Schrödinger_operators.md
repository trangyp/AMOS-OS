Uncertainty principles and lower bounds
for Schr¨odinger operators
A. B¨ottcher1, P. Stollmann2, and M. Tautenhahn3
Abstract
We prove that two different abstract quantitative uncertainty principles
are equivalent to the strict positivity of the associated abstract Schr¨odin-
ger operators. We also discuss the case of continuum Schr¨odinger oper-
ators, in which case our method provides an explicitly computable lower
bound as well as a control theoretic application.
1
Introduction
This article is concerned with uncertainty principles at low energy for Schr¨o-
dinger operators. A typical manifestation of such an uncertainty principle might
be
PEV PE ≥κPE,
(1)
where, in a Hilbert space H, PE = 1[0,E](H) is the spectral projector associated
to some selfadjoint operator H ≥0 onto energies below E ≥min σ(H), V is a
non-negative selfadjoint operator, and κ = κ(E) is some positive constant. The
most prominent example is the case where H = −∆is the negative Laplacian
in L2(Rd) and V = 1D with a so-called thick set D ⊂Rd. In that case, the
Logvinenko-Sereda theorem implies (1) for all E > 0 with an explicit constant
κ depending on E and the geometric properties of the set D; see [LS74, Kov00,
Kov01]. It is called an uncertainty principle since it says that
∥f1D∥2 ≥∥f∥2 for all f ∈ran(PE),
so that states of low energy are spread out in configuration space in the sense
that they carry a reasonable amount of mass on the set D. The proof of the
Logvinenko-Sereda theorem strongly relies on harmonic analysis. The presence
of a potential and of more general domains Ω⊂Rd, i.e., considering H =
−∆+ W in L2(Ω), places the problem beyond the reach of purely harmonic
analysis methods. This setting has been investigated, e.g., in [Kle13, RMV13,
NTTV18, LM19, DSV24] under different assumptions on the set D and the
potential W.

From the viewpoint of applications, uncertainty relations of the type (1) have
become an essential tool in spectral theory of random Schr¨odinger operators,
control theory, or harmonic analysis, to mention a few. In the theory of ran-
dom Schr¨odinger operators such uncertainty relations are typically used to prove
Wegner estimates and in turn Anderson localization; see, for example, [BLS11,
Kle13, RMV13, NTTV18, SS21]. In control theory, uncertainty relations serve
as an input to the Lebeau-Robbiano strategy [LR95], originally formulated for
the Laplace-Beltrami operator on compact Riemannian manifolds M. The gen-
eral philosophy here is that an uncertainty relation implies an observability esti-
mate, and by Douglas’ lemma [Dou66], this is equivalent to null-controllability
of a certain linear control problem. Subsequently, this strategy has been studied
in various mathematical situations, including abstract Hilbert or Banach spaces;
see, e.g., [LZ98, JL99, Mil10, TT11, WZ17, BPS18, NTTV20, GST20]. While
the original Lebeau-Robbiano strategy requires (1) to hold for all E > 0 with
a constant κ = κ(E) not vanishing too fast as E tends to infinity, there exist
also weak versions where the validity of (1) for some (eventually small) fixed E
is sufficient; cf. the recent papers [EGST24, MSST26].
Let us now turn to our contribution. Our key result is Theorem 3.2, which
gives a computable lower bound for abstract Schr¨odinger operators. This theo-
rem plays a crucial role in our proof of the equivalence of the uncertainty princi-
ple known from [CHK03, CHK00, BLS11] and the strict positivity of Schr¨odinger
operators. Moreover, this theorem allows us to pass from potentials of the form
V = 1D with relatively dense sets D to non-negative potentials V subject to
what we call (R, ϱ)-thickness.
It is evident that the technique employed here could equally well be applied
in the discrete case of graph Laplacians; we refer to [LSS19] for earlier results on
graphs and to the upcoming [LS], which features a metric measure space set-up
that includes graphs and continuum configuration spaces.
In contrast to the equivalent quantitative uncertainty principles mentioned
above, a qualitative uncertainty principle for H and V can be thought of as a
unique-continuation statement: for suitable E, it requires that if f ∈ran(PE)
and V f = 0, then necessarily f = 0. It is a rather simple observation that such a
unique-continuation is a consequence of the quantitative uncertainty principles
mentioned earlier. For H = −∆, the fact that functions in the range of the
spectral projector are analytic shows that in this case the qualitative uncertainty
principle holds for arbitrary E and every V ≥0 that is not 0 almost everywhere,
whereas a quantitative uncertainty principle requires V to be (R, ϱ)-thick for
some R, ϱ > 0.
In the final section we sketch an application of our method to control theory:
it provides α-controllability with arbitrary α > 0 for a new and fairly general
class of control problems.

Main results
We start in a general setting: (H, (·|·)) denotes a Hilbert space, H, V are self-
adjoint operators in H, V is supposed to be bounded, and we require that
H, V ≥0. As usual, if A is selfadjoint and ε ∈R, then A ≥ε means that
(Af|f) ≥ε(f|f) for all f ∈dom(A), which in turn is equivalent to the require-
ment that the spectrum σ(A) of A is contained in [ε, ∞). We call A strictly
positive and write A ≫0 if A ≥ε for some ε > 0. Most of what follows is only
interesting in the case where 0 ∈σ(H), that is, where H is not strictly positive.
For E > 0, we denote by PE := 1[0,E](H) the spectral projector of H associ-
ated with [0, E]. Following [BLS11], we say that a quantitative uncertainty
principle for spectral projectors holds for the pair H, V at E if there is a
κ = κ(H, V, E) > 0 such that
PEV PE ≥κPE,
(σUP)
where, of course, PEV PE ≥κPE means that PEV PE −κPE ≥0.
We say that a quantitative uncertainty principle at low energy holds
for the pair H, V at E if there exists a µ = µ(H, V, E) > 0 such that
f ∈dom(H) ∧(Hf|f) ≤E∥f∥2
=⇒(V f|f) ≥µ∥f∥2.
(EUP)
Clearly, (EUP) implies (σUP) with κ = µ: since (HPEf|PEf) ≤E∥PEf∥2, we
infer from (EUP) that (PEV PEf|PEf) = (V PEf|PEf) ≥µ∥PEf∥2.

(i) H + V ≫0;

(iii) H and V obey (EUP) at some E = E2 > 0 with suitable µ.

Based on earlier works [TWX20, HWW21, LWXY22], it has recently been
observed in [EGST24] that an uncertainty relation at low energy of the form
(σUP) is sufficient to guarantee α-controllability for all α > 0 and sufficiently
large T > 0. This is in contrast to earlier results, where even for α-controllability
an uncertainty relation at all energies was required. Indeed, as a consequence of
our Corollary 5.1 in combination with [EGST24, Proposition 3.1] we obtain a so-
called weak observability estimate. Note that the first assumption of [EGST24,
Proposition 3.1] is satisfied by Corollary 5.1, while its second assumption follows
easily from the spectral theorem. This weak observability estimate is in turn
equivalent to α-controllability, see [TWX20, Theorem 1] for the case of Hilbert
spaces (i.e. r = 2), and [EGST24, Theorem 2.8] for the general framework of
Banach spaces. Thus, we obtain the following corollary.
Corollary 6.1. Assume that V is (R, ϱ)-thick for some R, ϱ > 0 and let α ∈
(0, 1). Then there exist T0, K > 0 such that for all T ≥T0 and all r ∈[1, ∞],
the linear control problem (5) is cost-uniformly α-controllable in time T with
respect to Lr((0, T); H) and cost K.
Note that in the present general context, we do not obtain α = 0, a property
called null-controllability. This would require an uncertainty relation at all en-
ergies. We want to stress the fact that we can treat a general class of operators
H, merely satisfying H ≥η(−∆) for some η > 0; see Section 5.2 for examples.
As our examples cover operators with irregular coefficients, it seems (currently)
beyond reach to show an uncertainty relation at all energies by standard meth-
ods to the best of our knowledge. Note, moreover, that in our previous result,
the control operator V is merely supposed to be a (R, ϱ)-thick potential, gen-
eralizing the case of indicator functions of thick sets treated previously in the
literature.
