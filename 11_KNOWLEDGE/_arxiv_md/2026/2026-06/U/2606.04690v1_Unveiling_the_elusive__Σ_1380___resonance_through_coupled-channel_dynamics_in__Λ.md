We investigate the Λ+
c →ηπ+Λ decay measured by the Belle and BESIII Collaborations, focusing
on the possible role of the Σ(1380) state with spin-parity JP = 1/2−. In our theoretical framework,
the Λ(1670) and a0(980) are dynamically generated from meson-baryon and meson-meson final-
state interactions, respectively, and the corresponding line shapes of these two states used here are
applicable to all relevant hadronic reactions. Furthermore, the contributions from the intermediate
Σ(1385) resonance and the possible Σ(1380) state are included explicitly. By comparing the invariant
mass and angular distributions obtained with and without the Σ(1380) state, we demonstrate that
this state plays an important role in improving the description of the experimental data. We also
identify the kinematic regions most sensitive to the possible Σ(1380) contribution. Future high-
precision measurements of this process will be instrumental for testing the existence of the Σ state
with JP = 1/2−.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The ground-state octet and decuplet baryons are well
established, but the nature of the low-lying excited Σ
baryon with I(JP ) = 1(1/2−) remains unsettled. In the
Review of Particle Physics (RPP) [1], the JP = 1/2−
state Σ(1620) is listed with only a one-star rating, in-
dicating that its existence requires further experimental
confirmation. In addition, chiral unitary approaches pre-
dict a dynamically generated Σ(1430) state with spin-
parity JP = 1/2−from S-wave pseudoscalar meson-
octet baryon scattering in the strangeness S = −1 sec-
tor, with a mass close to the ¯KN threshold [2–9]. Fur-
thermore, a reexamination of the γp →KΣπ reac-
tion using CLAS data indicates a potential signal near
1430 MeV [10, 11]. To further constrain the properties
of the Σ(1/2−) state, a wide array of alternative pro-
cesses has been proposed [12–24]. Belle has observed a
signal close to the ¯KN threshold in the Λπ invariant
mass distribution of the process Λ+
c →Λπ+π+π−[25],
which could be associated with the Σ(1/2−) [26, 27]. For
a detailed and up-to-date discussion on the status of this
elusive state, we refer the reader to the recent review in
Ref. [28].
On the other hand, several theoretical and phenomeno-
logical studies suggest the existence of a lower mass
I(JP ) = 1(1/2−) candidate around 1380 MeV (denoted

For example, the Σ(1380) has

To
further investigate this resonance, the Λ+
c →ηΛπ+ de-
cay was proposed as a promising channel to search for the
Σ(1380) state [38, 39]. The Belle Collaboration analyzed
this process in 2021 [40]. Subsequently, Ref. [41] studied
this decay and emphasized its potential for probing the
low-lying state Σ(1380). More recently, the BESIII Col-
laboration analyzed this process and reported evidence
for the Σ(1380) with JP = 1/2−at a statistical signifi-
cance exceeding 3σ [42, 43]. However, a subsequent study
argued that the BESIII data could be described without
introducing the Σ(1380) contribution [44]. Nevertheless,
visible deviations between the calculation and the data
remain in the low energy region of the π+Λ invariant
mass distribution. This unresolved tension motivates a
reanalysis of this decay with careful consideration of the
possible Σ(1380) contribution.
In the present work, we investigate the role of the
Σ(1380) state in the Λ+
c →ηΛπ+ decay and identify key
observables that are sensitive to its contribution. In addi-
tion to the Σ(1380), our model incorporates the Λ(1670)
generated dynamically from meson-baryon interactions,
the a0(980) generated from meson-meson interactions,
and the intermediate Σ(1385) resonance. We analyze the
Monte Carlo (MC) sample provided by the BESIII Col-
laboration together with the Belle data [45], and present
detailed results for the ηΛ, π+η, and π+Λ invariant mass
distributions, as well as the corresponding angular distri-

Another motivation for our detailed re-examination of
this process is that the formulas employed in experi-
mental data analysis are in urgent need of moderniza-
tion [46]. In the data analyses conducted by BESIII [42]
and Belle [40], the conventional Flatt´e and Breit-Wigner
(BW) parametrizations have been used for the a0(980)
and Λ(1670) resonances, respectively. When applied to
high-statistics experimental data, the inherent limita-
tions of these parametrizations, such as their failure to
satisfy unitarity and the large number of free parame-
ters, can compromise the quality of the data description.
This issue is further exacerbated by the fact that different
line shapes have been adopted in many analyses of the
a0(980) resonance [46]. Therefore, in this work we aim
to build upon our previous descriptions of hadronic reso-
nances with strong coupled-channel interactions, includ-
ing the a0(980) and Λ(1670). These descriptions feature
fewer free parameters and more universal line shapes,
which will reduce systematic uncertainties when extract-
ing resonance information from the data.

III.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS
In this work, we consider two scenarios. Case I does
not include the Σ(1380) contribution and contains five
free parameters, namely A, β, γ, ϕ, and ϕ′.
Case II
includes the Σ(1380) contribution and involves seven free
parameters: A, β, γ, ϕ, ϕ′, VΣ(1380), and ϕ′′.
To constrain these parameters, we first employ the MC
sample provided by the BESIII Collaboration for the de-
cay Λ+
c →ηπ+Λ. Compared with the distributions re-
ported in the published BESIII [42] and Belle [40] anal-
yses, this MC sample contains more detailed kinematic
information with background subtraction and efficiency
corrections, and therefore allows for a more detailed test
for the theoretical model. Specifically, the MC sample
contains 105 events. From this sample, we extract the
invariant mass distributions of the ηΛ, π+η, and π+Λ
systems, together with the angular distribution in cos θ.
The bin widths are set to 2 MeV for the invariant mass
distributions and 0.02 for the angular distribution. These
observables provide complementary constraints on the
model parameters and enable a more stringent compar-
ison between the theoretical distributions and the MC
simulation.
We then perform two fits to the BESIII MC sample,
corresponding to Case I (w/o Σ(1380)) and Case II (w
Σ(1380)). The fitted parameters are summarized in Ta-
ble I, from which we can find the inclusion of the Σ(1380)
contribution leads to a significant improvement in the
value of χ2/d.o.f.. Notably, the fitted magnitude of γ is
less than 1, consistent with the expectation that the in-
ternal emission mechanism is color-suppressed relative to
the external emission mechanism.

mass distributions, together with the angular distribu-
tion, obtained without including the Σ(1380) contribu-
tion. The corresponding results including the Σ(1380)
contribution are shown in Fig. 4.

IV.
CONCLUSIONS
In this work, we have performed a comprehensive the-
oretical study of the Λ+
c →ηΛπ+ decay to investigate
the possible role of the Σ(1380) state with JP = 1/2−.
Our theoretical framework incorporates the tree-level
contribution, meson-baryon and meson-meson final-state
interactions in coupled channels, and the intermediate
Σ(1385) and Σ(1380) resonances. Within this approach,
the Λ(1670) and a0(980) states are dynamically gener-
ated from the meson-baryon and meson-meson interac-
tions, respectively, providing a natural explanation for
the corresponding structures in the invariant mass dis-
tributions.
By comparing our model predictions with the MC sam-
ple from the BESIII Collaboration and the experimental
data from the Belle Collaboration, we find that the inclu-
sion of the Σ(1380) contribution substantially improves
the description of the relevant distributions. Specifically,
incorporating the Σ(1380) contribution reduces the value
of χ2/d.o.f. in both fits and allows the model to better
reproduce the experimental data. Furthermore, our anal-
ysis identifies several specific kinematic regions that are
particularly sensitive to the possible Σ(1380) contribu-
tion. These include the 1000 ∼1130 MeV region of the
π+η invariant mass distribution, the 1300 ∼1350 MeV
region of the π+Λ invariant mass distribution, and the
whole region of the angular distribution.
Therefore, with Belle II now accumulating a substan-
tially larger data sample, a combined Belle and Belle II
analysis of this decay would be highly valuable. Such an
effort could provide a more stringent test of the possible
Σ(1380) state and further clarify the spectrum of low-
lying excited hyperons in the isovector I = 1 sector. Fur-
thermore, the present theoretical model provides a useful
parametrization for future experimental data analyses, as
