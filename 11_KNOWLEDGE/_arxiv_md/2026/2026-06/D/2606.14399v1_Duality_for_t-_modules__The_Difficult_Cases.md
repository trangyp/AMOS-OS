DUALITY FOR t-MODULES: THE DIFFICULT CASES
DAWID EDMUND KĘDZIERSKI AND PIOTR KRASOŃ
Abstract. This paper continues our previous work on duality for Anderson t-modules.
We study two-dimensional triangular t-modules in a specific reduced form. Computer-
assisted symbolic computations with matrices over a skew field revealed a reduction
pattern for t-modules satisfying the ALD condition. Using this pattern, we prove that
such t-modules are isomorphic to their double duals and extend the validity of the
Cartier–Nishi theorem and the Weil–Barsotti formula to a substantially broader class
of t-modules.
1. Introduction
Drinfeld modules and their higher dimensional generalizations, since their invention,
have been central objects of intensive studies in number theory. In recent years, many
algorithmic methods have been developed for the t-modules and the associated t-motives
and t-co-motives e.g. [KP22], [M26], [ABCLNP26]. This creates a natural environment
for experimental mathematics. The present paper represents one of the first experimen-
tal approaches to problems in function field arithmetic over global function fields. We
address the concept of duality for t-modules. The duality for Drinfeld modules, in terms
of algebraic geometry, was investigated in [T95]. In [PR03], the authors defined dual-
ity for Drinfeld modules of rank at least two by means of the Ext1
τ-functor and proved
the Weil-Barsotti formula, as well as the Cartier-Nishi theorem [PR03, Theorem 1.1]. In
[KK24], we showed that, for a large class of pairs of t-modules Φ and Ψ, Ext1(Φ, Ψ) can
be endowed with a natural structure of a t-module. This was the starting point for our
further investigation of the duality. In [GKK25] we developed an algorithm, called the
t-reduction, which implements these results. Computing endomorphism rings of Drinfeld
modules is a highly nontrivial problem (see [KP22] and [GP22]). For a t-module Υ com-
puting a double dual module Υ∨∨and finding that Υ∨∨∼= Υ is by no means easy. Notice,
that one cannot expect the Cartier-Nishi theorem and the Weil-Barsotti formula to hold
for every t-module Υ (see[KK26, Example 4.1]). In [KK26] we proved these for Υ a non-
nilpotent, stictly pure t-module of degree at least two. Since the proof was done in the
language of biderivations it required careful reductions by inner biderivations. In [KK25]
we introduced triangular t-modules i.e. those with the composition series having Drinfeld
modules as τ-simple sub-quotients. For such modules allowing for low degree bideriva-
tions we were able to prove the Cartier-Nishi theorem and the Weil-Barsotti formula. In
this paper, we deal with the most difficult cases where substantial coefficient swell occurs.
This phenomenon constitutes a serious computational obstacle. To determine whether
any patterns emerge in reductions leading to the isomorphism Υ∨∨∼= Υ, we performed
a large number of computer experiments. We restricted our attention to triangular t-
modules of dimension two. These experiments enabled us to extend the aforementioned
Cartier-Nishi theorem and Weil-Barsotti formula to two-dimensional t-modules allowing

5. Symbolic complexity of dual and double dual computations
The explicit computation of duals and double duals of t-modules requires working with
concrete elements of the twisted polynomial ring K{τ}. Although the algebraic struc-
ture of these objects is well understood at an abstract level, their explicit representations
often involve symbolic expressions whose size and complexity grow rapidly during compu-
tation. This phenomenon is closely related to the well-known problem of expression swell
in symbolic computation; see [vdH22]. In the context of Drinfeld modules and t-modules,
similar computational challenges have been observed in explicit calculations over K{τ};
see [PR03, GKK25, KK25]. These works indicate that, even when degrees and ranks can
be controlled theoretically, the resulting coefficients may become prohibitively large from
a computational perspective. In this section, we investigate this phenomenon experimen-
tally for triangular t-modules of dimension two. We first study the dual construction and
then the double dual, demonstrating that symbolic complexity increases significantly at
each stage. Our experiments show that computer-assisted methods become indispensable
even for examples of moderate size.

10. Discussion and future directions
Our main theorem on Cartier–Nishi duality for two-dimensional triangular t-modules
allowing for ALD-biderivations extends the class of modules for which the duality is
known to hold. Recall that in [KK25] we proved the Cartier–Nishi theorem for triangular
t-modules of arbitrary dimension, but under a more restrictive degree condition, namely
for those allowing for LD-biderivations. The latter case appears to be more tractable,
since the relevant isomorphisms are induced by constant polynomials. Both proofs rely
crucially on the existence of a reduced form, established in [KK25, Theorem 4.1].
If
one attempts to work with non-reduced forms of t-modules, the formulas appearing in
Lemma 8.2 become so complicated as to be practically intractable. For t-modules that do
not allow for ALD-biderivations, the computational complexity increases substantially:
one obtains highly irregular formulas and, moreover, must work over a finite extension of
the field of definition of the t-module.
Despite these obstacles, we believe that Cartier–Nishi duality should hold in a much
broader setting, possibly for all dimensions. This belief motivates the general conjectures
stated above. However, establishing such a result will likely require a modification of
approach developed in the present paper.
