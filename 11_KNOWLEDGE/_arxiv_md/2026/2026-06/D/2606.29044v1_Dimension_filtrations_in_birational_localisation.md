1. Introduction
Let F be a field, and let Sm(F) denote the category of smooth, integral, separated
schemes of finite type over F. We silently replace each essentially small category by
a fixed small skeleton before localising; this does not change any of the Hom-sets
considered below. We use Gabriel–Zisman localisations of categories [2]. We write
Sb for the class of birational morphisms in Sm(F). For n ≥0, let d≤nSm(F) be the
full subcategory formed by varieties of dimension at most n, and put

Kahn and Sujatha asked whether ρn,∞is fully faithful [4, Question 8.8(3)].
Also in [3, Remark 8.12], the same authors stated that the transition between the
corresponding smooth projective categories is fully faithful in characteristic zero.
Our results show that the projective assertion announced there cannot hold as
stated, already for n = 1; see Remark 1.2. We answer the later question and obtain
a stronger statement.

(1) The functor ρ0,∞is fully faithful.
(2) For every n ≥1 and every N with n + 1 ≤N ≤∞, the functor ρn,N is not

Remark 1.2. This also shows that the assertion announced without proof in [3,
Remark 8.12] cannot hold as stated. Indeed, in the birational localisation of smooth
projective varieties of dimension at most one, birational morphisms of smooth
projective curves are already isomorphisms, so the self-maps [u : v] 7→[um : vm] of
P1 remain pairwise distinct. After allowing smooth projective surfaces, the Colliot–
Thélène surface argument makes P1 →Spec F invertible, and all these self-maps
become the identity. Thus the dimension-one to dimension-two transition is not
faithful over any field.
The mechanism is elementary. The category Ln admits a “top-dimensional degree”
functor which vanishes below dimension n and sends a dominant endomorphism of
an n-fold to its total generic degree. This detects morphisms which cannot yet be
identified inside Ln. The upper bound in our dimension threshold is obtained by
keeping track of dimensions in the surface argument of Colliot-Thélène [4, Appendix
A]; the degree functor proves that this bound is optimal.
More precisely, we prove the following sharp form of homotopy invariance.
Theorem 1.3. Let X ∈Sm(F) have dimension d, and let r ≥1 and d + r ≤n.
The projection
X × Ar −→X
is an isomorphism in Ln if and only if d + r ≤n −1.
Thus the projection first becomes invertible one step after the dimension of
its source. This exact threshold explains why every consecutive map Ln →Ln+1
already fails to be faithful. We also prove the same phenomenon in the smooth
proper and smooth projective subcategories; there the degree detector is simply
the top-dimensional Chow group. The full birational localisation and its relation
to R-equivalence and A1-homotopy have recently been revisited in [1, 5]; the point
here is the unstable behaviour caused by imposing a dimension bound.

Theorem 2.4. Let X ∈Sm(F) have dimension d, and let r ≥1 with d + r ≤n.
Then the projection
pX,r : X × Ar −→X
is an isomorphism in Ln if and only if d + r ≤n −1.
Proof. Assume first that d + r ≤n −1. For 1 ≤j ≤r, put Xj = X × Aj−1. Then
dim Xj = d + j −1, and Lemma 2.3 shows that
Xj × P1 −→Xj

Remark 2.5. Equivalently, among integers N ≥d + r, the smallest N for which

with A1 replaced by P1 when r = 1.
3. Failure of faithfulness
We now prove the negative part of Theorem 1.1 in a form which exhibits infinitely
many morphisms in one fibre.
Theorem 3.1. Let n ≥1, and let N satisfy n + 1 ≤N ≤∞. For m ≥1, define
µm : An −→An,

The morphisms µm are pairwise distinct in Ln, but all have the same image, namely
the identity, in LN. Consequently ρn,N is not faithful and has an infinite fibre on
End(An).
Proof. The generic degree of µm is m.
Indeed, after adjoining the first n −1

It remains to treat dimension zero.
Proposition 3.4. The functor ρ0,∞: L0 →L∞is fully faithful.
Proof. Every object of d≤0Sm(F) is Spec K for a finite separable extension K/F,
and every birational morphism between such objects is an isomorphism. Hence
L0 = d≤0Sm(F).
Let U = Spec K and V = Spec E be two such objects. The target V is smooth
and proper. The Hom formula of Kahn and Sujatha [4, Theorem 6.6.3] gives
HomL∞(U, V ) ≃V (K)/R,
where the quotient on the right is taken with respect to the classical R-equivalence
of Manin [6, Ch. II, §14]; see also [4, Definition 6.6.1]. The relation R is equality
on V (K). Indeed, a rational map P1
K 99K VK, defined at two prescribed points,
extends uniquely to a morphism because P1
K is a regular curve and VK is proper.
More explicitly, writing VK = Spec A, such a morphism is equivalent to a K-algebra
homomorphism A →Γ(P1

of VK. Thus every direct R-equivalence is trivial, and therefore the equivalence
relation generated by direct R-equivalence is equality. Consequently,
V (K)/R = V (K) = HomF (Spec K, Spec E).
