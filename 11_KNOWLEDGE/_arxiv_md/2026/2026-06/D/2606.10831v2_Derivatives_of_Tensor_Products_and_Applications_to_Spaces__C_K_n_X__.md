Abstract. In this paper we develop an abstract theory of derivatives for Banach spaces based on
objects that we call bidual assignments. This framework encompasses both the Semadeni derivative
and the recently introduced Semadeni–Pe lczy´nski derivative.
More generally, suitable ideals of
subsets of the dual space give rise to a broad family of derivatives within this setting.
We establish addition and product formulas for these derivatives, showing that they behave
naturally with respect to direct sums and injective tensor products. As an application, we compute
iterated derivatives for a number of spaces C(K) associated with scattered compacta, including
scattered compact lines and compact trees.
As a further application, we establish classification results for spaces of the form C(Kn, X). In
particular, for uncountable ordinals α and β, an integer n ≥1, and Banach spaces X satisfying
suitable rigidity assumptions, we prove that
C([0, α]n, X) ∼C([0, β]n, X)
if and only if
C([0, α]) ∼C([0, β]).
This extends Kislyakov’s classification of spaces C([0, α]) and its vector-valued extension due to
Galego to finite powers of ordinal intervals.
1. Introduction
The notion of a derivative of a Banach space, as understood in the present work, originates in
a seminal paper of Semadeni [26]. Given a Banach space X, Semadeni considered the subspace
∆(X) ⊂X∗∗consisting of all weak∗-sequentially continuous functionals on X∗, and observed that
the quotient
S(X) = ∆(X)/X
is an isomorphic invariant of X. He computed
S(C([0, ω1])) ∼R
and
S(C([0, ω1]) ⊕C([0, ω1])) ∼R2,
thereby proving that C([0, ω1]) is not isomorphic to its square. This provided the first example of
a Banach space X satisfying X ̸∼X ⊕X, solving a problem posed by Banach [1].
Semadeni’s ideas were subsequently developed by Kislyakov [16], who employed related derivatives
to obtain a classification of the spaces C([0, α]), where α is an uncountable ordinal. Later, Galego
extended these methods to the vector-valued setting, obtaining several classification results for
spaces of the form C(K, X), where K is an ordinal interval and X is a Banach space; see, for
instance, [8, 9, 10, 11].
More recently, Korpalski [17, 18] introduced the Semadeni–Pe lczy´nski derivative and studied it
systematically in the setting of spaces of continuous functions on compact lines. In particular, he
applied this derivative to spaces of the form C(Kn), where K is a compact line. His work builds on
ideas originating in Semadeni’s work and further developed in [4].

The present paper is motivated by the observation that all the constructions mentioned above
share a common underlying structure. We introduce an abstract notion of derivative based on what
we call a bidual assignment (Definition 3.1), that is, a rule assigning to each Banach space X a
closed subspace
X ⊂∆(X) ⊂X∗∗
which behaves functorially with respect to bounded linear operators.
Associated with such an
assignment, we consider the derivative
S∆(X) = ∆(X)/X,
which provides an isomorphic invariant of the Banach space X.
One of the main sources of examples in this paper is provided by what we call testing ideals I
(Definition 4.1), namely ideals of subsets of dual spaces satisfying suitable stability properties. Such
ideals induce bidual assignments ∆I, and therefore derivatives SI.
Among the main structural results of the paper are addition and product formulas for deriva-
tives associated with testing ideals (Theorems 3.14 and 4.7). Under suitable assumptions, these
derivatives interact naturally with c0-sums, ℓp-sums and injective tensor products, see Theorem 6.3.
These structural results are then applied to spaces of continuous functions associated with scat-
tered compact Hausdorff spaces, including compact trees, ordinal intervals, and their finite products.
The resulting formulas for the corresponding derivatives (Theorem 4.10) lead to our main applica-
tion, Theorem 6.10, namely a classification theorem for spaces of the form C([0, α]n, X), where α
is an uncountable ordinal and X is a Banach space satisfying suitable rigidity assumptions. More
precisely, we prove that
C([0, α]n, X) ∼C([0, β]n, X)
if and only if
C([0, α]) ∼C([0, β]).
whenever X is a Mazur space with the Gelfand–Phillips property, contains no copy of c0, and sat-
isfies Xp ̸∼Xq for distinct positive integers p and q. Our classification theorem extends Kislyakov’s
classification of spaces C([0, α]) and its vector-valued extension due to Galego from ordinal intervals
to their finite powers.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the basic terminology and notation
used throughout the paper. In Section 3, we define bidual assignments and the derivatives they
generate, and study their behavior with respect to direct sums. In Section 4, we introduce testing
ideals and establish a product formula for the associated derivatives under suitable assumptions. In
Section 5, we verify these assumptions for Banach spaces of the form C(K). More specifically, we
consider scattered compact Hausdorff spaces and compact lines, and isolate conditions under which
the results of the previous sections apply. Finally, in Section 6, we apply the general theory to the
testing ideals Iκ and I<κ, obtain explicit formulas for compact trees and finite products of ordinal
intervals, and prove the main classification theorem. The appendix contains two auxiliary results
on compact lines that are needed for some applications developed in the paper.
2. Terminology and Preliminaries
In this section we collect the basic terminology, notation, and conventions used throughout the
paper.
Given a topological space K, the character of a point x ∈K, denoted by χ(K, x), is the least
cardinality of a local base at x.
We shall also consider compact trees endowed with the interval topology, henceforth simply called
compact trees. Recall that a tree K is a partially ordered set such that, for every x ∈K, the set of
predecessors pred(x) = {y ∈K : y < x} is well ordered. The interval topology on K is generated
by sets of the form (s, t] = {x ∈K : s < x ≤t}, together with all singletons {x} such that x is a
minimal element of K; see [28].

3. Bidual Assignments and Derivatives of Banach Spaces
The aim of this section is to isolate a general framework underlying several concepts that share a
common mechanism, as seen in works such as [26, 4, 16, 17, 18] and in Galego’s classification theory
for spaces of continuous vector-valued functions [8, 9, 10, 11].

From a general perspective, one expects a derivative of a Banach space C(K) to produce a

We now specialize the general theory developed above to the testing ideals Iκ and I<κ from

explicit computations in the settings of scattered compact spaces and compact lines.
