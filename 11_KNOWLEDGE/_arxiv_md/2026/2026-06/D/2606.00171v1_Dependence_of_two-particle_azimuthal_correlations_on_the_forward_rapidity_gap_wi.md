Dependence of two-particle azimuthal correlations on the
forward rapidity gap width in pPb collisions
at √sNN = 8.16 TeV
The CMS Collaboration*
Abstract
One of the most striking features of relativistic heavy ion collisions is the presence
of collective flow of thousands of produced particles. This flow can be characterized
by the Fourier coefficients (Vn∆) of the azimuthal angular distributions of charged
particles, and its existence can be explained by the formation of a quark gluon plasma,
which behaves as a fluid. Surprisingly, the angular distributions of particles from very
small systems such as proton-lead (pPb), proton-proton (pp), electron-positron, and
photon-proton (γp) collisions also exhibit non-zero Fourier coefficients, raising the
question of whether collective flow is present. This paper presents measurements of
Vn∆from a sample of pPb events at √sNN = 8.16 TeV that are enriched in photon-lead
(γPb) and pomeron-lead (IPPb) interactions by requiring no particles in the proton-
going region. Measurements are made as a function of the forward rapidity gap width
(the rapidity range in which no particles are found), the transverse momentum of the
particles, and the multiplicity of particles in the event. The results are compared to
previous measurements of pp, pPb, and γp + IPp events as well as modern event
generators.

Introduction
Two-particle azimuthal correlations can provide insights into the so-called collectivity of sys-
tems produced in high-energy collisions. Collectivity refers to the correlations among multiple
particles in the final state of these interactions. These correlations can help in understanding
the system produced in the collision and its hydrodynamic properties. The evidence for collec-
tivity includes both long-range rapidity correlations (|∆η| > 2), known as the “ridge”, for pairs
of particles that are close in azimuthal angle (ϕ), as well as the observation of multiparticle
azimuthal correlations in proton-proton (pp) [1–5], proton-lead (pPb) [6–18], and a wide range
of high-energy nucleus-nucleus collisions [19–28] using the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) at
CERN and the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) at BNL.
Azimuthal correlations between particle pairs can be quantified via their Fourier decomposi-
tion, dNpair/d∆ϕ ∝1 + ∑n 2Vn∆cos(n∆ϕ), where Vn∆are the two-particle Fourier coefficients,
Npair is the number of pairs, and ∆ϕ is the angle difference of the pair. If factorization is
assumed, the single-particle azimuthal anisotropy Fourier coefficients vn can be extracted as
vn = √Vn∆[29]. The second- (v2) and third-order (v3) coefficients are known as elliptic and
triangular flow, respectively [30]. In hydrodynamical models, v2 and v3 are directly related to
the initial collision geometry and its fluctuations, respectively [31–33]. In general, these Fourier
components provide insights into the transport properties of the medium.
In recent years, collective behavior has been found in small systems such as those produced
in pp and pPb collisions [1, 8]. This has generated a series of studies at RHIC, KEKB, and
the LHC [15, 34–39]. For high-multiplicity events, the v2 and v3 Fourier coefficients depend
upon the hadron species. For pPb and PbPb collisions, the value of v2 scales with the number
of valence quarks in the hadron [15]. While these results in small systems are consistent with
a hydrodynamic expansion, alternative models based on gluon saturation in the initial state
can also reproduce the main features of the correlation data (recent reviews are provided, e.g.,
in Refs. [40, 41]). While long-range azimuthal correlations have been established in inclusive
high-multiplicity pp and pPb events, their microscopic origin remains actively debated. They
thus reinforce the interest in correlation measurements for other small systems, such as those
produced in electron-positron, electron-proton, photon-proton (γp), and low multiplicity pp
collisions [39, 42–46], as well as in photon-lead (γPb) [47], oxygen-oxygen, and neon-neon
systems [48], and in high-multiplicity jets [49].
Event selections that suppress multiparton interactions and modify color reconnection pro-
cesses provide an important discriminator between scenarios based on a collective final-state
response and those dominated by initial-state correlations. Selecting events with no particles in
a portion of the proton-going η region (called the “forward rapidity gap width”) can offer such
a handle since this enhances semi-exclusive topologies associated with color-singlet exchange
and photo-induced processes. Therefore, these events allow the study of azimuthal correla-
tions in samples that are complementary to the inclusive measurements. The forward rapidity
gap width (∆ηF) is defined as the width of the rapidity region (starting at η = 3 and extend-
ing inward in the proton-going direction) where no particles are detected. This paper presents
the first search for long-range azimuthal correlations as a function of the forward rapidity gap
width in pPb collisions.
The pPb collision data were taken with the CMS experiment at the LHC at a center-of-mass en-
ergy of √sNN = 8.16 TeV [50]. Events with a large ∆ηF are asymmetric in pseudorapidity, with
most activity in the part of the detector opposite to the direction of the outgoing proton beam,
and almost no activity on the other side. This asymmetry enhances semi-exclusive events, in
which the proton remains intact, or dissociates into a low-mass state that escapes undetected,

Experimental apparatus
The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6 m internal diame-
ter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8 T. Within the solenoid volume is the silicon tracker, a lead
tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter, and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter,
each composed of a barrel (EB and HB) and two endcap (EE and HE) sections, and covering
the pseudorapidity range |η| < 3.0. The silicon tracker measures charged particles within the

range |η| < 2.5; it consists of 1 440 silicon pixel and 15 148 silicon strip detector modules and
provides an impact parameter resolution of ≈15 µm. The event selection for this analysis ex-
ploits the HF detectors (HF+ and HF−) in the 3.0 < |η| < 5.2 region, which use quartz fibers
embedded in a steel absorber [57].
The analysis in the region |η| < 3.0 is based upon objects produced by the CMS particle-flow
(PF) algorithm [58], which reconstructs and identifies final-state particles with an optimized
combination of information from the various elements of the CMS detector.
A more detailed description of the CMS detector, together with a definition of the relevant
kinematic variables, can be found in Ref. [50]. The coordinate system used in this paper is
defined as follows: x points in the direction of the center of the LHC ring, y is upwards and z is
along the beam line according to the right-hand rule. The η is defined such as to have positive
values when z > 0.
3
Data samples
The analysis is performed using data recorded by CMS during the LHC pPb run in 2016 cor-
responding to an integrated luminosity of 174.5 nb−1 [59]. The beam energies were 6.5 TeV for
the protons and 2.56 TeV per nucleon for the lead nuclei in the laboratory frame, resulting in
a center-of-mass energy per nucleon pair (√sNN) of 8.16 TeV. The data include samples with
the proton going towards the positive and towards the negative η direction. As a result of the
energy difference between the colliding beams, the proton-nucleus center-of-mass frame for a
pPb collision is not at rest with respect to the laboratory frame. Massless particles emitted at
ηcm = 0 in the pPb center-of-mass frame are detected at η = +0.465 in the laboratory frame.
The data were selected online with a two level trigger system consisting of the level-1, based
on custom hardware processors, and the high-level trigger (HLT), running a version of the full
event reconstruction software optimized for fast processing [60, 61].
Monte Carlo samples are used for comparing models to the measurements. Samples are gener-
ated with EPOS-LHC [53], and PYTHIA 8.3.10 event generators [62]. The EPOS-LHC generator is
based on the Gribov–Regge model [53], which includes nondiffractive (ND), central-diffractive
(CD), single-diffractive (SD), and double-diffractive (DD) events, but no photo-induced pro-
cesses; collective effects are simulated. The EPOS-LHC samples are subsequently processed
with GEANT4 to simulate the response of the CMS detector [63]. Samples were produced with
the proton beam in both directions. The PYTHIA 8.3.10 generator is based on the ANGANTYR
model [56]. Separate samples of ND, SD, DD events were produced. The generator does not
include collective effects nor photo-induced processes. The PYTHIA 8.3.10 samples were only
used to compare to data corrected to the generator level and, therefore, were not processed to
detector level using GEANT4.

Event selection
The event selection and the definition of the data samples follow closely those of Ref. [52].
Events are selected by the level-1 trigger if energy deposits are present in at least one of the
HF calorimeters above a threshold of approximately 1 GeV. The presence of at least one track
with pT > 0.4 GeV in the pixel tracker is required at the HLT. The track reconstruction is per-
formed online as part of the HLT trigger with reconstruction algorithms identical to those used
offline [64]. For each event, the reconstructed vertex with the highest number of associated
tracks is selected as the primary vertex (PV).


## Page 6


4
The reconstructed PV is required to be within 15 cm of the nominal interaction point along the
beam axis (z) and within 0.15 cm in the transverse plane. High-purity tracks [64] are used. At
least two reconstructed tracks with |η| < 2.4, pT > 0.4 GeV are required to be associated with
the PV. Beam related background is suppressed by rejecting events for which less than 25%
of all reconstructed tracks pass the track selection criteria mentioned above. Additionally, the
impact parameter significance of reconstructed tracks with respect to the PV in the longitudinal
and transverse directions is required to be less than 3 standard deviations. Finally, the relative
uncertainty of the track pT is required to be less than 10%.
A forward rapidity gap is a region with low detector activity and is defined as follows. The
region −3.0 < η < 3.0 is divided into 12 bins that are 0.5 units wide. Energy thresholds
are assigned to each bin such that at least 99.7% of the signals due to detector noise or beam
gas events [52] are below the threshold. These are 6 GeV for the central region |η| < 2.5, for
which all PF candidates are considered, and 13.4 GeV for |η| > 2.5 for which only neutral
hadronic PF candidates are considered due to noise in the electromagnetic calorimeter. A bin is
considered empty if the total energy from all PF candidates is below the threshold. In addition,
for the central region |η| < 2.5, no high-purity tracks with pT > 200 MeV are allowed. The
∆ηF variable is then defined as the difference between η = 3.0 and the higher edge of the first
nonempty η bin. The definition is reversed when the direction of the proton beam is towards
negative values of η.
Events are selected as follows. The hadronic forward HF+ calorimeter on the positive η side
is required to have all towers with an energy deposit below 2 GeV. The HF−calorimeter on
the negative side is required to have at least one tower with an energy deposit above 10 GeV.
Additionally, the η bin [+2.5, +3.0) is required to be empty, reflecting the lack of activity in the
direction of the outgoing proton. Events with a presence of a high-purity track with η > 2.5 and
pT > 200 MeV were removed since their effect on ∆ηF is not well understood. This selection is
referred to in the following as the “nominal selection” and the resulting sample as the “nominal
sample”. The definitions are reversed in η when the direction of the proton beam is towards
negative values of η. The analysis is performed in different ∆ηF bins from 0.5 to 2.5 in intervals
0.5 units wide and a final bin from 2.5 onwards.
In Ref. [52] it was found that the EPOS-LHC generator predicts a ∆ηF spectrum with a shape
reasonably consistent with that of the data but with a different yield. The results suggest only
a small nondiffractive contribution when the rapidity gap cut ∆ηF > 2.5 is applied; an ad-
ditional set of measurements with such a cut is carried out here. This selection is referred to
as “diffraction-enhanced selection” in the following, and the resulting sample is obtained by
applying the nominal selection but restricting the events to the last ∆ηF bin. For events with
rapidity gaps on the negative side (not analyzed here), MC studies indicate that they are dom-
inated by γp interactions [52].

Systematic uncertainties
In this section, the sources of the systematic uncertainties in the Vn∆Fourier coefficients are dis-
cussed. The systematic uncertainties are evaluated as a function of ∆ηF and N|η|<2.4, pT>0.3 GeV
ch
by varying the way the coefficients are determined.
The general procedure for determining the systematic uncertainties is described in Ref. [67].
The systematic uncertainty is considered to be significant (and taken into account) when it
affects the result by more than its statistical uncertainty, having considered the correlation be-
tween the sample modified by the systematic variation and the nominal sample.
The systematic effects considered are the following:
• The effect of tracking inefficiency and misreconstructed track rate is studied by vary-
ing the track quality requirements. The selection thresholds on the significance of the
transverse and longitudinal track impact parameters are varied from 2 to 5 standard
deviations. In addition, the relative track pT uncertainty is varied from 5–10%.
• The sensitivity to the PV position along the beam axis (zvtx) is studied by comparing
events with different zvtx locations from −15 to +15 cm.
• The possible contamination from overlapping pPb collisions in a single event, re-
ferred to as pileup, is studied by analyzing two subsets of data with high and low
pileup, respectively, corresponding to high and low luminosity periods. On average
the high-pileup sample has a pileup of 0.25 while the low-pileup has 0.10.
• The trigger efficiency decreases substantially for Noffline
trk
< 10. To study the effect
of this decrease, a parallel sample is studied with events weighted by the inverse of
the efficiency, which depends on Noffline
trk
. The full difference of the results with and
without the reweighting is taken as the uncertainty.
• When the proton beam direction changes, the detectors used for the measurement
are different. To study this effect, the results obtained for the two proton beam di-
rections are compared.

trigger efficiency are the main contributors. This can be observed in Table 1, which shows the
unweighted average (across all ∆ηF bins) of the systematic uncertainty contribution by source

amount to about twice the statistical uncertainty. The total uncertainty is obtained by sum-

Summary
Long-range azimuthal (ϕ) two-particle correlations have been studied for in proton-lead (pPb)
events at √sNN = 8.16 TeV as a function of the forward rapidity gap width (∆ηF) and the
charged-particle multiplicity (N|η|<2.4, pT>0.3 GeV
ch
), where pT is the transverse momentum. Vary-
ing ∆ηF provides a continuous handle on the degree of exclusivity of the selected pPb events
and therefore complements inclusive small-system correlation measurements. Events have
been selected by requiring energy deposition in one of the forward calorimeters and no activity
in the forward calorimeter on the opposite side. In addition, we require no energy in the region
2.5 < η < 3, favoring events with activity in the direction of the outgoing Pb beam. For events
with ∆ηF > 2.5, the fraction of diffractive events is enhanced, and these events were further
studied as a function of charged particle multiplicity. The average track multiplicity is lower
in the diffractive sample than for inclusive pPb events, but larger than for photon-proton (γp)
events. The slope of the multiplicity distribution becomes steeper with increasing ∆ηF. The
mean track multiplicity ⟨Noffline
trk
⟩decreases by 48% when ∆ηF changes from 0.5 < ∆ηF < 1.0
to ∆ηF > 2.5. The two-dimensional two-particle correlation functions have been averaged
over |∆η| > 2 and the resulting ∆ϕ distributions have been studied in terms of their Fourier
coefficients V1∆and V2∆, as a function of ∆ηF and N|η|<2.4, pT>0.3 GeV
ch
.
The measured values of V1∆are negative and decrease with ∆ηF, while the central values of V2∆
increase monotonically with ∆ηF, although they are consistent with zero within uncertainties
in two of the bins. The results have been compared with the predictions of the PYTHIA 8.3.10
model, which does not include collective effects, and with EPOS-LHC model, which includes
such effects. The measured values of the elliptic flow coefficient (v2) are provided for the bins
[0.5, 1), [1, 1.5), [2, 2.5). In the other bins, the v2 results are consistent with zero. In these bins,
95% confidence level upper limits have been determined; they are stable across the ∆ηF and
N|η|<2.4, pT>0.3 GeV
ch
regions studied, indicating comparable sensitivity in all bins, and reach val-
ues up to 0.13 for the most inclusive Noffline
trk
bin. The values of the upper limits are similar to
the values measured in γp events.
These results constrain scenarios in which ridge-like correlations are expected to persist uni-
versally across small systems independent of event topology. They also provide a reference for
models that predict reduced collective response when the multiparton interactions and color
reconnection processes are suppressed.
