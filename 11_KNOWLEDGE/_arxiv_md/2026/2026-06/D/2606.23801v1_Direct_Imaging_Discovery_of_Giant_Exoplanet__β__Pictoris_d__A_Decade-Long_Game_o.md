ABSTRACT
We report the direct imaging discovery of a third exoplanet in the β Pictoris system. We detect
β Pictoris d in non-coronagraphic observations obtained with VLT/ERIS as well as multi-epoch archival
datasets from JWST/NIRCam and VLT/SPHERE. Astrometric measurements over an 11-year baseline
demonstrate that it is consistent with a gravitationally-bound source with orbital motion. Joint multi-
planet orbit fits of all three planets in the system yield a semi-major axis of 26.0
+2.2
−6.1 au and inclination
89.0
+0.7
−0.6 deg for planet d. β Pictoris d has a larger orbital semi-major axis than the other known planets
in the system, but is coplanar with the inner two planets, and its orbit is consistent with sculpting the
inner edge of the debris disk. β Pictoris d has a contrast of ∆L′ = 12.11 ± 0.15 mag, with colors and
luminosity that closely match those of 51 Eri b, another exoplanet in the β Pictoris moving group. Its
VLT/ERIS and JWST/NIRCam colors are distinct from those of free-floating planetary-mass objects
of a similar age and temperature. Its red F410M −F444W color indicates strong CO2 absorption in
its atmosphere and suggests significant enhancement in metals compared to free-floating objects. From
the ATMO hot-start evolutionary models, we estimate an effective temperature of 600
+45
−60 K and mass
of 2.4 ± 0.6 MJup, which also closely matches similar estimates for 51 Eri b. β Pictoris d is among
the lowest-mass exoplanets imaged from the ground. This discovery highlights the deep sensitivity
achievable with ground-based imaging in the mid-infrared and the discovery potential of future high-
contrast observations with the Extremely Large Telescope.

1. INTRODUCTION
Since the detection of the first directly imaged exo-
planets such as β Pictoris b (A.-M. Lagrange et al. 2010)
and HR 8799 bcd (C. Marois et al. 2008), near-infrared
(near-IR) direct imaging has revealed a population of
dozens of young >3 MJup planets with effective tem-
peratures of 800–2000 K (see
T. Currie et al. 2023a,
for a recent review).
Many of these planets reside in

multi-planet systems (e.g. J. Milli et al. 2017; M. Kep-
pler et al. 2018; S. Y. Haffert et al. 2019; A. J. Bohn et al.
2020; S. Hinkley et al. 2023; R. F. van Capelleveen et al.
2025a,b; L. M. Close et al. 2025), including both of the
two emblematic first directly imaged exoplanetary sys-
tems; HR 8799 e (C. Marois et al. 2010) was announced
soon after the other three HR 8799 planets, and β Pic-
toris c was detected indirectly in 2019 (A.-M. Lagrange
et al. 2019a). Following this trend, we report the discov-
ery of a third giant exoplanet in the β Pictoris system.
It seems likely that other directly imaged exoplanetary
systems harbor additional lower mass planets that are
not detectable with current instrumentation.
Recent improvements in sensitivity in mid-infrared in-
strumentation have enabled high-contrast imaging ob-
servations of a growing number of cold (≲700 K) gi-
ant exoplanets at 3–5 µm wavelengths close to their
peak blackbody emission, including the detections of
ε Ind A b (E. C. Matthews et al. 2024), TWA 7 b (A.-
M. Lagrange et al. 2025; K. A. Crotts et al. 2025), and
14 Her c (D. C. Bardalez Gagliuffi et al. 2025) with
JWST. At young ages (<100 Myr), this translates into
the possibility to detect <3 MJup exoplanets, which are
more challenging to detect in the near-IR. While space-
based facilities offer inherent stability and access to
longer wavelengths, the largest ground-based telescopes
remain highly complementary for mid-IR detections of
such planets.
This is in part because of the greater
light-collecting power and angular resolution provided
by their mirror sizes, which exceed that of JWST, e.g.
the Very Large Telescope (VLT) Enhanced Resolution
Imager and Spectrograph (ERIS; R. Davies et al. 2023).
With an age of 23 ± 8 Myr (R. A. Lee et al. 2024) and
distance of 19.63±0.06 pc ( Gaia Collaboration et al.
2016, 2023), the A6V star β Pictoris hosts two known
exoplanetary companions, β Pictoris b (β Pic b; A.-M.
Lagrange et al. 2010) and β Pictoris c (β Pic c; A.-M. La-
grange et al. 2019a; M. Nowak et al. 2020). The outer
planet, β Pic b, has a dynamical mass of 9.3
+2.6
−2.5 MJup
and a semi-major axis of 10.26±0.10 au (A. M. Lagrange
et al. 2020; G. M. Brandt et al. 2021), while β Pic c
has a comparable dynamical mass of 8.89±0.75 MJup
(J. Kammerer et al. 2025) but a much closer-in or-
bital semi-major axis of 2.68±0.02 au (S. Lacour et al.
2021). Photometric and multi-resolution spectroscopic
observations of β Pic b find a partially cloudy atmo-
sphere with water and carbon monoxide, Teff ∼1700 K,
log g ∼4.2 dex, and rotational velocity v sin(i) ∼20
km s−1 (e.g., M. Bonnefoy et al. 2013; T. Currie et al.
2013; K. M. Morzinski et al. 2015; J. Chilcote et al.
2017; GRAVITY Collaboration et al. 2020; T. Stolker
et al. 2020; J. Kammerer et al. 2024; L. T. Parker et al.
2024; R. Landman et al. 2024; M. Houll´e et al. 2025;
M. Ravet et al. 2025).
Spectroscopy in K-band for
β Pic c obtained with VLTI/GRAVITY (J. Kammerer
et al. 2025) suggests a similar effective temperature and
early-L spectral type as β Pic b.
β Pictoris also possesses a large edge-on circumstel-
lar debris disk (spanning between ∼30-200 au from the
star, B. A. Smith & R. J. Terrile 1984; I. Rebollido et al.
2024; Y. Han et al. 2026) that has long been connected
to potential exoplanet formation in this system (e.g. H.
Scholl et al. 1993; D. Lazzaro et al. 1994; C. J. Bur-
rows et al. 1995; D. Mouillet et al. 1997; S. R. Heap
et al. 2000; Z. Wahhaj et al. 2003; W. R. F. Dent et al.
2014; Y. Han et al. 2023; N. Skaf et al. 2023).
The
known planets are believed to have played a significant
role in sculpting disk structures, in particular, the sig-
nificant warp observed in the β Pictoris disk, with the
inner ∼80 au tilted by ∼5 deg relative to the outer disk
(C. J. Burrows et al. 1995; D. A. Golimowski et al. 2006).
Both planets reside in near edge-on orbits coplanar with
each other, although neither transit (L. van Sluijs et al.
2019; M. A. Kenworthy et al. 2021). The orbits of these
planets are thought to be inclined relative to the outer
disk, and hence drive the warp (D. Mouillet et al. 1997;
J. L. Smallwood 2023). Recently, A. Lacquement et al.
(2025) proposed the existence of an additional planet in
the β Pic system to explain the observed inner debris
disk edge at 30–50 au (Y. Han et al. 2026). They found
that the two known planets are unable to sculpt the disk
out to this radius, and that a planet capable of doing so
would necessarily reside beyond the orbit of β Pic b.
In this letter, we present the discovery of a third,
wider-separation planet in the β Pictoris system, β Pic-
toris d (hereinafter β Pic d), initially identified in
non-coronagraphic direct imaging observations with
VLT/ERIS. We confirm the planet through additional
detections in earlier epochs of archival datasets from
other instruments. We describe the corresponding data
reduction methodology in Section 2. In Section 3, we
use astrometry to confirm that the observed source is
gravitationally-bound to its host star and fit its or-
bital motion. We also use photometric measurements
to begin characterising the atmosphere of the planet
and estimate its mass.
We then discuss the implica-
tions of the results for the β Pic system in Section 4.
Lastly, we summarize this work in Section 5. An inde-
pendent and contemporaneous discovery of β Pictoris d
using JWST/NIRSpec+MIRI observations is separately
reported by Gibbs et al. (2026, accepted).

2. OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION
We present results derived from observations obtained
with four different instruments over 11 years. A sum-
mary of these observations can be found in Table 1.
2.1. VLT/ERIS Observations
We observed the β Pictoris system on 2025 December
03 (02:15:33–05:18:07 UT) with the ERIS Near Infrared
Camera System (NIX; D. Pearson et al. 2016) on the
VLT as part of a UK Guaranteed Time Observations
programme with ESO programme no. 116.28WQ.001
(PI: Birkby, Biller). We used the 13 mas pixel−1 camera
and the L′-band filter, which has a central wavelength of
3.79 µm, bandwidth of 0.60 µm, and zeropoint flux den-
sity of 239.24 Jy in the Vega system1. To maximize sen-
sitivity, we used the LM-pupil without a coronagraph.
Compared to the Lyot stop of the vortex coronagraph
(G. Orban de Xivry et al. 2024), this provides better core
throughput, enabling deeper background limits. The ob-
servations were obtained in pupil-stabilised mode, which
permits the use of Angular Differential Imaging (ADI;
C. Marois et al. 2006) for point spread function (PSF)
subtraction. We acquired 212 science frames on-target,
each consisting of 4000 coadded frames with an integra-
tion time of 8.7214 ms per frame, for a total of 7395 s
(∼2 h) after removing 4 frames with poor AO correc-
tion.
We used nodding to facilitate background sub-
traction while avoiding regions of clustered bad pixels.
These short individual exposure times ensured that the
images remained unsaturated, which is essential to min-

imize artifacts caused by detector cross-talk and poten-
tial detector persistence.
We reduced the data using eristools, a Python
package for processing VLT/ERIS HCI data based on
PynPoint (T. Stolker et al. 2019).
The pipeline will
be described in detail in a forthcoming publication
(Bonse et al. in prep.). Using eristools, we perform
the following steps: First, we subtract dark frames and
apply a flat-field correction using sky flats obtained
at similar airmasses to those of the science frames.
VLT/ERIS data also require correction for detector non-
linearity.
We use esorex (E. C. D. Team 2015) to
fit a second-order polynomial to each individual pixel.
The resulting non-linearity coefficients are imported into
eristools to correct for the detector non-linearity. Sec-
ond, we subtract the background from each science
frame using the frames at the opposite nod position.
eristools identifies bad pixels in two steps. First, we
generate a bad-pixel map from the detector non-linearity
coefficients.
We then identify any remaining outliers
through a 4σ-clipping within a local region of size 5 × 5
pixels. In the final step, we aligned all frames relative to
each other using sub-pixel cross-correlations, then cen-
tred them by fitting the unsaturated PSF with a Gaus-
sian.

et al. (2022). We explored different sets of processing
parameters. For PCA we calculated residuals with 1 to
50 principal components. For the 4S reductions, the im-

2.2. JWST/NIRCam Observations
We use two archival epochs of coronagraphic imaging
observations targeting β Pictoris with the JWST (J. P.
Gardner et al. 2006, 2023) Near Infrared Camera (NIR-
Cam; M. J. Rieke et al. 2003, 2023).
The first of these was obtained on 2023 March 18 UT
as part of GTO 1411 and was previously described by J.
Kammerer et al. (2024). This dataset consists of obser-
vations in six filters, two in NIRCam’s short-wavelength
channel (F182M, F210M) using the MASK210R coron-
agraphic occulting mask, and four in its long-wavelength
channel (F250M, F300M, F335M, F444W) using the
MASK335R mask.
The short and long-wavelength
observations have pixel scales of 31 mas pixel−1 and
63 mas pixel−1, respectively.
The total integration in
each filter is given in Table 1, and in Appendix A. Each
observation was obtained at two roll positions, sepa-
rated by 10.2 deg, to enable PSF subtraction through
ADI, and observations of a guide star, α Pictoris, were
also acquired to allow Reference-star Differential Imag-
ing (RDI; e.g. J. E. Krist et al. 1998). The data reduc-
tion process for this dataset was carried out using the
pyKLIP (J. J. Wang et al. 2015) and spaceKLIP (J. H.
Girard et al. 2022; J. Kammerer et al. 2022; A. L. Carter
et al. 2023) pipelines and is described in detail in J.
Kammerer et al. (2024).
The second JWST/NIRCam epoch was obtained on
2025 March 21–22 (11:23:20–03:34:07 UT) as part of
GO 4758 (Zhou et al. submitted). These observations
were acquired using the F210M and F410M filters in
dual-band coronagraphic imaging mode and using the
MASK335R occulting mask.
Each exposure covered
2,352 s of integration time. This was repeated 10 times
for each of two rolls, separated by 9.9 deg, giving 23,520 s
per roll and 47,040 s (∼13.07 h) in total. As with the

additional IRDIS datasets (see details in Appendix A).

for a subsequent paper. For the three imaging epochs,
we consider the nominal pixel scale and true north values

their stability over time (A.-L. Maire et al. 2016, 2021)

spectral differential imaging (SDI; W. Sparks & H. Ford

extend calculation time. For the 2019 and 2020 epochs,

while a smaller annulus (4-FWHM wide around the po-

the final image after subtracting planet b from the data
cube and carrying out a number of tests to assess the
impact of this subtraction on the parameters estimated
for planet d (more details in Sec. 3.1 and Appendix B).

3. RESULTS
3.1. Detections of β Pictoris d
We present the final images from the epochs at which
we detect β Pic d in Figure 1. We detect a clear point
source, the putative β Pic d, South-West of the star in
the VLT/ERIS data with a S/N of 14.9. We detect a
compatible point source in the longest wavelength fil-
ters for each of the two JWST/NIRCam epochs, and for
the three VLT/SPHERE epochs, including detections
in both the K12 and H23 bands.
We do not detect
the source in the VLT/NaCo epochs.
We summarise
all of the detections in Table 1, and non-detections in
Appendix A.
As we progressively recovered the β Pic d candidate
in more datasets, we used Octofitter (W. Thompson
et al. 2023) and orbitize! (S. Blunt et al. 2020, 2024)
to fit preliminary single-planet orbits to the later epochs
and predict the expected position of the candidate in
earlier datasets. For the earliest SPHERE epoch, from
2014, these indicated that the β Pic d candidate would
likely be very close to β Pic b. We therefore first sub-
tracted the much brighter planet b from the 2014 dat-
acube using the position and flux found by the negative
fake companion method (NegFC; A.-M. Lagrange et al.
2010; O. Wertz et al. 2017; T. Stolker et al. 2019). After
removing β Pic b, we processed this cube with PCA-
ADI and identified a point source located in the wings
of the PSF of planet b (∼45 mas apart).
This detec-
tion at S/N∼7.5 is significant (D. Mawet et al. 2014;
M. J. Bonse et al. 2023). It is also robust with respect
to the uncertainties on the parameters of planet b used
for the subtraction, although the latter dominate the
uncertainty budget for the parameters estimated for the
planet d candidate.
We provide additional details in
Appendix B.
For the 2019 and 2020 VLT/SPHERE epochs, we com-
bined SDI and ADI to achieve a deeper contrast. This
was necessary as the β Pic d candidate falls close to
the extreme-AO corrected radius of VLT/SPHERE. At
these separations, fast varying speckles affect the con-
trast achieved with ADI only. We only detect the can-
didate when combining SDI and ADI to remove these
speckles.
At the epochs of available VLT/NaCo observations
(between 2003 and 2015), our preliminary orbit fits pre-
dicted the candidate to be <0.5′′ from the star.
We
therefore did not detect the candidate at these separa-
tions in any of the VLT/NaCo observations because it
was too close to the star or much brighter planet b. How-

separation was sufficient to recover a source with the L′-
band flux of the β Pic d candidate, these observations
remained highly valuable for ruling out the possibility
that the point source was a background source (see Sec-
tion 3.3).

3.4. Orbit fitting
With an 11-year baseline and significant orbital mo-

constraints on its configuration. The detailed descrip-

astrometric constraints, and S. B. Brown-Sevilla et al.
(2023) estimated a mass range of 2–4 MJup from forward
modeling of VLT/SPHERE spectroscopy.
Since 51 Eri b is such a close analog to β Pic d, we
estimated a bolometric correction for β Pic d based on
the colors of 51 Eri b following the method of Section 4.2
of K. Franson et al. (2023). This allows us to extrap-
olate a bolometric luminosity from the available pho-
tometry with a model-independent method. We adopt
a bolometric correction of BC(L′) = 0.06 ± 0.27 mag
from log (Lbol/L⊙) = −5.55 ± 0.10 dex (W. O. Balmer
et al. 2025b) and L′ = 16.20 ± 0.11 mag (A. Rajan
et al. 2017) at a distance of 29.9 pc.
Thus, for
β Pic d, with ML′ = 14.11 ± 0.16 mag, our BC implies
log (Lbol/L⊙) = −5.67 ± 0.13 dex.
This luminosity is
consistent with a range of initial entropies, including
some low enough to correspond to ‘cold start’ formation
scenarios (e.g., M. S. Marley et al. 2007; G.-D. Marleau
& A. Cumming 2014). If the cold-start scenario is in-
deed favored for β Pic d, β Pic would become the first
system known to host planets formed through both hot-
and cold-start pathways, with β Pic b and c representing
the hot start regime. However, to obtain an estimate of
its mass from models, we adopt the conservative limiting
case of ‘hot start’ initial entropy.
Using this bolometric luminosity estimate for β Pic d
with an age range of 23 ± 8 Myr, we applied rejection
sampling following T. J. Dupuy et al. (2023); B. E.
Miles et al. (2023); A. L. Carter et al. (2023), to es-
timate the physical properties of β Pic d.

4. DISCUSSION
4.1. Implications for the β Pic system
β Pic is an extremely well-studied and dynamically ac-
tive system, comprising multiple giant planets, a warped
debris disk, signs of recent giant collisions, and intense
exocometary activity near the star. Our discovery of an
additional, massive planet in the system naturally raises
several dynamical questions.
First, is β Pic d consistent with the disk warp? The
main disk spans roughly 30–200 au (Y. Han et al. 2026),
with the inner ∼80 au tilted by ∼5 deg relative to the
outer disk (C. J. Burrows et al. 1995; D. A. Golimowski
et al. 2006). This warp was ascribed to planets b and c,
whose shared orbital plane is tilted relative to the outer
disk (D. Mouillet et al. 1997; S. Lacour et al. 2021; J. L.
Smallwood 2023; I. Macias et al. 2026). Our discovery
of β Pic d is consistent with this picture; the new planet
shares the orbital plane of b and c, as required by the
warp model. This is further evidence that the inner and
outer regions of β Pic are generally misaligned, perhaps
hinting that some past dynamical event, such as a flyby,
tilted the outer system.
Second, is β Pic d sculpting the disk inner edge? The
other planets are too far inwards, so it was long in-
ferred that another planet lay beyond b and set the
disk edge through planetesimal scattering (e.g. A. Lac-
quement et al. 2025).
Our discovery of β Pic d sup-
ports this picture. The exact location of the disk edge
is difficult to constrain, because the disk is viewed edge-
on to Earth; a non-parametric fit to ALMA disk data
yields a shallow, uncertain radial profile, whose inner
edge could be anywhere from 30 to 50 au (Y. Han et al.
2026). Assuming an inner edge of 40 ± 5 au, we apply
the SculptingPlanet code4 (T. D. Pearce et al. 2022) to
dynamically predict the properties of a planet sculpting
the disk edge, assuming a star mass of 1.83 ± 0.03 M⊙
and age 23 ± 8 Myr. The result is shown in Figure 6.

Third, is β Pic d related to the exocomets?
β Pic
displays significant exocomet activity, with star-grazing
comets transiting every few days (R. Ferlet et al. 1987;
A. Lecavelier des Etangs et al. 2022). This likely requires
planets to drive comets from a debris belt inwards to-
wards the star (e.g. A. J. Mustill et al. 2026). One possi-
bility is that planets b and c excite bodies from a close-in
asteroid belt at ∼1 au (H. Beust et al. 2024), whilst an
alternative is that planets scatter bodies inwards from
the outer belt at ∼40 au. β Pic d could do the latter;
it appears close enough to the outer belt to scatter de-
bris, some of which could be passed inwards via planets
b and c towards the star. If β Pic d is scattering comets
inwards, then the back reaction of comets on the planet
would drive β Pic d outwards into the belt, exposing it
to fresh material and keeping the interaction going (e.g.,
A. Bonsor et al. 2014).
4.2. The β Pic moving group as a laboratory for
exoplanet formation
The BPMG contains 5 imaged exoplanets (β Pic bcd,
51 Eri b, and AF Lep b), as well as several free-floating
planetary mass objects with similar properties to the
imaged exoplanets (e.g. M. C. Liu et al. 2013; Z. Zhang
et al. 2021). Together, these objects form a cohort of ob-
jects with a range of temperatures and masses but sim-
ilar ages, both in disks around stars of varying masses
and in the form of isolated planetary mass objects. This
makes the BPMG a valuable laboratory for studying
different modes of exoplanet formation, as well as the
atmospheres and evolution of these objects (e.g. K. I.
¨Oberg et al. 2011; P. Molli`ere et al. 2022; E. Nasedkin
et al. 2024; J. Wang 2025; J.-B. Ruffio et al. 2026; J. W.
Xuan et al. 2026). However, we note here that as planet
formation may proceed at different rates in different sys-
tems, these planets likely do not share the exact same
age (e.g. J. B. Pollack et al. 1996; R. H. Durisen et al.
2007; T. Currie et al. 2009, 2013).
The colors of β Pic d and 51 Eri b are notably similar.
Comparative spectroscopic studies of these planets and
other BPMG objects will reveal how the composition
and chemistry of their atmospheres differ, and ideally
also the imprint of the formation environment on these
atmospheres. For the exoplanet companions, eventual
dynamical mass measurements for the full sample will
then allow these properties to be traced specifically as a
function of mass, composition, age, and formation envi-
ronment.
4.3. A game of hide-and-seek: the necessity of long
time baselines for exoplanet confirmation
One of the main lessons of this study is that patience,
as well as repeated observations, are necessary to search
for and robustly confirm common proper motion for di-
rectly imaged exoplanet candidates. Due to the edge-
on viewing angle of the β Pic system, all three of the
exoplanets spend significant amounts of time in unde-
tectable positions behind the star or within the radii of
coronagraphic masks. Earlier studies of the β Pic system
missed β Pic d largely because this planet was either at
an undetectable separation from the star or very close to
β Pic b in a given epoch of observation. This was also
the case for AF Lep b; although this system was tar-
geted by several imaging surveys (e.g. J. M. Stone et al.

2018; E. L. Nielsen et al. 2019; R. Launhardt et al. 2020),
the planet’s orbital motion and high orbital inclination
meant that it was not discovered until much later (R. J.
De Rosa et al. 2023; K. Franson et al. 2023; D. Mesa
et al. 2023). Planets with orbital semi-major axes ≤30
au which display significant orbital motion like β Pic d
will routinely require multiple epochs to confirm. In the
era of the Extremely Large Telescopes (ELTs), when di-
rect imaging begins to observe e.g. known radial velocity
exoplanets with orbital semi-major axes < 10 AU, this
problem will persist, as these planets may lie behind the
coronagraph mask for large portions of their orbits.
Orbital motion is not the only concern here, as stars
with significant proper motions of their own can in some
cases mimic a common-proper motion exoplanet com-
panion, at least for a few epochs. This has led to the re-
traction of multiple claims of directly imaged exoplanets,
when, after a sufficiently large time baseline, it has be-
come clear that the exoplanet candidate was in fact just
a background star with significantly different proper mo-
tion than the rest of the background field (E. L. Nielsen
et al. 2017; M. Kenworthy et al. 2025). However, supple-
mental information can also provide compelling evidence
that a candidate companion is a bonafide exoplanet be-
fore common-proper motion confirmation; in the case
of β Pic d, its red colors (compared to the blue colors
expected for background stars and galaxies), orbital mo-
tion along the plane of the known circumstellar disk, and
consistency with the expected dynamical picture of the
disk strongly supported that it was indeed an exoplanet.
4.4. The potential of ELT/METIS, as revealed by
ERIS-NIX
Like JWST, ERIS-NIX opens up a new discovery
space for young directly imaged exoplanets by achiev-
ing deep sensitivity at wider separations. This gain in
sensitivity results from the reduced number of warm op-
tical surfaces, made possible by mounting the instru-
ment at the Cassegrain focus behind the deformable
secondary mirror (R. Davies et al. 2023).
ERIS-NIX
is equipped with two coronagraphs designed for high-
contrast imaging, a vector vortex coronagraph (G. Or-
ban de Xivry et al. 2024) and a vector Apodizing Phase
Plate coronagraph (vAPP; D. S. Doelman et al. 2021;
M. A. Kenworthy et al. 2026), however, the loss of
throughput from coronagraphic optics limits sensitiv-
ity outside the contrast-limited regime. The ERIS-NIX
non-coronagraphic imaging reported here achieved deep
sensitivity, enabling a high-S/N detection of β Pic d in
the L′ band.
This high-S/N L′ detection then facili-
tated our lower S/N detections at shorter wavelengths
and tighter separations. These results highlight the dis-
covery potential of the upcoming Mid-infrared ELT Im-
ager and Spectrograph (METIS; B. Brandl et al. 2021)
instrument for the Extremely Large Telescope (ELT),
which will have both coronagraphic optics and the pos-
sibility to obtain ultra-deep, non-coronagraphic imaging
with a 39-m aperture.

separated giant planet in the β Pictoris system, with
detections in 6 epochs of observations covering a total
baseline of 11 years. Our findings are summarised below:

B. SUBTRACTION OF β PIC B IN THE 2014
VLT/SPHERE DATA
In 2014, β Pic d was very close to β Pic b, which
outshines the faint companion by several orders of mag-
nitudes. To detect and characterize β Pic d, we used
the NegFC technique implemented in VIP (C. A. Gomez
Gonzalez et al. 2017; V. Christiaens et al. 2023b). We
used NegFC twice: first, to model and subtract β Pic b
to enable the detection of β Pic d, and second, to fit the
astrometry and photometry of β Pic d. The analysis
was carried out separately for the K1 and K2 bands.
B.1. Detection of β Pic d in 2014
Unlike explicit forward models (e.g., L. Pueyo 2016),
which estimate the signature of a companion in the PCA
residuals, the NegFC technique inserts a negative fake
companion into the raw frames before PCA. This ap-
proach achieves a better accuracy than forward models
to recover the astrometry and photometry of bright com-
panions (e.g. F. Cantalloube et al. 2024). We refer to the
data after the fake companion is subtracted as the empty
cube. The empty cube is treated as a new dataset; that
is, a new PCA basis is calculated to obtain the residual
image. Using Nelder–Mead minimization, we optimized
the position and flux of the inserted negative companion
by minimizing an objective function defined as the stan-
dard deviation of the residual pixel intensities within a

B.2. Astrometry and photometry of β Pic d
As for the other epochs, we estimated the astrometry
and photometry of β Pic d using NegFC, but this time
on the empty cube after subtracting β Pic b. To propa-
gate the uncertainties associated with the subtraction of
β Pic b into the retrieved parameters of β Pic d, we used
the MCMC implementation of NegFC in VIP to draw
samples from the posterior distribution of the position
and flux of β Pic b. For each posterior sample of the
β Pic b parameters, we generated a new empty cube and
repeated the NegFC inference for β Pic d. We performed
this procedure for 30 randomly selected posterior sam-
ples. For each realization, we considered two aperture
radii for the objective function, 1 FWHM and 2 FWHM,
and six 20-component PCA ranges between 10–30, and
60–80 components. Within each range, the correspond-
ing PCA residuals were median-combined before evalu-
ating the NegFC objective function. These choices re-
flect the facts that the speckle noise and residuals from
β Pic b vary depending on the number of principal com-
ponents, and that either choice of aperture size yielded
plausible residual-minimization solutions. Through this
analysis, we found that the uncertainty associated with
the subtraction of β Pic b dominates the error budget of
the inferred β Pic d parameters. By contrast, injection-
and-recovery tests of individual fake planets with the
inferred β Pic d parameters, but placed at different az-
imuths, yielded negligible additional uncertainties.
The final adopted uncertainties reflect the spread in
the retrieved β Pic d parameters induced by varying the
PCA range, the aperture radius used for the NegFC ob-
jective function, and the posterior sample of the β Pic b
parameters used to construct the empty cube. We es-
timated contrast uncertainties for β Pic d of ∼0.7 mag
and ∼0.6 mag in the K1 and K2 bands, respectively.
Our final astrometric uncertainties reflect the spread of
radial separation and PA values inferred for both K1
and K2 (Table 1).
C. ORBIT ANALYSIS
We performed an analysis of β Pic d’s orbit using our
new relative astrometry as well as data from the litera-
ture. We used orvara (v1.1.2; T. D. Brandt et al. 2021),
which can fit relative astrometry for multiple planets,
host-star RVs, and absolute astrometry from Hipparcos


## Page 19

and Gaia DR3. It accounts for the astrometric offsets of
any planets interior to a given planet and neglects the
influence of outer planets, which has been shown to be
valid for the orbits of β Pic c and β Pic b to ≈20 µas over
20 years (G. M. Brandt et al. 2021). The same work also
showed that the impact of ignoring β Pic c when fitting
β Pic b astrometry led to systematics of ∼10 mas. Given
that β Pic b’s orbit is about 4× larger than β Pic c, its
influence on β Pic d’s astrometry could be even larger,
although the timescale of orbit variations is correspond-
ingly longer, which may reduce its influence.
In the following, we adopted priors that are uniform
in log M⋆and log σjit for the host star and log a, e, Ω,
ω, λ, and sin i, for all planets. To improve convergence,
we used a start file to initialize our chains at values near
the highest-likelihood parameters found in preliminary
exploratory chains, and we imposed loose Gaussian pri-
ors on the masses of planets b and c of 0.009±0.018 M⊙
and 0.008 ± 0.016 M⊙, respectively. Mainly due to the
uncertain nature of the mass of β Pic d, we chose to
neglect its mass in our orbit fits. By construction, it
would not influence orvara’s fit of the inner two planets
at all, and it is expected to be much smaller in mass than
the inner planets, so it should induce a comparatively
negligible acceleration on the host star (∝md/r2
d). We
used 100 walkers, five parallel-tempering temperatures,
106 steps, thinned every 50th step, and we discarded
the first 50% of steps as burn-in. The configuration files
used for our orbit fit are provided in the Zenodo data
package connected to this manuscript.
C.1. Literature data
To perform our multi-planet orbit analysis, we in-
cluded literature measurements of relative astrometry
and RVs, as well as the most recent Hipparcos–Gaia
Catalog of Accelerations (HGCA; T. D. Brandt 2021).
We used the host-star RVs from A.-M. Lagrange et al.
(2019a) cleaned of stellar activity as presented by T.
Vandal et al. (2020). For the innermost planet β Pic c,
only VLTI/GRAVITY has resolved it, and we used all
four reprocessed measurements from S. Lacour et al.
(2021).
For β Pic b, we use the following: all nine
VLT/NACO measurements from 2003–2011 processed
by G. Chauvin et al. (2012); the 2008 M ′-band de-
tection from T. Currie et al. (2011); the 2012 epoch
from M. Bonnefoy et al. (2013); eight Gemini/NICI
and Magellan/MagAO measurements over 2009–2012
from E. L. Nielsen et al. (2014, which excludes one dis-
crepant CH4S measurement from 2012); fourteen Gem-
ini/GPI measurements spanning 2013–2018 from J. J.
Wang et al. (2016) as reported in E. L. Nielsen et al.
(2020, which excludes one 2013 measurement with an
anomalously high PA); all 34 VLT/SPHERE measure-
ments spanning 2014–2020 from A.-M. Lagrange et al.
(2019b) and A. M. Lagrange et al. (2020); and all seven
VLT/GRAVITY measurements from 2018–2021 as re-
processed by S. Lacour et al. (2021). The HGCA does
not contain a significant detection of astrometric accel-
eration (χ2 = 0.935), but it can still be useful in con-
straining the planet masses as the Hipparcos and Gaia
proper motion anomaly nondetections have uncertain-
ties of about 0.4 mas yr−2 and 0.3 mas yr−2, respectively,
so we include it.
C.2. Results
The best-fit orbit, i.e., the parameters that minimize
χ2, resulted in χ2 = 161 for the relative astrometry
alone, which is reasonable given that we fit 84 measure-
ments with ≈21 free parameters (approximate because
some are also constrained by RVs) and thereby 147 de-
grees of freedom.
The subset of our new relative as-
trometry for planet d independently has χ2 = 3.2 for 6
degrees of freedom, implying that the astrometric errors
from our analysis of VLT/ERIS, VLT/SPHERE, and
JWST/NIRCam data are reasonable.
We report the resulting orbital parameters of all three
planets in Table 4. The marginalized posteriors (Fig-
ure 8) for many of the orbital parameters are quite
narrow and thus not influenced by our priors.
These
include all of the parameters for planet b and all but
the mean longitude at the reference epoch for planet c
(λc = 288
+23
−13 deg). While uniform priors on angles like λ
are generally well-justified, the same is not true for as-
trophysical parameters like eccentricity and semimajor
axis.
The parameters that we determine to be prior-
independent for β Pic d are the inclination id and longi-
tude of ascending node Ωd. In Table 5, we report results
for planet d from the subset of orbital solutions that do
not cross the orbit of planet b.
In addition to our baseline prior, we also experimented
with allowing planet d to have some mass in our orbit
fits. However, this seemed to result in attempts to ‘over-
fit’ the residuals of the astrometric and radial accelera-
tions (after removing b and c) by exploring unrealisti-
cally massive and eccentric orbits for planet d. Given
that we expect planet d to have a negligible impact on
the host star acceleration, these experiments justify our
choice of a massless prior on planet d (implemented as
10−6 ± 10−9 M⊙in orvara).
