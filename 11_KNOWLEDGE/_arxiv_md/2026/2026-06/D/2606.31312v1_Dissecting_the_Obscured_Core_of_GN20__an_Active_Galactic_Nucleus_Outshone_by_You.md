ABSTRACT
Aims. We investigate the relative contributions of star formation and AGN activity to the total energy budget of GN20, one of the
most luminous dusty star-forming galaxies known at z > 4, through spatially resolved spectral energy distribution decomposition.
Methods. We perform Bayesian SED fitting with CIGALE on two spatially distinct apertures: the nuclear core (r = 0.14′′, ∼1 kpc
physical) and the full galaxy (r = 1.4′′, 9.9 kpc), combining JWST/NIRCam and MIRI broadband imaging, JWST/NIRSpec PRISM
IFU pseudo-continuum photometry spanning 42 wavelength bins across rest-frame 0.12–1.05 µm, and archival HST and millimeter
interferometry data from NOEMA and PdBI.
Results. The integrated SED is dominated by stellar-heated dust, with only a marginal AGN contribution at galaxy-wide scales
(f int
AGN = 0.09 ± 0.02). The nuclear core, however, requires a significant AGN component ( fAGN = 0.34 ± 0.05 ) to account for a
mid-infrared excess at rest-frame ∼2.5–3.6 µm characteristic of AGN-heated torus dust. The AGN accounts for ∼34% of the nuclear
infrared luminosity but only ∼9% of the total integrated LIR, explaining its weak signature in integrated diagnostics and its consistency
with existing upper limits from Spitzer spectroscopy. The inferred black hole mass places GN20 within the local MBH–Mbulge relation
at the Eddington limit, and in the overmassive regime at sub-Eddington accretion rates, suggesting early and rapid black hole assembly
concurrent with the dominant starburst. GN20 exemplifies a class of systems where nuclear-scale SED decomposition, enabled by the
angular resolution and infrared sensitivity of JWST, is the only means to uncover a buried AGN overwhelmed by galaxy-wide star
formation.
1. Introduction
Dusty star-forming galaxies (DSFGs) represent one of the most
important galaxy populations for understanding cosmic mass as-
sembly and the formation of the most massive systems in the
Universe (Chapman et al. 2005; Gruppioni et al. 2013; Casey
et al. 2014). They dominated the dust-obscured cosmic star for-
mation, accounting for more than half of the total star formation
rate (SFR) density at z ∼2–4, (Pérez-González et al. 2005; Mag-
nelli et al. 2013; Zavala et al. 2021), despite representing only a
small fraction of the galaxy population (with space densities of
only ∼10−4 Mpc−3, Dudzeviˇci¯ut˙e et al. 2020). First discovered
in submillimeter surveys (Smail et al. 1997; Hughes et al. 1998;
Blain et al. 1999), these systems are characterized by their ex-
treme dust content, which reprocesses ultraviolet (UV) and op-
tical emission from young stars into the far-infrared (FIR) and
submillimeter, rendering them faint or invisible at rest-frame
UV and optical wavelengths (Casey et al. 2014; Gruppioni et al.
2020; Hamed et al. 2021; Barrufet et al. 2023; Pérez-González
et al. 2023).
The peak activity of DSFGs at z ∼2–3 coincides with the
peak epoch of supermassive black hole accretion (Richards et al.
2006; Hopkins et al. 2008; Madau & Dickinson 2014; Aird et al.
2015), suggesting a fundamental connection between extreme
star formation and black hole growth during this critical phase of
massive galaxy evolution (Alexander et al. 2008; Kormendy &
Ho 2013; Hickox et al. 2014). Understanding the role of AGN in
DSFGs is essential for constraining their evolutionary pathways
and the mechanisms driving supermassive black hole growth in
extreme environments (Casey et al. 2014). However, identify-
ing and characterizing AGN in these heavily obscured systems
presents significant observational challenges. These challenges
include X-ray absorption by high column densities, dilution of
AGN signatures by dominant host star formation, and spatial
mixing of nuclear and extended emission in integrated diagnos-
tics.
X-ray observations have revealed AGN activity in a sig-
nificant fraction of DSFGs, though reported AGN fractions
vary considerably depending on the sample, observation depth,
and classification criteria (Alexander et al. 2005; Pope et al.

2006; Laird et al. 2010; Wang et al. 2013; Franco et al. 2018;

reflects observational challenges inherent to studying heavily
obscured systems: X-ray emission can be absorbed by high
column densities (Alexander et al. 2005; Daddi et al. 2007),
requiring multi-wavelength approaches including mid infrared
coverage to identify AGN missed in X-ray surveys (Mateos
et al. 2012; Lacy et al. 2004; Stern et al. 2005; Alonso-Herrero
et al. 2006; Menéndez-Delmestre et al. 2009; Donley et al.
2012). Many DSFGs are expected to harbor Compton-thick
AGN that remain undetected even in deep X-ray observations
(Treister et al. 2009, 2010; Luo et al. 2017; D’Amato et al. 2020).
While the combination of X-ray and infrared diagnostics
described above has been successful in identifying AGN
candidates, these approaches rely on integrated photometry and
therefore face limitations in disentangling nuclear from extended
emission in galaxies (D’Agostino et al. 2019; Barquín-González
et al. 2024). In spatially extended systems, AGN signatures
can be significantly diluted by host galaxy star formation
in integrated photometry, which leads to underestimation of
AGN contributions (Treister et al. 2012). This spatial mixing
particularly affects optical emission line diagnostics (Baldwin
et al. 1981; Veilleux & Osterbrock 1987; Kewley et al. 2001), as
even moderately luminous AGN can be obscured in integrated
line ratios when the host galaxy harbors substantial distributed
star formation (Kauffmann et al. 2003). Spatially resolved
observations are therefore essential to isolate nuclear emission
from surrounding star-forming regions and properly characterize
the relative contributions of AGN and star formation across the
galaxy (Cresci et al. 2015; Venturi et al. 2018; Backhaus et al.
2023).
The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has transformed
the study of high-redshift dusty galaxies, providing simultane-
ous access to rest-frame optical emission lines redshifted into
the near-infrared (NIR) and to the rest-frame NIR and mid-
infrared (MIR) continuum (Rieke et al. 2023; Bohn et al. 2023;
Böker et al. 2022; Arribas et al. 2024; González-Martín et al.
2025). NIRSpec spatially resolved spectroscopy combined with
NIRCam and MIRI imaging now constrains stellar populations,
ionized gas, and dust-obscured activity on sub-kpc scales at red-
shifts beyond the reach of previous facilities (Álvarez-Márquez
et al. 2023; Colina et al. 2023; Marshall et al. 2023; Übler et al.
2023; Bik et al. 2024; Crespo Gómez et al. 2024), allowing star
formation and AGN activity to be disentangled within individual
galaxies.
Pre-launch, MIRI was expected to be the instrument best
placed to separate obscured nuclear activity from intense star
formation in IR-luminous galaxies, accessing MIR diagnostics
out to z ∼4 that were beyond Spitzer for all but the brightest
sources (Kirkpatrick et al. 2017). JWST/MIRI surveys have
confirmed this expectation, uncovering large populations of
obscured AGN out to z ∼5, including composite SF–AGN
sources whose MIR SEDs cannot be reproduced by star for-
mation alone, with the obscured fraction rising progressively
with redshift up to z ∼4 (Yang et al. 2023; Lyu et al. 2024).
Obscured accretion is therefore common among IR-luminous
galaxies both locally (e.g., Alonso-Herrero et al. 2012) and
at high redshift, but routinely escapes detection in integrated
photometry, where the AGN signal is diluted by host emission.
JWST/MIRI has also revealed the population of little red dots
(LRDs): compact, high-z sources with characteristic v-shaped
SEDs (blue UV, red rest optical) often showing broad Balmer
emission (Labbé et al. 2023; Matthee et al. 2024; Greene et al.
2024; Pérez-González et al. 2024). LRDs, and high-z JWST
AGN more broadly, frequently host black holes well above the
local MBH–M⋆relation, populating the overmassive regime now
widely reported at z > 4 (Pacucci et al. 2023; Harikane et al.
2023; Maiolino et al. 2024).
The growing evidence for obscured, often overmassive
black holes in IR-luminous and compact high-z systems makes
it increasingly relevant to ask whether such an AGN com-
ponent is present, and energetically important, in individual
DSFGs. AGN feedback is one of the proposed mechanisms for
quenching star formation in massive high-z DSFGs, in scenarios
that link them to the compact quiescent galaxies observed at
z ∼2 (Hopkins et al. 2008; Toft et al. 2014). Constraining the
AGN content of DSFGs is a necessary step in testing this picture.
We apply JWST’s spatially resolved spectroscopic and
imaging capabilities to one of the most luminous DSFGs known
at z > 4, GN20 (z = 4.055, Pope et al. 2005; Daddi et al. 2009),
located in the GOODS-North field (Giavalisco et al. 2004).
This DSFG has been the subject of extensive multiwavelength
observations. Initial submillimeter surveys established GN20 as
an exceptionally bright source, with extreme infrared luminosity
and a massive dust reservoir (LIR > 1013 L⊙; Hodge et al. 2013;
Tan et al. 2013, 2014; Hodge et al. 2015). High-resolution
NOEMA observations revealed complex extended morphology
across the molecular gas and dust components (Hodge et al.
2013; Boogaard et al. 2026b).
Recent JWST/MIRI imaging revealed a compact stellar
nucleus embedded within an extended stellar envelope, with
dust-obscured star formation (Colina et al. 2023; Bik et al.
2024; Crespo Gómez et al. 2024). High-resolution (R ∼2700)
NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy detected broad Hα emission in the
nuclear region, which can be interpreted either as a broad-line
region of an accreting black hole or as an AGN-driven outflow
(Übler et al. 2024). Furthermore, Boogaard et al. (2026a)
identified a stellar bar-like structure in GN20, which provides
a plausible mechanism for funneling gas toward the nucleus
and fueling both the central starburst and any AGN activity.
SED decomposition, including JWST/MIRI data probing the
rest-frame near and MIR, offers an independent and comple-
mentary diagnostic to confirm the presence of AGN-heated dust
and quantify its energetic contribution, irrespective of the origin
of the broad emission line component. However, the energy
contribution of AGN relative to star formation remain poorly
constrained.
In this work, we combine GA-NIFS (ga-nifs.github.io)
low-resolution NIRSpec PRISM (R ∼100) IFU observations,
which provide simultaneous coverage of the rest-frame optical
continuum and emission lines across the full 0.6–5.3 µm
range, with NIRCam and MIRI imaging and ancillary data to
decompose the SED of GN20 into AGN and star-formation
components in a nuclear and an integrated aperture, and to
quantify the energetic contribution of the AGN relative to the
host starburst.

2 describes the JWST observations and data reduction proce-
dures, along with the ancillary data. Section 3 presents our SED
technique. We present our results and discuss their implications

2. Data
2.1. Broadband Imaging
The multi-wavelength imaging of GN20 used in this work com-
bines archival HST data with JWST observations and ground-
based millimeter interferometry, which covers rest-frame UV
through millimeter wavelengths.
2.1.1. JWST/MIRI and NIRCam Imaging
GN20 was observed with JWST/MIRI in four filters: F560W,
F770W (Colina et al. 2023), F1280W, and F1800W (Crespo
Gómez et al. 2024) (program 1264). Observations in F560W,
F770W, and F1280W used FASTR1 readout mode with a five-
dither medium-size cycling pattern for 1498 s total integration.
F1800W was obtained simultaneously during MRS background
acquisitions with 1911 s integration using a two-point dither pat-
tern. MIRI imaging data were reduced using the JWST Science
Calibration pipeline version 1.12.0 with CRDS context 1140, in-
cluding additional corrections for striping and background gra-
dients, described in Pérez-González et al. (2024) and Östlin et al.
(2025). We refer the reader to Crespo Gómez et al. (2024) for full
details of the reduction procedure for the specific GN20 dataset.
Final mosaics have 0.06′′/pixel scale. At z = 4.055, these filters
trace rest-frame ∼1-3.8µm, sampling the stellar continuum and
warm dust emission from AGN-heated torus dust.
The JWST/NIRCam imaging was obtained in six filters span-
ning rest-frame ∼0.2-1 µm: F115W, F150W, F200W, F277W,
F356W, and F444W (Boogaard et al. 2026b). These observa-
tions were taken as part of program 1264 (PI: Luis Colina).
Imaging in F200W and F356W used 1825 s. total integration
distributed over five dithers, while F115W, F150W, F277W, and
F444W used 2555 s. over seven dithers. All observations used
the SHALLOW4 readout mode with seven groups per integra-
tion. NIRCam imaging data were reduced using a custom pro-
cedure built on the JWST Science Calibration pipeline version
1.12.3 with CRDS context 1145, including the removal of snow-
balls and wisps and a background homogenization. We refer the
reader to Boogaard et al. (2026a,b) for full details of the reduc-
tion procedure.
2.1.2. Ancillary Data
We utilize archival HST/ACS imaging in F435W, F606W,
F775W, and F814W, with pixel scale 0.06 ′′ (Hodge et al. 2015).
We also included archival NOEMA data at 1.1 mm with synthe-
sized beam 0.26′′ × 0.20′′ (Boogaard et al. 2026b), along with
the Plateau de Bure Interferometer (PdBI) observation at 880µm
(340 GHz) with synthesized beam 0.30′′ × 0.20′′ at position an-
gle 37◦(Carilli et al. 2010; Hodge et al. 2015). Both millime-
ter maps were resampled onto the common 0.06′′ pixel grid.
Each elliptical beam was approximated by a circular Gaussian
of FWHM equal to the geometric mean of its major and minor
axes, and matched to the F770W PSF by convolution with a cir-
cular Gaussian kernel. These millimeter detections constrain the
Rayleigh-Jeans tail of the dust SED at rest-frame ∼180–220 µm,
complementing the shorter-wavelength FIR coverage described
below.
We further include archival MIR and FIR photometry of
GN20 from Spitzer/MIPS at 24 µm and Herschel/PACS at 100
and 160 µm and SPIRE at 250, 350, and 500 µm (Magnelli et al.
2013; Tan et al. 2014; Liu et al. 2018; Crespo Gómez et al. 2024).
These data sample the rest-frame MIR and the peak of the dust
thermal emission of GN20. The angular resolution of these data
(PACS FWHM ∼7′′, SPIRE FWHM ∼18–36′′) is too coarse for
nuclear photometric extraction. We therefore use these fluxes as
detections in the integrated SED fit (r = 1.4′′), and as upper lim-
its in the nuclear SED fit (r = 0.14′′), under the conservative
assumption that the nuclear region contributes at most the total
galaxy-integrated flux at these wavelengths.
2.2. NIRSpec IFU Data
GN20 was observed with JWST/NIRSpec in integral field spec-
troscopy mode as part of program 1264. Observations were
conducted using PRISM/CLEAR with the IRS2RAPID readout
mode, providing continuous wavelength coverage from 0.6 to
5.3 µm with spectral resolution R ∼30–300 (Jakobsen et al.
2022). The observations used a four-point medium cycling dither
pattern with one hour total integration time. The 3′′×3′′ field of
view with 0.1′′spatial sampling fully encompasses GN20’s spa-
tial extent (Figure 1).

2.3. Data Preparation for SED Fitting
To enable spatially resolved SED fitting of GN20’s distinct com-
ponents, all imaging and spectroscopic data were processed to a
common spatial resolution and astrometric frame. We verified
the astrometry by comparing the position of GN20’s compact
nuclear emission across all datasets. The NIRCam and MIRI
imaging showed consistent centering on the nuclear source.
The NIRSpec IFU data cube exhibited a systematic offset of
∼-0.21′′ in RA and ∼0.09′′ in Dec relative to the imaging. We
aligned the NIRSpec data cube to the NIRCam F444W imaging
by matching the continuum emission peak of the core region at
observed ∼4.44 µm. After applying this correction, the NIRSpec
continuum emission at all other wavelengths was verified to be
spatially consistent with the corresponding NIRCam bands. All
data were resampled to a uniform pixel scale of 0.06′′/pixel.
We PSF-matched all maps to the JWST/MIRI F770W reso-
lution (FWHM = 0.28′′, measured from stars in the field), which
corresponds to a physical scale of 1.9 kpc at z = 4.055 and en-
ables the separation of GN20’s distinct spatial components. Pho-
tometry was extracted in two apertures: a nuclear core aperture
with r=0.14′′ (∼1 kpc), corresponding to the F770W PSF half-
width at half-maximum (HWHM = FWHM/2), and centered on
the brightest F770W pixel (Colina et al. 2023), and an integrated
galaxy aperture with r = 1.4′′(9.9 kpc) as in Crespo Gómez et al.
(2024). For the PSF-matched bands (HST, NIRCam, NIRSpec,
MIRI F560W, and F770W), core fluxes were extracted using
the r = 0.14′′aperture. MIRI F1280W and F1800W were ex-

the other MIRI bands, resulting in a less well-characterized
PSF and higher photometric uncertainty at this wavelength. We
verify in Section 4 that treating the F1280W and F1800W fluxes
as upper limits does not significantly alter the derived AGN

the F770W resolution using the wavelength-dependent PSF
model from D’Eugenio et al. (2024). They characterized the
NIRSpec/IFS PRISM PSF using three independent methods:
direct stellar observations, serendipitous stars in the IFS field of

from the NIRSpec cube with observed NIRCam imaging. We
adopted their empirical wavelength-dependent PSF model,
computed the effective circular FWHM at each wavelength, and
convolved each spectral slice with a Gaussian kernel to match
the F770W PSF. To avoid noise correlation introduced by PSF

empty aperture method: 2000 apertures of the same radius are
placed at random source-free positions on the original image,

aperture error directly, naturally accounting for pixel-to-pixel
correlations introduced by drizzle resampling. For the integrated

3. Spectral energy distribution modeling
We perform spectral energy distribution fitting using CIGALE
(Boquien et al. 2019), a Bayesian SED fitting code that models
galaxy emission from the FUV to the FIR. CIGALE constructs
model SEDs by combining stellar population synthesis, nebu-
lar emission, dust attenuation, and dust re-emission, based on a
defined grid of physical parameters. CIGALE constructs prob-
ability distribution functions for the physical parameters using
a Bayesian approach (Burgarella et al. 2005; Noll et al. 2009;
Boquien et al. 2019).
In our fitting strategy, we define the separation between
young and old stellar populations at 10 Myr, corresponding to
the timescale over which massive stars produce ionizing radia-
tion and drive nebular emission.
Nebular continuum and line emission are computed using
CLOUDY photoionization models (Ferland et al. 2017) that
are integrated into CIGALE. We explore ionization parameters
log(U) between −3 and −1, and set the electron density to
ne = 100 cm−3. We assume zero Lyman continuum escape
fraction (fesc = 0) given GN20’s high dust content, and set the
line width to 300 km s−1.
We fit GN20’s integrated photometry over a 1.4′′ radius
aperture (9.9 kpc at z = 4.055), encompassing the full spatial ex-
tent of the galaxy. This aperture was also used in Crespo Gómez
et al. (2024). Additionally, we improve that SED fitting analysis
by including the NIRSpec/IFS pseudo-continuum photometry
extracted from the line-excluded spectral cube (Section 2.3),
providing densely sampled rest-frame optical and NIR con-
straints on the stellar continuum shape. We adopt the galaxy’s
spectroscopic redshift z = 4.055 (Carilli et al. 2011) and apply a
15% systematic uncertainty added in quadrature to all photomet-
ric uncertainties to account for potential calibration systematics
and aperture corrections. To investigate the nuclear starburst
separately, we also perform SED fitting on a compact nuclear
aperture with radius equal to the F770W PSF HWHM (0.14′′
or 1 kpc), centered on the brightest F770W pixel corresponding
to the nuclear center (Colina et al. 2023). This aperture isolates
the heavily obscured nuclear starburst from the outer disk and
star-forming clumps (Bik et al. 2024). The treatment of the FIR
photometry differs between the two apertures. In the integrated

3.1. Star Formation History Constraints
The rotationally-dominated kinematics of GN20 (vrot/σ0 ∼5.3;
Übler et al. 2024), together with the smooth stellar disk
morphology revealed by MIRI imaging (Colina et al. 2023;
Crespo Gómez et al. 2024), favor sustained star formation
over a recent merger-driven starburst. We therefore model the
SFH using a delayed exponential as the primary component,
with SFR(t) ∝t exp(−t/τmain), exploring τmain = 0.1–2 Gyr to
allow for a range of formation histories from an initial burst
to relatively constant star formation over the last Gyr. To test
whether a recent starburst component is required by the data,
we include an optional recent burst with age tburst = 1–10 Myr
and mass fraction fburst = 0.0–0.99. The inclusion of fburst = 0.0
as a parameter allows the SED fitting to determine whether
sustained star formation alone can reproduce the observations,
or whether an additional recent burst component improves
the fit. We verified that even when allowing burst fractions up
to 0.99, the best-fit value remains low (fburst ∼0.05), which
confirms that the data favor a dominant mature stellar population
independently of the prior range adopted.
3.2. Dust Attenuation and Emission Constraints
GN20 exhibits extreme dust obscuration. Bik et al. (2024)
derived AV = 14.8 ± 0.3 mag from the ratio of dust-corrected
Paα-based to integrated infrared-based SFR, indicating that
the observed Paα emission reveals only 11.0 ± 0.5% of the
total SFR. This extreme extinction is primarily associated with
GN20’s heavily obscured nuclear starburst (Hodge et al. 2015;
Colina et al. 2023; Übler et al. 2024; Boogaard et al. 2026a).
We model dust attenuation of the stellar continuum using the
modified Calzetti et al. (2000) attenuation law as described by
Noll et al. (2009), following the approach of Crespo Gómez et al.
(2024). We explore color excess values E(B−V)young = 0–3 mag
for the young stellar population. This range is appropriate for
fitting the flux-weighted average continuum extinction traced by
our broadband photometry and pseudo-continuum of NIRSpec,
which samples stellar populations distributed across both the
nuclear region and the more diffuse outer disk. Following Noll
et al. (2009) prescription, we adopt a power-law modification to
the attenuation curve with slope δ = −0.4 to +0.6 to allow for
variations in the wavelength dependence of extinction, and set
the UV bump amplitude to zero.
We note that star-forming regions may be more deeply em-
bedded in dust than the average stellar population, as indicated
by the higher extinction derived specifically from Paα emission
(AV = 14.8 mag; Bik et al. 2024). This differential attenuation
between nebular and continuum emission is commonly observed
in dusty starbursts, where star-forming regions are concentrated
in the most obscured nuclear environments while older stellar
populations contribute to the less attenuated continuum light

3.3. AGN Contribution
The presence of a compact and heavily obscured nuclear com-
ponent in GN20’s core (Colina et al. 2023; Bik et al. 2024; Übler
et al. 2024), with an estimated AGN contribution of ≲15-20%
to the total infrared luminosity (Riechers et al. 2014), suggest a
potential buried AGN contribution. To test this possibility, we
perform additional SED fitting runs including AGN emission
for both the integrated galaxy and the nuclear aperture.
We model the AGN torus emission using the Fritz et al.
(2006) models, which parameterize the dusty torus geometry
and radiative transfer. We adopt a torus outer-to-inner radius
ratio rratio = 60, angular distribution parameter β = −0.5, and
radial density gradient γ = 4.0 following typical values for
obscured AGN. We explore optical depths at 9.7 µm spanning
the full Fritz et al. (2006) grid range to allow for moderately
to heavily obscured tori. We explore viewing angles ψ ranging
from 0.001◦, to 89.990◦between edge-on and face-on AGN. For
the polar dust component, we adopt an SMC-like extinction law
with E(B −V)polar = 0.03 mag. For the integrated aperture fit,
we fix the AGN torus parameters (viewing angle, optical depth,
and torus geometry) to the best-fit values derived from the
nuclear core fit, allowing only the AGN fraction fAGN to vary.
This ensures physical consistency between the two apertures, as
the torus properties are intrinsic to the central engine and should
not change with the extraction aperture.
The Fritz et al. (2006) AGN model includes continuum
emission from the dusty torus and accretion disk, but does not
model emission lines from the AGN. In contrast, CIGALE’s
nebular module assumes all emission lines arise from star
formation. To account for this model limitation and the potential
AGN contamination of our observed emission lines, we inflated
the uncertainties on all optical emission line fluxes by adding a
50% systematic uncertainty in quadrature. This systematic also
accounts for uncertainties in extracting line fluxes from blended
complexes at R ∼100 spectral resolution, where individual
components within each blend cannot be resolved. The inflated
uncertainties reduce the weight of emission lines in the fit,
which in turn allows the multiwavelength continuum SED to
provide the primary constraints on physical parameters. To
verify that emission line constraints do not bias the derived
physical parameters, we performed an additional fit excluding
all emission line fluxes. The resulting best-fit parameters are
consistent within the uncertainties with those obtained with the
inflated-error approach, confirming that the continuum SED
provides the primary constraints on the AGN fraction and stellar
population properties.
We explore AGN fractions, defined as the fractional con-
tribution of the AGN to the total IR luminosity, with a range
of fAGN = 0.0–0.9. The inclusion of fAGN = 0.0 allows direct
comparison between pure starburst and starburst+AGN models.
We compare the reduced χ2 values and examine whether AGN
emission significantly improves the fit quality, particularly in
the MIR regime where AGN-heated dust emission is most
prominent. For the nuclear aperture specifically, we also exam-
ine whether a higher AGN fraction is required compared to the
integrated galaxy.
We note that our photometric coverage in the nuclear core fit
extends to rest-frame ∼3.6 µm at the level of detections, with the
longer-wavelength FIR data entering only as upper limits. Nev-
ertheless, recent JWST studies have demonstrated robust AGN
identification through SED fitting with comparable or shorter
rest-frame wavelength baselines: Delvecchio et al. (2025) identi-
fied AGN-heated dust at rest-frame 1-3 µm in stacked Little Red
Dots at z ∼6 using CIGALE, while Ronayne et al. (2025) found
AGN signatures in individual sources at z ∼5–9 with MIRI data
probing similar rest-frame wavelengths. At rest-frame ≳2 µm,
AGN-heated dust produces excess emission over the stellar con-
tinuum that can be identified through MIR color selections (Lacy
et al. 2004; Stern et al. 2005) and SED fitting (Leja et al. 2018;
Ciesla et al. 2015). In our case, the dense sampling (42 NIR-
Spec pseudo-continuum bins combined with 10 broadband fil-
ters) provides strong constraints on the stellar continuum shape,
making any MIR excess at F1280W and F1800W highly signifi-
cant. The independent detection of broad Hα emission by Übler
et al. (2024) further corroborates the AGN interpretation.

∼9% AGN contribution in the integrated aperture.

resulting AGN fraction (fAGN = 0.29 ± 0.10) is consistent with

wavelength MIRI photometry.

2014, log(M⋆/M⊙) = 10.93 ± 0.30 and 11.04 ± 0.20, respec-
tively). We find A(V) = 2.60 ± 0.21 mag, higher than the
1.50 ± 0.01 mag reported by Crespo Gómez et al. (2024) with
CIGALE without NIRSpec constraints. The denser sampling of
the rest-frame optical and NIR continuum provided by the 42
NIRSpec IFU pseudo-continuum points tightens the constraint
on A(V), which directly affects the stellar mass estimate. Our
results are also in broad agreement with the earlier estimate of
log(M⋆/M⊙) = 11.36 ± 0.20 by Daddi et al. (2009) based on
eight photometric bands spanning HST/ACS to Spitzer/IRAC.
Our SED model predicts a continuum flux density of
13.5 ± 2.0 mJy at rest-frame 205 µm (observed ∼1.04 mm),
in agreement within ∼1σ with the 16.2 ± 2.5 mJy measured
by Kolupuri et al. (2025) from [NII] 205 µm continuum
observations with the IRAM interferometer using a larger
aperture (beam of 1.78′′×1.65′′). We get [Nii] 205 µm line flux
of (2.08 ± 0.94) × 10−20 W m−2, in excellent agreement with
the (2.22 ± 0.68) × 10−20 W m−2 derived from the line flux of
2.3 ± 0.7 Jy km s−1 measured by Kolupuri et al. (2025).
Crespo Gómez et al. (2024) performed integrated SED
fitting of GN20 including Spitzer/MIPS and Herschel/PACS and
SPIRE photometry spanning rest-frame ∼5–120 µm, with no
AGN model at galaxy-wide scales. The denser sampling of the
rest-frame optical and NIR continuum from our NIRSpec data,
combined with the same FIR constraints (used as detections
at integrated scales and as upper limits at the nuclear scale,
Section 2.1), enables the marginal recovery of an otherwise
heavily diluted AGN signal at the few-percent level.
4.4. GN20 in the context of obscured AGN at high redshift
GN20 adds to the growing population of obscured AGN in
massive, IR-luminous galaxies whose nuclear AGN signatures
are heavily diluted in galaxy-integrated diagnostics. Pre-JWST
studies of IR-bright galaxies at z < 3 found that more than 40%
of IR-selected samples host an AGN, with composite systems
where both star formation and AGN contribute to the MIR
comprising ∼30% of the population (Kirkpatrick et al. 2015).
Similarly, Riechers et al. (2014) found AGN contributions of
∼11–36% to LIR in z ∼2 SMGs with comparable mid-infrared
AGN fractions, broadly consistent with GN20’s integrated
value.
JWST/MIRI has now extended this census to higher redshifts
and lower luminosities. Using the eight-band SMILES survey,
Lyu et al. (2024) identified a large population of AGN candi-
dates in the GOODS-S/HUDF field, with the obscured AGN
fraction increasing progressively from z ∼0 to z ∼4 (Lyu et al.
2024; Alberts et al. 2024). These results indicate that a fraction
of accreting supermassive black holes coexist with intense
starbursts across cosmic time, with their signatures diluted in
galaxy-wide photometry.

The GN20 fit illustrates how integrated photometry can mask
a nuclear AGN. As shown in Section 4.3, our integrated r = 1.4′′
fit converges to f int

contribution recovered at the nuclear scale. The MIR excess driv-

in the integrated aperture by the surrounding star-forming and
stellar mass regions, which dominate the rest-frame NIR to FIR
emission of the system. Spatially resolved SED decomposition
is therefore necessary to identify buried AGN in DSFGs.
The AGN bolometric luminosity of LAGN ≃1.7×1045 erg s−1
would be detected in galaxies with typical SFR at similar red-
shifts (Madau & Dickinson 2014). The AGN is outshone
in GN20 because the host starburst (LIR
∼1.4 × 1013 L⊙)
overwhelms the nuclear emission. This suggests that AGN of
comparable luminosity hosted by DSFGs may be systematically
missed in precisely the systems where black hole growth is
more active.

In both GN20’s nuclear core and GNz7q, the AGN contributes
significantly to the MIR, but in GN20 this excess is detectable
only in the spatially resolved core aperture and is diluted be-

a MIR excess comparable in shape to GN20’s nuclear SED, de-
spite having been modeled with pure-starburst components only
(Álvarez-Márquez et al. 2023). An AGN-inclusive fit was not
performed for SPT0311-58, and the question of a buried AGN in
that system remains open.
GN20 illustrates a broader methodological point. Obscured
AGN fractions inferred from JWST surveys at z ≳2 (Lyu et al.
2024; Alberts et al. 2024) that rely on galaxy-integrated SED
fitting may be conservative, since a buried AGN contribution
can be partially accommodated by warm-dust starburst compo-
nents when AGN models are not included. The most luminous,
dust-rich, and morphologically complex high-redshift systems,
in which rapidly accreting central black holes might be present,
are those for which integrated SED diagnostics are least sensi-
tive to a nuclear AGN. Spatially-resolved SED decomposition
with AGN model components, applied here to the nuclear core
of GN20, is required to identify and characterize this hidden pop-
ulation.
5. Conclusions
We have presented a spatially resolved SED decomposition
of the z = 4.055 dusty star-forming galaxy GN20, combining
JWST/NIRCam, MIRI, and NIRSpec PRISM IFU observations
with archival HST and millimeter interferometry data. Using
CIGALE Bayesian SED fitting across two apertures, the nuclear
core (r = 0.14′′, ∼1 kpc) and the full galaxy (r = 1.4′′, 9.9 kpc),
we characterize the relative contributions of star formation and
AGN activity to the total energy budget of GN20.
