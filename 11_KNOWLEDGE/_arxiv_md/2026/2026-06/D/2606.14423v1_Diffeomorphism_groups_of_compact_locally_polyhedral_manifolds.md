Diffeomorphism groups of compact locally
polyhedral manifolds
Johanna Jakob
Abstract
A locally polyhedral manifold M is a manifold which is locally diffeo-
morph to open subsets of convex polytopes. Manifolds with corners are a
special case of locally polyhedral manifolds. We turn the group Diff∞(M)
of all smooth diffeomorphisms f : M →M for compact smooth M into
a regular Lie group.
1
Introduction and statement of results
Manifolds of mappings are an essential concept in global analysis (cf. [6, 15]).
It is well-known that the set Ck(M, N) of all Ck-maps f : M →N can be
turned into a smooth manifold for all k ∈N0 ∪{∞} and σ-compact, finite-
dimensional smooth manifolds M and N, where M (but not N) may have a
boundary or corners (cf. [6, 16, 15, 3, 18, 9]). This is of particular interest for
compact M without boundary, because then the group Diff∞(M) of all C∞-
diffeomorphisms f : M →M is an open subset of C∞(M, M) and a regular
Lie group (see [15]). Michor [16] was the first to discuss a smooth Lie group
structure on Diff∞(M) for a smooth manifold M with corners, but Glöckner [7]
recently noticed a flaw in his construction. However, Diff∞(M) can be turned
into a regular Lie group if M is a compact smooth manifold with boundary
(see [12]) or a convex polytope (see [7]), but for a general smooth manifold M
with corners, there is no valid Lie group structure on Diff∞(M) available so
far. This article provides a new, correct construction of a Lie group structure
on Diff∞(M) for compact M and takes the step from manifolds with corners
to the more natural, broader class of locally polyhedral manifolds.
1.1 Definition. Let n ∈N0 and E be an n-dimensional real vector space.
Following [7], a locally polyhedral C∞-manifold of dimension n is a Hausdorff
topological space M, together with a set A of homeomorphisms φ: Uφ →Vφ
from open subsets Uφ ⊆M onto open subsets of a convex polytope Pφ ⊆E
with non-empty interior, such that A is a polyhedral smooth atlas in the sense
that S
φ∈A Uφ = M and φ ◦ψ−1 is C∞for all φ, ψ ∈A, and A is maximal
among such atlases. Notably, M is a C∞-manifold with rough boundary in the
sense of [9].
Of course, each open subset V ⊆P of a convex polytope P ⊆E is a locally
polyhedral C∞-manifold. Further examples are traditional C∞-manifolds with
boundary or corners, since they have an atlas of charts with open images in a
cube [0, 1]n.
