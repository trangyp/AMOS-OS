Commutative topological algebras on
translation-invariant reproducing kernel
Hilbert spaces
Miguel Angel Rodriguez Rodriguez

Abstract
We study commutative topological algebras naturally associated
with translation-invariant reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces whose di-
rect integral decomposition has one-dimensional fibers. Starting from
the bounded algebra of translation-invariant operators, we pass to
a common dense domain generated by reproducing kernels and iden-
tify the corresponding diagonalizable operators with multiplication by
symbols in an intersection of weighted L2-spaces. On the symbol side
this gives a canonical space F0 and a maximal multiplicative subalge-
bra FM, which is a complete locally convex ∗-algebra. Transporting
the structure back yields corresponding algebras of operators and in-
tegral kernels. We also discuss when the inclusions
L∞(Ω) = F∞⊂FM ⊂F0
are strict, and illustrate the results with vertical and radial operators
on classical Bergman and Fock spaces.
Keywords and phrases. Translation-invariant reproducing kernel Hilbert
spaces; unbounded operators; commutative topological algebras; integral ker-
nels; Bergman spaces; Fock spaces.

Introduction
Reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, or RKHSs, and the operators acting on
them form an active area of research with connections to function theory, op-
erator theory, harmonic analysis, quantum mechanics and machine learning.
In this paper we study RKHSs H embedded in L2(G×Y, ν ⊗λ), where G
is a locally compact abelian group with Haar measure ν, (Y, λ) is a measure
space, and H is invariant under translations in the G-variable. The precise
assumptions are recalled in Section 2.1. This setting includes, in some cases
after a unitary transformation, classical examples such as Bergman, Fock
and Hardy spaces.
In [1], it was shown that spaces of this type admit a direct integral rep-
resentation
R: H −→
Z
ΩHξ dbν(ξ),
where Ω⊂bG and (Hξ)ξ∈Ωis a measurable family of Hilbert spaces. When the
fibers Hξ are one-dimensional, this representation identifies the algebra S∞
of bounded G-translation-invariant operators on H with the multiplication
algebra L∞(Ω). Thus S∞is a commutative W ∗-algebra.
A second realization of the same algebra is obtained from integral kernels.
Indeed, every bounded operator T on H has an integral kernel KT defined in
terms of the reproducing kernel of H. In [2], the algebraic structure of L∞(Ω)
was transported to these kernels. This gives a commutative W ∗-algebra A∞
of functions on G × Y × Y , with a convolution-type product denoted here by
⊙. In the bounded case, one therefore has three equivalent models:
S∞←→A∞←→L∞(Ω).
The purpose of this paper is to extend this picture to a natural class of
unbounded operators. Let
D0 = span{Ku,v : (u, v) ∈G × Y }
be the dense subspace generated by the reproducing kernels of H.
The
bounded kernel algebra A∞is contained in a larger space A0, whose ele-
ments define possibly unbounded diagonalizable operators on the common
domain D0. We denote the corresponding space of operators by S0. The
question motivating this work is how far the product ⊙on A∞can be ex-
tended inside A0, and what this extension means on the operator and symbol
sides.

The algebra AM is the largest subalgebra of A0 to which the kernel prod-

the product on AM is transported from pointwise multiplication on FM and
agrees with the formal integral product whenever that integral product de-
fines an element of A0. Hence the problem of extending the bounded ker-
nel product to all of A0 is reduced to the problem of understanding when
FM = F0. We do not give a complete characterization of this equality, but we
prove several partial results and present examples showing that the inclusions
above may be strict.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we recall the direct integral
representation of translation-invariant RKHSs. In Section 3 we review the
bounded theory and introduce the spaces S0, A0 and F0. Section 4 contains
the construction of FM and the proof of its main algebraic and topological
properties. In Section 5 we transfer these results to the operator and kernel
realizations. Section 6 discusses examples coming from the upper half-plane,
the Bergman space on the unit disk and the Fock space. Finally, Section 7
collects some open questions.
Most existing work in this direction concerns bounded operator algebras,
especially Banach algebras, C∗-algebras and von Neumann algebras. There

Open questions
We end this work by stating the open questions and problems collected
throughout this paper.
1. Do the hypotheses on the space L2(G × Y, ν × λ) suffice to prove that
F0 is a Fréchet space?
2. Find a complete characterization of the equalities F∞= FM and FM =
F0. Does one need the Fréchet assumption?
3. Give an explicit characterization of the space D = T

4. If the direct integral decomposition of H is not one-dimensional, one

translation-invariant operators. These spaces will consist of matrix-
valued functions (see [1]). How far can one extend the results from this
paper to this non-commutative setting?
