Abstract. In this article, we employ a gluing construction and a perturbation argument to pro-
duce complete Calabi–Yau metrics on Lefschetz K3 fibrations M over C, whose sectional curvature
is unbounded at infinity.
1. Introduction
Since Yau’s seminal work on Ricci curvature [18], the study of Calabi–Yau metrics has become
a central topic in Kähler geometry, with a major challenge being the construction of complete
Calabi–Yau metrics on non-compact Kähler manifolds.
Early on, sporadic concrete examples
appeared, such as complete hyperKähler metrics constructed via the Gibbons–Hawking ansatz [3].
However, these examples rely on explicit metric expressions and are therefore diﬀicult to generalize
to higher-dimensional manifolds. The first systematic investigation of the existence of complete
Calabi–Yau metrics on non-compact Kähler manifolds was initiated by Tian and Yau in their work
[15]. Their paper focuses primarily on constructing such metrics on a Fano manifold with a smooth
anti-canonical divisor removed. It also addresses the case of a non-Fano Calabi–Yau fibration from
which a smooth fiber has been excised.
Following their works, Hein considered the construction of such metrics on rational elliptic
surfaces which are not Fano with certain singular fibers removed in his thesis [7].In these cases, the
fibration structure is crucial for formulating an explicit metric ansatz. Moreover, the asymptotic
behavior of the metric at infinity is determined by the type of singular fiber.
It is natural to ask whether such constructions can be generalized to higher-dimensional fibra-
tions. One attempt in this direction is the work of the author and his collaborator in [11], where
such metrics are constructed on abelian fibrations. [16] also considered constructions on isotrivial
Calabi–Yau fibrations. A more challenging problem, however, is to consider general Calabi–Yau
fibrations where the complex structure varies. Unlike the elliptic fibration case, the construction of
a metric ansatz in this setting is more implicit, and its properties are far less favorable than those
of the semi-flat metric in the elliptic case. In this paper, we attempt to provide a construction
modeled on Lefschetz K3 fibrations.

To the best of the author’s knowledge, a complete non-compact Calabi–Yau metric with unbounded
sectional curvature has not been specified in the existing literature. Of course, using the method in
[1], one could consider the Taub–Nut spaces with infinitely many marked points {p1, p2, . . . } and
choose their positions appropriately — for instance, letting pi and pi+1 be mutually close while
pi tends to infinity — which should also yield a complete non-compact Calabi–Yau metric with
unbounded sectional curvature. However, the topology type of the resulting space is infinite; in
particular, it cannot be realized as a compact Kähler manifold minus a divisor.
In our situation, direct construction of an explicit Calabi–Yau metric is hard. We adopt the
current mainstream approach, namely, first constructing an approximate metric and then solving
the complex Monge–Ampère equation. However, the unboundedness of the sectional curvature
poses a serious diﬀiculty, as the Tian–Yau–Hein package (first shown in [15] and later refined in
[7]) can no longer be applied. The essential reason is that most existing methods for solving the
non-compact complex Monge–Ampère equation aim to follow Yau’s approach for the compact case,

Specifically, we hope to obtain the final result solely by solving the Poisson equation, i.e., the
linearization of the Monge–Ampère equation. This requires that our initial approximate metric be
suﬀiciently close to the final Calabi–Yau metric, meaning its Ricci potential is suﬀiciently small, so
that we can perturb the initial data using the implicit function theorem between Banach spaces to
obtain a genuine solution. To ensure that the initial approximate metric is suﬀiciently close to the
final answer, we fully exploit the fibration structure and introduce a collapsing process. Collapsing
reduces the influence of the fibers, so that even in the finite region the Ricci potential becomes
very small.
However, collapsing brings another diﬀiculty: whether the norm of inverse of the Laplacian under
the collapsing metric is uniformly bounded independent of ε. In the classical case of collapsing
the elliptic K3 fibration as discuss in [4], the authers are not bothered by this diﬀiculty. This is
because their initial gluing ansatz is already very close to the final genuine Calabi–Yau metric.
More precisely, the Ricci potential decays exponentially with respect to the collapsing parameter
ε, while the norm of the inverse Laplacian is at most of order O(ε−1). Consequently, even without
the sharpest estimates for the Laplacian, the use of the implicit function theorem between Banach
spaces is already guaranteed. In contrast, for K3 fibrations or more general Calabi–Yau fibrations,
the decay of the Ricci potential associated with the semi-Ricci-flat metric is only of O(ε) order, so
crude estimates are no longer suﬀicient.
In this paper, we aim to imitate the gluing method of Yang Li [9] on compact K3 fibrations
and extend it to the non-compact case. We divide the manifold into several regions according to
their geometric properties, assign appropriate weight functions on each region, and define suitable
weighted Hölder spaces, thereby obtaining a fairly precise linear theory. The main advantage of this
approach is that on each region, we can construct the inverse of the Laplacian in a relatively explicit
manner, which allows us to derive an ε-independent bound on the inverse of the Laplacian. Finally,
we glue these local inverse operators together to obtain an approximate inverse of the Laplacian
on the entire manifold. An important reason why this method works is that we collapse the fibers,
so that the error caused by gluing is ε-small.
The novelty of this paper compared to Yang Li’s work lies in the fact that we also need to consider
a new model at infinity. Here the generalized Kähler–Einstein metric pulled back from the base
has an |y|−2 factor, so its geometric properties are completely different from those near the singular
fiber in the finite region. The most notable difference is that the semi-Ricci-flat approximation
at infinity has favorable properties. Although the sectional curvature is unbounded, we can still
obtain a quite decent decay of the Ricci potential, as provided by Proposition 4.2. This is very
different from the geometric behavior near the singular fiber in the bounded region treated by
Yang Li, because in his case, once one enters the quantum scale, the semi-Ricci-flat approximation

The passage is arranged as follows:
• In Section 2, we begin by giving a rough description of the ansatz. We then review several
model metrics on specific geometric models to clarify the gluing construction. We end up this
section by establishing the well-definedness of the ansatz.
• In Section 3, we further investigate the geometric properties of the model metrics introduced
in Section 2, with a focus on their Laplacian operators. This analysis motivates the introduction
of a global weighted norm on M, developed in Section 4, and provides the foundation for the
construction of a global approximate inverse to the gluing ansatz in Section 5.
• In Section 4, we introduce a global weighted norm on M and prove that the Ricci potential of
the gluing ansatz is small with respect to this norm. We further compare the gluing ansatz with
the model metrics in their respective regions and derive quantitative estimates for their differences.

for the Green operator via decomposition and patching techniques.
• In Section 6, we use the implicit function theorem to perturb the gluing ansatz into a genuine
complete Calabi–Yau metric. A contradiction argument then demonstrates that the resulting com-
plete Calabi–Yau metric from Section 5 still possesses unbounded sectional curvature at infinity.
• In Section 7, we present some further discussions.

Suppose a quartic surface S = V (Q) is singular at a point p ∈P3. Then Q(p) = 0, dQ(p) = 0.
For fixed p, these impose 4 independent linear conditions on the coeﬀicients of Q. Since p varies in a
3-dimensional family, the locus of singular quartics has codimension 1, hence forms a hypersurface.
If a quartic surface has two singular points, then we obtain 8 linear conditions, while the pair
of points contributes 6 parameters. Therefore the locus of quartics with at least two singular
points has codimension 2. Similarly, a singularity worse than an ordinary double point requires
degeneration of the Hessian matrix. This imposes one additional equation inside ∆, so the locus of
non-nodal singularities also has codimension at least 2. Consequently, Sing(∆) is a closed subset
of codimension at least 2 in P34.
Since our pencil corresponds to a line L ≃P1 ⊂P34, a generic choice of L satisfies L∩Sing(∆) =
∅. Therefore every intersection point L ∩∆lies in the smooth locus of the discriminant hyper-
surface. Smooth points of ∆correspond precisely to quartic surfaces with exactly one ordinary
double point. Hence every singular fiber of π has exactly one node. Thus π : X →P1 is a Lefschetz
fibration with at worst one node point on each fiber.
Finally, since ∆⊂P34 is a hypersurface of degree 108, its intersection with a line L ≃P1 is a
divisor of degree 108. Hence, by intersection theory on P1, we obtain |L ∩∆| = 108 for a generic
line L, and the intersection is transverse. Therefore π has exactly 108 singular fibers. Moreover,
since L ∩Sing(∆) = ∅, each singular fiber contains exactly one ordinary double point.
