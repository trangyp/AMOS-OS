CLOCK SPACING FOR TWO-SIDED JACOBI MATRICES
BENJAMIN EICHINGER, MILIVOJE LUKIĆ, AND GIORGIO YOUNG
Abstract. We study local eigenvalue spacing for finite truncations of a two-
sided Jacobi matrix with two movable endpoints. In particular, we show that a
suitable analog of clock spacing follows from a pointwise reflectionlessness con-
dition. We obtain this as a consequence of a new scaling limit for Christoffel–
Darboux kernels with a movable starting point.
Without reflectionlessness,
we obtain a new class of limit kernels, which combine distinct contributions
from ±∞. We also show that clock spacing in the two-sided setting is a fragile
phenomenon, which can be destroyed by the change of a single Jacobi coef-
ficient; in particular, it is not merely a consequence of absolutely continuous
spectrum.

It may seem surprising that two-sided clock behavior is so fragile; the reflec-
tionless condition (1.8) is destroyed by the change of a single Jacobi coefficient a0,

The main novelty in our proofs is the analysis of CD kernels associated to a
movable left endpoint. Whereas prior work required analysis of orthogonal polyno-
mials, our results require control over all formal eigensolutions; we obtain this by
using scaling limits of half-line transfer matrices derived in [6].
The main novelty in our results is the connection to the reflectionless property.
If (1.8) holds on a set A of positive Lebesgue measure, the Jacobi matrix is said
to be reflectionless on A. By work of Kotani [9] and Remling [16], reflectionless
property is closely related to a.c. spectrum; in particular, for an almost periodic
Jacobi matrix, (1.8) holds a.e. on an essential support of a.c. spectrum.
Thus,
Theorem 1.3 generalizes Avila–Last–Simon [1] in the sense that it proves more
general clock behavior results for almost periodic Jacobi matrices.
Part of the
motivation for our work comes precisely from the ergodic setting; there, it is a
standard fact that different ways of taking larger and larger truncations lead to the
same density of states measure.
The reflectionless property has a central role in inverse spectral theory [17]; it
also has dynamical and scattering interpretations, compare [3, 8]. Our work offers
a new interpretation.
In Section 2, we will further discuss the solution ψ and prove Lemma 1.1. In
Section 3, we will introduce kernels corresponding to truncations J[k,n], and prove a
scaling limit of such kernels to a sine kernel under the assumptions of Theorem 1.2.
By a standard application of the Freud-Levin theorem, this will prove Theorem 1.2.
In Section 4, we will study the case of two endpoints going to opposite infinities. We
will encounter a mixed limit kernel which depends on both behaviors at ±∞. We
will see how the reflectionless assumption (1.8) leads again to a sine kernel limit,
which will prove Theorem 1.3. In Section 5, we prove Example 1.4. Finally, in
Section 6 we prove Schrödinger operator analogs of Theorems 1.2, 1.3 in Theorems
6.1, 6.3.
