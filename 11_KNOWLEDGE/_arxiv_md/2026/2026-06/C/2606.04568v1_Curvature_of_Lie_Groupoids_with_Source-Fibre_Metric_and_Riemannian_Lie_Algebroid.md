Abstract. Classical curvature formulas for Lie groups, principal bundles, and Riemannian submersions
are usually treated as separate theories.
This paper shows that they can be understood within a
common framework using Lie groupoids with source-fibre metrics and their infinitesimal counterparts,
Riemannian Lie algebroids.
We derive a sectional curvature formula for Lie groupoids with right-
invariant source metrics, extending the Arnold-Milnor “1-2-3-4” formula for Lie groups, and we establish
a Lie algebroid version of O’Neill’s curvature formulas for Riemannian submersion Lie algebroid. The
examples include the action of diffeomorphisms on densities on the torus, linking the Euler equation to
the Wasserstein space of densities and optimal transport, as well as a geometric model for the rotational
configuration space of the Earth.

1. Introduction
The relation between curvature and symmetry is one of the recurring themes in differential geometry.
On a Riemannian manifold, curvature measures how its differential geometry deviates from the one
in Euclidean space; in the theory of principal bundles, curvature measures the failure of a horizontal
distribution to be integrable; and in Lie theory, curvature formulas for invariant metrics express geometric
quantities in terms of algebraic data.
While, these viewpoints are closely related, they are usually
formulated in different terms.
Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids provide a natural framework in which such structures can be treated
simultaneously. A Lie groupoid is a smooth space of arrows between points of a manifold, generalizing
both Lie groups and equivalence relations. Its infinitesimal object is a Lie algebroid, which generalizes
both Lie algebras and tangent bundles. Thus a Lie algebroid combines infinitesimal directions on the base
manifold with additional internal directions coming from isotropy. In the transitive case this is expressed
by the short exact sequence

This point of view has classical roots. Atiyah’s work on connections in principal bundles led to what
is now called the Atiyah algebroid, whose exact sequence encodes the vertical infinitesimal symmetries
of a principal bundle together with the tangent directions of its base [4]. Mackenzie’s development of Lie
groupoids and Lie algebroids clarified the role of such exact sequences as intrinsic infinitesimal objects
associated to groupoids and principal bundles [18,19].
There is also a parallel history on the Riemannian side. For Lie groups with left- or right-invariant
metrics, the Levi-Civita connection and the curvature tensor can be written entirely in terms of the Lie
algebra and the inner product. This point of view appears prominently in Arnold’s interpretation of
hydrodynamics as geodesic motion on diffeomorphism groups [1], and in Milnor’s systematic study of
curvatures of left-invariant metrics on finite-dimensional Lie groups [20]. Another important source is
O’Neill’s theory of Riemannian submersions [24], where the curvature of a total space is decomposed into
horizontal and vertical contributions.
These constructions exhibit a striking formal resemblance. A transitive Lie algebroid fits into a short
exact sequence whose kernel is a bundle of Lie algebras and whose quotient is the tangent bundle of the
base. Atiyah algebroids of principal bundles form a central class of such examples, and, in many settings,
provide the geometric model for transitive Lie algebroids. Lie groups arise as the special case over a
point, where the infinitesimal object is a single Lie algebra. At the opposite end, the tangent bundle
itself is a Lie algebroid, with no vertical part and with the usual bracket of vector fields. A Riemannian
submersion similarly decomposes the tangent bundle into vertical directions, tangent to the fibres, and
horizontal directions identified with the tangent bundle of the base. Together, these examples suggest a
common principle: Lie algebroids provide a natural language for geometries in which tangent directions
on a base are lifted to a larger infinitesimal structure.
A substantial part of the modern framework surrounding Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids is due to
the work of Crainic, Fernandes, del Hoyo, and others, whose contributions established both the structural
foundations of the theory and many of its broader geometric applications. In particular, the solution by
Crainic and Fernandes to the integrability problem for Lie algebroids [5,6] clarified when an infinitesimal
Lie algebroid structure arises from a global Lie groupoid. Their work, together with Fernandes’ develop-
ment of Lie algebroid connections, representations, and characteristic classes [10], emphasized that curva-
ture in the algebroid setting is naturally tied to the failure of splittings, connections, or anchor-preserving
maps to respect bracket structures. This perspective has been an important conceptual influence on the
present work.
More recently, del Hoyo and Fernandes developed a far-reaching theory of Riemannian metrics on
Lie groupoids, introducing compatible metric structures of considerable generality and applying them to
problems such as linearization and differentiable stacks [7, 8]. Their framework addresses Riemannian
groupoids at a level of generality substantially broader than the one considered here, and provides a
powerful global theory for metric structures on groupoids.
The aim of the present work is to revisit several classical curvature constructions—Riemannian ge-
ometry on Lie groups with invariant metrics, principal bundles with metric-compatible connections, and
O’Neill’s theory of Riemannian submersions—and to reinterpret these systematically in the language of

Recent years have also seen a growing range of concrete applications in which Lie groupoids equipped
with source-fibre metrics play a direct geometric role.
In mathematical hydrodynamics, extending
Arnold’s interpretation of the Euler equation from Lie groups to Lie groupoids has led to groupoid-
geometric formulations of fluid systems whose configuration spaces are not naturally groups. Notably,
Izosimov and Khesin developed Lie groupoid and Lie algebroid frameworks for vortex sheets, discontin-

as geodesic or Euler-Arnold equations on groupoids endowed with right-invariant source-fibre metrics

terfaces, discontinuities, and generalized transport phenomena into a common geometric framework while
preserving many of the variational and Hamiltonian structures familiar from classical hydrodynamics.
At the same time, related geometric ideas have appeared in optimal transport, information geometry,
and geometric mechanics, where Riemannian structures compatible with bundle or groupoid-type sym-
metry are used to reinterpret classical constructions such as matrix decompositions, transport maps, and
density flows in geometric terms [22]. In Section 5, we consider the action Lie groupoid Diff(T)⋉Dens(T),
arising from the natural action of the diffeomorphism group of the torus on the space of densities. This
example illustrates the connection described above: the isotropy groups recover the geometry of incom-
pressible fluids, while the base Dens(T) carries the Wasserstein metric. Thus the groupoid framework
links Arnold’s hydrodynamical interpretation with optimal transport.

2.3. Lie groupoids. In this and in the following section, we introduce the basic definitions of Lie

3. Riemannian structure on Lie algebroids
A Lie algebroid is, in particular, a vector bundle over a manifold. Equipping a vector bundle with a
smoothly varying inner product—also called a bundle metric—allows one to define norms, angles, and
horizontal lifts, tools that are fundamental for studying geodesics, variational principles, and physical
models on manifolds. However, unlike the tangent bundle, an arbitrary vector bundle lacks a natural
bracket of sections and therefore has no notion of torsion. Consequently, there is no canonical choice of
a metric-connection. The Lie algebroid structure resolves this by providing a bracket that enables the
construction of a distinguished connection.

8. Future projects
We finish by indicating some directions for future work.
Project 1: Lie subalgebroids and induced curvature maps
A natural continuation of this work is to study curvature identities for Lie subalgebroids of Riemannian
Lie algebroids.
In [19, Def. 4.3.14], Mackenzie defines a Lie subalgebroid as follows.
Definition 8.1. Let A be a Lie algebroid on B and let B′ ⊂B be a closed embedded submanifold. A
Lie subalgebroid of A over B′ is a vector subbundle A′ →B′ of A|B′ →B′ such that:
(1) the anchor # : A →TB restricts to a map #′ : A′ →TB′;
(2) if X, Y ∈ΓA have X|B′, Y |B′ ∈ΓA′, then [X, Y ]|B′ ∈ΓA′ also;
(3) if X, Y ∈ΓA have X|B′ = 0, and Y |B′ ∈ΓA′, then [X, Y ]|B′ = 0.

Project 2: The role of curvature for groupoid Euler-Arnold equations
In analogy with Arnold’s interpretation of Riemannian curvature in Euler-Arnold dynamics on Lie groups,
study the role of sectional curvature, Ricci curvature, and conjugate points for the right-invariant source-
fibre metrics underlying groupoid Euler-Arnold equations. This direction appears in [14, Problem 3].
Project 3: Weakening the metric assumption
A different direction for future work is to replace the Riemannian metric by more general geometric data.
In the present paper, the metric is mainly used to select a horizontal lift γ : TB →A and to define a
canonical Levi-Civita connection ∇on A; the curvature theory is then expressed through these maps
and their failure to preserve Lie algebroid brackets. This suggests developing analogous theories for Lie
algebroids equipped with non-metric auxiliary data, for example a prescribed splitting and connection;
a Cartan connection; or degenerate and partial geometric structures, like pseudo-Riemannian or sub-
Riemannian metrics.
