CONNECTING H∞-FUNCTIONAL CALCULUS AND ISOMETRIC
DILATIONS FOR COMMUTING FAMILIES OF RITTE OPERATORS
CHRISTIAN LE MERDY AND M.N. RESHMI
Abstract. Let (T1, . . . , Td) be a commuting d-tuple of RittE operators on some UMD
Banach space X. We show that (T1, . . . , Td) admits a bounded H∞-functional calculus if and
only if Tk is an R-RittE operator for every k = 1, . . . , d, and the d-tuple (T1, . . . , Td) admits
an isometric dilation (U1, . . . , Ud) on some UMD Banach space Y such that (U1, . . . , Ud) is
polynomially bounded. In the case where X further possesses property (α), we establish
other characterizations of the H∞-functional calculus property for (T1, . . . , Td) in terms of
isometric dilations.
2000 Mathematics Subject Classification : 47A60, 47A20, 47A13.
Key words: Dilations, functional calculus.
1. Introduction and main statements
In Banach space operator theory, H∞-functional calculus occupies a significant position
due to its applications to the harmonic analysis of operators or C0-semigroups, ergodic
theory, multiplier theory and evolution problems. Throughout its development, isometric
or isomorphic dilations have always played a major role. The interactions between H∞-
functional calculus and dilations are visible from the earliest works concerning sectorial
operators. They were definitely highlighted by a remarkable paper of A. Frohlich and L. Weis
[14], in which it is shown that if X is a UMD Banach space and if A is an R-sectorial operator
of type < π
2 on X, then A has a bounded H∞-functional calculus if and only if the semigroup
(e−tA)t≥0 admits a dilation into a bounded C0-group on the Bochner space L2([0, 1]; X). Since
then, connections between H∞-functional calculus and dilations for sectorial operators have
been refined in various situations, and then it was studied for Ritt operators in [2, 3], see
also [26, Chapter 10].
H∞-functional calculus for commuting d-tuples of sectorial operators was introduced in
[1] and then investigated from various perspectives in [13], [21] and [19]. The more recent
paper [6] studies dilations in this context.
Finally, the study of H∞-functional calculus
for commuting d-tuples of Ritt operators was addressed in [4, 5, 30] and dilation results of
various kinds were established in these papers.

operators. Let T = {z ∈C : |z| = 1} denote the unit circle and let D = {z ∈C : |z| < 1}
denote the open unit disc. Let ξ1, . . . , ξN be distinct elements of T, for some N ≥1, and let

In [27], we showed that if X is a Hilbert space and if T is a RittE operator on X that
admits a bounded H∞-functional calculus, then it admits an isometric dilation on a Hilbert
space Y . Then, we used the specific form of this dilation to deduce that if a commuting
d-tuple (T1, . . . , Td) of RittE operators on X is such that each Tk possesses a bounded H∞-
functional calculus, then (T1, . . . , Td) admits an isometric dilation on a Hilbert space Y .
This approach was extended in [31] to the case where X is simply a reflexive Banach space
such that X and X∗have finite cotype. Precisely, it is proven therein that if (T1, . . . , Td)
is a commuting d-tuple of RittE operators on such a space X, and if each Tk possesses a
bounded H∞-functional calculus, then for any 1 < p < ∞, (T1, . . . , Td) admits an isometric
dilation on a Bochner space Y = Lp(X; X).
This result and this approach are interesting in themselves, but for commuting d-tuple
(T1, . . . , Td) of RittE operators, they cannot lead to any characterization of the bounded H∞-
functional calculus by a dilation property. Indeed, it only takes into account the bounded
H∞-functional calculus property for each individual Tk, which is generally a weaker property
than the bounded H∞-functional calculus property of the family (T1, . . . , Td), see Remark
5.2. The main novelty of the work presented here is to consider the bounded H∞-functional
calculus of a family (T1, . . . , Td) (not just the bounded H∞-functional calculus of each of
its elements) and to introduce square functions associated with this property, see Section 3.
In the proof of Theorem 1.2, see Section 4, these square functions are precisely what allow
for the construction of polynomially bounded isometric dilations, a property that proves to
be essential in establishing a characterization of the bounded H∞-functional calculus via
dilations.

2.4. Background on Banach space geometry. Results on H∞-functional calculus often
require geometric assumptions on the underlying Banach space. This well-known phenome-
non explicitly appears upon reading [18] or [26].
In this context, UMD Banach spaces were recognized as essential already in the seminal
work of G. Dore and A. Venni on bounded imaginary powers [12]. We refer to [17, 33] for the
definition and a comprehensive information on UMD Banach spaces. For readers unfamiliar
with Banach space geometry, we mention a few important properties: If X is UMD, then
any Banach space isomorphic to X is UMD; If X is UMD, then any subspace of X is UMD;
If X is UMD, then its dual space X∗is UMD. All UMD Banach spaces are reflexive; All
Hilbert spaces, and all Lp-spaces, with 1 < p < ∞, are UMD; More generally, if X is a UMD
Banach space, then any Bochner space Lp(X; X), with 1 < p < ∞, is a UMD Banach space
as well.
We will use the notion of finite cotype, for which we refer to [29] (see also [18, Chapter
7]). We note that all UMD Banach spaces have finite cotype.
