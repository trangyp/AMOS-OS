CORRIGENDUM: ORDER EXTREME POINTS
AND SOLID CONVEX HULLS
A. IANINA AND T. OIKHBERG AND M.A. TURSI
Abstract. We correct some errors found in [T. Oikhberg and M.A. Tursi,
Order extreme points and solid convex hulls, The Mathematical Legacy
of Victor Lomonosov (ed. R. Aron et.al.), de Gryuter, 2020, 297–315.]
The goal of this note is two-fold: (i) to correct some annoying typos in [2],
and (ii) to correct some of the proofs. The enumeration of sections and
statements (theorems, definitions, examples etc.) in the Arxiv preprint is
different from that in the published version; the numbers from the published
version will be given in square brackets.
(1) In several statements in Sections 3 [19.3] and 4 [19.4], the convexity
assumptions are omitted. Specifically:
• Proposition 3.1 [19.7] – Separation – should read:
Suppose τ is a sufficiently rich topology on a Banach lattice X, and A ⊂
X+ is a τ-closed positive-solid bounded convex subset of X+. Suppose,
furthermore, x ∈X+ does not belong to A. Then there exists f ∈Xτ
+ so
that f(x) > supa∈A f(a).
• Theorem 4.1 [19.10] – “Solid” Krein-Milman – should read:
Any τ-compact positive-solid convex subset A of X+ coincides with the
τ-closed positive-solid convex hull of its order extreme points.
• Corollary 4.2 [19.11] should read:
Any τ-compact solid convex subset of X coincides with the τ-closed solid
convex hull of its order extreme points.
Without convexity, these results may fail. For instance, for Corollary 4.2,
we can take X = (R2, ∥· ∥∞), and A = {(x, 0) : |x| ≤1} ∪{(0, y) : |y| ≤1}.
The set A is closed when τ is the norm topology and solid, but the convex
hull of A is much larger than A itself.
(2) There is a hole in the proof of Theorem 7.1 [19.30]: on page 17 line -2
[page 313 line -6], it is claimed that, for any x ∈C+\{0}, we have x∧u ̸= 0.
The current argument doesn’t permit to reach this conclusion. However,
this can be fixed as follows:

Patch on the proof of Theorem 19.30. Denote by B ⊂X the band gener-
ated by u in X. By [1, Proposition 1.2.3], B is closed (and solid); by [1,
Proposition 1.2.3], B+ consists of all z ∈X+ so that z = ∨n(z ∧nu). As u
is a quasi-interior point of Y , we conclude that C′ ⊂Y ⊂B, hence C ⊂B.
So, x = ∨n(x ∧nu), which implies x ∧nu ̸= 0 for large enough n.
As
x ∧nu ≤n(x ∧u), we conclude that x ∧u ̸= 0.
□
(3) The proof of the implication (1) ⇒(2) in Proposition 4.3 [19.12] is
incorrect: the set D constructed on page 10 [306], first paragraph, is not
solid, hence it is not guaranteed to have any order extreme points.
Proposition 4.3 [19.12] can be established in a rather roundabout way. More
specifically, Theorem 7.1 [19.30] implies:
Corollary. For a Banach lattice X, the following statements are equivalent:
(1) X has the Radon-Nikodym Property (RNP).
(2) X has the Krein-Milman Property (KMP).
(3) X has the Solid Krein-Milman Property (SKMP) – that is, any closed
bounded convex solid subset of X is the closure of the solid convex
hull of its order extreme points.
(4) Any closed bounded convex solid subset of X has an order extreme
points.
Above, item (4) is a verbatim repetition of Proposition 4.3[19.12](1), while
item (3) is nothing but a restatement of Proposition 4.3[19.12](2).
Proof. The implication (3) ⇒(4) is immediate, and (2) ⇒(3) follows by
combining Theorem 2.2 [19.2] with Corollary 2.4 [19.4]. The equivalence
(1) ⇔(2) is known, see the references cited in [2].
The proof of Theorem 7.1 [19.30] actually produces, for any Banach lattice
X failing the RNP, a closed bounded convex solid E ⊂X without any order
extreme points. This gives ¬(1) ⇒¬(4), or equivalently, (4) ⇒(1).
□
