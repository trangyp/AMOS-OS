COMPLETE FAMILIES OF CURVES IN THE MODULI SPACE OF GENUS G CURVES
CHLOE STEWART
ABSTRACT. Let Mg be the moduli space of smooth curves of genus g. The image of a non-constant
morphism from a curve T to Mg is a curve in Mg. By work of Gonz´alez D´ıez and Harvey, for every
integer g ≥3, there exists a complete curve in Mg. Here we generalize the construction to produce
new complete curves in Mg. We also find a formula for the genus of each curve T using Galois
theory for function fields.
MSC20 classifications: primary 11S15, 14H10, 14H30; secondary 11R32, 14D20, 14H37
Keywords: curve, automorphism, moduli space, cover, complete, branch point
1. INTRODUCTION
The points of Mg represent isomorphism classes of smooth, projective, and connected genus g
curves. A curve in Mg is the image of a non-constant morphism T −→Mg, where T is a curve.
Let C be the image of T in Mg. Then C is projective (or complete) if T is projective. To define a
complete curve in Mg we can define a non-isotrivial family of genus g curves over a projective
curve such that every fiber is smooth.
If g ≥3 then many complete curves exist in Mg. One can abstractly construct complete curves
in Mg by intersecting hypersurfaces [Har83]. The first examples of complete curves were con-
structed by Gonz´alez D´ıez and Harvey [GDH91a], [GDH91b]. The first paper contains an explicit
construction for a complete curve for each g > 3. The second paper handles the g = 3 case, but
is not as explicit and uses a different strategy. Later, Zaal returned to the g = 3 case and found a
more explicit construction [Zaa95].
For g > 3, the construction in [GDH91a] amounts to specifying a family of genus g curves. The
family is given by varying the branch points of a double cover of a fixed genus 2 curve. In this
paper, we generalize this work by generating families where each curve is a double cover of a fixed
genus g ≥2 curve. Let Mg,g ⊂Mg denote the sublocus whose points represent isomorphism
classes of smooth genus g curves which are double covers of a genus g curve.
Theorem 1.1 ([GDH91a]). For every g > 3, there exists a complete curve in Mg,2.
We prove the following theorem in Section 2. We work over an algebraically closed field de-
noted by k where char(k) ̸= 2.
Theorem 1.2. For every g ≥2 and g > 2g −1, there exists a complete curve C in Mg,g over k.
In Sections 4 and 5, we investigate the geometry of the curves arising from this construction.
After making a choice of the base curve and imposing a simple condition, we calculate the genus
of every T using the Riemann Hurwitz formula.
This work is connected to an important open question pertaining to Mg. The maximal dimen-
sion for a complete subvariety in Mg for g ≥4 is unknown. This question is interesting because
the maximal dimension of a complete subvariety in a moduli space is a measure of how close
the moduli space is to being projective or affine. For g = 1, 2 the only complete subvarieties are
points, and M1 and M2 are affine. In M3, the maximal dimension of a complete subvariety is
1. However, it is unknown if a 2-dimensional complete subvariety exists in M4. There are sev-
eral results bounding the maximal dimension of a complete subvariety of Mg. Diaz proved, in
