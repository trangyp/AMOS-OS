CURVES CONTAINED IN A QUARTIC DETERMINANTAL SURFACE
CONTAINING A LINE
ABEL CASTORENA AND MONSERRAT VITE
Abstract. Let X ⊆P3 be a very general element of the Noether–Lefschetz divisor that
parametrizing smooth quartic surfaces containing a line. Let L ⊆X denote the correspond-
ing line. We study the curves contained in X and analyze their behavior in the Hilbert
scheme. We first determine which linear systems contain smooth irreducible curves. For
most classes, we verify that the general member is a smooth point of the expected Hilbert
scheme. Finally we compute the Rao function of any curve on X.
1. Introduction
The geometry of curves on K3 surfaces is a central theme in algebraic geometry, owing
to the rich structure of the linear systems defined upon them, the deformation theory of
embedded curves, and the geometry of components of the Hilbert scheme parametrizing
curves in P3. Among K3 surfaces, smooth quartics in P3 are the simplest examples accessible
to explicit computation, making them an ideal setting for studying the geometric properties
of curves contained in them.
A natural first step in understanding the curves on such a surface is to determine which
of them are arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay (ACM). In [CV26], the authors give a complete
classification of ACM curves on a surface of degree d in P3 in terms of weak admissible pairs,
and apply this classification to describe explicitly all ACM curves contained in a very general
smooth determinantal quartic surface X ⊆P3. In particular, when X contains a line L, so
that Pic(X) is generated by the hyperplane class H and the line class L, the classification
of [CV26] shows that a curve with class aH + bL on X is ACM if and only if b ∈{0, 1, −1}.
This result serves as a starting point and primary motivation for the present work: our goal
is to go beyond the ACM locus and study all curves contained in X, whether ACM or not.
In this paper we focus on a smooth quartic surface X ⊆P3 containing a line L, so that
Pic(X) is generated by H and L with H2 = 4, H · L = 1, and L2 = −2. The purpose of this
article is threefold.
The first goal is to determine in which linear systems |(k + m)H −mL| and |kH + mL|
there exist smooth irreducible curves. These classes represent all possible effective curves
on X: by varying k and m over the natural numbers, one obtains the effective cone of X.
To distinguish classes with smooth members from those whose general element is necessarily
reducible or degenerate, we rely on a criterion due to Kleppe [Kle17]. Specifically, we apply
[Kle17, Lema 3.1] to show that smooth irreducible curves exist in the classes |(k+m)H−mL|
for k, m ≥1, and in |kH + mL| for m ≥1 and k ≥2m. On the other hand, the classes
|(2m−1)H+mL| are shown to have only reducible general members, consisting of L together
with a smooth curve in |(2m −1)H + (m −1)L|.
The second goal is to understand the position of these curves in the Hilbert scheme Hd,g
of curves of degree d and genus g in P3. We describe the behavior of curves on X inside

2. Smooth curves in a Picard class
Let X = V (F) ⊆P3 be a very general smooth surface of degree 4 containing a line ℓ. The
purpose of these notes is to obtain information of the curves that are contained in X. We
know that the Picard group of X is of rank 2 and that the line ℓis the unique line contained
in X. We denote the class of the line ℓby L, then Pic(X) is generated by H and L with
H2 = 4, H.L = 1 and L2 = −2.
The effective cone of X is generated by the classes H −L and L, this implies that an
effective curve has class aH + (b −a)L with a, b ∈N. Then we have the following corollary
from the Proposition 19 of [CV26]:
Corollary 2.1. Let C be a curve in X with class aH + (b −a)L, then C is an ACM curve
if and only if b −a ∈{0, 1, −1}.
In order to know which classes have smooth irreducible curves we use a result of [Kle17]
that in our notation says:
Lemma 2.2 ([Kle17, Lem. 3.1]). Let D be an effective divisor on X. If D.L ≥0 and
D.(H −L) > 0, then the linear system |D| contains smooth irreducible curves.
