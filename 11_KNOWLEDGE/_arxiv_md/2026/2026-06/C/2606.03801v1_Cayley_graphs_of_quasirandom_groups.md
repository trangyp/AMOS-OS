LUCA SABATINI
Abstract. A finite group G is ε-quasirandom if all its nontrivial irreducible
complex representations have degree at least |G|ε. Building on recent work
of Golsefidy–Srinivas, we prove that expansion in a quasirandom group is
controlled by expansion in its simple quotients. As a consequence, we remove
the product theorem from the hypotheses of the Bourgain–Gamburd expansion
machine. Moreover, we combine this result with crown theory to deduce that
1+⌊ε−1⌋random elements give an expander Cayley graph with high probability.
Finally, generalizing results of Breuillard–Green–Tao and Pyber–Szab´o, we
prove that the diameter of any connected Cayley graph of a quasirandom
group is polylogarithmic.
1. Introduction
Expander graphs are highly connected sparse graphs and play a fundamental
role in various areas of mathematics and computer science [HLW06, Lub12].
Among their remarkable properties, there is the fact that their diameter is
logarithmic in the number of vertices.
Several notable constructions of expanders involve Cayley graphs. In a spectac-
ular breakthrough, Bourgain and Gamburd [BG08] devised an ingenious method,
starting from the so-called Sarnak–Xue trick [SX91], to show that 2 random
elements in SL2(p) give an expander Cayley graph with high probability. Using
the same strategy, the companion paper of Breuillard–Green–Guralnick–Tao
[BGGT15] generalized their theorem to all finite simple groups of Lie type of
bounded rank. There are also weaker results that have the advantage of be-
ing valid for literally all generating sets. In a parallel development, Helfgott
[Hel08] proved that the diameter of every connected Cayley graph of SL2(p) is
polylogarithmic, while Breuillard–Green–Tao [BGT11] and Pyber–Szab´o [PS16]
generalized his theorem to all finite simple groups of Lie type of bounded rank.
The following property of finite simple groups of bounded Lie rank was first
isolated by Gowers [Gow08], and plays a crucial role in the proofs of these results:
Definition. Let ε > 0. A finite group G is ε-quasirandom if every nontrivial
irreducible complex representation of G has degree at least |G|ε.

We are interested in this property when ε is fixed and G is large, so we simply
refer to quasirandom group(s) in this case. Quasirandom groups have several
characterizations [BS25, NP11], and in the context of expansion and diameter
bounds, they can be thought of as the groups where the “tricks” of Sarnak–Xue
[SX91] and Gowers [NP11] are available efficiently. It is natural to ask: how far
quasirandomness alone drives expansion phenomena? The present paper answers
this question completely.
Our first main result builds on the recent work of Golsefidy and Srinivas
[GS24, GS25]. They study group extensions

where Q is quasirandom.
In particular, they give hypotheses under which
expansion in Cay(G, S) follows from expansion in Cay(G/N, S) (in fact, they first
prove that such hypotheses induce quasirandomness to G itself). For instance,
they require G to be a specific direct product of N and Q, or N to be small and
abelian, or nilpotent of bounded class, together with suitable technical conditions
on the conjugation action of G on N. We show that no additional hypothesis
is needed when G is quasirandom: expansion in quasirandom groups is always
controlled by expansion in the simple quotients.
Theorem 1.1. Let G be ε-quasirandom. If G = ⟨S⟩, then
gap(G, S) ≫ε,|S| min{gap(G/N, S) : N a maximal normal subgroup of G}.
Here gap(G, S) denotes the spectral gap of the Cayley graph. It is remarkable
that Theorem 1.1 fails dramatically for non-quasirandom groups: a trivial example
is a large cyclic 2-group, but we refer the reader to Example 5.3 for a decisive
example with a large unique simple quotient.
The proof of Theorem 1.1 does not require a delicate analysis of random walks
as in [GS24, GS25]. Instead, we use some of their results as a black box and
proceed with a different strategy. A fundamental observation is that, by Gr¨un’s
lemma on perfect groups, we can implement a process where at each step we
quotient the ambient group by its center and then by a non-central minimal
abelian normal subgroup. This process stops when the solvable radical is trivial,
and it lasts at most ε−1 steps by quasirandomness. To handle the non-central
steps we combine a theorem in [GS25] with the “affine conjugating trick” by
Eberhard–Murphy–Pyber–Szab´o [EMPS25]. The case of central extensions re-
quires a separate argument that relies on Kazhdan constants (Lemma 5.1). When
the solvable radical is trivial, we actually have a direct product of simple groups
by a result of Barbieri and the author [BS25]. Finally, this situation can be
handled by an iterative application of another theorem in [GS25].
Theorem 1.1 has some applications that we now describe. To start, Theorems C,
D and E in [GS25] are not used in our proof and in fact can be considered special
cases. Notably, it also allows us to remove the product theorem (Stage 2) from the

A different application of Theorem 1.1 concerns uniform expansion.
The
following deep conjecture of Breuillard is reported as “folklore” in [Bre14, Con-
jecture 4.12]: If G is a finite simple group of Lie type of bounded rank, then all
connected Cayley graphs of G are uniform expanders. Progress on this problem
was made by Breuillard and Gamburd [BG10] (see also Becker–Breuillard [BB26]),
but the general conjecture remains open. By Theorem 1.1, at least for sets of
bounded cardinality, Breuillard’s conjecture is equivalent to a statement for all
quasirandom groups. We push this analogy further and propose the following
conjecture for all generating sets:
Conjecture. If G is ε-quasirandom, then all connected Cayley graphs of G are
ε-uniform expanders.
This appears to be a very difficult problem. Using our methods we are able to
prove a weaker, unconditional uniform result concerning diameters. Let us write
diam(G) for the maximum diameter over all connected Cayley graphs of G.
Theorem 1.3. If G is ε-quasirandom, then diam(G) ≤(log |G|)Oε(1).
This extends the aforementioned theorems of Breuillard–Green–Tao [BGT11]
and Pyber–Szab´o [PS16]. In the recent paper [EMST26], the author together
with S. Eberhard, E. Maini and G. Tracey proved a general-purpose polylog-type
diameter bound for arbitrary finite groups, but a different argument is required to
obtain Theorem 1.3. A new ingredient is that every element of an ε-quasirandom
group is the product of at most ε−1 commutators (Lemma 4.3).
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we present the necessary
background. Moreover, we prove that the solvable radical of an ε-quasirandom
group has ε-bounded derived length (in fact the proof of this result inspired
the multi-step process that we have described above). In Section 3 we show
that 1 + ⌊ε−1⌋random elements generate an ε-quasirandom group with high
probability. It is important to do so here, in order to apply the affine conjugating
trick efficiently in later stages. In Section 4 we prove Theorem 1.3, and in Section 5
we deal with central extensions and complete the proofs of Theorems 1.1 and 1.2.

2. Preliminaries
2.1. Quasirandom groups. Every group in this paper is finite. A finite group
is ε-quasirandom for some ε > 0 if and only if it is perfect. It is known that
there is no 0.35-quasirandom group, and that the only 1
3-quasirandom groups are
two sporadic simple groups [BS25, Corollary D]. In particular, in the relevant
cases we can always assume ε < 1/3. We say that a group (or more precisely a
sequence of groups) is quasirandom to mean that it is ε-quasirandom for a fixed
ε > 0.
It follows from the definition that if G is ε-quasirandom and N is a proper
normal subgroup of G, then |G/N| ≥|G|ε and G/N is again ε-quasirandom
(this fact will be used without further mention). We now report an elegant
characterization of quasirandom groups with respect to group actions, which is
due to Nikolov and Pyber [NP11].
Lemma 2.1. Let G be a finite group. If G is ε-quasirandom and acts non-trivially
on a finite set Ω, then |Ω| > |G|ε.
Conversely, if for all nontrivial actions of G on a finite set Ωwe have |Ω| ≥|G|ε,
then G is ε′-quasirandom for some ε′(ε) > 0.
Proof. Let |Ω| = n. We have a nontrivial homomorphism G →Sym(n), and
Sym(n) can be naturally embedded in GLn(C). Removing the diagonal subspace
we obtain a nontrivial linear representation of degree n −1 and the claim follows.
Conversely, suppose that G has a nontrivial irreducible linear representation of
degree n. By [NP11, Theorem 4] we obtain a nontrivial action of G on a set of
cardinality at most c0n2 where c0 is an absolute constant. Therefore c0n2 ≥|G|ε
and the proof follows.
□
We will often use the following very special case.
Corollary 2.2. Let G be ε-quasirandom, and H, N normal subgroups of G with
N ⊆H. If H/N is not central in G/N, then |H : N| > |G|ε.
Proof. Observe that G/N, and so G, acts non-trivially on H/N by conjugation
(the identity element is fixed), and use Lemma 2.1.
□
Moreover, if H < G and we consider the action on the right cosets by multipli-
cation, we obtain |G : H| > |G|ε, i.e. |H| < |G|1−ε.
The solvable radical Sol(G) is the largest solvable normal subgroup of G. The
following result from [BS25] gives a structural characterization of quasirandom

2.3. Expander Cayley graphs. If G is a finite group and S ⊆G, the Cayley
graph Cay(G, S) is the undirected graph with vertex set G and edges (g, s±1g)
for g ∈G and s ∈S. We allow multiple edges, so Cay(G, S) is always a d-regular
graph for d = 2|S|. When N ⊴G, with an abuse of notation we write Cay(G/N, S)
to denote the Cayley graph with respect to the projection of S in G/N (note that
this projection is a multiset in general). We write gap(G, S) to denote the spectral
gap of the normalized adjacency matrix of Cay(G, S). Thus gap(G, S) > 0 if
and only if Cay(G, S) is connected if and only if G = ⟨S⟩. Finally we say that
Cay(G, S) is an expander if gap(G, S) ≥δ for some constant δ > 0 depending
only on the relevant parameters. For instance, the conclusion of the following
theorem is that gap(G, S) ≥δ(ε) > 0 with probability 1 −o(1) as |G| →∞.
Theorem 2.7 (Breuillard–Green–Guralnick–Tao). Fix ε > 0, and let G be an
ε-quasirandom simple group. Then 2 random elements give an expander Cayley
graph with high probability.
Proof. A large finite simple group is ε-quasirandom if and only if it is a group of
Lie type of rank O(ε−1). Therefore, this is [BGGT15, Theorem 1.2].
□

• G1 × G2 is ε-quasirandom, and
• for each x ∈Gi we have ⟨xGi⟩= ((x±1)Gi)c for some constant c ≥1.

gap(G1 × G2, S) ≫ε,c,|S| min{gap(G1, S), gap(G2, S)}.
Proof. This is [GS25, Theorem A]. It is easy to check that quasirandomness of
G1 × G2 is equivalent to a statement about quasirandomness of G1 and G2 and

We come to the second result from [GS25]. In [LV16], Lindenstrauss and
