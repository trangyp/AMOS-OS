Introduction
Since the axiomatic definition of lattice vertex algebras by R. Borcherds [Bor86] in his work on the
Monstrous Moonshine conjecture, and that of vertex operator algebras (VOAs) by I. Frenkel, J.
Lepowsky and A. Meurman [FLM88], vertex algebra research has established a wealth of relations
to different areas of mathematics and thereby proven to be immensely prolific. Among these we
find the representation theory of Lie algebras, geometry — notably algebraic geometry of curves,
moduli spaces and singular varieties; Poisson geometry and quantization of singular varieties;
derived geometry and homological algebra involving BRST cohomology — all related to the present
work, as well as category theory and number theory (through vertex algebra modules), integrable
systems, low-dimensional topology and mathematical physics applications in string and quantum
field theory.
While there exists no single universal construction of vertex algebras, a toolkit
of complementary constructions has instead been developed over the past decades, with each
approach adapted to the particular origin – geometric, algebraic or physical – of the vertex algebra.

vacuum modules of affine Lie algebras and which often admit realizations inside free-field VOAs.
In contrast, the central node hosts more sophisticated constructions which start from a parent
vertex algebra defined using the fundamental building blocks, and which are based on reductions
through orbifolds, coset / commutants, or via BRST / quantum Hamiltonian reductions. BRST
reduction, in particular, implements a derived semi-infinite quotient of a parent vertex algebra
by a current algebra using a ghost system and BRST differential, and its cohomology realizes
the corresponding reduced vertex algebra, such as affine W-algebras [FF90], [KRW03], [AKM15].
[Ara17], [FBZ01] and [AM24] are excellent recent resources with pedagogical presentations.
A complementary constructive approach towards vertex algebras comes from geometry, from
the left-most node in Figure 1 and the chiral quantization of affine Poisson varieties or Poisson
schemes.
Such a classical geometry represents the associated variety of the vertex algebra in
question, where the arc space of the associated variety captures geometrical aspects of the vertex
algebra through the associated graded algebra. The monograph [AM24] is currently the most
comprehensive and up to date exposition emphasizing this geometrical aspect of vertex algebras.
The relation between left-most and central nodes of Figure 1, which has been explored for instance
in [AKM15, Kuw17, AKM23], will play a key role in the work presented in this paper.
The third perspective in the right-most node in Figure 1 is from the physical origin of vertex
algebras as distinguished algebras of operators inside quantum field theories in three dimensions or
higher. An established example is the 3D/2D correspondence between Chern-Simons gauge theory
with compact gauge group at integer level, placed on a three-dimensional manifold with bound-
ary, and Wess-Zumino-Witten algebra supported on the boundary [Wit88, EMSS89]. A modern
analog of this correspondence is realized within N = 4 supersymmetric quantum field theories in
three dimensions on manifolds with a transverse holomorphic foliation structure, whose topologi-
cally twisted sector supports boundary vertex algebra [Gai19, CG19]. These new correspondences
have been studied intensely in recent years, in particular in the context of 3D N = 4 Abelian
gauge theories [BF23, FS24, BCDN23], the so-called rank-0 theories [GKS24, FGK24] which have
zero-dimensional moduli spaces of vacua, 3D N = 4 Chern-Simons-matter theories [CDGG24]
which admit, upon a topological twist, holomorphic boundary conditions supporting logarithmic
vertex operator algebras, and a systematic study of 3D N = 4 non-Abelian quiver gauge theo-
ries [CSYZ25]. From this physical perspective, the role of the classical Poisson geometry from
Figure 1, which is the associated variety of the vertex algebra under consideration and related
to this via chiral quantization, is played by (a branch of) the moduli space of vacua of three-
dimensional theory. This type of analysis has in turn uncovered surprising vertex algebra dualities
which had been motivated physically and related to 3D mirror symmetry [CGK25, BCDN23],

three-dimensional quantum field theory, which end on the vertex algebra-supporting boundary,

boundary vertex algebras [CSYZ25] has allowed for their detailed local analysis, and the study of

In the following subsection, we will give a brief introduction to the precise context in which
we construct and analyze vertex algebras in this paper, specifically via the global and respectively
local chiral quantizations of certain Poisson schemes defined from representation spaces of quivers.
We will first state in a somewhat informal manner the key mathematical results of the paper —
which will ultimately characterize the precise relation between vertex algebras resulting from the
local and global chiral quantization procedures — together with the logic flow which connects
the core statements and the intuition which lies behind them, deferring the presentation of the
rigorous statements and their proofs to the subsequent sections.
1.1
Core mathematical results
[CSYZ25] initiated a systematic study of vertex operator algebras constructed as chiralizations, or
chiral quantizations [AM24], of quiver varieties. In this context, features of this classical geometry
are reflected in the resulting vertex algebra structure, with the quiver variety recovered as the
associated variety of the VOA. The input data for the chiralization procedure is comprised of a
quiver Q and dimension vectors v, w, which define an associated Cartan matrix C and reductive

1.2
Structure of the paper
In Section 2 we review key statements pertaining to the geometry of extended quiver (super)-
varieties. Propositions 2.1-2.3 in particular address the flatness of the moment map µ, equivalent
conditions on the quiver and dimension data and their implications for the reducedness and irre-
ducibility of preimages under the moment map, as well as natural maps between function rings.
Proposition 2.4 then lists equivalent conditions for the flatness of extended moment maps. This
section also summarizes the construction of the simple locus in the representation space of a
quiver, with Propositions 2.5-2.6 providing numerical conditions for the regular locus µ−1(0)reg to
be non-empty and implications for the reducedness and irreducibility of µ−1(0), and flatness of the
moment map. This covers the classical aspects of the chiralization procedure of quiver varieties.
Section 3 then covers the semi-classical aspects of this procedure. In particular, Proposition
3.1 introduces the properties ( eP1) — that the extended moment map eµ be flat, and that eµ−1(0) be
reduced, irreducible and with rational singularities — and ( eP2) (jet-flatness of eµ), as well as their
non-extended counterparts (P1) −(P2), and demonstrates how property ( eP1) implies the others.

Two key theorems in this section are Theorems 3.1-3.2, which provide conditions (dimension

Sections 4-6 are then focused on the chiral quantum aspects of the chiralization procedure.
Specifically, Sections 4-5 review the construction of quiver vertex superalgebras from [CSYZ25]
as chiralizations of quiver varieties via the global and respectively local BRST reduction proce-
dures, which we have outlined in Figure 3, and which have the semi-classical description presented

flatness of the moment map. Theorem 6.3 then proves the injectivity of natural maps between the
quiver vertex superalgebras constructed in Sections 4-5, under the conditions that the extended
moment map eµ is flat and eµ−1(0) is reduced, irreducible and with rational singularities (property
( eP1)) — with Theorem 6.4 providing the analogous statement for modules of the quiver vertex
superalgebra. Having demonstrated these results, Theorem 6.5 then proves their applicability to
specific classes of quivers and dimension data.
The appendices contain the derivation of technical results, and some relevant vertex algebra
background. In particular, Appendix A reviews and generalizes the étale slice construction of
quiver varieties from [CB03], while Appendix B collects vertex algebra and vertex Poisson algebra
definitions.
