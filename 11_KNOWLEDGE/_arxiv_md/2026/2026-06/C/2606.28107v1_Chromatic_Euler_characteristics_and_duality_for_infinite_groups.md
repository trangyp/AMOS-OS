Chromatic Euler characteristics and duality for
infinite groups
Gijs Heuts and Irakli Patchkoria
June 29, 2026
Abstract
We study a family of generalizations of the notion of Euler character-
istic of discrete groups (or of orbifolds, depending on one’s perspective)
indexed on the natural numbers. For n = 0, this is the classical orbifold
Euler characteristic as studied by Wall and Serre, whereas for n ≥1 and
finite groups, this is the chromatic cardinality as studied by Ben-Moshe–
Carmeli–Schlank–Yanovski. For general n, we show that our generalized
Euler characteristic admits a natural interpretation in terms of the Morava
E-theories. Our work involves showing that the generalized cohomology
of infinite groups G with finite universal space for proper actions EG has
a good theory of duality, as expressed by a new duality functor on the cat-
egory of proper G-equivariant spectra. In particular, for such groups we
prove the vanishing of Klein’s generalized Farrell–Tate cohomology with
T(n)-local coefficients. We compute our generalized orbifold Euler char-
acteristics in a large number of examples. This includes many mapping
class groups, where the classical calculation is a result of Harer–Zagier,
and many arithmetic groups, whose classical orbifold Euler characteristics
were computed by Harder.

Introduction
The aim of this paper is to study generalizations of the notion of Euler char-
acteristic of a group (or of an orbifold) arising from the cohomology theories
used in chromatic homotopy theory (e.g., p-completed complex K-theory) and
compute them in a number of interesting examples. Our primary interest is in
groups G for which there exists a finite classifying space for proper actions EG.
In more detail, this EG should be a finite G-CW complex where each cell has
finite stabilizer and for each finite subgroup H ≤G, the fixed points EGH are
contractible. Important examples include arithmetic groups and mapping class
groups. The cohomology of such a group has strong finiteness properties (see
e.g., [Bro94, Theorem VIII.11.1]).

Part I: Duality and norm maps for infinite groups
The key ingredient in our construction of the E-theory orbifold Euler charac-
teristic of a group will be a good understanding of duality for the generalized
homology and cohomology of a group G with finite EG. Let C be a presentably
symmetric monoidal stable ∞-category.
(For us, this will typically be some
Bousfield localization of the ∞-category of spectra.) If X is an object of C with
G-action, then homology and cohomology of G with coefficients in X are related
through the norm map
NmBG : (X ⊗DBG)hG →XhG,
where DBG denotes an object of Fun(BG, C) called the dualizing complex. Such
norm maps feature in the classical Bieri–Eckmann theory of duality groups
[BE73] and have been generalized, in increasing levels of abstraction, by Klein
[Kle01, Kle02], Nikolaus–Scholze [NS18], and Cnossen [Cno23]. We will give a
review of the norm and the dualizing complex in Section 3, in which we also
include some material on the naturality of the norm that we were unable to find
in the literature.
A question of particular interest is when the norm map is invertible. Equiva-
lently, this is the question of when Farrell–Tate cohomology of G (defined to
be the cofiber of the norm) with coefficients in C vanishes. A simple example
is where G is finite and C is the derived category D(Q) of the rational num-
bers, or any commutative ring in which the order of G is invertible.
Many
more examples are given by the rational duality groups of Bieri–Eckmann (cf.
Remark 4.11). However, there exist more sophisticated settings where Farrell–
Tate cohomology vanishes. The ambidexterity results of Hopkins–Lurie [HL13]
and Carmeli–Schlank–Yanovski [CSY22] imply that the norm map is always an
equivalence if the group G is finite (in which case the dualizing complex DBG
turns out to be just the unit) and the ∞-category C is that of K(n)-local or
T(n)-local spectra. We extend this result to a larger class of groups as follows
(see Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 5.13):
Theorem 1.5. Let G be a group with finite stable EG and take C to be the
∞-category of T(n)- or K(n)-local spectra. Then for any X ∈Fun(BG, C), the
norm map (X ⊗DBG)hG →XhG is an equivalence in C.

The classifying space for proper actions
In this section we review and collect well-known material on the universal space
for proper G-actions EG, which we will need in later sections. Along the way
we take the opportunity to point out some important examples.
For a discrete group G, there are many flavors of G-equivariant homotopy theory.

Recall that an ∞-category C is called 1-semiadditive if it is pointed, finite prod-

Duality maps and duality spaces
Recall that a group Γ satisfying the FP-finiteness property is called a Bieri–
Eckmann duality group if there is a dualizing Γ-module DΓ and a number n
such that for any Z[Γ]-module M, there is an isomorphism
Hn−∗(Γ; DΓ ⊗Z M) ∼= H∗(Γ; M).
There are many equivalent characterizations of this condition [Bro94, Section
VIII.10]. In particular it implies that the number n is the cohomological dimen-
sion of Γ, the module DΓ is isomorphic to Hn(Γ; Z[Γ]), and
Hi(Γ; Z[Γ]) = 0 for all i ̸= n.
In this case it is known that DΓ is a torsion-free (possibly infinitely generated)
abelian group.
Any duality group is torsion-free. For groups which contain torsion, there is a
generalization of the notion of duality. We recall the following definition from
[Bro94, Section VIII.11]:
Definition 4.1. A group G is called a virtual duality group if it has a finite
index subgroup which is a Bieri–Eckmann duality group [BE73].
For G a virtual duality group, Hn(G; Z[G]) is torsion-free and Hi(G; Z[G]) = 0
for all i ̸= n.
The module Hn(G; Z[G]) serves as the dualizing module for
20


## Page 21


the finite index duality subgroup. The number n is the virtual cohomological
dimension of G [Bro94, Section VIII.11]. Examples of virtual duality groups
include arithmetic groups [BS73] and mapping class groups [Har86].
It turns out that if a group Γ is a duality group in the classical sense of Bieri–
Eckmann, then the dualizing complex DBΓ in the derived category C = D(Z)
is concentrated in a single cohomological degree, which equals the cohomolog-
ical dimension of Γ. Furthermore, the norm map NmBΓ is an equivalence and
induces the above isomorphism between cohomology and homology with coeffi-
cients in the dualizing module.
One of the main themes of this paper is that upon changing the coefficients from
D(Z) to ∞-categories such as SpT (n) or SpK(n), there are many more groups
that behave like ‘duality groups’ in the above sense. We extend the classical
terminology as follows:
Definition 4.2. A map f : X →Y is a C-duality map if the norm map Nmf is
an equivalence for every local system F ∈Fun(X, C). A space X is a C-duality
space if X →∗is a C-duality map.

ambidextrous map in [Cno23].
