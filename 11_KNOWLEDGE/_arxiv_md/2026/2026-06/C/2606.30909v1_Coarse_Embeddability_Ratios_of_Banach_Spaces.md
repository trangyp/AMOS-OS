Abstract. Given two Banach spaces X and E, one can associate a numerical invariant
CR(X, E), called the coarse embeddability ratio, which provides a criterion for coarse and
uniform embeddability. We compute the coarse embeddability ratio for several important
classes of Banach spaces, using various tools from the nonlinear theory of Banach spaces.
Finally, we find pairs of Banach spaces with arbitrarily large coarse embeddability ratio,
resolving an open problem of Rosendal in the negative.

1. Introduction
The traditional theory of Banach spaces was focused solely on their linear aspects. Indeed,
the most natural maps to consider between two vector spaces are of course linear maps.
However, one could also consider various other types of maps by “forgetting” various parts
of the Banach space structure, such as the linear structure, the norm structure, or even the
metric structure.
One of the first results concerning the nonlinear theory of Banach spaces is the Mazur-
Ulam theorem of 1932, which states that any surjective isometry between real Banach spaces
is an affine mapping [14]. Thus, linear structure of a Banach space is completely determined
by its metric structure (up to choice of which point is distinguished to be 0).
Several decades later, in 1967, Kadec proved that all separable infinite-dimensional Ba-
nach spaces are homeomorphic [10]. In 1982, Toruńczyk extended Kadec’s result to show
that any two Banach spaces with the same density character are homeomorphic [25]. Thus,
we see the linear structure of a Banach space is left completely undetermined by its topo-
logical structure.
These results suggest that somewhere in between keeping only topological structure and
keeping the entire metric structure, we could hope for various types of maps which preserve
some of the linear properties of Banach spaces. A big result in this direction is due to Ribe,
which states that a uniform homeomorphism (see Section 2 for definitions) between Banach
spaces preserve finite-dimensional subspaces [21].

Ribe’s theorem suggests that “local” (that is, “finite-dimensional”) properties of Banach
spaces should be preserved under uniform homeomorphism, and thus, should be able to
be formulated in purely metric terms. This led to the development of the Ribe program,
in which local properties are formulated in metric terms, and then studied in the broader
context of metric spaces and uniform embeddings.
In contrast to uniform embeddings, there is also a notion of coarse embedding, introduced
by Gromov in [7].
Coarse embeddings preserve “large-scale structure”, and are thus in
opposition to uniform embeddings. Despite this, the following problem has remained open.
Problem. Let X and Y be Banach spaces. Are the following equivalent:
(1) X coarsely embeds into Y .
(2) X uniformly embeds into Y .
In fact, neither direction of the above problem is known. Several partial and related
results have been obtained. In [12], Kalton provided an example of two separable Banach
spaces which are coarsely homeomorphic, but not uniformly homeomorphic. In [20], Ran-
drianarivony proved that if Y is a Hilbert space, then the above problem is resolved in the
positive.
In [22], Rosendal showed that if X uniformly embeds into Y , then there is a simultaneously
uniform and coarse embedding of X into ℓp(Y ) for all 1 ≤p < ∞. Thus, if there is a coarse
embedding from ℓp(Y ) into Y , then a uniform embedding from X into Y implies a coarse
embedding from X into Y . In the same paper, Rosendal showed that if X uniformly embeds
into closed unit ball BY of Y , then there is a uniformly continuous coarse embedding of
X into Y ⊕Y , providing another route to showing uniform embeddability implies coarse
embeddability.
Following this, in [23], Rosendal introduced the notion of a coarse embeddability ratio
between Banach spaces, and strengthened his previous result, showing that if X uniformly
embeds into Y and Y ⊕Y linearly embeds into Y , then X coarsely embeds into Y .
On the other hand, far less progress has been made in showing that a coarse embedding
implies a uniform embedding. In [18], Naor showed the existence of separable Banach spaces
X and Y , as well as a net N of X and a Lipschitz function f : N →Y such that for all
uniformly continuous maps F : X →Y ,
sup
x∈N
∥F(x) −f(x)∥= ∞.
This suggests that proving the existence of a uniform embedding given a coarse embedding
is perhaps less likely than the converse.
Here, we calculate several bounds on different coarse embeddability ratios between clas-
sical Banach spaces. In Section 2, we define separation ratios and coarse embeddability
ratios and derive some lower bounds on coarse embeddability ratios. In Section 3, we recall
Kalton’s property Q, and find an upper bound on the coarse embeddability ratio of a space
without property Q into a space with property Q. In Section 4, we establish upper bounds
on the coarse embeddability ratios from important classes of spaces into Tsirelson’s space,
a classical (counter)example in the linear theory. In Section 5, we find an upper bound on
the coarse embeddability ratio between Lp(µ) spaces. Finally, in Section 6, we resolve an
open problem of Rosendal.

3. Coarse Embeddability Ratios and Kalton’s Property Q

Banach space. During his investigations into this problem, he formulated the Q-property,

Definition 3.1. Let M ⊆N. Then for all r ∈N, [M]r denotes the collection of all subsets

in terms of metric structure.
