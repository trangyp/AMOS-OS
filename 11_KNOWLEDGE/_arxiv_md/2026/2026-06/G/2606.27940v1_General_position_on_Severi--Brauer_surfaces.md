GENERAL POSITION ON SEVERI–BRAUER SURFACES
JACK RITSCHEL
Abstract. The blowing-up of the projective plane at a finite set of points
yields a del Pezzo surface if and only if the points lie in general position. In
this note, we generalize this result to Severi–Brauer surfaces over arbitrary
ground fields.
Using Galois descent, intersection theory and combinatorial
arguments, we provide explicit arithmetic and geometric conditions on the
centre of the blowing-up.

Introduction
Smooth algebraic curves, surfaces, threefolds, etc., over a ground field k can be
broadly grouped into three classes, depending on whether their dualizing sheaf is
ample, trivial, or anti-ample. An algebraic surface X is called a del Pezzo surface
if it is smooth, proper, and its dualizing sheaf ωX is anti-ample. The most basic
example is the projective plane, with ωP2 = OP2(−3).
It is a well-understood problem to determine the conditions under which the
blowing-up BlZ(P2
k) →P2
k at r ≥1 rational points Z = {a1, . . . , ar} yields a del
Pezzo surface. It turns out the points must lie in a certain configuration, called
general position. We recall this in Theorem 1.1.
There exist twisted forms of projective space, the so-called Severi–Brauer vari-
eties. These are k-schemes for which there exists a field extension k ⊂L such that
the base change becomes isomorphic to Pn
L. When the Brauer group Br(k) of the
field is not trivial, there may exist many such non-isomorphic schemes over k. In
dimension two, we speak of Severi–Brauer surfaces.
Since the ampleness of invertible sheaves can be checked after base-change,
Severi–Brauer surfaces are always del Pezzo surfaces.
It is therefore natural to
ask how the classical criteria adapt to this setting. In this note, we determine the
conditions under which the blowing-up of a Severi–Brauer surface BlZ(X) →X at
a collection Z = {a1, . . . , ar} of r ≥1 closed points yields a del Pezzo surface.
Here we consider closed points endowed with their reduced induced subscheme
structure. We exclude non-reduced schemes as centres for blowing-ups, as this often
results in singular surfaces.

Severi–Brauer surfaces have different Chow groups CH(X) than the projective
plane. In particular, the degree of any closed point a ∈X is a multiple of 3 (see
Proposition 1.2). Consequently, one is forced to blow up at non-rational points,
which is a delicate matter.
Because blowing-up commutes with flat base change, we can pass to the sepa-
rable closure ksep, over which the surface Xksep ≃P2
ksep splits. Thus we determine
conditions under which the points u−1(Z) in the preimage of u : Xksep →X lie in
general position.
In the first section, we work out restrictions on Z = {a1, . . . , ar}. By Propo-
sition 1.5, the residue fields k ⊂κ(ai) must all be separable. It follows from the
classical version that the total degree of the points is bounded by 8, see Proposi-
tion 1.6. Since every point also has degree divisible by 3, this leaves only three
possibilities: Z = {a} may consist of a single point of degree 3 or 6, or Z = {a, a′}
consists of two points, of degree 3 each.
In the second section, we deal with these three cases. Using combinatorial ar-
guments and descent theory, we see that blowing up at a single point Z = {a}
of degree 3 or 6 always yields a del Pezzo surface (see Proposition 2.1). This was
shown already in [7]. We have included a proof because the arguments set up the
remaining case, which has not been dealt with before.
Namely, if Z = {a, a′}, the situation turns out to be more complicated. The
conditions now depend on the arithmetic of the residue fields, and the configuration
of the points in the preimage is more involved. In particular, a certain nodal curve
plays a distinguished role. It arises as follows: Any degree 3 point a ∈X has as
preimage a non-collinear triple. This triple yields a triangle Ca of lines through the
points. By descent, we get a nodal curve Ca ⊂X, whose properties we examine in
Proposition 2.5.
Summarizing, when the centre Z = {a1, . . . , ar} ⊂X is reduced and consists of
closed points, we arrive at the following conditions:
Theorem (see Thm. 2.6). Let X be a non-split Severi–Brauer surface over k. The
blowing-up BlZ(X) →X along the centre Z is a del Pezzo surface if and only if
the residue field of each point is separable and one of the following holds:
(i) Z = {a} consists of a degree-3 or 6 point.
(ii) Z = {a, a′} consists of two degree-3 points, with κ(a) ̸≃κ(a′).
(iii) Z = {a, a′} consists of two degree-3 points, with κ(a) ≃κ(a′) such that a′ /∈Ca
and a /∈Ca′.

2. Points in geometric general position
For a non-split Severi–Brauer surface X and Z = {a1, . . . , ar} as above, we wish
to understand whether BlZ(X) is a del Pezzo surface. Equivalently, we ask whether
the blowing-up of the pullback Blu−1(Z)(Xksep) along the preimage u−1(Z) is a del
Pezzo surface. To this end, we say that the points of Z lie in geometric general
position if the points in the preimage u−1(Z) ⊂Xksep lie in general position in the
sense of Theorem 1.1.
The possible blowing-up centres Z are highly restricted. Indeed, by Proposi-
tion 1.2 and Proposition 1.6, respectively, we have 3 | deg(ai), and the total degree
is at most 8. Thus, we are left with the following three possibilities: Z = {a} may
consist of a single point of degree 3 or 6, or Z = {a, a′} consists of two points of
degree 3 each. Further, by Proposition 1.5, the respective residue fields are always
separable.
Let us start with the simplest case, where Z = {a} consists of a degree-3 point.
We seek to understand whether the points in the preimage u−1(a) = {z1, z2, z3} ⊂
Xksep are collinear.
That is, we ask if they are contained in a line L ⊂Xksep.
We notice that the points in the preimage are permuted by the action of the
absolute Galois group.
More precisely, we obtain a permutation representation
Gal(ksep/k) →G with G = S3 or C3. Suppose L contains all three points. Then
it is G-stable and, by descent theory, the line L ⊂X is defined over k. This yields
an invertible sheaf OX(L) of degree 1, and X must be split by Proposition 1.2—a
contradiction.
Therefore, the points lie in general position.
Consequently, the blowing-up
Bla(X) is always a del Pezzo surface.

by symmetry, a /∈Ca′.
□
