Abstract. In this paper, we introduce generalized phase retrieval (brieﬂy, g-phase retrieval) and gen-
eralized norm retrieval (brieﬂy, g-norm retrieval). Then, we present some properties of these concepts
in Hilbert spaces, with special emphasis on Rn. Finally, the stability of g-norm retrieval for Rn is
proved.
1. Introduction
The concept of frames in a separable Hilbert space was originally introduced by Duﬃn and Schaeﬀer
in the context of non-harmonic Fourier series [5]. Frames are redundant sets of vectors that provide
a stable but non-unique representation for each vector in the space [1, 4, 6, 8, 7, 10, 9]. Generalized
frames (g-frames) and g-Riesz bases were introduced by Sun [10] and further developed in subsequent
works.
Phase retrieval and norm retrieval are among the most actively studied topics in applied harmonic
analysis. Phase retrieval for Hilbert space frames was introduced in [2] and has since become a signif-
icant area of research[3, 11]. In general, phase retrieval aims to recover a signal (up to a unimodular
constant, e.g., a sign in the real case or a complex phase in the complex case) from the magnitudes
of its measurements obtained through a redundant linear system. Norm retrieval, on the other hand,
focuses on recovering only the norm of the signal from such measurements.
Although much work has been devoted to complex inﬁnite-dimensional phase retrieval, only a few
papers have addressed inﬁnite-dimensional real phase retrieval or norm retrieval. In this paper, we
introduce two new generalizations: generalized phase retrieval (g-phase retrieval) and generalized norm
retrieval (g-norm retrieval). These notions extend the classical framework by replacing vectors with
bounded linear operators between Hilbert spaces, thus providing a uniﬁed approach that encompasses
both standard frames and g-frames.
We present several properties of g-phase retrieval and g-norm retrieval in general Hilbert spaces, with
a particular focus on the ﬁnite-dimensional Euclidean space Rn. Among the main contributions, we
establish the stability of g-norm retrieval for Rn. More precisely, we show that if a family of operators
performs g-norm retrieval up to a small error, then it is possible to construct a nearby family that
