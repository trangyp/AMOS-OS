Gravitational waves from graviton bremsstrahlung in scalar leptoquark
decays
Qian-Jiu Wang, Hua Tong, and Zhao-Huan Yu∗
School of Physics, Sun Yat-Sen University, Guangzhou 510275, China
We study the stochastic gravitational wave background originated from graviton
bremsstrahlung in decays of scalar leptoquarks, which are colored scalar bosons simulta-
neously coupling to a quark and a lepton. We take the scalar leptoquarks in the SU(5)
grand unified theory as a concrete example. Stringent experimental bounds on proton decay
force these particles to be superheavy, which in turn renders their graviton bremsstrahlung,
induced by quantum gravity effects, less suppressed. By solving the relevant Boltzmann equa-
tion, we trace the evolution of the scalar leptoquark number density in the early universe and
use it to compute the resulting gravitational wave spectrum. We find that high-frequency
gravitational wave detectors employing resonant cavity techniques offer a promising means
to probe such signals.

I.
INTRODUCTION
The discovery of gravitational waves (GWs) by the Laser Interferometer Gravitational Ob-
servatory (LIGO) in 2015 [1] opened a unique window onto the universe. GWs now serve as a
new cosmic messenger for exploring new physics beyond the standard model (SM). Unlike elec-
tromagnetic radiation, which was opaque in the early universe until photon decoupling, GWs
propagate freely through space, carrying pristine information from the earliest epochs directly
to modern observatories.
New physics theories typically predict heavy particles that were produced in the early uni-
verse. Their decays and scattering are expected to be accompanied by graviton bremsstrahlung
via quantum gravitational processes [2, 3], thereby inducing a stochastic GW background
(SGWB) that is potentially detectable. Relevant processes include inflaton decays [3–10], infla-
ton scattering [11, 12], decays of right-handed neutrinos during leptogenesis [13–15], freeze-in
production of dark matter [16–18], particle scattering in the thermal plasma [19–26], as well as
decays of superheavy particles from preheating [27, 28], from evaporation of primordial black
holes [29], from the type-II seesaw model [30], and from axion models [31].
The branching ratio for particle decays that emit a bremsstrahlung graviton is suppressed
by the square of the ratio of the parent particle mass to the Planck mass, within the effective
quantum field theory based on Einstein gravity [3]. Consequently, to achieve an appreciable
SGWB for GW observatories, the parent particle needs to be sufficiently heavy. For a parent
particle with a mass of 1012 GeV, the resulting SGWB spectrum is typically predicted to span
the frequency range of 107 to 1012 Hz [16, 31]. Such a prediction overlaps with the 105–109 Hz
sensitivity band of proposed high-frequency GW experiments that employ resonant cavity tech-
niques [32, 33]. Therefore, this provides a unique avenue for probing superheavy particles far
above the weak scale, a class of particles that is challenging to detect in conventional particle
physics experiments.
In this work, we focus on graviton bremsstrahlung in decays of scalar leptoquarks, which are
scalar bosons simultaneously coupling to a quark and a lepton [34]. They commonly appear in the
Higgs sectors of grand unified theories (GUTs) [35, 36]. Interactions of scalar leptoquarks violate
both lepton and baryon numbers, triggering proton decay. Consequently, stringent experimental
limits on proton decay have pushed their lower mass bounds to O(1011)–O(1013) GeV [37]. The
graviton bremsstrahlung from decays of such superheavy leptoquarks thus offers a promising
source for an SGWB.
As an illustrative case study, we consider the color-triplet scalar leptoquarks from the 5
representation in the SU(5) GUT. Their masses are constrained by experimental searches for
proton decay. We then compute the evolution of their number density in the early universe by
solving the Boltzmann equation. Based on this number density, the spectrum of the SGWB
generated via graviton bremsstrahlung in scalar leptoquark decays is estimated. Finally, we
assess the detection prospects for this SGWB at future GW observatories.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Sec. II, we describe the SU(5) GUT frame-
work. Section III discusses the constraints imposed by proton decay on the parameter space. In

II.
THE SU(5) SETUP
As the first attempt at grand unification, the Georgi-Glashow model [35] embeds the SM
gauge group SU(3)C ×SU(2)L ×U(1)Y into the SU(5) group, unifying quarks and leptons within
its irreducible representations. However, the minimal SU(5) realization has been ruled out due
to its incorrect predictions for fermion masses and mixing parameters, as well as its failure
to achieve precise gauge coupling unification.
To rescue the SU(5) GUT, extended versions
incorporating higher-dimensional operators [38–41] and additional fields [37, 42–51] have been
extensively explored.
In the work, our primary focus is on the properties of scalar leptoquarks, rather than on con-
structing a complete SU(5) GUT that satisfies all phenomenological requirements. To proceed,
we adopt a simple framework in which the masses of SM fermions are generated by Higgs fields in
the 5H and 45H representations, where the subscript H denotes Higgs representations. The vac-
uum expectation value (VEV) of a 24H Higgs multiplet at the GUT scale ∼O(1015) GeV breaks
the SU(5) gauge symmetry down to SU(3)C×SU(2)L×U(1)Y, while the VEVs of 5H and 45H at
the electroweak scale further break the gauge symmetry to SU(3)C ×U(1)EM. Three generations
of right-handed neutrinos are introduced as three fermionic SU(5) singlets 1F,i (i = 1, 2, 3), and
the tiny active neutrino masses are generated via the type-I seesaw mechanism [52–54]. This
constitutes a minimal, renormalizable setup capable of reproducing viable fermion masses and
mixing patterns [37, 42].

accompanied by graviton bremsstrahlung. The resulting graviton emissions produce an SGWB

As an explicit example, we considered the scalar leptoquarks ∆a contained in the 5H Higgs
multiplet of an minimal, renormalizable SU(5) GUT, which is capable of reproducing viable
fermion masses and mixing patterns. To obtain quantitative predictions, we performed a random
scan over the parameter space and impose current experimental limits on proton decay. We
found that the p →K+¯ν channel gives the strongest bound, excluding parameter points with
m∆≲1013 GeV. Among the parameter points that satisfy the proton decay bounds, we selected
three BPs with scalar leptoquark masses m∆= 1.15 × 1013,1.41 × 1014, and 1.42 × 1015 GeV for
further analysis.
We then computed the thermal evolution of the scalar leptoquark number density by solv-
ing the Boltzmann equation, including annihilation, coannihilation, and decay processes. The
resulting number density closely tracks its equilibrium value, because the total interaction rate
is larger than the Hubble rate throughout the regime of interest. The SGWB spectra originated
from graviton bremsstrahlung in scalar leptoquark decays are further derived.
Our analysis
shows that the SGWB spectra for the three BPs lie in the high-frequency range, peaking around
10 GHz. Importantly, a portion of these spectra falls within the sensitivity band of proposed
high-frequency GW detectors that employ resonant cavity techniques operating in the MHz to
GHz range. In particular, the resonant modes around 10–100 MHz offer promising detection
prospects for such signals.
