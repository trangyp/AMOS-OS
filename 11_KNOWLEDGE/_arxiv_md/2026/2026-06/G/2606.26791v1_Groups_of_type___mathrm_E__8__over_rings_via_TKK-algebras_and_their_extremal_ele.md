Abstract. Over any commutative ring containing 1

of type E8 that arise from the Tits–Kantor–Koecher (TKK) construction on a

pairs of extremal elements in L. When L arises from the TKK-construction,

show that this torsor is non-trivial, yielding isomorphic Lie algebras of type
E8 that are not graded isomorphic, as opposed to the behaviour over fields.
1. Introduction
In the setting of Lie groups, algebraic groups over fields, or affine group schemes
over commutative rings, groups of type E8 are the largest of the exceptional groups,
having dimension 248. Their study presents particular difficulties, largely owing to
the fact that they do not have non-trivial linear representations of low dimension,
a fact that sets them apart from the smaller exceptional groups of types G2, (D4),
F4, E6 and E7. This is a major reason why groups of type E8 are seen as “the most
exceptional”, as noted by Garibaldi in [Gar2], to which we refer for a historical
survey and outlook, particularly over fields.
One way to get around this difficulty is to construct varieties or schemes of lower
dimension that arise naturally in some sense, and on which groups of type E8 act.
This is the aim of the present work, set over commutative rings where 6 is invertible.
A significant class of groups of type E8 consists of automorphism groups of TKK-
algebras, i.e. algebras that arise from the TKK-construction, on a Brown algebra.
We use this to express the automorphism group of such an algebra L as a fibration
(more precisely a homogeneous space or torsor) over a certain scheme Y, with
fibres being torsors under groups of type E7. The scheme Y consists of pairs of
extremal elements in L, and part of the work is to view these elements from a
scheme theoretic perspective.
Actions of Lie groups of type E8 and their stabilizers have been studied in the
literature. Notably, [IY] and [YIS] describe, over R and C, the homogeneous space
G/H where G is the split or real compact Lie group of type E8, and H is of type
E7. This is indeed the kind of homogeneous space of interest to us, and the paper
[YIS] served as an inspiration for us, even though the setting is quite different. The
authors do express real exceptional Lie groups, their Lie algebras and their Killing
forms using nonassociative algebras, building on the work [Fre] of Freudenthal. In
[GKN], the authors, again over R and C, study non-linear actions of groups of type
E8 on 57-dimensional linear spaces. This was also a primer in our direction. On
the other hand, the general theory of flag varieties of algebraic groups provides a
conceptual framework but does not provide an explicit construction in terms of

Extremal elements in Lie algebras are elements spanning one-dimensional inner
ideals. An inner ideal in a Lie algebra L over R is an R-submodule I ⊆L such that
[I, [I, L]] ⊆I. The systematic study of inner ideals in Lie algebras was initiated by
Georgia Benkart in [Ben]. Every ideal in a Lie algebra is, a fortiori, an inner ideal,
but inner ideals exist plentifully also in simple Lie algebras. Extremal elements, i.e.
elements x satisfying [x, [x, L]] ∈Rx, have been studied in e.g. [CSUW] and [DMM].
They constitute a natural subset in any Lie algebra. Requiring extremal elements
to be unimodular removes the singularities, leaving us with a smooth scheme Y of
pairs of extremal elements which will play the key role in our parametrization.
The TKK-construction on Brown algebras over fields has been studied by various
authors since Allison in [All]. Rigby, in his PhD thesis [Rig], established the theory
in a modern language, using algebraic groups. Our presentation is based on his
treatment.
The theory of Brown algebras rests on that of Albert algebras, for
which the standard reference is [GPR2].
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls and lays out the preliminaries
of Brown algebras that are necessary for the TKK-construction on Brown algebras,
which is presented in Section 3. There, we also give an expression of the associative
bilinear form in terms of the TKK-construction, not assuming that 5 is invertible.
In Section 4, we prove the smoothness of the affine group schemes related to these
algebras and constructions. Section 5 introduces the functor of extremal elements
and establishes its basic properties, while in Section 6 we show that pairs of extremal

the point stabilizers are of type E7.
The torsors that this action provides are
studied in the final Section 7, where their twists are linked to gradings of the TKK-

5. Extremal elements
We start by establishing the basics of extremal elements in Lie algebras over
rings. We shall require these elements to be unimodular; recall that an element x
in an R-module M is unimodular if there is a linear form α : M →R such that
α(m) = 1. This requirement avoids degenerate cases and is, in this setting, the
natural generalization to rings of the property of being non-zero over fields.
Definition 5.1. Let L be a Lie algebra over R.
(i) An R-submodule I ⊆L is said to be an inner ideal of L if [I, [I, L]] ⊆I.
(ii) An element x ∈L is said to be extremal if x is unimodular and Rx is an inner
ideal of L.
Thus a unimodular element x ∈L is extremal if and only if for each y ∈L there
exists r = rx(y) ∈R such that [x, [x, y]] = rx(y)x. This r is unique, for if rx = r′x,
then r −r′ = 0 since it annihilates the unimodular element x. For each extremal
element x ∈L we thus have a linear map rx : L →R. Clearly rx(x) = 0.
Example 5.2. If L = sl2(R), with its usual Chevalley basis (e, f, h), then e and f
are extremal, and we have
