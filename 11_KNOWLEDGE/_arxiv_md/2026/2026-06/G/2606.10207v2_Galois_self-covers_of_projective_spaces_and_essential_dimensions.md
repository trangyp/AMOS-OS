1. Introduction
Throughout this article, we work over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic zero.
A surjective morphism f : Pn →Pn is called a Galois cover if the corresponding extension
of function fields is Galois with Galois group G (then G acts biregularly on Pn with quotient
being isomorphic to Pn).
For such a morphism, there is a unique integer q ≥1 such that
f∗OPn(1) ∼= OPn(q). In particular, deg(f) = qn. In this paper, we consider the nontrivial case
q > 1 and give a complete description of Galois self-covers of projective spaces.
Recall that a linear transformation in GL(n, k) is called a pseudoreflection if it fixes a hyper-
plane pointwise. Furthermore, a finite subgroup of GL(n, k) is called a pseudoreflection group if
it is generated by pseudoreflections. Shephard and Todd [20] provided a complete classification
of pseudoreflection groups over the complex numbers. By the Lefschetz principle, the same clas-
sification works over algebraically closed field k of characteristic zero. Our structure theorem

Now we study essential dimensions of Galois self-covers of Pn.
Essential dimension was
introduced in a modern context by Buhler and Reichstein [3, 4], and it has been studied for
decades (see [17] for more on its history). Recall that the essential dimension of a generically
finite (dominant rational) map f : X 99K Y , denoted by ed(f), is the smallest integer d such that
f is birational to the pullback of a map of varieties of dimension d (see [3] and Definition 2.5).
A generically finite map f : X 99K Y is called incompressible if its essential dimension attains
the maximum possible value dim(X) (cf. [3, 7, 11]).
It is known that the p-th power maps on toric varieties are even p-incompressible for all p,
see [19, Theorem 1.2] and [1, Proposition 10]. Hence, a direct consequence of Corollary 1.3 says
that non-trivial abelian self-covers of projective spaces are incompressible. In this paper (see
Section 5), we prove the following.

2. Preliminaries
We adopt the standard notation as in [8], [9], [12] and [16].
2.1. Reflection groups.
Definition 2.1. Let V be a finite-dimensional vector space over k. A nontrivial element σ ∈
GL(V ) of finite order is called a pseudoreflection if it fixes a hyperplane in V pointwise. A finite
subgroup Γ ⊂GL(V ) is called a pseudoreflection group if it is generated by pseudoreflections.
We shall use the following two standard theorems.
Theorem 2.2 (Chevalley–Shephard–Todd Theorem [20, 5]). Let V be a vector space of dimen-
sion n and let Γ ⊂GL(V ) be a finite group. Then k[V ]Γ is a polynomial algebra if and only
if Γ is generated by pseudoreflections. If k[V ]Γ = k[F1, . . . , Fn] with Fi homogeneous of degrees
di, then |Γ| = d1 · · · dn.
The di = deg Fi (1 ≤i ≤n) are called the invariant degrees of Γ and are, up to re-ordering,
independent of the choice of the generators Fi of the invariant ring (cf. [20, II. 5.1]).
