Global polarization of Λ, Ξ−, and Ω−hyperons in Au+Au collisions
at RHIC BES-II energies
Gen-Hui Li,1, ∗Cong Yi,2, † Xiang-Yu Wu,3, ‡ Shi Pu,1, 4, § and Guang-You Qin2, ¶
1Department of Modern Physics, University of Science and Technology of China, Anhui 230026, China
2Institute of Particle Physics and Key Laboratory of Quark and Lepton Physics (MOE),
Central China Normal University, Wuhan 430079, China
3Department of Physics, McGill University, Montreal, QC, Canada H3A 2T8
4Southern Center for Nuclear-Science Theory (SCNT), Institute of Modern Physics,
Chinese Academy of Sciences, Huizhou 516000, Guangdong Province, China
We investigate the global spin polarization of Λ hyperons and the multi-strange hyperons Ξ−and
Ω−in Au+Au collisions across the RHIC Beam Energy Scan II (BES-II) energy range, √sNN = 7.7–
27 GeV. The polarization is computed using the modified Cooper–Frye formula, which includes
contributions from thermal vorticity, the thermal shear tensor, and the gradient of the baryon
chemical potential, combined with the (3+1)-dimensional viscous hydrodynamic framework CLVisc
with SMASH initial conditions. We present the global polarization as a function of collision en-
ergy, centrality, transverse momentum, and rapidity. We find that the global polarization of Ω−
is systematically larger than those of Λ and Ξ−because of its larger spin quantum number, but it
remains below the central value of the recent STAR measurement. This discrepancy may suggest
that additional mechanisms, such as spin correlations among strange quarks inside the Ω−, could
contribute to the observed Ω−polarization. We also find that the global-polarization splitting be-
tween hyperons and anti-hyperons increases toward lower collision energies and is dominated by the
chemical-potential-gradient contribution.
I.
INTRODUCTION
In noncentral relativistic heavy-ion collisions,
the
quark-gluon plasma (QGP) created in the overlap re-
gion carries enormous orbital angular momentum and
behaves as the most vortical fluid observed so far, with
an angular velocity reaching up to 1022 s−1 [1].
Such
an immense vorticity can polarize the spins of final-state
hadrons along the direction of the initial orbital angu-
lar momentum through spin–orbit coupling [2, 3]. This
phenomenon is known as global spin polarization in rel-
ativistic heavy-ion collisions. Over the past decade, the
global polarization of Λ and ¯Λ hyperons has been ex-
tensively measured from a few GeV up to the TeV scale
and in different collision systems [1, 4–9]. On the the-
oretical side, numerical calculations based on the spin-
polarization vector derived from the quantum statisti-
cal model [10] or from quantum kinetic theory [11] have
described the collision-energy dependence of the global
polarization of Λ hyperons over √sNN = 7.7–200 GeV
[10–29].
At low collision energies, the global polariza-
tion of Λ hyperons is found to continue increasing as the
collision energy decreases [5, 30]. Although several stud-
ies have investigated the effects of vorticity [31–34], the
mechanism underlying the global polarization at such low
collision energies is still not fully understood. Very re-
cently, the global polarization of hypernuclei and protons

Although significant progress has been made, several
issues concerning global polarization remain to be un-

the collision-energy dependence of the global polariza-
tion, while its dependence on rapidity, centrality, and
transverse momentum still requires further investigation,
especially in view of future experimental measurements.
Second, it has been found that the polarization induced
by the shear tensor and by the gradient of the baryon
chemical potential [39–50], which are closely related to
spin Hall-type effects, can affect the polarization of hy-
perons along the beam direction [33, 51–63]. Therefore,
a natural question is how large the contributions from
shear-induced polarization and spin Hall effects are to
the global polarization. Third, the polarization of multi-
strange hyperons [64] has also attracted considerable at-
tention. These particles provide a useful probe of whether
spin correlations [65–84] exist among strange quarks in-
side a hyperon and, if so, how such correlations affect
the observed global polarization. This remains an open
issue that calls for systematic theoretical investigation.
Finally, the origin of the global-polarization splitting be-
tween hyperons and anti-hyperons remains unclear. Be-
sides the splitting induced by electromagnetic fields [85–
89], gradients of the chemical potential can also con-
tribute to this splitting [28, 46]. Therefore, it is impor-
tant to clarify the relative contributions of these different
mechanisms.
Interestingly, the high-statistics data from the RHIC
Beam Energy Scan II (BES-II) [91] are expected to pro-
vide more precise measurements of both the global polar-
ization of multi-strange hyperons. Therefore, it is timely

III.
NUMERICAL SETUPS
In this section, we introduce the numerical setup used
in this work. We implement the (3+1)-dimensional hy-
drodynamic framework CLVisc with SMASH initial con-
ditions to simulate the global spin polarization of Λ,
Ξ−, and Ω−hyperons in 20–50% Au+Au collisions at
√sNN = 7.7–27 GeV.
Following our previous works [28, 102], the initial col-
lision geometry and energy deposition are described by
the hadronic transport model Simulating Many Accel-
erated Strongly-interacting Hadrons (SMASH) [95]. We
collect all particles at the hydrodynamic initial proper
time, which is approximated by the average nuclear over-
lap time as
τ0 = 2R/
q

(8)
Here, R = 6.38 fm is the average radius of the Au nucleus
in the Woods–Saxon distribution, √sNN is the collision
energy, and mN is the nucleon mass.
We then apply
Gaussian smearing with a normalization factor K and
smearing widths σr and ση in the transverse and longi-
tudinal directions, respectively [28, 102], to generate the
initial energy-momentum tensor T µν
0

the (3+1)-dimensional hydrodynamic framework CLVisc
through the coupled energy-momentum and particle con-

In this work, we neglect bulk viscosity and set the
baryon-diffusion coefficient to CB = 0 for simplicity. We
treat the specific shear viscosity Cηv as a free parameter.
These parameters are tuned to reproduce soft-particle
yields and transverse-momentum spectra at √sNN =
7.7, 19.6, and 27 GeV. We find that a single set of
Gaussian smearing parameters, K, σr, and ση, is suf-
ficient to describe the spectra at all three collision ener-
gies, suggesting that the hadronic transport model pro-
vides a reasonable description of the initial energy de-
position.
This is consistent with the parameters used
with the Ultra-Relativistic Quantum Molecular Dynam-
ics (UrQMD) initial conditions in Ref. [103]. We there-
fore adopt the common Gaussian smearing parameters in
our simulations, while choosing the same specific shear
viscosity Cηv as that used in Ref. [103]. All parameters
used in the simulations are listed in Table I. Further de-
tails about the model and parameter choices can be found
in Refs. [28, 92–95, 102].

BQS equation of state [104, 105], which smoothly con-

IV.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
We now discuss our results for the global polarization
of Λ, Ξ−, and Ω−hyperons as functions of collision en-
ergy, centrality, transverse momentum, and rapidity.
In Fig. 1(a), we show the global polarization PH as
functions of collision energy for Λ, Ξ−, and Ω−hyper-
ons We first observe that the global polarization of all
three hyperon species increases as the collision energy de-
creases. This trend can be understood as a consequence
of stronger net-baryon stopping, which deposits a larger
fraction of the initial angular momentum into the pro-
duced medium, together with the smaller particle multi-
plicity near midrapidity at lower collision energies.
We next examine the global polarization of each hy-
peron species. For Λ and Ξ−hyperons, since they have
similar masses and the same spin quantum number, their
primary polarizations computed from Eq. (1) are close
to each other.
On the other hand, according to Ref.
[16, 18, 106, 107], feed-down effects enhance the Ξ−po-
larization while reducing the Λ polarization. After in-
cluding feed-down effects, our results for the global po-
larization of Ξ−hyperons are consistent with the exper-
imental data, while those for Λ hyperons are close to
the data but slightly smaller at intermediate collision en-
ergies.
We now discuss the global polarization of the
Ω−hyperon. Since the spin of the Ω−hyperon is 3/2,
Eq. (1) gives the simple estimate PΩ≈5PΛ/3 if the mass
difference between Ω−and Λ and feed-down effects are
neglected [2, 66, 67, 74]. Our numerical results are con-
sistent with this estimate and show an enhancement of
the global polarization of Ω−. Nevertheless, our results
remain well below the experimental central value, simi-
lar to earlier studies based on kinetic approaches [107].
This discrepancy may indicate that additional mecha-
nisms, such as two- and three-particle spin-spin correla-
tions among strange quarks inside the Ω−hyperon, could
contribute to the observed Ω−polarization [66, 67, 74].
In Fig. 1(b), we present the global-polarization split-
ting ∆PH as a function of collision energy. We find that
anti-hyperons systematically carry a larger global polar-
ization than the corresponding hyperons. This difference

In Fig. 2, we observe that the centrality dependence
of the global polarization of hyperons at intermediate
collision energies is similar to that found at high colli-
sion energies [6, 7, 9, 64]. Namely, the polarization in-
creases toward more peripheral collisions. This behav-
ior can be attributed to the larger vorticity generated
by stronger velocity gradients in more peripheral colli-
sions, which enhances the global polarization. We also
find that, at a fixed centrality, the global polarization be-
comes larger as the collision energy decreases, consistent
with the collision-energy dependence shown in Fig. 1.
In Fig. 3, we observe that the global polarization de-
creases with increasing pT . This trend is consistent with
that observed in the experimental data. In Fig. 4, we also
find that the global polarization of hyperons is nearly flat
around midrapidity |Y | ∈[0, 1] and then increases rapidly
with increasing |Y | for |Y | ≥1.
Comparing the global polarization of different hyper-
ons in Figs. 2, 3, and 4, we observe that PΞ >∼PΛ and
PΩ≃5PΛ/3 at fixed collision energy, centrality, pT ,
and Y , consistent with the spin and mass dependence
in Eq. (1).
V.
SUMMARY
In this work, we have studied the global spin polar-
ization of Λ, Ξ−, and Ω−hyperons in 20–50% Au+Au
collisions over √sNN = 7.7–27 GeV, using the mod-
ified Cooper–Frye formula in Eq. (1) combined with
the (3+1)-dimensional viscous hydrodynamic framework
CLVisc with SMASH initial conditions.
We first show the global polarization of hyperons, PH,
as a function of collision energy in Fig. 1(a). We find
that the global polarization of all three hyperon species
increases as the collision energy decreases, in agreement
with the trend observed by STAR. We observe a slight
difference between the global polarizations of Ξ−and Λ
due to feed-down effects. In particular, feed-down effects
enhance the global polarization of Ξ−but reduce that
of Λ.
The global polarization of Ω−is systematically
larger than those of Λ and Ξ−owing to its larger spin
quantum number. Nevertheless, our result for the global
polarization of Ω−still underestimates the central value
of the recent STAR measurement. This discrepancy may
indicate the presence of two- and three-particle short-
range spin correlations among the strange quarks inside
the Ω−.
In Fig. 1(b), we also present the global-polarization
splitting ∆PH as a function of collision energy. We find
that the global polarization of anti-hyperons is larger
than that of the corresponding hyperons. The splitting
increases toward lower collision energies and is dominated
by the chemical-potential-gradient contribution. Thus,
the spin Hall effect can influence the global polarization
in a way qualitatively similar to the electromagnetic-field
contribution.
Finally, we plot the global polarization of hyperons as
functions of centrality, transverse momentum pT , and ra-
pidity Y in Figs. 2, 3, and 4, respectively. For all three
hyperon species, the global polarization increases to-

across the RHIC BES-II energy range. They also suggest
that short-range spin correlations among strange quarks
inside the Ω−may be a promising direction for future
theoretical and experimental investigation.
