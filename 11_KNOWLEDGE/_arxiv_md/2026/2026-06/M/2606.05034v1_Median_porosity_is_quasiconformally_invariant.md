MEDIAN POROSITY IS QUASICONFORMALLY INVARIANT
TERO KILPEL¨AINEN AND ANTTI V. V¨AH¨AKANGAS
Abstract. A set in Rn is median porous if the logarithm of its distance function
has bounded mean oscillation. We show that this property is preserved under qua-
siconformal mappings. In particular, median porosity is quasiconformally invariant.
We also show that the stronger notion of weak porosity, by contrast, is not quasi-
conformally invariant.
1. Introduction
A set E ⊂Rn is porous if every ball contains a smaller ball, with comparable
radius, that does not touch E. Recently, two weaker notions of porosity have been
characterized in terms of the distance function δE = dist(·, E): a set E is weakly porous
(Definition 4.2) if and only if δ−α
E
∈A1 for some α > 0 [17, 2], and median porous (in
the sense of [10]) if and only if δ−α
E
∈A∞for some α > 0. These notions satisfy
E is porous =⇒E is weakly porous =⇒E is median porous ,
and both implications are strict [2, 10]. See also [6] for related characterizations of Ap
distance weights.
It follows from standard distortion theorems that entire quasiconformal mappings
preserve porosity; V¨ais¨al¨a [16] extended this to quasisymmetric mappings defined only
on the porous set itself. In this note we prove that median porosity is invariant under
entire quasiconformal mappings. We also show that weak porosity is not quasiconfor-
mally invariant (see Theorem 4.1).
Theorem 1.1. Let n ≥2 and let f : Rn →Rn be a quasiconformal mapping.
If
E ⊂Rn is median porous, then f(E) is median porous.
The median porosity of E is equivalent to log δE ∈BMO(Rn) by the standard
characterization of A∞; see [5] and [10, Theorem 1.5]. Thus Theorem 1.1 reduces
to showing that this property is preserved under quasiconformal mappings; we will
establish this in Theorem 3.3, which is a quantitative variant of Theorem 1.1. We will
employ Reimann’s BMO isomorphism theorem [11], which yields log δE ◦f −1 ∈BMO.
In general this function does not coincide with log δf(E). However, the resulting defect
log δf(E) −log δE ◦f −1
is shown to lie in BMO by using Astala–Gehring’s quasiconformal Koebe distortion
theorem [3] together with a variable-radius averaging theorem for BMO. The last
result is established in Section 2 (Theorem 2.4) and applied in Section 3 to complete
the proof of Theorem 1.1.
