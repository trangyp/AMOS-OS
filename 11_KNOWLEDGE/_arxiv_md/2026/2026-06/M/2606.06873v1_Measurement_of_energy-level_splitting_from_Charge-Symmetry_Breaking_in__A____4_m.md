Measurement of energy-level splitting from Charge-Symmetry
Breaking in A = 4 mirror hypernuclei
The STAR Collaboration
Abstract
Breaking of fundamental symmetries is a ubiquitous phenomenon in physics, underlying the origin
of mass and the emerging structure in the universe. The charge symmetry of Λ hyperon–nucleon
interactions can be probed through the difference in the Λ binding energy (BΛ) between mirror
hypernuclei. In this paper, the BΛ of mirror hypernuclei with atomic mass number A = 4, 4
ΛH and
4
ΛHe, are measured in Au+Au collisions at the center-of-mass energy of √sNN = 3 GeV with the
STAR experiment at RHIC. For the ground states, we obtain BΛ(4
ΛH) = 2.24 ± 0.02 (stat.) ± 0.04
(syst.) MeV and BΛ(4
ΛHe) = 2.39 ± 0.05 (stat.) ± 0.05 (syst.) MeV, yielding a charge-symmetry
breaking (CSB) effect at the level of 0.15 ± 0.05 (stat.) ± 0.04 (syst.) MeV. In combination with
previous measurements of γ-ray transitions from their 1+ excited states, the CSB in excited states
is determined to be −0.17 ± 0.05 (stat.) ± 0.04 (syst.) MeV. These measurements provide a precise
determination of CSB in the hypernuclear system, and establish that the Λ binding energy differences
in ground and excited states are comparable in magnitude but opposite in sign, offering new insight
to the CSB effect in Λ–nucleon interactions.
In nuclear physics, charge symmetry [1, 2] per-
tains to the invariance of the nuclear force under
interchange of proton and neutron. It is a fun-
damental feature of the strong interaction and
closely related to the isospin symmetry [3, 4]
in which the proton and neutron are two states
of the nucleon (N) with isospin I
=
1
2. If
charge symmetry were exact, a pair of mirror
nuclei, whose proton and neutron numbers are
swapped, would exhibit identical binding energies
once electromagnetic contributions are removed.
Deviations from this expectation provide a direct
probe of charge symmetry breaking (CSB). A
well-known example arises from triton (t) and
3He, whose binding energy difference after sub-
tracting the Coulomb contribution is B(3He) −
B(t) ≈67 ± 9 keV [5], a clear manifestation of
CSB. This CSB in nucleon-nucleon (NN) inter-
actions can be partially attributed to the mixing
of the rho and omega measons, originating from
the mass difference between the up and down
quarks [1, 5].
The Λ hypernuclei provide a unique labora-
tory where the role of CSB in the Λ–nucleon (ΛN)
interaction can be directly investigated [6–9], as
they contain both nucleons and a Λ hyperon. The
Λ binding energy (BΛ, also called Λ separation
energy) of a hypernucleus is defined as:
BΛ = (mcore + mΛ −mhyp)c2,
(1)
in which the mcore, mΛ, and mhyp are the
masses of the nucleon core, the Λ hyperon, and
the hypernucleus, respectively. This observable
directly relates to the ΛN interaction and provides
constraints on hyperon-nucleon (Y N) interactions
that enter neutron star equation-of-state calcu-
lations [10]. For the pair of mirror hypernuclei
with atomic mass number A = 4, 4
ΛH and 4
ΛHe,
whose nucleon cores are t and 3He, respectively,
they should have nearly the same BΛ under charge
symmetry, since the charge-neutral Λ hyperon
minimizes the Coulomb effect [11].

Measurements of Λ binding energies for hyper-
nuclei also provide important inputs for astro-
physical studies. For example, the authors of
Refs. [23, 24] calculated the equation of state and
the mass–radius relation of neutron stars by intro-
ducing a ΛNN three-body force, constrained by
the measured Λ binding energies of hypernuclei.
They demonstrated that different experimental
constraints, particularly those from light hypernu-
clei,significantly affect the predicted neutron star
mass [23, 24].
In this paper, we present a new high-precision
measurement based on a large dataset of approx-
imately 2 billion events collected in 2021. The
experimental setup consists of a stationary gold
target with 0.25 mm thickness mounted inside
the beam pipe and approximately 2 m away from
the center of the STAR Time Projection Cham-
ber (TPC) [25]. A gold beam with an energy
of 3.85 GeV per nucleon hits the gold target
with the produced particles flying towards the
STAR detector.
4
ΛH and 4
ΛHe produced in col-
lisions are reconstructed through their 2-body,
4
ΛH →4He + π−, and 3-body, 4
ΛHe →3He + p +
π−, decay channels, respectively. Identification of
decay daughters is achieved primarily by mea-
suring their mean ionization energy loss per unit
length ⟨dE/dx⟩in the TPC. A solenoidal magnetic
field of 0.5 T, aligned along the beam direction,
enables the determination of particle momenta
based on the curvature of their helical tracks in the
TPC. Additionally, velocity measurements based
on the Time-of-Flight (TOF) detector [26, 27] are
used to identify particle species when they have
left signals in TOF.
The decay topology of the parent hypernucleus
is reconstructed from the identified decay daugh-
ters by using the KFParticle [28] package based
on the Kalman filter. The covariance matrices of
momenta and spatial coordinates of tracks are
used to calculate the χ2 between two tracks or one
track to the collision vertex at the points of dis-
tance of closest approach (DCA). Selections based
on the χ2 of different DCAs are applied to increase
the significance of the reconstructed signal. For the
4
ΛHe →3He+p+π−decay, a cut on χ2 between the
p + π−pair and the collision vertex is introduced
to suppress contamination from Λ random coin-
cidences. The hypernucleus invariant mass is cal-
culated as mhyp =
p

The new STAR measurements indicate that
the Λ binding-energy differences between 4
ΛH and
4
ΛHe in their ground and excited states have sim-
ilar magnitudes with opposite signs (∆B4
Λ(1+)≈
−∆B4

level splitting from the CSB effect (see Methods
for more details). In quantum mechanics, when
a symmetry is broken in a system that has only
two separated energy levels with a double-well
potential, or equivalently, when an off-diagonal
perturbation is added to a Hamiltonian diago-
nalized in a two-dimensional space, the resulting
energy shifts of the two energy levels should have
opposite signs and similar magnitudes [47]. Apply-
ing this concept to the A = 4 hypernuclear
system, the Λ binding energy shifts of 4
ΛHe rel-
ative to 4
ΛH in their ground and excited states
should exhibit opposite signs and similar magni-
tudes under CSB. The new STAR measurements
align with this expectation.

The new STAR measurements presented here
achieve substantially higher precision than those
obtained from the 2018 data [21], providing strong
inputs for theoretical studies. As suggested in
Ref. [49], the measured CSB in 4

be used to constrain the scattering length of the
Λ-neutron interaction, which is difficult to mea-
sure directly by scattering experiment. A recent
review [8] emphasizes that the approach used in
Ref. [49] should be repeated with new data, with
proper consideration of experimental uncertain-
ties. The measured CSB can also be used to
predict the Λ binding energies and CSB of heavy
hypernuclei. The authors of Ref. [50] and Ref. [8]
calculated the CSB in A = 7 and 8 hypernuclei
with an ab-initio no-core shell model plus CSB
ΛN potential constrained by measured Λ bind-
ing energies and their difference in 4

ΛHe.

dependent components of CSB that underpin
modern descriptions of light hypernuclei [8]. In
doing so, they reduce longstanding model ambi-
guities associated with Λ-Σ mixing [52, 53], the
interplay of two- and three-body hyperon–nucleon
forces [23, 24, 54], and the contact versus spin-
dependent contributions required to reproduce the
A = 4 level structure [40, 41]. As the Y NN inter-
action inferred from light-hypernuclear separation
energies also provides the repulsive contribution
needed to counteract the softening induced by
the inclusion of the Λ hyperons at the high den-
sities, such as inside neutron stars, the present
results further restrict the range of Y N and
Y NN strengths consistent with few-body data.
Together, these advances strengthen the empirical
foundation for assessing hyperon effects in dense
matter and sharpen the constraints relevant to
proposed resolutions of the hyperon puzzle [23, 24,
55, 56].

A.1 Energy loss correction
When particles from the collisions travel through
the detector materials, they lose energy, resulting
in the measured momenta being smaller than the
true values. Energy loss corrections for the decay
daughters are therefore needed for accurate recon-
struction of hypernuclei. During the official data
production at STAR, the energy losses of tracks
have been corrected by assuming all the tracks are
pions. Thus additional energy loss corrections are
needed for the momenta of 4He, 3He, and proton in
this work. The correction method is similar to the
one used in Ref. [21]. 4
ΛH and 4
ΛHe samples, gener-
ated by Monte Carlo and propagated through the
GEANT3 simulation of the STAR detector geom-
etry and material, are embedded into real data
and reconstructed using the same software as data
production. The momentum shift of each particle
track can be determined by calculating the dif-
ference between the measured momentum value
pmeas and the corresponding truth value preal from
Monte Carlo.
As an example, the open circles in Fig. 5 (a)
show the average momentum shift pmeas - preal
versus pmeas of 4He in the center-of-mass rapid-
ity region −1 < ycm < 0 from simulation. The
energy loss effect is significant when the momen-
tum is low, causing the measured 4
ΛH mass in the
low transverse momentum (pT) region to be larger
than that in the high pT region, shown as the cir-
cles in Fig. 5 (b). The open circles in Fig. 5 (a)
are fitted with the correction function:
pmeas −preal = δ0 + δ


m2

(2)
where m is the mass of 4He, and δ0, δ, and α
are fitting parameters. In this work, the momen-
tum shifts are extracted and fit in ten rapidity
bins with an equal interval of 0.1 in the region
−1 < ycm < 0. The pmeas of particles in these
rapidity regions are corrected with the correspond-
ing correction functions obtained by fitting and
then used in the reconstruction of hypernuclei.
Shown as the open triangles in Fig. 5 (a), the
average momentum shift of 4He is approximately
zero after energy loss corrections. Consequently,
the extracted 4
ΛH mass is independent of 4
ΛH pT,
as illustrated in Fig. 5 (b).
A.2 Systematic uncertainties
The systematic uncertainties of this measurement
arise from the corrections on track momentum and
energy loss, as well as variations in decay topology
selection criteria.
A conservative absolute uncertainty of ±0.03%
is determined for the correction factor of 99.8% on
the track momentum. This is based on the resid-
ual difference of the measured K0
S mass to the
PDG value after applying the correction factor as
well as its statistical uncertainty. Half of the maxi-
mum changes in the measured hypernuclei masses
resulting from this source are taken the systematic
uncertainties.
In simulations, the extracted masses for Λ,
4
ΛH and 4
ΛHe show almost identical deviations, at
the level of 0.03 MeV/c2, to the input values after
applying energy loss corrections on proton, 4He
and 3He. This suggests that Λ can be used as a
benchmark to evaluate the performance of energy
loss corrections on the measurements of 4
ΛH and
4
ΛHe. The measured Λ mass in data, after track
momentum and energy loss corrections, deviates
from the PDG value within ±0.01 MeV/c2. To be
conservative, it is propagated to the systematic
uncertainties of 4
ΛH and 4
ΛHe by scaling it with
the ratio of the difference between the measured
hypernuclei masses with and without energy loss
corrections to this difference of the Λ masses.
The systematic uncertainties from decay topol-
ogy reconstruction are evaluated by varying the
selection criteria. Half of the maximum resulting
changes in the measured masses are taken as the
uncertainties. These systematic uncertainties are
summarized in Table 1 and added in quadrature
to obtain the total uncertainties.
The systematic uncertainties from the fit pro-
cedure, bin width, and the centrality calibration
are found to be negligible.
For the binding-energy difference, the sys-
tematic uncertainties from track momentum and
energy loss corrections cancel, while uncertainties
from decay topology reconstruction and measure-
ments of γ-ray energies for excited states [15] are
added in quadrature. Systematic uncertainties for
the binding-energy difference are summarized in
Table 2.
