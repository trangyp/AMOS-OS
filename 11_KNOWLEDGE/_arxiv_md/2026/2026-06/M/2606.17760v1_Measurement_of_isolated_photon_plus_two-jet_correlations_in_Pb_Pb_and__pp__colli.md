This paper presents a measurement of photon plus two-jet events in 𝑝𝑝and Pb+Pb collisions,
i.e. events in which the transverse momentum of a single photon is balanced by two distinct jets.
The measurement was performed using 𝑝𝑝data taken in 2017 with an integrated luminosity
260 pb−1, and Pb+Pb data taken in 2018 with an integrated luminosity 1.72 nb−1, both at
√𝑠NN =5.02 TeV, as recorded by the ATLAS detector. Events with photons in the transverse
momentum range 90–180 GeV and at least two anti-𝑘𝑡𝑅= 0.2 jets with a 𝑝T > 30 GeV
are selected, and three observables are measured: xJJ𝛾, AJJ𝛾, and Δ𝑅JJ. These observables
characterise the overall energy loss of the multiparton system from medium interactions (xJJ𝛾),
the relative energy loss between the two colour-charge carriers (AJJ𝛾), and medium-induced
modifications to their opening angle (Δ𝑅JJ). The observables are corrected for uncorrelated
combinatoric background contributions using a novel multijet mixing technique, for photon
purity, and for detector resolution effects via iterative unfolding. Final results are presented
per photon, and the ratio (𝐼AA) is taken between measurements in Pb+Pb and 𝑝𝑝collisions,
for Pb+Pb centrality intervals of 30–80%, 10–30%, and 0–10%. Significant suppression
of per photon two-jet yields in all three observables, 𝐼AA < 1, is observed as a result of
parton-medium interactions. The experimental measurements are compared to three different
jet quenching models: Jewel, Jetscape, and Lbt.

1 Introduction
Collisions of ultra-relativistic heavy ions at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) and the Relativistic Heavy
Ion Collider (RHIC) produce a state of matter called the quark–gluon plasma (QGP) where quarks and
gluons exist outside of the colour-neutral configurations that define typical cold matter. Studies of this
hot-and-dense state of deconfined partons have revealed many interesting characteristics: collective motion
well described by relativistic hydrodynamic flow, sequential suppression of bound quarkonia states, and
the suppression of observed final-state products such as jets from hard-scattered partons [1]. This last
phenomenon was first observed at RHIC with the study of large transverse momentum (𝑝T) hadrons [2]
and later at the LHC with fully reconstructed jets [3]. It is interpreted as the result of the colour-opaque
medium interacting with and attenuating the energy of these energetic quarks and gluons produced in the
initial collision before the formation of the medium, a process referred to as jet quenching. For a recent
review of jet quenching studies in the QGP at both colliders, see Ref. [4].
Measurements of jet quenching are a key part of understanding the structure and nature of the QGP. By
comparing the kinematics of jets produced in Pb+Pb collisions to the well-measured characteristics of
jets produced in pp collisions, the induced modifications from the QGP interacting with the developing
parton shower can be studied. While many studies of modified jet production have been performed [5–11],
there are unresolved questions on the exact nature of parton–medium interactions. These include questions
on how much energy is lost to interactions with medium partons, how the energy loss depends on parton
flavour, and whether there is a minimum separation between colour charges before the medium can resolve
them as separate and independent [12–14].
The measurements presented here address these questions by studying events containing an isolated photon
with its transverse momentum balanced by at least two distinct jets, 𝛾+ 2 jets + 𝑋. As the photon has
no colour charge, it sets the scale of the initial hard scattering that produced it and the balancing two-jet
system, without any biases on this selection from quenching effects. Photon plus two-jet measurements
were previously reported by ATLAS in pp collisions at 13 TeV [15]. Measurements of photon-tagged
inclusive jets, i.e. 𝛾+ 1 jet + 𝑋, in pp and Pb+Pb collisions, were performed previously by ATLAS [16,
17] and CMS [18]. This analysis presents the first photon plus two-jet measurement in Pb+Pb collisions.

The multijet system can produce one quark and one gluon jet opposite the photon, such that measurements
of the jet–jet energy asymmetry probe if different colour charges have different interactions with the
medium. The measurement is also sensitive to fluctuations in the energy loss as the two jets traverse similar
regions of QGP, in contrast to the case for back-to-back dijets. Finally, studies as a function of the angle
between the jet pair can be used to probe the existence of the colour coherence regime. Although some
estimates predict such coherence effects only at very small angular separations, and there are existing
measurements probing that phase space [14, 19, 20], the new data presented in this paper can provide
constraints on possible effects at larger angular separation.

photon in azimuth. Within a given photon+jet pair, “1” or “leading” denotes the jet of higher 𝑝T, and “2”

photon (AJJ𝛾), and the angle between the jet pair (Δ𝑅JJ), in events containing a photon and at least two jets.

2 ATLAS detector
The ATLAS experiment [21] at the LHC is a multipurpose particle detector with a forward–backward
symmetric cylindrical geometry and a near 4𝜋coverage in solid angle. It consists of an inner tracking
detector surrounded by a thin superconducting solenoid providing a 2 T axial magnetic field, electromagnetic
and hadron calorimeters, and a muon spectrometer. The inner tracking detector covers the pseudorapidity
range |𝜂| < 2.5. It consists of silicon pixel, silicon microstrip, and transition radiation tracking detectors.
Lead/liquid-argon (LAr) sampling calorimeters provide electromagnetic (EM) energy measurements
with high granularity. A steel/scintillator-tile hadron calorimeter covers the central pseudorapidity range
(|𝜂| < 1.7). The endcap and forward regions are instrumented with LAr calorimeters for both the EM and
hadronic energy measurements up to |𝜂| = 4.9. The muon spectrometer surrounds the calorimeters and is
based on three large superconducting air-core toroidal magnets with eight coils each. The field integral of
the toroids ranges between 2.0 and 6.0 T m across most of the detector. The muon spectrometer includes
a system of precision tracking chambers and fast detectors for triggering. Two zero-degree calorimeters
(ZDCs) were situated at |𝜂| > 8.3 during Pb+Pb data-taking and is mostly sensitive to spectator neutrons
from the fragmenting nuclei in Pb+Pb collisions.
A two-level trigger system is used to select events. The first-level trigger is implemented in hardware
and uses a subset of the detector information to accept events at a rate below 100 kHz. This is followed
by a software-based trigger that reduces the accepted event rate to 1 kHz on average depending on the
data-taking conditions. An extensive software suite [22] is used in data simulation, in the reconstruction
and analysis of real and simulated data, in detector operations, and in the trigger and data acquisition
systems of the experiment.
3 Data selection, reconstruction, and simulation
The data samples, physics object reconstruction, and simulation samples used in this measurement are
identical to those used in a previous measurement of photon-tagged jet production [17], and are briefly
summarised here.
Events in data are selected using triggers requiring a reconstructed photon with 𝑝T above 35 GeV (20 GeV)
in pp (Pb+Pb) collisions [23, 24]. These triggers sample the full luminosity delivered in both 2017 𝑝𝑝and
2018 Pb+Pb data and are fully efficient for the photon selection used in this analysis. Events containing
multiple Pb+Pb interactions within the same or nearby bunch crossings are rejected using an offline
selection based on the correlation between signals measured in the forward calorimeters and the ZDCs.
Photons are reconstructed following the method used previously in Pb+Pb measurements [16, 25]. This
procedure is the same as that used in 𝑝𝑝collisions [26] after an event-by-event estimate and subtraction of
the UE contribution to the energy deposited in each calorimeter cell [27]. Photon candidates must satisfy
shower-shape requirements [28] designed to reject photons from neutral meson decays and other hadrons.
Photon candidates must also be isolated by requiring an upper limit of 3 GeV on the sum of the transverse
energy (after subtracting the contributions from the photon itself and the UE) in calorimeter cells within
Δ𝑅< 0.3 from the photon.
Jets are reconstructed following the procedure previously used in Pb+Pb measurements [5, 27]. The anti-𝑘𝑡
algorithm [29, 30] with radius parameter R = 0.2 is applied to cells in all calorimeter layers combined into
logical towers of size Δ𝜂× Δ𝜙= 0.1 × 𝜋/32. The contribution to the energy deposited in towers by the

4 Analysis
The signal definition for the measurement presented here requires events having an isolated photon in
the range of 90 < 𝑝T,𝛾< 180 GeV and |𝜂𝛾| < 2.37 (excluding the barrel-to-endcap transition region
of 1.37 < |𝜂𝛾| < 1.52), and at least two R = 0.2 jets with 𝑝T > 30 GeV and |𝜂jet| < 2.8 that are in the
azimuthal hemisphere opposite to the photon, Δ𝜙jet,𝛾=
𝜙jet −𝜙𝛾 > 𝜋/2. For each event satisfying this
initial selection, the photon together with every possible combination of two jets is considered as a set.
For each set, three additional criteria are required: (1) both jets must be separated from the photon by
Δ𝑅> 0.5, (2) the two jet axes must be separated by Δ𝑅JJ > 0.4, and (3) the azimuthal separation between
the two-jet system and the photon must have Δ𝜙JJ𝛾> 7𝜋/8. Since at tree level a prompt photon plus two
partons are expected to have approximately balanced momenta in the transverse plane, this last requirement
reduces the rate of the combinatorial contributions while minimally impacting the number of signal events.

In the candidate Pb+Pb events, a subset of the selected jets do not arise from the same hard scattering as
the photon, but rather from an unrelated nucleon–nucleon scattering, or from jets that are reconstructed
from localised fluctuations of the UE. These background jets are produced predominantly at low 𝑝T
and isotropically relative to the photon direction. Such background jets can influence the observables by
forming a signal-jet + background-jet pair, or even background-jet + background-jet pair. The influence
of these jets on the observables is removed statistically using a mixed-event subtraction procedure. A
simpler form of this procedure was previously used for photon + inclusive jet analyses [16, 17]. Due to the
complexity of multiple jets contributing to the observables, this technique was developed further.
For each signal event (consisting of a photon and at least two jets), two minimum-bias Pb+Pb data events
are chosen that have similar centrality and second-order event plane angle [37] as the signal event, and thus
have on average the same distribution of background jets. The distributions are then constructed using
three different combinations of the photon and jets in the signal event and the jets in the minimum-bias
events, as follows:
1. First, the contribution from background-jet + background-jet pairs that occur in data must be
accounted for. The observable of choice is constructed using the photon from the signal event in
combination with all possible jet pairs in the first minimum-bias event.
2. The second step accounts for signal-jet and background-jet combinations. For each individual jet
satisfying selections in the signal event, the observable is constructed using the photon and that one
jet in combination with all the jets in the first minimum-bias event. However, since the jet from
the signal event may itself be a background jet, this estimated background will generally include
background-jet (from the signal event) and background-jet (from the first minimum bias event) pairs.
Since these background–background pairs are from different minimum-bias events, this contribution
is not present in data.
3. The third step accounts for the potential over-subtraction described in the second step. Here,
the observable of choice is constructed from the photon in the signal event, each jet in the first
minimum-bias event satisfying selections, and each jet in the second minimum-bias event satisfying
selections. If the first minimum-bias event contains two jets, and the second minimum-bias event
contains three jets, this results in six total pairs, assuming no pair is excluded by jet–jet selections
like the ΔR > 0.4 selection. This contribution accounts for the background-jet plus background-jet
pairs from different events that are introduced by the second step.
While this procedure is described for a single minimum-bias-event pair per signal event, the average of ten
minimum-bias-event pairs per signal event was employed in the analysis to improve statistical precision.
For each observable, the per-photon yields from steps one and two are subtracted from the initial yields,
and the yields from step three are added (to correct for the background over-subtraction), to produce a set of
combinatorial-background-subtracted distributions. The procedure is tested using the Pythia-8 and Pb+Pb
data overlay events (described in Sec. 3), where reconstructed jets matched to a Pythia-8 particle-level
jet are taken to be the signal jets, and the other jets in the event, i.e. those in the Pb+Pb data event, are
the background jets. Complications such as the potential merging of jets at reconstruction level results in
some residual non-closure of the background procedure, which is taken as a systematic uncertainty in the
measurement (discussed in Sec. 5). The observed background correction in Pb+Pb data is of comparable
magnitude to the signal in the central events and is greater at large AJJ𝛾, large Δ𝑅JJ, and the tails of the xJJ𝛾

An additional background arises from photons produced in decays of neutral meson and other hadrons.
The purity of the selected photons is estimated following the data-driven, double-sideband method used
in ATLAS photon measurements [38–41]. The obtained purity ranges from 80–95% depending on the
collision system and 𝑝T,𝛾. The contribution to the jet–jet combinations from this background is estimated
in data and statistically subtracted using the same methods applied in previous measurements [16, 17, 25].
The method is briefly summarised here. An inverted set of photon selection requirements designed to
enhance the contribution from background is used to select events, and the analysis is repeated, including
the combinatorial background subtraction. The resulting jet–jet yields in these background events are
scaled according to the estimated photon purity and are statistically subtracted from the yields obtained
with the nominal photon selection.
An unfolding procedure is applied to the data to correct for the kinematic-bin migration introduced by
detector resolution and UE effects and for the finite reconstruction and selection efficiency. This correction
is performed using the iterative Bayesian method [42] in the RooUnfold [43] software package. The
simulation samples described in Sec. 3 are used to produce response matrices in pp and Pb+Pb collisions.
The response matrices are observed to be predominantly diagonal and well-behaved, demonstrating limited
migration between neighbouring bins. The simulation samples are also used to test the unfolding procedure
by using half of the simulation to construct response matrices while treating the other half as data to be
unfolded and compared with particle level kinematics. For each of the three observables, the unfolding is
performed in three dimensions: the given observable (for example, xJJ𝛾), 𝑝T,𝛾, and 𝑝T,2. The latter two
variables are included since the response along the observable axis is found to be particularly dependent on
their values. The kinematic distributions are constructed in a wider range than the region in which the final
results are reported, to allow for the unfolding to properly account for the migration into and out of the
measurement region. Before the unfolding procedure is applied, the simulated events are first reweighted
to better describe the distributions as observed in data. The weights are applied at the particle-level,
as a function of 𝑝T,𝛾and 𝑝T,2, and the specific observable, on a photon-jet pair basis. The number of
iterations is chosen by minimizing the quadrature sum of the statistical uncertainties with the change in the
distributions from the previous iteration, and is typically two or three depending on the observable and
event class.

The uncertainty associated with the mixed-event background subtraction procedure, present only for
Pb+Pb collisions, is evaluated using input from the closure test of the procedure in the Pb+Pb data
overlay simulation described in Sec. 4. For each of the three observables, the differences between the
background-subtracted distribution and the reconstructed-level signal distribution in simulation are applied
to the data bin-by-bin. The unfolding is then performed on these modified input distributions.
The uncertainty associated with the unfolding procedure has three components. The first is the sensitivity
to the initial distributions used in the iterative unfolding, which is evaluated by using the simulation samples
without reweighting to the data. Second, the reconstructed jet 𝑝T threshold is raised from 30 GeV to
35 GeV, but still unfolded to the original kinematic range. Such a variation was used in Ref. [6] as a
check against residual background jet contamination at low jet 𝑝T. Finally, the impact of finite statistical
uncertainty in the simulation sample is evaluated with a pseudo-data re-sampling technique implemented
in the RooUnfold package [43]. This last component is much smaller than the statistical uncertainties in
the data.
For the measurements in Pb+Pb data, the total uncertainties for each observable are typically 20–40%,
but reach as high as 100% in the low-yield tails. The photon isolation and purity, the combinatorial
background subtraction, and the unfolding uncertainties have similar values and are co-dominant. For the
pp measurement, the total uncertainties are typically 5–10%, with the jet energy scale and the unfolding
uncertainties (which include an observed unfolding non-closure from a simulation study) being dominant.
Variations for sources common to the Pb+Pb and pp data are considered together when evaluating 𝐼AA,
the ratio of distributions in these systems. This results in a modest reduction of the uncertainties in 𝐼AA
compared with the relative uncertainties in the pp or Pb+Pb distributions alone.

The experimental results in the Pb+Pb 0–10% centrality interval are compared with three theoretical jet
quenching models referred to as Jewel, Jetscape, and Lbt. The Jewel MC generator modifies the parton
shower evolution via interactions with a medium, modelled as a two-dimensional outwardly expanding
QGP. Jewel does not incorporate colour-coherence effects. Version 2.2.0 of Jewel is publicly available
following the initial release detailed in Ref. [51]. Jewel was run using the photon+jet hard-scattering
process [52] that includes only direct (leading-order) photon production. The photon and jet kinematic
selections used in the data analysis are applied and the observables are calculated.
Jetscape (Jet Energy-loss Tomography with a Statistically and Computationally Advanced Program
Envelope) is a framework that enables different modules to be plugged in for modelling the initial collision
geometry, different jet kinematics, and different physics processes of jet quenching [53]. The Jetscape
authors have provided calculation results for pp (based on Pythia-8) and 0–10% Pb+Pb collisions using
the plug-in modules Matter (Modular All Twist Transverse-scattering Elastic-drag and Radiation) + Lbt
(Linearized Boltzmann Transport) with the Jetscape v3.5 AA22 tune, identical to that used in Refs. [53–55].
The Matter module handles the medium interactions at high virtuality (above some 𝑄0 parameter) where
modified coherence effects diminish medium-induced radiation, and a lower virtuality phase is modelled
using the Linear Boltzmann Transport model (Lbt) [56], where parton splittings are fully resolved by the
medium as they undergo multiple-scattering-induced energy loss.
Lbt calculations were also run separately (without the Matter module) using MadGraph [45] for the
pp baseline and for initial hard scatterings as input to the QGP medium propagation.
Figure 6 shows the three theoretical models compared with experimental data for all three observables.
The xJJ𝛾distribution is qualitatively described by all models, with a substantially larger suppression, i.e.
smaller 𝐼AA, at xJJ𝛾> 0.8. Similarly, the AJJ𝛾distribution is well described by all models, with the Jewel
result slightly over-predicting the 𝐼AA. Lastly, the Δ𝑅JJ distribution is described by both Jetscape and Lbt,
but has the completely opposite trend in Jewel. The data indicates somewhat less suppression of dijet
events with larger angular separation, whereas Jewel has a substantial suppression of dijet events with
small angular separation.
Figure 7 shows results from the Jewel pp Monte Carlo generator for the correlation between the different
observables with panel (a) showing xJJ𝛾versus 𝑝T,𝛾, panel (b) showing AJJ𝛾versus xJJ𝛾, and (c) showing
Δ𝑅JJ versus xJJ𝛾. It is notable that at the lowest 𝑝T,𝛾= 90 GeV, the acceptance in AJJ𝛾is limited to values
predominantly below 0.3, and larger AJJ𝛾values are only populated from higher 𝑝T,𝛾events. For xJJ𝛾and
Δ𝑅JJ there are no such strong kinematic correlations. The MC generator calculations have the identical
kinematic selections as the data. It is notable that all generators show a suppression at xJJ𝛾≈1, which
automatically suppresses the yield for AJJ𝛾> 0.3.

7 Conclusion
This paper presents results of a measurement by ATLAS of photon plus two-jet observables in pp and Pb+Pb
collisions at 5.02 TeV collected in 2017 and 2018, respectively, to study the impact of medium-parton
interactions on nearby quark and gluon probes of the QGP. Photons are selected with 90 < 𝑝T,𝛾< 180 GeV
and 𝑅= 0.2 jets are selected with 𝑝T > 30 GeV. The unfolded distributions are measured for three
observables, xJJ𝛾, AJJ𝛾, and Δ𝑅JJ, in pp data and three Pb+Pb centrality intervals (30–80%, 10–30%, and
0–10%). Ratios of the distributions in each Pb+Pb centrality interval to those in pp collisions (𝐼AA) are also
measured. Significant suppression (𝐼AA < 1) is observed in all cases, with the suppression larger in the most
central relative to the most peripheral centrality intervals. Monte Carlo calculations from the jet quenching
models Jewel, Jetscape, and Lbt are compared with the experimental data. These measurements provide
new constraints on the microscopic processes of energy loss in the QGP, and further tests of the underlying
assumptions of theoretical models will help refine their description of these processes.
