Abstract. We establish structure theorems for polarised flag type quo-
tients arising in the study of the moduli space of supersingular abelian
varieties. In particular, we prove a refined normal form for the top level
of these flags and derive an explicit, computable descent criterion for
quasi-polarisations. These results provide a complete classification of
the first and last step of these flags.
As an application, we compute polarised flag type quotients in di-
mensions g ≤5, describing the fibers of the truncation morphisms as
classification objects of supersingular abelian varieties.

1. Introduction
The moduli space Ag of principally polarised abelian varieties and its
supersingular locus Sg are central objects in arithmetic geometry. While the
existence of Sg as a (coarse) moduli space follows from geometric invariant
theory, this approach does not provide an explicit description of its geometry.
After preliminary work due to Oda and Oort in [OO77], a powerful strategy
aimed at solving this problem was introduced by Li and Oort in [LO98] via
the framework of polarised flag type quotients (PFTQs for short). However,
while the theoretical framework is well-established, explicit calculations of
these PFTQs remain cumbersome. Therefore, the existing literature only
contains partial results (see, for example [Har22; Din21; Pie22]) focusing on
isolated steps of the PFTQs in selected dimensions rather than providing a
systematic treatment of these structures.

1.1. Structural results. The main contribution of this article is to bundle
the computations into two structural results that can be used as black boxes.
(1) Theorem 4.1 and its corollary establish a normal form for the quasi-
polarised superspecial Dieudonn´e module at the top of a PFTQ,
refining the structure theorem due to Li and Oort [LO98, Theorem
6.1] by exploiting the additional constraints of the PFTQ structure.
(2) Lemma 4.1 replaces the abstract descent condition in the definition of
PFTQs by an explicit integrality condition on the quasi-polarisation,
giving a computable criterion for descending down the PFTQ.
These two results provide a roadmap for calculations and suffice to classify
the fibers of the first and last truncation morphisms in arbitrary dimension.
1.2. Application in low dimensions. We carry out the explicit calcula-
tions in dimensions g ≤5 and systematically discuss each dimension: The
lowest-dimensional case is not of particular interest as supersingular elliptic
curves can be fully described by classical methods, see [Deu41] and [Igu85].
Thus, our calculations in Section 5 start in dimension g = 2. For g = 2, 3,
the structure is fully determined by the black-box theorems and we recover
known descriptions of the moduli spaces, see [Mor81] and [IKY25]. Dimen-
sion g = 4 is particularly interesting as we have to use a new approach to
describe all PFTQs and not only the ones leading to supergeneral abelian
varieties. Lastly, we discuss PFTQs in dimension g = 5 obtaining novel
equations describing generic parameter choices.

2. Dieudonn´e theory of abelian varieties
In this section, we give a short review of supersingular abelian varieties
and their moduli. Furthermore, we introduce the main technical tool trans-
lating their study into semilinear algebra, namely Dieudonn´e theory.
2.1. Supersingular abelian varieties. Let k be a field of characteristic p
and let k be its algebraic closure. We call an elliptic curve E supersingular if
its group of k-rational p-torsion points is trivial, i.e. if E[p](k) = {0}. There
are two related notions in higher dimension g ≥2:

Definition 2.1. Let X be a g-dimensional abelian variety over k.
(i) X is called supersingular if it is isogenous to a product of g supersin-
gular elliptic curves over its algebraic closure: X ≃k E1 × . . . × Eg.
(ii) X is called superspecial if it is isomorphic to a product of g supersin-
gular elliptic curves over its algebraic closure: X ∼=k E1 × . . . × Eg.
Remark 2.1. By a result due to Deligne, Ogus, and Shioda [Shi79, Theorem
3.5], for any supersingular elliptic curves E1, . . . E2g with g ≥2, we have
E1 × . . . × Eg ∼= Eg+1 × . . . × E2g.
In particular, all supersingular (resp. superspecial) abelian varieties of di-
mension g ≥2 are isogenous (resp. isomorphic), and the above definition is
independent of the choice of supersingular elliptic curves.
As all supersingular abelian varieties are of p-rank 0, this invariant is not
useful for analysing these objects. However, the a-number will often be used:
Definition 2.2. Let X be an abelian variety over k. Its a-number is defined
to be
a(X) = dimk(Hom(αp, X)).
A supersingular abelian variety X over k is called supergeneral if a(X) = 1.

Let Ag be the moduli space of g-dimensional principally polarised abelian

2.2. Dieudonn´e theory. Let k be a perfect field of characteristic p and let
W(k) be the ring of Witt vectors over k. We denote the Frobenius on W(k)

3. Polarised flag type quotients
Aiming to describe the moduli space of supersingular abelian varieties
more explicitly, the previous theorem provides a crucial first step by resolving
the superspecial case: Assuming that the base field is algebraically closed, X
corresponds to a superspecial Dieudonn´e module with a quasi-polarisation,
and we have established a structure theorem classifying such objects.
Building on this, it is natural to ask whether supersingular abelian vari-
eties can be linked to superspecial ones in a way that allows us to leverage
this classification. This motivates the framework of flag type quotients.
The key insight behind (polarised) flag type quotients is that any super-
singular abelian variety can be connected to a superspecial abelian variety
via a purely inseparable isogeny. As the kernel of this isogeny is constructed
from successive extensions of the group scheme αp, the isogeny can be fac-
tored into a chain of isogenies, each having a predetermined kernel rank.
