1. Introduction
A primary question in arithmetic dynamics concerns pre-periodic points of a polynomial map
f : Q →Q. Let f(n) denote the n-th iterate of f under composition. We say that a point p is
periodic of order n if f(n)(p) = p and if f(i)(p) ̸= p for 0 < i < n. A point is pre-periodic if there
exists a non-negative integer m such that the point f(m)(p) is periodic.
It was conjectured in [4] that if f is quadratic, then it has no rational periodic points of order
n > 3. This is also known as Poonen’s conjecture, due to the progress made in [9]. There is evidence
for the 6-cycle case in [4]. However, it was also shown in [2] that if, instead, f : P1 →P1 is a degree
two rational map, there are several infinite families of maps and rational periodic points of order 6.
The collection of rational maps and points (f, p1, ..., pn) that exhibit a prescribed dynamic
structure naturally forms a moduli space. The study of these moduli spaces is motivated by the
Uniform Boundedness Conjecture posed by Morton and Silverman [8]. The conjecture asserts
that for every D ≥1, N ≥1, and d ≥2, there is a constant C(D, N, d) such that for all number
fields K/Q, satisfying [K : Q] ≤D and all endomorphisms of PN(K) of degree d, the number of
pre-periodic points of f is bounded above by a constant, C(D, N, d). Even in the simplest case,
(D, N, d) = (1, 1, 2), the answer is not known.
Although this conjecture is rooted in number theory and arithmetic dynamics, we can understand
it from the perspective of geometry. These moduli spaces, denoted as MN
d [P] where d is the degree
of the rational maps f, N is the dimension of the projective space, and P is the prescribed dynamic
structure, which were constructed in [3], naturally arise from this problem.
Silverman and Doyle’s conjecture [3, Conjecture 10.6] asserts that if N ≥1, d ≥1, then there
is a constant C(N, d) such that for all unweighted pre-periodic portraits P with more points than
C(N, d) and MN
d [P] ̸= ∅, the variety MN
d [P] ⊗Z C is an irreducible variety of general type. The
moduli space structure identifies rational tuples on MN
d [P] with rational points exhibiting the
portrait structure. Since varieties of general type are conjectured to have sparse rational points, a
general type moduli space would indicate that the rational points exhibiting the portrait structure
are not Zariski dense.

map on P1 over Q. Then the moduli space of degree two rational maps that exhibit the prescribed
structure can be realized as a surface in P3. Blanc, Canci, and Elkies showed in [2] that if P6 is a
