SYMMETRIC TENSOR DECOMPOSITION ON RATIONAL VARIETIES
MATTEO BECHERE, SALMA KUHLMANN, AND BERNARD MOURRAIN
Abstract. We study the Waring decomposition of symmetric tensors with nodes on a rational
variety. We provide an explicit characterisation of the existence of such a decomposition under
some technical assumption, and introduce an efficient algorithm to decompose this novel class of
structured symmetric tensors. The framework directly generalizes Hankel tensors (Qi 2015) to the
multivariate setting. We analyse in details the case of toric varieties and rational curves. Proving
the existence of a quadrature formula of even strength 2N with at most N + 1 nodes, that avoids
a prescribed finite set of points, we establish new sharp upper bounds on the minimal number of
nodes for quadrature formulae on rational curves. Numerical experimentation demonstrates the
gain of this approach, compared to classical direct approaches.
1. Introduction
Tensors are a central tool in many branches of mathematics, such as in linear algebra, differential
geometry, and in other fields, such as signal processing, electrical engineering, statistics and finance,
thanks to their modelling power. Hence, efficiently manipulating and understanding tensors is crucial
for many real-world problems involving multidimensional data. The purpose of tensor decomposition
is to express a tensor as a sum of simpler tensors. This process unveils hidden information and
additional properties which are often invisible in the starting form. Moreover, the minimal length
of a tensor decomposition, also known as rank, is a measure of the complexity of the tensor. Among
tensors, symmetric tensors occupy a special place: they remain invariant under permutations of their
arguments, reflecting inherent symmetries in the system they model. This property makes them
especially important in models where order does not matter, such as stress tensors in continuum
mechanics or covariance matrices in statistics. Furthermore, a symmetric tensor can be identified
with a homogeneous polynomial. This perspective opens the door to interactions with algebraic
geometry. In the setting of homogeneous polynomials, symmetric tensor decompositions are also
known as Waring decompositions.
The primary motivation of our work is to analyze decompositions of tensors with nodes in a certain
algebraic variety X. The general Waring decomposition of symmetric tensors of degree d is a special
case where X is either the projective space Pn or its Veronese embedding Vd,n in degree d. For the
decomposition of multilinear tensors, X is the Segre variety. For further details on this geometric
point of view and on methods for computing such decompositions, we refer to [5, 10, 11, 13, 32, 44].
The problem of decomposing a form as a weighted sum of k-th power of degree-h forms is known as
k-th Waring decomposition problem [34, 45] and it corresponds to the case where X is the Veronese
embedding in degree k of the space of degree h forms.
Remarkably, such tensor decompositions play a central role in the study of multiplication tensors in
algebraic complexity theory. In particular, the tensor associated with matrix multiplication encodes
the complexity of matrix products: its rank and its border rank are closely tied to the exponent of
matrix multiplication [31, 58].
The general problem of symmetric tensor decomposition on a variety given by equations has been
studied in [43], where tensor decompositions on X are obtained using a refinement on the techniques
used in [13, 41, 44]. The case of varieties given by linear projections of projective varieties from
linear subspaces is studied in [6].
In this paper, we consider projective varieties, given as the image of a rational map q. The case
where q is the one associated to all binary monomials of a given degree is considered in [48]. This
leads to the concept of Hankel tensors, introduced in [46] and further studied in [42, 48]. They have
been used in fields such as geophysics [3, 63] and data analysis [57].
Specializing to positive decompositions, the problem becomes strictly connected to solving finite
dimensional truncated moment problems, as is illustrated in Proposition 3.21. Furthermore, the

the authors study quadrature formulae with nodes lying on algebraic curves on the plane, by means
of optimization techniques.
Computing such a minimal Waring decomposition of a tensor is not a simple task. In fact, it is
known to be a NP-hard problem [28]. Another motivation of our work is to demonstrate that if the
tensor has a structure, this decomposition problem can be addressed more efficiently. Algorithmic
aspects of this decomposition problem have been only partially investigated in [13, 26].
Hereafter, we show that if the tensor is q-Symmetric (see Definition 3.4), its decomposition reduces
to the decomposition of a higher order tensor in smaller dimension, with the same rank, which is
easier to handle by standard tensor decomposition methods (see Section 4.3).
Contributions: In this paper, we study the decomposition properties of q-Symmetric tensors, which
form a subspace of the vector space of symmetric tensors. For a choice of forms q, such that the
substitution map Wq is surjective, we prove in Theorem 3.18 that the q-Symmetric tensors are
exactly those having a Waring decomposition on the rational variety Vq parametrized by q (i.e. the
projective points corresponding to the Waring decomposition lie in the image of the polynomial map
q), by proving the existence of a length-preserving bijective correspondence between q-Symmetric
decompositions of a q-Symmetric tensor p and Waring decompositions of an associated form ψq(p)
(see Theorem 3.17). Given the length-preserving property of the correspondence, our method also
results in an effective method to obtain Waring decomposition of high rank forms, if they lie in the
space of q-Symmetric tensors, as is presented in Section 4.1. In Theorem 3.20, we prove that, over
the real numbers, the cone dual to the cone of positive q-Symmetric tensors is the cone of forms
which are non-negative on Vq. A special instance of the binary monomial q-Symmetric tensors,
the Hankel tensors, were studied in [48]. We generalize this theory to higher dimension and general
maps q. In Proposition 3.26 we also improve the bound in [48, Thm. 4.1] on the length of a minimal
q-Symmetric decomposition of a q-Symmetric tensor. Furthermore, we improve the proof of [48,
Thm. 3.1] and answer [48, Question 4.2] positively.
Additionally, we link the positive q-Symmetric tensor decomposition problem to the problem of
computing quadrature formulae supported on algebraic curves. Under some additional technical
condition, in Theorem 3.32, we improve the bound of [55, Thm.1.1 (2)], on the number of nodes
of quadrature formulae of even strength. As a tool to prove the aforementioned theorem, in Theo-
rem 3.31, we prove that it is possible to construct a quadrature formula of strength 2N with N + 1
nodes for a non-degenerate truncated moment sequence (i.e. the Hankel matrix is invertible) on the
line which avoids a finite set of points. This theorem treats the problem of excluding prescribed
points from the node set, a topic that, to the best of our knowledge, has not been investigated in the
past and is complementary to the question of prescribing the quadrature formula to have a specific
node (see e.g. [12, Corollary 2.3]) or multiple prescribed nodes (see [39, Thm. 3.1])
Furthermore, we address the surjectivity assumption on Wq by characterizing it in terms of the
Hilbert function of the vanishing ideal of Vq and also by stating sufficient conditions on the set q
(see Proposition 3.9 and Proposition 3.11). We formalize our results in Algorithm 1 to compute
q-Symmetric decompositions of any q-Symmetric tensor p, provided one is able to find Waring
decompositions of ψq(p). Algorithm 1 is implemented in the QSymDecomposition.jl Julia package
[9] and in Section 4 we demonstrate its effectiveness at decomposing tensors of higher rank.
