SYMPLECTIC LEAVES OF MEROMORPHIC HITCHIN SYSTEMS
JIA CHOON LEE AND SUKJOO LEE
Abstract. The moduli space of meromorphic Higgs bundles admits a Poisson structure due
to the independent work of Bottacin and Markman. In this paper, we revisit the symplectic
leaves of this Poisson structure for the tame case. We study the partial compactification of
the restricted Hitchin map on the symplectic leaves to an algebraically completely integrable
system. In particular, we show that such a partial compactification is realized by the moduli
spaces of ⃗ξ-parabolic Higgs bundles. These same moduli spaces also provide a symplectic
resolution of the normalization of the closure of the corresponding symplectic leaves. Finally,
we discuss connectedness results for the corresponding Betti moduli spaces under the tame
non-abelian Hodge correspondence.

1. Introduction
1.1. Overview. Higgs bundles were introduced by Hitchin in his study of the self-duality
equations on Riemann surfaces [Hit87b]. One of the fundamental features of the moduli space
of stable Higgs bundles is that it carries a natural holomorphic symplectic structure, and the
Hitchin map defines an algebraically completely integrable system, now known as the Hitchin
system [Hit87a; DM96].
A natural generalization is obtained by allowing the Higgs field to have poles at marked
points. Let C be a smooth projective curve and let D ⊂C be a reduced effective divisor.
For simplicity, only in this overview section, we discuss the case where D consists of a single

point p. A meromorphic Higgs bundle is a pair (E, Φ), where E is a rank r vector bundle

independent work of Bottacin [Bot95] and Markman [Mar94].
For a Poisson variety, a basic geometric problem is to understand its symplectic leaves. In
the setting of meromorphic Higgs bundles, the residue at the pole provides the relevant local
model. The residue resp(Φ) is an element of glr. Identifying glr ∼= gl∗
r under the trace pairing,
the adjoint orbits in glr are the symplectic leaves of the standard Lie–Poisson structure and
their induced symplectic forms are the Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau forms. This suggests that,
on the global moduli space Higgss(C, D), the loci obtained by requiring the residue to lie in
a fixed adjoint orbit O should play the role of symplectic leaves. We denote these loci by
HiggsO,s(C, D).
Question 1.1. For an arbitrary adjoint orbit O, is HiggsO,s(C, D) a symplectic leaf of
Higgss(C, D)?
Recall that a symplectic leaf of a Poisson variety is a maximal connected locally closed
smooth subvariety tangent to the characteristic distribution of the Poisson structure.
Up
to the question of connectedness, this picture is already present in the work of Bottacin
[Bot95, Theorem 4.7.5] and Markman [Mar94, Corollary 8.10], especially for symplectic leaves
of maximal rank. For the purposes of the present paper, we will need to keep track of the
explicit infinitesimal description of HiggsO,s(C, D) in terms of characteristic distribution and
to address the connectedness of HiggsO,s(C, D) to ensure that it is a single symplectic leaf.
Once the symplectic leaf is identified, a further question is to understand the Hitchin map
restricted to it. It is shown in [Bot95, Section 4.7] and [Mar94, Section 8.3] that the Hitchin
map on Higgss(C, D) is a Poisson integrable system in a generalized sense. However, this does
not immediately imply that its restriction to an arbitrary symplectic leaf is an algebraically
completely integrable system in the sense that it is a proper flat Lagrangian fibration with
generic fibers abelian varieties. There are two basic issues. First, when the symplectic leaf
is not maximal rank, the corresponding spectral curves are typically singular, so the usual
description of the generic Hitchin fibers as Jacobians of smooth spectral curves no longer
applies directly. Second, if the symplectic leaf is not closed in Higgss(C, D), the restricted
Hitchin map may fail to be proper.
Question 1.2. For an arbitrary adjoint orbit O, can the restricted Hitchin map on
HiggsO,s(C, D) be partially compactified to an algebraically completely integrable system?
This question is closely related to the ”Lax project” formulated by Boalch in [Boa18, Section
1]. Boalch proposes to classify integrable systems admitting a Lax representation, meaning
that it is isomorphic to a symplectic leaf of a meromorphic Hitchin system, with the Hamil-
tonians given by restricting the Hitchin map.1 However, Boalch restricts the program to a
class of ”good” Lax representations, which correspond to closed symplectic leaves of maximal
rank in our case, since a Poisson integrable system does not necessarily restrict to an alge-
braically completely integrable system on every symplectic leaf. Thus the present question
can be viewed as complementary to the ”good” Lax representations by considering symplectic
leaves of arbitrary rank.

There are natural candidates for such a partial compactification. First, one may take the
closure of HiggsO,s(C, D) in Higgss(C, D). Second, one may replace the residue orbit O by
its closure O and consider the corresponding moduli space HiggsO,s(C, D). While these two
constructions are not a priori the same, one of our results is that they coincide. However, this
partial compactification is generally singular, reflecting the singularities of O.
The key point is that the singularities of O admit a natural resolution by adding compatible
flag data. This suggests replacing a meromorphic Higgs bundle with residue in O by a para-
bolic Higgs bundle with conditions on the flags determined by O. This leads us to consider
the moduli space of ⃗ξ-parabolic Higgs bundles, whose smoothness was established previously
in [LL24; LL25], in order to resolve HiggsO,s(C, D). Moreover, these moduli spaces form alge-
braically completely integrable systems, providing an answer to the partial compactification
question above.
As a byproduct, we obtain some topological consequences for certain Betti moduli spaces
via Simpson’s tame non-abelian Hodge correspondence [Sim90].
In particular, we obtain
connectedness results for moduli spaces of filtered local systems and for irreducible loci of
character varieties with prescribed local monodromy.

5.1. Integrable systems. In this section, we study integrable system structures for both
HiggsO,s(C, D) and Higgspar(C, D, ⃗m, ⃗α). There are many different conventions for the defini-
tion of an integrable system. We first set this clearly.
Definition 5.1. Let X be a symplectic variety and B be a smooth variety such that dimX =
2dimB.
A morphism h : X →B is called an algebraically completely integrable system
(simply called integrable system) if h is a proper flat morphism whose generic smooth fibers
are Lagrangian abelian varieties (see [DM96, Definition 2.9]).
Remark 5.2. In general, one may not need to require the properness of h. On the other hand,
for the base B, it is often required to be affine (See [Boa18, Definition 1]).
More generally, one can consider the case where X is Poisson, not necessarily symplectic,
and dimX < 2dimB. In this case, a generic fiber is required to be coisotropic, instead of
Lagrangian. We call such h : X →B a Poisson integrable system.
Since Higgs
⃗ξ-par(C, D, ⃗m, ⃗α) is a symplectic leaf of Higgspar(C, D, ⃗m, ⃗α) (Theorem 4.11)

whether the restriction of the Hitchin map h gives an integrable system. We first consider the
⃗ξ-parabolic case.
