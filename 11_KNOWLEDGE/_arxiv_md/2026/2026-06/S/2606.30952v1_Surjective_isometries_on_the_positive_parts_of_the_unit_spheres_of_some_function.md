1. Introduction
Let E and F be Banach spaces, and let A and B be subsets of E and F, respectively. A
mapping ∆: A →B is called an isometry if
∥∆(f) −∆(g)∥= ∥f −g∥
for all f, g ∈A.
In 1972, Mankiewicz [17] proved that every surjective isometry between convex bodies in real
Banach spaces can be extended to a surjective affine isometry between entire spaces. Subse-
quently, in 1987, Tingley [22] posed the following question: Given normed spaces E and F, does
every surjective isometry between their unit spheres S(E) and S(F) extend to a surjective real-
linear isometry between E and F? This question is now known as Tingley’s problem and has
motivated a substantial amount of research. Among the studies on function spaces, we refer the
reader to [1,4,5,7–10,23,24]. Although many affirmative results have been obtained in various
Banach spaces, the problem remains open in full generality.
More recently, Peralta [18] proposed several variants of Tingley’s problem in the context of
operator algebras. In particular, Peralta [19] considered the question of whether every surjective
isometry between the sets of positive norm-one elements in two C∗-algebras can be extended to a
surjective complex-linear isometry between the C∗-algebras themselves. He obtained affirmative
answers in the cases of the C∗-algebra of all bounded operators on Hilbert space and the C∗-
algebra of all compact operators on separable Hilbert space.
