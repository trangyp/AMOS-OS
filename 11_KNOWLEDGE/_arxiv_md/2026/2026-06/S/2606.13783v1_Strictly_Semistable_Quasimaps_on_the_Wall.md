Strictly Semistable Quasimaps on the Wall
Sara Stephens
June 2026
Abstract
Moduli spaces of ϵ-stable quasimaps exhibit a wall-and-chamber structure, inter-
polating between the moduli of stable quasimaps and Kontsevich’s moduli spaces of
stable maps. In the case where the target is projective space, we develop an intrinsic
framework for the wall-crossing phenomenon by constructing an algebraic stack where
strictly semistable objects appear at a wall in the space of ϵ-stability conditions. We
show this algebraic stack admits a proper good moduli space and analyze the variation
of Θ-stratification on this stack. This yields a new approach to developing a K-theoretic
wall-crossing formula for ϵ-stable quasimap invariants via non-abelian localization.

Introduction
A classical problem in enumerative geometry asks for the number of rational curves contained
in an algebraic variety that satisfy prescribed incidence conditions. In modern enumerative
geometry, rigorous answers are provided by curve counting theories, which give a systematic
framework that assigns virtual numbers to curves (of fixed genus and degree) inside a given
smooth projective variety X.
Prominent among these is Gromov–Witten theory, whose
central object is the moduli space Mg,n(X, d) of degree d stable maps from an n-marked
nodal curve of genus g into X. This moduli space is a proper Deligne–Mumford stack with
a virtual fundamental class, and the Gromov–Witten invariants are defined as integrals over
it. The open substack Mg,n(X, d) ⊂Mg,n(X, d) of maps with smooth domain curve is not
compact; other natural compactifications exist, yielding different invariants closely related
to Gromov–Witten invariants of [KM94].
A family of such compactifications is provided by the theory of ϵ-stable quasimaps, es-
tablished by Ciocan-Fontanine, Kim, and Maulik in [CFKM14], for a large class of GIT
quotients of affine varieties. This theory unifies many earlier constructions [Kon95,MOP11,
CFK10,Tod11,MM07]. The moduli stacks QMapϵ
g,n(X, d) are proper and separated Deligne–
Mumford stacks of finite type, with stability depending on a positive rational parameter ϵ.
As ϵ varies, these spaces QMapϵ
g,n(X, d) interpolate between two classical moduli problems:
stable maps and stable quasimaps. As ϵ →∞, the theory recovers the usual Gromov–Witten
theory of X, and the quasimaps coincide with the familiar stable maps, where the source
curve has many rational tails and no basepoints. On the other hand, when ϵ →0, we obtain
stable quasimaps, coinciding with stable quotients defined by Marian, Oprea, and Pandhari-
pande [MOP11]. These quasimaps have no rational tails and many basepoints. The simpler
domain curves allow certain quasimap invariants to be explicitly computed, especially in
genus 0.

chamber structure. The theory changes only at discrete values of ϵ, called walls. Assuming
for simplicity that (g, n) is not (0, 0), the walls occur at ϵ0 = 1/m0 with m0 ∈N. For
fixed numerical data (g, n, d), there are a finite number of walls, and the moduli spaces are

of the classical theory of variation of GIT quotient [DH98], by varying the Θ-stratification

Related work.
The most notable comparison to our work is that of Zhang and Zhou
[ZZ20], which builds on earlier work of Zhou [Zho22]. They prove a K-theoretic wall-crossing
formula relating ϵ-stable quasimap invariants across different stability chambers for all targets
and all genera, including the orbifold case. Their proof uses virtual localization on a larger
moduli space, called a “master space”.
The fixed-point loci of the master space are in
one-to-one correspondence with the terms in the wall-crossing formula.
The master space constructed in [Zho22] plays a similar role to QMapϵ0−ss
g,n (PN, d), but
it is built very differently. It is a P1-bundle over the stack of “entangled” tails, where the
entanglement comes from a sequence of blowups on the moduli stack of weighted curves. This
results in the master space being a proper Deligne–Mumford stack with a global C∗-action,
as opposed to our algebraic stack QMapϵ0−ss
g,n (PN, d) which allows objects with higher rank
torus automorphism groups. Our construction is more directly motivated by the inherent
geometry of the wall crossing, and avoids the auxiliary construction of a master space.
