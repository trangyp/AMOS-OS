Selection-Aware Diagnostics
for Chain-of-Thought Answer Hijacking
Jianwei Tai
School of Internet, Anhui University
24012@ahu.edu.cn
Abstract
We study a controlled numeric proxy for chain-of-thought
(CoT) answer hijacking, motivated by attacks in which
benign-looking reasoning steers a harmful final answer. CoT
wrappers on GSM8K and MATH-500 flip final answers away
from gold labels. Rather than treating activation patching
as clean-trace restoration, we ask where hijacked trajecto-
ries are fragile and whether recovery depends on a same-
problem clean source. Across Qwen2.5-7B and Llama3-
8B on GSM8K few-shot, puzzle, and sycophant hijacks,
three few-shot/puzzle cells pass confirmatory K=1 local-
ization after Bonferroni correction. A selection-aware 50/50
band validation preserves held-out in-band minus out-of-
band gaps of +32.6, +45.1, and +17.7 points for Qwen-
puzzle, Llama3-fewshot, and Llama3-puzzle, while exact L⋆
agreement is much less stable. Qwen-fewshot remains ex-
ploratory, and sycophant cells are temporal-diffuse under
short patches. A BF16 Qwen-puzzle full-band sweep pre-
serves the band signal (n=30, spread 0.33 at K=1, peak
layer 20), supporting the conclusion that the band is not
only an INT4 artifact. Fixed-hook GSM8K reruns preserve
recovery in both primary puzzle cells: Qwen-puzzle re-
covers 47.0% at n=100 (47/100; Wilson 95% CI [37.5%,
56.7%]), while Llama3-puzzle recovers 39.0% at n=100
(39/100; [30.0%, 48.8%]). Frozen transfer to MATH-500 re-
covers 26.0% of qualified cases in the largest fixed-transfer
run (13/50; Wilson 95% CI [15.9%, 39.6%]). Source con-
trols change the mechanism interpretation. Paired bootstraps
give finite-sample non-separation between clean and ran-
dom sources in Qwen-fewshot (+3.0 points, 95% CI [-
18.2,+27.3]) and Llama3-puzzle at expanded n=60 (clean–
random -8.3 [-21.7,+5.0]), while Llama3-fewshot is content-
mediated (+40.0 [+16.7,+60.0]). Thus successful patching is
not automatically evidence that the clean trace uniquely car-
ries the recoverable semantic state. We present the method as
a diagnostic protocol for numeric CoT answer-hijack recov-
ery, not an adaptive-robust safety evaluation.

Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) deployed with chain-of-
thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al. 2022) are vulnera-
ble to a class of attacks where the visible reasoning is
benign but the final answer is harmful. Chain-of-Thought
Hijacking (Zhao et al. 2025) demonstrated that padding a

harmful query with long puzzle reasoning pushes attack
success above 94% on frontier models including GPT-4o
and Claude. We use this result as motivation but evalu-
ate a narrower controlled proxy: GSM8K and MATH-500
prompts whose in-context CoT wrapper flips the final nu-
meric answer away from the gold label. The proxy iso-
lates the answer-trajectory mechanism without claiming that
arithmetic answer recovery is itself a harmful-query safety
benchmark.
Threat model and non-claims.
We study open-weight
LLMs under a white-box hidden-state hook and report nu-
meric answer recovery on filtered hijacked examples. We do
not evaluate harmful-query completion, black-box deploy-
ment, adaptive jailbreak robustness, or production refusal
behavior. We also do not treat a successful patch as proof
that a clean trace uniquely mediates the recoverable seman-
tic state; source dependence is tested separately.
Existing intervention methods for CoT-related failures fall
into two camps. The first is steering-vector design: meth-
ods such as ActAdd (Turner et al. 2023), Inference-Time
Intervention (Li et al. 2023), and Representation Engineer-
ing (Zou et al. 2023a) compute a population-level direction
in activation space and add it to model hidden states at in-
ference. These methods require knowing what to steer to-
ward, such as a truthfulness direction or a refusal direction.
The second is patching from clean traces: causal mediation
analysis (Vig et al. 2020; Meng et al. 2022; Conmy et al.
2023) restores activations from a benign reference run to
undo a corrupted computation. This approach requires or-
acle access to a clean problem with the same structure as the
attacked one.
Both classes assume that the task-relevant signal is
content-like: it can be steered along a meaningful direction
or restored from a clean trace. We instead ask where the
answer-hijack trajectory is fragile. The answer is not a sin-
gle universal layer. Few-shot and puzzle hijacks expose mid-
network fragility bands, sycophancy is temporally diffuse,
and one few-shot cell behaves as a content mediator.

Related Work
Causal mediation and activation patching.
Causal me-
diation analysis was introduced to NLP by Vig et al. (Vig
et al. 2020), who localized gender-bias effects to a small set
of attention heads and feed-forward neurons in GPT-2 by
intervening on activations. Meng et al. (Meng et al. 2022)
extended this paradigm to factual recall with ROME, identi-
fying mid-layer MLPs as the dominant mediators of subject–
relation associations and editing them via rank-one updates;
MEMIT (Meng et al. 2023) scales the same locate-then-
edit recipe to thousands of facts. Wang et al. (Wang et al.
2023) pushed activation patching from single-token recall to
a multi-step circuit (indirect object identification) in GPT-
2 Small, and Conmy et al. (Conmy et al. 2023) automated
this style of circuit discovery (ACDC) by greedy edge prun-
ing under a faithfulness metric. Ghandeharioun et al. (Ghan-
deharioun et al. 2024) unified these inspection-by-patching
variants under the Patchscopes framework. Our work dif-
fers by treating mid-late-layer patching not only as a local-
ization tool that recovers a clean computation, but also as
a disruption operator: same-layer clean, random, and zero
patches do not separate under Wilson intervals in the Qwen-
fewshot source-control cell, while a cross-layer source is
much weaker. This is consistent with a finite-sample no-
detected-clean-advantage fragility pattern rather than a faith-
ful mediator.
Chain-of-thought prompting and its failure modes.
Wei
et al. (Wei et al. 2022) showed that interleaving intermedi-
ate steps before the final answer (CoT) elicits arithmetic and
commonsense reasoning in large LMs, with GSM8K (Cobbe
et al. 2021) as a canonical benchmark. Subsequent work has
documented that the visible chain is often not the operative
computation: Turpin et al. (Turpin et al. 2023) demonstrated
that biasing few-shot exemplars yields plausible but unfaith-
ful CoTs, and Lanham et al. (Lanham et al. 2023) found
that perturbing the chain often leaves the answer unchanged.
Sycophantic behavior is a closely related failure where the
model prefers user-pleasing answers even when they conflict
with its own reasoning (Sharma et al. 2024). The most di-
rect attack-side prior to our setting is Chain-of-Thought Hi-
jacking (Zhao et al. 2025), which pads harmful queries with
long benign puzzle reasoning to push attack success above
94% on frontier models. We use that result as motivation
for a narrower numeric answer-hijack proxy rather than as
evidence that GSM8K answer recovery measures harmful-
query safety. A concurrent line (Huang et al. 2026) attacks
CoT in vision-language-action models via adversarial image
patches; despite the surface-level naming overlap (“CoT hi-
jack”), their threat model is image-side perturbation of an
embodied policy, distinct from our text-prompt CoT injec-
tion on text-only LLMs. Our work differs by probing local-
ized recovery for two of these hijack families (puzzle and
few-shot) rather than auditing the full chain or claiming a
complete safety evaluation.
Activation steering and intervention-based safety meth-
ods.
A complementary line of work intervenes on acti-
vations rather than weights to control behavior. Turner et
al. (Turner et al. 2023) introduced ActAdd, adding contrast-
derived steering vectors at inference time without any opti-
mization, and Inference-Time Intervention (Li et al. 2023)
showed that head-level shifts along a truthfulness direction
raise TruthfulQA accuracy on Llama. Representation Engi-
neering (Zou et al. 2023a) generalizes these directions to a
family of population-level reading and control vectors. On
the safety side, Circuit Breakers (Zou et al. 2024) re-routes
harmful representations during fine-tuning rather than at in-
ference, and Olsson et al. (Olsson et al. 2022) provided
the mechanistic substrate (induction heads) that many in-
context attacks, including hijacking, are believed to exploit.
Three concurrent activation-based defenses share our setting
most closely: a token-activation jailbreak defense for small
language models (Mia et al. 2026), an activated-concept-
analysis defense that recognizes toxic concepts in jailbreak
prompts (Zhang et al. 2025), and a global-optimization
framework for attributing safety vectors (Chu et al. 2026).
All three identify a meaningful subspace (toxic-concept di-
rection, safety-vector direction, or token-activation classi-
fier) and steer along it. Our work differs by replacing opti-
mization or steering-vector design with a single-layer K-shot
patch from a clean reference forward pass in a controlled
answer-hijack proxy: we make no assumption about which
direction is harmful, only that the L⋆activations of a benign
reference, random source, zero source, or universal donor
can interrupt the tested answer-hijack trajectory in selected
cells.
Cross-architecture circuit universality.
Whether inter-
pretability findings transfer across model families is con-
tested. Olsson et al. (Olsson et al. 2022) argued that in-
duction heads are universal across attention-only and full
transformer models, and Wang et al. (Wang et al. 2023)
reported that the IOI circuit reappears in larger GPT-2
variants. More recent cross-architecture studies are mixed:
Tan et al. (Tan et al. 2024) find partial mechanistic simi-
larity between transformer and Mamba models on shared
tasks but also family-specific implementations. Patchscopes-
style methods (Ghandeharioun et al. 2024) explicitly sup-

port cross-model patching, yet practitioners observe that the
same intervention rarely produces identical effects in Qwen-

Negative results and the limits of localization.
A grow-
ing literature pushes back on confident localization claims.
Hase et al. (Hase et al. 2023) have shown that the layers
“causally important” for ROME-style edits are not the lay-
ers that store the information, and Lanham et al. (Lanham
et al. 2023) suggest CoT may be largely post-hoc; “Stop
Anthropomorphizing Intermediate Tokens” (Gundawar et al.
2025) argues that intermediate tokens should not be read as
faithful traces at all. Our results align with this skeptical
line in two ways. First, sycophancy at K=1 has markedly
smaller spread (0.07–0.13) than few-shot or puzzle hijacks
(0.32–0.57); only at K=16 does Qwen-sycophant local-
ize, echoing two-stage sycophancy emergence (Wang et al.
2025; Sharma et al. 2024). Second, our source-control and
donor checks show that clean-source identity is not the only
relevant variable: finite-sample no-detected-clean-advantage
cells do not show a clean-source advantage under Wilson
intervals, but this is not an equivalence proof, and zero-
magnitude controls can still fail in Qwen-puzzle. Thus L⋆
often reflects fragility, not content, but the required inter-
vention magnitude is cell-dependent. Our work differs by
testing this as bounded conditional recovery: a lightweight
K-shot disruption at a fragile layer produces numeric an-
swer recovery even when no same-problem clean activation
is available in the tested Qwen-puzzle cell.
Position relative to layer-aware code-LLM robustness
editing.
The closest concurrent work is Lin et al. (Lin
et al. 2025), who edit specific transformer layers in Code-
Gen and CodeLlama to improve robustness against ReCode-
style perturbations. Both papers identify mid-late layers as
privileged intervention sites, but the intervention and threat
model differ: their method edits weights to improve clean
code robustness, while ours patches activations at inference
to probe active CoT answer hijacks. Table 1 gives the com-
pact axis-by-axis comparison.

3.3
Localized Fragility Hypothesis
If L⋆is a faithful mediator of the hijack signal, clean patches
should outperform random patches by a wide margin: ran-
dom patches inject noise rather than the operative compu-
tation. If instead L⋆is a fragility bottleneck, any sufficient
disruption breaks the hijack pathway, and clean and random
patches should produce similar recovery rates; zero patches
provide a stricter magnitude control that can fail when non-
trivial activation energy is needed.
We test this hypothesis directly in §6.

Experimental Setup
Models.
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Qwen Team 2024) (28
layers) and Llama3-8B-Instruct (Llama Team, AI @ Meta
2024) (32 layers), both INT4 NF4 quantized for hardware
feasibility on a 24 GB GPU.
Hijack types.
Three hijack templates spanning the ma-
jor published failure modes: (1) few-shot mislabeled exem-
plars where the answer to a prior puzzle is wrong; (2) puzzle
prompts that interleave a misdirection riddle with the user’s
question; and (3) sycophant injections that prefix the user’s
question with a confident but incorrect claim. Prompts are
generated by instantiating these templates on each filtered
benchmark problem.
Benchmark.
GSM8K (Cobbe et al. 2021), n up to 30
hijacked-and-flipped problems per cell. We filter for prob-
lems where the clean answer matches ground truth and the
hijacked answer differs.
Layer sweep.
Every other layer for the full architecture:
{0, 2, 4, . . . , L −2, L −1}, yielding 14–16 layer probes per
model. Patch lengths K ∈{1, 2, 4, 8, 16}.
Statistical reporting.
We report point recovery rates and
the spread metric. Confidence intervals use Wilson score on
the binary recovery indicator. The cross-architecture spread
surface uses n = 19–30 per cell; later source-control,
universal-donor, transfer, and padding probes use the sample
size reported in their tables. We use spread@K=1 ≥0.30
as a descriptive localization threshold and report the within-
cell layer-permutation null separately (per-problem recovery
labels permuted across layers, 5,000 trials per cell; null 95th
percentile is 0.32 for n=19×15 and 0.27 for n=30×17).
For the K-sweep we report pbonf ≤0.05 as the permutation-
confirmed cutoff. A cell carries a confirmatory band-level
localization claim only when this cutoff is paired with a pos-
itive held-out in-band minus out-of-band gap. We also mark
pbonf ≤0.20 as an exploratory small-n signal; cells in this
range are not treated as confirmatory localization results.

Evaluation setting.
We assume a white-box operator with
control over a layer-level hidden-state hook on an open-
weights LLM serving system. The input can use arbitrary
text prompts, including the three answer-hijack families
above, but the evaluation does not modify model weights or
the patching machinery. We do not assume a clean-trace or-
acle for the attacked input; the universal-donor variant (§7)
requires only one fixed donor activation. We do not claim
robustness to a fully adaptive attacker with full knowledge
of L⋆and the patch length K (e.g. a gradient-based suf-

the patch hook). As a cheap stress test, we run a one-token
coordinate-search padding attack on Qwen-fewshot in Lim-
itations (iv); it partially degrades recovery, so adaptive eval-
uation remains an open boundary rather than a solved threat
model.

Patch Source Controls and Margin
Diagnostics
To test whether the representative band hook is a faithful me-
diator of the hijack signal or a no-detected-clean-advantage
fragility point, we replace the clean patch source with three
controls: random Gaussian (matched-std), zero, and cross-

The band view gives a descriptive taxonomy.

Discussion
Mediator vs. fragility bottleneck vs. band.
The clas-
sical mediation framework (Vig et al. 2020; Meng et al.
2022; Conmy et al. 2023) treats activation patching as a
content-restoring operation: a clean trace overwrites a cor-
rupted computation. Our results suggest a complementary
phenomenon: a fragility band in which localized interven-
tion susceptibility can recover the answer without estab-
lishing same-problem clean-source mediation. The Llama3-
puzzle addendum separates these notions. The fixed hook re-
mains stable at larger n, recovering 39.0% at n=100, so the
cell is not a small-sample artifact. At the same time, the ex-
panded source-control check does not favor same-problem
clean activations: clean-source recovery is 38.3%, while ran-
dom and zero interventions recover 46.7% and 33.3%, and
a cross-layer source inside the narrow band recovers 56.7%.
This combination supports the central diagnostic claim: lo-
calization can be stable even when clean-source seman-
tic mediation is absent. Llama3-fewshot is different: clean
outperforms random by +40.0 points, making it a content-
mediator cell. ROME-style facts and IOI-style circuits are
content-bound; CoT hijack signals are often band-bound,
but the regime is heterogeneous. Future work should test
whether other safety-relevant phenomena (e.g. jailbreaks via
gradient-based suffix attacks (Zou et al. 2023b)) follow a
fragility, mediator, or mixed regime, and whether the regime
correlates with hijack type or training recipe rather than
scale alone.
Sycophancy localizes only at long K.
Our spread@K=1
of 0.07–0.13 on sycophant hijacks at K=1 is not a measure-
ment failure of the localization apparatus: both sycophant
cells show their strongest structure only at longer patch
lengths. Qwen-sycophant reaches spreadK=0.37 at K=16
with within-cell layer-permutation punc=0.020 (Bonferroni-
corrected 0.101 over the 5-K sweep). Llama3-sycophant
is weaker: the original 5-K grid stays below threshold,
while the dense sweep reaches only threshold-level spread at
K=12 (spreadK=0.30, L⋆=10), without permutation con-
firmation. The slow emergence is consistent with reports that
sycophancy involves a two-stage process across mid- and
late-layer representations (Wang et al. 2025) rather than a
content-bound circuit such as IOI (Wang et al. 2023). Con-
current work argues that sycophancy is also localizable in
multi-agent debate (Kumarappan and Mujoo 2026), where
activation patching pinpoints corruption to a narrow mid-
layer attention window; combined with our long-K result,
this suggests that sycophancy admits localization under mul-
tiple instantiations (longer patch on a single prompt, or
single-token patch in a multi-turn setting), in contrast to the
original RLHF-globally-distributed picture of (Sharma et al.
2024).
Relation to noise-as-defense baselines.
A natural con-
cern is whether random-Gaussian patching is merely input-
side smoothing such as RESTA (Hase et al. 2025). The
mechanisms differ: our intervention is a single hidden-state
replacement at a localized mid-network band, while in-
put smoothing perturbs embeddings and aggregates mul-
tiple noisy generations. The direct baseline suite in §7
confirms the empirical distinction on two puzzle cells.

Limitations.
(i) Two model families and one main bench-
mark (GSM8K) is a small sample for cross-architecture
claims. The frozen MATH-500 transfer check in §7 re-
covers 26.0% of qualified hijacked instances in the largest
fixed-transfer run (13/50, Wilson 95% CI [15.9%, 39.6%]);
smaller n=30 and n=40 runs give 33.3% and 30.0%, re-
spectively. This supports nonzero transfer but not strong
dataset-universal robustness; MMLU-Pro and AGIEval re-
main open. Scale beyond 8B is unverified. A Qwen-puzzle
BF16 full-band sweep preserves a mid/late band signal
(n=30, spread 0.33 over ten probed layers, peak layer 20),
but exact layer identity still differs from the INT4 argmax.
(ii) L⋆is selected as arg maxℓrec(ℓ, K) on the full n
at the cell’s best K and should be read as a representa-
tive hook within a band, not as the stable scientific esti-
mand. Held-out 50/50 splits recover the same L⋆on Qwen-
few-shot and Llama-puzzle but disagree on the other four
cells; out-of-sample spread is on average 0.05–0.10 below
in-sample, indicating modest argmax-selection bias. A 3-
seed sensitivity check on Qwen-puzzle (seeds 42, 43, 44) re-
turns L⋆∈{18, 20, 26} with spread@K=1 in [0.43, 0.57]
and all seeds passing the localization threshold; the band
assignment is stable across these seeds, but the specific
L⋆index within the band is not. (iii) The intervention is
competitive with self-consistency 5-vote on Qwen-puzzle
hijacks (n=30, patch 60% vs. self-consistency 53%, both
Wilson CIs overlap). Against additional baselines on the
same Qwen-puzzle cell, patching is stronger than RESTA-
style input smoothing (60% vs. 23%, paired exact p=0.013)
and competitive with same-model paraphrase sanitization
(60% vs. 50%, paired exact p=0.581). A second Llama3-
puzzle baseline suite preserves the same sanity-check pat-
tern (patch 40.0%, RESTA-style smoothing 10.0%, para-
phrase 33.3%, perplexity filtering 0.0%). These are still
paired-cell comparators, not a universal defense ranking.
(iv) A one-token coordinate-search padding stress test on
Qwen-fewshot (n=19, frozen L⋆=16, K=1) partially de-
grades patch recovery: the original hijack recovers 32%
(6/19, Wilson 95% CI [15%,54%]), the optimized padding
token recovers 21% (4/19, [9%,43%]), and random one-
token padding remains 32% (6/19, [15%,54%]). The paired
exact McNemar/sign test does not reject equality at this
sample size (p=0.625). We therefore report a cheap adap-
tive stress boundary, not a full bypass and not an adaptive-
robustness guarantee. Larger-n and multi-token gradient-
based suffix attacks remain future work. (v) Production de-
ployment (latency, multi-prompt batching) is untested at
scale; our microbenchmark places the per-token overhead
in the ±5% noise band relative to no-hook generation on a
single 4090.
