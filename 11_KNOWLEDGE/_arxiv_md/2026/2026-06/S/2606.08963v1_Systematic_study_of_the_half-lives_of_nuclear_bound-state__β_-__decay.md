Systematic study of the half-lives of nuclear bound-state β−decay
Jing-Wen Ran and Long-Jun Wang∗
School of Physical Science and Technology, Southwest University, Chongqing 400715, China
(Dated: June 9, 2026)
Nuclear bound-state β−decay (βb decay) is a novel weak-interaction process that becomes possi-
ble when atoms are highly ionized, such as in stellar environments or heavy-ion storage rings. In this
work we present a systematic theoretical calculations for the βb-decay half-lives of interesting candi-
dates for the first time, where both allowed Gamow-Teller transitions and first-forbidden transitions
are taken into account by the microscopic projected shell model, and the lepton phase space is calcu-
lated by the Takahashi-Yokoi model. We analyzed the structure informations for hundreds of nuclei
near the β-stability line, and select 16 interesting candidates belonging to two categories, i.e., nuclei
with negative Q values and positive Q values in neutral atoms respectively. Among these candi-
dates, we recommend 243Am95+, 194Os76+, 227Ac89+, 228Ra88+, 241Pu94+, 247Cm96+ and 250Cm96+
as promising ones for future studies of storage-ring experiments because their βb-decay half-lives
are predicted to be much shorter than the half-lives in neutral atoms. These findings provide es-
sential nuclear inputs for astrophysical models and identify specific candidates where experimental
verification would be most valuable.
I.

Nuclear bound-state β−decay is one of the novel weak-
interaction processes. Different from the continuum β−

and antineutrino are emitted into the continuum states,
in the bound-state β−decay (βb decay, see Eq. (1b))

The exotic βb-decay channel is possible in stellar envi-
ronments with high temperature or in heavy-ion storage
rings where heavy atoms get highly ionized. The nuclear
βb decay is important for understanding the nature of
neutrinos and the slow (s-) neutron-capture processes [2].
The concept of the βb decay was first proposed by Daudel
et al. in 1947 [3]. A practical theoretical framework for
calculating the βb-decay half-life of highly ionized atoms
was developed by Takahashi and Yokoi in the 1980s [4].
Over the past decades, experiments at heavy-ion storage
rings were conducted and βb decays have been observed
sequentially in 163Dy66+ [5], 187Re75+ [6], 207Tl81+ [7]
and 205Tl81+ [8, 9]. More important candidates of the
nuclear βb decay are expected to be detected and stud-
ied, for which theoretical calculations and analysis should
be meaningful.
Theoretically, calculations of the βb-decay half-lives
are basically based on the Takahashi-Yokoi model [4]
where nuclear transition strengths, including both al-
lowed and forbidden transitions, are indispensable key

cause most of the βb-decay candidates are heavy nuclei
and it is challenging to describe the allowed and forbid-
den transitions of heavy nuclei.
Recently, we developed the traditional projected shell
model (PSM) [16, 17] to consider extended quasiparti-
cle (qp) configuration space [18, 19], and then to de-
scribe allowed Gamow-Teller (GT) transition [20, 21] and
first-forbidden transition [22] of nuclear β decay, with
the help of the Pfaffian and other algorithms [23–25].
The extended PSM method is then combined with the
Takahashi-Yokoi model to provide a microscopic method
to calculate the βb-decay half-lives for the first time
[26].
Accordingly, the βb-decay half-lives of 163Dy66+
and 187Re75+ are described successfully and the half-life
of 205Tl81+ is predicted [26].
Further systematic cal-
culations and research should be meaningful and ben-
eficial for future experimental studies.

III.
CALCULATIONS AND ANALYSIS
In this section we provide our systematic calculations
of nuclear βb-decay half-lives of interesting candidates.
As can be seen from Eq. (3) that Qβb usually differs from
Qβc. When atoms get highly ionized to be hydrogen-like
or even bare nuclei in stellar environments or heavy-ion
storage ring, the produced electron during βb decay can
occupy the K-shell orbitals of the daughter atom.
In
such a case, as BK
e−is usually larger than ∆Be−, Qβb is
then usually larger than Qβc. This indicates that, on one
hand, a stable atom with negative Qβc would become an
unstable nucleus if Qβb is positive and then the βb decay
channel is available in highly ionized environments. Typ-
ical examples of this type and the corresponding Q-value
information can be seen from Table I. On the other hand,
for some nuclei that can βc decay with small Qβc value,
the increased Qβb would enable transitions to more low-
lying states of the daughter nucleus, which may lead to
totally different βb-decay rate compared with βc-decay
rate. Typical examples of this type and the correspond-
ing Q-value information can be seen from Table II.
Before systematic calculations, we analyzed the Q-
value information and excitation levels for hundreds of
nuclei near the β-stability line on the nuclear chart,
and selected promising candidate nuclei according to the
above two categories. For the first category with nega-
tive Qβc but positive Qβb, except for the two examples
of 163Dy and 205Tl that have been studied in Ref. [26],
as well as the two special examples of 148Eu and 244Pu
for which the corresponding transitions are fifth forbid-
den transitions so that the βb decay should be negligible,
all the remaining candidates are listed in Table I. The
second category consists of nuclei that are already β−-
unstable in the neutral atoms (Qβc > 0), for which the
βb channel provides additional decay paths to low-lying
states of the daughter nucleus. In this case, since for large
Qβc value, the βb decay channel would be negligible com-
pared with the βc decay channel because the latter has a
much larger lepton phase space than the former, accord-
ingly, we restrict our selection in this category to nuclei
with Qβc ≲200 keV and ensuring that more transitions
to low-lying states become possible when the Q-value in-

It is seen from Table III that the βb-decay half-lives
are usually different from the corresponding half-lives in
neutral atoms. Experimentally, more interest lies in cases
and examples where the βb-decay half-life is much shorter
than that of the neutral atom. Actually, the βb-decay
half-life is mainly determined by the Qβb value and the
ft value, as can be seen from Eqs. (2, 8). The larger
the Qβb value and/or the smaller the logft value, the
shorter the half-life. As shown in Tables I and III, for
193Ir which is stable in neutral atom, since the Qβb value
is as small as 18.16 keV and the logft values are predicted
to be ≳7, the βb-decay half-life is as large as 198.3 years.
The unique first-forbidden transition is usually very weak
with a large logft value, as shown in the three examples
202Tl, 222Rn and 246Bk in Table III, their logft values
are indeed large (logft ≳10), which lead to that the βb-
decay half-lives of these three nuclei are longer than 105
years. For 194Au, 213Po and 215At, although the corre-
sponding first-forbidden transitions are very strong with
logft ≈6, and their corresponding βb-decay half-lives as-
sociated with these transitions range from 1 to 20 years,
the half-lives in neutral atoms by electron capture, β+
decay or α decay are much shorter, ranging from hours
down to microseconds. These findings indicate that the
above seven candidates in the first category, i.e., 193Ir,
194Au, 202Tl, 213Po, 215At, 222Rn and 246Bk should not
be considered promising for future experimental studies
as their βb-decay half-lives are either too long or much
longer than the corresponding half-lives in neutral atoms.
A special candidate is 243Am, which has the largest
Qβb value in the first category with Qβb
= 114.84
keV as shown in Table I. In addition, the correspond-
ing ground-state to ground-state transition that has the
largest lepton phase space is predicted to be strong with
logft = 6.777. These two aspects result in a very short
βb-decay half-life of about 55 days. By comparison, in
neutral atoms, the half-life via α decay and spontaneous
fission is as long as 7345 years, which is longer than the
predicted βb-decay half-life by about five orders of mag-
nitude. Therefore, we recommend 243Am as a promising
candidate for future experimental studies of nuclear βb
decay.

IV.
SUMMARY
In summary, βb decay represents an essential decay
channel for nuclei immersed in stellar plasmas or heavy-
ion storage ring. In this work, we perform a comprehen-
sive survey across the nuclear landscape to identify can-
didate nuclides that can undergo βb decay under highly
ionized conditions. Interesting candidates are considered
according to two distinct categories: (i) nuclides with
negative Qβc values in neutral atoms, for which the βb
decay channel becomes energetically allowed only upon
highly ionization; and (ii) nuclides are already unstable
with positive Qβc values in neutral atoms, while addi-
tional transitions are possible since the effective Q value
is enlarged from Qβc to Qβb. For each category, eight
representative nuclides are selected for detailed investi-
gation. Their log ft values are calculated microscopically
for the first time, by the PSM where both the allowed GT
and first-forbidden transitions are treated on an equal
footing. These transition strengths are then combined
with phase space factors obtained from the Takahashi-
Yokoi model to yield the βb-decay half-lives.

cally, among all the candidates studied, particular atten-
tion is drawn to 243Am, 247Cm, and 250Cm. For highly
ionized 243Am, the predicted βb decay half-life is about
55 days, in striking contrast to its neutral-atom α-decay
half-life of 7345 years — a reduction of nearly five or-
ders of magnitude. For 247Cm, the half-life plunges from
about 107 years in the neutral state (dominated by α de-
cay) to merely 0.026 years (9.52 days) under highly ion-
ized conditions, corresponding to a compression of nine
orders of magnitude. For 250Cm, the half-life decreases
from about 8300 years to 0.011 years (3.86 days), a reduc-
tion of almost six orders of magnitude. These extreme
cases vividly demonstrate how the ionization state can
fundamentally alter the decay dynamics of heavy nuclei,
transforming nuclides conventionally classified as typical
α emitters into rapid β−decayers in astrophysical envi-
ronments.
The present work provides the first systematic theoret-
ical investigation of βb decay half-lives for a diverse set of
candidate nuclei within a unified microscopic framework.
The predictions provide valuable guidance for future ex-
perimental efforts, particularly those at storage-ring fa-
cilities, and establish a foundation for more realistic as-
trophysical simulations in which highly ionized species
prevail. Future extensions of this work will incorporate
the Saha equation to determine the equilibrium popula-
tions of various ionization states under stellar conditions.
