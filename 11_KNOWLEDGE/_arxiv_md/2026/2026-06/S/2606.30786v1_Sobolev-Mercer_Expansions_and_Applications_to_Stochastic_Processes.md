Abstract
We establish a fundamental extension of Mercer’s celebrated theorem by introducing a class of higher-order kernel
operators acting on Sobolev spaces Hk(Θ), where Θ ⊂Rd is a bounded domain and k ∈N0 corresponds to the
order of weak differentiability. These operators transcend the classical L2-framework by explicitly incorporating the
information encoded in the (weak) derivatives of the kernel and correspond precisely to the Hilbert-Schmidt mappings
between Sobolev spaces. The spectral decomposition of these operators then yields Mercer-type expansions that are
optimal in Hk(Θ × Θ). Notably, we derive from the embedding properties of Sobolev spaces, that for k > d, these
expansions also converge uniformly without requiring the kernel to be positive definite. For positive definite kernels,
we confirm the nuclearity of these higher-order operators and establish a significant refinement of Mercer’s Theorem,
which ensures uniform convergence of all term-wise derivatives and provides explicit convergence rates (including
derivatives) tied to the spectral decay. These results lead to novel spectral representations of Reproducing Kernel
Hilbert Spaces and have subtle implications for stochastic analysis. Applied to the covariance kernels of weakly
differentiable random fields, our theory provides refined Karhunen-Loève expansions that facilitate the simultaneous
mean-square optimal approximation of both the process and its derivatives.
Keywords: Mercer’s Theorem, Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space, Karhunen-Loève, Positive Definite Kernel,
Weakly Differentiable Stoch. Process, Covariance Operator

1. Introduction
Since its publication in 1909, Mercer’s theorem [22] - named after James Mercer - has remained a cornerstone in
the spectral theory of compact operators. Originally emerging from the foundational work of Hilbert [15] and Schmidt
[31], the theorem now underpins several diverse fields. Most notably, it is central to integral equations and Fredholm
theory, serves as the basis for the Karhunen–Loève representation [18] in stochastic processes, and has more recently
become a vital theoretical tool for kernel methods in machine learning (e.g., [9]). The advancements presented in this
work significantly refine these classic results, which now emerge as specific instances of a more general framework.
To provide context for these developments, we begin with a concise review of the theorem’s current status.

Relevant literature. Due to their fundamental importance across the various applications mentioned above, numerous
generalizations of Mercer’s findings, the KL-expansion, and related results have emerged in the literature ever since.
Without claiming to be exhaustive, a few relevant sources are listed below: Regarding the underlying domain, the in-
terval [a, b] can be replaced by any compact subset of Rd, or more generally, any compact Hausdorff space equipped
with a finite Borel measure (e.g., [47]). The trace formula (1.5) was later established for a broader class of kernels
by Brislawn [3]. Extensions to unbounded domains in R were obtained by Buesco [4] and Buesco et al. [5]. Fur-
thermore, Mercer’s theorem has been extended to matrix-valued kernels by De Vito et al. [11] and only recently, the
equivalence of conditions (1.3) and (1.4) was confirmed for this setting by Neumann and Tuschmann [24]. A different
generalization of Mercer’s theorem by Ferreira and Menegatto [12] emphasized the connection between positive defi-
nite kernels, their integrability over the diagonal, and the nuclearity of the associated L2-integral operators. Similarly,
Steinwart and Scovel [32] and Steinwart [33] explored the extend to which topological assumptions on the domain
can be relaxed while ensuring that Mercer-like expansions and KL-representations still converge (almost everywhere)
pointwise or uniformly. The notion of a KL-representation has also been recently adapted to random measures by
Vergara [36] and to point processes by Picard et al. [25].
The term-wise differentiability of (1.1) and (1.6) was first investigated by Kadota [16, 17]. The decay rate of eigenval-
ues for the operator (1.2) was linked to the kernel’s order of differentiability by Reade [27, 28, 29], Ha [13] and later,
among others, by Buesco [7]. Most recently, Takhanov [34] demonstrated how kernel smoothness affects the rate of
uniform convergence by deriving the first explicit bounds using the powerful Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality.
Contributions and outline. As discussed in the introduction, Mercer expansions of the form (1.1) and the related
KL-representations (1.6) fundamentally rely on the spectral decomposition of the classic L2-integral operator (1.2).
The objective of this work is to transcend this limitation, enabling a more refined analysis for (weakly) differentiable
kernels.
In section 3, we show that the classic L2-integral operator is a special case within a broader class of so called kernel
operators Th,k of order k ∈N0 (see (3.1) for the definition). These operators can be associated with any sufficiently
weakly differentiable kernel h: Θ × Θ →R on a bounded domain Θ ⊂Rd. This framework allows to incorporate the
additional information encoded in the weak derivatives of a kernel more accurately and we show (Lemma 3.2), that
operators of this type correspond precisely to the class of Hilbert-Schmidt mappings between Sobolev spaces.
Leveraging the spectral theory for compact operators, we utilize the resulting eigenvalues and eigenvectors to con-
struct series representations of the form (1.1) for symmetric kernels, which turn out to be optimal for simultaneously
approximating both the kernel and its derivatives. If k > d and the boundary of Θ × Θ satisfies the cone property,
we derive (Theorem 3.4) uniform convergence of the series - including derivatives of order less than k −d - from a
suitable Sobolev embedding, notably without requiring positive definiteness. Furthermore, we also show that k > d/2
is sufficient to ensure the universal uniform boundedness of the eigenfunctions. These mark the first positive findings
of this kind for arbitrary symmetric indefinite kernels.
Without relying on a Sobolev embedding we then establish a significant refinement of Mercer’s theorem for strongly
differentiable positive definite kernels (Theorem 3.6) by showing uniform convergence of all term-wise derivatives in-
dependent of the dimension and the geometry of the domain. Provided Θ does satisfy the cone property and k > d/2,
the universal boundedness of the eigenfunctions translates into rates for the uniform convergence based solely on
eigenvalue decay (Corollary 3.8) in this case - including derivatives of order less than k −d/2. Moreover, we confirm
non-negativity and nuclearity of higher-order kernel operators (Corollary 3.7), leading to a direct extension of the
trace formula (1.5).
As an immediate consequence (Theorem 3.12), this leads to novel spectral representations for the reproducing kernel
Hilbert space (RKHS) associated with a positive definite kernel.
In section 4, we also present a second application by linking higher-order kernel operators to covariance operators of
weakly differentiable random fields: We establish (Lemma 4.2) a generalized version of the variance identity (1.7) and
refine the KL-representation (1.6) accordingly (Theorem 4.8), including specific rates (Corollary 4.9) and path-wise
convergence for Gaussian elements (Corollary 4.10).
Finally, we apply this theory to the integrated Brownian motion process (Examle 2). By analytically deriving its
H1-eigenvalues and -eigenfunctions (Example 3), we provide a direct comparison to standard L2-theory, undistorted
by numerical inaccuracies.

Sobolev embeddings. The prominence and utility of Sobolev spaces stems primarily from their embedding character-
istics (e.g., [1], [39]). These embeddings guarantee, among other things, the existence of continuous representatives
for sufficiently smooth domains, provided that certain dimension-dependent regularity conditions are met. A basic
assumption on the domain Θ in this context is the following:
Assumption 1 (Cone property). Given two points x, z ∈Rd and two open balls Bε(x) and Bτ(z) such that x < Bτ(z),
the set Cx = Bε(x) ∩{x + λ(y −x) | y ∈Bτ(z), λ > 0} is called a finite cone in Rd with vertex x. A bounded domain Θ
is said to have the cone property if there exists a finite cone C such that every point x ∈Θ is the vertex of a finite cone
Cx ⊆Θ that is congruent to C (i.e. Cx is obtained from C via rigid motion).
It is worth noting that common domains, such as rectangular or, more generally, convex domains, satisfy this
property. Furthermore, all results in this work requiring the cone property remain valid under the measure theoretic
weak cone condition introduced in [1], which is even less restrictive.
