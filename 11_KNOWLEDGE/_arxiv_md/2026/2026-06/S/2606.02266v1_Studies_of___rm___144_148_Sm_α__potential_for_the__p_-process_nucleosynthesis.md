Abstract
Nucleosynthesis reaction networks leading to p-nuclei involve a combination of
different types of photodisintegration and capture reactions, as well as β+ decays
or electron captures. Photodisintegration reactions involving α particles present a
particular interest as they serve as branching points of the reaction networks. The
cross sections of these reactions depend crucially on the α-nucleus interaction. The
α optical model potential (AOMP) is determined mostly by means of experimental
differential elastic scattering distributions. Several previous studies have focused
on the case of 144Sm, an intriguing p-nucleus that is semi-magic with 82 neutrons.
This work presents new experimental data on α elastic and inelastic scattering on
148Sm, its closest stable isotope. Isotopic effects on the description of the AOMP are
studied, as well as their consequences on the prediction of α-induced reaction cross
sections at astrophysical energies. It is shown that the isotopic ratio for (α, γ) cross
sections can be multiplied up to a factor of two when these effects are included.

Introduction
Reactions involving α particles play a key role in many nucleosynthesis processes, in-
cluding large reaction networks with thousands of exotic nuclei occurring in explosive
events. One of these situations is the p-process [1, 2], producing around 30 stable nuclei
between 74Se and 196Hg, called p-nuclei: these nuclei are neutron deficient and have very
low isotopic abundances. They are shielded from the dominant processes that produce
nuclei beyond iron, namely the s- and r-processes, which alternate between neutron cap-
tures and β-decays [3, 4]. The main scenario to explain the production of these p-nuclei
is called γ-process [5, 6]. It starts from seed nuclei produced beyond iron by neutron
captures, which then encounter very high temperatures (a few GK) during a supernova
explosion. At such temperatures, photodisintegrations first remove neutrons by (γ, n) re-
actions until reaching the neutron-deficient part of the nuclear chart, then the reactions
proceed mostly with (γ, p), (γ, α), (n, γ), and in the low-mass region, (p, γ) [2].
The modeling of such reaction network requires theoretical calculations based on the
Hauser-Feshbach statistical formalism [7], giving cross sections that depend especially on
the particle transmission probabilities, nuclear level densities and γ strength functions.
The α transmission is usually determined in the framework of the optical model, using
an α-nucleus optical model potential (AOMP) made of a real part, which accounts for
elastic scattering, and an imaginary part responsible for all non-elastic channels. Many
efforts are devoted to the improvement of a global AOMP that would be reliable for a
large range of nuclei, at energies relevant to astrophysical processes. In the case of (α, γ)
reactions in the p-process, the corresponding Gamow peak is typically around 5 to 10
MeV.
In a pioneering work by McFadden and Satchler in 1966 [8], the authors analysed
α scattering data at 24 MeV on 19 nuclei from oxygen to uranium to obtain the mass-
dependent parametrization of a Woods-Saxon AOMP. This work has been widely used in
nucleosynthesis calculations. However, in 1997, a measurement of the 144Sm(α, γ)148Gd
reaction in the 10-13 MeV energy range by Somorjai et al. [9] attracted attention on the
inadequacy of existing AOMP to reproduce the α reactions at astrophysical energies,
predicting in this case far too large cross sections. Modern attemps to define a global
AOMP suitable for nuclear astrophysics are now made compatible with this constraint,
either through an energy dependance of the parameters [10, 11], or by defining an imag-
inary part that allows to reproduce the results of a simpler barrier transmission model
for the determination of the (α, γ) cross section [12].
The case of 144Sm, among the p-nuclei, has attracted a lot of attention even before the
challenging results by Somorjai, since the 146Sm/144Sm production rate was involed in a
method proposed in [13] to characterize the chronometry of p-process contributions to the
solar-system composition. In order to determine the α −144Sm AOMP, in 1997, Mohr et
al. [14] measured with high accuracy the 144Sm(α, α) elastic scattering cross section at 20
MeV. A more recent measurement was performed by Kiss et al. in 2022 [15], confirming
these previous results and extending them down to 16.13 MeV. In such elastic-scattering
measurements, the angular distribution at backward angles is the most impacted by the

Given that 144Sm is a semi-magic nucleus (N = 82), we complement in this work the
existing efforts focused on this nucleus with a study of its closest stable neighbour, 148Sm,
in order to examine the role of magicity on the AOMP properties that are extracted.
Isotopic effects in α elastic scattering were suggested to be a key observable to constraint
the AOMP, for instance by Palumbo et al. [18] who studied the tellurium isotopic chain
from 120Te to 130Te. Concerning samarium, little attention has been given to the AOMP
of the different isotopes since the work performed by Badawy et al. in 1978 [19], in which
the excitation function of the elastic α scattering close to 180◦was analyzed for several
nuclei, including 144,148,150,152Sm.
In the present work, we performed a measurement of α elastic scattering angular
distribution at 20 MeV (beam energy) on 144Sm and 148Sm in order to compare the
properties of the AOMP for the two isotopes, semi-magic and non-magic, and check the
reproduction of existing data on 144Sm. The experiment was performed at the ALTO
facility (Accélérateur Linéaire et Tandem d’Orsay) of IJCLab (Laboratoire de Physique
des 2 Infinis Irène Joliot-Curie), using the Split-Pole magnetic spectrometer [20], with
targets of high isotopic purity. We will show that relative observables comparing the two
isotopes are of great interest for the characterization of the AOMP.
The experimental setup is described in Section 2, target production and characteriza-
tion in Section 3. The data analysis procedure is explained in Section 4, and Section 5 is
dedicated to the discussion, comparing the elastic and inelastic data we obtained with the
predictions of different models calculated with the TALYS-2 reaction code [21]. Standard
AOMP models available are used, and some AOMP models adapted to the comparison of
the two samarium isotopes are built. The impact of isotopic effect on (α, γ) cross sections
at astrophysical energies, and on (α, n) cross sections below 20 MeV, is also discussed.

Experimental setup
The experiment was performed at the ALTO facility, located at IJCLab in Orsay, France.
The accelerator used for the present measurement is the 15 MV Tandem Van de Graaff,
capable of delivering high-intensity and stable ion beams for nuclear physics experiments.
In this study, a mono-energetic beam of α particles was accelerated to a laboratory energy
of 20 MeV. The typical beam current ranged from 20 enA to 100 enA, optimized to ensure
high statistics while minimizing damage on the targets and pile-up effects in the detectors.
The α beam was directed onto an isotopically enriched samarium target, either 144Sm
or 148Sm, mounted at the center of a high-vacuum scattering chamber. To perform angu-
lar distribution measurements, we employed a hybrid detection system combining high-

The Split-Pole spectrometer of ALTO uses three complementary detectors situated at

Samarium target characterization
For this experiment, highly enriched samarium targets were fabricated at the SIDONIE
isotope separator facility of IJCLab
[23]. SIDONIE is a 40 kV electromagnetic mass
separator, based on a Nier–Bernas ion source [24], capable of delivering rare isotopes
with high isotopic purity (> 99.9%) and high fluence (exceeding 1017 atoms/cm2 per
day) via low-energy (∼150 eV) ion beam deposition or implantation. The deposition
was controlled to achieve uniform layers by scanning the ion beam over the backing
substrates.
The enriched samarium isotopes were ionized in the Nier–Bernas source,
separated by mass, then decelerated to approximately 150 eV and deposited.
The two targets used in the experiment were prepared by ion-beam deposition onto
thin carbon backings of 50 µg/cm2, resulting in the following samarium surface densities:
• 144Sm target: 44 ± 4 µg/cm2;
• 148Sm target: 74 ± 6 µg/cm2.
These targets have been characterized after the data acquisition by Rutherford Backscat-
tering Spectrometry (RBS), to determine their effective thickness, spatial homogene-
ity and presence of contaminants.
The RBS measurements were performed with the
ARAMIS accelerator of IJCLab [25]. A 1.4 MeV beam of α particles was directed at
each target and the backscattered particles were detected at 165◦by a silicon detector.
The backscattered spectra were analyzed using the SIMNRA software [26] to extract
surface densities. Figure 3 displays a representative RBS spectrum fitted with SIMNRA.
Three distinct peaks are clearly identified: a samarium peak , a carbon peak correspond-
ing to the backing, and an oxygen peak arising from target oxidation.
This oxygen
contamination did not impact the analysis given the large difference in kinematics be-
tween elastic scattering on oxygen and samarium, suppressing any ambiguity in the peak
identification.
The variations between the thickest and thinnest regions reached 20%.
However,
given the typical beam profile during the main experiment and the impact area verified
by electronic microscopy, the uncertainty on the target thickness is limited to 7%.

5
Discussion
Let us now compare experimental results with model predictions in order to determine
to what extent the AOMP can be characterized by these observables. A typical study
would be to fit the elastic angular distributions, in order to define for the first time a
local α optical potential for 148Sm and compare it to the well-studied case of 144Sm, but
a fitting procedure is not significative here due to the large uncertainties on our data.
However, interesting features can be discussed by studying the isotopic ratio of elastic
scattering differential cross sections. We also investigate the model dependence of the
inelastic scattering to the first excited level of 148Sm. Finally, we will discuss the role
of AOMP in low-energy α-induced reactions that impact astrophysical rates involved in
the p-process.

Let us now introduce our adapted potentials. Local potentials for 144Sm(α, α) have
been fitted in previous work by Mohr-1997 [14] and Kiss-2022 [15]. In the present work,
however, we want to investigate the role of potential shape in the comparison between
α elastic scattering on 144Sm and 148Sm. For this, we work on potentials involving a
minimal number of parameters that can be reasonably adapted from 144Sm to 148Sm.
We have determined some of these parameters by fitting the Mohr-1997 data, using a
χ2 minimization procedure. The corresponding parameters are given with a precision
that verifies the following prescription: changing the last digit of the parameter value
increases the χ2 value at the percent level.
Our first adapted potential is based on the AOMP by McFadden and Satchler,
aop2 [8]. This well-known model was a pioneering attempt to obtain an AOMP that
can be used for a wide range of nuclei, where both real and imaginary parts are de-
scribed by a Woods-Saxon volume term with identical reduced radius ro and diffuseness
ao. The corresponding parameters for 148Sm have been studied in the work by Badawy-
1978 [19], where excitation functions of ASm(α, α)ASm (among others) are studied for
different samarium isotopes at collision energies around the Coulomb barrier. With fixed
values of the depths, V = 200 MeV and W = 20 MeV, it is shown in [19] that the radius
R and diffuseness a for 148Sm obey the following relationship:
R + α × a = 11.19 ± 0.03 fm
(7)
with α ≃7 (common to the various nuclei under study).
We have first used the data from Mohr-1997 [14] to fit aop2 radius and diffuseness
parameters for 144Sm, adopting the depth values used in [19].
We call the resulting
potential aop2-M. Then, we have used Eq. (7) to define three AOMP versions for 148Sm,
called aop2-Ba, aop2-Bb and aop2-Bc. The corresponding parameters are summerized
in Table 4. Note that the default aop2 parameters are identical for 144Sm and 148Sm,
while the inclusion of the Badawy constraint (7) leads to a larger spatial extension of the
potential for 148Sm:
• For aop2-Ba, this corresponds to a larger diffuseness (with a reduced radius taken
from aop2-M).
• For aop2-Bc, this corresponds to a larger radius (with a diffuseness taken from
aop2-M).
• aop2-Bb corresponds to an average of the two above choices.

5.4.1
Impact on (α, γ) reaction at astrophysical energy
The α-nucleus potential is a key ingredient for the numerous (γ, α) photodisintegrations

Conclusion and perspectives
The study of α elastic scattering isotopic ratios is important to characterize the isotopic
dependence of the AOMP. In the present work, we have measured the scattering of a
20 MeV α beam on two samarium targets of high isotopic purity, 144Sm and 148Sm, in
order to compare the AOMP properties of these nuclei. Angular distributions have been
measured between 31◦and 85◦with the Split-Pole magnetic spectrometer, and between
80◦and 163◦with silicon detector telescopes. New experimental data have been obtained
for relative cross sections: the elastic isotopic ratio Rel(148Sm/144Sm) and the inelastic
to elastic ratio Rinel.1(148Sm) (involving inelastic scattering to the first excited state).
While the case of 144Sm has already been the focus of many studies, very few data
are available on its closest stable isotope 148Sm. Since 144Sm is semi-magic, attention
should also be given to non-magic nuclei of this region in order to better constrain the
AOMP and obtain a reliable modeling of the nucleosynthetic reaction network in this
region, where the 152Gd(γ, α)148Sm reaction is in competition with 152Gd(γ, n)151Gd.
Concerning the isotopic ratio for elastic scattering, it is appearant that the AOMP
models whose real part is of Woods-Saxon type predict larger values than the ones whose
real part is calculated by double-folding. This is reflected by the different behavior of
the isotopic ratio of the real part of the potential, especially in the peripheric region.
However, it was not possible to extract a specific value of the distance for which the
value of this potential ratio would be decisive whatever the potential shape elsewhere.
The various characteristics of the AOMP present many correlations, some of which are
well-known (such as the so-called continuous ambiguities, see e.g. [8]), and others are
more intricate. In this context, machine-learning approaches are promising methods to
increase our knowledge of such correlations and their consequences. For instance, a recent
study by Marshall et al. [41] uses a bayesian framework to analyse the impact of α elastic
scattering data on 86Sr, of interest for the weak r-process [42]. We also plan to perform
bayesian studies in future works, extending the kind of studies we have performed in [43]
for nuclear level densities.
Concerning inelastic scattering, the obtained values for Rinel.1(148Sm) are highly pre-
cise, with small uncertainties. However, such observable does not allow to constrain the
AOMP, due to the large theoretical uncertainty on the treatment of inelastic scattering.
The coupled-channel approach gives results that differ from the DWBA approach, with
strong differences depending on the type of coupling (vibrational or rotational) and the
value of the deformation parameter. The best reproduction of our data is obtained for
a rotational coupling scheme with the experimental value of the deformation parameter
β2, although it overestimates the cross section at large angles. For such model, the sen-
sitivity to the AOMP is very weak. Anyway, a better control of the modeling of inelastic
cross section, including a precise value of the deformation parameter, is needed before
we can envisage to use it to constrain the AOMP.
We have also considered the impact of the AOMP on α-induced reactions at low en-
ergy, especially the (α, γ) cross section in the astrophysical energy range. Both (α, n) and
(α, γ) below neutron threshold are essentially sensitive to the α transmission probability.
26


## Page 27


In both cases, we have observed that the isotopic ratio of the cross sections for 148Sm
and 144Sm increases when we use versions of the AOMP that we have modified to obey
constraints from previous experimental data on these nuclei. Further, when adding two
of the treatments that have proved successful to reproduce the experimental 144Sm(α, γ)
cross section at low energy, we have found that they result in different isotopic ratios: the
parameter energy dependence from [11] reduces the isotopic ratio for (α, γ) and leaves it
roughly unchanged for (α, n), while setting a sharp imaginary part like in [12] results in
a significant increase of these ratios. In the case of (α, γ), the two prescriptions result in
isotopic ratios differing by roughly a factor of 2 below neutron threshold. We conclude
that it would be very valuable to perform measurements of α-induced reactions on 148Sm
at low energy to obtain experimental values of such ratios. This would help to improve
our knowledge of the isotopic dependence of the AOMP, address the magicity effect, and
better constrain the description of the optical potential toward the astrophysical energy
range.
