Abstract
Evolutionary entropy, introduced by Demetrius, is a demographic invariant that quantifies the temporal orga-
nization of structured populations. Explicit sensitivity expressions for this quantity were derived by Demetrius,
Gundlach and Ziehe for age-structured Leslie matrices, establishing the foundations of entropy-based perturbation
theory.
In this paper we develop a complete sensitivity theory for evolutionary entropy in irreducible Lefkovitch ma-
trices. Using the Perron–Frobenius representation of the associated Markov chain, we derive explicit closed-form
expressions for the stationary distribution, generation time, evolutionary entropy and its partial derivatives with
respect to fertility, transition and retention parameters. The resulting identities are expressed directly in terms of
demographic coefficients, Perron eigenvectors, the dominant eigenvalue and the reproductive potential.
The entropy representation obtained here gives a natural decomposition into transition and retention components
and clarifies the distinct mechanisms through which demographic uncertainty is generated in stage-structured
populations. We further show that the theory specializes immediately to open-group Leslie matrices, a class that
has been shown to comprise a large fraction of empirical demographic models.
The results extend the entropy sensitivity theory of Demetrius–Gundlach–Ziehe from age-structured to gen-
eral stage-structured populations and provide practical tools for comparative demographic analysis, perturbation
studies, demographic robustness, and the investigation of life-history strategies. Several biological examples are pre-
sented, illustrating how entropy decomposition and sensitivity analysis reveal complementary aspects of population
organization.
Keywords: evolutionary entropy; Lefkovitch matrices; entropy sensitivity; open-group Leslie matrices; stage-structured
populations; demographic robustness.

Introduction
Structured population models constitute one of the most successful applications of matrix theory to biology. Since
the pioneering works of Leslie [13,14], matrix population models have provided a mathematical setting for describing
population growth, stable demographic distributions, reproductive value and long-term population dynamics.
To
overcome the limitations of purely age-structured descriptions, Lefkovitch [12] introduced stage-structured matrices
allowing individuals to remain within the same developmental stage for variable periods of time.
These models
have become standard tools in ecology, conservation biology, forestry, plant demography and evolutionary biology
[3,11,15–17,19–21]. Today, matrix population models form the mathematical core of large demographic repositories
such as COMADRE and COMPADRE and are routinely used to analyse structured populations across a broad range
of ecological contexts.
A complementary line of research was initiated by Demetrius [7–9], who introduced evolutionary entropy as a
quantitative measure of demographic organisation. Whereas the dominant eigenvalue describes asymptotic population
growth, evolutionary entropy characterises the temporal organisation of demographic trajectories generated by the
underlying life cycle. Through its interpretation in terms of Markov chains, it connects demographic structure, robust-
ness, adaptation and organisation, thereby providing information complementary to that contained in the population
growth rate.

An important aspect of this theory concerns sensitivity analysis. Demetrius [6] derived sensitivity expressions
for the dominant eigenvalue, relating population growth to reproductive value and age-specific vital rates.
This
theory was subsequently developed by Caswell and collaborators [3, 4].
Subsequently, Demetrius, Gundlach and
Ziehe [10] obtained explicit sensitivity formulae for evolutionary entropy in age-structured Leslie matrices.
Their
results established evolutionary entropy as a natural object for perturbation analysis and provided formulae directly
expressed in terms of biologically meaningful demographic parameters.
The theory of [10], however, is restricted to Leslie matrices.
This limitation is significant because many con-
temporary applications involve stage-, size- or state-structured populations for which Lefkovitch matrices provide a
more realistic description. Although sensitivity and elasticity theory for population growth is well developed in the
Lefkovitch setting, explicit sensitivity formulae for evolutionary entropy do not appear to have been obtained.
A key observation motivating the present work is the similarity theorem established in [1], which shows that every
irreducible Lefkovitch matrix is similar to a suitable pseudo-Leslie matrix. This result establishes a bridge between
age-structured and stage-structured population models and suggests that an entropy sensitivity theory for Lefkovitch
matrices should exist.
The purpose of this paper is to derive explicit closed-form expressions for evolutionary entropy, generation time,
the associated Markov representation and the corresponding sensitivity coefficients in irreducible Lefkovitch matrices.
The resulting formulae are expressed directly in terms of fertility, transition and retention parameters, the dominant
eigenvalue and the associated reproductive potential.
The extension from Leslie to Lefkovitch matrices is not obtained by a simple replacement of variables. Stage-
retention effects introduce new demographic quantities, additional sources of uncertainty and new sensitivity directions.

Decomposition of evolutionary entropy
The similarity transformation established in [1] shows that every irreducible Lefkovitch matrix admits an associated
pseudo-Leslie representation. However, evolutionary entropy is not preserved by this transformation, and the pseudo-
Leslie matrix may contain negative pseudo-fertilities. Hence, entropy cannot in general be identified with an entropy
associated with the pseudo-Leslie representation.

conceptually distinct contributions. The first is associated with the transition structure encoded by the distribution
qj, whereas the second is generated by the retention mechanisms characteristic of Lefkovitch models.

Examples
The theoretical results obtained in the previous sections transform evolutionary entropy into a directly computable
demographic quantity. For any irreducible Lefkovitch matrix, one may determine the associated Markov chain, gen-
eration time, reproductive potential, entropy decomposition and sensitivity coefficients.
The examples considered below illustrate these concepts in a variety of biological settings. Particular attention
is given to the comparison between population growth and evolutionary entropy, the role of stage retention, and the
specialization of the theory to open-group Leslie matrices, a demographic structure frequently encountered in empirical
applications.

Conclusions
The explicit entropy sensitivity formulae obtained by Demetrius, Gundlach and Ziehe for Leslie matrices extend
naturally to the broader class of irreducible Lefkovitch matrices. The resulting expressions provide closed-form rep-
resentations for evolutionary entropy, its decomposition, and its sensitivities with respect to fertility, transition and
retention parameters.
The analysis shows that stage retention introduces a distinct source of demographic uncertainty which has no
counterpart in purely age-structured models. This contribution appears explicitly through the retention component
of entropy and may dominate the demographic organisation of stage-structured populations, as illustrated by the
biological examples considered here.
The theory specializes immediately to open-group Leslie matrices, a class that encompasses a large proportion
of the animal population models currently available in COMADRE and many demographic models used in human
population studies. The expressions derived here therefore apply to a substantial part of the structured populations
encountered in practice.
By placing Lefkovitch and open-group Leslie models within a common entropy-based perturbation context, the
present results enlarge the scope of evolutionary entropy as a quantitative tool for demographic analysis and provide
a foundation for future large-scale studies of the COMADRE and COMPADRE databases and beyond.
