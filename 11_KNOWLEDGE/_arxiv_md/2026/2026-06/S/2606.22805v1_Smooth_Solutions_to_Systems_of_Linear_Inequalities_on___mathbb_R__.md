In [11], the authors ask whether an inequality analog for Theorem 1.1 holds for the system
(1.1) on Rn, i.e., whether solvability of (1.1) can likewise be characterized by finitely many linear
differential inequalities of the form Lνfi ≥0 or Lνfi > 0. In the same paper, the authors proved

infinite intersection of convex polytopes. Such an intersection need not be describable by finitely
many linear inequalities. This is the mechanism behind the higher-dimensional counterexample
in [11]. However, the case n = 1 was left open.
In the present paper, we restrict our attention to the univariate case. In dimension one, after
a finite semialgebraic decomposition of the line, all limiting behavior is controlled by finitely many
one-sided germs. This allows the Glaeser refinement process, which can be phrased in terms of
limits of divided differences, to remain finitely describable at every stage.
