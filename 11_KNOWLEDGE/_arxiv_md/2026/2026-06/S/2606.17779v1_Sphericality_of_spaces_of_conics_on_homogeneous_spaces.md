SPHERICALITY OF SPACES OF CONICS
ON HOMOGENEOUS SPACES
MINSEONG KWON AND NICOLAS PERRIN
Abstract. We prove that the Hilbert scheme of conics on X = G/P, a pro-
jective rational homogeneous space with Picard rank one, is G-spherical if and
only if P is associated to a long root of the Dynkin diagram.
1. Introduction
1.1. Motivation. We work over C, the field of complex numbers. Rational curves
have been widely used in the study of Fano manifolds.
Rational homogeneous
spaces form an important class of Fano manifolds, and the geometry of lines on
rational homogeneous spaces is well understood. Various characteristic properties of
rational homogeneous spaces of Picard rank one have been revealed based on a good
understanding of the space of lines. Notable examples include: characterisation
of rational homogeneous spaces via their spaces of lines ([HH05]); deformation
rigidity of rational homogeneous spaces ([HM02]); characterisation of surjective
morphisms from rational homogeneous spaces of Picard rank one (answer to the
so-called Lazarsfeld conjecture; see [Hw01, §4.5]). We refer to [LM04] for a further
discussion.
It is natural to expect that the study of rational curves of higher degree should
provide a useful tool. However, spaces of rational curves of higher degree have not
been fully described, even in the case of conics, i.e., rational curves of degree two.
The main difficulty is that the space of smooth rational curves is no more compact
if the degree is at least two, and hence one need to consider its compactification.
A natural compactification is the Hilbert scheme of conics, and in this paper, we
investigate the Hilbert scheme of conics on a rational homogeneous space. Our
main theorem states that for a rational homogeneous space of Picard rank one, the
Hilbert scheme of conics is a spherical variety with only few exceptions.
1.2. Statement of main theorem. Let X be a rational homogeneous space of
Picard rank one. Then X = G/P for a simple Lie group G and a maximal parabolic
subgroup P ⊂G. Denote by ϖ the fundamental weight defining P and by δ the
simple root with ⟨δ∨, ϖ⟩= 1.
Define Hilbd(X) := Hilbdt+1(X ⊂P(Vϖ)), the
Hilbert scheme of closed subschemes with Hilbert polynomial dt+1. The structure
of Hilb1(X), the Hilbert scheme of lines on X, is well understood.

Indeed, it turns out that, whether δ is long or not, Hilb1(X) is a G-spherical

It is known in the literature that for some X, the Hilbert scheme Hilb2(X) of
conics is also G-spherical: see Corniani–Massarenti [CM22] when X = LG(2, 4),
and the first author [Kw23] when X is an adjoint variety not of type A nor C. On
the other hand, in contrast to the case of lines, Hilb2(X) is not always G-spherical,
as shown in the following examples.

Now a natural question arises: for which X is Hilb2(X) G-spherical? Our main
theorem gives a complete answer to this question.
Proposition 1.3. Hilb2(X) is a smooth irreducible variety.
Theorem 1.4. Hilb2(X) is G-spherical if and only if δ is a long simple root.
Notice that given a normal G-variety, being G-spherical only depends on the
open G-orbit. Therefore the G-sphericality still holds true for other (normalized)
compactified spaces of conics on X where the natural G-action extends such as the
Chow variety or the space of unmarked stable maps, provided that δ is long.
It is worth mentioning that the length condition in Theorem 1.4 is same with
that in Theorem 1.1. Thus we conclude that Hilb1(X) is G-homogeneous if and
only if Hilb2(X) is G-spherical.

Corollary 1.7. In Notation 1.6, Hilb2(X) is Aut(X)-spherical.
Recall that B¨arligea ([B¨a18, B¨a21]) proved existence of open dense Aut(X)-
orbit in M 0,3(X, d), the Kontsevich moduli space of genus zero stable maps with
3 marked points on X (see [FP97]), for small d. In particular, Hilb2(X), which is

stronger statement:
Corollary 1.8. In Notation 1.6, Hilb2(X) has only finitely many Aut(X)-orbits.
Finally, we record another corollary of Theorem 1.4, which is of practical impor-
tance from the birational geometric point of view.
Corollary 1.9. In Notation 1.6, Hilb2(X) is a Mori dream space.
Some particular cases of Corollary 1.9 are known in the literature: see Chen–
Coskun [CC11] when X is a projective space of small dimension, and Corniani–
Massarenti [CM22] when X is a Lagrangian Grassmannian. Corollary 1.9 follows
from Theorem 1.4 together with the well-known fact that any Q-factorial spherical
variety is a Mori dream space, as shown by Brion [Br93].
1.4. Outline of two proofs. We present two independent proofs of Theorem 1.4.
A geometric proof based on the fact that we may replace conics by bigger quadrics
in X and obtain a fibration of the open G-orbit in Hilb2(X) to a smaller ho-
mogeneous space (see Sections 3–5). More precisely, for x, y ∈X, set d(x, y) =
min{d | x and y are on a degree d rational curve} and define Γd(x, y) as the locus
covered by degree d rational curves passing through x an y. B¨arligea’s Theorem
implies that Aut(X) acts transitively on pairs of elements x, y ∈X general with
d(x, y) = 2. In particular for such x, y the variety Γ2(x, y) does not depend on
x and y general . We denote by Γ2 this variety (see Section 3 for an alternative
definition of Γ2). In particular, we prove the following result.
Proposition 1.10. Assume that X is not a projective space.
(1) A general conic is contained in a unique translate of Γ2.
(2) Γ2 is a smooth quadric maximal for the inclusion.
(3) StabG(Γ2) is always connected and spherical except for X = F4/P3.
Remark 1.11. The quadric Γ2 is not a quadric of maximal dimension contained
in X in general (see Example 3.13) but there is no quadric Q with Γ2 ⊊Q ⊂X.

The second proof of Theorem 1.4 is based on representation theoretic computa-
tions of normal spaces of closed orbits in Hilb2(X) (see Sections 6–7). To be precise,
consider a B-stable conic C on X and the G-orbit G.C in Hilb2(X). Then a de-

orem, the normal space NC := TC Hilb2(X)/TC(G.C), equipped with the isotropy

by case. In the process of the proof, we obtain the following result.
Proposition 1.14. If δ is long, then Hilb2(X) is a G-spherical variety of rank at
most 4.
Actually for δ long, the rank of Hilb2(X) as a G-spherical variety is 2, 3 or 4.
