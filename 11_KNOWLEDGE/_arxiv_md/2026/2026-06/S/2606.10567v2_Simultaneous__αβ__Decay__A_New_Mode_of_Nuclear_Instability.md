Simultaneous αβ Decay: A New Mode of Nuclear Instability
W. Q. Zhang1, 2, ∗and C. Qi3, †
1State Key Laboratory of Heavy Ion Science and Technology,
Institute of Modern Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Lanzhou 730000, China
2University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100049, China
3KTH, Alba Nova University Center, SE-10691 Stockholm, Sweden
(Dated: July 13, 2026)
We propose simultaneous αβ decay as a novel mode of nuclear instability that involves the strong
and weak interactions in a single quantum transition. We develop a theoretical framework to predict
its branching ratios and α-energy spectra, establishing exclusive and inclusive criteria based on
whether the individual α and β channels are closed or open. A global survey of the nuclear chart
identifies five exclusive αβ−candidates, all predicted to be experimentally inaccessible, and ranks
the leading inclusive candidates for both the αβ−and αβ+ modes. Remarkably, six of the top
αβ+ candidates coincide with known β-delayed-α precursors. The observed α spectra are naturally
accounted for by simultaneous αβ+ emission, suggesting direct decay as the dominant underlying
mechanism. Our findings establish simultaneous αβ decay as a distinct radioactive process and a
sensitive probe of the interplay between the strong and weak interactions.
Radioactive decay is a fundamental quantum process
driving unstable nuclei toward energetically favorable
configurations
via
the
strong,
electromagnetic,
or
weak interaction.
The conventional α,
β,
and γ
decay modes have long formed the cornerstones of
our understanding of nuclear instability.
In recent
decades, exotic multiparticle decay modes involving the
simultaneous emission of two or more identical particles,
including the 2ν2β and 0ν2β decays driven by the weak
interaction [1, 2], electromagnetic 2γ decay [3–5], and
hadronic 2n [6], 2p [7–9] and 2α [10–12] decays, have
been systematically investigated, greatly enriching the
landscape of known radioactive processes.
These rare
decay modes can serve as sensitive probes of few-body
correlations [13], shell effects [14], and the limits of
nuclear stability [15]. Yet, despite substantial attention
to multiparticle extensions of single established decay
modes, simultaneous transitions involving two distinct
decay modes have rarely been considered.
This raises
a fundamental question:
if two decay modes of a
given nucleus are energetically forbidden or disfavored
individually but allowed simultaneously, can the nucleus
decay through the combined process as a single quantum
transition?
In
this
work,
we
investigate
the
possibility
of
simultaneous αβ decay as a distinct mode of nuclear
instability. It can be classified into three types: αβ−,
αβ+, and αEC. Taking the αβ+ type as an example,
Figs. 1(a) and 1(b) illustrate the decay process and the
corresponding path on the nuclear chart, respectively.
Unlike
hypothetical
baryon-number-violating
proton
decay [16] and lepton-number-violating 0ν2β decay [1],
simultaneous αβ decay does not violate any known exact
conservation law and is therefore allowed in principle.
It provides a rare example in which weak-interaction
operators act directly on cluster configurations, offering
a unique testing ground for the interplay between
the strong and weak interactions.
Through detailed
balance,
the same matrix element formally defines
a weak-force-assisted α capture process, although its
astrophysical impact remains to be explored.
We focus our theoretical framework on the αβ−
and αβ+ modes, where the emitted leptons provide a
well-defined leptonic phase space.

We now apply this framework to a global search
for simultaneous αβ-decay candidates.
It is essential
to distinguish the exclusive and inclusive cases of
simultaneous αβ
decay.
The former requires the
combined channel to be open and the individual α and
β channels to be closed, whereas the latter requires
only the simultaneous channel to be open.
We first
consider the exclusive case, defined by Qαβ > 0, Qα < 0,
and Qβ < 0 (where Qβ denotes the Q value of the

However, statistical-model calculations [36, 37]
for β-delayed-particle emission predicted much broader
α spectra and much smaller α branching ratios than
those measured [33, 34]. Tidemand-Petersson et al. [33]
showed that agreement with the data required three

(1) an enhanced level density, (2) a Gaussian resonance in

Consequently,
the

However, a recent experimental study of
116Cs [35]

a width of 2431 keV in the β-decay daughter nucleus
116Xe.
Moreover, at the same excitation energy, a
much narrower proton-emitting structure with a width
of 111 keV is resolved in the delayed-proton spectrum,
whereas no corresponding structure is observed in the
delayed-α spectrum.
These difficulties motivate a comparison between
the
β-delayed-α
emission
interpretation
and
the
simultaneous αβ+ decay interpretation.
The essential
difference between the two pictures lies in the decay
mechanism.
Conventional β-delayed-α emission is a
sequential two-step process that requires the population
of real excited states in the intermediate nucleus.
By
contrast, simultaneous αβ+ decay proceeds, through
virtual
intermediate
states,
as
a
single
quantum
transition directly to the same four-body final state.
Consequently, the simultaneous αβ+ component may
become increasingly competitive at large Qαβ+, where
the decay energy can be shared continuously among the
α particle and the leptons, allowing the α particle to
carry enough energy to overcome the Coulomb barrier
while still leaving sufficient energy for the leptonic phase
space.
Nevertheless, in both cases the positron and α particle
would appear experimentally “simultaneous”, making it
almost impossible to distinguish the two mechanisms
through their time correlation.
Thus, the distinction
relies mainly on the comparison between the predicted
and experimental α-energy spectra.

above, reproducing the observed Gaussian-like α-energy
spectra within the β-delayed-α interpretation requires
the
introduction
of
a
Gaussian
resonance
in
the
β-strength function [33]. For simultaneous αβ+ decay,
the α-energy distribution is determined solely by the
integrand g(Eα, lα), in analogy with the Fermi integral
that gives the shape of ordinary β spectra.

This leads to a central question:
were the β-α

simultaneous αβ+ decay?
Although no conclusive

The

known cases; a dedicated search for this high-energy

different nuclear region.
In summary, we have proposed and formulated a
theoretical description of simultaneous αβ decay as a
new mode of nuclear instability.
Under the exclusive
criterion,
only five αβ−
candidates were identified
across
the
nuclear
chart,
all
beyond
experimental
reach.
We
evaluated
inclusive
candidates
with
potentially accessible branching ratios,
finding that
the leading αβ−candidates cluster near the doubly
magic nuclei 132Sn and 208Pb, while the leading αβ+
candidates lie near the doubly magic nucleus
100Sn.
Remarkably, six αβ+ candidates are known β-delayed-α
precursors.
Their observed Gaussian-like α spectra
are naturally reproduced by our simultaneous-emission
framework without free parameters.
This indicates
that phenomena historically interpreted as sequential
β-delayed-α
decay
may
be
dominated
by,
or
at
least include substantial contributions from,
direct,
simultaneous αβ+ emission. Future high-precision decay
spectroscopy and microscopic many-body calculations
will be essential to verify this decay mode and further
probe the limits of nuclear stability.
