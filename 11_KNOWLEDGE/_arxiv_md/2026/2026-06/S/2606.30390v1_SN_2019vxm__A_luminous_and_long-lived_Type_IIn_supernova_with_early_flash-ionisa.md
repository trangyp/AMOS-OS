SN 2019vxm: A luminous and long-lived Type IIn supernova with
early flash-ionisation features
Y.-Z. Cai, A. Pastorello, T. J. Moriya, X.-F. Wang, A. Reguitti, A. V. Filippenko, L. Tomasella, K. ˇCotar, et al.
(Full author list and affiliations details can be found after the references)
Received 2026; accepted 2026
ABSTRACT
We present the photometric and spectroscopic analysis of the luminous and long-lasting Type IIn supernova (SN) 2019vxm. The SN reaches a peak
V-band absolute magnitude of MV = −20.01 ± 0.13 mag in 35.0 days, and displays slow evolution in both the light curves and spectra, resembling
that of long-lived SNe IIn. A mid-infrared (MIR) excess is detected starting from seven months after maximum brightness, suggesting a few 10−3
M⊙of dust are newly formed at ≳210 days (and up to 0.01 M⊙at +4.5 yr). The spectra are dominated by a blue continuum at early stages, with
narrow, symmetric Balmer lines and flash-ionisation emission lines of C iii, N iii, and He ii. Comparing our flash-ionised spectrum with early
interacting SN spectral models, we estimate a lower limit for the mass-loss rate of the progenitor of ˙M ≳10−2 M⊙yr−1. A weak P Cygni absorption
feature is detected in the Hβ profile of the high-resolution Echelle spectrum at +19.7 d, suggesting the presence of slow-moving (60 ± 10 km s−1),
unshocked circumstellar material (CSM) arising from the pre-SN wind of the progenitor. The Hα and Hβ profiles gradually evolve and become
broader and asymmetric, showing a progressively increasing blueshift, with a clear flux deficit in the red wings of the broad velocity component
after +102 days. Our observed bolometric light curve before ∼100 days can be well fitted by a power-law function (L(t) = 2 × 1044(t/day)−0.49
erg s−1), which is very similar to SN 2010jl. The bolometric luminosity evolution indicates that the progenitor mass-loss rate was ∼0.05 M⊙yr−1
if we assume ρCSM ∝r−2. These findings are consistent with an asymmetric, dense circumstellar environment, indicative of a massive, compact
progenitor such as a luminous blue variable (LBV) embedded in a clumpy, asymmetric medium. However, we cannot entirely rule out an extreme
red supergiant (RSG) with superwinds or a transitional post-RSG/LBV star as the potential progenitor.
Key words. circumstellar matter – supernovae: general – supernovae: individual: SN 2019vxm
1. Introduction
Type IIn supernovae (SNe IIn) were first introduced by Schlegel
(1990) (but see also Filippenko 1989, for earlier examples) to
describe a subclass of hydrogen-rich SNe characterised by rel-
atively narrow hydrogen emission lines, ranging from ∼100 to
∼1000 km s−1, superposed on a blue continuum in their early-
time spectra (see Filippenko 1997, for a review). These emission
lines indicate the presence of a slowly expanding circumstellar
medium (CSM). The interaction between the rapidly expanding
SN ejecta and the CSM generates a forward shock that prop-
agates into the CSM and a reverse shock that moves into the
ejecta (see, e.g., Chevalier & Fransson 1994; Chugai 1997). The
presence of ejecta-CSM interaction is revealed by typical ob-
servables such as strong X-ray and radio emission, a blue spec-
tral continuum, and multicomponent line profiles, sometimes
with boxy components (see, e.g., Fraser 2020; Dessart & Hillier
2022; Dessart 2024; Gangopadhyay 2024).
Type IIn SNe are relatively rare, representing around 10%
of all core-collapse (CC) SNe in the local Universe (Smartt
2009; Smith et al. 2011a; Li et al. 2011; Cappellaro et al. 2015;
Perley et al. 2020; Ma et al. 2025a,b), while Eldridge et al.
(2013) even estimated a much lower rate of 2.4%. SNe IIn
display tremendous heterogeneity in their light curves and
spectra (see, e.g., Kiewe et al. 2012; Pastorello et al. 2019;
Nyholm et al. 2020; Reguitti et al. 2024; Salmaso et al. 2025),
with absolute magnitudes at peak ranging from Mr ≈−16 mag
for the faintest events to Mr ≈−22 mag for very luminous ob-
jects (e.g., Nyholm et al. 2020; Hiramatsu et al. 2024). The light-
curve shapes are divided into three main subgroups on the ba-
sis of their morphology (Taddia et al. 2013, 2015): the plateau-
like SNe IIn-P (e.g., Kankare et al. 2012; Mauerhan et al. 2013;
Elias-Rosa et al. 2024), the fast and linearly (in magnitudes per
day) declining SNe IIn-L (e.g., Fassia et al. 2000; Di Carlo et al.
2002; Pastorello et al. 2002), and the long-lasting SNe IIn (e.g.,
Turatto et al. 1993; Zhang et al. 2012; Stritzinger et al. 2012;
Tartaglia et al. 2020; Moran et al. 2023). Members of this last
category are generally more luminous than SNe IIn-P, although
without reaching the brightness of superluminous (SL) SNe.
In addition, several studies suggest that SNe IIn may be
more prone to containing warm dust compared to other types
of CC SNe, as they typically exhibit late-time (≳100 days) in-
frared (IR) emission associated with warm dust (e.g., Fox et al.
2009, 2010, 2011; Miller et al. 2010a,b; Maeda et al. 2013;
Gall et al. 2014; Bevan et al. 2019, 2020; Dwek et al. 2021;
Baer-Way et al. 2025; Shahbandeh et al. 2025). This photomet-
ric diversity, along with spectral information such as CSM and
ejecta velocities, reflects a wide range of CSM geometric config-
urations, masses, and density profiles — hence, in general, dif-
ferent properties of the progenitors and their local environments.
The variety of SN IIn observables suggests the exis-
tence of multiple progenitor channels (see, e.g., Dwarkadas
2011; Moriya et al. 2013b; Leloudas et al. 2015; Smith 2017;
Ransome & Villar 2025). In particular, luminous blue vari-
ables (LBVs) have been proposed as plausible progeni-
tors by Kotak & Vink (2006), Gal-Yam et al. (2007), and
Gal-Yam & Leonard (2009), as they expel significant amounts
of mass during their eruptive phases at velocities compara-
ble to those observed in the spectra of SNe IIn (Kiewe et al.
2012; Taddia et al. 2013). Specifically, this scenario is strongly

ant branch (AGB) stars (e.g., Moriya et al. 2014) or blue super-
giants (BSGs; Moriya & Menon 2024) as progenitors — remain
plausible.
On the other hand, the most extreme exemplars, such as over-
luminous and long-lasting events, significantly broaden our un-
derstanding of SNe IIn. For instance, superluminous SNe IIn
require both a high kinetic energy in the inner shell and a
large mass reservoir in the outer shell (see, e.g., Moriya et al.
2013a; Dessart et al. 2015). Some good examples of such SNe
IIn are SN 2008am (Chatzopoulos et al. 2011), SN 2010jl (e.g.,
Andrews et al. 2011; Stoll et al. 2011; Smith et al. 2011b, 2012;
Zhang et al. 2012; Maeda et al. 2013; Ofek et al. 2014, 2019;
Fransson et al. 2014; Jencson et al. 2016; Chugai 2018), SN
2015da (Tartaglia et al. 2020; Smith et al. 2024), and more re-
cently SN 2017hcc (e.g., Prieto et al. 2017; Smith & Andrews
2020; Moran et al. 2023; Sethulakshmi et al. 2026). In this study,
we present observations of the luminous, slowly evolving Type
IIn SN 2019vxm, monitored for more than two years. Relevant
datasets for this SN have been published by Vallely et al. (2021),
Tsvetkov et al. (2023, 2024), Lane et al. (2026), and Lelkes et al.
(2026), and will be discussed later.
This paper is structured as follows. Sect. 2 describes the basic
information on SN 2019vxm, including discussions of distance
and reddening. The photometric and spectroscopic analysis are
presented in Sects. 3 and 4, respectively. Sect. 5 discusses our
results. We report all the photometric and spectroscopic observa-
tions, as well as the data-reduction techniques, in Appendix A.
2. Basic information for SN 2019vxm
SN 2019vxm (also known as ASASSN-19acc, ATLAS19bchy,
Gaia19fje, and PS20nyb) was first announced by the All-Sky
Automated Survey for Supernovae (ASAS-SN; Shappee et al.
2014; Kochanek et al. 2017) on 2019 December 1 UTC (JD
= 2,458,818.54; UTC dates are used throughout the paper), at
g ≈15.4 mag (AB mag; Oke & Gunn 1983) (Stanek 2019).
The source was later classified by Leadbeater (2019) as a young
Type IIn SN based on the similarity of its spectrum to that of
SN 1998S about 20 days before maximum light.
SN 2019vxm (RA = 19hr58m28s.54, Dec. = +62◦08′15′′.83;
J2000) has a projected offset of 3.19 kpc from the core of its
possible host galaxy SDSS J195828.83+620824.3, and is 8′′.53
south and 2′′.07 west of the galaxy centre (see Fig. 1). Given the
absence of a published redshift for the putative host galaxy, we
adopt z = 0.019±0.001 based on the fit to the early Hα emission
line of SN 2019vxm, consistent with the value adopted by others
(Leadbeater 2019; Tsvetkov et al. 2024; Lane et al. 2026). This
redshift corresponds to a luminosity distance of dL = 79.2 ±
4.2 Mpc and a distance modulus of µL = 34.49 ± 0.12 mag.

3. Photometry
We monitored the photometric evolution of SN 2019vxm for
∼700 days from discovery in the optical band, and for ∼4 yr in
the mid-infrared (MIR) domain. Swift-UVOT observations were
obtained in the ultraviolet (UV) and the blue optical filters, span-
ning a period from ∼16 to 50 days after maximum light. Ba-
sic information on the photometric observations of SN 2019vxm
and the data-reduction techniques are provided in Sect. A.1,
while the multiband light curves are shown in Fig. 3.
3.1. Apparent magnitude light curves
We adopt the explosion time of MJD = 58804.03±0.30 precisely
constrained by Lane et al. (2026). From the Swift V-band rise
time of 35.0+1.8
−1.7 d estimated by Lane et al. (2026), the maximum-
light time is MJD 58839.0. For each UV band, the epoch of the
first Swift-UVOT data point was adopted to estimate the decline
slopes. Notably, the R- and I-band photometry of SN 2019vxm
is concentrated around the peak luminosity and at phases later
than 600 d (see Fig. 3); consequently, the decline slopes were
computed only for these two bands at phases beyond 600 d.
The decline rates for different bands, summarised in Ap-
pendix Table D.2, exhibit a somewhat unexpected heterogeneity
across the filters. After maximum light, SN 2019vxm displays
a slow brightness decline with several changes in the decline
rate. The UV light curves exhibit an approximately linear (in
mag/day) decline, with rates significantly steeper than those in
the optical bands (e.g., γ0−40(UVW2) ≈4.96 mag/100 d). Over
the period 0–100 d after peak light, the blue optical filters fade
more rapidly (e.g., γ0−100(B) ≈1.32 mag/100 d), whereas the
redder ones evolve more slowly (e.g., γ0−100(r) ≈0.61 mag/100
d). Between 100 and 400 d, the decline rates in the optical bands
decrease more slowly (e.g., γ100−400(r) ≈0.25 mag/100 d), indi-
cating a plateau-like evolution. After 400 d, the decline rates in-
crease again, approaching the 56Co–56Fe radioactive decay rate
of 0.98 mag/100 d (e.g., γ400−700(r) ≈0.83 mag/100 d). As noted
by Lelkes et al. (2026), this apparent alignment of the decline
rate with the 56Co–56Fe slope is likely coincidental.
In the MIR domain, the light curves of SN 2019vxm exhibit
a different evolutionary behaviour compared to those at shorter
wavelengths (Fig. 3). The first detection is dated MJD 58810.94
(15.97±0.40 mag) in the W1 band, while the source was not
detected at that epoch in the W2 band. However, the timing
of the MIR peak is poorly constrained with the available MIR
data. From the epoch of maximum in the V band, the MIR light
curves may exhibit a peak within the first 0–200 d; however,
the sparse sampling prevents a robust determination. Between
200 and 300 d, the luminosity remains approximately constant,
and is followed by a second phase of brightening extending to
∼500 d. Meanwhile, the optical light curves show a pronounced
decline, which may indicate the formation of dust (see Sect. 3.5
for a detailed discussion). Given the limited number of data
points and the nonmonotonic evolution, we refrain from report-
ing quantitative rise or decline rates. Between 500 and 750 d,
both the W1 and W2 bands show a declining trend, with decline
rates of γ500−750(W1) ≈0.21 mag/100 d and γ500−750(W2) ≈
0.20 mag/100 d. At later epochs, between 1000 and 1700 d, the
MIR luminosity continues to decline. The decline rate in the
W1 band increases slightly to γ1000−1700(W1) ≈0.24 mag/100 d,
whereas that in the W2 band decreases to γ1000−1700(W2) ≈
0.12 mag/100 d. Owing to the sparse data coverage at these late
epochs, these estimates are subject to large uncertainties.
3.2. Absolute magnitude light curves
In this study, we compile a comparison sample of Type IIn SNe
to serve as a reference for the photometric and spectroscopic
analysis of SN 2019vxm. The comparison objects either have
luminosities similar to the “optical” luminosity of SN 2019vxm,
or with a long evolution time. All comparison SNe, listed in Ap-
pendix Table D.1, are shown with their absolute V-band light
curves in Fig. 4.
Using the distance and extinction values from Sect. 2,
a second-order polynomial fit to the V-band light curve of
SN 2019vxm yields a peak absolute magnitude of MV
=
−20.01 ± 0.13 mag at MJD = 58839.0 ± 1.7. While this lu-
minosity is high in an absolute sense, it places SN 2019vxm at
the fainter end of the comparison sample when SLSNe-IIn are
included. Owing to their diverse progenitor channels and com-
plex CSM interactions, Type IIn SNe exhibit a wide range of
absolute magnitudes, spanning more than 6 mag (Smith 2017).
SN 2019vxm reaches a higher peak luminosity, approaching that
of SNe 2010jl (∼−19.43 mag) and 2015da (∼−20.2 mag). Al-
though it is not as luminous as SLSNe-IIn, SN 2019vxm exhibits
a long-duration evolution similar to that of some extremely lumi-
nous SNe IIn (e.g., SNe 2003ma and 2017hcc). This is also con-
sistent with the findings of Nyholm et al. (2020) that the most
luminous SNe IIn are generally found to be longer lasting.
3.3. Colour evolution
The intrinsic colour evolution of SN 2019vxm, compared with
those of the SN IIn comparison sample, is shown in Fig. 5. From
∼20 d before maximum light to ∼200 d after, the B −V colour
increases from ∼0.04 mag to a peak value of ∼0.87 mag, in-
dicating an overall trend for the object to become redder with
time. Such colour evolution alone does not uniquely imply a de-
crease in temperature, as dust extinction can preferentially atten-
uate blue photons and thus produce a redder observed colour.
Moreover, line blanketing, which becomes more effective as
the temperature decreases and the ionisation state of the ejecta
evolves, can further enhance the reddening. At later phases, the
B −V colour remains relatively stable (∼0.67–0.80 mag). Over-
all, the colour evolution of SN 2019vxm closely resembles that
of SN 2017hcc. By contrast, the B −V colour of SNe 2005ip
and 2010jl remains relatively stable (∼0.30–0.50 mag) through-
out the observed phases. The B −V colour of SNe 2006tf and
2015da exhibits an increasing trend at early times; however, the
B−V colour of SN 2015da decreases after the peak before finally
reaching a stable value.
In terms of the r −i colour evolution, SN 2019vxm shows
minor differences with respect to the other objects in the sample,
particularly at phases later than 300 d (see the bottom panel in
Fig. 5). From ∼20 d before maximum light to about 30 d after
peak, the r −i colour of SN 2019vxm increases from −0.18 mag
to a peak value of ∼0.17 mag. Subsequently, the r −i colour
exhibits an apparent blueward trend, declining to ∼−1.53 mag
by +700 d. This behaviour is likely driven by the increasing
contribution of the Hα emission to the r band relative to the
i band, rather than reflecting an intrinsic continuum evolution.

reddening trend over the same phases. The early evolution of

3.4. Pseudobolometric light curves
To enable a robust comparison between SN 2019vxm and other
SNe IIn, we constructed pseudobolometric light curves using the
available photometry in the bands from B to z, and derived the
evolution of the luminosity through black-body fits to the spec-
tral energy distribution (SED), as shown in Fig. 6. The properties
adopted for the comparison sample are summarised in Table D.1.
Within the selected sample, most SNe IIn reach peak luminosi-
ties above 1043 erg s−1. The peak luminosity of SN 2019vxm is
L ≈1.59 × 1043 erg s−1, one order of magnitude higher than that
of SN 2005ip (L ≈1.66×1042 erg s−1) and marginally exceeding
that of SN 2010jl (L ≈1.39 × 1043 erg s−1). However, its lumi-
nosity remains systematically below those of the other SNe IIn
in the comparison sample. SN 2019vxm is more luminous than
only SN 2010jl within the comparison sample, while most ob-
jects exhibit higher peak luminosities. Nevertheless, compared
with the overall population of SNe IIn, it remains toward the lu-
minous end of the distribution (∼1042–1044 erg s−1; Kiewe et al.
2012; Taddia et al. 2013; Smith 2017).

4.1. Low-resolution spectral sequence
The time series of 38 optical spectra of SN 2019vxm is shown
in Fig. 9. In the following, we analyse in detail the spectral evo-
lution during the three temporal windows identified in Sect. 3.1
(see also Table D.2). In the same three time windows, we also
note major changes in the spectra of SN 2019vxm, especially in
the continuum temperature and the Hα profile.
Early phases: < +100 days: The early-time spectra of
SN 2019vxm are characterised by a strong blue continuum with
prominent, relatively narrow Balmer emission lines (from Hα to
Hδ ), which are typically seen in Type IIn SNe. The obvious
bump detected at ∼4600–4700 Å in the first two spectra (at
+14.73 and +17.68 days) is the classical flash-ionisation feature
visible in a number of CC SNe, due to a blend of emission lines
of C iii λ4648, N iii λ4640, and He ii λ4686 (see the top-left
panel of Fig. 14 in Sect. 4.4). In particular, these flash-ionisation
signatures are frequently observed in SNe II within the first
few days after the explosion (e.g., SNe 1998S, 2013fs, 2013cu,
2024cld, and PTF 11iqb; Leonard et al. 2000; Shivvers et al.
2015; Gal-Yam et al. 2014; Dessart et al. 2017; Killestein et al.
2026; Smith et al. 2015), indicating that enhanced mass loss just
before the explosion is common among Type II SN progeni-
tors (Bruch et al. 2023; Jacobson-Galán et al. 2024). However,
the flash-ionisation features remained detectable in SN 2019vxm
until ∼17 days after the explosion. This prolonged duration sug-
gests that the CSM environment of SN 2019vxm is highly dense,
massive, and/or extended, indicating stronger mass-loss activity
compared to typical SNe II. Further details are shown in the top-
left panel of Fig. 14 and discussed in Sect. 4.4. In addition, we
detect weak emission features of He i λ5876 and λ7065.

a black-body function, following the methodology described by
Cai et al. (2026). The best-fit results give a photospheric temper-
ature of TBB = 20, 100+1,450

which then falls rapidly to TBB = 14, 250+400

spectrum is presented in Sect. 4.2.
The other pre- and near-maximum spectra were mostly ob-
tained with the 2.16 m Xinglong telescope (XLT) (see Table
C.1 in Appendix A.2), and do not show significant evolution.
They are just marginally redder than earlier spectra, and flash-
ionisation features have completely disappeared. In all cases, the
low spectral resolution does not allow for a reliable measure-
ment of the full width at half-maximum intensity (FWHM) ve-
locity for the narrow component. The highest-quality spectrum,
in terms of both signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) and resolution, was
obtained with the 3 m Shane telescope at +49.1 days. The Hα
profile in this spectrum is well-fitted by a two-component Gaus-
sian function, comprising a relatively narrow emission com-
ponent and a broader one. These two components were cho-
sen purely based on their ability to accurately reproduce the
observed line profile. The narrower component is unresolved,
whereas the broad emission component exhibits a FWHM veloc-
ity of 2500 ± 50 km s−1 which is likely attributed to the electron-
scattering wings (Huang & Chevalier 2018). After an observa-
tional gap of about one month, a new Copernico/AFOSC spec-
trum was obtained at +76.2 days. This spectrum is still quite
blue and dominated by the unresolved Balmer components.
However, a broader base in the Hα profile with a vFWHM =
9500 ± 180 km s−1 is now more evident, and broad undulations
are marginally visible in the blue region. The broad component
likely originates from the high-velocity component of the ejecta,
as the outer part of the CSM gradually becomes optically thin.
Furthermore, the emergence of metal lines in the blue region of
this spectrum suggests that some of the inner parts of the envi-
ronment are gradually becoming visible. These features indicate
this spectrum is transitioning toward the intermediate phases.
Intermediate phases, +100 to +400 days: The spectral
monitoring of this phase starts after an observational gap of ∼2
months. In this period, we note a dramatic change in the Hα
and Hβ profiles: these are no longer dominated by the narrow
component, as the broader component becomes progressively
more prominent and exhibits an increasingly asymmetric pro-
file. In particular, the full line profile of Hα is now reproduced
with two components. The broad component has a FWHM ve-
locity range of 6100–8400 km s−1 and its centre is blueshifted
by a maximum value of ∼1370 km s−1 with respect to the rest-
frame position. Furthermore, the narrower emission is now re-
solved in most spectra, with a FWHM velocity range of 1200–
1800 km s−1, suggesting the presence of an intermediate-width
component or a broadening of the previously unresolved nar-
row emission. The nature of this component can be clarified by
analysing a higher-resolution spectrum. Specifically, we use a
moderate-resolution GTC/R2500R spectrum at +166.2 d to pose
tighter constraints on the wind velocity during this intermedi-
ate phase. This moderate-resolution spectrum shows a compos-
ite Hα profile, suggesting three different kinematic components:
a very narrow component attributed to the unshocked CSM, an
intermediate-width component due to shocked material, and a
broad component produced by the SN ejecta (see Sect. 4.2).
The narrow and intermediate-width components cannot be dis-
tinguised in the low-resolution spectra. In this spectrum, a nar-
row P Cygni feature attributed to He i λ5876 is superposed on
the broad P Cygni profile of Na i D, while the He i λ7065 line is
observed on the red side of the spectrum (see further details in
Sect. 4.2).
Later spectra exhibit minor changes. Their spectral contin-
uum is relatively red, maintaining an almost constant tempera-
ture, fluctuating between TBB ≈5650 K and TBB ≈5150 K.
Such spectral evolution is consistent with the observed photo-
metric evolution characterised by a sort of plateau. This be-
haviour is largely driven by hydrogen recombination occurring
within the expanding cold dense shell (CDS). As the opacity out-
side the recombination front becomes negligible (Arnett & Fu
1989), the recombination process dominates this zone and pro-
duces the broad, boxy emission lines observed. Finally, these
spectra confirm the presence of He i λ5876 and He i λ7065 lines;
in particular, He i λ7065 is clearly detected in the high-S/N spec-
tra and exhibits minor evolution in its profile width. Additionally,
the blend between He i λ5876 and Na i D becomes more promi-
nent with time. The blue region of the spectra exhibits a pseudo-
continuum. This feature is likely caused by the line-blanketing
effect within the expanding CDS, which primarily originates
from metal lines such as Fe ii. Finally, the systematic blueshift of
the Hα lines, the asymmetry in the hydrogen-line profiles, and
the IR excess observed in the light curves after +210 d, reveal
the signature of new dust formation.

4.3. Evolution of the H lines
Figure 12 presents the temporal evolution of the Hα and Hβ line
profiles in the spectra of SN 2019vxm obtained from +17.7 d to
+597.0 d relative to the explosion epoch. The line profiles are
displayed in velocity space after the normalisation to the contin-
uum and a vertical offset has been applied for clarity. The dashed
vertical line marks zero velocity, corresponding to the rest frame.
At early epochs (≲100 d), the symmetric Hα and Hβ pro-
files are well reproduced by a single Lorentzian function. The
Hα and Hβ profiles during this phase are symmetric, and no sig-
nificant systematic blueshift is observed. The narrow component
is resolved only in the Copernico/Echelle spectrum at +19.7 d
(see Sect. 4.2). We note that H emission lines undergoing elec-
tron scattering in a hot medium result in symmetric profiles, as
the velocity broadening is driven by the microscopic, thermal ve-
locities of the electrons (Münch 1948), as frequently observed in
luminous SNe IIn (e.g., Smith et al. 2012; Tartaglia et al. 2020;
Moran et al. 2023).
Between +100 d and +400 d, the Balmer lines undergo sub-
stantial morphological evolution. The intermediate-width com-
ponent of Hα becomes more prominent and broader, and the
line peak gradually shifts toward the blue. Simultaneously, the
red wing is progressively suppressed, producing an increasingly
asymmetric, blue-skewed profile. Hβ follows the same quali-
tative trend. The persistence of a strong intermediate compo-
nent over several hundred days indicates sustained and powerful
ejecta-CSM interaction and likely originates from the shocked
material, by analogy with other long-lived Type IIn events such
as SN 2010jl (Fransson et al. 2014). The systematic blueshift
and attenuation of the red wing are usually interpreted as effects
of the increasing optical depth due to newly formed dust within
the dense post-shock region, which preferentially obscures the
receding emission (Smith et al. 2008b), or intrinsic asymmetry
in the CSM distribution (Fransson et al. 2014; Andrews et al.
2016).
At epochs later than +400 d, both Hα and Hβ remain
clearly detectable, confirming the long-lived nature of the in-
teraction. Although the overall widths decrease slightly com-
pared to the peak interaction phase, the profiles retain their
asymmetric, blue-dominated morphology, with a continued sup-
pression of the red wing. Notably, dust is expected to form
within the inner, metal-rich ejecta in standard Type II SNe on
a one-year timescale. However, dust formation can be more effi-
cient and occur earlier in interacting SNe. In these cases (e.g.,
SN 2006jc, SN 2016iog; Mattila et al. 2008; Peng et al. 2026),
dust forms within the compressed CDS at the ejecta-CSM in-
teracting interface even at earlier phases, where the high den-
sity in the radiative post-shock layers facilitate the rapid post-
shock gas cooling (e.g., Smith et al. 2012; Sarangi & Slavin
2022; Dessart et al. 2025). The concurrently observed signatures
of systematic blueshift and wavelength-dependent asymmetries
of the Hα and Hβ emission lines (after ∼100 days), and IR
brightening accompanied by optical fading (after ∼400 days),
suggest new dust condensation in SN 2019vxm. Similar fea-
tures have also been observed in SN 2010jl, where they were
attributed to the formation of new dust (see, e.g., Smith et al.
2012; Gall et al. 2014). However, Fransson et al. (2014) quanti-
tatively interpret the blueshifted peak and symmetric Hα pro-
files in SN 2010jl as resulting from a scattering medium moving
coherently toward the observer. Following the methodology of
Fransson et al. (2014) (see also Tartaglia et al. 2020), we mir-
rored the red wings of the Hα profiles with respect to the com-
puted centroid at each epoch (Fig. 13). The symmetric line pro-
file with respect to the shifted centroid suggests that the observed
blueshift of the Hα in SN 2019vxm (see Fig. 12) is also a macro-
scopic velocity effect.

5. Discussion and conclusions
In this paper, we have presented the photometric and spectro-
scopic properties of SN 2019vxm, a luminous and long-lived
Type IIn SN exhibiting slow spectroscopic and photometric
evolution, together with an evident MIR excess at late times.
SN 2019vxm shares obvious similarity with other luminous
Type IIn SNe, such as SN 2010jl and SN 2017hcc, especially
in terms of its overall brightness and spectral evolution.
The light curve of SN 2019vxm exhibits a relatively slow rise
to the peak brightness (35.0 ± 1.8 days in the Swift V band),
which is within the slow-rise SN IIn population of Nyholm et al.
(2020). This behaviour is consistent with interaction with mas-
sive and dense CSM due to the long diffusion timescale for pho-
tons travelling through optically thick CSM. The SN is more lu-
minous (MV = −20.01 ± 0.13 mag) than most SNe IIn (e.g.,
the average peak brightness of Mpeak
r
≈−19.18 ± 1.32 mag in
the sample of Nyholm et al. 2020). The estimated “optical” peak
luminosity of SN 2019vxm is L ≈1.59 × 1043 erg s−1. The rise
is followed by a monotonic decline with slow evolution (e.g.,
γ(r) ≈0.55 mag/100 d) until the SN disappeared ∼660 days
after maximum light. The light-curve evolution of SN 2019vxm
is consistent with the findings of Nyholm et al. (2020): higher-
luminosity SNe IIn are generally found to be longer-lasting,
while slowly rising SNe IIn generally decline more slowly in
luminosity. In addition, we examined the B −V and r −i colour
evolution of SN 2019vxm in comparison with several SNe IIn.
These objects show significant diversity in their colour behaviour
over time. Such variation likely arises from differences in the
physical processes that influence the thermal and radiative prop-
erties of the emitting regions.
To model the light curve of SN 2019vxm, we found a

SN 2010jl (Moriya et al. 2013b). Additionally, hydrogen recom-
bination takes place during this phase as the temperature de-
creases to ∼6000 K, leading to a relatively flat evolution of the
light curve, as the CDS forms and the broad Hα emission line
emerges. After ∼300 days, the decline rate of the light curve be-
comes significantly steeper, caused by intense dust formation. A
prominent MIR excess is also observed at this phase. Grey-body
fitting to the SED in the MIR part suggests a lower mass limit of
∼10−4 M⊙for graphite dust or ∼10−3 M⊙for silicate dust, with
temperatures of ∼1000 K. Furthermore, because the CSM inter-
action weakens in the late phase as the expanding CDS becomes
optically thin, radioactive heating likely dominates the tail of the
light curve, resulting in a faster decline that tracks radioactive
decay. From the model fitting, the estimated mass-loss rates are
˙M ≈5 × 10−2 M⊙yr−1 and ˙M ≈1 × 10−1 M⊙yr−1 with dif-
ferent parameter assumptions, which are considered to be high
values, supporting that there was an intense mass-loss activity
decades before the explosion. However, we note that the lower
bound of mass-loss rate is approximately one order of magnitude
lower than the values inferred for SN 2010jl (∼0.1 M⊙yr−1) and
SN 2015da (∼0.6 M⊙yr−1), while the upper bound is roughly
consistent with these objects.
The early-time spectra of SN 2019vxm from +14.7 d to
+76.2 d show very blue continua (T ≈7400–20,100 K) and
prominent, narrow Balmer emission lines. The earliest two spec-
tra are characterised by the prominent flash-ionisation features of
C iii , N iii , and He ii , from which we estimate a lower limit for
the mass-loss rate of the progenitor of ˙M ≳10−2 M⊙yr−1. These
features were also observed in the early spectra of SN 1998S
(Fassia et al. 2000; Anupama et al. 2001). However, the emer-
gence of these features in SN 2019vxm occurs at a much later
epoch compared to SN 1998S. Given that the diffusion timescale
in the CSM interaction is expressed as tCSM ∝κMCSM/cRCSM,
this long-lasting flash ionisation can be explained by massive
or dense CSM. Additionally, the presence of a highly extended
CSM environment cannot be ruled out. This estimate is consis-
tent with the value inferred from the light-curve modelling and
is also similar to that of SN 2010jl (see details in Sect. 3.6). The
short duration (≲5 days) of these flash-ionisation features in-
dicates that the CSM is spatially confined and originates from
enhanced mass loss occurring shortly (months to a few years)
before the explosion.
The Echelle spectrum may show a narrow P Cygni absorp-
tion imposed on Hβ (clearly detected in He i λ5876 line), in-
dicative of absorption by slowly moving optically thick CSM
along the line of sight with a velocity of 60 ± 10 km s−1. This
velocity falls marginally below the lower end of values typically
inferred for LBVs (102 −103 km s−1; Smith et al. 2011c; Smith
2017), while it is slightly higher than velocities observed for
red supergiants (RSG, 10–50 km s−1; Jura & Kleinmann 1990;
Smith 2017). However, the mass-loss rates inferred from both
the light curve and early spectral modelling are approximately
three orders of magnitude higher than typical values of RSG
winds. This makes an RSG progenitor unlikely, but the rates
are consistent with an LBV or binary RLOF scenario. Never-
theless, pre-SN superwinds might be occurring in RSGs (e.g.,
see the case of the Galactic RSG VY Canis Majoris; Smith et al.
2009b). Taken together, these lines of evidence (similar to that
of SNe 2010jl and 2015da) favour a very massive star as a pro-
genitor for SN 2019vxm, most likely an LBV. However, the pos-
sibility that the progenitor was an extreme RSG star with a su-
perwind, or a post-RSG/LBV transitional star, cannot be entirely
ruled out.
After +41.2 days, a broad emission component emerges in
the Balmer lines, marking the formation of the CDS. At epochs
later than +200 days post-explosion, the broad component nar-
rows, with the FWHM velocity exhibiting a linear decline. This
gradual narrowing of the profile suggests that the outer inter-
action region becomes increasingly optically thin, accompanied
by the recession of the photosphere. Moreover, the peak of the
broad component consistently exhibits a blueshift throughout the
evolution. In the early phase, similar to other Type II SNe, this
blueshift can be explained by line formation occurring within
the photosphere radius by fitting the continuum; the probability
of photon destruction is reduced in a medium where the opacity
is dominated by electron scattering (Anderson et al. 2014). In
addition, increasing optical depth in the receding direction will
also produce an asymmetric profile, and suppress the red-wing
emission. At late phases, this asymmetric profile is likely driven
by dust formation, which attenuates the red wing of the emis-

Our MIR photometry of SN 2019vxm spans 7–1690 days
from the explosion, and shows an IR excess starting from 210
days. At the first epoch, at a phase of ∼7 d, a single black body
adequately fits the SED, but a second, cooler black body is re-
quired to reproduce the SEDs after ∼210 days (see Fig. 7 in
Sect. 3.5). This reveals that there is a general trend of increas-
ing dust mass and decreasing dust temperature with the phase. It
thereby seems likely that the IR emission in SN 2019vxm orig-
inated from the newly formed dust similar to a number of other
overluminous Type IIn SNe, such as SN 2010jl and SN 2017hcc.
In summary, we propose that SN 2019vxm originated from
the explosion of an LBV embedded in structured, asymmet-
ric CSM. Nevertheless, the possibility of an extreme RSG
progenitor with a superwind, or a post-RSG/LBV transitional
star, cannot be entirely excluded. Although SN 2019vxm was
studied through multiwavelength observations, we expect fur-
ther observations (particularly in the IR domain) can improve
our understanding of its properties. For instance, multiband IR
photometry and spectra covering multiple epochs at very late
phases, combined with detailed modelling of spectral line pro-
files, would refine estimates of the dust properties. New spec-
troscopic instrumentation such as SOXS (Son Of X-Shooter;
Radhakrishnan Santhakumari et al. 2024), installed on the NTT
at the La Silla Observatory in Chile, will be crucial for expand-
ing the spectral monitoring of SN 2019vxm-like events toward
the NIR region. Even more, existing IR space facilities such as
the James Webb Space Telescope can further extend their mon-
itoring to longer wavelengths and to very late phases of their
evolution.

Appendix A: Observations and data reduction
A.1. Photometric data
Soon after the discovery announcement of SN 2019vxm, we con-
ducted a comprehensive multiband follow-up campaign in the
Johnson-Cousins UBVRI and Sloan ugriz filters. The informa-
tion on the instruments used are reported in Table B.1 (Ap-
pendix B).
Swift/UVOT UV and optical data were retrieved from
the NASA Swift Data Archive3 and measured with the stan-
dard UVOT data-reduction pipeline HEASoft4 (version 6.19,
Nasa Heasarc 2014). The optical photometric data obtained with
ground-based telescopes were reduced with the dedicated ec-
snoopy5 pipeline, following standard procedures as described
by Cai et al. (2018). We also collected archival data from pub-
lic surveys, such as ASAS-SN, Asteroid Terrestrial-impact Last
Alert System (ATLAS; Tonry et al. 2018; Smith et al. 2020;
Shingles et al. 2021), and Pan-STARRS (e.g., Chambers et al.
2016; Flewelling et al. 2020; Magnier et al. 2020). ATLAS or-
ange (o) and cyan (c) magnitudes were processed through
the ATLAS Forced Photometry service6 (Shingles et al. 2021),
while Pan-STARRS1 (PS1) magnitudes were directly adopted
from Lane et al. (2026). We retrieved single-exposure frames in
the W1 (3.4 µm) and W2 (4.6 µm) filters from the NASA/IPAC
Infrared Science Archive (IRSA), which were observed by the
Near-Earth Object WISE (NEOWISE) Reactivation mission7
(Mainzer et al. 2014). During each passage, fields often receive
multiple observations over a few days, which we coadded into
a single stacked image. Finally, we performed point-spread-
function (PSF) fitting photometry on template-subtracted im-
ages using pre-explosion archival images obtained in May 2019.
The WISE magnitudes were calibrated against the WISE All-Sky
Data Release catalogue (Cutri & et al. 2012). The final UV, op-
tical, and MIR magnitudes of SN 2019vxm are published at the
Strasbourg astronomical Data Centre (CDS).

the 3 m Shane telescope at Lick Observatory, USA; the Astro-

Dual Imaging Spectrograph (DIS) at the Apache Point Obser-
