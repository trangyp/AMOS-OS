Probing jet evolution with charged energy correlators in small systems
ALICE Collaboration*
Abstract
The ALICE Collaboration presents measurements of charged energy–energy correlators (charged
EECs) within charged-particle jets at √sNN = 5.02 TeV in proton–proton and proton–lead collisions
at the LHC. Charged EECs are a class of jet substructure observables that trace the flow of energy
and electric charge within a jet, and provide a tool for disentangling the energy scales involved in the
jet fragmentation process through the angular separation and charges of particle pairs. The interplay
between energy distribution and charge conservation enables charged EECs to provide novel con-
straints on hadronization mechanisms. Measurements of charged EECs in proton–proton collisions
in charged-particle jets with 20 < pT,chjet < 80 GeV/c are compared to event generators to investigate
different hadronization mechanisms and parton shower models. These model comparisons show that
the like-sign EECs are sensitive to changes in parton shower modeling, while unlike-sign EECs are
sensitive to different hadronization schemes. Measurements in proton–lead collisions indicate that
cold nuclear matter effects on charged EECs are consistent with charge-independent behavior.

Introduction
Jets of hadrons are produced in particle collisions at high-energy colliders, and their evolution spans a
wide range of energy scales within quantum chromodynamics (QCD). They arise from highly energetic
quarks and gluons (collectively termed partons), produced in hard-scattering processes between the con-
stituents of the colliding particles. These partons undergo successive QCD splittings, developing into a
parton shower through gluon radiation and gluon splitting into quark–antiquark pairs. When the result-
ing partons reach a characteristic energy scale ΛQCD [1], they combine into color-neutral hadrons in a
process known as hadronization. The resulting final-state hadrons can then be clustered into jets with
jet clustering algorithms [2]. The evolution of a jet thus involves multiple QCD regimes through various
energy scales, from the large momentum transfer of the initial scattering (at up to hundreds of GeV) to
hadronization (at around ΛQCD). Therefore, an accurate description of the internal structure of QCD jets
demands a full understanding of QCD itself at all scales. Disentangling the contributions from dynamics
at different scales requires observables that are sensitive to different stages of the jet evolution across this
hierarchy of scales.
The two-point energy–energy correlator (EEC) is an observable that tracks the energy flow in a jet.
Originally proposed as a basis for precision studies of QCD [3], EECs have recently received renewed
theoretical and experimental interest, thanks to the large jet production cross sections at hadron colliders
combined with high-luminosity datasets, enabling precise measurements of EECs in the collinear limit
(i.e. at small angles). EECs have been measured in a variety of contexts: at the Relativistic Heavy
Ion Collider (RHIC) [4] and the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), from small [4–7] to large collision sys-
tems [8], and in inclusive and D0 meson-tagged jets [9]. Applications of EECs at hadron colliders include
precision extractions of the strong coupling constant [6, 10], gluon polarimetry [11], and modifications
of jet substructure in quark–gluon plasma, at both early [12] and late times [13] in the fireball evolution.
For a recent comprehensive review, see [14].

The hard scattering cross section and the first few emissions in the parton shower are calculable with
pQCD techniques. However, an exact description of low-energy phenomena, in particular hadronization,
from first-principles QCD remains elusive. Monte Carlo (MC) event generators that simulate parti-
cle collisions thus often rely on QCD-inspired phenomenological models to produce a set of colorless
hadrons from the color-charged partons that arise from a parton shower. There are two general classes
of hadronization models: those based on the idea of breaking one-dimensional QCD flux tubes, termed
Lund string-breaking models [17, 18]; and those based on treating partons close in phase space as clus-
ters that fragment into hadrons, termed cluster models. The development of Lund string models has
progressed primarily in the context of the PYTHIA event generator [19], while two different implementa-
tions of cluster hadronization have evolved within Herwig [20] and SHERPA [21]. Comparisons against
a variety of measurements therefore serve several purposes. First, they can corroborate and constrain
new methods and techniques in shower modeling. Second, they can discriminate among hadronization
models. Third, they can establish how sensitive an observable is to different ways that the evolution of a
jet can be modeled. The comparison of charged EEC measurements with different event generators gives
new insights into non-perturbative QCD phenomena.
In addition, the differential nature of the charged EECs can help investigate modifications in different
collision systems. Previous ALICE measurements of the inclusive EEC [7] show that the EEC is modified
in p–Pb collisions relative to pp collisions, with a suppression of the EEC distribution at small angles and
an enhancement at large angle. The charge dependence of this modification can be tested with charged
EECs.

charged-particle jets, using their opening angle and their charges to separate the energy scales of the
jet evolution. This analysis utilizes the charged-particle tracking systems of the ALICE experiment to

addresses the associated systematic uncertainties. Section 5 compares the measurements to predictions
from Monte Carlo event generators, as well as state-of-the-art pQCD calculations. Detailed studies of
the transition region are also presented.

The ALICE detector and data
This analysis makes use of data collected in pp and p–Pb collisions during LHC Run 2 at √sNN = 5.02 TeV
in 2017 and 2016, respectively, the same data as those in previous ALICE EEC measurements in pp [5]
and p–Pb collisions [7]. Minimum-bias events are identified by coincident signals in the V0A and V0C
scintillator hodoscopes [22], located at far forward (2.8 < η < 5.1) and far backward (−3.7 < η < −1.7)
pseudorapidity, respectively. To ensure optimal and uniform detector performance, reconstructed primary
vertices are required to be within 10 cm of the nominal interaction point along the beam direction. Fur-
ther selections are performed on event properties to minimize pileup [5]. Charged-particle tracks with
0.15 < pT < 100 GeV/c and |η| < 0.9 are reconstructed with the central barrel tracking systems: the In-

Experimental method
Reconstructed charged-particle tracks are clustered into anti-kT jets [2] with jet radius parameter R = 0.4
and E-scheme recombination [26] with the FastJet package (version 3.3.4) [27]. For the purposes of
jet finding, all particles are assumed to have the charged-pion mass. Only jets fully within the TPC
acceptance of |η| < 0.9 are considered in this analysis, i.e. |ηjet| < 0.9 −R = 0.5. For the purposes
of pairing particles for the EEC, only jet constituents with pT > 1 GeV/c are considered, to efficiently
eliminate contributions from pairs in which one or both particles are from the underlying event (UE).
This value of the cutoff is commonly used in EEC measurements [5–9]. In pp collisions, no other steps
are taken to account for the UE. Results from p–Pb data, as well as the pp baseline measurement used to
compare to the p–Pb data, incorporate two additional background subtraction methods applied to each
of the charged and inclusive EECs: an area-based pT,chjet correction described in [28, 29], as well as
a perpendicular cone subtraction that removes contributions from pairs that include a particle from the
UE. This perpendicular cone subtraction method is applied as introduced in [7]: two cones with radius
of R = 0.4 that are perpendicular in azimuth to the jet axis are constructed, and all particles within the
cones are assumed to originate from the UE. The contributions in the jet arising from pairs with one hard-
scattering track and one UE track (signal–background) and from pairs with two UE tracks (background–
background) can then be estimated by creating pairs between jet particles and cone particles, particles
within the same cone, and between particles in different cones. This is done by azimuthally rotating each
perpendicular cone’s axis such that it coincides with the jet axis. Three sets of EECs are then calculated
from particular kinds of pairs, using the area-subtracted pT,chjet of the original jet in the denominator of
the pair’s energy weight. The jet-cone EEC is calculated only from pairs where one particle is from a
cone and the other from the jet, averaged over the two cones,

Detector effects are estimated with simulated collision events that are propagated through a GEANT3 [30]
representation of the ALICE detector reproducing its response. Hard scattering events with PYTHIA
8.243 [31] (Monash 2013 tune [32]) and ˆpT (the transverse momentum of the hard scattering) greater than
5 GeV/c were used for pp collisions, and minimum-bias collisions with DPMJET (version 3.0-5) [33–35]
were used for p–Pb collisions. The reconstruction of the simulated collision events is anchored to the
detector conditions during the relevant data-taking periods.
Because the ALICE track-pair angular resolution is much smaller than the width of the RL intervals, no
angular bin migrations are considered in the corrections [5]. A two-dimensional bin-by-bin correction
is employed to account for migrations in pT,chjet, the pair energy weights, and detector efficiency. This
is the same method used in previous ALICE EEC measurements [5, 7, 9], and optimizes the statistical
precision of the corrections [5].
The detector effects that affect charged EECs are the reconstructed track pT resolution and the recon-
struction efficiencies for single tracks and track pairs. The track pT resolution alters the energy weight for
reconstructed pairs. The single-track reconstruction efficiency skews the pT,chjet of a reconstructed jet to
lower values on average relative to the true pT,chjet, which also alters the energy weight, and reduces the
number of pairs in the EECs when one or both tracks in the pair are not reconstructed. The efficiency of
track-pair reconstruction can also decrease when the two particles have the same charge sign and similar
pT, pseudorapidity, and azimuthal angle. Since they trace similar trajectories within the TPC volume, the
partially- or fully-overlapping clusters in the TPC can cause the two trajectories to be reconstructed as a
single track, or as no track at all. Previous ALICE analyses in EECs [5] and correlation femtoscopy [36]
have shown that the pair efficiency is largely driven by the single-track reconstruction efficiency at large
angular separation, but drops dramatically for pairs with RL < 0.01 (with decreases of up to 30% for pairs
at higher pT). Consequently, the charged EECs are measured as a function of the pair opening angle RL
from 0.01 to 0.4 and are reported in three intervals of pT,chjet: 20–40 GeV/c, 40–60 GeV/c, and 60–80
GeV/c.
Another possible detector effect is the charge-reconstruction inefficiency (the likelihood for a track to be
reconstructed with the wrong charge sign), which can shift track pairs from one charge-selected EEC to
another. The inefficiency was estimated in Monte Carlo simulation by measuring the frequency of charge
misreconstruction for reconstructed particles, i.e. the particle was reconstructed but with the opposite
charge sign. The charge-reconstruction inefficiency for single tracks was found to be fully consistent
with zero within uncertainties.

Systematic uncertainties
The main sources of systematic uncertainty arising from detector effects are the determination of the
reconstruction efficiency of single tracks and of track pairs.
Two sources of systematic uncertainty related to the bin-by-bin correction were evaluated: the depen-
dence on the choice of Monte Carlo generator used to derive the bin-by-bin correction, and the use of a
Bayesian unfolding technique instead of a bin-by-bin correction. The sources of systematic uncertainties,
and the methods used to estimate them, are identical to those in previous ALICE EEC measurements in
pp [5] and p–Pb collisions [7].
By varying track-selection criteria, previous ALICE studies have determined that the uncertainty in the
single-track reconstruction efficiency is approximately 3% [37]. Following the methodology established
in [5], 3% of reconstructed tracks are randomly removed and the bin-by-bin correction is recalculated.
The difference with respect to the nominal bin-by-bin correction is used as the systematic uncertainty.
The pair reconstruction efficiency systematic uncertainty was determined by imposing various pair-
selection parameters on the reconstructed track pairs and studying the resulting variation in the EECs.

Conclusions
Measurements of charged energy–energy correlators in pp and p–Pb collisions are reported in this paper.
The differential nature of the charged EECs provides insights into charge and energy flow dynamics that
are hidden in inclusive EECs. Detailed comparisons to Monte Carlo event generators reveal that changes
to jet evolution modeling may be isolated by charge-selected EECs. In particular, the like-sign EECs
show sensitivity to modifications in shower modeling, and unlike-sign EECs are sensitive to different
hadronization schemes. Models predict a hierarchy in the transition scale between different charge-
selected EECs, with unlike-sign EECs peaking at smaller angles than like-sign EECs. However, the
data are not yet precise enough to observe this behavior experimentally. The rebalancing of the charged
EEC asymmetry at angles below the transition scale for low pT,chjet jets is entirely missing from model
predictions, indicating incomplete dynamics in the models. In comparing charged EEC measurements
in p–Pb to pp collisions, no significant charge dependence is observed within current uncertainties, and
theoretical predictions are able to account for cold nuclear matter and charge in describing charged
EECs.
