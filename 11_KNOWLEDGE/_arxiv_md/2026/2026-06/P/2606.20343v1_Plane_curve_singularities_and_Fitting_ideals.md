PLANE CURVE SINGULARITIES AND FITTING IDEALS
ALEXANDRU DIMCA1 AND GABRIEL STICLARU
Abstract. In this note we investigate the Fitting ideals associated to the Tjurina
ideal of a non quasi-homogeneous plane curve singularity. Special properties occur
when the difference between Milnor number and Tjurina number is at most 2.
1. Introduction
Let R = C{x, y} be the convergent power series ring in 2 variables x and y with
complex coefficients, and let X : f = 0 be an isolated curve singularity at the origin
0 ∈C2, with f ∈R. We denote by Jf the Jacobian ideal of f, i.e. the ideal in R
spanned by the partial derivatives
fx = ∂xf and fy = ∂yf.
and by M(f) = R/Jf the corresponding quotient ring, called the Milnor algebra
of f.
We denote by If the Tjurina ideal of f, i.e.
the ideal in R spanned by
fx = ∂xf, fy = ∂yf and f, and by T(f) = R/If the corresponding quotient ring,
called the Tjurina algebra of f. Then both M(f) and T(f) are Artinian local C-
algebras, and
µ(f) = dimC M(f) and τ(f) = dimC T(f)
are the Milnor and resp. Tjurina number of f.
It is well known that X : f = 0 is a quasi-homogeneous singularity if and only if
If = Jf, or equivalently µ(f) = τ(f), see [6]. In this case the minimal resolution of
If, which is a complete intersection, has the following form

2. Proof of Proposition 1.1
The claims (1), (2) and (3) are clear from the discussion proceeding this result.
Using now Hilbert-Burch Theorem, see [5, Theorem 20.15], it follows that If =
aI2(U), where a ∈R. Since If has finite codimension in R, it follows that a has to
be a unit, and hence we get the claim (4).
To prove (5), let
(2.1)
ρj = (aj, bj, cj)
for j = 1, 2 be a basis of the free module Syz(f). Then g ∈Ann(f) if and only if
there exists a′, b′ ∈R such that
a′fx + b′fy + gf = 0.
This means that (a′, b′, g) ∈Syz(f), and hence this happens if and only if g ∈(c1, c2),
the ideal generated by c1 and c2 in R. Suppose that Ann(f) = (c1, c2) is a principal
ideal in R, say generated by c ∈R. Then c1 and c2 are divisible by c, and this shows
that a basis of the free module Syz can have the following form
ρ′
1 = (a, b, c) and ρ′
2 = (−fy, fx, 0).
Then the ideal Fitt1(If) = I2(U) is generated by cfx, cfy and afx + bfy = −cf. This
implies that If = I2(U) ⊂(c), which is a contradiction, since the principal ideal (c)
has infinite codimension (as a vector subspace) in R.

that
a1b2 −a2b1 = hf, a1c2 −a2c1 = −hfy and b1c2 −b2c1 = hfx.
