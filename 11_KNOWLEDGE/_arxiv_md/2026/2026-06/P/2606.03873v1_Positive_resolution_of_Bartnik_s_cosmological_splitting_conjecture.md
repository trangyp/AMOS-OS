Positive resolution of Bartnik’s cosmological splitting conjecture
Robert McCann∗
Argam Ohanyan†
June 3, 2026
Abstract
We give a proof of the cosmological splitting conjecture of Robert Bartnik from 1988,
which expresses the rigidity of the cosmological Hawking–Penrose singularity theorem.
It states that a timelike geodesically complete, globally hyperbolic spacetime which has
compact Cauchy surfaces and satisfies the strong energy condition must split isometri-
cally as a Lorentzian product. Our methods combine the construction of global viscosity
solutions to the Lorentzian eikonal equation by Zhu–Wu–Cui with our recently developed
elliptic approach to the proof of Lorentzian splitting theorems in joint work with Braun,
Gigli and S¨amann, where we make use of the p-d’Alembertian operator for p < 1.
Keywords: Rigidity of Hawking–Penrose, Lorentzian splitting, cosmological spacetimes,
Bartnik’s conjecture

Introduction
The influential work of Hawking and Penrose on spacetime singularities show that spacetimes
are generically singular, provided some physically reasonable energy and initial/boundary
conditions are satisfied. We refer to standard texts (e.g. Hawking–Ellis [16], Beem–Ehrlich–
Easley [2], O’Neill [21]) for accessible treatments of these results.
While the singularity
theorems are very well understood by now, the same cannot be said about their corresponding
rigidity results. While the situation is rather classical for the Hawking theorem and may be
attributed to Bartnik [1], the rigidity of the Penrose singularity theorem was only recently
investigated by Ling–Galloway [10]. Much more difficult is the setting of the Hawking–Penrose
theorem [17], the most refined of the classical spacetime singularity theorems. The question
of its rigidity in the cosmological case is the content of this article. Before recalling it, we
note that, by definition, a cosmological spacetime is a spacetime which is globally hyperbolic
with compact Cauchy surfaces and which satisfies the strong energy condition: Ric(v, v) ≥0
for every timelike vector v ∈TM.
Theorem 1.1 (Cosmological Hawking–Penrose singularity theorem). Let (M, g) be a cos-
mological spacetime.
Suppose that the generic condition is satisfied, i.e., for every inex-
tendible causal geodesic γ there exists t for which γ(t) is defined and the tidal force operator
R(·, γ′(t))γ′(t) : Tγ(t)M →Tγ(t)M is not the zero operator. Then (M, g) is causally geodesi-
cally incomplete.
In [1], Bartnik conjectured (a slightly stronger statement than) the rigidity of this theorem,
i.e., that every timelike geodesically complete cosmological spacetime must split isometrically
as a product R × S with metric dt2 −˜g, where (S, ˜g) is a compact Riemannian manifold
with nonnegative Ricci curvature.
The intuition underlying this conjecture is that if the
conclusion (causal geodesic incompleteness) fails, not only does the genericity hypothesis fail,
but the geometry should become rigid, in this case meaning dynamically trivial. Equivalently:
when all inertial observers are eternal in a cosmological spacetime, then the dynamics should
trivialize. We answer this question in the affirmative in this article. First however, let us
briefly digress with an account of a different perspective on Lorentzian rigidity.
In 1982, motivated by the Riemannian result of Cheeger–Gromoll [8], Yau [23] asked
whether a timelike geodesically complete spacetime which satisfies the strong energy condition
and contains a timelike line (i.e., an everywhere maximizing timelike geodesic defined on the
entire real line) must split isometrically as a Lorentzian product.
Major progress in this
direction was achieved by Eschenburg [9], who had to assume global hyperbolicity in addition
to timelike geodesic completeness. A year later, Galloway [11] removed the assumption of
timelike geodesic completeness from Eschenburg’s result. Finally, in 1990, Newman [20] gave
a proof of Yau’s original splitting conjecture.
The proofs given in [9, 11, 20] must all overcome a fundamental challenge that is not
present in the Riemannian result of Cheeger–Gromoll [8], namely that the d’Alembertian
(i.e., Lorentzian Laplacian) is a hyperbolic operator in contrast with the elliptic Laplacian
on Riemannian manifolds.
Recently, in collaboration with Braun, Gigli and S¨amann [5],
we gave a proof of the classical Lorentzian splitting theorems by utilizing the degenerate
elliptic p-d’Alembertian operator, which is defined in complete analogy with the p-Laplacian
in Riemannian geometry, except that p < 1: □pf := −div(|∇f|p−2
g
∇f). Via this ellipticity,
we were able to overcome many of the technical challenges faced by Eschenburg, Galloway
and Newman.

Having concluded this digression, we present our main result, which is the cosmological
splitting conjecture as formulated by Bartnik [1]:
Theorem 1.2. If a cosmological spacetime (M, g) is timelike geodesically complete, then it
is isometric to a product (R × S, dt2 −˜g), where (S, ˜g) is a compact Riemannian manifold of
nonnegative Ricci curvature.
This conjecture has been established under a variety of additional assumptions, we refer
to Galloway’s excellent survey [12] where he describes these developments. Some newer de-
velopments on this topic not discussed there include [14, 18, 19]. Notably, the conjecture is
known to hold under the stronger assumption of nonnegative timelike sectional (instead of
Ricci) curvature due to the work of Ling–Galloway [13].

Notation and conventions
Lorentzian metrics have signature (+, −, . . . , −), so g(v, v) > 0 means v is timelike, g(v, v) = 0
means v is null, and g(v, v) < 0 means v is spacelike. Spacetimes are connected, time oriented
Lorentzian manifolds with smooth metric tensors gij ∈C∞and have dimension dim M =:
n ≥2. We fix a background complete Riemannian metric h throughout. If X is the time

v is causal, we write |v|g :=
p
g(v, v) for its Lorentzian norm. A spacelike hypersurface is a
hypersurface such that the restriction of g on it is negative definite. The mean curvature of a
spacelike hypersurface is Tr(∇N), where N is the future unit normal. The function ℓ: M2 →
{−∞} ∪[0, ∞) denotes the (extended) time separation function, by convention ℓ(x, y) = −∞
if x ̸≤y. Given subsets A, B ⊆M, we write I±(A) = ∪a∈AI±(a) for the timelike future and
past of A, J±(A) for the causal future and past of A, and denote I(A, B) := I+(A)∩I−(B) and

curves and the sets J(x, y) are compact for all x, y ∈M (equivalently, there exists a Cauchy
surface, i.e., a set that is uniquely met by each inextendible causal curve and can without
loss of generality be taken to be a smooth spacelike hypersurface). A spacetime is timelike
geodesically complete if the maximal domain of every solution to the geodesic equation with
timelike initial vector is R. Local equi-Lipschitzness and equi-semiconcavity of a family of
functions is understood with respect to the background Riemann metric h or, equivalently,
in coordinates; we refer to [5] for a more detailed discussion.

Outlook
Our proof of cosmological rigidity in the setting of Bartnik’s conjecture seems adaptable to
both the (weighted) Lorentz–Finsler and low regularity settings (cf. [7, 6]), which are worth
exploring.
While our resolution of Bartnik’s splitting conjecture describes the rigidity in the cos-
mological Hawking–Penrose theorem, more general rigidity results pertaining to singularity
theorems are hitherto unexplored. Even in the setting of the Hawking–Penrose theorem itself,
more general causality conditions and/or initial conditions could also be considered.
