The second part of this paper is Section 4 which is devoted to regular and
irregular sets on the graphs of continuous functions. By the Bolzano–Darboux
property there can be at most one direction in which the projection of a continuous
function defined on an interval does not contain an interval. Hence the “regular”
part of the graph of a continuous function is always non-empty. It is much more
interesting to find irregular sets on the graphs of fractal continuous functions. In
Subsection 4.1 the main result is Theorem 4.1 which gives a natural decomposition
of the graph of the Takagi function into regular and irregular parts.
In the next subsection we consider typical/generic continuous functions in the
Baire category sense. The key definitions are Definition 4.2 of the micro tangent
sets and Definition 4.6 of UMT(f) the set of the universal-micro tangent points.
In this subsection the main results are Theorems 4.10 and 4.12 telling that for
almost every x the point (x; f(x)) is in UMT(f), but the projection of UMT(f)
onto the y-axis is of Lebesgue measure zero, in fact, the projection of this set is of
zero measure into any other direction different from the projection onto the x-axis.
This way we obtain a naturally defined irregular 1-set on the graph of a typical
continuous function. This subsection is mainly based on the paper [Buc03].
Theorem 4.8 shows that “most” points on the graph of the typical continuous
function in the sense of one-dimensional Hausdorff measure are not in UMT(f).
Hence it is interesting to consider on the graph of the typical continuous function
the other, non-UMT points. This is the topic of Subsection 4.3, which is based
on the paper [BR06]. The concept of vertical universal points, V MT(f) is intro-
duced. According to Theorems 4.16, 4.17 and 4.19 at most points we have vertical
universality, but as Theorem 4.18 shows there are still exceptional points.
Subsection 4.4 contains some remarks about the micro tangent sets of the Takagi
function and of the 1D Brownian motion. In Theorem 4.20 we see that at almost
every x there is a nice micro self-similarity property of the Takagi function while
Theorem 4.21 tells that the Brownian motion is too wild, with probability one
its universal micro tangent set is empty and the central component of its micro
tangent set is just one vertical line segment.
Subsection 4.5 is devoted to the Weierstrass–Cellerier nowhere differentiable
function and is based on papers [Buc08] and [Buc10]. In Theorem 4.22 we mention

different sections of this survey. In earlier sections it is mentioned that almost every
level set of the Takagi function is finite and its occupation measure is singular with
respect to the Lebesgue measure. Level sets of the typical continuous function are
much larger, but its occupation measure is still singular.
On the other hand,

2. Unrectifiability
The natural numbers are denoted by N = {1, 2, ...}.
Given A⊂Rd, by |A|, int(A), and cl(A) we mean its diameter, interior, and
closure, respectively. The Lebesgue measure of A ⊂Rd is denoted by Ld(A), in
the one-dimensional case we use L(A) instead of L1(A).
The k-dimensional Hausdorff measure is denoted by Hk, the Hausdorff dimen-
sion of A is denoted by dimH A. For the definition of the Hausdorff measure and
for some other basic concepts of fractal geometry we refer to [Fal14]. First we
recall the definitions of rectifiability and unrectifiability.
Definition 2.1. A Borel set E ⊂Rd is a rectifiable subset of dimension k if
dimH E = k and there exists a countable family of Lipschitz maps fi : Rk →Rd
such that Hk(E \ ∪fi(Rk)) = 0, that is their images cover Hk-almost all of E.
The classical planar case, with one dimensional Lipschitz curves was studied by
Besicovitch see [Bes28, Bes38, Bes39] and see also Falconer’s book [Fal86]. For the
higher dimensional case we refer to Mattila’s monograph [Mat95].
Definition 2.2. A Borel set of Hausdorff dimension k which is not rectifiable is
called unrectifiable.
An unrectifiable k-dimensional set E ⊂Rd is called purely k-unrectifiable if its
intersection with any k-dimensional rectifiable set is an Hk-null set.
Previous definitions imply that an unrectifiable set is purely unrectifiable if and
only if its intersection with the image of an arbitrary Lipschitz map f : Rk →Rd
is an Hk-null set.
The following characterisation of pure k-unrectifiability can be found in [Mat95,
Theorem 15.21] or [Fed69, Theorem 3.2.29].
Theorem 2.3. Let k ∈N. A set F ⊂Rd is purely k-unrectifiable if Hk(M∩F) = 0
for all k-dimensional C1-submanifolds M ⊂Rd.
The following is a simple consequence of the above and of Fubini’s theorem.
Corollary 2.4. If F ⊂Rd is a purely 1-unrectifiable Borel set, then it is also
purely k-unrectifiable for any k ∈N.
In [BJJ+21] we considered α-H¨older curves instead of Lipschitz or C1 ones.
Next we suppose that I ⊂R is a closed and bounded interval.
Definition 2.5. Assume that 0 < α ≤1 and H ≥0. A curve γ : I →Rd is
(H, α)-H¨older at a ∈I, if for every b ∈I,
|γ(a) −γ(b)| ≤H|a −b|α.

There is a vast literature about fractal percolation. For some further information
we refer to [Cha95] and [Gri99], or to the recent survey [OS25].
The name fractal percolation comes from the fact that first connectedness ques-
tions were studied of the set E, which is now called the percolation set. The planar,
two-dimensional case was considered by J.T. Chayes, L. Chayes and R. Durrett in
[CCD88]. They showed that there is a critical probability 0 < pc < 1 such that if
p < pc, then E is totally disconnected with probability one, this also includes the
cases when E is empty.
On the other hand, p ≥pc the opposing sides of Q0 are connected by a connected
component of E with positive probability.
The exact value of pc is not known. In [Don15] for the case shown on our figures,
that is for the planar case with 3 × 3 subdivisions of the square the estimate is
0.784 < pc < 0.940. For the creation of the figures we used p = 1 −1/7 ≈0.857.
There is also a survey preprint [KT25] for further information.
For the higher dimensional cases, d > 2 analogous results are true, see for
example [FG92].
There are several further results about the connectedness properties of E. If
p ≥pc Meester in [Mee92] showed that under the usual assumptions E contains a
non-trivial path connected component.
The results in [Cha96] imply that E does not contain uniform α-H¨older curves
for α close to 1 and hence, E does not contain any rectifiable curve.
In the planar case when p ≥pc Broman, Camia, Joosten, and Meester in
[BCJM13] showed that there is a decomposition of E into a totally disconnected
part Ed and a part Ec which consists of non-trivial connected components of E.
They also showed that dimH Ec < dimH Ed = dimH E and depending on the pa-
rameters there exists 0 < β < 1, such that Ec is an uncountable union of non-trivial
β-H¨older curves.
Our main result in [BJJ+21] is the following theorem:
Theorem 3.1 (Theorem 6.13 of [BJJ+21]). For all 0 ≤p < 1, there exists α0 =
α0(p, d, N) < 1 such that, for Pp-almost all ω ∈Ω, the set E(ω) is purely α-
unrectifiable for all α0 < α ≤1.
