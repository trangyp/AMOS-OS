Abstract. We study complex subvarieties in certain non-Kähler holo-
morphically symplectic manifolds X, called the Bogomolov-Guan man-
ifolds. Let E be an elliptic curve, L an ample line bundle on E, S ⊂E2
a complex curve, and p1, p2 the corresponding projections of S to E.
The curve S is called a quasi-diagonal if p∗
1L ⊗p∗
2L−1 is a torsion
line bundle. We show that there are at most countably many quasi-
diagonals for any (E, L). Using the quasi-diagonals, we classify the pro-
jective subvarieties in the Bogomolov-Guan manifold. The Bogomolov-
Guan manifold is equipped with a Lagrangian fibration π : X →CP n.
We show that an irreducible complex subvariety Z ⊂X is Moishezon
if and only if π(Z) is a point or a certain complex curve which is de-

general Bogomolov-Guan manifold, any projective subvariety belongs
to a fiber of π.

Introduction
1.1
Bogomolov-Guan manifolds
In an MPIM preprint [To], A. Todorov claimed that any compact, simply
connected holomorphically symplectic manifold with h2,0 = 1 is Kähler.
However, in [Gu1, Gu2, Gu3], D. Guan constructed examples of manifolds
which are compact, simply connected, holomorphically symplectic but non-
Kähler. These examples, constructed from nilmanifolds, were later studied
further and explained by F. Bogomolov ([Bo]), who used the Hilbert scheme
of the Kodaira surface. We call these complex manifolds Bogomolov-Guan

manifolds or BG-manifolds, and they are the central objects of study in

Although Bogomolov’s construction of the Bogomolov-Guan manifolds
is quite intricate (see Section 3.7 for the details) a step-by-step analysis
reveals that their geometry resembles, in many respects, that of irreducible
holomorphic symplectic manifolds. In particular, these manifolds have even
dimensions, and the first example of such a manifold occurs in dimension 4.
The first systematic study of the geometric properties of Bogomolov-
Guan manifolds was carried out by Kurnosov and Verbitsky in [KV]. They
proved that the holomorphically symplectic deformations of these manifolds
are unobstructed, and demonstrated that they admit an analogue of the
Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki form.
Furthermore, it was shown in [BKKY] that the algebraic reduction of
a 2n-dimensional Bogomolov-Guan manifold BG is a morphism P : BG →
Pn to a projective space of half the dimension. The generic fiber of P is
an abelian variety, and the symplectic form vanishes on the fibers; thus,
BG admits a Lagrangian fibration. The study of algebraic subvarieties in
[BKKY] also revealed a strong restriction: any subvariety of BG whose
image under the Lagrangian fibration P has dimension at least 2 cannot
be projective.
1.2
Bogomolov-Guan precursors
Bogomolov’s construction of Bogomolov-Guan manifolds is long and com-
plicated. Therefore, in this paper, we focus on their precursors: these are
complex manifolds that are simpler to construct but closely related to the
original ones.
As in the original construction of Bogomolov, we start from a (primary)
Kodaira surface Kod (Definition 3.16), which can be obtained as follows. Let
Tot◦(L) be the total space of all non-zero vectors in an ample bundle over an
elliptic curve E; it is a principal C∗-fibration over E. Let Z act on Tot◦(L)
by homothety, multiplying each vector by a fixed complex number λ, with
|λ| > 1. The Kodaira surface is defined as the quotient Tot◦(L)/Z; it is a
holomorphic principal elliptic fibration over an elliptic curve.
The precursor is constructed as follows. We fix a Kodaira surface Kod
and consider its algebraic reduction π: Kod →E, where both the base E and
the general fiber E1 are elliptic curves. Let σ: En+1 →E be the morphism
σ(x1, . . . , xn+1) = x1 + · · · + xn+1. We denote by D the fiber of the com-

1.4
Quasi-diagonals and complex geometry of BG-precursor
Surprisingly, we were able to apply our argument in the converse direction,
deducing theorems about quasi-diagonals from results on complex geometry
of Bogomolov-Guan precursors.
Let X →En be a BG-precursor. It is not hard to see that as well as
the Kodaira surface, the corresponding BG-precursor is a nilmanifold (see
Subsection 3.5), that is, a quotient of a nilpotent Lie group by a cocom-
pact, discrete subgroup acting by translations. This nilpotent group is not
a complex Lie group. However, it carries an invariant complex structure,
obtained from a complex structure operator on its Lie algebra. The inte-
grability of this complex structure is interpreted as a Lie algebra condition
(Definition 3.24).
A Hermitian metric ω on a complex manifold is called pluriclosed, or
SKT, if it satisfies ddcω = 0.
Special metrics on nilmanifolds are related to 2-forms on their Lie alge-
bra. If we identify the space of invariant forms on a nilmanifold with the
Grassmann algebra of its Lie algebra g, de Rham differential is induced from
a map Λ1(g) →Λ2(g) which is dual to the Lie bracket. Knowing an explicit
expression for the Lie bracket and the complex structure, it is not hard to
show that a given 2-form is ddc-closed.

1.5
Main results
Our first result describes projective and Fujiki class C subvarieties in a BG-
manifold and its precursor. Here we use notation introduced in Section 1.2.
Theorem 1.3: Let Y be a subvariety of a Bogomolov-Guan manifold BG
(or of its precursor X). Set Z = α−1(P(Y )) (or Z = PX(Y ), respectively).
Then Y has the following properties:
(1) Y is projective if and only if all components of Z are points or quasi-
diagonals1 over (E, L) where E and L are associated with the Kodaira
surface Kod (see Definition 3.16).
(2) Y is in Fujiki class C if and only if each component S of Z is either a
point or a curve such that the degrees of the projections pi : S →E to
the components of the product are the same for all 1 ⩽i ⩽n + 1.
This theorem, as well as the methods we used in its proof (see Theo-
rem 3.38, Theorem 4.8, Theorem 4.9) are very similar to [BKKY, Theorem
B]. However, by analyzing the construction in more detail we have slightly
improved the result. In particular, we showed that a Moishezon subvariety
of a Bogomolov-Guan manifold is necessarily projective. Also we show that
any smooth projective subvariety in a BG-precursor is necessarily Kähler
(Theorem 4.8).
In order to understand better the projective subvarieties in Bogomolov-
Guan manifolds and precursors, we study quasi-diagonals. Our second result
shows that quasi-diagonals never come in continuous families.

Theorem 1.4: Let E be an elliptic curve and L be an ample line bundle on
E. Then, for any integer n ⩾2, the product En contains at most a countable
set of quasi-diagonals over (E, L).
We provide two different proofs of this theorem using distinct methods.
In both proofs the first step is to reduce to the case n = 2, see Theorem 2.19.
The first proof in Section 2.5 uses purely algebro-geometric methods:
we assume that there exists a family of quasi-diagonals over (E, L) in E2
parametrized by a variety T, consider the universal space π: X →T of the
family and study the inverse image of the bundle M = p∗
1L ⊗p∗
2L−1 to X.
On the one hand, the property of quasi-diagonals implies that M equals a
pull-back of a line bundle on T. On the other hand, we show that the inverse
image of the line bundle p∗
i L to X is supported on a multisection of π. This
leads us to a contradiction.
The second proof in Section 5.5 uses Campana’s coreduction theorem.
This theorem states that if X is a sufficiently good compact complex variety
then there exists a meromorphic map X →Y which contracts almost all
Moishezon subvarieties in X. If E2 contains a continuous family of quasi-
diagonals then the variety X = Kod2/E1 satisfies the coreduction theorem
and two general points of X are connected by a chain of projective varieties
similarly to Theorem 1.3. Thus, the coreduction map contracts X to a point,
which is impossible since X is not Moishezon (a Kodaira surface Kod can be
embedded in X).
Theorem 1.4 demonstrates that quasi-diagonals are quite rare. Thus, one
can ask whether there exists a quasi-diagonal or restricted quasi-diagonal
over (E, L) for a given elliptic curve E and a line bundle L ∈Pic(E). We
construct several examples which show that in some cases we get large sets
of (restricted) quasi-diagonals while generally there are no restricted quasi-
diagonals.
Proposition 1.5: Let E be an elliptic curve and L be an ample line bundle
on E. Then the following assertions hold.
(1) For each elliptic curve E, a line bundle L ∈Picd(E) for d ⩾1 and
integer n ⩾2 there is a countable set of quasi-diagonals over (E, L) in
En.
(2) If n is even or if the j-invariant of E equals 0 then there exists L ∈
Picd(E) where d ⩾1 such that there is a countable set of restricted
quasi-diagonals over (E, L) in En.

1.6
Structure of this paper
In Section 2, we formally define quasi-diagonals and restricted quasi-diagonals,
provide several explicit examples (including symmetric curves and non-elliptic
quasi-diagonals), and reduce the study of quasi-diagonals in En to E2. The
section concludes with the algebraic proof of Theorem 1.4 and states several
open questions.
In Section 3, we review the theory of principal toric and elliptic fibrations,
describe the construction of Kodaira surfaces as nilmanifolds, and detail the
construction of Bogomolov-Guan manifolds and their precursors. Using the
theory of principal toric bundles, we then prove the classification theorems for
projective and Fujiki class C subvarieties in the Bogomolov-Guan manifolds.
In Section 4, we prove the analogous classification result for subvarieties
in the Bogomolov-Guan precursor, completing the proof of Theorem 1.3.
Section 5 provides a complex-analytic perspective on our rigidity results.
We recall the definitions of Barlet spaces, Campana’s coreduction map, and
SKT (pluriclosed) metrics. We show that the Bogomolov-Guan precursor
admits an SKT metric. Using Gromov’s compactness theorem for Barlet
spaces, we provide an independent geometric proof of Theorem 1.4.
Finally, Appendix A presents a detailed analysis of the preimages of
general lines in a 4-dimensional Bogomolov-Guan manifold, demonstrating
that they do not yield quasi-diagonals in general and further illustrating the
scarcity of restricted quasi-diagonals.
