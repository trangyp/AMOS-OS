1. Introduction
Transformers are neural-network architectures in which self-attention updates each token by
comparing it with the other tokens through query–key inner products and then aggregating
the corresponding value vectors [VSP+17]. Recent mathematical work isolates this mechanism
by considering deep, continuous-time limits of attention layers [GLPR25] (see also the works
[CRBD18, E17, HR17, LLH+20, DGCC21, SABP22] for a machine learning perspective). After
layer normalization, token embeddings may be viewed as directions on a sphere, and the attention
update becomes an interacting particle system on Sd−1; its large-token, or mean-field, limit is
a McKean–Vlasov equation, alternatively a Wasserstein gradient flow of a certain free energy
[GLPR25, Rig25]. In this note, we consider a simplified architecture called the unnormalized
self-attention (USA) dynamics in the literature, which features an isotropic spherical attention
interaction as we now describe.
