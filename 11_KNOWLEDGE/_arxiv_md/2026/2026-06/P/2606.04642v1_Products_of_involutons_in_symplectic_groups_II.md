PRODUCTS OF INVOLUTONS IN SYMPLECTIC GROUPS II
KLAUS NIELSEN
Abstract. We show that the symplectic groups Sp(2n, K) over a field K are
5-reflectional if 2n ≥4. If n is even and (2n, K) ̸= (4, GF(3)) they are even
4-reflectional.
1. Introduction
This is a continuation of our paper [4]. So we assume familiarity with the def-
initions and results of that paper. We always assume that K denotes a field of
characteristic different from two.
We prove the following 2 theorems announced in [4].
Theorem 1.1. Sp(2n, K) is 4 reflectional if n is even and (n, |K|) ̸= (2, 3).
Theorem 1.2. Sp(2n, K) is 5 reflectional.
In [7, Proposition 4.1], C. de Seguins Pazzis has shown the following result.
Theorem 1.3. Let K be infinite. If Sp(2n, K) is k-reflectional, then Sp(2n+4, K)
is k-reflectional.
We have a similar result:
Theorem 1.4. Assume that Sp(6, K) is 4-reflectional. Then
(1) Sp(2n, K) is 4-reflectional for 2n ≥14.
(2) If |K| ≥9, then Sp(10, K) is 4-reflectional.
And as a byproduct of our observations, we have
Theorem 1.5. Let P ∈Sp(2n, K).
(1) P is the product of 2 big transvections if and only if P has no elementary
divisor (x + 1)d of odd degree d.
(2) P is the product of 3 big transvections.
In the spirit of O’Meara, we say that a linear transformation φ is a big transvec-
tion if its minimal polynomial is µ(φ) = (x −1)2.
[O’Meara [6] calls a linear
transformation a big dilation if its minimal polynomial is (x −1)(x −λ), where
λ ̸= 0, 1.]
It is easy to show that a matrix P ∈GL(n, K) is the product of 2 big transvec-
tions if and only if P is similar to its inverse and has no elementary divisor (x+1)d
of odd degree d. Further it is well known that a matrix with determinant one is
the product of 2 unipotent matrices, hence it is the product of 4 big transvections.

Without proof we also mention the following result:
Remark 6.1. Let Ωbe the set of noncentral involutions in Sp(4, 3).
Let P ∈
Sp(4, 3). Then
(1) P ∈Ω6.
(2) P ̸∈Ω5 if and only if P or −P is a transvection.
(3) P ∈Ω3 ∩Ω4 if and only if
(a) µP = x2 ± 1 or
(b) µP = x4 + 1 or
(c) µP = (x −1)2(x + 1)2 and P and −P −1 are conjugate
(4) P ∈Ω3 −Ω4 if and only if µP = (x −1)2(x + 1) or µP = (x + 1)2(x −1).
(5) P ̸∈Ω3 ∪Ω4 if and only if
(a) µP = (x2 + 1)(x ± 1)2 or
(b) µP = (x −1)2(x + 1)2 and P and −P are conjugate.

time 8 h) and recently checked it again using the computer algebra system GAP
[2] on a meteorlake cpu (runtime less than 30 s). It is not difficult to prove 6.1 by
hand once you know what to show.
