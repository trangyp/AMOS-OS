PATHOLOGICAL FUNCTION SPACES AND
AN UNSOLVED ANALYSIS I PROBLEM
THOMAS RANSFORD
Abstract. The spaces in the title are holomorphic function spaces on
the unit disk containing a function with the bizarre property that, al-
though it can be approximated in norm by polynomials, it cannot be
approximated by the partial sums of its own Taylor series, nor by any
linear combinations thereof. The search for concrete examples of such
spaces leads to an elementary problem about convergent series that is
still unsolved.

(III) Polynomials are not norm-dense in X. Evidently, in this case, X

The existence of function spaces X of the type (IV), which we label ‘patho-
logical spaces’, was established only relatively recently. The first examples,
presented in [7], were based on Banach spaces not having the bounded ap-
proximation property.
Later, in [5], it was shown that there even exist
pathological Hilbert function spaces. In fact, more is true:
Theorem 1.1 ([5, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2]). There exist a Hilbert
holomorphic function space H and a function f ∈H such that:
• H contains the polynomials as a dense subspace;
• f lies outside the closed linear span of {sn(f) : n ≥0}.

Remarks. (i) The bound ∥zn∥H ≤1 + ωn is useful in certain applications

(ii) As presented in [5], the space H is an artificial construct, not a nat-

the problem in the hope that it will eventually be solved.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. We develop the necessary
background on general Hilbert function spaces in §2 and on de Branges–
Rovnyak spaces in §3. Theorem 1.2 is proved in §4. The remainder of the
article is devoted to a discussion of Problem 1.3. In §5 we show that the
possible solutions to this problem are subject to certain quite restrictive
constraints. Finally, in §6, we describe our best attempt at a solution to
Problem 1.3. We show that the problem can be reformulated as a ques-
tion about one sequence instead of three, albeit with a more complicated
condition on the sequence.

3. De Branges–Rovnyak spaces
The spaces now called de Branges–Rovnyak spaces were introduced by
de Branges and Rovnyak in the appendix of [1] and further studied in [2].
They were later popularized by the book of Sarason [10]. It is now known
that these spaces have a variety of connections with other topics in complex
analysis and operator theory, and they have attracted the interest of many
authors.
In this section we present a brief review of these spaces.
The
interested reader may find much more information in the books of Sarason
[10] and Fricain–Mashreghi [3].

Let b ∈H∞with ∥b∥H∞≤1. The map (z, w) 7→(1−b(w)b(z))/(1−wz) is
positive-definite on D×D, so it is the reproducing kernel of a unique Hilbert
space of functions on D. This space is called the de Branges–Rovnyak space
associated to b, and is denoted by H(b).
Each H(b)-space is contractively contained in the Hardy space H2. In
particular, convergence in the norm of H(b) implies local uniform conver-
gence in D. This is one of the two qualifying conditions to be a holomorphic
function space on D. The other, namely that H(b) contain the polynomials,
is only satisfied for certain b. This is the subject of the following result. We
write T for the unit circle.
Theorem 3.1 ([10, §§IV-2, IV-3, NIV-1, V-I]). Let b ∈H∞with ∥b∥H∞≤1.
Then the following statements are equivalent:
(i) H(b) contains the polynomials;
(ii) b is a non-extreme point of the unit ball of H∞;
(iii) log(1 −|b|2) ∈L1(T).
Moreover, if (i)–(iii) hold, then the polynomials are dense in H(b).
Using the equivalence between (ii) and (iii) in Theorem 3.1, we see that
every non-extreme b has a unique Pythagorean mate, namely an outer func-
tion a on D such that a(0) > 0 and |b|2 + |a|2 = 1 a.e. on T. Conversely, if
b has a Pythagorean mate a, then it is non-extreme.

6. An attempt at a solution
In this section we describe our best attempt at a solution to Problem 1.3.
Part of the difficulty with this problem is that, with three sequences to
juggle, it is unclear where to begin. The basic strategy behind our attempt
