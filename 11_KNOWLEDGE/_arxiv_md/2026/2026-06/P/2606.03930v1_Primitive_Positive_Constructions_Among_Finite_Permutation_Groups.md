Abstract
Primitive positive constructions of first order structures have been
shown to be a very useful tool in universal algebra for the study of con-
straint satisfaction problems.
However, they seemed to be very rarely
studied in classical algebra such as group theory.
This paper fills in
this gaps by looking at structures and obstructions based on permutation
groups and giving a full classification in this sub-area. This special case
is also very important for the generalization to all first order structures
as every permutation group describes an easy-to-check necessary condi-
tion for the existence of primitive positive constructions, also between
structures that are not at all linked to permutation groups.

Introduction
Many problems in theoretical computer science, such as HORN-SAT, 3-SAT,
3-colorability of graphs, directed reachability in directed graphs, reachability in
undirected graphs, may be formulated as a finite constraint satisfaction problem
(CSP). In this formulation, one has to distribute a certain amount of variables
onto a fixed finite template such that some conditions are satisfied respect-
ively return that this is impossible. Depending on the shape of the template,
this computational problem can be in different complexity classes. The above
examples are NP-complete for 3-coloring of graphs and 3-SAT problems, the
HORN-SAT problem is P complete, directed reachability is NL-complete and
undirected reachability can be solved in L, on a logarithmic space. So an obvious
question is, given any CSP, determine its complexity class. Primitive positive
constructions have been proven to be a very fruitful tool to this task as on one
side a primitive positive construction gives a log-space, and thus polynomial
time, reduction of the related problems. On the other side, the existence of a
2


## Page 3


primitive positive construction is equivalent to a purely algebraic condition and
thus can be studied completely within the framework of universal algebra. In
this setting, every template becomes a first-order structure and the existence of
a primitive positive construction is completely described in terms of the poly-
morphism minion, a generalization of the endomorphism monoid. Note that the
existence of a primitive positive construction is transitive and thus it defines a
preorder on all finite structures.
A general classification of all structures up to primitive positive construc-
tions is not known and probably similar complicated as the classification of
all finite simple groups. In fact we have so far some partial classifications as
the classification on undirected graphs [HN90], 2-element structures [BV20] and
smooth digraphs [BSV21].
This paper considers itself as part of the project to classify all finite struc-
tures up to primitive positive constructions. In this problem, group-actions or,
historically correct, permutation groups appear in two contexts: As a structure
within the primitive positive constructability poset and moreover as a minor
condition. The second one can be considered within the poset of implications.
We give a full classification in both contexts, linking both posets to the epi-
morphism poset of groups with a trivial Frattini subgroup.
Permutation group are of a special interest for multiple reasons.
When
studying CSPs it is of a wide interest to study for a given algorithm the set of
structure templates whose CSP can be solved by the algorithm. For multiple
algorithms, this set can be described by permutation groups:
• A CSP can be solved by basic linear programming robustly in polynomial
time if and only if the corresponding structure does not construct any
permutation group [KOT+12, Theorem 2], see also Theorem 6.6.
• A CSP can be solved with the Zhuk Algorithm in polynomial time if and
only if the corresponding structure admits a Taylor polymorphism [Zhu17,
Zhu20].
(It is NP complete otherwise.)
By the cyclic terms theorem
[BK12, Theorem 4.2], this is eqivalent to the existence of a permutation
group which is not primitively positively constructed.
These applications are usually phrases in terms of minor conditions. Getting
to know which minor conditions correspond to which structures and which of
them pp-construct each other is really helpful.
Moreover, there is the recent result by Meyer and Starke [MS24] analysing
the full poset of all finite structures ordered by pp-constructions which gives
a classification of the simplest non-trivial layer of this poset.
It consists of
equivalence classes of structures, where representations are given by T3, a graph
on three vertices, and for each finite simple group G a specific G-set called P(G).
So studying all G-sets for all groups G is the next step to take. We give in
this paper a new explanation why among all groups exactly the simple groups
appear: These groups are the topmost groups in the poset of all finite groups
ordered by epimorphisms.

Preliminaries from group theory
All sets and groups are considered to be finite. We assume the reader to be
familiar with the notion of a group.
The notions are similar as in [MS24] whenever we overlap with this paper.
We write [n] for the set {1, 2, . . . , n} = N ∩[1, n].
2.1
Group Actions
A group action of a group G on a set X is a map α: G × X →X such that
α((gh), x) = α(g, α(h.x)) and α(1, x) = x for all g, h ∈G and x ∈X. This
action is denoted by G ↷α X. If α is clear from the context, we also write
G ↷X for G ↷α X and g.x for α(g, x). A G-set is a set together with a
G-action. Note that X might be empty.
If G ↷X and H ≤G is a subgroup, we denote the fixed points {x ∈X |
∀h ∈H : h.x = x} of H in X with fixX(H) and the set of H-orbits with X/H.
The orbit of x ∈X is denoted H.x. The (set) stabiliser of a subset S ⊆X with
respect to G is denoted by StabG(S). If G is clear from the context we also
write Stab(S) instead of StabG(S). If S = {s} we also write StabG(s) instead
of StabG({s}).
A homomorphism f of G-sets from G ↷X to G ↷Y is a map from X
to Y such that f(g.x) = g.f(x) for all g ∈G and x ∈X.
The set of all
homomorphism from X to Y is denoted by HomG(X, Y ). We call two G-sets X
and Y homomorphically equivalent, if both HomG(X, Y ) and HomG(Y, X) are
non-empty.
A sub-quotient of a group G is a group that is isomorphic to S/N, where
S ≤G is a subgroup and N ⊴S a normal subgroup.
We will use many common G-sets in this article, such as the following ones:
• A group G is itself a G-set, where the action is left multiplication. This
action is called regular action and will be relevant mostly for the groups
Z/nZ of integers modulo n.

2.2
The Frattini action
It will turn out that for each group, there is one very interesting group action
given by the disjoint union of all primitive group actions.
2.2.1
Maximal subgroups and primitive actions
We explain primitive group actions in this section. The following definition and
the two observations are taken from [MS24, Section 2.1].
Definition 2.1. A group action G ↷X is called primitive if X has more than
one element and the only partitions of X that are respected by the G action are
{X} (a single partition) and {{x} | x ∈X} (the discrete partition). A G-set is
primitive if its action is.
Observation 2.2. For the group action G ↷G/H on the left-H-cosets, we
have StabG({H}) = H. Conversely, if G ↷X is a group action with a single
orbit and x ∈X, then X is isomorphic to G/ StabG(x) as G-set, where an
isomorphism is given by G/ StabG(x) →X, g StabG(x) 7→g.x. This map is
well-defined.
For different points x and g.x in the same orbit of a G-action G ↷X, we
have
StabG(g.x) = g StabG(x)g−1,
so the stabilizers are conjugated.
Similarly, the G-sets G/H and G/H′ are
isomorphic if and only if H and H′ are conjugated.
Observation 2.3. It is well known that primitive actions are closely related to
maximal subgroups. The following are equivalent for a G-action G ↷X:
1. G ↷X is primitive.
2. G ↷X is isomorphic to G/M as G-set for a maximal subgroup M < G.
3. G ↷X is transitive and there exists x ∈X, such that StabG(x) is a
maximal subgroup of G.
4. G ↷X is transitive and for all x ∈X, StabG(x) is a maximal subgroup
of G.
The G-sets G/M and G/M ′ are isomorphic if and only if M and M ′ are con-
jugated. See for example [DM96, Corollary 1.4A and 1.5A].

The Frattini subgroup and minimal fixed-point free actions
We explain the Frattini action action and its close link to the Frattini subgroup
in this subsection.

as the conjugations of a maximal subgroups are maximal.
We call a G-action on a set X minimal fixed-point free, if it has no fixed
point, but every subgroup action H ↷X of a proper subgroup H ≤G has
a fixed point. Let P(G) for G ̸= {1} be the internal union of all primitive G-
actions. We call the G action G ↷P(G) Frattini action. The set P({1}) is the
empty set as we will discuss in Section 2.3.
Lemma 2.4 (Lemma 4.6 from [MS24]). Let G be a finite group of order at
least two acting on a set X. Then S(G ↷X) is homomorphically equivalent to
S(G ↷P(G)) if and only if the action of G on X is a minimal fixed point free
action.
In particular, the action of G on P(G) is a minimal fixed point free action.
Moreover, G ↷P(G) is a group action, such that an element g ∈G acts
trivial if and only if it is in the Frattini subgroup Φ(G), as it needs to be in all
point stabilizer goups, which are exactly the maximal subgroups.
If N ⊴G is a normal subgroup then the maximal subgroups of G/N are
exactly the quotients M/N where M ≤G is a maximal subgroup that contains
N. This follows immediately from the fact that subgroups of G/N are given by
subgroups of G that contain N. Moreover, the G/N-group action P(G/N) and
the quotient group sub-action on fixP(G)(N) are isomorphic.
The quotient G/Φ(G) has a trivial Frattini subgroup as the maximal sub-
groups of G/Φ(G) are exactly the quotients of the maximal subgroups of G.
As fixP(G)(Φ(G)) is all of P(G), the quotient group action G/Φ(G) ↷P(G) is
well-defined and isomorphic to the Frattini action G/Φ(G) ↷P(G/Φ(G)).
2.3
The empty action
We allow in addition to non-empty G-sets also the empty action G ↷∅, the
action of any group on an empty set. This decision differs from [MS24] but
simplifies the theory. As {1} has no primitive action, we define P({1}) to be ∅.
Note that this definition extends Lemma 2.4 to the trivial group. Recall that
we defined the G-set ∅0 to be a single point with the trivial action.

3.1
Preliminaries on primitive positive constructions
We refer to [Hod97] for the definition of a (fist-order) structure, as well as
of products and homomorphisms of them. We assume for simplicity that the
structure is relational as every function symbol can be replaced by a relational
symbol and we allow the base set to be empty. A primitive positive definition
(pp-definition) over a σ-structure A is a first-order definition which is build
recursively from
1. atomic positive relations, that are
• relations from σ, including = between arbitrary variables,
• true (but not false),
2. ∃x : ϕ whenever ϕ is already pp-defined and x is a variable,
3. ϕ ∧ψ whenever ϕ and ψ are already pp-defined.
Such definition with free variable set V naturally defines a subset of AV by
evaluating the formula. We write x |= ϕ if x ∈AV is inside the set defined by
ϕ. We do not distinguish the structure and its underlying set in our notation.
It is also common to allow false as atomic relation. We do not follow this
convention and consider nullary polymorphisms instead to obtain a clean theory.
We say that a σ-structure A primitively positively constructs or pp-constructs
a τ-structure B, if there exists a non-negative integer n, a primitive postitive

Nullary polymorphisms and constant unary polymorphisms are closely re-
lated and divide the structures into three classes:
• If a structure A has a nullary polymorphism, then it also has a constant
polymorphism and moreover, for every element a ∈A, there is a constant
polymorphism onto a if and only if there is a nullary polymorphism onto
a. Such structures exist, such as sets over an empty signature.
• There are structures without a nullary polymorphisms, that have no con-
stant polymorphism such as a graph without any edge. For such a graph
G, its 0-th power is the graph with a single point and a loop, which cannot
be mapped to G.
• If a structure has no constant polymorphism, then it has no nullary poly-
morphism by the first bullet. A graph that has an edge, but no loop is an
example of such a structure.

6.4
Applications
This paper brings a new insight, why finite simple groups showed up in the
primitive positive constructability poset: In fact, the poset of all groups with
trivial Frattini subgroup ordered by group epimorphisms is a subposet of the
poset of all finite structures ordered by pp-constructions. As the simple groups
are on top of this list, they appeared in [MS24].
In addition, we are now able to give a different proof of a variation of [MS24,
Theorem 4.10] by using the classification of all group actions up to elementary
implications to improve [KOT+12, Theorem 2]:
Theorem 6.6. For a structure A, the following is equivalent:
1. The structure A satisfies Σ(Sn ↷[n]) for all n.
2. The structure A satisfies Σ(G ↷X) for all fixed-point free group actions
G ↷X.
3. The structure A satisfies Σ(G ↷P(G)) for all finite simple groups G.
4. The structure A cannot pp-construct S(G ↷X) for any fixed-point free
group actions G ↷X.
Moreover, it it known from the literature, that the previous conditions are equi-

programming robustly in polynomial time.
6. The structure A has width 1.
7. The structure A has a set operation.
8. The structure A has a measure operation.
9. The structure A has tree duality.
Proof. 1 implies 3 as G ↷P(G) is a subgroup action of S|P(G)| ↷[|P(G))|]

6.5
Open problems
With a view toward classifying all structures up to primitive positive construc-
tions, there are two canonical questions concerning permutation groups:
Problem 6.7. Given any finite structure A, decide wether it is primitively
positively interconstrucable with a permutation group.
It is easy to see that such a structure needs to admit a Pixley operation,
named after [Pix63], that is a polymorphism p satisfying
p(x, y, x) = p(y, y, x) = p(x, y, y) = p(x, x, x)
as every permutation group has such an operation. However this condition is
not enough as the structure
({0, 1, −1}; {1}, {(x, y) | |x| = |y|}, {(x, y) | x + y = 0}),
called M1 in [FKRV25], has a Pixley operation but cannot be pp-constructed
from a permutation group.
We have a conjectured answer to this question:
Conjecture 6.8. Given any structure A. The following are equivalent:
1. The structure A has a Maltsev operation i.e.
∃f ∈Pol(A) : ∀x, y : f(x, y, y) = f(x, x, x) = f(y, y, x)
and it has a guarded fully symmetric operation of all arities i.e. for all n,
we have
∃f ∈Pol(A) :
∀x, y1, . . . , yn, ∀σ ∈Sn : f(x; y1, . . . , yn) = f(x; yσ(1), . . . , yσ(n)),
∀x1, x2, y : f(x1; y, . . . , y) = f(x2; y, . . . , y)
2. The structure A is pp-interconstructable with a permutation group or P1,
the structure ({0, 1}; <).
The other question takes any structure A and looks at the generalization of
the set χpp(A). Given any structure A, define χpp(A) to be the set of all finite
groups G with trivial Frattini subgroup such that A pp-constructs S(G ↷P(G)).
Problem 6.9. For which sets S of groups is there a finite structure A such that
χpp(A) = S?
For every finite set, A exists and can be chosen to be a permutation group
by this results. For infinite sets, it is known by the cyclic terms theorem [BK12,
Theorem 4.2] that S has to contain all cyclic groups (in which case A is not
Taylor) or it contains only finitely many of them. We believe to be able to
generalize the cyclic terms theorem to get the following classification:
