Abstract
We describe the Poisson bracket of a Lagrangian field theory expressed in the framework
of L∞algebras.
We show that the recently proposed symplectic structure implies that
the associated Poisson bracket can be computed through the Peierls formula. We consider
Poisson brackets in p-adic string theory, where interesting complications arise. In addition
we give an elegant interpretation of the inverse relation between the Poisson bracket and
symplectic structure in the language of homological algebra, extending some ideas in the
mathematical physics literature.

Introduction
This is part of a series of articles [1, 2] describing a new approach to Hamiltonian mechanics based
on field theories formulated in terms of L∞algebras [3, 4]. The goal is to use the covariant phase
space formalism [5, 6, 7, 8] and L∞data to construct phase space observables without reference
to the derivative structure of the Lagrangian. We then can formulate Hamiltonian mechanics in
nonlocal field theories where the standard canonical formalism breaks down. Some applications to
string field theory [9, 10, 11, 12] are discussed in [13, 14, 15, 16].
The focus here is on the Poisson bracket. We want a construction which inverts the symplectic
form
Ω= 1
2ω(δΦ, [QΦ, σ]δΦ),
(1.1)
introduced in [1]. We show that the relevant inverse is computed using the causal propagator,
defining the Poisson bracket as originally described by Peierls [17]. This agrees with the well-
known result of the covariant phase space formalism [18, 19], which indirectly implies that the
symplectic structure (1.1) should also be standard, at least for finite derivative theories.
See
also [20], which explains the origin of (1.1) from the covariant phase space method and establishes
a connection to the Barnich-Brandt symplectic form [21].
This paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we summarize the formalism introduced in [1, 2],
and in section 3 we formally define the Poisson bracket. This starts in subsection 3.1 with an
argument that every physical observable defines a symmetry of the symplectic structure as defined
by (1.1). The symmetry transformation can be constructed via the causal propagator, reproducing
the standard Peierls formula. In subsection 3.2 we show that, conversely, every symmetry of the
symplectic structure as defined by (1.1) defines a physical observable. The result is an on-shell
version of the conserved charge constructed in [2]. In subsection 3.3 we review the definition and

of conserved charges, giving a specific formula for the central charge. In section 4 we discuss

can be discarded, though it is unclear if is consistent to do so. Finally in section 5 we discuss

of homological algebra, a question of interest in connection to algebraic quantum field theory in
curved space. The idea is to realize the causal propagator as the differential of an exact sequence,
which in a sense implies that the causal propagator is invertible. The inverse is related to the
symplectic form. After discussing this at the level of cohomology in subsection 5.1, in subsection
5.2 we give a far-reaching generalization which accounts for gauge symmetries. We refer to this as
the Peierls complex. Using the sigmoid we are able to construct an explicit contracting homotopy
for the complex, which substantially simplifies and generalizes much previous work on the subject.
Conventions
We assume that the products of the L∞algebra have odd parity, are graded commutative and
carry grade +1. All commutators are graded according to whether the object is commuting or
anticommuting.

3
Formalism
In this section we describe the construction of the Poisson bracket. Unlike the symplectic structure
and conserved charges, the Poisson bracket follows from the definition of the propagator whose
existence does not necessarily require a finite derivative Lagrangian. Therefore we do not need
a fundamentally new approach to the Poisson bracket.
We define it based on the formula of
Peierls [17]. Aside from expressing this in the context of our formalism, the main new result is
establishing that the Peierls formula inverts the symplectic structure as defined in (1.1).

Considering the effort invested articulating the right concept, the simplicity of the Peierls
complex is quite remarkable. One can ask why it was not recognized before. One reason is that
there did not appear to be a natural operator on H which would represent the action of the
symplectic form. In the standard description the symplectic form is displayed as an integral of
a current density over an initial value surface, which does not suggest an interpretation as an
operator on H. If described as an operator, it would be a delta function which localizes to the
initial value surface, requiring us to work with a singular space of fields. The sigmoid softens the
time slice and makes it natural to think about an inverse for the causal propagator. Something
related to the Peierls complex appears in [33], but it is embedded in a highly technical formalism.
