Abstract. We consider a bound of degrees of curves by their
genera in the log pair (Pn × C,D), where C is a smooth complex
projective curve and D is a very general smooth divisor on Pn ×C.
Our study is motivated by a function ﬁeld analogue of the Lang-
Vojta conjecture.
1. Introduction and Main result
In this paper we consider a bound of degrees of curves in the log pair
(Pn × C,D), where C is a smooth projective curve and D is a smooth
divisor on Pn × C. Our result is motivated by a function ﬁeld analogue
of the Lang-Vojta conjecture.
1.1. Lang-Vojta Conjecture. Lang-Vojta conjecture ([HS, Conjec-
ture F.5.3.6]) predicts that if X is a smooth projective variety over
a number ﬁeld k, S is a ﬁnite set of places of k containing all inﬁnite
places, D is a normal crossing divisor on X such that the divisor KX+D
is big, then no set of S-integral k-rational points on X ∖D is Zariski
dense.
Pursuing an analogy between diophantine problems and Nevanlinna
Theory, Vojta proposed the following inequality :
Conjecture 1.1. ([Vojta, Conjecture 15.6]) Let X, k and S be as
above. Let D be a normal crossing divisor on X, and let A be an ample
line bundle on X. Then for any ǫ > 0, there is a proper Zariski-closed
subset Z of X such that for all c ∈R, the inequality
(1.1)
NS(D,x) ≥hKX(D),k(x) −hA,k(x) −c
holds for almost x ∈(X ∖Z)(k).
Here NS(D,x) is the counting function (see [Vojta, §11]), hKX(D),k(x)
and hA,k(x) are heights of x with respect to KX(D) and A respectively,
and “almost all” means “all but ﬁnitely many.”
Conjecture 1.1 implies Lang-Vojta conjecture (cf. [Vojta, Proposi-
tion 15.9]). Vojta also proposes a stronger inequality :

1.4. Outline of Proof of Theorem 1.5. In [A24], the author studied
subvarieties of very general log projective spaces employing a method,
which uses the positivity of normal bundles, invented by Ein ([E88],
[E91]), and then developed by Voisin ([V96], [V98]), Pacienza ([P03],
[P04]), Clemens and Ran ([CR]) and Pacienza and Rousseau ([PR]).
The proof of Theorem 1.5 is similar to that of [A24, Theorem 8.1]; we
only repeat the argument in [A24] relatively over the curve C. Specif-
ically, if a curve Y ⊂Pn × C not contained in Dσ does not satisfy the
inequality in Theorem 1.5 (i), then we show that for a general point
y ∈Y , there exists a vertical line ly passing through y that intersects
Dσ in at most two points. To this end, we ﬁrst show that to a gen-
eral points y ∈Y , we can associate a vertical line ly passing through
y in such a way that for general two points y and y′ ∈Y , the divi-
sors Dσ∣ly on the pointed line (ly,y) and Dσ∣ly′ on the pointed line
(ly′,y′) are equivalent, that is, there exists an isomorphism ly ≃ly′ of
lines mapping y to y′ such that Dσ∣ly and Dσ∣ly′ correspond under the
isomorphism. Then using a moduli space of lines intersecting Dσ “in a
ﬁxed divisor,” we show that if ly intersects Dσ in more than two points,
then Y satisﬁes the inequality in Theorem 1.5 (i).
