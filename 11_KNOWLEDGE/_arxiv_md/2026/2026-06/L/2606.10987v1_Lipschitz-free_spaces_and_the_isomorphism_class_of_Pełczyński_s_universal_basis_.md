LIPSCHITZ-FREE SPACES AND THE ISOMORPHISM CLASS OF
PE LCZY ´NSKI’S UNIVERSAL BASIS SPACE
RICHARD J. SMITH
Abstract. We show that the class of complete separable metric spaces whose Lipschitz-
free space is isomorphic to Pe lczy´nski’s universal basis space P is Σ1
1-complete.
1. Introduction
Let M be a metric space with base point 0M, i.e. a pointed metric space. The Lipschitz
space Lip0(M) is the Banach space of real-valued Lipschitz functions on M that vanish
at 0M, whose norm assigns to each function its optimal Lipschitz constant Lip(f). The
Lipschitz-free (hereafter free) space F(M) over M (also called the transportation cost
space or the Arens-Eells space and denoted Æ(M)) is the closed linear span in Lip0(M)∗
of the evaluation functionals δ(x), x ∈M, defined by ⟨f, δ(x)⟩= f(x), f ∈Lip0(M). Free
spaces enjoy close links with metric geometry and optimal transport theory, and have been
a popular topic of study by functional analysts for nearly 25 years. For the fundamental
properties of these spaces, the reader is encouraged to consult the standard sources [15,31].
We present some of the many intriguing characteristics of these spaces. The first is their
universal property, which allows us to linearise certain Lipschitz maps: if X is a Banach
space and f : M →X is a Lipschitz map satisfying f(0M) = 0, then there exists a unique
linear operator T : F(M) →X satisfying f = T ◦δ and ∥T∥= Lip(f) [31, Theorem 3.6].
Second is the fact that a Banach space X has the bounded approximation property (BAP)
if and only if F(X) has the BAP [15, Theorem 5.3]. Third is the intriguing fact, which
follows in part from the second, that P ∼= F(P), where P denotes Pe lczy´nski’s universal
basis space (see [25] and [1, Section 15.3]) and ∼= means linear isomorphism [15, p. 139].
Concerning P in particular, it follows from [19, Corollary 3.3] and the above that
P ∼= F(BP) (where BP is the closed unit ball of P), and P ∼= F(K) for some compact
convex set K ⊆P [13, Theorem 1]. This leads one to suspect that the class of separable
metric spaces M such that P ∼= F(M) could be rather diverse. The main result of this
paper shows that the class of such M satisfying P ∼= F(M) has maximal complexity,
relative to the appropriate descriptive set-theoretic framework.
In general, the classification of separable free spaces up to isomorphism is notoriously
difficult.
Despite several advances made over the last two decades in particular, still
relatively little is known. It follows from the universal property that if M and N are
Lipschitz isomorphic then F(M) ∼= F(N), but the converse fails badly (as seen above)
and the answers to many easily stated questions, such as whether F(R2) ∼= F(R3), still

2. Notation and preliminaries
The notation used for Banach spaces will be standard throughout. Recall that, given
λ ⩾1, a Banach space X is said to have the λ-bounded approximation property (λ-BAP)
if, given ε > 0 and x1, . . . , xn ∈X, there is a finite-rank linear operator T : X →X such
that ∥T∥⩽λ and ∥Txi −xi∥< ε for 1 ⩽i ⩽n. The space X has the BAP if it has the
λ-BAP for some λ ⩾1. The 1-BAP is called the metric approximation property or MAP.
Next, we recall some standard facts about free spaces. First, we will exploit the natural
identification Lip0(M) ≡F(M)∗throughout. Second, δ : M →F(M) is an isometric
embedding and δ(M \{0M}) is a linearly independent set. Third, given A ⊆M containing
0M, the universal property applied to the embedding of A into M yields a linear embedding
of F(A) into F(M), which is isometric by virtue of McShane’s extension theorem; this
means we can and do naturally identify F(A) with a subspace of F(M).

A Banach lattice X is said to have the λ-positive-BAP if the operators T in the definition
of λ-BAP can be chosen to be positive as well [24]. Free spaces enjoy the following weak
notion of positivity. An element m ∈F(M) is said to be positive, written m ⩾0, if

never Banach lattices with respect to the induced partial order, this idea is nevertheless

Problem 3.8. Let E0 be the equivalence relation on 2ω defined by xE0y if and only if
x(i) ̸= y(i) for finitely many i ∈ω. Does there exist a Borel map f : 2ω →R such that
xE0y if and only if f(x) ≈f(y)?
We remark that, as E0 has continuum many equivalance classes, a positive answer
to Problem 3.8 would imply that there is a family of separable metric spaces of size
continuum whose free spaces are pairwise non-isomorphic. Showing the existence of a
suitable family of this size is probably a necessary first step in any positive answer to
Problem 3.8. Currently, all we know (in ZFC) is that there are families of this kind that
are uncountable.
