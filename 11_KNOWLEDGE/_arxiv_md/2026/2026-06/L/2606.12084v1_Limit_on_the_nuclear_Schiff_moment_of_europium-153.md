Abstract
The Schiff moment of a nucleus is a symmetry-violating nuclear moment that indicates
new physics beyond the Standard Model. We place the limit, |𝒮(153Eu)| < 1.7 × 10−8 𝑒fm3
(95% confidence), on the Schiff moment of the 153Eu nucleus. The measurement of 𝒮(153Eu)
is obtained from nuclear spin resonance frequencies in two sub-ensembles of 153Eu3+ ions in
a Y2SiO5 crystal that act as mutual comagnetometers. This measurement using octupolar
nuclei in a mm-scale crystal constrains new physics at the TeV energy-scale.
The matter-antimatter imbalance of the Universe cannot be explained within the
Standard Model of particle physics [1]. This motivates the search for new physics
that breaks time-reversal (T) symmetry [2, 3]. New sources of T-violation that couple
to quarks and gluons in nuclei induce symmetry-violating nuclear moments, whose
existence can be detected by precision measurements of nuclear spin transitions [4–8].
One such quantity is the nuclear Schiff moment (NSM), an electrostatic moment that
is odd under parity (P) and T, and which can be generated by T-violation in nuclei with
spin 𝐼≥1/2 [9–11]. Larger NSMs are induced in octupolar nuclei than in spherical
nuclei [12–14].
Our measurements use 153Eu (𝐼= 5/2), chosen for its octupolar enhancement of
T-violation effects. The NSM of 153Eu interacts with the electrons in electrically-
polarized Eu3+ ions producing P,T-odd hyperfine energy shifts. The measurements
reported here were performed with Eu3+ ions that are statically polarized when doped
into non-centrosymmetric sites in the Y2SiO5 (YSO) crystal.

Calculation of 𝒮
We performed multi-reference covariant density functional theory (MR-CDFT) cal-
culations to evaluate the NSM of 153Eu [16], using a similar method as in Ref. [17].
We first carried out a self-consistent CDFT calculation to generate a set of mean-
field configurations with different quadrupole and octupole deformation parameters,
(𝛽2, 𝛽3), for 153Eu with the false quasiparticle vacuum approximation [18]. We then
used particle-number, parity and angular momentum projection to restore the miss-
ing quantum numbers (nucleon numbers 𝑁, 𝑍and spin-parity 𝐽𝜋) of the symmetry-
breaking states. We found the ground state 𝐽𝜋= 5/2+ to have (𝛽2, 𝛽3) ≈(0.40, 0.08),
indicating that it has a prolate quadrupole deformation with appreciable octupole
deformation. The excited state 𝐽𝜋= 5/2−(97.4 keV) was found to have (𝛽2, 𝛽3) ≈
(0.39, 0.075).
The wavefunctions of nuclear states were constructed by mixing the wavefunctions
of intrinsic states on the (𝛽2, 𝛽3) deformation plane, with the mixing weights de-
termined variationally. These wavefunctions incorporate beyond-mean-field corre-
lations associated with symmetry restoration, as well as both static and dynamical

Measurement of Ω
We measured the resonance frequencies 𝑓0(𝜌) for the 𝑏−¯𝑏transition, which occurs at
𝑓0(𝜌) ≈230.5 kHz in the static magnetic field B𝑥≈350 G applied to the Eu:YSO crys-
tal. Each measurement cycle consisted of a state-preparation step, radio-frequency
(rf) spectroscopy step, and state-detection step. State-preparation using optical pump-
ing, and state-detection using frequency-modulated optical absorption spectroscopy
were performed using a laser tuned to the 7𝐹0 −5𝐷0 optical transition [16]. The fre-
quency of the laser, 𝜈𝐿, was used to select a spectral class (∼1 MHz linewidth) out of
the inhomogeneously-broadened 7𝐹0 −5𝐷0 line (∼1 GHz linewidth).
A static electric field ®EDC = ±33 ˆ𝑥V/cm was applied across the crystal in order to
resolve the 𝜌= ±1 optical lines during the state-detection step. The sign of EDC was
reversed between consecutive measurements in order to cancel the effect of detuning-
dependent optical power during state-detection [16]. Note that EDC is only required
during the state-detection step, in order to distinguish the nuclear spin state popu-
lations for the 𝜌= ±1 sub-ensembles. The sign of EDC exchanges the frequencies of
the optical absorption lines corresponding to the 𝜌= ±1 sub-ensembles. The average
of sequential 𝑓𝑑measurements with ±EDC, denoted as ¯𝑓𝑑, is the principal quantity
measured in the experiment. This quantity ¯𝑓𝑑has the same sensitivity to Ω as in
Equation (5) but has reduced sensitivity to fluctuations in the frequencies of the opti-
cal absorption lines.

physics theories using octupolar nuclei in crystals.
