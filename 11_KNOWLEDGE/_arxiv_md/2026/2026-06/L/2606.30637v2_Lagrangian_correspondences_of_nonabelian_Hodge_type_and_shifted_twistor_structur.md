SHIFTED TWISTOR STRUCTURES
JACOB KRYCZKA, YUUJI TANAKA, AND SHING–TUNG YAU
Abstract. Classical nonabelian Hodge theory identifies Dolbeault and de Rham moduli spaces by providing
a real-analytic isomorphism. In this paper, motivated by the Kapustin–Witten theory, we study this corre-
spondence in the more general framework of perfect complexes on proper varieties, paying special attention to
the surface case. We establish a Lagrangian correspondence which relates the shifted symplectic geometries
by Pantev–To¨en–Vaqui´e–Vezzosi (PTVV) between the derived stacks of flat and Higgs perfect complexes.
Furthermore, we investigate the existence of the derived twistor structure of hyperk¨ahler type on the
moduli stack of perfect complexes endowed with λ-connections by Deligne–Hitchin–Simpson. We establish a
version of the AKSZ/PTVV transgression, Lagrangian intersection, and (hyperk¨ahler) symplectic reduction
theorems in this context. Moreover, we prove that the derived Riemann–Hilbert correspondence of Porta
and Holstein–Porta, which states an equivalence of derived analytic stacks of perfect complexes on XBetti
and XDR, is compatible with the natural shifted–symplectic structures.
We then study the relation between the shifted twistor structure and the shifted symplectic forms on the
fibers, and prove that the analytic Deligne–Hitchin–Simpson moduli stack on a smooth projective variety
X has a canonical 2(1 −dim X) shifted twistor structure over P1
C, a result which has been anticipated for
some time. In particular, the moduli stack of solutions to the Kapustin–Witten equations modulo gauge
equivalence on a smooth proper complex algebraic surface exibits a (−2)-shifted (pre-)twistor structure as a
family over P1
C.

1. Introduction
In this paper, we initiate the study of hyperk¨ahler structures in derived algebraic and analytic geometry.
1.1. Overview. Our main results can be summarized as follows:
1. We define derived analytic (pre)-twistor spaces and study their formal properties. Roughly speaking,
they are locally geometric relative derived C-analytic stacks endowed with the action of the Galois
group Gal(C/R) which encodes a real structure, together with a relative shifted symplectic structure
invariant for the action. This requires a detailed study of shifted symplectic structures on derived
analytic stacks, modelled as in [HP25] (see references therein). Using this formalism, we answer ques-
tions of Katzarkov–Pandit–Spaide [KPS21, Problems 6.5,6.6] on their interaction with fundamental
constructions in shifted symplectic geometry [PTVV13]:
• We prove three new existence results for shifted twistor families which are of use in constructing
new examples of such hyperk¨ahler manifolds. We prove twistor structures are compatible with:
the derived Alexandrov–Kontsevich–Schwarz–Zaboronsky (AKSZ) construction (Thm. 1.13),
forming derived (hyper-)Lagrangian intersections (Thm. 1.15) and derived symplectic reduc-
tion1 along connected complex analytic reductive groups G (Thm. 1.16).
• We study these structures in relation to various moduli problems coming from nonabelian Hodge
theory and its relation with Kapustin–Witten gauge theory explored by the second author with
Rayan and Liu [LRT22].
2. We apply the shifted twistor formalism to study the Hodge stack of perfect complexes with λ-
connections on a smooth projective complex variety X with parameter λ ∈A1, introduced by Simpson
[Sim09]. Recall the derived stack of perfect complexes is the mapping stack

1.2. Background. Every hyperk¨ahler manifold is holomorphic symplectic; conversely, by the Calabi–Yau
theorem [Yau78] any compact holomorphic symplectic manifold admits a hyperk¨ahler metric in each K¨ahler
class. This deep correspondence connects differential-geometric extremal problems to algebro-geometric and
categorical stability properties [Don83, Don85, Don87, UY86]. It plays a central role in Mirror Symmetry
[Kon94, SYZ96] and for Bridgeland stability [Bri07],[BLM+21].
Compared with holomorphic symplectic manifolds, examples of hyperk¨ahler manifolds are generally harder
to come by; examples are Hilbert–Douady schemes S[n] of n-points on a K3 surface S and generalized Kummer
varieties associated to abelian surfaces [Bea83], the Kirwan blowup of moduli of semistable torsion-free sheaves
of rank two M(2,0,4)(S) with c1 = 0, c2 = 4 on a K3 surface S [O’G99] (or abelian surface [O’G03]).
Therefore it is important to find methods to discover new examples or deduce when a smooth projective
variety is hyperk¨ahler or not. As an example of the latter, if X and Y are derived equivalent smooth projective
varieties then X is hyperk¨ahler if and only if Y is hyperk¨ahler [HNW11].
While holomorphic symplectic geometry has seen substantial advancements via the theory of shifted sym-
plectic and Poisson structures on derived Artin stacks [PTVV13], later extended to the relative [CHS25] and
non-geometric settings [CS26], there is at present no satisfactory derived analogue of a hyperk¨ahler structure,
despite its central role in moduli theory.
In the present paper, we take up the fundamental question:
Question 1.3. What is the appropriate notion of a hyperk¨ahler structure on a derived stack?
Motivated in part by the relationship between Kapustin–Witten gauge theory [KW07] and nonabelian
Hodge theory explored in [LRT22], we propose an approach of both an algebraic and analytic nature to con-
struct hyperk¨ahler structures on derived stacks in terms of their twistor-theoretic description, summarized
in § 1.4. Employing well-known results in derived symplectic geometry, such as the AKSZ/PTVV transgres-
sion and derived Lagrangian intersection theorems [PTVV13, CPT+17], we can construct new hyperk¨ahler
structures with relative ease compared to classical approaches.
We now briefly recall the classical nonabelian Hodge (NAH) and Riemann–Hilbert (RH) correspondences,
together with the hyperk¨ahler geometry of the associated Deligne–Hitchin–Simpson twistor family; the pri-
mary objective of this paper is to study these structures from the derived analytic viewpoint.
1.3. Recollections. Let G be a reductive algebraic group and let Σ be a compact Riemann surface. The
moduli space Mss

natural hyperk¨ahler metric defined by the Hitchin equations [Fuj91], whose symplectic geometry is governed

semiflat metric [Fre99]. However, classically, the hyperk¨ahler metrics are generally defined only over smooth
loci, with the full moduli space (highly) singular and non-compact, particularly in higher dimensions. Ex-
tending the metric near singularities e.g. in the large Higgs field limit, leads to substantial analytic difficulties
[GMN13], such as the failure of metric perturbation methods to achieve transversality.

From the viewpoint of derived geometry, the moduli stack of perfect complexes of local systems on compact
oriented four-manifolds carries a natural (−2)-shifted symplectic structure. This is viewed as corresponding
to the t ̸= 0 regime of Kapustin–Witten gauge theory and suggests the full moduli space of solutions should
admit a uniform shifted symplectic derived enhancement. Indeed, when the underlying four-manifold is a
complex projective surface, such a structure is known to exist, and the orientation problem for this moduli
space at t = 0 has been resolved [JTU20, Thm. 4.9], with the same conclusion extending to t ̸= 0.
1.3.1. This paper. In this paper, we propose replacing classical analytic methods used e.g. in proving ex-
istence of harmonic metrics, with cohomological tools using ideas of derived algebraic [TV10] and ana-
lytic geometry [Lur11a]. In this way, we recast nonlinear elliptic PDE problems in formal and categorical
terms. This turns out to be fruitful and necessary for a number of other PDE-related problems (see e.g.
[Par24],[Ste24],[KSY23],[KSY24],[PT24],[HPT24]).
In fact, such a perspective is already implicit in observations of Deligne, Hitchin [HKLR87] and Simpson.
Namely, the twistor construction encodes the nonabelian Hodge correspondence in a purely geometric manner,
bypassing much of the analytic input3.
As discussed in § 1.1 above, our approach is a formal one based on Simpson’s shape operations (see [PS20]),
which have favorable functorial properties extending to various ∞-toposes of derived stacks [Lur09]. Thanks
to the series of works due to Holstein, Porta, Sala, Yu, and others e.g. [PY16, PY19, PY20, HP25], many
properties of these operations are well understood, and their behaviour in relation with analytification is
known for large classes of geometric objects useful for applications e.g. locally geometric derived algebraic
stacks. This simplifies our situation greatly, since by working with Perf, many properties can be transported
between algebraic and analytic settings, subject to some other natural properties. This abstract approach
permits the extension of hyperk¨ahler metrics beyond smooth loci to the full singular moduli stack, when
treated as an appropriately derived object in the language of stable ∞-categories [Lur12].

1.4. Hyperk¨ahler structures via shifted twistor spaces. The starting point is the classical fact that a
hyperk¨ahler structure can be encoded in purely holomorphic data via Penrose’s twistor geometry [HKLR87,
Thm. 3.3]. In the classical picture, a hyperk¨ahler manifold M gives rise to a holomorphic fibration π : Z →
P1, whose fibers parametrize the different complex structures on M. Moreover, Z carries a holomorphic
symplectic structure varying quadratically along P1, and a real structure covering the antipodal map.
Our goal is to extend this paradigm to derived geometry.
Instead of working with smooth manifolds
and classical moduli spaces (such as the Deligne–Hitchin space), we consider derived stacks and encode hy-
perk¨ahler structures entirely in terms of their twistor geometry, following and extending ideas4 of Katzarkov–
Pandit–Spaide [KPS21].
In this approach, one replaces metric data by holomorphic and homotopical structures, as we now explain
in further detail.

1.6. Motivation from Gauge theory. In prior work [LRT22], the second author studied nonlinear PDE

equations naturally fit into the framework of nonabelian Hodge theory [Sim90, Sim92, Sim97b]. This ulti-
mately relies on Simpson’s extension of the Uhlenbeck–Yau continuity method [UY86], showing that a Higgs
bundle admits a Hermitian–Yang–Mills metric if and only if it is polystable [Sim88].
Over a curve C, this relationship interacts with the hyperk¨ahler structure Mss

transformation selects distinguished branes that are either symplectic or complex with regards to each complex
structure in the moduli space that are transformed under mirror symmetry, which itself is a form of global
Langlands duality, discussed in detail by Donagi–Pantev [DP12].
In higher dimensions these moduli spaces become highly singular, making them natural objects for shifted
symplectic derived geometry [PTVV13, CPT+17, CHS25]; their shifted analogues exist, recalled in Sub-
section. 3.4.2. A significant goal and formadible open problem is to understand the derived enhancements
of the moduli spaces underlying the Corlette–Donaldson–Hitchin–Uhlenbeck–Yau–Simpson correspondence
[Cor88, Cor92] and the natural structures they possess in gauge theory and complex geometry.
Note that there is another topological twist by Vafa and Witten [VW94]. For this twist, one expects
that the generating series of invariants defined through the moduli spaces of solutions to the gauge-theoretic
equations arising from the twist may exhibit modular properties reflecting S-duality, which N = 4 super
Yang–Mills theory in four dimensions would have. For projective surfaces, Thomas and the second author of
this paper [TT20, TT17] gave a mathematical definition of these invariants using moduli spaces of semistable
Higgs bundles, via a Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau type theorem for this system [ACGP03, Tan15]. Vafa and
Witten [VW94] also discussed that the blowup formula in this twist should possess modular properties, which
ought to be explained in terms of conformal field theory. On the mathematical side, Kuhn, Leigh, and the
second author of this paper [KLT22] obtained exactly the same modular forms predicted by Vafa and Witten
for the instanton part of the Vafa–Witten invariants, by using more general blowup formulas for enumerative
invariants on projective surfaces by Kuhn and the second author [KT21]. We do not pursue this direction
here; instead, our focus is on the comparison and relationship between the moduli spaces arising from the
Kapustin–Witten theory and their Derived enhancements.
1.7. Gauge-theoretic moduli spaces in the Kapustin–Witten theory. Derived algebraic geometry
offers a powerful alternative approach that circumvents certain analytic difficulties in gauge theory, such as
the failure of standard metric perturbation methods to obtain the transversality of the moduli spaces in
analytic settings. On one hand, the moduli stack of perfect complexes of local systems on a compact oriented
topological four-manifold carries a canonical (−2)-shifted symplectic structure [PTVV13], which corresponds
to the t ̸= 0 side of Kapustin—Witten theory. Therefore, it is natural to expect that the moduli spaces of
solutions to the Kapustin–Witten equations for t ̸= 0 would carry a (−2)-shifted symplectic structure, as they
do when the underlying four-manifold is a complex projective surface. On the other hand, the orientation
problem for the moduli space of solutions to the Kapustin–Witten equations at t = 0 was solved in [JTU20,
Thm. 4.9], and the same conclusion extends to the t ̸= 0 case.
Derived algebraic geometry has been used to describe moduli spaces arising from topological twists of
N = 4 super Yang–Mills theory. Elliott–Yoo [EY18] describe the geometric quantization of the derived stack
assigned to C ×C for each twist, where C is a compact Riemann surface. In their sequel [EY25], they further
explain the role of these moduli spaces in the (quantum) categorical geometric Langlands programme (see
Subsection 1.8.3).
Two natural problems arise: (a) understand the deformation spaces of solutions to the Kapustin–Witten
equations for both t = 0 and t ̸= 0 on a closed four-manifold in an appropriate sense and to compare these
moduli spaces to obtain a certain form of correspondence predicted by nonabelian Hodge theory, (b) define,
study and ultimately clarify the hyperk¨ahler geometry arising in this context.
