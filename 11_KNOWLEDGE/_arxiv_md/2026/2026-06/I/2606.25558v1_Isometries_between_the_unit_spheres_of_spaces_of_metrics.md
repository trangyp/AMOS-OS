ISOMETRIES BETWEEN THE UNIT SPHERES OF SPACES OF METRICS
KATSUHISA KOSHINO
Abstract. Given a topological space Z, let PM(Z) be the space of bounded continuous pseudo-
metrics on Z, which is endowed with the sup-norm, and let SPM(Z) be the unit sphere of PM(Z). In
this paper, we shall prove that for all non-degenerate compact metrizable spaces X and Y , and for
any surjective isometry T : SPM(X) →SPM(Y ), there exists a homeomorphism ϕ : Y →X such
that for any metric d ∈SPM(X) and for any pair of points (x, y) ∈Y 2, T(d)(x, y) = d(ϕ(x), ϕ(y)).
As a corollary, we can solve a variant of Tingley’s problem on spaces of metics.
1. Introduction
Tingley’s problem [18], which was conjectured in 1987, is one of the most significant questions in
functional analysis. This asks whether a surjective isometry between the unit spheres of two Banach
spaces can be extended to a real-linear surjective isometry over the whole spaces, refer to [15] and
[6, Introduction] on the recent developments. T. Banakh [2] solved the problem for 2-dimensional
Banach spaces affirmatively. Although this question remains open in general Banach spaces, it has
been solved for specific function spaces, for instance, spaces of continuous functions on topological
spaces, which traces back to the result due to R. Wang [20]. In this paper, we shall solve a variant
of Tingley’s problem on function spaces consisting of metrics on metrizable spaces. These spaces do
not admit the standard algebraic techniques applied in the ones of continuous functions. Indeed,
they lack the unit element and are not closed under multiplication by Urysohn’s maps, which
frequently play key roles in solving Tingley’s Problems.
Furthermore, the multivariate nature
and the symmetry of metrics prevents the simple peaking structures like the case of univariate
functions.
The Banach-Stone theorem [1, 17] is one of the most fundamental results in the study of isome-
tries between function spaces (cf. [5] as a historical note). Throughout the paper, an isometry
means a surjective isometry. Given a topological space Z, assume that C(Z) = (C(Z), ∥· ∥) is the
Banach space consisting of real-valued bounded continuous functions on Z with the sup-norm. Let
PM(Z) ⊂C(Z2) be the subspace of pseudometrics on Z, and let AM(Z) ⊂PM(Z) be the subspace
of admissible metrics, that is, metrics compatible with the original topology on Z. Moreover, put
M(Z) be the subspaces Pc(Z) or Pp(Z) of admissible metrics defined as follows:
• For every d ∈Pc(Z), there are a doubleton {z, w} ⊂Z and a compact set K ⊂Z such
that d(z, w) = ∥d∥and if d(x, y) = ∥d∥, then x, y ∈K.
• For each d ∈Pp(Z), there only exists {z, w} ⊂Z such that d(z, w) = ∥d∥.
If Z is compact, then AM(Z) = Pc(Z). The author [12] (cf. [16]) showed a Banach-Stone type
theorem on spaces of metrics as follows:

2. Preliminaries
In this section, we introduce some basic properties of spaces of metrics, which will be used
later.
The readers can refer to [10, 11, 9] for the topological and metric structures of spaces
consisting of metrics. On isometries between spaces of metrics, the author [13] proved a variant of
the Banach-Stone theorem on spaces consisting of uniformly continuous and lipschitz continuous
metrics besides [12]. For a metrizable space Z, AM(Z) is dense in PM(Z) (cf. [10, Proposition 5]).
Similarly, we have the following:
Proposition 2.1. For every metrizable space Z, SPM(Z) ∩AM(Z) is dense in SPM(Z).

5. Proof of Main Theorem
Now we are in a position to show Main Theorem and Corollary 1.2.
Proof of Main Theorem. The formula (∗) follows from Propositions 4.2 and 4.3 directly. In the
case that the cardinality of X and Y is greater than 2, we can show the uniqueness of ϕ easily, see
the proof of Main Theorem in [12]. We complete the proof. □
Proof of Corollary 1.2. Remark that for any metrizable space Z, SPM(Z) is a complete metric
space and SPM(Z) ∩AM(Z) is dense in SPM(Z) by Proposition 2.1. Hence each isometry T :
SPM(X) ∩AM(X) →SPM(Y ) ∩AM(Y ) can be extended to an isometry between SPM(X) and
SPM(Y ). By virtue of Main Theorem, for every isometry T : SPM(X) →SPM(Y ), there exists
a homeomorphism ϕ : Y →X such that T(d)(x, y) = d(ϕ(x), ϕ(y)) for all pseudometrics d ∈
SPM(X) and all points x, y ∈Y . Then using the homeomorphism ϕ, we can define T : PM(X) →
PM(Y ) by T(d)(x, y) = d(ϕ(x), ϕ(y)). It is obvious that T is an extension of T, which is real-linear.
The uniqueness of T follows form the linearity of T. Moreover, by the same argument as the proof
of the implication (1) ⇒(2) in [12, Main Theorem], T is an isometry. The proof is completed. □
