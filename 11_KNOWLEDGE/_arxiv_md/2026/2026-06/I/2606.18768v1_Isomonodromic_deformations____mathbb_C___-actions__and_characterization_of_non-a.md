ISOMONODROMIC DEFORMATIONS, C∗-ACTIONS, AND
CHARACTERIZATION OF NON-ABELIAN NOETHER-LEFSCHETZ LOCI
ON DOLBEAULT MODULI SPACES
TIANZHI HU, RUIRAN SUN, JINBANG YANG, AND KANG ZUO
Abstract. Let f : X →S be a smooth proper family of smooth projective varieties, and
let σDol : S →MDol(X/S) be the real analytic family of Higgs bundles obtained from an
isomonodromic deformation via the relative non-abelian Hodge correspondence. We study the
interaction between isomonodromic deformation and the natural C∗-action on Dolbeault moduli
spaces. For λ ∈S1, we prove that, on any complex analytic subvariety U ⊂S, the rescaled
family λ · σDol|U is again isomonodromic if σDol|U is holomorphic. Conversely, we prove that
σDol|U must be holomorphic if there exists λ ∈S1\{±1} such that λ · σDol|U is isomonodromic.
The proof is based on the study of real analytic deformations of Higgs bundles and the variation
of harmonic metrics.
As an application, we give a simplified proof of a local characterization of Simpson’s non-
abelian Noether–Lefschetz locus firstly proved in [12, Theorem 1.2].
Namely, if the initial
local system underlies a polarized complex variation of Hodge structures, then the non-abelian
Noether–Lefschetz locus is precisely the maximal complex analytic subvariety of S on which the
real analytic section σDol becomes holomorphic. This gives an affirmative answer to a question
of Esnault and Kerz.

1. Introduction
In our recent paper [12], we used higher-order deformation theory of Higgs bundles to give a
local characterization of the non-abelian Noether–Lefschetz locus in [12, Theorem 1.2], but that
involved complicated computations of higher-order classes. In discussions with H´el`ene Esnault
and Moritz Kerz on [12], we realized that first order deformation theory suffices for a simpler
proof. Kerz’s Question 1.1 below is central here; it probes the commutativity of isomonodromic
deformations with C∗-actions. Using first order deformation theory and harmonic metrics, we
obtain partial answers to this question, for instance Theorem 1.3 , which then yields a simple
proof of [12, Theorem 1.2]; see Theorem 1.5.

1.2. Characterizations of non-abelian Noether-Lefschetz locus. The classical Noether–
Lefschetz theorem concerns the variation of algebraic cycles in a family of smooth projective va-
rieties. For instance, if X →B is a family of smooth hypersurfaces in P3, the Noether–Lefschetz
locus consists of those points t ∈B for which the Picard rank of Xt jumps. Equivalently, it is
the locus where some flat cohomology class remains of Hodge type (1, 1). More generally, for a
polarized Q-variation of Hodge structures (V, F •, ∇, Q) of even weight 2k over B, and for a flat
(multi-valued) section γ with γ(0) ∈F kV := F k(V ⊗OB), the Hodge locus is
HLγ := { t ∈B | γ(t) ∈F kV }.

(1) The first one is a global criterion, known as Deligne-Griffiths fixed part theorem: let γ

monodromy orbit of γ is finite, then γ is always of type (k, k).
(2) The second one is a local study, stating that its Zariski tangent space is controlled by
the Higgs field: (cf. [24, Section 5.3.2.])

A related question is: when X/S is the universal curve C/Tg over the Teichm¨uller space, can
