INCOMPRESSIBLE PLANAR SURFACES IN HYPERBOLIC LINK EXTERIORS IN THE
3-SPHERE
LUIS G. VALDEZ-SÁNCHEZ
ABSTRACT. For each integer N ≥3 we construct examples of N-component hyperbolic links L ⊂S3 whose
exterior contains an incompressible spanning planar surface P ⊂XL with one boundary component on each
boundary torus of XL of nonmeridional and nonintegral slope, thus providing counterexamples to a recent
conjecture of M. Eudave-Muñoz and M. Ozawa.
The case N = 3 is the crucial one to consider: all such link pairs (L,P) are classified and found to be
generated by the structure of the exterior of hyperbolic Eudave-Muñoz knots. More generally, necessary and
sufficient conditions on integers p1, p2, p3 ≥2 are given for the existence of a 3-component link in S3 whose
exterior contains a spanning pants with boundary slopes of the form ai/pi.
A key role in the analysis of 3-component link pairs is played by the properties of the embeddings of
three mutually disjoint and nonparallel primitive circles on the boundary of a genus two handlebody. These are
classified in general and in the special case when the handlebody is part of a genus two Heegaard decomposition
of S3 associated with a 3-component link pair.
The hyperbolic links with N ≥4 components whose exterior contains a spanning planar surface with non-
meridional and nonintegral boundary slopes are constructed via an inductive process that starts with any of the
classified 3-component hyperbolic link pairs.
1. INTRODUCTION
Let L = K1 ⊔K2 ⊔···⊔KN ⊂S3 be an N ≥1 component link with exterior XL = S3 \int N(L). A properly
embedded surface F ⊂XL is essential if it is geometrically incompressible and not parallel to any component
of ∂XL. The exterior of the surface F is the manifold X(F) = cl[XL \N(F)] ⊂XL.
The isotopy class of a circle on ∂XL is the slope of the circle. We use standard meridian-longitude
coordinates on each torus component of ∂XL to represent the slope ri ⊂∂N(Ki) of the boundary circles
F ∩∂N(Ki) in the form ri = ai/pi for some integers ai and pi ≥0. The surface F ⊂XL has large boundary
slopes if pi ≥2 for each i with F ∩∂N(Ki) ̸= /0, that is, if the boundary slopes of F are all nonmeridional
and nonintegral.
The surface F ⊂XL is a spanning surface if it has one boundary component on each component of
∂XL. Following [6], we say that a spanning planar surface P ⊂XL is of type X0(p1, p2,..., pN), and write
P = X0(p1, p2,..., pN) ⊂XL, if for each 1 ≤i ≤N the slope of the boundary circle ri = ∂P∩∂N(Ki) ⊂∂P
is of the form ri = ai/pi for some integers pi ≥0. There are disjoint copies of the boundary slopes of F on
the boundary surface ∂X(F) of the exterior of F, as represented in Fig. 18 in the case where F is a spanning
pants.

A link pair (L,P) consisting of a link L ⊂S3 and a spanning planar surface P ⊂XL is minimal if no proper

In [6], M. Eudave-Munoz and M. Ozawa proposed the following conjecture:
Conjecture 1.1: There does not exist an essential n-punctured sphere with non-meridional, non-integral
boundary slopes in a hyperbolic link exterior in the 3-sphere.
Evidence in support of this conjecture is discussed in [6]. For instance, in the case of 1-component links,
it is a consequence of [9] that any essential planar surface in a hyperbolic knot exterior must have meridional
or integral boundary slope.
In this paper we present counterexamples to Conjecture 1.1. Specifically, we construct hyperbolic links
of N ≥3 components that contain essential spanning planar surfaces with large boundary slopes.

found in Section 2.3.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 contains the notation, definitions and various constructions
we use throughout. Primitive, power and Seifert circles on the boundary of a genus two handlebody are
introduced and their properties used to further analyze the structure of the exterior of a hyperbolic Eudave-
Muñoz knot K ⊂S3 as presented in the work of C. Gordon and J. Luecke [11]. In particular, using the results
in [11], it is proved in Lemma 2.3 that the Seifert circle r1 ⊂∂H in item (T3)(i) above splits in H: that is,
there is a nontrivial separating disk in H which intersects the circle r1 ⊂∂H transversely and minimally in
2 points. It is this property of the slope r1 that makes possible the construction in §2.1.9 of the core circles
K2 ⊔K3 ⊂H indicated in (T3)(ii) and of the collection of link pairs (L,P) in §2.3.
Section 3 presents the general properties of 3-component links L in S3 whose exterior XL contains a
spanning planar surface P with large boundary slopes. These properties are then used to give a complete set
of conditions in Lemma 3.2 for such a link to be hyperbolic, which include that the exterior X(P) ⊂XL be a
genus two handlebody.
Beyond Lemma 3.2, the proof of Theorems 1.1 in Section 3.2 requires the following technical facts: (A)
the uniqueness, up to isotopy, of the incompressible twice punctured torus in the exterior of a hyperbolic

of Section 6, and (B) the classification of sets of 3 mutually disjoint and nonparallel primitive circles on

2. PRELIMINARIES
For definitions of basic concepts in 3-manifold topology, knot theory and combinatorial group theory see
[12], [13], [14], [17]. We work in the PL category.
The union of disjoint sets A,B is denoted by A ⊔B. For a set or topological space A, |A| denotes the
cardinality or the number of connected components of A.
Unless otherwise stated, we assume that manifolds are compact and orientable and submanifolds are
properly embedded in the ambient manifold. For a manifold M and a subset A ⊂M, cl(A), int(A) and
N(A) denote the closure, interior and regular neighborhood in M of the subset A, respectively. The frontier
of a codimension zero submanifold A ⊂M is the submanifold fr(A) = cl[∂N(A) \ ∂M]; thus fr(A) is a
codimension 1 submanifold properly embedded in M.
We refer to the closure in M of a component of M \A as a closed component of M \A.
Let S be a surface. A 1-submanifold Γ ⊂S is nontrivial if no component of Γ bounds a disk in S or is
parallel to ∂S.
Let M be a 3-manifold and S ⊂M a surface. We denote the boundary components of S by ∂1S,∂2S,..., so
that ∂S = ∂1S ⊔∂2S ⊔···. A disk D ⊂M is a compression disk for S if D∩S = ∂D and ∂D does not bound

is an arc in ∂M which does not cobound a disk with ∂S in ∂M. The surface S is compressible or boundary
compressible in M if it admits a compression or boundary compression disk, respectively, and otherwise it
is incompressible or boundary incompressible.

Two disjoint and connected codimension 1 submanifolds A,B in a manifold M are parallel if some closed
component of M\(A⊔B) is homeomorphic to A×[0,1], with A and B corresponding to A×{0} and A×{1},

A surface in M other than a 2-sphere is essential if it is incompressible and not parallel into ∂M; notice
that the essential surface may be boundary compressible. A 2-sphere is essential in M if it does not bound a

For Γ a closed 1-submanifold of ∂M, M(Γ) denotes the 3-manifold obtained by adding a 2-handle to M
along each of the components of Γ and closing any resulting 2-sphere boundary components with 3-balls.
For a surface S ⊂M with each component of ∂S ⊂∂M having the same slope as some component of Γ, we

The intersection between two submanifolds is said to be minimal if, when compared to the intersections
between all their isotopic copies, the submanifolds intersect transversely in as few components as possible.
In particular, if two surfaces F and G in a 3-manifold M intersect minimally, we may assume that ∂F and
∂G intersect minimally in ∂M.
A 3-manifold M is toroidal or annular if, respectively, there is an essential torus or annulus in M; oth-
erwise, M is atoroidal or anannular. By the work of W. Thurston [19], a compact 3-manifold with each
boundary component a torus is hyperbolic (ie, its interior admits a complete hyperbolic metric structure of
constant negative curvature) iff the manifold is irreducible, boundary irreducible (ie, its boundary compo-
nents are incompressible), atoroidal and anannular.
For F a compact orientable surface, typically a disk D2, an annulus A2 or a 2-sphere S2, the notation
F(p,q,...), or simply F(∗,∗,···), is used to represent a Seifert fiber space over F with singular fibers of
indices p,q,··· ≥2.
In particular, for p ≥2 a manifold of the form Lp = S2(p) is a lens space; S3 = S2(1) and S2 ×S1 = S2(0)
are not considered lens spaces.
Let K0 ⊂S3 be a knot with regular neighborhood N(K0) ⊂S3. A circle K ⊂∂N(K0) of slope p/q with
q ≥2 is a (p,q)-cable of K0, or simply a q-cable of K0. In the special case that K0 is the trivial knot, K is
a torus knot of type (p,q) if |p|,q ≥2, hence a nontrivial knot; this is the case iff XK = D2(|p|,|q|). For

2.1.2. Primitive and power circles. A circle γ ⊂∂H is a primitive circle in H if the word γ(x,y) ∈π1(H) is
primitive, or, equivalently, if there is a disk in H which intersects γ minimally in one point.
The circle γ is a power circle in H if the word γ(x,y) ∈π1(H) is a nontrivial power of some nontrivial
element of π1(H).
A primitive or power circle γ ⊂∂H is nonseparating in ∂H. In fact, a circle γ ⊂∂H is a primitive or
power circle in H iff ∂H \ γ compresses in H, in which case there is a unique nonseparating compression
disk D ⊂H for ∂H \ γ. If V ⊂H is the solid torus closed component of H \ D, then γ homologically runs
p ≥1 times around V, where p = 1 if γ is a primitive circle and p ≥2 if γ is a power circle. In particular,
if γ′ ⊂H is a core of the solid torus V ⊂H then, in π1(H) = ⟨x,y | −⟩, γ′(x,y) is a primitive word and
γ(x,y) = [γ′(x,y)]p. If p ≥2 then we say that γ is a p-power circle in H.

each annulus A1,A2 is nonseparating in H, which by §2.1.4 implies that the slope of γ1 is primitive
or a power in H.

trivial circle in A. This implies that γ0 bounds a disk in H, contradicting the hypothesis.
Therefore the lemma follows.
