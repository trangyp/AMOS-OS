IMPROVED UNIRATIONALITY FOR GL-VARIETIES
ARTHUR BIK, JAN DRAISMA, ROB EGGERMONT, AND ANDREW SNOWDEN
Abstract. A GL-variety is a typically infinite dimensional variety equipped with a suitable
action of the infinite general linear group GL. In earlier work, we established the unira-
tionality theorem: an irreducible GL-variety admits a dominant map from a particularly
simple GL-variety, namely, the product of an irreducible finite-dimensional variety with
trivial GL-action and an infinite-dimensional affine space on which GL acts linearly. The
main result of this paper states that this map can in fact be constructed to be surjective
rather than merely dominant. An immediate application is that secant varieties to vari-
eties of tensors, which are typically constructed as image closures of certain GL-equivariant
maps, are in fact also images of (more complicated) GL-equivariant maps. We derive several
consequences of this improved unirationality theorem.

1. Introduction
1.1. Overview. Fix an algebraically closed field K of characteristic 0. A GL-variety is a
(typically infinite dimensional) affine variety over K equipped with an action of the infinite
general linear group GL, such that the coordinate ring is a GL-finitely generated GL-
algebra; see §1.3 and §2 for details. In [BDES1], we introduced this class of varieties and
proved several fundamental results about them, and in [BDES2], we proved some deeper
results. This paper builds on these previous two papers.
One of the important results from [BDES1] is the unirationality theorem: if X is an
irreducible GL-variety, then there is a dominant GL-equivariant map φ: B × Aλ →X,
where B is an irreducible finite-dimensional variety with trivial GL-action and Aλ is an
affine space on which GL acts linearly. Here λ is a finite tuple of permutations that encodes
the action of GL; see §1.3.

In what follows, we fix an
irreducible GL-variety X.
Theorem A (Strong unirationality theorem). There is a surjective map of GL-varieties
φ: B × Aµ →X for some irreducible affine variety B and some tuple µ.

first, simple consequence concerns curves (see §1.6 for our definition of curve).
Theorem B (Existence of curves). Let x and y be K-points of X. Then there exists an
irreducible curve C over K and a map j : C →X of K-schemes such that x, y ∈im(j).

1.5. Sketch of proof. We make a few comments on the proof of Theorem A. Let X be
an irreducible GL-variety, and choose a closed embedding X ⊂Aλ. By the unirationality
theorem proved in [BDES1], there is a dominant map φ: B × Aµ →X with B a finite
dimensional irreducible variety. For simplicity, we assume here that B is a point, and thus
omit it. The proof has three main steps.
(a) Enlarging the image. Suppose there is a K-point point x ∈X that is not in the image
of φ. By the results of [BDES2], we can realize x as a limit of a 1-parameter family with
bounded denominators. Concretely, this means that there are K-points v−n, . . . , vm of Aµ
such that
x = lim
t→0 φ(v−nt−n + · · · + tmvm).
Let s = n + m + 1, and let As·µ denote the space (Aµ)s. For w ∈As·µ, let pw(t) be the
Laurent polynomial w−nt−n +· · ·+tmwm. The limit φ(pw(t))) as t →0 converges if and only
if the coefficients of negative powers of t vanish. This is a system of polynomial equations

2. Background on GL-varieties
In this section, we recall the basic definitions surrounding GL-varieties, and also prove
some simple new results relevant to the current paper. We refer to [BDES1] and [BDES2]
for detailed background material on GL-varieties.
2.1. GL-varieties. A GL-algebra R is a commutative K-algebra equipped with an action of
GL via automorphisms that turns it into a polynomial representation of GL, i.e., a subquo-
tient of a (typically infinite) direct sum of tensor powers of V. By standard representation
theory, it is then a direct sum of representations of the form Sλ(V).
We say that R is
GL-finitely generated if it is generated by the GL-orbits of finitely many elements; it is
then a quotient of GL-algebra of the form Nr
i=1 Sym(Sλi(V)) for some tuple λ. An affine
GL-variety is the spectrum of a reduced, GL-finitely generated GL-algebra. Equivalently,
it is a GL-stable closed and reduced subscheme of some Aλ. A quasi-affine GL-variety is
GL-stable open subscheme of some GL-variety. A morphism (or, informally, just map) of
GL-varieties is a GL-equivariant morphism of schemes.
2.2. GL-varieties as functors. Let M be a polynomial representation of GL. Then M
admits a canonical direct sum decomposition L
λ Mλ⊗Sλ(V) where the sum is over partitions
and Mλ is a multiplicitiy space. For a vector space V , we define M{V } to be L
λ Mλ⊗Sλ(V ).
In this way, V 7→M{V } is a functor of the vector space V . We use curly braces here to
avoid confusion with functor of points in the geometric context.
Suppose A is a GL-algebra. Then V 7→A{V } is a functor to the category of K-algebras.
In particular, the natural inclusion Kn →V gives an inclusion of algebras A{Kn} →A{V },
while the natural surjection V →Kn yields a surjection of algebras A →A{Kn}. If A is
GL-finitely generated then each A{Kn} is finitely generated.
Dually, an affine GL-variety X gives a contravariant functor from finite-dimensional vec-
tor spaces to varieties; we write X{V } for the evaluation of that functor at V . We have
morphisms of schemes from X to X{Kn} and vice versa, and the former is a left inverse of
the latter. We note that a quasi-affine GL-variety does not yield such a functor.

2.3. The central grading. A GL-algebra R has a natural Z≥0-grading, called the central
grading, in which f ∈R has degree d if (t · idn)f = tdf for all t ∈K∗and all n ≫0. In this
grading, the elements of Vλ have degree |λ|. If X is an affine GL-variety, then the central
grading of its coordinate ring K[X] gives rise to an action of the multiplicative group Gm
on X, called the central Gm-action. Every morphism of affine GL-varieties is equivariant
with respect to the central Gm-action. We write X0 for the spectrum of the degree-0 part
of K[X]; this is a variety isomorphic to X{K0}.
We write K((t)) for the field of Laurent series over K. The central action on X yields an
action of (Z, +) on K((t))-valued points of X. Indeed, a K((t))-point of X corresponds to a K-
algebra homomorphism f : K[X] →K((t)), and we let n ∈Z send f to the homomorphism,
denoted tn⋆f, that maps a homogeneous element r ∈K[X] of degree d in the central grading
to tdnf(r). Note that tm ⋆(tn ⋆f) = tm+n ⋆f as desired.

6.1. The main result. For a tuple λ, let ∥λ∥be the maximum size of a partition in λ.

minimal d for which there is a closed immersion X →Aλ for some tuple λ with ∥λ∥= d.
