INFINITE ECH CAPACITIES AND ANOSOV FLOWS
GABRIEL BEINER
Abstract. This article relates the theory of embedded contact homology (ECH)
with the dynamics of Anosov flows. We show that in many cases the ECH capacities
of a symplectic 4-manifold are infinite, including cotangent disk bundles over closed
oriented surfaces of genus at least two. We prove that ECH obstructs Reeb Anosov
and Hamiltonian Anosov flows, addressing the four-dimensional case of a question
posed by Herman in 1998. Further, we obtain Floer-theoretic obstructions to a 3-
manifold admitting any Anosov flow.
As an application, we give new constraints
on the existence of embedded Lagrangians of genus at least two in symplectic 4-
manifolds.
In an appendix, some related results in all dimensions are proved for
capacities constructed from rational symplectic field theory.

The ECH capacities give many powerful insights into four-dimensional symplectic

This paper concerns some relations between ECH capacities and the theory of Anosov
flows. More precisely, we will show that finiteness of the ECH capacities obstructs the
existence of Anosov flows in various settings. This will in turn allow us to determine
that the ECH capacities are infinite in many new cases.
1.1. Statement of main results. In order to study symplectic embeddings and dy-
namics in dimension four, it is an important problem to compute the ECH capacities
for a given Liouville domain. Examples where the capacities have been determined
include convex and concave toric domains [Hut11, CCG+], and the unit cotangent disk
bundles of S2 [FR22, FRV], RP 2 [FR22], T 2 [Hut11], and the Klein bottle [MR25] with
respect to standard metrics. In general, it may be difficult to even determine if the ECH
capacities are finite—this has been an open question for the cotangent disk bundles of
all higher genus surfaces. Our first main result concerns a simple condition under which
all ECH capacities must be infinite; in particular, it implies the capacities are infinite
for all cotangent disk bundles over oriented surfaces of genus at least two. We begin by
recalling some standard terminology.
Definition 1.1. Consider closed (possibly disconnected) contact manifolds (Y+, λ+)
and (Y−, λ−) and a symplectic manifold (X, ω) with oriented boundary ∂X = Y+⊔−Y−.
• (X, ω) is a strong symplectic cobordism from (Y+, λ+) to (Y−, λ−) if ω agrees with
dλ± on Y±.
• (X, ω) is a weakly exact symplectic cobordism from (Y+, λ+) to (Y−, λ−) if it is a strong
symplectic cobordism and ω is exact.
• (X, ω) is an exact symplectic cobordism if ω has a global primitive λ, called a Liouville
form, which restricts to λ± on Y±.
• (X, ω) is a weak symplectic cobordism from (Y+, ξ+) to (Y−, ξ−) if ω|ξ± > 0.

In each case, we call Y+ (respectively Y−) the convex (resp. concave) boundary of X.
When a cobordism of one of the above types has empty concave boundary, it is called a
filling. A (weakly) exact filling is also called a (weak) Liouville domain. When a strong
cobordism has empty convex boundary, we call it a strong symplectic cap. When we
speak of strong or exact cobordisms for (Y±, ξ±), we mean there are contact forms λ±
for ξ± for which the corresponding cobordism is strong or exact.
1.1.1. Capacities of domains with disconnected boundary. Our first theorem concerns
the ECH capacities of a Liouville domain with disconnected boundary.
Theorem 1.2. Suppose (X, ω) is a 4-dimensional connected weak Liouville domain
with disconnected boundary. Then the ECH spectrum of each boundary component and
the ECH capacities of (X, ω) are infinite.
We note that Theorem 1.2 will follow purely from formal properties of ECH. Nev-
ertheless, it can be applied to yield a number of results exposing connections between
Anosov dynamics and symplectic and contact geometry.
1.1.2. Anosov Liouville domains. The main examples of Liouville domains with discon-
nected boundary have the following form. We say that a closed 3-manifold Y admits
a Liouville pair (α+, α−) if α+ and α−are respectively positive and negative contact
forms on Y and
λ = e−tα−+ etα+
is a Liouville form on Rt × Y . For N large enough, [−N, N] × Y is a Liouville filling
of (Y, ker α+) ⊔(−Y, ker α−).
Theorem 1.2 implies the ECH spectra of the contact
structures defined by either element of a Liouville pair are infinite.
Many examples of three-dimensional Liouville pairs come from the theory of Anosov
flows. A closed oriented 3-manifold Y admits an Anosov flow ψt if there is a pair of
one-dimensional “stable” and “unstable” foliations on Y transverse to the flow whose
tangent vectors exponentially contract and expand under ψt; a precise definition is given
in Section 2.4. We will always consider our flows to be smooth. We will also almost
always assume our Anosov flows are oriented.
Definition 1.3. We say an Anosov flow on Y is oriented Anosov if its stable and
unstable foliations are orientable and given orientations compatible with that of Y .
Given an oriented Anosov flow on Y , Eliashberg–Thurston [ET98] and Mitsumatsu
[Mit95] show that Y admits a pair of contact structures (ξ+ = ker α+, ξ−= ker α−)
whose transverse intersection ξ+ ⋔ξ−spans the flow, and so that (α+, α−) is a Liouville
pair. Following [CLMM], we call the corresponding domain I ×Y , which gives an exact
filling of (Y, ξ+) ⊔(−Y, ξ−), an Anosov Liouville domain.
While the general construction of Anosov Liouville domains is due to Mitsumatsu
[Mit95], some explicit examples of these domains were described earlier by McDuff
[McD91] and Geiges [Gei95]. The details of these constructions are given in Section 2.
For any contact structure on the boundary of an Anosov Liouville domain, Theorem 1.2
will imply its ECH spectrum is infinite (see Theorem 2.17).
For a genus g surface Σg, the standard symplectic form ωcan = dλcan on T ∗Σg de-

1.1.3. Anosov contact manifolds. Even given an explicit Anosov flow, it may be difficult
to tell which contact structures bound an Anosov Liouville domain. We study another
family of contact structures coming from Anosov dynamics which always have infinite
ECH spectrum.
Definition 1.6. An Anosov contact 3-manifold is a closed contact 3-manifold (Y, ξ)
admitting a contact form whose Reeb flow is oriented Anosov.
The basic example of an Anosov contact manifold is the cotangent circle bundle
(S∗Σg, ξcan) of an oriented hyperbolic surface.
More Anosov contact manifolds can
be constructed explicitly thanks to a surgery technique of Foulon–Hasselblatt [FH17].
Broad existence results and construction methods also follow from the recent work of
Marty [Mar24]. Several authors have used Floer theory and symplectic field theory to
obstruct and describe these Reeb Anosov flows [FHV, MP12, CP25]. Hozoori [Hoz20]
asked how the existence of a Reeb Anosov flow for a contact structure restricts its
contact topology; the following result constitutes some progress in this direction. The
statement involves a distinguished element c(Y, ξ) ∈ECH(Y, ξ) called the contact
invariant; its definition is given in Section 2.2.
Theorem 1.7. Suppose (Y, ξ) is an Anosov contact 3-manifold.
Then it has non-
vanishing contact invariant, but the ECH spectrum of Y is infinite for any contact
form for ξ and the ECH capacities of any weakly exact filling of (Y, ξ) are infinite.
ECH also possesses an endomorphism U: ECH(Y, ξ) →ECH(Y, ξ) called the U map,
defined in Section 2.2. Finiteness of the first element of the ECH spectrum corresponds
exactly to the contact invariant lying in the image of the U map. Thus, Theorem 1.7
can be reinterpreted as giving restrictions on when a contact structure (Y, ξ) can admit
a Reeb Anosov flow, in terms of the structure of ECH as an F2[U]-module.
Corollary 1.8. A contact 3-manifold (Y, ξ) can be Anosov only if c(Y, ξ) is non-zero
and not in the image of the U map on ECH(Y, ξ, 0).

As further motivation for Theorem 1.8, a central problem in the modern theory of
Anosov flows (which dates back at least to Smale [Sma67]) is their classification on a
given 3-manifold up to orbit equivalence (see Section 2.4 for a definition). One important
class of Anosov flows are the skew R-covered flows. Recent work of Barthelm´e–Mann
and Marty [BM24, Mar24] implies the classification of oriented skew R-covered Anosov
flows is equivalent to the classification of Anosov contact structures up to contactomor-
phism. By Theorem 1.8, it suffices to restrict to contact structures with a very specific
property of their contact invariant.
1.1.4. Hamiltonian Anosov flows. Given a symplectic manifold (X2n, ω) and a smooth
closed hypersurface Y 2n−1 ⊂X, the one-dimensional distribution ker(ω|Y ) ⊂TY in-
tegrates to a characteristic foliation of Y . This foliation has the following dynamical
interpretation: given a smooth function H: X →R with Y as a regular level set, the
flow of the associated Hamiltonian vector field XH is tangent to Y and along Y its flow
lines are integral curves of the characteristic foliation. Thus, it makes sense to talk
about the characteristic flow of Y (at least up to reparametrization). In his 1998 ICM
address, Michael Herman posed the following question.
Question 1.9 (Herman, 1998 ICM [Her98]). Given a smooth compact Hamiltonian
hypersurface Y 2n−1 ⊂R2n, can its characteristic Hamiltonian flow be Anosov?
One motivation for studying Anosov Hamiltonian flows is a classical theorem of
Newhouse [New77], which implies that given a regular Hamiltonian hypersurface H−1(c)
in a symplectic 4-manifold, in a C2-open neighbourhood of smooth functions eH near
H, a C2-dense open set of hypersurfaces eH−1(c) either has an elliptic orbit or its flow is
Anosov. Hence Anosov Hamiltonian flows exactly obstruct the C2-genericity of elliptic
periodic orbits.
To the author’s knowledge, Herman’s question has not yet been addressed in the
literature.
Here we concern ourselves with the case n = 2, since Anosov flows are
best understood and known to exist plentifully in dimension three. First, we give an
elementary construction of many such hypersurfaces.
Proposition 1.10. Every symplectic 4-manifold admits infinitely many pairwise non-
homeomorphic Hamiltonian hypersurfaces with an Anosov characteristic flow.
Corollary 1.11. In any symplectic 4-manifold, the collection of Hamiltonian hyper-
surfaces with an elliptic orbit of their characteristic foliation is not C2-Baire generic in
the space of regular level sets of smooth Hamiltonians.
All the hypersurfaces we will construct carry a non-orientable Anosov flow, and so it
is reasonable to modify Herman’s question by demanding that the characteristic flow
be oriented Anosov.
In a spirit analogous to Theorem 1.7, one can give a general
obstruction to oriented Anosov Hamiltonian flows.
Theorem 1.12. Suppose that (X, ω) is a four-dimensional weak Liouville domain with
cECH
1
(X, ω) < ∞. Let H : X →R be a smooth Hamiltonian and M = H−1(c) a compact
regular level set. Then the characteristic Hamiltonian flow on M cannot be oriented
Anosov.

1.1.5. Strong co-fillings and Anosov flows. We now observe a Floer-theoretic obstruc-
tion to symplectic co-fillings, and by proxy, Anosov flows.
Definition 1.13. A connected contact manifold (Y, ξ) is called strongly co-fillable if
there exists a contact manifold (Y ′, ξ′), non-empty but possibly disconnected, so that
(Y, ξ) ⊔(Y ′, ξ′) admits a connected strong filling.
Remark 1.14. Note that being strongly co-fillable is a more restrictive condition than
being strongly fillable in dimension three, since any strong co-filling of (Y, ξ) can have
its other boundary component(s) capped off [EH02] to produce a strong filling of (Y, ξ).
The tight contact structure on S3 gives an example of a strongly fillable but not strongly
co-fillable contact manifold, as was first shown by McDuff [McD91].
To avoid confusion, we note there is also an older notion of strong semi-fillability
of (Y, ξ), which is usually taken to mean (Y, ξ) is one component of the boundary of
a strong filling (that may or may not have disconnected boundary). The existence of
symplectic caps [EH02] implies strong semi-fillability and strong fillability are equivalent
in dimension three.
Mitsumatsu [Mit95, p. 1420] asks when a 3-manifold can or cannot be realized as
a boundary component of a symplectic manifold with disconnected convex boundary.
Some previous obstructions to contact structures being co-fillable were found by Wendl
[Wen12] using the notion of partially planar domains. We now give a Floer-theoretic
obstruction to a 3-manifold admitting any strongly co-fillable contact structure. The
Anosov Liouville construction then implies this is also an obstruction to Anosov flows.
We phrase the result in terms of monopole Floer cohomology rather than ECH to
emphasize its topological nature, independent of a choice of contact structure. Given
an oriented 2-plane field ξ on a closed 3-manifold Y (for example a contact structure),
one can associate to it a spinc-structure sξ (see [Hut10, §8.1]).

The obstruction to Anosov flows given in Theorem 1.15 has been obtained inde-
pendently by Hedden–Raoux–Van Horn-Morris [HRVHM] in terms of Heegaard Floer
homology. Using this obstruction, they find the first examples of hyperbolic 3-manifolds
Y with b1(Y ) > 0 admitting no oriented Anosov flows (c.f. the discussion in [Yaz20, p.
363]). Note, by Gabai’s theorem, such examples have many taut foliations.
Hedden–Raoux–Van Horn-Morris’ work builds on a result of Lin [Lin24], who makes
use of the TQFT properties of monopole Floer theory to give an obstruction to co-
orientable taut foliations on rational homology spheres. This is an enhancement of
older work of Ozsv´ath and Szab´o [OS04], who prove that an L-space Y admits no co-
orientable contact structure with a weak co-filling of the form I × Y (and therefore, by
Eliashberg–Thurston [ET98], no co-orientable taut foliation). This result was part of
the genesis of the L-space conjecture (c.f. [BGW, Juh15]).
Since Anosov flows give rise to taut foliations, Ozsv´ath–Szab´o and Lin’s conclusions
are stronger but require the extra assumption that Y is a rational homology sphere.
We note for example that their results do not apply to T 3; and indeed, T 3 admits a
taut foliation and hence a weak co-filling, but it is well known (and our result gives
another proof) that it cannot have a strong co-filling or Anosov flow. One can recover
Lin’s result from Theorem 1.15 by noting that when Y is a rational homology sphere,
every weak filling I × Y can be deformed to a strong filling [Eli04].
Remark 1.17. The assumption that our Anosov flows are orientable is essential through-
out. The geodesic flow on the cotangent circle bundle of a hyperbolic non-orientable
surface is a non-orientable Reeb Anosov flow. By Theorem 1.19, it has finite ECH
spectrum, which contradicts Theorem 1.7 if one does not require orientability.
As noted by Baldwin–Sivek–Zung [BSZ, p. 2], 56/3-surgery on the pretzel knot
P(−2, 3, 7) gives rise to a hyperbolic L-space admitting an Anosov flow which is not
transversely orientable. Because this is an L-space, it provides a counterexample to
Theorem 1.15 if one drops the oriented assumption.

On the other hand, whenever high genus Lagrangians are already known to exist
inside (X, ω), Theorem 1.18 implies its ECH capacities are infinite. This proves the
ECH capacities are infinite in some new cases, including the standard symplectic form
on the 4-torus or more generally any product symplectic structure on Σg × Σg′ with
min{g, g′} ≥1.
1.1.7. Cotangent bundles of non-orientable surfaces. We have seen that for an oriented
surface Σg, the ECH capacities of its cotangent disk bundle D∗Σg are finite if and only
if g = 0 or 1. One might also be interested in the case of cotangent disk bundles of
non-orientable surfaces. For RP 2 and the Klein bottle, the capacities are known to be
finite [FR22, MR25]. We extend this to all non-orientable surfaces.
Proposition 1.19. For any closed non-orientable surface N, the ECH capacities of a
cotangent disk bundle (D∗N, ωcan) are finite.
We therefore have a complete classification of which closed two-manifolds have finite
ECH capacities of their cotangent disk bundles.

Outline. In Section 2, we recall necessary background on both ECH and Anosov flows,
in hope that this paper will be accessible to a reader conversant in one subject but not
the other. In Section 3, we prove our main results, namely Theorems 1.2, 1.7, 1.12
and 1.15. In Section 4, we generalize our results to the setting of hypertaut foliations,
prove Theorems 1.10 and 1.19, discuss high-genus Lagrangians, and speculate on ele-

2.2. Structures and properties of ECH.
2.2.1. The contact invariant. Given a contact 3-manifold (Y, ξ), there is a distinguished
element sensitive to the contact structure c(Y, ξ) ∈ECH(Y, ξ, 0) called the contact
invariant. This is the homology class represented by the empty orbit set [∅]. Note that
there are no holomorphic curves in R × Y without positive ends by Stokes’ theorem,
so that ∅is necessarily a cycle. It may be that ∅is also a boundary, in which case
c(Y, ξ) vanishes (this holds for example if ξ is overtwisted or contains Giroux torsion),
but c(Y, ξ) is always non-trivial when (Y, ξ) is strongly fillable [Hut, Ech20].
There are also contact invariants defined by different means in monopole and Hee-
gaard Floer theories and they all coincide under the isomorphisms [CoGH, KLT, Tau10].
2.2.2. The U map. Assume Y is connected. Given a generic point y ∈Y , one can
define a homomorphism

Under the isomorphism (2.1), the U map for the ith component of Y1 ⊔· · ·⊔Yn is given

An important motivation for defining Anosov flows is that they are structurally stable:
given a vector field X generating an Anosov flow, C1-small perturbations of X also
generate an Anosov flow and one which will be orbit-equivalent to X [FH19]. Here,
we say that flows on Y are orbit equivalent if there is a homeomorphism of Y mapping
(oriented but unparametrized) orbits to orbits.
A central problem in the study of
Anosov flows is the finiteness conjecture, which asserts every closed 3-manifold can
only admit finitely many Anosov flows up to orbit equivalence.
If Y has an Anosov flow, then its universal cover eY is homeomorphic to R3 and the
weak stable and unstable foliations Fu and Fs lift in eY to foliations eFu and eFs with
leaves homeomorphic to R2 [FH19].
Definition 2.10. The orbit space Oψ of an Anosov flow ψ on Y is the quotient of the
universal cover eY ∼= R3 by the lifted flow. Barbot [Bar95] and Fenley [Fen94] show that
Oψ is homeomorphic to a plane, bifoliated1 by the lifts eFu and eFs modulo ψ.
The Anosov flow ψ is R-covered if the space of leaves of one (equivalently both)
of the foliations eFu or eFs is Hausdorff (equiv. homeomorphic to R). Such flows are
always transitive, meaning they have a dense orbit. By Barbot and Fenley, the orbit
space Oψ of an R-covered Anosov flow ψ considered as a bi-foliated plane can either
be homeomorphic to R2 with foliations by vertical and horizontal lines, or the diagonal
strip {(x, y) ∈R2 : |x−y| < 1} with foliations by vertical and horizontal line segments.
In the former case, we call ψ a product Anosov flow and it must be orbit equivalent to
a suspension flow (see Theorem 2.12). In the latter case, we call ψ a skew Anosov flow.

Appendix A. Analogues for rational symplectic field theory
The proof of Theorem 1.2 is general enough to apply to some other SFT and Floer-
type spectral invariants, including in higher dimensions. For example, an analogous
theorem will hold for the Gutt–Hutchings capacities [GH18] of Liouville domains with
disconnected boundary. However, this is not so interesting because, as noted in The-
orem 2.14, the Liouville domains I × Y we consider have hypertight contact forms on
their boundary (including the examples in higher dimensions).
Heuristically, the first Gutt–Hutchings capacity of a Liouville domain (X, ω) measures
the action of a Reeb orbit which bounds a (perturbed) index two pseudoholomorphic
plane. In particular, the orbit must be contractible in X. But if (Y, λ) is hypertight,
then all its Reeb orbits cannot be contractible in the domain I × Y . Hence the first
and all higher Gutt–Hutchings capacities of I × Y will be infinite if its boundary is
hypertight.
Remark A.1. It is an interesting question if there is a Liouville domain of the form
I×Y whose boundary components do not admit hypertight contact forms. Note one can
find more complicated Liouville domains with disconnected non-hypertight boundary.
For example, one can take an Anosov Liouville domain and attach a pair of Liouville
domains with virtually overtwisted boundary to the two boundary components via We-
instein one-handles. The result is a Liouville domain with two virtually overtwisted (and
hence not hypertight) boundary components. Of course the Gutt–Hutchings capacities
of this must be infinite because it contains a Liouville embedding of a subdomain with
hypertight boundary.
