FRACTAL ANTI-TORI
PIERRE-EMMANUEL CAPRACE AND JUSTIN VAST
Abstract. Let Γ be a group acting properly and cocompactly on the product
of two trees T1 and T2. An anti-torus is a non-periodic flat plane in T1 × T2
that is the convex hull of two secant periodic lines. That notion was introduced
by Dani Wise as a tool to show that Γ is irreducible.
We establish a new
criterion ensuring the existence of anti-tori, and use it to prove that if Γ is an
S-arithmetic lattice in a product of simple algebraic groups of rank one, then
T1 × T2 contains anti-tori. We also introduce a new class of irreducible lattices
acting regularly on the vertex set of a product of trees, containing anti-tori that
are fractal aperiodic tilings of the plane. This establishes a connection between
lattices in products of trees and substitution tilings.
1. Introduction
Let X be a CAT(0) space and Γ be a group acting properly and cocompactly
on X by isometries. We say that a flat P in X is periodic if its stabilizer in Γ
acts cocompactly on P. An anti-torus is a non-periodic flat in X which contains
two secant periodic lines. In other words, an anti-torus is a 2-dimensional flat P
which is the convex hull of two non-parallel geodesic lines, that are translation
axes for two hyperbolic isometries γ1, γ2 ∈Γ, and such that the stabilizer of P in
Γ does not act cocompactly on P. We say that the anti-torus P is spanned by
γ1 and γ2. The concept of an anti-torus was introduced by D. Wise (see [Wis96]
and [Wis07]), who identified it as an irreducibility criterion in case X is a product
of trees (see [Cap19, Proposition 4.11]). Our first main result is the following
criterion ensuring the existence of an anti-torus.
Theorem 1.1. Let T1, T2 be locally finite trees with a cocompact automorphism
group, and let Γ ≤Aut(T1) × Aut(T2) be a cocompact lattice.
Let also (x1, x2) ∈V (T1) × V (T2) be a vertex, and Γ1 = Γx1 and Γ2 = Γx2 be the
respective stabilizers of x1 and x2. Let γ1 ∈Γ1 and γ2 ∈Γ2 and let φ: Γ →G be
a homomorphism to a group G satisfying the following conditions:
(i) φ(γ1) and φ(γ2) do not commute.
(ii) For i = 1, 2, the order of φ(γi) is finite, and relatively prime to the order
of the local action of Γ at every vertex of Ti.

Given a group G acting on a tree T, the local action of G at a vertex v ∈V (T)
is the permutation group formed by the action of the stabilizer Gv on the set
of edges emanating from v. If T is locally finite, this is a finite group for every
vertex.
Following the terminology introduced in [Cap19], in the special case where the
lattice Γ acts regularly (i.e. sharply transitively) on the vertex set V (T1)×V (T2),
we say that Γ is a BMW group. In that case, the Γ-action on both trees T1 and
T2 is vertex-transitive, so that both are homogeneous. The pair (d1, d2) formed
by the respective valencies of T1 and T2 is called the degree of Γ. Anti-tori have
been mostly studied so far in the special case of BMW groups. Theorem 1.1 is
valid for arbitrary lattices in products of trees.
It is an open question to determine whether every irreducible BMW group and,
more generally, every irreducible lattice in a product of two trees, contains an anti-
torus. Theorem 1.1 allows us to answer that question in the case of S-arithmetic
lattices in products of simple groups of rank one:
Corollary 1.2. Let Γ is an S-arithmetic group in the product of two simple alge-
braic groups of rank-one over local fields, whose respective Bruhat–Tits trees are
denoted by T1 and T2. Then T1 × T2 contains an anti-torus.
For special families of S-arithmetic BMW groups of quaternionic type, the exis-
tence of anti-tori has been established by D. Rattaggi [Rat05] and Bondarenko–
Bondarenko [BB24].
As observed by D. Wise [Wis96, Wis07], an anti-torus in a BMW group Γ ≤
Aut(T1)× Aut(T2) gives rise to an aperiodic square tiling of the plane: the square
complex T1 × T2 can be viewed as a presentation complex of Γ, whose squares are
naturally equipped by a labelling by the defining relators of Γ. Each flat plane in
T1 × T2 inherits the structure of a square tiling colored by those relators. When
the flat is an anti-torus, that tiling is aperiodic. This is illustrated in Figure 1,
that represents an anti-torus found by Janzen–Wise in a BMW group ΓJW of
degree (4, 4). As we shall see in Section 2, Theorem 1.1 allows one to recover
that anti-torus (see Proposition 2.2), and to answer positively a question asked in
[JW09, Section 2] concerning another lattice in a product of two tetravalent trees
(see Proposition 2.3).

[Cap19, Section 4]. In our exploration, we observed that Theorem 1.1 applies to
