FACTORIZATION THROUGH LORENTZ CONES
GUILLAUME AUBRUN, FRANCESCA LA PIANA, AND ALEXANDER M¨ULLER-HERMES
Abstract. A pair of proper cones pC1, C2q is said to have the Lorentz factor-
ization property (LFP) if every pC1, C2q-positive map factors through a direct
sum of Lorentzian cones, i.e., cones over Euclidean balls. Clearly, pC1, C2q has
the LFP if either C1 or C2 is a direct sum of Lorentzian cones, and our main
goal is to find other examples. We show that such examples cannot be found
for pairs pC1, C2q where C1 “ C2, or in the case where both C1 and C2 are
polyhedral. We also focus on the case where C1 “ C˝ is the square-based cone
in R3. Here, we show that pC˝, Cq has the LFP whenever C is a symmetric
cone, i.e., a direct sum of Lorentz cones, cones of positive semidefinite matrices
over the real numbers, complex numbers or quaternions, and the cone of 3 ˆ 3
positive semidefinite matrices over the octonions. We leave open the question
whether there are more examples, but we show that this list cannot be ex-
tended by any strictly convex cone C or for a cone C with dimpCq ď 5. Finally,
we discuss an application to a problem in quantum information theory.

The cone LorFactpC1, C2q is also proper in the vector space LpV1, V2q and in partic-

it is a natural question when the containment is strict. We say that a pair of proper
cones pC1, C2q has the Lorentz factorization property (LFP) if LorFactpC1, C2q “
PospC1, C2q, i.e., if every pC1, C2q-positive map factors through Lorentz cones.
Similar concepts have been studied in the context of Banach spaces. The Hilbert
space factorization norm γ2, which goes back to Grothendieck, quantifies the factor-
ization of linear operators between Banach spaces through Hilbert spaces (see [TJ89,
§13]). The notion of Lorentz factorizable maps is a generalization of the concept
of Hilbert-space factorizable operators to the setting of ordered vector spaces (see
also [LMH26] for a quantitative connection for cones over finite-dimensional normed
spaces). The analogue of the LFP in the setting of the isometric theory of Banach
spaces has been studied in [AMH25].
Our main motivation for studying when pairs of cones have the LFP comes from
a problem inspired by quantum information theory. In [AMH23] two of the authors
introduced the notion of resilience for a pair of cones pC1, C2q, where a certain
analogue of a well-known problem in quantum information theory has a negative
answer. They also showed that a pair pC1, C2q having the LFP implies that the
pair pC2, C1q is resilient. For more information on resilience see Section 5.
Main results. Clearly, the pair pC1, C2q has the LFP if C1 or C2 is Lorentzian
and more generally if C1 or C2 is isomorphic to a direct sum of Lorentz cones. It
is expected that having the LFP is quite restrictive. Our first main result confirms
this intuition:
Theorem 1. Let C be a proper cone. The following are equivalent
(1) The pair pC, Cq has the LFP.
(2) The cone C is isomorphic to a direct sum of Lorentz cones.
In particular, a proper cone C is a direct sum of Lorentz cones if and only if
the pairs pC, C1q have the LFP for any proper cone C1. Restricting to the family
of polyhedral cones, i.e., cones with a finite number of extremal rays, we have a
stronger result.

Theorem 2. Let C1 and C2 be proper polyhedral cones. The following are equivalent
(1) The pair pC1, C2q has the LFP.
(2) Either C1 or C2 is classical.

2.1. Proper cones and their geometry. Let V denote a real finite-dimensional
vector space and C Ď V a convex cone. We say that C is pointed if it does not
contain a line, i.e., if C X p´Cq “ t0u, and we say that C is generating if it spans
the entire ambient space V . Equivalently, a convex cone is generating if its interior
is not empty. If C is pointed, generating and closed, then we call it a proper cone.
If C Ď V is a proper cone, then its dual

We say that a convex cone C is a direct sum of convex cones C1 and C2 if
spanpC1q X spanpC2q “ t0u and C “ C1 ` C2. We write C “ C1 ‘ C2 in this case. A
convex cone C is called decomposable if there exist non-zero convex cones C1 and C2
such that C “ C1 ‘ C2, and indecomposable otherwise.
A convex cone F Ď C is called a face of C if for any x P F and y, z P C with
x “ y ` z, we have y, z P F. We will sometimes need the following equivalent
characterization of faces: A subset F Ď C is a face of C if for any x P F and y P C
such that for some ε ą 0 we have x ´ εy P C it follows that y P F. The non-trivial
faces of a cone C are in 1-to-1 correspondence with faces of a base of the cone. An

if R`x is an extremal ray (note that 0 P R`). For any x P C we set facepxq Ď C to
