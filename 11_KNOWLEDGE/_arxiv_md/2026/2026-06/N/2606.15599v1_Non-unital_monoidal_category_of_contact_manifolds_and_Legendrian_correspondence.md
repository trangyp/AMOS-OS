NON-UNITAL MONOIDAL CATEGORY OF CONTACT

YONG-GEUN OH, JUNHYUK PARK
Abstract. There are two purposes of the present paper which are interre-
lated. The first goal is to construct the structure of a non-unital monoidal
category Cont of contact manifolds, not necessarily coorientable, by develop-
ing the contact topology without contact forms.
The non-unital monoidal
product is the functorial contact product ⋆, called star product, introduced in
[Ohb]. We prove that the product ⋆is associative and there exist a collection
α = {αX,Y,Z} of the associator isomorphisms αX,Y,Z : X⋆(Y ⋆Z) ∼
= (X⋆Y )⋆Z
for X, Y, Z ∈Cont, that satisfy the pentagon axiom, i.e., that the triples
(Cont, ⋆, α) form a nonunital monoidal category.
The second goal is to develop the calculus of Legendrian correspondences,
which are by definition embedded Legendrian submanifolds of the contact prod-
uct Q ⋆Q′. Legendrian correspondences will play the role of 1-morphisms in
the 2-categorical structure to be equipped with Cont whose two morphisms are
contact instanton cohomologies HI(Rab, R′
ab) associated to a pair of Legen-
drian correspondences Rab, R′
ab ∈Leg(Qa, Qb). With this future application
in mind, we define the composition of Legendrian correspondences and prove
that the composition of a generic pair is again embedded and hence canonically
becomes a Legendrian correspondence.

1.2. Ideal boundary of the product of Liouville manifolds. We recall that
the set of manifolds with corners forms a monoid under the product.
We now
show that this monoidal structure is compatible with the contact product under
the operation of taking the ideal boundary of Liouville manifolds or more generally
ideal boundary of Liouville sectors.

assume that each connected component of the end is diffeomorphic to Q × [0, ∞).
In particular, when (M, λ) is a Liouville manifold, the ideal boundary ∂∞M = Q
which also carries a canonical contact structure.

which play the role of 1-morphisms in the symplectic category Symp introduced by
Weinstein [Wei82, Wei77]. More recently, they play a crucial role in the construc-
tion of 2-functor from symp to the 2-category of filtered A∞categories constructed
by Wehrheim-Woodward [WW10] and by Fukaya [Fuk25]. For this purpose, it is
important to include the Lagrangian immersions to make the construction flexible
enough to prove the axioms of a 2-functor [Fuk25]. This is because in general the
composition of two Lagrangian correspondences is not embedded even when the
given correspondences are embedded from the nature of Lagrangian submanifolds
and the definition of composition. Because the appearance of self-intersections is
a stable phenomenon for Lagrangian submanifolds in symplectic topology, there is
no simple way of avoiding the self-intersections. This is one of the sources that
makes the relevant Lagrangian Floer theory technically quite hard and the con-
struction of the aforementioned monoidal 2-functor technically highly non–trivial
as demonstrated in [Fuk25].
On the other hand, appearance of self-intersections of Legendrian submanifolds is
unstable and can be generically avoided. This makes the first-named author’s theory
of bordered contact instantons appearing in the construction of the Fukaya-type
Legendrian category can be applied for the construction of a similar 2-category Cont
and the relevant 2-functor technically simpler, to the level that there is essentially
no non-trivial analysis, such as the kind of technicalities [FOOO09a, FOOO09b]
arising in the analysis of pseudoholomorphic curves in symplectic geometry. On
the other hand, it involves a new type of technicality arising from noncompactness
of contact product [Oha, Ohb]. (See [Ohc] for its first step.)
There is one crucial difference between the monoidal products in Symp and Cont:
In the former, the canonical Cartesian product X × X′ of the obvious direct sum
−ω ⊕ω′ is used, but in the latter, the contact product (X, ξ) ⋆(X′, ξ′) is used
where the definition of the product ⋆already involves contact structures ξ, ξ′ and
some nontrivial constructions of the resulting contact structure, which we denote
by ξ ⋆ξ′. (See [Ohb, Appendix] and Section 5 of the present paper.)
Definition 1.11 (Legendrian correspondence). By a Legendrian correspondence of
(Q, ξ) and (Q′, ξ′), we mean an embedded Legendrian submanifold R of the contact
manifold (Q ⋆Q′, ξ ⋆ξ′).

2. Contact manifold as a Jacobi manifold
Since we consider contact manifolds which are not necessarily coorientable, we
regard a contact manifold as a distinguished class of (transitive) abstract Jacobi
manifolds following the description of [Loo98], [LOTV18].
We start with recalling the popular definition of contact manifold.
Definition 2.1. A contact structure on a smooth manifold Q is a maximally non-
integrable hyperplane distribution ξ on Q. A contact manifold is a smooth manifold
Q equipped with a contact structure ξ.
Denote by Iξ := TQ/ξ the quotient line bundle. By construction this has struc-
ture group Z2 and so a flat line bundle, i.e., defines a local system.
Definition 2.2 (Coorientation line bundle). We call the line bundle Iξ →Q the
coorientation local system of the contact structure ξ. We say (Q, ξ) is coorientable
if Iξ is trivial, and non-coorientable otherwise.

nondegeneracy of dα for any contact form α, i.e., one-form ker α = ξ. However when
ξ is not coorientable, such a description can be given only locally. Furthermore the
contact form has been used to study contact (Hamiltonian) dynamics, which forces
one to restrict to the coorientable case.

Part 2. Calculus of Legendrian correspondence
In symplectic topology, the Cartesian product of symplectic manifolds is again
a symplectic manifold, and Lagrangian submanifolds of this product are called the
’Lagrangian correspondence’. This Lagrangian manifolds is the main building block
of the ’Symplectic Category’. (See [Wei82] and [WW10].)
However Cartesian products of contact manifolds are not contact manifolds and
one should at least replace the Cartesian product by the contact product to think
of a Legendrian correspondence in the contact category Cont. As mentioned before,
the presence of Legendrian immersion is not a stable phenomenon unlike that of
Lagrangian immersion. We anticipate that this makes the calculus of Legendrian
correspondence technically easier to construct in contact topology than in symplec-
tic topology, when one construct the Fukaya-type 2-category using the theory of
quilted contact instanton homology as the 1-morphisms.
Definition 10.4 (Legendrian correspondence). We call an embedded Legendrian
submanifold R of the contact product Q0 ⋆Q1 a Legendrian correspondence from
Q0 to Q1.
Remark 10.5. Unlike the symplectic case, there is no obvious ‘identity’ element
for the ⋆product. It is an interesting question to see whether one should treat the
contact monoid as ‘unital’ or not in a weaker sense, such as ‘up to homotopy.’
We will show that similarly as for the Lagrangian correspondence, contacto-
morphisms from Q0 to Q1 can be regarded as a Legendrian correspondence via
the process of Legendrianization through the new monoidal structure the contact
product in the contact category we define. In the coorientable case equipped with
contact forms, the correspondence can be easily described as in [Lyc77], [Bhu01],
[Ohb] which we recall.

Appendix A. Bifunctor
In this appendix, we recall basic definitions of bifunctors from the category
theory. First we recall the definition of category and functor.
Definition A.1 (Category). A category C consists of two sets(or more gener-
ally classes) Obj(called objects) and Mor(called morphisms) with the structure
map(called composition)
◦: Mor(X0, X1) × Mor(X1, X2) →Mor(X0, X2)
for any Xi ∈Obj, satisfying a commutativity criterion.
Definition A.2 (Functor). Let C and D be categories. A map
F : C →D
sending objects to objects and morphisms to morphisms is called a functor, if it
respects structure maps(compositions) of its domain and codomain. More precisely,
F(f ◦g) = F(f) ◦F(g)
for any f, g ∈MorC.
Now we have the following simple way of defining the bifunctor using so called
’product category’.
Definition A.3 (Product Category). Let C be a category. The Product Category
C × C has its objects set
Obj C×C := Obj C × Obj C
and morphism set
Mor C×C := Mor C × Mor C.
The composition is given by the obvious relation:
(f, g) ◦C×C (h, i) = (f ◦C h, g ◦C i)
for f, g, h, i ∈MorC.
Definition A.4 (Bifunctor). Let C, D and E be categories. A functor F
F : C × D →E
from the product category C × D to E is called a bifunctor.
