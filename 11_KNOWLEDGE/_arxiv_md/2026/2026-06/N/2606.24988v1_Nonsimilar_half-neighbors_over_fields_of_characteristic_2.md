NONSIMILAR HALF-NEIGHBORS OVER FIELDS OF
CHARACTERISTIC 2
DETLEV W. HOFFMANN AND MAGNUS WIEDEKING
Abstract. The total isotropy index of a quadratic form φ over a field F is the
maximum dimension of any totally isotropic subspace of φ. If φ is anisotropic and
ψ is another anisotropic quadratic form over F of the same dimension, then φ and
ψ are called Vishik-equivalent if, over any field extension E/F, their total isotropy
indices are the same. In characteristic ̸= 2, Vishik-equivalence implies similarity
in all dimensions ≤7 and in all odd dimensions, but there are counterexamples in
all even dimensions ≥8. In this paper, we construct semi-singular anisotropic
quadratic forms of dimension 2m for any m ≥3 and defined over a suitable
extension of any given field F0 of characteristic 2 that are Vishik-equivalent but
not similar, thus completing the list of such examples provided earlier by the first
author and Krist´yna Zemkov´a.
1. Introduction
Consider two quadratic forms φ and ψ over a field F and of the same finite dimen-
sion such that their associated projective quadrics Xφ and Xψ are smooth, i.e., such
that the (bilinear) radicals of these forms have dimension at most 1 and the restric-
tion to the radical is anisotropic. Such forms are called nondegenerate, and radicals
of dimension 1 can then only occur in characteristic 2 for odd-dimensional forms.
Now these two forms are similar iff their quadrics are isomorphic as varieties (see
[EKM, Cor. 69.5]). In this situation, if E/F is any field extension, then obviously,
the total isotropy index of φ and ψ after scalar extension to E (i.e., the dimensions
of any maximal totally isotropic subspace of φE resp. ψE) will be the same. Now
a theorem first proved by Vishik [V] in characteristic 0, later by Karpenko [K] in
characteristic not 2 (for a characteristic free version, see [EKM, Theorem 93.1]),
states that the latter condition on the equality of the total isotropy indices over any
field extension is equivalent to the fact that Xφ and Xψ have isomorphic motives
in the category of (integral or mod 2) Chow motives, in which case we call φ and ψ
motivically equivalent. To study the relation between similarity and motivic equiv-
alence, one may restrict to the study of anisotropic forms, and so we may ask: Are
two motivically equivalent anisotropic quadratic forms similar?
In characteristic not 2, it was shown by Izhboldin [I1], [I2] that the answer is
positive for forms of dimension ≤7 as well as for forms of odd dimension, but
that there are counter-examples in all even dimensions ≥8 except possibly 12.† In
characteristic 2, the answer is still positive for odd dimensional nondegenerate forms,
see [EKM, Theorem 27.3]. The proofs actually use the criterion on the equality of
the total isotropy indices over any field extension. So we give this criterion a name
and call two (anisotropic) quadratic forms φ and ψ over F of the same dimension

• d ≤5 ([HZ, Theorem 4.2] and further references there);
• s = 1 ([EKM, Theorem 27.3]);
• s = 3 ([HZ, Theorem 3.1]);

• one of the forms is a nondegenerate so-called excellent form ([HZ, Corol-

dimension d = 2r + s that are Vishik-equivalent but not similar for the following
types:
• d = 2n and type (2n−1, 0), n ≥3 ([HZ, Theorem 6.5, Corollary 6.6]);
• d = 23 and types (3, 2), (2, 4), (1, 6) ([HZ, §6.3]).
In this paper, we complete the list of such examples in dimensions 2n ≥8 that are
not totally singular (i.e., of type (r, s) with r ≥1). The main result of this paper is
the following.
Main Theorem. To any nonnegative integers n, r, s with n ≥3, r ≥1 and 2n =
2r + s, and for any field F0 of characteristic 2, there exist a field extension F/F0
and anisotropic quadratic forms φ and ψ over F of type (r, s) such that φ and ψ are
Vishik-equivalent but not similar.
Our constructions will also invoke the theory of half-neighbors. A crucial ingredi-

(0, s)), and in particular the construction of totally singular forms σ whose group of
similarity factors GF(σ) is trivial, i.e., GF(σ) = F ∗2.

For all undefined terminology and facts concerning the theory of quadratic forms

A quadratic form φ of dimension n has a decomposition φ ∼= φ0 ⊥ql(φ) with φ0

• nonsingular if s = 0;
• singular if s > 0;

5. Construction of nonsimilar half-neighbors
