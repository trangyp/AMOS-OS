Abstract
In finite dimensions, every doubly stochastic matrix has the ℓp-operator norm
equal to 1 for all 1 ≤p ≤∞. However, in the infinite-dimensional setting,
this property may fail since the norm can be strictly smaller than 1 when
1 < p < ∞. In this paper, a complete characterization of infinite doubly
stochastic matrices for which the norm remains equal to 1 is obtained. More
precisely, for 1 < p < ∞, it is shown that
∥D∥ℓp(I)→ℓp(I) = 1
⇐⇒
Θ(D∗D) = 1,
where Θ measures the maximal average mass of a finite square submatrix.
Thus, the norm is equal to 1 precisely when the matrix contains arbitrarily
large finite regions in which it behaves almost like a finite doubly stochastic
matrix.
The proof uses a Cheeger-type argument, highlighting a natural
connection with ideas from spectral graph theory.
Keywords:
Doubly stochastic matrices, operator norms, Cheeger inequality

Eshkaftaki notably proved that any such matrix defines an operator from

infinite matrix and simply use the terminology doubly stochastic operator (on

In addition to proving that any doubly stochastic operator is an operator
of ℓp(I) →ℓp(I) for any 1 ≤p ≤∞, Eshkaftaki also established that its
norm is bounded above by 1, that is, ∥D∥ℓp(I)→ℓp(I) ≤1.
Moreover, the
author proved that for any 1 < p < ∞, there exist infinite doubly stochastic
matrices for which the norm is strictly smaller than 1, and, in fact, the norm
can be as close to zero as we wish. This contrasts sharply with the finite
case, where all doubly stochastic matrices have a norm precisely equal to
1. In his work, Eshkaftaki only provided one example of an infinite doubly
stochastic matrix D with ∥D∥ℓp(I)→ℓp(I) < 1. Naturally, one may wonder
when exactly the norm of an infinite doubly stochastic is strictly smaller
than 1. It is precisely this question that is answered in this paper, as we
provide a complete characterization of these operators.
The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we present the main
result in Theorem 1. The proof of this theorem is based on several lemmas
and one crucial inequality, which has its roots in spectral graph theory. This
role is discussed in Section 3, where we present a Cheeger-type coarea lemma.
Section 4 contains several technical lemmas which are needed for the proof
of the main result. In Section 5 we use them to prove Theorem 1. Moreover,
we also record an exact quantitative refinement showing that while Θ(D∗D)
alone does not determine the exact ℓ2-norm, the sequence Θ((D∗D)n) does,
and we show that the quantity Θ(D∗D) cannot be replaced by Θ(D).

In other words, the theorem shows that the only feature that prevents
an infinite doubly stochastic matrix from being a strict contraction on ℓp(I)
is the existence of finite subsets in I on which D∗D acts almost as a finite
doubly stochastic matrix. More explicitly, the condition Θ(D∗D) = 1 means
that one can find finite sets S ⊆I such that D∗D retains almost all of its mass
inside S, on average. In this sense, the theorem says that ∥D∥ℓp(I)→ℓp(I) =
1 exactly when D∗D admits arbitrarily good finite approximations to the
norm-preserving behavior of the finite-dimensional case. Otherwise, some
positive proportion of the mass always escapes, forcing the operator norm to
be strictly smaller than 1.

6. Concluding remarks
We proved that, for 1 < p < ∞, an infinite doubly stochastic matrix D
satisfies
∥D∥ℓp(I)→ℓp(I) = 1
if and only if
Θ(D∗D) = 1.
Thus, the quantity Θ(D∗D) characterizes exactly when the finite-dimensional
norm identity persists in the infinite setting. We also observed that the one-
step quantity Θ(D∗D) yields quantitative bounds on the ℓ2-norm. While
Θ(D∗D) alone does not determine the exact norm in general, the sequence
Θ((D∗D)n) recovers it through a spectral-radius formula.
The proof elegantly uses a generalized Cheeger inequality to relate the
ℓ2-operator norm to the escape of mass from finite subsets. Cheeger-type
inequalities such as this one have also proved useful more broadly in opera-
tor theory, notably in the study of spectral gaps for Markov operators and
Markov processes, as well as in related questions for positive operators and
semigroups. This suggests that the present characterization could fit into
a wider connection between isoperimetric quantities and spectral properties
of positive operators. Exploring this connection further may be a natural
direction for future work.
