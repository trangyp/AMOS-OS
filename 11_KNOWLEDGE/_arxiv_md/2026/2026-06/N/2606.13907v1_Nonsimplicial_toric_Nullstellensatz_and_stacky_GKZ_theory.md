CHRISTINE BERKESCH, DANIEL ERMAN, AND DAVID FAVERO
Abstract. We introduce a variant of the Cox ring using Q-Cartier divisors and use this
to remedy various deficiencies of nonsimplicial toric varieties. Our main applications are:
Cox’s ideal-variety correspondence, an explicit classification of subschemes and sheaves in
terms of multigraded modules, an associated toric Deligne-Mumford stack, and a stacky
extension of the GKZ/Mori theory of toric varieties.
Projective geometry and graded algebra are classically intertwined. Due to pioneering work
of Cox [Cox95] and others [Mus87,Aud91], there is an enhancement of this algebro-geometric
dictionary connecting the geometry of simplicial toric varieties to multigraded algebra. For
example, Cox’s toric ideal-variety correspondence [Cox95, Proposition 2.4] directly general-
izes Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz for Pn to simplicial toric varieties.
Theorem 1.1 (Cox’s Ideal-Variety Correspondence). If X is a simplicial toric variety
with Cox ring S := L
d∈Cl(X) H0(X, OX(d)) and irrelevant ideal B, then there is a bijective
correspondence between closed subvarieties of X and radical homogeneous ideals I ⊆B ⊆S.
For nonsimplicial toric varieties, Lemma 1.1 simply fails.
Example 1.2. Consider the nonsimplicial fan Σ given by the faces of the unique maximal
cone on the rays (0, 0, 1), (1, 0, 1), (0, 1, 1), (1, 1, 1). The Cox ring for X = XΣ in this case is
the polynomial ring S = C[a, b, c, d] with deg(a) = deg(d) = 1 and deg(b) = deg(c) = −1,
and the irrelevant ideal is B = ⟨1⟩. Lemma 1.1 breaks down in this case. For instance, the
ideals ⟨a, d⟩and ⟨b, c⟩are distinct, both belong to B (this is trivial because B is the unit
ideal), and both correspond to the same subvariety of X, namely, the torus-fixed point.
⋄
Geometrically, a semiprojective nonsimplicial toric variety1 is no longer a geometric quo-
tient, which has consequences beyond the ideal-variety correspondence. The main idea of
this paper is that results like Cox’s ideal-variety correspondence can be remedied in the
nonsimplicial case if the Cox ring S and irrelevant ideal B are replaced by a variant that
only considers Q-Cartier degrees.

3. Stacks, graded modules, and quotients
Lemma 2.11 demonstrates that closed subvarieties of X can be described equally well in
terms of radical ideals in ˇS or SPic. The comparison with subschemes of X, or more generally,
quasi-coherent sheaves on X, is more subtle, and we will use the language of stacks to clarify
these results. In this section, we motivate the presence of toric stacks in this article by
explaining how their quasi-coherent sheaves are more naturally related to graded modules
on ˇS (or SPic). Then we obtain a toric Nullstellensatz result for XQ-Car, and provide a proof
for Theorem B.(1).
To begin, note that one key property of quotient stacks (all stacks in this paper are
quotient stacks), which is that quasi-coherent sheaves on a quotient [Y/G] are naturally
equivalent to G-equivariant sheaves on Y . This allows for concrete descriptions of sheaves
and subschemes in terms of ideals and graded modules on ˇS (or SPic), analogous to results
of Cox for the simplicial case. For background on stacks, there are several major references
such as [Ols16,Vis05,Aut26,Alp26], though we will utilize only a few specialized results from
this literature.
