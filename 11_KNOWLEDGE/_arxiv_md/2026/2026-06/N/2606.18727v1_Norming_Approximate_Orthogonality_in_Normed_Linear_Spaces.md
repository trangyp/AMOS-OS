Abstract
We introduce and study the notion of norming approximate orthogonality, a two-parameter
generalization of Birkhoff–James orthogonality in normed linear spaces. For δ, ε ∈[0, 1)
with ε < (1 −δ)2, we say x ⊥ε
δ y in X if there exists f ∈X∗with | f (x)| ≥(1 −δ)∥f ∥∥x∥
and | f (y)| ≤
ε
1−δ∥f ∥∥y∥, simultaneously relaxing both the norming condition on x and the
vanishing condition on y. It is proved that
x ⊥ε
δ y ⇐⇒∥x + λy∥≥(1 −δ)∥x∥−
ε
1 −δ ∥λy∥
∀scalars λ.
This framework interpolates between two notions of approximate orthogonality in normed
linear spaces due to Chmieli´nski and Dragomir, and recovers three existing notions of or-
thogonality in extreme cases: exact Birkhoff–James orthogonality at δ = ε = 0, the ap-
proximate orthogonality of Chmieli´nski at δ = 0, and the approximate orthogonality of
Dragomir at ε = 0. In Hilbert spaces such an orthogonality is characterized by an inner
product bound L(δ, ε) < 1, namely
x ⊥ε
δ y ⇐⇒|⟨x, y⟩| ≤L(δ, ε)∥x∥∥y∥.

tablished. The forward and converse implications are governed by the distinct thresholds
ε

ε

ness of this gap is confirmed by counterexamples in ℓ2∞. A dual formulation of norming
approximate orthogonality is established with a complete equivalence in the reflexive case.
We apply our results to (vector-valued) continuous function spaces, which extends some
earlier results and recovers few operator theoretical results with alternative proofs using
measure theoretic techniques.

1.1. Motivation and Background. Orthogonality in a normed linear space is cap-
tured by the Birkhoff–James relation [2]: x ⊥B y if ∥x + λy∥≥∥x∥for all scalars
λ, or equivalently, if there exists f ∈X∗with ∥f ∥= 1, f (x) = ∥x∥and f (y) = 0.
Birkhoff-James orthogonality has a deep connection with the norm attainment set
of functionals [13, 14, 17], namely x ⊥B y if and only if there exists a continuous
linear functional that attains its norm at x and vanishes at y. Two independent
lines of relaxation have been pursued in the literature. Dragomir [12] introduced
the first approximate version, defining x ⊥ε
D y for ε ∈[0, 1) by the following norm
inequality
∥x + λy∥≥(1 −ε)∥x∥
for all scalars λ,
Another notion of approximate orthogonality is introduced by Chmieli´nski [5]
defining x ⊥ε

for all scalars λ,
and this relation has since been studied extensively [8, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 20, 21, 16, 1].
While x ⊥ε

als attaining norm at x and approximately vanishes at y [10, 20]

present paper addresses both simultaneously, asking what orthogonality is in-
duced when one requires a functional that approximately attains its norm at x and
approximately vanishes at y.
