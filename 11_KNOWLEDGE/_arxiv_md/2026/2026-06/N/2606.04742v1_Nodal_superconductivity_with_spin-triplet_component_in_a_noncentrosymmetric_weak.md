Nodal superconductivity with spin-triplet component in
a noncentrosymmetric weakly-correlated metal
Marcel Strohmeier,1 Andriy Smolyanyuk,2 Karsten Held,2 Michael Smidman,3, ∗
Geetha Balakrishnan,3 Wolfgang Belzig,1 Elke Scheer,1 and Angelo Di Bernardo1, †
1Department of Physics, University of Konstanz, 78457 Konstanz, Germany
2Institute of Solid State Physics, TU Wien, 1040 Vienna, Austria
3Department of Physics, University of Warwick, Coventry CV4 7AL, United Kingdom

In conventional superconductors, Cooper pairs form in an even-parity spin-singlet state. Non-
centrosymmetric superconductors, which lack inversion symmetry, exhibit antisymmetric spin-orbit
coupling (ASOC) that can combine even-parity spin-singlet and odd-parity spin-triplet pairs into
a mixed-parity order parameter. Spin-triplet components are highly beneficial for superspintronic
devices. Whether ASOC alone −without strong electronic correlations −is sufficient to generate
a measurable triplet component remains a central open question. Here, we resolve this question
in Nb18Re82 (Nb-Re), a weakly-correlated noncentrosymmetric metal whose superconducting pair-
ing symmetry has been actively debated. Using low-temperature scanning tunneling spectroscopy
on single crystals with four distinct crystallographic orientations, find a pronounced orientation-
dependent anisotropy in the local density of states. Supported by a symmetry-constrained model,
we show that the complete set of tunneling spectra requires a mixed-parity order parameter with
the triplet amplitude reaching up to half of the singlet component.
These results reconcile the
conflicting reports in the literature on Nb-Re and demonstrate that ASOC is sufficient to foster a
sizable spin-triplet component even without strong electronic correlations, suggesting that mixed-
parity superconducting states may be more widespread than previously assumed. Since Nb-Re can
be readily fabricated in thin-film form, these findings position it as an accessible platform for super-
spintronic devices and establish orientation-resolved tunneling spectroscopy as a general protocol
for the detection of mixed-parity order parameters.
I.
INTRODUCTION
In their first microscopic theory of superconductivity,
Bardeen, Cooper and Schrieffer (BCS) (1) describe the
condensation of electron pairs (Cooper pairs) into an
even-parity spin-singlet state with a fully-gapped den-
sity of states (DoS). It is now understood that the Cooper
pair wavefunction in a superconductor can exhibit a much
richer variety of symmetries in its spin, orbital, and tem-
poral (frequency) components than those originally con-
templated by BCS theory (1), provided that the over-
all fermionic wavefunction remains antisymmetric un-
der particle exchange (2, 3). Such classification allows
for superconducting states with spin-triplet symmetry,
anisotropic orbital symmetry in momentum (k) space, or
odd-frequency symmetry.
Among these unconventional pairing states,
spin-
triplet pairing is particularly compelling because spin
triplets
can
penetrate
ferromagnets
without
pair-
breaking, enabling dissipationless spin transport (2, 4, 5),
and provide a primary route toward topological super-
conductivity and fault-tolerant quantum computing (6–
8).
A central challenge in the field, however, is to

Noncentrosymmetric superconductors (NCS) −mate-

−provide a natural platform to study the microscopic
mechanisms leading to the formation of an intrinsic spin-
triplet component in the superconducting order param-

which lifts spin degeneracy and enforces a mixing of even-
parity spin-singlet and odd-parity spin-triplet Cooper
pairs, fostering a mixed-parity order parameter (13, 14).
The parity mixing in NCS is an intrinsic consequence of
their crystal symmetry, which requires neither magnetic
ordering nor proximity to a ferromagnet for it to occur.
However, whether ASOC alone is sufficient to induce
a measurable triplet component −without the strong
electronic correlations and flat bands present in heavy-
fermion systems such as CePt3Si (15–17) or UTe2(18–22)
−remains an open question, with compelling experimen-
tal evidence still lacking (11–13).
Re-based superconductors with the noncentrosymmet-

tion because they feature weak electronic correlations
(23–31). Among them, Nb18Re82 (Nb-Re) has attracted
particular attention because it can be fabricated in thin-
film form suitable for device integration (32–34).

In addition, our findings demonstrate that

component in a weakly-correlated metal — suggest-

widespread across noncentrosymmetric materials than
previously assumed — and they consolidate orientation-
resolved tunneling spectroscopy as a general protocol for
identifying mixed-parity order parameters in other super-
conductors, with direct implications for their application
in superconducting spintronics devices.
II.
RESULTS
A.
Spatially-resolved tunneling spectroscopy on
Nb-Re thin films
Nb-Re crystallizes into a cubic crystal structure (space
group I43m, lattice constant of 9.651 ˚A). The complex
unit cell with many atoms results in pronounced differ-
ences in atomic packing across different crystallographic
planes (Fig. 1D). A representative X-ray diffraction pat-
tern (XRD) confirming the crystallographic quality of
a (311)-oriented Nb-Re single crystal measured in this
study is shown in Fig. 1E.
The structural anisotropy of Nb-Re underpins our ex-
perimental strategy.
If the superconducting order pa-
rameter −which defines the symmetry of pairing cor-
relations −has a non-trivial (i.e., anisotropic) symme-
try in k-space, different orientations of the crystal sur-
face expose distinct cross-sections of the superconducting
gap structure. Probing surfaces with different crystallo-
graphic orientations therefore provides direct access to
the k-space symmetry of the superconducting order pa-
rameter −information that orientation-averaged or non-
local measurements cannot provide.
Density functional theory (DFT) calculations reveal a
complex manifold of electronic bands in Nb-Re (Figs. 1A-
C), with two bands dominating the DoS near the Fermi
level EF. Due to their large Fermi surface volume and
relatively low Fermi velocity vF these bands are likely the
most relevant to superconducting pairing [see materials
and methods (42) for details].
As a first test for anisotropy in the superconducting
order parameter, we performed low-T STS on polycrys-
talline Nb-Re thin films. These thin films have grain size
of ≈5 −10 nm comparable to both the superconducting
coherence length ξ0 ≈4 −5 nm in Nb-Re (36, 38) and
to the lateral resolution of our STM (Fig. 2B) (43). This
is a crucial point: since the STM probes the local DoS
on a length scale comparable to the grain size, each spec-
troscopic measurement effectively samples a single crys-
tallite with a well-defined but unknown orientation. A
polycrystalline thin film therefore naturally provides ac-
cess to a statistical ensemble of differently-oriented nano-
crystals within a single sample. If the order parameter is
anisotropic in k-space, one would expect to observe a rich
variety of spectral shapes across different sample areas —
each reflecting the gap structure projected onto a differ-
ent crystallographic plane. Conversely, a fully isotropic
order parameter would yield identical BCS-like spectra
regardless of the underlying local crystallographic ori-
entation [see materials and methods (42) for details on
the STM measurement setup and thin-film growth con-
ditions].
In some areas of the Nb-Re thin films, the differen-
tial conductance (dI/dV ) −which is proportional to the
DoS −shows a fully-gapped shape whose gap amplitude,
extracted by fitting the dI/dV curves with the Dynes
model, follows a dependence on temperature T and on
magnetic field B quantitatively consistent with BCS the-
ory (Figs. 2C,D) [see materials and methods (42) for de-
tails on the fitting with Dynes model].
In other sam-
ple areas, however, we observe a markedly different DoS:
some spectra show in-gap features such as a change in
slope within the gap and wider quasiparticle coherence
peaks, while others display V-shaped gaps with energy-
split coherence peaks (Figs. 2E). These spectral features
are incompatible with an isotropic s-wave order param-

eter and are instead indicative of a nodal gap structure
that we manage to probe when tunneling into grains with
specific crystallographic orientations. Further evidence
for nodes comes from the B dependence of the zero-
bias conductance (ZBC): for the non-BCS spectra, the
ZBC shows an immediate onset of increase upon apply-
ing B, followed by a linear growth with B (Figs. 2F,G).
This behavior contrasts sharply with that of the fully-
gapped BCS-like spectra, also measured in other areas
on the same films, for which no such instantaneous in-
crease is observed at low B. The immediate onset of the
ZBC under B is a direct consequence of the presence of
nodal quasiparticles at B = 0: in a nodal superconduc-
tor, low-energy quasiparticle states are already present
at B = 0 and are immediately redistributed by the ap-
plied B, producing a linear ZBC response from the out-
set.
In a fully-gapped superconductor, by contrast, a
finite B is required to create quasiparticles by breaking
Cooper pairs, so no instantaneous ZBC increase occurs.
This contrasting B-dependence, illustrated schematically
in Fig. 2H, therefore provides independent evidence for a
nodal component in the superconducting order parame-
ter of Nb-Re.
Taken together, the coexistence of fully-gapped spectra
and spectra with nodal features across different areas of
the same thin film provides a first, spatially-resolved indi-
cation of a k-space anisotropic order parameter in Nb-Re.
Reproducing the shape of these DoS spectra with theo-
retical fits also requires both an isotropic and anisotropic
component (Fig. 2F), which provides a first hint for a su-
perconducting order parameter with a complex structure
in k-space.
B.
Orientation-dependent density of states in
Nb-Re single crystals
While the Nb-Re thin-film measurements establish the
presence of orientation-dependent spectral features, they
do not allow a reliable determination of the local grain
orientation, which is essential for a direct and unam-
biguous correlation between spectroscopic signatures and
specific crystallographic directions.
We therefore car-
ried out low-T STS on Nb-Re single crystals with four
well-defined surface orientations, confirmed by electron
backscatter diffraction (EBSD) and XRD [Fig. 3B and
materials and methods (42)].
On (001)- and (311)-oriented Nb-Re crystals (Fig. 3A),
the dI/dV spectra measured at T = 300 mK are BCS-like
yielding a gap amplitude ∆≈1.0 meV (Fig. 3C), which is
consistent with the fully gapped tunnel spectra measured
on certain area of the Nb-Re thin films (Figs. 2C,D).
The picture changes dramatically on the (212)-oriented
crystal. For this surface orientation, we recover the full
range of non-BCS spectral shapes seen in the thin films
— fully gapped spectra, double-peak features, V-shaped
gaps, and energy-split coherence peaks (Fig. 4A) −now
unambiguously associated with a single crystallographic
orientation.
Importantly, the double-peak spectra are
perfectly symmetric about zero-voltage bias even under
applied B, which rules out Yu-Shiba-Rusinov (44) or
other surface bound states as their origin, and establishes
their intrinsic superconducting nature (Fig. 4B). Further-
more, the DoS on the (212) surface responds anisotrop-
ically to B: at a given field amplitude, a magnetic field
B∥applied parallel to the surface produces significantly
more V-shaped spectra than a field B⊥applied perpen-
dicular to the surface (Fig. 4C). This behavior is consis-
tently reproduced across several sample areas and sug-
gests an anisotropic B-induced pair-breaking linked to a
non-trivial pairing symmetry in Nb-Re.
On the (110)-oriented crystal, we observe a similar va-
riety of spectral features with a stronger tendency to-
ward V-shaped characteristics and additionally find an
enlarged superconducting gap with ∆∼2 meV (Fig. 4D)
alongside zero-bias conductance peaks −signatures con-
sistent with nodal gap directions and surface-induced An-
dreev Bound states (45, 46).
Further details and a quantitative cluster-based anal-
ysis of the spectral variation measured on the (110) and
(212) Nb-Re single-crystal surfaces are provided in the
supplementary text (42).

to the overall gap function. Such odd-parity function fills
in the low-energy gap of the even-parity component by
expanding the amplitude of its narrower lobes, thereby
removing the V-shaped background while leaving the
outer coherence peak position unchanged (Fig. 5A,B).

fects: the nodal even-parity component tends to produce
V-shaped spectra, while the odd-parity triplet compo-
nent partially fills in the low-energy DoS, and the bal-

tral shape observed at any given location on the crystal
surface.

The spatial variation of this ratio reflects local inhomo-

modulate the local ASOC strength and hence the degree
of parity mixing.
This mixed-parity order parameter therefore accounts
for the complete set of dI/dV spectra measured across
all four probed crystallographic orientations, establish-
ing the coexistence of spin-singlet and spin-triplet com-

order parameter of Nb-Re.

III.
DISCUSSION
The orientation-dependent tunneling spectra reported
here, and their quantitative reproduction with a mixed-
parity superconducting order parameter constrained by
the point-group symmetry of Nb-Re, provide direct spec-
troscopic evidence that the superconducting order pa-
rameter of Nb-Re intrinsically hosts coexisting spin-
singlet and spin-triplet components. This conclusion re-
solves a long-standing controversy in the literature. Pre-
vious studies reporting BCS-like behavior in Nb-Re thin-
film junctions and bulk transport measurements (36–39)
are consistent with our observations on (001)- and (311)-
oriented surfaces, which present a fully gapped BCS-like
DoS. Conversely, reports of double-gap behavior in point-
contact spectroscopy (41) and anomalously large Bc2 (35)

suggesting unconventional superconductivity find a cor-
respondence in the nodal even-parity component and in
the orientation-dependent gap structure that we identify
here.
The apparent contradiction between these bod-
ies of work, therefore, reflects not an inconsistency in
the material but the orientation sensitivity of a mixed-
parity order parameter −a subtlety that is fundamen-
tally inaccessible to non-local, orientation-averaged, or
thin-film measurements, all of which inevitably sample a
superposition of crystallographic directions. Our local,
orientation-resolved approach resolves this ambiguity di-
rectly.
The central implication of our results concerns the role
of electronic correlations in enabling spin-triplet pair-
ing. To date, the clearest evidence for spin-triplet su-
perconductivity in NCS has come from heavy-fermion
systems such as CePt3Si,where strong electronic correla-
tions and flat bands near the Fermi level are thought to
amplify the triplet component (15–17). Nb-Re, by con-
trast, is a weakly-correlated metal: thermodynamic and
transport measurements yield a Wilson ratio RW = 0.94
and a Kadowaki-Woods ratio (KWR) A/γ2 = 0.24 ×
10−5 µΩcm/(mJ/mol K)2 (38), both well below the char-
acteristic scales of strongly correlated Fermi liquids, with
no evidence of electron mass enhancement. The short su-
perconducting coherence length ξ0 ≈4 −5 nm in Nb-Re
(36, 38) is not a signature of strong correlations but can
be attributed to a combination of reduced Fermi velocity
due to band renormalization with strong disorder inher-
ent to the α-Mn structure, which places Nb-Re in the
dirty limit, where ξ ∝
p
ℏD/∆is strongly suppressed by
the short electronic mean free path l ≈5 nm (39).
Our demonstration that thus weakly-correlated metal
hosts a significant spin-triplet component reaching up
to 50% of the even-parity spin-singlet amplitude demon-
strates that ASOC alone −without strong correlations
−is sufficient to foster mixed-parity superconductiv-
ity. Although the degree of parity mixing (i.e., ∆o/∆e)
varies across the surface, the triplet fraction is remark-
able: it is comparable in magnitude to that reported in
heavy-fermion NCS (12, 13), despite the absence of the
correlation-driven mechanisms thought to be responsi-
ble in those systems. This result therefore suggests that
parity-mixed order parameters may be a generic feature
of NCS with sufficiently strong ASOC, rather than an
exceptional property of correlated electron systems —
with significant implications for how this broad class of
materials is understood and exploited.
Several simplifying assumptions in our model deserve
comment. The spherical FS approximation neglects the
multi-band complexity revealed by our DFT calculations,
and a more complete treatment accounting for the full FS
topology may refine the quantitative extraction of the
singlet-to-triplet ratio. The spatial variation of this ra-
tio across the crystal surface, which we attribute to local
inhomogeneities in the structural and electronic surface
properties modulating the ASOC strength, also points
to the importance of disorder in governing the local de-
gree of parity mixing in the dirty limit. Additionally, the
DoS is not sensitive to the frequency (temporal) symme-
try of the pairing correlations or to the relative phase
between the singlet and triplet components −aspects of
the order parameter that remain open to future phase-
sensitive measurements. Time-reversal symmetry break-
ing, reported in Nb-Re by muon spin spectroscopy (30),
may also give rise to higher-orbital gap functions not con-
sidered in our current model.
The thin-film compatibility of Nb-Re, combined with
the intrinsic spin-triplet component established here, po-
sitions it as a particularly attractive platform for su-
perspintronic devices.
Spin-triplet Cooper pairs can
penetrate ferromagnetic barriers without pair-breaking,
enabling dissipationless spin currents in superconduc-
tor/ferromagnet hybrid structures (30).
Unlike heavy-
fermion NCS, which are difficult to grow as thin films and
less readily compatible with standard device fabrication,
Nb-Re can be deposited by sputtering and patterned by
established lithographic techniques (32, 47), making it
directly integrable into superconducting spintronic archi-
tectures. Our results further suggest that other weakly
correlated NCS −particularly those in the Re-based α-
Mn family −may similarly host mixed-parity order pa-
rameters, broadening the materials landscape for super-
spintronic applications.
Beyond Nb-Re,
our results establish orientation-
resolved tunneling spectroscopy as a general experimen-
tal protocol for identifying mixed-parity order parame-
ters in noncentrosymmetric superconductors.
The key
requirement is the availability of single crystals with
well-defined surface orientations exposing distinct cross-
sections of the k-space gap structure. This condition is
met by a broad class of NCS with spin-split Fermi sur-
faces, including for example NbSe2, for which a mixed
s + f order parameter with nodes and sixfold symme-
try has been proposed and preliminary evidence compat-
ible with it found in vortex-based measurements (48–50).
The combination of orientation control and local spec-
troscopy demonstrated here can provide precisely the ex-
perimental tool needed to address such questions in other
materials.
To fully resolve the exact shape of the order parameter
in Nb-Re and quantify how the singlet and triplet ampli-
tudes evolve with B and T, future studies with angular-
dependent B with amplitude approaching Bc2 would be
particularly informative. Andreev spectroscopy on well-
defined superconductor/ferromagnet interfaces based on
Nb-Re thin films, combined with the crystallographic ori-
entation control demonstrated here, could further map
the directional dependence of the triplet penetration
depth.
Taken together, our results establish Nb-Re as a model
NCS in which the interplay between crystal symmetry
and spin-orbit coupling gives rise to intrinsic mixed-
parity superconductivity −and demonstrates that the
search for spin-triplet pairing does not need to be con-
fined to strongly-correlated electron systems.
