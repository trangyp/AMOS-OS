Nonlinear Freezing of Vibrational Polariton Transport via Mesoscale Simulations
Xinwei Ji1 and Tao E. Li1, ∗
1Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Delaware, Newark, Delaware 19716, USA
Two-dimensional real-space imaging of vibrational polariton transport in planar Fabry–Pérot mi-
crocavities is numerically simulated via the mesoscale cavity molecular dynamics approach, which
self-consistently propagates ∼2 × 104 realistic molecular simulation cells on a two-dimensional grid
coupled to the same number of cavity modes. Beyond the well-known polariton ballistic-to-diffusive
turnover in the linear response regime, these atomistic simulations reveal a nonlinear freezing mech-
anism of vibrational polariton transport, i.e., under strong pumping of the upper polariton, the ini-
tially ballistically propagating upper polariton completely freezes and localizes energy to molecules
at specific locations. This mechanism originates from pump-induced breaking of the in-plane transla-
tion symmetry: significant molecular excitations at the pulse hot spot broaden the polariton density
of states, thus funneling population to the k∥→0 band edge with vanishing group velocities.
Introduction. Vibrational polaritons form when a vi-
brational mode of a large ensemble of molecules couples
strongly to an optical or plasmonic microcavity [1–4]. In
this vibrational strong coupling (VSC) regime, experi-
ments report modified thermally-activated chemical re-
action rates and crystallization processes [5–8], enhanced
remote energy transfer [9], and altered infrared (IR) pho-
tochemistry [10, 11]. These experiments raise an intrigu-
ing question: How can vibrational polaritons significantly
impact local molecular processes, with or without ex-
ternal pumping, even though the light-matter coupling
per molecule is intrinsically small for most cavity setups
[12–17]? Addressing this question poses significant chal-
lenges to existing theoretical models and numerical tool-
boxes, as both mode-resolved cavity structures and realis-
tic molecular disorder must be properly treated [18, 19],
not to mention the necessity of approaching the large
N limit to distinguish collective molecular response from
predictions under single- or few-molecule strong coupling
[20, 21].
Recent two-dimensional (2D) real-space imaging ex-
periments of exciton-polariton [22, 23] and vibrational
polariton transport [24] reveal ballistic-to-diffusive trans-
port along the lateral direction of planar Fabry–Pérot
cavities:
Upon spatially selective pumping at a well-
defined in-plane momentum (k∥), the excited upper po-
lariton (UP) initially propagates ballistically with a fixed
group velocity, followed by diffusive transport arising
from molecular disorder and dephasing [22–24].
This
ballistic-to-diffusive turnover has been studied using both
quantum-mechanical calculations and analytical treat-
ments [25–33]. Despite these exciting advances, atom-
istically resolved 2D real-space imaging of vibrational po-
lariton dynamics remains challenging. This gap hinders
direct microscopic understanding of how propagating po-
laritons localize their energy to individual molecules,
a key question for resolving the mysteries in polariton
chemistry experiments.
Here, we report atomistic simulations of 2D real-
space imaging of vibrational polariton dynamics in pla-
nar Fabry–Pérot cavities.
With these simulations, we
not only characterize the ballistic-to-diffusive turnover
in the linear response regime, by also reveal a novel
mechanism of UP transport under strong pumping: from
ballistic propagation to nonlinear freezing.
Our calcu-
lations employ the recently developed mesoscale cavity
molecular dynamics (CavMD) approach [34], which in-
volves the self-consistent Maxwell-MD simulation of up
to 20, 736 molecular simulation cells (each an indepen-
dent LAMMPS MD engine [35]) coupled to the same
number of cavity normal modes on a 2D spatial grid;
see Fig. 1a for the simulation setup and Supplemental
Material (SM) for simulation details.

Mechanism.
To identify the origin of UP nonlinear
freezing, in Figs.
4a,b we display the nonequilibrium
polariton dispersion relations from the 1D UP pump-
ing simulations at k∥= 450 cm−1 (Figs. 2a,c). Under
weak excitation (Fig. 4a), the UPs near k∥= 450 cm−1
are strongly excited. The mode-resolved photonic energy
dynamics in Fig. 4c, particularly the delayed excitation
of cavity modes at 312.5 ≤k∥≤375 cm−1, indicates
polariton-polariton scattering to neighboring k∥values
[34].
However, cavity loss and UP relaxation to dark
modes prevent the efficient energy transfer to the band
edge (k∥= 0).
Under strong pumping (Fig. 4b), the nonequilibrium
polariton dispersion relation differs significantly. Here,
the UP branch near k∥= 0 becomes meaningfully excited
and broadened with red-shifted frequencies [47]. The cor-
responding mode-resolved photonic energy dynamics in
Fig. 4d also confirm the strong delayed excitation of cav-
ity modes near k∥= 0 (red arrow). Since the polariton
group velocity vanishes at small k∥, this delayed popula-
tion of the k∥≈0 cavity modes is directly responsible for
the nonlinear freezing of the UP propagation observed in
Figs. 1 and 2.
We attribute the physical origin of this enhanced k∥≈
0 UP population to the pump-induced breaking of in-
plane translational symmetry.
Under strong localized
pumping, the effective oscillator strength for the fun-
damental 0 →1 vibrational transition is reduced near
the pulse hot spot due to partial depletion of the vi-
brational ground-state population [47]. In the classical
CavMD picture, the same effect manifests as a reduced
local linear vibrational susceptibility of anharmonically
excited molecules. With the effective oscillator-strength
density denoted by n0→1(x, t), and noting that the col-
lective Rabi frequency ΩR scales as √n0→1, the pulse
pumping generates a spatially localized Rabi contraction
δΩR(x, t) = Ω(0)

Consequently, as demonstrated in Fig. 4f, the excited
UP can scatter its energy to a greater number of polariton
states at progressively smaller k∥, which significantly en-

modes.

bilized by stimulated scattering [54], whereas the present
mechanism here requires neither condensation forma-

The mesoscale CavMD approach [1] was implemented in the open source software package MaxwellLink [2]. The
associated simulation data are available at Github: https://github.com/TaoELi/maxwelllink_examples.
In this work, we performed calculations for simulating both one-dimensional (1D) and two-dimensional (2D) Fabry–
Pérot cavities coupled to liquid-phase molecules. In these simulations, the molecules were evenly distributed along
a 1D or 2D grid, with each grid point representing a single MD simulation cell consisting of Nsimu = 36 explicitly
simulated CO2 molecules under periodic boundary conditions. For each MD simulation cell, the cell length was set to
25.263 a.u. = 13.368 Å, corresponding to a molecular density of 1.101 g/cm−3. The CO2 molecules were modeled by a
non-polarizable, anharmonic force field used in pervious CavMD work [3]. In each MD simulation cell, intermolecular
Coulombic interactions were calculated using the Ewald summation.
