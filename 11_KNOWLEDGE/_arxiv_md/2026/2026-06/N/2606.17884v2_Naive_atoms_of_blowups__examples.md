NAIVE ATOMS OF BLOWUPS: EXAMPLES
CHRISTIAN B¨OHNING, HANS-CHRISTIAN GRAF VON BOTHMER, AND ZAC SU’A
Abstract. We define naive atomic decompositions of smooth projective varieties. We show
that they satisfy a naive version of Iritani’s blowup formula in several examples that are
complicated enough to show most interesting features of the general theory while being
simple enough to be computable by elementary methods.
1. Introduction
The theory of atoms and equivariant atoms [KKPY25, CKK25] is a seminal development
in birational geometry and has already had profound influence on the field [ESS25,BGMP26,
Fay26].
In these foundational articles, the atoms are obtained from the spectrum of the
quantum multiplication by the Euler vector field in the big (algebraic) quantum cohomol-
ogy. Unfortunately, computations in the big quantum cohomology are notoriously difficult in
general. Therefore, one would like to restrict to a computation with the small quantum co-
homology. Quantum multiplication by the Euler vector field then restricts to small quantum
multiplication by the anticanonical bundle. Thus we make
Definition 1.1. For X a smooth projective Fano variety, let (−KX)⋆be the operator, on
the algebraic cohomology of X, of quantum multiplication by the anticanonical bundle in
the small sense. We define the naive atomic decomposition of X as the ordered sequence of
multiplicities of eigenvalues of (−KX)⋆, for generic values of the Novikov parameters.
Moreover, if QH•
alg(X) denotes the small algebraic quantum cohomology algebra, we define
the small atomic decomposition of X as the ordered sequence of lengths of connected compo-
nents of the Artinian scheme Spec QH•
alg(X), for generic values of the Novikov parameters.
The small atomic decomposition is a refinement of the naive atomic decomposition. We
will mostly be concerned with computing naive atomic decompositions, which in most cases
agree with the small atomic decompositions. But in some cases there are subtle differences,
cf. Example 12.11.
In particular, in the notation of [KKPY25], the sequence (ρα)α for α running over the
Hodge atoms of X and ρα the associated dimension of the space of Hodge classes, is in gen-
eral a refinement of the naive atomic decomposition of X (but as we will see also often equal
to it). Here we do not consider additional invariants such as the Hodge polynomial Pα of an
atom α although they are essential in irrationality, and in the equivariant birational setting,

Specifically, we compute the naive atomic decomposition for the following
examples:
a) Smooth cubic fourfolds (with arbitrary rank r of the space of codimension 2 cycles) in
Section 4. Here the naive atomic decomposition is (1 + r, 1, 1, 1).
b) A complete intersection of a quadric and cubic in P5 in Section 9. This is particu-
larly interesting from the point of view of derived categories and Kontsevich’s con-
jectural canonical semiorthogonal decompositions: in this case there are two natural
semiorthogonal decompositions that are conjecturally not in the same mutation orbit,
and we explain how this ambiguity gets resolved from the (naive) atomic point of view.
c) Smooth Picard rank 1 Fano threefolds in Section 10. In all cases the naive atomic
decompositions agree with what is expected from known semiorthogonal decompositions
of the derived categories. Quantum cohomology of these Fanos is a very classical subject
studied by lots of people [Gol07], [Pr06], [Pr07a,Pr07b]
d) Verra fourfolds in Section 11. This is interesting because it is an example with Picard
rank 2 which is not a blowup.
The following variants of Iritani’s blowup formula [Iritani23] in this setting are useful.
Definition 1.2. Let e

a) We say that the naive variant of Iritani’s theorem holds for (X, Z) if the naive atomic

P2 blown up in eight points, the naive and small atomic decomposition of P4 is (1, 1, 1, 1, 1),
that of the Castelnuovo surface consists of eleven ones in both cases, but the blowup Y has
naive decomposition 9 and seven ones. Notice that Y can also be obtained by blowing up
the complete intersection X of two quadrics in P6 in a line. X has naive and small atomic
decomposition (9, 1, 1, 1), and the line has (1, 1). For this blowup the naive variant of Iritani’s
theorem holds. The small variant of Iritani’s theorem cannot hold for both Y →X and
Y →P4.
We write N1(Y, Z) = im(CH1(Y ) →H2(Y, Q)) for a smooth projective variety Y . The
failure of the naive and small variants of Iritani’s theorem in this example seems to be due to
the fact that the inclusion Z ,→X does not induce an embedding N1(Z, Z) ,→N1(X, Z). If
on the contrary one assumes that it does, then we know of no counterexample to the small
variant of Iritani’s theorem. However, the naive version can fail for other reasons, namely
that the naive atomic decompositions do not coincide with the small atomic decompositions
in general, and the small ones appear to be much better behaved, see Example 12.11.

necessary to treat the examples discussed in them.

Definition 2.3. The naive atomic decomposition of X is the ordered sequence of multiplic-
ities of eigenvalues of M(X,−KX) for an assignment of generic values (in C here, say) to the
parameters qβ.
The small atomic decomposition of X is the ordered sequence of lengths of connected com-
ponents of the Artinian scheme Spec QH•
alg(X), for generic values of the Novikov parameters.
Remark 2.4. Hodge atoms α of X as defined in [KKPY25] are highly structured entities,
and the information our naive and small atomic decompositions capture is much coarser. In
particular, their atoms are labelled by the eigenvalues of an operator that is a generisation
of (−KX)⋆, namely, multiplication by the Euler vector field using the big quantum coho-
mology. Compare in particular, the example of a complete intersection of type (2, 2) in P6
in [CKK25, Example 2.13, p. 28] where the naive atomic decomposition is (9, 1, 1, 1), but
quantum multiplication by the Euler vector field has 12 simple eigenvalues [Hu21]. See also
Section 12. Still, naive atomic decompositions are much easier to compute with, and they
also admit a variant for G-varieties, G a finite group, where in addition to multiplicities one
takes dimensions of G-invariants in generalised eigenspaces into account as well.
For a connection between the eigenvalues of M(X,−KX) and the coordinates of singular fibres
of associated Landau–Ginzburg models compare [Pr18,Pr26].
We will also use quantum periods and recursion relations derived from differential equations
expressing the flatness of the quantum connection in Sections 9, 10, 11. We will explain that
approach further in the relevant sections, and refer to [CCGGK14] for general information
about quantum periods and further references to the literature.
3. Naive and small variants of Iritani’s theorem
In view of Iritani’s blowup formula [Iritani23] it is natural to define the following in our
situation.
Definition 3.1. Let e
X be the blowup of X in a smooth codimension c subvariety Z.
a) We say that the naive variant of Iritani’s theorem holds for (X, Z) if the naive atomic
decomposition of e
X is the sequence obtained by concatenating and sorting the naive
atomic decompositions of X and (c −1) times the naive atomic decomposition of Z.
b) We say that the small variant of Iritani’s theorem holds for (X, Z) if the small atomic
decomposition of e
X is the sequence obtained by concatenating and sorting the small
atomic decompositions of X and (c −1) times the small atomic decomposition of Z.
Here it may be safer to assume X, Z and e
X to be Fano to avoid convergence problems for
quantum products, but we ignore these for the time being because they play no role in our
examples and have been addressed in [KKPY25] using nonarchimedean methods.
In subsequent sections we will see that, in the situation of Definition 3.1, the character-
istic polynomials of (−KX)⋆and (−KZ)⋆can be recovered from that of (−K e
X)⋆by some
specialisation procedure in examples, or more precisely, as limits that correspond to taking
specific leading terms. So the characteristic polynomial on the blowup is some generisation
of the corresponding polynomials on X and Z. We consider it as one of the basic miracles of

Remark 12.8. Since Z and P4 have naive atomic decompositions consisting only of 1’s, the

Remark 12.9. In [CKK25, Example 2.13] the naive decomposition for X is also mentioned,
and it is pointed out that the correct atomic decomposition, looking at the eigenvalues of
the big quantum multiplication by the Euler vector field, should consist of 12 atoms with
multiplicity one. This is confirmed in [Hu21].
So for the big quantum cohomology ring Iritanis blowup formula holds since P4, Y and
X all have only atoms with associated spaces of Hodge classes of dimension 1. In the small
quantum cohomology ring of Y there are far fewer deformation parameters (namely, two) than
in P4 and Z together (i.e. 1 + 11). From this point of view, the small quantum cohomology
ring of Y is just a specialisation of the big quantum cohomology ring of Y which is not generic
enough. Geometrically the problem is that the del Pezzo surface Z has a very interesting and
complex cone of curves/Novikov ring of effective curve classes. However, most of these are
just lines when viewed in the ambient P4.
