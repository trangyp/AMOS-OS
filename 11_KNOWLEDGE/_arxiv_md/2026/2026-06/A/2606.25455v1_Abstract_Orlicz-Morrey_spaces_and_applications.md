Abstract
This work introduces a class of abstract Orlicz-Morrey spaces endowed with a ball-basis on
general measure spaces and defines the associated concept of 𝚿-bounded oscillation oper-
ators. Within this framework, we establish pointwise estimates and norm inequalities for
these operators via sparse domination techniques. As applications, we verify that this class
of 𝚿-bounded oscillation operators includes maximal operators and Carleson-type opera-
tors on general measure spaces, as well as 𝝎-Calder´on-Zygmund operators and intrinsic
square operators on R𝒏, thus providing a generalization of certain classical Orlicz-Morrey
spaces and their associated operator theory.
Keywords: abstract Orlicz-Morrey spaces, 𝚿-bounded oscillation operators, maximal operators,
sparse operators, norm estimation.
1 Introduction
To unify the study of diverse operators—including maximal operators, 𝜔-Calder´on-Zygmund
operators, intrinsic square operators, and Carleson-type operators—within a single frame-
work, we develop the theory of abstract Orlicz-Morrey spaces endowed with a ball-basis (cf.
Definition B.1) and introduce Ψ-bounded oscillation operators (Ψ-BOOs, cf. Definition 1.4).

(1) The theory of Orlicz-Morrey function spaces
To our knowledge, multiple definitions exist for Orlicz-Morrey spaces on the underlying
space (R𝑛, L𝑛)—L𝑛being the standard Lebesgue measure—as seen in [12, 27, 39, 55, 66].
A foundational contribution was made by Kokilashvili and Krbec [39], who introduced the
Orlicz-Morrey class in 1991. Subsequent developments on this class appear in [50, 62, 70].
In 2004, Nakai [55] formulated the Orlicz-Morrey space and showed that it generalizes
𝐿𝑝(R𝑛, L𝑛) spaces, Morrey spaces, and Orlicz spaces. Furthermore, he established the bound-
edness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on this space. Further work by Nakai [54]
proved the boundedness of Calder´on-Zygmund operators in Orlicz-Morrey spaces and derived
modular inequalities for Orlicz spaces. Detailed structural properties were later provided in
[57, 58], where Nakai verified that Orlicz-Morrey spaces contain 𝐿𝑝(R𝑛, L𝑛), 𝐿∞(R𝑛, L𝑛),
(generalized) Morrey spaces, and Orlicz spaces; defined weak Orlicz-Morrey spaces; estab-
lished H¨older’s inequality; characterized their predual spaces; and clarified inclusion relations
among different Orlicz-Morrey spaces. Additional relevant results in this theory can be found
in [35, 36, 65, 68, 77, 78].
The theory of Orlicz-Morrey spaces, when developed over a variety of underlying spaces,
has given rise to numerous variants. These include central Orlicz-Morrey spaces [51],
vanishing generalized Orlicz-Morrey spaces [20, 21], Orlicz-Morrey spaces on spaces of
homogeneous type [9, 18, 72], weighted Orlicz-Morrey spaces [36], and local Orlicz-Morrey
spaces [2, 79], among others. For a broader overview of contributions to this theory, we refer
to [9–11, 15, 19, 22–25, 29, 72].

Based on the background discussion above related to the present work, the primary
motivation for our research stems from the following three aspects:
• Recent developments in singular integral operator theory, particularly the trend toward
abstraction in its formulation, continue to drive the evolution of the field. Building
upon the pioneering contribution of Karagulyan [30], the theoretical framework was
subsequently advanced by Cao et al. [4]. Their work provides a robust foundation for
studying other theories on abstract Lebesgue spaces endowed with a ball-basis [33, 34]
and establishes a theoretical groundwork for other types of abstract function spaces
possessing a ball-basis [31, 32, 67, 80]. Inspired by this question of extendibility, our
work aims to generalize these advances to broader classes of function spaces.
• Both historically emerging from distinct mathematical needs, Orlicz and Morrey spaces
were developed to extend and complement the classical Lebesgue framework. The
inception of Morrey spaces by Morrey [52] was driven by problems in elliptic par-
tial differential equations, specifically to quantify the local regularity of their solutions.
Independently, Orlicz spaces found their utility in analyzing the limiting behavior of the
Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator near 𝐿1(R𝑛, L𝑛) [6, 37, 38]. The subsequent unifi-
cation into Orlicz-Morrey spaces represents a synthesis of these ideas, yielding a theory
of considerable breadth and depth, as evidenced by the extensive body of subsequent
research [56–64, 69, 71, 76]. A parallel and equally important aim is the construction
of a rigorous, ball-basis-dependent framework for abstract Orlicz-Morrey spaces, which
we believe would greatly broaden the reach of the classical theory.
• For Calder´on-Zygmund type operators, pointwise bounds and norm estimates are com-
monly used to describe their behavior. Fundamentally, this is because the oscillatory
nature of such operators plays a crucial role. In the Euclidean setting (R𝑛, L𝑛), Lerner’s
foundational work revealed that the technique of local mean oscillation [42] and the
method of sparse domination [46–48] provide powerful tools for controlling Calder´on-
Zygmund type operators. We note that [48, Theorem 4.2] and various forms of 𝐾-type
sparse domination imply that oscillation-type operators must satisfy a weak bound-
edness condition in order to be pointwise dominated by sparse operators. Following

The principal contributions of this study are summarized as follows:
• Our framework of Banach-valued Ψ-BOOs unifies the Orlicz-Morrey maximal operator,
𝜔-Calder´on-Zygmund operators, as well as operators beyond Calder´on-Zygmund theory.
Notably, this framework incorporates the Orlicz-Morrey maximal operator into the theory
of singular integrals, while categorizing the intrinsic square operators and Carleson-type
operators as examples that extend beyond classical Calder´on-Zygmund theory.
• We develop a framework of abstract Orlicz-Morrey spaces endowed with a ball-basis that
is both broad and unifying. This framework admits as special cases a range of abstract
spaces (for example, abstract Orlicz spaces and abstract generalized Morrey spaces; cf.
Appendix B) and also recovers key classical examples. The latter include central Orlicz-
Morrey spaces [51], their counterparts on spaces of homogeneous type [18], and local
Orlicz-Morrey spaces [2]. The generality of the Orlicz-Morrey setting ensures that other
families, such as Orlicz-type spaces and Morrey-type spaces, are also contained within
this framework.
• Our Theorem 1.1 establishes a pointwise estimate (a local characterization) for the
Ψ-BOO in abstract Orlicz-Morrey spaces endowed with a ball-basis. This result is
then used to derive Theorem 1.2. The methodology here differs from classical proofs
[22, 54, 55, 57], as the principal difficulty stems from the lack of geometric structure, such
as the one inherent in (R𝑛, L𝑛), in general measure spaces. Consequently, the operator
boundedness results obtained differ from their classical counterparts. Nevertheless, the
required inequalities are successfully established via Theorems 1.1 and 1.2.
• As an application, we provide several classical examples within Orlicz-Morrey spaces in
Sect. 2: the Orlicz-Morrey maximal operator, 𝜔-Calder´on-Zygmund operators, intrinsic
square operators, and Carleson-type operators. Utilizing the results from Theorem 1.1
and Theorem 1.2, our framework recovers the results presented in [22, 54, 55, 57].

1.4 Structure of the article
The organization of the subsequent material is as follows. Concrete instances of Ψ-BOOs are
developed in Section 2, alongside refinements of select classical theorems, to highlight the
applicability of our principal results. Section 3 commences with a summary of established facts
about the ball-basis and proceeds to examine fundamental properties of Ψ-BOOs, culminating
in the proof of a sparse domination principle for these operators. A supplementary segment
offers elaboration on several concepts and properties employed in Section 3. Appendix A
recalls two significant function classes within Orlicz-Morrey spaces and supplies pertinent
illustrative examples. In Appendix B, we first revisit the conventional Luxemburg norm and
