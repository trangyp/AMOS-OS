KENTA HASHIZUME
Abstract. We establish the generalized canonical bundle formula for generalized
lc-trivial fibrations without the assumption on the nef part in the complex analytic
setting. We also record the corresponding algebraic statement.

1. Introduction
In [Has26], the author proved the following (see [Has26, Theorem 1.1]).
Theorem 1.1. Let π: (X, B + M) →S be a generalized sub-pair with the nef part M,
and let f : (X, B + M) →Z be a generalized lc-trivial fibration over S. Let W ⊂S be
a Stein compact subset. Suppose that
• M is a finite R>0-linear combination of b-nef/S Q-b-Cartier Q-b-divisors on X,
and
• there exists a Zariski open dense subset U ⊂Z such that B|f−1(U) is effective.
Then, after replacing S with a suitable open neighborhood of W, we have the following
properties.
(i) Let G (resp. N) be the discriminant R-b-divisor (resp. the moduli R-b-divisor)
associated to f. Then N is a finite R>0-linear combination of b-nef/S Q-b-Cartier
Q-b-divisors on Z. In particular, (Z, GZ +NZ) →S is a generalized sub-pair with
the nef part N. Furthermore, if (X, B+M) →S is generalized lc (resp. generalized
klt), then (Z, GZ + NZ) →S is also generalized lc (resp. generalized klt).
(ii) For any open subset ˜S ⊂S that does not necessarily contain W, if we put ˜X ⊂X
and ˜Z ⊂Z as the inverse images of ˜S, then the moduli R-b-divisor ˜N associated
to the generalized lc-trivial fibration ( ˜X, B| ˜

X) →˜Z over ˜S satisfies (i).
In particular, we have ˜N = N| ˜Z.

In this note, we remove the assumption on the nef part M in Theorem 1.1.
Theorem 1.2 (Main result, complex analytic setting). Let π: (X, B + M) →S be a
generalized sub-pair with the nef part M, and let f : (X, B + M) →Z be a generalized
lc-trivial fibration over S. Let W ⊂S be a Stein compact subset. Suppose that there
exists a Zariski open dense subset U ⊂Z such that B|f−1(U) is effective. Then, after
replacing S with a suitable open neighborhood of W, we have the following properties.
(i) Let G (resp. N) be the discriminant R-b-divisor (resp. the moduli R-b-divisor)
associated to f. Then N is a b-nef/S R-b-Cartier R-b-divisors on Z. In particular,
(Z, GZ + NZ) →S is a generalized sub-pair with the nef part N. Furthermore, if
(X, B + M) →S is generalized lc (resp. generalized klt), then (Z, GZ + NZ) →S
is also generalized lc (resp. generalized klt).
(ii) For any open subset ˜S ⊂S that does not necessarily contain W, if we put ˜X ⊂X
and ˜Z ⊂Z as the inverse images of ˜S, then the moduli R-b-divisor ˜N associated
to the generalized lc-trivial fibration ( ˜X, B| ˜
X + M| ˜
X) →˜Z over ˜S satisfies (i).
In particular, we have ˜N = N| ˜Z. Moreover, if M is a finite R>0-linear combination of
b-nef/S Q-b-Cartier Q-b-divisors, then so is N.
The following theorem is the corresponding algebraic setting.
Theorem 1.3 (Main result, algebraic setting). Let π: (X, B+M) →S be a generalized
sub-pair over a quasi-projective scheme S with the nef part M. Let f : (X, B +M) →Z
be a generalized lc-trivial fibration over S. Suppose that B|f−1(U) is effective for some
Zariski open subset U ⊂Z. Let G (resp. N) be the discriminant R-b-divisor (resp. the
moduli R-b-divisor) associated to f. Then N is a b-nef/S R-b-Cartier R-b-divisors on
Z, and (Z, GZ + NZ) →S is a generalized sub-pair with the nef part N. Furthermore,
if M is a finite R>0-linear combination of b-nef/S Q-b-Cartier Q-b-divisors, then so is
N. If (X, B +M) →S is generalized lc (resp. generalized klt), then (Z, GZ +NZ) →S
is also generalized lc (resp. generalized klt).
Theorem 1.3 is new and it is a generalization of [FiSv23, Theorem 2.20].

2. Proofs of main results
For the reader’s convenience, we first treat the algebraic setting (Theorem 1.3), and
then we treat the complex analytic setting (Theorem 1.2).
We refer the reader to
[FiSv23, Definition 2.19] and [Has26, Definition 3.1] for the definition of the generalized
canonical bundle formula. Although [FiSv23, Definition 2.19] is stated in a setting with
rational coefficients, the same definition applies to arbitrary generalized pairs (see also
[Has26, Definition 3.1]).

Remark 2.2. We emphasize that all the statements in [Has26, Section 3] are necessary
to prove Theorem 1.2. Indeed, to prove Theorem 1.2 we need Theorem 1.1 for M = 0.
Since we do not use the variations of mixed Hodge structures in [Has26], even in the

formula for generalized klt-trivial fibration, and then we need to extend it to the case
of generalized lc-trivial fibrations as in Theorem 1.1.
Finally, we provide a supplement to define the canonical b-divisors and the moduli
R-b-divisors associated to generalized lc-trivial fibrations ([Has26, Definition 3.1]).
Theorem 2.3. Let π: X →S be a projective morphism from a normal analytic variety
X to a complex analytic space S, and let W ⊂S be a Stein compact subset. Then, after
shrinking S around W suitably, we can globally define the canonical b-divisor K on
X, which is a Z-b-divisor whose traces are the canonical divisors. In other words, for
any proper bimeromorphic morphism X′ →X from a normal analytic variety X′, the
canonical divisor KX′ on X′ is well defined as a Weil divisor on X′, and furthermore, we
can define these canonical divisors so that for any two proper bimeromorphic morphisms
X1 →X and X2 →X such that the induced bimeromorphic map ϕ: X1 99K X2 is a
bimeromorphic contraction, we have ϕ∗KX1 = KX2 as Weil divisors on X2.
Proof. Let f : Y →X be a resolution of X. By shrinking S around W, we may assume
that S is Stein, π ◦f : Y →S is projective, and there exists a (π ◦f)-very ample divisor
A on Y that defines an embedding of Y into S × PN for some N ([EH25, Lemma 2.2]).
From now on, we do not shrink S anymore.
