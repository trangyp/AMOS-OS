ABSTRACT
We present the results of an effort to derive interstellar gas-phase C II abundances along the lines of
sight toward ρ Oph A and B. Our analysis is based on high-resolution NUV and FUV archival spectra
acquired with the Space Telescope Imaging Spectrograph on the Hubble Space Telescope. Column
densities of C II are derived both from fits to the weak C II] λ2325 intersystem transition and from fits
to the damping wings of the strong C II λ1334 line. We find that the results from the weak-line and
strong-line determinations agree with each other remarkably well for both sight lines, demonstrating
the reliability of the f-values of the C II transitions. Furthermore, the gas-phase C abundance that we
obtain for ρ Oph A is in very good agreement with previous determinations of interstellar C abundances
from measurements of the weak C II] λ2325 line. By demonstrating the reliability of the damping wing
fitting technique for the C II λ1334 line, our analysis opens the door to future surveys of interstellar
C abundances using the same methodology.

1. INTRODUCTION
The gas-phase interstellar carbon abundance is a cru-
cial parameter that bears on the ionization balance in
neutral diffuse interstellar clouds and on the abundance
of carbonaceous dust grains in the interstellar medium
(ISM). Determinations of interstellar C II abundances
(C+ being the dominant ionization state of C in diffuse
clouds) have typically followed one of three approaches.
The most secure determinations are derived from obser-
vations of the weak C II] λ2325 intersystem transition
in absorption against background stars (J. A. Cardelli
et al. 1996; U. J. Sofia et al. 2004).
This line is op-
tically thin along diffuse and translucent sight lines so
that the column density is directly proportional to the
equivalent width.
However, the line is typically ex-
tremely weak (with equivalent widths of ∼1−2 mÅ or
less) and therefore requires high resolution, very high
signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio NUV spectra to be detected.
Another method for deriving C II abundances involves
fitting the damping wings of the strong, typically heavily
saturated C II λ1334 transition (U. J. Sofia et al. 2011;
V. S. Parvathi et al. 2012). However, this approach can
yield unreliable results if the underlying continuum is

obscure the Lorentzian damping wings of the main low-

et al. 2015; C. J. R. Clark et al. 2025). In this case, un-
certainties arise from the assumptions being made about
the excitation conditions in the interstellar gas and from
attempts to disentangle absorption and emission along
complicated lines of sight.
Prior to 2011, most measurements of interstellar C
abundances were derived using the weak intersystem
transition at 2325.4 Å. With the exception of a few out-
liers, these earlier measurements are generally consistent
with an average gas-phase carbon abundance of C/H
∼1.6 × 10−4 (e.g., U. J. Sofia et al. 2004). However,
the total number of sight lines with secure detections
of the weak C II] λ2325 feature is small (∼13) and the
prospects for expanding the sample are limited by the
need for high-resolution, very high S/N ratio NUV spec-
tra, which generally require long exposure times with
the only instrument currently capable of obtaining such
data, the Space Telescope Imaging Spectrograph (STIS)
on the Hubble Space Telescope (HST). Thus, U. J. Sofia
et al. (2011) explored using the strong C II λ1334 tran-
sition to derive C abundances via fits to the Lorentzian

2. OBSERVATIONS AND DATA PROCESSING
HST/STIS observations of ρ Oph A and B were ob-
tained in 2015 under program GO 13365 (PI: Martin
Cordiner). The observations employed both the E140H
grating, with the wavelength setting at 1271 Å, and
the E230H grating, with the wavelength setting at 2263
Å, allowing coverage of both C II transitions at high-
resolution (R ∼120, 000). The sight lines to ρ Oph A
and B were chosen for this study because (1) the com-
ponent structure along the two lines of sight is relatively
simple, with ∼90% of the column density in the ‘main’
low-velocity component (as determined from the weak
O I λ1355 line), (2) the total hydrogen column density
is quite large (1021.7 cm−2 toward ρ Oph A), meaning
that the weak C II λ2325 line is easily detectable toward
both ρ Oph A and ρ Oph B, and (3) the stars are part of
the same binary system, which means we can compare
column densities from the strong and weak C II transi-
tions for two closely spaced sight lines as an additional
check on the accuracy of our results.

3. COLUMN DENSITY DETERMINATIONS
3.1. Comparison of Oscillator Strengths
As an explanation of the disparate results on C II col-
umn densities derived from the strong and weak tran-
sitions, U. J. Sofia et al. (2011) suggested that the os-
cillator strength of the weak intersystem line may not
be accurate. However, this suggestion is not supported
by a review of C II oscillator strengths reported in
the literature. D. C. Morton (2003) adopts a value of
log fλ = −3.954 ± 0.003 for the C II λ2325 transition.
This value (and its small uncertainty) is derived from
the experimental results of E. Trabert et al. (1999), who
measured precise intercombination transition rates us-
ing a heavy-ion storage ring. Other experimental and
theoretical determinations (e.g., S. J. Smith et al. 1999;
G. Tachiev & C. Froese Fischer 2000; G. Corrégé &
A. Hibbert 2002) agree with the oscillator strength from
E. Trabert et al. (1999) at the 5% level (see Table 1).
Thus, in light of the good agreement among the var-
ious experimental and theoretical determinations, the
f-value of the C II λ2325 transition appears to be well
established.
As a dipole-allowed transition, the strong C II λ1334
line is generally regarded as having a well-determined
oscillator strength.

Y. Yan et al. (1987) yield a value of log fλ = 2.233
for the λ1334 transition. (This is essentially the value

3.2. Weak-line Measurements
Our analysis of the C II λ2325 features toward ρ Oph
A and ρ Oph B followed the methodology we have
adopted in previous analyses of weak interstellar absorp-
tion lines (A. M. Ritchey et al. 2011, 2018). Small seg-
ments of spectra (∼2 Å wide) centered on the C II λ2325
(and O I λ1355) lines were isolated and then continuum-
normalized by fitting low-order polynomials to regions
free of interstellar absorption. The S/N ratio (per pixel)
near the C II λ2325 lines is ∼90 for both sight lines. As
anticipated, the C II λ2325 (and O I λ1355) absorption
profiles are nearly identical for the two closely spaced
sight lines.5 We find equivalent widths of Wλ = 1.9±0.5
mÅ for the weak C II line in both directions.
The
O I λ1355 equivalent widths are 15.8±0.2 mÅ for ρ Oph
A and 16.2 ± 0.3 mÅ for ρ Oph B.
Despite the relatively high S/N of the NUV STIS spec-
tra, the weakness of the C II absorption features makes
it diﬀicult to discern the detailed component structure
in the line profiles. Thus, we use the stronger intersys-
tem transition of O I at 1355.6 Å to construct a profile
template for C II λ2325. Since both O0 and C+ are dom-
inant ions, and are typically only lightly depleted into in-

Previous efforts to derive C II abundances from fits to
the damping wings (U. J. Sofia et al. 2011; V. S. Par-
vathi et al. 2012) employed synthetic stellar models to
fit the continuum near the interstellar C II lines. While
such an approach may be appealing from a theoretical
standpoint, the problem is that the synthetic spectrum
invariably does not provide a perfect match to the ac-
tual observed spectrum. The continuum fitting proce-
dure adopted in U. J. Sofia et al. (2011), for example,
required seven free parameters to fine-tune the modelled
continuum, and even then there are cases where the syn-
thetic spectrum does not appear to match the observed
spectrum particularly well.
For our analysis, we adopt a more direct approach to
fitting the continuum. First, we isolate the interstellar
C II λ1334 line within a small spectral window with a
width of 2 Å. (A width of 2 Å at 1334.5 Å corresponds
to a velocity width of 450 km s−1.) We then fit a low-
order polynomial to regions very far from the line and
well outside the obvious damping wings. (Essentially,
we choose continuum points at the very edges of the
spectral window.) For both sight lines, we found that a
first-order cubic spline function provided a good repre-
sentation of the underlying continuum. Our continuum
fits for the C II λ1334 lines toward ρ Oph A and B
are shown in Figure 2. We also explored how changes
in the order of the polynomial, and differences in the
placement of the continuum points, affected the derived
column densities.
These alternate continuum fits are
also shown in Figure 2. For ρ Oph A, the alterations in
the fitting parameters did not significantly change the
resulting continuum fit. For ρ Oph B, however, subtle
differences in the structure of the noise in the continuum
regions led to more significant variations in the alternate
continuum fits.
Once the data were continuum-normalized, we used
our multi-component Voigt profile fitting routine IS-
MOD to fit the C II λ1334 absorption lines.6 For the
ρ Oph A and B sight lines, the damping wings are dom-
inated by a single low-velocity absorption component
(i.e., the ‘main’ component seen in the O I λ1355 pro-
file).
However, excess absorption on the blue side of
the C II profile requires that additional low column den-
sity components be included in the fit. The component
structure cannot be directly evaluated using the heav-
ily saturated C II profile. Thus, we used a combination
of the O I λ1355 line, which probes the highest column
density components, and the S II λ1250 line, which is
useful for examining the relative strengths of the lower
column density components at higher velocity. An ini-
tial prediction for the component structure in the C II
line was obtained from the results of ISMOD fits to the
O I and S II profiles (adjusting for differences in the
cosmic abundances of the elements and in the expected
amount of dust depletion).

Our Voigt profile fits to the C II λ1334 lines toward
ρ Oph A and B are presented in Figure 3. The total
C II column densities derived from these fits are given
in Table 2. There are two major sources of uncertainty
in the column densities derived from the strong C II
transition. First, there are the statistical uncertainties
in the measured intensities that affect the Voigt profile
fit. The quality of the fit may be judged by the degree to
which the damping wings from the synthetic absorption
profile match the observed spectrum. To examine the
effect of statistical uncertainties, we increased and de-
creased the total column density of the synthetic profile
to match the fluctuations in the intensity points in the
wings of the profile. In particular, the wing regions clos-
est to the core of the line are the most important in the
fitting process. Small changes in the synthetic profile in
these regions lead to large variations in the fit residuals.
For both sight lines, we found that the allowed range
in column density due to statistical uncertainties corre-
sponded to ±0.06 dex. (This range in column density is
indicated by the dotted red lines in Figure 3.)
In addition to the statistical uncertainties, there are
systematic effects related to the placement of the con-
tinuum. This is particularly important for strong-line
determinations where the strength of the damping wings
can be greatly impacted by the choice of continuum. For
each of the alternate continuum fits described above (see
Figure 2), we employed the same Voigt profile fitting
procedure as for our nominal continuum fit to derive
the total C II column density. We then evaluated the
standard deviation of the resulting values. For ρ Oph
A, the range of plausible continuum fits was small and
the resulting spread in the derived column densities was
±0.02 dex. For ρ Oph B, the variation in continuum
fits was larger and the spread in column densities corre-
sponded to ±0.06 dex. These systematic uncertainties
were added in quadrature with the statistical uncertain-
ties to arrive at the values given in Table 2.
4. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS
The main conclusion from our analysis is that the C II
column densities derived from the weak C II λ2325 line
and the strong C II λ1334 feature agree with each other
remarkably well for both sight lines toward ρ Oph A
and B (Table 2). Furthermore, the column densities we
find toward ρ Oph A are indistinguishable from those
toward ρ Oph B, as would be expected given the binary
nature of the targets and the similarity in the absorp-

tion profiles. We also note that the uncertainties asso-
ciated with the strong-line determinations are smaller
than those from the weak-line measurements, indicating
that, in cases where the damping wings are clearly vis-
ible, Voigt profile fitting of the C II λ1334 feature can
be a reliable method for deriving C II abundances.
Taking the weighted mean of the results from the
strong-line and weak-line determinations, we find total
C II column densities of log N(C II) = 17.95 ± 0.06 for
ρ Oph A and 17.94±0.07 for ρ Oph B.7 The atomic and
molecular hydrogen column densities toward ρ Oph A
are log N(H I) = 21.63 ± 0.09 (A. Diplas & B. D. Sav-
age 1994) and log N(H2) = 20.57 ± 0.15 (R. C. Bohlin
et al. 1978), yielding a total hydrogen column density
of log N(Htot) = 21.70 ± 0.08. While published column
densities of H I and H2 are not available for ρ Oph B,
presumably they would be very similar to those toward
ρ Oph A. Indeed, our own analysis of the H I Lyα ab-
sorption features from the available STIS spectra yield
column densities of log N(H I) = 21.60 ± 0.03 for ρ Oph
A and 21.61 ± 0.03 for ρ Oph B. These results are con-
sistent not only with each other but also with the H I
column density from A. Diplas & B. D. Savage (1994).
There is no direct measurement of N(H2) toward ρ Oph
B. However, the similarity in the column density of CH
(and CN) toward ρ Oph A and B (K. Pan et al. 2004)
suggests that the H2 column density would also be sim-
ilar in the two directions.
Adopting the previously published column densities
of H I and H2 toward ρ Oph A, and our derived value
for the C II column density, we find a gas-phase car-
bon abundance of C/H = (1.77 ± 0.46) × 10−4. While
some of the C in diffuse clouds exists in the form of C0
and CO, these species do not contribute meaningfully
to the total gas-phase C abundance toward ρ Oph A.
J. Zsargó et al. (1997) find a total C I column density
toward ρ Oph A of log N(C I) = 15.52 ± 0.01, while
S. R. Federman et al. (2003) report a CO column den-
sity of log N(CO) = 15.28 ± 0.05. The combined con-
tributions from C I and CO are thus 0.6% of the total
C column density (which corresponds to a logarithmic
increase of 0.003 dex).
The dust-phase C abundance
toward ρ Oph A may be obtained by subtracting the
gas-phase abundance from an assumed total interstellar
abundance.

column densities from analyses of the λ1334 and λ2325
transitions toward ρ Oph A and B, demonstrating that
there is no tension between the f-values of these transi-
tions. Moreover, as shown in Table 1, there is consensus
among theoretical and experimental efforts that the os-
cillator strengths of the C II transitions are secure.
The C II column densities derived by V. S. Parvathi et
al. (2012) using the λ1334 transition span a much larger
range than those of most earlier efforts. Their determi-
nations of gas-phase C abundances, which incorporate
the results of U. J. Sofia et al. (2011), span the range
from C/H = 69 ppm to 464 ppm, with an average of 201
ppm and a standard deviation of 116 ppm. The likely
reason for the large spread in derived C abundances is
the considerable systematic uncertainties involved in ob-
taining C II column densities from the λ1334 transition.
We chose to study ρ Oph A and B because the line-of-
sight component structure is very simple and the damp-
ing wings in the λ1334 line are easy to identify and to
fit. Many sight lines are not so well-suited for this type
of analysis. In many directions, there are weaker C II
absorption components at higher velocities that tend to
obscure the damping wings from the main low-velocity
component. This appears to be the case for many of
the sight lines analyzed by V. S. Parvathi et al. (2012,
see their Figure 2).
While fits to the damping wings
of the C II λ1334 line can be a reliable method for ob-
taining gas-phase C abundances, the sight lines must be
carefully chosen to avoid these uncertainties.

In summary, we examined high-resolution NUV and
FUV HST/STIS spectra for the sight lines toward ρ Oph
A and B in an effort to derive C II abundances from
both the weak C II λ2325 intersystem transition and
the strongly saturated C II λ1334 line. Our main find-
ings are that the C II column densities derived from the
two transitions agree with each other remarkably well
for both sight lines, demonstrating that the oscillator
strengths of the two C II transitions are reliable and
well established. The C abundance obtained for the line
of sight to ρ Oph A is consistent with many previous
determinations of C abundances derived from the weak
intersystem transition, providing further clarity on the
gas-phase abundance of C in the ISM. By demonstrat-
ing the reliability of the damping wing fitting technique
for analyses of the C II λ1334 line, our analysis opens
the door to future surveys of interstellar C abundances
using the same methodology, with the caveat that the
sample of sight lines must be carefully selected to avoid
the systematic uncertainties that seem to have plagued
previous investigations.
