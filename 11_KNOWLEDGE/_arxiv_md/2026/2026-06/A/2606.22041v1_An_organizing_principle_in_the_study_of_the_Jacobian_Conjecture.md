An organizing principle in the study of the
Jacobian Conjecture
Frederico Xavier
Abstract
Let Ωbe an irreducible component of the locus of polynomial maps F : Cn →Cn
satisfying degF ≤k and detDF = 1. It is shown that either Ω⊂Aut(Cn), as
claimed by the Jacobian Conjecture, or the general F ∈Ωis not an automorphism.

Introduction
The Jacobian Conjecture (JC) claims that every polynomial map F : Cn →Cn satis-
fying detDF = 1 is invertible [1, 2]. Let Pk
n be the vector space of all polynomial maps
F = (F1, . . . , Fn) : Cn →Cn with degF

Our goal is to investigate JC at the finer level of a fixed irreducible component Ωof V k

Theorem 1.1. Either every F ∈Ωis invertible or the general F ∈Ωis non-invertible.
Thus, either JC holds in Ωor almost every F ∈Ωis actually a counterexample of JC.
As injective polynomial maps Cn →Cn are bijective (the Ax-Grothendieck theorem),
one obtains the following probabilistic criterion to resolve the ambiguity in the dichotomy:
Almost surely, the correct alternative in Thm.1.1 is the first one if and only if for a
random choice of (F, c) ∈Ω× Cn the solution of F(z) = c in Cn exists and is unique.
JC has been verified for several classes of maps with special properties (e.g. quadratic
maps [3]), but all attempts at establishing it in full generality were found to be faulty.
The fact that Thm.1.1 is a dichotomy puts this history in perspective and opens up the
possibility that maybe JC fails for some pair (V k
n , Ω). It is conceivable that an application
of the above criterion to all components of all V k
n may lead to counterexamples. The
silver lining in such a difficult task is that one only needs for the algorithm to reach the
conjectural “right”Ω, as the counterexamples would then be nearly “impossible”to miss.

Related results
Regarding the problem in real algebraic geometry of understanding when a polynomial
map G : Rn →Rn can be inverted, in [5] we use ideas from nonlinear optimization and the
Tarski-Seidenberg theorem to establish the existence of a subset ker(G) of Rn satisfying:
i) ker(G) is semialgebraic.
ii) 0 ∈ker(G) and ker(G) = {0} if and only if G is invertible.
iii) If G is linear then ker(G) is the usual kernel.
An earlier version of these results, valid for polynomial local diffeomorphisms, can be
found in [4]. For analytic, topological, and geometric mechanisms of global inversion of
maps in the smooth category, we refer to the semi-expository paper [6] and the references
therein.
