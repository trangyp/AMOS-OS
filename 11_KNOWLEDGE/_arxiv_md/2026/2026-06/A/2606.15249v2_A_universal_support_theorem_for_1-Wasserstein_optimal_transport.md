A universal support theorem for 1-Wasserstein optimal transport
Ng Ze-An
Abstract
We show that every optimal transport plan for the 1-Wasserstein distance in Rn is supported on a
closed n + 1 rectifiable set. In particular, the support of any optimal plan has Hausdorff dimension at
most n + 1.
1
Introduction
The optimal transport problem, first investigated by Monge and later extended by Kantorovich, asks for
the most efficient way to transport a prescribed mass distribution onto a target distribution with respect to
some cost functional. In its modern formulation, it takes the following form.
Let µ, ν be probability measures on Rn. A coupling π of µ and ν is a probability measures π on Rn × Rn
whose marginals on the first and second factor equal µ and ν respectively - that is, π(E × Rn) = µ(E) and
π(Rn × E) = ν(E) for all Borel subsets E of Rn.
Let c : Rn × Rn →R+ be some non-negative cost function. The Monge-Kantorovich optimal transport
problem in its general form asks to minimize
Z
Rn×Rn c(x, y) dπ(x, y)
over all couplings π of µ, ν. Such couplings π are customarily denoted as transport plans.
Of all cost functions, the most fundamental and well studied are the Wasserstein costs c(x, y) := d(x, y)p,
with 1 ≤p ≤∞. When p = 1, the cost is just the distance function, which was the original cost investigated
by Monge in early formulations of the problem. The resulting metric on probability measures
W1(µ, ν) :=

(1)

measures η whose first moment

is finite. We refer to the book [1] by Maggi for an excellent overview of the general theory, including a
detailed analysis of the 1-Wasserstein transport problem.
In this note, we prove a support theorem for optimal plans for the optimal transport problem (1) with respect
to the distance cost.
Our main result, Theorem 1 is that the support of any optimal plan between measures in P1(Rn) is n + 1
rectifiable, in the sense of geometric measure theory. This is a strong form of geometric regularity which
implies, in particular that the support of any optimal plan has Hausdorff dimension at most n + 1. Because
transport plans live in Rn × Rn, the Hausdorff dimension can a priori be up to 2n. Thus the main result
shows that optimal transport plans are supported on a much thinner set than the ambient space.
Due to Kantorovich duality, the analysis of optimal transport plans for the 1-Wasserstein distance is inti-
mately tied with the geometry of 1-Lipschitz functions. The proof of the main Theorem 1 thus relies crucially
on a theorem in the fine analysis of Lipschitz functions, Theorem 2, which may be of independent interest.

Main Theorems
Theorem 1 (Support theorem). Let π be an optimal transport plan between µ, ν ∈P1(Rn) for the 1-
Wasserstein distance on Rn. Then the support of π is contained within a closed n + 1 rectifiable set, which
can be taken to be the stretch set of any associated Kantorovich potential. In particular, the support of π has
Hausdorff dimension at most n + 1.
We recall that as a consequence of Kantorovich duality, to every optimal plan π there is associated a 1-
Lipschitz Kantorovich potential f : Rn →R such that π(Γ(f)) = 1 ([1], Theorem 3.17). Theorem 1 is thus
an immediate consequence of the following general theorem on Lipschitz functions.
Theorem 2 (Rectifiability of the stretch set). Let f : Rn →R be a Lipschitz function. Then the stretch set
Γ(f) is closed and n + 1 rectifiable.
Proof of Theorem 1 assuming Theorem 2. Let π be an optimal transport map for the 1-Wasserstein distance.
By Kantorovich duality, choose any Kantorovich potential f such that π(Γ(f)) = 1. Since Γ(f) is closed,
we have that supp(π) ⊆Γ(f). Both claims then follow immediately from Theorem 2.
The rest of this section will thus be dedicated to proving Theorem 2. We first make an important pair of
definitions.

Further Questions
We conclude with some natural further directions for research. We note that although the example in Section
4 shows that the upper bound cannot be improved in general, it does not preclude the existence of optimal
transport plans with lower dimensional supports. It is natural to ask whether under sufficient regularity of
the source and target measures there exists at least some lower dimensional optimal transport plan. We thus
pose:
Question 1: Let µ = f Ln, ν = g Ln be absolutely continuous probability measures on Rn with smooth,
compactly supported densities f, g. Does there exist an optimal transport plan π with dimH(supp(π)) ≤n?
Noting the key role of Theorem 2, we are led to ask the following natural generalization, which is itself of
intrinsic interest but may also be of some future value in the theory of vector valued optimal transport.
Question 2: Let f : Rn →Rm be a Lipschitz map, for n > m. Is it true that the stretch set of f is n + m
rectifiable?
