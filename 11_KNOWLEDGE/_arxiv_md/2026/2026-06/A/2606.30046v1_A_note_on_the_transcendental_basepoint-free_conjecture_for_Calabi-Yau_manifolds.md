A NOTE ON THE TRANSCENDENTAL BASEPOINT-FREE
CONJECTURE FOR CALABI-YAU MANIFOLDS
BASTIEN PHILIPPE
Abstract. In this note, we prove that the transcendental basepoint-free conjecture for
Calabi-Yau manifolds holds if it holds for its hyperkähler factors in its Beauville-Bogomolov
decomposition. Based on a contraction theorem due to Bakker and Lehn, we show that the
conjecture holds for a big and nef class α on a hyperkähler manifold under a mild condition
on the dimension of the space generated by classes of rational curves on which α vanishes.
1. Introduction
Let X be a compact Kähler manifold. A class α ∈H1,1(X, R) is said to be semi-ample
if there exists a holomorphic contraction ρ : X →X′ where X′ is a normal analytic space
together with a Kähler class ω ∈H1,1
BC(X′) such that ρ∗ω = α. The goal of this note is to
study the following conjecture due to Tosatti.
Conjecture 1.1 ([Tos09, Conjecture 6.1], [Tos25, Conjecture 2.7]). Let X be a Calabi-Yau
manifold and let α ∈H1,1(X, R) be a big and nef class. Then α is semi-ample.
Here, the term Calabi-Yau manifold refers to a compact Kähler manifold X such that
c1(KX) = 0 ∈H1,1(X, R). The above conjecture can be seen as a special case of a conjectural
generalization to the transcendental setting of Kawamata’s basepoint-free theorem and can
also be formulated for non-Calabi-Yau manifolds, see for example [FT18, Conjecture 1.2]. In
the case where X is projective and α is the class of a big and nef R-divisor, Conjecture 1.1
follows from the usual basepoint-free theorem ([BCHM10, Theorem 3.9.1]). Conjecture 1.1 is
also known when dim(X) ⩽3 ([FT18, Hör21]) and when X is projective ([DH24]).
Our first result is that Conjecture 1.1 can be reduced to the case of hyperkähler manifolds.

Let us now discuss the proofs of these results. The proof of Proposition 1.2 relies on the
following observations :
• for tori, Conjecture 1.1 is automatically satisfied as every big and nef class is Kähler ;
• Calabi-Yau manfiolds carry no global holomorphic 2-forms, thus they are always pro-
jective and every (1, 1) class is the class of a R-divisor. Conjecture 1.1 thus follows
from the basepoint-free theorem for R-divisors on projective manifolds ([BCHM10,
Theorem 3.9.1]).
In particular, Proposition 1.2 amounts to showing that Conjecture 1.1 is stable under product
and finite étale morphism. This is to content of Section 3.1. Let us point out that in [Hör21]
Höring has treated the case of K-trivial threefolds with at most terminal singularities, also
by using the Beauville-Bogomolov decomposition. In his proof, he reduces to the case of a
product of an elliptic curve with a K3 surface and obtains an explicit description as a product
of the null locus associated to the corresponding big and nef class to show that the contraction
descends under the quasi-étale map. Our proof differs from his in this aspect.
To prove Proposition 1.3, we first observe that the assumption implies that α lies inside
the interior of the positive cone with respect to the Beauville-Bogomolov form. We then use
the description of the Kähler cone obtained by Huybrechts ([Huy03]) and Boucksom ([Bou01])
together with the boundedness of squares of MBM classes ([AV17]) to conclude that α belongs
to a face of the nef cone of X defined as the orthogonal of a set of rational curves on X. We
then use a contraction theorem of Bakker and Lehn ([BL21]) stating that, provided that the
dimension of this face isn’t too low, there exists a contraction morphism contracting precisely
the curves defining the face. Finally, using techniques from the transcendental minimal model
program (especially Lemma 2.4 and Lemma 2.7), we show that there exists a Kähler class
on the contracted variety pulling back to α. This last part addresses a point made in [BL21]
concerning the refined period mapping attached to the locally trivial marked moduli space of
primitive symplectic varieties admitting crepant resolutions, see Remark 3.6.
We end this introduction with a few comments on the assumption in Proposition 1.3. First,
the maximal possible dimension for N is b2(X) −3. This bound is however attained ; there
are examples of contractions ρ : X →X′ where X is a hyperkähler manifold and X′ is Kähler
with b2(X′) = 3 ([BL21, Remark 5.10]). The assumption is needed in order to apply [AV17]
and [BL21], both of which ultimately relying on the deep work of Verbitsky on the orbits of the
action of monodromy groups on moduli spaces of marked hyperkähler manifolds and associated
period domains ([Ver15, Ver17]). The contraction used in Proposition 1.3 is constructed in
[BL21] as a small deformation of a contraction on a projective manifold, where the last one is
produced by applying the usual basepoint-free theorem. It would be interesting to see if this
approach consisting of obtaining the desired contraction as a deformation of one on a projective
manifold could be carried further to fully show Conjecture 1.1. Obtaining the analogue of the
surjectivity of the period map in [Huy99, Theorem 8.1] for the locally trivial marked moduli

a certain "Kähler type" chamber decomposition containing φ(K¨ah(X)) ([BL21, Proposition
5.21]). They however note that their proof doesn’t imply that the chamber in question coincides
with the image of the Kähler cone of the variety. The proof of Proposition 1.3 implies that
this is indeed the case ; the chambers of the Kähler type decomposition do correspond to the
Kähler cones of the parametrized varieties, making the situation completely analogous to that
of hyperkähler manifolds ([Mar11, Theorem 5.16]).
