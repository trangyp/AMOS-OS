ALGEBRAIC COBORDISM RINGS OF WONDERFUL VARIETIES AND MATROIDS
RAJ GANDHI AND ETHAN PARTIDA
Abstract. We give two combinatorial presentations for the algebraic cobordism ring Ω∗(M) of the toric
variety of the Bergman fan of any loopless matroid M. As a consequence of our presentations, we obtain
an Ω∗(pt)-algebra isomorphism Ω∗(M) ≃CH∗(M) ⊗Z Ω∗(pt), where CH∗(M) is the Chow ring of M and
Ω∗(pt) is the algebraic cobordism ring of the point. This isomorphism generalizes, in part, the exceptional
integral isomorphism between the Chow ring and K-ring of a matroid, studied in the recent works of Berget–
Eur–Spink–Tseng and Larson–Li–Payne–Proudfoot. For a complex hyperplane arrangement H, we prove
that the algebraic cobordism ring of the wonderful variety WH of H and the algebraic cobordism ring of the
toric variety of the matroid underlying H are isomorphic, and that both rings coincide with the complex
cobordism ring of WH.

1. Introduction
Let H be an arrangement of hyperplanes in a complex vector space L whose intersection is the origin in L.
The arrangement H gives rise to a loopless matroid MH whose flats are indexed by the various intersections
of hyperplanes in H. De Concini and Procesi defined a smooth compactification WH of the complement of
the hyperplane arrangement in L, known as the wonderful compactification of H [10], whose boundary is
a simple normal crossings divisor. Much of the geometry of WH is dictated by invariants of the matroid
MH. As WH is a smooth, projective variety, such invariants of MH must satisfy strong structural properties.
Sometimes these properties can be given a purely combinatorial explanation. This, in turn, lets one prove
new structural results about matroids, even those not realizable over any field.

Given a loopless matroid M, there is a (usually non-compact) toric variety XM canonically associated to
M whose Chow ring CH∗(XM) has an explicit description [16]. Feichtner and Yuzvinsky proved that when
MH is the matroid of a complex hyperplane arrangement H, there is a canonical inclusion WH ,→XMH
which induces an isomorphism of Chow rings CH∗(XMH) ≃CH∗(WH) [16]. As the cycle class map is an
isomorphism for WH (Lemma 5.4), this gives the Chow ring of XMH the structure of the cohomology ring of
a smooth, complex, projective variety. An important consequence of this is that CH∗(XMH) has the K¨ahler
package [15]. Remarkably, Adiprasito–Huh–Katz proved that CH∗(XM) has the K¨ahler package for every
loopless matroid M, regardless of whether or not M is realizable [1]. This result is a key step in their proof
of the Heron–Rota–Welsh conjecture [1].
In recent work [27], Larson–Li–Payne–Proudfoot studied the Grothendieck ring of algebraic vector bundles
on WH, which we denote by K0(WH) (following the notation of Levine and Morel [29]), and refer to as
the K-ring of WH.
They proved that the inclusion WH ,→XMH induces an isomorphism of K-rings
K0(XMH) ≃K0(WH).
The authors also proved that, for any loopless matroid M, the K-ring of XM
admits structures such as Adams operations, an Euler characteristic map, and Serre duality. Surprisingly,
they also showed that the Chow ring and K-ring of XM are integrally isomorphic. This isomorphism is known
as an exceptional isomorphism in the literature and was first proven in the Boolean case by Berget–Eur–
Spink–Tseng [5]. The exceptional isomorphism is not directly related to the Chern character isomorphism
between the rational K-ring and the rational Chow ring of WH.
The functors CH∗(−) and K0(−) which send a smooth complex variety to its Chow ring and K-ring
are examples of oriented cohomology theories (Section 3.2). There is a universal oriented cohomology the-
ory Ω∗(−) called algebraic cobordism constructed by Levine and Morel [29]. Levine and Morel’s result is
an analogue of the fundamental observation of Quillen that complex cobordism MU∗(−) is the universal
complex-oriented cohomology theory [30]. For a smooth complex variety X, there is a natural morphism
Ω∗(X) →MU ∗(X) which is an isomorphism when X is a point, but is not an isomorphism in general
(Lemma 3.15). For any free oriented cohomology theory h∗(−), of which CH∗(−) and K0(−) are examples,
the natural morphism Ω∗(X) →h∗(X) is surjective and h∗(X) can be recovered from Ω∗(X) (Lemma 3.20).
Computations of algebraic cobordism rings are generally very difficult and explicit ring presentations are
uncommon. Some of the few families of varieties for which presentations of their algebraic cobordism rings
are known are smooth toric varieties [25], complete flag varieties [21, 7], Pfister quadrics [37], and wonderful
symmetric varieties of minimal rank [23]. We refer the reader to Section 3 for an introduction to algebraic
cobordism.
In this paper, we study the algebraic cobordism rings of wonderful compactifications WH and toric
varieties of Bergman fans of loopless matroids XM. Our first result is a “simplicial” presentation of the
ring Ω∗(XM). In this presentation, the generators of Ω∗(XM) are lifts of the “simplicial generators” for
CH∗(XM), introduced by Backman–Eur–Simpson [3], to the algebraic cobordism ring of XM. We derive
the simplicial presentation from careful manipulations of the universal formal group laws that appear in the
presentation of Ω∗(XM) deduced from Krishna and Uma’s description of the algebraic cobordism ring of a
smooth toric variety [25]. In the following, we use pt = Spec(C) and refer the reader to Sections 2 and 3 for
undefined terminology.

2.1. Matroids and hyperplane arrangements. We will recall the definition of a matroid, and describe

Definition 2.1. Let E be a finite set. A loopless matroid M on the ground set E is a collection F of
subsets of E called flats that satisfy:
• ∅∈F.
• If G, H ∈F, then G ∩H ∈F.

For two flats G and H, their meet G ∧H is the intersection G ∩H and their join G ∨H is the smallest flat
containing G ∪H. Two flats H and G are called incomparable if H ̸⊆G and G ̸⊆H. If H and G are not
incomparable, then they are comparable. When ordered by inclusion, the set F forms a geometric lattice.
The rank rk(F) of a flat F is equal to the length of the largest chain of comparable flats properly contained
in F. An atom is a flat of rank 1. It follows from the axioms that E ∈F. Let r be the rank of E. In this
case, we say that M has rank r. The corank of a flat G of M is the number r −rk(G).

2.3. Toric varieties and Bergman fans. Let N be a lattice of rank n and NR be the n-dimensional vector
space NR := N ⊗Z R. A k-dimensional, rational, simplicial cone σ ⊂NR is a subset that can be written as
σ = cone{v1, . . . , vk} := {λ1v1 + . . . + λkvk : λi ≥0} ⊂NR
where v1, . . . , vk are k linearly independent vectors in N. A cone τ is a face of σ if it can be written as
τ = cone{vi1, . . . , viℓ} for some subset {vi1, . . . , viℓ} ⊂{v1, . . . , vk}. We declare cone(∅) = {0} so that {0} is
a face of every cone. A rational, simplicial fan Σ in NR is a non-empty collection of rational, simplicial
cones σ ∈Σ in NR such that
• If σ ∈Σ and τ is a face of σ, then τ ∈Σ, and
• Every two cones σ, τ ∈Σ intersect along a common face.
We say that Σ is complete if the union of all of its cones is equal to the whole vector space NR. Let Σ(1)
denote the set of rays, i.e. one dimensional cones, of Σ. For a ray ρ ∈Σ(1), the primitive ray generator
νρ ∈N of ρ is the generator of the semigroup N ∩ρ. We say that Σ is unimodular if, for all cones σ ∈Σ,
the set of primitive ray generators of σ can be extended to a basis of N.
Let T be an n-dimensional complex torus, and let N = Hom(C∗, T) be its lattice of one-parameter
subgroups. Denote the lattice of characters Hom(T, C∗) of T by N ∨. A rational fan Σ in NR gives rise to a
toric variety XΣ with torus T. There is a rich dictionary between the combinatorics of Σ and the geometry
of XΣ. We refer the reader to the textbook [19] for details.

3.1. Formal group laws. Formal group laws are an important technical tool in the study of oriented
cohomology theories. We explain in Section 3.2 that every oriented cohomology theory comes equipped with
a formal group law which governs how the first Chern class of the tensor product of two line bundles on a
smooth complex variety can be expressed in terms of the first Chern classes of the individual line bundles.

as we explain in Lemma 3.2. In this subsection, we review the basic properties of formal group laws that we
need in this article, closely following the exposition in [18].
