A CLASS OF II1 FACTORS WITHOUT NON-TRIVIAL
CROSSED PRODUCT DECOMPOSITIONS
ADRIANA FERN´ANDEZ QUERO, ADRIAN IOANA, AND HUI TAN
Abstract. We introduce a class of separable II1 factors M admitting no non-trivial crossed product
decompositions: M ̸∼= B ⋊σ G, for any trace preserving action G ↷σ (B, τ) of an infinite countable
group G on a tracial von Neumann algebra (B, τ). These provide the first examples of II1 factors
that do not arise as crossed products of noncommutative dynamical systems. Our approach relies
on a novel construction of separable II1 factors M whose embeddings into their tensor product
square M⊗M all arise from the canonical embeddings x 7→x ⊗1 and x 7→1 ⊗x.
1. Introduction
1.1. Background and motivation. The crossed product construction is a central tool in the
theory of von Neumann algebras, providing a powerful connection between operator algebras and
dynamics. Every trace preserving action G ↷σ (B, τ) of a countable discrete group G on a tracial
von Neumann algebra (B, τ) gives rise to the crossed product von Neumann algebra B ⋊σ G. This
construction was introduced by Murray and von Neumann in their seminal articles [MvN36, MvN43]
in the cases B = C and, more generally, B = L∞(X), for a probability space (X, µ). In these cases,
one obtains the group von Neumann algebra L(G) and the group measure space von Neumann
algebra L∞(X) ⋊σ G, respectively. The construction was later extended in [Tur58, NT58, Suz59]
to arbitrary trace preserving actions G ↷σ (B, τ). The resulting algebra B ⋊σ G is again tracial
and is often a II1 factor, e.g., if G is icc and σ is ergodic. A fundamental problem is to understand
to what extent general II1 factors can be realized through the crossed product construction.
The main goal of this paper is to give a definitive negative answer to this problem by constructing
the first class of separable II1 factors admitting no non-trivial crossed product decompositions.
Over the past five decades, this problem has received considerable attention and led to major
advances. In the mid 1970s, Connes obtained the first examples of type II1 factors [Con75] (see
also [Jon80]) that are not isomorphic to group or group measure space von Neumann algebras. His
argument shows that the factors M studied, unlike group and group measure space von Neumann
algebras, have no anti-automorphisms, i.e., M ̸∼= Mop. Two decades later, Voiculescu [Voi95] used
free entropy theory to prove that the free group factors L(Fn), for 2 ≤n ≤∞, have no Cartan
subalgebras (see [Pop83b] for the case of uncountable free groups).
Moreover, he showed that
L(Fn) has no regular diffuse hyperfinite von Neumann subalgebras (equivalently, no regular diffuse
amenable subalgebras by [Con76]). Consequently, L(Fn) cannot be realized as a crossed product of
the form B ⋊σ G, with B diffuse and amenable.
Since the early 2000s, Popa’s deformation/rigidity theory has led to remarkable progress in the
study of crossed product II1 factors (see the surveys [Pop07, Vae10a, Ioa18]). We highlight several
advances most relevant to the present paper, which dramatically expanded the list of examples
of II1 factors lacking various types of crossed product decompositions.

non-amenable II1 factor can be written as B ⋊σ G, with B = L(Fn) a free group factor.
1.2. Absence of crossed product decompositions. We settle this problem by introducing a
class of II1 factors possessing only trivial crossed product decompositions. If P, Q are II1 factors,
then P stably embeds into Q [PV21] if there exists an embedding (i.e., a unital ∗-homomorphism)
θ : P →Qt, for some t > 0, where Qt is the amplification of Q by t. If this holds when t = 1, we
simply say that P embeds into Q.
Theorem A. There exists a separable II1 factor M such that M ̸∼= B⋊σG, for any trace preserving
action G ↷σ (B, τ) of any countable infinite group G on any tracial von Neumann algebra (B, τ).
Moreover, there exist 2ℵ0 pairwise non-stably embeddable separable II1 factors with this property.
Before discussing the proof of Theorem A, we make several remarks on its statement.
Remark 1.1.
(1) The conclusion of Theorem A does not hold without restricting to infinite
groups. For a finite group G, we have M|G|(C) ∼= B(ℓ2(G)) = ℓ∞(G) ⋊λ G, where λ denotes
the left regular representation of G. Hence, every II1 factor M decomposes trivially as a
crossed product by a finite group G: (⋆) M ∼= M|G|(C)⊗M1/|G| ∼= (ℓ∞(G)⊗M1/|G|)⋊λ⊗IdG.
(2) The II1 factors M from Theorem A satisfy that any embedding θ : M →Mt, t > 0, is
trivial, see Corollary D(1). This allows us to improve Theorem A and additionally show the
following: if M = B ⋊σ G, for a trace preserving action σ of a finite group G, then σ is of a
specific form which generalizes (⋆) and only depends on G (see Theorem 2.18). This yields
the first class of II1 factors for which crossed product decompositions can be completely
classified, exhibiting a new form of superrigidity for II1 factors.

e.g., if G is a non-discrete SIN group acting trivially on a tracial von Neumann algebra B.
However, if B ⋊σ G is a II1 factor, then G must be discrete, see Lemma 2.19. Hence, if M
is a II1 factor as in Theorem A, then M ̸∼= B ⋊σ G, for any continuous action G ↷σ B of an

1.3. Rigidity for embeddings. The proof of Theorem A follows the general strategy for studying
crossed product decompositions of II1 factors introduced in [Ioa10, IPV10]. For a II1 factor M, any
decomposition M = B ⋊σ G gives rise to an embedding ∆: M →M⊗M [PV09], defined by
∆(bug) = bug ⊗ug, for all b ∈B and g ∈G.
Note that ∆is a comultiplication, i.e., an embedding which is co-associative in the sense that
(∆⊗Id) ◦∆= (Id ⊗∆) ◦∆. One then aims to classify such embeddings M ,→M⊗M. Ideally, this
classification is precise enough to determine all triples (B, G, σ) such that M = B ⋊σ G.
This strategy has been applied to great effect to study group and group measure space von Neumann
algebra decompositions of II1 factors. It was used in [Ioa10] to prove that Bernoulli actions of icc
property (T) groups are W∗-superrigid. The same strategy was employed in [IPV10] to give the first
examples of countable icc groups which are W∗-superrigid and in all subsequent works providing
new classes of W∗-superrigid groups (see, e.g., [BV12, CI17, CDD21, CDD20, CIOS21, DV24]).
These results were obtained by classifying the comultiplication embeddings ∆: M →M⊗M
associated to the group or group measure space von Neumann decompositions M = B ⋊σ G of
the II1 factors M studied. Such classifications are possible when B is trivial or abelian, in which
case ∆behaves analogously to the diagonal embedding, being coarse [DV24] in the following sense:
L2(M⊗M) is coarse as both ∆(M)-(M⊗1) and ∆(M)-(1⊗M) bimodules.
However, ∆is coarse if and only if B is amenable, and therefore this approach does not apply
when B is nonamenable. Moreover, it is generally believed that classifying arbitrary embeddings
M ,→M⊗M is out of reach (see, e.g., [DV25]). As such, while general embeddings M ,→M
have been classified for several classes of II1 factors M [Ioa10, Dep13, PV21, CIOS24], not a single
example of a II1 factor M for which all embeddings M ,→M⊗M are understood is currently
known.

1.4. Applications. The proof of Theorem B can be used to strengthen Theorem A by allowing
amplifications, twisting by scalar 2-cocycles, and groupoid actions. Given a trace preserving action
G ↷σ (B, τ) of a countable group G on a tracial von Neumann algebra (B, τ) and a 2-cocycle
c ∈Z2(G, T) one obtains the twisted crossed product von Neumann algebra B ⋊σ,c G.
More
generally, any trace preserving action G ↷σ B of a discrete pmp groupoid G on a measurable field
of tracial von Neumann algebras B = (Bx, τx)x∈G(0) together with a 2-cocycle c ∈Z2(G, T) yields
the twisted crossed product von Neumann algebra B ⋊σ,c G (see, e.g., [Yam93, Cha24]). When
B = C and B = (C)x∈G(0), these reduce to the twisted group von Neumann algebra Lc(G) and the
twisted groupoid von Neumann algebra Lc(G), respectively.
Examples of II1 factors N not isomorphic to twisted group von Neumann algebras were found
in [Ioa10, Corollary G], which further showed that no corner pNp has this form. Only recently,
[DV24, Theorem B] gave examples of II1 factors N none of whose amplifications Nt is isomorphic
to a group von Neumann algebra or, more generally, to a twisted groupoid von Neumann algebra.
We strengthen these results by providing II1 factors whose amplifications cannot be realized as a
twisted crossed product B ⋊σ,c G, for any action of an infinite groupoid and any scalar 2-cocycle.

1.5. A strengthening of Popa’s coarseness theorem. We now turn to the principal technical
contribution of the paper. Although different in flavor from the previous results, we highlight it
here because of its independent interest. Recall that a subfactor P of a II1 factor M is coarse if the
P-bimodule L2(M)⊖L2(P) is isomorphic to a subbimodule of a multiple of the coarse P-bimodule,
L2(P) ⊗L2(P). A striking theorem of Popa [Pop18b] shows that every separable II1 factor M
contains a coarse copy of the hyperfinite II1 factor R.
The proof of Theorem B relies on the following strengthening of Popa’s coarseness theorem.
Theorem F. Let M be a separable II1 factor and (N, τ) a separable tracial von Neumann algebra.
Let H be a separable M-M⊗N-bimodule such that the M-N-bimodule MH1⊗N is left weakly mixing.
Let K ⊂H be the closed subspace of vectors ξ ∈H such that xξ = ξ(x ⊗1), for every x ∈M.
Then there is a hyperfinite subfactor R ⊂M such that the R-R⊗N-bimodule H⊖sp(RK) is coarse.
Here, sp(RK) denotes the ∥· ∥2-closure of the linear span of RK. We note that sp(RK) is naturally
an R-R⊗N-bimodule, as used implicitly in Theorem F. In fact, one can show that sp(RK) is
isomorphic, as an R-R⊗N-bimodule, to L2(R) ⊗L, for some right N-module L.
For a more general version of Theorem F, which treats additional types of bimodules needed in
the proof of Theorem B, see Theorem 6.1. Their proof follows Popa’s influential idea from [Pop81,
Pop83a] of recursively constructing a copy of the hyperfinite II1 factor R inside a given factor as the
inductive limit of finite dimensional subalgebras that increasingly satisfy certain desired properties.
This strategy has since found many applications, including in the proof of the coarseness theorem
in [Pop18b] and [Pop19].
The assumptions in Theorem F are necessary. First, the separability assumption on H cannot be
dropped. Indeed, the conclusion fails if H is the direct sum, over all hyperfinite subfactors R ⊂M,
of the M-M⊗N-bimodules L2(⟨M, eR⟩) ⊗L2(N). Second, since H has to be assumed separable, M
and N must also be separable whenever H is a faithful right M⊗N-module. Finally, the conclusion

1.6. Comments on the proof of Theorem B. We end the introduction with an outline of the
proof of Theorem B. For simplicity, we explain the construction in the case M = MN := ⊗n∈NM.
The factors M arise as amalgamated free products M = M1 ∗R M2, where M1, M2 are II1 factors
with property (T) (for instance, M1 = L(PSLm(Z)) and M2 = L(PSLn(Z)), for m, n ≥3) and R is
the hyperfinite II1 factor.
An amalgamated free product of this form does not, in general, satisfy Theorem B. The key point is
that the conclusion does hold for suitably chosen embeddings of R into M1 and M2. More precisely,
we construct coarse copies of the hyperfinite II1 factor R1 ⊂M1 and R2 ⊂M2 for which Theorem
B holds for M1 ∗R1=R2 M2, whenever R1, R2 are identified via an isomorphism R1 ∼= R2.
