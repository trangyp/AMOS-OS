A NOTE ON O-MINIMAL TROPICALIZATIONS
LORENZO BALDI† AND MÁTÉ L. TELEK‡
Abstract. In this note, we provide several equivalent descriptions of the tropicalization of definable sets in
a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field. We show that the tropicalization can be
described in multiple ways: as the image under the valuation map, as the image under the tropicalization map of
the archimedean points in the o-minimal spectrum, in terms of initial degenerations of the definable set and initial
nonnegativity cones, or via the real tropicalization of polynomials belonging to the corresponding nonnegativity
cone. Moreover, we prove analogous results for the fine tropicalization of definable sets, where the valuation map
is replaced by the RV sort map, which records not only the valuation of a point but also its angular component.
1. Introduction
The origins of tropical geometry can be traced back to the work of Bergman [Ber71] and Viro [Vir83].
Since their pioneering work, the theory has developed in several directions and has found applications
in both pure and applied mathematics. Tropical geometry studies algebraic varieties (or more generally,
definable sets) via their degenerations, often referred to as their polyhedral shadows. These polyhedral
shadows are typically easier to work with than their algebraic counterparts, while still retaining significant
information about the original objects. For example, they have been used to compute Gromov–Witten
invariants [Mik05], study toric compactifications of algebraic varieties [Tev07], and construct real algebraic
varieties with prescribed topology [Vir08; BLR24; RRS25]. On the applied side, tropical geometry has been
used to determine the generic number of solutions of parametrized polynomial systems [HR25; Fel+26],
solve such systems via homotopy continuation methods [HS95; LY19; HHR24], and compute Feynman
integrals [BMT23].
One of the ways to define the tropicalization Trop(X) of a set X ⊂(F \ {0})n, where F is a valued field, is
as the closure, in the euclidean topology of Rn, of the image of X under the coordinate-wise valuation map.
While this definition is straightforward, it is convenient to have other equivalent descriptions of the points
in Trop(X), that are often better suited for understanding how properties of X translate to properties of
Trop(X), and vice versa. When F is algebraically closed and X is an algebraic set, the Fundamental Theorem
of Tropical Algebraic Geometry gives these equivalent descriptions for Trop(X), which can be characterized
in terms of the initial degenerations of X, of the Berkovich analytification of X, or of the tropicalizations of
all polynomials vanishing on X (see [MS15, Th. 3.2.3], [Dra08, Th. 4.2], and [EKL06]). These results rely on
the algebraically closedness of the ground field, but can be adapted to give interesting information also for
algebraic sets defined over a real closed field, see e.g. [SW05; BLS23].
However, this approach is not sufficient if one wants to take into account the order of the real closed field,
which makes possible to define semialgebraic sets (or more generally, definable sets in an o-minimal structure)
in addition to algebraic sets. This necessity led to the works [DY07] (for the special case of polyhedra) and
[Ale13], where the tropicalization of semialgebraic sets is systematically studied for the first time. Since
then, this topic has attracted an increasing attention, see e.g. [AGS20; Tel24; Ble+25]. In particular, in
their seminal paper, Jell, Scheiderer, and Yu established an analogue of the Fundamental Theorem in the
semialgebraic setting [JSY20, Th. 6.9].
In this note, we show that this theorem can be generalized to definable sets in polynomially bounded
o-minimal structures. While retaining the tameness properties of semialgebraic sets, definable sets in
polynomially bounded o-minimal structures are considerably more general, as they include for instance
globally subanalytic sets, see e.g. [DM96], and allows the use of real exponents for monomial functions, with
potential applications e.g. to the study of irrational toric varieties [PSV15].

2.1. Real closed fields and valuations. A real field is a field K that admits an ordering ≤, that is, a total
order ≤on K that is compatible with the field operations: for all a,b,c ∈K, we have
a ≤b ⇒a + c ≤b + c,

For an ordered field (K,≤), we write K>0 for the set of elements a ∈K with a > 0, and define an absolute value
as |a| B max{a,−a}. A field K is real closed if it admits a unique ordering and every odd degree polynomial
with coefficients in K has a root in K. We refer the reader to [BPR07, Sec. 2.2] or [Sch24, Sec. 1.2] for other
equivalent characterizations. The inclusion K ⊂F is an extension of real closed fields if K and F are real
closed fields and the ordering on F , when restricted to K, coincides with the ordering on K.

2.2. Hyperfields. Hyperfields are generalizations of fields, where addition can be a multivalued map.
Their study goes back at least to the work of Krasner [Kra60; Kra83], and their first appearance in tropical
geometry occurred in works of Viro [Vir10; Vir11]. In this section, we recall some background on hyperfields
that is necessary for Section 3.2. Our exposition closely follows [MS24].
Definition 2.2.1. A hyperfield is a tuple (H,⊕,⊙,0,1) where H× B H \ {0}, (H×,⊙,1) is an abelian group, and

2.3. Model theory and o-minimal geometry. Both of our main theorems (Theorems A and B) concern
definable sets in a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field. In this subsection, we
recall the related necessary notions from model theory, following [Dri98; Mar02].
Definition 2.3.1. A structure on a nonempty set R is a sequence S = (Sn)n∈N such that, for each n ∈N:
• Sn is a boolean algebra of subsets of Rn, i.e. a collection of subsets of Rn containing ∅such that, if
A,B ∈Sn, then A ∪B ∈Sn and Rn \ A ∈Sn;
• if A ∈Sn, then A × R ∈Sn+1 and R × A ∈Sn+1;
• {(a1,...,an) ∈Rn | a1 = an } ∈Sn;
• if π: Rn+1 →Rn is the projection onto the first n coordinates and A ∈Sn+1, then π(A) ∈Sn.
Given a structure S on R, we say that A ⊂Rn is definable if A ∈Sn. We say that a function is definable if its
graph is definable.

We are interested in sets equipped with an ordering, such as real closed fields and, more generally, ordered
groups. A (linearly) ordered set (R,<) is called dense if for all a,b ∈R with a < b, there exists c ∈R such
that a < c < b. We say that R has no endpoints if R has no smallest or largest element. Following the French
tradition, we denote by ]a,b[ the open intervals in R, and by [a,b] the closed intervals. For all n ∈N, we
equip Rn with the box topology, i.e. the topology generated by the open sets of the form ]a1,b1[×···×]an,bn[.
In case R is a real closed field, we also call the box topology the strong topology, as usual.
Definition 2.3.2. Let (R,<) be a dense linearly ordered nonempty set without endpoints. We call a structure
S on (R,<) order-minimal (or o-minimal) if
• {(a,b) ∈R2 | a < b} ∈S2;
• the sets in S1 are exactly finite unions of (possibly unbounded) intervals and points.
A structure S on a set R is usually introduced specifying relations and functions that "generate" S. For
instance, the structure over a real closed field K generated by addition, multiplication, and by K (identified
with the set of functions from K0 = {0} to K) will consist exactly of semialgebraic sets.
Definition 2.3.3. A model-theoretic structure R = (R,(Ri)i∈I,(fj)j∈J) consists of a nonempty set R, of relations
Ri ⊂Rm(i) for i ∈I and m(i) ∈N, and of functions fj : Rn(j) →R for j ∈J and n(j) ∈N. Given a model-
theoretic structure R on R, we denote Def(R) to be the smallest structure on R that contains each relation
and the graph of each function in R. Moreover, for a subset C ⊂R, we denote RC the model-theoretic
structure obtained by adding to the functions of R the constant functions c: R0 →R for c ∈C. A set A is
definable in R with parameters from C, or simply C-definable, if it is definable in RC.
