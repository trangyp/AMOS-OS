AMITSUR GROUPS OF PRIMITIVE FANO THREEFOLDS
SHREYA SHARMA
Abstract. We classify possible Amitsur groups of smooth primitive Fano three-
folds defined over C that admit a faithful action of a finite group. We also classify
the Amitsur groups for toric Fano threefolds.
1. Introduction
Let X be a proper variety over C with a faithful action of a finite group G,
and L be a line bundle on X. We say L is G-invariant if there are isomorphisms
ϕg ∶g∗L →L for all g ∈G. If the isomorphisms {ϕg}g∈G can be chosen such that
ϕgh = ϕh ○h∗(ϕg) for all g,h ∈G, then we say L is G-linearizable. In other words,
we say L is G-linearizable if the G-action on X lifts to a fiber-wise linear action on
the total space of L. A particular choice of this lift is called a linearization of L.
A morphism of G-linearizable line bundles is a morphism of line bundles that is
equivariant as a map on their total spaces. We denote the group of isomorphism
classes of G-invariant line bundles on X by Pic(X)G and the group of isomorphism
classes of G-linearizable line bundles with a choice of linearization by Pic(X,G).
Clearly, every G-linearizable line bundle is also G-invariant, so there is a forgetful
group homomorphism, Pic(X,G) →Pic(X)G.
The Amitsur subgroup measures the failure of line bundles to be G-linearizable.
More precisely, given a line bundle L ∈Pic(X)G, we can construct a cohomology
class, ∂(L) ∈H2(G,C×); see [BCDP23, §A]) that gives the exact sequence of abelian
groups
(1.1)
1 →Hom(G,C×) →Pic(X,G)
ϵÐ→Pic(X)G ∂Ð→H2(G,C×).
The Amitsur subgroup is defined as the image of the map ∂,
Am(X,G) ∶= Im(∂).
From (1.1), we note that this group is isomorphic to coker(Pic(X,G) →Pic(X)G).
In this article, we will be mainly concerned with the isomorphism classes of G-
linearized line bundles rather than the embedding into H2(G,C×), therefore, we
will refer the Amitsur subgroup as the Amitsur group.
The Amitsur group has been shown to be an equivariant birational invariant in
[BCDP23], where the authors use it to distinguish conjugacy classes of finite sub-
groups of Cr3(C). It provides an obstruction to equivariant versions of rationality,
such as linearizability; see for example, [CTZ25, Lemma 2.2]. Dolgachev determines
the Amitsur groups for smooth curves in [Dol99]. In [BCDP23, A.7], the authors

A Fano threefold is imprimitive if it is isomorphic to the blow-up of a Fano
threefold along an irreducible smooth curve, otherwise it is primitive. By Mori-
Mukai classification, the Picard rank ρ of a smooth primitive Fano threefold is at
most 3 [MM81]. There are 17 deformation families of smooth Fano threefolds with
ρ = 1, also called prime Fano threefolds, and there are 13 deformation families of
smooth primitive Fano threefolds with ρ > 1.
The main objective of this article is to classify the possible Amitsur groups of
smooth primitive Fano threefolds. In [DS25, §6], the authors provide a method for
classifying the Amitsur groups of a smooth projective toric variety; see also [KT25a].
As an application, we also give a classification of possible Amitsur groups of smooth
toric Fano threefolds.

2. Preliminaries
2.1. Notation and Conventions. We work over the field of complex numbers C.
For a finite group G, we say X is a G-variety if G acts on X by morphisms. A
morphism (or, a rational map) ϕ ∶X →Y of G-varieties X and Y is said to be
G-equivariant if ϕ(gx) = gϕ(x) for all g ∈G and x ∈X.
