ANISOTROPIC 2D FUP AND QUANTUM OPEN BAKER’S MAP
LONG JIN, AN ZHANG AND HONG ZHANG
Abstract. We prove an essential spectral gap for 2D anisotropic quantum open
baker’s map. This extends the 1D results of Dyatlov–Jin [DJ17] and the isotropic
2D results of Cohen [Coh25a]. The key ingredient is the anisotropic discrete fractal
uncertainty principle (FUP) associated with a 2D anisotropic fractal set called the
Bedford–McMullen carpet. We also study the relation between our anisotropic dis-
crete FUP and its continuous counterpart in the spirit of Dyatlov–Jin [DJ18a] and
[Coh25a]. In particular, we prove continuous FUP for 2D anisotropic porous sets,
extending the (high-dimensional) isotropic results of Cohen [Coh25b]. To the best of
our knowledge, the anisotropic (line) porosity condition—a variant of Cohen’s line
porosity and stronger than ball porosity—appears to be new to the literature.

1. Introduction
The study of quantum chaos seeks to understand how chaotic classical dynamics
manifest in quantum mechanical properties. For open quantum chaotic systems, it is
expected that the uniform hyperbolicity of the trapped set leads to a positive essential
spectral gap for the imaginary part of the scattering resonances (see, e.g., [Zwo17,
Conjecture 3]). This has been confirmed for convex cocompact hyperbolic surfaces
by Bourgain–Dyatlov [BD18], for two-dimensional Gaspard–Rice system (see [GR89])
by Vacossin [Vac24], and for negatively curved asymptotically hyperbolic surfaces by
Tao [Tao24]. These results rely on the one-dimensional fractal uncertainty principle
(FUP), which was proposed by Dyatlov–Zahl [DZ16]. For higher-dimensional models,
the recent breakthrough by Cohen [Coh25b] on the FUP in higher dimensions implies
a positive essential spectral gap for a large family of convex cocompact hyperbolic
manifolds (see Tao [Tao25] and the authors [JZZ26]). However, in general, an open
quantum chaotic system in higher dimensions (e.g., 3D Gaspard–Rice model) exhibits
anisotropic properties, meaning that there are different Lyapunov exponents along
different expanding directions. It seems that previous works on FUP are not directly
applicable to such systems.
In this paper, we prove an essential spectral gap for a discrete model, namely the 2D
anisotropic quantum open baker’s map whose trapped set is the Bedford–McMullen
carpet [McM84], which is the simplest anisotropic fractal set, having different scaling
properties in different directions. This extends the one-dimensional results of Dyatlov–
Jin [DJ17] and the isotropic version of Cohen [Coh25a] in two dimensions.

1.3. Background and related works. Earlier studies of essential spectral gap for
open quantum chaotic systems usually assume the pressure condition, which means
the trapped set is “small” in some sense, see e.g. Nonnenmacher-Zworski [NZ09]. The
first breakthrough was given by Dyatlov–Zahl [DZ16] who first proved positive essen-
tial spectral gap for some specific convex cocompact hyperbolic surfaces for which the
pressure conditions fails. They reduced the spectral gap problem for convex cocom-
pact hyperbolic manifolds to a fractal uncertainty principle (FUP): a nonzero function
cannot be supported on one fractal set and have its Fourier transform supported on
another fractal set. A subsequent milestone paper by Bourgain–Dyatlov [BD18] proved
the first and now-famous continuous FUP in one dimension, which was used to obtain
the spectral gap for all convex cocompact hyperbolic surfaces. This continuous FUP
has found another striking application in closed quantum chaotic systems, see, e.g.,
[DJ18b], [DJN22] and [ADM25].
The high-dimensional theory of FUP is more challenging. Han–Schlag [HS20] proved
a continuous FUP where the physical set is δ-regular and the Fourier support is a
special Cartesian product of regular sets, using damping functions. Cladek–Tao [CT21]
proved an FUP for regular sets in odd dimensions with δ near d/2, using additive energy
estimates. Backus–Leng–Tao [BLT25] proved a high-dimensional FUP via Dolgopyat’s
method for regular sets with δ < d/2, avoiding certain orthogonal cases; this extends
the one-dimensional result of [DJ18a]. Recently, Cohen [Coh25b] established a general
FUP in high dimensions for sets exhibiting porosity along lines, using a quantitative
higher-dimensional Beurling–Malliavin theorem.
Open quantum maps are discrete-time models of open quantum chaotic systems,
which are attractive due to their simplicity in both theoretical and numerical aspects
(see Nonnenmacher’s review paper [Non11] for mathematical results in open quan-
tum chaos, and Novaes [Nov13] for the physics literature on open quantum maps).
Nonnenmacher–Zworski [NZ07] first studied the essential spectral gap problem for
Walsh-quantized open baker’s maps. Dyatlov–Jin [DJ17] gave the first systematic the-
oretical analysis of Fourier-quantized open baker’s map using the FUP. In particular,
they established the discrete FUP in 1D and proved a positive quantitative essential
spectral gap for all parameters. For further studies of 1D discrete FUP, see [EH23],

1.4. Structure of the paper. Section 2 is devoted to the proof of Theorem 1.1,
establishing the existence of a spectral gap β > 0 for the anisotropic quantum open
baker’s map. We use propagation of singularities to reduce the spectral gap problem
to an anisotropic discrete FUP, which is the main Theorem 1.2. Section 3 is devoted
to the anisotropic discrete FUP under the condition that the two alphabets do not
simultaneously have a full row in one alphabet and a full column in the other. A key
role is played by an anisotropic version of a line support lemma derived from a number-
theoretic result counting cyclotomic points on algebraic curves, due to Ruppert and
Beukers–Smyth. We prove Theorem 1.2 by combining the line support lemma with the
key observation that, due to its anisotropy, the limiting Cantor set X either contains
no line (implying a separation from lines) or contains only horizontal or vertical lines.
In the final Section 4, we focus on the anisotropic continuous FUPs.
The first
subsection 4.1 introduces a continuous FUP where both the fractal sets and the Fourier
transform are anisotropic; if such FUP holds, it implies Theorem 1.2 (see Theorem 4.1).
The key tool for this implication is that the discrete FUP can be controlled by the
standard Fourier transform on R2 for translated separated sets, with arbitrarily small
errors. It is unfortunate that we can not prove such continuous FUP, and we do not
even know whether this FUP is true in general.
Section 4.2 provides the proof of
a continuous FUP for anisotropic fractal sets but using the (isotropic) semiclassical
Fourier transform. We introduce a novel anisotropic porosity (Definition 4.7), use the
Weyl quantization and a metaplectic operator to perform normalization, and apply
symbol calculus associated to a coisotropic space to prove the almost-orthogonality of
piecewise cutoff operators. The theorem is then proved by combining these ingredients
with the porosity of the projection set and a one-dimensional FUP. This follows the
work of [DJ24], which considers semiclassical measures for quantum cat maps in high
dimensions. In Section 4.3, we prove a rescaled FUP which implies a reflected FUP
involving anisotropic semiclassical Fourier transform and Bedford-McMullen carpets.
