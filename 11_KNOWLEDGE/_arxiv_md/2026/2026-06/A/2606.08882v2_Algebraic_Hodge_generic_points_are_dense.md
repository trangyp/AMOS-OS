Algebraic Hodge generic points are dense
Gregorio Baldi, Gal Binyamini, and David Urbanik
July 7, 2026
Abstract
Let f : X Ñ S be a quasi-projective family of varieties defined over Q Ă C. We show
that the points of SpQq that are Hodge generic for the variation of Hodge structures
associated to f are analytically dense in SpCq. In fact, in the spirit of the Grothendieck
period conjecture and under a large monodromy assumption, we prove the density of
the points of SpQq where the periods of the fibre do not satisfy extra relations “up
to degree δ”. As a by-product, we also establish new instances of the Mumford-Tate
conjecture, beyond the realm of abelian motives. When the base S is a curve, we
provide quantitative estimates for points satisfying these properties.
The main technical contribution is a new result on relations satisfied by solutions of
G-operators, which relies on height estimates due to Bombieri and André.

Algebraic Hodge generic points
Let S be a smooth complex algebraic variety. Given a graded polarizable (rational) variation
of mixed Hodge structures (referred to as a QVHS from now on) V on S there is a notion

All QVHSs appearing in our paper will be derived from the natural QVHSs Rjf˚Q through
some combination of passing to primitive cohomology, taking direct sums, tensor powers,
and extensions. (For an introduction to the primitive cohomology we refer to Voisin’s book

part will be denoted Vprim.). We refer to §6.1 for more details (especially about the mixed
case) and the notion of K-geometric VHS.
Since Hodge theory is at its core a transcendental theory, understanding its properties
over number fields can be challenging. The main result of the paper is the following, which
was previously known only in the pure case and under the absolute Hodge conjecture, cf.
[51, Thm. 1.11]. (See also Section 2.4 for a more detailed discussion of related work.)
Theorem 1.3. Let f : X Ñ S be a quasi-projective family defined over a number field K.
Fix an integer j ě 0, and set V “ Rjf˚Q to be the associated graded polarizable QVHS (see
§6.1.2). Then the locus of Hodge generic points in SpQq for V is analytically dense in SpCq.
Put differently, the locus of points in SpQq that do not lie in the (tensorial) Hodge locus of V
is analytically dense in SpCq.

the idea of considering a curve in a self-product of the base appears also in the work of

generalizations of Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem, they study relations among special values
of G-functions.

necessarily a point at which there are no extra algebraic cycles. It is moreover known by the
Cattani-Deligne-Kaplan theorem [19] that the condition of carrying an extra Hodge class
gives rise to a countable union of closed algebraic subschemes of S, called the Hodge locus.
But because a Hodge structure is a transcendental object constructed from periods, it is
difficult to certify for any given point s P SpCq that s does not lie in the Hodge locus. In
particular if f is defined over Q, one would like to know that there are points in SpQq that
do not lie in the Hodge locus. Theorem 1.3 result shows this is the case.

Related work
The most common approach to the existence of Hodge generic points is via the “easy direction”
of the Mumford-Tate conjecture: roughly, the statement that Hodge cycles associated to
a cohomology group HjpXs, Qq above a point s P SpKq should constrain the image of
corresponding ℓ-adic Galois representations ρs : GalpK{Kq Ñ GLpHjpXs,K, Qℓqq. This is
automatic under the Hodge conjecture, as well as weaker versions of the Hodge conjecture
that assert such cycles have appropriate motivic properties. For instance, Voisin in [51, Thm.
1.11] explains how the existence of many Hodge generic SpQq-points can be proven using the
conjecture that Hodge cycles are absolute Hodge. In a similar vein, André in [5, Thm 5.2(3)]
gives a similar statement for points in SpQq admitting extra cycles that are motivated in an
appropriate sense, and [44, Thm. 1] gives such arguments in the explicit case of complete
intersections and where the Hodge cycles one wishes to avoid come from line bundles.
The basic principle of all such arguments can be found in [44]. The idea is to assemble the
representations ρs into a family by considering the associated representation ρ : πét

GLpHjpXs0,K, Qℓqq of the étale fundamental group at some base-point s0 P SpKq, and then

A result of Serre [39, §10.6] (cf. the introduction to [16] and [18, Fact 3.3.1.1]) can be used
