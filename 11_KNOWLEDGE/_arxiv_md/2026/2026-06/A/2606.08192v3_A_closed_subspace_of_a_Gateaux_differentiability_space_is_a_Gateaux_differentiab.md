Abstract. This paper establishes for the first time the iterative and rigid the-

convex sets under the pure weak∗topology can achieve localization, diameter
control, and fine structural analysis. It fundamentally transforms the tradi-

pioneers a new direction in non-metric weak∗slice geometry. By developing a
new technique involving intricate manipulations of weak∗slices and a carefully
designed iterative selection process, we prove that if M is a closed subspace

space. As a Corollary, we get that if X is a weak Asplund space and M is a
closed subspace of X, then X is a Gˆateaux differentiability space. Thus, we
definitively solve an open problem raised 46 years ago by D.G. Larman and
R.R. Phelps (J. London Math. Soc., 20(1979), 115–127).
1. Introduction and preliminaries
Geometric intuition drawn from finite-dimensional analysis often fails in infinite-
dimensional settings. While smoothness and regularity are well understood for
finite-dimensional manifolds, Euclidean structural rigidity no longer persists in
general Banach spaces. A fundamental open problem in infinite-dimensional con-
vex geometry asks whether differentiability properties inherit to closed subspaces.
It has long been conjectured that delicate geometric properties such as Gˆateaux
differentiability do not survive closed subspace restrictions, as their characteristic
geometric features vanish under subspace containment. Following the pioneer-
ing work of Larman and Phelps in 1979, most existing literature has sought to
confirm this phenomenon by constructing counterexamples.
Nonetheless, this
conventional belief has never been rigorously justified. Therefore, we may rea-
sonably conclude that Gˆateaux differentiability spaces are hereditary; the failure
of relevant research merely results from the ineffectiveness of traditional meth-
ods for studying Gˆateaux differentiability spaces. In this article, we settle this
long-standing open problem. We prove that every closed subspace of a Gˆateaux
differentiability space is itself a Gˆateaux differentiability space. This result re-
solves the classical question initiated by Larman and Phelps, and clarifies the

While some progress has been achieved in the research on Gˆateaux differentia-
bility space and weak Asplund space theory, the field remains largely unexplored.
In 1979, D.G. Larman and R.R. Phelps raised the following open problems in [1]:
Problem 1.5. Let X be a Gˆateaux differentiability space. Must X be a weak
Asplund space?
Problem 1.6. Let X be a weak Asplund spaces. Must the space X × R be a
weak Asplund space?
Problem 1.7. Let X be a weak Asplund space and M be a closed subspace of
X. Must M be a weak Asplund space?
Problem 1.8. Let X be a Gˆateaux differentiability space and M be a closed
subspace of X. Must M be a Gˆateaux differentiability space?
These problems are the basis of theory of Gˆateaux differentiability space and
weak Asplund space. Moreover, these three problems are closely related to the
application of Gˆateaux differentiability space and weak Asplund space.
In the mid-1980s, M. Fabian proved that if X is a Gˆateaux differentiability
space, then X ×R is also a Gˆateaux differentiability space. In 2001, Lixin Cheng
and M. Fabian proved that the product of a Gˆateaux differentiability space and a
separable space is again a Gˆateaux differentiability space (see[3]). In 2006, War-
ren B. Moors and Sivajah Somasundaram showed that there exists a Gˆateaux
differentiability space which is not a weak Asplund space (see[2]). In 2025, Shao-
qiang Shang proved that if X is a weak Asplund space, then X ×R is also a weak
Asplund space. Regarding problem 1.7, mathematicians tend to believe that a
closed subspace of a Gˆateaux differentiability space is not necessarily a Gˆateaux
differentiability space, and this property is not hereditary. Mathematicians have
been trying to find a counterexample.
In this paper, we provide an affirmative answer to this question. By developing
a new technique involving intricate manipulations of weak∗slices and a carefully
designed iterative selection process, we prove that if X is a Gˆateaux differentiabil-
ity space and M is a closed subspace of X, then M is a Gˆateaux differentiability
space. These results completes a key part of the theory surrounding Gˆateaux
differentiability space and resolves a problem that has remained open for over
forty-six years.
