Abstract
We present a novel and non-standard derivation of the Bickley-Naylor func-
tions Kin, n ∈N0. A 3 × 3 system of equations is developed, the solution of
which yields new explicit expressions for the Bickley-Naylor functions Ki2, Ki3,
and Ki4. Using a recurrence relation, expressions follow for any other order of
Bickley-Naylor functions. We then characterize the infinite set of Kin, n ∈N0
as a four-dimensional span of modified Bessel and Struve functions over the ring
of real polynomials, thus providing a new computational and modeling tool for
studying and applying the Bickley-Naylor functions.
Keywords: Bickley-Naylor functions, modified Bessel functions, modified Struve
functions, multivariate power substitution
MSC Classification: 2020 MSC: 35Q30 , 65M06

It remains to prove that the infinite family of the Bickley-Naylor functions, denoted
BN = {Kin : n ∈N0} is indeed a subset of this four-generator module.
A straightforward complete induction can be derived using the recurrence relation

Theorem 2 (Finite-Generation Theorem)
{Kin|n ≥0} ⊂⟨1, K0, K1, A⟩R[x] = SpanR[x]({1, K0, K1, A}).
(18)
Proof
1. Ki0 = K0 ∈M, Ki1 ∈M.
2. Induction Hypothesis: Assume Kin−2, Kin−1, Kin ∈M. From the recurrence rela-
tion nKin+1 = −xKin + (n −1)Kin−1 + Kin−2, and the fact that M is a
R[x]−module, we derive xKin, Kin−1, xKin−2 ∈M. Therefore Kin+1 ∈M.
3. In conclusion, Kin ∈M, ∀n ≥0.
Hence, the claim.
□
Note that our claim does not establish an equality between the infinite set of
Bickley-Naylor functions or the span of the Bickley functions over R[x] and the four-
generator R[x]−module M. Indeed, the constant function 1 is not a Bickley function,
as these functions actually exponentially decay as x →∞. Nonetheless, this result is
indeed profound.
3 Why the Finitely Generated Module Structure
Matters
We reveal with the Finite-Generation Theorem that, beyond the explicit representa-
tion of Ki2, Ki3, Ki4, the family of the Bickley-Naylor functions possesses a structure
of a R[x]−module generated by the four functions {1, K0, K1, A}, with A(x) =
K0 L−1 + K1 L0. This is indeed a new structural perspective on the Bickley-Naylor
functions which has several implications.
1. Finite Generation and Dimension Reduction: The Bickley-Naylor family of func-
tions has traditionally been treated as an unstructured collection of independent
“special functions” to be tabulated, approximated, or evaluated numerically. It is

now revealed as a four-generator R[x]−module. That is, every Kin, n ≥0 can now
be evaluated through only four modified Bessel and Struve functions involving some
polynomial arithmetic.
2. Computational consequences: The Finite-Generation Theorem reduces the compu-
tation and evaluation of a Bickley-Naylor function of any order to the computation
of the generators K0, K1, A with some polynomial operations. This is indeed an
alternative computational framework to the traditional use of tabulation, recursive
processes, or numerical approximation.
3. Analytical and modeling implications: Every element Kin, n ≥0 of the BN is also
an element of the module generated by {1, K0, K1, A}. Therefore it inherits any
known feature and property of the modified Bessel-Struve generators. For exam-
ple, the well-known asymptotics of K0, K1,L−1,L0 infer asymptotic expansions
for every Kin, n ∈N0. They certainly lead to closed-form evaluations, new recur-
rence forms, and integral transforms in the applied sciences. This could prove useful
in applications involving radiative heat transfer, neutron transport, and related
kernel-based models.
Therefore, the Finite-Generation Theorem uncovers an algebraic structure under-
lying the Bickley–Naylor family of functions. Such a structure would suggest new direc-
tions for both theoretical and computational investigations. For example, a finitely
generated R[x]−module structure may direct future research to the use of Gr¨obner-
basis methods for algorithmic generation and simplification of the Bickley-Naylor
functions.
4 Concluding Remarks
We studied the Bickley-Naylor family of functions Kin, n ≥0, using a non-standard
approach based on the multivariate power substitution. We then derived and solved
a novel 3 × 3 system of equations to obtain explicit representations of Ki2, Ki3, and
Ki4. These representations were naturally extended through the classical recurrence
relation to higher-order Bickley-Naylor functions.
More significantly, we showed that every Bickley-Naylor function of the BN

applications. Future work may extend the finite-generation framework to other special
