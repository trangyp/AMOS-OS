λ, ρ, and σ Regge trajectories for the hexaquark (¯u(cc))(b(¯b¯b)) in the
triquark-antitriquark picture
Xin-Ru Liu,1, ∗Qi Liu,1, † and Jiao-Kai Chen1, ‡
1School of Physics and Electronic Engineering, Shanxi Normal University, Taiyuan 030031, China
We propose Regge trajectory relations for the hexaquark (¯u(cc))(b(¯b¯b)) by using the Regge tra-
jectory relations for diquarks and triquarks.
With these newly derived relations, we investigate
ﬁve series of hexaquark Regge trajectories: the λ-, ρ1-, ρ2-, σ1-, and σ2-trajectories. We demon-
strate that, apart from the simplest λ1-trajectories, the ρ1-, ρ2-, σ1-, and σ2-trajectories cannot be
constructed by merely mimicking the meson Regge trajectories, since mesons possess no internal
substructures. To derive these trajectories, one must account for the structure and internal sub-
structure of hexaquark. Without this structural information, the ρ1-, ρ2-, σ1-, and σ2-trajectories
could only be obtained through direct ﬁts to available theoretical predictions or future experimental
data. We demonstrate that the ρ1-, ρ2-, σ1-, and σ2-trajectories for the hexaquark do not corre-
spond respectively to the Regge trajectories for the triquark, antitriquark, diquark, and antidiquark.
Nevertheless, their behaviors match those of the Regge trajectories for the triquark (¯u(cc)), the an-
titriquark (b(¯b¯b)), the diquark (cc), and the antidiquark (¯b¯b), in that respective order. Furthermore,
we present rough mass estimates for the excited states corresponding to the λ-, ρ1-, ρ2-, σ1-, and
σ2-trajectories.
I.
INTRODUCTION
In 1964, Dyson and Xuong ﬁrst proposed the possibil-
ity of dibaryon hexaquark states [1]. In the original work
[2], Jaﬀe considered the H dibaryon with quark content
uuddss, which stimulated extensive theoretical investi-
gations and experimental searches. In recent years, the
hexaquark d∗(2380) has been observed and its properties
have been determined [3–7]. Hexaquarks have been stud-
ied in various pictures, including dibaryon picture [2, 8–
12], baryon-antibaryon picture [13–18], diquark-diquark-
diquark picture [19–21], meson-meson-meson picture [22–
26], triquark-antitriquark picture [27], six-quark picture
[28–31], and others.
In the present work, we systematically investigate ﬁve
families of Regge trajectories for the quintuply heavy
hexaquark (¯u(cc))(b(¯b¯b)) in the triquark-antitriquark pic-
ture.
The Regge trajectory is one of the eﬀective ap-
proaches widely used in the study of hadron spectra [32–
71].
Studies on hexaquark Regge trajectories remain
scarce [57–59] owing to their complexity, and all prior
works only addressed λ-trajectories.
We not only ex-
plore the λ-trajectories for the hexaquark (¯u(cc))(b(¯b¯b))
but also construct the ρ1-, ρ2, σ1- and σ2-trajectories
for the quintuply heavy hexaquark, on the basis of es-
tablished diquark Regge trajectory relations [72] and tri-
quark Regge trajectories [73, 74].
We present the be-
haviors of these ﬁve families of Regge trajectories and
provide rough mass predictions for the excited states of
all ﬁve families.

In the triquark-antitriquark picture, a hexaquark con-
sists of one triquark and one antitriquark (see Fig. 1). λ
corresponds to the separation between the triquark and
the antitriquark. ρ1 separates the antiquark ¯q1 and the
diquark (q2q2) inside the triquark (¯q1(q2q3)). Similarly,
ρ2 separates the quark q4 and the antidiquark (¯q5¯q6)
within the antitriquark (q4(¯q5¯q6)) subsystem. σ1 corre-
sponds to the separation between quark q2 and quark q3,
while σ2 corresponds to the separation between the an-
tiquark ¯q5 and antiquark ¯q6. Five excited modes arise
from these relative coordinates: the σ1-mode involves ra-
dial and orbital excitations within the diquark (q2q3);
the σ2-mode corresponds to radial and orbital excitations
inside the antidiquark (¯q5¯q6); the ρ1-mode accounts for
radial and orbital excitations between the antiquark ¯q1
and diquark (q2q3) insde the triquark; the ρ2-mode in-
volves excitations between quark q4 and the antidiquark
(¯q5¯q6) within the antitriquark; the λ-mode involves ra-
dial and orbital excitations arising from the relative mo-
tion of the entire triquark and antitriquark subsystems.
Accordingly, ﬁve series of Regge trajectories can be clas-
siﬁed from these modes: two series of σ-trajectories, two
series of ρ-trajectories, and one sere of λ-trajectories.
A diquark (q2q3) couples to two irreducible color rep-
resentations via the product 3c ⊗3c = ¯3c ⊕6c. The ¯3c
channel supports an attractive interaction, whereas the
6c representation yields repulsive interquark forces within
the diquark pair.
Some previous analyses incorporate
both ¯3c and 6c color channels [e.g.,Refs. [75, 76]], whereas
other studies employ only the ¯3c channel [e.g., Refs. [77–
79]]. Consistent with the treatments in Refs. [77–79], we
adopt only the ¯3c diquark conﬁguration throughout this
paper. By the same logic, the triquark (¯q1(q2q3)) studied
here forms a 3c bound state assembled from a ¯3c diquark
(q2q3) and a ¯3c antiquark ¯q1. In the triquark-antitriquark
model, the color-singlet hexaquarks under consideration
are constructed from a 3c triquark and a ¯3c antitriquark.

IV.
CONCLUSION
In this work, we propose Regge trajectory relations for
the quintuply heavy hexaquark (¯u(cc))(b(¯b¯b)) by employ-
ing the diquark and triquark Regge trajectories. Using
these newly derived relations, we systematically investi-
gate ﬁve series of hexaquark Regge trajectories, namely
the λ-, ρ1-, ρ2-, σ1-, and σ2-trajectories. The masses of
the λ-, ρ1-, ρ2-, σ1-, and σ2-excited states are roughly
estimated.
The complete forms of the Regge trajectories for hex-
aquark (¯u(cc))(b(¯b¯b)) are lengthy and cumbersome. Ex-
cept for the λ-trajectories, the ρ1-, ρ2-, σ2-, and σ2-
trajectories cannot be constructed by simply mimick-
ing the meson Regge trajectories; instead, they should
be constructed based on hexaquark’s structures and
substructures.
Otherwise, the ρ1-, ρ2-, σ1-, and σ2-
trajectories can only be determined by ﬁtting either the-
oretical predictions or experimental measurements. The
fundamental relationship between the slopes of the ob-
tained trajectories, constituents’ masses, and string ten-
sion would become obscure.
We demonstrate that the ρ1-, ρ2-, σ1-, and σ2-
trajectories are not the triquark, antitriquark, diquark,
and antidiquark Regge trajectories, respectively. How-
ever, they share identical trajectory behaviors, respec-
tively. We show that the lengthy complete forms of the
ρ1-, ρ2-, σ1-, and σ2-trajectories can be well approxi-
mated by the simple ﬁtted formulas. The ρ1-trajectories
behave as M∼√xρ1 (xρ1 = nr1, l1), while the λ-, ρ2-,
σ1-, and σ2-trajectories all exhibit behavior of M∼x2/3,
where x = Nr, L, , nr2, l2, nr3, l3, nr4, l4.
Ref.
[105] discusses hexaquarks with various quark
contents, including qQQQQQ. Both (¯u(cc))(b(¯b¯b)) and
qQQQQQ are quintuply heavy hexaquarks.
Although
there is no suﬃcient experimental information on these
hexaquarks, the results still have a certain guiding sig-
niﬁcance for future research.
