ρ-FREQUENTLY HYPERCYCLIC OPERATORS
TOM CARROLL AND CLIFFORD GILMORE
Abstract. The concept of ρ-frequent hypercyclicity is introduced in or-
der to provide a refined form of frequent hypercyclicity. This is achieved by
replacing the denominator in the definition of frequent hypercyclicity by an
appropriately chosen calibration function ρ. A ρ-Frequent Hypercyclicity
Criterion is determined and the ρ-frequent hypercyclicity of weighted back-
ward shifts is investigated.
1. Introduction
A foundational concept in linear dynamics is hypercyclicity. A continuous
linear operator T acting on an infinite dimensional separable Fr´echet space is
hypercyclic if the orbit of some element is dense in the space. This natural
concept, that can be thought of as ‘linear chaos’, is equivalent to topological
transitivity, meaning that the orbit of any nonempty open set under T inter-
sects any other open set in the space - this is Birkhoff’s Transitivity Theorem
(cf. [2, Theorem 1.2]). An element in the space whose orbit under T is dense is
called a hypercyclic vector for T. The books by Bayart and Matheron [2] and
by Grosse-Erdmann and Peris [11] remain excellent references for the subject,
while the review article [10] points also to more recent developments.
It is natural to ask how often the orbit of a hypercyclic vector may visit an
open set. In this regard, the most that one can expect is that the orbit spends
a positive fraction of time in each open subset of the space. This concept,
known as frequent hypercyclicity, was introduced by Bayart and Grivaux [1].
To be precise, for an operator T acting on a Fr´echet space X, for x ∈X and
for a nonempty open subset U of X, the return set is defined to be

When X is separable and infinite dimensional, the operator T is said to be
frequently hypercyclic if there is some x in X for which dens(NT(x, U)) is
positive for every nonempty open subset U of X. Many important and clas-
sical operators are known to be frequently hypercyclic.
These include the
differentiation and translation operators acting on the space H(C) of entire
functions in the complex plane.
We introduce here a refined form of frequent hypercyclicity by replacing
the denominator in (1.1) by ρ(N), where ρ(t) is a function that grows slower
than t. Though the concept overlaps with ideas already well-developed in the
literature, as will be explained in detail later, the formulation presented here
may have some advantages.
The properties required of the ‘calibration function’ ρ and some straight-
forward preliminary results for sets with positive lower ρ-density are set out
in Section 2. For example, the lower ρ-density of a set A ⊆N is interpreted
in terms of the growth of the sequence of natural numbers that makes up
A. The concept of ρ-frequent hypercyclicity is formally defined in Section 3,
and a version of the Frequent Hypercyclicity Criterion adapted to ρ-frequent
hypercyclicity is derived. In Section 4, the ρ-Frequent Hypercyclicity Criterion
leads to a sufficient condition for a weighted backward shift on the sequence
space ℓp(N) to be ρ-frequently hypercyclic. The necessity of this condition is
established for a restricted class of weighted backward shifts. This, in turn,
permits the construction in Section 5 of examples to show that different cali-
bration functions give rise to distinct notions of ρ-frequent hypercyclicity. In
the final Section 6, we survey several definitions and associated results from
the literature that overlap with the idea of ρ-frequent hypercyclicity.
2. Densities relative to a calibration function ρ.
2.1. Calibration Functions and Density. We first list the properties re-
quired of a calibration function ρ.
Definition 2.1. A function ρ: R+ →R+ is called a calibration function if it
has the following properties:
(1) ρ is continuous, strictly increasing and unbounded;
(2) ρ is Lip(1, 1) so that |ρ(t1) −ρ(t2)| ≤|t1 −t2|, t1, t2 ≥0;
(3) ρ is concave so that ρ(λt1+(1−λ)t2) ≥λρ(t1)+(1−λ)ρ(t2), 0 ≤λ ≤1
and t1, t2 ≥0;
(4) ρ(t)/t is a non-increasing function of t.
We note, without further comment, that it is sufficient that these properties
hold for all sufficiently large t. With this in mind, we may assume that ρ(0) =
0. While the primary example of a calibration function is simply ρ(t) = t,
each of ρ(t) = tα (0 < α < 1), ρ(t) = t/ log t, ρ(t) = log t, ρ(t) = log log t is a
calibration function.

The case ρ(t) = t equates to frequent hypercyclicity as introduced in [1].
Indeed, ρ-frequent hypercyclicity is intended as a spectrum of ever stronger
notions of hypercyclicity that interpolates between hypercyclicity at one end
and frequent hypercyclicity at the other. In Section 6, we will set out in detail
how our notion of ρ-frequent hypercyclicity overlaps with similar concepts in
the linear dynamics literature.
The Hypercyclicity Criterion (cf. [2, Theorem 1.6]) is a sufficient condition
for an operator to be hypercyclic. It has been adapted to cover stronger forms

6. Related formulations of the concept of frequent
hypercyclicity
It is incumbent on us to clarify the relationship between the notion of
ρ-frequent hypercyclicity as set out herein and similar, indeed overlapping,
notions that are already to be found in the literature. Bonilla and Grosse-
Erdmann [6] observed that frequent hypercyclicity can be re-formulated as
follows: x is a frequently hypercyclic vector for T if and only if the return set
NT(x, U) of each nonempty open set U in X can be enumerated as an infinite
strictly increasing sequence (nk) for which nk = O(k) as k →∞: this also fol-
lows from (2.3) with ρ(t) = t. Bayart and Matheron [3] adopt this viewpoint
when they propose a generalisation of frequent hypercyclicity in the course of
their study of how ‘frequently hypercyclic’ an operator can be without being
weakly mixing.
Definition 6.1. Let (mk)k≥1 be a strictly increasing sequence of natural num-
bers and let (nk)∞
k=1 be a strictly increasing sequence that is (mk)-bounded,
that is nk = O(mk) as k →∞. An operator T is said to be (mk)-frequently
hypercyclic1 if and only if there is an element x in the space for which the
return set NT(x, U) of each nonempty open set U in X can be enumerated as
an infinite strictly increasing sequence (nk) that is (mk)-bounded.
The above definition is described in [3] as ‘the most obvious generalisation
of frequent hypercyclicity’, nonetheless ρ-frequent hypercyclicity could also be
considered a reasonable generalisation of this concept. It is, perhaps, a matter
of preference. If (mk) is any increasing sequence of natural numbers such that
limk→∞mk/k = ∞, in [3, Theorem 1.2] they construct an operator on ℓ1(N)
that is (mk)-frequently hypercyclic, but not weakly mixing.
Intuitively, in ρ-frequent hypercyclicity we count how often (at least about
ρ(N) times) the orbit of x up to time N lies in a given open set U, whereas
in (mk)-frequent hypercyclicity we count how long we need to wait (at most
about mk iterates) until the orbit of x has landed in the open set k times.
These are therefore dual notions.
However, they are not equivalent - the
enemy here is if either ρ grows slowly or mk grows quickly. This distinction
was also pointed out by Kosti´c [16] (see Remark 1 and the example preceding
it) who also gave a positive result similar to Proposition 6.4. The distinction
is between the conditions ρ(nk) = O(k) and nk = O(mk) with mk = ⌊ρ−1(k)⌋.

that have positive lower ρ-density forms a proper Furstenberg family. That T
is ρ-frequently hypercyclic is then a special case of A-hypercyclicity as defined
by B`es et al. [5] or of F-hypercyclicity as defined by Kosti´c [16].
