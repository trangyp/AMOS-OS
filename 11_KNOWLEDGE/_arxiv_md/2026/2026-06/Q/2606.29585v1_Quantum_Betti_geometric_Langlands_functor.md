1.1.6. Topological factorization homology. The theory of factorization homology derives from the fac-
torization algebras of Beilinson and Drinfeld ([BD04]), and is a topological version of their theory.
Factorization homology with coefficients in n-disk algebras are homology theories for topological man-
ifolds satisfying a generalization of the Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms for ordinary homology; as such, it
generalizes ordinary homology in a way that is only defined on n-manifolds and not necessarily on
arbitrary topological spaces. Second, these homology theories define topological quantum field theories
([CG17]). An important special case is that of associative algebras where factorization homology over
the circle recovers Hochschild homology.
Let us comment on the definition of
R

in the sense of [Lur17, 5.4]. We can think of A as a (appropriately twisted) family of En-algebras Ax
parameterized by x ∈M. In [Lur17, Chapter 5], Lurie proposes a convenient geometric way to encode
this data. He defines the topological Ran space Ran(M) as the collection of all nonempty finite subsets
of M. Then he defines a cosheaf FA, whose stalk at S ∈Ran(M) is ⊗s∈SAs. We can view FA as
a constructible cosheaf of Ran(M), obtained by gluing together locally constant cosheaves along the
locally closet subsets of Ran(M).
We refer to the global sections FA(Ran(M)) as the topological factorization homology of M with
coefficients in A. A collection of striking properties of this construction is formulated and proved in
[Lur17, Chapter 5.5].

1.4. Notations and conventions.
1.4.1. Categories. We use the formalism of ∞-categories developed in [Lur09], [Lur17], and theory of
DG categories as understood in [GR17]. By a DG category we mean k-linear stable ∞-category. We let
DGCat be the category of presentable DG categories with continuous functors with monoidal structure
given by the Lurie tensor product ⊗.
1.4.2. Lie theory. Throughout the paper G will stand for a reductive group over k of adjoint type. We
choose Borel subgroup B ⊂G, the opposite Borel B−⊂G and the Cartan subgroup T = B ∩B−. Let
N (N −) be the unipotent radical of B (B−). Let Λ (ˇΛ) denote the lattice of weights (coweights) of G.
Let Λ+ (ˇΛ+) denote the subset of dominant weights (coweights). Let I denote the set of nodes for the
Dynkin diagram of G. For i ∈I, we let αi (ˇαi) denote the corresponding simple root (coroot). Let 2ρ
denote the sum of simple roots and 2ˇρ the sum of simple coroots. Let ˇG be the Langlands dual group
over k for G. We denote by LG (L+G) the loop (arc) group of G.
Let girr ⊂g denote the reduced closed subscheme of irregular elements. We let N ⊂g denote the
nilpotent cone. We let N irr := N ∩girr denote the subscheme of irregular nilpotent elements. We let
N reg ⊂N denote the open complement to N irr, which parametrizes of regular nilpotent elements.
1.4.3. Levels. A level κ for G is a G-invariant symmetric bilinear form
κ : Sym2(g) →C.
Denote by κg,crit the critical level, i.e. −1
2 times the Killing form. Throughout the paper we will assume
that the level κ is non-degenerate, i.e. that κ −κg,crit is nondegenerate as a bilinear form. The dual
level ˇκ on ˇG for κ is the unique nondegenerate level such that the restriction of ˇκ −κˇg,crit to t∗and the
restriction of κ −κg,crit to t are dual symmetric bilinear forms.
Suppose G is simple. A level κ is rational if κ is a rational multiple of the Killing form and irrational
otherwise. A level κ is positive if κ −κg,crit is a positive rational multiple of the Killing form. A level is
negative if it is not positive or critical. For general reductive G, we say a level κ is rational, irrational,
