Abstract. We show that for a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme X with an ample family of
line bundles, the connective half QCoh(X)≥0 of the standard t-structure on the derived ∞-category
of quasi-coherent sheaves is compactly generated by a connective perfect object if and only if X is
quasi-affine.

1. Introduction
In recent years there has been considerable attention to presentable stable ∞-categories equipped
with a single compact generator. For such categories, [AJS02, Theorem A.1] says that there exists
a t-structure whose connective half is compactly generated by the given single compact generator,
in the sense that the connective half is the smallest full subcategory closed under extensions and
small colimits containing the generator. A well-known result of Bondal and Van den Bergh [BdB02,
Theorem 3.1.1] shows the derived stable ∞-category of quasi-coherent sheaves QCoh(X) on a quasi-
compact quasi-separated scheme X admits such compact generator. For applications, it suffices to
know that this t-structure is equivalent (in the sense of [Nee25, Definition 0.18]) to the standard t-
structure, a result proved in [Nee24, Theorem 3.2]. In any case, it is interesting to understand if the
two t-structures agree, or, in equivalent terms, if the standard t-structure is compactly generated by
a single object. Our main result shows that, under a mild assumption, this occurs precisely when the
scheme is quasi-affine.
Theorem 1.1. Let X be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme. Then X is quasi-affine if and
only if it admits an ample family of line bundles and the connective half QCoh(X)≥0 is compactly
generated by a connective perfect object.
Notice that every quasi-affine scheme admits an ample line bundle, the structure sheaf. The key
input on the “geometric” side is the 1-resolution property.
Under the assumption that X admits
an ample family of line bundles, [DHM20, Proposition 3.4] shows that the 1-resolution property
forces X to be quasi-affine. On the “categorical” side, starting from a connective perfect generator
G ∈QCoh(X)≥0, the ample-family hypothesis implies that G can be chosen strict, that is, represented
by a bounded complex of vector bundles. From such a representative we extract an associated vector
bundle EG = ⊕i Ei. We then show that EG generates the heart QCoh(X)♡, and a simple argument
implies that every object of finite-type of QCoh(X)♡is a quotient of a finite sum of copies of EG. In
other words, EG is a special vector bundle and X satisfies the 1-resolution property, so the conclusion
follows from [DHM20, Proposition 3.4].
Finally, our result is compatible with recent nonexistence results: for instance, [BDH+25, Theo-
rem A.1] proves that if X is a smooth projective curve over the complex numbers, then the standard
connective half QCoh(X)≥0 is not singly compactly generated. Since such schemes admit an ample
family of line bundles but are not quasi-affine, this phenomenon is predicted by our characterization.
Organization. In section 2 we recall basic facts on Grothendieck prestable ∞-categories and recall
the notion of finite type objects in the heart. In section 3 we relate strict compact generators to the
1-resolution property and prove the main theorem. In section 4 we discuss the optimality of the main
result.

Conventions. We freely use the language of stable ∞-categories and t-structures on them, which
we grade homologically. We denote by homC(−,−) the mapping spectrum of a stable ∞-category C.
Given a scheme X, we denote by QCoh(X) the derived stable ∞-category of quasi-coherent sheaves
on X. We will also denote by QCoh(X)≥0 the connective half of the standard t-structure, and by
QCoh(X)♡the Grothendieck abelian 1-category of discrete quasi-coherent sheaves on X. If A is a
ring, we will denote by ModA the derived stable ∞-category of modules over A.

2. Grothendieck prestable ∞-categories
We begin with some reminders on Grothendieck prestable categories that the reader can find in
[Lur18, Appendix C].
Remark 2.1. Recall that an ∞-category is prestable if it is pointed, it admits finite colimits, the
suspension functor is fully-faithful and every span 0 →Σx ←Ð y can be completed to a pullback, and
the resulting square is also a pushout. Among prestable categories, the one admitting finite limits may
be characterized as the connective half of a non-canonical t-structure on certain stable ∞-categories
(usually taken to be the Spanier-Whitehead stabilization or the spectrum objects stabilization). Given
a prestable ∞-category C, there is a notion of heart C♡, defined to be the full subcategory of discrete
objects. In the case where C admits finite limits, the heart C♡may be identified with the heart of the
non-canonical t-structure.
Remark 2.2. Recall that a prestable ∞-category C is Grothendieck prestable if it is presentable
and filtered colimits are left exact. In this case, the heart C♡is a Grothendieck abelian 1-category.
Furthermore, every Grothendieck abelian 1-category arises as the heart of a Grothendieck prestable
∞-category. A great number of examples of Grothendieck prestable ∞-categories is given by prestable
compactly generated ∞-categories.
The main example for us is the following.
Example 2.3. Let X be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme and let QCoh(X)≥0 be the connec-
tive half of the standard t-structure on the derived ∞-category QCoh(X) of quasi-coherent sheaves.
Then QCoh(X)≥0 is Grothendieck prestable, being compactly-generated by the connective perfect
objects. See [Lur18, Proposition 9.6.1.2].
The problem of compact generation of t-structure is well understood.
Remark 2.4. Let C be a presentable stable ∞-category and let E be an object.
Then [Lur17,
Proposition 1.4.4.11] states that there exists an accessible t-structure whose connective half ⟨E⟩≥0 is
the smallest full subcategory which contains E and is closed under extensions and small colimits. If E
is furthermore compact, then ⟨E⟩≥0 is Grothendieck prestable, since it is compactly generated by the
closure under retracts of the smallest full subcategory which contains E and is closed under extensions
and finite colimits. In other terms, E is a compact generator for ⟨E⟩≥0 and the t-structure is singly
compactly generated.
In the case of compact generators, the previous observation can be improved.
Remark 2.5. Let C be a presentable stable ∞-category with a single compact generator G. Then
[Lur18, Proposition C.6.3.1] states that there exists an accessible, compatible with filtered colimits,
right-complete t-structure whose connective half ⟨G⟩≥0 is the smallest full subcategory which contains
G and is closed under extensions and small colimits. This connective half is Grothendieck prestable
(being compactly generated by the closure under retracts of the smallest full subcategory which

Remark 2.6. Let C be a Grothendieck prestable ∞-category. Recall that a generating subcategory is

is the case where C0 consists of a single object G (which is not necessarily compact); this is the case
of Remark 2.4 and Remark 2.5. In this case the truncation π0G is a generator for the Grothendieck
abelian 1-category C♡.
We conclude this section with an observation.
Definition 2.7. Let C♡be a Grothendieck abelian 1-category. An object x ∈C♡is called of finite
type if it satisfies the following equivalent conditions:
(1) For every filtered diagram (yi)i∈I in C♡such that each transition map yi →yj is a monomor-
phism, the canonical map colimi∈I HomC♡(x,yi) →HomC♡(x,colimi∈I yi) is an isomorphism.
(2) If (xi)i∈I is a filtered system of subobjects xi ⊆x whose union is x, then x = xi0 for some
i0 ∈I.
The equivalence of these conditions is shown in [Gar99, Theorem 1.8].
We recall the following well known result.
Lemma 2.8. Let C♡be a Grothendieck abelian 1-category and let E ∈C♡be a generator. Then every
object x ∈C♡of finite type admits an epimorphism ⊕j∈J0E →x from a finite direct sum.
Proof. Since E is a generator, there exists a set I and an epimorphism p ∶⊕i∈I E ↠x.

3. The 1-resolution property
Recall that a scheme X has the resolution property if every discrete quasi-coherent module of finite
type is the quotient of a vector bundle.
Example 3.1. Let X be a scheme. If X has an ample family of line bundles, then [Aut18, Lemma
0GMM] implies that X has the resolution property.
Remark 3.2. For us [Aut18, Lemma 0F8F] is the main result: it states that, for a quasi-compact
quasi-separated scheme with the resolution property, every perfect complex can be represented by a
bounded complex of vector bundles. In other terms, every perfect complex is strict.
We now need a stronger resolution property, which resolves every quasi-coherent module of finite
type by a single vector bundle.
Definition 3.3. Let X be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme. We will say that X has the
1-resolution property if there exists a vector bundle E such that every discrete quasi-coherent sheaf of
finite type on X is a quotient of ⊕i∈IE for some finite set I. In this case, E is called a special vector
bundle.
Remark 3.4. Let X be a quasi-compact scheme admitting an ample family of line bundles. If X has
the 1-resolution property then it is quasi-affine. This is [DHM20, Proposition 3.4].

Lemma 3.6. Let X be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme. Let G ∈QCoh(X)≥0 be a strict
compact generator for the connective half and let EG be an associated vector bundle. Then:
(1) There is an equality QCoh(X)≥0 = ⟨EG⟩≥0.
(2) The object EG is a generator of QCoh(X)♡.
Proof. Consider point (1). Since QCoh(X)≥0 is closed under extension and small colimits and contains
EG, it follows that ⟨EG⟩≥0 ⊆QCoh(X)≥0 = ⟨G⟩≥0. For the converse, it suffices to show that G lies in
⟨EG⟩≥0. The proof now goes by induction on 0 ≤i ≤d, where d is the coconnectivity of G. Let σ≤i be
the stupid truncation and consider the cofibre sequence
σ≤i−1G →σ≤iG →ΣiEi.
If i = 0 then the connectivity of G implies that σ≤−1G ≃0, so that σ≤G ≃E0 belongs to ⟨EG⟩≥0, being
E0 a retract of EG. The inductive hypothesis assumes now σ≤i−1G ∈⟨EG⟩≥0. Since the last term
belongs to ⟨EG⟩≥0, being a suspension of a retract of EG, it follows that σ≤iG ∈⟨EG⟩≥0, being ⟨EG⟩≥0
closed under extensions. Thus G ≃σ≤dG belongs to ⟨EG⟩≥0.
For (2), since point (1) shows that EG is a generator for QCoh(X)≥0, it suffices to apply Remark 2.6
to deduce that EG ≃π0EG is a generator for QCoh(X)♡.

The following is the main result of these pages.
Lemma 3.7. Let X be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme. Let G ∈QCoh(X)≥0 be a strict
compact generator and let EG be the associated vector bundle. Then EG is a special vector bundle
for QCoh(X)♡. In particular, X has the 1-resolution property.
Proof. Since Lemma 3.6 implies that the object EG ∈QCoh(X)♡is a generator, an application of
Lemma 2.8 shows that every discrete quasi-coherent sheaf of finite type admits a surjection from a
finite sum of copies of EG. Since EG is a vector bundle by definition, the claim follows.
□
Corollary 3.8. Let X be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme and assume that the standard
t-structure is generated by a strict connective perfect complex.
(1) If X is a noetherian normal scheme which is J-2, then it is quasi-affine.
(2) If X is a normal noetherian Q-factorial scheme (for example, regular), then it is quasi-affine.
Proof. Standing Lemma 3.7, point (1) follows from [DHM20, Theorem 3.13] and point (2) from
[DHM20, Corollary 3.8].
□
We summarize our discussion with the following.
Theorem 3.9. Let X be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme. Then X is quasi-affine if and
only if it admits an ample family of line bundles and the connective half QCoh(X)≥0 is compactly
generated by a connective perfect object.
Proof. Let X be quasi-affine and let Γ(X,OX) denote the ordinary ring of global sections of the
structure sheaf. Then the canonical map j ∶X ↪Spec(Γ(X,OX)) is an open immersion. It follows
that j∗∶ModΓ(X,OX) →QCoh(X) is a t-exact localization, and hence OX ≃j∗(1Γ(X,OX)) compactly
generates QCoh(X)≥0. Since the monoidal unit is ample, one direction follows.

Conversely, assume that QCoh(X)≥0 is compactly generated by a connective perfect object. Since
X admits an ample family of line bundles, this connective perfect object is strict by Example 3.1
and Remark 3.2. By Lemma 3.7 the scheme X satisfies the 1-resolution property, and since it has an
ample family of line bundles, Remark 3.4 implies that X is quasi-affine.
□
4. Final observations
The main result recovers an “horrible discovery”.
Example 4.1. In [BDH+25, Theorem A.1] it is stated that a smooth projective curve over the complex
numbers has connective half which is not singly compactly generated. This is an example of a scheme
with an ample family of line bundles (and actually, a single one), for which Theorem 3.9 applies.
Notice furthermore that Theorem 3.9 provides a different proof (since [BDH+25, Theorem A.1] relies
on the Harder-Narasimhan filtration).
One question remains: are there quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes with singly compactly
generated connective half which are not quasi-affine?
