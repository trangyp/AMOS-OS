RELATIVE BIEXACTNESS AND MIXING IN VON NEUMANN
ALGEBRAS
SRIVATSAV KUNNAWALKAM ELAYAVALLI AND ZHIYUAN YANG
Abstract. We develop a new technique to upgrade relative biexactness in
general von Neumann algebras: suppose that {Ni}i∈I ⊂M are mixing and
biexact subalgebras of a separable von Neumann algebra with expectation,
and if M is biexact relative to {Ni}i∈I, then M is biexact. This result yields
several new examples of biexact von Neumann algebras, notably including
amalgamated free products. By generalizing the relative biexactness results of
Hoshino to the von Neumann algebra setting and applying our result above
along with certain bimodule computations, we in fact obtain, as an applica-
tion, a new classification result for biexactness for graph products of finite
dimensional von Neumann algebras. This yields significant extensions of prior
works of Caspers-Borst, and Blufstein-Goldman-Oyakawa.
1. Introduction
The study of biexact groups was initiated by Ozawa in his article [Oza04].
This article not only settled the problem of Ge, proving solidity for L(Fn), but
also opened up line of research that settled multiple long standing open prob-
lems [OP10, CS13, PV14, BIP21]. Biexactness demands that the left action of the
group on the small at infinity boundary (the fixed points of the Stone-Cech re-
mainder under the right action) is topologically amenable. Multiple groups are
known to be biexact, including families of hyperbolic groups, relatively hyperbolic
groups, wreath products, small cancelation groups, etc. On the other hand, G is
not biexact whenever it contains a copy of K1 × K2 where K1 is infinite and K2 is
nonamenable. More generally, L(G) is solid, i.e, the commutant of any diffuse sub-
algebra is amenable. Combined with the weak amenability [CH89], one in fact has
strong solidity of L(G) [CS13], which demands that the normalizer of any diffuse
amenable subalgebra is amenable.
Generalizing this notion to von Neumann algebras is an apriori hard problem be-
cause of the fundamental diﬀiculties stemming from works of Johnson-Parrot [JP72]
and Popa [Pop87] in obtaining a satisfactory analogue of the small at infinity bound-
ary. This was circumvented in the article [DKP23], wherein a natural von Neu-
mann algebra boundary theory was developed with various applications.
They
introduced a fruitful new topological perspective to study von Neumann alge-
bras building on the theory of operator M-bimodules developed in works of Ma-
gajna [Mag97, Mag00].

Theorem 1.1. Let M be a separable von Neumann algebra and N = {Ni}i∈I be a
family of subalgebras with expectation and with commuting boundary pieces. If M
is biexact relative to N, and each Ni is mixing and biexact, then M is biexact.
We first clarify the precise technical meaning of “commuting boundary pieces”
can be seen in Theorem 3.7; it asks for the support projections of the boundary
pieces in the relevant bidual space to commute. This condition may appear rather
unmotivated to the naked eye, but it holds in most cases in practice. Mixingness
is a standard notion in the bimodule theory of von Neumann algebras, motivated
from the analogous notion in ergodic theory. For the definition we direct the reader
to [ADP]. As a first concrete application of our result, we produce a new source of
biexact von Neumann algebras arising from amalgamated free products. This result
follows from Theorem 1.1 combined with the relative biexactness result of [TY25].
In particular this offers a significant generalization of Corollary 1.7 of [TY25].
Corollary 1.2. Let M1, M2 be two separable biexact tracial von Neumann algebras
with a common subalgebra B. If B is amenable and mixing in both M1 and M2,
then the amalgamated free product M = M1 ∗B M2 is biexact.
As a simple case, our main Theorem 1.1 yields examples even at the group level.
Let G be a countable group. Recall that a subgroup H < G is almost malnormal
if gHg−1 ∩H is finite for every g ∈G \ H. It is well known that H < G is almost
malnormal implies that L(H) ⊂L(G) is mixing (see for instance [BC17]). Now let
G be a countable group that is biexact relative to a family of subgroups {Hi}i∈I.
Theorem 1.1 then implies that if each Hi is biexact, and almost malnormal in G,
then G is biexact. In Section 5 we give two independent and more accessible proofs
of this (this point will be revisited at the end of the introduction). This result
allows us to access biexactness for a concretely new family of groups combining
with the result of Oyakawa [Oya24].
Corollary 1.3. Suppose that Γ is a uniformly fine hyperbolic countable graph with
girth(Γ) > 20 and that G = {Gv}v∈V (Γ) is a collection of non-trivial finite groups.
Then, the graph product ΓG is biexact.
As the above result suggests, a natural setting where our main result can apply
naturally is to the setting of graph products of von Neumann algebras [CF17], which
has seen immense activity in recent years (see [HI25] and the references therein). In
fact, our next main results will give vast generalizations of the above Corollary 1.3.

5. Relatively biexact groups over almost malnormal subgroups
In this section, we isolate two self-contained proofs for the specific case of our

The first proof uses the characterization of the relative biexactness of a group Γ via
the action of Γ × Γ. The second proof uses a similar strategy using the bidual, as
in the proof of our main theorem.
Let Γ be a discrete group, and {Hi}i∈I be a family of subgroups Γ. For a function
f ∈ℓ∞(Γ) and s ∈Γ, we always denote the left translation by s · f, and the right

A Γ-boundary piece is a left and right Γ-invariant closed ideal I ⊂ℓ∞(Γ). In
particular, we are interested in the subgroups boundary piece I = c0(Γ, {Hi}i∈I)
