REMARKS ON CAUSAL DECREASE, OMNISCIENT FOLIATIONS
AND NULL LINES IN SPACETIMES
A. BAUTISTA
Abstract. This work provides a counterexample to the previously asserted non–
existence of future null lines in strongly causal spacetimes (M, g) foliated by a past–
omniscient timelike foliation F with a compact leaf space Q.
We introduce a new
hypothesis requiring the timelike vector field that defines F to be causally decreasing.
Under this condition, the causality requirement on M can be relaxed to the distin-
guishing level. In the absence of this hypothesis, we demonstrate that more restrictive
conditions, such as global hyperbolicity, are necessary to ensure the absence of future
null lines. Finally, we derive sufficient conditions on the scale function of Generalized
Robertson–Walker (GRW) spacetimes to ensure that the natural timelike vector field
is causally decreasing, thereby guaranteeing the non–existence of future null lines.
1. Introduction
In [1], Bartnik introduced his well–known conjecture on cosmological spacetimes: a
globally hyperbolic spacetime M with compact Cauchy surfaces that satisfies the timelike
convergence condition, that is Ric(V, V ) ≥0 for any timelike V ∈TM, must either
be timelike geodesically incomplete or split as M = R × S with the product metric
g = −dt2 ⊕h, where (S, h) is a compact Riemannian manifold.
Several attempts have been made to verify this conjecture. Notable contributions by
Galloway [6, 7, 8] introduced the non–existence of null lines as an additional hypoth-
esis. Consequently, the presence or absence of achronal null geodesics has become an
important property in determining whether M admits a metric splitting.
Later, Harris and Low [11] studied chronological spacetimes foliated by timelike curves
to determine how the leaf space Q represents the shape of space. They showed that
each timelike foliation F is defined by a complete timelike vector field [11, Lem. 1.1].
Furthermore, if there are no ancestral pairs in M, that is there are no γ1, γ2 leaves of
F such that p1 ⊂I+(p2) for all pi ∈γi with i = 1, 2, then Q is Hausdorff and M is
diffeomorphic to the product R × Q [11, Thm. 1.2]. It is important to note that this
provides a topological decomposition rather than a metric splitting in Bartnik’s sense,
as the metric is not necessarily a product.
In [11], the concepts of omniscient foliation and causal monotonicity were also in-
troduced. The former implies that every leaf γ of the timelike foliation F can receive
information from any point in the spacetime, that means I−(γ) = M for all γ ∈F.
The latter, described as a generalization of conformal–Killing vector fields, requires the
existence of a timelike vector field whose forward flow preserves causal vectors (causal

In [5], the authors investigated whether a past–omniscient foliation F implies that the
chronological past of any future–inextensible timelike curve λ covers the entire spacetime,
that is I−(λ) = M. Under the assumptions of strong causality, a compact leaf space Q,

However, the proof provided in [5] is flawed; it overlooks the fact that since the
hypersurfaces Qs = {s} × Q are not necessarily achronal, the canonical projection onto
R of a future–inextensible causal curve may diverge toward −∞as its parameter tends
toward its future limit. Consequently, the theorem as stated does not hold.
The first objective of this article is to provide a counterexample (Example 9) that
demonstrates the failure of the aforementioned result. This establishes the necessity of
additional hypotheses to ensure the same conclusion. Our second objective is to propose
these new hypotheses. Specifically, we show that by requiring the timelike vector field
defining F to be causally decreasing, the causality condition can be relaxed such that M
is distinguishing. Alternatively, without the causal decrease assumption, more restrictive
conditions such as global hyperbolicity must be imposed. Under this latter condition,
the statement in [5, Prop. 2.1] can be recovered.
Finally, we apply our results to Generalized Robertson–Walker (GRW) spacetimes to
find sufficient conditions on the warping function that guarantee causal decrease, thus
ensuring the absence of null lines.
The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 provides the mathematical background
on causality, timelike foliations and causal decrease. Section 3 presents the proof of the
main theorem and its corollaries. Finally, Section 4 discusses the application of the main
result to GRW spacetimes.

2.2. Timelike foliations. Following [11], we will introduce some concepts we will use
later.
It is known [11, Lem. 1.1] that, in a chronological spacetime M, any (differentiable)
timelike foliation F can be defined by the integral curves of a complete timelike vector
field U ∈X(M). Then the set of leaves of F coincides with the space of integral curves
of U and it will be denoted by Q1.
Definition 6. Let U ∈X(M) be a timelike vector field.
(1) The timelike foliation F defined by integral curves of the vector field U is said
to be past–omniscient. Dually, one can define future–omniscience.
(2) Two inextendible timelike curves γ1, γ2 ⊂M form an ancestral pair if for all
p ∈γ1 and q ∈γ2 we have p ∈I−(q).
In this case, we will say that γ1 is
ancestral to γ2.

of naked singularities. Indeed, if γ1 is ancestral to γ2, since p ∈I−(q) for all p ∈γ1
and q ∈γ2 then γ1 ⊂I−(q) and γ2 ⊂I+(p) and, therefore, I−(γ1) ⊂I−(q) and
I+(γ2) ⊂I+(p).

1.3].

5. Conclusion
In this work, Example 9 demonstrates the existence of a strongly causal spacetime M
equipped with a complete timelike vector field U ∈X(M) whose orbit space Q is compact
and whose integral curves are past–omniscient, yet fails to satisfy the NFOH condition
and consequently possesses future null lines. This result serves as a counterexample to
[5, Prop. 2.1], indicating that additional hypotheses are required to recover the original
result.
In Theorem 15, we establish that by assuming U is causally decreasing, the causality
requirement on M can be relaxed to the level of a distinguishing spacetime.
Without the assumption of U being causally decreasing, it becomes necessary to
impose causality conditions stronger than stable causality to ensure the non–existence
of future null lines. This case is addressed in Theorem 19 and Corollary 20 for globally
hyperbolic spacetimes. Whether these results hold for intermediate steps of the causal
ladder, such as causal continuity or causal simplicity, remains an open question.
