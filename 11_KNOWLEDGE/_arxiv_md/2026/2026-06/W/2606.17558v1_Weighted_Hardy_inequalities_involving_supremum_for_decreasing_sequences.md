Abstract. In this paper, we provide a complete characterization of the weighted Hardy
inequalities involving the supremum operator, restricted to the cone of non-increasing se-
quences, for all positive parameters. We reduce such inequalities to equivalent ones on the
cone of non-negative sequences. The latter setting provides a broader framework for analysis
and significantly expands the range of proofs that can be established.

For the discrete weighted Hardy inequality, in the case 1 < p, q < ∞, an effective ap-
proach is based on Sawyer’s duality principle [15], which reduces inequalities of the form
(1.1) for non-negative, non-increasing sequences to corresponding modified inequalities for
non-negative sequences. The discrete analog of Sawyer’s duality theorem was obtained by
Oinarov and Shalginbaeva [14] and Bennett and Grosse-Erdmann [1, 2]. However, Sawyer’s
duality principle cannot be applied to the supremum operator appearing in (1.1), since this
operator is non-linear. We therefore develop a new reduction method that is valid for all
parameters 0 < p, q < ∞and is applicable to non-linear operators. The inclusion of this class
of operators is essential, as supremal operators naturally emerge when investigating fractional
maximal operators on classical Lorentz spaces [4, 8]. In fact, establishing inequality (1.1) con-
stitutes a fundamental step in analyzing the boundedness of the discrete fractional maximal
operator on Lorentz sequence spaces. We intend to address this in our next study.
Reduction theorems for the weighted Hardy inequality involving the supremum for non-
increasing functions in the continuous setting, namely, for inequalities involving functions
rather than sequences, were obtained in [9, Corollary 3.2] for 0 < p ≤1 and 0 < q < ∞.
For 1 ≤p < ∞and 0 < q < ∞, a general reduction theorem for monotone operators was
obtained in [11] and [12], which contains the Hardy operator involving the supremum as a
special case. A complete characterization in the continuous setting is provided in [7]. For a
detailed history of related results, see the papers cited therein.
In the case 1 < p < ∞, a fundamental difference arises between the discrete and continuous
settings. The proof used in the continuous case cannot be applied directly to the discrete
one, since the power rule, which holds in the continuous case, fails in the discrete setting (see
[16]). Moreover, our approach requires a new version of Copson’s inequality (Lemma 4.6),
which was stated without proof in [3]; for completeness, we provide the proof in this paper.
In the case 0 < p < 1, our proofs are essentially based on the results from [6].
