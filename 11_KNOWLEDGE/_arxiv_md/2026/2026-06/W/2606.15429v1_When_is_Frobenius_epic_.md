WHEN IS FROBENIUS EPIC?
JAVIER CARVAJAL-ROJAS, RANKEYA DATTA, NOAH OLANDER, AND AXEL ST ¨ABLER
ABSTRACT. We prove that a homomorphism of rings of prime characteristic is b-nil formally
unramified if and only if its relative Frobenius is an epimorphism. We specialize this result
under different finiteness conditions such as (relative) F-finiteness and Noetherianity. We
give an example where the absolute Frobenius is an epimorphism but is not surjective,
as well as an example of a formally unramified homomorphism that is not b-nil formally
unramified.
1. INTRODUCTION
In the early development of scheme theory, Grothendieck advocated the use of formal
lifting properties to define classes of morphisms. For example, he defined smooth mor-
phisms as formally smooth morphisms (in the discrete sense) that are locally of finite
presentation [Gro67, D´efinition 17.3.1]. This definition is particularly useful for checking
that a moduli space is smooth over the base, as it can be stated entirely in terms of the
functor of points. Grothendieck also proved the deep result that formally smooth, but
not necessarily finitely presented, maps of Noetherian rings are regular; that is, flat with
geometrically regular fibers (see [Gro67, Corollaire 19.6.5]). However, in the absence of
any finiteness hypotheses, there are many pathological examples of formally smooth, ´etale,
and unramified ring maps. For example, a formally ´etale algebra over a field need not be
reduced; see [Bha12].
In the work [DO25], the second and third named authors of this article investigate a
strengthening of these lifting properties that was introduced by Berthelot in [Ber74, IV,
D´efinition 1.5.1]. Briefly, instead of considering lifts of ring maps with respect to nilpotent
ideals, one considers lifts with respect to ideals that are nil of bounded index; see Section 4.
The resulting analogs of formally smooth, ´etale, and unramified ring maps are called
b-nil formally smooth, ´etale, and unramified ring maps. Datta and Olander prove several
structural properties of b-nil formally ´etale and b-nil formally smooth maps in prime
characteristic p > 0 without any finiteness hypotheses. Namely, b-nil formally smooth
algebras over fields of positive characteristic are reduced; for b-nil formally smooth ring
maps of Fp-algebras, the relative Frobenius is flat, and it even exhibits the target as a
projective module over the source; and a map of Fp-algebras is b-nil formally ´etale if and
only if its relative Frobenius is an isomorphism. The work loc. cit. leaves open the question
of whether there is a characterization of b-nil formally unramified ring maps of Fp-algebras
in terms of the relative Frobenius.

In the independent work [CRS26], the first and fourth named authors develop the
theory of flat morphisms of Fp-schemes with isomorphic relative Frobenius, which they
call pristine morphisms, and they show that this class of morphisms behaves well from
the perspective of F-singularity theory of locally Noetherian schemes. An important
technical result in their paper is that a map of Fp-algebras R −→S, for which both S and its
Frobenius twist S(p) = F∗R ⊗R S are Noetherian, is formally unramified if and only if the
relative Frobenius FS/R : S(p) −→S is surjective [CRS26, Theorem 4.1]. In this paper, we
prove an analogous characterization of b-nil formally unramified morphisms in positive
characteristic with no finiteness assumptions.
Theorem 1.1. (Theorem 4.4) Let φ: R −→S be a map of Fp-algebras. The following are equivalent:
(a) φ is b-nil formally unramified.
(b) FS/R is b-nil formally unramified.
(c) FS/R is an epimorphism of rings.
We give an example to show that the result does not hold when “epimorphism” is
replaced by “surjection.” In fact, we construct an example of an Fp-algebra A whose
absolute Frobenius is an epimorphism but not surjective; see Section 6. This answers
a Mathoverflow question from Matthieu Romagny [Rom] and shows that Theorem 1.1
is optimal because the absolute Frobenius of A is the relative Frobenius of Fp −→A.
We provide another example to show that a formally unramified map of rings of prime
characteristic p > 0 need not be b-nil formally unramified; see Section 7. Thus, b-nil
formal unramifiedness is a stronger property than formal unramifiedness. In addition,
we prove several strengthenings of Theorem 1.1 under various finiteness conditions. For
instance, in Proposition 5.1, we offer another perspective on the characterization of formally
unramified maps of Noetherian Fp-algebras that was recently given in [CRS26, Theorem
4.1] by the first and fourth authors. We prove in Theorem 4.14 that if a homomorphism of
prime characteristic rings has finite relative Frobenius, then it is formally unramified if and
only if it is b-nil formally unramified. This is the analog of [DO25, Theorem 1.5], which
states that if a homomorphism of prime characteristic rings has finitely presented relative
Frobenius, then it is b-nil formally smooth (resp. ´etale) if and only if it is formally smooth
(resp. ´etale). The thrust of these enhancements of Theorem 1.1 is that under finiteness
conditions, epimorphisms are often surjective.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 3, we collect for later use some generalities
on epimorphisms in the category of rings. This notion is surprisingly subtle and was the
subject of a seminar by Samuel; see [Sam68]. In Section 4, we give the proof of Theorem 1.1.
In Section 5, we discuss consequences in the Noetherian case, and in particular, the
relationship between Theorem 1.1 and [CRS26, Theorem 4.1]. Finally, in Section 6 and
Section 7, we give examples of, respectively, a ring of prime characteristic whose absolute
Frobenius is an epimorphism but not surjective, and a ring map of prime characteristic
that is formally unramified but not b-nil formally unramified.

Lemma 3.1. Let φ: R −→S be a ring map. Then the following are equivalent:
(a) φ is an epimorphism (in the category of rings).
(b) The two canonical maps S −→S ⊗R S are equal.
(c) Either of the two canonical injections S −→S ⊗R S is an isomorphism.
(d) S ⊗R coker(φ) = 0.
(e) The multiplication map µφ : S ⊗R S ↠S is an isomorphism.
Lemma 3.1 immediately implies the following two corollaries.
Corollary 3.2. Let φ: R −→S be a ring epimorphism.
(a) The base change idT ⊗Rφ: T −→T ⊗R S is an epimorphism for all R-algebras T.
(b) φ is formally unramified.
(c) For every S-module M, the canonical homomorphism M −→M ⊗R S is an isomorphism. In
other words, every S-module is the base change of an R-module.
Corollary 3.3. Let φ: R −→S be a ring map and I ⊂R be a nilpotent ideal (i.e. In = 0 for
some n > 0). If the induced map φ ⊗R idR/I : R/I −→S/IS is an epimorphism, then φ is an
epimorphism.
Proof. We have coker(φ ⊗R idR/I) = coker(φ) ⊗R R/I. Thus,
S/IS ⊗R/I coker(φ ⊗R idR/I) = (S ⊗R coker(φ)) ⊗R R/I.
Since φ ⊗R idR/I is an epimorphism, this vanishes by Lemma 3.1. Therefore, by the
nilpotent version of Nakayama’s Lemma [Sta, Tag 00DV (9)], S ⊗R coker(φ) = 0, and so φ
is an epimorphism by Lemma 3.1.
□
Example 3.4 (Ring epimorphisms need not be surjective.). The canonical map associated
with the localization of a ring at a multiplicative set is always an epimorphism, but it
is usually not surjective. Thus, unlike in the category of modules, epimorphisms in the
category of commutative rings are not always surjective.
Recall that a ring map R −→S is universally injective (or pure) if for all R-modules M, the
induced map M −→S ⊗R M is injective. Taking M = R, we see that universally injective
ring maps are injective. Faithfully flat ring maps are universally injective. If R −→S admits
an R-linear left inverse, then R −→S is universally injective.
Following [RG71], we introduce the following notion.
Definition 3.5. A ring map R −→S satisfies property (O) if for all R-modules M, whenever
S ⊗R M = 0, then M = 0.
Remark 3.6. Let φ: R −→S be a ring map.
(a) If φ is universally injective, then φ satisfies (O). This follows because M −→S ⊗R M
is injective for all R-modules M.
(b) More generally, suppose φ descends flatness. That is, suppose for all R-modules M,
if S ⊗R M is S-flat, then M is R-flat. If φ is additionally injective, then it satisfies
(O). Indeed, S ⊗R M = 0 implies M is R-flat. Then M −→S ⊗R M is injective by the
injectivity of φ, and so M = 0. Note that a universally injective ring map is injective
and descends flatness [Sta, Tag 08XD]. If φ is injective and R is Noetherian, then φ
satisfies (O) if and only if φ descends flatness by [RG71, Part II, Corollaire 1.2.10].

7. A FORMALLY UNRAMIFIED MAP THAT IS NOT B-NIL FORMALLY UNRAMIFIED
In this section, we show that there is a formally unramified ring map in positive prime
characteristic that is not b-nil-formally unramified. The example is a very minor modifica-
tion of an example by Gabber of a Q-algebra that is formally unramified but not reduced,
which we learned about from the paper by Mukhopadhyay–Smith [MS21].
Let p > 0 be a prime number and k be a field of characteristic p. Consider the category
C of local k-algebras (A, m, k) such that m[p] = 0. Alternatively, this is the category of
k-algebras A with an ideal I ⊂A such that:
(1) k −→A/I is an isomorphism, and
(2) I[p] = 0.
In particular, any k-algebra homomorphism between two such k-algebras is local. Note also
that if (A, m, k) ∈C, then k ,→A is an integral extension because Ap = kp ⊂k ⊂A. Indeed,
if a ∈A, then we can write a = u + x where u ∈k and x ∈m. Then ap = up + xp = up ∈kp.
