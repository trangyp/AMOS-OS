Abstract. Let (A, L) be a polarized abelian variety of dimension g ≥1 over an algebraically
closed field of characteristic zero. We prove that every nontrivial line bundle P in the con-
nected component Pic0(A) of the Picard variety is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay (ACM)
with respect to L. For g ≥2 and any fixed nontrivial P ∈Pic0(A), we construct by induc-
tion an infinite sequence of indecomposable ACM vector bundles Er of every rank r ≥1. In
addition, this paper studies classification questions for ACM line bundles and shows that, for
abelian varieties of dimension at least two, the category of ACM bundles is of wild representa-
tion type. This paper settles the existence problem for nontrivial ACM bundles on polarized
abelian varieties and supply large explicit families of indecomposable examples
1. Introduction
An important theme in algebraic geometry is to understand a projective variety through the
vector bundles it carries. Among these, two closely related classes have received a great deal
of attention: arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay (ACM) bundles and Ulrich bundles. If (X, L) is
a polarized projective variety, a vector bundle E is ACM with respect to L if
Hi(X, E ⊗L⊗m) = 0
for all m ∈Z and 0 < i < dim X.
Ulrich bundles are the ACM bundles with the strongest possible cohomological behavior, and
they often serve as the most rigid and useful objects in this circle of ideas.
These bundles matter for several reasons. In commutative algebra, ACM bundles correspond
to maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules over homogeneous coordinate rings, so they reflect subtle
properties of singularities, resolutions, and syzygies; see [5].
In algebraic geometry, Ulrich
bundles became especially prominent through the work of Eisenbud, Schreyer, and Weyman,
who showed that they give determinantal and Pfaffian expressions for Chow forms; see [6].
Beauville’s papers and survey give a clear picture of the importance of Ulrich bundles and of
the main existence problems around them; see [2, 3].
Two broad questions organize much of the subject.
The first is the existence problem:
given a polarized projective variety, does it carry an ACM bundle or an Ulrich bundle? The
second is the classification problem: if such bundles exist, how many indecomposable ones
are there, and how complicated are their families? A useful language for the second question
comes from representation theory. Following the now standard viewpoint, one studies the ACM
representation type of a polarized variety, which may be finite, tame, or wild depending on the
complexity of its indecomposable ACM bundles; see, for example, [14]. This perspective has
proved effective in measuring how complicated the geometry of ACM bundles really is.
Much of the existing literature concerns varieties whose geometry already favors cohomo-
logical vanishing, such as projective spaces, quadrics, Grassmannians, and several classes of

show that the ACM complexity of (A, L) is equal to one.
The rank-one case is only the beginning. Fix a nontrivial line bundle P ∈Pic0(A). For
every g ≥2 and every rank r ≥1, we construct an indecomposable ACM vector bundle Er of
rank r by iterated non-split extensions starting from P. The main difficulty is to prove that
suitable nontrivial extension classes exist at every step and that the resulting bundles remain
indecomposable. To do this, we use Mukai’s Fourier–Mukai transform [11, 15] and relate the
extension problem to finite-length modules over a regular local ring.
This viewpoint also leads to a representation-theoretic consequence.
Using the Fourier–
Mukai correspondence, we identify unipotent bundles on an abelian variety with finite-length
modules over a regular complete local ring. From this, we deduce that for abelian varieties
of dimension at least two, the category of ACM bundles is of wild representation type. Thus
polarized abelian varieties support not only explicit ACM bundles in every rank, but also a
maximally complicated ACM theory from the point of view of classification.
We also study classification questions for ACM line bundles. Under the assumption that the
Picard number is one, we characterize exactly which line bundles are ACM with respect to a
fixed polarization. For abelian surfaces, we give a more refined criterion in terms of intersection
theory and elliptic quotient structures.
Taken together, our results show that polarized abelian varieties carry abundant ACM bun-
dles despite the fact that the structure sheaf itself fails to be ACM in dimension at least
two. They provide explicit families of indecomposable ACM bundles in every rank, clarify the
behavior of numerically trivial line bundles with respect to a polarization, and place abelian
varieties firmly on the wild side of the ACM representation-type spectrum.

2.4. ACM and Ulrich sheaves.
Definition 2.9. Let X be a projective variety of dimension n, and let H be an ample line
bundle on X. A coherent sheaf F on X is called arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay, or ACM,
with respect to H, if
Hi(X, F ⊗H⊗m) = 0
for every m ∈Z and every 0 < i < n.
Remark 2.10. If n = 1, the ACM condition is vacuous, since there is no integer i satisfying
0 < i < 1.
Definition 2.11. Let X be a projective variety of dimension n, and let H be an ample line
bundle. A vector bundle E on X is called Ulrich with respect to H if
Hi(X, E ⊗H−j) = 0
for all i and all 1 ≤j ≤n.
Remark 2.12. When H is very ample, this cohomological definition is equivalent to the usual
definition that the graded module associated to E has a linear resolution over the homogeneous
coordinate ring. It is also equivalent to saying that E is ACM and has the maximal possible
number of global sections:
h0(X, E) = rk(E) degH(X).
See [6, 2].

4. Proof of the main theorem
We now prove the ACM vanishing.
Proposition 4.1 (Positive twists). Let A be an abelian variety, let L be ample, and let P ∈
Pic0(A). If m > 0, then
Hi(A, P ⊗L⊗m) = 0
for all i > 0.
Proof. By Lemma 2.4, P ⊗L⊗m is ample. Since char k = 0, Kodaira vanishing gives
Hi(A, ωA ⊗P ⊗L⊗m) = 0
for i > 0. By Lemma 2.5, ωA ≃OA. Therefore
Hi(A, P ⊗L⊗m) = 0

and let P ∈Pic0(A). If m < 0, then
Hi(A, P ⊗L⊗m) = 0

Theorem 10.5. Let A be a complex abelian variety of dimension g ≥2, and let L be an ample
line bundle on A. The category of Arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay (ACM) vector bundles on
A with respect to the polarization defined by L is of wild representation type.
Proof. Let U be the category of unipotent bundles on A. The Fourier-Mukai transform yields an
equivalence U ≃flmod(k[[x1, . . . , xg]]), which is classically known to be of wild representation
type for g ≥2. Fix a nontrivial line bundle P ∈Pic0(A). The exact functor Φ : U →Coh(A)
defined by U 7→P ⊗U is fully faithful, embedding U as a subcategory. By Lemma 10.4 ,
the essential image of Φ consists entirely of ACM bundles. Since a wild category embeds fully
faithfully into the category of ACM bundles, the latter is strictly wild.
