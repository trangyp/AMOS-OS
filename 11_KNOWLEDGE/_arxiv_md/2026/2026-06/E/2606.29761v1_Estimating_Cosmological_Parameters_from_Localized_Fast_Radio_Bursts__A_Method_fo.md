Estimating Cosmological Parameters from Localized Fast Radio Bursts: A Method for Removing
Milky Way Dispersion-Measure Contributions

ABSTRACT
Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are emerging as powerful probes for cosmology. However, cosmological
inference based on FRB dispersion measures (DMs) is limited by uncertainties in the Milky Way con-
tribution, including those from the Galactic interstellar medium and the Galactic halo. In this Letter,
we propose a method that eliminates the Milky Way contribution by using DM differences between
localized FRBs within the same sky region. The method removes the need to adopt a specific Galactic
electron-density model or a prior assumption for the Galactic halo DM. We validate the reliability
of the method using mock FRB samples and show that it successfully recovers the fiducial cosmo-
logical parameter. Applying the method to current localized FRB data, we obtain a constraint on
Γ ≡ΩbH0fd that differs from that inferred using the conventional treatment of the Milky Way con-
tribution. This difference highlights the importance of Milky Way DM systematics in FRB cosmology
and demonstrates the potential of differential DM methods for future large samples of localized FRBs.
1. INTRODUCTION
Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are millisecond-duration ra-
dio transients, typically observed at radiation frequen-
cies of order ∼GHz, first discovered by Lorimer et al.
in archival pulsar survey data (D. R. Lorimer et al.
2007). As FRB signals travel from their sources to the
observer, they interact with free electrons long the line
of sight, producing a frequency-dependent arrival-time
delay. The corresponding dispersion measure (DM) ac-
cumulates with propagation distance and therefore en-
codes information about the distribution of ionized mat-
ter in the Universe. Localized FRBs with identified host
galaxies and measured redshifts can thus be used as cos-
mological probes through their DM-redshift relation.
They have been used to explore the cosmic reioniza-
tion history (Z. Zheng et al. 2014; M. Caleb et al. 2019;

Since only
DMcos(z) directly carries the cosmological information,
conventional FRB cosmology requires subtracting the
contributions of DMISM, DMhalo and DMhost from the
observed DM. The Milky Way ISM contribution is usu-
ally estimated using Galactic electron-density models,
such as NE2001, YMW16, and NE2025 (J. M. Cordes
& T. J. W. Lazio 2002; J. M. Yao et al. 2017; S. K.
Ocker & J. M. Cordes 2026). Howver, different Galactic
electron density models can lead to noticeably differ-
ent constraints on the Hubble constant H0 (Y.-Y. Wang
et al. 2025).
The Galactic halo contribution, DMhalo, is commonly
treated either as a fixed value or as a random variable
drawn from an assumed distribution. However, current
observational estimates remain model dependent. Based
on constraints derived from the all-sky HI 21 cm emis-
sion map, OVII absorption, and the DM to the Large
Magellanic Cloud, together with hydrostatic halo-gas
models, DMhalo has been estimated to fall within in the
range of 50–80 pc cm−3. (J. X. Prochaska & Y. Zheng
2019).
By contrast, FRB 20220319D gives a conser-
vative upper limit of 28.7 or 47.3 pc cm−3 (V. Ravi
et al. 2025). Analyses of FRB data at Galactic latitudes
|b| > 30o yields upper limits on DMhalo, ranging from 52
to 111 pc cm−3 (A. M. Cook et al. 2023). In addition, a
data-driven reconstruction of the all-sky distribution of
the dispersion measure contribution from the Galactic
halo indicates that DMhalo may be anisotropic (Y. Liu
et al. 2026a). As a result, there is currently no unique or
model-independent estimate of DMhalo. Previous stud-
ies have also shown that FRB-based cosmological con-
straints are sensitive to the assumed Galactic-halo DM
distribution (C. Xu et al. 2025; Y. Liu et al. 2025). This
suggests that adopting a fixed value or a prescribed dis-
tribution for DMhalo may introduce systematic uncer-
tainties or biases into the inferred cosmological param-
eters.
It is therefore important either to model the Milky
Way contribution more accurately or to construct ob-
servables that are insensitive to it. In this Letter, we
propose a differential DM method that eliminates the
Milky Way contribution when extracting cosmological
information from localized FRBs. The central idea is
to compare FRBs located within the same sky region,
where the Milky Way ISM and halo contributions are
approximately common. Their difference then removes
the Milky Way term.
We validate the method using
simulated FRB samples and then apply it to current
localized FRB data.

3. SIMULATION
Our method is based on the assumption that both
DMISM and DMhalo vary negligibly within each adopted
sky region. We first test the validity of this assumption.
We generate 64,800 mock sky positions uniformly dis-
tributed over the sky and assign each position a Milky
Way contribution of DMMW = DMISM + DMhalo. The
ISM contribution DMISM is generated using the NE2025
Galactic electron-density model (S. K. Ocker & J. M.
Cordes 2026), while the halo contribution DMhalo is
generated using the YT2020 model (S. Yamasaki & T.
Totani 2020). We exclude the low-Galactic-latitude re-
gion |b| < 15◦, where the dense and complex Galactic
environment produces large and rapidly varying Milky
Way DMs.
We further exclude an overdense region
around l ≈260◦and b ≈−15◦, associated with the
Gum and Vela clumps considered in (S. K. Ocker &
J. M. Cordes 2026).
In this region.
DMMW exhibits
significant spatial variation. None of observed localized
FRBs used below lies within this excluded region.
To quantify the spatial variation of DMMW, we em-
ploy the coefficient of variation (CV), defined as CV ≡
σ(DMMW)/µ(DMMW), where σ and µ denote the stan-
dard deviation and mean of DMMW within a given sky
region, respectively. A smaller CV indicates a more ho-
mogeneous distribution of DMMW. We adopt CV < 0.2
as the criterion for approximate homogeneity. We then
examine different sky-partitioning schemes.
Starting
with the full sky, we divide the Galactic longitude range
0◦-360◦and Galactic latitude range −90◦-90◦into 2×2,
4 × 4, and 8 × 8 bins, corresponding to 4, 16, and 64 sky
regions, respectively. Without partitioning, and also in
the 2 × 2 scheme, the CV values greatly exceed 0.2, in-
dicating substantial variations in DMMW. In the 4 × 4
svhem, most regions still have CV > 0.2. In the 8 × 8
scheme, all regions satisfy the criterion CV < 0.2, ex-
cept for the overdense Gum-Vela region discussed above.
These results motivate the use of an 8 × 8 sky partition.
The CV distributions for the 4 × 4 and 8 × 8 schemes
are shown in Fig. 1.

4. CONSTRAINT ON Γ FROM LOCALIZED FRBS
We now apply the method to 127 observed localized
FRBs, listed in Appendix. A. In our analysis, we ex-
clude FRB20190520B and FRB20220831A because their
unusually large DMs are likely dominated by the lo-
cal source environments (Q. Wu et al. 2022; L. Connor
et al. 2025). We also exclude FRB20221027A because
of ambiguity in its host-galaxy association (K. Sharma
et al. 2024). Furthermore, we remove FRBs with red-
shift z < 0.02 or Galactic latitude |b| < 15◦(Y. C. Zhang
et al. 2026; Y. Liu et al. 2026a). Finally, one FRB with
redshift z > 1.5 is excluded, because the simulations
used to calibrate the host-galaxy DM distribution ex-
tend only to z = 1.5 (G. Q. Zhang et al. 2020). This
leaves 99 FRBs in the final sample. Their sky distribu-
tion is shown in Fig. 2.
We use the 8 × 8 sky-partitioning scheme established
above. In each sky region, the lowest-redshift FRB is
taken as the reference event, corresponding to DMobs,1,
while the remaining FRBs in the same region are used
as DMobs,2.
We then construct ∆DMobs and use the
likelihood described in Sec. 2 to constrain Γ.
After performing the MCMC analysis using the emcee
package (D. Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013), we obtain
Γ = 2.88 ± 0.33.
The corresponding posterior distri-
bution is presented by the red curve in Fig. 3. Fixing
Ωbh2 = 0.02237 and H0 = 67.36 km s−1 Mpc−1, we find
fd = 0.867 ± 0.099 from the constraint on Γ. For com-
parison, we also analyze the same FRB sample using the
conventional method. In this case, the ISM contribution
is subtracted using the NE2025 model, while DMhalo is
treated as a free parameter. The resulting posterior dis-
tribution is shown by the blue curve in Fig. 3. We find
Γ = 3.55 ± 0.17 and DMhalo = 51+8
−5 pc cm−3. With the
same fixed values of Ωbh2 and H0, this corresponds to
fd = 1.069 ± 0.051. The value of Γ inferred from our
differential method is lower than that obtained using
the conventional method by about 1.8σ. This difference
suggests that the treatment of DMMW can have a signif-
icant impact on cosmological parameter estimation from
FRBs. This conclusion is consistent with previous stud-
ies showing that different Galactic electron-density mod-
els and different assumptions about the Galactic halo
contribution can lead to noticeably different constraints
on H0 or fd (Y.-Y. Wang et al. 2025; C. Xu et al. 2025;
Y. Liu et al. 2025).
5. CONCLUSIONS AND DISCUSSIONS
Localized FRBs provide a promising probe of cos-
mology through the relation between DM and redshift.
However, extracting the cosmological DM component
from the observed DM requires accounting for several
foreground and environmental contributions. In partic-
ular, the Milky Way ISM and halo contributions are usu-
ally estimated using Galactic electron-density models
and assumed halo-DM prescriptions. The uncertainties
in these terms propagate directly into the inferred cos-
mic DM and can therefore bias cosmological constraints.
In this Letter, we have proposed a differential method
that removes the Milky Way contribution without re-
lying on a specific Galactic electron-density model or a
prior assumption about DMhalo. The method compares
localized FRBs within the same sky region and uses their
DM differences. Under the assumption that the Milky
Way contribution varies weakly within each region, the
ISM and halo terms cancel in the difference, leaving a
quantity sensitive only to the cosmic and host-galaxy
DM differences.
Using mock FRB samples, we tested the reliability of
the method. For simulations generated with the fiducial
value Γ = 3.12, the method recovers Γ = 3.10 ± 0.19,
demonstrating that it can accurately infer the input cos-
mological parameter under controlled conditions. Ap-
plying the method to current localized FRB data, we ob-
tain Γ = 2.88±0.33, corresponding to fd = 0.867±0.099
when Ωbh2 and H0 are fixed to their Planck 2018 val-
ues. By contrast, the conventional method applied to
the same sample gives Γ = 3.55 ± 0.17 and DMhalo =
51+8
−5 pc cm−3, corresponding to fd = 1.069±0.051. The
discrepancy between the two approaches indicates that
