ABSTRACT. Prior work has shown that shifted contact derived Artin stacks admit smooth
Darboux atlases. However, establishing enumerative invariants and linearizing these cate-
gorical structures requires equivariant local models. Working over an algebraically closed
field K of characteristic zero, we establish an equivariant Darboux theorem for −1-shifted
contact derived Artin stacks. We prove that, in the smooth topology, these stacks admit
smooth atlases by the derived contact Darboux scheme ∆loc(s) associated to the derived dis-
criminant locus of a relative section s. In the presence of reductive stabilizers G, this refines
to the equivariant geometric quotient stack [∆loc(s)/G]. By applying the BBDJS minimal
model to the derived symplectification and descending algebraically along the structural free
Gm-action, we construct an ℓ-adic perverse sheaf on any oriented −1-shifted contact stack.
We utilize Verdier’s specialization equivalence for monodromic sheaves to equip this per-
verse sheaf with a tame geometric monodromy automorphism T. This structure allows for
the extraction of derived enumerative invariants via the ℓ-adic Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace,
thereby resolving the issue of generic topological acyclicity.
The content of the other main results in this paper relies on a prior work, in which we
have shown that derived intersections of n-shifted Legendrians yield (n −1)-shifted contact
stacks and formulated the non-linear 2-categories of Legendrians Fc(X) and Legn. Using this
geometric setup, we formulate in this paper a contact analogue of Joyce’s conjecture to lin-
earize these structures. We then construct the categorified Legendrian 2-categories LFc(X)
and LLeg0 via ℓ-adic Fourier-Mukai pull-push functors, connecting the study of derived con-
tact moduli spaces to microlocal sheaf theory.

1. INTRODUCTION AND SUMMARY
The local structure of Artin stacks in derived symplectic geometry is governed by the
Darboux theorem of Ben-Bassat, Brav, Bussi, and Joyce [2, Theorem 2.10], which guarantees
the existence of smooth atlases in Darboux form. For the purposes of Donaldson-Thomas
enumerative theory, Kresch’s theorem [19, Proposition 3.5.9] combined with the assign-
ment of invariant d-critical structures [16, Example 2.55] locally refines this model to a
geometric quotient stack [dCrit( f )/G] by a reductive group G. A perverse sheaf of van-
ishing cycles on a −1-shifted symplectic stack directly descends from this local model [3,
Theorem 4.4]. These local-to-global assignments provide the setup for the categorification
of Lagrangian correspondences in the sense of Amorim and Ben-Bassat [1, Theorem 1.1].
In the contact setting, the local algebraic theory of shifted contact structures was es-
tablished in [6, Section 3]. The generalization demonstrating that shifted contact derived
Artin stacks admit smooth Darboux atlases was proven in [7, Theorem 3.7]. In addition,
prior work [14, Theorem 3.1], [15, Theorem 1.1], has developed the global geometric setup
for shifted contact stacks. We proved that the derived intersection of Legendrians yields
contact spaces and constructed the global non-linear derived Legendrian categories Fc(X)
and Legn.
To define homological invariants and linearize these intersection categories, a smooth
atlas is insufficient. One requires a geometric quotient by a reductive group to calculate
equivariant ´etale vanishing cycles over a base field. This paper transports the equivariant
Darboux theorem and the BBDJS perverse sheaf formalism into the context of derived con-
tact geometry. By establishing the equivariant contact Darboux theorem, we prove that a
−1-shifted contact stack admits a smooth derived Darboux atlas, which is locally equiv-
alent to a geometric quotient stack in the presence of stabilizing groups in the smooth
topology. We use this approach to linearize the derived Legendrian categories and define
enumerative invariants, connecting contact moduli spaces to microlocal sheaf theory.
Conventions and notations. Let K denote an algebraically closed field of characteristic
zero. All commutative differential graded algebras (cdgas) are concentrated in nonposi-
tive degrees and defined over K [24]. Classical K-schemes are locally of finite type, and all
derived K-schemes and stacks X are locally finitely presented. For constructible sheaves
and perverse sheaves, we work in the derived category Db
c(Xet, F) of algebraically con-
structible ℓ-adic sheaves on the Lisse-´etale topos, where F is a finite extension of Qℓor Qℓ
for a prime ℓ. We rely on the formalisms of Laszlo-Olsson [20, Section 2] and Liu-Zheng
[22, Section 6].
Summary of results. We present the main theorems. The first theorem establishes that the
Darboux models of shifted contact stacks can be naturally represented via the derived dis-
criminant locus.
Theorem 1.1 (Equivariant Contact Darboux Theorem). Every −1-shifted contact derived Artin
stack Z over K admits a smooth atlas by the contact Darboux scheme Spec (A) ≃∆loc(s), where
s is a regular relative section. Furthermore, if Z has reductive stabilizers G, it is locally equivalent
in the smooth topology to the geometric quotient stack [∆loc(s)/G].
Using derived symplectification and Verdier’s specialization equivalence for monodromic
perverse sheaves, we descend the BBDJS vanishing cycles from the symplectification to the
contact stack.

3. THE EQUIVARIANT STACKY CONTACT DARBOUX THEOREM
We represent shifted contact stacks using smooth Darboux atlases, which refine to quo-
tient stacks in the presence of reductive stabilizers over K.
Theorem 3.1 (Equivariant Contact Darboux Theorem). Every −1-shifted contact derived Artin
stack Z over K admits a smooth atlas by a contact Darboux scheme Spec (A) ≃∆loc(s), where s
is a regular function. If Z has a reductive stabilizer group G, it is locally equivalent in the smooth
topology to the quotient stack [∆loc(s)/G].
Proof. By the Darboux theorem for derived Artin stacks [2, Theorem 2.10], the derived sym-
plectification eZ admits a smooth atlas by a Darboux scheme dCrit( ef ) inside a local affine
chart U ≃Spec (K[x1, . . . , xn]) × Gm. If Z has a reductive stabilizer group G, we assume
it acts by symmetries preserving the contact line bundle, so that its action commutes with
the Gm-scaling action on the symplectification. This allows the local model to be made
(G × Gm)-equivariant by combining Kresch’s theorem on the stratification of Artin stacks
by quotient stacks [19] with the assignment of invariant d-critical structures [16]. This pro-
duces the local quotient stacks [dCrit( ef )/G] for eZ and [dCrit( ef )/(G × Gm)] for Z in the
smooth topology.
The Gm-action scales the symplectic form by weight 1 [6, Section 4.2], implying the ho-
mogeneity condition ef (x, t) = t · s(x) for a regular function s ∈O(Spec (K[x1, . . . , xn])).

5. THE CONTACT JOYCE CONJECTURE

we formulate the contact analogue of Joyce’s conjecture [1, Conjecture 5.18].

derived stack over K. Let φ: L →X be a proper oriented Legendrian. There exists a morphism in

8. OUTLOOK AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS
The geometric and cohomological structures established in this paper outline methods
for future research in derived contact geometry and representation theory.
8.1. Derived Contactomorphisms and Legendromorphisms. The categorification of La-
grangian correspondences in [1] requires an evaluation of symplectomorphisms between
local Darboux models. In the derived contact setting, formulating a rigid definition for
an n-shifted contactomorphism f : X1 →X2 remains an open question. We expect this
structure to involve an equivalence of the contact distributions K1
∼
−→f ∗K2 compatible
with the tangent complexes, alongside a specified local condition on the space of shifted
1-forms. Evaluating the coordinate transformations between the local Darboux models es-
tablished in Theorem 3.1 provides a concrete method to study these expected equivalences
and their local deformations. This analysis is required to formulate Gray’s Stability The-
orem for shifted contact structures. This setup must also extend to contactomorphisms of
pairs (Legendrian equivalences), which represent equivalences of Legendrian correspon-
dences compatible with the contact structures. Investigating the auto-equivalences of the
categorified category LFc(X) could provide a categorical approach to defining derived
contactomorphisms.
8.2. Motivic Contact DT Invariants and CoHAs. The Gm-equivariant local quotient con-
structions and the BBDJS minimal models are algebraic. This property allows the ℓ-adic
perverse sheaf PZ to lift to the motivic setting [8]. Over a finite field Fq, by virtue of
Deligne’s proof of the Weil conjectures (Weil II [26]), the sheaf admits a mixed ℓ-adic struc-
ture equipped with a weight filtration [3]. The Lefschetz trace of the geometric Frobenius
Frobq acting on the stalks yields an arithmetic motivic Contact DT Invariant. Extracting the
invariant at the level of the Grothendieck ring of Hodge modules yields a Hodge-Deligne
polynomial tracking the Hodge weight filtration and quantum dimension gradings. The
exactness of the ℓ-adic Fourier-Mukai pull-push correspondence functors formulated in
LFc(X) establishes that shifted contact moduli spaces encode the operations required to
define Contact Cohomological Hall Algebras [18]. Applying this formalism to the derived
contact mapping torus of ℓ-adic local systems could provide the geometric representation
theory necessary to recover quantum affine algebras and Yangians. The non-commutative
deformations governed by the A∞-algebra structure specifically act as the algebraic gener-
ators for these Cohomological Hall Algebras (CoHAs).
8.3. Microlocal Sheaf Theory and Contact Homological Mirror Symmetry. In classical
symplectic topology, the Nadler-Zaslow theorem [23] identifies the Fukaya category of a
cotangent bundle with constructible sheaves on the base. For shifted contact stacks, our
categorified Legendrian category LFc(X) acts as the algebraic analogue of the category
of microlocal sheaves. This formulation establishes a basis for Contact Homological Mir-
ror Symmetry, where LFc(X) serves as a constructible sheaf model for the category of
Legendrian branes. A Contact Nadler-Zaslow correspondence utilizing this perverse lin-
earization connects the derived intersection theory of contact stacks to microlocal Morse
theory.
8.4. Derived Contact Surgery and SFT. In classical symplectic topology, Symplectic Field
Theory evaluates pseudo-holomorphic curves with boundary on Legendrians in contact
manifolds [10, Section 2]. By replacing pseudo-holomorphic curves with derived mapping
stacks of topological cobordisms, and replacing Floer chain complexes with ℓ-adic perverse
