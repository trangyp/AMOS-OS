Electric charge fluctuations χQ
n allow comparisons between theory and experiment, but are elusive
on the lattice due to severe cutoff effects. We use a 4HEX action to obtain χQ
2 and, for the first
time ever, χQ
4 in the continuum limit. We find disagreement with the hadron resonance gas (HRG)
model, which we cannot explain with finite volume effects. We include light meson interactions in
the HRG model via the S-matrix, reducing the tension for χQ
4 , but worsening the agreement for χQ
2 .
We propose measuring the ratio χQ
4 /χQ
2 at the LHC to investigate this tension.
Introduction
The phase structure of matter described
by Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) is very rich, and
has been meticulously scrutinized in the last decades,
especially in the low baryon density region.
A low-
temperature hadronic phase is set apart from a high-
temperature quark gluon plasma (QGP) phase by a
smooth crossover around T = 155−160 MeV [1–3]. First-
principles lattice simulations are the most robust tool
to investigate QCD thermodynamics at low-to-moderate
baryon densities in the nonperturbative regime, i.e. in
the vicinity of the transition (see Refs. [4–6] for a review).
Fluctuations of conserved charges of different orders
enable a detailed study of the QCD medium, as they
are sensitive to hadronic interactions [7–9], remnants
of possible critical behavior [10, 11] and carry informa-
tion about the effective degrees of freedom in the sys-
tem [12–17].
Compared to other observables, fluctua-
tions can be more directly related to measurable quan-
tities in heavy-ion collisions (HICs), namely the event-
by-event fluctuations in the number of particles carry-
ing baryon number B, electric charge Q, or strangeness
S [4, 18–20], although a number of experimental effects
ought to be accounted for before direct comparisons can
be made [21, 22].
Fluctuations of the electric charge
have a particular advantage in this regard: in contrast
to fluctuations of baryon number and strangeness, they
are measured directly and do not require the use of prox-
ies to account for electrically neutral hadrons that are
not measured [23, 24].
From a theoretical standpoint, fluctuations of con-
served charges are relatively easy to calculate in the
grand canonical ensemble, and have been studied ex-
tensively with perturbative methods [25–28], with func-
tional approaches [29] such as the functional renormal-
ization group (FRG) [30–32] and Dyson-Schwinger equa-
tions (DSE) [33–35], lattice simulations and models such
as the hadron resonance gas (HRG) [9, 24, 36–39] (see
also Ref. [40] for a review). They are defined as deriva-
tives of the free energy with respect to the corresponding
chemical potentials:

In recent years, many thermodynamic quantities of
QCD have been calculated by more than one lattice col-
laboration, showing in general very good agreement be-
tween different discretizations of the QCD action: a fact
that in turn further validates these results. This is the
case for the QCD transition line [2, 3, 41], the equation of
state both at zero and finite chemical potential [42, 43],
as well as low order fluctuations of conserved charges at
zero [24, 44–48] and finite density [20, 49]. Fluctuations
of the electric charge are a notable exception.
Electric charge fluctuations are particularly difficult
observables in lattice QCD because they are largely dom-
inated by pions, which in staggered discretizations suf-
fer more than other hadrons from the breaking of taste
symmetry at finite lattice spacing, due to their smaller
mass. Although this effect disappears in the continuum
theory, continuum extrapolations for electric charge fluc-
tuations are especially delicate due to the need for very
fine lattices. Continuum-extrapolated results exist from
the HotQCD collaboration (with the HISQ action) for
χQ

In this Letter we present results for the second and –
for the first time in the literature – fourth order electric
charge fluctuations in the range T = 120 −160 MeV in
the continuum limit. We utilize our 4HEX action [52],
where the taste breaking is strongly suppressed (see Fig.
3 in the Supplemental Material of Ref. [52]) compared to
the HISQ and 4stout actions. This allows us to perform a
continuum limit without the need to resort to extremely
fine lattices.
We compare our results to predictions from the HRG
model, finding no agreement for χQ
4 and a tension on χQ
2
below T = 130 MeV. Since it has been suggested that the
finite volume typical of lattice simulations can spoil the
agreement with HRG model predictions [47], we perform
HRG calculations for the same finite volume we simulate
on the lattice.
We find that finite volume effects, al-
though present, cannot account for the discrepancies we
observe. We also perform dedicated lattice QCD simula-
tions in smaller volumes for T = 130 MeV, and find that
finite volume effects act in the opposite direction from
that needed to ease the tension.
We then carry out an analysis based on the S-matrix
formulation of the HRG model [53], including experimen-
tal input for hadron scattering in the π-π and π-K chan-
nels. We find that the tension between lattice and HRG
results is reduced for χQ
4 , but at the same time, the agree-
ment for χQ
2 is lost. We show in Fig. 1 our continuum
extrapolated results for the ratio χQ
4 /χQ
2 , compared to re-
sults from the HRG model with and without the inclusion
of light meson scattering information. Although this in-
clusion reduces the tension, the results remain markedly
different.
We also include continuum-extrapolated re-
sults for the same ratio obtained with the 4stout action
above T = 150 MeV. We advocate measuring this ratio
in HICs at the LHC to shed light on the role of meson
interactions in the QCD medium, as existing measure-
ments at the highest RHIC energy [54, 55] do not have
sufficient precision to do so.
Lattice analysis
In this Letter we employ our 4HEX
action [52] with Nf = 2 + 1 rooted staggered dynamical
flavors with physical masses and the DBW2 action [56]
in the gauge sector, while in the fermion sector the links
are improved with 4 levels of HEX smearing [57]. The
main advantage of this action is the reduced taste break-
ing in the pion sector, particularly important for observ-
ables dominated by pion dynamics. We employ two dif-
ferent scale settings [52], one based on the pion decay
constant fπ, the other (w1) on the modified Wilson-flow-
based w0 [58].

We note that HRG models
are often extended to incorporate π-N interactions and
repulsive baryon-baryon interactions [37].
The former
were discussed in the context of the proton yield in HICs
and the baryon-charge correlator [68, 69, 75], while the
latter, incorporated via an excluded volume, primarily
affect baryon number susceptibilities [9]. Using the im-
plementation of these effects in Thermal-FIST [39], we
have verified that they have a negligible influence on χQ
2
and χQ
4 at temperatures T ≲150 MeV.
These results indicate that the description of pion in-
teractions within the HRG model is incomplete, even
within its extended S-matrix formulation, which takes
into account the role of two-body, but not three-body
(or more) interactions. In the Supplemental Material we
further explore this issue by considering an alternative
implementation of pion interactions based on an effective
mass model (EMM) [76, 77]. The EMM leads to a quali-
tatively different hierarchy of interaction effects where χQ
2
is affected only weakly, while χQ
4 receives a visible correc-
tion. This illustrates that the simultaneous description of
χQ
2 and χQ
4 is sensitive to how pion interactions are mod-
eled, and likely requires physics beyond the present two-
body S-matrix HRG treatment. It is also worth noting
that lattice studies of in-medium pion spectral properties
above the chiral crossover find pion-like excitations with
modified spectral structure [78]. Although these results
apply at temperatures above the range considered here,
they suggest that charge fluctuations may be sensitive
to in-medium modifications of the pion sector beyond a
two-body scattering treatment.

Conclusions
In
this
Letter,
we
presented
our
continuum-extrapolated results for the second- and
fourth-order electric charge fluctuations. Our results for
χQ
2 confirm our previous ones [63], while for χQ
4 it is the
first such result in the literature. We compared our re-
sults to the HRG model, finding fair agreement for χQ
2
but a large tension for χQ
4 . We investigated the role of a
finite volume both within the HRG, with calculations in
a finite box, and within lattice QCD via dedicated runs
at T = 130 MeV on LT = 2, 3 lattices. We find that
smaller volumes increase the values obtained for both
observables, at all temperatures, both in the HRG model
and from lattice simulations, but the effect at LT = 4
is rather small. We then incorporated corrections to the
HRG description via the S-matrix approach by including
phase shifts for π-π and π-K channels. The inclusion of
these interactions reduced the tension for χQ
4 , but at the
same time spoiled the agreement for χQ
2 . The tension is
also reduced for the ratio χQ
4 /χQ
2 , but remains sizable at
all temperatures relevant for HICs. We thus suggest the
experimental measurement of this quantity to clarify the
source of this disagreement.

Treatment of pion interactions in the HRG model
We explore how charge susceptibilities are affected by
different implementations of interactions in the HRG
model. Fig. 5 shows the effect of successively including
π-π interactions (light blue) and then π-K interactions
(orange, as in the main text). The comparison demon-
strates that the dominant contribution comes from the
π-π channel, while the additional effect of the π-K chan-
nel is relatively small.
The S-matrix corrections shown in Fig. 5 are evaluated
in the Beth–Uhlenbeck form, using elastic phase shifts in
the relevant non-resonant two-meson channels. For the
π-π contribution, we include the isotensor channels with
I = 2, namely the repulsive J = 0 S-wave and the J = 2
D-wave. The corresponding phase shifts are taken from
the conformal parameterizations of Ref. [70]. The I = 2,
J = 0 channel uses the low- and intermediate-energy pa-
rameterizations matched at √s = 0.85 GeV, while the
I = 2, J = 2 channel is included up to √s = 1.42 GeV.
For the π-K contribution, we include the S-wave chan-
nels with I = 3/2 and I = 1/2, using the conformal
parameterizations of Ref. [71]. The I = 3/2 channel is
repulsive and is integrated up to √s = 1.74 GeV, while
the attractive I = 1/2 channel is restricted to the elastic
region below the K-η threshold. The appropriate spin
degeneracy factors and charge weights are included for
each isospin multiplet when computing χQ
2 and χQ
4 .
