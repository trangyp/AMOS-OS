Abstract
We prove that the Gromov–Witten invariants of the elliptic orbifold lines P1
3,3,3, P1
2,4,4, and

analogue of the so-called Toda conjecture in the Gromov-Witten theory of P1 or more precisely
its non-extended version. A new feature in our constructions is a certain bilinear operator whose
principal symbol can be expressed in terms of elliptic theta functions.

Introduction
1.1
Background of the problem
Gromov–Witten (GW) invariants were introduced by Witten in [36]. They stimulated very fast and
very exciting developments in many areas of mathematics, such as, algebraic/complex geometry,
symplectic geometry, and integrable systems. Especially when it comes to integrable systems, the
impact of GW theory is remarkable. The starting point is what is now called Witten’s conjecture,
that is, Witten conjectured in [36] that the generating function of GW invariants of the point is a
tau-function of the KdV hierarchy. The conjecture was proved shortly afterwards by Kontsevich [24].
Although, Witten was skeptical that his conjecture can be generalized to other target manifolds,
it turns out that GW theory provides a natural tool for constructing commuting flows, that is
integrable hierarchies.
The first general result in this direction is due to Dubrovin and Zhang.
They developed a general formalism in [12] for constructing bi-Hamiltonian integrable hierarchies
and they conjectured the existence of a bi-Hamiltonian integrable hierarchy which in particular
generalizes Witten’s conjecture in the case when the target manifold has semi-simple quantum
cohomology. The conjectures of Dubrovin and Zhang were established in [7] and [25]. The resulting
integrable hierarchy is now known as the Dubrovin-Zhang (DZ) hierarchy. More generally, using
ideas from Symplectic Field Theory, Buryak [6] was able to generalize the construction of Dubrovin
and Zhang, that is, associate an integrable hierarchy for any (partial) Cohomological Field Theory.
Buryak’s hierarchy is known as the Double Ramification (DR) hierarchy. In case the cohomological
field theory is semi-simple, it was proved by Blot–Lewanski–Shadrin in [4] that the DR hierarchy
2


## Page 3


is Miura equivalent to the DZ hierarchy. Therefore, indeed the DR hierarchy is a generalization of
the DZ hierarchy.
The main drawback of the DR and the DZ hierarchies is that they both rely on GW theory to
define the flows. In some sense, both theories should be viewed as existence results. The natural
question is to determine what kind of initial information is necessary to define the hierarchies.
Of course knowing the entire cohomological field theory is sufficient but usually the necessary
information is much smaller. In this paper we will be interested in the semi-simple case. Everything
is determined by genus-0, that is, by the Frobenius manifold. Furthermore, since a semi-simple
Frobenius manifold can be viewed as a solution to a classical Riemann-Hilbert problem, we expect
that the flows of the DZ (or equivalently DR) hierarchy can be described explicitly in terms of the
monodromy data of the Frobenius manifold.
Let us focus now on the case of Frobenius manifolds coming from quantum cohomology. Let X be
a complex orbifold such that its orbit space is a projective variety. Following Abramovich–Graber–
Vistoli (see [1]) or Chen–Ruan (see [8]) we can define orbifold GW invariants of X. In particular, by
using genus-0 GW invariants, we can define orbifold quantum cohomology. It is a very interesting
problem in algebraic geometry to classify all orbifolds X whose quantum cohomology is semi-simple.
According to Givental and Dubrovin–Zhang, such orbifolds must be very special because their GW
theory is integrable, i.e., we can expect explicit formulas for the GW invariants. The classification in
complex dimension 1 is already known: X must be an orbifold projective line P1
a1,...,ar where ai ≥2
are the orders of the isotropy groups of the orbifold points. The question that motivates our work is
to obtain an explicit description of the integrable hierarchies that govern the GW theory of P1
a1,...,ar.
Let us point out that although the DZ hierarchy is known to exist, it is very difficult to obtain an
explicit description of its flows. In some sense, only if we already have a concrete candidate for the
DZ hierarchy, we have a good chance to prove that it coincides with the DZ hierarchy. In that sense,
it is important to find an alternative more explicit method to construct the integrable hierarchy.
Such a method was proposed by Givental in [18]. It is based on his higher genus reconstruction
formalism. The main observation of Givental is that the Kac–Wakimoto, or more accurately the
Sato, approach to integrable hierarchies, based on vertex operators and tau-functions, fits very
naturally the higher genus reconstruction formalism.
The current paper is a continuation of my joint work with Yefeng Shen and Hsian-Hua Tseng. We
constructed in [32] an integrable hierarchy in the form of Hirota Bilinear Equations for every orbifold
line of positive orbifold Euler characteristic. Moreover, we were able to identify our hierarchy with
one of the Kac–Wakimoto hierarchies. In this paper we consider the case of elliptic orbifold lines,
that is, orbifold lines whose orbifold Euler characteristic is 0. There are four of them: P1
3,3,3, P1
2,4,4,
P1
2,3,6, and P1
2,2,2,2. We will prove that the total descendant potential satisfies a system of HQEs
similar to the HQEs of a Kac–Wakimoto hierarchy. Although our construction works for all 4 cases,
when it comes to explicit formulas, the last case, that is, P1
2,2,2,2 requires different notation, so we
decided to drop it from this paper. As a byproduct of our proof, we established a new property of the
Kac–Wakimoto form of the KdV hierarchy. Namely, the bilinear equations of the Kac–Wakimoto
hierarchy yield an infinite sequence of bilinear relations corresponding to the higher modes of the
Casimir operator – see Proposition 6.1. I learned this fact from Bojko Bakalov and apparently a
similar property holds for all Kac–Wakimoto hierarchies. Our integrable hierarchy does not include
the dynamics in the variables qk,0,0 (k > 1). These variables correspond to the descendant insertions
of ϕ0,0 = 1. They are viewed as parameters in this paper. Thanks to Proposition 6.1, the same
construction as in [26] should work in the current settings. We leave the details for a separate paper.

1.6
Structure of the paper
Section 2 can be viewed as an extension of the introduction. We introduce the necessary back-
ground on gamma integral structures and work out the explicit formula for the bilinear operator
Ωaff. Givental’s formula for the total descendant potential is recalled in Section 3. The formula is
introduced in two steps. First, in Section 3.2, by using the so-called calibration operator S(t, z) and
its quantization bS(t), the total descendant potential is transformed into the so called total ancestor
potential. The later has a geometric interpretation – it is the potential of the cohomological field
theory constructed via the Gromov–Witten theory of P1
a. In the second step, thanks to Teleman’s
theorem (see [35]), the total ancestor potential is transformed to a product of tau-functions of the
KdV hierarchy (see Section 3.3). The material in Sections 2 and 3 is well known. The proof of
Theorem 1.4 starts in Section 4. Conjugating by bS(t), we transform the HQEs for the total de-
scendant potential to HQEs for the total ancestor potential. This step is more or less the same as
in the original paper by Givental (see [18]). Nevertheless, now we have a better formula for the
coefficients of the bilinear operator which was discovered in [14] – see formulas (17) and (63). Also
we found an interpretation of the Virasoro operator L(λ) in terms of the Borcherds (−1)-product
(see [5]). These two improvements simplify the conjugation to some extent. The core of our proof
11


## Page 12


is in Section 5. The coefficients of the vertex operators of the ancestor HQEs are the so-called
periods of the Frobenius structure. Similarly to what was done in [18], using the tameness of the
total ancestor potential, we prove that the coefficients of the ancestor HQEs can be expressed in
terms of polynomials and theta series in the periods. This is done in Sections 5.1 and 5.5. The
next step is to prove that coefficients of the ancestor HQEs are single valued analytic functions with
possible poles only along the discriminant of the Frobenius manifold. This is the first place where
the techniques from [18] and [19] can not be used. There are two new ingredients: the first one is
the connection formula for the propagator series – see Proposition 5.8 and the second one is the
locality (in the sense of vertex algebras [13, 23]) of the vertex operators which leads to the formula
in Proposition 5.9. The locality is not a deep result but it requires some long computations (see
[27, 30]). The proof of the connection formula is based on the so-called Painleve property of a semi-
simple Frobenius manifold. It is a rather involved argument making use of deep results from the
theory of isomonodromic deformations. The proofs of both propositions are available in the draft
of our book [30] written jointly with Kyoji Saito. Using these results, we prove that the ancestor
HQEs are equivalent to proving that the coefficients of the ancestor HQEs extend analytically across
the discriminant. This is a local computation done in the last section of the paper, i.e., Section 6.
Again compared to the original work of Givental [18], the local computation can be simplified by
using the properties of the propagator series studied in Section 5.

with rφ(α) = α −(α|φ)φ. We will prove that the contribution of each pair is holomorphic at λ = ui

will see that the resulting operator is conjugate to the bilinear operator of the Kac–Wakimoto form
of the KdV hierarchy.
