Energy-energy correlators in p–Pb collisions at √sNN = 5.02 TeV
ALICE Collaboration*
Abstract
This paper presents the first measurement of the two-point energy–energy correlator (EEC) inside
charged-particle jets in p–Pb collisions at √sNN = 5.02 TeV with the ALICE experiment. The two-
point EEC, an infrared- and collinear-safe measure of angular energy flow within jets, is used to
probe both perturbative and non-perturbative stages of the jet evolution. The EEC is reported in
20–80 GeV/c jets in p–Pb collisions and compared to a measurement of the EEC in pp events at
√s = 5.02 TeV. A modification is observed in the 20–40 GeV/c interval, with an enhancement at
large opening angles and a suppression at small angles relative to pp collisions. The dependence
of this modification on jet transverse momentum, rapidity, and forward event activity is investigated.
These results provide new constraints on cold nuclear matter effects relevant for disentangling initial-
and final-state contributions to jet-structure modifications. An understanding of these cold nuclear
matter effects is also relevant for interpreting EEC measurements in heavy-ion collisions.

Introduction
In high-energy hadronic collisions, showers of secondary partons (quarks and gluons) are produced in
hard scattering processes between the quarks and gluons of the colliding particles. The high transverse-
momentum (pT) partons from the initial scattering undergo quantum chromodynamic (QCD) radiation,
splitting into lower-energy partons, until the hadronization energy scale is achieved. Below this scale,
partons undergo confinement into color-neutral hadrons, which ultimately stream into detectors. The
collimated sprays of hadrons, called jets, provide a clean probe of perturbative and non-perturbative
QCD dynamics. Their internal structure, called jet substructure, encodes the pattern of parton branching
and hadronization [1, 2].
The energy–energy correlator (EEC) quantifies angular correlations of energy flow within jets, providing
direct sensitivity to both early-time perturbative splittings and late-time hadronization dynamics [3–5].
The EEC was originally proposed as an event-shape observable for e+e−collisions [6–9], and was also
suggested as a tool for constructing jet discriminators [10–12]. It is infrared- and collinear-safe (IRC-
safe) [13] and, for vacuum collisions, can be perturbatively calculated from first principles in QCD [9].
EEC observables offer precision probes of perturbative and non-perturbative QCD dynamics across a
wide range of collision types [14].

The pT weight suppresses soft radiation, reducing the EEC’s sensitivity to higher-order corrections, the

Theoretical developments have enabled the calculation of the EEC in proton-proton (pp) collisions to
high accuracy [3, 15]. Various theoretical predictions exist for the EEC in jets produced in lead-lead
(Pb–Pb) collisions, where a host of other modifications come into effect [16–26].
The EEC has been previously reported in pp and Pb–Pb collisions by ALICE, ATLAS, and CMS at the
LHC [27–30], and in pp collisions by STAR at RHIC [31]. The pp measurements [27, 29, 31] show that
the perturbative and non-perturbative phases of jet evolution populate different angular regions. Addi-
tionally, the strong coupling constant αs can be extracted via ratios of the three-point energy correlator
(EEEC) to the two-point correlator (EEC) in pp collisions [28, 29], as proposed in [15]. The Pb–Pb
measurements by CMS [30] indicate that the presence of the quark–gluon plasma (QGP) leads to modi-
fications in the large RL region of the EEC.
This work presents the first measurement of the EEC inside jets produced in p–Pb collisions at √sNN =
5.02 TeV at the LHC. Comparing jet substructure between pp and proton-nucleus (pA) collisions pro-
vides insights into the effects of a cold (non-deconfined) QCD medium surrounding the colliding nucle-
ons [32–35]. Multiple scatterings in the nucleus or interactions between cold nuclear matter (CNM) and
the early parton shower can make an imprint on jet-substructure observables. However, jet measurements
in p–Pb collisions generally show small or no modifications relative to pp collisions. Observables such
as the jet nuclear modification factor (RpPb) [36], the jet fragmentation transverse momentum (jT) distri-
butions [37], the jet mass [38], the fragmentation function [39, 40], and the dijet kT [41] do not indicate

Experimental setup and datasets
ALICE is a general-purpose heavy-ion experiment at the LHC. This analysis utilizes the central-barrel
tracking system to measure jets and forward/backward detectors for triggering and to determine event
activity. The central tracker consists of the Inner Tracking System (ITS) [47] and the Time Projection
Chamber (TPC) [48]. These sit inside a 0.5 T magnetic field and have a high efficiency for detection
of charged particles with pT (pT,track) between 0.15 and 100 GeV/c, at pseudorapidity |η| < 0.9, and
azimuthal angle 0 < ϕ < 2π. The single-track efficiency is pT dependent, increasing from ∼60% at 0.15
GeV/c to ∼80% at 1 GeV/c, and remaining above ∼75% at higher pT. The momentum resolution, σp/p,
is ≈1% at pT,track = 1 GeV/c, increasing to ≈4% at pT,track = 4 GeV/c [27]. The angular resolution
is ≈1 mrad at pT,track = 1 GeV/c, decreasing to < 0.6 mrad for pT,track > 5 GeV/c [49]. More detailed
descriptions of the ALICE detector can be found in Refs. [50, 51].
The results presented here are from LHC Run 2 data; pp data were collected at √s = 5.02 TeV in 2017,
and p–Pb data were collected at √sNN = 5.02 TeV in 2016. Both data samples were collected with a
minimum bias (MB) trigger, requiring coincident hits in the V0 detector, which is composed of two arrays
of scintillator counters (V0A, V0C) at forward and backward rapidity [52]. The V0 arrays were located
on either side of the nominal interaction point, covering the full azimuth within the ranges 2.8 < η < 5.1
for V0A, and −3.7 < η < −1.7 for V0C. The amplitude in the V0A, located in the Pb-going direction,
was also used to determine the event activity of the collisions and is frequently used as a centrality
estimator in p–Pb measurements [53]. In this analysis, the V0A amplitude is used to define two event
activity classes (high-/low-multiplicity) for p–Pb events.
Events were required to have a primary vertex, reconstructed using the Silicon Pixel Detector (SPD),
within 10 cm of the nominal interaction point to provide uniform acceptance and high tracking efficiency.
Events with more than one reconstructed collision vertex were removed to avoid pileup. In total, 870
million MB pp events passed the event selection criteria, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
18.0 ± 0.4 nb−1 [54]. For the p–Pb data, 624 million events passed these criteria, corresponding to an
integrated luminosity of 298 ± 11 µb−1 [55]. Because of the asymmetry of the colliding beam energies
(protons at 4 TeV, and fully stripped 208
82 Pb ions at 1.58 TeV per nucleon [55]), a rapidity shift ∆y =

3.2
EEC background subtraction
To reduce the impact of non-jet particles from the UE, only constituents inside the reconstructed jets with
pT,track > 1 GeV/c are selected to enter the EEC. This 1 GeV/c threshold is varied during the analysis;
results are shown in section 5.1. Some UE particles will still remain in the jet, so the EEC distribution
itself has to be corrected for contributions from track pairs containing one or two background tracks.
These are hereafter referred to as signal-background and background-background pairs.
A perpendicular cone method is used to estimate these contributions [63]. Consider a circular cone with
a radius equal to the signal jet’s resolution parameter, located at the same η but perpendicular in ϕ:
∆ϕ = ±90◦. For each signal jet, there are two such perpendicular cones. The UE is the predominant
contribution to the tracks in these cones, which are located in the transverse region away from the jet.

The tracks in the perpendicular cones can be treated as a proxy for the UE tracks under the signal jet if
the UE in p–Pb events is azimuthally symmetric. Measurements of elliptic flow indicate the degree of
azimuthal anisotropy. In p–Pb collisions, the integrated v2 is ∼0.06, roughly the same value as in 13

Pb–Pb events [65]. This suggests that the degree of asymmetry in p–Pb events is small, so the UE is
assumed to be nearly azimuthally symmetric.
To estimate the background-background and signal-background correlations, the tracks from one per-
pendicular cone are rotated by ∓90◦so they lie on top of the signal jet, giving a new combined jet. The
tracks in the combined jet are tagged either as tracks from the original jet (“jet” tracks) or tracks from
the perpendicular cone (“perp” tracks). Pairs can now be constructed from the combined jet. This is
performed separately for each of the two ±90◦perpendicular cones.
These pairs fall into one of three types: jet-jet pairs, jet-perp pairs, or perp-perp pairs. The ΣEEC ob-

3.3
Detector effects
Detector effects are corrected using p–Pb collisions simulated with DPMJET [66] and propagated through
a GEANT3 model [67] of the ALICE detector. DPMJET is a multipurpose Monte Carlo event generator
that uses the Dual Parton Model (DPM) [68] to describe soft interactions for hadron-hadron, hadron-
nucleus, and nucleus-nucleus interactions. It uses the Glauber-Gribov multiple scattering formalism, and
includes models for photoproduction, diffractive processes, the intranuclear cascade, and soft hadron-
nucleus interactions. The corrections for the pp baseline were performed using collisions simulated with
PYTHIA 8 (version 8.210) [69] Monash tune [70].
The dominant detector effects considered in this measurement are the track-momentum resolution, angu-
lar resolution, and efficiency for both single tracks and pairs of tracks. The track-momentum resolution
and efficiency affect the calculated energy weights, the number of reconstructed pairs, and the recon-
structed pchjet
T
. Track pairs with very small angular separation have very similar trajectories, which can
cause one or both of the tracks to be missed or reconstructed incorrectly. This is called the track-merging
effect, and was quantified in ALICE femtoscopy measurements [71]. Track merging decreases the pair

Systematic uncertainties
The main sources of systematic uncertainty for this analysis are the single-track efficiency, pair effi-
ciency, background subtraction, detector-effect correction method, and event-generator dependence of
the correction factors.
The single-track efficiency uncertainty is estimated to be ∼3% in pp events based on previous studies,
where the track selection and ITS-TPC matching criteria were varied [74]. In p–Pb collisions, the value
was found to increase with pT from 1% at low pT (∼0.5 GeV/c) up to 2.5% at high pT (∼14 GeV/c) [36].

factors are recalculated. In both p–Pb and pp events, an RL-dependent uncertainty is quoted for this,

reconstructed pchjet

Dependence on forward multiplicity
The dependence of the modification on event activity is tested by measuring the EEC in two event-activity
classes, determined using forward charged-particle multiplicity measured with the V0A detector. The
V0A amplitude is frequently used as a centrality estimator in p–Pb measurements [53]. Here, it is used
to define two percentile-based multiplicity classes, high-multiplicity (HM) and low-multiplicity (LM).
The low-multiplicity events are defined as all those below the 95% percentile (5–100% V0A), and high-
multiplicity events as those with the 0–5% highest V0A activity. The EEC is separately constructed for
jets in each event class, and the event-activity dependence is studied by taking the ΣEEC HM/ΣEEC LM
ratio, shown in the bottom panel of Fig. 4. Here, as well, the EECs agree within 5% for most of the
reported RL range, indicating no evidence of event-activity dependence. This suggests that the initial
collision geometry does not influence the EEC in p–Pb events.

5.4
Model comparisons
To study the possible origins of the p–Pb modification, the data are compared to two Monte Carlo event
generators in Fig. 6. First, to isolate the effect of the nuclear parton distribution function (nPDF) alone
on the EEC, PYTHIA8 [69] is used with the LHAPDF6 package [78], which supplies nPDFs to describe
the non-perturbative initial state. In particular, the CT14 NLO free-proton PDF set is used for one of
the colliding protons [79], and the EPPS21 NLO nPDF set is used for the other proton [80]. For the pp
reference, the CT14 NLO PDF set is used for both colliding protons. The EEC is not modified by the
introduction of the nPDF, within the uncertainties of the nPDF set.
The data are also compared to PYTHIA Angantyr [81], which more comprehensively models multi-
parton interactions and diffractive excitation because of fluctuations in nucleon partonic substructure.
Angantyr provides a more complete simulation of p–Pb collisions, and it predicts a modification to the
EEC. The p–Pb/pp EEC ratio in Angantyr indicates a suppression at small RL, but does not capture the
enhancement at large RL measured in the data. The initial-state differences introduced into p–Pb colli-
sions by these models are insufficient to explain the measured EEC modification relative to pp collisions,
suggesting a potential role for final-state effects when jets propagate through CNM.

The comover model uses a higher-twist formalism to account for interactions between the jet and co-

non-perturbative RL region, and a leading-logarithmic calculation of a hard function and a jet function to
capture the perturbative region. To model p–Pb collisions, the perturbative calculation includes an nPDF
and a transport parameter [82], and the non-perturbative sector accounts for multiple scattering and the
resulting pT broadening. This model does reasonably well with the 20–40 GeV/c data, capturing small
RL suppression as well as large RL enhancement. The enhancement is largely driven by the perturbative
modification. However, this model also predicts a modification in the 60–80 GeV/c jet pT interval, which
is not observed in the data presented earlier.
The third model exploits the fact that energy correlators can be expressed in terms of a light-ray operator
product expansion (OPE), which turns them into a series in RL (called θ in Ref. [25]). The perturbative
scaling of the EEC in pp collisions is related to the anomalous dimensions of twist-2 light ray operators.
In pA events, the modification of the EEC is related to the matrix elements of twist-4 light-ray operators.
In this model, the twist-2 and twist-4 terms are fit to the 20–40 GeV/c data, leading to excellent agreement
in this jet pT interval. However, the authors also find good agreement at higher jet pT, validating the pT-
dependence of this model.

quantitative description. Additional differential measurements and refined theoretical calculations will
