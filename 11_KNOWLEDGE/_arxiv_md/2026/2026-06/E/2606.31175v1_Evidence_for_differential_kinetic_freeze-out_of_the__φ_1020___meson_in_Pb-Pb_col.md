Evidence for differential kinetic freeze-out of the ϕ(1020) meson in Pb–Pb collisions at
√sNN = 2.76 TeV
Neeraj1, ∗and Amal Sarkar1, †
1Indian Institute of Technology Mandi, Kamand, Himachal Pradesh, India
In heavy-ion collisions, hadronic species with small interaction cross sections may decouple from
the evolving fireball earlier than the bulk, yet quantitative evidence for this differential freeze-out
has remained elusive. We report that the ϕ(1020) meson does not kinetically freeze out with the
bulk hadrons in 0–5% central Pb–Pb collisions at √sNN = 2.76 TeV: a Boltzmann–Gibbs blast-wave
contour analysis of ALICE ϕ(1020) pT transverse-momentum spectra shows that the bulk π/K/p
freeze-out point is excluded at 4.1σ (∆χ2 = 21.7).
Despite its proton-like mass, the ϕ exhibits
freeze-out parameters incompatible with those of the bulk hadrons, implying that the observed
spectral hardening cannot be attributed solely to mass-dependent collective expansion. Instead, it
is naturally explained by the OZI-suppressed ϕ-hadron interaction cross section which causes ϕ to
decouple earlier and probe a distinct freeze-out surface. The exclusion is robust under all systematic
variations tested and is qualitatively reproduced by SMASH hadronic transport simulations. These
findings establish the ϕ meson as a clean probe of species-dependent hadronization, and provide
quantitative evidence for a kinetic freeze-out hierarchy in ultra-relativistic heavy-ion collisions.
Ultra-relativistic heavy-ion collisions at RHIC and
the LHC create a strongly coupled quark-gluon plasma
(QGP) that expands collectively and hadronizes at Tc ≈
156 MeV [1, 2]. Following the hadronization, elastic and
pseudo-elastic rescattering among hadrons continues un-
til kinetic freeze-out, at which all interactions cease. The
bulk kinetic freeze-out temperature Tkin and the aver-
age transverse expansion velocity ⟨β⟩are routinely ex-
tracted from the Boltzmann–Gibbs blast-wave (BGBW)
fits to the pT spectra of π, K, and p [3–5]. For 0–5%
central Pb–Pb collisions at √sNN = 2.76 TeV, a si-
multaneous BGBW fit yields Tkin = 95 ± 10 MeV and
⟨β⟩= 0.651±0.020 [5], which implies substantial cooling
from Tc before the fireball decouples.
Whether kinetic freeze-out is universal across hadron
species remains an open question.
Species with small
hadronic cross sections may decouple earlier, retaining
a higher effective temperature or a different flow veloc-
ity. This sequential freeze-out was predicted by RQMD
transport calculations [6] and quantified in UrQMD for
multi-strange baryons, where the temperature of the last-
collision increases with the strangeness content [7, 8]. Ex-
perimentally, slope systematics of the Ξ and Ωspectra at
RHIC [9, 10] have been interpreted as evidence for early
multi-strange freeze-out, though no prior study has pro-
vided a quantitative exclusion with controlled systemat-
ics.
The ϕ(1020) meson is an ideal probe for isolating
the cross-section effect. With proton-like mass (mϕ =
1019 MeV/c2) and OZI-suppressed hadronic interactions
(effective cross section σϕ-hadron ≲2 mb [11, 12], vs.
σπN ∼30 mb), it separates cross-section effects from
mass-dependent kinematics that complicate Ω–p com-
parisons.
As a pure s¯s state, its OZI-suppressed cou-
pling to non-strange hadrons [11] means that differences
in its pT spectrum relative to the bulk must originate
from the hadronic interaction strength, not from mass-
ordering alone.
Previous analyses [13, 14] identified a
harder ϕ spectrum in Pb–Pb collisions but interpreted
it using slope parameters without a full (T, ⟨β⟩) contour
analysis.
Crucially, a single-species blast-wave fit con-
strains only a combination of temperature and flow, not
either parameter independently; the resulting T–⟨β⟩de-
generacy means a displacement from the bulk cannot be
quantified without a 2D χ2 landscape.
In this Letter,
we present this analysis for the first time, demonstrat-
ing a statistically significant exclusion of the bulk freeze-
out point and testing the cross-section mechanism with
SMASH hadronic transport.

higher temperature, enhanced flow, or any combination
along the ridge, and cannot be disentangled from a single
species at one collision energy. Crucially, the ridge as a
whole is displaced from the bulk point at ∆χ2 = 21.7,
confirming that ϕ(1020) does not share the bulk π/K/p
freeze-out surface, regardless of how the physical decom-
position is resolved.
This degeneracy has a natural physical origin [19]: the
ϕ, with σϕ-hadron ≲2 mb [12], undergoes fewer rescat-
terings than π (σπN ∼30 mb), producing a higher T or
lower flow, exactly the ridge direction observed. Break-
ing this degeneracy would require simultaneous coverage
of the ϕ-pT spectrum across multiple collision energies,
with the ridge direction rotating as the lifetime of the
hadronic phase changes.

Summary and outlook.—We have demonstrated that
the ϕ(1020) meson does not share the bulk π/K/p ki-
netic freeze-out surface in 0–5% Pb–Pb collisions at
√sNN = 2.76 TeV, with a model-independent signifi-
cance of 4.1σ. Because the ϕ’s OZI-suppressed hadronic
cross section limits rescattering in the hadronic phase,
ϕ decouples earlier than the bulk and retains the im-
print of a hotter or faster-expanding stage, producing
a harder pT spectrum. In the (T, ⟨β⟩) plane, this mani-
fests itself as a ∆χ2 landscape whose degenerate χ2 ridge,
connecting the high-T/bulk-flow and bulk-T/enhanced-
flow solutions, does not pass through the bulk freeze-out
point. The physical decomposition of the spectral hard-
ness into earlier thermal decoupling, differential flow ac-
quisition, or non-thermal contributions remains degen-
erate from the six data points at a single collision en-
ergy; this limitation is intrinsic to single-species blast-
wave fits and cannot be overcome by systematic varia-
tions. The displacement is robust against all systematic
variations tested (Table I) and is qualitatively reproduced
by SMASH hadronic transport, confirming that the OZI-
suppressed ϕ–hadron cross section drives earlier kinetic
decoupling.

High-statistics ϕ measurements from the RHIC BES-

toward lower √s.

a simultaneous pT-spectrum+v2 fit is not yet feasible,
since the ALICE ϕ v2 at 0–10% centrality [27] carries
large uncertainties from the small geometric eccentricity.
The observed ∆χ2 = 21.7 provides a quantitative bench-
mark for viscous-hydrodynamics+transport hybrid mod-
els [26], which should reproduce a comparable displace-
ment of the ϕ confidence contour from the bulk locus.
By excluding a common freeze-out surface for ϕ(1020)
at 4.1σ, this work provides the first quantitative evi-
dence for differential kinetic decoupling and establishes
species-dependent kinetic freeze-out as a precision probe
of hadronic-phase dynamics.

T–β DEGENERACY AND RESCATTERING
DEPTH
The BGBW model exhibits an intrinsic T–⟨β⟩anti-
correlation: increasing temperature can be compensated
by reducing the flow to produce similar spectral shapes.
For ϕ, this degeneracy manifests as a χ2 ridge spanning
(T, ⟨β⟩) ≈(60–150 MeV, 0.65–0.69), passing through
both the free-βs minimum (≈90, 0.68) and the fixed-
βs solution (150, 0.65).
Neither endpoint uniquely de-
termines the physical freeze-out mechanism; only the
displacement of the entire ridge from the bulk point is
model-independent.
The direction of the ridge can be understood phys-
ically: a particle with nscatt rescatterings after chem-
ical freeze-out acquires collective flow that scales with
the number of interactions, while its effective temper-
ature decreases as the fireball cools [8].
The ϕ, with
σϕ-hadron ≲2 mb [9] versus σπN ∼30 mb for pions,
undergoes fewer rescatterings than π or K, naturally re-
sulting in either a higher T or weaker flow, exactly along
the observed ridge direction.
We estimate the number of hadronic rescatterings as
Nscatt = σ · nhadron · Leff,
(3)
where nhadron ≈0.34 fm−3 is the density of the hadron
at chemical freeze-out [10] and Leff = 4
3Rside is the mean
chord length through the freeze-out volume [11], with
Rside = 6.2 ± 0.5 fm from the ALICE HBT measure-
ments [12].
This yields Nscatt ∼0.5 for ϕ, ∼1.4 for
Ω, ∼3 for Ξ, and ∼4–11 for bulk hadrons—consistent
with the observed pattern of BGBW fit quality: ϕ un-
dergoes just enough rescattering to partially thermalize
while remaining decoupled from the bulk cooling, while
Ωis too transparent to fully thermalize. These estimates
are upper bounds because the straight-line chord approx-
imation overestimates the actual path through the dense
hadronic phase.
