EXTENSION OF ULRICH BUNDLES
SUPRAVAT SARKAR
Abstract. We study extension of Ulrich bundles from a smooth non-
degenerate subvariety X of Pn. If X is a complete intersection of di-
mension ≥2, we show that the extension is not possible except in the
trivial case. For an arbitrary X, we characterize when the extension is
possible, assuming some condition on the extended vector bundle. As
an application, we generalize previous results of López and Zamora. We
also give several classes of examples of Ulrich bundles on curves that
extend to the ambient projective space.
Keywords: Ulrich bundle, extension
MSC Number:14J60, 14H60
1. Introduction
We work throughout over the field k = C of complex numbers. Ulrich bundles
are important objects of study from the viewpoints of both commutative
algebra and algebraic geometry. Let us recall the definition. Let X be a
smooth subvariety of Pn, and E a nonzero vector bundle on X. In [11], the
following were proved to be equivalent:
(1) There exists a linear resolution
0 →Lc →Lc−1 →· · · →L0 →E →0,
with c = codim(X, Pn) and Li = OPn(−i)⊕bi.
(2) The cohomology H•(X, E(−p)) vanishes for 1 ≤p ≤dim(X).
(3) If π : X →Pdim(X) is a finite linear projection, then the vector bundle
π∗E is trivial.
We call E an Ulrich bundle if these equivalent conditions are satisfied. By
condition (2), Ulrichness of a vector bundle on X depends only on the very
ample line bundle OX(1), not on the space of sections giving the projective
embedding. So for any smooth projective variety X and very ample line
bundle L on X, we call a vector bundle E on X to be L-Ulrich if Hi(X, E ⊗
L−p) = 0 for all i and 1 ≤p ≤dim X. We usually omit L when L = OX(1)
for a given projective embedding of X. For details about Ulrich bundles, see
[3], [5] or [9]. Ulrich bundles are closely related to several important areas:

The goal of this article is to study when an Ulrich bundle on X extends
to a vector bundle on the linear span of X in the ambient space Pn. To
avoid unnecessarily complicated notations and trivialities, we will assume X

would also follow from [20]. Our new content here is the global generation,
which is a very strong property for vector bundles on Pn, see [1].

2. Extending Ulrich bundles from complete intersections
In this section, we will prove Theorem A. First we prove some Lemmas.
Recall that a vector bundle E on a subvariety X on PN is called arithmetically
Cohen-Macaulay (ACM for short) if Hi(X, E(k)) = 0 for all k ∈Z and
0 < i < dim X. Ulrich bundles are ACM by [3, §2, (2.1)].

0 < i < n. The short exact sequence
