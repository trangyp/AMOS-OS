EXTREMAL EFFECTIVE CYCLES AND NEF LINE BUNDLES ON Mg,n
DAEBEOM CHOI
Abstract. There has been a growing body of work devoted to the study of effective cones of
codimension-k cycles Effk(Mg,n) on Mg,n, the moduli space of n pointed stable curves of genus
g. In this paper, we remove the genus-dependence present in previous bounds on the number
of marked points, and prove the following results: (1) Effk(Mg,n) has infinitely many extremal
rays for k ≥2, g ≥3 and n ≥2k −2, and (2) Effk(Mg,n) is non-polyhedral for k ≥2, g ≥1
and n ≥k + 5. Moreover, we show that (3) every rational tails boundary stratum spans an
extremal ray. Our method refines that of Chen and Coskun by extending arguments based
on morphisms, or equivalently semiample divisors, to a setting that also allows for the use of
nef divisors. Certain non-semiample nef divisors on Mg,n, namely so-called semigroup kappa
divisors of a particular kind, play a crucial role.
1. Introduction
The moduli space of stable genus g curves with n marked points, Mg,n, is a central object
in algebraic geometry. One question about Mg,n that has attracted considerable attention is to
describe its cone of effective divisors Eff1(Mg,n), because of its relation to the Kodaira dimension
and birational models of Mg,n (see, e.g. [HM82; EH87; HT02; Ver02; FP05; CC14; Opi16;
Mul17; Mul21; Cas+23; FJP25; Mul25]).
There has been growing interest in investigating
the geometry of a projective variety X through its effective (resp. pseudo-effective) cones of
higher-codimension cycles Effk(X) (resp. Eff
k(X), see Section 2.1), e.g. [DJV13; FL16; FL17b;
FL17a]. Correspondingly, there have been a number of papers devoted to the study of such
higher-codimension effective cycles on Mg,n, e.g. [CC15; Sch15; CT16; Mul20; Bla22; Cho25].
As explained in [Bla22], there are two main types of results concerning cones of (pseudo)
effective cycles on Mg,n. The first is to determine whether they are polyhedral. The second is
to prove that certain special strata in Mg,n generate extremal rays in Effk(Mg,n) or Eff
k(Mg,n).
In this work, we obtain results of both sorts.
First, in Theorem 1.1, we identify new cones of effective codimension-k cycles on Mg,n that
are not polyhedral. Compared to previous results, which we will review, the main advance of
Theorem 1.1 is that the lower bound on n is independent of g.
Theorem 1.1.
(1) For k ≥2, g ≥3, and n ≥2k −2, there are infinitely many extremal
effective codimension k cycles on Mg,n, i.e. Effk(Mg,n) has infinitely many extremal rays.
(2) For k ≥2 and g ≥1, Effk(Mg,n) is not polyhedral for n ≥k + 5.
Second, extending [Bla22], we obtain new examples of extremal boundary strata.
Theorem 1.2. Any rational tails boundary stratum is extremal.
The common new ingredient in the proofs of Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 is Lemma 3.2,
which allows us to prove the extremality of the pushforward of an extremal effective cycle. It
extends the argument of Chen and Coskun [CC15, Proposition 2.1], which uses morphisms, or
equivalently, their associated semiample divisors, to a numerical argument using the exceptional
locus of nef divisors. The nef divisors needed for these arguments are supplied by the semigroup
kappa divisors in Proposition 4.1 (see also [Cho25, Section 7]). Since the particular nef divisors
used in the proofs are not semiample (cf. Remark 6.2), Lemma 3.2 is essential.

To clarify, we next explain three kinds of statements, all of which appear in the main theorems
here and in previous work regarding non-polyhedrality of effective cones.
Let C be a full-
dimensional cone in a Euclidean space, possibly non-closed. In our case, this will be Effk(X)
for some projective variety X. Here are three statements:
(1) C has infinitely many extremal rays.
(2) The closure C of C is not polyhedral.
(3) C is not polyhedral.
Each of (1) and (2) implies (3), but the other implications do not hold in general.
For
example, the interior of a circular (resp. polyhedral) cone satisfies (2) but not (1) (resp. (3)
but not (1) or (2)). If we take the union of the interior of a cube and a closed disk on one of
the six faces of the cube, then this set is convex; if we let C be the 4-dimensional cone over it,
then this gives a counterexample to (1) ⇒(2). Although the author is unaware of whether such
a counterexample can occur for C = Effk(X), we attempt here to be precise and distinguish
these three statements.

• Boundary divisors: See [Rul01; CC15; Che18; Bla22].
• Codimension 2 boundary strata of M0,n and Mg, and certain higher-codimension bound-
ary strata [CC15, Theorem 5.6, Theorem 6.2].
• All boundary strata of M0,n [Sch15; Bla22].
• Certain rational tails boundary strata (cf. Definition 6.4); more specifically, such strata

We next describe our contributions, and the method used to extend the work of Chen
and Coskun [CC15, Proposition 2.1]. Following the strategy of [CC15; Bla22], we prove the
extremality of the pushforward of certain extremal effective cycles. Therefore, the first step is
to identify suitable extremal effective cycles to start with. For this step, we do not construct
such cycles ourselves; instead, we rely on cycles constructed in previous work, all of which were
listed above. In this sense, the paper gives a general procedure for producing new extremal
effective cycles from previously known ones. For example, if we know that there are infinitely
many extremal effective divisors on M0,n for sufficiently large n, then we can strengthen part
(2) of Theorem 1.1 to a stronger statement asserting the infinitude of extremal effective cycles.
In particular, we take pushforwards of certain extremal effective cycles along ι : M →Mg,n,
where M is a certain product of moduli spaces of curves and ι is the corresponding clutching map.

are easy to construct, and the structure of semigroup kappa divisors makes it easier to keep track
of their behavior on the boundary. For the main theorems, we only need a very special class
of semigroup kappa divisors, described in Proposition 4.1, but other semigroup kappa divisors
are also useful for extremality problems, as we will see in Proposition 4.2. This suggests that
semigroup kappa divisors may provide a useful source of nef divisors for studying extremality
problems beyond the cases considered in this paper.
The final step is the injectivity of certain pushforwards, established in [Bla22, Proposition
4.2] and Proposition 5.2. Unlike similar results in [CC15; Bla22], we convert the injectivity
statement into a surjectivity statement on the dual groups, and then prove surjectivity using the
combinatorics of dual graphs, which will be reviewed in Section 2. This part is the bottleneck of
the current method: if we can improve Proposition 5.2, then we can further improve Theorem 1.1
(cf. Remark 5.4 (2)). However, as the codimension grows, the combinatorics of dual graphs
become more complicated and further obstructions arise (cf. Remark 5.4 (3)).
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review the basics of dual graphs and
intersection theory. In Section 3, we present the lemmas used to transfer extremality, following
[CC15; Bla22]. In Section 4, we introduce a simple class of semigroup kappa divisors, which will
be used to prove extremality. In Section 5, we prove and discuss the injectivity of pushforward
maps between numerical Chow groups. Finally, in Section 6, we prove the main theorems.

in this case is the same as the proof of [Bla22, Corollary 4.9]. Let Γ′ := Γ −v and let l ≥3 be
the number of legs on v. Then Γ′ is of rational tails type with codimension k −1. Therefore,
∆Γ′ is extremal by the induction hypothesis. Note that under the clutching map
ι : M0,l+1 × Mg,n−l+1 →Mg,n,
we have ∆Γ = ι(M0,l+1 × ∆Γ′). By [Bla22, Lemma 4.4] or by Lemma 3.4 and Lemma 6.1, ∆Γ
is also extremal.
Now assume that there is an edge e satisfying Lemma 6.5 (2). By splitting e in Γ into two
legs l1 and l2, we obtain two dual graphs Γ1 and Γ2, where Γ1 has genus 0, ∆Γ1 has dimension
0, Γ2 has genus g and is of rational tails type. Let I be the set of markings of Γ contained in
Γ1. Then, under the clutching map
ι : M0,I+1 × Mg,Ic+1 →Mg,n,
we have ∆Γ = ι(∆Γ1 × ∆Γ2). We now check that we can apply Lemma 6.1. Condition (1)
of Lemma 6.1 is trivial. By the induction hypothesis, ∆Γ2 is extremal. Since ∆Γ1 is a point,
Lemma 3.5 shows that ∆Γ1 × ∆Γ2 is extremal. Hence condition (2) of Lemma 6.1 holds. Note
that the vertex of Γ2 containing l2 is either non-trivalent or of genus g ≥1. In both cases,
dim π2,s(∆Γ2) < dim ∆Γ2.
Thus condition (3) of Lemma 6.1 also holds. Therefore, ∆Γ = ι(∆Γ1 × ∆Γ2) is extremal. This
completes the induction and hence the proof.
□
