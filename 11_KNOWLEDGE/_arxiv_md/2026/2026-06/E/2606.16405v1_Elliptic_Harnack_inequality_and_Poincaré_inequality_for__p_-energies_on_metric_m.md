Elliptic Harnack inequality and Poincar´e inequality for p-energies on metric measure spaces
Meng Yang
Abstract
For p > 1 and a p-energy on a volume doubling metric measure space, we prove the
Poincar´e inequality under the elliptic Harnack inequality, two-sided capacity bounds, and
additional geometric and analytic assumptions. Combining this with our previous results, we
obtain, under the same structural assumptions, the equivalence between the elliptic Harnack
inequality together with two-sided capacity bounds and the conjunction of the Poincar´e
inequality and the cutoff Sobolev inequality.
1
Introduction
For strongly local regular Dirichlet forms, the relationship among heat kernel estimates,
Harnack inequalities, Poincar´e inequalities, cutoff Sobolev inequalities, and capacity bounds
is by now well understood; see, for example, [5, 17, 14, 2, 15, 12] and the references therein.
In particular, for a strongly local regular Dirichlet form on a volume doubling metric measure
space, the elliptic Harnack inequality together with two-sided capacity bounds is equivalent
to the conjunction of the Poincar´e inequality and the cutoff Sobolev inequality:
EHI + cap(Ψ) ⇔PI(Ψ) + CS(Ψ),
where Ψ is a suitable general scaling function. This equivalence plays a fundamental role in
the stability theory for the elliptic Harnack inequality; see [6, 21].
For nonlinear p-energies, there is generally neither a linear semi-group nor a heat kernel.
Nevertheless, many functional inequalities still make sense. In particular, one may formulate
p-versions of the elliptic Harnack inequality, the Poincar´e inequality, the cutoff Sobolev
inequality, and the two-sided capacity bounds. It is therefore natural to ask whether the
above equivalence has a nonlinear counterpart. We refer to [9, 27, 7, 25, 23, 10, 1, 3] for
recent developments on nonlinear p-energies on fractals and metric measure spaces.
In our previous work [28], we proved that, under suitable geometric and analytic assump-
tions, the elliptic Harnack inequality and the two-sided capacity bounds imply the cutoff
Sobolev inequality:
EHI + cap(Ψ) ⇒CS(Ψ).
(1.1)
We also proved the converse implication
PI(Ψ) + CS(Ψ) ⇒EHI + cap(Ψ).
(1.2)
Thus, the remaining step toward the full nonlinear analogue of the classical equivalence is to
derive the Poincar´e inequality from the elliptic Harnack inequality and the two-sided capacity
bounds.
The main result of the present paper establishes precisely this implication. More specifi-
cally, for a p-energy on a volume doubling metric measure space, under the same structural
assumptions as those used to prove (1.1) in [28], we prove that
EHI + cap(Ψ) ⇒PI(Ψ).

Combining this implication with (1.1) and (1.2), we obtain the equivalence
EHI + cap(Ψ) ⇔PI(Ψ) + CS(Ψ),
(1.3)
under the same structural assumptions.
As a consequence, the nonlinear theory now has the same structural form as the classical
Dirichlet form theory at the elliptic level: the Harnack–capacity side and the Poincar´e–cutoff
side are equivalent. This gives a robust substitute, in the absence of heat kernels, for the
classical equivalences known in the linear setting.
The proof combines the technique developed in our previous work [28] with a very
recent technique introduced in [11]. In [11], the authors established, for the first time
in the nonlinear setting, the equivalence between the parabolic Harnack inequality PHI
and the conjunction of the volume doubling condition and the Poincar´e inequality PI, on
metric measure spaces endowed with an upper gradient structure. Certain arguments in
[11], however, are sufficiently robust to be adapted to more general metric measure spaces,
including fractals. Following [11, Lemmas 2.5 and 7.5, Theorem 7.6], the proof of PI(Ψ)
can be reduced to establishing EHI together with a version of the Faber–Krahn inequality
FK(Ψ), which they call the Poincar´e-type inequality for functions with zero boundary values;
see [11, (7.1), (7.10)]. In the setting of [11], both FK and EHI are obtained from PHI: FK
follows from [11, Proposition 7.1], while PHI directly implies EHI. In our setting, since EHI
is assumed, it remains to establish FK(Ψ). Building on our previous result in [28] on Wolff
potential estimates under EHI, cap(Ψ), and certain geometric and analytic assumptions, we
first prove an estimate of the measure of a set in terms of its relative capacity. Combining
this estimate with a standard Maz’ya decomposition technique, we obtain FK(Ψ), and hence
PI(Ψ).
