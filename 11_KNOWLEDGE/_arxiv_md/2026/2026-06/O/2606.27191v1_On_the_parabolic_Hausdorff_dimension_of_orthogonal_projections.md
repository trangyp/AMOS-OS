ON THE PARABOLIC HAUSDORFF DIMENSION OF
ORTHOGONAL PROJECTIONS
TERENCE L. J. HARRIS AND PERTTI MATTILA
Abstract. For Borel sets A ⊂Rn × R we prove lower bounds for the parabolic
Hausdorff dimension of the orthogonal projections of A on generic m-dimensional
linear subspaces of Rn × R.
1. Introduction
Marstrand’s projection theorem [Mar54] tells us the following, see, for example,
[Mat95] or [Mat15]:
Theorem 1.1. Let A ⊂Rn be a Borel set.
(1) If dimE A ≤m, then dimE PV (A) = dimE A for almost all V ∈G(n, m).
(2) If dimE A > m, then Lm(PV (A)) > 0 for almost all V ∈G(n, m).
Here G(n, m) is the Grassmannian of linear m-dimensional subspaces of the Eu-
clidean n-space Rn, PV : Rn →V is the orthogonal projection, Lm is the Lebesgue
measure on Rm, and on the m-planes V ∈G(n, m), and dimE denotes the Hausdorff
dimension with respect to the Euclidean metric.
In this paper we shall investigate analogous results for the Hausdorff dimension
dim with respect to the parabolic metric: let ∥· ∥be the parabolic ‘norm’,
∥(x, t)∥=
p
|x|2 + |t|,
in Pn = Rn × R and d the corresponding metric d(p, q) = ∥p −q∥. Here, and later,
|x| is the Euclidean norm of x. We have dim Pn = n+2 and dim V = m+1 for every
V ∈G(n + 1, m), which is not contained in the horizontal plane {(x, 0) : x ∈Rn}.
For n = 2 we shall prove the complete result:
Theorem 1.2. Let m = 1 or 2 and let A ⊂P2 be a Borel set.
(1) If dim A ≤m + 1, then dim PV (A) ≥dim A for almost all V ∈G(3, m).
(2) If dim A > m + 1, then Lm(PV (A)) > 0 for almost all V ∈G(3, m).
In general dimensions we have
Theorem 1.3. Let 1 ≤m ≤n and let A ⊂Pn be a Borel set.
(1) If dim A ≤2, then dim PV (A) ≥dim A for almost all V ∈G(n + 1, m).
(2) If dimE A ≤1, then dim PV (A) = 2 dimE PV (A) = 2 dimE A ≥dim A for
almost all V ∈G(n + 1, m).
(3) If dim A > m + 1, then Lm(PV (A)) > 0 for almost all V ∈G(n + 1, m).

4. Projections onto homogeneous linear subspaces
In this section we discuss the easy case of projections onto homogeneous subspaces.
They are the m-planes V ∈G(n + 1, m) which are invariant under the dilations
(x, t) 7→(rx, r2t), (x, t) ∈Pn, r > 0. These are exactly the horizontal planes V ⊂
Hn and their orthogonal complements. The latter are the vertical planes; those
containing the t-axis {(0, t) : t ∈R}.
The projections onto homogeneous linear subspaces are Lipschitz maps and do not
increase the parabolic Hausdorff dimension. The vertical m-planes planes are given
by VW = W + T, W ∈G(Hn, m −1), T = {(0, t) : t ∈R}. Then dim VW = m + 1.
Let ev = (0, 1) ∈Pn. For p = (x, t) the projection is given by
PVW : Pn →VW, PVW (p) = PW(p) + ⟨ev, p⟩ev = (PW(x), t).

orthogonal projections onto general linear subspaces. Consequently, they have re-

questions studied in this paper is not about homogeneous groups but about geomet-

parabolic Hausdorff dimension under general orthogonal projections.
