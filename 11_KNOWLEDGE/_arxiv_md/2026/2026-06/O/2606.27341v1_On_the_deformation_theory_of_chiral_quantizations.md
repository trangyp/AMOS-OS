ON THE DEFORMATION THEORY OF CHIRAL QUANTIZATIONS
DYLAN BUTSON AND SUJAY NAIR
Abstract. We give an operadic approach to deformation quantization of vertex Poisson algebras, a
chiral analogue of the traditional problem of deformation quantization of Poisson algebras. Our main
result is an order-by-order deformation-obstruction theory for such quantizations, controlled by the
chiral analogue of Poisson cohomology. In the special case of chiral quantizations of affine symplectic
varieties, quantizations of the vertex Poisson algebras of functions on their arc spaces, we prove
that this deformation-obstruction theory is controlled by their de Rham cohomology. As another
application, we prove that the boundary Virasoro minimal models are rigid under deformations.

1. Introduction
Let A be a Poisson algebra, and recall that a quantization of A is an ℏ-adic associative algebra
Aℏover the ring of formal power series K[[ℏ]] such that the central fibre A0 = Aℏ⊗K[[ℏ]] K0 is
commutative, and thus canonically Poisson, with an isomorphism of as Poisson algebras A0 ∼= A.
Similarly, for R a vertex Poisson algebra, a quantization of R is an ℏ-adic vertex algebra Rℏsuch
that the central fibre R0 = Rℏ⊗K[[ℏ]] K0 is a commutative vertex algebra, and thus canonically a
vertex Poisson algebra, with an isomorphism of vertex Poisson algebras R0 ∼= R.
In this work we view the quantization problem through the lens of deformation theory. In his ICM
address [Tam03], Tamarkin introduced the deformation-obstruction theory for chiral algebras, in the
sense of Beilinson–Drinfeld [BD04], a generalization of vertex algebras defined over smooth curves
X which reduces to the traditional definition in the case X = A1. More recently, in [BDSHK19],
Bakalov–de Sole–Heluani–Kac developed this theory in detail in the case of vertex algebras.
In fact, Tamarkin conjectures an analogue of the celebrated Kontsevich formality theorem [Kon03],
and states that the importance of this conjecture can be seen from the corollary that deformations
of chiral algebras are equivalent to those of coisson algebras, the analogous generalization of vertex
Poisson algebras to other curves. The main theorem of this work achieves a somewhat similar goal,
parameterizing the space of quantizations of a coisson algebra R, or in particular a vertex Poisson
algebra, in terms of the cohomology H•
c (R) of its operadic deformation-obstruction complex:
Theorem 1.1. Let R be a vertex Poisson algebra and let Rℏ/ℏm be a quantization of R defined
modulo ℏm for m ≥1. Then there exists a quantization of R defined modulo ℏm+1, agreeing with
Rℏ/ℏm modulo ℏm, if and only if a certain obstruction class determined by Rℏ/ℏm vanishes, that is,
[Obs(Rℏ/ℏm)] = 0 ∈H2
c (R) .
Further, if the obstruction class vanishes, the moduli space of such extensions, up to isomorphism,
is a torsor for H1
c (R).

The space of functions O(J Y ) on the arc space J Y of an affine Poisson variety Y is naturally a
vertex Poisson algebra, and this constitutes a rich source of examples of such. We call a quantization
of the vertex Poisson algebra O(J Y ) a chiral quantization of Y , following [Ara18], though our
definition is closer to that of strict chiral quantization in loc. cit. Chiral quantization has become
increasingly relevant in the physics literature; see for example [BR18].
In the case that Y is symplectic, we show that the order-by-order deformation-obstruction theory
for chiral quantizations of Y is controlled by its de Rham cohomology:
Theorem 1.2. Let Y be an affine symplectic variety, and let Rℏ/ℏm be a quantization of O(J Y )
defined modulo ℏm for m ≥1. Then there exists a quantization of O(J Y ) defined modulo ℏm+1,
agreeing with Rℏ/ℏm modulo ℏm, if and only if the obstruction class vanishes, that is,
[Obs(Rℏ/ℏm)] = 0 ∈H4
dR(Y ) .
Further, if the obstruction class vanishes, the moduli space of such extensions, up to isomorphism,
is a torsor for H3
dR(Y ).
This result is evocative of the classical work [dWL83, Fed94, Del95, BK04] on the deformation
quantization of symplectic manifolds or varieties, which provide a full parametrization of the space
of quantizations of Y in terms of H2
dR(Y ). It also provides a partial generalization of the results
of [GMS00, GMS04] and [BD04] on vertex algebras of chiral differential operators, which implicitly
classify the chiral quantizations of Y in the case Y = T ∗W is a global cotangent bundle.
Although our result is abstractly weaker, in the sense that it fails to provide a trivialization of the
iterated torsor describing formal quantizations, it is often still strong enough to draw the necessary
conclusions about full spaces of quantizations in many applications.
Finally, we discuss the application of our results to boundary Virasoro minimal models: it has
long been folklore that rational conformal field theories are isolated, and so in particular their vertex
algebras are rigid under deformations. The boundary Virasoro minimal models are rational vertex
algebras that are quantizations of a certain vertex Poisson algebra structure on the arc space of the
thickened point Spec K[T]/T n. Using the technical machinery developed in this paper, we prove
the following result on the rigidity of these vertex algebras under deformations:
Theorem 1.3. Let Mn be the boundary Virasoro minimal model for (p, q) = (2, 2n+1), the simple
quotient of the Virasoro algebra Virc at c = cp,q = 1 −
6pq
(p−q)2 . Then Mn is rigid, that is, it does not
admit non-trivial deformations.
1.1. Relation to previous work. The problem of quantizing vertex Poisson algebras was first
introduced by Li in [LI04], following the analogy with deformation quantization of Poisson algebras.
In recent years, chiral quantization has become increasingly relevant in the physics literature. In
the SCFT/VOA correspondence of [BLL+15], the resulting vertex algebras are widely expected to be
chiral quantizations of the Higgs branch of the four-dimensional theory. Similarly, in the boundary
vertex algebra construction of [CG18] the resulting vertex algebras are chiral quantizations—albeit
in a looser sense—of the Higgs and Coulomb branches.
Quantizations of vertex Poisson algebras also play an important role in the (micro)localization
theory of vertex algebras; see for example [AKM15, AKM23]. Following [KR08, DK25], the mi-
crolocalization of a vertex algebra is defined by considering it as a (sheafy) quantization of the sheaf
of functions on the arc space of its associated scheme. Localization has been used to great effect,
recently, to produce free-field realizations of a whole host of vertex algebras.

The deformation theory of chiral algebras was first introduced by Tamarkin in his ICM address
[Tam03], as mentioned above.
In a series of works [BDSHK19, BDSHK20, BDSH+21] (subsets
of) Bakalov, de Sole, Heluani, Kac, and Vignoli give a very concrete account of the deformation-
obstruction complex for vertex algebras and vertex Poisson algebras.
A different cohomology theory for (graded) vertex algebras was developed by Huang in [Hua14a,
Hua14b], with the property that its second cohomology group parameterizes first order deformations,
up to isomorphism.
This cohomology theory has been studied by [KQ24] in the case of freely
generated vertex algebras to parameterize their first order deformations. Recently, [LQ26] used
Huang’s cohomology theory to show that simple affine vertex algebras with positive integral levels
were rigid. Like our results on the minimal models, this gives further evidence, in the affirmative,
for the belief that strongly rational vertex algebras are rigid.
As mentioned, our Theorem 1.2 is reminiscent of the classification of quantizations of symplectic
varieties of [BK04], following the approach of Fedosov [Fed94] for symplectic manifolds. Optimisti-
cally, one could hope for an analogous construction for chiral quantizations, with an analogue of the
noncommutative period map of [BK04] giving an explicit description of the moduli space of chiral
quantizations. While this is not pursued in the current text, we believe that this could be a fruitful
direction in future.
There have also been several approaches to the deformation theory of vertex algebras, and the
quantization of vertex Poisson algebras, in terms of the BV formalism in mathematical physics.
In [Li23], Si Li proves that the quantum master equation for deformations of two-dimensional
holomorphic field theories in the BV formalism is equivalent to the Maurer-Cartan equations for
deformations of vertex algebras.
More recently, in [KZ25], the authors define a three-dimensional classical field theory associated
to a vertex Poisson algebra, in analogy with the construction of the two dimensional Poisson sigma
model associated to a Poisson algebra. Both these classical field theories can also be defined more
tersely in terms of the universal bulk theory construction of [BY17]. Optimistically, an analysis of
the bulk-boundary Feynman diagrams in a quantization of this theory could give a quantization of
the input vertex Poisson algebra structure, in analogy with the approach of Kontsevich [Kon03].
Relatedly, and again optimistically, we hope that an analogue of the Kontsevich formality theorem
could be proved in the chiral setting. In particular, we hope that an analogue of Tamarkin’s proof
of Kontsevich formality [Tam98, Hin00] could also apply to the chiral setting.
1.2. Outline. We begin in Section 2 with some recollections on operads, which form the backbone
of many of our arguments. We also recall the classical examples of the operads governing familiar
algebraic structures, such as associative, commutative, Lie, and Poisson algebras.
In Section 3, we recall some preliminaries regarding D-modules on smooth algebraic varieties,
with a particular focus on D-modules over an algebraic curve. In 3.2 we recall the various pseudo
tensor structures on the category of D-modules on an algebraic curve X, and define chiral and
coisson algebras.
In Section 4, we recall the, perhaps more familiar, notions of vertex algebras and vertex Poisson
algebras. Following [BD04, BDSHK19], we also provide an operadic definition of these and relate
them to the weakly translation invariant D-modules on A1. To conclude this section we present an
initial definition of the quantization of a vertex Poisson algebra, following [AKM15].
In Section 5 we introduce the DG Lie algebra gP,Q associated to a pair of P and Q, whose
Maurer–Cartan elements parameterize the P-algebra structures internal to Q, and introduce the
deformation-obstruction complex associated to a fixed P-algebra internal to Q.
