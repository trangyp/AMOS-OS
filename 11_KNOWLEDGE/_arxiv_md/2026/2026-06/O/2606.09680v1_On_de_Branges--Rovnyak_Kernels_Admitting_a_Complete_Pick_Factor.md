HARIPADA SAU
Abstract. For a contractive multiplier φ in the multiplier algebra M(k) of a
kernel k, the associated de Branges–Rovnyak kernel is given by kφ(x, y) = (1−
φ(x)φ(y))k(x, y). Motivated by recent developments clarifying the structural
and geometric features of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces associated with
kernels admitting a complete Pick factor, we investigate the precise conditions
for a general de Branges-Rovnyak kernel to admit a complete Pick factor,
thereby extending the framework introduced by Ahmed, Das and Panja (J.
Geom. Anal., 2025). In this paper, we characterize the existence of a complete
Pick factor for kφ across a broad class of base kernels encompassing both
complete Pick and non-complete Pick architectures (such as the Szeg˝o kernel
on the polydisk). Our first characterization is formulated in terms of operator-
valued holomorphic functions satisfying an interpolation condition. We also
show that kφ admits a complete Pick factor if and only if (ek)φ is itself a
complete Pick kernel, where ek is an auxiliary kernel constructed from the given
data. Notably, our main result is completely new even when specialized to the
classical Szeg¨o kernel of the unit disk. As an application of our framework, we
obtain a structural insight into a classical theorem of Chu (J. Funct. Anal.,
2020) and provide an alternative proof of a recent result by Luo and Zhu
(Canad. J. Math., 2024). The results are illustrated by concrete examples.
1. Introduction
1.1. Background. A kernel k on a set X is a positive semi-definite function k :
X ×X →C, denoted by k ⪰0, if for each finite set of points {xj} in X, the matrix
(k(xi, xj)) is positive semi-definite (i.e., (k(xi, xj)) ⪰0). A kernel is said to be
normalized at a point x0 ∈X if k(x, x0) = 1 for every x ∈X. We do not require
the kernel function to be holomorphic in the first variable and anti-holomorphic in
the second variable; however, when this property holds, we shall explicitly refer to
them as holomorphic kernels.
We denote by H(k) the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) associated
with a kernel k, and by M(k) the corresponding multiplier algebra endowed with
the operator norm. It is well known that when k is holomorphic, both H(k) and
M(k) comprise spaces of holomorphic functions. We refer the reader to the seminal
paper by Aronszajn [5] and the monograph by Paulsen and Raghupathi [25] for the
foundational theory of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces.

Ever since their inception by Aronszajn [5] (see also the pioneering monograph
by Bergman [9]), reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces have remained a fertile ground
for researchers working at the vibrant interface of holomorphic function theory and
operator theory. Beyond the classical finite-dimensional setting, the landscape of
infinite-dimensional RKHSs comprises such canonical structures as the Hardy space
of the unit disk, the Drury–Arveson space, the Dirichlet space, and the space of
Dirichlet series. Recent advances in the field have elegantly unified these disparate
spaces under the singular umbrella of complete Pick spaces.
The original definition of complete Pick kernels was formulated by Agler and
McCarthy [1] within the framework of the Pick interpolation problem. We also
refer the reader to an earlier paper by McCullough [21] for work on kernels on the
unit disk D for which a version of Carath´eodory interpolation holds. An elegant
characterization of complete Pick kernels was established by Agler and McCarthy
in [1, Theorem 3.1] (see also related work by Quiggin [26, 27] and McCullough [22]).
This characterization asserts that a non-vanishing, normalized kernel is a complete
Pick kernel if and only if 1−1/k ⪰0, or equivalently, if there exists a Hilbert space

1.2. The main problem of interest. Recent developments demonstrate that ker-
nels admitting a complete Pick factor enjoy a rich function- and operator-theoretic
structure, even when they fail to be complete Pick themselves.
For instance,
Clouˆatre, Hartz, and Schillo established in [13] that the associated spaces admit a
version of the celebrated Beurling–Lax–Halmos theorem for characterizing invari-
ant subspaces. When specialized to the complete Pick setting, their result recovers
a seminal theorem of McCullough and Trent [24]. Furthermore, Bhattacharyya and
Jindal proved in [11, Theorem 2.13] that every kernel possessing a complete Pick
factor admits a characteristic function, thereby extending their earlier work [10,
Theorem 3.2] from the standard complete Pick framework. We refer the reader
to other recent works [4, 23, 32] exploring the structure of kernels admitting a
complete Pick factor. Motivated by this line of inquiry, we consider the following
generalization of Problem 1.2.
Problem 1.3. Given a kernel k on a set X, characterize those contractive multi-
pliers φ ∈M(k) for which the de Branges-Rovnyak kernel kφ admits a complete
Pick factor.

1.3. Main results. Our first main result, Theorem 2.3, answers Problem 1.3 for
kernels of the form (1.3). This result appears to be new even in the classical setting
of the unit disk. Regardless of whether k is holomorphic, the result provides a
characterization in terms of an operator-valued holomorphic function on the unit
disk D satisfying an interpolation condition (see item (2) of Theorem 2.3).
Furthermore, Theorem 2.3 item (3) shows that solving Problem 1.3 for a kernel
k of the form (1.3) is equivalent to solving Problem 1.2 for an appropriately con-
structed auxiliary kernel ek again of the form (1.3). More precisely, given a kernel
k as in (1.3), we construct a kernel ek of the form (1.3) and show that kφ admits
a complete Pick factor if and only if (ek)φ is a complete Pick kernel. The kernel
ek, however, depends not only on k but also on the non-complete Pick factor of
kφ. We will discuss how our result naturally recovers the main result of [3] (and
consequently, the results of Chu [12] for the Szeg¨o kernel on D and Sautel [29] for
the Drury-Arveson kernel on the Euclidean Ball) when specialized to the complete
Pick setting.

Vinnikov [8]. To the author’s knowledge, neither of these tools is available for gen-

instead makes crucial use of the now-classical lurking isometry argument.
