An yrast, Jπ = 5−, spin-trap isomer has been previously identified in 32Si. The isomeric state
decays predominantly via a hindered E3 transition [B(E3) = 0.0841(10) W.u.], bypassing a nearby
E2 decay path to the first excited 3−level. The single-neutron aspects of these negative parity levels
were investigated via the 31Si(d,p)32Si reaction at 9.6 MeV/u using HELIOS and the ATLAS in-flight
facility. The 5−state appears as a dominant ℓ= 3 transfer with a relatively large spectroscopic
factor, confirming its single-particle ν0f7/2 character.
The yrast 3−level had a reduced ℓ= 3
spectroscopic factor of ≈0.44 compared to that of the 5−
1 level. This is similar to the situation
observed in nearby 34S which by contrast has a measured B(E2, 5−→3−) transition strength closer
to 1 W.u.. It has been concluded that the hinderance of the 5−
1 →3−
1 transition in 32Si is not
primarily due to the differing overlaps in the neutron structure. Instead, the lack of participation by
both the protons and the neutrons in the transition is proposed as the transition-strength reduction
mechanism.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The Z = 14, N = 18 nucleus 32Si was discovered
in 1953 and its excited-state level structure has been
experimentally studied through various complementary
methods [1–11]. At intermediate excitation energies, the
32Si nucleus has an yrast spin-trap nanosecond isomeric
state —a rare occurrence in even-even 1s −0d-shell nu-
clei.
The state has been definitively assigned a spin-
parity of Jπ = 5−and determined to exhibit a mean
lifetime (τmean) of 46.9(5) ns [9–11].
The isomer de-
cays predominantly (> 99%) via a high-energy electric-
octupole (E3) transition of 3562.84(14) keV to the 2+
1
first-excited state. The reduced transition probability of
this E3 decay, B(E3; 5−→2+
1 ) = 0.0841(10) W.u., is
significantly hindered compared to typical 3−
1 →0+
1 and
other 5−
1 →2+
1 E3 transitions in this mass region [12, 13].
The present manuscript reports on an experimental
study of the excited levels in 32Si over an excitation
range of Ex ≈3.5 −8.5 MeV using the (d,p) reaction at
9.6 MeV/u. This approach provides the single-neutron

III.
MEASUREMENT
The measurement was carried out at the ATLAS User
Facility located at Argonne National Laboratory, USA.
The short-lived 31Si beam was produced using the AT-
LAS in-flight system [29]. The primary beam was 30Si
at an energy 11.5 MeV/u and a (d,p) production reac-
tion took place on a cryo-cooled gas cell (90 K) filled
to 1050 Torr with gaseous deuterium [30]. The resulting
31Si14+ beam had an energy of 9.6 MeV/u, with a typical
beam-on-target rate of 2 × 104 pps and an average 32Si
purity of 30%. The majority of the beam contamination
was due to the unreacted 30Si13+ primary beam which
was accepted at an energy of ∼9 MeV/u.
The HELIOS spectrometer [31, 32] measured outgoing
protons in coincidence with 32Si recoils from the 31Si(d,p)
reaction at 9.6 MeV/u. The experimental setup, all con-
tained within the 2.5 T solenoidal field, paralleled that
shown in Fig. 2 of Ref. [33]. A 150 µg/cm2 CD2 reac-
tion target was located near the center of the magnetic
field on the beam axis. The position-sensitive silicon de-
tector (PSD) array was located upstream of the target
covering a distance between -52 cm to -17 cm relative to
the CD2 target position and consisted of 30 individual
detectors arranged evenly over six sides. The Si detec-
tors measured total energies and positions of the pro-
tons. Because of the limited length of the PSD array, the
strength of the magnetic field, and the reaction kinemat-
ics, the lowest-lying states were purposely excluded and
only states above Ex ≈3.5 MeV in 32Si were accepted
with sufficient angular coverage. The center-of-mass an-
gular range for the Ex = 5.505 MeV, Jπ = 5−state was
20◦≲Θcm ≲40◦.
The recoil detection took place 1100 cm downstream
of the CD2 target location using four sets of ∆E −E
Si detectors. Each set covered 1/4 of the azimuthal (ϕ)
angular range and had nominal thicknesses of 50 µm for
the ∆E and 1000 µm for the E. The radial coverage was
from an inner radius of 9 mm, so as to allow unreacted
beam to pass through, to an outer radius of 48 mm.
A background-free excitation energy spectrum (Fig. 2)
was constructed from the measured proton properties
and the reaction kinematics for 32Si bound states. Pro-
tons were selected by the relative time difference between
a proton signal in the silicon detector array and a recoil
signal in one of the ∆E detectors. This relative time is
proportional to the cyclotron period of a charged-particle
orbit within the uniform solenoid field [31]. The 32Si re-
coils of interest were selected by the combination of the
∆E and E Si-detector information.
Angular distributions were constructed from detector
yields subtending the same position along the PSD ar-
ray. Each bin had the same solid angle coverage for a
chosen Ex and was a singular center-of-mass angle, Θcm.
The Θcm of each bin was taken as the average angle over
the detector length as calculated from the reaction kine-
matics. The uncertainty on Θcm was < 0.5◦with the
largest contributions coming from the detector-target po-
sitioning and the beam energy. An absolute scale on the
differential cross sections was not determined.
A Distorted Wave Born approximation (DWBA) anal-
ysis was applied to the data using the finite-range soft-
ware PTOLEMY [34]. The global optical model parameter
sets of Refs. [35, 36] were applied for the deuteron and
proton parameters, respectively. The sets of Refs. [37, 38]
were also explored, producing consistent results within
the uncertainties. The deuteron bound-state wave func-
tion was defined through the Argonne v18 potential [39].
The neutron bound-state wave function for final states
in 32Si utilized a Woods-Saxon potential. Parameters of
r0 = 1.25 fm, a = 0.65 fm, and a variable depth adjusted
to match the final-state binding energy, were adopted
for the central potential. Parameters of Vso = 5.0 MeV,
rso = 1.1 fm, and aso = 0.65 fm were adopted for the
spin-orbit potential.
A systematic uncertainty of 10%
was applied to the C2S (relative) values to account for
sensitives to the DWBA optical model and other param-
eters.
A check of the event selection procedures and DWBA
analysis was done using (d,p) reaction data collected on
the 30Si beam contaminant in comparison with previous
work [14]. A Q-value resolution of ∼300 keV was achieved
for the 30Si(d,p) reaction. Known ℓtransfer values and
C2S values were reproduced for states in 31Si below Ex ≈
4 MeV. Uncertainties on the C2S were dominated by the
statistics and resulting DWBA fits to the relative cross
sections.

V.
DISCUSSION
A.
The ν0f7/2 configuration in the 5−isomeric
state
As laid out in Section I, the (0d3/2)1(0f7/2)1 single-
neutron configuration should dominate the wave func-
tion of the 5−isomeric level in 32Si. This independent-
particle coupling picture was supported by the energy be-
haviors of the 5−levels in the even-A systems relative to
their corresponding N = 17 and 19 single-neutron energy
differences (Fig. 1). The current single-neutron transfer
data establishes the dominant ℓ= 3 neutron transfer to
the 5−state.
The upper limit on the ℓ= 1 neutron
transfer strength is small (< 10%) dispelling any signifi-
cant contribution from the 1p3/2 neutron orbital in this
Ex region [Fig. 3(c)]. Furthermore, the extracted C2S
is relatively large in reference to those of the 2+
2 and 3−
1
levels (Table I). The combined ℓ= 1 + 3 C2S of the
3(−)
2
state at Ex = 5.772 MeV does have a comparable
total. However, as further discussed in sub-Section V C,
it is probable that additional states contribute to the
cross section around this excitation energy region. There-
fore, the single-neutron transfer data confirms a domi-
nant (0d3/2)1(0f7/2)1 single-neutron configuration for the
32Si 5−state’s wave function.

of valence single-particle transitions outside a polariz-
able core, the single-particle matrix elements, Mp(n), and
the effective charges, ep(n), are related to the transition
strength by B(E2) ∝|epMp + enMn|2 [25, 41]. There-
fore, it is proposed that there is a lack of participation
by both of the nucleons in the transition which results in
the hindrance of the transition strength in 32Si. In 32Si, a
small Mn would follow from the close matching and lack
of re-configuration of the (0d3/2)1(0f7/2)1 neutron config-
urations between the two states. Considering the relative
context of these neutron configurations in two 34S states,
as was discussed above, this alone would not be enough
to produce the hindrance.
This would imply that the
single-proton configurations similarly do not differ sig-
nificantly between the two states and that the Mp in 32Si
is also small. Analogous to the neutron case, because the
B(E2; 5−
1 →3−
1 ) ≈1 W.u. in nearby 30Si [13], the proton
behavior alone could not produce the hindrance. Namely,
both 30Si and 32Si have evidence for robust Z = 14 sub-
shell spacings [24], however, they differ in the dominant
neutron configurations of the 3−
1 levels, with the former
having a dominant (1s1/2)1(0f7/2)1 neutron configura-
tion [42, 43] as opposed to (0d3/2)1(0f7/2)1. In addition
to the discussion above, different signs for the Mp and
Mn could lead to a further reduction in the total transi-
tion strength. Finally, we note that isoscalar (M0) and
isovector (M1) single-particle transition matrix elements
must also be reduced and similar in magnitude for small
Mp(n), as Mp ∝M0 + M1 and Mn ∝M0 −M1 [44].
The 30Si, 32Si and 34Si ground-state proton configura-
tions are each consistent with a robust Z = 14 shell spac-
ing (See Ref. [24] and references therein). For 32Si and
34Si, the lowest-lying 3−and 5−levels are also connected
through complementary single-neutron configurations in-
volving the ν0d3/2 and ν0f7/2 orbitals, swapping only a
particle for a hole between them in the 0d3/2 orbital.
This is not the case for the neutron configuration of the
lowest 3−
1 level in 30Si which has a large (1s1/2)1(0f7/2)1
neutron component [42, 43]. The similar structures of
32Si and 34Si suggest that the observation of a very hin-
dered B(E2; 5−
1 →3−
1 ) transition in the N = 20 nu-
cleus 34Si is probable [45]. In fact, experimental work
on the γ-ray spectrum of 34Si has yet to observe a con-
necting transition from the 5−
1 state (Ex = 4.970 MeV)
to the 3−
1 level (Ex = 4.255 MeV) [28, 46–50]. This is
consistent with a hindered E2 transition, however, the
lifetime of the 5−
1 level has yet to be determined and
there is an intermediate 4−
1 level at Ex = 4.379 MeV. In
the neighboring N = 20 isotones, 36S and 38Ar, the hin-
drance should be reduced and indeed both nuclei show
5−
1
→3−
1 linking transitions in their excited-level de-
cay schemes [51, 52]. In 38Ar the extracted transition
strength, B(E2; 5−
1 →3−
1 ) = 0.223(20) W.u. [53, 54],
is similar to the values found in N = 18 34S and 36Ar
[Fig. 1(c)]. In the case of 36S, there is no empirical value
for the lifetime of the 5−
1 level.

The multi-fit result of the 5.772-MeV angular distribu-

of the 5−level [C2S = 0.91(15)]. It has sizable contribu-

of total strength for a lone 3(−) level. An equally prob-

region. A number of levels are yet to be determined from
the (0d3/2)1(0f7/2)1 multiplet. For instance, a missing
2−

strength observed at Ex ≈5.1 −5.2 MeV. The ℓ= 1

VI.
SUMMARY
The single-neutron properties of excited levels in 32Si
were characterized via the
31Si(d,p) reaction.
The
5−isomeric state was confirmed to have a dominant
(0d3/2)1(0f7/2)1 neutron configuration, consistent with
the expectations garnered from the cooperative insight
of the N = 17 −19 isotones in the region. The relative
ℓ= 3 single-neutron spectroscopic strengths of the 3−
1
to 5−levels in 32Si (≈0.44) were found to be compara-
ble to those in 34S (≈0.25). These results were inter-
preted as each system having similar relative changes in
their single-neutron occupancies between the two states.
The consistency of the 34S and 32Si single-neutron results
contrasts sharply with the order-of-magnitude difference
in their B(E2, 5−→3−) transition strengths.
Conse-
quently, the hindered E2 decay in 32Si is most probably
associated with proton participation, or lack thereof, in
the transition. It is of interest whether the same condi-
tions persist in the N = 20 isotones.
