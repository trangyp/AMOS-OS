On superspecial hyperelliptic curves of Rosenhain forms
Ryo Ohashi
June 10, 2026
Abstract
Any genus-g hyperelliptic curve C defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p ≥3 can
be written in a Rosenhain form as y2 = x(x −1) Q2g−1
i=1 (x −λi). In this paper, we first show that, if C is
superspecial, then each of λi, 1 −λi, and λi −λj is a square in Fp2. As an application, we propose a new
algorithm for enumerating superspecial hyperelliptic curves in small characteristic. By implementing our
algorithm, we successfully computed the number of isomorphism classes of such curves of genera 4 and 5
in all characteristics p ≤41, and of genus 6 in all characteristics p ≤31.

Introduction
Throughout this paper, a curve always means a non-singular projective variety of dimension one defined over
a field of characteristic p > 0. All isomorphisms are taken over the algebraic closure of the base field, unless
otherwise stated. A curve is said to be superspecial if its Jacobian is isomorphic to a product of supersingular
elliptic curves (as unpolarized abelian varieties). In recent years, superspecial curves have found applications
in areas such as isogeny-based cryptography and algebraic geometry codes. Consequently, understanding the
properties of such curves has become an important research topic. For a positive integer g and a prime p, it
is known (cf. [5, Corollary 1.2]) that the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial curves of genus g in
characteristic p is finite, and the following is a fundamental problem:
Problem 1.1. For a given pair (g, p), determine the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial genus-g
curves in characteristic p.
This problem has been solved for genera g ≤3, but for g ≥4, even the existence of such curves remains open
in general p. We refer to [2] for a survey of related works.
On the other hand, the focus of this paper is restricted to hyperelliptic curves. More precisely, we consider
the following problem:
Problem 1.2. For a given pair (g, p), determine the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial genus-g
hyperelliptic curves in characteristic p.
Since every genus-2 curve is hyperelliptic, Problem 1.2 is exactly the same as Problem 1.1 for the case g = 2,
and hence has already been solved, as mentioned above. However, for every g ≥3, Problem 1.2 remains open
in general p. Nevertheless, for small characteristics p, the answers to Problem 1.2 are known in several cases,
as summarized below:
• Ekedahl showed in [1, Theorem 1.1] that if there exists a superspecial hyperelliptic curve of genus g ≥2
in characteristic p, then 2g ≤p −1 holds. Furthermore, Valentini proved in [9, Theorem 1] that there
exists a unique isomorphism class of such curves, namely the curve y2 = xp −x, when 2g = p −1 holds.
In other words, the answer to Problem 1.2 is 0 for p ≤2g −1, and is 1 for p = 2g + 1.
• In the case of genus g = 3, the number of isomorphism classes for every characteristic 7 < p < 100 was
determined in [8] by using the superspecial Richelot isogeny graph.
• In the case of genus g = 4, Kudo and Harashita determined in [3, 4] the number of isomorphism classes
for the characteristics p = 11, 13, 17, and 19 by using Gr¨obner basis computations.
To the best of our knowledge, for genus g ∈{5, 6}, the number of isomorphism classes has not been determined
for any characteristic p > 2g + 1.

The main purpose of this paper is to extend the known results on Problem 1.2 by determining the number
of isomorphism classes of superspecial hyperelliptic curves of genera 4, 5, and 6 in characteristics beyond the
range previously covered in the literature. To achieve this goal, we first establish the following useful theorem,
which provides a necessary condition for hyperelliptic curves in Rosenhain forms to be superspecial:

This theorem can be seen as a generalization of [7, Main Theorem A(1)], which corresponds to the case g = 2.
As an application of this theorem, we propose a new algorithm (Algorithm 3.1) for enumerating superspecial
genus-g hyperelliptic curves in characteristic p for fixed (g, p). Essentially, the algorithm consists of collecting
all sets {λ1, . . . , λ2g−1} satisfying the condition of Theorem 1.3 and then checking whether the Cartier-Manin
matrix of the corresponding curve is zero (for details on the Cartier-Manin matrix, see [11, Section 2]). Our
approach has the advantage of avoiding Gr¨obner basis computations and allowing isomorphism testing to be
performed relatively easily. For small characteristics p, since the number of candidate sets {λ1, . . . , λ2g−1} is
sufficiently small, our algorithm is computationally feasible. By implementing and running the algorithm in
Magma, we obtain the following main results:
Theorem 1.4. The number of isomorphism classes of superspecial genus-4 hyperelliptic curves is as follows:
(1) There are precisely 4 isomorphism classes of such curves in characteristic 23.
(2) There are precisely 8 isomorphism classes of such curves in characteristic 29.
(3) There are precisely 10 isomorphism classes of such curves in characteristic 31.
(4) There are precisely 23 isomorphism classes of such curves in characteristic 37.
(5) There are precisely 34 isomorphism classes of such curves in characteristic 41.
Theorem 1.5. The number of isomorphism classes of superspecial genus-5 hyperelliptic curves is as follows:
(1) There are no such curves in characteristics 13 and 17.
(2) There is a unique isomorphism class of such curves in characteristics 19 and 29.
(3) There are precisely 2 isomorphism classes of such curves in characteristic 23.
(4) There are precisely 6 isomorphism classes of such curves in characteristic 31.
(5) There are precisely 5 isomorphism classes of such curves in characteristic 37.
(6) There are precisely 3 isomorphism classes of such curves in characteristic 41.
Theorem 1.6. The number of isomorphism classes of superspecial genus-6 hyperelliptic curves is as follows:
(1) There are no such curves in characteristics 17, 19, and 29.
(2) There is a unique isomorphism class of such curves in characteristics 23 and 31.
