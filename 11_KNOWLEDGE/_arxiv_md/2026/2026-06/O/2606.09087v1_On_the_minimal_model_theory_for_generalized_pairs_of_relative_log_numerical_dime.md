1 .Introduction
We work over the field of complex numbers C.
The concept of generalized pairs, established by Birkar and Zhang in [BZ16] to inves-
tigate the properties of Iitaka fibrations, has become a standard framework in birational
geometry. Roughly speaking, a generalized pair (X/Z, B, M) consists of a projective mor-
phism X →Z between normal quasi-projective varieties, a boundary R-divisor B on X
alongside a nef part M descending from a higher model. Compared to usual pairs, this
added flexibility provides a powerful framework for inductive arguments. While specific
instances of this concept appeared in the work of Birkar and Hu [BH14], its origins can
be traced back to Kawamata’s pioneering analysis of canonical bundle formulas [Kaw98].
More recently, the notion of generalized pairs has proven indispensable in the resolution
of major conjectures, including the BAB Conjecture [Bir19, Bir21] and the McKernan-
Shokurov Conjecture [Bir23]. As such, the machinery of generalized pairs is essential even
if one is only interested in studying the birational geometry of varieties.

A central problem in birational geometry is to characterize the classes of generalized
pairs that admit a minimal model.
Many results in the literature are proved assum-
ing the nef part is NQC (i.e., M is a positive linear combination of nef b-Cartier b-
divisors) because this allows one to use similar techniques from the theory for usual pairs
( [BZ16, HL18, HL20, HL21, LX22], etc). However, the transition from classical pairs to
the generalized setting – particularly within the category of non-NQC generalized pairs –
reveals unexpected phenomena. A notable example is the failure of the Generalized Non-
vanishing Conjecture, originally formulated by Lazi´c-Peternell [LP20] and independently
by Han-Liu [HL20], which predicts that a pseudo-effective generalized log canonical divisor
should be numerically equivalent to an effective divisor. Without the NQC assumption,
this property breaks down in general (See [HL20, Example 1.3] or [Hu25, Example 1.2]).
It is also worth noting that non-NQC generalized pairs link naturally to the birational
theory of K¨ahler geometry [DH23,DH24,DHY24,HP24] and foliation theory [CHLX23].
In this paper, we investigate the existence of minimal models for (non-NQC) generalized
pairs of relative log numerical dimension zero (see Section 2 for the precise definition):
Conjecture 1.1. Let (X/Z, B, M) be a g-lc pair such that κσ(X/Z, KX + B + MX) = 0.
Then (X/Z, B, M) has a minimal model.
In the classical setting (i.e., M = 0), Gongyo [Gon11, Theorem 1.2] established Conjec-
ture 1.1 when Z is a point, while Fujino [Fuj11, Theorem 1.3] proved it assuming Z is a
smooth curve. Notably, when Z is a point, the arguments of [Gon11, Theorem 1.2] remain
valid even when M ̸= 0, see [CHLX23, Proposition 4.2.5].
Our main result confirms the conjecture for g-klt pairs in all dimensions over an arbitrary
base Z, provided the Generalized Nonvanishing is satisfied:
Theorem 1.2. Let (X/Z, B, M) be a g-klt pair such that
• κσ(X/Z, KX + B + MX) = 0, and
• KX + B + MX ≡Z D ≥0 for some effective R-divisor D on X.
Then
(1) (X/Z, B, M) has a minimal model (X′/Z, B′, M) such that KX′ + B′ + MX′ is
numerically equivalent to a semi-ample/Z R-divisor over Z, and
(2) if X is Q-factorial, then any (KX + B + MX)-MMP/Z with scaling of an ample/Z
divisor terminates with a minimal model (Y/Z, BY , M) such that KY + BY + MY is
numerically equivalent to a semi-ample/Z R-divisor over Z.
We remark that the second condition in Theorem 1.2 (the Generalized Nonvanishing
assumption) is implied by the first whenever M = 0 or Z is a point (see [Gon11, Corollary
6.3] and Lemma 2.2, respectively). It is unknown whether this implication holds in general.
If (X/Z, B, M) admits a minimal model (X′/Z, B′, M) where KX′ + B′ + MX′ is nu-
merically equivalent to a semi-ample/Z R-divisor over Z, then we say (X/Z, B, M) has a
numerically good minimal model (see Definition 3.4). It is worth noting that one cannot
expect the existence of a good minimal model (in the strict sense) under the hypotheses

• V is Q-factorial,
• (X, B, M) is g-lc over the generic point of V , and
• KX + B + MX ≡V 0.

• KX + B + MX ≡Z D for some (not necessarily effective) R-Cartier divisor D that

A primary challenge in extending the generalized canonical bundle formula to the nu-
merical setting lies in the descent of divisors. Specifically, to construct BV and MV , one
must determine whether a divisor KX +B +MX that is relatively numerically trivial over
V is numerically equivalent to a pullback from V over Z. While this property is automatic
for R-linear equivalence, it is much more subtle for numerical equivalence. When Z is a
point, Lehmann [Leh15] proved that any pseudo-effective R-Cartier divisor that is numer-
ically trivial on the fibers of a contraction f : X →V is indeed numerically equivalent to
a pullback from V . However, the extension of this result to a general base Z of higher
dimension, to the best of the author’s knowledge, remains an open challenge. In Theorem
1.4, we circumvent this obstruction by assuming the numerical verticality. This additional
assumption not only facilitates the required numerical descent but also aligns naturally
with the setting of Theorem 1.2.

Generalized pairs. A generalized sub-pair (g-sub-pair for short) (X/Z, B, M) constists
of a normal quasi-projective variety X associated with a projective morphism X →Z,
an R-divisor B on X, and a b-nef/Z R-b-divisor M over X, such that KX + B + MX is
R-Cartier.
If M is NQC/Z, then we say that (X/Z, B, M) is an NQC g-sub-pair.
If Z is a point, we usually drop Z and say that (X, B, M) is projective.
A g-sub-pair (X/Z, B, M) is called a g-pair if B ≥0.
In the previous definition, if Z is not important, we may also drop Z. This usually
happens when we want to emphasize the structures of (X, B, M) that are independent of
the choice of Z, such as the singularities of (X, B, M).
