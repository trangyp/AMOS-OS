ON ZIEGLER PAIRS OF LINE ARRANGEMENTS: FROM
NON-EXISTENCE TO ABUNDANCE
ALEXANDRU DIMCA AND PIOTR POKORA
Abstract. We study Ziegler pairs of line arrangements from both numerical and
homological perspectives. First, we show that for arrangements of d < 9 lines the
intersection lattice determines the exponent data considered here. Then we list
seven distinct Ziegler pairs with d = 10. In particular, we construct higher-degree
examples with the same intersection lattice, the same minimal degree of a Jacobian
relation, and the same Hilbert function of the Milnor algebra, but with different
minimal graded free resolutions.
1. Introduction
The purpose of this note is to discuss Ziegler pairs of line arrangements from two
complementary points of view. The first one is numerical and concerns the extent
to which the intersection lattice of a line arrangement determines the exponents
of its module of Jacobian syzygies. The second one is homological and concerns
pairs of arrangements with the same intersection lattice and with the same minimal
degree of a Jacobian relation, but with different minimal graded free resolutions of
the corresponding Milnor algebras.
Let A : f = 0 be a line arrangement in P2, where d = deg f is the number of lines,
and let m = m(A) be the maximal multiplicity of an intersection point of A. We
write
r = r(A) = mdr(f) = indeg D0(f)
for the minimal degree of a Jacobian syzygy. If
e1 ≤e2 ≤· · · ≤es ≤d −2
denotes the ordered sequence of exponents of A, then r = e1 and the upper bound
es ≤d −2 follows from [21].
A Ziegler pair is a pair of line arrangements (A, A′) in P2 such that the intersection
lattices L(A) and L(A′) are isomorphic, but the arrangements are distinguished
by their Jacobian-syzygy data, see Definition 2.1 for more details. In the classical
examples of Ziegler and Yuzvinsky, the two arrangements have d = 9 lines and the
same intersection lattice, but their minimal degrees of Jacobian relations are different.
In this sense, the minimal degree itself detects the arrangement in this pair.

Definition 2.1. Two line arrangements A : f = 0 and A′ : f ′ = 0 in P2 are said to be
a Ziegler pair if they have isomorphic intersection lattices L(A) and L(A′), obtained
from the corresponding central arrangements in C3, and distinct sets of graded Betti
numbers coming from the minimal resolutions of their Jacobian algebras M(f) and
M(f ′).
A Ziegler pair A : f = 0 and A′ : f ′ = 0 may have one or several of the following
additional properties.
(1) We say that the Ziegler pair A : f = 0 and A′ : f ′ = 0 satisfies the condition
(HF) if the Jacobian algebras M(f) and M(f ′) have the same Hilbert function,
that is
dim M(f)k = dim M(f ′)k for any integer k.
(2) We say that the Ziegler pair A : f = 0 and A′ : f ′ = 0 satisfies condition
(MDR) if the Jacobian algebras M(f) and M(f ′) have the same minimal
degree of a first Jacobian syzygy, that is
mdr(f) = mdr(f ′).
(3) We say that the Ziegler pair A : f = 0 and A′ : f ′ = 0 satisfies condition
(SPEC0) if the arrangement A is a specialization of the arrangement A′. More
precisely, this means that there exists an interval I = [0, t0] ⊂R and a smooth
family of homogeneous polynomials ft, for t ∈I, such that
f0 = f,
ft0 = f ′,
and the corresponding family of hyperplane arrangements At : ft = 0 has
constant intersection lattice, i.e.
