´ARP´AD B´ENYI, BINGYANG HU AND XIAOJING ZHOU
Abstract. We introduce Carleson analogs of the Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu spaces B
and B0 by measuring mean oscillation over upper Carleson tents. For these spaces, denoted
by Bp
C and Bp
C,0, we prove two types of structural results. First, we show that they con-
tain several natural classes of functions, including BMO/VMO–Carleson spaces, tent-space
potential classes, and fractional Sobolev classes.
Second, motivated by Zhu’s structural
theorem for BMO spaces induced by the Bergman metric, we establish decompositions of
Bp
C and Bp
C,0 into bounded-oscillation and bounded-average components. We then revisit
the Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu rigidity phenomenon in the Carleson setting. Although the
direct rigidity statement fails for Bp
C,0, we introduce a natural Bp
C-trace and prove that the
rigidity theorem survives at the level of traces.

Remark 1.1.
(1) The oscillation in (1.1) is inspired by the mean oscillation over Bergman
balls of fixed radius. Such oscillations play a central role in the study of BMO-type
spaces in the Bergman metric and in their operator-theoretic applications; see, for
instance, [1,7,15,18].
(2) When n = 1, the space Bp
C is closely related to the corresponding Carleson-tent BMO
space on the unit disc. More precisely, one may define BMOC,p(D) by replacing cubes
in (1.3) with arcs I ⊂T and using the associated upper Carleson tents. This type
of space is naturally connected with the boundedness and compactness of Hankel-
type operators, or equivalently with commutators involving the Bergman projection;
see [1,10–13,15,18].
(3) In the original BBM-construction over Q0, the space B is defined using the L1 mean
oscillation M(f, Q). This choice is consistent with the classical definition of BMO(Q0)
and the John–Nirenberg theory, which makes the corresponding Lp oscillation norms
equivalent. By contrast, in the Bergman/Carleson setting such a John–Nirenberg
phenomenon is no longer available: the corresponding BMO spaces BMOC,p(D) gen-
erally depend on the exponent p; see, for instance, [18]. This is one reason why, in
the present Carleson setting, it is natural to consider the full family of spaces Bp
C.
