ON THE BNSR INVARIANTS OF LINK GROUPS
YUTA NOZAKI
Abstract. For a finitely generated group G, the Bieri-Neumann-Strebel-
Renz (BNSR) invariants are subsets of the character sphere of G that
govern the finiteness properties of normal subgroups containing the com-
mutator subgroup. We investigate the BNSR invariants of link groups
and 2-knot groups. In particular, for a link L with at least two com-
ponents, we prove that the commutator subgroup of the link group is
finitely generated if and only if L is a Hopf link. Moreover, we show that
there exists a ribbon 2-knot whose knot group has a non-symmetric BNS
invariant.
1. Introduction
The study of finiteness properties of groups has been an attractive theme
in group theory. A landmark development in this field was the introduction
of the BNS invariant Σ1(G) for a finitely generated group G by Bieri, Neu-
mann, and Strebel [1] (see [17, Theorem C1.13]). Let S(G) denote the set
(Hom(G, R)\{0})/R>0 of non-trivial characters G →R up to multiplication
by positive scalars. The BNS invariant is defined as a subset of the character
sphere S(G) and provides a geometric criterion to determine which kernels
of characters χ: G →R are finitely generated. In particular, a normal sub-
group N containing the commutator subgroup [G, G] is finitely generated
if and only if the characters vanishing on N lie in Σ1(G). Building upon
this framework, Bieri and Renz [2] generalized the original BNS invariant
to higher dimensions (see also [15]). These are categorized into two types:
homological BNSR invariants Σm(G; R) and homotopical BNSR invariants
Σm(G) for positive integers m, where R is a commutative ring with unity.
The Σm(G; R) (resp. Σm(G)) characterizes whether the kernel of a character
satisfies the homological finiteness property FPm over R (resp. homotopical
finiteness property Fm). We refer the reader to Geoghegan’s book [8, Chap-
ter 8] for finiteness properties and to a comprehensive survey [17] by Strebel
for details of the BNS invariant.
In the realm of low-dimensional topology, the BNS invariants have emerged
as a fundamental bridge between the algebraic structure of the fundamental
group and the geometric properties of the underlying manifold.

In this paper, we focus on the BNSR invariants of link groups. Let L be
a d-link in the (d + 2)-sphere Sd+2, that is, a (locally flat) submanifold of
Sd+2 which is homeomorphic to a disjoint union of d-spheres. We simply
call it a link when d = 1, and the L is called a d-knot if it is connected.
We write E(L) for the exterior of L and G(L) for the fundamental group of
E(L).
It is shown in [1, Corollary F] that, for a 3-manifold M, Σ1(π1(M)) is
symmetric, i.e., invariant under the multiplication by −1. In particular, if
H1(M; Z) ∼= Z, we conclude that Σ1(π1(M)) is either empty or S(π1(M)) =
{[ab], [−ab]}, where ab: π1(M) →Z is the abelianization.
Proposition 1.1 (folklore). For a knot K in S3 and a positive integer m,
Σm(G(K)) is S(G(K)) if K is fibered, empty if not. The same holds for
Σm(G(K); R).
This proposition immediately follows from a basic property of Σm and
the following result by Stallings [16]. For an n-component oriented link L
and its meridians µ1, . . . , µn, define a homomorphism G(L) ↠Z by µi 7→1
for all i. Then, the kernel of the map is finitely generated if and only if L is
fibered. Note that the kernel coincides with [G(L), G(L)] if n = 1 and it is
larger than [G(L), G(L)] if n ≥2. Therefore, it is a subtle problem whether
[G(L), G(L)] is finitely generated when n ≥2. Theorem 1.2 below provides
a complete answer to this problem: [G(L), G(L)] is finitely generated if and
only if L is the Hopf link.
Theorem 1.2. For a link L in S3 of at least two components, Σ1(G(L)) =
S(G(L)) if L is the Hopf link and Σ1(G(L)) ⊊S(G(L)) otherwise. In addi-
tion, if L is non-split, then Σm(G(L)) ∩SQ(G(L)) = Σ1(G(L)) ∩SQ(G(L))
for a positive integer m. The same holds for Σm(G(L); R).
Here, for a group G, let SQ(G) denote the image of the natural map
Hom(G, Q) →S(G). Let us turn our attention to 2-knots in S4. We are in-
terested in the difference of the BNSR invariants for classical link groups and
2-knot groups. For instance, Σ1(G(L)) is symmetric as mentioned above. It
is natural to ask whether there exists a 2-knot K such that Σ1(G(K)) is not
symmetric. The next proposition answers this question.
Proposition 1.3. There exists a ribbon 2-knot K such that G(K) is iso-
morphic to the Baumslag-Solitar group BS(1, 2), and then Σ1(G(K)) is a
singleton {[ab]}.
We next focus on twist-spun knots. For a knot in S3 and an integer k, let
Kk denote the k-twist-spun knot in S4. See [21] for the precise definition.

2.2. The Thurston norm and fibered faces. This subsection is devoted
to a brief review of the Thurston norm which is a crucial ingredient in the
proof of Theorem 1.2. We refer the reader to [18] and [12].
Let Y be a compact, connected, oriented 3-manifold and let G = π1(Y ).
For ϕ ∈H1(Y ; Z), define the Thurston norm xY (ϕ) ∈Z by
xY (ϕ) = min{χ−(S) | S is a properly embedded surface in Y dual to ϕ}.
Here, χ−(S) = P
i max{−χ(Si), 0}, where the sum runs over all connected
components of S. It is known that xY (ϕ) extends to a seminorm on H1(Y ; R).
The set BY = {ϕ ∈H1(Y ; R) | xY (ϕ) ≤1} is called the Thurston norm
ball, which is known to be a (possibly non-compact) convex polyhedron in
H1(Y ; R).
Let Σ(Y ) denote the subset of S(G) consisting of [χ] such that there exists
nowhere vanishing closed 1-form ω on Y satisfying χ = ω in H1(Y ; R) =
H1
dR(Y ). Then, Thurston [18, Theorem 5] proved the following theorem.
Theorem 2.3. Let Y be a 3-manifold fibering over a circle whose fiber has
negative Euler characteristic. Then, there exist top-dimensional faces Fi of
BY such that ϕ ∈H1(Y ; Z) is fibered if and only if ϕ ∈S
i Int Cone(Fi).
Furthermore, Σ(Y ) = S
i Int Cone(Fi)/R>0.
Here, Int Cone(Fi) denotes the interior of the cone on the face Fi. See also
[12, Theorem 5.2.8]. Furthermore, we recall from [1] a result which connects
Σ(Y ) and the BNS invariant.
Theorem 2.4 ([1, Theorem E]). Let Y be a compact connected 3-manifold.
Then, Σ1(π1(Y )) = Σ(Y ).
Note that, in light of the resolution of the Poincar´e conjecture, the state-
ment has been simplified. Combining Theorems 2.3 and 2.4, one obtains the
next result.
Corollary 2.5. Let Y be a 3-manifold fibering over a circle whose fiber
has negative Euler characteristic. If dim H1(Y ; R) ≥2, then Σ1(π1(Y )) ⊊
S(π1(Y )).

3. Proofs of main theorems
3.1. Links in S3. We first prepare some results for proving Theorem 1.2.
Proposition 3.1. Let Y be a compact, connected, orientable, irreducible
3-manifold whose fundamental group G = π1(Y ) is infinite. Then, Σm(G)∩
SQ(G) = Σ(Y ) ∩SQ(G) for m ≥2. The same holds for Σm(G(K); R),
where R is a commutative ring with unity.
Proof. The inclusion ⊂follows from Theorem 2.4. To show ⊃, let [χ] ∈
Σ(Y )∩SQ(G) and we may assume χ: G →Z. Then, it follows from [19] that
χ is represented by a smooth fibration, that is, there exists f : Y →S1 such
that f∗= χ. Let θ0 be a regular value of f. Then, F = f−1(θ0) is a compact
surface in Y . Using the exact sequence {1} →π1(F) →π1(Y )
χ−→π1(S1),
we have π1(F) = Ker χ, and thus Ker χ is of type F∞.
Now, by Theorem 2.2, it suffices to see that G is of type F∞. Since Y is
irreducible, the sphere theorem implies π2(Y ) = 0. It follows from |G| = ∞
that H3(eY ; Z) = 0 for the universal cover eY of Y . Hence, the Hurewicz
theorem shows that Y is a K(G, 1) space, which completes the proof.
Finally, the same holds for Σm(G(K); R) since it contains Σm(G(K)) by
[15, Korollar 1.6].
□
Lemma 3.2. Let N be a normal subgroup of a group G containing [G, G].
If G and [G, G] are finitely generated, then N is finitely generated.
Proof. Since [G, G] and N/[G, G] are finitely generated, the exact sequence
1 →[G, G] →N →N/[G, G] →1 implies that N is finitely generated.
□
Proof of Theorem 1.2. Let L be a link in S3 with at least two components.
If L is a Hopf link, then G(L) ∼= Z2, and thus Σ1(G(L)) = S(G(L)). Suppose
L is not a Hopf link. There are two cases: (I) L is non-fibered and (II) L is
fibered.
In the case (I), it follows from [16] that the kernel of the homomorphism
G(L) →Z sending meridians to 1 is not finitely generated.
Then, by
Lemma 3.2, [G(L), G(L)] is not finitely generated, and thus Σ1(G(L)) ⊊
S(G(L)) by Theorem 2.2.
In the case (II), let F be a fiber surface. If χ(F) < 0, then Corollary 2.5
implies Σ1(G(L)) ⊊S(G(L)). If χ(F) ≥0, then F is homeomorphic to
an annulus, and the monodromy must be the ±1 twist along the core since
H1(S3; Z) = 0. This contradicts the assumption that L is not a Hopf link.

3.2. 2-knots in S4. In this subsection, we will use the fact shown in [20]
that, for a finitely presentable group G, there exists a ribbon 2-knot whose
knot group is isomorphic to G if and only if Gab ∼= Z and G has a Wirtinger
presentation of deficiency one. For integers m and n, let BS(m, n) = ⟨x, t |
txmt−1x−n⟩denote the Baumslag-Solitar group.
Proof of Proposition 1.3. By the above fact, it suffices to show that BS(1, 2)ab ∼=
Z and that BS(1, 2) has a Wirtinger presentation of deficiency one. First,
one can see that BS(1, 2)ab is the infinite cyclic group generated by t. Next,
let y = xtx−1. We then have txt−1x−2 = ty−1x−1, and thus BS(1, 2) ∼=
⟨y, t | y = (ty−1)t(ty−1)−1⟩.
Finally, note that Σ1(BS(1, 2)) = {[−ab]} is well known. See, for example,
[17, Section A2.1a] and [8, Section 18.3.B].
□
We recall basic facts about twist-spun knots from [21]. For a knot K in S3
and an integer k, one can construct a 2-knot Kk in S4 called the k-twist-spun
knot. It is known that, if k ̸= 0, Kk is a fibered knot whose fiber is the k-fold
branched cover Σk(K) of S3 branched over K with an open 3-ball removed.
In this case, we have an exact sequence 1 →π1(Σk(K)) →G(Kk) →Z →0.
Proof of Theorem 1.4. Let G = G(Kk). By Theorems 2.1 and 2.2, it suffices
to show that G′ = [G, G] is of type F∞. If G′ is finite, then it is of type
F∞by [8, Corollary 7.2.5]. Assume G′ is infinite. Since K is a prime knot,
the equivariant sphere theorem shows that Σk(K) is irreducible (see [5]).
Then, Σk(K) is a K(G′, 1) space by the same argument as the proof of
Proposition 3.1, and thus G′ is of type F∞.
□
Proof of Corollary 1.5. In [21], it is shown that the knot group G(K5) is
isomorphic to 2I × Z, where 2I denotes the binary icosahedral group. Since
2I × Z contains Z as a finite index subgroup, it is of type F∞by [8, Corol-
lary 7.2.4]. Therefore, Theorem 1.4 completes the proof.
□
3.3. Difference between knot groups for classical knots and 2-knots.
As seen in the previous subsection, knot groups of 2-knots and those of
classical knots have a different property in light of the BNS invariant. We
finally observe another group-theoretic difference between them.
Let def(G) denote the deficiency of a group G, that is, the minimum of
s −r, where G admits a presentation with s generators and r relators. Let
cd(G) denote the cohomological dimension of G.
Lemma 3.3 ([10, Lemma 1]). Let G be a finitely presentable group such that
[G, G] is finitely generated and Gab ∼= Z. Then, the following are equivalent.
(i) [G, G] is a free group.
(ii) cd(G) ≤2.

(iii) def(G) = 1.
Proposition 3.4. Let K be a prime knot in S3 and G the fundamental
group of the exterior of the k-twist-spun knot Kk in S4 with |k| ≥2. Then,
the geometric dimension of G is greater than or equal to 3 and def(G) ≤0.
Proof. By Lemma 3.3, it suffices to show that [G, G] is not a free group.
Suppose, to the contrary, that [G, G] is a free group. Then, Σk(K) is home-
omorphic to S3 or a connected sum of several S1 × S2 by [9, Theorem 5.2].
On the other hand, since K is prime, Σk(K) is irreducible and not homeo-
morphic to S3. This is a contradiction.
□
