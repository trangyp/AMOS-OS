ON THE EQUIVALENCE OF BV NOTIONS
IN METRIC MEASURE SPACES
LUIGI AMBROSIO, ENRICO PASQUALETTO, AND NAGESWARI SHANMUGALINGAM
Abstract. The aim of the paper is to compare in detail several notions of BV space of
functions of bounded variation in metric measure spaces (X, d, m).
Informally, they can
be grouped in two classes, either by a relaxation procedure starting from a class of nicer
functions (and with different notions of pseudo-gradient in the relaxation procedure) or by
requiring good behaviour along a rich class of absolutely continuous curves. In the second
approach, richness can be understood according to the notion of approximation modulus of
[20] or according to the notion of test plan introduced in [5]. Extending [1], we prove that all
these approaches are isometrically equivalent in any locally complete metric measure space.

1. Introduction
In this paper we study in depth several notions of BV classes in metric measure spaces
(X, d, m). After the seminal paper [23], which provided a definition based on the relaxation of
the local Lipschitz constant for Lipschitz functions, in [1] an equivalent notion was provided,
based on the behaviour of the function along almost every curve, in the sense provided by
the theory of transport plans introduced in [5]. This equivalence result, based on ideas first
developed in the Sobolev setting (see for instance [4, 16]) was established in a very strong
sense in complete metric measure spaces, independently of any other structural assumption,
with exact coincidence of the norms arising from the two definitions.
After these papers, more recently other notions of BV classes based on good behaviour
along curves appeared, relying on the notion of AM-modulus, see [20, 18] and also [12], where
an equivalence result with other consolidated definitions has been established under doubling
and Poincar´e assumptions on the metric measure structure. The results of [12], which also
provide equivalence between different notions of Poincar´e inequalities, are in part reproduced
in Section 6, with the aim to rectify a typo in the definition of the so-called AM-bounding
sequence, see also the discussion after Definition 4.11 and Example 4.14.1
The aim of this paper is to improve the result of [12], getting an equivalence result between
various (and basically all) definitions independently of doubling and Poincar´e assumptions,
in any locally complete metric measure space (hence, in any open subset of a complete metric
space). Our result, which relies essentially on a refinement and adaptation of the tools devel-
oped in [1], provides also exact coincidence of the norms arising from the different definitions.

measure space, thanks to a direct comparison of the definitions (see Theorem 5.1):

• Question 1. We prove in Lemma 4.15 (see also Proposition B.2) that BV functions have
essential bounded variation along AM0-almost every curve. A more difficult question would be
to prove the existence of a precise representative f∗in the Lebesgue equivalence class with the
property that f∗◦γ has pointwise finite variation along AM0-almost every curve, as it happens

essential variation of f is denoted eV(f) while the pointwise finite variation is denoted pV(f).

• Question 2. Do we have an example of metric measure space where BV (X) ̸= BV ⋆
tp(X)?
In view of Theorem 5.3, such a space cannot be locally complete.
• Question 3. According to the chosen axiomatisation in this note, the reference measure
m of a metric measure space is assumed to be Radon (and also test plans are assumed to be
Radon). However, since (in general) we are not assuming separability nor completeness of
the metric space, it is not clear whether the total variation measure |Df| of a BV function,
though σ-additive on Borel sets, is Radon. Are there examples where |Df| is not Radon?
