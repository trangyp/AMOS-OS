Introduction
The goal of this note is prove the following statement for smooth
commutative group schemes over smooth curves, conjectured by Koll´ar
and Sacc`a in [KS25].
Theorem. (=Corollary 2.5) Let C be a geometrically connected, smooth
projective curve over a field k, and A/C a smooth commutative group
scheme with connected fibres, whose generic fibre Aη is an abelian va-
riety which has no abelian subvarieties defined over k.
Let Z0 be the zero section and MW(A/C) the Mordell-Weil group.
Then the following set map

1. Extensions of cubist invertible sheaves
Let k be a field and S an irreducible normal scheme over k. In this
note we denote by A a smooth commutative group scheme over S. If we
assume that the generic fibre Aη is an abelian variety, all line bundles
on Aη verify the Theorem of the Cube [Mum70, Corollary 2, page 58].
In particular, it is natural to ask under which conditions on A (and S),
a line bundle Lη on Aη can be extended to L on A still verifying the
Theorem of the Cube. It turns out, after the work of Breen [Bre83] (see
also [MB85]), that when S is a curve and A/S has connected fibres, a
(unique) cubist extension exists for any line bundle on Aη.
The notion of G-torsor endowed with a cubist structure (or cubist G-
torsor), where G is a commutative group scheme, is rather general and
we will introduce here only what is relevant for our work (see [MB85,
Ch. I, Definition 2.4.5]).

2. The conjecture
In [KS25], the authors prove the following rigidity result, motivated
by the work [BKV25].
Theorem 2.1. [KS25, Proposition 1] Let S be a smooth, projective
surface over C such that Pic(S) = Z[H], where ∣H∣is basepoint-free,
and members of ∣H∣have at worst nodes in codimension 1 on ∣H∣. Let
p∶J(S,H)

eral line, JL ∶= p−1(L), and g the genus of the curves in ∣H∣. Let Z ⊂JL

The proof reduces to a monodromy argument combined with an in-
jectivity statement for the map

One is induced to consider the previous map in a generalised setting
(see [KS25, Conjecture 7]). First, we remark that the map (B) is not
a group homomorphism.
Example 2.2. Keep the same hypothesis as in Theorem 2.1. Choose
any non-zero Z ∈MW(JL/L) (the K/C-trace of JL is automatically
trivial and MW(JL/L) is torsion-free, see [Shi99, Theorem 3]) and any
L ∈Pic(JL) relatively ample over L, rigidified and even (i.e. such that
[−1]∗L ≅L). We know that [2]∗L = L⊗4. Moreover, we remark that
(L ⋅Z) = hL(Zη), where hL is the canonical N´eron-Tate height (see
[Lan83, Ch. 12, Proposition 3.5]), so (L ⋅Z) > 0. By the projection
formula, ([2]∗L ⋅Z) = (L ⋅[2]∗Z) and we deduce that
(L ⋅[2]∗Z) = 4(L ⋅Z).
Since (L ⋅2Z) = 2(L ⋅Z) < 4(L ⋅Z), we have proved that
2[Z] ≠[[2]∗Z] + [Z0] in N1(JL).
In particular, the map (B) is not a group homomorphism.
It turns out that the previous example explains the only obstruction
for (B) to be a homomorphism.
