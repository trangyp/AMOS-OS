ABSTRACT
Context. Atomic oxygen (O i), OH, H2O, and CO are the main carriers of oxygen in dense interstellar gas. The far-
infrared lines of these species are considered important coolants of shock-heated dense gas associated with protostellar
outﬂows. Their relative abundances depend on the nature of the shock and usually remain uncertain in observational
studies.
Aims. We determine the relative abundances of O i, OH, H2O, and CO in the warm inner parts of the protostellar
clump OMC-2 FIR4 in Orion A. The clump contains several young stellar objects.
Methods. The upGREAT receiver including the High Frequency Array (HFA, operating at 4.74 THz, λ = 63 µm)
onboard the Stratospheric Observatory for Far-Infrared Astronomy (SOFIA) was used to observe OMC-2 FIR4 in the
lines of O i, OH, OD, HDO, and CO. Additional HDO lines were observed with the Atacama Pathﬁnder Experiment
(APEX). Archival H2O and CO spectra observed by the Herschel satellite were included in the analysis. The observed
lines were reasonably well reproduced by an expanding spherical shell model.
Results. The O i spectrum at 63 µm towards OMC-2 FIR4 is dominated by a broad line component, on top of which
medium-wide and narrow line components can be discerned. The same components are present in the OH, H2O, and
high-J CO spectra towards this source. We ﬁnd that O i is more abundant than H2O in the shocked gas. In the broad
line component, the following abundance ratios are derived: O i/H2O ∼700, O i/OH ∼300, O i/CO ∼4. The high
relative abundance of atomic oxygen there suggests an origin in dissociative J-shocks that are associated with strong
ultraviolet radiation. The O i/CO ratio decreases below unity in the components with a smaller velocity dispersion,
and these components also have higher abundances of H2O than the broad line component, although remaining below
that of CO. The HDO/H2O ratio in the low-velocity components corresponds to the average ratio in the icy mantles
of dust grains, and the presence of water there could also be understood in terms of sublimation without invoking
high-temperature chemistry.

1. Introduction
Winds and jets from embedded protostars give rise to
collisionally heated and accelerated dense gas that can
be traced by broad far-infrared (FIR) lines of H2O,
CO, OH and atomic oxygen, O i (Kristensen et al. 2010;
Karska et al. 2014a; Mottram et al. 2014; Gusdorf et al.
2017; Kristensen et al. 2017b; van Dishoeck et al. 2021
and references therein). These lines are considered ma-
jor coolants of shocked gas and, therefore, are important

relative abundances of the mentioned species and their
contributions to the cooling of FIR lines change from
source to source (Karska et al. 2018). These changes de-
pend on the nature of the shocks associated with the out-
ﬂows and, ultimately, on the luminosity and evolution-
ary stage of the central source and the physical condi-
tions in the envelope. The two main types of shocks, dis-
sociative J-shocks and smoother magnetohydrodynamic C-
shocks (Draine 1980), are predicted to leave their traces

1989;

vational side, the abundance of water is typically found
to be lower than predicted by chemical shock models ac-
cording to which all volatile oxygen not locked up in CO
goes to H2O. There are also indications that the cool-
ing by O i is more important for Class I protostars com-
pared to the earlier Class 0 stage (Karska et al. 2018).

Both observational results have been suggested to be trace-
able to ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the central star(s)
or strong shocks impinging on non-dissociative C-shocks
(Karska et al. 2013; Kristensen et al. 2013; Karska et al.
2014b; Kristensen et al. 2017b). On the other hand, the O i
line at 63 µm is predicted to be the main coolant of dense
dissociative J-shocks (Hollenbach & McKee 1989), and con-
tributions from multiple shocks with diﬀerent strengths are
typically needed to explain the observed FIR line ratios. An
obstacle to determining the oxygen budget in protostellar
outﬂows is the scarcity of spectrally resolved O i observa-
tions.
Observationally, the analysis is complicated as bright
O i emission also originates in photodissociation regions
(PDRs) on the surfaces of dense clouds exposed to strong
UV radiation from nearby hot stars. In regions forming stel-
lar clusters it is possible to see O i ﬁne structure lines aris-
ing from two separate components of warm gas: out-ﬂowing
gas near the protostars that is heated both mechanically
and by radiation, and gas at cloud surfaces heated pri-
marily by the photoelectric eﬀect (Herrmann et al. 1997;
González-García et al. 2016). An additional diﬃculty is
that O i lines from massive-star-forming regions are often
self-absorbed or absorbed by unrelated foreground clouds
(Leurini et al. 2015; Goldsmith et al. 2021; Guevara et al.
2024).
Here, we present the O i, OH, and HDO spectra ob-
served with the upGREAT receiver on the Stratospheric
Observatory for Far-Infrared Astronomy (SOFIA) towards
the strong FIR source OMC-2 FIR4 (Mezger et al. 1990;
Chini et al. 1997; Johnstone & Bally 1999) in Orion Molec-
ular Cloud 2 (OMC-2). OMC-2 is part of the integral-
shaped ﬁlament in the Orion A molecular cloud com-
plex. The distance of the cloud is approximately 390 pc
(Kounkel et al. 2017). In the analysis, we include several
H2O and CO lines previously observed in the course of
the Herschel key programme "Chemical HErschel Surveys
of Star-forming regions" (CHESS; Ceccarelli et al. 2010),
and two HDO lines observed with the Atacama Pathﬁnder
Experiment (APEX). The dense molecular clump OMC-2
FIR4 with a mass of approximately 30 M⊙dominates the
FIR continuum emission longward of λ = 160 µm and is
the strongest source of high-excitation FIR lines of H2O,
OH, CO, and O i in OMC-2 (Manoj et al. 2013; Kama et al.
2013;
Furlan et al.
2014;
González-García et al.
2016).
OMC-2 FIR4 contains several compact millimetre contin-
uum sources (Kainulainen et al. 2017; Shimajiri et al. 2008;
Sato et al. 2023) of which four are associated with known
young stellar objects (YSOs). These are HOPS-108 and
HOPS-64, detected in the course of the Herschel Orion Pro-
tostar Survey (HOPS; Fischer et al. 2013), and the compact
radio continuum sources VLA 15 and VLA 16 detected by
Osorio et al. (2017). The nature of these objects has also
been discussed in Adams et al. (2012), Furlan et al. (2016),
and Tobin et al. (2019).
The OMC-2 FIR4 clump is exposed to strong FUV radi-
ation from the Orion Nebula Cluster (Jørgensen et al. 2006;
Favre et al. 2018), and it is also claimed to be pervaded by
an extremely high ﬂux of cosmic-ray-like particles, possibly
originating from an internal source (Ceccarelli et al. 2014;
Fontani et al. 2017; Favre et al. 2018). In addition, several
authors have presented evidence that a powerful outﬂow
from the nearby intermediate-mass protostar HOPS-370 lo-
cated in the OMC-2 FIR3 clump collides with the FIR4
clump, causing shocks that cannot be easily attributed to
outﬂows from protostars inside the clump (Shimajiri et al.
2008; González-García et al. 2016; Chahine et al. 2022;
Sato et al. 2023). According to Sato et al. (2023), the four
embedded YSOs and two other λ = 1.3 mm sources are,
however, associated with compact outﬂows detected in the
CO J = 2 −1 and SiO J = 5 −4 lines (see Figure 10 of
Sato et al. 2023). In addition, the Class 0 protostar HOPS-
108 possibly drives a collimated molecular jet detected in
SiO (Lattanzi et al. 2023).
The purpose of the present work is to derive the rel-
ative abundances of O i, OH, H2O, and CO in the warm
inner parts of the OMC-2 FIR4 clump and in the PDR
at its surface. The abundance ratios have an immediate
connection to the oxygen budget and the role of the FUV
ﬁeld in the two regimes. We employ an expanding spheri-
cal shell model for the clump. The expansion, implied by
the observed line shapes, is assumed to be driven by winds
from the embedded protostars. Although complex and dis-
ordered structures are seen in interferometric images of this
object (Chahine et al. 2022; Sato et al. 2023), the simple
model can produce spectral lines that resemble the single-
dish spectra presented here.
2. Observations
2.1. SOFIA
The observations towards OMC-2 FIR4 were made dur-
ing SOFIA Cycle 6 on December 11, 2018 on a ﬂight
starting from Palmdale, California. These were part of
project 06_0179 (PI P. Caselli) employing the German RE-
ceiver for Astronomy at Terahertz Frequencies (GREAT;
Heyminck et al. 2012). The front-end unit consisted of the
upGREAT High Frequency Array (HFA; Risacher et al.
2016) and the 4GREAT (4G) receiver (Duran et al. 2021)
with four co-aligned pixels at four diﬀerent frequencies.
The HFA was tuned to the frequency of the ground-state
ﬁne structure line of O i, [O i] 3P 1 −3P 2 at ∼4.74 THz
(63.2 µm). The line was observed in the lower sideband
(LSB) of the receiver. The array has seven pixels in a hexag-
onal geometry. The mean separation between pixels is 13′′.8.
The footprint of the HFA array superposed on the 100 µm
surface brightness map from Herschel/PACS is shown in
Fig. 1. The 4G channels, coaligned with and operating in
parallel to the HFA central pixel, were tuned to cover the
fundamental rotational lines of OH, OD and HDO at 2.51,
1.39, and 0.89 THz, respectively, and the J = 5 −4 line
of CO at 576 GHz. The OH line at 2.51 THz was observed
in the upper sideband (USB) of the 4G-4 receiver whereas
the OD, HDO, and CO lines were placed in the LSB. The
observed transitions, their frequencies, and the upper state
energies are listed in Table 1. The table also gives the tele-
scope beam sizes at the frequencies used. The pointed ob-
servations were targeted at R.A. 05:35:27.0, Dec. -05:09:57
(J2000.0). This position corresponds to the millimetre and
submillimetre peak of OMC-2 but lies 3′′ north of the mid-
infrared peak (see Table 4 in Furlan et al. 2014).
The observations were made in the dual beam switching
mode, where the secondary mirror was chopped between the
source and two reference positions lying symmetrically to
the equatorial east and west from the target. The switching
amplitude was 125′′ (chop throw 250′′) and the switching
frequency was 2.5 Hz. An on-oﬀcycle was executed by ﬁrst

Despite the relatively large chop throw, post-ﬂight
data inspection revealed O i emission in both oﬀ-source
chop phase signals; this manifested itself in pseudo-self-
absorption in the on-source spectra after subtraction of
the oﬀ-phase signal. However, thanks to the high quality
of the deep pointed integration towards OMC-2 FIR4 we
were able to recover the underlying spectra. By extracting
data of the on- and oﬀ-source integrations for each pixel,
for each chop phase separately, plus the calibration data
on the hot and cold loads, it was possible to reference the
oﬀ-source signal for each chop phase against the hot load
(mimicking "load switching"). This provided the astronom-
ical oﬀ-source "contamination" superimposed on the emis-
sion spectrum of the FIR sky. The good system stability of
the GREAT receivers was beneﬁcial to the process.
Re-processing the data revealed about 2 K1 O i signals in
the oﬀ-chop positions (3 −4 km s−1 in width), with slightly
diﬀerent proﬁles between the two oﬀ-positions (500′′ apart).
We ﬁtted Gaussian proﬁles to the oﬀ-spectra. Here, we
co-added the data of all array pixels assuming that the
emission does not change signiﬁcantly over the 27′′ ﬁeld-
of-view of the HFA. The averaged O i spectra of the two
oﬀ-positions are shown in Fig. A.1 and their line parameters
are listed in Table A.1. For ﬁve (out of seven) pixels, no dif-
ference within the noise could be discerned if this step were
performed pixel-by-pixel; the other two were noise limited.
The Gaussian proﬁles were then added (separately for the
two chop phases) to the on-source spectra. The corrected
O i spectra towards OMC-2 FIR4 are shown in Fig. 2.
We inspected the data from the 4G receivers operated
in parallel with the HFA and found that the CO J = 5 −
4 spectrum was also aﬀected by contamination from the
oﬀ-position, whereas the other transitions appeared to be
clean. We processed the CO data in a similar manner as
described above. The corrected CO J = 5 −4 spectrum
is shown in Fig. B.3 in the Appendix together with three
high-J spectra observed with Herschel/HIFI. The OD line
at 1391.5 GHz was not detected in the spectrum with an
RMS noise level of 0.25 K (TMB). This line is not discussed
below.

2.3. Herschel/HIFI
In our analysis, we included archived spectra from the Her-
schel HIFI (de Graauw et al. 2010) spectral scan towards
OMC2-FIR4, performed in the course of the CHESS pro-
gramme (PI C. Ceccarelli; Ceccarelli et al. 2010). The re-

available at the Herschel Science Archive2. From these data,
we have extracted lines of ortho- and para-H2O, HDO,
and high-J CO. These spectra have previously been pre-
sented in Kama et al. (2013), and some of them are shown
in Figs. B.1, B.2, and B.3 in the Appendix. HIFI obser-
vations were made in dual beam switching mode with the
reference oﬀ-position set to 180′′ from the on-position. Ac-

in question are not severely aﬀected by contamination from
the oﬀ-source signal and the self-absorption in the H2O
spectra "appears to be related to the source".
3. Results
3.1. [OI]
The seven [O i] 63 µm spectra observed with the upGREAT
HFA array are shown in Fig. 2. The actual positions
of the pixels deviate from the regular hexagonal pat-
tern shown in Fig. 1 by ∼1 −2′′. The centre position
(+0′′.7, −1′′.6) shows a slightly asymmetric broad emission
line that extends from vLSR ∼−16 km s−1 to ∼54 km s−1.
The integrated intensity of the line is 62.0 ± 0.2 K km s−1
(6.8 × 10−6 W m−2 sr−1), which translates into a total
line ﬂux of F = 7.2 × 10−15 W m−2, assuming that the
emission comes from the solid angle spanned by the 6′′.3
beam. Adopting the distance 390 pc, one obtains a line
luminosity of L(O i63 µm) = 3.4 × 10−2 L⊙. These num-
bers are comparable with previous, spectrally unresolved
measurements with Herschel/PACS for FIR4 reported by
González-García et al. (2016, their Table 2). The broad
emission line towards the centre peaks at an LSR veloc-
ity of vLSR ∼10.8 km s−1. The oﬀ-centre positions show
mainly narrow lines, except in the north-east where the
pixel encompasses the Class II YSO MIPS 2297 and part
of the dense ridge seemingly connecting FIR4 to FIR3 (see
Fig. 1). Gaussian ﬁts to the oﬀ-centre spectra give peak LSR
velocities in the range 10.0 −10.7 km s−1 and line widths
(FWHM) between 3.2 km s−1 and 4.3 km s−1. It seems nat-
ural to assume that the narrow lines originate in the PDR
on the surface of the clump, whereas the broad emission
line comes from the clump centre where shocks caused by
protostellar outﬂows give rise to a large velocity dispersion.
The narrow peak on top of the broad emission probably
originates in the PDR.

in the foreground. The two strong hyperﬁne components

4. Spherical shell model of OMC-2 FIR4
The spectral lines observed in the course of the present sur-
vey with SOFIA and APEX, as well as several other lines
observed previously with Herschel/HIFI, were simulated us-
ing a 1-D spherical model. The goal was to estimate the rel-
ative abundances of the main oxygen bearing species, O i,
OH, H2O, and CO in the warm (∼300 −500 K) and cool
(30-40 K) components of the clump and in the PDR at its
surface. In addition, we attempted to constrain the abun-
dance of HDO in the shocked and cool gas components.
The envelope around the protostar(s) was assumed to
extend from a distance of 1000 au (2′′.6) to 8 000 au (21′′)
from the centre, and was assumed to be surrounded by a
PDR extending to a radius of 12 000 au (31′′). The outer
radius of the envelope corresponds approximately to the
apparent radius of the clump in the 250 µm continuum, as
observed with Herschel/SPIRE. We tested diﬀerent inner
radii from 100 au upwards. Values of the order of 1000 au
give the best overall agreement with the observations that
we modelled and were performed using beam sizes ranging
from ∼6′′ to ∼40′′. This size scale is consistent with the
LVG analysis of the HCO+ and N2H+ lines from OMC-
2 FIR4 by Ceccarelli et al. (2014), who found the best ﬁt
with the line intensities and ratios assuming a warm (120 K)
shell with a radius of ∼2000 au and a cool envelope (40 K)
with a radius of ∼4000 au. In addition, the intensities of
the lowest rotational lines of H2O observed by Herschel
indicate that the extent of the warm component is a few
1000 au. This agrees with previous estimates for the sizes of
"cavity shocks" associated with outﬂows from intermediate-
mass and massive protostars (San José-García et al. 2016;
van Dishoeck et al. 2021 and references therein).
The density distribution was assumed to follow a power
law n ∝r−2 with a maximum n(H2) = 3 × 107 cm−3 at the
inner boundary (1000 au). The density at the outer edge
of the envelope (8000 au) was then ∼5 × 105 cm−3. The
surrounding PDR was assumed to have a constant density
of 3 × 104 cm−3. The dust temperature distribution was
calculated taking into account the heating of the dust by
both internal and external sources of radiation. The con-
tinuum radiative transfer program CRT (Juvela 2005) was
used here. The external radiation ﬁeld was assumed to be
104 times stronger than the interstellar radiation ﬁeld in the
solar neighbourhood, as estimated by Mezger et al. (1982).

5. Discussion
5.1. PDR
The present model of OMC-2 FIR4 which consists of ﬁve
shells was invoked in order to estimate the relative abun-
dances of the major oxygen bearing species near the surface
and in the inner, shocked parts of the clump. The outermost
shell represents the PDR that has previously been found
to sheathe the molecular ridge of OMC-2 (Herrmann et al.
1997; González-García et al. 2016). Assuming constant
abundances in this PDR as we have done is a simpliﬁca-
tion. According to the models of Hollenbach et al. (2009),
the relative abundances of O i, OH, H2O, and CO change
rapidly in a PDR as functions of the visual extinction AV
calculated from the cloud surface (see their Figure 3). This
depends on photodissociation near the surface, accretion of
atomic oxygen on the grain surfaces, and subsequent forma-
tion of water ice and its photo-desorption. In the models of
Hollenbach et al. (2009) the gas-phase abundances of H2O,
OH, O2, and CO reach a maximum at depths AV ∼a few
magnitudes into the cloud, depending on the external FUV
ﬁeld, and remain almost constant for another ∆AV ∼a
few magnitudes, before freezing out. The thickness of the
outermost shell in our model corresponds to a hydrogen
column density of NH ∼3.6 × 1021 cm−2 or a visual ex-
tinction of AV ∼3.6mag. However, the clump is embedded
in an ambient cloud, so the eﬀective visual extinction at
the bottom of this layer is probably higher than that. Us-
ing the analytical formula of Hollenbach et al. (2009) for
the plateau where these abundances peak (their Eqs. (20),
(31), and (32)), the relative abundances of O, OH, and H2O
estimated in the outer layer, ∼2200 : 1.7 : 1 (see Table 2),
correspond to a visual extinction of AV ∼4.2mag (assuming
G0 = 104 and σH = 5 × 10−22 cm2).

5.2. Warm inner envelope
The emission of atomic oxygen associated with proto-
stars can originate in wide-angle winds or collimated jets
launched by protostar-disk systems, terminal or internal
shocks within jets, or shocks occurring when the wide-angle
winds collide with the molecular envelope surrounding
the protostar (Hollenbach 1985; van Kempen et al. 2010;
Nisini et al. 2015; Kristensen et al. 2017a; Gusdorf et al.
2017; Sperling et al. 2021). The diﬀerent structural com-
ponents have kinematic characteristics that may be rec-
ognized in resolved O i spectra. The present O i spectra
with the detection range extending from vLSR ∼−50 to
∼+100 km s−1 show no evidence of very high velocity jets
or bullets. Similarly, the CO 5 −4 spectrum observed with
SOFIA is clean of line emission outside the velocity range
shown in Fig. B.3 up to vLSR ∼±200 km s−1. Judging from
the size of the emission region and its physical conditions
inferred from the modelling we assume that the observed
broad line emissions of O i, OH and H2O come from out-
ﬂow cavity shocks or jet terminal shocks in the inner parts
of the FIR4 clump.
The O i spectrum towards OMC-2 FIR4 (excluding the
PDR component) is made up of a broad (σv = 15 km s−1),
nearly Gaussian component, and two narrower components
(σv = 5 km s−1 and σv = 2 km s−1), which are also seen in
the OH, H2O and high-J CO spectra towards this source.
The spectrum is measured towards the most prominent pro-
tostars, HOPS-108 and HOPS-64, embedded in the clump.
The broadest line component corresponds to the inner-
most zone of our model (Table 2), where the abundance
of O i is approximately 700 times that of (ortho+para)
H2O and 300 times that of OH. The high relative abun-
dance of atomic oxygen suggests that the broad compo-
nent originates in the cooling gas behind the fast molecu-
lar shocks that have been modelled by Neufeld & Dalgarno
(1989), Hollenbach & McKee (1989), and, more recently, by
Lehmann et al. (2020). According to these models, shocks
moving at speeds exceeding the ion magnetosonic speed
of the medium (typically above ∼30 km s−1) generate
strong UV radiation that ionises and dissociates atoms and
molecules in both the pre-shock and post-shock gas com-
ponents, and heats these through the photoelectric eﬀect.
The component responsible for the broad O i emission may
correspond to the second temperature plateau of a disso-
ciative J-shock, where the re-formation of H2 on the grains
maintains the temperature at ∼400 −500 K (see Fig. 14
of Neufeld & Dalgarno 1989). The plateau appears at high
pre-shock densities (n > 105 cm−3), where freshly formed
H2 in excited vibrational states is collisionally de-excited by
atomic hydrogen. This transforms the vibrational energy to
the kinetic energy of the atoms and delays radiative cooling
(Hollenbach & McKee 1989).
In lower-velocity C-shocks, where neutrals and the ion-
electron ﬂuid have diﬀerent ﬂow velocities and temper-
atures, and the impact is mediated by ion-neutral colli-
sions, H2 is not signiﬁcantly dissociated. In these condi-
tions, H2O and CO are supposed to become the most im-
portant coolants (Kaufman & Neufeld 1996; Godard et al.
2019; Kristensen et al. 2023). Low-velocity shocks (with
speeds around ∼5 km s−1) where the gas temperature does
not rise high enough for eﬃcient H2O formation are an ex-
ception, where the abundance of atomic O is predicted to be
much higher than those of H2O and CO (see, for example,
Table 2 in Godard et al. 2019).
The two model zones in the inner envelope with σv =
5 km s−1 (zone 2) and σv = 2 km s−1 (zone 3) have higher
fractional abundances of H2O than zone 1, although re-
maining below those of O i and CO. The O i/CO ratio de-
creases from ∼4 in zone 1 to below 1 in zones 2 and 3.
These tendencies indicate an increase in the proportion of
molecular gas in zones 2 and 3. These zones may repre-
sent post-shock gas behind irradiated shocks or gas adja-
cent to shock fronts and heated by radiative or magnetic
precursors, where water is produced in high-temperature
gas-phase reactions or released from grains (and is also de-
stroyed by photodissociation) (Jiménez-Serra et al. 2008).
The latter alternative is supported by the fact that the
HDO/H2O ratio derived in these zones is ∼0.001 −0.003
(Table 2), which corresponds to values measured in mas-
sive hot cores, where the ices have been completely sub-
limated (van Dishoeck et al. 2021 and references therein;
Slavicinska et al. 2024). According to Table 2, the H2O/OH
ratio increases strongly from zone 2 (σv = 5 km s−1) to zone
3 (σv = 2 km s−1), suggesting a weakening of the radiation
ﬁeld.
The physical conditions in an envelope are likely to
change gradually with distance along and from the outﬂow
axis (see, e.g., Fig. 8 in San José-García et al. 2016), so the
geometry around a single outﬂow is cylindrical or conical
rather than spherical. The OMC-2 FIR4 clump contains
several jet-outﬂow systems with diﬀerent orientations. The
protostars HOPS-108 (Class 0) and HOPS-64 (Class I), and
the millimetre source "FraSCO 33" (Sato et al. 2023) are
located within 3′′ of the observed position and are therefore
encompassed in the telescope beam for all the lines observed
here. According to Sato et al. (2023), the three objects are
associated with their own compact but rather disordered
CO outﬂows: "ﬂow-6", "ﬂow-4", and "ﬂow-5" (see Figure
10 in Sato et al. 2023). The outﬂow associated with HOPS-
108, "ﬂow-6", has the largest velocity range, extending from
−5 km s−1 to ∼50 km s−1. The collimated SiO jet discussed
by Lattanzi et al. (2023) extends to the south-east from
the vicinity of HOPS-108 and may therefore be related to
"ﬂow-6". It is probably identiﬁable with the prominent SiO

HOPS-370 outﬂow on the FIR4 clump seems solid, we con-

wings seen in the O i, CO, and H2O lines towards the centre
of the clump arise from shocks induced by internal sources.
In accordance with the conclusions of van Kempen et al.
(2010) in the case of the low-mass protostar HH-46, we
suggest that the broad O i line originates in high-velocity
J-shocks near the embedded YSOs. Then it would be nat-
ural to assume that the wings of the H2O and CO lines
come from the same gas. Judging from the low H2O/O i
and H2O/CO ratios, the narrower line components also
come from dissociative J-shocks or externally irradiated C-
shocks. In the former case, the narrow components could
represent cooler gas further downstream of the broad com-
ponent, which is the brightest in O i. As to the origin of the
line wings, these ideas diverge from the concept presented
in several previous studies of low-mass star-forming regions
where the broad Gaussian component seen in the H2O
and high-J CO spectra is attributed to non-dissociative
shocks along the outﬂow cavities, whereas "medium broad"

6. Conclusions
Spectrally resolved observations with the High Frequency
Array (HFA) of the upGREAT receiver onboard SOFIA
show that the strong O i emission at 63 µm towards the
protostellar clump OMC-2 FIR4 is dominated by broad
wings that are similar to those seen in OH, H2O and
high-J CO lines towards this source. We assume that
these lines originate in shocks caused by protostars em-
bedded in the clump. Using an expanding spherical shell
model that reproduces the observed lines reasonably well,
we infer that oxygen is mainly atomic in the broad wing
component. This component probably represents the post-
shock cooling gas behind dissociative J-shocks that generate
strong UV radiation as described in Neufeld & Dalgarno
(1989), Hollenbach & McKee (1989), and Lehmann et al.
(2020). According to our shell model, the components with
medium-wide lines (σv ≤5 km s−1) still have more O i
than H2O but the proportion of molecular gas is greater;
the abundance ratio O i/CO is less than unity there. The
HDO/H2O abundance ratio in this component is of the or-
der of 10−3, which is supposed to represent the average deu-
terium fractionation ratio in the icy mantles of dust grains.
Therefore, it is not necessary to invoke high-temperature
chemistry in addition to ice sublimation to explain the pres-
ence of gaseous water in the component with medium-wide
lines.
The six oﬀ-centre pixels of the upGREAT/HFA receiver
show narrow 63 µm O i lines that are likely to originate
in the extended PDR at the surface of the OMC-2 cloud
(Herrmann et al. 1997; González-García et al. 2016). We
suggest that the elongated O i feature seen previously in
the Herschel/PACS O i map between FIR3 and FIR4 prob-
ably has a contribution from the PDR in the direction of
the dense ridge connecting the two clumps.

Appendix B: Observed and simulated
Herschel/HIFI spectra
The four lowest para- and ortho-H2O lines observed towards
OMC-2 FIR4 with Herschel/HIFI are shown in Figs. B.1
and B.2. The simulated spectra using the physical model
and the fractional abundances given in Table 2 are shown
as red curves. The CO J = 5 −4 spectrum observed with
SOFIA is shown in Fig. B.3 (top left panel). The other
panels of this ﬁgure show the CO J = 13 −12, 14 −13,
and 15 −14 lines observed with Herschel/HIFI. The simu-
lated spectra are coloured red. One can see that the model
that approximately reproduces the high-J CO lines does
not work for the CO 5 −4 line that was observed with a
beam of 55′′. As discussed in Kristensen et al. (2013) and
Mottram et al. (2014), low-J (≤6 −5) CO lines trace cool,
entrained outﬂow gas on large scales, whereas high-J lines
come from fresh shocked gas, the same component that also
gives rise to the bright H2O lines. However, by adjusting
the CO abundances in the inner and the outermost zones
of the model, a proﬁle can be achieved that resembles the
observed CO 5-4 line (dashed curve in the top left panel).
The narrow emission feature seen in the CO 5-4 line proba-
bly originates in the PDR (zone 5 of the model). The high-J
CO lines are not excited in the cool quiescent gas and the
PDR (zones 4 and 5), and the CO abundance there does
not aﬀect these proﬁles.
Appendix C: Composition of model spectra
The spherical shell model of the OMC-2 FIR4 clump con-
sists of ﬁve zones described in Table 2. The contributions of
these zones to the simulated O i, OH, and HDO spectra are
illustrated in Fig. C.1. This ﬁgure shows spectra (coloured
dashed curves) that would be observed if the abundance of
the species were non-zero and equal to the value listed in
Table 2 in only one of the zones. When the emission and
absorption of the line in other zones are taken into account,
the combined spectrum (black curve) is generally not equal
to the sum of the spectra of the individual zones.
Appendix D: Comparison between the [CII] and
[OI] spectra
The [C ii] 158 µm spectra towards the positions of our O i
observations are shown in Fig. D.1, together with the
[O i] 63.2 µm spectra. The C ii data come from the large-
scale survey of Orion A presented in Higgins et al. (2021),
and are available at the Strasbourg astronomical Data Cen-
ter (CDS)3. The narrow [O i] line has LSR velocity and
width similar to [C ii] and its integrated intensity is approx-
imately twice that of [C ii]. This indicates that the narrow
[O i] line component originates in the PDR.
