ON THE ASCENT AND THE ANGLE BETWEEN THE NULL SPACE
AND THE RANGE OF ELEMENTARY OPERATORS
JANKO BRAČIČ, BOJAN KUZMA, AND HRANISLAV STANKOVIĆ
Abstract. We study the angle between the null space and the range of elementary oper-
ators of length one or two acting on B(X ), the Banach algebra of all bounded linear oper-
ators on a complex Banach space X . For the multiplication operator µA,B(X) = AXB,
we characterize positivity of this angle in terms of the corresponding angles for A and B∗.
For elementary operators of length two ∆A,B = µA1,B1 −µA2,B2, we establish conditions
under which the angle is positive, and the ascent of ∆A,B equals one. Finally, for a
generalized derivation δA,B and an injective holomorphic function f on a neighborhood
of σ(A)∪σ(B), we show that the angle between the null space and the range of δf(A),f(B)
is positive whenever the angle between the null space and the range of δA,B is positive.
1. Introduction and preliminaries
Elementary operators constitute one of the fundamental classes of linear transforma-
tions acting on operator algebras.
Besides their intrinsic importance, they provide a
common framework for multiplication operators, generalized derivations, commutators,
and many classes of linear preservers. Their algebraic and spectral properties have been
studied extensively, and numerous applications have been found in operator theory and
operator algebras. We refer, for example, to the work of Molnár and Šemrl [13] on ele-
mentary operators on standard operator algebras.
While the algebraic structure of elementary operators has been investigated in consid-
erable detail, comparatively little attention has been paid to the geometry of the pair
consisting of the null space and the range of such operators. The purpose of the present
paper is to show that this geometry carries substantial information about the operator
itself. More precisely, we demonstrate that the angle between the null space and the range
provides a natural geometric invariant that is closely connected with the ascent.

Among elementary operators, derivations δA(X) = AX −XA and generalized deriva-
tions δA,B(X) = AX −XB occupy a distinguished position because of their close relation-
ship with commutator theory, invariant subspaces, and spectral properties of operators.
Classical theorems of Kleinecke [9] and Shirokov [15] imply that second-order commuta-
tor relations force quasinilpotence, while more recent localization results of Bračič and
Kuzma [3] considerably strengthened this picture. Another remarkable theorem due to
Anderson [1] asserts that if A is an isometry and B is a contraction on a Hilbert space,
then the null space and the range of δA,B are orthogonal (in the sense of [7]). Furthermore,
Weber [18] proved that for every function f holomorphic in a neighborhood of the spec-
trum σ(A), the range of δf(A) is contained in the range of δA, that is, R(δf(A)) ⊆R(δA),
revealing an unexpected compatibility between derivations and holomorphic functional
calculus. Related range inclusion problems for derivations have also been investigated by
Magajna [12], who established deep connections between inclusions of derivation ranges
and bicommutant relations.
Although these results originate from rather different techniques and settings, they
share a common geometric flavor.
The present paper approaches range inclusion and
ascent questions for elementary operators from the viewpoint of the relative position of
the null space N (∆) and the range R(∆) of an elementary operator ∆. In particular,
the positivity of the angle between these subspaces turns out to be closely related to the
ascent-one property N (∆) ∩R(∆) = {0}, which plays a central role throughout the
paper.
The first part of the paper develops this geometric approach for elementary operators
of the form ∆A,B(X) = A1XB1 −A2XB2, where A = (A1, A2) and B = (B1, B2) are
commuting pairs of operators. We establish sufficient conditions guaranteeing the positiv-
ity of the angle between the null space and the range in terms of the power boundedness
of the associated multiplication operators µAi,Bi(X) = AiXBi and the power-bounded
below behavior on their common null space. As an immediate consequence, we obtain
new ascent-one criteria for elementary operators of length two. Several applications are
presented, including generalized derivations generated by doubly-bounded operators and
by pairs consisting of an isometry and a contraction, recovering Anderson’s Orthogonality
Theorem from the general geometric principle.

The second part of the paper studies generalized derivations under holomorphic func-
tional calculus. Using elementary identities together with Runge approximation, we es-
tablish range inclusion results for rational and holomorphic functions, and prove that
R(δf(A),f(B)) ⊆R(δA,B) for every function f holomorphic in a neighborhood of σ(A) ∪
σ(B). When f is injective, equality holds for the closures of the ranges, and the corre-
sponding null spaces coincide. This extends Weber’s Theorem from derivations to gener-
alized derivations by a direct and elementary argument.
In the rest of this section, we introduce notation and basic terminology. Let X be
a complex Banach space and let B(X ) be the Banach algebra of all bounded linear
operators on X . The dual space of X is denoted by X ∗, and the pairing between these
two Banach spaces is given by ⟨x, ξ⟩= ξ(x) for all x ∈X and ξ ∈X ∗. Recall that the
adjoint operator of A ∈B(X ) is the operator A∗∈B(X ∗) satisfying ⟨Ax, ξ⟩= ⟨x, A∗ξ⟩
for all x ∈X and ξ ∈X ∗.
Recall that the ascent asc(A) of A is the smallest non-negative integer k such that
N (Ak) = N (Ak+1). If there is no non-negative integer with this property, then asc(A) =
∞(see [14, §20]). It is easily seen that asc(A) is a non-negative integer k if and only if
R(Ak) ∩N (A) = {0}. Hence, asc(A) ≤1 if and only if the intersection R(A) ∩N (A)
is trivial; in particular, if A is an injective operator, then its ascent is 0.
If A, T ∈B(X ) are operators such that the commutator [A, T] := AT −TA commutes
with A, then the celebrated Kleinecke-Shirokov Theorem [9, 15] asserts that the commu-
tator [A, T] is a quasinilpotent operator (see [1, 3, 8, 16] for some generalizations). If X is
finite-dimensional, then the Kleinecke-Shirokov Theorem reduces to Jacobson’s Lemma [6,
Lemma 2], which says that the commutator [A, T] is nilpotent whenever [A, [A, T]] = 0.
It is clear that the condition [A, [A, T]] = 0 is equivalent to [A, T] ∈R(δA) ∩N (δA).
Hence, the intersection R(δA) ∩N (δA) measures the second-order commutation. Instead
of analyzing this algebraically, we quantify how far the two spaces are from each other
geometrically. In this paper, we are concerned with a slightly more general question of
when the intersection R(∆) ∩N (∆) is trivial for an elementary operator ∆of length at
most two. Our approach relies on the notion of the angle between two linear submani-
folds. Since the intersection R(∆) ∩N (∆) is trivial whenever the angle between R(∆)
and N (∆) is positive, our main results are related to the question of the positivity of the
angle between these two linear submanifolds of operators.

3. Elementary operators
For operators A, B ∈B(X ), let LA : X 7→AX and RB : X 7→XB denote the left
and the right multiplication operators on B(X ) induced by A and B, respectively. It
is easily seen that ∥LA∥= ∥A∥and ∥RB∥= ∥B∥. Let n be a positive integer and let
A = (A1, . . . , An) and B = (B1, . . . , Bn) be n-tuples of linearly independent operators
on X . The elementary operator on B(X ) induced by A and B is given by ΓA,B =
LA1RB1 + · · · + LAnRBn. We will also use the following notation: µA,B = LARB, δA,B =
LA −RB, δA = δA,A, τA,B = µA,B −µI,I, and ∆A,B = µA1,B1 −µA2,B2 for pairs of operators
A = (A1, A2) and B = (B1, B2). Since a left multiplication commutes with every right
multiplication, we see that µA1,B1 and µA2,B2 commute if A = (A1, A2) and B = (B1, B2)
are commuting pairs.
