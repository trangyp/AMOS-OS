Observation of Strong ϕ-meson Directed Flow at High Baryon Density
The STAR Collaboration
We report the first observation of a large directed flow (v1) of ϕ mesons in mid-central Au+Au
collisions at √sNN = 3.0–4.5 GeV, together with the √sNN = 7.7 GeV collider-energy result for
the collision-energy dependence, measured by the STAR experiment at RHIC. Despite its mesonic
nature, the ϕ-meson v1 exhibits a magnitude comparable to that of protons and Λ baryons, and
is significantly larger (by a factor of ≳3) than that of K0
S mesons at the lowest energies. The
midrapidity slope dv1/dy shows a pronounced energy dependence similar to that observed for protons
and Λ baryons, in contrast to lighter mesons, in the high-baryon-density region.
Comparisons
with hadronic transport calculations indicate that the observed ϕ-meson directed flow is consistent
with production via high-mass baryon resonances and the collective motion of baryons influenced
by baryonic mean-field interactions.
Due to its long lifetime and relatively weak effective ϕ–N
interaction, the ϕ meson can retain sensitivity to collective dynamics established during the dense
baryonic stage of the collision.
These results provide new experimental constraints on baryon-
resonance production and strange-hadron transport dynamics in dense QCD matter.
Relativistic heavy-ion collisions aim to explore the
phase structure of strongly interacting matter gov-
erned by Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) under
extreme temperature and baryon density conditions.
Anisotropic flow, particularly the directed flow (v1), the
first harmonic coefficient, is a key observable that is sen-
sitive to the early-time dynamics and the equation of
state (EoS) of the created medium [1, 2]. In the RHIC
Beam Energy Scan Phase-I (BES-I), the STAR exper-
iment observed a nonmonotonic energy dependence of
the proton and Λ v1 slope (dv1/dy), with a minimum
around √sNN ∼10–20 GeV [3–5]. This behavior has
been discussed in connection with possible changes in
the QCD equation of state; however, existing transport
and hydrodynamic models have not provided a consis-
tent description of the detailed energy dependence [6–
9]. These discrepancies highlight the need for further
experimental constraints, especially in the high-baryon-
density regime where baryonic interactions and mean-
field effects are expected to play a dominant role.
The ϕ meson, the lightest strange quark–antiquark
bound state (s¯s) with a mass comparable to that
of the proton, provides a unique probe of the high-
baryon-density regime in relativistic heavy-ion collisions
[4, 10, 11].
In baryon-rich matter, collective dynam-
ics is dominated by baryonic interactions and mean-
field effects, while lighter mesons are strongly affected
by late-stage hadronic rescattering. Owing to its hid-
den strangeness, long lifetime (∼46 fm/c)[12], and rela-
tively weak effective ϕ–N interaction in nuclear matter
governing rescattering and absorption in baryonic mat-
ter [13–15], the ϕ meson undergoes limited rescattering
and retains enhanced sensitivity to collective dynamics
established during the dense baryonic stage. Such in-
formation is mostly inaccessible through measurements
of lighter mesons, which are strongly modified by late-
stage rescattering, or baryons alone, whose flow is in-
trinsically entangled with baryon number transport and
mean-field effects [16–18].
Transport model studies indicate that at high baryon
chemical potential (µB) and near production thresh-
old, ϕ mesons are predominantly produced via decays
of high-mass baryon resonances [19–23]. In this regime,
ϕ mesons are created in close association with baryons
and can inherit collective motion driven by baryonic
mean-field interactions, in contrast to top RHIC ener-
gies where ϕ-meson elliptic flow is commonly attributed
to partonic collectivity [24–26].
Measurements of ϕ-
meson flow at low collision energies therefore provide di-
rect sensitivity to baryon-resonance dynamics in dense
QCD matter.
Following their production, the propa-
gation of ϕ mesons in the dense medium is governed
by their weak and medium-dependent ϕ–N interaction
cross section [14, 23]. Together with their longer life-
time, this allows ϕ mesons to preserve information on
collective dynamics developed during the dense bary-
onic stage. Consequently, systematic measurements of
ϕ-meson directed flow as a function of beam energy of-
fer new experimental sensitivity to baryon-resonance,
vector-meson transport and in-medium dynamics in the
high-µB region [13–15, 27].
In this letter, we report the ϕ-meson rapidity-odd
component of directed flow (v1) in Au+Au mid-central
collisions at center-of-mass energies √sNN = 3.0, 3.2,
3.5, 3.9, 4.5, and 7.7 GeV. The √sNN
= 3.0–4.5
GeV data were collected with the STAR detector dur-
ing the 2019–2021 RHIC Beam Energy Scan Fixed-
Target (FXT) program, while the √sNN = 7.7 GeV
collider-energy result follows the procedure described in
Ref. [28]. In the FXT configuration, a gold beam im-
pinges on a gold target positioned at the entrance of
the Time Projection Chamber (TPC) [29]. Events are
required to have a primary vertex within 2 cm of the
target along the beam direction and within 2 cm radi-
ally to suppress background interactions with the beam
pipe. Minimum-bias events are triggered using signals
from the Time-of-Flight (TOF), Beam-Beam Counter
(BBC), Event Plane Detector (EPD) and Vertex Posi-
tion Detector (VPD) detectors [30, 31].
Collision centrality is determined from charged-
particle multiplicities measured by the TPC, combined
with a Monte Carlo (MC) Glauber model simulation

In this paper, pseudorapidity η is defined in
the laboratory frame, while rapidity y is given in the
center-of-mass frame unless otherwise specified. The di-
rected flow is measured with respect to the first-order
Event Plane (EP) reconstructed using the EPD. For
the √sNN = 7.7 GeV collider-energy data, the event-
plane reconstruction follows the procedure described in
Ref. [28]. For the FXT data, the EP is reconstructed
using the east side of the EPD, covering the pseudo-
rapidity range −5.3 < η < −2.6 [34].
Owing to the
asymmetric phase-space acceptance in the FXT geom-
etry, the event-plane resolution is evaluated using the
three-subevent method [16, 35]. In this procedure, two
independent EPD subevents defined on the east side of
the EPD in the pseudorapidity ranges −5.3 < η < −3.3
and −3.3 < η < −2.9 are used together with a TPC-
based subevent at midrapidity (−1 < η < 0), providing
a large separation in η between the flow measurement
region and the event plane determination. The result-
ing first-order event-plane resolution ranges from 55%
to 75% for √sNN = 3.0–4.5 GeV. A centrality range
of 10–40% is selected to ensure both good event-plane
resolution and sufficient ϕ-meson yields.
The ϕ mesons are reconstructed via the decay channel
ϕ →K+K−. Charged tracks are identified using com-
bined specific energy loss (dE/dx) measurements from
the TPC and time-of-flight information from the TOF
and eTOF detectors [36–38]. The invariant-mass distri-
butions of K+K−pairs are constructed in the center-
of-mass frame as shown in Fig. 1(a), with combinato-
rial background estimated using the mixed-event (ME)
technique, where K+ and K−from different events with
similar characteristics (e.g., centrality and event-plane
angle) are paired. The background-subtracted distribu-
tions (red solid circles) are fitted with a Breit–Wigner
function for the signal and a linear term for the residual
background.

A striking feature of the present results is that the
ϕ-meson directed flow is comparable in magnitude to
those of protons and Λ baryons, despite its mesonic na-
ture. The observed similarity between the ϕ-meson and
baryon directed flow suggests that the ϕ meson retains
sensitivity to collective baryon-driven dynamics in the
high-baryon-density region.
For comparison, hadronic transport model calcula-
tions from UrQMD v4.0 [6, 22], which include contribu-
tions from high-mass baryon resonances, are also shown.

the baryonic mean-field (BMF) potential are included,
the model reproduces the observed energy dependence
of dv1/dy for protons and ϕ mesons reasonably well over
the range 3.0 ≤√sNN ≤4.5 GeV.

and UrQMD calculations indicates that BMF interac-

of baryons in the high baryon density region [16]. Al-

As discussed in Ref. [22, 23], the agreement between
the data and UrQMD calculations suggests that excita-
tion and decay of high-mass baryon resonances at high
baryon density,
N + N →N ∗+ N →N + ϕ + N,
(2)
provides a natural mechanism for generating the ob-
served ϕ-meson directed flow. In the UrQMD implemen-
tation used for this comparison, high-mass baryon res-
onances contributing to this channel–such as N ∗(1990),
N ∗(2080), N ∗(2190), N ∗(2220), and N ∗(2250)–are in-
cluded.
Through such intermediate baryonic states,
ϕ mesons are produced in association with baryons
and can inherit the collective motion of the baryonic
medium, resulting in a v1 magnitude comparable to that
of protons and Λ baryons. This production scenario is
also consistent with previously observed enhancements
in the yield ratios N(ϕ)/N(K−) and N(ϕ)/N(Ξ−) in
3 GeV Au+Au collisions [20]. When BMF interactions
are included but high-mass N ∗resonances are absent,
UrQMD calculations fail to reproduce the measured ϕ-
meson directed flow, indicating that such resonances
play an important role in describing both the enhanced
ϕ yield and the baryon-like ϕ-meson v1 in the high-
baryon-density region.
Within current hadronic transport frameworks, the
involvement of high-mass baryon resonances offers a
consistent interpretation of both the production and
collective dynamics of ϕ mesons in baryon-rich mat-
ter. Further theoretical refinements may help to clarify
the detailed composition of these resonance contribu-
tions, while the present data indicate that such reso-
nances play a significant role in shaping the observed

At higher beam energies, in-

both UrQMD configurations underestimate the mea-

the present hadronic transport implementation in the

We note that a corresponding
UrQMD comparison for K0
S is not shown in Fig. 4; nev-

S directed-flow magnitude
over the same energy range suggests a distinct meson-
species dependence of the collective dynamics.
For ϕ-meson production at high baryon density, we
observe an enhanced ϕ/K−ratio [20] and a strong col-
lectivity similar to that of protons and Λ baryons. Other
transport model calculations, besides UrQMD, have also
offered partial explanations. PHQMD [47], employing
an alternative implementation of the baryonic mean
field, can reproduce the ϕ/K−ratio.
JAM2 [48, 49]
calculations reproduce the v1 of protons and Λ baryons,
and preliminary results suggest a qualitatively consis-
tent description of the ϕ v1.
The present observation thus opens a new window to
explore baryon resonance dynamics and vector-meson
transport in dense baryonic matter, and provides ex-
perimental sensitivity to the in-medium propagation
of ϕ mesons, including the effective ϕ–N interaction
strength, as suggested in Ref. [23].
In summary, we report a large directed flow of
ϕ mesons in Au+Au collisions at √sNN = 3.0–4.5
GeV from the STAR FXT program, together with the
√sNN = 7.7 GeV collider-energy result included in
the collision-energy dependence.
The magnitude of
the ϕ-meson v1 is comparable to that of protons and
Λ baryons, and significantly larger than that of K0
S
mesons, indicating that ϕ mesons participate in the col-
lective dynamics of baryon-rich matter.
Comparisons with hadronic transport calculations
suggest that the measured ϕ-meson directed flow is
consistent with production via high-mass baryon res-
onances and collective baryonic motion governed by
mean-field interactions. Owing to its long lifetime and
relatively weak effective ϕ–N interaction, the ϕ meson
can preserve sensitivity to collective dynamics estab-
lished during the dense baryonic stage of the collision.
The observed strong ϕ-meson directed flow thus pro-
vides new experimental sensitivity to baryon-resonance
production and strange-hadron transport in the high-
baryon-density region.
