JAUME DE DIOS PONT, LUKAS LIEHR, AND MITCHELL A. TAYLOR
Abstract. For every dimension d > 1, we establish explicit criteria on lattices
Λ ⊂R2d with density D(Λ) > 1 such that no function with a continuous Zak
transform generates a Gabor frame along Λ. In particular, this gives a negative
answer to the existence problem of Gabor frames with window functions in the
Schwartz space, the Feichtinger algebra, and the Fourier-invariant Wiener space.
Our result is based on a characterization of when a collection of quasiperiodic
functions admits a common zero, which may be of independent interest. We
also include a formalization of our main result in Lean 4.
1. Introduction and main results
1.1.

1.4.
In contrast to what was expected from the literature and the series of pos-

a general obstruction to the frame property of Gabor systems G(g, Λ). Our ob-

g ∈CZ(Rd). As a consequence, this yields a negative resolution to the existence
problem for Gabor frames in S(Rd) and S0(Rd).
Theorem 1.1. For every d > 1 there exists a lattice Λ ⊂R2d with density
D(Λ) > 1 having the following property: there exists no g ∈CZ(Rd) such that
G(g, Λ) is a frame for L2(Rd).

1.8.
We conclude this section by introducing a key ingredient in the proof of The-
orem 1.2, related to the study of zeros of quasiperiodic functions. In what follows
we call a function s : Rd × Rd →C Zak-quasiperiodic (or simply quasiperiodic) if
it satisfies the relation
s(u + n, v + m) = e2πi n·vs(u, v)
for every u, v ∈Rd and every n, m ∈Zd. The notion of (Zak-)quasiperiodicity
stems from the fact that the Zak transform of every g ∈L2(Rd) is quasiperiodic.
The structure of the zero set of the Zak transform is a central topic in the
analysis of Gabor frames.
For instance, a result of Vinogradov and Ulitskaya
establishes the existence of a unique zero for the Zak transform of totally positive
functions [45]; this fact plays a significant role in the frame set conjecture of totally
positive functions [18, 23]. It is well-known that every continuous quasiperiodic
function has a zero. This result was proven by Boon and Zak [7] and independently
by Janssen [30]. A topological proof of the result was obtained by Auslander and
Tolimieri [2, Theorem II.2] in the context of nilmanifolds. A proof based on Chern-
Weil theory was given by Enstad [11, Example 5.6]. Extensions of the result to
the setting of locally compact abelian groups have been developed in [12,31].
The proof of Theorem 1.2 makes use (cf. Lemma 2.1) of the following statement
on common zeros of multiple quasiperiodic functions, which we prove in Section 3
using a topological argument involving Chern classes.

2.3. Chern classes. Throughout this section, all vector bundles are complex
vector bundles unless explicitly stated otherwise.
Let X be a topological space. For an Abelian group A, Hq(X; A) denotes the q-
th singular cohomology group of X with coeﬀicients in A. The cohomology groups
form a graded ring under the cup product
⌣: Hp(X; A) × Hq(X; A) →Hp+q(X; A),
which is graded-commutative: α ⌣β = (−1)pqβ ⌣α. If X is a smooth manifold,
then de Rham cohomology identifies Hq(X; R) with closed smooth q-forms modulo
exact q-forms. Under this identification, the cup product corresponds to the wedge
product of differential forms. Thus, if ω is a closed differential form, we write
[ω] ∈Hq(X; R) for its cohomology class.
A complex vector bundle of rank r over a topological space X is a map π :
E →X such that for every x ∈X, the fiber Ex := π−1(x) is a complex vector
space isomorphic to Cr, and such that locally over X the bundle is isomorphic to
a product: π−1(U) ∼= U × Cr. A section of E is a continuous map S : X →E
such that π(S(x)) = x for every x ∈X. The trivial complex line bundle over X is
denoted by C := X × C →X. More generally, the trivial rank-r complex vector
bundle is Cr := X × Cr →X. If E →X and F →X are complex vector bundles,
their direct sum is the bundle E⊕F →X whose fiber over x is (E⊕F)x = Ex⊕Fx.
To every complex vector bundle E →X of rank r, one associates cohomology
classes
ck(E) ∈H2k(X; Z),
k = 0, 1, . . . , r,
called the Chern classes of E. The class ck(E) has cohomological degree 2k. The
total Chern class is the formal sum
c(E) := 1 + c1(E) + c2(E) + · · · + cr(E).

6. Appendix: Lean formalization
The main statement of this work (Theorem 1.2) has been verified in the Lean lan-
guage [9], using the Mathlib library. The result was auto-formalized using GPT 5.5
in Codex with custom prompt skills and Oliver Dressler’s MCP [10]. The formal-
ization is available at https://github.com/jaumededios/Schwartz_Gabor_Frames.
The files Showcase_CZ.lean and Showcase_Schwartz.lean, produced and exten-
sively reviewed by us, provide a self-contained version of the main statement that
was Lean verified, and contain all necessary definitions that were not already in
Mathlib. The reader may also consult [1, 6, 26, 28] for other recent examples of
formalizations involving AI.
This verification relies on many pre-existing and important mathematical con-
cepts, which exist in Lean thanks to the enormous efforts by the Mathlib com-
munity [44] in carefully formalizing mathematical objects and ensuring that their
meaning is what the mathematical community expects. These include the defini-
tions of quotient groups, the Fourier transform, and even completely elementary
concepts such as the real numbers themselves. The construction of these objects
is not detailed in this section, but they have been thoughtfully designed and very
carefully reviewed by the Mathlib community.
The goal of this section is to communicate the Lean translation to people who
may not be experts in Lean. For the Lean expert, this includes defining abbrevia-
tions such as ℝ^[d] for Rd instead of the more common (Fin d -> ℝ). The result
that was formalized in Lean may be stated as follows.

which is topologically discrete and a Z-lattice (the R-span is the whole

components).
