Abstract
We study the positivity properties of finite flat quotients of a normal projective variety. The numerical
groups and the positive cones of these quotient varieties are related to those of the original variety.
Keywords Finite quotient, nef cones, pseudo-effective cone
MSC Number: 14C20; 14L24; 14L30
1 Introduction
Let X be a normal projective variety over the complex numbers C. Denote by Nk(X) the numerical group
of dimension k-cycles with R-coefficients. The pseudoeffective cone Effk(X) is defined as the closure in
Nk(X) of the cone generated by classes of k-dimensional subvarieties of X. Then Nef k(X) is defined for
smooth X as the dual of Effk(X) with respect to the intersection pairing. When X is singular, we work
instead in the space of dual cycle classes, the abstract dual N k(X) of Nk(X).
It is well-known that proper pushforward maps between projective varieties descend naturally to
numerical groups. Moreover, Fulger and Lehmann [FL1, FL2] showed that these pushforward maps induce
surjection between the pseudoeffective cones and have trivial kernels. Extending this framework, it has
also been shown by Dang in [Da] that the pullback map on cycles induced by a flat morphism between
normal projective varieties descends to maps on numerical groups.
Quotients under group actions play a central role in numerous settings, particularly in the classifi-
cation of geometric objects. Many naturally occurring moduli spaces and classification spaces arise by
identifying points via group actions. In algebraic geometry, Geometric Invariant Theory (GIT) provides
the fundamental framework for constructing well-behaved quotients of varieties or schemes under the

2.2 Positive cones
2.2.1 The pseudoeffective cone
We say that a class α ∈Nk(X) is effective if α = [Z] for some effective cycle Z. This notion is closed
under positive linear combinations, and hence it is natural to consider the following:
Definition 2.2. The closure of the convex cone generated by effective k-cycles on X in Nk(X) is denoted
Effk(X). It is called the pseudoeffective cone. A class α ∈Nk(X) is called pseudoeffective (respectively,
big) if it lies in Effk(X) (respectively, in the interior of Effk(X)).
Note that Eff1(X) is referred to as the closed cone of curves, which is also denoted by NE(X) in the
literature.
