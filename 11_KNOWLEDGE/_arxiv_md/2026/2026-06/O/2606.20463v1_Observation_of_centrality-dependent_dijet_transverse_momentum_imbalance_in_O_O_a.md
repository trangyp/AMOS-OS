Observation of centrality-dependent dijet transverse
momentum imbalance in O+O and Ne+Ne collisions
at √𝒔NN = 5.36 TeV with the ATLAS detector
The ATLAS Collaboration
The ATLAS experiment presents an observation of a centrality-dependent dijet transverse
momentum imbalance in O+O and Ne+Ne collisions at a nucleon–nucleon center-of-mass
energy of 5.36 TeV at the Large Hadron Collider. The measurement uses 8.0 nb−1 of O+O
and 1.0 nb−1 of Ne+Ne data collected in 2025, together with 386 pb−1 of pp data at the same
energy used as a reference. The dijet momentum balance is quantified using the ratio of
the sub-leading jet transverse momentum to that of the leading jet, 𝑥𝐽. For dijets produced
azimuthally back-to-back, the self-normalized 𝑥𝐽distributions exhibit increasingly large
deviations from the pp reference as collisions become more central, corresponding to an
increasing overlap of the colliding nuclei. The observed centrality dependence is consistent
with medium-induced partonic energy loss in O+O and Ne+Ne collisions, demonstrating that
such effects persist in collision systems considerably smaller than Pb+Pb and Xe+Xe. These
results establish a new regime for investigating the path-length dependence of jet quenching
and constrain the onset of quark–gluon plasma effects in small nuclear collision systems.

One of the primary goals of the heavy-ion program at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) is to characterize
the quark–gluon plasma (QGP), a deconfined state of strongly interacting matter produced at extreme
temperature and density [1]. High-transverse-momentum (𝑝T) jets provide a powerful probe of the QGP
over a broad range of resolution scales [2]. As energetic partons traverse the medium, they undergo jet
quenching, manifested through a reduction of the jet energy and a redistribution of energy to large angles
via collisional and medium-induced radiative processes [3]. The magnitude of the energy loss is expected
to depend on the medium properties, including density and temperature, and the in-medium path length
traversed by the parton [4–10], with additional jet-by-jet fluctuations arising from the stochastic nature of
the energy-loss process [11].
Evidence for path-length-dependent energy loss has been obtained in non-central Pb+Pb collisions through
azimuthal modulations of high-𝑝T particle and jet production relative to the event plane [12–14]. However,
fluctuations in the initial-state nucleon configuration and energy deposition complicate the determination
of the effective path length, particularly in peripheral collisions. In proton–nucleus collisions, signatures
of collective behavior have been observed [15], while unambiguous evidence for jet quenching remains
elusive [16, 17].
Collisions of light ions provide a new opportunity to investigate the path-length dependence of jet quenching
in systems with a substantially smaller transverse extent than Pb+Pb collisions. Central O+O and Ne+Ne
collisions retain an extended and approximately symmetric transverse overlap geometry while reducing
the characteristic in-medium path length relative to central Pb+Pb collisions, for which the typical path
length is expected to be approximately a factor of two larger. This results in a correspondingly smaller
expected magnitude of energy loss [18]. Furthermore, the more symmetric overlap geometry of central
light-ion collisions relative to peripheral Pb+Pb interactions is expected to reduce geometric uncertainties
associated with the interpretation of path-length-dependent effects [19].
Recent measurements [20–22] show evidence of collective behavior in both O+O and Ne+Ne collisions,
suggesting the formation of a QGP. Complementary measurements of hard probes have also been used to
search for signatures of medium-induced effects in light-ion collisions. The CMS Collaboration measured
the charged-hadron nuclear modification factor in centrality-integrated O+O [23] and Ne+Ne [24] collisions
at √𝑠NN = 5.36 TeV and observed a suppression relative to unity that is not described by calculations
including only nuclear modifications to parton distributions. Additionally, the STAR Collaboration at
RHIC observed a suppression of hadron–hadron and hadron–jet correlations in central O+O collisions
relative to a peripheral reference at √𝑠NN = 200 GeV [25]. Both of these measurements are suggestive of
partonic energy loss in light-ion collisions.
In small collision systems, the interpretation of jet-quenching observables is complicated by correlations
between hard-process production and the soft underlying-event activity used to characterize the collision
geometry [26–28]. These “centrality bias” effects can become sizable in measurements based on absolute
jet yields or Glauber-model scaling quantities such as ⟨𝑇AA⟩or ⟨𝑁coll⟩[29–32]. Measurements that do not
depend on absolute rates or quantities extracted from Glauber modeling and are based on distributions
normalized to unit area are expected to be significantly less sensitive to such effects.
In Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), jet production proceeds through the fragmentation of quarks and
gluons from a short-distance scattering process. At leading order, pairs of jets (dijets) are produced
approximately balanced in the plane transverse to the beam (back-to-back). Dijet observables are sensitive
to path-length-dependent energy loss, since the two jets may traverse different distances through the
medium [33]. Additionally, they are less sensitive than jet yields to centrality-bias effects [28] and to
uncertainties associated with nuclear parton distributions [34], which remain poorly constrained for oxygen

An event-by-event background subtraction procedure uses estimates of the average underlying-event

In contrast to Pb+Pb collisions, the azimuthal modulation of the underlying-event background arising from
collective particle flow is neglected, since its effect on jet reconstruction is negligible for the jet kinematics
considered.
Simulation-based 𝜂- and 𝑝T-dependent corrections are applied to measured jets to calibrate the jet energy
response and jet energy resolution [62, 63]. Additional corrections derived from in situ studies of 𝛾+jet,
𝑍+jet, and dijet events are applied to account for residual differences between data and simulation [64].
The absolute jet energy scale (JES), the 𝜂-intercalibration, and the jet energy resolution (JER) are derived
from in situ studies using Run 3 𝑝𝑝data at √𝑠= 13.6 TeV. A cross-calibration then transfers the absolute
JES from high-luminosity 13.6 TeV collisions to low-luminosity 5.36 TeV data [62].
To evaluate the jet reconstruction performance and detector response, dijet Monte Carlo samples were
simulated using Pythia8 [65] with the A14 tune [66] and the NNPDF23LO parton distribution function
set [67]. The detector response was modeled using the full ATLAS detector simulation based on the
Geant4 toolkit [68]. For O+O and Ne+Ne collisions, the Geant4 simulation output from Pythia8 events
were overlaid with minimum-bias data events of the same collision system to reproduce the underlying-event
conditions observed in data. These “data overlay” samples were recorded using a combination of 𝐸T-based
triggers to enhance the fraction of central collisions and, at the level of analysis, were reweighted on an
event-by-event basis to match the centrality distribution of the jet-triggered data sample.
The analysis and dijet selections closely follow the previous ATLAS measurement in Pb+Pb collisions [39],
to facilitate comparisons between the results. The leading dijet pair is formed from the two highest-𝑝T jets
in the event. This jet pair must satisfy the fiducial dijet selections that require reconstructed 𝑝T1 > 50 GeV,
𝑝T2 > 20 GeV, |𝑦| < 2.1, and Δ𝜙> 7𝜋/8. Results are reported for 63 < 𝑝T1 < 251 GeV and 𝑥J > 0.32,
while events outside this range are retained in underflow bins for the unfolding procedure. For all collision
systems, 𝑥J is measured in the same range, with five bins in Ne+Ne and ten bins in O+O. The binning in 𝑥J
was chosen to be coarser in Ne+Ne collisions to account for the smaller data sample size. For comparisons
to O+O (Ne+Ne), the 𝑝𝑝reference is measured using the same ten-bin (five-bin) 𝑥J binning.
The measured (𝑝T1, 𝑝T2) distributions include contributions from both correlated and uncorrelated jet pairs.
The latter arise from independent hard scatterings involving different nucleon pairs within the same ion
collision and are uniformly distributed in Δ𝜙. Their contribution is estimated by using a sideband method.
A symmetrized (𝑝T1, 𝑝T2) distribution constructed from dijets satisfying 0.8 < Δ𝜙< 1.1 is normalized
to the signal region and subtracted from the data. The presence of uncorrelated jets also introduces an
inefficiency for genuine dijets when one jet in the pair is replaced by an uncorrelated third jet. This effect
is corrected using the measured inclusive jet spectrum in minimum-bias events following the procedure
of Ref. [38]. The combination of the sideband subtraction and the correction for the uncorrelated jet
inefficiency results in a net correction of at most 3.2% to the final 𝑥J distributions in the most central 0–10%
interval, and significantly less in more peripheral collisions. Contributions from three-jet topologies to the
sideband region are found to be less than 0.5% in simulation and neglected. Potential contributions from
the azimuthal anisotropy of particle production ("harmonic flow"), which can induce a modulation of the
uncorrelated dijet background as a function of Δ𝜙, are evaluated through the centrality dependence of the
Δ𝜙sideband modulation [38]. The resulting effect on the measurement is found to be negligible in O+O
and Ne+Ne collisions.

bin migration of both jets and possible interchange of the leading and sub-leading jet ordering. The response

In addition to biases arising from correlations between hard-scattering and soft particle production, the
centrality determination could also be biased directly by particles produced in the hard process itself
including, for example, the presence of additional jets within the acceptance of the centrality estimator.
This effect is studied using simulated Pythia8 dijet events overlaid with events generated with Hijing [72]
and processed with the same centrality selection as used in data. Since the dijet signal is generated with
unquenched Pythia8 in both 𝑝𝑝and light-ion collisions, any modification to the 𝑥J distributions relative to
𝑝𝑝would indicate a topology-driven bias coming from the centrality determination. Deviations relative to
𝑝𝑝are found to be small and generally comparable to the statistical uncertainties, indicating that multijet
final states do not significantly affect the measured 𝑥J distributions or the interpretation of the results.
Systematic uncertainties in the measured 𝑥J distributions arise from the JES, JER, combinatorial background
subtraction, and unfolding procedure. The JES uncertainty includes contributions from in situ calibration,
cross-calibration, flavor response, flavor composition, and, for O+O and Ne+Ne collisions, possible
modifications of the calorimetric response due to medium-modified parton showers [39, 62]. The JER
uncertainty is evaluated by varying the jet resolution in simulation according to in situ measurements
based on dijet momentum balance in 𝑝𝑝collisions [73]. The use of Monte Carlo samples with data
overlay avoids the need to assign an additional centrality-dependent uncertainty to account for residual
differences between underlying-event fluctuations in data and simulation. The resulting JER variations are
symmetrized. Their impact is evaluated by repeating the full analysis with modified response matrices and
comparing the resulting self-normalized 𝑥J distributions with the nominal result. An uncertainty in the
combinatorial background subtraction is evaluated by varying the Δ𝜙window that defines the sideband
region. The nominal window is varied (1.1 < Δ𝜙< 1.4) and the full analysis is repeated. The resulting
difference is symmetrized and taken as a systematic uncertainty.
Additional systematic uncertainties arise from the unfolding procedure. The sensitivity to the Bayesian
prior is evaluated by reweighting the prior to approximately reproduce the reconstructed distributions in
data and repeating the unfolding. The difference from the nominal unfolded result is symmetrized and
assigned as a systematic uncertainty. An additional systematic uncertainty is assigned to account for the
closure after experimental effects were re-applied to the unfolded result by means of the response matrix.
The closure is consistent with unity within statistical uncertainties, which are mostly below 3% in 𝑝𝑝
collisions and increase in O+O and Ne+Ne collisions, particularly at low 𝑥J where they can reach 15–20%.
Any deviations from unity are conservatively taken as an additional systematic uncertainty.
Generally, the JES and the JER dominate the total systematic uncertainties. For most bins of 𝑥J the total
systematic uncertainty is below 10–15%, only reaching higher values in the lowest bins of 𝑥J. At low 𝑝T1

To quantify the modification of the 𝑥J distributions in O+O and Ne+Ne collisions, their ratios to those
measured in the 𝑝𝑝reference are evaluated for each centrality interval. Uncertainties in the ratio arising
from the JES, JER, and combinatorial background subtraction are correlated, and thus partially cancel out
in the ratio. The significance of the deviation of the ratios from a null hypothesis of no modification (unity)
is determined using a 𝜒2 test based on the full covariance matrix of the self-normalized ratios, including
bin-to-bin correlations from statistical and systematic uncertainties. For asymmetric uncertainties, the
covariance matrix corresponding to the uncertainty variation that yields the smallest deviation from unity
is used, resulting in a conservative estimate of the significance.

This Letter presented measurements of the dijet momentum balance, 𝑥J, in O+O and Ne+Ne collisions
at √𝑠NN = 5.36 TeV for five centrality intervals, compared with 𝑝𝑝collisions at the same energy. The
measurement is performed for leading jets between 63 and 251 GeV, with dijet pairs selected with
Δ𝜙> 7𝜋/8, and both jets having |𝑦| < 2.1. The 𝑥J distributions are unfolded for detector effects, and
the distributions are normalized by the yield of dijets in each leading jet selection. An increase in the
relative contribution of lower 𝑥J dijets in 0–10% central O+O and Ne+Ne collisions compared with both
𝑝𝑝and peripheral collisions for each light ion system is observed over the range of leading jet 𝑝T reported
in this measurement. The significance of the modification is quantified by comparing the ratios of the
𝑥J distributions in O+O and Ne+Ne collisions to those in 𝑝𝑝collisions with the null hypothesis unity,
corresponding to no modification. The modification is largest in the most central collisions and remains
statistically significant in O+O (Ne+Ne) collisions up to the 20–40% (10–20%) centrality interval. In
0–10% central O+O (Ne+Ne) collisions, significant deviations from the no-modification hypothesis are
observed up to 𝑝T1 141 (126) GeV.
The observed modifications of the 𝑥J distributions are qualitatively similar to those previously measured in
nucleus–nucleus collisions [14, 40], while occurring in a regime where projectile-configuration effects [31,
32] and event-activity selection biases [28] are explicitly constrained. These measurements provide new
constraints on the onset and path-length dependence of jet quenching in small collision systems, extending
the study of partonic energy loss toward the smallest quark–gluon plasma droplets accessible at the LHC.

(3)

statistical and systematic bin-to-bin correlations after self-normalization. For asymmetric uncertainties,
the covariance matrix corresponding to the uncertainty variation that yields the smallest deviation from
unity is used.
