On Automorphism Groups of (1, 2)-Surfaces
Hang Zhao

Abstract
Let S be a minimal surface of general type with K2
S = 1 and pg(S) = 2, commonly
referred to as a (1, 2)-surface. The automorphism groups of such surfaces have been classified
by D. Wen using algebraic methods via the canonical ring, establishing the bound |Aut(S)| ≤
200. In this paper, we provide a geometric recovery of this bound from the double cover of
the Hirzebruch surface Σ2. We compute the automorphisms of Σ2 from its Cox ring and
analyze the induced action on the base P1 together with its vertical kernel. Applying this
to automorphisms preserving the branch divisor R = ∆0 + R0, R0 ∈|5∆0 + 10Γ|, gives a
geometric framework for the vertical and horizontal parts of the automorphism group and
recovers Wen’s bound |Aut(S)| ≤200.

Introduction
The study of automorphism groups of surfaces of general type is a classical problem in algebraic
geometry. For a minimal surface S of general type, the automorphism group Aut(S) is finite.
A specific class of interest is the family of surfaces with K2
S = 1 and pg(S) = 2, denoted as
(1, 2)-surfaces.
Beyond their intrinsic appeal as surfaces lying on the second Noether line K2 = 2pg −3
with the smallest possible invariants, (1, 2)-surfaces also arise naturally in the explicit birational
geometry of threefolds of general type. The Noether inequality for projective threefolds of general
type, vol(X) ≥4
3pg(X) −10
3 , was established through a sequence of works. J. A. Chen, M. Chen
and C. Jiang first proved it for pg(X) ≤4 and pg(X) ≥21 [CCJ20b], and their addendum
improved the range to pg(X) ≥11 [CCJ20a]. The remaining cases 5 ≤pg(X) ≤10 were settled
by M. Chen, Y. Hu and C. Jiang [CHJ25], completing the proof of the inequality in full generality.
The inequality is sharp: M. Kobayashi [Kob92] constructed examples attaining equality, lying on
the three-dimensional Noether line vol(X) = 4
3pg(X) −10
3 . Such extremal threefolds are closely
tied to (1, 2)-surfaces. Y. Hu and T. Zhang proved that the sharp lower bound for the slope
of fibred threefolds over curves is 4
3, with equality occurring precisely when the general fibre is
a (1, 2)-surface [HZ21], and they further classified the equality case of the Noether inequality
for pg(X) ≥11 by describing the explicit structure of the relative canonical model [HZ25].
S. Coughlan and R. Pignatelli introduced and studied simple fibrations in (1, 2)-surfaces, showing
that almost all Gorenstein simple fibrations over P1 with at worst canonical singularities give
canonical threefolds on the Noether line [CP23]. More recently, S. Coughlan, Y. Hu, R. Pignatelli
and T. Zhang proved, in the setting of the refined Noether line, that such threefolds admit simple
fibrations in (1, 2)-surfaces up to crepant birational modification, and used this description to
study the corresponding moduli spaces [CHPZ24].
Thus the moduli, canonical models and
automorphism theory of (1, 2)-surfaces provide essential input for understanding threefolds near
the Noether line.
Recently, D. Wen [Wen21] classified the automorphism groups of these surfaces.

In each case R is therefore reduced with only negligible singularities and R0 ∩∆0 = ∅, so
by the converse construction of Section 2.3 it defines a (1, 2)-surface S, to which Theorem 4.4
applies. The subgroup G ⊆Aut(S) exhibited above has order equal to the upper bound of
Theorem 4.4 for the relevant K0; since |Aut(S)| cannot exceed that bound, Aut(S) = G. Thus
each bound of Theorem 4.4 is attained, and the inequalities there are optimal.
Remark 5.2. Proposition 5.1 realizes the optimal horizontal bounds of Theorem 4.4 in the cases
K0 ∼= C5, K0 ∼= C4, and K0 = 1, with |H0| = 20, 24, 40 respectively, realized by D20, S4 and
C40. In the case K0 ∼= C2 the apparent sharpness candidate H0 ∼= T12 is inadmissible: it forces
P8 ∈⟨P 2
4 ⟩and hence a non-negligible branch singularity. The sharp admissible horizontal group
is therefore D8, realized by the construction above, attaining |H0| = 8 and |Aut(S)| = 32.
Remark 5.3. Proposition 5.1 organizes the extremal (1, 2)-surfaces by the four values of K0; no
separate examples section is needed. In particular Wen’s bound is attained, the K0 ∼= C5 surface
having Aut(S) ∼= D20 × C10 of order 200.
