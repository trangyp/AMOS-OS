ABSTRACT
Recent observations with JWST have revealed a remarkable population of surprisingly luminous galaxies within the first 500 Myr of cosmic time at
redshifts z > 10. Their abundance exceed predictions from simulations and empirical extrapolations from lower redshifts, suggesting a transition in
the physical conditions under which the first stars formed. Here we investigate the physical conditions of a select sample of 25 galaxies with robust
redshift measurements at zspec ≥10 observed with JWST/NIRSpec Prism. We characterize their star-formation efficiency, ‘burstiness’, and presence
of strong rest-frame UV nebular lines in relation to the density of the local neutral atomic hydrogen (H i) gas reservoirs they are embedded in.
We find that the prominence of strong rest-UV lines are correlated with the burstiness of the galaxies, defined as SFR10 Myr/SFR100 Myr. In contrast,
there are no strong connections between the H i gas column density derived from the damped Lyα absorption (DLA) and the MUV brightness,
SFR10 Myr/SFR100 Myr, and prominence of rest-UV lines. We find evidence for the most bursty galaxies showing a large variation in star-formation
efficiencies and H i gas surface densities, though typically with very short depletion timescales, tdep ≲20 Myr. This necessites rapid gas depletion
times and external replenishment from infalling, pristine gas, powering starburst episodes on equally short timescales. We further quantify the
impact of strong DLAs in galaxy spectra on photometric and Lyα-break redshift-inferences, finding average redshift biases of ⟨z⟩= 0.39 and
0.14, respectively, when not incorporating DLAs on the emergent spectra. We show the effect of this bias on new measurements of the cosmic
UV luminosity density, ρUV, derived here at z > 10, finding that this has a marginal impact on the UV luminosity function. In conclusion, we
provide observational evidence for highly efficient star formation occurring in dense gas with rapid depletion timescales for galaxies at z > 10,
and highlight the perils of not accounting for strong Lyα absorption in photometric or Lyman-break redshift determinations.

1. Introduction
The first stars and galaxies are believed to emerge from the ‘dark
ages’ of the Universe at redshifts z ≈20 −30 (Dayal & Ferrara
2018; Robertson 2022; Stark et al. 2025; Bouwens 2026),
heralding cosmic dawn. This process is initiated by the grav-
itational infall and collapse of primordial neutral atomic hy-
drogen (H i) onto dark matter halos, which serve as the pri-
mary fuel for the formation of the first stellar sources. With
the recent advent of powerful space-based observatories, in par-
ticular the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST, Gardner et al.
2006), we are now starting to approach this redshift frontier,
with robust spectroscopic measurements of luminous galaxies
at z ≳14 (Carniani et al. 2024; Naidu et al. 2025; Wu et al.
2025) and photometric measurements extending potentially out
to z ∼15 −25 (e.g., Robertson et al. 2024; Whitler et al. 2025b;
Weibel et al. 2025; Pérez-González et al. 2025; Castellano et al.
2025a).
One of the perhaps most surprising discoveries in early
JWST observations was the apparent overabundance of UV-
bright galaxies at z ≳10 (e.g. Castellano et al. 2022; Naidu et al.
2022; Atek et al. 2023; Bouwens et al. 2023; Donnan et al.
2023; Finkelstein et al. 2023; Harikane et al. 2023; Adams et al.
2024; McLeod et al. 2024; Robertson et al. 2024; Carniani et al.
2024), far greater in number and luminosity than pre-
dicted from (mostly pre-JWST) simulations and extrapo-
lations from the lower-redshift galaxy population. Several
physical scenarios have been proposed to explain this, in-
cluding extremely stochastic or ‘bursty’ star-formation his-
tories (Sun et al. 2023; Mason et al. 2023; Shen et al. 2023;
Gelli et al. 2024; Kravtsov & Belokurov 2024; Endsley et al.
2025; Muñoz et al. 2026), feedback-free starburst episodes
(Dekel et al. 2023; Li et al. 2024), increased star-formation
efficiencies (Somerville et al. 2025; Mauerhofer et al. 2025;
Pollock et al. 2026b), differences in the dust attenuation or
the entire removal of dust from the galaxies (Ferrara et al.
2023; McKinney et al. 2025), higher UV-light to mass ra-
tios driven by a more pronounced ‘top-heavy’ stellar ini-
tial mass function (IMF; Cowley et al. 2018; Inayoshi et al.
2022; Yung et al. 2024; Hutter et al. 2025; Mauerhofer et al.
2025; Lu et al. 2025), strong nebular emission continuum
(Cameron et al. 2024; Katz et al. 2024; Reumert et al. 2026), or
substantial contributions from active galactic nuclei (AGN, e.g.,
Hegde et al. 2024). Fundamentally, the majority of these scenar-
ios imply a transition in the primary mode of star formation in
galaxies within the first 500 Myr of cosmic time.
The spectroscopic JWST legacy samples are now start-
ing to become statistically significant at z > 10, enabling

they show more prominent and prevalent nebular emission
lines (Tang et al. 2025), hinting at more rapid star-formation
timescales or a large diversity in the occurrence between bursts
and lulls of star formation (Roberts-Borsani et al. 2025). Fi-
nally, the majority of galaxies at z > 10 show broad damped
Lyα absorption (Heintz et al. 2024, 2025b; Umeda et al. 2023;
Carniani et al. 2024; Pollock et al. 2026b), indicating the pres-
ence of extremely dense H i gas in the local surroundings of these
galaxies (Rowland et al. 2025; Gelli et al. 2025).
We here seek to resolve the underlying physical mechanism
that drives the remarkable UV luminosity of galaxies at z > 10
using a similar statistical sample of 22 galaxies, all spectroscop-
ically confirmed to be at z > 10, and with high-quality spectro-
scopic and photometric data. We investigate two potential sce-
narios that could either drive or mimic extreme UV brightness
in this early galaxy population: i) recent elevated star-formation
efficiencies and ii) overestimated distances and intrinsic lumi-
nosities. Specifically, we aim to understand the impact of the
dense, local H i gas on driving the observed star-formation prop-
erties and the induced bias from strong DLAs on the derived
redshifts when only information from photometry or the Lyα
break in spectroscopy is available. The latter point is particu-
larly important at z > 10 where most of the prominent rest-frame
optical nebular lines are redshifted out of the JWST/NIRSpec
wavelength coverage. In the companion paper by Pollock et al.
(2026b), we have already shown that galaxies at these redshifts
generally have high star-formation efficiencies (SFEs) or short
gas depletion timescales based on their offset from the local
‘Kennicutt-Schmidt’ relation (Kennicutt & Evans 2012). Here,
we focus on deriving the impact of the gas density itself on the
burstiness and SFE of this early galaxy population.
We have structured the paper as follows. In Sect. 2, we
present the compilation of JWST data used in this work and de-
tail the spectro-photometric analysis of each source, including
a careful evaluation of the derived redshifts inferred from pho-

Sect. 3 presents the results on the inferred star-formation effi-
ciency and ‘burstiness’ of the target galaxies. Finally, in Sect. 4
we quantify the redshift bias when considering only the pho-
tometric or Lyα-break inferences and show the implications of
this bias on a new constructed UV luminosity function curve
here and compared to the literature. In Sect. 5 we summarize
and conclude on our work.

2. Observations and analysis
2.1. Observations and z > 10 sample
The spectroscopic data entering the main sample analyzed in this
work is extracted from the DAWN JWST archive (DJA)1, in-
cluding all publicly available spectra taken with JWST to date.
We only consider the spectra taken with the JWST/NIRSpec
Prism configuration (Jakobsen et al. 2022), using the data prod-
ucts from the fourth DJA version (v4, see Valentino et al. 2025;
Pollock et al. 2025) building on previous releases (Brammer
2023; Heintz et al. 2025a; de Graaff et al. 2025). Notably, this
new version includes a bar shadow correction, greatly improving
the absolute and color-dependent flux calibration, and extends
the nominal wavelength range up to 0.6 −5.5µm at a spectral
resolving power of R = 30 −300. The delivered spectral reso-
lutions are typically higher by 30 −50% (de Graaff et al. 2024;
Pollock et al. 2025), depending on the size of the source and its
location within the shutter. We compile all spectra from the DJA
with secure spectroscopic redshifts z ≥10 and with S/N > 3 per
wavelength bin in the rest-frame UV. We include new data for
two galaxies at z > 10 from the ‘Mirage or Miracle’ (MoM) sur-
vey (Prog. ID = 5224, PIs: Oesch & Naidu), two of them already
presented as MoM-z14 and MoM-z11 in Naidu et al. (2025) and
Roberts-Borsani et al. (2025).
The full set of JWST/NIRSpec Prism spectra for the com-
piled galaxies at z ≥10 considered in this work are shown in
Figure 1, visualizing their similarities and diversities, and their
source properties are summarized in Table 1, including refer-
ences to the original survey papers or previous works on the
same sources in the literature.
2.2. Redshift estimates
Since most of the prominent rest-frame optical nebular emis-
sion lines are redshifted out of the wavelengths covered by
JWST’s NIRSpec’s wavelength coverage at z ≳10, it is chal-
lenging to accurately constrain these high spectroscopic red-
shifts (zspec). For each galaxy, we thus carefully evaluate the
rest-frame UV continuum and potential line emission, typi-
cally relying on the detection of C iii] λ1909 which is one of
the strongest rest-frame UV lines detected at z > 10 (see
Roberts-Borsani et al. 2025; Pollock et al. 2026b, for more de-
tails). We use the redshifts inferred from photometry, zphot, com-
piled from the literature or DJA and the Lyα break, zbreak, as pri-
ors on the upper bound of zspec when searching for emission-line
features, since these might overestimate the true systemic red-
shift due to prominent DLAs mimicking a higher redshift Lyα
break (see e.g., Arrabal Haro et al. 2023a; Fujimoto et al. 2023;
Finkelstein et al. 2024; Heintz et al. 2024, 2025a; Hainline et al.
2024, 2026; Asada et al. 2025; Witstok et al. 2025b). This will
be discussed in more detail in Sect. 2.3 below.
Where available, we also include the systemic line redshift
for the sample galaxies from the literature, when recovered, for
instance, in targeted follow-up observations of the far-infrared
(FIR) [O iii]-88µm line emission with the Atacama Large Mil-
limetre/submillimetre Array (ALMA; see e.g., Witstok et al.
2025b; Schouws et al. 2024; Carniani et al. 2025). This accu-
rately pin-points zspec at FIR wavelengths (typically of order
∆z = 0.0001) and enables a detailed characterization of the Lyα
damping wings, even when no prominent emission lines are de-
tected in the rest-frame UV to optical JWST spectra. The various
redshift estimates from zspec, zbreak, and zphot, are summarized in

2.3. Damped Lyman-α absorption modeling
For each of the galaxies at z ≥10, we fit the observed Lyα damp-
ing wings assuming two different physical components: H i in the
IGM along the line of sight and H i in the local environment, fol-
lowing previous works (Heintz et al. 2024, 2025b; Pollock et al.
2026b). Briefly, we describe the shape of the Lyα damping wings
as imprinted from the H i in the IGM following the prescrip-
tion by Miralda-Escudé (1998), though with the corrections by
Totani et al. (2006). The DLAs representing the local dense H i
gas is modeled as Voigt profiles, following the approximation by
Tepper-García (2006). We fit each of the compiled galaxy spec-
tra using three different iterations:
– IGM+ISM. Our default model includes Lyα damping wing
absorption from a combination of a partly to fully neutral
hydrogen fraction, xHI ≡nHI/nH,tot, in the IGM along the
line of sights and the column density of H i gas, NHI, in
the local environments of the galaxies (ISM or CGM). In
this default model, we fix the DLA redshift and the up-
per bound on the IGM component to zspec. We assume this
scenario to be the most common and physically motivated,
since most of the galaxies observed at z > 10 in the litera-
ture have implied H i column densities greatly in excess of
that imprinted from even a fully neutral IGM (Heintz et al.
2024; Pollock et al. 2026b, though see Mason et al. 2026).
From this model, we extract the median and 16th to 84th
percentiles from the posterior distributions of the main out-
put parameters xHI and NHI. This model thus provides the
best constraints on the joint H i absorption in the two phys-
ically distinct components. We caution, however, that xHI
will typically be degenerate with NHI at low column densi-
ties (NHI ≲1021 cm−2), and is difficult to constrain at higher

column densities to complete saturation from the DLA (e.g.,

– IGM only. To gauge the bias on estimating the galaxy red-
shifts from the Lyα break alone without an additional DLA
component, we also model each spectra with only the IGM
component where zbreak and xHI are left as free parameters.
This scenario represents the cases where the exact systemic
redshifts cannot be determined from any identifiable emis-
sion lines, and only serves as a way to quantify the Lyα-break
redshift bias.
– ISM only. In this iteration, we remain agnostic about the ex-
act location of the bulk H i gas. This is due to the expected de-
generacies between the IGM component contributing to xHI
and the dense, local gas contributing to NHI. We thus simply
model the Lyα damping wing as a DLA with the NHI output
representing the total amount of H i gas integrated along the
line of sight to each source. This provides an upper bound on
the local H i gas density in each galaxy.
The general Lyα damping wing modeling is described in de-
tail in Pollock et al. (2026b). Briefly, we use dynesty (Speagle
2020) to perform the statistical modeling for each of the itera-
tions described above to estimate the posterior distributions for
each of the free parameters and the total evidence used for model
comparison. We consider a broad range in local H i column den-
sity, log(NHI/cm−2) ∈[18, 24], and a zero to fully neutral IGM,
xHI ∈[0, 1]. Due to the low spectral resolution of the NIRSpec
Prism observations, there is a large degeneracy between these
two quantities at lower NHI ≲1021 cm−2 (Heintz et al. 2024;
Huberty et al. 2025; Pollock et al. 2026b), such that only galax-
ies with strong DLAs can be robustly attributed to the local ISM
or CGM (Heintz et al. 2025a). The rest-frame UV galaxy con-
tinuum is modeled as a simple power-law, with Fλ ∝λβUV with
βUV ∈[−4, 0], superimposed with Gaussian emission-line pro-
files at zspec. An example spectrum and the best-fit models from
the two scenarios are shown in Fig. 2, highlighting the redshift
bias from considering the Lyα break and IGM component alone.
2.4. Physical properties and star-formation histories
For each of the compiled galaxies at z
>
10, we also
derive their physical properties. First, we perform spectro-
photometric fitting of their spectral energy distributions (SEDs)
with the Bagpipes tool (Carnall et al. 2018), as described in de-
tail in Pollock et al. (2026b). Briefly, we photometrically cor-
rect the spectra by scaling the overall flux density to match
the magnitude in the closest rest-frame UV filter (typically
JWST/NIRCam’s F200W) and for differential slit loss based on
the location of the source on the shutter. The redshifts of each
galaxy is fixed to that derived from the most prominent emis-
sion lines or from the constraints from the Lyα damping wings.
We adopt a non-parametric continuity star-formation history
(Leja et al. 2019), with lookback time bins in 10 equally spaced,
logarithmic time intervals, and a flexible dust attenuation curve
(Salim et al. 2018, modified from Calzetti et al. 2000) to model
the photometrically-scaled spectra and the available photometry
simultaneously (see Xiao et al., submitted and Gottumukkala et
al. in prep. for further details on the Bagpipes modeling done
for the entire DJA spectroscopic repository, including constraints
from the continuum and emission-line properties). From the out-

3. Results
3.1. Rest-frame UV brightness
To investigate the properties of the galaxy population at z > 10,
we first consider their absolute UV magnitude as a function of
cosmic time and derived H i column density in Fig. 3. Over-
all, the compiled sample galaxies span a range of luminosi-
ties from MUV = −18 to −22 mag and H i column densities
NHI = 1020.5 −1022.5 cm−2. The H i column densities here and
in the figure are assuming the maximum allowed ISM contri-
bution for the non-converged cases. We contextualize these re-
sults by comparing to the evolutionary track of a constant star-
formation rate of SFR = 1 M⊙yr−1 and predictions for the UV lu-
minosity of a galaxy following a maximal possible accretion sce-
nario allowed in standard ΛCDM cosmology assuming a smooth
gas accretion to SFR (Dekel et al. 2013), see also Kokorev et al.
(2024). The large majority of the sample galaxies show higher
equivalent MUV than the constant SFR track, consistent with
the typical galaxy at z ∼10 having higher SFRs than one so-
lar mass per year, at least for stellar masses M⋆≳108 M⊙
(e.g., Heintz et al. 2023; Pollock et al. 2025). The two galax-
ies with fainter MUV are JADES-GS-z13-0 at z = 12.85 and
JADES-GS-z10-0, respectively (Curtis-Lake et al. 2023), con-
sistent with their low stellar masses and SFRs derived from pho-
tometry (Robertson et al. 2023).
On the opposite end, only the source JADES-GS-z14-0 at
z = 14.18 (Carniani et al. 2024) appears to be located in the
ΛCDM-excluded region. This is at odds with the expected sce-
nario where this source would be observed in a snapshot during

2023; Muñoz et al. 2026), since it shows no apparent strong
emission lines indicating recent starburst activity and has a rela-
tive low SFR10Myr/SFR100 Myr ≈1.2. Intriguingly, this galaxy is
embedded in a pristine, dense H i gas reservoir, with a derived

3.2. Bursty star formation
We then consider the effect of dense H i gas on the ‘bursti-
ness’ of the galaxies at z > 10, here quantified as the frac-
tion of short- (10 Myr) to long-lived (∼100 Myr) star-formation
rate, SFR10 Myr/SFR100 Myr (as is typically done in the litera-
ture, e.g., Endsley et al. 2025; Cole et al. 2025; Kokorev et al.
2025; Carvajal-Bohorquez et al. 2025; Simmonds et al. 2025),
integrated over the star-formation history from the SED mod-
eling. In Fig. 4, we show SFR10 Myr/SFR100 Myr as a function
of cosmic time, again color-coded with NHI. Now a more clear
picture emerges: Five out of seven galaxies with strong rest-
frame UV lines are observed in a bursting phase with a recent
SFR 2 −5× higher than the average over the past 100 Myr.
This is consistent with the scenario where these are the most

Overall, we find that full sample shows evidence for an ex-
cess of short- to long-lived SFR, with a mean and median value
of SFR10 Myr/SFR100 Myr = 1.74 and 1.31, respectively, with a
scatter of 0.3 dex. This is consistent with predictions from the hy-
drodynamical First Light And Reionization Epoch Simulations
(FLARES; Lovell et al. 2021; Wilkins et al. 2023a,b), which is
mainly a representation of the smooth dark matter accretion rate
and the active assembly and build-up of stars. Only galaxies
above this relation should be treated as being in a strong burst
phase. We find no apparent trend between the H i column den-
sity and SFR10 Myr/SFR100 Myr, with a large variation in column
densities (1021 −1022.5 cm−2) for the most bursty galaxies. How-
ever, we note that the majority of the sample with prominent UV
lines are found with lower NHI < 1022 cm−2, and vice versa, all
galaxies with NHI > 1022 cm−2 show no evidence for strong rest-
UV lines except for JADES-GS-z12-0 (Curtis-Lake et al. 2023;
D’Eugenio et al. 2024).
Generally, we note that the short- to long-lived SFR ratio
used as a proxy for the burstiness of the target galaxies here,
likely also or alternatively reflects the young ages of the av-
erage stellar population in these systems. Examining the star-
formation histories (SFHs) indeed shows that the majority of
galaxies in the sample have predicted formation times only
∼30 −50 Myr before the time of observation. The excess
SFR10 Myr/SFR100 Myr ratio thus mainly trace the current ris-
ing SFHs of the target, UV-bright galaxies. Complementarily,
Endsley et al. (2024) found evidence for the brightest galaxies
at z ≳6 tend to have more very-recently rising SFHs com-
pared to fainter galaxies at the same redshift, albeit with a
large scatter. The most bursty galaxies were also found to have
the most prominent nebular emission lines (Endsley et al. 2024;
Roberts-Borsani et al. 2025), in agreement with our results.

Comparing the SFE or gas depletion timescales of the sample
galaxies to their derived burstiness, we also observe a continu-
ous distribution where both bursty and less active star-forming
galaxies appear to span the entire parameter space from the
most efficient star-formation (or shortest gas depletion times), to
long ∼100 Myr depletion timescales and relatively inefficient
star formation. In particular, some of the most bursty galax-
ies with SFR10 Myr/SFR100 Myr > 3 show H i gas surface den-
sities from Σgas ∼10 to ∼300 M⊙pc−2. The galaxies with
SFR10 Myr ∼SFR100 Myr are observed to span a similar range.
This suggests that we are likely observing an evolving popula-
tion of galaxies with rapidly rising SFHs that has not yet ex-
hausted their available gas reservoirs to systems that have con-
sumed or ejected most of their available gas in this scenario,
showing an artificially high SFE. The latter will thus not be
able to sustain their intense star formation unless there is sub-
stantial gas replenishment from the intergalactic medium. In-
triguingly, Muñoz et al. (2026, see also Simmonds et al. 2025)
find typical star-formation burst timescales of approximately 20
Myr, which is consistent with the gas free-fall times derived
by Pollock et al. (2026b) at z > 9 and with expectations from
supernova feedback (Furlanetto & Mirocha 2022). Complemen-
tary to this, Langeroodi & Hjorth (2024) find an apparent dearth
of bursting galaxies at z ∼4, coincident with the redshift where
most of the available IGM H i gas is expected to have accreted
onto galaxies (e.g., Heintz et al. 2022).
We argue that all these results point to a physical scenario
where excessive H i gas infall at z > 10 drive the rapid and
stochastic star formation, manifesting as an observed large vari-
ation in Σgas vs ΣSFR due to the approximately equal star forming
burst and gas depletion timescales at these redshifts. This sce-
nario was first proposed by Tacchella et al. (2020) based on sim-

5. Summary & Outlook
In this work, we presented a detailed characterization of a repre-
sentative sample of 25 galaxies brighter than MUV = −18 mag,
that have all been spectroscopically confirmed at z > 10 with
JWST. These galaxies represent the brightest sources detected
to date within the first 500 Myr of cosmic time. The compiled
galaxies are required to have sufficient S/N at rest-frame UV
wavelengths to properly constrain the Lyα damping wings (see
also the companion paper by Pollock et al. 2026b). The main fo-
cus of this work was to understand the impact of strong DLA ab-
sorption features, which are observed to be prevalent and promi-
nent at these redshifts, on the redshift estimates and the apparent
overabundance, UV-brightness, and prominent nebular emission
lines of galaxies at this epoch.
We modeled the Lyα damping wings of the sample galaxies
using three different iterations; one providing the best-fit local
H i gas column density, NHI, and neutral hydrogen fraction in the
IGM, xHI, at the redshift of the galaxies. The other were fixed to
model the IGM only, to assess the potential redshift bias a strong
DLA could introduce when not properly accounting for this in
the redshift-inferences, and the third was set to only include an
ISM component to determine the maximum upper bound on NHI
in the line of sight. We found that including an IGM-only model
to derive the redshift from the Lyα break could overestimate
the actual spectroscopic emission-line measured redshift up to
∆z = 0.4 (∆z = 0.14 on average). This redshift bias was even
more severe for photometric inferences that could overestimate
the redshift by ∆z > 1 in case of very prominent DLAs (see
also Witstok et al. 2025b), and on average by ∆z = 0.39. We fur-
ther modeled the Lyα optical depth as a function of redshift and
NHI, and demonstrated that the Lyα break redshift bias clearly in-
creases with both parameters. However, we found that the DLA
redshift bias had a marginal effect on the excess UV luminosity
function at z > 10.
To understand which physical conditions might instead drive
this excess UV-bright population of galaxies, we first inves-
tigated the connection between the dense local H i gas and
the absolute UV brightness, representing also the dark mat-
ter halo mass of each source. The former serves as the funda-
mental fuel of gas available for star formation before it turns
molecular. We found no apparent correlation between these
quantities, noting that low column-density absorbers are found
among the brightest and faintest of the sources considered
here. The same was evident for that connection between the
H i gas content and the ‘burstiness’ of the galaxies, here de-
fined as SFR10 Myr/SFR100 Myr. This quantity was derived from
the star-formation histories of the galaxies inferred via spectro-
photometric fitting using Bagpipes. However, we recovered a
significant relation between the galaxy burstiness and the pres-
ence of prominent rest-frame UV nebular emission lines in the
spectra (see also Roberts-Borsani et al. 2025). This supports the
hypothesis that these high-ionization lines mainly originate from
highly star-forming galaxies caught during a recent, short-lived
starburst episode. We also established a new, empirical calibra-
tion relating the short-lived SFR10 Myr to the C iii] λ1909 line lu-
minosity for galaxies at z > 10, which provides a more direct
measure of the burstiness when compared to the UV continuum
luminosity. This calibration is further applicable to the planning
of future JWST observations targeting galaxies at this epoch,
to estimate the required sensitivity to detect at least one of the
prominent rest-frame UV nebular lines for accurate redshift de-
terminations.
We then investigated the location of the sample galaxies on
an equivalent ‘Kennicutt-Schmidt’ plane, comparing their lo-
cal H i gas surface densities to their UV-derived SFR surface
densities (first introduced in Pollock et al. 2026b). We observed
a continuous distribution in the location of galaxies with the
most prominent recent starbursts, with SFR10 Myr/SFR100 Myr > 3
across a range of offsets from the local Kennicutt-Schmidt plane
indicating a large variation in the efficiency of star formation or
gas depletion timescales. Comparing the observations with pre-
dictions from hydrodynamical simulations (Lagos et al. 2025;
Shen et al. 2026) showed that the theoretical models were gen-
erally able to reproduce the range in local H i gas surface densi-
ties, though severely underestimating the SFR surface densities.
From the observations we surmised that these particular galaxies
at z > 10 likely cycle through a phase of rapid gas consumption
to star formation, requiring rapid replenishment of continuously
infalling IGM gas, such that this external process is the main
driver of the starburst episodes in the first galaxies. Finally, we
emphasized the observation that the sample galaxies showed a
continuous distribution in the UV-based SFR surface densities as
well, alleviating the proposed dichotomy in galaxy sizes as pre-
viously reported for these redshifts (e.g., Harikane et al. 2025;
Naidu et al. 2025).
In conclusion, our results suggested that enhanced star for-
mation efficiencies, short gas depletion timescales, and highly
stochastic starbursts are in combination the most likely physi-
cal origin of the larger population of more UV-bright galaxies at
z > 10. We note, however, that a subset of galaxies may have arti-
ficially boosted their apparent stellar UV luminosity due to dom-
inant nebular free-bound or two-photon continuum emission (see
also Cameron et al. 2024; Katz et al. 2024). This effect has been

Appendix A: Establishing C iii]λ1909 as a proxy for
bursty star formation
The
C iii] λλ1907, 1909
doublet
transition
(hence
simply
C iii] λ1909) is typically one of the most prominent emission
line features in high-redshift galaxies seen by JWST (e.g.,
Roberts-Borsani et al. 2024; Pollock et al. 2026b). At lower red-
shifts, z ∼2 −3, it has been shown to correlate with the Lyα
equivalent width (Shapley et al. 2003; Stark et al. 2014) and is
increasingly detected in more metal-poor, highly ionized galax-
ies (Erb et al. 2010; Stark et al. 2015). This makes C iii] λ1909
one of the primary lines to target at high redshifts, both to ac-
curately pin-point the redshift and to understand their physi-
cal properties in terms of metallicity, ionization, and short-lived
SFR, given also that Lyα is decreasingly detected at z ≳6
due to increasing neutral hydrogen fraction of the IGM (e.g.,
Miralda-Escudé 1998; McQuinn et al. 2007). Indeed, in the sam-
ple of galaxies at z > 10 studied here, the C iii] λ1909 line is the
most commonly detected.
To fully optimize the use of C iii] λ1909 at these early cos-
mic times, we need to understand its origin. Theoretically, the
upper state of the C iii] λ1909 transition is mainly populated by
photo-ionization of massive stars in sub-solar (Z ≲0.5 Z⊙) sys-
tems with young stellar populations. This implies that the to-
tal emission should trace the current star formation on short,
∼3 −10 Myr timescales (e.g., Erb et al. 2010; Stark et al. 2014,
2015). Establishing such a relation would serve as a much
more direct measure of the ‘bursty’ star formation, compar-
ing the C iii] λ1909 line luminosity to the UV continuum. This
has so far mainly been derived based on the star-formation his-
tory from more complex SED fitting (e.g., Endsley et al. 2025;
Cole et al. 2025; Kokorev et al. 2025; Carvajal-Bohorquez et al.
2025; Simmonds et al. 2025).
In Figure A.1, we compare the SED-derived SFRs over the
past 10 Myr for the sample galaxies studied here to the measured
C iii] λ1909 line luminosities, LCIII], or upper bounds. We also in-
clude the sample from Pollock et al. (2025) at z = 9 −10, which
has a more direct estimate of SFR10 Myr from the Hβ line lumi-
nosity at almost comparable redshifts. We find that these two
samples are well in agreement, and derive a log-linear relation of
log(LCIII]/erg s−1) = a×log(SFR10 Myr/M⊙yr−1)+b, with a best-
fit slope and intercept a = 0.69 ± 0.14 and b = 41.46 ± 0.14, re-
spectively. For comparison, we also create synthetic galaxy spec-
tra from stellar population models with metallicities in the range
Z = [0.05, 0.2] and ionization parameters log U = [−2, −1] us-
ing Bagpipes, at a fixed stellar mass of M⋆= 108 M⊙and
dust attenuation, AV = 0.1 mag and integrating the SFR over
the past 10 Myr. These are set to represent the average galaxy
population at these redshifts. From these mock spectra we mea-
sure the range of output C iii] λ1909 line luminosities, shown by
the blue-shaded region in Fig. A.1. This is in remarkable agree-
ment with the empirically derived relation, though suggesting a
slightly steeper slope near unity between LCIII] and SFR10 Myr.
These relations provide useful, empirical starting points for
estimating the required sensitivity to detect at least one rest-
frame UV nebular emission line at z > 10 with JWST/NIRSpec
for accurate redshift determinations given an approximate MUV
and an estimate of the burstiness of the galaxy based on the SED.
