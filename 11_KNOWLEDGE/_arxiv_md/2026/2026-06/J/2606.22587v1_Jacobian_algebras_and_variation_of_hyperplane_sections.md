GIOVANNA ILARDI, ABBAS NASROLLAH NEJAD, AND SAEED TAFAZOLIAN
Abstract. We study the variation in moduli of hyperplane sections of a hypersurface
V (f) ⊆Pn with at most isolated singularities. Using the Milnor algebra M(f), we give
an infinitesimal quotient criterion for the hyperplane-section map
Φ(f) : Pn∗99K M(d, n −1)
to be generically finite onto its image. The passage from the infinitesimal quotient to
the coarse moduli space is justified by a local GIT slice argument. Our approach gives
a Jacobian-algebraic extension of the Beauville–Patel–Riedl–Tseng theory from smooth
hypersurfaces to hypersurfaces with isolated singularities. In the smooth case it recovers
the Lefschetz criterion and, using recent weak Lefschetz results, gives generic finiteness
for n ≥3 in the range d ≥n + 2.
In the singular case a new obstruction appears:
a linear Jacobian syzygy, equivalently, for non-cones, a positive-dimensional projective
automorphism group. After this obstruction is excluded, maximal infinitesimal variation
is governed by the injectivity of the critical Lefschetz map ℓ: M(f)d−1 →M(f)d. We
apply the criterion to plane curves, surfaces in P3, and hypersurfaces admitting singular
hyperplane sections, obtaining new criteria involving nodal sections and an application
to the Schoen quintic threefold.

Question 1.1 (Variation of hyperplane sections). Let X = V (f) ⊆Pn be a degree d
hypersurface with at most isolated singularities. Determine algebraic and geometric con-
ditions on f under which the rational map Φ(f) is generically finite onto its image. More
generally, determine when Φ(f) has maximal rank.
Question 1.1 is closely related to recent work of Beauville and of Patel–Riedl–Tseng.
Beauville studied the case of a smooth cubic threefold X ⊆P4. In this case a hyperplane
section is a cubic surface, and Beauville proved that a general smooth cubic surface is
isomorphic to a hyperplane section of any smooth cubic threefold.
His proof reduces
the dominance of the corresponding moduli map to a Weak Lefschetz statement for the
Jacobian ring of X; see [1]. In the later work [2], Beauville formulated the broader notion
of maximal variation of a linear system and showed again that, for smooth hypersurfaces,
the relevant infinitesimal condition is governed by multiplication by a general linear form
on the Jacobian ring.
Patel–Riedl–Tseng studied the variation of linear slices of smooth hypersurfaces in pro-
jective space. They completely classified the plane curves whose line sections do not vary
maximally in moduli, and in higher dimension they proved that the family of hyperplane
sections of any smooth degree d hypersurface in Pn varies maximally when d ≥n + 3; see
[18]. Their approach is geometric and uses vector bundle methods, including generaliza-
tions of the classical Grauert–M¨ulich theorem.
The point of the present paper is to place these results in a Jacobian-algebraic frame-
work which also applies to hypersurfaces with isolated singularities. In the smooth case,
our criterion recovers the Lefschetz criterion appearing in the work of Beauville and, com-
bined with recent weak Lefschetz results, gives generic finiteness in the range d ≥n + 2.
For singular hypersurfaces, however, a new obstruction appears: the existence of a linear
Jacobian syzygy. We show that, for non-cones, this is equivalent to the existence of a
positive-dimensional projective automorphism group. Thus the failure of maximal infin-
itesimal variation is controlled by two explicit algebraic mechanisms: the automorphism
obstruction and the failure of the critical Lefschetz map. The passage from this infinitesi-
mal quotient to the coarse moduli space is justified by a local GIT argument, using Luna’s
´etale slice theorem. This perspective also leads to new criteria using singular hyperplane
sections.

Proposition 5.1. Let X = V (f) ⊆P3 be a cubic surface with isolated singularities. If X
is not a cone, then Φ(f) has maximal rank. In particular, it is not generically finite onto
its image. If X is a cone, then Φ(f) is constant.
Proof. Let H = V (ℓ) be a general plane. We choose ℓso that H ∈Uf and so that the
Lefschetz injectivity used below holds. After a projective linear change of coordinates, we
may assume that ℓ= x0, so H = V (x0). We keep the notation f for the transformed
equation. Set R = C[x1, x2, x3] and write h = h(0, x1, x2, x3) for h ∈S.
Since H ∈Uf, the plane cubic V (f) ⊆P2 is smooth. Thus Jf = (f1, f2, f3) is a complete
intersection of type (2, 2, 2). Hence A = R/Jf is Artinian Gorenstein with socle degree 3,
and dimC A3 = 1. The infinitesimal quotient differential of Φ(f) at H is represented by
ψ(f, x0) : C3 →R3/(Jf)3,
(b1, b2, b3) 7→[(b1x1 + b2x2 + b3x3)f0].

x0 : M(f)1 →M(f)2.
This contradicts [14, Proposition 3.1], which applies to cubic surfaces with isolated singu-

It follows that the infinitesimal quotient differential has rank 1 at a general point. Since
dim M(3, 2) = 1, the map Φ(f) has maximal rank.
Since dim P3∗= 3, it cannot be
generically finite onto its image.
Finally suppose that X is a cone and has isolated singularities. Then X is the cone over
a smooth plane cubic. For every plane H not passing through the vertex, projection from
the vertex identifies H projectively with the plane of the base cubic and sends X ∩H to
the base cubic. Hence all general hyperplane sections are projectively equivalent, and Φ(f)
is constant.

We now recover the smooth surface case. The case d = 3 is the cubic case above. For
d ≥4, the general criterion applies once the required Lefschetz multiplication map is known.
This is supplied by the weak Lefschetz theorem for height four complete intersections. The
following statement improves the general smooth high-degree corollary in the case n = 3,
and it also occurs in [2, Corollary 1, 2)].
Theorem 5.2. Let X = V (f) ⊆P3 be a smooth surface of degree d ≥3. If d = 3, then
Φ(f) has maximal rank but is not generically finite onto its image. If d ≥4, then
Φ(f) : P3∗99K M(d, 2)
is generically finite onto its image.
Proof. If d = 3, then X is not a cone, and the result follows from Proposition 5.1.

Since X is smooth, τ(X) = 0, so Corollary 2.12 gives
dim AutP3(X) = 0. Moreover, Jf = (f0, f1, f2, f3) is an Artinian complete intersection

algebra M(f) satisfies WLP in degree d. Since n = 3 and d ≥4, we are in the numerical

onto its image.

6. Singular hyperplane sections
In this section we explain how a suitable singular hyperplane section can be used to
verify the Lefschetz condition appearing in Theorem 3.3. Throughout, X = V (f) ⊆Pn is
a hypersurface of degree d ≥3 with at most isolated singularities, where n ≥3.
Let H′ = V (ℓ) ⊆Pn be a hyperplane such that H′ ∩Sing(X) = ∅, and assume that
the section W = X ∩H′ ⊆H′ ≃Pn−1 is singular.
Choose homogeneous coordinates
y1, . . . , yn on H′ and let g ∈R′ = C[y1, . . . , yn] be a defining equation of W. We write

g

These numbers do not depend on the chosen coordinates on H′. Since W is singular, one
has s(g) > 0. Moreover, r(g) = 0 if and only if W is a cone.
We shall use the following Lefschetz injectivity theorem of Dimca–Ilardi.
Theorem 6.1 ([9, Theorem 1.11]). Let X = V (f) ⊆Pn be a hypersurface of degree d, and
let H′ = V (ℓ) be a hyperplane such that H′ ∩Sing(X) = ∅. Assume that W = X ∩H′ is

by ℓinduces an injective map
ℓ: M(f)k →M(f)k+1
for every
k ≤min{d −3 + r(g), d −3 + s(g)}.
The point is that the hyperplane H′ in Theorem 6.1 is not a general hyperplane for
the map Φ(f), since X ∩H′ is singular. Nevertheless, it can still be used to prove the
Lefschetz condition for a general hyperplane: injectivity of a fixed multiplication map is
an open condition on ℓ∈S1. Thus, if one special linear form ℓgives an injective map
M(f)d−1 →M(f)d, then multiplication by a general linear form is also injective in this
degree.
Theorem 6.2. Let X = V (f) ⊆Pn be a hypersurface of degree d, with n, d ≥3 and

(1) dim AutPn(X) = 0;
(2) r(g) = indeg(Syz(Jg)) > 1;
