ABSTRACT
We present resolutions as to the nature of six speculative candidate companions proposed in the final data release of the
Pan-Pacific Planet Search, a 6-year radial-velocity survey of 164 southern evolved stars using the now-decommissioned UCLES
spectrograph on the 3.9m Anglo-Australian Telescope. New radial-velocity observations, TESS asteroseismology, and Hipparcos-
Gaia astrometry are incorporated to refine the companion and host-star parameters. We confirm that HD 126105b is a giant
planet (𝑃= 524.0±2.9 d, 𝑚sin 𝑖= 1.67+0.19
−0.17 𝑀J), and that HD 205577B is a massive, eccentric brown dwarf (𝑃∼11.2 yr,
𝑚= 77+11
−9 𝑀J, 𝑒= 0.68). HD 115066B and HD 121156B are low-mass stellar companions, while HD 114899 and HD 159743
are shown to be unadorned by any detectable companions whatsoever. This demonstrates the utility of astrometric information
to help overcome the temporal limitations of incomplete radial-velocity data sets and elucidate the true nature of suspected
companion bodies.

1 INTRODUCTION
While many thousands of planets have been confirmed orbiting other
stars, the vast majority of them orbit main-sequence stars. Only 7%
of confirmed exoplanets orbit evolved stars, defined as log 𝑔< 4.01.
The detailed demographic properties of planets orbiting evolved stars
are thus far less constrained than those orbiting main-sequence stars,

The search for planets orbiting evolved stars was borne out of the
desire to understand the properties and occurrence of planets orbiting
stars more massive than our Sun. The early radial-velocity planet

narrow spectral lines: the types of spectra characteristic of main-

early F) were cast aside as lacking sufficient spectral information
to measure radial velocities useful for exoplanet detection. Their
spectral lines are simply too sparse and the stars rotate too quickly to
achieve the ∼3-5 m s−1 precision necessary.
Stellar astrophysics, though sometimes cruel and uncaring, can
on occasion proffer a ray of hope to the dedicated astronomer. The
solution all along is to wait. When earlier-type stars, of masses 1.5-
3 𝑀⊙, evolve off the main sequence, their spectroscopic intransigence
gives way to an abundance of narrow lines more suited for precise
radial-velocity measurements. This is a result of their atmospheres
expanding and cooling as they evolve: both effects are favourable as
they allow neutral metal lines to form without the ∼50-100 km s−1
rotational broadening exhibited on the main sequence. So it goes
that in the mid-2000s, several pioneering campaigns2 began to take
advantage of this rare gift from physics to explore the population of
planets around the “retired A stars” (e.g. Sato et al. 2005; Johnson
et al. 2006; Wittenmyer et al. 2011; Jones et al. 2011a; Reffert et al.
2015; Ottoni et al. 2022).
The legacy radial-velocity sample of evolved stars, combined with
a growing sample from the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite
(TESS) including main-sequence intermediate-mass stars (e.g. Zhou
et al. 2019; Grunblatt et al. 2019; Saunders et al. 2022), has permitted
some preliminary demographic analysis of the population of planets
as a function of host-star mass (e.g. Jones et al. 2016; Wittenmyer
et al. 2020a; Wolthoff et al. 2022; Teng et al. 2023). In brief, the
occurrence, masses, and multiplicity of planetary systems hosted
by these stars is positively correlated with stellar metallicity. The
planet occurrence rate appears to peak for ∼1.7 𝑀⊙hosts, falling off
precipitously at higher stellar masses.
Among the ∼400 planets confirmed to orbit these evolved stars,
there are of course some intriguing individual systems. HD 76920b
was found to be the most eccentric planet to orbit an evolved
star (Wittenmyer et al. 2017b; Bergmann et al. 2021), with 𝑒=
0.8782±0.0025. Hon et al. (2023) revealed an “impossible” close-in
planet orbiting the core helium-burning giant 8 UMi that appears
to have escaped engulfment during the star’s first ascent onto the
red giant branch. Longer-baseline radial-velocity observations have
revealed planet pairs in low-order mean-motion resonances, such as
HD 200964 and 24 Sextantis (Johnson et al. 2011; Wittenmyer et al.
2012), HD 33844 (Wittenmyer et al. 2016b), and 7 CMa (Luque et al.
2019). Recently, Teng et al. (2022) reported a unique compact triple-
giant-planet system orbiting HD 184010, where the period ratios of
neighbouring planet pairs are less than 2:1. While it may appear
that some planets are more equal than others, for the purposes of
understanding the population, we must consider all planets of equal
value, even the “boring ones.” Getting it right is key. For example,
Wittenmyer et al. (2017a) considered and rejected a planet candi-
date orbiting HD 29399; it was later confirmed as a genuine planet
with further data and a more rigorous stellar activity analysis by
Pezzotti et al. (2022). The 1.57 𝑀J planet moving on an 893-day,
nearly-circular orbit is fairly typical of the planets known to orbit
these evolved stars, but that makes it no less important for adding to
our understanding of the overall population.
Wittenmyer et al. (2020a) presented the final data release for the
Pan-Pacific Planet Search (Wittenmyer et al. 2011), a collaboration
between Australia, China, and the US, which obtained precise radial

In this paper, we report the discovery and confirmation of four
multifarious companions orbiting evolved stars: the giant planet
HD 126105b, the high-mass brown dwarf HD 205577B, and the stel-
lar companions HD 115066B and HD 121156B. The latter two ob-

uncertain, incomplete orbits in Bluhm et al. (2016). HD 205577B is
a rare highly eccentric brown dwarf in a ∼11 yr orbit, with a derived

these objects, and we give our results and conclusions in Section 5.
2 OBSERVATIONS AND DATA REDUCTION
All six stars considered here were observed with AAT/UCLES as part
of the Pan-Pacific Planet Search. The radial velocity data for these and
all survey stars are given in Wittenmyer et al. (2020a) and reprised
here in the Appendix for convenience (Tables A1-A). Bluhm et al.
(2016) also presented FEROS and CHIRON data from the EXPRESS
survey (Jones et al. 2011b) for HD 115066 and HD 121156, which
have been included in the fitting process as described in Section 4.
2.1 SONG
We obtained 13 radial velocity measurements of HD 126105 with
the robotic 1m Hertzsprung SONG telescope located at the Teide
Observatory in Tenerife (Grundahl et al. 2007, 2017; Andersen et al.
2019). Light from the telescope is guided to the slit-based high-
resolution echelle spectrograph using a coudé path. Slit number 6 was
used which provides a resolution of 𝑅= 90, 000. HD 126105 was
observed between 2019 Dec 13 and 2022 July 14 with an exposure
time of 40 minutes. Each spectrum covers 440 – 690 nm and the
stellar light passes through a heated iodine cell for precise wavelength
calibration. The radial velocities were extracted from the spectra
using the pyodine software described by Heeren et al. (2023).
2.2 MINERVA-Australis
We carried out supplemental spectroscopic observations using the
Minerva-Australis facility (Addison et al. 2019; Wittenmyer et al.
2022; Clark et al. 2023). Minerva-Australis consists of an array
of four independently operated 0.7 m CDK700 telescopes situated at
the Mount Kent Observatory in Queensland, Australia (Addison et al.
2019). Each telescope simultaneously feeds stellar light via fibre optic
cables to a single KiwiSpec R4-100 high-resolution (𝑅= 80, 000)
spectrograph (Barnes et al. 2012) with wavelength coverage from
480 to 620 nm. Wavelength calibration is achieved via archival Th-
Ar wavelength solution. The wavelength solution is corrected for

3 STELLAR PROPERTIES
Complete stellar parameters for the six stars considered in this
work are given in Table 1. We present updated stellar parameters
for HD 126105 incorporating new MINERVA-Australis spectra and
Gaia DR3 information. For HD 115066 and HD 205577, we have
derived stellar parameters from a new asteroseismic analysis of
high-precision photometry from NASA’s Transiting Exoplanet Sur-
vey Satellite (TESS) (Ricker et al. 2015).
3.1 Spectroscopic and photometric analysis of HD 126105
For HD 126105, the eight spectra with the highest SNRs were con-
volved into a single spectrum with a resulting SNR of approximately
133. The spectrum was binned every 0.02 Angstroms using the bin-
ningx0dt function found within PyAstronomy 3 to reduce the influ-
ence of any outliers on the data fitting (Czesla et al. 2019). Before
convolving the spectrum, all spectra were first corrected for their
barycentric velocity at the time of observation and then corrected for
radial velocities measured from a cross correlation with the mean
spectrum of the target. Then, utilising iSpec (Blanco-Cuaresma et al.
2014; Blanco-Cuaresma 2019), the convolved spectrum was fitted
with a model for effective temperature (𝑇eff), surface gravity (log 𝑔),
and metallicity ([M/H]). MARCS (Gustafsson et al. 2008) was used
as the atmospheric model when performing standard iSpec proce-
dures.
The astroARIADNE package was used (Vines & Jenkins 2022) to
verify 𝑇eff, log 𝑔, and [M/H], while also extracting the radius (𝑅★),
luminosity (𝐿★), and mass (𝑀★) of HD 126105. AstroARIADNE is
a python package that utilizes photometric data from Gaia Data Re-
lease 3 (DR3) to fit stellar atmospheric models with nested sampling
algorithms (Skilling 2004; Skilling 2006). AstroARIADNE also per-
forms Bayesian model averaging (BMA) to average posterior samples
from multiple stellar atmosphere models while correcting for any bi-
ases found within each model. Four models: Phoenix (Husser et al.
2013), BT-Settl (Allard et al. 2012), Kurucz (Kurucz 1993), and
Castelli & Kurucz (Castelli & Kurucz 2004) were used in the BMA
processes for HD 126105. All fitted parameters: 𝑇eff, log 𝑔, [M/H],
𝑅★, 𝐿★, and 𝑀★, are listed in Table 1.

3.2 Asteroseismic analysis of HD 205577
Solar-like oscillations can be used to constrain the properties of red
giants, such as mass, radius and age (see reviews by Chaplin &
Miglio 2013; García & Ballot 2019; Jackiewicz 2021). To measure
oscillations in HD 205577, we analyzed the three available sectors
of TESS data (Sectors 1, 28, and 94). We created a light curve
from the TESS target pixel files using the lightkurve package
(Lightkurve Collaboration et al. 2018) and high-pass filtered the
light curve to remove slow variations. The resulting light curve is
shown in Fig. 1, and oscillations with a period of about 0.3 d are
visible. This is confirmed by the Generalised Lomb-Scargle (GLS)
power spectrum of the light curve (Fig. 2), which shows an excess
centred at 𝜈max ≃40 𝜇Hz from oscillations, as well as a rising level
of power towards low frequencies that is due to granulation on the
stellar surface.

4.2 Astrometric Evidence
Three of our targets – namely HD 115066, HD 121156, and
HD 205577 – show strong evidence for massive, long-period com-
panions in our RV observations. We therefore explore the possibility
of extracting further information on the nature of the companions to
our targets using astrometry. All six of our target stars are present in
both the Hipparcos (Perryman et al. 1997; van Leeuwen 2007) and
Gaia (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2016, 2023), allowing us to make use
of cross-calibrated Hipparcos-Gaia astrometry to explore long-term
variations in the tangential velocities of our targets, complement-
ing our RV observations. We query the Gaia DR3 version of the
Hipparcos-Gaia Catalog of Accelerations (HGCA; Brandt 2021) for
each of our targets. Four of these stars, HD 115066 (HIP 64647),
HD 121156 (HIP 67890), HD 126105 (HIP 70382), and HD 205577

HGCA, the goodness-of-fit values for the constant proper motion
hypothesis for these three giant stars are 𝜒2 = 4659, 5416, 3068,
and 309 respectively, which all lie far above the 𝜒2 = 11.8 crite-
rion that equates to a 0.3% Gaussian false alarm rate (Brandt 2021).
In contrast, HD 114899 and HD 159743 have insignificant values of
𝜒2 = 0.5 and 0.6 respectively, which accords with their absence of
coherent RV variability (Section 4.4).

For HD 126105, the magnitude of the astrometric variability is
somewhat surprising given the comparatively small RV signal. The
RV solution described in the preceding section has a semi-amplitude
𝐾of 37.2+2.8
−2.6 m s−1, whereas in the HGCA the net difference be-
tween the Gaia and time-averaged Hipparcos-Gaia proper motion is
∼2.19 mas yr−1, equivalent to a net change in tangential velocity of
Δv ≈850 m s−1 at the distance of the star. If the astrometric signal
was generated by HD 126105 b, this might imply that the true mass
might be much larger than the RV minimum mass.
However, several lines of evidence suggest that the astrometric
acceleration has a separate origin. The Gaia DR3-based Hipparcos-
Gaia astrometry has low sensitivity to orbits with periods shorter
than the 1038 day observing duration of Gaia DR3, and notably
has asymptotes in sensitivity at the harmonics of this duration.
The 524±2.9 d orbital period of HD 126105 b lies at almost ex-
actly the first harmonic of the Gaia DR3 duration (i.e. 1038 / 2 =
519 days), meaning that in order to generate the observed proper
motion anomaly the true mass would therefore have to be extremely
large, probably in the stellar regime. In this case we would expect
to observe an excess in the Gaia Renormalised Unit Weight Error
(RUWE), which is highly sensitive to binaries with similar orbital
periods (e.g. Belokurov et al. 2020; Castro-Ginard et al. 2024). How-
ever, the Gaia DR3 RUWE is only 1.03, where RUWE = 1 suggests an
ideal fit to the astrometry (Lindegren et al. 2021), suggesting that the
astrometric motion of HD 126105 was effectively linear during Gaia
observations. We might also expect to observe a significant difference
between the Gaia DR2 and DR3 proper motions since these have dif-
ferent timespans, however the two sets of parameters are identical at
the 1𝜎level (Δ𝜇𝛼, Δ𝜇𝛿= [+0.14±0.10, +0.04±0.08] mas yr−1). All
of this suggests that the proper motion of HD 126105 does not vary
at a timescale consistent with the orbital period of HD 126105 b.
Instead, the astrometric acceleration of HD 126105 appears to re-
flect a second long-period companion in the system, one that is
not presently evident in the RVs. Given the large velocity anomaly
this may be a stellar companion, reminiscent of systems such as
HD 59686 (Ortiz et al. 2016; Trifonov et al. 2018). However, since
we do not observe any significant RV acceleration corresponding to a
second companion on a wider orbit than HD 126105 b, this remains
hypothetical. To explain the apparent absence of a significant RV
acceleration it is for instance possible that the RVs may cover an in-
opportune orbital phase, or that the orbit for the hypothetical second
companion may be observed near to pole-on.
In contrast, the three remaining targets, HD 115066 (HIP 64647),
HD 121156 (HIP 67890), and HD 205577 (HIP 106699), simultane-
ously demonstrate clear evidence for massive long-period compan-
ions in both the RVs and astrometry. We therefore attribute the highly
significant astrometric accelerations to the same massive long-period
companions evident in the RVs, and perform joint fits to both RVs
and astrometry.
4.3 Radial Velocity + Astrometric Joint Fits
For HD 115066, HD 121156, and HD 205577, we simultaneously
model our RV data with the available Hipparcos-Gaia astrometry.
The addition of astrometric information allows us to constrain the
orbital inclinations (𝑖) for their companions, hence resolving the RV
sin𝑖degeneracy and providing their true masses. For this purpose
we use a model based on the one developed for Venner et al. (2021)
with minimal modifications. Briefly, this model allows us to jointly
fit the RVs and Hipparcos-Gaia astrometry for each system using a
two-body Keplerian model, with the following variable parameters:
the parallax (𝜛) and stellar mass (𝑀⊙) for the visible primary star
in each system, with Gaussian priors derived from Table 1; seven
variables to describe the companion and its orbit, namely the true
companion mass (𝑚), orbital period (𝑃), eccentricity and argument of
periastron (𝑒and 𝜔, reparameterized as √𝑒sin 𝜔and √𝑒cos 𝜔), the
epoch of periastron (𝑇P), the orbital inclination (𝑖), and the longitude
of ascending node (Ω); two proper motion parameters corresponding
to the constant motion of the system barycenter (𝜇𝛼,bary, 𝜇𝛿,bary);
and finally an offset and jitter parameter for each RV dataset for
each relevant star. All parameters are sampled uniformly within the
physical parameter space except for 𝑃and 𝑚, which are sampled
log-uniformly, and 𝑖, which is sampled uniformly in sin𝑖.
Since the proper motion data used in this model are time-averaged
over the observing baselines of both Hipparcos and Gaia, we use the
htof package (Brandt et al. 2021) to construct the corresponding
observing cadences (actual in case of Hipparcos; simulated in case
of Gaia), and resample the model over the resulting set of epochs.
While this approach worked well for HD 115066 and HD 121156,
limitations and incompleteness of the observational data for
HD 205577 meant that the same model failed to achieve a unique
orbital solution for its companion. As a result, we attempted to in-
corporate additional information from the Gaia RVs, calibrating our
Minerva-Australis observations into the same RV system. We detail
the more involved analysis developed for this system and the corre-
sponding parameters derived for the companion in Section 4.3.3.

4.3.3 HD 205577
Wittenmyer et al. (2020b) reported 8 UCLES RV observations for
HD 205577 spanning 4.0 years (2009 - 2013), which demonstrate
a long-term acceleration of the stellar RV with amplitude above
>300 m s−1. A speculative orbital solution was proposed on the
basis of these observations, with 𝑃= 1686 d, 𝐾= 613 m s−1, and
𝑒= 0.97. However, this would entail an orbital period longer than the
RV duration and a rapid periastron passage lying outside of existing
observations, and hence requires validation.
We have acquired 61 new RV observations with Minerva-
Australis spanning 3.3 years (2021 - 2024) with a median uncer-
tainty of 8.5 m s−1, continuing on approximately 8 years after the
last UCLES observation. As with the UCLES data, the RV variability
observed by Minerva-Australis can mainly be described by a long-
term acceleration. The phase coverage for the companion’s orbit is
therefore evidently incomplete, and the same joint RV + astrome-
try model used in the preceding sections fails to converge given the
available data. This necessitates a more involved analysis to retrieve
the properties of the companion in the system.
We begin by first placing our new Minerva-Australis RVs on a
consistent absolute reference frame. Minerva-Australis underwent
an instrument intervention in December 2022, which introduced an
offset in the spectrograph RV zero-point. We use observations of the
classical RV standard HD 10700 (𝜏Ceti) to calibrate for the effects of
this offset. Comparing the medians of the pre- and post-intervention
RVs, we calculate a (Minerva-Apost −Minerva-Apre) RV offset of
−47 ± 9 m s−1 for HD 10700.
In the left panel of Figure 8 we show the Minerva-Australis RVs,
normalized to the post-intervention RV system. It can be seen that
the first three seasons of observations are consistent with a linear
acceleration that crosses the instrument intervention, visualised with
a fiducial 70 m s−1 yr−1 acceleration determined from a least-squares
fit to these three seasons of RVs. However, the most recent data from
the 2024 season diverge from a purely linear solution. While the
magnitude of change is modest, this indicates that Minerva-Australis
observations have captured a non-negligible fraction of the orbit of
the companion.
As UCLES RVs are relative (instead of absolute), we cannot di-
rectly place these into the same reference frame as the Minerva-
Australis RVs. However, we can marshal absolute RVs measured by
Gaia to greatly extend our absolute RV baseline. both Gaia DR2
and Gaia DR3 have reported time-averaged mean RVs measured
by the Gaia Radial Velocity Spectrometer. This allows us to ex-
tend the absolute RV baseline backwards to ∼2015, facilitating a
stronger comparison with the preceding UCLES RVs. To do this,
we must first convert between the Gaia and Minerva-Australis
RV reference frames. For this purpose we again turn to observa-
tions of HD 10700. The median post-intervention Minerva-Australis
RV of HD 10700 is −17.380 ± 0.008 km s−1, which compares to
−16.597 ± 0.0002 km s−1 in the Gaia reference frame (Soubiran
et al. 2018).4 Hence the (Minerva-A −Gaia) systematic RV offset
is calibrated to -0.783±0.008 km s−1.

of 43.01 km s−1 in the Gaia reference frame. The actual mean RV

5 DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS
We have followed up on six candidate companions that were tenta-
tively proposed in Wittenmyer et al. (2020a) as part of the program’s
final data release. Of these, we now confirm one giant planet, one
high-mass brown dwarf, two stellar-mass companions, and we show
the remaining two candidate signals to be spurious. The companions
orbiting HD 115066 and HD 121156 were presented in Bluhm et al.
(2016) as likely stellar-mass objects moving on long, incomplete or-
bits. Here we have analysed new RV data combined with Hipparcos-
Gaia astrometric accelerations to confirm the nature of these objects
as being low-mass stars. Any discussion of radial-velocity detected
companions must of course pay heed to the possibility of stellar ac-
tivity. The amplitudes of the signals we have confirmed here (cf.

The eccentricities of giant planets, brown dwarfs and low-mass
stars will shed light on whether systems with different mass ratios
resemble each other, offering a unique way to trace their evolution
histories. Recent work from Gan et al. (2025) looked into the ec-
centricity distributions of transiting long-period (𝑃> 10 days or
0.1 ≤𝑎≤1.5 AU) giant planets, brown dwarfs and low-mass stars.
The authors found that giant planets and brown dwarfs exhibit simi-
lar eccentricity distributions, preferring low-eccentricity orbits with
a long tail toward high eccentricities. The low-mass stars, instead,

tend to have moderately eccentric orbits with a peak at 0.3, a feature
also found in massive stellar binaries (Duquennoy & Mayor 1991;
Wu et al. 2025). However, wide-orbit companions seem to have dif-
ferent behaviors. Through the direct imaging method, several studies
found that cold (5 ≤𝑎≤100 AU) Jupiters and brown dwarfs have
significantly different eccentricity distributions (Bowler et al. 2020;
Do Ó et al. 2023; Nagpal et al. 2023). To explain such a discrep-
ancy, Gan et al. (2025) proposed that giant planets and brown dwarfs
probably form at exterior orbits through different channels, which
result in different eccentricities, but then undergo an analogous evo-
lution pathway, leading to similar eccentricity distributions of those
inner systems. Therefore, enlarging the sample of systems with semi-
major axis in-between (1.5 ≤𝑎≤5 AU), a parameter region that
astrometry and RV are sensitive to, and investigating their eccen-
tricity distributions will complement the picture, providing a more
comprehensive insight.
