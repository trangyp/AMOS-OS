TRANSVERSAL UNFOLDINGS OF DERIVED FOLIATIONS
MAURÍCIO CORRÊA
ABSTRACT. We develop a CE-presented, homotopically invariant formalism for transversal unfoldings
of relative derived foliations. An unfolding is regarded as an integrable transverse deformation: the
parameter directions are lifted to flat infinitesimal symmetries of the relative derived foliation. The
infinitesimal object controlling such liftings, which we call the transverse controller, is the homotopy
quotient of basic weight-zero graded-mixed derivations of the derived de Rham algebra by tangent
inner derivations. In Chevalley–Eilenberg models this is represented by basic first-order Lie derivations
modulo inner derivations, and in the non-effective case by a crossed module retaining central isotropy.
For strict affine CE models we compute the derived space of transversal unfoldings. In the effective case
its connected components are classified by flat splittings of the transverse controller, and the basic part
reconstructs the full absolute algebroid without auxiliary generation hypotheses. We then pass to the
intrinsic graded-mixed controller, formulate presentation independence through derived splitting spaces,
and give a conditional global descent theorem under explicit CE-presentability and controller-descent
hypotheses. The construction also identifies the Atiyah–Kodaira–Spencer class, the curvature obstruction,
the Maurer–Cartan deformation complex, split normal forms, the representable leaf-space interpretation,
and Gauss–Manin transport for coherently Cartan-linearised crystals. The examples recover the classical,
Lie-algebroid, logarithmic, shifted Poisson, and representable leaf-space cases.
1. INTRODUCTION
Derived foliations were introduced by Toën and Vezzosi as graded mixed de Rham objects
which enhance classical, possibly singular, foliations by homotopical data [25]. Their perspective is
consonant with a guiding principle of derived algebraic geometry: singular geometric structures are
often better governed after one retains the derived structures suppressed by their classical truncations
[9, 17, 2, 23]. Thus a singular foliation is not treated here merely as a distribution which fails to be
locally free or involutive in an elementary sense. Rather, it may be regarded as the truncation of
a derived object whose higher homotopical structures record integrability, formal geometry, and
cohomological information.
The basic language is that of graded mixed commutative differential graded algebras, as developed
and used systematically in the definition of derived foliations [25, Chapters 1–2]. If X = Spec A, the
derived de Rham algebra DR(A/k) is the graded mixed cdga whose underlying graded algebra
is SymA(LA/k[1]). A derived foliation F on X is a graded mixed cdga DR(F), equipped with a
morphism
DR(A/k) −→DR(F),
whose underlying graded cdga is quasi-isomorphic to SymA(LF[1]) for a cotangent complex LF.
The weight-one piece plays the role of the leafwise cotangent complex, and the morphism from
DR(A/k) gives the cotangent anchor
LA/k −→LF.
For a smooth classical foliation this is the quotient map from ordinary one-forms to leafwise one-
forms. In the derived situation the mixed differential is the leafwise de Rham differential together
with all higher homotopies expressing its multiplicativity, its compatibility with the ambient de
Rham differential, and the equation that its square vanishes.
This formalism supplies the geometric background for the present paper. Derived foliations
possess a foliated de Rham theory, obtained by Tate realisation of their graded mixed de Rham
algebra, and a Hodge filtration whose graded pieces are described by foliated cohomology with
coefficients in powers of the normal complex [25, Sections 3.2–3.3]. For almost perfect derived folia-
tions on derived Artin stacks locally of finite presentation, Toën–Vezzosi prove formal integrability
around points; in particular one obtains formal leaves and formal leaf spaces in that setting [25,
Section 2.3]. This distinction is relevant because not every classical singular distribution admits
such a derived enhancement. In the holomorphic setting, quasi-smooth rigid derived foliations
can often be integrated analytically; the classical singular Frobenius theorem of Malgrange is an

The present work develops a CE-presented and homotopically invariant formalism for the cor-
responding derived theory of unfoldings. An unfolding should be distinguished from an arbitrary
deformation of the relative foliation. A deformation records how the leafwise structure changes with
the parameter. An unfolding consists of the additional datum that the parameter direction itself lifts
to the total space and acts by infinitesimal transverse symmetries; the lifted action is required to be
integrable, in the Frobenius–flatness sense. Classically, in Suwa’s theory [21], if a codimension-one
foliation is locally defined by an integrable form ω, then an infinitesimal relative deformation has
the form
ωε = ω + εη,
whereas an unfolding has an additional transverse component eωε = ω + εη + h dε. The term h dε is
not an auxiliary artefact: it is the local expression of a lift of the parameter direction to the total space.
The integrability equation for eωε then says that these lifted parameter directions act by transverse
infinitesimal symmetries, with closed brackets and zero curvature. In this sense, an unfolding is an
integrable transverse deformation: it is a transverse deformation together with a flat lift of the parameter
directions. In the Chevalley–Eilenberg model used below, the same condition becomes a flat splitting
of the transverse controller; in the split normal form it becomes the Maurer–Cartan equation for
the added transverse Lie-derivative term. The classification of transversal unfoldings for singular
holomorphic foliations and Lie algebroids is given in [7, 20, 6] in terms of flat connections on a Lie
algebroid over the base, with particular inspiration from Quallbrunn’s treatment of the holomorphic
foliation case [20]. Here we develop the corresponding theory for derived foliations: Lie-algebroid
sheaves are replaced by graded mixed de Rham algebras, and ordinary quotient algebroids are
replaced by homotopy quotient controllers when isotropy is present.

The anchor sequence of the controller carries an Atiyah–Kodaira–Spencer class; its vanishing is
equivalent to the existence of a transverse splitting, and the curvature of a chosen splitting is
the obstruction to flatness. Around a flat splitting, infinitesimal deformations are governed by a
Maurer–Cartan dg-Lie algebra of base forms with values in the vertical kernel of the controller. In
a split normal form the absolute mixed differential is obtained from the relative mixed differential
by adding the base de Rham differential and a Lie-derivative term, and the condition that the
total mixed differential square to zero is the Maurer–Cartan curvature equation. If the relative
derived foliation has a representable leaf space, the leaf-space-basic unfoldings are flat Ehresmann
connections on that leaf-space fibration. For a coherently Cartan-linearised crystal, the foliated
de Rham cohomology carries an induced Gauss–Manin connection in the derived sense; since
the transverse operators have weight zero in this setting, the induced connection preserves the
corresponding Cartan-linearised weight/Hodge filtration, and the foliated Chern character of a
perfect Cartan-linearised crystal is horizontal. These characteristic-class statements are not additional
data; they follow from the classification theorem. After a transversal unfolding is identified with a
flat splitting of the transverse controller, every coherently Cartan-linearised coefficient system carries
Gauss–Manin transport, and its foliated Chern character is horizontal along the base. Equivalently,
characteristic classes are transported by the flat transverse splitting, and their horizontality is the
corresponding cohomological form of the Maurer–Cartan equation. We prove these consequences
in Proposition 13.2, Corollary 13.5, Proposition 12.2, Proposition 15.3, Theorem 16.3, Theorem 10.6,
Proposition 17.2, and Proposition 17.3, respectively.

a vector field on the base acts by the adjoint representation on the relative dg-Lie algebroid. The
Lie–Rinehart identity shows that this adjoint action is a first-order Lie derivation with basic symbol.

2. DERIVED FOLIATIONS AND CE PRESENTATIONS
This section fixes the conventions on derived foliations, graded mixed algebras and CE presen-
tations. The definitions are those of Toën–Vezzosi [25]; the use of cotangent complexes is Illusie’s
convention [10]; and the Lie–Rinehart language for algebroids follows the classical algebraic model
of Rinehart and its dg form as developed by Nuiten [19, 15]. The ground field k has characteristic
zero. Strict CE computations are made in cofibrant strictly perfect models.
2.1. Graded mixed de Rham algebras. Throughout the paper, a derivation of a graded mixed cdga
means a weight-zero derivation preserving the grading and commuting with the mixed structure,
unless another weight is explicitly indicated. This convention will be used throughout: transverse
symmetries of a derived foliation preserve the weight grading, and the higher-weight derivations
belong instead to the internal deformation complex of the graded mixed algebra.

Section 1.2], a graded mixed complex is a graded complex E = L

18. EXAMPLES AND ILLUSTRATIONS
The examples in this section exhibit the geometric content of the classification theorem in its
standard models. In the classical and logarithmic cases the controller is a familiar quotient of
projectable vector fields; in the representable leaf-space case it is the tangent algebroid of the leaf-
space fibration; in derived pullback and formal-leaf situations it is the homotopical replacement
for that tangent object; and for Cartan-linearised crystals it is the source of Gauss–Manin transport.
Thus the same flat transverse splitting governs the examples from classical transverse symmetries to
filtered foliated cohomology.

18.2. Tautological derived foliations. Let X = Spec A. Toën–Vezzosi distinguish two tautological
objects: the pointwise foliation and the de Rham foliation. The pointwise foliation is obtained by the
augmentation DR(A/k) →A; the de Rham foliation is obtained from the identity of DR(A/k). They

They are not direct applications of the strict theorem. Following the tautological examples in
[25, Section 2.1], they serve as calibration cases for the definition of the controller. In the pointwise
foliation there is no leafwise direction and every ambient direction is transverse. In the de Rham
foliation every ambient direction is leafwise and there is no transverse quotient. Any intrinsic
definition of Uder(F/S) must reproduce these two limiting cases.
18.3. Relative de Rham foliations induced by a morphism. Let f: X = Spec A →Y = Spec B be a

18.6. Derived pullbacks of smooth foliations. Let G be a smooth classical foliation on a smooth
scheme Y, and let f: X →Y be any morphism. The functoriality of derived foliations [25, Section 2.1]

This example illustrates that a controller defined only from a singular classical distribution is
insufficient. The functorial object is the graded mixed algebra DR(f∗G), and the transverse controller
must be computed from its basic graded-mixed derivations.
18.7. Shifted Poisson structures and derived symplectic foliations. The shifted Poisson example
must be read in the same homotopical sense as the rest of the paper. A shifted Poisson structure
is naturally encoded by a Hamiltonian L∞-algebroid, or equivalently by a suitable Pn+1-algebraic
structure, rather than by a strict dg-Lie algebroid without choices. Assume therefore that the given
shifted Poisson structure on X is represented by a strict Hamiltonian L∞- or CE presentation gπ,
whose underlying complex is the shifted cotangent complex and whose anchor is induced by the
Poisson tensor. The associated Chevalley–Eilenberg graded mixed algebra then defines the derived
symplectic foliation to which the preceding theory applies.
For a family X →S, a transversal unfolding of this Hamiltonian presentation is a flat deformation
of the derived symplectic foliation along the base. Its transverse controller is the basic-derivation
controller of gπ, interpreted as a crossed controller when the Hamiltonian presentation has isotropy.
Thus the classification theorem applies to strict Hamiltonian presentations and is invariant under
replacement by an equivalent presentation through the intrinsic graded-mixed controller.
In the non-degenerate shifted symplectic case the Hamiltonian foliation is the de Rham foliation.
The controller then reduces to the familiar object controlling flat Ehresmann transport of the family.
In degenerate shifted Poisson situations the same formula records the transverse symmetries of the
derived symplectic leaves, including higher stabilisers coming from the Hamiltonian L∞-structure.

18.9. Formal leaves and formal leaf spaces. Almost perfect derived foliations on derived Artin

interpreted as a flat infinitesimal transport of the formal leaf space along the base. If the formal leaf
space is not represented by a scheme or analytic space, the controller Uder(F/S) is the infinitesimal
replacement for the tangent object of that missing leaf space.
