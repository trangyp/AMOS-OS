THE INTEGRAL CLOSEDNESS OF LATTICE SIMPLICES WITH
LARGE LATTICE LENGTH
LEI SONG, HUANQI WEN AND ZHIXIAN ZHU
Abstract. We prove that every n-dimensional lattice simplex P whose lattice
length L(P) ≥n−1 is integrally closed. As an application, we obtain a simple
criterion for the projective normality of ample line bundles on Q-factorial toric
Fano varieties with Picard number one. We further obtain a refinement of this
result in terms of the invariant ΓP introduced in [GZ22].
1. Introduction
Let M be a free Z-module of rank n ≥1 and let MR = M ⊗Z R be the associated
n-dimensional vector space. A polytope P ⊆MR is defined as the convex hull of a
finite set {u0, . . . , um} ⊆MR, denoted by P = Conv(u0, · · · , um). After removing
redundant elements, we may assume that u0, . . . , um are the vertices of P. We call
P a lattice polytope if ui ∈M for all i. Throughout this paper, all polytopes are
assumed to be full-dimensional.
Let P, P1, P2 be polytopes in MR and r ∈R. The Minkowski sum P1 + P2 :=
{u1 + u2 | u1 ∈P1, u2 ∈P2} and the dilation by scalar rP := {ru | u ∈P} . When
r is a natural number, rP = Pr
i=1 P.
A fundamental problem concerning lattice polytopes is:
Problem 1.1. For which lattice polytope P does the equality
(1)
P ∩M + (rP) ∩M = (r + 1)P ∩M

grally closed. From the perspective of algebraic geometry, the integral closedness
of P is equivalent to the projective normality of the polarized toric variety (X, L)
associated with P.
Let us briefly recall some basics in toric geometry (cf. [Ful93, CLS11]).

Projective normality for a general smooth projective variety is already highly
nontrivial even for surfaces. For instance, Mukai’s conjecture (cf. [EL93]) remains
widely open at the level of projective normality. Inspired by the Mukai conjecture,
the first and third authors proposed the following conjecture in the toric setting,
see [SWZ24].
Conjecture 1.2. Let P ⊆MR be an n-dimensional lattice polytope. If L(P) ≥
n −1, then the equality (1) holds for all r ∈Z>0, i.e., P is integrally closed.
Here L(P) denotes the lattice length of P (see Definition 2.1).
In the toric
setting, we can interpret the lattice length in terms of intersection numbers. More
precisely, each edge e of P corresponds to a T-invariant curve C on X, and the
intersection number L · C is equal to the lattice length of e, where L is the line
bundle associated with P as above. By Definition 2.1, the lattice length of P is
the minimum of the lattice lengths of all edges of P. Therefore, Conjecture 1.2 can
be rephrased as follows: if L · C ≥n −1 for every T-invariant curve C, then L is
projectively normal.
Several partial results toward this conjecture are known.
In [ON02], Ogata
and Nakagawa proved that every dilated polytope P = sQ with s ≥n −1 is
integrally closed. By Gubeladze [Gub12], P is projectively normal provided that
L(P) ≥4n(n + 1), and the condition can be relaxed to L(P) ≥n(n + 1) for lattice
simplices. However, it is desirable to have a condition that is linear in dimension.
In [SWZ24], by special covering, we proved the conjecture in dimension 3 for every
lattice simplex and in dimension 4 for most lattice simplices.
In this paper, we prove Conjecture 1.2 for lattice simplices.
Theorem 1.3. Let P ⊆MR ≃Rn be a lattice simplex. Suppose L(P) ≥n −1.
Then P is integrally closed.
As explained in [Fuj03], every Q-factorial toric Fano variety with Picard number
one can be obtained from a lattice simplex, so we have
Corollary 1.4. Let X be an n-dimensional Q-factorial toric Fano variety with
Picard number one and L be an ample line bundle on X. Suppose L · C ≥n −1 for
any T-invariant curve C on X. Then L is projectively normal.

In [GZ22], Gonz´alez and the third author introduced an invariant ΓP associated
with P to study the k-jet ampleness of line bundles on toric varieties. The invariant
ΓP is a nonnegative rational number that measures the singularity of P: the more
singular P is, the larger ΓP becomes. In general, ΓP ≤n −2 whenever n ≥2.
The same line of reasoning as in the proof of Theorem 1.3 yields a more refined
statement when P is “sufficiently singular”.

5. Some Open Problems
In [Oda08], Oda asked whether any smooth lattice polytope P is integrally closed.
Since smooth polytopes satisfy ΓP = 0, it is natural to consider the following general
question:
Problem 5.1. Let P ⊆MR be a lattice polytope. If L(P) ≥ΓP + 1, then is P
integrally closed?
The problem can be viewed as a unifying framework that includes both Oda’s
conjecture and Conjecture 1.2 as special cases: indeed, in light of Lemma 2.4,
an affirmative answer would immediately imply the validity of both conjectures.
Theorem 1.5 provides some evidence in this direction.
Finally, in the toric setting, integral closedness is equivalent to projective nor-
mality, which amounts to Property Np (cf. [Laz04]) for p = 0. Inspired by Mukai’s
conjecture, the first and third authors propose the following conjecture.
Conjecture 5.2. Let L be an ample line bundle on a projective toric variety X of
dimension n ≥2. Suppose L · C ≥n −1 + p for any T-invariant curve C. Then L
satisfies Property Np.
The conjecture is true for n = 2 by [Sch04], and for arbitrary n but L = A⊗k
with k ≥n−1+p by [HSS06]. In a recent paper [SW26], the conjecture is proved for
a class of smooth projective toric varieties, including the blowup of Pn at k points
in general position, for 0 ≤k ≤n+1; the blowup of Pn along Pk, for 0 ≤k ≤n−1;
and projective bundles P(O⊕r1
Pn
⊕OPn(1)⊕r2), for r1, r2 ≥1. Moreover, the class is
closed under taking finite products.
