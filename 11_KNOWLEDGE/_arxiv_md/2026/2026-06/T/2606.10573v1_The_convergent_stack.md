THE CONVERGENT STACK
MARCO D’ADDEZIO
Abstract. Inspired by Simpson’s de Rham stack and Drinfeld’s crystalline stack, we develop a stacky
approach to convergent cohomology and convergent isocrystals in positive characteristic. To any scheme X
over Fp we attach a convergent stack Xconv. When X is of finite type over a perfect field, its finitely generated
quasi-coherent O[ 1
p]-modules are equivalent to convergent isocrystals over X, compatibly with cohomology.
When X embeds into a smooth p-adic formal scheme, we describe Xconv explicitly as the quotient of an
open tube by a p-adic formal groupoid.
For f-semiperfect schemes, by contrast, the convergent stack is
representable by a preperfectoid adic space over Qp.

1. Introduction
1.1. Main results. The theory of crystalline cohomology provides a powerful tool for studying algebraic
varieties and p-divisible groups in positive characteristic. To a smooth proper variety over a perfect field
of characteristic p > 0, it associates cohomology groups with coefficients in the ring of Witt vectors, which
in many ways behave like the singular cohomology of a complex variety.
However, for singular or non-
proper varieties, crystalline cohomology is not well-behaved, and several variants have been introduced to
address these problems. One such variant is convergent cohomology, introduced by Berthelot [Ber86] and
reinterpreted by Ogus via the convergent site [Ogu84]. This yields a rational finitely generated cohomology
theory for proper varieties.
In this article, we revisit Ogus’ convergent site by allowing schemes that are not of finite type over a field,
and we study the associated convergent stack perspective, inspired by Simpson’s de Rham stack [Sim96].

1.2. Further directions. The crystalline stack of [Dri22] was used in the proof of the Hecke orbit conjecture
to associate Dieudonn´e vector bundles to the universal p-divisible groups of Shimura varieties [DvH22].
For this application, it was important to assume that the deformation spaces were formally smooth, so
that crystalline cohomology had good properties and one could work with equivariant vector bundles. The
convergent stack is designed to handle non-smooth situations. We expect that it will help in understanding
p-divisible groups over more general deformation spaces. A further goal is the construction of a moduli space
of convergent isocrystals. With the stacky point of view, this reduces to constructing a moduli space of vector
bundles over the convergent stack.
The material of this article was originally part of [D’A26]. We defer the extension of the results to rigid
cohomology and overconvergent isocrystals to [ibid.]. The prismatic analogues will appear instead in [D’A].
Overview of the paper. In §2 we introduce the convergent stack in the relative setting (Definition 2.1.1)
and develop its basic properties: change of level and ´etale descent. We also define the category of convergent
isocrystals in §2.4. In §3 we give concrete descriptions of the convergent stack. We prove that when X embeds
into a smooth p-adic formal scheme, the stack is presented as the quotient of a p-adic tube by a formal groupoid
(Theorem 3.1.6), and that for f-semiperfect schemes it is representable by a preperfectoid space (Theorem
3.3.8).
In §4 we compute convergent cohomology.
After proving a convergent Poincar´e lemma (Lemma
4.1.1) and a vanishing of higher limits (Proposition 4.2.2), we obtain comparison isomorphisms between the
cohomology of the convergent stack and the de Rham cohomology of the tube (Theorems 4.4.7, 4.5.3 and
Corollary 4.5.5).

2. Convergent cohomology revisited
This section sets up the foundational framework. For a p-complete p-torsion free base ring A and a scheme
X over A/p, we define the convergent stack of X over A of level m for every m ≥0. In this way, we drop the
finite type hypothesis of Ogus’ convergent site [Ogu90]. We then study the behaviour of the convergent stack
under change of level (Lemma 2.2.2), prove ´etale descent in X (Proposition 2.3.2), introduce the coefficient
objects, and record a stacky form of Dwork’s trick (Lemma 2.4.3).
