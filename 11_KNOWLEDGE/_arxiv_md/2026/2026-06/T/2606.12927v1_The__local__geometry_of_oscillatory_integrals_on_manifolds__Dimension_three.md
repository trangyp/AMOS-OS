The (local) geometry of oscillatory integrals on
manifolds: Dimension three
Song Dai, Liuwei Gong and Shaoming Guo
Abstract
Sogge [Sog99] studied Kakeya problems on two extreme types of three
dimensional Riemannian manifolds: Manifolds with the most symmetries
(manifolds of constant sectional curvature) and manifolds with the least
symmetries, which he called manifolds with chaotic curvature and variably
curved manifolds. In the same paper, Sogge proposed studying manifolds
with intermediate symmetry, such as (locally) symmetric spaces.
In the current paper, we propose a classification of curvature conditions
in the spirit of Sogge’s program. In particular, these curvature conditions
give a complete geometric characterization of the contact order condi-
tions (for Riemannian distance functions), introduced by the authors of
[DGGZ24] and [CGG25] when studying H¨ormander-type oscillatory inte-
gral operators. (See Theorem 2.2.)
One of these conditions generalizes Sogge’s chaotic curvature condition
to all finite orders: The chaotic curvature condition of order ď k for every
k ě 1, with the case k “ 1 corresponding to Sogge’s original condition
for variably curved manifolds.
As byproducts of our main results (see
Theorem 2.7), we show that there are no manifolds satisfying the chaotic
curvature condition of order ď 1. We also show that both the chaotic
curvature condition of order ď 2 and its failure can occur robustly under
small smooth perturbations, and for every k ě 3, a “generic” manifold
satisfies the chaotic curvature condition of order ď k.
It turns out that the chaotic curvature condition of order ď k is
precisely the same as the notion of non-pk ` 2q-exceptional, where k-
exceptional is introduced by Lytchak and Petrunin [LP22] when studying
convex sets and the non-existence of totally geodesic sub-manifolds. Thus
our results imply, in particular, that every manifold is 3-exceptional.

Chaotic curvatures of order ď 1 along geodesics were introduced by Sogge
[Sog99] when he was studying Kakeya problems on Riemannian manifolds. In
Sogge’s paper [Sog99], chaotic curvature of order ď 1 was simply called chaotic
curvature. Moreover, he used the name “variably curved manifold” if a manifold
M satisfies the chaotic curvature condition of order ď 1 (along every geodesic).
Definition 1.5 generalizes Sogge’s chaotic curvature from order ď 1 to order ď k
for every k.
Let us also remark here that in [Sog99], Sogge studied Kakeya problems
not only on manifolds satisfying the chaotic curvature condition, but also on
manifolds (of dimension three) that have constant sectional curvature. Later,
Xi [Xi17] extended Sogge’s result for three dimensional manifolds of constant
sectional curvature to every higher dimension.
Manifolds of constant sectional curvature admit certain “best” possible be-
havior in the study of Kakeya problems. For instance, it was proven in [GWZ24]
and [DGGZ24] that the distance function distϵpx, yq (given in (1.21)) satisfies
Bourgain’s condition for every ϵ, x and y if and only if the manifold has constant
sectional curvature (see also Corollary 2.3 below).
On the other hand, manifolds satisfying the chaotic curvature condition have
“least possible” symmetries2, and this explains the name “chaotic” by [Sog99].
At the end of [Sog99], Sogge proposed to study Kakeya problems on general
(locally) symmetric spaces, which, in terms of symmetries, lie between manifolds
