THE BISHOP–PHELPS–BOLLOBÁS PROPERTY FOR EXTREMALLY
DISCONNECTED RANGES: SEPARABLE AND LOW-DENSITY DOMAINS
TATTWAMASI AMRUTAM, PRIYADARSHI DEY, CHUNLIN LIU*, AND MONIKA
Abstract. We prove a Bishop–Phelps–Bollobás theorem for operators into spaces of contin-
uous scalar-valued functions on extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff spaces over both
the real and complex scalar fields. The main result applies whenever the density character of
the domain is strictly smaller than the Baire number of the underlying compact space. The
proof also yields an explicit quadratic Bishop–Phelps–Bollobás modulus. In particular, every
separable Banach space paired with such a function space has the Bishop–Phelps–Bollobás
property for operators.
1. Introduction
The Bishop-Phelps theorem [BP61] establishes that for every Banach space X, the set of
norm-attaining functionals is dense in X∗. Bollobás [Bol70] subsequently strengthened this
result by proving that both the functional and the point at which it attains its norm can be
simultaneously approximated. Specifically, if x ∈SX and x∗∈SX∗satisfy |1 −x∗(x)| < ε2/2,
there exist y ∈SX and y∗∈SX∗such that y∗(y) = 1, ∥y −x∥< ε, and ∥y∗−x∗∥< ε.
Acosta, Aron, García, and Maestre [AAGM08] introduced a quantitative generalization of
this phenomenon for bounded linear operators between Banach spaces. A pair of Banach spaces
(X, Y ) is said to have the Bishop–Phelps–Bollobás property (BPBp) if for every ε ∈(0, 1), there
exists an η(ε) > 0 such that whenever T ∈SL(X,Y ) and x0 ∈SX satisfy ∥Tx0∥> 1 −η(ε),
there exist an operator S ∈SL(X,Y ) and a vector u0 ∈SX such that ∥Su0∥= 1, ∥u0 −x0∥< ε,
and ∥S −T∥< ε.
The BPBp has since been studied extensively. For example, (X, Y ) has the BPBp whenever
X is uniformly convex [KL14], and the case where X = L1(µ) was fully classified by Choi, Kim,
Lee, and Martín [CKLM14]. We refer the reader to [DGMR22] for a comprehensive survey of
recent developments.
This paper focuses on the delicate case where the target space is C(K), the space of con-
tinuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space. Historically, establishing the BPBp in this
setting has required strong geometric assumptions on the domain, restrictions on the operator,
or topological conditions on the target. For instance, Johnson and Wolfe [JW79] proved the
density of norm-attaining operators from C(K) into C(S) in the real case, and Acosta et al.
[ABGC+14] later established the BPBp for this pair.
Aron, Cascales, and Kozhushkina [ACK11] proved a Bishop–Phelps–Bollobás theorem for
Asplund operators from real Banach spaces into C0(L). In particular, their result yields the
BPBp for (X, C0(L)) whenever X is an Asplund space. Cascales, Guirao, and Kadets [CGK13]
extended related techniques to operators into uniform algebras, which consequently establishes
the BPBp for compact operators into C(K) for any Banach space X. Cheng, Cheng, Xu,

Furthermore, when K is a scattered compact space, C(K) enjoys property β, which auto-

operators from sequence spaces to function spaces.
However, the landscape changes drastically when K is not scattered. A prominent and highly
structured class of non-scattered spaces are the extremally disconnected (Stonean) spaces,
where the closure of every open set is open. The BPBp for (X, C(L)), where L is an arbitrary
extremally disconnected compact space, appears to have remained open.
Our main result settles this question in the affirmative for all separable domains.
Theorem A. Let X be a separable Banach space and let L be an extremally disconnected
compact Hausdorff space. Then the pair (X, C(L)) has the Bishop–Phelps–Bollobás property.
Theorem A is new for non-Asplund operators into extremally disconnected ranges. The
result of [ACK11] handles the case of Asplund domains, and [CCX+20] handles the case of
Asplund operators, regardless of separability of X. Our theorem complements both results
by covering the remaining case: separable X with arbitrary, possibly non-Asplund, bounded
linear operators into C(L) for extremally disconnected L.
A concrete example is the pair
(C[0, 1], L∞[0, 1]). Indeed, L∞[0, 1] is isometric to C(L) for a hyperstonean compact space L,
and C[0, 1] is separable. Thus Theorem A applies to every bounded operator T : C[0, 1] →
L∞[0, 1]. This includes operators which are not covered by the Asplund-operator theorem of
[CCX+20]; for example, the canonical isometric embedding
J : C[0, 1] −→L∞[0, 1],
Jf = [f],
is not an Asplund operator.
A closer inspection of the proof of Theorem A shows that, in the separable case, the relevant
Baire-category argument involves only countably many dense Gδ sets. In the general case the
same argument works for a family indexed by a set of cardinality at most dens(X).
Recall that the Baire number κ(L) of a topological space L is the least cardinal κ such
that some family of κ dense open subsets of L has a non-dense intersection. For any non-
empty compact Hausdorff space, κ(L) ≥ℵ1. Replacing the countable Baire argument by an
intersection of dens(X)-many dense Gδ sets, where dens(X) denotes the density character of
X, yields the following generalization.
Theorem B. Let X be a Banach space and let L be an extremally disconnected compact
Hausdorff space such that dens(X) < κ(L). Then the pair (X, C(L)) has the Bishop–Phelps–
Bollobás property.
Since κ(L) ≥ℵ1 always, every separable X satisfies dens(X) = ℵ0 < κ(L), so Theorem B
contains Theorem A as a special case. When L is the Stone space of a complete Boolean
algebra that is λ-distributive for every λ < κ – for instance, the completion of any < κ-closed
forcing notion, such as Add(κ, 1) for regular κ – one has κ(L) ≥κ [BS89]. Since κ may be
taken arbitrarily large, Theorem B applies to non-separable domains X of correspondingly
large density character. We emphasize, however, that Theorem B is new only when L has no
dense set of isolated points; if isolated points are dense in L then κ(L) = ∞and the result
reduces to a case covered by [AAGM08] via property β. The general non-separable case for
extremally disconnected L, without the cardinality hypothesis dens(X) < κ(L), remains open.

Organization of the paper. The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we fix the
notation and recall the basic facts on extremally disconnected compact spaces that will be used
throughout the proof. We review the injectivity of C(L), Gleason’s projectivity theorem, and
the Baire number of a compact space. We also introduce the admissible scalar sets associated
with an operator T : X →C(L), prove the scalar-to-operator lifting lemma, and establish the

In Section 3, we prove the main low-density theorem (Theorem B). The proof first uses the
Baire-number hypothesis to obtain a simultaneous continuity point for the relevant distance
functions, then applies the scalar Bishop–Phelps–Bollobás theorem, constructs a continuous
scalar selection by means of clopen patching and Gleason’s theorem, and finally lifts this
selection to the desired operator. The separable case stated in Theorem A is then obtained as
a corollary.

Throughout the paper F denotes either R or C. If L is a compact Hausdorff space, then

supremum norm. We write

norm-dense subset of X.
We also use two standard theorems about extremally disconnected compact spaces. Recall
that a compact Hausdorff space L is called extremally disconnected if the closure of every open
subset of L is again open, or equivalently, if L has a base of clopen sets and every two disjoint
open sets have disjoint closures. Prominent examples include the Stone–Čech compactification
βΓ of any discrete set Γ, the hyperstonean spaces arising as Gelfand spectra of commutative
von Neumann algebras, and the Stone spaces of complete Boolean algebras.

connects extremal disconnectedness to the extension property for operators.

The structural richness of extremally disconnected spaces rests on the abundance of clopen
sets.
The following lemma records two basic facts about their topology that will be used
repeatedly in the construction of the continuous selection in the main proof.
Lemma 1. Let L be an extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff space. Then:
(i) L has a base of clopen neighborhoods.
(ii) Every clopen subspace of L is again extremally disconnected compact Hausdorff.
Proof. For part (i), let p ∈L and let U be an open neighborhood of p. Since L is compact
Hausdorff, it is regular. Then there exists an open set W with p ∈W ⊂W ⊂U. By extremal
disconnectedness, W is open, hence clopen. Setting V := W yields a clopen neighborhood of
p contained in U.
Part (ii) is immediate, since a clopen subspace inherits both compactness and the property
that open closures are open.
□
We will also use the Baire number of L to quantify the failure of separability that the
construction can tolerate.
Definition 1 (Baire number). Let L be a topological space. The Baire number (or Novak
number) κ(L) is the least cardinal κ for which there exists a family of κ dense open subsets of
L whose intersection is not dense. If no such family exists, set κ(L) = ∞.
For the Stone–Čech remainder N∗= βN \ N one has κ(N∗) = h, the distributivity number
of P(N)/fin, which satisfies ℵ1 ≤h ≤c and may consistently exceed ℵ1 [BPS80]. On the other
hand, the Stone space of a complete Boolean algebra that is λ-distributive for all λ < κ – e.g.
the completion of a < κ-closed forcing notion – has Baire number at least κ, so Baire numbers
may be arbitrarily large [BS89].
Lemma 2. Let L be a compact Hausdorff space and let λ < κ(L) be a cardinal. Then the
intersection of any family of λ dense Gδ subsets of L is dense.
Proof. Write each dense Gδ set as a countable intersection of dense open sets. If λ is infinite,
the total number of dense open sets involved is at most λ · ℵ0 = λ < κ(L), and the conclusion
follows from the definition of κ(L).
If λ is finite and non-zero, then the total number is
countable; since every non-empty compact Hausdorff space is Baire, equivalently ℵ0 < κ(L),
the intersection is dense. The case λ = 0 is trivial.
□

3. Proof of main theorems
To establish our main results, our approach avoids standard geometric requirements on X
by modularizing the problem into a topological and analytic construction over L. The proof
proceeds in three steps. First, using the hypothesis dens(X) < κ(L), and a Baire-category
argument on L, we produce a point p ∈L that is simultaneously a near-norming point for Tx0
and a continuity point for a family of distance functions indexed by D ×E ×Q+, of cardinality
at most dens(X). Second, the classical scalar Bishop–Phelps–Bollobás theorem, applied at
p, yields a perturbed unit vector in X together with a target scalar value. Third, a patching
construction over the clopen base of L, driven by Gleason’s projectivity theorem, glues together
a continuous scalar function on L taking values in the admissible sets and attaining the target
value at p. The lifting lemma (Lemma 3), the 1-injectivity of C(L), then converts this scalar
function into the desired operator S : X →C(L).

Remark 1. The hypothesis dens(X) < κ(L) is strictly weaker than separability of X for
suitable L. For instance, if B is a complete Boolean algebra that is λ-distributive for every
λ < κ for some uncountable κ – for example, the completion of a < κ-closed forcing notion –
and L is its Stone space, then κ(L) ≥κ [BS89, Jec03], and Theorem 3 yields the BPBp for
(X, C(L)) for every Banach space X with dens(X) < κ. When L has a dense set of isolated
points, in particular, when L = βΓ for a discrete set Γ, we have C(L) = ℓ∞(Γ). In that case,
every dense open subset of L contains all isolated points, hence κ(L) = ∞and Theorem 3
applies to every Banach space X. This case is, however, already covered by the property-β
argument of Acosta–Aron–García–Maestre [AAGM08].
Theorem 3 covers the case where L has no dense set of isolated points, for example,
the Gelfand spectrum of L∞(µ) for a non-atomic measure µ, or the Stone space of a non-
atomic complete Boolean algebra – in which C(L) does not have property β and the result of
[AAGM08] does not apply. The general non-separable case for such L, without the cardinality
hypothesis dens(X) < κ(L), remains open.
