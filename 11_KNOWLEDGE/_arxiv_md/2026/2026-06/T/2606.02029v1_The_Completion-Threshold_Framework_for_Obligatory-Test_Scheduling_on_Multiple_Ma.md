Abstract
We study online scheduling with obligatory testing on m identical machines with the objective of
minimizing the sum of completion times. In this model, every job must undergo a test before its actual
processing time is revealed. Consequently, the central algorithmic challenge is no longer whether to
acquire information, but how to optimally balance machine capacity between revealing unknown
jobs and processing currently known ones. While this tradeoff becomes structurally richer in the
multiple-machine setting, the only prior explicit deterministic lower bound for this objective was
√
2,
established strictly for a single machine [5]. Our core conceptual contribution is demonstrating that
completion-threshold quantities, denoted TX, serve as the fundamental analytical metric for this
setting. Because every completed job must first pass through the testing phase, delayed revelation
inherently forces delayed completion. By bounding these TX thresholds, we systematically derive
strong lower bounds on the total completion time. Utilizing this framework, we establish the first
substantial deterministic lower bounds for multiple machines, including a three-type bound of 1.4811
and a multi-type dyadic construction that asymptotically approaches 3/2. Finally, we complement
these theoretical limits with a deterministic 2-competitive list-scheduling algorithm for arbitrary
test times.

Introduction
The framework of scheduling with testing involves problems in which the processing time
of a job is not known in advance, but can be revealed by performing a test. Most previous
work considers the optional-test model, where the scheduler may decide whether to test a job
first or to execute it directly using prior information. In contrast, Dogeas et al. [5] recently
introduced the obligatory-test model, in which every job must be tested before its processing
part can begin, and studied the single-machine case with the objective of minimizing the
sum of completion times. In this paper, we extend the obligatory-test model to m identical
parallel machines. Each job j consists of a test of known length tj and a processing operation
of unknown length pj, where pj is revealed only upon completion of the test. All jobs are
available at time 0, and the objective is to minimize P
j Cj, where Cj denotes the completion
time of job j. We consider both standard test-time settings. In the uniform setting, all jobs
have the same test time, and we assume without loss of generality that tj = 1 for all j. In
the non-uniform setting, test times may vary across jobs.
The obligatory-test model is structurally different from the optional one. In the optional
model, the scheduler must decide whether information acquisition is worthwhile. In the
obligatory model, this decision disappears, since every job must eventually be tested. The
algorithm must instead decide how to divide machine time between two competing activities:
testing jobs whose processing times are still unknown, and processing jobs whose test
outcomes have already been revealed. If too much time is spent on testing, information is

Related Work.
The framework of scheduling with testing can be studied under both
stochastic and adversarial uncertainty. In the stochastic setting, testing reveals random
job characteristics, and the objective is to minimize expected costs [13]. This probabilistic
approach differs from our adversarial online setting, which originated with the optional-test
model introduced by Dürr et al. [7, 8]. In this model, jobs have known upper bounds, and the
scheduler decides whether to test them to reveal actual processing times. For a single machine
with uniform test times, Dürr et al. established tight bounds for makespan minimization and
provided competitive algorithms and lower bounds for minimizing the sum of completion
times. This was later extended to arbitrary test times by Albers and Eckl [1], with subsequent
algorithmic improvements by Liu et al. [14] via a refined amortized analysis. The broader
paradigm of explorable uncertainty has also inspired related oracle-based variants [6] and

this, we focus on closing the upper and lower bound gaps for the unweighted total completion

Preliminaries
In this section, we formally define the obligatory-test scheduling on identical parallel machines
and establish the necessary notation. We then briefly review the concept of competitive
analysis. Finally, we introduce the completion-threshold quantities TX, which provide a
mathematically convenient framework for our lower-bound arguments in Section 3.
Problem Definition.
We study the problem of scheduling with testing on m identical
parallel machines, where a set of jobs J = {1, ..., N} is available at time 0. Each job j ∈J
requires two operations:
a test of known length tj ≥0, and
a processing operation of unknown length pj ≥0.
The exact value of pj is revealed only upon the completion of job j’s test. In the obligatory-
test model, testing is a strict prerequisite for processing. Thus, the processing operation of
job j cannot begin until its corresponding test has finished. The processing operation may
be executed on any machine and does not need to start immediately after the test completes

(i.e., idle time or the execution of other operations may occur between a job’s test and its
processing).
We denote the total size of job j by σj := tj + pj. The completion time of job j, denoted
by Cj, is defined as the time at which its processing operation finishes. Our objective is
to minimize the sum of completion times, P

settings: the uniform setting, where all jobs share the same test time (assumed without
loss of generality to be tj = 1 for all j ∈J), and the non-uniform setting, where test times
may vary across jobs. For comparison, in the more widely studied optional-test model, the
scheduler may choose to either test a job first or execute it directly using a known upper
bound uj ≥pj on its processing time.
Competitive Ratio.
Let ALG(I) denote the objective value achieved by the online algorithm
on a given instance I, and let OPT(I) denote the value of an optimal offline schedule for
the same instance. When the instance I is clear from the context, we abbreviate ALG(I)
and OPT(I) as ALG and OPT, respectively. We say that a deterministic online algorithm is
ρ-competitive if ALG(I) ≤ρ · OPT(I) for every instance I.

3
Lower Bounds
Our principal technical contribution is a lower-bound framework grounded in the notion
of completion thresholds. Building on the identity ALG = PN
X=1 TX from Section 2, we
interpret the quantities TX as the fundamental analytical metric for obligatory testing on
parallel machines. The key advantage of the obligatory-testing model is that each job must
first complete a revealed testing phase before its processing time is revealed and it can
contribute to the objective function. As a result, structural constraints on the order in
which information is revealed translate directly into constraints on the feasible completion
thresholds. We leverage this perspective in two main constructions. First, we introduce an
adversarial three-type instance family that demonstrates the core logic of the TX framework
and establishes a lower bound strictly greater than the prior
√
2 single-machine barrier.
Subsequently, we demonstrate that this viewpoint extends naturally to a dyadic family with
multiple job types, which admits a transparent recursive analysis and yields a deterministic
lower bound asymptotically approaching 3/2. Finally, we conclude with a brief observation
regarding the optional-testing variant. Although this mechanism no longer applies in such
a direct manner, we show that a dedicated strategy still yields a simple lower bound of φ.
Throughout this section, we use the notation (x)+ := max{x, 0} to denote the positive part
of a real number x.

An Observation for the Optional-Test Variant
The completion-threshold framework developed above relies crucially on the obligatory nature
of testing: every completed job must pass through a revealed test stage, allowing us to
translate slow revelation into delayed completion. In the optional-test model, this mechanism

4
A Baseline Algorithm
Having established the theoretical lower bounds, we now focus on the algorithmic aspect of
the problem. In this section, we present a deterministic priority-based list-scheduling rule for
the obligatory-test model on m identical machines. We show that for the natural parameter
choice of γ = 1, the algorithm achieves a competitive ratio of 2 for arbitrary test times. We
subsequently demonstrate that this parameterized priority family inherently degrades when
γ ̸= 1. Specifically, for γ < 1, the asymptotic competitive ratio remains at least 2, while for
γ > 1, it is no less than 1 + 1/γ.
4.1
Description of the Algorithm
We define a priority-based list-scheduling algorithm, denoted as Aγ, parameterized by a
scalar γ > 0.
Maintain two disjoint sets of available operations:
T: untested jobs, ordered by nondecreasing test time tj.
U: tested but unprocessed jobs, ordered by nondecreasing total size σj = tj + pj.
Whenever a machine becomes idle:
1. If U = ∅, select the test operation of a minimum-test-time job in T.
2. Else if T = ∅, select the processing operation of a minimum-total-size job in U.
3. If both T and U are non-empty, let jT ∈arg mini∈T ti and jU ∈arg mini∈U σi. If
γtjT ≤σjU , select the test of jT ; otherwise, select the processing operation of jU.
Ties are broken in favor of testing operations. The selected operation is assigned via list
scheduling to the earliest available machine. If a processing operation is selected exactly
at the moment its corresponding test completes, it is scheduled immediately after the test
on the same machine.
This rule effectively compares the shortest available test against the smallest revealed
total size. By doing so, it balances two competing objectives: revealing the processing times
of unknown jobs and clearing currently known small jobs.

5
Conclusion
We explore the obligatory-test scheduling problem on m identical machines with the objective
of minimizing the sum of completion times. Our primary contribution is a novel lower-bound
framework based on completion thresholds. Conceptually, this perspective demonstrates
that the adversarial mechanisms underlying obligatory testing are not confined to the single-
machine setting, but rigorously extend to identical parallel machines. By translating the
slow revelation of processing times into explicit lower bounds on the total completion time,
this approach yields the first substantial deterministic lower bounds for multiple-machine
obligatory testing. These results include a three-type bound of 1.4811 and a multi-type dyadic
construction that asymptotically approaches 3/2. On the algorithmic side, we complement

these lower bounds with a simple, deterministic 2-competitive algorithm for arbitrary test
times. For the closely related single-machine setting with uniform test times, the current best
deterministic upper bound stands at 1.585 [5], while our work establishes 3/2 as a natural
theoretical barrier. This raises the intriguing possibility that the optimal competitive ratio
for uniform obligatory testing may indeed be exactly 3/2.
Several natural directions remain for future research. A particularly appealing open
problem is to design a deterministic 3/2-competitive algorithm for the single-machine uniform
setting. Another promising direction is to develop stronger lower-bound techniques for
optional testing on multiple machines by synthesizing the most effective current proof
strategies. Finally, on the upper-bound side, it remains an intriguing open question whether
the 1.861-competitive algorithm for the single-machine non-uniform obligatory model can be
generalized to the multiple-machine setting.
