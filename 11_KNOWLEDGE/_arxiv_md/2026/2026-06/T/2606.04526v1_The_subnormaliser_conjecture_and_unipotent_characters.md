THE SUBNORMALISER CONJECTURE
AND UNIPOTENT CHARACTERS
GUNTER MALLE
Abstract. We prove instances of the subnormaliser conjecture on character bijections
for finite groups by deriving generic versions of such bijections for unipotent characters of
nearly simple groups of Lie type. For this, we formulate an extension of d-Harish-Chandra
theory to what we call generic subnormalisers, which are certain, usually disconnected,
reductive subgroups of a simple algebraic group.
For very good primes the generic
bijections give rise to bijections to certain subgroups that should contain subnormalisers
and thus be suitable for an eventual inductive approach. If the group in question has
abelian Sylow ℓ-subgroups for some prime ℓthen our bijections satisfy the properties
predicted by the subnormaliser conjecture, and moreover preserve character values up to
sign, Brauer ℓ-blocks, and are Galois equivariant.
1. Introduction
Character correspondences are a recurrent and central theme in representation theory
of finite groups. A fundamentally new type was recently proposed in the manuscript [20]
of Alex Moret´o and Noelia Rizo. For any prime number ℓthey predict the existence of
bijections with special properties between ordinary irreducible characters of a finite group
G and those of subnormalisers of ℓ-elements of G. Here, for x ∈G its subnormaliser in
G is defined as

The strong subnormaliser conjecture was shown to hold for all symmetric groups Sn by
Mart´ınez [17] for the prime ℓ= 2. He also proved it for any prime ℓfor those ℓ-elements
x ∈Sn contained in a unique Sylow ℓ-subgroup (so-called picky elements). In joint work
with Schaeffer Fry [15] we showed the subnormaliser conjecture for picky ℓ-elements of
groups of Lie type in characteristic different from ℓ, and the strong form when moreover
ℓ= 2. For p-solvable groups the picky case was shown by Moret´o, Navarro and Rizo [19].
Navarro [21] conjectured that there exist McKay bijections with respect to the prime ℓ
preserving character fields over the ℓ-adic numbers Qℓ. It seems likely that the conjectured
Moret´o bijections can also be chosen with this property.
In this paper we obtain further instances of the strong form of the Moret´o conjecture for
unipotent characters of nearly simple groups of Lie type with abelian Sylow ℓ-subgroups,
moreover preserving blocks and realised by Galois equivariant bijections:
Theorem 2. Let G be a simple linear algebraic group with Steinberg map F : G →G.
Let ℓbe a prime such that Sylow ℓ-subgroups of G := GF are abelian. Then the Moret´o
Conjecture holds for the unipotent characters of G for all ℓ-elements x ∈G.
More precisely, there is a bijection
f : Uchx(G) →Uchx(SubG(x))
as in Conjecture 1, depending only on SubG(x) but not on x, such that
(1) f(χ)(xy) = ±χ(xy) for every ℓ′-element y ∈CG(x)ℓ′ and every χ ∈Uchx(G);
(2) f preserves ℓ-blocks; and
(3) for all χ ∈Uchx(G) the character fields Qℓ(χ) = Qℓ(f(χ)) agree.
That is, the bijection preserves character values up to sign even on all elements of G with
ℓ-part equal to x. The set Uch(SubG(x)) of ‘unipotent characters’ of the subnormaliser
will be defined at the beginning of Section 2.2.
In our situation there is of course a strong connection between the existence of a Ga-
lois equivariant bijection as above and the Alperin–McKay–Navarro (AMN) conjecture.
Indeed, if Sylow ℓ-subgroups of G are abelian, all characters have height zero in their
block by the proven, but highly non-trivial Brauer height zero conjecture. Then, by the
(unproven) AMN conjecture applied to both the group G and the subnormaliser SubG(x)
in question we would obtain Galois equivariant bijections between height zero characters
in corresponding blocks of G and SubG(x). But whether they respect non-vanishing at x,
and even the value at x up to sign, is not at all clear. Note also that the AMN conjecture
has at present not been reduced to a question on nearly simple groups.
In fact, our result extends to all very good primes in a slightly weaker form, involving
a subgroup ˆS(C) to be defined in Section 2.1, which still seems strong enough for an
eventual inductive approach:
