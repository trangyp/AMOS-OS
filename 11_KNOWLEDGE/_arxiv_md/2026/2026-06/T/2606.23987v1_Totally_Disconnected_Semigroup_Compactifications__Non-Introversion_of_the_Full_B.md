Totally Disconnected Semigroup Compactifications:
Non-Introversion of the Full Boolean Algebra of Clopen Sets
Joshua Basman Monterrubio∗, Thomas Czyzowicz†, Ross Stokke‡, and Emily Thevenot§
Abstract
In terms of the existence of a single clopen set and two related nets, we characterize when
the full Boolean algebra, B(G), of clopen subsets of a topological group G is left introverted.
We employ this characterization to show that when G is a first countable, σ-compact, totally
disconnected locally compact group, B(G) is left introverted if and only if G is compact or
discrete, thus providing a strong positive answer to a question posed in [10].
Examples of
clopen sets and nets witnessing our non-introversion theorem are presented. Some hereditary
properties of left introversion of B(G) are proved and then employed to extend our main result
to other classes of topological groups.
MSC codes: Primary 22A10, 22A20, 06E15, 54D35
Key words and phrases: semigroup compactification, totally disconnected locally compact group,
Boolean algebra, Stone space, introversion, Arens product

Introduction
Totally disconnected locally compact (tdlc) groups are an important class of topological groups
that have been studied by many authors in a wide variety of contexts. A recent survey article
on tdlc groups is [4]; also see [2, 3, 7, 11].
Semigroup compactification theory has meanwhile
served as a fundamental tool in the study of topological groups and semigroups, e.g., see [1, 9].
Motivated by the desire to remain within the class of totally disconnected spaces when studying
the semigroup compactifications of a totally disconnected group, the authors of [10] introduced
definitions of left/right introversion of a Boolean algebra, B, of clopen subsets of a topological
group G and left/right Arens products on the associated Stone space, νBG, with respect to which
νBG is a totally disconnected (right/left topological) semigroup compactification of G. Moreover,
every totally disconnected semigroup compactification of G is equivalent to νBG, with left Arens
product, for a unique left introverted Boolean algebra of clopen subsets of G [10, Theorem 3.3]. The
universal totally disconnected right topological semigroup, universal semitopological semigroup, and
universal topological group compactifications of G, νLMCG, νWAP G and νAP G, respectively, were
described in [10] by their associated Boolean algebras of clopen subsets of G, BLMC, BWAP and
BAP , respectively.
To help motivate the theory developed in [10], the referee of that paper suggested that the
authors should aim to find an example of a topological group G for which the universal totally dis-
connected compactification of G, νBG, is not equivalent to the universal semigroup compactification

coset ring of G), BWAP , BLUC and BLMC, as defined in [10], are all topologist’s compactifications
in the sense that their associated compactification homomorphisms are homeomorphisms onto their
images [10, Corollary 4.15]; that is, though they may be properly contained in B, each of the left
introverted Boolean algebras Ω, BWAP , BLUC and BLMC contain sufficiently many clopen sets to
yield topologist’s compactifications of tdlc groups.

Assume now that G is a (Hausdorff) topological group. For a subset B of G and s ∈G, let
B · s = s−1B and s · B = Bs−1. If B is a G-invariant Boolean subalgebra of B(G) and V ∈νBG,
we let
V · B = {s ∈G : B · s ∈V}
and
B · V = {s ∈G : s · B ∈V},
and say that B is left (respectively right) introverted if V · B ∈B (respectively B · V ∈B) for every
V ∈νBG and B ∈B. When B is left (respectively right) introverted, (νBG, νB) becomes a totally
disconnected right (left) topological semigroup compactification of G with respect its left (right)
Arens product defined by
U □V = {B ∈B : V · B ∈U}
(respectively U ⋄V = {B ∈B : B · U ∈V}).
Moreover, every totally disconnected right topological semigroup compactification of G is equiv-
alent, as a semigroup compactification, to (νBG, νB) for some left introverted Boolean subalgebra
B of B(G) [10, Theorem 3.3]. Additional details, definitions, references, and motivation for these
notions can be found in [10].

Proposition 2.1. Suppose that B is a clopen subset of a topological group G for which

Theorem 2.2. Let G be a topological group. Then B is not left introverted if and only if there is
a clopen subset B of G with property (NB).
Proof. Supposing that B is not left introverted, there is a B-ultrafilter V and a clopen subset A of
G such that V ·A is not clopen; since G\(V ·A) = V ·(G\A), we can assume that V ·A is not closed.
We can also assume that eG ∈V · A\V ·A. (To see this, take x ∈V · A\V ·A. Since g 7→g·x := x−1g
is a homeomorphism of G, eG ∈(V · A\V · A) · x = (V · A) · x\(V · A) · x = V · (A · x)\V · (A · x),
so we can replace A by A · x if necessary.) Take a net (cκ)κ∈K in V · A such that limκ cκ = eG
and let B = G\A. Since eG is not in V · A, A = A · eG does not belong to the B-ultrafilter V, so
B ∈V; equivalently, V ∈λB(B). Let (aγ)γ∈Γ be a net in G such that limγ νB(aγ) = V. Assuming
without loss of generality that each νB(aγ) belongs to the open neighbourhood λB(B) of V, (aγ)γ∈Γ
is a net contained in B. Fix κ in K. Since cκ ∈V · A, A · cκ ∈V, and therefore λB(A · cκ) is an
open neighbourhood of V in νBG; hence, we can take γκ in Γ such that νB(aγ) ∈λB(A · cκ) for
γ ≥γκ. Equivalently, cκaγ ∈A, and therefore cκaγ /∈B, for γ ≥γκ. Hence B has property (NB).
Proposition 2.1 includes the converse.
Proposition 2.3. If G is either a discrete group or a compact group, then B is introverted.
Proof. Trivially, B is introverted when G is discrete. For s ∈G and B ∈B, νB(s) · B = s · B, B ·
νB(s) = B · s ∈B [10, Lemma 3.2] and every B-ultrafilter is fixed when G is compact. Hence, B
is introverted when G is compact.

Proposition 2.8. Let G be a tdlc group satisfying one of the following conditions:
(i) G is σ-compact and is not compact-by-discrete;
(ii) G is noncompact and does not contain a noncompact, σ-compact, compact-by-discrete open
subgroup;
(iii) G is noncompact and contains a sequence of neighbourhoods of the identity, (Un)n , such that
T Un has empty interior.
Then B is not left introverted.
Proof. If G satisfies condition (ii) or (iii), take H to be a noncompact, σ-compact open subgroup
of G (via Lemma 2.7); if G satisfies condition (i), let H = G. By Proposition 2.6, it suffices to
show that B(H) is not left introverted. Using the Kakutani-Kodaira Theorem [6, Theorem 8.7],
take N to be a compact normal subgroup of H such that H/N is metrizable; in the case that G
satisfies condition (iii), assume as well that N is contained in T Un. In any case, N is not open, so
H/N is a first countable, σ-compact, noncompact, nondiscrete tdlc group, so B(H/N) is not left
introverted by Theorem 2.4. By Proposition 2.6, B(H) is not left introverted, as needed.
Remark 2.9. 1. If G is a σ-compact locally compact group, then there is a sequence of neighbour-

and only if G is metrizable.
2. Let G be a locally compact group with connected component of the identity, Ge. If G/Ge
satisfies one of the three conditions stated in Proposition 2.8, then B is not left introverted by
