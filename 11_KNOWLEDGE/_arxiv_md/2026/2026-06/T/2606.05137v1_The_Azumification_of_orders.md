THE AZUMIFICATION OF ORDERS
TIMOTHY DE DEYN
Abstract. We construct a stacky resolution of singularities for certain non-
commutative spaces which can be viewed as ‘finite noncommutative extensions’
of schemes. More precisely, we show that any order over a reduced separated
finite type scheme over a field of characteristic zero can be resolved by an
Azumaya algebra over a smooth Deligne–Mumford stack by a sequence of
stacky blow-ups.
1. Introduction
Noncommutative algebraic geometry is a vast field. Consequently, there are many
different flavours of what a ‘noncommutative space’ is. In this work, we consider
those that can be viewed as ‘finite noncommutative extensions’ of schemes. More
precisely, we consider those spaces known as orders over schemes, which are types
of coherent sheaves of algebras over schemes; see §2.2 for the exact definition. Our
main result is a resolution of singularities for orders over varieties by Azumaya
algebras over Deligne–Mumford stacks. We call this process the Azumification of
the order. Let us start by stating our main theorem, after which we give some
motivation. In the statement a stacky blow-up refers to either a usual blow-up or
taking a root stack.
Theorem 1.1. Let A be an order over a reduced separated scheme X of finite
type over a field 𝕜of characteristic zero. Then there exists a sheaf of Azumaya
algebras B over a smooth (over 𝕜) quasi-compact separated Deligne–Mumford stack
X together with a proper birational morphism π: X →X, obtained as the composition
of stacky blow-ups, and a morphism of OX-algebras π∗A →B that is generically an
isomorphism.
Remark 1.2. The induced morphism A →π∗B is automatically injective, and so
is necessarily an isomorphism when A is a maximal order. In this case, A is the
pushforward of an Azumaya algebra over a Deligne–Mumford stack.
Thus one can resolve any order by an Azumaya algebra, granted one leaves the
realm of schemes and moves to Deligne–Mumford stacks. It is necessary to extend
to stacks to obtain a birational morphism. If one wants to stay inside the realm of
schemes, one can do this at the cost of considering alterations, i.e. generically finite
morphisms, instead birational morphisms.
The result above should be compared to the results in the recent paper by
Faber–Ingalls–Okawa–Satriano [FIOS25]. There the authors show that, for any
‘sufficiently nice’ order (see Definition 1.4 of ibid.) over a normal connected quasi-
projective surface over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero or at least
seven, that the order is always Morita equivalent to an Azumaya algebra over a
Deligne–Mumford stack; that is their categories of (quasi-)coherent sheaves are

Let us say some words about the proof. The idea is to first reduce to the case
where the non-Azumaya locus is a subset of a simple normal crossings divisor. Then,
through taking an iterated root stack, we reduce to A being Azumaya in codimension
one; that is the non-Azumaya locus contains no divisors. Next, using purity, we
further reduce to the case where A is ´etale locally the endomorphism ring of a
reflexive module. Lastly, by a ‘universal flattening’ in the sense of [Ros68, Vil06],
we make this reflexive module locally free, thus yielding an Azumaya algebra.
The structure of the note is as follows. We start with some preliminaries in
§2, this includes stacky recollections, how to remove ramification of orders via
root stacks, and how to blow-up modules on schemes. This being done, we prove
Theorem 1.1 in §3.
Conventions. As we will need (strong) resolutions of singularities, everything takes

over 𝕜.
Disclaimer. For ease, we restrict the main theorem to reduced separated schemes of
finite type over 𝕜, using more elaborate resolution machinery one can weaken this,

2.2. Orders over schemes and stacks. A quasi-coherent sheaf F over a scheme
X is torsion-free when, for all x ∈X, Fx is a torsion-free OX,x-module. More
generally, a quasi-coherent sheaf F over a (reduced locally Noetherian1) algebraic
stack X is torsion-free when p∗F is torsion-free for some smooth atlas p: U →X
(and hence all).

concretely, over an integral scheme with generic point η this means that there are

To define maximal orders we restrict for simplicity to the case where X is an
integral Noetherian scheme with generic point2 η. In this case, let A be a central

on X (and A) maximal orders containing a given order exist, see [Yu11, Theorem

algebra. However, it should be noted that maximal orders are generally not unique.
Lastly, recall that an order (again over a scheme for simplicity) is called tame, if
it is reflexive as OX-module and its stalks at codimension one points are hereditary
rings, i.e. they have global dimension one. Maximal orders are always tame over
normal schemes.
2.3. Ramification. We define the ramification data of tame orders. This measures
the failure of the order to be Azumaya.
2.3.1. Local. Let (R, m) be a DVR and denote its residue field κ := R/m. Assume
for simplicity that κ is perfect.
Consider a tame, i.e. hereditary, R-order Λ (in a central simple algebra). Let
κ′ := Z(Λ/ rad Λ), where rad denotes the Jacobson radical; it is a product of cyclic
field extension of κ (this follows from the structure theorem of hereditary orders over
complete DVRs). Define the ramification index of Λ to be the integer r := dimκ κ′.
One has that r is the minimal integer such that (rad Λ)r = mΛ. The ramification
index measures the failure of Λ to be Azumaya, i.e. of Λ/mΛ being a central simple
algebra, as one can show that Λ is Azumaya if and only if r = 1.
2.3.2. Global. Let A be a tame order over a normal Noetherian scheme and let
Ram(A) denote the ramification locus of A, i.e. the non-Azumaya locus
{x ∈X | Ax is not an Azumaya OX,x-algebra}.
This is a proper closed3 subset (which we can view as a reduced subscheme, if we
want). We define the ramification data of A to be the divisors E = (E1, . . . , En)
contained in Ram(A) and the corresponding ramification indices r = (r1, . . . , rn) at
their generic points, i.e. ri is the ramification index of AEi as OX,Ei-order, where
by abuse of notation we let Ei also denote its generic point.

2.5. Blowing up modules. In general, even for Noetherian schemes, the sheaf of
meromorphic functions KX need not be quasi-coherent [Kle79]. Therefore, defining
a ‘correct’ notion of quasi-coherent torsion submodule (and getting this to behave
well with respect to pulling back along flat morphisms) is subtle. In the reduced
locally Noetherian setting these issues disappear, so we restrict to those schemes in
this subsection.
In fact, for a reduced locally Noetherian scheme X and a (quasi-)coherent module
M, the torsion submodule
tors(M) := ker (M →M ⊗OX KX) ⊆M
is (quasi-)coherent and has as sections over an open subset U ⊆X those sections
in M(U) that become zero in the stalks at the generic points of the irreducible
components of U. One can see this using, e.g., [Sta26, Tag 02OW].

Definition 2.3. Let X be a reduced locally Noetherian scheme and M a coherent
OX-module of constant generic rank r, i.e. M is locally free of rank r on a dense
open subset of X5. A morphism f : Y →X (from a reduced locally Noetherian
scheme Y ) is called a flattening of M if
f ♭(M) := f ∗(M)/ tors(f ∗(M))
is locally free of rank r. The flattening f is called universal if every other flattening
of M factors uniquely through f. The universal flattening of M exists (see below)
and is called the blow-up of X at M; it (or rather, its source) is denoted by BlM(X).
Remark 2.4.
i) A finitely presented flat module is locally free. So, requiring f ♭(M) to be
locally free (of finite rank) is the same as requiring it to be flat; this explains
the name flattening.
ii) The notion of flattening makes sense for more general locally Noetherian
schemes (as long as the sheaf of meromorphic functions is quasi-coherent),
but the conditions on M are a bit more subtle. One requires M ⊗OX KX =
(KX)r for some r.
iii) The rank condition is necessary for obtaining a universal property. For
example, let R = k[x, y] be the polynomial ring in two variables and
I = (x, y) the maximal ideal of the origin (ideals, of integral schemes, are
always of constant generic rank 1). Consider f : Spec(R/I) ,→Spec R,
then f ♭(I) = k2. Showing that the rank can increase. For the blow-up
g: BlI(Spec R) →Spec R we have that g♭(I) = g−1(I) · O is locally free
of rank one (one way to see the equality is using that g is a dominant
morphism between integral scheme). Suppose there existed a universal
flattening h: X →Spec R without fixing the rank, i.e. we only require
I := h♭(I) to be locally free. Then for f to factor through h we need I

The universal flattening exists, and thus is unique up to unique isomorphism. It
can be constructed as a certain subscheme of a Quot scheme, or by blowing up a

see [Ros68, Rie71, Vil06]. The latter construction shows that the blow-up is indeed
a reduced locally Noetherian scheme, and is quasi-compact resp. integral when X is.
Moreover, it follows that the blow-up is projective and birational6.

3. Proof of Theorem 1.1
Suppose A is an order over a scheme X that is locally free as OX-module. In this
case being Azumaya is equivalent to being Azumaya in codimension one. Therefore
the strategy of the proof is to achieve the following:
i) make A Azumaya in codimension one,
ii) make A locally free.
For i) we will use root stacks and for ii) we will use blow-ups. Of course, the order
and the exact way of doing this is important and so requires some care.
Step 1. Let (X, A) be as in Theorem 1.1. First resolve X and replacing A by
π♭A := π∗A/ tors(π∗A),
where π denotes the resolution (the pullback will generally only remain torsion free
when π is flat). We may thus assume, by looking per connected component, that X
is a smooth variety. Moreover, as the morphism π is birational, and hence induces
an isomorphism at the generic points, π♭A remains an order and the quotient map

Step 4. We have now reduced to the case where the order A is an Azumaya
algebra, but the Deligne–Mumford stack X is not-necessarily smooth any more,
nor is the morphism π used to arrive at this situation a composition of blow-ups
(in the previous step the morphism is only so locally). However, as blow-ups are
‘cofinal’ [Ryd25, Theorem 3.1] one can take further blow-ups of X to ensure that π
is a composition of (stacky) blow-ups. Lastly, a final resolution of singularities (by
blow-ups) of X, e.g. these exist as smooth functorial resolutions exist in characteristic
zero and by cofinality of blow-ups, and replacing A by its pullback finishes the proof
of Theorem 1.1.
