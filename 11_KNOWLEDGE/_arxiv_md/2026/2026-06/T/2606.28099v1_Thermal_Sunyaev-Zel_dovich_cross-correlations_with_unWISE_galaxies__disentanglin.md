Abstract
Cross-correlations between the thermal Sunyaev-Zel’dovich (tSZ) effect and galaxy surveys provide
a sensitive probe of hot gas in low-mass halos, but on the angular scales of greatest astrophysical
interest they are also highly vulnerable to residual foreground contamination. We analyze the cross-
correlation of two low-redshift unWISE galaxy samples with Planck PR4 and ACT DR6 microwave
temperature sky maps directly in harmonic space, fitting simultaneously for correlated tSZ, cosmic
infrared background (CIB), and radio emission.
Using the nine Planck bands to perform model
selection, we find that a three-component model consisting of tSZ, radio emission, and a CIB amplitude
term is strongly preferred over a model that omits radio contamination, with a significance of 9.5σ for
the unWISE Low-z sample and 11σ for the unWISE Mid-z sample respectively. For the unWISE Low-z
sample (⟨z⟩L = 0.14) the preferred Planck fit gives an effective CIB emissivity index β0 = 1.79 ± 0.29,
an effective CIB dust temperature T0 = 22.0 ± 5.1 K, and a radio spectral index βr = −2.18 ± 0.24;
for the unWISE Mid-z sample (⟨z⟩M = 0.23) we find β0 = 1.41 ± 0.13, T0 = 28.0 ± 4.2 K, and
βr = −2.62 ± 0.26. We apply this radio-inclusive recipe to the ACT+Planck 90, 150, and 220 GHz
maps to obtain the best small-scale measurements. This removes the apparent negative galaxy-tSZ
cross-correlation seen in the fiducial ACT DR6 NILC reconstruction and in no-radio fits. The cleaned
tSZ × unWISE spectra remain positive to ℓ≃6000 and are broadly consistent with the Planck-only
reconstruction on overlapping scales. We show that these radio-cleaned spectra are well described by
a conventional halo model calibrated to the galaxy population and a two-parameter generalized NFW
electron pressure profile. Our main conclusion is that radio contamination separation is crucial to
interpreting small-scale tSZ × galaxy measurements.
1. INTRODUCTION
The thermal Sunyaev-Zel’dovich (tSZ) effect is one of the most important probes of hot ionized gas in the late-
time universe. Inverse-Compton scattering of Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) photons by energetic electrons
imprints a characteristic frequency-dependent distortion that is proportional to the line-of-sight integral of the electron
pressure (Sunyaev & Zeldovich 1972; Birkinshaw 1999; Carlstrom et al. 2002). In massive clusters, this effect has long
been used together with X-ray observations to study ICM thermodynamics, pressure profiles, gas fractions, and the
impact of baryonic feedback (Sarazin 1986; Voit 2005; Kravtsov & Borgani 2012; Arnaud et al. 2010; Battaglia et al.
2012a,b, 2013; Eckert et al. 2017; Walker et al. 2019). These same baryonic processes are now understood to have
important consequences for cosmological observables on non-linear scales, motivating feedback-calibrated modeling
frameworks (van Daalen et al. 2011; McCarthy et al. 2017; Henden et al. 2019). The more difficult and arguably more
interesting regime is the lower-mass halo population, where the tSZ signal is weaker but carries direct information
about circumgalactic gas, baryon redistribution, and the missing-baryon problem (Cen & Ostriker 1999; Afshordi et al.
2007; de Graaff et al. 2019; Tanimura et al. 2019; Chadayammuri et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2024a,b).
Galaxy–CMB cross-correlations are a natural way to reach this regime because they amplify the signal from halos
that are too faint to detect individually. The unWISE catalog is especially useful in this context: it provides all-sky

infrared-selected tracers with broad redshift coverage and high surface density (Lang 2014; Schlafly et al. 2019). In
this work, we are particularly interested in probing the tSZ effect at low halo masses, and so rather than using the
unWISE Blue, Green and Red samples used in previous work (Schlafly et al. 2019; Krolewski et al. 2020), we use

On the microwave side, Planck provides broad frequency leverage from 30 to 857 GHz, while Atacama Cosmology
Telescope (ACT) DR6 provides arcminute-scale resolution in the 90, 150, and 220 GHz bands (Naess et al. 2025; Coulton
et al. 2024). Together, they offer a powerful combination: Planck can identify the relevant spectral components, and
ACT can extend the measurement to the scales where halo-pressure modeling becomes most informative.
The main obstacle is that component-separated Compton-y maps are not guaranteed to isolate the true low-redshift
tSZ signal on galaxy scales. Other temperature fluctuations from diffuse components such as Cosmic Infrared Back-
ground (CIB) can bury the true tSZ signal, especially at angular scales ℓ> 1000 (Reichardt et al. 2012). The CIB
is mainly attributed to thermal dust emission, and, due to uncertainty in the CIB’s Spectral Energy Density (SED),
it is difficult to remove via component separation. Both ACT and Planck collaboration made map-level component
separation for tSZ to suppress the contamination using Needlet Internal Linear Combination (NILC) method, includ-
ing additional marginalization over the uncertainty in the CIB spectral index β (McCarthy & Hill 2024; Chandran
et al. 2023; Coulton et al. 2024). However, as discussed by Chandran et al. (2026), the NILC map products still
show residual correlation with CIB and need to be handled with caution in cross-correlation analysis. Recent work by
Liu et al. (2025) also emphasized that small-scale galaxy–y measurements can remain strongly dependent on the CIB
model even after map-level CIB deprojection. For low-redshift, infrared-selected galaxies such as unWISE, there is an
additional concern: radio emission correlated with the galaxy sample can mimic or bias the frequency dependence of
the tSZ decrement at 90–150 GHz. If that contaminant is not removed, a physically sensible positive-pressure signal
can be mistaken for a suppressed or even negative galaxy–y correlation.
In this work, we disentangle the contamination by modeling the galaxy-temperature cross-spectra directly in har-
monic space. We first use the nine Planck bands to compare several spectral recipes and determine which components
are required by the data. We then apply the preferred recipe to the ACT+Planck frequency maps, where the high-
est multipole extends to ℓ≃6000. Then, we test whether the cleaned tSZ × unWISE spectra allow a conventional
halo-model description using a Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) fit to the galaxy clustering and a generalized
Navarro-Frenk-White (GNFW) pressure profile for the gas. Finally, we show the inferred SZ flux–halo mass (YSZ −M)
relation, and compare to different SZ group and cluster observations.

and 220 GHz. In this work, we use the ACT DR6 maps coadded with Planck and with source subtraction applied, in

6. CONCLUSION
Our result using galaxy-single frequency cross-correlations shows that the multi-frequency SED fitting to the Planck
cross-spectra favors a three-component recipe consisting of tSZ, radio emission, and a CIB amplitude term, using the
broad frequency availability of Planck. By BIC standards, the preference against a radio-less recipe is 9.5σ for Low-z
galaxy sample and 11σ for Mid-z galaxy sample.
To extract a physically meaningful galaxy-tSZ cross-correlation, the Planck analysis sets the preferred foreground
model for necessary foreground components before moving to higher-resolution data from ACT. In recipes that omit
radio contamination, the recovered small-scale reconstructed g × y signal can become strongly suppressed or even
spuriously negative. With the preferred SED recipe from Planck cross-correlations, tSZ + Radio + CIB-amplitude,
the spurious negative behavior is removed. By including the radio term, the reconstructed g × y spectrum remains
physically sensible on small angular scales and can be described by a standard halo-model interpretation. We therefore
conclude that radio contamination is not a subdominant correction in this analysis, but a necessary component of the
cleaning procedure.4
The comparison with NILC-based analyses further clarifies this point. As shown in Appendix B, the harmonic-space
component-separation result obtained with the tSZ + CIB + CIB-δβ recipe is consistent with the CIB-deprojected

potentially valuable information about the astrophysics of galaxies and their surrounding environments, including
star-formation activity, non-thermal emission, and feedback processes. In the present work, we focus primarily on the
analysis of the galaxy-tSZ signal. However, with a more robust model of the halo profiles, these same cross-correlations
for CIB and Radio will be more physically informative. An important direction for future work will be to investigate
how these components can be used together with tSZ to constrain feedback and baryonic processes in low-mass halos.
In addition, the current modeling of Radio signal as an effective spectral index could be further improved to extract
physical information about the Radio contributions.
An important limitation of our analysis is the limited frequency in ACT. The limitation to three nominal bands
restricts the maximum component to three, and cannot differentiate between different CIB and Radio spectral param-

CMB observatories such as Simons Observatory (Ade et al. 2019), which observes in 6 nominal bands from 23GHz to

In summary, Fig. 17 should be read as a consistency and sensitivity check. The current analysis robustly constrains
the galaxy × tSZ cross-correlation amplitude, but it does not separately determine the high-mass tail of the HOD dis-
tribution, the intrinsic pressure normalization P0, and the correlation factor ρgy. A calibrated YSZ −Mh interpretation
would require external mass information or an independent prior on ρgy.
