THE STRONG ARNOL’D CHORD CONJECTURE FOR THE
BOUNDARY OF A UNIFORMLY CONVEX DOMAIN IN R4
DYLAN CANT
Abstract. Following the idea of Jungsoo Kang and Jun Zhang, we
prove the strong Arnol’d chord conjecture for the boundary of a uni-
formly convex domain in R4, using an ellipsoid embedding construction
due to Oliver Edtmair. We prove a general structural result for Legen-
drians L which are eventually equivariantly essential (E3), in the sense
that the kth Gutt–Hutchings capacity ck(D∗TL) is infinite for k large
enough. We show that any E3 Legendrian in the boundary of a Liouville
domain Ωbounds a chord of length at most lim inf ck(Ω)/k.
1. Introduction
The strong Arnol’d conjecture is said to hold for a Liouville domain (Ω, λ)
provided each Legendrian L ⊂∂Ωbounds a Reeb chord with distinct end-
points. Here we recall the characteristic foliation of ∂Ωis spanned by a
vector field R satisfying λ(R) = 1, and this vector field is the Reeb flow we
consider throughout this text. In this paper we prove the following:
Theorem 1. If Ω⊂R4 is a uniformly convex domain, then every Legen-
drian L ⊂∂Ωbounds a Reeb chord whose length is strictly smaller than the
minimal positive period of a Reeb orbit. In particular this chord must have
distinct endpoints, and so Ωsolves the strong Arnol’d chord conjecture.
In [Kan26] this result is proved, in R2n for 2n ≥4, under a non-degeneracy
assumption on the Reeb flow (the assumption is that there is a Morse-Bott
component in the space of systoles). Our proof has a different flavour, and
we obtain the result (only in R4) without any assumptions on Ωbesides its
uniform convexity.
The engine behind our upper bound is the sequence of Gutt–Hutchings ca-
pacities ck(Ω) defined in [GH18] (see Definition A.10). The paper [KZ24]
already uses the relationship between Gutt–Hutchings capacities and the
Lagrangian capacity of [CM18] established in [Per22, Per25] to prove some
cases of the strong Arnol’d chord conjecture.1 This paper is a mild variation

To state our next result, we introduce the following class of manifolds:
Definition 2. A compact manifold L is said to be eventually equivariantly
essential (E3) provided that ck(DT ∗L) = ∞for k sufficiently large.
Lemma 3. All compact aspherical manifolds are E3.
Remark 1.2. [Zha16, Corollary 1.1.6, Lemma 4.2.5] and Definition A.6 imply
that spin E3 manifolds are rationally essential.
Theorem 4. Let Ωbe a Liouville domain. If L ⊂∂Ωis an E3 Legendrian
submanifold, then L bounds a Reeb chord whose length is at most the limit
infimum of ck(Ω)/k as k →∞, provided this limit infimum is finite.
If Ωis 4-dimensional, then L is 1-dimensional, so L must be a disjoint union
of circles, and hence L is E3 by Lemma 3. This is what enables us to prove
an unconditional statement in Theorem 1.
In higher dimensions, there are examples of Legendrians which fail the con-
clusion of Theorem 4, and thus are not E3. For instance:
Example 1.3. Any Lagrangian in ¯L ⊂CP n−1 = ∂B(1)/S1 whose rationality
constant ρ(¯L) equals 1/d admits a d-fold Legendrian cover L ⊂∂B(1) whose
minimal chord length is 1/d. In particular if d < n, this lift L is not E3.
This shows Sn−1 is not E3, although this also follows from Remark 1.2. The
example also implies:
Corollary 5 ([CM18, Theorem 1.1]). Any aspherical Lagrangian ¯L in CP n−1
has rationality constant ρ(¯L) at most 1/n.
Proof. If ρ(¯L) ̸= 0, then ρ(¯L) = 1/d for some d, since ¯L bounds some
disk with symplectic area 1. Example 1.3 applies, and we conclude a d-fold
Legendrian lift L →¯L. Via Lemma 3 we conclude that L is E3. Theorem 4
applies to L ⊂∂B(1), implying d ≥n, as desired.

Appendix A. On the Gutt–Hutchings capacities
The goals of this section are threefold:
• review the construction of the Gutt–Hutchings capacities;
• prove the case of the Cartesian product formula (Theorem 10) needed
in the body of the text;
• prove Lemma 3 that aspherical manifolds are E3.
For the history and development of the Gutt–Hutchings capacities, we refer
the reader to [GH18, §5] and the references therein [Vit99, Sei08, BO17,
Gut17]. Other researchers discuss S1-equivariant symplectic homology in a
manner similar to the way we will, e.g., [Zha16, Zho21, Zho22, Li23]. The
framework is based on the theory of pseudo-holomorphic curves [Gro85].
A.1. Filtered symplectic homology for a Liouville domain Ω. For the basic
set-up of symplectic homology, we follow the notations of [BC26]; briefly:

of a compact set;
