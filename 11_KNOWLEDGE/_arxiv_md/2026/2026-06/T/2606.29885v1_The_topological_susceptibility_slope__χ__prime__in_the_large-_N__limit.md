This paper presents the first non-perturbative lattice determination of the Yang–Mills topological
susceptibility slope χ′ in the large-N limit. This quantity represents the O(p2) term of the momen-
tum expansion of the topological charge density two-point correlator, and has important theoretical
and phenomenological implications for strong interactions.
This calculation is based on a novel
algorithm that avoids topological freezing at large N on fine lattices, and on a novel method to
reliably compute χ′ on the lattice. The results of this study are relevant for the description of the
proton spin in deep inelastic scattering experiments via the Shore–Veneziano formula.

LATTICE METHODS
To compute χ′
YM at large N, simulations were per-
formed for N = 3, 4, 5, 6 using the standard plaque-
tte Yang–Mills action on an hypercubic periodic lattice,

keeping a constant lattice volume ℓ4 ≃(1.5 fm)4 in all
cases (the conversion from lattice to physical units of
dimensionful quantities will be done via the string ten-
sion σ).
For all values of N, 4-5 values of the lattice
spacing a were considered to enable a continuum limit
extrapolation.
Simulated values of the lattice spacing
were drawn from a uniform range across all N values,
0.1 fm ≳a ≳0.05 fm, to ensure that lattice artifacts
are under control as the large-N limit is approach. This
choice would be computationally prohibitive when adopt-
ing standard local Monte Carlo algorithms due to the
infamous topological freezing problem [42–45].
When approaching the continuum limit a →0, the
autocorrelation time τQ of the topological charge Q =
R
d4x q(x) (i.e., the number of Monte Carlo steps nec-
essary to draw two uncorrelated samples of Q) grows
rapidly as a function of 1/a and of N.
This makes
it unfeasible with standard methods to reliably sam-
ple the topological charge distribution at large N even
at coarse lattice spacing.
While in principle χ′, being
the second moment of the two-point function ⟨q(x)q(0)⟩,
could in principle be computed even in the presence of
a fixed topological charge, its value turns out in prac-
tice to be strongly correlated with the topological back-
ground [23, 29]. Thus, a sampling problem in Q would
lead to a large bias on the actual physical value of χ′,
manifesting as somewhat large power-like finite-size ef-
fects instead of exponentially-suppressed ones [46].
To circumvent this problem, I adopted the Parallel
Tempering on Boundary Conditions algorithm [47–49].
This algorithm efficiently combines simulations with pe-
riodic boundary conditions (PBC) and open boundary
conditions (OBC) in a parallel tempering framework, al-
lowing to dramatically mitigate the N- and a-scaling of
τQ, and thus to avoid topological freezing in the parame-
ter space explored here. This algorithm was shown in [50]
to outperform, at fixed computational effort, both simu-
lations done with PBC or OBC. In that study, the algo-
rithm was employed to compute the topological suscepti-
bility χYM in the large-N limit from finer lattice spacings
compared to those employed in previous PBC and OBC
studies [51–56]. Here, the same simulations of [50] are
used for the computation of χ′
YM. More technical details
are reported in the Supplemental Material [31].

A.3 The topological susceptibility slope and topological freezing

q(x), turns out to be strongly correlated with the global topological background of the gauge configuration Q =
