1. Introduction and results
Frames generated by operator orbits are a central model in dynamical sampling; see [2, 3]
for the basic dynamical-sampling viewpoint and background survey. As such, they have
generated considerable interest and appear in a large body of recent research (see [3, and
references therein]). Despite their rigidity, normal-operator orbits are of particular interest, as
they can be analyzed with a richer variety of tools and are potentially better implementable
in applications [27]. The problem solved in this manuscript originates in the work of Aldroubi,
Cabrelli, Çakmak, Molter, and Petrosyan [1], which is a prime example of an analysis of
iterative actions of normal operators. We also note the work of Philipp, who studied Bessel
orbits of normal operators in [30], as well as the finite-generator and finite-index research
which appears in [7, 8].
The normalized-orbit question is closely related to the broader problem of rescaling a
sequence of vectors so that it becomes a frame. For this point of view see Yu’s work on
frame-normalizable sequences [34]. A recent preprint of Yu [35] studies spectral restrictions
for normal operators whose iterative systems can be rescaled to frames. The present example
is diagonal and has pure point spectrum inside the open unit disk, with accumulation on the
unit circle. Thus it is designed to avoid the continuous spectrum obstructions emphasized in
[35].
For the unnormalized diagonal-contraction version of the problem, the standard Hardy-space
model brings in Carleson interpolation and normalized reproducing kernels. The classical
references are Carleson’s interpolation theorem [9] and the Shapiro–Shields characterization
of interpolating sequences for Hardy spaces [32]; useful book references include Garnett [19],

The construction forces the normalized orbit to spend a long interval of time almost entirely
inside one block, where it runs through a Fourier basis. A subsequence is therefore a small
perturbation of an orthonormal basis, while a Schur-test estimate gives the Bessel bound for
the whole sequence. This construction illustrates that the orbits of normal operators provide
enough flexibility for frame generation, unlike those of self-adjoint operators, which lack the
possibility of spectral rotation.
The penultimate section of this manuscript records a related perturbation result: an
arbitrarily small diagonal normal perturbation of the operator constructed in Theorem 1
produces an unnormalized operator-orbit Carleson frame.
