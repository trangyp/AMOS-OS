THE PARITY OF INVARIANT CHARACTERISTICS
LINDEN DISNEY-HOGG
ID
Abstract. We demonstrate a method to prove that a theta characteristic on
Riemann surface S invariant under the action of G ≤Aut(S) has even parity by
considering the representation theory of the double cover ˜G = 2 · G. We apply
this to certain Hurwitz curves, and prove a recent conjecture of Broughton &
Disney-Hogg.
1. Introduction
A theta characteristic on a (smooth, compact, connected) Riemann surface S is
a line bundle L →S such that L⊗2 is isomorphic to KS the canonical bundle. The
parity of a theta characteristic q(L) := dim Γ(L) mod 2(= dim H0(S, L) mod 2)
is an important invariant under holomorphic deformations of L →S [Ati71]. Given
a symplectic basis for the homology H1(S, Z), one may express theta characteristics
as binary vectors (u, v) ∈Z2g where g = g(S) is the genus of the surface, whereby
the parity is simply q = u · v [Joh80]. Historically, odd and even theta characteris-
tics arose from the consideration of odd and even theta functions on the Jacobian
of S; the modern study was given new impetus when Atiyah showed that theta
characteristics are equivalent to spin structures on a Riemann surface [Ati71]. For
a more complete history of theta characteristics and their modern application, see
[Far12].
The number of theta characteristics on a curve of genus g is 22g, 2g−1(2g −1) odd
and 2g−1(2g + 1) even, which are permuted by the induced action of G ≤Aut(S).
Throughout we shall assume g ≥2, so that Aut(S) is finite. Atiyah initially studied
characteristics invariant under f ∈G [Ati71], and recently a more comprehensive
survey was made in [BDH25], which amongst other results classified which Hur-
witz curves with simple automorphism group had a unique invariant characteristic,
though the parity of these invariant characteristics was not determined. Knowing
the parity of invariant characteristics on a curve has been applied to, for example,
determine K-stability of associated Fano threefolds [CLMP24].
The reason that [BDH25] was able to prove existence of unique invariant charac-
teristics on Hurwitz curves but not determine their parity is because of a disconnect
between the two main current methods for studying theta characteristics.

New efficient computational tools have increased the range of genera for which
approach (1) is useful [BDH26]. In that paper the family of modular curves X(p)
(p an odd prime, p ≥7) was considered, for which approach (2) may be used to
prove the existence of a unique invariant characteristic. Given that the parity of
this characteristic is even for p = 7, 11, 13, 17 (determined through approach (1)),
the following conjecture was made.
Conjecture 1.2 ([BDH26]). Let p be an odd prime, p ≥7. The unique invariant
theta characteristic on the modular curve X(p) is always even.
We shall prove this conjecture by demonstrating a method which uses the rep-
resentation theory of the double cover ˜G = 2 · G. The method will be sufficiently
general to be able to determine the parity of the invariant theta characteristics on
all Hurwitz curves with simple automorphism group. In particular we will prove
the following.
Theorem 1.3. If S is a Hurwitz curve with simple automorphism group and unique
invariant characteristic L, then L is even.

3. Application to known curves
3.1. A5. The smallest perfect group is G = A5, which also has H2(G, C×) = C2
corresponding to the double cover 2 · A5 →A5. We consider two actions of this
group on curves of low genus.
(1) A5 acts with signature (0; 2, 5, 5) on a surface S of genus 4: this is Bring’s
curve, which has full automorphism S5 [BDH23]. Bring’s curve is known to
have a unique even characteristic invariant under the action of A5 (indeed
also under the full S5), and the method laid out in §2 again verifies this
fact.
(2) A5 acts with signature (0; 3, 3, 5) on a hyperelliptic surface of genus 5.
This curve admits a unique invariant characteristic which is odd [BDH25],
demonstrating that the condition (4) on the parity of F as determined by
A is not vacuous.
3.2. X(p). In the case considered in Conjecture 1.2, S = X(p) has full automor-
phism group G = PSL(2, p), which is simple and so necessarily perfect, acting with
signature (0; 2, 3, p) on S. Moreover, H2(G, C×) = C2 corresponding to the double
cover SL(2, p) →PSL(2, p). As such, all the necessary conditions are satisfied, and
the conjecture of [BDH26] is proven.

those with maximal automorphism group for their genus, namely having |Aut(S)| =
84(g −1), which enforces that the signature of the action is (0; 2, 3, 7). Hurwitz
curves have perfect automorphism group [Con90], and those with simple automor-
