ABSTRACT
Discovery of dark-matter (DM) free dwarf galaxies in the NGC 1052 neighborhood has had a considerable impact on modern cosmol-
ogy. They have been explained through a dwarf–dwarf head-on collision that is a rare event. We find that they could alternatively be
associated with a head-on, 1:1 merger after it has been tuned to generate the E4 morphology of NGC 1052. Our simulations show that
such mergers produce long-lived tidal features, associated with the remnant galaxy, and in the form of large tidal tails including tidal
dwarf galaxies (TDGs). We underline that such tidal features are predicted by the hierarchical scenario in which massive galaxies are
formed by galaxy mergers. The latter can reproduce both the tidal features in the NGC1052 outskirts and the observed dwarf galaxies.
The simulated TDGs have similar sizes to those observed, while they are ten times smaller in the dwarf bullet scenario. However,
we cannot reproduce the luminous globular cluster systems due to resolution limitations. Resolving the radial distance between the
DM-free dwarfs is necessary to identify the scenario of their formation. We suggest that there should be many other examples of
DM-free dwarf galaxies in the neighborhood of local massive galaxies and galaxy groups.
Key words. Elliptical galaxies – dark matter – merger – dynamics
1. Introduction
The discovery of DM-deficient dwarf galaxies in the NGC 1052
group has attracted considerable attention and generated many
debates in the last 8 years (van Dokkum et al. 2018, 2019).
For example, Monelli & Trujillo (2019) argued that the mass
based on dynamics of surrounding globular clusters (GCs) of
NGC1052-DF2 could have been underestimated due to a dis-
tance overestimate. However, a more accurate distance based on
the tip of red-giant branch (TRGB) stars has been obtained (Shen
et al. 2021), confirming the distance of two DM deficient dwarfs
(DF2 and DF4) at ∼20 Mpc (see also Tang et al. 2026).
Galaxies are known to grow within a dominant DM halo in
the current cosmological frame. To reconcile the latter with the
absence of DM in some dwarf galaxies, Silk (2019) proposed
the bullet dwarf collision scenario, in which two dwarfs are ex-
periencing a collision sufficiently energetic to generate DM-free
galaxies. The scenario has been explored by several numerical
simulations (Lee et al. 2024, and references therein). Lee et al.
(2024) have shown from comparison with cosmological simula-
tions that such an event could occur, despite being a rare event in
the field. Their model approximately reproduces the remarkable
alignment of at least five additional dwarfs with the line drawn
from NGC1054-DF2 to DF4 , both on sky (van Dokkum et al.
2022) and in radial velocity (Keim et al. 2025) projections.
The alignment may also suggest a TDG origin since many
tidal tails formed during galaxy merger episodes show an ex-
tended linear appearance. The NGC 1052 group is made of a
core including 4 massive galaxies within a projected radius of
150 kpc, which favors a rich merger history. NGC 1052 is a mas-
sive elliptical galaxy that shows signs of tidal perturbations such
as a narrow and linear stellar stream that points near NGC1052-
DF2 (Müller et al. 2019). Using numerical simulations, Bour-
naud & Duc (2006) showed that tidal satellites should be found
more frequently around early-type galaxies. The formation of an
elliptical galaxy has been modeled by, e.g., Wang et al. (2020)
to reproduce Cen A properties. Both Cen A and NGC 1052 have
Seyfert 2 type active nuclei and massive black holes ((Wang et al.
2020; Koss et al. 2022), whose formation and growth are likely
related to mergers (Hopkins et al. 2006).
In this paper we investigate whether the chain of DM-
deficient dwarfs could be associated with a tidal tail, which
would have been formed during a merger similar to that de-
scribed by Wang et al. (2020). The goal is not to fully reproduce
the observations of the NGC 1052 field, but to verify whether
this hypothesis may be compelling in comparison with the bullet
dwarf model described by Lee et al. (2024) and by Keim et al.
(2025). Section 2 describes the mechanism and associated pa-
rameters necessary to form an elliptical galaxy such as Cen A
and NGC 1052. Section 3 shows how such a model may generate
tidal tails and associated tidal dwarfs and it compares how real-
istically it may reproduce the observations. Section 4 compares
and discusses strengths and weaknesses of the two competitive
models, which result is summarized in Section 5.

(Hammer et al. 2025). More generally it is expected that gas-rich

The formation of elliptical galaxies requires a different
scheme, because in that case the goal is to minimize the resid-
ual angular momentum in the merger remnant, and this has been
successfully realized by Wang et al. (2020). Because such a sim-
ulation provides very elongated tidal tails, we decided to adopt
their Model-6 (see Table 1 of Wang et al. 2020 for which the
initial gas-to-baryon fraction is 20%) with very few adaptations
for generating Model-6a, i.e., reducing the mass of each parti-
cle by a factor 2, because the stellar mass of NGC 1052 (∼1011
M⊙, Forbes et al. 2019) is approximately half that of Cen A, and
also slightly reduces the initial fraction of DM to 80% (instead
of 90%) to align our modeling to that of M31. Our modeling
is intended to be indicative of the structures formed during the
major merger that has occurred in the NGC 1052 past history. It
does not intend to present an accurate reproduction of this galaxy
(see Section 4). To adapt our simulations1 to the ages of the DM-
deficient dwarfs (8.9 Gyr for DF2 and DF4, Fensch et al. 2019),
we have considered simulation durations of more than 10 Gyr
after the merger conclusion.

3. Reproduction of the main observed properties in
the NGC 1052 field
Figure 1 shows a remnant elliptical galaxy 8.4 Gyr after the
merger with tidal features and with TDGs in the field. As in
Wang et al. (2020), two almost aligned tidal tails are generated,
with a total extent that can reach ∼1 Mpc. Here, we have chosen
to present a configuration that may resemble NGC 1052 and its
accompanying dwarfs, namely DF2, DF4 and DF7 for illustrat-
ing that a tail may encompass the whole field of view discussed
in Keim et al. (2025), which is larger than the one-degree field
observed by Müller et al. (2019).

4. Discussion
We have verified that the properties of the DM-deficient dwarfs
in the NGC 1052 field can be explained by TDGs populating
tidal tails coming from the major merger resulting in a giant el-
liptical galaxy such as NGC 1052. We compare this scenario
with that of the bullet-dwarf promoted by Silk (2019). We no-
tice that both scenarios have similarities, i.e., (1) for both kinds
of progenitors the collision is producing tidal material that is
DM free, i.e., TDGs, and (2) the collision of the two progeni-
tor dwarfs should be head-on as well as that of the two galaxy
progenitors of NGC 1052. Despite this, and for clarity reasons,
we continue to name both alternatives, the TDG and the bullet-
dwarf scenarios, respectively.

The estimated 3D distance of 1.7±0.5 Mpc between DF2 and
DF4 (Shen et al. 2023) would mean that the trail of dwarfs is
extending almost only along the line of sight. This estimate is
essential for the bullet-dwarf scenario, because it is the only way
to report for an ancient-dwarf collision consistent with the DF2
age, while it could be fatal for the TDG scenario, which predicts
a tidal elongated along all directions but limited by ∼1 Mpc
in length. During the refereeing process, a new study by Tang
et al. (2026) has changed the TRGB distance estimate of DF2
from 21.2±1.7 (Shen et al. 2023) to 17.6±0.6 Mpc, the latter
value being based on robust JWST observations. Such a consid-
erable change put serious doubts about the ability to determine
the radial distance from DF2 to DF4 using the former HST mea-
surements. On the other hand, according to Tang et al. (2026)’s
Table 1, DF2 and DF4 could have the same distance within less
than 1σ. However, much deeper photometric data from JWST
are necessary for a robust estimate of the distance between DF2
and DF4.
In this letter, we did not search for a precise reproduction
of the observations, but only to establish whether or not a TDG
scenario could be plausible2. This is because (1) distances of all
the concerned galaxies require TRGB measurements with JWST,
(2) some features such as TDG number, positions and velocities
vary temporally, (3) the absence of knowledge about the NGC
1052 merger and star formation histories, and (4), that the pa-
rameter space is much more gigantic than that of the collision of
two dwarfs, for which Lee et al. (2024) has tested more than 1.5
million simulations. The 3 models (see Table A.1) shown here
are of the same family, i.e., we only change very few parameters
in passing from a model to another. One can be surprised that
this small exploration of parameters presented here provides re-
sults as consistent as those of Lee et al. (2024) for reproducing
DM-deficient dwarf properties, as well as their potential align-
ment.
The TDGs produced in our modeling are faint, extended, and
with low surface brightness, while Lee et al. (2024) instead re-
cover a compact DF2 and for all but one case, a compact DF4.
Depending on the model parameters, the recovered TDGs may
have masses similar to those of DF2 and DF4. This is in agree-
ment with observations (Kaviraj et al. 2012) establishing that for
merger remnant mass like that of M31 or NGC 1052, the TDG
stellar mass can reach 3 ×109 M⊙(Fouquet et al. 2012).
We are not able to reproduce the population of GCs sur-
rounding DF2 and DF4 because of a technical resolution issue3.
However, mergers of galaxies are accompanied by a period of
enhanced star formation. This process can lead to the formation
of star clusters, some of which may survive as GCs (Valenzuela
et al. 2024) if they remain outside the dense regions, as for ex-
ample if they are transported in a tidal tail. The over-luminous
dwarf GCs appear to support the bullet-dwarf scenario4.

which is essential for the TDG scenario. However, the offset is
explained by our modeling because TDG1 (alias DF2), which
is the closest dwarf in projection to NGC 1052, is coming back
from a tidal tail and starts to be captured and then orbiting around
the giant elliptical. Perhaps the main argument against a TDG
interpretation is related to their supposed metal richness while
DF2 and DF4 are metal poor. However, this cannot apply if NGC
1052 is a several Gyr old-merger that would form TDGs inherit-

itor, which could be low at high redshift (Recchi et al. 2015).
Both scenarios predict progenitor remnant(s), which is NGC
1052 for the TDG scenario. For the bullet-dwarf scenario, RCP
32 and DF7 have been invoked by van Dokkum et al. (2022) to
be the progenitor dwarf remnants, however they are reproduced
in the Lee et al. (2024, see the bottom row of their Figure 5)
simulations with similar stellar surface-brightnesses than that of
DF2 or DF4. In particular, RCP32 has a central surface bright-
ness 3 magnitudes fainter than that of DF2 or DF4 (Román et al.
2021). Reproducing it as a bullet-dwarf progenitor would be an
astonishing result, because it is uneasy to figure out how a dwarf
can keep its DM-content but lose all its baryons after a very vio-
lent collision.
5. Conclusions
In this Letter, we have verified that a major merger experienced
by NGC 1052 produces tidal tails and TDGs that match the
intrinsic properties of DM-deficient dwarfs discovered by van
Dokkum et al. (2018, 2019), as well as their alignment along a
trail. This results from simple adaptations of the N-body hydro-
dynamical simulations made by Wang et al. (2020) to reproduce
the properties of Cen A, and we have addressed the supposed
failures of the TDG scenario. The only limitation of our model-
ing is coming from its very large parameter space together with
insufficiently precise constraints on NGC 1052 and associated
dwarfs, which prevents us from experimenting with a more ac-
curate reproduction of the observations.
According to the hierarchical scenario, the present-day E4
morphology of NGC 1052 takes its origin from its last ma-
jor merger, which requires radial orbits, moderate gas fraction,
mass ratio close to 1:1, and specific orientations of the progeni-
tor galaxies (Wang et al. 2020). The latter conditions are neces-
sary to remove most of the angular momentum in the remnant,
which is currently accompanied by large tidal tails and TDGs,
and match better the observations when compared to the bullet-
dwarf scenario.
The bullet-dwarf scenario suffers from two major difficul-
ties, first to reproduce the dwarf progenitors, and second, to pro-
vide far too compact DM-deficient dwarfs. It also neglects that
(2026) identified DF9 as DM deficient from its stellar velocity disper-
sion since it possesses a single central GC. Thus, bullet-dwarf scenario
is favored, but one cannot exclude a specific TDG scenario providing
over-luminous GCs in some DM-deficient dwarfs.
NGC 1052 is an active nucleus E4 galaxy showing tides, which
has likely experienced a major merger that can produce TDGs
resembling the observed DM-deficient dwarfs. The likely oc-
currence of massive galaxy mergers near NGC 1052 has to be
compared to the very rare dwarf-dwarf collision events in the
field, for which only 10 of them are found in a (100Mpc)3 vol-
ume from z=3 to z=0 (Lee et al. 2024). Future observations of
dwarf radial distances will probe which of the two scenarios is
the most likely. The dwarf GC over-luminosity appears to sup-
port the bullet-dwarf scenario, though there might be subtle se-
lection effects associated with this property.
The above makes plausible an association of DM-deficient
dwarfs and their trail with TDGs lying in a tidal tail formed dur-
ing a major merger, whose remnant is consistent with the E4
morphology of NGC 1052. It would be welcome to estimate bet-
ter when NGC 1052 experienced its last merger, which is uncon-
strained due to the lack of observations for establishing its star
formation history. This Letter demonstrates that the numerous
mergers that massive galaxies have experienced in their past may
have also formed TDGs (Barnes & Hernquist 1992; Okazaki &
Taniguchi 2000). The detection of DM-deficient dwarfs in the
NGC 1052 field may confirm that TDGs can survive 8 Gyr after
their formation, and they may represent a non-negligible part of
the dwarf population (see also Pawlowski et al. 2014).

1052 center
NGC 1052 center shows a fast rotating (Vrot= 200 kms−1) gas
component (van Gorkom et al. 1986), which represents less than
one percent of its baryonic mass. Such a component is likely re-
sponsible for the tiny recent star formation in the very inner part
of NGC 1052, with ages varying from 1 to 4 Gyr (Fernández-
Ontiveros et al. 2011). Perhaps surprisingly, 8.4 Gyr after the
merger occurrence, the simulated galaxy still shows the pres-
ence of rotating gas and of recent star formation in the center.
Many elliptical galaxies present residual HI gas in their center,
and like Cen A, NGC 1052 shows a gas-disk orientation that
is almost perpendicular to the major optical axis (van Gorkom
et al. 1986). Wang et al. (2020) was able to reproduce a simi-
lar behavior for Cen A, for a shorter post-merger epoch than for
NGC 1052. Recent simulations (Porter et al. 2026) have shown
that the gas component may survive and star formation may be
mostly prevented because of Coriolis forces that let the (light)
gas component to precess.
To test whether the gas rotating disk can be found on an axis
almost perpendicular to the simulated stellar ellipsoid that repre-
sents NGC 1052, we explored a range of merger orbital param-
eters (pericenters and inclination of progenitor galaxies), requir-
ing the model to also reproduce the general properties of NGC
1052 and the alignment of the dwarf galaxies. Our goal has been
to warrant the formation of a perpendicular residual gas disk as
being strongly linked to the progenitor spins, favoring highly ret-
rograde and polar orientations with respect to the orbital angular
momentum. Here, we show one of such model (Model-6b) in
which we have decreased the pericenter to 7 kpc (instead of 20
kpc), and by flipping the orientations of the two galaxies (see
Table A.1). The first goal has been realized, i.e., the gas rotat-
ing disk can be found on an axis almost perpendicular to the
simulated NGC 1052, and interestingly, we have improved the
TDG mass, velocities, and orientation of the NGC 1052 stellar
halo (compare Figure A.1 to Figure 1). Figure A.1 shows how
this adapted model reproduces the observations, but at an epoch
closer to the merger (4.7 Gyr instead of 8.4 Gyr). However, since
few stars have been recently formed in such a gas-poor model,
we have verified that most of the TDG stars are very old and con-
sistent with the Fensch et al. (2019) age of 8.9± 1.8 Gyr. More-
over, this simulation provides TDGs with large stellar masses
that are even larger than that of DF2, DF4 and DF7 (see Table 1).
This can be explained by the fact that aggressive radial orbits
are accompanied by large tidal tails in order to remove most of
the residual angular momentum. Notice also that TDG3b is very
elongated as it is observed for DF7 by Keim et al. (2025). We
also notice that the shape of the HI emission may included some
distorted features, which could have been taken as an evidence
for a more recent merger event.
Besides this, we do not analyze the gas content of the
TDGs, because in a galaxy group such as that of NGC 1052
the ram pressure stripping provides a means of depleting the
low-density gas reservoir (see Silk 2019, and references therein).
