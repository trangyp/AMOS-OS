1. Introduction
An important step towards the categorical local Langlands conjecture as formulated by Fargues–
Scholze in [FS24] was Fargues’ observation that Deligne’s geometric proof of unramified global class field
theory, when carried over to the Fargues–Fontaine curve, yields local class field theory. More recently,
by adapting techniques introduced by Camargo in [Cam24], Scholze made significant progress towards
a geometrisation of the local Langlands correspondence over archimedean local fields. In particular, in
[Sch24], he introduced the key definition of families of twistor-P1s, which are the archimedean analogue
of relative Fargues–Fontaine curves.

We note that Theorem 1.1 is an archimedean analogue of Fargues’ result from [Far20] that rank one
ℓ-adic ´etale local systems on the stack Div1,FF of degree 1 divisors on the Fargues–Fontaine curve are
pulled back along the Abel–Jacobi map. His proof strategy is the following: Given a rank one local
system L on Div1,FF, its d-th symmetric power L⊠d yields a line bundle on Symd Div1,FF ∼= Divd,FF.
Since the fibres of the d-th Abel–Jacobi map AJd,FF : Divd,FF →Picd,FF are simply connected for
d ≥2, one can then descend L⊠d to Picd,FF ∼= Pic1,FF.
In our context, there are two reasons this strategy fails. The first of these is that Divd is not
isomorphic to the d-th symmetric power Symd Div1 of Div1, see Example 4.13. The second is that,
since we are working with quasi-coherent instead of ℓ-adic sheaves, to obtain descent of line bundles
along the Abel–Jacobi map, we would need the fibres to be “contractible” instead of just simply
connected; however, the map AJd is only “(d −1)-connective”. To address the first issue, we use
the following simple idea motivated from algebraic geometry: For any d, we single out an open
locus Divd
HT≤1 in Divd whose complement has “codimension two” and over which the natural map
Symd Div1 →Divd is an isomorphism; then we show that vector bundles extend uniquely from Divd
HT≤1
to all of Divd. Meanwhile, the solution to the second issue is to pass to an Abel–Jacobi map “at
infinite level”: we assemble all the degree d Abel–Jacobi maps into a single one, which will then have
contractible fibers.
We caution the reader, however, that this discussion should only be regarded as an intuition: Indeed,
since we are working with analytic stacks throughout the paper, we have no robust notion of dimension,
fundamental group or codimension and hence none of the arguments sketched above are purely formal.
