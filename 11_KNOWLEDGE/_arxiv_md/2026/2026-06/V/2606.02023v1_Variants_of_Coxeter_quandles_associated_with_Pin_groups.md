Abstract. We introduce two families of quandles arising from Coxeter quan-
dles. One, which we call a double covering, is the set of roots with binary
operation deﬁned by using the negatives of reﬂections. The double covering is
realized as a conjugation quandle in a Pin group. The other, which we call a
rotational Dn quandle, is the set of some right angle rotations in the Coxeter
group of type Dn with binary operation given by conjugation. We determine
their inner automorphism groups, and observe that they are quite similar.

2. Quandles
Deﬁnition 2.1. A quandle is a set X with a binary operation ∗: X × X →X
satisfying
(Q1) x ∗x = x (x ∈X),
(Q2) ∀y ∈X, ∗y : X →X,
x 7→x ∗y is a bijection,
(Q3) (x ∗y) ∗z = (x ∗z) ∗(y ∗z)
(x, y, z ∈X).
If it satisﬁes the axioms (Q2) and (Q3) but may not (Q1), (X, ∗) is called a rack.
Let G be a group. For g, h ∈G, we deﬁne ∗by g ∗h = h−1gh. It is easy to check
that ∗satisﬁes (Q1) and (Q3). Thus, for a subset S ⊂G closed under conjugation
(i.e. g, h ∈S implies h−1gh, hgh−1 ∈S), (S, ∗) is a quandle.
For quandles X, Y , a map f : X →Y is called a homomorphism if it satisﬁes
f(x∗y) = f(x)∗f(y). A bijective homomorphism is called an isomorphism. Remark
that the inverse map of an isomorphism is also an isomorphism. Thus the set of
all isomorphisms X →X forms a group with multiplication given by composition
of isomorphisms. This groups is called the automorphism group of X, and denoted
by Aut(X).
For any y ∈X, ∗y : X →X x 7→x∗y is an isomorphism by (Q2) and (Q3). The
subgroup of Aut(X) generated by {∗y | y ∈X} is called an inner automorphism
group, and denoted by Inn(X). If X is ﬁnite, Aut(X) and Inn(X) are explicitly

6. Vendramin’s classification
Vendramin classiﬁed connected quandles of order ≤35 in [Ven12]. The list was
expanded in [RIG] for connected quandles of order ≤47, Using Vendramin’s GAP
package RIG, we determined QW , DQW , Qr
Dn in the Vendramin’s list. The results
are collected in Table 1. In the table, Cn is the cyclic group, An is the alternating
group, Sn is the symmetric group, and A : B is a semi-direct product A ⋊B. We
remark that |QAn| = |DQAn|/2 = n(n + 1)/2 and |QDn| = |DQDn|/2 = n(n −1)/2
since the order of DQW coincides with the number of roots of W. Some of them are
described as Galkin quandles in [HSV16], which were introduced in [CEHSY13].
We also collect the inner automorphism groups of QW , DQW and ΦW for excep-
tional ﬁnite Coxeter groups in Table 2. We remark that Inn(ΦW ) ∼= W by Lemma
3.1.
It is known that the symmetric group S6 has a triple cover. So QA5 might have a
kind of triple cover. Since |QA5| = 15, the expected triple cover must have order 45.
In Vendramin’s list, Q45,29 is the only non-faithful connected quandle of order 45.
Moreover Inn(Q45,29) ∼= S6 and the map Q →Inn(Q45,29) is 3-to-1. Thus Q45,29
might be a kind of triple cover of QA5.
