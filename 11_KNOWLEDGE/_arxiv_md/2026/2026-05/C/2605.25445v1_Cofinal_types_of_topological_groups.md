COFINAL TYPES OF TOPOLOGICAL GROUPS
XUAN GONG AND DEKUI PENG*
Abstract. We investigate the local topological structure of non-metrizable topological groups
through the lens of Tukey order and cofinal types. Motivated by recent advances in topological
groups admitting an ωω-base, we introduce the fineness index, denoted fi(P), for arbitrary
directed partially ordered sets. This cardinal invariant fundamentally generalizes the bounding
number b by capturing the exact threshold where a poset evades domination by its countable
subsets, thereby establishing a universal lower bound for the character of topological groups
with a P-base: χ(G) ∈{1, ω} ∪[fi(P), cof(P)].
Furthermore, we resolve a structural problem regarding the exact cofinal types of free topo-
logical groups over uniform spaces. While classical results by Nickolas, Tkachenko, and others
successfully computed the character of these groups via cardinal equalities (e.g., χ(F(X, U)) =
cof(Uω)), lifting these equalities to strict Tukey equivalences has remained a persistent combina-
torial challenge. By developing the novel machinery of neat trees to refine uniform covering trees,
we overcome the structural obstructions and prove the Tukey equivalence Ne(F(X, U)) =T Uω
for any compact uniform space (X, U).
1. Introduction
The study of neighborhood bases at the identity in topological groups has long served as a
vital intersection between general topology, topological algebra, and combinatorial set theory.
Given a topological group G, the neighborhood filter at the identity, denoted by Ne(G) and
ordered by reverse inclusion, forms a directed partially ordered set (poset). The combinatorial
complexity of this poset intrinsically encodes profound structural properties of the group. A
remarkably delicate and powerful tool for classifying these local structures is the Tukey order.
For two directed posets P and Q, we say P is Tukey below Q, denoted P ≤T Q, if there exists
a map from Q to P carrying cofinal sets to cofinal sets.
In recent years, the local structure of non-metrizable topological groups has attracted signif-
icant attention, particularly the class of groups admitting a P-base (i.e., when there exists a
cofinal, order-preserving map from a directed poset P to Ne(G)), see for example [6, 8, 14, 16].
A celebrated instance is the class of topological groups with an ωω-base [2, 3, 10, 17, 18].
Initiated in the context of uniform spaces by Cascales and Orihuela [4], and systematically
explored for topological groups by Gabriyelyan, Kąkol, and Leiderman [10], it was shown that
the character χ(G) of a non-metrizable group with an ωω-base must fall within the interval
[b, d]. The question of whether the lower bound b can be attained was affirmatively resolved
by Leiderman, Pestov, and Tomita [17], who constructed a group whose neighborhood base is
Tukey equivalent to any given regular cardinal Tukey below ωω.
Motivated by these deep results, the first objective of this paper is to seek an intrinsically
defined cardinal invariant for general directed posets that dictates the strict lower bounds of
character and pseudocharacter. We introduce the fineness index, denoted fi(P). This invariant
elegantly isolates the critical threshold at which a poset ceases to be dominated by its countable
subsets.

The second major focus of this paper confronts a structural problem regarding the exact

establishing this equivalence has proven highly non-trivial due to the rigid requirement of Tukey
maps to preserve cofinality across all scales, an obstruction that cardinal equalities inherently
ignore.
In this paper, we bridge this gap for a broad class of uniform spaces. The crux of our approach
lies in overcoming the combinatorial rigidity of standard uniform covering trees. We introduce
the mechanism of neat trees, a refinement that meticulously orchestrates the branch structures
to bypass the “product traps” that traditionally obstruct Tukey embeddings. By deploying
this machinery, we establish that for any add(U)-precompact uniform space (X, U), the strict
equivalence Ne(F(X, U)) =T Uω is achieved. Especially, this equivalence holds for any compact
space.
Finally, we translate this abstract Tukey equivalence into exact evaluations for specific topo-
logical classes. To achieve this, we invoke Shelah’s Strong Hypothesis (SSH). Arising from pcf
(possible cofinalities) theory, SSH asserts that the pseudo-power pp(λ) = λ+ for every singular
cardinal λ. Utilizing SSH, we precisely evaluate the cofinality of the countable power of [τ]<ω
and collapse the product space, proving that for an infinite precompact uniform space of weight
τ with cof(τ) > ω, both Ne(F(X, U)) and Ne(A(X, U)) are exactly Tukey equivalent to [τ]<ω.
1.1. Tukey order of (pre-)ordered sets. Let P be a set. A quasi-order (or pre-order) ≤
on P is a reflexive and transitive binary relation. If ≤is also antisymmetric, then it is called a
partial order. In this case, the pair (P, ≤) is called a partially ordered set (briefly, a poset).
A pre-ordered set (P, ≤) is called directed if for every x, y ∈P there exists z ∈P such that
x ≤z and y ≤z.
For A, B ⊆P, we say that A is dominated by B if for every a ∈A there exists b ∈B such
that a ≤b.
Let (P, ≤) be a directed pre-ordered set. A subset A ⊆P is called
• cofinal in P if P is dominated by A;
• bounded in P if A is dominated by a single point of P.
We associate two cardinals with P:
• the cofinality
cof(P) = min{ |A| : A is cofinal in P };
• the additivity
add(P) = min{ |A| : A is unbounded in P }.
Note that a pre-ordered set may have no nonempty unbounded subset; this happens precisely
when P has a (not necessarily unique) largest element. In this case, we put add(P) = 1, which
is equivalent to cof(P) = 1.
It is easy to see that if add(P) ̸= 1, then add(P) is a regular cardinal and add(P) ≤cof(P).
The well-known cardinal characteristics b and d are precisely the additivity and the cofinality
of (ωω, ≤∗), respectively, where f ≤∗g means that f(n) ≤g(n) for all but finitely many n ∈ω.

We now introduce a central notion in the theory of directed posets, due to Tukey [26].
Let (P, ≤P) and (Q, ≤Q) be directed pre-ordered sets.
We say that Q is Tukey above P,
or equivalently that P is Tukey below Q, and write P ≤T Q, if there exists a cofinal map
φ : Q →P, that is, a map which sends cofinal subsets of Q to cofinal subsets of P.
By a characterization of Schmidt [23, 25], this is equivalent to the existence of an unbounded
map ψ : P →Q, that is, a map sending unbounded subsets of P to unbounded subsets of Q.
If, in addition, P and Q are Dedekind complete (i.e., every bounded subset has a least upper
bound), then P ≤T Q if and only if there exists an order-preserving map f : Q →P such that
f(Q) is cofinal in P [12].
Two posets P and Q are said to be Tukey equivalent, written P =T Q, if P ≤T Q and
Q ≤T P. A cofinal type is a Tukey equivalent class. We list several well-known examples, which
will also be used throughout the paper, to better understand the Tukey order.

1.3. Free Topological Groups over Uniform Spaces. Let (X, U) be a uniform space. The
free (Abelian) topological group over (X, U), denoted by F(X, U) (A(F, U)), is the free (abelian)
group generated by X endowed with the finest group topology such that every uniformly contin-
uous map from (X, U) into a topological (Abelian) group (equipped with its natural two-sided
uniformity) uniquely extends to a continuous group homomorphism.
This framework naturally generalizes the classical Markov free topological (Abelian) group
over a Tychonoff space X. Indeed, if UX denotes the fine uniformity on X, then F(X) is
topologically isomorphic to F(X, UX). For any compatible uniformity U on X, the relation
U ⊆UX implies that the identity map induces a continuous isomorphism from F(X) onto
F(X, U); hence, the topology of F(X, U) is coarser than or equal to that of F(X). The parallel
results hold for free topological Abelian groups.
The transition to uniform spaces provides a decisive methodological advantage. While an-
alyzing the identity neighborhood filter Ne(F(X)) is notoriously difficult due to the lack of
quantitative control in arbitrary Tychonoff spaces, the entourages and uniform coverings of U
offer a precise combinatorial framework. This allows one to orchestrate neighborhood bases
systematically via uniform covering trees, bypassing classical topological obstructions [21, 22].
2. Fineness Index of Posets and P-Base
2.1. Fineness index: definition and basic computation. For a topological space X and
x ∈X, we denote by Nx(X) the neighbourhood filter at x, ordered by reverse inclusion.
Let P be a directed poset. Following Dow and Feng [6], we say that a topological group G has
a P-base if there exists an order-preserving map from P to Ne(G) with cofinal image. Clearly,
this is stronger than the condition Ne(G) ≤T P. Since Ne(G) is always Dedekind complete,
i.e., every bounded set admits a least bound, the two notions coincide, see [12].
Among non-metrizable cases, the most extensively studied instance is P = ωω; see [2, 8,
10, 17]. Such groups are said to have a G-base. One defines uniform spaces with an ωω-base
similarly. Such spaces were first studied in [4], where, among other things, every compact space
with an ωω-base was shown to be metrizable. For further results on topological spaces, groups,
and uniform spaces with an ωω-base, we refer the reader to [2].
Topological groups with an ωω-base were systematically studied in [10] by Gabriyelyan, Kąkol
and Leiderman. Among other results, it was shown that if a topological group admits an ωω-
base, then its character, namely the cofinality of Ne(G), belongs to
{1, ω} ∪[b, d].
This result may seem somewhat “weird”, since b is not a cardinal invariant arising directly
from ωω, but rather from (ωω, ≤∗), while the additivity of ωω is countable. It is therefore natural
to look for another intrinsically defined cardinal invariant of posets that can serve as a lower
bound for non-metrizable topological groups with a P-base. The following notion provides such
a tool.

Definition 2.1. A directed pre-ordered set (P, ≤) is called κ-fine if every subset A ⊆P with
|A| ≤κ is dominated by a countable subset of P.
When cof(P) > ω, let fi(P) denote the least cardinal λ such that P is not λ-fine. This
cardinal is called the fineness index of P.
For completeness, we set fi(P) = 1 if cof(P) = 1, and fi(P) = ω if cof(P) = ω.
Remark 2.2. The cardinal invariant fi(P) is closely related to add(P): informally, one obtains
fi(P) from the definition of add(P) by replacing “1” with “ω” (when cof(P) > ω). Indeed,
add(P) is the least cardinality of a subset of P that cannot be dominated by a subset of
cardinality 1.
The notion of κ-fineness was first introduced for topological groups by He, Tkachenko, Zhang
and the second-listed author [13]: a topological group G is called κ-fine if Ne(G) is κ-fine.
The invariant fi(G) was then introduced naturally in subsequent work [27]. The weakest such
property, namely ω1-fineness, already deserves special attention; see, for example, [19], where
ω1-filters are called A-filters.
In these papers, κ-fineness is shown to be closely related to an important topic in the theory
of topological groups, namely R-factorizable topological groups [1, Chap. 8].
It is straightforward to see that if P and Q are Dedekind complete directed posets and
P ≤T Q, then the κ-fineness of Q implies the κ-fineness of P. The next lemma shows that the
assumption of Dedekind completeness can in fact be omitted.
Lemma 2.3. Let κ be uncountable, and let P and Q be directed posets such that P ≤T Q. If
Q is κ-fine, then so is P.
Proof. Fix a map φ : P →Q sending unbounded subsets of P to unbounded subsets of Q.
Let A ⊆P with |A| ≤κ. Since Q is κ-fine, the set φ(A) is dominated by a countable set
{qn : n ∈ω} in Q.
For each n ∈ω, define An = {a ∈A : φ(a) ≤qn}. Then φ(An) is bounded in Q, and by the
choice of φ, the set An must be bounded in P. Thus, for each n one can choose bn ∈P such
that a ≤bn for all a ∈An.
Since A = S
n∈ω An, the countable set {bn : n ∈ω} dominates A. Hence P is κ-fine.

3. Cofinal Type of Free Topological (Abelian) Groups
If (X, U) is a uniform space, then U, viewed as a subset of the power set of X × X, can be
ordered by reverse inclusion, i.e., U ≤V if and only if U ⊇V . With this order, U becomes a
directed poset.
The set of all uniformly continuous pseudometrics on (X, U) bounded by 1 is denoted by
P(X, U), and is ordered by d1 ≤d2 if d1(x, y) ≤d2(x, y) for all x, y ∈X.
The following description of the local Tukey structure of free Abelian topological groups was
made explicit by Gartside in [11, Theorem 3.1]. It is worth noting, however, that this result had
