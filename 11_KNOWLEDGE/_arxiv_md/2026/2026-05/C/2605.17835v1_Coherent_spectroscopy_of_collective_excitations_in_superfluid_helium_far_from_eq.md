Coherent spectroscopy of collective excitations in superfluid helium far from
equilibrium
Gabriel Voith, Alexander A. Milner and Valery Milner
Department of Physics & Astronomy, The University of British Columbia, Vancouver, Canada
(Dated: May 19, 2026)
Ultrafast dynamics of collective excitations in superfluids remains largely unexplored beyond the
roton regime, despite its importance for understanding nonequilibrium processes in these systems.
Here, we employ ultrafast coherent control with sequences of femtosecond pulses to perform spec-
troscopy of multiple branches of the Landau excitation spectrum in superfluid helium far from
equilibrium.
By measuring the time-resolved optical birefringence, we track the nonequilibrium
dynamics of maxon pairs and Pitaevskii plateau excitations alongside the previously studied roton
pairs, revealing surprisingly strong binding energy of maxon pairs, their extremely short lifetime, and
the influence of the quasiparticle effective mass on the phase of the coherent response. These results
demonstrate the ability to extract previously inaccessible information about collective excitations
in a strongly interacting quantum fluid by probing its nonequilibrium dynamics on femtosecond and
picosecond timescales.
Superfluid helium is one of the paradigm systems
for studying collective excitations in strongly interact-
ing many-body quantum systems. As first proposed by
Landau [1, 2], elementary excitations in superfluid 4He
consist of phonons, maxons, and rotons. Many aspects
of superfluidity can be described and explained by the
dispersion properties of these quasiparticles and their in-
teractions [3–5]. Until recently, collective excitations in
superfluid helium have been studied predominantly with
experimental tools sensitive to the equilibrium steady-
state dynamics of the superfluid – neutron scattering
[6, 7] and spontaneous Raman scattering [8–10] (for a
recent review, see Ref. 11). Neither of these approaches
can reveal the fast nonequilibrium dynamics of the sys-
tem: neutron and spontaneous Raman scattering average
over timescales far longer than those governing quasipar-
ticle interactions, and can only probe the system in ther-
mal equilibrium.
Recently,
we
introduced
a
time-resolved
optical
method for exciting and tracking roton pairs in super-
fluid helium on femtosecond and picosecond timescales
[12, 13], exposing rich nonequilibrium dynamics with
both the energy and linewidth of the two-roton state
evolving rapidly during equilibration with the superfluid
bath. Even though the oscillatory birefringence response
of the superfluid to an intense laser pulse has recently
been treated theoretically in terms of quantum squeezing
of quasiparticle pairs [14], the time-dependent evolution
of the roton response remains unexplained.
While rotons have received the most attention both
theoretically and experimentally, the full Landau disper-
sion curve of superfluid 4He also features maxons - quasi-
particles at the local energy maximum of the spectrum
- and a broad flat region at higher momenta known as
Pitaevskii plateau [15, 16].
The properties of maxons
and their interactions with other quasiparticles are far
less understood than those of rotons. Neutron scattering
experiments have established the equilibrium energy and
linewidth of maxons [6, 7], while their behavior far from
equilibrium remains entirely unexplored. The properties
of the Pitaevskii plateau are also not fully understood:
while neutron scattering measurements have established
its energy at twice the roton energy [7, 17], several as-
pects of its interpretation remain debated [6].
Here, we demonstrate the observation of maxon pairs
and Pitaevskii plateau excitations in the time-resolved
optical birefringence of superfluid helium. While hints of
these contributions are already visible in the single-pulse
excitation spectrum, we show that coherent control with
sequences of pump pulses greatly facilitates the extrac-
tion of their properties, enabling more accurate determi-
nation of their energies, linewidths, and phases.
We find a large binding energy of maxon pairs of al-
most 2 K – an order of magnitude larger than that of
roton pairs. The Pitaevskii plateau contribution appears
at an energy below its expected equilibrium value. Time-
resolved measurements show that maxon pairs decay on a
few-picosecond timescale – an order of magnitude shorter
than the lifetime of roton pairs – indicating extremely
fast equilibration with the superfluid bath. Finally, anal-
ysis of the oscillation phases reveals that the roton and
maxon responses are out of phase, in agreement with the
recent theoretical prediction by Melnikovsky [14] that the
birefringence response of superfluid helium to an intense
laser pulse contains contributions from all quasiparticles
across the dispersion curve, with the phase of each con-
tribution determined by the sign of the effective mass at
the corresponding stationary point of the dispersion [16].
Our experimental setup, shown in Figure 1, is similar
to that described in our previous work [12]. Briefly, lin-
early polarized infrared femtosecond pulses (≈70 fs pulse
length, 1 kHz repetition rate, 798 nm central wavelength,
intensity ≈1012 W/cm2) are focused in the bulk liquid
4He, condensed in a custom-built optical cryostat (Lake
Shore Cryotronics).
By pumping the helium gas from
the cryostat, the temperature of the liquid is stabilized at
1.4 K at the saturated vapor pressure (SVP). The laser
beam passes through a Michelson interferometer, pro-
ducing pairs of pulses with a variable separation τ and
controllable relative amplitude. The pump-induced time-

Figure 2 shows the time-dependent optical birefrin-
gence of superfluid helium induced by a single femtosec-
ond pump pulse.
The oscillatory signal is dominated
by the two-roton response around 350 GHz, consistent
with our earlier observations [12].
As reported previ-
ously, the instantaneous frequency of this signal is not
constant but changes on a picosecond timescale. Our re-
cent observation that the signal scales linearly with the
pump pulse energy [18], with the initial roton frequency
remaining unchanged, suggests that the frequency evolu-
tion is not driven by laser-induced temperature changes.
Instead, we show that the roton signal can be described
very well by a superposition of two decaying sinusoidal
components with two constant but different frequencies.
We attribute the lower-frequency component (≈348 GHz)
with a faster decay to bound two-roton states, and the
higher-frequency component (≈355 GHz) to unbound ro-
ton pairs, consistent with the known binding energy of
roton pairs in superfluid helium [19, 20]. Owing to the
different phases and decay constants of the two compo-
nents, their interference gives rise to the observed fre-
quency chirp in the time-domain signal [12]. As we dis-
cuss below, incorporating an additional maxon compo-
nent into this model further improves the quality of the
fit, as illustrated by the insets in Figure 2.
The power spectrum of the birefringence signal, shown
in Figure 3 on the logarithmic scale, is dominated by the
two-roton peak ‘R’, in agreement with our earlier work
[12]. In addition, two weak features are visible at higher
frequencies, located at around twice the maxon energy
(‘M’) and twice the energy of the Pitaevskii plateau (‘P’),
in agreement with theoretical predictions [14]. The ex-
istence of these weak resonances is corroborated by the
spectral phase, which exhibits a characteristic sharp step
across each of the corresponding frequencies – a hall-
mark of a resonant contribution to the signal. We notice
that the ‘M’ and ‘P’ peaks ride on the shoulder of the
two-roton peak, which is two to three orders of mag-
nitude stronger, making it difficult to reliably extract
their precise frequencies and linewidths. This limitation
motivates implementation of the double-pulse excitation
scheme, described below.

exploits the linearity of the system’s response to the laser

many-body environment.
Figure 4(a) shows the time-dependent birefringence

excitations oscillating at frequencies distinct from that of
rotons are not suppressed by the roton-targeted destruc-
tive interference, and their contributions become more
prominent in the residual signal.

The extracted phase reveals that the roton and maxon
responses clearly start out of phase: when described by
a single oscillation close to t = 0, the bi-roton phase
of 1.0 ± 0.3 rad and that of the maxon response (φM =
−2.5 ± 0.5 rad) differ by approximately π, as illustrated
by the dashed blue and black curves in Figure 5, respec-
tively. We note that the apparently more straightforward
approach of extracting the maxon phase directly from the
double-pulse residual signal (Fig. 4) is in fact unreliable,
as at τ = 1.45 ps, both the roton and maxon responses
to the first pulse have not yet decayed, and the second
pulse may therefore alter their relative phase.
To conclude, the apparent frequency chirp of the ro-
ton response, reported in our earlier work [12], admits an
alternative interpretation in light of the present results:
rather than a single oscillation with a time-varying fre-
quency, the signal is well described by a superposition of
just two components with distinct but time-independent
frequencies, decay constants, and phases. We tentatively
attribute these two components to bound and unbound
bi-roton states, whose frequency difference is consistent
with the known binding energy of roton pairs in super-
fluid helium, though rigorous theoretical confirmation is
still needed.
By applying the method of coherent control — specifi-
cally, the destructive interference of the material response
to two successive pump pulses — we demonstrate a new
approach to the spectroscopy of collective excitations
in superfluid helium far from equilibrium.
Selectively
suppressing the dominant roton response reveals weaker
spectral features that are otherwise obscured, enabling
optical observation of maxon pairs and the Pitaevskii
plateau. The detection of both excitations in the laser-
induced birefringence signal is consistent with recent the-
oretical developments [14], which showed that the bire-
fringence response of the superfluid to an intense laser
pulse should contain contributions from all quasiparti-
cles across the dispersion curve.
Our results suggest that the bi-maxon pair frequency
lies below twice the single-maxon energy, with the deficit
about an order of magnitude larger than the binding en-
ergy of bi-roton pairs. Similarly, the bi-maxon lifetime
is about an order of magnitude shorter than that of bi-
rotons. The Pitaevskii plateau peak also appears below
the value of 4∆R inferred from neutron scattering data.
The asymmetric line shapes of both peaks, the shifts of
their energies below equilibrium values, and the short life-
times could reflect quasiparticle binding, nonequilibrium
dynamics, or a combination of both. Their quantitative
interpretation requires further experimental and theoret-
ical investigation.
Finally, the phase analysis of the maxon response pro-
vides new insight into the nature of the laser-superfluid
interaction.
Although the accuracy of the extracted
maxon phase is limited to ±0.5 rad, it is clearly distinct
from the roton phase, with the two responses being ap-
proximately π out of phase. This is qualitatively con-
