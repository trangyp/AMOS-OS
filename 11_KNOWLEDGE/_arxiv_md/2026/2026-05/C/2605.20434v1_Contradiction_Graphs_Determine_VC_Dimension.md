Contradiction Graphs Determine VC Dimension

Department of Mathematics, Statistics, & Computer Science

Abstract
We study the contradiction graphs associated with binary concept classes.
For a class
H ⊆{0, 1}X, the order-m contradiction graph Gm(H) has as vertices the H-realizable labeled
sequences of length m, with two vertices adjacent when the two sequences assign opposite labels
to some common domain point. Our main result is that the single graph Gm(H) determines the
threshold predicate VCdim(H) ≥m. Consequently, the full sequence (Gm(H))m≥1 determines
the exact VC dimension and, in particular, detects finite versus infinite VC dimension, answering
a question posed by Alon et al. [AMSY24].
1
Introduction
The Vapnik–Chervonenkis dimension is the fundamental combinatorial parameter of distribution-free
binary classification. Introduced by Vapnik and Chervonenkis in their work on uniform convergence
[VC71], and closely connected to the Sauer–Shelah lemma [Sau72, She72], it characterizes classical
PAC learnability [Val84, BEHW89, EHKV89]. In particular, finite VC dimension is equivalent to
distribution-free learnability.
This paper asks whether that finite-versus-infinite VC dichotomy is still visible after replacing a
concept class by its contradiction graphs. For a binary class H ⊆{0, 1}X, the order-m contradiction
graph Gm(H) has as vertices the H-realizable labeled samples of length m, with an edge between
two samples if they assign opposite labels to some common domain point. Throughout, samples are
ordered sequences, and repetitions of domain points are allowed, subject to consistent labeling.
We use the contradiction-graph framework introduced by Alon et al. in their graph-theoretic
characterization of private learnability [AMSY24]. They ask whether two binary classes can have
isomorphic contradiction graphs at every level while one has finite VC dimension and the other has
infinite VC dimension.
Problem 1.1 (Alon et al. [AMSY24]). Do there exist binary classes H0, H1 such that VCdim(H0) <
∞, VCdim(H1) = ∞, and Gm(H0) ∼= Gm(H1) for every m ≥1?
We prove that no such pair exists. In fact, the single graph Gm(H) already determines whether
VCdim(H) ≥m. The full sequence (Gm(H))m≥1 thus determines the exact VC dimension of H.
Our proof gives a graph-intrinsic certificate for the predicate VCdim(H) ≥m. If H shatters an
m-point set, then the 2m labelings of that set form a clique in Gm(H). This clique has a special
trace property: for every vertex, the members of the clique nonadjacent to it form a Boolean subcube
under the natural identification with {0, 1}m. We call such a clique a cube-trace clique.
Our main structural result is the converse. If Gm(H) contains a cube-trace clique of size 2m,
then H shatters an m-point set. Thus VCdim(H) ≥m if and only if Gm(H) contains a cube-trace

Related work
VC dimension was introduced in the study of uniform convergence [VC71]. The Sauer–Shelah
lemma [Sau72, She72] gives the basic growth bound for classes of bounded VC dimension, and the
work of Valiant [Val84], Blumer et al. [BEHW89], and Ehrenfeucht et al. [EHKV89] established
the central role of VC dimension in PAC learnability.
The present paper does not alter this
classical characterization; instead, it asks whether the same VC information survives after passing to
contradiction graphs.
Graph-theoretic approaches to shattering have appeared in other forms. For example, Kozma
and Moran related shattering and strong shattering to orientations and connectivity properties of
graphs [KM13]. The contradiction graphs studied here are different: their vertices are realizable
labeled samples, and their edges record inconsistent labels on common domain points. Our result
shows that these graphs encode the exact VC thresholds VCdim(H) ≥m.
The original motivation for the contradiction graph comes from private-learning, specifically the
area of differential privacy, introduced by Dwork et al. [DMNS06, DMNS17]; see also [DKM+06,
DR14]. Private PAC learning was initiated by Kasiviswanathan et al. [KLN+11], and subsequent
work studied sample complexity, data release, proper learning, and pure versus approximate privacy
[CH11, BLR13, BBKN14, FX15, BNS16, BNS19]. A related line connects private learning with
online learning and Littlestone dimension. Littlestone introduced the dimension now bearing his name
in the online mistake-bound model [Lit88]. Alon et al. [ABL+22] proved a qualitative equivalence
between private and online learnability, while later work studied proper learning, approximate
privacy, and computational separations [GGKM21, BCD24].
The work most directly related to this paper is that of Alon et al. [AMSY24], who introduced
the contradiction-graph framework and used clique-type graph parameters to characterize private
learnability, with these parameters quantitatively related to Littlestone dimension. The present
paper addresses their question of whether the graph sequence determines the finite-versus-infinite
VC dichotomy. We show that it does, and more strongly that the level-m graph alone determines
whether VCdim(H) ≥m.

Definitions
We begin with the standard definition of VC dimension.
Definition 3.1 (VC dimension [VC71]). A finite set R ⊆X is shattered by H ⊆{0, 1}X if every
labeling R →{0, 1} is realized by some h ∈H. The VC dimension of H, denoted VCdim(H), is
VCdim(H) = sup{|R| : R ⊆X is finite, nonempty, and shattered by H},
with the convention that the supremum of the empty set is 0. Thus VCdim(H) ∈N0 ∪{∞}, where
N0 = {0, 1, 2, . . . }.
Before defining contradiction graphs, we fix our convention for labeled samples and realizability.
Definition 3.2 (H-realizable labeled sequence). A length-m labeled sequence is a tuple
S = ((x1, y1), . . . , (xm, ym)) ∈(X × {0, 1})m.

It is H-realizable if there is h ∈H such that h(xi) = yi for all i. Repetitions of domain points are
allowed; realizability implies that repeated occurrences of the same point receive the same label. We
write (x, b) ∈S if the signed point (x, b) occurs as an entry of S, and
supp(S) = {xi : i ∈[m]}.
We now define the contradiction graphs associated with H.
Definition 3.3 (Contradiction graph [AMSY24]). The graph Gm(H) has as vertices the H-realizable
length-m sequences. Two distinct vertices S, T are adjacent if there is a domain point x ∈X and a
bit b ∈{0, 1} such that
(x, b) ∈S
and
(x, 1 −b) ∈T.
To state the graph-intrinsic certificate used in the main theorem, we need to record how an
arbitrary vertex sees a distinguished set of vertices.
Definition 3.4 (Non-neighbor trace). Let G be a simple graph and let Q ⊆V (G). For T ∈V (G),
the non-neighbor trace of T on Q is
MQ(T) = {S ∈Q : S is not adjacent to T}.
Since the graph is simple, a vertex is not adjacent to itself; thus if T ∈Q and Q is a clique, then
MQ(T) = {T}.
The traces arising from shattered sets will be Boolean subcubes, so we fix that terminology as
well.
Definition 3.5 (Boolean subcube). A Boolean subcube of {0, 1}m is a nonempty set of the form
{σ ∈{0, 1}m : σi = τi for all i ∈I},
where I ⊆[m] and τ ∈{0, 1}I. The case I = ∅gives the full cube, and the case I = [m] gives a
singleton.
4
Preliminary results and examples
The examples in this section serve two purposes. First, finite prefixes of the graph sequence cannot
determine the exact VC dimension. This calibrates the strongest possible conclusion: one should
expect the level-m graph to determine the threshold predicate VCdim ≥m, but not to reveal all
larger thresholds. Second, as was implicit in the work of Alon et al. [AMSY24], the mere existence
of a clique of size 2m in Gm(H) does not imply that H shatters an m-point set. This shows that the
main certificate must use not only the clique itself, but also the pattern of non-neighborhood traces
around the clique. These examples motivate the cube-trace certificate introduced in Section 5.
4.1
Finite prefixes do not determine exact VC dimension
The first preliminary construction compares the full class on an n-point domain with the even-parity
subclass. These classes differ in VC dimension by exactly one, but the difference is invisible to samples
of length less than n. This subsection explains why the main theorem is naturally a level-by-level
threshold statement rather than a finite-prefix reconstruction theorem.

Proposition 4.1. For every n ≥2, VCdim(Un) = n, and VCdim(En) = n −1.
Proof. The class Un realizes every labeling on [n], hence VCdim(Un) = n. For En, any set of n −1

from small contradiction graphs: any length-m < n sample misses at least one coordinate, and the
missing coordinate can be used to repair parity.
Theorem 4.2. For every n ≥2 and every 1 ≤m < n, Gm(En) = Gm(Un). Consequently, for every

determine VCdim(H) for all binary concept classes.
Proof. Apply Theorem 4.2 with the given d. The classes Cd and Dd have the same graphs through
level d, but their VC dimensions are d and d + 1, respectively. Hence any invariant depending only
on those first d graphs takes the same value on Cd and Dd, and therefore cannot determine the exact
VC dimension in general.
4.2
Large cliques without shattering
The second preliminary point is subtler. A shattered m-point set certainly creates a 2m-clique in
Gm(H): take the 2m possible labelings of that one fixed support. The converse is false unless the
clique is known to live on one common m-point support. The results in this subsection isolate the
obstruction and explain why the later cube-trace condition is needed.
Proposition 4.4. Let H ⊆{0, 1}X, let m ≥1, and let Q ⊆V (Gm(H)) be a clique of size 2m.
Suppose there exist distinct points x1, . . . , xm ∈X such that every sequence in Q uses exactly the
support {x1, . . . , xm}. Then {x1, . . . , xm} is shattered by H.

through subcube-shaped non-neighborhoods.
5
Cube-trace cliques
The clique example in the previous section shows what the certificate must rule out: a large clique
whose adjacencies arise from contradictions occurring across many different supports. A genuine
shattered cube has a much more rigid external profile. If an outside sample fixes some of the shattered
coordinates, then the clique vertices nonadjacent to it are exactly those cube labels agreeing on
those fixed coordinates. This is precisely a Boolean subcube. The following definition abstracts that
profile inside the graph.
Definition 5.1 (Cube-trace clique). Let m ≥1, and let Q ⊆V (Gm(H)) be a clique of size 2m. We
say that Q is a cube-trace clique if there is a bijection ϕ : Q →{0, 1}m such that, for every vertex
T ∈V (Gm(H)), the set ϕ(MQ(T)) ⊆{0, 1}m is a Boolean subcube.

6
Discussion
The finite-prefix example in Theorem 4.2 is compatible with the main theorem: the first d graphs
can fail to distinguish VC dimension d from VC dimension d + 1, because both classes have VC
dimension at least m for every m ≤d. The missing information appears exactly at level d + 1. Thus
no bounded prefix determines exact VC dimension, while the full sequence does.
The proof does not say that an arbitrary clique of size 2m in Gm(H) forces VCdim(H) ≥m.
The class P in Theorem 4.7 has VC dimension one but has 2m-cliques in Gm(P) for every m. The
certificate is stronger: a 2m-clique must have the trace behavior of a shattered cube. Thus the result
uses the full graph structure around the clique, not only the number ω(Gm(H)).
The reverse implication uses the vertices ((x, 0), . . . , (x, 0)) and ((x, 1), . . . , (x, 1)). Therefore the
proof applies to the repeated-sample contradiction graph, but it does not automatically apply to a
duplicate-free variant in which each sample must use m distinct domain points.
The question addressed here is the finite-versus-infinite VC question posed by Alon et al. [AMSY24]:
can two classes with finite and infinite VC dimension have isomorphic contradiction graphs at every
level? The answer is no. The proof yields the stronger statement that the level-m graph detects the
threshold VCdim ≥m, so the full sequence determines the exact VC dimension.
