Cyclotomic finite-field Fourier spectra: Galois
descent, native subfields, and residual coding
David Kumallagov, Daniil Sizikov, Anton Zarubin
May 20, 2026
Abstract
We develop a Galois descent approach to finite field Fourier spectra over an
arbitrary finite base field. Let K = Fq and L = Fqm. If a Fourier transform is applied
to a K-valued vector, then its spectrum is not an arbitrary element of Ln: it satisfies
the Frobenius consistency relation
V q
s = Vqs mod n.
We prove a general Galois-descent theorem for Fourier transforms on finite abelian
groups, characterize the one-dimensional spectra as products of subfields indexed
by q-cyclotomic classes, and show that the orbit-seed representation is optimal in
base-field coordinates.
For arbitrary vectors in Ln, we study a two-stage representation g = f + h, where
f is class-consistent and h is a residual. The residual optimization separates over
cyclotomic classes. We give exact support minimization, a symbol weight enumerator
for the class-consistent code, recovery guarantees, global covering-radius formulas,
random residual tail bounds, and entropy-form lower bounds. We also consider
implementation consequences for trace decompositions, normal bases, canonical
subfield embeddings, and sparse-polynomial residual backends.
Keywords: Finite fields, Fourier transform, Frobenius automorphism, cyclotomic classes,
Galois descent, Hamming weight, residual coding, sparse polynomials.

probability, and counting lower bounds.
Main results
The main results of the work are as follows:
1) We generalize the classical cyclotomic technique of DFT by using Galois descent for
the Fourier transform on finite Abelian groups.
2) We present Algorithm 1, an exact q−cyclotomic spectral coding algorithm. It
stores a single initial value of the original subfield in each Frobenius orbit and
reconstructs the entire spectrum by repeatedly applying q−Frobenius. We prove
that this representation in orbital coordinates is lossless and optimal in the basic
field coordinates.
3) We present Algorithm 2, a direct transform using trace tables for class-consistent
vectors. It generalizes the trace table philosophy used for the binary DFT to the
base field Fq and to the subfields Fqℓ⊆Fqm.
4) We present Algorithm 3, a two-stage class-consistent residual representation g =
f + h for arbitrary vectors over L. We also introduce Algorithm 4, an exact
selection procedure that minimizes the support for each class.

Optimality and the two meanings of compression
The orbit representation compresses the number of stored spectral positions over the ambi-
ent field, but it cannot reduce the number of base-field coordinates below the information
content of the original vector.
Theorem 5.1. Let the q-cyclotomic classes modulo n have lengths ℓ1, . . . , ℓr. Then every
V ∈Sn is represented by one seed from Fqℓj on the j-th class, and the total number of
required K-coordinates is
ℓ1 + · · · + ℓr = n.
Moreover, no lossless fixed-length representation of all spectra in Sn over the alphabet K
can use fewer than n symbols.
Proof. By Theorem 4.2, a class of length ℓj contributes one seed in Fqℓj , which has ℓj
coordinates over K. Summing over the classes gives n.
The Fourier transform gives a bijection Kn ≃Sn, so |Sn| = qn. A fixed-length code over
K using fewer than n symbols has fewer than qn codewords and cannot be injective on
Sn.
Theorem 5.2. Let Sn be the Frobenius-consistent spectral space. Then:
(i) as a K-linear space, Sn has dimension n;
(ii) as an orbit-coordinate object, each V ∈Sn is determined by κq(n) spectral seeds;
(iii) the base-field coordinate cost is exactly n, while the number of stored ambient spectral
positions is reduced from n to κq(n).
Proof. The first assertion follows from Theorem 4.1. The second is Corollary 4.3. The
third is precisely the comparison between the number of seeds and the sum of their subfield
dimensions in Theorem 5.1.

Related work
Characters and Fourier analysis on finite abelian groups are classical; for the background
used in the Galois-descent formulation, see [1, 2]. Finite-field Fourier transforms and their
role in coding theory go back to the transform methods of Blahut [4, 5] and related work
on finite-field FFTs [20], [10], [9].

The classical cyclic-code language describes the same orbits as q-cyclotomic cosets
and identifies them with irreducible factors of Xn −1 over Fq. Our contribution is not
the rediscovery of these cosets, but their use as a native-subfield coordinate system for
finite-field Fourier spectra over arbitrary base fields, together with an algorithmic residual
layer for arbitrary ambient-field vectors. The trace viewpoint is also related to Delsarte’s
use of trace representations and subfield subcodes [8].
Canonical embeddings of finite fields and compatible towers are treated in [6, 16, 22];
standard finite-field background can be found in [15, 18]. Normal bases and optimal
normal bases are discussed in [14, 19]. Sparse-polynomial interpolation and lacunary
representations are classical in symbolic computation [3, 11, 12, 13, 21]. The binary
DFT case is recovered as the special case q = 2 and is closely related with the classwise
constructions used in [9].

Conclusion
We have formulated cyclotomic finite-field Fourier spectra as a Galois-descent problem. For
spectra of K = Fq-valued data, the Frobenius consistency relation exactly characterizes the
image of the finite-field Fourier transform. The resulting spectral space decomposes into
native subfield seeds indexed by q-cyclotomic classes. This gives an optimal n-coordinate
representation over the base field and an ambient-position reduction from n to κq(n).
For arbitrary vectors over L, the Frobenius-consistent space Fn = Sn provides a
structured residual-coding layer. The residual optimization separates class by class, admits
exact support minimization, and can be interpreted as nearest-codeword approximation for
a K-linear code over the alphabet L. Thus, the main new component is the residual coding
layer attached to the classical Frobenius-cyclotomic spectral structure. The covering-radius,
random-tail, and entropy lower bounds show that small residuals cannot be guaranteed
for unstructured ambient data.
The algorithms and formulas developed here combine finite-field arithmetic, trace
reconstruction, and optional sparse residual backends. This makes the framework suitable
for finite-field transform computation, cyclic-code computations, and computer-algebra
implementations involving compatible finite-field towers.
