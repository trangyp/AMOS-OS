Abstract. In this paper, we discuss combinatorial aspects of normal ordering
of 3-dimensional skew polynomial rings defined and classified by Bell and Smith
[3].
With some help of the Mathematical software SageMath, we are able
to reduce the length of computation of PBW forms and normal orderings
appearing in commutation rules of these algebras.
1. Introduction
Just as Schork [37, Section 1] asserted, “Maybe the first explicit results concerning
normal ordering were derived by Scherk [35] in his dissertation from 1823.” In his
thesis (and using the current terminology in the literature), he considered the action
of two operators X and D on functions of a real variable defined by
(1.1)

and

As it can be seen, these two operators do not commute, and if I
denotes the identity operator, they satisfy the commutation relation given by
(1.2)

As is well-known, this algebra is called the first Weyl algebra and is defined over the
field of complex numbers C as the C-algebra A1(C) = C{X, D}/⟨XD −DX −I⟩.
History and a detailed study of this noncommutative ring was made by Coutinho
[10].
If we consider the definition of an arbitrary word in the indeterminates X and D,
then we mean an object ω that can be written as ω = Xr1Ds1Xr2Ds2 · · · XrnDsn
for some elements rk, sk belonging to the set of natural numbers N (we consider
the zero element as a natural number). Having in mind the relation (1.2), it is
necessary to respect the order of the operators when acting on a function f; this
motivates the following definition: a word ω in the letters X and D is said to be
in normal ordered form if ω = ar,sXrDs for r, s ∈N (and arbitrary coefficients ar,s
in some kind of ring or field - not necessarily the field of complex numbers). With
this, an expression that consists of a sum of words is called normal ordered if each
of the summand is normal ordered. The process of expressing a word (or a sum

A major theme in subsequent work is that changing the commutation rule (or
moving to deformations and variants of the Weyl algebra) produces new families
of Stirling-type numbers with rich combinatorics. A striking example is the inter-
pretation of normal order coefficients in terms of rook numbers on Ferrers boards,
leading to explicit formulas and factorization phenomena as shown by Varvak [39].
Deformations also connect normal ordering to q-analogues and q-Stirling families
[36], while more refined operator strings yield generalized Bell-Stirling structures
with graph-theoretic models and additional combinatorial interpretations [19, 9].
These contributions establish normal ordering as a robust combinatorial machine
that turns commutation relations into computable, structured coefficient systems.
In the literature, we can find a large number of works that have addressed the
study of normal orders for various families of non-commutative algebras such as
the quantum plane, the q-deformed Weyl algebra, the algebras in physical models of
quantum theory defined by the creation operator and the annihilation operator, and
other generalizations of the Weyl algebra (e.g. [5, 6, 7, 12, 17, 18, 20, 37], and refer-
ences therein). Recently, Briand et al. [8] studied normally ordered forms for pow-
ers of differential operators, deriving generalized Stirling numbers and arithmetic
properties of the coefficients, while Mansour and Schork [16] developed Ore-Stirling
numbers as normal ordering coefficients in a more general setting than Patrias and
Pylyavskyy [23]. As it can be seen in all these works, normal ordering becomes
an efficient bridge between algebraic identities and concrete combinatorial classes
(set partitions, contractions, graphs), yielding recurrences, generating functions and
closed forms for the associated coefficient arrays. It is important to note that all
structures considered above have only one nontrivial commutation relation.
The algebras of interest in this paper are the 3-dimensional skew polynomial
rings introduced by Bell and Smith [3] as a class of noncommutative algebras on

three generators admitting a PBW basis of standard monomials which are defined
by three nontrivial commutation relations.
Beyond their intrinsic ring-theoretic
interest, 3-dimensional skew polynomial algebras naturally capture familiar struc-
tures from Lie theory and quantum groups such as the universal enveloping algebra
U(sl(2)), the dispin algebra U(osp(1, 2)) and Woronowicz-type algebras associated
to noncommutative differential calculi appear within this framework [29, 40].
Our purpose in this paper is to develop the algebraic combinatorics underlying
PBW normal forms in the class of 3-dimensional skew polynomial algebras. Fixing
the PBW order x ≺y ≺z, we regard PBW reduction as a rewriting process that
eliminates the forbidden adjacent pairs yx, zy and zx via the affine relations (as
expected, our ideas are based on Bergman’s paper [4], see also [29]). This viewpoint
turns the computation of products and powers into a systematic normal ordering
problem: given a noncommutative expression, determine its unique expansion in
the PBW basis consisting of the monomials xiyjzl with i, j, l ∈N. Our approach
is uniform and computable: we work in a parameterized 3-skew family and then
specialize the parameters to cover the 15 isomorphism types in the Bell and Smith’s
classification. In our treatment, we keep in mind Schork’s words about algebras
with more than two generators: “When considering algebras with three or more
generators where none of the generators is central (hence, one has at least three
nontrivial commutation relations) one can clearly define normal ordering in an
analogous fashion but the considerations quickly become tedious” [37, p. 4]. These
words motivated us to look for computational alternatives to reduce calculations,
and it is the reason why we developed a code for it in the mathematical software
SageMath.
The article is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the definition and classifi-
cation of 3-dimensional skew polynomial rings. Next, Section 3 contains the original
results of the paper. With some help of the Mathematical software SageMath, we
are able to compute PBW forms and hence normal orderings appearing in commuta-
tion rules. More exactly, for each of the fifteen 3-dimensional skew polynomial rings
(Proposition 2.2), we establish the fundamental two letter normal ordering iden-
tities (such as ynxm, znxm, and znym) that serve as basic rewriting rules. These
identities are then used as building blocks to derive the higher-order expansions
required in our combinatorial results, including powers of blocks and mixed words
(e.g. (xnym)s, (xyz)s, (xnymzt)s) and the corresponding coefficient recursions. We
want to remark that the software SageMath is used in an elementary way only to
reduce the length of the computation and all computation can be done by hand
without assistance of it. Precisely, Section 4 contains the code we implemented in
SageMath that allowed us to compute the normal ordered forms of every algebra.
Finally, Section 5 presents some ideas for possible future work related to normal
ordering issues.
Throughout the paper, N denotes the set of natural numbers including the zero
element. The term ring means an associative ring with identity, not necessarily
commutative. All vector spaces and algebras (always associative and unital) are
over a fixed field k, which is assumed to have characteristic zero. As usual, k× :=
k \ {0}.

4. Computational setup
Having in mind Definition 2.1 (ii), throughout the paper we fix the PBW mono-
mial order x ≺y ≺z, so that the intended normal forms are k-linear combinations
of PBW monomials xiyjzk. In this section, we implement a deterministic reduc-
tion procedure that repeatedly rewrites the leftmost forbidden adjacent pair among
yx, zy and zx. For the fifteen algebras classified in Proposition 2.2 Bergman’s di-
amond lemma [4] is applied (c.f. [29]), so the output of the code below coincides
with the unique PBW normal form.
We rewrite 3-dimensional skew polynomial algebras in the following form: A is
a k-algebra generated by the indeterminates x, y, z restricted to relations
yz −αzy = aλx + bλy + cλz + dλ,
zx −βxz = aµx + bµy + cµz + dµ,
and
(4.1)
xy −γyx = aνx + bνy + cνz + dν,
with the elements a’s, b’s, c’s, d’s belonging to k and α, β, γ ∈k×.

5. Future work
As a natural continuation of the present work, we want to extend the combi-
natorial and computational analysis of PBW normal ordering developed here to
other relevant classes of noncommutative algebras admitting structured rewriting
systems, in particular to diffusion algebras [11, 13, 24, 32] and bi-quadratic algebras
on three generators with PBW basis [2, 33]. Our aim is to determine whether these
families also support a uniform normal ordering theory in terms of explicit recursive
reduction procedures and coefficient arrays generalizing the Stirling-type patterns
that arise in the skew polynomial setting.
