Conductivity of a Non-Galilean–Invariant Fermi Liquid:
Exact Solution of the Kinetic Equation
Tatia Kiliptari,1, ∗Vladimir I. Yudson,2 and Dmitrii L. Maslov1
1Department of Physics, University of Florida, Gainesville, FL 32611, USA
2Laboratory for Condensed Matter Physics, HSE University, 20 Myasnitskaya St., Moscow, 101000 Russia
(Dated: May 22, 2026)
We obtain an exact expression for the conductivity of a disordered, non-Galilean-invariant Fermi
liquid by solving the kinetic equation with both screened Coulomb and z = 3 Pomeranchuk
critical interactions. While consistent with previous asymptotic results, our solution shows that
electron-electron interactions enter the conductivity solely via the quasiparticle scattering time, τee.
Accordingly, the crossovers between the collisionless and hydrodynamic regimes occur when 1/τee
becomes comparable to the larger of the impurity scattering rate and the probe frequency, Ω. In
addition, the exact solution yields the optical response in the hydrodynamic regime, Ω≪1/τee,
which is inaccessible within perturbation theory. Near a z = 3 Pomeranchuk quantum critical point,
consistency between the kinetic-equation and Kubo approaches requires proper inclusion of mass
renormalization within the Eliashberg approximation, which also ensures that the crossover between
the collisionless and hydrodynamic regimes in the optical conductivity occurs at the Planckian scale
Ω∼T.
Introduction.
The temperature (T) and frequency (Ω)
dependencies of the electronic conductivity provide key in-
sights into the scattering mechanisms that govern electron
dynamics. Traditionally, a Fermi liquid (FL) is expected
to exhibit a T 2 dc resistivity and Gurzhi-like optical
conductivity, Re σ(Ω, T) ∝(Ω2 + 4π2T 2)/Ω2 [1], while
the deviations are interpreted as signatures of non-FL
physics. However, the T 2 resistivity requires either umk-
lapp scattering [2] or compensated electron-hole bands
[3].
If these conditions are not met, normal electron-
electron ee collisions still affect the conductivity of a
non-Galilean-invariant FL, provided that momentum is
relaxed by another mechanism, e.g., impurity scattering.
However, the ee contribution to the conductivity exhibits
non-canonical scaling with T and Ω.
In the low-temperature limit, the dc resistivity of a
disordered, isotropic, non-Galilean–invariant FL is given
by ρ(T) = ρi + δρ(T), where ρi is the residual resistiv-
ity and δρ(T) ∝T 4 (up to a logarithmic factor in two
dimensions) is independent of impurity scattering [4–6].
This form appears to obey Matthiessen’s rule and thus
suggests a suppressed “current relaxation rate” due to ee
interactions, 1/τJ ∝T 4 ≪1/τee ∝T 2, where τee is the
quasiparticle scattering time. The collisionless limit of
the optical conductivity Re σ(Ω, T) ∝max{Ω4, T 4}/Ω2
[7–10], reinforces this interpretation: viewed as a Drude
tail, it implies that 1/τJ ∝max{Ω4, T 4}. The same in-
terpretation would place the low-T/high-T crossover at
δρ(T) ∼ρi, implying an O(1) ee contribution to the re-
sistivity, large enough to attribute sizable experimental
variations of ρ to normal ee collisions.
In this Letter, we obtain an exact result for the con-
ductivity by solving the kinetic equation both at zero
and finite Ω. It shows that the low-T/high-T crossover
in the dc resistivity and the collisionless/hydrodynamic
crossover in the optical conductivity are controlled by
τee/τi and Ωτee, respectively, where τi is the impurity
scattering time. Therefore, interpreting the previously
known asymptotic results in terms of a current relaxation
rate is misleading. In particular, in the dc case we find
that δρ(T) ≪ρi throughout the temperature range of the
Fermi-liquid regime.
Model.
As a concrete realization of a non-Galilean–
invariant FL, we consider a 2D Dirac system with an
energy-independent τi and study two models of the ee
interaction.
The first model is the screened Coulomb (C) interaction,
UC(q, ω), treated within the random-phase approximation
(RPA). We assume weak coupling, which justifies the RPA
treatment and, at the level of the kinetic equation, allows
us to neglect Fermi-liquid renormalizations of the effective
mass and the deviation of the Z-factor from unity. We
focus on the regime of E ≡max{|Ω, T} ≪Ωp ≲εF, where
Ωp = vFκ is the characteristic plasma frequency, vF is the
Fermi velocity, κ is the screening wavevector, and εF is the
Fermi energy; in this regime screening is effectively static.
The extension to dynamic screening does not modify the
main conclusions and will be discussed elsewhere.
The second model is a nearly critical FL with the
effective Hertz-Millis (HM) interaction between fermions
near a z = 3 quantum critical point (QCP) [11, 12],
