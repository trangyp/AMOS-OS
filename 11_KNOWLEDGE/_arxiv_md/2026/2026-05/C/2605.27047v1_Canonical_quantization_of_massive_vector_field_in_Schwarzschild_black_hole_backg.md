Canonical quantization of massive vector field in Schwarzschild black hole background
Chandra Prakash1, ∗and Rajesh Karmakar2, †
1Department of Physics, Indian Institute of Technology Guwahati, Assam 781039, India
2Department of Physics, Shanghai University, 99 Shangda Road, Shanghai, 200444, China
We perform a first-principles canonical quantization of a massive vector field, often referred to
as the Proca field, in a Schwarzschild spacetime background. While scalar, fermionic, and electro-
magnetic fields are well studied in this context, the Proca field requires a more nuanced treatment
because of the physical nature of the longitudinal polarization mode and the constrained dynamics
of the field variables. By implementing the Dirac bracket formalism to treat the constraints inher-
ent in the Proca action, we derive a consistent framework for the commutator algebra of creation
and annihilation operators. Following this construction, we define the usual Boulware, Unruh, and
Hartle–Hawking vacua. Using the Unruh vacuum, we derive and analyze the Hawking spectrum of
the Proca field. Furthermore, we numerically evaluate the Proca condensate constructed from the
two-point correlation function ⟨Aµ(x)Aν(x′)⟩, defined on all three vacuum states. We find that the
condensate becomes significant near the boundary of the future horizon. Our results highlight the
interplay among the different polarization modes and the significance of the Proca mass in quantum
observables.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The semiclassical approach to gravitational interac-
tion, in which quantum fields propagate on a fixed clas-
sical spacetime background, captures several profound
phenomena. It has led to successful predictions in early
universe cosmology, particularly for structure formation
[1]. On the other hand, the same approach led to the
discovery of Hawking radiation [2], and vacuum polar-
ization [3] in BH spacetime, opening an entirely new
arena of phenomena associated with BH physics. While
the implementation of quantization within the semiclas-
sical framework is relatively well understood in the early
universe [4], governed by an isotropic and homogeneous
expanding background, the difficulty arises in the pres-
ence of horizons in BH spacetimes (see e.g., [5, 6]). Pre-
dictably, substantial effort has been devoted over the
decades to the quantization of fundamental fields in BH
backgrounds and to the computation of vacuum expec-
tation values of the stress-energy tensor [7–10].
Even after decades since Hawking’s original discovery
[2], together with several alternative derivations of the
radiation flux [11–20], a complete understanding of the
semiclassical evaporation process remains an active area
of research.
It requires the computation of the renor-
malized stress energy tensor to study the semiclassical
Einstein equation, Gαβ = ⟨Tαβ⟩[21]. As a primary step,
a major approach in this regard has been to quantize
the fields in a canonical manner. The procedure, natu-
rally, becomes difficult with the increase of spin of the
matter fields, as is evident from the existing literature on
spin–0 (scalar) [22–24], spin−1/2 (fermion) [25], massless
spin−1 (photon) [26, 27], etc. [28]. However, the inclu-

sion of massive fields introduces additional difficulties.
For example, one cannot use anomaly-based computa-
tions to study near-horizon features [21]. Nevertheless,
in the canonical prescription, the extension to the mas-
sive case is comparatively straightforward for scalar and
fermion fields, allowing the field modes to exhibit bound
states in addition to scattering states [23]. In contrast,
the corresponding extension for a vector field is nontrivial
due to the loss of gauge invariance induced by the mass
term, which, importantly, promotes the longitudinal po-
larization to a physical propagating degree of freedom.
Concerning the computability, this leads to a coupled
system of equations that makes the numerical procedure
difficult [29]. Later, the strategy developed by Frolov,
Krtous, Kubizn´ak, and Santos (FKKS) provided a major
breakthrough by establishing an exact separable ansatz
for the Proca equations in BH spacetime [30].
The motivation to rigorously study the Proca field
(hereafter used interchangeably with massive vector field)
in a BH background is twofold.
First, from a phe-
nomenological standpoint, massive vector bosons, such
as dark photons, are compelling dark matter candidates
[31–34]. The longitudinal polarization of the massive vec-
tor field, which may be understood through the Stueck-
elberg mechanism [35–37], represents a physical degree
of freedom absent in standard Maxwell electrodynamics.
Understanding how quantization modifies the dynamics
and thermal behaviour of this additional mode is there-
fore of direct physical interest.
The explicit breaking of U(1) gauge symmetry by the
Proca mass term also raises an interesting question in
the context of infrared approaches to the BH informa-
tion problem. Proposals relating information retention
to soft hair and large gauge transformations at null in-
finity rely crucially on the existence of asymptotic gauge
symmetries and their associated soft sectors [38]. In con-
trast, a massive vector field possesses no corresponding

In the present work, we study the massive vector field
in the test-field approximation on a fixed Schwarzschild
background, leaving the extension to the rotating sce-
nario for future work. We begin with the construction
of the classical solutions using the FKKS ansatz and ex-
plicitly verify that the modes are orthonormal under the
Proca inner product. This step is crucial in the even-
parity sector, where the orthogonality of the longitudinal
and transverse degrees of freedom is not automatically
implied by parity alone. Finally, using these orthonor-
malized mode functions, we perform a canonical quanti-
zation by utilizing the Dirac-Bergmann algorithm [40–44]
to handle the system’s second-class constraints [45], and
establish the consistent bracket structure. Afterwards,
we define the Boulware, Unruh, and Hartle–Hawking vac-
uum states. As an important quantum observable, the
number operator has been evaluated in the past Unruh
state to deduce the Hawking spectrum at future null in-
finity. Compared to the massless case [46], it is imper-
ative to study how the breaking of degeneracy is affect-
ing the Hawking spectrum for the massive field. Moti-
vated by this, we analyze spectrum for each polarization
of the Proca field. On the other hand, as a fundamental
building block of the quantum stress-energy tensor, and
investigation of the back reaction, it demands the com-
putation of the two point Green function as a first step
[5, 6]. Hence, we compute this physical quantity across
all the vacuum states, from which the structure of the
Proca condensate is further studied following numerical
analysis.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Sec-
tion II, we utilize the FKKS ansatz and derive the clas-
sical solution to EoM, which forms the fundamental ba-
sis of our investigation. In Section III, we impose the
boundary condition on these solutions and define the
key properties and identities to be satisfied by the re-
flection and transmission coefficients. Building upon this
in Section IV, we utilize the Proca inner product and or-
thonormalize the classical solutions. Using this orthonor-
mal basis in Section V, we promote the Dirac bracket to
commutator and perform the canonical quantization and
then consistently pass down the commutation relation
from the field and its conjugate momenta down to the cre-
ation and annihilation operators. Once the quantization
procedure has been outlined, we perform several analytic
continuations and define the relevant vacua. Next, in Sec-
tion VI, we derive the Hawking flux from first principles,
and then we move to Section VII, where we compute the
two point function and interpret the results. Finally, in
Section VIII, we conclude with possible future extensions
of the present work.

VIII.
CONCLUSION AND OUTLOOK
In the present work, we have presented a framework
for the canonical quantization of a massive vector field


## Page 17


17
in a Schwarzschild BH background, and explored the
significance of the mass term in the ultimate quantum
mechanical observables. The procedure begins with the
evaluation of classical mode functions, where we have
introduced a combination of VSH and FKKS basis to
handle the field components based on earlier classical
analysis of the Proca field [47, 50]. Moving forward to
the quantization steps, a central difficulty of the Proca
theory, absent in the scalar case [22, 23], is the pres-
ence of second-class constraints associated with the van-
ishing of the timelike component of conjugate momenta
[45, 66].
To overcome this step, we implemented the
Dirac–Bergmann algorithm [43, 66], explicitly identified
the symplectic structure of the constrained system, and
constructed the commutator algebra.
Using the orthonormalized basis, we define the Boul-
ware, Unruh, and Hartle–Hawking vacua and, as a pri-
mary observable, derived Hawking flux and two-point
correlation functions.
The Hawking spectrum exhibits
several characteristic features unique to the Proca field
due to the presence of an additional physical longitudi-
nal polarization. We observed that the exact degeneracy
between the e+ and o modes, expected in the massless
limit, is mildly lifted for a massive Proca field. Neverthe-
less, the two sectors remain remarkably close numerically
even for finite mass. In the massless limit, the e−polar-
ization becomes pure gauge. On the other hand, in the
massive case, it is a physical degree of freedom. Impor-
tantly, the monopole mode being contained entirely in
this new degree of freedom, the analogue of its dynami-
cal behaviour is completely absent in the massless case.
The total Hawking flux is dominated by the monopole
mode, with higher modes being suppressed due to the
centrifugal barrier. In the latter case, we also find that
polarization of e−is relatively suppressed compared to
e+ and o polarizations. Nevertheless, the relative sup-
pression of the e−sector becomes weaker at larger ℓ, indi-
cating a nontrivial polarization dependence in the Proca
greybody factors [80].
The Proca mass µ also introduces a physically impor-
tant threshold in the spectrum. For frequencies satisfy-
ing ω > µ, the field exhibits standard Hawking radiation
together with the additional longitudinal emission chan-
nel absent in Maxwell theory. By contrast, modes with
ω < µ are unable to propagate to null infinity due to the
effective gravitational potential barrier.
Instead, these
low-frequency excitations remain localized near the BH
and behave as trapped Proca fluctuations, potentially
relevant for the accumulation of ultralight dark photon
configurations around BHs [47].
We further analyzed differences of expectation values
between distinct quantum states, which provide finite ob-
servables without the need for explicit renormalization
(see [22] for the scalar field). In particular, near the hori-
zon, or asymptotically, we find that

exhibit a leading-order behavior proportional to 1/r2
with complicated radial dependence in the intermediate
region. The strong near-horizon enhancement reflects the
growth of quantum fluctuations in the thermal states rel-
ative to the Boulware vacuum. The vacuum states and
two-point functions constructed here provide the neces-
sary starting point for future computations of the renor-
malized stress-energy tensor ⟨Tµν⟩[81].
Such analyses
are essential for understanding the semiclassical backre-
action of massive vector fields on BH geometries.
Based on the present work, several directions can be
explored in future.
A natural extension is the quanti-
zation of the Proca field in rotating BH backgrounds,
where one must carefully derive the appropriate symplec-
tic structure and analyze the role of superradiant modes
in the construction of physical vacua [82]. Another im-
portant concern arises in the consistent definition and
renormalization of quantum states inside the BH horizon
(r < rh) [83, 84].
