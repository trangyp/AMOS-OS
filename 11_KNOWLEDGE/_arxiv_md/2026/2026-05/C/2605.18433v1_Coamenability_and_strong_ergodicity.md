COAMENABILITY AND STRONG ERGODICITY
BEN HAYES
Abstract. Following methods of Bannon-Marrakchi-Ozawa, we show that for coamenable inclu-
sion S ≤R of ergodic, probability measure-preserving relations, we have that R is strongly ergodic
if and only if S is strongly ergodic. More general results are given when S ≤R is coamenable, R is
strongly ergodic, but we do not assume ergodicity of S. As a consequence, if Λ ≤Γ is a coamenable
inclusion of groups, then any strongly ergodic Γ action has countably many ergodic components for
the Λ action, each of which is strongly ergodic.
1. Introduction
Strong ergodicity is a concept that arose out of the works [12, 36, 37] and is a fundamental
topic in the study of ergodic theory of nonamenable groups and measured group theory. If Γ is
a countable group, and (X, µ) is a standard probability space, we say that Γ ↷(X, µ) is strongly
ergodic if whenever (En)n is a sequence of measurable subsets of X with µ(gEn∆En) →n→∞0
for all g ∈Γ, then we necessarily have that µ(En)(1 −µ(En)) →0 (so up to subsequences, either
µ(En) →0 or µ(En) →1). It is direct to see that if (En)n is a sequence of measurable subsets of
X with µ(gEn∆En) →n→∞0 for all g ∈Γ, then µ(γEn∆En) →0 for all γ in the full group, [RΓ,X]
of Γ ↷(X, µ) defined by
[RΓ,X] = {γ : X →X : γ is a bimeasurable bijection and γ(x) ∈Γx for all almost every x ∈X}.
It can thus be regarded as property of the orbit equivalence relation of this action, RΓ,X =
{(x, γ(x)) : X ∈X}. More generally, there is a general notion of strong ergodicity for discrete, prob-
ability measure-preserving, orbit equivalence relations (we remark that there are genuine subtleties
in considering strong ergodicity for actions when dropping the measure-preserving assumption, see
e.g the recent paper [24]). In particular, strong ergodicity of the action is an orbit equivalence
invariant for the action.
Part of its importance is its connection to many notions and problems in the field.
Strong
ergodicity is implied by spectral gap (i.e. absence of almost-invariant unit vectors) of the Koopman
representation of Γ on L2-functions with mean zero (the converse is false, see [37, Example 2.7])
and is equivalent to spectral gap of the Koopman representation of the full group of Γ ↷(X, µ) (see
[36, Theorem 2.4]). It can thus be viewed as both the equivalence relation version of spectral gap,
and as such asserts some version of expansion (in the sense of expander graphs) for the relation.
It also admits a cohomological characterization see [36], and can be characterized (in the ergodic

There is a fruitful analogy between strongly ergodic relations and full von Neumann algebras, in
the sense of Connes [9, 8]. Indeed, it is direct to see from the definitions that if the von Neumann
algebra of an orbit equivalence relation is full, then that relation is strongly ergodic. The converse
is false (see [11] for a counterexample).
There is a natural notion of coamenability for inclusions of discrete, probability measure-preserving
equivalence relations, e.g. one could simply demand that the corresponding inclusion of von Neu-
mann algebras is a relatively amenable inclusion in the sense of [33]. In [25], we investigated several
other equivalent ways of defining an inclusion of relations to be coamenable, including several equiv-
alences which are analogous to characterizations of amenability of relations given in [10, 31, 41, 40].
In [2], Bannon-Marrakchi-Ozawa showed that if N ≤M is an inclusion of von Neumann algebras
which are factors (i.e. with trivial center), and if M is full, then so is N (in fact, the authors have
general results only assuming fullness and factoriality of M). This resolved a question of Popa from
[33]. Due to the analogy between fullness and strong ergodicity, at the CIRM conference “‘Orbit
equivalence and topological and measurable dynamics” Marrakchi asked us the following: if S ≤R
is a coamenable inclusion of ergodic, probability measure-preserving orbit equivalence relations, is
it true that S is strongly ergodic if and only if R is strongly ergodic? In this paper, we showed that
the answer to Marrakchi’s question is yes.
Theorem 1.1. Let S ≤R be ergodic, discrete, measure-preserving equivalence relations on a stan-
dard probability space (X, µ). If S ≤R is coamenable, then R is strongly ergodic if and only if S is
strongly ergodic.
By the ergodic decomposition, the study of inclusions S ≤R of probability measure-preserving
equivalence relations can be reduced to the case where R is ergodic. However, we cannot further
reduce to the case that S is also ergodic. Thus, it is natural to investigate a generalization of the
above Theorem to the case where R is assumed strongly ergodic and where we only assume S is
coamenable in R.
Theorem 1.2. Let S ≤R be ergodic, discrete, measure-preserving equivalence relations on a stan-
dard probability space (X, µ). If S ≤R is coamenable, and R is strongly ergodic, then there is a
positive measure, S-invariant set E ⊆X so that S|E is strongly ergodic.
Recall that a probability measure-preserving relation R on a standard probability space (X, µ)
has countable ergodic decomposition if there is a countable set I, and a partition (modulo null sets)
(Ei)i∈I by positive measure, R-invariant subsets of X so that R|Ei is ergodic for each i ∈I. We
refer to R|Ei as the ergodic components of R.

It would be natural to iterate Theorem 1.2 via a measure exhaustion argument to deduce that a
coamenable subrelation of a strongly ergodic relation has countably many ergodic components, each
of which are strongly ergodic. To do this, one would simply have to prove that any positive measure
subset F ⊆X contains a positive measure set F0 for which S|F0 is strongly ergodic. However, a
subtlety appears when attempting this argument which is that (see [25, Example 1]) there are
cases where S ≤R is coamenable, but nevertheless there are positive measure subsets E of X for
which S|E ≤R|E is not coamenable. The relevant notion that fixes this is to assume that S ≤R is
everywhere coamenable, meaning that S|E ≤R|E is coamenable for every positive measure E ⊆X.
We refer to [25, Section 4] for a detailed discussion of other equivalent notions. This is precisely the
condition we need to apply a measure exhaustion argument, and Theorem 1.2 implies the following
corollary.
Corollary 1.3. Let S ≤R be ergodic, discrete, measure-preserving equivalence relations on a
standard probability space (X, µ). If S ≤R is everywhere coamenable, and R is strongly ergodic,
then S has countable ergodic decomposition and each ergodic component of S is strongly ergodic.
We remark that the conclusion of the above theorem is related to work of Chifan-Ioana [6],
indeed they proved the remarkable result that if S is a nowhere amenable relation (in the sense
that all nontrivial restrictions are nonamenable) which is a subquotient relation of a Bernoulli orbit
equivalence relation of a nonamenable group, then S has countable ergodic decomposition and each
ergodic component of S is strongly ergodic. As discussed in [6], this property is of utility in the
study of percolations of graphs (see [15] for recent connections) and can be used to simplify part
of the celebrated Gaboriau-Lyons theorem [23] asserting that every nonamenable group contains a
“measurable” copy of the free group on 2-generators.
In [25, Proposition 7.2] we show that if Λ ≤Γ is a coamenable inclusion of countable groups,
and if Γ ↷(X, µ) is any probability measure-preserving action (not assumed essentially free) on a
standard probability space (X, µ), then the orbit equivalence relation for the Λ action is everywhere
coamenable in the orbit equivalence relation for the Γ action. Thus Corollary 1.3 immediately
implies the following.
Corollary 1.4. Let Λ ≤Γ be a coamenable inclusion of countable groups.
Suppose (X, µ) is
a standard probability space and Γ ↷(X, µ) is a strongly ergodic probability measure-preserving
action. Then Λ ↷(X, µ) has countably many ergodic components, each of which is strongly ergodic.

1.2. Strategy of the proof. Our main strategy follows that of [2]. We sketch the proof in the
case that both S, R are ergodic. First, as mentioned above, we show (see Corollary 4.10) that strong
ergodicity of R is equivalent to saying that if a representation H is weakly equivalent to L2(X) (in
the sense of weak containment, see the discussion in Section 4), then L2(X) embeds in H. This is
similar to [2, Proposition 3.2].
We also use that S being strongly ergodic is the same as the action of [S] on the ultrapower
algebra L∞(X)ω (discussed in Section 5.1 ) having no nonconstant invariant vectors. Adapting
[2, Proof of Theorem A], we build an intermediate relation S ≤bS ≤R with the property that if
γ ∈[R] acts trivially on all elements of L∞(X)ω which are fixed by bS then γ ∈[bS]. Adapting
[2, Lemma 3.4], we show that this property implies that L2(R/bS) is weakly contained in L2(X).
Coamenability of S ≤R then forces L2(X) to be weakly equivalent to L2(R/bS). As mentioned
above, strong ergodicity of R then implies that L2(R/S) has an invariant vector, and analogous
to the group setting (see Corollary 4.10) we must have that bS is finite index in R. This implies
that bS is strongly ergodic, and as in [2, Proof of Theorem A], the way bS is constructed will then
imply strong ergodicity of S. Minor changes have to be made here in the case that S is not ergodic,
namely we end up only having that bS is finite index in R after restricting to a positive measure
S-invariant set. Ultimately this leads to the proof of 1.2, which as mentioned implies Corollaries
1.3,1.4.
