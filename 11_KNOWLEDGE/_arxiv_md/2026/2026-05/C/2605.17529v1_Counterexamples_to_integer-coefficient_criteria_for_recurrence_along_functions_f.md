COUNTEREXAMPLES TO INTEGER-COEFFICIENT
CRITERIA FOR RECURRENCE ALONG FUNCTIONS
FROM A HARDY FIELD
KANGBO OUYANG, LEIYE XU, AND SHUHAO ZHANG
Abstract. We give negative answers to two questions of Bergelson,
Moreira, and Richter concerning recurrence along functions from a Hardy
field. For the pair f1(t) = t3/2 and f2(t) = λt3/2 + t, where λ ∈R \ Q,
singled out in their integer-coefficient derivative-span question, we prove
that every F ∈∇Z-span(f1, f2) satisfies limt→∞|F(t)| ∈{0, ∞}. Nev-
ertheless, there is a set E ⊂N of positive natural density such that
Rf1(E) ∩Rf2(E) is piecewise syndetic and not thick.
Thus the pro-
posed integer-coefficient replacement does not imply thickness.
We further show that, even under the same full integer derivative-
span condition, the common return-time set may be empty. This stronger
obstruction also gives a negative answer to their question asking whether
the recurrence conclusion of Theorem A follows from joint intersectivity
of the integer polynomials in poly(f1, . . . , fk).
The constructions use
elementary Bohr sets.
1. Introduction and main results
Throughout, N = {1, 2, . . .}. Questions about recurrence along prescribed
sequences are a central theme in ergodic Ramsey theory. Furstenberg’s er-
godic proof of Szemer´edi’s theorem initiated the use of multiple recurrence
to study arithmetic configurations in sets of positive density [11]. Polyno-
mial refinements were obtained by Bergelson and Leibman [1], and the cor-
responding joint-intersectivity criterion for polynomial configurations was
proved by Bergelson, Leibman, and Lesigne [3].
Beyond polynomial it-
erates, recurrence results along Hardy-field sequences were developed by
Frantzikinakis and Wierdl [10], by Frantzikinakis [8, 9], and more recently
by Bergelson, Moreira, and Richter [4, 5].
If E ⊂N and u : N →Z, write E −r = {m ∈N : m + r ∈E} and
Ru(E) = {n ∈N : E ∩(E −u(n)) ̸= ∅}. Thus n ∈Ru(E) means that E
contains two elements whose difference is u(n). For a real-valued function
f, we put Rf(E) = R⌊f⌋(E). The problem considered here is how large
Rf1(E) ∩· · · ∩Rfk(E) must be when f1, . . . , fk are functions of polynomial
growth from a Hardy field.
