Abstract
We propose a Bayesian latent variable model to estimate covariate-assisted dependence structures across mul-
tiple modalities of multivariate data that may be observed asynchronously. This setting commonly arises in
longitudinal biomedical research, especially in observational and clinical studies of complex diseases, where
dynamic and heterogeneous dependence across biomarker modalities can be pathologically and clinically
informative. For example, the biological diagnosis and staging of Alzheimer’s disease require integrated eval-
uation of multimodal biomarkers, including imaging and biofluid biomarkers, and the Alzheimer’s Disease
Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) study has collected biomarker data longitudinally for over two decades.
However, quantitative analysis is often challenged by asynchronous collection of multimodal profiles due to
study design and data collection constraints. Common analytic strategies that restrict inference to complete
observations or analyze each modality separately can lose information and introduce bias. Therefore, we
aim to jointly model all available data and estimate the population-level cross-modal dependence structure
that evolves over time and varies across demographic or clinical groups, where the cross-covariance matrices
for modality pairs serve as the primary quantities of interest. The proposed model uses modality-specific
low-rank loading structures with shared latent variables to borrow information across modalities, visits, and
subjects while accounting for repeated measurements. The application to ADNI data reveals clinically mean-
ingful patterns in longitudinal cross-modal biomarker dependence, and the simulation study shows improved
recovery under limited modality synchrony.
Keywords: multimodal multivariate data, asynchronous longitudinal data, covariance matrix, dependence
structure, Bayesian inference, latent variable model, multimodal biomarkers

Introduction
Understanding the dynamics and heterogeneity in dependence across multiple modalities of biomarkers
facilitates biological, pathogenic and therapeutic research (Strimbu et al. 2010, Califf 2018), especially for
complex diseases that are chronic and influenced by various factors (Schork 1997, Johansson et al. 2023). For
example, the biological diagnosis and staging of Alzheimer’s disease requires joint consideration of multimodal
biomarker profiles, such as amyloid and tau positron emission tomography (PET), cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)
and plasma biomarkers, and the pathology varies by population (Therriault et al. 2024, Jack et al. 2024).
Quantitatively, the data profiles of many biomarker modalities can be naturally represented as multivariate
random vectors, and the interactive mechanisms across modalities could be captured by the cross-covariance

Existing methods for asynchronous longitudinal data typically focus on settings in which time-varying
response and covariate processes are observed irregularly on different time grids (Xiong et al. 2010, S¸ent¨urk
et al. 2013, Cao et al. 2015, Cao et al. 2016, Chen et al. 2017, Sun et al. 2021, Li et al. 2022, Li et al. 2023,
Chang et al. 2023, Liu et al. 2023, Zhong et al. 2024, Lu et al. 2025), with the objective of quantifying the
association strength between the two sets of processes. Additionally, Liu et al. 2026 proposed to estimate
time-varying odds ratio and relative risk for bivariate binary variables. In contrast to existing approaches, the
proposed work is developed for a different setting. Here, asynchronous multimodal data refer to settings in
which different modalities of multivariate response vectors may not be simultaneously collected, so that each
record contains all or only a subset of the modalities. The covariates are either time-invariant or observed
concurrently with the available response measurements. Rather than modeling the association between a
response process and a covariate process, our objective is to estimate the cross-covariance structure among
biomarker modalities conditional on time and participant characteristics. Credible intervals and associated
summaries for individual cross-covariance elements are used to quantify uncertainty in pairwise dependence
patterns. The proposed model is suitable for observational and clinical studies with a limited number of
repeated measurements per subject, making subject-specific smoothing or trajectory estimation unreliable
for standard functional data analysis methods. Existing practice relies primarily on naive procedures to
restrict the analysis to complete observations only within data subsets defined by time and demographic
variables, leading to loss of information and potential bias, especially when the total number of observations
is limited.

2
Motivation
The goal of this work is to fully utilize real-world longitudinal multimodal data to characterize population-
level dependence across biomarker modalities over time and across heterogeneous groups, thereby providing
quantitative summaries for biological and clinical evaluation. Statistically, the proposed method aims
• to model covariate-dependent residual cross-covariance matrices for Gaussian random vectors jointly
with fixed and random effects;
• to incorporate asynchronously observed random vectors through a multivariate statistical approach.

3.3.4
Identifiability
We constrain A to be upper triangular with positive diagonal entries to ensure identifiability for compu-
tational stability, following the convention of the thin QR decomposition (Golub et al. 2013). The param-
eterization in the model inference involves the product ΓijA which remains the same for any nonsingular
rotation applied on Γij and A. Although the individual matrix is not of interest for interpretation and an
identifiable combination of the quantities is sufficient for estimation, the ambiguity causes practical problems
in Bayesian sampling such as slow mixing and multiple equivalent modes due to equivalent representation
of the same likelihood.
3.3.5
Rank Determination
The proposed model employs a truncated estimation approach to recover the main underlying structure, and
the determination of rank may affect the model performance. Therefore, we conducted simulation studies to
examine its impact to guide practical use.

Comparator
We propose the multivariate method to address practical challenges commonly encountered in applied analy-
sis involving longitudinal multimodal data. There are no existent off-the-shelf methods that directly estimate
the cross-covariance when covariates are continuous. When the covariates are categorical (e.g., gender, race),
the naive strategy is to first fit mixed effect models for each response variable individually followed by cal-
culating the pairwise residual covariance on the data subset corresponding to each category. Therefore, we
set x1 and x2 to be categorical to compare against this naive analytic strategy.

Simulation Results
4.2.1
Cross-covariance
As illustrated in the top row of Figure 1, the estimation of cross-covariance from the proposed method out-
performs the naive approach consistently across all simulation settings, regardless of the norm used in the
evaluation. Comparably, the percentage of synchronous observations has a greater impact on performance,
with a mild increasing trend in error observed as this percentage decreases. Notably, there is a mild in-
creasing trend in norms of contrast with respect to the fitted rank and sample size because the likelihood is
jointly maximized across multiple sets of parameters involving fixed and random effects, as well as variance
and covariance. As the results indicate, in this simulation study, the quadratic term of Γ that is more closely
related to variance estimation brings more distinguishable likelihood gain as the rank increases and there-
fore prioritized compared to the linear term. Besides, under settings with small sample sizes and limited
synchronous data, introducing large rank may lead to unstable estimates. Increasing sample size enhances
likelihood information for fixed effect estimation, whereas dependence estimation shows a slight increase in
error.
4.2.2
Cross-correlation
The bottom row of Figure 1 shows that the estimation of cross-correlation benefits from rank increase in the
model fitting. Under favorable data conditions with larger sample size and more synchronous observations,
higher rank could be employed for better performance. When the sample size is small, the proposed method
demonstrates superior performance even under relatively low-rank specifications, and a moderate rank choice
is adequate in the presence of substantial asynchronous data, whereas large ranks may reduce model stability.

Application

Initiative (ADNI) data with the proposed model. Clinically, PET testing results are usually recommended
for confirmed disease evaluation but their clinical use is limited by cost and accessibility (Altomare et al.
2023). In comparison, plasma biomarkers are promising in the diagnosis and prognosis of Alzheimer’s disease
which are noninvasive and convenient to collect, while their performance in primary care requires further
confirmation (Hansson et al. 2022).

Analytically, most clinical studies have focused on individual assessment of biomarkers or their joint utility
for prediction, whereas the dependence structure across biomarkers remains less well understood. Therefore,
we applied the proposed multivariate method and analyzed a total of 490 observations from 112 participants
each with at least four repeated measurements, where 125 (25.5%) observations have both plasma and tau
PET biomarker profiles. Specifically, 6 plasma biomarkers (p-tau217, Aβ42, Aβ42/Aβ40, ptau217/Aβ42,

NfL and GFAP) that have been widely studied in Alzheimer’s disease were included, and tau PET measures
from 6 regions that are most often to be positive (entorhinal cortex, inferior temporal, the amygdalae, the
parahippocampal gyri, the middle temporal and the fusiform gyri) were incorporated (St-Onge et al. 2024)
with log transformation. The visit-level covariates included diagnostic status (cognitively unimpaired and
mild cognitive impairment) and age, and the subject-level covariates included gender, Apolipoprotein E
(APOE) ε4 carrier status and race.
Figure 2 shows that the overall findings from the proposed model are consistent with existing literature
that p-tau217 and Aβ42 are positively and negatively associated with tau PET pathology, respectively, and
involved biomarker ratios are also associated with tau PET burden. GFAP and NfL are broadly positively
associated with tau PET. The time- and subgroup-specific results indicate that the dependence structure
remains relatively stable partly due to the population-level modeling. The present analysis is intended to
illustrate the application of the proposed model, and a more comprehensive analysis of the ADNI data for
clinical interpretation may require additional consideration of study design and data collection details, as
well as covariate interaction effects.
6
Discussion
The proposed model characterizes the covariate-dependent cross-covariance matrices among asynchronously
observed random vectors in longitudinal studies.
Several features of the proposed framework are worth
emphasizing.
First, the target of inference is a population-level cross-covariance, indexed by time and
demographic or clinical variables, rather than an individualized structure.
The subject-specific random
effects are included to account for within-subject correlation and to improve population-level inference.
Second, the low-rank representation is intended to recover dominant shared dependence patterns across
modalities, and therefore the method is well suited for real-world data with substantial noise. Third, because
the likelihood is constructed from the observed modality blocks at each visit, the method can use partially
observed multimodal records that would otherwise be discarded in complete-case analyses.
Although the proposed model is appropriate for settings where each subject contributes a limited number
of repeated measurements, convergence issues may arise and the estimation of random effects can become
unstable when the observations are sparse such that most subjects have only two or three longitudinal
measurements.
In addition, the model performs well when the signals are concentrated in a moderate
number of dominant directions. When the variance is distributed more evenly across many directions, the
posterior mixing tends to be slow with reduced estimation performance.
Further investigation is required to establish a hypothesis testing procedure to confirm the factors as-
sociated with the dependence structure.
In current work, the cross-covariance matrices are modeled as
covariate-dependent with the goal of making estimation at specified time points and subgroups rather than
conducting formal association tests. Accordingly, covariates primarily serve to index subgroups for inter-
pretation, whereas adjusting for potential confounders facilitates exploratory comparisons by aligning de-
mographic characteristics across groups. Such estimation cannot be readily performed using naive analytic
methods especially when covariates of interest are continuous, and the development of a rigorous testing
framework for identifying key determinants of the global or specific cross-modal dependence could provide
further insight into the underlying mechanisms. Moreover, the current model assumes Gaussian distributions
which are appropriate for most biomarkers on the raw or log-transformed scale, and requires each modality
to be represented as a random vector rather than a single variable. Further developments are needed to
accommodate diverse forms of multimodal data encountered in practice.
