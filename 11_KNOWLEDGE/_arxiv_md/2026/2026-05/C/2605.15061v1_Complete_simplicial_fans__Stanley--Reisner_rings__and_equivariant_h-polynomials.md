Complete Simplicial Fans, Stanley–Reisner Rings, and equivariant
h-polynomial
Tao Gui
Abstract. We derive a graded character formula for the action of any finite
group on the Artinian reduction of the Stanley–Reisner ring of any complete
simplicial fan, which is given by an equivariant version of the classical h-
polynomial. This gives the graded character formula for the representation of
the group on the cohomology of the associated toric variety when the fan is
rational. As an application, we use a navel tool, which we called hybrid fan, to
compute the Poincar´e polynomial of the invariants of the Artinian reduction of
the Stanley–Reisner ring of any complete simplicial fan under a finite reflection
group action. This implies that the Poincar´e polynomial of the quotient of a
compact toric orbifold by any finite reflection group is equal to the Poincar´e
polynomial of the compact toric orbifold associated to the hybrid fan.
1. Introduction
A fundamental theorem in toric geometry states that there is a one-to-one cor-
respondence between (normal) toric varieties and fans of strongly convex rational
(with respect to some lattice) polyhedral cones[13]. This correspondence gives a
rich dictionary between the geometry of a toric variety and the combinatorics of
its associated fan. For example, the toric variety is an orbifold (that is, it has only
finite quotient singularity) if and only if the associated fan is simplicial, and the
toric variety is compact (in the classical topology) if and only if the associated fan
is complete[10, Theorem 3.1.19]. Furthermore, the toric variety is projective if and
only if the associated fan is polytopal (that is, it is the normal fan of some rational
convex polytope)[10, Theorem 6.2.1].
If a finite group W acts on a polytope or a fan preserving the defining lattice,
then this action induces an action of W on the associated toric variety, and hence
induces a graded representation of W on its cohomology. For the case of a Weyl
group W acting on the standard W-permutohedron and the normal fan consisting of
Weyl chambers, this representation has been extensively studied by Procesi[33], by
Dolgachev–Lunts[15], by Stembridge[41], and by Lehrer[28]. Each of these papers
provides a different perspective.
However, polytopes and fans can be non-rational with respect to any lattice1. In
such cases, the associated toric varieties does not exist. Nevertheless, there is always
a finite poset, the face poset of the polytope or fan, which encodes the torus orbit

2. Preliminaries
In this section, we fix our notations and definitions for later use.
2.1. Polytopes and Fans
Let V be a d-dimensional Euclidean space with a positive definite bilinear form
⟨−, −⟩, let P denote a d-dimensional polytope in V .
A (convex polyhedral) cone in V is a set of the form P R≥0vi with vi ∈V, i =
1, · · · , n, it is strongly convex if it does not contain any vector subspace other than
the origin. A fan Σ in V is a finite collection of strongly convex polyhedral cones
in V so that every face of a strongly convex cone in Σ is again in Σ, and the
intersection of any two cones in Σ is a face of each. We write Σk to denote the set
of all cones in Σ of dimension k. A one-dimensional cone in Σ1 is called a ray. Note
that the set of all faces of a cone σ forms a fan [σ], and we denotes σ(1) to be the
set of rays of this fan.
A polytope P in V gives rise to two fans ∆P and ΣP in V . The first fan ∆P is
known as the central fan of P, by choosing the origin to be an interior point of P
and coning off all faces F ̸= P. The second fan ΣP is known as the normal fan of
P, which can be defined to be the central fan of the polar dual P ∗of P.
One can get a poset F(Σ) out of a fan Σ, called the face poset or combinatorial
type of Σ, where the elements in F(Σ) are the cones in Σ and the partial order
τ ≤σ means a cone τ is a face of σ.
The support |Σ| of Σ is the union of all its cones. A fan Σ in V is complete
if |Σ| = V . The two fans ∆P and ΣP from a polytope are complete, but there
are combinatorial types of a complete fan which can not be realized as that of the
normal fan of any convex polytope, see for example [43, Example 5.10].
A fan Σ in V is simplicial if each cone in Σ is a cone over a simplex. Thus the
fan ∆P is a simplicial fan if and only if P is a simplicial polytope, which means
each face of P is a simplex.

did not involve toric varieties and so worked for a non-rational polytope P. For a
complete simplicial fan Σ (or more generally, for a simplicial sphere), the difficulty
to prove the g-conjecture is the lack of “ample” elements when one tries to establish
the hard Lefschetz theorem for A(Σ), see for example, [2] and [27].

of G on the face poset F(Σ). For any σ ∈F(Σ), let Gσ = {g ∈G : g · σ = σ}

ψσ : Gσ →GL (V/Vσ).

However, the converse of the above Proposition 4.8 is false—the hybrid fan can
be simplicial even if the original fan is non-simplicial.
Example 4.9. When a reflection group acts on a fan, the orbit of a set of rays
WJ ·{ρi} can easily contain more rays than the dimension of the space. The hybrid
fan effectively “compresses” or “folds” this complex orbit into just the |I| + |J|
generators. Thus, ΣW can have an exactly correct number of linearly independent
generators while representing a cone in Σ with far too many rays. We can visualize
this geometrically using quotient polytopes. Let the reflection group W = Z3

normal fan of the regular octahedron. Because all the vertices (the vertices on the
coordinate axes) of the octahedron are meeting by 4 facets, its normal fan contains
3-dimensional cones generated by 4 rays. Therefore, Σ is not simplicial. However,
by Proposition 4.6, the hybrid fan ΣW is the normal fan of the quotient polytope

The intersection of the regular octahedron with the positive octant is the standard
3-simplex (a tetrahedron). A tetrahedron is a simple polytope—exactly 3 faces meet
at every vertex. Thus, its normal fan ΣW is simplicial. The hybrid fan resolves the
non-simplicial nature of the original fan by folding the WJ-orbits out of the picture.

5. Questions and future directions
We end the paper by presenting some open problems and directions for future
research.
As explained in the introduction, Theorem 1.2 gives an evidence of the existence
of an isomorphism on the “cohomology” level, we leave the following problem to
the interested reader.
Problem 5.1. For a finite reflection group W acting linearly on V preserving a
complete simplicial fan Σ, construct an explicit graded ring isomorphism from the
Artinian reduction of the Stanley–Reisner ring A(ΣW ) of the hybrid fan to the
W-invariants A(Σ)
W of the Artinian reduction of the Stanley–Reisner ring of the
original fan Σ.
The desired isomorphism in the above problem is constructed for the normal fan
of a weight polytope (which is always simple) associated with a regular weight under
the action of the corresponding finite reflection group W in [21] and generalized to
any simple non-degenerate W-symmetric polytopes in [17]. Here, the assumption
of being non-degenerate is equivalent to the assumption that the W-action on the
normal fan is proper, see [17, Lemma 2.2]. We expect that the definition of the
hybrid fan can handle the difficulty of the action being non-proper.
Actually, we conjecture that there exists an isomorphism on the variety level.
Conjecture 5.2. Let W ⊂GL(V ) be a finite reflection group preserving a lattice
M ⊂V and an M-rational complete simplicial fan Σ ⊂V . Then the quotient
XΣ/W of the associated toric variety XΣ under the induced action of W is isomor-
phic to the toric variety XΣW associated with the hybrid fan ΣW .
As mentioned in the introduction, by virtue of Proposition 4.6, Crowley–Gong–
Simpson’s result [12, Corollary 1.3] show that the above Conjecture 5.2 is true for
(possibly non-simplicial) polytopal (hence complete) fans. Besides, their result [12,
Theorem 1.2] implies the above conjecture is also true for the fan [σ] (which is non-
complete) consist of the faces of a single strongly convex M-rational polyhedral cone
σ (by taking the saturated semigroup S in [12, Theorem 1.2’] to be the intersection
of the dual cone σ∨with M). So it is natural to ask the following question.6

Question 5.3. Is the above Conjecture 5.2 true for any W-invariant M-rational
fan? That is, is the quotient of any normal toric variety by a finite reflection group
still toric, and isomorphic to the toric variety associated to the hybrid fan?
As mentioned in the introduction, the Betti numbers and the usual cohomology
of a general toric variety are not invariants of the combinatorics of the associated
fan in general [29]. A better substitute of the usual cohomology for a general toric
variety is the intersection cohomology of Goresky–MacPherson[19, 20]. Stanley[37]
introduced the toric h-polynomial for any convex polytope P, which generalize
the h-polynomial defined for simplicial polytopes. Actually, Stanley defined this
polynomial for any Eulerian poset, which including the face poset F(Σ) of any
fan Σ.
It is known that Stanley’s toric h-polynomial compute the intersection
cohomology Poincar´e polynomial of the associated toric variety when P is ratio-
nal [14, 16]. Barthel–Brasselet–Fieseler–Kaup[3], Bressler–Lunts[8], Karu[26],and
Braden[7] developed a theory of combinatorial intersection cohomology of fans
(possibly non-rational, which do not define a toric variety). A beautiful result of
Barthel–Brasselet–Fieseler–Kaup implies that for any complete fan Σ, the Poincar´e
polynomial of the combinatorial intersection cohomology of Σ is given by Stanley’s
toric h-polynomial of F(Σ), see [7, Theorem 3.2]. Consider finite group actions on
fans, we have the following
Question 5.4. Can one derive a graded character formula for the action of a finite
group G on the combinatorial intersection group of a complete fan Σ in V , which
is induced by a linear action of G on V preserving Σ.
The approach of Dolgachev–Lunts[15] for the character formula of a Weyl group
acting on the cohomology of the toric variety associated with Weyl chambers might
be useful to the above question. We expect that the above formula should be given
by an equivariant version of Stanley’s toric h-polynomial, the equivariant Kazhdan–
Lusztig–Stanley polynomial introduced by Proudfoot [34], with the equivariant p-
kernel given by Stapledon in [39, Example 1.7] for the Eulerian poset F(Σ).
If one obtain a graded character formula for Question 5.4, one may wonder
whether a similar statement in Theorem 1.2 is true for any complete fan Σ by
replacing A(Σ) to the combinatorial intersection cohomology of Σ, in the spirit
of Conjecture 5.2. However, even if Conjecture 5.2 holds for any complete fans,
we do not expect such a simple “W-invariant of the combinatorial intersection
cohomology of Σ equals the combinatorial intersection cohomology of the hybrid
fan ΣW ” holds. Because even when the involved fans are rational, computing the
intersection cohomology of the quotient need to use the Decompostion theorem of
Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber[4] applying to the quotient map to analyze
the possible contributions from the singularities of the quotient. We end with
Problem 5.5. For a finite reflection group W acting linearly on V preserving a
complete fan Σ, derive a formula which relates the W-invariants of the combinato-
rial intersection cohomology of Σ and the combinatorial intersection cohomology of
the hybrid fan ΣW .
