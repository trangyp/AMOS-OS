Abstract
In Section 6 of the paper “Fra¨ıss´e’s Conjecture and big Ramsey de-
grees of structures admitting finite monomorphic decomposition”, we
applied the methods developed in earlier sections to show that a cer-
tain reduct of the generic permutation has finite big Ramsey degrees.
Unfortunately, this reduct was incorrectly identified as the generic par-
tial order. We are grateful to Jan Hubiˇcka for bringing this error to
our attention.
In this note we correct the statements that rely on this misidentifi-
cation and demonstrate that the reduct in question is in fact the generic
2-dimensional partial order. We emphasize that the arguments pre-
sented in Section 6 remain valid, with the sole exception of the Claim
in the proof of Theorem 6.4, whose role was to (incorrectly) identify
the reduct of the generic permutation as the generic partial order.
This correction has an unexpected positive consequence. Rather
than reproving a well-known result whose existing proof is already
notably elegant, this note demonstrates that our general framework

The importance of the weak amalgamation property was first recognized
by Ivanov [29] and later independently by Kechris and Rosendal [31] in their
study of generic automorphisms. The importance of the weak amalgamation
property with respect to the existence of generic structures was recognized
independently by Kubi´s [34], and Panagiotopoulos and Tent [41]. Namely,
if K is an age of a countable relational structure with (JEP) and (WAP),
then there is a unique (up to isomorphism) countable structure Ωon ω =
{0, 1, 2, . . .} with Age(Ω) = K whose isomorphism class is comeager in a
naturally defined Baire space of all structures on ω whose age is K, (see [41]
for details). We call Ωthe weak Fra¨ıss´e limit of K and say that Ωis generic
among countable structures whose age is K.

Addendum
The profile of a class K of finite relational structures is the integer-valued
function φK : N →N that assigns to each nonnegative integer n the num-
ber of structures in K with n elements, counted up to isomorphism. The
behavior of this function has been extensively studied, particularly in the
case where K is hereditary (i.e., closed under taking substructures) and
consists of objects such as graphs (directed or undirected), tournaments,
ordered sets, ordered graphs, or ordered hypergraphs. Moreover, a result of
Cameron [27] shows that the study of permutations—originating from the
Stanley–Wilf conjecture and its resolution by Marcus and Tardos [35]—fits
naturally into the framework of profiles of hereditary classes of ordered re-
lational structures (see [38, 39]).
These results demonstrate that the profile cannot exhibit arbitrary growth:
there are “jumps” in the possible growth rates. Typically, the growth is ei-
ther polynomial or faster than any polynomial (see [42, 43]). For several
10


## Page 11


classes of structures, the profile grows at least exponentially (e.g., tour-
naments [24, 26], ordered graphs and hypergraphs [23, 32], and permuta-
tions [30]) or at least as fast as the partition function (e.g., graphs [25]). For
further details, see the survey by Klazar [33].
As noted in [40], there are very few cases for which the profile φK of a
hereditary class K is actually a polynomial. This is why we introduce the
following relaxation: we say that φK is eventually polynomial if for some
non-negative integer n, the restriction of φK to {n, n + 1, n + 2, . . .} is a
polynomial function. In particular, Pouzet and Oudrar proved the following
in [40], where an interval decomposition of an ordered relational structure is
a monomorphic decomposition of the structure into intervals:
Theorem D. [40, cf. Theorem 1.1] Let K be a hereditary class of finite
ordered relational structures over a finite relational language. Then:
• either there exists an integer k such that every member of K has an
interval decomposition into at most k + 1 blocks, in which case K is
a finite union of ages of ordered relational structures, each having an
interval decomposition into at most k + 1 blocks, and the profile of K
is eventually polynomial (of degree at most k);
• or the profile of K is at least exponential.
Theorem E. Let S be a countable relational structure with a total order
relation < in its language. If the profile of Age(S) is eventually polynomial,
then:
(a) S has a finite monomorphic decomposition, and
(b) S has finite big Ramsey degrees if and only if every substructure of
S induced by a block in its minimal monomorphic decomposition is chained
by a chain having finite big Ramsey degrees.
Proof. (b) follows from (a) by Theorems 4.4 and 4.8, while (a) follows from
Theorem D by an easy application of K˝onig’s lemma. Let us quickly sketch
the argument for (a).
Let S be a countable first-order structure with a total order relation <
in its language, and assume that the profile of K = Age(S) is eventually
polynomial. Then by Theorem D we know that there exists an integer k
such that every member of K has an interval decomposition into at most
k + 1 blocks. Note that if B ⩽C are two finite substructures of S, then
every interval decomposition of C into at most k + 1 blocks has a restriction
that is an interval decomposition of B into at most k + 1 blocks. Now, take
any sequence S1 ⩽S2 ⩽S3 ⩽. . . of finite substructures of S such that
