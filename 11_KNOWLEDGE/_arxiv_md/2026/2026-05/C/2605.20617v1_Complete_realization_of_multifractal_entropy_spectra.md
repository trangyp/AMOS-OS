Abstract
We prove a complete realization theorem for multifractal entropy spectra of con-
tinuous potentials on a broad class of dynamical systems. More precisely, for every
H > 0 and every continuous concave function on a compact interval with maxi-
mum value H attained at a unique point, each system in this class with topological
entropy H admits a continuous potential whose multifractal entropy spectrum is
exactly that function. The same spectrum can moreover be realized by arbitrarily
many pairwise non-cohomologous potentials.
We also study the potential dependence of entropy spectra in the Hausdorff graph
metric, proving general lower semicontinuity and dense failure of upper semiconti-
nuity for non-trivial mixing subshifts of finite type.
Finally, as an application of the realization theorem, we use the Legendre trans-
form duality between multifractal entropy spectra and pressure functions to derive
a pressure flexibility theorem for pressure functions defined on the whole real line,
thereby giving an affirmative answer to a question of Kucherenko and Quas [34].

Introduction
A natural motivation for our problem comes from the flexibility program in dynam-
ics, initiated by Katok, which asks whether dynamical invariants can realize all values
compatible with their general constraints within a prescribed class of systems. Consid-
erable progress has been made in this direction for several important invariants, includ-
ing entropy, Birkhoff averages, Lyapunov exponents, and pressure functions; see, for in-
stance, [32, 25, 37, 54, 9, 10, 49, 34, 21, 5, 56, 38, 59, 33, 15, 55, 17, 23] and the references
therein. Most of these results concern numerical invariants. We instead study a func-
tional invariant: the multifractal entropy spectrum associated with a continuous poten-
tial.
This question arises naturally from multifractal analysis. Multifractal analysis inves-
tigates level sets defined by asymptotic quantities, such as Birkhoff averages, Lyapunov
exponents, and local dimensions, and quantifies their size by entropy, dimension, or re-
lated characteristics; see, for instance, [18, 22, 2, 12, 30, 28, 15, 26, 27, 31, 45, 40, 3, 4,
35, 44]. The associated spectra record how these asymptotic behaviors are distributed
throughout the system.
Such spectra satisfy strong general constraints. Under standard assumptions, en-
tropy spectra are concave functions on compact intervals, and their maximal value is
bounded by the topological entropy of the system. One is therefore led to ask a flexibility
question not about individual numerical invariants, but about the spectrum itself. To
the best of our knowledge, despite the extensive development of multifractal analysis,
this realization problem has not been addressed:
Under the general constraints, which functions can arise as multifractal spec-
tra for continuous potentials on systems in a prescribed class?

The main result of this paper shows that, for a broad class of dynamical systems, the
evident qualitative constraints on multifractal entropy spectra are the only ones. More
precisely, once the topological entropy H of the system is fixed, every continuous con-
cave function on a compact interval whose maximum equals H and whose maximizer
is unique can be realized exactly as the entropy spectrum of Birkhoff level sets for some
continuous potential. Thus the realization problem admits a complete solution:
continuous concavity with prescribed maximum H
⇕
realizability as a multifractal entropy spectrum.
The realization theorem above leads to two further questions: the stability of multifractal
entropy spectra under perturbations of the potential, and the corresponding flexibility
problem for topological pressure.
Since the rotation set varies with the potential, spectra associated with different po-
tentials do not in general have a common domain. It is therefore natural to compare
them via their graphs in the Hausdorff metric. In this metric, the spectrum map is lower
semicontinuous under our general assumptions, whereas upper semicontinuity fails on
a dense subset even for non-trivial mixing subshifts of finite type.
We use the realization theorem to study topological pressure. Via the Legendre trans-
form duality between multifractal entropy spectra and pressure functions, we obtain a
pressure flexibility theorem for pressure functions defined on the whole real line, thereby
answering a question of Kucherenko and Quas [34]. More generally, the full shift normal-
ization F(0) = logd is replaced by the intrinsic entropy constraint F(0) = htop(f ) = H, so
the pressure flexibility phenomenon extends to systems in our class with arbitrary pre-
scribed positive topological entropy.

1.1
Complete realization of multifractal entropy spectra
The realization results are formulated for dynamical systems satisfying a set of struc-
tural hypotheses. These hypotheses are designed to capture the common structural fea-
tures shared by a broad class of standard symbolic and uniformly hyperbolic models; see
Theorem 2.6 for the corresponding examples. This abstract formulation avoids model-
by-model constructions and isolates a general construction that produces multifractal
entropy spectra from thermodynamic formalism, saturated sets, and zero entropy ap-
proximation. We use these assumptions throughout the paper. Precise definitions are
recalled in Section 2.
(H1) the entropy map µ 7−→hµ(f ) is upper semicontinuous on Mf (X );
(H2) every Lipschitz potential on X has a unique equilibrium state;
(H3) (X , f ) has the saturated property for every invariant measure;
(H4) there exist two distinct uniquely ergodic measures with zero entropy;
(H5) ergodic measures with zero entropy are dense in Mf (X ).
We briefly describe the mechanism behind the realization theorem. It consists of
three steps.
4


## Page 5


First, we construct an entropy scale inside the ergodic measures: a compact family of
ergodic measures whose entropies vary continuously from 0 to htop(f ) along one branch
and then back to 0 along a second branch, with the two branches meeting only at the
measure of maximal entropy. In our setting, this structure is obtained from (H2), which
provides unique equilibrium states for Lipschitz potentials, together with the zero-entropy
uniquely ergodic measures in (H4).
Second, we encode the prescribed concave spectrum by an affine functional on the
simplex of invariant measures. The construction is first carried out on the compact er-
godic family, then extended to the face it generates, and finally to the whole simplex. The
upper semicontinuity in (H1) ensures the lower semicontinuity of the relevant set-valued
constraint map, which is the key input for the affine selection argument.
Third, we pass from this measure theoretic construction to level set entropy. The
saturated property in (H3) yields the required entropy formula for Birkhoff level sets,
thereby completing the realization of the prescribed spectrum.
The hypotheses are not intended as a minimal axiom system. They provide a unified
setting in which the same realization mechanism applies to standard symbolic and uni-
formly hyperbolic models. In particular, (H5) is only used for the multiplicity statement
in Theorem B, not for the single spectrum realization in Theorem A. The proof uses (H2)
only through the entropy monotone family of ergodic equilibrium states constructed in
Section 3. The assumptions are chosen for uniform applicability rather than optimal
generality. Theorem 2.6 shows that these hypotheses hold for the standard symbolic and
uniformly hyperbolic models considered here.

We next relate the realization theorem to the existing multifractal formalism. Classi-
cal multifractal analysis usually starts from a prescribed observable and studies the en-
tropy, pressure, or dimension of the corresponding Birkhoff level sets. In that direction,
one obtains variational formulae and regularity properties for the resulting spectra, such
as concavity, continuity under suitable hypotheses, and Legendre type duality with pres-
sure.
Here we address the complementary inverse problem. Instead of fixing a potential
and analyzing the spectrum it produces, we prescribe the entire entropy curve in ad-
vance and ask whether it can be realized by some continuous potential. Theorem A
shows that, for the systems considered here, the evident formal restrictions are also suffi-
cient: once the ambient entropy H = htop(f ) is fixed, every continuous concave function
on a compact interval with maximum H attained at a unique point occurs as the multi-
fractal entropy spectrum of a continuous potential. Our contribution is not a new con-
cavity or variational principle for spectra of fixed potentials, but a realization theorem
for all admissible spectral shapes.
1.2
Discontinuity phenomena for spectrum maps
The complete realization theorem describes the range of the spectrum map. We next
study its stability under perturbations of the potential. The resulting picture is asym-
metric: spectra persist under small perturbations in the lower semicontinuity sense, but
upper semicontinuity can fail densely even in the classical setting of mixing subshifts of
finite type.

tentials need not have a common domain. We therefore compare spectra through their

1.3
Application: pressure flexibility
Kucherenko and Quas [34] proved the following pressure flexibility theorem for full shifts:
Theorem 1.3. Let α > 0 and let F(t) be a convex function on (α,∞) such that the support-
ing lines to F at each t ∈(α,∞) have vertical axis intercepts in a closed interval [b,c] ⊂
[0,∞). Then there exists a full shift on a finite alphabet and a continuous potential ϕ such
that P f (tϕ) = F(t) for all t ∈(α,∞).
They also observed that including the origin is not automatic. Indeed, on the full
shift over d symbols, the pressure at the origin is forced to be
P f (0) = htop(f ) = logd.
Thus including the origin imposes an additional rigidity which is absent from the domain
(α,∞). In particular, once the origin is included, the value of the pressure function fixes
the topological entropy of the underlying system. This leads to the following question,
explicitly raised by Kucherenko and Quas (their original problem is formulated in the
multidimensional setting).
Question. [34, Question 1] Let F be a convex function on R whose supporting lines in-
tercepts lie in a bounded subinterval of R≥0 and such that F(0) = logd for some integer
d ≥2. Does there exist a continuous function ϕ on the full shift on d symbols such that
P f (tϕ) = F(t) for all t ∈R?
Using the realization theorem for multifractal entropy spectra, we prove a pressure
flexibility result giving an affirmative answer to this question. The following consequence

Methods of proof
The proof of Theorem A is the core of the paper. The proof combines thermodynamic
formalism, affine geometry on the simplex of invariant measures, and convex selection.
The thermodynamic ingredient is an entropy scale inside the ergodic measures. Us-
ing uniqueness of equilibrium states for Lipschitz potentials, we construct a continuous
path µL −→mmax −→µR of ergodic measures such that
hµL(f ) = 0,
hmmax(f ) = htop(f ),
hµR(f ) = 0,
the entropy varies monotonically on each side of mmax, and the two branches intersect
only at mmax. This path provides the entropy data used to encode the prescribed concave
function.
The convex geometric ingredient is a two-step affine extension procedure. One first
prescribes the desired affine data on a compact family of ergodic measures and extends
them to the compact face it generates. One then applies Lazar’s affine selection theorem
to obtain a continuous affine functional on the whole simplex Mf (X ). This functional
is represented in the form µ 7−→

semicontinuity in (H1) is used to ensure the lower semicontinuity of the associated set-
valued constraint map required by the affine selection argument.
The dynamical ingredient is the passage from measure theoretic constraints to level
set entropy. The saturated property in (H3) yields the required entropy formula for Birkhoff
level sets, thereby showing that the potential constructed above realizes the prescribed
spectrum. This proves the realization theorem without relying on model-specific sym-
bolic constructions, and applies uniformly to the classes covered by Theorem 2.6.
Theorem B is obtained by refining the same construction. The key point is that one
can perturb the affine data on suitably chosen zero entropy ergodic measures outside
the compact set without changing the prescribed spectrum, thereby producing many
non-cohomologous realizing potentials.
The proof of Theorem C is direct. Starting from a point on the graph of the limiting
spectrum, one realizes it by an invariant measure and then uses upper semicontinuity
of the entropy map together with continuity of the integral functional to transport this
point to the multifractal entropy spectra of nearby potentials.
The proof of Theorem D uses a perturbative construction in the symbolic setting.
Starting from a periodic measure, we construct a continuous path of ergodic measures
along which the metric entropy varies strictly monotonically. Combined with a Shinoda-
type perturbation argument, this yields a dense subset of C(X ) consisting of potentials
whose sets of maximizing measures contain uncountably many ergodic measures with
pairwise distinct entropies. A further small perturbation can then isolate one of these
measures as the unique maximizing measure, while causing a definite drop in the spec-
trum at the corresponding rotation value. Thus arbitrarily small perturbations in the
supremum norm can produce a definite change in the graph of the spectrum in the met-
ric dms, upper semicontinuity therefore fails on a dense subset.
Finally, Corollary E is obtained from multifractal entropy spectra by Legendre trans-
form duality. In much of the existing literature, the direction of argument is typically the

reverse: pressure functions are used as a principal tool for analyzing multifractal entropy

Outline of the paper
Section 2 reviews the basic framework used throughout the paper: the variational prop-
erties of multifractal entropy spectra, the structural hypotheses and examples, and the
convex analytic tools needed for the affine extension and Legendre duality arguments.
Section 3 develops the general realization mechanism behind the main theorem. We
first construct a compact family of ergodic equilibrium states carrying the required en-
tropy curve, and then carry out a two-step affine extension procedure: the target data
are extended from a compact ergodic set C to the face F = conv(C), and then from F to
the whole simplex Mf (X ) by means of an affine selection theorem. This yields the re-
alizing potentials and also the non-cohomologous multiplicity result. Section 4 studies
the dependence of the spectrum on the potential. We prove lower semicontinuity under
an abstract upper semicontinuity assumption on the entropy map, and then show that
upper semicontinuity fails on a dense subset for mixing subshifts of finite type. Finally,
Section 5 applies the realization theorem to topological pressure. Using the Legendre
transform duality between multifractal entropy spectra and pressure functions, we ob-
tain a pressure flexibility result for pressure functions defined on the whole real line.
