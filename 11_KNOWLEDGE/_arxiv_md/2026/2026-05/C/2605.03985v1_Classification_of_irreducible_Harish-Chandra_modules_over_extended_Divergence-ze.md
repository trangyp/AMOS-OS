1. Introduction:
Infinite dimensional Lie algebras and their representations have been studied extensively
because of their importance in both mathematics and mathematical physics. In particular,
they arise naturally in conformal field theory, string theory, and soliton theory. An
important class of representations consists of those that decompose into finite-dimensional
weight spaces.
These representations are known as Harish-Chandra modules in the
literature, and their classification for various classes of infinite dimensional Lie algebras
has been studied by many authors.
One of the most prominent and widely studied infinite-dimensional Lie algebras is
the Virasoro algebra, which plays a fundamental role in theoretical physics. It can be
realized as the one dimensional central extension of the Lie algebra of diffeomorphisms
of the circle, or equivalently, as the one dimensional central extension of the Lie algebra
of derivations of the Laurent polynomial ring in one variable. The classification of all
irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over the Virasoro algebra was conjectured by V. Kac
[KR], and later proved by O. Mathieu in 1992 [M].
The higher dimensional analogue of the Virasoro algebra is the Witt algebra. Let An
denote the Laurent polynomial ring in n variables. Its derivation algebra Wn = Der (An)
is popularly known as the Witt algebra and can be identified with the Lie algebra of
polynomial vector fields on an n-dimensional torus (see Section 2). In contrast to the
one-variable case, Wn is centrally closed for n ≥2. The representation theory of Witt
algebras has been studied extensively by many mathematicians (see [BF, E1, E2, GLZ,
GZ, L1, L2, MZ]). In [S], Shen introduced a class of modules over the Witt algebra Wn
arising from irreducible modules over the general linear Lie algebra gln, which were also
given by Larsson in [L2]. Their irreducibility was determined by Eswara Rao in [E1].
Later, Billig and Futorny gave the complete classification of all irreducible Harish-Chandra
modules over Wn [BF].
Extended affine Lie algebras (EALAs for short; see [E3] for the definition) were
introduced by Hoegh-Krohn and Torresani in [HT] under the name of quasisimple Lie
algebras. Over the last three decades, their structure theory has been intensively studied by
many mathematicians (see [AABGP, BGK, N, Y] and references therein). These algebras
provide a natural framework extending finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras (nullity 0)
and affine Kac–Moody algebras (nullity 1). Other important examples include toroidal
EALAs [B3], Hamiltonian EALAs, contact EALAs, and minimal EALAs [E3]. It is known
that full toroidal Lie algebras are not EALAs, since they do not admit a nondegenerate
symmetric invariant bilinear form. To obtain an EALA structure, one considers an
important subalgebra of Wn consisting of all skew derivations of An. This subalgebra of
Wn is called the Lie algebra of divergence-zero vector fields on a torus, denoted by Dn.
In [CLT], it was shown that the classification of irreducible integrable Harish-Chandra
modules over nullity 2 toroidal EALAs of type A1 reduces to the classification of Harish-
Chandra modules over D2. The connection of the Lie algebra D2 with the vertex algebra
has been established in [GW]. The restrictions of the tensor modules over Wn (introduced

The Witt algebra Wn acts naturally on the polynomial algebra An by derivations.
Therefore, the emerging semidirect product ¯Gn := Wn ⋉An arises naturally and is called
the extended Witt algebra. In [E2], Eswara Rao classified irreducible modules with finite-
dimensional weight spaces over ¯Gn that admit compatible actions of both the Lie algebra
Wn and An. This result was extended to a classification of indecomposable modules in
this category [B2]. Irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over Gn with nontrivial An-action
were classified in [GLZ].
In this paper, we study irreducible representations of the subalgebra Gn := Dn ⋉An of
¯Gn that have finite-dimensional weight spaces. Since the classification of irreducible Harish-
Chandra modules over Dn is still unknown, we impose the additional assumption that
the modules have nontrivial A′

actions of Dn and An. For the two variable case, representations of G2 were studied in
[GL, LT, LTW].
Our methods are inspired by and adapted from the work of Guo-Liu [GL], Lin-Tan
[LT], and Lu–Zhao [LZ] on higher rank Virasoro like algebras. A key difference is that
higher-rank Virasoro algebras contain a solenoidal Lie algebra (see [P] for the definition),
which is isomorphic to the centerless Virasoro algebra and plays a crucial role in the
classification problem. In contrast, the divergence-zero Lie algebra does not contain any
subalgebra isomorphic to the centerless Virasoro algebra. In particular, the techniques
in [GLZ, LZ], which rely on the representation theory of the Virasoro algebra, cannot
be applied in our setting. So, the divergence-zero setting requires several additional
arguments, since one must work with the subalgebra Dn in place of the full derivation
algebra. We first show that any irreducible uniformly bounded Gn-module with nontrivial
A′
n-action admits compatible actions of Dn and An. This implies that such modules are
jet modules (see Section 2 for the definition). By applying a result of Billig and Talboom
[BT], we then classify all such modules and show that they are precisely the modules of
the form V (λ, α, c) (see Section 2 for the definition). We next prove that every nontrivial
irreducible Harish-Chandra module over Gn is either cuspidal or a generalized highest
weight module (Theorem 4.3). Irreducible Harish-Chandra GHW modules over G2 were
classified in [LTW]. However, the classification of irreducible GHW modules over Gn were
not known for n ≥3. We prove that every irreducible generalized highest weight module
over Gn is isomorphic to a highest weight module for Gn (Theorem 5.1). Combining these
results, we obtain a complete classification of irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over
Gn with nontrivial A′
n-action. The paper is organized as follows.
