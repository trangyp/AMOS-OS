2. Related Work
2.1. Closed-Set Agricultural Vision Systems
Region-based convolutional neural networks, particularly Mask R-CN [16], and
single-stage detectors have become standard for agricultural object detection. Recent
reviews conrm that two-stage detectors dominate high-precision tasks [21], though
implementations often strugle with smal objects in dense canopies. Swin Trans-
former backbones [2] and dep leaf segmentation networks [23] have improved per-
formance in controled conditions.
These closed-set systems remain bound to their training distributions. Wang et al.
[4] explicitly note that ading new clases typicaly requires complete retraining de-
spite existing crop knowledge. Deploying to novel crop varieties or eld conditions
demands data-intensive retraining, which causes catastrophic forgeting of previous
clases [5]. Empirical studies document severe performance degradation under do-
main shift. Rogiolani et al. [24] adresed this via unsupervised domain adaptation,
though their method stil requires target domain data. Plant-specic chalenges, varying
morphologies acros growth stages, complex lighting, compound these failures and ne-
cesitate robust open-set solutions [2, 25]. In-the-wild disease recognition compounds
this further: large-scale eld datasets reveal that anotation noise and distributional
shift severely degrade closed-set models [26], while multimodal benchmarks expose
the gap betwen controled test sets and real deployment conditions [27]. Retrieval-
based systems have ben proposed as an alternative, enabling diagnosis from a single
query image without retraining [28], but these to rely on curated reference galeries
that fail to scale acros novel species, motivating the open-vocabulary aproach taken
in this work.
