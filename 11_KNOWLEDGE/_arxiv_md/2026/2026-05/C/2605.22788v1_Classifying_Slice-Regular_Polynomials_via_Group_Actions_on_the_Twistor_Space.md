CLASSIFYING SLICE-REGULAR POLYNOMIALS VIA GROUP ACTIONS ON
THE TWISTOR SPACE
CHUNLIN LIU, GIOVANNI MORENO, AND HAIPAN SHI∗
Abstract. We study the equivalence classes of slice–regular functions f : Ω→H on a symmetric
slice domain Ω, and of their subclass made of polynomial slice–regular functions, with respect to the
natural action of PGL(2, H) and its subgroups, by employing the twistor construction. In particular,
we characterize slice–regular functions whose twistor lift is planar and belongs to a given orbit, and
we find normal classes of slice–regular polynomials with respect to the action of a parabolic subgroup
of GL(2, H).

Let S be the unit sphere of imaginary units in H. A fundamental milestone in the development of

G. Gentili, S. Salamon and C. Stoppato [10]: building on the natural link between twistor geometry

In this paper we exploit the natural group actions on Gr2(C4) to introduce equivalence relations
on the set of slice–regular functions SR(Ω, H) on a symmetric slice domain Ω⊂H.
Our work relates to the general problem of finding invariants of slice–regular function, that has
been tackled, with different techniques, by various authors. For instance, C. Bisi and J. Winkelmann
have recently studied the fundamental invariants of slice–regular functions with respect to the autu-
morphism groups of (complexified) quaternions [5]. A special class of slice–regular functions appears
also in the paper [2] by A. Altavilla and C. de Fabritiis, where the authors characterize the property
of preserving one complex slice.
Our approach relies on the homogeneous structure of the Grassmann manifold Gr2(C4), that is the
target space of the twistor lifts of slice–regular functions. A noteworthy example that exploits such
an approach is the work [16] by X. Jiao and J. Peng, prior to the introduction of the idea of a slice–
regular function: the authors uses the first and second fundamental forms to classify holomorphic
curves in complex Grassmann manifolds, see also [17,18,20].
More accurately, we study the orbits in the Pl¨ucker embedding space P(Λ2C4) of Gr2(C4) with
respect to the natural action of PGL(2, H) (Theorem 3.10) and then we use this result to classify
planar slice–regular functions, i.e., elements of SR(Ω, H), whose twistor lift lies inside a hyperplane
section of P(Λ2C4) of a given type.
Structure of the paper
In Section 2 we cover all necessary definitions an theorems. We fix the notations and the con-
ventions for the complex and quaternionic variables and the related linear algebra, with a particular
emphasis on GL(2, H) and GL(4, C).
We recall the structure of Gr2(C4), its minimal projective
embedding in P(Λ2C4), its local affine charts, and the notion of a hyperplane section. We review
the twistor construction allowing to lift a slice–regular function to a holomorphic curve with values
in Gr2(C4): this leads naturally to the notion of planarity and hyperplane type of a slice–regular
function, see Definition 2.10 and Definition 2.14, respectively.
In Section 3 we carry out a full classifications of PGL(2, H)–orbits in CP5 = P(Λ2C4) (Theo-
rem 3.10) that has an immediate counterpart in the orbit classification of projective dual space
(CP5)∨of hyperplanes of CP5.
The results of Section 3 are applied in Section 4 to the class of slice–regular polynomials: we give
a criterion for planarity (Theorem 4.3) and we show that the twistor lift of a given slice–regular
function can be contained in several hyperplane sections (Theorem 4.6), thus leading, in some cases,
non a non–uniqueness of the planar type (Theorem 4.7).
In the final Section 5 we study more in depth how the action of GL(2, H) on Gr2(C4) is mirrored by
a partial action on SR(Ω, H) (Corollary 5.5) and a global action of a smaller group (Theorem 5.16).
The latter allows to find normal forms of slice–regular polynomials, up to a small residual freedom
given by the simultaneous inner conjugation of all intermediate coefficients (Corollary 5.22).
