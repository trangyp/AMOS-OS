We investigate whether a timelike naked singularity of negative-mass Schwarzschild type can be
causally dressed by a static anisotropic matter distribution in classical general relativity. Working
within a spherically symmetric framework, we solve Einstein’s equations for a general density profile
ρ(r) and show that the horizon structure is governed by the auxiliary function Φ(r) = 2m(r) −r,
whose zeros determine the existence and multiplicity of horizons. We derive sufficient conditions for
the formation of a unique outer event horizon in terms of the total added mass, the localization of
the matter profile, and the monotonic behavior of the effective compactness function 8πr2ρ(r). In
particular, non-negative and sufficiently localized density profiles can cloak the timelike singularity
when the cumulative matter contribution overcomes the negative bare mass, whereas non-monotonic
profiles generically lead to multi-horizon geometries. We illustrate the formalism with discontinuous
and smooth power-law profiles, logarithmic branches, and T-duality-inspired limiting configurations.
These results provide a sufficient-condition framework for horizon formation around timelike naked
singularities and clarify how the radial organization of matter controls causal accessibility in static
general relativity.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The appearance of spacetime singularities remains one
of the deepest conceptual problems of general relativity,
challenging the predictive character of the theory itself.
Under broad and physically reasonable assumptions, the
singularity theorems of Hawking and Penrose imply that
singular behavior is generically unavoidable in gravita-
tional collapse and cosmology [1–3]. In many situations
these singularities are hidden behind event horizons and
therefore remain causally disconnected from asymptotic
observers. However, Einstein’s equations also admit so-
lutions containing naked singularities, namely singular
regions not protected by horizons and therefore causally
accessible from infinity [4–7].
Such configurations are
particularly problematic because they potentially spoil
deterministic evolution and the global predictability of
spacetime.
The possibility that physically realistic gravitational col-
lapse should never produce naked singularities moti-
vated Penrose’s cosmic censorship conjecture [8]. Despite
decades of work, no general proof of the conjecture exists,
and numerous collapse scenarios exhibiting naked singu-
larities have been reported in the literature [5, 9, 10]. At
the same time, several approaches have been developed
to either regularize singular geometries or shield them be-
hind horizons. These include regular black hole construc-
tions [11–13], gravastar and shell-like models [14, 15], and
semiclassical dressing mechanisms [16].

The purpose of the present work is different.
Rather
than proposing new black hole solutions, many of which
are already known in the literature, we investigate a clas-
sification problem: which classes of matter distributions
are capable of generating event horizons around a pre-
existing timelike naked singularity?
In this sense, the
problem addressed here should be understood as a causal
classification problem rather than a solution-generating
program.
Our goal is to identify general properties of
the matter sector that are sufficient to enforce or prevent
single horizon formation.
Timelike naked singularities are especially relevant in
this context because they remain causally connected with
asymptotic observers. The negative-mass Schwarzschild
geometry provides perhaps the simplest prototype of such
a configuration. Although negative mass is itself exotic,
the spacetime is asymptotically flat, horizonless, and pos-
sesses a timelike singularity at the origin. This makes it
an ideal testing ground to study whether an exterior mat-
ter distribution can alter the causal accessibility of the
singular region. It is worth mentioning that this solution
has been proven to be unstable [17].
The usual direction in gravitational-collapse studies is
forward: one specifies matter content and initial condi-
tions and subsequently determines whether collapse leads
to a black hole or to a naked singularity [5, 6]. Here we
adopt the opposite viewpoint. Starting from an already
existing timelike naked singularity, we ask what proper-
ties of a static anisotropic matter distribution are suffi-
cient to produce an outer event horizon. From this per-
spective, the central object of interest is not the explicit
geometry itself, but rather the relation between matter
organization and causal structure.
This inverse viewpoint is conceptually useful for several

In this work we analyze the problem entirely within clas-
sical general relativity.
Assuming a static and spheri-
cally symmetric anisotropic source, we solve Einstein’s
equations exactly and express the geometry in terms of
a general density profile ρ(r). The horizon structure can
then be reduced to the global analysis of an auxiliary
function Φ(r) = 2m(r) −r, whose zeros determine the
existence and multiplicity of horizons. This allows us to
formulate the problem geometrically in terms of the ex-
trema structure of Φ(r) and the behavior of the quantity
r2ρ(r).
Our main result is a dressability theorem providing
sufficient conditions for horizon formation.
Roughly
speaking, we show that if the density profile is non-
negative, sufficiently localized, monotonic in the appro-
priate sense, and large enough to compensate the nega-
tive Schwarzschild contribution, then the resulting geom-
etry develops a unique outer event horizon with no addi-
tional horizons in the asymptotically accessible exterior
region. Conversely, violations of monotonicity generically
generate multiple extrema of the horizon function and
naturally lead to multi-horizon geometries reminiscent of
Reissner–Nordstr¨om-like causal structures.
To illustrate the formalism we study several analytically
tractable matter profiles, including discontinuous exte-
rior layers, smooth continuous distributions, logarith-
mic branches, and Reissner–Nordstr¨om-like asymptotics.
Particular attention is devoted to identifying which fea-
tures of these profiles are genuinely responsible for hori-
zon formation and which merely modify the detailed form
of the metric. In this sense, matter distributions behave
as operators acting on the causal accessibility of space-
time.
We emphasize that the present construction does not at-
tempt to regularize or remove the central singularity. The
singularity remains present throughout the analysis. The
problem addressed here is instead one of causal cloaking:
namely, whether physically admissible anisotropic matter
distributions can render a timelike naked singularity in-
accessible to asymptotic observers through the formation
of an outer event horizon.

In

In Sect. IV

In Sect. V we study representative dress-

Finally, in Sect. VI we discuss the
physical implications of the results, emphasizing the in-
terpretation of matter distributions as causal dressing

VI.
CONCLUSIONS
In this work we addressed the inverse problem of
whether a timelike naked singularity of negative-mass
Schwarzschild type can be causally dressed by a clas-
sical anisotropic matter distribution.
Within a static
and spherically symmetric framework, we showed that
the horizon structure is completely governed by the aux-
iliary function
Φ(r) = 2m(r) −r,
(67)
whose zeros determine the existence and multiplicity of
horizons. On this basis, we formulated an exterior dress-
ability theorem that provides sufficient conditions for the
formation of an outer event horizon around the singular
core.
Our main result is that non-negative, sufficiently local-
ized, and monotonic matter profiles can generate causal
shielding whenever the cumulative matter contribution
compensates the negative bare mass. Importantly, hori-
zon formation is not controlled by the total added mass
alone, but by the local radial organization of the matter
sector. A matter distribution may carry enough asymp-
totic mass and still fail to produce a horizon if its in-
ner compactness is too weak. In this sense, the dressing
mechanism is intrinsically local-global: the profile must
both lift Φ(r) near the core and maintain sufficient cu-
mulative growth in the exterior region.
Monotonicity plays a decisive role in organizing the
causal structure. When r2ρ(r) is monotonic, the result-
ing geometry admits a unique outer event horizon and a
causally simple exterior. By contrast, non-monotonic or
oscillatory profiles generically produce multiple extrema
of Φ(r), leading to multi-horizon configurations with a
richer causal structure. This shows that the radial be-
havior of the matter profile is encoded directly in the
global horizon structure of the spacetime.
The representative examples analyzed in this paper il-
lustrate the versatility of the formalism. Asymptotically
Schwarzschild power-law layers provide the simplest real-
ization of the theorem, the marginal logarithmic branch
n = 3 marks a critical transition between localized
and non-localized dressings, and RN-like lapse functions
emerge naturally from specific anisotropic profiles with-
out invoking Maxwell sources. These cases demonstrate
that familiar causal structures can arise purely from the
geometric organization of classical matter.
It is important to stress that the singularity is never re-
moved in the present construction. Unlike regular black
hole models based on de Sitter cores or nonlinear elec-
tromagnetic sectors, our analysis does not regularize the
central divergence. The result obtained here is therefore
purely causal: suitable matter distributions can hide the
singular region behind an outer horizon, but they do not
eliminate the singularity itself.
The analysis is restricted to static configurations and
should thus be interpreted as an existence result rather

8
than a dynamical formation mechanism. Questions re-
lated to gravitational collapse, perturbative stability,
quasi-normal modes, and nonlinear evolution remain
open.
Even so, the present work provides a concrete
example in which a timelike naked singularity can be
effectively cloaked by physically admissible anisotropic
matter within classical general relativity.
More broadly, the results suggest that matter profiles
may be classified according to their causal efficacy: some
generate horizon formation, some fail to shield the singu-
larity, and others produce more intricate multi-horizon
geometries. In this sense, cosmic censorship in the static
regime can be viewed not as an abstract principle alone,
but as a consequence of how matter distributions reorga-
nize the causal accessibility of spacetime.
