CANONICAL PROJECTIVIZATION OF SMOOTH COMPLETE TORIC
VARIETIES
PARSA BAKHTARY
Abstract. We prove that every smooth complete toric variety can be made projective by a finite
sequence of toric blow-ups along smooth torus-invariant centers. More precisely, for any smooth
complete fan Σ in Rn, after fixing an ordered lattice basis, there is a canonical smooth complete pro-
jective fan bΣ refining Σ, and the induced toric morphism XbΣ →XΣ factors as a finite composition
of blow-ups along smooth torus-invariant subvarieties of codimension at least two. The construction
combines three ingredients: a projective wall-arrangement fan canonically attached to Σ, a sign-
adaptation algorithm that refines Σ to a smooth fan subordinate to the wall-arrangement fan using
only codimension-two blow-ups, and a barycentric derived subdivision that forces projectivity. The
proof is uniform in all dimensions.

1. Introduction
A fundamental question in toric geometry asks: given a smooth complete toric variety XΣ, can
one find a smooth projective toric variety dominating it via a proper birational toric morphism? In
dimension two this is already settled, since every smooth complete toric surface is projective; the
first genuinely nontrivial case is dimension three.
The absolute existence of such a dominating variety is classical.
The toric Chow lemma [4,
Theorem 6.1.18] provides a projective (but generally singular) refinement of any complete fan, and
toric resolution of singularities [4, Theorem 11.1.9] then produces a smooth fan.
Star subdivi-
sions preserve projectivity [4, Proposition 11.1.6(c)], so the desingularized fan is both smooth and
projective.

However, this classical approach passes through singular intermediate fans. The Chow fan of
a smooth complete fan need not be smooth or even simplicial, and the desingularization process
introduces star subdivisions whose centers may not be smooth subvarieties of a smooth ambient
variety. The question of whether the entire projectivization can be achieved through smooth in-
termediate varieties, with every morphism an ordinary blow-up along a smooth center, is more
delicate. It is related to the smooth stellar factorization problem studied by Morelli [9] and to the
weak factorization theorem of W lodarczyk [12]; see also [2]. Oda [10] asked whether every proper
birational toric morphism factors as a sequence of smooth blow-ups and blow-downs; the weak fac-
torization theorem confirms this for birational maps between smooth varieties, but the constructive
statement for a single dominating variety by blow-ups only requires a different approach.
Bonavero [3] showed that certain smooth non-projective toric threefolds become projective after
a single blow-up along an invariant curve. In the study of non-projective Moishezon threefolds,
Peternell [11] developed a philosophy of “bad curves” that witness non-projectivity. Motivated by
these ideas, we prove a toric projectivization theorem in which the “bad” objects are torus-invariant
codimension-two subvarieties whose corresponding two-cones cross the canonical projectivity walls
of the fan.

Intuition. Projectivity is a global condition (existence of a strictly convex support function), while
smoothness is local. Our strategy identifies an intrinsic projective fan AΣ, the wall-arrangement
fan, and systematically refines Σ until it becomes subordinate to AΣ. The refinement uses only
codimension-two blow-ups, each replacing an invariant subvariety whose corresponding two-cone
crosses one of the projective walls. Once the fan refines the projective reference, a single round of
barycentric subdivision forces projectivity.
Relationship to prior work. Our result is not a consequence of the weak factorization theo-
rem [12, 2], which factors a birational map into blow-ups and blow-downs. We construct a specific
smooth projective variety dominating XΣ by blow-ups only, with no blow-downs. Our result is
also stronger than the classical toric Chow lemma combined with desingularization, because every
intermediate variety in our sequence is smooth. It is distinct from the Fujino–Sato framework [7],
which studies projectivization through Q-factorial modifications, flops, and anti-flips, producing a
variety isomorphic in codimension one rather than a dominating one. Rossi [8] extends this view-
point via Cox rings and Mori dream spaces, organizing small Q-factorial modifications through
the GKZ decomposition of the moving cone. Our approach differs in kind: we produce a smooth
variety dominating the original by blow-ups along smooth centers, rather than a variety related by
flips. The theorem of Adiprasito and Pak [1] proves a weighted version of Oda’s strong factoriza-
tion conjecture and a common stellar subdivision theorem for PL-homeomorphic polyhedra. In its

2. Conventions
Let N ∼= Zn be a lattice with dual lattice M = Hom(N, Z). Set NR = N ⊗ZR and MR = M ⊗ZR.
A cone is always a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone in NR. A fan is a collection of cones
closed under taking faces and with pairwise intersections being faces.
For primitive lattice vectors u1, . . . , uk ∈N, write ⟨u1, . . . , uk⟩for the cone R≥0u1 +· · ·+R≥0uk.
A cone is simplicial if its primitive generators are linearly independent, and smooth (or unimodular)
if they extend to a lattice basis of N. A fan is smooth if every cone is smooth.
A fan Γ refines a fan Σ, written Γ ⪯Σ, if every cone of Γ is contained in a cone of Σ. A complete
fan is projective if it admits a strictly upper convex piecewise-linear support function.
For a smooth cone σ = ⟨u1, . . . , uk⟩in a fan Σ, the star subdivision of Σ at the ray R≥0(u1 +
· · · + uk) replaces every cone of Σ containing σ by the cones obtained by removing one generator
of σ at a time and inserting u1 + · · · + uk. Geometrically, this is the toric blow-up of the orbit
closure V (σ); see [4, §11.1].
3. The wall-arrangement fan
Definition 3.1. Let Σ be a smooth complete fan in NR. For each codimension-one cone τ ∈
Σ(n−1), let Hτ = spanR(τ). Choose the primitive normal mτ ∈M such that Hτ = ker(mτ),
with sign determined by requiring the first nonzero coordinate of mτ in the fixed dual basis to be
positive. Remove duplicates, and write MΣ = {m1, . . . , ms}. Define the wall-arrangement fan AΣ
to be the complete fan cut out by the central hyperplane arrangement {ker(m1), . . . , ker(ms)}.
This is the toric analogue of Danilov’s approach to the toric Chow lemma [5]: extending each
codimension-one cone of a complete fan to a full hyperplane produces a projective subdivision.
Lemma 3.2. AΣ is a complete projective fan refining Σ.
Proof. Completeness. A finite central hyperplane arrangement decomposes NR into finitely many
closed polyhedral cones whose union is NR. Each cone is an intersection of closed half-spaces defined
by rational hyperplanes, hence is a rational polyhedral cone.
Refinement. Each maximal cone σ = ⟨r1, . . . , rn⟩of Σ is the intersection of the n closed half-
spaces defined by its facet planes. Since these facet planes are among the hyperplanes defining AΣ,
any chamber meeting the interior of σ is contained in σ.
Projectivity. The zonotope ZΣ = Ps
i=1[−mi, mi] ⊂MR has AΣ as its normal fan, by the standard
zonotope-hyperplane-arrangement duality [13, Theorem 7.16]. It is full-dimensional because the mi

4. The sign-adaptation algorithm
Definition 4.1. Let Φ be a smooth fan and m ∈M primitive. A two-cone τ = ⟨u, v⟩∈Φ(2) is
m-bad if m(u) · m(v) < 0. The fan Φ is m-adapted if it has no m-bad two-cones. The m-weight of
a bad two-cone is wm(τ) = |m(u)| + |m(v)|.
Remark 4.2. Since the fan is smooth, every cone is simplicial. For a simplicial cone σ, the absence
of m-bad two-faces is equivalent to all ray generators of σ lying in one of the closed half-spaces
{m ≥0} or {m ≤0}: if no two-face has generators of opposite sign, then the signs (and zeros) of
m on the generators are all nonnegative or all nonpositive.
Lemma 4.3 (Sign-adaptation). Let Φ be a smooth complete fan and m ∈M primitive. There
is a canonical finite sequence of star subdivisions along m-bad two-cones that produces a smooth
m-adapted fan. Each star subdivision inserts u + v for a bad two-cone ⟨u, v⟩and is the blow-up of
a smooth codimension-two invariant center.
Proof. Algorithm. Choose an m-bad two-cone of maximal weight. Ties are broken by ordering
each two-cone ⟨u, v⟩as the pair (min(u, v), max(u, v)) under the lexicographic order on N induced
by the fixed basis. Star-subdivide the chosen cone ⟨u, v⟩by inserting the ray through s = u + v.
Repeat until no bad two-cone remains. Since ⟨u, v⟩is smooth, u and v are part of a lattice basis;
hence u + v is primitive.
Smoothness. If σ = ⟨u, v, w1, . . . , wk⟩is a cone containing ⟨u, v⟩, then u, v, w1, . . . , wk are part of
a lattice basis. The cones ⟨u, s, w1, . . . , wk⟩and ⟨s, v, w1, . . . , wk⟩have generator matrices obtained
by elementary column operations from this basis, hence are smooth. Thus every intermediate fan
is smooth and complete, and the toric morphism is the blow-up of the smooth invariant subvariety
V (⟨u, v⟩) of codimension two.
Termination. Write m(u) = a > 0 and m(v) = −b with b > 0, after interchanging u and v if
necessary, and put W = a + b. Then m(s) = a −b. The old bad cone ⟨u, v⟩disappears. The two

5. Sequential adaptation and monotonicity
Lemma 5.1 (Monotonicity). If a smooth fan Φ is m-adapted, then any star subdivision of a two-
cone of Φ at the sum of its generators preserves m-adaptation.
Proof. Since Φ is m-adapted, every cone has all its ray generators in one of the closed half-spaces
{m ≥0} or {m ≤0}. Let σ ⊃⟨u, v⟩be a cone of Φ. All rays of σ lie in one such closed half-space.
The new ray s = u+v satisfies m(s) = m(u)+m(v), which lies in the same closed half-space (the sum
of two nonnegative numbers is nonnegative; the sum of two nonpositive numbers is nonpositive).
Therefore every new cone produced by the star subdivision, including cones of the form ⟨s, w⟩for
other rays w of σ, has all its generators in one closed half-space. Hence no new m-bad two-cone is
created.
□

Define Γ(Σ) := Φs.

6. The barycentric wrapper
The fan Γ(Σ) is smooth and complete and refines the projective fan AΣ, but need not itself
be projective. The reference fan AΣ may be neither simplicial nor smooth, and subordination to
a projective fan does not by itself force a subdivision to be projective. This is the role of the
barycentric wrapper.
Definition 6.1. Let Φ be a smooth fan. For each nonzero cone γ = ⟨r1, . . . , rk⟩∈Φ, define its
barycenter ray bγ = r1 + · · · + rk. The barycentric subdivision Bar(Φ) has maximal cones indexed
by maximal flags γ1 ⊂· · · ⊂γn of nonzero cones, with generators bγ1, . . . , bγn.
Lemma 6.2. If Φ is a smooth n-dimensional complete fan, then Bar(Φ) is smooth and complete.
Proof. Completeness: barycentric subdivision only subdivides existing cones. Smoothness: for a
maximal cone σ = ⟨r1, . . . , rn⟩with lattice basis r1, . . . , rn, a maximal cone of Bar(σ) given by a
permutation (i1, . . . , in) has generators ri1, ri1 +ri2, . . . , ri1 +· · ·+rin, forming an upper triangular
matrix with diagonal entries 1.
□
Lemma 6.3 (Blow-up factorization). Bar(Φ) can be obtained from Φ by a canonical sequence of
star subdivisions at barycenter rays of cones of dimension n, n−1, . . . , 2. Each star subdivision is
the blow-up of a smooth torus-invariant center of codimension 2 to n. Within each fixed dimension,
cones are processed in lexicographic order of their barycenter coordinates.
Proof. Only cones of dimension ≥2 contribute new rays, since the barycenter of a one-dimensional
cone ⟨r⟩is r itself. For a cone γ = ⟨r1, . . . , rk⟩of Φ, the vector bγ = r1 + · · · + rk lies in relint(γ).
Since distinct cones of a fan have disjoint relative interiors, the rays R≥0bγ are distinct.
Process the original cones of Φ in decreasing dimension. We first record that every unprocessed
original cone remains a cone of the current fan. Indeed, stellar subdivision at a cone γ′ only removes
cones containing γ′. If an unprocessed cone γ of smaller dimension is a face of a removed cone,
then γ ̸⊇γ′, so some generator of γ′ is not in γ; the replacement cone obtained by omitting that
generator still contains γ as a face. Hence γ persists until it is processed.
We argue by induction over the sequence. The current fan is smooth. When the original cone
γ = ⟨r1, . . . , rk⟩is processed, it is a smooth cone of the current fan, and bγ is the sum of its primitive
ray generators. The star subdivision at this ray is therefore the toric blow-up of the smooth orbit
closure V (γ), of codimension dim γ, and it preserves smoothness: in each current maximal cone
containing γ, the generators of γ are a subset of a lattice basis, and replacing one of them by their
sum is an elementary unimodular column operation.
The usual derived-subdivision induction shows that, after all original cones of dimensions n, n −
1, . . . , 2 have been processed, the maximal cones are exactly those associated to maximal flags
γ1 ⊂· · · ⊂γn of nonzero cones of Φ, with generators bγ1, . . . , bγn. This is Bar(Φ).
□

9. Specialization to dimension three
Corollary 9.1. Every smooth complete toric threefold admits a canonical projectivization by smooth
toric blow-ups. The wall-adaptation centers are invariant curves; the barycentric centers are in-
variant curves and torus fixed points.
Proof. This is immediate from Theorem 1.1 and the dimension counts. In dimension three, two-
dimensional cones correspond to invariant curves, while three-dimensional cones correspond to torus
fixed points.
□
Remark 9.2 (The cube-ray case). When all rays lie in {−1, 0, 1}3 \ {0}, an alternative construction
uses the coordinate octant fan Σoct as an external projective reference and analyzes the common
refinement Σ∧Σoct. A finite classification of local cell types, combined with a midpoint repair rule,
yields tighter bounds (| Bar(E(Σ))(3)| ≤144 |Σ(3)|, coordinate bound |xi| ≤6). The present proof

11. Remarks
Remark 11.1 (Canonicity). The construction is canonical relative to a fixed ordered lattice basis,
which orients wall normals, orders them for sequential processing, and breaks ties among bad two-
cones. The wall-arrangement fan AΣ is intrinsic to Σ; only the ordering of the adaptation steps
requires the basis.
Remark 11.2 (Bad subvarieties). The wall-adaptation centers are orbit closures of m-bad two-
cones: smooth invariant (n−2)-folds. For n = 3, they are invariant curves, matching the spirit of
Peternell’s “bad curve” picture [11] and Bonavero’s single-blow-up projectivization [3].
Remark 11.3 (The Chow-then-desingularize approach). The classical approach projectivizes first
(possibly introducing singularities) and then desingularizes. Our construction reverses the order,
maintaining smoothness throughout, because the smooth input allows two-cone star subdivisions
that preserve unimodularity.
Remark 11.4 (The smoothness hypothesis). If Σ is not smooth, u + v need not be primitive when
⟨u, v⟩is not smooth, so the sign-adaptation algorithm does not directly apply.
