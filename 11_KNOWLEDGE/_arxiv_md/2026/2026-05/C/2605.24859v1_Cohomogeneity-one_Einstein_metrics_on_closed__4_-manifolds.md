COHOMOGENEITY-ONE EINSTEIN METRICS ON
CLOSED 4-MANIFOLDS
KYLE BRODER
Abstract. We classify the Einstein metrics on closed 4-manifolds whose isom-
etry group has dimension at least 3. Consequently, we show that the cohomogeneity-
one Einstein metrics on a closed 4-manifold are locally symmetric or homothetic
to the Page metric on CP2♯CP
2.
1. Introduction
A Riemannian metric g on a closed manifold M n is Einstein if
Ric(g)
=
λg,
λ ∈R.
One of the most effective mechanisms for producing Einstein metrics with λ > 0
comes from considering cohomogeneity-one actions—that is, a compact Lie group
G acts isometrically on (M, g) with one-dimensional orbit space (see, e.g., [Zil09]).
B¨ohm [B¨oh98] used cohomogeneity-one techniques to produce the first inhomo-
geneous Einstein metrics on spheres. Moreover, to date, the only non-round Ein-
stein metrics on even-dimensional spheres have been found using cohomogeneity-
one methods [FH17, Chi24, NW25, BH26].
On closed 4-manifolds, methods from complex and symplectic geometry
have led to several classification results for Einstein metrics. LeBrun [LeB12]
proved that a Hermitian Einstein metric on a closed 4-manifold is either K¨ahler–
Einstein or, up to rescaling and isometry, one of two exceptional conformally
K¨ahler examples: the Page metric [Pag78] on CP2♯CP
2 and the Chen–LeBrun–
Weber metric [CLW08] on CP2♯2CP
2. Earlier, LeBrun [LeB97] showed that a
compact complex surface admitting a non-K¨ahler Einstein Hermitian metric must
be the blow-up of CP2 at one, two, or three points, and that the isometry group
of the metric contains a two-torus. The closed 4-manifolds admitting K¨ahler–
Einstein metrics with λ > 0 were classified by Tian [Tia87, Tia90].

In contrast, general classification results for cohomogeneity-one Einstein
metrics are scarce. The problem of classifying cohomogeneity-one Einstein met-
rics is raised explicitly by Anderson [And10, page 6] (cf. [Dam04, Don25, Ses04,
Wan26]). One of the strongest previous rigidity results is due to B´erard-Bergery
and Derdzi´nski [BB82, Der83] (see also [Bes87, Theorem 9.127] and [PZ95]).
It implies that if (M 4, g) is closed, simply connected, and Einstein, and either
dim Isom(M, g) ≥4 or M admits an isometric SO(3)-action with principal orbits
S2 or RP2, then (M, g) is either locally symmetric or homothetic to the Page
metric. Recent work has continued to develop numerical and computer-assisted
approaches to this problem [Don25, Wan26].
The main result of this paper is the following classification theorem.
Theorem 1.1. Let (M 4, g) be a connected closed Einstein 4-manifold with
dim Isom(M, g) ≥3. Then either g is flat or the universal cover is homothetic to
one of the following:
(S4, grd),
(CP2, gFS),
(S2 × S2, grd ⊕grd),
(CP2♯CP
2, gPage).
In particular, S4 is the first even-dimensional sphere of dimension n ≥4 for which
the round metric is the only cohomogeneity-one Einstein metric, up to scaling
(cf. [B¨oh98, FH17, Chi24, NW25, BH26]).
In the more general category of orbifold metrics, Hitchin [Hit96] discovered,
for each integer k > 0, an SO(3)-invariant cohomogeneity-one self-dual Einstein
orbifold metric gH,k on an orbifold Ok homeomorphic to S4 (cf. [GWZ08, Zil09]).
In the final section of the paper, we prove the following result.
Theorem 1.2. For an integer k ≥1, let g be an SO(3)-invariant Einstein orbifold
metric on the Hitchin orbifold Ok. Then g is homothetic to gH,k.
In Section 2, we show that the proof of Theorem 1.1 reduces to the classifica-
tion of cohomogeneity-one Einstein metrics with G = SU(2) or G = SO(3). On
the union of the principal orbits, a G-invariant Einstein metric is then described
by three functions subject to boundary conditions. The key insight is to con-
sider the induced system of evolution equations for the connection coefficients
on the bundles of self-dual and anti-self-dual 2-forms, and for the eigenvalues of
the curvature operator on Λ2
±. These evolution equations allow us to treat the
SO(3)-actions (see Sections 3.2, 4.2, and 6). The SU(2)-actions on S4 and CP2
are handled by a maximum principle argument (see Sections 3.1 and 4.1). The
remaining SU(2)-action on CP2♯CP
2 is also handled by a maximum principle
argument after changing the group diagram presentation (see Section 5).

2. Preliminaries
We first reduce Theorem 1.1 to the classification of cohomogeneity-one Einstein
manifolds. The assumption on the isometry group implies that λ ≥0, and if
λ = 0, the metric is flat (see, e.g., [Pet16, Section 8.2]). So assume λ > 0. Since
π1(M) is finite, after passing to the universal cover, we may assume that M is
simply connected. Write G := Isom0(M, g) for the identity component of the
isometry group. From [BB82, Der83], we may assume dim G = 3. Let G/H be a
principal orbit. Since H is principal, the slice representation is trivial. Thus, the
induced G-action on G/H is effective. In particular, if d := dim G/H ≤2, then G
acts effectively and transitively on a compact d-dimensional manifold. So d = 2
and G/H is a compact homogeneous surface with a three-dimensional connected
isometry group. Hence, G = SO(3) and G/H is S2 or RP2, which is again covered
by [BB82, Der83]. It remains to consider the effective cohomogeneity-one actions
with dim G = 3. By the Parker–Hoelscher classification [Par86, Hoe10] (see also
[Don25]), Theorem 1.1 is reduced to understanding the Einstein metrics invariant
under the SU(2) actions on S4, CP2, CP2♯CP
2, and the standard SO(3)-actions
on S4, CP2, and S2 × S2. This will occupy Sections 2–6.

2.1. Structure of cohomogeneity-one manifolds. Following [GZ11, BK19],
let (M, g) be a closed simply connected 4-manifold. A group G acting isomet-
rically on (M, g) is said to have cohomogeneity-one if the orbit space M/G is
diffeomorphic to [0, T] (see, e.g., [GZ11, Zil09, BK19, Don25]). If π : M →[0, T]
denotes the quotient map, the principal orbits are the codimension one orbits
π−1(t) for 0 < t < T, and the singular orbits are π−1(0) and π−1(T).

interval [0, T]. Let c : [0, T] →M be a geodesic orthogonal to the orbits. For
t0, t ∈(0, T), the principal isotropy H := Gc(t0) ≃Gc(t). The two singular orbits
have isotropy K−:= Gc(0) and K+ := Gc(T), and M is equivariantly diffeomorphic
