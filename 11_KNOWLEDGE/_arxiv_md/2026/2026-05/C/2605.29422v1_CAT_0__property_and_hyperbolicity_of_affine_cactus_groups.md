TAKATOSHI HAMA
Abstract. We show that the affine cactus group is a CAT(0) group for all
degrees.
Furthermore, we show that the affine cactus group AJ3 of degree
three is a hyperbolic group.
1. Introduction
A presentation of a group, later referred to as the cactus group, first appeared
in the study of a twisted real form of the Deligne-Mumford space M 0,n+1 of stable
genus 0 curves with n + 1 marked points [5]. Furthermore, research on this group
from the perspective of geometric group theory has been conducted by Genevois
in [6]. Analogously, the affine cactus group was introduced by Ilin, Kamnitzer, Li,
Przytycki, and Rybnikov for the study of the moduli space of the cactus flower
curves, which serves as an analogue to the aforementioned twisted real form of the
Deligne-Mumford space [9]. In addition, Chemin has investigated the affine cactus
group from the view point of combinatorial group theory in [3]. In this paper, we
focus on the affine cactus group from the perspective of geometric group theory.
One of the topics in geometric group theory is the study of CAT(0) groups. Indeed,
Genevois established a criterion equivalent to the statement that a cactus group
of any degree is a CAT(0) group.
On the other hand, the CAT(0) property of
affine cactus groups has remained unexplored until now. In this paper, we show
the following.
Theorem 1.1. For an integer n ≥3, the affine cactus group AJn is a CAT(0)
group.
Another major topic in geometric group theory is the hyperbolicity of groups.
Regarding the hyperbolicity of the cactus groups, it is known that they are hyper-
bolic for degrees n ≤4, while they are not hyperbolic for n ≥5. The second result
of this paper clarifies that the affine cactus group of 3 is hyperbolic.
Theorem 1.2. The affine cactus group AJ3 of degree three is a hyperbolic group.
Remark 1.3. In [6], it has been shown that the cactus group whose degree is 6 or
more is not a hyperbolic group. The pure cactus group of degree 5 is the fundamental
group of the Deligne-Mumford space M 0,6 [1, Example 3.22]. Here, the pure cactus
group is the kernel of the natural surjection from the cactus group to the symmetric
group of the same degree. This implies that J5 is not a hyperbolic group.
