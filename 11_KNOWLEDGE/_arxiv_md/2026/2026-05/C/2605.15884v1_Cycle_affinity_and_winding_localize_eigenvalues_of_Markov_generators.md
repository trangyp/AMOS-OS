The complex eigenvalues of Markov generators govern oscillatory properties of relaxation, autocorrelation,
and linear response. Here we show that these eigenvalues are localized by nonequilibrium cycles of the generator,
thus revealing a fundamental tradeoff between thermodynamic driving, oscillation, and decay of eigenmodes.
Specifically, we prove that each complex eigenvalue is confined to a region determined by the cycle affinity and
the eigenvector “winding number” of some nonequilibrium cycle. In unicyclic systems, we also demonstrate that
the winding number coincides with the ordered eigenvalue index, yielding new thermodynamic bounds on the
slowest and fastest relaxation modes. In multicyclic systems, our approach unifies and extends several previous
inequalities and proves the Uhl–Seifert ellipse conjecture.
Markov chains are used to study various nonequilibrium
systems, ranging from chemical reaction networks [1, 2] and
biomolecular machines [3–5] to population dynamics [6] and
social processes [7]. In a continuous-time Markov chain, the
probability distribution p(t) evolves according to the master
equation dtp(t) = Rp(t), where R is the generator (also
called rate matrix). The eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the
generator govern many important properties of the dynamics.
In particular, each nonzero eigenvalue λ of R corresponds to
an eigenmode with decay rate −Re λ and oscillation frequency
| Im λ|. Understanding what governs oscillatory and relaxation
properties of Markovian dynamics has important implications.
For instance, it is relevant for quantifying functional tradeoffs
in biological and artificial systems, such as biochemical clocks,
oscillators, and sensors [8–14]. It is also relevant for capturing
essential dynamical features, such as metastable behavior [15]
and phase transitions [15–18].
A Markov generator can also be characterized by whether
it satisfies or violates the principle of detailed balance (DB).
DB is satisfied if and only if the cycle affinity (defined below)
vanishes for every cycle [2, 19] or (equivalently) if the generator
has an equilibrium steady state in which all net fluxes vanish.
It is known that DB generators have only real-valued eigenval-
ues [20, 21], so they cannot sustain stochastic oscillations.
Conversely, DB is violated if there is at least one cycle
with nonvanishing affinity or (equivalently) if the generator
has a nonequilibrium steady state with nonvanishing fluxes.
At the microscopic level, the breaking of DB results from
thermodynamic driving, such as coupling to nonequilibrium
chemical potentials, temperature differences, stirring, etc. [2,
19]. The cycle affinity measures the total thermodynamic force
accumulated around a cycle, and it provides a quantitative
measure of the driving strength. Unlike the entropy production
rate, another common quantifier of nonequilibrium, the cycle
affinity is determined by macroscopic parameters and does not
depend on details of the steady state [22].
In this Letter, we demonstrate a fundamental relationship
between thermodynamic driving (cycle affinity) and the location
of eigenvalues in the complex plane. Specifically, we show
that each eigenvalue is confined to a region determined by the
cycle affinity and the eigenvector winding number on some
nonequilibrium cycle of the generator, where the winding
number quantifies the eigenvector’s spatial frequency. We also
prove that in unicyclic systems, the winding number coincides
with the ordered eigenvalue index, yielding new thermodynamic
constraints on the slowest and fastest eigenvalues. Thus, our
result explains how spectral properties of the generator depend
on thermodynamic driving and topology of the generator, i.e.,
the cycle structure.
Our result contributes to a growing body of literature on the
relationship between spectral and thermodynamic properties
of Markovian systems [8–14, 22–26], and it provides a unified
route to derive various existing bounds.
In particular, we
derive a sharpened version of our previous eigenvalue sector
bound [22] (itself inspired by the Barato-Seifert conjecture [8]),
resolve the Uhl–Seifert ellipse conjecture [12], and uncover
several novel eigenvalue inequalities. Many of our results
remain nontrivial even in the regime of absolute irreversibility,
where they provide refinements of classical inequalities such
as the Dmitriev–Dynkin bound [27] and the Kellogg–Stephens
bound [28].
In the following, we state our results for continuous-time
generators. They can be adapted to discrete-time transition ma-
trices via the standard embedding T = I + R/(maxi(−Rii)),
which rescales and shifts the eigenvalues along the real axis.

Discussion.— In this Letter, we showed that the complex
eigenvalues of a Markov generator are localized by its nonequi-
librium cycles. Each complex eigenvalue is confined to a
region determined by the cycle affinity and the eigenvector
winding number on some particular nonequilibrium cycle. The
resulting bounds reveal that strong oscillations are incompatible
with either slow or fast decay. Moreover, in unicyclic systems,
the winding number coincides with the ordered eigenvalue
index, leading to strong constraints on the fastest and slowest
eigenmodes.
Overall, our results elucidate how topological and thermody-
namic properties jointly constrain the eigenvalues of Markov
generators. Natural directions for future work include mul-
ticyclic generalization of the winding-index correspondence
(Theorem 2) and extension of the one-dimensional diffusion
bound (6) to higher-dimensional continuous systems.
