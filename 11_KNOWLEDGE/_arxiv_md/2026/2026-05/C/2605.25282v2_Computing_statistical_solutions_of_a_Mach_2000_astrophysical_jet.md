Abstract
The simulation of extreme Mach astrophysical flows is traditionally viewed through the lens of deterministic positivity-
preserving schemes. However, due to Kelvin–Helmholtz instabilities and shock anomalies, the multi-dimensional Eu-
ler equations admit a variety of non-unique entropy solutions in turbulent regimes. Here, we computationally explore
the limits of weak-strong uniqueness of a Mach 2000 jet by defining the statistical solution as the pushforward of a
probability measure through a vectorial lattice Boltzmann method operator. Utilizing optimized CUDA kernels, we
compute an ensemble of 1000 Monte Carlo samples across a sequence of highly refined spatial grids of up to 3.2
million cells and subsequently post-process the empirical measures via memory-mapped CPU streaming. We contrast
the strong sample-wise L1 error divergence with the convergence of the probability measure in the 1-point Wasserstein
distance via empirical Cauchy rates. Our results demonstrate that while individual flow realizations physically diverge
due to chaotic shear-layer instabilities, the statistical solution converges to an admissible limit measure at a rate of 0.5.
Consequently, we provide numerical evidence that the statistical solution to the considered problem is non-Dirac and
remains stable in the extreme compressible regime.
Keywords: statistical solutions, Wasserstein distance, vectorial LBM, high-Mach flows, GPU computing
1. Introduction
Astrophysical jets at Mach numbers exceeding 103 represent a significant challenge in computational fluid dy-
namics. The Mach 2000 parameter regime serves two critical purposes. First, it is directly physically representative
of protostellar jets (Herbig–Haro objects) propagating into ultra-cold (T ∼10 K) molecular clouds, where ambient
sound speeds drop below 0.3 km/s, yielding classical Mach numbers in excess of 103 [13]. Second, it serves as a
canonical mathematical benchmark for the high-energy limits of the classical Euler equations. By pushing the de-
terministic breakdown to the extreme compressible limit, we provide a mathematical proxy for the shock-dominated,
hyper-turbulent topologies found in more complex relativistic flows, such as active galactic nuclei and gamma-ray
bursts [14, 12].
While traditional numerical research utilizes the Mach 2000 jet primarily as a short-duration stress test to validate
positivity-preserving limiters [15, 16], the underlying mathematical question of uniqueness in the long-time, fully
turbulent regime remains challenging. State-of-the-art high-order frameworks, such as subcell-limited discontinuous
Galerkin methods, typically restrict this benchmark to early transient phases (e.g., t ≤0.0015) [18, 17]. Recent ad-
vancements have successfully leveraged kinetic theory and Boltzmann-derived bounds to deterministically stabilize
astrophysical jets up to Mach 800 [19]. In this chaotic regime, foundational convex integration results [1] demonstrate
that the multi-dimensional Euler equations may admit infinitely many entropy solutions for the exact same initial
data. To recover a well-posed theoretical framework, DiPerna introduced measure-valued solutions [2], which were
subsequently refined into statistical solutions [3]. These shift the focus from individual deterministic trajectories to a
time-evolving probability measure. Although conceptually promising, the computation of such statistical solutions for

In this letter, we transition the Mach 2000 astrophysical jet problem from a deterministic stability test into an
extensive study of statistical hyper-compressible turbulence. To investigate the statistical convergence, we propose
a probabilistic LBM based on combining a Monte Carlo sampling approach with the vectorial LBM (VLBM) for
compressible Euler equations proposed by Wissocq et al. [6]. We extend the temporal horizon to t = 0.003 and
compute an ensemble of M = 1000 samples on highly resolved spatial grids up to 4000 × 800. We demonstrate the
pointwise divergence of single sample trajectories and numerically explore the limits of weak-strong uniqueness of
a Mach 2000 astrophysical jet by evaluating the convergence of the numerical solution measure in the Wasserstein
metric using an optimized, highly parallel Monte Carlo pipeline on contemporary hardware. Our results suggest that
even under severe numerical shock-compression, a dissipative statistical solution is approximated, effectively bridging
foundational mathematics with the phenomenology of extreme astrophysical environments.

3. Weak-strong uniqueness and convergence metrics

zero variance. In highly turbulent, high-Mach regimes, the existence of such strong solutions breaks down globally.

4. Numerical results
4.1. Implementation
The methodology from [6] (see Section 2.1) has been ported to GPU for the present work. The Monte Carlo
ensemble was implemented using Python and CuPy for CUDA-accelerated array processing. To isolate the spatial
convergence of the probability measure and separate grid discretization error from statistical sampling noise, the
Monte Carlo ensemble is computed across a sequence of progressively refined grids. The spatial resolution scales
incrementally, doubling from a coarse baseline of 500 × 100 cells to a highly resolved grid of 4000 × 800 discrete
lattice cells. The time-stepping is implemented as described in [6]. The entire pipeline was executed in an array of
batch jobs with 100 samples per GPU node on HoreKa Ruby (4x NVIDIA H200 GPUs each). A single batch took
30 hours on average to compute all grids for 100 sample trajectories on one GPU node, including I/O for roughly 20
minutes to process the data into error metrics and visualizations on the CPU.

5. Conclusions
We have computed the statistical solution of an extreme Mach 2000 astrophysical jet up to highly resolved do-
mains of 4000×800. By defining the solution as the pushforward of a probability measure via the VLBM operator, we
mitigated the traditional instability of deterministic schemes in near-vacuum regimes. The observed Cauchy conver-
gence in the 1-point Wasserstein metric W1, contrasted against the continuous breakdown of strong L1 convergence,
provides empirical evidence for the statistical stability of chaotic high-Mach flows. This offers a highly parallelizable,
mathematically robust path for uncertainty quantification in extreme compressible astrophysics, confirming that while
pointwise deterministic uniqueness is lost, robust convergence to a statistical limit is preserved.
Because the extreme compressibility of the flow inherently precludes the existence of a strong statistical solution,
the system falls outside the purview of statistical weak-strong uniqueness. Consequently, we do not claim global
uniqueness for the underlying continuous problem. However, our specific numerical scheme stably selects and con-
verges to a non-degenerate dissipative statistical solution, representing a physically admissible measure-valued state
dictated by the numerical dissipation.

sample simulations, the statistical footprint of the turbulence, and thus the macroscopic structure of the observable
emission, is recovered under the Wasserstein metric.
