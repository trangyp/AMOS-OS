Carrier-doping effect and anomalous transport properties in Ni-doped CeCoIn5
investigated by Hall resistivity measurements

We investigated the effects of Ni doping on carrier density and anomalous electrical transport
properties in CeCo1−xNixIn5 (x ≤0.3) by performing Hall resistivity measurements. The carrier
density, estimated from the Hall coefficient RH at a temperature of 0.5 K in high magnetic fields,
increases linearly with x, indicating that the doped Ni ions act as electron dopants. In CeCoIn5,
the magnitude of −RH is strongly enhanced at magnetic fields near the superconducting upper
critical field Hc2 and in the low-field region above the superconducting transition temperature Tc.
However, these anomalies are found to be significantly suppressed by Ni doping. Possible origins of
this suppression in −RH are discussed.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Hall resistivity measurements are a powerful technique
for investigating quantum critical phenomena in various
itinerant electron systems [1, 2]. The quantum critical
phenomena arising from antiferromagnetic (AFM) spin
fluctuations are typically induced by continuously sup-
pressing the AFM order through the application of mag-
netic fields, pressure, or chemical substitutions.
The
resulting critical condition, where the AFM transition
temperature approaches zero (TN →0), is referred to
as the AFM quantum critical point (QCP). In itinerant
AFM compounds exhibiting quantum criticality, such as
YbRh2Si2 and Cr, the Hall resistivity measurements have
revealed changes in the carrier density across the QCP
[3–6]. Thus, it is expected that the carrier density near
the AFM QCP provides valuable insights into the origin
of QCP formation, as many theoretical models have been
proposed to explain it [7–10].
The heavy-fermion superconductor CeCoIn5 exhibits
quantum critical phenomena arising from itinerant quasi-
particles in various physical quantities, including Hall re-
sistivity [11–15]. CeCoIn5 crystallizes in the HoCoGa5-
type tetragonal structure (see Fig. 1 inset) and exhibits
superconductivity with a dx2−y2-type gap symmetry be-
low the superconducting (SC) transition temperature
Tc = 2.3 K [16–19].
Anomalous SC properties have
been reported thus far, and most of them likely origi-
nate from AFM spin correlations and quantum critical-
ity [20–22]. For instance, a strong Pauli paramagnetic

Various ion-substituted systems have been investigated

When ions with atomic numbers one less
than those of the constituent atoms in CeCoIn5 are sub-
stituted (e.g., Cd, Hg, and Zn at the In site), increasing
the substitution level suppresses the SC order and simul-
taneously induces AFM orders [37–49]. In contrast, when
ions with atomic numbers one higher than those of the

the non-Fermi liquid (NFL) state without inducing mag-
netic order [50–56]. These systematic characteristics sug-
gest that carrier doping is key for tuning the SC, AFM,
and NFL states realized in CeCoIn5 and its doped alloys
[57–61]. Therefore, quantitative information on changes
in carrier density is essential for elucidating properties of
these states.
In the mixed compound CeCo1−xNixIn5, the Ni doping
continuously suppresses the SC phase, with both Tc and
Hc2 reaching zero at the critical concentration xc = 0.25
[53]. These features can be confirmed in the H-T-x phase
diagram shown in Fig. 1 [53]. The AFM spin correlations
govern the paramagnetic state after the SC order is com-
pletely suppressed at xc. Specific heat, electrical resistiv-
ity, and magnetic susceptibility all exhibit NFL behavior
at zero magnetic fields for xc [53, 54]. The temperature
dependence of the nuclear spin-lattice relaxation rate in-

these anomalies remains a subject of intense debate due
to the sensitivity of RH to both magnetic correlations and
Fermi surface topology. Given that CeCoIn5 is consid-

structure and strong correlations [62–64], it is of interest
to determine how Ni doping modifies these anomalous
Hall features.
Thus,
we have measured the Hall resistivity of
CeCo1−xNixIn5, including at the critical concentration
of xc = 0.25, to clarify the effects of Ni doping on the
carrier density and the evolution of anomalous RH be-
havior.
II.
EXPERIMENT DETAILS
Single crystals of CeCo1−xNixIn5 for x ≤0.3 were
grown with an indium flux method, the details of which
have been described in the previous report [53].
The
chemical composition was analyzed using an inductively
coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) system
(7500cx, Agilent Technologies). Figure 1(a) and Table
I show the relationship between the actual (measured)
and nominal (pre-production stage) Ni concentrations.

ratio of Ni to the total constituent atoms in the chemi-

molar ratios of Ce, (Co + Ni), and In are nearly 1, 1,
and 5, respectively, and that the actual Ni concentrations
closely match the nominal concentrations, as reported
previously [53]. For simplicity, we use the nominal x val-
ues throughout this article unless otherwise indicated to
preserve the consistency with previous reports [53–56].
For current Hall resistivity measurements, we selected
thin plate-shaped samples with a thickness t along the
c-axis of ∼0.1 mm and a large c-plane surface (typical
dimensions of 3 × 2 mm2) to ensure the Hall component

RESULTS

agnetic state at T = 0.5 K, indicating that the carrier
density can be reliably estimated [Fig. 3(a)]. However,
for x ≤0.125, the non-linear field dependence is observed
near Hc2 in RH(H), which tends to weaken as the mag-
netic field increases up to 9 T at 0.5 K [Fig. 3(a)]. This
enhancement in −RH cannot be explained by the simple
single band model; it is likely due to the specific band
structure and/or strong spin correlations. In fact, band
structure calculations and de Haas–van Alphen measure-
ments suggest that CeCoIn5 is a compensated multiband
metal with several bands crossing the Fermi level [62–
64]. We have used the RH values at 9 T, the maximum
magnetic field applied in the current measurements, to
estimate the carrier density. However, it is important to
consider whether this magnetic field strength is sufficient.
We confirmed that pure CeCoIn5 exhibits the strongest
field dependence of RH above Hc2 among all Ni compo-
sitions [Fig. 3(a)]. In this compound, RH(H) has been
measured up to 73 T at 0.5 K, and it remains constant
above 15 T [60].
This suggests that the nontrivial ef-
fects causing the non-linear RH(H) behavior are likely
negligible in this high magnetic field region. As shown
in Fig. 3(b), the carrier density estimated from the RH

at 9 T and 0.5 K, and the other low-temperature data
at 55 mK and 8 T [14], within experimental accuracy.
Furthermore, the differences in the carrier density val-
ues among the Hall resistivity experiments in CeCoIn5
are found to be significantly smaller than the changes in
the carrier density induced by the Ni doping [Fig. 3(b)].

2.3 K) at low magnetic fields, as well as at low temper-

where the enhancement of −RH is most pronounced in
the H-T-x space, we plot the RH(H) curves for x = 0,
0.04, and 0.125 in Figs. 4(a)–4(c), respectively. For pure
CeCoIn5, the evolution of −RH(H) as the magnetic field
decreases toward zero for T > Tc (2.3 K) is more pro-
nounced than that observed for H > Hc2 at T = 0.5 K
[Fig. 4(a)]. This enhanced behavior in −RH(H) is sig-
nificantly suppressed at x = 0.04 [Fig. 4(b)] and subse-
quently changes into an approximately linear field depen-
dence in RH(H) for x ≥0.125 [Fig. 4(c)]. To emphasize
the trends above induced by the Ni substitution, we sum-
marized the x dependence of RH at T = 2.6 K in Fig.
4(d). It is evident that −RH significantly decreases as
the magnetic field or Ni concentration initially increases,
while its variation is relaxed at high fields or elevated
Ni concentrations. As for weak magnetic fields and tem-
peratures above Tc in CeCoIn5, previous experiments re-
vealed that −RH exhibits a maximum at ∼4 K, which
is similar to the behavior observed in our measurements
[Fig. 4(a)] [11–13].
Here, we compare the anomalous enhancement of −RH
with certain characteristics observed in the electrical re-
sistivity. Figures 5(a), 5(c), and 5(e) illustrate the mag-
netic field dependence of the electrical resistivity ρ(H)
for CeCo1−xNixIn5, with x = 0, 0.04, and 0.125, re-
spectively, as obtained from the current measurements.
For x = 0, ρ(H) exhibits a maximum at the field Hmax
for T > Tc [Fig. 5(a)]. This feature has also been ob-
served in the previous electrical resistivity measurements
[20], and both sets of the measurements provide nearly
identical positions of Hmax in the H-T phase diagram
[Fig. 5(b)]. Note that in the H-T phase diagram, Hmax
defines the boundary that separates the regions where
dρ/dH > 0 and dρ/dH < 0 in the NFL state [20]. It is
found that the Hmax boundary shifts to lower magnetic
fields for x = 0.04 [Figs. 5(c) and 5(d)], and it subse-
quently disappears entirely for x = 0.125 [Figs. 5(e) and
5(f)]. Similar results have been obtained in our recent
electrical resistivity measurements, in which ρ(H) de-
creases monotonically with H without exhibiting a max-
imum for x ≥0.05 [56]. Interestingly, the suppression of
the divergent-like behavior in −RH above Tc at low mag-
netic fields is likely coupled with the disappearance of the
NFL region characterized by the condition of dρ/dH > 0
because both features occur sensitively with the doping
of small amounts of Ni ions. In the NFL region where
dρ/dH > 0 for x = 0 and 0.04, −RH(H) exhibits a
nonlinear decrease as the magnetic field increases [Figs.
4(a) and 4(b)].
However, the −RH(H) curve changes
to a weak linear dependence on the magnetic field for
x ≥0.125 [Fig. 4(c)], where the region with dρ/dH > 0
disappears in the H-T phase diagram [Fig. 5(f)].
As
shown in Figs. 5(b), 5(d), and 5(f), the correspondence
between the regions where −RH is significantly enhanced
and where dρ/dH > 0 holds true can also be confirmed
by the color maps of the −RH values.
The total Hall
coefficient is generally expressed as the sum of a normal
term, which primarily reflects the carrier density, and an
anomalous term, which reflects other effects [1, 2]. As
discussed in relation to Fig. 3(b), we assume that the RH
value at 0.5 K and 9 T represents the normal term. To
separate the variations in the carrier density due to Ni
substitution, the −RH values in the color maps represent
the total Hall coefficient minus the RH value at 0.5 K
and 9 T.

IV.
DISCUSSION
A.
The effect of electron doping due to Ni
substitution
We observed that −1/eRH at 0.5 K and 9 T contin-
uously increases with increasing x [Fig. 3(b)]. This in-
crease is attributed to the rise in conduction electrons
due to the Ni substitutions, as expected from the elec-
tron configurations of Co [3d7 4s2] and Ni [3d8 4s2]. How-
ever, the rate of change in the carrier density caused by
doping Ni ion, ∂n/∂x, is approximately 6.5 times greater
than unity, which is expected when assuming that an ex-
cess 3d electron in the doped Ni ion solely contributes
to the conduction electrons in Ni-doped CeCoIn5. The
observed enhancement in the electron number suggests
that doping Ni into CeCoIn5 not only increases the num-
ber of conduction electrons but also yields some modifi-
cations of the band structure around the Fermi energy.
Note that the effective magnetic moment for x ≤0.3,
estimated from the temperature dependence of magnetic
susceptibility, exhibits little variation with x and closely
matches the value estimated from the J = 5/2 multi-
plet of the Ce3+ ion [53]. This suggests that the Ni 3d
electrons do not significantly contribute to the magnetic
properties through the formation of localized moments in
Ni-substituted CeCoIn5. In this regard, it is interesting
to compare the variations in carrier density for Ni-doped
alloys with those for Sn substitutions, the latter involving
similar doping conditions concerning the electron config-
urations: In [5s2 5p1] and Sn [5s2 5p2]. Figure 3(c) shows
the magnitude of −1/eRH (interpreted as n) at T = 0.5 K

7
as a function of Tc, obtained by using actual Ni concen-
tration x′ in CeCo1−x′Nix′In5 and the actual Sn concen-
tration y′ in CeCoIn5−y′Sny′ as implicit parameters [60].
Here, we express the chemical formula as CeCoIn5−y′Sny′
instead of CeCo(In1−y′Sny′)5 to ensure consistency in the
amount of substitution with CeCo1−x′Nix′In5. The mag-
nitude of −1/eRH at the critical concentrations, charac-
terized by the Tc →0 condition, is comparable between
these alloys.
Therefore, it is considered that both Ni
and Sn ions significantly influence the electronic states,
exceeding the effect of merely increasing their number.
However, −1/eRH exhibits different curves with decreas-
ing Tc between these alloys.
For the Ni-doped alloys,
−1/eRH increases with an approximately linear depen-
dence on Tc, whereas in the Sn-substituted alloys, it is
steeply enhanced over a small range of y′ with a rate of
change ∂n/∂y′ = 27(3) number/(f.u. Sn), followed by
a tendency of saturation around Tc ∼2 K [60]. These
discrepancies in the −1/eRH curves are likely caused by
the differences in the effects of ion doping into CeCoIn5.
More specifically, the Fermi surfaces and charge compen-
sation conditions in CeCoIn5 are suggested to be highly
sensitive to the type of doped ions [62–64], because the
orbital angular momentum of the excess electron in Ni-
and Sn-doped CeCoIn5 exhibits d-like and p-like char-
acteristics, respectively, and the doped ions occupy the
different crystallographic sites between these alloys. Al-
ternatively, this may also be related to the instability
associated with the recently proposed quantum phase
transition in Sn-substituted CeCoIn5, which involves a
localized-to-delocalized crossover of Ce 4f electrons with-
out magnetic ordering [60]. We expect that the photoe-
mission spectroscopy investigation would provide valu-
able information on this issue.
The relationship between the Fermi surface and quan-
tum criticality is a subject of intense research interest. In
AFM compounds such as YbRh2Si2 and Cr, the evolu-
tion of the Fermi surface volume across the QCP has been
investigated through Hall resistivity measurements. The
results suggest that the QCP in YbRh2Si2 arises from the
so-called Kondo breakdown mechanism, whereas in Cr, it
is associated with the spin-density-wave mechanism [3–
6]. In contrast, such an investigation appears challenging
for Ni-substituted CeCoIn5 because the QCP is known
to be located very close to Hc2(0) for x ≤0.25 [56], at
which the Hall effect is significantly influenced by the SC
phase. Furthermore, −RH does not show a discontinuous
change with x, but instead exhibits a linear increase up
to x ∼0.30 [Fig. 3(b)], although the quantum critical
behavior is significantly weakened at x = 0.30 after the
SC order completely disappears, reflecting that Ni doping
suppresses superconductivity without inducing an AFM
phase [53]. Consequently, based on the current data, it is
difficult to conclude whether the underlying mechanism
of the quantum criticality corresponds to Fermi surface
reconstruction (the Kondo destruction scenario) or scat-
tering anisotropy associated with magnetic ordering (the
spin-density-wave scenario). Further measurements that
tune the system across a magnetic QCP, such as apply-
ing pressure or co-doping with other ions, are required to
distinguish between these two scenarios.
B.
The origin of the enhancement in the Hall
coefficient
The current investigation revealed that at low Ni dop-
ing levels in CeCo1−xNixIn5, the magnitude of −RH is
strongly enhanced at magnetic fields near the supercon-
ducting upper critical field Hc2 and in the low-field re-
gion above the superconducting transition temperature
Tc. However, these enhancements are diminished by fur-
ther increases in Ni concentration. In general, the Hall
coefficient reflects not only the carrier density but also
the shape of the Fermi surface resulting from complex
band structures and magnetic correlations.
Therefore,
identifying the origin of its anomalous behavior is chal-
lenging in many strongly correlated electron systems.
This is also the case for CeCoIn5. The weak depen-
dence of −RH on temperature and magnetic field is ob-
served in the paramagnetic state of the non-4f isostruc-
tural compound LaCoIn5 at low temperatures; however,
its magnitude is approximately one-fiftieth of that in
CeCoIn5 at H →0 and T = 2.5 K [12, 13]. This com-
parison between CeCoIn5 and LaCoIn5 suggests that the
anomalous enhancements of −RH in CeCoIn5 are pri-
marily caused by the involvement of Ce 4f electrons.
Here, we discuss the effects of the effective charge density
of renormalized quasiparticles and/or magnetic correla-
tions on the increase in −RH induced by Ce 4f electrons.
This discussion is based on comparisons with previous
interpretations of the enhancement of RH in CeCoIn5
[12, 13, 15], as well as physical quantities observed in
Ni-substituted systems.
Thus far, two scenarios have been proposed to ex-
plain the enhancement of −RH in CeCoIn5.
The first
is the two-fluid Kondo model, which attributes the en-
hancement of −RH to a reduction in the effective carrier
density associated with the formation of Kondo singlets
[12, 15].
Indeed, at low magnetic fields, the tempera-
ture at which −RH begins to increase (T ∼50 K) with
decreasing temperature coincides with the Kondo lattice
coherence temperature derived from this model [67]. In
this context, introducing Ni ions as impurities is expected
to disrupt the periodic potential of the Kondo lattice and
hinders the formation of the coherent Kondo state, offer-
ing a straightforward explanation for the suppression of
−RH with Ni doping [Fig. 4(d)]. However, the electri-
cal resistivity measurements revealed the opposite trend;
Ni substitution increases the coherence temperature Tcoh
defined by the broad peak in the temperature depen-
dence of electrical resistivity, from 46(1) K at x = 0
to 60(1) K at x = 0.25 [53]. Along with this increase,
the electronic specific heat coefficient γ at µ0H = 7 T
(H ∥c) also decreases from 0.9(1) J/(K2 · mol) at x = 0
to 0.36(6) J/(K2 · mol) at x = 0.25 [21, 54]. Since γ is in-


## Page 8


8
versely proportional to the Kondo temperature TK, and
Tcoh generally correlates with TK in most heavy-fermion
systems [68, 69], these experimental results suggest that
the coherent Kondo state does not become unstable with
increasing Ni concentration. To further investigate the
validity of this interpretation, it is informative to com-
pare these features with those observed in Cd-, Hg-, or
Zn-substituted systems, which are ascribed to possible
hole-doped systems where Tcoh is expected to decrease
with ion doping [42, 57].
In the second scenario, the increase in −RH above Tc at
low magnetic fields is attributed to AFM quantum criti-
cal fluctuations (QCFs) arising from anisotropic scatter-
ing times at hot spots on the Fermi surface. This inter-
pretation aligns with previous Hall resistivity studies of
CeCoIn5 [13]. This feature may be associated with the
NFL behavior corresponding to a linear T-dependence of
resistivity at zero magnetic field. Interestingly, similar
signatures indicative of a possible NFL state in the low-
field region have been observed in the Nernst coefficient
of CeCoIn5, an off-diagonal transport quantity analogous
to the Hall resistivity [70]. Although these indications
are limited to transport quantities, the anomalies con-
sistently appear in the same temperature and magnetic
field regions, suggesting a common origin. However, sev-
eral issues remain unresolved in this scenario. It is un-
clear why the QCFs persist significantly up to ∼4 K or
higher, whereas they often weaken around 1 K due to
thermal effects in heavy-fermion compounds exhibiting
quantum criticality.
In addition, magnetic order asso-
ciated with these possible QCFs emerging in the region
where dρ/dH > 0 has not yet been observed in CeCoIn5
and its doped alloys. For Ni-doped CeCoIn5, the NFL
behavior in the temperature dependence of electrical re-
sistivity persists up to x = 0.25 [53], whereas the en-
hancement in −RH rapidly diminishes for x ≥0.125 [Fig.
4(d)]. A direct observation of QCFs using inelastic neu-
tron scattering would clarify whether the increase in −RH
in the dρ/dH > 0 regime is attributable to the QCFs.
We compare our Hall effect results with existing ex-
perimental data in the H-T phase diagram.
We find
that the region where −RH(H) is strongly enhanced (at
H ∼0 and T > Tc) does not overlap with the quantum
critical region [∼Hc2(0)] identified by specific heat and
electrical resistivity measurements in CeCoIn5 [20, 21].
In CeCoIn5, the QCP at Hc2(0) is characterized by a
−ln T divergence in specific heat and a pronounced en-
hancement of the A coefficient in electrical resistivity
(ρ ∝T 2) [20, 21]. The NFL–FL crossover temperature
T L
FL and the characteristic field Hmax both extrapolate
toward Hc2(0) as T →0 [Fig.
5(b)], suggesting their
association with the QCP [20]. However, the Ni-doped
samples reveal a different trend. While the QCP persists
at Hc2(0) with increasing x up to 0.25, as evidenced by
the divergence of the A coefficient [56], even slight Ni
substitution markedly shifts the Hmax boundary toward
lower fields, leading to its disappearance for x ≥0.05
[Figs. 5(b), 5(d), and 5(f)]. This decoupling indicates
that the Hmax boundary is not intrinsically tied to the
QCP at Hc2(0).
Intriguingly, the strong enhancement
of −RH at H ∼0 and T > Tc is confined to H < Hmax
within a narrow Ni concentration range [Fig. 4(d)], where
ρ shows no significant anomalies beyond dρ/dH > 0 and
ρ ∝T. Conversely, near Hc2 at 0.5 K, the magnitude
of −RH remains modest for all x ≤0.25, despite clear
QCFs appearing in ρ [56]. These results suggest that the
anomalous transport properties near Hc2 and those in
the low-field region originate from distinct mechanisms:
the former is primarily coupled with ρ, while the latter
is coupled with RH. Nevertheless, the NFL–FL crossover
in the dρ/dH < 0 region is detectable in both ρ(T) and
RH(T) (Fig. 2), suggesting a partial coupling between
QCFs and these transport quantities.
V.
CONCLUSION
We investigated the effect of Ni substitution on the
carrier density and anomalous electrical transport prop-
erties in CeCo1−xNixIn5, with x ≤0.3, using Hall re-
sistivity measurements. The Hall coefficient RH exhibits
a characteristic temperature dependence associated with
the NFL–FL crossover in the normal state; a power-law
temperature dependence indicative of the NFL behav-
ior changes into a nearly temperature-independent be-
havior at low temperatures, corresponding to the forma-
tion of the FL state. The carrier density estimated from
−1/eRH at T = 0.5 K in sufficiently high magnetic fields
increases linearly with Ni doping, reflecting an increase in
the electron carrier concentration provided by the doped
Ni ions.
It was also found that in pure and lightly Ni doped
CeCoIn5, −RH(H) is significantly enhanced in the high-
field regime above Hc2 at low temperatures, as well as
in the low-field regime above Tc. However, the enhance-
ment of −RH(H) in both regimes is suppressed by Ni
doping. In particular, in the low-field region above Tc
where the condition dρ/dH > 0 is simultaneously sat-
isfied, this feature is rapidly diminished as the Ni con-
centration increases beyond x = 0.05. We discuss the
possible origins of this anomalous behavior in −RH(H)
from the viewpoints of the Kondo effect and QCFs.
