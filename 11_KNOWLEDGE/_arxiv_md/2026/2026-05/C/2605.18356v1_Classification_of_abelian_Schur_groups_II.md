CLASSIFICATION OF ABELIAN SCHUR GROUPS II
GRIGORY RYABOV
Abstract. A finite group G is called a Schur group if every Schur ring over G is schurian,
i.e. associated in a natural way with a subgroup of the symmetric group Sym(G) that con-
tains all right translations of G. The list of all possible abelian Schur groups was obtained by
Evdokimov, Kov´acs, and Ponomarenko in 2016. In two papers, we complete a classification of
abelian Schur groups. In the present paper, we prove that several groups of nonpowerful order
from the list are Schur groups. By that, we obtain a classification of abelian Schur groups.
Keywords: S-rings, Schur groups, permutation groups.
MSC: 05E30, 20B25.
1. Introduction
In the present paper, we continue to study the schurity problem for S-rings over abelian
groups. Recall that an S-ring (Schur ring) over a finite group G is a subring of the group
ring ZG which is a free Z-module spanned by a partition of G closed under taking inverse
and containing the identity element e of G as a class.
The theory of S-rings goes back
to the classical paper of Schur [21]. Later, the S-ring theory was substantially developed
by Wielandt (see Chapter IV of the monograph [22]).
S-rings have many applications in
algebraic combinatorics, in particular, in studying Cayley graphs, permutation group theory,
representation theory etc. The interested reader can find more information and details on
S-rings in the monograph [2] and survey [11]
One of the sources of S-rings over G is permutation groups having a regular subgroup
isomorphic to G. Namely, the partition of G into the orbits of a one-point-stabilizer in such
group defines an S-ring. As Wielandt wrote [23, p. 54], Schur had conjectured that every
S-ring can be constructed in such way. This was disproved by Wielandt [22]. An S-ring is
said to be schurian if it arises from the action of an appropriate permutation group having
a regular subgroup and a finite group is defined to be a Schur group if every S-ring over
this group is schurian. The last two notions were introduced in [15]. In the same paper, the
following problem was posed.
Problem (P¨oschel, 1974). Determine all Schur groups.
The above problem seems to be hard, in particular, because the number of S-rings over a
given group can be exponential in the order of the group. On the other hand, it is unclear in
the general case how to find an appropriate permutation group for a given S-ring or to prove
that there is no such group.
More information on schurian S-rings, Schur groups, and the P¨oschel problem is provided
in the first paper on the classification of abelian Schur groups [20] (see also [4, 5]). Several
results on schurity of nonabelian groups can be found in [12, 14, 16, 18, 19]. It should be
noted that almost all of them state that some nonabelian groups are non-Schur groups. On

Theorem 1.3. Every nontrivial S-ring over one of the groups C4 × C2p, E8 × Cp, C6 × C3k,

abelian groups in [5, 11, 13, 17, 20]. On the other hand, the above statement does not hold
for every abelian group (see [22]). It seems interesting to characterize all groups whose all
nontrivial S-rings are cyclotomic or nontrivial tensor or generalized wreath products.
Summarizing the results of [4, Theorem 1.1], [5, Theorems 1.2-1.3], [12, Theorem 1.1], [13,
Theorem 1.1], [17, Theorem 1.1], [20, Theorems 1.2-1.4], and Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, we obtain
a classification of abelian Schur groups.
Theorem (Classification of abelian Schur groups). An abelian group is a Schur group
if and only if it is isomorphic to one of the following groups:
(1) Cn, where n ∈{pk, pqk, 2pqk, pqr, 2pqr}, p, q, and r are primes, and k ≥0 is an
integer;
(2) En, where n ∈{4, 8, 9, 16, 27, 32};
(3) C2 × C2k, C4 × C2p, E4 × Cpk, E4 × Cpq, E16 × C3, where p ̸= 2 and q are primes and
k ≥1 is an integer;
(4) C3 × C3k, C6 × C3k, E9 × Cq, E9 × C2q, where q is a prime and k ≥1 is an integer.
One can also deduce the statement below from the above classification, Theorem 1.3, the
results of the papers [5, 9, 10, 11, 13, 17, 20], and computations using the package [7].
Corollary. Every nontrivial S-ring over an abelian Schur group is cyclotomic or a nontrivial
tensor or generalized wreath product.

Theorem 8.1. Let A be a nontrivial S-ring over G, H1 a maximal A-subgroup contained in
H, and P1 the least A-subgroup containing P. Then one of the following statements holds:
(1) H1P1 = G, |H1| > 1, and A = AH1 ⋆AP1;
(2) |H1| > 1 and A = AH1 ≀TG/H1;
(3) A is the nontrivial (H1P1)/P1-wreath product and AP1 is ⊗-complemented in AH1P1;
(4) A is the nontrivial (H1P1)/P1- and H1/(H1 ∩P1)-wreath products.
Proof. Due to Lemma A5.4, we have AH1P1 = AH1 ⋆AP1. If H1 = H, then H1P1 = G and
Statement (1) of the theorem holds as required. So we may assume that H1 ⪇H. Then
Lemma A5.3 holds for A. If Statement (1) of Lemma A5.3 holds for A, i.e. H1P1 = G,
P1 ⪇G, and A = AH1 ⋆AP1, then Statement (1) of the theorem holds as desired. Suppose
that Statement (2) of Lemma A5.3 holds for A, i.e. A = AH1 ≀AG/H1 with AG/H1 = TG/H1.
Together with the condition that A is nontrivial, this yields that Statement (2) of the theorem
holds as desired.

wreath product. If H1 ∩P1 is trivial, then AH1P1 = AH1 ⋆AP1 = AH1 ⊗AP1. This means that
