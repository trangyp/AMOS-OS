Abstract
In this paper, we study the complete gradient Ricci solitons (M n, g, f) with zero
radial Weyl curvature, which means that the interior product of ∇f with the Weyl
tensor W is zero, i.e., i∇fW = 0. We classify completely the complete gradient
Ricci solitons with zero radial Weyl curvature for the dimension n ≥4.
2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 53C40, 53C42.
Key words: gradient Ricci soliton, Ricci flow, Weyl tensor, radial Weyl curvature.

Introduction
An n-dimensional Riemannian manifold (Mn, g) with complete Riemannian metric
g is a gradient Ricci soliton (Mn, g, f, λ) if there is a smooth function f on Mn and a
constant λ satisfying
(1.1)
Ricg + Hess(f) = λg,
where Ricg is the Ricci tensor of g, Hess(f) denotes the Hessian of f. When λ > 0,
the Ricci soliton is shrinking; When λ = 0, it is steady and λ < 0 it is expanding. The
function f is called the potential function. When the potential function f is constant,
then the gradient Ricci soliton is Einstein. Thus the gradient Ricci soliton is a natural
generalization of the Einstein manifold. On the other hand, gradient Ricci solitons play

an important role in Hamilton’s Ricci flow as they correspond to self-similar solutions,
and often arise as singularity models. Therefore, it is important to understand the
geometry of gradient Ricci solitons, especially in classifying gradient Ricci solitons.
Hamilton in [16] showed that any 2-dimensional compact shrinking Ricci soliton must
be Einstein. Any complete 3-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci soliton is a finite
quotient of either the round sphere S3, or of the Gaussian shrinking soliton R3, or of
the round cylinder S2×R (see Ivey [17] for compact case and Perelman [22], Ni-Wallach
[21] and H.D. Cao-B.L. Chen-X.P. Zhu[2] for the complete case). In [23], Peterson-Wylie
proposed the concept of rigidity. A gradient soliton is rigid if it is a flat bundle N ×ΓRk,
where Γ acts freely on the Einstein manifold N and by orthogonal transformations on
Rk. Thus the complete 3-dimensional gradient shrinking Ricci solitons are rigid.
For high-dimensional shrinking Ricci soliton, it has been proved that complete locally
conformally flat gradient shrinking Ricci solitons are rigid (this was first due to Zhang
[28], based on the work of Ni and Wallach [23]; see also the works of Eminenti, La
Nave, and Mantegazza [13], of Petersen and Wylie [24], of Cao, Wang, and Zhang [9]).
There exist stronger results in four dimensional case,. X. Chen-Y. Wang [12] (or H.D.
Cao-Q. Chen [3]) proved that half conformally flat (i.e. W ± = 0) gradient shrinking
Ricci solitons are rigid. Moreover, it follows from the works of Fern´andez-L´opez and
Garc´ıa-R´ıo [14] and of Munteanu and Sesum [20] that n-dimensional complete gradient
shrinking solitons with harmonic Weyl tensor are rigid.
For 4-dimensional case, a
stronger result have been obtained by J.Y. Wu-P. Wu-Wylie [27], assuming the Weyl
tensor is half harmonic (i.e. div(W ±) = 0).
Gradient steady Ricci solitons are less rigid. It is well-known that compact gradient
steady solitons must be Ricci flat. In dimension n = 2, the only gradient steady Ricci
soliton with positive curvature is the Hamilton’s cigar(see Hamilton [15]). For three
dimensional case, the classification of complete gradient steady Ricci solitons is still
open. There are many results in the same spirit with shrinking gradient Ricci solitons
under the same curvature conditions(see [1],[4],[5],[7],[18],[11],[19]). Similarly, the case
of expanding solitons is clearly the less rigid. Some several interesting results under
vanishing curvature conditions have been obtained (see [4],[12],[18]).
For numerous
studies on the geometry and classifications of gradient Ricci solitons, we refer the reader
