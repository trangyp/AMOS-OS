CONJUGACY OF ISOMETRIES IN REAL ORTHOGONAL GROUPS
ZIYANG ZHU
Abstract. We determine all orthogonal transformations of a quadratic space over reals
such that any orthogonal transformation which is conjugate to one of them in the linear
group is conjugate in the orthogonal group.
1.
Introduction
To investigate the topological cobordism groups of high-dimensional knots, Levine developed
an algebraic cobordism theory based on matrices [Le69a]. Given the striking similarity between
the Alexander polynomial of a knot and the characteristic polynomial of an isometry on a qua-
dratic space, Levine further extended this algebraic theory to the framework of isometric spaces
[Le69b], following Milnor’s systematic study of the characteristic polynomials and conjugacy
classes of such isometries [Mi69]. This approach eventually led to the determination of the
general structure of topological cobordism groups for knots in high odd dimensions [Le69b,
Theorem 24]. Conversely, there has been considerable research, such as [Ta24], on identifying
the invariants of the spaces (often constructed via the local-global principle) from which a
given polynomial might originate. Milnor [Mi69, Theorem 2.1] proved that two isometries of
a quadratic space over a local field k (with char(k) ̸= 2) sharing the same irreducible minimal
polynomial are conjugate in O(V ), which was conjectured by Levine. It is natural to pose the
following more general question.
Problem 1. Let V be a non-degenerated quadratic space over a field k. Determine all orthog-
onal transformations σ ∈O(V ) satisfying that any orthogonal transformation in O(V ) which
is conjugate to σ in GL(V ) is conjugate to σ in O(V ).
When k is a non-archimedean local field with char(k) ̸= 2, the answer to the above problem is
given by [XZ26, Theorem 1.2]. For archimedean local fields, the only interesting case is k = R,
the problem in this context is what we will focus on and answer in this paper. Throughout the
subsequent discussion, we let V be a non-degenerated real quadratic space.

2. Decompositions Induced by Orthogonal Transformations
In this section, in addition to introducing fundamental concepts, we demonstrate how to
apply the key techniques from [Mi69].
These methods allow us to reduce the problem of
determining whether two similarity classes are orthogonally conjugate to the comparison of
invariants (namely, signatures) of certain quadratic subspaces.
2.1. Quadratic Spaces. We begin by reviewing several key concepts from the theory of qua-
dratic forms.
Definition 3 (Isometric Spaces). A non-degenerate quadratic space (V, ⟨∗, ∗⟩) over R is a
finite-dimensional R-linear space V equipped with a symmetric inner product ⟨∗, ∗⟩on V such
that det(⟨ei, ej⟩) ̸= 0 for a basis {e1, · · · , en} of V . A morphism between quadratic spaces is
an isometry, which is an invertible R-linear map preserves inner products. In particular, the
orthogonal group O(V ) (also denoted O(r, s)) of (V, ⟨∗, ∗⟩) is
O(V ) := {σ ∈GL(V ) : ⟨σu, σv⟩= ⟨u, v⟩, for u, v ∈V },
elements in it are called orthogonal transformations. An isometric space is a triple (V, ⟨∗, ∗⟩, σ),
where σ ∈O(V ) is an orthogonal transformation.
The complete invariant of a non-degenerate (V, ⟨∗, ∗⟩) over R is the signature (r, s) of ⟨∗, ∗⟩,
