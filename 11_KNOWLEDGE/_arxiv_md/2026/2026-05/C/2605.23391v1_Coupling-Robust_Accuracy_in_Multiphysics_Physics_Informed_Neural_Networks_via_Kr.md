Abstract
Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) for coupled multiphysics systems suffer systematic
accuracy degradation as inter-equation coupling strengthens. We provide a theoretical explanation
for this phenomenon through neural tangent kernel (NTK) analysis: for linearly coupled systems,
we prove that the standard NTK’s spectral radius grows as Ω(γ2) with coupling strength γ,
shrinking the stable learning rate, while block-diagonal Gauss–Newton (GN) preconditioning
yields a preconditioned NTK KP = JH+J⊤(where H is the block-diagonal GN Hessian) whose
spectral radius is bounded by S (S = number of networks), independent of γ. We verify the
Ω(γ2) growth numerically across symmetric, asymmetric, and nonlinear coupled PDE systems,
and confirm λmax(KP ) = S with equality in all cases. Combining the Kronecker-preconditioned
optimizer SOAP with inverse-gradient-norm loss balancing (SOAP+GN) yields coupling-robust
accuracy: across 234 experiments spanning three 1D systems of increasing nonlinearity and a
2D electroosmotic flow benchmark, SOAP+GN maintains final-epoch L2 degradation ≤1.1×
(ratio of strong- to weak-coupling error) even as coupling parameters vary over one to two
orders of magnitude, compared with > 102× for Adam+GN. SOAP+GN further scales to a 2D,
6-PDE electroosmotic flow system at EDL-resolved conditions—a regime that all prior PINN
electrokinetics studies have avoided through simplified physics—where Adam+GN fails entirely
(L2 > 0.9).

Introduction
Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) [Raissi et al., 2019] embed PDE residuals into neural
network loss functions, enabling mesh-free solutions. While PINNs have achieved broad success on
single-equation problems [Karniadakis et al., 2021, Wang et al., 2022, Hao et al., 2024], coupled
multiphysics systems—where multiple PDEs interact through shared variables—remain challenging
[Sun et al., 2024].
A fundamental difficulty is that coupled PDE residuals compete for optimizer capacity. Each
loss term’s gradient may conflict with others, and relative magnitudes depend on coupling strength.

As coupling intensifies, this imbalance worsens, causing accuracy degradation far beyond the
single-equation setting. Despite extensive work on adaptive weighting—both global loss balancing
[Wang et al., 2021] and local pointwise schemes [McClenny and Braga-Neto, 2023, Anagnostopoulos
et al., 2024]—and characterization of PINN failure modes in single-equation settings [Krishnapriyan
et al., 2021, Wang et al., 2022], no prior work has systematically measured how accuracy scales
with coupling strength across multiple systems, nor provided a theoretical explanation for why
preconditioning resolves this challenge.
Contributions.
(1) We prove that for any linearly coupled system, the standard NTK’s
spectral radius grows as Ω(γ2) with coupling strength, while block-diagonal GN preconditioning
bounds the preconditioned NTK at S (number of networks) independent of coupling, and verify
these predictions numerically across four benchmark systems (Sections 4 and 5). (2) Through a
234-experiment factorial comparison (2 optimizers × 3 balancing schemes × 4 systems), we show
that SOAP+GN is the only method achieving coupling degradation ≤1.1× across all regimes.
(3) We demonstrate SOAP+GN on a 2D, 6-PDE electroosmotic flow system that directly resolves the
electric double layer (EDL)—a regime all prior PINN electrokinetics studies have avoided through

2.2
SOAP Optimizer
SOAP [Vyas et al., 2024] applies Kronecker-factored preconditioning to each weight matrix: the
preconditioner P ≈(L ⊗R)−1/2 replaces the standard gradient update θ ←θ −η∇L with θ ←
θ −η P ∇L, where L and R are running gradient outer-product averages per weight matrix. Wang
et al. [2025] showed SOAP achieves state-of-the-art single-PDE PINN accuracy across 10 benchmarks
and formally established that SOAP’s update rule approximates Newton’s method: the Kronecker
factorization diagonalizes the Gauss–Newton Hessian in a rotated coordinate system, and Adam’s
per-coordinate scaling in this space recovers the inverse Hessian (Theorem 1 therein). However, all
benchmarks involve single-physics systems (reaction–diffusion, Navier–Stokes); its effectiveness on
cross-domain multiphysics coupling remains unverified. SOAP’s Kronecker preconditioning operates
per weight matrix (layer-wise). In our segregated-network setting, where each network maintains
disjoint parameters, there are no cross-network weight matrices, so SOAP’s layer-wise block-diagonal
structure is automatically network-wise block-diagonal—a property we formalize in Proposition 1
and connect to the GN preconditioning analyzed in Section 4. This gap motivates the present study.

our theory does not provide a formal mechanism for why gradient descent fails in this regime—only
for why block-diagonal GN preconditioning maintains bounded spectral radius regardless (Theorem 2).

manifests during training. A formal analysis of training-time spectral dynamics for nonlinearly
coupled PINNs is left to future work.

7.2
Factor Decomposition
Preconditioning (SOAP). Comparing Adam vs SOAP (both unbalanced) at strongest coupling:
SOAP improves Final L2 by 143× in Thermo, 138× in RD, and 41× in NP+P. This is consistent
with the NTK analysis: SOAP approximates block-diagonal GN through Kronecker-factored inverse
Hessian estimation [Wang et al., 2025], replacing the coupling-dependent λmax(K) = Ω(γ2) with the
coupling-independent λmax(KP ) = O(1) (Theorems 1 and 2). While SOAP involves multiple levels
of approximation to exact block-diagonal GN (Kronecker factorization, diagonal Hessian assumption
in the rotated space, EMA-based preconditioner updates), the spectral bound λmax(KP ) ≤S is
an algebraic property of the projector decomposition (Theorem 2) that holds at any parameter
configuration θ, independent of the optimizer. This is confirmed empirically: λmax(KP ) = 2.000 at
every training snapshot, for both Adam and SOAP+GN trajectories (Figure 2). The role of SOAP
is not to establish the bound itself, but to realize the coupling-independent conditioning that the
bound implies in the actual optimization trajectory.
Gradient balancing (GN). In linear systems, SOAP alone is already robust (degradation
≤1.2×). In nonlinear NP+P, SOAP alone degrades 10.9×; adding GN reduces this to 1.0×. The
NTK theory explains this asymmetry: while λmax(KP ) is bounded regardless of GN, the equal-norm
condition enforced by GN ensures that the convergence bound is tight across all PDE residuals
simultaneously.

Discussion
Theory–experiment connection. The NTK analysis (Section 4) provides three levels of theoret-
ical support for the empirical results.
Linear coupling (formal).
For systems where coupling enters the Jacobian as Ji,θk = γ Cik,
Theorem 1 proves λmax(K) = Ω(γ2) and Theorem 2 proves λmax(KP ) ≤S, independent of γ. Both
bounds are verified numerically with the predicted scaling (Figure 1 and Table 3).
Nonlinear coupling (heuristic).
For NP+P, the Ω(γ2) scaling does not hold at initialization
(Table 3), but an effective spectral growth emerges during training as the solution develops O(1/ε)
gradients (Remark 2). The GN bound λmax(KP ) ≤S remains exact throughout training (Figure 2).
SOAP as approximate GN (structural). SOAP’s layer-wise Kronecker preconditioning is auto-
matically network-wise block-diagonal in the segregated-network setting (Proposition 1), yielding
λmax(KSOAP
P
) ≤SL—a looser but still γ-independent bound. Closing the gap between SL and the
network-wise bound S remains open; the empirical coupling robustness of SOAP is additionally
supported by the SOAP–Newton connection of Wang et al. [2025].
Why Adam does not resolve coupling.
As analyzed in Section 4, Adam’s diagonal
preconditioner (≈diag(H)−1) cannot compensate for the Ω(γ2) spectral growth that originates from
cross-network Jacobian blocks (Equation (15)), because these block-level interactions are invisible
to per-parameter scaling. This is reflected empirically: at strong coupling, SOAP (unbalanced)
improves over Adam by 41–143× in absolute L2 (Table 5).
Limitations. (1) The GD spectral bound (Theorem 1) applies only to linearly coupled systems;
for nonlinearly coupled systems such as NP+P, we provide a heuristic mechanism (Remark 2) but not
a formal proof of training-time spectral growth. (2) The SOAP spectral bound SL (Proposition 1)
is looser than the exact GN bound S; closing this gap is an open problem.
Computational cost. SOAP incurs ∼1.6× wall-clock overhead per epoch compared to Adam
due to Kronecker factor maintenance. Given the 40–100× accuracy improvement, this is modest.
9
Conclusion
We have provided both theoretical and empirical evidence that Kronecker-preconditioned opti-
mization achieves coupling-robust accuracy in multiphysics PINNs. The NTK analysis reveals the
mechanism: for linearly coupled systems, the standard NTK’s spectral radius grows as Ω(γ2) with
coupling strength, while block-diagonal GN preconditioning bounds the preconditioned NTK at
S (number of networks)—independent of coupling, PDE structure, and network parameterization.
This spectral bound characterizes the conditioning of linearized residual dynamics; while not a
global convergence guarantee, the qualitative contrast ηGD
max = O(1/γ2) versus ηGN
max = O(1) correctly
predicts the empirical coupling robustness. Numerical verification confirms λmax(KP ) = S across
27 symmetric-model experiments and 4 benchmark systems, while λmax(K) spans 4 orders of
magnitude.
