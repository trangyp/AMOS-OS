CENTRAL SERIES OF COCOMMUTATIVE HOPF BRACES
MARIA BEVILACQUA, MARINO GRAN, ANDREA SCIANDRA
Abstract. By extending some classical results known for groups and skew braces, we define
and investigate central series of cocommutative Hopf braces. Both left and right central series
are defined using a ⋆-product that measures the difference between the two algebra operations,
and naturally leads to introducing the notions of socle and of annihilator of a cocommutative
Hopf brace. We characterize the central extensions relative to the subcategories of cocommuta-
tive Hopf algebras and of commutative and cocommutative Hopf algebras, respectively. Since
the category of cocommutative Hopf braces is semi-abelian and it has enough projectives with
respect to the class of cleft extensions, one can then establish suitable Hopf formulae for their
homology. These are expressed in terms of the corresponding notions of relative commutators of
cocommutative Hopf braces. In particular, the one relative to the subcategory of commutative
and cocommutative Hopf algebras turns out to be the Huq commutator.
1. Introduction
Skew (left) braces are very interesting algebraic structures that were introduced in [20] to study
solutions of the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation (see the book [10] for a thorough introduction
to the subject). Hopf braces [3] can be seen as a natural Hopf-theoretic generalization of skew
braces, providing solutions for the quantum Yang–Baxter equation in the cocommutative setting.
A Hopf brace is a datum (H, ·, •, 1, ∆, ε, S, T), where (H, ·, 1, ∆, ε, S) and (H, •, 1, ∆, ε, T) are Hopf
algebras satisfying the following compatibility condition
(1)
a • (b · c) = (a1 • b) · S(a2) · (a3 • c),
for all a, b, c ∈H,
where we are employing the Sweedler notation for the comultiplication. A morphism of Hopf braces
is a Hopf algebra map with respect to both the two Hopf algebra structures. The category HBrcoc of
cocommutative Hopf braces, i.e. the ones having a cocommutative comultiplication ∆, was recently
shown [18] to be a semi-abelian category [23], so that it is natural to investigate commutators,
central series, and homology theories in this context.
In the present article we first show that it is possible to extend the definition of left and right
nilpotency from skew braces [9] to cocommutative Hopf braces (Definition 2.0.9). It turns out that
several interesting results can be established in this more general context, many of which depend
on some nice properties of the ⋆-product of Hopf braces (see Lemma 2.0.4, that extends [28, Lemma
2.7]). This operation ⋆: H ⊗H →H, defined by
a ⋆b := S(a1) · (a2 • b1) · S(b2),
for any a, b ∈H
measures the difference between the two multiplications · and • of the Hopf brace (H, ·, •). The
existence of the ⋆-product of Hopf braces naturally leads to introducing a definition of the socle
Soc(H) and of the annihilator Ann(H) of a cocommutative Hopf brace (H, ·, •), that we then prove
to be normal Hopf subbraces of (H, ·, •) (Proposition 2.0.17).

2. Central series for cocommutative Hopf braces
We will usually denote a cocommutative Hopf brace simply by (H, ·, •), and adopt the notations
H· := (H, ·, 1, ∆, ε, S) and H• := (H, •, 1, ∆, ε, T) for the two Hopf algebra structures. The Hopf
algebra H· of a Hopf brace (H, ·, •) is a left H•-module algebra (see [3]), where the left H•-action
is defined by:
(4)
a ⇀b := S(a1) · (a2 • b),
for all a, b ∈H.
In particular, the following equations are satisfied, for all a, b, c ∈H:
a ⇀1 = ε(a)1,
a ⇀(b · c) = (a1 ⇀b) · (a2 ⇀c).
Moreover, H· becomes a Hopf algebra in the category of left H•-modules (with braiding given by
the flip map), so the following equalities hold, for all a, b ∈H:
∆(a ⇀b) = (a1 ⇀b1) ⊗(a2 ⇀b2),
ε(a ⇀b) = ε(a)ε(b),
S(a ⇀b) = a ⇀S(b).
The definition of Hopf brace also implies the following equalities (see [3])
a • b = a1 · (a2 ⇀b),
a · b = a1 • (T(a2) ⇀b),
S(a) = a1 ⇀T(a2).
By extending the notion of left ideal for a skew brace, we introduce:
Definition 2.0.1. Let (H, ·, •) be a cocommutative Hopf brace. A vector subspace B ⊆H is a
Hopf strong subbrace if it is a Hopf subalgebra B· of H· and h ⇀b ∈B, for all h ∈H, b ∈B.

It is known that ⋆is not associative in the case of skew braces, thus the same can be said at the
level of cocommutative Hopf braces. Therefore, it matters whether we ⋆-multiply H on the left or
on the right. For any vector subspaces X, Y of H, we define X ⋆Y to be the Hopf subalgebra of
H· generated by the elements x ⋆y, for x ∈X and y ∈Y . This is defined as the smallest Hopf
subalgebra of H· containing the vector space {x ⋆y | x ∈X, y ∈Y }.

Huq, namely if and only if [Hker(f), A]Huq = k1A. By [18, Lemma 8.2], this is also equivalent to
the fact that a · k = k · a = k • a = a • k for any k ∈Hker(f) and any a ∈A.
We now define another central series that can be interpreted as a generalization of the lower
central series of skew braces defined in [5] (see also [24]). Given a cocommutative Hopf brace
(H, ·, •), we define
Γ1(H) := H,
Γn+1(H) := ⟨{i ⋆h, h ⋆i, [h, i]· | h ∈H, i ∈Γn(H)}⟩·
where ⟨−⟩· denotes the generated Hopf subalgebra of H·.
Proposition 5.0.1. Let (H, ·, •) be a cocommutative Hopf brace. For any n ≥1, Γn(H) is a
normal Hopf subbrace of (H, ·, •).
Proof. We prove the thesis by induction on n. For n = 1, the statement is trivial. Now, we assume
that Γn(H) is a normal Hopf subbrace of (H, ·, •) for an arbitrary n and we prove that Γn+1(H)
is normal as well. We first observe that, since i ⋆h, h ⋆i ∈Γn(H) for any i ∈Γn(H) and h ∈H by
Corollary 2.0.7 and [h, i]· ∈Γn(H) as well, we have Γn+1(H) ⊆Γn(H).
1) Firstly, we check that H ⇀Γn+1(H) ⊆Γn+1(H). It suffices to check it for the generating
elements of Γn+1(H). Using 3) of Lemma 2.0.4, for any x, y ∈H and a ∈Γn(H), we get:
x ⇀(a ⋆y) = (x1 • a • T(x2)) ⋆(x3 ⇀y) ∈Γn(H) ⋆H ⊆Γn+1(H)
x ⇀(y ⋆a) = (x1 • y • T(x2)) ⋆(x3 ⇀a) ∈H ⋆Γn(H) ⊆Γn+1(H)
x ⇀[y, a]· = (x1 ⇀y1) · (x2 ⇀a1) · (x3 ⇀S(y2)) · (x4 ⇀S(a2))
= (x1 ⇀y1) · (x3 ⇀a1) · S(x2 ⇀y2) · S(x4 ⇀a2)
= [x1 ⇀y, x2 ⇀a] ∈[H, Γn(H)]· ⊆Γn+1(H),
as Γn(H) is a normal in H• and H ⇀Γn(H) ⊆Γn(H).
2) For any x, y ∈H and a ∈Γn(H), using 1) of Lemma 2.0.4, we obtain
x1 · (a ⋆y) · S(x2) = S(a1 ⋆x1) · (a2 ⋆x2) · x3 · (a3 ⋆y) · S(x4)
= S(a1 ⋆x1) · (a2 ⋆(x2 · y)) ∈Γn(H) ⋆H ⊆Γn+1(H).
Furthermore, since y ⋆a ∈Γn(H) (Corollary 2.0.7) and Γn(H) is normal in H·, we have
x1 · (y ⋆a) · S(x2) = [x1, y1 ⋆a1]· · (y2 ⋆a2) ∈[H, Γn(H)]· · (H ⋆Γn(H)) ⊆Γn+1(H)
x1 · [y, a]· · S(x2) = [x1 · y · S(x2), x3 · a · S(x4)]· ∈[H, Γn(H)]· ⊆Γn+1(H).
Thus, we obtain that Γn+1(H) is normal in H·.
3) Finally, we prove that Γn+1(H) is normal in H•.
Given a ∈Γn+1(H) and x ∈H, since
Γn+1(H) ⊆Γn(H), we have a1 · (a2 ⋆T(x)) ∈Γn+1(H) · (Γn(H) ⋆H) ⊆Γn+1(H), so that, by 4) of
Lemma 2.0.4 and points 1) and 2) proven above, we obtain:
x1 • a • T(x2) = x1 · (x2 ⇀(a1 · (a2 ⋆T(x3)))) · S(x4) ∈Γn+1(H).
□
We can now show the link between the series just defined and the Huq commutator, that is also
the commutator relative to the adjunction (3).

Proposition 5.0.2. For any n ≥1, the equality Γn+1(H) = [Γn(H), H]Huq holds.
Proof. The inclusion from left to right follows from the fact that the Huq commutator is symmetric,
namely, for any two normal Hopf subbraces (I, ·, •) and (J, ·, •) of (H, ·, •), we have [I, J]Huq =
[J, I]Huq. Therefore, we have that
H ⋆Γn(H), [H, Γn(H)]· ⊆[H, Γn(H)]Huq = [Γn(H), H]Huq ⊇Γn(H) ⋆H
which implies Γn+1(H) ⊆[Γn(H), H]Huq. To prove the converse inclusion [H, Γn(H)]Huq ⊆Γn+1(H),
we just have to verify that [H, Γn(H)]• ⊆Γn+1(H). But, for any x ∈H and a ∈Γn(H), we have:
x1 • a1 • T(x2) • T(a2) ∈Γn+1(H) • Γn+1(H) = Γn+1(H)
because Γn+1(H) is normal by Proposition 5.0.1.
□
