Comments on higher-derivative corrections in the AdS/CFT duality
Makoto Natsuume∗
KEK Theory Center, Institute of Particle and Nuclear Studies,
High Energy Accelerator Research Organization, Tsukuba, Ibaraki, 305-0801, Japan
We point out that some works on higher-derivative corrections in the AdS/CFT duality use inap-
propriate “AdS/CFT dictionary.” We illustrate the problem using a class of holographic supercon-
ductors in the Gauss-Bonnet black hole background. We also point out another problem in previous
works. These modifications can change the qualitative behavior of physical quantities such as the
spontaneous condensate. Whether the condensate increases or decreases under higher-derivative
corrections depends on the system one considers, and there is no universality in this sense.
Essay written for the Gravity Research Foundation 2026 Awards for Essays on Gravitation
The AdS/CFT duality or the holographic duality claims that strongly-coupled field theories are equivalent to
classical gravitational theories in AdS spacetime [1–4]. Strongly-coupled field theories are difficult to solve in general,
so AdS/CFT is applied to solve strongly-coupled theories in “real world.” Examples are quark-gluon plasma, hadron
physics, nonequilibrium physics, nonlinear physics, condensed-matter physics, quantum information, and so on (see,
e.g., Refs. [5–10]). For that purpose, AdS/CFT has been used not only by AdS/CFT experts but also by people in
different fields. Such collaborations among different fields produce many fruitful results.
The AdS/CFT duality consists of a set of rules called the “AdS/CFT dictionary.” We see one example below. But
one should not take the dictionary at face value. These rules are not laws, rather they are empirical relations. In
general, one needs to derive the AdS/CFT dictionary using a more fundamental relation such as the GKP-Witten
relation. In particular, the standard dictionary assumes the asymptotically AdS geometry in the “canonical” form:

This is well-known among experts, but it is not often emphasized enough, and there are some confusions in the
literature. The purpose of this note is to emphasize this point.
As an example, we consider bulk 5-dimensional holographic superconductors in the Gauss-Bonnet (GB) AdS black
hole background. The holographic superconductor [11–13] describes a superconductor and is one of the most studied
systems in AdS/CFT. The original holographic superconductor uses Einstein gravity. From the modern perspective,
Einstein gravity is just the leading term in the effective theory expansion. Higher-derivative corrections such as GB
gravity describe next order terms in the expansion.
The effect of higher-derivative corrections to holographic superconductors has been initiated in Ref. [14]. Since
then, various works appeared. However, as we discuss below, previous works typically have 2 problems:
• Previous works typically use the standard AdS/CFT dictionary. But the AdS/CFT dictionary gets higher-
derivative corrections in the GB AdS black hole background. This will be clear if one rewrites the GB AdS
black hole (3) in the “canonical form” (1).
• Previous works implicitly assume that the kinetic term of the dual boundary theory has the canonical normal-
ization, but this does not hold in general. In particular, the kinetic term normalization gets higher-derivative
corrections.
• These modifications can change the qualitative behavior of physical quantities such as the spontaneous conden-
sate under higher-derivative corrections.

As we saw, there are at least 3 additional places where λGB appears:
1. The factor NGB in the metric,
2. The factors Leff in the dictionary (13),
3. The GL kinetic term normalization c (16),
but they are mostly ignored in previous literature.
Usually, these modifications just change overall factors and are relatively harmless. But these factors get higher-
derivative corrections, so one needs to take them into account. The modifications can change the qualitative behavior
of physical quantities such as the spontaneous condensate.
For example, we previously obtained the analytic solution for a bulk 5-dimensional minimal holographic supercon-
ductor in the GB black hole background [18]. If one ignores these factors, the condensate |ψ|2 “decreases” for the
system (in the sense below). However, if one takes these factors into account, these factors add up together and the
condensate | ˜ψ|2 “increases.” Namely, these factors overwhelm the other background geometry effects.
However, there is no reason to believe that this is always the case. As a further example, consider “nonminimal”
holographic superconductors with A, B ̸= 0. As in our previous analysis [18], we consider the case ∆± = 2 because
we can construct the analytic solution (see,e.g., Refs. [17, 20, 21] for the other analytic solutions of holographic
superconductors).
A holographic superconductor has 2 dimensionful control parameters, temperature T and chemical potential µ, so
the system is parametrized by a dimensionless parameter µ/T. We fix T and vary µ. Then, the deviation from the
critical point is expressed by ϵµ = µ −µc, where µc is the critical point. For simplicity, we also set the AdS radius
L = 1 and we work in the unit πT = 1.

For the minimal holographic superconductor with A = B = 0, the condensate increases since the O(λGB) correction is

As we argued, most previous works do not consider the issues raised in this note, so their results must be reexamined.
We illustrate the issues using holographic superconductors as examples, but the other works on higher-derivative
corrections, in particular works using the GB AdS black hole, may have the same problems. We hope that this note
is helpful for future works on higher-derivative corrections in the AdS/CFT duality.
