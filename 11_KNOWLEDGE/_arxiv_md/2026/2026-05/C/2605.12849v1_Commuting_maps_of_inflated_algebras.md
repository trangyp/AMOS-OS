COMMUTING MAPS OF INFLATED ALGEBRAS
HONGYU JIA1 AND ZHANKUI XIAO1,*
Abstract. Commuting maps on a class of algebras called inflated algebras
are investigated. In particular, we can prove that every commuting map θ on
such an algebra is of the form θ(x) = cx + µ(x), where c belongs to the base
field K of characteristic not 2, and µ is a central-valued linear map.
1. Introduction
Throughout this paper, we assume that K is a field of char(K) ̸= 2. Let A
be an associative algebra over K (not necessarily unital). Then A becomes a Lie
algebra with respect to the Lie bracket operation [x, y] := xy −yx. A K-linear map
θ : A →A is called commuting if [θ(x), x] = 0 for all x ∈A. A commuting map θ
of A is said to be proper if it is of the form
θ(x) = λx + µ(x),
∀x ∈A,
where λ ∈Z(A), the center of A, and µ is a K-linear map with range in Z(A).
A commuting map that is not proper will be called improper. If the associative
algebra A is not unital, it is clear that, for any scalar c ∈K, the map of A defined
by x 7→cx is a typical example of commuting maps. Hence we call a commuting
map θ of A standard if it is of the form
θ(x) = cx + λx + µ(x),
∀x ∈A,
where c ∈K, λ ∈Z(A) and µ is a K-linear map with range in Z(A). For a unital
associative algebra A, the notion of a proper commuting map coincides with that
of a standard one. If A is an associative algebra without unity, we denote by ˆ
A the
unitization of A. Then a similar proof of [8, Proposition 1] shows that A has no
non-standard commuting map if and only if ˆ
A has no improper commuting map.
To our knowledge, the first important results on commuting maps are due to
Divinsky [9] and Posner [18]. The renowned Posner’s second theorem states that
the existence of a nonzero commuting derivation on a prime ring A implies the com-
mutativity of A (see [18, Theorems 1 and 2]). Motivated by Posner’s work, Breˇsar
in [2] studied general additive commuting maps on prime rings. Furthermore, in
[3] he found an intrinsic connection between commuting maps and the Herstein’s
Lie-type mapping research program (see [11]), which in turn promoted the theory
of functional identities. We encourage the reader to read the well-written survey
paper [5], in which Breˇsar outlined the theory of commuting maps, and particu-
lar emphasis was placed on its application in Lie theory. More results related to
commuting maps can be found in [1, 4, 7, 10, 15, 16, 20], etc.

It was Cheung [8] who initiated the study of commuting maps on formal matrix
algebras, and he determined a class of triangular algebras on which every commut-
ing map is proper. The second author of this paper and Wei [19] extended the
main results of [8] to generalized matrix algebras, a kind of Morita context rings.
Recently, the second and third authors of this paper in [12] established a sufficient
and necessary condition on incidence algebras such that every commuting map is
proper. Motivated by the aforementioned results, we will study commuting maps
on inflated algebras, which are deformations of the full matrix algebras Mn(K) and
play an important role in the representation theory of classical groups.
The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we introduce the notion of
inflated algebras M and present some properties of M for later use. Section 3 is
devoted to the study of commuting maps on M. By an explicitly description of the
action of a commuting map on a basis of M, we can prove that every commuting
map of an inflated algebra is standard. Here we also extend our results to a broader
class of Munn’s semigroup algebras.
2. Inflated algebras
In this section, we recall the definition and some basic properties of inflated
algebras. Let K be a field and V be a K-linear space. Given a K-bilinear form
γ : V × V →K, we define an associative algebra M = M(V, γ) as follows: as a
K-linear space, M is equal to V ⊗K V and the multiplication is defined by
(a ⊗b) · (c ⊗d) := γ(b, c)a ⊗d, R
for all a, b, c, d ∈V . This definition makes M become an associative K-algebra and
M is called the inflated algebra of K along V .
Inflated algebras appeared naturally in the representation theory of classical
groups and quantum groups (see [13] and the references therein).
In fact, [13,
Theorem 4.1] shows that every cellular algebra (for instance, Hecke algebras of
finite Coxeter groups, Brauer algebras, etc) over K is an iterated inflation of finitely
many copies of K.
Let Mn(K) be the set of all n × n matrices over K. Since V ⊗K V ∼= Mn(K) as
vector spaces, where n = dim(V ), we can realize the structure of an inflated algebra
in Mn(K). Let {v1, v2, · · · , vn} be a basis of V . Then the bilinear form γ can be
characterized by an n × n matrix P over K, that is P = (γ(vi, vj)) for 1 ≤i, j ≤n.
Now we define a new multiplication ‘·’ on the set Mn(K) by
A · B = APB
for all A, B ∈Mn(K).
Under the usual linear operations and the multiplication · , Mn(K) becomes an
associative K-algebra, denoted by (Mn(K), P). It can be shown that the inflated
algebra M(V, γ) is isomorphic to (Mn(K), P) (see [14, Lemma 4.1]). Notice that
the inflated algebra (Mn(K), P) is a generalized matrix algebra in the sense of
Brown [6], and is also a Munn’s semigroup algebra in the sense of Munn [17]. The
following technical lemma is to some extent well-known and we sketch the proof
here for reader’s convenience.
Lemma 2.1. With notations as above, if A and B are invertible n × n matrices
over K, then (Mn(K), P) and (Mn(K), APB) are isomorphic as algebras.
Proof. The map ϕ : (Mn(K), P) →(Mn(K), APB) defined by ϕ(X) = B−1XA−1
is an isomorphism of algebras.

Remark 3.11. Let m, n be two positive integers. Let Mm×n(K) be the set of all
m×n matrices over K. Given an n×m matrix P over K, we define a multiplication
‘·’ on Mm×n(K) by A·B = APB. It is clear that under the usual linear operations
and the multiplication · , Mm×n(K) becomes an associative K-algebra, denoted by
M(m, n, P).
The algebra M(m, n, P) was introduced independently by Munn [17] when he
studied the representation theory of semigroups, and by Brown [6] when he studied
the representation theory of orthogonal groups. Nowadays, M(m, n, P) is known as
a Munn’s semigroup algebra or a generalized matrix algebra in the sense of Brown,
which is generally not a generalized matrix algebra introduced by the third author
and Wei [19]. In fact, M(m, n, P) is simple if and only if it possesses an identity
element [6, Theorem].
