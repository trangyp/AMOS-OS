Continuous Temporal Representations of Event-Based
Signals via Interference-Based Wave Modeling
Magnus Bengtsson1
Department of Engineering, University of Borås, Sweden
Abstract
Spatio-temporal signals arising from event-driven biological processes,
such as surface electromyography (sEMG), exhibit asynchronous and highly
structured activation patterns that are challenging to model using conven-
tional discrete or purely real-valued representations.
In this work, we propose a continuous temporal modeling framework
based on interference-based wave representations. The approach maps event-
like input signals into a complex-valued latent wave field, where temporal
structure is encoded through phase modulation and interactions between la-
tent components.
By projecting the resulting wave field onto an energy domain, the model
induces structured activation patterns that capture both temporal localiza-
tion and relational dependencies within finite observation windows, without
relying on explicit recurrence or causal state propagation.
The proposed formulation is particularly suited for event-driven biosig-
nals, where continuous representations enable efficient gradient-based opti-
mization and robust feature extraction. In particular, the method is designed
to support learning from sEMG data for downstream control tasks in biome-
chanical systems, such as prosthetic devices and exoskeletons.
Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed interference-based
wave model provides improved representation quality compared to purely
real-valued representations, while maintaining computational efficiency suit-
able for practical deployment.

1. Introduction
Neural activity is often modeled at the level of discrete spike events, as
in spiking neural networks (SNNs) [1, 2], where information is represented
through temporally precise firing patterns.
Such models aim to capture
the dynamics of individual neurons and their interactions, and have shown
promise as a biologically inspired framework for event-driven computation.
However, in many practical settings, neural signals are not observed at
this microscopic level.
Instead, measurements such as electromyography
(EMG) [3] or surface electromyography (sEMG) [4] reflect aggregate activ-
ity arising from large populations of neurons, resulting in continuous signals
with structured temporal patterns.
This distinction motivates an alternative perspective. Rather than ex-
plicitly modeling spike generation, it may be sufficient to represent neural
activity at the level of observable macroscopic signals. In this setting, in-
formation is not necessarily encoded in individual spike events, but in the
temporal distribution of activation over finite time intervals.
From this viewpoint, SNNs can be seen as one possible approach for
modeling event-driven signals, but they do not directly address the problem
of representing the probability of event occurrence in continuous time. In
many applications, including biosignal analysis and control of biomechanical
systems, it is more relevant to estimate when meaningful events are likely to
occur, rather than to reconstruct the exact underlying spike sequence. This
suggests the need for complementary modeling frameworks that operate on
a probabilistic and continuous temporal representation.
A key advantage of continuous representations is that they are naturally
compatible with standard optimization techniques.
Unlike discrete spike-
based models, which often require surrogate gradient methods or specialized
training procedures, continuous formulations allow the use of conventional
gradient-based learning and efficient GPU acceleration.
In this work, we propose a continuous wave-based representation for mod-
eling asynchronous neural activity. The model encodes input signals as prob-
abilistic wave fields, where phase-modulated continuous wave representations
capture temporal structure within finite temporal regions. Through inter-
ference between latent components and energy-based projection, the model
induces structured activation patterns that reflect both temporal localization
and relational dependencies.
The proposed framework is particularly motivated by event-driven biosig-

1.1. Interaural Phase Difference (IPD) Classification Task
The experimental setup is conceptually inspired by educational and research-
oriented examples of temporal coding and binaural processing presented in
the COSYNE 2022 tutorial on spiking neural networks [5].
In these ex-
amples, interaural phase differences (IPD) are encoded through temporally
structured neural activity and processed using spiking network models.
In contrast to the biologically grounded formulations used in the tutorial,
the present work adopts a simplified and discretized classification setting,
where IPD is mapped to discrete output classes. This abstraction allows
controlled evaluation of temporal representation mechanisms while preserv-
ing the essential phase-based structure of the input signals.
The proposed models are evaluated on a synthetic IPD classification task,
which serves as a controlled benchmark for event-based temporal processing.
The setup isolates the model’s ability to capture temporal structure inde-
pendently of biological constraints by removing confounding biological or
environmental factors, while retaining the core phase-based encoding princi-
ple.
In this setup, each sample consists of a temporal event tensor
x ∈RT×D,
where T denotes the number of time steps and D = 2 × Near corresponds to
the number of input channels. The channels are divided into two groups rep-
resenting left and right “ears”, each containing Near = 200 channels, resulting
in D = 400 input channels.
Spike trains are generated using phase-shifted sinusoidal processes, where
a global interaural phase difference parameter ∆ϕ controls the relative delay
between the two channel groups. This parameter is sampled uniformly and
discretized into C = 12 classes, forming the target labels for classification.

5. Discussion
The proposed framework demonstrates that asynchronous spike activity
can be effectively represented in a continuous temporal domain through an
energy-based wave formulation. This representation enables efficient gradient-
based optimization while preserving essential temporal structure.
The experimental results indicate that the proposed Interference-Based
Probabilistic Wave Network (IPWN) achieves consistently high classification
performance within a narrow accuracy range (88–92%), while maintaining
stable training dynamics and significantly reduced training time compared
to the SNN baseline. In contrast, the SNN exhibits a wider performance vari-
ation (80–94%), reflecting a higher sensitivity to initialization and training
conditions.
From an energy perspective, the two models exhibit fundamentally dif-
ferent behaviors. The SNN produces sparse, discrete spike events with low
overall energy and short active durations, whereas the proposed wave-based
model generates continuous activation patterns with higher energy levels dis-
tributed over localized temporal regions. Despite this difference, both mod-
els capture the underlying temporal structure of the task, suggesting that
discrete spike representations and continuous wave representations encode
similar information in different forms.
The visualization of latent activations further highlights this distinction.
The IPWN encodes information through structured wave-like activation pat-
terns, characterized by localized temporal pulses with varying width and am-
plitude. In contrast, the SNN represents information through discrete spike
events, resulting in highly sparse and temporally precise activation patterns.
These findings suggest that event-based temporal data can be effectively
modeled either through discrete spike mechanisms or through continuous
probabilistic wave representations. The latter provides a complementary per-
spective, where temporal information is encoded as energy distributions over
finite intervals, enabling stable training and efficient computation without
relying on explicit spike dynamics.
Overall, the results support the hypothesis that continuous wave-based
representations offer a viable and robust alternative to conventional spike-
based models for event-driven learning tasks.
The proposed model can be interpreted as a learned, phase-modulated
alternative to kernel-based spike smoothing, where temporal structure and
interactions are jointly optimized rather than predefined.

Limitations. A limitation of the proposed approach is that the continuous
representation does not explicitly model discrete spike generation, which may
be important in applications where precise event timing is critical.
6. Conclusion
In this work, we introduced a continuous probabilistic wave-based frame-
work for modeling asynchronous temporal activity.
Starting from a real-
valued temporal embedding of spike trains, we identified limitations in repre-
senting interactions between latent components and addressed these through
a complex-valued formulation incorporating phase and interference.
The resulting Interference-Based Probabilistic Wave Network (IPWN)
enables the representation of temporal structure through phase-modulated
continuous wave fields, where relational information emerges via interference
mechanisms. This formulation allows efficient gradient-based training while
preserving key characteristics of event-driven data.
Experimental evaluation on a synthetic interaural phase difference (IPD)
classification task demonstrates that the proposed model achieves stable and
competitive performance, while significantly reducing training time compared
to a spiking neural network baseline.
In addition, the analysis of energy
and activation patterns reveals that the model encodes information through
structured temporal energy distributions rather than discrete spike events.
These results suggest that continuous wave-based representations provide
a complementary perspective to spike-based models for event-driven learning.
Rather than replacing discrete formulations, the proposed approach offers an
alternative representation framework that enables efficient computation and
robust training for temporally structured data.
Future work will focus on extending the framework to real-world biosig-
nals such as surface electromyography (sEMG), and exploring its integration
in control systems for biomechanical prostheses and exoskeletons.
