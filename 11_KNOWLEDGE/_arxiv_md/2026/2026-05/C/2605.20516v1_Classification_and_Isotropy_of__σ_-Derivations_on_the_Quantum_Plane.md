Classification and Isotropy of σ-Derivations on
the Quantum Plane
R. Baltazar and R. Cavalheiro
Abstract
Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and let
kq[x, y] be the quantum plane.
We study σ-derivations of kq[x, y] and
their isotropy groups under the conjugation action of automorphisms. For
q ̸= ±1, we classify all σ-derivations for an arbitrary σ ∈Aut(kq[x, y]), de-
composing them into inner σ-derivations and explicit non-inner families.
This extends, for the quantum plane, the classification of Almulhem and
Brzezi´nski. Using this classification, we determine the isotropy groups of
arbitrary σ-derivations. These groups are described by character equa-
tions on the torus k∗2, reducing the problem to arithmetic conditions.
We recover the ordinary derivation case when σ = id and exhibit new
phenomena for nontrivial σ-derivations, including cases where q is a root
of unity. We also analyze the singular case q = −1. In this setting, we
classify the σ-derivations and describe the corresponding isotropy groups.
In particular, for σ = id, we obtain an explicit description of the isotropy
groups of ordinary derivations of k−1[x, y], completing the singular case
left open in previous work [7].

Introduction
Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, let A be a k-
algebra and let σ ∈Aut(A) be a fixed automorphism. A k-linear map δ : A →A
is called a σ-derivation if it satisfies the twisted Leibniz rule
δ(ab) = δ(a)σ(b) + aδ(b),
a, b ∈A.
We denote by Derσ(A) the set of all σ-derivations of A. As in the ordinary case,
there is a natural class of inner examples: for each w ∈A, the map
[w, ]σ : A →A,
[w, b]σ = wσ(b) −bw,
is a σ-derivation, called the inner σ-derivation induced by w.
The isotropy of derivations, and more generally of σ-derivations, offers a nat-
ural perspective on the symmetries of algebraic operators. Although isotropy
subgroups arise in several contexts, their structure is still far from being com-
pletely understood. They appear, for instance, in the study of symmetries of

In this paper, for a fixed automorphism σ ∈Aut(A) and a σ-derivation
δ ∈Derσ(A), we consider the isotropy group of δ in the centralizer of σ:
Autδ(A) = {ρ ∈Aut(A) | ρσ = σρ and ρδ = δρ}.
When σ = id, this recovers the usual isotropy group of an ordinary deriva-
tion. This notion is well-defined in general, since the conditions require com-
patibility of ρ both with the σ-derivation structure and with the twisting au-
tomorphism σ. Indeed, requiring that ρ commutes with σ guarantees that the
action of ρ preserves the structure of the σ-derivation.
In the particular case of the quantum plane A = kq[x, y], Alev and Chamarie
([1, Proposition 1.4.4]) proved that, for q ̸= ±1, the automorphism group is
purely toric: ρ(x) = µ1x, ρ(y) = µ2y, with µ1, µ2 ∈k∗. Consequently, when
studying isotropy groups of σ-derivations on the quantum plane with q2 ̸= 1, it
is enough to consider toric automorphisms ρ satisfying ρδ = δρ.
The case q = −1 exhibits a different behavior. In this case, by ([1, Proposi-
tion 1.4.4]), we have:
Aut(k−1[x, y]) ∼= (k∗× k∗) ⋊⟨τ⟩,
where τ(x) = y, τ(y) = x. Thus, in the case q = −1, the isotropy problem must
be considered in the centralizer:
CAut(k−1[x,y])(σ) = {ρ ∈Aut(k−1[x, y]) | ρσ = σρ}.
In [7], A. Santana, R. Baltazar, R. Vinciguerra and W. Araujo analyzed the
isotropy of ordinary derivations on the quantum plane under the assumption
q2 ̸= 1, a setting that includes the case where q is a root of unity.
They
showed that the isotropy group associated with a derivation can be trivial,
cyclic, finite of prescribed cardinality, or infinite. In this way, the structure of
these isotropy groups is completely described in terms of elementary arithmetic
invariants together with the algebraic curves defined by the associated character
equations.

In Section 2 we provide an explicit characterization of the σ-derivations of the
quantum plane kq[x, y] under the only assumption that q ̸= ±1. More precisely,
we consider an arbitrary automorphism σ and describe every σ-derivation as
a sum of an inner σ-derivation and a finite number of non-inner components.
This extends, in the case of the quantum plane, the classification obtained by
Almulhem and Brzezi´nski in Theorem 1 for the automorphisms σµ, since our
description applies to all automorphisms of kq[x, y], including the case where q
is a root of unity.
In Section 3 we study the isotropy groups of the σ-derivations classified in
Section 2. We explicitly describe the isotropy groups of each family appearing
in Theorem 3. Since these families form independent Aut(kq[x, y])-submodules,
the isotropy group of an arbitrary σ-derivation is obtained as the intersection of
the isotropy groups of its components. Equivalently, it is described by character
equations on the torus k∗2, and hence by arithmetic conditions in the lattice Z2.
This allows us to recover the ordinary derivation case of [7] when σ = id. At the
same time, for nontrivial σ-derivations we obtain new phenomena: when q is not
a root of unity, every finite subgroup of k∗2 occurs as an isotropy group, while
in the root-of-unity case some finite groups, which do not occur for ordinary
derivations, may occur for suitable choices of σ.
In Section 4 we study the singular case q = −1. We first characterize the σ-
derivations of k−1[x, y], distinguishing between toric and flip automorphisms σ.

Remark 23. The condition on the inner component in Derσ(kq[x, y]) is always
governed by the σ-twisted center.
Indeed, ρ ∈Autσ(kq[x, y]) if and only if
ρ(w) −w ∈Zσ(kq[x, y]).
In the case q ̸= ±1, this is precisely the condition appearing in Proposition
11. In the singular case q = −1, the same principle applies. For toric automor-
phisms σ(x) = γx, σ(y) = γy, the possible twisted centers are 0, k[x2, y2] or
k[x2, y2]yx. In this case, the condition is written as ρ(w) −w ∈Zσ(k−1[x, y]).
On the other hand, for flip automorphisms σ(x) = αy, σ(y) = βx, we have
Zσ(k−1[x, y]) = 0. Therefore, in the flip case, fixing the inner component is
equivalent to ρ(w) = w.
A similar phenomenon also appears for ordinary inner derivations on the
differential Ore extension Ah = k[x][t; d], with d = h(x)∂x. Since the center of
Ah is k, the isotropy of an inner derivation adw is described by the condition
ρ(w) −w ∈k. However, in several important situations this is equivalent to
requiring that ρ(w) = w; see, for instance, [4, Proposition 25, 27 and 28].
