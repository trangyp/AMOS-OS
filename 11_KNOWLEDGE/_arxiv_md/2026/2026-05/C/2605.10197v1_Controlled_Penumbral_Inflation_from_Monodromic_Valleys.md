Long monodromic valleys arise in the penumbra of complex-structure moduli space. We show that
their local branch data already determine whether they support controlled inflation, and thereby
isolate the first controlled penumbral inflationary window.
In the axion–saxion effective theory
given in Eq. (4), a branch-displacing odd term generates a plateau when ∆≡p + 2ν −q > 0,
while covariant single-clock control further requires p < 2, or p = 2 with 12Apm2/V0 ≫1 over
the observational window. This splits penumbral valleys into no plateau, uncontrolled plateau, and
controlled plateau before global completion is attempted. We identify a minimal analytic family with
a closed-form valley and an invariant attractor equation for the full two-field dynamics, providing
the first exactly solvable penumbral realization that remains predictive under the next penumbral
order. The penumbra is thus promoted from a geometric suggestion to a predictive search principle.
Introduction.–Axion monodromy remains one of the
clearest routes to parametrically long inflationary tra-
jectories in string theory [1–3].
The decisive obstruc-
tion is no longer field range alone. A long valley may
still fail because heavy-sector backreaction, canonical dis-
tance, or loss of adiabaticity destroys the single-clock de-
scription [4, 5]. F-term constructions, hierarchy analyses,
universal axion backreaction, four-form descriptions, and
large-distance EFT studies established how monodromy
reshapes potentials and exposes UV sensitivity [6–16].
Hyperbolic attractors explain why logarithmic canonical
variables generate plateau-like observables [17–21]. The
decisive need for top-down work is a local discrimina-
tor: which long monodromic valleys are controlled infla-
tionary targets near, though not at, an infinite-distance
boundary?
Recent work isolated the penumbra
of complex-
structure moduli space as the crossover region where
uplift-like positive terms coexist with surviving poly-
nomial monodromic corrections and long axion val-
leys [22, 23].
Throughout this Letter, umbra denotes
the strict asymptotic full-shadow regime near an infinite-
distance boundary, whereas penumbra denotes the near-
boundary partial-shadow regime where those terms still
coexist; Fig. 1 fixes the terminology. That analysis made
the regime concrete and showed why geometry alone does
not secure inflation: the late de Sitter slope remains too
large, other sectors run away, or the orthogonal mode
fails to stay adiabatically heavy. The decisive local input
is a criterion that either upgrades a monodromic penum-
bral valley to a controlled inflationary EFT or rejects it
locally.
Here we derive that criterion.

boundaries, curved-field EFT is rich enough to reveal
flattening yet deliberately local: it cannot by itself settle
global viability [9–11, 13–16, 24, 25]. If local data already

Local Control Theorem. A penumbral valley supports
a controlled plateau if and only if ∆> 0 and either p < 2,

no plateau (∆≤0), plateau without control (∆> 0 and
p > 2), and controlled plateau.

selection rule. A long valley is not enough. The same

Tameness, Scope, and Remaining Global In-
put.— The penumbra and tameness literature impose
a sharper requirement: local couplings must belong to a
controlled definable class rather than exhibit arbitrarily
wild behavior near the boundary [22, 24–27]. Our con-
struction isolates a finite-distance penumbral patch on
which inverse powers of s, log s, exponentials of log s,
and periodic functions of the compact axion remain de-
finable [24, 26]. At the quantum-gravity level, because
the inflationary window lies at finite distance, the strict
infinite-distance obstruction is avoided at the level of the
local EFT. Any admissible completion must still satisfy

This perspective sharpens the classification of penum-
bral valleys. Long uplift-like valleys do not automatically
yield inflation [22, 23, 39, 40]: the slow-roll slope remains
too large, other sectors such as K¨ahler moduli or the axio-
dilaton run away, or the orthogonal mode fails to remain
adiabatically heavy. The theorem isolates the third ob-
struction locally and splits valleys into no plateau, un-
controlled plateau, and controlled plateau. Within the
one-modulus local EFT, it is a necessary-and-sufficient
control criterion; for compactification, it becomes the fil-
ter deciding which valleys merit a global embedding and
which can be excluded at the local level. It is a local
reducer of the compactification burden of proof.
The implications are broader than one benchmark.
The distinction from standard attractor logic is struc-
tural [18, 19, 21]: hyperbolic geometry supplies the log-
arithmic field map, while the monodromic branch dis-
placement supplies the saxionic rotation of the tangent
mode, adiabatic control of the orthogonal sector, and
predictive stability of the observable corridor under the
next penumbral order. Those features follow from the
local monodromic EFT together with the heavy-sector
criterion. The integer q labels discrete penumbral fami-
lies with common leading tilt and parallel running–tensor

Conclusion.—This work establishes a local, covari-
ant, and falsifiable selection rule for top-down inflation-
ary model building. A penumbral construction that real-
izes this mechanism must exhibit three linked signatures:
branch-induced saxionic rotation, the heavy-control in-
equality, and the associated (ns, r, αs) family.
The lo-
cal analysis is the decision rule for which penumbral val-
leys deserve elevation to full compactifications. The im-
mediate target is a survey of known Calabi–Yau orien-
tifolds for explicit flux choices realizing the (p, ∆) = (2, 3)
benchmark, together with an extension of the local con-
trol theorem that incorporates K¨ahler-moduli stabiliza-
tion effects at next-to-leading order in the large-volume
expansion.
