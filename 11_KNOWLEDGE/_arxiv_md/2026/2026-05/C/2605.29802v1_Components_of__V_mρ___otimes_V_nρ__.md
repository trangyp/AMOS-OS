Abstract
Let g be a symmetrizable Kac-Moody Lie algebra and let ρ denote the sum of the
fundamental weights.
The irreducible highest weight representations V (mρ) occupy a
distinguished position in representation theory due to their rich symmetry and geometric
significance. In this paper, we study the tensor products
V (mρ) ⊗V (nρ),
m, n ∈N,
and investigate the structure of their irreducible decompositions. Motivated by the classi-
cal conjecture of Kostant, which predicts a highly structured behavior in simpler settings,
we propose a general framework describing the irreducible components appearing in such
tensor products for finite-dimensional semisimple or affine Kac-Moody Lie algebras g. Our
results identify a family of dominant weights governing the decomposition and provide
criteria for their occurrence. This work extends the scope of Kostant-type phenomena
and reveals new structural patterns in tensor products associated with multiples of the
Weyl vector.

Introduction
1.1
Kostant’s V (ρ) ⊗V (ρ) conjecture
The representation theory of complex semisimple Lie algebras is a central area of modern
mathematics, with deep connections to algebraic geometry, combinatorics, and mathematical
physics. A fundamental problem in this theory is to understand the decomposition of tensor
products of irreducible representations into irreducible components. While general tools such
as the Weyl character formula and crystal bases provide theoretical answers, explicit and
uniform descriptions of tensor product decompositions remain difficult to obtain in most cases.
Let g be a complex semisimple Lie algebra with root system Φ, and let ρ denote the
half-sum of positive roots. For each dominant integral weight λ ∈P +, we denote by V (λ)
the irreducible highest weight representation of highest weight λ. Among these, the represen-
tations V (mρ), for m ∈N, are of particular importance. They arise naturally in harmonic
analysis, the study of primitive ideals, and the geometry of flag varieties, and reflect deep
symmetries inherent in the structure of g.
A classical conjecture of Kostant [3] suggests that tensor products involving representations
related to ρ exhibit remarkable regularity: all irreducible summands which can appear must
actually appear.

Conjecture 1. Let g be a complex semisimple Lie algebra, and λ ∈P + a dominant integral
weight such that λ ≤2ρ in the dominance order. Then V (λ) ⊆V (ρ) ⊗V (ρ).
This conjecture was first proven for g = sl(n + 1) by Berenstein-Zelevinsky [3], where
the conjecture first appeared in the literature.
Chiriví-Kumar-Maffei [8] showed that this
conjecture holds for all g, up to a saturation factor (cf. Definition 2), recovering the theorem
for sl(n + 1). The conjecture was later extended by Kumar and the second author to affine
Kac-Moody Lie algebras (where ρ is interpreted as the sum of the fundamental weights) and
shown to hold for g = bsl(n + 1), and again for all affine Kac-Moody Lie algebras up to a
saturation factor.
To date, Conjecture 1 remains open for all other classical simple Lie algebras, having been
checked directly for g of exceptional types.
1.2
A generalization to unequal parameters
By the same proofs as in [8, 14], Conjecture 1 can easily be extended to tensor products
of the form V (nρ) ⊗V (nρ) for any integer n ≥1, with the condition that λ ≤2nρ being
sufficient. We refer to this as the “equal parameter" or “diagonal" case.
In this note, we propose a generalization of Conjecture 1 to include the unequal parameter
or non-diagonal case by considering tensor products of the form
V (mρ) ⊗V (nρ)
for m ≥n ≥0. In order to pose a generalization of Kostant’s conjecture that covers the
non-diagonal case, we first need a suitable replacement for the proposed sufficient condition
λ ≤2ρ. The first natural guess, requiring λ ≤(m + n)ρ, turns out to be insufficient, as in the
following example.
Example 1. Let g be of type B2. Then there are sixteen dominant integral weights λ ≤7ρ
such that V (λ) ̸⊆V (5ρ) ⊗V (2ρ). One such example is λ = ω2.
Instead, we are motivated by a different necessary condition on tensor product decompo-
sitions; namely, if V (ν) ⊆V (λ) ⊗V (µ) for dominant integral weights λ, µ, ν, then necessarily
ν = λ + β
where β is some weight appearing in the representation V (µ).
By ([8], Proposition 9), a
dominant integral weight λ satisfies λ ≤2ρ if and only if λ = ρ + β where β is a weight of
V (ρ). Thus, we make the following modified conjecture, which is the topic of this note.
Conjecture 2. Let g be a simple Lie algebra or untwisted affine Kac-Moody Lie algebra. Let
m ≥n ≥0 be two nonnegative integers, and let λ ∈P + be a dominant integral weight such
that
λ = mρ + β
for some weight β of the representation V (nρ). Then V (λ) ⊆V (mρ) ⊗V (nρ).
That is, the dominant weights occurring in the tensor product appear to be obtained
simply by translating the weight set of V (nρ) by mρ and considering the resulting dominant
weights. Such a description is unexpected to hold for tensor products at large.

1.3
A connection to embeddings of tensor products
We highlight a compatibility of Conjecture 2 and embeddings of tensor products of irre-
ducible representations. The latter question originates in the study of fusion products of cyclic
g[t]-modules, where g[t] := g ⊗C C[t] is the current algebra associated to a simple Lie algebra.
See [4, 7, 9, 23] for an overview of the following problem.
Let λ, µ, λ′, µ′ ∈P + be four dominant integral weights such that λ + µ = λ′ + µ′. Then
we say that (λ, µ) ⪯(λ′, µ′) if
min{λ(β∨), µ(β∨)} ≤min{λ′(β∨), µ′(β∨)}
for all positive coroots β∨. The following conjecture relates this ordering on pairs of dominant
weights to embeddings of tensor products.
Conjecture 3. Suppose (λ, µ) ⪯(λ′, µ′). Then V (λ) ⊗V (µ) ⊆V (λ′) ⊗V (µ′).
The above “Schur positivity" conjecture was originally formulated in [23] for the Lie algebra
sln. It was subsequently extended to arbitrary simple Lie algebras in [7], where it was verified
in the case of sl3 and for multiples of minuscule fundamental weights using crystal-theoretic
methods. A weaker form of the conjecture, namely support containment, was established in
[9]. Further progress in this direction was made in [4], where support containment was proved
for certain special cases of simply laced symmetrizable Kac–Moody algebras, again via crystal
theory. Finally, in [27], a complete proof of the conjecture for sln was obtained through the
construction of a new combinatorial model for Littlewood–Richardson coefficients.

Tensor cones and inequalities
We fix g to be either a simple (finite-dimensional) Lie algebra or an untwisted affine Kac-
Moody Lie algebra associated to a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra. While the proofs of
Theorem 1(1) in each case are identical, we will adopt the notational conventions of [6] and
[26] from the affine Kac-Moody setting.
Definition 1. The saturated tensor cone Γ2(g) is given by the set
Γ2(g) := {(λ, µ, ν) ∈(P +)3 : ∃N ≥1 with V (Nν) ⊆V (Nλ) ⊗V (Nµ)}.
The saturated tensor cone often appears under the name the saturated tensor semigroup or
the additive eigencone of g, and has a natural generalization to include more than two tensor
factors.
For g = sln+1(C), this recovers the classical Horn cone related to the Hermitian
additivie eigenvalue problem.
When g is finite-dimensional, this is finitely-generated polyhedral cone; in the affine setting,
the cone is no longer finitely generated and is locally polyhedral.
In the latter case, the
existence of a saturation factor, as in the following definition, is not immediate but has been
determined by Ressayre [26].
Definition 2. A saturation factor d ≥1 of Γ2(g) is a positive integer such that, for any
(λ, µ, ν) ∈(P +)3 where λ+µ−ν ∈Q and (λ, µ, ν) ∈Γ2(g), we have V (dν) ⊆V (dλ)⊗V (dµ).
