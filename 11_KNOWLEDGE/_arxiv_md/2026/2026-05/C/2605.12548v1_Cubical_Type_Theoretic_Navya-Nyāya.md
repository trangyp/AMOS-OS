Abstract
We present a formalization of the technical language of Navya-Ny¯aya — the “New Logic”
school of late-classical Indian philosophy — in CCHM De Morgan cubical type theory (CTT).
Previous formalization attempts in first-order logic (Matilal), higher-order logic (Ganeri),
and Martin-Löf type theory (Bhattacharyya) each lose load-bearing structure: dependent
delimitation (avacchedaka), typed absence (abh¯ava), non-extensional identity (t¯ad¯atmya),
or unbounded relational depth (parampar¯a-sambandha). We argue that CTT closes this
gap natively. We give CTT encodings for seven core constructs (sambandha, avacchedaka,
abh¯ava, vy¯apti, t¯ad¯atmya, higher relations, pary¯apti) plus the qualifier-qualificand structure;
develop a stratified-universe foundation for the pad¯artha system; and prove four signa-
ture theorems internal to the encoding (Involution of abh¯ava, Keval¯anvayi-irreducibility,
Coextension-without-identity, No-h-set-collapse) and six metatheoretic results (soundness,
conservativity, faithfulness, distinction-preservation, decidability, commentarial conserva-
tivity). We close with worked encodings of fifteen Tattvacint¯aman. i passages, comparison
with prior formalizations, an implementation sketch in Cubical Agda, and five distinguishing
predictions — including a novel argument from Navya-Ny¯aya’s involutive-negation doctrine
for the necessity of De Morgan over Cartesian cubical foundations.
Keywords: Navya-Ny¯aya, cubical type theory, formal philosophy, dependent types, higher
inductive types, autoformalization.

Introduction
1.1
Navya-Ny¯aya as a precision logic of relations
Navya-Ny¯aya — the “New Logic” — emerged in fourteenth-century Mithil¯a in the work of
Ga˙ngeśa Up¯adhy¯aya and reached expressive maturity in seventeenth-century Navadv¯ıpa under
Raghun¯atha Śiroman.i, Mathur¯an¯atha Tarkav¯ag¯ıśa, Jagad¯ıśa Tark¯alam. k¯ara, and Gad¯adhara
Bhat.t.¯ac¯arya. Its philosophical commitments — robust realism about universals, categorial
pluralism, substantival ontology of relations — are continuous with the older Ny¯aya–Vaiśes.ika
synthesis [26, 15]. Its technique is not: over four centuries the school constructed a precision-
engineered technical idiom that exhibits logical structure at the surface of expression.
A canonical Navya-Ny¯aya sentence such as
bh¯utale ghat.a-pratiyogika-sam. yoga-sambandh¯avacchinna-pratiyogit¯a-nis.t.ha-atyant¯abh¯ava-
vat
— “(the floor) is the locus of an absolute absence whose counter-correlate-ness is delimited by
the relation of contact whose counter-correlate is a pot” — does not so much describe a logical
structure as exhibit it [17, 21, 11]. Agglutinative -t¯a abstracts (pratiyogi-t¯a, avacchedaka-t¯a),
systematic compounding of delimitor and delimited, and iterated nesting of qualifications produce
a notation in which every relational role is morphologically marked.
The morphological precision reflects an underlying metatheoretic stance: Navya-Ny¯aya takes
relations as ontologically primary objects of theory, not predicative auxiliaries. The relational
vocabulary — sambandha, avacchedaka, pratiyogin/anuyogin, vy¯apti, abh¯ava, pary¯apti, viśes.an. a-
viśes.ya, parampar¯a-sambandha, t¯ad¯atmya — is the surface of a coherent theory of relational
structure. Daniel Ingalls famously called the result “the most precise calculus of properties
produced before Frege” [17, p. 1].
We retain this judgement with one refinement.
Navya-Ny¯aya is not a calculus in the
Hilbert/Frege sense but a precision logic: a discipline of expressing logical structure that does
not separate object-level reasoning from metatheoretic discourse. The same vocabulary asserting
a logical claim is used to argue about logical claims. Navya-Ny¯aya is self-presenting — its
inferential structure is meant to be visible from within the same expressive apparatus that does
the inferring.
1.2
The autoformalization gap
Three sustained efforts illustrate the pattern of forced compromise.
Matilal (FOL).
Bimal Krishna Matilal’s career-long project [21, 22, 23] renders Navya-Ny¯aya
in first-order predicate logic. The strategy succeeds for many surface logical claims but has
three load-bearing failures. First, avacchedaka — the delimitor restricting a relation’s scope
— has no proper FOL home; rendering it as subscript or extra argument-place captures the

fact of delimitation but loses the type-level binding. Second, abh¯ava — the positive entity
Navya-Ny¯aya associates with absence — is treated as logical ¬, losing the data (pratiyogin,
delimitor, mode of relation) that distinguishes Navya-Ny¯aya absence from logical negation. Third,
parampar¯a-sambandha — relation-via-mediation, of unbounded depth — has no satisfactory
FOL rendering.
Ganeri (HOL).
Jonardon Ganeri [11, 12, 13] advances to higher-order logic and recovers
properties-of-properties and abstraction over predicates. But HOL does not give dependency:
an avacchedaka delimiting a pratiyogin is a type-level binder, not a free variable. HOL has
predicates and predicates-of-predicates but not predicates indexed by terms. HOL also retains
classical extensionality, conflicting with Navya-Ny¯aya’s robust intensionalism (ghat.atva/kalaśatva
coextension distinction; [24, ch. 4]).
Bhattacharyya (MLTT).
Sibajiban Bhattacharyya [6, 7] takes the next step into dependent
type theory in the spirit of Martin-Löf. This recovers avacchedaka-as-Π and a typed pratiyogin.
For substantial fragments of Navya-Ny¯aya the framework is adequate. But standard MLTT has
propositional equality (Id) without path equality: identity is too rigid for t¯ad¯atmya-relations
between cognitively indistinguishable entities, and h-set collapse defeats parampar¯a-sambandha.
Pattern.
The pattern is interlocking.
An inadequate avacchedaka contaminates abh¯ava,
which contaminates vy¯apti, which contaminates anum¯ana as a whole. By the time one reaches
Gad¯adhara’s Vyutpattiv¯ada or Jagad¯ıśa’s Śabdaśakti-prak¯aśik¯a, every early compromise is paid
for. We call this structural mismatch the autoformalization gap.
1.3
Why cubical type theory
Cubical type theory in the CCHM presentation [9] arose from a different motivating problem:
making Voevodsky’s univalence axiom compute. By taking paths (rather than identity types)
as primitive — represented as functions from an interval object I with endpoints 0, 1 — and
equipping the interval with a De Morgan structure (meet, join, involution), CCHM makes
univalence a theorem rather than an axiom and transp(ua(e), x) = e(x) a definitional equality.
Each expressive demand that defeated previous formalizations is met natively by some specific
CTT feature.
• Sambandha and paths. A relation in Navya-Ny¯aya can stand as a relatum of further
relations. In CTT a path is itself a term of a path-type, which itself supports paths.
Sambandha encodes as Path A x y; parampar¯a-sambandha’s unbounded depth corresponds
to path-types iterating without truncation.
• Avacchedaka and Π. A delimitor encodes as a Π-binding; the relation’s type depends on
its delimitor.
• Abh¯ava and HITs. A higher inductive type whose path-constructor records pratiyogin-
data realizes typed absence directly.
• T¯ad¯atmya and computational univalence. CTT’s path-type with computational ua
is rich enough to represent indiscernibility while remaining decidable.
• De Morgan and abh¯ava-of-abh¯ava. The De Morgan involution 1 −r on I encodes
Raghun¯atha’s doctrine that abh¯ava of abh¯ava is bh¯ava as a definitional equality. This is
novel here and yields a distinguishing argument for CCHM over Cartesian cubical (§17.5).

2.2
The pad¯artha architecture
The Vaiśes.ika categorial system, as inherited by Navya-Ny¯aya, recognizes seven pad¯arthas:
dravya (substance), gun. a (quality), karman (motion), s¯am¯anya (universal), viśes.a (ultimate
differentiator), samav¯aya (inherence), and abh¯ava (absence). The categorial separation principle

— sam. kara-dos.a — forbids a token of one category from inhabiting another: a gun. a cannot be a
dravya; a s¯am¯anya cannot itself instantiate a further s¯am¯anya (the anavasth¯a argument). The
hierarchy is strict and supports the universe-stratification we develop in §4.
2.3
Sambandha (relation)
A sambandha in Navya-Ny¯aya is an entity, not a predicate. It has relata, modes (praty¯asatti),
and can stand as a relatum of further relations. The principal modes are:
• Sam. yoga (contact): symmetric, occurrent between substances.
• Samav¯aya (inherence): asymmetric; obtains between universal and instance, substance
and quality, whole and part.
• Svar¯upa-sambandha (self-linking): the relation an entity bears to itself.
• T¯ad¯atmya (identity-relation): the relation between an entity and what is, in the relevant
respect, indiscernible from it.
Parampar¯a-sambandha is mediated relation: A is related to C via B. Navya-Ny¯aya admits
arbitrary mediation depth, treats the mediated relation as a single object, and allows it to itself
stand in further relations [28, §iii].
First commitment.
A relation is not a 0-truncated proposition; it has structure, modes, and
admits relations-of-relations.
2.4
Avacchedaka (delimitor)
Whenever a relation R obtains, it obtains under a delimitor. The pratiyogin of an abh¯ava is
delimited by some property A, the anuyogin by another, the sambandha itself by yet another.
The delimitor restricts the scope: “the absence of pots-as-delimited-by-clay-substanceness on
the floor” is distinct from “the absence of pots-as-delimited-by-narrowness on the floor.”
Delimitation iterates: a delimitor can have its own delimitor (avacchedaka-t¯a-avacchedaka).
The non-redundancy condition (l¯aghava) requires the delimitation to do real work.
The delimitor is not an external parameter; it binds into the type of the relation it qualifies.
This is the technical move that makes Navya-Ny¯aya’s calculus precise.
2.5
Abh¯ava (typed absence)
Navya-Ny¯aya treats absence as a positive entity with structured data:
• pratiyogin (counter-correlate — the thing absent),
• anuyogin (correlate — the locus of the absence),
• avacchedaka on the pratiyogin,
• sambandha by which the pratiyogin would have been related to the anuyogin had the
absence not held.
Four canonical sub-types:
• Pr¯agabh¯ava — prior absence; obtains before the entity comes into being.
• Pradhvam. s¯abh¯ava — posterior absence; obtains after destruction.
• Atyant¯abh¯ava — constant/absolute absence; obtains across all time.
• Anyony¯abh¯ava — mutual absence; “x is not y,” construed as a positive relation.
A doctrine load-bearing in our treatment: abh¯ava of abh¯ava is bh¯ava — double absence
collapses to presence, an involutive structure [28, §iv].

Vy¯apti and anum¯ana
The anum¯ana-khan. d. a of TC develops the theory of inference. The hetu pervades the s¯adhya

Pary¯apti (numerical exhaustion)
When Navya-Ny¯aya says “two-ness inheres in two pots,” the two-ness is pary¯aptimat — exhaus-
tively distributed in the pair, neither over- nor under-counted. Pary¯apti is a typed cardinality:
closer to type-theoretic Σ-over-Fin than to set-cardinality.
2.8
Viśes.an. a-viśes.ya-bh¯ava
The structure of cognition (jñ¯ana) is qualifier-qualificand: a cognition has a principal qualificand

The structure iterates: a qualifier can itself be qualified.

Preliminaries: Cubical Type Theory
We summarize CCHM De Morgan cubical type theory at a level adequate for the encodings.
References: [9, 16, 33].
3.1
Why CCHM rather than Cartesian cubical
CTT comes in two principal variants. The CCHM [9] variant equips the interval with a De
Morgan algebra (meet ∧, join ∨, involution 1−r). The Cartesian variant [1] drops the involution.
For most foundational mathematics the difference is technical. For Navya-Ny¯aya it is decisive:
§17.5 argues the involutive structure of abh¯ava-of-abh¯ava selects CCHM on philosophical grounds.
3.2
The interval object I
The interval is a primitive judgement-form. We have endpoints 0 : I, 1 : I, and operations: meet
r ∧s, join r ∨s, and involution 1 −r. These satisfy:
• 1 −(1 −r) = r (involutivity),
• 1 −(r ∧s) = (1 −r) ∨(1 −s) (De Morgan),
• lattice laws.
Involutivity is the algebraic structure underlying our encoding of abh¯ava in §7.

3.3
Path types
For type A and x, y : A, the path type Path A x y consists of p : I →A with p 0 = x, p 1 = y. We
write p i for application. Reflexivity: refl x := λ_. x : Path A x x. Symmetry: p−1 := λi. p (1 −i) :
Path A y x.
3.4
hcomp and transp
Homogeneous composition hcomp fills in cubes given partial boundary data. Transport transp
moves elements along type-level paths: given P : I →Type, r : I, and x : P r, we obtain
transp P r x : P 1. When P is constant, transport reduces to identity. These two equip path-
types with the full higher groupoid structure.
3.5
Glue types and computational univalence
The Glue construction takes B and e : A ≃B and produces Glue B [e] definitionally equal to
A along i = 0 and to B along i = 1. From this we derive ua : (A ≃B) →Path Type A B, with
transp (ua e) x = e.fst x holding definitionally. This is computational univalence.

9.5
Coextension-without-identity theorem
Theorem 9.1 (Coextension-without-identity). Two universals P, Q : Locus →Type with the
same extension — (x : Locus) →P x ≃Q x — need not be path-equal as functions in the
encoding.
Proof. Path-equality of functions in CTT requires funext, which holds in CCHM. Coextension
gives (x : Locus) →P x ≃Q x, and funext gives Path (Locus →Type) P Q.
However, the
universals P and Q are not merely set-functions but Σ-typed objects (per §4.4) carrying
anugama-witnesses. Two universals can be coextensive without their anugama components being
equal; the Σ-equality therefore fails.
This is the formal counterpart to Navya-Ny¯aya’s robust intensionalism.

forms. The encoding works mathematically but is not autoformalization-grade: typechecking
does not decide.
In MLTT/CIC the encoding does not work: there is no ua, and Id-types collapse paths under
K.

a determinate categorial universe, and aikya across universes is blocked by §4.

Metatheoretic Results
14.1
Soundness
Theorem 14.1 (Soundness). Let |=NN be the model-theoretic semantics of Navya-Ny¯aya given
by Ganeri [11, ch. 4] extended with Bhattacharyya’s [7] treatment of identity. Let ⊢CTT be
derivability in our CTT encoding. Then for every Navya-Ny¯aya sentence φ and its encoding
⌜φ⌝: if |=NN φ then ⊢CTT ⌜φ⌝.
Proof sketch. Induction on the logical form of Navya-Ny¯aya sentences. The base cases — atomic
relational claims — are handled by §5; the avacchedaka operator by §6; the abh¯ava operator by
§7; vy¯apti by §8; qualifier-qualificand by §12. Each clause preserves model-theoretic truth in the
encoding’s path-typing.
14.2
Conservativity
Theorem 14.2 (Conservativity). The encoding adds no new theorems to CCHM CTT. For any
CTT-only formula ψ (not involving Navya-Ny¯aya-vocabulary), if ⊢CTT+NN ψ then ⊢CTT ψ.
Proof sketch. The Navya-Ny¯aya-encoding introduces no new axioms; every primitive is a Σ, Π,
Path, or HIT in CCHM. The encoding is a definitional extension.
14.3
Faithfulness
Theorem 14.3 (Faithfulness). Navya-Ny¯aya-derivability under the standard Navya-Ny¯aya
inferential rules iff CTT-inhabitation under the encoding: ⊢NN φ iff ⊢CTT ⌜φ⌝.
Proof sketch. Forward direction is Soundness (Theorem 14.1). Reverse: by induction on the
structure of CTT proofs of ⌜φ⌝, each rule application is mirrored by an Navya-Ny¯aya inferential
move (paramarśa-construction, abh¯ava-introduction, etc.). The crucial step is that the NoUp¯adhi
component is constructive: any CTT proof of Vyapti H S exhibits a paramarśa constructible in
Navya-Ny¯aya.
14.4
Distinction-preservation
Theorem 14.4 (Distinction-preservation). Every distinction the tradition treats as logically
substantive maps to a non-trivial type-inequivalence in the encoding. For distinct entities a, b in
the Navya-Ny¯aya ontology with a ̸≡NN b, the encoded ⌜a⌝, ⌜b⌝satisfy ¬Path Type ⌜a⌝⌜b⌝.
Proof. By construction.
The categorial-universe stratification (§4.2) blocks cross-category
identification. The avacchedaka-Π-binding (§6.1) blocks coextension-collapse. The abh¯ava-
HIT (§7.2) blocks pratiyogin-collapse. The anugama-Σ (§4.4) blocks universal-coextension-
collapse.
14.5
Decidability
Theorem 14.5 (Decidability of the encoded fragment). Type-checking of Navya-Ny¯aya-encodings
in our system is decidable, modulo the decidability of the host CTT.
Proof. Cubical Agda (the practical realization of CCHM) has decidable type-checking. The
encoding is a fragment of CCHM (Conservativity).

Comparison with Prior Formalizations
15.1
Matilal’s first-order reconstruction
Matilal’s FOL is a translation of Navya-Ny¯aya’s content but not its structure. Avacchedaka
becomes a subscript, abh¯ava becomes ¬, parampar¯a becomes a chain. What FOL captures:
surface inferential validity of single-step anum¯ana; basic Boolean-combinatorial structure. What
FOL loses: dependent delimitation; positive negation; iterated relational structure; intensional
identity.
15.2
Ganeri’s higher-order reconstruction
HOL adds: predicate-of-predicates; intensional treatments of universals (via possible-worlds or
hyperintensional semantics). HOL still loses: dependency proper; computational identity; De
Morgan involution.
15.3
Bhattacharyya’s MLTT direction
The closest prior approach. MLTT adds: dependency, Σ/Π discipline, universe-stratification.
MLTT loses: path-equality; computational univalence; HITs; De Morgan involution. Bhat-
tacharyya remarks [7, §iii] that the treatment of identity is the weakest point.
15.4
Apoha and other Indic logics for contrast
Buddhist apoha theory (Dign¯aga, Dharmak¯ırti) treats meaning negatively: a word denotes
by exclusion of contraries. The structure is profoundly different from Navya-Ny¯aya’s positive-
negation: an apoha-encoding would parametrize meanings by their exclusion-classes rather than
by the positive pratiyogin-structure of abh¯ava. Sketch in §17.

Philosophical Implications
17.1
What CTT formalization reveals about Navya-Ny¯aya’s metatheory
Carrying out the formalization makes visible certain commitments Navya-Ny¯aya holds tacitly.
Navya-Ny¯aya’s relations must be path-shaped (not predicative) because only paths support the
simultaneous demands of mode-distinction, iteration, and substitutivity. Tacit in the tradition;
explicit in the formalization.
A second commitment: Navya-Ny¯aya’s identity must be univalent — equivalence-induces-
equality with the equation computing — because t¯ad¯atmya’s working role in derivations requires
substitutivity that runs to completion.
17.2
The neti-neti method as foundational
The neti-neti elimination procedure is, formally, the same shape as the type-theoretic constraint-
satisfaction procedure. The convergence is not coincidental: Navya-Ny¯aya’s metatheoretic
23


## Page 24


discipline, when made formal, is a constraint-satisfaction procedure, and CCHM is the constraint
type’s unique inhabitant.
17.3
Indiscernibility-of-indiscernibles in two traditions
Leibniz’s principle of identity-of-indiscernibles is, in the analytic tradition, an axiom. In Navya-
Ny¯aya it is a theorem (about t¯ad¯atmya) and follows from the structure of jñ¯ana. In CTT it is,
similarly, a consequence of univalence. The three traditions converge.
17.4
Computational univalence as a 14th-c. desideratum, retrospectively
A speculative claim: the computational univalence of CCHM is what Navya-Ny¯aya’s working
metatheory needed, and the absence of which forced certain contortions in the tradition’s discursive
treatment of identity. The 14th-century writers had no tools to formulate the requirement; the
21st-century type theory has, almost incidentally, supplied the tool.
17.5
De Morgan necessity
The argument that Navya-Ny¯aya’s abh¯ava-of-abh¯ava doctrine selects CCHM over Cartesian
cubical is, to our knowledge, the first principled argument from a non-mathematical tradition
for choosing between cubical foundations. The argument’s force is conditional on accepting
Navya-Ny¯aya’s involutive-negation doctrine; readers who reject the doctrine are not bound by
the conclusion.
But the argument’s existence is significant. Cubical type theory’s foundational choices
are usually adjudicated on internal mathematical grounds. Here a 14th-century philosophical
tradition adjudicates one of those choices on its own grounds — and the verdict is independent
of internal mathematical considerations. CCHM is selected by Navya-Ny¯aya’s commitments; it
is also selected by mathematical considerations; the agreement is a robust convergence.

Conclusion
We have argued that CCHM De Morgan cubical type theory closes the autoformalization gap
that previous attempts could not close. The argument runs along several converging lines: the
structural fit between Navya-Ny¯aya’s relational vocabulary and CTT’s path-typing; the recovery
of dependent delimitation, typed absence, and intensional identity; the metatheoretic results of
§14; and the philosophical convergence of §17.
The result is of interest beyond the narrow project of Navya-Ny¯aya formalization.
It
illustrates a more general phenomenon: that pre-modern philosophical traditions developed
expressive demands that twentieth-century logic struggled to meet, and that twenty-first-century
type theory — developed for entirely different reasons — has incidentally supplied the needed
structure. Where this convergence holds, the tradition’s formalization is not a forcing but a
natural fit.
The deeper question — whether CCHM is the foundational system for relational logic
generally, or merely one fit among possible alternatives — is open. We have not argued for the
strong claim. What we have shown is that for one fourteenth-century tradition with documented
metatheoretic commitments, CCHM is uniquely faithful. Whether other traditions force the
same answer or different ones is a project we leave to future work.

C.1
Proof of Theorem 7.1 (Involution of abh¯ava)
We unfold the HIT Abhava P L s and consider the doubled construction Abhava (Abhava P L
s) L s. The involution constructor of the inner HIT supplies, by direct pattern-matching, an
inhabitant of P. The De Morgan involution 1 −(1 −r) = r on I ensures that the path-component
of the doubled construction reduces, definitionally, to the identity path on P. Computational
univalence (§3.5) carries this to a definitional equality on the type itself.
C.2
Proof of Theorem 8.1 (Keval¯anvayi-irreducibility)
Suppose, for contradiction, an inhabitant f : KevalanvayiVyapti H S →AnvayaVyatirekiVyapti H S.
Apply f to a keval¯anvayi-witness kw : KevalanvayiVyapti H S. By definition (§8.4), kw.snd :
¬ Σ x (¬ S x). The result f kw : AnvayaVyatirekiVyapti H S contains, at its third Σ-component,
neg : Σ y (¬H y × ¬S y), which by projection yields Σ y (¬S y). Apply kw.snd to this projection
to derive ⊥.
C.3
Proof of Theorem 9.1 (Coextension-without-identity)
Two universals P, Q : Locus →Type with (x : Locus) →P x ≃Q x give us, by funext,
Path (Locus →Type) P Q. But P, Q are not bare functions in our encoding: per §4.4, they are
Σ-typed with anugama-witnesses. Equality of Σ-pairs requires equality of both components.

The function-component is established; the anugama-component is independently typed. Two
distinct anugamas — distinct unifying properties — yield distinct Σ-pairs even when the
function-components coextend. Hence coextension does not entail Σ-equality.
C.4
Proof of Theorem 10.1 (No-h-set-collapse)
Suppose UIP: for any A and any p, q : Path A x y, Path (Path A x y) p q. A parampar¯a-relation
of depth 2 is a path between paths: R12 : Path Type (Path A x y) (Path B u v). Under UIP, all
paths between fixed endpoints are themselves path-equal, and R12 becomes (effectively) a unique
witness modulo the Path-Path identification.
Raghun¯atha’s textually-attested distinctions
between alternative depth-2 mediations [28, §iii] thus collapse.
C.5
Proof of Theorem 14.3 (Faithfulness, sketch)
Forward direction: Soundness (Theorem 14.1). Reverse: induction on the structure of CTT
proofs. Each path-introduction corresponds to a sambandha-construction in Navya-Ny¯aya; each
Π-application to an avacchedaka-binding; each HIT-elimination to an abh¯ava-introduction. The
crucial step is constructive paramarśa: a CTT proof of Vyapti H S exhibits a function and a
NoUp¯adhi-witness, both of which translate to Navya-Ny¯aya inferential moves.
