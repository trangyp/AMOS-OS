Constant time testability of first-order logic with
modulo counting on finitary graphs
Isolde Adler∗
Jenny Stimpson†
Abstract
This paper studies algorithmic meta theorems for property testing
with constant running time in the bounded degree model. In (Adler,
Harwath 2018) it was shown that on graph classes Cw
d consisting of
all graphs with both degree at most d and treewidth at most w, ev-
ery problem expressible in monadic second-order logic with counting
(CMSO) is testable with polylogarithmic running time (where d, w ∈N
are fixed). It was left open whether this can be improved to constant
running time.
In this paper we give a positive answer for testing CMSO on classes
Cc
d, where d bounds the degree and c bounds the component size. Our
main result shows constant time testability of first-order logic with
modulo counting (FOMOD) on Cc
d. For our proof we tailor Hanf normal
form of FOMOD to our setting, and we exhibit a number-theoretic
‘patchability’ condition that allows to infer global information on the
input graph from a local sample of constant size.
We believe that
our ‘patchability’ might be of independent interest.
The step from
FOMOD to CMSO then follows from a result by (Eickmeyer, Elberfeld,
Harwath, 2017) on the expressive power of order invariant monadic
second-order logic on classes of bounded treedepth.
Keywords: Property testing, constant time algorithms, bounded
degree model, algorithmic meta-theorems, first-order logic with modulo
counting, monadic second-order logic.

Introduction
The past decades have seen a tremendous increase in the volume of stored
data, posing a huge challenge to computational power. Moreover, the data
is often stored remotely, making access costly. In this context, even just
reading the whole input once may be infeasible. Property Testing addresses
this by the design of algorithms that derive global information on the struc-
ture of the input by only exploring a small number of local parts of it. These
algorithms are randomised and allow for a small error. A property testing
algorithm is required to distinguish inputs that satisfy a given property from
inputs that are ε-far from satisfying the property with high probability cor-
rectly. Intuitively, an input is called ε-far from satisfying a property if more
than an ε-fraction of the input needs to be modified in order to make it
satisfy the property. The exact definition of the distance measure depends
on the model.
Our work is based on the bounded degree graph model of property testing
introduced by Goldreich and Ron [11]. This model considers graphs whose
degree (i. e. the number of edges incident to any vertex) is bounded from
above by a constant d ∈N. Formally, a property P is a class of graphs
that is closed under isomorphism. Let ε ∈(0, 1). Two d-bounded degree
graphs G and H, both on n vertices, are called ε-close, if they can be made
isomorphic by modifying (adding or deleting) at most εdn edges. If G and
H are not ε-close they are called ε-far.
For a property P of d-bounded
degree graphs, a graph G is ε-close to P, if G is ε-close to some member of
P. Otherwise G is ε-far from P. The algorithms have oracle access to an
input graph G of maximum degree d. Here we assume that the vertex set of
G is {1, 2, . . . , n}. More precisely, an algorithm gets the number n as input,
and it can access G via neighbour queries to an oracle, i. e. for any specified
vertex v and index i ∈{1, 2, . . . , d}, the oracle returns the i-th neighbour of
v if it exists, and a special symbol ⊥otherwise, in constant time.
A property P is testable with query complexity q(n) in the bounded degree
model, if for every ε ∈(0, 1) and every input size n there is a probabilistic
algorithm Aε,n (called an ε-tester), that accepts G with probability at least
2/3, if G ∈P, and rejects G with probability at least 2/3 if G is ε-far
from P, while using at most q(n) oracle queries. If q is independent of n,
then P is constant-query testable.
(Here q may depend on ε, d and P.)
Until now, despite some effort, a characterisation of the properties that are
constant-query testable in the bounded degree model is still elusive.
Towards this goal, Newman and Sohler [15] proved a general result, show-
ing that every property of planar graphs (and, more generally, of hyperfinite

graphs) is constant-query testable. This theorem is a meta-theorem in the
sense that it treats many properties simultaneously.
In this paper we are interested in the running time, which is measured
as usual in terms of n. For many individual properties is it easy to see that
the query complexity of a tester dominates its running time. However, while
a bound on the running time is also a bound on the query complexity, the
converse is not true in general. Indeed, using the Newman-Sohler theorem
it is not hard to see that there are undecidable properties that are constant
query testable, as is noted in [2]; the idea is to let the number of vertices of
edgeless graphs represent the Goedel numbering of Turing machines, and to
let the property be the class of graphs representing a Turing machine that
halts on empty input. Since this property is a class of edgeless graphs it is
hyperfinite and thus constant-query testable.
In practice, sublinear running time is highly desirable. This motivates our
aim of providing meta-theorems for property testing with constant running
time. Towards this we use the framework of logic, which allows to treat
many properties simultaneously. First-order logic on graphs (FO) speaks
about the edge relation and allows quantification over vertices as usual.
The logic FOMOD extends FO by additional modulo counting quantifiers.
Monadic second-order logic (MSO) extends FO by quantification over sub-
sets of vertices, and CMSO extends MSO by modulo counting quantifiers.
The following result was shown in [2]. (Indeed, it was shown in the slightly
more general setting of relational structures.)
Theorem 1 ([2]). Let d, w ∈N. On classes Cw
d containing all graphs with
both degree at most d and treewidth at most w every problem expressible in
CMSO is testable with polylogarithmic running time.
In [2] it was stated as a open problem whether this can be improved to con-
stant running time.1 In this paper we approach this question by considering
finitary graph classes (i. e. graph classes with a uniform upper bound on the
component size).
Results.
By the classical Feferman-Vaught Theorem [10] it is known that
satisfaction of a first-order formula only depends on the formulas that are
satisfied by the connected components. Our contribution goes further by
showing constant time testability.

It is known that all monotone, hyperfinite graph properties are testable
with constant running time [6], as well as hereditary, hyperfinite proper-
ties [7] (this follows from the proofs). Here monotone means closed under
taking subgraphs and hereditary means closed under taking induced sub-
graphs.
It is easy to see that there are properties definable in FO that
are neither hereditary nor monotone. In fact our motivating example (cf.
Example 3) is such a property.
Techniques.
Our proof techniques rely on a version of Hanf normal form
for FOMOD formulas, which is based on a result by Nurmonen [16] and
was further investigated in [12]. We carefully tailor it to our classes Cc
d in
such a way that it can be translated into speaking about isomorphism types
of connected components. This allows us to reduce testing an FOMOD-
property φ to testing for satisfaction of a k-capped component histogram
vector with modulo counting. Such a histogram vector v is indexed by the
isomorphism types of connected components of size at most c. For each such
component type t the entry in v[t] is either an integer between 0 and k −1
(rare types), or it is of the form ‘≥k and congruent i mod m’ (frequent
types). Here k, i, m ∈N are natural numbers with i < m, that only depend
on φ. We say that a graph G ∈C satisfies v if for each isomorphism type t,

Further related work.
In [1] a slight relaxation of the bounded degree
model was introduced, where the ‘closeness’ measure of graphs allows a small
fraction of the vertices to be removed or added, in addition to the usual edge
modifications. For this model, a theorem similar to Theorem 1 was proven,
however, with constant running time. This suggests that the intricacy arises
when the number of vertices cannot be modified. This is also the case in our
setting. In particular, our main algorithm (cf. Theorem 16) solves the issue
of understanding from local information only, whether the property can be
realised on the given number n of input vertices.
In [3, 4], testability of FO on general bounded degree graphs was studied
and it was shown that the fragment Σ2 (i. e. the fragment of FO of formulas
in the ∃∗∀∗-prefix class) is testable with constant query complexity in the
bounded-degree model, whereas there is a property in Π2 (the ∀∗∃∗-prefix
class) that is not testable with constant query complexity in this model. In
the dense graph model, a similar picture is known [5]. However, the proof
techniques differ fundamentally.

Preliminaries
We will use N to denote the natural numbers including 0 and Z+ := N\{0}.
We let [n] := {1, . . . , n}.
Graphs
A graph is a pair G = (V (G), E(G)) where V (G) is a finite sets
and E(G) ⊆{e ∈P(V (G)) | |e| = 2}. Elements of V (G) are called vertices
(of G) and elements of E(G) are called edges (of G). An edge {x, y} may also
be denoted xy. If xy ∈V (G) we say that x and y are adjacent or that x is a
neighbour of y. A graph H is a subgraph of a graph G if V (H) ⊆V (G) and
E(H) ⊆E(G). For a graph G and S ⊆V (G) we let G[S] := (S, {{v, w} ∈
E(G) : v, w ∈S}) be the subgraph of G induced by S.
A subgraph H
of G is an induced subgraph of G if H = G[S] for some set S ⊆V (G).
For vertices v, w ∈V (G) we say that v is a neighbour of w if {v, w} ∈
E(G).
A path of length n from p0 to pn is a graph P = (V (P), E(P))
with V (P) = {p0, p1, . . . , pn} and E(P) = {p0p1, p1p2, . . . , pn−1pn} such
that p0 ̸= pn. A subset X ⊆V (G) is called connected if for every pair of
distinct vertices x, y ∈X, G contains a path from x to y as a subgraph.
The distance distG(x, y) between vertices x and y in G is the length of the
shortest path from x to y. For a graph G, a vertex v ∈V (G) and r ∈N we let
NG(v, r) := {u ∈V (G) | distG(v, u) ≤r}. A subset X ⊆V (G) is a connected
component of G if it is connected and no set Y satisfying X ⊊Y ⊆V (G) is
connected. A graph G is connected if V (G) is connected. An isomorphism
from graph G to graph H is a bijective function f : V (G) →V (H) such that
{u, v} ∈E(G) if and only if {f(u), f(v)} ∈E(H). Two graphs are called
isomorphic if there exists an isomorphism between them. A graph property
is a class of graphs which is closed under isomorphism. We say a graph G
has property P if G ∈P. If P is a property and C is a class of graphs such
that P ⊆C, we say that P is a property on C. A monotone property is a
property closed under taking subgraphs. A hereditary property is a property
closed under taking induced subgraphs.
The degree of v ∈V (G) is the number of vertices adjacent to v in G.
The degree of a graph G is the maximum degree of a vertex in V (G). A
class of graphs has d-bounded degree if every graph in the class has degree
at most d. The class of all d-bounded degree graphs is denoted Cd.
A rooted graph is a pair (G, vG) where G is a graph vG ∈V (G) is the root.

Monadic second-order logic (MSO) extends FO by also additionally al-

logic with counting (CMSO) extends MSO by modular counting quantifiers

Conclusion
We showed that on graph classes containg all graphs that have both a fixed
upper bound on the degree and on the component size, every FOMOD-
property is testable with constant running time (Theorem 16). Since CMSO
and FOMOD have the same expressive power on these classes, it follows
that every CMSO-property is testable on these graph classes as well (Corol-
lary 17). Indeed, it is not hard to see that this theorem can be generalised
to relational structures of bounded degree, in the model introduced in [2],
and we get the following.
Theorem 18. Let σ be a finite relational signature and let d, c ∈N. Let Cc
d
be the class consisting of all σ-structures that have both degree at most d and
connected component size at most c. For every sentence φ of FOMOD[σ]
the property Pφ ∩Cc
d is testable with constant running time on C.
We see this as a step towards answering an open question from [2],
which asks if CMSO is constant time testable on classes that contain all
relational structures which have both degree at most d and treewidth at
most t. However, the question remains open.

Proof of corollary 17
Let c, d ∈N.
For every sentence φ of CMSO, the property Pφ ∩Cc
d is
uniformly testable in constant time on Cc
d.
We will make use of the following theorem of Eickmeyer, Elberfeld and
Harwath [8].
Theorem 19 ([8]). Order-invariant MSO and FOMOD have the same ex-
pressive power on graphs of bounded tree-depth.
As it is also known that order-invariant MSO has at least the expressive
power of CMSO on any graph class [8]. It follows that on graphs of bounded
tree-depth, FOMOD has at least the expressive power of CMSO. Further
each sentence of FOMOD is also a sentence of CMSO as CMSO extends
FOMOD. Thus on classes of bounded tree depth CMSO and FOMOD have
the same expressive power.
Corollary 17 is now an immediate consequence of Theorem 16 and the
fact that finitary graph classes have bounded tree-depth.
