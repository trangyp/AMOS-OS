Abstract
The simulation of extreme Mach astrophysical flows is traditionally viewed through the lens of deterministic positivity-
preserving schemes. However, due to phenomena such as Kelvin–Helmholtz instabilities and shock anomalies, the
multi-dimensional Euler equations admit a plethora of non-unique entropy solutions in turbulent regimes. For the first
time, we computationally explore the weak-strong uniqueness of a Mach 2000 jet by defining the statistical solution
as the pushforward of a probability measure through a vectorial lattice Boltzmann method (VLBM) operator. Utiliz-
ing highly optimized CUDA kernels, we compute an ensemble of 1000 Monte Carlo samples across a sequence of
unprecedentedly refined spatial grids of up to 3.2 million cells, and subsequently post-process the empirical measures
via memory-mapped CPU streaming. We contrast the strong sample-wise L1 error divergence with the convergence
of the probability measure in the 1-point Wasserstein distance via empirical Cauchy rates. Our mathematical re-
sults demonstrate that while individual flow realizations physically diverge due to chaotic shear-layer instabilities, the
macroscopic statistical solution converges to a well-defined limit measure at a rate of 0.5. Conclusively, we provide
the first numerical verification of statistical solution stability in the extreme compressible regime.
Keywords: statistical solutions, Wasserstein distance, weak-strong uniqueness, vectorial LBM, high-Mach flows,
GPU computing
1. Introduction
Astrophysical jets at Mach numbers exceeding 103 represent a fundamental challenge in computational fluid
dynamics. The Mach 2000 parameter regime serves two critical purposes. First, it is directly physically representative
of protostellar jets (Herbig–Haro objects) propagating into ultra-cold (T ∼10 K) molecular clouds, where ambient
sound speeds drop below 0.3 km/s, yielding classical Mach numbers in excess of 103 [11]. Second, it serves as a
rigorous, canonical mathematical stress-test for the high-energy limits of the classical Euler equations. By pushing
the deterministic breakdown to the extreme compressible limit, we provide a mathematical proxy for the shock-
dominated, hyper-turbulent topologies found in more complex relativistic flows, such as active galactic nuclei and
gamma-ray bursts [12, 10].
While traditional numerical research utilizes the Mach 2000 jet primarily as a short-duration torture test to val-
idate positivity-preserving limiters [13, 14], the underlying mathematical question of uniqueness in the long-time,
fully turbulent regime remains fraught. State-of-the-art high-order frameworks, such as subcell-limited discontinuous
Galerkin methods, typically restrict this benchmark to early transient phases (e.g., t ≤0.0015) [16, 15]. By extending
the temporal horizon to t = 0.003 on unprecedented, highly resolved spatial grids up to 4000 × 800, we transition this
problem from a deterministic stability test into an extensive study of statistical hyper-compressible turbulence. In this
chaotic regime, foundational convex integration results [1] demonstrate that the multi-dimensional Euler equations
may admit infinitely many wild entropy solutions for the exact same initial data. To recover a well-posed theoret-
ical framework, DiPerna introduced measure-valued solutions [2], which were subsequently refined into statistical
solutions [3]. These shift the focus from individual deterministic trajectories to a time-evolving probability measure.

3. Weak-strong uniqueness and convergence metrics

4. Numerical results
4.1. Implementation
The methodology from [6] (see Section 2.1) has been ported to GPU for the present work. The Monte Carlo
ensemble was implemented using Python and CuPy for CUDA-accelerated array processing. To isolate the spatial
convergence of the probability measure and separate grid discretization error from statistical sampling noise, the
Monte Carlo ensemble is computed across a sequence of progressively refined grids. The spatial resolution scales
incrementally, doubling from a coarse baseline of 500×100 cells to a highly resolved grid of 4000×800 discrete lattice

5. Conclusions
We have computed the statistical solution of an extreme Mach 2000 astrophysical jet up to highly resolved do-
mains of 4000 × 800. By defining the solution as the pushforward of a probability measure via the VLBM operator,
we circumvented the traditional instability of deterministic schemes in near-vacuum regimes. The observed Cauchy
convergence in the 1-point Wasserstein metric W1, contrasted against the continuous breakdown of strong L1 con-
vergence, provides rigorous empirical evidence for the statistical stability of chaotic high-Mach flows. This offers a
highly parallelizable, mathematically robust path for uncertainty quantification in extreme compressible astrophysics,
confirming that while pointwise deterministic uniqueness is lost, statistical uniqueness is preserved.
In observational astrophysics, the visible structures of extreme jets (such as emission knots and bow shocks)
are directly governed by the turbulent dissipation of kinetic energy at internal shock fronts [9, 10]. Our results
demonstrate that while the exact deterministic location of these shocks is gradually lost upon grid refinement, the
statistical footprint of the turbulence, and thus the macroscopic structure of the observable emission, is mathematically
recovered and unique under the Wasserstein metric.
