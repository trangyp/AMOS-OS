In this work, we review gravitational-wave generation in a two-scalar-field cosmo-
logical model. The framework relies on a two-field scenario in which warm inflation is
unified with the dark sector within a single theoretical description. It has recently been
shown that this setup leads to a gravitational-wave spectrum potentially detectable by
future experiments. Here we also demonstrate that the resulting spectrum is largely
independent of the ϕ field initial condition, thereby extending previous findings.
Keywords: Gravitational waves; Inflation; Dark matter; Dark energy
detection
PACS numbers: 04.30.-w, 95.36.+x, 95.35.+d, 98.80.Cq, 03.50.-z
1. Introduction
A new era in cosmology began with the first direct detection of gravitational waves.1
So far, gravitational-wave astronomy has primarily observed binary stellar-mass
black hole systems. In the near future, next-generation detectors are expected to
probe a wider range of phenomena.2
Among the most compelling targets is the generation of primordial gravitational
waves in the early stages of the Universe, which could provide valuable information
about inflation and the transition to the radiation-dominated era.3
Because of the lack of direct observational constraints on these epochs, sev-
eral theoretical models have been proposed. In this context, unification scenarios
are particularly interestinga, as they aim to describe the dark sector and inflation
within a single framework.8–11 A triple unification scheme can be realized in a two-
scalar-field cosmological model that also considers a warm inflation scenario.12–14
In Refs. 15,16, the generation of gravitational waves in this model was extensively
studied. In this contribution, we review those results, which predict spectra poten-
tially detectable by next-generation detectors, and we further investigate the role
of the initial conditions of the dark energy field.

4. Conslusions and perspectives
In this contribution, we started by briefly reviewing the recent works in Refs. 15,16.
Specifically, we explored a triple-unification cosmological model consisting of a two-
scalar-field scenario within a warm inflation epoch. This framework is used to an-
alyze the generation of primordial gravitational waves by means of the continuous
Bogoliubov coefficients method. Then, we extended the results previously obtained
by showing that the resulting spectrum is largely independent of the initial con-
dition of the dark energy field ϕ. Moreover, the spectra appear to be within the
sensitivity range of future detectors, thereby reinforcing the role of gravitational-
wave astronomy as a crucial tool for investigating the very early Universe. Within
this perspective, further studies on these phenomena are needed.
