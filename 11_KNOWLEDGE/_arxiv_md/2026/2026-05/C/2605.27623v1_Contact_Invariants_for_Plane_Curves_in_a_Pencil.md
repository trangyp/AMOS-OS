CONTACT INVARIANTS FOR PLANE CURVES IN A PENCIL
CIRO CILIBERTO, RICK MIRANDA, AND JOAQUIM RO´E
ABSTRACT. Let P be a general pencil of curves of degree d in the projective plane. In
this paper we review the computation of the number of curves in P that have a hyperflex
line, a flex bitangent line and a tritangent line. Then we focus on the curves in the dual
plane described by the flex tangents and the bitangents of the curves of P and the curves in
the original plane described by the flexes and the points of bitangencies of the curves in P.
Some of these curves have been studied already: we mainly focus here on the ones that still
have not been treated systematically, and we compute their degree, genus, and singularities.

INTRODUCTION
Consider the projective space |OP2(d)| (of dimension Nd = d(d + 3)/2) of all plane
curves of degree d, i.e., all homogeneous polynomials in [x : y : z] of degree d, up to
multiplication by a non–zero factor. The d = 1 case is the dual plane (P2)∗parametrizing
lines in the plane; it has dimension two.
Given a line L, a plane curve C, and a point p ∈C, we say that L has contact to order
at least m with C at p if multp(L, C) ⩾m. The m = 1 case is just that L passes through
p; if p is a smooth point of C, the m = 2 case is that L is the tangent line to C at p.

2. A DEGENERATION RECURSION METHOD
Let P be a general pencil of curves of degree d. In this section we introduce a degen-
eration method to compute the degree of M(2,2),d,P, i.e., the curve of bitangent lines to
curves in P.
2.1. The degeneration. Consider a general pencil P′ of curves of degree d −2, and take
a general fixed conic Γ; we take ¯P = Γ + P′ as a degeneration of a general pencil P of
curves of degree d, and use this to generate a recursion method to compute the degree of
M(2,2),d,P.
The bitangents to curves in ¯P are of the following types:
(i) the bitangents to curves in P′;
(ii) the tangents to curves in P′ that are also tangent to Γ;
(iii) given a curve Γ + C with C ∈P′, the lines that pass through a point p ∈Γ ∩C and
are also tangent to C somewhere;
(iv) given a curve Γ + C with C ∈P′, the lines that pass through a pair of points p, q ∈
Γ ∩C.
2.2. Degeneration multiplicities. In the list above, some of the bitangents have to be
counted with multiplicity; namely, each curve of type (iii) (resp. type (iv)) is the limit of
ν > 1 (resp. µ > 1) bitangents to curves in general pencils P. Our goal in this subsection
is to compute ν and µ.
Proposition 2.1. One has ν = 2 and µ = 4.
Proof. Since the computation is local, we may determine ν by computing the contribution
(to the number of bitangents for a curve of degree d) of a line that contains a node of that
curve, and is tangent elsewhere.
We may assume that the curve is a general quartic with a node. This curve has 16 proper
bitangents, i.e., bitangent lines whose contact points are smooth points of the curve. It has
