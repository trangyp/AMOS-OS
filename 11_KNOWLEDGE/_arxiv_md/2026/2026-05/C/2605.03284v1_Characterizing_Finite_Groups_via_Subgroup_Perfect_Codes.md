Abstract. A perfect code in a graph Γ “ pV, Eq is a subset C of V such that no two
vertices in C are adjacent and every vertex in V zC is adjacent to exactly one vertex in
C. A subgroup H of a group G is called a subgroup perfect code of G if it is a perfect
code in some Cayley graph of G. In this paper, we study the set ∆pGq of conjugacy
classes of nontrivial subgroup perfect codes of G, with a focus on its relation to |πpGq|,
the number of prime divisors of |G|. We prove that |∆pGq| ě |πpGq| with only three
exceptional families, which leads to the natural question: when is this bound attained or
nearly attained? We completely classify finite groups G satisfying |∆pGq| “ |πpGq| and
|∆pGq| “ |πpGq| ` 1, and we further characterize all insolvable groups with |∆pGq| ď 6.
Our approach is based on the classification of primitive groups of odd degree, as well as
the classification of primitive groups of square-free degree.
Keywords. Cayley graphs; Subgroup perfect codes; Primitive groups; Conjugacy classes
1. Introduction
In this paper, all groups and graphs are finite, and all graphs are simple and undirected.
Let Γ be a graph with vertex set V . A perfect code in a graph Γ is an independent subset
C of V such that every vertex in V zC is adjacent to exactly one vertex in C. The study
of perfect codes in graphs originated in coding theory [2].
Classical examples such as
Hamming and Golay codes [28] occur in Hamming graphs, which are Cayley graphs of
elementary abelian groups. This motivates a natural generalization to perfect codes in
arbitrary Cayley graphs. Let G be a group and S Ď Gzt1u an inverse-closed subset. The
Cayley graph CaypG, Sq has vertex set G, and two distinct vertices x and y are adjacent
if and only if yx´1 P S.
Perfect codes in Cayley graphs have been studied extensively (see, for example, [9,10,
33]). A particularly active line of research asks when a given subgroup of a group can
be realized as a perfect code in some Cayley graph of the group.
The formal notion
of a subgroup perfect code was introduced by Huang et al. [16]: a subgroup H of G
is called a subgroup perfect code if H is a perfect code in some Cayley graph of G. In
that work, the authors gave a necessary and sufficient condition for a normal subgroup to
be a perfect code. Their result motivated efforts to characterize subgroup perfect codes,
leading to classifications in families such as dihedral groups [16] and generalized quaternion
groups [27], as well as studies involving maximal subgroups of finite simple groups [32].

Subgroup perfect codes in Cayley graph are important not only in coding theory and
graph theory, but also in group theory. A factorization of a group G into two factors is a
pair pA, Bq of subsets of G such that every element of G can be uniquely written as ab with
a P A and b P B, so that G “ AB. If 1 P A X B, we call it a tiling of G. As shown in [16],
if pA, Bq is a tiling with A´1 “ A, then B is a perfect code in CaypG, Azt1uq; conversely,
any perfect code in a Cayley graph induces a tilling. Now let B be a subgroup perfect
code of G. There exists an inverse-closed subset A of G such that pA, Bq is a factorization
of G. In particular, if A is also a subgroup, the factorization G “ AB is called an exact
factorization. Exact factorizations of permutation groups play a special role, owing to a
classical observation: for a transitive group action on a set Ω, a subgroup A acts regularly
on Ωif and only if G admits an exact factorization G “ AGα for some point stabilizer
Gα. For recent developments on exact factorizations, see [11,12,24,29].
A subgroup perfect code H of a group G is called nontrivial if 1 ă H ă G. Recently, an
interesting problem is to explore the connections between the number of subgroup perfect
codes in a group relates to its structure. For instance, Ma et al. [27] characterized groups
in which every subgroup is a subgroup perfect code, showing that such groups contain no
element of order 4. Conversely, Chen et al. [7] proved that groups possessing no nontrivial
subgroup perfect codes are precisely cyclic groups of odd prime order, cyclic 2-groups,
or generalized quaternion 2-groups. Furthermore, Khaefi et al. [22] established that any
group with exactly two nontrivial subgroup perfect codes must be abelian.
Since the property of being a subgroup perfect code is preserved under conjugation, a
natural and refined measure is to count these subgroups up to conjugacy. This leads to
an interesting question in group theory:
Problem 1.1. How is the global structure of a group G determined by the number of
conjugacy classes of its nontrivial subgroup perfect codes?
In this paper, we always let πpGq denote the set of all prime divisors of the order of a
group G and ∆pGq the set of all conjugacy classes of nontrivial subgroup perfect codes of
G.
We now introduce a new perspective rooted in the Sylow subgroups of G. A fundamental
result by Zhang [32] shows that every Sylow subgroup of G is its a subgroup perfect code.
If G is not a p-group, its Sylow subgroups for distinct primes are non-conjugate and
nontrivial. This yields at least |πpGq| distinct conjugacy classes of such subgroups. What

2.2. Standard group-theoretic tools. We recall standard results on Hall subgroups in

Definition 2.9 ( [17, p. 659, Definition 1.2]). Let π be a set of primes and π1 the set of

(2) A subgroup H of G is called a π-Hall subgroup if H is a π-group and |G : H| is

subgroups for every subset π of πpGq. Any two π-Hall subgroups of G are conjugate in G,
and every π-subgroup of G is contained in a π-Hall subgroup of G.
