Elements of Ωcan be understood as distances or weights or truth-values.
Canonical extensions arise as certain MacNeille completions. MacNeille completions, in turn, are
given by the fixed points of the MacNeille-Birkhoff-Isbell-Lawvere adjunction

This “blacktriangle calculus” provides a useful bridge between quantale-enriched category theory
on the one hand and lattice theory, algebraic logic and proof theory on the other hand.
The reason for paying attention to the language in which we formulate our results is not only to
make our work accessible to category theorists and to lattice theorists: In our own ongoing work
on applications to logic, discussed below, we need to have easy access to general category theoretic
results and to an algebraic language in the style of lattice theory and logic. This approach also
sheds new light on known results in lattice theory.
In the remainder of this section, we discuss applications and related work.
1.1
Applications to Logic
The present article is situated in a line of research which develops and studies logical formalisms
that describe and reason about categorization systems (with applications to decision theory and AI)
arising across disciplines. The main methodological tool of this endeavor is based on Wille’s formal
concept analysis (FCA) [27]. In [20] the epistemic logic of categories and concepts was introduced,
based on a general framework of non-distributive logics developed in [17]. In [8, 39], formal concept
analysis was lifted to the fuzzy context, where relations that take value over commutative unital
quantales and their corresponding fuzzy Galois connection were studied. Recently, combining these
approaches, correspondence theory for fuzzy non-distributive modal logics is developed in [21]; in
[14] such logical frameworks have been used for developing unsupervised learning algorithms for
outlier detection that also provide explanations of their results. In this context, the present article
is a stepping stone for generalizing algorithmic correspondence [19, 17] results from the algebraic
to the quantale-enriched setting.
1.2
Related Work
While the specific motivation of our work are the applications to logic discussed above, we build on
insights and results from various areas of mathematics which we sketch out now.
Lattice Theory
In Birkhoff’s monograph Lattice Theory [13], under the heading Polarity, one
finds the definition of what we call the MacNeille completion of a binary relation I ∶X ↬A in the
special case Ω= 2, that is, where the quantale Ωis the familiar set 2 = {0 < 1} of truth-values. For
a modern introduction to lattice theory see Davey and Priestley [22].
Formal Context Analysis
As already shown by Birkhoff, relations I ∶X ↬A can be considered
(via their MacNeille completions) as representations of complete lattices.
The Formal Context
Analysis (FCA) of Ganter and Wille [27] takes this as a starting point for a theory of data-analysis
in which the ‘formal context’ or ‘context’ or ‘incidence relation’, I relates ‘objects’ in X with
‘attributes’ in A. While we will be interested in the applications of FCA in future work, most
relevant for our work here are theoretical investigations into generalizations of FCA to many-valued
(or fuzzy or quantitative) context analysis as in the work of Belohlavek [8] and Pavlovic [39].
Canonical Extensions
Canonical extensions of Boolean algebras were introduced by Jonsson
and Tarski [33, 32] in order to study relation algebras and, more generally, modal algebras (Boolean
algebras with operators). In particular, they pioneered the use of canonical extensions to prove
3


## Page 4


completeness of modal logics via representation theorems generalizing Stone duality for Boolean
algebras. This line of research was extended by Gehrke and Jonsson [29] to distributive lattices
and by Dunn, Gehrke and Palmigiano [23] to lattices and posets, that is, in our terms, to the case
Ω= 2.
Substructural Logics, Algebraic Logic
On the one hand, quantales can be seen as particular
residuated lattices, which constitute the algebraic semantics of substructural logics, see Galatos,
Jipsen, Kowalski and Ono [26]. Here, substructural refers to the omission of (some of) the rules
of exchange, weakening and contraction in the corresponding sequent calcului. On the other hand,
MacNeille completions of relations allow us to give syntactic representations of non-distributive
logics. Our work will allow us to generalize duality-based results in modal logic to non-distributive
substructural logics.
Category Theory - Lawvere Metric Spaces
Like so many others, we are inspired by Lawvere’s
seminal Metric spaces, generalized logic and closed categories [36]. Lawvere’s article suggests that it
should be possible to produce a uniform account of a considerable body of results on many-valued
logics parametrically in the quantale Ωof truth-values and here we continue previous work in this
line of research [12, 4, 6, 5]. Our running example of automata as categories enriched over a (non-
commutative) quantale in Section 2, due to Betti and collaborators [9, 10, 11], was also inspired by
[36]. Another indirect influence of [36] on our work is via Willerton’s [49] which gives a detailed
analysis of the MacNeille completion of Lawvere metric spaces. Hofmann and Stubbe [30] consider
the generalisation to partial metrics. The MacNeille completion of quantale-enriched categories
has been studied in Shen and Zhang [44], Garner [28], and Fujii [25].
The latter extends the
characterization by Banaschewski and Bruns [7] of the MacNeille completion as the injective hull
from the case of Ω= 2 to all quantales.
Category Theory - Isbell Conjugacy
Isbell [31] generalized the MacNeille-Birkhoff adjunction
from orders to categories and Lawvere [37] called it the Isbell conjugacy. Isbell also generalized the
MacNeille completion to categories (then often called the reflexive completion). Avery and Lein-
ster [3] investigate it in detail. Pavlovic and Hughes [40] and then Ferrer [24] give a generalization
of the MacNeille completion to categories based on tight limits. In a different direction, Kurz and
Rosicky [35] used the Isbell conjugacy to generalize the Jonsson-Tarski representation theorem for
Boolean algebras with operators to modal algebras that dualize coalgebras for set-functors.
Domain Theory
Lawvere metric spaces [36] are asymetric and can represent both order-theoretic
and metric structure. Wagner [47, 48] and Rutten and collaborators [42, 43, 15] used this obser-
vation to unify order-theoretic and metric domain theory. This work shows that quantale-enriched
categories provide a wide range of different models of computations.
Coalgebraic Modal Logic
Coalgebraic modal logic [38, 16] has been successful in generalizing
Abramsky’s Domain Theory in Logical Form [1] from particular type constructors to a rather general
theory parameterized by a functor on a suitable category such as sets or posets. While there has
been work on further parameterizing coalgebraic logic by a quantale [12, 4, 6, 5], it remains an open
question how far this program can be pushed.

1. The two-chain 2 = {0 ⊑1} is a commutative quantale in which multiplication

2. The Lawvere quantale [0,∞] is a subset of the extended real numbers [36]. It is ordered by ≥

2.5
Truth-Values vs Distances
As can be seen from the examples, the elements of the quantale can be interpreted as truth values
or as distances. For example, [0,∞] with 0 as top and ∞as bottom is naturally interpreted as
a quantale of distances. On the other hand, there is an isomorphism of quantales [0,∞] →[0,1]
defined by x ↦e−x mapping 0 to 1 and ∞to 0. While both quantales give rise to isomorphic
quantale enriched categories, the second quantale is more naturally interpreted as a quantale of
truth values.
In this example, in the truth-value interpretation we have X(a,b) ⋅X(b,c) ≤X(a,c) while in the
distance interpretation we have X(a,b) + X(b,c) ≥X(a,c), also known as the triangle inequality of
metric spaces. Similarly, ⊓is conjunction in the truth-value interpretation and supremum in the
distance interpretation.
In the following, for Ω-spaces X, we often find it convenient to speak of X(x,y) as the distance from
x to y without making assumptions on Ωor implying that we favour the distance interpretation
over the truth-value interpretation.

Duality
Dualising the order of the multiplication of Ω, X is an Ωo-space if it satisfies
X(y,z) ⋅X(x,y) ⊑X(x,z).
In addition, one can also dualise the order of Ω. This leads to 4 different ways in which Ωitself can
be turned into a quantale space A:
1. A(a,b) = a ⊳b
2. A(a,b) = a ⊲b
3. A(a,b) = b ⊳a
4. A(a,b) = b ⊲a
In cases 1 and 4 the order defined by a ≤b ⇔e ⊑A(a,b) agrees with the order of Ω, in cases 2
and 3 it is the dual. In cases 1 and 2, A is an Ω-space and, in cases 3 and 4, A is an Ωo-space. The
A of case 1 is the Ao of case 3 and the A of case 2 is the Ao of case 4. If Ωis commutative, case 1
equals case 4 and case 2 equals case 3.
Remark 2.5. X is an Ω-space iff Xo is an Ωo-space.

We call case 1 the history automaton of all languages because we think of a state L as recording
its history, starting from the initial state ϵ. The hom L ⊳M contains the words which extend
all words in L to a word in M. In particular, P(Σ∗)(ϵ,L) = ϵ ⊳L = L.
We call case 2 the prophecy automaton of all languages because a state L consists of all words
that lead from L to a final state, where we consider a state to be final if it contains ϵ. In
particular, P(Σ∗)(L,ϵ) = L ⊲ϵ = L and if ϵ ∈M then P(Σ∗)(L,M) ⊆L.

Valuable resources on the MacNeille completion of an Ω-enriched category are Stubbe [45], Gar-

We define the MacNeille completion for a relation rather than only for a category. This will allow us

Remark 4.14. The σ- and π-extension of functors will play a crucial role (to be pursued in future
work) in the application of canonical extenions to the logic of relational structures on quantale-

In these applications, there will be logical connectives stemming from the

Canonical extensions offer a modular and generic method of proving completeness of finitary modal
logics with respect to various relational semantics.
The method hinges on extending functora
f ∶A →B that preserve finite finite weighted (co)limits to maps on the canonical extensions
f σ,f π ∶Aδ →Bδ that preserve arbitrary (co)limits and, hence, have adjoints.
Indeed, on the syntactic side, modalities such as ◻and ◊can be seen as monotone operations that
preserve finite meets and joins respectively on the Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra of the logic. On the
semantic side, such modalities arise from relations on sets, and therefore preserve arbitrary meets
and joins and are right and left adjoints respectively. Then completeness of such modal logics is
shown via canonicity: ◻π and ◊σ do have adjoints and therefore can correspond to relations on the
dual space of the canonical extension.

5
Conclusion
We generalized the canonical extension construction from lattices and posets to quantale-enriched
categories and developed the basic theory of these canonical extensions. On the category theoretic
side this adds to a line of developement that started with Lawvere [36] and continued through, for
example, [45, 44, 49, 25]. On the logic side, our work is a stepping stone as part of the larger project
of developing the logical foundations of categorization theory [20, 18]. In particular, our result is
the theoretical groundwork needed for the development of the duality theory of quantale-enriched
categories with many-valued polarities and the introduction of logical systems with modal operations
and consequence relations weighted over a quantale, and the development of their proof theory and
relational semantics.
The work in the present article will allow the systematic development of
classes of such logical systems, and allow to develop tools from the theory of algorithmic canonicity
in the context of quantale enriched categories.
