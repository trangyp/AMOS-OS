Abstract
The study of the Ornstein–Zernike decay of subcritical two-point functions in equilibrium
statistical mechanics has a history going back over a century. Despite this, the crossover from
Ornstein–Zernike decay to critical power-law decay has received scant attention in the literature.
We prove a general theorem which, under appropriate hypotheses, identifies the asymptotic be-
haviour of the solution to an Ornstein–Zernike equation on Zd as that of the Green function for
Brownian motion with drift, multiplied by an anisotropic exponentially decaying factor. The theo-
rem applies to a wide class of random walks, to nearest-neighbour self-avoiding walk in dimensions
d ≥5, and to nearest-neighbour percolation in dimensions d ≥15. Wide-ranging consequences
follow, including details of the crossover from Ornstein–Zernike to critical decay on the scale of the
correlation length, and the fact that all finite-order correlation lengths are equivalent up to uni-
versal constants. The proof is based on a variational characterisation of the direction-dependent
rate of exponential decay and a major extension of Hara’s 2008 Gaussian Lemma to noncentred
kernels.

There is an extensive literature on OZ decay in the subcritical regime. In dimensions d ≥2, the
OZ decay (1.2) has been proved for self-avoiding walk [15, 16, 38, 39, 44], for percolation [11, 12], for
the Ising model [13], and for random cluster models [14, 17]. There is also an extensive literature
on the critical decay (1.4) for high-dimensional statistical-mechanical models. Most relevant for our
results are the fact that (1.4) is proved, using the lace expansion, for self-avoiding walk in dimensions
d ≥5 [29,34], and for percolation in dimensions d ≥11 [23,29,31], but the literature is much larger
(e.g., [9, 19, 24, 30, 42, 43, 55]). In this paper, we give new proofs of OZ decay for self-avoiding walk
in dimensions d ≥5 and percolation in dimensions d ≥15, for z ∈[zc −δ, zc) for some δ > 0. These
new proofs reveal, for the first time, the crossover present in (1.5) in these two contexts. We do not
attempt to apply our methods to the high-dimensional Ising or |φ|4 models, or to lattice trees and
lattice animals, but we expect that they should apply when combined with the lace expansions for
these models [9,32,55,56].
