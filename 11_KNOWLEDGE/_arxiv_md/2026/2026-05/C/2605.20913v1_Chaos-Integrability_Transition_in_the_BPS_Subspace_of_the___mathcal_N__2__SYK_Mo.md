Chaos-Integrability Transition in the BPS Subspace of the N = 2 SYK Model
Leon Miyahara1, ∗and Shono Shibuya1, †
1Department of Physics, Nagoya University, Nagoya, Aichi 464-8602, Japan
We study chaos-integrability transition purely within a BPS subspace of a specific supersym-
metric model that interpolates between the chaotic N = 2 SYK model and an integrable N = 2
“commuting” SYK model.
Using the framework of BPS chaos, we analyze the spectrum of an
operator projected onto the BPS subspace. We numerically find that its spectral statistics exhibit
random-matrix behavior near the SYK limit and smoothly transitions to Poisson statistics near the
integrable limit. Our results provide a direct example of a chaos-integrability crossover diagnosed
solely from BPS states.
1. Introduction.— Quantum chaos plays a central role
in understanding the quantum nature of black holes [1, 2].
The Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model [3–5] provides a
tractable setting for studying this connection, as its low-
energy dynamics is closely related to nearly-AdS2 gravity
through the Schwarzian effective theory [6, 7]. Various
deformations of SYK models have been discussed to in-
vestigate chaos-integrability transition [8–11], offering a
useful laboratory to explore the relation between chaos
and black hole dynamics.
Supersymmetric black holes provide a particularly
valuable setting for microscopic studies of black hole
physics; for instance, their protected entropies can often
be counted from the dual CFT. However, the BPS states
are all degenerate ground states, so the conventional di-
agnostics of quantum chaos based on spectral correlations
of the Hamiltonian cannot be directly applied.
A recent proposal, known as BPS chaos, addresses this
issue by considering the spectrum of a “simple” operator
O projected onto the BPS subspace [12–14],
OBPS = PBPSOPBPS .
(1)
Random-matrix statistics in the spectrum of OBPS are
then interpreted as a signature of quantum chaos within
the BPS subspace. Relatedly, the notion of fortuity clas-
sifies BPS states according to whether they can be contin-
ued to larger N [15]. The associated chaos-invasion pic-
ture suggests that the spectral properties of finite-energy
states may be inherited by fortuitous BPS states [14].
These developments motivate a natural question: Can
a chaos-integrability transition be detected solely in a
BPS subspace?
In this work, we answer this question affirmatively in
a deformed N = 2 supersymmetric SYK model. We con-
sider a supercharge that interpolates between the usual
chaotic N = 2 SYK supercharge [16] and an integrable
“commuting” supercharge. After confirming the chaos-
integrability transition in the conventional setting of a
non-BPS sector, we focus instead on a BPS subspace
and study the spectrum of a projected operator.
We
numerically find a clear crossover from random-matrix
behavior near the SYK limit to Poisson statistics near
the integrable limit. This provides a direct example of a
chaos-integrability transition diagnosed purely within a
BPS subspace.

3. BPS chaos and fortuity.— We would like to diag-
nose the chaos-integrability crossover by focusing solely
on the BPS subspace of the deformed model (12). Since
all BPS states are exactly degenerate zero-energy ground
states, ordinary level statistics of the Hamiltonian cannot
be used to probe chaos within this subspace. Neverthe-
less, the notion of BPS chaos, originally motivated by
LMRS [12, 13] and further developed in [14], provides a
way to characterize chaotic behavior among BPS states.
Fortuity provides a classification of BPS states accord-
ing to their behavior as the rank N is varied. Roughly
speaking, monotone states belong to sequences of BPS
cohomology classes that persist to arbitrarily large N,
whereas fortuitous states exist only over a finite range of
N and become non-BPS beyond that range [15]. It has
been conjectured that fortuitous states account for typ-
ical microstates of supersymmetric black holes, whereas
monotone states are associated with smooth horizonless
geometries [15]. This motivates the expectation that for-
tuitous BPS states should exhibit stronger signatures
of quantum chaos than monotone ones. The proposed
mechanism behind this expectation is chaos invasion. In
this picture, a fortuitous BPS state at a given N orig-
inates from a finite-energy non-BPS state at larger N,
therefore, if the original finite-energy states are chaotic,
their random matrix character may persist after they en-
ter the BPS subspace.
Following the LMRS proposal [12, 13], chaos within
a BPS subspace, or BPS chaos, can be probed by pro-
jecting a generic simple operator O onto that subspace
as
OBPS = PBPSOPBPS .
(1)
The proposal is that a chaotic BPS subspace can be char-
acterized by random matrix behavior in the spectrum of
OBPS.
Motivated by this criterion, we will diagnose chaos in
the BPS subspace of the deformed SYK model by study-
ing the spectral statistics of a projected simple operator.
4. Chaos-integrability transition in the BPS sub-
space.— A key observation here is that the BPS states
of both QSYK and the integrable deformation Qcom are
all fortuitous. The chaos invasion picture suggests that
the spectral statistics of finite-energy states can be in-
herited by fortuitous BPS states.
Therefore, QSYK is
expected to transmit chaotic random-matrix behavior to
the BPS subspace, whereas the deformation Qcom may
instead transmit Poisson-like statistics from its integra-
bility. This motivates us to investigate whether the de-
formed model (12) exhibits a chaos-integrability transi-
tion directly within its BPS subspace.
The fortuitous nature of these BPS states can be un-
derstood as follows. For QSYK, the BPS cohomology ex-
hibits the R-charge concentration discussed in [24]: BPS
states are concentrated with in an O(1) range around
Hf=N/2. Since monotone states preserve their fermion
number while remaining BPS as N is varied, such concen-
trated BPS states are necessarily fortuitous. For Qcom,
we can see from (11), that a state is BPS only when each
block (labeled by a) contains either one or two fermions.
Therefore, the BPS subspace is supported in the range

demonstrated that a chaos-integrability crossover can be
observed directly within the BPS subspace of a deformed
N = 2 SYK model, using the diagnostic framework of
BPS chaos. In particular, the projected operator spec-
trum transitions from GUE to Poisson statistics as the
deformation parameter is varied. Since both endpoints

Whether this finite N crossover sharpens into a gen-
uine transition in the large N limit is an open question.
It would be worthwhile to investigate whether an ana-
lytic large N description can be developed for our model,
perhaps in line with the analysis of the deformed SYK
model in [8]. Another promising direction is to study the
double-scaling limit of [27] and formulate the model in
terms of a chord path integral established in the analysis
of chaos-integrability transition in ordinary double-scaled
SYK [28].
It would also be interesting to explore a possible grav-
itational interpretation of the transition.

JT supergravity and its dual matrix model [29] may pro-

the precise relation between fortuity and chaos is another
important problem. The present model provides a use-
ful example in this regard: Qcom have only fortuitous

distribution of BPS states is correlated with quantum
chaos, since for QSYK, the BPS states are concentrated
within O(1) charge window, whereas for Qcom they are
supported over O(N) range, N/3 ≤f ≤2N/3. Another
model with fortuitous BPS states distributed over O(N)
range of R-charge, while being integrable, was proposed

Although we have used the spectral form factor of
a projected operator as a diagnostic of BPS chaos, it
would be interesting to ask whether other probes can
be adapted to detect the same crossover within the BPS
sector. Possible candidates include OTOC [1, 2], Krylov
complexity, and (generating function of) spectral com-
plexity [31–33]. For extensions to more general super-
symmetric systems, the spectrum of the Berry curvature
may provide an intrinsic diagnostic of BPS chaos [34].
