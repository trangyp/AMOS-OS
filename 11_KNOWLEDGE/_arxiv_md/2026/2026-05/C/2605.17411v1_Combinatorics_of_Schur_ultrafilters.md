COMBINATORICS OF SCHUR ULTRAFILTERS
SERHII BARDYLA
Abstract. In this paper, we provide a combinatorial characterization of the elements
of Schur ultrafilters on countable commutative groups. Using this characterization, we
construct a free Schur ultrafilter on Z that is not infinitary Schur. Moreover, assuming
the Continuum Hypothesis, we establish the existence of a free Schur P-point on Z.
1. Introduction and main results
A family F of subsets of a set X is called a filter on X if
(i) ∅/∈F;
(ii) A ∩B ∈F for every A, B ∈F;
(iii) if A ∈F and B ⊇A, then B ∈F.
A filter F on a set X is called an ultrafilter if F is maximal with respect to the inclusion
among all filters on X or, equivalently, for each A ⊆X either A ∈F or X \ A ∈F. A
family B ⊆F is called a base of a filter F if for each F ∈F there exists B ∈B such that
B ⊆F. A filter F is called free if T F = ∅.
The Stone-ˇCech compactification β(X) of a discrete space X is the set of all ultrafilters
on X endowed with a topology τ generated by the base B = {⟨A⟩: A ⊆X}, where
⟨A⟩= {u ∈β(X) : A ∈u}.
Recall that each element x ∈X is identified with the principal ultrafilter {A ⊆X : x ∈A}.
If S is a discrete semigroup, then the semigroup operation on S can be canonically lifted
to a semigroup operation on β(S) as follows: if u, v ∈β(S), then uv is a filter generated by
the base consisting of the sets S
x∈U xVx, where U ∈u and {Vx : x ∈U} ⊆v are arbitrary.
By [4, Theorem 4.1], the defined above semigroup operation on β(S) is unique among those
extending the operation of S and satisfying the following two natural conditions:
(i) for each u ∈β(S) the right shift ρu : β(S) →β(S), x 7→xu is continuous;
(ii) for each s ∈S the left shift λs : β(S) →β(S), x 7→sx is continuous.

The classical Ellis’s theorem (see [4, Theorem 2.5]) implies the existence of free idem-
potent ultrafilters on every infinite semigroup. In other words, for each infinite semigroup
S there exists a free ultrafilter u ∈β(S) such that uu = u. For more about the algebraic
structure of β(S) see the monograph [4] and references therein.
Definition. An ultrafilter u on a semigroup is called
(i) Schur if for each U ∈u there exist a, b ∈U such that ab ∈U;
(ii) infinitary Schur if for each U ∈u there exist a ∈U and an infinite subset B ⊆U
such that aB ⊆U.
It is straightforward to check that an ultrafilter u on a semigroup is idempotent, if and
only if for each U ∈u there exists x ∈U and Vx ∈u such that xVx ⊆U. Thus, for a free
ultrafilter u on a semigroup the following implications hold:
u is idempotent =⇒u is infinitary Schur =⇒u is Schur.
Schur ultrafilters were introduced by Protasov [7] to construct certain group topologies.
Recently [1, 9] it was shown that these ultrafilters play a crucial role in a description of
the Bohr compactification of topological groups, as well as in the automatic continuity of
group operations in so-called chart groups.
Definition. For a discrete semigroup S let
(i) Sch(S) be the subspace of β(S) consisting of all free Schur ultrafilters;
(ii) Sch∞(S) be the subspace of β(S) consisting of all free infinitary Schur ultrafilters;
(iii) Schfin(S) be the subspace of β(S) consisting of all free Schur ultrafilters which are
not infinitary Schur.
The following problem was posed in [1, Problem 3.15]
Problem 1.1 (Bardyla, Zlatoˇs). Does there exist a free Schur ultrafilter on a countable
group G that is not infinitary Schur?
The first principal result of this paper is the following solution of Problem 1.1.
Theorem 1.2. The set Schfin(Z) is an open dense subset of Sch(Z).
Definition. An ultrafilter u on a countable set X is called
(i) weak P-point if u is not in the closure of any countable subset of βX \ (X ∪{u});
(ii) P-point if for every countable subset {Un : n ∈N} of u, there exists U ∈u such that
U ⊆∗Un for all n ∈N;
(iii) selective if for every partition {Cn : n ∈N} of X such that Cn /∈u for all n ∈N, there
exists U ∈u such that |U ∩Cn| ≤1 for all n ∈N.

It is known that each selective ultrafilter is a P-point and each P-point is a weak P-point.
Kunen [5] showed that ZFC implies the existence of a free weak P-point on Z. However,
the existence of free P-points and selective ultrafilters on Z is consistent with ZFC, but
cannot be derived from ZFC alone (see [2, 8]). Notice that a free idempotent ultrafilter u
on a countable group G cannot be a weak P-point, as u is an accumulation point of the
countable family {xu : x ∈G} ⊆βG \ G.
The following result was proven in [1, Propositions 3.12 and 3.13]
Proposition 1.3 (Bardyla, Zlatoˇs). For a countable group G the following assertions hold:
(i) if u ∈Sch∞(G), then u is not a P-point;
(ii) if u ∈Sch(G) and G is commutative, then u is not selective.
Proposition 1.3 motivates the following problem, posed in [1, Problem 3.16].
Problem 1.4 (Bardyla, Zlatoˇs). Is the existence of a free Schur ultrafilter on Z that is a
P-point consistent with ZFC?
By Schp(Z) we denote the set of all free Schur ultrafilters on Z which are P-points. The
second principal result of this paper solves Problem 1.4 under the Continuum Hypothesis
(shortly, CH).
Theorem 1.5. (CH) The set Schp(Z) is dense in Sch(Z).
We left the following question open:
Question 1.6. Does ZFC imply there existence of a free infinitary Schur ultrafilter on Z
that is a weak P-point?
A surjective function c : X →{1, . . . , r} is called an r-coloring of a set X.
Definition. Let G be a countable commutative group. A subset A = {xn : n ∈N} ⊆G is
called Schur if for every r, k ∈N there exists a minimal number S(r, k) ∈N such that for
every r-coloring of X = {xn : n ≤S(r, k)} there exist a ∈X and {b1, . . . , bk} ⊆X such
that {a} ∪{b1, . . . , bk} ∪{a + bi : i ≤k} is a monochromatic subset of X.
It is easy to see that the Schur property of a subset A does not depend on the enumeration
of A. In contrast, the numbers S(r, k) do depend on the enumeration of A. The following
combinatorial description of elements of free Schur ultrafilters on countable commutative
groups, which is crucial for the proofs of Theorems 1.2 and 1.5, is the third principal result
of this paper.
Theorem 1.7. Let G be a countable commutative group. A subset A ⊆G belongs to some
free Schur ultrafilter on G if and only if A is Schur.

It is clear that the set N of positive integers with its usual increasing enumeration is
a Schur subset of Z. Thus, for each r, k ∈N the positive integer S(r, k) is well-defined.
We refer to these integers as two dimensional Schur numbers. Recall that the classical
Schur numbers S(r), r ∈N are defined as follows: S(r) is the minimal positive integer
such that for every r-coloring of the set X = {n ∈N : n ≤S(r)} there exist a, b ∈X such
that the set {a, b, a + b} is monochromatic. In our terminology, we have S(r) = S(r, 1)
for each r ∈N. Despite its relatively simple definition, the computational complexity of
the classical Schur numbers is extremely high. In particular, the equality S(5) = 161 was
established as recently as 2017 by Heule [3] through the use of massive parallel computing.
In particular, their computation required 2 petabytes of space, see the news article [6].
This motivate the following fairly general question:
Question 1.8. What can be said about the values of two-dimensional Schur numbers
S(r, n), where r, n ∈N?
2. Proofs of main results
The following result was proven in [1, Corollary 3.11].
Proposition 2.1 (Bardyla, Zlatoˇs). Let u be a Schur ultrafilter on a commutative group
G. Then for each U ∈u and n ∈N there exists x ∈U such that |{y ∈U : x+y ∈U}| ≥n.
Proposition 2.2. Let u be a free Schur ultrafilter on a countable commutative group G.
Then every U ∈u is a Schur set.
Proof. Fix an enumeration U = {xn : n ∈N}. Assuming the contrary, there exist r, k ∈N
such that for every p ∈N exists an r-coloring ϕp of {xn : n ≤p} with the following
property: for each monochromatic subset B of {xn : n ≤p}, for each y ∈B we have
|{z ∈B : y + z ∈B}| < k. The Pigeonhole Principle yields a color c1 ∈{1, . . . , r} such
that the set M[1] = {p ∈N : ϕp(x1) = c1} is infinite. Similarly, there exists a color
c2 ∈{1, . . . , r} such that the set M[2] = {p ∈M[1] : ϕp(x2) = c2} is infinite. Proceeding
in this way, we obtain a decreasing family {M[n] : n ∈N} of infinite subsets of N, and a
finite coloring ψ of U that colors xn into cn. Since u is an ultrafilter, there exists q ≤r
such that ψ−1(q) ∈u. By Proposition 2.1, there exist a ∈ψ−1(q) and B ⊆ψ−1(q) such
that |B| = k and a + B ⊆ψ−1(q).
Let p′ be any positive integer such that for each
xn ∈{a} ∪B ∪(a + B) we have n ≤p′. Fix any d ∈M[p′] and notice that the colorings
ϕd and ψ have the same traces on {xn : n ≤p′}. But then the monochromatic subset
{a} ∪B ∪(a + B) ⊆{xn : n ≤p′} contradicts the choice of ϕd.
□
