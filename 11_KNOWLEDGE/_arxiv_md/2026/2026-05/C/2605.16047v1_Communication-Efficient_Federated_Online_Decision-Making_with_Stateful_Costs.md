Abstract
We study dynamic regret in federated online decision-making with stateful incurred costs
under block-based synchronization and partial client participation. In this setting, sparse
communication affects not only the pointwise update quality but also the realized state
trajectory along which costs are incurred. We propose BLADE, a projected blockwise
federated online decision method. BLADE uses only O(T/K) communication and achieves
a dynamic-regret bound for the incurred cost against path-length-bounded comparator
sequences; under K = ⌈

Introduction
Federated online decision-making arises in many large-scale networked systems, including online resource
allocation, distributed model-serving infrastructure, and adaptive workload management. In these systems,
decisions are made across spatially distributed clients, but the clients are not independent components. They
often share operational decision sets, such as client-level capacity limits. This makes federated online decision-
making fundamentally different from unconstrained federated learning: the learner must update decisions
under limited communication while those decisions drive a separate evolving system. The problem therefore
lies at the intersection of communication-efficient federated learning and dynamic online decision-making
with stateful costs.
Several adjacent technical lines address parts of this setting, but none fully captures their joint interaction.
The first line is communication-efficient and online federated learning, where sparse synchronization, partial
participation, and client heterogeneity are central issues Karimireddy et al. (2020); Malinovskiy et al. (2020);
Jhunjhunwala et al. (2022). These works, however, typically study pointwise objectives, so communication
mainly affects sampling error, client drift, or participation-induced optimization error. The second line is
dynamic-regret and tracking-regret analysis, which evaluates an online learner by comparing its decisions
with hindsight optimal time-varying benchmarks in nonstationary environments Zinkevich (2003); Hall &
Willett (2013); Jadbabaie et al. (2015); Zhao et al. (2020). This line captures the need to track a drifting

In our setting, sparse communication changes not only the optimization error at each round, but also which
states the system visits and therefore which costs are incurred.
The resulting difficulty is more than a direct combination of these existing issues. Under a block-based
communication protocol, the learner can update the deployed decision only at block boundaries, so the online
decision is held fixed within each communication block. The operational system, however, continues to evolve
at every round. Consequently, a stale block decision does not only increase the instantaneous optimization
error; it also drives the system through a different state trajectory, and this trajectory mismatch changes
the incurred cumulative cost. The difficulty is further amplified by the dynamic-regret benchmark. The
comparator is chosen in hindsight from a path-length-bounded admissible class and is not required to obey the
same sparse communication protocol. Thus, while the online learner must act with block-held decisions, the
comparator may vary at the per-round time scale within the same path-length budget. Sparse communication
therefore induces both a trajectory-level cost distortion and a block-alignment gap against the hindsight
dynamic comparator. The main analytical challenge is to account for these two effects simultaneously in a
regret analysis for the incurred state-dependent cost.
Motivated by this challenge, and inspired by the adjacent lines discussed above, we propose BLADE, a
communication-efficient federated method for stateful online decision-making. The contributions of this paper
are summarized as follows.
1. We formulate a federated online decision problem in which sparse block communication affects the
realized state trajectory and hence the incurred cumulative cost. Performance is measured by dynamic
regret against path-length-bounded hindsight comparators.
2. We develop BLADE, a blockwise projected federated update based on finite-memory surrogate
losses and partial client participation. The analysis isolates how block synchronization, state memory,
and client sampling enter the incurred-cost regret bound.
3. We prove a dynamic-regret bound for the incurred cost along the realized trajectory and validate the
predicted mechanisms through controlled sweeps over communication period, memory length, client
participation, disturbance variation, and horizon length.

3.3
Communication Model
Full synchronization at every round can incur a prohibitive communication overhead, and such a high
communication cost is often unacceptable in practical federated decision systems. We therefore adopt a
block-based periodic communication mechanism, rather than performing full synchronization at every round.
Given a block length K ∈{1, . . . , T}, we partition the time horizon into BT := ⌈T/K⌉blocks and denote the
time-index set of block b by
Ib := {(b −1)K + 1, . . . , min(bK, T)},
b = 1, . . . , BT .
(3)
Within each block, the server communicates with the clients only once at the block boundary. Moreover,
each communication round activates only m clients rather than all N clients. Let the activated client set in
block b be Sb ⊆{1, . . . , N}, with |Sb| = m. Unless otherwise stated, the active set Sb is sampled uniformly
without replacement from all size-m subsets of {1, . . . , N}.
Thus, the communication frequency is controlled by K, while the communication scale per communication
round is controlled by m.
The subsequent algorithm will operate under this time-segmented partial-
participation communication model, which naturally introduces analytical issues such as partial client
information and within-segment decision propagation.

Algorithm
Algorithm 1 implements the above time-segmented partial-participation communication model using the local
stationary surrogate costs ci,t. At the beginning of block b, the server deploys the current block iterate ¯ub, and
the realized online decision satisfies ut = ¯ub for all t ∈Ib. After the losses, disturbances, and local surrogate
information on Ib have been observed, the active subset Sb is sampled for the post-block communication
round, the activated clients evaluate their local block gradients at ¯ub, and the resulting update produces ¯ub+1,
which is used only in the next block. Thus the protocol is causal: no gradient from a future round is used to
choose the decision deployed at that round. The only structural point needed here is that the server updates
each block iterate through projection onto Z. Because the block update is projected onto Z and ut = ¯ub
within block b, all deployed decisions lie in Z by construction.

and partial participation. BLADE uses blockwise projected federated updates and admits a dynamic-regret
guarantee for the incurred cost along the realized state trajectory. Controlled synthetic experiments validate
the predicted communication–regret tradeoff and show how memory length, client participation, disturbance
variation, and horizon length affect incurred-cost dynamic regret.
