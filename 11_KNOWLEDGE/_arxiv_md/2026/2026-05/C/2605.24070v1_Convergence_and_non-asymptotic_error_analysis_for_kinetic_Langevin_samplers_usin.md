Convergence and non-asymptotic error analysis for kinetic
Langevin samplers using the exact harmonic Langevin integrator
Katharina Schuh∗
May 26, 2026
Abstract
We propose a novel kinetic Langevin sampler based on a specific splitting scheme using
the exact harmonic Langevin integrator.
For strongly log-concave target measures, the
sampler exploits a decomposition of the strongly convex potential into a quadratic part and
a convex perturbation with Lipschitz continuous gradient. For the resulting first- and second-
order schemes associated with this splitting we establish convergence rates in L2-Wasserstein
distance as well as non-asymptotic error bounds. In particular, the contraction rate is of
the same order as that of the underlying continuous dynamics. To achieve ε-accuracy, the
required step size for the second-order scheme is comparable to that of established splitting
schemes such as OBABO or UBU, which are widely used in machine learning and molecular
dynamics.
Keywords Markov Chain Monte Carlo; Langevin diffusion; Wasserstein convergence;
kinetic Langevin Sampler; numerical analysis of SDEs

Overview of the literature
Sampling efficiently from high-dimensional log-concave distri-
butions is a central problem of significant interests in many applications including statistical
physics [33], machine learning [2] or Bayesian sampling [25].
The Langevin dynamics (1) and its first-order variant, which is known as the overdamped
Langevin dynamics and is used to sample µx, form a basis for many sampling algorithms.
For the overdamped Langevin dynamics convergence and contraction rates are shown both
by functional inequalities [4, 11] and probabilistic tools such as coupling techniques [23]. Con-
sidering numerical approximation, the unadjusted Langevin algorithm (ULA) and its variants
form a very popular class of Langevin-based sampling methods. Their convergence and non-
asymptotic complexity bounds have been extensively studied (see e.g. [16, 21, 22]). However
for a κ-strongly log-concave distribution a contraction rate of order O(κ) is the optimal achiev-
able rate for first order methods which motivates to consider sampling algorithms based on
higher-order dynamics, in particular second-order Langevin dynamics.
The continuous second-order Langevin dynamics given in (1) was object of many works
(see e.g. [3, 10, 15, 17, 19, 20, 24, 43, 45]) and for strongly κ-convex potentials contraction
in L2-Wasserstein distance with rates of order O(√κ) can be achieved. The same rate carries
over to a broad class of kinetic Langevin sampler building on numerical approximations of the
continuous dynamics. Besides the classical Euler-Maruyama discretization, splitting schemes
form a popular family of kinetic Langevin sampler. In [15, 17, 32, 42] the long-time behaviour
of various kinetic Langevin sampler is analysed and contraction rates matching to the one of
the continuous dynamics are obtained.
The contraction results highlight the potential acceleration obtained by incorporating mo-
mentum variables into the sampling procedure. In parallel, the Hamiltonian Monte Carlo al-
gorithm [38] forms another prominent momentum-based algorithm by using the Hamiltonian
dynamics. For the convergence analysis of the exact Hamiltonian Monte Carlo method we refer
to [34, 5]. In [26] the generalized HMC, a unified framework for both HMC and the splitting
scheme OBABO is introduced and its long-time behaviour is analysed.
The numerical discretization of both the Hamiltonian based and the Langevin dynamics
based algorithms introduce an additional error. Bounds on the efficiency of the resulting al-
gorithms are studied for the Langevin dynamics in [9, 15, 32, 37] and for HMC in [6, 14, 34].
Alternatively, the error can be avoided by an additional Metropolis acceptance-rejection step
which allows to sample exactly from the correct target measure [36, 27].
The Metropolis-
adjusted algorithms are not scope of this work and we focus on bounding the additional bias
term induced by inexact sampling and studying the dependence on the dimension and condition
number of the obtained complexity guarantees.
Recent advances have been made in the analysis of sampling non-log-concave distributions.
Often sampling guarantees are obtained under relaxed dissipativity guarantees by using reflec-
tion couplings [23, 24]. We assume that the proposed kinetic Langevin sampler is applicable for
sampling non log-concave distributions and sampling guarantees hold under a similar setup as
in [44].
Finally, we remark that in many practical applications the exact gradient evaluation is not
available or computationally too costly. In these settings, one typically makes use of a stochastic
gradient approximation. In the context of Bayesian Learning, [46] proposed sampling based on

Our main contribution
The contribution of the paper is three-fold.
• Firstly, we propose a new splitting of (1) into two components using the decomposition of
the potential (2) and present the precise numerical implementation of the corresponding

those of the standard splitting schemes as the OBABO or UBU scheme ([32, 42]). In particular
the proposed splitting scheme is beneficial for Bayesian sampling when the prior is given by a
Gaussian measure, since the structure of the log-likelihood function matches with the remainder
term of the splitting.

Splitting schemes
Recently, advanced numerical schemes of the Langevin dynamics are obtained by using splitting
methods. The key idea of these methods is based on splitting the dynamics into components
which can be integrated exactly, see [35] for an overview on splitting methods. Due to their
good performance they are frequently used in molecular dynamics [8].
A common splitting is given by considering the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (O) and the position part
(A) and the velocity part (B) corresponding to the Hamiltonian part of (1). Then, integrating
exactly this terms successively for some time h > 0 leads to first-order schemes of the form
OBA, BAO ... Building symmetric higher-order splitting schemes like OBABO and BAOAB
provide numerical splitting schemes with errors of better order (see e.g. [29, 30, 32], [37]). We
also refer to [12] for the asymptotic analysis in the non-strongly convex setting. Recently the
splitting where the parts A and O are combined to the part U became famous resulting in the
splitting schemes UB and UBU [47]. The symmetric second-order method has weak and strong
error of order two [42, 40].
