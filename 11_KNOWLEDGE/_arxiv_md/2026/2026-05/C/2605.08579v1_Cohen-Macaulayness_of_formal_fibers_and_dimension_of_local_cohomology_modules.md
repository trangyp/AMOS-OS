TRAN DO MINH CHAU

Abstract 1. Let (R, m) be a Noetherian local ring, M a finitely generated R-module of
dimension d. Set a(M) := a0(M) · · · ad−1(M), where ai(M) := AnnR Hi
m(M) for i ≥0. In
this paper, we study the Cohen-Macaulayness of formal fibers of R in the relation with the
dimension dim(R/a(M)). We prove that dim(R/a(M)) < d if and only if R/p is unmixed and
the generic formal fiber of R/p is Cohen-Macaulay for all p ∈SuppR(M) with dim(R/p) = d.
In general, R/p is unmixed and the generic formal fiber of R/p is Cohen-Macaulay for all
p ∈SuppR(M) with dim(R/p) > dim(R/a(M)). As applications, we explore the structure
of local rings and the dimension, the closedness of non Cohen-Macaulay locus of finitely
generated modules.

Introduction
Throughout this paper, let (R, m) be a Noetherian local ring, M a finitely generated
R-module with dimR(M) = d. Denote by bR and c
M the m-adic completions of R and M
respectively.
For p ∈Spec(R), recall that bR ⊗R k(p) is called the formal fiber ring of R at p and the
spectrum of bR⊗Rk(p) is called the formal fiber of R at p, where k(p) := Rp/pRp is the residue
field. Note that there is a natural bijection between the formal fiber of R at p and the inverse
image of p via the induced map Spec( bR) →Spec(R). If R is a domain then the formal fiber
of R at the prime ideal 0 is called the generic formal fiber of R. The precise definition of
formal fiber was given by Grothendieck [13] in the early 1960s, where the research on formal
fibers continued to develope after the works by Zariski and Nagata in the 1950s (see [18]).
Many different problems on formal fibers have attracted the interest of mathematicians.
About the dimension of formal fibers, Matsumura [19] proposed to study the number α(R)
defined as the maximum of the dimensions of all formal fiber rings of R at prime ideals, he
gave examples of local rings R with α(R) = i for each i ∈{0, dim(R)−1, dim(R)−2}. Then
Rotthaus [23] constructed local rings R with α(R) = i for each i ∈{1, . . . , dim(R) −3}. Re-
garding the structure of local rings, Heinzer-Rotthaus-Sally [14] studied the interrelatedness

The following theorem is the first main result of this paper, that gives some criteria for
the Cohen-Macaulayness of formal fibers of R in relation with dimension dim(R/a(M)).
Theorem 1.1. The following statements are true.
(a) dim(R/a(M)) < d if and only if R/p is unmixed and the formal fiber of R at p is
Cohen-Macaulay for all p ∈SuppR(M) with dim(R/p) = d.
(b) If p ∈SuppR(M) with dim(R/p) > dim(R/a(M)) then R/p is unmixed and the formal
fiber of R at p is Cohen-Macaulay.
We denote by nCM(M) the non Cohen-Macaulay locus of M, which is defined as follows
nCM(M) = {p ∈Spec(R)| Mp is not Cohen-Macaulay}.
In the following cases, nCM(M) is a closed subset of Spec(R) under Zariski topology: R is
an excellent ring (see [13]); R is a quotient of a Gorenstein local ring (see [26, 25, 8]); R is a
quotient of a Cohen-Macaulay local ring (see [10]); all formal fibers of R are Cohen-Macaulay
(see [11]).
