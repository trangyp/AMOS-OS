Cyclicity via weak∗sequentially cyclicity in Radially
weighted Besov spaces
Anusrika Datta and Stefan Richter
May 6, 2026
Abstract
A radially weighted Besov space H is a space of holomorphic functions on the unit
ball Bd ⊆Cd whose N-th radial derivative is square integrable with respect to a given
admissible radial measure. We write Mult(H) for its multiplier algebra. The cyclic
vectors in H are those functions f whose multiplier multiples are dense in H.
We call a multiplier f ∈Mult(H) weak∗sequentially cyclic if its multiplier multiples
are weak∗sequentially dense in Mult(H). It is immediate that every weak∗sequentially
cyclic multiplier is cyclic, and it turns out that the two notions coincide whenever H
has the complete Pick property. However, in more general radially weighted Besov
spaces there may be multipliers that are cyclic, but not weak∗sequentially cyclic.
For bounded holomorphic functions f with no zeros in Bd, we obtain a condition
on log f that implies the cyclicity of f in H and yields invertibility properties for 1/f
within an associated Smirnov-type class. This condition is formulated in terms of weak∗
sequentially cyclic multipliers and can often be verified using a comparison principle:
if f, g ∈Mult(H) satisfy |f| ≤|g| and if f is weak∗sequentially cyclic, then g is also
weak∗sequentially cyclic.
These results provide new insights into cyclicity phenomena in radially weighted
Besov spaces in settings, where H fails to be a complete Pick space.

Multipliers and weak∗closed ideals
Let H be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of analytic functions on Bd. It is clear that
linear combinations of certain countable sets of reproducing kernels are dense in H, hence
H is separable. Since H is separable, the trace class operators on H are a separable predual
of B(H), the algebra of all bounded linear transformations on H. We write Mult(H) for the
multiplier algebra of H. Then each element ϕ of Mult(H) induces a bounded multiplication
operator Mϕ ∈B(H) and Mult(H) becomes a Banach space with norm ∥ϕ∥Mult(H) =
