Computable Scott Sentences and the
Friedman-Stanley embedding
David Gonzalez and Julia Knight

Abstract
Friedman and Stanley [9] developed the notion of Borel reducibility and
illustrated its use in comparing classification problems for some familiar
classes of countable structures. For many embeddings, the fact that the
embedding is 1 −1 on isomorphism types is explained by the existence
of simple formulas that, uniformly, interpret the input structure in the
output structure. For the embeddings of graphs in trees, and in linear or-
derings, there is no uniform interpretation [16], [20]. We focus on a version
of the Friedman-Stanley embedding from [16] that takes each structure A
for the language of graphs to a labeled tree TA. Gonzalez and Rossegger
[13] showed that this embedding preserves Scott complexity. We refine
this result, showing that for an X-computable ordinal, if one of A, TA
has a computable infinitary Scott sentence, then so does the other, and
the complexities match. Let T be the class of labeled trees isomorphic to
those in the range of the embedding, and let Tα be the subclass consisting
of structures of Scott rank at most α. It follows from results of Gao [10]
that T is not Borel. We show that for each α, Tα is Borel. In fact, if α is
an X-computable ordinal, then Tα is complete X-effective Π2α+2.

Introduction
A good deal of work, in different branches of mathematics, involves trying to
“classify” the objects in some class up to some notion of sameness, using rel-
atively simple invariants.
For a countable language L, Mod(L) denotes the
class of L-structures with universe ω.
Our objects are elements of Mod(L),
for some countable language L. Our classes are subclasses of Mod(L), always
closed under isomorphism. Our notion of sameness is isomorphism. The class of
Q-vector spaces is nicely classified by dimension. Nobody would try to classify
directed graphs. Of course, there are only countably many isomorphism types
of Q-vector spaces, while there are 2ℵ0 isomorphism types of countable directed
graphs. Friedman and Stanley [9] developed the notion of “Borel reducibility” to
compare classification problems for classes of countable structures, using more
than just cardinality.

Definition 1. For classes K ⊆Mod(L) and K′ ⊆Mod(L′), we say that K
is Borel reducible to K′, and we write K ≤B K′, if there is a Borel function
Φ : K →K′ such that for A, B ∈K, A ∼= B iff Φ(A) ∼= Φ(B).
In [9], Friedman and Stanley located various familiar classes of structures
under Borel reducibility, showing that undirected graphs, fields, 2-step nilpo-
tent groups, and linear orderings lie on top, while Q-vector spaces and Abelian
p-groups lie strictly below the top.
There has been a great deal of further
work locating further interesting classes. For the class TFAbn of torsion-free
Abelian groups of rank n, Hjorth [17] and Thomas [27] showed that TFAbn <B
TFAbn+1. Paolini and Shelah [25] showed that torsion-free Abelian groups of
infinite rank lie on top. For some of their results, Friedman and Stanley used
known embeddings. To show that linear orderings and trees lie on top, they gave
new embeddings. We focus on a version of the Friedman-Stanley embedding,
described in [16], that, for a finite relational language L, takes each A ∈Mod(L)
to a labeled tree TA.
Let A ∈Mod(L), where L is the language of graphs, and let TA be the
corresponding tree structure. In [13], it is shown that A and TA have the same
Scott rank. Hence, if one of the structures has a Πα+1 Scott sentence, then
so does the other. They had asked whether the same is true for computable
infinitary Scott sentences. In Section 2, we give a positive answer.
Theorem A. For all A ∈Mod(L), if one of A, TA has a computable Πα+1
Scott sentence, then so does the other.
Remark: Our proof shows more. Analagously to the work in [13], we see that,
if α is a computable limit ordinal and A has a computable Πα Scott sentence,
then so does TA.
Let T be the class of labeled trees isomorphic to one of the form TA for
A ∈Mod(L). Let Tα consist of the elements of T of rank at most α. The full
class T is not Borel. Gao [10] gave results implying that for any embedding
of graphs in linear orderings, the class of orderings isomorphic to those in the
range is not Borel. The same reasoning shows that T is not Borel (see [13] or
[12]). In Section 3, we show that for all countable ordinals α, Tα is Borel, and
we give the precise complexity. In Section 4, we show that our given complexity
is the best possible.
Theorem B. For all X-computable ordinals α ≥1, Tα is complete X-effective
Π2α+2.

1.1.1
Tree of tuples
In this subsection, we describe the embedding A →TA in a precise way, follow-
ing [16], see also [13], p. 4. Let L be a finite relational language. Let X be the
set of variables x1, x2, . . .. We assign to each L-structure A, with universe ω, a
tree structure TA. The language of the tree structures, L∗, consists of a unary
function symbol p (the predecessor function) and unary relation symbols UD,
where D assigns an atomic type to the variables x1, . . . , xn for some n, making
the variables all distinct.
For each tuple ¯a = (a1, . . . , an) of distinct elements of A, let ¯x = (x1, . . . , xn)
be the corresponding tuple of variables from X, and let D¯a(¯x) be the conjunc-
tion of the formulas ±α(¯x) true of ¯a, where α(¯x) is atomic. Each node of TA
represents some tuple ¯a and carries exactly the label UD, where D = D¯a(¯x) for
the appropriate initial tuple of variables ¯x. We ensure that every possible tuple
from A is represented among the nodes of TA. A predecessor of a node will
represent the tuple with one less element. We define TA more precisely below.
Definition 2 (Tree of tuples TA). At level 0, TA has a unique node λ, represent-
ing ∅. The nodes at level n represent tuples ¯a of length n. A node representing
a tuple ¯a is labeled with a code for the atomic diagram of ¯a. For each node σ
representing an n-tuple ¯a, and each further element a′, σ has infinitely many
extensions representing the tuple (¯a, a′).

1.1.4
Scott complexity
In this subsubsection, we recall the Scott Isomorphism Theorem [26] and define
a Scott sentence. We also state a result of Montalb´an [22], which yields the
notion of Scott rank we will use.
Theorem 1.1 (Scott Isomorphism Theorem). For each countable structure A
for a countable language L, there is a sentence φ of Lω1ω whose countable models
are just the isomorphic copies of A.
Definition 3. A Scott sentence for A is a sentence φ that characterizes A up
to isomorphism, as in Scott’s Theorem.
Theorem 1.2 (Montalb´an). Let α ≥1 be a countable ordinal.
For A ∈
Mod(L), the following are equivalent:
1. A has a Πα+1 Scott sentence,
2. for each tuple ¯a, the orbit is defined by a Σα formula,
3. for each tuple ¯a, there is a Σα-formula that implies (in A) all Πα formulas
satisfied by ¯a.
Definition 4. The Scott rank of A, denoted by SR(A), is the least α such
that the orbits of all tuples are defined by computable Σα formulas. If α is a
limit ordinal, then for a structure A of Scott rank α, the orbit of each tuple is
defined by a Σβ formula for some β < α. The structure A will have a Πα+1
Scott sentence. It may or may not have a Scott sentence that is Πα.
Alvir, Knight, and McCoy [2] gave the following partial effective version of
Montalb´an’s result.
Theorem 1.3. If A has a computable Πα Scott sentence, then the orbit of each
tuple is defined by a computable Σβ formula for some β < α.

1.1.6
Embeddings that preserve complexity
We consider variants of the notion of Borel embedding that satisfy a “Pullback”
theorem. The following definition is from [6].
Definition 6 (Turing computable embedding). Let L and L′ be computable
languages and take classes K ⊆Mod(L), K′ ⊆Mod(L′). A Turing computable
embedding of K in K′ is a Turing operator Φ : K →K′ such that A ∼= B iff
Φ(A) ∼= Φ(B). We write K ≤tc K′ if there is such an embedding.
The result below is from [19].
Theorem 1.7 (Pull-back Theorem). Suppose K ≤tc K′, where K ⊆Mod(L)
and K′ ⊆Mod(L′). For any computable infinitary L′-sentence φ, we can effec-
tively find a computable infinitary L-sentence φ∗, of the same complexity, such
that for all A ∈K, Φ(A) |= φ iff A |= φ∗.
The notion of Turing computable embedding relativizes.
Fact: An embedding is continuous iff it is X-computable for some X.
The Pull-back Theorem relativizes to give the following.
Corollary 1.8. Let Φ be a continuous embedding of K in K′, where K ⊆
Mod(L) and K′ ⊆Mod(L′). Then for any Lω1ω sentence φ, there is an Lω1ω
sentence φ∗, of the same complexity, such that Φ(A) |= φ iff A |= φ∗.
The following is well-known; see, for example [23] Theorem VI.27 and Lemma
VI.29.
Theorem 1.9. Let L be a computable relational language. There is a Turing
computable embedding Φ of Mod(L) in the class of undirected graphs. Moreover,
1. the class K consisting of graphs G such that for some A ∈Mod(L),
G ∼= Φ(A) is effective Π2,

in Φ(A); we have formulas dU(x), defining the universe of the copy, and,

Transfer of computable Scott sentences
In this section, we prove the first of our two main results. In [13], Gonzalez
and Rossegger proved that for a structure A ∈Mod(L), A and TA have the
same Scott rank. By Montalb´an’s Theorem, if one of the structures has a Scott
sentence that is Πα+1, then so does the other. Gonzalez and Rossegger asked
whether the same is true for computable infinitary Scott sentences. We give a
positive answer.
Theorem A. Let L be a finite relational language. If one of the structures A or
TA has a computable Πα+1 Scott sentence, then so does the other. Moreover,
we can pass effectively from a Scott sentence for one to a Scott sentence for the
other.
Remark: We can vary Theorem A, letting the language L be computable, but
not necessarily finite.
We split the proof of Theorem A in two. The result below gives the easier
implication.
Proposition 2.1. Given a computable infinitary Scott sentence φ for TA, we
can effectively pass to a computable infinitary Scott sentence φ∗for A, where
the complexity of φ∗matches that of φ.
Proof. The embedding A →TA is Turing computable. Applying the Pullback
Theorem, we take φ∗to be the pullback of φ. The two sentences have the same
complexity, and for B ∈Mod(L), A ∼= B iff TA ∼= TB. Since φ is a Scott sentence
for TA, we have that
B |= φ∗⇐⇒TB |= φ ⇐⇒TB ∼= TA ⇐⇒A ∼= B,
and so φ∗is a Scott sentence for A.
Every Lω1ω-sentence is X-computable for some X. Relativizing Proposi-
tion 2.1, we get the result, already proved by Gonzalez and Rossegger [13],
saying that if TA has a Scott sentence that is Πα, then so does A.
The result below gives the harder implication in Theorem A.

Outline for the proof of Proposition 2.2
We begin by isolating some simple properties of trees TA for A ∈Mod(L). For

2.2
Simple properties of trees TA
The basic properties listed below are simple to state, and they are clearly true
of the tree structures TA. We will give further properties after we have defined
forcing.
Levels: Each node satisfies exactly one UD. Moreover, if σ is at level n, then
σ satisfies UD for some D assigning a complete atomic type to the variables
(x1, . . . , xn) of σ.
Consistency: If τ ⊇σ, then the atomic type assigned to τ implies the one
assigned to σ.
Replication: For any x, if x has at least one successor satisfying UD, then it
has infinitely many.
