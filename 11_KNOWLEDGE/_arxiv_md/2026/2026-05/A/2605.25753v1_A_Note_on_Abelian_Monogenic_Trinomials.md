A NOTE ON ABELIAN MONOGENIC TRINOMIALS
LENNY JONES
Abstract. An abelian monogenic polynomial f(x) ∈Z[x] is a monic polyno-
mial of degree N that is irreducible over Q, such that the Galois group of f(x)
over Q is abelian, and {1, θ, θ2, . . . , θN−1} is a basis for the ring of integers
of Q(θ), where f(θ) = 0. In this article, we determine all abelian monogenic
trinomials of the form x2n + axn + b, where n, a, b ∈Z with n ≥1 and ab ̸= 0.
1. Introduction
We say that a monic polynomial f(x) ∈Z[x] is monogenic if f(x) is irreducible
over Q and {1, θ, θ2, . . . , θdeg(f)−1} is a basis for the ring of integers, ZK, of K =
Q(θ), where f(θ) = 0. It is well known [1] that
(1.1)
∆(f) = [ZK : Z[θ]]2 ∆(K),
where ∆(f) and ∆(K) denote the discriminants over Q, respectively, of f(x) and
the number field K. We refer to [ZK : Z[θ]] as the index of f(x), and we denote
it index(f).
Thus, from (1.1), f(x) is monogenic if and only if ∆(f) = ∆(K)
or equivalently, index(f)=1.
We say that f(x) is abelian monogenic if f(x) is
monogenic and the Galois group of f(x) over Q, denoted Gal(f), is abelian.
The investigations in this note were motivated by the following work of Marie-
Nicole Gras.
Theorem 1.1.
[2] Let N ≥5 be an integer with gcd(N, 6) = 1. There exist only
finitely many abelian extensions of Q of degree N whose ring of algebraic integers
is monogenic.
Theorem 1.2.
[3] Let ℓ≥5 be prime.
Then there exists at most one cyclic
extension K/Q of degree ℓ. Moreover, if 2ℓ+ 1 is prime, then K is the maximal
real subfield of the cyclotomic field of index 2ℓ+ 1, and if 2ℓ+ 1 is not prime, then
no such cyclic extension exists.
Theorem 1.3.
[4] Let K/Q be a cyclic extension of degree ℓr, where ℓ≥5 is
prime and r ≥2. Let ZK denote the ring of integers of K.
(1) If 2ℓr + 1 is not prime, then ZK is not monogenic.
(2) If 2ℓr + 1 is a prime p, and if there exists a prime q ̸= p such that q is
totally ramified in K, then ZK is not monogenic.
We see that Theorems 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3 are concerned with abelian monogenic
number fields whose degree over Q is relatively prime to 6. Our focus in this note

If Fn,a,b(x) is irreducible over Q, then we make the following two observations: the
degree of the number field Q(α), where Fn,a,b(α) = 0, is not relatively prime to 6,
and if d is a divisor of n with d ≥1, then Fd,a,b(x) is also irreducible over Q. It is
our goal to determine all abelian monogenic trinomials of the form Fn,a,b(x).
We make some notational remarks. For Fn,a,b(x), we let
W := (a2 −4b)/ gcd(2, a)2.
For any integer N ≥1, we let CN denote the cyclic group of order N, and ΦN(x)
denote the cyclotomic polynomial of index N and degree ϕ(N).
For integers r
and m with m ≥2, we let the notation “r mod m” denote the unique integer
z ∈{0, 1, 2, . . . , m −1} such that r ≡z (mod m). That is, r mod m = z. For any
positive integer n ≥2, we define the radical of n, denoted rad(n), to be the product
of the distinct prime divisors of n.
Our main result is the following:
Theorem 1.4. The trinomial Fn,a,b(x) is abelian monogenic if and only if (n, a, b)
is contained in one of the following pairwise-disjoint sets:
(1) {(1, a, b) : W is squarefree, and
(a mod 4, b mod 4) ∈{(0, 1), (0, 2), (2, 2), (2, 3)} if 2 | a}.
In this case, we have Gal(F1,a,b) ≃C2.
(2) {(2, ±4, 2), (2, −5, 5)}.
In this case, we have Gal(F2,a,b) ≃C4.
(3) {(2, a, 1) : W is squarefree, and a mod 4 ∈{0, 3}}.
In this case, we have Gal(F2,a,1) ≃C2 × C2.
(4) {(2r, −1, 1) : r ≥2}.
In this case, we have F2r,−1,1(x) = Φ2r+1·3(x) so that
Gal(F2r,−1,1) ≃C2 × C2 × C2r−1.
(5) {(3s, 1, 1) : s ≥1}.
In this case, we have F3s,1,1(x) = Φ3s+1(x) so that Gal(F3s,1,1) ≃C2·3s.
(6) {(3s, −1, 1) : s ≥1}.
In this case, we have F3s,−1,1(x) = Φ2·3s+1(x) so that Gal(F3s,−1,1) ≃C2·3s.
(7) {(2r3s, −1, 1) : rs ≥1}.
In this case, we have F2r3s,−1,1(x) = Φ2r+13s+1(x) so that
Gal(F2r3s,−1,1) ≃C2 × C2 × C2r−1 × C3s.
2. Preliminaries
The following lemma shows that there are severe restrictions on the value of n
if Gal(Fn,a,b) is abelian.
Lemma 2.1. Suppose that Fn,a,b(x) is irreducible over Q, and that Gal(Fn,a,b) is
abelian. Then n = 2r3s for some nonnegative integers r and s. Furthermore, if
d ≥1 is a divisor of n, then Gal(Fd,a,b) is abelian.
Proof. Note that if n = 1, then Gal(F1,a,b) ≃C2 is abelian.
So, suppose that
n ≥2, and let p be a prime divisor of n. Then, Fp,a,b(x) is irreducible over Q
since Fn,a,b(x) = Fp,a,b(xn/p) is irreducible over Q. Observe that if α is a zero

The following four theorems describe the abelian monogenic trinomials Fn,a,b(x)
for, respectively, n ∈{1, 2, 3, 6}. The proofs can be found in [6].
Theorem 2.2. Suppose that F1,a,b(x) is irreducible over Q.
Then F1,a,b(x) is
abelian monogenic if and only if W is squarefree and
(a mod 4, b mod 4) ∈{(0, 1), (0, 2), (2, 2), (2, 3)}
if 2 | a.
Theorem 2.3. Suppose that F2,a,b(x) is irreducible over Q.
Then F2,a,b(x) is
abelian monogenic with Gal(F2,a,b) ≃
(1) C4 if and only if F2,a,b(x) ∈{x4 ± 4x2 + 2, x4 −5x2 + 5};
(2) C2 × C2 if and only if W is squarefree and
F2,a,b(x) ∈{x4 + ax2 + 1 : a mod 4 ∈{0, 3}}.
Theorem 2.4. Suppose that F3,a,b(x) is irreducible over Q.
Then F3,a,b(x) is
abelian monogenic if and only if F3,a,b(x) ∈{x6 −x3 + 1, x6 + x3 + 1}, in which
case, Gal(F3,a,b) ≃C6.
Theorem 2.5. Suppose that F6,a,b(x) is irreducible over Q.
Then F6,a,b(x) is
abelian monogenic if and only if F6,a,b(x) = x12−x6+1, in which case, Gal(F6,a,b) ≃
C2 × C6.
The next lemma is an application to our specific situation of a theorem due to
Kaur, Kumar and Remete [7, Theorem 1.1].
Lemma 2.6. Let n, a, b, r, s ∈Z, such that n = 2r3s with n ̸∈{1, 2, 3, 6} and
ab ̸= 0. Let k = n/rad(n). Then k > 1 and
Fn,a,b(x) = Frad(n),a,b(xk).
If Fn,a,b(x) is irreducible over Q, then Fn,a,b(x) is monogenic if and only if all of
the following conditions are satisfied:
(1) b is squarefree,
(2) q does not divide the index(Fn,a,b) for all prime divisors q of k,
(3) Frad(n),a,b(x) is monogenic.
