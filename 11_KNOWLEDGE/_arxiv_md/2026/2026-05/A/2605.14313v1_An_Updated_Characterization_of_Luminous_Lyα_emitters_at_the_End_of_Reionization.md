ABSTRACT
We present a multi-wavelength physical characterization of 14 luminous Lyα emitters (LAEs) at z ≈
6, integrating deep ground-based Magellan/M2FS spectroscopy with heterogeneous JWST/NIRCam
broad- and medium-band imaging. Identified via strong Lyα lines with extreme Lyα luminosities of
>1042.6 erg s−1, the sample exhibits very large rest-frame equivalent widths (≳100 Å) and steeply blue
UV continua (βmedian ≃−2.2, −18.2 > M1500 > −20.2 mag). Crucially, the integration of NIRCam
medium-band photometry (F410M) breaks the degeneracy between strong rest-optical nebular emission
and Balmer breaks, resolving prior mass overestimations.
The tightly constrained spectral energy
distribution modeling demonstrates that these luminous LAEs tend to be unequivocally low-mass,
ultra-young dwarf starbursts; half the sample is characterized by stellar masses of M∗< 108M⊙, ages
≲10 Myr, and negligible dust attenuation. We also map the production eﬀiciency of ionizing photons
and Lyα escape fractions (f Lyα
esc ). The f Lyα
esc
values are exceptionally high, with a median of ≳40%,
increasing for the bluer UV continua. Finally, analyzing spatial offsets between the Lyα centroid and
the stellar counterpart, we demonstrate empirically that internal dust content, rather than neutral
hydrogen gas, dominate the suppression of Lyα radiative transfer. Our study reveals that strong Lyα
emission of the luminous LAEs are generally attributed to both the vigorous starburst activities and
the high f Lyα
esc . Resembling Lyman continuum leakers, these extreme dwarf systems function as highly
eﬀicient ionizing engines at the conclusion of the Epoch of Reionization.

1. INTRODUCTION
Lyα emitting galaxies are powerful probes to explore
galaxy formation and evolution in the early universe.
Theoretically expected as a “spectral beacon” (Partridge
& Peebles 1967), Lyα helps to gradually find galax-
ies at higher redshift (Hu & McMahon 1996; Rhoads
et al. 2000). After the first galaxy to be spectroscop-
ically confirmed by Lyα at a redshift greater than 6
(Hu et al. 2002), Lyα selected galaxies, the Lyα emit-
ters (LAEs) found by narrowband (NB) technique, keep
spectroscopic redshift record as the most distant galax-
ies for a decade (e.g., Rhoads et al. 2004; Taniguchi et al.
2005; Iye et al. 2006; Martin et al. 2008; Tilvi et al. 2010;
Hu et al. 2010).
As a complementary method, the drop-out technique
help select Lyman Break Galaxies (LBGs) or high-
redshift (high-z) candidates (e.g., Steidel et al. 1996; Gi-
avalisco 2002; Douglas et al. 2009; Bouwens et al. 2015;
Pentericci et al. 2018). Especially, Hubble Space Tele-
scope (HST) pushed the redshift frontier to z ∼9 with
the installation of the Wide Field Camera 3 (WFC3/IR)
(e.g., Ellis et al. 2013; McLeod et al. 2016; Oesch et al.
2018; Bouwens et al. 2021). After the launch in 2022,
James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) substantially en-
larges the sample at z > 9 through deep infrared imag-

minous ones which typically represent the extreme cases.
Although recent JWST works have specifically stud-
ied high-z LAEs (e.g., Goovaerts et al. 2024; Iani et al.
2024), most of the LAEs are not luminous and not
representative of those already identified by the large
ground-based surveys based on the NB technique (e.g.,
Kashikawa et al. 2011; Ning et al. 2020; Kikuta et al.
2023). On the other hand, some JWST works study the
LAEs from ground-based surveys but most of them have
not been spectroscopically confirmed, lacking the key
redshift information (e.g., Shimizu et al. 2025). More
luminous (confirmed) LAEs thus need to be further an-
alyzed, which could also in turn indirectly help prepar-
ing the candidates for the future ground-based surveys
taken by, for example, the MUltiplexed Survey Tele-
scope (MUST; Cai et al. 2025; Zhao et al. 2024).
We carried out a ground-based, multi-object Magel-
lan/M2FS spectroscopic survey spectroscopic survey to
establish a large sample of high-z galaxies (Jiang et al.
2017), including LAEs at z ≈5.7 (Ning et al. 2020) and
z ≈6.6 (Ning et al. 2022), and LBGs at z ∼6 (Fu
et al. 2024). These galaxies are spectroscopically con-
firmed via Lyα lines from the high-z candidates which
are designed to be selected from famous fields, wait-
ing for JWST covering, including A370, CDFS, COS-
MOS, SXDS, and SSA22. As JWST data is gradually
accumulated by more surveys, more than 10 luminous
LAEs from our M2FS survey have been covered by a
series (>4) of infrared (IR) bands, especially including
the medium band (F410M). In this work, we make use
of the imaging data to further explore these luminous
LAEs, focusing on comparing their Lyα properties and
physical properties.
We organize this paper as follows. In Section 2, we
briefly state the LAE sample from the M2FS spectro-
scopic observations, NIRCam imaging dataset from sev-
eral JWST surveys, and photometric measurements. In
Section 3, we describe the methods and present the
results including the physical properties from spectral
energy distribution (SED) fitting, UV and Hα-related
quantities, Lyα properties (line width, misalignment to
stellar counterpart, escape fraction).
We give further
discussions in Section 4. We summarize our paper in
Section 5. Throughout this work, we adopt a standard
flat cosmology with H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1, Ωm = 0.3
and ΩΛ = 0.7. All magnitudes refer to the AB system.
2. SAMPLE AND DATA
In this section, we present the sample of the LAEs at
z ∼6 with the JWST/NIRCam imaging data. Then we
describe how to detect the sources and take photometry
from the images for the LAE sample.
2.1. Sample & Imaging Data
Our parent sample is drawn from the Magellan/M2FS
spectroscopic survey of high-z galaxies presented in Ning
et al. (2020, 2022).
With the high-throughput spec-
trograph M2FS on the 6.5m Magellan Clay telescope,
the effective integration time per pointing is averagely
∼5 hr (equivalently 35.8 hr in total for the sample in
this work). The LAEs are selected as candidates based
on the Subaru/Suprime broadband and narrowband im-
ages and then identified with (strong) Lyα lines by our
Magellan/M2FS spectroscopic survey (in a Lyα lumi-
nosity range from 1042.3 to 1043.7 erg s−1). The survey
spectroscopically confirmed 260 LAEs at z ≈5.7 and
36 LAEs at z ≈6.6 in total over nearly 2 deg2 in the
sky. So far as JWST has collected more extensive IR
data across various fields, a fraction of our confirmed
LAEs have been covered in more NIRCam bands. In
this work, we retrieve and adopt the JWST imaging
data from DAWN JWST Archive (DJA1), a repository
of public JWST galaxy data from a number of JWST
surveys. The JWST imaging data are reduced based on
grizli2 (Brammer 2023). Valentino et al. (2023) pro-
vides the NIRCam data reduction in detail. The version
of the imaging dataset is as of December 2025.
Among the JWST/NIRCam bands, the medium ones
are especially sensitive to the presence/absence of emis-
sion line. Including photometric data only covering op-
tical continua can break the degeneracy between strong
nebular lines of younger galaxies and prominent Balmer

We use SEP (Barbary 2016), a python implementa-
tion of SExtractor (Bertin & Arnouts 1996), to detect
sources and measure the photometry of the objects in
the bands. We create the empirical point-spread func-
tion (PSF) by selecting and stacking at least 100 bright
(not saturated) stars for each band.
The images are
PSF-homogenized to F444W and stacked to feed the
detection image.
We then take the source detection
by adopting a minimum area of five pixels, a thresh-
old of 2σ, 64 deblending levels, and a contrast of 0.001
(no gaussian kernel in pixels). Then we match the out-
put catalogs to the original Lyα centroid (from the NB
imaging data). In the candidate selection of our Magel-
lan/M2FS program, the photometric apertures have an
angular radius of 1.′′0. The spectroscopic fibers have an
angular radius of 0.′′6. We set the distance tolerance by
a radius of 0.′′8, averaging the above two values.
Next we also use SEP to perform photometry in all
bands for each detected source. This procedure is same
as the dual-image mode in SExtractor. We measure
the total MAG_AUTO flux with elliptical apertures. Kron
factor and the minimum aperture size are set to be 2.0
and 2.5, respectively. The aperture corrections are also
measured by increasing both the parameters, which is
equivalent to the standard PHOT_AUTOPARAMS values of
2.5 and 3.5. Our sample only includes the sources with
a single component detected above 3σ in the F444W
band. Such single component dominates Hα emission
and contributes Lyα emission so that it is proper to ob-
tain the corresponding Lyα escape fraction when com-
paring other properties.
3. RESULTS
In this section, we first give the results of the physical
properties based on the SED fitting. We then constrain
the UV continua using the photometric data, estimate
Hα lines with the best-fit models from the SED fitting
and calculate their production eﬀiciency of ionizing pho-
tons. Next we measure their Lyα properties, including
the line width, offset to stellar counterpart, and escape
fraction. We list the results in Table 1. We compare the
potential relationship between them in Figure 3.
3.1. Physical Properties
To constrain and obtain the physical properties, we
perform SED fitting using BAGPIPES, a Python frame-
work for self-consistently modeling spectra and photom-
etry with galactic properties (Carnall et al. 2018). We
run the latest version with the pure Python Nautilus
nested sampling algorithm (Lange 2023). In the fitting,
we do not include the F090W data due to the Lyα line
strongly boosting this band. We do not also consider
the Lyα line in the posterior models of spectra. Flux of
this line is mainly up to magnitude of the corresponding
NB data as stated as follows.
We employ a constant star-forming history (SFH)
model to parametrize that of each source in the sam-
ple. We start to perform the SED fitting within a broad
parameter space. The age varies logarithmically from
0.1 Myr to the cosmic age at the redshift of each source
(∼1 Gyr). The formed mass is set in the logarithmic
range of 6 −11. The metallicity also varies logarithmi-
cally within the range of 0.001 and 10 times the solar
metallicity. The nebular emission has a Cloudy model
with an ionization parameter of −4 < log(U) < 0. As-
suming a Calzetti law (Calzetti et al. 2000) for dust
attenuation, AV (absolute attenuation in the V band)
is assigned to vary between 0 and 4 magnitudes.
The SED fitting results are shown with 1σ uncertainty
region in Figure 2(a). Derived physical properties (Age,
AV , and M∗) are listed in the Column 5, 6, and 7 of
Table 1 (the upper part), respectively. LAEs generally
tend to be young galaxies (e.g., Jiang et al. 2016; Iani
et al. 2024; Firestone et al. 2025), which is confirmed by
the output properties of the LAE sample in this work.
Strikingly, half of them are very young with ages smaller
than 10 Myr. Only one has an age exceeding 100 Myr.
For most of the LAEs in the sample, their small AV
show the low content of dust (AV < 1) while only one
has AV > 0.5. The median value of AV is 0.12. The
output M∗has a logarithmic range of ∼7.5 −8.5 and
a median of <8.0. Previous works show the luminous
LAEs, identified by the ground-based observations, have
a typical stellar mass range of log10M∗/M⊙≳8−9 (e.g.,
Jiang et al. 2016; Ouchi 2019; Ning et al. 2024). Now
with a series of JWST/NIRCam bands, especially the
medium band F410M covering the optical continuum,
M∗is better constrained and apparently reduced by a
factor of ∼0.5 dex. The results reveal that these lumi-
nous LAEs have a similar M∗range to the fainter LAEs
(for example, from Iani et al. 2024).

3.3.2. Lyα Offset from Stellar Counterpart
Recent JWST observations covering Lyα show the
non-negligible misalignment between Lyα emission and
stellar counterparts (Jiang et al. 2024; Napolitano et al.
2024; Ning et al. 2024). As shown by Figure 1, the stellar
counterparts of the LAEs revealed by JWST/NIRCam
do not locate at the corresponding Lyα centroid, the
center of the dashed circular aperture.
We have re-
ported such phenomenon of the misalignment between
Lyα emission and the stellar counterpart of luminous
LAEs in our previous work Ning et al. (2024). In this
work, we explore how such misalignment relates to sev-
eral properties. The Lyα and stellar centroids are de-
tected still based on the Subaru/Suprime NB images
and the JWST/NIRCam images, respectively. The posi-
tional distances are computed to obtain the Lyα-stellar
projected offsets ∆dLyα.
We follow the same method
and procedure as Ning et al. (2024, see the Section 3.4)
to measure and evaluate the corresponding errors.
The astrometric accuracy is first addressed. We use
the detected sources within a circle of 2.′0 diameter
and compare their positions in the JWST/NIRCam and
Suprime NB images. The systematic offsets are obtained
by computing the median and eliminated from ∆dLyα.
The astrometric precision is also addressed. We perform
a Monte Carlo simulation by inserting the mock LAEs
randomly in the NB images. We obtain the positional
error of source detections as a function of LAE bright-
ness. Fainter LAEs have larger measurement errors. For
each source in the sample, the error of ∆dLyα is gener-
ated by interpolating its NB photometric data to the
above magnitude-dependent relations. More details are
provided in the Section 3.4 of Ning et al. (2024).

4.2. Lyα Transfer and Escape
Our LAE sample exhibits non-negligible misalign-
ment between Lyα emission and stellar components.
We also reveal such phenomenon in our previous work
Ning et al. (2024).
We discussed a tentative rela-
tion between ∆dLyα and EW0(Lyα).
For luminous
LAEs, Lyα emission would displace farther with larger
EW0(Lyα). In this work, we constrain physical prop-
erties of the LAEs better with photometric data from
more JWST/NIRCam bands. We can explore whether
other properties determine ∆dLyα more effectively.
Unlike other emission lines such as Hα, Lyα suffer res-
onant scattering by neutral Hydrogen (H i) atoms and
passes longer routines before absorbed by dust (e.g., Di-
jkstra et al. 2011; Dijkstra 2014). Lyα emission thus dis-
tributes more complex in/around galaxies (e.g., Hayes
2015).
Besides gas turbulence or outflow altering the
pathways of Lyα emission (e.g., Rivera-Thorsen et al.
2015), possible low-opacity channels in the ISM/CGM,
driven by Supernova (SN) feedback (e.g., Kong et al.
2026) and/or AGN (e.g., Wang et al. 2024), may also
help Lyα and ionizing photons leaking (e.g., Markov
et al. 2025).
Lyα photons thus propagate inhomoge-
neously and anisotropically so that its overall distribu-
tion could misalign with the corresponding stellar pop-
ulation.
In Figure 5, we compare f Lyα
esc
and ∆dLyα with AV
and FWHM(Lyα), respectively. The plots shows that
both f Lyα
esc
and ∆dLyα are obviously suppressed by in-
creasing AV but much less influenced when changing
FWHM(Lyα).
The trends can be explained by the
mechanism of Lyα transfer process. AV quantitatively
measures the attenuation of dust content. The Lyα line
width due to Doppler or (multiple) scattering broaden-
ing, expressed by FWHM(Lyα) in this work, is directly
influenced by the density, temperature, and kinematic
state of the H i atoms (Dijkstra 2014).
In general, more H i would increase times of resonant
scattering and distances of propagation for Lyα photons,
which boosts velocity offset with respect to the systemic
redshift ∆νLyα and broadens the Lyα line width (e.g.,
Dijkstra et al. 2016; Prieto-Lyon et al. 2025). However,
H i gas content can not determine how much Lyα pho-
tons survive out of galaxies and how far they reach when
scattered to velocities where the gas is transparent. Al-
though literature works report broad H i (absorption)
velocity width results in lower f Lyα
esc , this anti-correlation
is demonstrated by a higher probability for Lyα photons
to be destroyed by dust grains when more likely residing
in the optically thick H i gas (e.g., Gazagnes et al. 2020).
If a galaxy contains more dust content, Lyα emission
would be less possible to escape and propagate far away
due to being absorbed by dust grains (Dijkstra 2014).
This trend is qualitatively represented by the dashed line
in the lower left panel. The dust content thus plays a
relatively dominant role in regulating Lyα transfer and
escape. When a galaxy has a smaller AV , its f Lyα
esc
could
be small due to high opacity in the anisotropic ISM dis-
tribution. Its ∆dLyα could also be small if Lyα is emit-
ted homogeneously and/or the (spatial) centroid locates
closed to the line of sight. The above demonstration can
be verified further with the ∆νLyα data measured from
the difference of observed wavelength between Lyα line
and any other optical lines such as [O iii], Hα and so on.
5. SUMMARY
In this work, we study a spectroscopically confirmed
sample of 14 luminous LAEs in terms of Lyα luminos-
ity (> 1042.6 erg s−1) over a large sky area.
In this
sample, each LAE is selected if associating with a single
star-forming galaxy. We utilize the imaging and spec-
troscopic datasets including Subaru/Suprime NB opti-
cal images, Magellan/M2FS multi-object fiber spectra,
and JWST/NIRCam IR images in six broad bands and
one medium band. By performing SED fitting, we con-
strain their physical properties (stellar mass, age, and
dust attenuation) and estimate flux of Hα lines.
We
also obtain the UV and Hα properties (UV slope and
ionizing photon production eﬀiciency), and Lyα proper-
ties (line width, Lyα offset, and escape fraction). This
work is summarized as follows:
• The
luminous
LAEs
in
the
sample
have
very high EW0(Lyα).
Most of them have

They are generally blue in an UV slope range of

• For our sample, these luminous LAEs are gener-

• Almost all LAEs in our sample clearly have recent

Resembling LyC leakers at high-z universe,
the luminous LAEs function as highly eﬀicient ion-

• The LAE sample reveals an non-negligible Lyα off-
set from the stellar population again. Compared
to Hydrogen gas, dust content can more effectively

and ∆dLyα.
This work exhibits strong potential to research high-z
objects utilizing public JWST data. Our study reveals
that the luminous LAEs tend to be UV-blue, low-mass,
and very young dwarf galaxies.
Their luminous Lyα
emission are generally attributed to both the bursty
star-formation activities and the large escape fraction
of Lyα photons.
As the extreme case of probable ef-
ficient ionizing engines, they are excellent subjects for
JWST spectroscopic follow-up observations, including
NIRSpec/IFU, to unravel the ISM conditions facilitat-
ing the escape of Lyα(/ionizing) photons at the end of
the EoR.
