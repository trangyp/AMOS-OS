A HOMOLOGICAL CHARACTERIZATION OF AF
GROUPOIDS
BENJAMIN STEINBERG
Abstract. An ample groupoid is said to be AF if it is a directed union
of compact open principal subgroupoids. In this paper, we provide a
complete homological characterization of these groupoids. Specifically,
we prove that an ample groupoid is AF if and only if it has homological
dimension zero. More generally, we characterize groupoids of homolog-
ical dimension zero over a unital ring R.
1. Introduction
An ample groupoid is AF (approximately finite) if it is a directed union
of compact open principal subgroupoids [4, 6, 7, 11, 14]. Note that the C∗-
algebra associated with an AF groupoid is itself an AF algebra. The signif-
icance of these groupoids was first recognized in the seminal work of Gior-
dano, Putnam, and Skau [7] on Cantor dynamical systems.
Foundational work of Matui [11] highlighted an important homological
feature: the homology of an AF groupoid with integer coefficients vanishes
in all dimensions n > 0, and the homology in degree 0 recovers the K0-group
of the C∗-algebra. This property was instrumental in Matui’s computation
of the homology for graph groupoids of finite graphs without sources.
In this paper, we prove that AF groupoids are characterized by a stronger
homological property, namely that their homology with coefficients in any
module vanishes in dimensions greater than 0.
Theorem A. An ample groupoid G is AF if and only if it is of homological
dimension 0.
More generally, the R-homological dimension of an ample groupoid G
with respect to a unital ring R is 0 if its homology with coefficients in any
RG-module vanishes in dimensions greater than 0. Theorem A is then the
special case R = Z of the following more general result.

R-homological dimension 0 if and only if the following two conditions hold:

2. Ample groupoids and their algebras
Following Bourbaki [2], we call a space X quasi-compact if each open
covering of X has a finite subcover and reserve the term compact for quasi-
compact Hausdorff spaces.
An ample groupoid is a topological groupoid G such that the unit space
G0 is locally compact, Hausdorff and totally disconnected and the source
map s: G →G0 is a local homeomorphism. We do not assume that G is
Hausdorff. The range map is denoted by r.
If X, Y ⊆G0, then GX = s−1(X), GY = r−1(Y ) and GY
X = GY ∩GX. An
element g ∈G is isotropy if s(g) = r(g). The group Gx
x is called the isotropy
group at x ∈G0. A group bundle is a groupoid consisting solely of isotropy.
The orbit of x ∈G0 is r(s−1(x)), i.e., it consists of those y ∈G0 with
an arrow from x to y. The orbit space of G is G0/R where R is the orbit
equivalence relation. A subset Y ⊆G0 is called full if it meets each orbit,
that is, r(GY ) = G0. A subset X ⊆G0 is invariant if it is a union of orbits.
A groupoid is principal if it has trivial isotropy. Note that a principal
ample groupoid is Hausdorff. Indeed, if g ̸= h ∈G, then either s(h) ̸= s(g)
or r(g) ̸= r(h). In the former case, we can choose disjoint neighborhoods
U, V of s(g) and s(h) in G0, and then s−1(U) and s−1(V ) give disjoint
neighborhoods of g, h. The same argument works if r(g) ̸= r(h).
An ample groupoid is said to be AF if it is a directed union of compact
open principal subgroupoids [6]. Note that an AF groupoid is principal and
hence Hausdorff. AF groupoids were extensively studied in [7,11,14].

use. The authors of [7] define an ample groupoid to be AF if it is principal,

A bisection of G is an open1 subset U such that s |U, r |U are injective. The
compact bisections form a basis for the topology on an ample groupoid G and
they also form an inverse semigroup under setwise product with pointwise
inverse for the involution. The reader is referred to [9] for inverse semigroups.
Following [17] we call an ample groupoid G uniformly bounded if there is
an integer M ≥1 with | r−1(x)| ≤M for all x ∈G0; note that M bounds the
size of each isotropy group and orbit of G. Every quasi-compact groupoid is
uniformly bounded [17, Lemma 3.3]. If G is a quasi-compact groupoid, the
unit space G0 = s(G) is compact.

(2) G is a directed union of uniformly bounded open subgroupoids.
