A note on connections between the F¨ollmer process and the
denoising diffusion probabilistic model
Yuta Koike *†
May 19, 2026
Abstract
The F¨ollmer process is a Brownian motion conditioned to have a pre-specified distribution at time
1. This process can be interpreted as an “augmented” time-compressed version of the reverse stochastic
differential equation (SDE) for the denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM). While this fact has
been indirectly used to analyze DDPM sampling errors via discretization of the reverse SDE, connections
between direct discretization of the F¨ollmer process and the DDPM sampler have not yet been fully ex-
plored. This note aims to clarify this point while surveying relevant results from existing work. We show
that discretized F¨ollmer processes give natural hyper-parameter settings of the DDPM sampler. More-
over, this allows us to systematically recover state-of-the-art results on DDPM sampling error bounds
with slight improvements.
Keywords: diffusion model, discretization, low-dimensional adaptation, sampling error, score-based gen-
erative modeling, stochastic localization.

Introduction
Let µ be a probability distribution on Rd. Following [17], we call a d-dimensional continuous stochastic
process X = (Xt)t∈[0,1] the F¨ollmer process associated with µ if X1 ∼µ and the conditional law of X
given X1 is the d-dimensional Brownian bridge from 0 to X1 on [0, 1]. This process has recently received
renewed attention in various fields of both theory and application such as studies of functional inequalities
[16, 17, 18, 36, 38, 45, 46] as well as high-dimensional central limit theorems [19, 21, 22], optimization
[9, 43], generative modeling [5, 59], sampling from probability distributions [20, 30, 55, 61] and Bayesian
inference [52, 57]. Historically, the F¨ollmer process appeared as the solution to an entropy minimization
problem posed by Schr¨odinger. A modern formulation of Schr¨odinger’s problem is as follows (see [39, 44]
for more details). Let Wd := C([0, 1], Rd) be the space of continuous functions from [0, 1] to Rd equipped
with the uniform topology. For a probability distribution Q on Wd, we denote by Q0 and Q1 the image
measures of Q by the maps w 7→w(0) and w 7→w(1), respectively. Then, given a pair (µ0, µ1) of
probability distributions on Rd and a probability distribution R on Wd, the dynamical Schr¨odinger problem
associated with the reference measure R is the following minimization problem with respect to probability
