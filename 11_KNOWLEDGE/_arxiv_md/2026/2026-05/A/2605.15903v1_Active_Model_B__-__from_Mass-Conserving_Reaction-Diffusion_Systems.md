We show that the late-time dynamics of a minimal three-component mass-conserving reaction–
diffusion system reduce to a scalar active field theory, Active Model B−(AMB−), in which a
density-dependent interfacial coefficient κ(ϕ) turns negative at high density. This drives a finite-
wavelength instability and stabilises microphase-separated patterns, in contrast to the unbounded
coarsening of two-component mass-conserving systems. Unlike Active Model B+, AMB−retains a
chemical potential that remains a state function, inherited from the underlying conservation law,
but admits no equation of state for the pressure.
Protein
pattern
formation
under
mass
conserva-
tion is a fundamental mechanism of intracellular self-
organisation [1–3] One paradigmatic example is the Min
system of E. coli, where MinD and MinE proteins un-
dergo an ATP-driven membrane-binding cycle that po-
sitions the division machinery at mid-cell [4]. In vitro,
Min proteins form a remarkable diversity of self-organised
patterns, including travelling waves and spirals [5]; mush-
rooms, amoebas, and bursts [6]; and quasi-stationary
dots, stripes, and foam-like meshes [7]; with no progres-
sive coarsening over experimental timescales. Explain-
ing wavelength and pattern selection within a strictly
mass-conserving framework remains a central theoretical
challenge.
Mass-conserving reaction–diffusion (McRD)
systems provide the minimal bottom-up description of
pattern formation under a global conservation law [1, 8–
12]. Two-component systems generically coarsen without
bound [13–17], with long-wavelength dynamics equiva-
lent to Cahn–Hilliard or conserved Allen–Cahn flows [16],
driven by mass redistribution between domains indepen-
dently of reaction kinetics [15]. Finite-wavelength selec-
tion has been recovered along two routes within McRD:
weak breaking of mass conservation, where a unified
mesoscopic mechanism is known [15, 16, 18, 19], and ad-
dition of a third reactive species, where the phenomenol-
ogy has been demonstrated case by case [20–22].
A
mechanism-level account of the second route, analogous
to that of the first, is missing.
A complementary top-down perspective on driven self-
organisation is provided by scalar active field theories
with a conserved order parameter [23].
The mini-
mal extension of Cahn–Hilliard dynamics by a nonequi-
librium contribution to the chemical potential, Active
Model B [24], generically coarsens to bulk phase sepa-
ration. Finite-wavelength selection can be recovered by
coupling Cahn–Hilliard dynamics to chemical reactions
that weakly break the conservation of the order parame-
ter [25, 26] , or, while preserving conservation, by adding
a non-gradient current as in Active Model B+ [27], which
reverses Ostwald ripening and stabilises microphase sep-
aration. Active Brownian particles establish one micro-
scopic route to such field theories [28], and their nonequi-
librium structure is well understood [23, 29, 30]. Whether
mass-conserving biochemical reaction networks, the nat-
ural setting for intracellular pattern formation, give rise
to active-field-theory dynamics is largely unexplored.
Both questions admit a common answer. We consider
a minimal three-component mass-conserving reaction-
diffusion model in which an inactive cytosolic reservoir
is coupled to the active dynamics by a reactivation rate
ν, motivated by nucleotide exchange in ATPase cycles.
Adiabatic elimination of the fast variables yields a closed
equation for the conserved total density ϕ. In the fast-
reactivation limit (ν →∞) this recovers passive Cahn–
Hilliard dynamics; in the slow reactivation limit (ν →0)
it maps to an activator–substrate model [31, 32], while
at finite ν it gives a distinct active scalar field theory,
Active Model B−(AMB−, Eq. (4)), a minimal active
field theory which supports finite-length instability. In
this theory, activity enters through a density-dependent
interfacial coefficient κ(ϕ) that becomes negative at high
density and explains the finite-wavelength selection ob-
served in the three-component model. Crucially, this is
achieved within a purely gradient-current structure: un-
like AMB+, which recovers wavelength selection by intro-
ducing a non-gradient active current, AMB−preserves
the gradient form of the current and locates activity en-
tirely in κ(ϕ). The reactivation rate ν interpolates con-
tinuously between regimes where patterns coarsen akin to
the Cahn–Hilliard model, and a regime of stable micro-
phase-separated patterns (dots, stripes, foams) in agree-
ment with Min system phenomenology.

Discussion & Outlook.
AMB−is the minimal active
scalar field theory showing microphase separation that
admits a chemical potential but no equation of state. It
arises as the single-field projection of a mass-conserving
reaction–diffusion (McRD) system.
The mapping pro-
vides an explicit microscopic origin for the active con-
tribution λ(∂xϕ)2 and the density-dependent interfacial
coefficient κ(ϕ) — which can become negative at high
density.
The same structural absence of an equation
of state characterises multi-component McRD, where
reaction–turnover balance is profile-dependent while the
mass-redistribution potential is constant at steady state.
This shared structure produces a shared phenomenol-
ogy:
macrophase separation, droplet coexistence, and
wavelength-selected microphases (stripes, dots, foam)
closely related to patterns of the Min protein system [1].
The mapping also raises two open questions.
The
profile-dependent ηstat controls pattern selection in 1D
and is expected to do so in higher dimensions and for
multi-wavelength coexistence; whether projection of the
well-characterised McRD noise yields a controlled noise
prescription for AMB−is the second, an issue so far
treated phenomenologically in scalar active field theories.
More broadly, our results take a step toward unifying the
languages of reaction–diffusion and active matter.
