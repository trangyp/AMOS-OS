Introduction
Under canonical ELECTRA cross-entropy fine-
tuning, 33.5% of NYT formal human articles cross
the AI decision threshold (threshold τ = 0.5); the
top decile is scored at P(AI) ≥0.992 more con-
fidently AI than the average output from any of
seven LLM families. Per-family means under the
same detector are LLaMA-2-7B 0.71, Qwen 0.71,
Gemini 0.85, GPT-4 0.85, Claude 0.86, GPT-4o
0.87, and LLaMA-3 0.90; the formal human arti-
cles sit above all seven (Kuznetsov et al., 2024).
This is the gap between what AI-text detectors are
designed to do and what they actually rank: under
the canonical training recipe, fluent formal humans
are flagged at higher confidence than the median
output of every modern instruction-tuned model.
We argue that the 33.5% figure is not a calibra-
tion bug to be patched but a direct prediction of
what fine-tuning a binary classifier on top of a pre-
trained encoder does to the geometry the encoder
already has: fine-tuned AI text detectors do not
construct an AI-vs-human boundary, they amplify
a pretrained typicality axis. On that reading, fluent
formal humans score above the median AI not be-
cause the detector misclassified them but because
they sit further along the pretrained typicality di-
rection than the HC3 AI population the detector
was trained against, and every standard fine-tuning
recipe (canonical cross-entropy, CC-loss, dealign-
f2c, LoRA, full fine-tuning) calibrates the same
fixed axis. The remainder of the paper establishes
this in three pillars (§§2, 3, 5) plus a methodolog-
ical contribution (§4): only an axis-manipulating
intervention can close the bias gap, because no
thresholding choice on the shared axis can.
We organise this paper around three pillars of
evidence for that claim and a single methodological
contribution: §2 establishes the pretrained typical-
ity axis directly; §3 shows a 24-example frozen-
encoder probe matches full fine-tuning; §4 devel-
ops the closed-form per-text predictor that is the
paper’s methodological contribution and transfers
to three deployed third-party RoBERTa detectors
at 16/16 oracle-equivalence (reducing the OpenAI
detector’s NYT-FPR by 57%); §5 shows three op-

Convention.
We report all detector comparisons

the same way.

Discussion
Returning to the 33.5% NYT-formal FPR from
§1: that number is a calibration of the pretrained
typicality axis, not a learned classification of AI
text every load-bearing detector phenomenon in
this paper follows from the single fact that fine-
tuning amplifies what pretraining already encodes.


## Page 10


The shared axis is read three ways (§2, §3, §5)
and parameterised in closed form (§4); the four
findings reduce to one statement and the central
claim’s most predictive consequence is the asym-
metry of §5 against §4. Because the variant zoo
is calibration-equivalent on the axis, no threshold-
ing choice on that axis can move the bias only the
closed-form rank-1 intervention of §4 can, and it
does so on the canonical detector by lifting deploy-
ment TPR from 0.000 to 0.904 at FPR = 1%.
What we describe in this paper is not a debiasing
method that happens to work; it is the only category
of intervention we have evidence for on the shared
axis, because every variant in the evaluated zoo
(canonical CE, CC-loss, dealign-f2c, LoRA, full-
FT) recalibrates rather than reconstructs. The prac-
titioner consequence is direct: AI-text detectors
currently deployed against fluent formal-register
populations are calibrating a pretrained typicality
axis these populations sit on the AI side of, and
variant-shopping across the standard recipe zoo
will not move them — the matched-TPR equiva-
lence of §5 closes that route off-the-shelf. What
can move them is the axis-manipulating interven-
tion parameterised by the §4 closed-form predictor,
which lifts deployment TPR from 0.000 to 0.904
on the canonical detector and transfers to three
third-party deployed detectors without retraining.
The same per-text Jacobian that parameterises
fairness intervention also parameterises attack cost:
in preliminary results deferred to future work,
predictor-selected ε-to-flip correlates with mea-
sured per-text adversarial robustness at Spearman
0.97. Three open questions follow from this duality.
First, the central claim is population/AUROC-level:
per-text mechanisms within the shared axis differ
across architectures (Limitations), so the shared
axis at population level does not imply per-text
mechanism universality. Second, the architectural
typology that organises four scattered RoBERTa
anomalies under one prediction (Limitations) re-
mains an observation at four backbones; whether it
is causally driven by pretraining objective, model
size, or pretraining data is open. Third, the mech-
anism magnitude we report is HC3-anchored: the
recipe generalises cross-data while the magnitude
does not (Limitations). The same closed-form pre-
dictor that flags which texts are easy to debias
should, by symmetry, flag which texts are easy to
attack; we leave the attack-cost reading for future
work.
Limitations
Our central claim is population/AUROC-level;
within the shared typicality axis, three architec-
tures route per-text variance through three differ-
ent surface features.
On the NYT-vs-HC3 an-
chor, raw projection inheritance of fine-tuned dis-
crimination is universal at the pool level, but the
per-text Baron-Kenny mediation test passes 3/3
seeds on ELECTRA and 0/3 seeds on RoBERTa-
base and DeBERTa-v3 the per-text mediation fram-
ing scopes to ELECTRA only.
The underly-
ing per-text features differ: ELECTRA couples
to function-word density (Cohen’s d = 1.49
on top/bot NYT deciles), RoBERTa-base to av-
erage sentence length (partial Pearson r(∆asl |
∆fwr) = +0.519 ± 0.038), and DeBERTa-v3 to
a near-degenerate geometric subspace (effective
rank 4.72 vs 6.55/6.15 on ELECTRA / RoBERTa-
base). Cross-architecture Spearman correlation per-
text is only +0.23 on the strongest pair (ELEC-
TRA, RoBERTa-base) with top-decile overlap
20%; ρ(DeBERTa, RoBERTa) = −0.14. The
within-class regression of fine-tuned P(AI) on raw
projection (§2.4) is ELECTRA-strongest: r =
+0.586/+0.481/+0.324 across the three architec-
tures. Our central claim scopes to population-level
shared axis; we do not claim per-text mechanism
universality.
Four findings that initially read as scattered
RoBERTa anomalies — fine-tuning-below-raw,
RoBERTa-base-L10 sign-flip, warm-RoBERTa-
family deployment failure, and RoBERTa-large
fine-tuning reversal cluster on the stochastic-wh
side of a 2 × 2 architectural typology and reduce
to one prediction. {ELECTRA, DeBERTa} share
one geometric regime (cross-seed cosine of wh is
0.998/0.972); {RoBERTa-base, RoBERTa-large}
share another (stochastic, 0.072/0.018, with no
observed values between 0.07 and 0.97 in our four-
backbone panel). The two regimes are orthogo-
nally crossed with an acquired-1D vs inherited-
1D split ({ELECTRA, RoBERTa-base} acquired;
{DeBERTa, RoBERTa-large} inherited). On the
“stochastic wh” side we observe: §2’s RoBERTa-
base fine-tuning-below-raw at NYT and Ghost-
buster; §4’s RoBERTa-base anti-rescue under
dtyp_HC3 ablation (scope-corrected to RoBERTa-
base-L10-specific, not architecturally invariant);
§6’s two warm-RoBERTa-family deployment fail-
ures (∥dtyp_NYT∥< 5 separator). Four scope-
flags reduce to one cross-architecture prediction.
