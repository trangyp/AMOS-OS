Adaptive 3D-RoPE: Physics-Aligned Rotary Positional Encoding for
Wireless Foundation Models
Chenyu Zhang, Xinchen Lyu, Chenshan Ren, Shuhan Liu, and Qimei Cui
Abstract—Positional encoding plays a pivotal role in determin-
ing the extrapolation and generalization performance of wireless
foundation models for channel state information (CSI) modeling,
latent characterization, and task-specific prediction. However,
existing CSI models inherit static or one-dimensional positional
priors from natural language and vision architectures, which
fundamentally misalign with the intrinsic physics of wireless
channels by lacking explicit relative decay, collapsing the 3D
spatio-temporal-frequency structure, and remaining scenario-
rigid. This paper proposes Adaptive 3D-RoPE, a physics-aligned
rotary positional encoding that establishes the structural corner-
stone for wireless foundation models. The framework integrates
a learnable, axis-decoupled 3D frequency bank to explicitly
disentangle multi-dimensional phase dependencies, coupled with
a lightweight channel-conditioned controller that dynamically
modulates the prior via compact global CSI descriptors. This
sample-adaptive mechanism transforms positional encoding from
a static transformer component into a dynamic, coherence-aware
inductive bias to resolve heterogeneous channel physics. Extensive
experiments across 100 datasets demonstrate the superiority of
the proposed scheme in both scale extrapolation and zero-shot
generalization. Compared to the state-of-the-art, our method
achieves up to a 10.7 dB reduction in normalized mean square
error (NMSE) under 8× antenna scale extrapolation. Given the
same CSI input scales, our method can also improve zero-shot
NMSE by 1.07 dB across unseen mobility scenarios and 0.90 dB
in low-frequency-to-millimeter-wave tasks.
Index Terms—Wireless foundation model, rotary positional
encoding, CSI prediction

A. Motivation: Positional Encoding vs. Channel Coherence

a structural prior that governs how self-attention interactions

scenario-dependent dynamics, conventional positional priors

B. Contribution and Paper Organization
To resolve the mismatch between attention mechanics and
channel physics, this paper proposes Adaptive 3D-RoPE, a
channel-coherent positional framework specifically engineered
for heterogeneous CSI. Departing from fixed or globally
optimized schedules, the proposed design couples a multi-axis
learnable spectral manifold that preserves the native temporal-
frequency-spatial layout with a compact, sample-level mod-
ulation mechanism. This architecture dynamically aligns the
effective attention geometry with the instantaneous coherence
state of each input instance, thereby elevating positional mod-
eling from a rigid preprocessing tag to a continuous, physics-
guided inductive process. By ensuring that the rotary prior
intrinsically tracks channel variations, our method establishes
a robust foundation for reliable scale extrapolation and zero-
shot transfer across diverse mobility and frequency regimes.
The key contributions are as follows:
• We reveal the fundamental limitations of existing posi-
tional priors in wireless foundation models. By bridg-
ing empirical channel coherence with the relative decay
mechanisms of self-attention, we analyze why inherited
1D-APE, 1D-RoPE, and static 3D designs are inherently
ill-equipped to handle heterogeneous CSI.
• We establish three foundational design principles for CSI-
oriented positional encoding: pure relative decay, explicit
structural integrity across the temporal, frequency, and
spatial dimensions, and physics alignment with highly
dynamic channel conditions.
• We develop Adaptive 3D-RoPE, a physics-aligned 3D ro-
tary positional encoding scheme for heterogeneous CSI. It
combines an axis-decoupled learnable 3D base frequency
bank with a lightweight adaptive controller, allowing the
rotary prior to evolve from fixed to learnable and further
to sample-adaptive across different dimensions.
Extensive experiments on diverse datasets generated via
QuaDRiGa demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed
scheme from two perspectives: (1) Scale Extrapolation gen-
eralization: in antenna extrapolation at 8× the training array
scale, Adaptive 3D-RoPE improves NMSE by about 3.1 dB
over learnable 3D-RoPE at the largest antenna-extrapolation
regime, and the best observed gain over fixed 3D-RoPE
reaches 10.7 dB at one antenna-extrapolation operating point;
and (2) Cross-Band Transfer and Mobility Generalization:
under matched input scales but unseen mobility/scenario con-
ditions, it achieves up to 1.07 dB NMSE reduction over fixed
3D-RoPE in speed/scenario zero-shot generalization, while in
low-frequency-to-mmWave transfer it attains up to 0.90 dB
zero-shot NMSE reduction. The source code is available at
the GitHub repository 1.

II. RELATED WORK
This section first reviews the evolution of positional encod-
ing mechanisms, particularly RoPE and its multi-dimensional
extensions, and identifies a fundamental modality gap when
applying them to 3D CSI. We then revisit CSI prediction, from
conventional estimators to wireless foundation models, where
this positional misalignment emerges as a critical bottleneck,
motivating an adaptive 3D-RoPE design for heterogeneous
wireless channels.
A. Evolution of Position Encodings
1) From Absolute to Relative Encodings: Early transform-
ers mainly adopted APE, where fixed sinusoidal functions
or learnable vectors are used to inject absolute coordinates
into token embeddings [4]. Although simple and effective,
absolute coordinates are tied to the observed sequence layout
and therefore provide limited length extrapolation and trans-
lation invariance. RPE [5] addresses this issue by explicitly
parameterizing relative token distances, and related relative-
bias variants have been adopted in transformer architectures
such as T5 [6]. Long-context studies further improve extrap-
olation through designs such as ALiBi [25] and xPos [26].
However, these methods are primarily designed for generic
sequence modeling and do not directly capture the structured
3D temporal-frequency-spatial dependencies of CSI.
2) Rotary Position Embedding: RoPE [7] provides a rotary
formulation of relative positional encoding. It applies absolute-
position-dependent rotations to query and key vectors, while
inducing relative-position-dependent attention scores through
their inner product. This multiplicative phase mechanism
preserves the relative-offset structure without introducing an
explicit additive bias. Recent studies have analyzed why rotary
encodings are effective and how their frequency allocation
affects extrapolation behavior [27], [28]. Owing to its compact
formulation and favorable length extrapolation properties [29],
[30], standard 1D-RoPE has become a widely used positional
encoding in modern sequence modeling.

3) Multi-Dimensional RoPE in Vision and 3D Domains:
Motivated by the success of RoPE in 1D sequences, recent
works have extended rotary encodings to structured visual
and geometric data. In computer vision, 2D-RoPE [8] applies
independent rotations along image height and width to im-
prove high-resolution extrapolation. In multimodal and video
modeling, MRoPE [31], VideoRoPE [9], VRoPE [32], and
HoPE [33] redesign spatial-temporal frequency allocation or
combine rotary and hybrid positional strategies. In 3D point-
cloud and volumetric domains, LieRE [34] and STRING [10]
exploit geometric structures to represent complex spatial re-
lationships. However, these designs are tailored to visual or
multimodal data, leaving the axis-specific physical semantics
and anisotropic coherence properties of 3D CSI unaddressed.
4) Dynamic Positional Encodings: Beyond fixed or glob-
ally learnable encodings, recent NLP studies have explored
adaptive rotary strategies for long-context modeling. Positional
Interpolation [29] rescales position indices to reuse pretrained
RoPE beyond the original context length, YaRN [30] im-
proves long-context extension through rotary rescaling and
fine-tuning, and LongRoPE [35] introduces non-uniform in-
terpolation for extreme context expansion. More recently,
CARoPE [36] generates context-aware rotary embeddings
from token representations. While these techniques demon-
strate the value of adapting positional priors, their adaptation
is mainly designed for 1D text sequences and long-context
extension. However, a channel-conditioned mechanism for
modulating axis-decoupled 3D rotary frequencies according to
the coherence state of each CSI sample remains unexplored.
B. Deep Learning and Wireless Foundation Models
1) Conventional Estimators and Task-Specific Models:
Traditional CSI acquisition and prediction typically rely on
analytical channel models and classical estimators. For CSI
acquisition, least-squares (LS) [37] and minimum mean square
error (MMSE) [38] remain standard baselines, while for tem-
poral prediction Kalman filtering [39] is a representative classi-
cal approach. While effective in stationary environments, these
methods struggle to capture the highly nonlinear dynamics
of modern massive MIMO systems. To address these limita-
tions, data-driven channel predictors based on deep learning
and machine learning have been widely deployed, including
recurrent [40], massive-MIMO channel-aging prediction [41],
and transformer-based CSI prediction or feedback enhance-
ment architectures [42], [43]. Nevertheless, these task-specific
models generally exhibit poor cross-scenario generalization,
frequently failing when evaluated on out-of-distribution fre-
quencies, array sizes, or mobility patterns.
2) Emergence of Wireless Foundation Models: Recently,
the paradigm has shifted toward wireless foundation mod-
els, which aim to learn transferable representations of radio
propagation across tasks and deployment conditions [44].
One line of research adapts pretrained models from mature
AI domains to wireless tasks. For example, LLM4CP [11]
transfers a language-model backbone to channel prediction,
LVM4CSI [15] leverages pretrained vision transformers for
CSI tasks, and semantically aligned transfer frameworks
bridge wireless data with pretrained LLM representations [45].
While these methods benefit from large-scale external pretrain-
ing, their modality and positional priors are still inherited from
text or image domains.
Another line develops wireless-specific foundation mod-
els directly from CSI data, including WiFo [19], Wireless-
GPT [12], ChannelGPT [13], BERT4MIMO [14], and CSI
feedback frameworks [16], [17]. Compared with backbone
adaptation, these models better match wireless data and
support broader CSI prediction, reconstruction, or feedback
tasks. Nevertheless, most existing designs still rely on static
positional encodings, leaving positional modeling as a key
bottleneck for generalization across heterogeneous array, tem-
poral, and frequency configurations.
3) The Modality Gap in Positional Representation: Despite
their strong generative and predictive capabilities, wireless
foundation models are inherently constrained by existing posi-
tional encoding mechanisms. Most CSI models still adopt 1D
positional encodings inherited from language or vision back-
bones, which are poorly aligned with the multi-dimensional
structure of radio propagation. WiFo [19] advances this di-
rection by using 3D-APE to preserve spatial, temporal, and
frequency coordinates, while LWM-Temporal [20] introduces
1D-RoPE into wireless channel pretraining. However, 3D-APE
is still an absolute and static prior, whereas 1D-RoPE models
relative phase interactions only along a flattened token order.
Consequently, existing designs fail to simultaneously support
score-level relative interaction, explicit temporal-frequency-
spatial decoupling, and sample-wise adaptation to heteroge-
neous channel coherence. This gap is especially critical for 3D
CSI, as its multidimensional coherence properties are highly
sensitive to environmental dynamics.

establish their correspondence at the relative-offset level to
derive the architectural principles of our framework.

IV. PROPOSED ADAPTIVE 3D-ROPE ENCODING SCHEME
This section details the proposed Adaptive 3D-RoPE ar-
chitecture. We begin by analyzing the structural mismatch
between empirical CSI coherence and inherited positional
priors to establish our design principles. Following this, we
present the overall framework, which encompasses the axis-
decoupled learnable 3D base frequency bank and the channel-
conditioned adaptive controller.
A. Design Analysis and Empirical Diagnosis
As mathematically and visually summarized in Fig. 1, APE
and RoPE affect attention scores through fundamentally dis-
tinct mechanisms. For heterogeneous CSI, the ideal positional
prior must organize self-attention strictly by relative temporal,
frequency, and spatial offsets while dynamically adapting to
the current channel coherence. By inspecting the interaction
formulas ⟨qm, kn⟩explicitly outlined in Fig. 1, we can
systematically diagnose why existing priors fail. First, APE
mixes absolute coordinates via addition (xm+pm), inherently
entangling position with channel content prior to the inner
product. Second, while RoPE resolves this by modulating
interactions via a relative rotation matrix R, standard 1D-
RoPE collapses the 3D geometry into a flat metric (n−m), and
static 3D-RoPE fails to adapt its phase frequencies to dynamic
coherence. We detail these structural bottlenecks from two
aspects: the score-level failure of APE and the geometric
rigidity of static 3D-RoPE.

is first added to the token embedding and then mixed by the
query and key projections. The attention score between two

product of the corresponding projected query and key vectors,

3) Derived Design Principles for CSI Positional Priors:
The preceding diagnosis leads to three design requirements for
CSI positional modeling. Pure Relative Decay: the induced
attention prior should depend on relative offsets rather than
absolute coordinates. Structural Integrity: the temporal, fre-
quency, and spatial axes should remain explicitly decoupled
instead of being collapsed into a shared 1D ordering. Physics
Alignment: the rotary geometry should adapt to channel-
dependent coherence variations.
These principles directly motivate the proposed Adaptive
3D-RoPE architecture. To guarantee pure relative decay and
structural integrity, we replace conventional 1D positional
indexing with a continuous, axis-decoupled 3D base fre-
quency bank. To achieve physics alignment, this globally
learnable prior is further modulated by a lightweight, channel-
conditioned controller, enabling sample-wise adaptation for
each CSI instance.

negligible overhead to fundamentally break the bottleneck of
static priors.
V. EXPERIMENTS
This section evaluates the effectiveness of Adaptive 3D-
RoPE under complementary generalization settings. We first
examine zero-shot scale extrapolation to assess its robust-
ness to unseen antenna, temporal, and frequency dimensions.
We then investigate cross-band transfer and zero-shot mobil-
ity/scenario generalization under matched input scales, further
testing its robustness beyond the pretraining distribution.

complexity of the channel. For instance, the NMSE reduction
in the highly dynamic UMi scenario rises sharply from 0.19
dB in the 0–3 km/h range to 1.07 dB in the extreme 120–300
km/h regimes, with similarly aggressive growth trajectories ob-
served in UMa and Indoor scenarios. This behavior rigorously
corroborates our core design intuition: as the propagation en-
vironment becomes more challenging and temporal coherence
decays more rapidly, a dynamic, sample-adaptive rotary prior
becomes decisively superior to a fixed positional prior, even
in the complete absence of target-domain data.
VI. CONCLUSION
This paper establishes that positional encoding is not a
peripheral transformer component but a fundamental archi-

attention operates over permutation-equivariant tokens, and
remains inherently blind to the structured geometry of wire-
