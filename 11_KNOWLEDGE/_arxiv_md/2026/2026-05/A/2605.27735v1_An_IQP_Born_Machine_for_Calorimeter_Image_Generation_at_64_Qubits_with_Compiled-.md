An IQP Born Machine for Calorimeter Image Generation at 64 Qubits with
Compiled-IQP Deployment

We train an instantaneous quantum polynomial-time (IQP) Born machine on real high-energy-
physics calorimeter shower images at 64 qubits and compile the trained model into a single sampling-
hard IQP circuit for quantum deployment. The pipeline has three components: a Mixture-of-IQP
(MoIQP) architecture, whose Walsh-diagonal MMD2 loss is classically trainable by Van den Nest
Fourier Monte Carlo; the Pearson-Stabilized Correlation Kernel (PSCK), a positive-definite MMD
kernel that biases descent toward correlation-sensitive directions through a data-evaluated Jacobian
of the empirical Pearson matrix; and an exact deferred-measurement compilation of MoIQP into
a single IQP circuit on n + ⌈log2 L⌉qubits (cIQP). Across five seeds at L = 8, 1500 epochs, the
model reaches MAEρ = 0.069±0.008 against a 0.052 encoding-fidelity floor on the training split and
0.071 ± 0.008 on a held-out test split, versus a Liu–Wang baseline at MAEρ = 0.100. The compiled
cIQP reproduces the MoIQP marginal to 0.591 ± 0.012 times the Monte Carlo noise floor.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Calorimeter shower simulation is one of the more de-
manding parts of the LHC analysis pipeline and is pro-
jected to consume millions of CPU-years per year during
the high-luminosity run [1, 2]. Classical generative sur-
rogates have made a dent in this over the last five years:
GANs [3, 4], normalizing flows [5–7], and diffusion mod-
els [8, 9] reach near-Geant4 fidelity with two to three
orders of magnitude speed-up, and several are already in
experiment software stacks. Quantum generative models
have been proposed as a further alternative [10–13], the
motivation being that the Born-rule probability repre-
sentation can express certain correlation structures with
fewer parameters than a classical parameterization [14].
The obstacle to scaling quantum generative models on
near-term hardware is that variational training of generic
circuit Born machines requires repeated quantum sam-
pling and is prone to barren plateaus [15, 16]. A use-
ful way around this was opened by restricting the model
class to instantaneous quantum polynomial-time (IQP)
circuits [17–19]. Two results make this class well suited to
scalable generative modeling. Van den Nest [20] showed
that expectation values of Pauli-Z words at the output
of an IQP circuit can be estimated classically in time
linear in the number of qubits and gates by a Fourier
Monte Carlo algorithm.
Rudolph et al. [21] then ob-
served that the MMD2 loss with a Walsh-diagonal kernel
decomposes as a mixture of such Pauli-Z expectations.
Together these give a fully classical training procedure
for IQP Born machines. Sampling from the trained cir-
cuit, on the other hand, is believed to be classically in-
tractable [18, 27], which is the point of the train on clas-
sical, deploy on quantum workflow [22].

That workflow has been explored recently on synthetic
and biological data. Recio-Armengol et al. [22] trained
models up to 1000 qubits in classical simulation. Lerch et
al. [23] trained 150-qubit models on genomic data with a
data-dependent initialization scheme. Ball´o-Gimbernat
et al. [24] ran trained shallow-IQP graph generators on
superconducting hardware at up to 153 qubits. In high-
energy physics the situation is different. Prior IQP and
generic quantum-circuit Born-machine work has stayed
at 8 to 12 qubits, typically modeling joint distributions
of two or three observables [10, 11]. We are not aware of
a demonstration that combines a real HEP imaging task
with the roughly 102-qubit scale needed for non-trivial
spatial granularity.
A second point worth flagging is that all existing IQP-
MMD work uses the Liu and Wang heat kernel [26], a
bandwidth-mixed Walsh-diagonal Gaussian that spreads
its Fourier weight fairly evenly across low-order Pauli-Z
correlators. That uniform weighting is not ideal when
the signal of physical interest is concentrated in pair-
wise correlation structure, as it is for calorimeter showers,
where the spatial energy-correlation pattern is what sep-
arates electromagnetic from hadronic showers and signal
from background. In our hands the Liu and Wang base-
line saturates at a correlation reconstruction error about
twice the encoding-fidelity floor and shows a systematic
∼30% amplitude compression on recovered Pearson cor-
relations.
This paper presents a method to train IQP Born ma-
chines on real HEP non-binary data at scale; Fig. 1
summarizes the pipeline. Our method first proposes a
Mixture-of-IQP (MoIQP) architecture in which L IQP
components share a fixed Erd˝os–R´enyi gate graph but
have independent trainable angles and are mixed uni-
formly at the output. Because mixture Z-correlators are
linear in the per-component Z-correlators, the Van den
Nest training machinery carries over with no changes.

E.
Classical trainability and sampling hardness
Since J acts only on the residual vector δz ∈RK and
not on the individual Z-correlators, the additional gra-
dient terms from the rank-P correction are classically
computable in time O(P · K) once the ⟨Zβ⟩θ values have
been obtained by the Van den Nest Monte Carlo estima-
tor. Three properties of the underlying Liu–Wang con-
struction are preserved.
• Classical trainability — the gradient ∂LPSCK/∂θG
needs only the same Van den Nest Monte Carlo
evaluations as Liu–Wang training, followed by a
fixed K × K matrix multiplication.
• Sampling hardness — the kernel affects only the
loss landscape, not the deployed circuit, so the
trained model is still IQP (or, after compilation,
cIQP) and its sampling complexity is unchanged.
• Positive-definiteness — at our chosen η = 5 the
condition number of KPSCK is ∼29 at n = 64,
comfortably within a numerically stable regime.

VI.
DISCUSSION
A.
Scope
Three main results come out of the work. First, IQP
Born machines can be trained on real HEP calorimeter
image data at 64 qubits.
With the PSCK kernel the
model reaches within 0.017 of the encoding-fidelity floor
on pairwise correlation reconstruction, averaged across
five independent seeds at L = 8, 1500 epochs. As far as
we are aware this is the largest qubit count at which an
IQP Born machine has been fit to real HEP data. Second,
the deferred-measurement cIQP compilation is exact to
sub-Monte-Carlo precision and consistent across training
seeds. It provides an explicit quantum-deployment path
for the trained MoIQP whose joint sampling hardness
follows from the standard Bremner–Jozsa–Shepherd ar-
gument on the compiled circuit. Third, neither PSCK
nor the Liu and Wang baseline has a barren plateau in
the 16–64-qubit regime we study, at small-perturbation
initialization and on sparse Erd˝os–R´enyi graph connec-
tivity.
B.

2 Z-
correlators, so it cannot constrain higher cumulants of in-
dividual feature marginals. On the three calorimeter cells
with strongly non-Gaussian distributions, f0 through f2,
the residual W1 is about 20 integer levels out of 256.

VII.
CONCLUSION
We have trained an IQP Born machine on real HEP
calorimeter image data at 64 qubits.
Three pieces go
into the result. The MoIQP mixture architecture widens
the IQP model class without breaking classical train-
ability.
The PSCK kernel adds a rank-P Pearson-
Jacobian correction to the heat-kernel baseline and re-
covers correlation amplitudes that the baseline cannot.
The cIQP compilation maps the trained MoIQP to a
single IQP circuit on n + ⌈log2 L⌉qubits for quantum
deployment. Across five independent training seeds at
L = 8, 1500 epochs the model reaches training-split
MAEρ = 0.069 ± 0.008 against a 0.052 floor and test-
split MAEρ = 0.071 ± 0.008 against a 0.055 floor, with a
five-seed train-test gap of 0.0023±0.0006 that sits below
the encoding-floor train-test gap of 0.0033. Per-feature
distributional metrics, computed exactly through Walsh–
Hadamard inversion of intra-feature Z-correlators with-
out sampling, split the eight calorimeter cells cleanly into
a near-Gaussian set captured at the single-level scale and
a heavy-tailed set whose mismatch traces directly to the
absence of weight ≥3 observables in the training objec-
tive. A gradient scan from 16 to 64 qubits rules out expo-
nential gradient decay for both PSCK and the Liu and
Wang baseline in the regime we study, and cIQP deploy-
ment is verified to sub-Monte-Carlo precision across all
five seeds. The PSCK construction is general: any phys-
ically motivated downstream functional with a closed-
form Jacobian on the model marginals fits the same tem-
plate.

Appendix A: Walsh–Hadamard compilation of
MoIQP into a single IQP circuit on n + a qubits
This appendix gives the full derivation of the cIQP
construction of Sec. III B and Fig. 4. The argument has
four parts:
1. the controlled-IQP unitary as a product of ancilla-
indexed Pauli-Z rotations;
2. the inverse-WHT relation between base angles and
compiled angles;
3. the deferred-measurement equivalence to MoIQP
through a Walsh-sum identity on the ancilla out-
come;
4. the gate-count and hardness analysis.
All four parts are cross-checked numerically, which ver-
ifies bit-level agreement between cIQP and MoIQP
marginals up to Monte Carlo noise.

5.
Sampling hardness
We distinguish two sampling tasks: the joint (n + a)-
qubit distribution and the data-only n-qubit marginal.
The generative-modeling deployment only ever uses the
marginal of Eq. (A15).
The joint is not operationally
relevant.
For the joint, U cIQP is IQP-form on n + a qubits by
construction. The original Bremner–Jozsa–Shepherd [18]
argument was stated for IQP circuits with Z-polynomial
degree at most 3. Our compiled circuit has maximum
Z-polynomial degree 2 + a, which for a = 3 is 5 and falls
inside the Bremner–Montanaro–Shepherd extension [19].
A sparse Erd˝os–R´enyi graph at ⟨k⟩= 6 of the kind we use
is qualitatively the kind of sparse-random structure that
extension covers under the usual anti-concentration hy-
potheses. Classical efficient sampling of the joint (n+a)-
qubit output would therefore imply a polynomial hier-
archy collapse to its second level under the conjectures
of the BMS framework. This is a conditional statement,
tied to whichever hypotheses one needs to apply BMS

to the specific gate-weight distribution of our compiled
circuit; a tight worst-case hardness theorem for the ex-
act compiled graph at a = 3 is not claimed and would
be of independent interest. For the marginal, sampling
hardness of the joint does not transfer automatically to
the data-register distribution, since marginalizing over an
entangled ancilla can in principle reduce distributional
complexity. We do not claim a rigorous hardness reduc-
tion from BMS to MoIQP-marginal sampling.
What
we do claim, and what is operationally relevant, is that
the compiled circuit reduces MoIQP marginal sampling
to a single quantum-hardware run of an IQP-form cir-
cuit with no classical post-mixing, and that each of the
L underlying single-IQP components admits the stan-
dard BJS/BMS hardness argument in isolation. Whether
the uniform mixture of L BJS-hard distributions is itself
BJS-hard is a separate theoretical question, not answered
by our construction.

polynomial growth Var ∝n1.87.
3.
Ruling out exponential decay
A barren plateau is canonically an exponential decay
Var ∝a e−cn with c > 0.
Fitting this hypothesis to
the same data gives cPSCK = 7 × 10−4 (indistinguishable
from zero; R2 = 0.003) and cLW = −5.2 × 10−2 (nega-
tive, i.e. growth, which is just polynomial scaling re-fit).
Comparison by residual sum-of-squares shows that for
LW the polynomial fit beats the exponential by a factor
of 26 (0.009 vs 0.229); for PSCK all three hypotheses
(polynomial, exponential, constant) fit equally well be-
cause none of them explains the small residual variance.
The canonical barren-plateau signature is not present for
either loss in this initialization regime.

6.
Limitations
Three explicit limitations of the scan.
First, single
graph realization per n. Each n uses one ER graph at
graph-seed 43, so graph-to-graph fluctuations at fixed
(n, ⟨k⟩) are not sampled. The residual variance in the
PSCK pgv fit (R2 = 0.015) is plausibly dominated by
that source. A multi-graph extension would tighten the
empirical slope bound on PSCK, though we would not
expect it to change the qualitative “no exponential de-
cay” finding.
Second, small-σ initialization regime only. The scan is
at σ = 0.1. Lerch et al. [23] show that MMD-type losses
hit barren plateaus at σ ∼π/2 (full-angle initialization),
which we stay away from. Extrapolation of our σ = 0.1
results to other amplitudes is not supported by the data
we show.
Third, fixed L.
The scan runs at L = 4; the L-
dependence of the gradient-variance scaling is not tested
here. For large L the MoIQP loss becomes a sum of L
single-IQP gradient variances under independent param-
eter sampling, for which a
√

tion would naively be expected, but we have not run that
experiment.
