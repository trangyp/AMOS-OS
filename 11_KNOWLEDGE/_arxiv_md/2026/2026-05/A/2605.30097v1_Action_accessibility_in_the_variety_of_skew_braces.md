Abstract
In this paper, we answer negatively to a question posed in the context of the Oberwolfach
Mini-Workshop “The Yang–Baxter Equation and Representations of Braid Groups” [22]
regarding the existence of split extensions classifiers in the category of skew braces. To
this end, we show that the variety of skew braces is not action accessible by investigating
the categorical notion of centraliser in the sense of Huq [31]. In light of their intrinsic
relationship with skew braces, an analogous result is proven for the variety of post-Lie
algebras over a fixed field.
Keywords:
skew brace, semi-abelian category, action accessibility, centraliser,
Yang–Baxter equation

Introduction
Skew brace theory finds its roots in early foundational works [25, 35, 43] related to
the investigation of non-degenerate solutions to the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation
[24]. These structures first arose in a commutative setting in [40] as a generalization of
Jacobson radical rings and were later extended in [30]. Following the formulation given
in [19, 30], a skew (left) brace is a set B equipped with two group operations + and ◦
satisfying the following identity
∀a, b, c ∈B
a ◦(b + c) = a ◦b −a + a ◦c.
Despite its evidently group theoretical flavour, the study of such structures has deeply
benefited from categorical and universal algebraic considerations. For instance, in [10] the
authors employed the Freese–McKenzie commutator theory to obtain a natural notion
of (central) nilpotency that turned out to be stronger than previous definitions already
present in the literature, see [40, 41]. In a similar vein, protomodular aspects of the
category Skb of skew braces were investigated by the authors of [12] who proved that

2. Action accessibility of skew braces
The aim of this section is to show that the variety Skb of skew braces is not action
accessible and therefore not action representable. We will proceed by first stating some
basic properties of skew braces and then by recalling the notion of centraliser of a subob-
ject in a semi-abelian category, essentially due to [31]. Our main argument relies on [21,
Corollary 2.6] and will be laid out by providing an example of a skew brace admitting a
proper ideal with a non-normal centraliser.

additive subgroup I ≤(B, +) which is closed under the ◦operation.

3. Action representability of post-Lie algebras
In this section, we show that the category PostLiek of post-Lie algebras (over a
fixed field k) is not action representable. In particular, we prove the stronger statement
according to which the subvariety PreLiek of pre-Lie algebras is not action accessible.
For more details on these structures, we refer the reader to recent papers [5, 17] and
references therein.
Let us begin by first recalling a useful characterisation of action accessibility for an
operadic variety, namely, a variety of non-associative algebras over a field k determined
by a set of multilinear identities (see [39]). Specifically, as proved in [28] and pointed out
in [15], we have the following rather useful result.
12


## Page 13


Proposition 3.1. Let V be an operadic variety of non-associative algebras over a field
k. Then the following are equivalent:
(1) V is action accessible;
(2) There exist scalars λ1, . . . , λ8, µ1, . . . , µ8 ∈k such that the following
x(yz) = λ1(xy)z + λ2(yx)z + λ3z(xy) + λ4z(yx) + λ5(xz)y
+ λ6(zx)y + λ7y(xz) + λ8y(zx) ,
(3.1)
(yz)x = µ1(xy)z + µ2(yx)z + µ3z(xy) + µ4z(yx)
+ µ5(xz)y + µ6(zx)y + µ7y(xz) + µ8y(zx)
(3.2)
are identities in V.
Let us now introduce the main definition of this section.
Definition 3.2. A post-Lie algebra [47] (over a field k) is the datum of a k-vector space
A together with two bilinear maps {·, ·}, · : A × A −→A such that
(1) (A, {·, ·}) is a Lie algebra;
(2) The identities
x{y, z} = {xy, z} + {y, xz} ,
(3.3)
{x, y}z = a(x, y, z) −a(y, x, z) ,
(3.4)
hold for all x, y, z ∈A, where a(x, y, z) := x(yz) −(xy)z, is the so-called associator
on (A, ·).
A pre-Lie algebra is a non-associative algebra (A, ·) such that
x(yz) −(xy)z = y(xz) −(yx)z ,
(3.5)
holds, for all x, y, z ∈A, or, equivalently such that
a(x, y, z) = a(y, x, z) ,
holds, for all x, y, z ∈A.
Remark 3.3.
(1) It is important to note that a pre-Lie algebra (A, ·) is a particular post-Lie algebra
(A, {·, ·}, ·) with trivial bracket {·, ·}.
Hence, the variety PreLie is a (proper)
subvariety of PostLie. Despite this fact, pre-Lie algebras have attracted interest
independently from post-Lie algebras, (see [5] for a comprehensive treatment).
(2) Any pre-Lie algebra satisfies the identity (3.1) in Proposition 3.1 (by considering
λ1 = λ7 = 1, λ2 = −1, and the other coefficient λi = 0).
Examples 3.4.
(1) An associative algebra is a pre-Lie algebra whose associator is identically zero, i.e.,
such that a(x, y, z) = 0, for all x, y, z ∈A.

(2) A Lie algebra (A, {·, ·}) always admits a structure of post-Lie algebra (A, {·, ·}, ·)
defined by setting x · y = 0, for all x, y ∈A.
(3) If (A, {·, ·}) is a 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra then (A, {·, ·}, {·, ·}) is a post-Lie
algebra.
Let us now turn our attention to the claim of this section.
Proposition 3.5. Let k be a field. The category PostLiek of post-Lie algebras over k
is not action accessible.
Proof. In light of Theorem 1.8, it is sufficient to prove the assertion for the variety
PreLiek of pre-Lie algebras over k. Moreover, thanks to Proposition 3.1, it is sufficient to
display a pre-Lie algebra in which either (3.1) or (3.2) does not hold. To this aim, consider
the four dimensional pre-Lie algebra I4 from [16, Example 1.1] with basis {e1, e2, e3, e4}
and whose non-trivial products are given by
e1e2 = e2 ,
e1e3 = e3 ,
e1e4 = e4 ,
e1e1 = 2e1 ,
e2e2 = e3e3 = e4e4 = e1 .
The term (e2e2)e3 = e3 does not belong to the subalgebra of I4 generated by the elements
(e3e2)e2 , (e2e3)e2 , e2(e3e2) , e2(e2e3) ,
due to the fact that the latter terms are all equal to zero. Consequently, the identity
(3.2) cannot be satisfied in A for any choice of µ1, . . . , µ8. Therefore, we conclude that
PreLiek is not action accessible.
As a consequence of Proposition 3.5 and Remark 1.7, we deduce that the category
PostLiek of post-Lie algebras (over any field k) is not (weakly) action representable. If
instead we restrict our attention to Novikov algebras, then we can prove the following
result.
Proposition 3.6. The variety of Novikov algebras (over an arbitrary field k) is action
accessible.
Proof. By (2) in Remark 3.3, the identity (3.1) is satisfied. Moreover, (3.2) trivially holds
(by considering as unique non-zero coefficient µ1 = 0). Therefore, the claim follows.
Let A be the pre-Lie algebra in the proof of Proposition 3.5. Let us underline that
this counterexample heavily relies on the fact that A is not Novikov since, for example,
(e2e2)e3 ̸= (e2e3)e2. Therefore, it is natural to state the following question.
Question 3.7. If V is an action accessible subvariety of PreLiek and A ∈V, is A a
