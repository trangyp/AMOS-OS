Anomaly-Informed Confidence Calibration
for Vision-Based Safety Prediction
Zhenjiang Mao1†, Jiawen Wu1†, Gabriel Wagner1, Zhongzheng Zhang1 and Ivan Ruchkin1
Abstract— Reliable confidence estimates are important for
safely deploying vision-based controllers in autonomous racing,
where safety predictions must be derived from camera images,
yet modern predictors become dangerously overconfident under
test-time distribution shifts. We identify a critical perception-
dynamics gap in existing anomaly signals: widely used scores,
such as autoencoder reconstruction error, capture visual cor-
ruptions but miss dynamics anomalies (e.g., actuation bias,
latency), where images remain plausible while the trajectory
degrades. To address this, we propose an Anomaly-Informed
Online Calibration approach that, without retraining any model
component, fuses two complementary anomaly scores extracted
from a world model: a perceptual score from reconstruc-
tion error and a dynamics score from epistemic uncertainty
and control-stream statistics. Based on these fused scores, a
lightweight temperature-scaling calibrator leverages test-time
augmentation to selectively reduce overconfidence under shift
while preserving nominal-condition performance. Experiments
on a physical DonkeyCar under four real-world anomaly
protocols unseen during training (darkness, blur, actuation bias,
processing latency) reduce average expected calibration error
from 0.184 to 0.116, a 37% improvement over the best baseline,
without modifying the base safety predictor.
I. INTRODUCTION
Reliable confidence estimates are key to deploying vision-
based robotic policies in safety-critical settings, yet mod-
ern predictors often exhibit miscalibration and dangerously
overconfident behavior under test-time anomalies [1]. The
safety evaluator must therefore provide confidence scores
that reflect the true likelihood of remaining safe. This
confidence miscalibration is significantly exacerbated when
encountering distribution shifts during deployment [2], [3].
A key obstacle is what we call the perception-dynamics
gap: standard anomaly scores operate entirely in observation
space and are therefore blind to failures that originate in
the dynamics-actuator bias, control latency, or unmodeled
friction — where the image stream appears nominal yet the
closed-loop trajectory silently diverges.
Standard post-hoc calibration methods, such as temper-
ature scaling, rely on a single learned scalar and lose
performance substantially under dataset shift [4]. Richer
uncertainty quantification techniques (MC Dropout [5], Deep
Ensembles [6]) improve robustness but incur computational
costs that conflict with real-time control, and treat all anoma-
lies as a monolithic source of uncertainty without distinguish-
ing perceptual from dynamics-driven deviations [7], [8].

Our core idea is to let the calibrator observe the kind
of anomaly and how severe it is, rather than relying on
a single fixed temperature. Thus, we propose a real-time
anomaly-informed online calibration framework for vision-
based robotic safety prediction. Our method repurposes sig-
nals from an anomaly-unaware world model to construct
two complementary anomaly scores: a perceptual score from
VAE reconstruction error, and a dynamics score derived from
epistemic uncertainty estimates and control-stream statistics.
We additionally apply lightweight test-time augmentation
(TTA) to smooth predictions under visual shift. These scores
are then fed into a lightweight temperature scaling module,
adjusting the effective temperature. Finally, confidence is
adjusted in response to perceptual or dynamics anomalies,
while nominal behavior is preserved otherwise.
The primary contributions of this work are as follows:
1) An online calibration framework that fuses anomaly
evidence from a world model with test-time augmen-
tation to adjust safety confidence without retraining.
2) A disentangled anomaly scoring strategy that separates
perceptual and dynamics scores, closing the gap left by
purely visual anomaly signals.
3) A physical DonkeyCar validation showing that cal-
ibration trained on synthetic augmentations transfers
to qualitatively different real-world corruptions, with
a 37% reduction in calibration error over the best
baseline across four unseen OOD protocols.

Section II establishes the formal problem setting. Section III

DonkeyCar platform and analyzes the results under four
real-world distribution shift protocols. Section V reviews
related work, and Section VI concludes with a discussion
of limitations and future directions.

II. PRELIMINARIES
We formalize the vision-based safety prediction problem
and then define the anomaly types and calibration objective.
Definition 1 (Vision-based Robotic System): A
vision-
based system s
=
(X, Y, U, x0, h, f, o) consists of a
continuous state space X, an image observation space
Y, a control action space U, an initial state x0 ∼P0, a
vision-based controller h : Y →U, an unknown dynamical
model f : X × U →X with xt+1 = f(xt, ut), and an
unknown observation model o : X →Y with yt = o(xt).
We instantiate this on an autonomous racing platform
where X is vehicle pose and velocity, Y is camera images,
and U is steering and throttle. We denote the closed-loop
trajectory τt = (xt, ut, xt+1, ut+1, . . .) under ut = h(o(xt))
and binary safety indicator ψ(x) : X →{0, 1}.
Definition 2 (Safety Confidence Predictor): Given history
length n and prediction horizon k, a safety confidence
predictor g takes the last n image observations yi−n+1:i as
input and outputs a predicted safety confidence ˆpi+k ∈[0, 1],
representing ˆpi+k ≈P(ψ(xi+k) = 1 | yi−n+1:i, h). The
predicted safety label is obtained by thresholding: ˆsi+k :=
1[ˆpi+k ≥0.5].
In our implementation, n = 1, so g takes a single image. The
policy inherently considers future actions in the predicted
safety confidence.
Anomalies. We categorize these shifts into two classes:
visual anomalies and dynamic anomalies. Visual anomalies
change observation function o into some alternative o′ ̸= o.
This change directly perturbs the image stream {yt}
without altering the underlying dynamics. Examples include
sensor degradation, lighting changes, lens blur, and visual
occlusions.
Dynamics
anomalies
change
the
dynamics
function f into some alternative f ′ ̸= f, inducing deviations
in the state trajectory {xt}. Examples include actuation bias,
control latency, and changes in surface friction and payload.
To account for such shifts, we assume access to anomaly
features that summarize the severity of each type of distri-
butional deviation. The anomaly injection protocols used for
evaluation are described in Section IV; the full dynamics
feature definitions are provided in Appendix .
Definition 3 (Anomaly Features): At each time i, we as-
sume access to an anomaly feature vector Ei = (ρi, δi):
• ρi ∈[0, 1] quantifies the severity of visual distribution
shift (perception anomaly),
• δi ∈[0, 1] quantifies the severity of dynamics distribu-
tion shift (dynamics anomaly).
The safety prediction problem thus decomposes into three
sub-tasks: (1) base safety prediction — estimating raw safety
confidence ˆpi+k without anomalies; (2) anomaly quantifica-
tion — estimating the anomaly features Ei that quantify the
severity of distribution shift; and (3) confidence calibration
— mapping the raw confidence ˆpi+k and the anomaly
features Ei to a better-calibrated confidence ˜pi+k.
Definition 4 (Anomaly-Informed Calibration): An
anomaly-informed calibration function C : [0, 1]×E →[0, 1]
maps a raw safety confidence ˆpi+k and anomaly features
Ei = (ρi, δi) to a calibrated confidence ˜pi+k = C(ˆpi+k, Ei)
such that ˜pi+k better reflects the true safety probability
P(ψ(xi+k) = 1 | yi−n+1:i, h).
In practice, the specific corruptions encountered at test
time are unknown a priori. To train the calibrator, we use
generic augmentations as a structural proxy for real-world
corruptions and unmodeled dynamics, enabling the model
to learn the relationship between anomaly intensity and
predictor reliability.

an in-distribution set Din (split into Dtrain

IV. EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION
A. Experimental Setup
We evaluate the proposed framework using a physical
DonkeyCar platform [15]. Autonomous racing is a com-
pelling testbed: controllers must map camera images to
actions under tight latency, and even brief miscalibration
can cause track departures. The vehicle is equipped with an
NVIDIA Jetson Nano for on-board inference and a front-
facing camera. Images are resized to 64 × 64 × 3 pixels for
model input. Experiments use a custom indoor track (∼20 m
circumference, tile surface) with sharp turns.
The overall pipeline consists of a frozen world model ω for
anomaly scoring and a separate image-based safety predictor
g for k-step safety confidence. The world model encoder and
decoder, (e, d), is a VAE operating on 64×64 RGB images
to zt ∈R64×4×4. The world model latent dynamics ˆfθ is
a two-layer ConvLSTM (128 hidden channels, dropout 0.3)
predicting latent evolution over horizon Kmax. The safety
predictor g is a CNN (4 conv + 3 FC layers) outputting a
binary safe/unsafe probability.
Both the world model (VAE reconstruction + latent pre-
diction loss, AdamW) and safety predictor (binary cross-
entropy, AdamW) are trained on Dtrain
in
and frozen thereafter;
full hyperparameters are in the Appendix .
Anomaly Injection Protocols. (1) Visual Anomalies: We in-
troduce environmental and sensor-level corruptions inspired
by the ImageNet-C benchmark [10] at five severity levels.
For real-world evaluation, we focus on two protocols: global
luminance reduction to simulate low-light conditions (Dark)
and Gaussian blur to simulate defocused or degraded optics
(Blur). During augmented-data construction (Daug), we ap-
ply nine corruption types that are strictly disjoint from the
test protocols: JPEG compression, desaturation, fog, speckle
noise, salt-and-pepper noise, pixelation, color jitter, and frost.
Notably, no brightness-, contrast-, or blur-family corruptions
are included in Daug, so the calibrator is not trained on the
same corruption families as the test-time visual protocols
(Dark/Blur). We emphasize that TTA applies mild photomet-
ric jitter only at inference time to smooth predictions, rather
than to provide training-time corruption coverage.

V. RELATED WORK
Confidence calibration. Post-hoc calibrators range from
parametric methods (beta calibration, Bayesian Binning)
to density-aware approaches; DAC [21], which modulates
temperature via KNN feature density, is the most related
baseline. Static methods degrade under drift [3], [4]; multi-
domain temperature scaling [8] explicitly leverages hetero-
geneity across multiple training domains to improve cal-
ibration robustness under distribution shift, outperforming
standard methods on both in-distribution and OOD test sets,
while MC Dropout [5] and Deep Ensembles [6] improve ro-
bustness at multi-pass cost. Test-time augmentation improves
calibration via stochastic input averaging [13]. Our method
differs by conditioning temperature on disentangled anomaly
scores while keeping the base predictor frozen.
OOD detection and anomaly scoring. The Maximum Soft-
max Probability baseline [16] and Mahalanobis distance in
feature space [1] are standard OOD detectors; covariate and
concept shift further challenge detection and calibration [7],
where covariate shift reflects style-level changes (e.g., noise)
and concept shift reflects semantic-level changes — each
requiring distinct score functions for reliable OOD detection
and confidence calibration; Feature Norm [17] and energy
scores provide alternatives. VAE reconstruction error [11]
is widely used for visual anomaly detection. Outlier expo-
sure [14] trains against auxiliary outlier data, with later work
synthesizing virtual outliers in feature space [23]. Unlike
standard detectors that produce binary flags, we use con-
tinuous anomaly scores to quantitatively adjust confidence,
and apply outlier exposure to train a separate calibrator
rather than the detector itself. Complementary visual OOD
signals include attention-map monitoring, vision-language
semantic detection, KNN feature distance, and activation
truncation [24]. These, like our ρ, target the observation
stream; our addition is a dynamics score δ for anomalies
invisible to image-level methods.
Safety prediction and world models. World-model-based
safety prediction [25] and autoencoder misbehavior detection
(SelfOracle [26], later extended with ensembles) are the
closest antecedents. Our work builds on the VAE-RNN
World Models paradigm [9], [27] and the end-to-end driving
approach [28] underlying DonkeyCar.

VI. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
We presented an anomaly-informed calibration framework
that fuses TTA with world-model-derived perceptual and
dynamics anomaly scores, reducing OOD-average ECE from
0.184 to 0.116 (37% over the best baseline) on a physical
DonkeyCar under four unseen anomaly protocols without
retraining. The ablation shows that ρ provides the dominant
signal (28% ECE reduction) since TTA already handles
visual robustness implicitly, while δ adds cross-seed stability
and stronger dynamics-anomaly detection. Transfer from
synthetic augmentations to real-world protocols supports As-
sumption 1, though extrapolation beyond the training severity
range remains a limitation. Future work includes closed-loop
safety integration, online calibrator updates, and conformal
prediction for calibration guarantees.
