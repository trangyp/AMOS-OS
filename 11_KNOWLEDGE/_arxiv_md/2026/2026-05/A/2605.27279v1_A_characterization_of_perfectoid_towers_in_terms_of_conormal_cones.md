1. Introduction
The theory of perfectoid spaces, introduced by P. Scholze, has played a central role in com-
mutative algebra in mixed characteristic. In order to apply perfectoid techniques to Noetherian
rings, the notion of perfectoid towers was introduced by S. Ishiro, K. Nakazato, and K. Shimo-
moto [INS25]; see also [Ha26a, Ha26c, HIS26, IS25, Ish26]. The deﬁnition of perfectoid towers
is based on a characterization of perfectoid rings in terms of their p-torsion parts, originally due
to O. Gabber and L. Ramero [GR24, Theorem 16.3.75]; see also [INS25, Theorem 3.50]. This
characterization can in turn be deduced from the following one in terms of conormal cones.
Theorem 1.1 (Gabber–Ramero). Let A be a commutative ring. Then A is a perfectoid ring
(in the sense of [BMS1, Deﬁnition 3.5]) if and only if A contains an element ̟ satisfying the
following conditions.
(1) p ∈̟pA, and A is ̟-adically complete and separated.

Appendix A. Zariskizations of pairs
In this appendix we give some of the results on Zariskizations of pairs used in the proof of
Corollary 3.13. We refer to [FKI, Chapter 0, §7.3(b)], [NS24, §7], and [Tan18, §3] as basic
references for generalities of Zariskizations of pairs.
Recall that by a pair we mean a couple (A, I) consisting of a ring A and an ideal I of A. A
morphism of pairs f : (A, I) →(B, J) is a ring homomorphism f : A →B that is continuous
with respect to the I-adic topology on A and the J-adic topology on B. For example, the pairs
(A, I) and (A, In) for n ≥1 are isomorphic to each other. We denote by Pair the category of
pairs.
First of all, let us recall the deﬁnition of Zariskian pairs ([FKI, Chapter 0, Proposition 7.3.2]).
Deﬁnition A.1. Let (A, I) be a pair. We say that A is I-adically Zariskian, or that the pair
(A, I) is Zariskian, if the following equivalent conditions are satisﬁed.
(1) 1 + I ⊂A×.
(2) An element a ∈A is invertible if and only if a mod I is invertible in A/I.
(3) I is contained in the Jacobson radical of A.
