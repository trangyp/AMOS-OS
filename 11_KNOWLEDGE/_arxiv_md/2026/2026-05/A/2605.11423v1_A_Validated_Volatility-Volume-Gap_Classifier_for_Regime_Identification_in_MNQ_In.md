Abstract 
This paper constructs and validates a composite day-classification system for Micro E-Mini 
Nasdaq 100 futures (MNQ) based on three simultaneously elevated pre-market observable 
conditions: the absolute magnitude of the first-30-minute return, the absolute overnight gap, and 
the deviation of first-bar volume from a rolling 20-day baseline. We term this the 
Volatility-Volume-Gap (VVG) classifier. Using 947 regular trading hours (RTH) trading days of 
five-minute bar data spanning 2021 through 2025, with all classification thresholds computed on 
an expanding window to prevent lookahead bias, we find that classifier-positive days exhibit 
statistically distinct behavioral characteristics from the broader trading day population. 
The VVG classifier activates on approximately 4.4% of trading days (40 days across the full 
dataset). Classifier-positive days show a mean next-day return spread of 25.6 basis points above 
non-classifier days, and 77.6% of classifier-positive days reverse from their intraday peak before 
the session close, with a mean peak-to-close giveback of 11.73 points. Full intraday path analysis 
at 30-minute intervals documents the characteristic structure of classifier-positive days: gradual 
directional drift through the morning, peak formation between 14:00 and 15:30, followed by 
systematic late-session reversal. 
Despite these statistically genuine descriptive characteristics, all eight tested directional trading 
strategies on classifier-positive days fail to survive institutional validation standards. No entry 
configuration achieves a T-statistic above 2.0 out-of-sample with positive net returns after

1. Introduction 
A recurring challenge in systematic intraday trading research is the distinction between statistical 
description and economic exploitability. A classifier that correctly identifies a structurally 
distinct type of trading day is a genuine research contribution even if it does not directly generate 
a tradable signal. The literature on volatility regimes, intraday seasonality, and market 
microstructure is rich with examples of phenomena that are real, documented, and yet impossible 
to exploit under realistic execution constraints (Andersen & Bollerslev, 1997; Wood, McInish, & 
Ord, 1985). 
This paper contributes to that literature in a specific way: we construct a day-classification 
system for MNQ futures, document its behavioral validity with institutional-grade rigor, and then 
systematically attempt to convert that behavioral validity into a tradable signal. The conversion 
fails. We document the failure in full. The result is a paper about a classifier that works as a 
descriptor and does not work as a signal generator—and why that distinction matters. 
The VVG classifier is motivated by a straightforward empirical observation: days where multiple 
early-session stress indicators simultaneously reach extreme levels appear to behave differently 
from ordinary days. The three conditions used—large first-30-minute return, large overnight gap, 
and elevated first-bar volume—are each individually associated with elevated volatility in the 
intraday finance literature (Admati & Pfleiderer, 1988; Gao, Han, Li, & Zhou, 2018). The 
hypothesis is that their simultaneous occurrence identifies a structurally distinct market state. 
The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 describes the data and classifier construction 
methodology. Section 3 presents the behavioral characterization of classifier-positive days in full. 
Section 4 documents all eight tested directional strategies and their failure modes. Section 5 
discusses the structural interpretation of the classifier’s descriptive validity and its potential 
future applications. Section 6 covers limitations and extensions. Section 7 concludes.

2.2 Classifier Feature Definitions 
Three features are computed for each trading day using only data available before or at the start 
of the classification window. All rolling statistics use expanding windows: the tercile boundary 
for day N is computed using data from days 0 through N−1 only, ensuring zero lookahead 
contamination. 
Feature 1 — Absolute first-30-minute return (|r1|): The absolute value of the return from the 
09:30 bar open to the 09:55 bar close (first six five-minute bars). This captures the magnitude of 
early directional commitment without taking a directional stance. 
Feature 2 — Absolute overnight gap (|gap|): The absolute value of the percentage change from 
the prior session close to the current session open. Overnight gaps reflect information arrival 
between sessions, institutional pre-market positioning, and macro news absorption. Large 
absolute gaps indicate elevated information asymmetry at session open. 
Feature 3 — First-bar volume deviation (vol_dev): The deviation of the first five-minute bar’s 
volume from its 20-day rolling mean, normalized by the rolling standard deviation (z-score). A 
high positive z-score indicates unusually elevated participation at the session open, which is 
associated with institutional order flow and elevated short-term volatility in the microstructure 
literature (Easley, Lopez de Prado, & O’Hara, 2012). 
2.3 Classifier Construction

A day is classified as VVG-positive if and only if all three features simultaneously exceed their 
expanding-window top tercile threshold on that day. The top tercile is defined as the 66.7th 
percentile of the prior distribution for each feature independently. The intersection 
requirement—all three conditions must be true simultaneously—is the key design choice. 
Individual condition filters were tested separately and found to produce substantially weaker 
behavioral differentiation than the intersection, confirming that the simultaneous occurrence of 
all three conditions identifies a more extreme and behaviorally distinct market state than any 
single condition alone. 
The expanding window design means the classifier becomes available for live use from the point 
where sufficient history exists to compute stable tercile estimates (approximately 60 trading 
days, or three calendar months, into the dataset). All statistics reported in this paper use the full 
2021–2025 dataset with expanding window thresholds applied throughout.

4. Directional Strategy Tests 
The behavioral characterization in Section 3 establishes that VVG classifier-positive days are 
genuinely distinct from other trading days. This section documents all attempts to convert that 
descriptive validity into a deployable trading signal. Eight distinct entry configurations are 
tested. All use the same execution framework: signal at bar close, entry at next bar open, 
two-point round-trip friction. Walk-forward validation uses expanding training windows 
identical to Mesfin (2026a). 
4.1 Validation Standards 
All eight strategies are evaluated against the same five-criterion institutional standard used 
throughout this research program: T-statistic ≥ 2.0 out-of-sample, ≥ 30 OOS trades, positive net 
return after 2-point friction, multi-year consistency, and permutation test p < 0.05 where 
applicable. A strategy that passes any subset of these criteria but not all five is logged as failed.

The reversal hypothesis predicts that classifier-positive days—characterized by large early 
moves—tend to reverse over the course of the session. The entry fades the opening direction: if 
the first-30-minute return is positive, enter short; if negative, enter long. Exit at session close.

4.6 Common Failure Modes Across All Eight Strategies 
The eight strategies fail for three distinct reasons, and understanding these failure modes is as 
important as the results themselves. 
Failure mode 1: Sample size constraint. The VVG classifier activates on only 40 days across four 
years—approximately 10 days per year. This is insufficient for walk-forward validation with 
meaningful out-of-sample periods. Any strategy tested on classifier-only days faces an 
irreducible sample size problem that cannot be resolved by parameter optimization or signal 
refinement. Only additional years of data can address this. 
Failure mode 2: Year instability driven by regime change. The behavioral character of 
classifier-positive days differs substantially across years. 2024 showed persistent upward drift 
while 2022–2023 showed reversal dominance and 2025 showed extreme intraday spikes 
followed by violent reversals. A strategy calibrated on one regime will fail in a different regime. 
This is not overfitting—it is a fundamental instability in the classifier’s underlying behavioral 
consequences across macro environments. 
Failure mode 3: Variance-to-edge ratio. The standard deviation of returns on classifier-positive 
days (35–173 points at different checkpoints) is vastly larger than the mean edge available (7–11 
points at best). Even with T = 1.46 at the intersection reversal configuration, the per-trade 
standard deviation of approximately 60 points means that a profitable year requires sustained 
directional consistency that the data does not show. The edge is real but insufficiently large 
relative to trade-level noise. 
5. The Classifier as a Research Asset 
5.1 What the Classifier Validates 
Despite the failure of all directional strategies, the VVG classifier produces three validated 
descriptive findings that constitute genuine research contributions. First, simultaneous extreme 
conditions in the three features identify a behaviorally distinct day type. The 25.6 basis point 
next-day return spread, 77.6% peak reversal rate, and characteristic intraday path structure are all 
statistically confirmed phenomena. Second, the regression relationship between opening return 
and last-30-minute return on classifier days (beta = −0.0416, p = 0.016, R² = 0.046) confirms that 
early momentum contains information about late-session structure on these specific days. Third, 
the bimodal peak timing distribution (early-morning peaks and late-afternoon peaks) suggests 
two structural subtypes of classifier-positive days that future research could attempt to 
distinguish ex ante.

5.2 Potential Future Applications 
The classifier has three plausible future applications that are beyond the scope of this paper but 
motivated by its validated behavioral characteristics. 
Application 1: Volatility prediction. Classifier-positive days consistently show higher realized 
session variance than non-classifier days. If this relationship is stable out-of-sample, the 
classifier could provide input to a realized volatility prediction model, which would be valuable 
for options positioning or ATR-based stop sizing in other strategies. 
Application 2: Negative filter for other signals. If classifier-positive days represent a genuinely 
distinct 
market 
regime, 
signals 
validated 
on 
non-classifier 
days 
may

differently—potentially worse—on classifier days. Testing whether established signals should be 
suppressed on classifier-positive days is a direct research extension. This was tested for the RTH 
Confluence Signal and London Signal B in Mesfin (2026a) and found to have no material 
impact, but other signals have not been tested. 
Application 3: Multi-signal composite. The classifier’s regression relationship (R² = 0.046) is too 
weak for standalone use but could be combined with other independently validated predictors. If 
two or three uncorrelated predictors each explaining 4–6% of variance are combined in a 
composite score, the joint explanatory power may be sufficient to generate tradable edge. This is 
the architectural approach of the RTH Confluence Signal, which combines regime classification, 
volume, and structure conditions to achieve T = 5.83. 
5.3 Why the Classifier Is Preserved Rather Than Closed 
In the research methodology used throughout this program, a branch is permanently closed when 
its hypothesis is definitively falsified—when the underlying directional claim is shown to be 
wrong. The VVG classifier is not closed because the hypothesis that it identifies a behaviorally 
distinct day type has been confirmed, not falsified. What has been falsified is the specific claim 
that this behavioral distinctiveness generates a deployable directional signal under current 
execution constraints. 
The distinction matters. A closed finding means “this approach is wrong and should not be 
revisited.” A deferred finding means “this approach is valid but insufficient under current 
constraints, and future research with different data or different execution assumptions may 
produce different results.” The VVG classifier is deferred, not closed. 
6. Limitations and Extensions 
6.1 Limitations 
The most significant limitation of this study is the small number of classifier-positive days (40 
across four years). This sample size is insufficient for robust walk-forward validation of 
directional strategies and means that all strategy results should be interpreted with caution. The

would substantially improve the reliability of directional strategy estimates. 
The three classifier features are computed from OHLCV data and represent publicly observable 
conditions. It is possible that the classifier captures the same structural information as existing 
volatility regime measures (VIX spikes, realized variance, etc.) that are better studied in the 
literature. The relationship between the VVG classifier and VIX-based regime measures was not 
tested in this study. 
The expanding window threshold design means classifier thresholds shift over time as the dataset 
grows. Early in the sample (2022), when only one year of prior data is available, the tercile 
boundaries are estimated from a relatively small sample and may be unstable. This could 
introduce noise in the early years of the dataset that is not present in later years when the 
expanding window covers more data. 
6.2 Extensions 
The most direct extension is to test the classifier on a longer historical dataset. MNQ data is 
available back to 2019 through Databento. Adding three years of pre-2022 data would 
approximately double the classifier-positive day count and enable more robust directional 
strategy validation. 
A natural test of the classifier’s macro-regime dependence is to condition it on a slow-moving 
volatility regime indicator such as the VIX percentile or a rolling realized volatility regime. If the 
classifier’s behavioral character (reversal vs. continuation) is predictable from the prevailing 
volatility environment, this could resolve the year instability problem by switching the entry 
direction based on the macro regime rather than using a fixed reversal or continuation 
assumption. 
The close fade configuration (Strategy 4) has the strongest structural motivation given the 77.6% 
peak reversal rate but failed solely on sample size grounds. An extension using the full pre-2022 
dataset combined with the 2022–2025 data would provide a more meaningful sample for this 
specific configuration. 
7. Conclusion 
This paper has documented the construction, behavioral validation, and directional strategy 
testing of the VVG classifier for MNQ intraday data. The classifier identifies days 
simultaneously exhibiting extreme first-30-minute return, extreme overnight gap, and extreme 
first-bar volume. These days constitute approximately 4.4% of the trading population and show 
genuine behavioral distinctiveness: a 25.6 basis point next-day return spread, a 77.6% peak 
reversal rate before the session close, a mean giveback of 11.73 points from peak to close, and a 
characteristic intraday path structure that differs materially from non-classifier days.

None of these behavioral characteristics translate into a deployable directional trading signal 
under institutional validation standards. All eight tested strategy configurations fail on at least 
one of the five required criteria, with the dominant failure modes being insufficient sample size 
(40 classifier days over four years), year instability driven by macro regime change, and a 
variance-to-edge ratio that makes per-trade returns too noisy relative to the available mean edge. 
The primary contribution of this paper is methodological: a documented framework for 
constructing, validating, and honestly evaluating a behavioral day classifier. The finding that a 
statistically genuine behavioral phenomenon does not automatically translate into a tradable 
signal is itself informative—and is a finding that is systematically underreported in retail and 
academic trading literature, where the emphasis on positive results creates a false impression that 
good description implies good strategy. 
The VVG classifier is preserved as a validated descriptive research asset with documented 
potential applications in volatility prediction, negative signal filtering, and multi-signal 
composite architectures. It is not closed. Further investigation with a larger dataset and 
conditional macro-regime modeling is warranted.

Appendix A: Classifier Construction Pseudocode 
The following describes the exact classifier construction logic in pseudocode for reproducibility. 
 
For each trading day D (starting from day 61): 
r1 = abs((close_09:55 - open_09:30) / open_09:30) 
gap = abs((open_09:30 - prior_close) / prior_close) 
vol_dev = (first_bar_volume - rolling_mean_20) / 
rolling_std_20 
r1_threshold = percentile_66.7(r1 for days 0 to D-1) 
gap_threshold = percentile_66.7(gap for days 0 to D-1) 
vol_threshold = percentile_66.7(vol_dev for days 0 to D-1) 
classifier_label[D] = 1 if (r1 > r1_threshold AND gap >
