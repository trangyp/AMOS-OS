Abstract
In this paper, we provide purely model-theoretic (algebraic) characteri-
sations for classes definable in second-order logic and for pseudo-elementary
classes (including PC and PC∆classes). Classical results of this flavor in-
clude Keisler–Shelah type theorems (characterising first-order definability by
closure under ultraproducts and ultraroots) and Birkhoff’s HSP theorem; a
key starting point for this paper is S´agi’s work [7], which provides an algebraic
description of classes definable by existential second-order sentences. Here we
resolve several open problems from [6] and [7].
Our main results are the following.
• We solve the long-standing problem of giving a purely algebraic charac-
terisation of pseudo-elementary classes: we characterise PC∆classes by
intrinsic closure properties. We also give a characterisation for the basic
pseudo-elementary classes (PC).
• We provide a structural classification of second-order equivalent struc-
tures, and we obtain purely algebraic characterisations of the classes
definable by second-order formulas as well as those definable by finitely
many second-order sentences.

Introduction
One of the fundamental tools of classical (first-order) model theory is the ultraprod-
uct construction. As it is well known, with the aid of this construction, one can
characterize (first-order) axiomatizable classes of structures. Further, a key result
that reveals the strength and elegance of the ultraproduct construction is the cele-
brated “Isomorphic Ultrapowers Theorem” of Keisler and Shelah. It states that two
structures A and B are elementarily equivalent (that is, A and B satisfy the same
first-order formulas) if and only if A and B have isomorphic ultrapowers. Assuming
the generalized continuum hypothesis, Keisler proved this theorem in 1968; later, in
[8] Shelah eliminated the generalized continuum hypothesis from the proof.
According to the above theorems, the interconnections between first-order for-
mulas and ultraproducts are well understood.
However.
The situation becomes
substantially more complicated when one passes to higher-order logics. As a moti-
vating example we note that ultraproducts, in general, do not preserve the validity
of second-order formulas. In fact, (in a fixed signature) one can always find finite
structures with an infinite ultraproduct, therefore if a first-order formula is true in all
finite structures, then it should also be true in some infinite structure, as well. Thus,
in another words “being finite” and “being infinite” are not first-order expressible.
However, as it is also well known, these properties (finiteness and infinity) can be
expressed by second-order formulas (see e.g. the proof of Theorem 3.8 below).
In view of the previous paragraph, it is natural to ask what can be said about
higher order formulas and ultraproducts of models of them. This question was the
starting point of S´agi’s paper [7] and here we continue related investigations.
A little bit more precisely, here we continue investigations initiated in [7] and
[4] and by developing new technical tools, we will study connections between ultra-
products, pseudo elementary classes and second-order formulas. In more detail, our
main results are as follows.
• We solve the long-standing problem of giving a purely algebraic character-
isation of pseudo-elementary classes.
In more detail, in Theorem 3.18 we
characterise PC∆classes by intrinsic closure properties. We also give a char-
acterisation for the basic pseudo-elementary classes (PC) in Lemma 3.14, and,
using this description, in Theorem 3.8 we obtain an internal (closure-based)
criterion for when a class of finite structures lies in NP.
• In Theorem 4.3 We provide a structural classification of second-order equiv-
alent structures, and in Theorem 4.6 we obtain purely algebraic character-
isations of the classes definable (axiomatizable) by second-order formulas.
Further, in Theorem 4.7 we characterize classes definable (axiomatizable) by
finitely many second-order sentences.
From a technical perspective, our approach combines classical ultraproduct meth-
ods with tools from descriptive set theory. We will establish interconnections be-
tween axiomatizability and closed subspaces of certain spaces of formulas.
This
approach allows us to formulate preservation conditions in a topologically robust
manner. More concretely, we will study certain subspaces of Cantor spaces associ-
ated to classes of structures with different axiomatizability properties, for the details
we refer to Subsection 2.3. We call these spaces “spaces of formulas”.

Theorem 4.3. Let A and B be two τ-structures. The following are equivalent:
(1) A ≡SO B, i.e. the same second-order formulas hold in both structures;
(2) {A} and {B} are completely inseparable from each other.
Proof. This follows from Lemma 4.2 and the elementary fact that the only ultrafilter
on a singleton set is trivial.

Now we turn to the characterization of classes axiomatizable in second-order
logic. First, consider the following definition.
Definition 4.5. Let K be a class of τ-structures.
(i) We say that K is closed under semi-complete inseparability if for every set
{Ai : i ∈I} ⊆K and τ-structure B, whenever {Ai : i ∈I} and {B} are
completely inseparable from each other, then B ∈K.
(ii) We say that K is closed under complete inseparability if for every sets {Ai :
i ∈I} ⊆K and {Bj : j ∈J} of τ-structures, whenever {Ai : i ∈I} and
{Bj : j ∈J} are completely inseparable from each other, then
{Bj : j ∈J} ∩K ̸= ∅.
