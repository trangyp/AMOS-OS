A Geometric Interpretation of Generalized Hurwitz–Radon
Numbers Defined by Kannaka–Tojo
Muneto Miyaji
Abstract
The Hurwitz–Radon number originates in the composition problem of quadratic forms and
is related to the maximum number of pointwise linearly independent vector fields on spheres.
Kannaka–Tojo [arXiv:2602.04544] reformulated the Hurwitz–Radon number in the setting of a
real reductive Lie algebra g and its faithful representation ι, and introduced two natural numbers
ρ(1)(g, ι) and ρ(2)(g, ι). For classical Lie algebras and their standard representations, these two
numbers coincide except for a few cases.
In this paper, fixing a Lie group G and a subspace s of g = Lie G , we define natural numbers
ρG,s(M, σ) and ρ±
G,s(M, σ, ∇) for a G-manifold (M, σ) and its affine connection ∇. These are
defined in terms of fundamental vector fields on M. In a special case, we show that ρG,s(M, σ)
coincides with ρ(2)(g, ι), and that ρ−
G,s(M, σ, ∇) coincides with ρ(1)(g, ι). Furthermore, we show
that ρ+
G,s(M, σ, ∇) is related to Clifford structures on M.

Introduction
The Hurwitz–Radon number ρ(N), associated with each positive integer N, is a classical invariant
arising from the composition problem of quadratic forms, originating in the work of Hurwitz [3] and
Radon [7].

## Page 2


When a positive integer N is expressed as
N = 24a+b(2c + 1)
(a, b, c ∈N, 0 ≤b ≤3),
the Hurwitz–Radon number ρ(N) is defined by
ρ(N) = 8a + 2b.
Adams [1] gave a geometric characterization of the Hurwitz–Radon number by showing that
ρ(N)−1 coincides with the maximum number of pointwise linearly independent vector fields on the
sphere SN−1. Furthermore, the Hurwitz–Radon number is known to appear in various contexts,
such as the invertibility problem for linear combinations of real matrices [2] and the study of
Clifford–Klein forms by Kobayashi–Yoshino [5].
Although the Hurwitz–Radon number is defined algebraically for positive integers, it has recently
been generalized by Kannaka–Tojo [4] in the framework of real reductive Lie algebras and their
representations.
Let (g, ι) be a pair of a real reductive Lie algebra g and a faithful representation ι : g →gl(N, C).
Assume that ι(g) is self-adjoint. Let θ be the associated involution of g, and let g = k ⊕p be the
associated Cartan decomposition(see Section 2 for details).
Definition 1.1 (Kannaka–Tojo [4]). In the setting above, we define two integers ρ(i)(g, ι) (i = 1, 2)
as the largest n ∈N for which there exists an R-linear map f : Rn →p such that
• ρ(1)(g, ι) : ι(f(v))2 = ∥v∥2IN for any v ∈Rn,
• ρ(2)(g, ι) : ι(f(v)) is invertible for any non-zero v ∈Rn.
Here, ∥v∥denotes the standard norm of v ∈Rn.
Clearly,
ρ(1)(g, ι) ≤ρ(2)(g, ι)
holds.
For example, for the standard representation ι : so(N, N) →gl(2N, C), one has
ρ(1)(so(N, N), ι) = ρ(2)(so(N, N), ι) = ρ(N).
Thus, ρ(1)(g, ι) and ρ(2)(g, ι) may be regarded as generalizations of the Hurwitz–Radon number.
For classical Lie algebras g and their standard representations ι, the values of ρ(1)(g, ι) and
ρ(2)(g, ι) were completely determined by Kannaka–Tojo, and if g ̸= sl(2N +1, D) (D = R, C, H, N ≥
1), then
ρ(1)(g, ι) = ρ(2)(g, ι)
holds.
Moreover, for certain symmetric spaces G/H and representations ι : g →gl(N, C), Kannaka–
Tojo showed that ρ(1)(g, ι) determines the largest n for which Spin(n, 1) can act properly on G/H.
Details are given in Section 2.
The Hurwitz–Radon number is geometrically characterized by vector fields on spheres. Fur-
thermore, Kannaka–Tojo introduced generalized Hurwitz–Radon numbers in the representation-
theoretic setting, which naturally raises the following question:
Problem. Can ρ(1)(g, ι) and ρ(2)(g, ι) be reformulated in a more geometric framework?
In this paper, we reformulate the generalized Hurwitz–Radon numbers introduced by Kannaka–
Tojo in the setting of G-manifolds.

In this subsection, we review the generalized Hurwitz–Radon numbers introduced by Kannaka–Tojo

Let (g, ι) be a pair of a real reductive Lie algebra g and a faithful representation ι : g →gl(N, C).
We assume that the image ι(g) is self-adjoint, namely, ι(g) is closed under talking adjoint operators
with respect to some positive definite hermitian form ⟨, ⟩on CN. Then there exists an involutive
automorphism θ of g such that
⟨ι(X)v, w⟩= −⟨v, ι(θ(X))w⟩

We denote by g = k + p the eigenspace decomposition of g with respect to θ namely, k (resp. p) is
the eigenspace of θ with eigenvalue 1 (resp. −1). If g is semisimple, then any faithful representation

• ρ(2)(g, ι) : ι(f(v)) is invertible for any non-zero v ∈Rn.
