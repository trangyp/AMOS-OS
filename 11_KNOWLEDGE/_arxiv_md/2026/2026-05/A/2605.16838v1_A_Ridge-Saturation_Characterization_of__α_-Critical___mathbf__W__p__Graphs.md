Abstract
We characterize the graphs which are simultaneously α-critical and members
of the class Wp. The characterization is stated in three equivalent languages.
In the graph itself, such a graph is a well-covered graph whose codimension-
one localization ﬁbers all have size at least p and whose edges are exactly
covered by the cliques induced by those ﬁbers. In the independence complex,
it is a pure ﬂag complex in which every ridge has degree at least p and every
missing edge is generated by the link of a ridge. In the complement, it is
a Kr+1-saturated graph, where r = α(G), all maximal cliques have size r,
and the minimum (r −1)-clique-codegree is at least p. This gives an exact
formula for the largest p for which a well-covered graph belongs to Wp. We
make this complement correspondence explicit, record saturation-theoretic
consequences including dense-complement rigidity and p-sensitive edge and
order bounds, and give a family of sharp examples showing that the local
suﬃcient condition from the recent work of Hoang, Levit and Mandrescu is
not necessary outside the locally triangle-free setting, for all p ≥2.
Keywords:
well-covered graph; Wp graph; α-critical graph; saturated
graph; independence complex; clique codegree.

1. Introduction
All graphs in this paper are ﬁnite, simple, and non-empty.
We write
Ω∗(G) for the family of all independent sets of G and Ω(G) for the family of
maximum independent sets. A graph is well-covered if all maximal indepen-
dent sets have the same cardinality. This notion was introduced by Plummer
and has since become a central object in the structure theory of indepen-
dence in graphs [21, 22]. General recognition and structural classiﬁcation of
well-covered graphs is diﬃcult; useful positive results are known only under
additional restrictions or in special families, including girth constraints, pla-
nar families, product constructions, and algebraic subclasses [9, 18, 26, 5, 27].
Staples introduced the classes Wp: a graph G belongs to Wp if every p
pairwise disjoint sets in Ω∗(G) can be extended to p pairwise disjoint sets in
Ω(G) [24, 25]. Thus W1 is the class of well-covered graphs, and the classes
form the descending chain
W1 ⊇W2 ⊇· · · ⊇Wp ⊇· · · .
The subclass W2 is the class of 1-well-covered graphs without isolated ver-
tices; it was studied by Staples, Pinter, and later by Levit and Mandrescu
through shedding vertices, localizations, and diﬀerential conditions [25, 20,
19, 16, 17, 4]. Further constructions and polynomial properties of Wp graphs
appear in [8, 10, 11]. Connections with Cohen–Macaulayness, Gorenstein
graphs, Buchsbaum properties, and edge ideals are developed in [12, 13, 15,
5, 27].
An edge e of G is α-critical if deleting e increases the independence num-
ber.
The graph is α-critical if every edge is α-critical.
This is the inde-
pendence formulation of the classical line-critical graphs studied by Erdős
and Gallai, Beineke–Harary–Plummer, Berge, and Plummer [6, 2, 3, 23].
Plummer asked for a characterization of graphs which are both α-critical
and well-covered, and in particular for the class W2 [22]. A recent paper of
Hoang, Levit and Mandrescu developed new characterizations of Wp graphs
and gave a characterization under a locally triangle-free hypothesis [14]. The
locally triangle-free part relies on earlier triangle-free and algebraic results of
Hoang and Trung [12, 13].
The purpose of this note is to give an exact characterization for all p. The
key point is that the Wp condition is not best viewed vertex by vertex. It
is a ridge-thickness condition on the independence complex. Let r = α(G),

3. The Wp property as ridge thickness
The following theorem is the structural form of the Wp property used
throughout the paper.
Theorem 3.1 (Ridge-thickness characterization of Wp). Let p ≥1, let G
be a graph with α(G) = r ≥1, and let ∆= ∆(G). Then the following are
equivalent:
(a) G ∈Wp
(b) ∆is pure and every ridge of ∆has degree at least p.
(c) G is well-covered and |FG(S)| ≥p for every S ∈Ω∗(G) with |S| = r−1.
Proof. (b) ⇐⇒(c) is immediate from the deﬁnitions.
(a) =⇒(c): Assume ﬁrst that G ∈Wp. Then G ∈W1, so G is well-
covered and ∆(G) is pure. Let S be a ridge. If r = 1, then S = ∅, and
|FG(S)| = |V (G)| ≥p by the deﬁnition of Wp. If r ≥2, then GS ∈Wp by
Theorem 2.1, and α(GS) = 1. Therefore GS is a complete graph on at least
p vertices. Since V (GS) = FG(S), we get |FG(S)| ≥p.
(b) =⇒(a): Assume that ∆(G) is pure of dimension r −1 and that every
ridge has degree at least p. We prove G ∈Wp by induction on r. If r = 1,
then G is complete and |V (G)| = deg∆(∅) ≥p, so G ∈Wp.
Let r ≥2. Fix x ∈V (G). Since ∆(G) is pure, every maximal independent
set of Gx has size r −1. Indeed, if I is a maximal independent set in Gx,
then I ∪{x} is also a maximal independent set in G and hence has size r.
Hence Gx is well-covered and α(Gx) = r −1. Now let T be a ridge of ∆(Gx),
so |T| = r −2. Then T ∪{x} is a ridge of ∆(G), and
FGx(T) = FG(T ∪{x}).

7. An algebraic corollary and a homological question
Fix a ﬁeld k. For a simple graph G with vertex set V (G) = {x1, . . . , xn},

say that G is Cohen–Macaulay, respectively Gorenstein, over k if R/I(G)
is Cohen–Macaulay, respectively Gorenstein. For a graph without isolated
vertices,

8. Closing remarks and further directions
The ridge-thickness characterization, Theorem 3.1, is independent of α-
criticality: for every well-covered graph it computes the exact largest p for
which the graph lies in Wp. The main theorem then adds one further con-
dition, namely generation of all missing edges by ridge links, or equivalently
Kr+1-saturation in the complement.
Thus the characterization separates a well-covered extension problem
from an edge-criticality problem. Examples 6.5 and 6.6 also show that the

edge-localization condition Gab ∈Wp−1 is a genuinely stronger local hypoth-
esis; it is useful as a suﬃcient condition, but it is not forced by α-criticality
together with membership in Wp.
This leaves a sharper local problem between the suﬃcient condition of
[14] and the global characterization above.

(r −2)-clique-codegree at least p −1.
The complement correspondence in Corollary 5.2 is meant to be used
in both directions.
Starting from G, it turns the independence-extension
condition into a clique-codegree lower bound in G; starting from a saturated
graph H, it constructs an α-critical graph H and computes its W-index.
The saturation consequences in Corollaries 6.3 and 6.4 illustrate this transfer:
extremal, degree, and order information about Kr+1-saturated complements
becomes structural information about α-critical Wp graphs. A natural next
saturation problem is to determine the sharp minimum of e(H) among n-
vertex graphs satisfying the three conditions in Corollary 5.2, as a function
of n, r, and p.
