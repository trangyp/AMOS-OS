ABSTRACT. We construct the derived Legendrian category Fc(X) for an n-shifted contact
derived Artin stack X and the (∞, 2)-category Legn of Legendrian correspondences in the
context of derived algebraic geometry, with several applications to moduli theory. In brief,
the objects of the category Fc(X) are Legendrian morphisms; the morphism spaces and com-
position operations are defined using equivariant descent. We also establish that Fc(X) em-
beds into an (∞, 2)-category of spans defined by the AKSZ construction. We further eval-
uate topological cobordisms as Lagrangian correspondences to define derived Legendrian
surgery.

1. INTRODUCTION
The (∞, 2)-category of shifted symplectic derived stacks, often denoted Lagn or Sympn,
formalizes the geometric relations between derived moduli spaces. In this setting, the mor-
phisms between shifted symplectic stacks are given by Lagrangian correspondences, with
composition defined via derived intersections. This categorical formulation of spans is
central to the AKSZ construction and extended topological field theories, as extensively
studied by Calaque, Haugseng, and Scheimbauer [4].

There exist, on the other hand, certain situations in derived symplectic geometry where
the symplectic structure is not fixed by group actions, but is only fixed up to a character,
meaning that the resulting quotient is not shifted symplectic anymore. A typical example is
the case of the Frobenius acting on the derived moduli of geometric ℓ-adic local systems on
a smooth projective variety over a finite field. Regarding the geometry of such quotients,
˙Izbudak and Berktav show in [1] that such kinds of examples of moduli problems, includ-
ing projective Higgs bundles, ℓ-adic local systems, and Lie 2-groups, are better encoded
in the context of contact geometry rather than the symplectic setting. In these cases, the
quotient is no longer shifted symplectic; instead, it in fact embodies some form of contact
structures, which we call shifted contact structures in the sense of [5, 6].
To formalize the geometric relations between derived moduli spaces that are not sym-
plectic as discussed above, the “odd-dimensional” counterpart, shifted contact geometry, re-
quires a corresponding categorical foundation where Lagrangian correspondences should
be replaced by the suitable notion of Legendrian correspondence. As established in [1], the
geometric relation between these two settings is governed by the derived symplectification
(or the contact reduction); namely, an n-shifted contact structure on a derived Artin stack
X corresponds to a weight 1 Gm-equivariant n-shifted symplectic structure on its principal
Gm-bundle eX. Consequently, defining a category of contact stacks relies on computing the
equivariant descent of derived Lagrangian intersections.
Results of the paper. In this paper, we construct the derived Legendrian category Fc(X)
for an n-shifted contact derived Artin stack X. In short, the objects of this category are
Legendrian morphisms into X. We define the morphism spaces and their composition opera-
tions via equivariant descent from the derived symplectification eX. To establish the higher
categorical structure, we lift the contact data to eX, where the weight 1 Gm-action imposes
homogeneity conditions on the shifted symplectic forms. The composition of derived in-
tersections is then evaluated using the (∞, 2)-category of Lagrangian spans.
Based on the outline for constructing the category Fc(X) above, we will, in this paper,
establish the proof of the following theorem, along with its several applications.
Theorem 1.1. Let X be an n-shifted contact derived Artin stack. There exists a well-defined de-
rived Legendrian category Fc(X) satisfying the following properties:
(1) The objects of Fc(X) are Legendrian morphisms f : L →X.
(2) The 1-morphism objects HomFc(X)(L1, L2) descend from the derived intersections of Gm-
equivariant Lagrangian structures defined on the derived symplectification eX.
(3) The 2-morphisms and higher composition operations descend from the derived symplecti-
fication via the weight 1 Gm-action and are given by the (∞, 2)-category of Lagrangian
spans.

into a (∞, 2)-category by descending the Gm-equivariant (∞, 2)-category of Lagrangian

9.3. Topological Field Theories and Functoriality. Let Y be a closed oriented topologi-
cal manifold of dimension d −1. Let eX be a k-shifted symplectic derived stack equipped
with a weight 1 Gm-action. By Theorem 3.1, the derived mapping stack Map(Y, eX) has
a (k −d + 1)-shifted symplectic structure. Since the fundamental class integration com-
mutes with the target Gm-action, Map(Y, eX) has a weight 1 Gm-action. The stack quotient
XY ≃[Map(Y, eX)/Gm] has a (k −d + 1)-shifted contact structure.
Let Bordd denote the symmetric monoidal ∞-category of oriented d-dimensional cobor-
disms [4, Section 1.2]. Let Legn denote the global (∞, 2)-category of n-shifted contact de-
rived stacks introduced in Corollary 8.1.
Proposition 9.3. The AKSZ construction extends to a symmetric monoidal ∞-functor
Zc : Bordd →Legk−d+1
capturing derived Legendrian surgery.
Proof. We construct the functor Zc by combining the classical AKSZ functor with equivari-
ant descent. To a closed oriented (d −1)-manifold Y, the functor assigns the (k −d + 1)-
shifted contact stack XY ≃[Map(Y, eX)/Gm]. To an oriented d-dimensional cobordism
W between Y1 and Y2, the functor assigns the derived stack LW ≃[Map(W, eX)/Gm]. By
Proposition 7.1, Map(W, eX) forms a Gm-equivariant Lagrangian span between Map(Y1, eX)
and Map(Y2, eX). Its descent LW thus defines a Legendrian correspondence between XY1
and XY2, representing a 1-morphism in Legk−d+1.
The topological gluing of cobordisms W1 ∪Y W2 corresponds to the homotopy pullback
of the mapping stacks over Map(Y, eX). By Theorem 6.1, this equivariant pullback descends
to the categorical composition of Legendrian spans in Legk−d+1. Therefore, Zc preserves
the composition structure of cobordisms and defines an ∞-functor.
□
This functoriality provides a categorical framework for derived Legendrian surgery. The
geometric gluing of cobordisms maps directly to the associative composition operation in
the global contact category. This evaluates the gluing of moduli problems on d-manifolds
as the composition of local Legendrian boundary conditions.
9.4. Outlook: Towards Derived Contact Surgery. Our construction of the derived Legen-
drian category Fc(X) and the formulation of topological cobordisms as Legendrian spans
(Theorem 7.2) provide the necessary setup to define surgery operations directly on contact
stacks. In classical contact topology, Weinstein surgery is performed by attaching isotropic
and Legendrian handles. In the derived setting, a derived contact surgery along a Legen-
drian morphism f : L →X should correspond to a specific quotient of the derived symplec-
tification of X, modified by the Lagrangian span associated with the surgery cobordism.
Future work will explore how to compute the effect of such surgeries on the resulting
shifted contact structures using this (∞, 2)-categorical framework. In particular, we aim to
establish how the derived mapping stacks of surgery traces behave under Gm-equivariant
descent. This will yield a systematic method for constructing new shifted contact moduli
spaces from existing ones via categorical gluing.
