In classical thermodynamics, the Mpemba effect refers to
the counterintuitive observation that hot water can freeze
faster than cold water, manifesting as an anomalous cross-
ing of dynamical trajectories. While analogues of this phe-
nomenon have been explored in quantum radiative systems
and spin-chain entanglement asymmetry, its connection
to the finite-time decoupling of quantum correlations re-
mains elusive. In this Letter, we uncover a distinct quantum
Mpemba effect associated with entanglement sudden death
(ESD). By analyzing two qubits interacting with local am-
plitude damping reservoirs, we demonstrate that a more
strongly entangled initial state can experience a faster col-
lapse into a separable state than a more weakly entangled
one. We provide an exact analytical derivation of the trajec-
tory crossover dynamics and the ESD time. Finally, we map
the phase diagram of initial state parameters to delineate
the regime where this anomalous entanglement Mpemba
effect occurs, offering insights into the control of quantum
resource lifetimes in dissipative environments.
Introduction
The Mpemba effect, a counterintuitive relaxation phenomenon
where a system initially further from equilibrium (‘hotter’)
reaches the target state faster than one closer to it (‘cooler’),
has reemerged as a focal point in non-equilibrium thermody-
namics. While its origins trace back to Aristotle and its scien-
tific revitalization began with Mpemba’s 1960s experiments
on freezing mixtures [1], a rigorous theoretical foundation
was only recently established. In 2017, Lu and Raz [2] pro-
vided a landmark analysis of classical systems by attributing
the effect to the non-monotonic decay of the slowest-relaxing
eigenmode of the dynamical process, a framework that also
predicted the inverse Mpemba effect. Following this paradigm,
investigations have rapidly expanded to anomalous relaxation
phenomena in classical and increasingly, open quantum sys-
tems [3–13], gaining validation across diverse experimental
platforms [14–17](for a comprehensive review, see Ref. [18–
21]). Furthermore, Chang et al. extended this anomalous
relaxation framework to closed quantum systems utilizing the
imaginary-time evolution approach [22, 23].
The extension of this effect into the quantum regime has
recently revealed novel manifestations that transcend classical
interpretations. In 2022, Ares et al. [24] identified an entan-
glement ‘asymmetry’ Mpemba effect in closed quantum sys-
tems, specifically tilted ferromagnetic states of the XX chain,
which subsequently stimulated extensive theoretical [25–34]
and experimental research [35–39]. Unlike classical thermal
relaxation, this purely quantum phenomenon arises from the
restoration of broken symmetries. Further developments, such
as the spin-wave analysis by Yamashika and Ares [40] and
the ‘Ergotropic Mpemba Effect’ in Gaussian quantum bat-
teries proposed by Medina et al. [41], which has also been
extended to other systems [42, 43], highlighting the growing
importance of understanding how quantum resources exhibit
anomalous decay. In practice, however, no system is perfectly
isolated. Interaction with the environment, whether via thermal
reservoirs, electromagnetic noise, or even relativistic effects
in spacetime, inevitably leads to decoherence and entangle-
ment degradation [44–49]. This decay limits the operational
lifetime of quantum devices and poses a significant hurdle for
long-distance quantum tasks. Crucially, the dynamics of this
degradation are sensitive not only to the environment but also
to the geometric and algebraic properties of the initial state.
In this Letter, we investigate the entanglement dynamics of
two-qubit systems undergoing local amplitude damping. We
demonstrate that within a specific parameter space, the system
exhibits an ‘Entanglement Mpemba Effect’: a state with higher
initial entanglement (the ‘hot’ state) can decay faster and reach
‘entanglement sudden death’ (the ‘frozen’ state) sooner than
a state with lower initial entanglement (the ‘cold’ state). By
providing an exact solution to the crossover dynamics, we
construct a comprehensive phase diagram that delineates the
boundaries of this effect. Our results reveal that the Mpemba
effect in quantum systems is not merely a thermal analogy
but a fundamental feature of dissipative entanglement evolu-

Dynamics.
We consider a composite system consisting of
two independent two-level subsystems (e.g., atoms or super-
conducting qubits), labeled A and B. As illustrated in Fig. 1,
each subsystem is weakly coupled to its own local bosonic
reservoir. We assume the subsystems are spatially separated
such that they do not interact directly, and no particle exchange
occurs between them. Under the Born-Markov and rotating-

by a quantum master equation. Given that the reservoirs are
maintained at zero temperature (T = 0), they act as purely dis-
sipative sinks that preclude thermal excitations. Consequently,
each subsystem undergoes a pure amplitude damping process,
physically corresponding to spontaneous emission.

Conclusion
In summary, while environmental dissipation typically
degrades quantum resources, leading to entanglement decay or
sudden death, this work uncovers a counterintuitive dynamical
phenomenon: the entanglement sudden death Mpemba Effect.
By quantifying entanglement via concurrence, we observed
a novel crossing behavior in the dynamical trajectories of
two-subsystem states. Crucially, under identical environmental
conditions, a system initialized with higher entanglement
can reach a completely separable state faster than one with
lower initial entanglement. Phase diagram analysis reveals that

the Mpemba effect manifests exclusively within a restricted
regime of physically accessible parameters, closely echoing
the classical thermodynamic Mpemba effect, which similarly
occurs only within specific initial temperature intervals.
Furthermore, we find that asymmetric dissipation between
the two subsystems merely modulates the magnitude of the
effect rather than altering the boundaries of its parameter
regime. Although actual system-environment couplings in
physical implementations may be more intricate, the existence
of the Mpemba effect carries profound implications for both
desktop quantum experiments and space-borne quantum
communications. It reveals that initial state engineering is a
critical factor in maximizing the effective lifetime of entangled
pairs, thereby offering valuable guidelines for future quantum
technologies.
