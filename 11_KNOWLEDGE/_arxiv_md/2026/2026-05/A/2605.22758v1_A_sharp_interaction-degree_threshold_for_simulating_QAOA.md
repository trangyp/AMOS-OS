A SHARP INTERACTION-DEGREE THRESHOLD FOR
SIMULATING QAOA
RALFS ĀBOLIŅŠ AND ANDRIS AMBAINIS
Center for Quantum Computing Science, University of Latvia
{ralfs.abolins, andris.ambainis}@lu.lv
Abstract. We identify a sharp interaction-degree threshold for the classical
simulation of QAOA with 2-local cost functions. At degree 3, classical sam-
pling from depth-1 QAOA with small multiplicative error would collapse the
polynomial hierarchy to its third level. At degree 2, exact classical sampling
from depth-p QAOA on n qubits runs in time nO(1) whenever p = O(log n).
The hard degree-3 instances have trivially optimizable cost functions, so sam-
pling hardness does not by itself imply a quantum optimization advantage.
1. Introduction
QAOA (the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm, [FGG14]) is one
of the main near-term quantum algorithms.
Farhi and Harrow [FH16] showed
that, already at depth 1, classical sampling from the output distribution of QAOA
with suﬀiciently small multiplicative error would collapse the polynomial hierarchy.
Their result is often cited as evidence that even very shallow QAOA can exhibit
behavior inaccessible to classical sampling.
We examine the structural conditions under which this hardness persists. We
consider 2-local cost functions C(x1, . . . , xn) = ∑
i Ci(x1, . . . , xn), where each term
depends on at most two variables, and parameterize instances by their interaction
degree, the maximum number of other variables coupled to any single variable.
We show that depth-1 QAOA remains classically hard to sample from when the
interaction degree is at most 3. Moreover, the hard instances can be chosen so that
the cost function is trivial to optimize classically, so sampling hardness alone does
not yield a quantum advantage for optimization.
We then show that at interaction degree 2, every marginal output probability of
a depth-p QAOA circuit can be computed classically in time polynomial in n and
exponential only in p. In particular, exact sampling is polynomial-time whenever
p = O(log n). The simulation uses the Markov–Shi algorithm for quantum circuits
of small cut width [MS08].
A comparable dichotomy holds classically for constraint satisfaction. SAT re-
stricted to instances in which every variable occurs in at most 3 clauses remains
NP-complete, but becomes polynomial-time solvable when every variable occurs in
at most 2 clauses [Tov84]. The transition from 2-SAT to 3-SAT [Kar72, GJ79] is the
most familiar instance of the same pattern. In the classical case the parameter sep-
arates P from NP-completeness, while for QAOA it separates classical simulability
from sampling hardness.
All simulation bounds in this paper count arithmetic operations on matrix entries.
Since every gate entry in our constructions lies in Q(eiπ/4), every intermediate
value in the simulation belongs to the same field and is represented by four rational
coeﬀicients whose bit lengths grow at most linearly in the circuit size. Consequently,
the same asymptotic bounds hold in the bit model.

Definition 1. Given a cost function expressed as a sum of 2-local terms C = ∑
i Ci,
the interaction graph of this decomposition is the simple graph on vertex set [n]
with an edge {j, k} whenever some term Ci depends nontrivially on both variables j
and k (that is, flipping either bit can change the value of Ci). The interaction
degree is the maximum vertex degree of this graph. A QAOA instance specifies
the decomposition, and we write degree for interaction degree when the meaning is
clear.
In a post-selected circuit the output distribution is conditioned on a designated
register yielding |0 · · · 0⟩. Since there is no bound on how small this conditioning
probability may be, post-selection is not physically realizable but is a theoretical
tool for relating complexity classes. The output probability of such a circuit is a
ratio of two event probabilities, and the relevant error notion must preserve such
ratios.
Definition 2. A classical algorithm samples with multiplicative error c ≥1 from
a distribution D on {0, 1}n if it produces samples from a distribution D′ satisfying
c−1D(x) ≤D′(x) ≤c D(x) for every x.
By summing the pointwise bounds, multiplicative error extends to event proba-
bilities. A multiplicative-error sampler therefore approximates both the numerator
and denominator of a post-selected acceptance probability, which is the mechanism
behind the hardness proofs below.

3. Hardness at interaction degree three
Two prior results establish sampling hardness for restricted circuit families using
similar post-selection arguments. Bremner, Jozsa, and Shepherd [BJS11] proved
PostIQP = PP, where IQP circuits have the form H⊗nD H⊗n |0n⟩with D diagonal
in the computational basis. Farhi and Harrow [FH16] extended this to QAOA by
proving PostQAOA1 = PostBQP. Both constructions reduce an arbitrary PostBQP
circuit to the restricted family by replacing each intermediate Hadamard with a
post-selected diagonal coupling to a fresh auxiliary qubit. The two constructions
differ only in the single-qubit gate F that completes each step, and hence in the
diagonal coupling it requires.
We construct the diagonal coupling for general F and add a preprocessing step
that reduces the interaction degree from unbounded to 3.

4. Tractability at interaction degree two
We now show that the degree-3 bound is tight. A graph of maximum degree 2
decomposes into paths, cycles, and isolated vertices, and the QAOA circuit on each
component has small cut width.
Definition 11. A gate on qubits S crosses cut i ∈{1, . . . , n −1} if min S ≤i <
max S. Single-qubit gates cross no cut. The cut width of a circuit on qubits [n] is
the maximum number of gates crossing any single cut.

Markov and Shi simulate a circuit by contracting its tensor network along a
linear qubit ordering, at cost exponential in the number of gates crossing any single
cut.
Proposition 12 (Markov–Shi [MS08, Proposition 5.1]). A quantum circuit of
size T and cut width r, in which each gate has an equal number of input and output
qubits, can be simulated deterministically in time T O(1) exp[O(r)].
By simulation we mean computing Pr[ZS = zS] for a specified subset S ⊆[n] and
outcome zS. Unmeasured qubits are traced out during contraction at no additional
asymptotic cost.
Theorem 13. Let Q be a depth-p QAOA circuit on n qubits whose interaction graph
has maximum degree 2. For any S ⊆[n] and zS ∈{0, 1}|S|, the marginal probability
Pr[ZS = zS] can be computed deterministically in time (np)O(1) exp(O(p)).
Proof. Phase gates couple only qubits within the same component and mixer gates
act on single qubits, so the state factors as |ψ⟩= ⊗
c |ψc⟩. It therefore suﬀices to
simulate each component independently.
A graph of maximum degree 2 decomposes into isolated vertices, paths, and
cycles. An isolated vertex sees only single-qubit gates, so its output distribution is
computable in O(p) operations. For a linear ordering of the vertices, let δ(i) denote
the number of interaction-graph edges crossing cut i. Each such edge contributes
one gate per phase layer, so the circuit cut width is p · maxi δ(i). For a path in
the natural ordering, each edge {i, i+1} crosses only cut i, so maxi δ(i) = 1 and
the cut width is p. For a cycle, the wrap-around edge {1, k} crosses every cut, so
maxi δ(i) = 2 and the cut width is 2p.
In every case the cut width is O(p) and the circuit has size O(kp). Proposition 12
gives runtime (kp)O(1) exp(O(p)) per component, and summing over components
whose sizes total n gives (np)O(1) exp(O(p)).
□
Corollary 14. The output distribution of a depth-p QAOA circuit on n qubits
with interaction degree at most 2 can be sampled exactly by a classical randomized
algorithm in time (np)O(1) exp(O(p)). When p = O(log n), this equals nO(1).
Proof. Sample z1, . . . , zn sequentially by the chain rule.
At step t, two calls to

5. Discussion
The simulation threshold for 2-local QAOA lies between interaction degrees 2
and 3.
Since exact simulation implies simulation with any multiplicative error,
degree-2 QAOA is classically easy under both error models when p = O(log n).
The degree-2 simulation runs in time exponential in p, and whether a different
method can improve this is open. Corollary 8 assumes multiplicative error, and
extending the hardness to additive error would require an anticoncentration bound
and an average-case hardness conjecture for random 2-local cost functions on degree-
3 graphs.
The analogous results for IQP [BMS16] do not transfer because the
anticoncentration proof uses completeness of the interaction graph and the average-
case families have unbounded degree. The hard instances have trivially optimizable
cost functions (Proposition 10), and whether there exist families for which both
optimization and sampling are hard remains open.
