Antisymmetric spontaneous resistivity anisotropy due to hard-axis collapse in
polycrystalline Co thin films
Y. Fernandes, J. Geshev,∗A. M. H. de Andrade, and A. D. C. Viegas
Instituto de F´ısica, Universidade Federal do Rio Grande do Sul,
Porto Alegre, 91501-970 Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil
(Dated: May 27, 2026)
We investigate magnetoresistance phenomena associated with the magnetization hard-axis col-
lapse in polycrystalline Co thin films. Transport measurements reveal that, for specific orientations
of the applied magnetic field, the system exhibits distinct remanent resistance levels in both the
in-plane longitudinal and transverse voltage responses.
In particular, the planar Hall resistance
shows multiple stable and reproducible levels at room temperature, enabling the identification of
at least three remanent states that can be distinguished and used for information storage. These
resistance levels originate from non-uniform magnetic configurations stabilized after the applica-
tion and removal of the external magnetic field in the hard-axis region. Since this phenomenon
remains largely unexplored, we present an incipient study addressing its potential implications from
an applied-physics perspective. The observation of such behavior in polycrystalline Co thin films
grown on Si substrates suggests a simple and low-cost platform for spintronic memory and sensing
devices based on the remanent planar Hall effect.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The increasing demand for higher-density and multi-
functional data storage technologies has stimulated the
search for alternative mechanisms for information en-
coding beyond conventional schemes.
In this context,
multi-level magnetic memory devices have attracted con-
siderable attention, as they enable the storage of more
than one bit per cell and therefore enhance informa-
tion density without scaling down device dimensions.
Magnetotransport phenomena in thin films are particu-
larly well suited for implementing such functionalities due
to their compatibility with planar device architectures
and electrical readout schemes, which are directly com-
patible with complementary metal–oxide–semiconductor
(CMOS) technology platforms that underpin modern in-
tegrated circuits.
Within this framework, the planar Hall effect (PHE),
which arises from the anisotropic magnetoresistance
(AMR) [1] in ferromagnetic (FM) materials, is partic-
ularly sensitive to the orientation of the magnetization
(M) with respect to the electric current. This sensitivity
enables the electrical detection of distinct magnetic con-
figurations, making it suitable for memory applications.
A remarkable phenomenon observed in thin films with
in-plane uniaxial magnetic anisotropy is the so-called
hard-axis collapse (HAC) [2, 3], in which the remanent
magnetic state (acheived after the application of a sat-
urating external magnetic field Hsat and its subsequent
reduction to zero) in the vicinity of the film magneti-
zation hard axis (HA), deviates significantly from that
predicted by the coherent rotation model [4, 5].
This
phenomenon is characterized by a behavior observed as
α, the angle between H and the nominal easy axis (EA),

approaches 90◦, where prominent peaks appear in the
remanent magnetization, Mrem.
As a result, the sys-
tem stabilizes in multiple remanent magnetic configura-
tions rather than in a single state, often associated with
non-uniform magnetization distributions that give rise to
measurable changes in the electrical resistance [6, 7].
Building on this scenario, we demonstrate that HAC
in polycrystalline Co thin films can induce at least three
stable and distinguishable resistance levels. These states
originate from underlying non-uniform magnetic con-
figurations and can be reproducibly accessed through
magnetic-field protocols. Notably, polycrystalline Co was
chosen due to its low cost and ease of deposition, in con-
trast to more complex material systems typically em-
ployed in integrated circuits.
These results may open
avenues for potential applications in magnetic memory
and sensing devices based on simple FM thin films.
II.
EXPERIMENT
A.
Sample Growth
It has been reported that HAC emerges in systems
exhibiting a certain degree of dispersion in the magnetic
anisotropy axes [2], for which polycrystallinity plays
a key role.
To satisfy this requirement, three poly-
crystalline 20-nm-thick Co films were grown at room
temperature onto naturally oxidized Si(100) substrates
with lateral dimensions of approximately 1 cm × 1 cm
in the presence of an in-plane magnetic field of 2 kOe,
applied to induce uniaxial magnetic anisotropy.
The
films were deposited by dc magnetron sputtering using
an AJA ATC Orion-8 system in a confocal configu-
ration, with the substrate holder rotating at 40 rpm,
a 5.8 inches target-to-substrate distance, and a base
pressure below 2 × 10−8 Torr prior to deposition.
All
targets had a purity exceeding 99.95%.

that the sign of TAMR alternates depending on the rema-

butions from domain-wall nucleation and the anomalous
Hall effect cannot be ruled out [11].
Although a similar result could, in principle, arise from
a longitudinal contribution caused by a misalignment of
the Hall cross, we do not believe this to be the dominant
mechanism, since such a contribution was not observed in
the measurements performed above technical saturation.
Furthermore, additional measurements performed with
the contact probes intentionally misaligned by approxi-
mately 45◦did not provide a conclusive evidence that the
antisymmetric behavior originates from a superposition
of LAMR and TAMR.
As already mentioned, it is well established [2, 3] that
the HAC is associated with remanent states exhibiting a
certain dispersion in the orientations of the magnetiza-
tion easy axes. In particular, when α is in the HA re-
gion, the minimum-energy configuration corresponds to
an average magnetization oriented along an intermediate
direction between the HA and the EA, distinguishing it
from other remanent states. Micromagnetic calculations
are able to reproduce similar behavior, indicating that
the HAC arises from remanent states characterized by
microdomains in which the magnetization vectors adopt
V-type configurations with respect to the HA [7].
We believe that the MR data shown in Fig. 3(c) may
arise from states similar to those predicted by micro-
magnetic simulations, i.e., in our real samples, when
90◦−ϵ < α < 90◦, for a certain critical angle ϵ, it is pos-
sible that the average magnetization is oriented along an
intermediate direction between 0◦and 90◦. Conversely,
for 90◦< α < 90◦+ ϵ, it becomes energetically favorable
for a fraction of the magnetic moments to rotate and
reverse their orientations, such that it is possible that
the average magnetization lies between 90◦and 180◦.
Thus, the measured resistance likely reflects these states
through their signatures in the average resistivity [9],
which can be expressed as an integral over all orienta-
tions of the magnetic moments, i.e., Rxx(xy) ∝⟨ρxx(xy)⟩.

peaks near α = 90◦as they are not able to distinguish be-
tween the remanent states described above. However, the
antisymmetric TAMR characteristics enables the identi-

least three distinct remanent states. In this context, it is
possible to encode information in these states, induced by

This suggests
that the key features of the HAC relevant for techno-
logical applications possess, to some extent, structural
tolerance. In addition, HAC has been observed over a
thickness range from 5 to at least 150 nm in Co films with
different buffer layers (for further details, see Refs. 12–
14), providing additional flexibility for device design.
IV.
SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
We perform magnetization and magnetoresistance
measurements in both saturated and remanent states
of polycrystalline Co thin films exhibiting magnetiza-
tion hard-axis collapse.
We demonstrate that distinct
remanent states can be clearly differentiated, and that
their differences can be readily and accurately detected
through longitudinal anisotropic magnetoresistance.
We conclude that the hard-axis collapse may be ex-
ploited for potential applications.
A key advantage of
the systems studied here lies in their simplicity and low
fabrication cost, as well as in the fact that they rely on
remanent states that naturally emerge after the applica-
tion of an external magnetic field within specific angular
ranges.
Therefore, no external stimulus is required to
maintain a given magnetic state.
In addition, the use of Si-based substrates makes these
systems directly compatible with standard CMOS pro-
cessing, which is a key requirement for large-scale inte-
gration into conventional semiconductor technology plat-
forms.
Nevertheless, further advances are required to
assess the scalability of this effect and to evaluate the
feasibility of reducing the physical dimensions of devices
operating based on this mechanism.
