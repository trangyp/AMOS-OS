Abstract
The variational model for a ferroelectric nematic bears close resemblance to the well-known
energy model for micromagnetics. Despite this similarity, the two models operate in fundamen-
tally distinct parameter regimes describing different physics. In this paper we establish that the
ferroelectric nematic energy functional Γ-converges to the energy of a nematic with high elastic
anisotropy.

to the underlying orientational order of the nematic.

Physical motivation and relationship to micromagnetics.
2.1
Ferroelectric nematics and high-splay models
A ferroelectric nematic liquid crystal [4] is a nematic liquid with a macroscopic spontaneous po-
larization P. This polarization is locally parallel to the nematic director n, which specifies the
preferred local molecular orientation [5]. The polarization direction can be aligned by confining
the material between two glass plates and/or applying an external electric field. The experiments
demonstrate formation of domain walls with a shape that is dictated by the anisotropic surface
interactions with the “easy axis” of the substrate and by the orientational elasticity of the ferroelec-
tric nematic [3, 2]. Crucially, in this process the splay of P is energetically costly because it creates
a bound space charge of bulk density proportional to div P and thus increases the electrostatic
energy.

be fully developed. The existing work [3] has focused on adapting the well-known director-based
approach to standard nematics, based on the Oseen-Frank energy,

should not obscure an important difference in the relevant asymptotic regimes. In micromagnetics,
the unscreened magnetostatic interaction gives rise to a variety of limiting behaviors depending
on the scaling and domain geometry: in some thin-film regimes it reduces at leading order to an
energetic penalty or a hard constraint on the normal component of magnetization [9, 12], while in
other regimes the nonlocal contribution is retained in a reduced limit [11].
For ferroelectric nematics the situation is different. The electrostatic field is generated by bound
polarization charge, whose bulk density is proportional to −div P, together with surface charge
proportional to P · ν. The electrostatic cost associated with these charges is large relative to the
orientational elastic energy. Therefore the distinction from micromagnetics is not merely that the
electrostatic potential in the present model satisfies a screened Helmholtz equation rather than
