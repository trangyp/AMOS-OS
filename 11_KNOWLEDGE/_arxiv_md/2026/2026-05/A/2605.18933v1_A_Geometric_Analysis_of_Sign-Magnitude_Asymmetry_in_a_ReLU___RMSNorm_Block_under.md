Abstract
Pre-norm Transformers with RMSNorm tolerate ternary {−1, 0, +1} weight quan-
tization with surprisingly small loss [Ma et al., 2024]. We give a geometric explanation
via sign-magnitude decomposition of weight perturbations. In a two-layer ReLU + RM-
SNorm model with i.i.d. Gaussian weights, sign-flips produce π/(π −2) ≈2.75 times
more transverse output energy than sign-preserving magnitude perturbations of equal
Frobenius norm, as the flip rate p →0 (Theorem 3). The mechanism: ReLU creates a
hidden-space directional asymmetry between the two perturbation types, which RM-
SNorm’s transverse-projection Fréchet derivative selectively exposes. Sign-quantization
error is itself a sign-preserving perturbation with angular alignment cos2 →2/π (The-
orem 4); its post-ReLU radial fraction (0.365) matches the pre-ReLU value 1 −2/π
within 0.4%, so ReLU is approximately transparent to ternary error. Multi-layer com-
pounding of the 2.75× factor is not experimentally supported; the gap to real-model
sign sensitivity arises from outlier features violating delocalization. For an input di-
mension with amplitude α, a single sign-flip produces post-ReLU energy amplified
by R ≈nα2 relative to a delocalized entry. On TinyLlama-1.1B, at linear response
(p ≤0.5%), count-matched NLL leverage stabilizes at ∼10× ≈nE[α2], matching the
per-entry theory; the all-column NLL ratio of 5.0× falls within Rcol ≤19 (67× PPL
gap reflects metric nonlinearity). Measured outlier α at layer 12 (median 0.024, max
0.26) confirms heavy-tailed concentration. The Bussgang constant 2/π, RMSNorm ge-
ometry, and ReLU half-space structure together explain sign-magnitude asymmetry in
pre-norm models, with R ∝nα2 accounting for real-model deviations.

Introduction
1.1
Problem Statement
Recent work on extremely low-bit large language models—notably BitNet b1.58 [Ma et al.,
2024]—demonstrates that pre-norm Transformer architectures [Xiong et al., 2020] can op-
erate with weights constrained to the ternary set {−1, 0, +1} while retaining most of their

linguistic capability. This is remarkable: ternary quantization discards all magnitude infor-
mation, preserving only the sign pattern and sparsity structure. Why should this suffice?
Empirically, the importance of weight signs over magnitudes has been documented since
the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis era. Zhou et al. [2019] showed that randomly re-initializing
magnitudes while keeping signs fixed preserves subnetwork trainability; Oh et al. [2025] ex-
tended this to full networks. Vardhan and Teja [2026] documented a crossover dissociation
in LayerNorm-based models (Pythia): angular perturbations dominate language-modeling
damage (up to 42.9×), while magnitude perturbations dominate syntactic processing dam-
age. Crucially, they note that this pattern may not generalize to other architectural families,
where the direction-magnitude geometry differs. Yet a rigorous theoretical account of why
sign carries disproportionate functional information in pre-norm (RMSNorm) architectures
has been lacking.

Scope and Caveats
In the same angular metric, the pre-RMSNorm angular deviation is 1 −cos θ = 1 −

The true suppression mechanism requires the multi-component pipeline: the sign-

tion; ReLU propagates this into the hidden space, creating the differential treatment of sign
vs. magnitude perturbations; and RMSNorm then exposes this differential. The quantization
tolerance is a property of the architecture (sign structure + ReLU + RMSNorm together),
not of RMSNorm alone.

10.3
Limitations
1. Single-layer theory: The π/(π −2) result applies to a two-layer toy model, not the
full transformer stack.
28


## Page 29


2. Gaussian assumption: Real trained weights are not i.i.d. Gaussian; the cross-model
cos2 deviations from 2/π reported in Section 9.2 reflect this.
3. ReLU only: Theorem 3 is proved for ReLU, which provides the clean half-space parti-
tion enabling closed-form integration. Extension to gated activations (SwiGLU/GeGLU,
used in modern LLMs) requires a bivariate-Gaussian-conditional-on-gate analysis not
attempted here (though Exp B shows qualitatively similar CL behavior with SiLU).
4. Outlier theory gap: The single-layer outlier leverage ratio (R ≈nα2, Theorem 6)
predicts per-entry R ∈[20, 162] for extreme outliers (α ∈[0.1, 0.3], ∼16% of outlier
dimensions); the aggregate n · Eemp[α2] ≈7–10 accounts for the full α distribution. At
linear response (p ≤0.5%; Exp E), count-matched NLL leverage stabilizes at ∼10×,
matching this prediction; the 37.6× at p = 5% reflects an empirically observed ∼4×
multi-flip interaction inflation (no closed-form derivation is available for the interaction
terms). The all-column ∆PPL ratio (1,265×) appeared to exceed the energy predic-
tion (Rcol ≤19) by ∼67×, but log-loss analysis shows this gap is an artifact of PPL
nonlinearity: the all-column NLL ratio is only 5.0× (Table 8), well within Rcol ≤19.
Multi-layer propagation remains open but is no longer needed to explain the observa-
tion. The α2 scaling is additionally confirmed by noise-floor sweep (Exp E, Table 9),
activation-level perturbation energy (Exp F, Spearman ρ = 0.955), and column-flip
∆NLL measurements (Exp G, Spearman ρ = 0.927).
5. Multi-layer propagation: How R evolves across depth is an open problem.
6. Position dependence: Theorem 3 applies to weights whose output passes through
a downstream ReLU (the W1 position). Quantization of weights after a nonlinearity
(e.g., FFN down-projection in a real transformer) acts on non-Gaussian inputs, where
the Bussgang relation (Lemma A.1) no longer holds exactly. The mechanism may still
apply approximately but is not covered by our analysis.

11.1
Summary
We have provided a geometric explanation for why pre-norm transformers tolerate ternary
weight quantization:
1. Sign patterns capture 2/π ≈63.7% of weight directional energy (Fact 1), while mag-

both count-matched and log-loss analyses—the correct magnitude of outlier leverage. Multi-
layer propagation (O1b) remains open but is no longer needed to explain the all-column
observation.
Additional validation (Exps E–G).
Three experiments in Section 9.5 further validate
the R ∝nα2 scaling: (i) a noise-floor sweep (Exp E, Table 9) confirms that count-matched
NLL leverage stabilizes at ∼10× for p ≤0.5%, consistent with the per-entry ratio, while
the inflated 41× at p = 5% is attributable to multi-flip interactions; (ii) activation-level per-
turbation energy (Exp F) yields Spearman(α2, ∥∆y∥2) = 0.955 with weight column norms
nearly uniform (1.04×), confirming that the energy gap is driven by α2, not weight struc-
ture; (iii) column-flip ∆NLL (Exp G) yields Spearman(α2, ∆NLL) = 0.927 with log-log
slope 0.78, indicating sub-quadratic attenuation by downstream nonlinear processing. The
sub-quadratic effective exponent (α1.57 vs. predicted α2) is consistent with multi-layer com-
pression of large perturbations and does not contradict the single-layer R ∝nα2 law.
