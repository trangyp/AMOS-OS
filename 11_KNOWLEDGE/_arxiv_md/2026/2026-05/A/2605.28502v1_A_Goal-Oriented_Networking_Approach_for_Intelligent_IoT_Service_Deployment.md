A Goal-Oriented Networking Approach
for Intelligent IoT Service Deployment
Federico Tonini, Davide Borsatti, Wint Yi Poe, Riccardo Trivisonno, Walter Cerroni
Abstract—The first 6G standardization efforts are about to
start, shaping the new generation of mobile networks. The IMT-
2030 extends the IMT-2020 by expanding its usage scenarios
to Immersive, Massive, and Hyper-Reliable and Low-Latency
Communications. It also introduces novel scenarios by integrating
Artificial Intelligence and Sensing with Communication and
supporting Ubiquitous Connectivity. Compared to the previous
generation, 6G is expected to improve not only throughput and
latency, but also coverage and energy efficiency. A paradigm
called Goal-Oriented (GO) communications has recently emerged
as a promising solution to improve network efficiency. It relies on
the fact that the goal of the communication network is to achieve a
specific task with a defined accuracy, rather than creating perfect
data delivery. Intelligent devices can pre-process data to send
only what is relevant to achieve the task, thus saving precious
network resources and energy. Recent works demonstrate that
incorporating service- and application-level KPIs in the network
allows to achieve higher communication efficiency for devices, but
the consequence of using such techniques on the network itself
has not yet been explored. This paper proposes a practical end-to-
end framework to assess energy consumption, latency, and goal
accuracy KPIs, which includes a Multi-Objective optimization
model to evaluate the trade-offs between the multiple KPIs
relevant to GO networking. We demonstrate, through simulation,
that the network can benefit from the application of the GO
paradigm, indicating its potential in future network architectures.
Index Terms—6G, goal-oriented communications, intelligent
IoT, energy efficiency

6G is expected to evolve into a more user-centric network [4],
enabling user-focused communications and dynamic service
delivery tailored to individual needs [5]. New paradigms that
break the traditional separation between communication and
computation are required to achieve these goals.
Goal-Oriented (GO) communications represent a relatively
new approach that prioritizes the transmission of only the most
relevant data necessary to achieve a specific task or goal, which
inherently leads to more efficient use of resources, including
energy [5]. Instead of focusing on delivering all bits with
a required quality, GO communications leverage intelligence
at the IoT devices and service level information to reduce
the amount of data to be sent through the network while
achieving the pre-defined goal with a specified accuracy level.
Recent solutions for intelligent IoT adopting GO communica-
tions mostly focus on reducing energy consumption during
transmission or optimizing communication and computing
resources, targeting Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) for
single IoT devices or cloud computing nodes.
However, to the best of our knowledge, what is still missing
is an evaluation methodology that allows to take into account
also the effects on other network components, considering the
impact of GO communications from an end-to-end perspec-
tive. Such a GO networking perspective is, in our opinion,
what must be considered to assess user-centered, service-level
KPIs to determine how satisfactorily an intended goal has
been achieved. Interestingly, the introduction of service-level
KPIs along with conventional network KPIs might generate
conflicting situations. For example, this is the case of a
system that aims to reduce energy consumption by running
low-complexity (and low-performing) AI algorithms, while
also seeking to maximize the overall acceptable accuracy
of the intended goal. In such cases, Multi-Objective (MO)
optimization problems with conflicting objectives must be
solved to allocate the right network resources and distribute
the most appropriate AI models.
In this paper, we propose a novel optimization framework
for deploying AI workloads in IoT services with intelligence
at the device level, following a GO networking approach. We
consider an IoT scenario with intelligent cameras for object
detection, where the AI-based processing can be performed
at the device, in the cloud, or in a hybrid configuration. In
particular, the main contributions of our work include:
• The original formulation of a MO optimization model
based on Integer Linear Programming (ILP), encompass-
ing both service and network requirements, aiming at
minimizing the energy consumption while achieving pre-
defined service goals.

II. EFFORTS ON IOT AND GO COMMUNICATIONS
TOWARDS 6G
This section first provides an overview of the state of the art
in GO communications for Intelligent IoT. Then, an overview
of the main standardization efforts and corresponding relevant
technical areas considered in the 6G standardization process
is presented.
A. State-of-the-art of GO Communications Strategies and In-
telligent IoT
GO communication can be defined as a communication
paradigm where the primary objective is the fulfillment of a
specific task or goal, characterized by a set of requirements
that, if attained, determine its accomplishment. This approach
emphasizes the relevance and significance of the transmitted
information in relation to the intended outcome, rather than
merely focusing on the accurate transmission of data. In this
context, the effectiveness of the communication is measured
by how well it supports the achievement of the predefined
goal, which can include various performance metrics such as
accuracy, latency, and resource efficiency [6]. While traditional
communication systems focus on making sure that messages
are sent and received accurately, without considering the
purpose of the communication, in GO communications the
communication system should be designed to help achieve
specific tasks, rather than just ensuring that the messages are
reconstructed perfectly [7]. This behavior has direct implica-
tions for the air interface by reducing traffic load, improving
spectrum usage, and enabling more efficient access strategies,
especially important in scenarios involving low-end, energy-
constrained devices.
GO communications are gaining increasing attention in the
research community, with a growing number of recent works
emerging on the topic. The authors in [8] employ a convo-
lutional encoder that is jointly trained with a convolutional
decoder at the edge server to minimize the amount of data
sent over the network, which directly contributes to lower
energy consumption during transmission. A similar approach
is reported in [9], showing the benefits of reducing the amount
of data that needs to be transmitted while simultaneously
improving robustness against communication channel errors
and compensating for poor Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR). In
[6], the authors propose to equip IoT devices with a GO com-
munication module that distills and transmits only the relevant
data features necessary to achieve the communication goal.
This module employs effective sensing techniques to ensure
that only pertinent information is collected, thereby optimizing
resource usage. The framework also includes a GO opti-
mization engine that dynamically manages network resources
based on real-time measurements of energy consumption and
goal effectiveness. The paper provides different examples of
practical applications of their approach in scenarios such as
edge inference, cooperative sensing, and Federated Learning
(FL), highlighting the substantial gains in energy efficiency,
latency, and accuracy achieved through GO communication
strategies.
The solution provided in [10] employs adaptive resource
management strategies that dynamically allocate communica-
tion and computational resources based on the current system
state and application requirements. The use of ensemble infer-
ence, where multiple models work collaboratively to improve
accuracy and efficiency, enhances energy efficiency compared
to standalone inference methods, as it allows relaxation of
the communication requirements for the User Equipments
(UEs). Other techniques can be used to reduce the required
transmitted bits while effectively achieving communication
goals. In [7], the authors analyze GO signal preprocessing
and quantization techniques that can operate on the input
signals and data by focusing on relevant features and removing
unnecessary data, thus reducing the amount of information that
needs to be transmitted. Data clusters can also be formed, e.g.,
in a scenario where IoT devices are tasked with monitoring
environmental conditions, so that they can transmit only sig-
nificant changes rather than continuous data streams.
All the aforementioned works contribute significantly to
advancing energy-efficient solutions for GO communications.
However, they primarily focus on the energy consumption and
latency of IoT devices and cloud nodes, failing to consider the
contribution of the rest of the network. Studies indicate that the
Radio Access Network (RAN) alone is responsible for 70%
of a mobile network energy consumption [11], and its contri-
bution cannot be neglected when evaluating GO networking
strategies. A true end-to-end analysis of KPIs such as energy
consumption, latency, and task accuracy must be provided
to support the adoption of existing and the development of
new GO strategies. Our prior work [12] introduced one of
the first comprehensive end-to-end KPI models capturing en-
ergy consumption, latency, and accuracy for IoT applications.
Building on this, we advance the state of the art by proposing

B. Standardization Initiatives and Relevance
International Telecommunication Union (ITU) provided a
Recommendation containing usage scenarios and capabilities
of IMT-2030 [4]. While scenarios such as immersive com-
munication, massive communication, and hyper-reliable and
low-latency communication represent a direct evolution of
IMT-2020 services, ITU also introduces integrated sensing
and communication, AI and communication, and ubiquitous
connectivity as new types of services envisioned for 6G.
Among others, the Recommendation highlights the need to
support distributed computing and AI applications as well as
the integration of AI and compute functionalities into IMT-
2030. Sensing, AI, and sustainability are notably among the
new capabilities that IMT-2030 is expected to provide.
The 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) has also
recently started its efforts in the definition of technical spec-
ifications for 6G. Among the potential areas that will lay the
foundations of future generation networks, the support for
different IoT device types (e.g., for Ambient-IoT (AIoT) [13]),
and service-aware intelligent networks have been considered,
along with the native integration of AI frameworks [14]
for intelligent IoT services. 6G capabilities will also allow
embodiment of sensing with Radio Frequency (RF) or other
non-RF sources (e.g., environmental detectors, cameras, etc.)
[15]. IoT devices can therefore contribute to enriching the
network sensing capabilities.
The support for IoT communications started with Release
16 with the introduction of cellular IoT and its evolution
for the 5G System, providing also enhancements for Narrow-
Band (NB)-IoT [16]. Release 17 extends the support to non-
terrestrial networks, industrial IoT while introducing energy
efficiency and power saving mechanisms [17]. Release 18
introduces support for personal IoT and residential networks
[18]. Release 19 introduces AIoT for IoT applications with
ultra-low cost and ultra-low power devices [13]. The 6G
System is expected to support diverse IoT device types and
use cases by design from day one [14]. This calls for mecha-
nisms that can efficiently manage intelligent, AI-enhanced IoT
devices and coordinate their capabilities within the network
[3].
In this paper, we focus on a use case where IoT devices
are able to sense the environment by taking images, which
are elaborated by AI models integrated and distributed in
both the devices and the network. We propose a strategy to
deploy such services on the network in an energy-efficient way,
while providing the necessary service guarantees to achieve
the communication goal. We prove by simulation the benefits
provided by the integration of intelligent IoT devices and
services in the network in terms of energy efficiency and
resource usage.

III. USE CASE AND KPIS DESCRIPTION
In IoT contexts, a common use case for GO communications
is object detection [6], [8]–[10], where the goal of the com-
munication system is to detect objects in images. A possible
scenario for intelligent IoT is depicted in Figure 1. AnIoT
device must be deployed to capture environmental data in its
area, like images of the surroundings. To do so, one must
decide on the specific AI model and where to execute inference
tasks based on energy, latency, and accuracy requirements.
Two types of devices are considered. Conventional IoT devices
rely on the network resources to deliver data and on AI models
on the cloud to perform inference tasks. Instead, Intelligent IoT
devices can perform data processing locally with AI models,
with or without network assistance, depending on targets like
latency, energy or task efficiency, and accuracy. A set of
Access Points (APs), optical links and devices, and routers
interconnects devices and edge/remote cloud nodes. In the fol-
lowing, we first present the IoT strategies for GO Networking.
Then, the KPIs modelling is proposed and discussed.
A. Intelligent IoT Strategies for GO Networking
We assume IoT devices to be equipped with cameras and
deployed in the target areas to capture images, which are then
analyzed by AI models based on neural networks to detect
objects. We define the following strategies:
• Traditional IoT (TIoT): IoT devices equipped with cam-
eras periodically send captured images to cloud nodes,
where object detection and recognition tasks are per-
formed. Figure 2a depicts an example of this approach.
This represents a traditional IoT scenario, where each
device continuously sends images to the cloud and the
inference is executed for all the data. In this case, network
energy and resources are consumed to send data, and a
certain latency is introduced as data is not immediately
processed at the source. The images are processed in
the cloud, where efficient hardware is typically available,
making the inference task both energy- and time-efficient.

IV. GO INTELLIGENT IOT SERVICE DEPLOYMENT
To deploy IoT services for object detection, a GO net-
working framework, aware of the service goals, is required
to select the most appropriate set of resources on the network.
This is the case of TIoT and IIoT-C. Let us consider the
sample network in Figure 1. When IoT devices need to send
data to the cloud for processing, data travels through the AP
and reaches a cloud node via the transport network, routed
through one or more UPFs along the path. Among all possible
paths connecting an AP to a cloud node, we assume that
the shortest path is selected. Therefore, it is possible to pre-
compute the shortest paths for each AP-cloud node pair (P).
On the cloud, inference tasks can be executed on any cloud
node c among the C cloud locations. A model m on the
cloud must be selected among a set of available models
(M), each characterized by a different amount of required
data, computational complexity, and accuracy (am). While the
accuracy in TIoT case is provided only by the model deployed
at the cloud, in the IIoT-C case, the accuracy depends on the
combination of models (see Figure 2). The resources required
to execute model m on cloud node c, rm,c, must not exceed
the available resources Rc. Running inference model m on
compute node c requires an energy cost em,c and a processing
time lm,c, corresponding to Eproc
UE,Comp and Lprocessing in (9),
respectively. The latency introduced by a path p ∈P, denoted
as lm,p, depends on the chosen model m since the data size to
be sent varies with different inference models. This quantity
corresponds to Lradio + Ltransport + Lrouting in (9). Similarly, the
energy consumption of path p, denoted as em,p, includes all
the energy consumption terms in (1) except those related to
performing the inference task.
Given the service goals, the required and available network
resources, we must find the most suitable set of resources
to achieve the goal. An optimization problem is formulated
to determine (i) the inference model configuration, (ii) the
compute node on the cloud for execution, and (iii) the path
connecting the IoT device to the compute node. This selection
must account for the energy required to execute the model and
the energy needed for data transfer, as well as latency over
different paths, an estimation of which can be provided by
data analytics functions of the Core Network. The accuracy
values can be obtained, e.g., by testing each model on a
labeled dataset and depend on data quality, model parameters
and training. In the following, we assume the minimization
of the three conflicting KPIs introduced in Section III-B:
energy consumption, latency, and inaccuracy. We introduce

A. Simulation Environment and Setup
We developed a custom simulator written in Python to
simulate a human detection system operating for 30 days,
assuming one image sent by each camera every 60 seconds,
for a total of 43200 images. We generate images containing
a human with a probability distributed according to a Poisson
process. We refer to the mean value of such a distribution
as event frequency. All the results are an average of 1000
simulation runs.
We consider the scenario depicted in Figure 1. For each
considered strategy, i.e., TIoT, IIoT-D, and IIoT-C, we assume
a single IoT device (also referred to as UE), connected to
a single AP and, depending on the strategy, a single cloud
node. The consumption of a network interface card (Pnic)
for backhaul traffic is considered to be equal to 1 W [20],
while the consumption of a 100 Gbps optical transponder
(Ptr) is assumed to be 110.9 W [25]. The IoT device sends
images either with resolution 640x640 with size (d) 1.25 MB
or 320x320 with size (d) 307 kB and a transmitted power
Pt of 0.2 W to the AP every 60 seconds. Regarding the
parameters for the AP (needed to compute P recv
AP and P proc
AP ), we
assume a 20 MHz single-antenna, full load, spectral efficiency
6 bps/Hz, and 24-bit quantization, with a maximum capacity
of 1 Gbps. The power amplifier consumes 390 W while the
overhead factor η is assumed to be 0.27. We also assume
that the device is served through a network slice providing it
with a rate (α) of 50 Mbps. The transport network is assumed
to introduce a delay (Ltransport) of 5 ms for the edge cloud
(two-hops away) and 168 ms for the remote cloud (15-hops
away). These values are within the range of real measurements
on Amazon clouds available at [26] and already include the
latency introduced by two UPFs (NUP F ).
For the human detection task, we rely on You Only Look
Once (YOLO)v10, a state-of-the-art deep learning model for
real-time object detection [30] [31]. We consider different
model sizes, depending on the number of parameters consti-

68.5, 87.6, 195.9 GFLOPS. We trained each model on the

and 128 GB of RAM. Solving a single instance of the MO-ILP
model requires less than 1s. To assess scalability, we increased
the problem size by considering up to 50 models, cloud
nodes, and paths, representing a large-scale scenario with
randomly generated resource parameters. Across several runs,
the solving time never exceeded 5s. This analysis demonstrates
that the proposed model can be solved efficiently even for
large-scale instances.
VI. CONCLUSION
This paper focuses on assessing the end-to-end energy con-
sumption, latency, and goal accuracy KPIs of GO networking
strategies. To this end, a practical framework is proposed that
jointly considers network and service KPIs. An optimization
model based on MO ILP is introduced to evaluate the trade-
offs between the various KPIs. Simulation results show that the
energy consumption and latency are heavily impacted by the
service accuracy requirement to be provided with models of
different complexity. Scenarios allowing for more inaccuracy

are the ones where the most benefits can be achieved, as simple
AI models can be efficiently executed by IoT devices or edge
clouds, effectively offloading the network. On the contrary,
when high accuracy is required, leveraging remote clouds is
proven to be the most efficient solution. Results also suggest
that, when frequent exchange of data is required, traditional
IoT solutions leveraging cloud resources are typically more
efficient. The methodology and framework developed in this
paper enable a focused evaluation of the proposed GO net-
working strategy within an object-detection-based intelligent
IoT scenario. While the study concentrates on this specific
use case, it demonstrates how GO principles can be applied
in practice and lays the foundation for extending the approach
to other tasks and modalities in future work.
