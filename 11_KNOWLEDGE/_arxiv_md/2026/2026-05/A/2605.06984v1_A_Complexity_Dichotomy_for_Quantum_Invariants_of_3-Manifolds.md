A COMPLEXITY DICHOTOMY FOR QUANTUM INVARIANTS
OF 3-MANIFOLDS
CÉSAR GALINDO
Abstract. We prove exact complexity dichotomies for two quantum invari-
ants of closed oriented three-manifolds, with the categorical data fixed. For
a modular category C, computing the Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant ZC(M)
from a framed-link surgery presentation is in FP exactly when C is pointed,
that is, when all simple objects are invertible under tensor product; otherwise
it is #P-hard.
For a spherical fusion category A, computing the Turaev–
Viro invariant |M|A from a triangulation, equivalently from a skeleton, is in
FP exactly when its Drinfeld center Z(A) is pointed, equivalently when A
is trivializable pointed; otherwise it is #P-hard. The polynomial-time cases
reduce to finite abelian linear algebra and Gauss sums. The reductions are
based on a genus-one graph-manifold family MG, indexed by graphs G. In
the anomaly-free case, the core calculation expresses ZC(MG) as the weighted
graph homomorphism partition function with matrix AC(i, j) = Si,j∗/(didj),
where S is the modular matrix, di is the quantum dimension of i, and j∗is
the dual label. Combining this formula with the Cai–Govorov dichotomy gives
the hard side; the remaining Reshetikhin–Turaev and Turaev–Viro cases then
follow by passing to Drinfeld centers.
1. Introduction
Quantum invariants of three-manifolds connect low-dimensional topology, ten-
sor categories, statistical mechanics, and quantum computation.
Among them,
the Reshetikhin–Turaev and Turaev–Viro invariants are defined from finite tensor-
categorical data: modular categories in the first case and spherical fusion categories
in the second [RT91, TV92, BW96, Tur16, TV17, BK01, EGNO15]. Both admit
finite combinatorial formulas, respectively from framed-link surgery presentations
and from triangulations or skeletons. The existence of such formulas, however, does
not answer the exact complexity question: for a fixed category, how hard is it to
evaluate the invariant as the three-manifold varies?
This paper studies this question in the setting of exact computation. The cat-
egory and a number field containing its structural constants and normalization
scalars are part of the fixed data; the input is only a combinatorial presentation of
the three-manifold. For a modular category C, we write
RT-EVAL(C)
for the exact problem of computing ZC(M) from a framed-link surgery presentation
of M. For a spherical fusion category A, we write

There is a long history of interactions between quantum topology and com-
plexity theory.
The complexity of evaluating the Jones and Tutte polynomials
was already studied by Jaeger, Vertigan, and Welsh [JVW90], and the approx-
imation of quantum link invariants is closely related to quantum computation
[FKW02, FLW02, AJL09, Kup15]. Connections between complexity assumptions
and Jones-polynomial evaluations in topology were further studied in [CFW16].
From the categorical point of view, these questions are also related to the classifica-
tion of anyonic systems and to finiteness and universality properties of braid group
representations [NR11, RW18]. The present paper is concerned with the problem
of exact evaluation of closed three-manifold invariants.
In the three-manifold setting, Kirby and Melvin gave an early example of exact
hardness. They showed that the SU(2) Witten–Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant at
the fourth root of unity, equivalently the level-two or Ising theory, determines the
number of zeros of cubic forms over Z2, and concluded that the corresponding exact
evaluation problem is NP-hard [KM04]. The Ising theory therefore gives an early
hardness result for exact evaluation. Related formulas at exceptional roots include
the sixth-root formula of Kirby–Melvin–Zhang and the metaplectic link invariants
of Goldschmidt–Jones [KMZ93, GJ89]; for computational aspects of metaplectic
modular categories, see [HNW14].
Further complexity phenomena for quantum
and finite-group three-manifold invariants were studied by Kuperberg and Sam-
perton [KS18, KS21]. On the algorithmic side, Turaev–Viro type invariants have
also been studied from the viewpoint of effective computation and parameterized
complexity [BMS18, MS16, MS20]. More recently, Delaney, Maria, and Samper-
ton studied Tambara–Yamagami quantum invariants, proving hardness results and
fixed-parameter algorithms in terms of the first Betti number [DMS25].
The present work is partly motivated by the work of Bridges and Samperton
[BS25]. They prove a polynomial-time/#P-hard dichotomy for tensor contraction
problems associated with Turaev–Viro–Barrett–Westbury and Reshetikhin–Turaev
type TQFTs, using the dichotomy theorem of Cai and Chen for weighted constraint
satisfaction problems over C [CC17]. They also distinguish this from a dichotomy
directly for the three-manifold invariants themselves, rather than for the larger
class of tensor networks built from the underlying fusion category. In this paper we
prove such a manifold-level dichotomy for the semisimple three-dimensional quan-
tum invariants considered here, namely Reshetikhin–Turaev invariants of modular
categories and Turaev–Viro invariants of spherical fusion categories. In addition, we
identify the polynomial-time case intrinsically: for Reshetikhin–Turaev invariants it
is exactly the condition that the modular category is pointed, while for Turaev–Viro
invariants it is exactly the condition that the Drinfeld center is pointed, equivalently
that the spherical fusion category is trivializable pointed.

3. Graph partition functions and exact complexity
This section fixes the exact computational conventions and the graph-theoretic
dichotomy used in the reductions.
The quantum invariants constructed below
are expressed as weighted graph homomorphism partition functions with algebraic
weights; their hardness follows from the bounded-degree graph-homomorphism di-
chotomy of [CG20].
3.1. Exact problems and reductions. All computational problems in this paper
are exact evaluation problems. The algebraic datum, such as a category or a matrix,
is fixed and is not part of the input. If the datum is a category over the algebraically
closed field k, it is part of the convention of the evaluation problem that the subfield
k0 ⊂k fixed in Section 2.2 is part of the data. We identify k0 with a number field
K ⊂C.
This choice fixes the exact arithmetic used below: algebraic identities, zero tests,
rank conditions, and the values of the fixed invariant are preserved under the chosen
field isomorphism.
By an exact K-valued problem we mean a function
F : X −→K,
where X is a class of finite combinatorial objects given by finite encodings. The
elements of K are represented as exact algebraic numbers. Since K is fixed, the
choice of primitive element or integral basis does not affect the polynomial-time
notion: changing the exact representation changes the running time by a polynomial
factor.
If F : X →K and G: Y →K are exact K-valued problems, a polynomial-time
Turing reduction
F ≤p
T G
is an algorithm which computes F(x), for x ∈X, in time polynomial in the size of
x, using exact arithmetic in K and oracle calls to G on inputs of size polynomially
bounded in the size of x. We use the same notation when the source is an integer-
valued counting problem, viewing Z ⊂K.
The class FP consists of the exact functions computable in polynomial time.
For K-valued exact problems this means polynomial-time computation in the fixed
exact algebraic-number model for K. The class #P is the class of functions counting
accepting paths of nondeterministic polynomial-time machines [Val79]. An exact
problem G is #P-hard if every function in #P polynomial-time Turing reduces to G.
We use the conventions for counting reductions and hardness from [Pap94, AB09].
