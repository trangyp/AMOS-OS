Proof systems for the positive modal formulas in the style of Dunn [5] were
presented for several modal logics. Dunn [5] provided proof systems for the positive
fragments of K, T, B, S4, S5, KB, K4, and K5, and proved their Kripke completeness.
While 2 and 3 in Dunn [5] have dual properties, Celani and Jansana [2] treated
these operators independently. They presented sequent systems for K, T, 4, B, S,
E, and D, in which 2 and 3 are independent, and proved the completeness theorem
of the systems with respect to the Kripke frames for intuitionistic modal logics. As
for GL, it was proved in Dashkov [3] that the strictly positive fragment, that is,
formulas constructed from ∧, 3, ⊤, and propositional variables, of GL coincides
with that of K4. However, the positive fragment of GL properly contains that of
K4, because, for example, it contains 2⊥∨32⊥. However, it seems that a proof
system for the positive fragment of GL has not been found yet.
Our proof system contains ω-rules, that is, inference rules that have countably
many premises. In Tanaka [10], a Gentzen system NQGL for a predicate extension
of GL was introduced and its Kripke completeness was proved. The system NQGL
does not include the L¨ob formula as an axiom, but has an ω-rule of the following
form:
Γ →Λ, 3n⊤, (∀n ∈ω)
Γ →∆
.
As the above ω-rule contains neither negation symbols nor implication symbols, we
make use of a variation of the above ω-rule to define our proof system GL⊤⊥
+ .

4. Equational logic for modal distributive lattices
In this section, we introduce an equational logic for modal distributive lattices.
This is a fragment of first-order logic with constant symbols c0 and c1, interpreted
as 0 and 1, respectively; unary function symbols f2 and f3, interpreted as 2
and 3, respectively; binary function symbols f⊔and f⊓, interpreted as ⊔and ⊓,
respectively; and a single predicate symbol ≈that is interpreted as equality (=).
Most of the terminology introduced here is standard for algebras of arbitrary type,
though our focus is on modal distributive lattices.
Definition 4.1. We fix a countable set X of variables. The set T of terms of modal
distributive lattices is the least set such that
(1) X ⊆T;
(2) c0 ∈T and c1 ∈T;
(3) if s ∈T then f2(s) and f3(s) are in T;
(4) if s and t are in T then f⊔(s, t) and f⊓(s, t) are in T.
An identity of modal distributive lattices is a first-order formula of the form s ≈t,
where s and t are terms of modal distributive lattices. A quasi-identity of modal
distributive lattices is a first-order formula of the form s1 ≈t1∧· · ·∧sn ≈tn ⊃s ≈t,
where s1 ≈t1, . . . , sn ≈tn and s ≈t are identities of modal distributive lattices. It
is clear that an identity is a quasi-identity.

GL is the least normal modal logic that contains K and the L¨ob formula 2(2p ⊃

Definition 5.1. The language of positive modal logic consists of the following
symbols:
(1) a countable set Prop of propositional variables;
(2) logical constants: ⊥and ⊤;
(3) logical connectives: ∨, ∧;
(4) modal operators: 3, 2.

Definition 5.3. An algebraic model for positive modal formulas is a pair ⟨A, v⟩,
where A is a modal distributive lattice and v is a function from the set Prop of

(1) v(⊥) = 0, v(⊤) = 1;
(2) v(ϕ ∨ψ) = v(ϕ) ⊔v(ψ), v(ϕ ∧ψ) = v(ϕ) ⊓v(ψ);
(3) v(3ϕ) = 3v(ϕ), v(2ϕ) = 2v(ϕ).
