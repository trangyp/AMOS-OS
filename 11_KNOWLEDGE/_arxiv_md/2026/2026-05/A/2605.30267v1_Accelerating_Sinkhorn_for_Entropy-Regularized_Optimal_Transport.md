Abstract
We propose Acc-Sinkhorn, a simple accelerated variant of Sinkhorn for entropy-
regularized optimal transport (EOT). The method is derived from a bilevel optimiza-
tion view: Sinkhorn row scaling solves the inner variable u exactly and defines the
reduced dual objective f(v) = minu F(u, v), while the remaining column scaling
is a unit-step dual mirror descent step in v. This structure yields a Hessian-driven
Nesterov acceleration that keeps Sinkhorn’s scaling form and per-iteration cost,
using only extrapolated combinations of Sinkhorn iterates. We prove an O(1/k2)
rate under a verifiable stability condition. For an ε-approximation of unregularized
OT, the resulting complexity is e
O(n2/ε), improved from e
O(n2/ε2) for Sinkhorn.
On synthetic problems, color transfer, and word alignment, Acc-Sinkhorn gives a
10×–30× speedup over Sinkhorn at small regularization.

Sinkhorn algorithm.
The Sinkhorn algorithm [29] alternately rescales the rows and columns of
the kernel matrix K = exp(−C/ε) to enforce the marginal constraints. Each iteration costs O(nm)
and is easy to parallelize. Its convergence has been studied through Hilbert’s projective metric,
block coordinate descent, sharp ε-dependent estimates, and mirror descent in continuous and discrete
time [17, 5, 20, 10, 27, 24, 21, 3, 11].
Despite these advances, a central question remains: can Sinkhorn be accelerated to improve its
accuracy dependence without losing its simplicity?
Related Work.
We call a method accelerated if its error decays as O(1/k2) rather than the O(1/k)
rate of Sinkhorn, where k is the number of full Sinkhorn-type iterations. For simplicity, we discuss
complexity for m = n. For an ε-approximation of the original OT problem, the total cost is often
O(np/εq) for positive p and q. Existing acceleration methods fall into three broad categories.
(i) Coordinate-wise and stochastic methods. Coordinate-wise and stochastic variants, such as
Greenkhorn, reduce the cost of each iteration by updating only part of the variables or by using
stochastic estimates [1, 22, 18]. These methods can improve practical efficiency, but they retain the
O(1/k) convergence behavior of Sinkhorn and thus do not improve its dependence on ε.
(ii) Nesterov acceleration and primal-dual methods. Accelerated primal-dual methods, such
as APDAGD, improve the ε-dependence of Sinkhorn by applying Nesterov-type acceleration or
accelerated mirror descent to the EOT dual problem [15, 22]. However, they typically require line
search or adaptation to local smoothness and are more complex to implement than Sinkhorn.
(iii) Sinkhorn variants with modified update rules. Sinkhorn variants with modified update rules,
such as overrelaxed Sinkhorn and annealed Sinkhorn, keep the scaling structure of Sinkhorn while
changing the update or regularization schedule [31, 8]. They can improve local convergence or
practical behavior, but they do not give a global O(1/k2) acceleration with a parameter-free update.
In short, existing methods do not simultaneously achieve an accelerated O(1/k2) rate, O(n2/ε)
complexity, and a parameter-free simple implementation; see Table 1.

Our contribution.
The main contribution of this paper is Acc-Sinkhorn, a simple accelerated
variant of Sinkhorn that keeps the O(nm) per-iteration cost of Sinkhorn while improving the
convergence rate from O(1/k) to O(1/k2). As shown in Table 1, Acc-Sinkhorn is the only method
in the table that combines accelerated convergence, Sinkhorn-level per-iteration cost, and a simple
update rule without line search. Its smoothness parameter is fixed by the geometry, namely L = 1, and
the strong-convexity parameter µ is handled by a homotopy schedule rather than by manual tuning.
Numerically, Acc-Sinkhorn achieves a 10×–30× speedup over Sinkhorn on synthetic datasets, color
transfer, and word alignment at small ε.

Limitations.
In our experiments, Acc-Sinkhorn uses the step size α = √2µ and shows stable
accelerated behavior. The current proof is more conservative: it gives the full accelerated guarantee
under a stability condition, which holds for sufficiently small α and can be enforced by linesearch.
The gap comes from controlling the metric-variation term ∥yk −x⋆∥2
Dk+1−Dk, which is small in
practice but hard to bound uniformly. Removing this condition and justifying the step size α = √2µ

Conclusion
This paper presents a simple and efficient accelerated Sinkhorn algorithm for computing entropy-
regularized optimal transport that maintains the per-iteration cost of Sinkhorn while achieving a
10×–30× speedup in the small ε regime. Lyapunov convergence analysis proves the O(1/k2) rate
under a verifiable stability condition and suggests the overall complexity e
O(n2/ε), compared to
O(1/k) and e
O(n2/ε2) for Sinkhorn. Numerical experiments on synthetic and real datasets support
the practical efficiency of the method. Removing the stability condition and proving the limit step
size α = √2µ directly remain important directions for future work.
