A RECIPROCITY THEOREM OF ROBINSON-BENSON-WEBB FOR
FINITE-DIMENSIONAL SYMMETRIC ALGEBRAS
SHIGEO KOSHITANI
Abstract. We generalize the reciprocity theorem of G.R. Robinson, D. Benson and P. Webb between a
finite group and its subgroup to the case of finite-dimensional symmetric algebras over a field which are
connected by a bimodule for the two algebras.
1 Introduction
In his paper [Rob89], G.R. Robinson presents a reciprocity theorem for simple/projective modules over the
group algebras kG and kH where k is a field and H is a subgroup of a finite group G (for the precise statement
see Corollary 1.2).
Our main theorem stated below claims that Robinson’s reciprocity theorem is extended for finite-dimensional
k-algebras A and B, and for an (A, B)-bimodule M instead of kG and kH, provided A and B both are sym-
metric algebras and M is projective as a left A-module and also as a right B-module. Actually the main
theorem should work nicely when we look for a kind of equivalence between the module categories of A-
modules and B-modules via the (A, B)-bimodule M. Now, our main result is:
Theorem 1.1. Let k be an algebraically closed field, and let A and B be finite-dimensional symmetric k-
algebras. Further, let M be an (A, B)-bimodule such that M is projective as a left A-module and also as a
right B-module. Then, for any simple right A-module S and any simple right B-module T,
[P(T) | S ⊗A M]B = [P(S) | T ⊗B M ∗]A
where M ∗:= Homk(M, k) is the k-dual of M, P(S) and P(T) are the projective covers of S and T, respectively,
and [P | U]A for a projective right A-module P and a right A-module U denotes the multiplicity of P as a
direct summand of U.
Then, we immediately get original Robinson’s reciprocity formula by applying Theorem 1.1 to the case that
A := kG, B := kH and M := (kG)↓G×G
G×H where the last term means the restriction of kG as a k(G×G)-module
to k(G × H)-module.
Corollary 1.2. [Theorem 3 in [Rob89] and Lemma 5.2 in [Gro02]] Let H be a subgroup of a finite group
G, and let S and T, respectively, be a simple kG-module and a simple kH-module. Then, [P(T) | S↓H]kH =
[P(S) | T↑G]kG where ↓H and ↑G, respectively, mean the restriction to H and the induction to G.
Remark 1.3. Footnote 1 on p.108 of [Rob89] says that D. Benson and P. Webb have known the same result
as Corollary 1.2.
