Abstract. We prove that every bounded linear operator between Lipschitz spaces admits a lifting
along the De Leeuw embedding. More precisely, given pointed metric spaces M and N and ϵ > 0,
every bounded linear operator S : Lip0(M) →Lip0(N) admits a lifting S : C(β ˜

1. Introduction
In this work we study spaces of real-valued Lipschitz functions defined on a metric space M that
vanish at a fixed distinguished point 0, denoted by Lip0(M). The study of these spaces, especially
their canonical preduals known as Lipschitz-free spaces F(M), has attracted considerable attention
in nonlinear functional analysis; see, for instance, [1], [2], [3], [4], [5],[8].
A useful approach in the study of Lip0(M) is to consider its canonical isometric embedding into
a space of continuous functions. More precisely, let ˜
M = {(x, y) ∈M × M : x ̸= y} be equipped
with a product metric, and let β ˜
M denote its Stone-ˇCech compactification. The De Leeuw map
ΦM : Lip0(M) →C(β ˜
M) (see [12, §2.4]) is the linear isometric embedding defined by extending
continuously to β ˜

This embedding allows one to study Lip0(M) within the framework of C(K) spaces, although
the topology of β ˜

The purpose of this paper is to show that bounded linear operators between Lipschitz spaces

2. Basic Terminology
A metric space (M, d) is said to be pointed if it is endowed with a fixed distinguished point 0,
called the origin. For a pointed metric space M, Lip0(M) denotes the Banach space of all Lipschitz
functions f : M →R such that f(0) = 0, equipped with the norm
∥f∥Lip0 =
sup
x̸=y∈M
|f(x) −f(y)|
d(x, y)
.
The Banach space Lip0(M) is a dual space whose canonical predual, denoted by F(M) and
called the Lipschitz-free space (or Arens–Eells space), is defined as the closed linear span of all
normalized elementary molecules in Lip0(M)∗. For every x, y ∈M, the normalized elementary
molecule associated to (x, y) is the functional mxy : Lip0(M) →R given by
⟨mxy, f⟩= f(x) −f(y)
d(x, y)
.
For a detailed exposition on this topic we refer the reader to [12].
If K is a Hausdorff compact space, C(K) denotes the Banach space of all real-valued continuous
functions on K, endowed with the supremum norm. The dual space C(K)∗is identified, via the
Riesz representation theorem, with the space M(K) of all signed Radon measures on K of bounded
variation, equipped with the variation norm.
For every t ∈K, δt ∈M(K) denotes the Dirac
measure concentrated at t. For further details concerning Banach spaces of the form C(K) see [11].
For topological spaces we adopt standard terminology: if U is a subset of a topological space,
then ˚U denotes the interior of U. As usual, for a Banach space X, the symbol BX stands for its
closed unit ball.
For Banach spaces X and Y , a bounded linear surjection S : X →Y is called a quotient mapping
if the induced operator bS : X/ ker(S) →Y is a linear isometric isomorphism.

4. Some Remarks
With regard to Theorem 1, the following question naturally arises.
Problem. Is it possible for a linear isomorphism from Lip0(M) onto Lip0(N) to be lifted to an
isomorphism from C(β ˜
M) onto C(β ˜N) in such a way that the above diagram commutes?
If (M, dM) and (N, dN) are pointed metric spaces, γ : N →M is a bi-Lipschitz isomorphism
such that γ(0N) = 0M, and r ̸= 0 is a real scalar, then the pair (γ, r) induces a linear isomorphism
S : Lip0(M) →Lip0(N) through the formula S(f)(y) = r · (f ◦γ)(y). This can be lifted to an
isomorphism S : C(β ˜
M) →C(β ˜N) given as follows: for each F ∈C(β ˜

continuous extension to β ˜N of the map
˜N ∋(x, y) 7→r · dM(γ(x), γ(y))
dN(x, y)
F(γ(x), γ(y)) ∈R.
It is readily seen that S(ΦM(f)) = ΦN(S(f)) for every f ∈Lip0(M). In particular, if M and
N are uniformly concave [12, Definition 3.33] and S : Lip0(M) →Lip0(N) is a linear isometric
isomorphism, then S is an operator of the above type; see [12, Theorem 3.56], and therefore admits
an appropriate lifting. The next example shows, however, that such isomorphic liftings cannot be
obtained in general.
