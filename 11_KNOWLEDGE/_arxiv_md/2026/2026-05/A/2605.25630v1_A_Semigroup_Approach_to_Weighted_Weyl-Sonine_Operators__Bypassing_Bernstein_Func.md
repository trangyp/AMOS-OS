Abstract. This paper establishes a rigorous functional analytic framework for weighted Weyl-
Sonine fractional operators on semi-infinite intervals. While the classical Phillips functional
calculus relies strictly on completely monotonic Bernstein functions to subordinate linear semi-
groups, we demonstrate that this structural restriction can be entirely bypassed. By exploiting
the intrinsic topological decay induced by an admissible amnesia weight (ω), we prove that
the abstract integral representation converges absolutely for general classes of tempered Sonine
kernels, thereby unifying the treatment of both diffusive relaxation and oscillatory non-local dy-
namics within a single framework. Employing the theory of strongly continuous semigroups, we
characterize these operators as the infinitesimal generators of a deformed translation semigroup
that simultaneously incorporates subjective aging scales (ψ) and history-attenuating amnesia
effects. This similarity-based approach not only provides a sound abstract foundation for the
hypersingular Marchaud representation of these operators but also guarantees their closedness
and the analytical well-posedness of the associated abstract fractional Cauchy problems in gen-
eral Banach spaces. Furthermore, we introduce the associated generalized fractional integral
and prove the fundamental inversion theorem, demonstrating the complete analytical symme-
try of the theory. Finally, we establish pointwise decay estimates for the solutions via weighted
Sobolev embeddings.
1. Introduction
In this work, we transition from the classical point-wise integral representation to a generator-
based semigroup approach. Building upon the operational definition of the Weighted Weyl-
Sonine derivative introduced in [11], we now provide its formal functional analytic foundation.
Rather than relying on classical subordination extensions—which often impose strict analytic
constraints on the memory kernels—we exploit the intrinsic topological decay provided by the
weighted functional spaces. We demonstrate that the amnesia weight naturally neutralizes the
asymptotic growth of the kernels, thereby establishing a framework that completely bypasses
the traditional requirement of complete monotonicity (Bernstein functions).
This allows for
the mathematically rigorous accommodation of general admissible Sonine kernels, unifying the
treatment of both diffusive and oscillatory non-local dynamics.
The study of Sonine kernels has recently experienced a major resurgence due to their central
role in general fractional calculus [5]. Recent applications have spanned from scale-invariant
systems via Mellin convolutions [6] to advanced Petrov-Galerkin numerical schemes [7]; however,
these approaches remain predominantly anchored to finite intervals or specific algebraic settings.
Unlike recent studies on generalized Sonine kernels [9, 10, 8], which are restricted to the Riemann-
Liouville or Caputo causal framework on the finite interval [0, T], this work extends the scope
of these operators to encompass the full history of the system. By defining the Weighted Weyl-
Sonine derivative on the semi-infinite interval (−∞, t], we capture the long-term hereditary
effects characteristic of aging media. The convergence of these non-local integrals at −∞, a
classic hurdle in Weyl-type calculus, is rigorously ensured here by the amnesia weight ω(t),
which acts as a topological regulator within our transmuted functional framework.

6. Conclusions
In this work, we have established a rigorous and unified functional analytic framework for
weighted Weyl-Sonine operators by transitioning from their classical integral representation to
a generator-based semigroup approach. By constructing the deformed translation semigroup
Tψ,ω through a transmutation isomorphism, we have successfully characterized these complex
non-local operators as infinitesimal generators on arbitrary Banach spaces.
The core contribution of this research lies in lifting the historical restriction of Bernstein func-
tions, which has traditionally limited the Phillips functional calculus to purely diffusive regimes.
Our framework demonstrates that by utilizing the structural similarity with the translation
generator and defining appropriate topological spaces, one can accommodate the full class of ad-
missible Sonine kernels. This effectively bridges the gap between strictly diffusive relaxation and
oscillatory inertial dynamics, providing a mathematical foundation for systems where positivity
is naturally lost—a regime previously inaccessible to standard subordination methods.
Furthermore, we have linked abstract operator theory with physical reality by proving the
well-posedness of the associated abstract Cauchy problem. By leveraging the weighted Sobolev
embedding theorems established in our potential-theoretic study [12], we have shown that the
solutions are not only mathematically unique but also physically constrained by the amnesia
weight ω(t), which acts as a fundamental decay envelope.
This unified approach opens several powerful avenues for future research.
A particularly
natural extension involves applying this transmutation framework to the spatial domain: by
conjugating the classical Laplacian rather than the translation operator, one can construct a
deformed heat semigroup governing diffusion in highly heterogeneous media. This approach will
allow for a rigorous generalization of the weighted fractional Laplacian (−∆ϕ,ω)s, whose spectral
properties were recently characterized in [13]. Establishing the strict mathematical equivalence
between the semigroup-based generator and the spectral multipliers derived in [13] represents a
major next step in our program, as it will facilitate the extension of these operators to arbitrary
Banach spaces beyond the L2 setting. Finally, the stability of these semigroups under non-linear
perturbations and the development of spectral numerical schemes remain essential goals to fully
address complex evolution processes in physical applications.
