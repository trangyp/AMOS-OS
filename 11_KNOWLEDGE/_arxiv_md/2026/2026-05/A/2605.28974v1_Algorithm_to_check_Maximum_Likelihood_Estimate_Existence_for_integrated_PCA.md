ALGORITHM TO CHECK MAXIMUM LIKELIHOOD ESTIMATE
EXISTENCE FOR INTEGRATED PCA
D.A.SHMELKIN
Abstract. Being encouraged by [AKRS] that provides an amazing bridge be-
tween Statistics and Invariant Theory, and especially by [FM], where quiver
semi-invariant techniques apply to verify the existence of MLE for a recent
iPCA model, we provide an enhancement to [FM]. Our Theorem 5.2 yields
necessary and sufficient conditions for MLE to exist generically for any di-
mension vector. The conditions can be easily checked with our software [T]
based on Derksen-Weyman algorithm and simplifying the application for sta-
tistics practitioners and non-specialists in quivers. For those deep in quiver
Representation Theory, Theorem 5.2 relates the MLE existence to the local
semi-simplicity of representations as introduced in [Sh07]. We also hope that
our elementary and short text can serve for the experts in both domains as a
warm start in a new category.

The authors of [AKRS] went further and proposed to consider a Gaussian Group

4. Matrix Normal Model from Quiver perspective
Based on Lemma 2.1, the authors of [DM] considered the Matrix Normal Model
from the perspective of Invariant Theory for representations of Kronecker quiver
with two vertices, n arrows of the same directions, and of dimension α = (p1, p2).

precise minimal values of n are found such that a general position X ∈Rp1 ⊗Rp2
is polystable or even stable, see [DM, Theorem 1.2], or in other words, MLE exists

5. Integrated PCA model
After [AKRS] was published, another interesting model, iPCA, was proposed in
[TA] such that the observations constitute the same p × n matrix X as for PCA,
each of n columns is therefore a vector of p features observed but the features are
grouped, with group sizes p1, p2, · · · , pk, k ≥1 such that p = p1 + p2 + · · · + pk. Let
Xj denote the pj × n submatrix of X corresponding to that group of pj features,
j = 1, · · · , k. In difference to PCA (and to the Matrix Normal Model as well), the
Integrated PCA model explores the diversity both among samples and features. In
particular, the iPCA model for k = 1, p1 = p is not the same as standard PCA that
only explores the diversity of features while samples are independent of each other.
Here, the iPCA model looks for a n × n invertible matrix Σ and pj × pj invertible
matrices ∆j, j = 1, · · · , k such that for every j = 1, · · · , k the whithened data

As one may observe in Lemma 2.1, n, the number of samples, plays the role of
the number of copies of a vector space, V , acted upon by G. As we mentioned
above, samples in iPCA aren’t treated independently as in PCA. That is why,
Lemma 2.1 applies to V = R(Qk, α) and the number of copies equal to just 1! At
first glance, it sounds like in iPCA we have just 1 sample and it may seem not
statistically significant. But this ”single” sample allows to address the statistical
inference problem, to which the Lemma applies. As a take away of [FM] we give a
statement that we slightly reformulate in our terms:

Based on Lemma 5.1, a set of results regading existence and uniqueness of MLE
for generic X ∈R(Qk, α) are provided in [FM], and the most explicit ones are
[FM, Theorem 1.1] that yields a sufficient condition for MLE to generically exist
uniquely, while in [FM, Theorem 1.2] the case of p1 = p2 = · · · pk = q is considered
and necessary and sufficient condition for MLE to generically exist or exist uniquely
are given for any (n, q, k). However, the general case of unequal pj is not covered
by explicit criteria or simply programmable algorithms.
The quiver representations with a closed GL(α)σ-orbit for a character σ are called
σ-polystable in [AKRS],[DM],[FM] and this has a clear relation to the theory of σ-
stable or polystable quiver representation that goes back to the brilliant A.D.King
paper [K]. In [Sh07] we called such representations locally semi-simple and provided
equivalent descriptions of such representations. Our main result in this paper is:

Theorem 5.2. For α = [n, p1, · · · , pk] set p = p1 + · · · + pk. Let α = m1β1 +
· · · + mlβl be the generic decomposition such that βi ̸= βj for i ̸= j. The following
conditions are equivalent:
a. MLE exists for a generic X ∈R(Qk, α)
b. Generic X ∈R(Qk, α) is locally semi-simple
c. For every j = 1, · · · , l holds pβj[0] = n(βj[1] + · · · + βj[k]).
d. ⟨βi, βj⟩= 0 for i ̸= j.
This theorem yields a practical efficient method to verify, whether or not, iPCA
problem for a generic measurement p × n matrix X with feature groups of sizes
p1, · · · , pk allows an MLE. Indeed, according to it, it is enough to compute the
generic decomposition and check the condition c.
And to compute generic de-
composition, one may use the wonderful Derksen-Weyman algorithm from [DW].
Moreover, in [Sh09],[T] we gave an implementation for Derksen-Weyman algorithm
and enhanced it to a one for generic locally semi-simple representation.
Remark 5.1. The equivalence of conditions a and c follows from Theorem 4.3 and
Lemma 5.2 in [FM] but in that paper the authors didn’t mention the Derksen-
Weyman algorithm as a method to convert this criterion into a practical tool avail-
able to statistics practitioners and non-specialists in Quiver Representations.
Remark 5.2. The dimension vectors α fulfilling the equivalent conditions of Theo-
rem 5.2 exist and not only the Schurian roots such that the generic decomposition
is trivial. To illustrate it as an example of 5.2 application, take k = 5, i.e., consider
iPCA with 5 groups of features. In terms of quiver, this is Q5, a wild quiver. Take
α = [5, 4, 3, 1, 1, 1], so n = 5, p = 10. Applying Derksen-Weyman algorithm [DW]
or running the software [T], we get the generic decomposition as follows:
[5, 4, 3, 1, 1, 1] = [3, 2, 1, 1, 1, 1] + 2 × [1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 0]
(6)
Then we check the condition c: 10 × 3 = 5(2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1) and 10 × 1 =
5(1+1+0+0+0) hold true both. Hence, a generic matrix X of just 5 observations
in K10, with groups of features of sizes 4,3,1,1,1 allows an MLE in iPCA model.
6. Proof of Theorem 5.2
Let X ∈R(Qk, α) be generic. By Lemma 5.1, If MLE exists for X, then GL(α)σ-
orbit of X is closed, so a implies b. That a is equivalent to c is proven in Theorem
4.3 and Lemma 5.1 and 5.2 in [FM]. Now assume b and let X = m1V1+· · ·+mlVl be
generic. Then, as it is well known (see, e.g., [Kac]), Ext(Vi, Vj) = 0 for 1 ≤i ̸= j ≤l.
It is also well known that for any representations U, V holds:
⟨dim U, dim V ⟩= dim Hom(U, V ) −dim Ext(U, V ).
(7)
In particular, we have ⟨βi, βj⟩= dim Hom(Vi, Vj). However, by our assumption,
X is locally semi-simple and, similar to simple representations, homomorphisms

Therefore, by formula (7), ⟨βi, βj⟩= 0. Conversely, if the equation ⟨βi, βj⟩= 0 for

properties of generic decompositions, the local quiver has no edges except for loops
and the number of summands is the same as for generic (as it is generic itself).
