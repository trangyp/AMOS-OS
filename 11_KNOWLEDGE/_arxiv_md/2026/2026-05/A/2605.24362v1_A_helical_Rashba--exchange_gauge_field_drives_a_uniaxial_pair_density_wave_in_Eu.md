A helical Rashba–exchange gauge field drives a uniaxial pair density wave in EuRbFe4As4
Pengfei Li1 and Yi Zhou2
1Department of Physics, The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, Clear Water Bay, Kowloon, Hong Kong, China
2Institute of Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, China
(Dated: May 26, 2026)
The recent discovery of an intrinsic pair density wave (PDW) in the iron-pnictide superconductor EuRbFe4As4
presents a fascinating puzzle. Structurally, the FeAs layers break local inversion symmetry, while the Eu2+
spins exhibit a period-four helical magnetic order. Here, we demonstrate that the interplay of induced Rashba
spin-orbit coupling and the helical exchange field generates a layer-rotating effective U(1) gauge field for the
Cooper pairs. Because this gauge field shares the symmetry of the Fe 3dxz/3dyz orbitals, it explicitly induces
an intertwined, orbital-selective pairing state. Using a phenomenological Ginzburg-Landau theory, we show
this mechanism naturally stabilizes a structurally modulated Bloch superconducting state. This state features
a strictly uniaxial (unidirectional) PDW, naturally capturing the phenomenology of recent scanning tunneling
microscopy experiments. Furthermore, we predict this PDW is accompanied by spontaneous loop currents
between adjacent layers, offering a distinctive signature for future µSR or scanning SQUID experiments.
Introduction. The pair density wave (PDW) is a supercon-
ducting (SC) state in which Cooper pairs condense with finite
center-of-mass momentum, spontaneously breaking transla-
tional symmetry and producing a spatially modulated order
parameter even in zero applied field [1–19].
As a parent
phase, it can give rise to vestigial orders—such as charge-
density waves, loop currents, or charge-4e superconductiv-
ity [20–24]. For a broad overview see Refs. [20, 25]. Histor-
ically, finite-momentum pairing was proposed independently
by Fulde and Ferrell (FF) [26] and by Larkin and Ovchinnikov
(LO) [27] to describe superconductors under a strong Zeeman
field. More recently, finite-momentum pairing driven by the
orbital effect of an in-plane magnetic field—termed the orbital
FFLO state—was proposed in engineered heterostructures
without large global spin splitting, such as bilayer transition-
metal dichalcogenides [28–31] or Bi2Te3/NbSe2 heterostruc-
tures [32]. Expanding on this within a framework equivalent
to the Lawrence-Doniach model [33, 34], Ref. [35] demon-
strated that this orbital FFLO mechanism can induce both a
conventional FF state and a structurally modulated “Bloch”
SC state.
Very recently, an intrinsic zero-field PDW was discov-
ered in the stoichiometric iron-pnictide EuRbFe4As4 [36].
Spectroscopic-imaging STM revealed a unidirectional, in-
commensurate modulation of the SC gap with a wavelength
of approximately 8 unit cells. This modulation appears strictly
below the magnetic transition temperature Tm ≈15 K, while
uniform superconductivity persists up to Tc ≈37 K [37–
39].
Structurally, EuRbFe4As4 consists of alternating Rb+
and Eu2+ planes sandwiching the superconducting FeAs lay-
ers. Neutron diffraction identifies a helical Eu spin order with
propagation vector Q = (0, 0, 1/4), corresponding to a 90◦
rotation of ferromagnetic moments between successive mag-
netic layers [40].
While the orbital FFLO mechanism [28, 35] is conceptu-
ally appealing for inducing a unidirectional modulation, ap-
plying its purely electromagnetic orbital effect via the Eu2+
dipole fields fails quantitatively. The resulting internal mag-
netic field (µ0M ∼0.4 T [39]) is far too weak, yielding a
macroscopic modulation wavelength (∼5 µm) rather than the
nanometer-scale (∼3 nm) PDW observed in experiments. In-
stead, we propose a mechanism rooted in the exact crystal-
lographic structure of EuRbFe4As4 [39]. Because each FeAs
layer is asymmetrically sandwiched between a non-magnetic
Rb+ layer and a magnetic Eu2+ layer, local inversion symme-
try is intrinsically broken, inducing a finite Rashba spin-orbit
coupling (SOC) [41]. When the Eu2+ spins order, they exert a
strong layer-dependent in-plane exchange field Hl on the su-
perconducting electrons. Crucially, the Rashba SOC converts
this exchange energy into a layer-dependent Cooper-pair mo-
mentum shift, or equivalently a formal effective U(1) gauge
field, Aeff

Symmetry and SC Order Parameter. The relevant zone-
center hole pockets in EuRbFe4As4 are predominantly com-
posed of the degenerate Fe 3dxz and 3dyz orbitals [44, 45]. Un-
der the crystal point group D4, these orbitals form a 2D irre-
ducible representation (Γ5). Consequently, superconductivity
developing on these pockets naturally hosts a two-component
orbital order parameter ⃗ψl ≡(ψl,xz, ψl,yz)T.
When the helical magnetic order sets in below Tm ≈15 K,
it induces the layer-dependent effective gauge field Al =
(Alx, Aly), which also transforms as Γ5. This in-plane gauge
field explicitly breaks the local C4 rotational symmetry and
asymmetrically shifts the dxz/dyz Fermi surfaces.
Conse-
quently, the zero-momentum uniform pairing is suppressed,
and Cooper pairs acquire a finite center-of-mass momentum
ql ∝Al. Because the direction of Al is inherently tied to the
lattice, it selects specific orbital orientations, naturally driving
the system into an orbital-selective pair density wave in the Γ5
channel.
Furthermore, the magnetic space group Pc4122 rigorously
constrains this spatial variation. The 41 screw axis couples
spatial rotations to layer translations. Because the underlying
effective gauge field rotates by 90◦along the c-axis, symmetry
strictly dictates that the two-component vector ⃗ψl must rotate
concurrently in the internal orbital space across adjacent lay-
ers (e.g., ⃗ψl+1(−y, x) = −iσy⃗ψl(x, y), where σi are Pauli matri-
ces acting on the orbital basis).

To visualize how a unidirectional pattern propagates
through the 3D crystal, one must consider the layer-by-layer
symmetries. Although the underlying exchange field rotates
by 90◦along the c-axis, the two stripe orientations related
by a 90◦rotation are degenerate. The ground state therefore
spontaneously selects one common orientation for the uni-
axial stripes on all superconducting layers. The most phys-
ically relevant scenario for EuRbFe4As4 is the diagonal ne-
matic state: because the Eu2+ spins order along the Fe-As
bonds, the gauge field axes (x, y) align with the Fe-As di-
rections. The diagonal nematic state thus naturally produces
a modulation along the Fe-Fe bonds, in excellent agreement
with the experimental STM observations [36]. In this state, all
layers may choose the same diagonal modulation wave vector,
for example along the x + y direction in Fig. 2(a), while the
x −y direction is its degenerate partner; the real-space stripes
run perpendicular to the selected wave vector. This is dynam-
ically coupled by the inter-layer Josephson tunneling currents
(Fig. 3). Viewed from the side, the spatially oscillating inter-
layer phase difference forces the out-of-plane transport to pe-
riodically reverse direction, generating a dense, regular array
of Josephson vortex-antivortex pairs across the spacer layers
(Fig. 4). The inter-layer current distribution also develops a
characteristic dimer-like pattern along the side-view direction,
with neighboring current loops grouped into paired structures.
Discussion and Conclusion.
Our phenomenological
Ginzburg-Landau analysis demonstrates that the combination
of local inversion symmetry breaking (Rashba SOC) and a

helical magnetic exchange field naturally generates a layer-
dependent effective U(1) gauge field. When applied to the
period-four magnetic structure of EuRbFe4As4, this effec-
tive gauge field naturally stabilizes uniaxial pair density wave
states.
The condensation into the 2D representation Γ5 is micro-
scopically mandated by the interplay of orbital physics and
magnetic symmetry breaking. Between Tc ≈37 K and Tm ≈
15 K, uniform superconductivity dominates. However, when
the helical magnetic order sets in below Tm, the induced ef-
fective gauge field asymmetrically shifts the degenerate Fe
3dxz/3dyz hole pockets. Because this gauge field explicitly
breaks local rotational symmetry, it suppresses the uniform
q = 0 state in these bands and forces the Cooper pairs to ac-
quire a finite momentum. This provides a rigorous symmetry-
based explanation for the STM observation that the unidirec-
tional PDW is not an independent instability, but emerges pre-
cisely below Tm as a consequence of the helical magnetic or-
der.
Quantitatively, the dimensionless momentum shift can be
estimated as q/kF ≈(|αR|kF)Hex/(2E2
F). Iron pnictides are
characterized by notably shallow Fermi pockets; for the rele-
vant dxz/dyz bands, EF ∼10–15 meV and kF ∼0.10–0.12 Å
−1.
For a shallow parabolic pocket, ℏvF ≃2EF/kF, so these val-
ues correspond to vF ∼3–4 × 104 m/s, consistent with the
estimate used above. The exchange field Hex on the conduc-
tion electrons, estimated via the RKKY mechanism from the
15 K ordering temperature, is Hex ≈26 meV. Combined with a
typical Rashba spin splitting |αR|kF ∼10–20 meV, the pertur-
bative expression gives q/kF ∼1–2, corresponding to a PDW
wavelength λ = 2π/q of a few nanometers, comparable to the
∼8-unit-cell STM modulation [36]. Since this estimate lies at
the edge of the controlled weak-exchange regime, it should be
regarded as an order-of-magnitude indication that the Rashba-
exchange mechanism naturally reaches the nanometer scale;
a fully quantitative wavelength requires a non-perturbative
multiband band-structure calculation.
EuRbFe4As4 is a multiband superconductor, and the
present GL theory focuses on the shallow dxz/dyz hole pock-
ets because they have the correct Γ5 orbital symmetry and the
smallest Fermi energies, making them the most susceptible to
a Rashba-exchange momentum shift. Other hole and electron
pockets can provide additional uniform superconducting stiff-
ness and interband Josephson locking, and may renormalize
the critical fields and modulation wave vector. Phenomeno-
logically, such effects are absorbed into the coefficients α,
g0, g2, and βi in Eq. (5); the GL theory commits only to the
Γ5 orbital symmetry of the dominant finite-momentum chan-
nel, not to a single-band microscopic model.
These addi-
tional bands do not, however, remove the symmetry-allowed
finite-momentum tendency in the dxz/dyz sector; a micro-
scopic multiband treatment is an important extension beyond
the scope of the present phenomenological theory.
Crucially, our phase diagrams (see Supplemental Material)
reveal that the structurally modulated Bloch SC state is sta-
bilized in the regime where the inter-layer Josephson cou-
pling g is relatively weak compared to the effective gauge field
energy. This is highly consistent with the crystallographic
nature of EuRbFe4As4. Because the superconducting FeAs
planes are separated by alternating, chemically distinct Rb+
and Eu2+ spacer layers, the material is a highly anisotropic
quasi-2D superconductor with a notably short c-axis coher-
ence length [39]. Consequently, the inter-layer coupling is
intrinsically weak, placing EuRbFe4As4 squarely within the
parameter regime where our theory predicts the uniaxial PDW
to be the robust ground state.
Furthermore, our theory yields a characteristic prediction:
the uniaxial PDW states are accompanied by complex, spon-
taneously generated 3D current patterns. The intra-layer uni-
axial flow patterns (see Fig. S4 in the Supplemental Material)
are coupled by the modulated inter-layer Josephson tunnel-
ing (Fig. 3), which manifests macroscopically as a periodic
lattice of Josephson vortex-antivortex pairs (Fig. 4). We es-
timate the local internal magnetic fields generated by these
vestigial 3D circulating currents to be on the order of a few
Gauss (∼0.1 −1 mT), spatially modulated at the PDW wave-
length. Although this signal is much smaller than the nearly
uniform dipolar field associated with the ordered Eu mo-
ments, it appears at the finite PDW wave vector rather than
at the uniform magnetic component, providing a momentum-
space distinction for local probes. Future experiments, such as
zero-field muon spin relaxation (µSR) or high-resolution scan-
ning SQUID microscopy, could directly detect these oscillat-
ing internal magnetic fields. Observing these unique loop-
current patterns and vortex-antivortex arrays would provide
a definitive, unambiguous confirmation of the helical gauge-
field mechanism in EuRbFe4As4.
