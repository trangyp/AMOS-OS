Abstract
When we consider a finite abelian group acting linearly on a polynomial ring, we
can find monomial generators for the subring of invariants. By Noether’s degree bound
and Hilbert’s finiteness theorem we know that there are finitely many minimal gener-
ators, but efficiently finding a generating set is not a trivial task. We present a new
algorithm for computing the invariant ring for elementary abelian groups acting on
polynomial rings with complex coefficients (or any other field of characteristic zero).
We follow a two-step process: first we generate a collection of n −k “seed” invariants
by calculating the kernel of a weight matrix that encodes our action. After we find the
seeds, we “grow” them into a generating set for the invariant ring by exploiting the
lattice structure of invariants modulo p. Our algorithm performs better than the one
currently available in Macaulay2, allowing us to compute more quickly invariants in
this setting.
Keywords: monomial invariants, diagonal linear action, elementary abelian groups,
macaulay2.

Introduction
Let G be an abelian group acting on a polynomial ring C[x1, . . . , xn] with complex coef-
ficients. A polynomial f ∈C[x1, . . . , xn] is invariant under the action of G if for all g ∈G,
g · f = f. The set of invariant polynomials form a finitely generated C−algebra called the
invariant ring. Even though there are infinitely many possible generating sets for the in-
variant ring, by Noether’s degree bound [5] we can find a finite minimal generating set of
invariants of degree less than or equal to |G|, the order of G. Moreover, since we restrict our
attention to finite abelian groups, after a change of basis every action is diagonal and we can
find generating sets consisting of only monomials. We study how to find minimal monomial
generating sets of the invariant ring arising from the action of a finite abelian group.

2.3
Abelian characters and weights
Above, we discussed the importance of working over a field C as this gives us a |G|-th root
of unity. Mapping each group element to a root of unity is an example of a character of the
group.
Definition 4. A multiplicative character is a group homomorphism χ : G →C∗where
C∗= C \ {0}.
The product of two multiplicative characters is a multiplicative character, so the set of
multiplicative characters forms a group bG. If the group homomorphism is an isomorphism,
then G ∼= bG. We will consider actions with trivial kernel so that we can identify the two
groups.
As G is abelian, the action of G on V is diagonalizable and so it can be represented
with a diagonal matrix. In fact, for ρ : G →V a linear representation of G, we have that
all matrices ρ(g) commute with each other and so they share eigenvalues and eigenvectors.
For this reason, they are simultaneously diagonalizable, i.e., diagonalizable under the same
change of basis.

2.4
Invariant rings
Let M denote the set of non-constant monomials in R = C[x1, . . . , xn]. Then, since the
action of the group G is diagonal, each monomial m ∈M is mapped to a scalar multiple of
itself.
Definition 5. A polynomial f ∈R is called invariant under the action of G if for all
g ∈G, we have that g · f = f.
Notice that a polynomial will be invariant if and only if each of its monomials is. As
a result, we can choose a set of invariant monomials as a minimal generating set for the
invariant ring.
Definition 6. The set of all invariant polynomials in n variables with complex coefficients
under the linear action of a finite group G forms a ring called the invariant ring, which
we will denote by RG.
For the action of a finite group on a polynomial ring with complex coefficients, Noether
[5] proved a bound on the degree of the generators of the invariant ring.

Luckily, Noether’s degree bound is achieved only when the group is cyclic (as proved by
Schmid [7]). In particular, in the case of elementary abelian groups, Olson [6] showed that
there is a better bound on the degree of the generators.

that G ∼= (Z/p)k. For a linear action of G, we can find minimal generators for the ring of
invariants of degree at most k(p −1) + 1.
For this reason, considering elementary abelian groups has the potential to lead to faster
methods of computing invariants. We introduce a two-step algorithm that achieves this goal.

Results
Our algorithm for computing minimal generating sets of invariant rings of elementary abelian
groups proceeds in 2 stages. First, with seed generation, we find a collection of n−k invariant
monomials called seeds whose exponents generate the kernel of the weight matrix W. The
second stage we call the seed growth, where we expand our small collection into a minimal
generating set for the ring of invariants.
We first illustrate a key idea of our method by considering the action of G = Zp ×Zp (for
p a prime) on the polynomial ring R = C[x, y, z]. Our target object is a minimal generating

Future Directions
The results in Section 3 allow us to create a new algorithm for elementary abelian groups,
so groups of the type (Z/p)k. Naturally, one might want to consider other p−groups or at
least groups of the type (Z/pm)k for m > 1. In this section we will start in that direction by
showing how we can use Nakayama’s Lemma to connect our elementary case to the (Z/pm)k
case, as well as discuss some related reductions.

4.1
Faithful representations
First, we will address a simple reduction.
If ϕ : Z/(pk) →GL(V ) is not faithful, i.e.,
H = ker ϕ ̸= {e}, then the weight matrix W has linearly dependent rows. In this case, one
can remove rows until we obtain a faithful representation of G/H on V and a new full rank
weight matrix W ′ with rk(W) = rk(W ′). Since the invariant ring depends only on the kernel
of the weight matrix, this reduction does not change RG. Thus, we may assume without loss
of generality that the representation is faithful.
