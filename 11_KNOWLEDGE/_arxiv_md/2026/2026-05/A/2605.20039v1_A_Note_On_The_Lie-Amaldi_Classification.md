A NOTE ON THE LIE–AMALDI CLASSIFICATION
HASSAN AZAD, INDRANIL BISWAS, AND RYAD GHANAM
Abstract. The Lie–Amaldi classification of finite dimensional nilpotent algebras of
vector fields is refined, using the rank of the center of the Lie algebra as an invariant.
1. Introduction
The Lie–Amaldi classification of finite dimensional algebras of vector fields in two and
three variables is given in Lie [LiEn] and Amaldi [Am1, Am2]. The classifications are in
geometric terms — whether the algebra is transitive or not and whether it has an invariant
foliation. The classifications are not given in terms of algebraic concepts like nilpotent
algebras, solvable but not nilpotent etc. etc. The word nilpotent does not occur in Amaldi
[Am1, Am2]. It is therefore desirable to give explicit normal forms of nilpotent algebras,
especially as nonabelian solvable algebras live in normalizers of nilpotent algebras. It is
in this sense that we have called the normal forms given in this paper a refinement of the
Lie–Amaldi classification for nilpotent algebras. If one approaches the subject from an
algebraic point of view, the proofs simplify considerably, as shown in [ABFM].
Whenever the algebra admits an invariant foliation, Lie and Amaldi study the leaf
space associated to the foliation to give a local classification of the finite dimensional Lie
algebras of vector fields in two and three variables. While the finite dimensional algebras
of vector fields in two variables were completely classified in [LiEn], this is no longer true
for finite dimensional algebras of vector fields in three variables.
For solvable Lie algebras, a classification, up to point equivalence, is not possible,
in general. The reason is that if an abelian Lie algebra of vector fields has dimension
greater than its rank, it is not possible, in general, to classify the algebra up to point
transformations.
Now, if G is a solvable non-abelian Lie algebra of vector fields, there are two possibilities:
The center Z(G′) of G′ = [G, G] is of rank three: in this case G lives in the normalizer
of translations and G can be classified easily. The other possibility is that Z(G′) gives a
nontrivial invariant foliation.
If the normal form of Z(G′) involves arbitrary functions, its classification, up to point
transformations, is therefore not possible. For this reason, we have organised the deter-
mination of the local canonical forms of all nilpotent algebras — including intransitive
algebras — according to the rank of the center and have given normal forms and proce-
dures for constructing finite dimensional nilpotent algebras with prescribed rank of the

3. Nilpotent subalgebras of vector fields with three variables
Let V (R3) denote the C∞vector fields with three variables.
As explained in the Introduction, the centers of nonabelian algebras always give invari-
ant foliations. These nontrivial centers give projections to lower dimensional nilpotent
algebras, and the kernels of these projections are also abelian algebras. As the kernels and
the centers in general involve arbitrary functions, it is not possible to give a classification
up to point equivalence.
Therefore, all one can do is to give constructive procedures for generating nilpotent
algebras. We proceed to do this according to the rank of the center of the algebra.
The forms of the nilpotent algebras show why it is not possible to do the same for
solvable non-nilpotent algebras. All one can say is that a solvable algebra must live in
the normalizer of a nilpotent algebra. Because of the presence of arbitrary functions, a
constructive procedure, for solvable non-nilpotent algebras is, in general, not available.
Let us first record the classification of abelian Lie subalgebras of V (R3).
3.1. The case of rank three. If g is an abelian Lie subalgebra of rank three, then
locally g — in suitable coordinates — is equivalent to ⟨∂x, ∂y, ∂z⟩. We use the notation
⟨∂x, ∂y, ∂z⟩to denote the algebra generated by ∂x, ∂y and ∂z.
3.2. The case of rank two. If g is abelian and of rank two, then g — in suitable
coordinates — is equivalent to an algebra with basis
⟨∂x, ∂y, f1(z)∂x + g1(z)∂y, · · · , fN(z)∂x + gN(z)∂y⟩.
