A tree-like fractal Dirichlet space lying between strong
and weak elliptic Harnack inequalities
Caoxu Huang1, Guanhua Liu1
Center for Applied Mathematics and KL-AAGDM, Tianjin University, Tianjin, 300072, China
Abstract
In this paper we construct a self-similar fractal configured as an infinitely branched
tree and equip it with a regular self-similar Dirichlet form. We show anomalous
behaviour of the mean exit time with respect to typical metric balls. Under prop-
erly selected self-similar measure, we further show the weak elliptic Harnack in-
equality holds but the strong analogue fails.
Keywords: self-similar fractal, Dirichlet form, mean exit time, weak elliptic
Harnack inequality, strong elliptic Harnack inequality
2020 MSC: 28A80, 31C25, 47D07, 60J46
1. Introduction
Harnack inequalities constitute a key part in the DeGiorgi–Nash–Moser theory
on elliptic and parabolic partial differential equations as well as their counterparts
for Markovian processes, controlling the ratio between the supremum and infi-
mum of a harmonic or caloric function in a metric ball by a constant independent
of the function and the ball. This idea extends to general metric measure spaces
equipped with regular Dirichlet forms, especially to self-similar fractals.
The parabolic Harnack inequality (for short, PHI) bridges geometry and en-
ergy structure to heat kernel estimates and regularity of solutions. The second
author [9] completed the theory of PHI and its weak version (wPH) on arbitrary
Dirichlet spaces. In particular, by [9, Corollary 2.5] (Corollary 1.2 in the second
ArXiv preprint), for a strongly local Dirichlet form on two-sided doubling spaces,
(PHI) ⇔(wPH) ⇔(EHI) + (E) ⇔(wEH) + (E),

the conditions involved therein, thus irrelevant to the exceptions.
We learn about the first exceptional case recently from Kajino and Murugan
[6]. Assuming some metric doubling property, they extract (EHI) from (PHI) –
that is exactly (EHI) \ (E) according to (1.1) – from the aspect of conformal walk
dimension. Meanwhile, their treatment gives no clue to weak Harnack properties.
Up to now there is still zero knowledge on the second exceptional case in
(1.2). This paper provides a first step to this problem. We construct for the first
time a concrete connected example where (E) truly fails under any possible scale,
and show on this example that (EHI) fails but (wEH) holds with respect to proper
self-similar weights of the reference measure.
