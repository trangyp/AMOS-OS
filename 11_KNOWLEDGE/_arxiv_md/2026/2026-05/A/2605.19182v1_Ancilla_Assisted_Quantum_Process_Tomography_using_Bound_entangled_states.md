Ancilla Assisted Quantum Process Tomography using Bound entangled states
Gurvir Singh∗
Department of Physical Sciences, Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Mohali,
Sector 81 SAS Nagar, Manauli PO 140306, Punjab, India
Lu et al. [Ann. Phys. (Berlin) 534, 2100550 (2022)] posed the question of whether bound entan-
gled states can be used for ancilla-assisted quantum process tomography (AAQPT). In this work,
we answer this question in the affirmative by explicitly demonstrating that certain bound entan-
gled states can be used for AAQPT. We further show that, although local filtering operations may
improve the trace norm of the realignment criterion, they are essentially ineffective for AAQPT, as
they render the resulting states unfaithful and therefore unsuitable for reliable process reconstruc-
tion. Further, we investigate the efficiency of bound entangled states for AAQPT and establish
quantitative bounds by comparing their performance with Werner and isotropic states. Our results
therefore provide a new application of bound entangled states in the context of ancilla-assisted
quantum process tomography.
I.
INTRODUCTION
One of the fundamental challenges in quantum infor-
mation is physical verification and characterization of
quantum states.
The standard technique for this pur-
pose is Quantum State Tomography [1]. Given the well
known duality between quantum states and quantum
channels via the Choi-Jamiolkowski isomorphism, one
can pose a similar question about characterization of
processes (equivalently, quantum channels). This proce-
dure is known as Quantum Process Tomography (QPT).
There are several standard techniques to implement QPT
in practice which can be broadly classified into direct
and indirect methods. Indirect techniques rely on quan-
tum state tomography to extract information about the
quantum process, requiring an additional step of apply-
ing an inversion map to the output data. The two main
indirect approaches are Standard Quantum Process To-
mography [2, 3] and Ancilla-Assisted Quantum Process
Tomography (AAQPT) [4, 5]. On the other hand, a di-
rect technique to perform QPT, known as Direct- Char-
acterization of Quantum Dynamics, has also been devel-
oped [6]. Comparative Resource analysis of these tech-
niques have subsequently been studied [7]. In the present
paper, we focus specifically on AAQPT, an indirect tech-
nique that inherently depends on the invertibility of the
output state. This property of invertibility, termed faith-
fulness, plays a crucial role in the success of QPT via
this method. Recently, an operational definition of faith-
fulness has been formulated for two-qubit systems [8].
A particularly intriguing observation in this context is
that even certain separable mixed states, Werner and
Isotropic states can be useful for performing AAQPT [5].
The study of Bound entangled states has been a long-
standing area of interest in quantum information. Even
though bound entanglement is not distillable, various
application have been discovered, including superactiva-
tion of bound entanglement (i.e. using tensor products of

bound entangled states to generate distillable states) [9],
positive key rates in quantum key distribution [10, 11]
and Quantum Metrology [12, 13], with the most recent
example of usefulness of Bound entangled states being
demonstrated for Prepare-and-Measure Scenarios [14].
Recently, Lu et al. [15] demonstrated that not all entan-
gled states are useful for AAQPT and provided and ex-
plicit example of a bound entangled state that is not use-
ful to perform AAQPT. They conclude their work with
an open question: Are all bound entangled states useless
for AAQPT?
In this work, we answer their question in the nega-
tive, demonstrating there exist bound entangled states
that can indeed be used for AAQPT. This provides yet
another application for bound entangled states, further
expanding their role in quantum information processing.
The rest of the paper is structured as follows: In Sec. II,
we introduce the necessary notions required to under-
stand AAQPT. In Sec. III, we present the main result,
where we show that bound entangled states are useful for
AAQPT. In Sec. IV, we discuss the role of local filtering
operation in the context of AAQPT. In Sec. V, we com-
pare the performance of bound entangled states to well
known states in context of AAQPT. Finally, we provide
a conclusion in Sec. VI.

IV.
EFFECT OF LOCAL OPERATIONS ON
AAQPT
Since the proposal by Gisin [26], local filtering has been
known to reveal operational advantages in several quan-
tum information tasks. For instance, appropriate filters
can be used for entanglement purification [27–29], reveal
hidden nonlocality [30, 31], study EPR steering [32], and
uncover hidden steerability [33]. It has also been used to
activate hidden teleportation power (HTP) [34]. Here,
we investigate the effects of local filtering operations on
AAQPT. Consider the local filtering operation:
ρ′ =
(A ⊗B) ρ (A ⊗B)†
Tr[(A ⊗B) ρ (A ⊗B)†],
(22)
where the local filters satisfy A†A ≤I and B†B ≤I.
In contrast to unitary local operations, which leave the
trace norm of the realigned operator invariant, general
local operations can significantly change the value of the
quantity ∥R(ρ)∥1 = ∥R(ρ)∥tr.
Quantum operations implementable by local opera-
tions and classical communication are generated by the
following elementary operations [35]: (i) attaching un-
correlated ancillas, (ii) tracing out subsystems, (iii) per-
forming local unitaries, and (iv) carrying out L¨uders–von
Neumann projective measurements. These transforma-
tions affect the realignment norm in distinct ways.
Proposition 1 (Rudolph [21]). The quantity ∥R(ρ)∥1
satisfies the following properties: (i) it is invariant un-
der local unitary transformations; (ii) it is non-increasing
under the addition of uncorrelated ancillas; (iii) it is non-
increasing under L¨uders–von Neumann projective mea-
surements; (iv) it may increase, decrease, or remain un-
changed under tracing out subsystems.
As an immediate consequence, the CCNR criterion is
not invariant under general local operations. In particu-
lar, even if a state satisfies the bound ∥R(ρ)∥1 ≤1, there
exist local trace– non-increasing transformations that can
map it to a state whose realignment norm exceeds this
threshold.
From the perspective of AAQPT, what matters is that
the realignment map R(·) remains full rank, since faith-
fulness is equivalent to invertibility of the associated
Choi–Jamio lkowski map.
Although some local opera-
tions, most notably local unitaries preserve this prop-
erty, others need not.
In particular, a class of physi-
cally relevant SLOCC maps are subspace filters: these
are rank-reducing contractions that preferentially select a
low-dimensional subspace while annihilating the orthog-
onal complement. Such filters can enhance certain oper-
ational quantities, yet they tend to introduce degenera-
cies in the singular spectrum of the realigned operator,
thereby destroying faithfulness and rendering the result-
ing state unusable for AAQPT.

For the bound entangled state ρCCNR ∈C4 ⊗C4 max-
imally violating the the CCNR criterion, we obtain the
same value,
F(ρCCNR) = 0.1667.
(36)
In contrast, the optimal separable isotropic probe
achieves only F(ρiso) = 1/10. This clearly demonstrates
that bound entangled states provide a genuine opera-
tional advantage over known full rank separable states
for AAQPT. Moreover, in C4 ⊗C4, the bound entan-
gled probe performs on par with the maximally entangled
Werner state.
VI.
CONCLUSION
In this work, we have demonstrated that bound entan-
gled states can be effectively utilized for AAQPT. More-
over, they offer advantage over full rank mixed separa-
ble states as they exhibit higher faithfulness, leading to
improved performance.
Further we have explored the
role of local filtering in regards to AAQPT. This study
also sheds light on the structure of complete tensor rank
bound entangled states.
An interesting open problem is the analytical charac-
terization of the maximal violation of the CCNR criterion
over the set of PPT entangled states. While numerical
optimization based on semidefinite programming and it-
erative see-saw techniques can be used to investigate this
problem, such approaches become computationally de-
manding as the local dimension increases. Therefore, ob-
taining analytical results beyond low dimensional cases
remains challenging. Another open problem is to charac-
terize the class of bound entangled states that are useful
for AAQPT. Such states must have maximal operator
Schmidt rank [5], however a complete characterization of
this class of states is an open problem. Our work iden-
tifies AAQPT as a potential application of bound entan-
gled states. Exploring the experimental implementation
of such schemes could be an interesting direction for fu-
ture research.
