3. The prime case
We shall use the following elementary consequence of the Smith normal form.
Lemma 3.1. Let A be an N × N integer matrix, let p be a prime, and let r ≥0. If
rankFp(A mod p) ≤N −r,
then pr divides det A.
Proof. Let
UAV = diag(s1, . . . , sN)
be a Smith normal form of A, where U, V are unimodular integer matrices.
The
reductions of U and V modulo p are invertible over Fp, and hence multiplication by
them preserves rank over Fp. Thus the rank of A modulo p is the number of diagonal
entries si whose residue modulo p is non-zero. If this rank is at most N −r, then at
least r of the si are divisible by p. Consequently
pr | s1 · · · sN = det(UAV) = ± det A,
as required. If some si = 0, the conclusion is of course still valid.
□
