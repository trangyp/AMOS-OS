ALGEBRAIC ASPECTS OF UNCONDITIONAL LATTICE POLYTOPES
KENTA MORI, RYO MOTOMURA, HIDEFUMI OHSUGI AND AKIYOSHI TSUCHIYA
Abstract. Unconditional polytopes are convex polytopes that are symmetric with respect
to all coordinate hyperplanes and arise naturally from anti-blocking polytopes by reflection.
This paper investigates algebraic relations between an anti-blocking lattice polytope and its
associated unconditional lattice polytope. We prove that the toric ring of an anti-blocking
lattice polytope is normal if and only if the toric ring of the associated unconditional lattice
polytope is normal. We also show that the toric ideal of an anti-blocking lattice polytope is
generated by quadratic binomials if and only if the same holds for the associated uncondi-
tional lattice polytope. As an application, we obtain a graph-theoretic characterization of
quadratic generation of symmetric stable set ideals.
1. Introduction
A lattice polytope is a convex polytope all of whose vertices have integer coordinates. A
lattice polytope P ⊂Rn
≥0 of dimension n is called anti-blocking if for any y = (y1, . . . , yn) ∈
P and x = (x1, . . . , xn) ∈Rn with 0 ≤xi ≤yi for all i, it holds that x ∈P.
Anti-
blocking polytopes were studied and named by Fulkerson [2, 3] in the context of combinatorial
optimization, but they are also known as convex corners or down-closed polytopes; see, for
example, [1]. Typical examples include stable set polytopes of graphs and chain polytopes
of posets.
For ε = (ε1, . . . , εn) ∈{−1, 1}n and x = (x1, . . . , xn) ∈Rn, set
εx := (ε1x1, . . . , εnxn).
Given an anti-blocking lattice polytope P ⊂Rn
≥0 of dimension n, we define
P± := {εx ∈Rn : ε ∈{−1, 1}n, x ∈P}.
Since P is anti-blocking, P± is a convex lattice polytope. Moreover, for any ε ∈{−1, 1}n and
x ∈P±, one has εx ∈P±. A lattice polytope Q ⊂Rn of dimension n is called unconditional
if εx ∈Q for every ε ∈{−1, 1}n and every x ∈Q. Thus P± is an unconditional lattice
polytope naturally associated with P. In particular, P± is symmetric with respect to all
coordinate hyperplanes, and hence the origin 0 belongs to the interior of P±. Unconditional
convex bodies form a classical class in convex geometry. From the viewpoint of lattice poly-
topes, unconditional polytopes have recently been studied in connection with combinatorics
and commutative algebra. For example, Kohl, Olsen and Sanyal investigated unconditional
reflexive polytopes and characterized them in terms of perfect graphs [5]. Moreover, uncondi-
tional polytopes are closely related to locally anti-blocking polytopes, whose h∗-polynomials
and γ-positivity have been studied [6]. These developments motivate the study of algebraic
properties of unconditional lattice polytopes arising from anti-blocking lattice polytopes.

The main purpose of this paper is to investigate algebraic relations between an anti-

we study to what extent algebraic properties of the toric rings and toric ideals of these two
polytopes are shared by each other. We show that this is indeed the case for normality and
quadraticity.

Theorem 1.1. Let P ⊂Rn be an anti-blocking lattice polytope. Then K[P] is normal if and
only if K[P±] is normal.
Our second main result concerns quadratic generation of toric ideals.

symmetric stable set ideals.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce locally anti-blocking lattice
polytopes and prove a more general normality result in this setting, from which Theorem 1.1
follows immediately. Section 3 is devoted to quadratic generation. In Subsection 3.1, we
prove the implication from IP to IP±. In Subsection 3.2, we prove the converse implication
from IP± to IP. Finally, in Subsection 3.3, we apply Theorem 1.2 to symmetric stable set
polytopes and obtain a characterization in terms of Kempe equivalence.

2. Normality of the toric rings of locally anti-blocking polytopes
In this section, we prove a more general normality result for locally anti-blocking lattice
polytopes, from which Theorem 1.1 follows immediately.
We begin by recalling the integer decomposition property. A lattice polytope P ⊂Rn is
said to have the integer decomposition property (IDP, for short) if for any integer t ≥1 and
any lattice point x ∈tP ∩Zn, there exist lattice points x1, . . . , xt ∈P ∩Zn such that
x = x1 + · · · + xt.
Here tP := {tα : α ∈P}. A lattice polytope which has the integer decomposition property
is called IDP. We also recall the notion of spanning. A lattice polytope P ⊂Rn is called
spanning if the lattice points in P affinely generate Zn. In general, normality and IDP are
not equivalent. However, for any spanning lattice polytope P, the toric ring K[P] is normal if
and only if P is IDP. Since every full-dimensional anti-blocking lattice polytope is spanning,
this equivalence applies to the polytopes considered in this paper.

Let G be a graph on the vertex set [n] := {1, 2, . . . , n} with the edge set E(G). Given
a subset S ⊂[n], let G[S] denote the induced subgraph of G on the vertex set S.
A
subset S ⊂[n] is called a stable set (or an independent set) of G if {i, j} /∈E(G) for all
i, j ∈S with i ̸= j. Namely, a subset S ⊂[n] is stable if and only if G[S] is an empty
graph. In particular, the empty set ∅and any singleton {i} with i ∈[n] are stable. Denote
S(G) = {S1, . . . , Sm} the set of all stable sets of G. Given a subset S ⊂[n], we associate
the (0, 1)-vector ρ(S) = P

The stable set polytope PG is anti-blocking of dimension n.
