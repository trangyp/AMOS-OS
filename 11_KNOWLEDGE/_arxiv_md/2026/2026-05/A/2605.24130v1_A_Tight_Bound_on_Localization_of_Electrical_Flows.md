A Tight Bound on Localization of Electrical Flows
Ori Gurel-Gurevich∗
Asaf Nachmias†
Sushant Sachdeva‡
Abstract
We prove that for any unweighted graph on n vertices the ℓ1 norm of a unit electric
current between the endpoints of a random edge is at most 2 log n. Furthermore, we show that
on any weighted graph the spectral norm of the entry-wise absolute value of the symmetric
transfer-current matrix is at most 2 log n. This bound is tight up to constants and improves the
O(log2 n) bound from [Schild-Rao-Srivastava, SODA ’18]. The initial proofs were generated
by OpenAI’s ChatGPT 5.5 Pro; the authors have verified and rewritten them to enhance
readability and provide additional context.

Introduction
Electrical flows are fundamental in modern graph algorithms. They minimize electrical energy,
which is an ℓ2
2 quantity, and can be efficiently computed in almost-linear time using the fast
Laplacian-system solvers of Spielman and Teng [ST11]. Many algorithmic applications, however,
require controlling localization quantities closer to ℓ1. Thus, it is natural to ask how far the unit
electric flow between two nearby vertices travels.
Schild, Rao, and Srivastava [SRS18] formulated a clean version of this question. For an
unweighted graph with n vertices and m edges and two directed edges e and f, let ie(f) denote
the electric current flowing on f when a unit current flows across the endpoints of e. The ℓ2
norm squared of the current vector ie is the effective electric resistance between the endpoints
of e, hence ∥ie∥2 ≤1 and Cauchy-Schwarz gives only ∥ie∥1 ≤√m. This bound can be attained
(see [SRS18, Example 1.1]) for a small number of edges, but not when averaging over e. Indeed,
in [SRS18, Theorem 1.4] it is proved that m−1 P

improving this bound to O(log n).

The original localization theorem is due to Schild, Rao, and Srivastava [SRS18]. They proved
an O(log2 n) bound for the spectral norm of the entrywise absolute transfer-current matrix and
derived the corresponding O(log2 n) average-flow-length bound for unweighted graphs.
Several later works use localization-type statements or closely related Schur-complement
and sparsification primitives. The almost-linear time algorithm of Schild [Sch18] for sampling
random spanning trees uses Laplacian solvers, Schur complements, and structural facts about
electrical flows. Liu, Peng, and Yang [LPY25] explicitly use the electrical-flow localization
theorem of Schild–Rao–Srivastava to find uncorrelated edge sets for approximate spanning-tree
counting. Forster, Goranci, Liu, Peng, Sun, and Ye [FGLPSY22] develop minor-based vertex
sparsifiers and a distributed Laplacian paradigm. Anagnostides, Lenzen, Haeupler, Zuzic, and
Gouleakis [ALHZG23] refine the resulting distributed Laplacian solvers using low-congestion
shortcuts.
Finally, the spectral subspace sparsification framework of Li and Schild [LS18]
generalizes Schur-complement sparsifiers. In all above results, our improved localization result
improves the sparsification primitives, though in downstream applications it propagates only as
hidden-polylogarithmic improvements.
