A New Self-Dual Gravitational Instanton Solution on a Local Conformal
K¨ahlerian Manifold in a Brane World Model
Reinoud Jan Slagter1,∗
Abstract
It is believed that black holes remain a clean laboratory for probing ideas about quantum gravity. Decades of work on
these obscure objects have shown they behave like ordinary thermodynamic systems with temperature and entropy, or
to the extreme, even as elementary particles. It remains a huge challenge to reconcile the large-scale properties with
the underlying quantum description. In this manuscript we extend on former research on the exact time-dependent
instanton solution on a vacuum Kerr-like warped spacetime in conformal dilaton gravity, where one writes gµν =
ω4/(n−2) ˜gµν, with ˜g considered as ’un-physical’ and ω a dilaton ’gravi’-scalar. Remarkably, the metric solution
results from a first-order PDE, allowing the connection with self-duality. The singular points are determined by a
quintic polynomial. This suggests that this is the highest possible polynomial in describing the singularities of black
holes of Petrov type D axially symmetric manifolds and don’t fits the Plebanski-Demianski classification of black holes
which is determined by a fourth order polynomial. The solution can be described by a locally conformal K¨ahlerian
manifold with Euclidean signature and a K¨ahler potential. As such, the self-dual nature makes the comparison with the
Yang-Mills instanton counterpart solution interesting. This is possible for the effective 4D manifold, despite the fact
that a K¨ahler manifold in 5D cannot be modeled. If fact, we are dealing with an effective 4D self-dual K¨ahler manifold
with a recurrent conformal structure. It is conjectured that it represents a gravitational instanton. This happened by
the projected Weyl tensor of 5D on the brane. The so-called Kaluza-Klein contribution contains information on bulk
gravity waves and effects the evolution of the brane geometry. We also used the two-fold Riemannian cover CP2
by the Klein topology. The topology of the gravitational instanton would be S3 × R/Z2. The antipodal boundary
condition on the hyper-surface of a Klein bottle ∼C1 × C1 is applied to describe the Hawking particles during the
evaporation process. We used the Hopf fibration to get S2 as the black hole horizon, where the centrix is not in a
torus but in the Klein bottle. The twist fits very well with the antipodal identification of the points on the horizon.
No ’cut and past’ is necessary, so the Hawing particles remain pure without instantaneous information transport. We
also suspect that the compact objects recently observed – the so-called ‘little red dots’ in the very early universe – are
linked to primordial black holes created by instantons. Finally, we reveal a connection between the description of the
interior of our new black hole solution and the similar model proposed by Janis, Newman and Winicour some time ago
of the Schwarzschild solution in complex coordinates with a zero rest mass scalar field, which develops a anomalous
asymmetry. The model can be easily extended to the non-vacuum situation by including a scalar field. Both the dilaton
(or gravi-scalar) and the scalar field can be treated as quantum fields when approaching the Planck area.
Keywords: Primordial black hole, conformal invariance, brane-world models, Klein surface, antipodicity, K¨ahler
manifolds, instantons

These primordial objects could also be related to the so-called instantons. Self-duality plays an important role
in classifying gravitational instantons. It became quite clear that these Euclidean self-dual solutions in Einstein’s
theory play a role in quantum gravity. These solutions have vanishing classical action and non-trivial topological
invariants. Moreover, there is a similarity with the YM Euclidean self-dual solutions [6]. Despite their Euclidean
origin, instantons, like solitons, play a fundamental role in quantum theories. Their importance arises, for example,
in tunneling effects using the WKB approximation. Instanton-like solutions are expected to be all self-dual, localized
in Euclidean spacetimes and free of singularities. They give a dominant contribution to the path integral that is as
important as that of the flat metric itself. They also have time-parity inversion. However, gravitational instantons do
not contribute to the zero-frequency modes of a spin-1/2 Dirac particle, while they produce zero-frequency solutions of
spin-3/2 particles. It is also conjectured ([7, 8, 9, 10]) that there is a deep connection between the gravitational instanton
and the alternating discrete symmetry group A5 which serves as a discrete subgroup of SO(4) of its boundary. A
gravitational instanton is a four-dimensional regular spacetime M, self-dual Ricci curvature and asymptotically local
Euclidean, whose boundary has a Ak,Dk,T,0,I symmetry2. In fact, every finite subgroup of S3 ∼SU(2), is either
cyclic or conjugate to these binary subgroups.
An important link can be established between an asymptotically local Euclidean (ALE) self-dual manifold and
the 3-sphere. The related classifications of S3 by symmetry groups, are well known, of which the icosahedral and
dodecahedral classifications are the most relevant to our situation. For the well known Eguchi-Hanson (EH) gravi-
tational instanton[6], it is the cyclic group Ak. One needs, however, a double copy to avoid singularities in physical
ALE spaces. For our 5D warped spacetime, our boundary is the Klein surface, embedded in R4 ∼C1 × C1 and the
symmetry the double cover group I of the icosahedron.
An interesting study has also been done by Gibbons[7]. He studied, on CP2, a gravitational instanton surrounded
by an event horizon and compared the solution with the SU(2) Yang-Mills instanton. This study is based on the
Riemannian solution of the Einstein equations with a cosmological constant on CP2 by applying the Hopf-fibering.
The Weyl tensor is then anti-self dual and they conclude that there is no Lorentzian counterpart connection, i.e., it
cannot be related to a real 4-dimensional manifold. Our model can. An overview of the huge amount of research, can
be found elsewhere[11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17].
We also treated the complexification of our model manifold. We know that differential geometry provide a power-
ful tool for the study of complex compact manifolds. Any complex manifold admits a Hermitian metric and is called
a K¨ahler manifold, if the associated differential 2-form is closed. A Hermitian manifold, moreover, has a real induced
Riemannian metric. The most well-known example is the Fubini-Study metric[19]. We conjecture that our model is
K¨ahlerian and self-dual, so represents a gravitational instanton. We use the fact that any complex 5D manifold can be
embedded on a projective space, is K¨ahler.

3.1. Introduction
A long time ago, Israel and Penrose formulated already the possible transition from Schwarzschild to Kerr and
visa versa in relation to the point singularity. The Schwarzschild solution is the only vacuum solution that has a
non-singular event horizon. A singular event horizon has to do with the the conjecture that a asymmetric collapsing
body must first radiate away all its higher multipole moments. Israels theorem prohibits the development of trapped
surfaces due to the formation of singularities at the event horizon. If there is a singular event horizon, then collapse
trough it becomes impossible, thus obviating Penroses theorem. Later, Janis, et al. found a peculiar Schwarzschild-
like exact solution (a ’truncated’), where a massless scalar field is incorporated. It turned out that the Schwarzschild
sphere becomes a point singularity. They conjecture that all asymtotically flat static solutions with a point singularity
and which possess mass and asymmetry, is in fact the truncated Schwarzschild solution. See appendices D, E and F
for more details. If this is true, then one has to revise the notion of collapse beyond the Schwarzschild radius. The
interior must possess a revised geometry. This is also the case for our model, where the antipodal boundary condition
is applied. Bear in mind that central singularity is not sustainable. It is, however believed that a correct description
of the interior of the black hole can only be achieved when quantum effects are included. This is where the issue
of gravitational instantons comes into play. We also know that Einstein and K¨ahler metrics are conformally related.

Let us summarize his method. We recall the following well-known fact. Given any differentiable fibre, where
the base space and the fibre are equipped with Riemannian metrics and the structural group G acts isometrically on
the fibres, then the specification of a connection on the fibre that is compatible with the action of the structural group
determines a Riemannian metric in the total fibre space, characterised solely by the following properties: 1. the metric
induced on the fibre above any point in the base is equivalent to the metric given in the abstract fibre by any structural
isomorphism between the two; 2. at every point on the fibre, any tangent vector that is horizontal with respect to the
connection is orthogonal to the fibre passing through that point; 3. the projection of the fibre onto the base space is
a Riemannian surjection (submersion), compatible everywhere with the metric given in the base space. On the other
hand, if the Riemannian structure of the base space and that of the fibres are K¨ahlerian structures, the same construction
of a metric structure on the total fibre space yields only a quasi-Hermitian structure, which in general is not integrable
into a complex structure and, even if it is, the defined metric is K¨ahlerian only in very rare cases; for example, in the
case of holomorphic vector fibres, a Hermitian structure constructed in this way is K¨ahlerian only if the curvature of
the connection is identically zero. Let M be an n-dimensional complex manifold, compact or otherwise, equipped with
a K¨ahler metric, which can be represented locally either by its Riemannian form

(4.29)

4.5. Almost complex self-duality of the FS
Almost (anti-) self-duality (or 2-forms) is related to the structure of the self-dual part of the curvature tensor,

structure. Many almost complex structures, even if associated with self-dual forms, are non-integrable. We shall see
that our manifold in integrable. Almost complex structures have important applications in symplectic geometry.
One can define Lie groups similar to those defined previously overR but working over C instead. One starts by

also sub-manifolds. These complex groups can be viewed in a real way, as a subgroup of the group of matrices that

checked. The real dimension of SL(n,C) is 2(n2 −1).
