A NILPOTENCE THEOREM FOR RATIONAL RIGID 2-RINGS
OF MODERATE GROWTH
LOGAN HYSLOP
Abstract. In this short note, we prove a general nilpotence theorem for a
rational rigid 2-ring all of whose objects satisfy a certain “moderate growth
condition” inspired from the theory of tensor categories. This applies in par-
ticular to the category of modules over a rational E∞-ring, to the derived
category of any super-Tannakian category in characteristic zero, and conjec-
turally to Voevodsky’s rational category of mixed motives over a field DMQ.
In fact, we further prove that any such category has enough tt-fields, which
can be chosen to be of the form Perf(L) for an even 2-periodic field L.

1. Introduction
In [Bal20a, Remark 5.15], Balmer had the nerves of steel to not conjecture that
the comparison map from the homological spectrum of a tt-category to its Balmer
spectrum is always a bijection, the statement then went on to become known as
the “nerves of steel conjecture.” In [BHR26, Theorem A], a counterexample was
constructed using free constructions in higher Zariski geometry, closely related to
an abelian tensor category introduced by Deligne in [Del07] as an example failing
to satisfy nice growth conditions. In the present paper, we restrict our attention,
in a sense, “away” from this bad locus, studying only rational rigid 2-rings with
objects satisfying some “mild growth conditions.” This will be made precise with
the notion of a “Schur-finite” rational rigid 2-ring (see Definition 3.1).
1.A. Some brief background. In an effort to keep this paper brief, we will for
the most part assume some knowledge of tensor triangular geometry, particularly
surrounding techniques used in Balmer spectra and homological spectra, referring
the reader to [BHR26, § 2] and the citations therein for relevant background. With
that said, we will however correct a sin from loc. cit. by way of introducing the
following definition, which is used numerous times without being given a name.
Definition 1.1. Say that a rational rigid 2-ring C is Nullstellensatzian-like (NS-like
for short) if the unit 1C is a Nullstellensatzian object in the category of commutative
algebras CAlg(Ind(C)) in C.
With this language, one major result from [BHR26] is that
Theorem 1.2 ([BHR26, Proposition 6.7, Theorem 7.1]). The homological spectrum
of a rational rigid 2-ring C can be described with points given by (equivalence classes
of) maps into NS-like objects, which act as a stable enhancement of homological
residue fields. Moreover, any such choice of “enhanced homological residue field” L
may be chosen so that the image of C →L generates the target.
Proof. By [BHR26, Theorem 7.1] and [BHR26, Proposition 6.7], for C a rational
rigid 2-ring, Spech(C) ≃Speccons
CAlg(Ind(C))(1C), where homological residue fields are
detected by (equivalence classes) of Nullstellensatzian commutative algebras L ∈
CAlg(Ind(C)). Setting L := PerfC(L) for any choice of representative L yields the
claim.
□
1.B. Layout of the paper. In §2, we provide some relevant background on parti-
tions and Schur functors which will be useful for the definition of what precisely we
mean by rational rigid 2-ring with “moderate growth.” In §3, we make precise this
notion via the definition of a Schur-finite rational rigid 2-ring in Definition 3.1, and
then we provide a proof of a criterion (Theorem 3.7) that makes it easy to check
when a rational rigid 2-ring is Schur-finite, allowing us to provide a great many
examples Proposition 3.9.
In §4, we prove the main theorem of the paper using techniques motivated from
Deligne’s work in [Del02] on symmetric tensor categories of moderate growth in
characteristic 0. To be precise, we prove:
Theorem 1.3 (Corollary 4.10; Theorem 4.12). Let C be a Schur-finite rational
rigid 2-ring. Then C has enough tt-fields in the sense of [BKS19], which can all
be chosen to be of the form Perf(L) for an even 2-perioidic field L. Furthermore,

Definition 2.1. A partition (of n) λ is a collection λ = (λ1, λ2, . . .) of non-negative

3. Schur-finite Rational Rigid 2-Rings
We begin by defining a condition which roughly tracks “moderate growth” for
rational rigid 2-rings.
Definition 3.1. An object X in a rational rigid 2-ring C is said to be Schur-finite
if there exists a partition λ such that the Schur functor Sλ annihilates X, that is
Sλ(X) ≃0. We say that a rational rigid 2-ring C is Schur-finite if every (rigid)
object X ∈C is Schur-finite.
Before proving the main theorem that generates examples for us, we take a short
detour which will be useful when dealing with fiber sequences.
3.A. A brief digression on filtered objects. In this subsection, we give a light-
ning recollection on filtered and graded objects, referring the reader to [BHS22,
Appendix B] and [Lur17, § 1.2.2] for more details.
Definition 3.2. Let C be a rational rigid 2-ring. Then the category Cfil of fil-
tered objects in C is the rational rigid 2-ring consisting of the compact objects in
the presheaf category Fun(Z≤, Ind(C)) of Ind(C)-valued functors from the preorder
category on Z, with the Day convolution symmetric monoidal structure. The cat-
egory Cgr of graded objects in C is similarly described as the compact objects in
the presheaf category Fun(Zop, Ind(C)) on the discrete ∞-category Z, with the Day
convolution symmetric monoidal structure.

3.B. Back to the main story. We can now prove the main theorem of this
section. A special case of the following result was proven in [Maz04, Lemma 3.6],
namely the case when C is the derived category of a Q-linear abelian ⊗-category.
Theorem 3.7. Suppose that a rational rigid 2-ring C is generated as an idempotent
complete rigid symmetric monoidal stable ∞-category by a set of objects {Xi} such
that each Xi is Schur-finite. Then C is Schur-finite.
Proof. It suffices to show that the property of being Schur-finite is closed under
taking tensor products, duals, summands, suspensions and cofibers.
For summands, this is clear since Sλ(X) is a summand of Sλ(X ⊕Y ), so the
vanishing of the latter implies the vanishing of the former.
For tensor products, sums and duals, this essentially follows from [Del02]. For
instance, one can use that an object being zero is detected at the level of the
homotopy category, and that C →hC is symmetric monoidal and additive, so
that it commutes with Schur functors, allowing us to reduce to the case of a Q-
linear symmetric monoidal additive 1-category, in which case the result follows
from [Del02, Corollary 1.13] and [Del02, Proposition 1.18].
For suspensions, recall from Corollary 2.22 that Sλ(ΣX) ≃Σ|λ|Sλt(X), from
which the result follows.
Finally, suppose we are given a fiber sequence X →Y →Z such that X and Y
are Schur-finite. We can define a filtered object Fil(Z) in C attached to this fiber
sequence by
Fil(Z) := . . . →0 →Y →Z →Z →. . . ,
whose associated graded object is Y ⊕Σ(X)(1). By Proposition 3.4, to see that
there is a Schur functor annihilating Fil(Z), it suffices to find a Schur functor which
annihilates the associated graded, and further by Lemma 3.6, it suffices to show
that there is a Schur functor which annihilates Y ⊕ΣX. Since Y and Σ(X) are
Schur-finite, we know already that their sum is too. Fixing any Schur functor which
annihilates Y ⊕ΣX, we see that it also annihilates Fil(Z), and inverting τ to get
back to C, we find that the chosen Schur functor must also annihilate Z, so Z is
Schur-finite as well.

P, we must have that (r)q−1 ∈P or (p −1)s ∈P, but this contradicts the choice of
(p)q as the unique minimal by inclusion rectangular partition contained in P, since
(p)q is incomparable with either (r)q−1 or (p −1)s.
To see that each ideal {λ: [(p)q] ⊆[λ]} is indeed prime, note that this is exactly
the kernel of the functor
Sym∗(Db(Q)) →Db(Q),
X 7→Q⊕q−1 ⊕ΣQ⊕p−1
by Proposition 2.23.
