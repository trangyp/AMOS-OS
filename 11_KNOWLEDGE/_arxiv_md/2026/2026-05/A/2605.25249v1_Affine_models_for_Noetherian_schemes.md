Affine models for Noetherian schemes
Alexey G. Gorinov, Egor S. Kosolapov
Abstract
Let S be a base scheme, assumed separated and Noetherian. We define adequate classes
of morphisms of S-schemes by formalizing certain properties of homotopy equivalences of
complex algebraic varieties. Other examples of adequate classes include morphisms of vari-
eties over an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic which induce an isomorphism
of ´etale cohomology, and weak equivalences in the cdh topology.
An affine model for a
Noetherian scheme X over S is an affine scheme MX equipped with an adequate morphism
MX →X.
In this paper we construct affine models for arbitrary separated schemes of finite type
over S.
Our construction can be viewed as a generalization of the Jouanolou trick.
As
an application, we construct a mixed Hodge structure on the Leray spectral sequence of
an arbitrary proper morphism f : X →Y of complex algebraic varieties, generalizing an
argument by Donu Arapura which assumed Y quasi-projective and f projective.

Introduction
Let S be a base scheme, which we assume separated and Noetherian unless stated otherwise.
Definition 1.1. A class W of morphisms in a subcategory C of schemes over S is called adequate
if the following conditions are satisfied:
• W is closed under composition;
• W contains the projections X ×S A1
S →X;
• For any morphism f : X →Y in C and any cover of X = X1 ∪X2 by closed subschemes and
Y = Y1 ∪Y2 by open subschemes, if f(Xi) ⊂Yi and the induced morphisms Xi →Yi and
X1 ∩X2 →Y1 ∩Y2 belong to W, then f ∈W.
One can think of arrows in W as homotopy equivalences of some type or another, see examples
below.
An affine model for a scheme X is a scheme Y ∈C affine over Z with a morphism m : Y →X
that belongs to W. One of the main results of this paper is as follows:
Theorem A (Theorem 3.1). Let C be the category of separated schemes of finite type over a
separated Noetherian scheme S, and let W be any class of adequate morphisms of C. Then every
scheme X ∈C admits an affine model.
Remark 1.1. The affine models from the theorem will also be affine over S by Lemma 3.3.

Affine models were inspired by the Jouanolou trick [Jo73], which we will now briefly review.
Let S = Spec(k) where k is an algebraically closed field. Suppose X is a quasi-projective variety
over k. We want to construct an affine algebraic variety MX over k, and a morphism MX →X of
k-varieties which would be a Zariski locally trivial fibre bundle with fibre An (for some n). We start
with X = Pm and take MPm to be the subvariety of the affine space of all (m+1)×(m+1)-matrices
A over k given by the equations A2 = A and rk A = 1. On the level of k-points, the morphism
MPm →Pm takes a matrix A to its image ∈Pm(k).
If X ⊂Pm is closed, we set MX = X ×Pm MPm. If X is arbitrary quasi-projective, let ¯X ⊃X
be the closure of X in some Pm ⊃X. We may assume that after blowing up ¯X −X if necessary
the complement ¯X −X is an effective Cartier divisor. Then the inclusion X →¯X is affine. Set

This construction was later generalized by Thomason [We89] to schemes having ample families of
line bundles, i.e. to divisorial schemes. Let us mention a few examples, as well as a non-example:
Example 1.4. Any projective scheme X over an affine base S is divisorial. A quasi-projective
variety over an algebraically closed field is divisorial.
Example 1.5. Every separated, locally factorial Noetherian scheme X over a field is divisorial. This
follows from the results of [Bo91]. In particular, every smooth scheme over a field is divisorial.
Example 1.6. A proper variety X over an algebraically closed field k with Pic(X) = 0 cannot be
divisorial. To give a concrete example, in [Ei92], Example 3.5, Eikelberg constructs a proper toric
variety XΣ with Pic(XΣ) = 0 defined by a fan Σ derived from the faces of a triangular prism with
one vertex perturbed.
In view of these examples it is natural to look for an analog of Jouanolou’s and Thomason’s
constructions which works for a larger class of schemes but retains some of the useful properties.
Among these properties, the first one that comes to mind is that over C, starting from a variety
X we get an affine variety MX and a morphism MX →X which is a homotopy equivalence on the
complex points. Axiomatizing this idea one arrives at the notion of an affine model.

As an application, we generalize a result of Arapura, proven in [Ar03] for projective morphisms
with quasi-projective target variety:
Theorem C (Theorem 5.2). Let f : X →Y be an arbitrary proper morphism between arbi-
trary complex algebraic varieties X and Y . Then the Leray spectral sequence associated with this
morphism is motivic.
Informally, a spectral sequence being motivic means that any functorial construction on the
cohomology groups of algebraic varieties (such as e.g. mixed Hodge structures) that is compatible
with the long exact sequence of relative cohomology groups extends to this spectral sequence. This
yields the following corollary:

Theorem D (Corollary 5.1). For an arbitrary proper morphism f : X →Y of complex algebraic
varieties, the Leray spectral sequence associated with f carries a natural mixed Hodge structure.
The idea of the proof of Theorem 3.1 in [Ar03] is as follows: Let f : X →Y be a morphism
of complex algerbaic varieties.
Firstly, one can define a spectral sequence associated with an
exhaustive filtration by closed subsets for any topological space. In the algebraic case these spectral
sequences are clearly motivic.
Secondly, when the target Y of the morphism f : X →Y is affine, there exists a filtration of Y
by closed subvarieties such that the spectral sequence obtained by filtering X by the preimages
is isomorphic to the Leray spectral sequence starting from the second page. This parallels the
construction of the Leray spectral sequence for a fiber bundle built from a filtration of Y by
cellular skeleta. In fact, by Beilinson’s lemma (see Lemma 5.3) one can generalize the notion of a
cellular filtration to any morphism f : X →Y and any constructible sheaf F on X.
Thirdly, if the target Y is not necessarily affine, one uses the Jouanolou trick to construct an
affine variety MY and a morphism MY →Y such that the Leray spectral sequence of f : X →Y
and of the pullback X ×Y MY →MY are isomorphic.
Our proof of Theorem C follows the same plan up until the last step, where we use our construction
of affine models, the Jouanolou trick not being available for non-quasi-projective varieties. For
affine models constructed using the Jouanolou trick it is straightforward to check that the Leray
spectral sequences of X →Y and X ×Y MY →MY are isomorphic. In our case this is still true
but requires some work.
Theorems B and C have ´etale analogs, namely Theorems 4.5 and 5.4 respectively.
Remark 1.3. In a companion paper [Ko] Theorems C and D will be extended to the case when f
is not necessarily proper.
Remark 1.4. Theorem B has an analog for complex analytic spaces such that the underlying
topological space is Hausdorff and has a countable dense subset; affine schemes get replaced with
Stein spaces. The details will appear elsewhere.
Remark 1.5. Another approach to equipping the Leray spectral sequence with a mixed Hodge
structure is via M. Saito’s theory of mixed Hodge modules, see [Sa90, Remark 4.6(2)] and [PS08,
Corollary 14.14]. We expect our approach to agree with M. Saito’s.
