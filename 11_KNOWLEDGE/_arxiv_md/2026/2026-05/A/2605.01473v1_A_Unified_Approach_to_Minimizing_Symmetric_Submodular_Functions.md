Symmetric Submodular Functions

Abstract
Symmetric submodular function minimization admits purely combinatorial algorithms using
special orderings of the ground set. Extending the minimum-cut algorithm of Nagamochi and
Ibaraki (1992), Queyranne (1998) showed that the maximum adjacency ordering yields a pendent
pair, which can be used to find a nontrivial minimizer. Nagamochi (2010) later introduced the
minimum degree ordering, which yields a flat pair and leads to the identification of extreme
sets. Despite the apparent similarity between these two algorithms, their connection remained
unclear.
In this paper, we introduce yet another ordering called minimum capacity ordering, and
extend it to a one-parameter family of orderings, called α-orderings, that unifies these two pre-
viously known orderings. We prove a general inequality for α-orderings, and our framework
recovers the known pendent-pair and flat-pair results as special cases, corresponding to α = −1
and α = 1, respectively. For each α ∈[−1, 1], the last two elements of an α-ordering form a
contractible pair, i.e., a pair whose contraction preserves the existence of a nontrivial minimizer,
which leads to a contraction algorithm that finds a nontrivial minimizer of a symmetric sub-
modular function in O(n3) oracle calls, where n is the cardinality of the ground set. In addition,
we discuss the ranges of α that ensure α-ordering to obtain these special pairs.

Introduction
Let V be a finite set of cardinality n. We denote by R and R+ the sets of reals and nonnegative
reals, respectively. A set function f : 2V →R is called submodular if
f(X) + f(Y ) ≥f(X ∪Y ) + f(X ∩Y ),
∀X, Y ⊆V.
Submodularity is a fundamental notion in discrete optimization.
It has been studied through
central structures such as matroids, polyhedra, and convexity [2, 6, 15].
The general problem
of minimizing a submodular function has a long history.

strongly polynomial algorithms were developed later by Iwata, Fleischer, and Fujishige [10] and by

In this paper, we focus on the symmetric case. A set function f : 2V →R is called symmetric if
f(X) = f(V \ X),
∀X ⊆V.
Symmetric submodular functions form an important subclass of submodular functions. Fujishige [4]
studied their structural properties and established a decomposition theory for them. For a sym-
metric submodular function f, both ∅and V are always minimizers. Accordingly, the symmetric
submodular function minimization problem asks for a nontrivial minimizer, i.e., a proper nonempty
subset that achieves the minimum value.
A canonical example of a symmetric submodular function is the cut capacity function of a
capacitated undirected graph. For an undirected graph G = (V, E) with an edge capacity function
c : E →R+, its cut capacity function κ : 2V →R+ is defined by
κ(X) :=

which is symmetric and submodular. The minimization of the cut function over nonempty proper
subsets coincides with the global minimum-cut problem in an undirected graph. A straightforward
polynomial-time approach to this problem is to fix a vertex s ∈V , compute a minimum (s, t)-cut
for each t ∈V \ {s} by a max-flow algorithm, and take the best one.
A key combinatorial approach to this problem originates in Nagamochi and Ibaraki [18], whose
correctness proof was later simplified by Frank [3] and by Stoer and Wagner [23]. The method is
based on constructing a maximum adjacency ordering (MA-ordering) of the vertices and using the
fact that the last two vertices in such an ordering form a pendent pair. This implies that, among
all cuts separating these two vertices, a minimum one is already represented by a singleton side,
so the pair can be contracted without losing any information needed to recover a global minimum
cut. Repeating this contraction step leads to one of the simplest and fastest deterministic global
minimum-cut algorithms.
Queyranne [20] extended this ordering-based contraction framework from graph cut functions to
arbitrary symmetric submodular functions, thereby obtaining a combinatorial strongly polynomial
algorithm that finds a nontrivial minimizer in O(n3) oracle calls.
For symmetric submodular
function minimization, this approach remains substantially simpler and faster than using strongly
polynomial algorithms for general submodular function minimization O(n) times.
More recently, Nagamochi [17] introduced the minimum degree ordering (MD-ordering) and
proved that the last two vertices in such an ordering form a flat pair.
In the graph setting,
contracting flat pairs yields not only a minimum cut but also all extreme sets (extreme subsets,
or LS-sets), which capture a finer structure of the connectivity function. The notion of extreme
sets predates this ordering-based framework: it was introduced by Luccio and Sami [16] as a
compact representation of graph connectivity through decomposition into minimally interconnected
subnetworks, and was later applied by Lawler [13] to hypergraph partitioning. Nagamochi [17]
further extended this viewpoint to symmetric submodular functions, showing that the ordering-
based contraction framework can recover not only a nontrivial minimizer but also the finer structural
information encoded by extreme sets with O(n3) oracle calls.
Exploiting the property of pendent pairs obtained by MA-ordering, Goemans and Soto [7]
presented an algorithm that finds a nontrivial minimizer of a symmetric submodular function in
a hereditary family of subsets using O(n3) oracle calls. They further extended this algorithm to
enumerate all minimal such optimal sets in the same asymptotic running time. Unlike symmetric
submodular function minimization, the problem itself breaks symmetry, and the MD-ordering has
not been applied to solve the same problem. In contrast, Goemans and Soto [7] also described

an algorithm that enumerates all extreme sets of a symmetric submodular function using O(n4)

From a higher perspective, however, the similarity between the algorithms of Queyranne and
Nagamochi suggests that the MA- and MD-orderings should be viewed not as isolated constructions,
but as manifestations of a broader common principle. The purpose of this paper is to clarify their
common structure.
To capture the algorithmic content of the pairs of elements obtained at the end of these orderings,
we introduce the notion of a contractible pair. This is a weaker property than being a pendent pair
or a flat pair, but it is still sufficient to support a contraction algorithm for finding a nontrivial
minimizer. We also introduce yet another ordering called the minimum capacity ordering (MC-
ordering). Our first main result (Theorem 4) shows that the last two elements of an MC-ordering
form a contractible pair, thereby obtaining the third ordering-based combinatorial algorithm for
finding a nontrivial minimizer of a symmetric submodular function with O(n3) oracle calls.
We then extend the MC-ordering to a one-parameter family of orderings, called α-orderings,
which interpolates between the two previously known orderings: the case α = −1 coincides with
the MA-ordering, while the case α = 1 corresponds to the MD-ordering. Our second main result is
a general inequality for α-orderings (Theorem 5). From this inequality, the pendent-pair theorem of
Queyranne and the flat-pair theorem of Nagamochi are both recovered as special cases. In addition,
for each α ∈[−1, 1], the last two elements of an α-ordering form a contractible pair (Corollary 1).
As a consequence, repeated contraction yields a nontrivial minimizer of a symmetric submodular
function with O(n3) oracle calls.
We also present complementary converse results showing that this range of parameters is tight
in a natural sense: for α /∈[−1, 1], the last two elements of an α-ordering need not be contractible.
Moreover, for α ̸= −1 and α ̸= 1, they need not be a pendent pair or a flat pair, respectively.
The outline of this paper is as follows. Section 2 summarizes the two previously known algo-
rithms for symmetric submodular function minimization. In Section 3, we introduce the concept of
contractible pairs, and then present how to use those pairs for minimizing symmetric submodular
functions. In Section 4, we introduce an MC-ordering, and show that the last two elements form
a contractible pair. Section 5 extends MC-ordering to α-ordering that unifies the MA- and MD-
ordering as its special cases, and shows that α-ordering for any α ∈[−1, 1] yields a contractible pair.
Finally, in Section 6, we examine the ranges of α that ensure α-ordering to obtain contractible,
pendent, and flat pairs.

Maximum Adjacency Ordering and Minimum Degree Ordering
In this section, we briefly summarize the two previously known ordering-based algorithms for sym-
metric submodular function minimization. Given an ordering (v1, . . . , vn) of V , we denote V0 := ∅
and Vi := {v1, . . . , vi} for i = 1, . . . , n.
Consider an undirected graph G = (V, E) with an edge capacity function c : E →R+. For a
subset W ⊊V and a vertex y ∈V \ W, let adj(W, y) denote the adjacency between W and y, i.e.,
the sum of c(x, y) for all edges {x, y} ∈E with x ∈W. Note that adj(W, y) can be expressed in
terms of the cut capacity function κ by
adj(W, y) = 1
2 [κ({y}) + κ(W) −κ(W ∪{y})] .

that vi attains the maximum value of adj(Vi−1, y) among all y ∈V \ Vi−1 for i = 2, . . . , n. The

key observation of Nagamochi and Ibaraki [18] is that the last two vertices vn−1 and vn of this
maximum adjacency ordering form a pendent pair, i.e., κ(X) ≥κ({vn}) holds for any X ⊊V with
|X ∩{vn−1, vn}| = 1. This allows us to contract the last two vertices into one without losing any
candidates of the minimum-cut value other than κ({vn}).
Stimulated by simple correctness proofs of the Nagamochi–Ibaraki algorithm given by Frank [3]
and by Stoer and Wagner [23], Queyranne [20] extended the concepts of maximum adjacency
ordering and pendent pair to the general setting of symmetric submodular functions, and presented
the first combinatorial algorithm for minimizing them.
Definition 1 (Maximum Adjacency Ordering). An ordering (v1, . . . , vn) of V is called a maximum
adjacency ordering (MA-ordering) with respect to a set function f on V if
f({vi}) −f(Vi−1 ∪{vi}) ≥f({vj}) −f(Vi−1 ∪{vj})
holds for every pair (i, j) such that 1 ≤i ≤j ≤n.
Definition 2 (Pendent Pair). A pair (u, v) of distinct elements of V is called a pendent pair of a
set function f on V if
f(X) ≥f({v})
holds for every subset X ⊊V with |X ∩{u, v}| = 1.
Theorem 1 (Queyranne [20]). Let f : 2V →R be a symmetric submodular function and (v1, . . . , vn)
be an MA-ordering of V with respect to f. Then (vn−1, vn) is a pendent pair.
Somewhat later, Nagamochi [17] designed another ordering-based algorithm for finding all the
extreme sets in a capacitated undirected graph. For each vertex y ∈V , the degree of y means the
sum of all the capacities of edges incident to y. The algorithm repeatedly selects a vertex that
attains the minimum degree and removes it from the graph. Let degW (y) denote the degree of y in
the graph obtained by removing W from G. Note that degW (y) can be expressed in terms of κ by
degW (y) = 1
2[κ({y}) −κ(W) + κ(W ∪{y})].
The two vertices u and v remained at the end of this procedure are shown to form a flat pair, i.e.,
any subset X ⊊V with |X ∩{u, v}| = 1 satisfies κ(X) ≥min{κ({x}) | x ∈X}. Then the algorithm
contracts u and v into one. Repeating this process O(n) times enables us to enumerate all the
extreme sets. Nagamochi [17] also extended this algorithm to the setting of symmetric submodular
functions as follows.
Definition 3 (Minimum Degree Ordering). An ordering (v1, . . . , vn) of V is called a minimum
degree ordering (MD-ordering) with respect to a set function f on V if
f(Vi−1 ∪{vi}) + f({vi}) ≤f(Vi−1 ∪{vj}) + f({vj})
holds for every pair (i, j) such that 1 ≤i ≤j ≤n.
Definition 4 (Flat Pair). A pair (u, v) of distinct elements of V is called a flat pair with respect
to a set function f on V if
f(X) ≥min
x∈X f({x})
holds for every subset X ⊊V with |X ∩{u, v}| = 1.
