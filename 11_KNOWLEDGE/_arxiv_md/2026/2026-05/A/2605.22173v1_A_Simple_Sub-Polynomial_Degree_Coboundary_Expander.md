Abstract
High dimensional expanders simultaneously satisfying spectral and combinatorial (coboundary)
expansion have recently played a major role in breakthroughs in PCP and coding theory, but
the only known construction of such complexes is extremely involved, requiring deep algebraic
number theory. In this work, we give an extremely simple combinatorial construction of a
sub-polynomial degree complex based on projections of the flags complex (subspace chains)
that is (i) a local spectral expander, (ii) a coboundary expander, and (iii) a swap coboundary
expander. As a corollary, we also give the first near-linear size combinatorial hypergraphs with
good agreement tests in the ‘1%’ regime, and a simple PCP construction with near-linear size.

Introduction
High dimensional expanders, sparse but robustly connected hypergraphs, have seen a surge of
interest and application in theoretical computer science, driving breakthrough progress in coding
theory [EKZ20, DDHR20, AJQ+20, JST21, PK22, FK22, DEL+22, KO22, GMWZ25], approximate
sampling [ALGV19, ALG+21, AJK+22, ALG24, LLG25], property testing [DK17, DD19, KM20,
GK23, DD24a, BM24, DDL24], hardness of approximation [DFHT21, HL22, BMVY24], and pseu-
dorandomness [HLM+25, DHIP26]. Unlike their widely-studied graph analogs, there are very few
known constructions of high dimensional expanders, and all known methods are algebraic. In this
work, we give a simple construction of sub-polynomial degree hypergraphs based on subspaces
that are both spectral and combinatorial high dimensional expanders, and a corresponding simple
near-linear size PCP construction.
We consider three core notions of expansion: local spectral expansion (see Definition 16),
a generalization of spectral expansion in graphs, coboundary expansion (see Definition 20), a
generalization of edge expansion, and swap-coboundary expansion, a key notion related to the
construction of PCPs measuring the coboundary expansion of higher level disjoint faces (see
Definition 25). Unlike the graph setting, these generalizations behave quite differently in high
dimensions, e.g., there are random two-dimensional simplicial complexes that are good local spectral
expanders but not good coboundary expanders with high probability [GW16]. Constructing sparse
hypergraphs satisfying both spectral and coboundary expansion has been a major challenge since
the notions were introduced [LM06, Gro10, KKL16], and has received renewed attention after it
was shown such hypergraphs admit low soundness ‘agreement tests’ [GK23, DD24a, BM24] and
efficient PCPs [BMVY25].
The first sparse hypergraphs satisfying all three notions of expansion were constructed recently
in [CL25, DDL24, BLM24], based on deep algebraic number theory. This was followed very recently
(and independently to our work) by [OS25], who gave a simpler algebraic construction based
on variants of the elementary coset complexes of [KO18]. In this work, we show that if one is
willing to sacrifice somewhat on sparsity, it is possible to give completely elementary combinatorial
constructions of local-spectral and (swap)-coboundary HDX of sub-polynomial degree. Following
[BMVY24], we show these give rise to a simpler PCP construction roughly matching the parameters
of the breakthrough algebraic PCP of [MR08] in the small constant soundness regime.
Our complexes are based upon projections of the flags complex, a well-known dense hypergraph
more typically studied as the local structure of [LSV05b]’s quotients of the affine building, and shown
to be a swap-coboundary expander in [DD24c]. The vertices of flags complex are the subspaces of

We briefly comment on the proof before moving on, which is divided into two main steps. First,
we need to argue that the projected flags complex (and all its links) have vanishing cohomology
(Lemma 32), meaning that every f : E →Γ with δf = 0 is a coboundary δg for some g : V →Γ.
This is a well-known fact for the full flags-complex, and can be derived for projections of the
flags complex via fairly standard tools in algebraic topology, e.g., from [Bjö80, Sur84]. We give a
simplified, self-contained proof via [Bjö80]’s theory of shellability.3
Second, we show that the central projections of the flags complex also satisfy “cocycle” expansion,
which states that if |δf| is small, then f must be close to a function in Ker(δ) (which due to
vanishing cohomology, is exactly the set of coboundaries). The proof relies on several techniques
from [EK16, DD24b] to reduce the analysis to simple local components of the complex. In particular,
we first use a local-to-global argument (Lemma 44) from [DD24b] to move to links of the complex,
and a ‘color restriction lemma’ (Lemma 45) to reduce to showing most projections of the links to
three colors are coboundary expanders. Finally, we use the cones method [KO21] (see Section 2.5)
to argue directly that most projections to three colors indeed have constant coboundary expansion.

Agreement testing:
An agreement test is a randomized property tester that, given an
ensemble of local functions {fs : s →Σ|s ∈F} on F ⊆2[n], tries to determine whether there is a
global function G : [n] →Σ such that fs = G|s for all s ∈F. Many recent works have examined
the case where the local functions are defined on some level of a high-dimensional expander. In
this work, we consider the standard two-query agreement test (the ‘V test’) where we pick two
functions fs, fs′ on sets of size ℓwhere |s ∩s′| =

Further Related Works
Simple High Dimensional Expanders.
Beyond the celebrated construction of Kaufman and
Oppenheim [KO18] and its variants [OP22, dPVB25], a number of recent works have considered
simplified constructions of local-spectral expanders. [LMY20, Gol21] construct bounded degree
local-spectral HDX via a product of expanders and the complete complex, but cannot achieve
strong enough expansion for most applications [DH24]. [Gol23] constructs local-spectral HDX of any
expansion from low-rank matrices, achieving roughly the same degree as our construction. This was
improved in [DLW25] via a recursive procedure to degree 2log(N)ε for any constant ε > 0. Neither
construction has vanishing cohomology, and therefore cannot be used in the construction of PCPs.
Comparison with [OS25].
In recent independent work, O’Donnell and Singer [OS25], building
on [KOW25], showed that [BMVY24]’s PCPs may be instantiated on a variant of the coset-complex
HDX construction of Kaufman and Oppenheim [KO18]. These complexes are remarkably simpler
than those originally used in [BMVY24], relying only elementary matrix groups, and achieve
parameters matching [BMVY24]’s construction while our flags-complex based PCP is closer to
the original construction of Moshkovitz-Raz [MR08]. Nevertheless, [OS25] still requires a fairly
substantial amount of algebraic manipulation, and while the complexes are undeniably simpler than
[CL25], they are certainly more involved than the completely elementary subspace-based construction
suggested in this work. Finally, as discussed our complexes have vanishing cohomology over all

Towards answering this question, we will work with the following basic restricted type of 2-query

Definition 62. (2-query agreement test) Let Σ be an arbitrary finite set which we will call
the alphabet. Given a family F of k element subsets of [n] and an ensemble of functions E =
{fs : s →Σ|s ∈F} from [n] to Σ which we will call local functions, a 2-query agreement test is a
distribution D over a pair of subsets s1, s2 ∈F, with the agreement of the ensemble being
agrD(E) =
P
s1,s2∼D [fs1|s1∩s2 = fs2|s1∩s2] .
Remark 63. In the literature, sometimes more general agreement tests are considered where the
distribution D is over ℓsubsets in D.
We note that for any property testing question we would like the test to be complete, i.e., we
would want an object having the property in consideration to pass the test. By design, agreement
tests (as described above) have completeness as one can clearly see restriction of a global function
to sets in F would pass any 2-query test of the above form.
We also want agreement tests to be sound, i.e., whenever the tests passes there is a global
function whose restrictions agree with most functions in the ensemble. There are two regimes of
soundness of agreement tests that are typically considered:
The 99% regime or the high acceptance regime. In this regime, we want to claim that there
is a global function G : [n] →Σ such that G|s = f|s for most s ∈F whenever fs|s∩s′ = fs′|s∩s′
for most s, s′ ∈F. More formally, we want to show
agrD({fs}s∈F) ⩾1 −ε

fraction of s, s′ ∈F so that fs|s∩s′ = fs′|s∩s′, we want to claim that there is a global function
G : [n] →Σ such that G|s ≈f|s for some fraction of s ∈F, say poly(ε). More formally, we

Notation.
For ν ∈[0, 1], let majν(σ1, . . . , σk) denote the threshold majority function on (σ1, . . . , σk).
If for some σ, we have σi = σ for at least ν fraction of indices i, then we take majη(σ1, . . . , σk) = σ;
otherwise majν(σ1, . . . , σk) = ⊥(signifying ‘invalid’). We take the threshold ν to be at least 0.5 and
it is typically close to 1 (e.g., ν = 0.99).
The routing problem of interest to us is the almost everywhere reliable transmission problem
from [DPPU88], where the goal is to design a sparse graph G = (V, E) that allows transference
of messages in a fault tolerant way. To elaborate, at the start of the routing, each vertex v has a
message from Σ and destination given by π(v) where π : V →V is an arbitrary permutation. The
goal is to design a T-round protocol to send the message in v to π(v). In each round, the protocol
works by sending symbols in Σ from each of the nodes to a subset of its neighbors in G. At the end
of the protocol, we’d like to ensure most v indeed transmit their message to π(v), even if a small
fraction of the vertices or edges behave maliciously. The parameters of interest in this problem are
as follows.

• Work Complexity: the work complexity of a protocol is the maximum computational
