Abstract
Banakh and Bazylevych introduced separation-axiom variants qi, for i = 1, 2, 2 1
2, of the
cardinal q, together with a cardinal adp lying between dp and ap. They asked whether adp
coincides with either of these two cardinals. We prove in ZFC that adp = dp. We define a dual
variant adp2 and show that adp2 = ap.
We further study the relation between ap and the weakened Q-space cardinals. We introduce
a tree analogue at of ap and prove q1 ≤at ≤q2 1
2 , hence ap ≤q2 1
2 . Assuming the Generalized
Continuum Hypothesis, we construct ccc forcing extensions with ap = ω1 < at = q2 1
2 = c, so
ap < at is consistent with ZFC.

Introduction
Cardinal characteristics of the continuum are cardinals between ℵ1, the first uncountable cardinal,
and c, the cardinality of the continuum. They measure combinatorial properties of the real numbers;
see [3] for background.
The symbol ω denotes the set of natural numbers, which includes 0 and is identified with the
first infinite ordinal.
The cardinal characteristics ap and dp, both related to the pseudointersection number p, were
studied by Brendle in [4]. We recall the relevant definitions.
• For a set S, [S]<ω denotes the collection of all finite subsets of S, and [S]ω denotes the
collection of all countably infinite subsets of S.
• Sets a, b ∈[ω]ℵ0 are orthogonal, written a ⊥b, if a ∩b is finite.
• Collections A, B ⊆[ω]ℵ0 are orthogonal, written A ⊥B, if a ⊥b for every a ∈A and b ∈B.
• If A, B ⊆[ω]ℵ0, then A is weakly separated from B if there exists X ⊆ω such that a ̸⊥X for
every a ∈A, and b ⊥X for every b ∈B. In this case, we say that X weakly separates (A, B).
• A ⊆[ω]ℵ0 is an almost disjoint family if a ⊥b for every two distinct a, b ∈A.

Our convention regarding the order of weak separation follows Banakh and Bazylevych [1]. It
is the reverse of Brendle’s convention for ordered pairs [4].
Since weak separation is not symmetric, the order of the two families matters in the following
definitions.
• dp is the least cardinal κ for which there exist A, B ⊆[ω]ℵ0 such that A ⊥B, |A ∪B| = κ,
and A is not weakly separated from B.
• ap is the least cardinal κ for which there exist A, B ⊆[ω]ℵ0 such that A ⊥B, |A ∪B| = κ,
A ∪B is an almost disjoint family, and A is not weakly separated from B.
It is immediate that dp ≤ap. This inequality can be strict: Brendle showed by forcing that
dp < ap is consistent [4]. Both cardinals are greater than or equal to p, and consistently strictly
greater than p [4]. Here p is the least cardinality of a centered family of infinite subsets of ω with
no pseudointersection; that is, every nonempty finite subfamily has infinite intersection, but there
is no single infinite set P such that P \ A is finite for every member A of the family.
If X is a topological space and Y ⊆X, we say that Y is a Gδ subset of X if there are open
subsets (Un)n∈ω of X such that Y = T
n<ω Un. Dually, Y is an Fσ subset of X if it is a countable
union of closed subsets of X. We say that X has a Gδ-diagonal if the set ∆X = {(x, x) : x ∈X} is
a Gδ as a subset of X2.
A Q-set is classically defined as an uncountable set of reals all of whose subsets are Gδ sets in
the subspace topology (equivalently, all of its subsets are Fσ sets in the subspace topology) [8, 5].
The existence of Q-sets is independent of the Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with the axiom of choice
(ZFC) [5, 8]. Indeed, if there is a Q-set, then it has at least 2ℵ1 subsets, all realized as traces of Gδ
subsets of the reals. Since there are only c many Gδ subsets of the reals, no Q-set can exist under
the Continuum Hypothesis (CH). On the other hand, every set of reals of cardinality less than ap
has all of its subsets Gδ in the subspace topology [4]; hence, if ℵ1 < ap = c, as happens for example
under Martin’s Axiom together with the negation of CH [3], then Q-sets exist.
Q-sets are also related to the separable case of the Normal Moore Space Conjecture: a non-
metrizable separable normal Moore space exists if and only if there is a Q-set [6].
The smallest cardinality of a non-Q-set is denoted by q [8, 4]. More generally, a Q-space is a
topological space in which every subset is a Gδ set. In this terminology, q is the least cardinality of
a metrizable separable non-Q-space, equivalently, of a regular second-countable non-Q-space.
These notions have been studied in more general settings. There are two natural directions
to pursue: weaken regularity to weaker separation axioms, or weaken second countability. In [1],
Banakh and Bazylevych followed the first direction and defined the cardinals q1, q2 and q2 1
2 by
considering second-countable non-Q-spaces which are, respectively, T1, Hausdorff and Urysohn.
Recall that a topological space is T1 if every singleton is closed; Hausdorff, or T2, if every two
distinct points have disjoint neighborhoods; and Urysohn, or T2 1
2 , if every two distinct points have
disjoint closed neighborhoods.
Banakh and Bazylevych also introduced a cardinal adp, which lies between dp and ap and is
useful in the study of Q-spaces.
Definition 1. adp is the least cardinal κ for which there exist A, B ⊆[ω]ℵ0 such that A ⊥B,
|A ∪B| = κ, A is an almost disjoint family, and A is not weakly separated from B.
They asked the following.
Problem 2 ([1, Problem 12]). Is adp = dp? Is adp = ap?

In Section 2, we answer the first part by proving adp = dp in ZFC. Together with the consistency
of dp < ap, this shows that adp = ap is not provable in ZFC.
The asymmetry of weak separation also suggests asking what happens if the almost disjointness
requirement in the definition of adp is imposed on B instead of on A.
Definition 3. adp2 is the least cardinal κ for which there exist A, B ⊆[ω]ℵ0 such that A ⊥B,
|A ∪B| = κ, B is an almost disjoint family, and A is not weakly separated from B.
Using the same technique, we show that adp2 = ap. Thus, in every model in which dp < ap,
this modified cardinal is distinct from dp.
Banakh and Bazylevych also asked:
Problem 4 ([1, Problem 11]). Is ap ≤q2?
This problem remains open, but we prove several related bounds. In Section 3, we introduce a
tree analogue of ap, denoted by at. This cardinal is obtained by replacing almost disjoint families
of subsets of ω with almost disjoint families of infinite subtrees of an ω-tree. It satisfies ap ≤at,
and we prove

In particular, this gives the ZFC bound

The proof of the Urysohn bound shows that the extra separation available in Urysohn spaces
can be encoded by almost disjoint subtrees. In Section 4, we isolate another form of this extra

Finally, in Section 5, we show that the tree cardinal at is not simply another presentation of

In particular, ap < at is consistent. The construction is based on Brendle’s model for ap < q and

On almost disjointness and Urysohn Q-spaces
As discussed in the introduction, the cardinal q is the least cardinality of a non-Q-set of reals. For
f, g ∈ωω, write f ≤∗g if f(n) ≤g(n) for all but finitely many n ∈ω. It is known that p ≤q ≤b,
where b is the bounding number, namely the least cardinality of a family in ωω that is not bounded
by any single function with respect to ≤∗[8].
In [1], Banakh and Bazylevych introduced the following separation-axiom variants of q (with
the classical invariant denoted by q0 in their notation).
Definition 8. The cardinal q1 is the least cardinal κ for which there exists a second-countable, T1,
non-Q-space of cardinality κ.
The cardinal q2 is the least cardinal κ for which there exists a second-countable, Hausdorff,
non-Q-space of cardinality κ.
The cardinal q2 1
2 is the least cardinal κ for which there exists a second-countable, Urysohn,
non-Q-space of cardinality κ.

Banakh and Bazylevych asked whether any of these inequalities between different versions of q can
be strict. They also proved that every submetrizable space, that is, every space admitting a coarser

from q must use non-submetrizable examples [1].

In this paper, an ω-tree is a rooted tree of height ω with finite levels, and subtrees are assumed
to be nonempty and downward closed. For m < ω, write

5.1
Intertwined families
The following notion, due to Brendle [4], is the small almost disjoint configuration that will be
preserved throughout the iteration.
Definition 18. A pair ⟨B, C⟩of disjoint subcollections of [ω]ω, each of cardinality ℵ1, is intertwined

such a family survives to the final extension, then ap = ω1: the family B ∪C is an almost disjoint
family of size ω1, and the defining implication for intertwinedness prevents C from being weakly
separated from B.

Concluding remarks
In this paper, we settled Question 12 of [1] by proving that adp = dp.
We also obtained the
analogous identity for the dual asymmetric version of this cardinal, adp2 = ap.
Moreover, we obtained a positive partial answer to Question 11 of [1] by proving ap ≤q2 1
2 . The
corresponding Hausdorff version remains open, namely whether ap ≤q2.
We also considered the intermediate Hausdorff invariant q2∆, defined by requiring a Gδ-diagonal,
and proved ap ≤q2∆.
The tree cardinal at introduced in Definition 10 gives a more precise form of this Urysohn bound.
We proved
q1 ≤at ≤q2 1
2 ,
and, since ap ≤at, the cardinal at is a common upper bound for both q1 and ap which is itself
bounded by q2 1
2 .
Finally, the forcing construction in Section 5 shows that at can consistently be strictly larger
than ap.
