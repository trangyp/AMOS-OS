A SUGAWARA–LEGENDRE MECHANISM
FOR THE HYPERELLIPTIC HEISENBERG ALGEBRA
FELIPE ALBINO DOS SANTOS
Abstract. We study the φ-Verma modules of the Heisenberg subalgebra Hm of
the universal central extension of sl2 ⊗Am, where Am is the coordinate ring of the
superelliptic curve um = P(t), and ask how the orthogonal polynomial families that
arise in the centre relations are controlled by the module theory of Hm. Our main
results are proved unconditionally for the hyperelliptic case m = 2, r = 1; corre-
sponding statements for m ≥3 are recorded as conjectures. In the hyperelliptic case
we prove three theorems. First, the canonical contravariant (Shapovalov) form on
M(φ) is diagonal in the polynomial basis { ˜Pn}n≥0 determined by the cocycle, with
Legendre squared norms hn = 2/(2n+1). Second, M(φ) is irreducible if and only if
φ is p-admissible, and this is equivalent to non-degeneracy of the Shapovalov form.
Third, there is an explicit intertwiner Φ: M(φ) →C[x] which sends the free-boson
Sugawara zero mode Ω= −L0(L0 +Id) ∈^
U(Hm) to the classical Legendre differen-
tial operator L = (1 −x2)∂2
x −2x∂x, the level-n image of the highest-weight vector
to the Legendre polynomial Pn(x), and the Casimir tower {Ωr}r≥1 to {Lr}r≥1. As
a companion result, M(φ) is canonically isomorphic to a bosonic Fock space with
the Shapovalov form identified with the Fock inner product.

1. Introduction
Background. The two strands of mathematics joined in this paper are the classical
theory of orthogonal polynomials and the representation theory of infinite-dimensional
Lie algebras attached to algebraic curves. The former goes back to the late eighteenth
century, when Legendre introduced what are now called the Legendre polynomials
in his study of gravitational potentials. The classical families — Legendre, Hermite,
Laguerre, Jacobi — and their orthogonality properties were codified in the nineteenth
century and brought into modern form in Szegő’s monograph [5]; see also [20] for the
analytic background. Each classical family is characterised by a three-term recurrence
and a second-order differential equation, two conditions which are equivalent under
the correspondence [5, §3.2].
The latter strand begins with Krichever and Novikov, who introduced algebras of
meromorphic vector fields on a Riemann surface — with poles confined to a finite
set of marked points — as higher-genus analogues of the Witt and Virasoro algebras
[18].
The general theory of Krichever–Novikov type algebras and their universal
central extensions has been developed systematically since; we refer to [8, 9] for
a comprehensive account.
The representation-theoretic side draws on the Verma-
module formalism for affine Lie algebras [6, 11] and the broader theory of contravariant
forms initiated by Shapovalov [7]; on the conformal side it is informed by the work
of Feigin and Fuchs [15] on Virasoro modules.
In a different direction, extended
affine and map-algebra modules have been the subject of an active recent literature
[14, 10, 19].
The two strands meet in the superelliptic setting. For the curve um = P(t), explicit
cocycle formulas for the universal central extension of sl2 ⊗Am were obtained in [3],
where it was observed empirically that the centre relations realise families of orthog-
onal polynomials that extend the classical list — with the Legendre family appearing
in the hyperelliptic case and new families in the higher branch degrees. Irreducibility
of the relevant φ-Verma modules in the hyperelliptic case was established in [1]. Left
open in both works was the representation-theoretic mechanism by which the analytic
phenomena — the orthogonality of the polynomial basis, the second-order differential
equation governing it, and (in special cases) completeness — arise from the module
theory of the underlying Heisenberg subalgebra Hm. The present paper supplies that
mechanism in the hyperelliptic case and conjectures its extension.
Motivation. In an earlier strand of the author’s work on universal central extensions
of Krichever–Novikov-type algebras attached to superelliptic curves um = P(x) [3, 1],
orthogonal polynomial families were observed to arise as a structural feature of the
centre. The centre of the universal central extension of sl2 ⊗Am is finite-dimensional,
and the generators satisfy three-term recurrences — the defining relation of orthogonal
polynomial systems. The dimension of the centre grows with the genus and the branch
degree, and for m ≥3 the corresponding polynomial families lie beyond the classical
list. In that earlier work, the analytic phenomena — orthogonality, the second-order
differential equation, and (in special cases) completeness of the polynomial basis —
were established by explicit computation [3, Thm. 3.4 and §4], but their relation to
the module theory of the superelliptic Heisenberg algebra Hm was left open.

The present paper addresses this gap in the hyperelliptic case (m = 2, r = 1), and
partially in the general superelliptic case. We show that:
(1) The canonical Shapovalov form on the φ-Verma module diagonalises in the
polynomial basis { ˜Pn}, identifying orthogonality as a property of the con-
travariant form on M(φ) (general m, r = 1).
(2) In the hyperelliptic case, the Legendre differential operator arises as the image,

(3) In the hyperelliptic case, irreducibility of the module and completeness of the

controlled by a single combinatorial criterion (p-admissibility) on the weight
functional. For m ≥3 the corresponding statement is conjectured (Conjec-

7. Outlook
7.1. Connection to P1 and P3. The current paper extends the representation-
theoretic framework begun by two prior papers:
[1] (P1) established the existence of φ-Verma modules, proved their irreducibility
criterion (p-admissibility), and computed explicit bases. The present work (P4) adds
the Shapovalov form on these modules, derives the orthogonality relation from the
form’s properties, and constructs the Casimir element that encodes the differential
operators. In this sense, P4 augments P1 with the contravariant form on the modules
studied there, and identifies the operator on M(φ) whose intertwining image on C[x]
is the Legendre operator.
A separate line of work studies an explicit dictionary between the cocycle data
in the UCE and families of orthogonal polynomials, in which the centre relations
match three-term recurrences and the generating function satisfies Sturm–Liouville
ODEs; this dictionary was discovered empirically and verified computationally. The
present paper provides a representation-theoretic account of part of that picture:
the polynomial vectors are special weight vectors in the Verma module, and their
orthogonality follows from the contravariant form, so the matching of three-term
recurrences with the centre relations is realised as a property of the module M(φ).
