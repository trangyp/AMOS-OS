Abstract. Peter Lappan in [9] proved that for each n ∈N = {1, 2, 3, . . . }, let f1,n, f2,n and
f3,n be three continuous functions on D := {z ∈C : |z| < 1} such that for each j = 1, 2, 3,
the sequence (fj,n) converges locally uniformly to a function fj on D. Suppose that the three
functions f1, f2, and f3 avoid each other on D. Let F = (gn) be a sequence of meromorphic
functions in D with the property that for each n, the four functions gn, f1,n, f2,n, and f3,n
avoid each other, then F is normal. We present here an analogue of this result in the setting of
quasiregular mappings. We also obtain analogues of a few other results by Peter Lappan in [9]
to quasiregular setting in the Euclidean space Rn for normal families and normal quasiregular
mappings.
1. Introduction
The genesis of the concept of a normal family is traceable to the Bolzano - Weierstrass
property, which guarantees that every infinite bounded set of points possesses a limit point in
the Euclidean space. Its functional analogue was progressively developed at the turn of the
twentieth century by Ascoli, Arzela and Vitali, who demonstrated that compactness for families
of continuous functions is equivalent to equicontinuity together with uniform boundedness.
Within this framework, Montel in 1907 [13] inaugurated the systematic study of normal families
of analytic functions.
The theory of normal family in planar domain is already very rich,
interested readers may refer to the monograph by Schiff [19] for detailed study.
Quasiregular mappings provide a natural framework for extending geometric function theory
from the complex plane to the Euclidean space Rn, with n ≥3. In higher dimensions, Liou-
ville’s Theorem asserts that the only conformal mappings are M¨obius transformations (see,
[6, 16]). Consequently, the conformal category is too restrictive and it becomes necessary to
allow mappings with controlled distortion in order to obtain a richer theory. Quasiregular
mappings supply this generalization and many classical results from complex analysis have
higher-dimensional analogues in this setting, for a comprehensive introduction to the subject,
we refer the readers to Rickman’s monograph [18] and book of Vuorinen [20].
Recently, there has been significant work in the study of quasiregular mappings from the
perspective of normal family theory (see [5, 2, 11, 4]). The present paper is also motivated
by developments in this direction. The avoidance criteria are not new in the theory of normal
families, one of the classical results states if F is a family of meromorphic functions such that
each function f ∈F omits the same three distinct values say α, β, and γ in C, then F is normal
family, formally known as Montel’s Theorem. It is also valid if these values are replaced by
continuous functions, provided they mutually avoid each other.

for each point z in their common domain. The functions f and g avoid each other uniformly

4. Rouche’s-type Theorem for quasiregular mappings
The following result proved by Lappan in [9] was much similar to Rouche’s Theorem, here
only one of the function was assumed to be analytic, unlike the Rouche’s Theorem where both
the functions are analytic.
Result 4.1. [9, Theorem 1] Let Dr := {z ∈C : |z| < r} be the disc of radius r > 0, f be an
analytic function and g be a continuous and bounded function on the closure of disc Dr such
that |f(z)| > |g(z)| for |z| = r. If there exits z0 ∈Dr such that f(z0) = 0, then f and g do not
avoid each other in Dr, i.e. there exists z′ in Dr such that f(z′) −g(z′) = 0.
We now give an analogue of the Result 4.1 to quasiregular setting, by taking quasiregular
mapping in place of analytic function and using the techniques from algebraic topology. We
are going to use this analogue of Rouche’s - type theorem in Section 5.

f(x0) = 0, then f and g do not avoid each other in Br, i.e. there exists some point x′ in Br
such that f(x′) −g(x′) = 0.
Proof. Let us assume that there exits x0 ∈Br such that f(x0) = 0.

h(x, t) = f(x) −tg(x), where t ∈I = [0, 1] and x ∈Br.

5. Avoidance criterion for normal families
Lappan proved the following similar result as that of the well-known rescaling lemma by
Zalcmann [22, §3(The main lemma)], to prove some of the generalised consequences of the
Result 1.1.
Result 5.1. [9, Lemma 1] Suppose that (fn) is a sequence of continuous functions on the unit
disc D ⊂C such that, (fn) converges locally uniformly on D to a function f. Suppose that (zn)
is a sequence in D converging to a point z0 ∈D and there is a sequence of positive reals say
(ρn) converging to zero such that the sequence of function (Gn(t) = gn(zn + ρnξ)) converges
locally uniformly on C to a non-constant meromorphic functions g : C →S2 and if, for each
n, fn and gn avoid each on D then g omits the value f(z0).
This raises the following question: whether a version of Result 5.1 remains valid for quasireg-
ular mappings or not? Using our analogue of Rouche’s-type Theorem 4.2, we prove the Propo-
sition 5.2, which is the analogue of Result 5.1, for quasiregular mappings in higher dimensions,
obtained by replacing sequence of analytic function by quasiregular mappings and it is similar
to that of Miniowitz version of Zalcmann re-scaling result (see Lemma 3.2).
Proposition 5.2. Let (fn) be a sequence of continuous functions on domain D ⊂Rn such
that, (fn) converges locally uniformly on D to a function f. Suppose that (xn) is a sequence
in D converging to a point x0 ∈D and there is a sequence of positive reals say (ρn) converging
to zero such that the sequence of function (Gn(t) = gn(xn + ρnξ)) converges locally uniformly
on Rn to a non-constant quasimeromorphic mapping g : Rn →Sn and if, for each n, fn and
gn avoid each on D then g omits the value f(x0).
Proof. We have been given a sequence (xn) in D converging to a point x0 ∈D and a sequence

The name normal function was given later by Lehto and Virtanen [10] in 1957, where they
introduced the general notion of normal meromorphic functions as follows: A meromorphic
function f in a hyperbolic domain D ⊂C is said to be normal if the family of its compositions
with automorphisms of D, namely
{f ◦ϕ : ϕ ∈Aut(D)},
forms a normal family in D with respect to the spherical metric.
These functions exhibit several features analogous to those of bounded analytic functions,
with invariance under M¨obius transformations playing a central role in their analysis. For a
more comprehensive study of normal meromorphic functions, we refer the reader to [15].
Motivated by the definition in the planar domain, Fletcher and Nicks in [5], defined the
following analogous definition of normal quasiregular mappings.
Definition 6.1. [5, Definition 4.1] For n ≥2, suppose X ⊂Sn is a domain equipped with a
metric dX induced by a conformal metric. Suppose that G is a family of transitive, conformal,
orientation-preserving and surjective self isometries of X.
(i) A quasiregular mapping f : X →Sn is said to be normal if the associated family
F = {f ◦A : A ∈G} ⊂QK(X, Sn)
forms a normal family.
(ii) A quasiregular mapping f : X →Rn is called normal if there exists a point x0 ∈X
such that the family
F = {f(A(x)) −f(A(x0)) : A ∈G} ⊂QK(X, Rn)
is normal.
Lappan in [9], proved the following version of Result 1.1 by Bargmann et al. for normal
functions in the planar domain.
Result 6.2. [9, Theorem 3] Suppose that f1, f2, and f3 be three continuous functions on a unit
disc D avoiding each other uniformly on D, such that the family Fj = {fj ◦φ : φ ∈Aut(D)} is
normal in D for each j = 1, 2 and 3. Let g be a meromorphic function in D with the property
that the four functions g, f1, f2, and f3 avoid each other on D, then g is a normal function.
Now one can ask a natural question: whether an analogue of Result 6.2 can be given in
quasiregular setting or not? Next, we are going to prove the Theorem 6.3 and Theorem 6.4,
which gives an assertive answer to this question that one can obtain the analogue of Result 1.1
for normal quasiregular mapping. First, we will prove the analogue for the quasiregular map-
pings from X into Sn as follows.
