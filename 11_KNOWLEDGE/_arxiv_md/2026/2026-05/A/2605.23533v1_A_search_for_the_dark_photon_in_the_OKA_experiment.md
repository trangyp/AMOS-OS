Abstract A search for the massless dark photon in the decay
K+
→
π+π0γ is performed with the OKA detector exposed to
17.7 GeV/c RF separated secondary beam of the U70 Proton Syn-
chrotron. A high-statistics data sample of the K+ decays is used for
a missing mass search of the stable massless invisible dark photon (γ) in
the final state. In the absence of a statistically valuable signal the upper
limit on the branching ratio of the decay Br < 2.0 × 10−6 is obtained
with a 90% confidence level based on a realistic matrix element of the
process. Currently, it is the best upper limit for this hypothetical decay
channel.

Introduction
A motivation for considering dark matter (DM) originally arose from astronomical observations
that required an additional invisible mass to explain abnormally high velocities of peripheral
stars and galaxies in large stellar formations [1]. Although, by initial assumption, DM does

not interact with an ordinary matter except through the gravitational interaction, there still
remains a possibility of a super-weak interaction between DM and SM, see recent review [2].
One promising approach to detect DM candidates is to hunt for the dark photons, assuming that
they have a small mass or are massless. Thus, an additional (dark) Abelian gauge symmetry
group U(1)D is introduced [3]. Depending on whether the U(1)D symmetry is broken or not, the
associated gauge boson – the dark photon (DP) – would be massive or strictly massless. It was
noted [4], that the main search path for dark photons has been that of massive DP [5, 6, 7], while
a strictly massless dark photon received less attention. Given that the viable parameter space
of the massive dark photon continues to shrink with accumulating null outcomes of its searches,
it is of great interest to turn to the alternative possibility [8]. In that case there is no mixing of
massless DP (often marked as γ) with electromagnetic photon γ. Hence, the interaction of γ,
with SM particles could only go through loops of massive DM particles, which would be the only
responsible candidates for the interaction with SM particles in that case. One of the scenarios
in which γ could be produced with potentially observable rates involves flavor-changing-neutral-
current (FCNC) decays of heavy flavors into a massless dark photon [9], which is considered
in details in [4, 8] with particular emphasis to kaon decays. Namely, the K+ →π+π0γ
was
proposed as a sensitive probe for massless dark photons with an estimate of branching fraction
≲1.6 × 10−7 assuming FCNC transition s →dγ
in a simplified model of the dark sector for
rather conservative choice of parameters, where bounds from astrophysics and K0 −K0 mixing
were taken into account [4].
The proposed FCNC approach for γ production was further explored for kaon and hyperon
decays in [8], where, model-independent constraints on the decays were drawn, assuming that
new physics connected with γ may be hiding in error bars of currently available branchings of
hyperon decays quoted in PDG [10]. As a result a softer limit of Br(K−→π−π0γ) < 2.4×10−6
was established, which is already within the achievable sensitivity of OKA data, cf. our recent
result on ALP [11]. In this article we perform a search for the massless dark photon. We use
eq. (14) in [8] in which an explicit matrix element for K−→π−π0γ
is given. Using that we

The OKA detector
The OKA is a fixed target experiment dedicated to investigation of kaon decays. It is located
at NRC ”Kurchatov Institute”-IHEP in Protvino (Russia).
A secondary hadron beam with
enhanced fraction of kaons is provided by the U-70 Proton Synchrotron using the beam line
equipped with two CERN [14] RF cavities operated in Panofsky scheme [15]. The beam has
about 12.5% of K+ with momentum of 17.7 GeV/c.
The OKA setup uses two magnetic spectrometers placed along the beam line with an 11 m
long Decay Volume (DV) in-between, see Fig. 1. The DV is filled with helium and contains 11
rings of guard system (GS) made of Lead-Scintillator sandwiches at its inner surface. The guard
system is complemented by an electromagnetic calorimeter BGD with a wide central opening.

Momenta measurement of the beam and of charged decay products is done with a help of
M1 and wide aperture 200×140 cm2 M2 magnets. The magnets are surrounded by a set of beam
proportional chambers BPC and a set of large apperture proportional chambers PC, straw tubes
ST and drift tubes DT. A matrix hodoscope HODO is used to improve the time resolution and
to link x–y projections of a track.
At the end of the setup there are the main electromagnetic calorimeter GAMS(ECAL), made
of ∼2300 18X0 lead glass blocks, the hadron calorimeter, GDA(HCAL), made of 100 5λ iron-
scintillator sandwiches and a muon counter µC, consisting of four partially overlapping 1×1m2
scintillator plates at the end of the setup. The data acquisition system of the OKA setup [16]
operates at ∼25 kHz event rate with the mean event size of ∼4 kByte. More details on the
OKA setup are available in our previous publications [17, 18].
To calculate the detection efficiency and the detector response, the Monte Carlo (MC) simu-
lation is performed using a software package based on the Geant-3.21 library [19], which includes
a realistic description of the setup. The trigger used in the experiment is also included in the
simulation. The MC events are passed through the full OKA reconstruction procedure, same as
for the experimental data. To estimate the background, samples of MC events are used for five
main decay channels of charged kaon with π0 in the final state: (K+→π+π0, π+π0γ, π+π0π0,
π0µ+ν, and π0e+ν). They are mixed according to their branching fractions. Monte Carlo events
use weights proportional to the square of the corresponding matrix elements according to the
PDG [10]. The generated MC statistics is ∼8 times larger than the data sample recorded in
the experiment.
A dedicated MC is produced for the process of interest K+ →π+π0γ. It uses realistic matrix
element from [8], see section 1.
3
The data set and the event selection
A following trigger: TrGAMS = S1×S2×S3×S4×ˇC1×ˇC2×Sbk ×(EGAMS > 2.5 GeV) is used to collect
data for the present analysis. The coincidence of four scintillation counters (S1, S2, S3, and S4)
selects a beam particle. The final selection of charged kaons is done with a combination of two
threshold Cherenkov counters (ˇC1 sees pions, ˇC2 sees pions and kaons). An anti-coincidence with
two scintillation counters (Sbk1 or Sbk2) located on the beam axis behind the M2 magnet is used
to avoid recording the events with undecayed beam particles. The cut on the energy deposition
in the GAMS e.m. calorimeter suppresses the dominating K+ →µ+ν decay and enhances decays
with photons and electrons.

Two sequential sets of data with a beam momentum of 17.7 GeV/c recorded by the OKA
collaboration in 2012 and 2013 (run14 and run15) are used in the analysis to search for the
massless dark photon. The total number of ∼2.65 × 109 events with TrGAMS are logged. One of
the tasks during data taking in run14 was a study of the K+Cu→K+π0Cu reaction [20], for
that a thin copper target was installed inside the DV near its exit window for the half time of
the run. A dedicated Monte-Carlo simulation (MC) for that period was performed.
As the first step of the reconstruction, events with kaon decay with a single charged track in
the final state are selected, with the decay vertex inside the DV and with reconstructed momenta
in both magnetic spectrometers. About 8 × 108 events are selected.
3.1
Event selection
The event selection for the decay K+ →π+π0γ starts from the reconstruction of π+π0 final
state. A single beam track and a single secondary track with a decay angle > 4 mrad and
with a vertex matching (distance of closest approach) below 1.25 cm is required. A moderate
chi-square cut for the charged track quality is applied.
To clean the tracking sample, it is
required that no extra track segments behind the M2 magnet are found. The vertex position is
required to be inside the DV within two sigma margins from its entrance and exit windows, and
outside the target position for the run periods in which the target was used. The beam particle
momentum is required within a range of 17.0 < pbeam < 18.6 GeV/c. The number of showers
in the GAMS calorimeter not associated with the track must be equal to 2. For these events,
the π0 identification is done by invariant mass selection: |mγγ −mπ0| < 15 MeV/c2 for both
gammas with Eγ > 0.3 GeV. After those selections the statistics is dominated by K+ →π+π0
decay. We obtain ∼3 × 107 K+ →π+π0 events (16.9 × 106 and 11.4 × 106 in run14 and run15
correspondingly). At this stage, the normalization of MC statistics to the data is performed.
4
Signal selection, background analysis
First, a cut Emis=(EK+ −Eπ+ −Eπ0)>2.7 GeV on the missing energy is applied in order to
disentangle the K+ →π+π0γ signal from its main background K+ →π+π0.
Figure 2 demonstrates MC Dalitz plots for the signal and for the main background channels.
To accept the signal of K+ →π+π0γ and sufficiently suppress the background we select an area
inside the dashed region as seen in Fig. 2. Despite the K+ →π+π0π0 looks fully accepted, it does
not affect signal region in the (mmis)2 plot since it is well separated from it as it is seen in Fig. 3.
This is not the case for K+ →µ+νπ0 and K+ →e+νπ0 which are considerably populating
the region of the signal with (mmis)2 ≈0. The area selected for the signal strongly minimizes
possible inputs from K+ →π+π0 and K+ →π+π0γ due to drastically different matrix elements.
To further suppress K+ →µ+(e+)νπ0 we use the π+/µ+/e+ separation by means of the
muon veto and the calorimetry. The muon veto requires absence of a signal from the muon
counter crossed by the extrapolated track. The requirements for the hadron shower in GAMS
include the track matching with a GAMS cluster within ∆R < 8 cm; the E/p < 0.65 cut to
avoid showers from e+, while preserving the "early" hadron (π+) showers for which an "anti"
cut on the cluster fit quality with an electromagnetic shower χ2
em > 0.4 is required, which is also
suited to reject muon clusters. The resolution of hadronic calorimeter HCAL in the energy range

5.1
Estimates of systematic error
The systematic error in εγ is about 5%, while the systematic error of the (εKπ2/NKπ2) ratio is
estimated as 3.5%, the corresponding statistical errors are negligible.
A systematic error of 11% is derived from the difference between the K+ →π+π0 and
K+ →π+π0π0 (with lost π0, which is detected by the missing mass spectrum) normalization
alternatives. For the K+ →π+π0π0 case, the veto (GS) cut is removed to avoid suppression of
the second (escaping) π0, while (in contrast to the K+ →π+π0) the Emis cut is kept unchanged.
To account for a possible systematic error due to the selection criteria, we repeated a hundred
standard analyses described earlier in this paper, where a set of the main cuts was randomly
chosen, within a window of ±σ (where σ is either the resolution of a cut variable, estimated
from the MC, or ∼15% for threshold cuts) around the original values. The distribution of
the resulting UL is shown in Fig. 5.
The most probable value upper limit is considered as
the final result, while the systematic error of the upper limit is estimated by rejecting the
tails of 16% from each side of the distribution (keeping inside 68% of the distribution), which
corresponds to UL on Br(K+→π+π0γ) < 2.0+0.36
−0.19 × 10−6. This leads to UL at the 90% CL on
Br(K+→π+π0γ) < 2.0+0.44
−0.32(syst.) × 10−6, if other systematic errors are added in quadrature.
