Abstract
Given a positive integer k and graph G, the Zk-Ramsey number R(G, Zk) is the least N
(if it exists) such that every coloring f : E(KN) →Zk contains a copy G′ of G such that
P
e∈E(G′) f(e) = 0. Motivated by a question of Caro and Mifsud, we study the Zk-Ramsey
number of graphs with a sufficiently large 2-packing, i.e. a set of vertices S ⊆V (G) such that
N[u] ∩N[v] = ∅for all distinct u, v ∈S. In particular, we prove that R(G, Zp) ≤n + 6p −9 for
all n-vertex graphs G and all primes p such that p divides e(G), the minimum degree of G is at
least 1, and there exists a 2-packing of G with size p−1. This upper bound improves depending
on vertex degrees in the 2-packing, with equality in certain cases. The result also implies an
upper bound of the form R(G, Zp) ≤n+C for n-vertex graphs G of bounded maximum degree.

Introduction
The Erd˝os-Ginzburg-Ziv theorem states that every sequence of 2k −1 integers contains a subse-
quence of k integers whose sum is congruent to 0 modulo k. Bialostocki and Dierker [2] initiated
the study of a graph-theoretic analogue of the theorem, establishing some of the first results in zero-
sum Ramsey theory. While classical Ramsey theory involves coloring combinatorial structures and
investigating the appearance of monochromatic substructures, zero-sum Ramsey theory involves
interchanging colors with elements from a group (often Zk) and instead focusing on zero-sum sub-
structures.
More precisely, given a graph G and positive integer k, the Ramsey number R(G, k) is the least
N for which every k-coloring f : E(KN) →{1, . . . , k} contains a monochromatic copy of G; its
existence is guaranteed by Ramsey’s Theorem [9]. If Γ is a finite abelian group, then the zero-sum
Ramsey number R(G, Γ) is the least N (if it exists) for which every Γ-coloring f : E(KN) →Γ
contains a zero-sum copy of G, i.e. a copy G′ of G such that P
e∈E(G′) f(e) = 0Γ. The exponent of
any finite group Γ, denoted exp(Γ), is the smallest positive integer r such that rg = 0 for all g ∈Γ.
Note that R(G, Γ) exists if and only if exp(Γ) divides e(G). If the divisibility condition holds, then
monochromatic copies of G are zero-sum, and therefore R(G, Γ) ≤R (G, |Γ|). If the divisibility
condition does not hold, then for all positive integers N, there exists a constant Γ-coloring of KN
that fails to contain a zero-sum copy of G. In our work, we restrict our focus to the Zk-Ramsey
number R(G, Zk), which exists if and only if exp(Zk) = k divides e(G).
Few exact values for the Zk-Ramsey number are known, even for small values of k. For example,
the Z2-Ramsey numbers were completely determined in all cases by Caro [3], but there are still

that R(G, Zp) ≤n+(3+3d)p for all d-degenerate n-vertex graphs G and all primes p dividing e(G)
