1. Introduction
The celebrated differential and discrete Painlev´e equations are among the central objects studied in modern
mathematics and mathematical physics. Their importance stems from their rich structures—they possess
symmetries generating affine Weyl groups, are related to special functions and orthogonal polynomials, can
be interpreted as moduli spaces of flat connections, character varieties and Poisson geometry on which are
one of the interests in modern mathematics—as well as their deep connections with integrable systems such
as the Toda chains, the Kadomtsev–Petviashvili equation, Calogero systems, and many others. Thanks to
the works of K. Okamoto and H. Sakai, these equations also admit a beautiful geometric interpretation.
Moreover, the Painlev´e equations possess natural higher-order extensions known as the Painlev´e hierarchies.
We refer the reader to the excellent survey on this topic given in [CM20].
In recent years, matrix and non-commutative generalizations of integrable systems have attracted growing
interest. This is motivated both by applications and by the intrinsic mathematical richness of evolution
equations with non-commuting dependent variables. The Painlev´e equations, together with their discrete
versions, provide a particularly compelling example of this phenomenon. For instance, the first Painlev´e
equation admits the following matrix analogue [BS98]:

We set up the non-commutative framework as follows. Let R be an associative unital division ring over a
field F with char(F) = 0. This may be viewed as a non-commutative generalization of the field of rational
functions. We identify the unit of F with that of R, and note that the elements of R need not commute. We
refer to elements of R informally as “functions”.
To work with non-commutative analogues of ordinary differential and discrete equations, we introduce
two operations on R: a derivation ∂and a translation T. A derivation of R is an F-linear map ∂: R →R
satisfying the Leibniz rule, and for any c ∈F we have ∂(c) = 0. Sometimes we use ∂≡′ if the subindex
does not need to be specified additionally. Let the function αn ∈R be an element at site n ∈Z, in other
words it is a non-commutative function depending on the index n. On the lattice Z we consider a translation
operator T, which is an F-linear map T : R →R such that T (αn) = αn+1 and T (c) = c for any constant c.
For the simplicity, we take F = C.

5. Discussions
As demonstrated by the explicit examples, our d-PInc
m hierarchy appears to be related to the PInc
m hierarchy.
To make this connection precise, the system d-PInc
m should be reformulated in terms of a discrete version of
the recursion operator, analogous to (38). Such an operator could, in principle, be derived from the discrete
Korteweg-de Vries hierarchy. We note that this problem remains open even in the commutative setting.
Another non-commutative version of the d-PI equation, given by (40), was obtained in [Bob24] through a
composition of B¨acklund transformations associated with the non-commutative fourth Painlev´e equation.
Since Painlev´e IV admits a matrix hierarchy [GP21] together with corresponding B¨acklund transformations,
one may expect a different hierarchy of the first discrete Painlev´e equation—with (40) as its first member—to
arise from an appropriate composition of the transformations constructed in [GP21].
It is also natural to investigate analogous hierarchies for other non-commutative discrete Painlev´e equa-
tions, for example those introduced in [Dol13], [Bob24], [Bob25], as well as to study their connection with
isomonodromic representations, orthogonal polynomials, and integrable systems.
