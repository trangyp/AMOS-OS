A RANDOM VERSION OF THE BURR–ERD˝OS–SPENCER THEOREM
ANDREA FRESCHI, RYAN R. MARTIN, AND ANDREW TREGLOWN
Abstract. A well-known result of Burr, Erd˝os and Spencer [Transactions of the American Math-
ematical Society, 1975] determines the 2-colour Ramsey number for any sufficiently large collection
of vertex-disjoint copies of a fixed graph H without isolated vertices. In this short note we prove
a random version of this result, thereby generalising the random Ramsey theorem of R¨odl and
Ruci´nski [Journal of the American Mathematical Society, 1995].
1. Introduction
1.1. Ramsey numbers of multiple copies of a graph. One of the most central topics in
combinatorics is Ramsey theory: the study of partitions of mathematical objects, and in particular,
what structures one can guarantee in such partitions. Ramsey’s foundational work [23] yields the
following result for graphs: given any r ∈N and any fixed graph H, there is n ∈N such that every
r-edge-colouring of the complete graph Kn contains a monochromatic copy of H. We write Rr(H)
to denote the smallest n for which the above holds, and set R(H) := R2(H).
Since the 1930s, there has been significant interest in determining the values of such Ramsey
numbers Rr(H).
The most famous subcase of this problem is the case when r = 2 and H is
a complete graph. The value of R(Kk) is only known exactly when k ≤4 and thus there has
been a focus on general upper and lower bounds for R(Kk). In particular, a famous probabilistic
argument of Erd˝os [15] yields the bound R(Kk) ≥2k/2, whilst Erd˝os and Szekeres [16] proved that
R(Kk) ≤4k. After a number of improvements to this latter upper bound [11, 27, 30], Campos,
Griffiths, Morris and Sahasrabudhe [9] recently gave the first exponential improvement: there is
an ε > 0 such that R(Kk) ≤(4 −ε)k; see also [3] for a recent general upper bound on Rr(Kk).
The method of [9] was then optimised by Gupta, Ndiaye, Norin and Wei [19] who proved that
R(Kk) ≤3.8k.
In general, there are relatively few graphs H for which the exact value of Rr(H) is known. One
class of graphs where we do have a better understanding of (two-colour) Ramsey numbers are so-
called tilings. For a fixed graph H, an H-tiling is a collection of vertex-disjoint copies of H. For
m ∈N, we write mH to denote an H-tiling consisting of m copies of H. In the late 1960s, Erd˝os [14,
Problem 9] raised the question of determining R(mKk) for k ≥3. The following well-known result
of Burr, Erd˝os and Spencer [8] answers this question for sufficiently large H-tilings more generally.

1.2. A generalisation of the random Ramsey theorem. The aim of this note is to provide a
random analogue of Theorem 1.1.
The random graph Gn,p has vertex set [n] := {1, . . . , n} where each possible edge is present with
probability p, independently of all other edges. The celebrated random Rado theorem of R¨odl and
Ruci´nski [24, 25, 26] essentially determines the values of p for which Gn,p is asymptotically almost
surely (a.a.s.) (H, r)-Ramsey. To state their result formally we require a few definitions. Given a
graph H, we say that G is (H, r)-Ramsey if every r-edge-colouring of G yields a monochromatic
copy of H in G. Given a graph H, set d2(H) := 0 if e(H) = 0; d2(H) := 1/2 when H is precisely an
edge and define d2(H) := (e(H) −1)/(|H| −2) otherwise. Then define m2(H) := maxH′⊆H d2(H′)
to be the 2-density of H.

We write V (G) and E(G) for the vertex and edge sets of a graph G, respectively, and define

An equipartition of a set V is a partition of V into classes whose sizes differ by at most 1.
Constants in hierarchies are chosen from right to left. For example, if we claim that a result
holds whenever 0 < a ≪b ≪c ≤1, then there are non-decreasing functions f : (0, 1] →(0, 1] and
g : (0, 1] →(0, 1] such that the result holds for all 0 < a, b, c ≤1 with b ≤f(c) and a ≤g(b). Note
that a ≪b implies that we may assume in the proof that, e.g., a < b or a < b2.

2.1. Coloured version of the sparse regularity lemma. In the proof of Theorem 1.3 we apply

Let p, ε > 0. Given two disjoint sets U, V of vertices in a graph G, the density dp(U, V ) of edges
between U and V with respect to p is
dp(U, V ) := e(G[U, V ])

A bipartite graph with vertex classes U, V is (ε, p)-regular if, for every U′ ⊆U and V ′ ⊆V with
|U′| ≥ε|U| and |V ′| ≥ε|V | we have that |dp(U′, V ′) −dp(U, V )| ≤ε. In this case we call (U, V )
an (ε, p)-regular pair. The following well-known fact follows immediately from the definition of an
(ε, p)-regular pair.
Fact 2.1. Let 0 < ε < α and ε′ := max{ε/α, 2ε}. Suppose that (U, V ) forms an (ε, p)-regular pair
with d := dp(U, V ). Suppose U′ ⊆U and V ′ ⊆V such that |U′| ≥α|U| and |V ′| ≥α|V |. Then
(U′, V ′) is an (ε′, p)-regular pair with d′ := dp(U′, V ′) such that |d′ −d| ≤ε.

2.2. The K LR conjecture for random graphs. Let ε, p > 0 and n ∈N. Let H be a graph on
vertex set {1, . . . , k} and let m = (mij)ij∈E(H) be a sequence of non-negative integers. We define

(ε, p)-regular pair with mij edges between the pair (Vi, Vj).
A canonical copy of H in such a G ∈G(H, n, m, p, ε) is a copy of H with vertices v1, . . . , vk where
vi ∈Vi for each i ∈V (H) and vivj ∈E(G) for every ij ∈E(H).
