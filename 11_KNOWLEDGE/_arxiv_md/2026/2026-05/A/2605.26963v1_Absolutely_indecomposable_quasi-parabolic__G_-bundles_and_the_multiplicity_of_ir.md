Abstract
Absolutely indecomposable vector bundle and parabolic vector bundles are well-studied
via quiver representations.
In this paper, we study absolutely indecomposable quasi-
parabolic G-bundles over P1 with generic additive character varieties. Furthermore, we
give a geometric interpretation of the multiplicity of the tensor product of irreducible
characters of finite reductive groups using generic additive character varieties.

Introduction
In this paper, G is a connected (untwisted) reductive group whose derived subgroup is simply
connected defined over k (where k is an algebraically closed field of Fq), F the Frobenius map
such that kF = Fq, T a maximal split torus of G, B a Borel subgroup containing T, P a

we denote the set of (complex) irreducible characters of a finite group H by bH.

1.1.2
Result
The main starting object of this paper is quasi-parabolic G-bundles with the trivial base G-
bundle corresponding to elements in (G/B)ℓ. Note that when E corresponds to an element p ∈
(G/B)ℓ, we have Aut(E) = StabG(p), where G acts on p by the diagonal left multiplication.
This is because an automorphism of E is determined by a regular function from P1 to G,
and such regular function is constant (due to the property of a projective variety). From
this observation, we consider elements in (G/B)ℓinstead of quasi-parabolic G-bundles over

1.1.3
Next topics
Our future goal is to compute the number of absolutely indecomposable parabolic G-bundles
(up to isomorphism) fixed by the Frobenius morphism F exactly. In §3.4, we give a way
to compute this number (when the base bundle is trivial), but this is not quite explicit. To
compute this, we think that we need to use the method in [Let16], with constructing parabolic
G-bundles carefully over a given base G-bundle.
Furthermore, in Theorem 4.4.2 in [Let16], Letellier gets a result about the number of Higgs
bundles by comparing Deligne-Lusztig characters of GLn(Fq) and gln(Fq). In addition, the
author also studied a relation between the multiplicity of irreducible characters of GLn(Fq)
and the size of a quiver variety in [Let13b, Theorem 7.3.3]. To get such a result, the author
also used Deligne-Lusztig characters of GLn(Fq) and gln(Fq), cf. [Let13b, Theorem 6.9.1].
From such observation, we also try to study a relation between Deligne-Lusztig characters
of GF and gF , and we get the next result in this paper about the multiplicity of irreducible
characters of GF and the size of generic additive characters. We believe that this would be
helpful to compute the number of absolutely indecomposable parabolic G-bundles.

of a finite group, a natural question is to determine the value of the inner product
⟨χ1 ⊗. . . ⊗χℓ, 1⟩.
(1.2)
This is related to the ring structure of the space of finite dimensional characters of GF . To the

In this section, we recall basic facts and notations about root systems, reductive groups and

2.1
Root systems
Let Φ be a (reduced crystallographic) root system in a finite dimensional Euclidean vector
space (V, (. , .)). We assume that Φ spans V and fix a base ∆of Φ. Then ∆is a basis of the
vector space V . Let W be the Weyl group generated by the simple reflections sα, α ∈∆.
2.1.1
Subsystems
A root subsystem of Φ is a subset Ψ ⊆Φ which is itself a root system. Equivalently, it satisfies
sα(β) ∈Ψ for all α, β ∈Ψ. A subsystem Ψ ⊆Φ is called closed if α, β ∈Ψ
and
α + β ∈Φ
implies α + β ∈Ψ. Given a subset S ⊆Φ, let ⟨S⟩Z denote the subgroup of V generated by S.
The subsystem of Φ generated by S is defined by
⟨S⟩:= ⟨S⟩Z ∩Φ.
It is easy to check that ⟨S⟩is a closed subsystem of Φ.
A Levi subsystem of Φ is a subsystem of the form Φ ∩U where U ⊆V is a subspace. A
Levi subsystem of Φ is closed in Φ. Note that every Levi subsystem of Φ is of the form w·⟨S⟩,
where S is a subset of ∆and w ∈W. In particular, if w = 1, then ⟨S⟩is called a standard
Levi subsystem. A subset S ⊆Φ is called isolated in Φ if |S| = |∆|, and this implies that ⟨S⟩
is not contained in a proper Levi subsystem of Φ.

2.1.2
Pseudo-Levi subsystems
Assume Φ is irreducible. Let θ be the highest root of Φ, and e∆:= ∆⊔{−θ}. A pseudo-Levi
subsystem is a subsystem of Φ of the form w · ⟨S⟩, where w ∈W and S is a subset of e∆. If
w = 1, then ⟨S⟩is called a standard pseudo-Levi subsystem. This story can be extended to
non-irreducible Φ easily.
2.1.3
Isolated pseudo-Levi subsystem
Let Ψ be a pseudo-Levi subsystem of an irreducible root system Φ. Then we have the following
equivalence: Ψ is isolated in Φ if and only if Ψ = w · ⟨S⟩where S ⊂˜∆has the same size as
∆. In other words, up to W-conjugation, isolated pseudo-Levis are those which are obtained
by removing just a single element of e∆(equivalently, a single node from the extended Dynkin
diagram).
2.2
Reductive groups
2.2.1
Levi and pseudo-Levi subgroups
A Levi subgroup of G is defined to be the centraliser L = CG(S) of a torus S ⊆G. If S ⊆T,
then L is said to be a standard Levi subgroup. In this case, the root system of L is a Levi
subsystem of Φ. If t ∈g is a semisimple element, then the connected centraliser CG(t) is a
Levi subgroup of G. Moreover, every Levi subgroup of G arises in this manner. Note that if
the characteristic of k is very good for G, then CG(t) is connected [Ste75, Theorem 3.14].
Let t ∈G be a semisimple element.
Recall that if the derived subgroup [G, G] of G
is simply connected, then the centraliser subgroup CG(t) of a semisimple element t ∈G is
connected. The connected centraliser CG(t) is called a pseudo-Levi subgroup of G, and if
t ∈T, then CG(t) is called a standard pseudo-Levi subgroup of G. We call a pseudo-Levi
subgroup is isolated if its root subsystem is isolated in Φ. Note that an isolated pseudo-Levi
subgroup is not in proper parabolic subgroup. For a pseudo-Levi subgroup E of G, we denote
the root subsystem of E in Φ by ΦE.

observation, we have the following useful lemma.

t ∈T such that Φt = Ψ.
