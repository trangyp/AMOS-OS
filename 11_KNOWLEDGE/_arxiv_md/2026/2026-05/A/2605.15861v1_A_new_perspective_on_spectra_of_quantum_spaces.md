A NEW PERSPECTIVE ON SPECTRA OF QUANTUM SPACES
ARNAUD BROTHIER
Abstract. We consider a class of C∗-algebras C(Xq) associated with quantum spaces
such as spheres, projective spaces, and lens spaces.
We introduce a non-self-adjoint
operator algebra A together with an explicit functor from the category of representa-
tions of A to that of C(Xq). We then construct explicitly a family of one-dimensional
representations of A that parametrise the entire spectrum of C(Xq).
Introduction
Vaughan Jones found unexpectedly an efficient way to construct unitary representations
of certain groups while searching for reconstruction of conformal field theories [Jon17],
see the survey [Bro20]. It was first applied to the groups F,T,V of Richard Thompson
[CFP96]. This method was then adapted to the Cuntz(–Dixmier) C∗-algebras On: any
representation of On can be constructed using an explicit inductive limit and a repre-
sentation (often of finite dimension) of a much simpler algebra [Dix64, Cun77, BJ19].
Powerful method were then developed to classify a large part of the spectrum of On and
in particular in obtaining a sequence of moduli spaces in [BW25, BW26, BW24a]. These
methods were then substancially adapted to the algebraic context of the Leavitt path
algebras [BW24b] and later on adapted to Cuntz-Krieger algebras (in fact to higher rank
graph C∗-algebras) [BSW26].
This article aims to present and apply these techniques to C∗-algebras of quantum
spaces. These are presented noncommutative C∗-algebras of type I obtained by deforming
algebras of function on topological spaces X. They are interpreted as spaces of functions
over a fictive “noncommutative topological space” Xq and thus often denoted C(Xq). We
will consider Vaksman-Soibelman quantum spheres (of odd and even dimension) and some
of their orbifold such as quantum projective spaces and quantum lens spaces appearing
in [SV90, HS03, BS18, GZ24].
The study of C∗-algebras of such quantum spaces is a rich and vibrant research topic
mixing operator algebraic techniques with geometric and algebraic ones. They first ap-
peared in the pioneered work of Woronowicz and Podle´s [Wor80, Wor87, Pod87]. They
are closely related to quantum groups as developed by Faddeev, Drinfeld, Jimbo and
many other, see [Dri86, Jim85] and the survey [Fad06]. Deep work have been pursued
in describing and studying their underlying topological and geometrical properties; for
instance in computing their K-theory, K-homology Fredholm modules, Chern characters
and even equipping certain of them with a Connes spectral triple structure, to cite a few
[DLM01, HL04, ADL08, Con94].
Remarkably, Hong and Szyma´nski have constructed graphs L = L(Xq) and explicit
isomorphisms φ ∶OL →C(Xq) from the Cuntz-Krieger algebra OL of the graph L to C(Xq)
for a number of quantum spaces Xq including quantum spheres, quantum projective spaces
and certain quantum lens spaces [HS02, HS03]. Both C∗-algebras OL and C(Xq) have

In this present paper we consider a non-self-adjoint algebra AL associated to a graph
L which is roughly obtained by taking the adjoints of the usual generators of OL subject
to all relations of OL except the one requiring them to be partial isometries. For generic
graphs L it is in practice much easier to construct AL-modules than representations of OL
(we use the terms module for actions of non-self-adjoint algebras and representations for
actions of C∗-algebras). In particular, AL usually admits many finite dimensional modules
while often OL has only infinite dimensional representations. We define an explicit functor
Π ∶Mod(AL) →Rep(OL) from AL-modules to representations of OL that is essentially
surjective (i.e. for all K ∈Rep(OL) there is H ∈Mod(AL) so that Π(H) ≃K). Moreover,
Π sends finite dimensional irreducible modules to irreducible representations and is fully
faithful on those. For each quantum object Xq with associated graph L = L(Xq), we
construct explicitly a set of one-dimensional AL-modules whose image under the functor
Π form a set of representatives for the spectrum of OL.
Theorem A. Fix n ≥1 and let C(Sq) be the C∗-algebra of the quantum sphere of dimen-
sion (2n −1). Identify C(Sq) with the Cuntz-Krieger algebra OL associated to the graph
L = L2n−1 of Hong and Szyma´nski and consider our non-self-ajoint algebra AL. Then for
each (j,z) with 1 ≤j ≤n and z in the unit circle there is a one-dimensional module Mj,z of
AL so that the set of Π(Mj,z) parametrises the spectrum of C(Sq). Similar results hold for
quantum projective spaces, quantum lens spaces and for 2n-dimensional quantum spheres
this latter having its spectrum parametrised by n circles and two additional isolated points.
Parametrising these spectra is not new. Indeed, Vaksman and Soibelman have given
explicit representatives of the spectra of all quantum spheres [SV90]. Then Hawkins and
Landi gave explicit representatives of the spectra of certain quantum spaces that turned
out to be isomorphic to Vaksman-Soibelman quantum spheres [HL04]. Another method
goes as follows: a general theorem computes the primitive spectrum of any Cuntz–Krieger
algebra [HR97]; see also [Hon04] and [BHRS02]. Using remarkable isomorphisms between
Cuntz-Krieger algebras and quantum spaces we deduce the primitive spectrum of C(Xq)
for Xq being a quantum sphere, projective space or lens space. Finally, using that C(Xq)
is a C∗-algebra of type I one obtains the spectrum since this latter is isomorphic to the
primitive spectrum via the obvious map π ↦ker(π).
The interest of our construction lies in a different point of view, based on the intro-
duction of a non-self-adjoint algebra AL and the lifting Π ∶Mod(AL) →Rep(OL) of all
AL-module to representations of OL ≃C(Xq). In particular, we directly work on repre-
sentations rather than on ideals and our techniques are explicit. Somewhat surprisingly,
every irreducible representation of C(Xq) come from one-dimensional modules of AL.
From this perspective, C(Xq) has a very basic representation theory, akin to that of a
commutative C∗-algebra. This situation contrasts sharply with that of non-type I C∗-
algebras such as the Cuntz algebra O2 in two generators. It admits a non-self-adjoint
operator algebra A2. The primitive spectrum of O2 consists of a single point, since O2
is simple, whereas the part of its spectrum corresponding to d-dimensional irreducible
A2-modules is parametrised by a smooth manifold of dimension 2d2 + 1 for all natural
number d ≥1 [BW24a].

Plan of the article. In Section 1 we define Cuntz-Krieger algebras OL associated to
finite directed graphs L. We define our non-self-adjoint algebra AL and explain how each
AL-module H dilates into a representation Π(H) of OL. We recall some key properties
of the process H ↦Π(H). In Section 2 we consider one by one each C∗-algebra C(Xq)
of quantum spaces studying here. We define a graph L ∶= L(Xq) so that OL →C(Xq).
Then we describe our non-self-adjoint algebra AL for this specific graph L and construct
explicitly a set of irreducible AL-modules so that their image by Π forms a set of repre-
sentatives for the spectra of OL (and thus of C(Xq)). We provide great details on the
first examples that are the odd-dimensional quantum spheres. Then we explain how to
derive similar results for quantum projective spaces and quantum lens spaces that are all
orbifolds of odd-dimensional quantum spheres. Lastly, we describe the spectrum of the
even-dimensional quantum spheres.

1. Lifting representations

been done for the larger class of row-finite and locally convex higher rank graphs L in

2. Quantum spaces

Let us determine the finite dimensional irreducible modules. Let H be a finite dimen-
sional irreducible module over the algebra A. We write aj ↦Aj and aji ↦Aji the action
of A on H. Set Hi the range (or domain) of the projection Ai and recall that Aji can

Therefore, Hn+1 ⊂H is a submodule. Moreover, note that since no operators of A acts
nontrivially on Hn+1 we have that any vector subspace of Hn+1 defines a submodule. Ir-

with Aji = 0 for all edges. Write Mn+1 this module structure. Similarly, define Mn+2
to be the module where H = Hn+2 ≃C with Aji = 0 for all edges. These are two irre-
ducible modules (since they are of dimension one) and are inequivalent. Assume now that
Hn+1 = Hn+2 = {0}. This implies that all the operators Aj,n+1 and Aj,n+2 are equal to 0 for
all 1 ≤j ≤n. Then H1 ⊂H is a submodule and thus H1 = H or {0} by irreducibility. In the
first case we have that A11 ∶H1 →H1 is an isometry and thus is a unitary since H1 is finite
dimensional by assumption. Moreover, note that all other edge operators Aji are equal
to 0. Hence, any invariant subspace of H1 under A11 defines a submodule. Irreducibility
implies that H1 is one-dimensional and thus A11 must be the multiplication by a scalar z
in the unit circle S1. Denote by M1,z this module structure, i.e. H = H1 ≃C with A11 = z
and Aji = 0 if (j,i) ≠(1,1). Assume now that H1 = {0}. We may then apply a similar
reasoning to H2 and define a module M2,z for each z ∈S1. Continuing inductively we
deduce that a finite dimensional irreducible module is necessarily conjugate to Mn+1 or
Mn+2 or Mj,z for some 1 ≤j ≤n and z ∈S1. It is rather immediate that they are all indeed
irreducible (since one-dimensional) and pairwise inequivalent. Thoerem 1.1 implies that
{Π(Mn+1),Π(Mn+2),Π(Mj,z) ∶1 ≤j ≤n,z ∈S1} provides a set of irreducible representa-
tions of O that are pairwise inequivalent. Moreover, if K ≃Π(H) with dim(H) < ∞so
that K is an irreducible representation of O, then K ≃Π(M) for M = Mn+i or M ≃Mj,z
for i = 1,2 or 1 ≤j ≤n,z ∈S1.

is not isomorphic to Π(Mn+i) or Π(Mj,z) for i = 1,2, 1 ≤j ≤n and z ∈S1. By essential
surjectivity of the functor Π there exists a module H so that K ≃Π(H). The module H is
necessarily indecomposable and, from the argument of above, does not contain any finite
dimensional nonzero submodule. As usual denote by Hi the range of Ai for 1 ≤i ≤n + 2.

by applying a similar argument than in the odd-dimensional quantum sphere case.
