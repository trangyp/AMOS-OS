Abstract
We study exact constant collapse in variational autoencoders: the deterministic
encoder mean becomes independent of the input. The prior remains the standard
Gaussian. Before VAE training, we select a fixed teacher posterior from a GMM-
based view of the data and attach a fixed latent-only simplex witness to the encoder
mean. This construction yields two linked objects. The first is a certificate: if
the witness prediction improves on the best constant predictor of the teacher,
the encoder mean cannot be input-independent constant. The second is a local
escape direction: on the collapsed manifold, the teacher residual gives a sample-
dependent descent direction for the alignment loss. For any full-support teacher
posterior, the same geometry also gives a closed-form latent code with zero teacher-
witness alignment error. Its scaled versions trace a margin-energy path from the
constant predictor to the exact teacher code, which quantifies non-collapse inside
the protected witness subspace. We instantiate the method on MNIST, CIFAR-10,
and CIFAR-100. With searched unsupervised PCA-GMM teachers, vanilla VAEs
fail the teacher-witness certificate in all five seeds on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100,
while RST variants pass in all five seeds. Under collapse-stress settings with
βKL ∈{2, 4, 8}, vanilla VAE again fails in all seeds, whereas RST-alpha-prefit
remains certificate-positive. Escape trajectories on both natural-image datasets
increase the witness margin from a low-margin initialization and exhibit nonzero
teacher-induced gradient norms. The analysis is confined to exact constant collapse
of the encoder mean; generation quality, decoder use, and other collapse modes
remain separate questions.
1
Introduction
Posterior collapse is usually diagnosed only after training: one trains a VAE and then asks whether
the latent variable has carried input-dependent information. That workflow is inadequate when a
particular collapse mode is to be excluded by design. This work isolates one such mode, exact
input-independent constant collapse of the encoder mean.
We keep the prior fixed as p(z) = N(0, I) and introduce a single teacher posterior over the data. The
teacher is chosen before VAE training and then held fixed. It enters neither the decoder nor the prior;
it defines a target view against which the latent mean is tested.
The mechanism is a teacher-student witness path. A searched GMM teacher gives a posterior vector
Tx for each data point. A fixed simplex witness maps the encoder mean µϕ(x) to S(µϕ(x)). The
loss LTS = ExKL(Tx∥S(µϕ(x))) compares these two distributions. Since the witness reads only
the latent mean, a constant encoder mean produces a constant witness prediction. Beating the best
constant predictor of the teacher therefore certifies that the encoder mean is not input-independent
constant.

The same loss also describes a local escape mechanism. At exact collapse, the negative alignment
gradient contains the teacher residual Tx −¯T. Thus the collapsed manifold is not an absorbing set
for the witness dynamics when the teacher is nontrivial and the encoder has a tangent direction that
can express the induced signal. This distinguishes RST from a purely post-hoc certificate.
This work builds on our earlier teacher-guided VAE certificate, which introduced a testable constant-
predictor baseline and used an inequality of the form L < IT as a final non-collapse certificate [6].
Here the teacher has an additional role: it defines a fixed latent-only witness whose alignment loss
supplies the transverse escape direction. The resulting framework treats feasibility, escape, and final
certification as parts of the same construction.
The fixed witness has an explicit inverse on the teacher view. For a full-support teacher posterior,
centered teacher log-odds embed into the standard-Gaussian latent space so that the witness exactly
recovers the teacher. This gives a closed-form teacher code and a computable latent mean energy.
Scaled codes along the ray from the prior center to this analytic target provide a margin-energy
tradeoff; see Appendix B. Teacher search is therefore guided by three quantities: teacher information,
log-odds energy, and stability.
The scaled path also clarifies the meaning of partial non-collapse. It is not a coordinate-wise active-
unit statement. Instead, it measures how much teacher information is expressed in the witness
subspace: α = 0 gives the best constant predictor, while α = 1 gives exact teacher matching.
Coarsened or hierarchical teacher views can be tested with their own constant baselines, yielding
certificates at progressively finer levels of the teacher view without asserting full-dimensional latent
usage.
Contributions.
1. We give a constant-collapse certificate based on one searched teacher posterior and one fixed
latent-only simplex witness.
2. We prove a function-space escape result: at exact collapse, the witness alignment loss has a
nonzero sample-dependent descent direction whenever the teacher is nontrivial.
3. We derive a closed-form latent target with zero teacher-witness alignment error, together with its
latent mean energy and its scaled margin-prior tradeoff.
4. We state the certificate’s scope precisely: scaled targets quantify the margin-energy tradeoff inside
the teacher-witness subspace, not full-dimensional latent usage.
Empirical protocol.
We evaluate the construction in five parts: a controlled MNIST sanity check,
searched-teacher feasibility on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, five-seed training certificates, collapse-
stress runs, and escape trajectories. The appendices record the supporting constructions: simplex
geometry, teacher search, scaled targets, witness variants, prior-matching diagnostics, and optional
reconstruction-side measurements.
Scope of the result.
The certificate concerns exact constant collapse of the deterministic encoder
path. The main theorem uses only the teacher-student witness path: the teacher gives Tx, the
witness reads µϕ(x), and the margin checks whether this prediction improves on the best constant
predictor. Reconstruction-side teacher evaluations, described in Appendix I, are diagnostics rather
than assumptions of the theorem. The result does not replace measurements of generation quality,
decoder use, per-dimension KL, active units, or other representation metrics.

This observation is weaker than full dimensional latent usage. The witness only sees the simplex
subspace
U = span{v1, . . . , vK},
whose dimension is at most K −1. A small LTS certifies nonconstant behavior in the teacher-witness

Coarser teacher views can be analyzed by applying the same constant-baseline argument after merging
teacher components. Such views are useful for interpreting partial non-collapse: a coarse teacher
may cross its own constant baseline before the full K-component teacher does. The main theorem,
however, remains the single-teacher certificate based on GT = IT −LTS. Appendix C gives the full
coarse-graining and hierarchical formulation.

Method
5.1
Teacher search
Teacher search balances information and cost. A useful score is
Score(ω) = λTSIT (ω) −αKL
K −1

The first term rewards nontrivial teacher information. The second penalizes log-odds energy. The
remaining terms encourage balanced component use, good GMM fit, and stability under seeds or
mild perturbations.
5.2
Training objective
The main training objective is
Ltotal = Lrec + αKLLKL + λTSLTS.
The teacher and witness enter the optimization only through LTS; the optimized variables are the
VAE parameters.
A minimal protocol is:
1. search a fixed GMM teacher and compute Tx, ¯T, IT , rk(x), and ET ;
2. construct the fixed simplex witness S;
3. train the VAE with the weighted alignment term;
4. report GT = IT −LTS after training.
The optional analytic target zT (x) can be used for prefit, but the main theorem only needs the final
margin. We report three RST variants. RST trains from random initialization with the VAE objective
plus λTSLTS. RST-prefit first pretrains the encoder mean toward the full analytic target zT (x) and
then runs the same RST objective. RST-alpha-prefit instead pretrains toward the minimum scaled
target α⋆zT (x) identified by the feasibility calculation and then runs the same RST objective. The
first variant tests the direct optimization effect of the witness loss, the second is a stronger engineering
initialization, and the third is the variant most directly tied to the scaled-target feasibility theorem.

Experiments
The experiments test the three claims in the paper: feasibility, training-time certification, and escape.
The core metric is always
GT = IT −LTS.
A positive value certifies that the encoder mean is not an exact input-independent constant in the
teacher-witness view. Active units, effective rank, reconstruction error, KL, and MMD are reported

model can have many coordinates with nonzero variance and still fail to beat the teacher constant

6.7
Summary
The experiments now match the intended evidence chain:
1. Feasibility: on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, a small scaled target αzT (x) crosses the constant
baseline with tiny mean KL cost.
2. Training certificate: on both datasets, vanilla VAE fails in all five seeds, while all RST variants
pass in all five seeds.
3. Stress: under βKL ∈{2, 4, 8}, vanilla VAE fails in all stress seeds, while RST-alpha-prefit
remains positive.
4. Escape: from low-margin initializations, the witness objective sharply increases GT and has
nonzero gradient norm.
Together these results do not prove optimizer-independent avoidance of every collapse mode. They
do show that exact input-independent constant collapse can be made a designable, trainable, and
auditable certificate problem.

7
Limitations and discussion
The prior remains standard Gaussian, while the aggregate encoder distribution need not be Gaus-
sian. Once the latent mean carries teacher-detectable variation, some mismatch with N(0, I) is
expected. When unconditional sampling is central, this mismatch should be monitored through
teacher smoothing, bounded witness gain, KL budgets, and explicit prior-matching diagnostics.
The escape result is local to the witness alignment component. It rules out exact collapse as a
stationary point of that component, but it is not a global convergence theorem for the full nonconvex
VAE objective. In practice, reconstruction and KL gradients can oppose the alignment force. The
alignment weight λTS gives a direct control parameter: if an exact cancellation occurs at one value
and the teacher-induced gradient is nonzero, changing the weight destroys that cancellation. This
motivates reporting λTS sweeps and gradient-dominance diagnostics, rather than relying on the
formal escape statement alone.
The certificate is also a view-level certificate. It concerns the teacher-witness subspace and exact
constant collapse of the encoder mean. It does not certify that every latent coordinate, posterior
variance, or decoder pathway is used. Reconstruction quality, sampling quality, decoder dependence,
per-dimension KL, active units, and linear probes remain separate measurements of representation
and generation quality.
Teacher construction has a cost. In this paper the teacher is searched once and then fixed, so the cost
is amortized across all RST runs. CPU-based PCA/GMM search is adequate for the moderate-scale
experiments considered here. Scaling the same design principle to ImageNet-scale data, stronger
feature backbones, or online teacher updates is an engineering and methodological direction beyond
the present scope.
More broadly, the work suggests a certificate-and-escape view of anti-collapse representation learning.
Such a method specifies a nontrivial view of the input, provides a latent-only witness for that view,
exerts a nonvanishing force on the associated collapsed manifold, and leaves a testable margin after
training. RST is one realization of this pattern for standard-Gaussian VAEs. Other views, modalities,
and world-model latents may admit analogous witness certificates.
8
Conclusion
RST combines feasibility, escape, and certification. A fixed simplex witness has a closed-form
teacher code, and scaled versions of this code expose the tradeoff between certificate margin and
prior proximity. The same alignment loss gives a teacher-residual escape direction at exact collapse.
Finally, the margin LTS < IT rules out exact input-independent constant collapse of the encoder
mean. The experiments instantiate the full chain: searched CIFAR teachers admit small-cost scaled
targets, RST variants pass the five-seed certificate on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, RST-alpha-prefit
remains positive under KL stress, and escape trajectories show large margin increases from low-
margin initializations. The contribution is therefore not an optimizer-independent guarantee for all
collapse modes, but a concrete way to design, train, and audit a standard-Gaussian VAE against a
specified collapse mode.
