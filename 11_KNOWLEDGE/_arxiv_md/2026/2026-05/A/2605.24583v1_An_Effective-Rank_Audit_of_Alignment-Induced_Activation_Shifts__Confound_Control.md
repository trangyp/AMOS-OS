Abstract
We audit alignment-induced shifts in residual-stream activations of three open-weight
instruction-tuned LLMs (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-it, Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct)
using the effective rank of the alignment modification matrix on safety-relevant inputs,
ρϵ := rankϵ(MDs)/d, which formalizes the single-refusal-direction observation of Arditi et al.
(2024) as a continuous quantity.
The paper has three contributions.
(1) Confound-
controlled measurement.
A four-variant decomposition of the modification matrix
(Mnaive, Mtemplate, Maligned, MDiD) separates the contributions of chat-template formatting,
alignment-stage shift, and the refusal-mediating direction, and recovers the Arditi refusal
direction on MDiD at | cos | ∈{0.77, 0.86, 0.50} (Llama/Gemma/Qwen), while Mtemplate
gives chat-template-controlled ρϵ ∈{0.0029, 0.0048, 0.0044}; the centered SVD residual is
4–7× larger, indicating the headline rank is dominated by a single mean direction. (2)
Constructive calibration with a sweet-spot vs. brittle distinction. On a 3-layer
MLP across ρϵ ∈{0.008, 0.17, 0.33, 0.40}, high ρϵ via mild rank-maximization regularization
(λ = 5) buys ablation robustness (≥0.97 compliance through projection of 62.5% of the

Introduction
Arditi et al. (2024) showed refusal in instruction-tuned LLMs is mediated by a single direction in activation
space, and that ablating that direction removes safety entirely. This is a striking empirical observation about
the geometry of current alignment: the shift between pre-trained and aligned activations on safety-relevant
inputs lives in a small number of directions, and a sub-capable adversary with a truncated SVD can identify
and remove them.
This paper audits the structural consequences of that observation by formalizing it as a continuous quantity.
Let MDs := [halign(xi)−hpre(xi)]i be the paired alignment modification at a fixed layer over a safety-relevant
input distribution Ds. The separability index is
ρϵ := rankϵ(MDs)/d

Contribution 1: confound-controlled measurement.

Contribution 2: constructive calibration with a sweet-spot vs. brittle distinction.
On a 3-layer
MLP across ρϵ ∈{0.008, 0.17, 0.33, 0.40} (§5, App. A.9), high ρϵ via mild rank-maximization regularization
(λ = 5, baseline compliance 0.86) buys ablation robustness (≥0.97 compliance through projection of 62.5%
of the representation), while strong regularization at the same nominal ρϵ (λ = 50, baseline compliance 0.71)
does not (post-ablation compliance 0.10 at r = 40). ρϵ is therefore a diagnostic; mechanical rank inflation
via stronger regularization does not buy structural robustness, so treating ρϵ as an optimization target fails
on the calibration testbed. Two structural consequences of low ρϵ support the diagnostic interpretation:
linear-projection fragility (Prop. 3.1, unconditional) and an O(ρϵd) achievability bound on faking footprint
(Prop. 3.5, representational only). The matching Ω(ρϵd) lower bound, which would make low ρϵ a structural
condition for fake-resistant safety, is left as an open problem (§8).
Contribution 3: limits of rank-based diagnostics.
We document three. (a) Not safety-specific.
The LRH baseline (App. A.7) gives arbitrary linear concepts ρϵ ≈0.008–0.010 in the same models, only
2–3× the safety value: low rank is a generic property of post-training linear concepts, and the bridge from
rank to behavior is supplied by causal ablation (Test 2, §6.4), not by the rank number. (b) SVD ordering
does not match causal ordering. On Llama narrow-band, u2 ranks second by squared variance but
has near-zero causal effect on refusal under projection ablation; cumulative top-k ablation is non-monotone
at k = 5 (Table 6, §6.4). Behavioral fragility must be measured directly by Test 2, not read off the SVD
spectrum. (c) The spectral-gap hypothesis required for the Mirsky lower-bound sketch fails. We
measure κemp across three publicly available unsafe-mode references for Llama (§8, App. A.10): κemp > 8 on
1/90 layer-reference pairs. In a controlled MLP sweep (App. A.11), 0/36 honest–unsafe combinations cross
the threshold, with the worst case in the distributed-vs-distributed regime the high-ρϵ regime targets. We
identify a geometric reason (Lemma 8.1: κlb ≤2/(ϵr)) that κ > 8 is structurally hard to satisfy whenever
the modal-difference signal has non-trivial effective rank.
The diagnostic is computable by a verifier strictly weaker than the diagnosed system, given whitebox paired
checkpoints and a fixed Ds (Remark 3.4); the capability burden is displaced onto constructing Ds, not
removed.

2.1
Separable Safety
Definition 2.1 (Separable Safety). An alignment procedure produces (k, ϵ)-separable safety on safety-
relevant distribution Ds if the alignment modification is concentrated in a low-dimensional subspace:
rankϵ(MDs) ≤k,
k ≪d.
(3)
Equivalently, the top-k principal components of MDs capture at least a (1 −ϵ)-fraction of the modification’s
variance. Safety is perfectly separable when the alignment shift is rank-one (rankϵ(MDs) = 1 for small ϵ).
Separability is a statement about the readout of the alignment modification at the residual stream, consistent
with arbitrarily complex internal computation producing the readout (Remark 2.2).
Remark 2.2 (Modification Level vs Mechanism). MDs records what alignment changes at the readout, not
the internal computation that produced the change. Mechanistic interpretability (Olah et al., 2020; Arditi
et al., 2024; Wollschläger et al., 2025) consistently finds distributed non-linear computations writing into
low-dimensional residual subspaces; our diagnostic is sensitive to that readout structure and silent on the
depth of computation. Fragility, fakeability, and verifiability claims thus concern residual-stream attacks
(projection, steering, mode-toggling), not arbitrary adversarial pressure on the mechanism.
Remark 2.3 (Linear Recoverability). Low rank means a rank-k projection P recovers (1−ϵ)-energy on P and
removes it on I−P; a linear probe with ≤k features achieves near-perfect aligned-vs-pre-trained classification
when the shift exceeds activation noise. Non-linear probes may find additional structure outside SVD.
Low ρϵ has one unconditional geometric consequence (Prop. 3.1: a rank-k projection removes (1−ϵ)-variance)
and one unconditional representational consequence (Prop. 3.5: an O(ρϵd) faking footprint is achievable).
Whether the representational fragility translates to behavioral fragility, and whether the achievability has a
matching Ωlower bound, are separate empirical (§6.4) and open-problem (§8) questions.

3
Consequences of Separability
The diagnostic carries one geometric implication unconditionally; behavioral and converse-direction impli-
cations require additional structure that this paper shows is not free (§8).

The narrow-band Llama trajectory 0.85 →
0.80 →0.92 →0.66 →0.10 (at k = 1, 3, 5, 10, 20) cannot be read off the SVD spectrum: tail singular
values do not decrease behavioral effect monotonically. The per-direction table decomposes the rebound:
u1 alone is load-bearing (refusal 0.84); u2 alone is null; u3–u5 are individually weak (∼0.94). At k = 3
the ablation already covers the load-bearing u1 together with the inert u2 and one of the weak directions,
dropping refusal to 0.80. At k = 5 the rebound to 0.92 is consistent with a redundancy-with-interaction
reading: ablating u1 together with u2, . . . , u5 would activate a compensatory pathway absent under u1-only
or u1 + {u2, u3} ablation, requiring k ≥10 to cover.
This reading is consistent with the concept-cone
refinement of Wollschläger et al. (2025) and with the wide-band k = 20 result that collapses refusal to 0.00,
but the present per-direction data alone does not distinguish it from alternatives (e.g. keyword-classifier
interaction with specific token distributions at k = 5, or layer-interaction effects across the narrow band);
combination ablations on non-consecutive subsets of the top-k block, which we do not run here, would be
required to verify. The audit takeaway is robust to which reading is correct: Test 2 must be evaluated as a
curve in k and the SVD ordering is not a substitute for per-direction or combination-aware ablation.
6.5
Summary
Principal rank-k ablation collapses refusal to Wilson 95% CI ⊂[0.00, 0.05] in all three models (Gemma at
narrow k = 1, Qwen at narrow k ≥20, Llama at wide k = 20) while random rank-k ablation at the same
layers leaves refusal intact. The diagnosed modification is therefore (k, 0.05)-separable with k ≤24 against
d ≥3584, so Propositions 3.1 and 3.5 apply.

7.1
Scope: What the Diagnostic Measures
ρϵ is a readout-level rank measurement on a pre-defined safety distribution. (1) Low rank is generic,
safety is at the concentrated end.
LRH baseline (App. A.7): arbitrary concepts give ρϵ ≈0.008–
0.010, 2–3× the safety value.
Test 2 supplies the bridge from rank to behavior.
(2) Readout, not
mechanism (Remark 2.2): a non-linear computation writing into a low-dim subspace tests as low-rank.
Fragility (Prop. 3.1) applies to residual-stream attacks; LoRA fine-tuning, prompt jailbreaks, and deceptive
alignment are out of scope. (3) Headline rank is the Arditi mean direction plus formatting variance
(§6.2): centered SVD residual is 4–7× uncentered. Our contribution is the index ρϵ and the constructive
calibration, not a multi-dim safety code.
ρϵ normalizes by nominal d (intrinsic dim is smaller, so reported numbers understate the occupied fraction).
Rank-and-layer ablation does not stratify by harm type; per-category structure is open.

7.2
Where the Two-Stage Paradigm Discussion Sits
It is tempting to argue that the two-stage paradigm itself causes low-rank alignment modifications: that a
narrow post-hoc objective on a frozen substrate must produce a low-rank shift. This argument is plausible but
exceeds our evidence. Our measurements show that three specific two-stage pipelines (Llama-3.1, Gemma-2,
Qwen-2.5 instruction tuning) produce low-ρϵ readouts; they do not show that this is necessary, nor that
single-stage joint training would produce high-ρϵ readouts. A single-stage training run with overwhelmingly
more data devoted to general capability than to safety might well produce the same readout structure for
the same data-imbalance reason. We therefore describe the two-stage observation as a correlation: this is
what current pipelines empirically do, and it is sufficient to make the linear-projection fragility of Prop. 3.1
applicable.
Whether the readout is low-rank because of the paradigm or because of the loss-imbalance
asymmetry between pre-training and alignment data is left open.
7.3
Rank Inflation Does Not Buy Robustness
The same ρϵ ≈0.40 can be either ablation-robust (λ = 5: ≥0.97 through r = 80) or brittle (λ = 50: 0.10 at
r = 40; App. A.9). ρϵ screens for fragility but does not certify that the high-rank shift is causally distributed;
Test 2 must be run separately. The right use is auditing existing alignments and flagging the failure mode
of Prop. 3.1; how to train high-ρϵ alignment at LLM scale is open.
Diagnosed checkpoints are SFT/RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022)/DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023); applying the
diagnostic to Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022), debate (Irving et al., 2018), or IDA is an obvious next step
(see §7.2 for what the present measurements do and do not imply about the two-stage paradigm in general).

Related Work
Empirical fragility.
Safety fine-tuning is empirically fragile (Qi et al., 2024; Lermen et al., 2023; Qi et al.,
2025; Betley et al., 2026), and models elastically resist alignment as a shallow overlay (Ji et al., 2025). The
rank diagnostic groups these phenomena under a common geometric prerequisite: low ρϵ at the relevant
residual layers makes them all available.
Mechanistic interpretability.
Arditi et al. (2024) introduced the intra-aligned refusal direction ¯∆Arditi =
EDs[halign] −EDc[halign], computed on the aligned model alone, and showed ablating it removes refusal. Our
MDs is a different object: the paired alignment-stage shift halign −hpre, which requires both checkpoints and
conflates alignment-induced structure with chat-template formatting structure (Mtemplate controls for the
chat-template confound at the collection level, MDiD further removes the cross-prompt template-asymmetry
component and recovers the Arditi direction with | cos | up to 0.86). Wollschläger et al. (2025) showed refusal
10


## Page 11


is mediated by concept cones—multiple causally relevant directions, not a single one—and our centered-SVD
residual rank ∼75–80 on MDs is consistent with this multi-dimensional picture rather than independent
of it: we re-measure the multi-dimensional structure under the four-variant control, but the existence of
multi-dimensional structure beyond the rank-1 direction is established by Wollschläger et al. (2025). Jain
et al. (2024); Lee et al. (2024) provide mechanistic accounts of safety fine-tuning and DPO (Rafailov et al.,
2023). Representation engineering (Zou et al., 2023; Turner et al., 2023) demonstrates the operational side
of what ρϵ measures: a distinguishable low-rank subspace can be steered or removed. Sparse-autoencoder
work (Templeton et al., 2024) provides an alternative decomposition basis with monosemantic features;
whether SAE-recovered features give a more causally aligned rank than SVD components on MDs is a
natural open question (we use SVD because it is sub-capable computable from paired checkpoints alone). Our
contribution is the formal index, the chat-template-controlled four-variant decomposition, and the Mirsky-
route scoping (Lemma 8.1); mechanism-level discovery is not claimed.
Deceptive alignment.
Greenblatt et al. (2024); Hubinger et al. (2024) demonstrate alignment faking and
its persistence. Proposition 3.5 formalizes the representational footprint of mode-toggling (O(ρϵd) achiev-
ability); the value-misalignment-driven adversarial pressure that produces deception is outside our scope.
Scalable oversight and theory.
Leike et al. (2018); Amodei et al. (2016); Irving et al. (2018) seek scalable
oversight; Wolf et al. (2024) proves information-theoretic alignment limits; Ngo et al. (2024) surveys the
field. The sub-capable computability of ρϵ (Remark 3.4) provides a complementary structural-measurement
primitive.
Linear representations.
LLMs encode many concepts linearly (Mikolov et al., 2013; Park et al., 2024;
Marks & Tegmark, 2023; Hernandez et al., 2024); sparse autoencoders (Templeton et al., 2024) decompose
this further. Low ρϵ on MDs is consistent with this null; Test 2 (causal ablation) measures the additional
content beyond linearity, namely whether the low-rank subspace is causally privileged.
LoRA, concept erasure, mode connectivity.
Hu et al. (2022)’s low-rank adaptation is weight-space;
our activation-space low-rank structure is consistent but not entailed. Concept erasure (Ravfogel et al.,
2020; Belrose et al., 2023) provides upper bounds on linear-projection ablation; mode-connectivity (Garipov
et al., 2018; Frankle et al., 2020) explains why small parameter perturbations (Lermen et al., 2023) can undo
alignment.
