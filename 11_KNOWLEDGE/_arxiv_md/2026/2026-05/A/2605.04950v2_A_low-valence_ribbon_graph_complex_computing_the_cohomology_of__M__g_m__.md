A LOW-VALENCE RIBBON GRAPH COMPLEX
COMPUTING THE COHOMOLOGY OF Mg,m
SERGEI A. MERKULOV
Abstract. It is proven that every cohomology class of the moduli space Mg,m for any 2g + m ≥3, m ≥1
can be represented combinatorially by a ribbon quiver with at most four-valent vertices.
The at most
four-valency condition is sharp.
1. Introduction
1.1. Graph complexes. The famous graph complex GCd, d ∈Z, was introduced in [K2] in the context of
deformation quantizations of Poisson structures and found applications in many branches of mathematics
(see [K3, W3] for overviews). It is generated by graphs with at least trivalent vertices (which are assigned the
cohomological degree d) and with edges (which are assigned the cohomological degree 1−d) whose directions
can be flipped with the sign factor (−1)d for each flip. There are essentially two complexes in the family
{GCd}d∈Z with non-isomorphic cohomologies, one for d even and one for d odd.
Fixing a direction on each edge and assuming that valencies of vertices are at least 2 (and at least one vertex
has valency ≥3) gives us a directed version dGCd of GCd. There is a quasi-isomorphism of complexes [W1]
(1)
GCd −→dGCd
which sends a graph Γ ∈GCd with no fixed directions on edges into a sum of graphs obtained from Γ by
choosing directions on edges in all possible ways. Thus the complex dGCd gives us essentially nothing new,
but it contains an interesting subcomplex OGCd ⊂dGCd spanned by graphs with no closed paths of directed
edges (such graphs are called often quivers or oriented graphs). It was proven in [W2, Z, MWW] that there
is a Lie∞-isomorphism of the two dg Lie algebras (note the shift in d),
GCd ≃OGCd+1.
The complexes OGCd controls the homotopy theory of (degree shifted) Lie bialgebras [MW2] and their
deformation quantizations [MW3]; they are also used in the study of the real locus of the moduli space of
genus zero curves [KW]. The complex OGCd+1 (and hence GCd) admits a surprisingly “small” model GCT
d
which is spanned by graphs whose all vertices are precisely trivalent (see §3.3 in [Me2]).
