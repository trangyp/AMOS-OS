AUTOMORPHISMS OF THE MODULI SPACE OF SMOOTH CUBIC
SURFACES AND ITS FUNDAMENTAL GROUP
GREGORIO BALDI, BENSON FARB, ARIYAN JAVANPEYKAR, AND MATTHEW STOVER
Abstract. Let C be the moduli space of smooth complex cubic surfaces and let π1(C) be its
(orbifold) fundamental group.
We prove that the “divisor subgroup” of π1(C) is characteristic.
This can be interpreted as saying that the group theory of π1(C) “remembers” the divisor of nodal
cubic surfaces. We deduce from this group-theoretic result and some basic complex analysis that C
has no nontrivial biholomorphic automorphisms as complex analytic orbifold.
1. Introduction
Let C be the moduli space of smooth, complex cubic surfaces, regarded as a complex analytic
orbifold. In this paper we prove two main results. First, we prove that “group theory remembers
algebraic geometry”: every automorphism of the orbifold fundamental group π1(C) leaves invariant
the “divisor subgroup” corresponding to the divisor D of nodal cubics surfaces; see below for a
precise statement. We then apply this group theory result to prove the following geometric result.
Theorem 1.1. The group Aut(C) of biholomorphic automorphisms of the moduli space C of smooth
complex cubic surfaces is the trivial group.
Here automorphisms of C are with respect to the analytic orbifold structure on C as described
in Section 2.1. Theorem 1.1 complements the classical theorems
Aut(Mg) = 1
Aut(Ag) = 1
for the moduli spaces Mg of smooth curves with genus g [15] and Ag of g-dimensional principally
polarized abelian varieties. More broadly, these results all fit within a larger program studying
rigidity theorems for moduli spaces. See [7] for further context, references, and discussion.
Underpinning rigidity of Ag is the fact that it is a locally symmetric variety, meaning that it is the
quotient of a hermitian symmetric domain by a discrete group Γ of biholomorphic automorphisms1.
More specifically, Ag is the quotient of the Siegel upper half-plane Hg by Γ := Sp(2g, Z). Then
Aut(Γ\Hg) is naturally identified with the normalizer of Γ in the real semisimple Lie group Aut(Hg),
reducing the computation of Aut(Γ\Hg) to a problem about the normalizer in Sp(2g, R) of the
arithmetic group Γ.
This strategy does not work for the moduli space C. Indeed, C is not even homotopy equivalent to
a locally symmetric variety (see [2, Thm. 1.2]). However, as explained in more detail in Section 2

automorphism bf of the cover B4 ∖H associated with K. Note that bf is a bounded holomorphic
map (B4 ∖H) →C4 since its image lies in B4 ⊂C4. Since H is a locally finite union of hyperplanes,
it is a nowhere dense analytic subset of B4. Applying the Riemann Extension Theorem [13, Prop.
4.2] to each coordinate function on C4 gives that bf extends to a holomorphic map bf : B4 →C4.
Then bf(B4) ⊆B4 by continuity and the Open Mapping Theorem [13, Prop. 1.4].
Since bf is a homotopy lift and PΓ4 is the orbifold deck group of the covering (B4 ∖H) →C, it
follows that for each γ ∈PΓ4 there is a β ∈PΓ4 so that bf ◦γ = β ◦bf. The Riemann extensions
of bf ◦γ and β ◦bf from B4 ∖H to B4 then agree on a nonempty open subset of B4, so they agree
everywhere. In particular, bf normalizes the extension of PΓ4 from B4 ∖H to B4.
Applying the same argument to g = f−1, lifting to an automorphism bg of B4 ∖H with respect
to the same base point, the extension of bg to B4 has the property that bg ◦bf is the identity on a
nonempty open subset of B4. Thus bg ◦bf is the identity everywhere, and so bf ∈Aut(B4) = PU(4, 1).
In particular, bf defines an element of the normalizer of PΓ4 in PU(4, 1), and thus it descends to an
automorphism of X extending f. The theorem then follows from Corollary 2.3.
□
Remark 5.1. We spell out the alternate approach mentioned in Remark 1.4, based on the Eckardt
divisor.
Allcock–Carlson–Toledo show that a projective biflection of a smooth cubic surface is
carried under the period map to a biflection in a long root [1, Lem. 11.4]. Conversely, using Segre’s
theorem, they recall that any smooth cubic surface with a nontrivial projective automorphism
admits a biflection [1, (11.3), (11.12)]. Since the monodromy group is transitive on the long roots
[1, Thm. 11.13], the long root mirror arrangement maps to a single irreducible divisor, namely the
Eckardt divisor.
The generic stabilizer along this divisor is Z/2Z, generated by the Eckardt involution. Since
every nontrivial automorphism of a smooth cubic surface gives a biflection, and since the long roots
form a single monodromy orbit, the Eckardt divisor is the unique divisor whose generic stabilizer is
Z/2Z. Any automorphism of the orbifold must therefore preserve this divisor. One can then replace
our argument using the short root (i.e., or nodal) divisor by an analogous argument using the long
roots (i.e., Eckardt) divisor, obtaining the same conclusion about the automorphism group.
This argument has two drawbacks. First, it does not recover the group-theoretic statement that
K is characteristic (Theorem 1.2). Second, it relies on the special role of the Eckardt divisor as a
distinguished divisorial component of the stabilizer locus, and is therefore less obviously adaptable
to other moduli spaces.
