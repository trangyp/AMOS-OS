A Penalty-Free Asymmetric Nitsche’s Method for Edge Elements
Tianwei Yu∗
Abstract
We show the stability of a penalty-free asymmetric Nitsche’s method using Nédélec edge elements
for solving curl-curl-type problems with tangential Dirichlet boundary conditions imposed weakly. The
main result is an inf-sup stability estimate for the asymmetric bilinear form under an isolated patch
condition on the tetrahedral mesh. Applications to a curl-elliptic problem and a magnetic advection-
diffusion problem are discussed.

1.2
Literature review
Approaches involving bilinear forms of type (1.6) (see also (3.2)) are called Nitsche’s method. The original
idea traces back to [Nit71] for enforcing Dirichlet BCs weakly in the context of scalar elliptic problems (see
Section 3.1 for a brief recap). This approach has been extensively studied and plays a fundamental role
in cutFEM/unfitted FEM for interface problems and complex geometries [HH02, HN03, AHD12, HLZ14,
BPV20, BHLZ25] and discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods [ABCM02]. Applications have been extensively
investigated for elastic contact problems [CHR15, GSV20] and fluid-structure interaction [BF14, MLLR15,
AFFL16].
Furthermore, weak imposition of Dirichlet BCs has shown advantages in treating singularly
perturbed problems displaying boundary layers, such as advection-diffusion problems [Sch08, Bur06], drift-
diffusion problems [HY25], Brinkman-Darcy-Stokes flows [HJ09, JS10], and incompressible Navier-Stokes
equations [BH07, BMCH07].
Extension of H1-conforming nodal-element-based Nitsche’s method to H(curl)-/H(div)-conforming ele-
ments has been a topic of extensive interest. See [CHO16, RSTK20, LZZZ20] for H(curl)-/H(div)-interface
problems and [WY07, KS11, BTZ26] for H(curl)-/H(div)-based Stokes flows.
The standard Nitsche’s method involves a symmetric bilinear form augmented with a boundary L2-
penalty term; see (1.6) and (3.2). The coefficient of such penalty term must be chosen sufficiently large
to guarantee the stability of the method. A variant of Nitsche’s method uses an asymmetric bilinear form
and dispenses with the penalty term (penalty-free); see (3.6) and (3.3). This approach was first proposed
and analyzed in the context of DG methods for advection-diffusion problems [BO99] (often referred to as
the Bauman-Oden method). Its stability on the H1-conforming element was established in [Bur12] under
suitable mesh conditions. The theory was later extended to linear elasticity problems [BB16] and Brinkman-
Darcy-Stokes flows [BCC+18]. Numerical evidence suggests certain benefits of this penalty-free asymmetric
variant [CHR15, BCC+18].
Overall, Nitsche’s method provides a consistent, stable, and flexible way of enforcing boundary condi-
tions/interface conditions that stem from certain diffusion terms. An abstract framework (of the symmetric
variant) is provided in [BET22] for general variational problems.

Outline
The main result of this work is an inf-sup stability estimate of the bilinear form (3.6) (see Lemma 3.5). To

7
Concluding remarks
We have shown the inf-sup stability of the penalty-free asymmetric Nitsche’s bilinear form aasym
curl,h in (3.6) (see
Lemma 3.5). This provides a valid approach for discretizing the curl-curl operator with the tangential BCs
imposed weakly. Its symmetric variant based on asym
curl,h in (1.6) requires choosing a penalty parameter Cp. A
poor choice of Cp can deteriorate the accuracy (see Section 6.1.4). In contrast, the penalty-free asymmetric
Nitsche’s method avoids this parameter selection. Its accuracy for the two model problems (4.1) and (5.1)
is confirmed by both the analysis (Section 4.2 and 5.2) and the experiments (Section 6.1.1 and 6.2.1).
Special care should be taken with the restrictions on the edge element spaces and mesh topology. The
analysis relies on Nédélec elements of the first kind (see definition in (2.3)) and on meshes satisfying the
isolated patch condition (see Section 3.2). Instability of using Nédélec elements of the second kind is con-
firmed numerically (see Figure 3). Failure on meshes violating the isolated patch condition is also observed
(see Figure 4), while this failure can be effectively avoided by a reasonable mesh generation strategy (see
Section 6.1.3).
Overall, the penalty-free asymmetric Nitsche’s method is a viable alternative for discretizing curl-curl-
type operators. Following its H1-counterpart for Laplacian-type operators, its application extends to many
other contexts, such as interface problems and unfitted mesh methods, with potential advantages over its
symmetric variant.
Fianlly, we remark that the H(div)-counterpart of the penalty-free asymmetric Nitsche’s method for
grad-div-type operators can be analyzed in a rather similar manner.
