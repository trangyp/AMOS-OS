Ángel D. Martínez

Abstract
In this paper we solve the polarization problem for real Hilbert spaces, a long-
standing conjecture that had remained open for nearly three decades. We also
confirm that the only extremal configurations are orthonormal sets. These are
consequences of our proof of a stronger statement conjectured by Ball.

Introduction
A fundamental geometric consequences of the Hahn–Banach theorem is that every vector
in a Banach space can be normed by a continuous linear functional: for every x ∈X,
there exists f ∈X∗with ∥f∥= 1 satisfying f(x) = ∥x∥. The picture becomes far more

Before we continue let us
briefly review state of the art. To the best of our knowledge, the first estimate for
products of linear forms over the reals appeared in the work of Marcus and Minc [19],
in connection with their study of permanent inequalities. Marcus later returned to this
question, obtaining an eigenvalue-dependent lower bound [18]. It also appears in the work
of Ryan and Turett [27]. Noting that, the problem seems to have been brought to their
attention by Sarantopoulos.
Several authors have since contributed to the real polarization problem, either by proving
the conjecture in special cases or by obtaining general upper bounds for cn(Rn). One line
of results seeks bounds of the form cn(Rn) ≤(Cn)n/2, with different absolute constants C.
Litvak, Milman and Schechtman obtained such a bound with C = 2eγ ≈3.56 (where γ
denotes the Euler–Mascheroni constant). This was refined by García-Vázquez and Villa,
who obtained sharper finite-dimensional estimates with the same asymptotic behavior.
Révész and Sarantopoulos obtained the estimate cn(Rn) ≤2n/2−1nn/2, which Frenkel
improved to cn(Rn) ≤33/2e−1nn/2. Finally, Muñoz-Fernández, Sarantopoulos and Seoane-
Sepúlveda proved the asymptotically sharper bound cn(Rn) ≤n2n/4nn/2. For more details
we refer the reader to [17, 11, 26, 10] and [21], respectively.
