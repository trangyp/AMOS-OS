A phenomenological model of the magnetic field re-emergence in
magnetars and discrepancy between the kinematic and
characteristic ages

and Sergei B. Popov 2*
1

Abstract: Robust age measurements for isolated neutron stars (NSs) are not easily available. That
is why, often the characteristic age τch = P/2 ˙P is used as a proxy. Here P is the spin period of the
NS and ˙P is the time derivative of P. Additional assumptions related to the initial properties and
spin-down evolution are made to derive τch. As a result, it is expected that τch is an upper limit
for the real age τreal. Recently, Chrimes et al. presented measurements of kinematic ages τkin for
several magnetars. Surprisingly, for the majority of these sources τkin > τch. We present a simple
model including a realistic approximation for the magnetic field decay in magnetars and a simple
phenomenological description of the field re-emergence after an episode of fallback after the birth of
a NS. We demonstrate that this simple model can explain the observed relation τkin > τch for realistic
sets of parameters.
Keywords: neutron stars; magnetic field; magnetars
1. Introduction
Young neutron stars (NSs) manifest themselves as sources with diverse properties that
reflect a variety of their initial properties and evolutionary paths [1–3]. In particular, non-
trivial behavior can be a result of initial fallback [4] and further magnetic field evolution
[5].
The magnetic field evolution is especially important for magnetars: anomalous X-ray
Pulsars (AXPs) and Soft γ-ray Repeaters (SGRs) [6]. Recently, Chrimes et al. [7] used new
observations with the Hubble Space Telescope and James Webb Space Telescope to measure
proper motions of several magnetars. The measurements allowed them to determine
the kinematic ages of these objects. Altogether, Chrimes et al. [7] present kinematic age
estimates for nine magnetars. Interestingly, for eight objects in the sample, the kinematic
ages, τkin, are greater than their characteristic ages τch ≡P/2 ˙P. Here P is the spin period
of a NS and ˙P — its first time derivative.
If correctly determined, a kinematic age represents the actual age (τreal) of the NS.
On the other hand, for the standard evolution according to the magneto-dipole formula
with a constant effective dipolar magnetic field, τch is an upper limit for τreal as the initial
spin period P0 can be not much smaller than the present-day period. If the magnetic
field significantly decays, as is usually assumed in the case of magnetars, we also expect
τch ≫τreal even for P0 ≪P.
The inequality τch < τreal indicates that the effective external dipolar magnetic field,
which is responsible for the spin-down, is growing. Potentially, this can occur due to
changes (growth) of the angle χ between the spin axis and the magnetic dipole axes [8].
Or, due to the dynamics of the field in the interiors of the NS [2,9,10]. Finally, the external

2. Model
In this study, the spin-down of an NS is calculated following a very basic approach
based on a modified spin-down formula:
P ˙P = 4π2B2R6
Ic3
(k0 + k1 sin2 χ).
(1)
Here, R = 106 cm is the NS radius, B(t) is the surface equatorial magnetic field, I =
1045 g cm2 is the moment of inertia. The values of the coefficients are taken as: k0 = 1,
k1 = 1.2 [15]. The magnetic obliquity angle is assumed to be constant χ = 45◦.
As we want to apply a model for the re-emerging magnetic field, we need to specify
B(t). Instead of direct calculations of the external field growth [11–13], we suggest a simple
equation, which potentially can represent the field growth due to different reasons, not only
re-emergence after a fallback episode (however, below we focus only on this possibility):
B(t) = Bi + (Bc(t) −Bi) × exp(−tcr/t).
(2)
Here, Bc(t) is the value of the field at the bottom of the crust. Initially, it is equal to the
total buried field Bmax. Without field decay, this is the maximum value that can be reached.
Bi is the initial external field; i.e., this is the field after the fallback episode is over. In
our calculations below, we typically assume Bi = 109 G and Bmax = 1015 G , as we are
interested in the evolution of magnetars. Finally, tcr is the characteristic time scale of field
growth.
To take into account the magnetic field decay, we apply an equation from [14]:
Bc(t) = Bmax
exp(−t/τOhm)
1 + (τOhm/τHall)(1 −exp(−t/τOhm).
(3)
Here, τOhm is the Ohmic time scale. And τHall is the Hall time scale. In the calculations,
we typically assumed that τOhm = 106 yrs and τhall = 104 yrs for Bc(t = 0) = Bmax. The
magnetic field evolution for several parameter values is shown in Fig. 1. Note that decay is
regulated by the value of the decaying field in the crust, Bc. This narrows the applicability
of our model only to the case of the field buried in a fallback. For other models of the
growth of the effective field, it is necessary to modify the equations.
In our calculations, we do not take into account the Hall attractor [16]. This stage is
expected to occur after ≈3 e-foldings of the field decay [17]. Substituting this condition into
eq. (3), we obtain the characteristic time of transition to the Hall attractor tHA = 1.7 × 105 yrs
for the initial field Bmax = 1015 G. This value exceeds the typical age of the magnetars under
study. But, if we take smaller values of τHall, accounting for the Hall attractor becomes
necessary. We discuss this in detail in Sec. 4.

4. Discussion
As our model is not based on detailed calculations of the field diffusion, its applications
are limited. In particular, we do not attempt to fit the parameters of individual sources
(magnetic field, amount of fallback ∆M, etc.). Some objects can be at the stage of field
re-emergence. In others, this stage can be over already, or the fallback was negligible and
the field had not been submerged. Our main goal is to demonstrate that for a natural choice
of parameters, it is possible to explain the situation where τch < τreal.
At least for one source in the sample from [7] — AXP 1E 2259+586 — it is observed that
τch > τreal. Obviously, this source can be explained naturally without invoking magnetic
field re-emergence. However, it is necessary to note that characteristic ages of magnetars
are a subject of significant uncertainty, as the first spin period derivative of these sources is
a subject of strong fluctuations. E.g., τch of PSR J1622 presented in [7] is taken from McGill
catalogue [21]. It is based on ˙P measurements from [22]. However, in the ATNF catalogue
the period derivative is an order of magnitude smaller [23]. If we use this value, then for
PSR J1622 we obtain τch > τkin (see Fig. 4).
We also face some difficulties in interpreting SGR 1806-20 as it requires extreme
parameters and some fine tuning. On the one hand, for this source τch < τkin and it

In our calculations, we neglect the stage of the Hall attractor. As originally proposed in
[16,17], this stage can be reached after the dipolar field has decayed by a factor ∼(e2 – e3).
In the case of magnetar-scale initial magnetic field and depending on the value of τHall, this
can happen in a few ×103 – 105 yrs.
We made additional runs of the code, accounting for the Hall attractor stage for the
same set of Bmax, Bi, τOhm, and the same three values of tcr. For τHall = 104 yrs, as we
mentioned above, the transition to the Hall attractor happens at t = 1.7 × 105 yrs. Thus,
in this case, the inclusion of this stage does not influence our results. If τHall = 103 yrs
then the transition happens at t = 1.9 × 104 yrs. This marginally modifies our results, but
leaves the main conclusions untouched, as we do not intend to fit all individual sources.
Thus, we conclude that as soon as the goal is to explain active magnetars, i.e., the sources
which, by definition, did not reach the Hall attractor stage, its inclusion is not necessary. It
is necessary only to ensure that the ages of objects in the model do not exceed the transition
time for the chosen set of parameters, in particular Bmax, τHall, tcr.
Finally, we have to make a brief comment on the very early behavior of the braking
index in our model. In Fig. 3, we neglected this stage as objects of interest are older than the
duration of this early phase. The early evolution of n, ˙P, and ¨P for tcr = 104 yrs is shown in
Fig. 5. Focusing on the early stages, we truncate this plot at t = 104 yrs. After this time, for
the chosen set of parameters, the field evolution is dominated by decay.
The evolution of ˙P (middle panel in Fig. 5) is rather obvious. At first, while t ≪tcr,
the period derivative is small as B(t) ≈Bi. Then, the period derivative grows as the field
starts to re-emerge. Finally, the growth saturates as τHall is of the same value as tcr.

5. Conclusions
In this study, we presented a simple model of the evolution of magnetars’ external
magnetic fields, aiming to explain the relation between kinematic and characteristic ages
reported by Chrimes et al. [7]. The key ingredient of our model is a simplified one-
parameter description of the magnetic field re-emergence after an episode of fallback. In
our framework, we reproduced successfully τkin > τch under the assumption that the
magnetic field of some magnetars has been significantly submerged due to fallback. The
high negative braking indices reported for several magnetars may indicate that they are
still in the stage of field re-emergence.
