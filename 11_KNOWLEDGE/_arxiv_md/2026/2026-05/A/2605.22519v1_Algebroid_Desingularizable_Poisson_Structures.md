ALGEBROID DESINGULARIZABLE POISSON STRUCTURES
SHANE RANKIN
Abstract. We introduce algebroid desingularizable Poisson manifolds, a class of Poisson manifolds
induced by symplectic Lie algebroids with almost-injective anchors, generalizing structures includ-
ing log-symplectic, bm-symplectic, E-symplectic manifolds, and hypersurface algebroids. We show
that the dual of real, finite-dimensional, non-abelian, reductive Lie algebras never admit such al-
gebroids. We finish by giving two infinite families of 2-step nilpotent Lie algebras, one of which is
desingularizable, and one of which is not.
1. Introduction and Results
Generically symplectic Poisson structures arise naturally across mathematical physics. The dy-
namics of pseudo-Riemannian manifolds [8], Painlevé equations [7], and the restricted three-body
problem [13] each produce Poisson structures that are necessarily degenerate: the dual 2-form to the
Poisson bivector does not exist as a smooth section of the cotangent bundle. In favorable situations,
however, one can construct a new vector bundle whose sections carry a Lie bracket analogous to the
Lie bracket on vector fields, and on which this dual 2-form exists as a smooth, closed, nondegenerate
element.
Such a bundle is a symplectic Lie algebroid.
Symplectic Lie algebroids were introduced
by Nest and Tsygan [15], who showed they admit Fedosov deformation quantization and canonically
induce a Poisson structure on the base manifold.
Subsequent work has shown that many important families of Poisson manifolds arise this way: log-
symplectic manifolds, bm-Poisson manifolds, and E-manifolds each admit a symplectic Lie algebroid
realizing their Poisson structure, see [5, 6, 14]. In the physics literature, these structures have been
studied under the name quasi-symplectic manifolds in connection with deformation quantization and
BRST theory [12]. More recently, deformation quantization of the underlying Poisson structure via
Toeplitz operators on the inducing algebroid has been developed in [4]. In [1], Bischoff and Witte
study the case in which the anchor map of the algebroid is generically an isomorphism onto the
tangent bundle, and extract substantial information about the underlying Poisson structure from the
algebroid’s symplectic geometry.
The common thread running through all of these examples is that the anchor map is injective on
an open dense subset of the base manifold. Such algebroids are called almost-injective, and we call
a Poisson manifold algebroid desingularizable or just desingularizable if it is induced by a symplec-
tic Lie algebroid with an almost-injective anchor. This notion unifies the examples above under a
single definition and raises a natural question: which Poisson manifolds are desingularizable? We
investigate this question for linear Poisson structures, that is, for duals of finite-dimensional real Lie
algebras equipped with the Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau Poisson structure.
A necessary condition for
desingularizability is that the Lie algebra is not nonabelian reductive; we prove the following:
Theorem. If g is a real, finite-dimensional, non-abelian, reductive Lie algebra, then g∗is non-
desingularizable.
The proof proceeds by showing that the existence of a desingularizing algebroid forces the existence
of a nonzero abelian ideal of the Lie algebra T ∗
0 g∗, which is impossible when g is semisimple, and which
persists to the reductive case by restricting to the semisimple part. In dimensions three or less, non-
abelian reductivity turns out to be the only obstruction:
Corollary. In dimension ≤3, g∗is desingularizable if and only if g is not semisimple.

2.2. Desingularization Algebroids.
Definition 4. A Lie algebroid (A, ρ, [·, ·]) over M is said to be almost-injective if the anchor map
ρ : A →TM is injective on an open dense set of M, or equivalently, if the induced map on sections
(which we denote with the same name by abuse of notation) ρ : Γ(A) →Γ(TM) is injective.
Proposition 5. If (A, ρA, [·, ·]) is almost-injective, then the maps on sections ∧kρ : Γ(Vk A) →
Γ(Vk(TM)) are injective for all k ≥0.
Proof. Let S := {p ∈M|ρp is injective}, and fix k ≥0, as well as X ∈ker(∧kρ). If p ∈S, then (∧kρ)p
is injective as well, as it’s the exterior power of an injective linear map between finite-dimensional
vector spaces. The condition that X ∈ker(∧kρ) in conjunction with injectivity of ∧kρ at points of
S forces X to vanish on S as well. Since X is a smooth function vanishing on an open dense set, it
must vanish globally, hence ∧kρ is injective as a map of sections.
□

3.2. Low-Dimensional Lie algebras. In dimension 3 or less, we can exactly classify which Lie
algebras are desingularizable:
Dimension 1: The only one dimensional Lie algebra (up to isomorphism) is the abelian one, and by
Proposition 9 it is desingularizable.
Dimension 2: Up to isomorphism, there are two Lie algebras of dimension two. The abelian one is
desingularizable, again by Proposition 9. The other Lie algebra is also desingularizable, and is in fact
b-symplectic. An explicit construction of a desingularizing algebroid is provided in [5, Example 1.1].
Dimension 3: We claim that all non-semisimple Lie algebras of dimension 3 are desingularizable, and
we can prove this uniformly.
Lemma 25. Let g be a non-abelian Lie algebra, and denote by π the KKS Poisson bivector on g∗.
Then the set of points µ ∈g∗such that πµ = 0 is nowhere dense in g∗.
Proof. By definition, π vanishes at µ ∈g∗if and only if for all x, y ∈g we have that ⟨µ, [x, y]⟩= 0,
and so µ must lie in (g/[g, g])∗⊂g∗. Since g is non-abelian this subspace is a proper subspace, which
is closed and has empty interior, thus nowhere dense.
□
