A condensed proof of the pro-étale
and étale exodromy theorems
Remy van Dobben de Bruyn
Abstract
The exodromy correspondence of Barwick, Glasman, and Haine computes construct-
ible sheaves of spaces on a scheme 𝑋as an ∞-category of continuous functors from
the profinite category Gal(𝑋). Viewing Gal(𝑋) instead as a condensed category, this
was extended by Wolf to an exodromy correspondence for pro-étale sheaves. Using
the condensed perspective from the outset, we give a quick and self-contained proof
of the pro-étale exodromy theorem. This is used to extract an exodromy theorem for
(Postnikov complete) étale sheaves that does not yet appear in the literature, which
is closely related to Lurie’s work on ultracategories. Finally, we use this to give a
new proof of the constructible étale exodromy correspondence of Barwick, Glasman,
and Haine. Without additional effort, our method removes the qcqs hypotheses
on the schemes, and gives versions for sheaves with coefficients in more general
∞-categories. Finally, we refine the methods to obtain a 𝜅-condensed statement
whenever 𝜅> |O𝑋(𝑈)| for every affine open 𝑈⊆𝑋.
Introduction
Background
If 𝑋is a reasonable topological space, the monodromy correspondence gives an equivalence
between locally constant sheaves on 𝑋with values in a presentable ∞-category ℰand
the functor category Fun(Π∞(𝑋),ℰ). For a scheme 𝑋, a similar description is given for
locally constant sheaves of finite sets (or, more generally, 𝜋-finite spaces) using the étale
fundamental group 𝜋et
1 (𝑋) [SGA1, Exp. V], [SGA3II, Exp. X, §6], [BS15, §7] (resp. étale
homotopy type Πet
∞(𝑋) [AM86, Fri82, Hoy18]).
This categorical description of locally constant sheaves on a topological space was exten-
ded to constructible sheaves by MacPherson (unpublished, first presented in Treumann’s
thesis [Tre07, Tre09]), and Lurie obtained an ∞-categorical version [HA, Thm. A.9.3].
An étale version was proven by Barwick, Glasman, and Haine [BGH18, Thm. 12.1.6],
who coined the term exodromy correspondence:

functors from the Galois category of 𝑋to 𝜋-finite spaces.

Here, the Galois category Gal(𝑋) is a pro-object in 𝜋-finite layered ∞-categories whose
‘materialisation’ (limit in Cat∞) is the 1-category Pts∗(𝑋et) = Fun∗(Sh(𝑋et), Set) of points
of the étale topos Sh(𝑋et), and continuous functors means functors in Pro(Cat∞), where S𝜋

Any profinite ∞-category, such as Gal(𝑋), can be viewed as a consensed (or pyknotic)
∞-category by taking the limit in Cond(Cat∞) of the diagram of ‘discrete’ ∞-categories,
as in [BGH18, §13.5]. Writing Gal(𝑋) for the condensed ∞-category defined by Gal(𝑋),
Wolf [Wol22, Cor. 1.2] extended the theorem above to a pro-étale exodromy theorem:
Theorem (Wolf). —- Let 𝑋be a qcqs scheme. Then there is an exodromy correspondence
Shhyp(𝑋proet, S)
∼−→Functs(Gal(𝑋), Cond(S)).
Here Cond(S) is the condensed ∞-category taking a profinite space 𝑆to Cond(S)/𝑆(equi-
valently, Sh(ProFin/𝑆, S)), and continuous functors are functors of condensed ∞-categories.
Main results
In this paper, we take up a condensed perspective from the outset to give independent
(and, in the author’s opinion, more geometric, more direct, and more uniform) proofs of
the theorems of Barwick–Glasman–Haine and Wolf, as well as a number of generalisations
and variants.
Using a direct definition of the condensed Galois category Gal(𝑋) (see Construction
of the Galois category further down in this introduction), we obtain a quick geometric
proof of the following generalisation of Wolf’s theorem:
Theorem 1 (Theorem 3.1.4). —- Let 𝑋be a scheme, and let ℰbe an ∞-category that has small
limits. Then there is an exodromy correspondence
Ex: Shhyp(𝑋proet,ℰ)
∼−→Functs(Gal(𝑋), Cond(ℰ)),

Given a qcqs scheme 𝑋, Barwick–Glasman–Haine construct the Galois category Gal(𝑋)
as a pro-object in (layered) ∞-categories [BGH18, Def. 12.1.3]. As a functor from 𝜋-finite

Such objects have not been classically studied in algebraic geometry (although this could
be interesting to work out). For a working algebraic geometer, to get a more concrete grip
on the exodromy correspondence, it is desirable to have a more geometric description of
the profinite category Gal(𝑋). This is the starting point of this paper.
As a first step, [BGH18, 12.1.5] gives a concrete description of the underlying category of
the profinite category Gal(𝑋) (obtained by taking the limit in Cat∞): this is given by the
category of points Pts∗(𝑋et) of the étale topos, which by [SGA4II, Exp. VIII, Thm. 7.9] is
equivalent to the opposite of the category of strictly Henselian local rings with a weakly
étale map to 𝑋. It is classical that an étale sheaf ℱcomes equipped with cospecialisation
maps ℱ¯𝑥→ℱ¯𝑦whenever 𝑥∈{𝑦} [SGA4II, Exp. VIII, 7.7]. Thus, ignoring continuity
issues, the étale exodromy functor of Theorem 2 and Theorem 3 should take ℱto the
functor Pts∗(𝑋et) →Set taking ¯𝑥to the stalk ℱ¯𝑥, and taking a map Spec Osh

To prove an exodromy correspondence via this approach, what is missing is a description
of the profinite topology on Gal(𝑋). We note that Gal(𝑋) becomes a category internal
to Stone spaces, so we need to describe a profinite set of objects and a profinite set of
morphisms with a continuous composition operation. While [BGH18, 0.0.2] explains the
profinite topology on Hom(¯𝑥, ¯𝑦) for geometric points ¯𝑥, ¯𝑦→𝑋, it is not clear how to
describe a Stone space of all objects or of all morphisms in Gal(𝑋).
The reason this is difficult to do is that the categorically/homotopically meaningful
(2, 1)-category of Stone categories is a localisation of category objects in Stone spaces
along the collection of internal functors that are (internally) fully faithful and essentially
surjective (see [Lur09, Thm. 1.2.13] for a version of this statement in an ∞-topos). Since
surjections of Stone spaces do not always split, this means that such representatives are
not unique, and to do this for Gal(𝑋) relies on making choices — similar to how a choice
of pro-étale hypercover by w-strictly local schemes leads to a model for the pro-étale ho-
motopy type, as suggested in [BS15, Rmk. 4.2.9] and worked out in [HRS23, Appendix A],
[Wol25, Prop. 7.3.1.11], and [HHL+25, Rmk. 3.20].
Instead of addressing this question directly, we opt to work with condensed categories
in favour of Stone categories, since internal category theory is better understood in an
(∞-)topos. We replace the profinite definition of Gal(𝑋) by a condensed one, defined as
Gal(𝑋) : ProFinop →Cat

Such a definition was already suggested by [BGH18, Ex. 13.5.4] (where the locally coherent
condition is missing). A detailed comparison between the profinite and condensed Galois
categories appeared in [Wol25, Prop. 7.1.2.8] (but see Remark 2.1.11), which was partially
motivated by the present paper (see Acknowledgements) as well as by [HHL+25].
While this paper was in preparation, the above definition was also used as the definition
in [HHL+25, Def. 3.24] and [Mai25, Rec. 2.2.30]. In particular, while the results of the
present paper are separate from those in [HHL+25], the underlying philosophy is very
similar.

On cardinality bounds
This paper heavily uses the language of condensed mathematics. Working with sheaves on
the category of profinite sets inevitably leads to set-theoretic issues. We resolve these by
restricting to profinite sets that are small with respect to some sufficiently large cardinal 𝜅.
We prove a more precise version of Theorem 1 taking these cardinals into account; see
Theorem 3.1.4 and Definition 1.1.7 for a precise formulation. We do a little extra work
to avoid additional hypotheses on the cardinal 𝜅: it need neither be regular nor a strong
limit cardinal. In particular, we do not have access to enough w-projective objects (see
Remark 1.2.2) in the pro-étale site. For example, this allows us to show that a version of
Theorem 1 holds in the light condensed setting as long as 𝒪𝑋(𝑈) is countable for every
affine open 𝑈⊆𝑋.
While it appears that the 𝜅-condensed category Gal𝜅(𝑋) of a scheme 𝑋depends on a
choice of auxiliary cardinal 𝜅, we prove an accessibility result in §4.2 for the functor
Gal: ProFinop →Cat, which implies that knowing Gal𝜅(𝑋) for a single (sufficiently
large) value of 𝜅determines Gal𝜆(𝑋) for every (sufficiently large) cardinal 𝜆.

Lower versus higher categories
The object of study in this paper is ℰ-valued sheaves on the étale and pro-étale sites of a
scheme 𝑋. We have separated the geometric and (1-categorical) site-theoretic arguments
on 𝑋et and 𝑋proet (§1–2) from the higher categorical arguments about ℰ-valued sheaves
(§3–4 and Appendix A). In particular, the proof of Theorem 5 is entirely 1-categorical.
Readers who are primarily interested in Set- and Ab-valued sheaves should be able to
make the required modifications in §3–4 to avoid higher category theory altogether. On
the other hand, we chose to present the proof with the most general coefficients where
it applies. For instance, applying this general version when ℰis the stable ∞-category
𝐷(Z) gives derived statements; see Example 3.1.7 and Example 3.2.10.

1. Preliminaries
1.1 Cardinality bounds
The main results of this paper are formulated in the language of condensed mathematics

1.2 Review of acyclic and w-strictly local schemes
Recall the following definition:
Definition 1.2.1. —- A scheme 𝑊is acyclic if it is quasi-compact and every étale surjection

assume that 𝑊is quasi-compact. We say that a scheme 𝑊is w-strictly local if it is acyclic

3. Exodromy theorems
In this section, we prove the three main exodromy theorems. We start in §3.1 with
the pro-étale exodromy theorem, using the classification of w-strictly local schemes of
Corollary 2.4.7 and Corollary 2.4.9. In §3.2, we use this to extract an étale exodromy
theorem, by identifying the essential image of the pullback Sh(𝑋et) →Sh(𝑋proet). This
