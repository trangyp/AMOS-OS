A PROOF OF ESTERLE’S CONJECTURE ON NEGATIVE
POWERS OF HILBERT-SPACE CONTRACTIONS
THOMAS RANSFORD
Abstract. We establish the following result, confirming a conjecture
of Jean Esterle. For each closed subset E of the unit circle of Lebesgue
measure zero, there exists a positive sequence un →∞with the following
property: if T is a contraction on a Hilbert space such that σ(T) ⊂E
and ∥T −n∥= O(un) as n →∞, then T is a unitary operator.
A key tool used in the proof is a result generalizing the well-known
fact that closed subsets E of the real axis of Lebesgue measure zero are
removable for bounded holomorphic functions. We show that such sets
remain removable even for certain unbounded holomorphic functions of
moderate growth near E, where the notion of ‘moderate’ depends on E.
1. Introduction and statement of main results
Let T be a bounded linear operator on a complex Hilbert space.
We
denote the operator norm and spectrum of T by ∥T∥and σ(T) respectively.
We say T is a contraction if ∥T∥≤1.
Our goal in this article is to establish the following theorem, which con-
firms a conjecture of Jean Esterle [4, Conjecture 1.3].
Theorem 1.1. For each closed subset E of the unit circle T of Lebesgue
measure zero, there exists a positive sequence un →∞with the following
property: if T is a contraction on a Hilbert space such that σ(T) ⊂E and
∥T −n∥= O(un) as n →∞, then T is a unitary operator.
Versions of this result have long been known for specific sets E: for ex-
ample countable compact sets [5], certain Cantor sets [1], and Carleson sets
[2, 3]. In each case, the corresponding sequence (un) was explicitly identi-
fied. It is also known that no single sequence (un) can work simultaneously
for all compact sets E of measure zero [3]. Also the condition that E have
measure zero is optimal: Theorem 1.1 breaks down for every closed set E of
positive measure [4]. Finally, the result also breaks down if ‘Hilbert space’
is replaced by ‘Banach space’ [2]. For a detailed account of the background
to Esterle’s conjecture, we refer to [4].
