Ab initio correlations between neutrinoless and two-neutrino double-beta decays in 48Ca
X. Lian,1 C. R. Ding,2,3 C. L. Bai,1, ∗and J. M. Yao2,3, 4, †
1College of Physics, Sichuan University, Chengdu 610065, China
2School of Physics and Astronomy, Sun Yat-sen University, Zhuhai 519082, P.R. China

(Dated: May 20, 2026)
We develop a novel ab initio in-medium no-core conﬁguration-interaction (IM-NCCI) framework for nuclear
charge-exchange processes by combining the in-medium similarity renormalization group with chiral nuclear
Hamiltonians, and apply it to the 2νββ and 0νββ decays of 48Ca. This framework reproduces the locations of
several main resonance peaks in the Gamow–Teller (GT) strength distribution for the 48Ca →48Sc transition.
The cumulative GT strength indicates missing contributions from two-body weak currents, corresponding to
an eﬀective quenching factor of q ≃0.84. Incorporating this quenching yields a 2νββ nuclear matrix element
(NME) in excellent agreement with experiment. Applying the same framework to 0νββ decay, and including the
contribution from short-range operators, we obtain a total NME of M0ν = 1.00–2.02. Using 34 non-implausible
chiral Hamiltonians, we establish from ﬁrst principles strong linear correlations between the 0νββ NME and the
NMEs governing 2νββ decay and double GT transitions. Combining these correlation relations within the 95%
conﬁdence level with the experimental 2νββ-decay data yields a constrained prediction of M0ν = 1.30–1.65.
This work establishes IM-NCCI as a complementary ab initio framework for nuclear weak decays and opens
a pathway toward constraining 0νββ NMEs in heavier candidate nuclei using experimentally accessible 2νββ-
decay data.
Introduction.−
Neutrino oscillation experiments have es-
tablished that neutrinos are massive, providing direct evidence
for physics beyond the Standard Model [1–4]. However, the
questions about the nature and absolute masses of neutrinos
remain unresolved. Neutrinoless double-β (0νββ) decay pro-
vides a unique probe of these questions. It is a second-order
weak process in which a nucleus (A, Z) decays into (A, Z + 2)
with the emission of two electrons and no neutrinos [5, 6]. Its
observation would establish lepton-numberviolation and have
profound implications for the origin of the matter–antimatter
asymmetry in the Universe.
If 0νββ decay is dominated by light-Majorana-neutrino ex-
change, its half-life can be related to the eﬀective Majorana
neutrino mass, ⟨mββ⟩= P
i U2
eimi through a nuclear matrix ele-
ment (NME) M0ν, where mi are the light-neutrino masses and
Uei are elements of the Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata
ﬂavor-mixing matrix. Extracting ⟨mββ⟩from a measured half-
life, or translating experimental limits into constraints on neu-
trino mass, thus requires reliable knowledge of the NME.
Accurate calculations of the M0ν are therefore essential for
both the interpretation of current searches and the design
of next-generation experiments [7]. Despite decades of ef-
fort, theoretical uncertainties in 0νββ NMEs remain substan-
tial, largely due to the model dependence of nuclear many-
body approaches [6–8]. These uncertainties are diﬃcult to
reduce within purely phenomenological frameworks, where
each model relies on its own assumptions, eﬀective interac-
tions, and uncontrolled approximations.
Ab initio nuclear methods based on interactions derived
from chiral eﬀective ﬁeld theory [9–11] provide a system-
atically improvable framework for nuclear structure and de-
cay [12–18]. Within this framework, recent advances have
enabled the ﬁrst multi-method calculations of 0νββ NMEs
in light- and medium-mass nuclei [19–21]. As the lightest
experimentally relevant 0νββ candidate and the isotope with
the largest Q value among known 2νββ emitters, 48Ca [22–
24] provides a unique benchmark for nuclear many-body
methods [25–28].
Accordingly, the in-medium generator
coordinate method (IM-GCM) [29], the valence-space in-
medium similarity renormalization group (VS-IMSRG) [30],
and coupled-cluster theory [31] have all been applied to 48Ca
using the same chiral nuclear interaction and decay opera-
tors, enabling direct cross-method comparisons beyond the
reach of phenomenological models. These benchmark stud-
ies demonstrated that the resulting NMEs agree within the
naively estimated theoretical uncertainties. More recently, a
rather full uncertainty quantiﬁcation has been performed for
the NME of 76Ge based on the IM-GCM and VS-IMSRG ap-
proaches [32]. Moreover, the VS-IMSRG has been extended
to most experimentally relevant candidate nuclei [33]. Never-
theless, uncertainties of approximately a factor of two remain,
primarily due to truncations in the nuclear interactions and
many-body model spaces. These truncations are systemati-
cally improvable in principle, but reducing them in practice
poses substantial computational challenges [32, 33].
To reduce the existing uncertainties, recent studies have
explored correlations between 0νββ NMEs and experimen-
tally accessible observables, including double Gamow–Teller
(DGT) transitions [34–38], double-γ transitions [39, 40],
nucleon–nucleon scattering observables [41], high-energy
probes of nuclear shape [42], and low-energy nuclear struc-
ture properties [36, 43, 44]. Among these observables, two-
neutrino double-β (2νββ) decay is of particular interest be-
cause its transition operator shares important similarities with
that of 0νββ decay, while its half-life is being measured with
increasingly high precision [45, 46]. Studies based on phe-
nomenological nuclear models have suggested possible corre-
lations between the corresponding matrix elements [37, 43].

Whether such correlations persist in ﬁrst-principles calcula-
tions, however, remains an open question. Addressing this
question is nontrivial, as it requires an ab initio framework
capable of describing both decay modes consistently and reli-
ably.
In this Letter, we develop a novel ab initio in-medium no-
core conﬁguration-interaction (IM-NCCI) framework for gen-
eral nuclear weak processes. In this approach, the in-medium
similarity renormalization group (IMSRG) is employed to de-
couple the reference state of the initial nucleus from its ex-
citations, while the NCCI method is used to describe the ﬁ-
nal states of the daughter nucleus. We demonstrate the pre-
dictive power of the framework through applications to GT
transition strengths and to the NMEs governing the 2νββ and
0νββ decays of 48Ca. Applying the same framework to 0νββ
decay, and including contributions from short-range opera-
tors, we obtain a total NME of M0ν = 1.00–2.02. Using
34 non-implausible chiral Hamiltonians, we further uncover
a strong linear correlation between M2ν and M0ν. Combining
this correlation with the experimental value of M2ν yields a
constrained prediction for the 0νββ NME, M0ν = 1.30–1.65.
This work provides a ﬁrst-principles foundation for constrain-
ing 0νββ NMEs in heavier candidate nuclei using experimen-
tally accessible 2νββ-decay data.

We have developed a novel ab initio in-
medium no-core conﬁguration-interaction (IM-NCCI) frame-
work based on chiral NN+3N interactions and applied it to
Gamow–Teller (GT) transition strengths and to the nuclear
matrix elements (NMEs) governing 2νββ and 0νββ decays in
48Ca. This framework enables, for the ﬁrst time from a ﬁrst-
principles perspective, a systematic investigation of the corre-
lation between the NMEs of 2νββ and 0νββ decays. The lo-
cations of several main resonance peaks in the Gamow–Teller
(GT) strength distribution for

48

Applying the same framework to 0νββ decay, we obtain
long-range NMEs consistent with previous ab initio calcula-

tion of the IM-NCCI framework and the uncertainty in the
low-energy constant gNN
ν . Using 34 non-implausible chiral
Hamiltonians, we uncover strong linear correlations between
M0ν and the NMEs governing 2νββ and double GT decays.
Combining these correlations with the experimental 2νββ-

M0ν = 1.30–1.65, with substantially reduced uncertainty.
This work establishes IM-NCCI as a complementary ab
initio many-body framework for describing general nuclear
weak processes. It also provides a foundation for constraining
0νββ NMEs in heavier candidate nuclei using experimentally
accessible information from 2νββ decay and double Gamow–
Teller transitions. Future work will focus on a quantitative
treatment of two-body-current contributions to both 2νββ and
0νββ decays, as well as to other weak processes, including
superallowed Fermi transitions, for which sub-percent theo-
retical precision is required for meaningful tests of CKM uni-
tarity [78].

The convergence behavior of NMEs and uncertainty estimation
Here we focus on the uncertainties in the NMEs arising
from the importance cutoﬀκmin, the ﬁnite single-particle basis

excitation-rank truncation.

We next estimate the uncertainties associated with the
choice of reference state and the particle-hole excitation-rank
truncation.
Figures 6(a) and 6(b) show, respectively, the
reference-state dependence of M0ν

adopt a conservative estimate for the lower-end uncertainty

LR,low = 0.82(20). The results for
the short-range transition operator are shown in Figs. 7 and 8.

For M2ν, we obtain M2ν = 0.068 MeV−1 from the cal-

are expected to be less important because they are suppressed
by larger energy denominators.
