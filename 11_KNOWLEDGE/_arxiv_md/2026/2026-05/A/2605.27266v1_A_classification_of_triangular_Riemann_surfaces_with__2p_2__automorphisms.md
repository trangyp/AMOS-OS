A CLASSIFICATION OF TRIANGULAR RIEMANN SURFACES
WITH 2p2 AUTOMORPHISMS
SEBASTI´AN REYES-CAROCCA AND YAZMIN RIVERA NENE
Abstract. In this article we provide a classification and description of compact Riemann surfaces admitting a
triangular action of a group of order 2p2, where p is an odd prime number. We obtain that all such Riemann
surfaces are isomorphic to curves defined over the rational numbers. As a by-product, we derive a classification
of orientably-regular hypermaps whose orientation-preserving automorphism group has order 2p2.
1. Introduction
The theory of compact Riemann surfaces has long been a central research area in pure mathematics, serving
to bridge diverse branches such as topology, geometry, algebra, combinatorics and complex analysis. The origins
of this subject can be traced back to the nineteenth century, with seminal contributions by Riemann, Klein and
Hurwitz, among others.
Throughout the development of the area, several significant progress has been achieved; however, two results
stand out as fundamental milestones. The first one is the celebrated Riemann-Roch Theorem, which implies that
the category of compact Riemann surfaces is equivalent to that of smooth complex projective algebraic curves.
The second one is the so-called Uniformization Theorem, which implies that compact Riemann surfaces of genus
at least two are isomorphic to quotients of the complex upper half-plane by the action of discrete groups of
automorphisms of it, known as Fuchsian groups.
Further fundamental results concern automorphisms of compact Riemann surfaces.
The finiteness of the
automorphism group for genus g ⩾2 was established by Schwarz in [40], and it was Hurwitz in [28] who showed
that its order is bounded above by 84(g −1). Much later, Greenberg in [21] proved that every finite group arises
as a group of automorphisms of some compact Riemann surface. This result introduced an additional ingredient
into the theory, namely, the role played by finite groups and the significance of their algebraic properties in the
geometry of the surfaces on which they act.
Let Mg denote the moduli space of isomorphism classes of compact Riemann surfaces of genus g ⩾2, which
is a complex analytic space of dimension 3g −3. Group actions on compact Riemann surfaces are closely related
with the singularities of Mg in the following sense: for g ⩾4 it is known that
Sing(Mg) = {[S] ∈Mg ∶Aut(S) is non-trivial}.
Moreover, according to [7] and [18], the locus consisting of all those Riemann surfaces admitting a fixed type of
group action form an irreducible subvariety of Mg.
Greenberg’s realization theorem, together with the aim of understanding the challenging geometry and topol-
ogy of Mg motivates a central problem in the classification of compact Riemann surfaces: determine necessary
and sufficient conditions under which a given abstract group can be realized as a group of automorphisms of some
Riemann surface under prescribed constraints. In this direction, several approaches have been taken, including
the following ones.

a. Fixing the genus. Several works address the problem of classifying Riemann surfaces of small genus (see,
for instance, [2], [4], [12], [32], [33] and [36]), while others study group actions in genera of a special form (see,
for instance, [3], [30], [37] and [39]).
b. Fixing classes of groups. Notable contributions include the case of cyclic, abelian and dihedral groups
(see [24], [35] and [9]). We also refer to [11], [22], [41] and [49] for other classes of groups, such as solvable,
supersolvable and nilpotent groups.
c. Fixing the order of the group. This case includes the study of Riemann surfaces of genus g with groups of
automorphisms of order 8g + 8 and 4g + 2, which yield the celebrated Accola-Maclachlan and Wiman curves; see
[34]. See also [10] and [15] for the cases of 4g and 4g + 4.
Beyond the problem of describing the singular locus of the moduli space, the questions above are further
motivated by the difficulty of determining the (full) automorphism group of a given Riemann surface or algebraic
curve. A powerful tool in this setting, that we will employ in this paper, is the combinatorial theory of Fuchsian
groups and the study of inclusions among them, as developed by Singerman in [42] and [43].
Group actions on Riemann surfaces are partially determined by their signature. The tuple
(h;m1,...,mr) ∈Zr+1 where h ⩾0 and mi ⩾2
is called the signature of the action of a group G on a compact Riemann surface S if the genus of the orbit space
S/G is h and the branched regular covering map S →S/G ramifies over exactly r values y1,...,yr and the fiber
over yi consists of points with G-stabiliser group of order mi for each i. If h = 0 and r = 3 then the action is
called triangular and S is called quasiplatonic.
The point of view we consider in this article is the one of fixing the type of signature. More precisely, we
consider triangular actions and quasiplatonic Riemann surfaces. The interest in this class of Riemann surfaces
arises from several perspectives, some of them we mention below.
a. They enjoy the property that cannot be deformed analytically in the moduli space keeping their automor-
phism group. Roughly speaking, they cannot form families of positive dimension.
b. The geometry of them is particularly rich, as their analytic structure can be obtained from some bipartite
graphs, called dessins d’enfants in Grothendieck’s terminology [23].
c. By the celebrated Belyi Theorem, they are defined over number fields (namely, they are isomorphic to
curves defined by polynomials with coefficients in ¯Q), and hence admit the action of the absolute Galois group.
d. Since curves and their Jacobian varieties can be defined over the same fields, these curves provide the
natural setting in which to seek Jacobians with complex multiplication.
The study of quasiplatonic Riemann surfaces has been considered by several authors. For instance, the case
of low genera was investigated in [14], while the action of the Galois group was addressed in [19]. The case of
abelian groups was studied in [26] and [45], where it was shown that such quasiplatonic Riemann surfaces are
defined over the rational numbers. This result was extended in [25] to certain semidirect products. See also [27].
We refer the reader to [47] for an excellent survey on the relationship between these surfaces and Jacobians with
complex multiplication. On the other hand, the correspondence between quasiplatonic Riemann surfaces and
orientably-regular hypermaps (or dessins d’enfants) has enriched their study through combinatorial methods and
has made it possible to exploit advances in computer algebra systems for computer-assisted classifications (see,
for instance, [13] and the references therein). We refer to [31] and [46] for more details concerning these objects
and the relations between them.
