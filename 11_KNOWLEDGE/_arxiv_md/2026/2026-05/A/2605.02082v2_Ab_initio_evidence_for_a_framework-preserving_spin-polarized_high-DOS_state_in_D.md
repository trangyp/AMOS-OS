Ab initio evidence for a framework-preserving spin-polarized
high-DOS state in D-type carbon schwarzite C136
Eugene Yashin
Haifa, Israel
May 8, 2026
Abstract
Negative Gaussian curvature provides an unusual route for designing electronic structure in
extended sp2 carbon networks. Here I report ab initio density-functional calculations on the
D-type carbon schwarzite C136, focusing on the response of the ideal high-symmetry framework
to spin polarization and fixed-cell ionic distortion. A partial spin-polarized fixed-cell relaxation
lowers the total energy by approximately 0.213 eV per 136-atom cell over six completed ionic steps.
The distortion remains moderate: the RMS atomic displacement is approximately 0.098 Å, the
maximum atomic displacement is approximately 0.200 Å, the RMS C–C bond-length change for
the 170 reference bonds shorter than 1.80 Å is only approximately 0.0107 Å, and no unphysically
short C–C contacts below 1.20 Å are found.
A separate clean from-scratch spin-polarized SCF calculation on the last saved distorted
geometry converges successfully to a magnetic state with total energy −2490.35442340 Ry, total
magnetization 10.63 µB/cell, and absolute magnetization 12.94 µB/cell. Spin-resolved DOS
calculations further show that the distorted geometry retains a high density of states near the
Fermi level. A 3 × 3 × 3 diagnostic DOS gives N(EF ) ≈42.84 states/eV/cell, while a 4 × 4 × 4
validation DOS gives N(EF ) ≈42.85 states/eV/cell, demonstrating that the high-DOS character
is robust with respect to this k-point refinement. These results support the interpretation of C136
as a negative-curvature carbon parent phase near coupled spin–lattice and high-DOS electronic
instabilities. Superconductivity is not established here; rather, the results motivate a search for
stabilized, distorted, doped, or intercalated descendants of D-type carbon schwarzites.

Introduction
The electronic properties of sp2 carbon are strongly shaped by geometry. Graphene provides the
canonical flat two-dimensional limit, while fullerenes and nanotubes show how positive curvature
modifies π-electron structure, strain, and reactivity. Carbon schwarzites occupy a complementary
regime: three-dimensional sp2 networks with negative Gaussian curvature, often described as periodic
anti-fullerene frameworks. Such structures were proposed and analyzed in early work on negatively
curved graphite and schwarzite carbon networks [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]. Their geometry suggests that curvature
itself may act as more than a structural motif; it may function as a design parameter for electronic
states.
The present work is motivated by a simple question: can negative curvature in an sp2 carbon
network generate robust electronic instabilities, such as high density of states near the Fermi level,
spin polarization, and soft lattice distortions? If so, ideal high-symmetry schwarzites may not be the
final target structures. Instead, they may be parent phases whose distorted, doped, or intercalated
descendants carry the relevant physics.

Here I focus on the D-type carbon schwarzite C136. Earlier electronic-structure calculations
indicated metallic behavior, high density of states near the Fermi level, and flat-band features.
The present calculations extend this analysis by testing whether the magnetic/high-DOS state
survives ionic distortion. The central result is that the last saved distorted C136 geometry remains
framework-preserving, strongly spin-polarized, and high-DOS near EF , with the high DOS confirmed
by both 3 × 3 × 3 and 4 × 4 × 4 k-point DOS checks.
This paper deliberately avoids claiming superconductivity. Instead, it identifies a more conserva-
tive and defensible result: D-type C136 appears to be a negative-curvature carbon framework near
coupled spin–lattice and electronic high-DOS instabilities.

Geometry diagnostics: moderate framework-preserving distortion
A key issue is whether the relaxation trajectory indicates a physically meaningful framework distortion
or a collapse of the carbon network. The geometry diagnostics support the former interpretation.
The initial and last saved geometries were compared. The RMS atomic displacement is approxi-
mately
∆rRMS = 0.0976 Å,
(4)
and the maximum atomic displacement is approximately
∆rmax = 0.1996 Å.
(5)
Using a typical sp2 C–C length of about 1.4 Å as a scale, the RMS displacement is roughly 7% of a
C–C bond length. This is a noticeable collective distortion, but not a large structural collapse.
A bond-distance diagnostic was also performed. The 170 reference C–C distances shorter than
1.80 Å in the initial geometry were tracked in the last saved geometry. For this reference bond set,
the RMS absolute bond-length change is
∆dRMS ≈0.0107 Å,
(6)
and the maximum absolute bond-length change is
∆dmax ≈0.0352 Å.
(7)
Relative to a 1.4 Å C–C bond, the RMS bond-length change is less than 1%, while the maximum
bond-length change is about 2.5%. No unphysically short C–C contacts below 1.20 Å were found.
This combination is important: the atoms move collectively by nearly 0.1 Å RMS, but the
individual C–C bond lengths change only weakly. The distortion is therefore better interpreted as
a framework-preserving symmetry-lowering or soft-mode-like response, rather than a catastrophic
disruption of the carbon network.

The near-identical 3 × 3 × 3 and 4 × 4 × 4 DOS values demonstrate that the high-DOS character
near EF is robust with respect to this increase in k-point sampling. Compared with the earlier ideal-
geometry value of approximately 34 states/eV/cell, the distorted-geometry value of approximately
42.85 states/eV/cell represents an increase of about 25%. Thus, the framework-preserving distortion
does not merely preserve the high-DOS character; within the present PBE setup, it enhances the
DOS at the Fermi level. This strengthens the interpretation that the distorted C136 state is not
merely magnetic, but also remains electronically high-DOS near the Fermi level.

Interpretation: negative curvature as an electronic-structure de-
sign parameter
Taken together, the calculations support the interpretation of C136 as a negative-curvature carbon
parent phase near coupled spin–lattice and electronic high-DOS instabilities.
The key result is not that ideal C136 is a stable final ground state. The partial relaxation is not
fully force-converged, and full phonon stability of the distorted geometry has not been established.
The key result is instead that the system exhibits a coherent pattern:
1. the spin-polarized fixed-cell trajectory lowers the total energy;
2. the distortion is moderate and framework-preserving;
3. a clean SCF calculation on the last saved distorted geometry converges to a robust magnetic
state;
4. the distorted geometry retains a high DOS near EF ;
5. the high DOS is confirmed by both 3 × 3 × 3 and 4 × 4 × 4 DOS calculations.
This pattern suggests that negative Gaussian curvature in sp2 carbon may act as an electronic-
structure control parameter. In this view, carbon schwarzites are not simply three-dimensional
analogues of graphene, nor merely anti-fullerene structural curiosities. They may represent a class of
curvature-frustrated π-electron systems in which high-DOS states, spin polarization, and lattice-
softening tendencies are coupled. This interpretation is consistent with the broader role of high DOS
and flat-band-like features in magnetic and structural instabilities, including Stoner-like, flat-band
ferromagnetic, and Peierls/density-wave mechanisms [12, 13, 14, 15, 16].
The most promising search space may therefore not be limited to ideal high-symmetry schwarzites.
Instead, it may include distorted, stabilized, doped, or intercalated descendants of high-DOS spin-
lattice-unstable parent phases. Such descendants could relieve the instability partially while preserving
enhanced DOS and electronic susceptibility.
The present calculations are intended to identify a physically motivated search direction rather
than to exhaust the full structural, phonon, and electron–phonon phase space of C136. The latter is
a natural target for larger-scale follow-up studies.

Limitations
Several limitations must be emphasized.
First, the fixed-cell relaxation was not carried to full force convergence. This was a practical and
methodological choice rather than a claim that the structure had already reached its final minimum.
A longer relaxation to a much tighter force threshold, for example forc_conv_thr = 1e-4 Ry/Bohr,
6


## Page 7


may further lower the energy and could reveal a more complete symmetry-lowered structure. For a
136-atom spin-polarized metallic carbon cell, continued relaxation is computationally expensive, and
the saved trajectory already provided the diagnostic information needed here: the energy decreased,
the magnetic state persisted, and the framework underwent a moderate, non-catastrophic distortion.
In addition, the subsequent clean from-scratch SCF calculation on the last saved distorted geometry
was used to verify the electronic state independently of the incomplete ionic relaxation. The last
saved distorted geometry is therefore not claimed to be the final relaxed ground state.
Second, although no catastrophic collapse of the C–C framework is observed, full phonon stability
of the distorted geometry has not yet been demonstrated. A finite-displacement phonon analysis,
for example using standard first-principles phonon workflows [17], is a natural follow-up calculation.
The term framework-preserving is used here in the geometric sense: the saved distortion preserves
reasonable C–C distances and does not produce unphysically short contacts. It should not be read
as a claim of full dynamical stability.
Third, superconductivity is not established in the present work. No electron–phonon coupling
calculation for the distorted geometry is reported here, and no superconducting Tc is claimed from
the present distorted-geometry calculations.
Fourth, the DOS calculations are diagnostic and validation-level calculations using 3 × 3 × 3
and 4 × 4 × 4 k-point meshes. The agreement between these meshes is encouraging, but still denser
k-point sampling may be useful for final publication-quality DOS plots and for a more detailed
description of the Fermi surface, especially in the presence of flat-band-like features. The DOS values
also depend on the chosen electronic smearing/broadening; here the 3 × 3 × 3 and 4 × 4 × 4 DOS
calculations were compared consistently using the same Marzari–Vanderbilt smearing parameter,
degauss = 0.01 Ry.
A further limitation is that only one spin-initialization route was tested in the present distorted-
geometry workflow. The calculations demonstrate that a ferromagnetically seeded spin-polarized
solution survives distortion and remains high-DOS near EF , but they do not exclude the pos-
sibility that antiferromagnetic, ferrimagnetic, spin-density-wave, or other broken-symmetry spin
configurations could be lower in energy. In addition, the present work reports only the integrated
magnetic moment; it does not yet provide a real-space spin-density map identifying which parts of
the negatively curved framework carry the dominant spin polarization. Such magnetic-order and
spin-density analyses are natural follow-up steps.
The use of the PBE functional is another important caveat. PBE is a standard and useful
first-principles starting point, but it can sometimes overestimate the stability of spin-polarized
solutions in itinerant systems. The magnetic state reported here should therefore be understood as
a PBE-level prediction requiring further validation with alternative functionals or more advanced
treatments.
Finally, a real-space spin-density visualization would be useful for identifying whether the magnetic
moment is concentrated in specific high-curvature neck regions or distributed more broadly over the
framework. Such a plot is not included in the present version, but it is a natural next diagnostic
because it would connect the integrated magnetic moment to the geometry of the negative-curvature
carbon network.
These limitations do not invalidate the central result: the last saved distorted C136 geometry
supports a converged PBE spin-polarized SCF solution and retains a high DOS near EF . They do,
however, define the scope of the claim.

Conclusion
Ab initio calculations on D-type carbon schwarzite C136 show that the spin-polarized state remains
electronically and structurally significant under fixed-cell ionic distortion. A partial spin-polarized
relaxation lowers the energy by approximately 0.213 eV/cell while producing only moderate atomic
displacements and very small C–C bond-length changes.
A separate clean from-scratch SCF
calculation on the last saved distorted geometry converges to a robust magnetic state with Mtotal =
10.63 µB/cell and Mabs = 12.94 µB/cell.
Spin-resolved DOS calculations show that the distorted geometry retains high density of states
near the Fermi level. The 3 × 3 × 3 and 4 × 4 × 4 DOS calculations give essentially identical
values, N(EF ) ≈42.84–42.85 states/eV/cell. Thus, the high-DOS character is not removed by the
framework-preserving distortion and is robust with respect to the tested k-point refinement.
The results support a conservative but potentially important interpretation: C136 is a negative-
curvature carbon parent phase near coupled spin–lattice and high-DOS electronic instabilities. The
present work does not demonstrate superconductivity. Instead, it identifies a search direction:
distorted, doped, stabilized, or intercalated descendants of D-type carbon schwarzites may be
promising systems in which negative curvature, spin polarization, and enhanced DOS cooperate to
produce unconventional electronic phases.
