An isometric immersion of a flat Klein bottle into
Euclidean 3-space
Stepan Paul
May 25, 2026
Abstract
We present an explicit piecewise linear map from a flat Klein bottle (i.e.
one that is locally isometric to the Euclidean plane) into Euclidean 3-space
and that is an isometric immersion—a path isometry that is locally injec-
tive. The image is a self-intersecting polyhedron with embedded vertex
figures where each vertex has zero angle defect. The construction of the
map enforces the path isometry property so long as certain numerically-
verifiable inequalities are satisfied, and we show that checking the local
injectivity property at each vertex via another set of inequalities suffices.
This work generalizes features from known piecewise linear isometric em-
beddings of flat tori and known piecewise smooth path isometries of flat
Klein bottles, and apparently is the first explicit isometric immersion of
a flat Klein bottle into R3.
In general, we say that a surface with a metric space structure is flat if it has
the local geometry of the Euclidean plane. The torus and Klein bottle are the
only compact surfaces that admit flat metrics, and while isometric embeddings
of flat tori into Euclidean 3-space have received much attention (see Section 2
for details), we present here what is apparently the first explicit example of an
isometric immersion—a path isometric map that is locally injective—of the flat
Klein bottle. Since a Klein bottle cannot be embedded into R3, an isometric
immersion preserves about as much of the topology and geometry of a flat Klein
bottle as one could hope for.

1.2
Piecewise linear maps
A piecewise-linear (PL) map from a manifold M (with or without boundary) to
Rn is a continuous function ϕ : M →Rn such that there exists a triangulation
T of M for which ϕ is affine on each simplex with respect to its barycentric
coordinates. In this way, ϕ is determined by its restriction to the zero-skeleton
T0 of the triangulation, and any ϕ0 : T0 →R3 for a simplicial complex T can
be extended to a piecewise-linear map on all of T .
We say ϕ is non-degenerate when it embeds each simplex. Note that ϕ0
extends to a non-degenerate PL map if and only if the vertices of each k-simplex
of T span an affine k-space; in this case we also call ϕ0 non-degenerate.
A non-degenerate PL map ϕ induces a pullback length structure on each
simplex, and these can be patched together on the triangulation T to give a
global length structure on M. Under this induced length structure on T , ϕ is
automatically a path isometry.
Note that this construction allows the image of M to self-intersect with-
out violating positive-definiteness of the length structure on M. We pull back
distances one simplex at a time, and each simplex is embedded. Then gluing
happens with respect to the combinatorial structure of the triangulation.
The length structure induced by a PL map from a surface (with or without
boundary) to R3 is automatically flat on the interior of each 2-simplex and 1-
simplex in the triangulation T . The metric is flat at an interior vertex if and
only if the angle sum at that vertex is 2π. That is, for a vertex u, let [w1, . . . , wk]
be the cyclically numbered vertices connected to u by edges, let ⃗vi be the vector
from ϕ(u) to ϕ(wi), and let θi be the angle between ⃗vi−1 and ⃗vi. Then flatness
at u is equivalent to θ1 + · · · + θk = 2π. Here, as elsewhere in this article, we
use the phrase “cyclically numbered” and brackets instead of parentheses when
there is some natural cyclic ordering to a set of k objects, and in this case we
regard the indices as being taken modulo k (so that, for example, w0 = wk).

If M is a surface (with or without boundary) with the structure of a regular
CW-complex, we will call M a CW-surface. We call the 0-cells of M vertices, the
closures of the 1-cells edges, and the closures of the 2-cells faces. Generalizing
the notion of a PL map to CW-surfaces, suppose ϕ0 is a map from the 0-skeleton
of M into R3 with the property that ϕ0 takes the cyclically-numbered vertices
around any face to the cyclically ordered vertices of a strictly convex embedded
planar polygon.
Then ϕ0 extends to the same PL on M for any triangular
refinement T of the CW-complex structure that adds no vertices to M. Thus,
under this “embedded convex polygon” condition, a PL map ϕ is determined
by where it sends the vertices of a CW-surface.
1.3
Relationship to differential geometry
Differential geometry is primarily concerned with the properties of smooth man-
ifolds, so we take a moment to position what has been said so far about metric
geometry into this context.
First we note that if a manifold M with a compatible length structure is
flat, it is isometric to a smooth Riemannian manifold. Applying this to sur-
faces, by the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem, any compact surface without boundary
with a globally flat length structure must have characteristic zero and is thus a
topological torus or Klein bottle.
For smooth manifolds (with or without boundary) M, N, a piecewise smooth
map f : M →N is a continuous function such that there exists a triangulation
of M such that f is smooth on each simplex.
Thus if the length structure
induced on a manifold M by a PL map from f : M →Rn is flat, M is a smooth
Riemannian manifold, and f is a piecewise smooth map.

Background
As mentioned in the introduction, the story of the flat Klein bottle mostly
mirrors that of the flat torus, but remains largely untold. While no C2 isometric
embedding of a flat torus nor immersion of a flat Klein bottle exist, the existence
of C1 maps with these properties is implied by the famous results of Nash [10]
and Kuiper [8] in the 1950s. Burago and Zalgaller proved results in 1960 that
imply the existence of a PL isometric embedding of a flat torus [4], and in 1995
of a PL isometric immersion of a flat Klein bottle [5]. These existence proofs are
in a sense constructive, but the first explicit examples of isometrically embedded
flat tori seem to be a family of PL examples due to Brehm in 1978 [2]. And in
2012, Borrelli, Jabrane, Lazarus, and Thibert gave an explicit C1 embedding of
a flat torus [1].
In 2021, the author exhibited a piecewise smooth path isometry from a flat
Klein bottle into R3 using the theory of curved-crease origami [11]; its image
is shown in Figure 2a.

Local Injectivity
Up until now, we have made no assumptions about the injectivity of the PL
maps we have defined. For example, cross-cap vertices and even dihedral angles
of zero have thus far been allowed.
While there is no global embedding of the Klein bottle in R3, we are aiming
for a locally injective map. The next theorem tells us that for PL maps, it
suffices to prove local injectivity at the vertices of the underlying CW-surface.
Theorem 5.1. Let M be a CW-surface, and let ϕ : M →R3 be a non-
degenerate PL map whose faces are mapped to strictly convex planar polygons.
Then ϕ is locally injective (and thus an isometric immersion with respect to
the induced length structure on M) if and only if it is locally injective at every
vertex.
Proof. Since ϕ is non-degenerate, each cell is embedded, and thus ϕ is always
locally injective at any interior point of a face. Now suppose ϕ is not locally
injective at an interior point x of an edge e; we will show that ϕ is also not
locally injective at an endpoint of e, which suffices to prove the theorem.

Take a neighborhood of x small enough that it contains only points from
the faces f1, f2 meeting at e. Since ϕ embeds e, the assumption that ϕ is not
locally injective at x implies that there exist points y1, y2 in the interiors of
f1, f2 respectively such that ϕ(y1) = ϕ(y2). Now let v be an endpoint of e and
U be any neighborhood of v. Then since ϕ|fi is an isometric affine embedding
