A TWO-STEP APPROACH TO CHOW QUOTIENTS
GIANLUCA OCCHETTA AND LUIS E. SOL´A CONDE
Abstract. The Chow quotient of a projective variety by the action of a com-
plex torus is known to have a very complicated geometry, even in the case of
simple varieties, such as rational homogeneous varieties. In this paper we pro-
pose an approach in which the geometry of the Chow quotient is encoded in a
projective toric variety and a finite subgroup of its birational automorphisms.
We then illustrate how to apply our strategy in the case of some particular
rational homogeneous varieties.
1. Introduction
The construction and study of quotients by algebraic group actions is a central
theme in algebraic geometry, with deep connections to birational geometry and the
theory of moduli spaces. Among the various notions of quotient, Chow quotients,
introduced by Kapranov [10], occupy a distinguished position: while they are often
difficult to compute explicitly, they tend to encode rich geometric information and
admit meaningful modular interpretations in special cases. Classical examples in-
clude their relationship with Grothendieck–Knudsen moduli spaces [12], as well as
with spaces of complete collineations and complete quadrics (see, e.g., [7]).
In this paper, we investigate the problem of constructing Chow quotients of
smooth projective varieties under the action of algebraic tori.
Our approach is
motivated by the observation that, despite their global nature, Chow quotients
often admit a description in terms of simpler, more combinatorial objects arising
from torus-invariant open subsets.
This perspective was explored in our previous work [2], where we studied the
action of a maximal torus on the complete flag variety of P3. In that setting, we
showed that, in favorable situations, the computation of the Chow quotient can
be naturally divided into two steps.
First, one computes certain combinatorial
quotients associated with torus-invariant open subsets. Second, one analyzes the
birational relations among these quotients in order to reconstruct the global geom-
etry. In the case of rational homogeneous varieties endowed with the action of a
maximal torus in their automorphism group, these birational relations are governed
by the action of the corresponding Weyl group.
The main goal of the present paper is to identify general conditions under which
this strategy applies. More precisely, we establish a set of hypotheses ensuring that
the Chow quotient can be reconstructed as an inverse limit of combinatorial quo-
tients associated with torus-invariant open subsets. This provides a conceptual and
effective framework for computing Chow quotients in a relevant class of examples,
namely those in which fixed points are isolated and their weights with respect to a

3. Affine covers
Definition 3.1. Let H be an algebraic torus acting on a finite dimensional complex
vector space U. We say that the action is fully definite if U H = {0} and there
exists a Z-basis of M(H) with respect to which all the weights of the action have
nonnegative coordinates. An action of H on a projective smooth algebraic variety
is said to be fully definite at x ∈XH, if it is fully definite on TX,x.
Lemma 3.2. Let H be an algebraic torus acting on a finite dimensional complex
vector space U. Assume that the action is fully definite. Then every GIT quotient
of U by the action of H is projective.
Proof. Let us start by considering a set of coordinates (x1, . . . , xN) in U with respect
to a basis of H-eigenvalues of U. Since the action is fully definite, we may identify
H ≃(C∗)n, M(H) ≃Zn so that we may write, for every h ∈H:
h(x1, . . . , xN) = (hm1x1, . . . , hmN xN), with mi ∈(Z≥0)n \ {0}.
In order to compute the GIT quotients of U, we consider it as an affine toric
variety with respect to the N-dimensional torus
T = {(x1, . . . , xN)| xi ̸= 0 for every i} ⊂U.
Denoting by P the nonnegative orthant in M(T) = ZN, we have that U = Spec(C[P∩
M(T)]), and, following [6, Definition 2.16, Remark 3.6], the GIT quotients of U are
in bijective correspondence with the chamber decomposition of the image of P via
the natural map:
π : M(T)R −→M(H)R (sending the canonical basis element ei to mi).
This chamber decomposition is given by the intersections of the images via π of the
faces of P. Given an element v ∈π(P) ∩M(H), the corresponding GIT quotient is
the T/H-toric variety associated to the (a priori non bounded) polytope π−1(v)∩P

show that π−1(v) ∩P is bounded for every v.

and a vector u ∈ker(π) such that u0 + ku ∈π−1(v) ∩P, for every k ≥0, from
which it would follow that R≥0u would be contained in P ∩ker(π). This contradicts
that, with respect to a certain set of coordinates, all the mi’s have nonnegative
coordinates, and no mi is equal to zero.

The hypotheses of the above Proposition are fulfilled when X is a rational ho-

parabolic subgroup by the action of a maximal torus H ⊂P. In fact, in this case
the fixed points of the action are known to be the elements of the form σB, where
σ is an element of the Weyl group W = NG(H)/H. The weights of the action –
with respect to a linearization of an ample line bundle L(w) associated to a weight
w ∈M(H)– are of the form −σ(w) ∈M(H), σ ∈W. We can then easily see that
these values are the vertices of the polytope they generate.
Given an element σ ∈W, multiplication by (a representative in NG(H) of) σ−1
provides an isomorphism σ−1 : UσB →UeB, that descends to an isomorphism
CUσB →CUeB, which we still denote by σ−1. Composing this with the birational
map CUeB 99K CUσB induced by the intersection, we obtain a birational automor-
phism of CUeB, that we call the mutation map associated to σ; this name is meant
to suggest a relation with the concept of cluster algebra, introduced by Fomin and

birational automorphisms of some particularly simple toric compactification of T ′

the Chow quotient of X = G/P by the action of H can be tackled in two steps:
1. compute the combinatorial quotient CUeB;
2. construct CX out of CUeB by using the mutation group µ(W) ⊂Bir(CUeB).
