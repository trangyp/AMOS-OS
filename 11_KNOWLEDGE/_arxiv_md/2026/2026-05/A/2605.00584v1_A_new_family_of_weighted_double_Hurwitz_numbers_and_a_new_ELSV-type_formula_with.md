A. ALEXANDROV, B. BYCHKOV, P. DUNIN-BARKOWSKI, M. KAZARIAN, AND S. SHADRIN
Dedicated to the memory of Vladimir Igorevich Arnold (1937–2010).
Abstract. We analyze a new family of weighted double Hurwitz numbers that was introduced
as a notable example in the context of the 𝑥−𝑦duality for logarithmic topological recursion.
We use this family to systematically demonstrate, refine and develop techniques that play a
crucial role in the interaction of hypergeometric (Orlov–Scherbin) KP tau functions and inter-
section theory of moduli spaces of curves. In particular, we discuss the subtleties related to
the derivation of the ELSV-type formulas in this context and derive a new, explicit ELSV-type
formula in terms of the so-called Ω-classes.

1. Introduction
Topological recursion (TR) [EO07] is a powerful tool in enumerative geometry and combina-
torics that connects these domains to the realm of integrability, cohomological field theories,
Frobenius manifolds, intersection theory of moduli spaces, etc. etc. Its recent generalization,
the so-called logarithmic topological recursion (LogTR) [ABDBKS24a], which itself is a natural
example for even more universal generalized TR developed in [ABDBKS25a], see also a survey
in [HS25], allows for more general input data that consists of a compact Riemann surface Σ
and a pair of functions (𝑥, 𝑦) such that 𝑑𝑥and 𝑑𝑦are meromorphic.

Most problems in enumerative combinatorics resolved by topological recursion (like maps,
hypermaps, constellations, various kinds of weighted Hurwitz numbers, quantum invariants
of toric knots) are KP integrable. Nowadays it is quite expected since any topological recur-
sion on the rational spectral curve is KP integrable [ABDBKS26a], and in these examples the
spectral curve is rational. A more subtle property of this type of problems is that the corre-
sponding exponential generating functions are the instances of the so-called hypergeometric
KP tau functions [KMMM95] (also known as Orlov–Scherbin tau functions [OS01a; OS01b]).
To this end, two big families of such tau functions were studied in [BDBKS24] and proved to
satisfy topological recursion generalizing, in particular, the results of [ACEH20]. These, the
so-called Family I and Family II, encompass virtually all known examples of this kind.
Logarithmic topological recursion presents more possibilities, and a new family of examples,
the so-called Family III (see Definition 3.3 below) was proposed in [ABDBKS24a, Sec. 3.11] as
the one satisfying LogTR. As it was mentioned in op. cit., this family of examples is interesting
in several different aspects: both 𝑥and 𝑦have logarithmic singularities; neither of the two
sides of 𝑥−𝑦duality in this case is trivial; and it was also announced that there is an ELSV-
type formula for a special case inside this family of examples. The goal of this paper is to put
this example under the microscope and to present all results and computations announced
in [ABDBKS24a, Sec. 3.11]:
Proof of LogTR for Family III (Theorem 3.6): The proof that we give here refines a
variety of arguments proposed in [BDBKS24], that is, we analyze the local behavior of
the explicit closed formulas [BDBKS22] for correlation differentials near the points of
potential singularities. This way we reveal some new combinatorics of the type that is
also highly demanded in computations related to free energies, as in [Hoc25].
Derivation of a new ELSV-type formula (Theorem 4.2 / Proposition 4.7): This pa-
per also contains a new instance of an ELSV-type formula for some special cases of
weighted Hurwitz numbers in Family III. This formula contains the so-called Ω-classes
[Chi08; GLN23], also known in the literature as Chiodo classes [LPSZ17], and the ef-
fects coming from the logarithmic singularities of 𝑥and 𝑦in this case lead to a variety
of new subtleties that have to be taken into account.
Remark 1.1. Let us also note that there exists an alternative proof of LogTR in this case, perhaps
more conceptual and more general, but less explicit. It goes through the interplay of symplectic
and 𝑥−𝑦dualities and is available in [ABDBKS24b]. We note also that in [ABDBKS24b,
Sec. 6.5] the Family III of hypergeometric KP tau functions is further extended to include more
examples, but we do not need this more general definition for the purposes of this paper.
1.1. Some background. The core of this paper is quite technical and requires a lot of care for
details, though the techniques that we use here are not new and can be collected from various
sources. So we feel that we have to briefly recall some context to explain why this particular
family of examples (Family III) is so illuminating and does deserve a detailed discussion.
1.1.1. Context of LogTR. LogTR was developed in [ABDBKS24a] to systematize a number of
insights collected by Hock [Hoc24] in attempts to apply the 𝑥−𝑦swap techniques in the pres-
ence of logarithmic singularities. Since then it got quite some applications, see e.g., [Hoc25;
BHM26; BH26]. It is still a rapidly evolving direction in the huge landscape of applications of
topological recursion, and we find it very important to have a fully worked out example where
LogTR is structurally indispensable to indicate what are the new technicalities that one might
have to deal with. Indeed, it appears to be quite non-trivial to directly trace and eliminate the
potentially emerging singularities, with the computations that substantially divert from the
arguments in [BDBKS24] and require new combinatorial insights.

1.1.2. Context of ELSV-type formulas. One of the most impressive applications of topological
recursion is a uniform proof of a variety of ELSV-type formulas [ELSV01] connecting enu-
merative combinatorics to geometry of the moduli spaces of curves. This idea is based on
Eynard’s formula for differentials [Eyn14] (recast in the language of cohomological field theo-
ries in [DBOSS14]). It was first applied in this way in the literature in [DBKOSS15] in order to
give a new proof of the original ELSV formula and in [DBLPS15] in order to give a new proof
of the Johnson–Pandharipande–Tseng formulas [JPT11]. Since then it was used in a variety
of cases, in particular, to prove the results that were out of reach of existing techniques in
algebraic geometry, see e.g., [BKLPS21; DBKPS23; BDKLM23; GKL25].
LogTR can be considered a special instance of blobbed TR [BS17], see also [ABDBKS26b],
and in principle [BS17, Sec. 3] provides technique to upgrade the Eynard-type formulas in the
presence of blobs. However, LogTR demands an extra term excluded in op. cit., which generates
the so-called string flow and has to be carefully incorporated. In Gromov–Witten theory this
procedure is known under the name materialization [Giv01], see also [Jan17; ABDBKS25b].
1.1.3. Context of Ω-classes. The so-called Ω-classes [Bin03; Chi08] become more and more
ubiquitous in a variety of applications and are subject of many interesting properties and
open conjectures [GLN23; BKL25; BLS25]. They are used, for instance, in ELSV-type formulas
(see the references above), in explicit formulas for geometrically defined cycles on moduli
spaces [JPPZ17], and in constructions of new types of integrable systems [BLS25; BLS26], just
to name a few.
As the above references indicate, multiple explicit links of the Ω classes to hypergeometric
KP tau functions highlight more and more of their properties, and the new formula that we
present here can definitely be used for their further study.
1.2. Organization of the paper. In Section 2 we very briefly recall the definition of LogTR.
In Section 3 we introduce the main object of study in this paper: the Family III of weighted
double Hurwitz numbers and discuss their basic properties. In Section 4 we prove a new ELSV-
type formula for a particular member of Family III. In Appendix A we prove the logarithmic
projection property for Family III in a direct combinatorial way in the vein of [BDBKS24]. Ap-
pendix B is devoted to computation of coefficients for Theorem 4.2 given explicitly by Propo-
sition 4.7.
