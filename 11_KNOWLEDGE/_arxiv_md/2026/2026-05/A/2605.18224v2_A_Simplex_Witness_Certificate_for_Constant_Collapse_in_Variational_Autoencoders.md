Abstract
We study exact constant collapse in variational autoencoders, where the deterministic encoder
path becomes independent of the input. The VAE prior is kept as the standard Gaussian.
Before VAE training, we construct a single fixed teacher posterior by searching a GMM-based
approximation of the data. We then attach a fixed latent-only simplex witness to the encoder
mean and compare its output with the teacher. The resulting alignment loss has an exact
constant-predictor baseline: if the latent witness beats this baseline, the encoder mean cannot
be input-independent constant. The same construction also gives a closed-form latent target
that realizes zero teacher-witness alignment error for any full-support teacher posterior. This
yields a concrete design principle: choose a teacher with nontrivial information but controlled
log-odds energy, fix the witness, train only the encoder and decoder, and certify non-collapse by
a positive margin. We present the theory, a minimal training protocol, and preliminary MNIST
sanity checks. The analysis targets exact constant collapse. Reconstruction quality, sampling
quality, and other collapse modes are evaluated with additional diagnostics rather than folded
into the certificate itself.
1
Introduction
Posterior collapse is often treated as a training accident discovered after the fact. A model is trained,
and only then do we check whether the latent variable is used. Such checks are useful, but they do
not give a clean design-time certificate against a specific failure mode.
This paper focuses on exact input-independent constant collapse of the encoder mean. The goal
is to isolate a failure mode for which a direct certificate can be derived: can a standard-Gaussian
VAE be equipped with a fixed latent-only witness that makes exact constant collapse both testable
and constructively avoidable?
The construction uses one searched teacher posterior over the dataset, denoted by Tx. This
posterior is fixed before VAE training and is used consistently in the witness alignment and in the
final certificate. All margins in the main development are therefore computed directly with respect
to Tx.
The main workflow is as follows. First, search a fixed GMM teacher over a representation of
the data. Second, compute the teacher information and the teacher log-odds energy. Third, fix a
simplex witness over the standard Gaussian latent space. Fourth, train the ordinary VAE encoder
and decoder with the witness alignment term. Finally, verify a positive certificate margin. The
teacher and the witness are fixed before VAE training; only the encoder and decoder are optimized.

The prior remains the standard Gaussian. The simplex witness is a latent-only certificate head:
if the latent mean is constant, the witness output is also constant. Consequently, beating the best
constant predictor of the searched teacher rules out an input-independent constant encoder mean.
A second contribution is constructive. The fixed simplex witness has an explicit inverse on
full-support teacher posteriors. The centered teacher log-odds can be embedded into the latent
space so that the witness exactly reproduces the teacher. This shows that zero witness-alignment
error is not merely a numerical hope; it is an analytic target, provided the latent dimension and
encoder capacity are sufficient.
Scope.
The certificate concerns exact constant collapse of the deterministic encoder path. Gener-
ation quality, decoder usage, and dimensional activity are evaluated separately, since they require
additional diagnostics beyond the constant-collapse certificate.

Training objective and protocol
5.1
Minimal objective
The minimal RST-VAE objective is
Ltotal = Lrec + αKLLKL + λTSLTS.
The teacher Tx, its average ¯T, the simplex vertices vk, the witness gain β, and the witness S are all
fixed before VAE training. Training updates only the encoder and decoder parameters.
The hyperparameters have different roles. The coefficient αKL controls the pressure toward the
standard Gaussian prior. The coefficient λTS controls the strength of the witness alignment. The
gain β controls the sensitivity of the fixed witness to movement in the latent mean. None of them is
learned by the main method, and none should be treated as arbitrary. They should be chosen with
the energy comparison and validation diagnostics in mind.

Teacher search
The teacher must be nontrivial, balanced enough to avoid a single component, stable under the

8
Discussion
Why a fixed witness?
The witness is fixed so that beating the constant baseline must come
from the latent code. If the witness were a high-capacity trainable classifier, the certificate would
be harder to interpret.
Relation to a GMM responsibility head.
The simplex witness can be viewed as a special
spherical-GMM responsibility map over the standard Gaussian latent space. If the GMM means
are avk, covariances are shared spherical, and mixture weights are ¯Tk, then the responsibility map
reduces to the simplex softmax with gain β = a/σ2. Thus, the method can be read as using a fixed
GMM-like witness head while keeping the VAE prior standard Gaussian.
Does the learned aggregate latent have to be Gaussian?
No. The prior remains standard
Gaussian, but the aggregate encoder distribution need not be exactly Gaussian. Some mismatch is
expected if the latent code carries teacher-detectable input variation. If high-quality unconditional
sampling is the goal, the mismatch should be controlled by teacher smoothing, bounded witness gain,
KL budget, margin scaling, or additional prior-matching diagnostics. This is a generation-quality
issue, not a failure of the constant-collapse certificate.
Orthogonality to generation-quality regularizers.
The RST certificate controls teacher-
detectable variation in the latent path. Reconstruction quality, decoder bypass, FID/KID, perceptual
quality, and probe accuracy should be reported as complementary metrics rather than substitutes
for the margin GT .
9
Limitations
The method certifies exact input-independent constant collapse of the encoder mean. Dimensional
activity, decoder usage, and unconditional generation quality require separate evaluation. The
first MNIST experiment uses labels as a controlled teacher; unsupervised experiments should use a
searched GMM teacher over fixed or self-supervised features.
The analytic code zT (x) proves feasibility, not convergence. Nonconvex training can still fail.
The energy comparison gives a design inequality, not a complete proof that stochastic gradient
descent reaches the noncollapsed solution.
10
Future work
Future work should replace the label-smoothed sanity teacher with searched GMM teachers on frozen
features, run CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 experiments, and test whether the certificate correlates with
downstream representation quality. Another direction is to study fixed GMM-responsibility witnesses
beyond the symmetric simplex case, while preserving the standard Gaussian prior. Generation
quality can be improved by orthogonal decoder and sampling diagnostics, but those additions should
not be confused with the core constant-collapse certificate.

GMM teacher provides a fixed nontrivial target, while a fixed simplex witness over the standard

VAE training. A candidate teacher is specified by a representation map ψω, a number of components
K, a covariance model, a regularization level, a random seed, a temperature, and a smoothing level.
After fitting a GMM in the feature space, the responsibility vector is smoothed to full support and
used as Tx.

Component-preserving transport aligns teacher components with regions of the fixed witness S(c | z)
while keeping the prior p(z) = N(0, I).
For a hard teacher assignment Tx ≈ek, a component-preserving initialization sends x to a latent

the simple inequality LTS < IT .

geometry. The proof that a positive witness margin rules out exact constant collapse uses only the
fixed teacher and witness margin.

Training variants without changing the main theorem
The minimal training objective is
Ltotal = Lrec + αKLLKL + λTSLTS.
The teacher Tx and the witness S are fixed. Only the encoder and decoder are trained.
One practical variant is analytic prefit. Before full VAE training, train the encoder mean to
approximate zT (x) or a scaled target azT (x), where a ∈[0, 1] is chosen to obtain a desired certificate
margin with smaller latent energy. This puts the encoder near a certified nonconstant region before
the reconstruction objective is fully optimized.
Another variant is a margin form of the certificate loss:
Lcert = [LTS −(IT −τ)]2
+ .
This loss activates when the certificate margin approaches the unsafe boundary and leaves the
reconstruction objective unperturbed once a positive margin is achieved.
These variants are optimization aids. The theorem remains the same: LTS < IT rules out exact
constant collapse of the deterministic encoder path.
G
Aggregate latent mismatch and generation quality
The prior remains p(z) = N(0, I). The learned aggregate encoder distribution need not be exactly
Gaussian. In fact, some deviation is expected if the latent code carries teacher-detectable information.
A perfectly input-independent mean is the collapsed case; a useful teacher-aligned mean must vary
with x.
This mismatch is not a problem for the constant-collapse certificate. It can matter for uncondi-
tional generation, because sampling z ∼N(0, I) may visit regions that the decoder saw less often
during training. The mismatch can be controlled by teacher smoothing, bounded witness gain,
margin scaling, KL budget, and optional prior-matching diagnostics.
Thus there is a tradeoff: semantic structure in the latent path versus exact prior matching for
sampling. RST addresses the former directly. High-quality unconditional generation remains an
additional empirical objective.
