A Classification of Unmixed Binomial Edge Ideals of König Type
David Williams
Abstract
We first characterise graphs with binomial edge ideals of König type as those for which the path
covering number is equal to a minor variant of the scattering number.
These are well-studied
graph-theoretic invariants, allowing us to apply known results in the field to binomial edge ideals.
In particular, we show that cocomparability graphs have binomial edge ideals of König type, settling a
question of LaClair and McCullough ([LM26, Question 10.5]), and without requiring their unmixedness
hypothesis.
Along with their work, this allows us to classify unmixed binomial edge ideals of König type as
precisely those for which the underlying graph is cocomparability, or, in the language of Matsuda
([Mat18]), weakly closed.
We then conjecture that AT-free graphs have binomial edge ideals of König type.
Unless specified otherwise, let
R = k[x1, . . . , xn, y1, . . . , yn]
for some field k (of arbitrary characteristic) and n ≥2. Furthermore, set δi,j ··= xiyj −xjyi for
1 ≤i < j ≤n.
Throughout this paper, all graphs will be finite, simple and undirected. For a graph G, we denote
by V (G) and E(G) the sets of vertices and edges {i, j} of G respectively. Unless specified otherwise,
all graphs will have at most n vertices, with vertex set {1, . . . , |V (G)|}.
We denote by Pm, Cm and Km the path, cycle and complete graphs on m vertices respectively,
with K0 denoting the null graph with V (K0) = ∅and E(K0) = ∅.

Preliminaries
1.1
Binomial Edge Ideals
Our objects of study are the binomial edge ideals of graphs. These were introduced in [Her+10], and
independently in [Oht11].
We begin with their definition (most of this subsection appeared previously in [Wil23, Section 1]):
Definition 1.1. Let G be a graph. We define the binomial edge ideal of G as
J (G) ··= (δi,j : {i, j} ∈E(G))
We may also denote this ideal by JG.
There is a rich interplay between the algebraic properties of these ideals and the combinatorial properties
of the corresponding graph. For an overview of some of these interactions, see [HHO18, Chapter 7].
A key fact about binomial edge ideals is the following:
Theorem 1.2. [Her+10, Corollary 2.2] Binomial edge ideals are radical.

1.2
Ideals of König Type
The notion of ideals of König type was introduced by Herzog, Hibi, and Moradi in [HHM22] as a
generalisation of (monomial) edge ideals of König graphs, for which the matching number is equal to
the vertex cover number (see [HHM22, Section 1] for the details). There are several ways to define these
ideals, but for binomial edge ideals there is a particularly elegant characterisation. We first introduce
some terminology and notation (most of this subsection appeared previously in [Wil23, Section 2]):
Definition 1.9. We say that a graph is a linear forest if every connected component is a path. For
a graph G, we say that a linear forest in G is maximum if no other linear forest in G has a greater
number of edges.
Note. In [HHM22] and [LaC23], the term semi-path is used in place of linear forest, however linear
forest appears the more commonly used graph-theoretic term, and so we adopt it here.

Notation 1.10. For a graph G, we denote by LF(G) the number of edges of a maximum linear forest in
G.
Definition 1.11. [HHM22, Theorem 3.5] Let G be a graph on n vertices. We say that J (G) is of König
type if and only if
gradeR(J (G)) = LF(G)
Note. The left hand side of this equality is stated in [HHM22, Theorem 3.5] as 2n −dim(R/J (G)), but
this is equal to gradeR(J (G)) by Remark 1.8.
Ideals of König type possess some desirable properties. In the case of binomial edge ideals of König type,
one such property is that their Cohen-Macaulayness is independent of the characteristic of the base field
k ([HHM22, Corollary 3.8]). It is of interest then to characterise these ideals.
Combinatorial characterisations of graphs with binomial edge ideals of König type were given independently
by LaClair in [LaC23], and the author in [Wil23].

2.1
Definitions & First Properties
We begin by defining several graph-theoretic invariants, and exploring some of their properties.
Definition 2.1. Let G be a graph. The path covering number of G is the minimum number of
vertex-disjoint paths in G required to cover its vertices. We denote this value by π(G).
Note. Notation for this value varies across the literature. We follow that of [DKS97], π0 is used in [Jun78],
and ρ in [GRT97]. This list is not exhaustive, but addresses the most relevant papers for our purposes.
Example 2.2. A graph G is traceable if and only if π(G) = 1.

The following invariant was introduced by Jung in [Jun78, Section 4]:
Definition 2.4. Let G be a graph. Then we define the scattering number of G as
sc(G) ··= max{cG(S) −|S| : S ⊆V (G) and cG(S) ̸= 1}
Note. Again, notation for this value varies. We follow that of [DKS97], while s is used in [Jun78] and
[GRT97].
For our purposes, a slight modification of this invariant is more natural:
Definition 2.5. Let G be a graph. Then we define the unrestricted scattering number of G as
sc∗(G) ··= max{cG(S) −|S| : S ⊆V (G)}
Equivalently (since G has at least 1 connected component, so cG(∅) −|∅| ≥1),
sc∗(G) ··= max{1, sc(G)}
Note. This is not established terminology or notation.

We do not need to consider all subsets of V (G) to compute this value:
Proposition 2.6. Let G be a graph. Then
sc∗(G) ··= max{cG(S) −|S| : S ∈C(G)}
Proof. Take any S ⊆V (G), and suppose that there exists some v ∈S such that adding back v to G
doesn’t reconnect any components of G \ S. Then
cG(S \ {v}) = cG(S)
and so
cG(S \ {v}) −|S \ {v}| = cG(S \ {v}) −(|S| −1) = cG(S) −|S| + 1 > cG(S) −|S|
This tells us that, if S maximises cG(S) −|S|, then adding back any vertex in S to G \ S must reconnect
some components of G, and therefore S ∈C(G).
Furthermore, we can relate π and sc∗via the following inequality:
Proposition 2.7. Let G be a graph. Then
sc∗(G) ≤π(G)
Proof. Let F be a path covering of G with π(G) components, and take any S ⊆V (G). Since each
component is a path, F \ S has at most π(G) + |S| connected components. Then, since E(F) ⊆E(G),
we have
cG(S) ≤cF (S) ≤π(G) + |S|
so
cG(S) −|S| ≤π(G)
for all S ⊆V (G), and the result follows.
2.2
The Characterisation
Theorem 2.8. Let G be a graph. Then J (G) is of König type if and only if
π(G) = sc∗(G)
Proof. By Remark 1.8 and Proposition 2.6 we have
gradeR(J (G)) = heightR(J (G)) = min{|V (G)| + |S| −cG(S) : S ∈C(G)} = |V (G)| −sc∗(G)
and so the result is immediate from Proposition 2.3.
This characterisation echoes that of graphs with (monomial) edge ideals of König type: by definition
([HHM22, Section 1]) these are precisely the graphs for which the matching number is equal to the vertex
cover number (König graphs).
Since we always have sc∗(G) ≤π(G) by Proposition 2.7, graphs with binomial edge ideals of König type
can be viewed as those for which this inequality is sharp.

Unmixed Binomial Edge Ideals of König Type
Theorem 2.8 allows us to leverage the existing literature on path covering and scattering numbers to
establish that certain classes of graphs have binomial edge ideals of König type. Equality of π(G) and
sc∗(G) has been established for several classes of graphs, notably forests in [Jun78, Theorem 4.1], also
P4-extendible graphs in [HT95, Theorem 2], and 3-sun-free semi-P4-sparse graphs in [GRT97, Section 4,
Theorem 7]. Crucially, it has also been proven for cocomparability graphs:
Theorem 3.1. [DKS97, Theorem 8] For any cocomparability graph G, we have
π(G) = max{1, sc(G)}
We can then deduce:
Theorem 3.2. For any cocomparability graph G, J (G) is of König type.
Proof. This follows immediately from Theorem 2.8 and Theorem 3.1 (using our sc∗notation).
Since all cographs are permutation graphs, and both permutation and interval graphs are subclasses of
cocomparability graphs (see [BLS99]), this proves the three conjectures of [Wil23, Section 2.2].
In [Mat18], Matsuda introduces the following class of graphs:
Definition 3.3. [Mat18, Definition 2.1] Let G be a graph on n vertices. We say that G is weakly closed
if there exists a labelling of the vertices of G with the following property: for all integers 1 ≤i < j < k ≤n,
if {i, k} ∈E(G), then {i, j} ∈E(G) or {j, k} ∈E(G).
He also gives the following important characterisation:
Theorem 3.4. [Mat18, Theorem 2.9] Let G be a graph. Then G is weakly closed if and only if G is
cocomparability.
This tells us that Theorem 3.2 answers [LM26, Question 10.5] in the affirmative, and without requiring
unmixedness. In the same paper, LaClair and McCullough prove the following:
Theorem 3.5. [LM26, Corollary 10.4] Let G be a graph, and suppose that J (G) is unmixed of König
type. Then G is weakly closed.
Theorem 3.2 allows us to immediately deduce the converse, and obtain a complete classification of unmixed
binomial edge ideals of König type:
Theorem 3.6. Let G be a graph, and suppose that J (G) is unmixed. Then J (G) is of König type if and
only if G is cocomparability.
Proof. If J (G) is of König type, then G is weakly closed by Theorem 3.5, and therefore cocomparability
by Theorem 3.4. Conversely, if G is cocomparability, then J (G) is of König type by Theorem 3.2.
4
A Conjecture for AT-Free Graphs
Definition 4.1. Let G be a graph. We say that three pairwise non-adjacent vertices of G form an
asteroidal triple if, for each pair of these vertices, there exists a path connecting them which avoids the
neighbourhood of the third.
Definition 4.2. We say that a graph G is AT-free if it contains no asteroidal triple.
Conjecture 4.3. For any AT-free graph G, J (G) is of König type.
We have verified this conjecture using Macaulay2 for all connected AT-free graphs with up to 9 vertices,
a total of 95,869 graphs. The conjecture then holds for all AT-free graphs with up to 9 vertices.
Cocomparability graphs comprise a notable subclass of AT-free graphs ([GMT84, Theorem 4]), and so
Theorem 3.2 lends further weight to Conjecture 4.3.
