1. Introduction
Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, and let X be a K3 surface
over k. Associated with X are two invariants specific to positive characteristic: the
height and the Artin invariant.
The height h(X) of X is defined as the height of the formal Brauer group of X,
and one has
h(X) ∈{1, 2, . . . , 10, ∞}.

If h(X) = ∞, then X is said to be supersingular. In this case, there exists an integer
σ(X) ∈{1, 2, . . . , 10}
such that

crystalline Torelli theory for supersingular K3 surfaces ([Ogu79], [Ogu83]).
The height and the Artin invariant are also important in that they give rise to
stratifications on the moduli stack F2D of primitively polarized K3 surfaces of degree
2D:
F2D,i = {(X, L) | h(X) ≥i},
Fj

These stratifications have been studied from several points of view, especially when
p ∤D, including their cycle classes and their relation to F-zips ([vdGK00], [EvdG15]).
On the other hand, in [Ogu01b], Ogus introduced an invariant τ(X, L), which refines
σ(X) and coincides with σ(X) when p ∤D, and studied in detail the structure of
these stratifications, including the case p | D.
However, it has been difficult to compute the height and the Artin invariant ex-
plicitly for a given K3 surface (cf. Remark 1.1). For the height, one approach is via
point counting over finite fields (cf. [YY08, Section 4] and [Tae16, Theorem 1]; see
also [Mat23, Proposition 5.10]). On the other hand, in special cases, including sextic
purely inseparable double planes in characteristic 2 and Delsarte surfaces, explicit
formulas for Artin invariants are also available ([Shi04b], [Got04] and [Yui99]).
More recently, in the case of smooth quartic surfaces in P3, a simple and explicit
formula was obtained in [KTY22] (cf. [Fed83], [Sti87]).
This formula is built on
Yobuko’s theorem that the quasi-F-split height coincides with the height.
Here,
the quasi-F-split height is an invariant which generalizes and quantifies the classical
notions of ordinarity and F-splitting.
Thus, Yobuko’s result may be regarded as a bridge between the height of a K3
surface and the theory of F-singularities. The aim of this paper is to establish a
similar connection between the Artin invariant and F-singularities for smooth quartic
surfaces, and to give an explicit formula for computing the Artin invariant in this
setting.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we recall and reformulate the
Fedder-type criteria for quasi-F-splitting. In Section 3, we study the non-splitting
index in positive characteristic. We first establish a Fedder-type criterion for com-
puting the non-splitting index, and then prove Theorem B. In Section 4, we recall
results of Ogus on K3 surfaces and their moduli. We then study families of smooth
K3 hypersurfaces. We compare the non-splitting index with τ(X, O(1)) for quartic
and weighted sextic K3 surfaces, and prove Theorem A. In Section 5, we introduce
the non-splitting index in mixed characteristic via the splitting-order sequence. We
compare the positive- and mixed-characteristic non-splitting indices, and also discuss
the possible values of the non-splitting index for lifts (Theorem 5.8). Finally, in Sec-
tion 6, we give several explicit examples. We exhibit equations of K3 surfaces with
Artin invariant 3, 4, . . . , 9 in characteristic 2 and with Artin invariant 1, 2, . . . , 10 in
characteristic 3. We also construct a smooth Calabi–Yau threefold whose defining
equation has non-splitting index 58, and we generalize Shioda–Goto’s formula for the
Artin invariant of Delsarte K3 surfaces (Theorem 6.7).
Remark 1.1. The Artin invariant has also been computed and studied for several
special classes of K3 surfaces. For example, [Shi86] and [Got96] gave explicit for-
mulas for K3 surfaces of (weighted) Delsarte type. Moreover, [RˇS78] constructed, in
characteristic 2, families of supersingular K3 surfaces realizing each possible Artin in-
variant with explicit Weierstrass equations. In addition, [Shi79] proves the existence
of supersingular K3 surfaces with all the possible Artin invariants for p > 2 (without
explicit equations). Furthermore, [Shi04b, Algorithm 9.4] (cf. [Shi04a] and [Shi06])
gives an algorithm for computing the Artin invariant of supersingular K3 surfaces
in characteristic 2 from their birational models given as a sextic purely inseparable
double plane (cf. [BL87]). On the other hand, [Ito20] calculates heights and Artin in-
variants of the reduction modulo p of K3 surfaces with complex multiplication. Also,
see [Shi04c], [Shi14], [SZ07a], [SZ07b], [KS20] and [PS06] for related results.
