Abstract. In this paper, we provide a new framework to interpret complex geometry, inspired by Bambozzi–Chiarellotto—Vanni’s
work on tempered cohomology. We define several ind-Banach C-algebras of overconvergent and holomorphic power series, and
verify some desirable properties to endow Berkovich spaces with “analytic” structures in analogy with Clausen–Scholze’s condensed
formalism. As an application, we obtain an abstract GAGA-type comparison in this setting.

1. Introduction
Historical background. In the 1930s, de Rham defined the analytic de Rham cohomology of complex manifolds via differential
forms, and compared with the singular cohomology giving many topological invariants ([dR31]). Building on this, in the 1950s,
Serre proved an important relationship between the analytic and algebraic viewpoints ([Ser56]), which is known as GAGA. In
the 1960s, Grothendieck defined the algebraic de Rham cohomology of algebraic varieties over an arbitrary field (not only over
C), and proved a comparison between it and the analytic de Rham cohomology above via analytification as in [Ser56]. These
classical works show that one can move flexibly between analytic and algebraic techniques.
On the other hand, the theory of Banach algebras (or the Gelfand–Naimark theory of C∗-algebras) provided a bridge between
functional analysis and geometry in the 1940s ([Gel41] and [GN43]). In the 1990s, Berkovich developed the theory of Berkovich
spaces (i.e., the spaces of multiplicative seminorms), which is based on not only “discrete” rings, but also Banach rings ([Ber90]).
This theory gives richer (topological) information than the classical spaces (i.e., spectral spaces). This includes, as an important
special case, the theory of rigid analytic spaces developed by Tate ([Tat71]).
In the theory of ind-Banach rings or bornological rings, Bambozzi, Ben-Bassat and some collaborators recently provided a
framework where both Archimedean and non-Archimedean worlds can be treated in a general categorical setting (for example,
by [BBM22]).
More recently, Clausen–Scholze introduced the notions of condensed mathematics and analytic rings/stacks, which have
the potential to unify some theories such as (non-)Archimedean theory, complex geometry, topological theory and so on. In
particular, they allow us to re-interpret complex geometry in [CC] via liquid structures. The idea was summarized as follows: to
equip the underlying topological spaces of complex manifolds with “analytic” (liquid) structures via categorified locales, which
were originally developed by Balmer–Krause–Stevenson ([BKS20]). Also, on the non-Archimedean side, Bambozzi–Chiarellotto–
Vanni developed a “tempered” analytic framework, which provides a new comparison for crystalline cohomology. They also
used the geometry of categorified locales over ind-Banach non-archimedean modules as above.
Results. First, we will define the ring C{|T| ≤1} which corresponds to the closed unit disc in the framework of ind-Banach
C-modules. Also, we will construct other rings C{|T| ≥1}, C{|T| < 1}, and prove that they satisfy some axiomatic properties.

We set the following assumption for “analytification” considering S(A, f) ⊂S(A) as the subset on which f is analytic.
Definition A.2 ([CC, Lecture VI], GAGA setup). A triple (R, C, (A1)an) is a GAGA setup if it satisfies the following properties:
(1) (constants are analytic) For any element f ∈R, we have S(R, f) = S(R).
(2) (the sum and product of analytic functions are analytic) For any R-algebra A of finite type, and any elements f, g ∈A,
S(A, f) ∩S(A, g) is contained in S(A, fg) and S(A, f + g) both.

(5) For any R-algebra A of finite type, and any elements f, g ∈A, Dan(f + g) is contained in Dan(f) S Dan(g).
