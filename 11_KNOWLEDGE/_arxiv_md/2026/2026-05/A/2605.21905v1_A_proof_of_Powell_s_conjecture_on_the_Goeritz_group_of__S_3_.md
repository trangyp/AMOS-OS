A PROOF OF POWELL’S CONJECTURE ON THE
GOERITZ GROUP OF S3
DAIKI IGUCHI
Abstract. For a genus g Heegaard splitting of the 3-sphere, the Goeritz
group is deﬁned to be the group of isotopy classes of diﬀeomorphisms
of the 3-sphere that preserve the splitting setwise. In this paper, we
prove the following conjecture proposed by Powell: For every g ≥3,
the Goeritz group of a genus g Heegaard splitting is generated by four
speciﬁc elements. Our proof relies crucially on the fact that a Heegaard
surface of the 3-sphere is topologically minimal, that is, its disk complex
has nontrivial homotopy group in some dimension. Along the way, we
also give a new proof of the fact that a genus g Heegaard surface of the
3-sphere has topological index 2g −1.
1. Introduction
Let M be a closed orientable 3-manifold. A genus g Heegaard splitting of
M is a decomposition of M into two handlebodies A and B along a genus
g surface T. We denote by Diﬀ(M, T) the group of those diﬀeomorphisms
τ : M →M such that τ(A) = A. The mapping class group MCG(M, T)
of (M, T) is the group of path components of Diﬀ(M, T), and the Goeritz
group G(S3, T) is the kernel of the map MCG(M, T) →MCG(M).
Alternatively, G(M, T) can be viewed as the quotient of the fundamental
group of the space Diﬀ(M)/ Diﬀ(M, T) of Heegaard splittings [JM13, Theo-
rem 1]: any element of G(M, T) is represented by an isotopy Tθ, θ ∈[0, 2π],
that starts with T and comes back to the initial position. For example, con-
sider a genus g Heegaard splitting (S3, T) of the 3-sphere. For g ≥3, Powell
proposed ﬁve isotopies that represent elements of G(S3, T) [Pow80]. Four
of the ﬁve isotopies are depicted in Figures 2 and 3. (One of the isotopies is
known to be redundant [Sch20].)
Although it was once believed to be proved that these isotopies represent
generators of G(S3, T), Scharlemann [Sch04] pointed out a subtle gap in the
proof in [Pow80]. So the problem remains open for g > 3 until now, while the
genus 3 case has recently been resolved [FS18, CKL26]. The main purpose
of the present paper is to give a new correct proof of Powell’s conjecture:

Theorem 1.1 (The Powell conjecture). For g ≥3, the Goeritz group
G(S3, T) of genus g Heegaard splitting of S3 is generated by the four el-
ements Dω, Dη, Dη12 and Dθ shown in Figures 2 and 3.
A remarkable feature of a Heegaard surface of S3 is that it is a topolog-
ically minimal surface. The notion of a topologically minimal surface was
introduced by Bachman [Bac10]. For example, if a surface F ⊂M is incom-
pressible or strongly irreducible, then F is a topologically minimal surface
of index 0 or 1, respectively. By [App10, CT20], it is known that a genus g
Heegaard surface of S3 has index 2g −1. We also give an alternative proof
of this fact in Section 6, as a byproduct of the proof of Theorem 1.1.
It is always natural to ask whether a result on incompressible or strongly
irreducible surfaces can be generalized to surfaces of higher index.
See
[Bac10] for examples. In relation to the Goeritz groups, the author [Igu20]
proved that any strongly irreducible Heegaard splitting of an irreducible
atoroidal 3-manifold has the ﬁnitely generated Goeritz group, based on ideas
by [CGK18]. The same also holds for Heegaard splittings of higher index
under some additional assumption. These facts suggest that a correct ap-
proach to Theorem 1.1 would rely on topological minimality of T, and this
is a motivation of the present paper.

of Theorem 1.1. In Section 3, we recall the deﬁnitions of the disk complex
and its variant, and discuss their basic properties. After reviewing sweepout

to Heegaard splittings of S3 in Section 5. At this point, we can prove some
interesting results. For example, we will prove an analogue of Theorem 3.2
in [Bac10]. As a corollary of this result, we also give a lower bound on the
index of a Heegaard splitting of S3. These results are proved in Section 6.
Key ideas of the proof of Theorem 1.1 are presented in Section 8, after

4.3. The graphic. The graphic determined by {Tt}t∈Bd and {Ss}s∈[−1,1] is
the subset G ⊂Bd×[−1, 1] consisting of those points (t, s) such that for some
u ∈Tt ∩Ss, d(f|Tt) has rank 0 at u. Here, we summarize basic properties of
the graphic G . Although the parameter space for t is d-dimensional, a local
picture of the intersection between Tt and Ss is rather simple: If {Tt} and
{Ss} are in a general position, then the germ of f|Tt at u ∈Tt is either
• f(x, y) = x (u a regular point),
• f(x, y) = x2 + y2 (u a center singularity),
• f(x, y) = x2 −y2 (u a saddle singularity), or
• f(x, y) = x3 ± y2 (u a birth/death singularity).
The graphic G consists of d-dimensional sheets, each of which corresponds
to either a center or saddle singularity, as well as cuspidal loci that corre-
spond to birth/death singularities. If (t, s) lies in the intersection of p sheets,
then Tt tangents Ss in at most p points.
For our present purpose, saddle tangencies are only essential. So, we will
focus on the reduced graphic Gred, which is obtained from G by deleting
cuspidal loci and sheets that correspond to center singularities. For the con-
venience of references, we summarize these properties of Gred in the following
proposition.
Proposition 4.2. Suppose that {Tt} and {Ss} are in a general position.
Then, Gred is the union of d-dimensional sheets that intersect each other
transversely. If (t, s) lies in the intersection of p sheets, then Tt∩Ss contains
exactly p saddle tangencies.
