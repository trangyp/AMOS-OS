Abstract. Let Γ be a cancellation monoid and R = L
α∈Γ Rα be a Γ-graded
ring. It is shown that R is graded left semihereditary if and only if R is graded
left coherent and every graded submodule of a flat left R-module is flat. Hence
it gives a new characterization of graded-Pr¨ufer domains.
1. Introduction and preliminaries
Let Γ be a cancelation monoid, and R = L
α∈Γ Rα be a Γ-graded ring with
identity. The authors in [1] defined and studied the notion of graded left (right)
semihereditary rings which is a generalization of graded-Pr¨ufer domains. Recall that
R is a graded left (right) semihereditary ring if each finitely generated homogeneous
left (right) ideal of R is a projective R-module. They gave some characterizations
of these kind of graded rings (see [1, Theorems 5.4, 5.8 and Proposition 5.5]). In
particular, it was shown that R is graded left semihereditary if and only if R is
graded left coherent and every homogeneous left ideal is flat [1, Proposition 5.5].
Recall that a graded ring R is a graded left coherent ring if every finitely generated
graded left ideal is finitely presented. This paper aims to obtain a generalization
of this characterization. In fact, we show that the Γ-graded ring R is graded left
semihereditary if and only if R is graded left coherent and every graded submodule
of a flat left R-module is flat. To prove this result, we need to study the graded
version of the notion of weak dimension of a ring.
Let Γ be a cancelation monoid, that is, Γ is a semigroup with a neutral element
ε, not necessarily commutative, and Γ satisfies the (left and right) cancelation law:
αα1 = αα2 implies α1 = α2, and α1α = α2α implies α1 = α2 for all α, α1, α2 ∈Γ.
By a Γ-graded ring R, we mean an associative ring with the multiplicative identity
1, which has a direct sum decomposition R = L
α∈Γ Rα, where each Rα is an
additive subgroup of R such that RαRβ ⊆Rαβ for all α, β ∈Γ. For each α ∈Γ,
we call 0 ̸= a ∈Rα a homogeneous element of degree α and write deg(a) = α. For
the basics about graded rings and graded modules we used in this paper, one may
refer to [9], though it mainly deals with group G-graded rings.

by homogeneous elements, then we say that I is a homogeneous left (resp. right)

Recall that a graded ring R is a graded left (right) coherent ring if every finitely
generated graded left (right) ideal is finitely presented [1]. In [4, Theorem 4.1],
Chase showed that a ring R is left semihereditary if and only if R is left coherent
and every submodule of a flat left R-module is flat. We shall now state and prove
the principal result of this paper which is a graded analogue of Chase’s result.
Theorem 2.10. Assume that R = L
α∈Γ Rα is a Γ-graded ring. Then R is graded
left semihereditary if and only if R is graded left coherent and every graded sub-
module of a gr-flat left R-module is gr-flat.
Proof. Assume first that R is graded left semihereditary. Then every finitely gen-
erated homogeneous left ideal I is (gr-)projective; hence, I is finitely presented,
by [10, Proposition 3.11]. Therefore R is graded left coherent. Since every finitely
generated homogeneous left ideal is (gr-)projective, it is (gr-)flat. It follows from
[1, Remark 3.14(1)] that every graded left ideal is gr-flat.
Hence every graded
submodule of a gr-flat left R-module is gr-flat by Corollary 2.9.
Conversely, if I is a finitely generated homogeneous left ideal, then I is a graded
submodule of the gr-flat module R, and so I is gr-flat; since R is left coherent, I is
also finitely presented. Hence, I is projective, by [1, Remark 3.14(2)], and so R is
graded left semihereditary.
□
Similarly one can prove the above statement by replacing “right” instead of
“left”.
