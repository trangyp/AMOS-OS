Abstract
In this paper, the 2-group BAut(X) of automorphisms of a Lie groupoid X is con-
structed. Considering the 2-group G action on X, we explain the equivalence between
2-group homomorphisms from G to BAut(X) with Kan fibrations over G with fiber X.
This justifies the notion of Kan fibration for 2-group actions on Lie groupoids. As
an application, we formulate Hamiltonian actions of ´etale Lie 2-groups on orbifolds in
terms of Kan fibrations and study the symplectic reductions. We show that, in general,
the reduction is in fact a symplectic Lie 2-groupoid, and under certain isotropic free
condition, the reduction is still an orbifold. Also the slice theorem of a group G action
on Lie groupoids is proved.

1. Introduction
A Lie groupoid is a fundamental concept in geometry and mathematical physics, closely
related to differential and algebraic stacks.
As a special case, an orbifold is equivalent
to a proper, ´etale Lie groupoid. Lie n-groupoids, for 0 ⩽n ⩽∞, generalise manifolds
and Lie groupoids: a manifold is a Lie 0-groupoid and a Lie groupoid is a Lie 1-groupoid.
Studying symmetries—and hence actions—on Lie n-groupoids is a natural and fundamental
problem. At present, Kan fibrations of Lie n-groupoids provide the standard device for
defining actions.
This paper and its sequel aim to understand the symmetries of Lie n-groupoids and,
consequently, actions on them. One of the main goals is to clarify how the point of view of
Kan fibrations fits into this framework.
Let us recall the definition of a group action on smooth manifolds. Let G be a group
and X be a smooth manifold. Write Diff(X) 1 for the group of diffeomorphisms of X. A
G-action on X is a group homomorphism
ρ: G →Diff(X).
Given such a ρ, one may associate to it a smooth map 2
Φ: X × G →X
(1.1)
such that Φ(·, g) is ρ(g). Hence Φ satisfies
Φ(x, e) = x
and
Φ(Φ(x, g), h) = Φ(x, gh).
(1.2)
The Φ is the geometric realization of the action ρ. Conversely, such a map Φ determines
a group homomorphism ρ: G →Diff(X). Let HG(X) denote the set of homomorphisms
from G to Diff(X) and KG(X) denote the set of maps Φ as above satisfying (1.2). Then
HG(X) and KG(X) are in bijective correspondence. If G is a Lie group. One says ρ is a
smooth action if the corresponding map Φ in (1.1) is a smooth map.
We follow this line to understand actions on Lie n-groupoids. In this paper we focus on
the case n = 1, i.e., actions on Lie (1-)groupoids. Let X be a Lie groupoid. The study of
actions on X consists of the following steps.
Step 1: Automorphisms. The first issue is to understand the automorphisms of X.
Let Aut(X) denote the group of automorphisms of X. In [3], it was shown thatAut(X)
admits a groupoid structure, i.e., it can be associated with a groupoid Aut(X) such that,
at the coarse set level, |Aut(X)| = Aut(X) (cf. Proposition 3.1). The group structure of
|Aut(X)| is reflected on a group-like structure carried by Aut(X). On the other hand, the

In §4.3 and §4.4 we consider two special cases of actions on Lie groupoids:
(I) 1-group BG-action on Lie groupoids; in particular we prove the slice theorem in this
case;
(II) 2-group G-action on orbifolds; we develop the G-equivariant differential geometry on
orbifolds; in particular, we give infinitesimal actions of ´etale Lie 2-groups.
In §5, we consider an application to symplectic reduction on orbifolds. Consider a sym-
plectic manifold (X, ω, G, µ) with a Hamiltonian G-action. If 0 ∈g∗is a regular value of
the moment map µ, the symplectic reduction X  G may be a symplectic orbifold, and if
the action on 0-level set is free, the reduction is still a manifold. We may interpret this
as that the symplectic reduction on Lie 0-groupoid X with respect to Hamiltonian 1-group
BG may be a Lie 1-groupoid; moreover, if the BG-action on 0-level set is free, the reduction
is still a Lie 0-groupoid. Hence, symplectic reduction is not closed in the category of Lie
0-groupoids.
One may ask: to what extent is the symplectic category closed under symplectic reduc-
tion? For example, is the symplectic reduction of an orbifold still an orbifold? One of the
goal of this paper is to give a complete answer to this question. The answer Theorem 5.15
has three parts:
1) If the 2-group G is a 1-group BG, the regular symplectic reduction is still an orbifold;
that is, the symplectic reduction of orbifolds with respect to Hamiltonian 1-group
actions remains an orbifold. In this sense, the symplectic reduction with respect to
1-groups is closed in the category of orbifolds.
2) The symplectic reduction of an orbifold with respect to a Hamiltonian ´etale 2-group
action may be a Lie 2-groupoid; hence, symplectic reduction is not closed in the
category of orbifolds.
3) If the 2-group G action on the level set is 1-free (cf. Definition 4.24), the symplectic
reduction is still an orbifold.
We remark that most of arguments in this paper work for ´etale X. A certain special case
of the symplectic reduction for symplectic ´etale Lie groupoids was already studied in [11].
Readers are refered to Remark 5.1 for the comparison of our work with the work in [11].

If the action is free, the

(1) G is a Lie 1-group;
(2) H is 2-isotropy free.
Proof. By theorem 5.10 and Proposition 5.13, the (Z, ω′) is a proper, ´etale symplectic
Lie 2-groupoid. When G is a Lie 1-groupoid, by Corollary 5.11, (Z, ω′) is a proper, ´etale
symplectic Lie 1-groupoid. When H is 2-isotropy free, by Proposition 5.14, the (Z, ω′) is
equivalent to a proper, ´etale symplectic Lie 1-groupoid.
q.e.d.
