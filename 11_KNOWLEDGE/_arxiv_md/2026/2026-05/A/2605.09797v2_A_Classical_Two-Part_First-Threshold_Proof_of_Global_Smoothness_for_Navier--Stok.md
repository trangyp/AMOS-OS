A CLASSICAL TWO-PART FIRST-THRESHOLD PROOF OF GLOBAL
SMOOTHNESS FOR NAVIER–STOKES: AXISYMMETRIC SWIRL
CLOSURE AND FULL-SYSTEM REDUCTION
RISHAD SHAHMUROV
Abstract. We prove global smooth continuation for smooth finite-energy solutions of the three-
dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations by a two-part first-threshold argument. Part
I proves the axisymmetric-with-swirl theorem in the exact five-dimensional lifted formulation.
The central variables are the lifted vorticity ratio G = ωθ/r, the regularized swirl derivative
F = uθ/r, and the squared source density H = F 2. In these variables the derivative source
in the G-equation and the compressive feedback generated by the recovered strain U = ur/r
form a single pair-transfer mechanism. The proof combines localized energy identities, Hardy–
Littlewood–Sobolev and Sobolev interpolation estimates, pair-threshold absorption, finite-overlap
descendant exclusion, localized temporal source-to-score estimates, compactness of endpoint
profiles, projected Pohozaev–Morawetz strictness, and an auxiliary recovery estimate for F.
Part II gives a full three-dimensional finite-threshold front-end. Starting from a hypothetical
singular terminal packet, it removes leakage, shell, pressure, tail, fragmentation, passive-strain,
angular phase-lock, and transfer-active temporal channels by finite-overlap descendants or strict
terminal loss. A zero final defect forces the active frame measure into either a constant-frame
locally two-dimensional class or a physical azimuthal orbit around one fixed axis. The first
alternative is excluded by the classical two-dimensional Navier–Stokes theory, and the second is
precisely the axisymmetric-with-swirl class proved in Part I. The analytic ingredients are localized
energy inequalities, Caccioppoli estimates, Calderón–Zygmund recovery, Hardy–Littlewood–
Sobolev and Sobolev interpolation, parabolic compactness, finite-dimensional compactness,
finite-overlap covering, and projected Pohozaev identities. All full-system terms used in Part II
are defined before the estimates in which they appear.

1.1. Proof map for Part I. The proof of Main Theorem 1.1 has five layers.
(1) The axisymmetric system is lifted to an SO(4)-radial five-dimensional parabolic system.
In this representation, G diffuses by ∆5, and ur/r is recovered by an order −1 operator
from G.
(2) Localized energy identities for G and H = F 2 produce a pair visibility Aχ[G, H] and a
pair transfer Jχ[G, H].
(3) A direct soft bridge, proved only with HLS, Sobolev, interpolation, and Young inequalities,
absorbs every packet whose local mass remainder is smaller than its pair visibility. Such
packets are called subcritical; the precise definition is given in Definition 2.3.
(4) The only remaining case is the normalized terminal packet. The temporal descendant
and localized inverse-potential estimates first give strong compactness of any endpoint-
saturating sequence. On the resulting strong endpoint profile, gauge-free amplitude
variations and the zero-error localized Pohozaev–Morawetz dilation identity give incom-
patible homogeneity identities, which yield the strict terminal bridge.
(5) The pair budgets control G and H. The auxiliary F-energy then gives the missing
swirl derivative information. The resulting vorticity control yields an axisymmetric
continuation criterion.

1.2. Proof map for Part II. The proof of Main Theorem 1.2 begins with a hypothetical singular
smooth solution of the full three-dimensional system. The critical envelope and Caffarelli–Kohn–
Nirenberg contrapositive select a normalized terminal packet. The packet is made canonically
minimal by the order defined in Definition 12.4. The full-system dictionary of Section 12 then
decomposes every possible terminal obstruction into finitely many channels: pressure and collar
leakage, shell and far-field export, fragmentation, passive strain, angular phase mismatch, parity
loss, stall loss, and transfer-active temporal concentration.
The Part II proof has four steps.
(1) Large noncompact physical-space channels produce finite-overlap descendants or enter
the direct error budget; see Lemma 15.4 and Proposition 16.2.
(2) Positive angular or passive-strain defect gives strict terminal loss in the normalized
balance; see Theorem 19.1.
(3) If the final defect vanishes, the active-frame measure is rigid. The zero-defect rigidity
theorem, Theorem 18.4, gives either a locally two-dimensional terminal branch or an
axisymmetric-with-swirl branch around one fixed axis.
(4) The locally two-dimensional branch is regular by the classical two-dimensional Navier–
Stokes theory of Ladyzhenskaya, Temam, and Galdi [10, 26, 19]. The axisymmetric
branch is regular by Main Theorem 1.1. Both alternatives contradict the assumed
terminal singularity.

1.5. Literature, standard tools, and relation with the first-threshold approach. The
starting point is Leray’s construction of finite-energy weak solutions and the classical continuation
philosophy behind the Beale–Kato–Majda criterion [12, 1]. The local-energy and partial-regularity
theory of Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg [2] is a benchmark for any local Navier–Stokes analysis.
We do not use a Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg epsilon-regularity theorem as a black-box estimate
in the proof below; rather, the argument uses localized energy identities and Caccioppoli-type
bookkeeping in the same local spirit. Thus no step of the proof rests on an unquoted CKN
regularity criterion.
The axisymmetric problem has a special history. In the no-swirl case, global regularity was
proved by Ladyzhenskaya and independently by Ukhovskii–Yudovich [9, 14]. The reason this
case is substantially easier is structural: if uθ = 0, then Γ = 0, hence F = H = 0, and the lifted
equation for G = ωθ/r has no derivative swirl source. The meridional vorticity is then governed
by a genuinely dissipative scalar equation. With swirl, by contrast, the derivative source ∂zH,
H = (uθ/r)2, couples swirl stretching back into the vorticity ratio and is the source of the main
difficulty. This is why the axisymmetric swirl problem is often treated as a central testing ground
for three-dimensional regularity methods: it preserves a genuine stretching mechanism and a
singular cylindrical geometry, but it still permits the exact scalar variables Γ, G, F, H and the
five-dimensional recovery formula used here.
A large literature studies regularity criteria, blow-up-rate restrictions, Liouville theorems, and
near-critical mechanisms for axisymmetric flows with swirl. Important benchmarks include the
lower blow-up-rate and local-regularity work of Chen–Strain–Tsai–Yau [5, 6], Liouville-theorem
approaches of Koch–Nadirashvili–Seregin–Šverák [8], the criticality viewpoint of Lei–Zhang
[11], regularity criteria such as Chae [3], Hou–Lei–Li and Hou–Li on anisotropic and stability
mechanisms [15, 16], Chen–Fang–Zhang [4], Wei [18], the survey perspective of Lei–Zhang [17],
and more recent local analyses such as Seregin [13]. The present proof is written so that the
local estimates, the exact source variables, and the terminal finite-stage strictness step are visible
without assuming the reader has these papers open.
The related direct-threshold manuscripts [27, 28] motivate the packet and descendant language
used here. The proof is independent in the following operational sense: Part I and Part II are
parts of the present single manuscript: Part I proves the axisymmetric theorem through the
pair variables (G, H), the direct soft bridge, and localized Pohozaev–Morawetz strictness; Part
II proves the full-system front-end by its own finite-threshold definitions, defect channels, and
rigidity lemmas. The citations locate the method within the direct-threshold program, while the
estimates needed for the two-part proof are stated in this manuscript.
The proof separates the argument into three layers. First, subcritical packets are absorbed
by HLS/Sobolev estimates. Second, noncompact channels are converted into descendants by
finite-overlap and endpoint-reselection arguments, in the concentration–compactness spirit of
Lions [23, 24] but with the dichotomy replaced here by the finite-threshold descendant order.
Third, the terminal packet is tested directly against localized amplitude and dilation multipliers.
The finite-stage commutator bookkeeping shows that possible temporal or frequency remnants
are already part of the terminal error budget.

necessary because the terminal balance has the form
Aχ ≤Jχ + lower-order errors.
To obtain a contradiction one must insert an estimate
|Jχ| ≤θAχ + lower-order errors,

and then absorb the right-hand principal term back into the left. If the best coefficient were
merely θ = 1, the inequality would reduce to Aχ ≤Aχ + errors, which gives no contradiction
and no gain. The reason classical estimates stop at this point is scaling: the pair transfer
is cubic in the active amplitude, while the pair visibility is quadratic. Any global HLS or
Gagliardo–Nirenberg estimate must be valid for the entire admissible function class; it therefore
produces a universal constant but cannot distinguish the precise terminal coherent profile from
all other shapes strongly enough to force a strict coefficient below one. This is a familiar
limitation of norm inequalities: they measure size, integrability, and differentiability, but not the
full geometric arrangement of a possible singular packet. In the present problem the decisive
information includes the relative sign of the recovered strain, the parity of the source density,
the alignment of the active core with the axis, and the way the two cubic transfers interact
under dilation. These features are invisible to a purely scalar norm bound, just as an Lp norm
in R3 cannot by itself distinguish two vector fields with the same amplitude distribution but
different vorticity alignment or parity.
This is the reason a terminal strictness step is introduced. The endpoint part of the proof
does not replace the classical estimates; it begins only after those estimates have reached their
scale-neutral endpoint. In the normalized terminal regime, ordinary inequalities leave open the
possibility of a coefficient-one saturator. The finite-stage Pohozaev–Morawetz argument tests
that potential saturator on the original smooth terminal packets: the amplitude homogeneity of
R G2U[G], the amplitude homogeneity of
R U[G]H2, and the spatial dilation homogeneity of the
Dirichlet visibilities give two incompatible identities up to the terminal error budget. Thus the
localized Pohozaev–Morawetz strictness theorem is the mechanism that breaks the scaling tie
left by classical harmonic analysis.
A detailed obstruction to replacing this step by a purely quadratic source-potential energy
is given in Theorem B.2 and Section B. That appendix shows, at the level of the exact lifted
model, that the natural bilinear cross energy improves the linear derivative source but necessarily
creates a trilinear term of the wrong amplitude homogeneity. This explains why the proof uses
terminal Pohozaev–Morawetz strictness at the final endpoint rather than treating the issue as a
matter of presentation.

17. Active frames and finite-dimensional phase-lock
Definition 17.1 (Frame space). The frame space F is the compact space whose points are

when only relative phase is measured. The compactness is the compactness of the Grassmannian
and the unit sphere.
Definition 17.2 (Active frame measure). On a normalized packet, decompose the active vorticity
into finitely many Littlewood–Paley and angular pieces after discarding subcritical tails. Each
piece with nonzero transfer is assigned its dominant frame in F and is weighted by its absolute
transfer contribution. The normalized weighted counting measure is the active frame measure
µfr. Passing to a subsequence gives weak convergence of µfr because F is compact.

(i) the constant-frame family R2D, where all active vorticity frames share one fixed direction
after rotation;
(ii) the azimuthal orbit family Raxi, where the frames are exactly the cylindrical angular
orbit around one fixed axis.

Appendix B. Why a purely quadratic source-potential energy does not close
This appendix records the direct-energy obstruction that motivates the finite-stage Pohozaev–
Morawetz endpoint argument used in the main proof. The discussion is included to separate
two issues. First, the derivative source in the lifted vorticity equation is harmless at the level of
classical energy estimates when the source density is transported and diffused independently.
Second, in the Navier–Stokes swirl system the same source density is compressed by the recovered
strain. That compression has the same critical scaling as the pair visibility and cannot be removed
by a quadratic Lyapunov correction.
The obstruction should also be understood geometrically. Standard energy, Sobolev, HLS, and
Calderón–Zygmund estimates are scalar norm estimates. They control how large a field is, how
much integrability it has, and how much derivative energy it carries. They do not record whether
a normalized packet is arranged with the parity, orientation, and strain-source alignment needed
for a coefficient-one transfer saturation. Two lifted profiles can have the same L2, H1, and critical
interpolation norms while generating different signs or alignments in U[G]H2. The endpoint
strictness argument tests this lost geometric information through amplitude homogeneity of the
strong endpoint profile and the localized Pohozaev–Morawetz dilation homogeneity.

Remark B.4 (Shape blindness of norm-only estimates). The preceding obstruction is the analytic
form of a geometric limitation. Norm estimates such as HLS, Sobolev interpolation, Calderón–
Zygmund estimates, and Young’s inequality are insensitive to the arrangement of the active
packet beyond size and derivative scale. They cannot distinguish, for example, whether the
recovered strain is aligned with the positive source density, whether the active profile has the
parity needed to maximize the transfer, or whether the mass lies in a collar/tail arrangement
that should instead be reselected as a descendant. This is why the proof first uses classical
estimates to eliminate all subcritical configurations and then passes, only at the scale-neutral
endpoint, to a compact-critical profile where the actual geometry of the packet can be tested by
amplitude and dilation identities.
