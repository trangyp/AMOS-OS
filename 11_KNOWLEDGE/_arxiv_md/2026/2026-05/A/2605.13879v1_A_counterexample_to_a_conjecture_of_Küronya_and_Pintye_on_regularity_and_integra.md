A COUNTEREXAMPLE TO A CONJECTURE OF KÜRONYA AND PINTYE ON
REGULARITY AND INTEGRAL CLOSURE
SOUMYADEEP MISRA
ABSTRACT. We exhibit an equigenerated monomial ideal I ⊆K[x,y,z,w] with reg(I) > reg(I). The
ideal I is generated in degree 4 and satisfies reg(I) = 4, while its integral closure I has a minimal
generator of degree 5 and satisfies reg(I) = 5. This gives a counterexample to the polynomial-ring
formulation of the Küronya–Pintye conjecture.
1. INTRODUCTION
Let S = K[x1,...,xn] be a standard graded polynomial ring, and let I ⊆S be homogeneous. The
Küronya–Pintye conjecture predicts that integral closure does not increase Castelnuovo–Mumford
regularity:
(1)
reg(I) ⩽reg(I),
where I denotes the integral closure of I.
The inequality is known for several classes of monomial ideals; see, for example, [4]. It also
holds for equigenerated monomial ideals in two and three variables by [1]. We show that it fails in
general already for an equigenerated monomial ideal in four variables.
Our counterexample is the ideal
I = (yz2w, y2zw, y2w2, y3w, xz2w, xz3) ⊆K[x,y,z,w].
We prove that I = I +(xyzw2) and that both I and I have linear quotients. Consequently reg(I) = 4
while reg(I) = 5, so (1) fails.
2. COMPUTATION OF THE INTEGRAL CLOSURE
Throughout, let S = K[x,y,z,w] and set
(2)
I = (yz2w, y2zw, y2w2, y3w, xz2w, xz3).
This ideal is generated in degree 4.
We use the Newton polyhedron criterion for integral closures of monomial ideals.
If L ⊆
K[x1,...,xn] is a monomial ideal and A (L) is the set of exponent vectors of its minimal mono-
mial generators, then NP(L) = conv(A (L))+Rn
⩾0, and for α ∈Nn one has
(3)
xα ∈L
⇐⇒
α ∈NP(L).

For the ideal I in (2), the exponent vectors of the six generators are (0,1,2,1), (0,2,1,1),
(0,2,0,2), (0,3,0,1), (1,0,2,1), and (1,0,3,0). We write a general exponent vector as (a,b,c,d),
corresponding to xaybzcwd.
Lemma 2.1. The monomial xyzw2 belongs to I.
Proof. Let u = xyzw2. Since (y2w2)(xz2w) = xy2z2w3 divides u2 = x2y2z2w4, we have u2 ∈I2.
Hence u is integral over I, so u ∈I.
□
Lemma 2.2. Every point (a,b,c,d) ∈NP(I) satisfies
a+b ⩾1,
a+d ⩾1,
b+c ⩾2,
c+3d ⩾3,
2a+2b+c ⩾4,
2b+3c+2d ⩾8.
Proof. Each of these six inequalities is satisfied by the six exponent vectors listed above. Since the
left-hand sides are linear, the inequalities remain valid on their convex hull. Since all coefficients
are nonnegative, they also remain valid after adding R4
⩾0. Hence they hold on NP(I).
□
Proposition 2.3. One has I = I +(xyzw2).
Proof. Set J = I +(xyzw2). By Lemma 2.1, J ⊆I.
Conversely, let xaybzcwd ∈I. By (3), (a,b,c,d) ∈NP(I), so the inequalities in Lemma 2.2 hold.
We show that xaybzcwd is divisible by one of the generators of J.
If d = 0, then a + d ⩾1 and c + 3d ⩾3 give a ⩾1 and c ⩾3. Hence xz3 divides xaybzcwd, so
the monomial lies in J. Now assume d ⩾1. If c ⩾2, then a+b ⩾1, so either xz2w or yz2w divides
xaybzcwd. Thus, unless the monomial is already in J, we may assume c ⩽1.
Suppose first that c = 0. Then b +c ⩾2 gives b ⩾2. If b ⩾3, then y3w divides the monomial,
while if b = 2 and d ⩾2, then y2w2 divides it. The only remaining possibility is b = 2, c = 0, and
d = 1, but then 2b+3c+2d = 6 < 8, contradicting Lemma 2.2.
It remains to consider c = 1. Then b + c ⩾2 gives b ⩾1. If b ⩾2, then y2zw divides the
monomial, so we may assume b = 1. Now 2b+3c+2d ⩾8 gives d ⩾2, and 2a+2b+c ⩾4 gives
a ⩾1. Therefore xyzw2 divides xaybzcwd. Hence every monomial in I lies in J, and so I = J.
□
3. THE COUNTEREXAMPLE
Theorem 3.1. For the ideal I in (2), one has
reg(I) = 5 > 4 = reg(I).
Consequently, the inequality reg(I) ⩽reg(I) fails for homogeneous ideals in polynomial rings.
Proof. We prove this by showing that both I and I have linear quotients. Order the generators of I
as
g1 = yz2w,
g2 = y2zw,
g3 = y2w2,
g4 = y3w,
g5 = xz2w,
g6 = xz3.
The successive colon ideals are
(g1) : g2 = (z),
(g1,g2) : g3 = (z),
(g1,g2,g3) : g4 = (z,w),
(g1,g2,g3,g4) : g5 = (y),
(g1,g2,g3,g4,g5) : g6 = (w).

Thus I has linear quotients. Since I is generated in degree 4, it has a 4-linear resolution, and hence
reg(I) = 4.
By Proposition 2.3, I = I + (xyzw2). Put g7 = xyzw2 and use the order g1,...,g7. The first five
colon computations are those above. For the last generator, one has
(g1,g2,g3,g4,g5,g6) : g7 = (y,z),
Indeed,
g1
gcd(g1,g7) = z,
g2
gcd(g2,g7)
= y,
g3
gcd(g3,g7) = y,
g4
gcd(g4,g7) = y2,
g5
gcd(g5,g7)
= z,
g6
gcd(g6,g7) = z2.
Hence I also has linear quotients.
The monomial xyzw2 has degree 5, while the other minimal generators of I have degree 4. Since
I has linear quotients with respect to an order of its minimal generators in nondecreasing degree,
its regularity equals the largest degree of a minimal generator [2]. Therefore reg(I) = 5, while
reg(I) = 4.
□
