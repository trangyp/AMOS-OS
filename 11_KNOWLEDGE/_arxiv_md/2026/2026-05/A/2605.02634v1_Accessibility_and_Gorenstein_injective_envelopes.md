ACCESSIBILITY AND GORENSTEIN INJECTIVE ENVELOPES
SERGIO ESTRADA AND JAMES GILLESPIE
Abstract. Let G be a Grothendieck category. We prove completeness of the
Gorenstein injective cotorsion pair whenever G admits a set of Tate trivial
generators, and show that having such generators is necessary for complete-
ness. In this case it must be a perfect cotorsion pair, cogenerated by a set,
and equivalent to an injective abelian model structure on G.
Examples in-
clude Grothendieck categories (possibly without enough projectives) that ad-
mit a generating set consisting of objects of finite projective dimension, such as
the category of quasi-coherent sheaves on a quasi-compact and semi-separated
scheme. More generally, for a given set S, we characterize the completeness
of the Gorenstein B-injective cotorsion pair, where B = S⊥, in terms of the
existence of a set of B-Tate trivial generators for G. The key ingredient to our
proof is the fact that any class of the form B := S⊥is an accessibly embedded,
accessible subcategory of G. The general approach allows for further appli-
cations such as the existence of Ding injective envelopes and other relative
Gorenstein injective envelopes without imposing additional assumptions on G.
1. Introduction
Let R be an arbitrary ring and R-Mod the category of left R-modules.
An
R-module M is said to be Gorenstein injective if it is equal to some cycle mod-
ule, M = ZnE, of some exact complex of injectives E having the property that
HomR(I, E) is again an exact complex for all injective R-modules I. Along with
the Gorenstein projective and Gorenstein flat modules, these are the fundamental
objects of Gorenstein homological algebra, a particular variant of relative homolog-
ical algebra that has been actively researched.
One naturally wonders which results of classical homological algebra have ana-
logues in Gorenstein homological algebra.
The notion of a Gorenstein injective
envelope of an R-module is the Gorenstein analogue of the classical notion of an
injective envelope. While the existence of injective envelopes was established in
1953 in [ES53], the existence of Gorenstein injective envelopes in R-Mod was only
recently proved by ˇSaroch and ˇSˇtov´ıˇcek in [SS20].
Module categories are the prototypical examples of Grothendieck categories, and
it is well known (for instance, see [Ste75, Cor. X.4.3]) that injective envelopes also
exist in any Grothendieck category. As pointed out in [SS20], their proof of the
existence of Gorenstein injective envelopes can be extended to module categories

The best result so far for general Grothendieck categories was obtained by Krause

In this paper, as a by-product of our more general results, we provide in Corol-
laries 5.8 and 6.3 a very satisfactory answer to the above question. We show that
(⊥GI, GI) is a complete (in fact, perfect) cotorsion pair in a Grothendieck category
G if and only if G admits a set of Tate trivial generators, in the sense of Defini-
tion 5.3. This amounts to a set U of generators for G such that U ⊆⊥GI. Such
generating sets exist for a wide class of Grothendieck categories, including those ad-
mitting a family of generators of finite projective dimension. We call such categories
locally finite dimensional Grothendieck categories; see Section 4.1. In particular,
these include Grothendieck categories with a generating set of small projective gen-
erators, as well as Grothendieck categories possibly without enough projectives. A
main example is the category of quasi-coherent sheaves on a quasi-compact and
semi-separated scheme X (see Example 7.1.1).
In fact, our techniques allow us to prove, simultaneously, and without any further
assumptions on G, the existence of envelopes for a number of relative Gorenstein
objects that have been considered in the literature.
These include Ding injec-
tive envelopes, Gorenstein AC-injective envelopes, and Gorenstein FPn-injective
envelopes, to name a few; see [GI22, BGH14, BGP19]. In all cases, we obtain im-
proved general results that hold under the assumption that G is a Grothendieck
category with a set of generators analogous to the above (see Example 7.2.1 for
details.)

given set S of objects in G. An object M ∈G is said to be Gorenstein B-injective if it
is equal to some cycle object, M = ZnE, of some exact complex of injective objects
E having the property that HomG(B, E) is an exact complex of abelian groups for
all B ∈B. Let GIB denote the class of all Gorenstein B-injective objects.
Theorem. (Corollary 6.3) Let G be a Grothendieck category with a set of generators
of finite projective dimension, and let S be any set of objects. Let B := S⊥. Then
(⊥GIB, GIB) is a perfect cotorsion pair. That is, the class ⊥GIB is covering and
the class GIB is enveloping.

envelopes, cotorsion pairs, and Quillen model structures on additive categories.

to obtain far reaching results in homological algebra. We start by recalling some of
the basic terminology of the theory, following the standard reference [AR94].
Recall that a regular cardinal is an infinite cardinal which is not the sum of a
smaller number of smaller cardinals. All infinite successor cardinals are regular.

(2) We say that K is λ-accessible if it has λ-direct limits and possesses a set T

Definition 2.2. [AR94, Def. 2.35] Let K be an accessible category. A subcategory

will also say that B ⊆K is λ-accessibly embedded.

by the inclusion functor. The argument for this easy fact appears below in the proof
of Lemma 2.3. Also, any accessibly embedded B must be isomorphism closed and
so is a strictly full subcategory of K.

Grothendieck category G, we obtain the Gorenstein injective model structure. We
also have Gorenstein injective envelopes from Corollary 6.3. Our main new appli-
cation of this is described in the next example.
Example 7.1.1. Let X be a quasi-compact and semi-separated scheme, with
{Ui}N
i=1 a semiseparating open affine covering of X.
We let Qco(X) denote the
category of quasi-coherent sheaves on X. Then Qco(X) admits a family of very flat
generators, as introduced by Positselski in [Pos12], and all such sheaves have finite
projective dimension not exceeding N. In more detail, by [Pos12, Cor. 4.1.2 (b),
Cor. 4.1.4 and Lemma B.1.2], the pair of classes (VF(X), CA(X)) of very flat and
contraadjusted quasi-coherent sheaves on X forms a complete hereditary cotorsion
pair on Qco(X). In particular, VF(X) contains a set of generators for Qco(X). Now,
by [Pos12, Lemma 5.5.12(c)], together with the equivalent conditions (3) and (4)
of [Pos12, Lemma B.1.9(c)], it follows that every very flat quasi-coherent sheaf has
finite projective dimension not exceeding N.
Therefore, the Gorenstein injective model structure exists on Qco(X), and ev-
ery quasi-coherent sheaf has a Gorenstein injective envelope.
We note that the
Quillen equivalence of Proposition 6.2 strengthens our previous result from [EG21,
Them. 5.2], which was for semi-separated Noetherian schemes.
Example 7.1.2. Let G be a locally noetherian Grothendieck category whose de-
rived category is compactly generated. In [Kra05, Them. 7.12], Krause shows that
the objects with vanishing Tate cohomology, along with the Gorenstein injectives,
form a complete cotorsion pair in G. In particular, the compact generation implies
the existence of Tate trivial generators for G.
7.2. Ding injectives, and Gorenstein FPn-injectives. The pursuit of Goren-
stein injective envelopes of modules over a ring led to a number of interesting
variants. These include the Ding injective modules, the Gorenstein AC-injective
modules, and the Gorenstein FPn-injective modules; see [BGH14], [Gil17b], and
[BGP19], and further references therein. It was shown in [GI22] that over any ring,
envelopes exist for each of these types of modules. Just like the case of Gorenstein
injectives this can be generalized to Grothendieck categories.
Example 7.2.1. For any Grothendieck category G, there is a set S of isomorphism
representatives for all objects of type FPn in the sense of [BGP19].
(This fact
is nicely explained in [BOPP22, §2.2].)
Such a set cogenerates the class B :=
S⊥, of all FPn-injective objects. So whenever G is locally finite dimensional we
obtain Gorenstein FPn-injective envelopes and model structures. It is notable that
we previously only knew this in the case that G is, in addition, an n-coherent

locally noetherian, Ding injectives when G is locally coherent, etc. See [BGP19,
Remark 6.3].)
7.3. Strongly FP-injectives, and other examples. Consider again, properties
(1) and (2) stated in Theorem 5.5. For any B := S⊥, where S is a given set, the
Gorenstein B-injective model structure is the best possible injective model structure
that ‘kills’ the class B. Here we point out just one interesting example of a class B
for which we were previously unaware that such a construction could be made.
Example 7.3.1. Following [LGO17], a module N over a ring R is said to be strongly
FP-injective if Exti
R(F, N) = 0 for all finitely presented modules F and all i ≥1. A
number of authors have studied these modules. For example, see [BHP24, EK24].
Let SFI denote the class of all strongly FP-injectives. Choose a set S = {Ωn(F)}
of all syzygies of all the modules in some set of isomorphism representatives for the
finitely presented modules. Then one can check that we have S⊥= SFI. Thus we
obtain Gorenstein SFI-injective envelopes and model structures for any ring R.
7.4. Generators for the stable derived category. When it comes to the prob-
lem of finding Tate trivial generating sets U, it is natural to consider exI, the class
of all exact complexes of injectives. After all, if each X ∈exI is U-acyclic then so
too is each X ∈exIB, for any class B. Note that if we take P to be the class of all
projective objects, then exI = exIP (even if 0 is the only existing projective). So in
the terminology of Definition 5.3, saying that U is a set of P-Tate trivial generators
is equivalent to saying that each X ∈exI is U-acyclic. Of course, a set of generators
of finite projective dimension is such a set; see Lemma 4.4. In this way we have the
following extension of [Gil17a, Them. 5.7].
Example 7.4.1. If G admits a set U of P-Tate trivial generators, then taking
A = U = B in Theorem 3.3 immediately yields MU = (All, W, exI). This is the
injective model structure for Krause’s stable derived category of G. The related
recollement also holds in the same way as [Gil17a, Cor. 5.8].
We do not know an example, but it seems feasible for a category to admit a
P-Tate trivial set of generators which are not of finite projective dimension. For
example, if G has a closed symmetric monoidal structure ⊗, then having a set
of ⊗-flat generators, along with having cotorsion periodicity (see [BCE20] for a
reference in module categories), might provide such an example. This approach
was taken in [EG21, Theorem 4.1], though as explained in Example 7.1.1, we do
have generators of finite projective dimension for such schemes X as considered
in [EG21, Theorem 4.1].
On the other hand, Neeman’s example [Nee14, Example 4.1] shows that there
exist locally noetherian Grothendieck categories which do not admit a set of P-Tate
trivial generators. In particular, Grothendieck categories need not admit B-Tate
trivial generators for every class B. But we don’t know whether or not this is the
case when B is the class of all injectives. One would like an example similar to
Neeman’s but for totally acyclic complexes of injectives.
7.5. PGF and PGFB modules. We just remark that the accessibility of right co-
torsion classes can also be used to approach the PGF-modules introduced in [SS20].
Given a ring R, a projectively coresolved Gorenstein flat R-module M, or PGF-
module for short, is a 0-cycle, M = Z0X, of some exact complex X of projective

all injective (right) R-modules. Let T be a set of injective (right) R-modules as in
