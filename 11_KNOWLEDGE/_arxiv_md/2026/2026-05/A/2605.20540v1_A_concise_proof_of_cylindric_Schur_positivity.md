A CONCISE PROOF OF CYLINDRIC SCHUR POSITIVITY
ALEXANDER DOBNER
Abstract. Cylindric Schur functions are a family of symmetric functions that generalize skew
Schur functions. We give a short proof that skew cylindric Schur functions expand positively
in terms of non-skew cylindric Schur functions.
In particular, we show that the expansion
coefficients are fusion coefficients.
1. Introduction
The cylindric Schur functions are a family of symmetric functions which are generating func-
tions for cylindric semistandard Young tableaux. They were introduced by Postnikov [6] in
his study of the quantum cohomology of Grassmannians. They are also related to fusion co-
efficients in type A. (The connection between quantum cohomology of Grassmannians and
fusion coefficients mirrors the connection between ordinary cohomology of Grassmannians and
Littlewood-Richardson coefficients.)
McNamara [4] conjectured that the skew cylindric Schur functions expand positively in terms
of the non-skew cylindric Schur functions. This was first proved by Lee [3], and a different proof
was given by Korff and Palazzo [2]. Both of these proofs are rather lengthy. In this note we
give a very short proof of this result.

2.1. Partitions and cylindric tableaux. A partition is a weakly decreasing sequence of
nonnegative integers λ = (λ1, λ2, . . .) with finitely many nonzero terms. The size of λ is |λ| =

A skew shape is an ordered pair of partitions (µ, λ) such that µi ≤λi for all i. We write this
ordered pair as λ/µ. The size of λ/µ is |λ/µ| = |λ|−|µ|. A horizontal strip is a skew shape λ/µ
such that λ1 ≥µ1 ≥λ2 ≥µ2 ≥· · · . (Note: in terms of Young diagrams, λ/µ is a horizontal
strip if it contains at most one box in each column.)
Fix a pair of positive integers (N, L) throughout the rest of the paper. An (N, L)-partition
is a partition λ such that ℓ(λ) ≤N and λ1 −λN ≤L. An (N, L)-horizontal strip is a horizontal
strip λ/µ such that ℓ(λ) ≤N and λ1 −µN ≤L. Note that if λ/µ is an (N, L)-horizontal strip
then λ and µ are necessarily (N, L)-partitions and |λ/µ| ≤L.
