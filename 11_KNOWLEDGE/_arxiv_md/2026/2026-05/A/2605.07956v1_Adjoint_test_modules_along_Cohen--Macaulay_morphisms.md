ADJOINT TEST MODULES ALONG COHEN–MACAULAY MORPHISMS
JAVIER CARVAJAL-ROJAS AND AXEL ST¨ABLER
Abstract. We provide a transformation rule for adjoint test modules along Cohen–Macaulay
maps between Cohen–Macaulay varieties that have F-rational geometric fibers. This is, in
part, an effective version of Enescu’s theorem on the ascent of F-rationality under local maps
with F-rational geometric fibers.
1. Introduction
Rational singularities are the gold standard for mild, nice singularities in algebraic ge-
ometry. Under reduction mod p, rationality corresponds to F-rationality in the theory of
F-singularities. A key foundational result in this theory is that of Florian Enescu [Ene00,
Theorem 2.24]. Enescu showed that, under mild technical hypotheses, F-rationality ascends
along flat local homomorphisms θ: (R, m, k) −→(S, n, l) with geometrically F-rational fibers;
i.e., k′ ⊗R S is F-rational for all field extensions k′/k. However, there are a few ways in
which one can measure F-rationality, and so it is natural to wonder about transformation
rules for these invariants that serve as effective generalizations of Enescu’s theorem. Here, we
focus on adjoint test modules.
Recall that F-rational singularities are normal and Cohen–Macaulay singularities. To see
what they are among them, let us fix an F-finite base field k (e.g., perfect), and consider
a normal Cohen–Macaulay k-variety X with canonical sheaf ωX. It comes equipped with
the so-called Cartier operator κ: F∗ωX −→ωX, and so (ωX, κ) is a Cartier module on X.
The adjoint test module of X is τ(ωX) ⊂ωX and is defined as the smallest non-trivial
Cartier OX-submodule of (ωX, κ), i.e., the smallest non-trivial submodule M ⊂ωX such that
κ(F∗M) ⊂M. Then, X has F-rational singularities if and only if τ(ωX) = ωX. Thus, the
annihilator ideal of ωX/τ(ωX) cuts out the non-F-rational locus of X, thereby measuring
the F-rationality of X. The above can be relaxed to the case where X is a Cohen–Macaulay
reduced k-scheme of finite type, at the expense of a more technical definition; see Definition 2.2.
The goal of this note is to prove the following transformation rule.
Main Theorem. Let X be a reduced Cohen–Macaulay scheme of finite type over an F-finite
field k. Let f : Y −→X be a finite-type Cohen–Macaulay map of relative dimension n with
dualizing sheaf ωf. Suppose that the fibers of f containing closed points of Y are geometrically
F-injective, and that those over the generic points of X (i.e. the generic fibers) are F-rational.
Then,
τ(ωY ) = ωf ⊗f ∗τ(ωX),
which entails that Y is reduced and Cohen–Macaulay. Thus, if X is F-rational then so is Y .

Our proof has two main steps. First, we make essential use of duality theory (for Co-
hen–Macaulay maps) to express explicitly the Cartier operator on Y in terms of the Cartier
operator of X and the relative Cartier operator. This is why our result needs the finite-type
hypotheses. Then, the F-injectivity hypothesis on the fibers is engineered for the relative
Cartier operator to be surjective. The formula then follows by direct calculation once one
constructs a common test element for X and Y . The F-rationality hypothesis on generic
fibers is used to ensure this, as well as for Y to also be reduced and Cohen–Macaulay.
We note that Enescu’s result does not require the morphism to be Cohen–Macaulay (in
fact, much of Enescu’s paper is devoted to showing that the relative Frobenius is parameter
pure in the situation he considers), so our assumption about the morphism can possibly be
weakened. We also note that Enescu has relaxed assumptions when the base is Gorenstein.
It is plausible that our hypothesis on fibers can also be weakened in this case.
In a slightly different direction, we also ask how the F-rational signature behaves in
such a situation. The F-rational signature is another prominent invariant that quantifies
F-rationality; see [HY22], [San15], and [ST24] for background on this notion. This is probably
difficult. For instance, the invariance of the rational signature for a regular morphism was
only proved recently by Shiji Lyu [Lyu24, Theorem 7.2].
For the rest of this note, all schemes and rings are defined over Fp (i.e., they have equal
characteristic p > 0). Given a scheme or ring X, we denote the e-th iterate of its (absolute)
Frobenius endomorphism by F e = F e
X : X −→X, and we use the usual shorthand notation
q := pe. It is said that X is F-finite if F is a finite morphism.
2. Preliminaries
In this section, we briefly explain the ingredients and concepts involved in the formulation
of our Main Theorem. We start with the following basic observation.
Lemma 2.1. Let R be an F-finite reduced Cohen–Macaulay ring with canonical module ωR.
For p ∈Spec R with residue field κ(p), the following statements hold.
(a) If p is non-minimal, then HomRp(κ(p), ωRp) = 0.
(b) If p is minimal, then HomRp(κ(p), ωRp) = Rp.
Proof. Suppose that p is minimal. Then, κ(p) = Rp as R is reduced. Hence, ωRp = Rp = κ(p)
and so part (b) follows at once.
Assume now that p is not minimal. Since Rp is Cohen–Macaulay, it contains a regular
element x that is not a unit. Let φ: κ(p) −→ωRp be a non-zero Rp-linear homomorphism.
Composing it with the canonical quotient Rp −→κ(p) yields a non-zero map φ′ : Rp −→ωRp
with ker φ′ ⊃p. However, x /∈ker φ′ by [Kov17, Lemma 3.23], which is a contradiction.

The final lemma is rather general and will be used to show that γ is surjective.
Lemma 3.4. Let f : Spec S −→Spec R be a map of noetherian rings and ϕ: M −→N be a
map of finitely generated S-modules. If ϕ is surjective after base change to all fibers of f that
contain a closed point of Spec S, then it is surjective.
Proof. This follows from a variant of Nakayama’s lemma (see [Sta18, Lemma 0GLX]): If
p ∈Spec R and q ∈f −1(p), then q ∩R = p and q ∩(R ∖p) = ∅. Assume that x1, . . . , xn are
generators of N base changed to the fiber f −1(p), and that y1, . . . , ym are generators of M
base changed to the fiber with yi 7→xi for 1 ≤i ≤n. Then, the lemma applied with the ideal
pS and the multiplicative system R ∖p shows that there is s ∈(R ∖p) + pS such that the
xj, yj are generators of Ns and Ms, respectively. We assert that q ∈D(s) ⊂Spec S. Indeed,
write s = x + y with x ∈R ∖p and y ∈pS ⊂q. If s ∈q, then x ∈q, but this is impossible
as x ∈R ∖p and q ∩(R ∖p) = ∅.
