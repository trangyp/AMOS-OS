Abstract
We study a family of above-below Ramsey functions AB(d)(k) defined for sequences of points in
Rd whose projections to Rd−1 have cyclic order type. The case d = 3 is the above-below function
AB(k) that was first introduced by Pohoata and Zakharov in their work on the Erdős–Szekeres
problem in R3. We prove the sharp estimate
AB(k) = 22Θ(k),
and, more generally, show that AB(d)(k) is closely related to the higher-order cup-cap function
of Eliáš and Matoušek and the monotone Ramsey numbers of Balko.

Introduction
In a seminal paper [5], Erdős and Szekeres initiated one of the central threads of combinatorial
geometry by proving that every sufficiently large set of points in the plane in general position
contains a large subset in convex position. More generally, for d ≥2, let ESd(n) denote the smallest
integer such that every set of at least ESd(n) points in Rd in general position contains n points
in convex position. The existence of ESd(n) follows from Ramsey’s theorem, but the asymptotic
behavior of these functions remained mysterious until recent years.
In the plane, it has been long known since 1935 that ES2(n) is exponential in n, and in a
celebrated paper from about ten years ago Suk [14] established that ES2(n) = 2n+o(n). See also
[8] for the best known quantitative bound, as well as a generalization to the pseudoline setting.
In higher dimensions, the situation is dramatically different. Answering a question of Erdős (see
for instance [2]), Pohoata and Zakharov established that ESd(n) = 2o(n) for every fixed d ≥3, by
showing that already in R3 substantially fewer points are needed to force a convex polytope on n
vertices than are required in the plane [11].
One of the ingredients in the proof from [11] is a Ramsey-type statement about points in space
with convexly ordered planar projections. Let x1, . . . , xN ∈R3 be points in general position whose
orthogonal projections onto the first two coordinates π(x1), . . . , π(xN) ∈R2 are the consecutive
vertices of a convex polygon. If i < i′ < j < j′, then the projected segments π(xi)π(xj) and
π(xi′)π(xj′) cross in exactly one point. We say that the segment xixj is above xi′xj′ if, over this
crossing point, the point of xixj has larger third coordinate than the corresponding point of xi′xj′.
Otherwise, we say that xixj is below xi′xj′.
