Abstract
This paper develops a unified framework for observables in n-plectic geome-
try, extending the L∞-algebra of Hamiltonian (n −1)-forms to Hamiltonian forms
of all degrees via a degree-shifting Grassmann variable u that encodes subman-
ifold codimension.
Interpreting k-form observables as k-dimensional topological
defects yields a recursive gluing construction that assembles into a semi-simplicial
set sOb•(M), which we prove satisfies the Kan filling property, thereby providing
an n-groupoid model for observables. From this semi-simplicial perspective we ex-
tract cohomological invariants and construct a recursive inner product leading to a
categorified pre-n-Hilbert space. The hierarchical structure of polarizations yields
a natural quantization scheme matching the 1-polarization classification of multi-
symplectic geometry. The resulting framework bridges higher algebraic structures
with higher categorical geometry and establishes a systematic foundation for the
geometric quantization of extended objects.
Keywords: multisymplectic geometry, L∞-algebra, geometric quantization

Introduction
Multisymplectic geometry generalizes symplectic geometry to manifolds M equipped with
a closed, nondegenerate (n+1)-form ω; the pair (M, ω) is called an n-plectic manifold [16,
29]. While symplectic geometry (n = 1) describes point particles, n-plectic geometry
governs the dynamics of (n −1)-dimensional extended objects [3, 4]. The coupling of
such objects to background fields is encoded by Wess–Zumino terms, generalizing the
minimal coupling of a point particle to an electromagnetic field [14, 15, 35]. Moreover,
multisymplectic methods retain manifest covariance, avoiding the 3 + 1 split of canonical
quantization, and therefore provide a natural framework for field theory.
In n-plectic geometry, observables are Hamiltonian forms α satisfying dα = −ιvαω
for some multivector field vα.
Seminal work by Baez, Rogers and others established
that the space of top-degree Hamiltonian forms (degree n −1) carries an L∞-algebra
structure, generalising the Poisson algebra of symplectic geometry [2, 28]. Restricting to
top-degree forms, however, is both mathematically unnatural and physically inadequate:
observables of various degrees appear naturally in extended topological field theories [1,
23], generalised symmetries [5, 6, 7, 13], and topological phases of matter [33, 26]; in
our framework such observables are precisely Hamiltonian forms, with a Hamiltonian
k-form interpreted as a k-dimensional observable. A complete theory of observables in
multisymplectic geometry must therefore encompass Hamiltonian forms of every degree.
The present work addresses this need and simultaneously lays the combinatorial ground-
work for higher geometric quantization. We proceed through the following constructions.
Algebraic construction. We extend the L∞-algebra of observables to all Hamiltonian
forms by introducing a formal variable u of bidegree (−1, 1). This variable shifts the
form degree and encodes the codimension of a submanifold inside the worldvolume of
an extended object. The resulting bigraded space Ln−1

higher brackets defined via interior products with Hamiltonian multivector fields. We
prove that these brackets satisfy the homotopy Jacobi identities, yielding a graded L∞-
algebra (Theorem 3.2) that unifies observables on submanifolds of different codimensions.
Within this algebra we identify a higher Heisenberg subalgebra generated by mutually
commuting Hamiltonian vector fields, which generalizes the classical Heisenberg algebra
of symplectic geometry.
Geometric construction. Interpreting k-form Hamiltonian observables as k-dimensional
topological defects, we develop a recursive gluing construction that assigns Hamiltonian
forms to submanifolds of every codimension. This structure naturally assembles into a
semi-simplicial set sOb•(M) (Definition 4.2.1), whose k-simplices are smooth singular k-
simplices equipped with Hamiltonian k-forms. We prove that sOb•(M) satisfies the Kan
filling property (Theorem 4.2), thereby providing an n-groupoid model for observables.
The linearization R[sOb•(M)] yields a combinatorial n-vector space, and morphisms are
captured by the path-space semi-simplicial set sOb•(DiffMan(∆1, M)) ∼= sOb•+1(M).

Higher geometric quantization. The framework developed in this paper also lays the
combinatorial foundation for the geometric quantization of n-plectic manifolds. Under the
prequantum condition [ω/2π] ∈Hn+1(M, Z), the semi-simplicial set sOb•(M) furnishes
a combinatorial model of the corresponding n-gerbe with connection. Quantum states
are defined as U(1)-valued cochains on sOb•(M), forming a cosimplicial set qOb•(M),
and a recursive inner product taking values in U(1)[qOb•+1(M)] endows the linearized
space with the structure of a categorified pre-n-Hilbert space. The resulting hierarchy
of polarizations matches the 1-polarization scheme of multisymplectic geometry [27] and
offers a systematic path toward higher quantisation.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls the necessary background on graded
linear algebra, multivector calculus, L∞-algebras, simplicial sets, and n-plectic geometry.
Section 3 contains the algebraic construction of the graded L∞-algebra of all Hamiltonian
forms and the higher Heisenberg subalgebra. Section 4 develops the geometric framework:
the semi-simplicial set sOb•(M), its Kan property, the associated n-vector space, and
cohomological invariants. Section 5 relates sOb•(M) to n-gerbes and prequantization,
introduces the cosimplicial set of quantum states, the recursive inner product, and the
hierarchy of polarizations. Section 6 concludes with an outlook on future directions.

Simplicial sets, Kan complexes, and semi-simplicial sets
In Section 4
we will assign to each k-dimensional submanifold a Hamiltonian k-form, organized into a

is recalled below; a standard reference is [12].
Definition 2.3. The simplicial category ∆has objects [n] = {0, 1, . . . , n} (n ≥0) and
order-preserving maps. A simplicial set is a contravariant functor X• : ∆op →Set. Thus
it consists of sets Xn (n-simplices) with face maps di : Xn →Xn−1 (0 ≤i ≤n) and
degeneracy maps si : Xn →Xn+1 (0 ≤i ≤n) satisfying the simplicial identities.
Definition 2.4. A semi-simplicial set (also called a ∆-set) is a presheaf on the sub-
category ∆inj of ∆containing only injective (i.e., face) maps. Thus it has face maps di
but no degeneracies.
A semi-simplicial set can be turned into a simplicial set by freely adding degeneracies;
however, if the semi-simplicial set already satisfies the Kan condition (every horn has a
filler), then the extension to a simplicial set is unique. More precisely:

Kan complex, i.e., every horn Λn

2.5
n-plectic geometry and observables
A multisymplectic (or n-plectic) man-
ifold generalizes a symplectic manifold: while symplectic geometry describes point par-
ticles, n-plectic geometry governs (n −1)-dimensional extended objects [3, 4]. Rogers
and others established that Hamiltonian (n −1)-forms on an n-plectic manifold form an
L∞-algebra [28, 2]. Here we extend this to Hamiltonian forms of all degrees.
Definition 2.6. An (n + 1)-form ω on a smooth manifold M is n-plectic if it is closed
and nondegenerate, i.e., ιvω = 0 ⇒v = 0 for every v ∈TxM. The pair (M, ω) is an
n-plectic manifold.
The n-plectic structure induces bundle maps T k : Xk(M) →Ωn−k+1(M), v 7→ιvω,
leading to the notion of Hamiltonian observables.
Definition 2.7. Let (M, ω) be n-plectic. An (n−k)-form α ∈Ωn−k(M) is Hamiltonian if
there exists vα ∈Xk(M) with dα = −ιvαω. The Poisson brace is {α, β} = (−1)|vβ| ιvβιvαω.
The spaces of such forms and multivector fields are denoted Ωn−k
Ham(M) and Xk
Ham(M).
Hamiltonian multivector fields satisfy Lvω = 0. A Hamiltonian form α determines
its associated multivector field vα only up to elements of Ker(T k), while vα determines α
only up to closed forms. This ambiguity reflects a gauge freedom inherent in the choice of
observables. Moreover, the Hamiltonian condition dα = −ιvω may admit solutions only
locally on contractible open subsets. We therefore introduce the following notion, which
will play a central role in the geometric constructions of Section 4.
Definition 2.8. A multivector field v ∈Xk(M) is n-plectic if Lvω = 0. For such v, on
any contractible open subset U ⊂M there exists a local (n −k)-form αU ∈Ωn−k(U) with
dαU = −ιvω|U, called a local observable associated with v. On overlaps, local observables
differ by closed forms, and their equivalence classes in local de Rham cohomology encode
topological charges [10, 31, 32].
Working assumptions.
For the constructions that follow we make two assumptions
that guarantee well-definedness.
1. Topological triviality (Section 3). The n-plectic manifold (M, ω) is topologically
trivial, i.e. its de Rham cohomology vanishes in positive degrees. This provides a
global primitive θ with dθ = ω and ensures that all Hamiltonian forms are globally
defined, simplifying the algebraic description of the L∞-algebra. The general case
is treated by gluing local data.
2. Hamiltonian translations (Sections 4–5). The infinitesimal generators of world-
volume translations are required to be Hamiltonian vector fields. Physically, the n-
dimensional worldvolume of an extended object carries a natural action of the trans-
lation group, and the corresponding conserved Noether charges (energy-momentum)
must be represented as genuine observables in the Hamiltonian algebra Ω•
Ham(M).
Mathematically, this assumption guarantees that the auxiliary vector fields in the
definition of sOb•(M) are Hamiltonian; their pairwise commutativity then follows
from the fact that they are the images of commuting coordinate vector fields under
the tangent map of τk. This commutativity is the essential ingredient in the proof
of the Kan property (Theorem 4.2), where the filler is obtained from the top face
simply by deleting the generating vector field that corresponds to the inward normal
direction. No global topological triviality of M is required; all constructions rely
only on the local Poincar´e lemma on contractible open sets.

Similar to the framework of generalized global sym-

topological defects and their action corresponds to a charge crossing a defect, we adopt
an analogous picture.
In our setting, k-form Hamiltonian observables are interpreted
as k-dimensional defects. Under orientation reversal, the Hamiltonian associated with a
manifold changes sign — a property that will be relevant when considering defect orienta-
tion. When an observable α crosses a defect γ, it transforms into β := l2(α, γ), encoding

forms encodes the properties of a global symmetry; indeed, the conserved charges of such
a symmetry are precisely given by Hamiltonian forms. A natural consistency condition
follows: if α can be continuously deformed around γ without crossing, then l2(α, γ) = 0,
reflecting trivial action. Higher algebraic relations, such as the homotopy Jacobi identity,
admit a geometric interpretation: they reflect the fact that the order in which an observ-
able crosses a network of defects can be rearranged — a phenomenon reminiscent of the

4.2.4
Relation to L∞-algebras
The semi-simplicial set sOb•(M) faithfully rep-
resents the totality of Hamiltonian forms on M. However, its face maps naturally close
only on the subalgebra generated by mutually commuting Hamiltonian vector fields, i.e.,
the higher Heisenberg algebra of Remark 3.1. The full non-abelian L∞-algebra L con-
structed in Theorem 3.2 should therefore be interpreted as the symmetry algebra that is
expected to act on sOb•(M), rather than as a structure directly carried by the simplicial
set itself.
A complete realisation of this action encounters a fundamental geometric obstruction.
Each k-simplex of sOb•(M) consists of two intertwined data: an underlying singular
simplex σk and a Hamiltonian form α. While the action on the Hamiltonian form can be
expressed algebraically via the higher Poisson brackets, the action on the singular simplex
would require a geometric deformation of σk along the Hamiltonian multivector field vx
associated with an element x ∈L, producing a new singular simplex whose dimension
reflects the degree shift of the bracket. This geometric deformation cannot be captured
by the purely combinatorial face maps of the semi-simplicial set and remains an open
problem. A systematic construction of such an L∞-module structure on sOb•(M), and
its dual action on the quantum state space, is left for future investigation.

Remark 5.2 (Anomaly cancellation, Wess-Zumino terms, and Wilson membranes). A

boundary amplitude is gauge-invariant. The cocycle condition δeiK = 1 is the algebraic
statement of this invariance. Equivalently, eiK is a Wilson membrane whose endpoints

Remark 5.3 (Relation to gerbe-section 2-Hilbert spaces and higher-rank generalisations).
In the 2-plectic case, Bunk–S¨amann–Szab´o [8] defined a 2-Hilbert space whose objects are
twisted vector bundles of arbitrary finite rank over the prequantum gerbe. The cosimpli-

sections of twisted line bundles over the same gerbe—the rank-1 subsector of their con-

geometric quantisation, quantum states are sections of the prequantum line bundle rather
than the bundle itself.
To promote this to higher-rank twisted vector bundles, and in particular to the 2-

set qOb•(M) then becomes a cosimplicial object in this 2-category, and its linearisation
can be understood as a combinatorial model for the 2-vector space of sections of a 2-line
bundle. A detailed investigation of this enriched setting is left for future work.

The hierarchy proceeds recursively from the top dimension downward. At level k = n,
U(1)[qObn(M)] is interpreted as the partition function on ∆n, while U(1)[qObn−1(M)]
gives the quantum states on its boundary. A polarization on Pn−1 yields the wave function
space familiar from ordinary field theory quantization. Repeating this from k = n down
to 0, each step performs a polarization: one conjugate momentum direction is eliminated,
and the transverse motion becomes a new positional degree of freedom. The form degree
decreases from k to k −1, capturing both dimensional reduction and the redistribution
of degrees of freedom.
The resulting hierarchy is compatible with the 1-polarization classification of [27], ac-
cording to which an n-plectic manifold admits n distinct orders of polarization. To see
this, recall that in the De Donder–Weyl formulation of classical field theory, the multi-
symplectic potential locally takes the form Θ = pµ ∧dϕ ∧dn−1xµ, where ϕ denotes the
field, pµ its conjugate polymomenta, and dn−1xµ the volume element on a codimension-1
hypersurface. A 1-polarization in this setting eliminates all polymomentum components
at once, projecting the prequantum state space onto functionals of the field configuration
ϕ and its domain coordinates x. In our framework, the phase space is stratified over the
hierarchy of mapping spaces, with each level carrying a single polymomentum component.
Applying a symplectic polarization at each level eliminates the pµ one by one; the cumu-
lative effect is therefore equivalent to a 1-polarization of [27]. The same recursive pattern
can also be interpreted as a multi-step condensation of topological excitations [17, 19]: at
each step a class of topological charges is condensed, driving the system to the next lower
level and suggesting that polarization may be regarded as a mathematical realization of
topological condensation.

Conclusion and Outlook
We have developed a unified framework for observables in multisymplectic geometry. Al-
gebraically, the introduction of a degree shifting Grassmann variable u extends Rogers’
L∞-algebra to Hamiltonian forms of all degrees, yielding a graded L∞-algebra (Theo-
rem 3.2) whose brackets are defined via interior products with Hamiltonian multivector
fields. Geometrically, interpreting k-form observables as k-dimensional defects leads to a
semi-simplicial set sOb•(M), which we prove is a Kan complex (Theorem 4.2) and thus
provides an n-groupoid model for observables. Its linearization yields a combinatorial n-
vector space, and the associated cochain complex Ch•
obs(M) encodes gluing obstructions
as well as adiabatic invariants. Under the prequantum condition [ω/2π] ∈Hn+1(M, Z),
sOb•(M) furnishes a combinatorial model of an n-gerbe with connection.
Quantum
states, defined as complex-valued cochains on sOb•(M), form a cosimplicial set qOb•(M),
and a recursive inner product taking values in U(1)[qOb•+1(M)] endows the linearized
space with the structure of a categorified pre-n-Hilbert space. The hierarchical reduction
of form degrees matches the 1-polarization scheme of [27].
Several directions remain for future investigation. A detailed comparison with the
2-Hilbert space of [8] for n = 2 are natural next steps. Moreover, the left L∞-module
structure on Cobs
• (M) indicates that the full observable algebra acts geometrically on
sOb•(M). Finally, the E∞-algebra structure on Ch•
obs(M) is expected to capture braiding
statistics of topological excitations, such as anyons in topological phases of matter.
