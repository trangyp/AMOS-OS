A SOLUTION TO THE YAU-TIAN-DONALDSON CONJECTURE
THROUGH SPECIAL FUJITA APPROXIMATIONS
ANTONIO TRUSIANI
Abstract. We show that any big line bundle on a smooth projective variety admits a special Fujita
approximation: the volume and the first Riemann-Roch coefficient are both approximated by those
of ample Q-line bundles on higher models. Exploiting previous works by Boucksom, Jonsson and
Li, we solve the Boucksom-Jonsson Regularization Conjecture on the Non-Archimedean entropy
functional. As a main consequence, we obtain a solution to the (uniform version of the) Yau-Tian-
Donaldson Conjecture: a polarized smooth projective variety (X, L) admits a cscK metric if and only
if it is Aut◦(X, L)-uniformly K-stable. This extends the known Yau-Tian-Donaldson correspondence
for smooth Fano varieties.
1. Introduction
A central theme in Complex Geometry is to relate the existence of special metrics to algebro-
geometric notions, and the following solution to the so-called Yau-Tian-Donaldson Conjecture ([130,
120, 61]) represents one of the most classical and studied instances of such phenomena.
Theorem A (Solution to the Yau-Tian-Donaldson Conjecture). Let L →X be an ample line
bundle on a smooth projective variety. Then the following are equivalent:
i) there exists a constant scalar curvature K¨ahler (cscK) metric in c1(L);
ii) (X, L) is Aut◦(X, L)-uniformly K-stable.
In particular if Aut◦(X, L) is trivial then the following are equivalent:
i) there exists a unique cscK metric in c1(L);
ii) (X, L) is uniformly K-stable.
Some comments are in order. We first recall that Aut◦(X, L) indicates the identity component
of the automorphism group of (X, L). Modulo the fiberwise action, Aut(X, L) reduces to the linear
automorphism group LAut(X) [66]. In the second part of Theorem A the triviality of Aut◦(X, L)
is intended as LAut(X) = {Id}. Following [120, 61] K-stability then requires that for any (normal)
ample test configurations (X, L) the Donaldson-Futaki invariant DF(X, L) is non-negative and equal
to zero exactly when (X, L) = (X ×P1, p∗
1L+cX0) for c ∈Q, i.e. when (X, L) is trivial [115, 98, 20].
Instead, the uniform K-stability holds if DF(X, L) ≥δ JNA(X, L) for a uniform constant δ, where
JNA(X, L) is a mininum norm which measures the triviality of ample test configurations [117, 51, 20].
As any non-trivial cocharacter C∗→Aut(X, L) defines a non-trivial product test configuration with
a vanishing Donaldson-Futaki invariant (which goes back to the classical Futaki invariant [69] of the
associated action), in the general case with automorphisms the notion of K-stability and its uniform
version must be refined. In this context the Aut◦(X, L)-uniform K-stability of Theorem A represents
the weakest uniform notion. It only deals with Aut◦(X, L)-equivariant ample test configurations and
asks that their Donaldson-Futaki invariants dominate a uniform multiple of an equivariant version
of the JNA-invariant that considers all the possible twists induced by cocharacters C∗→Aut(X, L)
[75, 76, 86, 87]. Observe that by definition Aut◦(X, L)-uniform K-stability requires Aut◦(X, L) to be
reductive, which is a classical known necessary condition for the existence of cscK metrics [89, 94].
It is worth stressing that Theorem A solves the version of the Yau-Tian-Donaldson Conjecture given
in [87, Conj. 2.26], but Theorem A can be also stated for a uniform version of K-polystability as
explained below in Corollary B.

Let us now discuss previous results regarding Theorem A. The implications (i) ⇒(ii) are known
and are given by the combination of many works as we are going to briefly describe. Following
previous works [90, 121, 7, 8, 36, 42, 10, 11], in [30, 31] the existence of cscK metrics has been char-
acterized in terms of the J-coercivity of the Mabuchi functional in a certain metric completion E1 of
the space of K¨ahler metrics representing c1(L). Then the Donaldson-Futaki invariant and the (equi-
variant) JNA-functional of an (equivariant) ample test configuration (X, L) can be essentially and
respectively obtained as the slope of the (equivariant) Mabuchi functional and of the (equivariant)
J-functional along a geodesic ray in E1 [103, 104, 109] associated to (X, L) [21].
Note that the existence of cscK metrics in c1(L) implies the K-polystability of (X, L) [114, 91].
However the vice versa seems to not be expected as consequence of [1, 73]. In this perspective the
uniform version of Theorem A is suspected to be optimal.
Let us also recall that Theorem A is known for smooth spherical polarized varieties. In the
toric case it follows from combining [75] and [30, 31]. In the general spherical case any big test
configuration is a Mori dream space (see the appendix of [45]), thus Aut◦(X, L)-uniform K-stability
is equivalent to the analogous notions for models and [87] gives the existence of cscK metrics.
The picture is clearer when L is proportional to the canonical bundle, and Theorem A is already
known in such cases. Indeed if L = KX then there are no non-trivial vector fields, a cscK metric in
c1(KX) is a K¨ahler-Einstein metric and it always exists as proved in [129, 3]. Moreover, K-stability
holds [99]. In the Fano case L = −KX, cscK metrics in c1(X) are positively curved K¨ahler-Einstein
metrics. In this setting the Yau-Tian-Donaldson Conjecture with respect to K-polystability has
been established in [32, 33, 34, 122] (see also [44, 118, 35, 131]). Furthermore the aforementioned
implication from the existence of K¨ahler-Einstein metrics to K-polystability has been also extended
in [6] to singular Fano varieties. Then the uniform version for Fano (not necessarily smooth) varieties
of Theorem A has been shown in [86], relying on previous works [9, 88].
Let us also underline that the K-stability notion has turned out to be extremely precious in
Algebraic Geometry in the last decades, for instance it has been tremendously advantageous in the
construction of moduli spaces of Fano varieties. This aspect is beyond our purposes and we invite
the interested readers to look at [128] and references therein.

How Theorem B implies Theorem A passing through a solution to the Boucksom-
Jonsson Regularization Conjecture. Theorem B solves [85, Conj. 4.4, 4.7]. In particular, as
explained in [85, Lem. 4.5], it also provides a solution to the Boucksom-Jonsson Regularization
Conjecture [22, Conj. 2.5] and to the Yau-Tian-Donaldson Conjecture of Theorem A (cf. [87, Prop.
6.7]). More precisely, following the Boucksom-Jonsson’s Non-Archimedean approach to K-stability
[23, 22, 25, 24], in [87] Li proved that a strengthening notion of Aut◦(X, L)-uniform K-stability
implies the equivariant coercivity of the Mabuchi functional, and hence the existence of cscK metrics
in c1(L) by [30, 31]. Such a stronger notion is called Aut◦(X, L)-uniform K-stability for models
and, roughly speaking, it also considers big test configurations (see also [85, 55]). In particular, a
strategy for proving Theorem A consists of checking that Aut◦(X, L)-uniform K-stability implies
the analogous stability for models. As observed in [87] (see also [85, Lem. 4.5]), such implication
would hold if someone solved the so-called Boucksom-Jonsson Regularization Conjecture. To explain
the latter, we recall that the set of ample test configurations HNA, interpreted as positive Non-
Archimedean metrics, can be endowed with a natural Darvas metric whose metric completion E1,NA
admits a meaningful description in terms of maximal geodesic rays [40, 9, 108]. This mimics the
Archimedean picture of the Mabuchi space H of K¨ahler metrics in c1(L) (see [90, 110, 60]) and its
metric completion E1 with respect to the Darvas metric [36]. The Boucksom-Jonsson Regularization
Conjecture then requires that the Non-Archimedean Entropy HNA(ϕ) of an element ϕ ∈E1,NA can be
continuously approximated by the Non-Archimedean Entropies HNA(ϕk) of a sequence {ϕk}k∈N ⊂
HNA such that ϕk →ϕ. The Archimedean analog of this regularizing process has been proved in
[10, Lem. 3.1]. Equivariant notions of these settings are also known. As a consequence of [87, Prop.
6.3], the desired regularization of the Non-Archimedean entropy can be reduced to the case when
ϕ ∈E1,NA is associated to a model, i.e. to a big test configuration (X, L) that is dominating and
simple normal crossing3. For big models HNA(X, L) = ⟨Ln⟩· KX/X×P1, up to base change, i.e. it
can be essentially expressed in terms of the first Riemann-Roch coefficient of the big line bundle
L →X [85, Prop. 3.2]. Thus Theorem B provides the right framework to solve Boucksom-Jonsson
Regularization Conjecture and hence the Yau-Tian-Donaldson Conjecture of Theorem A.

be a reductive Lie group and let (X, L) be a G-equivariant model of (X, L) that is dominating and
simple normal crossing. Then there exists a sequence of G-equivariant smooth ample test configura-

HNA(Xk, Lk) −→HNA(X, L)
as k →+∞. In particular the equivariant version of Boucksom-Jonsson Regularization Conjecture
[22, Conj. 2.5] holds.

Entropy of the associated element in E1,NA (this last result has also been recently obtained in [26,

an useful weighted relative formalism to study several special metrics such as for instance Calabi’s
extremal metrics and K¨ahler-Ricci solitons; they are all weighted extremal metrics. More precisely,
for our purposes, let L →X be an ample line bundle, let T ⊂Aut◦(X, L) be a maximal compact

C∞(t∨) and assume that v, w are positive on the moment polytope P = mω(X). We refer to
[80, 2, 28, 101, 57, 58, 71] for the precise definitions and for a deep study of this enlarged setting.

Theorem C. Let L →X be an ample line bundle, let T ⊂Aut◦(X, L) be a maximal compact torus,
let ω ∈c1(X) be a T-invariant metric, let mω : X →t∨and let v, w ∈C∞(t∨) that are positive on
the moment polytope P = mω(X). Then the following are equivalent:
(i) the (v, w)-weighted relative Mabuchi functional is TC-coercive;
(ii) (X, L) is TC-uniformly (v, w)-weighted relative K-stable.
In particular if v is log-concave then the following are equivalent:
(i) there exists a (v, w)-weighted extremal K¨ahler metric in c1(L);
(ii) (X, L) is TC-uniformly (v, w)-weighted relative K-stable.
Considering the unweighted case (v, w) = (1, 1), Theorem C in particular provides a Yau-Tian-
Donaldson correspondence for Calabi’s extremal K¨ahler metrics (see also [78, 79]).
On the recent Boucksom-Jonsson and Darvas-Zhang papers & equivalent stability no-
tions. In addition to the already cited papers, two interesting articles on solutions to the Yau-
Tian-Donaldson Conjecture have recently appeared: [43, 26] (see also [27]). The current manuscript
is independent of them. As mentioned above, they solved [87, Conj. 1.6], establishing a version of
Theorem A where the uniform K-stability notions are strengthened by those for models. Moreover,
in both papers, the existence of cscK metrics has been linked to algebro-geometric notions given as
variants of the original one.
In [43, Thm. 1.1] a Yau-Tian-Donaldson correspondence has been achieved in terms of uniform
Kβ-stability for some β > 0, where the latter is a transcendental quantized version of uniform
K-stability, following the analytic picture of the space of K¨ahler metrics. Kβ-stability takes into
consideration a twisted version of the Donaldson-Futaki invariant for ample test configurations,
which pointwise increasingly approximates the original Donaldson-Futaki invariant. Moreover, they
also made progress in the Boucksom-Jonsson Regularization Conjecture of Corollary A, showing
that it was enough to consider a special class of big models (“log discrepancy models”), yielding a
version of Theorem A with respect to Aut◦(X, L)-uniform K-stability for log discrepancy models.
In [26], following their deeply developed Non-Archimedean formalism, the authors instead showed
a Yau-Tian-Donaldson correspondence with respect to (uniform) bK-polystability. Interestingly, bK-
polystability is proved to be equivalent to its uniform version. bK-polystability extends the definition
of K-polystability to all the elements in E1,NA, not only for those given by ample test configura-
tions. However, although it regards the whole space E1,NA, bK-polystability has a concrete algebro-
geometric formulation in terms of divisorial valuations as explained in [26, Sec. 8] (cf. [24]). Replacing
E1,NA with HNA would give a uniform K-polystability notion, as the authors have observed. Lastly,

As a consequence of Theorem B, we can prove the following result, which in particular unifies
these algebro-geometric notions.
Corollary B. Let L →X be an ample line bundle on a smooth projective variety. Then the following
are equivalent:
i) (X, L) is Aut◦(X, L)-uniformly K-stable;
ii) (X, L) is uniformly K-polystable;
iii) (X, L) is Aut◦(X, L)-uniformly K-stable for log discrepancies models;
iv) (X, L) is Aut◦(X, L)-uniformly K-stable for models;
v) (X, L) is uniformly bK-polystable;
vi) (X, L) is bK-polystable.
In the case Aut◦(X, L) is trivial, we also have that they are all equivalent to
vii) (X, L) is uniformly Kβ-stable for some β > 0.
We refer to [26, 43] for the original definitions in (ii), (iii), (v), (vi) and (vii). Combining Theorem
A, [43, Thm. 1.1, Thm 1.3], [26, Thm. A] and other previous results, the unique implication we
prove in Corollary B is (ii) ⇒(v). As anticipated this is again a consequence of Theorem B.
Observe that the equivalences (i) ⇔(iii) ⇔(iv) and (ii) ⇔(v) are proved algebraically. It is
then natural to wonder if there is an algebraic proof connecting them, for instance by checking that
(i) ⇔(ii). Similarly, an algebraic proof linking (vi) (and also (vii), in the trivial automorphism
case) to the others does not seem to be available yet, as already observed in [26].
It is also worth to underline that (i), (ii) are classical K-stability notions as they deal with the
Donaldson-Futaki invariants of ample test configurations. In discrete automorphisms setting they
both coincide with uniform K-stability. In such case (vii) also considers ample test configurations.
However it deals with a β-twisted version of Donaldson-Futaki invariants, and to get back to classical
K-stability one would need to take the limit as β →+∞.
Let us finally stress that Theorem A, in particular, can be also stated with respect to uniform
K-polystability, providing an equivalent version of the Yau-Tian-Donaldson correspondence.
Combining Theorem C with [26, Thm. B] one can also deduce an analog result of Corollary B
for the weighted relative setting.
Other future possible developments. In the companion paper [124] a transcendental version
of Theorem B is investigated, obtaining conditions that ensure the existence of Special Fujita Ap-
proximations for general K¨ahler classes on K¨ahler manifolds. As a consequence some progress on
the transcendental Yau-Tian-Donaldson Conjecture has also been made (following [56, 112, 52, 113,
105, 106]).
It is also natural to try to generalize Theorem A to singular varieties. Although there are still
many challenges, recent progress on singular cscK metrics has been made (see [102, 28, 119, 101]) and
on the Yau-Tian-Donaldson Conjecture (see [70], following Boucksom-Jonsson’s Non-Archimedean
formalism). Similarly to the Fano setting, obtaining a singular version of the Yau-Tian-Donaldson
correspondence may lead to the construction of moduli spaces and other related topics (see [67, 53]
in this perspective). Note that, as previously explained, a version of Theorem B for singular varieties
can be easily stated.
