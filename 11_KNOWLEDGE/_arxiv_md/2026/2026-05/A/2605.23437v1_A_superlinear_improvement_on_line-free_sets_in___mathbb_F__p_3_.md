A superlinear improvement on line-free sets in F3
p
Benedek Kov´acs∗
Abstract
Building on an earlier result of the author together with Elsholtz, F¨uhrer, F¨uredi, Pach, Simon
and Velich [3], we present an improved construction for a line-free set in F3
p, showing that rp(F3
p) ≥
(p −1)3 + 1
8p3/2 −O(p) as p →∞. This results in the first superlinear-term improvement over the
standard hypercube construction {0, 1, . . . , p −2}3. By taking the complement of our set, we also get
a new upper bound of 3p2 −1
8p3/2 +O(p) on the smallest size of a 2-blocking set in the affine geometry
AG(3, p).

Introduction
In this manuscript, we investigate the following extremal combinatorial problem: what is the maximum
possible size of a subset S of F3
p which does not contain a full line? Here F3
p is the 3-dimensional vector
space over the p-element field for some prime p, corresponding to the points of the affine geometry
AG(3, p).
More generally, we denote by rk(Fn
p) the largest size of a subset S ⊆Fn
p that does not contain any
nontrivial k-term arithmetic progression (that is, one with nonzero difference); note that for k = p this
condition is equivalent to saying that S does not contain any full line.
The famous cap set problem investigates the value of r3(Fn
3); note that three distinct points a, b, c ∈Fn
3
form a line if and only if a + b + c = 0. By the result of Ellenberg and Gijswijt [5], r3(Fn
3) = o(2.756n).
They achieved this upper bound using a polynomial technique introduced by Croot, Lev and Pach [2],
which was later reformulated by Tao and Sawin [11, 12] using the slice rank of tensors. By a more detailed
analysis of the argument, Jiang [8] later improved this upper bound by a factor of √n, yielding the best
known improvement so far. The best known lower bound is Ω(2.2202n) by Romera-Paredes et al. [9].
In the general case when k = p, the hypercube construction {0, 1, . . . , p −2}n gives a lower bound of
rp(Fn
p) ≥(p −1)n. For n = 1 this is obviously sharp, and for n = 2 its sharpness was proven by Jamison
[7] and Brouwer and Schrijver [1] using the polynomial method. However, for n = 3, better constructions
exist. The author together with Elsholtz, F¨uhrer, F¨uredi, Pach, Simon and Velich [3] proved the following
lower bound by applying a slight modification to the hypercube construction involving Θ(p) points:
Theorem 1.1 (Elsholtz, F¨uhrer, F¨uredi, Kov´acs, Pach, Simon, Velich [3]). For all primes p ≥5, we
have
rp(F3
p) ≥(p −1)3 + p −2√p = p3 −3p2 + 4p −2√p −1.

In finite geometry, a d-blocking set B of an affine or projective space T is a set of points B ⊆T that
intersects every subspace of T of codimension d in at least one point. Note that B is a d-blocking set if
and only if its complement T \ B does not contain any full subspace of codimension d. For d = n −1,
this means that T \ B is line-free. So our main result, Theorem 1.3, can be stated in terms of 2-blocking
sets in the affine space F3
p:
Definition 1.4. For integers n, d, q such that n ≥2, 1 ≤d ≤n −1 and q is a prime power, let ad(n, q)
denote the smallest size of a d-blocking set in the affine geometry AG(n, q).
Corollary 1.5. For primes p,
a2(3, p) ≤3p2 −1
8p3/2 + O(p)
holds as p →∞.

Constructing our line-free set
The goal of this section is to prove Theorem 1.3. Throughout the section, p ≥3 is a fixed prime, and we
identify Fp with the set {0, 1, . . . , p −1} as usual, inheriting the natural ordering from Z. In Fn
p, a line
always refers to an affine line, that is, a subset of the form {a + λv : λ ∈Fp} for some a, v ∈Fn
p with
v ̸= 0. For m ≤n, the notation [m, n] denotes the set {m, m + 1, . . . , n}, where m and n are integers or
elements of Fp.
To prove Theorem 1.3, we improve upon the construction in [3] by the author together with Elsholtz,
F¨uhrer, F¨uredi, Pach, Simon and Velich (see Theorem 1.1). Similarly to that construction, the layers of
our 3-dimensional line-free set will be constructed using complements of planar blocking sets. Namely,
we will make use of the following observation:
