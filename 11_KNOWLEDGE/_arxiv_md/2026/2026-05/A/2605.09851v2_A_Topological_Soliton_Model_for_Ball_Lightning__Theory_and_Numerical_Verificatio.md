Numerical Verification with the 3D Gross-Pitaevskii Equation

Abstract
Despite decades of study, ball lightning is still a mysterious atmospheric phenomenon. Its long
lifetime, penetrative ability, and stability are difficult to explain with traditional physical models.
We propose a novel theoretical framework that interprets ball lightning as a projection of a high-
dimensional topological soliton into three-dimensional space. Its essence is described by a nonlinear
Schr¨odinger equation with attractive interactions, protected by a non-zero topological charge.
Through numerical simulation of the three-dimensional Gross-Pitaevskii equation, we verify the
following core predictions of this model. In a Bose-Einstein condensate with attractive interactions,
solitons carrying topological charge exhibit: (1) long-lived stability (topological charge conserved
under perturbations); (2) low transmission probability (due to minimal overlap integral resulting
from orthogonality with the ground state wavefunction); (3) energy and size scales consistent with
actual observations.The soliton lifetime is governed by the system’s decoherence rate (τ ∼

physical explanation for ball lightning, and at the same time provides a concrete scheme for the
experimental preparation and observation of three-dimensional topological solitons. Keywords:

Introduction
Ball lightning is a rare and mysterious atmospheric phenomenon, appearing as a luminous sphere
10-100 cm in diameter, with a lifetime of up to several seconds. It can penetrate obstacles like glass
and sometimes vanishes with an explosion [16].
Despite thousands of reliable eyewitness records,
its physical nature remains an unsolved mystery. Existing theoretical models mainly fall into three
categories: (1) plasma models [12]; (2) chemiluminescence models[1]; (3) electromagnetic resonance
models [7].However, none of these models can simultaneously explain all the key characteristics of
ball lightning: long lifetime, penetrability, and spherical stability. Topological solitons, at the inter-
disciplinary frontier of topological and nonlinear physics, are significant in condensed matter physics,
nonlinear optics, and particle physics [13, 4, 6]. Their core feature is stability protected by topo-
logical invariants (such as winding number, Hopf invariant), rendering the soliton structure robust
against external perturbations. In particular, stable three-dimensional soliton solutions can exist in
Bose-Einstein condensates (BECs) with attractive interactions [9, 17, 22, 19]. This paper proposes a
novel theoretical framework: ball lightning is a projection of a high-dimensional topological soliton into
three-dimensional space. Specifically, we model it as a system with attractive interactions described
by the nonlinear Schr¨odinger equation (Gross-Pitaevskii equation), whose stability is protected by a
non-zero topological charge (topological winding number). This model naturally explains: (1) long life-
time (topological protection),(2) penetrability (low transmission due to wavefunction orthogonality),
(3) spherical structure (rotational symmetry). The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 introduces
the theoretical model, Section 3 details numerical methods, Section 4 presents results and analysis,
Section 5 discusses physical implications, and Section 6 concludes and provides an outlook. Keywords:
Ball lightning; Topological soliton; Nonlinear Schr¨odinger equation; Topological charge; Bose-Einstein
condensate; Gross-Pitaevskii equation; Numerical simulation

5.3
Suggestions for Experimental Realization
Although ball lightning in nature is difficult to reproduce in the laboratory, topological solitons can
be realized on several platforms:
1. Bose-Einstein Condensate: Utilize the Feshbach resonance of atoms like 87Rb to adjust g < 0,
preparing an attractive-interaction BEC. Implant angular momentum using Laguerre-Gaussian
beams to generate vortex solitons. Experiments have already prepared 2D vortices[8, 11]. [14];
extension to 3D is a natural development.
2. Nonlinear Optical Systems: In nonlinear media, the optical field satisfies the nonlinear Schr¨odinger
equation. Using spatial light modulators to prepare optical solitons with topological charge can
verify topological protection [3, 15]
3. Superfluid Helium:
The macroscopic wavefunction of superfluid helium satisfies the Gross-
Pitaevskii equation. Vortices can be implanted by rotation to form topological solitons [2].
Microscopic mechanism of topological protection: The conservation of topological charge Q stems
from the non-trivial homotopy of the order parameter space (S2). Any continuous deformation cannot
change Q, hence the soliton structure is stable. Thermal fluctuations and collisions in the atmosphere
cause decohere.
5.4
Model Limitations and Extensions
Limitations: (1) Coupling to the electromagnetic field is not considered; actual ball lightning is ac-
companied by electromagnetic radiation. (2) The atmospheric environment is complex; this model
uses a uniform medium approximation. (3) The formation mechanism of the soliton is not discussed
in detail.Future extensions: (1) Couple with Maxwell’s equations to study electromagnetic radiation.
(2) Introduce inhomogeneous media and turbulence. (3) Study interactions between multiple solitons.

Comparison with Recent Experimental Breakthroughs
A concurrent experimental breakthrough provides strong, independent support for the soliton-based

They achieved this by
guiding an intense terahertz surface wave to a nanoscale tip, creating a localized relativistic-strength
field. Injecting a supersonic argon gas jet into this field led to rapid ionization and the formation of
a spherical plasma cavity. A delicate balance between the internal radiation pressure and the shell’s

Conclusion
This paper proposes a topological soliton model for ball lightning and verifies its core predictions
through numerical simulation of the three-dimensional Gross-Pitaevskii equation. The main conclu-
sions are as follows:
1. Theoretical Innovation: Ball lightning is interpreted as a projection of a high-dimensional topo-
logical soliton into three-dimensional space. Its stability is protected by topological charge, and
its penetrability is explained by wavefunction orthogonality.
2. Numerical Verification: We successfully simulated attractive-interaction solitons with topological
charge, verifying their long-term stability, topological protection, and low-transmission behavior.
3. Quantitative Agreement: The model’s predicted lifetime, size, and energy agree well with obser-
vational data within reasonable parameter ranges.
4. Experimental Feasibility: Concrete schemes for realizing topological solitons on platforms like
BECs and nonlinear optics are proposed, laying the groundwork for experimental verification.
This work not only provides a new solution to the century-old puzzle of ball lightning but also opens
new directions for the study of topological solitons in three-dimensional nonlinear systems. Future work
should focus on key issues such as experimental realization, electromagnetic coupling, and formation
mechanisms.
