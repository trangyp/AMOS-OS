DOUBLE VERONESE CONES WITH SINGULARITIES
YURI PROKHOROV
Abstract. We study double Veronese cones – three-dimensional del Pezzo varieties of degree
one – with terminal Gorenstein singularities. We prove sharp bounds for the number of nodes,
determine the structure of the automorphism group, and establish criteria for rationality and
unirationality. In particular, we exhibit a Q-factorial nodal double Veronese cone with 21
nodes.
1. Introduction
A double Veronese cone is a three-dimensional del Pezzo variety of degree 1, i.e. a projective
threefold X whose anticanonical class has a decomposition −KX = 2AX, where AX is an
ample Cartier divisor such that A3
X = 1. These varieties can be described as double covers
of the cone Y ⊂P6 over the Veronese surface branched over the section of Y by a cubic
hypersurface. Equivalently, such a variety admits an embedding into the weighted projective
space P(13, 2, 3) as a hypersurface of degree 6. The geometry of such threefolds is intimately
related to the elliptic fibration obtained by blowing up the base point of the half-anticanonical
linear system |AX|.
In this paper we systematically study double Veronese cones with terminal Gorenstein
singularities. Our main focus lies on several complementary aspects:
Singularities and Q-factoriality. We prove that a Q-factorial nodal double Veronese cone can
have at most 21 nodes, and that this bound is attained by some example (Theorem 5.3).
Conversely, a non-Q-factorial nodal double Veronese cone must have at least 12 nodes, and
the case of exactly 12 nodes occurs.
Automorphism group. We show that the identity component of the automorphism group
Aut(X) of a double Veronese cone is either a torus Gm or the additive group Ga; explicit
equations for the corresponding actions are provided (Propositions 6.11 and 6.7). Moreover,
for nodal double Veronese cones the automorphism group is finite.
Rationality and unirationality. We establish that every singular double Veronese cone is
unirational. Furthermore, we give some sufficient conditions for rationality (Theorem 7.1).
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 collects notation and basic facts about double
Veronese cones. In Section 3 we analyze the singularities and their relation to the certain
plane curves. Section 4 is devoted to the geometry of lines and their families, which is essential
for the study of certain birational contractions. Section 5 discusses the class group and
Q-factoriality; here we prove the sharp bounds on the number of nodes (Theorem 5.3). The

7. Rationality and unirationality
It is known that a smooth double Veronese cone is not rational. For general varieties of this
type this was proved via degeneration of intermediate Jacobians [Tyu80]. The non-rationality
of arbitrary smooth double Veronese cones was established in [Gri04]. Moreover [Gri04] proves
that there are no any birational conic bundle structures in the smooth case. In this section
we discuss rationality and unirationality questions of singular double Veronese cones. Our
main result is Theorem 7.1.
Theorem 7.1. Let X be a double Veronese cone.
(i) If X is singular, then X is unirational.
(ii) If X has a singular point P of type cE7, cE8 or cDn with n ≥6, then X is rational.
(iii) If X has a singular point P that is not of type cA1, cA2 nor of type cD4, then X has
a (birational) conic bundle structure.
Remark 7.2.
(i) The existence of conic bundle structures on singular Fano threefolds
was studied in [Pro22]. Thus the part (iii) of the theorem is a small supplement to
Theorem 1.2 in [Pro22].
(ii) Note that a double Veronese cone X is rational if r(X) ≥4 and it has a conic bundle
structure r(X) ≥3 (see [Pro13]). Also any double Veronese cone whose automorphism
group is infinite (see Theorem 6.2) is also rational.
