Abstract. We study vector-valued incidence maps obtained from ordinary graph incidence
maps by linear observation of the free vertex space.
Let F be a field, D = (X, E, s, t) a
finite directed multigraph, U an F-vector space, and ϕ : X →U a vertex labeling with F-
linear extension ˆϕ : FX →U.
The vector-valued incidence map ∂ϕ : FE →U, ∂ϕ(1e) =
ϕ(t(e)) −ϕ(s(e)), factors as ∂ϕ = ˆϕ ◦BD, where BD is the classical incidence map of D. We
prove the formula
dimF Ker(∂ϕ) = |E| −|X| + c(D) + δϕ,
where c(D) is the number of weakly connected components of D and δϕ := dimF(Im(BD) ∩
Ker(ˆϕ)) is the defect invariant.
We apply this framework to directed tensor-labeled hyper-
graphs H = (Q0, Q1, β), in which each hyperedge carries a pair of boundary tensors (Ae, Be)
in the tensor algebra T(FQ0), and prove that δ(H) = 0 over any field for each of the six stan-
dard constructions, including symmetric encodings that degenerate in positive characteristic.
Over F = R, the edge Gram operator Lβ = ∂∗
β∂β has rank |Vmacro| −cmacro −δ(H), and its
degree-truncated operators form a Loewner-monotone filtration whose rank increments equal
the decrements of the defect filtration. We further realize the cycle space of every oriented
hypergraph (in the sense of Reff–Rusnak) as Ker(∂β) within this framework, and exhibit a
four-edge inclusion–exclusion example with δ(H) = 1.

The correspondence F embeds oriented hypergraphs faithfully into Definition 3.4 (Remark
7.3), but F(Ho) generally does not follow a single standard construction and belongs to an
extended class allowing linear combinations of vertex vectors. Conversely, our standard con-
structions employ higher-degree symmetric tensors Symk as boundaries and do not fit into the
signed-scalar framework {−1, 0, +1}. The two theories differ in three respects: (i) the incidence
data is restricted to signed scalars in oriented hypergraphs, while ours allows source and target
tensors of arbitrary degree; (ii) this difference enables the direct treatment of multiset hyper-
edges via Symk in our framework; (iii) the oriented hypergraph cycle space is defined as the
kernel of an incidence matrix, while Z(H) is the kernel of a difference map within the tensor
algebra, with the two connected through observation maps (Section 5). Theorem 7.4 and Ex-
ample 7.6 concretely bridge this gap. A recent extension of oriented hypergraph theory in a
different direction is given in [15], where the Harary–Sachs theorem is generalized to integer
matrices via incidence-based cycle covers; this remains within the signed-scalar incidence regime
and is complementary to the tensor-valued framework developed here.
