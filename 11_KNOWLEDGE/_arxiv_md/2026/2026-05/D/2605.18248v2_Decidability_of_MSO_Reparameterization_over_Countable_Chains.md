Abstract. Interpretations are a fundamental tool in mathematical logic,
allowing structures to be encoded within other structures via logical def-
initions. We study MSO multidimensional point interpretations, where
elements of an interpreted structure are represented by tuples of ele-
ments of the host structure, and address the problem of simplifying such
interpretations by reducing their representation dimension.
To formalize simplification, we use the notion of reparameterization. Our
main result shows that, over the class of countable labelled linear orders,
it is decidable whether a given MSO formula admits a d-dimensional
reparameterization. As a consequence, every interpretation whose do-
main admits such a reparameterization is equivalent to a d-dimensional
point interpretation.

Introduction
The notion of interpretation was first carefully defined and developed in the
foundational work of Tarski, Mostowski, and Robinson [9]. Interpretations are a
fundamental tool in mathematical logic, playing a central role in the foundations
of mathematics, as well as in the philosophy of science.
Consider Monadic Second-Order1 (MSO) d-dimensional (point) interpreta-
tions of a structure A in a structure B. Under such an interpretation, each
element of A is represented within B by a d-tuple of elements of B.
A central problem is whether a given interpretation can be simplified, for
instance by reducing the dimension of the representation. The domain of the
interpreted structure is specified by an MSO formula φ(x1, . . . , xm) over B. To
formalize this notion of simplification, we use the notion of reparameterization,
introduced in [4] for MSO-definable relations.
Throughout, ¯x and ¯y denote tuples of first-order variables.

C be a class of structures. A formula G(x, y) is a (functional) reparameterization
of φ over C if the following conditions hold:

interpretation (see Proposition 3.3).
Bojańczyk [2] studied polyregular functions, namely string-to-string functions
defined by MSO interpretations. He proved that it is decidable whether an MSO
formula admits a d-dimensional reparameterization over finite words. In this
work, we generalize this result to arbitrary countable labelled chains.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we recall standard notions
concerning chains and monadic second-order logic, and introduce the basic tools
of the composition method used throughout the paper. In Section 3 we recall the
notion of interpretation and motivate the reparameterization problem by show-
ing how d-dimensional reparameterizations yield d-dimensional interpretations.
Section 4 contains our main result, showing that it is decidable whether an MSO
formula admits a d-dimensional reparameterization over the class of countable
chains. In Section 5, following Bojańczyk [2], we study the growth rate of MSO
interpretations. Extending results of [2,4], we show that the growth rate is poly-
nomial, with degree equal to the minimal reparameterization dimension. Due to
space limitations, we provide only proof sketches.

2.1
Chains
A k-chain is a structure M = (M, <, P), where (M, <) is a linear order and
P = (P1, . . . , Pk) is a k-tuple of unary (monadic) predicates on M. When k is
clear from the context, we simply refer to M as a chain or a labelled chain.
An interval I is a subset I ⊆M such that if b, c ∈I and b < d < c then
d ∈I. We denote by M↾I the induced substructure on I.

2.2
Monadic Second-Order Logic
We work in monadic second-order logic (MSO) over the signature {<, P1, . . . , Pk},
where each Pi is a unary (monadic) predicate symbol. First-order variables
x, y, . . . range over elements of the domain, and second-order variables X, Y, . . .
range over subsets of the domain. The atomic formulas are x < y, x = y, Pi(x),
and X(x). Formulas are built from atomic formulas using Boolean connectives
and the quantifiers ∃x, ∀x, ∃X, and ∀X.
Definition 2.1 (Definability). Let ψ(z1, . . . , zm) be an MSO formula and let
M be a structure with domain D. The relation defined by ψ in M is the set
ψM := {(a1, . . . , am) ∈Dm | M |= ψ(a1, . . . , am)}.
A relation R ⊆Dm is said to be MSO-definable in M if there exists an MSO
formula ψ(z1, . . . , zm) such that R = ψM.
We use standard abbreviations. In particular, ∃≥Nx φ(x) expresses that there
are at least N distinct elements satisfying φ(x).

relation such that all relations of B are invariant under E. In this case, the
elements of the output structure correspond to the E-equivalence classes.

Growth Rate
Bojańczyk [2] introduced the notion of the growth rate of a string-to-string func-
tion as the function that maps an input length n ∈{0, 1, . . . } to the maximal
size of an output produced on inputs of length at most n.
This notion was later generalized by Gallot et al. [4], who showed that the
output size of an MSO set interpretation from finite trees to relational structures
grows either polynomially or exponentially in the input size, with a computable
degree k ∈N ∪{∞}.

arbitrary structures, including infinite ones. We show that the growth rate of
an MSO point interpretation is polynomial, with degree equal to the minimal

Conclusion and Further Results
Bojańczyk [2] studied reparameterizations of MSO-definable relations over finite
words and proved that it is decidable whether an m-dimensional point MSO
interpretation admits a d-dimensional point MSO reparameterization. In this
paper, we extend this decidability result to arbitrary countable labelled chains.
In many situations, however, one considers MSO interpretations that are
more general than point interpretations. In finite-set and set interpretations,
elements of the interpreted structure are represented by tuples of finite sets or
arbitrary sets, respectively.
Interpretations of A in B. We distinguish three standard flavours of d-dimensional
MSO interpretations of a structure A in a structure B. In each case, an element
of A is encoded inside B by a d-tuple:
Set interpretations (MSO, d-dimensional): each element of A is represented
by a d-tuple of arbitrary subsets of B.
Finite-set interpretations (MSO, d-dimensional): each element of A is rep-
resented by a d-tuple of finite subsets of B.
Point interpretations (MSO, d-dimensional): each element of A is repre-
sented by a d-tuple of elements of B.
A natural question is whether a given interpretation can be simplified; for in-
stance, whether a set or finite-set interpretation is equivalent to a point inter-
pretation.
Gallot, Lhote, and Nguyen [4] studied finite-set-to-point reparameterizations
over finite words and trees and proved that it is decidable whether a finite-set
interpretation admits a point reparameterization.
In [7] we extended this decidability result to arbitrary countable labelled
chains: given an MSO formula φ with free finite-set variables, it is decidable
whether φ admits a point reparameterization.
Over linear orders, a canonical class of simple subsets is given by cuts, i.e.,
downward-closed sets. Cuts correspond to points in the Dedekind completion
and form a structurally robust family of subsets. The analogue of our main
result, Theorem 4.1, also holds when first-order domain and image variables are
replaced by variables interpreted as cuts.
Finally, building on the techniques of [1], we also proved in [7] that it is
decidable whether an MSO formula φ with free set variables admits a many-
dimensional finite-set reparameterization over Dedekind-complete countable la-
belled chains.
