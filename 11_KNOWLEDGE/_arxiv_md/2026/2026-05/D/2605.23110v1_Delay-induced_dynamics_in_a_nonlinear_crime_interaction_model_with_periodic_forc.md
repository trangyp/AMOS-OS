DELAY-INDUCED DYNAMICS IN A NONLINEAR CRIME
INTERACTION MODEL WITH PERIODIC FORCING
PABLO AMSTER1,2, ANDR´ES RIVERA3, SEBASTI´AN PEDERSEN1
Abstract. A nonlinear time-delay model is proposed to describe the interaction dynam-
ics between criminal and non-criminal populations, combining social influence mechanisms,
saturation effects represented by a Holling type II functional response, and time-dependent
law-enforcement actions. The delay accounts for the latency between exposure to criminal
behavior and behavioral response, introducing memory effects that naturally lead to a de-
lay differential equations framework. Fundamental analytical properties, including positivity,
global existence, and invariance of the feasible region, are established to ensure the math-
ematical consistency of the population interpretation. In the autonomous setting, explicit
threshold conditions governing the stability of the criminal-free equilibrium and the emer-
gence of coexistence states are derived, while the delay is shown to induce stability switches
and oscillatory regimes through characteristic root crossings. In the non-autonomous case,
topological degree arguments guaranty the existence of strictly positive periodic solutions,
indicating that long-term dynamics depend primarily on the averaged law enforcement inten-
sity measures rather than on short-term fluctuations. These results identify time delay as a
key structural mechanism underlying recurrent patterns and complex temporal behavior in
crime dynamics.
1. Introduction
In recent years, various approaches have emerged to analyze the criminal behavior of an
arbitrary group in a society, in order to consolidate models and/or strategies that help
to understand or mitigate criminal behavior and promote safer social development. These
approaches include, for example, population dynamics, population behavior theory, opti-
mal control theory, and stochastic modeling (see [30] and the references therein, and see
[10, 14, 16, 32, 27, 28, 20, 15, 18, 19, 21, 31, 8]).
In terms of mathematical modeling, one approach consists of models inspired by the classic
predator-prey model (for example see [1, 33] and the references therein) by essentially consid-
ering the total population divided into two groups. Group C = Individuals who commit one

Continuing this line of research, this article proposes a time-delayed differential equations
model. After reviewing the current literature on mathematical models for this problem (see
references above), the use of the theory of differential equations with delay remains quite unex-
plored. We also introduce a more general growth function for the population group N, which
includes the logistic, we consider the parameter that accounts for the public safety measures
implemented to mitigate the criminal population C to be time-dependent and periodic, and we
model the rate at which criminal individuals victimize/capture individuals in N as a Holling
type II function. The use of a Holling type II function is based on the fact that it is more
realistic than the classic standard or mass incidence functions, because it takes into account
saturation effects due to high levels of crime or preventive measures adopted by non-criminal
individuals, and at the same time it is a modeling method that is not as widely explored (see
[30] and the references therein). This Holling type II function has been previously employed
to model criminal dynamics (see, for example, reference [75] of [30]), and we believe it is
worthwhile to continue exploring that path in our case.

4. Non-autonomous case: the existence of periodic solutions
In this section, we shall consider the original non-autonomous system (1), in which it shall be
assumed that the nonnegative law enforcement function le is continuous and T-periodic. In
particular, we shall focus on the existence of strictly positive T-periodic solutions.
We observe that if the amplitude of le is small, then an argument based on the implicit function
theorem can be established to obtain a T-periodic solution near a positive equilibrium of
an autonomous system. This procedure is analogous to the one employed in [3]. However,
we emphasize the fact that, due to different factors, the law enforcement function may have
oscillations of large amplitude; in consequence, we are interested in finding sufficient conditions
that depend only on the average of le.
With this in mind, we shall consider two different scenarios: on the one and, we shall assume
that the counteroffensive measures expressed by σ are large enough to compensate the effect of
the large oscillations in le; on the other hand, when σ is small, it shall be shown that T-periodic
solutions exist, as a natural extension of the co-existence equilibrium in the autonomous case.
In this latter situation, it may be understood that, if the average of le is large, then the
criminal population tends to extinction, and no periodic solutions exist while, otherwise, the
criminal and non-criminal population will coexist periodically.

In general terms, in any case we want to find conditions for which the criminal population is

▷The global decrease in crime (law enforcement measures le to combat crime plus natural
mortality η) must remain above the overall growth of crime (criminalization rate γ
minus law enforcement measures and natural mortality, weighted by the maximum
possible theoretical non-criminal population (N†)).

▷The criminalization rate γ must remain above the global decrease in crime (averaged

Conclusions
A nonlinear time-delay model has been proposed and analyzed to describe the interaction be-
tween criminal and non-criminal populations, incorporating social influence, saturation effects
through a Holling type II response, and time-dependent enforcement. The delay captures
the latency between exposure and behavioral change, introducing memory as a structural
component of the dynamics.
In the autonomous framework, explicit threshold conditions determine whether the criminal
population is eliminated or persists, showing how the balance between recruitment into crime
and mitigation mechanisms governs the stability of the criminal-free state. The delay is shown
23


## Page 24


Delay-induced dynamics in a nonlinear crime interaction model with periodic forcing
to play a nontrivial role, being capable of inducing stability switches and oscillatory regimes
that provide a mathematical basis for recurrent patterns in crime dynamics.
The most distinctive results arise in the non-autonomous setting with periodic law enforce-
ment. The analysis proves the existence of strictly positive periodic solutions under suitable
average conditions, showing that even strong seasonal or policy oscillations can sustain re-
current coexistence dynamics.
This shows that long-term behavior is primarily controlled
by the average effectiveness of law enforcement rather than by short-term fluctuations, un-
derscoring the importance of sustained interventions over intermittent measures. However,
the model has limitations. It assumes homogeneous mixing, deterministic interactions, and
a single discrete delay, which can oversimplify the diversity of social responses and spatial
heterogeneity observed in real settings.
Future research may address these aspects by in-
corporating distributed or state-dependent delays, spatial or network structures, stochastic
effects, and control-theoretic formulations aimed at optimizing enforcement strategies.
In general, the study highlights time delay and temporal variability as key mechanisms shaping
complex social dynamics, contributing to a broader understanding of delay-induced phenom-
ena in nonlinear systems subject to periodic forcing.
