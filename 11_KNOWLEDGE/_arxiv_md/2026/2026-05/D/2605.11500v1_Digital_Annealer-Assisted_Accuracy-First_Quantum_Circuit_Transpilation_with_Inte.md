Abstract—In the Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ)
era, limited qubit counts and high gate error rates directly
constrain circuit fidelity, making the minimization of CNOT gate
counts crucial. While conventional compilers prioritize heuristic
efficiency, there is a compelling need for ”accuracy-first” tran-
spilation that prioritizes gate reduction over compilation latency.
We propose a framework leveraging the Digital Annealer (DA)
via two complementary strategies: (1) Hybrid, which uses DA-
driven global initial mapping combined with high-speed heuristic
routing by Qiskit, and (2) Full DA, which solves mapping and
routing as separate DA-assisted QUBO subproblems within an
iterative workflow.
Benchmarks demonstrate that our Hybrid approach achieves
an average CNOT reduction of 13.7% (up to 57.4%) compared
to Qiskit’s highest optimization level, with the largest gains on
structured circuits such as GHZ and ASP where the initial
layout is decisive. The Full DA approach matches Hybrid on
structured circuits and outperforms ISAAQ by 23.1% on average
(maximum 90.8%), but degrades on circuits with random or
concentrated connectivity—exposing a trade-off between QUBO
size and solution quality when the entire circuit is encoded in a
single annealing pass. Although these global optimizations incur
higher computational overhead than pure heuristics, our results
indicate that for high-precision workflows where gate noise is the
primary bottleneck, DA-assisted global initial placement provides
a practical ”time-for-quality” trade-off for enhancing the utility
of near-term quantum hardware.
I. INTRODUCTION
The scalability of noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ)
devices is limited by the finite number of qubits and high
gate error rates. Circuit partitioning emerged as a method
to circumvent these limitations by decomposing large-scale
circuits into executable subcircuits [1]–[3]. However, this
approach introduces a massive sampling overhead resulting
from quasi-probability decomposition, where the total number
of hardware executions required to reconstruct the final result
scales exponentially with the number of cuts. Since this
overhead forces the total execution time to be dominated by
repetitive quantum hardware sampling, minimizing the CNOT
count within each subcircuit becomes the primary bottleneck
for both reducing cumulative error and ensuring the practical
feasibility of the computation.
Under such circumstances, we argue that an “accuracy-
first” compilation strategy is valuable. Finding an optimal
mapping and routing is an NP-hard combinatorial optimization
problem [4], [5]. While conventional transpilation frameworks
such as Qiskit (SABRE) [6]–[8] and t |ket⟩[9] prioritize
low-latency heuristic searches, they often converge to local
optima, yielding suboptimal gate counts. In partitioning-based
workflows, where quantum runtime can extend to hours or
days due to the exponential sampling overhead, the classical
computational cost of finding an exact or high-quality mapping
becomes negligible. Therefore, investing classical resources
into global optimization to minimize gate overhead represents
an extremely reasonable trade-off to maximize overall execu-
tion fidelity.
In this paper, we propose an annealing-assisted optimization
framework leveraging the global search capabilities of Fu-
jitsu’s Digital Annealer (DA) [10]. We categorize our technical
approach into two complementary strategies:
1) Hybrid: A strategic integration of DA-driven global
initial mapping with high-speed heuristic routing by
Qiskit.
2) Full DA: An integrated QUBO formulation that solves
both qubit mapping and routing on the DA, used here
to characterize the practical limits of monolithic global
optimization at current annealer scales.
Our primary contribution is the empirical demonstration that
DA-driven initial placement, when combined with heuristic
routing, achieves consistent CNOT reductions across diverse
benchmarks. Specifically, our Hybrid approach yields average
improvements of 13.7% over Qiskit (Level 3) and 11.5% over
t |ket⟩, reaching 57.4% on layout-sensitive structured circuits.
As a complementary result, the Full DA approach achieves
an average CNOT reduction of 23.1% (up to 90.8%) over
ISAAQ [11], while simultaneously exposing the QUBO-size
trade-off that limits monolithic optimization on circuits with

II. BACKGROUND AND RELATED WORK
A. Circuit Partitioning and Sampling Overhead
Quantum circuit partitioning (wire cutting and gate cutting)
enables the execution of large circuits on smaller devices
by decomposing them into subcircuits. While effective, this
technique introduces a massive sampling overhead to recon-
struct expectation values via quasi-probability decomposition.
For Ms gate cuts and Mt wire cuts, the total execution
time scales by a factor of approximately 9Ms × 16Mt [2].
In such a partitioning-heavy workflow, the total execution
time is dominated by the repetitive sampling of subcircuits.
Consequently, the overhead incurred by our ”accuracy-first”
transpilation becomes negligible relative to the total hard-
ware execution time. Furthermore, since the final result is
reconstructed from an exponential number of noisy samples,
minimizing the CNOT count of each subcircuit is valuable
to suppress error accumulation and ensure the reliability of
the reconstructed expectation values. These factors provide the
technical justification for prioritizing gate-count minimization
over classical compilation latency.

III. PROPOSED METHOD
A. Target Device Topology
We assume a 64-qubit superconducting quantum computer
with an 8 × 8 square lattice as the target device. This con-
figuration reflects the typical architecture of Fujitsu’s super-
conducting quantum processors [12], [13]. While this study
focuses on a grid architecture, our QUBO formulations are
defined based on a general distance matrix dpq (representing
the shortest path distance between physical qubits p and q).
Therefore, the proposed framework is inherently flexible and
applicable to arbitrary device topologies, including the heavy-
hex lattices employed in IBM Quantum systems.

C. Proposed Method 1: Hybrid Strategy
The Hybrid Strategy combines the DA’s global optimization
with high-performance classical routing heuristics to balance
circuit quality and computational overhead. In this workflow,
the initial qubit assignment is determined by solving the
mapping QUBO (Section III-B), which accounts for gate
dependencies across the entire circuit. Once this optimal
placement is obtained, the routing phase—including SWAP
insertion and gate synthesis—is delegated to Qiskit’s heuristic
transpiler.
By replacing the stochastic or locally-searched initial map-
pings typical of algorithms like SABRE with a DA-driven
global placement, this approach provides a strategically supe-
rior starting point. This is particularly effective for structured
circuits (e.g., GHZ), where the initial layout has a decisive
impact on the final CNOT count. The Hybrid strategy offers
a stable performance profile competitive with standard Qiskit,
particularly on layout-sensitive structured circuits, while pro-
viding a flexible trade-off between classical resources and
output quality for practical-scale workflows.

the decoder detects cyclic permutations and decomposes them
into a valid sequence of physical SWAP gates. This approach
ensures strict adherence to device topology and maintains
logical integrity while keeping the QUBO size manageable
for the DA.
IV. EVALUATION
We evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed methods by
benchmarking them against ISAAQ [11], Qiskit (Optimization
Level 3) [6]–[8], [16], and t|ket⟩[9] across a suite of circuits
with diverse gate densities and connectivity structures.

The experimental environment integrates Fujitsu’s Digital
Annealer (DA) with a classical software stack for circuit pro-
cessing. We utilize the Qiskit framework for circuit generation
and analysis, while Fixstars

The proposed framework was implemented in Python 3.10

C. Performance Evaluation
We evaluated the performance of our proposed meth-
ods—Full DA and Hybrid—using the benchmark circuits
summarized in Table III. The primary evaluation metric is
the equivalent CNOT count after mapping to an 8 × 8 grid
topology.
Fig. 2 presents the comparative results between the proposed
and existing methods. Fig. 2a specifically compares Full DA
and ISAAQ, both of which are annealing-based transpilers.
As shown in Fig.2a, the Full DA approach significantly
outperforms ISAAQ in all benchmarks except for bv and
qv, achieving an average CNOT reduction of 23.1%, with a
maximum reduction of 90.8%. These results indicate that for
structured circuits where additional SWAPs are not extensively
required, the DA is highly effective at finding a near-optimal
global layout that minimizes SWAP overhead compared to
existing annealing-based approaches. Conversely, Full DA
showed limitations in performance for circuits with less struc-
tured, near-random connectivity or those with concentrated
CNOT operations on specific qubits.
Fig. 2b compares our Hybrid approach with Qiskit and
t|ket⟩, from which several key trends emerged. For circuits
such as ghz, qaoa grid, and asp, Hybrid significantly reduced
CNOT counts. Specifically, it achieved an average reduction
of 13.7% (with a maximum of 57.4%) compared to Qiskit,
and an average reduction of 11.5% (up to 35.0%) relative to
t|ket⟩. This demonstrates that global layout optimization is par-
ticularly advantageous for structured circuits where additional
SWAPs are not extensively required.

Two distinct regimes emerge. On structured circuits such as

the routing search space grows large enough that the DA
cannot consistently locate near-optimal solutions within the
8,192-variable budget at T=2.
We note one limitation of the current evaluation: Hybrid
and Qiskit benefit from internal multi-trial heuristics within
SABRE, whereas Full DA is evaluated on a single annealing
execution. A trial-matched comparison is left for future work.

functions as a robust transpiler that effectively combines the
respective strengths of both Qiskit and t|ket⟩, while the Full
DA results delineate the regime in which monolithic global
QUBO optimization remains practical.

V. CONCLUSION
This paper proposed an annealing-assisted transpilation
framework designed to minimize CNOT counts for circuit
partitioning workflows. Our “accuracy-first” approach prior-
itizes gate reduction over compilation latency, reflecting the
reality that total execution time in partitioning-heavy tasks is
dominated by hardware sampling overhead.
We established the efficacy of global optimization in quan-
tum transpilation by introducing two distinct strategies: a
unified QUBO-based Full DA approach and a performance-
balanced Hybrid framework. Benchmark results quantitatively
validated that these methods effectively circumvent the local
optima inherent in standard heuristics, yielding significant
CNOT count reductions that are valuable for the reliability
of high-precision NISQ workflows.
Future work will explore refinements to the QUBO for-
mulation that may relax the size–quality trade-off observed
in the Full DA results, as well as the incorporation of
hardware-specific noise profiles (e.g., T1, T2, and gate error
rates) directly into the QUBO cost terms. We also intend to
evaluate the generalizability of our framework across diverse
architectures such as heavy-hex lattices. These directions,
together with broader benchmarking on partitioning-heavy
workflows, will help clarify the regime in which annealing-
assisted transpilation provides the largest practical benefit.
