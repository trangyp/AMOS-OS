ABSTRACT
We present optical and near-infrared observations of the fast-declining Type Ia supernova (SN Ia)
2022an. The photometric and spectroscopic properties identify it as a standard 91bg-like event; how-
ever, our data reveal a relatively narrow absorption feature with a full width at half maximum (FWHM)
of 75 ˚A, near 1.037 µm in the rest frame of the observed spectra that persists from around 30 days to
nearly 90 days after maximum light. We attribute this feature to He I 1.083 µm line with a blueshifted
velocity of 1.3 × 104 km s−1 and a FWHM of 2.1 × 103 km s−1, supported by the detection of multi-
ple optical He I transitions in earlier epochs at a higher velocity around 1.5 × 104 km s−1. The high
velocity of the helium could not be explained by helium external to the progenitor at the explosion,
such as the stripped surface helium from a companion star. The properties of the helium absorption
in SN 2022an spectra instead point to unburnt material in the outer ejecta, thus providing the most
compelling evidence to date for helium-bearing ejecta in a 91bg-like SN Ia. Such helium has been
predicted for sub-Chandrasekhar-mass double-detonation explosions involving a surface helium shell.
No theoretical calculations of modern helium-shell double detonation have been performed at epochs
similar to those observed for SN 2022an to study the effect of helium on their spectra, revealing a gap
between observations and theoretical calculations in understanding the manifestation of helium in SNe
Ia. Nevertheless, the discovery of persistent helium absorption in SN 2022an demonstrates the diag-

1. INTRODUCTION
Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) play a central role in as-
trophysics as cosmological distance indicators and as the
result of close-binary evolution (see reviews, e.g., Goo-
bar & Leibundgut 2011; Liu et al. 2023b).
Although
the consensus that SNe Ia result from the thermonu-
clear explosion of white dwarfs has been established on
both theoretical and observational grounds, the detailed
progenitor system and the actual explosion mechanism
remain challenging open questions.
The diversity within the SN Ia population remains
an active area of study (see, e.g., Taubenberger 2017;
Jha et al. 2019; Dimitriadis et al. 2025; Alburai et al.
2026) and reflects a variety of candidate explosion chan-
nels and progenitor configurations (see, e.g., Maoz et al.
2014; Livio & Mazzali 2018; Hillebrandt & Niemeyer
2000; Liu et al. 2023b).
The most well-studied pho-
tometric properties of SNe Ia are their distribution in
the parameter space of peak luminosity and decline
rate, on which the width–luminosity relation (WLR;
see, e.g., Phillips & Burns 2017) has been established.
The WLR and SNe Ia obeying the relation (hereafter
WLR-sequence SNe Ia) serve as the cornerstone for stan-
dardization of the “standardizable candle”. Some pe-
culiar SN Ia subtypes, such as 02cx-like SN Ia (also
known as SN Iax), 02es-like SN Ia, and 09dc-like SN
Ia, clearly do not obey the WLR (Taubenberger 2017;
Chen et al. 2019). 91T-like SNe Ia with SN 1991T as the
prototype (Filippenko et al. 1992a; Phillips et al. 1992)
and 91bg-like SNe Ia with SN 1991bg as the prototype
(Filippenko et al. 1992b; Leibundgut et al. 1993a) are
two other peculiar groups which represent the luminous
slow-declining end and the dim fast-declining end of the
SN Ia population, respectively. While 91T-like SNe Ia
have commonly been treated as WLR-sequence SNe Ia
(Phillips et al. 2024), it remains a matter of debate
whether 91bg-like events represent the low-luminosity
and fast-declining end of the WLR (Phillips et al. 2026).
This is an essential matter to address in understanding
the progenitor and explosion models of SNe Ia, particu-
larly if we believe that all WLR-sequence SNe Ia share
the same explosion model (Sharon & Kushnir 2022).
Specifically, it is crucial to determine whether 91bg-like
events represent a distinct explosion channel or arise
from the same channel with only extreme parameters.
The introduction of the color–stretch parameter sBV
demonstrates that 91bg-like SNe Ia also follow the WLR
(Burns et al. 2014, 2018; Chen et al. 2023; Graur 2024;
Phillips et al. 2026), making them an indispensable com-
ponent of any unified physical framework aimed at ex-
plaining the full width–luminosity sequence of SNe Ia.
In this context, the WLR-sequence SNe Ia include 91T-
like SNe Ia (Phillips et al. 2024), normal-luminosity
SNe Ia, which largely overlap with the core-normal
objects in the Branch diagram (Branch et al. 2006),
transitional SNe Ia (Hsiao et al. 2015), and 91bg-like
SNe Ia. This suggests that the apparent diversity among
these subclasses may arise from a continuous distribu-
tion of explosion properties within a common physical
framework. These subgroups, therefore, should not be
treated as isolated or fundamentally distinct classes, but
rather as closely related manifestations of a continuous
SN Ia population governed by similar underlying explo-
sion physics.
From a physical perspective, the defining observa-
tional properties of 91bg-like SNe Ia, including rapid
photometric evolution,
low peak luminosities,
and
strong low-ionization features such as enhanced Ti II
absorption, collectively point to ejecta with low mass,
reduced radioactive heating, and cooler thermodynamic
conditions (Mazzali et al. 1997).
These characteris-
tics are naturally produced in sub-Chandrasekhar-mass
(sub-MCh) explosions, which synthesize less 56Ni and
have shorter diffusion timescales (Blondin et al. 2017;
Goldstein & Kasen 2018; Shen et al. 2021a), thereby
providing a physically motivated explanation for the
observed properties of this subclass.
In addition to
their photometric and spectroscopic properties, 91bg-
like SNe Ia preferentially occur in early-type host galax-
ies, indicating old progenitor populations and long delay
times (Howell 2001; Panther et al. 2019; Barkhudaryan
et al. 2019).
This is compatible with sub-MCh deto-
nations, although such environments do not uniquely
distinguish between different explosion channels.
The sub-Chandrasekhar detonation of a low-mass WD
with mass around 0.85–0.9 M⊙matches well with obser-
vations of 91bg-like SNe Ia (Sim et al. 2010; Ruiter et al.
2013; Blondin et al. 2017, 2018; Shen et al. 2021a,b).
Specifically, Blondin et al. (2018) performed radiative

A long-standing theoretical prediction for the helium
detonation triggered sub-MCh detonations is the pres-
ence of unburnt helium at high velocities (Woosley &
Weaver 1994; Fink et al. 2010; Kromer et al. 2010; Shen
& Moore 2014). Non-local-thermodynamic-equilibrium
(NLTE) radiative transfer calculations including He I
excitation by non-thermal electrons consistently predict
observable absorption features in the early-time spec-
tra, particularly through the strong He I 1.083 µm and
2.058 µm transitions (Dessart & Hillier 2015; Boyle et al.
2017; Collins et al. 2023; Callan et al. 2025). Detect-
ing such features would provide direct evidence for a
helium-rich shell and thus offer strong support for sub-
Chandrasekhar detonations.
Despite this theoretical expectation, observational
claims of helium in SNe Ia have remained sparse and
ambiguous. The NIR “W-shaped” absorption near 1.05
µm commonly observed before maximum light in fast-
declining SNe Ia has been variously attributed to Mg II
1.0927 µm, C I 1.0693 µm, or He I 1.083 µm (Hsiao
et al. 2015; Wyatt et al. 2021a; Li et al. 2022; Lu et al.
2023; Pearson et al. 2024).
The degeneracy among
these species, combined with severe NLTE effects, lim-
ited wavelength coverage, and rapid line evolution, has
prevented a definitive identification. Clear helium sig-
natures have been detected in some interacting SNe Ia
(e.g., the He-rich CSM interaction in SN 2020eyj; Kool
et al. 2023), but these features arise from circumstellar
material rather than the ejecta and thus do not directly
probe the explosion physics.

In this context, SN 2022an presents a rare opportu-

erties firmly place it among the 91bg-like SNe Ia, our
optical and NIR follow-up reveal an absorption feature
near 1.037 µm that persists to nearly +90 days past
maximum, which is much later than the typical dis-
appearance of the 1.05 µm complex in other SNe Ia
(Lu et al. 2023).
The feature is consistently repro-
duced by He I 1.083 µm absorption with a blueshift of
∼1.2 −1.3 × 104 km s−1, and its identification is inde-
pendently supported by the presence of weaker optical
He I transitions at matching velocities. Such persistent,
multi-line helium absorption has never been previously
confirmed in a 91bg-like SN Ia.
In this paper, we present multi-epoch optical and
NIR observations of SN 2022an and report the detec-
tion of persistent He I absorption features in both spec-
tral regimes. Section 2 summarizes the observations and
data reduction.
Section 3 establishes SN 2022an as a
typical 91bg-like SN Ia. Section 4 presents the identi-
fication and temporal evolution of the helium features.
Section 5 discusses the origin and implications of the de-
tected helium in the context of SN Ia explosion models
and progenitor channels.

sistent with the preference of 91bg-like SNe Ia for early-

formation rates. The luminosity distance to NGC 4645B
derived from the redshift is dL
= 37.6 Mpc (dis-
tance modulus µ = 32.88 mag) by simply adopting
a standard ΛCDM cosmology model with parameters
H0 = 73 km s−1 Mpc−1, Ωm = 0.27 and ΩΛ = 0.73.
NGC 4645B belongs to the Centaurus Cluster of galax-

2000). This implies that the peculiar galaxy velocity rel-
ative to the Hubble flow constitutes a significant fraction

We first test the candidate ions observationally by
checking for other transitions of the same species under
LTE assumptions, deferring questions of physical feasi-
bility. The narrow observed absorption sits atop a broad
emission pseudo-continuum, so any comparable narrow
feature would stand out clearly. Except for He I, each
ion capable of producing the observed trough has a cor-
responding transition of comparable or greater strength
(Marion et al. 2009); the non-detection of these lines
therefore argues against their identification. For exam-
ple, attributing the 1.037 µm trough to Mg II 1.0927 µm
would require the companion Mg II 0.9227 µm line to be
present. Under LTE conditions at 5000–10000 K, the
latter is expected to be 2–3 times stronger (Marion et al.
2009).
Non-LTE model spectra (Blondin et al. 2017,
2018) independently confirm that the 0.9227 µm line is of
comparable or greater strength whenever the 1.0927 µm
line appears. The absence of this corresponding absorp-
tion (Fig. 9) therefore argues against the Mg II identifi-
cation. We applied the same test to the remaining candi-
date ions, simulating expected companion-line strengths
using LTE ratios over the same temperature range and
adopting conservative values equal to or smaller than
the theoretical predictions.
The He I 1.083 µm line provides a viable explana-
tion for the observed absorption in SN 2022an.
For
He I, the high excitation energy of the 1.083 µm tran-
sition requires non-thermal processes. The NLTE cal-
culations by Callan et al. (2025) predict a ratio of
EW(He I 2.058 µm)/EW(He I 1.083 µm) ≈0.4 (see panel
(b) of Fig. 11 and Section 4.4). The expected line profile
for the He I 2.058 µm transition, with an EW half that
of the 1.083 µm line, is shown as the green curve in the
bottom panel of Fig. 9. The observed spectrum is con-
sistent with an absorption there, matching the NLTE
prediction from Callan et al. (2025).
The inferred blueshift velocity of 12,740 km s−1 places
the absorbing helium outside the silicon layer, whose
blueshift velocity is 9,400 km s−1 in the +5.3-day opti-
cal spectrum. Radiative-transfer calculations of helium-
shell double-detonation models likewise produce compa-
rable blueshifts of the He I 1.083 µm line (Fig. 11).
In conclusion, none of the alternative ions can repro-
duce the observed 1.037 µm absorption feature with-
out simultaneously predicting additional, comparable,
or even stronger lines that are clearly absent in the
data. In contrast, the He I identification is fully con-
sistent with the observed spectral morphology, expected
line ratios, and blueshifted velocities. We therefore fa-
vor He I 1.083 µm as the most plausible origin of the
absorption feature in SN 2022an.

4.4. Comparison with Model Predictions
Helium is an essential ingredient in the helium-
shell double-detonation scenario (Nomoto 1982a,b). In
this framework,
a surface helium layer on a sub-
Chandrasekhar-mass white dwarf detonates, triggering
a secondary carbon detonation in the core.
Numer-
ical simulations of such explosions generally predict
that a significant fraction of unburnt helium remains
in the outermost ejecta (Woosley & Weaver 1994; Fink
et al. 2010; Shen & Moore 2014).
Early radiative-
transfer calculations, however, neglected non-thermal
excitation and ionization, and therefore produced spec-
tra that lacked He I features (Shen et al. 2010; Wald-
man et al. 2011). Dessart & Hillier (2015) carried out
time-dependent NLTE radiative-transfer simulations of
the helium-shell detonation model COp45HEp2 of Wald-
man et al. (2011), demonstrating that non-thermal pro-
cesses naturally yield unambiguous He I absorption in
both the optical and NIR at epochs around maximum
light. Their models were tailored to faint, rapidly evolv-
ing transients such as .Ia explosions and Ca-rich events,
where the ejecta originate only from the helium-shell
detonation itself.
Boyle et al. (2017) investigated the visibility of un-
burnt helium in more contemporary double-detonation
models designed to explain normal SNe Ia.
Using an
analytical approximation for the He I level populations
and the explosion models of Fink et al. (2010), they pre-
dicted strong high-velocity absorption of He I 1.083 µm
and He I 2.058 µm for a low-luminosity model contain-
ing 0.077 M⊙of helium. Their approach assumed that
He II dominates the ionization balance, an assumption
that may not hold in all regimes and therefore affects the
predicted strength and temporal evolution of the He I
lines.
A more complete treatment was presented by Collins
et al. (2023), who computed full NLTE ionization and
excitation, including non-thermal electrons for the M2a
double-detonation model of Gronow et al. (2020). Their

5. DISCUSSION
5.1. Origin of the Helium
Several possible origins for the helium must be consid-
ered. In the helium-shell double detonation scenario, a
significant fraction of unburnt helium remaining in the
outermost ejecta has long been predicted (Woosley &
Weaver 1994; Fink et al. 2010; Shen & Moore 2014).
Such helium around the progenitor could be accreted
from the degenerate or non-degenerate companion he-
lium star. It could also result from the merger of the
progenitor with a low-mass helium white dwarf (Dan
et al. 2014, 2015).
An alternative possibility is helium stripped from a
non-degenerate helium-star companion. Hydrodynamic
simulations show that a small fraction of the compan-
ion envelope becomes unbound, typically of order 10−3
to a few ×10−2 M⊙depending on the binary separa-
tion and stellar structure (e.g., Wang et al. 2009a; Pan
et al. 2010; Liu et al. 2013). The stripped material is
mixed into the 56Ni-rich ejecta with a velocity distri-
bution strongly peaked at low speeds: most of the un-
bound helium resides at ≲(2–5)×103 km s−1, with only
a weak high-velocity tail. Such material is therefore ex-
pected to produce narrow nebular emission (Boty´anszki
et al. 2018; Dessart et al. 2020) rather than the high-
velocity absorption. The helium absorption detected in
SN 2022an, by contrast, appears at much higher veloci-
ties of ∼(1.3–1.5) × 104 km s−1 and persists for several
months, strongly disfavoring this scenario.
We also consider whether the helium could originate
from a very low-mass helium white dwarf, such as an
AM CVn star analogous to GP Com (M ≈0.01 M⊙)
that orbits the progenitor at explosion.

Overall, the most natural explanation for the helium
in SN 2022an is unburnt helium that was already present
on the white dwarf at the time of explosion, residing in
the outermost layers of the progenitor and subsequently
ejected at high velocities during the detonation. In this
picture, the detected helium is an intrinsic component
of the progenitor system rather than material stripped
from or dynamically accelerated by a companion star.
5.2. Physical Implications of the Helium Absorption
The detection of persistent He
I absorption in
SN 2022an provides strong evidence for the presence of
helium embedded in the outer ejecta. The nearly con-
stant line width (FWHM ∼2000 km s−1) and the slow
decline of the EW over ∼60 days imply that the absorb-
ing helium occupies a well-defined velocity layer and re-
mains at least partially optically thick well into the post-
maximum phase. Such long-lived absorption requires a
sustained population of the metastable He I levels, which
must be maintained by non-thermal excitation from fast
electrons produced in the radioactive decay of 56Ni and
56Co. This, in turn, indicates that a non-negligible frac-
tion of radioactive material or its decay products must
penetrate into the high-velocity helium-rich layers.
At the same time, establishing a direct quantitative
connection between the helium mass, its velocity dis-
tribution, and the resulting spectroscopic signatures re-
mains challenging.
The appearance of He I lines de-
pends sensitively on the spatial distribution of 56Ni, the
degree of mixing, the local radiation field, and non-
local-thermodynamic-equilibrium effects.
Even within
a given explosion model, the amount of unburnt helium
and its observable velocity range can vary significantly
with viewing angle. Consequently, reproducing the ob-
served helium features in SN 2022an will require ded-
icated multi-dimensional hydrodynamic and radiative-
transfer calculations tailored to individual progenitor
configurations.
5.3. Implications for progenitor and explosion models
of 91bg-like SNe Ia and other WLR-sequence SNe
Ia
The observed helium absorption in SN 2022an de-
mands a helium-rich outer layer that underwent highly
incomplete burning during the explosion.
In sub-
Chandrasekhar-mass progenitors, the mass of the ac-
creted helium shell is expected to increase toward lower
white dwarf masses, as a larger helium mass is re-
quired to reach ignition conditions on lower-mass, larger-
radius white dwarfs (Shen & Bildsten 2009; Fink et al.
2010; Woosley & Kasen 2011b). For 91bg-like SNe Ia,
helium-shell double-detonation models occupy a partic-
ularly favorable region of parameter space for produc-
ing observable helium signatures. These events are best
matched by low-mass sub-Chandrasekhar white dwarfs
(MWD ∼0.85–0.95 M⊙), which require comparatively
massive helium shells to reach ignition conditions (Fink
et al. 2010; Sim et al. 2010). In this low-mass regime,
helium burning is expected to be highly incomplete due
to lower shell densities and stronger detonation curva-
ture, leaving a substantial fraction of unburnt helium
at high velocities (Shen & Moore 2014; Gronow et al.
2020). Consequently, 91bg-like SNe Ia are expected to
be the subclass most likely to exhibit detectable He I ab-
sorption, while such features may remain weak or absent
in higher-luminosity SNe Ia with thinner helium shells
and more complete burning. In this picture, SN 2022an
represents an extreme but physically informative real-
ization of a sub-Chandrasekhar-mass explosion with an
unusually prominent helium signature.
Double-degenerate merger channels are not mutually
exclusive with helium-shell double detonation models;
instead, they can provide a natural physical pathway
to such explosions.
In particular, mergers between a
C–O white dwarf and a low-mass helium white dwarf
lead to the deposition of a substantial helium layer onto
the primary, which may subsequently ignite under de-
generate conditions and trigger a secondary detonation
in the C–O core (Woosley et al. 1986; Bildsten et al.
2007; Shen et al. 2010; Fink et al. 2010; Dan et al. 2014,
2015). Such C–O+He WD mergers preferentially occur
at long delay times and yield lower total ejecta masses
and reduced 56Ni production, naturally aligning with
the fast-evolving, subluminous properties and old host
environments of 91bg-like SNe Ia. By contrast, mergers
involving two C–O white dwarfs generally lack a sub-
stantial helium reservoir and are therefore less likely to
produce observable helium-rich outer ejecta.

5.4. Limitations and Future Prospects
The primary limitation of our analysis is the lack of
early-time NIR spectra, when the development of the
He I 1.083 µm feature could be directly traced and com-
pared to model predictions for its initial formation. A
complete temporal sequence combining optical and NIR
spectroscopy from the pre-maximum phase onward is es-
sential for constraining the excitation mechanism, ion-
ization balance, and helium mass in the outer ejecta.
Future facilities optimized for rapid-transient follow-up,
such as SoXS (Schipani et al. 2018), will provide the
sensitivity and cadence needed to systematically search
for helium in faint, fast-declining SNe Ia, and conduct
detailed studies. A larger sample will clarify whether
SN 2022an represents a rare outlier or a key example
of a broader population of helium-bearing sub-MCh ex-
plosions.
On the theoretical side, systematic NLTE
radiative-transfer calculations spanning the low-mass
sub-MCh regime are needed to determine under what
conditions helium absorption becomes detectable.

APPENDIX
A. IMAGING AND PHOTOMETRY OF SN 2022AN
The multiband photometric observations of SN 2022an
are summarized as follows.
LCOGT-1m/Sinistro —We observed SN 2022an in
BV gri bands using the Sinistro cameras mounted on
the 1 m class telescopes of the Las Cumbres Observa-
tory Global Telescope Network (LCOGT; Brown et al.
2013). The observations were conducted from 2022 Jan-
uary 9 to April 2. The images were reduced with the
LCOGT/BANZAI pipeline (McCully et al. 2018). We
downloaded the reduced frames from the Las Cumbres
Observatory science archive.
NTT/EFOSC2 —We observed two epochs of photo-
metric data in BV bands with the ESO Faint Object
Spectrograph and Camera version 2 (EFOSC2; Buzzoni
et al. 1984) on the ESO 3.58m New Technology Tele-
scope (NTT). The basic data reduction, including bias
subtraction, flat-field correction, and astrometric solu-
tion, was performed using the PESSTO pipeline (Smartt
et al. 2015b). We also obtained four epochs of acquisi-
tion images in the V band from the spectroscopic obser-
vations.
Magellan/IMACS —We obtained three epochs of gri
images with the Inamori-Magellan Areal Camera and
Spectrograph (IMACS; Dressler et al. 2011). The im-
ages were reduced using IRAF (Tody 1986, 1993), in-
cluding bias subtraction and flat-field correction.
NTT/SOFI —We observed four epochs of NIR images
of SN 2022an in JHKs bands with the Son OF ISAAC
(SOFI; Moorwood et al. 1998) on the NTT telescope.
We performed PSF photometry with the daophot task
in IRAF (Tody 1986, 1993). The host galaxy background
flux was modelled using an isophote model and iter-
atively subtracted from the image (see the method in
Chen et al. 2022). We utilize the ATLAS All-sky Stel-
lar Reference Catalog (ATLAS-REFCAT2; Tonry et al.
2018) to derive the photometric zero-point of the optical
photometry. Before being used for photometric calibra-
tion of our target, the ATLAS-REFCAT2 magnitudes
of the reference stars in the fields are first converted to
the Johnson BV and Sloan-griz bands using the trans-
formations given in Tonry et al. (2012). Reference stars
in the field of view from the 2MASS catalog (Skrut-
skie et al. 2006) were used to derive the photometric
zeropoint for the JHKs bands. All these photometry
procedures were performed using the pmpyeasy pipeline
(Chen et al. 2022).
The photometry data for SN 2022an are summarized
in Table 1 for the BV gri bands and in Table 2 for the
JHK bands. The gri magnitudes are reported in the
AB system, while the BV JHK magnitudes are based
on the Vega system.
We also obtained All-Sky Automated Survey for
Supernovae (ASAS-SN) g-band photometry (Shappee
et al. 2014) from the ASAS-SN Sky Patrol 3 (Kochanek
et al. 2017).
We adopted the “Image Subtraction
(No reference flux added)” photometry method, which
performs aperture photometry on the coadded image-
subtracted data for each epoch but does not add the
source flux from the reference image to the light curve.
The result is present in Table 3.

with IRAF (Tody 1986, 1993), including bias subtraction,

the target observation), and flux calibration with spec-
