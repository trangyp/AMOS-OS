Abstract
A linear code is said to be ∆-divisible if the Hamming weights of all its codewords
are divisible by ∆. The p-adic valuation of a code is defined as the greatest integer t such
that the code is pt-divisible. In this paper, we establish a divisibility criterion for trace
codes. Specifically, this criterion provides a systematic method to determine the p-adic
valuation of the associated trace code, thereby extending Ward’s classical divisibility
criterion from standard generating sets (or matrices) to generalized generator matrices
over an extension field. Furthermore, we present two applications of our framework.
The first application provides a concise proof of the celebrated divisibility results on
abelian codes established by Delsarte and McEliece. The second application establishes
several explicit lower bounds on the p-adic valuation of the number of solutions over
Fqm (where q = pe) to the Artin-Schreier type equation f(x1, . . . , xk) = yq −y. In
particular, under the condition

The profound influence of Ax’s theorem extends deeply into algebraic coding theory,
particularly inspiring the study of the p-divisibility of linear codes. Recall that a linear code
C over Fq is termed ∆-divisible if the Hamming weight wt(c) of every codeword c ∈C is a
multiple of ∆[18], and the greatest integer t for which C is pt-divisible is defined as the p-
adic valuation of C. In [13], McEliece established a fundamental theorem characterizing the
p-adic valuation of cyclic codes over prime fields Fp. Delsarte and McEliece later extended
this result to abelian codes over arbitrary finite fields [3, 4]. More recently, D. J. Katz [8]
generalized this theorem to the p-divisibility of t-wise Hamming weight of abelian codes.
Determining the p-adic valuation of an arbitrary linear code directly from its definition
requires computing the weights of all its codewords, which is generally computationally
intractable. To overcome this, Ward developed the powerful “weight polarization” method
[17] and established a fundamental divisibility criterion [19]. Ward’s criterion is remarkable
because it determines the divisibility of a code C solely by examining the combinatorial
properties of a given additive spanning set (or a basis) of C, thereby significantly reducing
the complexity of the problem. As an application, Ward’s criterion can recover Ax’s theorem
when applied to Reed-Muller (RM) codes [19]. Besides, another important application of
Ward’s criterion is to obtain the divisibility of Griesmer codes [20, 5], which can be used to
prove their non-existence [12, 21, 10].
In this paper, we establish a generalized divisibility criterion for trace codes by integrating
the arithmetic machinery of Stickelberger’s theorem with the technique of p-adic valuation-
preserving transformations.
Utilizing this criterion, we can easily deduce the celebrated
Delsarte-McEliece divisibility results on abelian codes.
As a further major application of our framework, we investigate the p-divisibility of the
number of solutions to the Artin-Schreier equation f = yq −y over finite fields. We derive
several explicit lower bounds for this p-adic valuation. Most notably, in the case where f is
a homogeneous polynomial of degree d satisfying a specific coprime condition, our method
enables us to determine a tight lower bound for the p-divisibility of the number of solutions.

Application to abelian codes
