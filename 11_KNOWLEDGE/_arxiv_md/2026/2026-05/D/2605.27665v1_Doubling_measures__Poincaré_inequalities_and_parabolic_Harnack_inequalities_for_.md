DOUBLING MEASURES, POINCAR´E INEQUALITIES AND
PARABOLIC HARNACK INEQUALITIES FOR A DOUBLY
NONLINEAR EQUATION
THEO ELENIUS AND JUHA KINNUNEN
Abstract. We characterize metric measure spaces satisfying parabolic Har-
nack inequalities for a doubly nonlinear equation in terms of volume doubling
and Poincar´e inequalities. Our approach uses purely analytical methods, based
on obtaining estimates for solutions to a related Cauchy problem. This extends
previous linear results to the nonlinear setting without relying on heat kernel
estimates and representation formulae.
1. Introduction
A fundamental problem in analysis on metric spaces is to determine whether
properties of the underlying space can be characterized via the behavior of solu-
tions to partial differential equations. A well-known result of Grigor’yan [14] and
Saloff-Coste [25, 26] states that the parabolic Harnack inequality for the heat equa-
tion characterizes volume doubling and a (1, 2)-Poincar´e inequality on Riemannian
manifolds. The corresponding result has later been obtained in several settings, in-
cluding locally finite graphs [11], Dirichlet forms on locally compact metric spaces
[29] and non-local Dirichlet forms [10], among others.

Doubling measures and Poincar´e inequalities play a central role in analysis on
metric measure spaces. Typically these conditions are imposed a priori. For ex-
ample, they imply Sobolev embedding theorems which are essential in many ap-
plications. We refer to the monographs [5, 17, 18] for comprehensive accounts of
this theory. Semmes [27] gave a geometric characterization of Poincar´e inequalities
in terms of families of curves, but in general few characterizations of these con-
ditions are known. Our main result establishes a connection between analysis on
metric spaces and the regularity theory of nonlinear parabolic partial differential
equations. Although our primary motivation comes from the setting of Riemannian
manifolds, we work in the more general framework of metric measure spaces.
Theorem 1.1. Let 1 < p < ∞.
Let (X, d, µ) be a metric measure space such
that X is geodesic and complete, the measure µ is finite and nonzero on balls,
the Sobolev space N 1,p(X) is reflexive and for p ̸= 2 assume that the Rellich–

(1) µ is doubling and a (1, p)-Poincar´e inequality holds in (X, d, µ), see (2.1)

Our proof of Theorem 1.1 begins in Section 4, by presenting a proof of the
parabolic Harnack inequality (1.2) starting from volume doubling and a (1, p)-
Poincar´e inequality. We give a self-contained proof using De Giorgi–Di Benedetto-
type iteration techniques and the expansion of positivity. The final argument for
showing the parabolic Harnack inequality uses a variant of the technique used for
the parabolic p-Laplace equation in [12].
Another contribution is that we only
assume the solution in (1.2) to be nonnegative, whereas previous sources in the
metric setting [21, 24] require that the solution be locally bounded away from zero.
This is non-trivial since constants cannot generally be added to solutions of (1.1)
to produce a new solution.
The assumptions on reflexivity and the Rellich–Kondrachov compactness prop-
erty in Theorem 1.1 are only applied to prove the existence of a solution to a Cauchy
problem for (1.1) in Section 5. These assumptions hold in geodesically complete
Riemannian manifolds, and in the metric setting they are implied by doubling and
Poincar´e, see [9, 15]. Our approach to solutions of (1.1) is variational, and the
existence proof is based on the well-known minimizing movements scheme, adapted
to our setting. We refer to [7] and the references therein for existence results in the
Euclidean setting, and [30] for existence in Riemannian manifolds.
The proof that the parabolic Harnack inequalities implies doubling and Poincar´e
will proceed in several steps, which we outline below. In Section 6 we prove that
the parabolic Harnack inequality implies volume doubling. Since the equation (1.1)

is nonlinear, solutions do not admit a representation formula in terms of the fun-

approximating the Dirac delta satisfy estimates analogous to those of the heat ker-

In Section 7, we show that the parabolic Harnack inequality implies a Poincar´e
inequality. First, we show a Poincar´e-type inequality for functions with zero bound-
ary values on a given ball in Proposition 7.1. To obtain this inequality, we use the
parabolic Harnack inequality to conclude quantitative decay of the Lp norm of so-
lutions to a Cauchy problem and apply the energy decreasing properties of (1.1).
Then we present a technique of proving a general Poincar´e inequality from an in-
equality for functions with zero boundary values, by applying the Dirichlet problem
for the p-Laplace equation. The p-Laplace is the stationary version of (1.1), and
hence the elliptic Harnack inequality is available for nonnegative solutions. The
elliptic Harnack inequality is applied to localize functions to smaller balls, and to
obtain H¨older continuity estimates.
As a by-product of our proof, we obtain that the conjunction of volume dou-
bling, a Poincar´e-type inequality for zero boundary values and the elliptic Harnack
inequality is equivalent with volume doubling and a Poincar´e inequality, and in
turn with the parabolic Harnack inequality. This equivalence was shown for p = 2
in [16], but their proof proceeds via the established equivalence of Gaussian heat
kernel estimates with volume doubling and a Poincar´e inequality, and hence is not
applicable in our setting.
More general forms of parabolic Harnack inequalities, involving a time lag of
order Ψ(r), for the heat equation have also been considered. Such parabolic Harnack
inequalities appear in the analysis of fractals, and have been shown to characterize
volume doubling, a Ψ-Poincar´e inequality and a Ψ-cutoff Sobolev inequality, see
[1, 3]. Related techniques have played an important role in the characterization and
stability theory of elliptic Harnack inequalities for the Laplace equation [2, 19, 4].
We hope that the methods developed in this work will be useful to study the
corresponding nonlinear problems as well.
As an immediate consequence of Theorem 1.1, we obtain that the parabolic Har-
nack inequality (1.2) is stable under perturbations of the underlying space which
preserve volume doubling and Poincar´e inequality. Theorem 1.1 also implies stabil-
ity of parabolic Harnack inequalities with respect to the parameter p. By a result of
Keith and Zhong [20], a (1, p)-Poincar´e inequality implies a (1, q)-Poincar´e inequal-
ity for some q < p. Consequently, if the parabolic Harnack inequality (1.2) holds
for some p, it holds for some q < p. We also note that a (1, p)-Poincar´e inequality
implies a (1, q)-Poincar´e inequality for every q > p.
