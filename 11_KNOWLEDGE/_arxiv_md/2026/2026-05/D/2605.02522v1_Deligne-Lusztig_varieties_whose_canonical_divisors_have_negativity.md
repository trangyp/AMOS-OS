DELIGNE–LUSZTIG VARIETIES WHOSE CANONICAL DIVISORS
HAVE NEGATIVITY
ULRICH G¨ORTZ AND STEFAN SCHR¨OER
5 May 2026
Abstract. We investigate compactified Deligne–Lusztig varieties whose canonical
divisor, when expressed as a linear combination of boundary divisors, has all coef-
ficients strictly negative or zero. In dimension two we obtain explicit descriptions:
The case C2 gives the supersingular K3 surface with Artin invariant σ = 1 in char-
acteristic two. The arguments rely on properties of the Tutte–Coxeter graph; in this
connection we also gain some insight into the arithmetic of quasi-elliptic Weierstrass
equations and rational double points. The twisted cases 2A2 and 2C2 and 2G2 yield
particular ruled surfaces, attached in a canonical way to supersingular elliptic curves
in characteristic two, or the Ree curve in characteristic three. The latter is a curve
of genus fifteen with outstanding symmetries. In the former cases, the surfaces can
also be expressed as symmetric squares. To obtain such results, we develop a new
general framework for Deligne–Lusztig varieties that relies on the Isogeny Theorem,
and works over prime fields rather than their algebraic closures, and includes the
notorious Suzuki–Ree cases without effort.

Introduction
In their groundbreaking work [19], Deligne and Lusztig introduced certain smooth
subschemes X(w) inside the flag variety G/B of a reductive group G over the field
k = Falg

in fixed relative position w to the flag φ(g)B. Here φ: G →G is the isogeny stemming
from the Frobenius power F s for a chosen reductive group G0 over a finite field k0 = Fps
that induces G. Note that the isogeny is purely inseparable, but in general not the
identity on the underlying topological space. The fixed set Gφ is a finite group of Lie
type, and the main goal of Deligne and Lusztig was to understand its representation
theory in terms of the ℓ-adic cohomology with compact support of X(w). Indeed,
the so-called Deligne–Lusztig varieties X(w) quickly became an indispensable tool for
representation theory in this area, and received an enormous echo, compare [55], [56],
[57], [23], [7], [6] and many others.

All the schemes X(w) are affine.

singularities. However, each reduced expression w = si1 . . . sin yields a resolution of
singularities, which by abuse of notation is often written as ¯X(w). The situation is
parallel to Schubert cells, Schubert varieties and their resolutions `a la Demazure and
Bott–Samelson. For example, the length n = ℓ(w) coincides with dim ¯X(w).
Summing up, the group-theoretical datum G and φ : G →G and w ∈W yields
a smooth proper scheme X = ¯X(w), which we also call Deligne–Lusztig variety. In
marked contrast to representation theory, to date the impact of these scheme in alge-
braic geometry is comparatively small. Noteworthy exceptions are J. Hansen’s anal-
ysis of Deligne–Lusztig curves [34], Rodier’s study in dimension two [66], S. Hansen’s
Canonical Bundle Formula [36], and closely related work by Ekedahl [26] and Langer
[52] on non-liftable Calabi–Yau threefolds.
In this paper, we view the smooth proper schemes X = ¯X(w) as natural objects
of algebraic geometry waiting to be explored, and initiate a systematic study of their
properties. In particular, we seek to understand their place in the classification of
all algebraic varieties, and to obtain for particular Weyl group elements explicit de-
scriptions in various other contexts. The following theorem, which is one of our main
results, illustrates what we have in mind:
Theorem A. (Compare Thm. 6.1) For the symplectic group G = Sp4 over the prime
field k = F2 with isogeny φ = F, the Frobenius morphism, the Deligne–Lusztig surface
X = ¯X(s2s1) is a supersingular K3 surface with Artin invariant σ = 1.
Recall that the K3 surfaces play a central role in the Enriques Classification of
algebraic surfaces, and can be characterized in terms of the numerical invariants by
c1 = 0 and b2 = 22. Supersingularity means that the non-degenerate lattice Pic(X ⊗
kalg) has rank ρ = 22, and the Artin invariant σ ≥1 measures how far the intersection
form deviates from unimodularity. For σ = 1 the surfaces are unique up to twisted
forms. In our theorem, X is actually given by the minimal resolution of singularities
for the family of cubic curves over P1 = Spec k[t] ∪Spec k[t−1] given by a Weierstraß
equation y2 = x3 + a6 with coefficient a6 = t5 + t7.

1.2. The Isogeny Theorem. In this section we summarize the theory of pinned

group G that is smooth, connected and reductive, together with a split maximal torus
T ⊂G. The conjugation action by T on the Lie algebra g = Lie(G) corresponds to a
