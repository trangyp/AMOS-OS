Differences Between Emission and Absorption Tracers of Spatially
Resolved Outflows in Clumpy z∼0.1 Star-forming Galaxies
Antonia Fernández-Figueroa1★, Glenn G. Kacprzak,1 Deanne B. Fisher,1 Magdalena Hamel-Bravo,1
Karl Glazebrook1
1Centre for Astrophysics and Supercomputing, Swinburne University of Technology, Hawthorn, Victoria 3122, Australia

ABSTRACT
We present spatially resolved Keck/LRIS spectroscopy of three clumpy star-forming galaxies at 𝑧∼0.1, comparing outflow
properties traced by H𝛼and Mgii emission with those probed by Mgii and Nai D absorption. Outflow velocities measured using
Mgii absorption (⟨𝑣out⟩= −560±30 km s−1) are consistently higher than those traced by H𝛼emission (⟨𝑣out⟩= −124±3 km s−1)
across ∼5 kpc2 regions. Despite this offset, the correlation between 𝑣out and galaxy properties, such as SFR and ΣSFR, show
similar slopes for both tracers, with Mgii absorption systematically offset by ∼0.4 dex. In two galaxies, Mgii emission is also
detected, yielding velocities consistent with H𝛼. In one galaxy we also detect outflows in Nai D absorption and find similar
velocities as Mgii in absorption, which leads to a ∼0.4 dex higher Nai D outflow velocities compared to those measured in
emission. Our spatially resolved results are consistent with those found for galactic-scale measurements, implying the outflow
relationships are similar from the sales of ∼1-2 kpc to global measurements. Combined with literature measurements, these
results suggest that the offset in velocities is driven not by ionisation state, but rather by the systematics associated to how
absorption and emission measures trace the gas density.
Key words: ISM: jets and outflows – galaxies: evolution – galaxies: starburst
1 INTRODUCTION
Star formation-driven outflows are a necessary component for galaxy
evolution models to be consistent with observations of galaxies. They
contribute to metal enrichment of the intergalactic medium (Garnett
2002; Tremonti et al. 2004; Dalcanton 2007), regulate star-formation
within galaxies (Hopkins et al. 2006; Cazzoli et al. 2014), and play a
fundamental role in shaping galaxy disks (Scannapieco et al. 2008).
These outflows are observed in high star-formation rate (SFR) galax-
ies at all redshifts (Veilleux et al. 2005; Förster Schreiber et al. 2019;
Carniani et al. 2024; Thompson & Heckman 2024). Given the im-
portant role outflows play in regulating star formation, it is important
to understand their properties.
There are two widely used tracers of outflows: optical emis-
sion lines and UV/near-UV absorption lines. Outflows in emis-
sion lines, such as the [Oii] 𝜆𝜆3727, 3729, [Oiii] 𝜆𝜆4959, 5007,
[Nii] 𝜆𝜆6548, 6583, [Sii] 𝜆𝜆6717, 6731 doublets, and the Balmer
series, are identified by finding broad blue shifted components in
their line profiles. Because the intensity of emission line gas scales
with density as 𝐼∝𝑛2, these measurements are naturally biased
towards higher-density regions of the outflow and therefore prefer-
entially trace gas located closer to the galaxy. In contrast, absorption
lines, such as Mgii 𝜆𝜆2796, 2803, trace the gas linearly with density,
giving access to more diffuse material, a wider range of column den-
sities, and gas located further away along the line of sight (Veilleux

known as ‘down-the-barrel’ spectroscopy (e.g., Martin 2005; Martin
& Bouché 2009; Bordoloi et al. 2011; Kornei et al. 2012; Kacprzak
et al. 2014; Rubin et al. 2014; Heckman et al. 2015). Direct compar-
ison of emission and absorption tracers can therefore not only reveal
the biases inherent to each method, but also provide a more complete
view of the outflow.
The vast majority of resolved outflow measurements are made
on emission lines. Work on resolved observations of outflows have
recently shown strong correlations between outflow velocity (𝑣out)
and star-formation rate surface density (ΣSFR, Reichardt Chu et al.
2022, 2025), which was unclear in unresolved studies (Rupke et al.
2005; Chen et al. 2010; Kornei et al. 2012; Newman et al. 2012;
Arribas et al. 2014; Bordoloi et al. 2014; Rubin et al. 2014; Chisholm
et al. 2015; Heckman et al. 2015; Fernández-Figueroa et al. 2025).
These correlations are interpreted to favour models of feedback and
test simulations (e.g., Kim et al. 2020; Rathjen et al. 2023). However,
it is unclear whether these resolved correlations remain for absorption
lines, or if they are weakened as this gas tracer favours larger distances
from the galaxy.
To date, only two resolved studies comparing outflow tracers have
been performed in the literature (Wood et al. 2015; Martín-Fernández
et al. 2016). Both works focus on singular galaxies and find that
outflow velocities traced in emission are systematically lower than
those traced in absorption. More recently, Xu et al. (2025) and Peng
et al. (2025) performed similar analyses for unresolved spectra of

3 SPECTRAL LINE MODELING
We modelled the H𝛼and [Nii] 𝜆𝜆6548, 6583 emission lines by two
sets of three Gaussians to the spectral profiles, with each set repre-
senting an ISM narrow component and a broad component associated
with outflowing material. We measure typical velocity dispersions of
50 km s−1 for the narrow component, which is approximately in the
range of previous DYNAMO measurements (Green et al. 2014). The
narrow and broad H𝛼components were modelled independently,
with amplitude, velocity and velocity dispersion free to vary. The
Nii components were set to have the same velocities and velocity
dispersions as the H𝛼lines. For each spectrum, we ran 1000 Monte
Carlo realisations, allowing the data to vary within a Gaussian distri-
bution with a standard deviation equal to the 1𝜎errors of the spectra.
We adopted the median and standard deviation of the resulting dis-
tributions as the final parameters values and uncertainties.
Similarly, we modelled the Mgii 𝜆𝜆2796, 2803 absorption and
emission lines by fitting narrow Gaussians to represent the ISM and
stellar absorption, along with broad Gaussians associated with out-
flows. The narrow absorption and emission components were mod-
elled based on the narrow H𝛼component, allowed to vary within a
small offset in velocity and velocity dispersion. The broad compo-
nents were allowed to vary more freely, but still using the correspond-
ing H𝛼values as a starting guess. The two lines of the doublet were
constrained to have the same velocities and velocity dispersions, but
their amplitudes were fitted independently. It is worth noting that the

While SFR is an important quantity, ΣSFR provides information
about the distribution of energy capable of driving outflows. Galaxies
with ΣSFR ≥0.1 are more likely to be undergoing outflows (Heckman
2002; Heckman et al. 2015; Reichardt Chu et al. 2022). Given the
SFR and effective radius of our galaxies (Fisher et al. 2017), we
expect all of them to be above this threshold. We calculate ΣSFR by
computing the area in kpc enclosed on each pixel, and dividing the
SFR by this quantity. As presented in Table 1, all of the galaxies in
our sample have regions with ΣSFR above this threshold. H10-2 spans
from ΣSFR = 0.01 to 0.2 𝑀⊙yr−1 kpc2. In the case of G13-1, values
range form ΣSFR = 0.1 to 0.2 𝑀⊙yr−1 kpc2. For G14-1, we measure
from ΣSFR = 0.1 to 0.4 𝑀⊙yr−1 kpc2.

but the scatter is large.

5 DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS
In this Letter we study outflows on three clumpy star-forming galax-
ies at 𝑧∼0.1 selected from the DYNAMO survey. Using spatially
resolved observations, we measure outflow velocities (𝑣out) employ-
ing two tracers: H𝛼emission and Mgii absorption. We find that
outflow velocities traced by absorption are consistently higher than
those traced by emission.
Only two previous works in the literature have performed resolved
studies of outflows comparing emission and absorption features.
Wood et al. (2015) measured 𝑣out = −290 km s−1 in H𝛼emission and
𝑣out = −900 km s−1 in absorption, concluding that a large fraction of
the outflowing gas is too diffuse to be detected in emission. They also
reported evidence that the line width in the H𝛼emission originates
from shocks near the disc of the galaxy, suggesting that H𝛼might
not be an ideal tracer of outflows. Similarly, Martín-Fernández et al.
(2016) studied a single galaxy and found 𝑣out = −300 km s−1 in H𝛼
emission and 𝑣out = −470 km s−1 in absorption. They also found
that the outflow detected in emission is compact and concentrated
in a region near the galactic centre, consistent with emission tracing
a denser, lower-velocity gas. In contrast, more extended, diffuse gas
remains undetected in emission. Our results expand on these studies

There is a general assumption that emission line gas traces denser
gas and is thus probing a component of the outflow that is located in
closer proximity to the launch site. The offset in velocities that we ob-
serve, consistent with other works, suggests that this interpretation is
valid. This is further supported by the fact that H𝛼and Mgii emission
trace similar velocities (although Mgii emission may be modified by

or ionisation potential. Moreover, there is the same offset in other
studies making this comparison, which use a variety of absorption
line tracers. Though we do not know the timescales associated with
the gas, it is plausible to expect higher velocity gas to travel farther
from the galaxy. Absorption-emission line comparisons of outflows,
therefore, offer an interesting and more holistic view of the outflow
for comparison to simulations. Future work is necessary to extend
this analysis to higher redshifts and test whether these trends persist.
