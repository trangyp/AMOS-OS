DL-Driven Optimization for ISAC System Equipped
With Pinching and Movable Antennas
Nemanja Stefan Perovi´c, Member, IEEE, Keshav Singh, Senior Member, IEEE, and Chih-Peng Li, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) is con-
sidered to be a promising technology for future wireless systems
due to its ability to provide communication and sensing services
using shared hardware and spectrum resources. Moreover, the
introduction of recently developed pinching antennas (PAs) and
movable antennas (MAs) has the potential to further improve the
performance gains of ISAC. Therefore, our goal is to study the
optimization of the sum-rate for an ISAC system equipped with
PAs and MAs, capable of satisfying minimal sensing requirements.
To achieve it, we derive a closed-form solution for the optimal
sensing receive combiner, and show that it is determined by other
optimization variables. For these other variables (i.e., the positions of
the transmit PAs, the positions of the users’ MAs, the communication
precoding matrices, and the sensing transmit beamformer), we
propose a deep learning (DL) network that ﬁnds their optimal values.
To train the network in an unsupervised manner, we formulate a loss
function consisting of the objective function, as well as the penalty
terms related to the constraints for the PAs and MAs positions.
Simulation results show that using PAs and MAs in ISAC systems
provides a larger sum-rate compared to ISAC systems with only
ﬁxed antennas, and that this performance advantage is increased
with the maximum transmit power. Furthermore, we demonstrate
that the communication performance of the considered system is
a bit more affected by the sensing signal-to-interference-plus-noise
ratio (SINR) threshold compared to the sensing performance.
Index Terms—Deep learning (DL), integrated sensing and commu-
nication (ISAC), movable antennas (MAs), pinching antennas (PAs).
I. INTRODUCTION
Future 6G systems need to achieve signiﬁcant performance
enhancements in order to simultaneously support envisioned
communication and sensing applications. For example, massive
MIMO is evolving towards extremely large-scale MIMO (XL-
MIMO) with an increase in the number of antennas to a few hun-
dreds or even thousands, achieving enormous spatial multiplexing
and beamforming gains. However, XL-MIMO implementation
with a large number of antennas entails high hardware complexity
and may incur high energy consumption. To alleviate these
limitations, several new antenna technologies have been proposed
instead of conventional antenna arrays for the next generation
integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) transceivers, such
as pinching antennas (PAs) and movable antennas (MAs).
PA technology is based on dielectric waveguides that can
emit electromagnetic waves when pinched by dielectric particles.
These particles are usually connected to plastic clips, form-
ing PAs. Therefore, adding and removing PAs along waveg-
uides that are typically several meters long, enables establishing
line-of-sight (LOS) links and consequently optimizing communi-
cation and sensing features of a pinching antenna system (PASS).
In [1], closed-form solutions for the optimal pinching antenna
location were derived under sensing-centric, communications-
centric, and Pareto-optimal designs. The sensing Cramér-Rao
lower bound (CRLB) for PASS was derived and later maximized

Recently, MAs have emerged as a new technology capable
of improving the performance of ISAC systems by allowing
local antenna movements. Unlike PAs, these antennas are moved
inside signiﬁcantly smaller regions, whose size is usually a
couple of wavelengths. Therefore, MAs are primarily intended
to deal with the channel fading by ﬁne-tuning their positions.
In [5], the CRLB for angle of arrival (AoA) estimation was
derived as a function of the MAs’ positions and later optimized.
An optimization framework for maximizing the sensing signal-
to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) in a multi-user bistatic
ISAC system, where MAs were deployed for adjusting the array
responses at both the transmit and the receive base stations (BSs),
was introduced in [6]. The maximization of the weighted sum
of the communication and the sensing rates in a full-duplex
ISAC communication system, where a single BS was equipped
with the transmit and the receive MAs was considered in [7].
Similarly, the authors in [8] studied the joint active beamforming
and MAs’ positions optimization for a full-duplex ISAC network
with multiple transmit and receive BSs that provided signiﬁcant
communication performance improvements, while maintaining
the guaranteed sensing requirements.
Despite all the previously mentioned works [1], [2], [6] and
[8], none of them considered systems that are simultaneously
equipped with PAs and MAs. To the best of the authors’ knowl-
edge, the only paper that studies commutation systems with both
PAs and MAs is [9], where a deep learning (DL) optimization
framework was proposed for the achievable rate optimization in a
point-to-point communication scenario. Against this background,
the contributions of this paper are listed as follows:
• We extend the DL framework developed in [9] to an ISAC
system with multiple communication users and a single
target. Within this framework, we formulate an optimization
problem with the aim to maximize the communication sum-
rate while maintaining the minimum sensing SINR.
• To solve this problem, we provide a closed-form solution
for the optimal sensing receive combiner and show that it
is determined by the values of other optimization variables.
Next, the optimal values of these variables (i.e., the positions
of the transmit PAs, the positions of the users’ MAs, the
communication precoding matrices, and the sensing transmit
beamformer) are obtained by the proposed DL network. To
train this network, we formulate a loss function that consists
of the objective function, as well as the penalty terms related
to the constraints for the PAs’ and MAs’ positions.

II. SYSTEM MODEL
We consider an ISAC system, in which the PA transmitter is
implemented by Nt parallel waveguides, where each waveguide
has exactly one PA. It transmits the ISAC signal, which simulta-
neously provides communication with K users and detection of a
sensing target. The reﬂected echo signal from the sensing target
is received by Nr ﬁxed antennas, which are spatially separated
from the PA transmitter. Every user possesses Nk MAs that can
be moved inside the region that is denoted as Ck.
To better illustrate the considered ISAC system, we introduce
3D coordinate system, as shown in Fig. 1. The transmit waveg-
uides are placed in the xy-plane, parallel to the y-axis. It is
assumed that all the waveguides have the same length of Dt
and are uniformly spaced over the interval [0, Dt]. Therefore,
the position of the PA that is allocated to the n-th waveguide is
given by tn = [xt,n, yt,n, 0]T , where xt,n = (n −1)Dt/(Nt −1)
and yt,n ∈[0, Dt]. The receive uniform linear array (ULA)

the second block to form the inputs of the third block. The

VI. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we studied the optimization of the sum-rate in an
ISAC system equipped with the transmit PAs and users’ MAs. We
derived the closed-form solution for the optimal sensing receive
combiner. In addition, we provided a DL network that optimize
the positions of the transmit PAs, the positions of the users’
MAs, the communication precoding matrices, and the sensing
transmit beamformer in the considered ISAC system. To train
this network, we formulated the loss function that consists of
the objective function, as well as the appropriate penalty terms
related to the constraints for the PAs’ and the MAs’ positions.
Simulation results demonstrated that the performance advantage
of the proposed scheme in comparison to a benchmark scheme
equipped with only ﬁxed antennas is gradually increased with
the maximum transmit power. Also, it was shown that the
communication features of the considered system were slightly
more affected by the change of the sensing SINR threshold than
the sensing ones.
