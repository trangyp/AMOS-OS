Distributionally Robust Safety Under Arbitrary Uncertainties:
A Safety Filtering Approach
Daniel M. Cherenson*, Haejoon Lee*, Taekyung Kim, and Dimitra Panagou
Abstract— In this work, we study how to ensure probabilistic
safety for nonlinear systems under distributional ambigu-
ity. Our approach builds on a backup-based safety filtering
framework that switches between a high-performance nominal
policy and a certified backup policy to ensure safety. To
handle arbitrary uncertainties from ambiguous distributions,
i.e., where the distribution is not of specific structure and the
true distribution is unknown, we adopt a distributionally robust
(DR) formulation using Wasserstein ambiguity sets. Rather than
solving a high-dimensional DR trajectory optimization problem
online, we exploit the structure of backup-based safety filtering
to reduce safety certification to a one-dimensional search over
the switching time between nominal and backup policies. We
then develop a sampling-based certification procedure with
finite-sample guarantees, where empirical failure probabilities
are compared against a Wasserstein-inflated threshold. We
validate our method through simulations across three systems,
from a Dubins vehicle to a high-speed racing car and a fighter
jet, demonstrating the broad applicability and computational
efficiency. [Project Page]a
I. INTRODUCTION
Ensuring the safety of autonomous robotic systems re-
mains a challenge in the presence of complex nonlinear robot
dynamics with actuation limits, operation under uncertainty
due to model mismatch, perception errors, and exogenous
disturbances. These uncertainties might be unknown or
poorly characterized, complicating the synthesis of a safe
controller that does not compromise mission performance.
Safety filters have enabled modular architectures, allow-
ing the use of high-performance, safety-agnostic nominal
policies and intervening only when a safety violation is
imminent [1]–[4]. In stochastic settings, robust safety fil-
ters [5], [6] provide guarantees under worst-case determinis-
tic bounds, while stochastic filters [7], [8] assume known
disturbance distributions. However, both approaches rely
on accurate or restrictive uncertainty models. Hence, their
guarantees may fail when the true distribution is unknown,
partially observed, or shifted.
Distributional shift or ambiguity is addressed by distribu-
tionally robust (DR) safety-critical planning and control [9],
[10]. These approaches account for worst-case uncertainty by
enforcing safety against an ambiguity set, that is, a family

Despite their benefits, integrating DR techniques directly

ambiguity sets. For instance, nonlinear DR-MPC frameworks
can require hundreds of seconds to solve per time step [19],
rendering them unsuitable for real-time safety filtering.
Several studies [17], [20]–[22] reformulate DR problems
into deterministic, computationally tractable optimization
problems. Nevertheless, these reformulations frequently re-

uncertainty structure (e.g., additive noises) [17], [22]. More
recently, [13] integrates DR methods with Control Barrier
Functions (CBFs) to construct safety filters under percep-
tion uncertainty. Nevertheless, this approach is restricted to
systems that are affine in both control input and noise.

for general dynamical systems under arbitrary uncertainty

• We introduce DRS-gatekeeper, a safety filter for
nonlinear systems with arbitrary disturbance structure

• We derive a finite-sample probabilistic safety certificate

upper bound on the worst-case failure probability with
a user-specified confidence level.
• We validate our method across three distinct domains
of increasing complexity: a Dubins vehicle, a Formula
1 racing car, and an F-16 fighter jet, demonstrating bet-
ter safety-performance trade-offs compared to baseline
methods and broad applicability.

C. Wasserstein Ambiguity Sets
The distribution Pt is meant to capture the aleatoric uncer-
tainty (inherent stochasticity) in the system and environment.
However, the true distribution Pt is often unknown. Thus, we
typically rely on a nominal distribution ˆPt constructed from
empirical data or simplified models to evaluate the chance
constraint (CC). This dependence on ˆPt introduces epistemic
uncertainty; a controller optimized solely against ˆPt may fail
if the actual distribution deviates from the nominal model.
Consequently, satisfying (CC) under the nominal model does
not guarantee safety in the real world.
To achieve robustness to such epistemic uncertainty, we
consider an ambiguity set centered around the nominal
distribution ˆPt that contains the true distribution P:

III. METHODOLOGY
A. DRS-gatekeeper
To address Problem 1, we develop a distributionally robust
backup-based safety filter. More specifically, the proposed
method extends gatekeeper [25] into the distribution-
ally robust stochastic gatekeeper (DRS-gatekeeper),
which combines a Wasserstein ambiguity model with a finite-
sample confidence analysis to determine when to switch
from nominal to backup policies with probabilistic safety
guarantees (see Theorem 1).
The original gatekeeper algorithm [25] provides an
efficient, policy-agnostic safety layer by rolling out a trajec-
tory to determine the latest possible time to switch from a
performance-oriented nominal policy to a certified backup
safety policy. The power of the gatekeeper lies in its
efficiency and minimal invasiveness: it intervenes only when
necessary to ensure safety. By prioritizing the nominal policy
until the latest possible “safe” moment, gatekeeper has
been shown to be more effective than other backup-based
safety filters (cf. [31]).
Despite these benefits, the vanilla gatekeeper is re-
stricted to deterministic systems or systems with a known
input-to-state stability property with respect to a bounded
disturbance. Thus, we introduce the Distributionally Ro-
bust Stochastic gatekeeper (DRS-gatekeeper). Our
method retains the simplicity and efficiency of the original
method while providing rigorous probabilistic safety guaran-
tees. Rather than using a single rollout, DRS-gatekeeper
rolls out in parallel a set of independent trajectories to en-
sure probabilistic safety against all disturbance distributions
within a Wasserstein ambiguity set containing the unknown
true distribution P.

As shown in Table III, the baseline method has a poor
success rate through the canyon because the offline optimal
trajectory was computed with a simplified dynamics model
and without wind. The observed 88% safety rate falls below
the prescribed threshold of 1 −ε = 0.95. This discrepancy
results from the probabilistic guarantee being defined per
iteration in Theorem 1 and hence, the cumulative proba-
bility of safety over the full simulation horizon decreases
as the individual success probabilities compound. Using
DRS-gatekeeper, we drastically improve the safety rate
with low backup intervention while keeping performance
comparable, i.e., slightly lower speed, slightly higher alti-
tude, and minimal computational burden.
Remark 5. The DR-CBF does not apply to the racecar or F-
16 models, as their uncertainty depends on the current state
and control input, violating the assumption of a fixed noise
distribution in the DR-CBF. Also, for the F-16, the high
relative degree and constrained input set make it challenging
to guarantee feasibility of the DR-CBF constraint. We note
that deterministic simplified control-affine dynamics models
without input constraints have been used to construct CBFs
for F-16 [37], but its use has been restricted to ground
collision avoidance and geofencing in large open areas.
