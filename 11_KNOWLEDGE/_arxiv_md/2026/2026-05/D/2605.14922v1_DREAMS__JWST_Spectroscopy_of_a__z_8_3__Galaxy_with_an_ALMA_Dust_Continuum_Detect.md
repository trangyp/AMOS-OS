ABSTRACT
We present a deep DREAMS JWST/NIRSpec MSA medium-grating spectrum of MACS0416-Y1, a
galaxy at z = 8.312 with the highest-redshift ALMA dust continuum detection to date, in order to char-
acterize its properties together with archival IFU and ALMA data. The deep NIRSpec spectrum reveals
a broad Hβ line with a width of ∼1100 km s−1. We interpret it as a broad-line AGN whose line diag-
nostics are consistent with AGN activity across its clumpy structure, given the absence of little red dot
signatures. MACS0416-Y1 clearly shows [Oiii] 4363 emission, suggesting a moderately low metallicity
of 12+log(O/H) = 7.86+0.09
−0.08 (0.15 Z⊙). The combination of [Cii] 158µm and dust continuum emission
indicates low dust mass ratios of log(Mdust/Mgas) = −3.60+0.29
−0.22 and log(Mdust/Mmetal) = −0.95+0.29
−0.20.
Because the metallicity of MACS0416-Y1 is around the critical metallicity of 0.1–0.2 Z⊙, the system is
expected to undergo dust growth, explaining these low dust mass ratios as well as its small dust mass,
Mdust ∼106 M⊙. The intense UV radiation from the AGN may contribute to a high dust temperature
of Tdust ≃91+62
−35 K, boosting the dust-continuum emission above the ALMA detection limit despite
the small Mdust at z > 8. We find a very high total flux ratio of [Oiii] 88µm/[Oiii] 5007 = 0.26 ± 0.06
in MACS0416-Y1, above predictions from single ionized nebular models at any electron density. This
discrepancy suggests that the [Oiii] 88µm and [Oiii] 5007 trace largely distinct regions, with the optical
line suppressed in dusty nebulae, and thus requires careful interpretation when combining optical and
infrared emission lines in JWST+ALMA studies.

1. INTRODUCTION
Dust is a fundamental component of the interstellar
medium (ISM) in galaxies, influencing the observational
properties, energy budget, and physical conditions. In
the local Universe, dust heated by interstellar radia-

tion fields typically reaches equilibrium temperatures of
∼20–40 K (Dunne et al. 2000; Casey 2012). However,
recent far-infrared (FIR) and submillimeter observations
have revealed that dust in high-redshift galaxies, partic-
ularly at z > 6, exhibits unexpectedly high temperatures
of 50–100 K, likely due to intense radiation fields asso-
ciated with compact star formation and/or conditions
that enhance dust heating, including low dust mass or
active galactic nucleus (AGN) activity (Nakazato et al.
2026; Viero et al. 2022; Sommovigo et al. 2022; Mit-
suhashi et al. 2024; Tsukui et al. 2023).
Understanding the origin of these high dust tem-
peratures and the physical conditions of the ISM re-
quires combining dust continuum measurements with
constraints on the gas properties. A multi-wavelength
approach that combines FIR/submillimeter continuum
observations with near-infrared spectroscopy is there-
fore essential for characterizing dust and ISM properties
in early galaxies. FIR and submillimeter observations
probe the emission from dust and FIR fine-structure
emission lines, while near-infrared spectroscopy provides
emission line diagnostics that trace gas properties in-
cluding electron temperature, density, ionization state,
and chemical composition (Osterbrock & Ferland 2006;
Draine 2011).
The combination of these datasets en-
ables a more comprehensive understanding of the phys-
ical conditions in high-z galaxies.
For example, mea-
surements of the gas-phase metallicity can provide con-
straints on grain growth processes in the early uni-
verse (e.g., Asano et al. 2013; Popping et al. 2017; Toy-
ouchi et al. 2025).
MACS J0416-Y1 (hereafter Y1) is a star-forming
galaxy at z = 8.31 that exhibits an elevated dust tem-
perature and a complex ISM structure characterized by
three emission peaks in the rest-frame UV and two emis-
sion peaks in the rest-frame FIR continuum.
It was
originally identified as a z > 8 Lyman break galaxy can-
didate based on multiband Hubble Space Telescope Wide
Field Camera 3 (HST/WFC3) observations (Infante et
al. 2015; Laporte et al. 2015) in the Hubble Frontier
Fields (HFF, Lotz et al. 2017) survey of the MACS J-
0416.1-2403 cluster. Follow up observations with the At-
acama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA)
spectroscopically confirmed its redshift, z
= 8.312,
through detections of the [Oiii] 88µm (Tamura et al.
2019) and [Cii] 158µm (Bakx et al. 2020) emission lines.
Y1 exhibits a high [Oiii] 88µm/[Cii] 158µm luminosity
ratio of ∼9 (Bakx et al. 2020). Hagimoto et al. (2025)
suggested that this can be explained by a porous ISM
geometry with a low neutral-gas covering fraction. Dust
continuum emission was first reported in ALMA Band
7 (Tamura et al. 2019), representing the highest-redshift
dust continuum detection to date.
Dust continuum
emission has subsequently been detected across multi-
ple ALMA bands (Tamura et al. 2019; Harshan et al.
2024; Bakx et al. 2025). By combining these detections
with upper limits from nondetections (Bakx et al. 2020;
Jones et al. 2024; Bakx et al. 2025), the dust tempera-
ture has been constrained to 91+62
−35 K (Bakx et al. 2025),
significantly warmer than typical values observed in lo-
cal galaxies (∼25 K; e.g., Schreiber et al. 2018). The
origin of the high dust temperature and the detection
of dust continuum at such a high redshift remain un-
der debate.
Tamura et al. (2019) suggested that the
observed dust mass requires an earlier episode of star
formation to pre-enrich the system, rather than produc-
tion in a single young burst.
Bakx et al. (2020) ar-
gue that the high inferred dust temperature itself re-
duces the required dust mass, alleviating the tension
with rapid dust production. More recently, observations
with the James Webb Space Telescope/Near Infrared
Spectrograph (JWST/NIRSpec) and JWST/Near In-
frared Camera (NIRCam) have revealed the clumpy
morphology, ionized gas properties, and stellar popula-
tions (e.g., Ma et al. 2024; Harshan et al. 2024). Harshan
et al. (2024) proposed that merger-driven star formation
and/or a possible AGN contribution inferred from emis-
sion line diagnostics could account for the strong dust
heating.
Despite these advances, several key questions remain
unresolved, including the origin of the dust heating,
the mechanism responsible for the dust enrichment,
and the connection between the rest-frame UV/optical-
emitting regions observed with JWST and the rest-
frame FIR-emitting regions observed with ALMA. Ad-
dressing these questions requires a multiwavelength
analysis of the ionized gas, cold gas, and dust compo-
nents using both JWST and ALMA observations. In
this study, we present new deep JWST/NIRSpec Micro-
Shutter Assembly (MSA) spectroscopy of Y1, combined
with archival NIRSpec Integral Field Unit (IFU) and
ALMA data, to investigate its ISM properties and the
mechanism of dust production and heating. The remain-
der of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 de-
scribes the observations and datasets used in this study.
Section 3 outlines the data reduction and analysis meth-
ods. In Section 4, we present the main results, including
emission-line properties and ISM diagnostics.
Section
5 discusses the implications for dust heating, produc-
tion, and ISM structure. Finally, Section 6 summarizes
our conclusions. Throughout this paper, we assume a
flat ΛCDM cosmology with H0 = 67.66 km s−1 Mpc−1,
Ωm = 0.3111, and ΩΛ = 0.6889 (Planck Collaboration
et al. 2020).

3. DATA REDUCTION AND ANALYSIS
3.1. Data Reduction
3.1.1. JWST/NIRSpec MSA
We reduced the data using the JWST Science Calibra-
tion Pipeline (version 1.17.1) with the Calibration Refer-
ence Data System (CRDS) context file jwst 1298.pmap.
A detailed description of the reduction procedure will be
presented in Nakajima et al. (in preparation; see also
Nakajima et al. 2025).
3.1.2. JWST/NIRSpec IFU
We reduced the raw data retrieved from the Mikulski
Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST) portal using the
JWST Science Calibration Pipeline with the CRDS con-
text file jwst 1364.pmap. We follow the NIRSpec/IFU
Data Reduction Pipeline developed by the JWST Early
Release Science (ERS) Targeting Extremely Magnified
Panchromatic Lensed Arcs and their Extended Star for-
mation (TEMPLATES; Rigby et al. 2025) team through
stage 1 and stage 2, and replace stage 3 with custom
processing using the reproject and reproject interp
packages. In stage 1, calwebb detector1 is applied to
perform detector-level corrections for each exposure, fol-
lowed by 1/f noise removal using the NSClean package.
In stage 2, calwebb spec2 is used for WCS assignment,
wavelength and flux calibration, flat-fielding, and pass-
loss correction. The calibrated data are then combined
using drizzle weighting to achieve a spaxel scale of 0.′′05.
Finally, we construct the median-stacked 3D data cube
using the reproject and reproject interp packages,
with the background modeled and subtracted as a poly-
nomial function of wavelength.
3.1.3. JWST/NIRCam Imaging
JWST/NIRCam raw imaging data were retrieved
from MAST archive and reduced using the JWST Cali-
bration Pipeline (version 1.12.5) and the CRDS context
file jwst 1193.pmap, with custom modifications follow-
ing Harikane et al. (2023a). The final images are drizzled
to a pixel scale of 0.′′015.

We calibrated the astrometry of the JWST NIRCam
and JWST NIRSpec/IFU data using an HST image
aligned to the Gaia DR3 (Gaia Collaboration et al.
2021) as the astrometric reference. For the HST image,

crease the number of Gaia sources for astrometric align-
ment, we utilized the HST F125W image to transfer
the astrometry from Gaia to JWST. The HST image
has a wider field of view and contains fewer saturated
sources than the JWST images. The HST astrometry
was first aligned to Gaia DR3 catalog coordinates us-
ing 4 Gaia sources within the field of view. The NIR-
Cam images were then aligned to the HST image using
4 stars detected in both datasets.
We constructed a
pseudo F444W image of Y1 from NIRSpec/IFU data
and aligned it to the NIRCam F444W image. After as-
trometric calibration, the mean positional offset among
the matched reference sources is 10 mas.
3.3. Line Flux and Profile Measurements
We fit the emission lines in the NIRSpec/MSA
and IFU spectra using a Markov Chain Monte Carlo
(MCMC) method with emcee (Foreman-Mackey et al.
2013). Flux uncertainties correspond to the 16th and
84th percentiles of the posterior distributions. The flux-
density uncertainties were estimated as 1σ of the flux
density in nearby line-free spectral windows within the
same extraction region. The emission-line FWHMs were
constrained to be larger than the FWHM of the instru-
mental line spread function (LSF) measured from plan-
etary nebula spectra in Isobe et al. (2023). The intrin-
sic emission-line velocity widths presented in this paper
were derived by deconvolving a Gaussian profile whose
FWHM matches that of the LSF measured by Isobe et
al. (2023).

4.2.1. Broad Hβ Emission
We perform multi-component Gaussian fitting to
search for broad components in the Hβ line.

A broad compo-
nent is added when the Akaike Information Crite-
rion (AIC; Akaike 1974) decreases by more than 10,
∆AIC=AICdouble−AICsingle < −10, following the sta-
tistical model-selection criterion of Burnham & Ander-
son (2004). Figure 6(a) shows the double-Gaussian fit
to the Hβ line for the spaxel corresponding to the re-
gion indicated by the red box in Figure 7, which is
the only spaxel satisfying the criterion of ∆AIC< −10.
The velocity FWHM of the broad Hβ component is
1100+800
−500 km s−1, exceeding the typical threshold used
to identify AGN, 1000 km s−1 (e.g., Harikane et al.
2023b; Matthee et al. 2024; Kiyota et al. 2025). Fig-
ure 6(b) shows the double component fit of [Oiii] 5007
(∆AIC = −7) for the same spaxel. The velocity FWHM
of the broad [Oiii] 5007 component is 420+120
−100 km s−1,
narrower than that of Hβ at the ∼1σ level.
Broad
[Oiii] 5007 emission is commonly associated with out-
flows, whereas the line is expected to be suppressed in
the high-density broad-line region (ne = 109–11 cm−3;
Osterbrock & Mathews 1986) because its critical den-
sity (ncrit = 6.8 × 105 cm−3; Osterbrock & Ferland
2006) is far below typical broad-line region densities.
If the broad Hβ emission originated from outflowing
gas, a comparable velocity width would be expected in
[Oiii] 5007. The observed difference in line width there-
fore argues against an outflow origin for the broad Hβ
component.
In addition, Xu et al. (2025) found that
galaxies without AGN typically exhibit outflow veloci-
ties of at most ∼500 km s−1, further arguing against
an origin of the broad Hβ component solely from out-
flows. Instead, this suggests that the broad Hβ line is
emitted from a compact and dense broad-line region in
the vicinity of an AGN. The observed Hβ broad-line
flux is fHβ,broad = 1.43+0.62
−0.60 × 10−19 erg s−1 cm−2. Af-
ter correcting for lensing magnification, the black hole
mass is estimated to be MBH = 1.6+3.0
−1.0 × 106 M⊙using
the relation in Greene & Ho (2005). Given the stellar
mass of M⋆∼109M⊙(Harshan et al. 2024), the in-
ferred black hole mass of MBH ∼106M⊙corresponds
to MBH/M⋆∼10−3, consistent with the MBH–M⋆scal-

5. DISCUSSION
5.1. Sources of Dust Heating
Y1 has been observed with ALMA Bands 3–9, with
continuum emission detection in Bands 5–9 (Tamura et
al. 2019, 2023; Bakx et al. 2020, 2025; Jones et al. 2024;
Harshan et al. 2024). Bakx et al. (2025) report dust tem-
perature and dust mass of 91+62
−35 K and 1.4+1.3
−0.5×106 M⊙,
respectively, based on SED fitting of these observations.
This temperature is higher than typical values inferred
for galaxies at z ≳7 (40–60 K; Sommovigo et al. 2022;
Mitsuhashi et al. 2024).
The origin of such elevated dust temperatures remains
under debate. A redshift evolution of dust temperature
has been reported, with Tdust increasing toward higher
redshift (B´ethermin et al. 2015; Sommovigo et al. 2022;
Mitsuhashi et al. 2024). Extrapolation of empirical re-
lations derived at z ≲7 predicts dust temperatures of
∼40–60 K at z ∼8.3.
This trend has been interpreted
as a consequence of the lower metallicity and dust mass
at high-z, which reduce dust shielding and enable more
efficient absorption of UV radiation (e.g., Sommovigo et
al. 2022). These effects are expected to be relevant for
Y1, which exhibits both low metallicity and low dust
mass.
Theoretical studies have shown that high dust
temperatures can be achieved in compact star-forming

Next, we consider the dust production mechanisms.
Dust production mechanisms are broadly categorized
into stellar sources (e.g., supernovae and AGB stars)
and grain growth in the ISM through accretion of gas-
phase metals onto pre-existing dust grains (Dwek 1998;

that stellar sources can dominate dust production in
low-metallicity galaxies, while grain growth in the ISM
becomes dominant as galaxies evolve and enrich met-

Our findings and comparison with other high-z galax-

observed optical and FIR emission to be dominated by
different regions within the galaxy. As a result, optical
and FIR lines do not necessarily trace the same ion-

optical line diagnostics and diagnostics combining opti-
cal and FIR lines. This interpretation is qualitatively
consistent with studies suggesting that FIR-emitting re-
gions are more compact than UV-emitting regions in
high-redshift galaxies (e.g., Fujimoto et al. 2017; Ru-
jopakarn et al. 2016; Boogaard et al. 2024; Kiyota et
al. 2026).

global ISM properties under single-zone assumptions.
6. SUMMARY
We have presented a comprehensive multi-wavelength
analysis of MACS0416-Y1, a galaxy at z = 8.312 with
the highest-redshift ALMA dust continuum detection to
date. By combining deep JWST/NIRSpec MSA spec-
troscopy with archival JWST/NIRSpec IFU and ALMA
data, we investigate the AGN activity, dust properties,
and ISM conditions of Y1. Our main findings are sum-
marized as follows.
1. The deep NIRSpec spectrum reveals a broad
Hβ component with a velocity width of ∼1100
km s−1, indicating the presence of a broad-line
AGN. In addition, all clumps lie in the AGN
regime of the MEx diagram, consistent with high
ionization conditions across the clumps, poten-
tially associated with AGN activity. We also per-
form a Little Red Dot test using the NIRCam
photometry and find that none of the individual
clumps or the integrated photometry of Y1 satisfy
the LRD selection criteria.
2. Using the direct method, we derive a gas-phase
metallicity of 12 + log(O/H)
=
7.86+0.09
−0.08 (∼
0.15 Z⊙). Strong-line metallicity estimate based
on the R3 index is consistent with this result. The
metallicity places Y1 near the critical metallicity
(∼0.1–0.2 Z⊙), where dust grain growth in the
ISM is becoming efficient.
3. Combining the metallicity,
ALMA dust con-
tinuum measurements,
and the gas mass in-
ferred from [Cii],
we derive low dust mass
ratios of log(Mdust/Mgas)
=
−3.60+0.29
−0.22
and
log(Mdust/Mmetal) = −0.95+0.29
−0.20. These values in-
dicate that Y1 is relatively dust-poor despite its
dust continuum detection, consistent with an early
stage of dust enrichment where grain growth is be-
coming efficient.
4. The high dust temperature of Tdust ≃91+62
−35 K
may be driven by multiple factors, including low
metallicity and dust mass, compact star forma-
tion, and AGN heating.
Previous studies have
shown that AGN can produce elevated dust tem-
perature.
The spatial coincidence between the
broad line emission and one of the dust contin-
uum peaks is consistent with such AGN-driven
dust heating in Y1, suggesting that AGN may con-
tribute to the high dust temperature in Y1.
5. We find a high integrated line ratio of [Oiii]
88µm/[Oiii] 5007 = 0.26 ± 0.06, which cannot be
reproduced by a single homogeneous ionized neb-
ula model for any electron density.
Explaining
this ratio requires dust attenuation, whereas the
Balmer decrement indicates little or no attenua-
tion in the observed optical emission. We propose
that dust attenuation causes the observed opti-
cal and FIR lines to preferentially trace different
regions within the galaxy, with observed optical
emission dominated by low-extinction regions and
FIR emission tracing more obscured regions. This
implies that joint analysis of JWST optical spec-
troscopy and ALMA FIR line observations should
be treated with caution, particularly when assum-
ing single-zone ISM.
