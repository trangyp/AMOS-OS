I. INTRODUCTION AND PROBLEM STATEMENT
A. Motivation and Problem Setting
The Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) [1] is one of the most
widely used numerical primitives in scientific computing,
with O(N log N) complexity dominating the computational
cost in applications such as radar, medical imaging, channel
estimation, and compressed sensing. In many such settings,
however, the underlying signal is sparse in the frequency
domain, with only k ≪N significant Fourier coefficients [2].
Sparse Fast Fourier Transform (sFFT) algorithms [3] exploit
this structure to achieve sublinear-in-N runtimes, but existing
approaches face fundamental tradeoffs between efficiency,
determinism, and generality.
B. Core Bottleneck
Despite significant progress, deterministic sparse FFT meth-
ods remain limited by two structural bottlenecks:
• an O(
√
N log N) preprocessing cost arising from multi-
view decimation, or
• an O(k2) candidate explosion resulting from unfiltered
Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT) reconstruction.
These bottlenecks prevent deterministic methods from
achieving both sublinear runtime and broad applicability si-
multaneously.
C. Prior Work
Probabilistic sFFT algorithms (Hassanieh et al. [4], [5]
and subsequent refinements) achieve near-optimal O(k log N)
complexity using randomized subsampling, hashing, and phase
shifts, but provide only probabilistic guarantees.
CRT-based
approaches
such
as
FFAST
[6]
achieve
O(k log k) complexity with sample-optimal subsampling, but
require structural constraints on the signal length (e.g., N =
k · 2t), limiting applicability.
Deterministic approaches based on structured modular con-
gruences (Iwen [7]; Plonka-Wannenwetsch et al.) eliminate
randomization but typically incur either an O(
√
N log N)
preprocessing cost or an O(k2) candidate enumeration.
A parallel signal-processing line addresses error-tolerant
CRT reconstruction under noisy or erroneous remainders:
Wang and Xia [8] established closed-form reconstruction
methods with bounded error for shared-factor moduli, and
Xiao, Xia, and Wang [9] extended these results to multidi-
mensional settings with matrix-valued moduli.
To our knowledge, prior work has not integrated multi-
view gating with deterministic candidate reduction from O(k2)
to O(k) within the CRT framework, while simultaneously
eliminating the preprocessing floor.
D. Our Approach (High-Level)
We present a sparse FFT framework that addresses both
limitations simultaneously by combining:
• multi-view decimation under coprime moduli,
• a keyed 2-of-3 gating mechanism that filters candidate
frequencies,
• iterative peeling validation operating entirely on deci-
mated views, and
• a self-reduction lemma enabling recursive bootstrapping.
The identification pipeline is deterministic given fixed pa-
rameters, while probabilistic guarantees arise only from as-
sumptions on frequency placement and hashing-based inde-
pendence.
Contributions
1) Keyed multi-view gating (core contribution): A 2-of-3
CRT agreement mechanism that reduces candidate pairs
from O(k2) to Θ(k) without randomized bucketization.
2) Peeling-only realization (implementation): A recov-
ery procedure that extracts residue pairs directly from
singleton bins, avoiding explicit pair enumeration.

II. TECHNICAL OVERVIEW
This section provides an intuitive explanation of our algo-
rithm before the formal treatment in later sections. The al-
gorithm is deterministic in execution; probabilistic guarantees
arise from assumptions on frequency placement and hashing-
based independence (Assumption IV.1). The core contribution
of this work is the keyed multi-view gating mechanism, which
determines the candidate set; all subsequent steps (peeling,
recursion, and verification) operate on this reduced set.
A. The Core Challenge: Pair Explosion in Multi-View CRT
Multi-view sparse FFT algorithms work by computing
multiple ”views” of the signal; each view is a decimated
(subsampled) FFT of length
√
N obtained by selecting every
mi-th time sample, where mi ≈
√
N are coprime moduli.
Each true frequency f hashes to a residue ri = f mod mi in
view i. The challenge is that multiple frequencies can hash
to the same residue (collisions), creating bins with multiple
occupants.
In a 2-view system with moduli m1 and m2, we observe
residues R1 and R2 containing the true frequencies’ projec-
tions. The Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT) tells us that
each pair (r1, r2) with r1 ∈R1 and r2 ∈R2 reconstructs to
a unique candidate frequency f ∈[0, N). However, when k
frequencies collide across two views with coverage factor α
(average bin occupancy), we generate |R1|×|R2| ≈(αk)2 =
O(k2) candidate pairs. For realistic sparsity levels (k ≥50)
and coverage factors (α ≈10 to 15), this produces hundreds of
thousands of candidates when only k = 50 are true, a 99.99%
false-positive rate matching the intro estimate.
B. Limitations of 2-View CRT
The root problem is that the two views provide insufficient
constraints. Each residue pair (r1, r2) satisfies the modular
equations f ≡r1 (mod m1) and f ≡r2 (mod m2), but
these two equations alone cannot distinguish true frequencies
from false combinations. Validating all O(k2) candidates by
checking their coefficients in the original signal would require
O(k2N) work, worse than the dense FFT.
Prior approaches attempted to mitigate this through:
• Multiple rounds with different moduli: Iteratively refine
candidates, but still fundamentally limited by O(k2)
scaling per round.
• Larger moduli: Using mi ≫
√
N reduces collisions
but increases view size, losing the O(
√
N) preprocessing
advantage.
• Probabilistic filtering: Random pruning of candidates,
but requires multiple trials with no deterministic guar-
antees.
This motivates the question: can sparsity be exploited to
achieve sub-linear complexity under a deterministic guarantee?

C. Keyed Gating with Third View: O(k2) →O(k) Reduction
Our solution introduces a third independent view with
modulus m3 and uses it as a filter (“gate”) rather than as
a third reconstruction dimension. The key insight is the 2-
of-3 agreement principle: for a true frequency f, all three
residues (r1, r2, r3) must be consistent with f under CRT.
For a false pair (r1, r2) that reconstructs to some f ′ ̸= f, the
probability that f ′ mod m3 happens to equal any occupied
residue r3 ∈R3 is approximately |R3|/m3 ≈αk/

D. Iterative Peeling for O(
√
N log k) Validation
After keyed gating reduces candidates to O(k), we must
still validate which candidates are true. Directly checking each
candidate against the time-domain signal would cost O(kN),
which is unacceptable. Instead, we perform validation entirely
on the decimated views through iterative peeling, a process
analogous to successive interference cancellation in coding
theory.
The peeling algorithm operates as follows:
1) Precompute S ∈{2, 3} time-shifted decimated FFTs per
view (achieved via phase rotation).
2) Detect singleton bins (residues with exactly one fre-
quency) using coefficient-of-variation (CV) tests across
the shifted FFTs. A bin with one frequency shows
consistent phase relationships across shifts, while multi-
frequency bins exhibit phase interference.
3) For each detected singleton, peel it: subtract its contri-
bution from all bins across all views using the recon-
structed frequency and coefficient.
4) Repeat for O(log k) rounds until all k frequencies are
extracted or a residual ”2-core” remains (handled by
fallback).
Each bin update during peeling requires O(1) arithmetic
operations. With O(
√
N) bins per view and O(log k) rounds,
the total peeling cost is O(
√
N log k). Under load factor
λ = k/
√
N < 1 and independence assumptions from affine
hashing, each round extracts a constant fraction of remaining
frequencies with high probability.

B. Open Problems and Future Directions
Theoretical Extensions:
1) Worst-Case Analysis: Current results assume random
or well-separated support. Can we prove O(k) gating
bound for adversarial frequency placement?
2) Off-Grid
Extension: Extend keyed gating to off-
grid frequencies with spectral leakage. Requires sta-

model.
3) Noisy Signal Analysis: Develop rigorous finite-precision
bounds for CV and phase linearity tests.
4) Multi-Dimensional
FFT: Extend 2-of-3 gating to
2D/3D sparse FFT for image/volume processing. Re-
quires tensor product CRT analysis.
5) Adaptive Gating: Optimize gating threshold dynami-
cally based on measured collision rates.
Algorithmic Improvements:
1) Hierarchical Gating: Use nested gating with 4+ views
for extremely high k.
2) Hybrid Approach: Combine
√
N-sized FFTs with k-
sized iterative refinement for adaptive algorithm.

We presented a deterministic sparse FFT framework that
achieves expected runtime O(

N log k) for k-sparse signals
in the regime k = o(

N), with O(N log N) deterministic
worst-case via dense-FFT fallback. This work establishes
O(
√
N log k) expected-time sparse FFT via four contributions:
(1) keyed gating proves O(k) candidate reduction from the
O(k2) pair explosion, (2) peeling-only mode (Section VI-B)
eliminates the O(k2) gating enumeration by extracting sin-

FFT preprocessing from O(

√

and bin-wise residual test, achieving correctness with prob-

runtime is O(

This work establishes keyed multi-view gating as a new
candidate selection paradigm for sparse FFT, separating struc-
tural identification from implementation efficiency. The frame-
work enables theoretical clarity and practical robustness, with
peeling, recursion, and verification operating on a provably
reduced candidate set.
