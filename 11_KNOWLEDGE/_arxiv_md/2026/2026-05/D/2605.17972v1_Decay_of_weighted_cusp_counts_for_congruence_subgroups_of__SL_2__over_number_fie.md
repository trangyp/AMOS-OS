SHENGYUAN ZHAO
Abstract. For congruence subgroups commensurable with SL2
over number fields, we study cusp counts with certain multiplic-
ities. We prove that the ratio of the total weighted cusp count
to the group index is bounded by a negative power of the norm
of the congruence level. This generalizes a theorem of Cox–Parry
over Q, and supports the heuristic that cusp terms occurring in
topological, arithmetic and representation-theoretical formulas are
subleading.
The proof proceeds by localizing at a prime and reducing the
problem to finite quotients, where it becomes a counting problem
for finite groups. The main technical part is a counting problem
for subgroups of SL2 over finite non-reduced principal local rings,
proved by an analysis reminiscent of additive combinatorics.

1. Introduction
We study non-cocompact irreducible arithmetic lattices in Lie groups
of the form PSL2(R)r × PSL2(C)s, acting on a product of two or three
dimensional real hyperbolic spaces (H2)r × (H3)s.
This amounts to
considering arithmetic subgroups of SL2(K) where K is a number field.
Serre’s theorem [25] asserts that all such arithmetic groups are con-
gruence when K is neither Q nor an imaginary quadratic field. We
are interested in the asymptotic number of cusps when the levels of the
congruence subgroups grow. For K = Q this was studied in the work of
Cox–Parry [13] where Rademacher’s conjecture on genera of congruence
covers of the modular curve was proved. In this paper we generalize
Cox–Parry’s result to arbitrary number fields. We also add one layer
of complexity: to each cusp σ we associate a multiplicity aσ ∈N∗(see
§4.4 for definition), and we count the sum of the multiplicities. This
sum is in general a higher order quantity than the bare number of cusps
because multiplicities may grow when the congruence level grows (see
§4.6 for examples). For K = Q all multiplicities are one, while for a
real quadratic K it is the multiplicity that Hirzebruch [18] introduced
in his study of resolution of singularities of Hilbert modular surfaces.
Our main result is the following:

from the identity have few fixed points. For elements close to the iden-
tity, the first non-zero term in the congruence filtration controls the
fixed set; a large fixed set can occur only when this first term is nilpo-
tent. The problem is therefore to understand how nilpotent directions
can persist, or fail to persist, through the congruence filtration.
The technical bulk of the paper is Section 3. One fixes a nilpotent
direction and a first coefficient, and then counts the possible slope pa-
rameters. If too many slopes occur, then many pairs of slopes have con-
trolled differences. The corresponding four-point configurations (which
we call parallelograms) are then inserted into explicit matrix identities.
These parallelograms force new infinitesimal directions at deeper con-
gruence levels. This is the mechanism which prevents the fixed-point
contribution from being too large. This use of differences and parallel-
ograms is inspired by the energy argument in additive combinatorics,
see [27, § 2.3]. The present argument, however, takes place inside the
congruence filtration of a finite principal local ring, and the output is
a propagation statement across levels rather than a growth statement
inside a single abelian group. A useful way to summarize the proof
is that all sources of asymptotic decay are eventually compressed into
four numerical quantities. The final fixed-point estimate has, up to
constants depending only on [K : Q], the schematic form
pȷ1+ȷ2+bc+|Π|.
The notations are introduced later; in this overview it is enough to re-
gard these four terms as measuring four possible obstructions to having
many cusp orbits. Two of them measure how late the relevant nilpo-
tent directions appear, one measures a transverse codimension, and one
counts levels at which a nilpotent direction is absent. The main point
is that exact level e forces at least one of these four quantities to be
comparable to e. This alternative is what turns the local analysis into
a uniform exponential decay in the level.
