Introduction
Divisibility properties play an important role in the regularity theory of C∗-algebras. One
of the key features of well-behaved C∗-algebras is that they possess nice divisibility prop-
erties. One source of the modern study of divisibility in C∗-algebras can be traced back
to Rørdam’s work on the structure of UHF-stable C∗-algebras ([34, 35]). Subsequently,
Rørdam’s ideas led to the concept of approximately divisible, developed by Blackadar-
Kumjian-Rørdam in [5]. In 2007, Lin proposed a tracial version of approximate divisibility
[28, Definition 5.3]. In 2013, Hirshberg and Orovitz [26] introduced tracial Z-absorbing
and, using techniques of Matui and Sato, showed that for simple, separable, nuclear, uni-
tal C∗-algebras, Z-stability is equivalent to tracial Z-absorbing — a central divisibility
property. Since then, various notions of tracial approximate divisibility have been intro-
duced by several authors, including Fu–Lin [22, 23], Fu–Li–Lin [21], Castillejos–Li–Szab´o
[12], and Amini–Golestani–Jamali–Phillips [1].
The divisibility properties discussed above share a common theme: each permits, in
one way or another, the tracially large completely positive contractive (c.p.c.) order zero
embedding of matrices of arbitrarily large rank into suitable central sequence algebras.

From this viewpoint, a weaker form of divisibility can be obtained by relaxing the cen-
trality requirement, allowing such embeddings into suitable sequence algebras without
insisting that the image of the embeddings be central. Among them are Winter’s al-
most divisibility and tracial almost divisibility [40], introduced to show that finite nuclear
dimension implies Z-stability; Property (TM), introduced by the author together with
H. Lin [24, Definition 8.1] for the study of stable rank one, tracial oscillation, and the
surjectivity of Γ. See also Robert–Rørdam ([33]) for more on divisibility properties. These
divisibility properties are all closely related to another important regularity property: the

N. P. Brown first raised the question of when Γ is surjective (see the remark after
Question 1.1 of [36]). The surjectivity of Γ plays a key role in the computation of the Cuntz
semigroups of C∗-algebras. Brown–Dadarlat–Toms showed that Γ is surjective for simple
unital exact finite Z-stable C∗-algebras [8, 5.5]. Elliott–Santiago–Robert generalized this
to simple finite Z-stable C∗-algebras [18, 6.8]. H. Thiel showed that Γ is surjective for
simple separable unital C∗-algebras of stable rank one ([36, Theorem 8.11]), without
using strict comparison. Antoine–Perera–Robert–Thiel removed the unital assumption in
Thiel’s result ([2, Theorem 7.13]).
In order to study the relations between stable rank one and other regularity proper-
ties, we introduced the notion of tracial approximate oscillation zero in [24] (joint work
with Lin) and showed that when strict comparison is provided, stable rank one, tracial
approximate oscillation zero, and Property (TM) are equivalent.
In another direction, L. G. Brown and G. K. Pedersen introduced the concept of
real rank zero [7], which is of fundamental importance. S. Zhang’s work [42] shows that
real rank zero implies tracial almost divisibility for simple C∗-algebras. The converse
of Zhang’s theorem does not hold in general, due to the possible lack of projections in
simple C∗-algebras. Nevertheless, the tracial sequence algebra l∞(A)/JA could be rich in
projections, where JA is the trace kernel ideal. Indeed, our recent work [20] shows that
if we additionally assume that A has stable rank one, then l∞(A)/JA has real rank zero.
One of our main results in this paper is that l∞(A)/JA has real rank zero if and only if A
is tracially almost divisible (see Theorem 1.1).
In [20], we introduced hereditary surjectivity and showed that for simple separable
C*-algebras, stable rank one implies hereditary surjectivity of Γ, which in turn implies
tracial approximate oscillation zero. In this paper, we continue the study of the relation
between tracial approximate oscillation zero and certain properties of the canonical map
Γ. One of our main results is that for exact C∗-algebras, tracial approximate oscillation
zero is equivalent to Γ being hereditary dense, which is a concept that blends uniform
denseness with some flavor of strict comparison, even though strict comparison itself is
absent (see Definition 4.3 and Theorem 1.1).
The last pieces to complete the puzzle are two divisibility properties: tracial diagonal
divisibility (see Definition 7.1), meaning that every positive element is close to an evenly
divided positive element with respect to the 2-norm; and weak tracial diagonal divisibility
(see Definition 7.3), meaning that every positive element is close to an evenly divided
positive element at the trace level.
With the tools of tracial approximate oscillation zero, hereditary denseness, tracial
diagonal divisibility, and weak tracial diagonal divisibility, we can now unify various
existing regularity properties in the following theorem:
