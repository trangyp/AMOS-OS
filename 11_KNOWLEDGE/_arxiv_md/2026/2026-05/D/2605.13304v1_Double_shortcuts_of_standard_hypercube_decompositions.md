DOUBLE SHORTCUTS OF STANDARD HYPERCUBE
DECOMPOSITIONS
MARGHERITA ZANNONI
Abstract. In this paper, we study the double shortcuts associated with pairs of standard
hypercube decompositions of arbitrary Bruhat intervals in the symmetric group. Our results
imply that a conjecture stated in [Bull. London Math. Soc., 57 (2025), no. 8] holds for
the class of standard hypercube decompositions.
If this conjecture were to hold for all
hypercube decompositions, then the Combinatorial Invariance Conjecture for Kazhdan–
Lusztig polynomials would follow.
1. Introduction
The Combinatorial Invariance Conjecture, independently formulated by Lusztig and Dyer
in the 1980s, is one of the most intriguing open problems in the theory of Kazhdan-Lusztig
polynomials. This conjecture asserts that Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials depend only on the
isomorphism type of the Bruhat interval as a poset.
Conjecture 1.1 (Combinatorial Invariance). Let W, W ′ be Coxeter groups, u, v ∈W, u′, v′ ∈
W ′. If [u, v] ∼= [u′, v′] as posets, then Pu,v(q) = Pu′,v′(q) (or, equivalently, eRu,v(q) = eRu′,v′(q)).
The conjecture has been focus of active research for the past forty years and it is known
to hold in various partial cases. For instance, it has been proved for intervals of length ≤6
([2]), for intervals in type ˜A2 ([9]), for intervals that are lattices ([11], 7.23, and [5]), and for
intervals that start from the identity of the group ([6]). A parabolic version of Conjecture 1.1
holds for intervals starting from the identity ([16], [17]), while certain purely poset-theoretic
generalizations of the conjecture fail ([7], [15]).
In recent years, two new approaches have been proposed to investigate this conjecture:
via hypercube decompositions and via flipclasses. The latter approach ([12], [14]) has led to
the proof of the combinatorial invariance of Kazhdan–Lusztig eR-polynomials of Weyl groups
modulo q7 and of Kazhdan–Lusztig eR-polynomials of type A Weyl groups modulo q8. As a
consequence, the Combinatorial Invariance Conjecture holds for all intervals up to length 8
in Weyl groups and up to length 10 in type A.
The approach via hypercube decomposition, introduced in [4] and [10] and developed
through the use of certain machine learning methods, appears very promising for tackling
the combinatorial invariance problem in the symmetric group. This technique has led to
conjectural recursive description of the P-polynomials ([4]) and of the eR-polynomials ([8],
and [1]). The analisys in [1] establishes the conjecture for elementary intervals in symmetric
groups.
The description in [8] is formulated in terms of shortcuts of a hypercube decomposition.
A recent work ([13]) introduces the notions of amazing hypercube decompositions and, given
two amazing hypercube decompositions z and z′, that of (z, z′)-double shortcut. This has

2. Preliminaries
We begin by setting the notation and recalling basic definitions. For standard background
on Coxeter groups, we refer the reader to [3].
The notion of hypercube decomposition is relevant only in the case of Bruhat intervals of
type A. So, from now on we let W be the Coxeter group of type An−1, that is, the symmetric
group Sn with simple transpositions as generators.
We recall the definition of hypercube decomposition of a Bruhat interval of W following [8],
dually from how it was originally introduced in [4]. Let E be a finite set. We call hypercube
the directed acyclic graph HE whose vertices are the subsets of E and whose edges connect
two elements if and only if the second is obtained by adding one element to the first one.
Let p ∈W. Let E be a set of edges of B(W) having the same target p. We say that E
spans a hypercube if there exists a unique embedding of directed graphs θE : HE →B(W)
such that, for any α ∈E, the edge (E \ {α} →E) is sent to α ∈B(W). Eventually, we say
that E spans a hypercube cluster if every subset E′ of E consisting of edges with pairwise
incomparable sources spans a hypercube.
Definition 2.1. Let u, v ∈W and z ∈[u, v]. We say that z is a hypercube decomposition of
the interval [u, v] provided that:
i. [z, v] is diamond complete with respect to [u, v], meaning that, if there exist x ∈[u, v]
and a1, a2, y ∈[z, v], a1 ̸= a2, such that x →a1 →y, x →a2 →y, then x ∈[z, v];
ii. for all p ∈[z, v], the set Ep = {x →p : x ∈X \ [z, v]} spans a hypercube cluster.
If Γ = (u = x0 →x1 →· · · →xr = v) is a Bruhat path from u to v in B(W), then r
is referred to as the length of Γ, denoted ℓ(Γ), and {xi : i ∈[0, r]} is called the support of
Γ, denoted supp(Γ). Furthermore, the distance from u to v, denoted d(u, v), is min{ℓ(Γ) :
Γ is a path from u to v}.

DS(z, z′) = DS(z′, z). This led to the formulation of the following two conjectures ([13],

both conjectures imply the Combinatorial Invariance Conjecture.
Conjecture 2.6. Let W be a Coxeter group of type A. The above relation is trivial (that is,
it has only one equivalence class).
Conjecture 2.7. Let W be a Coxeter group of type A. Every equivalence class of the above
relation contains an amazing R-element.
Up to A5, computer calculations have verified that Conjecture 2.6 holds. Indeed, a much
stronger statement has been verified, that is,
DS(z, z′) = DS(z′, z)

type An−1, all standard hypercube decompositions lie in the same equivalence class.
