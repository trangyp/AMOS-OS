Disorder-Induced Phase Transitions in Altermagnetic Josephson Junctions
Chang-An Li1, 2, 3, ∗
1Hefei National Laboratory, Hefei 230088, China
2School of Emerging Technology, University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei 230026, China
3Institute for Theoretical Physics and Astrophysics,
University of Würzburg, 97074 Würzburg, Germany
(Dated: May 25, 2026)
Altermagnetic Josephson junctions (AMJJs) can host unconventional π phase and φ phase despite
vanishing net magnetizations.
Whether these phases are stable against disorder existing in real
materials remains an open question. Here, we investigate impact of disorder on exotic phases in
two-dimensional d-wave AMJJs. We show that disorder is able to induce phase transitions between
the exotic π and conventional 0 phases, accompanied by a strong suppression of critical current. This
behavior is attributed to modifications of the tunneling Cooper-pair phase shift and superconducting
decoherence. Remarkably, the anomalous φ phase is highly fragile in presence of disorder and can
be driven to either a π phase or 0 phase in a nonreciprocal manner. Across such transitions, the
first harmonic of current-phase relation changes sign, while the higher-order harmonics are rapidly
suppressed. Our findings reveal the crucial role of disorder in tailoring distinct phases of AMJJs
and shed new light on their potential functionalities.
Introduction.—Altermagnets (AMs) have emerged as a
new class of magnetic phase characterized by momentum-
dependent spin splitting but with vanishing net magneti-
zation [1–10]. The AMs combine key features of conven-
tional ferromagnets with spin-split band structures and
antiferromagnets with net zero magnetization, thereby
providing a fertile platform for novel physics and po-
tential applications [11–36].
In particular, the vanish-
ing net magnetization eliminates the detrimental stray
fields when proximitized to superconductors (SCs) [37–
39]. Henceforth, the hybrid structure of AM-SC provides
promising functionalities [40–44]. A prominent example
is the altermagnetic Josephson junction (AMJJ) [45–54].
With d-wave AMs, the AMJJ demonstrates tunable 0-
π phase oscillations as a function of the junction length
[45–48], analogous to those previously studied in ferro-
magnetic or antiferromagnetic Josephson junctions with
finite magnetizations [55–58]. Such controllable π-phase
Josephson junctions are of particular interest for super-
conducting qubits and digital circuits [59–61]. In addi-
tion, AMJJs can host anomalous φ phase with super-
current nodal points residing at general phase differences
ϕ ̸= 0, π [62].
However, to construct AMJJs with the
confirmed AM materials, such as KV2Se2O [63–65] and
CrSb [66–69], the presence of disorder will significantly
modify their quantum transport properties [36]. Hence-
forth, a crucial open question is how will these exotic
phases of AMJJs be influenced by disorder? Addressing
this problem will not only reveal the unique interplay
between AMs, disorder, and superconductivity, but also
provide useful guidance for practical implementations of
AM-based Josephson devices.
In this work, we systematically investigate the impact
of disorder on exotic phases of d-wave AMJJs in two di-
mensions (2D). The AMJJ is consisted of an SC-AM hy-
brid structure as shown in Fig. 1(a), where disorder is
introduced in the altermagnetic junction region. We find
that the exotic π phase in AMJJs is sensitive to disorder
and can be driven to the conventional 0 phase, even at
a relatively weak disorder strength. The corresponding
critical supercurrent will be strongly suppressed. Disor-
der can also induce a transition from the conventional 0
phase to the exotic π phase in the reverse direction, mak-
ing 0-π phase transition reciprocally possible [see Fig.
1(b)]. Remarkably, the anomalous φ phase turns out to
be highly fragile against disorder. It is eventually driven
to either a π phase or 0 phase in a non-reciprocal manner.
These results hold generally for different altermagnetic
orientations and junction details. We demonstrate strong
numerical evidence for these phenomena and provide di-
rect interpretations based on disorder-induced phase shift
of Cooper pairs tunneling across the junction.

As we have shown, the impact of disorder in AMJJs
manifests in two main aspects: (i) it drives exotic phase
transitions; (ii) it suppresses the critical supercurrent.
We elucidate these two points as follows. In AMJJs, the
Cooper pairs tunneling through the junction will acquire
a finite momentum δq ∝tJ even there is no net magne-
tization [45, 47]. The supercurrent across the junction
of length Lx is thus determined by an extra phase factor
δqLx as I ∝cos(δqLx) [47, 56]. Disorder modifies this be-
havior by effectively reducing the altermagnetic spin-split
strength, tJ →t′
J, due to the smearing of the anisotropic
spin-dependent band structure [36, 77]. This thus leads
to a renormalized phase shift to δq′L.
Therefore, the
critical current can flip its sign with changing the phase
shift, enabling the transition between the 0 and π phases.
Note that the scattering from disorder is detrimental to
superconducting coherence [71]. The strong disorder de-
stroys the coherence of tunneling Cooper pairs such that
the critical current is substantially suppressed, giving rise
to the strongly fluctuating regime.

Conclusion and discussion.—In summary, we have
demonstrated that disorder in AMJJs plays a crucial role:
it induces phase transitions between distinct phases. In
particular, disorder drives phase transition from the ex-
otic π phase to conventional 0 phase even at a weak dis-
order strength, while the 0 to π phase transition is also
enabled, establishing a reciprocal manner. Furthermore,
we reveal that the anomalous φ phase is intrinsically frag-
ile in presence of disorder. It is driven to either a 0 phase
or π phase, depending on sign of the first harmonic com-
ponent of the CPR. Our findings reveal the pronounced
sensitivity of AMJJs to disorder such that the disorder
effect should be considered for further functionalities of
AMJJs. Given that many AM candidates are found to
be metallic [63–69], our results are of direct experimental
relevance.
The disorder-induced phase transitions of AMJJs are
general phenomena even considering different types of
disorder such as magnetic disorder [79]. Under the mag-
netic disorder, transitions between the π phase and the
0 phase also occur and the fragility of φ phase remains
[75]. In contrast, when the d-wave AM is replaced by odd-
parity p-wave unconventional magnets, no phase transi-
tions are observed [75]. It is interesting to further explore
impact of the pairing amplitude symmetries in disordered
AMJJs, such as the odd-parity pairing.

Appendix S1: Evolution of current-phase relations under disorder
In this section, we present the current-phase relations (CPRs) of the disordered AMJJs before and after different
types of phase transitions, corresponding to Fig. 2 in the main text. In the clean limit, the CPRs change as a
function of the junction length Lx with a fixed width Ly. As shown in Fig. S1(a), the AMJJ exhibits the conventional
0 phase, the exotic π phase, and the anomalous φ phase at different junction lengths. Upon introducing disorder,
the the system undergoes phase transitions that modify the form of the CPR. Figures S1(b)-S1(d) demonstrate
representative evolution of the CPR across such transitions. As demonstrated in Fig. S1(b), the system is in π phase
at the clean limit with W = 0. With increasing the disorder strength W, the system is driven to the conventional 0
phase at a critical disorder strength W ≈1.2t. Note that the CPR at W = 1.5t clearly corresponds to the 0 phase.
For the φ phase, disorder can also induce a transition into the conventional 0 phase, as illustrated in Fig. S1(c). At
weak disorder strength W = 1.0t, the AMJJ is already locating in a 0 phase and the critical current is even enhanced
compared to the clean limit. Increasing W further, the critical current will be gradually suppressed as expected.
Alternatively, the φ phase can be driven to π phase with different initial conditions, as shown in Fig. S1(d). In this
case, the system experiences phase transition at around W ≈1.0t and it is well within the π phase for W = 2.0t.
These results indicate that the anomalous φ phase is fragile in presence of disorder.
