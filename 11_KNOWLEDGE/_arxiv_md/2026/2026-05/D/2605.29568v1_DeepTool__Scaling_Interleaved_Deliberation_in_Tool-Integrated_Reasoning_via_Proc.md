Yang He 1 Xiao Ding 1 Bibo Cai 1 Yufei Zhang 1 Kai Xiong 1 Zhouhao Sun 1 Bing Qin 1 Ting Liu 1
Abstract
Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) extends LLM
capabilities by leveraging external environments.
However, existing methods lack the deliberation
during sequential tool invocation required for
strategic planning and self-correction.
While
RL mitigates this, conventional approaches for
Tool-Integrated Reasoning are hindered by sparse
outcome-based rewards, failing to supervise
intermediate reasoning steps and tool invocations.
To address this, we propose DeepTool, a novel
framework that scales deliberate thinking within
the interleaved process of thinking, action, and
observation at each turn. In DeepTool, we first in-
troduce a synthesis pipeline that evolves extended
thinking into interleaved trajectories, integrating
adversarial perturbations to ensure robustness and
self-correction. Secondly, we devise Process-
Supervised Reinforcement Learning based on
GRPO, which utilizes an Action-Centric Pro-
cess Reward to reinforce intermediate interleaved
thinking and enforce precise tool invocation at
every turn. Extensive experiments demonstrate
that DeepTool achieves superior performance,
boosting Qwen2.5-7B significantly across six
benchmarks (e.g., AIME24: 3.2% →40.4% and
HMMT25: 0.0% →28.6%). Furthermore, the
token cost-effectiveness analysis confirms the util-
ity of interleaved thinking, demonstrating Deep-
Tool’s optimal balance between performance and
token efficiency.
1. Introduction
Large language models have recently demonstrated sub-
stantial progress across a diverse range of reasoning tasks,
including mathematical reasoning (Shao et al., 2024; Ahn
et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024), logical puzzles (Di et al., 2025;

Xie et al., 2025b) and code generation (Zhong et al., 2024; Li
& Wang, 2025). Despite these advances, purely text-based
LLMs are fundamentally limited by their probabilistic na-
ture. They frequently suffer from hallucinations (Zhang
et al., 2025; Dhuliawala et al., 2024), struggle with tasks
requiring precise logic or computation, lack access to real-
time information, and are unable to perform actions be-
yond language generation. To mitigate these inherent short-
comings, tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) has emerged as a
promising paradigm (Mialon et al.; Qu et al., 2025). By em-
powering LLMs with diverse external utilities, ranging from
code interpreters for exact execution (Feng et al., 2025; Xue
et al., 2025) and search engines for up-to-date retrieval (Su
et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2023b) to multimodal encoders for
perceptual tasks (Shi et al.; Wu et al., 2024), this approach
significantly extends the boundaries and reliability of foun-
dation models (Chen et al.; 2025b; Qin et al.).
Existing approaches for TIR generally fall into two
paradigms: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement
learning (RL). SFT methods instruct models to invoke tools
by imitating curated trajectories (Tang et al., 2023; Li et al.,
2023). However, since they rely heavily on static examples,
they often struggle to generalize beyond their training dis-
tribution (Hao et al., 2025; Dong et al., 2024; Foster et al.,
2024; Kirk et al., 2023). While RL approaches mitigate this
by encouraging the exploration of diverse paths (Feng et al.,
2025; Li et al., 2025b), they still face significant challenges
in maintaining logical coherence and robust error recovery
mechanisms (Shinn et al., 2023; Kumar et al., 2024; Huang
et al., 2023), primarily due to the scarcity of gold supervi-
sion signals for intermediate reasoning and tool invocation
processes. We posit that addressing the aspect of computa-
tion budget allocation can markedly enhance their efficacy.
Existing frameworks tend to apply uniform “shallow think-
ing” across all steps (Ghoshal & Al-Bustami, 2026; Qin
et al., 2025). Consequently, they fail to engage in deep de-
liberation specifically during complex tool invocations (Xie
et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2023). This deficiency restricts the
model’s capacity to internalize sophisticated tool-use strate-
gies, leaving them prone to cascading failures when initial
actions yield ambiguous or erroneous outputs.
To address these limitations, we introduce DeepTool, a

3. Experiment
3.1. Setup
Training
We evaluate Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Qwen et al.,
2025) and Qwen3-4B-Base (Yang et al., 2025) across three
configurations: a w/o thinking SFT baseline, a interleaved
thinking SFT model, and its further optimization via process-
supervised Reinforcement Learning (RL). The SFT stage
samples 8k instances from OpenR1-Math-220k (Open-R1
Team, 2025) with a Mosaic parameter of ρ = 0.1, while
the RL stage employs the LIMO dataset (Ye et al., 2025)
with ρ = 0.2. Detailed hyperparameter configurations for
the SFT and RL stages, as well as the specifications for data
synthesis, are provided in Appendix A.1.
Evaluation
We evaluate our method on a comprehen-
sive set of mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including
MATH500 (Hendrycks et al.), AIME 2024, AIME 2025,
AMC 23, HMMT Feb 2025, OlympiadBench (He et al.,
2024), and GPQA-Diamond (Rein et al., 2024). We report
the average@8 scores to minimize variance. For compari-
son, we categorize baselines into three groups: search-based
approaches, such as Search-R1 (Jin et al., 2025) and Search-
o1 (Li et al., 2025a); RL-enhanced tool reasoning methods,
including ToRL (Li et al., 2025b), ReTool (Feng et al., 2025),
and ARPO (Dong et al., 2025); and Zero-RL paradigms like
SimpleRL-Zoo (Zeng et al., 2025) and ZeroTIR (Mai et al.,
2025).

4. Related Work
System 2 Deliberation and Inference Scaling.
The emer-
gence of Chain-of-Thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022)
marked a paradigm shift in Large Language Models (LLMs),
enabling them to decompose complex problems into inter-
mediate steps (Zhou et al.). The field has moved beyond
fixed-computation paradigms toward test-time compute scal-
ing, often termed System 2 reasoning (Li et al., 2025c; Xie
et al., 2025a), where models allocate inference resources
to deliberate before generating. More recently, large-scale
reinforcement learning has unlocked models capable of "ex-
tended thinking," such as OpenAI’s o1 (Jaech et al., 2024)
series and DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025). However, these
pure-language reasoning models remain prone to hallucina-
tion in computational tasks (Farquhar et al., 2024), necessi-
tating the integration of external tools (Chen et al.).
Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR).
TIR emerged to en-
able LLMs to solve computationally intensive problems by
leveraging external programming tools (Chen et al.; Song
et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024). While Code-as-Reasoning
approaches like PAL (Gao et al., 2023a) and PoT (Chen
et al.) delegate reasoning entirely to executable programs,
they sacrifice the flexibility of natural language abstraction.
More recent frameworks, such as ReAct (Yao et al., 2022),
Toolformer (Schick et al., 2023) and Search-R1 (Jin et al.,
2025), interleave text generation with API calls. However,
these methods typically rely on linear, greedy decision tra-
jectories that are prone to error propagation and struggle
with self-correction in complex multi-step settings (Chen
et al., 2025a; Liu et al.). In contrast, DeepTool integrates
System-2 deliberation into the tool-use loop, enabling dy-
namic verification and robust self-correction through inter-
leaved thinking and execution.
Reinforcement Learning for TIR.
Reinforcement Learn-
ing has advanced TIR by enabling autonomous exploration,
utilizing verifiable outcome rewards to enhance generaliza-
tion and optimize reasoning paths (Li et al., 2025b; Feng
et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2025). However, these approaches
suffer from the credit assignment problem in long-horizon
tasks, where sparse rewards fail to distinguish between
sound reasoning and spurious successful runs, and often ne-
glecting the correctness of the thinking process itself (Light-
man et al., 2023; Uesato et al., 2022). While process super-
vision has proven effective for text-based reasoning (Deng
et al., 2025), its extension to multi-turn tool invocation re-
mains unexplored. DeepTool bridges this gap by implement-
ing Process-Supervised RL with an Action-Centric Process
Reward. This approach provides dense step-wise supervi-
sion, resolving reward ambiguity and effectively fostering
robust, adaptive System 2 interleaved thinking.
5. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose DeepTool, a novel framework
that scales deliberate thinking within the interleaved pro-
cess of thinking, action, and observation. To address shal-
low planning and sparse outcome-based rewards, we intro-
duced two innovations: the MOSAIC synthesis pipeline,
which incorporates interleaved deliberation and injects ad-
versarial perturbations to cultivate intrinsic robustness and
self-correction, and a Process-Supervised Reinforcement
Learning strategy that leverages an Action-Centric Pro-
cess Reward to further scale interleaved deliberation and
enforce precise tool invocations. Extensive experiments con-
firm DeepTool’s superiority across challenging benchmarks.
Moreover, our analysis validates the cost-effectiveness of
“interleaved deliberate thinking”, establishing a resilient
paradigm that seamlessly unites cognitive depth with precise
execution.
