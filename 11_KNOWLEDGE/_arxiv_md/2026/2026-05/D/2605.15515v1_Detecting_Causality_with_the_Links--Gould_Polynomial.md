DETECTING CAUSALITY WITH THE LINKS–GOULD POLYNOMIAL
VLADIMIR CHERNOV, MATTHEW HARPER, AND BEN-MICHAEL KOHLI
Abstract. The conjectures of Low [Low88] and Natario–Tod [NT04], and Penrose’s ques-
tion on Arnold’s Problem list [Arn, Arn04] ask if causality in spacetimes can be formulated
in terms of linking of spheres of light rays in the manifold of all light rays. For (2 + 1)-
dimensional spacetimes, this link happens in the manifold coverable by a solid torus S1×R2.
This was solved positively by Chernov and Nemirovski [CN10a, CN10b], see also [Che18],
which raises the question of which link invariants can be used to study causality. Cher-
nov, Martin and Petkova [CMP20] proved that Heegaard–Floer and Khovanov homology
completely capture causality.
Allen–Swenberg [AS21] conjectured that the Jones polynomial, which is obtained as an
alternating Euler characteristic from Khovanov homology, is also sufficient. But they con-
structed complicated examples of links AS(n)∞
n=1 that suggest that the Alexander–Conway
polynomial – which is the Euler characteristic of Heegaard–Floer homology – is not enough.
The Links–Gould polynomial is a quantum invariant that specializes to the classical
Alexander–Conway polynomial in two different ways [Ish06, Koh16, KPM17] and somewhat
surprisingly inherits some of its characteristic classical features, see [KT, LNvdV, HKST].
We show that it distinguishes all the Allen-Swenberg links from the link of causally unrelated
events and hence detects causality in all known examples where the Alexander–Conway
polynomial is not sufficient. This suggests that it may completely capture causality. The
work on the categorification of the Links–Gould Polynomial is an ongoing and hard problem,
and it is not a subject of this paper.
As a corollary, we also compute the Seifert genus of all Allen–Swenberg links.

1. Introduction
1.1. Causality and Linking. A spacetime X is a time-oriented Lorentz manifold with
signature (+, · · · , +, −). The spacetime is globally hyperbolic if it has a Cauchy surface,
i.e. a subset Σ such that every maximal causal curve γ – a curve with γ′(t) · γ′(t) ≤0 –
intersects it exactly once, see Hawking and Ellis [HE73, pp. 211-212]. Such curves represent
particles moving not faster than light. The Cauchy surface can be taken to be smooth and
spacelike, i.e. such that the restriction of the Lorentz metric to it is Riemann; and this
is where one gives the initial conditions for the Einstein equations. A globally hyperbolic
spacetime is diffeomorphic to Σ × R [BS06, BS05, BS03] which strengthens the similar and
much earlier homeomorphism result of Geroch [Ger70].
Globally hyperbolic spacetimes are the most important and studied class of spacetimes.
One of the versions of the Strong Cosmic Censorship Conjecture of Penrose [Pen98] is that
all physically relevant spacetimes are like this, that is, if you delete the black holes along
their horizons. Nothing can escape black holes, so what happens inside is not relevant to us
who live outside of them.
An alternative and equivalent definition of a globally hyperbolic spacetime is that it does
not have closed causal curves (i.e. there is no time travel) and that it does not have naked
singularities (i.e. the intersection of the causal future and causal past of any two points is
compact and there are no particles that disappear from the view point of the observer), see
Bernal–Sanchez [BS07],
Two points (events) x, y ∈X are causally related if there is a curve γ connecting them
with γ′(t) · γ′(t) ≤0, i.e. if one can get from one point to the other moving not faster than
the light speed. Such curves are called causal curves. As was shown by Low [Low88], the
space NX of future-directed unparameterized light rays in X can be naturally identified with
the total space ST ∗Σ of the spherical cotangent bundle of any spacelike Cauchy surface Σ.
The set of light rays through a point x is then identified with the sphere Sx ⊂NX called the
sky of x. Thus, the space NX is a natural contact manifold and Sx is a Legendrian sphere,
see [Low98] and [NT04].
Remark 1.1. Two events x, y are chronologically related if there is a curve γ(t) between
them such that the velocity vector along it is everywhere timelike i.e.
γ′(t) · γ′(t) < 0.
Such curves are called timelike. It is well known that if two events are causally but not
chronologically related, then the two events belong to the common light ray between x and
y. Thus, the sky link (Sx, Sy) is singular, and the two skies have a double point. Such links
are called nontrivial by definition.
△
Assuming that the Cauchy surface Σ is not homeomorphic to RP 2 or S2, Nemirovski
and Chernov proved the Low Conjecture [Low88, Low90, Low94, Low98], which states that
events x, y in a (2 + 1)-dimensional globally hyperbolic spacetime X (with Σ ̸= S2, RP 2)
are causally related if and only if their skies Sx, Sy are linked in NX, see [CN10a]. This
statement is false for Σ = S2, RP 2 [CR08], and in general when a higher dimensional Σ
admits a structure of a Y ℓ
x manifold, see [Bes78]. This also answered Penrose’s question from
the Arnold problem list [Arn04]. Here linked means that the pair is not isotopic to a pair
of fibers of the S1-bundle ST ∗Σ →Σ or that Sx, Sy intersect, meaning that x, y are on a

Remark 1.2. Similar conjectures and statements were proved in dimensions (3 + 1) and

for the future event there is a so called nonnegative Legendrian isotopy to the one in the
past, but not the other way round, see [CN10a, CN10b]. Telling the past event from the
future event if one looks at the topological link of skies is not possible.
△
For the case where the Cauchy surface of X is homeomorphic to R2, these skies are unlinked
parallel circles in the solid torus NX = S1 × R2, each isotopic to the longitude of the solid
torus. The solid torus can be represented as R3 minus a trivial knot, so the two component
link of two parallel longitudes in the solid torus corresponds to a three component link in
R3 that is a connected sum of two Hopf links.
Thus, the question of studying causality in (2 + 1)-dimensional globally hyperbolic space-
times with Cauchy surface Σ ̸= S2, RP 2 is now a question about finding link invariants that
distinguish 3-component links from the connected sum of two Hopf links H#H.
Chernov, Martin and Petkova [CMP20] showed that Heegaard–Floer [OS04a, OS04b] and
Khovanov homology theories [Kho00] completely capture causality in such spacetimes. This
raised the question of whether their Euler characteristics, the Alexander–Conway and Jones
polynomials, capture causality as well. This was thoroughly studied by Allen and Swen-
berg [AS21] who conjectured that the Jones polynomial completely captures causality. They
also found a sequence of 3-component links AS(n)∞
n=1 that look like a link of two skies but
are not distinguishable by the Alexander–Conway polynomial from H#H. See Figure 1 for
a representation of the first Allen–Swenberg link. Note that there are no known examples
of globally hyperbolic spacetimes where AS(n) links indeed arise as links corresponding to
skies, and there are no other known examples where the Alexander–Conway polynomial does
not capture causality.
In this work we study the Links–Gould polynomial LG, a two-variable generalization of
the Alexander–Conway polynomial. We show that LG distinguishes all Allen–Swenberg links
from the causally unrelated situation H#H. Thus, LG completely captures causality in all
known examples of 2 + 1-dimensional globally hyperbolic spacetimes.
Theorem 1.3. LG distinguishes all AS(n) links from H#H thus completely capturing
causality in all known examples of globally hyperbolic spacetimes with Cauchy Surface Σ ̸=
S2, RP 2.
Conjecture 1.4. We conjecture that LG completely captures causality in such spacetimes.
This conjecture is in the spirit of the Allen–Swenberg conjecture for the Jones polyno-
mial [AS21]. Indeed LG does not have the deficiencies found by Allen and Swenberg for the
Alexander–Conway polynomial, which is a specialization of LG.

1.2. Organization of the paper. In the following Section 2 we define LG and introduce
its main properties. Then we give an algorithm to compute LG on Allen–Swenberg links,
which we formally state as Theorem 2.2. We use the theorem to determine the leading and
trailing terms of LGAS(n) for all n in Corollary 2.4. This proves that LG distinguishes all
Allen-Swenberg links from the link of causally unrelated events, and we also determine the
3-genus of these links.

2. Computing the Links–Gould polynomial of an Allen–Swenberg link
2.1. The Links–Gould polynomial: definition and useful properties. The Links–
Gould polynomial LG is derived from the 4-dimensional irreducible representation of the
unrolled quantum group of sl(2|1) with highest weight (0, α) where α ∈C. We denote the
quantum group by Uq and this 4-dimensional representation by V (0, α). We will assume that
α is generic in the sense that α(−1−α) ̸= 0 and that q ∈C is not a root of unity. With this
assumption on α, the representation V (0, α) is irreducible. See [GHK+] for our conventions
for the quantum group and preferred basis of these representations. We will write s = qα
and {x} = qx −q−x.
We also recall that LG is computed from the modified trace construction of [GPMT09,
GKPM11]. Because the quantum dimension of V (0, α) is zero, the invariant of a link colored
by this representation is computed by cutting an arbitrary component and applying the
Reshetikhin–Turaev functor (RT) to the resulting 1-tangle. Irreducibility of V (0, α) will
imply that the image of such a 1-tangle is a scalar matrix, and the Links–Gould polynomial
is that associated scalar. Since the components of links considered in the present article are
all colored by V (0, α) it is not necessary to consider the modified dimension function for the
