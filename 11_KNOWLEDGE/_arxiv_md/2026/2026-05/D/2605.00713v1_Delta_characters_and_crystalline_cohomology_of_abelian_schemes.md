ABELIAN SCHEMES
SUDIP PANDIT
Abstract. We provide an explicit description of the smallest filtered sub-
isocrystal generated by the Hodge filtered piece of the crystalline cohomology
for an abelian scheme over a p-adic ring. Our method is based on the theory
of arithmetic jet spaces and delta characters associated to the abelian scheme,
introduced by Buium and studied later by Borger and Saha with a functor of
points approach. In particular, we prove that the delta isocrystal constructed
by Borger and Saha is indeed isomorphic to the fundamental smallest sub-
isocrystal of the crystalline cohomology in the category of filtered F-isocrystals.
As an application, we establish a comparison isomorphism between the delta
isocrystal and the crystalline cohomology of abelian schemes, which is governed
by the group of order 1 delta characters of the abelian scheme.

Buium developed the theory of arithmetic jet spaces in the category of p-formal
schemes over R in a series of articles [15, 17, 18, 19, 20] in the realm of delta geom-
etry. The theory was further explored in several articles including [1, 7, 21, 22, 26].
Most notably in [15], Buium introduced the additive characters of arithmetic jet
spaces of a smooth commutative group scheme G, which are known as the delta
characters of G. The study of these delta characters was motivated by their function-
field companions called the differential characters (also known as the Manin char-
acters) studied earlier by Manin and then by Buium, which led to the proof of
Mordell–Lang conjecture over function fields [11, 13, 28].
Following a similar principle, the arithmetic jet spaces and delta characters found
explicit Diophantine applications over number fields using p-adic methods.
To
mention a few selectively, Buium proved the Manin–Mumford conjecture for curves,
obtaining an explicit bound in [16].
Buium–Poonen proved the finiteness of an
unlikely intersection in Modular–Elliptic correspondences in [23].
Recently, the
author and Dogra obtained an explicit Mordell–Lang bound for curves in [24], and
Miller–Morrow obtained an explicit prime-to-p Manin–Mumford bound for higher-
dimensional varieties in [30].
On the other hand, Borger developed the geometry of Witt vectors and algebraic
jet spaces as a functor of points in [5, 6]. Later, the two theories of jet spaces have
been reconciled by Bartapelle–Previato–Saha in [2]. Using the functor of points
approach, Borger–Saha revived the study of delta characters by relating them with
the p-adic cohomology in [9]. More specifically, their approach led to constructing
a filtered semilinear object (Hδ(G), f∗, (Hδ(G) ⊃Xprim(G) ⊃{0}) associated to a
smooth commutative group scheme G (cf. section 3.5 for the construction). After-
wards, the author and Saha have further developed the delta geometric foundation,
pinning down the explicit relation between the arithmetic jets and their kernels, and
established a comparison isomorphism between Hδ(A)K and the crystalline coho-
mology H1
cris(A0)K, for an elliptic curve A over Zp in [32]. Subsequently in [25],
the author with Gurney and Saha proved that the filtered semilinear object due to
