DERIVED COMPLETE INTERSECTIONS AND POLYNOMIAL GROWTH OF
BETTI NUMBERS OVER DG-ALGEBRAS
MICHAEL K. BROWN AND JUSTIN LYLE
Abstract. A theorem of Gulliksen states that a local ring is a complete intersection if and only if
the Betti numbers of its finitely generated modules grow polynomially. We prove a derived version of
Gulliksen’s Theorem. More precisely, we prove a structure theorem for dg-algebras whose modules
exhibit polynomial Betti growth. As a key ingredient in the proof, we establish the existence and
uniqueness of minimal models and acyclic closures of morphisms of dg-algebras in a broader setting
than was previously known. We also extend to dg-algebras a theorem of Halperin on the vanishing
of deviations of local rings, recovering Gulliksen’s Theorem as an immediate consequence.
1. Introduction

The integers βi(M) are called the Betti numbers of M over R. A fundamental question that has
shaped the history of commutative algebra is: what can one say about the asymptotic growth
of these Betti numbers? For instance, the Auslander-Buchsbaum-Serre Theorem states that R is
regular if and only if βi(M) = 0 for i ≫0 and all M. Similarly, a theorem of Gulliksen characterizes
when R is a complete intersection in terms of asymptotic growth of Betti numbers. We recall that R
is a complete intersection if, given a minimal Cohen presentation φ: S ↠bR, where S is a regular
local ring and bR is the m-adic completion of R, the ideal ker(φ) is generated by a regular sequence.
Gulliksen’s Theorem is stated as follows:
Theorem 1.1 ([Gul80] Theorem 2.3). The local ring R is a complete intersection if and only if
the Betti numbers of every finitely generated R-module grow polynomially.
Gulliksen’s Theorem has become a landmark result in the study of free resolutions in commutative
algebra. See e.g. [AH87, Hal87, Jac82, Løf84] for expansions on Gulliksen’s Theorem, and see also
earlier work of Assmus [Ass59] for a related result.
Suppose now that A is a local differential graded (dg) algebra with residue field k (Defini-
tion 2.13).
For instance, if x1, . . . , xc is a (not-necessarily-regular) sequence of elements of the
maximal ideal m ⊆R, then the Koszul complex K on this sequence is a local dg-algebra. Suitably
finite dg-A-modules admit analogues of Betti numbers (see Section 2.2 for background). In this
paper, we ask: what can one say about the asymptotic growth of Betti numbers over A? For
instance, work of Pollitz [Pol21] shows that, if R is regular, and K is the above Koszul complex
over R, then the Betti numbers of (suitably finite) dg-K-modules admit polynomial growth. Our
first main result characterizes the local dg-algebras with this property, giving a differential graded
analogue of Gulliksen’s Theorem.

Remark 6.3. The arguments of Proposition 5.4 and Theorem 6.2 can be extended to cover not
only the intermediate subalgebras U(i) and V (i), but also those given by adjoining each successive
variable in the constructions of U and V . This is a key point in the proof of the main theorem
