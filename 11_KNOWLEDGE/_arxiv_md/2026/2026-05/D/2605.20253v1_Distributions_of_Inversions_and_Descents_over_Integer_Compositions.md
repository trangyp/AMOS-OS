Abstract
We derive a generating function for the number of integer compositions of n into k
parts (i.e., k-compositions of n) with a given number of inversions, and obtain similar
results for k-compositions of n with a given number of descents. Our approach relies on
a known bijection that associates each integer composition σ with a pair (π, λ), where
π is a permutation and λ is an integer partition. We show that the distribution of
inversions and the distribution of descents over k-compositions are related, respectively,
to the distribution of (maj,inv) and to the distribution of (inv,des) over permutations
of {1, 2, . . . , k}, where maj, inv, and des denote the classical permutation statistics
major index, inversion number, and descent number, respectively.

Introduction
A composition σ of the integer n into exactly k parts (or a k-composition of n) is an ordered
k-tuple of positive integers (σ1, σ2, . . . , σk) such that σ1+σ2+· · ·+σk = n. The set containing
all k-compositions, for unrestricted n, is denoted by Ck. Similarly, a partition λ of the integer
n into exactly k parts (or a k-partition of n) is a k-tuple of weakly decreasing positive integers
(λ1, λ2, . . . , λk) such that λ1 + λ2 + · · · + λk = n. If λ is a partition of n we may also write
λ ⊢n. The length of a partition λ, denoted by l(λ), is its total number of parts and the
multiplicity of part i in λ, denoted by mλ

We recall some standard definitions related to permutation statistics that can be applied
in the study of compositions as well. Let α = (α1, α2, . . . , αk) be k-tuple of positive integers,
with repetitions allowed. An inversion of α is a pair of indices (i, j) such that i < j and
αi > αj. A descent of α is an index i such that αi > αi+1. The descent set, D(α), is the
set that contains all descents of α and its number of elements is the descent number of α,
denoted by des(α). The inversion number, inv(α), is defined as the number of inversions
of α, while the major index, maj(α), is defined as the sum of all descents of α. We also
define the comajor index, comaj(α), as the sum of the complements to k of all descents
of α, i.e., comaj(α) = P
i∈D(α)(k −i). The sum statistic is |α| = α1 + α2 + · · · + αk and
generically denoted by | · |.

Our approach is based on the fact that every composition σ can be bijectively mapped to
a pair (π, λ), where π is a permutation and λ is a partition, with σ, π, and λ satisfying
maj(π) + |λ| = |σ|. Since λ has a fixed order of parts, we expect that all information about
inv(σ), des(σ), and other related statistics is encoded in π. In fact, in Section 3, Theorem 10,
we prove that the distributions of several statistics on Ck are directly related to distributions
of corresponding statistics on Sk.

π ∈Sk. Then, there exists a bijective map σ 7→(π, λ) such that π is the unique permutation
defined in Lemma 7 and |λ| + maj(π) = |σ|.
Proof. Let σ = (σ1, σ2, . . . , σk) be a k-composition and let π = π1π2 . . . πk be the respective
unique permutation defined in Lemma 7. We build a k-partition λ as follows. First, let
µ = (µ1, µ2, . . . , µk) = (σπ1, σπ2, . . . , σπk). Then, for 1 ≤j < k, subtract from each µj the
number of descents of π appearing to the right of or at position j in the one-line notation of

πj > πj+1. After this procedure, the total reduction in µ is maj(π), resulting in µ′, and
taking λ = µ′ yields |λ| + maj(π) = |σ|. Conversely, let λ be a k-partition and π be a
permutation of [k]. We can proceed backwards computing the integers µi from λ and π, and
then reconstruct the composition σ by applying π−1 to µ. It is straightforward to see that
the resulting σ will be a k-composition satisfying |σ| = |λ| + maj(π).
