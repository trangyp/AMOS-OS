DISCRETE PAULI PAIRS
TORGEIR KEUN LYSEN
Abstract. We determine sharp density thresholds for when discrete Pauli pairs with Gauss-
ian decay must be classical Pauli pairs. More precisely, we identify when equality of the
sampled moduli of two functions and their Fourier transforms forces equality of the global
moduli in time and frequency, thereby answering a question of Ramos and Sousa. We also
determine the sharp threshold for when discrete Pauli pairs must be weak Pauli pairs, where
either the time-side or the frequency-side moduli agree identically. These can both be seen
as phaseless versions of the results of Kulikov, Nazarov, and Sodin on Fourier uniqueness
pairs.
1. Introduction
The Pauli problem asks to what extent a function is determined by the modulus of the
function and the modulus of its Fourier transform. This problem was motivated by problems
in quantum mechanics [16]. More broadly, it is an instance of phase retrieval, where one
seeks to reconstruct an object from phaseless intensity measurements. This kind of problem
has gained substantial interest due to its applications in radiography [2], tomography [22],
X-ray crystallography [4, 14, 24], microscopy and diffraction imaging [3, 13, 23], quantum
mechanics [15, 21], data analysis [12], and other fields. For a mathematical survey of the
topic see [7].
In this paper we study a sampled version of the problem: when is equality of these two moduli
on discrete sets enough to force equality of the global moduli? The problem is motivated by a
question of W. Pauli, where the probability density of a particle was known only at countably
many spatial points at two different times. Note that it is impossible for us to recover the
phase under these conditions as there are infinitely many functions satisfying |f(x)| ≡|g(x)|
and | ˆf(ξ)| ≡|ˆg(ξ)| without having f(x) ≡eiϑg(x) for some ϑ ∈R. We use the following
terminology to distinguish the global problem from its sampled analogue.
Definition A (Pauli pair, weak Pauli pair, and discrete Pauli pair). Let f, g : R →C.
(i) We say that f and g form a Pauli pair if both |f(x)| = |g(x)| almost everywhere and
| ˆf(ξ)| = |ˆg(ξ)| almost everywhere.
(ii) We say that f and g form a weak Pauli pair if |f(x)| = |g(x)| almost everywhere or
| ˆf(ξ)| = |ˆg(ξ)| almost everywhere.
(iii) We say that f and g form a discrete Pauli pair for a pair of discrete sets Λ, M ⊂R
if |f(λ)| = |g(λ)| for all λ ∈Λ and | ˆf(µ)| = |ˆg(µ)| for all µ ∈M.
