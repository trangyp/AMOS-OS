method. We evaluate its mass and current coupling using the two-point SR approach. Our result
m = (6290 ± 50) MeV for the mass of M indicates that it can decay to a pair of mesons J/ψJ/ψ.

due to annihilation of constituent cc quarks to pairs of light quarks qq and ss. This mechanism

I.
INTRODUCTION
The four X resonances observed by LHCb-ATLAS-
CMS collaborations in the di-J/ψ and J/ψψ′ mass dis-
tributions with masses covering the region 6.2 −7.2 GeV
and their parameters provide valuable information about
fully charmed exotic mesons [1–4]. The reason is that
these structures are presumably composed of four charm
quarks cccc investigation of which can shed light on prop-
erties of such states.
Although exotic particles built of four heavy quarks
were already among priorities of theoretical studies, re-
cent discoveries triggered new investigations devoted to
their analysis [5–14]. In these publications parameters
of cccc mesons were calculated using all available mod-
els and methods. Such structures with various quantum
numbers were studied in the framework of the diquark-
antidiquark and hadronic molecule models which are
mostly employed ones. It is interesting that alternative
interpretations of X states were proposed as well.
In
fact, these structures were considered in Ref. [11] by uti-
lizing a coupled-channel approach. It was demonstrated
that in the di-J/ψ system exists a near-threshold state
X(6200) bearing quantum numbers 0++ or 2++. Due to
coupled-channel eﬀects X(6900) may also be generated
as a pole structure [12]. Additionally, the authors pre-
dicted existence of a bound state X(6200) with prefer-
able quantum numbers
0++, and resonances X(6680)
and X(7200) with 2++, respectively.
Possible assignments of the X resonances were also
considered in Refs. [15–23]. Thus, resonances X(6600),
X(6900) and X(7300) were analyzed as states with
JPC = 0++ or 1+−in Ref. [15]. The author employed the
diquark-antidiquark model and used SR method in con-
junction with Regge trajectory techniques. In the frame-
work of the relativistic quark model [16] X(6200) was
explored as a ground-state scalar tetraquark JPC = 0++,
whereas X(6600) identiﬁed with either 1S tensor state
JPC = 2++ or a radial excitation (2S) of the scalar
structure. The X(6900) state maybe is 2S excited tensor
tetraquark or 1D scalar/tensor exotic mesons.
The X resonances were studied in our works [24–27]
as well, where we evaluated parameters of various all-
charmed scalar structures. Analyses were done using the
QCD sum rule (SR) method and diquark-antidiquark
and hadronic molecule models.
It was found that the
resonance X(6200) is presumably ηcηc molecule [25].
The structure X(6600) was considered in the diquark-
antidiquark picture as a state built of axial-vector compo-
nents [24]. Properties of resonance X(6900), i.e., its mass
and full width agree with data provided one models it as
a tetraquark composed of pseudoscalar diquarks and/or
as a hadronic molecule χc0χc0 [25, 26]. Therefore, it was
interpreted as an admixture of the diquark-antidiquark
and molecule-type states. The resonance X(7300) was
treated in Ref. [27] as a superposition of the molecule
χc1χc1 and ﬁrst radial excitation of X(6600).
Recently the CMS collaboration informed about ﬁrst
measurements of X resonances’ quantum numbers [28].
The parity P and charge conjugation C symmetries of
these structures were found to be +1. Their spin J
is
consistent with J = 2, whereas J = 0 and J = 1 pos-
sibilities were excluded at 95% and 99% conﬁdence lev-
els, respectively.
The collaboration also made sugges-
tions about diquark-antidiquark nature of X(6600) and
considered structures X(6900) and X(7100) as its radial
excitations.
The tensor diquark-antidiquark state with internal
composition Cγµ ⊗γνC was studied in our work [29]. We
calculated the mass (6609 ± 50) MeV and decay width
(165 ± 23) MeV of this tetraquark and compared them
with available experimental data. We found that within
theoretical and experimental errors the mass agrees with
experimental data for thr resonance X(6600).
At the
same time, for the width of the tetraquark SR method

VI.
ANALYSIS AND FINAL REMARKS
The hadronic tensor molecule M =J/ψJ/ψ explored
in this article in the context of QCD sum rule method is
a unstable structure with the mass m = (6290±50) MeV
and width Γ[M] = (149 ± 21) MeV. The mass of this
molecule is comparable with experimental data for the
mass of the resonance X(6200) reported by ATLAS col-
laboration
6220 ± 50+40
−50 MeV.
(78)
The width of X(6200) measured by the same experi-
mental group amounts to
310 ± 120+70
−80 MeV,
(79)
which is, comparing central values, considerably larger
than Γ[M]. Nevertheless, there is the overlapping region
110 −170 MeV between the theoretical prediction for
Γ[M] and experimental information on width of the res-
onance X(6200). This fact allows us to interpret the ten-
sor molecule M as a candidate to the resonance X(6200).
It is possible that the physical state X(6200) only partly
consists of the molecular component J/ψJ/ψ, but this
component is evidently important to explain observed
features of the resonance X(6200).
One of the ways to improve the agreement between
the theory and experiment is to include into analysis an-
other decay channels of the molecule M. More precise
measurements are also necessary to reduce large errors
in Eq. (79) and make strong conclusions about structure
of X(6200).
Our studies give also qualitative information on the
mass of the excited molecule M(2S). In fact, the mass
of the ground-state molecule M was obtained using the
continuum threshold parameter s0 ∈[45, 46] GeV2. This
means that radially excited molecule should have a mass
m2(2S) > 45 GeV2 or m(2S) > 6708 MeV, which ex-
cludes the resonance X(6600), but not the next structure
X(6900). Stated diﬀerently, the resonance X(6900) may
contain an excited molecular component. This is only
quantitative analysis: For more strong statements one
needs to calculate parameters of M(2S) which is beyond
the scope of the present article.
The physics of four-charmed tensor mesons is far from
being complete. A credible interpretation of the X res-
onances is not straightforward and requires additional
detailed investigations.
