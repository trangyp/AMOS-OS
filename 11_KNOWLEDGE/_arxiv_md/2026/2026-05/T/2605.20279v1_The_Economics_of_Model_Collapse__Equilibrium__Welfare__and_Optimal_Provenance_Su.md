Abstract—Generative artificial intelligence is rapidly trans-
forming the supply side of training data: an increasing share
of new tokens, images, and structured records is produced by
previous-generation models rather than by human originators.
Recursive training on such synthetic content induces a measur-
able and often irreversible loss of distributional fidelity, a phe-
nomenon known as model collapse. We develop the first unified
microeconomic theory of synthetic data markets under model
collapse. We introduce the Synthetic Data Contamination Equi-
librium (SDCE), prove existence and generic uniqueness, derive a
welfare decomposition W = Wprod+Wcons−Lcoll−Linfo, estab-
lish a Wasserstein-gradient-flow mean-field collapse limit, prove
an impossibility of information-constrained implementation, and
obtain closed-form expressions for the welfare-maximizing prove-
nance subsidy s∗= KL(q∥p)/(2κ) and the welfare- maximiz-
ing watermark strength w∗= (1 −ψ) KL(q∥p)/(2κψ). We
prove an information-theoretic Cramer-Rao lower bound on
any provenance estimator using only producer-side observa-
tions and show that the Provenance-Market Iterative Retraining
(PMIR) algorithm attains this bound up to constants while
converging to an ϵ-SDCE in O(ϵ−2 log T) iterations. A reduced-
form OLS estimation on a C4-synthetic benchmark over ten
retraining generations yields a collapse-rate coefficient ˆb = 0.181
(HAC s.e. 0.024), within one standard error of the structural
prediction 0.183. Calibrated experiments raise generation-ten
model quality by 23.1 percent over the unregulated benchmark
while lowering the 2-Wasserstein drift on a held-out diversity
probe from 0.318 to 0.142. Scaling experiments over generations
t ∈{1, . . . , 10} recover a logarithmic-in-t collapse law log Qt =
log Q0 −0.183 t ρ2 with R2 = 0.962. The framework unifies
the empirical regularities of language-model self-consumption,
diffusion-model MADness, and recommendation-system feedback
loops, providing a single equilibrium scaffold for the empirical
study of contaminated data economies.

I. INTRODUCTION
The training of foundation models increasingly relies on
data that is itself the output of previous-generation models.
Three independent literatures now document that recursive
training on such synthetic content produces measurable and
often irreversible degradation in distributional fidelity [1]–
[5]. Concurrently, the data factor of production has shifted
from open scraping toward licensed corpora, with publicly
reported multi-billion-dollar transactions between content pro-
ducers and model developers [16]. These two developments
raise a question that classical information economics did not
anticipate [10]–[12]: when the quality of a tradable good (data)
endogenously deteriorates as a function of its own market
share (synthetic content), what equilibrium concept governs
the market, which of its properties are robust to the underlying
agent architecture, and which policy instruments restore Pareto
efficiency?
This paper develops the Synthetic Data Contamination Equi-
librium (SDCE), a microeconomic equilibrium concept tai-
lored to data markets in which model quality is produced by a
contaminated technology and provenance is a priced character-
istic. SDCE nests Arrow-Debreu competitive equilibrium [25]
as the no- contamination limit and Nash equilibrium [24] as
the no-market-clearing limit, while remaining computationally
tractable through a market-coupled iterative training algorithm.

Why SDCE Matters Now
Three empirical phenomena currently lack a unified theoret-
ical scaffold: self-consumption loops in language-model pre-
training [1], [4], [6], MADness in diffusion-image models [2],
[3], and positive-feedback drift in recommendation systems
[9]. SDCE provides a single equilibrium concept under which
all three arise as special cases of the same contamination-
externality mechanism (Section X).

the PMIR algorithm. Section V details the calibration. Sec-

II. RELATED WORK
A. Model Collapse and Self-Consuming Training
The phenomenon of model collapse under recursive training
is established in [1] for language models and in [2], [3] for
diffusion image models. Subsequent work refines the result by
characterizing the rate of distributional drift [5], the conditions
under which collapse is inevitable [6], and the sensitivity of
language models to their own output [4], [7]. SDCE is most
closely related to the stability framework of [3] but introduces
explicit market clearing and welfare accounting.
B. Economics of Data and Information
The economics-of-data literature treats data primarily as
a nonrival factor of production [8], [9], extending the clas-
sical economics of information [10]–[12] to digital goods.
Foundation-model training and the legal status of training cor-
pora are analyzed in [15], [16]. The closely related literature
on memorization, extraction, and watermarking provides the
empirical microfoundations for provenance detectability [17]–
[20].
C. Production-Function and Scaling Foundations
The classical Cobb-Douglas production function [13] and
the labor-displacement literature [14] ground the production-
side primitives. Scaling laws for neural networks [21]–[23]
motivate the parameterization of model quality as a function
of training-set size and composition. Differential privacy [27]
and information theory [26] provide the formal language for
quantifying contamination.

training built on the generative-adversarial [34] and diffusion-
probabilistic [35] families, each generation samples its train-
ing set from the previous-generation model. SDCE predicts
collapse to a small set of attractors with rate governed by the
Wasserstein contraction of Theorem 3, matching the MADness
phenomenon documented in [2], [3].
Recommendation feedback loops. In recommendation sys-
tems in which synthetic user feedback is mixed into training
data, SDCE predicts endogenous concentration in publisher
market share with a logarithmic-in-t collapse of long-tail
content, matching empirical regularities surveyed in [9].
In each case, the same contamination-externality mechanism
underlies the phenomenon, and Corollary 1 suggests a domain-
portable policy lever.
XI. DISCUSSION
The endogenous deterioration of model quality from sym-
metric initial conditions admits a structural rather than behav-
ioral interpretation: the share of synthetic content compounds
through elasticity-shift in the production function, a mecha-
nism distinct from classical capital depreciation. Theorem 2
formalizes the producer- versus-consumer-surplus tradeoff and
Corollary 1 provides a closed-form first-best instrument. The-
orem 4 clarifies the informational limits of decentralized
provenance certification, rationalizing the use of mechanism-
design-free instruments such as flat human-data subsidies.
Comparison with the lemons market. The classical lemons
result of [10] establishes market non-existence under static
adverse selection. SDCE differs in three respects. (i) The
contamination ratio is endogenous rather than primitive, which
enables closed-form policy characterization (Corollary 1). (ii)
The production technology is parameterized by contaminated
elasticities, making the producer- versus-consumer-surplus
tradeoff explicit through Theorem 2. (iii) The mean-field
collapse limit (Theorem 3) places SDCE in the Wasserstein-
gradient- flow framework, enabling analytical scaling results
not available in [10].
Limitations. The contamination dynamics are abstracted
from the full distributional-drift problem, which would re-
quire an explicit stochastic differential equation on probability
measures. The provenance signal is exogenously detectable;
allowing adversarial provenance spoofing would convert the
environment into a robust- mechanism-design problem. Cali-
bration is partial and should be refined as transaction-level data
on licensing contracts and synthetic-content corpora become
available.
XII. REPRODUCIBILITY
All training scripts, calibration files, the 32 replication
seeds {17, 31, 42, 53, . . . , 9973}, and the full set of figures
will be released at github.com/olaflaitinen/datacollapse under
the MIT license. Pre-computed checkpoints for generations
t ∈{1, 5, 10} will be distributed via Hugging Face. The full
sweep consumed approximately 1,120 A100-GPU-hours and
emitted an estimated 81 kg CO2e at the Swedish grid carbon
intensity. Per-generation wall-clock budgets are reported in the
supplementary material.
XIII. CONCLUSION
We introduced the Synthetic Data Contamination Equi-
librium
(SDCE),
proved
existence,
generic
uniqueness,
a
welfare
decomposition,
a
mean-field
collapse
limit,
and an information-constrained impossibility result with a
closed-form optimal provenance subsidy. The accompanying
Provenance-Market Iterative Retraining algorithm operational-
izes SDCE at scale, and ten-generation experiments recover a
logarithmic-in-t collapse law. The framework unifies, under
a single equilibrium concept, three empirically documented
phenomena of synthetic-data economies. Future work includes
endogenizing watermarking technology, incorporating adver-
sarial provenance spoofing, and extending SDCE to multi-
modal markets that span text, image, and structured-record
data.
