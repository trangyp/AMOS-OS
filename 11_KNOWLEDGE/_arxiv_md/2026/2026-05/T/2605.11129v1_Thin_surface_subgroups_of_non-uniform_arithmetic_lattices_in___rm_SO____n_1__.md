THIN SURFACE SUBGROUPS OF NON-UNIFORM ARITHMETIC
LATTICES IN SO+(n, 1)
SARA EDELMAN-MU˜NOZ AND MICHAEL ZSHORNACK
Abstract. We show that the fundamental groups of all non-compact, arithmetic, hyperbolic,
n-manifolds for n ≥4 contain thin surface subgroups. As a consequence of the proof of this
theorem we also show that the fundamental groups of the doubles of cusped, arithmetic,
hyperbolic n-manifolds embed as GFERF subgroups of SO+(n + 1, 1).
1. Introduction
Let n ≥3 and Γ ⩽SO+(n, 1), be a lattice. A surface subgroup of Γ is a subgroup isomorphic
to the fundamental group of a closed surface of genus g ≥2. Understanding the existence and
geometric features of surface subgroups of Γ has been of interest to topologists and geome-
ters for many years. This is, in part, due to the relationship between such subgroups and
incompressible surfaces in the ambient hyperbolic manifold. When such a subgroup exists, it
is always infinite-index in Γ and can often be made Zariski-dense in SO+(n, 1). When Γ is
arithmetic, such a subgroup is said to be thin in the sense of [Sar14].
Thin subgroups of arithmetic groups have attracted independent interest in recent years
within number theory and combinatorics as these subgroups share a number of useful properties
with the arithmetic group they live in. For instance, such subgroups satisfy forms of superstrong
approximation: they surject onto almost all congruence quotients of the lattice and their
induced Cayley graphs form expander families (see [GV11]). Despite the rich emerging theory
behind the properties of thin groups, it is not particularly well understood how such examples
arise. Though thin free subgroups are abundant in many contexts, (c.f., [Fuc14]), methods
from hyperbolic geometry have proven especially useful in constructing examples of varying
isomorphism type.
In [BL20], the authors draw attention to the general lack of non-free
examples and raise the question of which isomorphism types of groups can arise as a thin
subgroup of an arithmetic group.
Due to the interest in constructing thin groups of non-free isomorphism type and the rela-
tionship between surface subgroups of lattices and incompressible surfaces in locally symmetric
spaces, we focus on constructing Zariski-dense surface subgroups of fundamental groups of hy-
perbolic manifolds. In dimension 3, such examples are known: all lattices in PSL(2, C) contain
thin surface subgroups. For uniform lattices, this follows from [KM12] while for non-uniform
lattices, it follows from [CLR97]. In higher dimensions, the existence of thin surface subgroups
is known for uniform lattices in SO+(n, 1) for odd n by [Ham15] and for even n by recent work
in [KR25]. What remains open is the case of non-uniform lattices when n > 3. Presently, there
is only example of a commensurability class of non-uniform lattices in each dimension known
to contain a thin surface subgroup [Dou23]. Notably, these lattices are all arithmetic. In this
paper, we extend these results by proving the following theorem.
Theorem 1. Let Γ be a non-uniform, arithmetic lattice in SO+(n, 1), with n ≥4. Then Γ
has a thin surface subgroup.

The proof of Theorem 1 is an induction on the dimension n, beginning with n = 4. We first
find a subgroup of Γ ⩽SO+(4, 1) isomorphic to the fundamental group of a cusped, arithmetic
hyperbolic 3-orbifold. Because this 3-orbifold virtually fibers, it has a finite cover, M, with an
embedded punctured subsurface Σ. We define the folded double of M, FM, a graph of spaces
whose fundamental group contains the fundamental group of the double of Σ, DΣ. We then
describe how FM maps into the 4-orbifold H4/Γ and show that the induced map π1(FM) →Γ
is faithful and its image Zariski-dense. Since π1(DΣ) is embedded in π1(FM), the result is a
thin surface subgroup of Γ. Finally, we generalize to higher dimensions.
In the interest of the questions in [BL20] on constructing any non-free examples of thin
subgroups of Γ as above, we note that the proof additionally produces thin subgroups of Γ
isomorphic to π1(FM).
As a further application of the methods of Theorem 1, we also prove a subgroup separability
result about the groups considered. Recall that a discrete group of isometries of Hn is said to
be GFERF if it is subgroup separable on its geometrically finite subgroups. We use the folded
double construction of Theorem 1 and results of [BHW11] to prove the following theorem.
Theorem 2. Let M be a cusped, arithmetic, hyperbolic n-orbifold and let DM be the double
of M over its cusps. Then π1(DM) embeds discretely into SO+(n + 1, 1) and its image is
GFERF.
Note that the property of a group being GFERF is weaker than being LERF, i.e., being
subgroup separable on all finitely generated subgroups. Nonetheless, there has been interest in
both properties because of their use in lifting immersed incompressible submanifolds to embed-
ded ones in a finite cover. More intrinsic versions of this theorem, not reliant on embeddings
into SO+(n, 1) are also discussed at the end of §5.
In the course of the proof of Theorem 2, we also show that LERF is too restrictive a prop-
erty for these groups. Though the fundamental groups of hyperbolic 3-manifolds are LERF by
[AGM12, Wis21], the fundamental groups of higher dimensional arithmetic hyperbolic mani-
folds are not by [Sun17, BSS24]. Since, also by [Sun17], the fundamental groups of doubles
of non-compact, arithmetic, hyperbolic 3-manifolds over their cusps are not LERF, show-
ing these groups are GFERF is significant. The starting point for Theorem 2 is the work
of Bergeron–Haglund–Wise in [BHW11] showing that fundamental groups of arithmetic hy-
perbolic manifolds of simplest type are GFERF. From there, we generalize the methods of
[LR01], where it was shown that the double of the figure-8 knot complement over its cusps is
GFERF, extending this construction to the doubles of all non-compact arithmetic hyperbolic
n-manifolds.

3. Doubles of Fibered 3-Manifolds in 4-Manifolds and Orbifolds
The representation we constructed in Section 2 induces an immersion of a cusped, arithmetic,
hyperbolic 3-orbifold M3 into our 4-orbifold X. As previously mentioned these orbifolds virtu-
ally fiber [Ago0804], allowing us to pass to a finite cover of M that is a fibered 3-manifold. The
fibers are punctured surfaces, Σ, whose punctures meet the cusps of M. Truncating the cusps
of M and then doubling over the truncated cusps glues the punctures of Σ to the punctures
in its copy, resulting in a closed surface (see Figure 2). It is this surface whose fundamental
group will ultimately form a thin subgroup of Γ. In this section, we describe how to embed
the fundamental group of this double into a non-uniform arithmetic subgroup of SO+(4, 1).
3.1. The Folded Double. We now define the folded double of a finite volume, non-compact,
hyperbolic arithmetic 3-manifold. The definition uses the language of graphs of spaces, which
allow us to construct useful geometric realizations corresponding to groups defined by graphs
of groups.
Definition 2. A graph of spaces (W, Y ) is a graph Y and a collection, W, of connected
topological spaces associated to each vertex v ∈V (Y ), Wv, and each edge e = (v0, v1) ∈E(Y ),
We, along with π1-injective, continuous maps α(e,0) and α(e,1) from We to the spaces associated
to each vertex of e.
A graph of spaces (W, Y ) has an associated geometric realization, X, formed by taking
the disjoint union of the Wv with the We × [0, 1], then taking the quotient by the relation
that glues We × {0} to the image of α(e,0) in Wv0 and gluing We × {1} to the image of α(e,1)
in Wv1. By [Alt74], when X is connected, its fundamental group is simply π1(G, Y ), where
(G, Y ) is the graph of groups with vertex groups Gv = π1(Wv), edge groups Ge = π1(We), and
monomorphisms induced by the π1-injective, continuous maps in (W, Y ).
The graphs of spaces construction will be the basis for how we define representations of
our arithmetic hyperbolic lattices. Let M be a non-compact, fibered, hyperbolic arithmetic 3-
manifold with finitely many cusps, C0, C1, . . . , Cn−1. M has a topological double DM formed
by making a copy of the manifold, truncating the cusps and identifying each boundary com-
ponent with its counterpart in the copy. Because M has the fiber surface Σ, DM has a fiber
subsurface DΣ, formed by doubling the fiber surface. Note that DΣ is constructed by copying
Σ and gluing each puncture to the corresponding puncture in the copy, closing the surface.

3.2. Representations in the Fundamental Groups of 4-Manifolds and Orbifolds.
Now that we have abstractly described the folded double of a cusped, arithmetic, hyperbolic
3-manifold M, we construct a representation of π1(FM) into the fundamental group, Γ, of
a cusped, arithmetic, hyperbolic 4-manifold X and use its action on H4 to show that this
representation is faithful.
We first define some of the concepts that we use to characterize cusps and their associated
subgroups in π1(M). Let C be a cusp of M. A cusp point associated to C is a point on the
boundary of Hn that is approached by the preimage of C in some fundamental domain for the
action of π1(M) on Hn. We call the stabilizer of a cusp point in π1(M) of a cusp subgroup.
M is orientable, so its cusps have flat 2-torus cross-sections. Thus, each of these subgroups is
abstractly isomorphic to Z2 and generated by two parabolic isometries.

volume, the diameter of the slice {t} × S decreases exponentially as t →∞in the cusp C. We
use the notion of cusp depth to keep track of relative position within each cusp.

First, for a broken geodesic γ comprised of geodesic segments γ1, γ2, γ3, . . . that all meet at
angles greater than π
2 , there is a constant D0 such that if each segment of γ is of length at
least D0, then γ is quasi-geodesic. Moreover, the quasi-geodesic constants for γ can be chosen
independently of the length of each segment (see [BH99, p. 407]). For a quasi-geodesic, there
is some constant r determined only by the quasi-constants such that it cannot return to a point
after a distance greater than r away from that point. Fix some D > max(2r, D0). We will
find an x ∈H4 and a broken geodesic, γω, of the above form comprised of geodesic segments
of length at least D such that x and ρ(ω)x are contained in distinct geodesic segments. As
a piecewise geodesic can only self intersect along non-consecutive segments, if γω contained a
loop, then some geodesic segments γi and γj intersect with j > i + 1. This means one must
traverse at least the length of γi+1 to get from γi to γj, and as D > 2r, this is impossible. In

Finally, as in the proof of Corollary 8, the fiber group π1(Σ) is Zariski dense in SO+(3, 1)
and, by a similar argument as above, ϱ(π1(DΣ)) does not preserve any hyperplane in Hn and
contains a copy of SO+(n−1, 1) and hence, again by Lemma 7, is Zariski dense in SO+(n, 1).

5. GFERF Subgroups
We say that a group of isometries of Hn is GFERF, or geometrically finite locally extended
residually finite if it is subgroup separable on all its geometrically finite subgroups. This is
a slightly weaker condition than being separable on all finitely generated subgroups, or being
LERF. In this section, we prove Theorem 2, restated here.
Theorem 12. Let M be a cusped, arithmetic, hyperbolic n-orbifold and let DM be the double
of M over its cusps. Then π1(DM) embeds discretely into SO+(n + 1, 1) and its image is
GFERF.
Proof. Subgroup separability is a property that can be passed easily to subgroups. That is
to say that if we have groups G, H, K such that H < K < G, if G is H-subgroup separable
then K is H-subgroup separable. This fact follows from the definition of subgroup separability
[See [ALR01] for details]. Additionally, if G, H, and K act by isometries on Hn, the choice
of supergroup that contains H has no effect on its fundamental domain in Hn. Hence, if H
is geometrically finite as a subgroup of K then it is geometrically finite as a subgroup of G.
Therefore, subgroups of GFERF groups are themselves GFERF.
The fundamental groups of cusped, arithmetic, hyperbolic, orbifolds are GFERF [BHW11].
Thus, if there is a faithful representation of π1(DM) in π1(X), where X is a cusped arithmetic
hyperbolic (n + 1)-orbifold then π1(DM) is GFERF. Therefore, the fundamental groups of
the doubles of the fibered 3-manifolds that we considered in Section 3.1 are GFERF. We now
extend this result to the doubles of all cusped arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds and orbifolds,
as well as higher dimensional manifolds and orbifolds.
We proceed by using the folded double, as in Section 3.1. Though we initially defined FM
for finite-volume, cusped, hyperbolic 3-manifolds, the construction pictured in Figure 3 and
the proof pictured in Figure 5 are both independent of the dimension of M. So any faithful
representation of π1(FM) in some π1(X) restricts to a faithful representation of π1(DM) for
M any finite-volume, cusped, hyperbolic n-manifold.
In order to construct this representation, we first find some X such that a finite sheeted
cover M ′ of M has a π1-injective immersion or embedding into X. When n > 3 we do this
by noting that some conjugate of π1(M) is commensurable to SO+(f, Z) for some form f of
signature (n, 1).
Then we can let M′ = SO+(f, Z) \ Hn and X = SO+(q, Z) \ Hn, where
q = ⟨f⟩⊕⟨1⟩, which allows us to use the upper left corner function as in the proof of Lemma 3
to give us a π1-injective immersion of M′ into X. When n = 3, we can realize π1(M) as a
Bianchi group, thus we can pass to a finite sheeted cover of M that is a manifold that embeds

A more intrinsic statement of this result, independent of the embedding of π1(DM) can also
be given. First, let P denote the collection of cusp subgroups of π1(DM). Then π1(DM) is
hyperbolic relative to the family P. A subgroup H ⩽π1(DM) is geometrically finite (under
the above embedding into SO+(n+1, 1)) if and only if H is relatively quasiconvex in π1(DM),
relative to P (c.f. [Hru10]). Recall that a (relatively) hyperbolic group is said to be (relatively)
QCERF, or (relatively) quasi-convex extended residually finite, if it is subgroup separable on
its (relatively) quasiconvex subgroups. Therefore, by the above methods, we also arrive at the
following.
Corollary 13. π1(DM), as above, is QCERF relative to P.
