Abstract. We prove a lower bound on the Weil height, the so-called Bogomolov
property, for the algebraic extensions of Q cut out by the adelic Galois repre-
sentations attached to certain eigenforms whose local component at a prime p is
supercuspidal. To this end, we give a method for constructing metric inequalities
over p-adic Lie extensions of fields over Q that are finitely ramified at p.

Introduction
A classical problem in arithmetic geometry and diophantine arithmetic is to un-
derstand lower bounds for heights. On the arithmetic side, the Weil height is a
measure of the complexity of an algebraic number.
Denoting by h(α) the Weil
height of an algebraic number α, an important conjecture of Lehmer predicts the
existence of a constant C such that

results, as seen for instance [28, 1, 16, 27, 25, 23, 26]. A natural analogue on the geo-
metric side is to consider an elliptic curve E defined over a number field, equipped
with the Néron-Tate height ˆh. Lang conjectured a lower bound on ˆh depending
on the discriminant of E; Szpiro conjectured another bound on the norm of the
discriminant of E, implying Lang’s bound and among other things the ABC conjec-
ture; see [36] for a survey. In higher dimension, the Bogomolov conjecture proposes
a description of subvarieties of abelian varieties containing many points of small

canonical height. This inspired Bombieri and Zannier [6] to propose an arithmetic
counterpart, for the Weil height over an algebraic extension L of Q (or in other
words, with the L-points of the abelian variety replaced with Gm(L)). Namely, a
field L of algebraic numbers is said to have the Bogomolov property (property (B) for
short) if there exists a positive constant c(L) > 0 such that every non-zero element
α ∈L×, which is not a root of unity, satisfies
h(α) ≥c(L).
It is a hard problem to determine whether an arbitrary L has property (B) or not.
By Northcott’s theorem, every number field has (B), so the interesting examples
are all of infinite degree. Note that fields with (B) are fields in which Lehmer’s
conjecture is trivially true, whereas Lehmer’s conjecture makes a prediction on the
whole Q, that does not have (B).
A series of works by many people produced various classes of infinite algebraic
extensions L/Q with the Bogomolov property (see the introductions of [22] and [24]).
It is particularly interesting to investigate this property in the case when L originates
from a geometric object, for instance, when it is generated by the coordinates of the
torsion points of an abelian variety over a number field. In such a setting, the field
L can be written as the fixed field of the kernel of a representation of an absolute
Galois group. While property (B) for arbitrary abelian varieties is the object of an
open conjecture of David (see [12, p.114]), it is known for the field generated by the
torsion points of an elliptic curve E over Q thanks to the work of Habegger [17].On
the other hand, Amoroso and Terracini [4] and Piras [22] showed an analogous result
for the field
Q(ρf) := Q
ker ρf,
fixed by the kernel of the adelic Galois representation attached to a modular form f,
under the additional assumption that f admits a strong supersingular prime, that
is, a prime p not dividing the level and such that the p-th Hecke eigenvalue ap(f) is
0.
Here and below, we only consider cuspidal non-CM eigenforms f: when ρf is
either abelian or induced, property (B) for Q(ρf) is already known by [3, 2].
The hypothesis that ap(f) = 0 for some prime not dividing the level is rather
restrictive.
Indeed, for weight strictly larger than 2 it occurs only rarely: for a
fixed p, Calegari and Sardari [7] show that there are only finitely many forms f of a
given level for which ap(f) = 0, while for a fixed f, the Atkin–Serre conjecture [33,
(4.11k?)] predicts that only finitely many primes p satisfy ap(f) = 0. Nevertheless,
in [4, 22] this condition plays two crucial roles. The first one is local at p: it allows
one to relate the local p-adic Galois representation to Lubin–Tate theory, hence to
obtain a precise control of the ramification groups. This ingredient is essential in
the construction of the metric inequalities which eventually force a uniform positive
lower bound for the height. The second one concerns the behaviour away from p:
the same condition is used to prove that the image of the representation modulo its
center is abelian. This allows one to apply a well-known lemma of Amoroso, David,
and Zannier [2] and get a lower bound for the heights in the prime-to-p part of the
representation.
The local aspect at p has been further developed in [10].
There, the original
argument of Habegger based on Lubin–Tate theory is extended to the setting of a
p-adic Lie extension L of a p-adic field K, under the assumption that L/K is totally
ramified at a place above p. This allowed the authors to prove property (B) for a
wide class of p-adic Galois representations having a big local image at p.

However, it is much less clear how to extend the argument away from p to modular
Galois representations beyond the strongly supersingular ones, and more generally
what conditions on the global Galois representation could be assumed to relax the
property of being abelian modulo center.
The starting point of the present paper is the observation that there are indeed
certain other modular forms for which the representation away from p is abelian
modulo its center. These are the modular forms for which the associated admissi-
ble representation of GL2(Qp), provided by the local Langlands correspondence, is
supercuspidal. This provides a natural replacement for the condition of strongly su-
persingularity at p; although the local representation at p is no longer described by
Lubin–Tate theory, the projective image away from p retains precisely the abelianity
needed in the global part of the argument.
Our main result is therefore an analogue of the main theorem of [4] in this setting,
and it identifies a new family of infinite extensions, arising from modular forms, for
which the Bogomolov property holds. More precisely, we prove the following theo-
rem. Given an eigenform f, we write ρf,p : Gal(Q/Q) →GL2(Qp) for the associated
p-adic Galois representation and πf,p for the associated admissible representation of
GL2(Qp). We also write Gp ⊂Gal(Q/Q) for a decomposition group at p.
Theorem A (cf. Theorem 7.1). Let k ≥2, N ≥1 be two integers, and let f be a
non-CM, cuspidal eigenform of weight k and level Γ0(N) such that:
(1) all of the Hecke eigenvalues of f belong to Q;
(2) there exists an odd prime p such that the admissible representation πf,p is su-
percuspidal, and if ρf,p|Gp is induced, then p ≥5 and the image of the mod p
reduction ρf,p contains SL2(Fp).
Then the corresponding adelic representation
ρf : GQ −→GL2(bZ)
has property (B).
In order to combine the local and global ingredients, we are led to generalize the
analysis of p-adic Lie extensions from [10] to a setting in which the base field is no
longer a finite extension of Qp, but rather only finitely ramified. This is necessary
since the level of a modular form which is supercuspidal at p is divisible by p, so
that the field cut out by the prime-to-p part of the representation is in general
(finitely) ramified at p. We show that, in this context, Sen’s theorem still provides
the required asymptotic control of the ramification filtration. More precisely, at each
level of the Lie filtration, the maximal index of the ramification groups dominates
the ramification index. This allows us to construct a system of metric inequalities
in the spirit of Habegger’s method. Their combined effect is to force a uniform
positive lower bound for the height of non-torsion elements in the Lie extension
generated by the p-adic representation. We refer to Theorems 3.2 and 4.2 for our
precise statements.
A main limitation in our work is the assumption that the Hecke field of the
supercuspidal form is Q; removing this assumption would need a generalization of
our argument in many points, and will be the object of future work.
We also point out that the assumptions in Theorem A are more restrictive when
the local Galois representation associated to f is induced. However, such represen-
tations may be rare, in view of the following consideration. Consider an irreducible
modular Galois representation ρf : GQ →GL2(Qp) of weight k ≥2. A well-known

Overview of the structure. The paper is organized as follows. Section 1 intro-
duces the main definitions and recalls some relevant facts about property (B). In
Section 2, we study totally ramified p-adic Lie extensions of finitely ramified exten-
sions of Qp, proving a regularity property for their ramification groups. Sections 3
and 4 establish criteria for property (B) for Galois algebraic extensions whose lo-
calizations at p belong to the class described above. We believe that these results
are of independent interest and may be useful for proving height lower bounds in a
variety of other settings.
In Section 5 we analyze the local Galois representations attached to modular forms
which are supercuspidal at p, with particular emphasis on the finiteness of the projec-
tive image away from p. In Section 6, we investigate the p-adic Galois representation
attached to a p-supercuspidal eigenform. Finally, in Section 7, we combine the local
p-adic properties with the prime-to-p study and prove the Bogomolov property for
the field cut out by the adelic representation. Section 8 contains some examples of
eigenforms that satisfy all of the requirements of our main theorem.

that the current statement of loc. cit. requires ρf,p to surject onto GL2(Fp), but it
is clear that the proof only needs that the image of ρf,p contains SL2(Fp).
Proposition 6.4. In case (ind), assume that the image of ρf,p contains SL2(Fp).
Then the representation ρf,p satisfies Assumption 1.2 (NC).
The next proposition summarizes the results of this section.
Proposition 6.5. Let f ∈Sk(Γ0(N)) be a new Hecke eigenform for some k ≥
2, N ≥1, with rational Hecke field and supercuspidal at p. If ρf,p|Gp is induced,
assume that p ≥5 and that the image of ρf,p contains SL2(Fp). Then the represen-
tation ρf,p satisfies Assumptions 1.2 (LPTR, NC, CE).
Proof. In case (open), ρf,p has big local image (BLI) in the sense of [10, Proposition
3.2]. In particular, it has (PTR, NC) trivially, and (CE) by loc. cit.. In case (ind),
if p ≥5 then ρf,p has (LPTR) by Proposition 6.3, and (CE) by the last paragraph
of the proof of [10, Proposition 4.12]. Assuming that the image of ρf,p contains
SL2(Fp), ρf,p also has (NC) by Proposition 6.4.
□
7. Supercuspidal forms: the adelic property (B)

8. Examples
We rely on work of Mascot [21] to construct some examples of forms f satisfy-
ing the assumptions of Theorem 7.1. For detecting congruences between forms of
possible different weight, we referred to [18] and [38, Corollary 9.19].
Let p be an odd prime, N a squarefree integer not divisible by p, and consider
a newform f ∈Sk(Γ0(Npr)) with r ≥2, twist minimal (in the sense that Npr is
minimal among the levels of the newforms attached to the twists of f by Dirichlet
characters) and with Hecke field Q. Then πf,p is supercuspidal at p [19, Proposition
2.8]; by Proposition 6.1, r = 2 if p ≥5. Assume that ¯ρf,p is irreducible; then it is
absolutely irreducible, as explained in [34, §3.3]. Let N′ be the prime-to-p part of
the Artin conductor of ¯ρf,p; since N is square-free, N ′ is the product of all primes
q ̸= p such that ¯ρf,p is ramified at q. By [21, Theorem 5], if N′ ̸= 1 then the image
of ¯ρf,p contains SL2(Fp) and Theorem 7.1 is applicable.
By Serre’s conjecture, there exists a newform g of level N′ such that ¯ρg,v ≃¯ρf,p
for a place v over p in the Hecke field of g. The weight of g is an integer k′ ≡k
mod p −1 such that 2 ≤k′ ≤p2 −1. It follows that if there are no g ∈Sk′(SL2(Z))
with k′ ≡k mod p −1, 2 ≤k′ ≤p2 −1 such that ¯ρg,v ≃¯ρf,p for a place v over p,
then the image of ¯ρf,p contains SL2(Fp).
Example 8.1. Let p = 5 and consider the form f ∈S4(Γ0(575)) named 575.4.a.c in
[40]. We have 575 = 25 · 23; moreover a17 ≡0 mod 5 and X2 −173 does not have
roots in F5. This shows that ¯ρf,p is irreducible. A direct search shows that there are
no congruences of f with forms in Sh(SL2(Z)), for h ∈{4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24}. Then
the image of ¯ρf,p contains SL2(Fp).
