THE NORTHCOTT PROPERTY FOR COMPOSITES OF NUMBER
FIELDS OF BOUNDED DEGREE
BENJAM´IN CASTILLO
Abstract. Let K be a number field and M/K an infinite algebraic Galois extension such
that Gal(M/K) has finite exponent. Extending a previous result of Bombieri and Zannier,
we prove that M has the Northcott property. In particular, for every positive integer d we
deduce that the composite of all number fields of degree bounded by d has the Northcott
property. The main novelty of our approach lies in the application of a theorem of Segal on
profinite groups.
1. Introduction
Let α ∈Q be an algebraic number and h(α) its absolute logarithmic Weil height. By a
theorem of Northcott, there exists only finitely many algebraic numbers with bounded degree
and height. Based on this result, Bombieri and Zannier introduced the notion of Northcott
property for any set of algebraic numbers. Namely, if A is a set of algebraic numbers, we say
that A has the Northcott property (N) if the set
A(T) = {α ∈A : h(α) < T}
is finite for every real number T > 0. See [CW13] and [Wid23] for a comprehensive survey
on the Northcott property for algebraic extensions of number fields, including connections
of property (N) with mathematical logic and algebraic dynamics.
Let K be a number field. Let K(d) be the composite of all extensions of K of degree at
most d, for some positive integer d. In the seminal paper [BZ01] it was proved that the field
K(d)
ab (which stands for the composite of all abelian extensions of K contained in K(d)) has
the property (N), and it was raised the question if the same holds for K(d) with d > 2. The
purpose of this note is to prove this affirmatively. We first show the following:
Theorem 1. Let K be a number field and M/K an infinite algebraic Galois extension
such that Gal(M/K) has finite exponent. Then the field M has the Northcott property.
Remark 1. If L/K is an infinite algebraic Galois extension, Checcoli showed ([Che13,
Theorem 1.2]) that Gal(L/K) having finite exponent is equivalent to the fact of L having
uniformly bounded local degrees at almost every prime of K. Moreover, if Gal(L/K) has
finite exponent and is also abelian, then L is contained in K(d) for some positive integer d
([Che13, Theorem 1.4]); so in this case it was known that L has the property (N) by being
a subfield of K(d)
ab .

Corollary 1. Let K be a number field. The field K(d) has the Northcott property for every
positive integer d.
Remark 2. By a result of Vidaux and Videla ([VV16, Theorem 2]), any subring of a ring
of totally real integers having property (N) has undecidable first order theory. In particular,
Corollary 1 solves in the affirmative [VV16, Question 5], implying that the composite of all
totally real extensions of Q of degree at most d has undecidable first order theory ([VV16,
Corollary 4]).
The proof of Theorem 1 relies on the following result, which may be of independent
interest.
Theorem 2. Let K be a number field. Let S be a finite set of prime numbers containing
the primes that ramify in K. Let D ∈N. There are only finitely many number fields F
such that F/K is a Galois extension unramified outside the primes in S with Galois group
of exponent D.
This theorem is a consequence of a deep theorem of Segal about profinite groups, see
Theorem 3 below. His proof of Theorem 3 relies heavily on the classification of finite simple
groups, it would be interesting if a simpler proof for Theorem 2 could be given.
2. Galois extensions of finite exponent
In order to prove Theorem 1 we state some previous results that enable us to replicate the
proof of Bombieri and Zannier for the case of K(d)
ab . The first one is quite elementary, but it
will be very useful.
Lemma 1. Let K be a number field and M/K an algebraic Galois extension (finite or
infinite) such that Gal(M/K) has finite exponent. Let L be an intermediate field of M/K
which is a finite Galois extension of K. If p is a prime dividing the order of Gal(L/K), then
p divides the exponent of Gal(M/K).
Proof. Let D be the exponent of Gal(M/K). By the Galois correspondence theorem we
know that Gal(L/K) is isomorphic to a quotient of Gal(M/K) by some normal subgroup.
If p divides |Gal(L/K)|, then Gal(L/K) has a subgroup of order p generated by some g ∈
Gal(L/K). Since gD = 1, the result follows.
□
Let K be a number field and S a finite set of prime numbers containing the primes that
ramify in K. A key aspect in the proof of [BZ01, Theorem 1] is that there are only finitely
many finite abelian extensions of K unramified outside the primes in S with Galois group of
bounded exponent. Fortunately, the same is true if we drop the assumption of the extensions
being abelian. We will be able to drop the abelian hypothesis using a theorem of Segal about
Small Profinite Groups, i.e, profinite groups having only finitely many open subgroups of
index n, for every n ∈N.
Theorem 3 (Segal). A profinite group P is small if and only if P m is an open normal
subgroup of P for every positive integer m.
Here P m = ⟨pm | p ∈P⟩is the subgroup generated algebraically by all m-th powers in P,
and the closure is being taken in P.

Theorem 2 follows from a combination of [Seg18, Corollary 3.2] and [Che13, Theorem 1.2],
but here we give a direct proof from Theorem 3.
Proof of Theorem 2. Let L be the composite of all finite Galois extensions of K unramified
outside the primes in S with Galois group of exponent D. Let G = Gal(L/K). Every open
subgroup of G corresponds with a finite extension H of K, and note that only the primes
in S are allowed to divide the discriminant of H. If we fix the index of the subgroup, we
are fixing the degree of H over K, and it is a known result that the power to which a prime
divides the discriminant of a number field is bounded in terms of the prime and the degree
of the number field (see [Nar04, Note 9, p. 79]). Thus, the discriminant of H is bounded.
By the Hermite-Minkowski theorem ([Nar04, Theorem 2.24]) there are only finitely many
options for H, hence, G is a small profinite group. On the other hand, clearly GD = {1},
and by Theorem 3 this means that {1} is an open subgroup of G, i.e, the group G is finite,
as desired.
□

Let C be the set of primes p > exp(2D[K : Q](2 log D + DT)) which are unramified in K.
The argument above showed that if p ∈C we must have βj = 0 for 1 ≤j ≤e −1. That is,
α ∈U, where U was the fixed field of any inertia subgroup above v in L(θ). Therefore, α lies
in the intersection of these fixed fields, which is an unramified extension of K above v. This
works for every v lying above a prime p ∈C, hence, K(α) is unramified above any p ∈C.
Note that instead of α we could have used any conjugate of it, since they share the same
height. As a consequence, L is unramified above any p ∈C. Thus, by Theorem 2 the degree
of the extension L/K is bounded with dependence only on K, D and T. Having bounded
the degree and the height of α, there can only be finitely many α’s.
