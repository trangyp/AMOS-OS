We introduce a framework where light-matter transitions, rather than states, are the primary dy-
namical objects. Successive compositions of elementary transitions yield multiphoton processes with
compact diagrammatic bookkeeping of resonant and off-resonant pathways. This approach enables
transparent derivations of effective high-order Hamiltonians in the dispersive regime, foundational
to quantum-information applications. Applied to the paradigmatic Jaynes-Cummings model, our
framework reveals a photon-number-independent intrinsic Rabi frequency and persistent polaritonic
hybridization in the dispersive regime, unifying resonant and dispersive limits.
Light-matter systems can operate in different in-
teraction regimes, ranging from weak coupling [1] –
where irreversible losses dominate – to strong cou-
pling [2] – where coherent exchange of excitations
governs the dynamics –, and extending to ultrastrong [3]
and deep-strong coupling [4], where the interaction
strength becomes comparable to or exceeds the system
frequencies.
A canonical illustration of light-matter
interaction in the closed-system limit is provided by the
Jaynes–Cummings model [5–7]. In the resonant or near-
resonant regime, where the coupling λ is parametrically
larger than the detuning ∆[8], an excitation is coher-
ently exchanged between atom and cavity, giving rise
to Rabi oscillations and dressed polaritonic eigenstates
in which photonic and atomic degrees of freedom are
hybridized. By contrast, when the detuning ∆is large
compared with the coupling λ, the system enters the
dispersive regime [9], where the eigenstates reduce to
approximately separable light-matter product states, so
that the polaritonic character effectively disappears and
the interaction manifests through energy shifts rather
than coherent excitation exchange.
The dispersive regime features rich high-order processes
central to modern quantum technologies: it underpins
superconducting-circuit
architectures
for
quantum
nondemolition
measurements,
lifetime
enhancement,
quantum gates, and collective effects [10–12]; enables
two-photon Raman transitions in atom interferome-
try [13, 14]; and has been implemented in solid-state
platforms for two-photon entangling gate [15].
Fur-
thermore,
even
paradigmatic
cavity-atom
systems
exhibit highly nontrivial multiphoton interactions in
the dispersive regime,
including three-photon reso-
nances as demonstrated by Ma and Law [16].
The
dispersive regime thus offers a canonical route to

Diagrammatic structure
The concatenation structure
maps directly onto a diagrammatic representation. In-
deed, each JLM transition from ˆHint (see Eq. (2)) is
an elementary block, and their successive composition
produces higher-order transitions whose detunings, op-
erator ordering and selection rules are encoded visu-
ally. Counter-rotating processes enter on the same foot-
ing as co-rotating contributions, appearing as additional
pathways, thereby unifying RWA and non-RWA disper-
sive regimes [25, 33, 34]. Crucially, while state-centric
perturbation theory navigates a fixed eigenstate space
{|k, n⟩}k=e,g, transition-based perturbation theory gen-
erates new effective transitions as dynamical objects
whose frequency content and origin are encoded directly
in their diagrammatic construction.

parent route to effective Hamiltonians at arbitrary per-
turbative order, which we now derive.

The intrin-

subspace in both the population sector {ˆnc, ˆπ} and the

maps directly onto the Bloch-vector description of the
density matrix: the Bloch vector always precesses, but
the precession axis – and hence which components show
apparent oscillations – changes with the interaction
regime; resonant or dispersive.
Conclusion and outlook
In conclusion, this work es-
tablishes light-matter operators as the native objects of
light-matter dynamics. We have shown that this shift de-
livers two concrete results. First, it yields a ready-to-use
diagrammatic perturbation theory in which higher-order
effective interactions emerge as concatenated elementary
transitions, with operator ordering, detunings, and se-
lection rules encoded directly in the diagrams, render-
ing virtual pathways explicit. The derivation of disper-
sive effective Hamiltonians is then transparent without
Hilbert-space truncation or frame transformations – as
illustrated by recovering a three-photon resonance with
minimal computation. Second, the framework uncovers
the persistent hybrid character of the interaction. The
joint population operator encodes polaritonic hybridiza-
tion across all regimes, manifesting as coupling renormal-
ization on resonance and as detuning renormalization in
the dispersive limit; so that the latter is not a polariton-
free regime but rather a reorganization of the same hy-

JLM diagrams general construction rules and workflow
We outline a practical set of rules and a workflow for
constructing JLM diagrams, enabling the derivation of
effective high-order Hamiltonians in the dispersive regime
up to second order (n ≤2). The procedure is adapted
from the companion article [26], where additional details
can be found.
• (S1) Start from the interaction Hamiltonian and
identify its fundamental building blocks, namely
the zeroth-order JLM transition operators.
• (S2) Construct the nth-order JLM diagrams by
concatenating n + 1 zeroth-order transition oper-
ators.
• (S3) Compute the weight of each nth-order opera-
tor (up to n = 2) using the weights W0(t), W1(t)
and W2(t) defined in the main text.
• (S4) Sum all nth-order JLM transition operators,
each multiplied by its corresponding weight Wn(t),
to obtain ∆ˆH(n)
int (t).
• (S5) Finally, perform the adiabatic elimination
by discarding off-resonant contributions over a
timescale T that is large compared to the inverse
of their associated detunings.
