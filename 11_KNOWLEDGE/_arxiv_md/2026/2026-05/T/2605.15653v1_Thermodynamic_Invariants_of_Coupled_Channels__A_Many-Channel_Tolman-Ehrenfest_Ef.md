Thermodynamic Invariants of Coupled Channels: A Many-Channel Tolman–Ehrenfest
Effect
Benjamin Hamblin,1, 2, ∗Victor Calo,1, 2, † and Klaus Regenauer-Lieb1, 3, ‡

When multiple thermodynamic channels are coupled, single-channel equilibrium conditions fail.
Extending the Tolman–Ehrenfest effect to the entropy manifold, we derive the unique n-channel
invariant ζiTi = C, where ζi is the holonomy of the Ruppeiner connection. For the granular volume–
stress ensemble, Rowe’s dilatancy ratio and energy restriction emerge as geometric consequences
of the off-diagonal curvature gV σ, and the 60-year puzzle of state-dependent Kµ is resolved: the
correction ζV reaches O(1) near jamming.
The prediction ζV χ = const across a shear band is
experimentally testable.
Introduction.—Classical thermodynamics achieves its
economy by assuming the entropy surface is effectively
diagonal: channels equilibrate independently. For dense
granular media, reactive geophysical fluids, and active
matter [1, 2], coupling between thermodynamic chan-
nels is the dominant physics.
Existing frameworks in-
clude Blumenfeld–Edwards granular statistical mechan-
ics [3, 4], gauge-theoretic thermodynamics [5], and the
cross-diffusion reduction of Biktashev and Tsyganov [6].
These approaches identify the need for coupling correc-
tions but do not derive the invariant that coupled chan-
nels share at equilibrium.
The Ruppeiner metric [7, 8], gij = −∂2S/∂qi∂qj,
quantifies this failure: gij (i ̸= j) measures how much
injecting qj shifts the conjugate intensity Ti at fixed
qi, so that ∂βi/∂qj = −gij.
It is the precision ma-
trix of equilibrium fluctuations and diverges at critical
points [8]. The Ruppeiner and Weinhold [9] metrics have
been applied extensively to phase transitions, black hole
thermodynamics, and information-geometric characteri-
sation of thermodynamic systems [10, 11]; what has not
been identified in this literature is the specific invariant
that replaces the bare intensive variable when channels
are coupled. In the relativistic Tolman–Ehrenfest (TE)
effect [12] the conserved quantity in a gravitational field
is the redshifted energy, forcing T(1+ϕ/c2) = const; here
inter-channel coupling plays the role of the gravitational
potential, and the geometric weight ζi is computed from
the entropy-surface curvature.

Remark 1 (System Parity). Although this paper ad-
dresses an even-parity system for illustration, where
the skew-symmetric Onsager coupling block has no zero
eigenvalue and inter-channel exchange is confined to
closed reversible orbits, the general formalism is parity-
agnostic. The N = 2 volume–stress granular ensemble
is the canonical realisation; subtleties specific to odd-
parity (N = 3, 5, . . .) systems, in which a topological
zero mode opens an irreversibility mechanisms and drives
non-associated plastic flow, will be treated in upcoming
works. Crucially, the N = 2 MCTE equilibrium consti-
tutes the thermodynamic background state upon which
the N = 3 instability grows: shear-band nucleation re-
quires a N = 3 realisation, but its energetics are set by
the N = 2 Stable Layer from which it departs.
The off-diagonal block gV σij = −∂χ−1/∂σij measures
how much the inverse compactivity shifts when stress is
varied at fixed volume. It vanishes in the dilute limit and
grows large near the jamming transition. A sign subtlety
merits emphasis: because gij = −∂2S/∂qi∂qj, the off-
diagonal entry gV σ is negative when the cross-derivative
∂2S/∂V ∂σ is positive (stress-softening coupling). Com-
puting gV σ with the wrong sign breaks the MCTE in-
variance ζV χ = C; in the toy model of the Supplemental
Material [13], this error causes ζ1T1 to drift by O(1) along
the level set even though S itself remains constant to ma-
chine precision, providing a stringent self-consistency di-
agnostic (see Supplemental Material, Section S10). The
correct equilibrium condition is ζV χ = C, not χ = const.

Conclusions.—We have shown that when multiple
thermodynamic channels are non-negligibly coupled, the
correct equilibrium condition is ζiTi = C: a single con-
served scalar, with coupling coefficient ζi equal to the
holonomy of the Ruppeiner connection on the entropy
level set.
This generalises both the classical uniform-
temperature condition and the relativistic Tolman tem-
perature, and reduces to each in the appropriate limit.
