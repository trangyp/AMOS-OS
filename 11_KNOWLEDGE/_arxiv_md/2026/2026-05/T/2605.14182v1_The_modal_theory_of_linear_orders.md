THE MODAL THEORY OF LINEAR ORDERS
WOJCIECH ALEKSANDER WOŁOSZYN
Abstract. I study the modal theory of linear orders under embeddings,
monotone maps, condensations, and end-extensions. I prove modality
elimination for embeddings and monotone maps, show that condensations
make scatteredness modally definable, and compute exact propositional
modal validities in the main cases.
Modal linear order theory investigates linear orders from a modal perspec-
tive. The worlds are always linear orders, but the allowed maps determine
what it means for one order to become another: embeddings insert points;
monotone maps collapse intervals; condensations produce ordered quotients;
and end-extensions append final tails. In each case we ask what becomes
expressible in the resulting modal language and exactly which propositional
modal principles are valid, both with and without parameters.
Main Theorem.
(1) In the category of linear orders and order-embeddings, the embedding
modality admits modality elimination4.2. Every occurrence of
θ can
be replaced by a finite disjunction of order-patterns together with upper
bounds on the successive intervals they determine. Consequently, finite
worlds have exact sentential modal logic S4.3cap
6.9, while infinite worlds
have exact sentential modal logic S56.10. In the presence of parameters, a
world has exact modal logic S4.26.12 precisely when it contains infinitely
many adjacent pairs, and exact modal logic S56.13 precisely when it is a
nonempty dense linear order without endpoints.
(2) In the category of linear orders and monotone maps, the monotone-map
modality likewise admits modality elimination4.3. For
θ, satisfiable
target order-patterns contribute the disjunction of all source patterns that
collapse to them, with one extra empty-order alternative in the sentence
case. Consequently, every nonempty world has exact sentential modal
logic S57.3. With parameters allowed, singleton worlds have exact modal
logic S57.4, while nonempty infinite worlds have exact modal logic S4.27.5.
(3) In the category of nonempty linear orders and condensations, modality
elimination fails. Indeed, scatteredness is itself modally definable5.1. Every
world validates S4.2.18.1 with parameters, and this logic is exact for every
non-scattered world8.4.
(4) In the category of linear orders and end-extensions, every finite world
validates exactly S49.5, even with parameters allowed, and every world
admits an end-extension validating S59.7 with parameters.

The theorem shows that the same class of structures has markedly different
modal behaviour depending on which maps are allowed. Under embeddings,
finite size constraints act like buttons. Namely, once sufficiently many points
have been inserted into the relevant intervals, those constraints are neces-
sary; an infinite order has already realized all such finite possibilities at the
sentential level. Under monotone maps, the sentential structure collapses
even further. Every nonempty order maps constantly onto any singleton and,
more generally, the internal order-pattern is invisible to sentential modal
assertions, yielding exact S5. With parameters, however, infinite worlds re-
cover enough room for the exact logic S4.2. Condensations behave differently.
Modality elimination fails, and the modal language can define scatteredness,
a classical non-first-order property of linear orders. In the non-scattered case
this expressivity is calibrated exactly by S4.2.1. Finally, end-extensions are
more rigid, since all new points must be added at the top; nevertheless finite
worlds still validate precisely S4, while every world can be extended to one
validating S5. For the exactness assertions above, exactness means that every
modal principle outside the stated logic fails under some substitution of the
indicated kind.
In this article, the order language is L≤= {≤}, with x < y abbreviating
x ≤y ∧x ̸= y. Linear orders may be empty unless explicitly excluded; in the
condensation category the worlds are nonempty linear orders.
Meanwhile, the modal theory of linear orders forms part of the broader
project of modal model theory [HW24], the study of structures inside classes
of similar structures equipped with a possibly refined substructure relation
giving rise to notions of possibility and necessity. Its antecedents include
Hamkins’s Simple Maximality Principle [Ham03], the modal logic of forcing
of Hamkins and Löwe [HL08], and the structural analysis of forcing classes
by Hamkins, Leibman, and Löwe [HLL15], where the button, switch, and
ratchet methods were systematically connected with modal validities. The
subject grew further through set-theoretic potentialism and the potentialist
maximality principles [HL22], as well as arithmetic potentialism [Ham26],
and has since expanded to a variety of structural settings, including the
modal logic of inner models [IL16], the modal logic of abelian groups [BBL23],
and modal group theory [Woł25].
The modal theory of the category of
sets introduced the Kripke-category framework used throughout this paper
[Woł26], allowing one to study arbitrary concrete categories of first-order
structures rather than only traditional extension-based potentialist systems.
This broader perspective is essential for linear orders: embeddings, monotone
maps, condensations, and end-extensions all act on the same underlying
class of structures, yet they induce strikingly different modal behaviours and
different propositional validities.

1. Modal semantics for a concrete category
Let me first recall the formal semantics for the Kripke-category framework.
A Kripke category packages the two ingredients needed for the modal inter-
pretation: the structures themselves and the maps by which one is allowed
to move from one structure to another. Possibility and necessity are then
interpreted inside the cone of worlds reachable from the present one. For the
standard finite-frame facts used below we refer to [BRV01; CZ97].
Main Definition. A Kripke category is a concrete category of L-structures
in a common first-order language L such that every morphism is an L-
homomorphism. Its objects are called worlds, and its morphisms are called
accessibility mappings.
Definition 1.1 (Cones and modal satisfaction). Let W be a world in a Kripke
category K. The cone above W, denoted Cone(W), is the full subcategory of
K whose objects are the worlds accessible from W.
If L

under modal propositional substitution. The normal closure of T is valid at
a world W with respect to a substitution collection Γ if and only if every
formula of T is true at every world in Cone(W) under every substitution
from Γ.

A finite rooted tree is a finite partial order with a least element, called the
root, such that the predecessors of each node are linearly ordered. A finite
pretree is a finite preorder whose quotient by mutual accessibility is a finite
rooted tree. A finite pretree is regular of type (k, m), where k, m ≥1, if its
quotient tree has every non-leaf with exactly k children and every cluster
has size m. Equivalently, it is obtained from a finite rooted k-ary tree by
replacing each node with an m-cluster. A finite preorder is rooted if it has
a distinguished point from which every point is accessible. A capped chain
is a finite rooted preorder whose cluster quotient is a finite linear order and
whose non-maximal clusters are singletons.

Theorem 9.5. If L is a finite world in LOend, then its propositional modal
validities are exactly S4. More precisely, for every intermediate language
L≤⊆L ⊆L≤(L),
ValLOend(L, L) = S4.
Proof. The lower bound S4 holds in every Kripke category. Conversely, Propo-
sition 3.11(3) reduces the upper bound to railyard labelings of finite regular
pretrees using assertions from L. Proposition 9.4 supplies such labelings
by parameter-free order-modal sentences, hence by assertions belonging to
every intermediate language L≤⊆L ⊆L≤(L). Hence ValLOend(L, L) ⊆S4,
and equality follows. In modal terms, finite end-extension worlds have no
unavoidable validities beyond reflexivity and transitivity, because their finite
tails can simulate any finite S4-frame.
□
Remark 9.6. By relativizing the railyard construction above a fixed parameter,
one obtains infinite linear orders whose formulaic validities in LOend are
exactly S4. In particular, the rational line has exact formulaic logic S4 in
the end-extension category. This argument does not settle the corresponding
question for ω.
