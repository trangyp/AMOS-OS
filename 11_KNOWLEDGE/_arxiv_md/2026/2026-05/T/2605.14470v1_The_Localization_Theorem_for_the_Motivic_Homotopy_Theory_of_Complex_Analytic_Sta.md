THE LOCALIZATION THEOREM FOR THE MOTIVIC HOMOTOPY THEORY OF
COMPLEX ANALYTIC STACKS AND OTHER GEOMETRIC SETTINGS
ROY MAGEN
Abstract. We prove the analog of the Morel-Voevodsky localization theorem over complex analytic stacks,
which is used in [Mag25a] to establish a 6-functor formalism of complex analytic motivic homotopy theory
and produce an analytification map that is compatible with the six operations. Along the way, we establish
general techniques for proving this theorem over other geometric settings, which also apply, for example, to
the settings of algebraic stacks and differentiable stacks.

1. Introduction
The Morel-Voevodsky localization theorem is a crucial result in motivic homotopy theory that underlies
many important properties such as cdh descent and Grothendieck’s six operations. This result was first shown
for the motivic homotopy theory of schemes in [MV99, Theorem 3.2.21], which can be formulated as follows:

This localization property of SH is incredibly important, and the problem of establishing analogs of it in

establish cdh descent for it, as well as to show that the natural realisation maps SH(X) →SHhol(Xan) are
compatible with the six operations.
Let us now mention some examples of the localization property in algebraic geometry:
(1) The localization property is one of the axioms listed in [Voe01] to be used in the construction of a
6-functor formalism, and indeed, this was used crucially by Ayoub in his thesis [Ayo07a, Ayo07b] to
establish the formalism of Grothendieck’s six operations for the stable motivic homotopy theory of
quasi-projective schemes.
(2) The localization property was studied extensively in [CD19, D´e07], where the authors were also
particularly interested in the setting of sheaves with transfers. The situation in this case is more
delicate, and the problem of showing the result in general for Voevodsky motives remains open.
Nevertheless, the results proven in that paper allowed the authors to extend Ayoub’s construction of
the six operations to schemes that are not necessarily quasi-projective. The localization property is
also used to establish cdh descent in this setting.
(3) In [Hoy17], Hoyois established the six operations for an equivariant version of SH using an equivariant
version of the localization theorem also established in that paper. The localization property also
allowed Hoyois to establish cdh descent for this 6-functor formalism, and deduce that equivariant
homotopy K-theory satisfies cdh descent in [Hoy20]. The localization property in the equivariant
setting was also used in [KR24] to deduce the localization property for stacks and thence obtain a
6-functor formalism of stable motivic homotopy theory for derived algebraic stacks that satisfies cdh
descent.

The localization property is also a topic of study outside of algebraic geometry. Apart from Theorem A

(1) Cnossen establishes the localization property in the setting of differentiable stacks in [Cno23], allowing
him to establish a 6-functor formalism of stable motivic homotopy theory on differentiable stacks.
(2) Volpe establishes the localization property for sheaves on topological spaces in [Vol25], allowing him
to establish a 6-functor formalism for sheaves on topological spaces. This was further studied in
[Zhu25, He25].
(3) The role of the localization property in establishing cdh descent and constructing 6-functor formalisms
for general geometric settings was studied in [Mag25a].
Due to the great interest in establishing the localization property in diverse settings, we have decided to
prove Theorem A by first producing some general techniques for establishing gluing that do not rely on the
specific geometric setting. Indeed, the proof of the localization property usually follows the following series of
reductions:
(1) Before establishing the localization property for SH, one establishes the so-called gluing property for
the unstable version H of SH. It is then possible to deduce the localization property for SH using
Lemma 3.15 and Proposition 3.19.
(2) In order to show that H has the gluing property for i : Z →S, one first shows that for certain
“smooth” maps X →S, and maps t : Z →X over S, the presheaf ˆθi(X, t) is “contractible” in an
appropriate sense. One then deduces that H has the gluing property using Theorem 4.9, or other
results from Section 4.1.
(3) When X →S is actually some sort of “vector bundle”, so that it admits a section 0 : S →X that is
actually a “homotopy inverse”, it is easy to show that ˆθi(X, 0 ◦i) is contractible. We must use the
properties of our specific geometric setting to show that it is possible to relate a sufficiently general
X →S (and map t : Z →X) to this case via some zigzag of “´etale neighbourhoods”. Theorem 4.16
then allows us to deduce that ˆθi(X, t) is contractible.
This strategy is discussed in more detail in Section 5. We have endeavored to make our results general
enough so that they should be useful for establishing the localization property for many different types of
geometric settings. Indeed, apart from proving Theorem A in Theorem 5.24, as a demonstration of the wider
applicability of our techniques, we also re-establish the localization property in the settings of algebraic and
differentiable stacks in Theorems 5.3 and 5.8.

Filling in a subtle detail. It is worth mentioning that the proof of the localization property is often somewhat
delicate, and in fact, some subtle errors have been found in the proofs appearing in the literature. Already in
the original localization theorem of [MV99, Theorem 3.2.21], there is a mistake that has even been repeated
several times in the literature when proving other versions of the localization property. This mistake involves
using a property that holds for presheaves on categories SchS of schemes over a base scheme S, but does not

hold if we instead consider presheaves on categories SmS of schemes smooth over S. See Remark 2.17 and

This error is fixable, and has been corrected in most cases and avoided in more recent proofs such as
that of [Hoy21, Corollary 5] and [KR24, Theorem 3.15], simply by working with presheaves over these larger
categories (analogous to SchS instead of SmS). This should lead to a statement that is at least as strong, but
in principle one should ask how to deduce the statement over SmS from the statement over SchS. Indeed,
the arguments proceed by showing that certain maps are τ-local equivalences in these presheaf categories,
but in general, it may be possible to have a τ-local equivalence between presheaves on SchS that does not
restrict to a τ-local equivalence of presheaves on SmS. This could happen if some smooth scheme over S
were τ-locally equivalent to a presheaf on SchS that does not come from a presheaf on SmS. This cannot
happen in the cases of interest because the notion of τ-local equivalence given by τ is generated by Nisnevich
covering sieves and A1-homotopy equivalence, both of which are given in some sense by smooth maps.
The general context of our results demands that we be more explicit about this deduction, and therefore
also gives us the opportunity to fill in this detail for the existing proofs of the localization property. Indeed,
the reductions given in Section 4.1 make precise the above subtle point about moving between presheaves
on SmS and SchS by using Lemma 2.21. Example 2.22 shows that this result does not hold in complete
generality, e.g. if we allowed for cdh covers or constructible covers as well as Nisnevich covers, or if instead of
presheaves on SmS, we were interested in presheaves on the category of schemes proper over S.

2.1. Pullback Formalisms. We recall some basic notions about pullback contexts and pullback formalisms
from [Mag25b, §1]. This is also summarized in greater detail in [Mag25a, §2.2].
Definition 2.1. A pullback context is a category C equipped with a collection of maps called quasi-admissible
maps, which is stable under composition and base change, and which contains all equivalences.
For any S ∈C, we write CS to denote the full subcategory of C/S consisting of quasi-admissible maps to S.
Say C is quasi-small if CS is equivalent to a small category for all S ∈C.
Definition 2.2. Given a pullback context C, we say that a full subcategory C′ ⊆C is a (full) anodyne pullback
subcontext if every quasi-admissible map to an object of C′ has domain given by an object of C′. The category
C′ then inherits the structure of a pullback context in which a map is quasi-admissible exactly when it is
quasi-admissible in C.

changes along quasi-admissible maps.

3.2. Stabilizing. The procedure of “stabilizing” pullback formalisms was studied in [Mag25b, §4]. Intuitively,

homotopy category, and the stabilization SHdiff(BG) is the category of genuine G-spectra.

5. Applications to Motivic Homotopy Theories on Stacks
We will now consider some applications of our general techniques for showing gluing. More specifically, we
will be interested in the contexts of [Mag25b, §5]. The results for the algebraic and differentiable contexts of
[Mag25b, §5.1 and §5.2] have already been addressed in [KR24, Hoy17] and [Cno23], but we have chosen to
include them to show how our general tools can be applied to diverse geometric contexts.
The general situation in which we are interested is the following: we have a small pullback context C
equipped with a quasi-admissible pseudotopology τ, and a collection of maps K for which we want to show
that Hτ has gluing. Recall that Hτ is the pullback formalism that sends an object S ∈C to the category
Pshτ(CS) of τ-invariant presheaves on CS. In fact, we also have some “stabilized” version SHτ of Hτ that is
given by formally adjoining certain ∧-inverses to the pointed version Hτ
• of Hτ, and we would like to show
that maps in K are actually SHτ-closed. Using Lemma 3.15 and Proposition 3.19, this reduces to showing
that maps in K are Hτ-closed, that is, if i : Z →S is in K, then Hτ has gluing for i, and i∗: Hτ(Z) →Hτ(S)
is conservative.
The fact that i∗: Hτ(Z) →Hτ(S) is conservative is generally shown by working locally on S using a
suitable quasi-admissible Hτ-pseudocover, and then giving some geometric argument showing roughly that
any quasi-admissible map to Z is “τ-locally” given by the base change along i of quasi-admissible maps to S.
It follows that Hτ(Z) is generated under colimits by the essential image of i∗, so i∗is conservative.
We are left with the task of showing that Hτ has gluing for maps in K. For this, we pick a suitable
collection G of well-behaved objects in C for which we can apply Theorem 4.9, allowing us to reduce to
showing that for any i : Z →S in K with S ∈G, if X ∈G, X →S is quasi-admissible, and t : Z →X is a
map over S, then ˆθi(X, t) is τ-locally contractible (recall Notation 4.5).

Theorem 4.9, but small enough for us to be able to show that the presheaves ˆθi(X, t) are τ-locally contractible.

results from Section 4.1, but we always end up reducing to the τ-local contractibility of presheaves of the
form ˆθi(X, t). One example of a more delicate reduction of this form is given in our proof for the algebraic

Pseudotopologies: We have the following small quasi-admissible pseudotopologies on Shv(AnC):
