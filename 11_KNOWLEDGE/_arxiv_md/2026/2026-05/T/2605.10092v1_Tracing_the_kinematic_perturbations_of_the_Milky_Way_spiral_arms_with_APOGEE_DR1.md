ABSTRACT
Aims. We constrain the dynamical perturbations of the spiral arms in the Milky Way disk, based on the non-axisymmetric streaming
motions of RGB stars revealed by APOGEE and Gaia.
Methods. We develop a revised steady-state radial-velocity response model that incorporates both the VR,sin and the dynamically
important VR,cos components for a two-armed logarithmic spiral potential. The model is validated using orbit integrations with AGAMA
and Bayesian parameter recovery with dynesty, and is applied to the smoothed two-dimensional radial-velocity field of RGB stars
while accounting for Lindblad and corotation resonances.
Results. The revised model reproduces the phase and amplitude of the mock radial-velocity field to the ∼2% level, substantially
improving upon earlier VR,sin-only formulations. Applied to the observational data, it yields a robust pitch angle of p ≃10◦and a
local surface density contrast of ξ ≃5–18% at the solar radius. The radial scale length is less well-constrained (hR,1 ≃40–50 kpc)
due to intrinsic parameter covariance. Resonance effects strongly shape the velocity field, thus affecting the fitting: the radial velocity
becomes extremely large near the Lindblad resonances, whereas it vanishes close to the corotation resonance.
Conclusions. Our results demonstrate that including both the VR,sin and VR,cos terms is essential for a physically consistent inter-
pretation of stellar streaming motions induced by a spiral potential. The observed kinematics constrain the spiral pattern speed to
Ωp ≈10–20 km s−1kpc−1.
Key words. Galaxy: kinematics and dynamics; Galaxy: structure; Galaxy: disk
1. Introduction
The morphology of the Milky Way spiral structure is still an open
question (Churchwell et al. 2009; Hou & Han 2014; Xu et al.
2016; Rezaei Kh. et al. 2018). Studies have suggested both a
two-arm (e.g. Xu et al. 2023) and a four-arm (e.g. Reid et al.
2019; Drimmel et al. 2025) grand-design spiral shape, compli-
cated by several spurs and segments. The classification of the
Local Arm, where the Sun resides, as a major arm remains
contentious. Numerous studies described it as a minor spur
(Georgelin & Georgelin 1976; Shen & Zheng 2020), whereas
Xu et al. (2016, 2018) suggested its properties are comparable
to those of major arms. On smaller spatial scales, the struc-
ture of the Local Arm is further complicated by coherent fea-
tures in the local interstellar medium (ISM). The recently iden-
tified Radcliffe wave (e.g. Alves et al. 2020; Li & Chen 2022;
Konietzka et al. 2024) is a kpc-scale, osculating structure com-
posed of molecular clouds and star-forming regions, indicating
that the local segment of the arm departs from a simple planar
spiral morphology.
The spiral structure can be mapped using various observa-
tional methods. In the solar neighborhood, young OB stars and
associated molecular clouds are effective tracers due to their

At greater distances, classical Cepheids, and Red Giant Branch
(RGB) stars are commonly used owing to their reliable dis-
tance estimates, though care is needed in the outer disk to sep-
arate spiral features from the Galactic warp (Churchwell et al.
2009; Rezaei Kh. et al. 2018; Lemasle et al. 2022). In addition
to these stellar tracers, recent advances in three-dimensional dust
mapping using Gaia mission have provided a new view of the
Milky Way’s structure(Green et al. 2019; Lallement et al. 2022;
Zucker et al. 2023, 2025; Edenhofer et al. 2024; Barbillon et al.
2025). These studies reconstruct the distribution of interstellar
dust and have significantly improved distance estimates, reveal-
ing coherent large-scale features associated with spiral arms. On
the other hand, spiral structures can also be revealed through
their dynamical imprints on stars and gas. Studies using per-
turbations theories (Eilers et al. 2020; Khalil et al. 2025) as well
as numerical simulations (Grand et al. 2012; Antoja et al. 2022;
Li et al. 2022; Hunter et al. 2024) have analyzed the nonaxisym-
metric velocity pattern due to the spiral potential, thereby con-
straining the spiral arm properties.
There are usually three major parameters to characterize
a spiral pattern, namely the pattern speed Ωp, the pitch an-
gle p, and the surface density contrast between the arm and
inter-arm regions. The pattern speed Ωp of spiral arms rep-

to 30 km s−1kpc−1 (Mishurov & Zenina 1999; Dias et al. 2019;
Bobylev & Bajkova 2023; Bobylev et al. 2025). However, early

speed near 13 km s−1kpc−1, supported by later findings (Vallée
2021; Khalil et al. 2025). Using large-scale radial velocity data,
Eilers et al. (2020) proposed a constant pattern speed of Ωp =
12 km s−1kpc−1, consistent with a two-armed spiral structure
associated with the Local and Outer-Norma arms. Addition-
ally, Castro-Ginard et al. (2021) analyzed open cluster distri-
butions and reported a radial decline in pattern speed, rang-
ing from approximately 50 km s−1kpc−1 in the inner Galaxy to
17 km s−1kpc−1 in the outer regions.
The pitch angle p characterizes the winding of spiral arms.
A pitch angle of approximately p ∼6◦is often adopted for two-
armed models (Schmeja et al. 2014), while four-armed models
typically assume p ∼13◦(Bobylev & Bajkova 2014). However,
a single global pitch angle is unlikely to accurately describe all
spiral arms. Vallée (2017) measured pitch angles for three inner-
disk arms using two methods: the twin-tangent method yielded
pitch angles of 12 −15◦for the Sagittarius arm, 11 −15◦for
the Scutum arm, and 11 −15◦for the Norma arm (including its
Cygnus extension); the parallax method gave broader ranges of
6 −19◦, 6 −20◦, and 2 −15◦, respectively.
The surface density contrast between the arms and inter-arm
regions can be directly revealed by star counts (e.g., Poggio et al.
2021), although observational biases and sample selection ef-
fects need to be carefully considered. On the other hand, the
radial and azimuthal stellar velocity patterns driven by a spiral
perturbation in the disk can be constrained by dynamical mod-
els (Antoja et al. 2011). Using the mean Galactocentric radial
velocity, Siebert et al. (2012) applied a two-armed perturbation
model and inferred a density enhancement with an amplitude
of about 14% relative to the solar-neighbourhood background,
while Eilers et al. (2020) used a similar method to propose a
smaller local surface density contrast of 10% at the solar radius.
Meanwhile, Khalil et al. (2025) found that the local contrast den-
sity depends on the number of spiral arms, with 9% contrast for
a three-arm spiral and 25% for a two-arm structure.
The physical nature of the Milky Way’s spiral structure re-
mains actively debated. In the classical quasi-stationary den-
sity wave framework, spiral arms are modeled as long-lived,
rigidly rotating global patterns (Lin & Shu 1964; Roberts 1969;
Bertin & Lin 1996; Shu 2016), and this approach has been
widely adopted to interpret stellar and gas kinematics in
the Milky Way (e.g. Siebert et al. 2012; Vallée 2021). How-
ever, an alternative “dynamic spiral” scenario describes spiral
arms as short-lived and recurrent features(Dobbs & Baba 2014;
Sellwood & Masters 2022). In this picture, kinematic substruc-
tures revealed by Gaia, such as phase-space ridges (Hunt et al.
2018), disrupting and growing signatures (Baba et al. 2018;
Funakoshi et al. 2024), as well as varying pattern speeds across
different arms (Castro-Ginard et al. 2021), are better explained.
In addition, tidally induced spirals driven by external perturba-
tions can also generate non-equilibrium spiral features in the
Galactic disk (Laporte et al. 2019; Antoja et al. 2022). A com-
prehensive review on this topic is given by Hunt & Vasiliev
(2025).
In this work, we revisit the kinematic imprints of spiral arms
on the Galactic disk using APOGEE DR17 with StarHorse
distances (Queiroz et al. 2023) and Gaia DR3 proper mo-
tions, together with a steady-state dynamical model inspired by
Eilers et al. (2020). Our goal is to constrain the aforementioned
three parameters by using Red Giant Branch (RGB) stars as lu-
minous tracers. The structure of this paper is as follows. Sec-
tion 2 describes the RGB sample used in our analysis, as well
as the construction of the observed mean radial-velocity map.
Section 3 introduces the steady-state toy model of the spiral arm
perturbations. Section 4 investigates, based on orbit-integration
results, how the physical parameters affect the model predictions
and outlines the dynesty fitting procedure. Sections 5 and 6
present the fitting results for RGB data and discuss them in the
context of previous studies. Finally, Section 7 summarizes our
main conclusions and provides prospects for future work. De-
tailed model expressions are provided in the Appendix.

5. Kinematic signature of spiral arms
We proceed to fit the VR model to the APOGEE DR17 and
Gaia DR3 RGB sample, excluding the highly noisy region with
X > 0 kpc. We perform three tests with different spiral pattern
speeds, Ωp: two with fixed values, Ωp = 2 and 12 km s−1kpc−1,
following Eilers et al. (2020), and one in which Ωp is treated as a
free parameter. The results are summarized in Table 3 and Fig. 6.
To avoid the influence of resonances, for the fixed represen-
tative values Ωp = 2 and 12 km s−1kpc−1, the fitting regions are
restricted to R = 5–20 kpc (entirely within the ILR) and R = 7–
18 kpc (between the ILR and the CR), respectively. Figure 6 il-
lustrates a comparison between the dataset and the best-fit mod-
els, where the fitted spiral-arms loci are marked with black lines.
As discussed in the previous section, for Ωp = 2 km s−1kpc−1
(top row) the model predicts a positive ∇RWVR (second column)
which leads to larger residuals in the inner disk (third column).
In contrast, for Ωp = 12 km s−1kpc−1 (second row) the fitted
region already crosses the inner Lindblad resonance, where the
model exhibits a decreasing radial velocity gradient and larger
residuals emerge in the outer disk. In addition, in the obser-
vational data the inner-disk signal appears more irregular and
tightly wound, which further complicates the fitting for the Ωp =
12 km s−1kpc−1 model. As a result, its reduced chi-square value,
χ2
red, is slightly larger than that obtained for Ωp = 2 km s−1kpc−1
(see Table 3). The fitted spiral-arm loci coincide with the max-
ima of the stellar surface density contrast (see forth column);
however, the arms traced by the Ωp = 2 km s−1kpc−1 model
generally show a weaker contrast and are located closer to the
Galactic center.
For the case in which Ωp is treated as a free parameter (with
a uniform prior of Ωp = 1–30 km s−1kpc−1), no additional re-
striction is imposed on the fitting region (R = 5–20 kpc). As
shown in Fig. 6, third row, this model inevitably encounters the
CR at R ≈13 kpc, where the predicted |VR| becomes very small,
slightly underestimating the observed values. On either side of
RCR, however, the model is able to simultaneously reproduce the
decreasing trend of the maximum VR amplitude in the inner disk
and the increasing trend in the outer disk. As a result, this model
yields the smallest χ2
red. In addition, the outermost spiral arm in
this model appears to be a continuation of the inner spiral arm
through azimuthal winding, and the corresponding stellar sur-
face density contrast map exhibits the most prominent spiral-arm
features.

6.1. Comparison with previous studies

40 to 50 kpc, consistent with the values reported by Eilers et al.

6.3. Limitations and future work
In this work, we restrict ourself within the classical quasi-steady
density wave scenario, similar to Eilers et al. (2020). We ac-
knowledge several important limitations. First, the model as-
sumes that spiral arms can be approximated as long-lived, rigidly
rotating global structures. However, both theoretical and numeri-
cal studies suggest that spiral arms can also be short-lived and re-
current (Dobbs & Baba 2014; Sellwood & Masters 2022), espe-
cially in the Milky Way(Hunt & Vasiliev 2025). In this context,
the global pattern speed derived in this work should be inter-
preted as an effective, time-averaged, or locally defined quantity,
rather than evidence for a strictly steady global mode.
Second, the observed VR spiral features are not perfectly con-
tinuous and/or globally symmetric, which makes it difficult for
the model to simultaneously reproduce all observed structures
within a single, coherent solution. While the red–blue transition
is prominent in the Y > 0 region, the inward-streaming signals
(blue, VR < 0) in the Y < 0 region do not smoothly connect with
their counterparts at positive Y. Although this asymmetry may
partly reflect limitations in signal-to-noise or spatial sampling,
it may also indicate that a rigid, symmetric grand-design spiral
model is an oversimplified representation of a more complex,
possibly fragmented or transient structure.
Third, the present analysis focuses on two-dimensional in-
plane kinematics (VR and Vϕ) and does not incorporate verti-
cal (Z-direction) phase-space information. It is now well estab-
lished that the Milky Way disk is in a state of disequilibrium
(Antoja et al. 2022; Hunt & Vasiliev 2025), with external pertur-
bations along vertical direction. The coupling between in-plane
spiral perturbations and vertical kinematic features is therefore
not captured within our current 2D steady-state framework.
In future work, we plan to relax the quasi-steady assump-
tion by incorporating time-dependent and transient gravitational
potentials. Extending the analysis to include vertical kinematics
will be essential for disentangling internal spiral dynamics from
externally driven perturbations, ultimately enabling a more com-
plete three-dimensional dynamical characterization of the Milky
Way’s spiral structure.
7. Summary
Based on the mean radial velocities of APOGEE DR17 and Gaia
DR3 RGB stars, we investigate the spiral signatures of the Milky
Way using a revised VR model that incorporates both the VR,sin
and VR,cos components. The model describes non-axisymmetric
perturbations under the assumption of a two-armed spiral pat-
tern.
To validate the revised model and the feasibility of the fitting
procedure, we performed orbit integrations using AGAMA over a
duration of ∼40 Gyr. The revised model successfully repro-
duces the phase of the radial velocity field predicted by the orbit
integrations. After excluding particles located near the transition
regions between inward and outward radial motions, where the
linear approximation is intrinsically unstable, the mean discrep-
ancy in the VR amplitude is reduced to ∼2%. This represents a
substantial improvement over the original VR,sin-only model.
We then used the AGAMA-generated mock dataset and applied
Bayesian inference with dynesty to recover the input parame-
ters. The results demonstrate that the local surface-density con-
trast Σmax(R⊙) and the pitch angle p can be reconstructed. How-
ever, the parameters hR,1 and t exhibit strong covariance because
both are periodic in our model. Consequently, hR,1 is recov-
ered with a ∼20% uncertainty, which is effectively irreducible
within the model. We also examined the influence of resonances
and pattern speed on the radial velocity. The Lindblad reso-
nances (LRs) cause strong deviations where linear theory breaks
down, while the corotation resonance (CR) suppresses the radial-
velocity signal. As the stellar location crosses each resonance,
both the sign of ∇RWVR and the phase of the spiral-induced ve-
locity pattern reverse. For example, for Ωp = 2 km s−1kpc−1,
we obtain ∇RWVR > 0, and the spiral-arm signal appears in re-
gions with VR > 0. In contrast, for Ωp = 12 km s−1kpc−1, we
find ∇RWVR < 0, and the spiral-arm signal is located in regions
with VR < 0. For Ωp ≃18 km s−1kpc−1, the presence of the
corotation resonance at R ≈13 kpc leads to a mixed behavior:
at R < 13 kpc the radial velocity gradient and the spiral-arm
phase resemble those of the Ωp = 12 km s−1kpc−1 case, while
at R > 13 kpc they are similar to the Ωp = 2 km s−1kpc−1 case,
albeit with an overall lower VR amplitude.
Using the same methodology, we fitted the smoothed two-
dimensional radial-velocity map from APOGEE and Gaia. For
the fixed-pattern-speed cases (Ωp = 2 and 12 km s−1kpc−1), we
masked the bulge and resonance-dominated regions and adopted
fitting ranges of R = 5–20 kpc and R = 7–18 kpc, respec-
tively. In contrast, for the free-pattern-speed model, no additional
masking of resonance regions was applied. First, the pitch an-
gle remains close to p ≃10◦, consistent with previous studies
(Bobylev & Bajkova 2014). Second, the revised VR model re-
duces the implied density-perturbation amplitude by roughly a
factor of two compared to earlier kinematic models for fixed pat-
tern speeds, yielding a surface-density contrast of ∼5% at the so-
lar radius. When the pattern speed is treated as a free parameter,
the fit instead favors a higher contrast, ξ ≃18%, consistent with
previous studies. Third, the inferred radial scale length of the
perturbation depends on the assumed pattern speed: for the fixed
pattern-speed cases, the fits favor large values of hR,1 ≳50 kpc,
whereas allowing Ωp to vary freely yields a more moderate scale
length, hR,1 ≈40 kpc. This behavior reflects parameter degen-
eracy driven by the non-monotonic radial profile of WVR.
The inferred locations of the primary spiral arms depend on
the assumed pattern speed. For Ωp = 2 km s−1kpc−1, the arms
are consistent with the Sagittarius–Carina arm and the Outer–
Norma arm, whereas for Ωp = 12 km s−1kpc−1, they shift
slightly outward to align with the Local arm and an arm between
the Outer–Norma and OSC arms. In the free-pattern-speed case
(Ωp = 17.77 km s−1kpc−1), the dominant spiral arms are located
further outward, corresponding to the Perseus arm and an arm
between the Outer–Norma and the OSC arm.

Appendix D: Effects of scale length and local peak surface density
Figures D1 and D2 illustrate the effects of the radial scale length hR,1 and the local peak surface density Σmax(R⊙) on the VR maps
in the corotating reference frame. Both hR,1 and Σmax(R⊙) primarily regulate the overall amplitude of the radial velocity signal.
For otherwise identical parameter configurations, variations in Σmax(R⊙) have a slightly stronger impact on the VR amplitude than
changes in hR,1. It is worth noting that, because the maps are constructed in the corotating frame, the spiral-arm loci remain fixed
across different models. In an inertial reference frame, however, hR,1 additionally affects the azimuthal phase of the disk response
through the rotation term in Eq. (10), leading to a strong degeneracy with the spiral rotation time t.
