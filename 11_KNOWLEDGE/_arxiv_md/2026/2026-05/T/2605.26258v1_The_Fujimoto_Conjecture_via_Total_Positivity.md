SHUHEI KATSUTA
Abstract. H. Fujimoto showed that for a complete minimal surface in Rm, if the Gauss
map is non-degenerate, then it omits at most m(m+1)
2

The main tool is a special planar network (Γ0, ω) in the theory of positive matrices.

2. Totally positive matrix and totally nonnegative matrix
In this section, we recall some basic results of the theory of totally positive and totally
nonnegative matrices. This theory fits very well with the situation we are considering. We
refer the reader to [3] by S. Fomin and A. Zelevinsky, which provides a detailed account of
the subject.
Let M be an n × n matrix, and I, J ⊂{1, 2, . . . , n} with |I| = |J|. We denote by MI,J
the submatrix obtained from M by deleting the rows indexed by {1, 2, . . . , n} \ I and the
columns indexed by {1, 2, . . . , n} \ J. The determinant of MI,J is called the minor of M
corresponding to I and J, and is denoted by ∆I,J.
Definition 2.1 (Totally positive matrix, totally nonnegative matrix). Let M be a (real)
matrix. The matrix M is totally positive (resp. totally nonnegative) if all minors are
positive (resp. nonnegative).
Definition 2.2 (Planar network). Let Γ be a directed acyclic planar graph. We denote the
set of edges (resp. vertices) of Γ by E(Γ) (resp. V (Γ)). A function ω : E(Γ) →R is called
an edge weight function, and the pair (Γ, ω) is called a planar network. For each integer
n ≥1, we define a special planar network (Γ0, ω = (li,j, mk, ri,j)) as in Figure 1, where Γ0 is
directed from left to right, and li,j, mk, and ri,j (1 ≤i, j ≤n −1, 2 ≤i + j ≤n, 1 ≤k ≤n)
are real numbers assigned to the red, green, and blue edges, respectively. Each black edge is
always assigned the weight 1.
