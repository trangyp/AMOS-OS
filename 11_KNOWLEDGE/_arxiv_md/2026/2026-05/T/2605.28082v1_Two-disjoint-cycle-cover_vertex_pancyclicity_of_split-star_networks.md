CHANG LIU1,†, RUICHAO NIU2,†, AND YUEFENG YANG1,∗
Abstract. Let r1 and r2 be positive integers with r1 ≤r2. A graph G is called 2-
DCC vertex [r1, r2]-pancyclic if, for any two distinct vertices of G and any integer ℓ∈
[r1, r2], there exist two vertex-disjoint cycles of lengths ℓand |V (G)|−ℓ, respectively,
containing the two vertices separately. In this paper, we investigate the two-disjoint-
cycle-cover vertex pancyclicity of the split-star network S2
n. We prove that S2
n is
2-DCC vertex [3, n!/2]-pancyclic for n ≥4.
Key words: split-star networks; vertex-disjoint cycles; two-disjoint-cycle-cover;
vertex-pancyclicity.
1. Introduction
The n-dimensional split-star network introduced by Cheng, Lipman and Park [2],
denoted by S2
n. Since then, many fundamental properties of S2
n have been extensively
studied. Zhao and Hao [4] determined the g-extra connectivity of S2
n. Gu, Hao and
Chang [5, 6] investigated the component connectivity and the edge connectivity of S2
n.
Lin, Xu, Zhou and Hsieh analyzed the conditional diagnosability [7] and Lin, Huang,
Wang and Xu analyzed the restricted connectivity of S2
n, and further established its
good-neighbor diagnosability [8]. More recently, Zhao and Wang [9] characterized
the structure connectivity and substructure connectivity of S2
n.
An interconnection network is often modeled as a graph. We follow Bondy and
Murty [13] for notation and terminology not defined in this paper. A graph G =
(V, E) is an ordered pair consisting of a vertex set V and an edge set E, where V is
a finite set and E ⊆{(u, v) | u, v ∈V, u ̸= v}. We often write G = (V (G), E(G)) to
emphasize the vertex set and the edge set of G. The number of vertices in G is
denoted by |G|. If e = (u, v) ∈E(G), then u and v are called the ends of e. A
path P is often denoted by P = ⟨v1, v2, · · · , vk⟩, where v1 is adjacent only to v2, vk is
adjacent only to vk−1, and vi is adjacent exactly to vi−1 and vi+1 for all 1 < i < k.
To emphasize the ends of a path, we also write P[v1, vk]. Similarly, a cycle is often
denoted by C = ⟨v1, v2, · · · , vk, v1⟩, which is obtained from the path P[v1, vk] by
adding an edge joining v1 and vk. The length of a path or a cycle is defined as the
number of its edges. A path or a cycle of length k is called a k-path or k-cycle. A
Hamiltonian path of a graph G is a spanning path in G, and a Hamiltonian cycle
of G is a spanning cycle in G. A graph G is Hamiltonian if it contains a Hamiltonian
cycle.
To explore the possibility of embedding multiple cycles simultaneously, Kung and
Chen [14] extended these concepts.

A graph G is said to be 2-DCC vertex [r1, r2]-pancyclic if, for any integer ℓwith r1 ≤

cycles C1 and C2 in G such that one of the following holds:
• C1 has length ℓand contains u, while C2 has length |V (G)| −ℓand contains v;
• C1 has length |V (G)| −ℓand contains u, while C2 has length ℓand contains v.
If G is 2-DCC vertex [r1, r2]-pancyclic, then necessarily r2 ≤|V (G)|/2.
Qiao, Meng and Sabir [10] investigated the 2-DCC vertex pancyclicity of augmented
cubes. Niu, Zhou and Xu [11] studied the 2-DCC vertex bipancyclicity of bipartite
hypercube-like networks. Related results for generalized hypercubes and balanced
hypercubes have also been progressively developed [12]. Li, Chen and Lu [1] investi-
gated the 2-DCC pancyclicity of S2

that S2

2. Preliminaries
For any positive integers m and n with m < n, the set of integers {m, m +
1, . . . , n} is denoted by [m, n]. We use [n] instead of [1, n]. For any integer n ≥3,
let Sn denote the set of all permutations of [n].
For two subgraphs G1 and G2 of G, we use G1⊕G2 to denote the induced subgraph
on the vertex set V (G1) ∪V (G2). The graphs G and H are isomorphic, written
as G ∼= H. An isomorphism from G to itself is referred to as an automorphism of G.
For subsets F1, . . . , Fs of V (G), the notation G−F1 −· · ·−Fs represents the induced
subgraph G[V (G) −(F1 ∪· · · ∪Fs)]. When H is a subgraph of G, we use G −H as
a shorthand for G −V (H).
The graph G is vertex-transitive if, for any two vertices u and v, there exists
an automorphism of G which maps u to v. A graph G is said to be Hamiltonian-
connected if, for every pair of distinct vertices u, v ∈V (G), there exists a Hamiltonian
path starting at u and ending at v.
