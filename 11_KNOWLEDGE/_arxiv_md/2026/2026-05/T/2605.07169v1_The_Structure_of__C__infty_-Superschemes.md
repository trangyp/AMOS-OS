THE STRUCTURE OF C∞-SUPERSCHEMES
CRISTIAN DANILO OLARTE, PEDRO RIZZO, AND ALEXANDER TORRES-GOMEZ
Abstract. This paper establishes a structural generalization of Batchelor’s theorem within the frame-
work of C∞-superschemes. Our main result proves that any Batchelor space satisfies a global splitness
condition, establishing an isomorphism between the structure sheaf and its associated graded sheaf.
Although this isomorphism is non-canonical, the existence of a splitting endows the structure sheaf with
a natural Z≥0-grading. This grading is shown to be equivalent to the data of an even superderivation,
which we term an Euler vector field. Consequently, global splittings of C∞-superspaces can be charac-
terized in terms of Euler vector fields, providing a differential-geometric formulation of the splitting.
Key words: C∞-Superrings, C∞-Superschemes, Batchelor’s theorem, Euler Vector Fields.

1. Introduction
In a seminal paper [1], Batchelor proved that every smooth supermanifold is globally split. This
means its structure sheaf is of the form ⋀C∞
M E, where E is a locally free sheaf over the sheaf of smooth
functions on a smooth manifold M. Consequently, every smooth supermanifold is non-canonically
isomorphic to a “twisted” (or non-commutative) version of a standard vector bundle over a smooth
manifold. In this paper, our primary goal is to generalize Batchelor’s theorem to C∞-superspaces,
which extend the class of smooth supermanifolds.
A C∞-superspace [21, Definition 4.26] is a ringed space consisting of a topological space equipped
with a sheaf of C∞-superrings. These are the geometric spaces associated with C∞-superrings (see
[21, Definition 3.1] and [13, Definition 1.4.1]). Broadly speaking, C∞-superspaces enlarge the class of
supermanifolds by allowing the structure sheaf to contain purely even nilpotent sections arising from
the underlying C∞-ringed structure.

The notion of a C∞-superring emerges as a synthesis of superrings and C∞-rings. Formally, it is a
superring whose even part is endowed with the structure of a C∞-ring [21, Definition 3.1]. On one hand,
superrings—and their associated geometric spaces, such as supermanifolds and superschemes—are non-
commutative structures that are relatively straightforward to handle, providing a general perspective
on non-commutative geometric spaces. For a comprehensive discussion and relevant references, see
[6, 3, 14, 23, 19]. On the other hand, C∞-rings are R-algebras equipped with n-ary operations param-
eterized by smooth real-valued functions on Rn. The C∞-ring framework allows for the simultaneous
study of smooth and singular spaces and enables the description of nilpotent objects often absent in
traditional differential geometry. Furthermore, the category of C∞-schemes possesses superior cate-
gorical properties compared to the category of smooth manifolds; for instance, all fiber products exist.
For further reading on C∞-rings and C∞-spaces, see [10, 16, 9, 12, 20].
Other approaches combining C∞-rings and superrings have been proposed by Carchedi and Royten-
berg [5], as well as Nishimura [18] and Yetter [24]. While Carchedi and Roytenberg provided a powerful
categorical approach via “super Fermat theories,” its complexity can be daunting for geometrically
oriented researchers. Conversely, Nishimura and Yetter focused primarily on constructing a theory of
synthetic differential supergeometry.
In [21], we developed an algebro-geometric framework for C∞-superrings and C∞-superschemes.
A key result of that work was the equivalence between the category of fair C∞-superrings and the
category of fair affine C∞-superschemes. While this mirrors results in classical commutative alge-
braic geometry, a current challenge in studying C∞-superschemes is that many algebraic concepts and
constructions for C∞-superrings remain undefined. Consequently, we must not only apply algebraic
constructions to understand geometric spaces but also develop the underlying algebra of these objects.
The central result of this paper is a structural generalization of Batchelor’s theorem for C∞-
superspaces. In analogy with the theory of supermanifolds, the isomorphism between the structure
sheaf of a C∞-superspace and the exterior algebra of its fermionic sheaf—referred to as the global
splitting condition—is inherently non-canonical. The collection of such splittings is equivalently char-
acterized by the existence of an even superderivation that is adapted to the filtration induced by the
sheaf of ideals generated by all odd nilpotent sections (see [11]); we refer to this as an Euler vector
field. We define and investigate this class of derivations, showing that they provide a natural char-
acterization of the Z≥0-grading induced by the splitting of the structure sheaf. Consequently, a split
C∞-superspace may be formally understood as a triple (X,OX ,E), consisting of a topological space
X, a structure sheaf of C∞-superrings OX , and a specified Euler vector field E.
We introduce the notion of a Batchelor space, defined as a C∞-superspace whose structure sheaf is
both locally split and fine. These represent the minimal conditions required to prove the C∞-version
of Batchelor’s theorem. The category of smooth supermanifolds is a subcategory of the category of
Batchelor spaces, which in turn is a subcategory of the category of projected C∞-superspaces. Further-
more, we provide a systematic method for constructing C∞-superspaces to illustrate the boundaries
of these classifications. Specifically, we construct: a C∞-superspace that is a supermanifold; a C∞-
superspace that is a Batchelor space but not a supermanifold; a C∞-superspace that fails to be a
Batchelor space; a C∞-superspace equipped with a fine sheaf that is not locally split; a non-projected
fair C∞-superspaces.
This paper is organized as follows.
Section 2 provides a concise review of C∞-rings and C∞-
superrings, addressing the compatibility of these structures with both superring theory and the C∞-
ring framework. Section 3 presents our main findings in C∞-supergeometry, including the generalized
Batchelor Theorem and the characterization of splittings via Euler vector fields.

3. C∞-SuperGeometry
This section is dedicated to a deeper examination of the structure of C∞-superspaces. We begin
by introducing fundamental definitions.
Specifically, we use the notion of C∞-superring to define
affine C∞-superschemes.
We then show that C∞-superspaces satisfying certain gluing conditions
can be patched together to form a new C∞-superspace. Subsequently, we introduce the concepts of
projected and split C∞-superspace, which are direct generalizations of the analogous concepts defined
for supermanifolds, as discussed in [19].
Definition 3.1. An affine C∞-superscheme is a locally ringed C∞-superspace that is isomorphic to
sSpec(R) for some C∞-superring R.

We aim to treat both singular and regular spaces on an equal footing. For this purpose, the notion
of a differentiable space provides the appropriate framework.
Differentiable spaces offer a natural
enlargement of the category of smooth manifolds, designed to accommodate singular spaces while re-
taining a sheaf-theoretic notion of smooth functions. Formally, they are locally ringed spaces modeled
on the zero loci of smooth functions in Euclidean space. In this way, they generalize the classical
extrinsic construction of manifolds. It’s important to note that the definition of a differentiable space
is not unique in the literature. Various frameworks, including those based on differentiable algebras,
sheaf-theoretic methods, and categorical approaches, offer distinct yet related definitions, all of which
seek to extend smooth manifolds to more general spaces while preserving a differentiable structure.
For further details, see [12], [17], and the references therein.

In the smooth category, all supermanifolds are globally split, a property that fails to hold in the
complex analytic category. This distinction is fundamentally rooted in the cohomology of sheaves.
Specifically, for any fine sheaf F over a topological space, the higher cohomology groups vanish:
Hi(M,F) = 0 for all i > 0 [22, Proposition 4.36]. A sheaf of modules over a sheaf of commutative rings
OM is defined as fine if OM admits partitions of unity [22, Proposition 4.35]. In the smooth setting,
the existence of smooth partitions of unity ensures that the relevant sheaves are fine, thereby guar-
anteeing the triviality of the obstructions to global splitting. Conversely, Batchelor’s Theorem does
not extend to the complex category because complex manifolds do not admit holomorphic partitions
of unity. Consequently, the associated sheaves are not fine, allowing for non-vanishing cohomology
groups that act as obstructions to a global split.
Given that C∞-superspaces constitute a category of smooth superspaces that properly contains
smooth supermanifolds, it is natural to investigate whether global splitness is a property unique to
the smooth supermanifold case. To delineate a broader class of C∞-superspaces susceptible to global
splitting, we isolate two sufficient conditions foundational to the proof of Batchelor’s Theorem (cf.
[14, Section 4.2]):
(1) Topological/Algebraic Condition: The structure sheaf must admit a partition of unity, ensuring
that the sheaf is fine and that higher cohomology groups vanish.
(2) Geometric Condition: The superspace must be locally split and possess a constant odd dimen-
sion.
