THE QUASI-LINEARITY PROBLEM FOR JORDAN BANACH
ALGEBRAS: A TOPOLOGICAL CHARACTERIZATION
GERARDO M. ESCOLANO
ABSTRACT. Let J be a JB∗-algebra with no quotients isomorphic to S2(C). Let
µ be a local quasi-linear Jordan functional on Jsa. We show that µ is a linear
functional on Jsa if and only if the restriction of µ to the closed unit ball of Jsa
is uniformly weakly continuous.
1. INTRODUCTION
The problem of quasi-linear mappings on C∗-algebras and von Neumann alge-
bras is a longstanding question in functional analysis. J. F. Aarnes first considered
this problem in [1, 2], acknowledging R. V. Kadison for bringing the question to his
attention, as a starting point to study whether a physical state on a unital C∗-algebra
A, i.e., a complex functional that acts as a positive linear functional of norm one on
each singly generated C∗-subalgebra, is necessarily linear on A. In its proper phys-
ical interpretation, this corresponds to the problem of the expectation functional on
the algebra of observables in quantum mechanics, c.f. Mackey [17]. In [1, 2] two
facts are noted: the first one that the answer is no, even for abelian C∗-algebras ; the
second one is that the problem was a special case of the famous Mackey-Gleason
problem for measures on the lattice of projections of a von Neumann algebra [12].
He then reformulated the question so that it remained meaningful in the physi-
cal context of observables, represented by self-adjoint elements of A, by defining
a quasi-state ρ to be a physical state with the further property that ρ(a + ib) =
ρ(a) + iρ(b) for every a,b ∈Asa, the set of self-adjoints elements of A. He then
proved then that any quasi-state is linear when A is abelian and that, under the ex-
tra assumption that ρ is continuous on A, ρ is linear for a quite large classes of
C∗-algebras.
This problem was closely related to the famous Mackey-Gleason problem for
von Neumann algebras, since it is a special case of this linearity problem for phys-
ical states. An outstanding result by L. J. Bunce and J. D. M. Wright in [6] and [7]
shows a positive solution to the Mackey-Gleason problem of signed measure on
the lattice of a von Neumann algebra without central summands of type I2. More-
over, in [8] the authors try to apply the new tools developed for this particular case

The foundational works [14] and [15] of Jordan, von Neumann, and Wigner sug-
gest that the most general mathematical framework for quantum mechanics is not
based on associative algebras, but rather on Jordan algebras. These structures arise
naturally from associative algebras through the symmetrization of the underlying
associative product.
The aim of this paper is to study quasi-linear functionals in the broader setting of
JB∗-algebras, which can be regarded as the Jordan analogues of C∗-algebras. Mo-
tivated by recent results on the Mackey-Gleason-Bunce-Wright problem obtained
in [10], it is natural to address the problem of quasi-linearity within the Jordan
framework. More precisely, our goal is to provide a topological characterization of
quasi-linear functionals in this setting, in the spirit of [8].
The first section of this paper is devoted to introducing the basic notions and
properties of Jordan algebras that will be needed in the sequel. In the second sec-
tion, we present the main result of this present work, Theorem 2.6.
1.1. Background.
This section is entirely devoted to recall some structure theory of JB∗-algebras
and JBW∗-algebras. We begin by recalling that a (real or complex) Jordan algebra
is a (real or complex) linear space J equipped with a bilinear mapping (a,b) →a◦b
(called the Jordan product) satisfying that
(J1) a◦b = b◦a, for all a,b ∈J;
(J2) a2 ◦(b◦a) = (a2 ◦b)◦a for all a,b ∈J (Jordan identity).
Moreover, if J is a (real or complex) Banach space satisfying
(J3) ∥a◦b∥≤∥b∥∥a∥, for all a,b ∈J,
J is called a (real or complex) Jordan Banach algebra. A Jordan algebra J is called
unital if there exists an element 1 ∈J (called the unit of J) such that 1◦a = a for
all a ∈J.
There are two closely related types of Jordan-Banach algebras, which are de-
fined by algebraic-geometric axioms, and are known as JB-algebras and JB∗-algebras.
A JB-algebra is a real Jordan-Banach algebra J in which the norm satisfies the fol-
lowing two additional conditions:
(JB1) ∥a2∥= ∥a∥2 for all a ∈J;
(JB2) ∥a2∥≤∥a2 +b2∥for all a,b ∈J.
The Jordan analogue to C∗-algebras is constituted by JB∗-algebras, a model in-
troduced by I. Kaplansky in 1976. A complex Jordan-Banach algebra J equipped
with an involution ∗is said to be a JB∗-algebra if the following axiom is satisfied:
(JB*1) ∥2a◦(a◦a∗)−a2 ◦a∗∥= ∥a∥3 for all a ∈J.
JB- and JB∗-algebras are mutually linked in the following way: the set Jsa of all
self-adjoint elements in a JB∗-algebra J, i.e. Jsa := {a ∈J : a∗= a}, is a JB-algebra

A JBW∗-algebra (resp., a JBW-algebra) is a JB∗-algebra (resp., a JB-algebra)
which is also a dual Banach space. Each JBW∗-algebra admits a unique (isometric)
predual (cf. [13, Theorem 4.4.16] or [5, Theorem 2.55]). Thus JBW∗-algebras can
be considered as the Jordan analogue of von Neumann algebras. It is known that a
JB∗-algebra J is a JBW∗-algebra if and only if Jsa is a JBW-algebra [9].
In [5, §2] and [9, Lemma 2.3], it is showed that the second dual of any JB∗-
algebra J, denoted by J∗∗, has the structure of a JBW∗-algebra under the Arens
product (see [9] for the definition).
For further information on the classical theory of Jordan–Banach algebras, the
reader is referred to [5], [10] and [13].
2. QUASI-LINEAR FUNCTIONALS ON JB*-ALGEBRAS
As it was said previously, positive quasi-linear functionals were originally in-
troduced by J. F. Aarnes to study the linearity of a physical state on an arbitrary
C∗-algebra. Our starting point then is the definition of what a quasi-linear func-
tional on the setting of JB∗-algebras is.
Definition 2.1. Let J be a JB∗-algebra where J stands for the self-adjoint part of
the algebra. A quasi-linear Jordan functional on J is a mapping µ : J →R such
that, whenever B is an associative Jordan subalgebra of µ, the restriction of µ to B
is linear. Furthermore, µ is bounded on the closed unit ball of J.
Observe now that given any quasi-linear Jordan functional µ on J, we can define
a functional µ : J →C such that µ(x+iy) = µ(x)+iµ(y) whenever x,y ∈J. Then,
µ will be linear on each maximal associative Jordan *-subalgebra of J. Neverthe-
less, under the hypothesis of the Definition 2.1, this extension is not unique, there
could exist ν : J →C such that ν is linear on each maximal associative Jordan
*-subalgebra of J, ν agrees with µ in J, but in general ν ̸= µ in J. We will see
that, under the hypothesis of Theorem 2.6, the uniqueness of the extension it is
recovered.
Remark 2.2. It is well known that M2(C), regarded as a unital C∗-algebra, exhibits
pathological behaviour with respect to quasi-linear functionals: there exist quasi-
linear functionals on M2(C) which fail to be linear [8].
Note that M2(C) itself is not a JB-algebra, but its self-adjoint part M2(C)sa,
equipped with the natural Jordan product a◦b = 1
2(ab+ba), is a canonical exam-
ple of a JB-algebra. In this setting, the same obstruction is already present. Indeed
same counterexamples work here.
This behaviour is not restricted to finite-dimensional cases. In the broader frame-
work of JBW∗-algebras, the same structural phenomenon persists: the existence of
quasi-linear functionals that are not linear is precisely linked to the presence of
direct summands of type I2. Conversely, as shown in [10] in the context of the
MGBW-problem, if a JBW∗-algebra J contains no direct summand of type I2, then
every quasi-linear functional on J is automatically linear.

For the purpose of the paper, we now present a weaker definition of quasi-
linearity.
Definition 2.3. Under the same hypothesis in Definition 2.1, a local quasi-linear
Jordan functional is a function µ : J →R such that, for each x ∈J, µ is linear on
the smallest norm-closed Jordan subalgebra of J containing x. Furthermore, µ is
bounded on the closed unit ball of J.
It is clear that each quasi-linear Jordan functional on J is a local quasi-linear
Jordan functional, although the converse is false, even in the associative case. The
counterexample given by J. F. Aarnes in [3] shows that there exist an abundance of
non-linear quasi-functional on C(X), where X is some compact Hausdorff space.
The construction remains valid in the Jordan setting since C(X), endowed with its
natural Jordan product, is a JB-algebra where every associative Jordan subalgebra
here coincides with the commutative subalgebras of C(X).
For this same reason, the result by C. A. Akemann and S. M. Newberger in [4]
also holds for associative JB-algebras, proving that every local quasi-linear Jordan
functional on an associative JB-algebras is a quasi-linear Jordan functional.

Lemma 2.5. Let J be a JB∗-algebra. If J has no quotient isomorphic to S2(C),
then the JBW∗-algebra M = J∗∗has no direct summands of type I2.
Proof. Assume that M has a direct summand of type I2. By the classical structure
theory of JBW∗-algebras, there exists a central projection z ∈P(M) such that
z ◦M ∼= S2(C). Let us consider the canonical projection π : M →z ◦M, which
is a surjective linear weak∗-continuous mapping. Since J is weak∗-dense in M by
Goldstine’s theorem, π(J) is also weak∗-dense in z◦M.
Observe that z◦M is finite-dimensional, since it is isomorphic to S2(C). Hence,
being weak∗-dense is equivalent to being dense with respect to the norm, which
implies that π(J) = z ◦M. Therefore, by the first isomorphism theorem, we get
that
J/kerπ ∼= π(J) = z◦M ∼= S2(C),
which is a contradiction.
□
