THE MINIMAL VOLUME OF STABLE SURFACES OF RANK ONE
JIHAO LIU AND WENFEI LIU
Abstract. We determine the minimal volume of a stable surface of rank one, and show that
the surface attaining this minimum is unique up to isomorphism. This resolves a conjecture of
Alexeev and the second author.
Of independent interest, the decisive step of the proof uses a plurigenus inequality re-derived
by an AI chatbot and applied as a pluricanonical filter; we further apply this filter to rule out
additional cases in the classification of small-volume threefolds of general type, and in Koll´ar’s
algebraic Montgomery–Yang problem. The underlying inequality has classical antecedents. To
our knowledge this is the first paper in birational geometry to claim a C2-level human–AI
collaboration in the sense of Feng et al., where the AI’s contribution is the recognition that this
inequality functions as the decisive pluricanonical filter in the basket analysis.

1. Introduction
We work over the field of complex numbers C. By a stable surface of rank one we mean
a projective surface X with semi-log canonical singularities, KX ample, and ρ(X) = 1; see
Definition 2.2. Throughout, “mld” stands for minimal log discrepancy (Definition 4.5, defined
for klt surface germs).

type — on automorphism groups, on the number of irreducible components of moduli spaces,
etc. — were raised already at the 1988 Taniguchi conference on birational geometry (see, e.g.,
G. Xiao’s problem list [Xia88]), and are part of the same circle of ideas.
(b) Bounds on volumes of klt surfaces of rank one. For projective klt surfaces with quotient
singularities, KX ample and ρ(X) = 1, Miyaoka [Miy84] bounded the number of quotient
singularities given the standard numerical invariants, while Alexeev [Ale94, Theorem 6.6] and
Alexeev–Mori [AM04, Theorem 1.1] proved that K2

constant. The bound supplied by [AM04] is, however, of the order 10−3·1010, very far from any
conjecturally optimal value. The problem of identifying the smallest possible value of K2
X in
this setting was explicitly raised by Koll´ar [Kol94, §6] (in connection with several related con-
jectures on log surfaces of general type, cf. also [Kol08, Problem 26]). The rank-one version
of the problem was made precise by Alexeev and the second author [AL19a], who based on an
explicit construction (recalled in Example 1.2 below) conjectured that the minimal volume of
a stable surface of rank one with quotient singularities is exactly 1/6351; this was confirmed
under the quite restrictive condition that the minimal resolution of the surface, together with
the exceptional divisors, maps into P2 with four lines ([AL19c]). Several refinements have ap-
peared since then, both in the surface case [LS23, Theorem 1.1], [Liu25, Theorem 1.2] and in
adjacent moduli problems [AAB24, ALS25]. Specific techniques drawn upon in the present pa-
per – classification of log canonical surface singularities (Alexeev [Ale92]), positivity of Euler
characteristics on singular surfaces (Blache [Bla94]), bounds on the number of singular points on
projective surfaces (Liu–Xie [LX25]), base-point-freeness for log canonical pairs (Fujino [Fuj09]),
and explicit complement-style estimates (J. Liu [Liu23]) – are used throughout the paper.
(c) Higher-dimensional and foliated analogues. In dimension ≥3, klt varieties of general type
with conjecturally minimal volume have been constructed by Totaro–Wang [TW23] and To-
taro [Tot24, Theorem 1.1]; the same authors with Esser have produced further explicit examples
of klt Calabi–Yau and general-type varieties with extreme volumes [ETW22, ETW23]. In the
threefold case, Birkar–Liu [BL23, Theorem 1.4] obtained the first explicit lower bounds for vol-
umes of stable threefolds of rank one and for related invariants, and Birkar–Lee [BL25a, BL25b]
have very recently used explicit birational geometry to derive bounds in the closely-related con-
text of 6-dimensional supergravity and elliptic Calabi–Yau threefolds. The local theory of mini-
mal log discrepancies on threefolds, on which much of the explicit machinery in dimension 3 rests,
has been developed by many authors, including Jiang [Jia21], Liu–Xiao [LX21], Liu–Luo [LL22],
Han–Liu [HL25], Kawakita [Kaw21, Kaw24, Kaw26]. For foliations, the analogous lower-volume
question (in the form of a positive minimum, or the DCC property for the set of volumes) goes
back to Pereira and was later formulated explicitly by Hacon–Langer [HL21, Question 1.1] for
foliated surfaces and by Cascini [Cas21, §5] in arbitrary dimension. On surfaces, partial progress
towards positivity and DCC of foliated volumes has been made by Spicer–Svaldi [SS23], Codogni–
Patakfalvi–Tasin [CPT25], and Han–Jiao–Li–Liu [HJLL24]; see also Spicer–Tasin [ST26] for very
recent work on the birational geometry of rank-one log canonical foliations. The general foliated
analogue remains open even in the surface case.
(d) Relation to the algebraic Montgomery–Yang problem. Koll´ar’s algebraic Montgomery–Yang
problem [Kol08, Conjecture 30] predicts that every klt rational surface X with ρ(X) = 1 and
π1(X\Sing(X)) = 0 has at most 3 singular points. Hwang–Keum [HK11a, HK11b, HK12, HK13,
HK14] reduced this conjecture to a finite computer-assisted check on 4-singularity surfaces, and
the most recent reduction [JPP25] leaves 16 values of p ≤50000 to be excluded. We will see
(Corollary 8.2 in §8) that our AI-rediscovered filter excludes 8 of those 16 remaining values.
The main result of this paper resolves the conjecture of Alexeev and the second author [AL19a].

2. Preliminaries
We adopt the standard notation and definitions in [Sho92, KM98, BCHM10] and will freely
use them.
Definition 2.1. A surface is a normal variety of dimension 2. A surface germ X ∋x consists
of a surface X and a closed point x ∈X. We say that X ∋x is smooth if X is smooth near x.
For any surface X and closed point x ∈X, if X ∋x is not smooth, then x is called a singularity
of X and we say that X ∋x is a surface singularity. The order of a surface germ X ∋x is the
order of the local fundamental group of X ∋x and is denoted by rx.
A Fano surface is a normal projective surface X such that −KX is ample. A klt Fano surface
is also called a log del Pezzo surface.

Definition 2.2. A projective surface X is called stable of rank one if X has semi-log canonical
(slc) singularities, KX is ample, and ρ(X) = 1. Throughout the paper, “rank one” is synonymous
with “Picard number one”. Following standard usage in the moduli theory of surfaces, “stable”
is the analog of KSBA-stability and only requires slc singularities and KX ample; in particular
a stable surface need not be normal. Nevertheless, our main theorem can be easily reduced to
the klt case via [LL23, Corollary 3.6] (cf. the discussion below Theorem 1.1).

8. Remarks on the formula re-discovered by AI
8.1. History of the formula. After the first version of this paper was posted to arXiv on May
7, 2026, four senior algebraic geometers – Hacon, C. Jiang, Koll´ar, and Totaro – independently
informed us that the inequality (6.55) of Lemma 6.18 has a substantial classical history that we
had not properly indicated in the first version of our paper. We summarize this history below
(and hope that we are not missing any earlier references).
The earliest reference we could find is [Har77, Lemma IV.5.5], which is stated only for curves
but whose proof works verbatim in any dimension. A key reference pointed out by the experts
is [ACGH85, p. 108], which states the lemma for finite-dimensional subspaces of a function
field and attributes the underlying argument to H. Hopf, calling it “one of the first applications
of topology to algebra.” The argument, in Hopf’s spirit, runs as follows (paraphrasing Totaro’s
exposition to us, for which we thank him). Given finite-dimensional C-linear subspaces A, B ⊂F
of a function field, multiplication of sections induces a continuous map
P(A) × P(B) −→P(AB),
([a], [b]) 7→[ab].
Writing u ∈H2(P(A); Z) and v ∈H2(P(B); Z) for the hyperplane classes pulled back from the
two factors, the hyperplane class on P(AB) pulls back to u + v. Since
(u + v)dim P(A)+dim P(B) ∈H∗(P(A) × P(B); Z)

explicit birational geometry seems particularly well suited for AI assistance. Output produced
by AI in mathematics is sometimes criticized for being “merely an optimization or a small

these objections apply to the present setting:
