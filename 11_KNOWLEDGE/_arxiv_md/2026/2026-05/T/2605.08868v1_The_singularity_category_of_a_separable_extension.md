THE SINGULARITY CATEGORY OF A SEPARABLE
EXTENSION
CHARALAMPOS VERASDANIS
Abstract. We prove that separable extensions of noetherian rings and finite
´etale morphisms of noetherian schemes give rise to separable extensions of
singularity categories.
1. Introduction
The theory of separable extensions in tensor-triangular geometry has received
great interest in the past couple of decades. Initiated by Balmer [2, 3] as a novel
technique of constructing new triangulated categories and demonstrated in com-
mutative algebra, it subsequently expanded to several equivariant contexts [7], to
modular representation theory [4, 6] and to algebraic geometry [5]. The general
idea was to uniformly capture naturally arising constructions that were beyond the
reach of the pre-existing theory (of localization for instance) by expressing them
as categories of modules over certain monads by establishing monadicity of ap-
propriate adjunctions. These include separable extensions of commutative rings,
separated ´etale morphisms of quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes and restric-
tion to subgroups, among others. More recent developments have also emerged in
the theory of ∞-categories [17, 20].
We expand the above list by incorporating two results concerning singularity cat-
egories (see Recollection 5.1) in analogy with the corresponding results [2, Theorem
6.5] and [5, Theorem 3.5] for derived categories.

Next we discuss separability of a monad and some of its consequences. Sepa-
rability is a key property under which the associated category of modules exhibits
compatibility with respect to the triangulated structure and, as we will see in the
sequel, the tensor structure.
Definition 2.5. A triangulated monad M : C →C is called separable if the mul-
tiplication µ: M 2 →M admits a section σ: M →M 2, meaning that µ ◦σ = IdM
(that we assume commutes with suspension) and Mµ ◦σM = σ ◦µ = µM ◦Mσ.
Theorem 2.6 ([2, Main Theorem 5.17]). With notation as in Recollection 2.4,
when M is separable and C is idempotent complete, the category ModC M inherits a
triangulated structure such that the functors FM and UM are triangulated functors.
The triangles of ModC M are precisely those triangles of modules whose underlying
diagram is a triangle in C. Further, there is an equivalence (FreeC M)♮≃ModC M.
If F ⊣G is an adjunction of triangulated functors that realizes M, then the com-
parison functor E : D →ModC M is a triangulated functor. If, moreover, D is
idempotent complete and the counit of the F ⊣G adjunction has a section, then E
is an equivalence of triangulated categories.
The last part of Theorem 2.6, regarding when the comparison functor is an
equivalence, has undergone a very useful improvement: It is enough to know that
the right adjoint G is conservative. The precise statement goes as follows.
Theorem 2.7 ([10]). Let C and D be idempotent complete triangulated categories
and F ⊣G an adjunction of triangulated functors that realizes a triangulated monad
M : C →C. If ModC M admits a triangulated structure that is compatible with FM
and UM and G is conservative, then the comparison functor E : D →ModC M is
an equivalence, which does not necessarily preserve the triangulated structures. If
the triangles of ModC M are precisely those whose underlying diagram is a triangle
in C, then E is a triangulated functor.

3. Ring objects
Let T = (T, ⊗, 1) be a tensor-triangulated category, i.e., a triangulated category
equipped with a symmetric tensor product that is a triangulated functor in both
variables. As in [9], we say that T is rigidly-compactly generated if T is compactly
generated, the tensor product preserves coproducts in both variables, the subcat-
egory Tc of compact objects is a tensor-triangulated subcategory, so 1 ∈Tc and
Tc ⊗Tc ⊆Tc, that coincides with the subcategory of rigid objects. We will not
assume that T is rigidly-compactly generated, unless stated explicitly.
A wide range of monads on a tensor-triangulated category is provided by ring
objects, referred to as monoids in most classical sources, e.g., [15].
Definition 3.1. A ring object in T is a triple A = (A, µ, η) that consists of an
object A ∈T and morphisms µ: A ⊗A →A and η: 1 →A such that the triple
(A ⊗−, µ ⊗−, η ⊗−) is a monad. A ring object A is called:
(i) commutative if µ ◦(1 2) = µ, where (1 2) is the swap of factors.
(ii) separable if µ: A⊗A →A has a section σ: A →A⊗A, with respect to which,
(A ⊗−, µ ⊗−, η ⊗−) is a separable monad.
Remark 3.2. At this point, an important distinction needs to be made. In the
definition of separable ring object, we require the multiplication map µ: A⊗A →A
to admit a section σ: A →A ⊗A that renders the corresponding monad separable.
If the monad A ⊗−is separable with section ξ : A ⊗−→A ⊗A ⊗−, then the
morphism ξ1 : A →A ⊗A is a section for µ: A ⊗A →A but it may not be the case
that ξA = ξ1 ⊗A and so, A may not be a separable ring object (at least not with
respect to ξ1); see [7, Remark 2.10] for further discussion.
Remark 3.3. If A ∈T is a commutative separable ring object and T is idem-
potent complete, then ModT A admits a tensor structure that renders it a tensor-
triangulated category such that the free module functor FA : T →ModT A is a
tensor-triangulated functor [3]. We will discuss this in more detail in Construc-
tion 4.1. When T is rigidly-compactly generated, ModT A is also rigidly-compactly
generated.

4. Monads via tensor-actions
From now on, we will tacitly assume that all categories involved are idempotent
complete. Let T be a tensor-triangulated category and let K be a triangulated
category. An action of T on K is a functor ∗: T × K →K that is triangulated in
each variable such that (X⊗Y )∗A ∼= X∗(Y ∗A) and 1∗A ∼= A, ∀X, Y ∈T, ∀A ∈K;
see [23] for more details. When T is rigidly-compactly generated and K is compactly
generated, we require the action to preserve coproducts in each variable.
