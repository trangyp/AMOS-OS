Introduction
A homotopy theory of (simple) graphs was introduced in [BKLW01, BL05,
BBdLL06], building on earlier ideas of Atkin [Atk74, Atk76]. The central invariant
of this theory is the A-group of a graph, denoted by An(G), which is an analogue
of the n-th homotopy group of a topological space. In [BBdLL06], it was conjec-
tured that An(G) is isomorphic to the n-th homotopy group of a certain topological
space. This conjecture was proved by Carranza and Kapulkin in [CK24], where they
constructed a functor to the category of cubical sets
N1 : Gra −→cSet
called the cubical nerve, and showed that An(G) ∼= πn(|N1G|).
Independently, a homotopy theory of digraphs (directed graphs) was introduced
in [GLMY23] and further developed in [LWYZ24, TWYZ25]. This theory is closely
related to the path homology theory, also known as the GLMY-theory, developed
earlier in [GLMY12]. This theory also includes an analogue of homotopy groups,
denoted by ¯πn(G). These groups extend A-groups to digraphs and also admit a
description in terms of cubical sets. For this reason we refer to these groups as
the cubical homotopy groups of G and denote them by An(G). Digraph maps that
induce isomorphisms on cubical homotopy groups are called cubical weak equiva-
lences.
In [CK26], Carranza and Kapulkin formulated the following discrete homotopy
hypothesis. Let Gra∞denote the (∞-categorical) localization of the category of
graphs Gra at the class of cubical weak equivalences. Then the cubical nerve induces
a functor to the ∞-category of spaces
N : Gra∞→Spc.
The discrete homotopy hypothesis asserts that this functor is an equivalence of
∞-categories. This conjecture remains open, although several partial results have
been obtained; see [KM24, Min26, CK26]. As explained in [CK26], the principal
obstruction to the original discrete homotopy hypothesis is that pushouts of graphs
admit a large number of non-degenerate cubes that are difficult to control. This
issue can be resolved by passing to the category of digraphs, where the presence
of directions imposes sufficient constraints to control the newly generated cubes
arising in pushouts.
Therefore, in this paper, we study an analogue of the discrete homotopy hypoth-
esis for digraphs. First we extend the results of [CK24] to the setting of digraphs.
In particular, we construct the cubical nerve functor N1 : DGra →cSet and prove
that An(G) ∼= πn(|N1G|). Let DGra∞denote the ∞-category obtained from DGra
by localizing at the class of cubical weak equivalences. Then the functor N1 induces
a functor to the ∞-category of spaces N : DGra∞→Spc. Our main result is the
following.
Theorem (Th. 5.5.3). The functor N is an equivalence of ∞-categories
DGra∞≃Spc.
As a corollary we obtain that for every pointed space X there exists a pointed
digraph G such that A∗(G) ∼= π∗(X).
We further describe finite limits and small colimits in DGra∞by equipping DGra
with structures of a category of fibrant objects and a homotopy cocomplete category

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 1, we develop a homotopy theory of
digraphs, where homotopies are defined by digraph maps G⊗I1 →H, and introduce
the cubical homotopy groups A∗(G). We consider the category of shrinkings Shr,
whose objects are intervals and morphisms are shrinkings. We define a notion of a
nice subcategory of shrinkings S ⊆Shr and show that A∗(G) admits an equivalent
definition in terms of any such S. This provides a unified framework encompassing
several approaches in the literature. We also define the path and loop digraphs PSG
and ΩSG respectively, depending on S, and prove that they satisfy the expected
properties whenever S is cofiltered.
In Section 2, generalizing the ideas of [CK24], for any m ≥1, we introduce a
functor Nm : DGra →cSet, called cubical m-nerve. For any digraph G, we consider
the colimit of a sequence
N1G →N2G →N4G →N6G →. . .
that defines a functor N∞: DGra →cSet. For technical reasons specific to the
directed setting, it is convenient to work with the sequence starting at N1G and
then passing through the even-indexed terms N2mG.
We extend the results of
[CK24] by proving that all maps in this sequence are anodyne, and N∞G is a Kan
complex, and A∗(G) ∼= π∗(N∞G). In particular, the functors N1, N2m, and N∞
induce the same functor of ∞-categories
N : DGra∞→Spc.
Furthermore, we show that NG can be described as a mapping space from the
one-point digraph in the ∞-category DGra∞:
NG ∼= DGra∞(∗, G).
In Section 3, we introduce the notion of a cubical fibration of digraphs and
show that DGra, together with the classes of cubical weak equivalences and cubical
fibrations, forms a category of fibrant objects. This framework allows us to describe
finite limits in DGra∞and to prove the long exact sequence of cubical homotopy
groups. To illustrate the notion of cubical fibration, we demonstrate that the 2-
coverings introduced in [DIMZ24] are cubical fibrations.
In Section 4, we introduce the notion of an in-closed subdigraph and prove a
nerve theorem for covers of a digraph by in-closed subdigraphs. This provides a
useful tool for computing the homotopy type of NG for certain classes of digraphs
G.
In Section 5, we introduce the notion of an in-closed cofibration and show that the
category DGra, equipped with the classes of cubical weak equivalences and in-closed
cofibrations, is a homotopy cocomplete category of cofibrant objects. Moreover, we
prove that N1 : DGra →cSet is a homotopy cocontinuous functor. Consequently,
DGra∞admits small colimits and N : DGra∞→Spc is a colimit-preserving functor.
By the universal property of the ∞-category of spaces Spc, we obtain a colimit-
preserving functor
R : Spc −→DGra∞
satisfying R(∗) = ∗. We then show that R and N are mutually inverse equivalences.

1. Cubical homotopy groups
This section provides a preliminary overview of the ordinary homotopy of di-
graphs, where homotopy is defined as a map G ⊗I1 →H. We define the category
of digraphs DGra, its homotopy category DGra/ ∼, the cubical homotopy groups
A∗(G), and the path and loop digraphs. While versions of this theory appear in the
literature (see [CK24, GLMY23, LWYZ24]), our primary contribution introduces
the concept of a nice subcategory of shrinkings S ⊆Shr, where Shr is the category
of intervals and their “shrinkings”. We show that the cubical homotopy groups
A∗(G) can be defined using any nice subcategory of shrinkings S, yielding many
equivalent definitions. We also define the path digraph PSG and the loop digraph
ΩSG relative to a specific S. We prove that these digraphs satisfy all expected
properties provided that S is cofiltered.

w : x →x′ followed by a fibration f : x′ →y.

5. Homotopy colimits
In this section, we describe colimits of the ∞-category DGra∞by introducing a
