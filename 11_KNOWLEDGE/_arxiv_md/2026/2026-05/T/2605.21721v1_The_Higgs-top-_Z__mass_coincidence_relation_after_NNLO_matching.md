Abstract
The relation M2
H ≃MZMt, previously proposed as a Higgs mass coincidence, is reconsidered with present electroweak inputs
and with a scheme-consistent matching analysis. With the 2025 PDG values for MZ, MW and MH, and the ATLAS–CMS direct
top-mass combination, the pole-level ratio is ρZt = MZMt/M2

predicts either MH = 125.426 ± 0.120 GeV or Mt = 171.898 ± 0.302 GeV, which is still a 1.4σ test rather than an exclusion.

4. Symmetry targets
The matching result constrains possible interpretations. A
literal unbroken SM symmetry cannot enforce Eq. (8), because
λ, yt and gZ are independent renormalized couplings and their
beta functions do not preserve gZyt/λ as an invariant. The one-
loop equations already show this: yt is strongly affected by
QCD, whereas λ is driven by a competition between y4
t and
electroweak terms. This is consistent with the standard near-
criticality picture of the Higgs potential [10, 11].
A viable explanation must therefore be more structured.
Three possibilities are suggested by the numbers.
First, one may take the pole-level equation (1) as the primary
relation. Then the symmetry would not be a symmetry of the
MS Lagrangian parameters themselves, but a statement about
physical thresholds. In this picture the 3–4% finite factor in
Eq. (19) is not a problem; it is part of the symmetry-breaking
map from running couplings to pole observables. The falsifi-
able prediction is then Mt = 171.90 ± 0.30 GeV in the pole
convention.
Second, one may postulate a broken top-Higgs custodial
extension. Ordinary custodial S U(2) explains why the elec-
troweak vector masses are organized by MW/MZ up to hyper-
charge breaking, but it does not relate the scalar quartic to the
top Yukawa coupling [12]. A larger spurionic symmetry could
assign gZ, yt and λ to a common top-Higgs sector and enforce

Third, a triality-like nonlinear symmetry could relate the
heaviest scalar, fermion and neutral vector sectors only after
electroweak symmetry breaking. Such a symmetry cannot com-
mute with the unbroken Lorentz and gauge structure in a con-
ventional linear way; it would have to be realized through com-
posite, pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone or threshold degrees of free-
dom.
Little-Higgs and composite-Higgs constructions show
how quartics can be induced by gauge and top interactions, but
they do not generically produce Eq. (20) without additional dy-
namics [13, 14]. The present calculation fixes the additional
dynamics required: it must account for a few-percent matching
factor while leaving the pole-level relation at the per-mille-to-
percent level.

5. Conclusions
The old Higgs mass coincidence has split into three sharply
different statements.
The pole-level geometric relation M2
H ≃MZMt remains nu-
merically interesting. It predicts Mt = 171.898 ± 0.302 GeV
from MH and MZ, and its present difference from the direct
ATLAS–CMS average is about 1.4σ before adding any top-
mass scheme ambiguity.
The arithmetic relation 2MH ≃MW + Mt should not be
used as an exact symmetry condition. With present data it is
a percent-level mnemonic, not a valid mass sum rule.
The exact MS boundary condition λ = gZyt/(4
√
2) at µ =
Mt is excluded by the NNLO matching evaluation. It predicts
MH ≃123.19 GeV or Mt ≃177.81 GeV, depending on which
mass is solved for. Therefore a viable theoretical explanation
