THE PRO-ÉTALE FUNDAMENTAL GROUP OF SINGULAR SCHEMES
JIU-KANG YU AND LEI ZHANG
With an appendix by Marcin Lara
Abstract. We compute the pro-étale fundamental group of a connected Nagata J-2
scheme in terms of the étale fundamental groups of the normalizations of its irreducible
components and a discrete free group. The result generalizes a formula of E. Lavanda
for semi-stable curves and relies on a combination of proper descent techniques for
étale morphisms and a combinatorial van Kampen construction for Noohi groups. As
a by-product we characterize when a continuous representation of the pro-étale funda-
mental group factors through a discrete quotient.

Our method relies on two geometric tools: (1) the proper descent theorem for geometric
covers due to Grothendieck and Rydh (Lemma 2.4), which allows one to glue geometric
covers along closed subschemes; (2) a van Kampen machine for Noohi groups (Propo-
sition 1.10) that translates the geometric gluing into an algebraic presentation of the
fundamental group. This approach does not depend on the characteristic, and reduces the
computation of πproét
1
pXq to an induction on the number of connected components and the
dimension of the singular locus. The result is a description of the pro-étale fundamental
group entirely in terms of the much better understood étale fundamental groups of normal
schemes and discrete free groups.

Appendix A. Comparison with other van Kampen formulas
by Marcin Lara
The goal of this appendix is to explain the relation of the formula presented in this
article to another general version of a van Kampen formula for πproét
1
that appeared in [6]
(and [7]).
This latter result follows the presentation of [12], generalizing it from the profinite group
πét

. In its most general form, [6, Corollary 3.19], it gives, for
any morphism of effective descent f : X1 Ñ X, a formula for πproét
1
pX, xq in terms of
fundamental groups of connected components of X1, a discrete fundamental group of a
certain “dual graph”, and relations coming from further fiber products.
Sometimes, this extra flexibility is useful, e.g.
taking f to be an alteration (in the
sense of de Jong) or an étale cover. But, as the groups πproét
1
pX, xq and πét
1 pX, xq match
for normal X, the most relevant case for capturing the “novel phenomena” coming from
working with πproét

is when f : X1 Ñ X is the normalization morphism. In this case, the
formula takes the more concrete form spelled out in [6, Remark 3.21]. In this form, the
formulas (the one in loc. cit. and the one described in Theorem I and II of the present
article) become closer to each other.
The main difference is the following: when the assumptions of Theorem II are satisfied,
[6, Remark 3.21] often produces extra generators in the discrete part. Those are then killed
by the relations (of course, as the resulting group is the same), but this still makes the
computation somewhat cumbersome. For actually computing the pro-étale fundamental
group (in terms of some πét

more straightforward.
