Abstract. The interpretation of the SN1987A neutrino data continues to be hindered by
significant absolute timing uncertainties and a well-known tension in the angular distributions.
We perform a quantitative, high-precision alignment of the Kamiokande-II and Baksan data
with the IMB clock, using for the first time the relative time offset (RTO) and reducing the
temporal uncertainty by two orders of magnitude to the sub-second level. The χ2-analysis
shows that Baksan’s absolute timestamps require an advancement of 30.4 s, while those of
Kamiokande-II require a delay of about 6.4 s. The knowledge of the unified timeline provides
a necessary and rigorous basis for testing whether the first Kamiokande-II event arose from
the neutronization burst — a possibility motivated by its angular properties. Our analysis
favors an accretion phase ¯νe origin over a neutronization-burst origin, with a likelihood ratio
of 3–6, depending on the specific MSW oscillation scenario.
This result corroborates the
standard interpretation—that only inverse beta decay events were detected. Our framework
yields the most stringent constraints to date on the SN1987A chronology and establishes a
precision benchmark for future Galactic supernova observations.

Introduction
On 23 February 1987, three neutrino detectors (Kamiokande-II, IMB, Baksan) recorded a few
dozen events [1–5] which appeared to be produced in a common time interval.
These are the first neutrinos that can be attributed with certainty to an extrasolar
source, the supernova SN1987A, and after so long they remain the only ones of this type.
They have been the focus of sustained investigations, as they represent a crucial diagnostic
tool of the extreme environment of a supernova.
The knowledge of the precise timing of the detected events and the nature of the interactions
in detectors are crucial and necessary steps to map the sequence of processes of the explosion
from the onset, such as the neutronization burst, accretion phase, and cooling of the proto-
neutron star. An accurate time distribution of the events can provide important information
on the neutrino flux, luminosity, and energy spectrum, whereas mis-timed events can distort
their estimates.
Moreover, understanding the temporal sequence of neutrinos enables correlations with
other signals, such as electromagnetic or gravitational waves, namely multimessenger astro-
physics.
The temporal alignment of the events recorded by the three neutrino detectors has long
remained an open issue, despite significant advances in neutrino physics, supernova dynamics,
and statistical methods over the past two decades. While internal relative timing was robust,
absolute UT offsets for Kamiokande-II and Baksan reached uncertainties of O(1min), blurring
the global collapse sequence [6]. In this work, we present the first high-precision estimate of
the absolute arrival times at the three detectors by performing a joint likelihood analysis. Our
approach integrates the full multi-dimensional information of each event – arrival time, energy,
and direction – while accounting for detector-specific response functions, their background

Another significant challenge concerns the nature of the detected events. Owing to its large

To determine whether there

A probability of ∼30% has been estimated for K1 being an ES event, based exclusively on
angular and energy measurements, and considering only the accretion+cooling phase [12].
There was also a suggestion of a substantial bias in the IMB data [13]. This remains the
major unresolved issue when interpreting the SN1987A data as IBD events [14], and it is a
powerful motivation for further investigation.
An exciting possibility that has so far escaped attention involves again the time distribution:
K1 could be an ES event linked to the early and intense neutronization burst emission and due
to the p + e →n + νe processes that occur at the onset of the collapse. Based on conservative
assumptions, we discuss which neutrino emission phase K1 is most likely to belong to.

2.2
Offset times
We formalize the temporal alignment of the SN1987A datasets by distinguishing between the
Intrinsic Timing Offset (ITO) and the Relative Timing Offset (RTO).
• The ITOs (ti, tk, tb) account for the stochastic delay inherent to discrete sampling of the
initial neutrino flux at each of the three detectors (IMB, Kamiokande-II and Baksan)
and are routinely estimated in contemporary emission models [14, 16–19].
• We introduce for the first time the RTOs ∆tk ≡tk −ti and ∆tb ≡tb −ti to quantify
the timing misalignment between independent experiments.
The RTO is evaluated by means of a likelihood analysis to align the Kamiokande-II and
Baksan clocks to the IMB reference, assuming a common SN1987A event. As demonstrated
in figure 5 of ref. [14], the statistical distribution of the ITOs parameters (called delay times in
that paper) is strongly non-Gaussian. Therefore, a rigorous statistical procedure is required
to combine the ITOs to obtain the RTOs, and above all to reliably estimate their uncertainty
intervals.
We proceed as follows. Marginalizing the likelihood over the astrophysical parameters
at tmax = 100 ms, we can derive L = L(ti, tk, tb); these parameters, as shown in [14], are not
correlated with the astrophysical parameters given in eq. 2.5. We obtain the values of the

3
The elastic scattering hypothesis
The event K1 is of particular interest. It is the most suitable event to originate from ES
rather than IBD [7, 10]. The above discussion on the absolute times has shown that K1 is
likely the first among all the events, raising a new possibility: that it was an ES event caused
by a neutrino produced before the accretion and cooling phases, during the neutronization
phase (NP). Thus, K1 could correspond to the first (and only) supernova neutrino detected.
If K1 originated from NP, its temporal location would be more precise, as the emission rises
quickly and lasts around 10 ms only. Given the robustness of the relative times, the timing
accuracy of all Kamiokande-II events would improve.
In light of the large impact on the reconstruction of the SN1987A timeline and on the
understanding of its early phase, we proceed to quantitatively evaluate this hypothesis with

3.3
Results and discussion
The temporal profile of the expected event rates, shown in figure 3, summarizes our discussion.
Based on the measured energy and angular information of the event K1, we obtain a maximum
likelihood for an ES origin during the NP of 0.042 for normal mass ordering and 0.10 for
inverted mass ordering according to the MSW theory [30]. The corresponding likelihood for
the IBD hypothesis is found to be 0.27 while the background is negligible.
Although there is a non-negligible likelihood that the K1 event has an ES origin, the
values of the likelihoods indicate that the IBD channel is 3–6 times more likely. The ES
contribution remains subdominant, especially when the mass spectrum is normal, since the
survival probability Pee is small due to the adiabatic conversion to the mass eigenstate νe →
ν3 [30] and the cross section of νµ and ντ is six times smaller than that of νe. This conclusion
could be overturned only if the NP had larger Lmax, ¯ϵ and/or if speculative (and at present
unexpected) new oscillation effects would produce Pee ∼1.

alignment enables a rigorous and quantitative characterization of the physical mechanisms
driving individual events.
Specifically, we addressed the hypothesis that the initial Kamiokande-II event originated
from electron elastic scattering during the NP—a scenario motivated by its observed angu-
lar distribution. However, our quantitative likelihood analysis favors an inverse beta decay
origin in the accretion phase, with a likelihood ratio of 3–6, depending on the specific MSW
oscillation scenario. These results, barring unexpected scenarios, reinforce the standard inter-
pretation, where all detected neutrinos events are attributed to IBD electron antineutrinos.
Our quantitative procedure can be applied to other model — for example, models of SN1987A
emission from numerical calculations, such as [6].
These results demonstrate that even with the sparse but unique data of SN1987A, sub-
dominant signals can be quantitatively constrained through a precise treatment of relative
timing. For a future Galactic supernova, with significantly higher statistics, the likelihood
analysis of the angular distribution presented here will be crucial for the unambiguous iden-
tification of the neutronization burst and the achievement of millisecond timing precision.
Such a detection would offer an unprecedented window into the early stages of gravitational
collapse and strengthen multi-messenger connections with gravitational-wave and electromag-
netic signals.

Detector positions and geometry
The neutrinos propagated through the Earth’s interior before reaching the detectors, as the
SN1987A progenitor was located in the Southern Sky and all three detectors were situated in
the Northern Hemisphere.
We adopt the IMB time and location as a global reference. The distance traveled within
the Earth for each site d is given by Ld = −2R⟨⃗nd,⃗n∗⟩, where R = 6371 km is the Earth’s
mean radius, see figure 4. In geocentric celestial coordinates, any unit vector is defined as
⃗n = (cos δ cos α, cos δ sin α, sin δ), where δ is the declination and α is the Right Ascension
(RA). For the detector unit vectors ⃗nd, the declination corresponds to the geographic latitude
λ, while the RA is derived from the Local Sidereal Time (LST) at the moment of detection
