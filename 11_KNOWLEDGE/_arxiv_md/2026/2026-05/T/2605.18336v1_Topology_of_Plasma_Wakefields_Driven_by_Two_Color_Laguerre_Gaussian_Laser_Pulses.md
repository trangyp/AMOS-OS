Abstract
Plasma wakefield excitation driven by two-color Laguerre–Gaussian laser pulses carrying orbital angular
momentum is investigated analytically and through quasi-cylindrical particle-in-cell simulations. Using a
perturbative framework together with the quasistatic approximation, the influence of the transverse laser
mode structure on the longitudinal and transverse wakefields in an underdense plasma is examined in the
weakly relativistic regime. The results show that drivers with finite azimuthal index produce reduced and
less regular on-axis longitudinal wakefields compared to conventional Gaussian drivers. However, radial–
longitudinal field distributions reveal that this reduction originates from a redistribution of the wakefield
energy toward finite radii rather than a simple loss of wake excitation. Orbital-angular-momentum-
carrying modes generate hollow and ring-shaped wake structures accompanied by strongly modified
transverse electric fields and broader plasma density perturbations. Mixed Gaussian–Laguerre–Gaussian
configurations exhibit intermediate behavior, combining weak on-axis acceleration with pronounced off-axis
wake excitation. The study demonstrates that structured two-color laser drivers fundamentally modify
the topology of plasma wakefields and provide an additional mechanism for controlling transverse plasma
dynamics, off-axis acceleration, and angular-momentum-mediated wakefield structures in plasma-based
accelerator schemes.

Introduction
Laser–plasma interaction has been a subject of intense research due to its applications ranging from terahertz
and harmonic generation to compact X-ray sources and advanced fusion concepts [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]. One of the
most promising applications is laser wakefield acceleration (LWFA), first proposed by Tajima and Dawson
[6], which utilizes the plasma waves excited by intense laser pulses to accelerate electrons. Plasma-based
accelerators provide acceleration gradients far exceeding those of conventional accelerators, which are limited
by material breakdown [7]. Theoretical and numerical studies [8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13], together with experiments
[9, 14, 15, 16], have demonstrated the potential of LWFA to generate multi-hundred-MeV to GeV-level electron
beams over centimeter-scale distances.
Several modifications of the LWFA scheme have been proposed to enhance the amplitude and controllability
of wakefields. One such approach is the use of two-color laser pulses, in which a fundamental laser pulse is
combined with its harmonic. The asymmetric ponderomotive force produced in this case leads to stronger
plasma-wave excitation and reduced electron-injection thresholds compared to single-color drivers. Analytical
and simulation studies [17, 18] have shown that wake amplitude and phase velocity strongly depend on the
phase difference and relative intensities of the two pulses. Experimental evidence further confirms improved
wake excitation and enhanced trapping efficiency in two-color configurations [19].
Another promising direction involves the use of twisted laser beams, or optical vortices, carrying orbital
angular momentum (OAM). Such beams, characterized by an azimuthal phase dependence of the form
exp(iℓϕ), are capable of transferring OAM to plasma particles and collective modes. This results in helical
plasma waves, azimuthal currents, and wakefields with nontrivial spatial structure [20, 21]. Simulation and
theoretical studies [22, 23, 27, 30] have predicted that twisted laser drivers can imprint helical features on
accelerated electron bunches, enabling new possibilities for structured particle beams and vortex radiation
sources. Optical control of wake topology has also been proposed as an all-optical method to manipulate
the wake phase and structure [28]. More recently, advanced simulations [24], theoretical models [25], and
comprehensive reviews [26, 29] have highlighted the potential of vortex beams for producing ultrashort,
low-divergence, and high-charge electron bunches, as well as OAM-rich secondary radiation. These works
underline the growing importance of twisted laser pulses as drivers for next-generation wakefield accelerators.
The present work combines these two approaches by undertaking a theoretical and simulation study of
wakefield generation using two-color twisted laser pulses in underdense plasma. An analytical framework
is developed, supported by numerical evaluation, to describe how frequency mixing and orbital angular
momentum jointly influence the amplitude and structure of the excited wakefields. The dependence of
wakefield strength on topological charge, interpulse phase, and intensity ratio is systematically analyzed.

Graphical Analysis
Equation (9) represents the longitudinal wakefield generated by two co-propagating Laguerre–Gaussian (LG)
laser pulses interacting with a homogeneous plasma. In the present analysis, the normalized vector potential
of the driving pulses is taken as a0 ≪1, corresponding to the weakly relativistic regime.
Figure 1 illustrates the longitudinal wakefield generated behind two co-propagating LG laser drivers. The
plasma parameters correspond to an underdense plasma with a plasma wavelength of approximately λp,
which determines the characteristic oscillation scale of the excited wakefield. The laser wavelength is chosen
as λ, and the pulse lengths of the two drivers are chosen to be close but slightly different to represent the
two-color configuration.
Figure 1 shows that the longitudinal wakefield oscillates periodically along the propagation direction
with a period determined by the plasma wavelength, indicating that the intrinsic plasma response governs
the longitudinal structure of the wake. When both driving pulses correspond to the fundamental Gaussian
configuration, the intensity distribution is strongly localized around the axis, resulting in the most efficient
ponderomotive forcing of plasma electrons. Consequently, the graphical plots show the largest wakefield
amplitudes and a well-defined oscillatory pattern along the propagation direction.
Introducing twisted LG modes changes the transverse intensity profile from an on-axis maximum to a
hollow distribution associated with orbital angular momentum (OAM). Consequently, the ponderomotive
force acting on plasma electrons near the axis is reduced, and the wake excitation is displaced away from the
beam center. The trends shown in Fig. 1 therefore indicate a gradual decrease in the peak on-axis wakefield
amplitude as the azimuthal mode index ℓincreases. This behavior supports the physical picture that the
laser energy is redistributed radially rather than concentrated on the propagation axis.
In the two-color configuration considered here, the total wakefield is produced by the combined action of
the individual pulses and their nonlinear coupling. Because the two pulses have slightly different frequencies,
their superposition forms a beat pattern that modulates the effective driving force on the plasma. When
the frequency separation approaches the natural plasma frequency ωp, the coupling becomes stronger and
the excited wakefield amplitude increases. The magnitude of this enhancement, however, depends on the
transverse overlap of the LG modes. Pulses with similar transverse structures couple more efficiently and
produce a larger wake response, whereas mismatched transverse modes reduce the spatial overlap and weaken
the plasma response.
Overall, the graphical trends predicted by Eq. (9) show that the wakefield amplitude in a two-color
LG system is governed by the interplay between resonant two-frequency coupling and the OAM-induced
redistribution of laser intensity. For the parameters used here, Gaussian drivers generate the strongest on-axis
wakefields, while twisted modes produce weaker but spatially structured wakes that extend away from the
propagation axis. This behavior agrees with the simulation results and confirms that structured laser modes
modify the spatial topology of plasma wake excitation rather than its fundamental oscillation scale.

Results and Discussion
The simulations were performed using the quasi-cylindrical particle-in-cell code FBPIC, which employs an
azimuthal Fourier decomposition of the electromagnetic fields. The computational domain was defined in
cylindrical coordinates (r, z) and evolved in a moving window propagating along the +z direction at the speed
of light c. The longitudinal extent of the simulation box was z ∈[−10 µm, 70 µm], discretized with Nz = 800
grid cells, corresponding to a longitudinal resolution ∆z = 0.1 µm. The radial extent was r ∈[0, 25 µm],
resolved with Nr = 50 cells, giving a radial resolution ∆r = 0.5 µm. The electromagnetic fields were expanded
using Nm = 2 azimuthal modes (m = 0, 1), sufficient to capture both the axisymmetric plasma wakefields
and the laser polarization structure. Open boundary conditions were applied in both the longitudinal and
radial directions.
The simulation time step was chosen as ∆t = ∆z/c, satisfying the Courant stability condition. The
plasma consisted of pre-ionized electrons with a peak density ne = 4.96×1024 m−3, while ions were treated as
a stationary neutralizing background. The plasma occupied the region z ∈[70 µm, 300 µm] and r ≤22 µm,
with a linear density up-ramp of length 80 µm starting at z = 70 µm, followed by a uniform-density plateau
and a sharp cutoff at z = 300 µm. Electrons were initialized with 2 × 2 × 4 macroparticles per cell in the
(z, r, θ) directions, respectively.
Two co-propagating laser pulses with different central wavelengths were initialized at the entrance of
the moving window. Both pulses had Laguerre-Gaussian transverse profiles with mode indices (p, m) and

Conclusion
The present work combines analytical modeling and quasi-cylindrical particle-in-cell simulations to investigate
plasma wakefield excitation driven by two-color Laguerre–Gaussian laser pulses carrying orbital angular
momentum. Using a perturbative framework together with the quasistatic approximation, the longitudinal
and transverse wakefields generated in an underdense homogeneous plasma are analyzed in the weakly
relativistic regime. The results show that, unlike conventional Gaussian drivers, OAM-carrying laser pulses
redistribute the wakefield energy from the propagation axis toward finite radii, producing hollow and ring-like
wake structures accompanied by strongly modified transverse electric fields and structured plasma density
perturbations.
The simulations further demonstrate that the wakefield topology depends strongly on the radial and
azimuthal mode indices of the interacting laser pulses. Gaussian-dominated configurations generate the
strongest and most coherent on-axis accelerating fields together with comparatively smooth density com-
pression, whereas twisted LG modes produce weaker on-axis wakefields, broader density modulation, and
pronounced off-axis accelerating regions. Mixed Gaussian–LG configurations exhibit intermediate behavior,
combining residual on-axis acceleration with structured transverse wake excitation. These results confirm
that orbital angular momentum modifies the spatial topology of plasma wake generation rather than simply
reducing the coupling efficiency.
Minor differences between analytical and simulation results arise primarily from the approximations
employed in the analytical treatment, including the quasistatic and weakly nonlinear assumptions, whereas
the simulations self-consistently include kinetic effects, nonlinear plasma dynamics, diffraction, and mode
coupling. Overall, the present study demonstrates that structured two-color LG laser pulses provide an
additional degree of control over plasma wake excitation, offering possibilities for tailored wakefield topology,
off-axis acceleration, transverse phase-space manipulation, and angular-momentum-mediated plasma dynamics
relevant to advanced plasma-based accelerator concepts.
