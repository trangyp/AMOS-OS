hard rods: Wertheim’s ﬁrst-order thermodynamic perturbation theory versus
exact results

We investigate the thermodynamic and structural properties of divalent patchy hard rods conﬁned to a one-
dimensional channel by modeling the bonding sites as attractive square-well (SW) patches located at the rod
tips. The zero-range sticky limit is recovered by letting the well width vanish while keeping the stickiness
parameter ﬁnite. While Wertheim’s ﬁrst-order thermodynamic perturbation theory (TPT1) becomes exact in
this sticky limit, it fails for ﬁnite-range site-site interactions. We show that the theory can be made exact in
one dimension by replacing the standard law of mass action with an exact relation between the density and
the fraction of unbonded sites, together with an exact bonding free-energy contribution. Finite-range SW
sites produce a richer structural behavior than sticky sites, including monotonic and oscillatory asymptotic
decay of the pair correlation function, separated by the Fisher–Widom line. In the monotonic regime, the
correlation length exhibits an absolute maximum deﬁning the Widom line, while in the oscillatory regime
it may display a local maximum and minimum, whose locus deﬁnes the “Extrema of the Correlation length
under Oscillatory decay” (ECO) line. These features disappear in the sticky limit, where the system remains
entirely in the oscillatory regime. We also show that the high-pressure behavior of the correlation length
changes from ξ ∼p2 for ﬁnite-range SW sites to ξ ∼p3 in the sticky limit.
I.
INTRODUCTION
In low-valence patchy colloidal systems, restricted co-
ordination numbers dictate the emergence of distinct hi-
erarchical architectures.1–3 These morphologies include
a wide variety of structures, ranging from ﬂexible lin-
ear chains and branched clusters to rings and intricate
network topologies.4,5 Although the constituent build-
ing blocks are relatively simple, the resulting collec-
tive behavior is governed by a subtle interplay of direc-
tional interactions arising from the spatial arrangement
of patches on the particle surface.6,7 This complexity is
further enhanced when the particle core is anisotropic—
for instance, rod- or disk-shaped—since the competi-
tion between directional repulsive and attractive forces
leads to a richer structural and phase behavior.8,9 Con-
sequently, a rigorous theoretical description is required
to achieve predictive insight into equilibrium structures
and to elucidate how patch geometry, particle shape,
and interaction range determine the formation of com-
plex self-assembled phases.10–13
Divalent patchy particles constitute a fundamental
class of building blocks for the self-assembly of low-
valence colloidal and polymeric systems.14–16 Charac-
terized by two bonding sites, they provide a direct link

the theory in describing chain formation.
In this work, we show that the behavior of a 1D ﬂuid
of hard rods with two attractive patches exhibits a sur-
prisingly rich phenomenology. At low and intermediate
densities, attractive interactions dominate, whereas re-
pulsive hard-core interactions control the behavior near
close packing.
By modeling the patches as SW sites,
we consider both the zero-range sticky limit and ﬁnite-
range interactions. We demonstrate that these two cases
display markedly different behavior, particularly in the
high-density regime near close packing. Speciﬁcally: (i)
the equation of state of hard rods with SW sites evolves
from a sticky-like to a hard-rod-like form as the pressure
increases; (ii) the Widom and Fisher–Widom (FW) lines
arise for ﬁnite-range interactions but are absent in the
sticky limit; and (iii) the correlation length diverges as p2
for SW interactions, whereas it scales as p3 in the sticky
case, where p is the longitudinal pressure. These differ-
ences persist across both intermediate and high densi-
ties. At intermediate densities, they are primarily sig-
naled by the presence or absence of the Widom and FW
lines, while at high densities they are reﬂected in the
pressure ratio relative to the reference hard-rod ﬂuid,
the behavior of the correlation length, and the emer-
gence of a novel structural line, hereafter referred to as
the “Extrema of the Correlation length under Oscillatory
decay” (ECO) line.
Furthermore, we show that Wertheim’s association
theory is exact for hard rods with zero-range sticky sites,
but only approximate for ﬁnite-range SW interactions.
By exploiting the exact solution, we introduce modiﬁca-
tions to both the bonding free-energy contribution and
the law of mass action, rendering this modiﬁed associa-
tion theory exact also for ﬁnite-range SW sites.

Section II introduces the zero-range sticky and ﬁnite-

II.
HARD RODS DECORATED WITH SW SITES
We study the thermodynamic behavior and structural
properties of divalent patchy hard rods conﬁned to a
strictly 1D channel. In this geometry, particles cannot ro-
tate, ﬂuctuate in the transverse directions, or exchange
their relative order along the channel. However, they
are free to translate along a straight line (the x-axis), and
each particle interacts only with its nearest neighbors to
the left and to the right. As a consequence, there is a
single bonding mechanism: the right (R) site of a given
particle can bind to the left (L) site of its right neigh-
bor. Bonds of type R-R and L-L are therefore excluded.

VI.
CONCLUSIONS
In this work we have examined the exact thermody-
namic and structural properties of divalent patchy hard
rods conﬁned to a strictly 1D channel, and we have as-
sessed the validity of the standard Wertheim association
theory (TPT1).
The bonding sites are modeled as attractive SW
patches located at the tips of the hard rods. The sticky
limit is recovered by taking the well depth to inﬁnity
(ǫ →∞) and the well range to zero (δ →0), while
keeping the stickiness parameter τ−1 = (eβǫ −1)δ/σ
ﬁnite. Throughout this work, we have used the inverse
stickiness parameter τ instead of temperature as the rel-
evant thermodynamic variable, since it provides a uni-
ﬁed description of ﬁnite-range SW systems and their
zero-range sticky limit.
Our results show that TPT1 is approximate for ﬁnite-
range SW sites (δ > 0), but it becomes exact in the zero-
range sticky limit (δ →0). We have shown that the as-
sociation theory can be reﬁned to provide an exact de-
scription for ﬁnite-range SW site-site interactions by in-
corporating the correct relationship between the num-
ber density ρ and the fraction of unbonded sites X [see
Eq. (4.7)]. This generalized law of mass action highlights
the limitations of the standard ﬁnite-range expression
[Eq. (3.7)], while correctly recovering the sticky result
[Eq. (3.12)] in the limit δ →0.
A key result is the derivation of an exact bonding
free-energy contribution as an explicit function of X
[Eq. (4.17)], which preserves the formal simplicity of the
TPT1 framework. Together with the generalized law of
mass action, this expression provides an exact alterna-
tive to the standard TPT1 bonding term and mass-action
equation for 1D systems with ﬁnite-range patches. Ex-
tending this reﬁned association theory to more general
patch geometries or to higher-dimensional systems re-
mains, however, a nontrivial challenge.
While TPT1 is naturally formulated in the canon-
ical ensemble, the exact solution is obtained in the
isothermal–isobaric ensemble, where the density can
be analytically expressed as a function of pressure, al-
though the inverse relation does not admit a closed form
except in the sticky limit. Conversely, the exact solu-
tion is restricted to strictly 1D geometries, whereas the
standard TPT1 formalism remains readily applicable to
higher-dimensional systems.
The exact solution also provides direct access to struc-
tural properties, a capability that is not inherently avail-
able within standard association theories.
Although
true thermodynamic phase transitions are absent in 1D
ﬂuids of divalent patchy hard rods,65 the pair correla-
tion function g(x) exhibits a rich structural behavior.
In particular, the asymptotic decay of g(x) −1 changes
from monotonic to oscillatory as density increases, the
FW line separating both regimes.
In the monotonic
regime, the correlation length displays an absolute max-
imum, giving rise to the Widom line, while in the os-
cillatory regime it may exhibit both a local maximum
and a local minimum, giving rise to the ECO line. These
features deﬁne a nontrivial structural map in the (τ, ρ)
plane, but they disappear in the sticky limit, where the
system remains in the oscillatory regime for any τ > 0.
A particularly important result is the identiﬁcation
of the ECO line, a structural locus not previously re-
ported in other 1D ﬂuids.66,67 This line appears at high
densities within the oscillatory regime and connects
the extrema of the correlation length beyond the FW
crossover. Because of its connection with strong posi-
tional ordering, we conjecture that the ECO line may
represent a precursor of freezing or isostructural solid–
solid transitions in two- and three-dimensional systems.
Whether an analogous feature exists in other 1D mod-
els or in higher-dimensional ﬂuids remains an open and
interesting question.
In this sense, the ECO line re-
veals an additional layer of structural complexity hid-
den in apparently simple systems conﬁned to restricted
geometries.68
Finally, we identiﬁed a qualitative change in the high-
pressure behavior of the correlation length. For ﬁnite-
range SW sites, the asymptotic behavior is ξ ∼p2, iden-
tical to that of ordinary hard rods, while for zero-range
sticky interactions the stronger divergence ξ ∼p3 is
found. If the attractive range is very small but ﬁnite, an
intermediate sticky-like regime with ξ ∼p3 appears at
high pressure under the condition βpδ ≪1, followed by
a crossover to the true asymptotic ﬁnite-range behavior
ξ ∼p2 when βpδ ≫1. This reﬂects the noncommutativ-
ity of the limits δ →0 and p →∞, and further empha-
sizes that ﬁnite-range and sticky interactions, although
closely related, are not thermodynamically equivalent.
Overall, our results show that even the simplest 1D
patchy ﬂuid displays a remarkably rich interplay be-
tween association, structure, and asymptotic correla-
tions, providing both an exact benchmark for associa-
tion theories and a useful reference framework for un-
derstanding more complex patchy systems in conﬁned
and higher-dimensional environments.
