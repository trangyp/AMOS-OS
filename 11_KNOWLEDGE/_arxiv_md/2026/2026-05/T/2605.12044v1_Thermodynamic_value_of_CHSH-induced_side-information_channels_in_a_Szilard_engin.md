We study the thermodynamic value of side-information channels induced by Bell-type correlations
through a CHSH prediction task embedded into a Szilard-type feedback engine. A two-level working

resource induces a controller variable G that acts as side information about X.

I.
INTRODUCTION
Thermodynamics and information theory are deeply
intertwined.
Since the seminal work of Szilard [1], it
has been understood that information about a physi-
cal system can be converted into work by feedback con-
trol.
Modern formulations, especially those of Sagawa
and Ueda [2, 3], make this relation quantitative: the max-
imal work extractable from a system coupled to a heat
bath is bounded by the mutual information between the
system and a controller. This provides a refined second
law in the presence of information.
In parallel, quantum information theory has developed
an operational understanding of nonclassical correlations.
Bell inequalities, and in particular the Clauser–Horne–
Shimony–Holt (CHSH) inequality [4], distinguish classi-
cal from quantum and more general nonsignalling cor-
relations. Quantum correlations obey Tsirelson’s bound
[5], while Popescu–Rohrlich boxes [6] saturate the alge-
braic CHSH value without enabling signalling. Under-
standing why physical correlations are quantum rather
than merely nonsignalling remains a central problem
[7, 8].
A natural question is whether Bell-type correlations
can induce side-information channels with a quantifiable
thermodynamic value. Previous work has explored the
thermodynamic value of information [9], work extraction
in single-shot thermodynamics [10], thermodynamic wit-
nesses of quantum correlations [11], and steering-assisted
work extraction [12]. The present work is complemen-
tary. Recent steering-based Szilard and work-extraction
protocols show that genuinely quantum correlations can
be witnessed thermodynamically through work bounds
and steering inequalities [12, 13].
Here, in contrast,
Bell-type correlations are not correlations between the
working medium and a quantum environment. They are
used only to induce an effective classical side-information
channel about an externally encoded thermal bit. The
resulting valuation depends only on the CHSH winning
probability of that induced channel. This should also be
distinguished from the battery-explicit CHSH energetic
witness of Ref. [14]. There, the CHSH success event di-
rectly routes a pre-supplied excitation into a binary bat-

The key conceptual point is that the physical mi-
crostate of the thermal system is not identified with an
abstract Bell-game variable. Instead, we explicitly em-
bed the physical bit X into a CHSH prediction task. A
trusted referee reads the thermal microstate X and de-
fines the auxiliary variables of the CHSH task so that
the feedback controller receives only a compressed bit G
generated from a nonsignalling correlation resource. The
referee is not a thermodynamic demon extracting work;
it defines a communication-limited prediction task. The
engine is evaluated only with respect to the information
available to the controller. In particular, the controller is
not given the auxiliary variables u, v, r separately, since
these would reveal X directly. Thus the quantity com-
puted here is the reversible feedback-work value of a sup-
plied effective channel P(G|X). The trusted referee, the
generation of the auxiliary variables, the CHSH devices,
and the computation of G are not treated as thermo-
dynamic subsystems. Consequently, the result does not
show that Bell nonlocality is a source of free energy. It
shows that, under a communication restriction in which
the controller receives only G, the CHSH winning prob-
ability determines the mutual information between the
controller and the physical microstate, and hence the
ideal feedback work.
If the referee, CHSH devices, or
preprocessing circuit are included in a fully autonomous
implementation, their measurement, storage, communi-
cation, preparation, and erasure costs must be added sep-
arately.

EXPERIMENTAL CONSIDERATIONS
The protocol requires the following operational prim-

source of classical, quantum, or nonsignalling correlations

form G = a⊕b⊕r, (iv) conditional Hamiltonian control,
(v) a work storage system, and (vi) memory reset.

The construction is not device-independent.
It as-

correlation resource as a black box generating classical
outputs. This is appropriate for assessing the thermo-

but not for loophole-free Bell certification. The energetic

CONCLUSION
We introduced a Szilard-type feedback protocol in
which Bell-type correlations, together with a trusted ref-
eree encoding, induce an effective side-information chan-
nel about a physical thermal microstate. The protocol
embeds the microstate into a CHSH prediction task and
converts the resulting effective controller information into
reversible feedback work.
The maximal feedback work is
E[Wmax] = kBT ln 2 I(X : G),
and for the CHSH-induced binary symmetric channel this
becomes

The result does not imply that nonlocal correlations
carry free energy. Rather, they improve inference about
a thermal microstate. This statement is conditional on
a supplied correlation resource; the energetic costs of
preparing and resetting the devices implementing that
resource are not included in the effective-channel valua-

fully consistent with the second law.
The framework suggests a resource-theoretic viewpoint
in which Bell-type correlations are assessed through the
thermodynamic value of the effective side-information
channels they can induce under specified preprocessing
assumptions. Future work could extend this approach to
multipartite Bell scenarios, continuous-variable systems,
autonomous implementations, and experimentally opti-
mized finite-time protocols.
