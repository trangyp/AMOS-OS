INTRODUCTION
Quantum contextuality—the impossibility of assign-
ing non-contextual hidden-variable values to measure-
ment outcomes [4]—is both a fundamental departure of
quantum mechanics from classical realism and a certi-
fied resource for quantum information processing [1–3].
Experimental demonstrations of contextuality have been
reported in photonic [18] and photon-OAM [17] plat-
forms, making the identification of strong contextuality
witnesses a practically relevant problem.
Cabello, Severini, and Winter (CSW) [5] provided a
graph-theoretic framework for quantum correlations from
projective measurements; see also Ac´ın et al. [20] for a
combinatorial extension to general probabilistic theories.
Given a set of events {e1, . . . , en}, one defines an exclu-
sion graph G = (V, E) by connecting ei and ej if the
corresponding projectors satisfy ΠiΠj = 0. For projec-
tive quantum models, the sum of probabilities satisfies

METHODS
Exhaustive Graph Enumeration
McKay’s database [10] (graph8.g6) contains all 12,346
non-isomorphic simple graphs on n = 8 vertices; of these,
11,117 are connected (OEIS A001349). We retrieved all
graphs, filtered for connectivity, and applied the analysis
pipeline to the connected subset. Non-isomorphic graphs
on n = 5 and n = 7 were obtained from NetworkX’s
Graph Atlas [11].
Independence Number
For each graph G we compute α(G) = ω( ¯G) via
maximum-weight clique search on the complement, im-
plemented in NetworkX. For n ≤8 this is exact; we
cross-validated all 11,117 results by independent brute-
force enumeration of all 28 = 256 vertex subsets, finding
perfect agreement across every graph.
Lov´asz Theta via SDP
We solve Eq. (3) using CVXPY [12] with the SCS
solver [13] for bulk screening of all 11,117 connected
graphs; SCS provides typical precision of 10−4–10−6,
sufficient for ranking.
The top 50 candidates were re-
solved with the CLARABEL solver (precision ∼10−10–
10−12), whose values are reported in all tables. For every
graph in the top 50, the SCS–CLARABEL discrepancy is
|ϑSCS−ϑCLARABEL| < 10−7 and the CLARABEL primal
feasibility residual satisfies rp < 4×10−10, confirming the
absence of numerical artefacts. The bulk scan completed
in under four minutes on a standard laptop CPU.
To certify uniqueness of Quad-C5, we computed ver-
ified enclosures by running each solver five times inde-
pendently on the top three graphs. The resulting inter-
vals are non-overlapping with a gap of 2.94 × 10−2 be-
tween Quad-C5 and the second-ranked graph (Tables II
and III), establishing that Quad-C5 is the certified unique
maximizer of ∆(G) among all connected eight-vertex
graphs.

DISCUSSION
Efficiency of Contextuality Geometry
The main surprise is that Quad-C5achieves a larger
contextuality gap than W with two fewer edges. Adding
an edge to an exclusion graph imposes an additional
orthogonality constraint in Eq. (3), generically reduc-
ing ϑ(G) while leaving α(G) unchanged or reducing it.
Naively, fewer edges should correlate with a smaller gap.
The fact that the optimal graph is sparser than the previ-
ously leading example points to a more efficient contextu-
ality geometry: the four interlocking KCBS pentagons in
Quad-C5encode more quantum advantage per edge than
the symmetric 3-regular structure of W.
The two-fold edge coverage by the four C5 subgraphs
(Table IV) appears to be the structural key.
Each
KCBS scenario contributes ∆(C5) ≈0.236; the gap of
Quad-C5(≈0.468) is nearly twice this value, suggesting
near-linear superposition of the four embedded scenar-
ios.
This is related to properties of the Lov´asz theta
under graph combinations [6]: shared edges prevent full
cancellation of the individual contributions, allowing the
four C5 copies to reinforce each other.

Physical Significance of the Qutrit–Two-Qubit
Distinction
A qutrit witness (d∗= 3) is testable on a single three-
level system: photon orbital angular momentum [17] or
an atomic Λ-system [18]. Such systems avoid the engi-
neering complexity of entangled bipartite platforms and
are more resilient to certain decoherence channels.
A (numerically indicated) pure two-qubit witness
(d∗= 4) cannot exploit contextuality in any three-
dimensional Hilbert-space embedding: the orthogonal-
ity constraints for its edge set require a four-dimensional
space. This makes such witnesses more demanding ex-
perimentally but potentially more useful as probes of
composite-system structure. Contextuality of this kind
remains distinct from Bell nonlocality, as no entangle-
ment between the two qubits is required [19].
Remarkably, W and Rank 5 are non-isomorphic graphs
but share identical numerically indicated dimensional be-
havior (η3 = α, η4 > α).
This structural coincidence
suggests an underlying algebraic invariant beyond ϑ and
α that governs dimensional rigidity.

CONCLUSION
We have carried out an exhaustive search over all
11,117 connected non-isomorphic simple graphs on eight
vertices and identified Quad-C5—an 8-vertex, 10-edge
graph containing four interlocking KCBS pentagons—
as the unique maximizer of the contextuality gap for
n = 8. With ∆= 0.46784, it surpasses the Wagner graph
(∆≈0.414) by ∼13% while using two fewer edges.
Unlike the Wagner graph, which numerical evidence
indicates requires a four-dimensional Hilbert space,
Quad-C5is a certified qutrit witness: we determine nu-
merically at 50-digit precision that η3(Quad-C5) = 1 +
√
5, placing this value in the same algebraic field Q(

as the KCBS pentagon.
In four dimensions, the full

α, η3, and n/d.
Any qutrit apparatus sufficient for a
KCBS test can therefore certify Quad-C5 contextuality
without relaxing noise requirements. At d = 4, Quad-C5
also strictly outperforms the Wagner graph in noise ro-
bustness, requiring 2.6% less visibility.
These results demonstrate that qutrit-accessible con-
textuality can be more efficient than two-qubit scenar-
ios, and provide concrete graph structures suitable for
experimental tests in three-level and four-level quantum
systems. The finding that the n = 8 maximum requires
fewer edges than the Wagner graph suggests that denser
exclusion graphs do not always yield stronger contextual-
ity; the relationship between graph sparsity and contex-
tuality strength warrants systematic future investigation.
