1. Introduction
One of the most basic invariants of a topological space is its fundamental group.
While originally defined in terms of maps into the space, it can equivalently be
recovered from its category of sheaves by identifying covering spaces with locally
constant sheaves. From this perspective, the van Kampen theorem becomes par-
ticularly transparent. More importantly, this viewpoint enabled the Grothendieck
school to attach a fundamental profinite groupoid π´et
≤1(X) to any scheme X, despite
the absence of a reasonable underlying topology, by means of the category of ´etale
sheaves on X, see [Gro63a],[Gro63b]. When X is of finite type over C, the resulting
invariant agrees with the classical fundamental groupoid of X(C) after profinite
completion, by the Riemann existence theorem.
Axiomatic Galois theory. More recently, Rognes [Rog08] and Mathew [Mat16]
isolated the categorical properties of the category of ´etale sheaves that allow one to
associate to any presentably symmetric monoidal stable ∞-category C a profinite
fundamental groupoid π≤1(C), often referred to as the Galois groupoid of C. This

give a satisfactory description far beyond the p-group case.
