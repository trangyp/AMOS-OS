2.2. Throughout this article, a weighted graph G is a finite connected undirected graph such that
• no edge connects a vertex to itself

• each vertex is decorated with an integer n ∈Z call a weight.

Let G be a weighted graph.
(a) Let v be a vertex of G. The blowing-up of G at v is the weighted graph obtained from G by adding a
vertex x of weight (−1), adding the edge {v, x}, and decreasing the weight of v by 1.
(b) Let ϵ = {u, v} be an edge of G. The blowing-up of G at ϵ is the weighted graph obtained from G by
deleting the edge ϵ, adding a vertex x of weight (−1) and two edges {u, x} and {v, x}, and decreasing
the weights of u and v by 1.
In (a) and (b), we refer to x as the vertex created by the blowing-up.
The degree of a vertex x is the number of edges incident to x. A vertex x of a weighted graph H is
superfluous if its weight is (−1), its degree is 1 or 2, and no two neighbors of x are neighbors of each other.
It is not hard to see that a vertex x of H is superfluous if and only if H is the result of blowing-up some
weighted graph G at some vertex or edge in such a way that x is the vertex created by that blowing-up.
The graph G is then uniquely determined by the pair (H, x), and is called the blowing-down of H at x. A
weighted graph is minimal if it does not contain any superfluous vertex, or equivalently, if it cannot be blown
down. Weighted graphs G1, G2 are equivalent (and we write G1 ∼G2) if G1 and G2 can be obtained from
one another via a sequence of blowing up and blowing down operations. Moreover, ∼defines an equivalence
relation on the set of weighted graphs. (Note that the assumption that our weighted graphs are connected
allows us to avoid considering the “free blowing up” operation which needs to be considered when studying
equivalence classes of weighted graphs that are not necessarily connected. See [Dai08, p.65] for example.)
