The quartic threefold is symplectically irrational
Jiaji Cai
Abstract
We prove that smooth quartic threefolds are symplectically irrational, i.e., cannot
be related to projective space by a series of symplectic blow-ups, blow-downs, and
deformations. This implies that they are algebraically irrational, recovering a classical
result of Iskovskikh-Manin. Our proof involves establishing a decomposition theorem
for quantum cohomology along symplectic blow-ups, following the work of Iritani.
1
Introduction
A smooth complex projective variety is said to be rational if it is isomorphic to projective
space outside of Zariski closed subsets. The weak factorization theorem [AKMWo02] implies
that this is equivalent to the existence of a sequence of blow-ups and blow-downs relating
said variety and Pn. This alternative definition can be generalized to symplectic manifolds
(first introduced by Guillemin-Sternberg [GS89]).
Definition 1.1. Two symplectic manifolds are symplectically birational if they are related
by a finite sequence of symplectic blow-ups, blow-downs, and symplectic deformations, in
any order. A symplectic manifold is symplectically rational if it is symplectically birational
to complex projective space; otherwise it is said to be symplectically irrational.
If a smooth complex projective variety is algebraically rational, then by the weak factor-
ization theorem it is also symplectically rational.
The notion of a symplectic blow-up along a symplectic submanifold was originally due to
Gromov. The construction was worked out in detail by McDuff, and used to construct the
first simply connected examples of non-K¨ahler symplectic manifolds [McD84]. A symplectic
blow-up is only well-defined up to deformation, so it is only natural to allow deformations
in the definition of a symplectic birational equivalence.
Gromov’s h-principle for isosymplectic embeddings [Gro86] tells us that in codimension
≥4 there are many more symplectic submanifolds than algebraic subvarieties, so symplectic
blow-ups can be much wilder than algebraic ones. Moreover, the (unavoidable) addition of
symplectic deformations in the definition of a symplectic birational equivalence gives it a
much more topological flavor.
Smooth complex hypersurfaces in projective space provide a first testing ground for un-
derstanding symplectic birational geometry. Consider the following natural question:
Question 1.2. Which smooth complex hypersurfaces in Pn+1 are symplectically rational?

Note that hypersurfaces of a fixed degree are all symplectomorphic. For n ≤3, the only
hypersurfaces for which symplectic rationality is unknown are the cubic and quartic in P4.
We resolve the degree 4 case:
Theorem 1.3. The quartic threefold is symplectically irrational.
This implies, in particular, that smooth quartic threefolds are algebraically irrational,
recovering a classical result of Iskovskikh-Manin [IM71]. We recall that they in fact prove
that a smooth quartic threefold has finitely many birational automorphisms, and hence must
be irrational (since P3 has infinitely many). One can see that their proof strategy does not
generalize easily to the symplectic setting.
Our proof of Theorem 1.3 relies on a decomposition theorem for big quantum cohomology
along symplectic blow-ups; a precise statement will be given later (Theorem 3.5). There is
a distinguished element in big quantum cohomology called the Euler vector field, and we
consider the eigenvalues of the operator “quantum multiplication by the Euler vector field”.
We show that for the quartic threefold, this operator has an eigenvalue of multiplicity 3;
whereas by the decomposition theorem, any symplectically rational 6-manifold can only
have eigenvalues of multiplicity at most 2. Importantly, we here ignore odd classes and
only consider even cohomology. We remark that our proof seems close in spirit to Smith’s
proposal in [Smi23] for the cubic threefold.
The fact that the Euler vector field of the quartic threefold has an eigenvalue of multi-
plicity 3 is quite remarkable. It is well-known that small quantum product with the first
Chern class has such an eigenvalue, but a priori it seems quite probable that the big quan-
tum cohomology deformation would split this eigenvalue into several eigenvalues of smaller
multiplicities. We prove that this does not happen (Theorem 5.3). The key point is that
powers of the Euler vector field satisfy certain commutator identities, and the eigenvalues
are completely determined via the Cayley-Hamilton theorem by a system of ODE’s and their
initial values at small quantum cohomology. This argument was very briefly alluded to in
[KKPY25, Remark 3.14].
The decomposition theorem has been proved by Iritani for K¨ahler blow-ups of smooth
projective varieties [Iri23]. Once we establish the virtual localization formula [Kon95] and
quantum Riemann-Roch theorem [CG07] in the symplectic setting, Iritani’s proof directly
generalizes. Thus the main technical difficulty is the symplectic virtual techniques. In this
paper we use global Kuranishi charts, introduced by Abouzaid-McLean-Smith [AMS21].
Our proof is also inspired by the work of Katzarkov-Kontsevich-Pantev-Yu [KKPY25].
Plan of the paper. In Section 2, we recall the construction of symplectic blow-ups. In
Section 3, we state the decomposition theorem and discuss the various aspects of Gromov-
Witten theory used in its proof.
In Section 4, we give the proof of the decomposition
theorm. In Section 5, we prove our result on symplectic irrationality. In Appendix A, we
discuss symplectic virtual technqiues and prove the quantum Riemann-Roch theorem.
