Abstract: We compute the two-loop massless QCD corrections to the amplitude of Higgs
boson decay to bottom quark pair and a gluon (H →b + ¯b + g) in the higher powers of
the dimensional regularization parameter ϵ. The calculation is performed by projecting the
amplitude onto the appropriate Lorentz structures related to the process. We also show
the numerical behaviour of the form factors for a few sample phase-space points. These
amplitudes are necessary ingredients for computing the three-loop virtual corrections to
bottom-quark annihilation to Higgs plus jet production at the hadron collisions.
Keywords: Higgs, perturbative QCD, LHC

Introduction
The Standard Model (SM) has been tested over decades by several experiments with very
high precision. The discovery of the Higgs boson by the ATLAS [1] and CMS [2] collabo-
rations at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) is an important milestone in establishing the
electroweak symmetry breaking mechanism in the SM. The generation of the masses of
the elementary particles in the SM is explained by the Higgs mechanism [3–7]. Studying
the CP properties of this discovered boson is important to understand whether or not the
discovered particle is the SM Higgs boson [8–12]. The mass of the Higgs boson is measured
with high precision and is found to be mH = 125.04±0.12 GeV [13], which is an important
parameter in the study of several processes in the SM.
The inclusive Higgs production is the dominant channel at the LHC, which, when
measured in the experiment, receives contributions from both the gluon fusion gg →H [14]
and the bottom annihilation channels b¯b →H. The other subdominant channels are vector
boson fusion (VBF) [15], associated Higgs production with vector boson (VH) [16], through
bottom annihilation, and top associated production channels (t¯tH) [17–20].
From the
theory side, the Higgs production cross section in these channels is known to a very good
accuracy with the help of predictions from the perturbation theory in QCD. The results up
to next-to-next-to-next-leading order (N3LO) in QCD are already available for the gluon
fusion channel
[14, 21–39] , vector boson fusion channel [40] and Higgs production in
association with a vector boson [41, 42]. In the context of the bottom annihilation process
b¯b →H, NNLO calculation has been done in [43] and partial three-loop N3LOsv results
are available [44]. The Higgs production through bottom annihilation is known till N3LO
accuracy [45].
Apart from inclusive Higgs boson production cross sections, the measurements of dif-
ferential distributions such as transverse momentum and rapidity distribution are useful in

probing the Higgs couplings to the SM fields. These differential distributions allow a di-
rect comparison of theoretical predictions with experimental measurements. An important
process in this direction is the Higgs production in association with a jet (Higgs +1jet)
in hadron collisions [46–52]. Higgs +1 jet production is a key process for experimental
analyses involving boosted Higgs bosons and jet-veto observables [53–55]. The dominant
contribution to the Higgs+1jet production in hadron collisions comes from the gluon fusion
channel. The NNLO QCD calculation for the Higgs+1jet process in the EFT framework has
been done in [56]. The next dominant contribution to Higgs+1jet production comes from
the bottom annihilation process which is about 3% of that in gluon-fusion channel [56, 57].
The NLO QCD correction to Higgs +1jet production via the bottom annihilation process
is available in the literature [58]. The NNLO QCD correction to this process was com-
puted in [57]. The observables with jet vetoes are especially useful, as they significantly
improve the signal sensitivity and help in studying the properties of the Higgs boson and
its interactions with other SM particles [59, 60].
Currently, a large amount of data is already available from Run 3 of the LHC, and an
even larger dataset is expected from the high-luminosity phase of the LHC. This requires
precision calculations (at least N3LO accuracy) to carry out phenomenological studies with
about 1% precision [61]. One of the important steps in extending the current results for
Higgs+jet to N3LO is to compute two-loop results to higher powers of the dimensional
regulator ϵ. These terms contribute to higher-order QCD results and are important ingre-
dients for the three-loop results. It is to be noted that for the Higgs production through
bottom annihilation, the dependence on the unphysical scales is seen to reduce at N3LO
level [45].
At the one-loop level, scattering amplitudes exhibit divergences that are typically
treated using dimensional regularization. In this approach, the calculation is performed
in d = 4 + ϵ dimensions, and the loop amplitudes are written as Laurent expansions
in ϵ. To achieve the N3LO results requires the knowledge of one-loop amplitudes up to
O(ϵ4), the two-loop amplitudes up to O(ϵ2), and the three-loop amplitudes up to O(ϵ0).
This work provides the first of these key components: the two-loop amplitudes of H →b¯bg,
evaluated up to O(ϵ2) and one loop results O(ϵ4). Previous studies obtained these two loop
amplitudes only up to O(ϵ0) [62, 63], which has already played a crucial role in NNLO QCD
analyses of Higgs+jet production [57] and in determining the Higgs transverse-momentum
spectrum [57, 64] through bottom quark annihilation.
Predictions for Higgs production in association with one jet through an effective gluon–
gluon–Higgs interaction in the infinite top-quark mass limit are available at the two-loop
level in QCD to higher powers of ϵ in Ref. [65]. As experimental data become more precise,
it becomes necessary to include subdominant channels, such as bottom-quark annihilation
to Higgs+jet. Pseudo-scalar Higgs boson case has already been studied for decay to three
partons at two-loop to higher power in ϵ up to O(ϵ2) in Ref. [66, 67]. In this work, we
present the one and two-loop amplitudes for the process H →b¯bg to higher powers of ϵ,
which can be analytically continued to obtain the amplitudes for b¯b →Hg, bg →Hb and
¯bg →H¯b. In this process, the Higgs boson couples to the bottom quarks via the Yukawa
coupling λ where, the exact bottom quark mass is retained, while neglecting the bottom

are obtained by applying the projectors (2.12) on the full amplitude and summing over the
gluon polarization defined in eq. (2.5). We use our in-house-developed FORM [69] routines

4
Summary
In this work, we computed the two-loop massless QCD correction to the Higgs boson
decay to a bottom quark pair in association with a gluon (H →b +¯b + g) at higher powers
of the dimensional regularization parameter ϵ. We have used the projection method to
extract the form factors from the amplitude. Our results of these amplitudes are crucial to
construct N3LO QCD correction to the Higgs boson production in the bottom anti-bottom
annihilation process in association with a jet. We have confirmed that our results reproduce
the expected infrared structure as predicted by Catani’s IR subtraction operators. We
observe that due to the presence of the higher weight GPLs in the coefficients of ϵ2, the
computational time for the form-factors rapidly increases compared to that of ϵ0. The
two-loop amplitudes computed in this work provide essential ingredients for future high-

precision (three-loop) calculations of Higgs boson production in bottom quark-initiated
channels at hadron colliders.
