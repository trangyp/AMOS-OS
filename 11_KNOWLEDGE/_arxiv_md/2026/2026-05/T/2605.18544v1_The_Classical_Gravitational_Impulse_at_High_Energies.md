The Classical Gravitational Impulse at High Energies
Michael Saavedra∗
Mani L. Bhaumik Institute for Theoretical Physics,
University of California at Los Angeles, Los Angeles, CA 90095, USA
(Dated: May 19, 2026)
We compute the gravitational impulse for two classical massive scalars in the ultrarelativistic limit
to all orders in Newton’s constant GN at fixed GNs/mb to O(m4/s2). By computing the 4 and
5-point scattering amplitudes in the small-mass regime of −t ∼m2, we are able to resum all large
GNs corrections. Applying the KMOC formula for the impulse and taking the large mass limit, we
recover the classical result at high energies. This resummation is in complete agreement with known
results in the post-Minkowski expansion, and in the massless limit we recover previous results for
the radiated energy. We use this resummed amplitude to predict the leading high-energy behavior
of the PM expansion to eleventh post-Minkowski order.
Introduction
Since
the
discovery
of
gravitational
waves [1–3], there has been an intense theoretical inter-
est in the gravitational two-body problem. A number of
tools have been developed to study the various aspects
of this problem, including the post-Newtonian (PN) [4–
8], post-Minkowskian (PM) [9–15], and self-force [16–19]
expansions, which may be based on classical methods or
effective field theory (EFT) [20, 21]. The current state-of-
the-art involves partial results at 6PN [22–25] and 5PM
[26–28] orders.
Despite the remarkable progress, there are still several
outstanding questions in the field.
One such question
concerns the fate of classical gravitational scattering in
the ultra-relativistic limit. Notably, calculations at 3 and
4PM [29, 30] show that for highly boosted projectiles, the
total energy radiated by gravitational waves grows much
faster than the center-of-mass energy, indicating a break-
down of perturbation theory. Such a breakdown has been
known since the 70s [31–33], and computations have gen-
erally been limited to the case of vanishingly weak cou-
pling G√s/b ≪m/√s where the PM expansion is valid,
for projectiles of generic masses m1 ∼m2 ∼m and center
of mass energy √s. A notable exception is [31], in which
a formula for the Bondi news was determined at leading
power in m2/s to all orders in GNs/(mb) although fur-
ther analytic computation, such as the radiated energy,
was determined to be intractable.
To address this limitation, several works have explored
techniques for computations at high energies. In [34], an
EFT based on Soft Collinear Effective Theory (SCET)
[35–37]1 with Glauber operators [43, 44] was developed
for gravity, and it was found that there is a tower of
large logarithms in the classical amplitude, generalizing
the results of Amati, Ciafaloni, and Veneziano [45] at
3PM. The leading log at 5PM order was subsequently
computed in [46], and a formalism based on shockwaves
was put forth [47] to allow for small-mass projectiles. For
classical observables, a series of papers [48–50] computed

the radiated energy spectrum using classical methods and
amplitudes-based methods. Remarkably, their results re-
sum an infinite tower of t-channel graviton exchanges,
and thus are all orders in GNs.
Moreover, they show
that the spectrum becomes non-analytic in GN as GNs
becomes very large.
In a similar vein, Refs.
[51, 52]
have explored the radiated energy spectrum in the zero-
frequency limit (ZFL), keeping full mass dependence.
This allowed them to explore the transition of the observ-
able from the perturbative regime with G√s/b ≪m/√s
to the massless regime at fixed G√s/b. However, a gen-
eral framework for high-energy gravitational scattering
away from the ZFL is still lacking.
In this letter, we begin to close this gap. Using EFT
methods based on [34], we compute the gravitational im-
pulse for small mass projectiles by summing the domi-
nant contributions to all orders in perturbation theory.
Noticing that one recovers the high-energy limit of clas-
sical gravity in the large mass limit of this theory, we are
able to extract the resummed classical impulse at fixed
GNs/(mb) at leading and next-to-leading order in powers
of m2/s ≪1 as a convolution in impact parameter space.
This allows us to extend the validity of the calculation
beyond the perturbative regime of GNs/(mb) ≪1 to the
strongly coupled case of GNs/(mb) ∼1. We find that the
impulse is exactly the tree-level impulse at leading power
in m2/s. Radiative corrections enter at O(m2/s), and are
entirely first order in self-force (1SF), in agreement with
the analysis of [53], while conservative corrections appear
at fixed order through 2PM and are 0SF. We then use the
resummation to compute the leading PM results for the
impulse to 11PM order. As a by-product, we are able to
reproduce the results of [48–50] for the radiated energy
spectrum for massless scattering. We find that while the
integrands for the radiated energy spectra in the massive
and massless cases are the same as mi →0, the con-
volution does not commute with the massless limit, and
the radiated energies in the two cases are qualitatively
different.

Massive High-Energy Scattering
We consider the
gravitational scattering of two massive bodies with initial
momenta p1 and p2, with center-of-mass energy squared
s = (p1 + p2)2 and momentum transfer q. In the for-
ward limit s ≫−q2, with impact parameter b ∼q−1,
one may consider different scalings of the masses with
respect to the kinematics to obtain different theories. In
the classical limit, one has s ∼m2, with the scattering
projectiles localizing onto world lines. The opposite limit
is the small-mass regime with m ∼q, which is closely
related to the massless case; this is the Regge limit of
gravitational scattering [47]. For perturbation theory to
be valid, both regimes are restricted to small-angle scat-
tering, with angle θ ∼GN
√s/b ≪1.
In the massive
case, there is a tighter bound, requiring θ√s/m ≪1 [31].
The regime in which s ≫m2 is particularly sensitive
to this bound.
This corresponds to the classical scat-
tering of highly boosted projectiles. Traditionally, this
is studied expanding the PM result in the large boosts
σ ∼s/m1m2. Here, we take the alternative approach
of starting with the light-mass theory and then expand-
ing in small q/m to reach this limit. In the latter case,
the class of Feynman diagrams which contribute classi-
cally take a relatively simple form, allowing one to resum
them to all orders in GN, as discussed in Appendix A.
To describe the light-mass theory, we use a slight gen-
eralization of the EFT in [34], based on SCETM[54, 55],
which will be presented in a forthcoming publication [56].
This EFT is an expansion in λ2 = −q2/s and is described
by collinear modes for both the gravitons and the scalars,
with momenta that scale as pn ∼(1, λ2, λ) and p¯n ∼
(λ2, 1, λ) , and soft modes with pS ∼(λ, λ, λ). These are
then mediated by Glauber modes with pG ∼(λ2, λ2, λ),
which generate the potential-like 1/p2
⊥t-channel propa-
gators characteristic of forward scattering. In going to
the classical limit q/m →0, the scalar fields localize
onto world lines, while the graviton collinear modes be-
come “collinear-soft” (csoft) modes with scalings pcs ∼
λ(√s/m, m/√s, 1) and pcs ∼λ(m/√s, √s/m, 1) [57, 58].
Taking the classical limit for the amplitudes computed
in Appendix A then reduces to expanding in the region
where the collinear graviton momentum has csoft scaling.
In the massless theory, a classical contribution at
(2j + 1)PM order scales at most as ∼GNs (GN
√s/b)2j,
with the dominant contribution to the amplitude occur-
ring at maximal SF order due to large logarithms [34, 46].
At low loop orders, the high-energy limit of the PM ex-
pansion seems to obey this scaling as well from explicit
computations through 3PM. Starting at 4PM, however,
this breaks, and one finds instead that the classical con-
tributions instead scale as GNs (GN
√s/b)j(√s/m)j−2,
where one finds extra enhancements by the rapidities
of boosted projectiles; this enhancement was first no-
ticed at 4PM in the conservative sector in [59] and
later confirmed in the presence of dissipative effects in
[30].
The origin of these enhancements can be under-
stood light-mass theory by considering terms which ap-
pear to scale superclassically2, that is contributions of
the form (GNs)j+1(GN

In passing from the

This is contrasted with both the PM expansion and the

In this power-counting, each soft or collinear loop leads
to 1/γ suppression in the boosted theory. In the light-

Taking the classical limit, the diagram then

Therefore, the leading

generically arises from diagrams with only Glauber ex-
changes, while the next-to-leading order contributions
are due to diagrams with one soft or collinear loop.

Conclusions
We have presented an all orders in GN
resummed formula for the classical gravitational impulse,
allowing for one to probe the case of strongly coupled
scattering with GNs/(m1b) ∼1.
Using this formula,
we gave predictions for the leading high-energy contri-
bution to the PM-expanded impulse through O(G11
N ).
We found agreement with all PM results through 4PM
[26, 30] along with a resummed result for the radiated
energy spectrum in the massless limit [48].
There are several interesting avenues for further study.
Most notably, we are restricted to GNs/(mb) ∼1, and
so gravitational scattering at arbitrarily high energies is
still out of reach. Due to the power-counting breakdown,
it seems different techniques will be needed to access this
region of phase space. Pushing this idea to higher or-
ders in 1/σ seems daunting, given the need to sum over
all possible Glauber exchanges. One possible solution is
to develop an EFT for this regime using some idea close
to that of [62, 63]. In principle, this could allow one to
work with the shockwave propagator [64] directly instead
of having to sum over Glauber exchanges by hand. Ad-
ditionally, this analysis ignores both Love tidal effects
and spin [21, 65, 66], which may play a role at high en-
ergies [47]; it would be interesting to see if these lead
to non-trivial effects. Similarly, we have not considered
