THE BALANCED STRUCTURE ON THE CATEGORY OF
REPRESENTATIONS OF A CONFORMAL NET
ADRI`A MAR´IN-SALVADOR
Abstract. Let A be a (not necessarily rational) conformal net. We show that the braided
W∗-tensor category Rep(A) of representations of A is canonically a balanced W∗-tensor
category. The balance is given by the action of e−2πiL0, where L0 denotes the generator of
rotations on S1. In future work [Mar26], we generalize this result to the larger context of
a group acting on A. We provide here a more accessible proof for the case where no group
is present.
1. Introduction
Conformal nets provide a formalization of 2-dimensional unitary chiral conformal field the-
ory [BMT88, BSM90, BGL93, GF93, Was95, KL04, BDH15]. A conformal net consists of a
vacuum Hilbert space H0 equipped with a unitary projective action of the group Diff+(S1)
of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms of the circle together with an assignment of a von
Neumann algebra A(I) ⊂B(H0) to every interval I ⊂S1 of the circle.
The assignment
I 7→A(I) is compatible with the inclusion of intervals and with the Diff+(S1) action.
Given a conformal net A = (A, H0), a representation of A is a Hilbert space H equipped
with ∗-actions of all the von Neumann algebras A(I) compatible with the inclusions A(J) ⊂
A(I) for all pairs of intervals I ⊂J. We denote by Rep(A) the category whose objects are A-
representations and whose morphisms are morphisms of Hilbert spaces intertwining the actions
of all the algebras A(I). Given a representation H ∈Rep(A), the algebra EndRep(A)(H) of
endomorphisms of H is a von Neumann algebra, making Rep(A) a W∗-category in the sense
of [HNP24]. In addition, Rep(A) is canonically a braided W∗-tensor category. The tensor
structure of Rep(A) can be constructed in different equivalent ways, see [GF93, Sec. IV.2.],
[Was98, Sec. 30], [BDH15, Sec. 1.C] and [Gui21, Sec. 2]. For the braiding, see [FRS89, Sec.
2], [Lon89, Sec. 7], [GF93, Def. 4.16] and the modern treatment of [Gui21, Sec. 2]. Recall
that, given a braided W∗-tensor category C, with braiding β, a balance (or categorical twist)
on C consists of a natural family of unitary isomorphisms θX : X →X such that
θX⊗Y = (θX ⊗θY ) ◦βY,X ◦βX,Y
for all X, Y ∈C. We prove that Rep(A) is canonically a balanced W∗-tensor category, see
Theorem 4.4.

A large part of the literature focuses on M¨obius covariant nets, where the vacuum H0 is only

canonical one in the case where A is rational, see Theorem 4.5 and the discussion leading to it.

in Section 3 we recall the braided tensor structure on Rep(A) of [Gui21], and in Section 4 we

2. Conformal nets
Let S1 := {z ∈C | |z| = 1} denote the standard circle. We define an interval of S1 to be
an open, connected, non-empty, non-dense subset of S1, and we denote by J the collection
of intervals of S1. Let us denote by M¨ob the set of M¨obius transformations of S1, that is,
transformations of the form

of angle t by Rt. In this paper, all Hilbert spaces and C∗-algebras are assumed to be separable.
Definition 2.1. A M¨obius covariant net on S1 is a tuple (H0, A, U, Ω) where H0 is a Hilbert
space equipped with a non-zero vector Ω∈H0, U is a strongly continuous unitary represen-

(v) uniqueness of the vacuum vector: Ω∈H0 is the unique vector, up to a phase, which

Let Diff+(S1) denote the group of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of S1. A strongly
continuous projective unitary representation V of Diff+(S1) on a Hilbert space H is a strongly
continuous homomorphism V : Diff+(S1) →PU(H). Note that M¨ob ⊂Diff+(S1). We say
that V is an extension of a unitary representation U of M¨ob on H if, for every φ ∈M¨ob, it
holds that V (φ) = [U(φ)], where [−] : U(H) →PU(H) denotes the projection map.
Definition 2.2. A M¨obius covariant net (H0, A, U, Ω) is said to be a conformal net if there
is an extension of U to a unitary projective representation of Diff+(S1), which we also denote
by U, such that for all I ∈J and φ ∈Diff+(S1), it holds that
(i) U(φ)A(I)U(φ)∗= A(φI);
(ii) if φ|I = idI, then Ad(U(φ))|A(I) = idA(I).
The following are well-known properties of any conformal net (H0, A, U, Ω).
(i) Haag duality: the commutant of A(I) in B(H0) is A(Ic), where Ic denotes the interior
of S1 \ I [GF93, Thm. 2.19];
(ii) The Reeh-Schlieder Theorem: the vacuum vector Ωis cyclic and separating for every
von Neumann algebra A(I) [GF93, Cor. 2.8];
(iii) Each von Neumann algebra A(I) is a type III1 factor [GL96, Prop. 1.2].
Let (H0, A, U, Ω) be a conformal net, which we will simply denote by A from now on. Let
us introduce the category of representations of A.
Definition 2.3. A representation of a conformal net A consists of a Hilbert space H and a
collection of ∗-homomorphisms πI : A(I) →B(H) for every interval I ∈J such that
πI|A(J) = πJ
whenever J ∈J is an interval such that J ⊂I. We write Rep(A) for the category whose
objects are representations of A and whose morphisms are maps of Hilbert spaces intertwining
the actions of all the algebras A(I).
The conformal net A has a canonical representation on its vacuum Hilbert space H0 by
definition, which we call the vacuum representation of A. Given I ∈J and x ∈A(I), we
write π0,I(x) or π0(x) for the action of x on the vacuum representation H0.
3. The braided category of representations of a conformal net
The category Rep(A) is a braided W∗-tensor category. The tensor structure of Rep(A) can
be constructed in different equivalent ways, see [GF93, Sec. IV.2.], [Was98, Sec. 30], [BDH15,
Sec. 1.C] and [Gui21, Sec. 2]. For the braiding, see [FRS89, Sec. 2], [Lon89, Sec. 7], [GF93,
Def. 4.16] and the modern treatment of [Gui21, Sec. 2]. We recall here the construction of
[Gui21]. Actually, we will produce the opposite of the braided monoidal category of Gui, in
order to agree with our conventions in the future work [Mar26] (see Remark 3.8).
