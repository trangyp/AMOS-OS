THE IDEAL STRUCTURE OF EXEL–PARDO ALGEBRAS AND THEIR
HIGHER RANK ANALOGUES
JOHANNES CHRISTENSEN AND SERGEY NESHVEYEV
Abstract. Given a pseudo-free self-similar action of a countable group G on a countable
directed graph E with amenable stabilizers of the vertices, we identify the exact conditions
under which these stabilizers do not contribute to the ideal structure of the corresponding Exel–
Pardo algebra OG,E. Under these conditions, we give a complete description of the primitive
ideal space of OG,E in graph-theoretic terms. Our results apply in particular to certain crossed
products OE ⋊G, where G acts on E by graph automorphisms. When G is trivial, this recovers
Hong–Szymański’s description of the ideal structure of the Cuntz–Krieger algebras OE. Similar
results are then obtained for self-similar actions of groups on row-finite higher rank graphs
without sources.
In order to obtain these results we formalize the notion of a graded groupoid with essentially
central isotropy, which generalizes essentially principal groupoids and groupoids injectively
graded by abelian groups. Under the amenability and second countability assumptions, we
describe the primitive ideal spaces of the corresponding C∗-algebras as topological spaces.
Introduction
Understanding the ideal structure is a fundamental problem in the analysis of nonsimple C∗-
algebras. The research on this problem has a long history. In the context of C∗-algebras of
étale groupoids, which this paper deals with, the first major result was obtained by Effros and
Hahn [EH67], who showed that if Γ is a countable amenable group acting freely on a second
countable locally compact space X, then the primitive ideal space of C0(X)⋊Γ is homeomorphic
to the quasi-orbit space (Γ\X)∼. This implies that the lattice of ideals in C0(X)⋊Γ is isomorphic
to the lattice of Γ-invariant open subsets of X. Using different methods, Renault showed in
his thesis [Ren80] that a similar isomorphism holds for all amenable second countable étale
groupoids G and, moreover, instead of freeness of the action G ↷G(0), that is, principality
of G, it suffices to assume that G is essentially principal, meaning that for every invariant closed
subset X ⊂G(0) the set of units x ∈X with trivial isotropy groups Gx
x is dense in X. This result
was essentially rediscovered by Kawamura and Tomiyama [KT90] for transformation groupoids.
Their paper implies that a version of Renault’s theorem for such groupoids is true beyond the
second countable case and, arguably more importantly, the assumption of essential principality
is not only sufficient but is also necessary for the result to be true. For étale groupoid C∗-
algebras this necessity was later also proved by Brown, Clark, Farthing and Sims [BCFS14]. A
short elegant proof of these results for transformation groupoids has been given by Archbold
and Spielberg [AS94], their arguments readily generalize to étale groupoids [CN24c]. Let us also
mention that in the recent years the progress in noncommutative boundary theory has led to a
similar description of the ideal structure of C∗
r (G) for some nonamenable groupoids that are not
essentially principal [KKL+21], but in this paper we are primarily interested in the amenable
case.

In the same period there have emerged a number of important examples of
amenable groupoids that are not always essentially principal, yet their C∗-algebras have a
tractable ideal structure, such as groupoids underlying Cuntz–Krieger algebras and their various
generalizations. In the absence of a clear strategy how to deal with the non-essentially-principal

a complete description of the ideal structure of C∗-algebras of countable directed graphs was
obtained without using groupoids by Hong and Szymański [HS04], culminating around twenty
years of research on the topic.
The lack of a strategy does not mean that there have been no general results on the ideal
structure of C∗(G). In fact, the origins of one of the most fundamental results on this topic
predate Renault’s theorem. Specifically, it was conjectured by Effros and Hahn [EH67] that
in the amenable second countable case every primitive ideal of C0(X) ⋊Γ is induced from an
isotropy group. This was proved by Sauvageot [Sau77], and later the result was extended to
amenable groupoids by Ionescu and Williams [IW09]. Therefore it has been known for quite some

We were led to the essentially injectively graded groupoids by our attempt to understand the
ideal structure of Exel–Pardo algebras OG,E, which are defined from self-similar actions G ↷E
of groups on directed graphs [EP17]. Under suitable conditions these algebras admit Hausdorff
groupoid models GG,E, which can be thought of as semidirect products of the groupoids GE
underlying the graph C∗-algebras OE and transformation groupoids G ⋉∂E, where ∂E = G(0)

algebraically each groupoid GG,E is injectively graded by the so-called lag group, but this grading
is in general discontinuous and what makes sense topologically is only a grading Φ: GG,E →Z
that is trivial on G ⋉∂E, while on GE it coincides with the standard grading defining the gauge
action on OE. More generally, Li and Yang [LY21a] defined algebras OG,Λ and the corresponding
groupoids GG,Λ from self-similar actions G ↷Λ of groups on k-graphs. In this case the standard
grading Φ on GG,Λ is Zk-valued.
Simplicity criteria for the C∗-algebras OG,Λ have been obtained in [EP17,LY21a,LY21b], but
apart from this not much is known about their ideal structure even when G acts on Λ by graph
automorphisms, in which case OG,Λ ∼= OΛ ⋊G. A theorem of Kumjian and Pask [KP99] states
that if a countable group G acts freely by graph automorphisms on a locally finite graph E, then
OE ⋊G is strongly Morita equivalent to OE/G. As was argued by Marelli and Raeburn [MR09],
the structure of OE ⋊G becomes much more mysterious when one allows even a small degree
of nonfreeness of the action.
Under some assumptions, the ideal structure of self-similar k-
graph C∗-algebras OG,Λ has been described by Li and Yang [LY21b]. Although the conditions
in [LY21b], which include the requirement that G acts trivially on the vertices of Λ, are rather
restrictive, they still allow situations where the groupoid GG,Λ is not essentially principal and
the Zk-grading is not injective, so that the results of [LY21b] cannot be deduced from Renault’s
theorem or [CN24b].
By construction, the kernels of Φ restricted to the isotropy groups of GG,Λ are built out of
subgroups of the G-stabilizers of vertices of Λ. It follows that in principle our results in [CN24c]
should allow one to understand the ideal structure of OG,Λ for a large class of self-similar k-
graphs, namely, it suffices to assume that the G-stabilizer of every vertex has local polynomial
growth. In practice this might quickly become a daunting task involving a careful analysis of
the isotropy groups and their representation theory for all boundary paths. On the other hand,
the results of [LY21b] imply that such an analysis is not always necessary, and indeed we can
conclude now that, under amenability and second countability assumptions on GG,Λ, the vertex
stabilizers and their contributions to the isotropy groups can be ignored in the analysis of the
primitive spectrum exactly when the grading Φ: GG,Λ →Zk is essentially injective.
In this
paper we describe when this happens in terms of the self-similar action G ↷Λ and then give a
description of the primitive spectrum in graph-theoretic terms.
The paper consists of five sections. Section 1 explains our conventions and collects various
auxiliary results on groupoids. Among other things, here we clarify the connections between
different classes of ideals that have appeared in the literature, such as dynamical, diagonal-
invariant and gauge-invariant ones. In particular, we show that in the amenable second countable
case all gauge-invariant ideals of a graded groupoid C∗-algebra are dynamical if and only if the
grading is essentially injective, which can be viewed as a graded version of Renault’s theorem.
In Section 2 we introduce our class of graded groupoids with essentially central isotropy. After
establishing some basic properties of such groupoids G, we explain how to adjust the arguments
in [CN24a] to classify the primitive ideals of C∗(G) when G is amenable and second countable.
We then apply results of [CN24b] to “good” units of G to describe the topology on Prim C∗(G).

In Section 3 we discuss the Exel–Pardo algebras and their higher rank analogues. There are
different ways of introducing these algebras, which in the higher rank case have been shown to
be equivalent only under certain conditions. We discuss exclusively the groupoid approach but
make no restrictions on the graphs (apart from the standard assumption of finite alignment in
the higher rank case), leaving a comparison with other approaches to the interested reader or
possibly another occasion. For a number of results in the subsequent sections, in order to deal
with Hausdorff groupoids, we need to assume that the self-similar actions that we consider are
pseudo-free, as defined in [EP17]. This assumption is trivially satisfied for the actions by graph
automorphisms, but it does exclude some interesting examples of self-similar actions.
The main new result of this section is a characterization of amenability of the groupoids GG,Λ
for pseudo-free self-similar actions G ↷Λ: we show that if Λ is an ordinary directed graph or a
row-finite higher rank graph, then the groupoid GG,Λ is amenable if and only if the G-stabilizer
of every vertex of Λ is amenable.
In Section 4 we study the groupoids GG,E for directed graphs E. Overall the approach here
is similar to our analysis of the groupoids GE in [CN24b]. The key result is a description of
the quasi-orbit space of GG,E in terms of G-equivariant versions of notions that have played
an important role in the analysis of graph C∗-algebras [BHRS02], such as maximal tails and
breaking vertices. This result does not need GG,E to be Hausdorff and is valid without pseudo-
freeness.
The action of G does create additional difficulties, so that we had to rethink the
strategy in [CN24b]. An outcome of this effort is that even when G is trivial, our description of
the quasi-orbit space improves on that given in [CN24b], which used Baire category arguments
to avoid analyzing periodic paths with nondiscrete orbits.
Once the quasi-orbit space of GG,E is described, it is not difficult to understand when the
Z-grading is essentially injective. Informally, this happens if and only if there are no substantial
parts of E on which some element g ̸= 1G acts trivially. In such cases we can apply results of
Section 2 and obtain a complete description of Prim C∗(GG,E) for amenable GG,E. When G is
trivial, we get the same result as in [CN24b], which is equivalent to Hong–Szymański’s description
of Prim OE.
In Section 5 we extend results of the previous section to row-finite higher rank graphs without
sources. The main new ingredient that we need here is a G-equivariant version of certain vertex
sets introduced by Carlsen, Kang, Shotwell and Sims in [CKSS14], which should be thought of
as higher rank substitutes for G-circuits without an entry analyzed in [EP17].

3. Exel–Pardo groupoids and their higher rank analogues
3.1. Higher rank graphs. We recall basic notions of the higher rank graph theory, see [KP00]
and [Yee07] for details.
Fix a natural number k ≥1. A k-graph is a pair (Λ, d), where Λ is a small category, thought
of as a set of morphisms, and d: Λ →Zk
+ is a functor with the unique factorization property,
meaning that if d(µ) = n+m for some µ ∈Λ and n, m ∈Zk
+, then there exist unique morphisms
α, β ∈Λ satisfying d(α) = n, d(β) = m and µ = αβ. For each n ∈Zk
+, we set Λn := d−1(n).
Then Λ0 coincides with the set of identity morphisms in the category, so it can be identified with
