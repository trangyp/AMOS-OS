Introduction
Biological radical-pair systems such as the avian
magnetic compass and brain monoamine oxi-
dase (MAO-A) operate at physiological tem-
perature in unstructured aqueous environments
yet appear to exploit quantum coherence with
a
signal-to-noise
ratio
that
is
sufficient
to
bias
chemical
reaction
yields
[3].
The
3-
Layer Quantum Brain Hypothesis [2] (here-
after “3-LQBH companion preprint”) reconciles
this with the “warm and wet” objection by
showing that (i) protein tumbling provides a
natural motional-narrowing dynamical decou-
pling, (ii) decoherence-free subspace (DFS) en-
coding exploits collective noise, and (iii) a noisy-
reference Petz recovery map [4] can preserve
entanglement beyond the entanglement-breaking
(EB) threshold γc ≃0.3. The system functions as
a quantum reservoir computer (QRC) [5] rather
than a fault-tolerant quantum computer.
At the same time, the SVILC qubit of Wakaura
and Koizumi [1] formulates eight concrete con-
ditions for a scalable nanoscale qubit built out
of topologically protected loop currents around
spin-vortices in a cuprate CuO2 plane, with an
external-feeding-current coupler providing scal-
able two-qubit operations.
We therefore ask whether the organisational
principles demonstrated in both biology and in
Wakaura–Koizumi’s cuprate theory can be re-
instantiated in engineered organic materials such
that (a) no applied magnetic field is required,
and (b) room-temperature operation is possible.
In this paper we answer both questions affir-
matively by numerical simulation, covering al-
gorithmic benchmarks, lattice physics, classical

2
Framework
Before detailing the framework, we make our
positioning explicit: this work unifies four ex-
isting research lines — radical-pair quantum
reservoir computing [3], molecular electron-spin
qubits with EDSR readout and optical address-
ability [6, 7, 8], the SVILC qubit proposal for
cuprates [1, 9, 10], and SSH-soliton qubits [11, 12]
— under a common CQEC-protected framework
inspired by the 3-Layer Quantum Brain Hypothe-
sis. No individual component is new; the novelty
lies in the unified analysis, the cross-path CQEC
comparison, and the manufacturing-cost projec-
tion. The SVILC picture is under active debate
in the cuprate community [9, 10]; our P3 analysis
is therefore a conditional prediction that assumes
the theory holds on the κ-(BEDT-TTF) geome-
try.

P2 electron-pair orientation and P3 “hole”

Because the two-qubit coupling mechanism of P2

P2:
how the 2-qubit PTM electron pair

6
Discussion
6.1
Link to 3-Layer Quantum Brain Hypothesis
The engineered organic paths achieve γeff that is
40 to 9×104 times smaller than biological MAO-
A, firmly below γc. This validates the three “es-
cape routes” of the 3-LQBH preprint [2] while also
showing that the engineered route is fundamen-
tally easier: no DD-via-tumbling, DFS, or Petz
noisy-reference is required to preserve coherence,
though each still adds value.
6.2
CQEC versus fault tolerance
A note on the relationship between CQEC and
fault tolerance:
CQEC is not fault tolerance.
It is a low-overhead, non-topological coherence-
preservation method that is most useful in the
dissipation-limited pre-threshold regime. It can-
not, on its own, drive logical error rates below an
arbitrarily low ceiling as concatenated or topo-
logical codes can. What our results show is that
CQEC and physical protection together lift the
effective error rate below the EB threshold in the
regime where an organic system naturally sits
(Paths 2–4), and in the noisier reservoir regime
of Path 1 it recovers a substantial fraction of the
lost fidelity. A full fault-tolerant layer (e.g. a sur-
face code [41]) on top of CQEC-protected organic
qubits is outside the scope of the present paper.
6.3
When does CQEC help most?
Figure 6 identifies the CQEC effectiveness peak
at γ ≃γc as a universal feature across algorithms
with ∆Fmax ∝log d. This observation strength-
ens the proposal of the 3-LQBH preprint [2] that
the Petz recovery map is an information-theoretic
complement to the raw channel when the latter
is on the brink of being entanglement-breaking.
6.4
Why the hybrid pipeline underperforms
Our current bridge from Path 1 to Path 2 (Sec. 3)
projects the reservoir density matrix onto its di-
agonal before re-encoding. This necessarily loses
the off-diagonal coherences that carry most of the
phase information, hence the degradation seen in

6.5
Limitations
Three limitations deserve emphasis:
(i) all re-
sults are density-matrix simulations; real device-
level fidelities will be lower due to control-field
imperfections, crosstalk, and readout noise not
modelled here.
(ii) Path 3 relies on SVILC
physics that remains experimentally unconfirmed
in cuprates [1, 9]; our κ-(BEDT-TTF) exten-
sion is doubly speculative. (iii) Our multi-copy
SWAP-based hybrid bridge (Sec. III.H) is not the
information-theoretically optimal bridge; a full
quantum channel simulation would require addi-
tional ancillas and is left to future work.
Auxiliary caveats documented for complete-
ness:
CQEC cost scales as O(d6) per round,
limiting us to d ≤64; photoswitch dynamics
is modelled by a single exponential [21]; inter-
bridge J variation is not modelled; the room-
temperature PTM T2 assumption of 3 µs is con-
servative compared to the T2 ∼148 µs reported
by Schäfter et al. [15] for PTM biradicals be-
low 100 K, but allows for the order-of-magnitude
shortening expected at room temperature and
from COF-induced dipolar coupling [7].

quantum reservoir computing can be performed
