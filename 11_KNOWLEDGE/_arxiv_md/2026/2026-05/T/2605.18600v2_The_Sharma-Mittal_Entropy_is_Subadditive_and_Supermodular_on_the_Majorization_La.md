Abstract
We prove that Sharma-Mittal entropy is a subadditive and supermodular function on the
lattice of all n-dimensional probability distributions, ordered according to the partial order
relation defined by majorization among vectors. Our result unifies and extends analogous
results presented in the literature for the Shannon entropy, the Tsallis entropy, and the Rényi
entropy.

Introduction
The mathematical concept of majorization has a rich history, with applications across a wide
range of disciplines. Majorization theory arose in mathematical economics [2], where it was
employed to rigorously explain the vague notion that the components of a given vector are “more
nearly equal" than the components of a different vector. Presently, majorization theory finds
applications in many areas, ranging from pure mathematics to combinatorics [27, 37, 38], from
information and communication theory [12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 30, 31, 33, 44, 48, 52, 55, 56, 62, 64, 65]
to thermodynamics and quantum theory [6, 54], from mathematical chemistry [8] to optimization
[18], among the others.
The quantification of uncertainty and diversity in complex systems relies heavily on general-
ized entropy measures. While the classical Shannon and Boltzmann-Gibbs entropies assume ex-
tensivity and independent subsystems, modern applications in non-extensive statistical mechan-
ics and complex systems demand more flexible frameworks [25]. The Sharma-Mittal entropy [58],
a versatile two-parameter generalization, serves this purpose. By decoupling the degree of non-
extensivity from the deformation of the probability distribution, it recovers both the Rényi and
Tsallis entropies as limiting cases [40]. Several other entropic functionals can be seen as particular
cases of the Sharma-Mittal entropy [60]. Consequently, the Sharma-Mittal entropy has found ap-
plications across diverse domains, including holographic dark energy modeling in cosmology [30],
topic modeling evaluation in natural language processing [34], and the algorithmic quantification
of generative design variety [1]. Numerous other applications of the Sharma-Mittal entropy in
Physics are discussed in [5, 11, 21, 22, 23, 24, 26, 29, 30, 32, 39, 42, 43, 46, 49, 53, 57, 63], and
references quoted therein.
Simultaneously, the mathematical framework of majorization has proven indispensable for
formally ordering probability distributions by their relative “disorder” or “concentration.” In par-
ticular, the probability simplex under the majorization pre-order forms a mathematical lattice—
the majorization lattice—equipped with well-defined meet (greatest lower bound) and join (least
upper bound) operations. The interplay between entropy measures and the majorization lattice
has important physical implications, most notably in Quantum Mechanics. State transforma-
tions under Local Operations and Classical Communication (LOCC) are strictly governed by
majorization [47]. Recent literature has extensively utilized the majorization lattice to model ap-
proximate bipartite entanglement transformations [9], probabilistic pure state conversions [20],

In this paper, we characterize the subadditive, superadditive and supermodular behavior of
the Sharma-Mittal entropy Sα,β(p) on the majorization lattice. Specifically, we prove that the
Sharma-Mittal entropy Sα,β(p) is subadditive for α ≥0 and β ≥1, is superadditive for α < 0
and β ≤1, and that is supermodular for α ≥1 and β ≤1. This provides a unified treatment
and extension of the results in [7, 12, 66]. We also investigate the regime where β > 1. We
demonstrate a fundamental structural breakdown: by constructing explicit counterexamples
(specifically evaluated at α = 2, β = 3), we prove that the Sharma-Mittal entropy generally
loses both supermodularity and submodularity when β > 1.
