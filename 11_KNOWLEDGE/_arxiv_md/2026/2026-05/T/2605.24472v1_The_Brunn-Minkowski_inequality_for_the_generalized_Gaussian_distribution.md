1. Introduction
The setting for this paper is the n-dimensional Euclidean space, Rn. Let µ be a finite Borel
measure on Rn. It is said that µ satisfies the Brunn-Minkowski inequality with exponent α, if
for all Borel sets K, L ⊆Rn and λ ∈[0, 1],
µ(λK + (1 −λ)L)α ≥λµ(K)α + (1 −λ)µ(L)α.
Here, λK + (1 −λ)L = {λx + (1 −λ)y : x ∈K, y ∈L} is the Minkowski combination of K and
L. By the H¨older inequality, if α1 > α2 and the inequality holds for α1, then it holds for α2. It
suggests that the inequality becomes stronger as α increases.
The Brunn-Minkowski inequality has connections with many other fundamental inequalities,
such as the isoperimetric inequality, the Pr´ekopa-Leindler inequality, the Sobolev inequality and
the Brascamp-Lieb inequality, etc. See, e.g., Barthe [4] and Bobkov and Ledoux [5, 6] for details.
The classical Brunn–Minkowski inequality reads: If K, L ⊆Rn are Borel sets and λ ∈[0, 1],
then for the n-dimensional Lebesgue measure Vn,

The Borell theorem accurately quantifies the exponent in the Brunn-Minkowski inequality for
measure µ over all Borel sets. It is remarkable that if the measure µ is defined on sets with more
geometric flavors, for example, the class of origin-symmetric convex bodies in Rn, the class of
convex bodies containing the origin in Rn, or the class of star bodies in Rn, a possible “better”
exponent may exist.
Recently, that looking for a better exponent in the Brunn-Minkowski inequality on some
special classes has become a central topic in the Brunn–Minkowski theory. Several fascinating
results and conjectures are appeared. See, e.g., [2, 3, 12, 13, 17, 19, 21, 22, 25, 29]. Moreover, this
topic is connected [20, 22, 26, 27] to the longstanding log-Brunn-Minkowski conjecture, which
is posed by B¨or¨oczky, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang [9] and states that the volume of the geometric
mean of two symmetric convex sets is bounded below by the geometric mean of their volumes.
