THE RESTRICTED SCHATTEN-CLASS GRASSMANIANN Grres,p(H) AS
AFFINE COADJOINT ORBIT
AMIN TAHIRI AND ALICE BARBORA TUMPACH
Abstract. In this paper, we consider the restricted p-Schatten class Grassmannian
Grres,p(H) consisting of infinite-dimensional and infinite codimensional subspaces W of
a polarized complex separable Hilbert space H = H+ ⊕H−such that the orthogonal
projection from W onto H+ is Fredholm and the orthogonal projection from W onto H−
is in the Schatten ideal Lp, p ≥1. The aim of this paper is to show that, for 1 ≤p ≤2,
the restricted p-Schatten class Grassmannian Grres,p(H) is an affine (co-)adjoint orbit
of an infinite-dimensional restricted unitary group Ures,p(H), and that it admits natural
weak symplectic structures. These results follow from the fact that the Lie algebra of
the restricted p-Schatten class unitary group Ures,p(H) admits a non-trivial 2-cocycle.

2. The restricted p-Schatten class Grassmannian
In the literature, the restricted Grassmannian refers usually either to the Schatten class
Grassmannian Grres,2(H) modeled on Hilbert-Schmidt operators [27], or to the Grassman-
nian Grres,∞(H) modeled on compact operators [28]. In the present paper, we are mainly
interested in the restricted p-Schatten class Grassmannian Grres,p(H) for 1 ≤p ≤2 [13, 29].

2.1. Definition of the restricted Grassmannian Grres,p(H).
Definition 1. The restricted p-Schatten class Grassmannian Grres,p(H) is defined
as the set of all closed subspaces V ⊆H such that
(1) the orthogonal projection pr+|V : V →H+ is Fredholm (i.e. has finite index);
(2) the orthogonal projection pr−|V : V →H−is of class Lp.
An alternative way of stating Definition 1 is as follows.
A subspace V belongs to
Grres,p(H) if it is the image of an injective operator v: H+ →H such v+ := pr+ ◦v is
Fredholm and v−:= pr−◦v is of class Lp.
2.2. Grres,p(H) as homogeneous space of Ures,p(H). We begin with a preliminary
lemma that will be used in proof of Proposition 2.
Lemma 1. Let A: H →H be Fredholm and B ∈B(H) such that BA = idH + P with
P ∈Lp(H). Then AB = idH + Q with Q ∈Lp(H).
Proof. Since A is Fredholm its range is closed, its kernel is finite dimensional, and its range
is of finite codimension ([8, XI. Theorem 3.1]). Choose closed complements H = ker(A)⊕N
and H = Ran(A)⊕M with dim(M) < ∞. Define T to be the inverse of A|N : N →Ran(A)
on Ran(A), and 0 on M. Then T is a bounded operator such that TA−idH and AT −idH
are finite-rank operators.
Multiplying BA = idH + P from the right by T we obtain BAT = (idH + P)T. Since
AT = idH+F for F a finite rank operator, this is equal to B(idH+F) = T +PT, therefore
B = T −BF + PT.
Multiplying from the left by A yields,
AB = AT −ABF + APT.
Using, again, that AT = idH + F, we get
AB = idH + F −ABF + APT.
The operators F and ABF are of finite rank, and APT is of class Lp. It follows that
AB −idH ∈Lp(H), as claimed.
