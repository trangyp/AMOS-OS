TENSOR PRODUCT SURFACES AND GRADED SYZYGIES
MATTHEW WEAVER
Abstract. Let U ⊆H0(OP1×P1(a, b)) be a four-dimensional vector space and consider the rational map
ϕU : P1×P1 99K P3 defined by its basis of bihomogeneous polynomials. The tensor product surface XU ⊆P3
is the closed image of ϕU, and a fundamental problem in this setting is to determine its implicit equation. As
these surfaces are ubiquitous within the field of geometric modeling and design, knowledge of their implicit
equations is particularly advantageous, allowing for more effective and efficient computations. In this article,
we expand upon work of Duarte and Schenck [14] and the present author [25] to solve this implicitization
problem when the bigraded ideal IU admits a singly graded syzygy.
1. Introduction
Given a map between projective spaces ϕ : Pm 99K Pn, a natural problem which arises is to describe
the closed image of ϕ as a subvariety of Pn. As such a map is defined by homogeneous polynomials in
the coordinates of Pm, one must obtain the equations of im ϕ in the coordinates of Pn. The implicitization
problem, determining the implicit equations of a parameterized variety, has become a fundamental question
studied to great length by algebraic geometers and commutative algebraists, using a variety of techniques.
In recent years, the implicitization problem has gained significant interest within the geometric model-
ing community for its applications to computer-aided geometric design (CAGD), see e.g. [12, 21, 22, 23].
Parameterized curves and surfaces are ubiquitous in this area, yet often present computational challenges.
Many fundamental tasks may be computationally intensive given a parametric form, but become trivial
given an implicit model. As such, the development of computationally effective implicitization methods has
become an active area of research, drawing upon ideas from algebraic geometry, commutative algebra, and
computational methods within both realms.
In this context, tensor product surfaces form a notable class of parameterized surfaces. These surfaces
arise as the image of a rational map P1 ×P1 99K P3, and their parameterizations inherit a rich structure from
the bigraded coordinate ring of P1 × P1. This bigraded structure, however, tends to complicate the implicit-
ization problem, introducing additional algebraic phenomena not witnessed in the singly graded setting. To
address this, a variety of techniques have been developed for the implicitization of these surfaces. Common
elimination techniques relying on Gr¨obner bases and resultants are straightforward to implement, but tend
to be computationally expensive, especially in the multigraded setting. In contrast, syzygy techniques and
methods borrowed from the study of Rees algebras tend to be much more effective and are hence preferable,
see e.g. [1, 2, 3, 4, 24, 26]. One such method in particular is the approximation complex Z developed by
Herzog, Simis, and Vasconcelos [18, 19]. Whereas this complex is rarely acyclic, it detects useful syzygetic
information and has been shown to be particularly effective for implicitization purposes [1, 3, 7, 14, 20, 25].
We refer the reader to [8] for a concise overview of syzygy methods used for implicitization and [7] for an
introduction to the approximation complex Z and its applications to the implicitization problem.
In this paper, we consider tensor product surfaces which are basepoint free, i.e. surfaces arising when the
rational map above is regular. Let R = K[s, t, u, v] be a polynomial ring, for K an algebraically closed field,
bigraded by setting bideg s, t = (1, 0) and bideg u, v = (0, 1). Notice that the global sections H0(OP1×P1(a, b))
may be identified with the bigraded components Ra,b of R. For a subspace U ⊆Ra,b with basis {p0, p1, p2, p3},
we assume that p0, p1, p2, p3 have no common zeros on P1 × P1. As such, the rational map
ϕU : P1 × P1 −→P3
defined by {p0, p1, p2, p3} is regular, as its base locus is empty, i.e.
U is basepoint free.

In [20], tensor product surfaces XU for U ⊂R2,1 are studied in this manner by determining all possible free
resolutions of IU. It is noted that the presence of a linear syzygy, in bidegree (0, 1) or (1, 0), on IU imposes
certain constraints and determines some of the differentials in a minimal resolution. This observation is
further developed in [14], where it is shown that the implicit equation of XU can be completely determined
from such a linear syzygy, via the approximation complex, without requiring the full syzygy module of IU.
More recently, in [25] these ideas were extended to the case where IU admits a quadratic syzygy in bidegree
(0, 2) or (2, 0). There it is shown that, similarly, such a syzygy determines a particular submodule of syz(IU)
that is sufficient to determine the implicit equation of XU.
In this paper, we continue this line of work from [14, 25] and consider the general setting that the
bigraded ideal IU admits a singly graded syzygy in any degree. We show that this syzygy S alone determines
a submodule of syz(IU) sufficient to determine the implicit equation of the surface XU, via the approximation
complex Z. The key construction used throughout is a subspace V ⊆U associated to the syzygy S, whose
basis may be taken as part of a generating set of IU. With this, there are a handful of cases to consider,
depending on the dimension of V , with each case exhibiting unique behavior. The main results of this article,
Theorems 4.4, 5.11 and 6.8 are summarized as follows.
Theorem. Assume that U ⊆H0(OP1×P1(a, b)) is basepoint free, and let IU denote the ideal of U. Assume
that IU has a minimal syzygy S in bidegree (0, n) and b ≥2n −1. Let V be the subspace of U associated to
the syzygy S.
(i) If dim V = 2, then IU has syzygies S1 and S2 in bidegree (a, b−n) such that dim⟨S, S1, S2⟩2a−1,b−1 =
2ab.
(ii) If dim V = 3, then IU has syzygies S1 in bidegree (a, b −n), S2 in bidegree (a, b −n + µ), and S3 in
bidegree (a, b −µ) for some µ, such that dim⟨S, S1, S2, S3⟩2a−1,b−1 = 2ab.
(iii) If dim V = 4, then IU has syzygies S1 in bidegree (a, b −n + µ1), S2 in bidegree (a, b −n + µ2), and
S3 in bidegree (a, b −µ1 −µ2) for some µ1, µ2, such that dim⟨S, S1, S2, S3⟩2a−1,b−1 = 2ab.
In each case, the first differential d1 of Z2a−1,b−1 is determined by these syzygies. Moreover, the determinant
of a 2ab × 2ab matrix representation of d1 is a power of the implicit equation of XU.
By symmetry, a similar result holds if a ≥2n −1 and IU has a syzygy in bidegree (n, 0). Moreover, we
note that the additional syzygies on IU are constructed explicitly and their description is formulaic, hence
these constructions may be easily implemented into a computer algebra system, such as Macaulay2 [16].
In particular, the methods presented here yield more efficient computation of the implicit equation of XU
as they do not require computation of the entire syzygy module syz(IU). Lastly, we note that this result
recovers the main result of [14] when n = 1, noting dim V = 2 in this case, and also recovers the main results
of [25] when n = 2, noting that dim V = 2 or dim V = 3 (in which case µ = 1) in this setting.
We briefly describe how this paper is organized. In Section 2, we introduce the necessary preliminary
material required for this article.
We review the construction of the approximation complex Z and its
relation to syzygies, and recall the techniques of [1] using this complex for implicitization. In Section 3,
we introduce the main setting of the paper for a bigraded tensor product surface admitting a singly graded
syzygy S. The subspace V ⊆U associated to S is introduced, and it is shown that there are three cases to
consider, depending on its dimension. In Sections 4, 5, and 6, we consider the cases that dim V = 2, 3, 4 and
construct the additional syzygies above, noting that a basis of V may be taken as part of a generating set of
IU. In Section 4, with this chosen generating set, the key observation is that the nonzero entries of S form
a regular sequence. Similarly, in Section 5 we use that the nonzero entries of S generate a perfect ideal of
codimension two, allowing for the use of the Hilbert-Burch theorem. Lastly, in Section 6 we use that, when
dim V = 4, we have V = U and hence a basis of V generates IU. In Section 7, we conclude the article with
several open questions and observations stemming from the results presented here.

fewer variables. Further computations show that the methods presented here tend to be faster overall.
7. Open questions
We conclude the article with some open questions stemming from the results presented here.
Question 7.1. The assumption that U is basepoint-free is actually used quite sparingly throughout. Re-
gardless of basepoints, the constructions presented here are syzygies on IU and hence appear within the
approximation complex. This raises the question of whether these constructions can be applied to determine
the implicit equation of XU when basepoints are present. In particular, can one determine the implicit
22


## Page 23


equation if IU has a singly graded syzygy and if there are basepoints present which are locally complete
intersections, or if the basepoints of ϕU are in generic position, as in [13]?
Question 7.2. Within the assumptions of Theorem 3.1, we assume that the singly graded syzygy has
minimal degree, in case there are multiple such syzygies. In [14], it is shown that basepoint-freeness ensures
there can be at most one such syzygy when n = 1. As the setting of [14] is similar to the case when dim V = 2
in Section 4, it seems plausible that one might be able to extend this result. Does this assumption limit the
number of singly graded minimal syzygies in general?
Question 7.3. In [14], Duarte and Schenck show that the singular locus of XU contains a line if IU has
a linear syzygy. As the setting of [14] is similar to the general case when dim V = 2 in Section 4, it seems
natural to ask whether this phenomenon persists in general. Do the matrix representations of the implicit
equation obtained here give insight into the singular locus of XU?
Question 7.4. As noted in the introduction, the importance of a singly graded syzygy of a tensor product
surface ideal IU was originally noticed by Schenck, Seceleanu, and Validashti in [20]. There, the presence of a
linear syzygy in bidegree (0, 1) or (1, 0) was shown to impose strong conditions on a bigraded free resolution
of IU when U ⊆H0(OP1×P1(2, 1)), motivating the further study by Duarte and Schenck in [14]. Does a
similar phenomenon occur in a free resolution of IU if it has a syzygy in bidegree (0, n) or (n, 0) in general?
In particular, the syzygies constructed here and the exact sequences in (5.13) and (6.14) may be taken as
summands of such a resolution.
