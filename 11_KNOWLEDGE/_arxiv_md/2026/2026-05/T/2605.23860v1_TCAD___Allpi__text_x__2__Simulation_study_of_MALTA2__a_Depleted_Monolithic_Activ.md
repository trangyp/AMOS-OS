Abstract: In this work, a hybrid simulation framework combining TCAD and Allpix2 is presented
to investigate the sensor properties of MALTA2, a depleted monolithic active pixel sensor designed
for future tracking. The study starts from 3D modeling and transient simulations in TCAD, with
generic doping profiles and simple well structures. The resulting doping profiles and electric field
are extracted and fed into Allpix2 for high-statistics Monte Carlo simulations in both DUT-only and
full-telescope mode.
Simulations reveal a strong dependence of sensor performance, specifically the detection
efficiency and cluster size, on the doping concentration of the N-type blanket at the sensor surface.
The doping concentration is then optimized by comparing simulations with measurement data.
The active depth of the depleted region of the MALTA2 sensor is estimated in both simulations
and measurements using a grazing angle method, in which the sensor is positioned at various
inclinations relative to the beam, covering angles from 0 to 60 degrees. Excellent agreement on
active depth is obtained with the optimal doping concentration, showing a deviation of 2% from the
measured value at a threshold of 450 e−. Consequently, the framework offers a generic toolkit for
sensor studies without requiring proprietary information.

Introduction
MALTA2 [1] is the second generation prototype sensor of the MALTA series, which is a Depleted
Monolithic Active Pixel Sensor (DMAPS) designed for precise tracking and high hit rate environ-
ments in future collider experiments. It is fabricated in a Tower 180 nm CMOS imaging technology,
featuring a matrix of 224 × 512 pixels with pitch size of 36.4 𝜇m. MALTA2 employs two types of
substrates, high resistivity epitaxial layer (EPI, > 1 kΩ·cm) and Czochralski (Cz, 3-4 kΩ·cm) silicon
[1, 2]. The thickness of the sensor varies from 50 to 300 𝜇m. A low does N-doped blanket (STD)
has been introduced to the sensor surface to achieve large depletion region [3]. Additionally, two
process modifications have been implemented for better charge collection: a gap in the N-blanket
(NGAP) at the pixel edge or an extra P-type implant at the same location (XDPW)[4].
A grazing angle study was performed on MALTA2 using the CERN SPS 180 GeV/c hadron
beam, in which the samples were in Cz substrate with thichness of 100 𝜇m and XDPW modification

[5]. The detection efficiency and cluster size were measured as a function of the grazing angle, before
and after the irradiation of 1 × 10151 MeVneq/cm2 for Non-Ionising Energy Loss. Accordingly, the

In this article, a generic-doping-profile-based TCAD + Allpix2 simulation framework will be
presented, with which the sensor properties of MALTA2 are studied in a test-beam environment.
The manuscript is organised as follows: a brief introduction of the framework is given in Section 2;
3D modeling and transient simulations in TCAD are elaborated in Section 3, Allpix2 simulations
(including fast simulations and full-telescope simulations) together with validations against test-
beam data are demonstrated in Section 4; and a conclusion is given in Section 5.

The workflow of the simulation framework is illustrated in Figure 1. MALTA (for telescope
reference planes) [6] and MALTA2 sensors are modeled according to geometrical parameters and
general assumptions on doping profiles, as described in Section 3.1. Key parameters — in particular,
the doping and the electric field distributions — are extracted for sensor description in the Allpix2
[7], after transient simulations in Sentaurus Technology Computer Aided Design (TCAD) [8]. The
MALTA telescope [9] is constructed and configured in Allpix2 according to the parameters listed
in Table 1. A customized module, [MALTA2TreeWriter], is developed to extract both spatial and
temporal hit information, event by event, on each plane. The hit information is then reformatted
in the same way as the MALTA readout[10] does, making the outputs compatible with the official
reconstruction and analysis software. To simplify the simulation, an incremental counter is used to
ensure that only one event is recorded per 500 ns DAQ window, in contrast to real measurements,
where multiple events may fall within the same window.

Transient simulations in TCAD
3.1
The sensor structures and assumptions
Sentaurus TCAD from Synopsys provides a comprehensive suite of simulation tools to evaluate
the electrical, thermal and optical properties of semiconductor devices using advanced numerical
analysis. In this section, transient simulations are performed on MALTA/MALTA2 sensor to study
the charge collection properties under the electric field arising from the doping and biasing of the
simulated devices.

The simulation starts from the 3D modeling in Sentaurus Structure Editor (SDE), where the
sensor structure is defined by the shapes and materials. The materials used in the simulation are:
aluminum for the electrodes, SiO2 for the dielectrics and silicon for the sensor bulk. A silicon
cuboid of dimensions 36.4 × 36.4 × 100𝜇m3 is defined for the sensor bulk, and a constant P-type
doping concentration of approximately 4.4×1012cm−3 (corresponding to the resistivity of 3 kΩ·cm)
is placed. As shown in Figure 2, a constant N-type doped layer (N-blanket) with a thickness of
4 𝜇m is introduced on top of the bulk. At the interface between the substrate and the N-blanket,
an error-function-distributed doping profile in the depth direction is applied to emulate a dopant
diffusion region with a depth of 0.5 𝜇m.

The Doping concentration of the N-blanket (referred to as N-dop) is the main input parameter
in this simulation, and its value is not known a priori. Inspired by Ref. [11], the N-dop values range
from 1014 cm−3 to 1015 cm−3. A value of 3 × 1014 cm−3 for N-dop is used for demonstration in
the following unless otherwise specified. The doping profiles for the wells range from 1015cm−3 to
1019cm−3 depending on the purpose. In each well, a multi-Gaussian superimposed profile is used
in depth direction, while an error-function profile is used laterally. It should be noted that the MOS
transistors shown in Figure 2 are not implemented; therefore, no readout electronics are included.
Once the geometry and doping profiles are defined, the refinement parameters are set for
Sentaurus Mesh (SMesh). A finer mesh improves simulation accuracy, but the simulation time
increases with the mesh size. In this work, two types of meshing strategies are used: a global
refinement with a fixed element size of 5 𝜇m applied to the whole device, and a doping-gradient-
dependent refinement with a maximum size of 0.5 𝜇m and a minimum size of 0.05 𝜇m applied in
wells and interfaces. The total number of elements is constrained to 1 million, considering the CPU
and memory costs.
3.2
Transient simulations with heavy ion model
After the device modeling as described previously, the sensors are simulated in both quasistationary
and transient modes using Sentaurus Device (SDevice). In the quasistationary simulation, the
DC operating point is determined and the electric field and carrier distributions are calculated
after ramping the bias voltage of P-well and substrate to -6V (-30V for telescope planes 3 and
4). In transient mode, the responses of all electrodes to a charged particle traversing the device
are simulated under assumptions of standard drift-diffusion transport, doping-dependent mobility,
Shockley-Read-Hall recombination (SRH) [12, 13]. A HeavyIon model is applied to emulate the
penetration of the charged particle. A normal traversing path is set from the back-side plane with
a length of 100 𝜇m and a transverse impact parameter of 1 𝜇m. The linear energy transfer factor
(let 𝑓) is set to 1.28 × 10−5 pC/𝜇m, corresponding to the energy deposition of minimum ionising
particles (MIPs) in silicon [14].
As shown in Figure 3(a), two pixel layouts are designed for two typical particle impingement
cases: one is a single diode (SD) with a hit directly on the electrode and the other is a 2 × 2 matrix
(MTX) with a hit centered among the four pixels. The induced currents on electrodes as a function
of time for both scenarios are demonstrated in Figure 3(b) along with their time integrals (i.e., the
charge collected). A delay of response of approximately 4 ns is observed in the MTX case when
comparing the peak time of the pulse to that of the SD case, indicating a charge drifting process
under the lateral electric field. Regarding the charge collection, approximately 3500 e−and 3600 e−
(900 e−for a single electrode in the figure) are collected within 50 ns simulation time in the SD
and MTX cases, respectively, demonstrating the highly efficient charge collection capabilities of
the simulated devices. Additionally, over 70% of charge is collected in 10 ns for both scenarios.
Consequently, the integration time in Allpix2 is set to 10 ns, based on the excellent timing response
observed in these transient simulations.

Monte Carlo simulations in Allpix2
TCAD provides the capability to simulate the response of a sensor to a single traversing particle.
However, performing high-statistics studies is not feasible due to the long simulation time and
intensive consumption of computing resources. A joint simulation framework, TCAD + Allpix2,
offers an alternative for high-statistics studies, combining the doping profile and electric field
generated in TCAD with the Monte Carlo methods in Allpix2. Figure 4(b) shows a clear example of
structured electron collection in the depleted region (0.03-0.05 mm in Z-direction). First, electrons
generated by the energy deposition of the incident particle drift vertically toward the wells. They are
then split laterally and collected by electrodes. The electron motions show good consistency with
the well-structured electric field in the sensor shown in Figure 4(a). In this section, the simulation
of the MALTA2 sensor is illustrated based on this joint framework. The operating bias voltage
and threshold are set to -6 V and 650 e−unless otherwise specified. Details of the modules and
configurations used in this work can be found in Appendix A

The pixel-wise detection efficiency, cluster size and timing performance of MALTA2 for the
N-dop values from 2 × 1014 cm−3 to 8 × 1014 cm−3 are shown in Figure 8 after the simulation
and reconstruction. A structured detection efficiency with average value of 99.06%, shown in
Figure 8(a), is obtained for the N-dop value of 2 × 1014 cm−3, for which nearly 100% efficiency
is achieved over most of the pixel area, decreasing only at the edges. Figure 8(e) gives the 2D
distribution of the cluster size for the same N-dop value. Single-pixel clusters are observed across
almost the entire pixel except at the edges, where charge sharing occurs, contributing to an average
cluster size of 1.09 pixels. The differences in timing between the leading hit in the cluster and the
average timing across the matrix, shown in Figure 8(i), exhibit behavior similar to that of the cluster
size, featuring a mean timing spread of 4.61 ns across the pixel. For N-dop from 2 × 1014 cm−3
to 5 × 1014 cm−3, higher and more uniform detection efficiencies are observed as N-dop increases.
Meanwhile, the cluster size increases and the timing spread decreases, indicating better charge
collection and sharing. However, the sensor properties degrade with a further increase in N-dop,
which is consistent with the observations in Figure 6.
These observations can be explained by the change in the electric field with respect to the
doping concentration of the N-blanket, shown in Figure 9. A stronger and edge-enhanced electric
field forms as N-dop increases from 2 × 1014cm−3 to 5 × 1014cm−3, explaining the improved charge
collection and sharing, particularly at the pixel edges. At the same time, the electric field around
the electrodes (marked with black arrows) weakens due to the decrease in the doping-concentration
gradient as N-dop increases.
This low-field region expands with a further increase in N-dop,
and eventually reaches a critical value where the field is too low to support the charge collection.
appearing as a blank region (|−→𝐸| < 500 V/cm) in Figure 9(d), leading to the degradation in sensor
performance.

5
Conclusion
In this contribution, a simulation study of MALTA2 sensor based on the TCAD + Allpix2 frame-
work is presented. Using simple assumptions on well structures and generic doping profiles, the
investigation starts with 3D modeling and transient simulations in TCAD. The resulting doping
profile and electric field are then imported into Allpix2 for high-statistics Monte Carlo simulations,
including fast DUT-only simulation and full-telescope studies in threshold and grazing angle scans.
Simulations are compared to measurements under the same conditions for validation and parameter
tuning.
TCAD simulations demonstrate efficient charge collection for two typical particle traversal
scenarios (hit on electrode and at pixel corner), with total collected charge exceeding 3500 e−and
over 70% of the charge collected within 10 ns.
In Allpix2, doping-concentration-dependent charge collection and charge sharing are observed,
arising from variations in the electric field. Within the simulation alone, a doping concentration of
5 × 1014 cm−3 for N-blanket yields the optimal charge collection and timing response. However,
when compared to measurements, lower doping concentrations — specifically 3 × 1014 cm−3 and
4×1014 cm−3 — provide better agreement. In threshold scan, 3×1014 cm−3 gives relative residuals
below 25% for detection efficiency and below 10% for cluster size. Distributions of cluster size
and time-of-arrival further support the methodology, while pixel-wise residuals indicate that the
descriptions of the doping profile and electric field could still be refined.
In the grazing angle study, simulations with a N-dop of 3 × 1014 cm−3 show better agreement
with measurements, particularly for the cluster size in the direction perpendicular to the rotational
axis. The active depth of the depleted region is estimated from both measurements and simulations,
At a threshold of 450 e−, the measured active depth is 25.9 𝜇m, and the simulated value (with N-dop
of 3×1014 cm−3) is 26.3 𝜇m — both in excellent agreement with the TCAD expectation of 25.3 𝜇m.
The conducted simulations, based on basic assumptions and generic doping profiles, provide
valuable insights into the sensor properties of MALTA2 and can serve as a generic toolkit applicable
to various sensor studies without requiring proprietary knowledge. The discrepancy between the
simulated-optimal doping (5 × 1014 cm−3) and the doping that best matches the measurements
(3 × 1014 cm−3) suggests either improvements for future sensor designs or the inclusion of further
physical mechanisms in simulations.

A.2
Charge carrier generation
To simulate the charge generation in the silicon sensor, a [DepositionGeant4] module is used.
This module interfaces with Geant4, allowing the use of Geant4 particles for energy deposition.
The number of electron/hole pairs created by a given energy deposition is calculated using the mean
pair creation energy [20], fluctuations are modeled using a Fano factor assuming Gaussian statistics
[21].
Shown in Listing 3, a 180GeV/c proton beam with an elliptical cross section (2.0mm × 4.4 mm)
is generated. The beam divergence is set to 1 mrad in both directions in the transverse plane. The
physics list FTFP_BERT_EMZ [17] is used, where the FTFP_BERT model handles high-energy hadrons
(180GeV/c proton in this case), and EMZ enables accurate electromagnetic physics models. The
parameter max_step_length defines the maximum step length in the Geant4 simulation; a value
of 2 𝜇m is chosen as a trade-off between accuracy and speed.

A.3
Import of electric field and doping profile from TCAD
Allpix2 provides a mesh converter tool, mesh_converter, to convert DF-ISE files from TCAD
simulation, containing doping and electrical information, into the mesh models in binary APF-
format or legacy INIT-format (the latter is used this work). As shown in Listing 4, the modules
[ElectricFieldReader] and [DopingProfileReader] are responsible for importing the elec-
tric field and doping profile in mesh models, repectively. To achieve an effective mapping of the
field in the sensor, the keyword PIXEL_FULL is provided for parameter field_mapping for a
single-pixel layout, and PIXEL_INVERSE for a 2 × 2 pixel matrix layout. The field depth is set to
100 𝜇m, which equals the thickness of the Cz-type sensor.

A.4
Charge carrier propagation
A [GenericPropagation] module is applied for charge carrier propagation in this work, with its
configuration shown in Listing 5. The masetti_canali mobility model [22] and the combined
Shockley-Read-Hall and Auger [23, 24] recombination model are used to handle the doping-
concentration-dependent charge carrier transport. The parameter charge_per_step defines the
number of charge carriers from a single energy deposition that are propagated as a group. A value
40 is chosen as a balance between precision and computational cost.

A.6
Signal digitization
The front-end electronics of the MALTA2 sensor are based on a voltage-sensitive amplifier (VSA)
architecture. In a VSA, the charge-to-voltage conversion is governed by the capacitance of the
collecting diode, which acts analogously to the feedback capacitor in a charge-sensitive amplifier
(CSA). Since Allpix2 does not provide a dedicated VSA model, the [CSADigitizer] module (in
Listing 7) is used as a behavioral proxy, with the feedback_capacitance set to 5 fF, corresponding
to the MALTA2 collecting diode capacitance [1].
