Introduction
A first-order real elliptic system on a planar domain Ω⊂R2 has — after the standard reduction to
skeleton form — eight real coefficients governing two real unknowns u, v. Such a system can always
be rewritten as a single complex equation in one complex unknown w = u + iv, but the rewriting is
not unique: different bundling strategies produce structurally different complex forms, each making
different trade-offs between algebraic cleanliness and the regularity demanded of the data. Three
such forms occupy distinct positions on this spectrum.
Vekua’s form w¯z+Aw+B ¯w = F [4, 5] is reached by first solving a Beltrami equation to uniformize
the principal part, then reading off the lower-order data in the new coordinates. The principal part
is consumed in the first stage; the resulting form is algebraically clean — one principal-part term, ¯w
in the conjugate slot — and admits the rich function theory of generalized analytic / pseudoanalytic
functions. The price is the PDE solve, which forces smoothness on the principal-part coefficients
(Vekua works in Dm+1,p on the principal part, Dm,p on lower-order data; see [4, §7.1]).
Bojarski’s form wz −q1w¯z −q2w¯z = Aw + B ¯w + C [7] is reached by an algebraic elimination:
complexify the skeleton, take the conjugate equation, and use the pair to eliminate wz without
solving any PDE. The construction descends to measurable coefficients q1, q2 with |q1| + |q2| < 1 —
the natural setting for quasiconformal mappings with merely measurable Beltrami data. The price
is that the principal part now carries two coefficients, and the conjugate slot holds w¯z rather than
¯w.

The classical reduction inside BV. Equipped with both equivalence relations, the classical
two-stage reduction — (i) solve the Beltrami equation to uniformize, (ii) gauge away the resulting
w-coupling — becomes a single composite operation. Section 6 carries it out and notes a struc-
tural reorganization of the classical pipeline: the diffeomorphism Φ that solves Φ¯z = µΦz already
supplies the integrating factor for the gauge step. What appears in the classical literature as two
independent variable-coefficient PDE problems is, in the BV framework, one — the second stage
reduces, after pullback through Φ, to a flat ¯∂-equation solvable by the standard Cauchy transform
(Proposition 6.4). The classical normal form (0, 0, ˜B, ˜F) falls out as a corollary. This observation is
conceptual rather than technical; the deeper contribution remains the invariant Θ.
Why F does not yield an analogous mass density. A natural question is whether the forcing
term F supports a similar construction. Lemma 8.4 shows that no density of the displayed algebraic
form built from |F|2 can be gauge-invariant: under gauge, F is multiplied by an unconstrained
scalar ϕ, while B is multiplied by the unimodular factor ϕ/¯ϕ. The asymmetry in the gauge action
— multiplicative-by-arbitrary-scalar versus multiplicative-by-phase — is the structural reason |B|
admits an intrinsic 2-form while |F| does not at this algebraic level. (The zero locus of F is, however,
preserved by both actions, just as for B.)
Regularity. The pipeline of Section 3 differentiates the principal-part coefficients of the original real
system once, so we work throughout in the smooth class of Definition 2.1: C1 on the principal part,
C0 on lower-order data and forcing. This is the natural regularity for any diffeomorphism-based
theory in this area — Vekua’s classical Dm+1,p / Dm,p split is the Sobolev counterpart, and the C1
requirement on the principal part is its classical subset. The algebraic portions of the present con-
struction (gauge laws, fiber-Vekua matching, Cayley conversion) admit natural Sobolev analogues
with pointwise identities interpreted a.e.; a full Sobolev formulation of the diffeomorphism action, the
Cauchy-transform step, and the associated invariant theory requires additional functional-analytic
bookkeeping and is not pursued here. Bojarski’s complementary route to measurable coefficients
eliminates one Wirtinger derivative algebraically rather than by a coordinate change [7], at the
cost of the doubled principal part discussed above; the bundle map between his form and the BV
form exists — both descend from the same skeleton — but the gauge-diffeomorphism invariant
theory developed here does not transport, since the action B 7→B ϕ/¯ϕ that produces pointwise
gauge-invariance of |B| requires the ¯w coupling rather than w¯z.

6.3
Residual symmetries
The normal form (0, 0, ˜B, ˜F) is unique up to gauges and diffeomorphisms preserving both µ = 0

is not classified here.

The equations are inequivalent. The function B1 is nowhere zero on D, while B2(z) = z has
exactly one zero on D, at the origin. Suppose for contradiction that D1 ∼D2. By Proposition 7.1,
the zero locus of B is preserved (as a set, modulo the relabelling induced by the diffeomorphism)
under the equivalence.
But the zero locus of B1 is empty while the zero locus of B2 is {0}, a
non-empty set. The gauge factor ϕ/¯ϕ is nowhere zero, so it cannot create or destroy zeros of B; a
diffeomorphism Φ relabels the zero locus of B via Φ, which sends the empty set to the empty set
bijectively, and therefore cannot account for the appearance of a zero. Contradiction.
Therefore D1 ̸∼D2 despite M(D1) = M(D2). The mass is one invariant among several; the
zero locus of B is another, and they detect different aspects of the equivalence class. (No appeal to
multiplicities is needed for this example: the cardinality of the zero set already separates the two
equations.)

Concluding remarks
Summary
We have shown that every smooth first-order real planar elliptic system can be written, by an al-
gebraic and differential pipeline, as a Beltrami–Vekua equation (31) with data (µ, A, B, F). On the
resulting space, the joint action of multiplicative gauges w 7→ϕw and orientation-preserving diffeo-
morphisms generates an equivalence relation ∼that absorbs both classical reductions of Vekua’s
program. The 2-form Θ = |B|2/(1 −|µ|2) dx dy is intrinsic to ∼-classes (Theorem 8.2), and its total
mass M(D) is a [0, ∞]-valued invariant (Theorem 9.2) vanishing exactly on the analytic class and
separating a continuous family of pseudo-analytic equations (Section 10.2).
Two structural by-products supplement the main result. First, the classical two-stage reduction
— uniformize, then gauge — requires only one variable-principal-part PDE solve in the BV frame-
work: the same diffeomorphism that solves the Beltrami equation reduces the subsequent gauge step
to a flat ¯∂-equation (Proposition 6.4). Second, the asymmetry between B and F in the gauge action
— multiplicative-by-phase versus multiplicative-by-arbitrary-scalar — is the structural reason |B|
admits a moduli-theoretic 2-form invariant while |F| does not at this algebraic level (Lemma 8.4).
Regularity scope
The results of this paper are stated and proved in the smooth class of Definition 2.1: principal-part
coefficients in C1, lower-order data and forcing in C0. Section 6 additionally requires µ ∈C1,α and
bounded domains for the Cauchy-transform step in the single-solve normal form. These are the
classical C1 subset of Vekua’s Sobolev tier (Dm+1,p on the principal part, Dm,p on lower-order data;

see [4, §7.1]), and the natural regularity for any diffeomorphism-based theory in this area. The
algebraic portions of the construction admit a natural Sobolev formulation with pointwise identities
interpreted a.e.; a full Sobolev development of the diffeomorphism action and the invariant 2-form
(in particular, specifying representatives, almost-everywhere pullbacks, and the function spaces in
which Θ lives) requires the standard quasiconformal apparatus and is not pursued here. Bojarski’s
complementary construction [7] works under measurability of all coefficients and produces a different
complex form (with two principal-part terms); the bundle map between his form and the BV form
exists — both come from the same skeleton (4) — but its explicit coefficient transformation is left
for a follow-up.
The lower-order coefficient A
The proof of Lemma 8.4 showed that gauge transformations can shift A by an arbitrary loga-
rithmic derivative, so no pointwise algebraic invariant of A exists. The transformation law A 7→
A −ϕ¯z/ϕ + µϕz/ϕ is, however, an additive shift by a deformed exterior derivative — in the case
µ = 0, the standard gauge transformation law of a connection 1-form on a line bundle. Gauge in-
variants involving A should therefore be differential (analogues of curvature), obtained by applying
a deformed ∂-operator to A. We have not pursued this construction here.
Connection to the variable-elliptic-structures obstruction
The pipeline of Section 3, building on the variable elliptic structures program [1], derives the BV
form via the intrinsic obstruction G = G1 + G2i measuring the failure of the moving generator
i ∈Az to be holomorphic on its own algebra. The obstruction G and the mass M live at different
levels: G is built from derivatives of the principal-part data α, β alone, while M is built from the
lower-order B together with the principal-part modulus |µ|. A BV equation can have G ≡0 (rigid
structure) and arbitrarily large mass (Section 10.1 has constant µ, hence G ≡0, but M takes any
value in (0, ∞)). The two invariants are independent.
