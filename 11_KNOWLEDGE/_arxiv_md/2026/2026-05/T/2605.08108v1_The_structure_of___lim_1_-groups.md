THE STRUCTURE OF lim1-GROUPS
IOANNIS EMMANOUIL
Abstract. If (An)n is a decreasing filtration of a module A and bA = limnA/An, then lim1
nAn
is identified with the cokernel of the canonical map A −→bA. In this note, we show that any
lim1-group is canonically of that form: For any inverse sequence of modules (Xn)n there exists
an inverse sequence (An)n as above and a morphism (An)n −→(Xn)n, depending functorially
on (Xn)n, that induces an isomorphism on lim1. The proof is based on Quillen’s small object
argument, as formulated by Eklof and Trlifaj in their investigation of the existence of enough
injective objects in certain cotorsion pairs, and also uses a construction by Salce that provides
enough projective objects therein.
0. Introduction
The right derived functors of the limit functor in module categories were introduced by Milnor
[8] and Yeh [13], in order to compute such topological invariants as the homotopy groups of
the limit of a sequence of fibrations or the cohomology of an ascending union of spaces. Roos
showed in [9] that the higher limits vanish in degrees ≥2, if the indexing category is the
ordered set of natural numbers. (This assumption will hold throughout this note.)
The most manageable criterion that implies the vanishing of lim1 is the Mittag-Leffler con-
dition, that was introduced by Grothendieck in [6]. This condition turns out to be equivalent
to the vanishing of lim1 for inverse sequences of countable abelian groups [5], but the relation
between the two conditions is slightly more complicated in general; cf. [1, Theorem 1.3] or [3,
Corollary 6]. It is shown in [2] that the vanishing of lim1 is equivalent to a certain transfinite
version of the Mittag-Leffler condition.
Another class of examples over which the computation of lim1 is well-understood is provided
by decreasing filtrations: If (An)n is a decreasing sequence of submodules of a module A = A0
and bA = limnA/An is the Hausdorff completion of A with respect to the linear topology that
is induced by the filtration, then lim1
n An is identified with the cokernel of the canonical map
A −→bA. Therefore, assuming that the linear topology on A is already Hausdorff, the group
lim1
n An measures the obstruction to completeness of A. The reader may consult [12, §3.5] for
a proof of this result. Our goal in this paper is to show that any lim1-group is of that form,
i.e. it coincides with the value of lim1 on an inverse sequence that is associated with a suitable
decreasing filtration. More precisely, we prove the following result.
Theorem. For any inverse sequence of modules (Xn)n there is an another inverse sequence
of modules (An)n and a morphism of inverse sequences f : (An)n −→(Xn)n, which depends
functorially on (Xn)n, such that:
(i) the inverse sequence (An)n represents a decreasing filtration of A = A0 by submodules,
(ii) the modules An are free for all n,
(iii) the successive quotients An/An+1 are free modules for all n and
(iv) f induces an isomorphism lim1
n An
∼
−→lim1
n Xn.

The proof proceeds by working in the abelian category C of inverse sequences of modules. We
express lim1 as the Ext1-functor on C that is associated with a certain inverse sequence and
consider the cotorsion pair in C that is cogenerated by that inverse sequence. The reader is
referred to [4] for the definition and basic properties of cotorsion pairs. We use the version of
Quillen’s small object that was formulated by Eklof and Trlifaj [4, 11] and a construction by
Salce [10] to obtain the completeness of that cotorsion pair. It will turn out that the result
stated above is an immediate consequence of this completeness.
Notations and terminology. We fix a unital associative ring R and consider only left modules
over R. As mentioned above, with the proof of the Theorem in mind, we only consider inverse
sequences of modules in this paper and refrain from considering diagrams of modules indexed
by more general directed sets or other small categories.
