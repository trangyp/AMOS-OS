TRACE DEFINABILITY IV: HIGHER ARITY NOTIONS
ERIK WALSBERG
Abstract. Motivated by the “composition theorems” of Chernikov-Hempel and Abd Aldaim-
Conant-Terry we introduce k-trace definability between first order theories. Any theory
which is k-trace definable in a NIP theory is k-NIP and any theory which is 2-trace de-
finable in a stable theory is 2-NFOP. All known examples of k-NIP theories are k-trace
definable in NIP theories. We show that for several of the main examples of k-NIP theories
T there is a NIP theory T ∗such that T is the (unique up to a certain notion of equivalence)
universal theory which is k-trace definable in T ∗. For example the theory of Hilbert space
is the universal theory which is 2-trace definable in RCF, the theory of the generic class k
nilpotent Lie algebra over Fp is the universal theory which is k-trace definable in the theory
of infinite Fp-vector spaces, the theory of the generic k-hypergraph is the universal theory
which is k-trace definable in the theory of a set with two elements, and the theory of Uryshon
space is the universal theory which is 2-trace definable in Th(R; +, <). We construct the
universal theory Dk(T) which is k-trace definable in an arbitrary theory T.

We make this precise using two notions of reducibility introduced in [24, 25]. We recall these
notions. We say that N trace defines M if one of the following equivalent conditions holds:
(1) Up to isomorphism, M is a subset of some N n and every M-definable subset of every
M m is of the form Y ∩M m for some N-definable Y ⊆N nm.
(2) There is an injection τ: M ,→N n for some n such that every M-definable subset of every
M m is a pullback via τ of an N-definable subset of N nm.
There is also a coarser “local” notion. We say that N locally trace defines M if for any
M-definable sets X1, . . . , Xd there is an injection τ: M ,→N n for some n such that each Xi is
the pullback via τ of an N-definable set. Several more definitions naturally follow. A theory
(locally) trace defines another if every (equivalently: some) model of the second is (locally)
trace definable in a model of the first. Two theories are (locally) trace equivalent if each is
(locally) trace definable in the other. Two structures are (locally) trace equivalent when their
theories are. Equivalently: two structures are (locally) trace equivalent if each is (locally)
trace definable in an elementary extension of the other. Classification-theoretic properties
such as superstability, strong dependence, total transcendence, finiteness of various ranks,
etc, are preserved under trace definability [24]. Properties that are defined locally such as
stability and k-NIP are preserved under local trace definability.
We show that any theory that can be produced from T using the Chernikov-Hempel technique
is locally trace definable in Dk(T), and Dk(T) is the unique theory with this property up to
local trace equivalence. If, as is usually the case, we only require finitely many functions,
then we can drop “locally” and “local” here. We need to explain what we mean by “produced
from T using the Chernikov-Hempel technique”. Perhaps surprisingly, this notion is a higher
arity version of trace definability. We say that N locally k-trace defines M if there is a
family E of functions M k →N such that every M-definable subset of every M m is of the
form
{(a1, . . . , am) ∈M m : (f1(ai1,1, . . . , ai1,k), . . . , fd(aid,1, . . . , aid,k)) ∈Y }
for some f1, . . . , fd ∈E, indices ij,ℓ∈{1, . . . , m}, and N-definable Y ⊆N d. We say that
N k-trace defines M if we may choose E to be finite. (Think of the f ∈E as N-valued
invariants of elements of M k.) We extend these definitions to theories as above. Local k-
trace definability in a NIP structure implies k-NIP. As far as I know, every example of a
k-NIP structure is k-trace definable in a NIP structure. As the terminology suggests (local)
1-trace definability is equivalent to (local) trace definability. For any theories T, T ∗we have:
• T ∗is k-trace definable in T if and only if it is trace definable in Dk(T), and Dk(T) is the
unique theory up to trace equivalence with this property.
• T ∗is locally k-trace definable in T if and only if it is locally trace definable in Dk(T), and
Dk(T) is the unique theory up to local trace equivalence with this property.

We now consider the relationship with trace definability. The present notion of (local) 1-trace
definability is equivalent to the notion of (local) trace definability introduced in [24, 25]. We
recall the previous definitions. First, M is trace definable in N if either of the following
equivalent conditions holds:
(1) Up to isomorphism M is a subset of some N n and every M-definable subset of every M m
is of the form Y ∩M m for some N-definable Y ⊆N nm.
(2) There is an injection τ: M ,→N n for some n such that every M-definable subset of every
M m is of the form
{(a1, . . . , am) ∈M m : (τ(a1), . . . , τ(am)) ∈Y }
for some N-definable Y ⊆N nm.
It is easy to see that (1) and (2) are equivalent. If τ is as in (2) and τ1, . . . , τn are the functions
M →N such that τ = (τ1, . . . , τn) then E = {τ1, . . . , τn} witnesses 1-trace definability of M
in N. Conversely, if {τ1, . . . , τn} is a set of functions M →N witnessing 1-trace definability
of M in N then the map τ: M →N n given by τ = (τ1, . . . , τn) satisfies the condition in (2).
Furthermore M is locally trace definable in N when the following equivalent conditions hold.
(3) For any M-definable set X ⊆M m there is an injection τ: M ,→N n for some n such that
X = {(a1, . . . , am) ∈M m : (τ(a1), . . . , τ(am)) ∈Y }
for some N-definable Y ⊆N nm.
(4) For any M-definable sets X1 ⊆M m1, . . . , Xd ⊆M md there is an injection τ: M ,→N n
for some n such that each Xi is of the form
{(a1, . . . , ami) ∈M mi : (τ(a1), . . . , τ(ami)) ∈Y }
for some N-definable Y ⊆N nmi.
(5) If L is a finite relational language then any M-definable L-structure embeds into an
N-definable L-structure.
See [25, Prop. 2.4] for a proof of the equivalence of these definitions, and a proof that local
trace definability is equivalent to local 1-trace definability. It is clear from these definitions
that (local) trace definability is a transitive relation between structures and between theories.

the other and two structures are (locally) trace equivalent when their theories are. We now

3.7. Uryshon spaces. Let R = (R; +, ⊴) be an ordered abelian group and X be a set. An
R-valued metric d on X is a function X2 →R satisfying the usual metric space axioms:
d(x, y) = 0 iff x = y, d is symmetric, and d(x, z) ⊴d(x, y) + d(y, z). We say that an R-
valued metric d on X is universal if every finite R-valued metric space isometrically embeds
into (X, d) and is homogeneous if every isometry between finite subsets of X extends to an
isometry X →X. We say that an R-valued metric is Uryshon if whenever (Y, d∗) is a finite
R-valued metric space, then any isometric embedding of a finite subset of Y into X extends
to an isometric embedding of Y into X. The classical Uryshon space is the unique up to
isometry separable complete (R; +, <)-valued Uryshon metric space.

R-valued metric space UR. Any universal homogeneous R-valued metric space is Uryshon.

If R = (Q; +, <) then UR is the rational Uryshon space and the completion of UR is the
classical Uryshon space. When R = (Z; +, <), UR is called the integral Uryshon space.
