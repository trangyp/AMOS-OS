THE EXTRA SLOW TAMARI LATTICE
SYLVIE CORTEEL, JIHYEUG JANG, AND BAPTISTE ROGNERUD
Abstract. We introduce the extra slow Tamari lattices, a new family of lattices defined on faithfully balanced tableaux.
These tableaux arise naturally from the representation theory of type A quivers, and our construction extends the
classical Tamari lattice and the slow Tamari lattice.
We explicitly describe meets and joins in the extra slow Tamari lattices, and then prove that they are lattices. We
then show that they are semidistributive, trim, polygonal, and congruence uniform. Their join-irreducible elements
are described in terms of a three-color analogue of the positive roots of type A, which leads to descriptions of their
spines and congruence lattices. We also obtain several enumerative results for the extra slow Tamari lattices and their
associated structures. Finally, we derive new structural and enumerative results for the slow Tamari lattices.

1. Introduction
Tamari lattices Tamn are partial orders on Catalan sets introduced by Tamari in his thesis. They are now classi-
cal and well-studied objects in combinatorics, and they appear in a remarkable number of recent developments in
mathematics such as algebra, computer science, category theory, geometry, topology. There are already many gen-
eralizations of Tamari lattices: Cambrian lattices [Rea06], framing lattices [vBC25], ν-Tamari lattices [PRV17],
s-weak order [CP24], torsion classes [Tho12, DIR+23], transfer systems [BBR21, LR26] and many others. The
aim of this article is to present another generalization of a slightly different flavor. Instead of producing a large
family of lattices that contains the Tamari lattices, we introduce a family of partial orders, indexed by the positive

integers, which we call the extra slow Tamari lattices. These new partial orders arise from the representation the-
ory of finite-dimensional algebras and finite quivers, but their definition is purely combinatorial. We view them
as a generalization of Tamari lattices for two reasons. On the one hand, they are defined on a class of objects that
contains a subclass in bijection with binary trees, and the order relation on these objects naturally corresponds to
the rotation of the binary trees. On the other hand, these new partial orders behave like a three-color version of
Tamari lattices, as will become clearer later in the introduction.
In the representation theory of quivers, the Tamari lattices have two classical interpretations. The best known
is the one related to the theory of cluster algebras, in which the Tamari lattices appear as particular examples of
Cambrian lattices of type A. Alternatively, the Tamari lattices appear as lattices of torsion classes [Tho12], or
equivalently as lattices of support τ-tilting modules in the sense of [AIR14]. On the other hand, the Tamari lattices
are also the lattices of tilting modules over equioriented quivers of type A; see [BK04] and also [Hil15]. The first
interpretation is very close to the description of the Tamari lattices using bracket vectors. The second is closer to
the one using binary trees.
It is with this second interpretation that our article begins. The tilting modules are known to be part of the much
larger class of faithfully balanced modules, also called modules with the double centralizer property (see [MS20]
for a recent summary on these modules). They were recently classified for the so-called Nakayama algebras in
[CBMRS21]. For the path algebra of an equioriented quiver of type A, a faithfully balanced module corresponds
to a partial filling of the cells of a Ferrers diagram of shape (n, n, n −1, . . . , 3, 2) with dots such that:
(1) The top-left cell and the border cells contain dots.
(2) If a cell, which is not the top-left cell, contains a dot, then there is a cell containing a dot on its left in the
same row or above it in the same column.
The border cells, highlighted in gray in our figures, are the rightmost cells of each row (the top row has no border
cell). In this article, we call such a filling a fb-tableau of size n. It is easy to see that a fb-tableau of size n contains
at least n dots outside the border cells, and we say that a fb-tableau is small if it contains exactly n such dots.
The tilting modules correspond to fb-tableaux without a free cell, where a cell is said to be free if it lies strictly
below a dot and strictly to the left of a dot. Such a tableau without a free cell is called a binary fb-tableau. Binary
fb-tableaux are small, and they are in immediate bijection with binary trees. There are n! small fb-tableaux of size
n and they are in simple bijection with tree-like tableaux [CBMRS21, ABN13] and alternative tableaux [Vie08].
There are many more fb-tableaux as they are counted by [n]2! := Qn

of order ideals on (the opposite of) the inclusion poset of colored intervals.
Trim lattices have a remarkable sublattice called the spine. This is an important sublattice: it is distributive and
contains the modular elements of the lattice. Since it is a distributive lattice, the spine is in general much easier to
understand than the rest of the trim lattice. In Section 7, we describe the fb-tableaux in the spine of the extra slow
Tamari lattice in terms of tableaux avoiding a specific configuration that we call a forbidden cohook. Since the
spine is a distributive lattice, it is isomorphic to the lattice of order ideals of its poset of join-irreducible elements.
For the Tamari lattice, this poset is isomorphic to the poset of intervals of a total order, endowed with the product
order. For the extra slow Tamari lattice, we obtain the analogous statement once the colors are taken into account.
Theorem 1.6 (Theorem 7.12). The spine of the extra slow Tamari lattice is isomorphic to the lattice of order ideals
on (the opposite of) the product poset of colored intervals.

Definition 2.1. A fb-tableau of size n is a filling of the cells of shape (n, n, n −1, n −2, . . . , 3, 2) with dots such
that
• each cell is either empty or contains a dot,
• the root (1, n) and border cells (2, 1), (3, 2), . . . , (n, n −1) contain dots, and
• every dot, except the one in the root (1, n), has a dot either to its left or above it.
By convention, the fb-tableau of size 1 consists of a single cell (1, 1) containing a dot. We first parse any fb-
tableau and replace some of the dots and border cells with arrows or dots. Every cell or border cell, except (1, n),
that has no dot to its left is replaced by a left-arrow ←. Every cell or border cell, except (1, n), that has no dot
above itself is replaced by an up-arrow ↑. The other dots are denoted by •. For the rest of the article, we use
the following convention: the elements of a fb-tableaux are the entries of the nonempty cells, the arrows are the
elements equal to ←or ↑, and the dots are the elements equal to •. An element • has a left-arrow to its left and an
up-arrow above itself, except if it is the root. However, in the proofs, the root will still behave like the other •. For
this reason, we give it the same notation.

Definition 2.2. We define the cohook H(i, j) at the position (i, j) to be the set consisting of the cell (i, j) together
with all cells to its left and above it.

Lemma 2.4. Let S and T be two fb-tableaux such that Col(S) ⊆Col(T) and Row(S) ⊇Row(T). Then

Proof. Let c be free in both S and T. Then c lies below the up-arrow of its column in S and T. The lowest

Lemma 7.8. Let L be a trim lattice. Then L and its spine have the same number of join- and meet-irreducible

Proof. Since L is trim, it is extremal. By definition, this means that the length of a longest chain in L is equal to
both the number of join- and meet-irreducible elements of L.
On the other hand, the spine of a trim lattice L is a distributive lattice. Every distributive lattice is extremal
by [Mar92], so the length of a longest chain in the spine is equal to both the number of join- and meet-irreducible
elements of the spine.
By construction, the spine contains a longest chain of L, so the spine and L have longest chains of the same
length. The proof follows from extremality.

Theorem 9.1. The congruence lattice of esTamn is isomorphic to the lattice of order ideals on the opposite of the

We refer to [Rea16] for the basic notation and fundamental properties of lattice congruences. The congru-
ence lattice Con L is the poset of all congruences of L, ordered by inclusion of equivalence relations. By [Rea16,
Proposition 9-5.13], if L is a lattice, then Con L is also a lattice. Moreover, the meet in Con L is given by the inter-
section of equivalence relations, and the join in Con L is given by the transitive closure of the union of equivalence
relations.
Since Con L is a lattice, for any elements x and y of L, there exists a unique smallest congruence α on L with
x ≡α y; we denote it by con(x, y). For a join-irreducible element j with a unique element j∗such that j∗⋖j, we
write con(j) = con(j∗, j).
Proposition 9.2 ([Rea16, Proposition 9-5.14]). Let L be a finite lattice and α ∈Con L. Then α is join-irreducible
in Con L if and only if α = con(j) for some join-irreducible element j of L.
By Theorem 9.2, the map j 7→con(j) is surjective, but it is not injective in general. For each meet-irreducible
m, we can define m∗, and consequently define a map m 7→con(m, m∗) =: con(m) in the same manner. A finite
lattice L is said to be congruence uniform if both two maps j 7→con(j) and m 7→con(m) are bijective.
We now show that our poset is congruence uniform.
Theorem 9.3. The extra slow Tamari lattice esTamn is congruence uniform.
Proof. By [CLCdPBM04, Corollary 1], every semidistributive polygonal lattice which has a polygonal labeling is
congruence uniform. In the terminology of that paper, such a lattice is called an HH-lattice, and the polygons are
called 2-facets.
By Theorem 6.4 and Theorem 8.1, the lattice esTamn is a semidistributive polygonal lattice. By Theorem 8.4,
it admits a polygonal labeling. Therefore, esTamn is congruence uniform.
□
In a lattice L, we say that a cover relation a ⋖b forces a cover relation c ⋖d if con(a, b) ≥con(c, d), or
equivalently if c ≡con(a,b) d. For join-irreducible elements j and j′ of L, we simply say j forces j′ if con(j) ≥
con(j′) in Con L. We define the forcing relation by j ≥j′ if j forces j′. For a lattice L, we define a new relation
F(L) on the join-irreducible elements of L as the transitive closure of the forcing relation. For a general finite
lattice L, the relation F(L) is a pre-ordered relation on the join irreducible elements of L. If two join-irreducible
elements j and j′ satisfy con(j) = con(j′), we say that they are equivalent. Under this equivalence relation, the
forcing relation becomes a partial order on the equivalence classes of join-irreducible elements. If L is congruence

theorem, we obtain the following result.

is isomorphic to the poset on order ideals of F(L), ordered by inclusion.

• a ⋖b is a bottom edge of the polygon, and c ⋖d is the opposite top edge or a side edge;
• a ⋖b is a top edge of the polygon, and c ⋖d is the opposite bottom edge or a side edge.
