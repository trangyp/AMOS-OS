TRACIALLY REFLEXIVE C∗-ALGEBRAS
LAURENT CANTIER
Abstract. Motivated by a question of L. Robert, asking whether L(T(A)) = LscC(T(A))
for any separable C∗-algebra A, we introduce and initiate the study of tracially reflexive
C∗-algebras. We first prove that commutative C∗-algebras are tracially reflexive. We also
prove that tracial reflexiveness satisfies permanence properties, such as being preserved
under inductive limits. Subsequently, we expose two criteria for tracial reflexiveness, using
the Cuntz semigroup and a weak version of the Schr¨oder-Simpson theorem, respectively.
In particular, separable topological dimension zero C∗-algebras are tracially reflexive. We
end the manuscript by closing remarks that could lead to further lines of investigation
involving tracial reflexiveness.
1. Introduction
The data encoded in tracial states of C∗-algebras has been proven crucial to understand
stably finite C∗-algebras. It is no surprise that this information is part of the Elliott in-
variant and has played a central role in achieving the classification of (unital) separable,
simple, Z-stable C∗-algebras satisfying the Universal Coefficient Theorem. See [12] and
[18, 19]. See also [34] for a general overview, and [8] for a remarkably detailed and inno-
vative exposition on the matter. Indeed, these C∗-algebras lie in stably finite ones. The
study of dimension functions and the Cuntz semigroup, done in the pioneer works of Cuntz
and Pedersen, proved the existence of tracial states for such C∗-algebras and their strong
link with equivalence classes of self-adjoint elements. See [10] and [11]. To this day, the
understanding of these tracial states remains key to the field, and hence, continues to be
the focus of many works. We highlight a state-of-the art work contained in [15].
For more general C∗-algebras, such as non-unital or non-simple, we know that tracial
states information may be unsatisfactory, as their existence might be compromised. It is
henceforth useful to look at more general objects, such as -unbounded- lower-semicontinuous
traces. The information contained in these maps can be hard to understand, especially
in the non-simple case. The works of Robert, together with Elliott and Santiago, tend to
generalize known facts about tracial states, in the broader context of traces. See [13, 27, 29].
They point out that the functionals on the Cuntz semigroup of a C∗-algebra encode the
information about its traces. The set of these functionals is often seen as a dual of the
Cuntz semigroup. Naturally, the notion of double dual has arised in the process. While
it was unclear whether the canonical candidate would form a Cu-semigroup, Robert has

Theorem. The following classes of C∗-algebras are tracially reflexive.
(i) Commutative C∗-algebras and their inductive limits.
(ii) Separable C∗-algebras such that Cu(A)⊗{0, ∞} is algebraic. Equivalently, separable
C∗-algebras with topological dimension zero.
(iii) C∗-algebra such that Ped(A)+ ∩I ⊆Ped(I)+, for any I ∈Lat(A).

theoretical formulation of the problem.

(Conjecture). Any stable rank one C∗-algebra is tracially reflexive.

2. Tracially reflexive C∗-algebras
This section sets the notion of tracially reflexive C∗-algebras. These C∗-algebras can be
thought of a stable analogue of tracially ordered C∗-algebras, introduced in [20], by taking
into account unbounded traces. We start off with basic facts (probably known by experts)
for the sake of nomenclature and clarity.
2.1. Tracial states and Cuntz-Pedersen relation. For a C∗-algebra A, we denote the
set of tracial states by T1(A). It is well-known that endowed with the weak*-topology, this
set becomes a Choquet simplex. In particular, it is a Hausdorff compact convex set and a
complete lattice with the Riesz interpolation property for the point-wise order.

Among tracially ordered C∗-algebras sit the following classes: simple unital exact stably
finite C∗-algebras, unital commutative C∗-algebras, dimension drop C∗-algebras. (See [20,
Proposition 2.7].) Nevertheless, we can already see that tracial orderedness does not behave
well with regard to permanence properties. Indeed, going to either inductive limits, the
non-unital setting, or the non-simple setting, the information given by tracial states may
be unsatisfactory. In fact, there might not be any bounded tracial functional on the C∗-
algebra at hand. E.g., the C∗-algebra K of compact operators over any infinite dimensional
Hilbert space, has an empty tracial state space and hence, is not tracially ordered. As a
consequence, it is not true that all AF-algebras are tracially ordered, nor it is known if
unital ones are. Similar questions arise for larger classes, such as AH-algebras or inductive
limits of dimension-drop C∗-algebras.
To remedy this issue, the notion of traces becomes the adequate generalization to con-
sider. In view of obtaining a robust analogue of tracial orderedness, we work with traces
instead of tracial states. Let us recall some facts that have been gathered around traces of
C∗-algebras. We refer the reader to [13, 27, 29] for more details.
2.4. The cone of traces and the Robert relation. By a trace on a C∗-algebra A,
we mean a cone morphism τ : A+ →[0, ∞] satisfying the trace identity, i.e.
such that
τ(xx∗) = τ(x∗x) for any x ∈A. By a lower-semicontinuous function on a topological space
X, we mean a continuous map f : X →[0, ∞], where [0, ∞] is given the Scott topology.
We recall that this topology is characterized by its closed sets being the lower-sets closed
under suprema of increasing sequences. Equivalently, any map f : X →[0, ∞] is lower-
semicontinuous, if f−1((r, ∞]) is open, for any r ∈R+.
We denote the set of lower-semicontinuous traces on a C∗-algebra A by T(A). It forms
an ordered monoid for the point-wise operations, which is a complete lattice. Following
[13], we consider a topology on T(A), in which a given net (τi)i converges to τ, if
lim sup τi((a −ϵ)+) ≤τ(a) ≤lim inf τi(a)
for any a ∈A+ and any ϵ > 0. (Here, (a −ϵ)+ denotes the ‘ϵ cut-down’ of a obtained via
functional calculus, after applying fϵ := max(0, id −ϵ).) We may refer to the latter as the
interval topology, that we denote τiv. (See § 3.1.)
Endowed with the interval topology, T(A) becomes topological cone (i.e., the addition
and scalar multiplication are jointly continuous) which is compact Hausdorff and (locally)
convex.

3. The commutative case
This section is dedicated to prove that (inductive limits of) commutative C∗-algebras
are tracially reflexive. The proof relies on arguments exposed in Keimel’s work (see [22]),
where he studied the Cuntz semigroup and the cone of traces of a C∗-algebra, through the
lenses of domain theory.
We highlight that these arguments have inspired the nomenclature of such C∗-algebras.
3.1. Domain Theory and Cuntz semigroups. It has been observed by Keimel and
Thiel that many facts around the Cuntz semigroup of a C∗-algebra, including facts on the
cone of lower-semicontinuous traces and its compact Hausdorff topology τiv (which coincides
with the cone of functionals on the Cuntz semigroup, as we will recall subsequently), could
be directly deduced from well-known results of domain theory. We refer the reader to [22]
and [33] for the close relationship between domain theory and the theory of Cu-semigroups,
and [17] for an overview on domain theory. Below, we summarize briefly but thoroughly,
the main bridges built in [22], on which we walk across to conclude that any commutative
C∗-algebra is tracially reflexive.
A preCuntz semigroup is a monoid (M, +), equipped with a transitive, interpolating,
additive binary relation ≺≺.
This relation naturally induces a topology τ≺≺.
A round-
ideal of M is a ≺≺-downward hereditary, ≺≺-directed subset of M.
E.g., m≺≺:= {n ∈
M | n ≺≺m} is a round-ideal, for any m ∈M. The set of round-ideals RJ (M) of a
preCuntz semigroup (M, +, ≺≺), equipped with the inclusion order and the addition given
by I + J := S
a∈I,b∈J(a + b)≺≺becomes a Cu-semigroup. Furthermore, {m≺≺} is a basis of
RJ (M) and the canonical map M →RJ (M) sending m 7→m≺≺is a (τ≺≺, τScott)-continuous
monoid morphism. (See [22, §3].)
The dual M∗of a preCuntz semigroup M is the set of lower-semicontinuous functions
on M that are also monoid morphisms, i.e.
monoid morphisms M →[0, ∞] that are

Lemma 3.2. Let S, T be Cu-semigroups and let α: S →T. Assume that there exists a
≪-dense subset B of S such that
(i) The restriction α|B : B →T is an order-embedding.
(ii) The image α(B) is ≪-dense in T
Then α is a Cu-isomorphism.
Proof. Let x, y ∈S be such that α(x) ≤α(y). Since B is ≪-dense, we can find ≪-increasing
sequences (bn)n, (cn)n of elements of B whose respective suprema are x, y. Applying α,
we obtain ≪-increasing sequences α(bn)n, α(cn)n whose respective suprema are α(x), α(y).
Therefore, for any n ∈N, there exists m ∈N such that α(bn) ≪α(bm). Since α|B is an
order-embedding, we deduce that for any n ∈N, there exists m ∈N such that bn ≤bm.
Taking suprema on the right side first, and left side then, we conclude that x ≤y and
hence, α is an order-embedding.
Next, take t ∈T. There exists a ≪-increasing sequence (bn)n of elements of B such that
t = sup α(bn). Write s := sup bn. Since α preserves suprema of increasing sequences, we
deduce that α(s) = t and hence, α is a Cu-isomorphism.

4.15. Classification of *-homomorphisms between W-stable C∗-algebras. L. Robert
has conjectured that the (scaled) cone of lower-semicontinuous traces T: C∗→CChoq classi-
fies separable nuclear C∗-algebras, up to W-tensorisation. That is, for any separable nuclear
C∗-algebras A and B, we would have T(A) ≃T(B) if and only if A ⊗W ≃B ⊗W.
(This version of the conjecture would be possible only under the mild condition of preserv-
ing a scaling element. Otherwise, the conjecture should be stated up to stable isomorphism.)
For the class of tracially reflexive C∗-algebras, we see that the functors T, LscC ◦T
and Cu can be recovered from one another. (See e.g. [5, §5.3], for more on the notion
of recovering functors.) More precisely, it follows from [13] that Cu recovers T, and from
construction that T recovers LscC ◦T. Also, for any W-stable tracially reflexive C∗-algebra,
we have that LscC(T(A)) ≃Cu(A) ⊗[0, ∞] ≃Cu(A). As a result, having classification of
these C∗-algebras and their *-homomorphisms in mind, these invariants contain the same
information.
As a consequence, combining the results contained in [28] and [31] with the above, we
deduce that the (scaled) Cuntz semigroup (or equivalently, either of the scaled functors T or
