TORIC RINGS OF SIGNED POSETS AND CONIC DIVISORIAL
IDEALS VIA MATROID THEORY
KOJI MATSUSHITA
Abstract. We study conic divisorial ideals from the viewpoint of matroid theory and
apply the resulting framework to toric rings arising from signed posets. For a toric ring,
we describe the polytope representing divisor classes corresponding to conic divisorial
ideals in terms of matroids. We then turn to the toric ring RP associated with a signed
poset P. We compute the divisor class group and characterize the (Q-)Gorenstein prop-
erty of RP in terms of P. Moreover, we also construct a polytope characterizing the
conic divisorial ideals of RP . This recovers and extends previous results on Hibi rings to
the setting of signed posets.

Conic divisorial ideals play important roles in non-commutative algebraic geometry
and commutative algebra with positive characteristic.
For example, non-commutative
(crepant) resolutions (NC(C)Rs, for short) of toric rings can be constructed by considering
endomorphism rings of direct sums of conic divisorial ideals (see, e.g., [6, 13, 20, 21, 24, 28,
33]). Moreover, conic divisorial ideals are used to analyze the structure of the Frobenius
push-forward of R. In particular, invariants such as the (generalized, dual) F-signatures
and the Hilbert–Kunz multiplicity can be computed using information on conic divisorial
ideals (see, e.g., [7, 22, 26]).
From the viewpoint of divisor class groups, conic modules admit a convenient descrip-
tion.
Indeed, every toric ring R can be realized as the degree 0 component S0 of a
Cl(R)-graded polynomial ring S := k[x1, . . . , xn], where Cl(R) denotes the divisor class
group of R, and that every conic module is isomorphic to Sa for some a ∈Cl(R). Let
βi := deg(xi) for i = 1, . . . , n, and call it a weight of R. Then the conic modules of R are
characterized by a polytope in Cl(R)⊗Z R determined by the weights of R (see Section 2).
In light of the above observations and current situation, it is important to determine
the weights of R and the polytope that characterizes its conic modules. For several classes
of toric rings arising from combinatorial objects, both the weights and the corresponding
polytope have been explicitly determined:
• Hibi rings ([21, 25]),
• edge rings of complete multipartite graphs ([20]),
• stable set rings of perfect graphs ([25]).
On the other hand, for general toric rings, the problem of determining these data is quite
difficult, and, outside the classes listed above, it has not been studied in much detail.
In a previous paper [25], the author suggested that the theory of oriented matroids
provides an effective framework for studying the weights and the polytope characterizing
conic modules of general toric rings. In this paper, we develop this idea in a more precise
and systematic way. As an application, we determine these data for a class of toric rings
arising from signed posets.
Let Φ ⊂Rd be a root system. A signed poset P is a subset of Φ satisfying certain
conditions.
We then define the toric ring of P, denoted by RP , to be the toric ring
associated with the dual cone of the cone generated by P (see Section 4.1 for the precise
definitions).
In [11], this toric ring RP is called the weight semigroup ring of P.
In this paper,
however, we will simply refer to it as the toric ring of P. Some of its properties, such as
defining ideals, Gr¨obner bases, and complete intersectionness, have already been studied
(see [5, 11, 14]).
The toric ring RP may be regarded as a generalization of Hibi rings, which are toric
rings arising from posets introduced by Hibi ([19]). For Hibi rings, various properties have
been investigated, including divisor class groups ([17]), Gorensteinness ([19]), and conic
divisorial ideals ([21, 25, 31]).
In this paper, we extend these results on Hibi rings to toric rings of signed posets.
We summarize our main results as follows:
• We describe the facet-defining inequalities of the polytope characterizing conic
divisorial ideals of a toric ring in terms of matroid theory (Theorem 3.2). Moreover,
we provide a formula for the number of isomorphism classes of conic divisorial ideals
of toric rings (Corollary 3.5).

3. Conic divisorial ideals via matroid theory
In this section, we introduce the necessary notation from matroid theory and establish
our results on conic divisorial ideals of toric rings.
3.1. Preliminaries on matroid theory. This subsection is devoted to recalling matroid
theory. We refer the reader to e.g., [3, 30] for the introduction to (oriented) matroid theory.
Let M be a matroid on the ground set E with the set of independent sets I, the set
of bases B and the rank function rk : 2E →Z≥0. We collect some fundamental objects
associated with matroids:
• For a subset A ⊂E, let cl(A) be the closure of A, that is, cl(A) := {e ∈E :
rk(A ∪{e}) = rk(A)}. We say that a subset F ⊂E is a flat if cl(F) = F.
• A subset S ⊂E is called spanning if it contains a basis of M.
• A subset C ⊂E is called a circuit if it is a minimal dependent set of M. We
denote the set of circuits of M by C(M).
• The dual matroid M∗:= (E, I∗) of M is defined via
I∗:= {J ⊆E : E \ J is a spanning set of M}
with the rank function rk∗(S) := rk(E \ S) + #S −rk(S) for S ⊂E.
• A hyperplane of M is a flat of rank rk(M) −1. It is known that a subset S ⊂E is
a circuit of M if and only if E \ S is a hyperplane of M∗([30, Proposition 2.1.6]).

essary definitions and notation for signed posets and signed graphs.
Let Φ ⊂Rd be a root system. For P ⊂Φ, the positive linear closure, P, is the set of
α ∈Φ which are non-negative linear combinations of elements of P, i.e. P = C(P) ∩Φ.
A Φ-poset is a subset P ⊂Φ such that (i) if α ∈P, then −α /∈P, and (ii) P = P.
For a Φ-poset P ⊂Φ, we are interested only in the primitive ray structure of the
cone C(P). Accordingly, we define eP as the set of primitive generators of the extremal

then replaces each remaining generator by the primitive lattice vector on the same ray.
Although eP may fail to be a subset of Φ, this is precisely the level of generality relevant
to the semigroup rings studied here.
We define the toric ring RP of a Φ-poset P as the toric ring determined by eP, that is,
RP := k[C∨( eP) ∩Zd].

In this paper, we mainly consider the root system of type B. When Φ = ΦBd, a Φ-poset

In this case, P admits a convenient combinatorial
description by means of an oriented signed graph, which we regard as its Hasse diagram.
To make this correspondence precise, we briefly recall the notion of an oriented signed
graph. We refer the reader to [35, 36] for an introduction to signed graphs.
Graphs are not assumed to be simple and may contain loops (edges whose two endpoints
coincide), multiple edges, halfedges (edges with exactly one endpoint) and loose edges
(edges with no endpoints). In this paper, however, we restrict attention to graphs with
neither loops nor loose edges.
This is sufficient for our purposes, since signed graphs
corresponding to signed posets of ΦBd have only (multiple) edges and halfedges.
A signed graph Σ is a pair (Γ, σ) consisting of a graph Γ on vertex set V (Γ) with edge
set E(Γ) and a map σ assigning a sign to each edge of Γ except for halfedges.
An orientation of a signed graph Σ = (Γ, σ) is a map τ from the set of vertex-edge
incidences to {±} such that σ(e) = −τ(e, u)τ(e, v) for every edge e = {u, v}. Equivalently,
one may begin with an unsigned graph Γ = (V (Γ), E(Γ)) and choose a bidirection τ on
Γ, that is, a map from the set of vertex-edge incidences to {±}. Then, for each edge
e = {u, v}, setting σ(e) = −τ(e, u)τ(e, v) produces a signed graph Σ = (Γ, σ) together
with an orientation τ. Thus, oriented signed graphs and bidirected graphs may be viewed
as equivalent objects. One may interpret τ as follows. If τ(e, v) = + (resp. τ(e, v) = −),
then the edge e is directed into (resp. away from) the vertex v, that is, the arrow at the
incidence (e, v) points toward (resp. away from) v.

• A walk is a sequence (e1, v1, e2, . . . , vℓ−1, eℓ) of vertices vi and edges ei, such that

• A signed graph Σ is connected if there exists a path between any two nodes.
• We say that a walk W = (e1, v1, e2, . . . , vℓ−1, eℓ) is closed if e1 and eℓare incident

• A circle is a closed path.
• A signed tree is a connected signed graph with no circles or halfedges.
• A signed halfedge-tree is a connected signed graph with no circles and a single
halfedge.
• A signed pseudo-tree is a connected signed graph with no halfedges that contains
a single negative circle.
• A signed pseudo-forest is a signed graph whose connected components are signed
trees, signed halfedge-trees, or signed pseudo-trees.
• A circuit is a signed subgraph with an inclusion minimal set of edges that is not
a pseudo-forest. We denote the set of circuits of Σ by C(Σ).

5. Toric rings of balanced signed posets
In this section, we focus on the toric rings of balanced signed posets.
5.1. Balanced signed posets. First, we recall balanced signed posets. Let Σ = (Γ, σ)
be a signed graph with orientation τ. Recall that Σ has no loops and no loose edges as in
Section 4.
We say that Σ is balanced if any circle on Σ is positive. It is known that Σ is balanced
if and only if Σ can be converted into a signed graph with only positive edges except for
halfedges by switching operations, that is, for a fixed vertex v flipping the sign of τ(v, e) for
all node-edge-incidences involving v ([35, Corollary 3.3]). Moreover, Σ is balanced if and
only if AΣ is totally unimodular, that is, every minor of AΣ is 0 or ±1 ([35, Theorem 8A.5]).
We say that a signed poset P ⊂ΦBd is balanced if its Hasse diagram HP is balanced.
We now explain that the study of the toric ring of a balanced signed poset reduces to that
of a signed poset in a root system of type A.
Let P ⊂ΦBd be a balanced signed poset whose Hasse diagram is connected.
We
construct from HP a new signed poset in ΦAd as follows. Since HP is balanced, we can
obtain a signed graph Σ such that every ordinary edge of Σ is positive by applying a
suitable sequence of switchings to HP .
If Σ has a halfedge, adjoin a new vertex d + 1 to the underlying graph of Σ. For each
halfedge e, replace it with an ordinary edge e′ joining its original endpoint v to d + 1, and
choose the local direction at v to be the same as that of the halfedge e. The local direction
at d + 1 is then determined so that the resulting edge is positive. If Σ has no halfedges,
we do nothing.
Let P b be the signed poset whose Hasse diagram coincides with this new oriented signed
graph. The Hasse diagram HP b has no halfedges and its all edges are positive, and hence
P b ⊂ΦAd. Moreover, its bidirection defines an ordinary orientation, and therefore HP b
may be viewed as a directed graph. We denote this orientation of ΓP b by OP b.
