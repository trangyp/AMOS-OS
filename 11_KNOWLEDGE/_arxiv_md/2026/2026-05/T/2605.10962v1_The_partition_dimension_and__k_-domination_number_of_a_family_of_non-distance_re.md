Abstract
A partition Σ = {S1, S2, . . . , Sk} of the vertex set V (G) is a resolving par-
tition if every pair of distinct vertices in G has a unique representation relative
to Σ. The partition dimension, pd(G), is the minimum cardinality of such a
partition. Additionally, a subset D ⊆V (G) is a k-dominating set if every ver-
tex in V (G)\D has at least k neighbors in D; the k-domination number, γk(G),
denotes the minimum size of such a set. Determining these parameters is NP-
complete and particularly challenging for non-distance-regular graphs. This
paper consider the Toeplitz graph T2n(W), a family of non-distance-regular
graphs. While some resolving parameters for this family have been estab-
lished, its partition dimension and k-domination number remain unknown.
We close this gap by computing both parameters for T2n(W).
Keywords: Resolving set, Partition dimension, Domination number.

to distinguish (or resolve) two vertices x and y if dG(v, x) ̸= dG(v, y). A subset S =
{v1, . . . , vt} ⊆V (G) is a resolving set of G if, for every v ∈V (G), the distance vector
D(v|S) = (dG(v, v1), . . . , dG(v, vt)) uniquely identifies v. The metric dimension of G,
denoted by dim(G), is the minimum cardinality of a resolving set. This parameter
has been extensively studied in the literature.
A fundamental problem in resolving set theory is determining the partition di-
mension, which generalizes the resolving set concept by classifying vertices into par-
tition classes. This notion was introduced by Chartrand et al. [5] and stems from the
study of metric dimension pioneered by Harary and Melter [10] and independently
by Slater [19].
A partition Σ = {S1, S2, . . . , Sk} of V (G) is a resolving partition if every pair of
distinct vertices u, v ∈V (G) has distinct representations relative to Σ. The parti-
tion dimension pd(G) is the minimum cardinality of such a partition. Finding pd(G)
is an NP-complete problem [7] with diverse applications in chemistry for modeling
compounds [12, 13], pattern recognition, image processing [14], and network veri-
fication [1, 4, 6]. Recent work has also explored the partition dimension of corona
product graphs [17] and some families of trees [16].
Domination is another fundamental concept in graph theory, introduced by Ore
in 1962 [15], largely influenced by games and recreational mathematics. A subset
D ⊆V (G) is a dominating set if every vertex in V (G)\D is adjacent to at least one
element in D. Borowiecki and Kuzak [3] generalized this to k-domination: a subset
D ⊆V (G) is k-dominating if every vertex in V (G) \ D has at least k neighbors in
D. By definition, for k ≥1, every (k + 1)-dominating set is also a k-dominating set.
The Toeplitz Graph T2n(W)
Let n ≥4 be an even integer and [x2n] = {x1, x2, . . . , x2n} be the vertex labels.
Define W1 = {x1, x3, . . . , x2n−1} as the set of odd indices and W2 = {xn}.
Let
W = W1 ∪W2. The Toeplitz graph T2n(W) is a graph where two vertices xi, xj are
adjacent if |j −i| = t for some xt ∈W [18]. Equivalently, T2n(W) consists of odd
vertices V1 and even vertices V2, forming an (n+1)-regular graph with the following
properties:
• Since all odd values are in W, T2n(W) is a complete bipartite-like structure
where every vertex in V1 is adjacent to every vertex in V2.
• Each vertex x ∈V1 has a unique true twin x + n ∈V1 (similarly for V2),
meaning they share identical neighborhoods.
• T2n(W) is isomorphic to the Cayley graph Cay(D2n, Ψ) over the dihedral group
D2n, where Ψ = {ab, a2b, . . . , an−1b, b} ∪{an/2} is an inverse-closed subset of
D2n \ {1}.

While the partition dimension and k-domination number of distance-regular
graphs, such as cocktail party graphs CP(m + 1), have been determined [20], the
problem is significantly more complex for non-distance-regular graphs. According
to [18], T2n(W) is not distance-regular. Although its metric dimension and inte-
grality are known, its partition dimension and k-domination number have remained
undetermined. In this paper, we compute these two parameters for the Toeplitz
graph T2n(W).

Definitions and Preliminaries
This section provides the foundational concepts, notation, and established results
from graph and algebraic graph theory necessary for the subsequent analysis. For
terminology and notation not explicitly defined herein, we refer the reader to [2,8].
Throughout this paper, we consider G to be a simple connected graph with vertex
set V (G) and edge set E(G). The adjacency matrix of G, denoted by A(G) = [aij],
is a 0-1 matrix of order n = |V (G)| where aij = 1 if vertices i and j are adjacent,
and aij = 0 otherwise. The eigenvalues of A(G) constitute the spectrum of G. A
graph is said to be integral if its spectrum consists entirely of integers [11].
For any vertex v ∈V (G), the open neighborhood N(v) is the set of vertices
adjacent to v. The closed neighborhood is defined as N[v] = N(v)∪{v}. Two distinct
vertices u and v are called true twins if they share the same closed neighborhood,
i.e., N[u] = N[v].
A matrix T = [tij] of order n is a Toeplitz matrix if tij = ti+1,j+1 for all 1 ≤i, j ≤
n −1 [9]. Such a matrix is characterized by constant entries along its diagonals
parallel to the main diagonal. A simple undirected graph Γ with vertex set {1, . . . , n}
is a Toeplitz graph if its adjacency matrix is a Toeplitz matrix.
A regular graph G with diameter d is distance-regular if, for any two vertices u
and v at distance r = d(u, v), the following intersection numbers depend only on r
and are independent of the choice of u and v:
• cr = |N(u) ∩Γr−1(v)|,
• br = |N(u) ∩Γr+1(v)|,
where Γk(v) denotes the set of vertices at distance k from v.
The collection {Γ0(v), Γ1(v), . . . , Γd(v)} is referred to as the distance partition of
G with respect to v.
Let G be a finite group with identity 1, and let Ω⊆G \ {1} be an inverse-closed
subset (i.e., x ∈Ω=⇒x−1 ∈Ω). The Cayley graph Γ = Cay(G, Ω) is the graph
with vertex set V (Γ) = G, where two vertices g and h are adjacent if and only if
g−1h ∈Ω.

Main results
Lemma 3.1 For any resolving partition Σ = {S1, . . . , Sk} of V (T2n(W)), any two
adjacent vertices in V1 (or V2) must belong to distinct parts of Σ.
Proof. Recall that V (T2n(W)) = V1 ∪V2, where V1 = {x1, x3, . . . , x2n−1} and V2 =
{x2, x4, . . . , x2n} represent the sets of odd and even indexed vertices, respectively.
Let Σ = {S1, . . . , Sk} be a resolving partition of V (T2n(W)).
Suppose xi, xj ∈V1 are adjacent vertices (the proof for V2 is analogous). It can
be verified that xi and xj possess identical neighborhoods in T2n(W), identifying
them as true twins. Consequently, for any vertex xt ∈V (T2n(W)) \ {xi, xj}, the
distances satisfy d(xi, xt) = d(xj, xt).
Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that xi and xj belong to the same part
Sl ∈Σ. Then, for every part St ∈Σ, the distance from each vertex to the part
is identical, i.e., d(xi, St) = d(xj, St). Specifically, for t = l, we have d(xi, Sl) =
d(xj, Sl) = 0. This implies that the representations r(xi|Σ) and r(xj|Σ) are identical,
which contradicts the definition of Σ as a resolving partition. Thus, adjacent vertices
in V1 (or V2) must be placed in distinct parts of Σ.
□
Proposition 3.2 Let T2n(W) be the Toeplitz graph with n ≥4 even, and let Σ =
{S1, . . . , Sk} be a partition of V (T2n(W)). If there exists a part Sl ∈Σ such that
Sl ⊆V1 (or Sl ⊆V2) and |Sl| > n
2, then Σ is not a resolving partition.
Proof. As established, V (T2n(W)) is partitioned into the odd vertices V1 and even
vertices V2. Suppose there exists a part Sl ∈Σ such that Sl ⊆V1 and |Sl| > n
2 (the
case for V2 is similar).
In the subgraph induced by V1, the maximum size of an independent set (a
subset where all vertices are at distance at least 2) is exactly n
2. Since |Sl| > n
2, by
the pigeonhole principle, Sl must contain at least two vertices that are adjacent in
T2n(W). According to Lemma 3.1, any partition containing adjacent vertices of V1
within the same part cannot be a resolving partition. Therefore, Σ is not a resolving
partition of V (T2n(W)).
□

Proposition 3.3 Let T2n(W) be the Toeplitz graph with n ≥4 even, and let Σ =
{S1, . . . , Sk} be a resolving partition of V (T2n(W)). If Sl ∈Σ is a maximum inde-
pendent set of the subgraph induced by V1 (or V2), then all vertices in V1 \ Sl (or
V2 \ Sl) must belong to distinct singleton parts in Σ.
Proof.
Let V1 = {x1, x3, . . . , x2n−1} and V2 = {x2, x4, . . . , x2n}. Suppose Sl ⊆V1
is a part in Σ such that all elements in Sl are at pairwise distance 2 (the case for V2
is analogous). Without loss of generality, let Sl = {x1, x3, . . . , xn−1}.
We observe that each vertex in Sl has a unique neighbor in V1\Sl and n neighbors
in V2. If there exists a part St ∈Σ (t ̸= l) that contains two or more vertices from
V1\Sl, the symmetry of the twin relations in T2n(W) implies that at least two vertices
in the part Sl will have identical distance representations relative to Σ. Specifically,
their distance to St and other parts would become indistinguishable, contradicting
the assumption that Σ is a resolving partition. Therefore, every vertex in V1 \ Sl
must reside in its own singleton part.
□

is γ1(T2n(W)) = 2.

Conclusion
In this paper, we investigated the resolving partition properties and domination
parameters of the Toeplitz graph T2n(W) with vertex set {x1, x2, . . . , x2n} and con-
nection set W = {x1, x3, . . . , x2n−1}∪{xn}. Our main contributions are summarized
as follows:
1. We established a lower bound for the partition dimension of this graph family,
proving that pd(T2n(W)) ≥⌈2√n −1⌉.
2. We determined the k-domination number for T2n(W), showing that γk(T2n(W)) =
2k for the specified ranges of k.
These results contribute to the structural understanding of non-distance-regular
graphs and provide a foundation for further exploration of other resolving parameters
within this class of Toeplitz graphs.
