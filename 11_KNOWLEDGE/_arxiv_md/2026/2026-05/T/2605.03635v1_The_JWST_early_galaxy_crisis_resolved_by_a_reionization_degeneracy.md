The JWST early galaxy crisis resolved by a
reionization degeneracy
Zihan Wang1* and Huanyuan Shan2*
1*Department of Physics, University of Oxford, Oxford, OX1 3PU, UK.
2*Shanghai Astronomical Observatory, Chinese Academy of Sciences,

Abstract
JWST’s discovery of unexpectedly bright z > 10 galaxies has triggered claims
that standard ΛCDM cannot reproduce their abundances, while estimates of the
ionizing escape fraction fesc at z > 6 have spanned a factor of four for over a
decade. Here we show that both tensions arise from a structural degeneracy in
reionization equations: global observables constrain only the product fesc × f⋆,0
(peak star formation efficiency), not individual parameters. We demonstrate that
this degeneracy, previously considered a limitation, provides a precise diagnostic
framework. By leveraging JWST UV luminosity function shapes to independently
constrain f⋆,0, we derive robust bounds on fesc. Joint profile-likelihood anal-
ysis across Gaussian, log-normal, and duty-cycle burst scatter models excludes
the proposed crisis threshold (ε > 3.5%) at 4.5σ confidence, with stochastic
star formation histories strengthening rather than weakening the result. Combin-
ing these constraints with constant and evolving f⋆,0 measurements yields the
first empirical reconstruction of fesc(z) across z = 7–12. A constant-efficiency
scenario (fesc ≈10–16%) connects smoothly to low-redshift direct detections,
whereas an evolving scenario (fesc ≈6% at z = 12) conflicts with low-
metallicity ISM porosity expectations. JWST Cycle 3–4 will distinguish these
pathways at > 2σ, transforming a long-standing fundamental inference barrier
into a powerful quantitative probe of early-universe physics.

Introduction
The discovery of unexpectedly UV-bright galaxies at z > 10 by JWST [1] appeared
to require baryon conversion efficiencies ε ≳3.5%, far exceeding the ∼1–2% predicted
by abundance-matching models and prompting claims of a crisis for ΛCDM. Pro-
posed solutions include enhanced star formation efficiency, feedback-free starbursts,
stochastic star formation histories [2], and modified cosmologies, yet no consensus has
emerged. Independently, published estimates of the ionizing escape fraction fesc at
z > 6 span a factor of four, from 5% [3] to 21% [4], despite being derived from iden-
tical Planck and JWST datasets [5, 6]. No systematic error has been identified, and
the disagreement has persisted for over a decade.
We show that these two apparently unrelated tensions share a single structural ori-
gin. The reionization equations constrain only the product fesc ×f⋆,0, where f⋆,0 is the
peak star formation efficiency, not the individual factors. Any combination preserving
this product fits all standard reionization observables identically. As shown in Fig. 1,
published fesc estimates trace different positions along a one-dimensional degeneracy
ridge in the (f⋆,0, fesc) plane, and the JWST crisis corresponds to one extreme of this
ridge where ε appears anomalously large. A companion analysis demonstrates that
this degeneracy is algebraically exact across all ˙nion-dependent probes, confirmed with
large suite of N-body simulations [7]. Here we exploit the degeneracy as a measurement
tool. JWST UV luminosity function measurements constrain f⋆,0(z) independently,
breaking the ridge and enabling the first empirical reconstruction of fesc(z) across
z = 7–12.
We perform a joint profile-likelihood analysis over the (f⋆,0, fesc) parameter space,
testing three physically motivated scatter models: Gaussian, log-normal asymmet-
ric, and a duty-cycle burst model that captures the core physics of stochastic
star formation histories. The crisis threshold ε > 3.5% is excluded at 4.5σ. Con-
trary to expectations, stochastic star formation histories strengthen rather than
weaken this exclusion, because the additional model freedom benefits the baseline
fit more than the crisis point. We combine the ridge constraint with three indepen-
dent JWST measurements of f⋆,0(z) obtained from UV luminosity functions [8], the
Uchuu-UniverseMachine model [9], and stellar mass functions [10], and propagate all
uncertainties through a 5,000-draw Monte Carlo.
The reconstruction yields two distinct physical pathways. A constant-efficiency
scenario produces fesc ≈10–16%, connecting smoothly to direct Lyman-continuum
detections at z < 4 [11, 12] and providing a parsimonious account of escape fraction
evolution across cosmic history. An evolving-efficiency scenario requires fesc to decline
to ∼6% by z = 12, in tension with the expected increase in interstellar medium
porosity at low metallicity [13], and independently produces a Thomson optical depth
τ = 0.036, rejected at 2.6σ by Planck. Three independent lines of evidence thus
disfavour the evolving pathway: the τ deficit, the contradiction with ISM porosity
models, and the convergence of two of three f⋆,0(z) methods on the constant-efficiency
reconstruction. For over a decade, every attempt to measure how ionizing photons
escaped the first galaxies produced a different answer. Every answer was correct.

The degeneracy ridge
The ionized fraction of the intergalactic medium evolves according to a balance
between ionizing photon production and recombination (see Methods). The key struc-
tural property is that the reionization observables—Thomson optical depth τe and
neutral fraction history ¯xHI(z)—depend on the galaxy population exclusively through
the comoving ionizing emissivity ˙nion ∝fesc ×f⋆,0 ×ξion. Any combination of fesc and
f⋆,0 that preserves this product yields identical reionization histories.
We calibrate the product using Planck 2018 cosmological parameters [14] and verify
the degeneracy numerically at five parameter pairs along the ridge (Table 1). All
five produce τe = 0.0551, ¯xHI(z = 7) = 0.234, and ¯xHI(z = 8) = 0.430 to four
significant figures, confirming that the degeneracy is exact. The calibrated product
is fesc × f⋆,0 = 0.00247 ± 0.00032, with the 13% error propagated from the Planck
τe uncertainty. The baryon conversion efficiency ranges from 1.2% to 4.9% across the
ridge, demonstrating that the crisis interpretation (ε > 3.5%) depends entirely on
which position is assumed but not on the data.
Figure 1 shows that eleven published fesc estimates from the past decade—derived
from photon budget models, direct Lyman continuum detections, and radiation-
hydrodynamic simulations—all fall along or near the degeneracy ridge within their
uncertainties. Values spanning fesc = 5% to 21% are not discrepant measurements;
they are consistent determinations of the same product, differing only in the assumed
f⋆,0.
Breaking the degeneracy with JWST
While reionization observables constrain only the product, the UV luminosity func-
tion constrains f⋆,0 independently through its shape. A higher f⋆,0 shifts the UV
luminosity–halo mass relation, changing the predicted UVLF normalisation, faint-end
slope, and bright-end cutoff. We quantify this with a joint profile likelihood over a
14×16 grid in (f⋆,0, fesc), profiling over the faint-end slope αlo and UV scatter σUV at
each point (see Methods). The UVLF data comprise 28 points from Donnan et al. [8]
at z = 9–12.5, supplemented by Harikane et al. [15].

The result (Fig. 2) shows that the ∆χ2 < 4 region follows the ridge at f⋆,0 ≲0.025

threshold ε = 3.5%, the profiled ∆χ2 = 13.4 (3.7σ). The mechanism is that high f⋆,0
overpredicts the bright end of the UVLF (Fig. 3c,d); the optimizer compensates by
pushing αlo > 3 and σUV < 0.5 mag, but the data reject these extreme values.
The crisis exclusion is robust to the choice of scatter model. We test three physi-
cally motivated prescriptions (see Methods): Gaussian (∆χ2 = 13.4, 3.7σ), log-normal
asymmetric (∆χ2 = 11.8, 3.4σ), and duty-cycle burst (∆χ2 = 20.2, 4.5σ). The duty-
cycle model, which most closely mimics the stochastic star formation histories invoked
to explain the bright-end excess [2, 16], produces the strongest exclusion. At the
baseline (f⋆,0 = 0.019), the burst component improves the fit by capturing residual
bright-end features. At the crisis point (f⋆,0 = 0.035), the optimizer drives the burst
fraction to zero because the model already overpredicts the bright end—adding bursts
makes it worse. The additional model freedom benefits the baseline more than the
crisis, widening the ∆χ2 gap. We validate the exclusion with 500-realisation Monte
Carlo (p < 0.005; see Methods).

Physical implications
The first empirical fesc(z) from z = 0 to 12. Direct Lyman continuum detections
at z < 4 give fesc ∼5–10% [12, 17–19]. The constant-efficiency reconstruction extends
this to fesc ≈10–16% at z = 7–12, producing a mildly rising trend across 12 billion
years. This is the first coherent picture of escape fraction evolution from a single self-
consistent framework. The mild increase toward higher redshift is physically expected:
galaxies at z > 7 have lower metallicities, more compact morphologies, and more
vigorous feedback driving low-column-density channels [6, 20, 21].

Crisis resolution. The crisis threshold ε > 3.5% is excluded at > 3σ under all
three scatter models, reaching 4.5σ under duty-cycle bursts. In the fesc framework,
the exclusion takes a physical form: ε > 3.5% requires fesc < 7%, below both the low-z
direct detections and the reconstructed high-z values under all three f⋆,0 inputs.
Discriminating power. Three independent lines of evidence disfavour the
evolving-efficiency scenario: (i) the Thomson optical depth rejects it at 2.6σ; (ii) it
requires a 47% decline in ISM porosity at low metallicity, contradicting all RHD sim-
ulations; (iii) two independent f⋆,0 methods (UVLF shape and SMF) converge on
constant efficiency while only the UniverseMachine predicts evolution. With current
data, the statistical separation is ∼1.0σ at z = 12; JWST Cycle 3–4 spectroscopic
surveys will provide f⋆,0(z) at ∼15% precision and NIRSpec will constrain ξion(z)
directly, projecting > 2σ statistical separation. SKA1-Low 21 cm observations [22, 23]
can constrain fesc through the ionization topology [24], providing an independent
cross-check that does not pass through the product degeneracy.
Conclusions
We have shown that the JWST early galaxy crisis and the long-standing scatter in fesc
estimates both originate from a structural degeneracy in the reionization equations.
By converting this degeneracy into a measurement tool, we obtain:
(i) The product fesc × f⋆,0 = 0.00247 ± 0.00032 defines a degeneracy ridge along
which ε ranges from 0.5% to 10%.
(ii) The crisis threshold (ε > 3.5%) is excluded at > 3σ under all three scatter
models tested, reaching 4.5σ under duty-cycle bursts, and independently disfavoured
by the implied fesc < 7%.
(iii) The first empirical fesc(z) reconstruction spanning z = 0–12: a mildly rising
fesc ≈5% at z ∼3 to ∼10–16% at z = 7–12 under constant efficiency, connecting
direct Lyman continuum detections to the reionization epoch.
(iv) Three independent lines of evidence disfavour evolving f⋆,0: the Thomson
optical depth rejects it at 2.6σ; the implied 47% decline in intrinsic ISM porosity
contradicts radiation-hydrodynamic simulations; and two of three independent f⋆,0
methods converge on constant efficiency.
The reionization degeneracy, properly exploited, resolves the JWST crisis and
converts a long-standing inference barrier into a quantitative probe of early-universe
physics.

Scatter model tests
We test three scatter prescriptions at the crisis point:
Gaussian (2 nuisance parameters: αlo, σUV): standard symmetric magnitude
scatter. ∆χ2 = 13.4 (3.7σ).

Mass-dependent fesc
The reconstruction assumes population-averaged fesc. Adopting the Ma et al. [6]
fesc(M) relation (∼20–40% at Mhalo ∼109 M⊙, ≲5% at 1011 M⊙), the mass-
weighting effect widens the gap from ∼4% to ∼6–7% at z = 12, increasing the
separation from 1.2σ to 1.8σ. Other simulations [20, 21] give somewhat different slopes,
introducing factor-of-two uncertainty in this correction.
