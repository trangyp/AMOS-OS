THE EQUATIONS OF GENERAL HASSETT MAXIMAL CUBIC FOURFOLDS
ELAD GAL AND HOWARD NUER
Abstract. In this note, we discuss Hassett maximal cubic fourfolds and construct an explicit
irreducible component of maximal dimension sixteen of the locus Z of Hassett maximal cubic
fourfolds. We utilize algebraic and arithmetic methods to analyze the associated lattice of these
fourfolds. By studying general integral quadratic forms and proving the ADC property for a specific
ternary form, we demonstrate that the primitive image of our lattice spans the entire Hassett subset,
confirming the Hassett maximality of the cubic fourfolds we describe.
1. Introduction
Cubic fourfolds occupy an important place in modern algebraic geometry, largely due to their rich
Hodge theory and the elusive nature of their rationality problem. For a cubic fourfold X ⊂P5, the
algebraic cohomology lattice A(X) := H4(X, Z) ∩H2,2(X) is positive-definite and always contains
the square of the hyperplane class, h2
X.
A cubic fourfold is termed special if the rank of its associated lattice A(X) is at least 2. Hassett
[5] showed that the locus of special cubic fourfolds is a union of irreducible divisors Cd, where
d is the discriminant, and these divisors are nonempty if and only if d is in the Hassett subset
H := {n ∈N≥8 : n ≡0, 2 (mod 6)}.
Generalizing this, Yang and Yu [11] defined an M-polarizable cubic fourfold as one admitting
a primitive embedding of the positive-definite lattice M into A(X) such that h2
X ∈M, and they
introduced the subvariety CM of the moduli space C of cubic fourfolds parametrizing those cubic
fourfolds that are M-polarizable. The intersection of all Hassett divisors, denoted Z := T
d∈H Cd,
represents fourfolds with maximally rich geometric structures and were coined Hassett maximal in
[7]. In our previous work [4], we established that the dimension of Z is 16 by first showing that
any irreducible component must be of the form CM for some positive definite lattice M of minimal
rank among those primitively representing H, and then showing that this minimal rank is four.
However, the explicit relationship between the lattice M and the geometry of those cubic four-
folds [X] ∈CM remained opaque. The purpose of this note is to construct an explicit family of
cubic polynomials whose vanishing sets are Hassett maximal cubic fourfolds, providing a concrete
geometric description of the cubic fourfolds in a sixteen dimensional irreducible component CM.
Specifically, we prove the following theorem:
Theorem 1.1. There exists an irreducible component of Z of dimension 16 whose general member
is isomorphic to the vanishing locus of some homogeneous cubic polynomial
F ∈(x, y, z) ∩(x, y, u) ∩(x, z, v) ∩(v −by, u −az, w)
for a, b ∈C.

Therefore, I(P2) shares a 2-dimensional subspace of linear forms

Similarly, P1 ∩P3 is a line, so I(P3) also shares a 2-dimensional subspace of linear forms with

out a point.

Now we show that this construction dominates the irreducible sixteen dimensional subvariety

Theorem 2.4. dim(N) = 16, implying that the generic element of CM is contained in N.
Proof. Consider the parameter space of the plane arrangements P := {(P1, P2, P3, P4) | Pi ∩
Pj is as required} ⊂Gr(3, 6)4. The group PGL(6, C) acts on P diagonally. Up to this action, the
planes can be classified by α, β ∈{0, 1}. The dimension of the PGL(6, C)-orbit Oα,β is 28−δα0−δβ0.
Analyzing the incidence correspondence S := {((P1, . . . , P4), X) | S Pi ⊂X} ⊂P ×|OP5(3)|, the
fiber dimension over a point in Oα,β is 23+δα0 +δβ0. Therefore, the total dimension of p−1
1 (Oα,β) is
51. Since a smooth cubic fourfold contains at most 405 planes [3], the projection to the moduli space
of cubics is quasi-finite. Factoring out the 35-dimensional PGL(6, C) action yields 51 −35 = 16 for
the image N in the moduli space C.
□
Remark 2.5. While technically we should have written Nα,β and Mα,β as the parameter space
P splits into four distinct PGL(6, C)-orbits corresponding to (α, β) ∈{(0, 0), (1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1)},
these four configurations define the exact same abstract lattice M up to isometry, and thus collapse
into a single irreducible component in the moduli space C.
Algebraically, if we denote the plane classes as a = [P1], b = [P2], c = [P3], and d = [P4], the (0, 0)
configuration corresponds to d · b = 0 and d · c = 0. Using the relation h2
X = [P1] + [P2] + [Presidual]
from Theorem 2.1, we can define new classes b′ := o −a −b and c′ := o −a −c. Substituting these
transforms the intersection numbers from 0 to 1, demonstrating that all four intersection matrices
are integer basis changes of the same lattice M.
Geometrically, Voisin’s theorem implies that the hyperplane H ∼= P4 spanned by the intersecting
planes P1 and P2 cuts out a cubic threefold in X that splits into P1 ∪P2 ∪Presidual. If P4 is disjoint
from P2, it must intersect Presidual in a point. Thus, the four orbits merely represent choices of
different planes within the exact same family of cubic fourfolds.
3. ADC Forms and Hassett-Maximality
Now that we have established that N fills an irreducible subvariety CM of dimension 16, it
remains to prove that CM is an irreducible component of the intersection of all Hassett divisors Z.
For this we must analyze the lattice L(M), see [4, Definition 3.2]. By utilizing the distinguished
element o, we obtain a rank-4 lattice with the associated positive-definite quadratic form:
F(x, y, z, u) = 4(2x2 + 2y2 + 2z2 −2xy −2xz + yz) + 2u(4u −x −y −z)

by a rational vector is also representable by an integer vector.

Theorem 3.2. The ternary quadratic form Q3(x, y, z) = x2 + y2 + 3z2 and the form G(x, y, z) =
x2 + 3y2 + 3z2 are ADC forms.
Proof. The fact that Q3(x, y, z) = x2 +y2 +3z2 is an ADC form is a classically established property
of regular ternary forms (for example see [2, Table 2]); a self-contained, geometric proof extending
Davenport-Cassels descent via torus partitioning is provided for completeness in Appendix A.
To see that G inherits the ADC property directly from Q3, observe the algebraic identity:
Q3(3y, 3z, x) = (3y)2 + (3z)2 + 3x2 = 3(x2 + 3y2 + 3z2) = 3G(x, y, z)
Suppose n ∈Z is represented rationally by G, meaning 3n is represented rationally by Q3. Because
Q3 is an ADC form, there exist integers A, B, C ∈Z such that Q3(A, B, C) = 3n, or A2+B2+3C2 =
3n. Reducing this equation modulo 3 yields A2 + B2 ≡0 (mod 3). Since −1 is not a quadratic
residue modulo 3, this requires A ≡0 (mod 3) and B ≡0 (mod 3). Writing A = 3Y and B = 3Z
for integers Y, Z, and letting X = C, we obtain 3n = Q3(3Y, 3Z, X) = 3G(X, Y, Z). Dividing by 3
gives n = G(X, Y, Z) over the integers, confirming that G is an ADC form.
□
