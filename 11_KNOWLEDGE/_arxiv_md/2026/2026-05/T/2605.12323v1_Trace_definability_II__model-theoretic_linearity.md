TRACE DEFINABILITY II: MODEL-THEORETIC LINEARITY
ERIK WALSBERG
Abstract. We give examples of NIP structures in which new algebraic structure appears in
the Shelah completion. In particular we construct a weakly o-minimal structure M such that
M does not interpret an infinite group but the Shelah completion of M interprets an infinite
field. We introduce a weak notion of interpretability called local trace definability between
first order structures and an associated weak notion of equivalence. We give a dichotomy
between “linearity” and “field structure” for dp-minimal expansions of archimedean ordered
abelian groups. We also prove several other results about trace definability and local trace
definability between various classes of structures.
Introduction
A theme in model theory is to show that some abstract notion of “linearity” or “triviality”
is equivalent to non-interpretability of some algebraic structure. Two examples:
(1) Zil’ber showed that a totally categorical structure does not interpret an infinite group if
and only if every interpretable strongly minimal set is disintegrated [56].
(2) Peterzil and Starchenko showed that an o-minimal structure does not interpret an infinite
group (field) if and only it is disintegrated (locally modular) [45].
Fact A is a special case of the Peterzil-Starchenko theorem.
Fact A. If M is an o-minimal expansion of an ordered abelian group (M; +, <) then the
following are equivalent.
(1) M is locally modular.
(2) M does not interpret an infinite field.
(3) There is an ordered division ring D and an ordered D-vector space V expanding (M; +, <)
such that every M-definable set is definable in V, i.e. every M-definable set is a boolean
combination of solution sets of D-linear inequalities.
We are interested in obtaining similar results over other classes of unstable NIP structures.
Let M range over NIP structures. We let MSh be the Shelah completion of M, i.e. the
expansion of M by all externally definable sets. (This is usually called the Shelah expansion.)
See Section 1 for background. In general NIP-theoretic properties transfer immediately from
M to MSh. Thus if non-interpretability of an infinite group (field) is equivalent to a NIP-
theoretic property over some reasonable class C of NIP structures then we expect that the
Shelah completion of any M ∈C interprets an infinite group (field) if and only if M does. So
we were surprised to find that new algebraic structure can appear in the Shelah completion.
Theorem A.
(1) There is a weakly o-minimal structure M such that M is disintegrated, no model of
Th(M) interprets an infinite group, but the Shelah completion of M interprets the field
of real numbers.

(2) There is a weakly o-minimal expansion Q of (Q; +, <) such that no model of Th(Q)
interprets an infinite field, but the Shelah completion of any ℵ1-saturated elementary
extension of Q interprets the field of real numbers.
(3) Given a p-adically closed field K let BK be the induced structure on the set of balls in K,
so BK is interpretable in K. Then BK does not interpret an infinite field but the Shelah
completion of BK interprets the field of p-adic numbers when K is ℵ1-saturated.
See Section 1 for the details of these examples. How can the situation be salvaged? We
believe that the right approach is to work with a weaker notion of interpretability. In [54]
we introduced a weak notion of interpretability called trace definability, see Section 2 for
background. This gives rise to a weak notion of equivalence which we call trace equivalence.
Any NIP structure is trace equivalent to its Shelah completion. It follows that Th(M) trace
defines a structure O when the Shelah completion of M interprets O. At present we also
introduce an even weaker notion of interpretability, local trace definability, which again gives
rise to a weaker notion of equivalence called local trace equivalence. We use these notions
to give a dichotomy between “linearity” and “field structure” for dp-minimal expansions
of archimedean ordered abelian groups. (An expansion of (R; +, <) is dp-minimal if and
only if it is o-minimal and an expansion of a divisible archimedean ordered abelian group is
dp-minimal if and only if it is weakly o-minimal [49, 50].)
Theorem B. Let R be a dp-minimal expansion of an archimedean ordered abelian group
(R; +, <), which we take to be a substructure of (R; +, <). Then the following are equivalent.
(1) The Shelah completion of a model of Th(R) cannot interpret (R; +, ×).
(2) Th(R) does not locally trace define an infinite field.
(3) R has near linear Zarankiewicz bounds.
(4) Every R-definable set is a boolean combination of (R; +)-definable sets and sets of the
form Y ∩Rn where Y ⊆Rn is the solution set of a system of linear inequalities.
Near linear Zarankiewicz bounds is a candidate for a NIP-theoretic notion of “linearity”. We
introduce this property, show that it implies NIP, is preserved under local trace definability,
and rules out local trace definability of characteristic zero rings and infinite rings without
zero divisors.
The proof of Theorem B goes through the following result.
Theorem C. Let R be as in Theorem B. Then R is trace equivalent to an o-minimal ex-
pansion S of (R; +, <) such that S is also interpretable in the Shelah completion of any
ℵ1-saturated elementary extension of R. Furthermore R is either locally trace equivalent to
(R; +, <) or locally trace equivalent to some o-minimal expansion of (R; +, ×).
The last part of Theorem C reduces to showing that any o-minimal expansion of (R; +, <)
is either locally trace equivalent to (R; +, <) or locally trace equivalent to an o-minimal
expansion of (R; +, ×). We also show that any o-minimal expansion of an ordered abelian
group is either locally trace equivalent to (R; +, <) or locally trace equivalent to an o-minimal
expansion of an ordered field.

1. New algebraic structure in the Shelah expansion
We recall some background on externally definable sets and the Shelah completion. Through-
out this section M is a NIP structure. A subset of M m is externally definable if it is of
the form Y ∩M m for a definable subset Y ⊆N m with N an elementary extension of M.
Fact 1.1. Suppose that X is an M-definable set and < is an M-definable linear order on X.
Then any <-convex subset of X is externally definable.
Fact 1.1 follows by the definitions and an obvious compactness argument. It also follows by
Fact 1.2. The latter is due to Chernikov and Simon [48, Prop. 3.21]. It requires NIP.
Fact 1.2. A subset X ⊆M n is externally definable if and only if there is a definable family
X of subsets of M n so that for every finite A ⊆X we have A ⊆X′ ⊆X for some X′ ∈X.
We now introduce some non-standard terminology. A structure is Shelah complete if every
externally definable set is already definable. The well-known characterization of stability in
terms of definable types shows that a theory is stable if and only if all of its models are
Shelah complete. The Marker-Steinhorn theorem shows that any o-minimal expansion of
(R; <) is Shelah complete [39] and Delon showed that the field Qp is Shelah complete [11].
The Shelah completion MSh of M is the expansion of M by all externally definable sets.
Up to interdefinability MSh is the structure induced on M by any |M|+-saturated elementary
extension of M. (This is usually referred to as the Shelah expansion, we prefer completion as
it is more descriptive and expansion is already extensively used in model theory.) Facts 1.3
and 1.2 together imply that the Shelah completion of a NIP structure is Shelah complete.
Fact 1.3. Every MSh-definable set is externally definable.
Fact 1.3 is due to Shelah [47]. It is also an easy consequence of Fact 1.2.
Fact 1.4. If N is an elementary extension of M then the structure induced on M by NSh is
interdefinable with MSh.

2. Trace definability and local trace definability
We recall trace definability, introduce local trace definability, and prove a number of useful
general results about both.
Fact 1.3 shows that if M is NIP and M →N is an elementary embedding with N highly
saturated then a subset of M m is MSh-definable if and only if it is a preimage of an N-definable
set under the natural map M m →N m. This allows one to show that many model-theoretic
properties pass from M to MSh. We generalize this idea. Given a map τ: M →N we abuse
notation by letting τ denote the map M n →N n given by applying τ componentwise for
each n ≥2. A map τ: M →N between arbitrary structures is a trace embedding if every
definable subset of every M m is of the form τ−1(Y ) for definable Y ⊆N m. Consideration
of the graph of equality shows that any trace embedding is injective. A trace definition
M ⇝N is a map τ: M →N n for some n such that every M-definable subset of every M m
is of the form τ−1(Y ) for N-definable Y ⊆N mn. We say that N trace defines M if there is
a trace definition M ⇝N. It is easy to see that trace definitions form a category and trace
definability is therefore transitive.
Fact 2.1. Elementary embeddings are trace embeddings. If an L-structure M admits quan-
tifier elimination then any embedding of M into another L-structure is a trace embedding.
Fact 2.1 is [54, Prop. 1.2]. We now give several equivalent definitions of trace definability.
Fact 2.2. The following are equivalent for arbitrary structures M and N.
(1) M is trace definable in N.
(2) Up to isomorphism M ⊆N n for some n and every M-definable subset of every M m is
of the form Y ∩M m for N-definable Y ⊆N mn.
(3) There is a finite set E of functions M →N such that every M-definable set is of the
form
{(a1, . . . , am) ∈M m : (f1(ai1), . . . , fn(ain)) ∈Y }
for some f1, . . . , fn ∈E, i1, . . . , in ∈{1, . . . , m}, and N-definable Y ⊆N n.

definable L-structure.

4.3. Cyclically ordered abelian groups and the trace equivalence class of (R; +, <).
We first recall some background. A cyclic order on a set J is a ternary relation C such
that for all a, b, c ∈J we have:
(1) If C(a, b, c) then a, b, c are distinct.
(2) If C(a, b, c) and C(a, c, d) then C(a, b, d).
(3) If a, b, c are distinct, then either C(a, b, c) or C(c, b, a).
(4) C(a, b, c), implies C(b, c, a) and C(a, b, c), implies C(c, b, a).
If ◁is a linear order on J then we define a cyclic order C◁on J by declaring
C◁(a, b, c)
⇐⇒
(a ◁b ◁c) ∨(b ◁c ◁a) ∨(c ◁a ◁b)
for all a, b, c ∈J.
A cyclic group order on an abelian group (J; +) is a cyclic order preserved under trans-
lation. In this case, we call (J; +, C) a cyclically ordered abelian group. The obvious
example is the counterclockwise cyclic order on R/Z. A cyclically ordered abelian group is
archimedean if it embeds into this.
Suppose that (H; +, ◁) is an ordered abelian group and u ∈H is positive. Let Iu = [0, u)
and let ⊕u be the binary operation on Iu given by β ⊕u β∗= β + β∗when β + β∗< u and
β ⊕u β∗= β + β∗−u otherwise. Then (Iu; ⊕u, C◁) is a cyclically ordered abelian group. We
describe a second view of this construction.
Let (J; +, C) be a cyclically ordered abelian group. Then (H; +, ◁, u, π) is the universal
cover of (J; +, C) if (H; +, ◁) is an ordered abelian group, u is a positive element of H such

any a, b, c ∈Iu we have C(π(a), π(b), π(c)) if and only if C◁(a, b, c). Note that the restriction
of π to Iu gives an isomorphism (Iu; ⊕u, C◁) →(J; +, C). Any cyclically ordered abelian

We showed that an o-minimal expansion of an ordered abelian group trace defines an infinite

Proposition 6.1. An o-minimal structure trace defines an infinite group if and only if it
