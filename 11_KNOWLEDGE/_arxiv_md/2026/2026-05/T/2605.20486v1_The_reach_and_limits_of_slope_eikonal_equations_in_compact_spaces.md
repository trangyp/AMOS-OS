The reach and limits of slope eikonal equations in compact
spaces
David Salas, Sebastián Tapia-García, Francisco Venegas M.
Abstract
It is a well known fact that the eikonal equation is well posed in complete length spaces.
Among the studied notions of solutions in the literature, there is one that can be defined
in any metric space using the local (descent) slope and considering pointwise solutions:
functionals such that their slope coincides with the prescribed data at every point of the
domain. In this work we explore the question “Can we characterize the class of compact
metric spaces in which every slope eikonal equation (under standard assumptions) always
admits a pointwise solution?”. We provide a purely metric characterization of these spaces,
as well as some interesting examples and counterexamples that illustrate the reach and
limitations of the concept.

equations, continuing to be a fruitful field of research. Typically, a (stationary) Hamilton-Jacobi

appealing features of (2) is that the notion of continuous pointwise solutions using local slopes
allows to formulate the eikonal equation in arbitrary complete metric spaces. The local slope is
always well-defined and, even more, the aforementioned determination results of [11–13] can be
seen as uniqueness results for (2), valid in arbitrary metric spaces.
At this point a warning arises concerning slope eikonal equations: in contrast with the previously
mentioned viscosity-type approaches, well-posedness (in the sense of existence and uniqueness of
solutions) is not guaranteed. The notion used in [1, 25] requires the metric space to be a length
space for well-posedness, while the one defined in [26] requires the existence of absolutely con-
tinuous curves connecting points in the domain of the equation with the boundary of said domain.
The goal of the present work is to characterize the class of metric spaces where, under some
standard assumptions on ℓand g, the slope eikonal equation (2) is well-posed, in the sense that it
admits continuous pointwise solutions. In this first study, we focus on the case of compact metric
spaces, considering only equations where the prescribed slope ℓ(·) is continuous and bounded,
with infΩℓ> 0. Even in this setting, it is not clear how to characterize these spaces. Nevertheless,
such a characterization delineates the reach and limits of slope eikonal equations and, in some
sense, of the local slope as first-order information.

Problem formulation and main contribution
Our main goal is to characterize the class of compact metric spaces for which slope eikonal
equations admit continuous pointwise solutions, which is a proper subclass of all compact metric
spaces, as we have just shown.

Outline of the manuscript:
After short preliminaries in Section 2, we show in Section 3 that
the existence of functions with continuous slope relates to rectifiable connectivity of the space,
as well as representation formula for the (unique) candidate of solution of the slope eikonal
equation. In Section 4, we provide several examples and counterexamples narrowing down what
eikonal spaces must be. In particular, we show that this notion is not stable by bi-Lipschitz
equivalence and we provide an example of a rectifiably connected space bi-Lipschitz equivalent
to a geodesic space which fails to admit solutions for any slope eikonal equation with continuous
data. We prove our main theorem in Section 5. We finish the work in Section 6 by comparing
our results with the theory of viscosity solutions of eikonal equations previously developed in
[1, 23, 25, 26, 31], and presenting some perspectives.

Examples and counterexamples
This section is devoted to presenting examples of compact eikonal spaces, as well as examples
where the property fails. To gain further insight on this concept, we explore notions that one
might expect to be related to the solvability of slope eikonal equations, which will help us narrow
down the search for a metric characterization of eikonal spaces.
We take as a starting point the well established fact that the slope eikonal equation (3) admits
a continuous solution whenever the space X is a length space. When studying equation (3) on a
rectifiably connected space (X, d), one might be tempted to replace the metric d with its intrinsic
version dI, which automatically turns (X, dI) into a length space, warrantying the existence of
solutions to any version of equation (3). This approach has a clear downside, as some eikonal
equations may not admit a solution on (X, d) at all. This change of metric fundamentally alters
the behavior of the eikonal equation, even when the identity mapping Id : (X, d) →(X, dI) is
bi-Lipschitz. This can be seen on forthcoming Example 4.3.

space if dI ≤Cd. A space (X, d) is said to be quasiconvex if there exists C ≥1 such that it is

Recall from [31] that when (X, d) is a length space, then the solutions of (3) coincide with the

Open questions:
In this manuscript we have studied in depth the slope eikonal equations in
compact spaces. From this, we would like to point out the following two interesting lines of
research: the study and classification of noncompact eikonal spaces, as well as, the study of well-
posedness of more complicated Hamilton-Jacobi equations. On the other hand, one can observe
from our proof in Section 5 that Corollary 1.4 can be improved by reducing the family of closed
sets only to countable ones: a compact metric space (X, d) is eikonal if and only if dI(·, K) has
slope 1 on X \K, for every K ⊂X closed and countable. We conjecture that this can be further
reduced to singletons.
