THE EQUIVARIANT MILNOR–WITT MOTIVE OF M1,2
NANJUN YANG
Abstract. We provide a decomposition of the equivariant Milnor-Witt motives for the moduli spaces
of stable curves M1,2.
1. Introduction
The Chow ring and motivic structure of moduli spaces of stable curves form a central pillar of algebraic
geometry.
In genus zero, the geometry is completely cellular.
Consequently, the integral Voevodsky
motive of the stable moduli space M0,n decomposes strictly as a direct sum of shifted Tate motives
Z(i)[2i]. Recently, this rigid structure was successfully lifted into the realm of Chow-Witt ring ([Fas07]),
namely, the Milnor–Witt (abbr. MW) motive ([BCDFØ25]) developed by Calm`es, D´eglise and Fasel,
refining the motivic category by quadratic information. More precisely, the Milnor–Witt motive of M0,n
must be a direct sum of Z(i)[2i], Z/2nη(i)[2i] and Z/p[i], where η is the Hopf element and p is a quadratic
lifting of the odd prime p ([FY25]).
Extending this quadratic enrichment to genus one presents profound structural and homological chal-
lenges due to the presence of stacky torsion and boundary singularities. For Chow motives with rational
coefficients, the structural ceiling is well understood: the rational motive of M1,n is known to be of pure
Tate type for n ≤10, a consequence of the tautological ring fully generating the even cohomology before
the emergence of non-vanishing odd cohomology breaks this property for n > 10. For integral coeff-
cients, classical methods face the formidable obstacle of stacky stabilizers. While remarkable progress
has recently been achieved for small n—such as Bishop’s analysis of the integral Chow ring of M1,n for
n ≤10 ([Bis24]), and Newman’s proof that the integral motive with compact support of M1,n is mixed
Tate for n ≤4 ([New26])—these integral computations fundamentally rely on exhaustive characteristic
class tracking and boundary stratifications. Such classical algebraic methods do not natively capture the
quadratic data of the Hopf map η and are entirely inapplicable within the refined Milnor-Witt motivic
category.
In this paper, we introduce a strictly geometric approach utilizing the canonical infinity section inf :
M1,1 →M1,2, together with an explicit splitting theorem for the Milnor–Witt motive Z(M1,2) (Theorem
20).
