THE DERIVED DEPTH FORMULA FOR MODULES OF FINITE
QUASI-PROJECTIVE DIMENSION
LUIGI FERRARO AND JUSTIN LYLE
Abstract. Let (R, m, k) be a commutative Noetherian local ring.
We prove a variety of new formulae
for modules of finite quasi-projective or finite quasi-injective dimension. These include the Derived Depth
Formula, itself an extension of Auslander famous depth formula, a variation of the Derived Depth Formula
for width, an extended version of Ischebeck’s Formula, and a Dependency formula in the vein of Jorgensen.
Several special cases of our main results are new even under stronger assumptions on the vanishing of various
complete intersection dimensions.
1. Introduction
Let (R, m, k) be a commutative Noetherian local ring.
The notion of quasi-projective dimension was
introduced by Gheibi-Jorgensen-Takahashi in [9] and has received intense study in the ensuing years (see
[4, 5, 7, 13] for a few examples). We direct the reader to Section 2 for the precise definition. One should
take as a philosophy that a (finitely-generated) module of finite quasi-projective dimension behaves like a
(finitely-generated) module over a complete intersection ring, but is orthogonal in a sense to other notions
that behave analogously. For instance, invariants such as the complete intersection dimension or complexity
are finite for the residue field k if and only if R is already a complete intersection (see [3, Theorem 1.3] and
[10, Theorem], respectively), while on the other hand qpdR k < ∞for any local ring R [9, Proposition 3.6].
However, that finiteness of qpdR M for every M characterizes the complete intersection condition remains
an open question ([9, Question 3.9]) in full generality, and one of some significance due to its connection to
the well-studied proxy-small condition believed itself to characterize complete intersections (see [4]). In fact,
the work of [4] uses the notion of quasi-projective dimension to establish this characterization in the case
where R is equipresented, or when R has large cohomological support in the sense of [15] (cf. [2]).
Given the connection modules of finite quasi-projective dimension have to complete intersections, and
given that [9, Question 3.9] remains unresolved in full generality, is natural to explore to what extent
modules of finite quasi-projective dimension inherit the expected behaviors of modules over a complete
intersection ring. In this work we focus primarily on three such behaviors, formulae that are known to
hold, with appropriate hypotheses, over complete intersection rings. The first two of which are the so-called
derived depth formula, established for complete intersections by Christensen-Jorgensen [6, Theorem 4.6 and
Proposition 6.5], and the derived width formula which can be readily seen to hold over complete intersections
from [6, Propositions 6.4-6.5]. The last of which is the dependency formula, established for modules of finite
complete intersection dimension by Jorgensen [14, Theorem 2.2]. To be precise, we present the following as
our main results. In stating these results, we direct the reader to Section 2 for the definitions of depth and
width of complexes:
Main Results. Suppose M is a finitely generated R-module of finite quasi-projective dimension, and sup-
pose N is an R-complex with bounded homology. Then we have the following:
(1) (The Derived Depth Formula) If M ⊗L
R N has bounded homology, then
depth M ⊗L
R N = depth M + depth N −depth R.
(2) (The Derived Width Formula) If RHomR(M, N) has bounded homology, then
width RHomR(M, N) = depth M + width N −depth R

Quasi-Projective Dimension. A quasi-projective resolution F of a (nonzero) R-module M is a complex
of R-modules with the following properties:
(1) Fi = 0 for i ≪0.

4. Derived Width Formula
In this section, we prove the Derived Width Formula (Theorem 4.3), which is a variation on [6, Propositions
6.4-6.5]. While it can be readily seen from their work that the Derived Width Formula holds over complete
intersections, our approach also gives a new proof of this fact (see Theorem 4.3 (2)).
We begin with the following lemma which gives a connection between quasi-projective and quasi-injective
dimensions through Matlis duality:
Lemma 4.1. Let (R, m, k) be a commutative local Noetherian ring with a dualizing complex and let E be
the injective hull of k. Let N be an R-module. If qidR N < ∞, then qpdR HomR(N, E) < ∞.
Proof. Let I be a bounded quasi-injective resolution of N. Then HomR(I, E) is a bounded complex of flat
modules whose homologies are direct sums of copies of HomR(N, E). By [18, Theorem 18.2.4] HomR(I, E)
has finite projective dimension. Let Q be a bounded semi-projective resolution of HomR(I, E), then Q is a
bounded quasi-projective resolution of HomR(N, E).
□
