Modular invariance of characters
of quasi-lisse vertex algebras
Tomoyuki Arakawa1, Jethro van Ekeren2
and
Hao Li3
Abstract. We study spaces of conformal blocks associated with line bundles over elliptic curves,
with coefficients in a vertex algebra. For vertex algebras satisfying suitable finiteness and semisim-
plicity conditions, which are met by all admissible affine vertex algebras as well as admissible
W-algebras associated with nilpotent elements of standard Levi type, we prove the holonomicity
of the sheaf of conformal blocks over the moduli space of bundles. Furthermore, we show that the
space of flat sections of the associated Jacobi-invariant connection is spanned by trace functions on
modules. This result provides a substantial generalization of the celebrated theorem of Yongchang
Zhu to quasi-lisse vertex algebras. As a special case, we deduce that for affine vertex algebras
at admissible level, the dimension of the space of conformal blocks coincides with the number of
admissible weights at that level.

Introduction
Let (V, ω) be a conformal vertex algebra of central charge c. In the seminal work [85] Zhu has
proved that if V is lisse and rational, then the trace functions SW on irreducible V -modules
W, defined by
SW(u, τ) = TrW u0qL0−c/24,
(1.1)
span the space of genus-one conformal blocks of V , and constitute a finite dimensional vector
valued modular form on SL2(Z). Zhu’s theorem is a key part of the mathematical description
of conformal field theories and has important applications to the structure theory of vertex
algebras.
The purpose of this article is to establish an analogue of Zhu’s theorem for a broad class
of quasi-lisse vertex algebras [10] by replacing ordinary conformal blocks on elliptic curves
by conformal blocks associated with pairs (E, L) consisting of an elliptic curve E together
with a line bundle L over E. The corresponding trace functions depend on both the elliptic
moduli parameter τ and another parameter α corresponding to the line bundle. The trace
functions are equivariant under an action of the Jacobi group SL2(Z) ⋉Z2 extending that
of the modular group SL2(Z).

Let h be an element of the weight-one space V1 of V , and suppose that h0 acts on V
semisimply with integer eigenvalues. We refer to such a vector as a current, and to the
eigenspace decomposition with respect to h0 as the charge grading of V . If X is a smooth
complex algebraic curve and L a holomorphic line bundle over X, one may associate to
(V, ω, h) a twisted chiral algebra over X, and a sheaf over the moduli space of pairs (X, L)
which we refer to as charged conformal blocks [16][43, Chapter 7]. In the genus-one case we
consider the family T →B of pairs (E, L)(α,τ) parametrised by (α, τ) ∈B = C × H, where
H is the complex upper half plane, E = Eτ is the elliptic curve with modulus τ and L is the
holomorphic line bundle of degree zero determined by α. See Section 2 below for details.
To formulate our results we introduce a charged analogue of the quasi-lisse condition. Let
RV denote Zhu’s Poisson algebra and let Rh
V be the Poisson algebra obtained as the quotient
of RV by the commutative algebra ideal generated by vectors of nonzero charge. We say
that (V, ω, h) is stably quasi-lisse if the Poisson variety Spec Rh
V has finitely many symplectic
leaves. This condition is satisfied in many important classes of examples which we discuss
below, including all quasi-lisse affine vertex algebras and all quasi-lisse W-algebras for an
appropriate choice of current h.
Our first result concerns the geometric structure of the sheaf of charged conformal blocks.
Theorem 1.1. Let (V, ω, h) be a charged conformal vertex algebra which we assume finitely
strongly generated and stably quasi-lisse. Then the sheaf C((E, L), V ) of charged conformal
blocks over B carries the structure of a holonomic D-module with regular singularities on
the lattice Nα ∈Z + Zτ, for some positive integer N. In particular, away from this lattice
C is a vector bundle with integrable connection ∇.
When h = 0 the charge grading is trivial and the condition of being stably quasi-lisse reduces
to the quasi-lisse condition. In this case Theorem 1.1 recovers the finite dimensionality result
of [10].

category of V -modules.

The appearance of modular and Jacobi forms in representation theory has a long history.
In [55] Kac and Peterson determined modular properties of characters of integrable repre-

sentations of affine Kac-Moody algebras by expressing them in terms of theta functions,
and deduced consequences such as asymptotic growth of representations. In [42] Feigin and
Fuchs developed analogous character formulas for minimal representations of the Virasoro
algebra. Further impetus came from the role of the moonshine module vertex algebra V ♮in
monstrous moonshine [45, 18].
These phenomena are naturally explained by Zhu’s theorem. Furthermore Zhu’s theorem
plays an important role in conformal field theory, and the resulting representation of SL2(Z)
on the space C∇(V, ω) of global flat sections, appears in Huang’s proof of the Verlinde for-
mula [51]. Modularity also has important structural applications for vertex algebras. For
example, [38, 76] use properties of weight 2 cusp forms, including Deligne’s bound on Fourier
coefficients [25], to obtain estimates on the weight-one spaces of orbifolds of vertex algebras.
These estimates were used in [36] to show that every holomorphic vertex algebra of central
charge 24 with nontrivial weight-one space is an orbifold of the Leech lattice vertex algebra.
Jacobi invariance is similarly important. Schellekens used it to constrain the weight-one Lie
algebra of a holomorphic conformal vertex algebra of central charge 24, leading to his list of
71 possibilities [82] [37, Section 6]; see also [28]. For lisse rational vertex algebras the Jacobi
property was established in [73, 63] by expanding (1.2) as a Taylor series in α, identifying the
coefficients with n-point functions, and applying Zhu’s results. Closer in spirit to the present
work is [47], where supertrace functions of topological N = 2 vertex algebras were shown to
be flat sections of superconformal blocks, see also [46]. The lisse condition is assumed in all
these works.
It has long been expected that analogues of Zhu’s theorem should hold under somewhat
weaker hypotheses. Indeed the space C(Eτ, V ) of ordinary conformal blocks is finite dimen-
sional not only for lisse vertex algebras, but for all quasi-lisse vertex algebras [10]. This
class includes the simple affine vertex algebras Lk(g) at admissible levels [5]. Although the
representation category of admissible Lk(g) is very large, it was shown in [1] for g = sl2
and in [7] in general that the Lk(g)-modules in category O for bg are precisely the admissible
modules of Kac and Wakimoto, who had shown that the characters of the latter are modular
invariant [56, 54].
The connection ∇on genus-one conformal blocks gives rise to modular linear differential
equations (MLDE) satisfied by vertex algebra characters. These equations have been studied
since the early days of conformal field theory [70] [31], and later in the context of vertex
algebras, providing useful constraints, even without assumptions of rationality [58, 10, 66, 65].
The 4d/2d duality of [13] relating 4-dimensional N = 2 SUSY theories with vertex algebras
provides further motivation.
Modular properties of Schur indices were observed in [81],
and in view of the 4d/2d duality, would be natural consequences of modularity for the
corresponding vertex algebras. The associated MLDEs have been investigated in detail in
[15], and flavoured analogues have been studied in [78].
Here the inclusion of “flavour”
roughly corresponds to the passage from trace functions of the form (1.1) to that of (1.2).
In particular, the vertex algebras associated with S-class theories [13, 15, 14] in genus 0
have been constructed in [8] as chiral quantisations of Moore-Tachikawa symplectic varieties

[19]. These vertex algebras are known to be quasi-lisse in type A and believed to be so in
general, and their characters are conjecturally quasi-modular and have interesting relations
to multiple q-zeta values [72]. Many non-admissible affine vertex algebras are also quasi-lisse
[11].
Thus motivated, it is reasonable to seek a description of conformal blocks in terms of trace
functions on appropriate modules for the whole class of quasi-lisse vertex algebras.
By
including moduli of line bundles in our approach, trace functions on a sufficiently large
category of modules are rendered convergent and, under a reasonable rationality condition,
span the corresponding spaces of conformal blocks.
There are many generalisations of Zhu’s theorem in the literature. Dong, Li and Mason
proved a generalisation to twisted modules [27], a result fundamental to orbifold construc-
tions of holomorphic vertex algebras [64, 75, 37, 34]. Generalisations to vertex superalgebras
with “good” and “bad” statistics were obtained in [29] and [30], respectively. A version for
vertex superalgebras graded by rational conformal weights was established in [32], and used
in [9] to lift the Kac–Wakimoto modular transformation formulas for admissible affine vertex
algebras from characters to trace functions; see also [26, 69]. Modularity of trace functions
involving intertwining operators has been applied by Huang to rigidity of tensor categories
[49, 50]. There has also been substantial interest in modularity for lisse but non-rational
vertex algebras [74]. Miyamoto showed that modularity is restored by adjoining suitable
“pseudotrace functions”, and the analysis of these functions was a key ingredient in McRae’s
proof that semisimplicity of Zhu’s algebra implies rationality for lisse vertex algebras [71, 22].
We note that while relatively few non-rational lisse vertex algebras are known [60, 2, 84],
there is by contrast a rich supply of non-rational quasi-lisse examples [8, 11, 3]. We expect
extensions of our results in all these directions to be possible.
Finally, our results describe charged conformal blocks in terms of stable V -modules, char-
acterised among weight modules by a support condition. Other categories of modules, such
as spectral flows of stable modules, arise by modifying this condition. It would be interest-
ing to relate charged conformal blocks associated with these different categories, perhaps in
connection with the proposal of Creutzig and Ridout to recover Verlinde-type formulas from
distributional characters [23], see also [61, 40].
