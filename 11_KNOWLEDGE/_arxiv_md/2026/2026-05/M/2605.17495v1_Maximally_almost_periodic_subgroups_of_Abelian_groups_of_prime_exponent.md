MAXIMALLY ALMOST PERIODIC SUBGROUPS
OF ABELIAN GROUPS OF PRIME EXPONENT⋆
OL’GA SIPACHEVA
Abstract. It is proved that any infinite Abelian topological group of
prime exponent has an infinite maximally almost periodic subgroup.
This paper is concerned with Hausdorff Abelian topological groups of prime
exponent p. It is well known that, algebraically, any such group G is a direct
sum of copies of the cyclic group Z/pZ of order p (see, e.g., [5, Theorem 8.5]).
Since Z/pZ is a vector space over the field Fp, it follows that so is G. Therefore,
G has a basis E, and every nonzero element of G can be uniquely (up to the
order of terms) represented as a linear combination of elements of E with
nonzero coefficients in Z/pZ; in other words, G = L
e∈E⟨e⟩, where ⟨e⟩denotes
the subgroup of G generated by e (this is the cyclic group of order p). Thus,
on any Abelian topological group G of prime exponent p with basis E, there
exists the natural topology induced by the Tychonoff product topology of
Q
e∈E⟨e⟩. In what follows, we refer to this topology as the product topology
on G associated with E.
Although the algebraic structure of G is simple, its topology may be very
complex and strongly differ from a product topology. For example, it may
happen that any continuous character of G, that is, any continuous homomor-
phism of G to the circle group T, is trivial [8, Example 5], i.e., has kernel G.
Recall that the intersection of the kernels of continuous characters of a topo-
logical group is called the von Neumann kernel of G and denoted by n(G); a
group G with n(G) = G is said to be minimally almost periodic and a group
G with trivial n(G) is said to be maximally almost periodic [7].
Remark 1. A Hausdorff Abelian topological group G of prime exponent is
maximally almost periodic if and only if there exists a basis E of G such
that the product topology on G associated with E is coarser than the original
topology of G.
Indeed, G is maximally almost periodic if and only if the family C of all
nontrivial continuous characters on G separates the points of G, that is, the
continuous diagonal homomorphism ∆C : G →T|C | is injective and G admits
a continuous isomorphism onto a topological subgroup of T|C |. Clearly, this
subgroup carries the product topology associated with some basis.

Yet another obvious criterion for G to be maximally almost periodic is that
the intersection of open subgroups of finite index in G is trivial, because the
image of any character of G is the discrete cyclic group Z/pZ and its kernel is
an open subgroup of G of index p. On the other hand, every open subgroup
of finite index in G is an intersection of open subgroups of index p, each of
which is the kernel of a continuous character.
Example 5 of [8] proves the existence of a Hausdorff minimally almost peri-
odic Abelian topological group of any prime exponent. In this paper, we show
that any infinite Abelian topological groups of prime exponent contains an in-
finite maximally almost periodic subgroup, that is, a subgroup whose topology
is finer than the product topology associated with some basis of this subgroup.
We also prove that any countable Abelian topological group of prime exponent
has a closed discrete basis.

Note that any Tychonoff space X is a basis of the free, free Abelian, and
free Boolean topological groups F(X), A(X), and B(X) on X, but it is never
topologically independent in these groups (unless X is discrete). Moreover, no
subspace Y of X with nondiscrete closure is topologically independent. Indeed,
let G be any of the groups F(X), A(X), and B(X); we denote the neutral
element of G by e. The subroup H of all words of even length is open in G,
because this is the kernel of the continuous homomorphism H →Z2 extending
the map X →{0, 1} = Z2 defined by x 7→1 for each x ∈X. Let U = H,
and let g be any limit point of Y . Since g−1 · g = e and the group operations
are continuous, it follows that, given any neighborhood V of e, there exists a
neighborhood W of g in G such that u−1·v ∈V for all u, v ∈W. In particular,
we have x−1 · y ∈V for any x, y ∈W ∩Y . On the other hand, x−1 and y do
not belong to U, and hence Y is not topologically independent.

Theorem 1. Any infinite Abelian topological group of prime exponent contains
an infinite maximally almost periodic subgroup.
Proof. Let G be an infinite Abelian topological group of prime exponent p
with zero element 0, and let H be an arbitrary countable subgroup of G.
The network weight of any countable topological space is countable, and any
topological group of countable network weight admits a not strictly coarser
second-countable Hausdorff group topology [1, Theorem 2]. Let τ be a second-
countable Hausdorff group topology on H coarser than the induced topology.
By Proposition 1 (H, τ) contains an infinite topologically independent set
A = {an : n ∈N}, where ai ̸= aj for i ̸= j. Since A is independent, it follows
that the subgroup ⟨A⟩of G generated by A is isomorphic to the direct sum
L

tion 4.7], the product topology on ⟨A⟩associated with A is coarser than the
topology induced from (H, τ); hence it is coarser than that induced from G.
According to Remark 1, ⟨A⟩is maximally almost periodic.
□
