Introduction
This is a condensed overview of the formal theory of monads in a 2-category. The
paper is limited to a few definitions and results per section, originally written as an
appendix to supplement [FCS26]. We cover monads, modules, lax and colax 1-cells
of monads, algebra objects [Str72] (a.k.a. Eilenberg-Moore objects), bimodules [CS10],
distributive laws [Bec69], codensity monads [Dub70], and pushforward monads [Mat25].
In Section 4 we also draw attention to two double categories Mnd(X) and EM(X)
of monads in a 2-category X, extending the two 2-categories Mnd(X) and EM(X)
from [LS02]. Both have the same objects (monads) and arrows (lax and colax 1-cells),
but the 2-cells are different. The first double category — whose 2-cells are called
simply monad 2-cells — can be viewed as a special case of the double category of
functors, lax and colax transformations, and modifications between two 2-categories
from [FS26, Proposition A.8]. It can also be viewed as a special case of the double
category Mnd(D) of loose monads in a double category from [FGK11]. The second —
whose 2-cells we call monad specializations1 — can be viewed as a special case of the
double category EM(D) of loose monads in a double category D mentioned in [Cla22,
Chapter 7].
Like [HM23, HM25], we use the string diagram calculus for 2-categories. This is
to make definitions and calculations more transparent by visualizing their structure.
For readers not comfortable with string diagrams, we include pasting diagrams as
well. Also, having the pasting diagrams beside the string diagrams permits more
artistic liberty in the string diagrams with less danger of confusion. We frequently
draw individual regions (0-cells), strings (1-cells), and nodes (2-cells) with multiple
colors or special textures, suggesting certain properties of the cells they represent. To
match the diagrams, we write composition in a 2-category in diagrammatic order 𝑓#𝑔,
rather than the usual Leibniz order 𝑔◦𝑓.
Knowledge of the definition of 2-category (and in Section 4, double category) is
assumed; see e.g. [KS74]. It is also useful to know about adjunctions and extensions in
2-categories, though we spell out details when these come up. The first three sections
are prerequisites for the others, which mostly do not depend on one another.
