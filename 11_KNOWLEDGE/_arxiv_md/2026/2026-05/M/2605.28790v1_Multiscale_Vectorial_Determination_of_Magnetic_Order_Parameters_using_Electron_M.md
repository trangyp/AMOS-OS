Multiscale Vectorial Determination of Magnetic Order Parameters using Electron
Magnetic Linear Dichroism
Jan Hajduček,1, ∗Jáchym Štindl,1 Ján Rusz,2 and Vojtěch Uhlíř1, †
1CEITEC BUT, Brno University of Technology, Purkyňova 123, 612 00 Brno, Czech Republic
2Department of Physics and Astronomy, Uppsala University, Box 516, 75120 Uppsala, Sweden
(Dated: May 28, 2026)
We demonstrate electron magnetic linear dichroism as a quantitative probe of vectorial magnetic
order parameters with nanometer resolution in transmission electron microscopy. Explicit inclusion
of vectorial core-level exchange splitting into mixed dynamic form factor simulations accounting
for dynamical diffraction enables direct reconstruction of the magnetic spin axis from momentum-
resolved electron energy-loss spectra. The resulting dichroic signal is intrinsically separable from
nonmagnetic anisotropy, exhibits a well-defined dependence on the Néel vector or magnetization
orientation, and remains robust down to the atomic scale. Applied to the collinear antiferromagnetic
and ferromagnetic phase of cubic FeRh, this approach allows quantitative real-space mapping of
the magnetic vector.
These results open a pathway to nanoscale spectroscopy and imaging of
antiferromagnets and altermagnets where the generalized approach to electron dichroism provides
direct access to different magnetic order parameters.
Introduction: Nanoscale reconstruction of magnetic or-
der remains a central challenge in spintronics and quan-
tum materials, particularly for compensated magnetic
phases such as antiferromagnetic (AF) [1, 2] and re-
cently described altermagnetic [3] order.
In these sys-
tems, the antiparallel alignment of neighboring atomic
magnetic moments yields a vanishing net magnetization,
making them inaccessible to conventional magnetization-
sensitive probes [4]. X-ray magnetic dichroism has be-
come the workhorse for element-specific magnetic imag-
ing, providing direct access to local spin and orbital
moments through circular dichroism (XMCD) [5] and
to the Néel vector orientation through linear dichroism
(XMLD) [6].
XMCD probes the magnetization com-
ponent along the X-ray helicity axis and enables imag-
ing of ferromagnetic (FM) textures such as skyrmions
and domain walls [7–9].
In contrast, XMLD arises
from anisotropic transitions governed by the local ex-
change field and provides sensitivity to AF domains and
spin axes [10–13]. Recently, their combination has en-
abled vectorial reconstruction of altermagnetic order [14],
supported by ab initio simulations of X-ray dichro-
ism [15, 16].
A complementary route to magnetic dichroism is pro-
vided by inelastic electron scattering in a transmission
electron microscope (TEM), commonly used for atomic-
scale materials characterization.
Momentum-resolved
electron energy-loss spectroscopy (EELS) enables detec-
tion of electron dichroism: energy loss resolves electronic
transitions, while momentum transfer encodes the chi-
rality required for circular dichroism [17, 18] and the
directional sensitivity underlying natural [17, 19, 20]
and magnetic linear dichroism [21].

ogy between polarized x-ray absorption and momentum-

resolved EELS led to the development of electron mag-
netic circular dichroism (EMCD) as the electron coun-
terpart of XMCD [22]. EMCD enables the detection of
magnetic order with atomic resolution [23–28], including
recently demonstrated sensitivity to ferrimagnetic [23]
and AF order [29] using atomically sized convergent elec-
tron probes. Despite these advances, the vectorial sen-
sitivity of EMCD remains only partially explored.
To
date, experiments have mainly accessed the projection
of the uncompensated magnetization, with simultaneous
sensitivity to both in-plane and out-of-plane components
demonstrated only in limited cases [30], and full vector
reconstruction remains largely conceptual [31].
While the circular form of electron dichroism is now
well established for high-resolution magnetic characteri-
zation, the linear form of electron dichroism (ELD), sen-
sitive to anisotropic transitions, has so far been explored
mainly in nonmagnetic systems for directions transversal
to the electron beam [20, 32–40], also known as natural
ELD (ENLD). The magnetic extension of ELD, electron
magnetic linear dichroism (EMLD), was only demon-
strated in AF hematite [21, 41], providing a spectro-
scopic signature of the Morin transition [42], revealing
the EMLD sensitivity to the Néel vector component along
the electron beam. However, the development of EMLD
as a robust imaging contrast has been limited by detec-
tor sensitivity and the lack of a predictive theoretical
framework. Although advances in direct electron detec-
tion technology [43] have largely resolved the former, the
latter remains a key challenge.
In this Letter, we show how electron magnetic dichro-
ism can be decomposed into its circular and linear terms
and establish a general route to vectorial reconstruction
of magnetic order parameters. Within a unified vecto-
rial simulation framework, the distinct components of
the dichroic signal are isolated and linked to the under-
lying magnetic degrees of freedom. Focusing on EMLD,
we demonstrate a reconstruction scheme that provides

Origin of EMCD and EMLD: The scattering geome-
try underlying EMD is shown in Fig. 1(a). An incident
electron beam undergoes elastic and inelastic scattering
in the FeRh crystal, forming a diffraction pattern in the
TEM back focal plane, with inelastic intensity localized
around Bragg reflections. By selecting energy and mo-
mentum transfer in this plane, specific electronic transi-
tions are probed. In transition metals, these correspond
to core-level excitations (e.g., 2p →3d at the L2,3 edges),
where the unoccupied 3d states carry magnetic informa-
tion. Spin–orbit coupling and core-level exchange split-
ting lead to anisotropic transition probabilities, project-
ing as EMLD, while spin-dependent asymmetry projects
as EMCD.
We consider a geometry with magnetic moments per-
pendicular to the incident beam (in-plane).
EMCD is
accessed via conjugate aperture positions Ω+ and Ω−
in the diffraction plane, typically under two- or three-
beam conditions that enhance sensitivity to chiral tran-
sitions [30, 31]. In contrast, the linear dichroic compo-
nent (EMLD/ENLD) is obtained by comparing intensi-
ties along orthogonal momentum-transfer directions, Ω∥
and Ω⊥, analogous to XMLD. Aperture positions are
guided by calculated dichroic signal maps, while their
size is maximized to ensure sufficient count rates with-
out compromising momentum selectivity.

without core-level exchange splitting and for AF and
FM phases of FeRh. The presence of spin polarization
near the Fermi level, characteristic of FM ordering, di-
rectly leads to EMCD in EELS spectra as long as the
beam splitter configuration conditions are met [17, 22].
Because valence-state polarization is present in the FM
phase but absent in the AF phase, EMCD is detectable
only in the FM phase, provided the electron interaction
volume extends beyond individual atomic columns [29].
Structural anisotropy, in turn, manifests in EELS as
ENLD [17]. In cubic metallic magnets with weak struc-
tural anisotropy, such as FeRh, the ENLD response
is small in both magnetic phases, unlike systems with
strong orbital anisotropy, including transition-metal ox-
ides, AF insulators, layered magnets, and compounds
with large magnetocrystalline anisotropy [51, 52].
By
including the exchange splitting, the degeneracy of spin-
dependent states is lifted, giving rise to EMLD signal.
As exchange splitting is present in both AF and FM
phases, EMLD can be detected in both, as is the case
for XMLD [53, 54]. Inclusion of exchange splitting is for-
malized in END MATTER. Although EMLD and ENLD
are accessed within the same aperture geometry, their
magnitudes differ significantly, and they can be experi-
mentally separated from each other by exploiting their
distinct physical origins. Pure ENLD can be isolated by
suppressing magnetic order (e.g., heating above the Néel
temperature, where EMLD vanishes). Conversely, pure
EMLD can be obtained by reversing or rotating the mag-
netic axis while keeping the detection geometry fixed,
for which ENLD remains invariant. This separation is
fully analogous to XMLD-based protocols for disentan-
gling magnetic and structural anisotropy [53, 55].
Overall, we show that magnetic dichroic components
can be separated via aperture selection, as demonstrated
for EMCD [30, 31] and EMLD. Quantitative separation
of the EMCD and EMLD contributions is evaluated in
Supp. Note S1 [56].

Correct treat-

approach enables the calculation of EMCD and EMLD
signal distributions in the diffraction plane, as well as
their corresponding spectral profiles at each ⃗q point, for
an arbitrary spin axis orientation. To illustrate this ca-

As demonstrated in Refs. [21, 41], sensitivity to the out-
of-plane (OOP) component of the magnetic vector in
EELS arises from tuning the relative contributions of
momentum-transfer components parallel and perpendic-
ular to the magnetic axis, which can be achieved using
concentric apertures of varying radii. Complementarily,
sensitivity to in-plane (IP) components is obtained by
employing off-center aperture positions in the diffraction
plane, enabling discrimination between orthogonal IP di-
rections, as illustrated in Fig. 1 for the AF phase of FeRh.
Taken together, this unified approach enables consis-
tent calculation and interpretation of both EMCD and
EMLD signals for arbitrary orientations of the magneti-
zation or Néel vector. It thereby provides a direct and
general route to vectorial magnetic reconstruction in both
AF and FM systems.

Discussion:
The framework presented for isolating
the magnetic circular, linear, and natural linear electron
dichroism components, highlighting the vectorial recon-
struction, opens the possibility for experimental recon-
struction of the spin axis in various magnetic orderings.
A key advantage of using EMLD for vectorial recon-
struction is its robustness across scales and parameter
space. First, EMLD persists over a broad range of crystal
and electron beam orientations. Unlike EMCD, which re-
quires near-ideal two- or three-beam conditions [22] (see
Supp. Note S3 [56]), the EMLD signal remains present
across an extended angular phase space of incident di-
rections ⃗ki (see Fig. 4).
Although the magnitude and
sign of EMLD vary continuously with crystal orienta-
tion (Fig. 4(a) for IP component), the presented simula-
tion framework allows its full predictive control. Notably,
a signal instability occurs for beam incidence along the
zone axis due to dynamical diffraction, which motivates
the use of a small tilt, as adopted throughout this work.
In contrast, the OOP component remains stable over the
same angular range (Fig. 4(b)). Second, the EMLD sig-
nal is robust with respect to sample thickness, retaining
both its sign and spectral shape (see Supp. Note S4 [56]).
Third, we show that EMLD retains sensitivity to AF
order and enables vectorial reconstruction over a wide
range of electron probe sizes from the µm-sized parallel
beam down to the atomically sized probes with large con-
vergence angles (for details on convergent beam EMLD
simulations see Supp. Note S5 [56]). In contrast, EMCD
in AF becomes detectable only with atomically confined
probes capable of spatially resolving sublattice asymme-
try, thus placing stringent demands on experimental de-
tection of AF order using EMCD.
Additionally,
EMLD reproduces the characteristic
sin2 φ dependence known from XMLD [53, 61] and previ-
ous EMLD experiments [21, 41]. The agreement between
our spectral simulations and the dielectric-tensor formal-
ism (Supp. Note S5 [56]) establishes a direct conceptual
connection between electron- and photon-based linear
dichroism. This correspondence enables the transfer of
established XMLD methodologies to the TEM, opening
routes toward quantitative evaluation of magnetocrys-
talline anisotropy and, in combination with EMCD, to-
ward probing altermagnetic order at the nanoscale.
Conclusion:
Our results identify EMLD as a prac-
tical and conceptually unified approach for resolving
compensated magnetic order in the TEM, rooted in
the anisotropic response of core-level excitations.
The
demonstrated vectorial sensitivity, combined with ro-
bustness against probe size and alignment, places experi-
mental realization within the reach of modern aberration-
corrected microscopes equipped with high-brightness
sources and advanced EELS instrumentation.
High-
resolution EMLD offers a pathway to map local magnetic
anisotropy, interfacial and two-dimensional magnetism,
and symmetry breaking induced by strain, providing ac-
cess to emergent phenomena in antiferromagnets and al-
termagnets at the nanoscale.

a fixed aperture geometry for the AF sublattices of FeRh.
Fig. S3(a) presents the simulated EMCD signal evaluated separately from FM aligned sublattices in AF FeRh with

signal, with non-zero contributions localized around three-beam conditions and nearby two-beam configurations. The

[11]. Figure S3(b) further illustrates the vectorial dependence of the IP EMCD signal as a function of the azimuthal

The vectorial reconstruction capability of EMLD becomes particularly powerful when combined with spatially
confined electron probes, enabling access to local magnetic information. For weakly convergent probes (α < 2 mrad),
EMCD and EMLD signals are well described by parallel-beam models [14]. With increasing convergence, finite-probe
effects and dynamical diffraction become increasingly important [15, 16].
To capture these effects, we employ the convergent-beam formalism of Refs. [17, 18] used for EMCD evaluation,
extended here for EMLD through its XMLD-equivalent formulation from dielectric tensor components [12].

For the simulations underlying Figs. S4 and S5, we used identical geometrical and microscope parameters for
the spectral and non-spectral calculations to ensure a consistent comparison. In the non-spectral case, dynamical
diffraction simulations were performed using a parallel electron beam and the mats code originally introduced in
Ref. [17], together with the improved summation regime of the mats.v2 method [18]. Thickness-dependent diffraction
patterns were evaluated on a reciprocal-space grid spanning ±10 mrad in both qx and qy, with a 0.25 mrad sampling
interval and a thickness step of 1.49 nm, using a 300 keV beam. The FeRh crystal was oriented in a three-beam
geometry with Bragg spots G = ±(100) and the incident beam aligned along ZA ∥[016]. The Bloch-wave coefficient
excitation threshold was set to 10−4. The resulting momentum-resolved EMCD/EMLD signals were normalized by
the corresponding non-magnetic contribution, following the Eqs. (20) and (22).
