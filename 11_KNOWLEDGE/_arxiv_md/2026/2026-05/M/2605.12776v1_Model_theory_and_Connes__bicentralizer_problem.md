MODEL THEORY AND CONNES’ BICENTRALIZER PROBLEM
HIROSHI ANDO AND ISAAC GOLDBRING
Abstract. We make a series of model-theoretic contributions to Connes’ bicen-
tralizer problem, one of the most prominent open problems in the theory of
von Neumann algebras. Our work builds on the recent result of Houdayer and
Marrakchi who show that, for separable diffuse W∗-probability spaces, having
trivial bicentralizer is equivalent to being selfless, that is, having the first factor
inclusion into the free product be an existential embedding. We first show that
the class of selfless W∗-probability spaces is ∀∃-axiomatizable. We then extend
the Houdayer-Marrakchi equivalence to all diffuse W∗-probability spaces, re-
moving the separability hypothesis. Combining these results, we show that for
any axiomatizable class of diffuse W∗-probability spaces, those with trivial bi-
centralizer form an ∀∃-axiomatizable class; in particular, the class of type III1
factors with trivial bicentralizer is ∀∃-axiomatizable. We give concrete axioms
for this class using totally bounded variants of Haagerup’s characterization of
the bicentralizer, which we develop here and believe to be of independent in-
terest. We also introduce the notion of pseudoperiodic III1 factors and show
that any such factor has trivial bicentralizer. In the final section, we prove that
the bicentralizer problem has a positive solution if and only if the bicentralizer
functor is a zeroset relative to the theory of III1 factors. We use this result to give
an equivalent formulation of the bicentralizer problem in terms of a uniformity
condition on Haagerup’s Dixmier-type characterization of the bicentralizer.

1. Introduction
The work presented here is a model theoretic contribution to Connes’ bicen-
tralizer problem, one of the most prominent open problems in von Neumann
algebra theory. To explain the problem, suppose that (M, φ) is a W∗-probability
space, that is, M is a σ-finite von Neumann algebra and φ is a faithful normal
state on M. The bicentralizer of (M, φ) is the set BC(M, φ) consisting of those
a ∈M satisfying the following: given ϵ > 0, there is δ > 0 so that, whenever
u ∈M is a unitary for which ∥uφ −φu∥:= sup{|φ(ub) −φ(bu)| : ∥b∥≤
1} < δ, then one has ∥u∗au −a∥φ < ϵ. (This is not Connes’ original definition,
but rather a reformulation due to Haagerup [20, Section 1].) The bicentralizer
BC(M, φ) is always a von Neumann subalgebra of M. In case M is a III1 factor,
BC(M, φ) is either trivial, that is, equals C, or else is a type III1 factor again.
The relevance of the bicentralizer stems from Connes’ classification of injective
von Neumann algebras. After having classified all injective factors with sep-
arable predual besides those of type III1, Connes proved that an injective type
III1 factor M with separable predual is isomorphic to the Araki-Woods factor R∞
provided for some (or any) faithful normal state φ on M, the modular flow σφ is
approximately inner, and he showed that this is the case if BC(M, φ) = C, which

Although there are various classes of III1 factors where the bicentralizers have
been shown to be trivial [5, 9, 20, 26, 29, 31, 35, 37], the general case is still open.
The relevance of the bicentralizer problem beyond the injective case can be best
understood by Haagerup’s characterization [20, Theorem 3.1] that if M is a fac-
tor of type III1 with separable predual, then it has trivial bicentralizer if and
only if there exists a faithful normal state φ with large centralizer, that is, if
M′
φ ∩M = C. The existence of such a state has proven to be of great importance
in applications, for example, in Houdayer and Isono’s works on the generaliza-
tion of Popa’s free independence theorem [27] and on the unique prime factor-
ization theorem for III1 factors [28]. In a more recent work, the first author with
Haagerup, Houdayer and Marrakchi [5] constructed a flow βφ, called the bi-
centralizer flow, on BC(M, φ) (or more generally, on the relative bicentralizer
BC(N ⊂M, φ)) and its ergodicity is shown to be equivalent to the existence
of an irreducible hyperfinite subfactor with expectation. Since then, there has
been remarkable progress made by Marrakchi, where the flow is indeed shown
to be ergodic [35], and this fact was used to show the weak relative Dixmier
property for inclusion of von Neumann algebras with expectation [36] and ap-
plications to Kadison’s problem and further generalization of the bicentralizer
conjecture [37]. Incidentally, Isono [32] confirmed the Haagerup–Størmer con-
jecture [22, 21, 23] about pointwise inner automorphisms of factors of type III1
under the trivial bicentralizer condition, where Marrakchi’s works played a key
role. All of these recent developments provide the motivation to study the bi-
centralizer problem beyond the injective case.
The starting point of our work is the recent article of Houdayer and Marrakchi
[30], who proved that, for separable, diffuse W∗-probability spaces, having a triv-
ial bicentralizer is equivalent to being selfless. Here, a W∗-probability space
(M, φ) is said to be selfless if and only if there is an embedding from the free
product (M, φ) ∗(M, φ) into the (Ocneanu) ultraproduct (M, φ)U such that,
when restricted to the first factor, yields the usual diagonal embedding of (M, φ)
into its ultrapower. Selflessness was first introduced and investigated by Robert
[44], motivated by Popa’s article [43], where it was shown (using the above
terminology) that any II1 factor (equipped with its canonical trace) is selfless.
Houdayer–Isono’s free independence result mentioned above in this context is
the statement that a W∗-probability space (M, φ) is selfless if M is a factor with
φ having large centralizer. This result played a key role in one direction of the
proof of the above equivalence. Selflessness has been a very active area of study
in C∗-algebra theory over the past couple of years, stemming from the recent

space is existential) is a III1 factor that tensorially absorbs R∞, which implies
that it must have trivial bicentralizer by [35, Theorem D]. That e.c. W∗-probability
spaces have trivial bicentralizer also follows from the aforementioned result of
Houdayer and Marrakchi.
Our first main result is the following:
Theorem (Corollary 4.11). The class of selfless W∗-probability spaces is an ∀∃-axiomatizable
class.
We do not prove this theorem by giving concrete axioms for this class, but rather
prove that this class is closed under ultraproducts, ultraroots, and direct limits.
In contrast, we note that the class of e.c. W∗-probability spaces is not closed
under ultraproducts (see [18, Corollary 3.11]).
Our next main result is to remove the separability assumption from the result
of Houdayer and Marrakchi:
Theorem (Theorem 4.21). For any (not necessarily separable) diffuse W∗-probability
space (M, φ), we have that (M, φ) is selfless if and only if BC(M, φ) = C.
The previous theorem is proven by showing that membership in either of the
above classes can be detected by separable elementary subalgebras; for bicen-
tralizers, this involves establishing that BC(M, φ) ∩N = BC(N, ψ) whenever
(N, ψ) is an elementary (even existential) substructure of (M, φ). Consequently,
we see that if the bicentralizer problem has a positive solution, then BC(M, φ) =
C for all III1 factors M, regardless of the density character of M.
Combining the previous theorem with our first theorem yields the following:
Theorem (Corollary 4.22). For any axiomatizable class K consisting only of diffuse
W∗-probability spaces, the elements of K with trivial bicentralizer form an axiomatizable
class, which is ∀∃-axiomatizable if K is ∀∃-axiomatizable. In particular, the class of III1
factors with trivial bicentralizer is axiomatizable.

After having shown that the class of III1 factors with trivial bicentralizer is ax-
iomatizable, we proceed to write down concrete axioms for this class. To do so,
we introduce a variant of Haagerup’s description of W∗-probability spaces with
trivial bicentralizer (which is the description given above) that is better suited
to dealing with ultraproducts and which makes use of the technology around
totally bounded elements introduced in [6]. We believe these results should be
of independent interest.
Ostensibly, the previous theorem should yield many new examples of III1 fac-
tors with trivial bicentralizer. Indeed, in analogy with the case of II1 factors
(see [15, Theorem 4.3]), it is conjectured that, for any separable W∗-probability
space (M, φ) with M a III1 factor, there are continuum many W∗-probability
spaces (Mα, φα)α<2ω elementarily equivalent to (M, φ) but with Mα ̸∼= Mβ for
α < β < 2ω (see also [18, Question 4.13]); if (M, φ) is one of the III1 factors
known to have trivial bicentralizer, then each Mα would also have trivial bicen-
tralizer.
In the final section, we give a “quantitative” reformulation of the bicentralizer
problem. To explain this, we follow Haagerup and define, for a W∗-probability
space (M, φ) and a ∈M, the quantity

(1) The bicentralizer problem has a positive solution.
(2) For each ϵ > 0, there is δ > 0 such that, for all W∗-probability spaces (M, φ)
with M a III1 factor, and all a ∈S1(BC(M, φ)), we have that ϵM(a, δ) < ϵ.

A main source of T-functors come from T-formulae. To explain this notion, one
defines a pseudometric dT,⃗x on formulae φ(⃗x) by
dT,⃗x(φ(⃗x), ψ(⃗x)) := sup{|φ(⃗a)M −ψ(⃗a)M| : M |= T, ⃗a ∈M}.
Thus, two formulae φ and ψ are dT,⃗x close if they are uniformly close in all mod-

in all models of T. One separates and completes this pseudometric space to ob-

every model M |= T, one has a uniformly continuous function θM : M⃗x →R
whose range is contained in some bounded interval in R (both the modulus of
uniform continuity and bound on the range of θM are uniform over all models
of T); this function is called the interpretation of θ in M. If θ(⃗x) is a T-formula,
then one obtains a T-functor, the zeroset of θ, denoted Z(θ), defined by
Z(θ)(M) := {⃗a ∈M⃗x : θM(⃗a) = 0}.
More generally, by a T-function over ⃗x we mean a mapping whose domain is
the set of all pairs (M, ⃗a) with M |= T and ⃗a ∈M⃗x and whose co-domain is a
bounded set in R. As we just saw, interpretations of T-formulae give examples
of T-functions (the T-formula θ(⃗x) sends the pair (M, ⃗a) to the value θM(⃗a));
T-functions of this form are called realized.
If X is a T-functor, then one obtains the T-function d(⃗x, X) which, upon input
(M, ⃗a), returns the value d(⃗a, X(M)).
Given a T-function Φ(⃗x,⃗y) and a T-functor X, one may quantify over X to obtain
a new T-function. More specifically, define sup⃗x∈X Φ(⃗x,⃗y) to be the T-function
which, upon input (M,⃗b), returns the value sup{Φ(M, ⃗a,⃗b) : ⃗a ∈X(M)}. One
defines inf⃗x∈X Φ(⃗x,⃗y) similarly.
