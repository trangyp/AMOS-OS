MODAL GROUP THEORY
WOJCIECH ALEKSANDER WOŁOSZYN
Abstract. I introduce modal group theory, in which we study the
category of all groups, considering embeddability as providing a notion
of modal possibility. Using HNN extensions and Britton’s lemma, I
demonstrate that the modal language of groups is more expressive than
the first-order language of groups. I interpret the theory of true arithmetic
in modal group theory, and show that, as sets of Gödel numbers, it is
computably isomorphic to the modal theory of finitely presented groups.
I answer an open question of Berger, Block, and Löwe [BBL23] by
showing that the formulaic propositional modal validities of groups under
embeddings are precisely S4.2. I also analyze sentential validities and
worlds validating S5.
1. Introduction
Modal group theory is about investigating the category of groups and
embeddings from a modal perspective. From the group-theoretic point of
view, this means studying what becomes visible when one is allowed to pass
from a group to its overgroups. In modal group theory, for example, we want
to know the expressive power of the modal language and exactly what the
propositional modal validities in the category of groups are.
We say that an assertion φ[¯g] is possible at a group G, and write G |=
φ[¯g],
if there is a group embedding j : G →H such that H |= φ[j(¯g)]. Similarly,
φ[¯g] is necessary at G if for every embedding j : G →H, we have H |= φ[j(¯g)],
in which case we write G |=
φ[¯g].
Throughout this paper, possibility
means embeddability; homomorphism and epimorphism versions are treated
separately in [Woł25a; Woł25b].
To illustrate, every group is possibly necessarily nonabelian. That is, any
group satisfies the assertion
∃x∃y xy ̸= yx. Indeed, one can embed any
group into a nonabelian group and it will remain nonabelian in all further
groups. The statement ∃x∃y xy ̸= yx is possible and once true, stays true
forever. Therefore, the property of being nonabelian is a button.
Similarly, it is necessarily possible that the center—the set of elements
that commute with all the elements of the group—is trivial, because every
group G embeds into G ∗F2, which has trivial center. But centerlessness,
even once true, is not necessary, since from any centerless group H one can
pass to H × C2, whose center is nontrivial. Therefore, being centerless is a
switch, because we can turn it on and off in all further extensions.

(1) Many important group-theoretic properties inexpressible in the first-

Meanwhile, modal group theory is part of the larger modal model theory pro-
gram, which investigates modal principles and validities in Kripke categories
generally, that is, in concrete categories of structures in a common first-order

3. Non-expressivity in first-order group theory
The modal definitions above genuinely go beyond first-order group theory.
Theorem 3.1. None of the following relations or properties are first-order
expressible in the ordinary language of groups:
(1) two elements have the same order;
(2) for any fixed n ≥1, an element belongs to the subgroup generated by
an n-tuple;
(3) for any fixed n ≥1, a group is generated by at most n elements; in
particular, a group is cyclic;
(4) an element is torsion;
(5) a group is torsion.
Moreover, by item (4), the set of torsion elements is not first-order definable
in general.
Proof. For item (1), consider the cyclic groups C2pn, where pn is the nth
prime. Let xn be a generator and yn = x2

nonprincipal ultraproduct Q

order, hence the same order. By Łoś’s theorem [CK90], equality of order is
not first-order expressible.
For item (2), fix n ≥1 and suppose there were a first-order formula
ϕ(y, x1, . . . , xn) expressing y ∈⟨x1, . . . , xn⟩. In the language of groups with

5. Propositional modal validities of groups
We now turn from expressive power to propositional modal validities.
Berger, Block, and Löwe proved that the class of abelian groups validates
S4.2 and asked whether the same is true for all groups [BBL23]. We answer
that question in the affirmative for the natural parameter language.
For this section, let L denote a language between LGrp and LGrp, and
write Grp,→for the category of groups and embeddings. If A ⊆G, then LA
denotes the class of assertions of L with parameters from A, that is, the
sentences of the expansion obtained by adding constant symbols naming the
chosen parameters. More generally, if L is a class of formulas of LGrp, then
LA denotes the corresponding class of assertions with parameters from A.
Under an embedding j : G →H, the constant naming a ∈A is interpreted in
H as j(a).
A propositional modal formula σ(p0, . . . , pn) is valid at G for substitution
instances from LA if every substitution instance σ(ψ0, . . . , ψn) with ψi ∈LA
is true at G. Equivalently, these are precisely the sentential substitution
instances obtained after naming the relevant parameters by constants. We
write ValGrp,→(G, LA) for the resulting set of propositional modal validities.
Thus ValGrp,→(G, L) refers to the sentential regime with no parameters, while
ValGrp,→(G, LG) refers to the formulaic regime of assertions with parameters
from the base group.
