Matching with Deliberation: Test-Time Evolutionary Hierarchical
Multi-Agents for Zero-Shot Compositional Image Retrieval
Xingtian Pei∗, Yukun Song∗, Changwei Wang, Shunpeng Chen, Rongtao Xu, Shengpeng Xu, Shibiao Xu†,
Abstract—Zero-Shot Compositional Image Retrieval (ZS-CIR)
requires both preserving the visual continuity of the reference
image and faithfully executing the semantic variables specified
in the modification text, which constitutes the core challenge of
the task. Existing methods often suffer from Perception Myopia
in a single space, or fall into Logic Drift in iterative collaboration
due to the perception ceiling of the underlying retriever. To
address this issue, we propose a one-stop hierarchical Perception-
to-Deliberation Framework (PDF), which, to the best of our
knowledge, is the first to introduce experience self-evolution
and Test-Time Scaling Law (TTS) into ZS-CIR. Relying on
a hierarchical multi-agent architecture, PDF first utilizes an
Intent Routing Manager to dynamically dispatch multi-view
Worker perception signals based on modification intents to
construct a high-recall candidate pool. Subsequently, the Decision
Manager combines a Training-free Reasoning Policy Distillation
mechanism with a Tournament-style TTS strategy to achieve
self-evolving fine-grained reasoning, yielding the final retrieval
results. Experimental results demonstrate that PDF achieves
SOTA performance on three benchmark datasets: CIRR, CIRCO,
and FashionIQ. This study indicates that experience-driven self-
evolution and TTS represent a highly promising and scalable
path for achieving zero-shot fine-grained multimedia retrieval.
The code will be made publicly available upon acceptance.
Index Terms—Composed Image Retrieval, Multimodal Agentic
Collaboration, Experience Self-evolution, Test-time Scaling Law.

increased inference cost into precision gains.
II. RELATED WORK
A. Zero-shot Composed Image Retrieval
Supervised composed image retrieval methods [16]–[20]
rely on costly triplet annotations to learn target matching
based on a reference image and a modification instruction.
To alleviate the dependence on annotations, early ZS-CIR
methods exploited the vision-language priors of pretrained
models through textual inversion and feature mapping [21]–
[25], thereby mitigating cross-modal asymmetry. However,
these methods are constrained by fixed representation spaces
and often struggle to sensitively capture fine-grained seman-
tic variations. Subsequent approaches introduced knowledge
distillation [26], diffusion models, and synthetic data [27]–
[29] to improve cross-modal alignment, yet they still face
limitations in cross-domain generalization. With the increasing
diversification and specialization of large language models,
training-free ZS-CIR has shifted toward leveraging the gener-
ation [30], [31] and reasoning [32]–[35] capabilities of LLMs
to bridge semantic gaps. Nevertheless, such flat paradigms re-
main limited in correcting the perceptual biases inherited from
the underlying retrievers. In the evolution from single-agent
systems to multi-agent collaboration, MRA-CIR [15] explicitly
decomposes intention and execution, AutoCIR [14] introduces
a corrective feedback loop to enhance system adaptivity, and
XR [36] further incorporates cross-modal collaborative plan-
ning to achieve multi-stage filtering. Despite their improved
accuracy, these methods are still constrained by heuristic and
static orchestration, making it difficult to optimally allocate
reasoning resources for specific query intents.
B. Agentic Collaboration and Heuristic Memory
Multi-agent systems (MAS) have demonstrated remarkable
collaborative effectiveness in multimedia processing [37], [38]
and interpretable reasoning [39]. Their hierarchical orchestra-
tion mechanisms achieve more robust strategic coordination
[40]. Meanwhile, external memory mechanisms provide es-
sential knowledge retention capabilities for complex decision-
making. In the CIR domain, MAI [4] is among the first to
introduce memory modules to address semantic forgetting and
information redundancy in multi-turn interactions. In addition,
the success of memory-enhanced methods in calibrating distri-
butional bias [41], [42] and ensuring navigation stability [43],
[44] further indicates that consolidating historical experience
is a promising pathway toward deeper system intelligence. As
summarized in the survey [45], as AI agents evolve from static
generation toward autonomous planning, memory has become
a core cognitive foundation that connects perception, reason-
ing, and self-evolution. Only by deeply coupling autonomous
decision-making with evolvable memory can a system achieve
genuine long-term adaptability.
The proposed PDF represents an innovative practice along
this emerging trend. We construct a hierarchical multi-agent
architecture and introduce an evolutionary experience repos-
itory through reasoning policy distillation. This mechanism
anchors the agent’s discriminative capability to a structured
experiential foundation, enabling the model to shift from static
matching to memory-driven evolution during the test stage.

sampling [47], and further to tree-structured heuristic search
[48]. With the release of OpenAI o1 [49], TTS has stimulated

III. METHODOLOGY
A. Problem Formulation and Framework Overview
Task Definition. ZS-CIR aims to retrieve target images
Itar satisfying the modification intent from a large-scale
gallery G = {I1, I2, . . . , IN} based on a composed query
q = (Iref, Tmod), without training on any annotated triplet
data D = {Iref, Tmod, Itar}. Here, Iref denotes the reference
image and Itar denotes the target image. The ground-truth set
corresponding to query q is denoted as Yq ⊆G. For single-
ground-truth datasets such as CIRR and FashionIQ, we have
|Yq| = 1; while for the multi-ground-truth dataset CIRCO, we
have |Yq| > 1.
Visual Semantic Proxy Construction. In ZS-CIR, Tmod
is an instruction-oriented text with operational tendencies. It
usually omits the subject or contextual anchors, yet implicitly
imposes complex executable constraints on the content of
Iref. Such a query form requires the model not only to
understand the semantics of Tmod, but also to extract sufficient
and complementary visual semantics from Iref. Therefore, we
construct a visual semantic proxy for I ∈G as follows:
C(I) = Φvsp(I),
(1)
where Φvsp(·) represents the visual semantic proxy con-
structor, and C(I) is a dense semantic transcription of I.
This representation preserves visual anchors as evidence for
subsequent deliberate reasoning.
Overview. Our PDF framework, as show in Fig. 2, first
employs the Semantic Imagination Worker Agent (SI-Worker),
Constraint Parsing Worker Agent (CP-Worker), and Reference
Consistency Worker Agent (RC-Worker) to extract priors
from three complementary perspectives and generate candidate
results. Subsequently, the Intent Routing Manager agent (IR-
Manager) adaptively fuses these three results according to
the prior intent of Tmod, thereby constructing a high-recall
candidate buffer BK (Sec. 3.2). The Deliberation Evolution
Manager agent (DE-Manager) then performs high-precision
candidate discrimination within BK based on the CI of candi-
date images through self-evolution-enhanced T-TTS (Sec. 3.3),
and directly outputs the final retrieval results.

IV. EXPERIMENTS
A. Experimental Setup
Datasets and Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate our PDF on
three representative CIR benchmark datasets: FashionIQ [64]
focuses on the fashion domain and contains three subcate-
gories: Dresses, Shirts, and Tops & Tees. We use Recall@K
(K ∈{10, 50}) for these three categories, as well as their
average values, as the evaluation metrics. CIRR [65] is the first
natural-scene CIR dataset with subset retrieval. Following the
standard protocol, we report Recall@K (K ∈{1, 5, 10, 50})
and Recallsubset@K (K
∈{1, 2, 3}). CIRCO [26] is the
first ZS-CIR dataset with multiple ground-truth annotations,
and we adopt mean Average Precision (mAP@K) (K ∈
{5, 10, 25, 50}) as the evaluation metric.
Implementation Details. We adopt the ViT-B/32 and
ViT-L/14 CLIP [66] models from OpenCLIP as the base
retrievers. For all LLM invocations, we use GPT-4o-mini by
default.The smoothing constant τ for weighted rank fusion is
set to 60. The candidate buffer size is set to K = 50, which
is divided into two reasoning stages (L = 2). All experiments
are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU.
Baselines.To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of the
proposed PDF framework, we compare it with the following
three categories of representative methods:
• Training-dependent representation approaches such as
Pic2Word [21], PALAVRA [16], SEARLE [26], LinCIR
[23], and FTI4CIR [22] achieve robust alignment through
specialized mappings but incur high training costs and
exhibit limited cross-domain generalization.
• Generative training-free baselines like CIReVL [32],
ImageScope [34], CoTMR [33], LDRE [30], IP-CIR [31],
and OSrCIR [35] leverage frozen large-scale models for
zero-shot alignment, yet they lack deep self-calibration
capabilities under complex modification intents.
• Strategic multi-agent architectures, including MRA-CIR
[15], AutoCIR [14], and XR [36], represent the current
state-of-the-art paradigm, though they are often prone to
logic drift and constrained by perceptual ceilings within
flat iterative processes. In contrast, PDF increases rea-
soning density through hierarchical decision-making and
Test-Time Scaling (TTS), enabling one-stop fine-grained
semantic discrimination without repeated retrieval.

B. Results
Table I and Table II report the quantitative comparison
results of the proposed PDF framework on CIRCO, CIRR, and
FashionIQ. Overall, PDF achieves the best results on 35 out
of 38 reported metrics, accounting for 92.1% of all evaluation
settings. The detailed observations are summarized as follows.
Overall Performance. PDF shows clear advantages on
CIRCO and FashionIQ. Compared with the strongest agentic
baseline XR [36], PDF achieves a 45.47% improvement
in mAP@5 on CIRCO under ViT-B/32, and improves the
average R@10 on FashionIQ by 7.4%. On CIRR, PDF also
reaches state-of-the-art performance and outperforms XR on
78.57% of the reported metrics. These results demonstrate the
effectiveness of the proposed hierarchical reasoning paradigm
in handling complex compositional intents.
Training-dependent Methods optimize a fixed representa-
tion space using costly annotated data. However, such static
representations are often insufficient when facing diverse se-
mantic variations. In contrast, PDF adaptively mobilizes three
complementary priors through the IPR mechanism. Compared
with FTI4CIR under ViT-L/14, PDF improves mAP@5 by
185.98% on CIRCO, Recall@1 by 68.53% on CIRR, and
the average R@10 by 34.98% on FashionIQ. These results
indicate that IPR exhibits stronger adaptability to semantic
shifts than fixed training-based features.
Generative Training-free Methods avoid additional pa-
rameter training, but they usually rely on a single generated
representation or a fixed retrieval pathway. PDF alleviates this
limitation by dynamically integrating three complementary pri-
ors. Compared with CoTMR under ViT-B/32, PDF improves
mAP@5 by 78.98% on CIRCO and Recall@1 by 26.11% on
CIRR. These improvements demonstrate that the multi-view
intent routing and fine-selection mechanism of PDF can better
handle noisy real-world retrieval spaces, where relying solely
on LLM-generated target descriptions may be insufficient.
Strategic Multi-agent Architectures. For current main-
stream
agentic
frameworks,
PDF
introduces
experience-
evolved TTS to break through the performance ceiling of
pure perception-based matching.Unlike AutoCIR [14], which
is constrained by iterative redundancy and the upper bound of
the retriever, PDF adopts a one-stop decision-making process
to more deeply exploit the potential of pretrained models.
Compared with the isolated verification of XR [36] under
static orchestration, PDF achieves step-wise expansion of log-
ical density through dynamic intent routing and tournament-
style fine selection. By strengthening horizontal competitive
discrimination within the candidate set, PDF drives a qualita-
tive shift of the retrieval paradigm from “intuitive alignment”
to “logical decision-making”.

C. Ablation Study
In this section, we conduct ablation experiments with ViT-
B/32 on FashionIQ and CIRCO to systematically analyze the
key designs of PDF. Specifically, we study the effectiveness
of the core components, the adaptiveness of intent-aware prior
routing, the influence of deliberation depth and experience
guidance, and the trade-off between candidate pool size and
test-time computational cost.
Table III presents the ablation results of the core modules in
the PDF framework. Under the same IPR strategy, the system
performance steadily improves as the orthogonal perception
priors Wkey and Wvis are progressively incorporated, demon-
strating the necessity of the multi-view perception scheme in
constructing a structured candidate basis. After introducing the
decision deliberation module, the full model achieves the best
performance. Compared with the variant using only Wpred, it
obtains relative improvements of 23.76% on FashionIQ R@10
and 58.94% on CIRCO mAP@5, confirming the feasibility of
trading increased reasoning density for stronger discriminative
resolution. Moreover, we observe a clear task-heterogeneity
sensitivity. The performance gains on FashionIQ rely more
heavily on the expansion of perception priors, increasing from
31.82 to 37.57, whereas CIRCO exhibits a stronger response to
the Delib. stage, improving from 30.78 to 40.02. We attribute
this phenomenon to the intrinsic characteristics of the two
datasets. In FashionIQ, the visual categories are relatively
stable, but the semantic descriptions are often subjective
and ambiguous, involving attributes such as color, style, and
fashion preference, which limits the discriminative resolution
of logical reasoning. In contrast, although real-world scene
datasets exhibit larger visual variations that may weaken the
effectiveness of perception priors, their descriptions of entities
and attributes are usually more deterministic and unique,
providing ideal logical anchors for fine-grained comparison
in the deliberation stage.
The comparison of fusion strategies further shows that dy-
namic IPR outperforms both average fusion (Avg.) and global
static fusion (Static). Notably, the performance on CIRCO
even degrades under the equal-weight setting, indicating that
naive aggregation without explicit orchestration dilutes critical
signals with visual noise. This further validates the superiority
of intent-driven resource scheduling. Fig. 3 visualizes the
dynamic weight allocation of the IPR mechanism under het-
erogeneous intents. The experimental results show that the IR-
Manager achieves precise scheduling of perceptual resources.

V. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we introduce a Perception-to-Deliberation
Framework, a hierarchical framework composed of Intent-
aware Prior Routing (IPR) and an Evolutionary Reasoning Re-
finer (ERR): IPR dynamically orchestrates three complemen-
tary priors, including target imagination, explicit constraints,
and visual anchors, effectively addressing the perceptual my-
opia and semantic drift caused by static fusion under diverse
modification intents. Guided by offline-distilled evolutionary
experience, ERR further improves reasoning density through a
tournament-style Test-Time scaling strategy. Both components
are training-free and plug-and-play, and achieve significant
performance improvements on three widely used benchmarks,
including CIRR, CIRCO, and FashionIQ, fully validating the
effectiveness of the “thinking instead of searching” paradigm.
Future research directions include extending this hierar-
chical deliberation concept to broader vision tasks, such as
multimodal generation and object detection, and exploring
dynamic allocation strategies of computational resources for
queries with different levels of difficulty. Although our frame-
work demonstrates strong robustness, its potential limitations
include that the fine-selection accuracy is still partially con-
strained by the quality of visual semantic proxies. Further
studies may explore more efficient multi-granularity image-
text representations. Nevertheless, our work provides valuable
insights into breaking the perceptual ceiling of frozen mul-
timodal models and opens up a new avenue toward more
scalable intelligent multimedia retrieval systems.
