MODEL-THEORETIC TAMENESS IN FINITE EXTENSIONS OF GROUPS
YATIR HALEVI AND SAHARON SHELAH
Abstract. It is shown that finite-index extensions and finite-index subgroups of ω-stable groups
can be model-theoretically wild. More precisely, there exists an ω-stable group G such that any
given countable first-order structure in a finite language is interpretable both in some finite-index
extension of G and in some finite-index subgroup of G.
1. Introduction
A basic and recurring structural question in group theory is: Which properties are inherited or
transferred in finite group extensions?
In the case of model-theoretic stability, the question is:
Question 1.1. Let G be an infinite group, and let H ≤G be a subgroup of finite index.
Is
stability preserved when passing between G and H? More precisely, if G is stable, must H be
stable? Conversely, if H is stable, must G be stable?
One can, of course, ask this for any model-theoretic tameness notion.
The direction of transferability of stability up in finite extensions is known for abelian groups:
abelian-by-finite groups are stable (folklore, essentially due to [?]). The general question in this
direction goes back at least to [Me˘ı86]. A counterexample was given by Simon Thomas and was
communicated by Baldwin in [Bal89, Example 3.2]: The semidirect product of SL2(C) and Z/2Z
under the action of complex conjugation.1
We answer the general question negatively. The situation can be made as bad as possible with
respect to model-theoretic tameness.
Theorem 1.2 (Proposition 4.5 and 3.3). Let p be an odd prime. There exists an ω-stable group H
of nilpotency class 2 and exponent p such that for any first-order countable structure M in a finite
language:
(1) There is a supergroup G ⊇H satisfying that [G : H] = 2 and that (G, ·) interprets M.
(2) There is a normal subgroup K ⊴H of index p such that (K, ·) interprets M.
Remark 1.3.
(1) This implies that the folklore result on the stability of abelian-by-finite groups
is strict in the following sense. Replacing “abelian” by “solvable” does not guarantee sta-
bility (this is already implied by a variant of Simon Thomas’s counterexample). As for the
other direction, if H is a perfect group with bounded commutator length and G/H is finite
abelian (or even solvable) then it is easy to see that H is definable in G.
(2) The countability assumption is not essential here.
One can find appropriate groups to
interpret structures of large cardinality as well.
The group is produced by applying Mekler’s construction [Mek81].
The desired supergroup
is obtained by producing an automorphism, and the subgroup is obtained with the aid of an
appropriate linear functional on the Fp-vector space H/Z(H).

2. Mekler’s Construction
We review the basic properties of Mekler groups we will need.
Definition 2.1. [Mek81, Definition 1.1] A nice graph (in the sense of Mekler) is a graph Γ = (V, E)
satisfying:
(1) The graph has at least two vertices and for every two distinct vertices v, u ∈V there is
some vertex, not equal to v or u, that is connected to v but not to u.
(2) There are no triangles or squares in the graph.
By [Mek81, Definition 2.1] (see also [Hod93, Appendix A.3]) to each nice graph Γ and odd prime
p, one associates a nilpotent group of class 2 and exponent p, H = H(Γ). It is the free group in
the variety of nilpotent groups of class 2 and exponent p generated by {xv : v ∈V } whose only
relations impose that xv and xv′ commute if and only if (v, v′) ∈E.
Note that the definition readily implies that every automorphism of the graph Γ induces an
automorphism of the group H.

Appendix A. Non-superstability
Showing that a certain group is model-theoretically tame is usually quite difficult; showing that
it is not is sometimes easier.
There is a non-exhaustive list of results showing that certain familiar groups are not superstable;
see [Poi83, MPS22, OH07, CPP25].
Our aim here is to prove that certain groups commensurable with classical groups are not super-
stable by mimicking Poizat’s proof for the free group. Although the proof is different, the results
are not new since they follow from [OH07].
Definition A.1. For a group G and integers n ≥2, m ≥1, define:
(1) An,m(G) = {g ∈G : gn has at most m n-th roots in G}.
(2) Gn = {gn : g ∈G}.
These are definable sets in the group language. We say that a group satisfies property Q if there
are integers n ≥2, m ≥1 such that An,m(G) is a generic2 set in G but Gn is not.
Property Q is precisely what is needed for Poizat’s proof of non-superstability of the free group.
The following is essentially his proof and is well known.
Proposition A.2. Any group satisfying property Q is not superstable.
Proof. Assume, toward a contradiction, that G is superstable and satisfies property Q. As Gn is
not generic, G \ Gn must be a generic definable set by stability.
By stability theory, each generic set contains a generic type. Let tp(a), for a ∈G ≻G in a
sufficiently saturated extension, be a generic type concentrated on the definable set An,m(G). Since
G is assumed to be superstable and a ∈acl(an), tp(an) is also a generic type [Poi01, Lemma 5.16].
Since tp(an) can contain only generic definable sets it must concentrate on G\Gn, contradiction.
□

□
Remark A.8. The fact that G satisfies unique root extraction (i.e. G = An,1(G) for all n) was used
in order to propagate the result to commensurable groups. If instead of unique root extraction,
G = An,1(G) for some n ≥2 (and Gn is not generic in G) then G is not superstable.
Corollary A.9. Any group which is commensurable with one of the following is not superstable:
(1) Non-abelian right-angled Artin groups.
(2) Surface groups π1(Σg) for g ≥2.
(3) Pure braid groups Pn for n ≥3.
Proof. (1) Right-angled Artin groups are finitely generated by definition, linear [HW99, Corollary
3.6], not virtually solvable by [Bau81, Theorem 1.2] and Tits alternative, and have unique root
extraction because they are biorderable as a graph product of copies of Z [Chi12].
(2) Surface groups are finitely generated by definition, it is classical that they are linear (there
are even many faithful representations [DK06]), not virtually solvable by [Put22, Second footnote]
and Tits alternative, and have unique root extraction because they are biorderable [CR16, Theorem
3.11].
(3) Pure braid groups are finitely generated [KT08, Corollary 1.19], linear [KT08, Theorem 3.18],
not virtually solvable [KT08, Theorem 1.16] and Tits alternative, and have unique root extraction
because they are biorderable [KT08, Theorem 7.8].
□
