MEETING SOLOMON MARCUS
FLORIN FELIX NICHITA
Abstract. Dedicated to Solomon Marcus, the current paper continues a recent series
about our meetings. Trying to recreate the spirit of those meetings, we first propose
a discussion which started as a high-school problem. The main part of the current
paper consists in a section about racks. It presents elements of trigonometry in racks,
and Euler formulas associated in this framework.
Keyword: racks; series; Euler’s identity; Euler’s relation; memories
MSC: 00A05; 01A05; 11D09; 16B50; 30D05; 33B10
1. Introduction
I received an invitation from the journal “Libertas Matematica (new series)” to write
an article about Solomon Marcus (see, for example, [1, 2]) on the occasion of one hun-
dred years from his birth (March 1-st, 1925). So, I thought about this invitation, I
recalled our meetings, I discussed with several people about Solomon Marcus, and I
decided that it was the right moment to publish some of my memories. I submitted my
manuscript ([3]), and it was accepted soon afterwards. This determined me to write
a second paper dedicated to Solomon Marcus. Writing about such a huge personality
is not an easy task, but certainly a rewarding one, especially when you recall some
important moments from our professional activity. When my second paper ([4]) was
still in the process of being evaluated for publication in “Libertas Matematica (new
series)”, I posted a survey of [3] and [4] on the Mathematical Archive ([5]), adding
some new content. For example, I gave applications of the B-ring Euler formula in
finding solutions for the braid condition. This was well-received, and my e-mail corre-
spondence led me to the remark that Euler, Erdos and Euclid are frequently mentioned
in my discussions with Solomon Marcus. Also, there were interesting interactions with
journals, colleagues etc. From Brown University (Rhode Island), I received four ar-
ticles about Solomon Marcus (see [6, 7, 8, 9]). Needless to say how much I valuated
them. I learned about: the life of Solomon Marcus in Providence, the “junior” Solomon
Marcus meeting Al. Rosetti, his work at “Poetica Mathematica”, the correspondence
between Solomon Marcus and Sanda Golopentia, the relationship between science and
literature, the contributions of Solomon Marcus to the literature journals (Romania
Literara, Viata Romaneasca, Dilema Veche etc) and so on. These four articles have
motivated me to write the fourth article in the series which starting with the paper
[3]. Also, I would like to mention another Mathematical Archive communication ([10]),
which was posted soon after our preprint ([5]), and it gives some weight to our results,
because the solutions to the braid equation lead to representations of the braid group.
More recently, Bradshaw and Vignat wrote a paper, in a similar manner with ours,
about another “beautiful mind” (see [11]).

3. Trigonometry and the Euler formula in racks
In this section, we will introduce elements of trigonometry in racks (see [19]). The
terminology might be slightly changed from the original theory. These adjustments will
help us to emphasize the new results. We will obtain Euler formulas and identities in
this framework.
3.1. Trigonometry in racks.
Definition 3.1. A rack is triple (S, ·, ⋄), where S is a set with two binary operations,
satisfying the following four axioms:
a · (b · c) = (a · b) · (a · c) ,
(a · b) ⋄a = b ,
a · (b ⋄a) = b ,
(c ⋄b) ⋄a = (c ⋄a) ⋄(b ⋄a) .
The operation · is called the main operation, we will write a · b = ab , and it has
priority over ⋄in formulas.

3.2. Weak racks.
Definition 3.4. A weak rack is triple (S, ·, ⋄), where S is a set with two binary
operations, satisfying the following three axioms:
a · (b · c) = (a · b) · (a · c) ,
(a · b) ⋄a = a · (b ⋄a) ,
(c ⋄b) ⋄a = (c ⋄a) ⋄(b ⋄a) .
Again, the for the main operation we will write a · b = ab , and it has priority over
⋄in formulas.
Remark 3.5. The following weak rack can be associated to a Boolean algebra:
ab = a →b,
a ⋄b = a \ b .
Also, the following weak rack can be associated:
ab = a ∨b, a ⋄b = a ∧b.
Let e, O ∈S, Π = eO, U = e(eO), cos x = ex , and sin x = x ⋄e. We have the
following properties:
cos Π = U ;
sin Π = O ; cos xy = cos x cos y ;
cos(x ⋄y) = cos x ⋄cos y ;
sin xy = sin x sin y ;
sin(x ⋄y) = sin x ⋄sin y .
The fundamental formula is the following:
sin (cos x) = cos(sin x).
3.3. The Euler formula and the Euler identity in (weak) racks.
Definition 3.6. We can define a dual rack with the opposite operations, (S, ∗, • ),
where the new operations are the following:
a ∗b = b ⋄a and
a • b = b · a .
Remark 3.7. The rack having the following operations is self-dual:
ab = b,
a ⋄b = a.
On the set S × S, we can put a rack structure obtained as the product of the initial
rack with its dual. Let us denote its main operation as follows:
(x, y) □(u, v) = (xu, v ⋄y) = (xu, y ∗v).
For a ∈S, we define an “exponential” function on S × S:
expa(x, y) = a(x, y) = (ax, a ∗y).
The “exponential” map has the property: a(x, y)□(u, v) = a(x, y) □a(u, v).
The diagonal map, ∆: S →S × S, x 7→(x, x) , is a rack morphism for a certain
rack structure on S × S.
