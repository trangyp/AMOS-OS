Abstract
We study mutually unbiased bases (MUBs) as structured finite initialization and adaptation families for variational quantum algorithms.
The central contribution is theoretical: in every dimension admitting a complete set of MUBs, the complete MUB ensemble maximizes
the isotropic Gaussian random-Hamiltonian width among all unions of 𝑑+1 orthonormal bases in ℂ𝑑. Equivalently, among basis-union
ensembles of the same size, no competitor has a smaller expected best-of-set minimum for random-Hamiltonian minimization. The
proof reduces each orthonormal basis to a regular-simplex Gaussian block and applies the centered-convex Gaussian correlation
inequality to show that the independent-block case, realized by complete MUBs, is stochastically extremal. We also record the
corresponding radial extension for Hamiltonians 𝐻= 𝑅𝐺, where 𝐺is isotropic Gaussian and 𝑅≥0 is an independent scalar,
and the unrestricted 𝑑= 2 case, where complete qubit MUBs are globally optimal among arbitrary six-state ensembles by the
Bloch-sphere/octahedron mean-width argument.
The algorithmic part separates this coverage theorem from training dynamics. For diagonal QUBO costs, the MUB-family dependence
of a fully matched construction collapses; for the canonical 𝑏= 0 label this reduces to ordinary 𝑋-mixer QAOA; therefore the empirical
method is framed as adaptive MUB-XRot warm-start QAOA, not as canonical matched-mixer MUB-QAOA. In a cross-problem statevector
benchmark over MaxCut, weighted MaxCut, MIS, weighted MIS, and knapsack, adaptive MUB-XRot is non-worse than standard QAOA
in 80.0% of 1500 paired instance-depth cases, with win/tie/loss counts 829∕371∕300, mean decoded-ratio improvement +0.1616, and
solved rate increasing from 28.0% to 43.8%. A separate QRAO MaxCut study shows that bit-flip MUB-family search provides a cheaper
local alternative to exhaustive family enumeration: the multi-start 2POLE variant reaches mean relaxed ratio 0.921, improves over
the 𝑋-variational baseline by +0.0608, and is non-worse in 80.0% of 360 paired cells. The empirical evidence is quality-oriented and
incurs substantial runtime overhead; no quantum-advantage claim is made.

nonnegative radial mixtures.

not prove optimality among arbitrary 𝑑(𝑑+ 1)-state ensembles. It
also does not model optimizer dynamics, ansatz expressivity, noise,

10. CONCLUSION
Complete mutually unbiased bases are not only moment-balanced
finite ensembles; they are extremal among unions of orthonormal
bases for isotropic random-Hamiltonian Gaussian width. The main
theorem proves that, in every dimension admitting a complete MUB
system, no other union of 𝑑+ 1 bases has a larger expected random-
Hamiltonian maximum or a smaller expected best-of-set minimum.
The proof isolates mutual unbiasedness as independence between
regular-simplex Gaussian blocks and uses a centered-convex Gaus-
sian correlation inequality to show that this independence is ex-
tremal. The same comparison extends to independent nonnegative
radial mixtures. In the special qubit case, the unrestricted six-state
problem reduces to the mean-width extremality of the regular octa-
hedron on the Bloch sphere.
Algorithmically, this coverage optimality must be used with care.
For diagonal costs, the MUB-family dependence of fully matched
MUB-QAOA collapses; in the canonical 𝑏= 0 case this reduces
to ordinary 𝑋-mixer QAOA. Useful mechanisms therefore require
warm-start usage, non-diagonal Hamiltonians, or both. The adap-
tive MUB-XRot benchmark gives the main empirical signal across
several small optimization families. The QRAO bit-flip search exper-
iment adds a scalability mechanism for MUB-family selection, while
the exhaustive QRAO and TFIM VQE studies provide secondary
support. The results support MUBs as theoretically principled finite
coverage ensembles and empirically useful warm-start structures in
selected settings, without implying quantum advantage or runtime
efficiency.
