MAGNITUDE OF METRIC MEASURE SPACES AND INTEGRALS
OVER GEODESICS
YOSHINORI HASHIMOTO
Abstract. We propose a definition of magnitude for a length space with a Borel measure,
which involves integrals over the set of geodesics. This quantity agrees with the magnitude
of finite metric spaces, up to re-scaling the metric to ensure the convergence, when we use
the counting measure on them. We also prove a version of the homogeneous magnitude
theorem, by showing that the new definition agrees with the volume when we use the weight
measure on a compact homogeneous Riemannian manifold. We compute various examples,
which suggest that this quantity can capture information of non-uniqueness of geodesics,
such as the injectivity radius, corresponding to the generating degrees of the magnitude
homology.

1. Introduction
1.1. Overview of the results. The magnitude is an invariant of metric spaces introduced
by Leinster [18], extensively studied recently, which is primarily defined for finite metric
spaces. There are mainly two approaches (see e.g. [18, §3] or [24, §2]) to extend this defini-
tion to infinite metric spaces: one is to take the supremum of magnitude over all its finite
subspaces (or the Gromov–Hausdorff limit of them [18,23,28]), the other is to compute the
volume with respect to the weight measure as done by Willerton [29]. Meckes [24, Theorems
2.3 and 2.4] proved that these definitions agree for positive definite metric spaces, such as
the ones listed in [24, Theorem 3.6], assuming that a weight measure exists.
The aim of this paper is to try to give a unified framework for these two approaches,
by proposing a new definition of magnitude for metric measure spaces.

(1) When (X, d) is a finite metric space and µ♯is the counting measure, Mag(X, d, µ♯)
agrees with the original definition of magnitude by re-scaling d to ensure the con-
vergence if necessary (Theorem 3.7). In this formalism, the magnitude of a finite
subspace Z ⊂X corresponds to choosing µ to be the counting measure supported
on Z (Lemma 3.8).
(2) If a weight measure µw exists for a compact Riemannian manifold (X, g) and if it is
absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, Mag(X, d, µw) is given
by the volume µw(X), up to taking a subsequence (Theorem 5.3). In particular,
Mag(X, d, µw) satisfies the homogeneous magnitude theorem as in [29] when (X, g)
is a compact homogeneous Riemannian manifold (Corollary 5.6).
(3) When (X, g) is a Riemannian manifold and d is its length metric, it is natural to
take µ to be the volume form dvolg of g. Examples in §4.2 and §4.3 indicate that
Mag(X, d, dvolg) contains information of the injectivity radius and the diameter of
(X, g), which seems to correspond to the non-trivial generators of the magnitude
homology group, or the jump in the number of geodesics connecting two points.
(4) In the form that involves integrals over the set of geodesics, we can define new in-
variants Mag(X, d, µ, Γ) by choosing various measures for Γ on the set of geodesics
(Definition 3.4). These invariants give different values when geodesics are not unique,
as we see for finite graphs (especially 4-cuts) in §3.3.

Concerning the classical approach of taking the supremum of magnitude over all finite
subspaces, the first point above gives a perspective of formulating this problem as approxi-
mating a given measure by a counting measure. We can consider a related problem by using
an empirical measure (the average of delta measures supported on finite points). Berman,
Boucksom, and Witt Nystr¨om [3, 4] proved that the empirical measure on the Fekete con-
figuration converges to the equilibrium measure on a compact K¨ahler manifold. We can
prove, by using this result, that the magnitude Mag(X, d, µ) computed for an equilibrium
measure µ is a limit of an appropriately scaled version of magnitude of the Fekete configu-
rations (Theorem 3.20). For CP1, we can also establish a relationship between the classical
magnitude and the limit of the magnitude of Fekete configurations (Corollary 5.9).
The second point above shows that Mag(X, d, µw) agrees with the classical magnitude for
spheres, which is positive definite, when we use the weight measure µw (see also Corollary
5.8). We also give an example (see §5.4) for which the new definition Mag(X, d, µ) does not
agree with the classical one when there is a singular component in the weight measure.
The third point above indicates that the invariant Mag(X, d, µ) may be related to the de-
categorification of the magnitude homology, at least for the examples covered in this paper,
which include R, S1, S2, and a 2-torus (see §4.2 and §4.3). Recall that the magnitude ho-
mology measures the non-uniqueness of geodesics, as proved by Asao [1] and Gomi [13]. The
examples in §4.3 show that the new definition of magnitude function can detect the injec-
tivity radius or the diameter, which is deeply connected to the non-uniqueness of geodesics,
as the rate of exponential decay. These are the length parameters for which the magnitude
homology is non-trivial, as computed by Gomi [13, Theorem 1.3]. When the manifold is
uniquely geodesic, such as (R, | · |) treated in §4.2, there seem to be no terms of such decay
(i.e. no terms of order e−ct for some constant c > 0). On the other hand, the example in
§4.3.4 shows that this naive picture overlooks the contributions from the boundary.
The fourth point above means the variety of definitions that we have, by choosing various
measures µ and Γ.
1.2. Organisation of the paper. We review the classical definition of magnitude for finite
metric spaces in §2, with a path-integral like interpretation to motivate the main part of the
text. The definitions of the invariants Mag(X, d, µ, Γ), Mag(X, d, µ) are given in §3, together
with some sufficient conditions to ensure their convergence, and also a relationship to the
Fekete configurations. In §4, we prove some preliminary results that apply for Riemann-
ian manifolds with weighted volume forms, before computing various examples in §4.2 and
§4.3. Finally, we treat the case when the metric space admits a positive weight measure in
§5, and prove various results including the homogeneous magnitude theorem for compact
homogeneous Riemannian manifolds.
1.3. Notational conventions and terminologies.
• Throughout this paper, a metric space (X, d) is either a finite metric space or a length
space. For a Borel measure µ on (X, d), we write µn for the product measure on Xn.
• When X is finite, µ♯denotes the counting measure, with the defining property
µ♯(A) = |A| for any A ⊂X, where we write |A| for the cardinality of A.
• For a Riemannian manifold (X, g), we write dg for the length metric with respect g,
