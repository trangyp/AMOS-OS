MEAN WEAK LENGTH
ZIHAN BAI AND BINGBING LIANG
Abstract. We introduce a weak version of the classical length function, termed
the weak length function, defined on subsets of R-modules over a unital ring R,
and further consider the concept of mean weak length for RΓ-modules associated
with an amenable group Γ. Under an appropriate upgrading condition together
with certain mild assumptions, we establish that the mean weak length is additive
with respect to short exact sequences.
This result has two applications. First, we provide a purely algebraic proof of
the additivity of algebraic entropy¡ªa property originally established via topolog-
ical entropy methods in [L12]¡ªthereby offering an independent perspective that
avoids dynamical systems machinery. Second, within our unified framework, we
give an alternative and conceptual proof of the additivity of mean length, previ-
ously obtained in [LL18] and [V19] using different approaches.
1. Introduction
The validity of additivity is a central problem for invariants on pure algebraic
objects. In the category of R-modules for a given untial ring R, a numerical invariant
i is said to have the property of additivity, or the so-called addition formula, if for
any left R-modules M1 ⊆M2, one has
i(M2) = i(M1) + i(M2/M1).
The notion of mean length, termed in [LL18, LL19], is a class of numerical invariants
for modules over the group ring RΓ of a discrete group Γ with coeﬀicients in R. Its
definition is based on a length function L on R-modules introduced by Northcott-
Reufel [NR65]. Salce-Zanardo first initiated an axiomatic approach for Γ = Z [SZ09]
and the addition formula was established by Scale-Vámos-Virili in the case that L
is discrete [SVV13]. Salce-Virili considered the case Γ = Zd and L is nondiscrete
[SV16]. Elek considered the case that R is any field and Γ is any amenable group
[E03]. Li-Liang dealt with the case that L(R) < ∞for amenable groups in [LL18]
and sofic groups in [LL19]. Virili established the aditivity for general length L and
any amenable group Γ in [V19]. Note that the validity of additivity for mean length
is restricted to the locally finite modules in the sense that all finitely generated
submodules have finite length values.

On the other hand, algebraic entropy for ZΓ-modules defined by Peters attracted
great attention due to its direct connection with the topological entropy of Pon-
tryagin dual actions [P79], which is the so-called Bridge theorem. Motivated by
the connection between Mahler measure and topological entropy established by
Yuzvinskii[Y67], one can even use the algebraic entropy to give a direct charac-
terization of Lehmer’s problem [LSW90, DG16]. Unfortunately, the definition of
algebraic entropy is not based on a length function (though torsion modules is lo-
cally finite and can be dealt within the tool of mean length) and its additivity is out
of reach of the approaches for mean lengths. Taking advantage of Bridge theorem,
for any amenable group Γ, Li confirmed the validity of additivity of algebraic en-
tropy by establishing the additivity of topological entropy for a class of dynamical
systems including algebraic actions [L12]. This motivates the following questions:
Question 1.1. Is there a pure algebraic proof of additivity for algebraic entropy?
Moreover, is there a unified approach of additivity for algebraic entropy and mean
length?
There has been much study on the additivity for algebraic entropy [W74]. A
complete solution for the case Γ = Z is given by Dikranjan-Giordano Bruno [DG16].
Recently the additivity is established for actions of monotileable ameanble groups by
Dikranjan-Fornasiero-Giordano Bruno-Salizzoni [DFGS23]. The crucial ingrediant
in [DFGS23] is to introduce a bivariant function on subsets of abelian groups as a
main tool to estimate the upper bound of big modules in terms of submodules.
In this paper we provide a satisfactory solution to the above questions. To unify
the distinct approaches of mean length and algebraic entropy, our idea is to first
weaken the requirements of length function and consider a weaker version of length
function in order to encomprass both length function and the function appeared in
the definition of algebraic entropy. We call such a function the weak length function,
which is defined on the subsets of R-modules. Based on this notion, we can define an
invariant on RΓ-modules, called mean weak length, to generalize both mean length
and algebraic entropy. To overcome the core diﬀiculty of upper bound estimation in
the addition formula, motivated from the bivariant functions of [DFGS23], we also
introduce the bivariant version of weak length functions. It turns out that as a weak
length function can be upgraded to a good bivariant weak length function, then one
can give a successful desired upper bound estimation. Our main result is as follows:
Theorem 1.2. Let R be a unital ring and Γ a discrete amenable group. Suppose
that ℓis a locally finite weak length function ℓon subsets of R-modules with strong
quotient properties. Then if ℓcan be upgraded to a proper bivariant R-weak length
function, the mean weak length mℓon RΓ-modules satisfies the addition formula.
That is, for any RΓ-modules M1 ⊆M2, we have
mℓ(M2) = mℓ(M1) + mℓ(M2/M1).

3. Weak length function
Let R be a untial ring. Denote by PR and FR the collection of nonempty subsets of
R-modules and nonempty finite subsets of R-modules respectively. For any subset
A of an R-module M denote by < A >R the R-submodule generated by A. For
another subset B ⊆M and C ⊆R, we write
A + B := {a + b : a ∈A, b ∈B}, and CA := {ra : r ∈C and a ∈A}.
A simple observation is that if 0 ∈A ∩B, then A + B contains A ∪B. Write F(G)
for the nonempty finite subsets of G and F0(G) for the finite subsets of G containing
zero element respectively.
Definition 3.1. A map ℓ: PR →R∪{+∞} is called a R-weak length if it satisfies
the following properties:
(1) regularity: ℓ({0}) = 0;
(2) product property: ℓ(A × B) = ℓ(A) + ℓ(B), for any A, B ∈PR;
(3) quotient property: for any R-module homomorphism φ: M →N, one has
ℓ(A) ≥ℓ(φ(A)) for any A ⊆M with A ∈PR;
(4) upper continuity: if An increases to A, then ℓ(An) increases to ℓ(A). In
particular, ℓis monotone for increasing subsets in the sense that whenever
A1 ⊆A2 one has ℓ(A1) ≤ℓ(A2).
We say ℓis locally finite if ℓ(A) < +∞for any A ∈FR; ℓhas the strong quotient
property if for any R-module homomorphism φ: M →N, one has
ℓ(A + B) ≥ℓ(φ(A)) + ℓ(B)
for any A ∈F0(M) and B ∈F0(ker φ). When ℓis partially defined on a subcollection
CR ⊆PR that is closed under taking product, quotient and direct union operations,
we would say ℓis a weak length function on CR.
Remark 3.2. In [SZ09, Section 1], Scale-Zanardo used some weaker axioms to
introduce the notion of subadditive invariants on modules. In contrast our preference
of weak length function is to relax the domain in order to prepare for the definition
of algebraic entropy and validity of addition formula.

Definition 3.6. A bivariant R-weak length function is an R ∪{∞}-valued
