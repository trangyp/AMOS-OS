performed in the 𝐻→𝑍𝑍∗→4ℓdecay channel. The analysis uses proton–proton collision
data produced at the Large Hadron Collider at a centre-of-mass energy of √𝑠= 13.6 TeV and

The inclusive fiducial cross-section for the 𝐻→𝑍𝑍∗→4ℓprocess is measured to be
𝜎fid = 3.65+0.35
−0.33 fb, in agreement with the Standard Model prediction of 𝜎SM

Higgs boson and the four-lepton final state.
Cross-sections are measured for the main

1 Introduction
The observation of the Higgs boson by the ATLAS and CMS Collaborations [1, 2] using proton–proton
(𝑝𝑝) collisions at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) marked a major milestone in the understanding of
the electroweak (EW) symmetry-breaking mechanism [3–5]. Subsequent studies of the Higgs boson
properties have established the observed particle as a scalar boson consistent with the Standard Model
(SM) predictions for a Higgs boson with a mass of 125.09 ± 0.24 GeV [6].
The 𝐻→𝑍𝑍∗→4ℓdecay channel, where ℓ= 𝑒, 𝜇, provides a particularly clear experimental signature,
featuring an excellent signal-to-background ratio and full reconstruction of the final state enabled by
the high-precision lepton momentum measurements of the ATLAS detector. Background contributions
are small and can be precisely estimated by using data-driven techniques. As a result, this channel has
played a central role in precision measurements of Higgs boson properties by both the ATLAS and CMS
experiments using the full LHC Run 2 integrated luminosity of 140 fb−1. At a centre-of-mass energy of
√𝑠= 13 TeV, measurements of the Higgs boson production and differential cross-sections [7–13], and
derived constraints on couplings, as well as its mass [14–16], tensor structure [17, 18], and width [16,
19], have been performed. More recently, first measurements at √𝑠= 13.6 TeV have been reported by
ATLAS [20] and CMS [21].
This paper presents measurements of Higgs boson production using 𝐻→𝑍𝑍∗→4ℓdecay candidates
recorded during Run 3 with the ATLAS experiment in 𝑝𝑝collisions at √𝑠= 13.6 TeV between the
2022 and 2024 data-taking periods, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 164 fb−1. Inclusive and
differential cross-sections for eight observables sensitive to the production and decay kinematics of the
Higgs boson, are measured within a well-defined fiducial phase space. In addition, cross-sections per Higgs
boson production-mode are extracted using the Simplified Template cross-section (STXS) framework [22,
23].
The cross-sections are determined using binned likelihood fits to the reconstructed four-lepton invariant
mass spectrum for the inclusive and differential cross-sections measurement, and are corrected for detector
efficiencies and resolution effects through an unfolding procedure. Neural-network-based (NN) discriminant
distributions are used for the production-mode cross-sections measurements.
The measured cross-sections are compared with SM predictions and are further interpreted to constrain
possible anomalous Higgs boson couplings. In particular, the differential production cross-sections are
interpreted in the context of the Standard Model effective field theory (SMEFT), while the cross-sections per
production-mode are interpreted in terms of modified Higgs boson couplings to vector bosons. Constraints
on the Higgs boson trilinear self-coupling are derived by exploiting both the Higgs boson transverse
momentum spectra and the production-mode cross-sections. Furthermore, a statistical combination of
Run 2 and Run 3 data is performed in the context of the coupling-modifier interpretation, significantly
improving the precision of the constraints.
Compared with previous results in this analysis channel [8, 9, 20], the results presented in this paper benefit
from additional data collected in 2023 and 2024. A new transformer-based [24] machine learning (ML)
algorithm is used to identify jets from the hadronisation of b-quarks, providing substantial improvements in
background rejection [25] for measuring the top-quark-associated Higgs production-mode. A data-driven
fake-factor method [26] is used to estimate backgrounds with non-prompt leptons, such as those arising
from 𝑍+jets, 𝑡¯𝑡, and 𝑊𝑍processes. A new double-differential distribution of the transverse momentum
of the four-lepton system as function of the invariant mass of the sub-leading dilepton pair is measured
for the first time, owing to its high sensitivity to possible beyond-the-Standard-Model (BSM) effects in

2 ATLAS detector
The ATLAS experiment [27, 28] at the LHC is a multipurpose particle detector with a forward–backward
symmetric cylindrical geometry and a near 4𝜋coverage in solid angle.1 It consists of an inner tracking
detector (ID) surrounded by a thin superconducting solenoid providing a 2 T axial magnetic field,
electromagnetic and hadronic calorimeters, and a muon spectrometer. The inner tracking detector covers
the pseudorapidity range |𝜂| < 2.5. It consists of silicon pixel, silicon microstrip, and transition radiation
tracking (TRT) detectors. Lead/liquid-argon (LAr) sampling calorimeters provide electromagnetic (EM)
energy measurements with high granularity within the region |𝜂| < 3.2. A steel/scintillator-tile hadronic
calorimeter covers the central pseudorapidity range (|𝜂| < 1.7). The endcap and forward regions are
instrumented with LAr calorimeters for EM and hadronic energy measurements up to |𝜂| = 4.9. The
muon spectrometer (MS) comprises separate trigger and high-precision tracking chambers measuring the
deflection of muons in a magnetic field generated by the superconducting air-core toroidal magnets. The
field integral of the toroids ranges between 2.0 and 6.0 T m across most of the detector. Three layers of
precision chambers, each consisting of layers of monitored drift tubes, cover the region |𝜂| < 2.7, except in
the innermost layer of the end-cap region, where layers of small-strip thin-gap chambers and Micromegas
chambers both provide precision tracking in the region 1.3 < |𝜂| < 2.7. The muon trigger system covers

3 Signal and background simulation
3.1 Data
The measurements presented in this paper are performed using 𝑝𝑝collision data recorded with the ATLAS
detector between 2022 and 2024, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 164 fb−1. Data are selected
only if all detector components relevant for each analysis channel are in good operating condition. The
uncertainty in the integrated luminosity is 1.9% [31], following the methodology described in Ref. [32].
The average number of interactions per bunch crossing (pile-up), averaged over all colliding bunch pairs, is
about 42 in 2022, increases to about 51 in 2023, and reaches approximately 57 in 2024.
Events used in the measurement are recorded using a combination of single-lepton, dilepton, and trilepton
triggers [33, 34]. The electron triggers used for the 2023 and 2024 data-taking periods exploit the new
electromagnetic feature extractors deployed for LHC Run 3 [29]. The transverse momentum thresholds of
the single-lepton triggers are 24 GeV for muons and 26 GeV for electrons. The dielectron triggers require a
minimum transverse momentum of 17 GeV per electron, while the dimuon triggers require at least 14 GeV
per muon. Mixed lepton flavour 𝑒𝜇triggers combine these thresholds, and trilepton triggers allow for lower
thresholds in exchange for a higher lepton multiplicity. For simulated Higgs boson candidates produced via
the gluon–gluon fusion process, the resulting total trigger efficiency is 98.1% for signal events satisfying
the final selection described in Section 4.
3.2 Simulated event samples
This analysis uses simulated event samples to model the Higgs boson signal and all relevant background
processes, and to derive corrections for detector effects.
3.2.1 Signal modelling
Higgs boson production and decay are simulated for the primary production-modes: gluon–gluon fusion
(ggF), vector-boson fusion (VBF), associated production with a vector boson (𝑉𝐻, where 𝑉= 𝑊, 𝑍),
top- or bottom-associated production2 (𝑡¯𝑡𝐻and 𝑏¯𝑏𝐻), and associated production with a single top quark
(𝑡-channel for 𝑡𝐻and 𝑡𝐻𝑊)3. Hard-scattering matrix-elements (MEs) for the nominal signal samples are
generated with the Powheg Box v2 [35–37] Monte Carlo (MC) generator. Next-to-leading-order (NLO)
accuracy in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) is achieved for the ggF and 𝑉𝐻processes with up to one
additional parton by employing the MiNLO [38–40] approach. In a subsequent step, the ggF simulation
is improved to next-to-next-to-leading-order (NNLO) [41, 42] accuracy for inclusive observables by
reweighting the Higgs boson rapidity spectrum in Hj-MiNLO [38–40] to that obtained with HNNLO [43].
The VBF and 𝑡¯𝑡𝐻processes are simulated at NLO accuracy in QCD, while the 𝑏¯𝑏𝐻sample is generated
in the four-flavour scheme. The loop-induced 𝑔𝑔→𝑍𝐻process is generated at leading-order in QCD.

3.2.2 Signal normalisation and branching ratios
The signal samples are normalised to state-of-the-art theoretical cross-section predictions [52] interpolated
to a centre-of-mass energy of 13.6 TeV [53]. The ggF sample is normalised to a N3LO QCD calculation
including NLO EW corrections [54–65]. The VBF and 𝑉𝐻samples are normalised to an approximate
NNLO QCD cross-section with NLO EW corrections [66–76]. The 𝑏¯𝑏𝐻contribution is normalised using
a calculation combines NLO contributions with the resummation of collinear logarithms up to NNLO [77,
78], and are cross-checked using an alternative matching scheme [79, 80]. The 𝑡¯𝑡𝐻cross-section is
taken from a calculation accurate to NLO in QCD with NLO EW corrections [81–84]. 𝑡𝐻and 𝑡𝐻𝑊are
normalized to cross-sections that are accurate to NLO in QCD [85, 86].
The Standard Model prediction for the 𝐻→𝑍𝑍∗→4ℓ(ℓ= 𝑒, 𝜇) branching ratio is obtained from
Prophecy4f [87, 88]. This prediction includes the full NLO EW corrections and interference effects
that lead to a branching ratio approximately 10% higher for same-flavour final states (4𝜇and 4𝑒) than for
different-flavour final states (2𝑒2𝜇and 2𝜇2𝑒). The predicted SM branching ratio is (1.24 ± 0.03) × 10−4
for a Higgs boson mass of 125.09 GeV.

3.2.4 Detector simulation and pile-up modelling
All generated signal and background events are passed through a full simulation of the ATLAS detector
based on Geant4 [104, 105]. Scale factors are applied to the simulation to match the performance of
algorithms used to reconstruct physics objects to the one observed in data.
The effects of multiple interactions in the same and neighbouring bunch crossings (pile-up) are modelled
by overlaying simulated hard-scattering events with inelastic 𝑝𝑝collisions generated using a mixture of
Epos 2.0.1.4 [106] and Pythia 8.308 [49]. The Epos events are generated with the EPOS LHC tune [107],
while the Pythia events use the A3 tune [108] and the NNPDF2.3lo PDF set. The Pythia pile-up events
include either a high-𝑝T jet, a prompt photon, or a lepton from a 𝑏-hadron decay, whereas Epos is used
to model the remaining pile-up activity. The individual samples are first reweighted to ensure a smooth
transition across jet 𝑝T, and the combined pile-up distribution is subsequently reweighted to match the
distribution of the average number of interactions per bunch crossing observed in data.
4 Event selection
Higgs boson candidates are formed by selecting quadruplets of reconstructed and identified charged leptons
(electrons and muons). The selection procedure follows that of Ref. [20] and is briefly summarised below.
4.1 Event reconstruction
Event reconstruction begins with the formation of tracks in the ID [109] from measurements in the
silicon detectors and the TRT, and in the MS from measurements in the muon chambers. ID tracks are
reconstructed for 𝑝T > 500 MeV and are used to form vertex candidates along the beam line [110, 111].
Candidate events are required to contain at least one vertex reconstructed from at least two ID tracks.
Among all reconstructed vertices in an event, the primary interaction vertex is defined as the one with the
largest summed 𝑝2
T of its associated tracks.
Electron candidates are reconstructed by matching electromagnetic calorimeter clusters to ID tracks [112].
A refit of the ID track, allowing for bremsstrahlung energy losses, is performed to improve the impact-
parameter6 resolution. Electron identification is based on a likelihood discriminant [113], using loose
criteria that combine track properties, electromagnetic shower-shape variables, and the quality of the
track–cluster matching. Electrons are required to satisfy 𝑝T > 7 GeV and |𝜂| < 2.47. The electron
energy is calibrated following the procedure described in Ref. [112]. Photons are reconstructed from
EM calorimeter signals exceeding noise thresholds using dynamic topological clustering, with additional
clusters included for photon conversion products when applicable [112]. The photon candidates are
calibrated [114] and identified by an algorithm that relies on the shape of the EM showers reconstructed in
the calorimeter [113].
Muon candidates are reconstructed in the pseudorapidity range |𝜂| < 2.5 by matching tracks reconstructed
in the MS to ID tracks. In the region |𝜂| < 0.1, where the MS is only partially instrumented, muons are
additionally reconstructed from ID tracks matched either to partial MS track segments or to calorimeter

4.2 Higgs boson candidate formation
Higgs boson candidates are formed from pairs of same-flavour, opposite-charge (SFOC) electron or muon
pairs. Each such combination, referred to as a quadruplet, is required to include the leptons that triggered
the event, as described in Section 3.1. The three highest-𝑝T leptons in the quadruplet are required to satisfy
𝑝T > 20, 15, and 10 GeV, respectively.
Further requirements are imposed on the invariant masses of the SFOC lepton pairs. For each quadruplet,
the lepton pair with invariant mass 𝑚12 closest to the 𝑍-boson mass [123] is defined as the leading pair,

as the subleading pair. On the selected SFOC lepton pairs, additional requirements are applied on the
angular separation between leptons, a 𝐽/𝜓veto, the lepton transverse impact-parameter significance
(|𝑑0|/𝜎(𝑑0)), and the quality of the four-lepton vertex. The four leptons are required to be fitted to a

5 Background estimation
5.1 Background process with prompt leptons
The dominant background contribution arises from continuum 𝑍𝑍∗production initiated by quarks or
gluons. Unlike the Higgs boson signal, the 𝑚4ℓspectrum of the 𝑍𝑍∗background does not exhibit a resonant
structure near the Higgs mass, 𝑚𝐻. This feature is exploited to constrain the normalisation of the 𝑍𝑍∗
background directly from data.
The shapes of the 𝑚4ℓtemplates and the differential distributions for the 𝑍𝑍∗background are taken from
the simulation described in Section 3. The relative contributions of the individual 𝑍𝑍∗sub-processes are
fixed to their simulated expectations, while the overall normalisation is left free in the statistical model
through one or more scale factors defined relative to the MC-predicted yields. The binning of these scale
factors differs between the two measurements and is described below.
The 𝑍𝑍∗normalisation is constrained using signal-depleted sideband regions defined by 105 < 𝑚4ℓ<
115 GeV and 130 < 𝑚4ℓ< 160 GeV. These regions are representative of the continuum background
process under the Higgs boson peak and avoid the resonant 𝑍→4ℓprocess before 105 GeV and the fully
resonant 𝑍𝑍production after 160 GeV.
For the fiducial cross-section measurements, including differential observables, the full fit is performed
over the full mass range 105 < 𝑚4ℓ< 160 GeV. For differential measurements, the 𝑍𝑍∗normalisation
is determined independently in each bin of the measured observable. To reduce the impact of statistical
fluctuations in categories with low expected event yields, neighbouring bins are combined when extracting
the corresponding normalisation parameters. For the production-mode cross-section measurements in the
STXS framework, the low and high mass sideband regions are merged together in 𝑚4ℓbut are further
categorised according to the reconstructed jet multiplicity (𝑁jets = 0, 1, and ≥2) to reduce theoretical
assumption on extrapolating results between different regions. Signal-region categories with the same jet
multiplicity are fitted simultaneously with their corresponding sideband regions, using a common 𝑍𝑍∗
normalisation parameter for each jet-multiplicity category.
This strategy substantially reduces theoretical uncertainties in the 𝑍𝑍∗background prediction for both
measurements, as the statistical model primarily relies on data to constrain the normalisations, leaving only
the relative normalisation between the signal and sideband regions and the modelling of the differential
shapes dependent on theoretical assumptions.
Top-quark-associated background processes are treated differently in the two measurement strategies. In
the fiducial and differential cross-section measurements, their contribution is small and is therefore taken
directly from simulation. In the production-mode cross-section measurements analysis, where sensitivity to
𝑡¯𝑡𝐻production is enhanced, a dedicated control region is included in the fit to constrain the normalisation
of these processes. A single normalisation parameter is determined in a simultaneous fit to the 𝑡¯𝑡𝐻signal
regions and the control region, defined following the Run 2 analysis strategy described in Ref. [8]. In this
control region, the upper bound of the 𝑚4ℓselection is extended to 350 GeV to improve the statistical
precision of the constraint, while avoiding the fully resonant top-associated production.
Contributions from triboson production are taken directly from simulation in all regions considered.

6 Analysis overview
The analysis performs two complementary measurements of the Higgs boson cross-section in the
𝐻→𝑍𝑍∗→4ℓdecay channel. In the first approach, inclusive and differential cross-sections are measured
in a fiducial phase space designed to closely mirror the detector-level selection, thereby minimising
extrapolation and reducing the dependence on theoretical assumptions. In this measurement, the relative
contributions of the different Higgs boson production-modes are fixed to the Standard Model expectation
when deriving acceptance and migration corrections. Signal yields are extracted from maximum-likelihood
fits to the reconstructed 𝑚4ℓspectrum, and detector effects are corrected using a likelihood-based matrix
unfolding procedure [9] to obtain particle-level results that are as model independent as possible and
sensitive to deviations from the Standard Model.
The second, complementary approach aims to measure the underlying Higgs boson production mechanisms.
Production-mode cross-sections are measured using event categories and multivariate discriminants
optimised for separation of ggF, VBF, 𝑉𝐻, and 𝑡¯𝑡𝐻contributions, and are interpreted within the STXS
framework. The STXS approach provides a common set of phase-space regions that enables increasingly
granular measurements, enhancing sensitivity to differences between production kinematics and potential
BSM effects.
The following sections describe these two strategies in detail, first presenting the fiducial inclusive and
differential cross-section measurement and then the production-mode measurement in the STXS framework.
Although both approaches provide an inclusive Higgs boson production cross-section, they do so using
complementary combinations and are impacted by different model dependences. In the fiducial analysis, the
inclusive cross-section is obtained by combining measurements performed separately in the four decay final
states, and depends on the modelling of the relative effects between the 4𝜇, 2𝑒2𝜇, 2𝜇2𝑒, and 4𝑒channels.
In the STXS measurement, the inclusive cross-section is obtained by summing the fitted production-mode
contributions across the reconstructed categories, and is sensitive to the modelling of the relative signal
composition across jet-multiplicity regions and categories.

6.2.3 Multivariate discriminants and Signal Extraction
In the STXS measurement, several Higgs boson production mechanisms contribute to each reconstructed
event category, together with multiple background processes. To enhance the separation power beyond the
cut-based categorisation, multivariate discriminants are constructed using neural networks (NNs) based

on a Set Transformer architecture [129]. The networks are trained independently in each reconstructed
category on simulated samples of the relevant Higgs-boson production modes and dominant non-Higgs-
boson backgrounds, and are optimised to classify events according to their most likely signal-production
or background process. The NN inputs are organised into event-level global observables and sets of
reconstructed physics objects. Lepton and jet information is provided as variable-length sets to Set
Transformer blocks, which provide permutation-invariant processing through attention mechanisms [24],
while global variables are combined with the learned set representations in a classifier head. Each of the
NN output nodes is labelled as NN𝑃, where 𝑃refers to one or multiple targeted processes, and is related to
the probability of an event originating from that process. The specific feature choices, the corresponding
training targets and the output nodes follow the strategy outlined in Table 3. Good agreement between data
and simulation is observed for the input variables used in the NN training, as illustrated in Figure 10. In
addition, validation studies are performed in 𝑍𝑍∗-enriched control regions to verify that the NN outputs
are well modelled and that no overtraining or simulation-specific features are learned. In categories with
limited statistical power, no NN discriminant is applied and only the observed yield enters the fit.

6.3 Statistical method
All measurements are performed with a binned profile-likelihood-ratio fit [132]. The likelihood is built as
product of Poisson terms for the fitted discriminant distributions in each differential bin or STXS category,
and includes nuisance parameters that account for experimental and theoretical systematic uncertainties
affecting the normalisation and shapes of the signal and background templates (Section 7). The parameters
of interest are the cross-sections and the background normalisation factor introduced in Sections 6.1
and 6.2 and are extracted by minimising −2 ln 𝜆, where 𝜆is the profile likelihood ratio. The asymptotic
approximation [132] is validated with pseudo-experiments; where fewer than three events are observed in a
bin, the quoted uncertainties are corrected to those obtained from pseudo-experiments.
The compatibility of a measured cross-section and its theoretical prediction is tested by computing a
𝑝-value based on the difference between the value of −2 ln 𝜆at the best-fit value and the value obtained
by fixing the cross-section in each bin to that predicted by SM theory. These 𝑝-values do not include the
uncertainties in the theoretical predictions.

7 Systematic uncertainties
The dominant uncertainty in the measurement arises from statistical uncertainty. In addition, many
systematic uncertainties are considered. Experimental uncertainties arise from object reconstruction,
identification, isolation, energy and momentum scale, resolution, and trigger efficiencies, the determination
of the integrated luminosity and the data-driven background estimate. Additional theoretical uncertainties
are related to the modelling of the signal and background processes.
7.1 Experimental uncertainties
The uncertainty in the integrated luminosity is 1.9% [31]. This affects the signal and background yields
predicted by simulation if no normalisation to data is used. The uncertainty due to pile-up modelling are
below 2% in the entire 𝑚4ℓrange considered.
The reconstruction and identification efficiencies for muons and electrons are determined using the methods
described in Refs. [112, 115]. For muons, these have associated uncertainties between 0.1% and 1.3%.
For electrons with 𝑝T < 15 GeV conservative uncertainties up to 17% are included to cover the change
19


## Page 20


in detector conditions between Runs 2 and 3 and updates to the simulation. The isolation efficiency
uncertainty is approximately 1% for electrons and 0.5 −2% for muons.
Lepton energy and momentum scale and resolution are corrected to describe the data using the procedures
documented in Refs. [114, 116], and the associated uncertainties have an impact at the most of 1 −1.5%
on the measured cross-sections.
Jet energy scale and resolution are calibrated using the procedure described in Ref. [119]. An additional
uncertainty is assigned to jets in the kinematic region of |𝜂| > 3.2 to account for an observed mismodelling
on the jet energy calibration in the forward calorimeters.
Uncertainties in the 𝑏-jet identification efficiency are taken from 2022 and 2023 data and applied also
to 2024 data and conservatively increased by ten times to reflect possible differences between different
data-taking periods. This is done only in the 𝑡¯𝑡𝐻category and 𝑡𝑋𝑋sideband, where 𝑏-jets are required
and the impact is at the level of 1%.
Uncertainties in the lepton trigger efficiency have a negligible impact on the analysis, as does the uncertainty
on the measured Higgs boson mass of 𝑚𝐻= 125.09 ± 0.24 GeV [14].
For the data-driven measurement of the reducible background, several sources of uncertainty are considered.
The dominant contribution arises from the statistical uncertainty in the region containing a quadruplet with
at least one baseline lepton failing to meet the tight requirements, where the fake factors are applied. An
additional significant contribution comes from the systematic uncertainty associated with the choice of
control regions used to derive the fake factors. This uncertainty reflects the variation of the result when the
fake factors are extracted using only CR𝑍or only CRtop, instead of their nominal combination, as described
in Section 5. Those uncertainties have an impact that ranges from 1% to 9% both in the differential and in
the production cross-section measurements. The statistical uncertainty due to the limited statistics in the
CRs is also propagated to the fake factor estimate and has a negligible impact on the final yields. Finally,
the uncertainty in the 𝑊𝑍is estimated by varying the fitted normalization within its statistical error and
has negligible impact on the estimate.
7.2 Theoretical uncertainties
Uncertainties in the signal prediction due to missing higher-order QCD effects are estimated by using the
scheme described in Ref. [133]. Parton shower and multiple-parton interaction modelling uncertainties
on the acceptance are estimated by using a prediction based on Herwig7 as alternative parton shower
generator and using tune eigenvector variations within the nominal parton shower generator tune, A14.
Uncertainties related to the PDF set are evaluated using 40 eigenvector variations of the PDF4LHC21 PDF
set. An additional uncertainty of the 𝐻→𝑍𝑍∗→4ℓdecay branching ratio of 2.2% [87, 134] is assigned
when extrapolating to the total phase-space. These theory-related uncertainties enter the measurements in
different ways. These uncertainties generally have small effects on both cross-section measurements, mainly
through acceptance and migration. In contrast, for the global strength measurement and interpretation of
the results, the parameters of interest (𝜇) are defined relative to the SM predictions. In this case the same
modelling uncertainties propagate directly to the predicted signal yields and have a larger impact.
Uncertainties in the prediction of the 𝑍𝑍∗background from missing higher-order QCD effects are taken
into account by varying the factorisation and renormalisation scales in the calculation by factors of two
and choosing the configuration with the largest impact. PDF-related uncertainties are evaluated using the
various PDFs within the NNPDF3.0 PDF set using the method outlined in [99]. A further modelling
20


## Page 21


uncertainty is assigned by comparing the prediction from the nominal Sherpa sample to a simulation in
MadGraph5_aMC@NLO matched to Pythia 8 with the FxFx prescription [44, 50]. As this background
contribution is normalised to data, only the change in differential shapes impacts the measurements (1−3%),
while the effect in the shapes of the NN discriminants and in the relative contribution of this background
between the sidebands and the signal regions for the STXS measurement is negligible. For the tXX process,
uncertainties due to PDF and missing higher-order QCD effects are estimated from simulation using a
similar procedure as the 𝑍𝑍∗process.
Finally, unfolding-related systematic uncertainties for the inclusive and differential cross-section meas-
urements arise from uncertainties in the production-mode composition that affect the response matrices.
Additional uncertainties come from a potential bias introduced by the unfolding method. This bias is
evaluated independently in each bin by comparing the unfolded cross-section in simulation with the
expected result when the underlying true cross-sections of the simulated sample are varied within their
expected statistical uncertainty. The impact of these uncertainties is negligible.

9.1 Constraints on Higgs boson couplings within the 𝜿-framework
To probe physics beyond the SM, the measured production-mode cross-sections are interpreted in the 𝜅
framework [135], which parameterises possible deviations of Higgs boson couplings from their SM values
using coupling-strength modifiers ®𝜅. Under the narrow-width approximation, Higgs production and decay
factorise, and 𝜎· B can be expressed in terms of modified Higgs production cross-sections and partial
widths. The ®𝜅dependence at √𝑠= 13.6 TeV is obtained by linear interpolation between the corresponding
terms at 13 and 14 TeV [41].
The interpretation is performed in a benchmark scenario with two universal modifiers: 𝜅𝑉= 𝜅𝑊= 𝜅𝑍
for Higgs-to-vector-boson couplings and 𝜅𝑓= 𝜅𝑡= 𝜅𝑏= 𝜅𝑐= 𝜅𝜏= 𝜅𝜇for Higgs-to-fermion couplings,
assuming a single Higgs boson state that is even under the combined charge-parity (CP) transformation,
with the SM tensor structure and no undetected or invisible decays. For loop-induced processes, the
resolved treatment is adopted, in which the effective loop couplings are expressed in terms of the modified
couplings to the massive SM particles.
The results are statistically combined with the corresponding measurements performed with the full Run 2
data set [8], accounting for the systematic uncertainties described in Section 7. The following experimental
uncertainties are treated as correlated between Run 2 and Run 3 results: the muon momentum scale
and reconstruction efficiency; the electron energy scale and energy resolution; and the jet energy scale
and resolution, except for Run 3-specific components. Other experimental uncertainties are treated as
uncorrelated. Theoretical uncertainties from missing higher-order corrections affecting the Higgs boson
signal cross-sections in exclusive kinematic regions, the Higgs boson mass uncertainty, and uncertainties
in background estimates are treated as correlated. All other theoretical uncertainties, including PDF and
parton shower uncertainties, are treated as uncorrelated.

10 Conclusion
A measurement of inclusive and differential Higgs boson production cross-sections in the 𝐻→𝑍𝑍∗→4ℓ
channel is presented using 164 fb−1 of √𝑠= 13.6 TeV 𝑝𝑝collision data recorded by the ATLAS detector at
the LHC between 2022 and 2024. These results constitute the most comprehensive ATLAS measurements
of Higgs boson production in the 𝐻→𝑍𝑍∗→4ℓchannel at √𝑠= 13.6 TeV to date, with a precision
already comparable to that obtained using the full Run 2 dataset at √𝑠= 13 TeV. The analysis exploits the
data using improved analysis and ML techniques in two complementary frameworks, measuring fiducial
cross-sections with minimal theoretical assumptions and production-mode and STXS cross-sections to

separate the contributions from different Higgs boson production mechanisms. The inclusive fiducial
cross-section is measured to be 𝜎fid = 3.65+0.35
−0.33 fb , in agreement with the Standard Model prediction
of 𝜎SM
fid
= 3.68 ± 0.17 fb, and is extrapolated to a total cross-section of 𝜎total = 59.3 ± 5.7 pb , also
consistent with the Standard Model and previous results. Differential cross-sections are measured as a
function of several Higgs boson production and decay observables and are found to be in agreement with
state-of-the-art SM predictions. Higgs boson candidate events are categorised into regions sensitive to
different production mechanisms and are used to measure production-mode cross-sections and a Reduced
STXS Stage 1.2 scheme. A product of inclusive production cross-section and branching ratio into 𝑍𝑍∗of
𝜎|𝑦𝐻|<2.5 · B (𝐻→𝑍𝑍∗) = 1.42 ± 0.16 pb is obtained for Higgs boson rapidities below 2.5, consistent
with the Standard Model prediction of 𝜎SM
|𝑦𝐻|<2.5 · BSM (𝐻→𝑍𝑍∗) = 1.44 ± 0.08 pb. The precision of
both the differential and STXS measurements is comparable to that achieved by ATLAS at √𝑠= 13 TeV
with Run 2 data [8, 9], and improves by approximately a factor of two relative to the first ATLAS
results at √𝑠= 13.6 TeV in this channel [20]. The differential measurements as a function of 𝜙and

on 𝜅𝑉(𝜅𝑓) limits. Limits are also derived on the trilinear Higgs self-coupling, and are consistent with the
