Minimizing the Expected Cost of Synchronization in Lossless
Power Networks
Gerald Ogbonna, Student Member, IEEE, David Bindel, Senior Member, C. L. Anderson, Senior Member
Abstract—The reliable operation of large-scale electric power
networks is increasingly challenging, particularly with the inte-
gration of stochastic renewable generation. In this work, we ad-
dress the problem of minimizing network transients by optimally
modifying the underlying network. We formulate the problem in
terms of graph Laplacian matrices and show that, under certain
assumptions, the problem is convex. We derive a linear matrix
inequality whose feasibility guarantees the existence and unique-
ness of phase cohesive steady-state angles; this condition can be
directly incorporated as a convex constraint in the optimization
framework and we provide several geometric interpretations of
the optimization problem. The proposed method is validated on
the IEEE 30-bus test system, where results demonstrate that
our approach effectively identifies critical links on the network.
Dynamic simulations show a significant reduction in network
transients and overall improvements across several performance
metrics. We explore the sparsity–optimality trade-off using a
reweighted ℓ1 heuristic.
Index Terms—Electrical power systems, graph theory, convex
optimization, coupled oscillators, synchronization.
I. INTRODUCTION
T
HE electric power system is currently undergoing rapid
transformation with the increasing integration of renew-
able generation. It is well known that reliable power system
operation depends strongly on the synchronization of multiple
oscillatory state variables to a nominal grid frequency [1],
[2]. The stochasticity of renewables poses new challenges to
the ability of the power network to remain synchronized and
return to synchrony following a perturbation from steady-state.
[3] showed that the number of frequency incidents per month
on the Nordic system is strongly correlated with the share
of generation from renewables, highlighting the need for fast
acting controllers and the design of resilience/robust networks.
Designing resilient networks provides several benefits to sys-
tem operations including ensuring that the system returns to
synchrony following a disturbance with minimal control effort
(i.e. reduce control burden on local controllers), improved ref-
erence tracking, and reduce equipment and cascaded failures
due to large deviations in the system frequency.
Previous studies [4]–[6], have considered the role of net-
work connectivity on the transient stability of power networks.

In [4], [7], the authors observe that geographically distributed
frequency oscillations (inter-area oscillations) are often the
result of weak global coupling. Similarly, Low et al. [8]
suggest that increasing network connectivity increases system
robustness against disturbances of relatively low frequency.
Overall, there’s some consensus in the literature that improving
network connectivity increases the overall system robustness
to disturbances, which motivates this work.
The problem of designing robust networks has been a
focus of recent research efforts [9]–[11]. To this end, several
measures of network robustness [12], [13] have been defined
in the literature for different classes of dynamical systems on
graphs. For networks in which all nodes have identical second-
order Kuramoto oscillators, Tyloo et al. [14] provide robust-
ness measures defined in terms of the generalized effective
resistance of the underlying graph. Specifically, they show a
strong correlation between their measures and the L2 norm of
the phase and frequency trajectories of the network dynamics
under perturbations following an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.
This measure is minimized in the work by Nagpal et al [10].
The use of effective resistance as a measure of network
connectivity (graph connectivity/network robustness) dates to
early works by Klein and Randic [15]. Boyd et al. [16]
provide a more recent review of various applications of
effective resistance to graph theoretic problems. Applications
of effective resistance in network analysis include growing
linear consensus networks [17], community detection [18],
power redispatch and network synchronization [19], improving
robustness under random time-varying topological network
changes [20], and designing fast mixing Markov processes
[21].
In this paper, we investigate the problem of designing robust
power networks by optimizing the structure and connectivity
of the transmission system. We model the power grid using
a coupled-oscillator framework connected over an undirected
graph and formulate optimization problems to improve the
network’s dynamic response to disturbances. The changes to
the transmission network proposed in this work may be im-
plemented through flexible AC transmission systems (FACTS)
devices [22], which allow the electrical characteristics of trans-
mission lines to be adjusted, or through strategic expansion of
certain transmission corridors. To address this problem, we
develop an optimization framework for network design that
connects the transient dynamics of the power system to the
underlying structure. The main contributions of this work are
as follows:
• We extend the results in [1] to show that the expected
L2 norm of the transient component of the angular
frequencies near a power flow solution is a function of

introduces notations, Kron reduction and effective resistance,
and the power system model. In Section III we derive a
measure of the dynamic performance of the network in terms
of effective resistance. In Section IV, we formulate several
convex optimization problems that first minimize the defined
measure, second incorporate synchronization constraints, and
finally employ a reweighted ℓ1 algorithm to extract sparse
solutions. Section V presents numerical results, and Section
VI concludes the paper.

D. Sparsity/Cardinality Minimization Heuristic
Implementing the solution x⋆incurs a real-world cost,
as modifying transmission line susceptances on a subset of
critical edges is less expensive than modifying all edges simul-
taneously. Identifying sparse solutions is therefore equivalent
to solving an optimal asset deployment problem, in which the
goal is to allocate budget α to the fewest, most impactful
edges. Sparse solutions become particularly important in cases
where the solution to the problem P2 results in a significant
number of small non-zero entries in the design vector x.

VI. CONCLUSION
In this work, we have shown that for a lossless power
network of identical generators, the expected value of the L2
norm of the angular frequency dynamics near a stationary
point can be decomposed into a transient component that is a
function of the effective resistance of the underlying graph and
a system-wide component that evolves in the nullspace of the
reduced Laplacian (in s-domain), which is completely inde-
pendent of the network. This decomposition reveals the extent
to which we can improve synchronization by modifying the
network and highlights the primary factors influencing network
transients. Most importantly, since the reduced Laplacian is
always singular, there’s a limit to the improvement in transient
response that can be achieved by modifying the network. We
showed that under typical assumptions on the power network,
the problem of minimizing the transient component of the
frequency response by modifying the susceptances of the net-

work is a convex problem and provide semidefinite programs
for solving it. We further provide a sufficient condition, in
terms of the algebraic connectivity λ2, for the existence and
uniqueness of a synchronized solution satisfying a specified
steady-state phase angle requirement. A convex constraint, in
the form of a linear matrix inequality, is also derived, whose
feasibility guarantees that these requirements are met for a set
of net power injections. We note that our sufficient condition
might be conservative, and could be improved in future work.
We provide several geometric interpretations of the solution
to the SDPs from a graph-theoretic and linear algebraic
perspective. Specifically, the problems can be viewed as
eigenvalue problems on the space of symmetric matrices: the
objective function is the sum of the eigenvalues of the Moore-
Penrose pseudoinverse of the Laplacian matrix of a Kron-
reduced graph, while the linear matrix inequality ensuring
steady-state phase angle cohesion enforces a constraint on the
second smallest eigenvalue of the Laplacian of the underlying
graph. We validate our proposed method on the IEEE 30-bus
test system. Results for randomly generated exogenous inputs
show that significant improvements in transient response can
be achieved by optimizing the susceptances of the transmission
network.
Finally, we explore optimality-sparsity trade offs for the
optimization problem with and without the synchronization
constraints using the reweighted ℓ1 framework. Our results
show that an explicit constraint on the algebraic connectivity
(required to ensure steady-state phase cohesion) can limit
further improvements to the sparsity of the desired solution.
