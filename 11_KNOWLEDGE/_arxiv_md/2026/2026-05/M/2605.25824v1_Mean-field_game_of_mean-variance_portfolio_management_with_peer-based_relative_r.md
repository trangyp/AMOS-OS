Abstract
This paper investigates a mean-field game (MFG) problem for mean-variance (MV)
portfolio management, highlighting a new type of relative performance encoded by the
peer-based risk aversion. Specifically, the risk aversion is formulated as a piecewise form
that depends on whether the individual’s wealth is above or below the population average.
Due to the inherent time-inconsistency in the MV criterion, together with the piecewise
risk aversion, we encounter a class of time-inconsistent MFG, new to the literature. Our
goal is to seek a mean-field equilibrium, characterized by a forward-backward stochastic
differential equation (FBSDE) system and a mean-field consistency condition. The new
challenge stems from the discontinuous coefficients induced by the piecewise risk aversion.
In response, we first propose a smooth regularization technique and obtain the existence
of the equilibrium in the intra-personal game for the representative agent by establishing
the solution to the discontinuous multi-dimensional FBSDE. Next, by invoking fixed-point
arguments and convergence analysis as smoothing regularization vanishes, we conclude
the existence of the mean-field equilibrium in the time-inconsistent MFG.
Keywords: Time-inconsistent MFG, MV portfolio, relative risk aversion, FBSDE with
discontinuous coefficients, smooth regularization, mean-field equilibrium

The shift of focus on relative performance from the EU framework to the mean-variance
(MV) criterion occurs recently. Guan and Hu (2022) pioneered the integration of an MV

and reinsurance. Subsequently, Guan, Hu, and Liang (2025) investigated competition among

In the aforementioned problem formulations based on EU and MV, relative performance
concerns are typically modeled by directly incorporating the relative wealth, such as Xi(T)−
¯X(T), into the agent’s objective functional, where Xi(T) denotes the agent’s individual wealth
and ¯X(T) represents the average wealth of peers. By contrast, we adopt a distinct, behav-
iorally motivated approach: relative performance does not enter the payoff directly, but in-
stead influences the agent’s risk aversion attitude. This perspective is motivated by prospect
theory and its advancement, cumulative prospect theory (CPT), pioneered by Kahneman and
Tversky (1979) and Tversky and Kahneman (1992), which posits that decision-makers eval-
uate outcomes relative to a reference point and exhibit asymmetric risk preferences on either
side of this threshold. Jin and Zhou (2008) investigated the behavioral portfolio selection
problem in a continuous-time setting, while He and Zhou (2011) carried out an analytical
treatment of a single-period portfolio choice model featuring reference-dependent, S-shaped
utility under CPT. Furthermore, highlighting the binary nature of psychological transitions,
K˝oszegi and Rabin (2006, 2007) formalized how individuals abruptly switch between distinct
evaluative mindsets when crossing a reference point. Motivated by these insights, our model
assumes that the risk parameter is modelled by a piecewise form that depends on the com-

This formulation introduces two essential features: time-inconsistency and mean-field in-
teractions. First, for a fixed m, it is well known that the variance term in the objective
functional (1.1) violates the iterated expectation property, which makes the dynamic opti-
mization problem time-inconsistent. In addition, the dependence of γ on the initial state
further contributes to this time-inconsistency. To address the issue of time-inconsistency, we
adopt the consistent planning (intra-personal equilibrium) approach, originally proposed by
Strotz (1955), which interprets the problem as a sequential intra-personal game among the
decision-maker’s temporal selves. This idea was first formulated in continuous time by Eke-
land and Lazrak (2010) (a previous version of this paper: Ekeland and Lazrak (2006)). In the
context of the MV problem, Basak and Chabakauri (2010) was the first to characterize the
corresponding intra-personal equilibrium. Subsequent extensions include the study of state-
dependent risk aversion in Bj¨ork, Murgoci, and Zhou (2014), as well as time-inconsistent
linear–quadratic (LQ) control problems in Hu, Jin, and Zhou (2012, 2017). In other general
continuous-time problems exhibiting time-inconsistency, Bj¨ork, Khapko, and Murgoci (2017),
Yan and Yong (2019), He and Jiang (2021), and Cao, Liang, Wang, and Yu (2025) further
investigated the existence and structural properties of equilibrium controls.
Second, the dependency of the risk aversion on m necessitates analyzing the equilibrium
strategy in the face of competition within the large population, leading to a new type of
MFG when the mean-field interaction occurs in the risk aversion coefficient. This theory
was independently initiated by Lasry and Lions (2007) and Huang, Malham´e, and Caines
(2006), with general mathematical and probabilistic frameworks expanded by Bensoussan,

Contrary to previous studies in either time-inconsistent control problems or MFG prob-
lems, our formulation poses a new technical challenge by its state-dependent piecewise risk
aversion parameter. To the best of our knowledge, this work appears as the first study to
handle the time-inconsistent MFG with discontinuous coefficients. In both the step of charac-
terizing the intra-personal equilibrium for a fixed mean-field aggregation and the step of seek-
ing the fixed point for the mean-field consistency m (i.e., m∗(t) = E[X ¯π∗(t)], see Definition
2.2 for details), we have to develop some non-standard mathematical techniques tailor-made
for discontinuous coefficients. To navigate these hurdles, we first establish a verification the-
orem that characterizes the intra-personal equilibrium via an associated forward-backward
stochastic differential equation (FBSDE) (3.3).
Building upon this characterization, our
methodology proceeds in two main steps:
• Step 1:
Intra-personal Equilibrium for a Fixed Mean-Field Aggregation.
The first step is to solve the FBSDE associated with the intra-personal equilibrium
for a representative agent. Different methods have been developed to address the FB-
SDE in the literature. For instance, Ma, Protter, and Yong (1994) and Delarue (2002)
established partial differential equation (PDE)-based methods such as the well-known
four-step scheme, and Ma, Wu, Zhang, and Zhang (2015) developed a unified decou-
pling field approach. However, these methods often require the Lipschitz continuity
conditions. Alternatively, Hu and Peng (1995) introduced the method of continuation
to relax some regularity requirements, which however imposes a strict monotonicity con-
dition that our formulation fails to satisfy. Indeed, due to the piecewise risk aversion,
the main theoretical challenge in our setting arises from the discontinuous coefficients
of the FBSDE. To surmount this hurdle, we first employ a mollification technique to
construct regularized quasi-linear parabolic PDEs and obtain their smooth solutions.
By deriving energy estimates, we establish the compactness of these regularized solu-
tions and subsequently extract a strong solution to the original PDE with discontinuous
coefficients. Furthermore, we leverage this strong solution with Krylov’s estimates to
get the existence of solution to the FBSDE.
• Step 2: Mean-Field Consistency and Equilibrium. We then seek a fixed point

consistency in the original MFG problem.
In summary, there are three main contributions of the present work, conceptually and
mathematically resolving the intertwined challenges of time-inconsistency, discontinuous co-
efficients, and mean-field consistency. First, from a modeling perspective, we incorporate
a prospect theory-style risk preference to capture peer-based relative risk aversion, thereby
formulating a class of time-inconsistent MFGs. Second, from a methodological standpoint,
we develop a smooth regularization approach to solve the associated FBSDE, where disconti-
nuities arise in the drift coefficients of both the forward and backward equations, as well as in
the diffusion coefficient of the forward equation. Third, we successfully establish the mean-
field equilibrium in the original system with discontinuous coefficients by first proving the
existence of equilibrium for the regularized MFG and subsequently deploying a delicate prob-
abilistic convergence analysis to verify that the limit also fulfills the mean-field consistency
condition when smoothing regularization vanishes.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 formulates the model,
introduces the peer-based risk aversion to depict the relative performance, and defines the
mean-field equilibrium. Section 3 tackles the existence of intra-personal equilibrium for a rep-
resentative agent by analyzing the associated FBSDE with discontinuous coefficients using
the smoothing regularization techniques. Section 4 further establishes the mean-field consis-
tency by utilizing the convergence analysis as smoothing effect vanishes, thereby confirming
the existence of mean-field equilibrium in the original time-inconsistent MFG. Some proofs
of auxiliary results are collected in Appendix.
