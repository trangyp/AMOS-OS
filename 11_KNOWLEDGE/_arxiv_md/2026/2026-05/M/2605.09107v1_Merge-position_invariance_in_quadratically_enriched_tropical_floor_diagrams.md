expression.

1. Introduction
1.1. Quadratically enriched tropical counts. Classical enumerative geometry counts com-
plex rational curves of a fixed toric degree through the expected number of point conditions.
For a smooth toric del Pezzo polygon ∆, the resulting genus 0 invariant is denoted N∆. Over R
the unsigned real count is not invariant, but Welschinger [8, 9] proved that a signed count W∆,s
is invariant when one imposes real point conditions together with s pairs of complex conjugate
conditions.

For a smooth toric del Pezzo polygon this is the expected number of point conditions for rational
curves of degree ∆, and it is the notation used in [2].
The floor-diagram formula starts from a vertically ordered configuration of r +2s simple point
conditions and then merges s adjacent pairs into double point conditions. The data of which
adjacent pairs are merged are the merge positions. The algebraic count on the left of Theorem 1.1
is independent of such auxiliary choices. In the floor-diagram product formula this invariance is
hidden by the local factors: changing a merge position changes which vertices carry the special
type-A factors involving the parameters dj.
The abstract independence of the resulting floor count is a formal consequence, in the range
of the JMPR correspondence, of the equality with the algebraic enriched count and the algebraic
invariance recalled in [2, Thm. 2.19 and Def. 2.20]. The present paper studies the same invariance
inside the floor-diagram expression. The main result is a tropical wall-crossing mechanism for
the floor formula: it gives a complete tropical proof of merge-position invariance over every

single algebraic specialisation used in the general case. It makes the known invariance visible in
the formula itself and isolates the precise residual mod-2 parity statement that would make the

the real signed setting, the tropical Welschinger count with conjugate point conditions requires
the broccoli count and its bridge comparison with Welschinger curves [1, §§3–5]. The global real
invariance statement quoted below is the scalar input used in the proof.

1.4. The real broccoli input. We recall the real input in the form needed later. Broccoli curves
were introduced in [1] to give a tropical proof of the invariance of real Welschinger numbers with

tropical count. The broccoli count is locally invariant under the relevant tropical wall-crossings

Lemma 3.10 (Merge-position graph and dissolution). Encode a merge configuration by a subset
P = {p1 < · · · < ps} ⊂{1, . . . , r+2s−1} with pi+1 ≥pi+2, where pi records the merged adjacent

different labelled pair is dissolved, the two resulting configurations again differ by the same unit
shift in the problem with s −1 double points and r + 2 simple points. If the shifted pair itself is
dissolved, the two resulting configurations are identical.
Proof. For connectivity, slide the leftmost pair until it starts at position 1, then slide the second
pair until it starts at position 3, and continue. At the i-th step the earlier pairs occupy starts
1, 3, . . . , 2i −3; if pi > 2i −1, then replacing pi by pi −1 still leaves distance at least 2 from the
previous start and only increases the distance from the next start. Thus every configuration is
connected to the canonical one {1, 3, . . . , 2s −1}.

Proposition 6.1 (Localisation). In (5), the extension dj appears only in the unique factor

Corollary 6.2 (dj-linearity and coefficient stability). The lifted multiplicity expression is multi-
affine in the variables ⟨d1⟩, . . . , ⟨ds⟩, with the relations ⟨dj⟩2 = ⟨1⟩. In particular, for each j there

8. Comparison with broccoli curves and an open direction
8.1. Scope of the reduction. Our argument reduces the positional invariance of the GW(k)-
valued tropical count to four universal inputs: broccoli invariance [1, Cor. 5.17], the elementary
Witt identity 2⟨a⟩= 2⟨2a⟩, the dissolution cascade, and a pointwise application of JMPR’s
algebraic invariance, Puiseux comparison, and correspondence [2, Thm. 2.19, Lem. 2.22, and
Thm. 1.2] over one iterated Laurent series field. The main novelty is the reduction itself : via the
cascade and the 2-torsion identification, the full field-independent invariance is reduced to a dis-
crete parity check on a single integer, which a single pointwise evaluation closes. All intermediate
algebra lives in the rank-3 universal ring GWuniv = Z[V4]/(⟨2⟩+ ⟨−2⟩−h).
The reduction still uses the real tropical invariance theorem of [1] as its scalar input. The
combinatorics of broccoli curves, the bridge construction, the local invariance at 4-valent walls,
and the matching of higher-valent moduli cones enter through that theorem. In the cascade, this
input determines the real signature relation n1 + n2 = 0, which is the scalar step leading to the
Pfister obstruction.

The natural next step is a residual bridge argument: construct the bridge graph whose vertices
are the problematic residual floor diagrams, orient its one-dimensional cells as in [1], and prove
that the residual multiplicity of Lemma 7.3 is balanced on every connected component. This
would prove Corollary 7.6 directly, and then Proposition 7.8 would close the theorem without
the pointwise algebraic specialisation used in Step 6.
8.2.2. A GW(k)-valued broccoli theory. A second natural direction, suggested by the structure
of our proof, is whether the broccoli construction itself can be refined to a GW(k)-valued theory.
Our proof shows that the quadratic enrichment is encoded, after Step 4, in the universal inte-
ger n1, and that the full wall-crossing difference collapses to a signed multiple of the 2-torsion
class ⟨⟨2, d1, . . . , ds⟩⟩.
This suggests looking at the quotient ring GW(k) := GW(k)/(h, ⟨1⟩−⟨2⟩) as the natural target
at which the wall-crossing vanishes identically. Modulo h and ⟨1⟩−⟨2⟩, our cascade already
yields ∆N ≡0, bypassing any parity argument. One might hope to define a GW(k)-valued
broccoli multiplicity whose local invariance in the style of [1, Thm. 3.6] could be proved directly
by analogy with the Welschinger case, thereby subsuming our argument and simultaneously
furnishing a purely tropical proof of the parity step.
We leave both programmes to future work.
