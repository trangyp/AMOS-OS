MAZUR MANIFOLDS AND SYMPLECTIC STRUCTURES
ALBERTO CAVALLO
Abstract. We use the Heegaard Floer homology cobordism maps to obstruct the existence of
a symplectic structure on the Akbulut-Kirby Mazur manifolds whose boundary is a Brieskorn
sphere Y among Σ(2, 3, 13), Σ(2, 5, 7) and Σ(3, 4, 5). Furthermore, we describe how our results
imply the existence of exotic pairs of simply connected 4-manifolds, with definite intersection
form, whose boundary is Y .
1. Introduction
In [7] we provide evidence for a positive answer to the generalized version of a conjecture of
Gompf; namely, that no Brieskorn sphere Y = Σ(a1, ..., an) arises as the boundary of a rational
homology ball symplectic filling. What we show in [7, Theorems 1.4 and 1.5] is that the contact
structure ξcan, usually called the Milnor fillable contact structure on Y , admits no such filling
except for some specific cases: the Briekorn spheres Σ(3, 4, 5), Σ(2, 5, 7) and Σ(2, 3, 6k + 1) when
k ⩾1. For the latter manifolds it is still unknown whether rational homology ball symplectic
fillings may exist or not. Note that it follows from our results, see [7, Proposition 1.7], that these
are the only canonically oriented Brieskorn spheres carrying exactly two tight structures, up to
isotopy, and with vanishing correction term.
The starting obstruction that we may look at is whether Y bounded a rational homology ball in
the first place. It is known from the literature that Σ(3, 4, 5), Σ(2, 5, 7), Σ(2, 3, 13) and Σ(2, 3, 25)
bound contractible Mazur manifolds, see [4, 13], while Σ(2, 3, 7) and Σ(2, 3, 19) both bound ra-
tional homology balls, but not an integral one because they have non-vanishing µ-invariant, see
[14, 5].

A Mazur manifold1 is a smooth 4-manifold X which can be decomposed as the union of only
one 0-handle and an equal number of 1- and 2-handles, so that its double D(X) := X ∪∂X −X is
the standard 4-sphere. In particular, the manifold X is contractible and has non-empty boundary.
Some of the first examples of Mazur manifolds were given by Akbulut and Kirby in [4], see Figure
2; this family includes the manifolds W −(m) with m = 2, 3, 4 which we are going to study in this
paper.

2. Tau-invariants of the knot γ−
Let us consider the Milnor fillable contact structure ξ on Y −(2, m−2) ∼= Y −(m) where m = 3, 4,
and then Y −(m) is either Σ(2, 5, 7) or Σ(3, 4, 5). We want to study the invariant τξ(γ−) defined

In general Hedden’s invariant requires the knot to be oriented, but reversing the orientation
acts in the same way as conjugating the structure: in other words, we have that τη(−K) = τη(K)
for every knot K in a contact 3-manifold (Y, η) for which the τ-invariant can be defined and
bc(η) ̸= 0. This implies that T (m) is actually an invariant of γ−as an unoriented knot, and thus
in the remaining of the paper we do not specify any orientation for γ−.
