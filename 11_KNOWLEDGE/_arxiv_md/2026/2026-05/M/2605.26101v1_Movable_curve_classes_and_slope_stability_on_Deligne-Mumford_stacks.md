MOVABLE CURVE CLASSES AND SLOPE STABILITY ON DELIGNE–MUMFORD
STACKS
SEBASTIAN CASALAINA-MARTIN AND SHEND ZHJEQI
ABSTRACT. We generalize some results in the literature on movable curve classes and slope stability
of coherent sheaves on smooth projective varieties to the case of smooth proper DM stacks admitting
projective coarse moduli spaces. As an application, we establish a Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality
on such stacks. This paper is the second in a series aiming to generalize results of Popa–Schnell and
Wei–Wu on Viehweg hyperbolicity to the setting of DM stacks, and in particular, to certain KSBA
moduli spaces.
INTRODUCTION
Slope stability of coherent sheaves with respect to an ample line bundle on a smooth complex
projective variety plays a central role in the study of coherent sheaves. Among many applications
are constructions of moduli spaces of sheaves, as well as various constraints on invariants of stable
sheaves, such as the Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality [Gie79]. In order to apply these techniques
in the setting of birational geometry, where the pull-back of an ample line bundle need not be
ample, it has become clear recently that one should consider, instead, slope stability with respect
to movable classes of curves, i.e., classes that pair non-negatively with all pseudo-effective divisors
[CP11, GKP14, GKP16, GKPT19, CP19]. Recall that for a nonzero torsion-free coherent sheaf E on
a smooth complex projective variety X and a movable curve class α ∈N1(X)R, one defines the
α-slope of E as
µα(E) := c1(E) · α
rk(E) .

The sheaf E is said to be α-semi-stable (resp. α-stable) if for all non-zero subsheaves (resp. non-zero
subsheaves of smaller rank than E) F ⊆E, one has µα(F) ≤µα(E) (resp. µα(F) < µα(E)).
In order to apply these techniques elsewhere, to study the birational geometry of moduli spaces,
in this paper we extend the notion of movable classes of curves, and slope stability with respect to
such classes, to the case of smooth proper Deligne–Mumford stacks with projective coarse moduli
spaces. We note that the case where X = [V/G] is a finite quotient stack, i.e., X is the quotient of
a smooth projective variety V by a finite group G acting on V, has been considered in [CP19], and
the results in this paper should be considered a generalization of those results to smooth proper
DM stacks over C with projective coarse moduli space.

the elementary fact that for normal projective varieties, any finite cover is dominated by a finite
Galois cover. The stability argument for Galois covers follows quickly, as one can easily descend
destabilizing sub-sheaves from the Galois cover.
This naturally leads to an approach for the DM stacks we consider here, since they all admit a
finite flat cover q : V →X from a smooth projective variety V. In other words, if one knew that
slope semi-stability for DM stacks was equivalent to slope semi-stability after pull back by a finite
cover, one could easily reduce the proof of Theorem A(d) to the case of varieties. Unfortunately,
for DM stacks, it is not the case that every finite cover q : V →X from a normal projective variety
V is dominated by a Galois cover of X from a normal projective variety, and so one must take a
different approach than in the case of varieties.
This is the main complication arising for the stacks appearing here that does not arise in the
case X = [V/G] considered in [CP19]. Consequently, the main technical point in proving Theo-
rem A(d) is to establish that slope semi-stability for DM stacks is equivalent to slope semi-stability
after pull back by a finite cover to a smooth projective variety; we prove this in Proposition 3.16.

stead use a result of Rydh [Ryd11, Thm. C], which implies that after a stacky blow-up X ′ →X , the
finite cover q : V →X is dominated by a Galois cover in the sense that there is a smooth DM stack
V′ with projective coarse moduli space, with V′ dominating V, and a finite group G acting on V′,
such that X ′ = [V′/G]; see Theorem 1.6. This turns out to be enough to prove Proposition 3.16.

1. PRELIMINARIES
1.1. Terminology. We work over C. A variety is an integral separated scheme of finite type over
C. An alteration X′ →X is a surjective projective generically finite morphism of schemes over C.
We use the definition of a Deligne–Mumford (DM) stack in [LMB00, Def. 4.1]. Note that this differs
from the definition in [Sta26] in that there is the additional hypothesis in [LMB00, Def. 4.1] that
the diagonal be representable, separated, and quasi-compact. We direct the reader to [CMW18,
App. B] for a discussion of the relationship among various definitions of DM stacks in the litera-
ture (see in particular [CMW18, Fig. 1]). We emphasize that, with the definition of DM stack that

1.2. Structure of DM stacks. The general set-up will be a smooth proper (resp. separated) integral
DM stack X of finite type over C with coarse moduli space π : X →X, with the added assumption
that the algebraic space X be a projective (resp. quasi-projective) variety. Recall that such a stack
admits a finite flat morphism q : V →X from a smooth projective scheme V ([KV04, Thm. 1] and
[Kre09, Thm. 4.4], see also [Sta26, §03B6]); note that q is schematic and projective. In this situation
we have that X is normal, Q-factorial, with at worst klt singularities. The morphism π : X →X
is flat over the smooth locus of X; flatness is an ´etale local property, and so it suffices to consider
the case X = [U/G] for some smooth variety U and a finite group G. Then, from say [GW20,
Cor. 14.12], it suffices to show that U →U/G is flat over the smooth locus of the quotient, which
follows from the miracle of flatness [Mat89, Thm. 23.1, p.179].
For brevity, we will say that such a stack X is a global DM quotient stack if there is a smooth
projective (resp. quasi-projective) variety V over C and a finite algebraic group G over C acting on
V such that X ∼= [V/G]; note that this implies that X = V/G. For context, recall that X is a global
quotient stack if and only if there exists a smooth projective (resp. quasi-projective) variety V′ and
a finite ´etale morphism q′ : V′ →X (see the proof of [LMB00, Thm. (6.1)]).
