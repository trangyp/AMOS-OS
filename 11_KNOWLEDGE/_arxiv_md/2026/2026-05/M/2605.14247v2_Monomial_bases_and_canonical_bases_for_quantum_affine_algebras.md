Monomial bases and canonical bases for quantum affine algebras
Toshiaki Shoji and Zhiping Zhou
Abstract. We construct a monomial basis of a quantum affine algebra of simply-
laced type, associated to the PBW basis of Beck-Nakajima. We show that there
exists a simple algorithm of computing the canonical basis in terms of the mono-
mial basis. We discuss the relations of the canonical basis obtained from this PBW
basis with Lusztig’s canonical basis constructed by using the geometry of quivers.
Introduction
Let U−
q be the negative half of a quantum affine algebra Uq associated to the
Cartan datum X = (I, ( , )). In [BN], Beck and Nakajima constructed a PBW
basis Xh of U−
q associated to a doubly infinite sequence h = (. . . , i−1, i0, i1, . . . )
of vertices in I, by using braid group actions Ti. They defined the canonical basis
Bh of U−
q by making use of Xh. Let g be the affine Lie algebra associated to X,
and g0 the corresponding Lie algebra of finite type. g has a realization as a central
extension of the loop algebra of g0, as g = Lg0 ⊕Cc⊕Cd. The sequence h is defined
by using the loop algebra structure of g, and the basis Xh has a good connection
with Kashiwara’s theory of extremal weight modules ([Ka2]).
More generally, consider the quantum group U−
q associated to the Cartan datum
X of Kac-Moody type, and let ⃗Q be a quiver related to X. In [L3], [L5], Lusztig
gave a categorification of U−
q in terms of the geometry of ⃗Q. For simplicity, assume
that X is simply-laced. For each representation space V of ⃗Q, he constructed a
certain category PV consisting of semisimple complexes on V by using the theory of
perverse sheaves on V, and showed that the direct sum of the Grothendieck group
KV of this category, for all non-isomorphic V, has a structure of an algebra which
is isomorphic to (the integral form of) U−
q . Then he defined the canonical basis of
U−
q as the set of simple perverse sheaves in the category PV for all V, under this
isomorphism. We denote this set of simple perverse sheaves in F
V PV as B.
By the general theory of perverse sheaves, a simple perverse sheaf can be ex-
pressed as an intersection cohomology complex.
It is an interesting problem to
describe a simple perverse sheaf contained in B as an explicit form of intersection
cohomology complex. In the case where X is simply-laced of affine type, this was
first achieved by Lusztig [L4] for the McKay quiver (a special type of the orienta-
tion), and was generalized by Li-Lin [LL] to the affine quiver of any orientation.
These results heavily depend on the representation theory of quivers.

Monomial bases of U−
q was originally constructed by Lusztig [L2] in the case
where X is simply-laced of finite type, by using the representation theory of quivers.
But in his paper, the terminology “monomial bases” does not appear, since the
monomial basis is just used as a step for constructing the canonical basis. In [SZ3],
Lusztig’s monomial basis was generalized to the case of non-symmetric finite type,
by applying the folding theory of quantum groups ([SZ1]). In particular, it is shown
in [SZ3] that there is a simple algorithm of computing the transition matrix between
canonical basis and PBW basis, by making use of the monomial basis.
We return to the case where X is of affine type, and assume that X is simply-
laced. It is known that U−
q is realized as a subalgebra of the Ringel-Hall algebra H∗
q
over Fq associated to the quiver ⃗Q, where Fq is a finite field of q-elements (here we
consider q as a generic parameter). Under this situation, the braid group action on
U−
q corresponds to the action of reflection functors on the representations of quivers.
In [XXZ], Xiao, Xu and Zhao constructed the PBW basis Xh′ in H∗
q associated to
the doubly infinite sequence h′, and then defined a monomial basis. (Their result was
generalized in [XX] to the non simply-laced case.) By making use of this monomial
basis, they constructed a certain basis of U−
q (they call it a bar-invariant basis), and
showed that this basis coincides with Lusztig’s canonical basis B. Here the doubly
infinite sequence h′ is defined by using an affine Coxeter element C = si0 · · · sin of
the Weyl group W with I = {i0, . . . , in}, and the sequence i0, i1, . . . , in is extended
to h′ in a periodic way. We require that the order in h′ is chosen so that h′ is
adapted (see 5.3 for the definition). This is a crucial condition for applying the
representation theory of quivers. From the construction, those PBW basis Xh and
Xh′ have similar theoretical structures. But h and h′ have no relations. Note that
h is not adapted (see the example in 1.9), and the representation theory of quivers
cannot be applied directly to Xh.
In this paper, we concentrate on the PBW basis Xh and the canonical basis
Bh. Assume that X is simply-laced affine type. We construct a monomial basis
associated to Xh. The construction of monomial basis by [L2] (finite case) and by
[XXZ] (affine case) depends on the representation theory of quivers. Instead, in our
construction, we apply Lusztig’s geometric construction of canonical basis B. Once
the monomial basis is constructed, the algorithm of computing the canonical basis
in terms of monomial basis can be extended to the affine case. It is interesting
that this algorithm is quite similar to the algorithm of computing generalized Green
functions in the theory of character sheaves ([L1]).
Finally, we discuss the relationship between the basis Bh and the basis B. By
using the parametrization of B based on the representation theory of quivers due to
[LL], we give a description of Bh in terms of the intersection cohomology complexes.
