MOSTOW RIGIDITY FOR SKEW SOLENOIDAL
MANIFOLDS
FERNANDO ALCALDE CUESTA, MATILDE MART´INEZ,
AND ALBERTO VERJOVSKY
Abstract. We prove a Mostow rigidity theorem for foliated bundles
over closed hyperbolic manifolds of dimension n ≥3 endowed with
a completely invariant measure of full support.
These include
solenoidal manifolds obtained as inverse limits of directed systems
of finite coverings of closed hyperbolic manifolds. This theorem
then extends to skew solenoidal manifolds for which the action of
the holonomy group is twisted by means of a cocycle.
1. Introduction
Mostow rigidity theorem is one of the most beautiful results of the
late 20th century at the crossroads between geometry and topology.
Assume that two closed Riemannian manifolds with constant negative
curvature and dimension n ≥3 have isomorphic fundamental groups.
Then both manifolds are isometric.
Other than the original proof by G. D. Mostow [25], there are several
proofs including that of G. Prasad in finite volume [27], the homological
approach by M. Gromov [16], and the geometric approach by G. Besson,
G. Courtois and S. Gallot [5]. Here we will adhere to the clear and
transparent vision of W. P. Thurston in his notes about the geometry
and topology of 3-manifolds [33]. The surveys of M. Bourdon [6, 7] and
A. L¨ucker [21] will be also very useful for adapting Mostow’s rigidity
theorem to foliated category.
All these proofs share the same central point, namely the construction
of a boundary map. Combining topological, geometric, analytic and
ergodic arguments, it can be proven that this boundary map is conformal
and, therefore, extends to an isometry between the manifolds.
Our aim in this paper is to extend Mostow rigidity from n-manifolds to
solenoidal n-manifolds, i.e second countable locally compact Hausdorff
spaces which are locally modeled on the product of a n-dimensional open
disk and a Cantor set. For a comprehensive study of solenoidal manifolds,

Theorem B (Mostow rigidity for inverse limit of hyperbolic manifolds).
Let X1 and X2 be two n-dimensional hyperbolic solenoidal manifolds

connected closed hyperbolic manifolds M1 and M2 of dimension n ≥3.

isometry I : X1 →X2 integrable homotopic to h.

map is an essential part of the proof, but the suspension hypothesis
facilitates the construction of the boundaries at infinity, on which this
map is defined.

3. Suspensions and inverse limits
Many important examples of solenoidal manifolds arise as suspensions
or, equivalently, as laminations of fibre bundles that are transverse to
the fibres. See Definition 4 for the meaning of transversality in our
context. We will start here by recalling the classical construction of
suspensions, see also [15] and [19]. Next, in Section 5, we will see
some conditions that appear to be more general but nevertheless fall
within this category. Our rigidity theorem will be first proved in this
context (see Section 4), and later will be extended to more general
solenoidal manifolds called skew to distinguish them from suspensions
(see Section 6).

Theorem 1. Let M1 and M2 be two connected closed hyperbolic mani-

Before stating a natural corollary of this theorem, we will clarify the
meaning of the condition imposed on the map h:
Definition 5. A leafwise homotopy equivalence h : Mρ1 →Mρ2 is a
homotopy equivalence, with homotopy inverse h′, such that
(i) h and h′ are foliated maps preserving the laminations of Mρ1 and
Mρ2,
(ii) the maps h′˚h and h˚h′ are homotopic to the identity by means of
integral homotopies along the leaves.
Notice that the first condition only states that the leaves of Mρ1 are
sent to leaves of Mρ2 by h (and conversely by h′), but the existence of
an integral homotopy implies that h′˚h (resp. h˚h′) leaves each leaf of
Mρ1 (resp. Mρ2) invariant.
Corollary 1. Let M be a connected closed hyperbolic manifold of
dimension n ≥3 with fundamental group Γ. Let Mρ be the suspension
of a representation ρ : Γ →G into a homeomorphism group G acting on
a compact space F. If ρ(Γ) preserves an ergodic probability measure on
F with full support, then Mρ admits a unique hyperbolic metric, namely,
the pull-back to Mρ of the unique hyperbolic metric on M. In other
words, the Theichm¨uller space of Mρ contains only one point.

(2) The second step will consist in observing that the lifted map is a

(3) In the third step, we will first construct a boundary map between
