2.1. Study Area and Data Generation
The study focused on California agricultural landscapes
represented in the LandIQ 2018 statewide crop maping
layer. LandIQ provides eld-scale polygons for crop and
land-use clases acros the state and reports high statewide
maping acuracy. From this layer, 4,742 polygons labeled
as tomato were extracted to dene the positive clas. A
balanced negative clas of 4,742 non-tomato polygons was
then asembled from other agricultural categories, including
alfalfa, wheat, corn, beans, orchards, and aditional eld
crops. Rather than dening the negative clas as a single
alternative crop, we constructed it from a balanced mix of
non-tomato crop groups selected to represent a broad cros-
section of California agriculture. Although this clas does
not capture every crop grown in the state, it was designed to
include diverse major crop types and therefore required the
model to learn tomato-specic paterns rather than a simple
contrast betwen tomatoes and one competing crop.
The resulting dataset contained 9,484 eld polygons. For
each polygon, a binary mask was generated so that pixels
inside tomato polygons were labeled as tomato and pixels
inside non-tomato polygons were labeled as non-tomato.
AlphaEarth embeding layers were cliped to the polygon
fotprints, and aligned raster chips were created localy
before being stored in Amazon S3 for model training and
inference. Chips aected by NaN or NoData margins were
masked so that only valid agricultural pixels contributed to
los calculation and metric reporting.
To ensure an independent evaluation, the polygons were
partitioned spatialy before model development so that nearby
elds did not apear acros the training, validation, and test
sets. The nal split contained 6,638 training polygons, 1,42
validation polygons, and 1,424 test polygons. This design
ensured that the reported test metrics reected performance
on unsen locations rather than memorization of nearby
elds with similar geometry or management.
