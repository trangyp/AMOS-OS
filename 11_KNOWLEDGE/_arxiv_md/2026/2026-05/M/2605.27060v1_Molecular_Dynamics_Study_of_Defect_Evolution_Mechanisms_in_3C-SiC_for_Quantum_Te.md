A B S T R A C T
The migration of point defects and formation of spin defects in 3C-SiC were investigated using
molecular dynamics simulations, with migration barriers obtained from Nudged Elastic Band (NEB)
calculations and finite temperature diffusivities evaluated using both mean square displacement
(MSD) and jump frequency approaches. While both methods reproduce Arrhenius behavior, the
jump frequency formulation exhibits improved statistical stability. Activation energies of 2.12 eV
for carbon vacancies and 0.88 eV for carbon interstitials are obtained, consistent with literature. The
resulting mobility hierarchy governs defect evolution and complex formations. Interstitial vacancy
recombination competes with vacancy aggregation into divacancies, influencing the stabilization of
spin active defect centers. The study also provides a consistent framework for diffusion analysis in
atomistic simulations.
Quantum materials provide engineered platforms where
coherent spin, charge, and optical phenomena can be har-
nessed for next generation information technologies. It has
been well established in previous research that point defects,
such as vacancies in silicon (𝑉𝑆𝑖), nitrogen vacancy centers
(NV center), antisites and carbon self interstitial vacancies
(𝐶𝑆𝑖𝑉𝐶), as well as defect clusters (e.g. carbon divacancies)
in wide band gap semiconductor materials, exhibit the dual
properties of being spin active and optically active [1, 2].
Among these, divacancies (𝑉𝑆𝑖𝑉𝐶) in cubic silicon carbide
are particularly promising due to their optical addressability
[3], near infrared spin photon interface[4], high fidelity read-
out via spin to charge conversion[5], and extended coherence
time [6]. These distinctive characteristics position them as
strong candidates for various applications in quantum tech-
nologies, such as quantum photonics, quantum sensing, and
quantum computation [7, 8]. Moreover silicon carbide is
quite mature material as widely been applied in semiconduc-
tor [9]. This work focuses on the 3C-SiC polytype, which
offers a structurally simpler framework compared to other
polytypes such as 4H-SiC and 6H-SiC [10, 11]. In particular,
3C-SiC exhibits a single crystallographic configuration for
intrinsic point defects (e.g., 𝑉𝑆𝑖, 𝑉𝐶, and 𝑉𝑆𝑖𝑉𝐶), making
it well suited for systematic investigation. Additionally, the
availability of established synthesis routes further supports
its selection as a model system.
Given the potential significance and delicate existence
of these defects, it is crucial to approach their study with
meticulous care. This is because these defects exist only

Spin defects, including divacancies, are typically generated

1. Computational strategy
We performed molecular dynamics simulations and en-
ergy minimization by using Large scale Atomic/Molecular
Massively Parallel Simulator (LAMMPS) to study defect
formation and evolution. 3C-SiC was represented by 1728
atoms cubic cell having diamond lattice with two atom types.
Periodic boundary conditions were enabled along all x,y
and z axis. A detailed benchmarking process was conducted
to select the most appropriate force field for modeling the
interatomic forces in silicon carbide. This process led to the
choice of the Environment Dependent Interatomic Potential
(EDIP) force field for subsequent studies.

were fully relaxed using energy minimization as imple-

1.4. Methodology
Molecular dynamics simulations were performed to gen-
erate atomistic trajectories for diffusion analysis under con-
trolled thermodynamic conditions using the LAMMPS sim-
ulation package [34]. The simulation workflow consisted of
an initial equilibration phase followed by production runs of
sufficient duration to capture statistically meaningful defect
migration events. Simulation parameters, including temper-
ature range and total simulation time, were selected to ensure
adequate sampling of defect dynamics and convergence of
diffusion related quantities.
Diffusion coefficients were estimated using both the ap-
proaches: the Einstein mean square displacement formalism
[43, 40], and the event based derived from jump frequency
analysis [44]. Vacancy positions and defect evolution were
tracked using Voronoi based void volume analysis [38, 39],
while discrete migration events were identified from trajec-
tory data using change point detection techniques [45].
The resulting datasets were subsequently used for statis-
tical analysis of jump processes and evaluation of diffusion
behavior within a consistent and reproducible computational
framework.
2. Results and Discussion
The results are organized into two complementary stages.
The first stage focuses on methodological validation and
potential assessment, thus establishing a reliable compu-
tational framework for subsequent defect investigations. In
this phase, structural properties and elastic constants were
evaluated to benchmark the employed interatomic potentials
and ensure consistency with reported literature.
The second stage addresses the core defect dynamics
and is divided into zero temperature and finite temperature
regimes. In the zero temperature limit, migration barriers
and minimum energy pathways were determined using NEB,
providing intrinsic activation energies for defect transfor-
mations. In contrast, the finite temperature regime captures
thermally activated diffusion processes through molecular
dynamics simulations performed in the NVT ensemble.
Statistical analyses of defect trajectories were used to extract
diffusion coefficients and effective migration barriers across
a range of temperatures.

Dynamics at Finite Temperature
While zero temperature migration pathways provide fun-
damental insight into the intrinsic energy landscape govern-
ing defect motion, they do not capture the thermally acti-
vated dynamics that control defect evolution under realistic
conditions. The central objective of this study is to identify
the physical mechanisms that influence defect interactions

and the formation of complex defect structures, such as
divacancies. This requires examining the time resolved evo-
lution of defects under finite temperature conditions, where
thermal fluctuations enable stochastic migration events. This
goal can be achieved by tracking defect motion over ex-
tended simulation times and across a range of temperatures.
Thus, it becomes possible to quantify diffusion behavior and
reveal the mechanisms that govern possible defect aggrega-

2.3.1. Melting Behavior and High Temperature
Stability
Accurate diffusion analysis requires that simulations are
performed within the thermodynamically stable solid phase.
To establish the upper temperature limit for reliable dif-
fusion calculations, the melting behavior of 3C–SiC was
examined using both Tersoff and EDIP potentials within the
NPT ensemble. Figure 5 presents the temperature dependent
volumetric response obtained using the EDIP potential. A
pronounced discontinuity in volume is observed at approx-
imately 2620 K after ∼20 ns of equilibration, indicating a
clear phase transition. This melting temperature is in good
agreement with reported experimental values [47], demon-
strating that EDIP provides a realistic description of thermal
softening and lattice destabilization.
In contrast, simulations performed with the Tersoff po-
tential did not exhibit a comparable melting transition even at
temperatures approaching 3000 K. The absence of volumet-
ric instability suggests an overestimation of lattice rigidity
and delayed structural breakdown at high temperature. This
behavior indicates that Tersoff significantly overpredicts the
thermal stability of the crystal phase.
These results establish an important temperature bound-
ary for diffusion studies. To ensure that defect transport is
evaluated strictly within the solid regime, all finite temper-
ature diffusion calculations were confined to temperatures
well below the EDIP melting point (i.e., 𝑇
< 2600 K).

4. Summary and Conclusions
In this work, we have systematically investigated the for-
mation, migration, and interaction of intrinsic point defects
in 3C–SiC using large scale classical molecular dynamics
simulations based on the environment dependent interatomic
potential (EDIP). A comprehensive benchmarking proce-
dure, including structural properties, defect formation ener-
gies, and melting behavior, confirmed the suitability of the
selected potential for high temperature defect studies and en-
sured the reliability of the atomistic simulations performed
in this work.

mechanism.
Arrhenius analysis of the diffusion coefficients yielded
effective migration barriers of 2.12 eV for carbon vacancies
and 0.88 eV for carbon interstitials, consistent with reported
literature values. These results establish a clear hierarchy of
defect mobility in 3C–SiC,
𝐼C > 𝐼Si > 𝑉C > 𝑉Si,
which plays a key role in governing defect evolution under
thermodynamic conditions. In particular, the relatively slow
diffusion of vacancies promotes vacancy aggregation and
the formation of divacancy complexes, whereas the high
mobility of carbon interstitials facilitates rapid interstitial
vacancy recombination. Our simulations show that while
divacancy formation is energetically favorable, interstitial
mediated annihilation releases significantly larger stabiliza-
tion energies and therefore represents the dominant recovery
mechanism when mobile interstitials are present.
Beyond the specific case of 3C–SiC, this work estab-
lishes a statistically rigorous framework for extracting dif-
fusion coefficients from classical molecular dynamics sim-
ulations. By combining jump frequency analysis, MSD val-
idation, and stochastic random walk modeling, the present
study provides a quantitative strategy for assessing uncer-
tainty and methodological bias in atomistic diffusion studies.
These insights contribute to a better understanding of defect
stabilization pathways in wide bandgap semiconductors and
provide guidance for defect engineering in materials relevant
to quantum technologies and radiation tolerant electronic
devices.
