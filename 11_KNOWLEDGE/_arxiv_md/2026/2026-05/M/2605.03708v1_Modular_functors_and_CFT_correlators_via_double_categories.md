Abstract
We point out that double categories provide a natural setting for modular functors obtained
by a bicategorical string-net construction: The source of the modular functor – which is now
a double functor – is a symmetric monoidal double category of bordisms, with bordisms as
horizontal morphisms and smooth embeddings of manifolds as vertical morphisms. The target
of the modular functor is a double category with profunctors and functors as horizontal and
vertical morphisms.
The correlators and field functors for a conformal field theory based on a pivotal monoidal
category C can then be understood in the unified setting of a vertical transformation between
the modular functors for two pointed pivotal bicategories, the delooping of C and the bicategory
of ∆-separable symmetric Frobenius algebras in C. Using skein theoretic methods, we show
that this vertical transformation is an equivalence, which implies that field functors are
equivalences of categories and that universal correlators are isomorphisms of vector spaces.

Introduction
String-net models have recently been found to provide an efficient handle on the study of
correlators in two-dimensional conformal field theories [FSY1, Ya, FSY2]. Concretely, the
following picture has emerged. Let C be a pivotal linear tensor category. (Readers directly
interested in conformal field theory may wish to imagine C as a category of representations
of a suitably nice vertex operator algebra, ignoring the braiding.) The monoidal category
C gives rise to two bicategories: On the one hand, BC, a bicategory with a single object
whose 1-morphisms are the objects in C and whose 2-morphisms are the morphisms in C. And
on the other hand, the bicategory Fr(C) of ∆-separable symmetric Frobenius algebras in
C, with bimodules as 1-morphisms. There is a forgetful functor u: Fr(C) −→BC, which has
the structure of a separable Frobenius functor. Both BC and Fr(C) are pivotal, and both
are pointed, i.e. endowed with a distinguished object. For BC the latter is, obviously, the
single object, while for Fr(C) the distinguished object is the Frobenius algebra defined on the
monoidal unit.
In [FSY2] we developed a bicategorical string-net construction, which in particular yields a
modular functor for each of the two bicategories BC and Fr(C), i.e. two symmetric monoidal
functors

sheets, that is, for surfaces that allow for the definition of conformal field theory correlators.
The data of such a decoration include a stratification of the surface and the specification
of a full conformal field theory on each 2-cell of the surface, which amounts to assigning a
∆-separable symmetric Frobenius algebra, an object of Fr(C), to each 2-cell. There is more
to the decoration of the surface: boundary conditions, in the form of modules over Frobenius
algebras, are assigned to free boundary segments, as well as defect conditions, realized as
bimodules over Frobenius algebras, on embedded line defects. The data for the world sheet
also determine which fields – boundary fields, bulk fields and, more generally, defect fields –
are inserted on the surface. The corresponding ‘insertion points’ come with local coordinates
and are thus really intervals (for boundary fields) or circles (for bulk and defect fields).

In the present paper we address two questions that arise in this context.
Question 1: Is there a natural way to package the field functors and the
universal correlators into a single mathematical quantity?
The answer to this question is affirmative. It combines in a novel way field content and
correlators of a quantum field theory in a single mathematical quantity. To arrive at this
conclusion requires a further refinement of the setup. Our starting point are the modular
functors given by string-net constructions. In our context, a modular functor is a functor from
a geometric two-dimensional category to an algebraic two-dimensional category. We observe
in this article that it is very natural to work with two-dimensional categorical structures that
are double categories rather than, as in [FSY2], bicategories. A double category has two
types of 1-morphisms, known as horizontal and vertical 1-morphisms. For each type there is
a composition, being strictly associative for vertical 1-morphisms, but associative only up to
associators for horizontal 1-morphisms.

A reminder about string-net constructions
In this paper, we use a string-net construction to obtain a modular functor. In the present
section and in Section 3 we work with the following variant of the notion of a modular functor:
An open-closed modular functor – or modular functor, for short – is a symmetric monoidal
pseudofunctor
Bl :

2

(2.1)

transformation Bl −→Bl′ of modular functors. This provides a natural notion of equivalence
of open-closed modular functors.

common such datum is a pivotal, or even spherical, fusion category C [LeW, Ki]. An object of

For the construction in the present paper, we work with a more general input datum
[FSY2, Thm. 3.36]: a bicategory B that is pointed, i.e. is endowed with a distinguished object
∗∈B, and strictly pivotal, i.e. rigid with a strict equality between the identity and double
dual pseudofunctors. This – mathematically natural – setting is also the appropriate one for
the applying the string-net construction to the construction of correlators of two-dimensional
conformal field theories.
Let us describe the basic idea of this bicategorical string-net construction. A B-decoration
a on a compact oriented one-manifold ℓ, possibly with boundary, consists of a pair of data:
first, a collection of finitely many oriented points in the interior of ℓ, whose complement
partitions ℓinto finitely many intervals; second, an assignment of an object in B to each of the
intervals in this partition, and of a compatible 1-morphism in B to each of the oriented points.
If ℓhas a non-empty boundary, then the object that is assigned to any intervals that contains
a boundary point is required to be the distinguished object ∗∈B. We allow for B-decorations
for which the collection of points on a connected component of ℓis empty; if such a connected
component is a circle, then we can assign to it any object of B. The pivotal structure on
B is, by definition, the identity on objects and induces an involution on 1-morphisms. As
a consequence, any B-decoration a on a one-manifold ℓinduces a B-decoration a on the
one-manifold ℓwith opposite orientation.
Now let Σ : ℓ
7−→ℓ′ be a two-dimensional bordism, and let a and a′ be B-decorations on
the one-manifolds ℓand ℓ′, respectively. (For our purposes, a bordim is a two-manifold with
boundary; we do not consider them up to diffeomorphism.) The basic building block of the
string-net construction is the assignment of a vector space snB(Σ; a, a′) to these data. An
element of snB(Σ; a, a′) is a linear combination of finite B-colored string diagrams on Σ that
are compatible with the boundary datum a′ on ℓand a on ℓ, modulo the local relations that
are captured by the graphical calculus for the pivotal bicategory B. The mapping class group
Map(Σ) of Σ acts naturally on the set of B-colored string diagrams; this induces a (genuine,
non-projective) action of Map(Σ) on the vector space snB(Σ; a, a′).
The first step in the construction of a modular functor consists in assigning to any one-
manifold ℓa k-linear category snB(ℓ), called the cylinder category of ℓ. By definition, snB(ℓ)
is the category that has B-decorations on the one-manifold ℓas objects and the vector spaces
snB(ℓ× I; a, a′), with I is the standard interval, as morphism spaces. Morphisms of snB(ℓ)
can thus be represented by B-colored string diagrams on the cylinder ℓ× I; composition in
the cylinder category is then induced by concatenating (or, in other words, stacking) string
diagrams.
We note a few properties of cylinder categories: For the one-manifold ℓwith reversed
orientation, the cylinder category snB(ℓ) is the opposite category of snB(ℓ). For a disjoint
union ℓ⊔ℓ′, the cyclinder category comes with a canonical equivalence to the tensor product
of the cylinder categories of ℓand ℓ′,
snB(ℓ⊔ℓ′) ≃snB(ℓ) ⊗snB(ℓ′) .
(2.3)

ordered pairs and morphism spaces are tensor products over k.

3.3
Conformal field theory correlators
A motivating application of the string-net constructions outlined in Section 2 is a well-struc-
tured approach to the construction of consistent systems of correlators for rational conformal
16


## Page 17


field theories. This application was developed in [Ya, FSY1, FSY2]. Here we recall a few of
its main aspects and provide an interpretation of Theorem 3.4 in that context.
There are two distinct entities that are referred to as a two-dimensional conformal field
theory, or CFT, for short: chiral CFT and full CFT; see e.g. [FSWY] for a short review. A
chiral CFT is governed by a vertex operator algebra V, which reflects conservation laws of the
CFT. The representations of V form a linear monoidal category C = Rep(V). We focus our
attention on rational conformal field theories, in which case C has the structure of a modular
fusion category. The string-net construction described in Section 2 works in fact in the much
more general setting that B is any k-linear pivotal bicategory, but at present its conformal
field theory ramifications are fully understood only in the rational case. (Considerable efforts
go, however, at the time of writing in getting control over larger classes of two-dimensional
conformal field theories see e.g. [FS2, FSSW, Wo, HR, Ye].) A full CFT combines a left and
a right chiral CFT, which are controlled by two modular fusion categories C and C′. We
restrict our attention to full CFTs for which C′ equals C up to a reversal of the braiding
and the ribbon twist, i.e. C′ = Crev = Rep(V)rev. In this case we have, thanks to modularity,
C ⊠Crev ≃Z(C) as modular fusion categories, so that the string-net construction produces
the appropriate cylinder category for the circle, see (2.2).
Having fixed the modular category C, we can relate string-net constructions to further
CFT terminology. In physics terms, a surface Σ carrying a Fr(C)-labeled string-net is called
a world sheet. For example, the labels of the 1-cells on the boundary of Σ describe boundary
conditions for the CFT on the world sheet that are compatible with the chiral symmetry
encoded in C, while labels for 1-cells in the interior of Σ describe types of topological defect
lines.
A central goal of any quantum field theory is to establish the existence of correlation
functions and understand there properties. For full CFTs with C′ = Crev this has been achieved
in the following setting: Starting from the vertex algebra V whose representation category is
C, one can construct conformal blocks. These are vector bundles, endowed with a projectively
flat connection, over the moduli space of complex structures and marked points on the world
sheet Σ. Thanks to the projectively flat connection, the conformal blocks are characterized,
up to isomorphism, by a representation of the mapping class group of Σ. The monodromy
data provided by the conformal blocks are captured by an open-closed modular functor SNB
(2.6), with the bicategory B being the delooping BC of the modular fusion category C.
The correlation functions of the full CFT are global horizontal sections in these bundles of
conformal blocks. In the description of conformal blocks via an open-closed modular functor,
the correlation functions are distinguished elements in vector spaces of conformal blocks
that are invariant under the action of a suitable subgroup [FSY1, Eq. (6.11)] of the mapping
class group and are compatible with sewing, i.e. with composition of 1-morphisms in the
bordism category. Such a specific vector is obtained by regarding the world sheet Σ as a
Fr(C)-colored string net with boundary data a and a′ that encode the fields whose correlator is
considered, and thereby as an element of the string-net space SNFr(C)(Σ; a, a′). (This element
was called a quantum world sheet in [FSY1]. As seen in [FSY2, Rem. 4.9], the modular
functor SNFr(C) classifies decorated world sheets up to a local graphical calculus of defects.)
By applying the field functor to the boundary values and the universal correlator (2.16) to
the distinguished vector, one obtains an element of the vector space SNC(Σ; Fℓa, Fℓ′ a′) that
17


## Page 18


captures the monodromy data of the conformal blocks with insertions on the complex double
bΣ, and this element is the correlator for the world sheet Σ.
The resulting interpretation of Theorem 3.4 in the CFT setting is as follows. Via the
vertex algebra V, the conservation laws of the chiral CFT with C = Rep(V) can be expressed
as linear differential equations, known as Ward identities, that are satisfied by the conformal
blocks. It is a priori by no means clear whether every solution to these equations plays a
role in full conformal field theory. The surjectivity of the universal correlator UCorC(Σ)a,a′
stated in Theorem 3.4 means that this is in fact the case: any C-labeled string net on a
world sheet Σ with boundary data (a, a′), can be expressed as a linear combination of CFT
correlators, provided that correlators involving arbitrary networks of defect lines, and thereby
also arbitrary types of defect fields, are admitted. Thinking about spaces of C-labeled string
nets as spaces of solutions to the Ward identities, this implies that every solution to these
differential equations contributes to some CFT correlator – there are no spurious solutions.
In short, CFT correlators exhaust the complete space of conformal blocks. This is a very
strong statement: in contrast, the correlators for ordinary bulk fields, to which attention has
traditionally often been confined in the past, typically make use of only a small subspace of
the space of all solutions. (In the early literature dealing with concrete solutions to the Ward
identities, the latter feature was well known, but not picked out as an important theme.)
To understand the bearing of injectivity of the universal correlator in the CFT context,
consider two world sheets that share the same surface Σ, which give rise to two vectors
in SNFr(C)(Σ; a, a′). Suppose that the universal correlator maps these two vectors to one
and the same C-colored string net. This means that the two world sheets have the same
correlator, and hence they cannot be distinguished by any ‘measurement’. Injectivity of the
universal correlator means that the quantum world sheets determined by the two world sheets
must be the same, and hence that they are related by a move in the bicategorical graphical
calculus of defects. It follows that all relations among the correlators of a full CFT are local
field-theoretical relations that come from the skein calculus of topological defects – there
aren’t any additional relations between correlators beyond the local relations implied by the
string-net construction. In this sense, Theorem 3.4 leads to direct physical consequences, in
terms of correlators, of the calculus of defects.

4.2
Monoidal double categories
We consider double categories together with double functors and vertical transformations,
as a symmetric monoidal 2-category Dbl by using the Cartesian product. A symmetric
monoidal double category is a symmetric pseudomonoid in Dbl. Slightly more explicitly,
we have (for a fully unpacked description see Definitions A.2.12 and A.2.13 of [Co]): A
pseudomonoid internal to a monoidal 2-category (B, ⊗, 1) is an object m in B together with
morphisms m ⊗m −→m and 1 −→m such that the usual associativity and unit properties hold
up to specified 2-isomorphisms that satisfy appropriate equalities [DS, Sect. 3]. A braided
pseudomonoid in (B, ⊗, 1) is a pseudomonoid m equipped with an isomorphism between ⊗
and the opposite tensor product that obeys the usual hexagon identities of a braided monoidal
category.
Definition 4.7. A monoidal double category is a double category A endowed with double
functors ⊗: A × A −→A and I : ∗−→A, with ∗the terminal double category, and with invertible
vertical transformations that are analogous to the associator and unitors of a monoidal category
and obey the same pentagon axiom and triangle identities as those.
Note that in a vertical transformation we use vertical 1-morphisms. Accordingly, the
structural 1-morphisms in a monoidal double category are vertical morphisms.
Remark 4.8. If the double category A is monoidal, then the two categories A0 and A1
introduced in Definition 4.5 are monoidal categories.
We denote by τ the double functor A × A −→A × A that flips the order of the members of
any pair of objects and of morphisms in A (i.e., of pairs of objects and of morphisms in the
categories A0 and A1).
Definition 4.9. A braided monoidal double category is a monoidal double category A equipped
with a braiding, that is, with an invertible vertical transformation β : ⊗=⇒⊗◦τ that satisfies
(on the nose) the same hexagon identities as the braiding in a monoidal category.
A symmetric monoidal double category is a braided one for which β squares to the identity
vertical transformation.
