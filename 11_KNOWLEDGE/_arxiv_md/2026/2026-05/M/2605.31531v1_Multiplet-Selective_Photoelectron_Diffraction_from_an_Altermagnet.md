Multiplet-Selective Photoelectron Diffraction from an Altermagnet
L. Plucinski1, 2, ∗
1Peter Gr¨unberg Institut (PGI-6), Forschungszentrum J¨ulich GmbH, 52428 J¨ulich, Germany

Direct real-space probes of altermagnetic order remain scarce.
Here we introduce multiplet-
selective photoelectron diffraction (PED), a methodology in which different regions of a transition-
metal core-level multiplet act as distinct photoemission source waves.
Using multiple-scattering
calculations for the metallic altermagnet candidate CrSb, we show that selected Cr 3p multiplet fea-
tures with predominantly Y +1
1
and Y −1
1
character generate robust diffraction asymmetries sensitive
to altermagnetic domains. We demonstrate that both circularly and linearly polarized light provide
access to the effect, while suitable combinations of domains, light polarizations, and multiplet-
energy windows suppress nonmagnetic diffraction backgrounds.
The proposed approach can be
implemented using standard momentum-resolved photoemission instrumentation and establishes
core-level PED as a practical route toward domain-resolved studies of altermagnets.
INTRODUCTION
Altermagnets (AM) constitute a recently identified
class
of
compensated
magnetic
materials
in
which
exchange-split electronic states emerge despite vanishing
net magnetization [1]. Unlike conventional collinear an-
tiferromagnets (AFMs), opposite magnetic sublattices in
AMs are related by rotational rather than translational
symmetry, leading to momentum-dependent spin split-
ting and unconventional spin textures even in the absence
of spin-orbit coupling.
A central experimental challenge in this field is the
direct detection of AM order.
Recent ARPES studies
on MnTe and CrSb revealed signatures broadly consis-
tent with theoretical expectations [2–5], however, realis-
tic samples contain multiple structural and magnetic do-
mains, surface terminations, and strong matrix-element
effects. In particular, circular dichroism (CD) can also
arise from purely geometrical final-state effects, including
the Daimon effect in photoelectron diffraction (PED) [6].
Experimental approaches capable of separating magnetic
and structural contributions therefore remain highly de-
sirable.
The present work is conceptually related to ear-
lier magnetic photoelectron diffraction studies pioneered
by Fadley and collaborators, where core-level multiplet
emission from magnetic systems was used as an inter-
nal source of spin-polarized photoelectrons [7, 8]. More
recently, Kr¨uger demonstrated theoretically that circu-
lar dichroism in resonant photoelectron diffraction can
probe staggered sublattice magnetization in AMs [9]
while recent micro-focused CD-ARPES measurements
on DyMn6Sn6 demonstrated magnetic-domain-sensitive
dichroism in the Mn 3p multiplet region [10]. Together,
these developments suggest that polarization-dependent
core-level PED can provide a route toward disentangling
magnetic and structural contributions in AMs.
In this work we investigate polarization-dependent
photoelectron diffraction from CrSb surfaces. CrSb is a
metallic NiAs-type AM candidate for which high-quality
cleaved CrSb(001) and CrSb(120) surfaces were demon-
strated experimentally [4]. In particular, the CrSb(120)
surface exposes antialigned Cr sublattices embedded in
inequivalent local scattering environments, making it a
natural platform for multiplet-selective PED.
The key ingredient of the proposed method is the
transition-metal 3p multiplet. Different energy regions of
the 3p53dn final-state manifold carry different mixtures
of spin and orbital angular momentum [10–12]. Conse-
quently, selected parts of the Cr 3p spectrum can ex-
hibit dominant Y +1
1
- or Y −1
1
-like character tied to the
local N´eel vector.
The experiment can therefore com-
bine several reversal operations: magnetic-domain rever-
sal, polarization reversal, and reversal between different
multiplet-energy windows. This redundancy is essential
because it allows suppression of nonmagnetic diffraction
backgrounds while isolating the component associated
with AM order.
Using polarization-dependent PED calculations [13],
we demonstrate that both circularly and linearly polar-
ized light generate robust sublattice-sensitive diffraction
asymmetries in CrSb.
Our results establish multiplet-
selective core-level PED as a practical real-space probe
of AM order compatible with modern micro-ARPES in-
strumentation.

THE CASE OF CRSB(120) SURFACE
As an example of the proposed formalism, we analyze
the CrSb(120) surface in detail. This surface is experi-
mentally accessible by cleaving [4] and, unlike the (001)
surface terminated by either Cr1 or Cr2 [18], preserves
compensated magnetic order.
The CrSb(120) surface exhibits two AM domains, de-
picted as A and B in Fig. 1(e), which differ by swapping
the local moment orientations on Cr1 and Cr2 sites. Let
us fix the energy on the spectral region E1 in Fig. 1(b),
corresponding predominantly to a Y −1
1
source wave in
the lower-energy exchange-split region of the Cr 3p spec-
trum.
Experimentally, this corresponds to selecting a
particular photoelectron kinetic energy Ekin in the PED
experiment.
Since at hν = 80 eV with C−and at E1 we predomi-

1

in the geometry shown in Fig. 1 (e), in this experimental

reverses for C+ light, where the dominant contributions
originate from the Cr1 site in domain B and the Cr2 site
in domain A.
An additional reversal between predominant probing of
Cr1 and Cr2 sites in domains A and B can be achieved
by adjusting the binding energy to the E2 spectral region
in Fig. 1 (b).
The final ingredient allowing one to distinguish Cr1
and Cr2 sites is the multiple scattering process in PED.
Since Cr1 and Cr2 are embedded in different local envi-
ronments, the resulting diffraction patterns must also be
different.

ing PED patterns on different regions of the surface and
comparing the resulting diffraction maps.

DISCUSSION
The above considerations demonstrate the intimate
connection between the ability to probe AM domains
using micro-focused PED and the ability to establish
the presence of AM order itself.
These two aspects
cannot be separated, but rather form parts of a uni-
fied methodology for detecting altermagnetism using
polarization-dependent photoelectron diffraction.
The
proposed observables do not rely solely on conven-
tional magnetic dichroism, but on the coupling between
multiplet-selective source-wave symmetry and inequiv-
alent scattering environments tied to the compensated
magnetic order.
It is important to estimate the practical sensitivity
of the proposed method in real materials.
Our calcu-
lated dichroic contrast reaches values exceeding 50% in
selected regions of the PED maps and remains of the or-
der of 30% within the most experimentally relevant an-
gular cone of θ < ±15◦. The main remaining uncertainty
is the degree of experimental separation of the Cr 3p mul-
tiplet features in the effective Y m
l
basis. Although dedi-
cated experimental data for Cr 3p are presently unavail-
able, our recent investigation of the kagome compound
DyMn6Sn6 [10] revealed dichroic contrast exceeding 10%
in the Mn 3p region at hν = 300 eV. Importantly, the
measured dichroic spectra exhibited two sign-reversing
features, qualitatively consistent with the proposed E1
and E2 regions shown in Fig. 1(b). Furthermore, previ-
ous studies of transition-metal core levels demonstrated
strong spin polarization and magnetic dichroism in Fe,
Co, and Ni 3p photoemission [11, 20, 21]. Even compar-
atively weak linear dichroism in Ni(110) at hν = 177 eV
could be resolved experimentally [22]. Together, these re-
sults suggest that the proposed mechanism is robust and
represents a generic property of transition-metal core-
level photoemission from magnetic local moments.

From the experimental perspective,
the proposed
method is compatible with standard modern hemispheri-
cal deflector analyzers and momentum microscopes avail-
able at synchrotron micro-ARPES beamlines. Since the
relevant asymmetry is accessible within an angular cone
of θ < ±15◦, the required PED maps can be acquired ef-
ficiently not only with momentum microscopes but also
with standard lens-deflector systems.
In the present work we considered Sb-terminated
CrSb(120) surface, ARPES from which was demon-
strated experimentally [4].
In searching for A and B
AM domains, one also needs to take into account the
Cr-terminated surface. We believe that in micro-ARPES
these two surfaces will be easily distinguishable from ra-
tios of Sb and Cr core level spectral intensities, due to sur-
face sensitivity of photoemission. In our method it is crit-
ical to compare A and B domains with the same termi-
nation (stable-ratio mixture of Sb- and Cr-terminations,
possibly would also work, it is likely realized in thin films,
that however may exhibit domains too small to be mea-
surable by micro-focused photoemission without micro-
patterning the sample [23]). The most direct experiment
would be to measure A and B N´eel-domains on both
terminations.
We discuss implications for other CrSb
surfaces in the Supplement [17].
The method has also consequences in interpretation
of valence band spectra from AMs, because they can
also be deconvoluted into orbital weights, and therefore
exhibit additional surface-termination-specific magnetic
contrasts due to PED.
SUMMARY AND OUTLOOK
In summary, we proposed multiplet-selective photo-
electron diffraction as a real-space probe of AM order
and demonstrated the methodology on the example of
the CrSb(120) surface.
The method exploits the in-
terplay between multiplet-selective core-level photoemis-
sion source waves and inequivalent local scattering en-
vironments of antialigned magnetic sublattices.
Using
multiple-scattering calculations, we demonstrated that
both circularly and linearly polarized light can generate
robust domain-sensitive PED asymmetries when com-
bined with suitable selection of Cr 3p multiplet regions.
The proposed approach differs from conventional core-
level magnetic dichroism because the magnetic contrast
is encoded not only in spin polarization itself, but also
in the real-space diffraction response of inequivalent lo-
cal environments characteristic of AMs.
The method
therefore combines element selectivity, local structural
sensitivity, and compatibility with modern momentum-
resolved photoemission instrumentation.
While CrSb provides a particularly convenient realiza-
tion due to its out-of-plane magnetization and high N´eel
temperature, the general approach should be applicable
to a broader class of NiAs-type altermagnetic and anti-
ferromagnetic compounds, including MnTe and related
transition-metal chalcogenides.

Proposed experimental procedures to determine AM order
In practice the experiment with a hemispherical analyzer (with lens deflector) would be performed in the following
way at the micro-ARPES beamline (beamline where sufficient photon beam microfocusing is available). First, the
surface Brillouin zone orientation need to be understood from the ARPES deflector scans on the area of the surface
that shows sharp features at low background, and sample needs to be aligned for example as described in Fig. S3.
Energy will be fixed in Cr 3p and Sb 4d regions, and lateral maps by rastering the sample surface will be performed
in the fixed mode. Rastering is routinely possible at the micro-ARPES beamlines. Fixed mode allows to measure
entire Cr 3p region in one shot, therefore without losing time. State-of-the-art hemispherical analyzers measure in
”one shot” E(k||) map along ±15◦line in surface Brillouin zone. Analyzer should be adjusted such that this line is
orthogonal to the reaction plane. One can also measure entire deflector map (scanning both parallel directions), but
this would be very time consuming, if the sample is supposed to be rastered.
This way we obtain a set of spectra E(x, y, k||), for different light polarizations, where x and y are the two lateral
surface directions. One compares intensity of Cr 3p and Sb 4d to establish Cr or Sb terminations. Now, knowing the
areas of the same surface termination, from these spectra one can create quantities ∆I%
AM and ∆Is,%
AM as defined in the
main text, which allows to locate AM domains. Once the domains are located one can follow up with spin-polarized
ARPES on single domains, to visualize the spin polarized surface and bulk projected bands.
Similar procedure can be applied in case of measurements performed with momentum microscopes, where it would
be beneficial to first locate energies E1 and E2, that is regions in the Cr 3p multiplet where C±-derived changes at
maximized (scanning Ekin is time-consuming with momentum microscopes).

In PED one often uses a picture of a spherical wave being emitted from one site and being scattered by neighboring
sites [8], which somewhat resembles time-dependent DFT attempts for calculating ARPES spectra [27]. However, in

Computational parameters
Bulk CrSb calculations were performed using the WIEN2k package [14] within density functional theory, including
spin–orbit coupling.
The calculations employed 84,854 k-points in the irreducible part of the magnetic Brillouin
zone and RKmax = 9, while all remaining parameters were kept at their default WIEN2k values. The experimental
lattice constants a = 4.103 ˚A and c = 5.463 ˚A were used. No additional Coulomb interaction term (U) was in-
cluded. The resulting magnetic moments inside the Cr muffin-tin spheres were approximately ±2.80, µB for the bulk
antiferromagnetic structure.
In addition, an Sb-terminated CrSb(120) slab was calculated. In this case the magnetic moment of the surface-most
Cr atom increased to approximately 3.08, µB, reflecting the reduced coordination at the surface.
