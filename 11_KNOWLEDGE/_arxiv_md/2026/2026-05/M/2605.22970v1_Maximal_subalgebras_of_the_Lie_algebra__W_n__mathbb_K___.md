MAXIMAL SUBALGEBRAS OF THE LIE ALGEBRA Wn(K)
Y.CHAPOVSKYI, A.PETRAVCHUK, O.TYSHCHENKO
Abstract. Let K be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, A = K[x1, . . . , xn]
the polynomial ring in n variables, and let Wn(K) be the Lie algebra of all K-derivations of
A. This Lie algebra also is the free A-module of rank n over the ring A, so every subalgebra of
Wn(K) has a rank ≤n over A. We prove that every maximal subalgebra of rank ≤n of Wn(K)
is a simple Lie algebra. If a maximal subalgebra L ⊂Wn(K) has rank n and is a submodule
of Wn(K) then L is not simple. Moreover, L is of the form L = {D ∈Wn(K) | D(I) ⊆I}
for some ideal I of the ring A. It is also proved that, for a simple derivation D on the ring
K[x, y], the subalgebra K[x, y]D is a maximal subalgebra of W2(K).

Theorem 1. Let M be a maximal subalgebra of the Lie algebra Wn with rkM = k < n. Then
(1) the only M-invariant ideals of the polynomial ring A are A and 0.
(2) M is a simple subalgebra of the Lie algebra Wn.
Proof. (1) Assume, to the contrary, that there exists a nonzero ideal I of the ring A, I ̸= A
such that M(I) ⊆I. Denote by M1 the Lie subalgebra of the form
M1 = {D ∈Wn | D(I) ⊆I}.
Obviously M ⊆M1. Note that M1 ̸= Wn because there exist no proper nonzero ideals I such
that Wn(I) ⊆I. It follows from the maximality of the subalgebra M that M = M1. Fix
any point p ∈Kn \ V (I), where V (I) is the affine algebraic variety with the defining ideal I.
By Lemma 1, for any vector ⃗u = (u1, . . . , un) ∈Kn there exists a derivation D ∈M1, such
that vD(p) = ⃗u. The latter means that {vD(p) | D ∈M1} = Kn. On the other hand, M is of
rank k < n over A, so the vector space {vD(p) | D ∈M} is obviously of dimension ≤k. The
obtained contradiction proves the part (1) of the theorem.
(2) Since the subalgebra M admits no proper non-zero invariant ideals of the polynomial
ring A we have by [6], Theorem 3, that M is a simple Lie algebra.

3. On maximal subalgebras of rank n of Wn
In this section, we consider maximal subalgebras of Wn of maximal rank. Note that every
finite-dimensional maximal subalgebra M of Wn is of rank n. Indeed, suppose, to the contrary,
that rkM = k < n. Then AM is of rank k and dim AM = ∞. Therefore M ⊊AM. Of course,
AM ̸= Wn since these algebras have distinct ranks over A. The latter contradicts maximality
of M and obtained contradiction shows that rkM = n. We also need some results concerning
the following subalgebras of Wn, which were studied in [9].
Definition 1. ([9]).
A Lie subalgebra L ⊆Wn(K) with dimK L = n is called a basic
subalgebra of Wn if the A-module AL generated by L coincides with Wn, i.e., AL = Wn(K).
Proposition 1 (Corollary 1, [9]). If L is a nilpotent or semisimple basic subalgebra of the
Lie algebra Wn(K), then L consists of divergence-free derivations.
Lemma 6. Let M be a finite-dimensional maximal subalgebra of Wn(K), where n ≥2. Then
every non-zero ideal of the Lie algebra M has rank n. In particular, M is not solvable.
Proof. Suppose that I is an ideal of M and that rk(I) = k < n. Consider the subalgebra
MI = M +AI. Then M ⊊MI because MI is an infinite dimensional subalgebra of Wn. Since
M is a maximal subalgebra of Wn we obtain MI = Wn. But [M, AI] ⊆AI, so AI is an ideal of
Wn = MI. This equality implies AI = Wn which is impossible because rk(AI) = k < n. The
obtained contradiction shows that rk(I) = n. If there existed a finite-dimensional solvable
maximal Lie subalgebra, then by Lie theorem it would have a one-dimensional ideal. By the
above mentioned this is impossible. Therefore every finite-dimensional maximal Lie algebra
is not solvable.
□
Theorem 3. Let M be a finite dimensional maximal subalgebra of the Lie algebra Wn. Then
dim M ≥n + 1.
Proof. First assume dim M = k < n. Then rkM ≤k < n. Consider the A-submodule (which
is also a subalgebra) AM = K[x1, . . . , xn]M. This subalgebra is strictly contained in Wn,
since rk(AM) = rkM < n. Furthermore, AM strictly contains M, because dim M < ∞
while AM is infinite-dimensional (as M ̸= 0). Thus, we have the chain M ⊊AM ⊊Wn,
which means M is not maximal. The latter contradicts the conditions of the lemma, therefore
dim M = k ≥n
Now, let dim M = n. If AM ̸= Wn, then, similarly to the case dim M < n, M is not
maximal, as M ⊊AM ⊊Wn. Therefore, AM = Wn, that is, M is a basic subalgebra of the
Lie algebra Wn. Since dim M = n the subalgebra M is simple by Lemma 6. Denote by L
the subalgebra of Wn consisting of all divergence-free derivations. By Proposition 1, we have
M ⊆L. But L is strictly contained in the subalgebra L1 consisting of all derivations with
constant divergences. The latter means that M is not maximal in Wn which contradicts our
assumption. Therefore dim M ≥n + 1.
