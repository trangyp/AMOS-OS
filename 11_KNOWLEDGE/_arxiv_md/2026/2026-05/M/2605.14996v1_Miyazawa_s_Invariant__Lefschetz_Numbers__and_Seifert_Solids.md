Abstract. We establish a formula expressing Miyazawa’s 2-knot invariant | deg | in terms of the Lefschetz
number of a map on ordinary (i.e., not real) monopole Floer homology. As an application, we deduce that
| deg | = 1 for any 2-knot in S4 which has a punctured L-space as a Seifert solid. In the course of the proof of the
main theorem, we show how Francesco Lin’s construction of monopole Floer homology with Pin(2)-equivariant
perturbations can be made to work with integer coefficients.
1. Introduction
The study of real Seiberg–Witten gauge theory has garnered much interest recently due to the apparent
potential for applications to the study of exoticism in smooth 4-dimensional topology. Of particular inter-
est is the Z≥0-valued real Bauer-Furuta degree invariant | deg |, which is defined for certain 4-manifolds
equipped with real spinc structures, and specializes to an invariant of 2-knots and RP2-knots embedded
in S4 by way of their double branched covers with their unique real spin structures. The invariant | deg |,
derived from the real Bauer-Furuta invariant defined in [KMT25], was used in [Miy23] to prove the ex-
istence of smoothly exotic, topologically unknotted embeddings RP2 ,→S4. Despite the recent attention
this invariant has received, methods for computing the invariant are sparse and it remains largely unclear
what type of information it contains.
Let S : S2 ,→S4 be a (smooth) 2-knot. Suppose that Y◦,→S4 is a Seifert solid for S, i.e., an orientable
hypersurface with ∂Y◦= S, and let Y be the corresponding closed 3-manifold. We can associate to S, Y a
cobordism W : Y →Y, as follows. Let X = X(S) be the 4-manifold obtained from surgery on S4 along S.
Then, we take W to be X-cut-along-Y. The homology of X is that of S1 × S3, and so there exists a unique
double cover ˜X →X. Let sX be the unique spinc structure on X and let sY, sW be the spinc structures
restricted to Y, W, respectively. The double cover ˜X can be built explicitly by gluing two copies of W end
to end. There is a unique real spinc structure on ˜X, and the underlying spinc structure is that pulled back
from X. The invariant | deg | is defined, and | deg( ˜X)| = | deg(S)| [MPT25, Proposition 7.3]. In the case
that Y is a rational homology sphere, we will express the invariant | deg(S)| in terms of a cobordism map
induced by W on the “ordinary” monopole Floer homology of Y, twisted by an involution ȷ∗. The spinc
structure sY is induced by a spin structure, and the involution ȷ∗is the complex conjugation isomorphism
induced by ¯sY ∼= sY. Our main result is the following.

embedded oriented rational homology 3-sphere Y ,→X representing a generator of H3(X; Z) ∼= Z. Let W be the

The majority of this paper is aimed at proving Theorem 1. As an immediate corollary to Theorem 1, we
observe that | deg | restricts the topology of Seifert solids which a 2-knot can bound. To our knowledge,
this is the first result of its type for invariants coming from real Seiberg–Witten theory.
Corollary 1.1. If a 2-knot S : S2 ,→S4 bounds a punctured L-space embedded in S4, then | deg(S)| = 1.
Our Theorem 1 is a real analogue of [LRS18, Theorem A], which gives a formula relating the count of
ordinary Seiberg–Witten solutions on a homology S1 × S3 to the Lefschetz number of the map induced
by a cobordism in a similar way. Unlike in [LRS18], our formula does not see the appearance of either a
Frøyshov-invariant-type term nor a metric correction term. The metric correction term does not appear for
the simple reason that the real irreducible is generically transverse; indeed, that this is possible is one of
the main points of departure of real Seiberg–Witten theory from the ordinary theory. The Frøyshov term
does not appear due to the form of the monopole chain complex for a Pin(2)-equivariant perturbation;
see Subsection 4.4.
The first technical input to the proof of Theorem 1 is a chain-level definition of ȷ∗that preserves the
perturbation. This is dealt with in Section 3, where we show how Francesco Lin’s construction of monopole
Floer homology with Pin(2)-equivariant perturbations [Lin18] can be upgraded from a theory with Z/2-
coefficients to a theory with Z-coefficients. Once this is accomplished, we can define ȷ as the involution
induced by j ∈Pin(2).
The second technical input to the proof of Theorem 1 is the comparison in Section 4 of various Seiberg–
Witten moduli spaces and their orientations, based on that in [LRS18]. As part of the appeal of real
Seiberg–Witten theory is its apparent departure from the ordinary theory, it may seem counterintuitive to
try to interpret the real invariant in terms of an ordinary one. The bridge across this gap is the relationship
between real monopoles on double covers and ordinary spinc−monopoles on the base.
Theorem 1 applies to 2-knots which bound rational homology balls. In Section 5, we give some exam-
ples of 2-knots which satisfy this hypothesis and some which do not, and we compare some computations
of | deg | with known results.

3. Monopole Floer Homology with Pin(2)-Equivariant Perturbations
In this section we define monopole Floer homology with Z coefficients using Pin(2)-equivariant per-
turbations by adding appropriate orientations to the characteristic 2 construction of [Lin18]. This will
allow us to work with a chain-level lift of the complex conjugation action ȷ on monopole Floer homology.
Throughout, Y will be a 3-manifold and sY a spinc structure on Y. Additional hypotheses will be added
as appropriate.

5. Examples
Our main theorem applies to smooth 2-knots in S4 which bound rational homology balls. The set of
smooth 2-knots in S4 is far from classified and likely far from classifiable. Even if we restrict attention to
the class of 2-knots which bound rational homology balls, there is likely no hope of classification. Further,
it is not clear whether this is a particularly large class.
The most famous and well-studied class of 2-knots consists of the spun knots and their cousins-in-law,
the deform-spun knots; see [Lit79]. Suppose K ,→S3 is a knot and γ an untwisted deformation of K. If
m ̸= 0, then the twisted deform-spun knot τmγK ,→S4 is fibered. The fiber is typically, but not always, a
rational homology ball. When γ is the identity, the fiber of the twist-spin τmK is the m-fold cyclic branched
cover Σm(K) and when γ = ρn is a roll, the fiber of the twist-roll-spin τmρnK is a Dehn surgery along a

nullhomologous knot in Σm(K) with nonzero framing. As a consequence of [Fox56, §6], the fiber of τmρnK
is a rational homology ball if and only if the Alexander polynomial ∆K does not have zeroes at any m-th
roots of unity.
For pure untwisted deformations (i.e., when m = 0), the corresponding deform-spun knots typically
do not bound rational homology balls. Primary topological obstructions to a 2-knot bounding a ratio-
nal homology ball come from the Alexander module.
Recall [CKS04, §3.3.1] that the (first) Alexander
module A(K) of a knot K in any dimension is the relative first homology of the infinite cyclic cover,
H1(X∞(K), π−1(x); Z), considered as a module over the ring ΛZ = Z[T−1, T] of Laurent polynomials. If
K bounds a rational homology ball, then A(K) ⊗Q = 0. Let ∆(K) ⊆ΛZ denote the Alexander ideal of
K, i.e., the first elementary ideal of A(K). Then, A(K) ⊗Q = 0 if and only if ∆(K) ⊗Q = ΛQ. For a
knot K ,→S3, the ideal ∆(K) is principal, generated by the Alexander polynomial ∆K(T). The Alexander
polynomial of K is monic. Thus, for 1-knots, the condition that A(K) is Z-torsion amounts to ∆(K) = 1.
Let us see how the Alexander module obstructs twist-roll-spun knots from bounding rational homology
balls. We start with the purely roll-spun knots.

S2(a1, . . . , an). Suppose Y is equipped with its Thurston geometry. Suppose further that |H1(Y; Z)| is odd,
and equip Y with its unique spin structure sY.
Position the cone points of O along the equator of S2. Note that this implicitly fixes an order of the
cone points up to cyclic and anti-cyclic permutations. Then, complex conjugation on CP1 induces an
orientation-reversing involution of O which fixes the cone points. This involution lifts to an orientation-
preserving involution ιY (the Montesinos involution) on Y which reverses the circle action, realizing Y as
the double branched cover of a Montesinos link in S3. The involution ιY in turn induces an involution ιY∗
on the various flavors of monopole Floer homology (preserving the part coming from the spin structure),
which turns out to be the same as the complex conjugation involution ȷ∗. For Seifert fibered manifolds,
the involution ιY∗= ȷ∗can be understood geometrically at the level of spinors using the description of the
moduli spaces in [MOY97], or combinatorially as the involution induced by reflection in a graded root as
in [Dai18]
Consider the mapping torus X = S1 ×ιY Y. The unique double cover of X is ˜X = S1 × Y with deck
transformation ι ˜X(eiθ, y) = (ei(θ+π), ιY(y)).
Since X is a homology S1 × S3 with a separating rational
homology sphere, we can apply Theorem 1 to compute | deg( ˜X)|. The cobordism W is the cylinder I × Y
twisted by the involution ιY. We have W∗= ιY∗= ȷ∗and so ȷ∗(W∗−1) = 1 −ιY∗. If we pass to R
coefficients, the Lefschetz number of 1 −ιY∗is twice the Euler characteristic of the ιY∗-anti-invariant part.
Theorem 1 gives
| deg( ˜X)| = |1 + 4χ(HM(Y, sY; R)−ιY∗)|
As a specific family of examples, consider the Brieskorn homology spheres Y = Σ(2, 3, r) where (6, r) =
1. The (in this case unique) Montesinos link associated to Y is a knot K = K(2, 3, r), and | deg( ˜X)| =
| deg(K)|, where for a knot K, | deg(K)| is defined to be the count of real Seiberg-Witten solutions over
the double branched cover with its unique real spin structure. If r = 12k + 1 or 12k + 5 where k ≥0, then
HM(Y, sY) is free of rank 2k concentrated in even Z/2 grading and ιY∗acts by freely permuting a basis.
