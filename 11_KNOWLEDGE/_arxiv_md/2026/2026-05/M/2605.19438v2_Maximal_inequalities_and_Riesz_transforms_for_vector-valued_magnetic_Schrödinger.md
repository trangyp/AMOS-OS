Abstract. We consider vector-valued magnetic Schr¨odinger operators −∆a +V with
magnetic potential a ∈L2

1. Introduction
Vector-valued Schr¨odinger operators of the form −∆+ V in Lp(Rd; Rm), p ∈[1, ∞),
for a suitable class of symmetric matrix-valued electric potentials V are studied in [1].
The aim there is to prove maximal inequalities for the operator −∆+ V and generation
results for the semigroups. More precisely, it is shown that the realization Tp of ∆−
V in Lp(Rd; Rm), p ∈[1, ∞), with maximal domain, generates a strongly continuous
semigroup, which is also analytic for p ∈(1, ∞). Under reverse H¨older estimates on
the minimal eigenvalue of V , it is also proved that the domain of Tp coincides with the
minimal domain in Lp(Rd; Rm) for p ∈[1, ∞).
The purpose of the present article is twofold. Firstly, we consider more general second
order differential operators, including vector-valued Schr¨odinger operators with magnetic
fields. Secondly, we improve the results from [1] by removing some assumptions on the
potential. Our approach, which is quite different from that of the aforementioned article,
yields more precise estimates. In addition, we study the boundedness on Lp(Rd; Cm) for
the Riesz transforms (∇−ia)(−∆a + V )−1
2 and V α(−∆a + V )−α.

potential-free estimates for the Riesz transforms of magnetic Schr¨odinger operators, with
no restriction on the dimension. To the best of our knowledge, this result is new. We
believe that one can adapt the proof from [8], based on singular integrals, to our general
vector-valued context.
However, the approach we adopt here gives some additional
information and in particular it provides that the estimates of the Riesz transform depend
on V and possibly on the dimension only through the constant κ in (1.2). We mention
that the question of the Riesz transform for Schr¨odinger operators has been studied
before in the scalar-valued case. In fact, the cases p ∈(1, 2] and p = 1 (in a weak-type
sense) were treated in [31], using the wave equation method and in [9] using Hardy space
techniques. The papers [5, 6] treat electric potential satisfying reverse H¨older estimates,
following the approach of [3] and [30] in the absence of a magnetic field.
The zero-
order Riesz type transforms V α(−∆+ V )−α were considered in [20] in the scalar-valued
case and nonnegative potentials V . We follow and adapt some of their arguments to
our general setting. The boundedness on Lp(Rd; C) of the zero-order Riesz transform is
proved in [20] by Stein’s complex interpolation theorem. We provide in the appendix
a vector-valued counterpart of this well-known result.
It is worth emphasizing that,
when working with complex interpolation techniques, the boundedness of the imaginary
powers of the operator A in Lp-spaces plays a crucial role. This property follows from a
recent result in [2], combined with the fact that the semigroup generated by A satisfies
Gaussian-type estimates.
Organization of the paper. In Section 2, we define the operator A via the theory of
sesquilinear forms. We also establish a key vector-valued result on the magnetic gradient,
which allows us to prove, in Section 3, the diamagnetic inequality. Maximal inequalities
in Lp(Rd; Cm) are established in Section 4, assuming some reverse H¨older estimates on
λV when p > 1. The case p = 1 plays a fundamental role in Section 5, where we prove
the boundedness of the Riesz transform (∇−ia)A−1
2 in Lp(Rd; Cm) for p ∈(1, 2] under
additional assumptions on a. We stress that such additional assumptions enable us to
prove the Kato’s distributional inequality for the magnetic Laplacian, obtained in [17]

result relies on a vector-valued Stein’s complex interpolation theorem which we present
