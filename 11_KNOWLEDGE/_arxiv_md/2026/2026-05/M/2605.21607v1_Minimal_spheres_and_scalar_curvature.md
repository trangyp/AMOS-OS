MINIMAL SPHERES AND SCALAR CURVATURE
TALANT TALIPOV
Abstract. In 1982, S.-T. Yau conjectured that there exist four distinct embedded
minimal two-spheres in any manifold diffeomorphic to S3. Wang–Zhou confirmed this
conjecture for Riemannian three-spheres when the metric is bumpy or has positive
Ricci curvature. We prove the following quantitative version of their theorem. Suppose
that (S3, g) has positive Ricci curvature and scalar curvature Rg ≥Λ0 > 0. Then
there exist four distinct embedded minimal two-spheres Σ1, . . . , Σ4 ⊂(S3, g) such
that areag(Σi) ≤12π(i + 1)/Λ0 for every i = 1, . . . , 4.
We apply this result to a
problem posed by S.-T. Yau in 1987 on whether the planar two-spheres are the only
minimal spheres in ellipsoids centered at the origin in R4. Haslhofer–Ketover proved
that ellipsoids with one sufficiently large semi-axis contain at least one non-planar
embedded minimal two-sphere. We prove that such ellipsoids contain at least three
non-planar embedded minimal two-spheres.
1. Introduction
In 1982, S.-T. Yau posed the following conjecture.
Conjecture 1.1 (S.-T. Yau [38, Problem 89]). There exist four distinct embedded
minimal two-spheres in any manifold diffeomorphic to S3.
Simon–Smith [34] proved the existence of at least one embedded minimal two-sphere
in S3 with an arbitrary metric; see also Colding–De Lellis [10], De Lellis–Pellandini
[12], and Ketover [19]. Later, White [36], using degree methods, proved the existence
of at least two embedded minimal two-spheres when the metric has positive Ricci cur-
vature, and at least four embedded minimal two-spheres when the metric is sufficiently
close to the round metric.
Using the catenoid estimate of Ketover–Marques–Neves
[20], Haslhofer–Ketover [14] proved the existence of at least two embedded minimal
two-spheres for bumpy metrics. We also remark that branched immersed minimal two-
spheres were obtained by Sacks–Uhlenbeck [30] using min-max theory for harmonic
maps; see also Colding–Minicozzi [11].
In a breakthrough paper, Wang–Zhou [35]
proved the existence of at least four embedded minimal two-spheres when the met-
ric is bumpy or has positive Ricci curvature, thus confirming Conjecture 1.1 in these
cases.
The main result of this paper is the following.

In 1987, S.-T. Yau asked the following question.
Question 1.3 (S.-T. Yau [39, Section 4]). Are the only minimal two-spheres in an
ellipsoid centered about the origin in R4 the planar ones?
Almgren [1] proved that in round spheres, the only immersed minimal two-spheres are
planar. White [37] proved that when a, b, c, and d are sufficiently close to each other,
all minimal two-spheres in E(a, b, c, d) are planar. In [14], Haslhofer–Ketover proved
that for fixed b, c and d, if a is chosen sufficiently large, then the ellipsoid E(a, b, c, d)
contains a non-planar embedded minimal two-sphere, thus obtaining a negative answer
to Question 1.3. Bettiol–Piccione [4] proved that if at least two of the semi-axes b, c, d
are equal, then there are arbitrarily many distinct non-planar embedded minimal two-
spheres in E(a, b, c, d), provided a is sufficiently large. As a corollary of Theorem 1.2,
we further address Question 1.3 and obtain the following result.
Theorem 1.4. For fixed b, c and d, if a is chosen sufficiently large, then the ellipsoid
E(a, b, c, d) contains at least three non-planar embedded minimal two-spheres.
