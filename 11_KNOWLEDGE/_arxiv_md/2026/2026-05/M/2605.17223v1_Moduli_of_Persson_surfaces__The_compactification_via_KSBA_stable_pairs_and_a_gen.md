MODULI OF PERSSON SURFACES:
THE COMPACTIFICATION VIA KSBA STABLE PAIRS
AND A GENERIC GLOBAL TORELLI TYPE THEOREM
HANLONG FANG, BIN NGUYEN, XIAN WU AND ZHENG ZHANG
Abstract. We study a family of canonically polarized surfaces introduced by
Persson, which arise as Galois G = (Z/2Z)4-covers of P2 branched along eight
general lines. For this family, we construct the compactified moduli space and ex-
plicitly describe the stable degenerations in the sense of Koll´ar, Shepherd-Barron,
and Alexeev (KSBA) via stable pairs of weighted hyperplane arrangements. By
computing the Q-Gorenstein obstructions and using the KSBA wall crossings, we
show that the resulting compactified moduli stack is smooth. Furthermore, we
establish a generic global Torelli type result: up to two possibilities, a generic
smooth Persson surface can be recovered from the Hodge structure on the anti-
invariant part of the second cohomology of its ´etale double cover, together with
the associated eG = (Z/2Z)5-action.

For surfaces of general types, due to Todorov’s famous counterexample in [Tod81],
global Torelli fails, that is, the surface cannot be determined by its Hodge structure
even up to finitely many possibilities. In this paper, we identify an appropriate
Hodge structure for each Persson surface that determines the surface up to two
possibilities. The construction of our period map and the approach to the generic
global Torelli problem are inspired by the following three ingredients.
(1) Global Torelli for Enriques surfaces: the correct Hodge structures determin-
ing the geometry are the orthogonal part of the second cohomology of the
´etale double cover K3 surfaces; see Horikawa [Hor77].
(2) Global Torelli for Prym varieties: the works Friedman–Smith [FS82] and
Kanev [Kan83] help us to specify the objects parametrized by the source
moduli of the period map; in particular, a choice of a 2-torsion line bundle
is needed.
(3) Global Torelli for a special family of Horikawa surfaces: Pearlstein–Zhang
in [PZ19] proved the generic global Torelli for a family of bidouble covers of
P2 (see Example 2.2), with respect to Pardini’s (Z/2Z)2-period map. The
key is the two intermediate K3 surfaces appearing in the bidouble cover.
Specifically, we consider the ´etale double covers ZL →X induced by a 2-torsion
line bundle L in Pic(X)[2] ∼= (Z/2Z)3 (see Lemma 2.6). The surface ZL is an abelian
cover of P2 with Galois group eG = (Z/2Z)5, branched along the same eight lines
as X, with its building data explicitly given in Proposition 4.1.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we analyze the geometric struc-
tures of smooth Persson surfaces. We compute the numerical invariants and the in-
tersection form lattice. We discuss the Picard group, and compute the dimension of
the deformation space. We interpret the G∗-eigenspaces via intermediate surfaces.
In Section 3.1, we briefly review the theory in [Ale15], then describe the KSBA
moduli and degenerations, of both the b-weighted line arrangements and Persson
surfaces. We also prove the smoothness of the compactified stack and classify singu-
larities appearing on stable Persson surfaces. For our weighted line arrangements,
we show that there is a morphism from the KSBA moduli to the standard GIT
moduli. In Section 3.2, we define another compactified moduli of Persson surfaces,
based on Oudompheng’s work on the moduli of a special family of Enriques surfaces,
then study its relation to the KSBA moduli. In Section 4.1, we construct a family of
surfaces whose cohomologies encode the Hodge structures capturing the geometry
of Persson surfaces. In Section 4.2, we discuss the monodromy of the eG-period map,
and prove that the eG-period map is generically of degree 2.
Throughout this paper, we work over the complex number field C.

4. Global Torelli problem with respect to Pardini’s period map
This section is devoted to investigating the generic global Torelli problem for
Persson surfaces. While our approach parallels that of [PZ19], a key distinction
is that no K3 surfaces appear within the covering tower of a Persson surface (see
Remark 2.4).
To address this, we instead utilize ´etale double covers of Persson
surfaces, as detailed in Section 4.1. In Section 4.2, we demonstrate that the Hodge
structure on the anti-invariant part is determined by four degree 2 K3 surfaces.
Building on this, we introduce a eG-period map and establish that it has generic
degree 2 for the abelian group eG = (Z/2Z)5.

(2) One may manually construct a degree 1 eG-period map by enlarging the period
domain.
For instance, instead of using a single ´etale double cover ZL, one can
simultaneously consider all seven double covers arising from Pic(X)[2]. By doing
so, the ambiguity caused by the transposition si in the proof of Theorem 4.16 would
be eliminated. However, we do not pursue this modified eG-period map here, since
such a construction looks not very natural.
