We present a minimalist, symmetry-guided interaction for microscopic cluster models based on
Wigner-SU(4) symmetry. Retaining only an SU(4)-invariant two-body attraction and a local three-
body repulsion, this framework is implemented via the generator coordinate method (GCM) to
describe α–α scattering phase shifts, the low-lying spectrum and transition properties of 12C, and
the cluster spectrum of 16O. We show that the long-standing structural tension between the 12C and
16O ground states can be mitigated within this restricted SU(4) operator space without introduc-
ing additional phenomenological complexity. These results indicate that Wigner-SU(4) symmetry
provides an effective organizing principle for Nα clustering, offering a more fundamental baseline
for understanding complex cluster structures.
Introduction— The microscopic description of cluster-
ing in light nuclei, particularly within Nα systems, is
a long-standing yet actively evolving frontier in nuclear
many-body physics [1–3]. It encompasses a rich array of
phenomena, providing essential insights into stellar nu-
cleosynthesis [4], high-energy heavy-ion collisions [5–9],
and exotic states analogous to the Bose-Einstein conden-
sate (BEC) [10, 11]. As illustrated by the Ikeda diagram
[12], developed Nα cluster structures emerge near the
breakup thresholds. Microscopic cluster models, notably
the GCM, serve as a versatile framework for investigating
these α-cluster structures [13, 14].
However, a persistent difficulty remains in the micro-
scopic description of Nα clustering. Originally proposed
in 1965, the Volkov interaction [15] remains highly pop-
ular in nuclear cluster models for describing Nα sys-
tems [3], as it successfully reproduces the energy spec-
trum and related observables of 12C [16, 17]. Yet this
effective nucleon-nucleon interaction completely fails to
reproduce the 4α cluster structure of 16O. To address
this deficiency, various improved interactions have been
developed over the years, including finite-range three-
body effective forces [18, 19], extended Volkov-type inter-
actions with additional Bartlett and Heisenberg exchange
terms [20], and realistic-interaction-based potentials [21].
Despite these advancements, achieving a unified descrip-
tion of the 3α and 4α systems remains a major obstacle.
Specifically, these potentials struggle to simultaneously
reconcile the highly developed, dilute 3α cluster config-
urations in 12C with the compact, shell-model-like 4α
ground state of 16O [21–25]. Employing more microscopic
or even bare nuclear interactions might appear to be a
preferable alternative [26–28]. Nevertheless, the inherent
complexity of such chiral interactions not only imposes
prohibitive computational demands, but also makes them
exceedingly difficult to adapt into the conventional clus-
ter model framework, which remains a prominent chal-
lenge today [29].
A possible way to understand this structural duality
lies in the fundamental symmetries of nuclear forces. In
recent years, while chiral effective field theory (ChEFT)
has seen widespread success in nuclear structure calcula-
tions [26–28, 30], a deeper structural insight emerges from
the large-Nc limit of quantum chromodynamics (QCD).
In this limit, the leading-order (LO) nucleon-nucleon
interaction naturally respects Wigner-SU(4) symmetry
[31–33]. Recent advancements in ab initio nuclear lat-
tice effective field theory (NLEFT) have shown that
SU(4)-symmetric interactions provide an efficient start-
ing point for describing spatial correlations and cluster-
ing in light nuclei [34–37]. Alternatively, this emergent
Wigner-SU(4) symmetry can also be viewed as a conse-
quence of the interplay between the unitary and chiral
limits [38].
In this paper, we construct a minimal Wigner-SU(4)-
symmetric effective interaction tailored to microscopic
cluster models.
Implemented in the GCM framework,
this Hamiltonian captures the main low-lying structures
of α-conjugate nuclei.
By comparing two representa-
tive parametrizations within the same restricted SU(4)-
symmetric operator space, we show that this minimal
structure helps reduce the persistent structural tension
between 12C and 16O without introducing additional phe-
nomenological operators. This supports Wigner-SU(4)
symmetry as a practical leading baseline for microscopic
studies of Nα systems.

Results and discussion— With only two parameters,
C and D, plus the resolution scale Λ, the interaction is
determined by two strategies, summarized in Table I. The
first strategy, denoted SU4A, anchors the parameters to
the ground-state energies of 4He and 12C at an optimized
resolution scale of Λ = 500 MeV (as detailed in the Sup-
plemental Material [43]). The second strategy, denoted
SU4B, represents a more standard and comprehensive
approach for fixing effective interactions in microscopic
cluster models. By simultaneously considering both the
3α and 4α systems, C and D are reoptimized to balance
the overall ground-state energies of 12C and 16O. This
dual approach decouples the physical capabilities of the
minimal SU(4) operator structure from the limitations
of fitting parameters to a single mass region.

Using the two interactions, we calculate the low-lying
spectra and transition rates of 12C via the GCM superpo-
sition of 695 3α Brink configurations. The results, sum-
marized in Fig. 2 and Table II, are compared with those
of ab initio NLEFT [36], REM [50], THSR [51, 52], and
experiment [47–49]. As illustrated in Fig. 2, our method
provides a good description of the 2+
1 state energy com-
pared to conventional phenomenological interactions. It
is well known that traditional cluster model interactions
underestimate this 2+
1 excitation energy — a chronic is-
sue that typically requires advanced frameworks like an-
tisymmetrized molecular dynamics (AMD) to partially
resolve. Furthermore, the observables derived from the
wave functions are in agreement with experimental data;
for example, the charge radius of the ground state and
the E0 transition matrix element from the structurally
sensitive Hoyle (0+
2 ) state are well reproduced. Overall,
the two parametrizations give a reasonable description of
the available 12C observables and remain broadly consis-
tent with other microscopic cluster calculations. How-
ever, while a precise description of 12C is encouraging, it
is not entirely surprising for a well-calibrated model; the
true, highly anticipated test of our framework lies in its
ability to capture the complex 4α dynamics of 16O.
A major obstacle for microscopic cluster models in ac-
curately describing the 4α cluster structure of 16O stems
directly from the deficiencies of the underlying interac-
tions. The 16O calculation superposes 795 Brink-GCM
basis states, including 4α and 12C+α configurations. As
shown in Fig. 3, traditional interactions like the Volkov-
No.2 potential fail to reproduce the correct threshold be-
havior, placing the 12C + α threshold far too deeply (de-
viating by more than 7.7 MeV relative to the experimen-
tal placement). Even the Tohsaki-F1 force suffers from
a severe overbinding of the 0+
1 ground state, overshoot-
ing the experimental level by roughly 5.6 MeV. Relative
to the 4α threshold, SU4A gives E(0+
1 ) ≃−19.1 MeV,
overbinding the ground state by about 4.6 MeV rela-
tive to the experimental value. SU4B moves this state
to E(0+
1 ) ≃−15.6 MeV, leaving a residual deviation of
about 1.2 MeV. At the same time, SU4B largely repro-
duces the placement of the 12C+α threshold and gives a
reasonable description of the low-lying cluster spectrum.
Considering its description of the α–α scattering phase
shifts and the 12C properties, SU4B substantially over-
comes the fundamental limitations of both traditional
and recently improved interactions. These results may
provide potentially useful microscopic input for future
studies of the astrophysical 12C(α, γ)16O reaction [53],
12C and 16O fusion reactions [54], high-energy heavy-ion
collisions for studying light nuclei [55–57], and the search
for new highly excited cluster states above the 4α thresh-
old [58].
As established in the seminal works of Wigner [31]
and Volkov [15], the interplay between SU(4) symme-
try and the Pauli principle dictates the maximum spa-
tial symmetry in the ground states of α-conjugate nu-
clei.
Our Hamiltonian produces clustered structures
through two mechanisms: an attractive two-body term
that leads to spatially correlated Nα clusters [59], and a
local three-body repulsion that ensures saturation. Low-
energy α-clustering is dominated by such symmetric con-
figurations.
By excluding explicit SU(4)-breaking ex-
change and non-central terms, the present central inter-
action provides a clean baseline for examining the SU(4)-
symmetric contribution to low-energy Nα dynamics.
As a further test of transferability, we apply the
present interaction to the open-shell 5α system 20Ne. In
the GCM-Brink calculations, the SU4A parametrization
yields a ground-state energy Egs = −161.80 MeV and a
charge radius rc = 2.93 fm, which show reasonable agree-
ment with the experimental values of −160.65 MeV and
3.0055(21) fm.
Conversely, SU4B produces an under-
bound ground state (Egs = −157.56 MeV, rc = 3.08 fm)
and a noticeably compressed low-lying spectrum. Com-
pared to traditional effective interactions, such as the
Volkov force—which fails to reproduce both the binding
energy and the charge radius of 20Ne even with adjusted
Majorana parameters — the SU4A result demonstrates
the potential of the minimal SU(4) framework. The ob-
served discrepancies, particularly for SU4B, suggest that
while this minimal interaction captures the bulk features
of Nα dynamics, its precision in heavier open-shell sys-
tems may be sensitive to the globally fixed harmonic-
oscillator size parameter b = 1.46 fm. A dynamic adjust-

Summary— In summary, we have developed a Wigner-
SU(4)-symmetric interaction with a two-body attraction
and a three-body repulsion, and applied it to α-conjugate
nuclei from 4He to 20Ne.
By examining two represen-
tative parametrizations of the same interaction form,
SU4A and SU4B, we show that this compact Hamiltonian
describes key features of α–α scattering, the low-lying
structure of 12C, and the cluster spectrum of 16O. The
comparison between SU4A and SU4B indicates that the
long-standing 12C–16O structural tension can be reduced

without introducing additional SU(4)-breaking opera-
tors. Calculations up to 20Ne further indicate that this
symmetry-guided interaction captures important bulk
features of Nα dynamics. Future work should test how
far this interaction can be extended to reaction observ-
ables and heavier α-conjugate systems, and a broader
range of nuclear systems, including self-conjugate odd-
odd nuclei and isotopic chains.
