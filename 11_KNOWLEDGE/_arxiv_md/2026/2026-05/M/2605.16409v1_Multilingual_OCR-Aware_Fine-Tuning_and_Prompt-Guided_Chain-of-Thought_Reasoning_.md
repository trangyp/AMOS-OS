Abstract
Optical character recognition (OCR) and multilingual text understanding remain
major failure modes of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), particularly
in real-world images containing cluttered layouts, small fonts, blur, occlusion, and
complex typography.
We present an OCR-aware multilingual multimodal training framework that com-
bines (i) large-scale synthetic OCR-to-translation data generation, (ii) OCR-aware
supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with LoRA adaptation, and (iii) structured visual
chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting for reasoning under uncertain visual condi-
tions. Using a LLaMA-based multimodal architecture, the proposed framework
substantially improves OCR completeness, multilingual translation accuracy, and
robustness under degraded visual conditions.
Experimental results on multilingual receipts, menus, posters, signs, handwritten
text, and document images demonstrate significantly improved visual-text ground-
ing compared with the baseline model. In particular, the proposed OCR-aware
post-training framework improves extraction of small, blurred, spatially scattered,
and partially occluded text while reducing reliance on language priors under un-
certain OCR conditions. Qualitative comparisons with frontier multimodal sys-
tems, including GPT-5-class and Gemini-family models, further suggest improved
OCR grounding and reduced hallucination under noisy and visually ambiguous
OCR scenarios.
Overall, the results indicate that data-centric OCR-aware multimodal post-training
provides an effective and scalable direction for improving multilingual OCR and
OCR-based visual question answering systems.

Introduction
1.1
Background
Text in images is ubiquitous in real-world environments, including menus, receipts, medication
labels, street signs, posters, and official documents. Correctly reading and translating such text is es-
sential for multimodal assistants and assistive AI systems. However, OCR in natural images remains
fundamentally more challenging than traditional document OCR due to blur, glare, perspective dis-
tortion, partial occlusion, cluttered layouts, and multilingual scripts.
Despite substantial progress in vision–language models (VLMs), including LLaVA, Qwen-VL,
PaLI-X, Kosmos-2, Gemini-family models, LLaMA-family multimodal systems, and GPT-4 and
5-class multimodal models, these systems still frequently hallucinate text, omit crucial tokens, or
generate mistranslations under degraded visual conditions. Even models achieving strong bench-

In practice, we observe several recurring failure modes:
(i) Small or degraded text may be skipped or inaccurately recognized.
(ii) OCR content distributed across multiple image regions may be only partially extracted.
(iii) Partially visible text may be autoregressively “completed” using language priors rather than
grounded visual evidence.
(iv) Numeric values may be hallucinated.
(v) Multilingual translations may drift when OCR uncertainty is high.
Recent frontier multimodal systems partially mitigate some of these issues through explicit visual
reasoning and tool-augmented inference strategies that analyze visible text before generating re-
sponses. However, such reasoning pipelines are often computationally expensive in large-scale set-
tings .
1.2
Objective
This paper investigates whether OCR robustness and multilingual text understanding in multimodal
large language models (MLLMs) can be substantially improved through OCR-aware data curation
and supervised post-training.
We introduce two complementary approaches:
(i) OCR-aware post-training using multilingual OCR datasets with LoRA-based supervised
fine-tuning (SFT).
(ii) A lightweight visual chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting strategy that provides auxiliary
structured reasoning over visible text regions.

Related Work
2.1
OCR-Capable Multimodal Large Language Models
Recent multimodal large language models (MLLMs), including Qwen-VL (Bai 2023),PaLI-X (chen
2023), LLaVA (Liu 2023), Kosmos-2 (Peng 2023), and mPLUG-Owl (Ye 2023), integrate visual
encoders with large language models to enable multimodal understanding and OCR-related rea-
soning. Although these systems demonstrate strong performance on multimodal benchmarks, they
often remain sensitive to visual degradation such as blur, rotation, occlusion, cluttered layouts, and
multilingual text, leading to incomplete OCR extraction and hallucinated text generation.
2.2
Document and Scene-Text Benchmarks
Benchmarks such as TextVQA (Singh 2019) and DocVQA (Mathew 2021) evaluate multimodal
reasoning over scene text and document images. While these datasets significantly advanced OCR-
oriented multimodal evaluation, they under-represent challenging in-the-wild conditions including
severe blur, low-light imaging, perspective distortion, multilingual text mixtures, and scattered OCR
regions commonly observed in mobile-camera environments.
2.3
Visual Reasoning and Chain-of-Thought Prompting
Recent multimodal systems have explored reasoning-guided inference strategies for improving OCR
and visual understanding. Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting (Wei 2022) chain-of-thought and mul-
timodal reasoning frameworks such as MM-ReAct (Yang 2023) and Visual ChatGPT (Wu 2023)
incorporate structured reasoning or external visual tools to improve perception under ambiguous
visual conditions. However, these approaches are often computationally expensive and difficult to
deploy efficiently at scale.

2.4
Data-Centric Multimodal Alignment
Recent work has increasingly emphasized the importance of data-centric post-training, synthetic
instruction generation, and preference optimization for improving multimodal grounding and re-
ducing hallucination (Wang 2023, Rafailov 2023). Our work follows this direction by focusing on
OCR-aware multilingual data curation and supervised multimodal post-training to improve OCR
completeness, translation robustness, and hallucination reduction under degraded visual conditions.

Methodologies
3.1
Model Architecture
We adopt an existing multimodal vision-language model (VLM) architecture inspired by recent
large-scale systems such as AnyMal (Moon et al., 2024), Flamingo (Alayrac et al., 2022), and
BLIP-2 (Li et al., 2023), with LLaMA-3 70B serving as the language backbone. Our work does not
introduce a new multimodal architecture, additional OCR-specific recognition modules, or external
OCR engines.
Given an input image, a frozen MetaCLIP-based ViT encoder (Xu et al., 2023) extracts dense visual
features, which are compressed into a fixed number of visual tokens through a Perceiver-based
resampling module (Jaegle et al., 2021). The visual tokens are aligned with the language embedding
space and concatenated with text tokens before being processed by the LLaMA decoder for end-to-
end multimodal reasoning and generation, as illustrated in Figure 1.
Figure 1: Model architecture: image features are embedded and aligned with text tokens as inputs
to the LLaMA decoder for multimodal generation. During training, the LLaMA and Perceiver
module weights are updated through backpropagation, while the ViT encoder remains frozen. Our
framework does not rely on an external OCR model.
Comparison with traditional OCR-based pipelines.
Conventional vision-language systems of-
ten rely on a modular OCR-centric pipeline, where text understanding is delegated to a dedicated
OCR subsystem. This typically involves a multi-stage process: (i) text detection (e.g., EAST (Zhou
et al., 2017), CRAFT (Baek et al., 2019)), (ii) text recognition (e.g., Tesseract (Smith, 2007), TrOCR
(Li et al., 2021)), and (iii) structured encoding of recognized text (often including bounding boxes
or layout metadata) before feeding into an LLM or downstream reasoning model. In some systems,
external OCR services such as Google Vision OCR are also used as black-box components.

While effective, such pipelines introduce several limitations, including increased latency, error prop-
agation across stages, and sensitivity to noisy or redundant detections (e.g., over-fragmented bound-
ing boxes or spurious text regions). Moreover, decoupling perception from reasoning can hinder
joint optimization across modalities.
Unlike some existing LLaMA3-based OCR systems that incorporate an additional OCR model for
explicit text extraction before LLM reasoning, our framework removes the separate OCR module
and learns OCR capability directly within the end-to-end multimodal model through OCR-aware
post-training. This end-to-end design reduces system complexity, improves robustness to noisy
visual inputs, and enables tighter coupling between visual perception and language reasoning.

gressive decoding. In such cases, the model may partially rely on dominant language priors when
visual evidence is incomplete or noisy.
3.3
Visual CoT Prompting
To improve robustness under these challenging conditions, we additionally incorporate visual
prompt chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning as an auxiliary grounding mechanism. This motivates
the introduction of reasoning-based supervision, where the model is encouraged to explicitly con-
struct intermediate visual evidence before producing the final answer. We implement this using
visual prompt CoT, introducing a latent reasoning trace R:
p(Y | I, q) =

pθ(Y | R, I, q) pθ(R | I, q).

Rather than directly generating the final output from uncertain visual observations, the model first
predicts an intermediate reasoning process:
R ∼pθ(R | I, q),
Y ∼pθ(Y | R, I, q).
(5)
This decomposition breaks the original multimodal reasoning problem into a sequence of simpler
conditional inference steps. Under noisy OCR conditions, directly modeling p(Y | I, q) often
encourages the autoregressive decoder to rely on dominant language priors when visual evidence is
incomplete. By introducing the latent reasoning state R, the model is encouraged to progressively
ground visual evidence before final answer generation, reducing error propagation from uncertain
OCR signals and mitigating hallucination.
While the problem formulation defines an abstract reasoning variable R, in practice we instantiate
R as an explicit language-based reasoning trace generated through structured prompting. Instead
of directly predicting Y from (I, q), the model is guided to produce intermediate reasoning before
answering.
Formally, the inference process can be viewed as:
R = CoT(I, q),
Y = pθ(Y | I, q, R),
(6)
where R is an explicitly generated visual-text reasoning trace induced by the prompt. This decom-
position reduces direct dependence on weak OCR signals by forcing the model to first synthesize
intermediate structured representations of visual text, improving robustness under uncertainty and
reducing hallucination.
Unlike standard system prompts used in supervised fine-tuning (e.g., “You are a helpful visual assis-
tant. ..”), we introduce structured step-by-step instructions that encourage OCR-aware visual rea-
soning:
• Examine the entire image to first understand the overall scene and global context.
• If the question involves small, distant, or off-center objects or text, systematically search
different image regions, including the foreground, background, left, and right areas, while
focusing on potentially relevant details.
• If the text appears blurry, low-contrast, partially occluded, or rotated, reason as if mentally
focusing on, enhancing, or re-orienting the relevant region to improve readability.
• When appropriate, briefly explain the visual evidence or reasoning process used to derive
the answer.
• Finally, provide a clear and precise answer grounded in the observed image evidence.
This structured prompting induces R as an explicit intermediate reasoning trace, effectively decom-
posing the inference process into (I, q) →R →Y . This encourages stronger visual grounding
on OCR content and reduces hallucination caused by reliance on language priors under ambiguous
visual conditions.
3.4
OCR Data Curation
In addition to existing large-scale image-English OCR datasets, we further curate multilingual and
visually challenging OCR scenarios that are relatively underrepresented in conventional OCR cor-
pora.
These include mixed-language composition, complex document layouts, rotated or low-
contrast text, dense OCR regions, and degraded visual conditions commonly encountered in the
wild scenes. To improve coverage of these long-tail OCR conditions, we combine real-world OCR
data with scalable multilingual synthetic generation (∼5M samples).

This formulation explicitly couples visual inputs with underlying OCR text and multilingual super-
vision, ensuring that translation depends on shared latent linguistic content rather than purely visual
correlation.
To bridge the gap between synthetic and real-world OCR conditions, we apply realistic visual degra-
dations such as blur, rotation, occlusion, perspective distortion, compression artifacts, and cluttered
layouts. These transformations simulate failures in p(Z | I), allowing the model to learn robustness
under noisy observation conditions.
Our synthetic pipeline differs from standard text-to-image generation in two key aspects:
• Text-first conditioning: Images are generated conditioned explicitly on sampled multilin-
gual OCR text, enabling controlled variation in typography, layout structure, and language
mixing.
• Context-aware rendering: Backgrounds are generated to remain semantically consistent
with embedded text, ensuring realistic co-occurrence between scene context and OCR con-
tent.
This design induces a training distribution that more closely matches in-the-wild OCR uncertainty,
improving visual-text alignment and reducing reliance on language priors when visual evidence is
incomplete. Consequently, the model learns more faithful grounding and exhibits reduced halluci-
nation in low-quality visual settings.
3.4.2
Modular Generative Visual Translation Framework
In addition to multilingual OCR-aware data generation, we introduce a Modular Generative Visual
Translation framework for in-situ OCR text replacement (Figure2–3). Unlike end-to-end generative
approaches, which jointly entangle scene synthesis and text generation, our framework decomposes
the translation process into three controllable stages:
(i) Segmentation (explicit localization): Pixel-level text masks are generated using DBNet or
SAM 2, enabling precise localization and spatial grounding of OCR regions.
(ii) Inpainting (background preservation): Generative inpainting reconstructs the underlying
background surface after text removal, isolating textual content from surrounding scene
structures.
(iii) Rendering (deterministic text insertion): Translated text is rendered with explicit geomet-
ric and stylistic control using homography transformation and alpha blending, preserving
perspective consistency and typographic alignment.
This modular decomposition introduces structural inductive bias largely absent in prior end-to-end
diffusion-based approaches. By explicitly separating localization, background reconstruction, and
text rendering, the framework reduces probabilistic text corruption while preserving non-textual
scene elements and maintaining typographic consistency under perspective distortion, blur, and vi-
sual noise.
Compared with fully generative OCR translation pipelines, the proposed framework provides
stronger controllability and visual fidelity, particularly in scenarios requiring accurate multilingual
text replacement and spatially grounded OCR reconstruction.
3.5
Comparative Methodology Analysis
Prior E2E diffusion approaches (Figure 2) are optimized for semantic generation, where global
coherence is prioritized over local fidelity. While effective for creative synthesis, they are funda-
mentally ill-suited for OCR translation tasks, as they treat text as part of the generative distribution
and thus remain susceptible to hallucination and typographic errors.
In contrast, our modular framework (Figure 3) explicitly separates what to preserve (background)
from what to modify (text). This separation enables deterministic control over text generation while
maintaining visual consistency, addressing a key limitation of prior MLLM-based OCR systems.

Experimental Setup
4.1
Training Setup
The proposed OCR-aware multimodal model is trained using LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning
(SFT) on the OCR-image question-answer samples consisting of multilingual receipts, menus,
7


## Page 8


posters, signs, handwritten text, and document images. The training corpus combines both the
public and author-curated synthetic-OCR data generated through the proposed multilingual OCR-
to-translation pipeline at large scale.
Training is performed using the AdamW optimizer with dynamic learning rate scheduling, includ-
ing linear warm-up followed by cosine decay. LoRA adapters are applied with a rank of 512 for
parameter-efficient multimodal adaptation.
To improve robustness under practical OCR conditions, training images are augmented with mul-
tiple visual degradations including blur, low resolution, rotation, perspective distortion, occlusion,
compression artifacts, cluttered layouts, and multilingual text mixtures.
4.2
Evaluation Metrics
We evaluate model performance using three primary metrics:
(i) OCR Completeness: percentage of correctly recovered OCR tokens, particularly for long-
context and spatially scattered OCR content.
(ii) Hallucination Rate: frequency of visually unsupported or incorrectly generated OCR con-
tent.
(iii) Translation Quality: multilingual OCR translation accuracy measured using one-gram
BLEU score.
Evaluation is conducted on challenging in-the-wild OCR scenarios including multilingual receipts,
restaurant menus, posters, street signs, handwritten notes, and document images under degraded
visual conditions.
4.3
Baselines and Comparative Evaluation
We compare the proposed OCR-aware SFT model, LlAMA3 VLM OCR-SFT against the origi-
nal baseline multimodal model without OCR-aware post-training - LlAMA3 VLM. In addition,
qualitative comparisons are conducted against frontier multimodal systems including GPT-4-class
multimodal models and Gemini-family models on challenging OCR and multilingual reasoning sce-
narios.

Conclusion
This work investigates the limitations of current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) un-
der practical OCR conditions and proposes an OCR-aware multimodal post-training framework to
improve multilingual OCR robustness and reduce hallucination. By combining multilingual OCR-
aware data curation, large-scale synthetic OCR generation, LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning
(SFT), and structured visual reasoning prompts, the proposed approach substantially improves OCR
completeness, multilingual translation accuracy, and robustness under degraded visual conditions.
Experimental results demonstrate both quantitative and qualitative improvements across multilin-
gual receipts, menus, posters, signs, handwritten text, and document images. Compared with the
baseline model, the proposed framework recovers more detailed OCR content while significantly
reducing hallucination under blur, occlusion, rotation, low-contrast, and cluttered visual environ-
ments.
Our analysis further suggests that hallucination in OCR-oriented MLLMs is closely related to weak
visual grounding under uncertain or incomplete visual evidence. OCR-aware post-training improves
robustness by encouraging stronger alignment between visual observations and generated text, re-
ducing reliance on language priors when OCR uncertainty is high. Overall, the results indicate
that data-centric OCR-aware multimodal training provides an effective and scalable direction for
improving practical multilingual OCR systems and OCR-based visual question answering.
Future work may explore stronger teacher-student distillation pipelines, larger multilingual OCR
datasets, preference-based multimodal alignment, and integration with region-aware visual encoders
to further improve OCR fidelity, robustness, and scalability.
