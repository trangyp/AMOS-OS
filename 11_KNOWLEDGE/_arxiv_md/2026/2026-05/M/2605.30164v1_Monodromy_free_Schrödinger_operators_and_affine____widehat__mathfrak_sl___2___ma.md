Abstract. Given a non-zero polynomial P(x), we study Fuchsian differential operators of the form
L = ∂2
x −u(x) such that for all λ ∈C the operator L + λP(x) is monodromy free. We prove that all

1. Introduction
In the classical works, [AM78], [AMM77], the rational tau functions of the KdV equations were
described by the Fuchsian operators of the form L = ∂2 −u(x), where u(x) is a rational function,
with the property that L + λ is monodromy free for all λ ∈C. Moreover, it was shown in [DG86]
that all such operators are obtained from ∂2 by a finite sequence of Darboux transformations.
The operators of the form L = ∂2 −x2 −u(x), where u(x) is a rational function decreasing at
∞, with the property that L + λ is monodromy free for all λ ∈C were classified in [O99]. All such
operators are obtained from ∂2−x2 by a finite sequence of Darboux transformations. These operators
were used to study Calogero systems with quadratically grown potentials, see [FV94]. There is no
known classification of similar operators with x2 replaced by xk, k ̸= 0, 2, see [GV09].
The interest to operators with similar properties spiked dramatically after the the seminal work
[BLZ96]. In the study of quantum KdV flows, [BLZ96] introduced the operators of the form ∂2
x −
u(x) + λx
¯k, where u(x) is a rational function, ¯k ∈C, with the property that for all λ ∈C the
monodromy around poles of u(x) is trivial. Such operators became known as Schr¨odinger operators
with monster potential. The correspondence between generating function of eigenvalues of quantum
KdV Hamiltonians and connection functions (in variable λ) of the operators with monster potential
is named by the ODE/IM correspondence.
In [MMR25] it was argued that the connection functions of interest in the operators with monster
potential coincide with the connection functions of another class of operators of the form ∂2 −u(x)+
λxk(x −1) which have no monodromy for all λ, and which are Fuchsian for λ = 0.
In this paper, we fix a non-zero polynomial P(x) and consider Fuchsian operators of the form
L = ∂2 −u(x), such that L+λP(x) are monodromy free for all λ. (More generally, we actually allow
monodromy to equal ±1.) We call such operators λ-monodromy free.
Our main tool is the populations of critical points of master functions. From that point of view,
the case of [AM78] corresponds to the trivial representation of bsl2, the case of [O99] to the Fock
module of bgl1, the case of [MMR25] to a tensor product of bsl2 vacuum level one module at x = 0
