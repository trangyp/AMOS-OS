MOTIVIC SEGRE CLASSES OF SCHUBERT CELLS AND THE
CONNECTIVE FORMAL GROUP LAW
RAJ GANDHI
ABSTRACT. We use the connective formal group law to define a one-parameter (β-)deformation of the motivic
Segre classes of Schubert cells in the d-step flag variety. This β-deformation specializes to the motivic Segre classes
of Schubert cells when β = 1 and to the Segre-Schwartz-MacPherson classes of Schubert cells when β = 0. We
define rational function representatives for the β-deformed classes in the d = 1 case in terms of a solvable lattice
model, and we prove a combinatorial formula for the structure constants in the β-deformed basis in the d = 1
case using Knutson-Tao puzzles. The proof of the puzzle formula involves intertwiners for representations of the
multi-parameter quantum group of type ba2. We show that our β-deformations can be viewed as quotients of
canonical elements in a quotient of the equivariant algebraic cobordism ring of the cotangent bundle of the flag
variety by proving that the canonical elements satisfy a GKM type condition.

1. INTRODUCTION
Schubert calculus is a field of mathematics that interacts with combinatorics, representation theory, and
algebraic geometry. In this paper, we will explore connections between these three disciplines, and for
the benefit of the reader, we have included a lot of the relevant background material in this introduction.
In Section 1.1, we will give an overview of the combinatorial aspects of Schubert calculus which will be
relevant to us. In particular, we will describe Knutson-Tao puzzles and explain how quantum integral
systems have been applied to compute the structure constants in the “Schubert basis” in the equivariant
cohomology ring and equivariant K-ring of the Grassmannian and its cotangent bundle. In Section 1.2,
we will give an overview of how Schubert classes can be defined for generalized cohomology rings of flag
varieties, and we will explain where formal group laws appear in this theory. The main results in this paper
are summarized in Section 1.3. The organization of this paper is described in Section 1.4.

1.2. Formal group laws and generalized Schubert calculus. A formal group law is a pair (R, F), where
R is a commutative, unital ring and F(x, y) = P
i,j≥0 ai,jxiyj is a formal power series in RJx, yK, satis-
fying three axioms: unity (i.e., F(0, x) = x), commutativity (i.e., F(x, y) = F(y, x)), and associativity (i.e.,
F(x, F(y, z)) = F(F(x, y), z)). These axioms impose relations on the coefficients ai,j. The unity axiom implies
that a0,0 = 0 and a1,0 = a0,1 = 1. The commutativity axiom implies that ai,j = aj,i. The relations arising
from associativity are very complicated. Exposition on formal group laws can be found in, for example,
[Fr¨o68]. Apart from being very interesting objects in themselves, formal group laws carry deep geometric
information. That is, a formal group law can be viewed as a combinatorial shadow of an algebraic invariant
of smooth algebraic varieties known as an algebraic oriented cohomology theory.
An (algebraic) oriented cohomology theory (OCT) is a (contravariant) functor
h∗: {categ. of smooth, separated, finite type C-schemes} →{categ. of graded, commutative, unital rings}
that satisfies various axioms generalizing the Eilenberg-Steenrod axioms from algebraic topology. OCTs were
defined by Levine and Morel in [LM07], and the concept of an OCT was inspired by the notion of a complex-
oriented cohomology theory, originally defined by Daniel Quillen in his paper [Qui71]. The Chow theory, which
sends a variety X to its Chow ring CH∗(X), and the Grothendieck K0 functor (which we will call “K-theory”),
which sends a variety X to the Grothendieck ring of vector bundles K0(X) on X, are examples of OCTs. As
this is the first time we are using the words “Chow ring,” we clarify that for any parabolic subgroup P, the
singular cohomology ring of G/P is isomorphic to the Chow ring of G/P due to the stratification of G/P
given by (1). Therefore, Schubert calculus in the Chow ring CH∗(G/P) of G/P is no different than Schubert
calculus in the singular cohomology ring H∗(G/P). Every OCT comes with Chern classes, which can be
constructed using a method of Grothendieck. For a vector bundle of V rank n on variety X, the i-th Chern
class ci(V) resides in hi(X). Chern classes commute with pullbacks and satisfy the “Whitney sum formula”.
Every OCT h∗is equipped with a formal group law, and the formal group law of an OCT is determined by
the following property: given two line bundles L1, L2 on a smooth variety X,
c1(L1 ⊗L2) = F(c1(L1), c1(L2)),
where F is the formal group law of h∗. Chow rings gives rise to the additive formal group law F(x, y) = x+y
over R = Z, and K-theory gives rise to the multiplicative formal group law F(x, y) = x + y −xy over
R = Z. The universal OCT is called algebraic cobordism Ω∗, and it has a unique map to any other oriented
cohomology theory. The algebraic cobordism ring of a smooth variety X can be constructed formally as the
set of projective morphisms Y →X, with Y being smooth, quasi-projective, and irreducible, modulo certain
relations. A geometric construction for algebraic cobordism was developed by Levine and Pandharipande
in [LP09] in terms of so-called ”double-point degenerations”. The formal group law of algebraic cobordism
is the universal formal group law defined over the Lazard ring.
Our primary focus will be the formal group law FC(x, y) = x + y −βxy defined over R = Z[β]. We
will hereby call the pair (R, FC) the connective formal group law. The universal OCT whose formal group
law is the connective one is called algebraic connective K-theory CK∗. A geometric construction for CK∗was
given by Cai in [Cai08], and by Dai and Levine in [DL14]. A key property of the functor CK∗is that for
any smooth algebraic variety X, there are always natural ring homomorphisms CK∗(X) →CH∗(X) and
CK∗(X) →K0(X) induced by evaluating the parameter β at 0 and at 1, respectively.

5. THE PUZZLE RULE FOR MULTIPLYING GRASSMANNIAN DEFORMED CLASSES
In this section, we will prove a positive formula for the structure constants in the basis of deformed
motivic Segre classes Sλ when d = 1 in terms of Knutson-Tao puzzles. As the Sλ are not simply the β-
homogenizations of the motivic Segre classes of Schubert cells defined in (4), our puzzle formula for the
structure constants in the β-deformed basis is a non-trivial β-deformation of the puzzle formula for the
structure constants in the basis of usual motivic Segre classes described in Section 2.6. As we will show
in Section 8, the deformed motivic Segre classes of Schubert cells are quotients of canonical elements in
a quotient of the bT-equivariant algebraic cobordism ring of T ∗(G/PΘ). Although our deformed classes
have this geometric description, we currently do not have a geometric understanding for why the structure
constants in the β-deformed basis are positive. The only proof that we have for the positivity of the structure
constants is the combinatorial proof given by Theorem 5.6. In the proof of the puzzle formula, we introduce
R, U, and D matrices that satisfy various Yang-Baxter type equations. We will show in Section 6 that these
matrices are intertwiners for representations of the multi-parameter quantum group of type ba2. That these
matrices are related to the representation theory of the multi-parameter quantum group is unexpected.
In Section 5.1, we define the fugacities of the rhombus and triangle puzzle pieces which are required for
the puzzle formula. In Section 5.2, we define several matrices, which are constructed from the fugacities in
Section 5.1, and we show that these matrices satisfy several Yang-Baxter type equations. In Section 5.3, we
prove the puzzle formula in Theorem 5.6 and show that the formula is suitably positive in Remark 5.7. We
also provide several examples of puzzle computations.

7. CONNECTIVE MOTIVIC SEGRE CLASSES AND INTERPOLATING BETWEEN PUZZLE RULES
The goal of this section is to explain the origins of the deformed motivic Segre classes of Section 3 using
the connective formal group law and the formal affine Demazure algebra. Using this approach, we define “con-
nective motivic Segre classes” of Schubert cells, we explain how to interpolate between the motivic Segre
classes and the Segre-Schwartz-MacPherson classes by evaluating a parameter β at 1 and 0, respectively,
and we interpolate between the puzzle formulas for multiplying Segre-Schwartz-MacPherson classes and
multiplying motivic Segre classes.
In Section 7.1, we recall the definition and properties of formal group laws and the construction of the
formal group algebra from [CPZ13]. The formal group algebra can be thought of an an algebraic model for
the so-called torus-equivariant oriented cohomology of a point, which is discussed in Section 8. In Section 7.2,
we define Demazure-Lusztig operators for arbitrary formal group laws, and we show that the operators
defined for the connective formal group law satisfy the braid relations. In Section 7.3, we define the “con-
nective motivic Segre classes” of Schubert cells using Demazure-Lusztig operators for the connective formal
group law. These classes interpolate between Segre-Schwartz-MacPherson classes (when β = 0) and the
deformed classes of Section 3 (when β ̸= 0). We then define “connective motivic Chern classes” of Schu-
bert cells and show that they satisfy the so-called GKM conditions (this implies that these classes have a
geometric realization as canonical elements in a quotient of the bT-equivariant algebraic cobordism ring of
T ∗(G/PΘ), which we will explore in Section 8). In Section 7.4, we prove a general puzzle formula for the
connective motivic Segre classes in the d = 1 case, which interpolates between the puzzle formula for mul-
tiplying Segre-Schwartz-MacPherson classes of Schubert cells and the puzzle formula of Theorem 5.6 for
multiplying the β-deformed motivic Segre classes.

Section 8.1, we give an overview of algebraic cobordism. In Section 8.2, we give an overview of equivariant

8.1. Algebraic cobordism. In this subsection, we give an overview of algebraic cobordism, following the
exposition in [LM07].
Let SchC be the category consisting of schemes that are separated and of finite type over C, and let SmC
be the full subcategory of SchC consisting of schemes that are smooth and quasi-projective over C. Let
Ring∗be the category of graded, commutative, unital rings. In [LM07], the authors defined the notion of
an oriented cohomology theory, which is a contravariant functor h∗: SmC →Ring∗satisfying several coho-
mological axioms, such as localization, homotopy invariance, existence of push-forwards along projective
morphisms (see [LM07, Def. 1.1.2]). A morphism of oriented cohomology theories is a natural transforma-
tion of functors that commutes with the push-forwards.
Given an oriented cohomology theory h∗and vector bundle E →X in SmC, there are unique elements
ci(E) ∈hi(X), where i = 0, 1, . . . , n, characterized by the axioms in [LM07, pp. 3], which include the “Whit-
ney sum formula,” and the requirement that the ci commute with pullbacks. Every oriented cohomology
theory h∗is equipped with a formal group law F over S = h∗(pt), where pt = Spec(C) is the point. This
formal group law is characterized by the following formula, called the Quillen formula: given line bundles
L1 →X, L2 →X in SmC, we have c1(L1 ⊗L2) = F(c1(L1), c1(L2)).
Example 8.1. The functor CH∗: SmC →Ring∗that sends a smooth scheme X to its Chow ring CH∗(X)
of algebraic cycles modulo rational equivalence is an oriented cohomology theory. The formal group law
associated to CH∗is the additive formal group law FA(x, y) = x + y over CH∗(pt) ≃Z.
Example 8.2. Consider the K0 functor3 that sends a smooth variety X to the Grothendieck ring K0(X) of
vector bundles on X. Form the graded ring K∗(X) := K0(X) ⊗Z Z[β, β−1], where deg(β) = −1. Then the
functor K∗: SmC →Ring∗that sends a smooth scheme X to K∗(X) is an oriented cohomology theory. The
formal group law associated to K∗is the multiplicative-periodic formal group law FM(x, y) = x + y −βxy
