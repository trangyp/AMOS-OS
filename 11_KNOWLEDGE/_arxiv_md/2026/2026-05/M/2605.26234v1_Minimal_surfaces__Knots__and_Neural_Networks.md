TANCREDI SCHETTINI GHERARDINI1 AND MARCO USULA2
Abstract. A recent conjecture by Joel Fine posits a relationship between the coefficients of the
HOMFLY polynomial of a knot K in the 3-sphere S3, and the signed count of minimal surfaces
in hyperbolic 4-space H4 meeting the sphere at infinity at K, with prescribed genus and self-
intersection number. In this paper, we develop a novel machine learning framework based on
Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to solve the minimal surface equation in hyperbolic
space. We utilise this framework to test Fine’s Conjecture by constructing near-minimal surfaces
bounding various families of knots in S3. Furthermore, we develop an algorithmic method to find
self-intersections and compute their sign. For every knot analysed, the computationally discovered
minimal surfaces and their self-intersection numbers perfectly align with the predictions of Fine’s
Conjecture, providing empirical evidence for it.

1. Introduction
The asymptotic Plateau problem is a classical, fundamental topic in geometric analysis. It re-
volves around the following question: given a closed submanifold M k ⊂Sn of the n-dimensional
sphere, does there exist a k +1-dimensional, properly immersed minimal submanifold of hyperbolic
space Hn+1 asymptotic to M at infinity? A foundational breakthrough in this area was achieved
by Anderson [4], who provided the following general existence result: for any closed submanifold
M ⊂Sn, there exists a complete, absolutely area-minimising submanifold of Hn+1 asymptotic to M
(cf. Theorem 2.18 for a more precise statement).
Not very much is known about the local and global structure of the space of solutions of the
asymptotic Plateau problem. This problem is of special interest in the case of minimal surfaces
properly immersed in the 4-dimensional hyperbolic space. Indeed, there is a fascinating interplay
between the study of these surfaces, and the study of knots in the 3-sphere S3. To explain this
connection, consider a knot K ⊂S3 and denote by M (K) the set of properly immersed minimal
surfaces in H4 asymptotic to K at infinity. A priori, we only know that M (K) is non-empty, by

Let us briefly describe Fine’s main results. Let Mg,d (K) ⊂M (K) be the subspace of properly
immersed minimal surfaces in H4 asymptotic to K at infinity, with genus g ∈N and self-intersection
number (the signed count of transverse double points) d ∈Z. Fine proved that, for a generic knot
K, the space Mg,d (K) is an oriented 0-dimensional manifold; that is, a discrete set of points, each
representing a minimal surface carrying a ±1 sign. Furthermore, for g = 0 (the case of minimal
discs), he proved that M0,d (K) is compact. Consequently, it consists of a finite number of surfaces,
which can then be counted with sign. One of the major results in [15] is that this signed count is
an invariant of K: it does not change if we replace K by another knot K′, smoothly isotopic to K.
In [15], Fine conjectures that the moduli spaces Mg,d (K) for g > 0 should be compact as well,
for K generic; moreover, he conjectures that the count with sign #Mg,d (K) should be closely
related to the coefficients of a fundamental polynomial invariant of knots, namely the HOMFLY
polynomial. This knot invariant, which we shall denote by P (K), is a Z-valued linear combination
of monomials of the form z2ha2k, where a, z are formal variables, k ∈Z, and h ∈N. The precise
form of Fine’s Conjecture is the following:
Conjecture. For a generic knot K ⊂S3, the signed count #Mg,d (K) corresponds to the integer
coefficient of z2ga2(d+g) in the HOMFLY polynomial P (K).
Proving Fine’s Conjecture would be a major breakthrough in the understanding of minimal
surfaces in hyperbolic 4-space. Indeed, if the conjecture were true, the fact that the coefficient of
z2ga2(g+d) in P (K) is non-zero would imply that (up to possibly slightly deforming the knot) there
exists a properly immersed minimal surface in H4, with genus g and self-intersection number d,
asymptotic to K at infinity. Note that this information goes well beyond the mere existence result
proved by Anderson, which does not give any information about the genus and the self-intersection
number of the absolutely area-minimising solution. We remark that, while the coefficients of the
HOMFLY polynomial are easy to compute1, it is a priori extremely difficult to gain information on
Mg,d (K); indeed, the minimal surface equation is a complicated second-order non-linear partial
differential equation, and at present we lack a general method to solve it explicitly.
The aim of this paper is to test and provide evidence for Fine’s Conjecture by finding minimal
surfaces in H4 predicted by the conjecture via machine learning. Specifically, we develop a machine
learning framework designed to:
(1) find near-minimal discs properly immersed in H4, asymptotic to a prescribed knot at in-
finity;
(2) find the points of self-intersection of these solutions, and compute the self-intersection
number.

Remark 2.7. Let us clarify the definition of τ (u). The differential du is a smooth bundle map

Remark 2.8. In general, if u : (N, G) →(M, g) is a smooth map between Riemannian manifolds,
one can define the tension field of u with respect to G and g as τ (u) = trG ∇du, where ∇is now
the connection on T ∗N ⊗u∗TM induced by the Levi-Civita connections of G and g. Solutions of
the equation τ (u) = 0 are called harmonic maps. In the definition above, u is required to be an
immersion and G is required to be the pull-back of g. In other words, an immersion is minimal
if and only if it is harmonic with respect to the pull-back metric. In the literature, the vector field
tru∗g ∇du is more often referred to as the mean curvature vector field of u.
From a variational point of view, τ (u) represents the negative gradient of the k-volume functional
Vk, i.e. the direction of steepest descent for Vk. This justifies the following well-known result:
Proposition 2.9. An immersion u : N →(M, g) is minimal if and only if τ (u) = 0.
This point of view emphasises the fact that the minimality equation is a quasi-linear second
order elliptic equation. This means that the linearisation of the map v 7→τ (v) at an immersion
u : N →M is a second-order, elliptic partial differential operator Ju (in fact, a Laplace-type
geometric operator) acting on sections of u∗TM.
2.2. Conformally compact manifolds and minimal p-submanifolds. The ultimate goal of
this paper is to find examples of minimal submanifolds of the hyperbolic space via machine learning.
Our framework is designed to search for these solutions in a special class of submanifolds, called
p-submanifolds.
To motivate this choice, in this short subsection we introduce the notions of
conformally compact manifold, and of p-submanifold of such manifolds.
Conformally compact
geometry is a very active area of geometric analysis and mathematical physics [7], pioneered by
Fefferman–Graham [14, 13], Graham–Lee [18], Mazzeo–Melrose [36, 35] and many others.
We
refer to these references for many more details on the geometry, and to §2 of [41] for a shorter
introduction, adopting the same language used in this paper.
Let M m+1 be a compact manifold with boundary.
Definition 2.10. A boundary defining function on M is a smooth function ρ : M →[0, +∞) such
that the zero locus ρ = 0 is precisely the boundary ∂M, and moreover the differential dρ is nowhere
zero along ∂M.
Definition 2.11. A conformally compact metric on M is a metric g defined on the interior M ◦of
M, such that for some (hence every) boundary defining function ρ on M, the conformal rescaling
ρ2g extends smoothly to a metric on M. The pair (M, g) is called a conformally compact manifold.

with minimal p-submanifolds of hyperbolic spaces. In this subsection, we explain why we chose to
work with this particular class of submanifolds.

submanifolds of Hn+1.

immersed minimal submanifolds of Hn+1. A properly immersed minimal submanifold is the image
of a proper minimal immersion u : S →Hn+1, where S is an open manifold without boundary.

5. Future developments
Higher genus surfaces, which were not discussed in this paper, are a natural subject for further
study. The main modification needed for such an extension is purely computational; while the
mathematical model is perfectly well-suited for higher genus surfaces, parametrising these surfaces
with non-trivial topology requires a more elaborate implementation.
Another possible way to
improve our results could involve developing a systematic way to parametrise a knot in R3 starting
from a knot diagram; indeed, mainly for simplicity, we limited our examples to knots with known
explicit parametrisations, while a more systematic method to generate parametrised knots could
lead to a more extensive testing of Fine’s Conjecture.
Finally, a natural and very promising continuation of this work consists of upgrading the near-
minimal surfaces we obtained to genuinely minimal ones. In view of the accuracy and stability of
the approximations produced in this paper, using the simplest MLP architecture and training for
roughly an hour on standard CPUs, lowering the error to values suitable for numerical verifica-
tion appears to be well within reach; indeed, the present computations suggest that a substantial
improvement in precision should be feasible with an upgraded implementation and increased com-
putational resources. Such a result would allow us to turn the present numerical evidence into

to a range of PDEs, with neural networks playing a key role in some cases [17, 43]. Analogous
