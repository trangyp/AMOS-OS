Generalized Reed-Solomon Codes
Yanli Wang1, Yanxin Chen1, and Tongjiang Yan1*

Abstract
This paper contributes to maximum distance separable (MDS) and near MDS (NMDS)
properties of the extended generalized twisted Reed-Solomon (TGRS) codes. Firstly, a family
of extended TGRS (ETGRS) are constructed by appending three columns to the generator
matrix of original TGRS codes.
Secondly, the necessary and sufficient conditions for these
codes to be MDS or almost MDS (AMDS) codes are derived. Then, by analyzing the AMDS
properties of their dual codes, the necessary and sufffcient conditions for them to be NMDS codes
are established. Furthermore, some examples are given to verify the main results. Finally, we
determine the non-generalized Reed-Solomon (non-GRS) characteristics of them via the Schur
product method.
Keywords Extended TGRS codes · MDS · NMDS · Non-GRS

Throughout this paper, let Fq be the finite field with q elements, where q = pm and p is a prime.
Let F∗

If d = n −k, then C is said to be an AMDS code. The Euclidean dual code of C is defined as

If both C and C⊥are AMDS codes, then C is called a NMDS code.
Reed-Solomon (RS) codes, generalized RS (GRS) codes and extended GRS (EGRS) codes are
classical families of MDS codes. Based on GRS codes, in [15], Roth and Lempel constructed a new
class of Roth-Lempel codes by adding two columns to its generator matrix, then they proved that the
code is a non-GRS type MDS code. In [4], inspired by Sheekey’s twisted Gabidulin codes, Beelen et
al. first introduced TGRS codes, but TGRS codes are neither necessarily MDS codes nor GRS-type.
Affected by their work, many researches have begun to focus on TGRS [3–5,7–10,12,16–18,21,24,
25,27] and ETGRS codes to be MDS, AMDS, NMDS codes or non-GRS type.

In 2024, Zhu et al. [26] introduced ETGRS codes by adding a column to the generator matrix of
TGRS codes. In 2025, Li et al. [11] further studied two classes of ETGRS codes obtained by adding
a column and deleting a row from the generator matrix of GRS codes. Both works showed that the
corresponding codes are MDS, AMDS and non-GRS type.
Different from those in [26] and [11], Zhang et al. [23] constructed another class of ETGRS
codes by adding a new column to TGRS generator matrices. Specifically, this column corresponds
to the twist term with the hook at any row. From this, they also obtained several non-GRS-type
self-orthogonal MDS codes.
In [13], Ma et al. introduced ETGRS codes by adding two columns to the generator matrix of
TGRS codes. They proved that the MDS codes are inequivalent to GRS codes, and further showed
that the NMDS codes are inequivalent to Roth-Lempel codes.
Zhang et al. [22] constructed another class of ETGRS codes. More precisely, they introduced two
extra columns corresponding to the twist term in the generator matrix of TGRS codes with the hook
placed at an arbitrary row, and proved that this codes are MDS or AMDS as well as non-GRS-type.
They also determined the covering radii and deep holes of these codes.
Motivated by above works, this paper mainly focuses on a class of defined in Definition 2 by
adding three additional columns to the generator matrix of TGRS codes . The rest of this paper is
organized as follows. In Section 2, we briefly introduce basic notations related to ETGRS codes, MDS
codes, NMDS codes, extended codes and Schur Product. In Section 3, we verify that the ETGRS
codes is an extended code of the extended TGRS code. In Section 4 and Section 5, we derive the
necessary and sufffcient conditions for this codes to be MDS and AMDS, respectively. In Section 6,
we give the necessary and sufficient conditions for its duals to be AMDS, then prove our codes are
NMDS. In Section 7, we discuss the non-GRS properties by means of the Schur product. Finally,
Section 8 concludes this paper.

Conclusion
In this paper, we mainly investigate some properties of C codes. In Theorem 1, we prove that C
is the extended code of C1. The necessary and sufficient conditions for C to be MDS and AMDS
are presented in Theorems 2 and 3, respectively. We also characterize the AMDS property of their
dual codes in Theorem 4 and show that C is NMDS. Furthermore, we verify in Theorem 7 that the
extended code C is of the non-GRS property. In future work, we will investigate the covering radii
and deep holes of these codes.
