INTRINSIC UNIFORM STRUCTURE ON MEDIAN ALGEBRAS
MICHAEL MEGRELISHVILI
Abstract. We introduce the median uniformity Um, an intrinsic precompact convex uniform
structure on a median algebra. It is Hausdorff under natural assumptions, for instance for finite-
rank median algebras. In the Hausdorff case, its uniform completion yields the Minimal Median
Compactification (MMC). The induced topology τm provides a natural higher-rank analogue of
the interval topology on linearly ordered sets and of the shadow topology on rank-one median
algebras. When all intervals in the median algebra X are finite, the MMC is the unique proper
median compactification of (X, τm); in particular, it coincides with the Roller compactification.
We apply this uniform framework to continuous actions of a topological group G by median
automorphisms. We show that the MMC is a median G-compactification. In the finite-rank case,
the resulting compact G-system is Rosenthal representable and hence dynamically tame.

1. Introduction
Abstract median algebra theory provides a geometric bridge between tree-like structures, linearly
ordered sets, and non-positively curved CAT(0) cube complexes, while also connecting naturally
with general convexity theory, topology, and metric geometry.
By recent results of [18], there exists a canonical precompact convex interval uniformity µ≤
compatible with the interval topology λ≤, whose uniform completion yields the classical Dedekind
compactification. Every linearly ordered set carries a natural median algebra structure of rank 1.
Our aim is to generalize this interval uniformity to median algebras.
The theory of convex structures developed by van de Vel [23] and many other authors provides a
rich framework for topologies and uniformities compatible with a given convexity.
The median uniformity Um introduced in the present paper is constructed in a median-specific
and purely algebraic way. Motivated by the uniformity µ≤on linearly ordered spaces, we introduce
an intrinsic median uniformity Um on median algebras (X, m), generated by two-member branch
covers {Bu

This uniformity Um is precompact and convex because every linear branch is convex.

the uniform completion b

2. Median algebras
Preliminaries. A median algebra is a set X with a ternary operation m: X3 →X satisfying the
standard median axioms. Frequently we write abc instead of m(a, b, c). Recall one possible system
of axioms (see [21, 23, 3]) defining median algebras:
(M1) The operation m is symmetric in all three variables.
(M2) abb = b.
(M3) (abd)cd = (acd)bd.
A map f : X1 →X2 between median algebras is said to be a homomorphism or median pre-
serving (MP) if f(xyz) = f(x)f(y)f(z). Equivalently: for every convex subset C ⊆Y the preimage
f −1(C) is convex in X (see, for example, [23, Ch.1, 6.11]).
For every pair x, y ∈X we have the interval [x, y]m := {z ∈X : xyz = z}. Usually, we omit
the subscript and write simply [x, y], when the context is clear. Always, [x, x] = {x}, [x, y] = [y, x].
For every triple x, y, z in (X, m) and the intervals [x, y], [y, z], [x, z] we have a unique common point
which is the median m(x, y, z)
[x, y] ∩[y, z] ∩[x, z] = {xyz}.
Following [3] sometimes we write x.z.y instead of z ∈[x, y].
For every pair a, b ∈X there is a natural partial order ≤a on [a, b] defined, for x, y ∈[a, b], by
x ≤a y
⇐⇒
x ∈[a, y].
Equivalently, x ≤a y iff m(a, x, y) = x. The opposite order is ≤b.
If [a, b] is a chain, that is, if it admits a linear order ⪯whose order-median coincides with the
median inherited from X, then ⪯is necessarily either ≤a or ≤b. Indeed, since [a, b] is the whole
interval between a and b, the points a and b must be the two endpoints of the order ⪯. If a is the
least element, then
x ⪯y
⇐⇒
m(a, x, y) = x
⇐⇒
x ≤a y.
If b is the least element, then ⪯=≤b.
A subset C ⊆X is convex if [x, y] ⊆C for all x, y ∈C. Every convex subset is a subalgebra. The
intersection of convex subsets is convex. We say that a pair of distinct elements a, b in X is adjacent
if [a, b] = {a, b}. Sometimes we write (a, b) ∈adj(X) and in this case we identify (a, b) with (b, a).
Several remarkable structures are median algebras under their natural medians. For instance,
distributive lattices (e.g. linear orders, Boolean algebras, and power sets P(S)).

A wall is a pair W = {W 0, W 1} of nonempty disjoint convex subsets whose union is X. The sets
W 0 and W 1 are called halfspaces. Denote by W(X) and H(X) all walls and halfspaces of X. Two

Definition 2.2. (see e.g., [23, 3, 7]) The rank of a nontrivial median algebra X (notation: rank(X))
is the supremum of the numbers n ∈N such that the Boolean hypercube {0, 1}n embeds as a median
subalgebra into X. Equivalently, rank(X) is the supremum of the cardinalities of finite families of
pairwise crossing walls.
The class of finite rank algebras is closed under taking subalgebras and finite products. The
rank of the product X1 × X2 of two median algebras is rank(X1) + rank(X2). Important examples
of algebras with rank k ∈N are Boolean hypercubes {0, 1}k, usual cubes [0, 1]k and CAT(0) cube
complexes with dimension k.
Onto homomorphisms cannot increase the rank.

Lemma 2.3. Let X and Y be median algebras, and let f : X →Y be a median homomorphism. If
C ⊆X is a chain, then its homomorphic image f(C) is a chain in Y .
Proof. Since C is a chain, for every a, b, c ∈C we have m(a, b, c) ∈{a, b, c}. Let x, y, z ∈f(C).

The latter is impossible because a 2-cube has rank 2. Hence f(C) is linear, i.e. a chain.

Rank-1 algebras are median pretrees (in terms of Bowditch). In this case ≤a is a linear ordering
on any median interval [a, b].
It is a useful treelike structure which naturally generalizes linear
orders and the betweenness relation on dendrons (e.g., dendrites), simplicial and R-trees. Median
preserving continuous group actions on median pretrees are investigated in [9]. Bounded variation
functions on median pretrees were introduced and studied in [14].
A topological median algebra (tma) is a topological space (X, τ) equipped with a continuous
median operation m: X3 →X. Mainly we assume that every tma is Hausdorff. The only exception
we allow is for canonical median topology which need not be Hausdorff in general (though makes
the median continuous).
Many important examples of tma come from median metric spaces, which play a major role in
metric geometry and group theory; see, for example, [3, 7, 23].
For every median algebra (X, m) and a pair of elements u, v ∈X we have a canonical median
retraction ϕu,v : X →[u, v], where ϕu,v(z) = m(u, z, v). If X is a tma then every ϕu,v is continuous.
Subalgebras and products of (locally convex) tmas, equipped with the coordinate-wise median,
are themselves tmas (resp. locally convex). Every projection on each coordinate is a continuous
MP map. In particular, the cube [0, 1]κ is a compact and locally convex median algebra for every
cardinal κ.

Median Uniformities and Compactifications. In this section, we formalize the abstract re-
lationship between uniform structures and compactifications in the category of median algebras.
Recall some auxiliary definitions.
For two covers α, β of a set X, we say that α refines β if for every A ∈α there exists B ∈β such
that A ⊆B. Notation: α ≻β. The star of a subset C ⊂X with respect to α is the set
St (C, α) = ∪{A ∈α : A ∩C ̸= ∅}.
If C := {c} is a singleton, we simply write St (c, α). So, St (c, α) = ∪{A ∈α : c ∈A}. The collection
α∗:= {St (A, α) : A ∈α} is a covering and is called the star of α. Always, α ≻α∗.
For the standard definitions about uniform structures in terms of coverings we refer to [11, 6].
Definition 2.6. [11, 6] Let U be a family of coverings on a set X. Then U is said to be a (covering)
uniformity on X if:
(C1) α, β ∈U implies that α ∧β := {A ∩B : A ∈α, B ∈β} ∈U;
(C2) α ∈U and α ≻β imply that β ∈U;
(C3) every element in U has a star-refinement in U (meaning that for every β ∈U there exists
α ∈U such that α∗≻β).
A subfamily B ⊂U such that each ε ∈U has a refinement δ ≻ε with δ ∈B, is said to be
a (uniform) base of U.

Γ∧:= {γ1 ∧· · · ∧γn : γi ∈Γ, n ∈N} is a base of U. An abstract set B of coverings on X is a base

Recall that a covering uniform space (X, U) is called precompact (or, totally bounded) if U has a
base consisting of finite covers of X. If the uniformity is Hausdorff, then it is equivalent to say that
the uniform completion is compact.
For median algebras (X, m) we say that a uniformity U is convex if there exists a uniform base
B of U where every member A ∈α of every α ∈B is convex (in usual median sense).
Definition 2.7. Let X be a topological median algebra. A median compactification of X is a pair
(K, η), where K is a compact Hausdorff topological median algebra and η: X →K is a continuous
median-preserving map with dense image.
We say that the compactification is proper if η is a topological embedding.
We consider the natural partial order on isomorphism classes of proper median compactifications
of a topological median algebra (X, m, τ), namely, by factor maps:
(Y1, η1) ⪯(Y2, η2)
⇐⇒
∃q: Y2 →Y1 continuous surjective with q ◦η2 = η1.
One may easily show that q automatically will be a median preserving map.
We order compatible precompact median uniformities by fineness. To systematically generate
median compactifications, the underlying uniform structure must interact properly with the median
operation.
Definition 2.8. A Hausdorff uniformity U on a median algebra X is called a median-compatible
uniformity if the ternary median operation m: X3 →X is uniformly continuous (where X3 carries
the product uniformity U × U × U). Let Unifmed denote the class of all median uniformities on
median algebras.
A fundamental source of precompact median-compatible uniformities comes directly from proper
compactifications: if Y is a compact topological median algebra, its unique topologically compatible
uniformity UY automatically makes mY uniformly continuous. Thus, the restriction of UY to any
dense median subalgebra X naturally yields a precompact median-compatible uniformity on X. The
class Unifmed is closed under the following uniform operations: Subspaces, Products, Completions,
Supremums.
