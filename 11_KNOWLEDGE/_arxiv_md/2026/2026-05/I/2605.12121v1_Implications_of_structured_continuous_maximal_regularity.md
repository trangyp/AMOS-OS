Implications of structured
continuous maximal regularity
Philip Preußler† and Felix L. Schwenninger‡
May 13, 2026
Abstract. We study how maximal regularity estimates with respect to the continuous
functions improve automatically in cases where the spatial norm is fundamentally
different from the supremum norm. More precisely, we invoke properties such as
weak compactness of convolution-type operators related to the mild solutions of
the underlying linear evolution equations to sharpen the a priori estimates. These
results have several applications: such as a new proof of Guerre-Delabriere’s result
on L1-maximal regularity and an extension of Baillon’s theorem; a simplification for
well-known perturbation theorems for generation of C0-semigroups; and we resolve an
open problem on input-to-state stability from control theory for a general abstract
class of systems.
Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 47D06, 34G10, 93C05
Keywords: maximal regularity, admissible operators, input-to-state stability, perturbations of
operator semigroups, Baillon’s theorem, infinite-dimensional control theory
1. Introduction
Well-posedness and regularity of (nonlinear) evolution equations, modeled via operator semigroups,
are closely related to properties of underlying convolution-type operators that formally arise from
the variation of constants formula. In this context, the interplay of temporal and spatial norms
is crucial to apply, e.g., fixed-point arguments or perturbation results. In this paper we deal
with the question whether a priori estimates with respect to limit case time norms, such as L1 or
L∞, can be sharpened to stronger estimates, taking into account structural assumptions on the
geometry of the Banach space. Our abstract results further impact several domains in partial
differential equations such as questions from control theory and maximal regularity.
To explain the setting, let A generate a strongly continuous semigroup T on a Banach space X.
Operator semigroups have a decades-long tradition in the structural study of evolution equations;
see, for example, [4, 41, 77]. While the theory is fundamentally built up for linear problems
given in the abstract form ˙x(t) = Ax(t), the setting readily allows for the treatment of nonlinear

continuous functions resp. the absolutely integrable functions.
1.1. Literature overview
The problem of extending operators such as Φ has been treated previously in special situations.
Structured maximal regularity as the main feature of the abstract setup and the generality of our
results are novel. In the context of control theory, there are preexisting results. We provide a
summary of the previous work in this area from the literature.
For U finite-dimensional and for analytic semigroups, generalizing earlier results from [56] and
[57] on diagonal semigroups, the article [61] considers such extensions from L∞to Orlicz spaces
under assumptions on the generator A, such as square function estimates or boundedness of the
H∞-calculus in the Hilbert space setting, see [61, Prop. 6, Thm. 7, Cor. 8]. The equivalence of
integral iss and iss under these assumptions is also noted in [61, Cor. 21].
In [59], the authors study boundedness of so-called Laplace–Carleson embeddings and the
connection to mappings such as Φ. In particular, for diagonal semigroups and finite-dimensional
U, the authors show in [59, Thm. 3.6, Cor. 3.7] that boundedness of Φ on L∞always implies
boundedness of Φ on a suitable Orlicz space. Moreover, in [59, Thm. 3.3], the authors even recover
L2-admissibility from L∞-admissibility if X and U are Hilbert spaces and A generates a C0-group
on X; this is shown by applying previous results by Weiss and Hansen–Weiss.
The relation between admissibility and Baillon-type results was previously considered in [60].
This includes the result [60, Thm. 2.9] that, in the case U = X and B0 = idX, the operator

1.2. Structure of the paper
In the first section, we give a quick overview on notation and selected concepts that will be used
throughout the paper.
Section 2 deals with a wide-ranging consequence of the classical de la Vall´ee Poussin theorem
for linear operators Ψ: W →L1([0, 1]; Y ) mapping into Lebesgue–Bochner spaces. In particular,
we note a folklore version of de la Vall´ee Poussin’s theorem which states that uniform integrability
of the image of the unit ball is necessary and sufficient for an operator, a priori bounded into
an L1 space, to also be bounded with respect to a stronger Orlicz space norm. We comment on
the application of weak compactness properties in this context, related to the Dunford–Pettis
theorem. Also, we present several sufficient conditions on the involved Banach spaces which yield
the requisite uniform integrability property for any bounded operator.
The third section contains the main results of this article. The results are phrased using the
language of admissible operators in Banach spaces. For operators Φτ stemming from structured
continuous maximal regularity as above, we use the framework of sun duality, a duality concept
that is adapted to C0-semigroups, to relate them to operators mapping into L1 spaces. As
L1([0, τ]; U′) is not the dual space of C([0, τ]; U), the duality arguments are subtle. The starting
point rests on [7, Thm. 3.1], where the authors show that bounded Φτ operators give rise to
boundedness of a related, formally dual, operator ˜Ψ defined on the sun dual space with values
in a vector-valued L1 space. We then show that uniform integrability of the image under ˜Ψ of
the unit ball in the sun dual space suffices to conclude that the original operator Φτ factorizes
through an Orlicz space.

1.3. Terminology and notation
Let X be a Banach space. We write X′ for the dual space of X. If x ∈X and x′ ∈X′, we use
any of the notations ⟨x, x′⟩X×X′ := ⟨x′, x⟩X′×X := x′(x) to refer to the dual pairing. The closed
unit ball of X is denoted by BX := {x ∈X : ∥x∥X ≤1}. Recall also that a subspace M ⊂X is
called complemented if there is a projection PM ∈L(X) with ran PM = M, see, e.g., [98, p. 37].
If Y is a second Banach space, we use the symbol L(X, Y ) to stand for the bounded linear
operators from X to Y . Recall that an operator T ∈L(X, Y ) is called weakly compact if the
image T(BX) is a relatively weakly compact subset of Y ; cf., e.g., [23, Def. VI.5.1]. We say
that an operator T ∈L(X, Y ) factors through a third Banach space W if there are operators
Q ∈L(X, W) and R ∈L(W, Y ) such that T = RQ.
Also, if A is a (possibly unbounded) operator on X, then R(λ, A) := (λ idX −A)−1 denotes
the resolvent of A for parameters λ ∈ρ(A), the resolvent set of A. If A: X ⊃D(A) →X is the
generator of a C0-semigroup on X, then we use the shorthand X1 to denote the domain D(A)
equipped with the graph norm.
For a Banach space Y and an interval I ⊂R and p ∈[1, ∞], we denote by Lp(I; Y ) the usual
Lebesgue–Bochner space. Lastly, we point out that we use [0, 1] generically as a bounded interval
for notational convenience. The results can also be stated for any other bounded interval with
left endpoint 0.

Since the Lebesgue measure is non-atomic, uniform integrability of M implies that M is bounded
in L1 norm; see, e.g., [3, p. 112]. Since bounded subsets of L1 need not be uniformly integrable,
cf., e.g., [3, Ex. 5.2.3 (iii)], uniform integrability is a strictly stronger property.
Due to the classical Dunford–Pettis theorem, there is a deep link between uniform integrability
and weak compactness in L1. For scalar L1 spaces, the theorem asserts that a subset M of
L1([0, 1]) is relatively weakly compact if and only if M is uniformly integrable. See, e.g., [3, Thm.
5.2.8] or [36, Thm. III.2.15] for proofs. On passing to vector-valued spaces, the two properties are
no longer equivalent. Uniform integrability of M ⊂L1([0, 1]; Y ) still follows from relative weak
compactness of M; see, e.g., [36, Thm. IV.2.4] and the discussion on [36, p. 104]. For general Y
the converse fails and additional assumptions are needed to recover relative weak compactness of
uniformly integrable sets. See also [36, p. 101 ff.], [32, p. 62 ff.] and the references [31, 33, 92]
mentioned in [32] for more on weak compactness in L1([0, 1]; Y ).

2.3.2. De la Vall´ee Poussin’s theorem
Let Y be a Banach space. It can be shown that, individually, each element of L1([0, 1]; Y )
lies in some Orlicz space, cf., e.g., [79, Theorem 4.2.5]. However, an arbitrary bounded set
M ⊂L1([0, 1]; Y ) need not be contained in a single Orlicz space. But under a suitable stronger
condition, which, due to the classical de la Vall´ee Poussin theorem, is uniform integrability of M,
containment in a shared Orlicz space does follow.

Proposition 2.6 (de la Vall´ee Poussin). Let M be a bounded subset of L1([0, 1]; Y ). Then the

a) there is a Young function G ∈∆2 such that M ⊂EG([0, 1]; Y ) is norm bounded;
b) there is a Young function F such that M is a norm bounded subset of LF ([0, 1]; Y );
c) M is uniformly integrable.

3.3.2. The Grothendieck property
A Banach space X is called a Grothendieck space if sequences in X′ converge weakly if and only if
they converge weakly*, see, e.g., [30, p. 97]; for more equivalent formulations of the Grothendieck
property, see also [34, Ch. Five, §2, Cor. 5]. Compare also the survey [43].
Some examples for Grothendieck spaces are reflexive spaces [30, p. 97], spaces of continuous
functions C(K) for compact sets K satisfying certain disjointness assumptions for closures in
K (e.g., Stonean and σ-Stonean spaces K) [43, pp. 269, 271]. By identifying L∞(Ω, µ) with a
suitable C(K) space, it follows that L∞(Ω, µ) is Grothendieck, see [43, Prop. 4.1.5].
If X is Grothendieck and Y is a weakly compactly generated Banach space, then any S ∈L(X, Y )
is weakly compact, see, e.g., [34, Ch. Five, §2, Cor. 5]. Note that, by definition, a space Y is
weakly compactly generated (wcg) if there exists a weakly compact subset of Y with dense span,
see, e.g., [34, p. 143]. From [34, p. 143], some examples for wcg spaces are reflexive spaces,
separable spaces, L1(µ) for σ-finite measures µ and the space c0. We also refer to [46] for more on
wcg Banach spaces.
Theorem 3.12. Let U be a Banach space such that L∞([0, 1]; U) has the Grothendieck property.
Assume that X is wcg and let T be a C0-semigroup on X. Then any L∞-admissible B ∈L(U, X−1)
is also EF -admissible for some Young function F. In particular, B is then also zero-class L∞-
admissible for T.
Proof. Since Φ1 ∈L(L∞([0, 1]; U), X) is weakly compact, this follows from Theorem 3.7.

4.1. Admissibility for observation operators
In systems theory, the notion of admissibility is also well-studied in the context of observation
operators, where the notion is a pivotal part of well-posedness of linear systems. The general setting
of “semigroup control systems”, supporting the modeling of unbounded observation and control
operators on Hilbert spaces, was introduced by Salamon in the fundamental paper [85]. There, the
notion of admissibility (with respect to Z = L2) of control operators appears as hypothesis (S2)
and L2-admissibility of observation operators appears as hypothesis (S3). Later, Weiss, in two
seminal works [95, 96] on admissible control and observation operators laid the foundation for the
study of the property of admissibility on Banach spaces, providing many general results in the field.
For example, an important result is [96, Thm. 6.9] on duality relations between Lp-admissible
observation and control operators. In this context we also mention the comprehensive treatise [88]
on well-posed systems by Staffans; among the many results on admissibility contained therein, we
highlight the treatment of admissibility with respect to the space of regulated functions, cf. [88,
Thm. 10.2.2]. Moreover, the author also considers many special cases, such as diagonal, normal
and contractive semigroups on Hilbert spaces [88, Sects. 10.6, 10.7]. See also the survey article
[58] by Jacob and Partington for an overview of results on admissibility theory.

4.6. Maximal regularity and the theorems of Guerre-Delabriere and Baillon
The classical maximal regularity with respect to C or L1 is considered ‘exotic’ by the dichotomy
results due to Baillon [8] and Guerre-Delabriere [45]. Both results show that this type of maximal
regularity can only occur if either the generator is bounded or the underlying Banach space contains
a (complemented) copy of c0 (ℓ1). Note that in PDE applications, the case of bounded generators
is typically irrelevant. The existing proof of Baillon’s theorem—see Baillon’s original article [8,
Theor`eme 1] and compare with [55, Thm. 17.4.4], [38, Thm. 0.5]—relies on a characterization
of spaces containing c0. More precisely, by assuming that A is unbounded, one can construct a
sequence which leads to a Schauder basis of an isomorphic copy of c0, see also [60, Prop. 2.5].
On the other hand, Guerre-Delabriere’s argument rests on a duality argument and, in turn, on
Baillon’s theorem.
Using our results, we give an alternative and direct proof of Guerre-Delabriere’s result [45,
Prop. 1] without making use of Baillon’s theorem.
Theorem 4.13 (Guerre-Delabriere; [45, Prop. 1]). Let X be a Banach space that does not contain
ℓ1 as a complemented subspace. If A has maximal L1-regularity, then A is bounded.
Proof. Suppose A has maximal L1-regularity. Due to [63, Thm. 3.6], see also [60, Prop. 2.1] A is
an L1-admissible observation operator for the semigroup generated by A. As X does not contain a
complemented subspace isomorphic to ℓ1, Theorem 4.1 implies that A is zero-class L1-admissible.
By [61, Prop. 16], A is bounded.
We can also prove a generalization of Baillon’s result for structured continuous maximal
regularity; however, we need a slightly stronger assumption on the Banach space X.
