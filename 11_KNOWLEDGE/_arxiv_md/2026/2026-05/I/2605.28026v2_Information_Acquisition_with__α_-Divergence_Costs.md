Abstract
Building on the 𝑓-information model of Bloedel et al. (2025), this paper introduces
a one-parameter family of information acquisition models and characterizes optimal in-
formation acquisition. This family extends the mutual information model (Matˇejka and
McKay, 2015) while preserving its analytical tractability. The information cost is de-
rived from the 𝛼-divergence, which nests the KL-divergence (𝛼= −1), the reverse KL-
divergence (𝛼= 1), and the squared Hellinger distance (𝛼= 0), and is represented in
closed form via the 𝛼-integration of Amari (2007).
The optimal choice probabilities
belong to the 𝑞-exponential family, which appears in nonextensive statistical mechanics
(Tsallis, 1988) and in the 𝑞-logit model of traffic route choice (Nakayama, 2013). In
the KL-divergence special case, this family reduces to the modified logit of Matˇejka and
McKay (2015).

Introduction
The rational inattention framework (Sims, 2003) models agents who optimally choose how
much and what kind of information to acquire before making a decision, subject to an in-
formation cost.
Matˇejka and McKay (2015) adopt the mutual information cost and show
that the optimal stochastic choice rule takes the modified logit form. Bloedel et al. (2025)
(henceforth BDP) introduce 𝑓-information, a general family of information costs based on the
𝑓-divergence. The 𝑓-information cost is defined as the value of a minimization problem over
reference signal marginal distributions. Hence the information acquisition problem maximizes
expected payoff minus a minimized divergence value, rather than expected payoff minus a cost
given in closed form. BDP exploit this variational structure through convex duality and char-
acterize the optimal choice rule in terms of the convex conjugate of 𝑓, under the assumption
of co-finiteness of 𝑓.
Building on the 𝑓-information model of Bloedel et al. (2025), this paper introduces a
one-parameter family of information acquisition models that extends the mutual information
model (Matˇejka and McKay, 2015) while preserving its analytical tractability. We adopt the 𝛼-
divergence as the 𝑓-divergence in BDP’s framework and characterize the optimal choice rule.
The 𝛼-divergence is a one-parameter family of 𝑓-divergences that nests the KL-divergence
(𝛼= −1), the reverse KL-divergence (𝛼= 1), and the squared Hellinger distance (𝛼= 0)
as special cases, and is central to information geometry (Amari, 2016). We refer to the 𝑓-
information in this case as the 𝛼-information. For a range of parameter values, co-finiteness
fails and BDP’s theorem does not directly apply. We instead derive optimality conditions
directly by using a closed-form expression for the 𝛼-information, which is given in terms of
the 𝛼-integration of Amari (2007).1
Our main result is a necessary and sufficient condition for the optimal choice rule, nesting
the optimality condition of Matˇejka and McKay (2015) and Caplin et al. (2019) for the mutual
information cost as a special case. The optimal choice probabilities belong to the 𝑞-exponential
family, a class of distributions defined in terms of the 𝑞-exponential function introduced by
Tsallis (1988) in nonextensive statistical mechanics, where it replaces the exponential in the
Boltzmann distribution. The same family appears in the 𝑞-logit analysis of traffic route choice
(Nakayama, 2013). When 𝛼= −1, this family reduces to the modified logit form, and the
support of optimal choice, or the consideration set, is common across states (Caplin et al.,
2019). By contrast, when 𝛼≠−1, the consideration set can vary across states.
