INFINITE TRANSITIVITY AND POLYNOMIAL VECTOR FIELDS
RAFAEL B. ANDRIST AND IVAN ARZHANTSEV
Abstract. We prove that for many pairs H1, H2 of root subgroups of the automorphism
group Aut(C2) the diagonal action of the group generated by H1, H2 on (C2)m has an
open orbit for any positive integer m. The result is based on the study of the Lie algebra
of polynomials in two variables with the standard Poisson bracket.
1. Introduction
In this work, we study the properties of multiple transitivity for groups acting on the
affine plane C2. It follows from the classical Jung theorem that the group of polynomial
automorphisms Aut(C2) is generated by the two-dimensional torus of diagonal matrices
and the one-parameter unipotent subgroups of the form
Lr : (x + αyr, y),
α ∈C
and
Rs : (x, y + βxs),
β ∈C,
where r and s run through the non-negative integers.
Let us consider the group Gr,s
generated by the two subgroups Lr and Rs.
We recall that an action of a group G on a set X is infinitely transitive if for any positive
integer m and any two m-tuples of pairwise distinct points in X there is an element g ∈G
that sends the first tuple to the second one. It is easy to see that the group Gr,s acts on
C2 with an open orbit. By [7], the action of Gr,s on its open orbit is infinitely transitive
only if rs = 2. It is asked in [7] whether rs = 2 implies that the action is indeed infinitely
transitive. The positive answer is given in [13, 9]. We give another proof of this result in
Section 4. This proof connects the property of infinite transitivity of an action with the
structure of a tangent algebra, and therefore appears promising for further generalizations.
Let us say that an action of a group G on an algebraic variety X is generically infinitely
transitive if for any positive integer m the diagonal action of G on Xm := X × . . . × X
(m times) has a Zariski open orbit. Clearly, any infinitely transitive action is generically
infinitely transitive.
The main result of this paper (Theorem 18) claims that the action of Gr,s on C2 is
generically infinitely transitive if and only if rs ⩾2. It is easy to see that the condition
rs ⩽1 is equivalent to the fact that Gr,s is a linear algebraic group.
The proof of Theorem 18 is based on the study of subalgebras of the Lie algebra of
polynomials C[x, y] with the standard Poisson bracket; see Section 2. We show that for the
subalgebra Lie(xp, yq) generated by the two monomials xp and yq we have
Lie(xp, yq) ∩C[x] = xpC[xpq−p−q]
and
Lie(xp, yq) ∩C[y] = yqC[ypq−p−q],
provided min(p, q) ≥2 and max(p, q) ≥3; see Proposition 7. Moreover, the subalgebra
Lie(xp, yq) has finite codimension in C[x, y] if and only if (p, q) = (2, 3) or (3, 2).
In the same Section 2 we find explicit pairs of derivations that generate the Lie algebra
of polynomial vector fields on C2 with zero divergence or with constant divergence; see

Denote by Ga the additive group of the ground field C. Any non-trivial regular action

examples of Ga-subgroups in Aut(C2). These are precisely the Ga-subgroups normalized
by the two-dimensional torus of diagonal matrices.
Such Ga-subgroups are called root

can be found in [5, 6, 7] and references therein.
In Corollary 29, we use the fact that the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields with zero
divergence on C2 is 2-generated and construct Ga-subgroups H1, H2 (one them is not root)
such that the group G generated by H1, H2 is infinitely transitive on C2.
It is important to notice that the results on Lie algebras in Section 2 are obtained by
algebraic methods and are valid over any field of characteristic zero. At the same time,
the results on transitivity properties of group actions are based on Propositions 23, 27, the
proofs of which use analytical methods, in particular the Implicit Function Theorem. It
would be interesting to obtain criteria for infinite transitivity or generic infinite transitivity
in terms of the corresponding Lie algebras applying purely algebraic arguments.

Definition 14. An action of a group G on an algebraic variety X is said to be generically
