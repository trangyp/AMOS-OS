ABSTRACT
We consider Constrained Online Convex Optimization (COCO) with adversarially chosen constraints.
At each round, the learner chooses an action before observing the loss and constraint function for
that round. The goal is to achieve small static regret against the best point satisfying all constraints
while also controlling cumulative constraint violation (CCV). For strongly convex losses, state-of-
the-art algorithms achieve O(log T) regret and O(√T log T) CCV. The corresponding best-known
bounds for convex losses is O(
√
T) regret and O(
√
T log T) CCV. In this paper, we give a simple
projection-based algorithm that simultaneously achieves O(log T) regret and O(log T) CCV for
strongly-convex losses, yielding an exponential improvement in the CCV. For the convex losses, our
algorithm improves the CCV to O(
√
T) while maintaining the optimal O(
√
T) regret. The key to our
improvement is a recent geometric result for self-contracted curves, which may be of independent
interest.

Introduction
Online convex optimization (OCO) is a standard framework for sequential decision making under uncertainty [Hazan,
2022]. In this problem, at the beginning of each round t ∈[T], a learner selects an action xt from a closed and convex

ft : X →R, and the learner incurs a cost of ft(xt) for round t. The objective of the learner is to minimize cumulative

Constrained Online Convex Optimization (COCO) generalizes the OCO framework by incorporating long-term
constraints. Similar to OCO, at the beginning of each round t ∈[T], the learner selects an action from a closed
and convex decision set X. Subsequently, the adversary reveals two functions: a G-Lipschitz convex cost function
ft : X 7→R and a G-Lipschitz constraint function gt : X 7→R. We assume the constraint function to be quasiconvex 1 ,
which includes the class of all convex functions. The constraint function specifies an instantaneous constraint of the
form gt(x) ⩽0. Since the cost and constraint functions are revealed after the learner selects its action at every round,
the learner cannot possibly satisfy the constraint on every round, incurring a constraint violation of (gt(xt))+. In the
COCO problem, the informal goal of the learner is to minimize the cumulative cost while minimizing the cumulative
constraint violation (CCV).

Main ideas:
We consider a natural class of first-order algorithms, called NP-OGD, given by Algorithm 1. Any
algorithm in this class takes a gradient step on the last revealed cost function at each round and then projects it onto the
intersection of the feasible sets revealed so far. In particular, the successive iterates of the algorithm are obtained as
xt+1 = ΠSt(xt −et),
where the sequence {et}t⩾1 represents gradient vectors appropriately scaled with the step size and ΠC(·) is the standard
Euclidean projection operator on the closed and convex set C.
It follows from a minor variation of the standard arguments that, with appropriate step sizes, the NP-OGD class of
algorithms achieves the optimal regret bound for both convex and strongly-convex losses. Hence, the main challenge is
to control the CCV for this class of algorithms. Using the Lipschitzness of the constraint functions, it can be shown that
the CCV is controlled by the total movement cost of the algorithm given by PT −1
t=1 ||xt+1 −xt||2. Due to successive
projection operations, this movement cost is not bounded by the step lengths alone: the point xt lies in St−1, but xt −et
need not lie in the set St, so the projection onto the smaller set St may create additional movement.

COCO via Nested Projected OGD
We consider a family of algorithms, called Nested Projected Online Gradient Descent (NP-OGD). At each round, any
NP-OGD algorithm first takes a gradient step on the last revealed cost function and then projects the result onto the
intersection of all feasible sets revealed so far (See Algorithm 1 for a Pseudocode). The NP-OGD algorithms only differ
in their choice of the step-sizes.

Remark 3.1 (Oracle requirement). Algorithm 1 only needs one first-order query ht ∈∂ft(xt) and one Euclidean
projection onto the accumulated feasible set St. For many structured constraints (e.g., half-spaces or 2-norm balls), this
operator can be implemented highly efficiently via standard active-set methods.

Bounding the Movement Cost of NP-OGD
We first state a movement lemma for the NP-OGD family of algorithms. We prove a somewhat general result where the
movement at each step could be arbitrary (not necessarily related to the gradients of the cost function). The lemma says
that if a pure nested projection curve is perturbed by vectors et, then its total movement is bounded by the diameter of
the initial body plus the total perturbation size, up to a dimension-dependent multiplicative constant. To build intuition,
consider the unperturbed case where et = 0, ∀t. In this setting, the trajectory itself is self-contracted and Theorem 2.2
immediately yields a constant movement bound independent of T. Lemma 4.1 shows that introducing perturbations
increases the total movement by at most an additive term proportional to the cumulative perturbation magnitude.

5
Conclusion
In this paper, we considered Constrained Online Convex Optimization (COCO) under the common feasibility assumption,
demonstrating that the nested geometry of the successive feasible sets can be exploited for obtaining improved violation
bounds. Using a simple projected gradient-based algorithm, we obtain O(log T) bounds for both regret and CCV
for strongly convex losses - an exponential improvement of CCV from the state-of-the-art result. Furthermore, we
achieve the optimal O(
√
T) Regret and CCV bounds for general convex losses, improving the CCV bound by a factor
of O(log T). These results are established by exploiting the approximate self-contraction property of the descent
curve traced out by the algorithm. Future research directions include considering Exp-concave functions, general
mirror-descent type updates, and establishing tight lower bounds. We also want to better understand the scaling of the
pre-factor Cd with the dimension d.
