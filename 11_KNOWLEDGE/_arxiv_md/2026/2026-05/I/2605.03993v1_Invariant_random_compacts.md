INVARIANT RANDOM COMPACTS
BRYNA KRA AND SCOTT SCHMIEDING
Abstract. For a compact metric space X with a group G acting on it con-
tinuously, an invariant random compact is a Borel probability measure on the
space of nonempty compact subsets of X that is invariant under the action
of G. The action is IC-rigid if, with respect to every invariant random com-
pact, every compact set is almost surely either finite or X. We give sufficient
conditions for an action to be IC-rigid, and show there are natural examples
of such actions. We further consider a notion of weak IC-rigidity, and prove
that the Chacon system is weakly IC-rigid but not IC-rigid. As an application,
we prove results concerning multiplicative largeness of dilations of sets on the
circle.
1. Introduction
1.1. Summary of the main results. Consider a group, or more generally a semi-
group, G acting continuously on a compact metric space X. The study of the in-
duced action of G on K(X), the space of nonempty compact subsets of X endowed
with the Hausdorff metric, has a long history (see for example [6, 5, 23, 25, 32]).
This induced action captures topological information about the orbits of compact
sets in K(X), and it is natural to consider the statistical distribution of the orbits
of compact sets. To do so, we study invariant measures for the induced action on
K(X) and their relation to the dynamics of the G-action on X.
We call a Borel probability measure µ on K(X) which is invariant under the ac-
tion of G on K(X) an invariant random compact (IRC for short). This terminology
is partly inspired by analogy with the notion of an invariant random subgroup of
a group (see for example [2, 1]), which is a conjugation-invariant measure on the
space of subgroups of a group. A compact subset Y ⊂X that is invariant under G,
meaning that gY = Y for all g ∈G, gives rise to an IRC by considering the delta
mass δY . In this sense, we view an IRC as a generalization of an invariant subset
for the action.
Any system with a G-invariant Borel probability measure has an IRC in a triv-
ial way: pushing such a measure µ forward via the map X →K(X) defined by
x 7→{x} yields an IRC for the system. Such an IRC is supported on the set of
singleton subsets in K(X). Analogously, there are IRCs supported on the subset
Kfin(X) ⊂K(X) of finite subsets of X arising from invariant measures for the di-
agonal action on the product of finitely many copies of X with itself. We refer to
these IRCs, meaning ones supported on the collection of finite subsets, as finitary.
The finitary IRCs are connected to self-joinings of the system, and in the amenable
case, all finitary IRCs are combinations of pushforwards of invariant measures for
the diagonal actions on self-products of X (see Section 7 for these results).

Thus we focus on IRCs that do not arise in this manner: IRCs µ for which
µ-almost every compact subset of X is infinite. We call such IRCs nonfinitary.
Assuming X is infinite and each g ∈G acts surjectively on X (we make this mild
assumption, which always holds when G is a group, throughout), there is always a
nonfinitary IRC, namely δX. More generally, an infinite compact invariant set Y ⊂

there are nonfinitary IRCs that are not δX (see Section 3.4 for several examples).
For instance, any amenable group acting by isometries, or any system having an
invariant measure for which the action is essentially free and has a rigidity sequence,
has nonfinitary IRCs other than δX (see Proposition 3.5 and Theorem 3.14).
Motivated by this, we say that the action of G on X is IC-rigid if the only
nonfinitary IRC is δX. It is immediate that an infinite IC-rigid system must be
almost minimal, meaning every proper compact G-invariant set is finite. But IC-
rigidity is a stronger notion, and this raises the question of whether there are natural
examples of such systems.

1.2. Questions. The study of invariant random compacts leads to several natural
directions for further research.
The first asks whether the weak IC-rigidity for the multiplicative action of N on
T can be upgraded to IC-rigidity.
Question 1.5. Is δT the only nonfinitary invariant random compact for the action
of the multiplicative semigroup N on the torus T = R/Z?
A positive answer to Question 1.5 would yield stronger versions of the results in
Section 5.2.
Furstenberg [21] showed that if p, q ≥2 are multiplicatively independent natural
numbers, then the only compact infinite subset of T invariant under multiplication
by p and q is T. In light of this, it is natural to consider whether these actions are
IC-rigid.
Question 1.6. If p, q ≥2 are multiplicatively independent natural numbers, is δT
the only nonfinitary invariant random compact for the action of multiplication by
p and q on the torus T?
Clearly, a positive answer to Question 1.5 implies a positive answer to Ques-
tion 1.6. More generally, there is the problem of determining all of the IRCs for the
action of multiplication by p and q on the torus T. The case of finitary IRCs sup-
ported on singleton sets is a trivial reformulation of Furstenberg’s question about
invariant measures for multiplication by p and q on the T. Maucourant’s result [34]
shows there are finitary IRCs for these systems, supported on sets of size at least
four, which do not arise by pushing forward Lebesgue on an entire torus.
The automorphism group Aut(σn) of a full shift (Xn, σn) is the group of self-

2. Background
2.1. Systems. By a topological dynamical system (X, G), or system for short, we
mean a group or semigroup G acting continuously on a compact metric space X.
Throughout, we assume that all groups and semigroups are countable, the space
X is infinite, and let dX denote the metric on X.
In the case of a semigroup,
we always assume each g ∈G acts surjectively on X, and that all semigroups are
left cancellative. The system is minimal if {gx : g ∈G} = X for all x ∈X and is
transitive if {gx : g ∈G} = X for some x ∈X. The system (X, G) is totally minimal
if for every finite index subgroup H ⊂G, the action of H on X is also minimal. We
say Y ⊂X is invariant if g(Y ) = Y for every g ∈G. For a set A ⊂X and ε > 0
we denote the ball of radius ε around A by Bε(A) = {x ∈X : d(x, A) < ε}. For
a Z-action defined by a homeomorphism T : X →X we often write simply (X, T)
for the system.
Throughout, we assume that for a semigroup G, every g ∈G acts surjectively
on X and that every g ∈G acts finite-to-one, in the sense that g−1(x) is finite for
every x ∈X. These assumptions obviously always hold when G is a group, and we
assume them for semigroup actions for technical reasons. In particular, they hold
for the multiplicative action of the semigroup N on the circle T.
For a compact metric space X, we let MG(X) denote the set of all G-invariant
Borel probability measures on X.
By a measure-preserving system, we mean a
triple (X, µ, G) where (X, G) is a topological dynamical system and µ ∈MG(X).
Throughout, we assume that for any measure-preserving system, the space X is a
compact metric space and the system is endowed with the Borel σ-algebra (we omit
the σ-algebra from the notation). The set Y ⊂X is G-invariant if gY = Y for all
g ∈G, where as usual equality is meant up to sets of measure zero. A measure-
preserving system is ergodic if any G-invariant Borel subset of X has measure zero
or one, and we let Me
