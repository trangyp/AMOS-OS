1. INTRODUCTION
Let k be a field of characteristic 0. An associative algebra (P, ·) is called a perm algebra if it satisfies
the left-commutative identity
x1x2x3 −x2x1x3 = 0
(1)
Perm algebras also have a natural operadic interpretation related to dialgebras and relations be-
tween quadratic operads (including Koszul duality and Manin products) [3, 9, 17, 21]. Perm algebras
also occur in studies of averaging operators and other constructions [19], which are closely related to
several constructions of perm bialgebras [23]. The class of metabelian Lie algebras is precisely charac-
terised by its relationship to perm algebras: they arise as commutator algebras of the latter, and every
metabelian Lie algebra can be embedded into a perm algebra [18]. Mutations of perm algebras provide
another source of derived multiplications and identity questions; in [11], mutation elements in free
perm algebras are described and polynomial identities of the mutated products are investigated. The
notion of binary perm algebras was introduced in [14]. On the bialgebra side, extension and factoriza-
tion viewpoints for perm bialgebras are developed in [10], and quasi-triangular/factorizable aspects are
studied in [15, 16]; see also the appearance of induced perm bialgebras from averaging infinitesimal
bialgebras [4] and related averaging/apre-perm phenomena [23].

Although they look similar, they behave in different ways. The first studies in this direction were by
Kolesnikov and Sartayev: for every differential perm algebra, the product a ≺b = ab′ is left-symmetric
(pre-Lie), and they obtained a criterion for when a given left-symmetric algebra is embedded in a
differential perm algebra via this product [13]. An additional part is developed in the paper [20], in
which the differential perm algebra, considered under derivative operations a ≻b := a′b, is used to
implement and study the structures of Novikov dialgebras. In particular, the authors provide explicit
monomial descriptions for generated subalgebras and obtain polynomial identities satisfied by these
derived products.
In this paper, we show that commutative product ♦satisfies Tortken-type identities [6, 7], while
the product • satisfies the corresponding dialgebraic analogues. In [8], the authors show using the
embedding into a differential commutative associative algebra in characteristic 0 that the space of
null Lagrangians in the free Novikov algebra is exactly the subspace closed under the symmetrized
product. We extend this idea to differential perm algebras: we describe the analogue symmetric and
weak-symmetric elements by giving explicit generators (and, in the multilinear component, bases and
dimension formulas) for ST⟨X⟩and ST⟨X⟩. The skew operation ♢yields a Lie-type bracket in the
free differential perm setting, and the bracket ◦leads to Leibniz-type structures (and more generally
to Leibniz brackets associated with δ–derivations). Thus the same differential perm algebra (P, ·, d)
produces several different structures (Tortken, Tortken dialgebra, Lie, and Leibniz).
