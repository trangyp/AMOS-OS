Indicators for phonon hydrodynamics from first principles predictions of thermal
conductivity
Nikhil Malviya and Navaneetha K. Ravichandran∗
Department of Mechanical Engineering, Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore 560012, India
(Dated: May 19, 2026)
Hydrodynamic heat flow, where out-of-equilibrium phonons collectively drift in response to an
applied temperature differential, has attracted renewed interest following its experimental observa-
tion in graphite at temperatures as high as 300 K. To accelerate discovery of material alternatives
to graphite and suitable experimental conditions for realizing this non-Fourier heat flow regime,
computationally efficient indicators derived from predictive first principles approaches are neces-
sary. Here we show that the enhancement of thermal conductivity (κ) achieved through a complete
solution of the linearized Peierls–Boltzmann equation (LPBE) for phonon transport (κLPBE), rela-
tive to that from the relaxation time approximation (RTA) for phonon decay (κRTA), is a low cost
indicator for phonon hydrodynamics. We show that collectively drifting non-equilibrium phonons
amplify the ratio of κLPBE to κRTA, while a small κLPBE/κRTA correlates with predominantly diffu-
sive phonon transport. On the other hand, we find that conventional approaches that rely only on
momentum-conserving Normal and momentum-dissipating Umklapp scattering rates, such as the
RTA and the Callaway approximations to the LPBE, are inadequate to predict phonon hydrody-
namics. Furthermore, our study reveals that the indicator ratio - κLPBE/κRTA, and therefore the
strength of hydrodynamic signatures, decrease with increasing Brillouin zone (BZ) sampling density
for several ultrahigh-κ materials at low temperatures, thus underscoring the need for careful BZ
sampling convergence studies to ensure robust predictions of phonon hydrodynamics. Our identi-
fication of κLPBE/κRTA as a computationally inexpensive indicator of phonon hydrodynamics will
accelerate the search for new materials that exhibit such unconventional heat flow regimes beyond
textbook Fourier’s law.
Heat in semiconductors is predominantly carried by
the quantized eigenmodes of the harmonic crystal po-
tential called phonons.
These phonons scatter among
themselves due to the crystal anharmonicity, resulting
in a resistance to heat flow and a finite thermal con-
ductivity (κ).
These scattering processes can be clas-
sified as momentum-conserving Normal scattering (N-
scattering) and momentum-dissipative Umklapp scat-
tering (U-scattering) processes. In materials where N-
scattering is much stronger than U-scattering, heat can
flow like a viscous fluid rather than a diffusive gas, re-
sulting in an unconventional heat flow regime called the
hydrodynamic heat flow [1–5], which cannot be described
by the commonly-used Fourier’s heat diffusion law. This
hydrodynamic heat flow phenomenon has captured the
attention of the research community owing to its ability
to introduce a directionality to heat flow through thermal
rectification — a thermal Tesla valve [6] and its potential
applications in thermal cloaking and shielding [7, 8].
The transient manifestation of this hydrodynamic
regime called the second sound, where heat can propa-
gate at speeds comparable to that of sound, has been ob-
served in sodium fluoride [9, 10] and bismuth [11] crystals
at temperatures below 20 K, and recently in sapphire be-
tween 10-100 K [12], in germanium between 15-300 K [13]
and in graphite around 100 K [14], around 200 K [15] and
around 300 K [16]. These experimental realizations of hy-
drodynamic heat flow in technologically important mate-
rials open up new possibilities to control and manipulate

heat in several classical and quantum electronic applica-
tions. Yet, these observations are restricted to a handful
of materials above cryogenic temperatures, due to lack
of low cost microscopic guidelines to search the materials
databases for alternate material candidates and suitable
experimental conditions to realize this unconventional,
non-Fourier heat flow regime.
Here, we uncover a computationally inexpensive indi-
cator for phonon hydrodynamics from the first-principles
predictions of the κ of a material. This indicator, which
depends on the ratio of κ obtained from the complete
solution of the governing equation for phonon transport
— the linearized Peierls-Boltzmann equation (LPBE) to
that obtained from the relaxation time approximation
(RTA) to the LPBE, exhibits a direct correlation with the
strength of hydrodynamic signatures in a material. We
show that this one-to-one correlation originates from the
off-diagonal terms of the phonon collision matrix that is
predominantly momentum-conserving. Therefore, com-
putational methods for predicting κ that rely only on the
diagonal part of the collision matrix, such as the Callaway
approximation to the LPBE, cannot capture this corre-
lation. Our identification of a computationally efficient
indicator for phonon hydrodynamic signatures will accel-
erate data-driven computational search efforts to identify
material alternatives and experimental conditions for re-
alizing hydrodynamic heat flow.

monolayer graphene must exhibit strong hydrodynamic

associated with the exact diagonalization of Ω.
Our
work, thus, provides a computationally-efficient predic-
tive tool that can accelerate data-driven computational
searches of ideal materials and experimental conditions
for realizing such unconventional non-diffusive heat flow
regimes that transcend the textbook Fourier’s law of
heat diffusion.
