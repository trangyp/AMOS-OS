Interaction-Split Edge Spectral Flow and Neutral Triplet Boundary Modes in a C = 2
Hubbard Pump
Yong-Feng Yang,1 Zhao-Rui Tian,1 Chen Cheng,1, ∗and Hong-Gang Luo2, 1, †
1Lanzhou Center for Theoretical Physics, Key Laboratory of Quantum Theory and Applications of MoE,
Key Laboratory of Theoretical Physics of Gansu Province,
and Gansu Provincial Research Center for Basic Disciplines of Quantum Physics,
Lanzhou University, Lanzhou, Gansu 730000, China
2Institute of Fundamental Physics and Quantum Technology, Ningbo University, Ningbo, 315211 China
We show that a sliding spatial modulation of the on-site Hubbard interaction realizes a many-
body Thouless pump whose boundary spectral flow is reconstructed by correlations. For a period-
three Hubbard chain at filling ρ = 2/3, density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) calculations
identify a correlated insulator with many-body Chern number C = +2, corresponding to two units
of charge pumped per cycle.
Its excitation spectrum separates the charge gap from the lowest
neutral spin gap, revealing an interacting bulk response beyond a simple spin-degenerate band
pump. Under open boundary conditions, this contrast becomes even more pronounced. A spin-
degenerate Hartree/Aubry-Andr´e-Harper reference pump exhibits simultaneous edge flow in the two
spin channels, whereas the full modulated Hubbard model suppresses the boundary doublon channel
and splits the spectral flow into two distinct edge events. Between these events, the boundary charge
is neutralized while a neutral, spinful triplet-like excitation is localized at the edge. The global µ−U
phase diagram reveals filling-dependent topology: the ρ = 2/3 regime remains in the C = +2 sector,
while ρ = 4/3 regime undergoes a spin-gap-closing transition from a C = −2 pump to a distinct
C = +1 topological Mott pump.
The interplay between topology and strong electronic
correlations remains a central theme in condensed mat-
ter physics [1–3].
In one dimension, correlations are
especially consequential:
charge and spin degrees of
freedom can separate, interaction-driven insulating gaps
can emerge, and boundary excitations often defy single-
particle expectations [4, 5]. A natural setting in which to
probe these effects is the Thouless pump, where an adia-
batic cycle transports a quantized charge determined, in
interacting systems, by a many-body Chern number [6–
11]. While interacting and fractional pumps have been
widely studied [12–20], less is known about how strong
local correlations reconstruct the open-boundary spec-
tral flow associated with an otherwise integer many-body
pump.
Recent progress has sharpened this question from sev-
eral directions.
In two-component Rice-Mele-Hubbard
pumps, interactions can split a pumping process inher-
ited from an underlying band pump [21].
Quantum-
gas experiments have demonstrated the quantization and
breakdown of Hubbard-Thouless pumping and shown
that interactions can enable pumping in nonsliding lat-
tices [22, 23].
Interaction-controlled pumps have also
been studied in spin chains, spinless fermions, and hard-
core bosons [24, 25], while spatiotemporal modulation of
interactions has been proposed as a route to pumping in
bosonic and nonlinear systems [26–29]. These works es-
tablish interactions as an active ingredient in topological
pumping. For spinful fermions, however, a distinct possi-
bility arises: a spatially modulated onsite Hubbard inter-
action can couple charge and spin sectors, thereby recon-
structing the boundary response into a neutral, spinful
edge sector.
Here we demonstrate this mechanism in a spinful Hub-
bard chain with a sliding period-three modulation of the
on-site interaction.
At filling ρ = 2/3, density-matrix
renormalization group (DMRG) calculations show that
the interaction modulation opens a correlated insulat-
ing phase with many-body Chern number C = 2, corre-
sponding to two units of charge pumped per cycle. Be-
cause a modulated Hubbard interaction at finite density
inevitably generates a Hartree channel, we compare the
full modulated Hubbard model with a spin-degenerate
Hartree/Aubry-Andr´e-Harper (AAH) [30, 31] reference
pump. This reference system realizes the same total in-
teger pump through independent spin channels and ex-
hibits simultaneous spin-degenerate edge flow.
The full modulated Hubbard pump realizes the same
integer bulk charge transport as the Hartree reference,
but with a reconstructed boundary spectral flow.
Lo-
cal correlations suppress the boundary singlet/doublon
channel and split the open-chain edge flow into two
distinct events.
Between these events, the boundary
charge response is neutralized, while a triplet-like spin
excitation remains localized at the edge.
The result
is therefore not a fractional charge pump, but an inte-
ger C = +2 many-body pump with a neutral, spinful
boundary sector.
The global µ-U phase diagram fur-
ther shows that interaction-modulated Hubbard chains
support filling-dependent topological pumps, including a
C = −2 →C = +1 transition at ρ = 4/3.

Discussion and Conclusion.— In summary, we have
demonstrated that a sliding spatial modulation of the
on-site Hubbard interaction provides a robust route
to interaction-reconstructed topological boundary trans-
port.
For the period-three Hubbard chain at filling
ρ = 2/3, the modulation drives a gapless, noninteract-
ing parent state into a C = +2 correlated insulating
pump phase. Unlike the spin-degenerate Hartree/AAH
reference pump, local correlations suppress the boundary
doublon channel and explicitly split the otherwise simul-
taneous edge spectral flow. Between these distinct edge
events, the boundary charge is neutralized, while a spin-
ful excitation remains localized near the edge. Protected
by the exact global SU(2) spin symmetry of the model,
this dynamic spatial decoupling strictly isolates a neutral
triplet boundary mode (S = 1).
Furthermore, the global µ −U phase diagram reveals
that this boundary reconstruction is part of a broader,
filling-dependent topology. Rather than simply suppress-
ing topological transport via Mott localization, the Hub-
bard interaction actively selects distinct topological man-
ifolds depending on the carrier density. Specifically, it
stabilizes the C = +2 pump with a triplet edge sector
at filling ρ = 2/3, while driving a topological phase tran-
sition into a C = +1 topological Mott pump hosting
fermionic edge modes at ρ = 4/3.
These results are directly relevant to ongoing efforts
to engineer correlated topological dynamics in quantum
simulators. The essential ingredients—a one-dimensional
lattice, tunable filling, and a spatially patterned onsite
interaction—are highly amenable to experiments utiliz-
ing ultracold fermionic atoms in optical lattices, where
spatially dependent interactions can be engineered us-
ing optical Feshbach techniques [43, 44] or modulated
cavity fields [45, 46]. Recent progress in tunable inter-
acting 1D channels in moir´e superlattices [47–49], tran-
sition metal dichalcogenide arrays [50, 51], and quasi-
1D van der Waals materials [52] also points to promis-
ing solid-state platforms.
In these settings, the defin-
ing experimental signatures would be the quantized bulk
charge pumping, the splitting of the open-boundary spec-
tral flow, and the emergence of boundary quasiparticles
carrying filling-dependent quantum numbers.
Looking ahead, the interaction-split edge spectral flow
provides a powerful organizing principle for correlated
topological pumps. A natural next step is to character-
ize the large-U, C = +1 phase at filling ρ = 4/3 in the
same depth as the ρ = 2/3 regime. More broadly, ex-
tending spatially modulated interactions to systems with
longer-range forces (e.g., Rydberg atom arrays) or higher-
dimensional geometries may uncover new routes to sta-
bilizing correlated topological boundary modes from oth-
erwise gapless or topologically trivial parent states.
