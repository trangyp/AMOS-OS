I.
INTRODUCTION
Quantum coherence is a defining feature of quantum
theory and a primary resource for quantum technologies.
In the standard resource theory [1], coherence is defined
relative to a fixed reference basis.
A complementary,
basis-independent notion was introduced by Designolle et
al. [2], which characterizes coherence as the impossibility
of simultaneously diagonalizing a set of quantum states
in a common basis. For triples of states with pairwise
overlaps rij = |⟨di|dj⟩|2, coherence-free models satisfy
nontrivial overlap inequalities [3, 4]. Violations of these
inequalities witness basis-independent coherence. Under
the operational equivalences assumed in generalized non-
contextuality frameworks [5, 6], the same violations also
witness preparation contextuality.
State overlaps are often accessed through tomogra-
phy or specialized overlap-estimation protocols such as
SWAP tests [7].
Multi-path interferometry provides
a natural operational alternative: coherence manifests
through pairwise interference visibilities Vij [8], which,
under ideal symmetric conditions, directly encode the
detector-state overlaps.
This connection is closely re-
lated to coherence-based formulations of multipath wave-
particle duality [9, 10].

IV.
CONNECTION TO PREPARATION
NONCONTEXTUALITY
For qubit detector states, the operational equivalences
needed for a preparation-noncontextuality test arise nat-
urally from the interferometric setup. A balanced n-path
interferometer assigns identical a priori probabilities to
each path when no which-path information is extracted.
When path i is paired with path j in a two-path interfer-
ence experiment, the observed visibility Vij operationally
determines the detector-state overlap |⟨di|dj⟩|. Observed
interference visibilities therefore directly determine the
overlaps entering the noncontextuality inequalities. Any
jointly diagonalizable description must therefore satisfy
the corresponding classical overlap inequalities.
The cyclic inequality derived in Sec. III provides the
operational translation of this noncontextuality condi-
tion. It bounds the admissible pairwise visibility config-
urations compatible with coherence-free detector states.
Within the framework of generalized noncontextual-
ity [5, 6], violations of this inequality admit an inter-
pretation in terms of preparation contextuality, with the
detector states {|di⟩} serving as operational preparations
and the projectors {|di⟩⟨di|} defining the measurements
used to access their overlaps.
In the interferometric realization considered here, the

VI.
DISCUSSION AND EXPERIMENTAL
FEASIBILITY
We provide an interferometric framework for test-
ing overlap inequalities and generalized noncontextuality
based on pairwise visibility measurements. Overlap in-
equalities were identified as basis-independent coherence
witnesses by Galv˜ao and Brod [3], and the event-graph
framework of Wagner et al. [4] unified coherence, contex-
tuality, and nonlocality, establishing cycle inequalities as
preparation-noncontextuality inequalities.
Recent experiments have demonstrated direct access
to pairwise overlaps in photonic systems [23], and n-cycle
contextuality inequalities have been tested using interfer-
ometric schemes such as two-photon conditional Young
interference [24]. The present approach reduces such con-
textuality tests to standard single-photon visibility mea-
surements. Pairwise visibilities are obtained by opening
one pair of paths at a time and extracting the correspond-
ing fringe contrast, without joint measurements, coinci-
dence detection, or state tomography, provided the path
amplitudes are known and stable. For cycle inequalities,
only the n pairwise visibilities appearing in the cycle need
to be measured.
Wagner et al. [12] applied this framework to Mach-
Zehnder interferometers using a discrete prepare-and-
measure approach.
The present work provides a di-
rect operational mapping from interference visibilities
to these inequalities, enabling contextuality tests using
standard fringe-contrast measurements.
A direct proof-of-principle implementation is provided
by the three-path configuration discussed in Sec. III B,
using polarization-encoded detector states
