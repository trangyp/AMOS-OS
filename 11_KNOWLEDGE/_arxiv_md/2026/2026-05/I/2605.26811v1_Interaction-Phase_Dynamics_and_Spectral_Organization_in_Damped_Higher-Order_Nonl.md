Interaction-Phase Dynamics and Spectral Organization in
Damped Higher-Order Nonlinear Schrödinger Models
C.M. Schober*
Department of Mathematics, University of Central Florida
May 27, 2026
Abstract
We investigate the dynamical mechanisms underlying the contrasting nonlinear Floquet spectral evolutions ob-
served in viscous and nonlinear mean-ﬂow damped higher-order nonlinear Schr"odinger (HONLS) systems. Mo-
tivated by the persistent organized Floquet-band structure under nonlinear mean-ﬂow damping and the repeated
Floquet-band reconnection observed under viscous damping, we derive a reduced ﬁve-mode carrier–sideband
truncation and reformulate the dynamics in amplitude–phase variables to isolate the dominant interaction phases
associated with the principal four-wave interaction products.
Within this framework, viscous damping acts primarily modewise and does not directly modify the leading
interaction-phase dynamics. By contrast, nonlinear mean-ﬂow damping contributes directly to the interaction-
phase evolution through interaction-dependent dissipative corrections. In the carrier–sideband regime, these
corrections generate terms of the form −κj sin(ψj), introducing dissipative feedback into the dominant carrier–
sideband interaction dynamics.
To interpret the resulting interaction-phase evolution, we examine recurrent ﬁnite-gap NLS benchmark so-
lutions whose modulation dynamics are independently understood. These benchmarks show that substantial
interaction-phase evolution and localized restructuring may occur even within organized quasiperiodic modu-
lation dynamics possessing invariant ﬁnite-gap Floquet spectral structure. Numerical diagnostics further show
that the nonlinear mean-ﬂow damped system exhibits persistent recurrent carrier–sideband focusing dynamics
together with organized Floquet-band evolution despite substantial interaction-phase restructuring, whereas the
viscous system exhibits progressively diffuse modulation dynamics together with repeated Floquet-band recon-
nection and weakening persistence of the recurrent carrier–sideband interaction structure.

Introduction
Two complementary perspectives have shaped the modern theory of nonlinear dispersive waves: the dynamical
description of resonant mode interactions and the development of soliton and inverse-scattering theory. Early
work of David J. Benney established that complex nonlinear wave evolution can often be reduced to the interaction
of a ﬁnite number of dominant modes, providing a systematic framework for describing nonlinear energy transfer
through low-dimensional dynamical systems [6, 7]. In the context of surface gravity waves, reduced evolution
equations derived directly from the Euler equations show how nonlinear boundary conditions generate coupled
mode interactions and constrained energy exchange among resonant modes [6, 7]. These ﬁnite-mode interaction
systems established a dynamical framework for modulational instability, sideband growth, nonlinear focusing
and recurrent wave evolution [34, 19].
This dynamical viewpoint was subsequently recast into a Hamiltonian framework in which resonant four-
wave interactions govern the transfer of energy and speciﬁc phase combinations govern the direction and strength
of nonlinear exchange [20, 10]. In this formulation, nonlinear wave evolution is organized not only by modal
amplitudes but also by the relative phases among interacting modes. The resulting amplitudephase description
remains central to the study of modulational instability, weak turbulence, and ﬁnite-mode truncation models
[12, 35]. In this setting, the interaction phases provide a natural description of coherent nonlinear energy exchange
among resonant modes.

A complementary spectral perspective emerged through the development of inverse-scattering thoery for non-
linear evolution equations. The work of Zakharov and Shabat established the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equa-
tion as an integrable system whose dynamics can be characterized by invariant nonlinear scattering data [33].
This framework was subsequently generalized by M. J. Ablowitz and collaborators through the AblowitzKaup-
NewellSegur (AKNS) formalism, extending the inverse-scattering methods to broad classes of nonlinear wave
equations [4, 1, 2] and providing the foundation for later developments in periodic inverse spectral and ﬁnite-gap
theory [3, 13].
For spatially periodic problems, the corresponding nonlinear spectral description arises through inverse spec-
tral theory and Floquet analysis of the associated Lax operator. In this setting, coherent nonlinear wave ﬁelds
are characterized by Floquet spectral data whose band structure encodes the underlying ﬁnite-gap dynamics
[8, 14]. Subsequent developments in ﬁnite-gap and algebraicgeometric integration further established the corre-
spondence between multi-phase nonlinear wave solutions and ﬁnite-band Floquet spectral data [17, 11].
Together, these developments established complementary dynamical and spectral frameworks for coherent
nonlinear wave evolution in integrable systems. On the one hand, ﬁnite-mode interaction theories emphasized
the role of resonant energy exchange and relative phase dynamics in organizing nonlinear wave motion. On the
other hand, inverse-scattering and ﬁnite-gap theories characterized coherent wave evolution through invariant
nonlinear spectral data and associated Floquet band structure.
For integrable equations, the nonlinear Floquet spectrum is invariant under the evolution and provides a
spectral characterization of the solution through its ﬁnite-gap structure. In weakly perturbed or near-integrable
settings, however, this spectral invariance is generally lost, and the evolution of the Floquet spectrum provides
a natural diagnostic for how perturbations alter coherent nonlinear dynamics. Subsequent studies of perturbed
near-integrable systems developed this perspective through analyses of nonlinear Floquet spectral evolution and
the associated spectral instabilities, using spectral band deformations and critical-point crossings to characterize
the onset of chaotic dynamics and the breakdown of coherent invariant structures under perturbation [15, 9, 16, 5,
18].
In a recent comparative study of rogue wave formation in dissipative higher-order nonlinear Schrödinger mod-
els, nonlinear Floquet spectral diagnostics revealed a striking structural distinction between viscous damping and
nonlinear mean-ﬂow damping [27]. Under viscous damping, the spectrum underwent repeated critical-point
crossings and continual spectral reconﬁguration, accompanied by broader multimode behavior and increasingly
diffuse rogue-wave events. In contrast, nonlinear mean-ﬂow damping produced sustained spectral band sepa-
ration with the complete absence of critical-point crossings and substantially lower phase variance. Long-lived
localized one- and two-band spectral conﬁgurations also persisted over extended time intervals. These observa-
tions suggested that the two dissipative mechanisms differ not only in how they dissipate energy, but also in how
they inﬂuence the organization of the underlying nonlinear modulation dynamics.
Although the two dissipative models exhibit markedly different nonlinear Floquet spectral dynamics, the
dynamical origin of this distinction is not immediately apparent from the governing equations themselves. In
particular, the observed differences suggest that the two damping mechanisms modify the dominant four-wave
interactions differently over the intermediate time regime considered here. This motivates the search for a re-
duced interaction-phase description capable of resolving how the dominant carrier-sideband interaction dynam-
ics evolve under the two dissipative mechanisms.
To investigate this distinction, we derive a reduced ﬁve-mode Fourier system that captures the dominant
local centersideband interactions underlying the nonlinear evolution. Reformulating the reduced dynamics in
amplitude-phase variables isolates a small set of interaction phases associated with the dominant carrier-sideband
four-wave interactions. The resulting reduced framework identiﬁes a low-dimensional interaction-phase mech-
anism strongly correlated with the persistence or breakdown of organized nonlinear Floquet spectral structure.
Within this framework, the two dissipative mechanisms enter differently at the level of the interaction-phase
dynamics. Viscous damping contributes primarily through modewise dissipation and does not directly modify
the principal interaction-phase dynamics. In contrast, nonlinear mean-ﬂow damping generates additional dissi-
pative contributions in the interaction-phase equations. In the spectrally localized regime, these terms produce
distinguished restoring-type contributions of the form −κj sin(ψj) introducing direct dissipative feedback into the
dominant carrier–sideband interaction dynamics.
This reduced interaction-phase description provides a dynamical framework for analyzing how recurrent
carrier–sideband interaction dynamics evolve during the nonlinear modulation dynamics. The interaction phases
are interpreted not as approximately conserved phase relations, but rather as diagnostics of the evolving carrier-
sideband interaction dynamics. Numerical diagnostics introduced later in the paper ﬁrst calibrate these phase vari-
ables using recurrent ﬁnite-gap NLS solutions, where the modulation dynamics are independently understood.

3
Five Mode Formulation
The signiﬁcant differences in the Floquet spectral evolution observed in Section 2 suggest that the essential dis-
tinction between the viscous and nonlocal mean-ﬂow damping regimes lies not simply in the number of active
Fourier modes, but in the persistence or breakdown of recurrent carrier–sideband interaction dynamics during
the dissipative evolution.
To examine these nonlinear interactions directly, we seek a reduced Fourier description that retains the min-
imal mode-coupling structure necessary to capture the leading four-wave mixing processes and their associated
interaction-phase dynamics. Modulational energy exchange is organized primarily through the carrier wave and
its nearest sidebands, while nonlinear coupling among the ﬁrst sidebands generates second-order harmonics. This
leads naturally to a ﬁve-mode Fourier truncation consisting of the carrier together with the ﬁrst two pairs of side-
bands. The purpose of this reduced system is therefore to clarify the carrier–sideband interaction mechanisms
through which the different damping terms inﬂuence interaction-phase evolution, recurrent modulation dynam-
ics, and the corresponding nonlinear Floquet spectral evolution.
Although the reduced model is derived in a carrier-centered modulational instability setting, the interaction
structure depends only on relative Fourier-mode separations. Consequently, the same reduced coupling structure
may also be interpreted relative to a shifted dominant spectral mode under moderate frequency downshifting.
In the numerical investigations of Section 5, however, attention is restricted to intermediate times for which the
dominant spectral peak remains centered near to the original carrier mode.

4.3
Interpretation of the reduced interaction-phase dynamics
The reduced interaction-phase equations reveal a distinguished structural difference between the viscous and
nonlinear mean-ﬂow damped systems.
In the viscous model, dissipation acts only through direct damping of the Fourier-mode amplitudes and does
not contribute explicitly to the leading interaction-phase equations. The interaction-phase evolution is therefore
governed by the conservative nonlinear interaction terms together with the indirect inﬂuence of viscous amplitude
decay.
By contrast, in the nonlinear mean-ﬂow damped system, the dissipative contribution enters directly through
the same nonlinear interaction structure governing the conservative mean-ﬂow feedback. At leading order, this
produces restoring-type contributions of the form
−κj sin(ψj)
in the principal interaction-phase equations.
The scalar equation
.ψ = −κ sin(ψ),

acts to oppose sustained monotone phase drift away from phase-aligned conﬁgurations. In the full reduced
interaction-phase equations, however, this restoring-type contribution remains coupled to the surrounding cubic,
mean-ﬂow, and self-steepening interactions. Consequently, the reduced equations do not imply phase locking or
bounded interaction-phase evolution in general. Rather, the reduced system indicates that nonlinear mean-ﬂow
damping contributes directly to the evolving carrier–sideband interaction dynamics through the same interaction

## Page 16


structure that governs recurrent modulation and focusing. The resulting interaction-phase evolution may there-
fore remain dynamically active and undergo substantial localized restructuring while still preserving persistent
recurrent carrier–sideband interaction organization.
In particular, the reduced interaction-phase equations suggest that nonlinear mean-ﬂow damping may in-
ﬂuence how the dominant interaction phases evolve relative to one another without suppressing substantial
interaction-phase evolution or localized restructuring. This distinction is important since recurrent and spectrally
organized nonlinear dynamics may coexist with substantial asymmetric interaction-phase evolution together with
recurrent focusing-associated restructuring events, as will be illustrated later using benchmark ﬁnite-gap solu-
tions.
The reduced interaction-phase equations are therefore intended primarily as a dynamical and diagnostic
framework for identifying leading carriersideband interaction mechanisms within the near-integrable modulation
regime, rather than as a complete asymptotic description of the full PDE dynamics or nonlinear Floquet spectral
evolution. The role of the reduced model is instead to isolate structural differences in how the two dissipative
mechanisms modify the dominant interaction-phase dynamics and to assess how these differences correlate with
the observed Floquet spectral organization in the full system.

Conclusion
In this work, we investigated the dynamical mechanisms underlying the contrasting nonlinear Floquet spectral
behavior observed in viscous and nonlinear mean-ﬂow damped higher-order nonlinear Schrödinger systems. Mo-
tivated by the persistent organized Floquet-band structure observed in the nonlinear mean-ﬂow damped case and
the repeated spectral reconﬁguration observed in the viscous case, we derived a reduced ﬁve-mode Fourier trun-
cation designed to identify the dominant carrier-sideband four-wave interactions.
Reformulating the reduced system in amplitude-phase variables showed that the essential dynamics is gov-
erned not by the individual modal phases, but by a small number of interaction phases arising directly from the
dominant carrier-sideband interaction products. In particular, the principal interaction phases ψ1 = ϕ1 + ϕ−1 −
2ϕ0 and ψ2 = ϕ2 + ϕ−2 −2ϕ0 provide reduced diagnostics of the evolving carrier-sideband interaction dynamics
associated with the dominant modulation structure.
The reduced interaction-phase equations revealed a clear structural distinction between the two dissipative
mechanisms. In the nonlinear mean-ﬂow damped system, the phase equations contain interaction-dependent
restoring contributions of the form −κj sin(ψj) absent from the viscous dynamics. These terms introduce direct
dissipative feedback into the dominant carriersideband interaction dynamics through the same interaction struc-
ture governing the conservative mean-ﬂow coupling. By contrast, viscous damping acts primarily through mod-
ewise dissipation and does not directly modify the leading interaction-phase dynamics in the same manner. The
reduced equations therefore suggest that the two dissipative mechanisms modify the dominant carrier-sideband
interaction dynamics differently over the recurrent intermediate time regime considered here.
The benchmark quasiperiodic ﬁnite-gap NLS solutions demonstrated that substantial cumulative interaction-
phase evolution may coexist with persistent recurrent focusing dynamics and ﬁnite-band Floquet structure. In par-
ticular, the benchmark examples showed that one interaction phase may remain comparatively bounded while the
other undergoes substantial cumulative drift without destroying the underlying recurrent modulation dynamics.
Numerical diagnostics of the full PDE evolution further showed that the interaction-phase evolution, recur-
rent modulation structure, and nonlinear Floquet spectral dynamics together provide a consistent dynamical
description of the dissipative evolutions. In the nonlinear mean-ﬂow damped system, the interaction phases
evolve asymmetrically, with ψ1 remaining comparatively bounded while ψ2 undergoes more substantial cumula-
tive drift. The largest restructuring events in the interaction phases occur near the strongest recurrent focusing
episodes, which likewise correlate with recurrent contraction of the localized Floquet bands. Despite the substan-
tial interaction-phase evolution and recurrent localized restructuring events, the corresponding Floquet spectra
remain persistently organized and evolve without critical point crossings or rapid spectral reconnection. Compar-
ison with the benchmark ﬁnite-gap solutions therefore suggests that the dominant carrier–sideband interactions
continue evolving within a persistent recurrent modulation regime throughout the nonlinear mean-ﬂow damped
evolution. The interaction-phase diagnostics further suggest that coherent recurrent carrier-sideband focusing it-
self remains closely tied to the localized restructuring of the dominant interaction dynamics during the dissipative
evolution.
In contrast, the viscous dynamics exhibit progressively less persistent recurrent carrier-sideband modulation
structure together with repeated critical-point crossings and Floquet-band reconnection. Although restructur-
ing events continue occurring in the interaction phases near both focusing episodes and spectral reconﬁguration
events, the interaction dynamics no longer exhibit the same recurrent focusing-associated organization observed
in the nonlinear mean-ﬂow damped evolution. The corresponding Floquet spectra undergo continual spectral re-
organization and repeated reconnection events, preventing individual Floquet bands from retaining a persistent
spectral identity over long times. Comparison with the benchmark ﬁnite-gap solutions therefore suggests pro-
gressively weakening persistence of the organized recurrent carrier-sideband interaction dynamics throughout
the viscous evolution.

Together these results suggest that the principal distinction between the two dissipative mechanisms lies not in
the presence of substantial interaction-phase evolution itself, but rather in the persistence or breakdown of recur-
rent carrier-sideband modulation structure during the dissipative evolution. In the nonlinear mean-ﬂow damped
system, the interaction-phase dynamics remain closely tied to recurrent localized focusing cycles and persistent
organized Floquet-band evolution, whereas the viscous dynamics exhibit progressively diffuse modulation struc-
ture together with continual Floquet-band reconnection and weakening recurrent carrier–sideband organization.
More broadly, the results demonstrate that reduced interaction-phase diagnostics, recurrent modulation dynamics,
and nonlinear Floquet spectral evolution together provide complementary perspectives for interpreting dissipa-
tive perturbations of near-integrable dispersive wave systems.
