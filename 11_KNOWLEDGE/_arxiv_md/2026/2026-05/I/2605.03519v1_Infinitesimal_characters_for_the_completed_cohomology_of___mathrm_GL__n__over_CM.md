Infinitesimal characters for the completed cohomology of
GLn over CM fields
Jelena Ivanˇci´c and Vaughan McDonald
Abstract
Let p be a prime, and let F be a CM field containing an imaginary quadratic field
in which p splits. We show that the locally analytic vectors of Hecke eigenspaces in the
(p-adic) completed cohomology of GLn /F localized at a non–Eisenstein decomposed
generic maximal ideal admit infinitesimal characters determined by the Hodge–Tate–
Sen weights of the corresponding Galois representations, thus confirming a conjecture
of Dospinescu–Paˇsk¯unas–Schraen in this case.

Introduction
Let p be a prime number and L/Qp a finite extension. For a connected reductive group
G/Q and a compact open level subgroup Kp ⊂G(Ap,∞
Q ), the completed cohomology groups
defined by Emerton [Eme06b] eHi := eHi(Kp, L) have proven to be a fruitful setting for
studying the p-adic Langlands program. These are p-adic Banach spaces with a continuous
action of both the group G(Qp) and a Hecke algebra TS(Kp), and contain rich information
about the relation between (p-adic) automorphic forms and Galois representations. One
recent version of this interplay is the study of infinitesimal characters, as systematically
initiated by Dospinescu–Paˇsk¯unas–Schraen in [DPS25]. Let us denote by eHi,la the space of
locally analytic vectors for G(Qp) in the completed cohomology eHi – this has an action of
the center of the universal enveloping algebra Z(g)Qp of g = Lie(G). This action captures
infinitesimal characters seen on the automorphic side, which by the Langlands program
should be related to p-adic Hodge theoretic properties of the corresponding local Galois
representations. In [DPS25], this is formulated as follows: given a Galois representation
ρ : GalF →LGf(Qp) (L–parameter), the authors construct a character ζC
ρ : Z(g)Qp →Qp
(using the Sen operator of ρ and a key shift from the L-group to the C-group) which encodes
(up to this shift) the Hodge–Tate–Sen weights of ρ. The authors of loc. cit. then conjecture
that the action of Z(g)Qp on eHi,la (up to a localization or passing to a Hecke eigenspace) is
given by ζC
ρ for a suitable choice of a L–parameter and a shift to the C–group, both choices
being governed by the global Langlands program.
More precisely, given a continuous ring homomorphism x : TS(Kp)[1/p] →Qp with
kernel mx, the global Langlands program predicts one can construct a Galois representation
ρx : GalQ,S →LGf(Qp) such that, at unramified primes ℓ/∈S, the conjugacy class of
ρx(Frobℓ) is related to the restriction of x to the local Hecke algebra at ℓunder the Satake
isomorphism.1 The recipe of [DPS25] produces from ρx a map ζC
ρx : Z(g)Qp →Qp. Their
conjecture then says the following:
Conjecture 1 ([DPS25, Conjecture 9.34]). Let x : TS(Kp)[1/p] →Qp be a continuous ring
homomorphism with the corresponding Galois representation ρx and i ≥0 an integer. Then
Z(g)Qp acts on (eHi[mx])la ⊗TS(Kp),x Qp via ζC
ρx.

ρ comes from Hodge-theoretic
properties of the Galois side, we propose the following terminology.2

Galois representation. We call the character ζC
ρ attached to ρ by [DPS25] a generalized
Hodge-Tate-Sen character of ρ. We use the same terminology for characters ζC
θ attached
to Galois pseudocharacters θ as in [PQ25] (which is a generalization of [DPS25]).
Our main theorem confirms Conjecture 1 (localized at a suitable maximal ideal of the

On the method of proof
To work around the obstacles mentioned above (in particular l0 > 0), we follow the lead
of recent work on the Langlands program over CM fields and relate the completed coho-
mology groups eHi,la

contains ResF/F + GLn as a Levi subgroup. Such a strategy has been exploited previously to
prove properties about automorphic Galois representations for GLn /F (see among others
[HLTT16, Sch15, ACC+23, CN23, A’C24, Hev23]). Most such papers proceed by looking at
cohomology with torsion coefficients, with the exception of second-named author’s previous
work [McD25]. Roughly speaking, all of these previous works realize the cohomology of
GLn /F (in any degree6) as a subquotient of the cohomology of eG in middle degree, which
generally is tightly connected to classical automorphic forms. This process is referred to as
“degree shifting”, and is often filled with technical obstacles.
In this work, we still prove that a certain middle degree cohomology of the unitary group

For convenience we briefly recall the definition of a determinant. Let A be a commutative
ring and M, N two A–modules. If CA denotes the category of commutative A-algebras, then
let M : CA →Set denote the functor B 7→M ⊗A B. An A–polynomial law P : M →N is a
natural transformation M →N, i.e. a collection of maps M ⊗A B →N ⊗A B, compatible
with scalar extensions via any morphism φ : B →B′ in CA. An A–polynomial law D is
called homogeneous of degree d ≥0 if D(bx) = bdD(x) for all B ∈CA, all b ∈B and all
x ∈M ⊗A B. If M and N are also A–algebras, then D is called multiplicative if it commutes
with multiplication morphisms, i.e. for all B ∈CA, all x, y ∈M ⊗A B, D(xy) = D(x)D(y).
Definition 3.1 ([Che14, p. 230]). Let A be a (topological) commutative ring, G a (topo-
logical) group and let B be a (topological) commutative A–algebra. A d-dimensional deter-
minant with values in B is a multiplicative and homogeneous of degree d A–polynomial law
D : A[G] →B. For any g ∈G the function D(X −g) ∈B[X] is called a characteristic
polynomial of g. We say D is continuous if the map G →B[X], g 7→D(X −g) is continuous
(equivalently, if all coefficient functions are continuous).
