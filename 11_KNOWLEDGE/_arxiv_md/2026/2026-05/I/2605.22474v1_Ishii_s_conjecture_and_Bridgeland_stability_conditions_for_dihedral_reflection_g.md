ISHII’S CONJECTURE AND BRIDGELAND STABILITY
CONDITIONS FOR DIHEDRAL REFLECTION GROUPS
SHU NIMURA
Abstract. We provide a new proof of Ishii’s conjecture for any dihedral reflec-
tion group G ⊂GL2(C) from the viewpoint of Bridgeland stability conditions.
Our strategy is to reduce the problem, via the derived McKay correspondence,
to a geometric construction of Bridgeland stability conditions on the root stack
of the maximal resolution along the strict transform of the discriminant divisor.
1. Introduction
1.1. Motivation. One of the central themes in the McKay correspondence is the
relationship between birational geometry and moduli spaces of representations
of noncommutative algebras. Craw and Ishii proposed a conjecture [11] on the
crepant resolutions of C3/G for any finite subgroup G ⊂SL3(C): every crepant
resolution arises as a moduli space of θ-stable representations by varying King
stability θ ∈Θ(G) [22]. The conjecture was proved for abelian groups in SL3(C) by
[11] and for general finite groups in SL3(C) by [39]. Furthermore, inspired by the
DK hypothesis, Ishii proposed the following conjecture in dimension two, extending
the Craw–Ishii conjecture to the non-crepant setting:
Conjecture 1.1 ([15]). Let G ⊂GL2(C) be any finite subgroup. Then, a resolution
of C2/G is dominated by the maximal resolution of the pair (C2/G, B) if and only
if X ∼= Mθ for some generic θ ∈Θ(G).
For the definitions, see §2. Here, we only note that if B = 0 (equivalently, if G acts
freely in codimension one), the crepant resolution, when it exists, is both minimal
and maximal. Ishii proved the “if” part in full generality, and also the “only if” part
for arbitrary abelian subgroups and for small finite subgroups G ⊂GL2(C), i.e.,
when G acts freely in codimension one. Later, [9] proved the conjecture for dihedral
reflection groups. This paper aims to give a new proof of Ishii’s conjecture in the case
of dihedral reflection groups via Bridgeland stability conditions and wall-crossing
phenomena.
1.2. Bridgeland stability. Bridgeland stability conditions were introduced by
Bridgeland [6] inspired by Douglas’s work on Π-stability [13]. Bridgeland stability
conditions play a central role in the study of triangulated categories arising in
birational geometry, representation theory, enumerative geometry, and mirror sym-
metry. Bridgeland stability can be regarded as a generalization of classical notions
of stability, such as Gieseker stability for sheaves on a variety and King stability for
representations of a noncommutative algebra.
In the context of birational geometry of surfaces, Toda interpreted the surface
MMP in terms of wall-crossing phenomena for moduli spaces of Bridgeland stable
objects [36]. We briefly review Toda’s work. Let Y be a smooth projective surface.

1.4. Relations with existing works. First, we discuss the relation with Ishii’s
approach. Our approach to Ishii’s conjecture is analogous to his. His essential case is
when G ⊃{±1} =: N. He considered the crepant resolution YN →C2/N, equipped
with an action of G. Given a smooth contraction Y →X, he proved that X can be
realized as a moduli space of G-constellations on YN. In other words, he realized
X as the moduli space of objects in cohc[YN/G] ⊂Dc([YN/G]), which is derived
equivalent to Dc([C2/G]). On the other hand, our first aim is to realize X as the
moduli space of complexes in Dc(Y), which is derived equivalent to Dc([C2/G]).
To put it simply, we aim to modify Ishii’s approach to a more flexible framework,
namely, Bridgeland moduli spaces. Ishii’s proof cannot be applied directly to our
case. Indeed, Ishii uses the fact that minimal resolution dominates YN/G, which is
not true in our situation since our C2/G is already the ‘minimal resolution’.
Next, we discuss the relation with existing works on Bridgeland stability. While
Bridgeland stability conditions have been extensively studied for many classes of
smooth projective surfaces and certain threefolds, relatively few works treat stability
conditions on Dc(X) for non-projective varieties X; see, for example, [2]. Our
formulation mainly follows the framework developed in [2].
On the other hand, there are several works on stability conditions for Deligne–
Mumford stacks, including projective Kleinian orbisurfaces whose coarse moduli
spaces are projective surfaces with only Kleinian singularities [25]. Stability con-
ditions have also been studied for root stacks over projective curves [32] and over
projective surfaces [26]. We note that [26] mainly constructs stability conditions
corresponding to the large volume limit. Although the class of stacks considered
here is rather special, our setting admits a rich family of stability conditions. Some
wall-crossing phenomena appearing in our construction (cf. Lemma 3.22, Lemma
4.12) already appeared implicitly in the one-dimensional projective settings [32].
Other wall-crossing phenomena can be regarded as direct generalizations of Toda’s
work [36].
It is also worth mentioning that induced stability provides a useful method
for studying Bridgeland stability conditions on G-equivariant derived categories, as
developed in [27] [31]. Such induced stability conditions are often easier to analyze.
This viewpoint plays a key role in the comparison between the two constructions of
stability conditions carried out in §4.
1.5. Plan of the paper. In §2, we review the precise statement of Ishii’s conjecture
and several related notions. We also recall some preliminary results on Bridgeland
stability conditions, especially for (non-projective) surfaces. In §3, we construct the
map µgeom using Toda’s construction. We also prove Theorem A except for the
proof of the existence of the Bridgeland moduli spaces. In §4, we prove Theorem B.
Using the gluing result established there, we also resolve the existence problem of
the moduli spaces, thereby completing the proof of Theorem A. Finally, in §5, for
the reader’s convenience, we sketch a proof of the support property for stability
conditions on non-projective surfaces.
1.6. Notation. Throughout this paper, all schemes, DM stacks, and algebras are
defined over C. We consider the left action of G on C2 induced by the embedding
G ⊂GL2(C). We frequently use Dc(X) to denote the full triangulated subcategory of
D(X) := Db(coh X) consisting of objects whose cohomology sheaves are compactly
supported. There is a natural equivalence Db(cohc X) ∼= Dc(X) (for example, see
[21, Proposition 1.7.11]). For subcategories A, B of a triangulated category C, we
use the notation A ∗B to denote the full subcategory consisting of objects E fitting
into an exact triangle A →E →B →A[1] for some A ∈A and B ∈B.
