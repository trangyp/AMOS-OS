Introduction
An alternative formulation for the Ramsey numbers R(H, Kk) is that every
graph G on at least R(H, Kk) vertices either contains H or has independence
number α(G) ≥k, that is it contains an induced Ek = Kk.
However, it is no longer true that R(H, F), where F /∈{Kk, Ek}, forces
that every graph G on at least R(H, F) vertices contains either H or F, as
evident by red G = En versus blue G = Kn for every n.
We put forward two problems concerning this situation, which we shall
present after introducing some necessary terminology.
Let A and B be two graphs. By standard definition A is a subgraph of
B if V (A) ⊆V (B) and E(A) ⊆E(B). Moreover, if two vertices in A are
adjacent whenever they are adjacent in B, then A is said to be an induced
subgraph of B.
If A is isomorphic to a subgraph (not necessarity induced) of B, then B
is said to contain A.
Let H be a graph on k vertices, and let G be a graph on at least k vertices.
Then G is said to be H-exact if any induced subgraph of G on k vertices is
either isomorphic to H or it neither contains nor is contained in H. Then,
Exact(H) is the family of all graphs G which are H-exact.
We say that a graph on k vertices is incomparable with H if it neither
contains nor is contained in H. The set of all such graphs is denoted by
Inc(H). Then we let Leg(H) be H ∪Inc(H). Therefore G is H-exact if all
its induced subgraphs on k vertices are in Leg(H). On the other hand if a
graph on k vertices contains or is contained in H, we say it is comparable
to H and the set of graphs comparable to H (including H) is denoted by
Com(H). We let Com∗(H) = Com(H)\{H}.
We now present the two main definitions:
For a graph H on k vertices, let
1. f(H) = max{n : ∃G ∈Exact(H), |V (G)| = n}.
2. g(H) =
min{n : ∀G, |V (G)| ≥n, G contains a k-vertex induced graph F ∈Com(H)}.
Our main concern in this paper is either to determine the exact value of
f(H) and g(H), or to find lower and upper bounds for them.
In Section 2 we present some simple constructions of H-exact graphs,
which further motivate the study of f(H), as well as some simple facts con-
cerning the notation.

Basic Observations and Simple Examples
We first give some simple examples to illustrate the concept of H-exact.
Construction via vertex transitive graphs: Let F be a vertex transitive
graph on k vertices and let G = tF (the union of t ≥1 vertex disjoint copies
of F). Let F ∗= F \{v}, which is independent of v since F is vertex transitive.
Deleting any vertex from G = tF, we get H = F ∗∪(t −1)F and hence
G is H-exact. Now G = tF (the complement of G) is also a vertex transitive
graph and is Q-exact where Q = G \ {v}.
Construction via regular graphs: For every r-regular graph G on k + 1
vertices and any subgraph H on k vertices, G is H-exact. This is because
for every k-vertex induced subgraph H, the number of edges is fixed at
e(H) = e(G) −r. Thus, either F, an induced subgraph of G on k vertices, is
isomorphic to H or F is incomparable with H, hence G is H-exact.
So, finding examples of H-exact graphs where |V (G)| = |V (H)|+1 is easy.
The situation becomes more involved if |V (G)| ≥|V (H)| + 2, since we know
that if in G every k vertices induce a subgraph having the same number of
edges, then either |V (G)| = k+1 and G is regular, or |V (G)| ≥k+2 and G ∈
{Kn, En}, as shown in [2]. Hence the problem of the existence/construction
of H-exact graphs with |V (G)| ≥|V (H)| + 2 as well as the determination or
bounding f(H) is of interest, and we shall deal with these problems in the
following sections.
We now give some useful observations which we shall use in the sequel.

3.1
The function g(H)
Recall that for a graph H on k vertices,
g(H) = min{n : ∀G, |V (G)| ≥n, contains a k-vertex induced graph F ∈Com(H)}.
The next theorem relates g(H) to R(H, H).
Theorem 3.1. g(H) = R(H, H) = R(H, H) = g(H).
Proof. Upper bound: Suppose |V (G)| ≥R(H, H). By the definition of Ram-
sey numbers, either G contains H or G contains H (not necessarily induced).

If G contains H (even not as an induced subgraph), then the induced graph
F on this vertex set contains H and hence F ∈Com(H). Otherwise, G
contains H. If H is induced in the k-vertex set, then in G this same k-vertex
set induces H. Else if H is not induced, then this vertex set induces, in G,
some F containing H; and the same vertex set induces, in G, the subgraph
F contained in H. Hence g(H) ≤R(H, H).
Lower bound: Consider the largest n such that E(Kn) has a red-blue
colouring without monochromatic red H or monochromatic blue H. Let G
be the red graph, then |V (G)| = R(H, H) −1 and G cannot contain H or a
k-vertex graph containing H. If it contains an induced k-vertex F contained
in H, then in G, F contains H, a contradiction. Hence g(H) = R(H, H).
By symmetry of Ramsey numbers, g(H) = R(H, H) = R(H, H) = g(H)
follows by the same argument.
Corollary 3.2. Every graph G on at least R(H, H) vertices contains a k-
vertex induced subgraph Q ∈Com(H) and a k-vertex induced subgraph F ∈
Com(H).

Proof. For H ∈{Kk, Kk\e, Ek, Kk\e}, this is trivial by direct checking, since

Conclusion
In this paper we were mainly interested in the following problem:
Given a graph H, determine f(H) = max{n : ∃G ∈Exact(H), |V (G)| = n}.
We have presented many results on f(H). We consider the following open
problems as a starting point for future research. .
1. For every tree T, determine f(T) or at least prove that
(α(T) −1)(|V (T)| −1) ≤f(T) ≤(α(T) −1)|V (T)|.
2. Prove that f(G) ≤R(G, Kk) holds for every graph G /∈{Kk.Ek} on
k ≥3 vertices.
