Interpreting Bohm quantum potentials in
”Computing quantum waves exactly from classical action”
Winfried Lohmiller and Jean-Jacques Slotine
Nonlinear Systems Laboratory
Massachusetts Institute of Technology
Cambridge, Massachusetts, 02139, USA
{wslohmil, jjs}@mit.edu
Abstract
The recent arXiv posting [11], commenting on the paper [7], argues that the proof
of Lemma 3.1 in [7] is missing the Bohm quantum potential [1, 2] of the Madelung
p.d.e. [9]. This short technical note extends the proof of Lemma 3.1 to introduce a
Bohm quantum potential explicitly, and then shows why this term can be assumed to
be zero in the wave construction, without loss of generality.
The continuity p.d.e. and the Hamilton-Jacobi p.d.e., extended by the Bohm poten-
tial, are undisputed. However, the actual action and density solutions depend on their
initialization at t = 0. In [7], this initialization is motivated by the Feynman kernel
[4], which is fundamentally different from the standard initialization of the Madelung
solution [9]. This in turn leads to different action and density solutions, and explains
why in one case the Bohm quantum potential disappears and in the other does not. The
resulting overall wave, however, is independent of this computational initialization.
Keywords −
Bohm quantum potential, Madelung equation, Computing quantum
waves exactly from classical action

Introduction
The following extends the proof of Lemma 3.1 in [7] to address the concern that a spatial
derivative term is missing in the kernel calculation of Lemma 3.1. To make this issue more
transparent, a derivation in fixed coordinates is used, where the Bohm quantum potential
[1, 2] appears explicitly.
Lemma 3.1 in [7] computes a Feynman kernel from a given initial position or momen-
tum. This allows to initialize the multi-paths with a common constant density. As we shall
see, this yields a pure time-varying divergence rate, which implies a vanishing Bohm quan-
tum potential. Because of the different initial conditions, this result is very different from
the standard Madelung density solution.

• At t = 0, the path starts at xo ⊻po. Assuming for instance a given initial xo this
implies that ϕ(xo, 0) = 0 and is undefined for x ̸= xo .
• At t = 0, all j ∈B multipaths are initialized without loss of generality with an
identical constant density √ρoj(xo ⊻po, 0). Hence at t = 0 it is guaranteed that the
Bohm potential Qj vanishes. This is in general not the case when using a general
density field ρ(x, t) = ψψ∗as in (3.1*).
• The density ρj is computed for each individual action branch j ∈B of Theorem 2.4.
We will see later on that these 3 key differences to the Madelung density (3.1*) ρ = ψψ∗
[12, 9], allow to exactly remove the Bohm quantum potential Qj in all classical and rela-
tivistic examples of this paper, including the Pauli and Dirac equations of Section 4.(b) and
the Maxwell equation of Section 4.(a).

separation of this last step with the kernel computation (3.1), introduced by Feynman [4],
is a key element in enabling the above argumentation.

Summary
Section 2 clarifies why the Bohm quantum potential ∆M√ρj/√ρj is exactly zero in the
computation of the Feynman kernel ψj(x, t, xo ⊻po) [7]. It provides a more complete
description and derivation of Lemma 3.1 of [7].
All Examples in [7], as well as the derivations in the paper of the Pauli, Dirac, and
Maxwell equations, analytically compute the classical density ρj of the Feynman kernel.
Since in all cases this density is only time-dependent, it can be directly concluded that the
Bohm quantum potential ℏ2
