IMAGES OF LIE POLYNOMIALS ON SIMPLE LIE ALGEBRAS
HARISH KISHNANI AND ANUPAM SINGH
Abstract. A Lie polynomial is an element of a free Lie algebra Fk on k-generators, which
defines a Lie map on a given Lie algebra L, by substituting k-elements of L. Similar to word
maps on groups and polynomial maps on algebras, one studies here questions analogous to
Waring-like problems, the L’vov-Kaplansky conjecture, etc. In this article, we would like to
address a problem for Lie algebras parallel to the one Lubotzky solved (Images of word maps
in finite simple groups, Glasg. Math. J., 56, no. 2, 465-469, 2014) for finite simple groups. It is
easy to verify that the image of a Lie map is (a) closed under automorphism, and (b) contains
0. In this article, we prove that for a simple Chevalley algebra over a finite field of “very good”
characteristic, these two properties are enough to classify all possible subsets that can be the
image of a Lie polynomial. The next question is to find such Lie polynomials for a given subset
satisfying the two properties. Contrary to the results over an algebraically closed field, we find
Lie polynomials in the case of Lie algebra sl2(q), for q odd, which give each GL2(q) conjugacy
class together with zero as an image.
1. Introduction
An element w of a free group on k-generators defines a word map on a group G. Images and
fibers of such maps (see, for example [LST11,GKP16,GLO+18]) have been extensively studied
in the context of Waring-like problems, including Ore’s conjecture. Several fundamental results
have been proved in this regard over the last 3 decades, especially for finite simple groups, Lie
groups, and algebraic groups. An analogous problem, motivated by the Waring problem, has
been studied over both associative and non-associative algebras (see, for example [KBMR16,
GM22,BˇS23,GM23,KS25,PSS25,PSS26]). A polynomial f(x1, . . . , xk) ∈F < x1, . . . , xk > in
k-variables (non-commuting) defines a polynomial map on a given associative F-algebra A
by evaluation. Surjectivity of such maps and when it is not, whether a finite sum of images
(analogous to the Waring problem) gives the whole algebra, are some of the main questions
in the subject. One of the most notable conjectures here is the L’vov-Kaplansky conjecture,

In this article, we focus on similar questions over Lie algebras.
Let L be a Lie algebra over a field F and Fk be a free Lie algebra of rank k over F generated
by x1, x2, . . . , xk. An element w ∈Fk is called a Lie polynomial. A Lie polynomial w ∈Fk,
induces a Lie polynomial map ˜w: Lk →L, defined by sending a k-tuple (v1, v2, . . . , vk)
to w(v1, v2, . . . , vk). As customary in the subject, we use the same notation w for the Lie
polynomial and the induced Lie polynomial map instead of ˜w. The image of the Lie polynomial
map induced by a word w is denoted by w(L). A simple example would be w = [ , ], the Lie
bracket on L, and the question would be if this induces a surjective map. This has been studied
by several authors (see, for example, [Bro63,Akh15,Kra23]) and doesn’t seem to be fully solved
over an arbitrary field. Questions about more general Lie polynomials have been studied in
the literature too; see, for example [BGKP12,AEV15,KBMR17,NR23,CF24], but seem quite
challenging. We are interested in studying what kind of subset of a Lie algebra L could be a
possible image of a Lie polynomial map, which we will make more precise below.
It is quite easy to see that the image of a word map on a group is closed under automorphisms
and contains the identity. In a brilliant work [Lub14], Lubotzky classified that any subset
A of a non-Abelian finite simple group G which is closed under automorphism, i.e., ϕ(A) ⊂
A, ∀ϕ ∈Aut(G), and contains the identity is an image of a word in two variables. In [Chu90],
Chuang classified images of a polynomial map without constants on matrix algebra over Fq
and showed that these are exactly the ones that contain 0 and are closed under conjugation
by invertible matrices (in view of Skolem-Noether theorem, these are all automorphisms). We
wish to consider the analogous question here for finite simple Lie algebras.
Let L be a simple Lie algebra over Fq of Chevalley type (also called Chevalley Lie algebra or
Chevalley algebra). We refer [Car89,Sel67] for the general theory. We assume the characteristic
of the field Fq is a “very good” prime. Note that the Lie algebra may have a center (e.g. sln(q)
for certain cases) over Fq, hence will be simple modulo its center. Let Aut(L) denote the set
of Lie algebra automorphisms for the Lie algebra L. Then, a subset A ⊂L has the following
properties:
(1) 0 ∈A.
(2) ϕ(A) = A, for all ϕ ∈Aut(L).
We ask the following questions:
Question 1.1. Let A ⊂L satisfying the two above properties. Does there exist a Lie polynomial
w in finitely many variables such that w(L) = A?
Question 1.2. If the answer to the Question 1.1 is positive, can we find some such Lie
polynomials that give the set A as its image?

In this article, we answer the first question affirmatively for Chevalley Lie algebras over finite
fields of “very good” characteristic. Our main theorem is following:
Theorem 1.3. Let L be a simple Chevalley algebra over a finite field F of “very good”
characteristic. Let k(L) denote the minimum number such that for each non-zero element a ∈L,
there exists elements a2, a3, . . . , ak(L) in L such that the set {a1 = a, a2, a3, . . . , ak(L)} generates
L. Then, every subset A ⊂L containing 0 and closed under automorphisms of L is an image
of some Lie polynomial from Fk(L).
The number k(L) is a finite number because of dimension reasons, although an absolute constant
is desirable. For finite simple groups, it is 2 what is called one-and-a-half generation, but it
doesn’t seem to be known for all finite simple Lie algebras (over an algebraically closed field, it
is known to be 2). For a brief discussion on this, see Subsection 2.3. The main Theorem is
proved in Section 3, Theorem 3.1. We collect several basic examples related to the Theorem in
Section 4.
The second question is more challenging. In [BGKP12], Bandman et. al. showed dominance
of Lie maps on Chevalley Lie algebras. In [KBMR17], Kanel-belov et. al. showed that over an
algebraically closed field of characteristic ̸= 2, the possible images of Lie polynomials on sl2 are
the whole set sl2, {0}, or the subset consisting of all non-nilpotent elements. Contrary to this, in
Section 5, 6, 7, 8, we take the Lie algebra sl2(q) and find Lie polynomials, often a combination
of Engel polynomials, and show that each of the automorphism orbits together with 0 can be
obtained as an image. Thus, a single conjugacy class of a semisimple element together with
zero is an image of some specific Lie polynomial. This is summarised in Theorem 9.1. We hope
to continue this work in the future to solve the Problem 1.2.

2. Preliminaries
Let g be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra over C. We know that for such an algebra,
there exists a Chevalley basis that defines the Lie algebra over Z, denoted as gZ. Let F be a
field. One defines a Lie algebra gF = gZ ⊗F, called a Chevalley algebra. For a detailed account,
we refer the reader to the excellent books [Car89,Sel67]. In this article, we fix the base field
F = Fq of “very good” characteristic and consider only these Lie algebras (which in the subject
of Modular Lie algebras are referred to as classical, but we refer to these as Chevalley algebras).
These Lie algebras gFq may not be simple, but are simple modulo their center. Thanks to
Steinberg’s work (see [Ste61], and also see Chapter III [Sel67]), the automorphisms of these Lie
algebras are well known. These are usually the adjoint action of the corresponding Chevalley
group together with diagonal and graph automorphisms (see Theorem III.5.1 [Sel67]).

2.1. Lie maps. Let X = {x1, . . . , xk} by a set and F be a field. A free Lie algebra Fk on the
set X with ι: X →Fk is a Lie algebra which has the following universal property: For any
Lie algebra g over F and a map χ: X →g there exists a unique Lie algebra homomorphism
˜χ: Fk →g such that χ = ˜χ ◦ι. Formally, the elements of Fk can be thought of as a sum of
finitely many Lie monomials, which are in turn, finitely many steps of brackets of brackets on
the elements xi. For example, e1(xi, xj) = [xi, xj] and the Engel words.
An element w ∈Fk over the field F defines a Lie map on a Lie algebra L over F, simply given
by evaluation. Let w(L) ⊂L be the image of L under w. Clearly, by taking all variables 0, we get
0 ∈w(L). Further, for any ψ ∈Aut(L), we know that ψ(w(x1, . . . , xk)) = w(ψ(x1), . . . , ψ(xk))
and hence ψ(w(L)) ⊂w(L). Our Question 1.1 asks if these properties are enough to classify
the possible subset of L that are the image of some w ∈Fk.
When L is an Abelian Lie algebra, we can answer this easily.
Proposition 2.1. Let L be an Abelian Lie algebra over a field F. Then, every subset A of L
containing 0 and closed under automorphisms is an image of some Lie polynomial.
Proof. Since L is Abelian, every vector space automorphism of L is also a Lie algebra automor-
phism. Thus, for any v, w ∈L\{0}, there exists α ∈Aut(L) such that α(v) = w. Hence, the
only possible subset of L which could be an image is {0} or L. These are images for the Lie
polynomials [x1, x2] and x1, respectively.
□

or a Lie algebra that has no maximal solvable ideal. We state the Jordan–H¨older theorem for
Lie algebras, which is required later in our work.

2.3. Few generators for simple Lie algebras. For our work, we need a bound on the
number of generators for simple Lie algebras. For non-Abelian finite simple groups, it has
been proved by Steinberg [Ste62] that each such group is generated by 2 elements. In fact,
a stronger result is known due to Guralnick and Kantor [GK00], called the one-and-a-half
generation. That is, for any fixed non-trivial element x, there exists another element y such
that x, y generate the group. Similar questions have been studied for simple Lie algebras.
Let F be a field. When F is characteristic 0, Kuranashi [Kur51] proved that simple Lie
algebras are generated by 2 elements. Ionescu [Ion76] proved that simple Lie algebras over
C are one-and-a-half generated. When F is an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 or
p ≥5, it has been proved by Bois [Boi09] that any Chevalley algebra is 2 generated. Further, it
has been proved that these are one-and-a-half generated.
For any element x ∈L, a simple Lie algebra over a finite field F, does there exist a generating
set x1 = x, x2, . . . , xk? If it exists, the smallest such k is denoted as k(L). Clearly, a vector space
basis of L would work as k, hence k(L) ≤dim(L). In fact, because of root space decomposition
for a simple Lie algebra, a better bound could be taken as the number of roots. For sln(q), it
has been proved in [CJZ25] that this Lie algebra is 2-generated. However, a clear answer to
k(L) seems not to be known.
3. Analog of Lubotzky’s Theorem for finite simple Lie algebras
We recall that we consider L a simple Chevalley Lie algebra over a field F of “very good”
characteristic. For L over F, let k(L) denote the minimum number such that for each non-
zero element a ∈L, there exists elements a2, a3, . . . , ak(L) in L such that the set {a1 =
a, a2, a3, . . . , ak(L)} generates L.
Since k(L) ≤dim(L), it is a finite number.

4. Some Examples
In this section, we present examples to illustrate the strength and limitations of the Theorem
proved earlier.
Example 4.1. The conclusion of Theorem 3.1 may not hold if L is not a simple Lie algebra.
For example, if L = sl2(q)⊕sl2(q), then the automorphism orbits of L are of the type (O1, O2)∪
(O2, O1), where O1 and O2 are automorphism orbits of sl2(q). Thus, if O1 ̸= O2, then for any Lie
polynomial w, w(L) = w(sl2(q) ⊕sl2(q)) = w(sl2(q)) ⊕w(sl2(q)) ̸= (O1, O2) ∪(O2, O1) ∪(0, 0).
Hence, (O1, O2) ∪(O2, O1) ∪(0, 0) satisfies the two required criteria in the theorem to be an
image, but is not an image.
Example 4.2. In [BGKP12], examples of Lie polynomials with dominant image are given
in Remark 3.6, 3.7, and Example 3.8. Further, taking the sum of images of two dominant
morphisms will give surjectivity. Using this, the authors show surjectivity of Engel polynomials
under some mild hypotheses.
