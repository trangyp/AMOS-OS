Abstract: We study a time-dependent non-Hermitian extension of the Sch¨utte-Da Providˆencia
spin-boson Hamiltonian with complex couplings. A time-dependent Dyson map containing
a squeezing transformation maps the model, in an admissible bounded regime, to a Her-
mitian Hamiltonian with real instantaneous energy spectrum. The squeezing contribution
generates a dilatation term allowing the Hermitian partner to be interpreted as a fixed-
domain representation of a system with moving boundaries. While the fixed-boundary
Hermitian model conserves Q = N −S0 and forbids transitions between sectors differing
by two bosonic quanta, the boundary motion opens such channels.
For closed bound-
ary protocols with constant background parameters the first-order integrated transition
amplitude vanishes, reflecting the unitary nature of constant squeezing. Nontrivial transi-
tion control arises when the non-Hermitian parameter varies during the boundary motion,
changing the dressed basis and allowing boundary-induced transitions to be suppressed or
enhanced by coherent interference.
Keywords: Non-Hermitian spin-boson models; pseudo/quasi-Hermiticity; PT-symmetric
quantum theory, time-dependent Dyson map; moving boundaries; squeezing transforma-
tions; boundary-induced transitions

Introduction
Non-Hermitian Hamiltonians have become an important tool for effective descriptions of
open quantum and wave systems, including systems with gain and loss, dissipative cou-
plings, PT symmetry, exceptional points, and non-reciprocal transport [1–4]. In general
such Hamiltonians need not possess real spectra or unitary time evolution. However, if
a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian is related to a Hermitian Hamiltonian by a suitable Dyson
map, the system may be interpreted as a consistent quasi-Hermitian/PT-symmetric quan-
tum theory [5–8]. In this setting the inner product is modified by a metric operator and
the physical observables are not necessarily the operators that appear most naturally in
the auxiliary Hilbert space [9].
For time-independent systems the central requirement is usually the quasi-Hermiticity
relation between a non-Hermitian Hamiltonian, a Hermitian counterpart, and a positive
metric. For time-dependent systems the situation is more subtle. The Dyson map itself
contributes to the generator of time evolution through an additional gauge-like term. As
a consequence, the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian that generates the Schr¨odinger equation is
not, in general, the physical energy observable [10, 11]. Instead, the observable energy is
obtained by transforming the Hermitian Hamiltonian back with the inverse Dyson map.
This distinction is essential in time-dependent non-Hermitian quantum mechanics and will
play a central role below.
Here we study such a setup in a time-dependent extension of the Sch¨utte-Da Providˆencia
spin-boson model [12]. The original model couples a bosonic mode to an SU(2) spin degree
of freedom through the interaction terms S+b† and S−b. Related boson-mapping techniques
have also been employed in finite-temperature many-body contexts, for instance within
thermo-field dynamics [13]. Time-independent pseudo-Hermitian versions of this model
have been considered previously, for example in [14], where an asymmetry parameter was
introduced in the spin-boson interaction and the thermodynamics of the resulting pseudo-
Hermitian model was analysed. The present work differs in that both the Hamiltonian
and the Dyson map are explicitly time-dependent, with the latter containing three parts:
a bosonic squeezing transformation, a bosonic number-operator scaling, and a spin-sector
scaling. The additional squeezing part of the Dyson map is used to establish an effective
moving-boundary interpretation and to study boundary-induced transitions.
A key point is that the squeezing part of the Dyson map has a geometric interpretation
as previously observed in [15]. In the standard coordinate representation the time derivative
of the squeezing parameter produces a term proportional to the generator of dilatations,
which is precisely the structure that appears when a Hermitian quantum system on a time-
dependent interval is transformed to a fixed reference interval. Thus the Hermitian partner
of the non-Hermitian fixed-domain Hamiltonian may also be viewed as the fixed-domain
representation of a Hermitian system with moving boundaries.
Within the regime that guarantees the boundedness of the Dyson map the physical
energy spectrum is real. Thus, here the main effect of the non-Hermitian deformation is not
the appearance of exceptional points and complex energy levels, but instead, the interesting
effects are dynamical. In the fixed-boundary Hermitian model the operator Q = N −S0 is

conserved, and the spin-boson Hilbert space decomposes into independent two-dimensional
sectors. The boundary motion breaks this conservation law: the dilatation term contains
b†2 and b2, and therefore couples states whose boson numbers differ by two. Hence moving
boundaries induce transitions between sectors that are decoupled in the fixed-boundary
model.
We analysed these induced transitions perturbatively for small squeezing amplitude
and boundary velocity. The first-order correction to the instantaneous energy levels van-
ishes, so the leading spectral shifts occur at second order.
However, the same matrix
elements that determine these second order energy corrections also determine first-order
transition amplitudes between dressed spin-boson sectors. This provides a direct way of
distinguishing the fixed-boundary model from the moving-boundary system.
A further effect appears when the non-Hermitian parameter is varied during the bound-
ary motion. For closed boundary protocols with time-independent background parameters,
the first-order integrated transition amplitude reduces to an endpoint term and vanishes in
the dressed basis. This cancellation reflects the fact that constant squeezing is only a uni-
tary change of basis. If, however, the non-Hermitian deformation changes the dressed basis
during the motion, the integrated amplitude need not vanish. The transition probability
can then be suppressed or enhanced by coherent interference. Thus the non-Hermitian
deformation acts as a control parameter for boundary-induced transitions, even though
the instantaneous physical energy spectrum remains real.
Boundary-induced transitions are familiar in Hermitian systems with time-dependent
domains. For example, a particle in an infinite well with a moving wall develops transi-
tions between instantaneous modes, and in field theory moving cavity boundaries lead to
parametric excitation of the field, known as the dynamical Casimir effect [16–18]. Related
non-Hermitian transition phenomena have been studied previously [19], where it was found
that non-Hermitian perturbations can induce asymmetric, unidirectional, or even transi-
tionless dynamics, and in exceptional-point encircling, where nonadiabatic transitions lead
to chiral state conversion.
The present work is different in that the moving-boundary
term is generated by a time-dependent Dyson map of a non-Hermitian spin-boson Hamil-
tonian, and the transition amplitudes can be controlled by the time dependence of the
non-Hermitian metric.
Our manuscript is organised as follows: In section 2 we construct the time-dependent
Dyson map and derive the Hermiticity conditions for the transformed Hamiltonian. We
then explain the moving-boundary interpretation of the squeezing-induced dilatation term.
In section 3 we discuss the physical energy operator and its instantaneous spectrum, in-
cluding the non-squeezing and constant-squeezing limits.
We then treat the genuinely
time-dependent squeezing regime perturbatively and compute the relevant second-order
energy corrections. In section 4 we use the same matrix elements to analyse boundary-
induced transition amplitudes and show how time-dependent non-Hermiticity can control
their suppression or enhancement. We state our conclusions in section 5.

We note that the spin-1/2 representation of our model (2.1) is closely related to,
but not identical with, the Jaynes-Cummings model [20].

This distinction should also be kept separate from exceptional-point physics.
In time-dependent non-Hermitian Jaynes-Cummings models with an imaginary coupling,
exceptional points arise because the block frequency contains a difference of squares [21].
In the present bounded quasi-Hermitian regime, by contrast, the block frequencies in (3.10)
do not generate exceptional points.
For the closely related collective light-matter models, the Tavis-Cummings model [22],
which is the many-atom or collective-spin rotating-wave generalisation of the Jaynes-
Cummings model, and the Dicke model [23], which also contains counter-rotating couplings,
several non-Hermitian extensions have been considered [24–27]. These studies typically in-
troduce non-Hermiticity through static imaginary couplings, phenomenological decay rates,
gain-loss terms, or effective time-independent Hamiltonians, and often focus on real spectra,
exceptional points, or non-Hermitian phase transitions. By contrast, the present construc-
tion involves an explicitly time-dependent quasi-Hermitian Dyson map whose squeezing
part admits an effective moving-boundary interpretation.

instantaneous matrix element remains generally nonzero, but the accumulated transition
amplitude can be suppressed or enhanced by coherent interference.
5
Conclusion
We have constructed a time-dependent quasi-Hermitian extension of the Sch¨utte-Da Providˆencia
spin-boson model with complex, time-dependent couplings. By solving the time-dependent
Dyson equation (2.2) with a Dyson map (2.3) containing a bosonic squeezing factor, a
number-operator scaling and a spin scaling, we obtained explicit conditions under which
the transformed Hamiltonian h(t) is Hermitian. Requiring the Dyson map and its inverse
to be bounded selects solution I as the natural regime for the subsequent analysis, while
solution II would require a more careful domain-dependent treatment.
A central observation is that the squeezing part of the Dyson map has a direct geometric
interpretation. Its time derivative produces the dilatation term (2.8), which is precisely the
inertial term arising when a Hermitian system on a time-dependent interval is transformed
to a fixed domain. Thus the Hermitian partner of the non-Hermitian fixed-domain model
may be interpreted as the fixed-domain representation of a Hermitian system with moving
boundaries.

We also analyse the properties of the physical energy observable eH(t), which in the
time-dependent quasi-Hermitian setting is distinct from the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H(t) which is the generator of time evolution.
In the admissible bounded regime this
operator is similar to the Hermitian Hamiltonian h(t), and therefore its instantaneous
spectrum is real. Hence the non-Hermitian deformation considered here does not lead to
exceptional points or complex physical energies. Level crossings, when present, are ordinary
Hermitian degeneracies rather than defective spectral singularities.
The genuinely new effects are instead dynamical. In the fixed-boundary Hermitian
model the operator Q = N −S0 is conserved, and the Hilbert space splits into two-
dimensional spin-boson sectors. The boundary motion breaks this conservation law through
terms containing b†2 and b2, thereby opening transitions between sectors whose boson num-
bers differ by two. The first-order correction to the instantaneous energy levels vanishes, so
the leading spectral shifts arise at second order. The corresponding corrected levels remain
real, as expected from the quasi-Hermitian construction.
Finally, we showed that the same matrix elements controlling the second-order energy
corrections also determine first-order transition amplitudes between dressed sectors. For
closed boundary protocols with constant background parameters, the integrated first-order
transition amplitude reduces to an endpoint contribution and vanishes. This reflects the
fact that constant squeezing is a unitary change of basis. Nontrivial control appears when
the non-Hermitian parameter varies during the boundary motion. In that case the dressed
basis changes in time, and the accumulated transition amplitude can be suppressed or
enhanced by coherent interference. Thus the admissible non-Hermitian deformation yields a
robust quasi-Hermitian framework: the physical energy spectrum remains real in the entire
parameter regime considered, while the time-dependent Dyson map provides a mechanism
for controlling boundary-induced transitions in the equivalent Hermitian moving-boundary
system.
