Varieties and quasivarieties of lattices
with complementation
V´aclav Cenker, Ivan Chajda, Jan K¨uhr and Helmut L¨anger
Abstract. We investigate (quasi)varieties of lattices with complementa-
tion, i.e., complemented lattices equipped with a fixed complementation
as a unary operation. We focus on subclasses satisfying additional con-
ditions, such as the quasi-identity (x′ ∧y ≈0 & x ∧y′ ≈0) ⇒x ≈y,
modularity, or De Morgan’s laws. We present a construction resembling
a semidirect product that yields infinitely many finite subdirectly ir-
reducible modular lattices with complementation satisfying this quasi-
identity. We axiomatize small varieties, each of which covers the variety
of Boolean algebras, generated by certain small modular lattices with
De Morgan complementation.
MSC2020. 06C15, 06C20, 06B20, 08B20, 08B26.
Keywords. Complemented lattice, modular lattice, complementation,
De Morgan’s laws, symmetric difference, quasivariety, variety, subdi-
rectly irreducible algebra, free algebra.
1. Introduction
By a lattice with complementation we mean an algebra L = (L, ∨, ∧, ′, 0, 1) of
type (2, 2, 1, 0, 0) such that (L, ∨, ∧, 0, 1) is a bounded lattice and, for every
a ∈L, a′ is a complement of a. Loosely speaking, this is simply a com-
plemented lattice equipped with a designated complementation as a unary
operation.
In this paper, we focus on lattices with complementation satisfying cer-
tain additional conditions that significantly affect the interaction between the
lattice structure and the chosen complementation. This can already be illus-
trated by the smallest nondistributive complemented lattices (see Figure 1).

Lemma 2.4. If L ∈W, then the complementation is injective.
Proof. Let L ∈W. For any a, b ∈L, if a′ = b′, then a+b = (a′ ∧b)∨(a∧b′) =

Lemma 2.6. A lattice with complementation satisfies quasi-identity (1) if
and only if it is a Boolean algebra.
Proof. Let L be a lattice with complementation. It is obvious that L satisfies
(1) when it is a Boolean algebra. Conversely, suppose that L satisfies (1).
Then, for every a ∈L, a′′ ∧a′ = 0 implies a′′ ≤a, and any of the following
statements implies the next one:
a′′′ ∧a′′ = 0;
a′′′ ≤a′;
a ∧a′′′ ≤a ∧a′ = 0;
a ≤a′′.
This shows a′′ = a. Moreover, for every a, b ∈L, any of the following state-
ments implies the next one:
a ≤b;
a ∧b′ = 0;
b′ ∧a′′ = 0;
b′ ≤a′.
Hence ′ is an antitone involution, and so L is an ortholattice. At this point,
it suffices to show that L is uniquely complemented, since it is known that
a uniquely complemented lattice satisfying De Morgan’s laws is distributive
(e.g. [3, Chapter 6]). To this end, suppose that b ∈L is a complement of
a ∈L. Then b∧a′′ = b∧a = 0 yields b ≤a′ by (1), and a∨b = 1 is equivalent
to a′ ∧b′ = 0, which yields a′ ≤b also by (1). Thus b = a′.
□

(i) L does not have a pentagon as a {0, 1}-sublattice;
(ii) L satisfies the quasi-identity (x ≤y & x′ ≈y′) ⇒x ≈y;
(iii) L does not have a subalgebra whose lattice reduct is a pentagon.
Then (i) ⇒(ii) ⇒(iii). If L ∈M, then L satisfies (i). If L ∈W, then L

Proof. (i) implies (ii) because a < b and a′ = b′ would mean that the elements

3. A construction
In order to avoid possible discrepancies in the signatures of the involved alge-
bras, we emphasize that we treat Boolean lattices as bounded lattices rather
than as lattices with complementation. (After all, we carefully distinguish
Boolean lattices and Boolean algebras throughout the paper.)
The following result describes all complementations on the direct prod-
uct K × B, where K is a complemented lattice and B is a Boolean lattice.
In brief, it suffices to associate a complementation on K with each element
of the Boolean lattice. We do not need to exclude the trivial Boolean lattice,
since the lattice with complementation constructed in Proposition 3.1 is then
just a copy of K equipped with the chosen complementation.
