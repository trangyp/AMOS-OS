AND TEICHM ¨ULLER MODULAR FORMS
SEBASTIANO CARPI AND GIULIO CODOGNI
Abstract. In the spirit of the geometric approach to two-dimensional confor-
mal field theory, we explicitly associate to every holomorphic vertex operator
algebra a section of a power of Hodge line bundle on the moduli space of curves
of arbitrary genus - or equivalently a Teichm¨uller modular form in any genus.
As a first application, we connect the geometry of the moduli space of curves to
the classification program for holomorphic vertex algebras. We then discuss how
to use the theory of holomorphic vertex algebras to reach new results about the
moduli space of curves. In the last part of the paper we study how the above
mentioned forms can be used to reconstruct the vertex algebra.

1. Introduction
In this paper, we study some aspects of the connection between the moduli space
of Riemann surfaces and two-dimensional conformal field theories (CFTs), trying
to reach new results on both sides.
Our starting point is an indication given almost forty years ago in a remarkable
paper by Friedan and Shenker [FS87]. Here, the authors, inspired by string the-
ory, consider as a fundamental object the partition function Z(Σ) of a modular
invariant two-dimensional CFT on any closed Riemann surface Σ. The “function”
Z(Σ) is formally defined through a Euclidean path integral

section of a suitable vector bundle on the compactified moduli space of Riemann

The Friedan-Shenker picture is closely related to the celebrated Segal axiomati-
zation of two-dimensional CFT [Segal]. Although the latter is heuristically based
on path integrals, the formulation of the corresponding axioms does not make use
of these integrals. The Friedan-Shenker picture should emerge by restricting the
Segal functor to closed Riemann surfaces. The difficulty of defining either path
integrals or the Segal functor in a mathematically rigorous way for concrete CFT
models is proverbial, see e.g. [GKRV, GKRV24, Hua03, Ten17, Ten19a, Ten19b].
The theory of vertex operator algebras (VOAs) [Bor86, BF, FHL, FLM88, DMS97,
Kac01, Gan06] gives a mathematical axiomatization of chiral two-dimensional con-
formal field theories (which are special two-dimensional CFTs generated by fields
depending on one light-ray coordinate only), and manageable language to extract
from CFTs various mathematical results and stimulating indications for future
research.
Heuristic arguments on the degeneration limit of the partition function Z(Σ)
when Σ approaches a singular complex curve with nodes give formal series involving
the correlation functions of the conformal quantum fields, see [FS87, GV09]. For a
vertex operator algebra V , of CFT type and self-dual - whose definition we recall
in Section 2 - one can take these formal series as a mathematically unambiguous
definition of the genus g partition functions ZV,g. This approach is taken, e.g., in
[GV09, GKV10, MT10, MT14, TW]. In Section 3 we define ZV,g as formal power
series.
For a chiral CFTs, the modular invariance requirement, necessary in the work of
Friedan and Shenker, is satisfied by the requirement that the corresponding VOA
is holomorphic, see Section 2. In this case the Friedan-Shenker picture suggests
that the genus g partition function Zg should give a section of a power of the
Hodge line bundle on Mg. Equivalently, Zg gives a Teichm¨uller modular forms (=
holomorphic functions on the Teichm¨uller space Tg which are modular invariant
with respect to the action of the mapping class group; full details are given in
Section 4).

A paradigmatic example is the moonshine VOA V ♮, let us discuss it in detail.
It is a holomorphic VOA of central charge 24 constructed by Frenkel, Lepowsky
and Meurman [FLM88]. Its automorphism group is the Monster M, the largest
sporadic finite simple group. One has
ZV ♮,1(τ) = e2πiτ(j(τ) −744) = 1 + 1968884 e4πiτ + · · · ,
where j(τ) is the Klein j-function.
This gives an interpretation of the “McKay equation” 1968884 = 1968883 + 1,
which relates the coefficient 1968884 in the expansion of j(τ) with the smallest
dimension 1968883 of the non-trivial representations of the Monster M. The re-
lation between the expansion of the Klein j-function and the dimension of the
representation of the Monster group leads to the so called monstrous moonshine
conjecture of Conway and Norton. The existence of the VOA V ♮turned out to be
the reason for this moonshine.
Frenkel, Lepowsky and Meurman conjectured that the moonshine VOA V ♮is
the unique holomorphic VOA of central charge 24 and dim V1 = 0, [FLM88, page
xxxiii]. This is a very important conjecture in the theory of vertex operator alge-
bras which is still open, see Section 14.
Observe that if V is another such VOA, then ZM(1),1(τ)−24(ZV ♮,1(τ) −ZV,1(τ))
is a modular form of weight 12 whose e2πiτ expansion (Fourier series) starts with
a term of order two in e2πiτ. It is well known that such a form is identically zero
[Serre, Theorem 4, Section 3.2], so the graded dimension of V is equal to the graded
dimension of V ♮.
For g > 1, the partition function ZV,g captures information about the state-field
correspondence Y , so it is natural to try to generalize the above approach using
our Theorem 1.1.
In a nutshell, the geometric invariant of Mg that governs the classification (of
partition functions) of holomorphic VOA is the slope sg of the effective cone of
divisors of Mg. It plays the role of “a modular form of weight 12 whose expansion
starts with a term of order two is identically zero” in the genus one case. We will
review its definition in Section 9.
The sequence of numbers {sg}g∈N has been widely studied over the last decades,
for reasons that have nothing to do with VOAs.
There are many results and
conjectures about it, again reviewed in Section 9. To make a long story short, we
have the following corollary of Theorem 1.1.

Corollary 1.2 (= Corollary 9.3). If sg > 6 for every g, then any holomorphic

The validity of the assumption on the slope sg is presently open, and we call

Other applications of our Theorem 1.1 are about the space of Teichm¨uller mod-
ular forms (Section 10) and the Schottky problem (Section 11).
From a mathematical perspective, especially in view of Corollary 1.2, it is natural
to ask if the partition function is a complete invariant of the vertex algebra, i.e.
if one can reconstruct the vertex algebra out of the knowledge of ZV,g for every g.
From the physics perspective we are asking if one can recover the Segal functor
and hence the CFT from the knowledge of the partition functions as argued in
[FS87]. In this regard Segal wrote “Friedan has conjectured that a field theory U is
completely determined by its restriction to closed surfaces.. . . This seems plausible
but I don’t know the proof”, see [Segal, page 457]. Let us spell out this problem
as a conjecture.
Conjecture 1.3 (Reconstruction Conjecture). Let U, V be two self-dual VOAs of
CFT type. If ZU,g = ZV,g for every g, then U and V are isomorphic.
We put self-dual and CFT type as assumptions because it is the most general
set-up where we can define unambiguously the partition function as a formal series.
However, it is possible that Conjecture 1.3 holds under some additional assumption
on at least one of the two VOAs, such as holomorphicity or unitarity. Let us stress
that there are VOAs whose partition functions are different only when g is big
enough, e.g. the partition functions of the VOAs associated to the lattices D+

We define a vertex subalgebra PV ⊂V , which we call the partition function
subalgebra, which can be extracted from the partition function ZV . This algebra
is contained in V Aut(V ). If V is unitary, PV is a unitary subalgebra containing the
conformal vector of V , see Lemma 12.7.
Theorem 1.4 (= Theorem 12.19 ). If U and V are two simple unitary vertex
operator algebras such that ZU,g = ZV,g for every g, then PU is isomorphic to PV ,
and U is isomorphic to V as PU (equivalently PV )- module.
As a consequence, if the PV -module V has, up to isomorphisms, a unique
unitary VOA structure, then V is determined by the partition function as a unitary
VOA, see Corollary 12.20.

We discuss various explicit examples of partition function subalgebras of unitary
VOAs and, in various cases, we are able to prove that PV = V Aut(V ) and/or that
the unitary VOA structure of V is determined by ZV . We conjecture that the
equality PV = V Aut(V ) always implies that the PV -module V has a unique unitary
VOA structure so that V is determined by ZV . We can prove, thanks to the results
by Codogni and Shephered-Barron in [CS-B14], that the unitary VOA structure of
a holomorphic lattice VOA VL is completely determined by its partition function
ZVL, see Theorem 13.9. Moreover, using previous results by Gaberdiel and Volpato
[GV09], we also prove that the same result holds for all holomorphic VOAs with
central charge c = 24 and weight-one subspace V1 ̸= {0} (the Schellekens VOAs),
see Theorem 14.2.
We conclude our paper with a conjecture on the partition function subalgebra
PV ♮of the moonshine VOA, see Conjecture 14.4. The conjecture says that PV ♮
is equal to the monster orbifold V ♮M. We prove that this conjecture, together
with our weak Harris-Morrison slope conjecture 9.2, and the conjectured strong
rationality of the monster orbifold would prove the Frenkel-Lepowsky-Meurmann
conjecture on the uniqueness of V ♮.

4. Moduli spaces and modular forms
4.1. Family of nodal curves. A Riemann surface, also known as smooth curve, is
a compact complex manifold of complex dimension one. In this paper we consider
or also nodal curves, i.e. one dimensional analytic spaces (= singular manifolds)
which are either smooth or with nodal singularities. We use the euclidean topology.
A family of nodal curves is a surjective morphism π: C →B, where C is a
reduced analytic space, B is a smooth complex manifold and the fibers of π are
nodal curves. In other words, for every point b of B, the fiber Cb = π−1(b) is a nodal
curve that varies holomorphically with b. The coordinates on B are sometimes
called moduli or the parameters of the family.
We often consider families with n sections si, where n is some positive integer and
the si’s are maps si : B →C such that π ◦si is the identity on B, the images of the
si are all disjoint and do not intersect the singular points of the fibers. Informally,
the family is a curve with n marked points which change in a holomorphic way. If
B is just a point, then C is a nodal curve C with n distinct smooth marked points
p1, . . . , pn.
Let π: C →B be a family of nodal curves over a smooth base with n sections
s1, . . . , sn. We say that π has the property (Q) if for every b in B, after hav-
ing removed the nodes, every connected component of the fiber Cb intersects at
least one section. An equivalent formulation that we will often use is that the
complement of the (image of the) sections is affine.

6.1. Generalities on Atiyah algebras of line bundles. We gather some gen-

Given a line bundle L on a smooth variety B or a smooth Deligne-Mumford
stack, its Atiyah algebra AL is the sheaf of derivations acting on sections of L,
with its natural structure of sheaf of Lie algebras. An important variant is the
log Atiyah algebra AL(−log(D)) associated to L and a normal crossing divisor D
in B: it is the sheaf of differential operators A such that for every section s of L
vanishing along D, also A(s) vanishes along D.
The symbol map naturally presents AL(−log(D)) as an extension of both sheaves
of Lie algebras and OB-modules
