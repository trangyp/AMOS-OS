Very weak subintuitionistic logics
Taishi Kurahashi∗†and Mashu Noguchi‡§
Abstract
We introduce a new propositional logic, called very weak subintuition-
istic logic VF, by adapting the relational semantics of Fitting, Marek, and
Truszczy´nski for the pure logic of necessitation N to the propositional set-
ting. We prove that VF and its closed negative extensions are sound and
complete with respect to this semantics, and that they have the disjunc-
tion property and the finite frame property. We also prove that VF is
strictly weaker than the weak subintuitionistic logic WF of Shirmoham-
madzadeh Maleki and de Jongh. Finally, we study modal companions of
VF and its closed negative extensions via Corsi’s modified G¨odel trans-
lation.

Introduction
In the Kripke semantics of intuitionistic propositional logic Int, frames are re-
quired to be preorders, and valuations are required to satisfy persistency. Logics
stronger than Int, that is, superintuitionistic logics, are obtained by impos-
ing additional frame conditions (see Chagrov and Zakharyaschev [2]). On the
other hand, Corsi [4] investigated what logics arise from dropping preorder and
persistency from Kripke models. In particular, Corsi introduced the logic F
corresponding to the class of Kripke models in which the preorder condition
and persistency are dropped. Logics weaker than Int are often called subin-
tuitionistic logics. See also Restall [18] and Visser [22] for related approaches
and motivations. The study of modal companions is also important in this area.
Among other things, Corsi proved that K is a modal companion of F via Corsi’s
modified G¨odel translation.
A further development in this direction was given by Shirmohammadzadeh
Maleki and de Jongh [21, 5].
They introduced neighborhood semantics for
propositional subintuitionistic logics, and obtained the weak subintuitionistic
logic WF, together with a family of its extensions. These logics are strictly
weaker than F.

On the modal side, there is a very weak non-normal modal logic, the pure
logic of necessitation N, introduced by Fitting, Marek, and Truszczy´nski [8].
This logic is obtained from classical propositional logic by adding only the ne-
cessitation rule. Fitting, Marek, and Truszczy´nski introduced a relational se-
mantics for N in which the accessibility relation is not a single binary relation,
but a family of binary relations indexed by modal formulas. The first author and
collaborators [11, 12, 13, 14, 20] have studied N and its extensions in the context
of provability logic, finite frame property, sequent calculus, and interpolation.
In this paper, we introduce a new logic, called very weak subintuitionistic
logic VF, together with its extensions. We introduce a propositional counterpart
of the semantics of Fitting, Marek, and Truszczy´nski, which we call propositional
FMT semantics. Using this semantics, we show that VF is strictly weaker than
the weak subintuitionistic logic WF, which justifies the name “very weak”.
We then study the completeness and finite frame property of extensions of
VF with respect to the FMT semantics.
In this paper, we mainly consider
extensions of VF obtained by adding axioms of the form ¬C, where ¬C is
closed and provable in Int. We call such formulas closed negative axioms. For
a finite set X of closed negative axioms, we write VF+X for the logic obtained
by adding all formulas in X as axioms to VF, and call it a closed negative
extension of VF. We first prove that every closed negative extension of VF
has the disjunction property. Using this, we then prove that VF + X is sound
and complete with respect to the class of all finite FMT frames validating all
elements of X.
Finally, we study modal companions of VF and its closed negative exten-
sions. We show that VF corresponds to the pure logic of necessitation N via
Corsi’s modified G¨odel translation. More generally, if X is a finite set of closed
negative axioms, we associate with X a finite set X∗of closed modal formulas
and prove that both N + X∗and N + X∗+ {¬□¬2k⊥| k ≥0} are modal
companions of VF + X.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce VF and its
closed negative extensions.
In Section 3, we prove the disjunction property
of these logics. In Section 4, we introduce propositional FMT semantics. We
then prove soundness of VF and its extensions and analyze frame conditions
for closed negative axioms. In Section 5, we prove the finite frame property for
closed negative extensions of VF. In Section 6, we discuss modal companions.
In Section 7, we conclude the present paper by mentioning several directions for
future work. Appendix is devoted to the finite frame property for closed modal
extensions of N.

Disjunction property
Before proving the completeness and finite frame property of VF and its exten-
sions, we prove the disjunction property (DP) of them. We say that a logic L
has DP if for any formulas A and B, if L ⊢A ∨B, then L ⊢A or L ⊢B. One
syntactic way to prove DP is using Aczel’s modified version [1] of Kleene’s slash
notation [10].
Definition 3.1. For each logic L, we define the relation |L A inductively as
follows:
1. |L p does not hold for each p ∈Prop,
2. |L ⊥does not hold,
3. |L A ∧B : ⇐⇒|L A and |L B,
4. |L A ∨B : ⇐⇒|L A or |L B,
5. |L A →B : ⇐⇒L ⊢A →B and (if |L A, then |L B).
Lemma 3.2. Let L be any closed negative extension of VF. For any A ∈FmlP,
L ⊢A if and only if |L A.
Proof. (⇐): This direction is proved by induction on the construction of A.
(⇒): We prove this direction by induction on the length of a proof of A in L.
It suffices to check that every axiom of L is slashed and that all rules preserve
the slash relation. It is easily verified that all axioms of VF are slashed simply
by the definition of |L.
Let ¬C be a closed negative axiom of L. Suppose |L C. By the already
proved direction (⇐), we have L ⊢C. Since L ⊢C →⊥, we obtain L ⊢⊥by
(MP), contradicting the consistency of L. Thus the condition “|L C ⇒|L ⊥” is
vacuously satisfied, and hence |L ¬C.
We show that the rules preserve the slash relation. For (RC), suppose |L A →
B and |L A →C. Then L ⊢A →B and L ⊢A →C, so by (RC), we have
L ⊢A →B ∧C. Moreover, if |L A, then we get |L B and |L C from |L A →B
and |L A →C. Hence |L B ∧C. We have proved |L A →B ∧C. The other cases
are similar.
Theorem 3.3 (Disjunction Property). Let L be any closed negative extension
of VF. Then L has DP.
Proof. Suppose L ⊢A ∨B. By Lemma 3.2, |L A ∨B. Then, by the definition,
|L A or |L B. By Lemma 3.2 again, we obtain L ⊢A or L ⊢B.

FMT semantics
In [8], Fitting, Marek, and Truszczy´nski introduced a relational semantics for the
pure logic of necessitation N, which is a very weak non-normal modal logic. In
their semantics, the accessibility relation is not given as a single binary relation,
but as a family of binary relations {RA} indexed by modal formulas A.
In this section, by appropriately modifying their idea for the present propo-
sitional setting, we introduce a new semantics for VF and its extensions. We
call this semantics propositional FMT semantics, or simply FMT semantics.
4.1
FMT models and soundness of VF
Definition 4.1 (FMT frames). An FMT frame F = ⟨W, {RA}A∈FmlP, r⟩is a
tuple consisting of the following.
• W is a non-empty set, called the set of worlds.
• RA is a binary relation on W indexed by a propositional formula A.
• r ∈W is called the root, in the sense that rRAx for all A ∈FmlP and
x ∈W.
Definition 4.2 (FMT models). An FMT model is a pair ⟨F, V ⟩, where F is
an FMT frame and V : W →2Prop is a valuation on F. The valuation V is
extended to a forcing relation between worlds and propositional formulas, that
is, a relation on W × FmlP, as follows:
• M, x ⊩p : ⇐⇒p ∈V (x) where p ∈Prop,
• M, x ⊩⊥never holds,
• M, x ⊩A ∧B : ⇐⇒M, x ⊩A and M, x ⊩B,
• M, x ⊩A ∨B : ⇐⇒M, x ⊩A or M, x ⊩B,
• M, x ⊩A →B : ⇐⇒
for any y ∈W satisfying xRA→By, M, y ⊩A
implies M, y ⊩B.
We say that a formula A is valid in an FMT model M (written M |= A) if
M, x ⊩A for all x ∈W. We say that A is valid in an FMT frame F (written
F |= A) if A is valid in all FMT models based on F.
We often simply write x ⊩A instead of M, x ⊩A. We write x ∈F or x ∈M
to indicate that x is a world of the frame F or the model M, respectively. By the
definition of the forcing relation, we immediately obtain the following clauses
for the abbreviations ¬, ⊤, and ↔.
• x ⊩¬A if and only if for any y, if xR¬Ay then y ⊮A.
• x ⊩⊤always holds.

Theorem 4.3 (Soundness of VF with respect to FMT semantics). For any
A ∈FmlP and FMT frame F, if VF ⊢A, then F |= A.
Proof. We prove the theorem by induction on the length of a proof of A in
VF. Let F = ⟨W, {RA}A∈FmlP, r⟩be an arbitrary FMT frame and let V be an
arbitrary valuation on F. Let M = ⟨W, {RA}A∈FmlP, r, V ⟩. It suffices to show
that every axiom of VF is valid on M and that all rules of VF preserve validity
on M.
First, we verify that all axioms of VF are valid in M. We only check the
axiom A ∧B →A, and the other axioms are verified similarly. Let x ∈W
be arbitrary and let y ∈W be such that xRA∧B→Ay. Suppose y ⊩A ∧B.
Then y ⊩A by the definition of the forcing relation for conjunction. Hence
x ⊩A ∧B →A. Since x was arbitrary, M |= A ∧B →A.
Next, we show that the rules of VF preserve validity on M. The cases of
(RC), (RD), (RI), and (AF) are immediate from the definition of the forcing
relation.
We show only the case (MP). Suppose M |= A and M |= A →B. Let x ∈W
be arbitrary. Since M |= A →B, we have in particular r ⊩A →B. Since r is
the root of F, we have rRA→Bx. Also, since M |= A, we have x ⊩A. Therefore,
by r ⊩A →B, we obtain x ⊩B. Since x ∈W is arbitrary, M |= B.
The definition of FMT frames requires the existence of a root. As the above
proof shows, this requirement is essential for the soundness of the rule (MP).

4.2
Frame conditions of closed negative axioms
In this subsection, we analyze frame conditions of FMT frames corresponding to
closed negative axioms. Since closed formulas contain no propositional variables,
their validity on an FMT frame is independent of valuations. So, for a closed
formula A, whether x ⊩A holds depends only on the FMT frame.
Lemma 4.5. Let A be a closed formula. If M and N are FMT models based
on the same FMT frame F, then for every x ∈F, we have M, x ⊩A if and only
if N, x ⊩A.
We investigate the behavior of closed negative formulas.
Proposition 4.6. Let C and D be closed formulas, and let F be an FMT frame.
1. F |= ¬C if and only if there is no x ∈F such that x ⊩C.
2. F |= ¬(C ∨D) if and only if F |= ¬C and F |= ¬D.
3. F |= ¬(C →D) if and only if for every world x of F, there exists a world
y such that xRC→Dy, y ⊩C, and y ⊮D.
4. F |= ¬¬C if and only if for every world x of F, there exists a world y
such that xR¬Cy and y ⊩C.

Modal companions
In this section, we study modal companions of VF and its closed negative exten-
sions via Corsi’s modified G¨odel translation. In Subsection 6.1, we recall some
background on modal companions of intuitionistic and subintuitionistic logics.
In Subsection 6.2, we show that closed negative extensions of VF correspond
to suitable closed modal extensions of the pure logic of necessitation N.
In
particular, we prove that N is one of the modal companions of VF.
6.1
Historical background
In this subsection, we recall some standard background on modal companions
of intuitionistic and subintuitionistic logics.
First of all, the celebrated result of G¨odel [9] and McKinsey–Tarski [15]
states that intuitionistic logic Int can be embedded into modal logic S4 via the
following G¨odel translation. Let FmlM be the set of all modal formulas.
Definition 6.1 (G¨odel translation). G¨odel translation (·)G : FmlP →FmlM is
defined as follows:
13


## Page 14


1. pG = □p where p ∈Prop,
2. ⊥G = ⊥,
3. (A ∧B)G = (AG ∧BG),
4. (A ∨B)G = (AG ∨BG),
5. (A →B)G = □(AG →BG).
Fact 6.2 (G¨odel–McKinsey–Tarski’s theorem [9, 15]). For any A ∈FmlP, Int ⊢
A if and only if S4 ⊢AG.
This embedding phenomenon is expressed by saying that S4 is a modal
companion of Int.
The key to this proof via Kripke semantics is that the
corresponding models for both Int and S4 are preorders (see [2, Theorem 3.83]).
Corsi [4] introduced subintuitionistic logics, that is, propositional logics cor-
responding to models that do not necessarily satisfy the preorder condition and
persistency, both of which are required for Int. Corsi also showed that these
logics have modal logics weaker than S4 as their modal companions, such as K,
KT, and K4.
When dealing with subintuitionistic logics, the original G¨odel translation
is no longer the most suitable one. The reason is that their semantics, such as
Kripke, neighborhood, and FMT semantics, do not always ensure persistency for
propositional variables. For this reason, Corsi used a modified G¨odel translation
in which propositional variables are translated as themselves.
Definition 6.3. Corsi’s modified G¨odel translation (·)C : FmlP →FmlM is
defined as follows:
1. pC = p for p ∈Prop,
2. ⊥C = ⊥,
3. (A ∧B)C = AC ∧BC,
4. (A ∨B)C = AC ∨BC,
5. (A →B)C = □(AC →BC).
Fact 6.4 (Corsi [4]). Let A ∈FmlP.
1. F ⊢A if and only if K ⊢AC,
2. F + ¬¬⊤⊢A if and only if KD ⊢AC.
In contrast to Corsi’s relational treatment of F, the weak subintuitionistic
logic WF of Shirmohammadzadeh Maleki and de Jongh is naturally connected
with neighborhood semantics. On the modal side, this corresponds to the non-
normal modal logic E and its extensions, which are weaker than K (see [3,
17]). De Jongh and Shirmohammadzadeh Maleki [5, 6] showed that suitable
14


## Page 15


extensions of WF have extensions of E as their modal companions via Corsi’s
modified G¨odel translation.
Thus, while F is linked to normal modal logics such as K and KD, and WF
is linked to neighborhood-based non-normal modal logics such as E, we shall
show in the next subsection that our still weaker logic VF is linked to the pure
logic of necessitation N.

7
Concluding remarks and Future work
In this paper, we have introduced a very weak propositional logic VF and its
extensions. We have also discussed fundamental facts concerning its semantics
and its connections to modal logics, especially the pure logic of necessitation
N. The study of these logics is still in the early stages, and we believe it can
be developed in many directions. We conclude the present paper by mentioning
the following three directions for future work.
18


## Page 19


The first direction is to develop a sequent calculus for VF. In particular, it
would be natural to find a sequent calculus adapted to FMT semantics in the
style of labelled sequent calculi (cf. [16, 7]). This is one of our ongoing projects.
The second is to investigate what happens when adding axioms other than
seriality, or more generally axioms other than closed negative axioms. Candidate
axioms are those introduced by Corsi [4] for reflexivity, transitivity, and other
properties of Kripke semantics. From the perspective of modal logic, extensions
of the pure logic of necessitation by transitivity axioms are discussed in [13, 14].
The third, which arises as a natural question, concerns whether it is possible
to introduce a logic weaker than VF and justify the existence of such a logic by
some semantics or other means.

Finite frame property for closed modal ex-

Kogure and Kurahashi [12] proved the finite frame property for the logic N +
¬□⊥. In this appendix, we prove Theorem 6.7, which extends their theorem by
dealing with finite sets of closed modal axioms.
Let Y be a finite set of closed modal formulas and let L = N + Y . For a
modal formula B, we define ∼B as follows: if B is of the form ¬C, then ∼B is
C; otherwise, ∼B is ¬B. For a modal formula A, let SubY (A) be the smallest
set of modal formulas satisfying the following conditions:
• A ∈SubY (A) and B ∈SubY (A) for every B ∈Y ,
• SubY (A) is closed under taking subformulas,
• if B ∈SubY (A), then ∼B ∈SubY (A).
Notice that SubY (A) is finite because Y is finite.
A subset Γ of SubY (A) is said to be L-consistent if L ⊬V Γ →⊥. We say
that Γ is (A, Y )-maximal L-consistent if Γ ⊆SubY (A), Γ is L-consistent, and
for every B ∈SubY (A), either B ∈Γ or ∼B ∈Γ. It is easy to show that every
L-consistent subset of SubY (A) is included in an (A, Y )-maximal L-consistent
set.
