Variable Muckenhoupt A∞Weights
Dachun Yang*, Wen Yuan and Zongze Zeng
Abstract:
In this article, with introducing concepts of variable scalar Ap(·),∞weights and
variable matrix Ap(·),∞weights, we seek a comprehensive theory of A∞weights within the
framework of variable exponent spaces. We first show that a weight belongs to Ap(·),∞if and
only if its p(·)-th power is an A∞weight. Using this, we characterize the Ap(·),∞condition by
the minimal operator. Then we establish the reverse H¨older’s inequality for Ap(·),∞weights
in variable Lebesgue spaces with explicit constants and, combining this with the previously
established relationship between Ap(·),∞weights and A∞weights, we prove that, for any
weight w, the reverse H¨older’s inequality holds in variable Lebesgue spaces if and only if w is
an Ap(·),∞weight. For the matrix Ap(·),∞weights, we first show the existence of the reducing
operators for matrix Ap(·),∞weights and then, combining the matrix Ap(·),∞weights with
the scalar Ap(·),∞weights, we establish the reverse H¨older’s inequality for Ap(·),∞weights
in variable Lebesgue spaces. Finally, for further applications to variable matrix-weighted
function spaces, we introduce the upper and the lower dimensions for Ap(·),∞weights and use
these concepts to establish the sharp estimate involving reducing operators.

Introduction
Throughout this article, we work in Rn, which serves as our default underlying space unless
otherwise specified.
The Muckenhoupt weights were introduced by Muckenhoupt [60] to characterize the bound-
edness of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator on weighted Lebesgue spaces. From then on,
the theory of Muckenhoupt weights has found wide applications in harmonic analysis (see, for
example, [22, 48, 49, 50]) and partial differential equations (see, for example, [8, 39, 44]). On the
other hand, the study of variable function spaces can be traced to Orlicz [65] in 1931 and, driven

Compared with the relationship between matrix Ap weights and scalar Ap weights, the differ-
ence between matrix A∞weights and scalar A∞weights is more distinct. By splitting the matrix
A∞condition into a family of conditions depending on p, Volberg [71] introduced the concept of
matrix Ap,∞weights with p ∈(0, ∞) as an analogue of the scalar A∞weights, which, in the scalar-
valued case, for any p ∈(0, ∞), Ap,∞= A∞, while this no longer holds true for matrix-valued
weights. Bu et. al. [15] later found several equivalent and simpler characterizations of matrix
Ap,∞weights; see also [11, 16, 17, 80] and the survey article [18] for more studies about matrix
Ap,∞weights and their applications.

In this article, we establish some fundamental properties of both Ap(·),∞weights and Ap(·),∞
weights. Observe that the p(·)-th power of Ap(·),∞weights naturally appears in the definition of
weighted variable Lebesgue spaces (see Definition 2.43) and hence plays a vital role in the studies
related to Ap(·),∞weights. Indeed, we prove that a scalar weight belongs to Ap(·),∞if and only
if its p(·)-th power is a scalar A∞weight. Using this, we characterize the Ap(·),∞condition by
the minimal operator [see (2.52) for the definition of the minimal operator]. Based on these, we
further establish the reverse H¨older’s inequality for Ap(·),∞weights in variable Lebesgue spaces
with explicit constants and, combining this with the aforementioned relationship between Ap(·),∞
weights and A∞weights, we prove that, for any weight w, the reverse H¨older’s inequality holds
in variable Lebesgue spaces if and only if w is an Ap(·),∞weight. For the matrix weight case,
we first introduce the reducing operators related to matrix Ap(·),∞weights. Then, by connecting
the matrix Ap(·),∞with the scalar Ap(·),∞weights, we establish the reverse H¨older’s inequality
for Ap(·),∞weights, which can further be used to characterize the Ap(·),∞weights. Finally, for
further applications to variable matrix-weighted function spaces, we introduce the upper and the
lower dimensions for Ap(·),∞weights and use these concepts to establish a sharp estimate involving
reducing operators.
The reason for introducing Ap(·),∞weights lies in that the constants in their related reverse
H¨older’s inequality depend only on the weight constant, not on the involved weight itself. This is
not the case for the A∞weights. Indeed, since we show that any A∞weight must be the p(·)-th
power of one Ap(·),∞weight, we can also establish a reverse H¨older’s inequality for any A∞weight

although w ∈Ap(·),∞if and only if wp(·) ∈A∞, in the proof of the reverse H¨older’s inequality for
Ap(·),∞weights we can not directly use the known reverse H¨older’s inequality for A∞weights (see,
for example, [50]) because, when p(·) is not a constant exponent, we can not compare [w]Ap(·),∞
with [wp(·)]A∞. To overcome this difficulty, we make full use of the equivalent expressions of A∞
weights and the properties of variable Lebesgue spaces.
We point out that these fundamental properties of Ap(·),∞obtained in this article play a key role
in developing a theory of variable matrix-weighted Besov spaces in [79] and will also surely be
crucial tools in establishing a corresponding theory of variable matrix-weighted Triebel–Lizorkin
spaces in a forthcoming article.
