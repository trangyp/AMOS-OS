VERTEX-PRIMITIVE s-ARC-TRANSITIVE CAYLEY DIGRAPHS
JING JIAN LI, YONG TANG SHI, YU WANG, AND BINZHOU XIA
Abstract. Determining an upper bound on s for vertex-primitive s-arc-transitive di-
graphs has been an open problem of considerable interest since a question asked by
Praeger in 1990. Although much progress has been made and an upper bound is conjec-
tured to be 2, a complete classification for s = 2 remains out of reach. In this paper, we
prove that the tight upper bound on s for finite vertex-primitive s-arc-transitive Cayley
digraphs is exactly 2. Furthermore, we completely characterize the structure of these
digraphs when s = 2.
Keywords: Cayley digraph; vertex-primitive; s-arc-transitive
MSC2020: 05C20; 05C25
1. Introduction
A digraph Γ is a pair (V, →) with a set V of vertices and an antisymmetric irreflexive
binary relation →on V . Denote by V (Γ) and Aut(Γ) the vertex set and the full automor-
phism group of Γ respectively. For a nonnegative integer s, an s-arc of Γ is an (s+1)-tuple
(v0, v1, . . . , vs) of vertices such that vi−1 →vi for each i ∈{1, . . . , s}. For a subgroup G
of Aut(Γ), we say that Γ is G-vertex-primitive if G acts primitively on V (Γ) (that is, G
does not preserve any nontrivial partition of V (Γ)), and that Γ is (G, s)-arc-transitive if
G acts transitively on the set of s-arcs of Γ. It is easy to see that a vertex-transitive
s-arc-transitive digraph with s ≥1 is also (s −1)-arc-transitive. A digraph Γ is sim-
ply called vertex-primitive if it is Aut(Γ)-vertex-primitive, and s-arc-transitive if it is
(Aut(Γ), s)-arc-transitive.
A well-known result of Weiss [30] establishes the tight upper bound s ≤7 for finite s-arc-
transitive graphs of valency at least 3 (note that s can be arbitrarily large for valency 2 as
cycles are s-arc-transitive for all s ≥0). In contrast, there exist infinite families of finite s-
arc-transitive digraphs with unbounded s other than directed cycles (see, for example, [27,
Theorem 2.8 and Proposition 2.11]). However, all such examples are imprimitive, and the
existence of finite vertex-primitive 2-arc-transitive digraphs aside from directed cycles
remained unknown until Giudici, Li and Xia [10] constructed the first family of such
digraphs in 2017. To date, no finite vertex-primitive 3-arc-transitive digraphs have been
discovered. Motivated by this construction, the following question is posed in [10].
Question 1.1. Is there an upper bound on s for finite vertex-primitive s-arc-transitive
digraphs that are not directed cycles?

Recall that a graph or digraph Γ is said to be Cayley on a group R if Aut(Γ) contains a
subgroup isomorphic to R that acts regularly on V (Γ). It is widely believed that the vast
majority of vertex-transitive graphs and digraphs in general are Cayley (see [25] and [26,
§8.4]). Thus, to further our understanding of Question 1.1, it is natural to focus on Cayley
digraphs. Another motivation, as can be seen by Proposition 3.4, is that the infinite fami-
lies of vertex-primitive 2-arc-transitive digraphs in [11, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.4] are
Cayley. In this paper, we determine the tight upper bound on s for finite vertex-primitive
s-arc-transitive Cayley digraphs and classify all such digraphs for s ≥2. Recalling the
definition of the direct product of digraphs from Section 2, our main result is stated as
follows.
Theorem 1.2. Let Γ be a finite vertex-primitive Cayley digraph.
Then Γ is 2-arc-
transitive if and only if one of the following holds:
(a) Γ is a directed cycle;
(b) Γ is isomorphic to the direct product of m copies of Γ(T) for some nonabelian simple
group T and positive integer m, where Γ(T) is the digraph defined in Example 2.1.
In particular, there exist no vertex-primitive 3-arc-transitive Cayley digraphs other than
directed cycles.
The proof of Theorem 1.2 proceeds by the primitive type. Let Γ be a G-vertex-primitive
(G, 2)-arc-transitive Cayley digraph that is not a directed cycle. By [27, Theorem 3.1], the
primitive type of G can only be Almost Simple (AS), Simple Diagonal (SD), Compound
Diagonal (CD), or Product Action (PA). In Section 3, for type AS, we prove that no such
vertex-primitive 2-arc-transitive Cayley digraph exists, a result stated as Proposition 3.3.
Moreover, we establish in the same section that G is of type SD or CD if and only if Γ
belongs to a specific family of digraphs constructed from those given in Example 2.1; this
is formulated as Proposition 3.4. In Section 4, we address the case where G is of type PA,
proving that no vertex-primitive 2-arc-transitive Cayley digraph exists in this case either,
which is formulated as Proposition 4.1.

2.3. Strategy to prove Theorem 1.2.
As outlined in the Introduction, the proof of Theorem 1.2 proceeds as follows. Let Γ
be a G-vertex-primitive (G, 2)-arc-transitive Cayley digraph that is not a directed cycle,
and let v be a vertex of Γ. By [20, Theorem], the primitive type of G is one of AS,
SD, CD, or PA, which we analyze separately. Among others, an important tool in our
analysis is the characterization of the vertex-stabilizer Gv, which admits a factorization as
in Lemma 2.3. Furthermore, in the case where (Gv)(∞) is quasisimple, this factorization
is described in [32, Lemma 3.3].
In Section 3, we investigate the cases where G has type AS, SD, or CD. First assume
that G is of type AS. Since the primitive group G has a regular subgroup R, the possible
triples (G, Gv, R) can be read off from [24, Tables 16.1–16.3]. We show that this case is
impossible for the infinite families in Lemma 3.1 and the remaining triples in Lemma 3.2,
combined with computation in Magma [2] when necessary. Therefore, there exists no G-
vertex-primitive (G, 2)-arc-transitive Cayley digraph with G almost simple. Next assume
that G is of type SD or CD. By [11, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.4], a G-vertex-primitive
(G, 2)-arc-transitive digraph where G is of type SD or CD is isomorphic to the direct
product of m copies of Γ(T) given in Example 2.1, and such a digraph is not 3-arc-
transitive. We prove that all such digraphs are Cayley, leading to a classification of SD
or CD type vertex-primitive 2-arc-transitive Cayley digraphs in Proposition 3.4.
In Section 4, we assume that G is of type PA. We first show that, by [23, Corollary 3(iv)],
it suffices to consider G-vertex-primitive (G, 2)-arc-transitive digraphs where G is almost
simple and contains a transitive core-free subgroup X. The existence of X restricts the
candidates for the pair (G, Gv), which can be determined using [22, Corollary]. Moreover,
if (Gv)(∞) is quasisimple, then [32, Lemma 3.3] provides an even stronger restriction. We
observe that for most of the candidates, the group X has a unique nonsolvable composition
factor T, and T satisfies the condition that |T| > |G|/|Gv| and that there exists a prime
p ∈π(G) \ π(Gv) with |T|p = |G|p. A key lemma, stated as Lemma 2.5, then asserts
that Aut(Γ) has no regular subgroups, yielding a contradiction. For the single case where
(Soc(G), (Soc(G)v)(∞)) = (PSL4(q), PSp4(q)), which cannot be ruled out through this
method as we cannot assure that X is nonsolvable, we prove that Guv and Gvw are not
conjugate in G. This is achieved by showing that the centralizers of (Guv)(∞) and (Gvw)(∞)
in G(∞) are not isomorphic, which contradicts Lemma 2.3(c). Summarizing these results,
we conclude that G cannot be of type PA, which is formulated as Proposition 4.1.
