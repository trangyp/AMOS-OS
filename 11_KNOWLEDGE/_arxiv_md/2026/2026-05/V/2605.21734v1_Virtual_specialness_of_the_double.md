VIRTUAL SPECIALNESS OF THE DOUBLE
CHANGQIAN LI
Abstract. Let G be a virtually compact special Gromov-hyperbolic group. We prove that the
double G ∗H G along a quasiconvex subgroup H is virtually compact special. More generally, we
show that if a finite graph of groups has constant vertex groups, with each vertex group virtually
compact special Gromov-hyperbolic and each edge group quasiconvex in its adjacent vertex groups,
then its fundamental group is virtually compact special.
1. Introduction
A group is (compact) special if it has a finite index subgroup that is the fundamental group of a
(compact) cube complex satisfying certain combinatorial hyperplane conditions (see Definition 3.5).
A group is virtually (compact) special if it admits a finite index (compact) special group. The theory
of virtually special groups was introduced by Haglund and Wise in [HW08]. Since then, it has led
to many important developments, among which the most notable is Agol’s proof of the Virtual
Haken Conjecture [Ago13].
Virtually compact special groups satisfy strong algebraic properties. In particular, since compact
special groups embed in finitely generated right-angled Artin groups and hence in SLn(Z), virtually
compact special groups are linear and residually finite.
In [DT26], Douba and Tsouvalas investigated the linearity of amalgams of subgroups of algebraic
groups. The following result from their paper is particularly relevant to the present work.
Theorem 1.1 (cf. [DT26, Theorem 1.4]). Let G be a virtually compact special Gromov-hyperbolic
group, and let H < G be a quasiconvex subgroup. Then the double G ∗H G is linear.
In the same paper, Douba and Tsouvalas suggest that the double G∗H G should remain virtually
compact special, which would give an alternative proof of linearity. Our main result confirms this
expectation.
Theorem 1.2. Let G be a virtually compact special Gromov-hyperbolic group, and let H < G be a
quasiconvex subgroup. Then the double G ∗H G is virtually compact special.
In general, the quasiconvexity assumption on H cannot simply be omitted. For example, let H be
a subgroup of a virtually compact special Gromov-hyperbolic group G such that H is not separable
in G (see [HW08, Example 10.3] for an example of construction). Then the double G ∗H G is not
residually finite, and hence is not virtually special. Even if H is separable but not quasiconvex in
G, the double G ∗H G may fail to be virtually compact special. However, it is not clear whether it
must still be virtually special.
More generally, we say that a graph of groups has constant vertex groups if there exists a fixed
group V such that all vertex groups are isomorphic to V , and for every edge, the two monomor-
phisms from the edge group to the adjacent vertex groups are compatible with isomorphisms (see
Definition 5.1). In particular, the double G ∗H G is the fundamental group of a graph of groups
with constant vertex groups, whose underlying graph consists of a single edge joining two vertices.
Theorem 1.2 is a special case of the following more general result.
Theorem 1.3. Let G be the fundamental group of a finite graph of groups with constant vertex
groups. Suppose that the vertex groups are virtually compact special Gromov-hyperbolic, and that
each edge group is quasiconvex in the adjacent vertex groups. Then G is virtually compact special.

2. CAT(0) cube complexes
We review the following notions of CAT(0) cube complexes from [Wis21]. In this paper, all cube
complexes are assumed to be connected.
Definition 2.1. An n-cube is a copy of [0, 1]n. Its subcubes are obtained by restricting some
coordinates to 0 and some to 1. A cube complex is obtained by gluing a collection of cubes along
their subcubes isometrically. A flag complex is a simplicial complex with the property that every
finite set of pairwise adjacent vertices spans a simplex. A cube complex X is nonpositively curved
if the link lk(v) of each vertex v of X is a flag complex. A nonpositively curved cube complex is
CAT(0) if it is simply-connected.
Definition 2.2. Let Q be an n-cube of a cube complex X. A midcube of Q ∼= [0, 1]n is the subspace
obtained by restricting one coordinate to 1/2. Let M be the disjoint union of the collection of
midcubes of cubes of X. Let D be the quotient space of M induced by identifying faces of midcubes
under the inclusion map. The connected components of D are called the hyperplanes of X.
Definition 2.3. Let f : Y →X be a combinatorial map of nonpositively curved cube complexes.
We say f is a local isometry if for each vertex v of Y , the induced map of links lk(v) →lk(f(v)) is
an embedding and has full image (a subcomplex A of a simplicial complex B is full if each simplex
of B whose vertices are in A is entirely contained in A).

Definition 3.4. Let X be a cube complex. We introduce the following terminology for hyperplanes

(1) A hyperplane H is 2-sided if there does not exist a directed edge −→e dual to H such that

8. Remarks on finite stature
The following notion was introduced in [HW19].
Definition 8.1. Let G be a group and let {Hλ}λ∈Λ be a collection of subgroups of G.
Then
(G, {Hλ}λ∈Λ) has finite stature if for each µ ∈Λ, there are finitely many Hµ-conjugacy classes of
infinite subgroups of the form Hµ ∩C, where C is an intersection of G-conjugates of elements of
{Hλ}λ∈λ.
The relation between finite stature and virtual specialness is given by the following:
Theorem 8.2 ([HW24, Theorem 1.4]). Let X be a compact cube complex that splits as a graph of
nonpositively curved cube complexes. Suppose each vertex group is Gromov-hyperbolic. Then the
following are equivalent.
(1) π1X has finite stature with respect to the vertex groups.
(2) X is virtually special.
Furthermore, the direction (2) =⇒(1) follows from [HW24, Theorem 6.12], which does not
reply on the hyperbolicity assumption. Thus, our results of virtual specialness in Theorem 1.4 and
Corollary 7.2 imply the following:
Corollary 8.3. Let X be a compact cube complex that splits as a graph of virtually special cube
complexes XΓ with locally constant vertex spaces. Then π1X has finite stature with respect to the
vertex groups.
Corollary 8.4. Let G be the fundamental group of a finite graph of groups with locally constant
vertex groups GΓ. Suppose that the monodromy group of GΓ is finite. If the vertex groups are
virtually compact special Gromov-hyperbolic, and the edge groups are quasiconvex in adjacent vertex
groups, then G has finite stature with respect to the vertex groups.
In fact, Corollaries 8.3 and 8.4 can be proved directly, without using virtual specialness. Together
with Theorem 8.2, this gives an alternative proof of Corollary 7.2. However, this approach does not
prove Theorem 1.4, because Theorem 8.2 assumes that the vertex groups are Gromov-hyperbolic.
