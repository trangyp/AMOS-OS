ON THE CATEGORY OF SEMI-GRADED MODULES
ARMANDO REYES
Dedicated to my Intellectual Father, Professor Oswaldo Lezama,
on the Occasion of His 70th Birthday
Abstract. Lezama [21] introduced the notion of semi-graded ring with the
aim of generalizing Z-graded rings and several families of noncommutative
rings of polynomial type non-N-graded such as the skew Poincaré-Birkhoff-
Witt extensions defined by him [15]. In a series of papers, [18, 19, 20, 21],
he studied problems of non-commutative projective algebraic geometry gener-
alizing the original ideas of Artin et al. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] on N-graded rings, in
the categorical context of the category SGR −R of left semi-graded modules
over a semi-graded ring R. In this note we prove that SGR −R possesses a
canonical set of free generators via shifted twists, which endows the category
with a Grothendieck structure and guarantees the existence of enough injective
and projective objects. This categorical robustness allows us to formulate a
semi-graded analogue of Baer’s criterion for injectivity and to establish a first
approach to the dual theory of projective resolutions using shifted twists.
1. Introduction
Lezama [21] introduced the semi-graded rings as a generalization of N-graded
rings and several families of noncommutative rings of polynomial type that are not
N-graded, such as Ore extensions [14, 25], PBW extensions (PBW means Poincaré-
Birkhoff-Witt) [7], 3-dimensional skew polynomial rings [6, 30], diffusion algebras
[17, 26], skew PBW extensions [13, 15, 22] and other families of PBW algebras [8,
29, 32]. Since its introduction, Lezama and some researchers have investigated some
topics concerning non-commutative projective algebraic geometry of semi graded-
rings, with the aim of extending those corresponding ideas formulated by Artin et
al. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] in the setting of N-graded rings. One of his key results is the
generalization of the Serre-Artin-Zhang-Verevkin theorem, which is known as the
non-commutative version of Serre’s theorem [33]. For more details on the subject,
see [5, 9, 12, 19, 36, 37].
Throughout the research of non-commutative geometry carried out in the above
papers, the category of left modules over a semi-graded ring has been an object of
great interest due to its properties and importance in the formulation of different
notions (e.g. noncommutative projective scheme, torsion, localization). In partic-
ular, the question about the existence of enough injective modules in this category
was formulated recently by Ramírez in her doctoral thesis [27, Section 3.4] (see also
2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 14A22, 16S38, 16S80, 16U20, 16W60.
Key words and phrases. Semi-graded ring, Grothendieck category, injective module, projective
module, Baer’s criterion.

3. Grothendieck category SGR −R
The objective of this section is to show that the category of left semi-graded
modules on a semi-graded ring is a Grothendieck category. In this way and as we
said above, we give an answer the question asked by Ramírez in her doctoral thesis
[27, Section 3.4] and [10, Section 4].

Remark 3.4. Theorem 3.3 emphasizes that the category SGR −R has an intrin-
sic geometric existence completely independent of any localization theory. Unlike
previous approaches which limited the foundational definitions to the subcategory
LSG−R of localizable left semi-graded modules over R whose morphisms are homo-
geneous R-linear maps of degree zero (e.g. [9, 10, 11, 12, 27]), this new framework
ensures that enough injectives exist globally in the semi-graded setting. In this way,
the constraints of schematicness and localizability considered in these works are not
needed to find injective objects, but rather serve as the specialized tools required
for the construction of subsequent coordinate map localizations.

4. Projective objects and resolutions
Having characterized the injective setting of the category SGR −R, our aim in
this section is to show that our canonical generating family formulated in Definition
3.1 provides an immediate source of projective objects.
Definition 4.1. An object P in SGR−R is said to projective if for any epimorphism
(surjective homogeneous morphism of degree zero) p : M →N and any degree-zero
morphism f : P →N, there exists a strict degree-zero morphism h : P →M such
that p ◦h = f. Equivalently, P is projective if and only if the covariant functor
HomSGR−R(P, −) is exact.
Theorem 4.2. For every n ∈Z, the free semi-graded shift R(n) is a projective
object in SGR −R.
Proof. Let n ∈Z. Suppose we are given an epimorphism p : M →N and a degree-
zero morphism f : R(n) →N. We must construct a lifting map h : R(n) →M.
Recall that the identity element 1R of the ring resides inside the homogeneous
component of degree −n of the shifted module, meaning 1R ∈(R(n))−n. Since f
is a strict morphism of degree zero, its evaluation at the identity must preserve the
degree, i.e. f(1R) ∈N−n. Because p : M →N is an epimorphism in SGR −R, it
is surjective component wise on each degree, which means p−n : M−n →N−n is a
surjective map of abelian groups. Since f(1R) ∈N−n, there exists a homogeneous
element m−n ∈M−n such that p(m−n) = f(1R).

We can now define and compute the overarching homological boundaries of the
category. The global dimension measures the maximum structural depth required
to resolve any semi-graded module as is well-known in Homological Algebra [31, 38].
Definition 4.5. Let M be an object in SGR −R.
The semi-graded projective
dimension of M, denoted pdSG(M), is defined as the infimum of the lengths of all
possible projective resolutions of M in the category SGR−R. If no finite projective
resolution exists, we set pdSG(M) = ∞.
Definition 4.6. Let R be a semi-graded ring. The semi-graded global dimension of
R, denoted gl.dimSG(R), is defined as the supremum of the semi-graded projective
dimensions of all semi-graded right (or left) R-modules, that is,
gl.dimSG(R) = sup {pdSG(M) | M ∈SGR −R} .
We now illustrate the projective theory developed in this section by explicitly
constructing projective resolutions for natural families of modules over examples of
semi-graded rings. In each case, the resolution consists of direct sums of the free
semi-graded shifts R(n), and the differentials are strictly homogeneous morphisms
of degree zero.
