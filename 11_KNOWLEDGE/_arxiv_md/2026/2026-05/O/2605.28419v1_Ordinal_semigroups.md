Ordinal semigroups
Paolo Lipparini
Abstract. In a previous paper we introduced a version of associativity for a partial
infinitary operation. We prove here that if γ is an infinite ordinal and some associative
infinitary operation is defined for all sequences indexed by ordinals ≤γ, then such
an operation can be uniquely expanded to apply to every sequence indexed by any
ordinal of cardinality |γ|.
In particular, if some associative operation is defined for all finite sequences as well
as for all ω-indexed sequences, then the operation can be uniquely expanded to apply
to every sequence indexed by a countable ordinal.
1. Introduction
Krob introduced (commutative) complete monoids in [9]. The notion im-
plicitly appeared earlier in Conway [5].
See Hebisch and Weinert [6] for a
survey with many applications. In [10] we generalized the notion to the non-
commutative case, dealing also with partially defined products, in particular,
encompassing former special cases treated, among others, by Tarski [14], Made-
vski, Trpenovski and ˇCupona [11], Belousov and Stojakovi´c [4], Perrin and Pin
[12], Bedon and Carton [2]. Our definition in [10] is modeled after the notion
of a ⋄-semigroup in Rispal and Carton [13], but we consider products of se-
quences indexed by arbitrary linearly ordered sets, while Rispal and Carton
deal only with sequences indexed by countable scattered linear orders. See also
Bedon and Rispal [3]. In detail a (partial) infinitary semigroup (Definition 2.1
below) is a structure with a partial “product operation” defined on certain
sequences indexed by a linearly ordered set. Such an operation is required to
satisfy a general form of associativity.
In the present paper we deal with the special case when the index sets
are ordinals, equivalently, well-ordered sets. We prove the extension theorem
mentioned in the abstract, see Theorem 3.1 for explicit details.

statement of Theorem 3.1 is quite involved, the reader might wish to consider
the particular case γ = ω first. In this case the content of the theorem is the
following. If we know every finite product, as well as every product of sequences

3. The main extension theorem
Theorem 3.1. Let γ be an infinite ordinal.
If (S, Q) is a partial ordinal semigroup such that Q
i<δ ai is defined exactly
for every nonzero ordinal δ ≤γ, then the operation Q can be uniquely expanded
to an operation Q∗(without extending the set S) such that Q∗
i<δ ai is defined
exactly for every nonzero ordinal δ such that |δ| = |γ| and in such a way that
the operations agree when both defined.
More formally, in the terminology from Definition 2.2, if µ = |γ|+ is the
successor of the cardinality of γ, then any ≤γ-semigroup S can be uniquely
expanded to a <µ-semigroup in such a way that only new products are defined,
no new element is added to S.
Proof. Let (S, Qγ) be a ≤γ-semigroup. It is enough to prove the following
statement.
(*) For every δ ≥γ such that |δ| = |γ|, (S, Qγ) can be uniquely expanded
to a ≤δ-semigroup (S, Qδ).
Indeed, suppose that we can prove (*), δ ≥ε ≥γ and |δ| = |ε| = |γ|.
Applying (*) twice to both δ and ε in place of δ, we get a ≤δ-semigroup (S, Qδ)
and a ≤ε-semigroup (S, Qε). If we restrict Qδ to those products which are on
sequences of order-type ≤ε, we get necessarily Qε, by the uniqueness condition
in (*). In other words, the sequence (Qδ)δ<|γ|+ is coherent, hence we can join
all the operations in order to get a <|γ|+-semigroup (S, Q∗). Property (Ord)
is satisfied in (S, Q∗) since, given a sequence as in (Ord), the condition can be
evaluated already in (S, Qδ), since π is assumed to be surjective, hence η ≤δ
and also each εj ≤δ.
Satisfaction of Property (U) is straightforward here and in all the arguments
below.
So let us prove (*). The proof goes by transfinite induction on those δ’s
such that δ ≥γ and |δ| = |γ|. The proof will go through the next two sections.
The basis of the induction δ = γ is straightforward: (S, Qγ) itself is the
only possible expansion (defining no additional product) and by assumption
it is a ≤γ-semigroup.
