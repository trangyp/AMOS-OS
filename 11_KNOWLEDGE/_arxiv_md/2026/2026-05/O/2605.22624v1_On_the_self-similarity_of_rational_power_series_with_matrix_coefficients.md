In this note, we shall view the map assigning to (m, n) the residue class of w(m, n)

of the prime field Fp. More generally, a n-dimensional tiling with color set
A is defined as a map T : Zn →A. Such a map may indeed be viewed as the
standard tiling of Rn by unit cubes, each of which is colored by an element of A
via the map T .
The Lucas’ property implies that the tiling (m, n) 7→w(m, n) mod p has fractal
features, similar to those enjoyed by the binomial coefficients. A generalization
of Razpet’s theorem to n-dimensional tilings with any finite field as a color set,
has been established in [Pan04]. The starting point of the present work was the
empirical observation that arrays of matrices with coefficients in a finite field,
defined by a recurrence relation as in (1.1), also enjoy remarkable self-similarity
properties. We refer to Figure 1 below for an illustration (see also Figure 3 in
the appendix). After having observed the tilings depicted in Figure 1, we were
naturally led to the following questions: In what precise sense are those tilings
self-similar? What is the algebraic explanation of this phenomenon? The goal
of this note is to provide a self-contained answer to those questions, that can
already be epitomized as follows: the self-similarity can be described through the
invariance under a so-called substitution, and the algebraic source of that intrinsic
symmetry is the Frobenius map.
Let us first review the notion of substitution-invariance. Sequences invariant under
substitutions have appeared in symbolic dynamics (see e.g. [Mor21] and [Got63])
and in information and communication theory (see e.g.
[Cob72]).
From the
point of view of tilings, sequences correspond to the one-dimensional case. The
idea of considering two- and higher-dimensional tilings that are invariant under
substitutions appears in the work of ˇCern´y–Gruska [ˇCG86] in theoretic computer
science, and in the work of O. Salon [Sal86, Sal87], that has a number theoretic
flavor, and pertains to the study of automatic sequences (see [AS03, Chapter 14]).
Salon’s work is mentioned by Shallit–Stolfi [SS89] in a paper that emphasizes the
fractals generated by two-dimensional substitutions. Independently, and roughly
at the same time, two-dimensional substitutions appear in the work of S. Mozes
[Moz89], that has a dynamical system flavor and is motivated by the study of
aperiodic tilings.

Remark 1.4. Following the terminology of O. Salon [Sal86], we say that M: Zn →
A with color set A is generated by a substitution of length p if there is a set
W, a tiling M′ with color set W, invariant under a substitution of length p, and
a map τ : W →A such that M = τ ◦M′. Note that the tiling M itself need
not be substitution-invariant. The fact that the tiling M from Theorem 1.3 is
indeed generated by a substitution of length p may be viewed as a consequence of
the main result of [Sal86]. Theorem 1.3 specifies explicitly how to construct the
substitution-invariant tiling M′ (namely M′ = T ). Moreover, it underlines the
linearity of all the maps involved, and it highlights the substitution-invariance of
the tiling Mpr. The proof of Theorem 1.3 presented below is self-contained.
