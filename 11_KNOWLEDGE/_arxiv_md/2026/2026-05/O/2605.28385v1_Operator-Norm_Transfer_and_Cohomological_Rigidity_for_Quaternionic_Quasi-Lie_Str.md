OPERATOR-NORM TRANSFER AND COHOMOLOGICAL RIGIDITY FOR
QUATERNIONIC QUASI-LIE STRUCTURES
WITH APPLICATION TO SLIDING MODE β-EXPONENTIAL STABILITY
NASSIM ATHMOUNI, NEJIB BRAHMIA, TAREK FAJRAOUI, AND FEHMI MABROUK
Abstract. We develop operator-theoretic and cohomological tools for quaternionic quasi-Lie structures, with
sliding mode control as a motivating application. Three main results are established. First, an exact operator-
norm transfer under the quaternionic anti-isomorphism a 7→¯a, which enables quantitative bounds from [1]
to transfer between left- and right-module conventions with no constant factor. Second, a transcription of
the cohomological rigidity result of [1] into a form usable here: in the homogeneous case, under a local
cohomological non-obstruction hypothesis, an explicit bilinear correction Ωproduces a bracket satisfying the
Jacobi identity exactly on a ball of admissible radius, with all quantitative constants expressed through C2 and
the admissible radius. Third, the projected Jacobi defect is shown to satisfy a generalized one-sided Lipschitz
condition with computable constants, obtained via a uniform-selection argument handling state-dependence
of the measurable selection. As an application, we develop a robust control framework with a cohomological
matching condition replacing pointwise verification: an integral sliding surface yields β-exponential stability
via an iterative linear matrix inequality (LMI) scheme. The work is purely analytical; closed-loop numerical
simulations for multidimensional systems are deferred to a companion paper. The framework is restricted to
the homogeneous quasi-Lie case; the Sobolev extension is conjectural, and algorithm termination is established
conditionally on sufficient continuity assumptions on the LMI solution map.

1. Introduction
Working with quaternionic systems forces a choice of convention. The state space Hn carries both a left- and
a right-H-module structure, and operator bounds proved under one convention do not, in general, translate
verbatim to the other — conjugation a 7→¯a is anti-multiplicative, not multiplicative. The recent quantitative
theory of quasi-Lie brackets on quaternionic Banach modules [1], on which we rely heavily, is set up for right
modules. Applications in attitude dynamics, where rotation quaternions act on the left, naturally favor the
opposite convention. Reconciling the two requires care.
The first half of this paper supplies the missing operator-theoretic ingredient. Given a bounded right-H-
linear operator T : Hn →Hn, we consider its conjugate T L := Φconj ◦T ◦Φconj, acting on the left-module
structure via componentwise conjugation Φconj. Because Φconj is an isometric involution, one expects ∥T L∥op
to be comparable to ∥T∥op; the question is whether equality holds, or only a bounded ratio. Theorem 2.1 shows
that equality holds. The proof is short and uses only the isometric property of Φconj, but the consequence is
what we need: every quantitative bound of [1] transfers to the left-module setting without a constant factor.

The Jacobi defect arises in linearized attitude dynamics [6, 7], and a system with non-
commutative uncertainties is exactly the setting where existing sliding-mode techniques run into trouble.

Definition 2.1 (Left and Right Quaternionic Modules). Hn is a left H-module when scalar multiplication is
(a, x) 7→ax (component-wise left multiplication). It is a right H-module when (x, a) 7→xa. The two structures
are related by the anti-isomorphism a 7→¯a: a right module becomes a left module by redefining a·L x := x·R ¯a,
and vice versa.
Remark 2.1 (Convention for this Paper). The state space Hn carries both left- and right-H-module structures.

H). Theorem 2.1 provides the tool to transfer quantitative bounds to the left-module convention, should this

2.4. Cohomological Rigidity of Quasi-Lie Brackets.
Definition 2.3 (Quasi-Lie Bracket and Jacobiator). Let L : Hn × Hn × [0, ∞) →Hn, (x, y, t) 7→L(x, y, t), be
a family of R-bilinear maps in (x, y) parametrized by t. We say L is a (time-dependent) quasi-Lie bracket if
it is skew-symmetric in (x, y) for every t and its time-dependent Jacobiator
J (x, y, z, t) = L(x, L(y, z, t), t) + L(y, L(z, x, t), t) + L(z, L(x, y, t), t)
satisfies ∥J (x, y, z, t)∥≤6C2∥x∥∥y∥∥z∥uniformly in t ≥0, for some C2 > 0. When L is time-independent, the
symbol J (x, y, z) is used and Jt ≡J becomes constant in t (this is the case for the test bracket of Section 7).
Throughout this paper, Jt and J (·, ·, ·, t) are used interchangeably.

8. Conclusion
We close with a brief assessment of what was achieved, what was not, and where the natural follow-up
problems lie.
On the operator-theoretic side, Theorem 2.1 gives an exact identity rather than the bounded ratio one
would expect from elementary considerations: ∥T L∥op = ∥T∥op, with no constant factor. The proof is short,
but its consequence is substantial — every quantitative bound of [1] now applies verbatim to left-module
formulations, which is the convention preferred in attitude dynamics. We also restate the rigidity theorem
of [1, Theorem 4.12] in a form (Theorem 2.2) that tracks the constants needed in the control argument,
and isolate the auxiliary residual R0 = J −dω0 that appears in the cohomological feedforward. The third
operator-theoretic ingredient, Lemma 2.8, is a GOSL bound for the projected Jacobi defect with constants

proof handles the state dependence by freezing the selection and using R-trilinearity — not a difficult trick,
but one that is easy to overlook.
These ingredients combine in Section 5 into a sliding-mode controller whose feedforward ∆app uses the

— a well-known practical nuisance — is resolved by the explicit ordering and halving loop of Algorithm 1.
Termination is established conditionally, under the sufficient conditions of Remark 3.5; both conditions are
classically expected but not proved here.
What remains open is worth stating clearly. The forward-invariance result (Proposition 3.2) gives a tra-
jectory bound ∥xr(t)∥≤R in the general case, but strict inclusion BR ⊆BM0 requires κ(P ∗) = 1. For
non-trivial Lyapunov shaping P ∗̸= I the strict inclusion remains beyond reach without further hypotheses.
Termination of the halving loop is conditional on continuity of the LMI solution map and strict feasibility
at the limit; both are expected to hold under mild hypotheses but a formal proof is not given. The strict
feasibility of the iterative LMI (57) is verified case by case in the test bracket; a general structural charac-
terization is open. Sobolev-type generalizations of the rigidity theorem would broaden the scope significantly
but are conjectural. Multidimensional closed-loop simulations are deferred to a companion paper, where the

Appendix B. Verification of the Test Bracket
We verify that the test bracket (61) L(x, y) = εb(¯xy −¯yx) has a non-zero Jacobiator. Direct computation
with x = i, y = j, z = k yields:
L(i, j) = εb(ij −ji) = εb(−ij −(−j)i) = εb(−k + ji) = εb(−k −k) = −2εb k,
L(j, k) = −2εb i,
L(k, i) = −2εb j.
Then, for x, y, z all purely imaginary, the bracket reduces to L(p, q) = εb(−pq+qp) = −εb [p, q]H (using ¯p = −p
for p purely imaginary). Hence on the imaginary sub-algebra (isomorphic to so(3)), L is proportional to the
Lie bracket of so(3), which satisfies the Jacobi identity exactly. In particular:
L(i, L(j, k)) = L(i, −2εbi) = −2ε2
b(ii −ii) = 0,
and similarly the other two terms vanish, so J (i, j, k) = 0. To exhibit a non-zero Jacobiator, we must choose
at least one argument with non-zero real part. Taking x = 1+i, y = j, z = k and εb = 0.1, direct computation
yields L(j, k) = −2εb i, L(k, 1 + i) = −2εb(j + k), L(1 + i, j) = 2εb(j −k), and
J (1 + i, j, k) = 0.04 i ̸= 0,
so ∥J ∥= 0.04 for these inputs. The general supremum bound supx,y,z ∥J (x, y, z)∥/(∥x∥∥y∥∥z∥) ≈0.0399
