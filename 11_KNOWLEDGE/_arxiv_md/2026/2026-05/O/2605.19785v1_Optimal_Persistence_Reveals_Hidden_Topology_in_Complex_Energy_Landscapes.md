INTRODUCTION
How does a persistent random walker explore a com-
plex energy landscape?
The accessible configuration
space is not static but dynamically enacted by the
walker’s own trajectory and timescales [1, 2]. Between
the limits of zero and infinite persistence lies an optimal
value — but is it set by system size, by temperature, or
by the spectrum itself?
Kent-Dobias recently showed that in the limit of in-
finite persistence, the walker’s ergodicity-breaking point
coincides exactly with the topological transition of the
landscape’s constant-energy slices [3]. Persistence thus
acts as a geometric detector. But that result concerns
the boundary of ergodicity. What happens at finite per-
sistence? Is the approach monotonic, or does an inter-
mediate, optimal persistence maximize exploration effi-
ciency?
In this Letter, we answer these questions by systemat-
ically studying a persistent Langevin walker on the p = 2
spherical spin glass [4, 5] across two orders of magni-
tude in system size (N = 16 to 1024) and temperatures
T = 0.05 to 0.30. We find that the route to infinite per-
sistence is non-monotonic, revealing a sharp size-driven
dynamical transition at low temperatures.
Main findings
We uncover an inverted-U relationship between explo-
ration efficiency Γ and persistence time τp (Fig. 1). The
optimal persistence τ ∗
p exhibits a rich dependence on tem-
perature and system size.
At high temperatures, τ ∗
p is independent of N; at low
temperature (T = 0.05), a sharp size-driven crossover
occurs as N increases through 128 (Figs. 2 and 3). This
crossover coincides with the discrete-to- quasi-continuous
transition in the GOE spectrum, where the entropic bot-
tleneck width reaches its maximum.
At fixed N = 128, τ ∗
p decreases with decreasing tem-
perature, revealing a thermal crossover that reflects ge-
ometric saturation of the bottleneck. These findings es-
tablish optimal persistence as a probe of hidden topology,
with the optimal memory time set by the landscape’s
spectral statistics rather than by temperature alone.
The behavior in the unsaturated regime (N < 128)
remains an open problem for future work.
Physical interpretation
The inverted-U follows from scanning efficiency (∝
τp) versus trajectory sparseness (∝1/τp), yielding a
Lorentzian form (End Matter Sec. S2).
The optimum
corresponds to ∂Γ/∂E = 0 at ec (End Matter Sec. S8),
where the Euler characteristic χ(E) exhibits a disconti-
nuity [3]. The T = 0.05 crossover is observed between
two regimes: discrete (τ ∗
p = 10) and quasi-continuous
(τ ∗
p = 5). Within the saturated regime (N ≥128), the
scaling is consistent with an RG fixed point. A full an-
alytical description of the crossover between regimes re-
mains an open question (End Matter Sec. S6).
Broader implications
This spectral-dynamical crossover reveals a distinct
regime: in the quasi-continuous regime, τ ∗
p = 5 is in-
dependent of ⟨∆λ⟩, consistent with a z = 1 dynamical
exponent (see End Matter Sec. S2) and hinting at an
infinite-randomness-like fixed point.
The principle of “optimal persistence” — matching the
walker’s memory time to the landscape’s characteristic
scale — extends beyond the p = 2 spherical model. In
active matter, colloids in porous media exhibit optimal
activity set by geometric constraints [6].
In optimiza-
tion, stochastic gradient descent with momentum faces
the same trade-off between jitter and overshoot. In glassy

Resonance mechanism

DISCUSSION
We have demonstrated that the exploration efficiency
Γ of a persistent walker on the p = 2 spherical spin glass
landscape is non-monotonic in τp, with an optimal per-
sistence τ ∗
p that depends on temperature and, in the low-
temperature regime, on system size.

increases (Figs. 2 and 3): τ ∗

Our data show that this condition is satisfied at τ ∗

to detect the topological transition, which depends on
the spectral density near ec.

This spectral-dynamical crossover reveals a distinct

The principle of “optimal persistence” — matching the
walker’s memory time to the landscape’s characteristic
scale — extends beyond the p = 2 spherical model. In
machine learning, stochastic gradient descent with mo-
mentum involves a similar trade-off [13]. In active mat-
ter, colloids in porous media may exhibit optimal activ-
ity set by geometric constraints [6]. In glassy dynamics,
the hierarchy of relaxation timescales may reflect spa-
tially varying local bottlenecks. We conjecture that this
spectral-dynamical crossover — optimal persistence gov-
erned by spectral statistics rather than geometry — is
generic in disordered landscapes with entropic bottle-
necks.
Limitations and outlook. Our conclusions at T = 0.05
are robust, based on consistent coarse-grid data across
all N. The N = 1024 data show a flat peak between
τp = 5 and 6; additional statistics are needed to deter-
mine whether the slight rise at τp = 6 reflects a genuine
trend or statistical fluctuation.
At T = 0.10, higher-
resolution scans for N ̸= 128 would clarify whether τ ∗
p is
truly independent of N or varies weakly. Extension to
p ≥3 spin glasses, where bottlenecks form hierarchical
tree structures, would test the universality of the reso-
nance principle.
CONCLUSION
We have shown that the canyon-finding rate Γ(τp) of a
persistent walker on the p = 2 spherical spin glass peaks
at an optimal τ ∗
p , forming an inverted-U profile.
At low temperature T = 0.05, τ ∗
p drops from 10 to 5 as
N crosses 128, marking the discrete-to-quasi-continuous
GOE crossover. For N ≥128 at T = 0.05, the canyon
width saturates at ξeff = 1, yielding τ ∗
p = 1/√2βT with
β = 0.4, i.e., τ ∗
p = 5.
At higher temperatures (T ≥0.15) and for N ≥128,
τ ∗
p = 10 and the scanning efficiency constant scales as
β(T) ∝1/T, reflecting an RG fixed point. (At T = 0.10,
high-resolution scans give τ ∗
p = 8.0.)
While infinite persistence marks the topological tran-
sition [3], finite persistence reveals an optimal memory
time — not too short (overdamped, trapped) and not
too long (ballistic, overshooting). This optimal persis-
tence unlocks the hidden topology at finite cost, making
it relevant for real physical systems where infinite persis-
tence is impossible.
The N ≤64 regime remains an open problem: despite
∆λ
√
N fluctuating around unity, τ ∗
p stays at 10 with
no simple theoretical explanation.
We conjecture that
the principle — tuning memory time to resonate with
the narrowest geometric bottleneck — may be generic.
In active matter, colloids in porous media exhibit opti-
mal spreading when run length matches pore size [6]. In
optimization, stochastic gradient descent with momen-
tum faces a similar trade-off between jitter and over-
shoot [13]. In glassy dynamics, scale-dependent bottle-
necks may generate a hierarchy of relaxation timescales
