ON MATRIX POLYNOMIALS AND THE JOINT SPECTRAL
RADIUS OVER MAX-ALGEBRAS
ASKAR ALI M1 AND SACHINDRANATH JAYARAMAN2,∗
Abstract. Our aim is to study matrix polynomials over max-algebras and their
growth in terms of a max-induced semi-norm. We investigate the relationship be-
tween the asymptotic growth of polynomial products and the joint spectral radius
of the associated coefficient set. Dynamics of max-linear maps and convergence
to periodic points under a single joint spectral radius condition and the existence
of common max-eigenvectors of the coefficient pool is also brought out.
1. Introduction
We work throughout over the field R of real numbers. By a max-algebra, we mean
the triple (R+, ⊕, ⊗), where R+ is the set of all nonnegative real numbers with the
binary operations a ⊕b = max{a, b} and a ⊗b = ab. Max-algebras provide an
idempotent linear-algebraic language for a broad class of nonlinear problems aris-
ing in discrete-event systems, scheduling, optimization and performance evaluation.
This setting is isomorphic (through the exponential map) to the max-plus model
commonly used in discrete-event dynamics. Excellent referencse on this subject are
the monographs [3, 5, 8].

the max-algebra described in the previous paragraph. Given any two such matrices

Motivated by these approaches, Muller and Peperko [15] developed a
detailed study of the joint spectral radius in max-algebras. Later on, the authors in
[7], extended these notions to matrix polynomials with a view to study various spec-
tral properties and norm inequalities in max-algebras. As Muller and Peperko point
out, there is an effective calculation of the joint spectral radius in max-algebras (for
bounded families), in terms of the maximum cycle geometric mean (see Proposition
2.1 of [15]). We shall exploit this to illustrate our results by means of examples.
The purpose of this article is to connect the above notions and define the joint-
spectral-radius of a family of max-matrix polynomials, in terms of the collection
of coefficient matrices. In particular, given a bounded collection Ψ of max-matrix
polynomials (see Definition 2.6), let us associate to it the coefficient pool Ψ0 ⊂
Mn(R+) obtained by collecting all coefficient matrices appearing in elements of Ψ.
Then, using the above defined max-induced seminorm η∥·∥, we define a set growth
rate for Ψ under polynomial multiplication and compare it to the max-algebraic
joint spectral radius of Ψ0. This provides a quantitative bridge between polynomial
algebra on the one hand and switched max-linear dynamics on the other hand.

In Section 3.3, we examine common eigenvectors and periodic points of a bounded
family of matrices (or correspondingly, polynomials with coefficients from this bounded
family), and its connection with the joint spectral radius. Periodic points arise nat-
urally in the dynamics of cone-preserving maps, and in particular in the iteration of
a nonnegative matrix acting on Rn
+. A classical consequence of nonlinear Perron–
Frobenius theory (see for instance Theorem B.4.7 of [12]) states that if a nonnegative
matrix A has spectral radius at most one, then along a suitable arithmetic subse-
quence of iterates, every bounded orbit converges to a periodic point: there exists
q ≥1 such that for each x with {Akx}k≥1 bounded, limk→∞Akqx = ξx, where ξx is a
periodic point of A and the period of ξx dividing q. This viewpoint motivates study-
ing convergence or limit sets through periodic points rather than through pointwise
convergence of Akx. In applications, one is often led not to a single matrix, but
to products drawn from a finite collection {A1, . . . , AN}, indexed by a word ω (fi-
nite or infinite). In the Euclidean (standard-algebra) setting, S. Jayaraman et. al.
[11] prove that if each generator has spectral radius 1 and the collection admits
a nontrivial set of common eigenvectors E, then for each finite word ω and each
initial condition x in the linear span LC(E), there exists an integer qω ≥1 such
that limk→∞Akq
ω x = ξ(x, ω), where ξ(x, ω) is a periodic point of the word product
Aω (with period dividing q). Moreover, once the word contains all generators (in
the sense that each symbol appears at least once), the integer qω and the limiting
periodic point becomes independent of the particular choice of such a word. Recent
work in the max-algebra setting extends periodic-point phenomena from a single
matrix to words under generator-wise cycle-mean bounds [10]. In the present paper
we strengthen this by replacing individual spectral constraints on the generators
with a single global stability condition expressed in terms of the max-algebraic joint

2. Preliminaries
We shall collect some of the basic definitions and preliminary notions needed
further. These may be found in [3] and [6]. We begin with the notion of a digraph
associated to A ∈Mn(R+).
Definition 2.1. For A = (aij) ∈Mn(R+), the directed graph GA associated to A
has vertices {1, . . . , n} with an edge i →j of weight aij if and only if aij > 0.
A (simple) circuit C has weight w(C) = Q aiℓiℓ+1; the cycle mean and the maximal
cycle mean of C are then given by µ(C) = w(C)1/|C| and µ(A) = max{µ(C) : C ⊆
GA} respectively. A (right) max–eigenpair satisfies Ax = λx with λ ∈R+ being a
max–eigenvalue.
Definition 2.2. A ∈Mn(Rn
