On the Ultraviolet Problem for the Ground State
Energy of the Translation-Invariant Pauli–Fierz
Model at Zero Total Momentum
Volker Bach, Miguel Ballesteros, Merten Mlinarzik
24-Oct-2025
Abstract
We study the ground state energy of the Pauli–Fierz model in the absence
of external potentials. We consider the fiber decomposition of the Pauli–Fierz
operator with respect to the spectral values, p, of the total momentum opera-
tor and focus on the case p = 0. The corresponding variational problem is ana-
lyzed to estimate the dependence of the ground state energy on the ultraviolet
cutoff Λ. We employ a Bogoliubov–Hartree–Fock approximation using pure,
quasifree states generated by Bogolubov transformations (parametrized by a
positive Hilbert–Schmidt operator z) and Weyl transformations (parametrized
by a vector η) applied to the vacuum. We prove that the resulting energy func-
tional is not a convex function of η and z. We identify the non-convex term
and remove it from the energy functional. The modified functional retains the
full interaction term and is shown to be strictly convex. We study the ground
state of the modified functional and prove the existence of a unique minimizer.
Furthermore, we construct an explicit partial minimizer (with respect to η,
for fixed z), which allows us to eliminate z and reduce the minimization prob-
lem to a single variable, η. Finally, we estimate the minimum of the modified
energy functional in terms of the ultraviolet cutoff Λ and demonstrate that,
up to a constant factor, it grows asymptotically as Λ3/2, as Λ →∞.

Estimating the ground state energy for the Pauli–Fierz model remains a longstanding
challenge, with numerous results addressing this problem in the literature.
The infrared divergence in translation-invariant models was first investigated in the
1970s in [30]. More recent work in [36] established the absence of ground states
in such translation-invariant systems.
Early progress on massive field couplings
appeared in [31], where the existence of a ground state for an electron interacting

with a massive bosonic field was proven under fixed total momentum conditions.
Subsequent developments in [1, 51] demonstrated ground state existence for systems
with x2-type external potentials and perturbations.
A significant breakthrough emerged with the introduction of spectral renormaliza-
tion techniques in [9, 10, 11], which enabled rigorous analysis of spectral properties
including ground states and resonances for infrared-regularized models with ultra-
violet cutoffs. These methods were later extended to non-regularized models in [12]
and further improved with optimal conditions in [33]. Complementary approaches
were developed in [8, 16, 38, 39] both for confined and for translation-invariant
systems. Alternative treatments under more restrictive hypotheses were presented
in [41], while related models were investigated in [2, 32, 28].
The multiscale analysis framework was introduced in [47] for ground state studies.
The complex multi-scale analysis suitable for resonances was developed in [6]. The
method of [6] has proven equally powerful as spectral renormalization for quantum
electrodynamics problems.
Resonances for non-regularized infrared problems were proven to exist in [49] (spec-
tral renormalization) and in [5] (multi-scale analysis).
The construction of the ultraviolet limit has been successfully carried out for some
models in quantum field theory: the Nelson model by a Gross transformation [35, 45],
the spin-boson Hamiltonian [25], the Fr¨ohlich Hamiltonian [34, 42], and the Yukawa
model [26].
