ON ALMOST PERIODICITY IN CRYSTALLINE MEASURES
JAN MAZ´AˇC, CHRISTOPH RICHARD, AND NICOLAE STRUNGARU
Abstract. Meyer defined crystalline measures as tempered distributions µ such that both
µ and its Fourier transform bµ are pure-point Radon measures of locally finite support. He
conjectured that every crystalline measure is almost periodic as a tempered distribution.
Favorov constructed a counterexample and asked whether crystalline measures are at least
almost periodic as general distributions. To resolve Favorov’s question, we first show that the
almost periodicity of a crystalline measure is characterised in terms of its translation bound-
edness, in any class of Radon measures, tempered distributions, or general distributions. We
then construct a crystalline Fourier eigenmeasure that fails to be translation bounded even
as a distribution. We finally construct a crystalline measure that fails to be a Fourier qua-
sicrystal (in particular, it fails to be slowly increasing), but it is an almost periodic tempered
distribution whose Fourier transform is even a norm almost periodic measure. Our examples
fully resolve the questions of Meyer and Favorov and sharply delineate the class boundary
of translation boundedness.
They also demonstrate the unusual behaviour of crystalline
measures beyond the class of Fourier quasicrystals.
1. Introduction
Aperiodic order has attracted substantial attention over the last few decades. In 2000, La-
garias gave an influential overview of the field [42], in which he identified important questions
and aspects deserving further study. To describe crystalline measures within that broader
context, we give a brief tour from model sets via Fourier quasicrystals to crystalline measures.
This also serves to motivate basic notions and to highlight important connections and recent
developments in the field. We will restrict our focus to crystalline measures from Section 1.5
onwards, and we present the main results of this paper in Section 1.7.
1.1. Aperiodic order of finite local complexity and almost periodicity. For a trans-
parent setting, let us start with the class of point sets Λ in Euclidean space such that both Λ
and its set of point differences Λ −Λ are uniformly discrete.
It contains all lattices and
their shifts. Point sets in this class are locally rigid in the sense that they can only have
finitely many local configurations up to shifts, like lattices. The above class is stable under
shifting and under taking subsets, which might be regarded as a source of disorder. It came
as a surprise that there exist ordered examples in that class beyond finite sums of shifted
lattices. They were found by Meyer in 1972 and are certain projections of subsets of a higher-
dimensional lattice [58, 60, 48]. Meyer’s regular model sets are, if restricted to be inversion
symmetric, the Euclidean examples of approximate lattices [16]. The above class does not
contain any other members than shifted subsets of approximate lattices.
The order might be described via almost periodicity.

1.2. Pure-point diffraction and quasicrystals. Another notion of order arises from op-
tics. It was formalised in 1930 by Wiener for the signal analysis of functions [82] and has
been adapted in 1995 to the class of translation-bounded measures with positive lower density
by Hof [36]. For point sets, one associates to Λ an autocorrelation measure, which captures
information about all frequencies of points in Λ−Λ. Its Fourier transform is called the diffrac-
tion measure. Then, the pure-pointedness of the diffraction measure is the footprint of order.
Note that adding points of zero density to a given point set does not change its diffraction
spectrum. In this sense, the above notion of order is weaker than almost periodicity. Regular
model sets are pure point diffractive. This central result in diffraction theory may be regarded
as a consequence of the Poisson summation formula for the higher-dimensional lattice (whose
importance was already stressed by Meyer, see also [73, 29] for more recent elaborations).
In fact, Wiener also studied the connection between pure-point diffractivity and various
types of almost periodicity [82, 83]. In the context of translation-bounded measures with
positive lower density, this question has been independently addressed by Gou´er´e [33]. There
is a decent understanding of that connection by now [49], which is sketched in Remark 2.17
below. We note that almost periodicity implies a pure-point diffraction measure [50]. We
also remark that pure-point diffraction can be characterised dynamically by the pure-point
spectrum of an associated dynamical system. This dynamical approach, which also dates back
to Wiener’s pioneering works, has proved itself very useful in diffraction theory [46, 7, 8]. We
refer to the monograph [6] for a detailed account on diffraction and aperiodic order.
The renewed interest in Wiener’s generalised harmonic analysis was triggered by the dis-
covery of certain highly ordered but aperiodic metallic alloys [75] in diffraction experiments
in 1982. They were called quasicrystals, and a number of mathematical descriptions were pro-
posed, which mimic constructions of the Penrose tiling [55], see Kramer’s review article [39].
The corresponding point sets were called cut-and-project sets. In 1986, de Bruijn studied
Fourier properties of these point sets [19], and their generalised Poisson summation formula
led de Bruijn to coin a notion of Poisson comb [20]. In 1995, these point sets were identified
as regular model sets [59].

unusual Poisson summation formulae. Thus, they belong to the class of de Bruijn’s Poisson
combs [20].
1.4. Fourier quasicrystals. After the discovery of modulated crystals and quasicrystals,
it was natural to ask whether aperiodic Poisson combs can have a locally finite set of non-
zero Fourier–Bohr coefficients. An answer to that question came from a different direction.
Almost periodicity was studied for analytic functions already by Bohr (compare [15]) and was
discovered in the 1950’s in (locally finite) zero sets of certain entire almost periodic functions
by Krein and Levin [54]. In 1998, Favorov, Rashkovskii, and Ronkin showed that every almost
periodic multiset on the line is the zero set of some entire almost periodic function [28]. In
2020, it came as another surprise that the latter class indeed contains aperiodic uniformly
discrete point sets with locally finite Fourier–Bohr spectrum. They were found by Kurasov and
Sarnak [41, 40] and were named Fourier quasicrystals1. Their Fourier transform is a measure
that fails to be translation bounded but is slowly increasing.
Olevskii and Ulanovski [68, 69] then showed that, on the line, such point sets are in one-
to-one correspondence with zero sets of finite trigonometric polynomials having simple real
roots. The underlying connection with Lee–Yang polynomials has recently been extended
to Dirac combs with integer weights [2] and to higher dimensions [3]. Meyer showed that
the above examples also arise from a generalised cut-and-project construction, which led him
to introduce a notion of curved model set [64]. A similar approach, which uses the group
compactification of such point sets together with techniques from algebraic geometry, has
been taken up by Lawton and Tsikh to yield examples in general Euclidean space [43, 44],
which overlap with higher-dimensional examples due to Meyer [65, 66].

for a tempered measure µ, which is either positive or one-dimensional, uniformly discrete
support of both µ and bµ results in a periodic structure. Meyer defined the class of crystalline
measures, i.e., tempered distributions µ such that both µ and its Fourier transform bµ are
pure-point measures of locally finite support [61]. This class contains the Dirac combs of

on a model set construction (which appear under the name enriched or projective model sets)

these fail to be positive or to have uniformly discrete support. The point sets by Kurasov and
Sarnak provided the first examples that are positive and do have uniformly discrete support.
It is an open question whether there exists an aperiodic crystalline measure supported
within a model set. For substantial subclasses, the existence of such a measure can be ruled
out [53, 12]. Another problem concerns crystalline measures that are Fourier eigenmeasures,
such as lattices of density one. In the periodic case on the line, these have been classified [10].
Recently, Fourier eigenmeasures that generalise Guinand’s measure to higher dimensions have
been found [1] via a surprising connection to modular forms (which also provides a classifica-
tion tool). Fourier quasicrystals are nowadays defined to be crystalline measures µ such that
both µ and bµ are slowly increasing measures. The above examples of crystalline measures
are all Fourier quasicrystals. Properties of general crystalline measures have been studied
notably by Favorov, see e.g. [24, 25, 26, 18].
1.6. Almost periodicity in crystalline measures. Major questions concerning the almost
periodicity of general crystalline measures have remained unresolved. If pure-point spectrum
and almost periodicity are considered to be Fourier dual notions, crystalline measures should
be almost periodic, in an appropriate sense. Initially, Meyer argued that all crystalline mea-
sures should be almost periodic tempered distributions [61]. He then realised a gap in his
argument, made his statement a conjecture [62, Conj. 2.1], and proved it for a particular sub-
class of crystalline measures. Favorov proved Meyer’s conjecture for all Fourier quasicrystals
[25, Cor. 2], and he essentially proved it for all positive crystalline measures [24, Thm. 1], as
we explain in Section 3 below. Based on Kolountzakis’ constructions of crystalline measures
[38], Favorov recently gave an intriguing example of a crystalline measure that is not a Fourier
quasicrystal. He showed that it is translation unbounded as a tempered distribution [26], and
thus it cannot be an almost periodic tempered distribution. This led him to interpret Meyer’s
conjecture as a statement about almost periodicity as a distribution.
1.7. Overview of the article. In this article, we study almost periodicity in general crys-
talline measures. We adapt methods from almost periodicity in translation-bounded measures
[30, 79] to the setting of tempered distributions, following [76, 78]. We speak of strong al-
most periodicity instead of almost periodicity, in order to distinguish this notion from weak
almost periodicity based on Eberlein’s weakly almost periodic functions, which generalise
Bohr almost periodic functions [21]. We show that, for crystalline measures, strong almost
periodicity is equivalent to translation boundedness, within the classes of measures, tempered
distributions, and general distributions (Theorem 3.9). This reduces the question of strong

In Section 2, we review the relevant notions, with
focus on Fourier quasicrystals. In Section 3, we clarify the relation between strong almost
