Abstract. In this paper, we establish a higher order Morrey’s inequality in
the framework of RCD(K, N)-spaces for K ∈R and N ∈N. We do so by first
introducing an alternate version of the second order Sobolev space W 2,p(X), which
contains amply many functions even when p > N.

In this paper, we establish a higher order Morrey’s inequality for functions in second
order Sobolev spaces in the framework of RCD(K, N)-spaces. Here, RCD(K, N)-spaces
are certain metric measure spaces having a notion of Ricci curvature bound from below
by K and dimension bounded from above by N with an added Riemannian structure.
The theory of W 1,p(X)-Sobolev spaces for p ∈] 1, +∞[ over a metric measure space
(X, d, m) was formulated by Ambrosio, Gigli, and Savaré ([2]) in terms of p-weak upper
gradients by using mass transport theory without assuming the RCD(K, N)-structure.
On the other hand, Gigli [22] established a theory of (non-smooth) second order
calculus on RCD(K, N)-spaces and defined the W 2,2(X)-Sobolev space over such a
space (X, d, m) in terms of an object called the L2-Hessian. For an RCD(K, N)-space
(X, d, m), there are a number of candidates for the W 2,p(X)-Sobolev space for any
p ∈] 1, +∞[, but there may be a possibility that W 2,p(X) consists of only constant
functions if the underlying space (X, d, m) itself is too singular, which may be the
case for certain Aleksandrov spaces. To avoid this trivial case, we introduce a new
modified Sobolev space W 2,p

of Morrey’s inequality for functions in W 2,p

A few comments on the notion of “W 2,p Sobolev space” when p ̸= 2 are in order.
When p = 2, the theory established by Gigli via the language of L2-normed L∞-
modules yields an object that can rightly be called “the Hessian” of a function for
some class of functions W 2,2(X) on an RCD(K, N) space (see [26, Chapters 4 and
6] for further details). There are at least two natural candidates to extend this
definition to spaces W 2,p(X) for p ̸= 2. The first is to define Hess f for a function
f ∈W 1,p(X) as an element of the module L2((T ∗)⊗2X) (as in [26, Definition 6.2.6]),
then declare f ∈W 2,p(X) if the real valued function |Hess f|HS belongs to Lp(X).
However, under this approach it may not be possible to show (with the current
technology) that W 2,p(X) contains any elements besides constant functions, even

Test∞(X) ⊂W 2,2(X) in [26, Section 6.2.3].
The other natural candidate is to declare Hess f for a function f to be an element
of the module Lp((T ∗)⊗2X) satisfying [26, Definition 6.2.6] for a different, suitable
class of test functions depending on the value of p. However, the tools used to
obtain the inclusion Test∞(X) ⊂W 2,2(X) in [26, Section 6.2.3] are specific to p = 2.
Additionally, it is not at all clear if elements of W 2,p(X), should they be defined
this way, can be found even for spaces that are limits of extremely nice sequences of
Riemannian manifolds.

We also mention some history of first order Morrey type inequalities. In the frame-
work of complete smooth Riemannian manifolds with positive injectivity radius and
bounded sectional curvature, Morrey’s inequality was implicitly proved by Aubin [7,
Subsection 2.23, Proof of the Sobolev embedding theorem 2.21]. In the framework of
N-dimensional Aleksandrov space with N ∈N and the lower bound curvature K ∈R,
Morrey’s inequality was shown by Kuwae–Machigashira–Shioya [32, Theorem 7.3].
In Hajłasz–Koskela [28, Theorem 5.1(3)], they proved a general Morrey’s inequality
in the framework of metric measure space admitting global volume doubling con-
dition, [28, (21)] and (1, p)-Poincaré inequality in terms of upper gradients. Also
in Alonso-Luiz–Baudoin [1, Theorem 5.9], a generalization of Morrey’s inequality is
proved in the framework of metric measure space satisfying a weak Bakry–Émery-type
condition, but [1, Theorem 5.9], or [28, Theorem 5.1(3)] requires a global volume
doubling condition as in Coulhon [15, (D)].

Next fix x0 ∈X, and let {ζℓ}ℓ∈N be the sequence constructed in Lemma A.2. If
f ∈W 1,p(X) then since
|(ζℓ−1)f| ≤2|f|,
|D(ζℓ−1)f| ≤|ζℓ−1||Df| + |f||Dζℓ| ≤|Df| + |f|,
we see {ζℓf}ℓ∈N ⊂W 1,p(X)bs and since after passing to a subsequence the above
implies |(ζℓ−1)f| + |D(ζℓ−1)f| →0 pointwise m-a.e., the dominated conver-
gence theorem applies to yield ζℓf →f in ∥·∥W 1,p(X).
In particular, by taking
f ∈Test∞(X) ∩W 1,p(X), since Test∞(X) is an algebra by [26, Theorem 6.1.11], this
implies that Test∞(X)bs is W 1,p-dense in Test∞(X) ∩W 1,p(X), finishing the proof
in this case.
When p ∈[ 2, +∞[, the argument above shows W 1,p(X)bs ∩W 1,2(X) = W 1,p(X)bs
is dense in W 1,p(X). Thus, we immediately find that Test∞(X) ∩W 1,p(X) is W 1,p-
dense in (W 1,p(X), ∥·∥W 1,p(X)) when p ∈[ 2, +∞[.
□
