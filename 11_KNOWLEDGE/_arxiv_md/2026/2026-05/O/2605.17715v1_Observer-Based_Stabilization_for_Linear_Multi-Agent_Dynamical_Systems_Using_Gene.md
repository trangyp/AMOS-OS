Abstract: We address the conditions and design of controllers and observers for homogeneous
networks of linear MIMO agents. We develop networked controllers and observers that ensure the
stability of both the system state and the estimation error, leveraging the concept of generalized
frequency variables. A separation principle for networks is then established, showing that the
observer and controller can be designed independently and combined to achieve a stable output
feedback. Our results are illustrated via a highly unstable, oscillatory network of locally actuated
pendulums on carts. Finally, necessary conditions for controllability and observability—derived
from agent properties and network structure—are established and discussed.
Keywords: Multi-agent systems, linear systems, control, estimation, separation principle.
1. INTRODUCTION
Multi-agent systems—those viewed as an interconnection
of (a large number of) sub-systems or agents—are of
particular research interest thanks to their numerous ap-
plications, e.g., in drones (Souli et al., 2023). To cope
with the challenges arising in such large-scale networks,
many studies have been conducted, e.g., on decentralized
control (Wang and Davison, 1973; Bakule, 2014). One
approach that can provide a unifying theoretical paradigm
for a general class of multi-agent systems—where agents
operate autonomously while exchanging information with
one another—is the notion of generalized frequency vari-
ables introduced in (Hara et al., 2007). In this view-
point, a network of identical linear single-input–single-
output (SISO) agents with transfer function h(s) can be
described by the transfer function G(s) := G(φ(s)), where
G(s) is a proper rational function representing only the
interactions between agents, or the network structure, and
φ(s) := 1/h(s). Within this framework, G(s) is obtained by
simply replacing the traditional frequency s in G(s) with
φ(s), motivating us to call φ(s) a generalized frequency
variable. This paradigm, extended to the case of multi-
input–multi-output (MIMO) agents in (Hara et al., 2019),
enables a separation of agent and network properties in
the analysis and reduces the computational burden.
In this work, we study conditions for, and the design of, ob-
servers and controllers for homogeneous networks of linear
time-invariant (LTI) MIMO agents using the language of
generalized frequency variables. The proposed results are
illustrated through a pendulum network example. Control-
lability and observability conditions for networks studied
in (Hara et al., 2011; Trumpf and Trentelman, 2019) are
revisited for improved checkability.

complex) numbers and by Rm×n (resp., Cm×n) the set of

4. ON CONTROLLABILITY AND OBSERVABILITY
We discuss the controllability and observability of sys-
tem (5), which correspond to those of the pairs (A, B) and
(A, C), respectively, and are suﬃcient conditions for con-
troller and observer design. Recall that in the MIMO agent
case (m > 1), (Trumpf and Trentelman, 2019, Theorem 9)
shows that, under the assumption rank(B) < N, the pair
(A, B) is controllable if and only if (Ah, Bh) is controllable,
(Ah, Ch) is observable, and the polynomial matrix
Ψ(s) := (IN ⊗Dh(s) −A ⊗Nh(s) −B ⊗Nh(s))
(16)
is left prime (Dh(s), Nh(s) given below (2)). For the SISO
agent case (m = 1), (Hara et al., 2011, Proposition 3.1)
shows that when rank(B) < N, (A, B) is controllable if
and only if (Ah, Bh) is controllable, (Ah, Ch) is observable,
and (A, B) is controllable. Observability conditions can
be stated as a duality. Now, we aim to derive alternative
conditions that cover the MIMO agent case while sepa-
rating the agent properties from those of their interaction
structures, which are more intuitive from application view-
points. We ﬁrst show that the multi-agent system cannot
be more controllable (or observable) than its structure.
Lemma 3. If (A, B) is controllable, then (A, B) is control-
lable. If (A, C) is observable, then (A, C) is observable.
Proof. We prove only controllability; observability fol-
lows by duality. Assume that (A, B) is uncontrollable.
Then, by the PBH test (see, e.g., Hespanha (2018)),
there exist λ ∈σ(A) and a non-zero v ∈CN such that
v⊤A = λv⊤and v⊤B = 0. Let µ ∈σ(Ah + λBhCh)
and pick a non-zero left eigenvector η ∈Cn such that
η⊤(Ah + λBhCh) = µη⊤. Deﬁne z⊤:= v⊤⊗η⊤∈C1×Nn,
which is non-zero. Using Kronecker identities, we get
z⊤A = (v⊤⊗η⊤)(IN ⊗Ah + A ⊗(BhCh))
= v⊤⊗(η⊤Ah) + (v⊤A) ⊗(η⊤BhCh)
= v⊤⊗(η⊤Ah) + (λv⊤) ⊗(η⊤BhCh)
= v⊤⊗(η⊤(Ah + λBhCh)) = µ(v⊤⊗η⊤) = µz⊤,
z⊤B = (v⊤⊗η⊤)(B ⊗Bh)
= (v⊤B) ⊗(η⊤Bh) = 0 ⊗(η⊤Bh) = 0.
Thus, there exists a left eigenvector z⊤of A such that
z⊤B = 0. So, (A, B) is uncontrollable by the PBH test. ■

If we combine Lemmas 3 and 4, controllability of (A, B)
implies that of (A, B), and so (i)(b) follows. While the
criterion of (Trumpf and Trentelman, 2019) provides a
complete necessary and suﬃcient condition for control-
lability of MIMO networks through the left-primeness of
the polynomial matrix Ψ(s), this condition is expressed
in frequency-domain and polynomial-matrix terms that
are diﬃcult to interpret in classical state-space language.
In contrast, for SISO agents, (Hara et al., 2011) shows
that this condition reduces to the simple PBH test for the
interconnection pair (A, B) under agent minimality. The
situation is fundamentally diﬀerent in the MIMO case. In
general, controllability of (A, B) together with minimality
of the agent is not suﬃcient for controllability of the
network, and the mechanism by which controllability is
lost is not directly visible from the PBH test, as seen next.
