On the transversals of Latin squares generated by
nonlinear bipermutive cellular automata
Alberto Dennunzio1, Maximilien Gadouleau2, and Luca Mariot3
1 Department of Informatics, Systems and Communication, University of
Milano-Bicocca, Italy
alberto.dennunzio@unimib.it

m.r.gadouleau@durham.ac.uk
3 Semantics, Cybersecurity and Services Group, University of Twente, The Netherlands

Abstract. In this short paper, we begin to investigate the conditions
under which a generic Bipermutive Cellular Automaton (BCA) with
no-boundary conditions of diameter d generates a Latin square of order
N = 2d−1 admitting an orthogonal mate, without relying on the linearity
of the local rule. Since an orthogonal mate exists if and only if the
Latin square can be partitioned into N disjoint transversals, we start by
characterizing the subclass of BCA whose Latin squares have a transversal
on the main diagonal. In particular, we prove that the main diagonal forms
a transversal if and only if the generating function of the bipermutive
local rule induces an invertible CA with periodic boundary conditions
on a configuration of size d −1. We then perform exhaustive search
experiments, showing that d = 6 is the smallest diameter for which
there exist nonlinear bipermutive CA that generate Latin squares with a
transversal on the main diagonal.
Keywords: cellular automata · Latin squares · bipermutivity · transversals ·
orthogonality
1
Introduction
Latin squares are combinatorial designs with applications in cryptography [1],
coding theory [4], the design of experiments [9], and several other fields. Despite
their simple definition, Latin squares to some of the most fascinating questions
in combinatorics and discrete mathematics [10].
A recent line of works considered how to construct Latin squares via Cellular
Automata (CA) (see e.g. [12] for a recent survey of relevant results). In particular,
the authors of [16] showed that a no-boundary CA of length 2(d −1) equipped
with a bipermutive local rule (i.e., a Bipermutive CA, or BCA) of diameter d
generates a Latin square of order N = 2d−1. Moreover, that work also investigated
under which conditions the Latin squares generated by two BCA are orthogonal,
that is, their superposition yields all the ordered pairs in the Cartesian product

[N] × [N], where [N] = {1, . . . , N}. More precisely, by focusing on the subclass
of Linear BCA (LBCA), the authors of [16] proved that the orthogonality of the
Latin squares is equivalent to the coprimality of the polynomials associated to the
underlying linear local rules. The motivation to study this type of constructions
is that families of Mutually Orthogonal Latin Squares (MOLS) can be used to
design threshold secret sharing schemes and authentication codes, among other
cryptographic primitives [20].
The rich body of literature leveraging the construction of orthogonal Latin
squares through LBCA [6,7,13,17] contrasts with what little is known about Latin
squares induced by nonlinear BCA. So far, only a necessary condition on the
local rules of two nonlinear BCA has been exhibited in [15] to generate orthogonal
Latin squares, while [18] proposed a heuristic approach based on evolutionary
algorithms to construct pairs of nonlinear BCA that generate orthogonal Latin
squares. A complete characterization of nonlinear BCA-based orthogonal Latin
squares is still a wide open problem.
In this exploratory paper, we start to tackle the study of Latin squares
generated by nonlinear BCA from the perspective of their transversals. The
reason is that a Latin square admits N disjoint transversals (with N being the
order of the square) if and only if it has an orthogonal mate, i.e. another Latin
square orthogonal to it [10]. In particular, in the context of this paper we focus
on a simpler problem, namely studying under which conditions a nonlinear BCA-
based Latin square has a transversal on its main diagonal. Our main result is a
characterization of such squares in terms of the invertibility of a periodic-boundary
CA that defines the main diagonal of the square, equipped with the generating
function of the BCA as a local rule. Finally, we perform a computational search
for such Latin squares generated by BCA of small diameters, observing that our
characterization needs at least diameter d = 6 to yield nonlinear rules.
2
Preliminaries
2.1
Latin Squares
Here, we only recall the essential definitions related to Latin squares. The reader
is referred to [10] for a more complete overview of this subject.
Definition 1. A Latin square of order N ∈N is an N ×N matrix L with entries
from [N] such that for all i, j, k ∈[N] with j ̸= k, one has that L(i, j) ̸= L(i, k)
and L(j, i) ̸= L(k, i).
Thus, in a Latin square each number from 1 to N occurs exactly once in each row
and in each column of a Latin square. Alternatively, each row and each column
of L forms a permutation of [N]. The next definition formalizes the notion of
orthogonal Latin squares:
Definition 2. Two Latin squares L1, L2 of order N ∈N are orthogonal if for
all distinct pairs of coordinates (i, j), (i′, j′) ∈[N] × [N] it holds that
(L1(i, j), L2(i, j)) ̸= (L1(i′, j′), L2(i′, j′)) .
(1)

Hence, two Latin squares of order N are orthogonal if their superposition yields
all ordered pairs of the Cartesian product [N] × [N] exactly once.
A transversal in a Latin square L of order N is a set T of N coordinates
pair T = {(i1, j1), . . . , (iN, jN)} where for each distinct pair (ik, jk), (il, jl) ∈T it
holds that ik ̸= il and jk ̸= jl, and {L(i1, j1), . . . , L(iN, jn)} is a permutation of
[N]. Stated otherwise, each number from 1 to N occurs only once in the entries
of L defined by the transversal T. It is well-known that a Latin square L admits
an orthogonal mate (i.e. another Latin square M that is orthogonal to M) if and
only if it can be decomposed into N disjoint transversals [10].
2.2
Cellular Automata

we consider No-Boundary CA (NBCA) and Periodic-Boundary CA (PBCA),
which we formally introduce below. Further, we consider only binary CA, meaning
that each cell can only be in a state s ∈F2 = {0, 1}.

Transversal on the Main Diagonal

Latin square L is equivalent to the existence of an orthogonal mate of L. However,
characterizing the conditions under which a BCA generates a Latin square with
N disjoint transversals seems like a tough combinatorial problem, which is beyond
the scope of an exploratory paper like the present one. Thus, we start with an
easier version of the CA transversal problem: namely, when does a BCA generate
a Latin square that features a transversal on the main diagonal? The formal
statement of the problem is given below:

bipermutive rule is invertible. Notice that invertibility does not necessarily mean
that the PBCA T must also be reversible: what we are looking for are the so-
called globally invertible rules i.e., rules inducing invertible PBCA only for certain
array sizes. A famous example of globally invertible rule is the transformation
χ : F3
2 →F2 defined as f(x1, x2, x3) = x1 ⊕x2 ⊕x2x3, which give an invertible
CA whenever the array size is odd [3]. However, χ is not a good example for our
construction: taken as a generating function of d −2 = 3 variables, it does not
yield an invertible PBCA of length d −1 = 4 cells, since the length of the array
is even. Other examples of these complementing landscapes rules1 can be found
in the appendix of Daemen’s PhD thesis [2], as well as in [8,19].
To make our investigation more systematic, we implemented an exhaustive
search algorithm that, given in input the diameter d of the bipermutive local rule,
search among all the 22d−2 generating functions of d−2 variables those that define
invertible PBCA of length d −1. In particular, we performed our exhaustive
search experiments up to d = 6, which means looking at 65 536 generating
functions in the largerst considered instance. Interestingly, up to diameter d = 5
our exhaustive search only found linear rules that generated invertible PBCA
of length d −1, or equivalently Latin squares with a transversal on the main
diagonal. On the other hand, for d = 6 our search yielded 472 rules in total, out of
which 456 were nonlinear. As an example, Figure 3 in the Appendix reports the
32× 32 Latin square generated by the BCA F : F10

is defined by the nonlinear generating function g(x1, x2, x3, x4) = x1 ⊕x3 ⊕x1x4.
5
Conclusions
Clearly, the road to a complete characterization of all transversals in BCA-based
Latin squares is still quite long: so far, we managed to find a class of rules that
induce a transversal on the main diagonal. Then, the next natural step of this
line of investigation is to extend the result to other transversals, with the goal of
constructing other N −1 disjoint ones beside the main diagonal. In turn, this
would yield a construction for nonlinear BCA that generate Latin squares with
an orthogonal mate. A interesting direction to tackle this problem is to define
other transversals in terms of the main diagonal. For example, one could think
of applying a linear transformation (such as an XOR shift) on the coordinates of
the diagonal, and then check if the new set of coordinates yields a permutation.
We plan to investigate this idea in future research.
