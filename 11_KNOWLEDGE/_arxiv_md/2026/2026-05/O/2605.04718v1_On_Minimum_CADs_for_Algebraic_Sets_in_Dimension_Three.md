On Minimum CADs for Algebraic Sets in Dimension Three
Lucas Michel
University of Liège
Belgium
lucas.michel@uliege.be
Abstract
Cylindrical Algebraic Decomposition (CAD) algorithms typically
produce a decomposition adapted to a finite family of semi-algebraic
sets F (i.e. every member of F is a union of cells). Different algo-
rithms may produce different outputs, and introduce unnecessary
cell divisions. Recent work by Michel, Mathonet, and Zénaïdi in
ISSAC 2024 formalised this issue by studying the refinement order
on the set of all CADs adapted to F and analysing the existence
of a minimum (coarsest) adapted CAD. It was shown that such a
minimum adapted CAD always exists for subsets of R and R2, but
not of R𝑛(𝑛⩾3) in general.
It is natural to seek natural classes of subsets of R𝑛that admit a
minimum adapted CAD. In this paper, we identify a class of subsets
of R3 that contains all algebraic sets for which minimum adapted
CADs do exist. This provides the first positive existence theorem
for minimum CAD for a non-trivial class of sets.
Keywords
Cylindrical Algebraic Decomposition, Algebraic set, Minimum CAD

Introduction
Cylindrical Algebraic Decomposition (CAD) (introduced by Collins
[5] for quantifier elimination over real closed fields), is a corner-
stone of computational real algebraic geometry and plays a central
role in several areas of computer algebra, including quantifier elim-
ination, symbolic computation, and SMT solving. Its importance
is reflected in the large body of work devoted to improving CAD-
based methods, both theoretically and practically. As a consequence,
different CAD algorithms may produce different decompositions
for the same input, often with superfluous cell divisions. In this
paper, we are interested in CADs that are as coarse as possible, and
hence free of such superfluous cell divisions.
A CAD can be seen as a datastructure to represent semi-algebraic
sets. Given a finite family F of semi-algebraic subsets of R𝑛, a CAD
C is adapted to (or represents) F if every member of F is a union
of cells of C . In [10] and the extended version [11], we introduced
a formal framework for comparing CADs adapted to F through
the refinement ordering (a CAD C is smaller than a CAD D if C
is coarser than D). This allows one to reason about CADs from an

order-theoretic perspective and to distinguish between minimal
CADs, which cannot be coarsened while remaining adapted, and
minimum CADs, which are canonical, redundancy-free CAD that
are coarser than any other adapted CAD. It was proved that every
(finite family of) semi-algebraic set in R and R2 admit a minimum
CAD. However, this property fails in higher dimensions: explicit
counterexamples in R𝑛(𝑛⩾3) exhibit several semi-algebraic sets
that admits several distinct minimal CADs, and hence no minimum
one. Thus, in dimensions three and higher, semi-algebraic sets need
not possess such a canonical CAD.
Since CAD algorithms typically take as input a finite family of
multivariate polynomials and produce a decomposition adapted
to all algebraic varieties they define, it is natural to ask whether
such finite families always admit a minimum CAD. In other words,
when restricting attention to algebraic (rather than arbitrary semi-
algebraic) sets, can we expect the existence of a canonical optimal
decomposition?
In this paper, we give a positive answer in dimension three
and obtain a stronger statement. We prove in Theorem 4.1 that
every finite family of closed and curtained (defined below) semi-
algebraic sets in R3 admits a minimum CAD. This extends the
existence results of R and R2 and shows that the non-existence
phenomena identified in R3 cannot occur under these topologi-
cal/algebraic/geometric constraints. As a consequence, we obtain
the following main result of the paper.
Theorem 1.1. Every finite family of algebraic sets in the space R3
admits a minimum CAD.
The only criterion currently available for showing that a given
finite family F admits a minimum CAD is Theorem 6.3 of [11].
However, its application depends heavily on F , which prevents
its use in our setting. In this paper, we instead provide a proof of
Theorem 1.1 that is independent of Theorem 6.3 of [11], relying on
some confluence property and topological arguments.
After recalling relevant material from [10, 11] in Section 2, we
present in Section 3 several preliminary technical result, which are
then used in the proof of the main theorem given in Section 4.

3
Technical results
In this section, we develop the machinery required for the proof
of the main result (Section 4). We still consider a finite family
F = (𝑆1, . . . ,𝑆𝑝) of semi-algebraic subsets of R𝑛, with 𝑛∈N∗.
In Subsection 3.1, we identify two properties (closedness and
curtainedness) that fail in the counterexamples of [10]. We will
show in Section 4 that these properties are actually sufficient to
guarantee the existence of a minimum adapted CAD (when 𝑛= 3).
In Subsection 3.2, we provide a characterization of this existence
in terms of the confluence (a property based on CAD(F ) reduc-
tions) of a strict subset of CAD(F ). The remaining subsections
provide the technical ingredients needed for the proof of the main
result based on that characterization. In Subsection 3.3, we give a
criterion ensuring that a tree reduction lifts to a CAD reduction,
based on the continuity of certain functions defined by unions. In
Subsection 3.4, we establish conditions guaranteeing the continuity
of such functions. Finally, in Subsection 3.5, we prove some topo-
logical properties of CAD cells of the plane, which are then used
to verify that these continuity conditions are satisfied in Section 4
under the closedness and curtainedness assumptions.
3.1
Closed and curtained sets
We motivate the assumptions of Theorem 4.1 by observing some
properties that are not satisfied in the counterexamples of [10]. First,
the Trousers T (see Definition 4.3 of [10]) is not closed, and therefore
cannot be expressed as a finite union of sections of a continuous
function (see the Closed Graph Theorem: Proposition 2.14 of [14]).
Note that this explains the non-confluence of CAD(T) discussed in
Subsection 5.3 of [10]. However, closedness alone is not sufficient,
as illustrated by Example 4.9 of [10], where certain closed sets
intersect a vertical line ℓ= {𝑝} × R ⊂R3 in infinitely many points
without coinciding with ℓ. This phenomenon is ruled out by the
curtained condition introduced in Definition 3.1 below. As we prove
in Theorem 4.1, assuming that all sets defining F are closed and
curtained ensures the existence of a minimum CAD (when 𝑛= 3).
The geometric notion of curtains frequently appears in the study
of CADs and related algorithms (see [13] and the references therein).
Definition 3.1. Let 𝑆be a semi-algebraic set of R𝑛and 𝑊⊆R𝑛−1.
We say that 𝑆has a curtain (in the last direction) at𝑊if𝑊× R ⊆𝑆.
We say that 𝑆is curtained if for every 𝑝∈R𝑛−1, the fibre 𝑆∩
({𝑝} × R) is either finite, or equal to the whole line {𝑝} × R (i.e. 𝑆
has a curtain at {𝑝}).
Proposition 3.2. Every algebraic set 𝑉in R𝑛is closed and cur-
tained.
The converse is not true: roughly speaking, most of the closed
and curtained semi-algebraic sets are not algebraic.
Proof. By definition, an algebraic set 𝑉⊆R𝑛is the common
zero locus of finitely many 𝑛-variate polynomials with real coef-
ficients 𝑃1, . . . , 𝑃𝐾∈R[𝑋1, . . . ,𝑋𝑛]. Since each 𝑃𝑘is continuous,
their common vanishing set 𝑉is closed.
Consider now 𝑝= (𝑥1, . . . ,𝑥𝑛−1) ∈R𝑛−1 such that the fibre
𝑉∩({𝑝} × R) is infinite. For each 𝑘∈{1, . . . , 𝐾}, consider the
univariate polynomial 𝑃𝑘(𝑥1, . . . ,𝑥𝑛−1,𝑋𝑛) ∈R[𝑋𝑛]. Since it has
infinitely many real roots (in 𝑋𝑛), it must be the zero polynomial.
Hence 𝑃𝑘vanishes identically on {𝑝} × R for every 𝑘, and therefore
{𝑝} × R ⊆𝑉. This shows that 𝑉is curtained.
□
Thus, the class of closed and curtained sets contains the class of
algebraic sets. Moreover, when 𝑛= 3, it enjoys useful topological
properties of specific interest here (see Corollary 3.23 below).
3.2
CADs irreducible at the last level
We introduce a strict subset CAD𝑛-irr(F ) of CAD(F ) that shares
the same minimal elements, and in which every section of the CADs
under consideration is directly related to the sets defining F . The
first property will reduce the discussion in the proof of the main
theorem, while the second is crucial for exploiting the assumptions
on F , specifically in Subsection 4.2.
To obtain this subset, we intuitively start from CAD(F ) and
perform some trivial CAD(F ) reductions, namely those occurring
at the last level. More precisely, for every C ∈CAD(F ), we observe
that every tree reduction rule Ψ𝐼defined on Tree(C, F ) at the
last level (i.e. with |𝐼| = 𝑛) lifts to a CAD(F ) reduction rule Φ𝐼
defined on C . In particular, no additional topological assumptions
are required, in contrast to the case where |𝐼| < 𝑛.
Definition 3.3. A CAD C ∈CAD(F ) is said to be irreducible at
the last level (or 𝑛-irreducible for short) if there exists no CAD(F )
reduction Φ𝐼defined on C with |𝐼| = 𝑛. We denote by CAD𝑛-irr(F )
the set of all 𝑛-irreducible CADs adapted to F .

5
Further Work
First, the results of this paper are primarily theoretical. To move
towards an effective implementation of an algorithm computing
minimum CADs, it will be necessary to develop concrete and com-
putationally feasible criteria for deciding when a tree reduction
lifts to a CAD reduction. We conjecture that the adjacency relations
between cells (see for instance [2] and [15]) will play a central role
in formulating such criteria.
Next, the grail would be the design of a projection operator
whose associated CAD algorithm directly produces a minimum
CAD adapted to the input. Such an operator is unlikely to rely
only on the classical discriminant and resultant based projection
operators, since these inherently consider all complex roots rather
than the real roots only.
Finally, the techniques employed here to prove Theorem 1.1
seem to not generalize immediately in R𝑛with 𝑛⩾4. First, the
case distinction would be more intricate. Second, Corollary 3.22
fails in general in R4 (see Proposition 3.5 of [9]).
Question. Does every finite family of algebraic sets
in R𝑛(𝑛∈N∗) admit a minimum CAD?
This question remains open for 𝑛⩾4.
