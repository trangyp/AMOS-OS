Abstract
A broadcast on graphs models is a natural and well-studied way to capture limited-range transmissions.
Formally, a broadcast on a connected graph G = (V, E) is a function f that assigns each vertex
v an integer f(v) with 0 ⩽f(v) ⩽ecc(v) where ecc(v) denotes the eccentricity of v. A vertex u
hears a broadcasting vertex v (with f(v) > 0) if u is at distance at most f(v) from v. Beyond
the classical broadcast domination problem, where every vertex is required to hear at least one
vertex, two variants raise intriguing combinatorial and algorithmic questions. In an independent
broadcast, no broadcasting vertex hears another broadcasting vertex, while a broadcast packing
requires that every vertex hears at most one broadcasting vertex. The corresponding problems
Broadcast Independence and Broadcast Packing ask for broadcasts of values at least k under
these constraints, where the value is the sum of the broadcast values. We initiate a systematic
study of the parameterized complexity of such problems. We prove that Broadcast Independence
and Broadcast Packing are FPT parameterized by the treewidth plus the diameter of G, with a
family of dynamic-programming algorithms over nice tree decompositions. We obtain as a corollary
that both problems are FPT parameterized by k and the treewidth of G and XP for treewidth only.
The latter result shows that the known algorithm for trees (Bessy and Rautenbach, DAM 2022) can
indeed be extended to bounded-treewidth graphs. On the negative side, we show that Broadcast
Independence is W[1]-hard parameterized by the pathwidth of G. Note that this result completes
the picture for parameter k and treewidth for Broadcast Independence since it is known to be
W[1]-hard for k only. We complement these results by showing that a weighted version of both
problems, where the input comes with a weight function on the edges, is W[1]-hard parameterized by
the vertex cover of G. Finally, we provide a constant-factor approximation algorithm parameterized
by treewidth for Broadcast Independence.

denotes the eccentricity of v, i.e. the maximum distance from v to any other vertex, and

Finding a broadcast of minimum value such that every vertex hears a broadcasting vertex is
thus a natural variant of the classical Dominating Set problem. Surprisingly and in sharp
contrast with Dominating Set which is one of Karp’s 21 NP-complete problems [16], a
beautiful polynomial-time algorithm finding an optimal broadcast domination in polynomial
time has been given by Heggernes and Lokshtanov [14].
Several other broadcast variants have been studied in the literature, notably broadcast
independence, irredundance and packing (see for instance the introductory section of [6]
for more details). In this paper, we focus on independence and packing. A broadcast σ is
independent1 if no broadcasting vertex hears another broadcasting vertex; more precisely,
for any distinct broadcasting vertices u, v, we must have dG(u, v) > max(σ(u), σ(v)) where
dG is the distance in G. A broadcast ρ is a packing1 if every vertex hears at most one
broadcasting vertex; or, equivalently, for any distinct broadcasting vertices u, v, we must
have dG(u, v) > ρ(u) + ρ(v). The corresponding optimization problems ask for a broadcast
of maximum value P
v∈V f(v) that satisfies one of the aforementioned properties. We denote
these problems by Broadcast Independence and Broadcast Packing, respectively,
and define them as follows in their decision version:
Broadcast Independence
Input: a graph G = (V, E), an integer k ∈N.
Output: a broadcast σ of G of value at least k such that for any u, v ∈V with u ̸= v
and σ(u) > 0, σ(v) > 0, it holds that dG(u, v) > max(σ(u), σ(v)).
Broadcast Packing
Input: a graph G = (V, E), an integer k ∈N.
Output: a broadcast ρ of G of value at least k such that for any u, v ∈V with u ̸= v
and ρ(u) > 0, ρ(v) > 0, it holds that dG(u, v) > ρ(u) + ρ(v).
Related work
Although many structural bounds have been given in the last decade about
both broadcast variants [1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 10, 15, 18] the complexity status remained open for quite
some time even on simple graph classes. For instance, the complexity status of Broadcast
Independence on trees was repeatedly asked for in the literature [1, 10, 13] and was settled
only recently by Bessy and Rautenbach [5] who gave an O(n9) time algorithm. This result
was subsequently improved to O(n8) in [18]. Algorithms for other specific subclasses of
trees (such as the one for locally uniform 2-lobster of fixed length) have also been developed,
see e.g. [1, 2]. Regarding the general case there is little hope to have a polynomial-time
algorithm as Bessy and Rautenbach proved that Broadcast Independence is NP-hard
to approximate within n1−ε on general graphs, and also NP-hard on planar graphs with
maximum degree 4 [5]. We note that the authors of [5] left open the possibility to adapt
their algorithm on trees to graphs of bounded treewidth. The Broadcast Independence
problem shares some similarity with the Distance p-independent set [12, 19] (also called

Our results
We follow this line of research by initiating a study of Broadcast Indepen-
dence and Broadcast Packing from the parameterized complexity viewpoint. In such a
context the problem comes together with some parameter k ∈N and the aim is to decide the
problem in time f(k) · poly(n) where f is any computable function and n is the size of the
instance. If such an algorithm exists then the problem is Fixed-Parameter Tractable (FPT
for short). It is possible to show that a problem is unlikely to be fixed-parameter tractable
by proving W[1]-hardness via parameterized reductions. In such a case one seeks an XP
algorithm with complexity f(k) · ng(k), where f and g are computable functions. We refer
the reader to [8] for precise definitions of the W-classes. A natural parameterization for both
problems is the value k required for the sought broadcast. We moreover consider several
structural parameterizations of the problems by mainly considering the well-known treewidth
(tw), pathwidth (pw), vertex cover (vc), and feedback vertex set (fvs) parameters. We refer
the reader to [8] for more definitions regarding parameterized complexity.
Our contributions are both positive (with algorithms) and negative (with parameterized
hardness), see also Figure 1 for an illustration. Below we state our main results for both
problems.

Preliminaries
We consider undirected finite connected graphs, denoted G = (V, E) where V (G) is the
vertex set of G and E(G) its edge set. For the sake of readability we use V and E whenever
the context is clear. Throughout the paper we let n := |V | and m := |E|. We sometimes
consider weighted graphs, with a weight function ω: E →N defined over the edges of G.
The (weighted) shortest distance between two vertices u and v is denoted by dG(u, v) (we
drop the subscript whenever the context is clear). The eccentricity of a vertex v is denoted
ecc(v) and is the largest distance between v and any other vertex of G. The diameter of a
graph G is denoted diam(G) and is the maximum eccentricity among vertices (note that
since we consider connected graphs the diameter will always be finite). Given any integer n,
we let [n] denote the set {1, . . . , n} and [n]0 = {0} ∪[n]. A function f : V →[diam(G)]0 is a
broadcast if f(v) ⩽ecc(v) for every vertex v. A vertex v is broadcasting (or a broadcaster)
if f(v) > 0. The value of f is P
v∈V f(v). A vertex u hears v if d(u, v) ⩽f(v). For a set
X ⊆V , we denote by Xf := {v ∈X | f(v) > 0} the set of broadcasting vertices in X. A
broadcast is independent if dG(u, v) > max(f(u), f(v)) for any u and v broadcasting vertices,
i.e. no broadcasting vertex hears another broadcasting vertex. Similarly, a broadcast is
packing if dG(u, v) > f(u) + f(v) for any broadcasting vertices u and v, i.e. no vertex hears
more than one vertex. Recall that in edge-weighted variants of both problems we consider
the distance-based definitions of independent and packing.Given any broadcast f, we denote

We also consider a variant for both problems where the value of any broadcasting vertex
is bounded by some fixed integer p. We call such broadcasts p-broadcasts and such problems

can assume that p ⩽diam(G) and that 1-Broadcast Independence is equivalent to the

Conclusion
In this work, we initiated the study of the parameterized complexity of Broadcast In-
dependence and Broadcast Packing for standard structural parameters. Prior to our
results it was only known that Broadcast Independence is W[1]-hard parameterized by
the natural parameter k through an easy reduction from Independent Set. Using dynamic
programming on nice tree decompositions, we obtained FPT algorithms parameterized by
tw plus diameter. Our result generalizes the known polynomial-time algorithm for trees
by Bessy and Rautenbach [5]. We complemented this result by providing hardness for
several parameterizations, namely vertex cover for weighted variants of both problems and
feedback vertex set size plus pathwidth for Broadcast Independence. Together, these
results imply hardness parameterized by treewidth for Broadcast Independence. We
moreover provided constant factor approximation algorithms parameterized by treewidth,
thus completing the picture for this problem-parameter combination.
The first natural open problem that arises from our work is to determine the complexity of
Broadcast Packing parameterized by treewidth. Note that if it turns out to be W[1]-hard

then Theorem 31 would provide a constant-factor parameterized approximation algorithm
as well. Finally, we believe that our algorithms can be extended to graphs of bounded
cliquewidth and generalized to other metric-distance problems. We leave this as an open
problem.
