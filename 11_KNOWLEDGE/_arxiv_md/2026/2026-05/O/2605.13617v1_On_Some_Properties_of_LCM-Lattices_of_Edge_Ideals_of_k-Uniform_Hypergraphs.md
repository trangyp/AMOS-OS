1. Abstract
In this article, we investigate the combinatorial and algebraic properties
of the lcm-lattice associated with the edge ideal of a hypergraph. Let H be
a hypergraph, I(H) its corresponding edge ideal in a polynomial ring in n
variables, and Icm(I(H)) the associated lcm-lattice. We establish conditions
under which the lcm-lattice of an edge ideal is Boolean, modular, or com-
plemented. Furthermore, we extend these results to the case of the product
of lcm-lattices in the complemented case. Additionally, we study the effects
of polarization on the lcm-lattices of I(H) and its polarized ideal.
AMS Classification: 06A06, 06A07, 06A11.
Keywords: posets, Boolean, modular, complemented lattices.
2. introduction
Hypergraphs and their associated algebraic structures have long been cen-
tral objects of study in combinatorial commutative algebra. Given a hyper-
graph H, its edge ideal I(H), defined in a polynomial ring over n variables,
encodes the combinatorial structure of H in an algebraic form. The study
of the lcm-lattice, denoted by Icm(I(H)), provides a lattice-theoretic per-
spective on the generators of I(H).
The structural properties of lcm-lattices such as being Boolean, modular,
or complemented which play a crucial role in determining the algebraic and
combinatorial behavior of the underlying ideal. In this article, we identify
precise conditions under which the lcm-lattice of a hypergraph’s edge ideal
possesses these properties. We further investigate how these properties ex-
tend to the product of lcm-lattices in the complemented case and analyze
the effect of polarization on the lcm-lattice structure.
Foundational background on monomial ideals can be found in the work of
Herzog and Hibi [7]. Classical results on posets and enumerative techniques,
which are central to the study of monomial ideals and their associated lat-
tices, are presented in Stanley’s monograph [11].
More recently, Dorang
[3, 4] investigated structural properties of lcm-lattices of monomial ideals.
The concept of the lcm-lattice of a monomial resolution was introduced by
Gasharov, Peeva, and Welker [12], who showed that if there exists a map be-
tween two lcm-lattices that is bijective on atoms and preserves joins, then a
resolution of one ideal induces a resolution of the other. In particular, if the

3. Results and Discussions
This section presents the basic concepts, results, and discussions.
Let
G = (V, E) be a simple undirected graph with vertex set V = {1, . . . , n} and
edge set E. More generally, a k-uniform hypergraph H = (V, E) consists
of a vertex set V = {1, . . . , n} and an edge set E, where each edge is a
k-element subset of V . The corresponding edge ideal I(H) is the monomial
ideal in the polynomial ring S = K[x1, . . . , xn] over a field K, generated by
the square-free monomials
xi1 · · · xik
for each {i1, . . . , ik} ∈E
(see [9, 6] for details).
Definition 3.1. A poset is a pair (S, ⪯), where S is a set and ⪯is a binary
relation on S satisfying:
• Reflexivity: x ⪯x for all x ∈S;
• Antisymmetry: if x ⪯y and y ⪯x, then x = y;
• Transitivity: if x ⪯y and y ⪯z, then x ⪯z.
If these conditions hold, the relation ⪯is called a partial order.
Definition 3.2. A lattice L is a poset (E, ≤) such that, for any two elements
a and b in L, there exists a unique greatest lower bound, denoted by a ∧b
and called the meet of a and b, and a unique least upper bound, denoted by
a ∨b and called the join of a and b.
A lattice can be visualized through its Hasse diagram, where elements are
arranged according to their rank, i.e., smaller elements (lower rank) appear
below larger ones (higher rank). Line segments are drawn to connect each
element with those it directly covers. For a detailed introduction to lattices
and order theory, we refer to [1, 2].
Definition 3.3. The lcm-lattice of I(G), denoted L(I(G)), is the lattice
whose elements are all least common multiples of subsets of the minimal
generators of I(G), partially ordered by divisibility. We denote the unique
maximal element (the least common multiple of all generators) by ˆ1 and the
minimal element by ˆ0.

3.3. Hypergraphs and Complemented Lattices.
Definition 3.10. If L is a bounded lattice then we say that y ∈L is a
complement of x ∈L if ∃ˆ0, ˆ1 such that x ∧y = 0 and x ∨y = 1. In
this case, we say that x is a complemented element of L. Clearly, every
complement of a complemented element is itself complemented. A lattice L
is a complemented lattice if every element of L is complemented.
