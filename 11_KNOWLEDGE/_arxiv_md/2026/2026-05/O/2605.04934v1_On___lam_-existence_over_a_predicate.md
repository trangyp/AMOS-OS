On λ-existence over a predicate
Alexander Usvyatsov∗
Abstract
We prove that in a countable theory T fully stable over a predicate P, any
λ-complete set A has the λ-existence property. This means that A can be ex-
tended to a λ-saturated model of T without changing the P-part. The notion
of λ-completeness, introduced in this paper, captures some obvious necessary
conditions for such an extension to be possible (for example, the P-part of A
has to be a λ-saturated model of the appropriate theory). So in a fully stable
theory T, λ-existence can only fail for trivial reasons. This generalizes results
of Chatzidakis in the context of difference fields of characteristic 0.
1
Introduction
Let T be a complete first order theory, P a distinguished unary predicate. Let
T P be the complete first order theory of P. More precisely, we will work in a
monster model C |= T, and T P will be the theory of CP . In addition, assume
that P is embedded and stably embedded; i.e., there is no additional structure
definable on P, which is not definable already in T P with parameters from
P.
One can ask the following natural question: does every model of T P “oc-
cur” as the P-part of some model of T? We know that, in general, the answer
to this question is “no”. So the question becomes: under what conditions on
T is the above the case for all models of T P ? Alternatively, one can ask:
under what conditions on the model of T P is the above the case for an ar-
bitrary T? These and related questions are addressed in several papers, e.g.,
[She86, SU22, SU25, Usv24a]. For example, in a recent paper [Usv24a], the
author has showed that if T is fully stable over P (see Definition 6.2), then the
answer is “yes” for any model of T P . In fact, more is true: any complete set A
(Definition 2.4) can be extended to a model of T without changing its P-part
(completeness is an obvious necessary condition on A for this to be possible).
A similar result has been recently proven in a preprint by Bayes, Kaplan, and
Simon assuming a different notion of stability over P (in particular, it holds
for any simple theory T).

One motivating example for this study is the theory of difference fields.
Afshordel [Afs14] has shown that any difference field (K, σ) with a pseudo-
finite fixed field k = Fix(σ)(K), can be extended to a model M of ACFA
without changing the fixed field, i.e., k = Fix(σ)(M). The author’s result
mentioned above generalizes this for the case of characteristic 0: it is shown
in [Usv24a] that ACFA0 is indeed fully stable over P = Fix(σ). The results
of Bayes, Kaplan, and Simon, generalizes this for all characteristics, since
ACFAp is simple for any value of p. At the same time, the results in [Usv24a]
give more: specifically, if T is fully stable, and A is a complete set, the model
M |= T extending A with P A = P M can be chosen to be locally constructible
over A. In particular, in case of ACFA0, this shows that every difference
field k of characteristic 0 with a pseudo-finite fixed field can be extended to
a model K of ACFA0 with Fix(σ)(K) = Fix(σ)(k) such that K is locally
constructible over k. It would be interesting to ask whether the same is true
for ACFAp.
This paper addresses a different (but related) question. Suppose N |= T P
is λ-saturated. Is there a λ-saturated model M of T with P M = N? If the
answer is “yes”, we say that T has the λ-existence property over N. One can
ask for more, e.g., M is λ-prime, or even λ-primary over N.
If e.g. λ = λ<λ and N is a saturated model of T P of cardinality λ, then the
answer to the above questions in “yes” [SU22]. If, however, N is a λ-saturated
model of T P of a larger cardinality, things become more complicated.
The original motivation comes, again, from the study of difference fields.
In [Cha23], Chatzidakis has shown that ACFA0 has λ-existence (over the
fixed field) for all uncountable λ (and even λ = ℵε).
Moreover, every λ-
saturated pseudo-finite field k of characteristic 0 can be extended to a λ-
saturated model K of ACFA0 with Fix(σ)(K) = k such that K is λ-prime
over k.
Motivated by Chatizidakis’ work, the author has shown in [Usv24b] that,
given a theory T fully stable over P and λ > |T|, the class Kλ(T, N) of λ-
saturated models of T with a fixed P-part N, admits prime models. This,
however, relies on the class Kλ(T, N) being non-empty.
It was left open
whether, given a prescribed model of T P which is λ-saturated, it always “oc-
curs” as the P-part of some λ-saturated model of T. In the case of ACFA0,
for example, this is not hard, and is one of the first steps in the proof of the
main theorem in [Cha23]. In the general context of model theory over P, it
is often the existence problems that prove more difficult, and require more
sophisticated techniques (in our case, stability theory).
In this paper we address this question and prove that if T is fully stable
over P, then T has λ-existence over P. Moreover, every complete set A whose
P-part is a λ-saturated model N of T P can be extended to a λ-saturated
model M of T with P M = N such that M is λ-constructible over N. This
generalizes the results in [Cha23], since ACFA0 is fully stable.
It is interesting to point out that, although we do not know whether a
similar result holds for ACFAp for p > 0, Chatzidakis has conjectured that
the answer is “no”. This would immediately imply that ACFAp is not fully

Preliminaries
In this section we set up the framework and recall basic notions and facts.
The reader is referred to previous works (e.g., [SU22, Usv24a]) for a more
detailed discussion.
2.1
Conventions and hypotheses
Convention 2.1. Let T be a complete first order theory, P a monadic pred-
icate in its vocabulary. For simplicity, we will assume that the language of T
does not contain function symbols (so that every subset of a model, containing
all the constant symbols, is a substructure; in fact, we will treat all subsets as
substructures). In addition, we assume that T implies that P is infinite.
Let C be the monster model of T. From now on, we assume that all models
of T are elementary submodels of C, and all sets are subsets of C.
For M |= T, we denote by M|P the set P M viewed as a substructure of
M. Similarly, for a subset A ⊆M, we denote by M|A the substructure of M
with universe A. We write A ≡B if Th(C|A) = Th(C|B).
We also denote CP = C|P and T P = Th(C|P ). For a set A, we denote
P A = A ∩P C.
When no confusion should arise, we will write P for P C. Also, for a set
A, we will often denote by A both the set and the substructure of C with
universe A. So for example, when we write that A ∩P C is λ-saturated, or
just that A ∩P is λ-saturated, we mean that the substructure C|A∩P C is a
λ-saturated model of the appropriate theory (if A ∩P ≺P, which will be the
case in this paper, then the appropriate theory is T P ).
Throughout the paper, we are going to make the following fundamental
assumptions on T:
Hypothesis 2.2.
(i) Every type over P C is definable. In other words, P
is stably embedded: subsets of P C that are definable with parameters
(in C), are already definable (in C) with parameters in P C.
(ii) In addition, subsets of P C that are 0-definable (in C), are already 0-
definable in CP (modulo T P ).
(iii) Moreover, T has quantifier elimination (even down to the level of pred-
icates).
See [SU22] for clarifications and “justifications” for making these assump-
tions.

2.2
The existence property, completeness, and sta-
bility
The main concept under discussion in this paper is the λ-existence property
(see also Definition 2.7 below). We recall some basic concepts from [Usv24a],
[SU22], and [SU25]:
Definition 2.3.
(i) We say that a set A has the existence property over P,
or simply the existence property if there exists M |= T such that A ⊆M
and P M = P A.
(ii) Let λ be a cardinal. If, in addition, in clause (i) there is M which is λ-
saturated, we say that A has the λ-existence property over P, or simply
the λ-existence property.
Given a set A, in order for their to be a chance for M as above to exist,
P A should be “suitable” for being the P-part of a model of T. For example,
P A should obviously be itself a model of T P . In fact, this is not enough. P A
should also be closed under the parameters necessary to define types of tuples
of A over P. These conditions are summarized in the following definition (see
also Fact 2.6).
Definition 2.4. A ⊆C is complete if for every formula ψ(¯x, ¯y) and ¯b ⊆A, |=
(∃¯x ∈P)ψ(¯x,¯b) implies (∃¯a ⊆P ∩A) |= ψ(¯a,¯b).
The following is clear:
Observation 2.5. If M ≺C and P M ⊆A ⊆M, then A is complete.
Clearly, in order for A to have the λ-existence property, P A also has to
be λ-saturated.
The following useful easy characterization offers another understanding of
the notion of completeness (Observation 4.2 in [SU22]):
Fact 2.6. A set A is complete if and only if for every ¯a ⊆A and ψ(¯x, ¯y) the
ψ-type tpψ(¯a/P C) is definable over A ∩P C and A ∩P C ≺P C.
Now that we have defined complete sets, we can recall the notion of full
existence:
Definition 2.7.
(i) We say that a theory T has the full existence property,
or just has full existence, if every complete set A has the existence
property.
(ii) We say that a theory T has the full λ-existence property, or just has full
λ-existence, if every complete set A with a λ-saturated P-part has the
λ-existence property.
