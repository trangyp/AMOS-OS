Observation of Switchable Chiral Magnons in an Altermagnet

Chiral magnons, the quanta of handed spin waves, transport spin angular momentum without
energy loss due to Joule heating.
The recently discovered altermagnets were proposed to host
chiral magnons arising from a non-relativistic exchange mechanism, similar to that in ferromagnets
but without net magnetization, oﬀering a stray-ﬁeld-free platform for eﬃcient magnon spin-current
manipulation. In this work, we directly observed chiral magnons in the altermagnetic prototype
MnTe using polarized inelastic neutron scattering. Furthermore, the magnon chirality was found
to be reversibly switched by magnetic-ﬁeld control, establishing a robust foundation for functional
altermagnetic magnonics.
Eﬀorts to exploit the spin degree of freedom in elec-
tronics have stimulated the utilization of magnons, the
collective excitations of ordered electron spins [1]. Be-
cause of their spin angular momentum and immunity
to Coulomb scattering, magnons serve as a promis-
ing medium for long-distance information transport and
readout [2–5].
Meanwhile, the spin torque delivered
by the magnons is powerful enough to switch mag-
netic domains for writing operations [6, 7].
The co-
herence of magnons further enables nearly dissipation-
less ballistic transport and interference-based process-
ing [7–10]. In addition, nontrivial topology in quantum
magnets [11–13] protects magnonic transport in chiral
edge modes [14, 15], leading to exotic phenomena such
as the magnon thermal Hall eﬀect [16] and the magnon
spin Nernst eﬀect [17, 18], thereby opening up broader
prospects for magnonics.
The net magnetization in ferromagnets (or ferrimag-
nets) breaks time-reversal symmetry (TRS), giving rise
to chiral magnons [19, 20] capable of carrying magnon
spin-current [1, 21], a crucial enabler of magnonics. How-
ever, the presence of net magnetization fundamentally
limits device compatibility and miniaturization. In con-
trast, in collinear antiferromagnets, the net magnetiza-
tion is eliminated and TRS is preserved in the non-
relativistic limit, resulting in degenerate, mutually com-
pensating chiral magnons [22].
Moreover, in the pres-
ence of crystal ﬁeld spin anisotropy, these modes be-
come mixed, and magnons generally lose their chiral-
ity.
Consequently, generating magnon spin-currents in
antiferromagnets typically requires either a canted mo-
ment induced by an external magnetic ﬁeld [3–5] or topo-
logical edge states driven by relativistic spin–orbit cou-
pling [17, 18].
Recently, a new fundamental class of magnets, alter-
magnets, has been proposed to elegantly resolve this
long-standing dilemma [23–26]: their distinct spin sym-
metry spontaneously breaks TRS without net magnetiza-
tion, leading to the emergence of intrinsic, nonrelativistic
chiral magnons in the absence of spin-orbit coupling [26–
31]. Owing to their exchange origin, altermagnetic chi-
ral magnons are expected to transport spin currents as
eﬃciently as ferromagnetic magnons [32, 33], oﬀering
promising functionalities for magnonics.
Following its
theoretical prediction, altermagnetism has been rapidly
conﬁrmed experimentally.
Photoemission spectroscopy
revealed spin-split electronic bands [34–39], and inelastic
neutron scattering (INS) detected magnon band split-
ting [27, 28]. The circular dichroism signal of magnon ex-
citations measured by resonant inelastic X-ray scattering
has been reported to primarily probe static magnetic do-
mains, rather than dynamical chiral magnons [40]. Thus,
the most decisive hallmark of altermagnetism—the chiral
magnons, i.e., their handed spin character—has remained
experimentally unveriﬁed.
Here we report the direct observation of chiral magnons
in an altermagnet by polarized INS (PINS). We study
the prototypical altermagnet MnTe, which hosts two
opposite-spin sublattices, MnA and MnB, related by a
sixfold improper rotation, as shown by the left panel of
Fig. 1(a). The magnetic easy axis lies along the crys-
tallographic a∗(or b∗) axis.
Altermagnetic exchange
lifts the degeneracy of the magnon modes Ω1 and Ω2
with opposite handedness [27, 29], as shown by the right
panel of Fig. 1(a).
Because TRS is broken, two an-
tiphase magnetic domains with antiparallel Néel vectors
(n = SA −SB, where SA and SB are the spin moments
of MnA and MnB) are present. The chirality of Ω1 and
Ω2 reverses under n →−n and is therefore opposite in
the two antiphase domains, as shown in the Figs. 1(a)
and 1(b). In addition, the C3 lattice symmetry yields
three 120◦magnetic orientational domains.

We have shown that MnTe, a magnet with zero net
magnetization and collinear order, has magnetic excita-
tions with opposite chirality, a crucial feature of alter-
magnets. Moreover, the chirality of both magnon modes
is controlled by ﬁeld cooling in a few mT ﬁeld — revers-
ing the sign of the ﬁeld reverses the chiralities. MnTe
possesses a bulk g-wave spin symmetry [23], which pro-
hibits magnon spin-current generation [58]. Nevertheless,
its spin symmetry can be tuned by strain into a p-wave
form [59], thereby enabling MnTe to serve as a viable
platform for magnonics. The search for chiral magnons
with d-wave symmetry in altermagnets [38, 39, 60] oﬀers
another promising direction for applications. Our results
establish the ﬁrst steps toward controlling altermagnetic
chiral magnons, which not only advance the emergent
ﬁeld of altermagnetism but also lay the groundwork for
its future functional exploitation.
