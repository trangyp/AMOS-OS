Abstract
We prove optimal sampling bounds achieving (1 ± ε)-relative error for a broad class of
Lipschitz continuous classification loss functions under various regularization terms.
This
includes important functions such as logistic and sigmoid loss, hinge loss, and ReLU loss, as
prominent and popular representative examples. In particular, we prove k2/ε2 upper and lower
bounds for ∥· ∥2/k regularization, and k/ε2 upper and lower bounds for ∥· ∥1/k regularization.
For ∥· ∥2
2/k regularization, the sampling complexity depends mainly on a bounded derivative
property: if |g′(x)| ≤g(x), and g(0) > 0, and g is monotonic or convex, then it admits linear in
k sampling complexity; otherwise the general bound is k2/ε2. However, if g(0) = 0, our results
indicate that no dimension-free bounds are possible, and even sublinear bounds are ruled out.
All upper bounds are complemented by matching lower bounds up to polylogarithmic terms.
Moreover, our work relies conceptually and algorithmically on simple uniform or (squared) norm
sampling and hereby improves over recent cubic k3/ε2 sensitivity sampling bounds of (Alishahi
and Phillips, ICML’24). This is achieved by refined arguments involving higher moment bounds
and empirical process analyses to avoid overcounting that appears in the de-facto standard
VC-dimension and sensitivity framework.

Classic related work.
Classically, for 0-1 loss (which counts the number of misclassifications),
let ξ be the Bayes’ optimal rate for linear classifiers; which is, for the best choice of linear classifier,
the probability a new data point will be misclassified by it. In this setting, the core bounds were

above ξ; on δ, the probability of exceeding that ξ + ε rate; and on the VC-dimension, which for

Contemporary related work.
More recently, through the perspective of coresets, this problem
was revisited by Munteanu et al. [2018]; and they achieved a relative (1 ± ε)-approximation bound.
It also removed the need to bound the radius of data D and scale of parameters with R, but instead
introduced a data dependent parameter µ(A). This µ(A) roughly captures how separable the data is.
They introduced a sampling bound, using VC-dimension arguments, of m = ˜O(µ(A)3d3/ε4), which
was improved to m = ˜O(µ(A)2d/ε2) by Mai et al. [2021], and m = ˜O(µ(A)d3/ε2) by Munteanu et al.
[2022], and finally to linear m = ˜O(µ(A)d/ε2) in [Munteanu and Omlor, 2024a]. Note that these
coreset approaches do not sample uniformly from the unknown distribution, but rather assume
there is already a large enough representative sample, that is further reduced to the target size.
Moreover, they also do not subsample uniformly from that larger sample. Instead they sample using
importance sampling via square-root leverage scores, ℓ1 Lewis weights, or a mixture of ℓ1 and ℓ2
leverage scores, and re-weight the sampled points for their use in the approximation. Also, this
reintroduces the dependence on the dimension d, which while natural from VC-dimension theory,
may be seen as unusual in the context of modern billion parameter models [Shoeybi et al., 2019,
Brown et al., 2020, Narayanan et al., 2021], or whole genome data analysis [Teschke et al., 2024].
Additional work from the coreset perspective, Samadian et al. [2020] and Tolochinsky et al.
[2022] both consider a variant that adds a regularization term R(x)/k for R(x) ∈{∥x∥2
2, ∥x∥2, ∥x∥1}.
As a result, no bound is required on the norm of x. Instead, it is constrained implicitly as part of
the loss function. While Tolochinsky et al. [2022] drew a sample proportional to the leverage scores,
Samadian et al. [2020] only need a uniform sample; using a VC-dimension-based argument they
require O(dk log k/ε2) samples (assuming D = 1). After this, Alishahi and Phillips [2024] revived
the Rademacher complexity approach, and this led to dimension-free bounds of m = O(k3/ε2) (for
D = 1); so the dependence on k has increased, but there is no more dependence on the dimension d.
In this paper we resolve the sample complexity of regularized linear classifiers (up
to polylog(k) factors), i.e. the number of samples that are required to achieve the guarantee
given in Equation (1). To this end, we leverage the weighted sampling scheme of coresets. Our
weights are simple, corresponding to a balanced mixture of uniform and norm-sampling, essentially
proportional to 1 + ∥a∥2, when
R
∥a∥2 dP(a) = B < ∞. The intuition is that points of high norm
tend to dominate the approximation error. The addition of norm-sampling allows us to control their
contribution. Further if in addition ∥a∥2 ≤D then uniform i.i.d. sampling from P provides the
same asymptotic upper bounds when the average norm B is replaced by the maximum norm D in
the sample complexity.
Our paper will demonstrate the sample complexity story is more intricate than has been revealed
up to this point. The tight answer depends on a combination of both the loss function and the
regularization term. Our first results apply widely to loss functions g that are only required to be
L-Lipschitz and obtain k2/ε2 upper bounds. We then improve to linear bounds of k/ε2 in settings
that do not have quadratic lower bounds. For a few combinations of loss and regularizer, we need to
restrict further to loss functions with bounded derivative g′ (specifically |g′(r)| < g(r) for all r ∈R),
in cases where this is necessary to improve quadratic upper bounds to k/ε2. As applications of
these bounds and for proving lower bounds, we choose more specific examples of highly popular loss

Further related work.
Our work combines Rademacher complexity Alishahi and Phillips [2024]
with empirical processes, by deriving higher moment bounds on a Rademacher process Cohen and
Peng [2015], Mai et al. [2021] and using the classic higher moment method Alon and Spencer [2008],
Clarkson and Woodruff [2009], Woodruff [2014]. Further, our work continues a recent series of works
that achieve optimal linear complexities (whenever possible) through empirical process analyses of
sensitivity sampling for ℓp-subspace embeddings [Woodruff and Yasuda, 2023, Munteanu and Omlor,
2024a], k-means/median clustering [Bansal et al., 2024], and sparsification of sums of semi-norms,
and generalizations of linear models [Jambulapati et al., 2023, 2024].

PAC learning with relative error.

Finite data or coresets.
In the coreset setting, [Phillips, 2017, Munteanu and Schwiegelshohn,
2018], we are given a finite amount of data, and we can simply calculate S (or estimate it using
standard streaming algorithms Woodruff [2014]); then our theorems apply directly without additional
measures. They thus yield optimal coresets for a variety of regularized classification loss functions
improving over previous works in this setting [Samadian et al., 2020, Tolochinsky et al., 2022,
Alishahi and Phillips, 2024].
Distributional setting.
In the distributional setting we only have access to samples from the
unknown distribution P, and we require additionally P(∥a∥2 > D) = 0 for some term D < ∞. As
discussed before, this is not needed for sampling from Q, but for setting the weights, it is required
to estimate S. Since the final estimate of S will be a (1 ± ε)-approximation, we actually feed
2s(a) to the sampler. The total sample size suffers an additive O(D log(1/δ)/ε2) compared to our
main theorems Theorems 3.1 to 3.3, see Lemmas A.2 and A.3. We note that S = B + 1. Then if
D = O(B), this is a lower order term in sample size.
Kernels and bounded spaces.
Most data distributions naturally have some bound B, or D
on the (average) norm of data points. If for no other reason, then values are standardized or
normalized before learning. Layer-normalization Ba et al. [2016] is, for instance, a very common
element of neural networks which leads to B = D = 1. This is also made explicit in kernel methods
Sch¨olkopf and Smola [2001], Hofmann et al. [2008] where a kernel support-vector-machine (SVM)
is mathematically equivalent to linear classification operating in a (perhaps infinite-dimensional)
kernel Hilbert space HK. Similarly, support vector data descriptor (SVDD) [Tax and Duin, 2004] is
equivalent to the kernel smallest enclosing ball problem [Krivosija and Munteanu, 2019]. For a wide
variety of common kernels K(a, x) (e.g., normalized angular K(a, x) = ⟨a, x⟩/(∥a∥∥x∥); Gaussian
K(a, x) = exp(−∥a−x∥2/σ2); Laplace K(a, x) = exp(−∥a−x∥/σ)), then again ∥a∥HK = K(a, a) =
1, and hence we have B = D = 1. When reproducing kernels are used, then this space HK has no
bound on the dimension, and a dimension-free bound (such as ours) is necessary for a finite sample
complexity bound. In all of these cases, our analysis directly applies with uniform random sampling.

Both the above improvements are achieved via refined empirical process analyses. The aforemen-

canonical Gaussian process. The Gaussian process induces a pseudo-metric, which can be bounded
in terms of the metric entropy E and the metric diameter D by Sudakov’s tail bound, also known
as Dudley’s bound [Dudley, 2016, Ledoux and Talagrand, 1991]. Recently, Woodruff and Yasuda
[2023] transformed this classic result into a higher moment bound involving the ratio of the two
quantities. The remaining challenge is to bound E, D appropriately and use the higher moment
method again to obtain the final approximation via Markov’s inequality.

A simple Ω(d log d) bound shows no dimension-free sampling is possible
for the combination of ReLU with R(·) = ∥·∥2

small enough, the regularizer has no effect, so |fS(x)−f(x)| = |f(x)| = η/d > ε(η/d+η2/k) = εf(x).
Thus, all d points need to be in the sample, and Ω(d log d) samples are required to succeed this task
by the coupon collector’s theorem [Erd˝os and R´enyi, 1961].
Our Ω(n log n) lower bound in Theorem 3.7 refines the above idea of a scaling mismatch near
the origin and highlights a limitation of the sampling model to provide a uniform relative-error
guarantee for sublinear sample size. The proof builds a geometric hard instance by placing n points
ai ∈Rd, i ∈[n] along the moment curve, so their convex hull is a cyclic polytope. As a result, each
point can be separated from all others by a supporting hyperplane. Equivalently, for every point
aj there exists a query direction x(j) such that aj has negative dot product while all other points
have non-negative dot products. For ReLU, this makes the loss at x(j) depend essentially on the
contribution of that single point. Ridge regularization does not prevent this isolation, because we can
scale the query by a tiny factor η > 0. The ReLU term scales as Θ(η), while the regularization scales
as Θ(η2). In particular, for the isolating direction x(j) we can choose η →0 so that η2/g(ηcj) →0,
and the regularization becomes negligible compared to the (already tiny) ReLU loss. Writing Nj for
the number of times aj appears in the m i.i.d. samples, in the limit η →0, the required relative-error
guarantee at xj essentially reduces to |Nj −ENj| ≤ε ENj. Because scores are normalized, many
points receive only O(1/n) sampling probability in each draw. Therefore, ensuring relative error
requires a coupon collector number of samples m = Ω(ε−2n log n).
