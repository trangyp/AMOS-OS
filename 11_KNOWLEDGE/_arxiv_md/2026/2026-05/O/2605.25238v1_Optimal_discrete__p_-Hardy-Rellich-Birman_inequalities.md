Abstract. We present a theory for constructing optimal lower bounds for the discrete
half-line p-Laplacian of higher order ℓ∈N and general p > 1. The abstract framework
introduces higher-order monotonicity and asymptotic constraints on a parameter sequence
that determines optimal weights. As a concrete application, we specialize the parameter
sequence to deduce new optimal discrete p-Hardy (ℓ= 1), p-Rellich (ℓ= 2), and p-Birman
(ℓ≥3) inequalities.

2.4. Related literature. The literature on Hardy inequalities is extraordinarily extensive,
making it impossible to cover all related results. Therefore, below, we explicitly mention
only closely related results and contributions specifically addressing discrete inequalities of
Hardy type.
Work of F. Fischer: In his doctoral dissertation [12] (subsequently published in [11, 13,
14]), Fischer developed a comprehensive framework for a general theory of optimal discrete
p-Hardy inequalities on graphs, extending previous works [31, 32] to the non-linear case
of general p > 1. Although our scope differs—as we restrict our attention to the simple
case of a discrete half-line graph but investigate higher-order Hardy-type inequalities
of Rellich and Birman type with an emphasis on concrete weights—our works intersect in
several aspects.
First, our definition of optimality is inspired by [12] and related previous works [8, 31],
although we adopt a stronger notion by replacing null-criticality with non-attainability (as
detailed in Remark 2.3). Second, we build upon the non-trivial fundamental inequalities
from [11, Lemma 3.8], extending them to the complex domain in Lemma 3.1. As our
motivation is disconnected from probability theory, complex sequence spaces are assumed
in accordance with standard functional analysis conventions. In this respect, the Hardy case
(ℓ= 1 and p > 1) of Theorem 5.1 extends the main result of [15] to complex sequences
and stricter optimality. Finally, a core idea of the supersolution construction technique

applied in [12] (and previously used also in [9, 31, 45]) is implicitly present in our proof of
Theorem 2.8.
Work of the authors for p = 2: This paper continues the investigation initiated in [48] for
the case p = 2. While the discrete Laplacian (2.3) is linear for p = 2 and inequality (2.4) can
be understood via quadratic forms on a Hilbert space, the current work for general p > 1
represents a transition to a non-linear setting. We highlight here the main differences and
challenges of this transition.
First, the crucial upper and lower bounds from (2.8) hold as identities when p = 2,
where, in addition, the error terms can be interpreted as non-negative linear operators;
see [46, Thm. 1]. This identity provides a more direct control of the key central term and
allows for an extension lemma (see [48, Lemma 10]) that is unavailable in the non-linear
case p > 1. Otherwise, the proofs of Theorems 2.6–2.8 remain methodologically similar to
the linear case.
Another essential innovation of the present work is the explicit choice of the parameter
sequence (2.10) that meets all the assumptions of Theorem 2.8, which was discovered by an
experienced guess. This choice would have been scarcely possible without insights from the
linear case, even though the p = 2 version of (2.10) does not appear in [48]. Furthermore,
the proof of Theorem 2.11 relies on the properties of (logarithmically) completely monotone
sequences and is incomparable with its much simpler counterpart for p = 2 or the similar
result from [48, Thm. 5]. The new connection between complete monotonicity and optimal
weights of Hardy type (whose even stronger form was crucial in our work [46]) is not yet
fully understood and might represent a promising avenue for future research.
Other relevant works: Diverse definitions exist for powers of the discrete Laplacian,
even within the linear setting p = 2 (see [48, Sec. 1.4]). The definition used here preserves
the Toeplitz structure of the discrete Laplacian on N, which makes it a close analogy
to its continuous counterpart. Alternative definitions for the fractional Laplacian (−∆)α
on N, with possibly non-integral α > 0, have been explored and Hardy-type inequalities
established in [16]. Their optimal improvements, for 0 < α < 1, were found recently in [6].
On the full line Z, optimal discrete fractional Hardy inequalities were found in [3], with
their optimality later verified in [29].
Other works devoted to discrete Hardy and related inequalities include [4, 5, 21] in
the one-dimensional setting, and [19, 20, 22, 27, 28] in higher dimensions. To our best
knowledge, all these works assume the linear framework (p = 2).
