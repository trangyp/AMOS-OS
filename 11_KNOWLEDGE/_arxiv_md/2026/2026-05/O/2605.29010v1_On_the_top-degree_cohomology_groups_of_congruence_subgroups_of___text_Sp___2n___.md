Abstract
Let Γω

Borel–Serre demonstrated that the cohomology of Γω

prove that Hn2(Γω

We
conclude that Hn2(Γω

Throughout this article R is a principal ideal domain. We work exclusively with finitely
generated (f.g.) free R-modules. All symplectic R-modules are free. We always equip R2n

Lemma 2.1. If V and W are summands of Rn and V ⊆W, then V is a summand of W.
Lemma 2.2. If V ⊆Rn is a summand and B = {⃗v1, . . . ,⃗vm} is a basis for V , then B can
be extended to a basis for Rn.
A partial basis is a subset of a basis for Rn. A partial basis {⃗v1, . . . ,⃗vm} for R2n is isotropic

symplectic basis for R2n of size 2n−1. The symplectic complement of a summand V ⊆R2n

V ⊥:= {⃗u ∈R2n | ω(⃗v, ⃗u) = 0 for all ⃗v ∈V }.

2.2
Posets and simplicial complexes
We use the topological properties of several posets and the simplicial complexes associated
to these posets to study the symplectic Steinberg module. Let A be a poset. If a0 < · · · <
ak = a is a maximal chain ending with a ∈A, then the height of a is k. We work exclusively
with posets whose elements have finite height. Let ht: A →Z denote the height function
that takes an element a to its height. The dimension of A is the maximum height attained
by any a ∈A. For a ∈A, we call the subposet A<a := {b ∈A | b < a} the lower link of a,
we call the subposet A>a := {b ∈A | b > a} the upper link of a, and if a < a′ ∈A then the
subposet A>a ∩A<a′ := {b ∈A | a < b < a′} is the interval from a to a′.
The simplicial complex associated to A, denoted S(A), is the simplicial complex whose
vertices correspond to elements a ∈A and whose k-simplices correspond to chains of (k+1)-
many elements: a0 < · · · < ak. If σ ∈S(A) is a k-simplex that corresponds to a0 < · · · < ak,
then the link of σ, denoted LinkS(A)(σ), is the full sub-complex of S(A) whose vertices
correspond to b ∈A such that b < a0, b > ak or ai < b < ai+1 for i ∈{0, . . . , k −1}.
And whose k-simplices are spanned by (k + 1)-many of these vertices. If F : A →B is a
non-decreasing poset map, then the simplicial map associated to F, S(F): S(A) →S(B),
is a simplicial map that sends a k-simplex associated to a chain a0 < · · · < ak to k-simplex
associated to the chain F(a0) < · · · < F(ak).
The geometric realization of A, denoted |A|, is isomorphic to the geometric realization of
S(A). For simplicity, when we say that a poset A or the simplicial complex associated
to A has a topological property, we mean that |A| has that property. For example, we
write H∗(A) when we mean H∗(|A|). We make the convention that all posets and simplicial
complexes are at least (−2)-connected and non-empty posets and simplicial complexes are
always at least (−1)-connected.
Definition 2.12. A poset A is Cohen–Macaulay (abbreviated CM) of dimension n if
• A is n-dimensional and (n −1)-connected;
• if for every a ∈A the lower link of a is (ht(a) −1)-dimensional and (ht(a) −2)-
connected, and the upper link of a is (n −ht(a) −1)-dimensional and (n −ht(a) −2)-
connected;
• and if for every pair a < a′ in A, the interval from a to a′ is (ht(a′) −ht(a) −2)-
dimensional and (ht(a′) −ht(a) −3)-connected.
