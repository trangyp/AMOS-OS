ON MULTIPLICITY OF UNIFORM NORMS AND MAXIMAL
SPECTRAL SUBSTRUCTURES IN COMMUTATIVE BANACH
ALGEBRAS
JEKWIN J. DABHI AND PRAKASH A. DABHI
Abstract. Let A be a semisimple commutative Banach algebra. It is shown
that either A has exactly one uniform norm or it admits uncountably many
uniform norms. Further, it is shown that there always exists a largest closed
subalgebra of A which is weakly regular, and that there always exist largest
closed ideals in A having unique uniform norm property (UUNP) and spectral
extension property (SEP) respectively.
1. Introduction
The algebraic and topological structures of commutative Banach algebras have
been profoundly linked since the inception of Gel’fand theory. A cornerstone result
in this domain is B. E. Johnson’s Uniqueness of Norm Theorem [10], which estab-
lishes that any semisimple commutative Banach algebra admits a unique complete
algebra norm topology. Given the uniqueness of complete norms, significant interest
naturally shifted toward the study of incomplete submultiplicative norms [14, 15, 9].
This investigation led to the introduction of the spectral extension property (SEP)
by Meyer [13], which characterizes when the spectral radius remains invariant under
algebra extensions.
The question of when a topological algebra admits a unique uniform norm was
first investigated by Bhatt and Karia [5]. Motivated by this foundational work and
the behavior of the SEP, Bhatt and Dedania [2] formally introduced the unique uni-
form norm property (UUNP) for Banach algebras. A commutative Banach algebra
A has the UUNP if it admits exactly one uniform norm, which must necessarily
be the spectral radius. The study of the UUNP revealed a natural hierarchy of
spectral properties: regularity implies weak regularity, which in turn implies the
SEP, which finally implies the UUNP [2, 3, 13]. These properties were subsequently
studied on various specific algebras by authors in [7, 8].
If a semisimple commutative Banach algebra fails to possess the UUNP, it admits
multiple uniform norms. A compelling question arises: how many distinct uniform
norms can such an algebra admit?

Parallel to the multiplicity of uniform norms, we investigate the existence of
maximal algebraic substructures possessing these hierarchical spectral properties.
In 1992, Inoue and Takahasi [11] proved that if a Banach algebra is not regular, it
always contains a largest closed regular subalgebra. Motivated by this phenomenon
of maximal structures, we investigate whether analogous largest subalgebras and
ideals exist for weaker properties.
We establish that if a Banach algebra lacks
weak regularity, UUNP, or SEP, it nevertheless always contains a largest closed
subalgebra (or ideal) possessing these respective properties.
Throughout the paper, A is assumed to be a commutative Banach algebra. A
nonzero multiplicative linear functional on A is a complex homomorphism on A. Let
ΦA be the collection of all complex homomorphisms on A. The set ΦA is equipped
with the weakest topology such that for each x ∈A, the mapping bx : ΦA →C
defined by bx(φ) = φ(x) is continuous.
The set ΦA with this Gel’fand topology
is the Gel’fand space of A, which is a locally compact Hausdorff space. If A is
unital, then ΦA is compact. A commutative Banach algebra A is semisimple if
∩φ∈ΦA ker φ = {0}, where ker φ = {x ∈A : φ(x) = 0} is the kernel of φ. Let I be a
closed ideal in A. Then the Gel’fand space of I is identified with ΦA \ h(I), where
h(I) = {φ ∈ΦA : I ⊂ker(φ)} is the hull of I.
Let X be a nonempty set. A subset R ⊂X is a boundary for a collection F
of bounded functions on X if for every f ∈F there exists a yf ∈R such that
∥f∥∞= |f(yf)| [12, Def. 3.3.1]. Let X be a locally compact Hausdorff space and

2. multiplicity of uniform norms
Proposition 2.1. Let A be a commutative Banach algebra. Then either A has
exactly one uniform norm or it has uncountably many uniform norms.
Proof. By [6, Theorem 2.1(i)], any two distinct uniform norms on the unitization
Ae of A corresponds to distinct uniform norms when restricted to A. So, we may
assume that A is unital.
Assume that A admits at least two distinct uniform norms. Then A will have
infinitely many uniform norms. So, we may choose uniform norms p1(·) and p2(·)
such that p1(·) ̸= r(·) and p2(·) ̸= r(·).

3. Largest subalgebras/ideals having spectral properties
Let A be a semisimple commutative Banach algebra.
A norm | · | on A is
a semisimple norm if the completion of A in the norm | · | is semisimple [15].
Let sN be the collection of all semisimple norms on A. By [13], the semisimple
permanent radius of A is defined as rs(x) = inf{|x| : | · | ∈sN} and the permanent
radius is defined as rp(x) = inf{rAp(x) : p ∈N}, where N is the collection of
all submultiplicative norms on A and rAp(x) is the spectral radius of x in the
completion of A with respect to p ∈N [15].
A Banach algebra A has a (P)-
property [13, 14] if for a given nonzero closed ideal I in A, there exists a nonzero
x ∈I such that rp(x) > 0.
We use the following results.
Theorem 3.1. [2, Theorem 2.3] Let A be a semisimple commutative Banach alge-
bra. Then the following statements are equivalent.
(1) A has UUNP.
(2) ∂A is the smallest closed set of uniqueness.
(3) If F ⊂ΦA is closed and not containing ∂A, then there exists a ∈A such
that r(a) > 0 and ba|F = 0.
(4) If F ⊂ΦA is closed and does not contain ∂A, then there exists a ∈A such
that rs(a) > 0 and ba|F = 0.
(5) r = rs on A.
Theorem 3.2. [2, Proposition 2.4] Let A be a semisimple commutative Banach
algebra. Then the following statements are equivalent.
(1) A has SEP.
(2) A has UUNP and rs = rp, i.e., A has UUNP and every norm dominates a
semisimple norm.
(3) A has UUNP and (P)- property.
Now, we move towards our results.
