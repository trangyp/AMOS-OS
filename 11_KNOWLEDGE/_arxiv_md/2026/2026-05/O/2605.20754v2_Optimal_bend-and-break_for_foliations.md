OPTIMAL BOUNDS IN BEND-AND-BREAK FOR FOLIATIONS
JIHAO LIU, ZEMING SUN, JIEDONG JIANG
Abstract. We show that for every foliation F of rank r on a normal projective variety, the
optimal constant in the bend-and-break inequality for tangent rational curves is r + 1. The
proof combines the method of Bogomolov–McQuillan and the bend-and-shatter method de-
veloped by Jovinelly–Lehmann–Riedl. The proof of the main result of this paper substantially
uses generative AI, particularly the Rethlas system.

1. Introduction
We work over the field of complex numbers C, unless otherwise specified. The proof of our
main theorem nevertheless passes through positive characteristic via Frobenius pullback and
spreading-out arguments; for the relevant intermediate results (in Sections 5 and 6) we are
explicit about the working field whenever this matters.
1.1. Bend-and-break bounds. Mori’s bend-and-break method [Mor82] is one of the cor-
nerstones of modern birational geometry: given a smooth projective variety X of dimension
n and a curve C ⊂X of KX-negative degree, it produces a rational curve through any chosen
point of C of explicitly bounded degree. In its sharpest form, for every nef divisor H on X,
through every point of C there exists a rational curve Σ ⊂X with
H · Σ ≤(n + 1)
H · C
−KX · C ,
and the constant n + 1 is optimal. The weaker bound with constant 2n is due to [MM86,
Theorem 5] (see also [Kol96, Theorem II.5.8] for the extension to nef R-Cartier H). The
optimal constant n + 1 was obtained for ample H by [Mor82] (see [KM98, Theorem 1.13]
for a textbook treatment). Combined with the cone theorem, this bend-and-break inequality
bounds the length of KX-negative extremal rays by n + 1, and underlies the construction
of contraction morphisms, Fano fibrations, and the inductive proofs of the minimal model
program; see [Kol96, KM98] for textbook treatments.

For singular varieties, the optimal bend-and-break constant remained out of reach for a
long time. Kawamata [Kaw91] obtained the analogous length bound for klt pairs with the
weaker constant 2n, and a sequence of subsequent works extended and refined this bound for
log canonical pairs and more general singular settings; see [Fuj11, Fuj17] and the references
therein. The optimal version was settled very recently, in the strongest possible form, by
Jovinelly–Lehmann–Riedl [JLR25b]: for every log canonical pair (X, ∆) of dimension n and
every sufficiently general (KX +∆)-negative complete-intersection curve C, through a general
point of C there exists a rational curve Σ with
H · Σ ≤(n + 1)
H · C
−(KX + ∆) · C .
Their breakthrough also supplies the central technical input, a relative bend-and-shatter
lemma, used in the present paper. The bound of [JLR25b] has already led to several im-
portant applications in birational geometry, such as the construction of free curves in the
smooth locus of terminal Fano threefolds [JLR25a] and the characterization of Pn [FJLR26].
1.2. Bend and break for foliations. For foliations, the analogue of bend-and-break reads
as follows. Let F ⊂TX be a foliation of rank r on a normal projective variety X with canonical
divisor KF. The natural counterpart of Mori’s question asks for the optimal constant in the
bend-and-break inequality for rational curves tangent to F. The history of this question starts
with Miyaoka [Miy87], who introduced the technique of deforming a curve along a foliation in
characteristic p; this was refined by Shepherd-Barron [SB92] into a foliated bend-and-break
inequality with the constant 2n, and improved by Bogomolov–McQuillan [BM16] to the better
constant 2r. Until now, the optimal bound r + 1 has been out of reach in full generality.
The main result of this paper resolves the optimal bend-and-break question for arbitrary
foliations.
Theorem 1.1 (Main theorem). Let X be a normal projective variety of dimension n, and let
F be a foliation on X of rank r. Let H1, . . . , Hn−1, H be ample divisors on X, and let C be
a general intersection of elements Di ∈|miHi| for 1 ≤i ≤n −1, with mi ≫0. Suppose that
KF · C < 0.
Then through a general point of C there exists a rational curve Σ tangent to F with
H · Σ ≤(r + 1)
H · C
−KF · C .
Theorem 1.1 is the optimal bend-and-break inequality for foliations.
It sharpens the
Bogomolov–McQuillan constant 2r of [BM16] to the optimal r + 1, and the two extreme
cases recover known results: the case r = n (F = TX) corresponds to the bend-and-break
bound for varieties [JLR25b] and the case r = 1 recovers the Bogomolov–McQuillan bound
[BM16] (as 2r = r + 1 = 2). Whenever a cone theorem for F becomes available, Theorem 1.1
translates into the optimal length bound r + 1 for KF-negative extremal rays. However, un-
like in the smooth and log canonical settings, the cone theorem for foliations (the framework
that converts bend-and-break into a length bound for extremal rays) has been proved only
in special cases (see for instance [ACSS21, CHLX23, Spi20, CS21, CS25a, CS25b]), and is
not available in the generality treated here. We therefore state our main result as a bend-
and-break inequality, and the corresponding length statement for KF-negative extremal rays
follows in any setting in which a foliated cone theorem is available.

1.3. Strategy of the proof. The proof combines three ingredients.
(i) The optimal bend-and-shatter lemma [JLR25b, Lemma 2.1]: a k-dimensional family of
stable maps from a fixed marked source, with the first k marked images held fixed, must
degenerate to a stable-map limit that contains k non-contracted rational components
through the marked points. This improves Mori’s classical bend-and-break (in the form
of [Kol96, Theorem II.5.8]) by passing to the k-pointed Kontsevich stable-map stack.
(ii) The graph-neighborhood relative model [BM16, KST07]: given a rank s foliation G ⊂
TX regular along a smooth complete curve C ⊂Xreg with G|C ample, the BM/KST
construction algebraizes the graph-neighborhood of the G-leaves along C and produces,
after passing to a finite cover ν : B →C of positive genus, a smooth projective relative
model
ρ: W →B,
ϕ: W →X,
σ: B →W,
where ρ is flat of relative dimension s and smooth near a section σ(B), the relative
tangent bundle along the section is the pulled-back foliation, and the general fiber com-
ponents near σ(B) are the (rationally connected, s-dimensional) leaves of G. In particular
foliation tangency in X corresponds to ρ-verticality in W.
(iii) The Campana–P˘aun algebraicity criterion [CP19]: combining the MR restriction theo-
rem with the Campana–P˘aun slope-positive algebraicity theorem, this produces, from
a KF-negative sufficiently general complete-intersection curve C on X, a positive-rank
algebraically integrable subfoliation G ⊂F of rank s ≤r such that G|C is ample and
−KG · C ≥−KF · C.
The novelty of this paper is the observation that these three ingredients compose into
the optimal bound. The bend-and-shatter lemma in (i) is absolute, while the model in (ii)
is relative over the base curve B; the bridge between them is a relative form of bend-and-
shatter (Lemma 6.1), applied to the relative Hom scheme HomB(C, W; h) of B-morphisms
with prescribed composition to B.
A Frobenius pullback amplifies the dimension of this
relative Hom scheme so that the relative bend-and-shatter lemma applies (Lemma 6.2), and a
closed-locus form of the spreading-out argument (Lemma 7.1) preserves verticality during the
passage from positive characteristic back to characteristic zero. Pushing forward by ϕ converts
the resulting ρ-vertical rational curves into G-tangent rational curves through a general point
of C; the rank slack s ≤r and the degree slack −KG · C ≥−KF · C from (iii) then upgrade
the bound (s + 1) H · C/(−KG · C) for G-tangent curves into the optimal bound (r + 1) H ·
C/(−KF · C) for F-tangent curves.

1.5. Use of generative AI. The question in this paper, the optimal bend-and-break bound
for foliations, was discussed by the first author, Paolo Cascini, and Calum Spicer when the
first author was in London in January 2026. The reason is simple: with our understanding
of [BM16] and since [JLR25b] is very short and easy to read, it seems that “something could
work by just putting them together”. However, [BM16, JLR25b] are written in completely
different styles (particularly considering that the first version of [BM16] was posted in 2001).
Putting them together requires a significant amount of work and energy.
In March 2026, the first author of the paper discussed with Florin Ambro, Federico Bon-
giorno, and Paolo Cascini what birational geometry questions generative AI can address. The
optimal bend-and-break question was raised again at that time. Later, the Rethlas system was
introduced to the first author. The prompt was simple: can you combine the methodologies
of Bogomolov–McQuillan and Jovinelly–Lehmann–Riedl to obtain an optimal bend-and-break
bound? After running the system for 12 hours, Rethlas obtained a complete proof.
Like other AI-generated papers, the writing of the AI is terrible. However, it is very easy to
check that the key bridge that connects the key ideas of [BM16] and [JLR25b], Lemma 6.1, is
correct (and we are very grateful to Eric Riedl, who was visiting Peking University in April,
for useful related comments). With this, we are confident in the correctness of the paper
despite the awkward writing at that time. In particular, as Eric Riedl and Calum Spicer
pointed out to us, the proof of Section 3 was overcomplicated, as Rethlas did not find [CP19],
and was later rewritten and simplified. The verification of all details of the paper was done
by all three authors.
See [Ju+26] for a detailed introduction to the Rethlas system. Due to the limitation of
generative AI, it is possible that we have missed some related references in the literature, and
we welcome any comments from experts.

2.2. Foliations.
Definition 2.1. A foliation F on a normal variety X is a saturated coherent subsheaf F ⊂TX
of the tangent sheaf that is closed under the Lie bracket. The rank of F is its generic rank
r = rank F, and the canonical class of F is the Weil divisor class
KF = −c1(F) = −det(F),
defined on the locally free locus and extended to all of X by reflexive extension.
In particular, for a complete curve C lying in the smooth locus of X and in the locally free
locus of F, one has
deg(F|C) = det(F) · C = −KF · C.
2.3. Mehta–Ramanathan restriction. We use the MR restriction theorem in the form
stated by KST [KST07, Notation 4]: for a torsion-free coherent sheaf E on a normal projective
variety X and ample classes H1, . . . , Hn−1 on X, a general complete-intersection curve C =
D1∩· · ·∩Dn−1 cut out by sufficiently large multiples Di ∈|miHi| is smooth, lies in the locally
free locus of E, and the HN filtration of E restricts to the HN filtration of E|C. We call such
a curve a sufficiently general MR curve for E.
