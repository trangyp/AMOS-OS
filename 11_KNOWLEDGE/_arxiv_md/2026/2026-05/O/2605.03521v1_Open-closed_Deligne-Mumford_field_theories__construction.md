OPEN-CLOSED DELIGNE–MUMFORD FIELD THEORIES: CONSTRUCTION
AMANDA HIRSCHI AND KAI HUGTENBURG
Abstract. Open-closed Deligne–Mumford field theories are chain-level field theories based on
moduli spaces of stable curves with boundary. We associate to a relatively spin embedded Lagrangian
L ⊂(X, ω) such an open-closed DMFT. It extends the Fukaya A∞algebra to curves of arbitrarily
high genus and with arbitrarily many boundary components and is unique up to homotopy. This is the
first step in proving Kontsevich’s conjecture that the Fukaya category determines the Gromov–Witten
invariants of X, following a strategy delineated by Costello.

1. Introduction
1.1. Context. Cohomological field theories were defined by Kontsevich–Manin, [KM94], in order to
describe the algebraic structure of Gromov–Witten invariants and are inspired by the seminal work
of Witten, [Wit91]. In this paper we define a generalisation of this notion, assigning chain-level
operations to chains on moduli spaces of stable curves with boundary. We call this algebraic
structure an open-closed Deligne–Mumford field theory to indicate that the underlying properad
differs from the properad previously used to define open-closed field theories. Open-closed field

theories were defined by Moore and Segal [Moo01, Seg01] and have been widely applied in as diverse
fields as string topology, mathematical physics, algebraic geometry and symplectic topology.
As with previous constructions of algebraic structures in symplectic topology, we use moduli
spaces of pseudo-holomorphic curves. They were introduced to symplectic topology by Gromov,
[Gro85], and have become the main tool to prove rigidity results. The first invariants based on
counts of pseudo-holomorphic curves are the aforementioned Gromov–Witten invariants as well
as Lagrangian Floer theory, the latter based on genus zero curves satisfying Lagrangian boundary
conditions, [Flo88]. In this case, the geometry of moduli spaces of holomorphic discs gives rise to an
A∞category, the Fukaya category defined in [FOOO09a, Sei08], respectively an A∞algebra when
considering a single Lagrangian. Higher-genus Lagrangian Floer theory has been only studied in
special cases such as in [Liu20, ES24], partially because a good algebraic framework was lacking.
In [Cos07], Costello defines the notion of an open-closed topological conformal field theory (TCFT),
and shows that any smooth proper Calabi–Yau A∞category A gives rise to such a structure, yielding
an action of the modular properad on the Hochschild homology of A. This is also discussed in
[KS09] and [WW16, Wah16]. Several papers, such as [Cos09] or [CT24, CLT21, AT22] show how
to extract invariants from a TCFT, called categorical enumerative invariants, given additionally
a splitting of the associated Hodge filtration. There is also recent work on defining open(-closed)
categorical enumerative invariants from an open-closed TCFT, see [Ulm25, Ulm26]. Crucially,
Costello connects open-closed TCFTs to one of the most important conjectures in symplectic
geometry, the Homological Mirror Symmetry conjecture of Kontsevich, [Kon95], by positing them
as a key tool for obtaining the Gromov–Witten theory of a symplectic manifold from its Fukaya
category. In order to obtain a cohomological field theory from a closed TCFT, one needs to trivialise
the S1 action coming from rotating the interior boundary punctures, as discussed by [Des22] for
properads and [DC14, OV20] in the operadic case.
1.2. Main results. This paper modifies Costello’s definition by replacing the modular properad
of smooth framed surfaces by modular properad of moduli spaces of stable curves with boundary,
carrying marked points. As in [Cos07], we do not phrase our algebraic structures in terms of
an algebra over this modular properad but as a functor from a symmetric monoidal dg category
C∗(Moc) whose objects are pairs of natural numbers and morphisms are chains on said moduli
spaces of curves with appropriate numbers of incoming/outgoing interior and boundary marked
points. We allow for disconnected curves, whence disjoint union gives rise to a symmetric monoidal
structure on f
OC. See §2.1 for the precise definition and §1.4 for a non-technical discussion. We can
now give the main definition of this paper.
Definition 1.1. An open-closed Deligne–Mumford field theory (DMFT) is a symmetric monoidal
A∞functor F: C∗(Moc) →ChΛ to the category of chain complexes.
A symmetric monoidal structure for an A∞functor consists of certain natural transformations,
defined in §2.2, which are inspired by [Amo16]. It is more involved than the definition of symmetric
monoidal dg functors, and our construction relies on the fact that C∗(Moc) is a dg category.
A first important property of open-closed DMFTs is that they define an A∞structure on the
chain complex F(0, 1), cf. Lemma 2.40. This mirrors a similar statement in [Cos07] for open-closed
TCFTS. In contrast to open-closed TCFTs, an open-closed DMFT also directly yields a chain-level
lift of a cohomological field theory restricting to moduli spaces of closed Riemann surfaces, cf. §1.4.
To be more precise, just as the Fukaya algebra of a Lagrangian is usually a curved A∞algebra,
the algebraic structure we naturally obtain from moduli spaces of pseudo-holomorphic curves forces
us to consider the more general notion of a curved open-closed DMFT, which is defined on the
category f

The main result of this paper is the construction of a curved open-closed DMFT based on
moduli spaces of pseudo-holomorphic stable maps with boundary on the Lagrangian. Since we only
require the Lagrangian to be relatively spin, embedded and weakly unobstructed, we use virtual
perturbations throughout. These geometric foundations, in particular coherent systems of global
Kuranishi charts, are built in [HH26].
Theorem A (Theorem 3.1). Suppose (X, ω) is a closed symplectic manifold and L ⊂X an embedded
Lagrangian equipped with a relative spin structure.
a) (X, L) admits a semi-strict operadic curved open-closed Deligne–Mumford field theory FL whose
associated A∞algebra AL is the Fukaya A∞algebra of L.
b) If L is equipped with a weak bounding cochain, then it admits a semi-strict operadic uncurved
open-closed Deligne–Mumford field theory.
In either case, the associated closed DMFT yields the Gromov–Witten theory of X.
Open-closed DMFTs allow for operations coming from curves with any number of outputs and
inputs in return for relaxing the assumption that F is a dg functor. By relying on a specific chain
model, we can achieve strictness of FL for certain subcategories, as indicated by the properties
semi-strict and operadic, which are defined in §2.3. A key feature of the property of being operadic
is that the open-closed DMFT is compatible with forgetting incoming marked points. In particular,
AL is a cyclic A∞algebra. Restricting the open-closed DMFT to stable spheres, we have the
following other immediate consequence.
Corollary 1.2. Any compact symplectic manifold (X, ω) admits a strict action of the operad of
stable genus-0 curves on Ω∗(X; Λ) lifting the quantum cup product.
One should compare this with [AGV24], where a strict chain-level action of the operad of framed
(stable) genus-0 curves on Hamiltonian Floer theory is constructed.

The theory of deformations of A∞structures carries over to open-closed DMFTs. Recall from
[FOOO09a] that a weak bounding cochain of a unital curved A∞algebra is a Maurer–Cartan element

While it may seem surprising that weak bounding cochains are sufficient to eliminate curvature in

The basis of the operations of the open-closed DMFT of Theorem A as well as the symmetric
monoidal structure and the homotopies of Theorem B are the correspondences of §3.2. We construct
them using moduli spaces of stable maps with Lagrangian boundary. Since we have to incorporate
chains coming from moduli space of stable curves, these correspondences are singular in general –
even after using regularisation techniques. Inspired by [She25], we use analytic chains. This means
we can apply the real analytic resolution of singularities algorithm of [BM08], extended to real
analytic orbifolds with corners in §A.
Remark 1.4. We expect the open-closed DMFT of Theorem A to be defined in any category in
which pull-push operations based on correspondences make sense. In the same vein, while we define
an A∞functor, our construction should also yield an ∞-functor to suitable ∞-categories without
further geometric work.
1.3. Applications. Mirror symmetry states that for every Calabi-Yau variety X there should be
a mirror Calabi-Yau variety Y such that, roughly speaking, the symplectic invariants of X are
‘isomorphic’ to complex or algebraic invariants of Y . The first example of this was given for the
quintic threefold X in CP4 by [CdlOGP92], where a correspondence between the Gromov–Witten
invariants of X and the period integrals of the mirror quintic Y was proven. This is known as
enumerative mirror symmetry. Kontsevich conjectured this should be the consequence of a more
fundamental equivalence, called homological mirror symmetry.
Conjecture 1 ([Kon95]). For mirror varieties X and Y , there exists an equivalence
Fuk(X) ∼= DbCoh(Y )
between the Fukaya category of X and the derived category of coherent sheaves on Y . Moreover, this
homological mirror symmetry implies enumerative mirror symmetry.
In particular, on the symplectic side, this would imply
Conjecture 2 ([Kon95]). The Fukaya category determines the Gromov-Witten invariants of the
symplectic manifold.
In [GPS15, GS25] this was proven in genus 0 for Calabi–Yau varieties, under suitable assumptions,
using Hodge-theoretic mirror symmetry. One of the major technical ingredients is their proof
that an S1-equivariant enhancement of the open-closed map intertwines the Getzler–Gauss–Manin
connection with the quantum connection. This result was also obtained in [Hug24a] under very
restrictive regularity assumptions. With similar methods it was shown in [Hug24b] that one can also
extract enumerative invariants of Lagrangians, so called genus 0 open Gromov–Witten invariants,
from the Fukaya category under suitable regularity assumptions. The present paper removes these
regularity requirements from these papers.
In order to prove Conjecture 2 in arbitrary genus, Costello [Cos07] associates categorical enumera-
tive invariants to a (weakly Calabi–Yau) A∞category, which should recover the usual Gromov–Witten
invariants in the case of the Fukaya category. These have been shown to be quite computable,
[CLT21], but it remains to be shown that they recover ‘geometric’ enumerative invariants. As a
first step in defining categorical enumerative invariants, Costello proved that any Calabi–Yau A∞
category determines an open-closed TCFT.

Remark 1.5. It is unknown whether the closed-open map of an arbitrary closed symplectic manifold
X is necessarily an isomorphism; in fact, it is unknown whether the Fukaya category is non-trivial.
In situations where homological mirror symmetry has been established, one can use the generation
criterion of [Abo10, Gan16] to show that the closed-open map is an isomorphism.
Remark 1.6. In order to obtain Gromov–Witten invariants from the Fukaya category, one needs to
equip the category with the correct trivialisation of the S1-action/splitting of the non-commutative
Hodge-de-Rham spectral sequence. When it is an isomorphism, the closed-open map provides this
trivialisation. In the Calabi–Yau case, [GPS15] use intrinsic properties of the Fukaya category to
determine the correct splitting. For a general symplectic manifold, one might need additional data.
1.4. Field theories and a construction in symplectic topology. In this section we describe
open-closed DMFTs in more detail and how they relate to more classical algebraic structures, and
give an overview of the construction.
1.4.1. Cohomological and Deligne–Mumford field theories. Before the first construction of Gromov–
Witten invariants was given, Kontsevich and Manin described the algebraic structure they should
yield, [KM94], inspired by the seminal work of Witten, [Wit91]. Let Mg,n denote the Deligne–
Mumford compactification of the moduli space of (closed) Riemann surfaces of genus g with n
marked points. A cohomological field theory consists of a graded vector space V equipped with a
graded symmetric pairing η on V , a distinguished element e ∈V 0, called the unit, and a family of

In this section we prove a major technical ingredient for the definition of the open-closed DMFT

A.1. Preliminaries on analytic spaces. A key ingredient of the proof is the resolution of
singularities of an analytic space as defined/constructed in [BM97]. We recall here the relevant

Definition A.1. A real analytic manifold with corners V is a Hausdorff space with an atlas
{(ϕi, Ui)}i∈I of homeomorphisms ϕi : Ui →Vi ⊂(R≥0)n, where Vi is an open subspace of (R≥0)n so
that the transition functions extend to real analytic functions in a neighbourhood of ϕi(Ui ∩Uj) for
any i, j ∈I.
Definition A.2. A analytic map between real analytic varieties with corners is a continuous map
that in local coordinates extends to a real analytic map between affine real analytic varieties.
This is a adaption of the notion of a smooth map between manifolds with corners in [Joy12b].

Definition A.4. Let V be a real analytic manifold with corners. A subspace Z ⊂V is analytic if
for each z ∈Z there exists an open neighbourhood U of z in V and an analytic function f : U →Rk
so that Z ∩U = f−1({0}).
