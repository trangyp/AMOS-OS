ON COUPLINGS FOR KINETIC LANGEVIN DIFFUSIONS
NAWAF BOU-RABEE, SONJA COX, AND ROY SCHIEVEN
Abstract. For the kinetic Langevin diffusion and its splitting discretizations, the hypoelliptic noise structure
makes the relationship between couplings and total variation (TV) bounds more subtle than in the elliptic
case. We establish that, for the kinetic Langevin equation with quadratic potential, no Markovian coupling
(continuous or discrete) captures the asymptotic decay rate of the TV distance between two solutions with
different initial values; the canonical iterated one-shot (or sticky) coupling, for which we derive an exact
contraction formula, saturates this lower bound. On the constructive side, we show that the recent sharp TV
bounds of Chak and Monmarch´e admit a natural interpretation through an explicit non-Markovian coupling,
built from an optimal coalescence trajectory characterized by a classical minimum-energy control problem.
For the OBABO splitting scheme, this approach additionally eliminates the Hessian-Lipschitz, step-size, and
final-time assumptions of [15].

Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithms for sampling from µtarget. To implement such an algorithm, however, the
dynamics must be discretized, and the sample complexity of the resulting chain is governed by its TV mixing
time. The present work concerns this TV mixing time, with particular attention to its dependence on the
time discretization step size.

Within this landscape, the present paper makes three contributions:
C.1 In Section 3 we construct an explicit non-Markovian coupling whose meeting probability realizes the
recent TV bound of Chak and Monmarch´e [15] for discretizations of (1.1). The coupling is built from
a coalescence map associated with a classical minimum-energy control problem; for quadratic U, its
meeting probability equals the TV distance exactly (Remark 3.19).
C.2 The coupling of C.1 is fundamentally non-Markovian. In Section 4 we show that this is unavoidable:
for the kinetic Langevin diffusion with a quadratic potential U(x) = α|x|2, in both continuous
and discrete time, the meeting probability of any Markovian coupling either fails to match the
asymptotic decay rate of the TV distance between two initial conditions, or does so only at the
cost of a multiplicative constant scaling like 1/h. This extends to the kinetic Langevin setting the
impossibility framework introduced by Banerjee and Kendall [2] for the Kolmogorov diffusion.
C.3 For the canonical Markovian coupling — the iterated one-shot (or sticky) coupling — which yields
sharp TV bounds for discretizations of the overdamped Langevin equation [23], we derive in Section 5
a closed-form expression for the meeting probability in the linear Markov chain setting (Theorem 5.1).
Applied to the kinetic Langevin diffusion, this expression saturates the worse of the two regimes
identified in C.2: the meeting probability captures the correct asymptotic rate in T, but only with a
1/h prefactor.
The impossibility result of C.2 explains why the non-Markovian construction of C.1 is unavoidable, while C.3
shows that the iterated one-shot coupling saturates this lower bound. Related work appears at the end of
this introduction.

The iterated one-shot coupling was introduced for Gaussian random walks in [12] and was first applied directly
to approximations of SDEs in [42], where the term “one-shot coupling” was coined. The coupling maximizes
the probability of meeting at the next step given the current position, and can be viewed as a discretization
of the reflection coupling for continuous processes (see [28, Remark 2.6] and [36]). The terminology sticky
coupling in [21, 22] refers to the same construction.
Durmus and Moulines used the iterated one-shot coupling to obtain asymptotically optimal TV contraction
rates for discretizations of the overdamped Langevin equation (1.2). Adapting their argument, we derive
an exact formula for the meeting probability of the iterated one-shot coupling on linear discretized SDEs
(Theorem 5.1). This formula yields the bound (1.7) and identifies the conditions under which the iterated
one-shot coupling does and does not reproduce sharp TV bounds.
Related work. The TV analysis of overdamped Langevin discretizations is well-developed.
Weak-error
expansions for the Euler-Maruyama discretization go back to [46]; non-asymptotic TV bounds for the un-
adjusted Langevin algorithm (ULA) were obtained by Dalalyan [19] under strong convexity and sharpened
in [24, 23], and the Metropolis-adjusted variant (MALA) eliminates the discretization bias at the cost of
an accept-reject step. The iterated one-shot coupling in [23] is the canonical Markovian coupling for these
discretizations and the natural starting point for our analysis in C.3.
For kinetic Langevin discretizations, Wasserstein contraction under strong convexity was established by [16,
20] using synchronous couplings, and in the non-strongly-convex case by [17] adapting the hybrid coupling
of [26] to discrete time. TV bounds via Wasserstein-to-TV regularization were obtained for the OBABO
splitting in [41] and for the larger generalized HMC class in [29]; one-step Wasserstein contraction rates for a
broad family of splittings appear in [34], with related Wasserstein bounds for further splittings in [44]. The
TV bounds of [41, 29] degenerate under vanishing step size, motivating the multi-step Wasserstein-to-TV
regularization of [15]. However, their argument does not produce an explicit coupling between the noise
increments; we construct such a coupling in C.1.
An alternative line of work analyzes TV mixing of kinetic Langevin discretizations via functional inequal-
ities [13, 33, 37, 40]; as argued in [15], the coupling-based approach is more flexible, accommodating in
particular stochastic-gradient approximations. Couplings have also been used extensively for Hamiltonian
Monte Carlo and its variants [8, 6, 11].

3. The non-Markovian finite-time coupling
The starting point for this section is the recent work of Chak and Monmarch´e [15], who establish Wasserstein-
to-TV regularization for a broad class of generalized Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (gHMC) Markov chains. The
OBABO discretization of the kinetic Langevin equation, with step size h > 0, generates a chain in this
class; we review the OBABO scheme in Section 3.1 and specialize to it throughout the remainder of this
section. The regularization mechanism is necessarily multi-step: the position component of a single step of
the OBABO chain has variance of order h3, too degenerate to support a Gaussian coupling on its own. To
circumvent this, [15] introduce a coalescence map, a measurable transport of the Gaussian increments driving
the chain from z onto Gaussian increments driving the chain from ˜z; controlling the deviation of this map
from the identity over n steps yields the bound of Theorem 1.1.
This section refines that analysis in three respects. First, we relax the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1: the step-
size restrictions hn ≤1 and h ≤h0 are removed for the OBABO chain (Theorem 3.2), so that the bound
holds for an arbitrary number of integration steps n ∈N and arbitrary step sizes h > 0, and the Lipschitz-
Hessian assumption on U in [15] is dropped, retaining only the Lipschitz-gradient assumption. Second, we
identify the coalescence map that is optimal for the linearized dynamics, as the unique minimizer of a discrete
linear-quadratic control problem (Section 3.3); this map differs from the choice in [15]. The proofs of the
first two refinements share a common structural ingredient: the Jacobian of the coalescence map associated
with the OBABO scheme is lower triangular (Lemma 3.11), which makes both the inversion required for
the optimal map and the bounds needed for arbitrary (h, n) tractable. Several other splitting schemes share
this lower-triangular structure (Remark 3.13). Third, we show that the resulting analysis admits a coupling
interpretation: it defines an explicit non-Markovian coupling of two OBABO chains started from different
initial values (Section 3.4), whose meeting probability coincides with the TV bound. When U is quadratic,
this coupling is maximal (Remark 3.19).

holds for arbitrary h > 0 and n ∈N, in contrast to the step-size restrictions hn ≤1 and h ≤h0 required
by Theorem 1.1. Third, the regularity hypothesis on U weakens to ∇U Lipschitz, the Lipschitz-Hessian
assumption of Theorem 1.1 being no longer needed. The first improvement is achieved by an optimization
argument (Section 3.3); the second and third both follow from a structural feature of the OBABO scheme
established in Section 3.2: the Jacobian of the associated coalescence map is lower triangular with identity
blocks on the diagonal, so the trace and log-determinant terms in the underlying Pinsker-type bound vanish
identically.

5. Quantifying the lack of optimality for iterated one-shot couplings
The iterated one-shot coupling is a coupling for Markov chains; it is defined such that the probability
of two chains meeting at time n ∈N is maximized given the state at time n −1. In particular, this is
Markovian coupling satisfying the now-equals-forever property in the sense of Definition 4.4, and it is the
natural discretization of the well-known reflection coupling for continuous diffusion equations. The coupling
can also be thought of as a greedy algorithm for maximizing the meeting probability iteratively. It has been
used effectively in e.g. [23] to bound the TV distance of Euler discretizations of the overdamped Langevin
equation, as well as in other places, such as [30].
