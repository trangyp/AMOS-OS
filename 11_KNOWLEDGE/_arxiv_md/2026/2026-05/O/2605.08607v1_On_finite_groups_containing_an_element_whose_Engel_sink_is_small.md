ON FINITE GROUPS CONTAINING AN ELEMENT
WHOSE ENGEL SINK IS SMALL
EVGENY KHUKHRO AND PAVEL SHUMYATSKY
Abstract. For an element g of a group G, a right Engel sink of g is a subset of G containing
all sufficiently long commutators [...[[g, x], x], . . . , x] for all x ∈G. A left Engel sink of g is
a subset of G containing all sufficiently long commutators [...[[x, g], g], . . . , g] for all x ∈G.
Using the classification of finite simple groups we prove that if a finite group G has an
element g such that G = [G, g], then the order of G is bounded in terms of a right Engel
sink of g, as well as in terms of a left Engel sink of g. Earlier Guralnick and Tracey proved
this in the case where g is an involution without using the classification.
1. Introduction
We use the abbreviated notation for Engel words [a, nb] = [...[[a, b], b], . . . , b], where b is
repeated n times. A right Engel sink of an element h of a group H is a subset R ⊆H
containing all sufficiently long commutators [h, nx], that is, for every x ∈H there is a
positive integer r(x, h) such that [h, nx] ∈R for any n ⩾r(x, h). A left Engel sink of h ∈H
is a subset L ⊆H containing all sufficiently long commutators [x, nh], that is, for every
x ∈H there is a positive integer l(x, h) such that [x, nh] ∈L for any n ⩾l(x, h). Clearly, in
a finite group H, every element h ∈H has a minimal right Engel sink R(h), and a minimal
left Engel sink L (h). An element h ∈H is right Engel precisely when R(h) = {1}, and
h ∈H is left Engel precisely when L (h) = {1}.
It was proved [17, 28, 13] that the (generalized) Fitting height and non-soluble length of
the left and right Engel sinks of a given element of a finite group are closely linked with
its ‘global’ position in the (generalized) Fitting series and upper nonsoluble series of the
group. These results can be viewed as generalizations of theorems of Baer [31, 12.3.7], by
which a left Engel element of a finite group belongs to the Fitting subgroup, and a right
Engel element belongs to the hypercentre. In particular, Guralnick and Tracey [13] proved
that if α is an automorphism of a finite group G such that G = [G, α], then G = ⟨L (α)⟩
(where L (α) is computed in the semidirect product G⟨α⟩). Of course, results in terms of
automorphisms can also be applied to inner automorphisms, that is, to group elements.
In this paper we consider finite groups G containing an element g such that G = [G, g]. We
prove that the order of G is bounded in terms of |R(g)|, as well as in terms of |L (g)|. These
results are obtained in more general form for the right and left sinks of an automorphism
φ such that G = [G, φ]. If φ is the inner automorphism of G induced by conjugation by
an element g, then in the semidirect product G⟨φ⟩we have [x, φ] = [x, g] for any x ∈G.
Hence the right and left sinks RG⟨φ⟩(φ) and LG⟨φ⟩(φ) of φ in the semidirect product G⟨φ⟩
are equal to R(g) and L (g), respectively. The corresponding results about automorphisms
are more general, since they apply not only to inner automorphisms.
We first state the results about the right sink.

Theorem 1.1. Suppose that φ is an automorphism of a finite group G such that G = [G, φ].
Then the order of G is bounded above in terms of the cardinality of the right Engel sink R(φ)
of φ in G⟨φ⟩.
Note that if R(φ) = {1}, then φ belongs to the hypercentre of G⟨φ⟩, and then the
condition G = [G, φ] implies that G = 1. In general, it is not true that |[G, φ]| is bounded
in terms of |R(φ)|. It is worth mentioning two cases where this is true. First, this is true if
G is a simple group. In fact, when G is a non-abelian simple group, the result is somewhat
stronger, namely, |G| is bounded in terms of the cardinality of a smaller set RG(φ), which
consists of all sufficiently long commutators [φ, nx] for x ∈G (rather than x ∈G⟨φ⟩).
Theorem 1.2. Suppose that φ is a nontrivial automorphism of a non-abelian finite simple
group G. Then the order of G is bounded above in terms of the cardinality of the right Engel
sink RG(φ).
Another case is that of a coprime automorphism.
Corollary 1.3. Suppose that φ is an automorphism of a finite group G of coprime order:
(|G|, |φ|) = 1. Then the order of [G, φ] is bounded above in terms of the cardinality of the
right Engel sink R(φ) of φ in G⟨φ⟩.
We now state the results about the left sink L (φ). Note that for left sinks we have
LG⟨φ⟩(φ) = LG(φ).
Theorem 1.4. Suppose that φ is an automorphism of a finite group G such that G = [G, φ].
Then the order of G is bounded above in terms of the cardinality of the left Engel sink L (φ).
Note that if L (φ) = {1}, then φ belongs to the Fitting subgroup of G⟨φ⟩, and then the
condition G = [G, φ] implies that G = 1. In general, it is not true that |[G, φ]| is bounded
in terms of |L (φ)|. This is clearly true when G is a simple group. Another case is that of
a coprime automorphism.
Corollary 1.5. Suppose that φ is an automorphism of a finite group G of coprime order:
(|G|, |φ|) = 1. Then the order of [G, φ] is bounded above in terms of the cardinality of the
left Engel sink L (φ).
The proofs of our results rely on the classification of finite simple groups. It is worth
mentioning that for an involution τ ∈Aut G such that G = [G, τ] Guralnick and Tracey [13,
Theorem 1.7] proved that |G| is bounded in terms of |L (τ)| without using the classification.
The same result for |R(τ)| follows easily from the case of |L (τ)|; see Corollary 2.7 below
for more details.
Other previous results concerning Engel sinks dealt with restrictions imposed on Engel
sinks of all elements of a group or a subgroup. Recall that a group G is called an Engel group
if all elements of G are left Engel, or equivalently, if all elements of G are right Engel. By
the elementary theorem of Zorn [31, 12.3.4], an Engel finite group is nilpotent. Much more
sophisticated results [30, 33] based on the works of Zelmanov [34, 35, 36] establish (local)
nilpotency of certain types of infinite groups with Engel-type conditions. But in general,
say, Engel residually finite groups need not be locally nilpotent, as first shown by Golod’s
examples [9]. Imposing restrictions on the right or left Engel sinks of group elements provides
natural generalizations of Engel groups. In several recent papers [2, 4, 18, 21, 22, 27] it was
shown that if a finite, or profinite, or compact group satisfies restrictions on the right or left
Engel sinks of all of its elements, then the group is close to be (locally) nilpotent. In other
papers restrictions on Engel sinks were imposed on all elements of the fixed-point subgroup
of an automorphism [1, 3, 24, 25, 26]. In this paper we use one of the results of Khukhro

Left Engel sinks. Let h be an element of a group H, and K ⩽H an h-invariant subgroup.
A left Engel sink of h in K is a subset L ⊆K containing all sufficiently long commutators
[x, nh] for x ∈K, that is, for every x ∈K there is a positive integer l(x, h) such that
[x, nh] ∈L for any n ⩾l(x, h). The intersection of two such left sinks is again a left sink of
h in K. Hence there is a minimal left sink of h in K denoted by LK(h). When K = H we
write simply L (h). Speaking of a left Engel sink of an element we shall always mean its
smallest left Engel sink.
The left sink LK(h) is h-invariant. Clearly, LK1(h) ⊆LK(h) for an h-invariant subgroup
K1 ⩽K, and the image of LK(h) in a quotient group ¯H = H/N is the left Engel sink L ¯
K(¯h)
of the image of h in the image of K. We shall freely use these properties without special
references.
For every element x ∈K, either [x, nh] = 1 for some n, or [x, nh] = [x, n+mh] ̸= 1 for some
positive integers m, n. When m+n is minimal possible in this equation, the elements [x, ih]
for i = n, n + 1, . . . , n + m −1 form a cycle under the mapping u →[u, h] and this limit
cycle is contained in LK(h). Clearly, LK(h) is the union of limit cycles of elements of K.
A limit cycle may consist of a single element, but all elements of limit cycles are nontrivial
by definition. If N is a normal subgroup, then a limit cycle is either entirely contained in
N or has empty intersection with N.
The following theorem was proved by Khukhro and Shumyatsky [18].
Theorem 2.2 ([18, Theorem 3.1]). Let G be a finite group, and m a positive integer.
Suppose that for every g ∈G the cardinality of the left Engel sink L (g) is at most m. Then
G has a normal subgroup N of order bounded in terms of m such that G/N is nilpotent.

Automorphisms. If φ is an automorphism of a group B we use the usual notation for
commutators [b, φ] = b−1bφ and commutator subgroups [B, φ] = ⟨[b, φ] | b ∈B⟩, as well as
for centralizers CB(φ) = {b ∈B | bφ = b}. The automorphism induced by φ on the quotient
by a normal φ-invariant subgroup is denoted by the same letter φ.
An automorphism φ of a finite group G is called a coprime automorphism if the orders
of φ and G are coprime: (|G|, |φ|) = 1. A coprime automorphism of a p-group is called a
p′-automorphism. The following lemma collects several well-known properties of coprime
automorphisms of finite groups, which will be often used without special references.
Lemma 2.3. Let φ be a coprime automorphism of a finite group G.
(a) If N is a normal φ-invariant subgroup of G, then the fixed points of φ in the quotient
G/N are covered by the fixed points of φ in G, that is, CG/N(φ) = CG(φ)N/N.
(b) We have [[G, φ], φ] = [G, φ].
(c) If G is abelian, then G = [G, φ] × CG(φ).
An automorphism of order pk for some prime p is called a p-automorphism. The following
fact is well-known.
Lemma 2.4. If α is a p-automorphism of an elementary abelian p-group V , then |V | is
bounded above in terms of |CV (α)| and |α|.
Proof. Consider V as an Fp⟨α⟩-module. The dimension of |CV (α)| is equal to the number
of Jordan blocks in the Jordan normal form of α. Each of the blocks has size at most |α|.
Hence the result.

3. Right sink: simple groups
In this section we prove Theorem 1.1 for simple groups in a stronger version given by
Theorem 1.2.
Proof of Theorem 1.2. Recall that φ is a nonrivial automorphism of a non-abelian finite
simple group G. We need to prove that |G| is bounded above in terms of the cardinality
of the right Engel sink RG(φ). Note that G = [G, φ], since G is simple. It is convenient
to fix the number m = |RG(φ)|; our task is to prove that the order |G| is m-bounded. We
can obviously ignore the sporadic simple groups. To deal with the alternating groups and
groups of Lie type, we shall make use of a ‘large’ cyclic Sylow subgroup with the help of the
following lemma.

Lemma 3.2. If G is isomorphic to an alternating group, then |G| is m-bounded.

It remains to consider simple groups of Lie type. From now on in this section we assume
that G ∼= Ld(q) is a group of Lie type L of Lie rank d over the finite field Fq of characteristic p.
The order of G is bounded in terms of the Lie rank d and the cardinality of the field q = pk.
First we shall show that the Lie rank is m-bounded, then that p is m-bounded, and finally
that k is m-bounded.
To show that the Lie rank d is m-bounded, we shall use the fact that every finite simple
group contains a cyclic Sylow s-subgroup for some prime s. This was noted by Brauer [6]
and is implicit in the classification, as well as was explicitly proved by Hiss [15]. Recall that
the order of a cyclic Sylow s-subgroup in our group G is m-bounded by Lemma 3.1.
Lemma 3.3. If G ∼= Ld(q) is a group of Lie type of rank d, then d is m-bounded, namely,
d ⩽max{8, 2(m −1)2}.
Proof. The Weyl groups are sections of groups of Lie type. It is known that the Weyl groups
of classical Chevalley and Steinberg groups of Lie rank d involve alternating groups Ad. Let
s be a prime such that G has a nontrivial cyclic Sylow s-subgroup. Then s ⩽(m −1)2 by
Lemma 3.1. But if d ⩾2s, then Ad has a non-cyclic Sylow s-subgroup. Hence d < 2s ⩽
2(m −1)2. The Lie ranks of all other exceptional groups of Lie type are at most 8.

4. Right sink: general case
Here we prove Theorem 1.1 about the right sink for an arbitrary finite group.
Proof of Theorem 1.1. Recall that φ is an automorphism of a finite group G such that
G = [G, φ]. Let m = |R(φ)|, where R(φ) is the minimal right Engel sink of φ in G⟨φ⟩. We
need to prove that |G| is m-bounded. We proceed by induction on m. For m = 1 the element
φ is hypercentral in G⟨φ⟩, so that [G, φ]⟨φ⟩is nilpotent and therefore G = [G, φ] = 1.
In general, there are two possibilities. One is when G contains a minimal normal φ-
invariant subgroup N such that |R( ¯φ)| < m in ¯G⟨¯φ⟩= G⟨φ⟩/N. The alternative is that G
has no such subgroups.
First we consider the case where G is abelian.
Lemma 4.1. Suppose that G is abelian. Then |G| = m.
Proof. When G is abelian, the condition G = [G, φ] implies that also G = [G, φ−1]. Then
G = L (φ−1) by Lemma 2.5(c), while L (φ−1) ⊆R(φ) by Lemma 2.6. Hence |G| ⩽m.
□
We now consider the case where G itself is a minimal normal φ-invariant subgroup.
Lemma 4.2. Suppose that G has no proper (nontrivial) normal φ-invariant subgroups.
Then the order of G is m-bounded.
Proof. If G is elementary abelian, then |G| is m-bounded by Lemma 4.1.
Now let G = S1 × · · · × Sk be a direct product of isomorphic non-abelian simple groups
transitively permuted by φ. We regard elements of G as k-tuples of elements of S ∼= S1

m-bounded by Theorem 1.2. Let k ⩾2. For an element g = (x, y, . . . ), where x, y ∈S,
we have [φ, g] = (y−1x, x−1y) when k = 2, and [φ, g] = (x, x−1y, y−1, . . . ) when k ⩾3. In
either case the commutator [φ, ng] has [x−1y, n−1y] in its second coordinate. When x varies,
the commutators [x−1y, n−1y] for big enough n fill the left Engel sink LS(y) in S. Since the
commutators [φ, ng] for big enough n belong to R(φ), which has cardinality m, it follows
that |LS(y)| ⩽m for any y ∈S. Then |S| is m-bounded by Theorem 2.2.
It remains to show that k is m-bounded. Clearly, we can assume that k ⩾3. Then for g =
(x, y, 1, . . . ), where x, y ∈S, the commutators [φ, ng] have the form (1, [x−1y, n−1y], 1, . . . ).
When x varies, the commutators [x−1y, n−1y] for big enough n fill the left Engel sink LS(y)

Before proceeding with the proof, we make an observation about limit cycles in R(φ).
Namely, for every element x ∈G there is a least positive integer i(x) such that [φ, i(x)x] ∈
R(φ). If [φ, i(x)x] ̸= 1, this means that [φ, i(x)x] belongs to a limit cycle in R(φ). Then
1 ̸= [φ, jx] ∈R(φ) for all j ⩾i(x). Let i0 = max{i(x) | x ∈G}.
Lemma 4.3. Suppose that N is a (nontrivial) minimal normal φ-invariant subgroup of G
such that |RG⟨φ⟩/N(φ)| < m. Then the order of G is m-bounded.
Proof. If N = G, then |G| is m-bounded by Lemma 4.2. Therefore we can assume that
N < G. By the induction hypothesis, the order of G/N is m-bounded. We need to show
that the order of N is also m-bounded.
First suppose that N is a direct product of non-abelian simple groups. Then [N, φ] ̸= 1,
as otherwise G = [G, φ] would centralize N, a contradiction. The normal subgroup [N, φ]
of N is a product of some of the simple factors of N. Note that CN([N, φ]) is φ-invariant
and is the product of the simple factors of N that are not in [N, φ]. By the same argument,
[N, φ] is a direct product of several (possibly one) minimal normal φ-invariant subgroups
of N, and if M is one of these subgroups, then [M, φ] = M. Then |M| is m-bounded by
Lemma 4.2. Since M is normal in N⟨φ⟩, there are at most |G/N| conjugates of M. Since
|G/N| is m-bounded, the normal closure of M in G⟨φ⟩, which is N, is of m-bounded order,
as required.
Now let N be abelian. If N is central in G, then the order of the derived subgroup G′ is
m-bounded by Schur’s theorem [32, Theorem 4.12]. The order of the abelian quotient group
¯G = G/G′ is at most m by Lemma 4.1. As a result, |G| is m-bounded.
Thus we can assume that N is not central in G. Note that then R(φ) ̸⊆CG(N), as
otherwise φ would be hypercentral in G/CG(N), so that we would have G = [G, φ] = CG(N)
and N would be central in G, contrary to our assumption.
Let a ∈R(φ) \ CG(N). There is x ∈G and a positive integer i such that a = [φ, ix].
Adding if necessary several times the length of the limit cycle of R(φ) containing a, we

Lemma 4.4. Suppose that G has no (nontrivial) minimal normal φ-invariant subgroups of

Hence |G| is m-bounded.

The proof of Theorem 1.1 is complete.
