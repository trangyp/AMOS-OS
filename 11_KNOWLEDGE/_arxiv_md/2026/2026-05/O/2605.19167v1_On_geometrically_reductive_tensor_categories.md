ON GEOMETRICALLY REDUCTIVE TENSOR CATEGORIES
KEVIN COULEMBIER
Abstract. We prove the conjecture that higher Verlinde categories are geometrically reductive.
This is one of the two properties required in order for recent results on algebraic geometry in
tensor categories to apply to these categories. We also reduce two further conjectures concerning
geometric reductivity for tensor categories to other conjectures appearing in the literature.
Introduction
Symmetric tensor categories of moderate growth over fields of characteristic zero are well-
understood due to classical work of Deligne, see [De1, De2]. Throughout, we let k be an algebraically
closed field, of prime characteristic p > 0. The current paper is part of the ongoing exploration of
symmetric tensor categories of moderate growth over k. By [CEO2, De1], every such tensor cate-
gory is a representation category of an affine group scheme internal to an ‘incompressible’ tensor
category, which in a sense reduces the study to the latter type of category. There is a concrete
conjecture for what these incompressible categories might be, namely the tensor subcategories of a
category Verp∞introduced in [BE, BEO, Co2], and studied further and generalised in [Dec, Ne].
Evidence for this conjecture can be found in [CEO1, EG] and references therein.
To exploit truly the realisation of a tensor category as a representation category in an incom-
pressible category, results in [Co3, Co4, CEO2, CS1, CS2] (concerned with algebraic geometry,
existence of homogeneous spaces and characterisations of simple algebras) demonstrate that the
incompressible categories should satisfy two fundamental properties introduced in [Co3, §3], they
should be ‘maximally nilpotent’ (MN) and ‘geometrically reductive’ (GR). The former means that
symmetric algebras of objects are as small as possible, while the latter is a direct generalisation of
the property with same name for affine group schemes. It is known by [CEO2] that Ver2∞satisfies
these conditions and the same is conjectured of Verp∞for all p in [Co3]. That Verp2 ⊂Verp∞is GR
was proved in [Co3, Proposition 3.1.8], with a method that unfortunately already fails for Verp3.
The two conditions MN and GR have rather different roles. The MN condition is extremely
restrictive, in fact expected to completely characterise incompressible categories. The GR condition
is expected to hold much more generally, although proving this is not always easy. For instance, the
classical conjecture of Mumford [Mu] that reductive groups are geometrically reductive was proved
by Haboush in [Ha].
In conclusion, the main aims of the line of research described above are showing that Verp∞
contains all incompressible tensor categories and is MN and GR. The current paper proves this last
condition, confirming [Co3, Conjecture 3.1.6(2)]:
Theorem A. For any p > 0, the tensor category Verp∞is geometrically reductive.
For p = 2, Theorem A was proved as [CEO1, Theorem 9.3.7] using different methods, relying on
the alternative construction of Ver2∞in [BE]. We therefore focus on a proof for p > 2 specifically,
which allows for some simplifications, culminating in Theorem 3.2.1. The approach to the proof is

1. preliminaries
Denote by Sn the permutation group of the set {1, 2, . . . , n}, and by |σ| the parity of a permu-
tation σ ∈Sn. We set N = Z≥0 and let k be an algebraically closed field.
1.1. Tensor categories.
1.1.1.
We use the notion of ‘tensor categories’ as in [EGNO], except that, since the topic is
fundamentally restricted to symmetric tensor categories, we leave out any reference to ‘symmetric’.
Concretely, an essentially small k-linear symmetric category (C, ⊗, 1) is a tensor category over
k if C is abelian with the length ℓ(X) of each object X ∈C finite, k →EndC(1) is an isomorphism
and the monoidal category (C, ⊗, 1) is rigid. The latter means that every X ∈C has a monoidal
dual X∗, with appropriate evaluation morphism evX : X∗⊗X →1 and coevaluation. An exact
symmetric monoidal functor between tensor categories is called a tensor functor.
A tensor category is of moderate growth if for each X ∈C there is C ∈R with ℓ(X⊗n) ≤Cn.
Examples of such categories are given by finite tensor categories C in the sense of [EGNO],
meaning C has enough projective objects and only finitely many simple objects up to isomorphism.
A tensor category C is finitely generated if there exists an X ∈C such that every object in C is
a subquotient of a polynomial in X and X∗.
The smallest tensor category over k is the category Vec of finite dimensional vector spaces. We
denote the symmetric monoidal category of all vector spaces by VEC. The tensor category sVec of
supervector spaces is the category of Z/2-graded vector spaces with braiding such that the braiding
isomorphism on ¯1⊗¯1 for the non-trivial simple object ¯1 is −1. For an affine group scheme G over k,
the category of finite dimensional rational representations RepkG is canonically a tensor category.
For any ind-object X in a tensor category C, we consider the ‘invariant’ subobject Xinv =
Hom(1, X). We can regard −inv as the right adjoint of the inclusion tensor functor Vec →C.
We denote by CAlg C the category of commutative ind-algebras in C (ring objects in IndC).
For A ∈CAlg, we have that Ainv ⊂A is its maximal subalgebra belonging to VEC ⊂IndC. An
ind-algebra is finitely generated if it is a quotient of an algebra S X, as defined next, for X ∈C.

1.1.3. Geometric reductivity. Following [Co3], a tensor category C is geometrically reductive
(GR) if for every non-zero morphism X →1, there is some m ∈Z>0 for which the induced
epimorphism Sm X ↠1 is split. For an affine group scheme G over k, the tensor category RepkG
is GR if and only if G is geometrically reductive in the classical sense. We recall the following
connection with ‘invariant theory’.
Lemma 1.1.4. [Co3, Theorem 3.1.5(1)] If for every finitely generated A ∈CAlg C, the invariant
algebra Ainv is finitely generated, then C is GR.
For C = RepkG this is actually ‘if and only if’, see [Na], and we prove a more general result for
tensor categories in that direction in the current paper, see Theorem 4.2.1(2).

4. Geometric reductivity of more general tensor categories
In this section we let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p > 0.
4.1. New connections between conjectures. The following theorem, reduces two conjectures
regarding geometric reductivity to other conjectures in the literature.
Theorem 4.1.1. Assume that Verp∞is MN, i.e. [Co3, Conjecture 3.2.3] is valid.
(1) If [BEO, Conjecture 1.4] is valid for finite tensor categories, then every finite tensor category
is GR. Moreover, then every finitely generated ind-algebra in a finite tensor category is of
finite type i.e. [CEO2, Conjecture 9.3.5] is true.
(2) If [BEO, Conjecture 1.4] is valid, then also [Co3, Conjecture 3.1.6(1)] is valid. Concretely,
a tensor category of moderate growth C is GR if and only if for every finite generated
A ∈CAlg C the k-algebra Ainv is finitely generated.
The theorem will follow from some unconditional statements, proved in the next subsection.

Definition 4.1.2. An algebra A ∈CAlg C is of finite type if the k-algebra Ainv := Hom(1, A) is

P(ρ(a)) = 0, which we can thus rewrite as ρ(P(a)) = 0. By injectivity of ρ we find P(a) = 0,
showing integrality.
For the second sentence, let a1, . . . , ar be generators of A as a k-algebra. Then, for each i, there
is a (monic) polynomial with coefficients in AG that annihilates ai. We let B be the subalgebra of
AG generated by the coefficients of these polynomials. Now A is a finite B-module by construction,
and hence (since B is noetherian) also AG is a finite B-module and thus also finitely generated as
a k-algebra.
□
4.2. Unconditional results. In this section, we fix D to be a GR + MN tensor category that is
also a union ∪n>0Dn of finite tensor subcategories D1 ⊂D2 ⊂· · · .
Theorem 4.2.1. Consider a tensor category C with tensor functor F : C →D.
(1) If C is finite, then it is GR and every finitely generated ind-algebra in C is of finite type.
(2) If C is GR, then for every finitely generated A ∈CAlg C, the k-algebra Ainv is finitely
generated.
Before proving the theorem, we review the notion of Frobenius twists in the sense of [CS1]. For
R ∈CAlg D we let R[1] be the image of the p-fold multiplication map (R⊗p)Sp →R restricted to
the Sp-invariants (R⊗p)Sp ⊂R⊗p. For s > 1 we define iteratively R[s] = (R[s−1])[1]. If G is an
affine group scheme in D, see [BP, Co3, CS1] for background, then k[G][s] represents a quotient
group scheme G[s], leading to a short exact sequence
1 →Gs →G →G[s] →1.
The s-th Frobenius kernel Gs of G is an infinitesimal group scheme, see [CS1, Proposition 4.16].
Proof of Theorem 4.2.1. For clarity, we will write Γ : IndD →VEC for the functor taking invariants
in D and reserve (−)inv for the corresponding functor for C. Since any finitely generated algebra
in CAlg C belongs to (the ind-completion of) a finitely generated tensor subcategory of C, we can
without loss of generality assume that C is finitely generated. It then follows that F takes values
in a finitely generated tensor subcategory of D, hence in one of the finite Dn ⊂D. We can and will
thus also assume that D is a finite tensor category.
Via F, and following [De1, §8], we can and will realise C as a tensor subcategory of the repre-
sentation category RepG = RepDG in D of an affine group scheme G in D. More concretely the
fundamental group π(D) of D has a canonical action on every object of D, there is a group homo-
morphism ε : π(D) →G, such that the adjoint action of π(D) on k[G] is the canonical action, and
C = Rep(G, ε) is the category of G-representations for which the restriction along ε produces the
canonical action. By [De1, Corollaire 8.18], for an ind-object X in C ⊂RepG, we have Xinv = XG,
the maximal G-invariant subobject, where the left-hand side is a priori Γ(XG).
Since C is finitely generated, G is of finite type, see [CEO2, Lemma 4.3.3]. By [CS1, Corol-
lary 4.21], it follows that G[r] is an affine group scheme over k, for large enough r ∈N, which we
now fix.

which concludes the proof of part (1).

and assume that C is GR. Since D is finite, π(D) is finite. Since G[r] is an ordinary k-group scheme,
the adjoint action of π(D), via π(D) →G →G[r], on k[G[r]] is trivial. In other words, the image
of π(D) →G[r] is central. We take the quotient Q of G[r] with respect to this central subgroup,
leading to a short exact sequence
1 →N →G →Q →1,
where N is still a finite group scheme (since Gr and π(D) were finite). As any tensor subcategory,
the tensor subcategory RepkQ = RepD(Q, ε) of C is again GR, so Q is geometrically reductive. Now
for a finitely generated ind-algebra A in C, we can observe that
Ainv = AG = (AN)Q.
By part (1) applied to the finite tensor category RepDN, the k-algebra Γ(AN) is finitely generated

also the submodule AN ⊂A is a finite Γ(AN)-module, so AN is a finitely generated algebra. Now
by the classical version of the theorem (Nagata’s Theorem from [Na]) applied to the geometrically
reductive group Q, it follows that Ainv is finitely generated.
