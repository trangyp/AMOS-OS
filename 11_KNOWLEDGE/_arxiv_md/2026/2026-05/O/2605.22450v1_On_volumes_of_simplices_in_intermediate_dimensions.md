JOS´E GAITAN MONTEJO AND EYVINDUR ARI PALSSON
Abstract. A variant of the Falconer distance problem asks for fixed k ⩾1 and d ⩾k+1, how
large does the Hausdorff dimension of a Borel set E ⊂Rd need to be to guarantee that there
exist x0, . . . , xk ∈E such that Vol(x0,...,xk)
k+1
(E) = {Volk+1(x0, . . . , xk, xk+1) : xk+1 ∈E}
has positive Lebesgue measure. Here Volk+1(x0, . . . , xk, xk+1) denotes the k + 1-volume of
the k + 1 simplex formed by x0, . . . , xk, xk+1. Recently, Shmerkin and Yavicoli established
a sharp dimensional threshold k in the case when d = k + 1. In this paper we extend their
result to k + 1 ⩽d ⩽2k and obtain a non-trivial dimensional threshold d −k when d > 2k.

A crucial part of the
argument is an application of work by Bright, Ortiz and Zakharov on a continuum Beck-
type theorem for hyperplanes as well as classic results of Marstrand on projections and slicing
theorems. In addition, we investigate a more elementary approach under a condition called
the Fubini property for Hausdorff dimension as introduced in the work of H´era, Keleti and
M´ath´e.

1.1. Affine k-planes spanned by points from a Borel set. One of the key steps in the
argument of [22, Theorem 1.1] is to demonstrate the existence of many affinely independent
k-planes spanned by k + 1 points from E. They achieve this in the case k + 1 = d by requiring
dimH(E) > d −1 and using Marstrand’s slicing theorem (Theorem 2.2 equation (2.4)) to
control the dimension of the the intersections of E with affine hyperplanes. They found that
in almost every slice it is possible to find at least one collection of affinely independent points
x0, . . . , xd−1 ∈E ∩H, and with abundant complement E ∩H⊥. This idea suggests that a
natural approach to obtain the result for volumes of k-simplices is, therefore, to slice E with
lower dimensional affine k-planes and hope to simultaneously have abundant intersections and
complements.
When d > k + 1, then iterating the strategy from [22] does not yield good
quantitative bounds on the abundance of affine k-planes spanned by E.
Recently, a related question was addressed by Bright, Ortiz and Zakharov [2]. Let Pk(E)
denote the set of k-planes spanned by k + 1 affinely independent points of E. The authors
obtained positive and optimal results about the dimension of Pk(E) under non-concentration
assumptions on the Borel set E. Their main goal was a continuum Beck-type theorem for hy-
perplanes, so the case of k = d−1 was of special interest to them. One of the non-concentration
assumptions they considered is to require E to admit an irreducible s-Frostman measure in
the following sense.
Definition 1.2. [2, Definition 1.5]. A measure µ supported on an affine subspace V ⊂Rd is
irreducible in V if µ(V ′) = 0 for any proper affine subspace V ′ ⊂V .
By iteratively identifying many irreducible pieces, µi, of the measure µ supported on E, they
obtained the following result.
Theorem 1.3. [2, Theorem 4.1] Let E ⊂Rd be a Borel set supporting an irreducible s-Frostman
probability measure µ, then for all 1 ⩽m ⩽d −1,
dimH Pm(E) ⩾(m + 1) min{s, d −m}.
(1.1)
As stated, this result is contingent on the existence of an irreducible s-Frostman measure
supported on E.
However, there exists a simple argument that allows us to eliminate the
irreducibility condition on µ while still obtaining the correct estimate of dimH Pm(E). Nev-
ertheless, the penalty for dropping the irreducibility condition is that the estimate (1.1) will
no longer be true for all subplane dimensions 1 ⩽m ⩽d −1, but only on a restricted range
of them, namely 1 ⩽m ⩽dµ −1, where dµ ⩽d is defined by (3.1). This restricted version
of Theorem 1.3 can be found in Lemma 3.2. When dµ < d Lemma 3.2 does not imply the
Continuum Beck-Type Theorem that was the main focus in [2, Theorem 1.2], but it is exactly
the right tool to prove our main Theorem 1.1 with no non-concentration conditions imposed
on E. The proofs for Lemma 3.2 and Theorem 1.1 can be found in Section 3.

Definition 3.1. Given a s-Frostman measure µ supported on E, we define the irreducible
dimension of µ, to be the number
dµ := min{m ∈N : ∃V ∈A(d, m) such that µ is irreducible in V }.

Proof of Lemma 3.2. Let E be a Borel set such that dimH(E) > s > k and µ be a s-Frostman
measure supported on E. If µ is irreducible as in Definition 1.2, then dµ = d, and we are back
in the situation of Theorem 1.3.

such that µ(E ∩V ) > 0 and it can be chosen to be of minimal dimension, that is V ∈A(d, dµ).
