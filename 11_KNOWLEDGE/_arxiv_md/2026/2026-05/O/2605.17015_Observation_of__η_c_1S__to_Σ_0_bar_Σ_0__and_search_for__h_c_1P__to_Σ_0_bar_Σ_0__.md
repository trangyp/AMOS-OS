Using (2712.4 ± 14.3) × 106 ψ(3686) events collected by the BESIII detector operating at the
BEPCII collider, the hadronic decay ηc →Σ0 ¯
Σ0 is observed for the ﬁrst time via the radiative
transition from ψ(3686). It is found that the branching fraction has a signiﬁcant dependence on the
interference pattern between ηc and non-ηc processes. They are determined to be B(ηc →Σ0 ¯Σ0) =
(2.59±0.14(stat)±0.44(syst))×10−3 and (1.18±0.12(stat)±0.21(syst))×10−3, for the destructive
and constructive interference scenarios, respectively. No signiﬁcant signal is observed for the decay
hc →Σ0 ¯Σ0 in the hadronic transition ψ(3686) →π0hc, and an upper limit on its branching fraction
is set to be 1.02 × 10−4 at the 90% conﬁdence level.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The charmonium states below the open-charm thresh-
old have been studied extensively, but questions about
their spin-singlet states, for example, the P-wave state
hc and the S-wave ground state ηc, remain unresolved.
Although the ηc meson has been investigated for near-
ly four decades since its discovery [1], most of its decay
modes remain unidentiﬁed. Even for the known chan-
nels, some of their measured branching fractions (BFs)
suﬀer from considerable uncertainties.
Early measure-
ments of the ηc BFs in J/ψ and ψ(3686) decays did
not suﬃciently take into account the interference eﬀects
between resonant and non-resonant processes. More re-
cent studies, such as the coherent amplitude analysis of
ηc →2(π+π−)η [2], have partially addressed this limita-
tion.
Experimental studies on exclusive charmonium decays
play an important role in testing the perturbative quan-
tum chromodynamics (pQCD). The complexity of QCD
remains unsolved in the charmonium mass region. There
are still many contradictions between pQCD calcula-
tions and experimental measurements. In particular, the
pQCD helicity selection rule
[3–5] is violated in many

exclusive charmonium decay processes. Charmonium de-
cay processes with baryon anti-baryon ﬁnal states, such
as ηc/hc →B8 ¯B8, where B8 ¯B8 denotes the octet bary-
on anti-baryon pair, are supposed to be highly sup-
pressed according to the pQCD helicity selection rule.
Theoretical predictions on the BFs of ηc/hc →B8 ¯B8 have
been made based on a model consideringthe long distance
contribution via charmed hadron loops [6]. Some exper-
imental measurements agree with the theoretical predic-
tions, e.g.
ηc →p¯p, Λ¯Λ and Ξ−¯Ξ+; while others do
not, e.g. ηc →Σ+Σ−. Meanwhile, its isospin partner
ηc →Σ0 ¯Σ0 has not been observed. A study of this de-
cay channel would allow for a test of isospin invariance.
In addition, theoretical predictions for the hc have not
been tested due to the lack of experimental information,
since its predominant decay mode is the electric dipole
(E1) transition of hc →γηc.
Recently, BESIII mea-
sured this E1 transition BF to be (57.66+3.62
−3.50±0.58)% [8],
which is reasonably consistent with some theoretical pre-
dictions [9, 10]. This implies that nearly half of the hc
decays proceed via a non-E1 transition.
However, the
decay modes of hc remain poorly understood experimen-
tally, even after decades of study.
Currently, the sum
of measured BFs, excluding the γηc channel, amounts to
only about 6% [7]. Up to now, no B8 ¯B8 ﬁnal state has
ever been observed.
In this paper, we present the ﬁrst observation of ηc →
Σ0 ¯Σ0 via ψ(3686) →γηc and search for hc →Σ0 ¯Σ0
via ψ(3686) →π0hc by analyzing (2712.4 ± 14.3) ×
106 ψ(3686) events collected by the BESIII detector [11].
II.
BESIII DETECTOR AND MONTE CARLO
SIMULATION
The BESIII detector [12] records symmetric e+e−col-
lisions provided by the BEPCII storage ring [13] in the
center-of-mass energy range from 1.84 to 4.95 GeV, with
a peak luminosity of 1.1 × 1033 cm−2s−1 achieved at
√s = 3.773 GeV.
Large data samples have been col-
lected in this energy region [14–16]. The cylindrical core
of the BESIII detector covers 93% of the full solid an-
gle and consists of a helium-based multilayer drift cham-
ber (MDC), a time-of-ﬂight system (TOF), and a CsI(Tl)
electromagnetic calorimeter (EMC), which are all en-
closed in a superconducting solenoidal magnet providing
a 1.0 T magnetic ﬁeld. The solenoid is surrounded by
an octagonal ﬂux-return yoke made of steel, interleaved
with resistive-plate-counter muon-identiﬁcation modules.
The charged-particle momentum resolution at 1 GeV/c is
0.5%, and the dE/dx resolution is 6% for electrons from
Bhabha scattering. The EMC measures photon energies
with a resolution of 2.5% (5%) at 1 GeV in the barrel
(end cap) region. The time resolution in the plastic scin-
tillator TOF barrel region is 68 ps, while that in the end
cap region was 110 ps. The end cap TOF system was
upgraded in 2015 using multigap resistive plate chamber
technology, providing a time resolution of 60 ps, which
beneﬁts 83% of the data used in this analysis [17–19].
Monte Carlo (MC) simulated data samples generat-
ed with a geant4-based [20] software package, which
includes the geometric description of the BESIII detec-
tor and its response, are used to determine detection
eﬃciencies and estimate backgrounds.
The simulation
models the beam energy spread and initial state radi-
ation (ISR) in e+e−annihilations using the generator
kkmc [21, 22]. The inclusive MC sample includes the
production of the ψ(3686) resonance, the ISR production
of the J/ψ, and the continuum processes incorporated in
kkmc [21, 22]. All particle decays are modeled with evt-
gen [23, 24] using branching fractions either taken from
the Particle Data Group [7], when available, or otherwise
estimated with lundcharm [25, 26].The exclusive decay
of ψ(3686) →γηc is generated with the angular distri-
bution of 1 + cos2 θγ, where θγ is the polar angle of the
radiative photon in the rest frame of the ψ(3686) [27, 28].
The ψ(3686) →π0hc and ηc/hc →Σ0 ¯Σ0 decays are gen-
erated uniformly in phase space (PHSP).
III.
EVENT SELECTION
The decays of interest in this analysis are the cas-
cade processes ψ(3686) →γηc with ηc →Σ0 ¯Σ0, and
ψ(3686) →π0hc with hc →Σ0 ¯Σ0, where Σ0 →γΛ(Λ →
pπ−) and ¯Σ0 →γ ¯Λ(¯Λ →¯pπ+). Charged tracks with the
polar angle (θ) in the range of | cos θ| < 0.93 are detect-
ed in the MDC. Since Λ/¯Λ contains secondary vertices,
no interaction vertex requirement is applied on candi-
date tracks. Each charged track is identiﬁed using parti-
cle identiﬁcation (PID), which combines measurements of
the energy deposited in the MDC (dE/dx) and the ﬂight
time in the TOF to form likelihoods L(h) (h = p, K, π)

protons when the proton hypothesis has the greatest like-
lihood (L(p) > L(K) and L(p) > L(π)), while charged
kaons and pions are identiﬁed by comparing the likeli-
hoods for the kaon and pion hypotheses, L(K) > L(π)
and L(π) > L(K), respectively. At least four charged
particles, p¯pπ+π−, need to be identiﬁed.

showers in the EMC. The deposited energy of each candi-

VII.
SUMMARY
Using (2712.4±14.3)×106 ψ(3686) events collected by
the BESIII detector, the ηc →Σ0 ¯Σ0 decay is observed for
the ﬁrst time. Its BF is measured to be B(ηc →Σ0 ¯Σ0) =
(2.59 ± 0.14(stat) ± 0.44(syst)) × 10−3 for destructive in-
terference and (1.18 ± 0.12(stat) ± 0.21(syst)) × 10−3 for
constructive interference. In addition, we search for the
decay hc →Σ0 ¯Σ0 for the ﬁrst time and no signiﬁcant sig-
nal is observed. The upper limit of its BF is determined
to be B(hc →Σ0 ¯Σ0) < 1.02 × 10−4 at the 90% C.L. The
obtained results are inconsistent with theoretical calcula-
tions, which give (4.82 ∼9.56) × 10−4 for B(ηc →Σ0 ¯Σ0)
and (5.57 ∼7.08) × 10−4 for B(hc →Σ0 ¯Σ0) [6], assum-
ing the isospin conservation between charged and neutral
modes.
