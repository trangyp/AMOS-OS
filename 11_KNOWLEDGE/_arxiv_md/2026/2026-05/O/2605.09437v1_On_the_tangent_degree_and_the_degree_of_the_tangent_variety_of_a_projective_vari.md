JORDI HERNANDEZ GOMEZ AND FRANCESCO RUSSO*
Dedicated to Ciro Ciliberto on the occasion of his 75th birthday
Abstract. The tangent degree τ(X) of a projective variety Xn ⊂PN is the number
of tangent spaces to X at smooth points passing through a general point of the tangent
variety Tan(X) ⊆PN, if positive and finite; it is equal to zero if dim(Tan(X)) < 2n.
In this paper we focus on general properties of τ(X) and of deg(Tan(X)). For example
τ(X) ̸= 1 if N = 2n and, as soon as Tan(X) does not coincide with the secant variety,
we prove a linear lower bound for the degree of Tan(X) in terms of its codimension in
the spirit of [6]. Then we consider the cases in which the previous two invariants attain
the lower bounds found here, either in small dimension/codimension and/or under the
smoothness assumption. Finally for N ≥2n + 1 we consider varieties Xn ⊂PN having
τ(X) > 1 and provide their classification in small dimension.
Introduction
The starting point of the topics we consider in this paper is the higher dimensional
generalization of the following elementary facts: if X ⊂P2 is an irreducible curve, then
τ(X) ≥2 if deg(X) ≥2 and τ(X) = 2 if and only if X is a conic. We first prove that
τ(X) ̸= 1 for an irreducible variety Xn ⊂P2n and that if τ(X) = 2, then X is birational to
its general tangent space via an explicit map (see Theorem 10 and Lemma 11) and hence
it is rational. Let us remark that if the singular locus of Xn ⊂P2n consists of at most a
finite number of nodes, then τ(X) ̸= 1 can be deduced in various ways. For example, under
these hypothesis Severi’s Double Point Formula in (15) implies that τ(X) is even (see also
Remark 13), which is clearly false for arbitrary singular varieties. This is analogous to the
case of plane curves, where Pl¨ucker’s Formulas imply that τ(X) is even for curves having
only nodes as singularities. Then we concentrate on the classification of varieties Xn ⊂P2n
with τ(X) = 2 and n ≥2. After showing the existence of (singular) rational scrolls in
P4 of arbitrary large degree having τ = 2 (see Example 18), we construct other (singular)
examples of arbitrary large dimension and degree, pointing out interesting connections with
notable classes of algebraic varieties like OADP-varieties, QEL-varieties of type δ = 1 or
twisted cubics over Jordan algebras (see Examples 12 and 17).

in P5 or X2 ⊂Y 3 ⊂PN with Y 3 a developable scroll in planes over a curve such that the
general plane intersects X in a curve of degree at least two. In the smooth case the previous
result is refined in Corollary 44, showing that there exist only two classes of examples: the
Veronese surface in P2 (τ = 2) and Roth surfaces X2 ⊂S(L, Z) ⊂PN of degree b(N −2)+1
for some b ≥2 and having τ = b2.
The main result of [11] is a characterization of Roth varieties as the unique smooth linearly
normal varieties Xn ⊂PN, N ≥n + 2, such that the double divisor of a general projection
onto Pn+1 is not ample. Mumford proved that these divisors vary in a base-point free linear
system (see [11, Introduction]). Corollary 44 provides a different geometric characterization
of Roth surfaces, leading to the natural question if this is also true for smooth linearly
normal non secant–defective varieties Xn ⊂PN with N ≥2n + 1 and n ≥3. As far as we
know this problem has not been previously addressed in the literature (neither for n = 2).
In the first section we present a general introduction to the theory of foci of the family
of tangent spaces to an algebraic variety and we collect various facts regarding the second
fundamental form of an algebraic variety and developable varieties. Since these results are
usually dispersed in the literature we thought that it would be useful to present them in an
unitary and concise way, without any pretension of completeness nor of originality and trying
to quote explicitly all the references we are aware of. This vast area of research, lying at
the crossroads of algebraic and differential geometry, has many long-standing contributions
so we apologize in advance if we forgot some of them.
