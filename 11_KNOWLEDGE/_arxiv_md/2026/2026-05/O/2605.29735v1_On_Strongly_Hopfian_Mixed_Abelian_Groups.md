ON STRONGLY HOPFIAN MIXED ABELIAN GROUPS
PETER V. DANCHEV AND PATRICK W. KEEF
Abstract. The classes of abelian groups that are (uniformly)
strongly Hopfian abelian groups, and dually, (uniformly) strongly
co-Hopfian abelian groups have been studied by several authors, in-
cluding Abdelalim (2015) and Abdelalim-Chillali-Essanouni (2015).
This paper extends these investivations in the case of (genuinely)
mixed groups. For example, it is shown that a reduced group that is
(uniformly) strongly co-Hopfian will always be (uniformly) strongly
Hopfian. In addition, a result of Chekhlov-Danchev (submitted)
characterizing when a torsion-free group is (uniformly) strongly
Hopfian is generalized to the case of (global) Warfield groups.
1. Introduction and Background
All groups considered and studied in the present research paper are
simultaneously additive and abelian; most of the basic notation and
terminology are in agreement with the classical books [7] and [8]. In
general, G will denote an abelian group with torsion T, and if p is
a prime, Tp will be its p-torsion subgroup; so T = ⊕pTp. As usual,
all “summand” means “direct summand”.
We will often use other
standard conventions, such as A ≤G to indicate that A is a subgroup
of G, A ≤⊕G to mean A is a summand of G, “rank” for “torsion-free
rank” and “iff” for “if and only if.”
A group G is called Hopfian iff it is not isomorphic to any of its proper
quotients; that is, each of its surjective endomorphisms is injective,
i.e., an automorphism. Mimicking [1], a group G is said to be strongly
Hopfian (or simply SH) provided that, for each endomorphism ϕ of G,
the ascending chain
ker ϕ ≤ker ϕ2 ≤· · · ≤ker ϕn ≤. . .
is eventually stationary, i.e., it stabilizes at some place.

If we let Gn := ϕn(G), then it is readily checked that G is SH iff,
for all endomorphisms ϕ : G →G, there is an integer n ∈N such that
ϕ restricts to an injection on Gn; we call such an n (if it exists) an
SH-exponent for ϕ. If this holds, then ϕ also restricts to an injection
on Gm ≤Gn for all m ≥n, so that ϕ restricts to an isomorphism
Gm ∼= Gm+1 and ker ϕn = ker ϕm for all m ≥n. In particular, if G is
SH and ϕ is surjective, then Gn = Gn+1 = G, so that we can conclude
that ϕ is an automorphism, i.e., G is Hopfian.
We say G is uniformly SH if there is a fixed n ∈N that is an
SH-exponent for all endomorphisms ϕ : G →G. Obviously, if G is
uniformly SH, then it is SH.
Dually, if the ascending chain ker ϕn is replaced by the descending
chain ϕn(G) = Gn, then we arrive at the notion of strongly co-Hopfian
(or simply Sco-H) groups, as well as uniformly Sco-H. The difference
between these two notions is well illustrated by the (easily checked)
fact that a torsion-free group of finite rank will always be SH (in fact,
uniformly SH), but it will be Sco-H iff it is divisible.
Characterizing any of the above four classes of groups quickly re-
duces to the case of reduced groups (see, for example, Corollary 2.9).
And if G is reduced and a member of any of these four classes, it will
follow that for any prime p, that Tp will be finite, and in particular,
it will be bounded (see, for example, Proposition 2.1). It follows from
Theorems 2.1 from both [1] and [2] that if G = T is reduced and tor-
sion, then it will be in any of these classes iff there is a fixed exponent
e ∈N such that for all primes p, Tp has at most pe elements (see also
Corollary 2.2).

rank 1 has a simple presentation, and is an element of B. It follows
that the class of Warfield group that are torsion-free coincides with the
completely decomposable groups.
The theory of Warfield groups makes extensive use of a category
called WALK, where two homomorphism f, g : H →G are considered
WALK-equivalent if (f −g)(H) ≤G (see, for e.g., [8, Section 15.8]).
This allows us to generalize the notion of the type of a rank one torsion-
free group to the WALK-type of an element of B. Using this, we are
able to characterize exactly when a Warfield group G whose torsion T is
SH also satisfies that property using the WALK-types of its summands
in a given decomposition of G into groups from B (Corollary 3.4). And
since a completely decomposable group is just a Warfield group with
T = 0 (which is obviously SH), this characteriation generalizes the
main result of [4] (see Corollary 3.5).
Deciding when a general Warfield group is SH, when we are not given
that T is SH, is a much more difficult problem. We provide an explicit
solution of only one case of that problem: when G has rank 1 and
each Tp is cyclic (Proposition 4.1). The difficulty of that computation
is evidence that giving a complete characterization of all groups that
are SH, especially those whose torsion is not SH, may ultimately be a
problem of insurmountable difficulty.
2. SH groups and sp-Groups
Often, for some fixed endomorphism ϕ : G →G and n ∈N, we will
denote ϕn(x) =: xn ∈Gn. A sequential way to express the definition
of SH groups is as follows:
A group G is SH iff, for each endomorphism ϕ : G →G, there is an
n ∈N such that for all x ∈G, if xn ̸= 0, then xm ̸= 0 for all m ≥n.
Again, a value n ∈N satisfying the above will be an SH-exponent
for the endomorphism ϕ, and a group G is SH if, and only if, every
map ϕ : G →G has an SH-index. Clearly, if n is an SH-exponent for
ϕ, then so is each natural number n′ ≥n.

3. SH groups and (global) Warfield groups
Recall that a group is Warfield if it is a summand of a simply pre-
sented group. In particular, it is clear that a summand of a Warfield
group will be Warfield. In addition, as any torsion-free rank 1 group
clearly has a simple presentation, any totally decomposable torsion-free
group is simply presented, and hence Warfield.
A perhaps more useful approach to Warfield groups involves the no-
tion of a nice decomposition basis, i.e., a subset {zi}i∈I ⊆G such that
Z = ⊕i∈I⟨zi⟩is a free and nice subgroup of G such that all p-height
functions restricted to Z respect the above decomposition. Then, G
is Warfield precisely when it has a nice decomposition base such that
G/Z is totally projective (see, for instance, [8, Theorem 15.7.6] as well
as, for a more detailed information, we refer the interested reader to
[10]). Fortunately, we are only concerned with Warfield groups with
bounded p-torsion, where the deep theory of (global) Warfield groups
is considerably more straightforward (compare with [6] as well).
