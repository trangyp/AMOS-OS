Abstract
Let X = SL3(R)/SO(3). Let DFR be the space of discrete faith-
ful representations of the modular group into Isom(X) which map the
order 2 generator to an isometry with a unique ﬁxed point. In this
paper, we prove that DFR has a component B, the so-called Barbot
component, that is homeomorphic to R2 ×[0, ∞). The boundary of B
parametrizes the Pappus representations and the interior consists of
Anosov representations.

Introduction
Let X = SL3(R)/SO(3).
This is a prototypical higher rank symmetric
space. In this paper we completely characterize one connected component of
the moduli space DFR of conjugacy classes of discrete and faithful represen-
tations of the modular group Z/3 ∗Z/2 into Isom(X) which map the order
2 elements to isometries having a unique ﬁxed point in X.
The Pappus representations are a 2-parameter subfamily of DFR which I
constructed in my 1993 paper [S0] and then revisited in my recent paper [S1].
These groups exhibit many features that, much later and more generally,
appeared in higher Teichmuller Theory, e.g. in [Lab], [GW], [Bar], [BCLS],
and [KL].
The Pappus representations are nowadays classiﬁed as relatively Anosov
groups in the Barbot component. This point of view is exposited in [BLV]

and [KL]. Let P ⊂DFR denote the subset consisting of Pappus modular

In [BLV], T. Barbot, G.-S. Lee, and V. P. Valerio build on [S0] and con-

terms of modiﬁed operations on marked boxes. Using their morphed marked
boxes (my terminology) they construct a 4-parameter family of representa-
tions of Z/3 ∗Z/3 into SL3(R), all of which are Anosov. They show that
a subset A of these extend to Anosov representations of the modular group
Z/3 ∗Z/2. They use an implicit function argument to show the existence of
A. Here I will complete the analysis.
Theorem 1.1 DFR has a connected component B which is homeomorphic
to R2×[0, ∞). The representations corresponding to R2×{0} are the Pappus
modular groups, and the representations corresponding to R2 × (0, ∞) are
Anosov representations.
Here is more information about B. We ﬁrst deﬁne a larger representation
space R of all suﬃciently generic representations of the group Z/3 ∗Z/2
into Isom(X) which map the order 2 element to an isometry having a unique
ﬁxed point in X. Here, suﬃciently generic means that the ﬁxed point of the
order 2 element is not contained in the ﬁxed set of the order 3 element. We
show that R is homeomorphic to R3 −{(0, 0, 0)} and that P is a properly
embedded plane in R, smooth away from one point. We show that B is the
closure of the component of R −P that does not contain the origin. The
boundary of B is exactly P.
Our proof of these results involves extending the analysis in [BLV] to fully
work out the set A. Our trick is to replace the transcendental parametrization
in [BLV] with a rational parametrization and then subject the resulting
formulas to computer algebra.

2.1
Projective Geometry
The projective plane P is the set of 1-dimensional subspaces of R3. The dual
plane P ∗is the set of 2-dimensional subspaces of R3. We represent points in
P in homogeneous coordinates. When c ̸= 0, the element [a : b : c] represents
(a/c, b/c) in the aﬃne patch. The aﬃne patch is a copy of R2 sitting inside
P . Any inner product g on R3 determines an analytic diﬀeomorphism from

ℓis the line extending the top edge. We deﬁne the bottom ﬂag similarly.
