On hamiltonian cycles of 1-tough (P2 ∪kP1)-free graphs
Masahiro Sanka∗
Department of Mathematics, Faculty of Science Division II,
Tokyo University of Science,
1-3 Kagurazaka, Shinjuku, Tokyo 162-8601, Japan
Abstract
Let k be a positive integer. A graph is said to be (P2 ∪kP1)-free if it does not
contain P2∪kP1 as an induced subgraph. Recently, Ota and the author asked whether
every 1-tough and k-connected (P2 ∪kP1)-free graph is hamiltonian or the Petersen
graph. Note that this problem is affirmative for k ∈{1, 2, 3} by the known results. In
this paper, we show that for each integer k ≥4, if G is a 1-tough and (k−1)-connected
(P2 ∪kP1)-free graph with |V (G)| ≥k2 + k + 1 and δ(G) ≥k, then G is hamiltonian.
This result implies that the above question is affirmative for large graphs.
Keywords. 1-tough graph; hamiltonian cycle; (P2 ∪kP1)-free graph

All graphs considered in this paper are finite and simple graphs. We use the terminology
not defined here in [8], and additional definitions will be given as needed.

It is well known that every hamiltonian graph is 1-tough, but the converse does not
hold. In 1973, Chv´atal conjectured that the converse holds at least in an approximate
sense.
Conjecture 1 ([6]). There exists a constant t0 > 0 such that every t0-tough graph on at
least three vertices is hamiltonian.
Bauer, Broersma and Veldman [2] showed that for any t <

For two graphs R1 and R2, we define R1 ∪R2 as the disjoint union of them. Also, for
a graph R and a positive integer m, we denote the disjoint union of m copies of R by mR.
For a graph R, we say that a graph G is R-free if it does not contain R as an induced
subgraph.
Conjecture 1 has been verified in various classes of graphs, such as chordal graphs
[5, 11], 2K2-free graphs [4, 15, 17], claw-free graphs [12], (P2 ∪P3)-free graphs [18], and
so on.
In this paper we consider the hamiltonicity of (P2 ∪kP1)-free graphs for each
positive integer k. Several results are known for this class of graphs, involving strengthened
toughness conditions [9, 10, 13, 14, 16, 19, 20]. In particular, Ota and the author proved
the following theorem.
Theorem A (Ota and Sanka [16]). Let k ≥2 be an integer and G be a 1-tough and
k-connected (P2 ∪kP1)-free graph. If δ(G) ≥3k−3

We discuss the sharpness of the connectivity and the minimum degree conditions in
Theorems B. For k = 2, Both Theorems A and B imply that every 1-tough and (P2∪2P1)-
free graph is hamiltonian, which is shown in [13]. For k = 3, Both Theorems A and B imply
that every 1-tough and 3-connected (P2 ∪3P1)-free graph is hamiltonian or isomorphic
to the Petersen graph. It is also known that there are infinitely many 1-tough and 2-
connected (but not 3-connected) (P2 ∪3P1)-free graph which are not hamiltonian. On
the other hand, for k ≥4, the sharpness of the connectivity and the minimum degree
conditions in Theorem B remains unknown. It has been noted that for each k ≥4, there
exist infinitely many 1-tough and (k −2)-connected (P2 ∪kP1)-free graphs that are not
hamiltonian.
In [16], Ota and the author states that the following problem might be
affirmative, which would constitute an extension of the classical theorem due to Chv´atal
and Erd˝os [7].
Problem 2 (Ota and Sanka [16]). For k ≥4, is every 1-tough and k-connected (P2∪kP1)-
free graph hamiltonian?
In this paper, we show the following theorem, which demonstrates that Problem 2 is
affirmative for large graphs.
Theorem 3. Let k ≥4 be an integer, and let G be a 1-tough and (k −1)-connected
(P2 ∪kP1)-free graph. If |V (G)| ≥k2 + k + 1 and δ(G) ≥k, then G is Hamiltonian.
In Theorem 3, the condition that the graph is k-connected is improved to a stronger
condition that the graph is (k −1)-connected with minimum degree at least k. For such
improvement, we will use the next theorem in the proof of Theorem 3. For a graph G and
a cycle C of G, we say that C is an edge-dominating cycle if every component of G−V (C)
consists of only one vertex.
Theorem 4. Let k ≥4 be an integer, and let G be a 1-tough and (k −1)-connected
(P2 ∪kP1)-free graph. Then, every longest cycle of G is an edge-dominating cycle of G.
Theorem 4 can be proven by using the proof method of the following theorem due to
Bigalke and Jung [3].
Theorem C (Bigalke and Jung [3]). Let G be a 1-tough graph with κ(G) ≥3. If α(G) ≤
κ(G) + 1, then G is hamiltonian or isomorphic to the Petersen graph.

## Page 4


In the proof of Theorem C, we consider a longest cycle C in the given graph G. If C
is not a hamiltonian cycle, then there is a component H of G −V (C). Then, if G is not
isomorphic to the Petersen graph, we can find an independent set X ⊂V (C)\NG(H) with
|X| ≥κ(G) + 1. However, then for a vertex h ∈V (H), the set X ∪{h} is an independent
set, a contradiction to α(G) ≤κ(G) + 1.
The proof of Theorem 4, which we only give its outline here, is quite similar to the
proof of Theorem C. For a 1-tough and (k−1)-connected (P2∪kP1)-free graph G, suppose
that there is a longest cycle C which is not an edge-dominating cycle. Then, there is a
component H of G −V (C) with |V (H)| ≥2. By considering the same way as the proof of
Theorem C, we can find an independent set X ⊂V (C) \ NG(H) with |X| ≥k. However,
then for an edge h1h2 ∈E(H), G[X ∪{h1, h2}] is isomorphic to P2 ∪kP1, a contradiction.
In this way, we obtain Theorem 4.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prepare several terms
and lemmas. In Section 3, we prove Theorem 3. Finally, in Section 4, we conclude the
paper and discuss open problems.

Remaining problems
In this paper, we have shown that for an integer k ≥4 and a 1-tough and (k−1)-connected
(P2 ∪kP1)-free graph G, if δ(G) ≥k and |V (G)| ≥k2 + k + 1, then G is hamiltonian. We
believe that Problem 2 is affirmative. Actually, for k ∈{4, 5}, we have succeeded to prove
that every 1-tough and (k −1)-connected (P2 ∪kP1)-free graph with minimum degree at
least k is hamiltonian. (We are preparing a paper on it now.)
Finally, we make some remarks on the minimum degree condition. Based on Theo-
rems 3 and C, there is a point of interest regarding the hamiltonicity of 1-tough and (k−1)-
connected (P2 ∪kP1)-free graph G with δ(G) = k −1. We have found a non-hamiltonian
1-tough and 3-connected (P2∪4P1)-free graph; an example is shown in Figure 1. However,
we do not find a large example. So we have the following problem.
