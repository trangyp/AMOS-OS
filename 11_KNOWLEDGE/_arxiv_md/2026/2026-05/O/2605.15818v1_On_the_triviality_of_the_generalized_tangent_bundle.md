Abstract
We study the relations between the triviality of the tangent bundle TM and the generalized
tangent bundle TM = TM ⊕T ∗M of a manifold. We show that the generalized tangent bundle
of a paralellizable manifold is trivial. We also prove that the converse implication does not hold,
by studying the cases of the M¨obius strip, spheres and projective spaces. Finally, we relate the
triviality of the generalized tangent bundle to generalized geometric structures.

Introduction
The generalized tangent bundle of a smooth manifold M, introduced by T. J. Courant in [4] and later
studied in depth by N. Hitchin, G. Cavalcanti and M. Gualtieri in [2,8,11], is defined as the Whitney
sum of its tangent and cotangent bundles, namely, TM := TM ⊕T ∗M →M. Sections of TM can
be seen as sums of vector fields and 1-forms, that is, Γ(TM) = X(M)⊕Ω1(M). Much of the existing
research in generalized geometry focuses on geometric structures on TM, such as generalized almost
complex and generalized almost paracomplex structures (see [7,12,14,15]). However, there appears
to be relatively little research focusing on specific manifolds; some studies that do address particular
manifolds include [3], concerning 4-dimensional manifolds, and [6], studying a version of the Hopf
problem on S6 in generalized geometry. Moreover, as far as we know, there are no studies relating
the topological properties of TM to those of the underlying manifold.
A noticeable topological property of a smooth manifold is its parallelizability. A vector bundle
E →M of rank k over a manifold M is said to be trivial if it is isomorphic as a vector bundle to

transforming these vector fields into 1-forms using an auxiliary Riemannian metric. We also show
that the generalized tangent bundle of any sphere is trivial. We then use Stiefel-Whitney classes to

2.1
Parallelizable manifolds
We begin by assuming that M is a real parallelizable n-dimensional smooth manifold (Hausdorff
and second countable).
Proposition 2.1. The generalized tangent bundle TM of a parallelizable manifold M is trivial.

Proof. Since M is parallelizable, there exist n vector fields X1, . . . , Xn ∈X(M) which are pointwise
linearly independent. Then, we can introduce n 1-forms using the isomorphism ♭g : X(M) →Ω1(M)
induced by a Riemannian metric g on M, such that (♭gX)Y = g(X, Y ) for every X, Y ∈X(M). The
differential forms ♭gX1, . . . , ♭gXn ∈Ω1(M) are also pointwise linearly independent. Thus, the 2n
sections X1, ♭gX1, X2, ♭gX2, . . . , Xn, ♭gXn ∈Γ(TM) are linearly independent at each point p ∈M,
which proves the result.
This result implies that the generalized tangent bundle of manifolds such as Lie groups or the
spheres S1, S3 and S7 is trivial. Other examples of parallelizable manifolds are listed in Table 1.

We study now the behavior of the generalized tangent bundle of the spheres Sn. It is widely
known that, even though TSn is trivial only for n = 1, 3, 7, it is always stably trivial: the Whitney
sum of TSn with the trivial bundle Sn × R is isomorphic to the trivial bundle Sn × Rn+1. The
following result, due to J. Allard [1], shows that the Whitney sum of several copies of a stably
trivial vector bundle may be trivial.

Relation of triviality with generalized geometric structures
As is well known, the triviality of the tangent bundle of a manifold entails significant topological
consequences. In particular, the manifold must be orientable, and if its dimension is even, it admits
an almost complex structure. Our aim is to establish analogous results concerning the triviality of
the generalized tangent bundle.

constitutues an important difference with respect to TM. However, geometric structures can still

a weak generalized almost complex structure, i.e., a vector bundle endomorphism J : TM →TM
