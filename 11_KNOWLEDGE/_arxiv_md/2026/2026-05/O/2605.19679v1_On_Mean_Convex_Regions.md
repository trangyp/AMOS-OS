ON MEAN CONVEX REGIONS
HAOTIAN XUE
Abstract. We prove that a connected mean convex region in ℝ𝑛+1 with at least two components
cannot have strictly positive mean curvature. This answers a question of Gromov. We also obtain
estimates for how quickly the mean curvature must decay at infinity, and generalize this result
to hyperbolic space.
1. Introduction
The celebrated halfspace theorem [HM90] by Hoffman and Meeks implies that in ℝ3, a mean
convex region with two boundary components is the slab between two parallel planes. This fails in
higher dimensions, since a catenoid of dimension greater than 3 could fit between parallel planes.
In this paper we study a variant of this problem where the boundary components are assumed to
have mean curvature with positive lower bounds. In particular, we prove
Theorem 1.1. Let Ω be a connected mean convex region in ℝ𝑛+1 whose boundary has at least two
components. Then each boundary component Σ is non-compact and inf 𝐻Σ = 0, where 𝐻Σ is the
mean curvature of Σ with respect to inward normals.
This resolves a conjecture by Gromov ([Gro20], conjecture 1). Moreover, it can be viewed as
a analog of the Frankel property, i.e. there do not exist two disjoint closed minimal surface in
a positive curvature space [Fra61].
Here we trade the positivity of background curvature with
positivity of boundary curvature.
Remark. We note that Jian Ge has recently independently obtained this result [Ge26] using a
related method. In this paper we also prove a quantitative estimate for the curvature decay rate,
as well as a generalization to hyperbolic space.
