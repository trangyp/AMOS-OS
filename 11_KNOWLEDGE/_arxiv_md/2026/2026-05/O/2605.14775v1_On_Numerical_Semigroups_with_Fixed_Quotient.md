ON NUMERICAL SEMIGROUPS WITH FIXED QUOTIENT
I. OJEDA AND J.C. ROSALES
Abstract. Let ∆be a numerical semigroup and let d ≥2 be an integer. We study
the fiber of the quotient map S 7→S/d over ∆. We describe its elements as semigroups
of the form ⟨X⟩+ d∆, for suitable finite sets X ⊆∆, and then analyze explicit and
computable regions of this fiber. In particular, we introduce a family ∆d(a) of multiples
with prescribed quotient and compute its generators, classical invariants, Ap´ery sets,
and presentations. We also show that this construction preserves Wilf’s inequality and
controls the depth. Finally, we introduce the Md(∆)-rank, determine its maximal value
in the fiber, relate it to the ordinary embedding dimension, characterize the rank-one ele-
ments, and give closed formulas for their Frobenius-type invariants and pseudo-Frobenius
numbers.
1. Introduction
Let N = {0, 1, 2, . . . } and let Z be the set of integers. A numerical semigroup is a
submonoid S ⊆(N, +) with finite complement N \ S. We write m(S) = min(S \ {0}) for
the multiplicity, F(S) = max(Z \ S) for the Frobenius number, and g(S) = |N \ S| for the
genus. Every numerical semigroup has a unique minimal system of generators, denoted
by msg(S); its cardinality is the embedding dimension e(S); see [8, Theorem 2.7]. The
classical Frobenius problem asks for expressions of F(S) and g(S) in terms of msg(S);
it is completely solved in embedding dimension two and remains widely open in general
[11, 9].
If A ⊆N and d ≥1, we write A/d = {x ∈N : dx ∈A}. For a numerical semigroup S,
the quotient S/d is again a numerical semigroup [10]. We say that T is a multiple of S if
T/d = S for some d ≥1. Multiples and quotients appear naturally in several constructions
and have been studied from different viewpoints, including arithmetic extensions and
related closure processes; see, for instance, [3, 1, 7].
In this paper we fix a numerical semigroup ∆̸= N and an integer d ≥2, and we study
the fiber
Md(∆) = {S : S is a numerical semigroup and S/d = ∆}.
Equivalently, if πd denotes the map on the set of numerical semigroups defined by πd(S) =
S/d, then Md(∆) = π−1
d (∆). In concrete terms, S ∈Md(∆) if and only if S ∩dN = d∆.
Thus the elements of S divisible by d are prescribed, and the remaining residue classes
modulo d are to be determined. Our guiding question is structural and effective at the
same time: how explicitly can one describe the whole family Md(∆), and how do classical
invariants vary along it? Fixing ∆imposes strong congruence constraints modulo d, and
these constraints turn out to be rigid enough to yield an explicit model for the fiber.

Our approach has two complementary strands.
First, we describe the whole fiber
Md(∆) in structural terms. We prove that its elements are precisely the numerical semi-
groups of the form ⟨X⟩+ d∆, where X ⊆∆is finite and satisfies explicit compatibility
conditions (Theorem 2.11). This is done by introducing Md(∆)-monoids and exploiting
their stability under finite intersections.
Second, we study tractable subfamilies and a rank filtration inside this fiber.
The
structural description shows that an arbitrary element of Md(∆) is obtained from d∆
by adjoining finitely many elements outside d∆; for arbitrary such choices one should
not expect simple closed formulas for the usual invariants. We therefore begin with a
particularly uniform construction: given an integer d ≥2 and an element a ∈∆\ {0}
with a+1 ∈∆, we construct a multiple ∆d(a) ∈Md(∆) (Theorem 3.1). This construction
adds one generator in each nonzero residue class modulo d, and it is explicit enough to yield
closed formulas for the embedding dimension, multiplicity, Frobenius number, genus, and
related quantities (Theorem 3.4), as well as descriptions of Ap´ery sets and presentations.
As an application of these formulas, we show that Wilf’s inequality is preserved under the
family ∆d(a) (Corollary 3.6), providing a systematic way to generate further examples
from known cases.
In connection with the recent terminology of [1], recall that a numerical semigroup
is a k-quotient if it can be written as T/d with e(T) = k, and the minimum such k
is the quotient rank. Our results around Corollary 3.3 show that the set of embedding
dimensions occurring among multiples of a fixed semigroup is upward closed above the
quotient rank. Moreover, k-quotients admit an equivalent polyhedral description in terms
of projections of cones with k extreme rays [2].
The same viewpoint also suggests an intrinsic way to measure how many generators are
needed outside d∆within a fixed fiber. For M/d = ∆, the Md(∆)-rank of M is defined
as the number of generators needed outside d∆in the unique minimal Md(∆)-system of
generators of M. We show that this rank filtration has a finite top layer: its maximal
value on Md(∆) is (d −1)m(∆).
We also relate the relative rank with the ordinary
embedding dimension by describing exactly which generators of d∆remain minimal after
adjoining the relative generators. Thus the construction ∆d(a) gives a uniform family of
Md(∆)-rank d −1; in general this is not maximal, but it provides one generator in each
nonzero residue class modulo d. In particular, for d = 2 it lies in the rank-one layer. The
last section analyzes this first genuinely numerical layer of the fiber in full generality. We
characterize the rank-one elements (Theorem 4.12) and, in the coprime case gcd(x, d) = 1,
derive closed formulas for Frobenius-type invariants and pseudo-Frobenius numbers for
semigroups of the form ⟨x⟩+ d∆(Theorems 4.14 and 4.15).
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 establishes the structural description of
Md(∆) via Md(∆)-monoids and finite-intersection properties. Section 3 develops the
explicit construction ∆d(a) and computes its invariants, Ap´ery sets, and presentations.
Section 4 introduces Md(∆)-rank, analyzes rank-one elements, and derives Frobenius-type
and pseudo-Frobenius data in that setting.
2. Structural description of Md(∆)
Throughout the rest of the paper, unless explicitly stated otherwise, ∆̸= N and d ≥2
are fixed as in the introduction.
This section contains the structural core of the paper. We show that the family Md(∆)
admits a concrete description in terms of associated monoids and, ultimately, semigroups
of the form ⟨X⟩+ d∆. This characterization is the key step in turning the fixed-quotient
problem into an effective one.

Definition 2.1. A submonoid M of (N, +) is called a Md(∆)-monoid if M/d = ∆.
We begin with a basic observation.
Lemma 2.2. If M is a submonoid of (N, +) and d ≥1, then M/d is also a submonoid
of (N, +).
Proof. Since 0 ∈M, we have d · 0 = 0 ∈M, and therefore 0 ∈M/d. Now let x, y ∈M/d.
Then dx ∈M and dy ∈M. As M is closed under addition, d(x + y) = dx + dy ∈M.
Hence x + y ∈M/d. Therefore M/d is a submonoid of (N, +).
□
Proposition 2.3. A submonoid M of (N, +) is a Md(∆)-monoid if and only if d∆⊆M
and M ∩d(N \ ∆) = ∅.
Proof. If M/d = ∆, then x ∈∆implies dx ∈M, so d∆⊆M. If dn ∈M for some n /∈∆,
then n ∈M/d = ∆, a contradiction. Conversely, assume d∆⊆M and M ∩d(N\∆) = ∅.
For every x ∈∆we have dx ∈M, hence x ∈M/d. If x ∈M/d, then dx ∈M, and by the
second condition x /∈N \ ∆. Therefore x ∈∆.
□
Lemma 2.4. If A and B are submonoids of (N, +), then A+B = {a+b : a ∈A, b ∈B}
is also a submonoid of (N, +).
Proof. Because 0 ∈A and 0 ∈B, we have 0 = 0 + 0 ∈A + B. If x, y ∈A + B, then there
exist a1, a2 ∈A and b1, b2 ∈B such that x = a1 + b1 and y = a2 + b2. Since A and B are
submonoids, a1 +a2 ∈A and b1 +b2 ∈B. Therefore x+y = (a1 +a2)+(b1 +b2) ∈A+B.
Thus A + B is a submonoid of (N, +).
□
Proposition 2.5. The family of all Md(∆)-monoids equals
{A + d∆: A is a submonoid of (N, +), A ∩d(N \ ∆) = ∅}.
Proof. If M is a Md(∆)-monoid, then M = M + d∆because d∆⊆M. Setting A = M,
the previous proposition gives the desired inclusion. Conversely, if A is a submonoid with
A ∩d(N \ ∆) = ∅, then A + d∆is a submonoid by the previous lemma, contains d∆.
Moreover it still avoids d(N \ ∆): indeed, if dn ∈A + d∆with n /∈∆, then dn = a + dδ
with a ∈A and δ ∈∆, so a = d(n −δ). Since a ∈A ⊆N, we must have n −δ ∈N.
Since δ ∈∆and ∆is a submonoid, n /∈∆forces n −δ /∈∆, hence a ∈A ∩d(N \ ∆), a
contradiction. Therefore (A+d∆)∩d(N\∆) = ∅, and A+d∆is an Md(∆)-monoid.
□
Proposition 2.6. The family of Md(∆)-monoids is closed under finite intersections.
Proof. Finite intersections of submonoids of (N, +) are submonoids. Let M and N be
Md(∆)-monoids. For x ∈N,

Lemma 2.7. If M is a Md(∆)-monoid, then M ⊆∆.
Proof. If x ∈M, then dx ∈M because M is a submonoid. Hence x ∈M/d = ∆.

some numerical semigroup T with e(T) = k and some d ≥1; the minimum such k is

k extreme rays.

upward closed in k. In contrast, computing the threshold emin (i.e., the quotient rank)
remains open in general; see, for instance, [7].

□

Recall that PF(S) = {x ∈Z \ S : x + s ∈S for all s ∈S \ {0}} and t(S) = | PF(S)|.
