Abstract
The Erd˝os–Rado sunflower problem admits two natural analogues in finite
vector spaces, corresponding to two different ways of generalising the set-theoretic
notion of a sunflower. The first, used by Ihringer and Kupavskii [FFA 110 (2026)
102746], requires the petals to be in general position over the kernel; the second,
used in the subspace codes literature (cf. Etzion–Raviv [DAM 186 (2015) 87-97],
Blokhuis–De Boeck–D’haeseleer [DCC 90 (2022) 2101-2111]), requires only that
the kernel equals the pairwise intersection of distinct petals. We refer to the
second version as a set-like sunflower, following Ihringer and Kupavskii.
In this note, we focus on the set-like setting. We observe that the construc-
tions of Ihringer–Kupavskii, although correct under their (stronger) definition,
do not yield set-like sunflower-free families: we exhibit explicit set-like sun-
flowers inside their Example 3.1. We then present a construction of set-like
s-sunflower-free families of k-spaces, based on a manipulated version of the
lifting construction. To our knowledge, this is the first systematic construction
tailored to this setting.
MSC: 05D05, 51E23.
Keywords: sunflower, ∆-system, subspace code, vector space, q-analogue,
MRD code, lifting construction.

Introduction
A family of s sets S1, . . . , Ss is called an s-sunflower (or ∆(s)-system) with kernel K
if K = Si ∩Sj for all distinct i, j.12 A family of k-sets is s-sunflower-free if no s of
its members form an s-sunflower. In 1960, Erd˝os and Rado [8] proved that for s ≥3
every s-sunflower-free family of k-sets has size at most k!(s −1)k, and conjectured a
much sharper bound:
Conjecture 1 (Erd˝os–Rado sunflower conjecture). For every s ≥3 there exists a
constant C such that every s-sunflower-free family of k-sets F satisfies |F| ≤(Cs)k.

1.1
Sunflowers in finite vector spaces
Let q be a prime power, Fq the finite field with q elements, and Fn
q the n-dimensional
vector space over Fq. When generalising the notion of a sunflower to finite vector
spaces, two natural choices arise.
Definition 3 (General-position sunflower; Ihringer–Kupavskii [11]). A family S1, . . . , Ss
of k-spaces of Fn
q is a (general-position) s-sunflower with kernel K if there exists
a d-space K with K = Si ∩Sj for all distinct i, j, and S1/K, . . . , Ss/K are in
general position, i.e. dim(S1/K + · · · + Ss/K) = Ps
i=1 dim(Si/K). Equivalently,
dim(S1 + · · · + Ss) = d + s(k −d).
Definition 4 (Set-like sunflower; cf. Etzion–Raviv [10]). A family S1, . . . , Ss of
k-spaces of Fn
q is a set-like s-sunflower with kernel K if K = Si ∩Sj for all distinct
i, j.
The terminology set-like sunflower is due to Ihringer and Kupavskii [11], who note
that a parallel literature focusing only on pairwise intersections has been developed in
the context of subspace codes; see, for example, Etzion and Raviv [10] and Blokhuis,
De Boeck and D’haeseleer [4].
Every general-position sunflower is a set-like sunflower, but not conversely. The
two definitions therefore lead to two distinct extremal problems. In the set case the
two notions coincide, because three sets pairwise intersecting in a common set K
automatically satisfy the analogous general-position condition; in the vector-space
case they do not, due to the failure of the inclusion-exclusion principle for sums of
three or more subspaces (see Section 3).
A family of k-spaces is (general-position) s-sunflower-free (respectively, set-like
s-sunflower-free) if it contains no s-sunflower in the corresponding sense. Since
a set-like sunflower is a more permissive notion, set-like sunflower-freeness is the
stronger property: every set-like s-sunflower-free family is also general-position
s-sunflower-free, but not conversely.

The role of inclusion-exclusion
For two subspaces U, V of a finite-dimensional vector space,
dim(U + V ) = dim U + dim V −dim(U ∩V ),
mirroring the inclusion-exclusion identity for two sets. The analogue fails for three
subspaces: in general,
dim(U + V + W) ̸= dim U + dim V + dim W
−dim(U ∩V ) −dim(U ∩W) −dim(V ∩W) + dim(U ∩V ∩W).
For example, take U = span {(1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0)}, V = span {(1, 0, 0), (0, 0, 1)}, W =
span {(1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 1)} in F3

Each pair intersects in span {(1, 0, 0)}, the triple
intersection is the same line, yet U + V + W = F3

the right-hand side above evaluates to 2 + 2 + 2 −1 −1 −1 + 1 = 4.
This failure is precisely what separates Definitions 3 and 4. In a general-position
s-sunflower the identity dim(S1 + · · · + Ss) = d + s(k −d) holds by definition (the
general-position condition is exactly this equality). In a set-like sunflower no such
identity is required, and the sum dimension may be strictly less than d + s(k −d).

5
Concluding remarks and open problems
The construction in Theorem 11 produces set-like s-sunflower-free families of size
qn−k. This is far below the upper bound Qk
i=1[i(s −1)]q inherited from [11], which
applies to the set-like setting as well: the proof of that upper bound (a Erd˝os–Rado-
style pigeonhole over 1-subspaces) uses only the pairwise intersection structure of a
sunflower and therefore goes through verbatim under Definition 4.
Several natural questions remain:
6


## Page 7


1. Order of magnitude. What is the order of magnitude of the largest set-like
s-sunflower-free family of k-spaces of Fn
q ? Is it polynomially, or exponentially,
smaller than the largest general-position s-sunflower-free family?
2. Iterated constructions. Can the construction of Theorem 11 be iterated or
nested, in the spirit of the iterative MRD-nesting of [11], in a way that preserves
set-like sunflower-freeness?
3. Dependence on n. The set-like problem is genuinely dimension-dependent (as
is also the case for the related sunflower-bound problems studied in [4]). For fixed
k and s, what is the dependence of the extremal size on n?
4. Bridging the two definitions. Is there a natural intermediate notion (for
instance, requiring general position only up to triples, or only on the level of r-fold
sums for fixed r) that interpolates between the two regimes?
