ROMAN DRNOVŠEK AND MARKO KANDIĆ
Abstract. In this paper we introduce the concept of a nonnegative rank of
a positive operator T : X →Y between ordered vector spaces.
In the case
of nonnegative matrices, our definition agrees with the standard definition of
a nonnegative rank. Under some natural and mild assumptions on the cone
Y+, we prove that the nonnegative rank and the rank agree whenever the rank
is at most two. This can be considered as the infinite-dimensional version of
[CR93, Theorem 4.1].
We also provide an example of a positive rank-three
operator on the Banach lattice C[0, 1] with an infinite nonnegative rank.exceed
⌈6 min{m, n}/7⌉.
1. Introduction and preliminaries
Let T be a nonnegative m × n matrix. Gregory and Pullman introduced in
[GP83] the nonnegative rank rank+(T) of T as the smallest nonnegative integer
k such that T can be expressed as a sum of k rank-one nonnegative matrices.
Equivalently, k is the nonnegative rank of T if and only if there exist nonnegative
matrices L ∈Rm×k and R ∈Rk×n such that T = LR, where k is minimal with
this property (see, for example, [CR93, Corollary 2.2]). It should be clear that
the nonnegative rank of a nonnegative m × n matrix is at most min{m, n}. This
upper bound can be reduced. In particular, Shitov proved in [Shi14, Theorem
3.2] that the nonnegative rank of a nonnegative m × n matrix does not exceed
⌈6 min{m, n}/7⌉.
If rank(T) ≤2, then [CR93, Theorem 4.1] implies that rank+(T) = rank(T). In
contrast, for matrices of rank three, the nonnegative rank can be strictly larger.
Indeed, Robbins (see [CR93]) provided an example of a nonnegative 4 × 4 matrix
of rank three

Let X be a vector space and let C be a cone in X. The cone C is generated by
a set S if every vector x ∈C can be written in the form x = λ1s1 + · · · + λnsn for

cone. For the terminology and details not explained here we refer the reader to
[LZ71], [AB06], [AA02] and [AT07].
