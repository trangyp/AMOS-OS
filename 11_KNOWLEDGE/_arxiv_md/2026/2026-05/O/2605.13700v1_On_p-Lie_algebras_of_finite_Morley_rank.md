On p-Lie algebras of finite Morley rank
Samuel Zamour
May 14, 2026
Abstract
We develop the theory of p-Lie algebras of finite Morley rank.
In
particular, we obtain a quite complete characterization in the soluble case.
1
Introduction
Lie rings have recently attracted renewed interest from model theorists, as ev-
idenced by the articles [5], [6] and [18]. An ambitious program for the clas-
sification of Lie rings of finite Morley rank has notably been set in [5]; from
this perspective, the ultimate goal would be to obtain a form of blueprint of
the classification of simple Lie algebras of finite dimension over an algebraically
closed field of positive characteristic (see [11], [12] and [13]). This is therefore
the counterpart for Lie rings of the ongoing classification of simple groups of
finite Morley rank; the following conjecture, analogous to the Cherlin–Zilber
conjecture, is the guiding line of this research program:
Conjecture 1.1. [5, “Logarithmic” Cherlin–Zilber Conjecture] Let g be a simple
Lie ring of characteristic p > 0. If p is sufficiently large, then g is isomorphic
to a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a (definable) algebraically closed field of
positive characteristic.
The Morley rank can be thought of as a dimension function on definable
sets, generalising in a certain sense the linear dimension. It admits the following
axiomatisation:
• Axiom A (Monotonicity): Let A be a definable set. Then MR(A) ≥n+1
if and only if A contains infinitely many pairwise disjoint definable subsets
of rank greater than or equal to n.
• Axiom B (Definability): If f : A →B is a definable function between A
and B, then for every integer n, the set
{b ∈B : MR(f −1(b)) = n}
is definable.
• Axiom C (Additivity): If f : A →B is a surjective definable function, and
if MR(f −1(b)) = n for all b ∈B, then MR(A) = n + MR(B).
• Axiom D (Elimination of the infinite quantifier): For every definable func-
tion f : A →B, there exists an integer m such that for every b ∈B, the
fibre f −1(b) is infinite as soon as it contains at least m elements.

Regarding groups of finite Morley rank, we refer the reader to [3] and [1] .
An essential step in the classification of finite-dimensional modular Lie algebras
was the understanding of the structure of simple p-Lie algebras (or restricted
Lie algebras) (see in particular [2]). In the case of a p-Lie algebra, one adds a
“raising to the power p” function to the Lie structure; for matrix Lie algebras
in positive characteristic, this really is the usual raising to the power p for ma-
trices. Note that in positive characteristic, the “classical” simple Lie algebras
associated with algebraic groups are p-restricted; moreover, a number of simple
Lie algebras of “Cartan type”, which are finite-dimensional analogues in positive
characteristic of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras of characteristic zero, are also
p-restricted (for example, the Witt algebra).
Analogously, the classification of p-Lie algebras of finite Morley rank seems
an indispensable prerequisite to significant progress towards the “logarithmic”
Cherlin–Zilber conjecture. In this article, we therefore propose to lay the foun-
dations for the study of p-Lie algebras of finite Morley rank. In particular, we
obtain a fairly exhaustive characterisation in the abelian, nilpotent and soluble
cases. We also clarify the articulation between semisimplicity and unipotence,
which seemed to be lacking in the case of abstract Lie rings without p-structure.
We prove in particular the following results:
Theorem 1.2. Let g be a p-Lie algebra of finite Morley rank and let t be a
torus that embeds definably as a p-Lie algebra into (K, +), where K is a definable
field of characteristic p > 0. Let V be a definable connected p-module. Then
V = V t⊕V1⊕· · ·⊕Vr, where each Vi is a definable connected irreducible t-module
(in the definable connected category) and V t = {v ∈V : [t, v] = 0}.
Theorem 1.3. Let g be a connected soluble non-nilpotent p-Lie algebra of finite
Morley rank such that p > MR(g). Then the following properties hold:
1. g has non-trivial tori.
2. c is a Cartan subalgebra if and only if h = Cg(t) where t is a non-trivial
maximal torus.
3. g = g′ + c for every Cartan subalgebra c.
In the second section, we present several algebraic and model-theoretic tools
for studying p-Lie algebras of finite Morley rank. The third section deals with
the structure of abelian and nilpotent p-Lie algebras; in particular, we introduce
an appropriate notion of “torus” and establish its basic properties. We then
move in Section 4 to the case of soluble p-Lie algebras; we clarify their internal
structure and develop the Frattini theory in this context. Finally, in a brief
fifth section, we formulate a classification program for “minimal” simple p-Lie
algebras.

Definition 3.18. Let g be a p-algebra of finite Morley rank.

3.3
The nilpotent case
In general, one cannot decompose a nilpotent p-algebra as a central product of
a torus and a p-nilpotent p-algebra. Nevertheless, we have the following result:
Proposition 3.22. Let n be a connected nilpotent p-Lie algebra of finite Morley
rank. Then every torus is central.
Proof. We proceed by induction on the rank of n. Let t be a torus of n. We
may assume that n is not abelian and we apply the induction hypothesis to
n/Z(n). Thus, t ⊆Z(n/Z(n)); in other words, t ⊆Z2(n). But by Proposition
3.15, n = nt +[n, t] = Cn(t)+[n, t]; now [n, t] ⊆Z(n) and therefore t ≤Z(n).
In the case where the p-algebra is “sufficiently” nilpotent, we obtain stronger
structure theorems.
Lemma 3.23. Let g be a connected p-algebra of finite Morley rank. If a torus
t is contained in a nilpotent ideal of nilpotency class less than or equal to p −1,
then t is central in g.
Proof. Since g = gt + [t, g], it suffices to verify that [t, g] = 0. Now, adp
t (x) =
[tp, x] = 0 for all t ∈t and we conclude by p-divisibility of t.
Proposition 3.24. Let n be a connected nilpotent p-algebra of nilpotency class
≤p of finite Morley rank. Then n = t(+)u where t is a central torus and u is a
p-nilpotent p-subgroup.
Proof. Since n is nilpotent of nilpotency class ≤p, np is a p-subalgebra contained
in Z(n). If np = 0, then n is p-nilpotent; otherwise, np contains the maximal
torus of n. Thus, there exists i ≥1 such that npi is the maximal torus. Now,
ker(ϕi) is a p-subgroup of n; moreover, ker(ϕi) ∩im(ϕi) is finite by virtue of the
structure theorem for abelian p-algebras. Thus, the additivity of the dimension
gives us n = npi(+) ker(ϕi).

Minimal simple p-algebras
We indicate how the preceding results could contribute to a classification project
for simple p-algebras of finite Morley rank that are “minimal” in a sense we will
now make precise.
Let us first recall the construction of the Witt algebra W(1, 1). We consider
the algebra of truncated polynomials A = K[X]/(Xp) = K[x], for K a field of
characteristic p > 0; we define the Witt algebra as the algebra of derivations
of A: this is a p-Lie algebra with the usual bracket for endomorphisms, where
the p-map simply corresponds to raising to the power p. Now, a derivation D
is entirely determined by the value D(x); denoting δ =
d
dx, we therefore have a
natural basis given by ei = xi+1δ for i ∈{−1, . . . , p −2}. In this case, the only
semisimple element of this basis is e0, which therefore generates an excellent
torus of dimension 1; moreover, ep

generate a copy of sl(2, K). The classification of subalgebras of W(1, 1) given
in [10] easily implies the following property: let b be a non-trivial soluble sub-
algebra; then NW (b) is soluble.
We are led to the following definition.

is simple, if MR(g) ≤p, and if for any non-trivial definable connected soluble
sub-p-algebra b, N ◦
