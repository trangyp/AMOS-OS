YUUYA YOSHIDA
Abstract. We optimize the trade-oﬀbetween privacy and utility in the high-privacy
regime. We adopt local diﬀerential privacy (LDP) and its quantum extension, quan-
tum local diﬀerential privacy (QLDP), for privacy protection, and investigate utility
functions including the Holevo information (which reduces to the mutual information
in the classical case) and the error exponents in symmetric and asymmetric hypothe-
sis testing. These utility functions have classical and quantum optimal values, which
are denoted by C and Q, respectively, in this abstract for simplicity. In this paper,
we provide optimal LDP and QLDP mechanisms achieving the classical and quantum
optimal values in the high-privacy regime, and prove that the asymptotic ratio Q/C in
this regime takes the same value regardless of the utility function. Our results reveal
quantum advantages (more precisely, Q/C ≥3/2) for the above utility functions when
the protected private data are n-ary with n ≥3.
1. Introduction
With the rapid advancement and pervasiveness of information technology in daily
life, it has become increasingly important to utilize private data while ensuring their
protection. In general, data utility and privacy protection stand in a trade-oﬀrelation,
which needs to be optimized.
In particular, diﬀerential privacy (DP) has attracted
attention as a rigorous framework for privacy protection, and various optimization
problems under the DP constraint have been extensively studied [13–15, 17, 20, 21, 27,
29,36,37]. Originally, DP was introduced in the context of global privacy [6,7], where a
trusted data collector (e.g., a corporation or government) releases statistical results or
synthetic data based on users’ data. Since then, this framework has evolved to support
private machine learning. Subsequently, the concept of DP was extended to the context
of local privacy [5], where data providers do not trust the data collector. This paper
focuses on the local privacy setting. Speciﬁcally, the data providers convert their private

information about X, such as its probability distribution or its expectation. Here, the

As stated above, it is a central line of research in DP to optimize the trade-oﬀbe-
tween privacy and utility. More speciﬁcally, the objective is to maximize the estimation
accuracy for the data collector while simultaneously protecting the private data X via
LDP. If this estimation accuracy is represented as a real-valued function Φ called a
utility function, the above optimization problem can be formulated as maximizing Φ
under the LDP constraint. Such optimization problems have been extensively stud-
ied within the framework of classical probability theory [13–15, 20, 21]. For instance.
Kairouz et al. [21] proved that the above optimization problem can be reduced to a
linear programming problem when Φ can be expressed as a sum of values of a sublinear
function. Except for special cases, further simpliﬁcation is not known, and it is diﬃcult
to solve the above optimization problem analytically and generally.
Furthermore, DP was introduced into quantum information theory, and quantum
extensions of the aforementioned optimization problem have been studied [17,27,29,36,
37]. Here, we consider the case of utilizing quantum states ρx, x ∈X , instead of the
classical data Y . Speciﬁcally, the data providers convert their private data X = x into
quantum states ρx, and then the data collector estimates certain information about X
by measuring these quantum states. In this case, the counterpart of LDP is a condition
for the collection of quantum states (ρx)x∈X, and is increasingly referred to as quantum
local diﬀerential privacy (QLDP). This quantum extension mathematically encompasses
the classical setting, since for each x ∈X the probability distribution PY |X(·|x) can be
identiﬁed with a diagonal matrix, which corresponds to a quantum state. Consequently,
the classical optimal value never exceeds the quantum optimal value in this framework.
This naturally raises the question whether the quantum optimal value strictly exceeds
its classical counterpart, i.e., whether there is a quantum advantage.
Against this background, Yoshida and Hayashi [37] proved that there is no quantum
advantage when the private data are binary, i.e., |X | = 2. On the other hand, when
|X | ≥3, Yoshida [36] proved a quantum advantage for a concrete utility function
Φ constructed by using the right logarithmic derivative (RLD) Fisher information of
a one-parameter family. Using this result, he evaluated the privacy loss incurred in
representing a QLDP mechanism as the image of a classical LDP mechanism by a
quantum channel. However, although his utility function Φ satisﬁes the data processing
inequality, it was not derived from a concrete information-processing scenario.

1.1. Main contributions. In this paper, we optimize the error exponents in sym-
metric and asymmetric hypothesis testing under the LDP and QLDP constraints, and
provide optimal LDP and QLDP mechanisms, in the high-privacy regime.
Conse-
quently, we show quantum advantages for the two error exponents in this regime when
|X | ≥3. Notably, when |X | ≥10, this work is the ﬁrst to reveal quantum advantages
in concrete information-processing scenarios. Furthermore, where the results of Nam et
al. [27] are available, i.e., when |X | ∈{3, 4, . . . , 9} (resp. |X | ∈{4, 9}), we resolve the
optimality question in the symmetric (resp. asymmetric) case. Speciﬁcally, our quan-
tum optimal value strictly improves upon their value when |X | ∈{5, 6, . . ., 9} (resp.
|X | = 9), while coinciding with their value when |X | ∈{3, 4} (resp. |X | = 4). We also
analytically clarify the privacy levels (i.e., the ranges of the parameter ε) leading to
quantum advantages.
Going beyond the two error exponents mentioned above, we reveal (i) both the clas-
sical and quantum optimal values and (ii) both optimal LDP and QLDP mechanisms,
in the high-privacy regime, for a broader class of utility functions satisfying certain
conditions. Hence, we demonstrate quantum advantages for this class when |X | ≥3.
Speciﬁcally, we assume that a classical utility function ΦC can be expressed as a sum of
values of a symmetric sublinear function, which corresponds to a symmetric variant of
the assumption of Kairouz et al. [21]. This class of classical utility functions contains
many information-theoretic quantities, such as mutual information and the sum of pair-
wise f-divergences. To verify the existence of a quantum advantage, the above utility
function ΦC must be extended to the quantum setting. To ensure the ﬂexibility and
broad applicability of our results, rather than assuming a speciﬁc form for a quantum
extension ΦQ of ΦC, we instead assume the form of the quadratic term of the Taylor
expansion of ΦQ. This class of utility functions contains many information-theoretic
quantities, such as Holevo information and the sum of pairwise Petz f-divergences. For
such utility functions ΦQ and ΦC, the asymptotic ratio of the classical and quantum
optimal values in the high-privacy regime takes the same value regardless of ΦQ and
ΦC. A main theorem of ours provides a uniﬁed explanation for this phenomenon.

6. Conclusion and future work
We have constructed the isoclinic mechanism ⃗σ∗, and have shown that it is an op-
timal ε-QLDP mechanism achieving the quantum optimal value OPTn(ε; ΦQ) in the
high-privacy regime. For the isoclinic mechanism ⃗σ∗, the minimum dimension of the
underlying space Cd is equal to 2a+1, where a = max{0, ⌈n/2⌉−2}. We do not know
whether it is the minimum dimension of the underlying spaces of all optimal ε-QLDP
mechanisms in this regime. Investigating this minimum dimension is an interesting
direction for future work. Incidentally, the binary mechanism has the underlying space
R2.
Since we have shown that the binary mechanism is an optimal ε-LDP mecha-
nism achieving the classical optimal value OPTn(ε; ΦC) in this regime, the minimum
dimension of the underlying spaces of all optimal ε-LDP mechanisms in this regime is
two.
