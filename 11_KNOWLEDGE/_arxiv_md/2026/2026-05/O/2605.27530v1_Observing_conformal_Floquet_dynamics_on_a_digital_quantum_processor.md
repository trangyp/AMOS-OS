Observing conformal Floquet dynamics on a digital quantum processor
Liang-Hong Mo,1, ∗Bastien Lapierre,2, ∗and Qiang Miao3, †
1Department of Physics, Princeton University, Princeton, New Jersey, 08544, USA

Quantum simulations are traditionally confined to exploring dynamics starting from unentangled or low-
entanglement states due to severe bottlenecks in protocol design, hardware performance, and classical verifica-
tion. Here, we report the first experimental observation of non-equilibrium dynamics initiated directly from a
many-body critical state. Using a fully-connected trapped-ion processor, we prepare the critical ground state
of a transverse-field Ising model via a hardware-tailored, logarithmic-depth quantum circuit based on multi-
scale entanglement renormalization. Following this initialization, we apply a deep Floquet drive that maintains
emergent conformal symmetry, enabling us to benchmark the lattice dynamics against analytical results from
continuum theory. In the resulting conformal heating phase, we extract a central charge consistent with the
Ising universality class (c = 1/2) from the universal decay of the Loschmidt echo and observe spatial energy
localization predicted by field theory. Conversely, the non-heating phase exhibits global finite-time revivals.
This work establishes a scalable and versatile framework for exploring critical quantum dynamics.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Programmable quantum processors offer a native, scalable
route for simulating quantum many-body dynamics [1–5], yet
require efficient circuit design for state preparation as co-
herence time is limited. Classical tensor network methods,
meanwhile, have proven remarkably powerful for represent-
ing well-structured many-body states in equilibrium [6–8],
but face a computational bottleneck when entanglement grows
under non-equilibrium dynamics [9, 10]. It is then natural
to ask whether combining these two approaches can preserve
the merits of each, opening a promising route to simulating
dynamics starting from complex many-body states, which is
hard to access by either approach alone.
Within the landscape of quantum many-body states, critical
states occupy a central place, as their low-energy correlations
are universal and insensitive to microscopic details. In one
spatial dimension, this universality is described by conformal
field theory (CFT) [11, 12], which captures long-distance cor-
relations and entanglement entropy. Recent quantum simu-
lation experiments have successfully probed such signatures
of CFT in critical ground states, including power-law cor-
relations, entanglement entropy scaling, and emergent low-
energy spectrum [13–17]. Beyond ground state physics, CFT
has offered numerous insights into universal quench dynam-
ics and thermalization in critical systems [18], and more re-
cently into heating dynamics in driven systems [19–25]. Re-
alizing this non-equilibrium universality experimentally re-
quires that the time evolution remains within the low-energy
sector, a condition that product-state initializations cannot sat-
isfy: they inject high-energy excitations at finite density into
a gapless spectrum, which obscures signatures of universality
and generically leads to featureless infinite-temperature heat-
ing [26, 27].

In this work, we report the first experimental observation of
non-equilibrium dynamics starting from a many-body critical
state. Using Quantinuum’s trapped-ion digital quantum pro-
cessors, we prepare the ground state of a critical Ising model
via a hardware-tailored, logarithmic-depth quantum circuit
based on the multi-scale entanglement renormalization ansatz
(MERA) [28, 29]. Starting from this critical state, we then
apply a Floquet circuit to directly probe universal driven dy-
namics, in quantitative agreement with predictions from the
emergent CFT description. We observe robust signatures of
the heating and non-heating phases for up to 16 Floquet cy-
cles through the time evolution of two complementary observ-
ables: the Loschmidt echo and the energy density.
Furthermore, we propose and realize a protocol to measure
the central charge c of the underlying critical Ising model from
the time evolution of the Loschmidt echo. This establishes a
new route to measuring the central charge in critical models
that bypasses entanglement-based observables, which are ex-
ponentially expensive in measurements. Specifically, we ex-
ploit the universal time dependence of the return probability
of the evolved critical ground state, which, in the CFT de-
scription, is fully determined by conformal invariance and de-
pends on the central charge as the only fitting parameter. Our
work thus provides a proof-of-principle realization of non-
equilibrium dynamics starting from a scale-invariant vacuum
rather than a product state, in a way that is both scalable and
controlled by low-energy universality.

As depicted in Fig. 1C, we prepare the ground state of the
critical TFIM H0 at a system size of N = 16 by applying
the unitary UMERA to an initial product state of 16 qubits,
|+⟩⊗16, where X|+⟩= |+⟩. The circuit follows a hierar-
chical, top-down architecture that builds the state from coarse
to fine length scales. It begins at the most macroscopic scale
with a single long-range gate (blue) acting on the two top-
level representative qubits, q0 and q8. Following this, the cir-
cuit proceeds through layers of isometries (blue) and entan-
glers (green). The isometry gates incorporate new degrees
of freedom by coupling the previously entangled qubits to
the remaining unentangled qubits (e.g., branching q0 into the
pair {q0, q4}, and q8 into {q8, q12}). Immediately after this
branching step, entangler gates are applied between these ad-
jacent branches (e.g., crossing q4 and q8) to capture the en-
tanglement at that specific intermediate scale. Repeating this
expand-and-entangle procedure layer by layer eventually in-
corporates all 16 qubits and constructs the full lattice state.
Consequently, long-range correlations and multi-scale entan-
glement can be efficiently encoded within a circuit of depth
O(log N).
The hierarchical geometry and isometric constraints of
MERA enforce strictly bounded causal cones for local ob-
servables, allowing us to classically evaluate the energy and
optimize the circuit parameters in polynomial time and cir-
cumvent the exponential costs typically associated with highly
connected tensor networks.
Benchmarking with the exact
ground state of the critical TFIM H0 at N = 16, the opti-
mized circuit achieves an energy-density error of 7.4 × 10−4,
and a state infidelity of 3.1 × 10−3.

namics by measuring the Loschmidt echo L. Figure 2A shows
the exact diagonalization phase diagram of L after 16 Floquet

VI.
SPATIAL STRUCTURE OF HEATING
Unlike generic driven many-body quantum systems, which
typically evolve toward a featureless state, the driven CFT in
the heating phase develops a nontrivial spatial structure. In
fact, the energy density and entanglement entropy develop 2q
sharp peaks, which correspond to sinks of gapless quasipar-
ticles [20]. Therefore, the full system only locally heats up,
while most of the system effectively cools down below the
ground state energy, which gives rise to a dynamical Casimir
effect [20, 36, 37]. In contrast, energy density oscillates peri-
odically in time in the non-heating phase, which leads to peri-
odic finite-time revivals to the initial ground state, the period
of which diverges at the heating phase transition.
Although CFT is defined in a continuous spacetime, where
the injected energy in the heating phase grows exponentially
and without bound, it significantly constrains the physics of
discrete lattice models, even at small system sizes. In our
experiment, we implement the driven Ising chain with ⃗κ1 =
(1.0, 1.2, −0.4). As shown in Fig. 3A, the measured local
energy density hi = −1

the continuous-space locations predicted by CFT. In contrast,
the non-heating phase (Fig. 3B) does not feature such energy
accumulation. While experimental noise and statistical uncer-
tainties obscure the predicted periodic oscillations of the en-
ergy density in the non-heating phase, our data clearly demon-
strate that the energy remains bounded. Here, we present the
raw data directly; embedded dynamical decoupling during the
digital Floquet drive is the only error mitigation strategy we
employ.
VII.
DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK
In this work, we have demonstrated the emergence of con-
formal Floquet dynamics in a TFIM chain on a digital trapped-
ion quantum processor. As shown in the App. A, this emer-
gent behavior is generic and not unique to the TFIM; numer-
ical simulations show agreement with CFT predictions across
other universality classes, including the non-integrable inter-
acting Ising chain (c = 1/2) and the integrable XX chain
(c = 1). Although our current experimental and numerical
benchmarks are carried out on a 16-qubit lattice, the discrete
lattice regularization approaches the continuum limit of CFT
as the system size N grows. The underlying physics is there-
fore robust against finite-size effects and is expected to mani-
fest even more cleanly on larger scales.
Our protocol is furthermore scalable across all three con-
stituent operations: state preparation, Floquet evolution, and
measurement. First, the classical computational complexity
of optimizing the MERA state preparation circuit is bounded
by O(N). This is owing to the strictly bounded, narrow causal
cones in the MERA architecture. Second, the digital Floquet
driving circuit is straightforward to implement, requiring a to-
tal driving depth that scales only linearly with N to allow gap-
less excitations to propagate across the system [20]. Finally,
extracting universal data via our protocol is also scalable;
whereas determining the central charge via subsystem entan-
glement entropy incurs an exponential overhead in terms of
measurement bases, we extract c directly from the Loschmidt
echo, which requires a single measurement basis. Alterna-
tively, c can be extracted, for sufficiently large systems, from
the dynamics of the total energy, a local observable that can
be estimated even more robustly.
A natural next step is to explore regimes in which CFT no
longer provides direct predictions for quantum dynamics. For
instance, periodically driving a critical ground state with rele-
vant bulk operators is generally expected to induce nonuni-
versal heating toward a featureless state.
Whether univer-
sal signatures nevertheless persist at early times remains an
open question, which our digital approach is well-suited to ad-
dress. Furthermore, recent theoretical investigations suggest
that universal finite-time dynamics can still emerge from off-
critical initial states [38], which could provide an alternative
route to extract conformal data. More broadly, digital quan-
tum simulators offer a promising route to studying driven dy-
namics of critical models beyond one spatial dimension. Since
both numerical and field-theoretical methods face severe lim-
itations in two dimensions, exploring the possible emergence
of heating and non-heating dynamical phases controlled by
conformal invariance represents an especially compelling di-
rection for future work.
Beyond its utility for exploring non-equilibrium universal-
ity, our protocol offers a physics-driven verification frame-
work for quantum hardware in regimes that lie beyond clas-
sical simulability. As digital quantum processors scale past
50-100 qubits, standard numerical cross-checking becomes
entirely intractable. However, because our protocol yields an-
alytically exact CFT predictions for the non-equilibrium dy-
namics, it establishes a reliable benchmarking tool for large-
scale hardware: one that verifies whether the intermediate-
time driven dynamics conform to the predicted dynamics of
observables without requiring classical lattice simulations.
