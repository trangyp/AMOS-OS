Orthogonal Least Squares with Integrated Information Theoretic
Criteria for Joint Number of Targets and DoA Estimation
Martin Willame, Graduated Student Member, IEEE, Gilles Monnoyer, Member, IEEE,
Franc¸ois Horlin, Fellow, IEEE and J´erˆome Louveaux, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—We address the joint estimation of the number
of targets and their direction-of-arrivals (DoAs) using antenna
arrays. Target-number estimation can be formulated as a model-
order selection problem and solved with the information theoretic
criteria (ITC). The ITC minimize an objective function that bal-
ances a likelihood term and a complexity penalty. However, direct
application of the ITC requires maximum-likelihood DoA esti-
mates for each candidate model order, which is computationally
prohibitive because it entails a multidimensional search over all
angle combinations. To reduce complexity, many radar processing
exploit greedy methods such as orthogonal least squares (OLS).
In this paper, we explore three distinct methods to integrate the
ITC model-order selection into the OLS estimation procedure for
joint target-number and DoA estimation. Specifically, we propose
the disjoint rank-based, the joint selection-based, and the hybrid
rank-and-selection-based ITC-OLS algorithms. Each algorithm is
derived under both the Akaike information criterion (AIC) and
the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) frameworks. Numerical
simulations show that the proposed hybrid ITC-OLS algorithm
consistently outperforms both the other proposed variants and a
baseline method from the literature.
Index Terms—Direction-of-arrival estimation, Model-order se-
lection, Information theoretic criteria, Orthogonal least squares,
Multi-target detection.
I. INTRODUCTION
I
N many radar applications, antenna arrays are used to
estimate the direction-of-arrivals (DoAs) of multiple tar-
gets from noisy observations [1]–[4]. Although the maxi-
mum likelihood (ML) estimator can asymptotically attain the
Cram´er-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB), its computational com-
plexity grows exponentially with the number of targets; conse-
quently, it is often unsuitable for real-time operation [5]. Low-
complexity heuristics such as MUSIC are commonly used in
DoAs estimation scenarios [6]. However, these subspace-based
methods degrade when target signals are highly correlated [4].
Alternatively, greedy techniques like orthogonal least squares
(OLS), can be used as fast approximate solvers for the above
ML problem [7]–[9]. This method iteratively build a solution
by selecting one DoA at a time based on a least squares
criterion. This significantly reduces the computational burden
compared to exhaustive search methods such as the ML esti-
mator. In the litterature, the number of targets, which typically
amounts to the number of steps in the greedy algorithm, is
often assumed to be known [4]. However, in practice, it is

Building upon this observation, this work addresses the joint
estimation of the number of targets and their DoAs within
an OLS-based framework. A common strategy is to stop the
greedy search using a residual-energy criterion [10]–[13]. Yet
fixed-threshold stopping remains difficult to tune in practice
because the optimal threshold depends on the noise and
received signal levels. In contrast, within the ML framework,
target-number estimation is naturally formulated as a model-
order selection problem. It can be solved by the information
theoretic criteria (ITC) that select the model minimizing an
objective function that balances a likelihood term against a
model complexity penalty [14]–[20]. The Akaike information
criterion (AIC) and the Bayesian information criterion (BIC)
are two widely used ITC that differ by their model complexity
penalty terms [17]. Nevertheless, a direct use of ITC requires
ML parameter estimation for every candidate model order, i.e.,
for each possible number of targets; this requirement again
limits real-time applicability.
Given the identified gaps in the litterature, our key contri-
butions are the following:
• We propose three novel algorithms that combine the ITC
target-number estimation with the OLS greedy estimation
of target DoAs: disjoint, joint, and hybrid ITC-OLS.
Each algorithm is derived under both the AIC and the
BIC formulations.
• We show that the need for ML estimates in ITC-OLS
can be circumvented by introducing an ML-correction
parameter in the ITC objective. This parameter controls
the tradeoff between hit rate and false-alarm rate
at high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) while preserving
performance in other regimes.
• The proposed ITC-OLS algorithms are applied in a
practical scenario considering a passive radar (PR)
setup based on unknown orthogonal frequency-division
multiplexing (OFDM) data frames. The PR estimates
DoAs without decoding the transmitted symbols.
• Through numerical simulations, we demonstrate that
among the proposed algorithms, hybrid ITC-OLS pro-
vides the best overall performance across relevant radar
parameters. We also show that, in this context, the AIC-
OLS consistently outperforms the BIC-OLS.

III. JOINT TARGET NUMBER AND DOA ESTIMATION
This section presents three algorithms that combine the ITC
for target-number detection with the OLS greedy estimation of
target DoAs. In this context, ITC estimate K by minimizing
an objective function balancing two terms: one likelihood term
that quantifies the fit of the model to the observed data, and
one model complexity penalty term that discourages overfitting
by penalizing a higher number of targets. In this work, the
term ITC refers to both the AIC and the BIC criteria, which
differ by their model complexity penalty terms. The three
approaches are detailed in the following subsections, and their
performance is compared in Section IV.
A. Disjoint rank-based ITC-OLS

estimated using rank-based ITC, agnostically of the DoA
estimator. Building upon this estimate, OLS is then used to
estimate the target DoAs for the inferred target number.

C. Hybrid Rank and Selection-based ITC-OLS
The selection-based ITC-OLS method can be further im-
proved by combining it with rank-based ITC. First, before
running OLS,the rank of the sample covariance matrix R
is estimated to provide a preliminary estimate bK using the
criterion in (8). Next, during the first bK iterations of OLS,
the estimated angles bθk+1 are systematically added to bΘk.
As a result, the algorithm enforces a minimum number of
selected targets determined by the estimated rank of R. For
subsequent steps, i.e., when k + 1 > bK, the newly estimated
angle bθk+1 is added only if it improves the selection-based
ITC-OLS objective in (13); therefore enabling the detection
of more than bK targets. This combination is motivated by
complementary operating regimes. At low SNRs, selection-
based ITC-OLS tend to underestimate the number of targets
due to the division by σ2 in (13). Rank-based ITC are typically
more robust to noise and provide a reliable, yet conservative,
target-count estimate. At medium SNRs, selection-based ITC-
OLS can improve the rank-based conservative target-count
estimate by leveraging accurate OLS angle estimates. At high
SNRs, the ML correction parameter σ2
c in (13) can be tuned
to control the tradeoff between hit and false-alarm rates.
Therefore preventing overestimation of the number of targets.

outperform the fixed-threshold method. Fig. 1a compares the
performance as a function of the SNR. As expected, at low
SNR, the joint selection-based AIC-OLS triggers the stopping
criterion in (14) too early because of the large division by
σ2 in (13), leading to a low hit rate. Consequently, in this
regime, the disjoint rank-based AIC outperforms the joint
approach. At medium SNR, the trend reverses: as the SNR
increases, detected peaks are more likely to correspond to true
targets, improving the hit rate of the joint AIC-OLS, whereas
the disjoint method remains agnostic to estimates quality. At
high SNR, both methods are reliable when the ML correction
parameter is properly tuned. Across the full SNR range, the
hybrid AIC-OLS method achieves the best performance by
leveraging the complementarity of the two strategies.
Fig. 1b depicts the performance as a function of the number
of subcarriers Q, while maintaining a constant sub-spacing
∆f. This is equivalent to illustrating the performance as a
function of the bandwidth. The performance of the disjoint
rank-based AIC-OLS improves significantly as Q increases
because target echos become less correlated. With the in-
creasing bandwidth, the rank-based AIC discriminate more
effectively between signal and noise subspaces. The joint AIC-
OLS method is less sensitive to Q because it relies primarily
on detected peaks rather than on explicit subspace separation.
Nevertheless, increasing Q still improves its performance by
providing more observations. Once again, the hybrid AIC-OLS
method outperforms both disjoint and joint methods across the
full Q range by combining their strengths.
Fig. 1c compares the performance of the hybrid AIC and
BIC methods, as a function of the ML correction parameter σ2

for different SNRs. At low SNR, hybrid-method performance

penalty term typically yields a higher hit rate. At medium and
high SNR, however, σ2

when no correction parameter is used (i.e., σ2

for the AIC (around −35 dB) than for the BIC (around −45

K and the SNR, but would require tuning for different M as
it defines the width of the hit region around the true target
DoA.
V. CONCLUSION
This paper studies the joint estimation of the number of
targets and their DoAs by integrating the ITC model-order
selection into the OLS greedy search. Three methodologies
are proposed: the disjoint rank-based, the joint selection-
based, and the hybrid rank and selection-based ITC-OLS
algorithms. Each algorithm was derived under both the AIC
and the BIC frameworks. Numerical simulations demonstrate
that the proposed hybrid ITC-OLS achieves the best overall
performance by exploiting the complementarity of the rank-
based and selection-based ITC components,across all con-
sidered parameters regimes. Furthermore, AIC consistently
outperforms BIC in the evaluated scenario. A key feature of
the joint and hybrid strategies is the ML correction parameter,
set to σ2
c ≈−35 dB for the AIC and σ2
c ≈−45 dB for the
BIC, which controls the hit/false-alarm tradeoff at high SNR
without degrading the performance at lower SNR regimes.
