ON THE SPECTRA OF CANTOR MEASURES
LEANDRO ZUBERMAN
Abstract. We consider Cantor measures on the line, with contraction factor
N−1 = p−α (where p a positive prime, α a positive integer) and m positive
integer digits lying in distinct residue classes modulo N. We obtain a complete
characterization of maximal orthogonal sets of exponentials in L2(µ), for a
class of such measures µ. It is proved that the n + 1-th digit in the base-N
expansion of frequencies in a maximal orthogonal set, with the first n digits
prescribed, has m possible values. In consequence, there are a correspondence
between labelings of the m-homogeneous rooted tree and maximal orthogonal
sets of frequencies.
1. Introduction
For certain measures µ, there exists a discrete set Λ satisfying that the set
E(Λ) := {e2πiλx : λ ∈Λ} is an orthonormal basis for the Hilbert space L2(µ). In
this case, the measure is said to be spectral and the set Λ is called a spectrum.
Spectral measures generalize spectral sets. A set Ω⊂Rd is said to be spectral
if there is an orthonormal basis of the type E(Λ) for L2(Ω). Fuglede conjectured
that spectral sets are those sets tilling the space Rd by translations [5]. Although
the conjecture has been proved to be false in full generality [14], it has caught the
interest of the community and has been shown to be valid in many particular cases.
For instance, recently it has been proved for convex sets [12].
Self similar Tilings have been thoroughly studied, and deep results have been
obtained in this direction [6, 10]. In an interplay between self-similar tilings and
Fuglede’s conjecture, Jorgensen and Pedersen gave an example of a non-atomic
singular measure which is spectral [7]. In fact, they constructed a spectrum for
a Cantor measure on the line with contraction ratio 1/4.
This result kept the
atention of the fractal community and was generalized in many ways. For instance,
Strichartz proved that some measures with a product structure are spectral and
exhibit a spectrum based on this product structure [13]. Laba and Wang proved
that Cantor self similar measures on the line (see definition below (1)) with digits
admitting a Hadamard pair (see Definition 3.8 below) are spectral [9]. Dutkay,
Haussermann and Lai extended this result to Euclidean spaces [2]. These results
reveal a vast family of Cantor measures that are spectral.
Spectral sets or measures can, of course, have many spectra. A natural question
is how the frequencies of the spectra are distributed in the line or in the space. A
classical result of Landau [11] gives necessary conditions for a discrete set to be a
spectrum for a set. Dutkay et al [4] extended Landau’s result to spectral singular
measures. They proved that certain spectra of Cantor measures necessarily have
Beurling dimension equal to the dimension of the measure itself. They conjectured

Dutkay, Han and Siu [3] revisited the seminal example by Jorgensen and Pedersen

a complete characterization of all maximal orthogonal sets of type E(Λ). This was
achieved by labeling the 2-homogeneous rooted tree using the base-4 expansion
of the elements of Λ. In fact, they proved that at every position, the digits in the
expansion have only two allowed values. Thus, the first n digits have 2n possibilities
out of the 4n possible. This can be thought of as a dimensional necessary condition
for the spectra, coinciding with the dimension of the measure.
In this article, we extend this result from a single measure to a family of Cantor
measures. Specifically, we consider Cantor measures µ on the line with a contraction
factor equal to the inverse of an integer that is a prime power, say N = pα, and digits
in a finite set D of positive integers in different residue classes modulo N. We prove
that if E(Λ) is orthogonal, then for each element of Λ whose first n digits in the
base-N expansion are prescribed, the number of possible digits at position n + 1 is
bounded by |D| (Theorem 3.1). As a consequence, each such set Λ can be arranged
in a homogeneous rooted tree. Furthermore, we analyze maximal orthogonal sets of
type E(Λ). Under additional hypotheses on the aforementioned Cantor measures,
we prove that the bound on the number of digits is always attained. That is, if we
look at digit n + 1 in the base-N expansion of elements of Λ with the first n digits
prescribed, we will find |D| possible values. Moreover, if L is the set of such digits
at position n + 1, then (N, D, L) is a Hadamard triple (see 3.8 for the definition
of a Hadamard triple and Theorem 3.10). Finally, we establish a correspondence
between sets Λ for which E(Λ) is a maximal orthogonal set and homogeneous rooted
trees (with homogeneity equal to |D|). In fact, we prove that for each such Λ there
is a labeling of that tree and conversely (Theorem 4.3). As in the original result,
this can be thought of as a dimensional necessary condition for Λ to be spectral.
Let us briefly comment on the hypothesis assumed and proofs. Orthogonality in
L2(µ) is naturally connected to the zeros of the Fourier transform of the measure,
ˆµ. As a consequence of the self-similarity, the Fourier transform of Cantor measures
can be expanded as a product (see (2)), which helps with finding its zeros. This
product expansion, has factors related to the polynomial PD(x) = P
d∈D xd. In
fact, a hypothesis assumed on the measure (actually on its digits) is that PD must
be a product of cyclotomic polynomials (Theorems 3.10 and 4.3) . The scale four
measure considered in [7, 3] has digits satisfying this condition. Although the results
presented in this article extend those obtained in [3], the proofs are quite different.
Many of them are based on ideas appearing in [8].
This article is structured as follows. In Section 2 we give most of the required
definitions and give context. In Section 3 we present the statements and proofs of
the main results bounding and estimating the number of digits in N-expansions.
In Section 4 we construct labelings for the rooted m homogeneous trees and prove
the correspondence with maximal orthogonal sets.

4. Trees
We can consider the rooted N-homogeneous tree and assign to each vertex a label
ℓwith 0 ≤ℓ< N. If the label of a vertex of order n is associated with the n-th digit
in base-N expansion of integers, then there is a correspondence between infinite
paths in the tree ending only with 0’s or only with N −1 and Z. In the previous
section, we proved that for an integer orthogonal set of frequencies, the number of
possible values for the n-th digit in the base-N expansion (for elements with the
first n−1 digits prescribed) are bounded by p|T |. Thus, for an integer orthonormal
set of frequencies, a subtree with homogeneity p|T | instead of N, suffices. Moreover,
under the hypothesis of Theorem 3.10, there is a correspondence between infinite
paths on this subtree and frequencies in a maximal orthogonal set. Let us be more
specific.

Recall some basic definitions on graphs and trees. A graph is a pair (V, E) where
V is a discrete set of vertices and E ⊂V × V is the collection of edges. A path
(finite or infinite) is a sequence of vertices (vn) such that (vn, vn+1) ∈E for each
n. A tree is a connected and loop free graph (a loop is a finite path (v1, . . . , vn)
with v1 = vn) and a rooted tree is a tree with one specific vertex designated as the
“root”. In rooted trees, a vertex is said of order n, if its distance to the root is
n, that is, if a path of length n joins the vertex to the root. A rooted tree is said
m-homogeneous if each vertex has m edges connecting to vertices in the next level.
Thus, in an m-homogeneous rooted tree, there are mn vertices of level n.
To each vertex of the rooted |D|-homogeneous tree assign a label from the set
{0, . . . , N −1}. This assignment is called a labeling. A labeling is said (N, D)-
spectral (or just spectral if it is clear by context) if the following conditions are
satisfied:
(1) There is an infinite path starting at the root with each vertex labeled 0.
(2) For any vertex v, there is (at least) an infinite path starting from v and
ending by nodes all labeled 0 or all labeled N −1.
(3) For any vertex v of order n, let L = Lv be the set of labels of the vertex in
level n + 1 sharing an edge with v. For all v ∈V , (N, D, L) is a Hadamard
triple.
