ON THE TRANSITIVE BINARY G-SPACES
PAVEL S. GEVORGYAN
Abstract. Distributive subsets of the group of all invertible continuous
binary operations on a topological space are considered, and it is proved
that the subgroups generated by them are also distributive. A criterion
for the distributivity of a binary action of a topological group G on
a space X is obtained.
The concept of transitive binary G-space is
introduced, and a classification of transitive distributive binary G-spaces
is given in the case of a compact group G.
1. Introduction
Representations of groups by invertible binary operations of some set play
an important role in various questions of algebra and other branches of math-
ematics. In terms of binary representations of groups, one of the old problems
of algebra was solved: the problem of describing those groups that can be
multiplicative groups of fields [1].
Studying binary representations of a topological group G, or binary G-
spaces, dates back to work [2]. In a more general sense, a representation
of a topological group G should be understood as a homomorphism of the
group G into the group of all invertible continuous binary operations of
a space X.
The category of binary G-spaces and biequivariant maps is
an extension of the category of G-spaces and equivariant maps. The class
of distributive binary G-spaces plays a special role; important notions and
results of equivariant topology extend to this class [3–9].
In the classification of G-spaces, as is well known, the central role is played
by the category of transitive G-spaces, or the category of G-orbits [10–12].
In the present paper we define the notion of a transitive binary G-space and
provide a complete classification of these spaces in the class of distributive
binary G-spaces with a compact group G (Definition 4.5 and Theorem 4.7).
For this purpose, we study the distributive subsets of the group of all invert-
ible continuous binary operations of a topological space X (Propositions 3.2,
3.3 and Theorem 3.4), prove a criterion for the distributivity of a binary
action of a group G on X (Theorem 3.5), and establish several properties of
stationary subgroups G(x,y) of the pair (x, y), x, y ∈X (Propositions 4.1–
4.4).

2. Preliminary Information
Let X be a topological space. By C2(X) we denote the space of all contin-
uous binary operations f : X2 →X with the compact-open topology. The
space C2(X) with the operation
(fφ)(x, y) = f(x, φ(x, y)),
where x, y ∈X, is a topological monoid with unity e(x, y) = y.
By H2(X) we denote the group of all invertible binary operations of the
monoid C2(X).
Let G be a topological group. A continuous map α : G × X2 →X is
called a binary action of the group G on a space X if, for arbitrary g, h ∈G
and x, y ∈X, the following equalities hold:
gh(x, y) = g(x, h(x, y)),
e(x, y) = y,
where e is the identity element of the group G, and g(x, y) = α(g, x, y). A
space X with a fixed binary action α of the group G, or a triple (G, X, α),
is called a binary G-space.
Any topological group G acts binarily on itself by means of the conjugate
left translation
(2.1)
g(x, y) = xgx−1y,
where g, x, y ∈G.
An arbitrary element g ∈G generates a continuous binary operation αg :
X2 →X by the formula
αg(x, y) = α(g, x, y).
The map g →αg defines a homomorphism
θ : G →H2(X)
of the group G into the group H2(X) of all invertible continuous binary
operations of the space X. The kernel of the homomorphism θ is called the
kernel of the binary action α. Thus,
ker α = {g ∈G;
g(x, y) = y, for all x, y ∈X}.
It is not hard to see that ker α is a closed normal subgroup of the group G.
A binary action α is called effective if ker α = e, that is, the map θ is
injective.
Let (G, X, α) and (G, Y, β) be binary G-spaces. A continuous map f :
X →Y is called biequivariant if the condition
f(α(g, x, y)) = β(g, f(x), f(y))
is fulfilled, or equivalently,
f(g(x, y)) = g(f(x), f(y))
for all g ∈G and x, y ∈X.

A biequivariant map f : X →Y which is also a homeomorphism is called
an equivalence of binary G-spaces, or a biequimorphism.
All binary G-spaces and biequivariant maps form a category which is a
natural extension of the category of G-spaces and equivariant maps.
A binary G-space (G, X, α) is called distributive if, for arbitrary x, y, z ∈X
and g, h ∈G, the following equality holds:
(2.2)
g(x, h(y, z)) = h(g(x, y), g(x, z)).
In this case α is called a distributive binary action of the group G on X.
The binary action (2.1) of the group G on itself by means of the conjugate
left translation is distributive, whereas the binary action of the group G on
itself given by the formula g(x, y) = x−1gxy, g, x, y ∈G, does not possess
this property.
3. Distributive Subsets of the Group of Invertible Binary
Operations on a Topological Space
Let g, h ∈H2(X) be invertible binary operations of the space X. We
call an ordered pair (g, h) a distributive pair if it satisfies the identity (2.2).
A subset D ⊂H2(X) is called distributive if all its elements are pairwise
distributive.
For an arbitrary x ∈X, we define a map gx : X →X by the formula
gx(y) = g(x, y). Note that the following equality holds:
(3.1)
(gh)x = gxhx
for arbitrary g, h ∈H2(X) and x ∈X.
Proposition 3.1. Let g, h ∈H2(X) be invertible binary operations of the
space X. Then the following statements are equivalent:
1. The pair (g, h) is a distributive pair.
2. The map gx : X →X satisfies the equality
gx(h(y, z)) = h(gx(y), gy(z))
for all x, y, z ∈X.
3. The equality
gxhy = hgx(y)gx
holds for all x, y ∈X.
We omit the proof of this proposition, since it is rather simple.
Proposition 3.2. Let g, h ∈H2(X) be invertible binary operations of the
space X. If (g, h) is a distributive pair, then (g, h−1), (g−1, h), and (g−1, h−1)
are also distributive pairs.

Proposition 3.3. Let g, h, k ∈H2(X) be invertible binary operations of the
space X. If (g, h) and (g, k) are distributive pairs, then (g, hk) is also a
distributive pair.
Proof. By Proposition 3.1, for arbitrary x, y ∈X the following equalities
hold:
gxhy = hgx(y)gx
and
gxky = kgx(y)gx.
It is not hard to see that then gxhyky = hgx(y)kgx(y)gx, or, taking into ac-

gx(hk)y = (hk)gx(y)gx.

The following two theorems immediately follow from Propositions 3.1, 3.2,
and 3.3.
Theorem 3.4. Let D ⊂H2(X) be a distributive subset. Then the subgroup
of the group H2(X) generated by the set D is a distributive subgroup.
Theorem 3.5. Let X be a binary G-space. Then the following statements
are equivalent:
1. The action of the group G on X has the distributivity property.
2. The map gx : X →X is a biequivariant homeomorphism for all g ∈G
and x ∈X.
3. For all g, h ∈G and x, y ∈X, the equality
gxhy = hgx(y)gx
holds.
4. Stationary Subgroups and Transitive Binary G-Spaces
Let X be a binary G-space, and let x, y ∈X be arbitrary points. The set
G(x,y) = {g ∈G, g(x, y) = y}
is a closed subgroup of the group G. This subgroup is called the stationary
subgroup of the pair (x, y).
The subgroup G(x,x) is called the stationary
subgroup of the point x.

Proposition 4.1. Let f : X →Y be a biequivariant map between binary
G-spaces X and Y . Then
G(x,y) ⊂G(f(x),f(y))
for any x, y ∈X.
This proposition can be proved elementary.
Proposition 4.2. Let X be an arbitrary binary G-space, and let x, y ∈X
and g ∈G be arbitrary. Then the equality
(4.1)
G(x,g(x,y)) = gG(x,y)g−1
holds.
Proof. Let g0 ∈G(x,y) be an arbitrary element. Then, for any g ∈G, we
have
gg0g−1(x, g(x, y)) = gg0(x, y) = g(x, g0(x, y)) = g(x, y).
Hence,
gG(x,y)g−1 ⊂G(x,g(x,y)).
In particular, the last inclusion implies that
g−1G(x,g(x,y))g ⊂G(x,g−1(x,g(x,y))) = G(x,y).
From this we obtain the inverse inclusion
G(x,g(x,y)) ⊂gG(x,y)g−1.
□
Proposition 4.3. Let X be a distributive binary G-space, and let x ∈X be
an arbitrary point. Then the stationary subgroup G(x,x) is a closed normal
subgroup of the group G.
Proof. Let g0 ∈G(x,x) and g ∈G be arbitrary elements. By distributivity,
we have
gg0g−1(x, x) = g(x, g0(x, g−1(x, x)))
= g(x, g−1(g0(x, x), g0(x, x))) = g(x, g−1(x, x)) = x,
that is, gg0g−1 ∈G(x,x).
Hence, gG(x,x)g−1 = G(x,x), which means that
G(x,x) is a normal subgroup.
□
Proposition 4.4. Let X be a distributive binary G-space. Then, for arbi-

(4.3)
G(x,g(x,x)) = G(x,x),
(4.4)

Definition 4.5. A binary action of a group G on X is called transitive if
G(x, x) = X for all x ∈X. In this case X is called a transitive binary
G-space.

Theorem 4.8. Let a binary action of a compact group G on a space X be ef-
fective, distributive, and transitive. Then X is biequivariantly homeomorphic
to the topological group G with the binary action by means of the conjugate
left translation.
As shown by the next proposition, a group G with the binary action by
means of the conjugate left translation is the only distributive transitive
binary G-space with an effective binary action.
Proposition 4.9. Let α : G × X2 →X be a transitive distributive binary
G-space, and let x ∈X be an arbitrary point. Then the kernel of the binary
action α coincides with the stationary subgroup of the point x:
ker α = G(x,x).
Proof. It suffices to prove that the equality G(y,z) = G(x,x) holds for arbitrary
points y, z ∈X. By the transitivity of the binary action, there exist elements
g, g′ ∈G such that g(x, x) = y and g′(x, x) = z. Now, taking (4.2) and (4.3)
into account, we obtain
G(y,z) = G(g(x,x),g′(x,x)) = G(g(x,x),g(x,g−1g′(x,x))) = G(x,g−1g′(x,x)) = G(x,x).
□
