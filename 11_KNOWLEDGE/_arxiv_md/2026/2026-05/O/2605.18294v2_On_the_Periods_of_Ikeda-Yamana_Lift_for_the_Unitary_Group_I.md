ON THE PERIODS OF IKEDA-YAMANA LIFT FOR THE UNITARY
GROUP I
JIN HIGASHITANI
Abstract. Let F be a totally real field and E be a quadratic CM extension field of F.
Let n be an odd positive integer. Yamana constructed a lift from Hermitian modular
forms to automorphic forms on the unitary group. We denote by In(f) the form obtained
by applying this lift to the Hermitian modular form f of weight (wλ)λ and level 1. We
then express the period ⟨In(f), In(f)⟩of In(f) for Hecke eigenforms f in terms of special
values of certain L-functions attached to f. This is an extension of Katsurada’s result
concerning Ikeda’s conjecture.
Keywords: automorphic forms, L-functions, periods, lifting.
1.
Introduction
A major problem in the theory of modular forms is the explicit determination of their
periods. Such periods are often related to special values of L-functions through explicit
lifting constructions, providing valuable information on Satake parameters and on the
transfer of automorphic representations via the L-group formalism.
Periods attached
to various lifts have been examined from an arithmetic perspective: Zagier [24] treated
the Doi-Nakamura lift, Murase and Sugano [17] studied a Kudla lift, and Ibukiyama,
Katsurada, and Kojima [7] investigated the Miyawaki-Ikeda lift. For symplectic groups
over Q, Ikeda [9] established the Duke-Imamoglu-Ikeda lift, settling the Duke–Imamoglu
conjecture. The unitary analogue was later constructed in [10], where Ikeda conjectured
an explicit period formula (cf. [10, Conj.17.5]). Let F be a totally real field and E a

The Main Theorem expresses the Petersson norm of the Ikeda-Yamana lift in terms
of special values of L-functions. In subsequent sections, we develop the local and global
theory necessary to prove this result. The strategy relies on the explicit construction of
the lift given by Yamana [23], and the Rankin-Selberg integral defined as in [12], together
with the residue method of Eisenstein series. We also determine the constant CF,E,n,κ
explicitly, extending earlier methods from the rational case [13] to the general setting. In
Section 2, we recall some basic facts to compute the Ikeda-Yamana lift and its period. In
Section 3, we explain the Ikeda-Yamana lift, particularly in the classical case. We then
recall some results about the period of the Duke-Imamoglu-Ikeda lift prior to this work
and state our main theorem. In Section 4, we introduce the Rankin-Selberg integral and
compute the period-side term by using the residue of the Eisenstein series. Moreover, we
decompose the Rankin-Selberg integral into Euler products. In Section 5, we compute
the Euler factor by the relations and special values of the Siegel series and local density,
and reduce them to the Koecher-Maass integral and the zeta integral. In Section 6, we
prove the main theorem by synthesizing the previous calculations. I would like to express
my gratitude to Professor Tamotsu Ikeda and Dr. Nobuki Takeda.
