I. INTRODUCTION
Development of quantum technologies pose an exis-
tential threat to classical cryptography. Quantum com-
munication has emerged as a promising alternative by
leveraging quantum mechanical principles. Among quan-
tum communication protocols, QKD enables two parties,
commonly referred to as Alice and Bob to establish a
secure cryptographic key. BB84 is most widely studied
protocol and was proposed by Bennett and Brassard in
1984[1].
Although BB84 is provably secure under ideal condi-
tions, practical quantum communication operate in noisy
environments that affect transmitted quantum states[[2].
Environmental imperfections may arise from imperfect
photon sources [3], detector limitations[4], information
leakage[5], and decoherence[6]. Such effects lead to in-
crease in QBER and may provide an opportunity for Eve
to extract information during communication process. In
particular, collective noise channels are crucial as they
model physical processes where all qubits experience
identical disturbance during propagation. Collective ro-
tation noise is highly relevant to optical fiber communi-
cation as well as polarization-based quantum systems.
Despite extensive studies, impact of collective rotational
noise on secret key rate(SKR) of QKD protocols has not
been studied yet. In this paper, we investigate the impact
of collective rotation noise on security parameters of
BB84 protocol. We analyze the behavior of QBER, mutual
information, and SKR under intercept and resend attack
in presence of collective rotation noise. Furthermore,
we examine if specific noise regime can reduce Eve’s
information while preserving an acceptable key rate. The
results provide an insight into interplay between noise and
security in realistic QKD channels.
II. PRELIMINARIES
A. BB84 Protocol
BB84 is a type of QKD protocol that belongs to
prepare and measure (P&M) category. Like every other
QKD
protocol,
BB84
leverages
non-cloning
theorem
and measurement property of quantum mechanics to
establish secure key. It combines one-time pad encryption
method with quantum key distribution method [1]. It
employs four quantum states encoded in two Pauli
eigenbases, i.e., Z basis (|0〉, |1〉) and X basis (|+〉,|−〉).
BB84 is structured such that Alice selects a random
bit sequence consisting of 0’s and 1’s and prepares a
quantum state in any Pauli bases. She sends this state
to Bob through an authenticated quantum channel. Bob
measures the received bit by choosing a random basis.
Alice and Bob perform basis reconciliation. Only those
bits are retained for which bases coincide.
To
monitor
Eve,
Alice
and
Bob
perform
parameter
estimation by publicly disclosing a subset of their sifted
bits and bases, which are immediately discarded after
QBER estimation. If QBER is within tolerable range i.e
11%, protocol is continued, otherwise it is aborted. Alice
and Bob might modify the bits of their shared key to
enhance the distribution process [7]. Traditional error
correction techniques, like Cascade [8] or Low-Density
Parity-Check codes [9], are used to minimize differences
in Alice and Bob keys. To reduce the information leakage
to Eve, privacy amplification i.e. another hash function
[10] is applied to reduce bit string size for key generation.

B. Collective rotation noise
Noise impacts qubits in such a way that fluctuations
in quantum channel either by environmental conditions
or by imperfect calibrations leads to distortion of qubits.
Collective noise includes all factors that affects all the
qubits equally or in a same manner [11]. As this noise has
collective effect on all qubits, these are globally rotated

in optical channels like fiber optic cable or a special
environmental condition where qubits are under effect of

V. CONCLUSIONS
In this work, we analyzed security parameters of the
BB84 protocol in the presence of collective rotation noise,
and observed the impact of noise on these parameters
particularly key rate under three scenarios: (i) collective
rotation noise present in the channel with no eaves-
dropper, (ii) an intercept-and-resend eavesdropper active
across the entire Alice to Bob channel also subject to
noise, and (iii) an eavesdropper whose devices are placed
near Alice’s laboratory, so that only the Eve to Bob segment
experiences channel noise. By deriving expressions for
QBER, mutual information and secret key rate as functions
of rotation angle (θ), we found non-trivial minimum in
Eve’s mutual information. At θ ≈0.13 radians Eve extract
20% less information than in zero noise or maximum
noise cases, while key rate remains meaningfully positive.
This finding motivates a noise engineering approach to
QKD systems that is integration of an optimal value
of noise in experimental setup of a QKD protocol for
enhanced security. Future work will extend this security
analysis to other eavesdropping strategies, finite key ef-
fects as well as experimental validation in free space and
fiber based QKD testbeds.
