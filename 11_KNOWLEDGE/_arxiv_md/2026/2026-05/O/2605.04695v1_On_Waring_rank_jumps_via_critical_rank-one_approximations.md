ON WARING RANK JUMPS
VIA CRITICAL RANK-ONE APPROXIMATIONS
ALESSANDRO ONETO, PIERPAOLA SANTARSIERO, AND ETTORE TEIXEIRA TURATTI
Abstract. We investigate whether eigenvectors, also known as critical rank-one approximations, of a sym-
metric tensor can be used to increase or decrease its Waring rank. First, we study the variety of degree-d
rank-r forms which admit an eigenvector as part of a minimal Waring decomposition. In the case of binary
forms, we show that this is of codimension-one in the r-th secant variety of the rational normal curve. On
the other hand, we prove that for any binary form of rank less than (d + 1)/2 (subgeneric), any eigenvector
increases the rank. Additionally, when the degree is odd, the same holds for generic forms of generic rank.
Our approach employs the strict relation between the apolar action and the Bombieri-Weyl product.
1. Introduction
For a rank-r matrix, it is well-known that singular pairs can be used to provide a decomposition of the
matrix as sum of r rank-one matrices. In the case of higher-order tensors a generalization of such a result is
known to be not true in general.
Given a order-d symmetric tensor F ∈Symd(Cn+1), or degree-d form, a Waring decomposition of F is an
expression of F as sum of powers of linear forms, i.e.,

We say that a linear form L ∈Sym1(Cn+1) is an eigenvector of F ∈Symd(Cn+1) if ∇F(L) = λL, for
some λ ∈C. Eigenvectors were introduced in [Lim05, Qi07] as a generalization of singular pairs of matrices
to higher order tensors from the perspective of optimization. In the literature, they are also referred to as
critical rank-one approximations, E-eigenvectors, or singular vector tuples for non-symmetric tensors.
From a geometric point of view, we consider the Veronese variety V d
n parametrizing powers of linear forms
inside the space of degree-d forms. Given F ∈Symd(Cn+1), a Waring decomposition corresponds to a set of
points of the Veronese variety whose linear span contains F, while eigenvectors are the critical points of the
squared Euclidean distance function from F to the Veronese variety.
Since computing minimal Waring decompositions is in general NP-hard [HL13], several approaches have
been proposed to study algebraic and geometric properties that have to be satisfied by minimal Waring
decompositions. Having in mind the ideal situation of matrices where the concepts of singular tuples and
minimal additive decompositions are strictly related, it is very natural to try understanding the relation
between eigenvectors of symmetric tensors and their minimal Waring decompositions.
Interestingly, eigenvectors are typically not part of a minimal Waring decomposition, even though suf-
ficiently general symmetric tensors admit a (non-minimal) Waring decomposition made of eigenvectors
[DOT18].
In [HT25], the authors explored the question of when a form has a SVD-like decomposition,
namely, when the process of replacing a symmetric tensor with its difference with an eigenvector eventually
gets to zero: it was shown that only weakly-odeco forms admit such property.
The latter question was
explored also in [RHST26]. Towards the opposite direction, in [SC10], the authors showed that subtracting
an eigenvector may actually increase the rank. This idea is interesting in view of the lack of knowledge
of explicit high-rank forms, see [BHMT18]. Motivated by these previous works, we consider the following
general problem.
Problem. Given F ∈Symd(Cn+1), can its eigenvectors be used to decrease or increase its Waring rank?

Our approach is based on the observation that the apolar action, which allows to study Waring decompo-
sitions of F via ideals of points contained in its annihilator with respect to derivation, and the Bombieri-Weyl

Outline of the paper and main results. In Section 2 we recall preliminary notions, including the
Bombieri-Weyl product and its connection with the apolar action.
In Section 3, we investigate the relation between two geometric loci associated to F: the Waring locus
W(F), i.e., the locus of linear forms that appear in a minimal decomposition of F, and the eigenscheme
Eig(F), i.e., the locus of all eigenvectors of F.
In other words, Eig(F) ∩W(F) consists of the set of
eigenvectors that can be employed to decrease the rank of F. In Lemma 3.2, we characterize Eig(F)∩W(F)
in terms of apolar ideals.
We employ such characterization to give a complete description of Eig(F) ∩
W(F) for identifiable binary forms (Proposition 3.7 and Remark 3.10) and monomials (Proposition 3.12 and
Theorem 3.16).
Section 4 is devoted to a geometric study of the critical Waring variety WEd

Remark 2.16. Let F ∈Symd(Cn+1). We say that F has s essential variables if there exists a vector
subspace V ⊆Sym1(Cn+1) with dim V = s such that F ∈Symd(V ) ⊆Symd(Cn+1). It is well-known that
minimal Waring decompositions of F involve only linear forms in its essential variables, in other words, we
have that W(F) ⊆P(V ), e.g., see [CCO17, Remark 2.4]. On the other hand, if s < n+1, then Eig(F) ̸⊂P(V )
since P(V ⊥) ⊂Eig(F). However, the critical points of P(V ⊥) are generally not so interesting as is evident
for instance in the case d = 2 where the singular pairs associated with zero singular values of a non-maximal
rank matrix provide no information on lower rank approximations of the matrix.

5. On eigenvectors of binary forms that increase Waring rank
The previous sections were devoted to understanding when eigenvectors can be used to decrease the
Waring rank. A natural converse question is to understand when an eigenvector increases the Waring rank.
For binary forms of subgeneric Waring rank, as a consequence of Theorem 4.10, we know that generically
the support of the eigenscheme is contained in the forbidden locus. An interesting question is to understand
if there are special elements in the eigenscheme, and more generally in the forbidden locus, that increase
the rank of the given form. [SC10] shows that, in the case of real binary cubics, eigenvectors can actually
increase the rank, even above the generic one. In this section we show that for generic [F] ∈σr(V d
