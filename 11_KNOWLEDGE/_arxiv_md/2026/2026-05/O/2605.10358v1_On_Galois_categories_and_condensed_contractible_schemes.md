On Galois categories and condensed contractible schemes
Catrin Mair
May 12, 2026
Abstract
We extend the study of the condensed Galois category of a scheme introduced by Barwick,
Glasman and Haine in their work on Exodromy [BGH20]. We elaborate its connection to
Lurie’s work on Ultracategories [Lur18] and provide a description in terms of 𝑤-contractible
rings. We give a classification of schemes whose Galois category has an initial, respectively, a
terminal object. This implies the condensed homotopy type of the scheme, which was studied
in more detail in [HHL+25], to be trivial. Furthermore, we compute a formula for the (under-
lying group of the) condensed fundamental group of a general Dedekind domain and show
that it is non-trivial for Spec(𝐙). This means that Spec(𝐙) is not condensed contractible.

Conventions
Set-theoretic conventions
Whenever working with condensed mathematics, there arise set-theoretic issues from the fact
that defining sites are large. We follow the approach used in the setting of pyknotic mathematics
[BH19; BGH20] to deal with these. More on this can be found in Warning 2.14. As an upshot:
We will neglect set-theoretic issues in the sense that we will implicitly work with 𝜅-small ob-
jects or functors for strongly inaccessible cardinals 𝜅. We use results on condensed/pyknotic
mathematics from [BH19; BGH20; Sch19b; Sch19a] without any further notice on set-theory.

Preliminaries
In this section, we collect basics on categorical aspects of condensed mathematics, recap Galois
categories of schemes and give an upshot on condensed homotopy types of schemes before we
provide more explicit descriptions, examples and computations on these objects in §3 and §4.
2.1
Recollection on condensed mathematics
We review condensed sets, condensed anima and condensed ∞-categories. As additional sources
consult [BH19] and [Sch19b]. Note Warning 2.14 for a comment on set-theoretic issues that come
along with definitions in the world of condensed mathematics.
Condensed Sets and condensed anima
The basic objects in condensed mathematics are condensed sets. Originally, these are defined as
sheaves of sets on the pro-étale site Pro ́Et∗of a point, i.e., the spectrum of a separably closed field.
See [BS15] for an introduction to the pro-étale topology of schemes. The category of condensed
sets is denoted by
Cond(Set) ≔Sh(Pro ́Et∗, Set) .
Sheaves on Pro ́Et∗can be identified with sheaves on certain subcategories of topological spaces.
The notion of condensed sets extends in the ∞-categorical world to the notion of condensed
anima, denoted by Cond(Ani), by considering hypersheaves with values in the ∞-category Ani
of anima (or spaces).
2.1 Notation. We denote by Pro(Setfin) the category of profinite sets. By Stone duality, the
category Pro(Setfin) can be identified with the full subcategory of totally disconnected spaces in
the category Comp of compact Hausdorff spaces. By a result by Gleason, stated in [Joh82, p. III
3.7], the projective objects in Comp are the extremally disconnected profinite sets Extr ⊂Comp.
2.2 Recollection. There are at least four different defining sites for the ∞-category Cond(Ani)
of condensed anima defining the same ∞-topos of hypercomplete ∞-sheaves:
(i) The pro-étale site Pro ́EtSpec(𝑘) for 𝑘any separably closed field,
(ii) the site Comp of compact Hausdorff spaces,
(iii) the site Pro(Setfin) of profinite sets,
(iv) the site Extr of extremally disconnected profinite sets.
Here, coverings for the sites (ii)-(iv) are given by jointly surjective families of continuous maps
of topological spaces. All of these sites define the same ∞-topos on hypercomplete ∞-sheaves:
Every compact Hausdorff space admits a surjection from an extremally disconnected compact
Hausdorff space, namely from the Čech–Stone compactification of its underlying discrete space.
Moreover, Pro(Setfin) identifies with the subcategory of affine schemes in Pro ́EtSpec(𝑘). Thus
there are hierarchies of bases Extr ⊂Pro(Setfin) ⊂Comp and Pro(Setfin) ⊂Pro ́EtSpec(𝑘). By
[Aok23, Corollary A.7], restriction of sites induces equivalences of hypercomplete ∞-topoi
(2.3)
Shhyp(Pro ́EtSpec(𝑘)) ⥲Shhyp(Pro(Setfin)) ⥲Shhyp(Extr) .
The ∞-topos Cond(Ani) of condensed anima is any of the equivalent ∞-topoi (2.3).
