Abstract: Understanding when linear immersions of nonlinear dynamical systems exist is
important since such immersions allow us to leverage the rich tools of linear system theory to
analyze nonlinear dynamics. Recently, Liu et al. (2023) showed that continuous-time dynamical
systems that admit countably many but more than one ω-limit sets cannot be immersed into
finite dimensional linear systems with a one-to-one and continuous mapping. In this paper, we
extend these results to discrete-time dynamics and show that similar obstructions exist also in
discrete time. We further consider a generalization involving α-limit sets. Several examples are
provided to demonstrate the results.
Keywords: Nonlinear systems, Immersions, Koopman operator
1. INTRODUCTION
The idea of embedding or immersing a complex nonlinear
dynamical system into a higher-dimensional yet simpler
one has important applications in analysis, design, and
learning of nonlinear systems. Koopman operator theory
formalizes this idea when the embedding dimension is
infinite and embedding system is linear (Koopman, 1931;
Mauroy et al., 2020; Bevanda et al., 2021). However, in
practice, one works with finite dimensional embeddings
(Sontag, 1979; Van Den Essen, 1994; Brunton et al., 2016).
When such embeddings exist, one can devise algorithms to
learn them or to directly infer properties of the underlying
nonlinear system from data (Williams et al., 2015; Wang
et al., 2023; Haseli and Cort´es, 2021).
Our work is inspired by the recent work by Liu et al.
(2023, 2025); Kvalheim and Arathoon (2026), which estab-
lishes necessary or sufficient conditions for (non)existence
of such embeddings into finite dimensional linear sys-
tems in continuous-time. Our goal is to extend some of
these results to discrete-time dynamical systems, which
are arguably more common in the context of data-driven
applications. In particular, we show that when a discrete-
time dynamical system admits countably many but more
than one ω-limit sets or α-limit sets and if it can be
immersed into a finite dimensional linear system with a
continuous mapping, this mapping collapses all limit sets;
i.e., it cannot be one-to-one. Since most parametrizations
(e.g., neural networks) used to learn the embedding func-
tions are continuous, our results reveal an obstruction
to obtaining one-to-one embedding functions that would

recovered from that of the embedding system.

2.2 Definitions
As in Liu et al. (2023, 2025), we study nonlinear systems
that contain multiple limit sets.
Definition 1. (ω-limit set). Given an initial state ξ ∈X,
the ω-limit set ωX (ξ) is the set of all x ∈X such that
there exists a sequence of positive indices kj →∞having
limj→∞f kj(ξ) = x (Hirsch et al. (2013)).
Definition 2. (α-limit set). When f is invertible, given an
initial state ξ ∈X, the α-limit set αX (ξ) is the set of all
x ∈X such that there exists a positive sequence of indices
kj →∞having limj→∞f −kj(ξ) = x. That is, αX (ξ) is the
ω-limit set of ξ when time is reversed.
When the domain of the α and ω limit sets is clear from
context, no subscript is used (i.e., α(·) and ω(·)).
Definition 3. (Unbounded). For initial state ξ ∈X, the
forward trajectory (f +k(ξ)) is unbounded if, for every
bounded set P ⊆X, there exists positive k such that
f k(ξ) /∈P. The forward trajectory is bounded if it is not
unbounded.

it indeed leaves every bounded set permanently.

3. MAIN THEOREMS
Theorem 1. Let Assumption 1 hold, and assume that f is
invertible. Suppose further that
(T1) xk+1 = f(xk) on X can be immersed by a continuous
mapping F in a system zk+1 = g(zk) on Z, g

where the lifted system and its time reversal both

Then, the set {F(S) | S ∈W∪A} has exactly one maximal
element, that is, there exists Smax ∈W ∪A such that
F(Smax) ⊇F(S) for all S ∈W ∪A. Further, if
(T4) For every Ω∈W and Γ ∈A, there exists at least
one forward precompact trajectory in D+
X (Ω) and
backward precompact trajectory in D−
X (Γ),
then {F(S) | S ∈W ∪A} has exactly one element.
That is to say, when conditions (T1)–(T4) hold, the
immersion F necessarily collapses all ω-limit and α-limit
sets. When only conditions (T1)–(T3) hold, then Theorem
1 suggests that the immersion F cannot fully distinguish
the limit sets in X, even when the limit sets of the original
system are disjoint.
Remark 4. Condition (T2) requires that the region of
interest X does not include trajectories which, loosely
speaking, come from and go to infinity.
Remark 5. Condition (T4) does not immediately follow
from conditions (T1)–(T3). For example, one can con-
struct a discrete-time trajectory which is backward pre-
compact but in forward time is unbounded as in Definition
3, but returns to and accumulates in a compact region,
causing it to have an ω-limit set. However, if the only
ω-limit and α-limit sets are equilibria or periodic orbits,
then (T4) is trivially satisfied, as, e.g., in the former case,
the constant trajectory contained by the limit set is both
forward and backward precompact.
Corollary 1. Suppose that conditions (T1)–(T4) hold, and
F is one-to-one. Then, W ∪A has exactly one element.
Corollary 1 implies that the original system has at most
one ω-limit or α-limit set. A direct consequence of this,
combined with Lemma 3, is the following corollary, which
is helpful for proving the nonexistence of one-to-one im-
mersions.
Corollary 2. Suppose that conditions (T2), (T3), and (T4)
hold. If X contains more than one limit set, then, this
system on X cannot be immersed by a one-to-one immer-
sion F in a system zk+1 = g(zk), having g invertible and
continuous, where the lifted system and its time reversal
have closed basins (e.g., a linear system).
Theorem 1 requires f from the original system to be
invertible. However, regardless of if f is invertible, a similar
result can be obtained for general discrete-time dynamical
systems. Analogously to Liu et al. (2023), if we relax
condition (T1) to only require the immersed system to
have closed basins, and we tighten condition (T2) to
require every trajectory to be forward precompact, then
the following theorem also holds.
Theorem 2. Under Assumption 1, suppose further that
(T1′) xk+1 = f(xk) on X can be immersed by a con-
tinuous mapping F in a system with closed basins
zk+1 = g(zk) on Z, having g continuous;
(T2′) Every trajectory of xk+1 = f(xk) on X is forward

(T3′) The set W is countable.

When we restrict ourselves to one-to-one immersions,

Corollary 3. Suppose that conditions (T1′)–(T3′) hold,

5. CONCLUSION
In this work, we have shown that a discrete-time system
with multiple limit sets cannot admit one-to-one con-
tinuous immersions to systems with closed basins under
the conditions that (T2), trajectories are precompact,
and (T3), there are at most countably many limit sets.
More generally, under these conditions, this work suggests
that continuous immersions necessarily collapse limit sets.
These results show that the same topological obstructions
which prevent continuous-time systems from admitting
immersions also apply to discrete-time systems.
