On the number of finite additive 2-bases
Stefan Weltge and Konrad Zyhalko
Technical University of Munich, Germany
Abstract
The number of finite additive 2-bases is known to grow exponentially. While
this fact has been established by Marzuola and Miller (2010) using complex an-
alytic techniques embedded in the study of numerical sets, we provide a direct,
short proof using elementary probabilistic arguments.
1
Introduction
For a non-negative integer n, let [n]0 := {0,1,...,n}. An additive 2-base for n is a sub-
set X ⊆[n]0 such that [n]0 ⊆X +X , where X +X = {x+y : x, y ∈X }. The study of addi-
tive 2-bases is a classical topic in additive number theory, initiated by Rohrbach [7]
whose work focused on the smallest cardinality of an additive 2-base for n. The
minimal size of an additive 2-base is known to grow asymptotically as pn, and more
precise bounds have been established over the years by various authors (see [2], [5],
[4], [1], [8]).
In this short note, we are interested in the number of additive 2-bases for n. To
this end, let Γ(n) be the family of all additive 2-bases for n. The values |Γ(n)| for n =
0,...,65 were calculated by Martin Fuller and are indexed in OEIS [6, A066062]. The
principal theoretical work yielding a strong lower bound on |Γ(n)| is due to Marzuola
and Miller [3], who primarily study “numerical sets with no small atoms” and show
that this notion is intimately related to additive 2-bases. As a consequence, they find
that |Γ(n)| grows exponentially in n and obtain a strong estimate for the asymptotic
constant. However, due to their focus on numerical sets, their approach involves
significant complexity.
In this short note, we provide a short and simple proof for the fact that the num-
ber of additive 2-bases for n grows exponentially in n.
Theorem 1.1. There exists some α > 0 such that |Γ(n)| ≥α·2n+1 holds for all integers
n ≥0.
In what follows, we focus on simplicity rather than optimizing constants, and
hence most bounds we obtain are not tight.
