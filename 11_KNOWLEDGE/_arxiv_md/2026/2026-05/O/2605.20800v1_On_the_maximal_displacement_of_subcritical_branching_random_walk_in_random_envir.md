Introduction and Main Results
The maximal displacements of spatial branching systems have been investigated by many authors. For
supercritical branching random walk (m > 1), it can be traced back to Hammersley [15], Kingman [20],
Biggins [6] and Bramson [10]. Since then extensively studies on this topics have appeared in recent years,
see for example [1, 4, 5, 8, 9, 16] and references therein.

displacement.

Preliminaries
Given the environment ξ, under Pξ, the branching system (T , V ) is a typical inhomogeneous branching
random walk. Conditional on the first generation of the branching process, it is not hard to verified
that the quenched probability Pξ(M ⩾x) satisfies a typical non-linear stochastic difference equation.
For differential equations, we can have the Feynman-Kac formula to formally construct the solutions.
However, in the discrete case, similar tools may need to be further refined. For example, in [21], they in-
troduced a discrete Feynman-Kac formula and investigate the maximal displacement of critical branching
random walk, and later in [23] and so on. It should be noted that, for the branching system, the recently
developed size-biased measure change method may be a relatively effective approach. Also, inspired by
our previous work [12] about homogeneous subcritical branching random walk, we can use the size-biased
measure change and optional line to express the quenched probability Pξ(M ⩾x).

2.3
An Annealed Coupling
Note that, under PE ⊗˜Pξ, besides the two-side environment ξ, there is a trajectory of spine particles,
denote by {V (ω0), · · · , V (ωτx)}. For fixed λ > 0, in annealed probability PE ⊗˜Pξ, the trajectory and
the environment are independent with each other. Furthermore, conditioned on the trajectory and the
environment, the full branching system can be generated according to a suitable rule. This insight inspires
us to consider constructing an annealed coupling. Also, inspired by our previous work [12], we can look
the size-biased branching system standing at time τx and position x, and desire to find a suitable coupling.
It is worth mentioning that for homogeneous branching systems, due to homogeneity, no matter the
specific value of τx, the branching mechanism remains identical when looking forward and backward from
time τx, therefore, it is not very hard to reconstruct the branching system (see [12] for more details).
However, for random environment setting, the time inhomogeneity brings an essential change, making
it infeasible to construct a coupling directly under the quenched probability measure ˜Pξ. Therefore, we
consider constructing a suitable coupling in the annealed sense.
The construction of the annealed coupling is divided into four steps.
• First, we specify a two-sided random environment. More precisely, we characterize the probabilistic
structure of the environment as viewed from time τx.
• Second, we determine the trajectory of the spine particles. This part is constructed mainly by the
excursion theory.
• Third, we describe the whole branching system. To do such, we use the language of transfer measure
(or can be translated into the language of conditional expectation). Formally, to obtain the whole
branching system, conditional on environment and the trajectory of spine particles, we attach some
subtrees to the spine.
• Finally, based on above constructions, we introduce the annealed coupling measure and several σ
fields. After that, we adopt the excursion theory and regard each excursion as a time scale. Then
we treat space as a new time index and construct the random walk {Rx, x ⩾0}. Also, from the
whole branching system, we construct a sequence of random variables {Bx, x ⩾0}. Moreover, we
use these random variables to obtain a coupling lemma (i.e. Lemma 6), which can be used to
express the annealed probability.
Environment:
Under PE ⊗˜Pξ, the random time τx is measurable with respect to G (i.e. the σ field generated only
by the trajectory of spine particles) and independent with F∞:= σ(ξ) (i.e. the σ field generated only
by environment ξ). Thus, under the annealed probability, if we look the system at time τx, the shifted
environment Tτxξ has the same distribution as ξ. Therefore, the annealed probability distribution of
the environment viewed from time τx is exactly given by PE. The reason for constructing a two-sided
environment lies in that the behavior of the subtrees attach to the spine constructed subsequently is
indeed affected by the environment before τx and after τx.
Therefore, we generate a two side random environment ξ according to PE, and denote by F∞:= σ(ξ)
the σ field generated by the environment.
