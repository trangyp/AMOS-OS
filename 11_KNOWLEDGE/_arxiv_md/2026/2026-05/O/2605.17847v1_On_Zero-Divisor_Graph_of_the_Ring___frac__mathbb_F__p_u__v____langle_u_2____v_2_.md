Abstract
In this article, we study the zero-divisor graph of the commutative non-chain
ring with identity Fp + uFp + vFp + uvFp, where u2 = 0, v2 = 0, uv = vu, and p is
an odd prime. We determine several graph-theoretic properties of the associated
zero-divisor graph Γ(R), including clique number, chromatic number, vertex con-
nectivity, edge connectivity, diameter, and girth. In addition, we compute certain
topological indices of Γ(R). Furthermore, we obtain the eigenvalues, energy, and
spectral radius of the adjacency matrix, the Laplacian matrix and the Eccentricity
matrix of Γ(R).
Keywords: Zero-divisor graph, Laplacian matrix, Spectral radius.
AMS Subject Classiﬁcation: 05C50, 05C25, 15A18.

Introduction
The zero-divisor graph has attracted considerable attention in recent years. In 1988,
Beck [7] introduced the notion of a zero-divisor graph, in which the additive identity
of a ring R was included in the set of vertex. His primary focus was on the coloring
of commutative rings.
Let Γ be a simple graph whose vertices are the zero-divisors
of the ring R, where two distinct vertices are adjacent if their product is zero. This
deﬁnition was later modiﬁed by Anderson and Livingston [1], who redeﬁned the zero-
divisor graph as a simple graph whose vertices consist only of the non-zero zero-divisors
of a commutative ring R, with adjacency deﬁned in the same way.

An edge-cut of a connected graph Γ is the set S ⊆E such that Γ −S = (V, E −S) is
disconnected. Edge-connectivity λ(Γ) is the minimum cardinality of an edge-cut. The
minimum k for which there exists a k-vertex cut is called the vertex connectivity, or
simply the connectivity of Γ it is denoted by κ(Γ).
For any connected graph Γ, we have λ(Γ) ≤δ(Γ) where δ(Γ) is the minimum degree
of the graph Γ.
The chromatic number of a graph Γ is the minimum number of colors needed to color
the vertices of Γ so that adjacent vertices of Γ receive distinct colors and is denoted by
χ(Γ). The clique number of a graph Γ is the maximum size of a subset C of V for which
xy = 0, for all x, y ∈C and it is denoted by ω(Γ). That means, ω(Γ) is the maximum
size of a complete subgraph of Γ. Note that for any graph Γ, ω(Γ) ≤χ(Γ).

where ω(Γ(R)), χ(Γ(R)) are the clique number and the chromatic number of Γ(R), re-
spectively.
He also veriﬁed that the conjecture is true for several examples of rings.

Theorem 2.2. The diameter of the zero-divisor graph diam(Γ(R)) = 2.
Proof. From Figure 1, we can see that the distances between any two distinct vertices
are 1 or 2. Therefore, the maximum distance between any two distinct vertices is 2.
Hence, diam(Γ(R)) = 2.
Theorem 2.3. The clique number ω(Γ(R)) of Γ(R) is 2p −2.
Proof. From Figure 1, Auv∪Au or Auv∪Av is a complete subgraph(clique) in Γ(R). Then
Auv ∪Au or Auv ∪Av forms a complete subgraph with maximum vertices. Therefore, the
clique number of Γ(R) is ω(Γ(R)) = |Auv ∪Au| = |Auv ∪Av| = p−1+p−1 = 2p−2.
Theorem 2.4. The chromatic number χ(Γ(R)) of Γ(R) is 2p −1.
Proof. Since Auv ∪Au or Auv ∪Av is a complete subgraph with 2p −2 vertices in Γ(R),
then at least 2p −2 diﬀerent colors needed to color the vertices of Auv ∪Au ∪Av.
We take the same color in Au to color vertices of Av as there is no direct edge between
Au, Av. Since there is no direct edge between two vertices in Au+v, Au+uv, Av+uv, Au+v+uv,
one diﬀerent colors enough to color the vertices of Au+v, Au+uv, Av+uv and Au+v+uv.
Therefore, minimum 2p−2+1 = 2p−1 diﬀerent colors are required for proper coloring.
Hence, the chromatic number χ(Γ(R)) is 2p −1.
The above two theorems show that the clique number and the chromatic number of
our graph are not the same.
Theorem 2.5. The girth of the graph Γ(R) is 3.
Proof. We know that the girth of a complete graph is 3. In Figure 1, Au, Av and Auv
are a complete subgraph of Γ(R) and hence the girth of Γ(R) is 3.
Theorem 2.6. The vertex connectivity κ(Γ(R)) of Γ(R) is p −1.
Proof. The degree of any vertex in Γ(R) is at least p −1. Therefore, the minimum p −1
vertices are removed from the graph to be disconnected. Hence, the connectivity of the
vertex is κ(Γ(R)) = p −1.
Theorem 2.7. The edge connectivity λ(Γ(R)) of Γ(R) is p −1.
Proof. As in the connected graph Γ(R), κ(Γ(R)) ≤λ(Γ(R)) ≤δ(Γ(R)). Since κ(Γ(R)) =
p −1 and δ(Γ(R)) = p −1, then λ(Γ(R)) = p −1.

Conclusion
In this article, we discuss the zero-divisor graph of a commutative ring with identity
Fp +uFp+vFp+uvFp where u2 = 0, v2 = 0, uv = vu and p is an odd prime. We ﬁnd the
clique number, chromatic number, vertex connectivity, edge connectivity, the diameter
and girth of a zero-divisor graph associated with the ring. We ﬁnd some of topological
indices of the zero-divisor graph Γ(R). Also, we ﬁnd the eigenvalues and spectral radius
of the adjacency matrix, the Laplacian matrix and the Eccentricity matrix of Γ(R).
