ON A QUESTION OF MAURI AND MORAGA
JIHAO LIU
Abstract. We give negative answers to both parts of a question of Mauri and Moraga on log
Calabi-Yau pairs whose boundary decomposes into big divisors. The main result of this paper
is obtained by generative AI, particularly Chatgpt 5.5 pro and the Rethlas system.
1. Introduction
We work over the field of complex numbers C.
Mauri and Moraga asked the following question [MM25, Question 9.9].
Question 1.1 ([MM25, Question 9.9]). Let (X, B) be an n-dimensional log Calabi-Yau pair
such that B admits a decomposition into (n + 1) big divisors.
(1) Is the pair (X, B) qdlt?
(2) Is D(X, B) isomorphic to the boundary of an n-dimensional standard simplex?
By [MM25, Theorem 1.10], the pair (X, B) is crepant birational to a weighted projective space
and D(X, B) is PL-homeomorphic to a PL-sphere. This provides strong evidence for a positive
answer to Question 1.1. However, both parts of Question 1.1 have negative answers.
Theorem 1.2. There exists a projective three-dimensional log canonical log Calabi-Yau pair (as
in Example 2.1)

X, B = P4
i=1 Di

such that each Di is a prime big divisor, but (X, B) is not
qdlt. In particular, Question 1.1(1) has a negative answer.
Theorem 1.3. There exists a surface qdlt log Calabi-Yau pair

S, ∆= P3
i=1 Ci

such that each
Ci is a big divisor but D(S, ∆) is not isomorphic to the boundary of the two-dimensional standard
simplex. In particular, Question 1.1(2) has a negative answer.
Remark 1.4. Both parts of Question 1.1 are obtained by generative AI. Question 1.1(2) is a
one-shot by Chatgpt 5.5 pro. Question 1.1(1) is an almost one-shot by Chatgpt 5.5 pro, but it
messed up on showing that the singularity
(v2 −u3 −τs = 0) ⊂A4
is not a quotient singularity. The paper was then forwarded to the Rethlas system, which solved
the problem via a very lengthy proof. The current proof of the corresponding part just refers to
[Rei87] as the classification of threefold cDV quotient singularities is well-known.
See [Ju+26] for a detailed introduction to the Rethlas system.
Due to the limitation of
generative AI, it is possible that we have missed some related references in the literature, and
we welcome any comments from experts.

2. The examples
In this section, we construct the examples proving Theorems 1.2 and 1.3.
Example 2.1. Let
T = P(1, 1, 1, 4) = Proj C[x, y, z, t],
deg x = deg y = deg z = 1,
deg t = 4.
Let
Dx = (x = 0),
Dy = (y = 0),
Dz = (z = 0),
and
Dt = (t = 0)
be the toric boundary divisors, and put
BT = Dx + Dy + Dz + Dt.
The unique singular point of T is
o = [0 : 0 : 0 : 1],
of quotient type 1
4(1, 1, 1). Since o /∈Dt, the divisor Dt lies in the smooth locus of T, and
Dt ≃P2
x,y,z.
Inside Dt ≃P2, take the cuspidal cubic
C = (h = 0),
h = y2z −x3.
Let
Z = (t = h = 0) ⊂T.
Thus Z = C ⊂Dt. The curve Z is irreducible and is a codimension-two local complete inter-
section in the smooth locus of T. It is singular only at the cusp
p = [0 : 0 : 1 : 0].
Let
π: X = BlZ T −→T
be the blow-up of T along Z, and let E be the exceptional divisor of π. Define
B = D1 + D2 + D3 + D4,
where D1, D2, D3, D4 denote the strict transforms of Dx, Dy, Dz, Dt on X respectively.
Lemma 2.2. The pair (X, B) in Example 2.1 is log canonical and satisfies
KX + B ∼Q 0.
Proof. The ideal of Z is locally generated by the regular sequence (t, h). Since π is the blow-up
of a codimension-two regular closed subset, we have
KX = π∗KT + E.
Since Z ⊂Dt with generic multiplicity one, while Z is not contained in Dx, Dy, or Dz, we have
(2.1)
π∗Dt = D4 + E,
π∗Dx = D1,
π∗Dy = D2,
and
π∗Dz = D3.
Thus
KX + B = π∗(KT + BT ) ∼0
as (T, BT ) is log toric and KT + BT ∼0. Since (T, BT ) is lc, (X, B) is lc.
□
Lemma 2.3. Notation as in Example 2.1. Then X is klt and has two isolated singularities q
and π−1(o), where q is locally analytically isomorphic to
{v2 −u3 −τs = 0} ⊂A4
u,v,τ,s.
In particular, q is a cDV singularity of type cA2 and is not a quotient singularity.

Proof of Theorem 1.2. By Lemma 2.5, q is an lc center of (X, B). Since the generic point of an lc
center of any qdlt pair is a quotient singularity by [dFKX17, Proposition 34 and Definition 35],
(X, B) is not qdlt by Lemma 2.3.
