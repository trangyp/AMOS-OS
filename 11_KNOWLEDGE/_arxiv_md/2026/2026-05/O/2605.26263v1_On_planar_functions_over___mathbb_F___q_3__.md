JOÃO PAULO GUARDIEIRO, ADLER MARQUES, LUCIANE QUOOS,
AND GUILHERME TIZZIOTTI
Abstract. Let Fq denote the finite field of order q. For q odd, we investigate
the planarity over Fq3 of the family
fE,A,B,C,D(X) := EX2 + AXq+1 + BXq2+1 + CX2q + DX2q2 ∈Fq[X].
Using results from the theory of q-polynomials, we establish conditions under
which these polynomials are planar functions. In particular, we provide charac-
terizations for the planarity property and present new families of planar trinomials,
quadrinomials, and pentanomials.
Introduction
Let Fq be the finite field with q elements, and F∗
q be the multiplicative group of non-
zero elements of Fq. Since Fq is finite, every function f from Fq to itself corresponds
to a unique polynomial f(X) in Fq[X] of degree at most q−1. A polynomial f(X) in
Fq[X] is called a permutation polynomial of Fq if the associated polynomial function
f : c 7→f(c) from Fq to Fq is a bijection. Permutation polynomials over finite
fields constitute a classical area of research, principally motivated by their diverse
applications in cryptography and coding theory.
In [9], Dembowski and Ostrom introduced the notion of planar functions in the
following way. Let q be odd, a function f : Fqn →Fqn is called a planar function if
for each ϵ ∈F∗
qn, the polynomial defined by
Fϵ(X) = f(X + ϵ) −f(X)
is a permutation polynomial of Fqn. That is, the equations f(X + ϵ) −f(X) = β
have exactly one solution for all ϵ, β ∈Fqn, with ϵ ̸= 0. Note that, if q is even, then
Fϵ(0) = Fϵ(ϵ) for every ϵ ̸= 0. Therefore, functions satisfying the previous condition
exist only for q odd. In the same paper, Dembowski and Ostrom also observed that
