Abstract
We study optimal experimental design for multi-
nomial logit (MNL) bandits, where an agent
repeatedly selects a subset of K items from a
ground set of size N and observes single-choice
feedback. Unlike linear or generalized linear ban-
dits, MNL bandits have a combinatorial action
space, which makes classical optimal design ap-
proaches and naive optimization over all subsets
computationally intractable. We propose a com-
putationally efficient optimal design framework

we establish near G-optimality guarantees and
characterize the induced statistical–computational

We study optimal experimental design in multinomial logit
(MNL) models (McFadden, 1977) with linear utilities. The
MNL model is a fundamental discrete choice model widely
used in economics, marketing, and machine learning, where
outcomes are generated from subset-dependent multinomial

from a subset of K ads presented on a page. Each ad can
be represented by high-dimensional features, such as image
or text embeddings produced by an off-the-shelf neural

From an experimental design perspective, this setting poses
significant challenges. Unlike linear or generalized lin-
ear models—where the design space consists of individual
arms— the design space in MNL models is combinatorial:
each experiment corresponds to selecting a subset of arms,
and the number of feasible experiments grows on the order
of N K. Moreover, even for a fixed subset, the information
induced by the MNL model is fundamentally different from
that in linear or generalized linear models. In particular,
the Fisher information is matrix-valued, depends nonlin-
early on the underlying parameter, and is determined by
the entire subset through MNL choice probabilities, rather
than decomposing additively across individual arms. As a
result, classical optimal design theory (Kiefer & Wolfowitz,
1960; Pukelsheim, 2006)—which relies on additive, per-arm
information contributions—does not directly apply.
In this paper, we develop an optimal design framework for
MNL models with combinatorial experiments that addresses
the structural and computational challenges described above.
Our approach builds on locally optimal design principles
to handle the inherent nonlinearity of the MNL model, and
leads to a Frank–Wolfe algorithm (Frank et al., 1956; Dunn
& Harshbarger, 1978) whose main computational challenge
is a linear maximization oracle (LMO) over a combinatorial
collection of feasible subsets. To address this bottleneck,
we propose two complementary approaches: (i) an exact
0–1 mixed-integer linear program (MILP) reformulation of
the LMO, allowing early stopping with guaranteed accuracy
up to a user-specified tolerance, and (ii) a polynomial-time
relaxation based on a Schur-complement lifting of the MNL
Fisher information with a bounded approximation error.

2. Related Work
MNL model in Bandits. The MNL feedback model has
been extensively studied in the bandit literature (Agrawal
et al., 2019; 2017; Ou et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2020; Oh
& Iyengar, 2019; 2021; Perivier & Goyal, 2022; Agrawal
et al., 2023; Lee & Oh, 2024). Compared to linear, gen-
eralized linear, and logistic bandits (Abbasi-Yadkori et al.,
2011; Faury et al., 2020; Abeille et al., 2021; Faury et al.,
2022; Lee et al., 2024), MNL bandits present additional
challenges due to their combinatorial action space and non-
uniform revenue parameters. Most prior work primarily
focuses on regret minimization (Chen et al., 2020; Oh &
Iyengar, 2019; 2021; Perivier & Goyal, 2022; Lee & Oh,
2024; 2025). Although a limited number of studies address
best-arm or best-assortment identification (Rusmevichien-
tong et al., 2010; Saur´e & Zeevi, 2013; Yang, 2021; Karpov
& Zhang, 2022), they are largely restricted to context-free
MNL settings. Consequently, best assortment identification
in MNL bandits with linear utilities remains underexplored.
Pure Exploration in Linear Bandits.
The pure explo-
ration problem has been extensively investigated in linear
bandits (Soare et al., 2014; Degenne et al., 2020; Li et al.,
2022; Jedra & Proutiere, 2020; Fiez et al., 2019; Komiyama
et al., 2022; Yang & Tan, 2022). In this line of work, G-
optimal design plays a central role in achieving near-optimal
sample complexity. These approaches aim to identify the
best arm or to certify near-optimality with high confidence,
yielding strong theoretical and practical guarantees. How-
ever, existing design-based methods rely on enumerating
individual arms and therefore do not extend to MNL ban-
dits, where the action space is inherently combinatorial.
Consequently, prior work provides no sample-complexity
guarantees for pure exploration in MNL bandits.

Early stopping with ϵLMO-optimality. Although the re-

based on branch-and-bound maintain both (i) the best fea-

verifiable early-stopping rule: the returned LMO solution

We empirically evaluate the performance of BSI-MNL by
measuring the sample complexity up to the stopping time.
All results are averaged over 10 independent problem in-
stances, and we report the mean together with the standard
deviation. In each instance, the true parameter θ‹ is drawn
uniformly from the d-dimensional Euclidean ball BdpBq of
radius B, and the feature vectors taiuN

are independently sampled as ri „Unifp0, 1q for all iPrNs.
To implement the Frank–Wolfe (FW) procedure (Algo-
rithm 1) within BSI-MNL, we consider two variants that
differ in how the linear minimization oracle (LMO) is com-
puted. The first variant, denoted BSI-MNL-MILP, solves
the LMO by reformulating the original combinatorial op-
timization problem as a mixed-integer linear program and
uses the Gurobi solver (Gurobi, 2024), as described in Sub-
section 3.4. The second variant, denoted BSI-MNL-Lift,
replaces the original LMO with a Schur-complement–based
lifted surrogate, which admits polynomial-time computa-
tion; see Subsection 3.5 for details. Since this work is, to our
knowledge, the first to study best assortment identification
with provable guarantees, there is no existing algorithm that
serves as a direct baseline. Instead, we include an oracle
version of our algorithm that computes the LMO exactly via
brute-force enumeration, denoted BSI-MNL-Oracle.
In the FW procedure, we use a stopping accuracy of ϵ “ 0.1.
For the MILP-based variant, BSI-MNL-MILP, the certified
optimality gap of the linear minimization oracle is set to
ϵLMO “ 0.1. For the confidence radius βpδq and the warm-
up threshold ζw, we use scaled-down versions of their the-
oretical expressions by a factor of 10, as the original theo-
retical values are overly conservative in practice and lead to
unnecessarily long warm-up phases and delayed stopping
times. Throughout, we set d“5, B “1, and δ“0.05.

6. Conclusion
In this paper, we studied optimal experimental design for
MNL models and proposed two computationally efficient
frameworks to address the combinatorial nature of the action
space. Building on these design methods, we developed a
best assortment identification algorithm for MNL bandits
and established an instance-dependent sample complexity.
