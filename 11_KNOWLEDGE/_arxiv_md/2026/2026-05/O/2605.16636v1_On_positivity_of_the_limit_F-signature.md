ON POSITIVITY OF THE LIMIT F-SIGNATURE
YUCHEN LIU AND SUCHITRA PANDE
Abstract. We study a conjecture of Carvajal-Rojas, Schwede and Tucker which states
that for a complex KLT singularity (R, m), the F-signatures of the reductions of R to
characteristic p ≫0 remain bounded away from zero as p →∞.
We prove that this
conjecture holds for three-dimensional non-weakly exceptional singularities by an inductive
argument. We also prove that the conjecture holds for smooth hypersurfaces of very low
degree by constructing isotrivial normal toric degenerations. By considering the version of
this conjecture for the Frobenius-alpha invariant, our techniques are inspired by K-stability
theory and involve using degenerations and birational geometry.
1. Introduction
The F-signature is a numerical invariant of singularities of local rings in positive charac-
teristics that measures the asymptotic flatness of the Frobenius morphism. For an F-finite
local ring R over Fp (meaning that the Frobenius map on Spec(R) is finite), the F-signature
s(R) is a real number between 0 and 1; it equals 1 exactly when R is a regular ring [HL02],
[Tuc12]. This latter fact is a strengthening of the classical theorem of Kunz that character-
izes regular rings via the flatness of the Frobenius map [Kun69]. Similarly, the F-signature
is positive exactly when R has only strongly F-regular singularities [AL03], a mild class
of singularities that implies that R is normal and Cohen-Macaulay [HH94]. The numeri-
cal measurement encoded by the F-signature has found important applications, notably to
bounding the local fundamental groups and torsion in divisor class groups of singularities
[CRST18], [Pol22], [Mar22].
Although strongly F-regular singularities are defined via splittings of the Frobenius map
of R, they turn out to be deeply connected to a class of complex singularities arising in the
minimal model program, namely, Kawamata log terminal (KLT) singularities. Indeed, it
follows from the works [HW02], [HY03] and [Tak04] that a normal, Q-Gorenstein ring R of
finite type over C is KLT if and only if the reduction of R to characteristic p ≫0 is strongly
F-regular for all p ≫0.
Since the F-signature is positive for strongly F-regular rings,
this suggests a connection between the properties of a KLT singularity R over C and the
F-signature of its reductions to positive characteristics. In this direction, Carvajal-Rojas,
Schwede and Tucker made the following conjecture in [CRST18]:
Conjecture 1.1. Let (R, m) be a KLT singularity that is essentially of finite type over C.
For any prime p ≫0, let sp(R) denote the F-signature of the reduction of R to characteristic
p (see Proposition 3.3 for a more precise formulation). Then, there is a constant C > 0
(independent of p) such that for all p ≫0, we have sp(R) > C. In other words, we have
lim inf
p→∞sp(R) > 0.

This conjecture has been established in the following cases: toric singularities [Sin05],
[VK12], finite quotient singularities [HL02], quadric hypersurfaces [Tri23], diagonal hyper-
surfaces [CSTZ24], full flag varieties [Pan23b] and reductive quotient singularities [TY25].
In this paper, we develop several tools to address this conjecture and apply them to prove it
in many new cases. Our first observation is that Proposition 1.1 for KLT singularities can
be reduced to a similar conjecture about the Frobenius-alpha invariant for log Fano pairs
(Proposition 2.18):
Conjecture 1.2. Let (X, ∆) be a projective log Fano pair over C (Proposition 2.3). There
exists a constant C > 0 such that for all p ≫0 and reductions (Xp, ∆p), we have αF (Xp, ∆p) ≥
C.
Relating the two conjectures, we prove:
Theorem 1.3. (Proposition 3.10, Proposition 3.7) Proposition 1.1 holds for all KLT pairs
(R, ∆) of dimension n ≥2 if and only if Proposition 1.2 holds for log Fano pairs in dimension
n −1.
This result was also independently obtained by Takagi and Yamaguchi in [TY25]. The
Frobenius-alpha invariant of a log Fano pair in positive characteristics was introduced in
[Pan23b] as an analog of Tian’s alpha-invariant from complex geometry. Thus, it is more
amenable to tools involving degenerations and birational geometry, which we exploit. We
also note that Proposition 1.2 for a Q-Fano variety X is equivalent to Proposition 1.1 for
the cone over X with respect to the embedding defined by any −rKX (for any r > 0).
Furthermore, we establish a partial inductive approach to proving Proposition 1.1. For
a projective log Fano pair (X, ∆), we let α(X, ∆) denote Tian’s alpha-invariant, or equiva-
lently, the global log canonical threshold (Proposition 2.4).
Theorem 1.4 (Proposition 6.1). Suppose Proposition 1.2 is true for all complex log Fano
pairs of dimension n ≥1. Then, Proposition 1.2 also holds for all log Fano pairs (X, ∆) of
dimension n + 1 with α(X, ∆) < 1.
We now discuss our results in more detail.
1.1. Threefold singularities. We apply Proposition 1.4 to complex threefold KLT singu-
larities that are not weakly exceptional. A weakly-exceptional singularity is defined as a KLT
singularity x ∈(X, ∆) that admits a unique PLT blow-up; see [Pro00]. Weakly exceptional
singularities are local versions of log Fano pairs (S, ∆S) such that α(S, ∆S) > 1. Propo-
sition 1.4 and Proposition 1.3 together reduce Proposition 1.1 for non-weakly exceptional
threefold singularities to the case of pairs (P1, ∆) where ∆has at most three components
(see Proposition 6.1). The latter has been established in [BCPT25] and thus we obtain:
Theorem 1.5. Proposition 1.1 holds for all non-weakly exceptional threefold KLT singulari-
ties (R, m) over C. Similarly, Proposition 1.2 holds for all log del Pezzo surfaces S satisfying
α(S) < 1.
This theorem covers the case of smooth del Pezzo surfaces of degree at least 2 and is new
for all the non-toric and non-diagonal ones. In forthcoming work, we use results of Monsky
[Mon06] to extend this result to all non-weakly exceptional pairs x ∈(X, ∆) in dimension 3.

2.1. Singularities of MMP and Negativity Lemma. We recall the relevant notions of
singularities of the MMP. Throughout this subsection a pair (X, ∆) will refer to a normal
variety over an algebraically closed field k and an effective Q-divisor ∆such that some
multiple of KX + ∆is Cartier, where KX denotes a canonical divisor of X.
Definition 2.1. A divisor E over X refers to some prime divisor E ⊂Y
π−→X, where Y is

discrepancy of such a divisor E with respect to the pair (X, ∆) is a rational number defined

A(X,∆)(E) = ordE(KY + ∆Y + E −π∗(KX + ∆X))
where ∆Y is the strict transform of ∆and KY is a canonical divisor of Y that is compatible
with KX on the locus where π is an isomorphism.
We use log discrepancies to define the following class of singularities.
Definition 2.2. A pair (X, ∆) is defined to be
(1) Kawamata log terminal (KLT) if A(X,∆)(E) > 0 for every divisor E over X.
(2) log canonical (LC) if for every divisor E over X, we have A(X,∆)(E) ≥0. Any divisor
E over X with A(X,∆)(E) = 0 is called an LC-place of (X, ∆).
(3) purely log terminal (PLT) if (X, ∆) is log canonical, S = ⌊∆⌋is a prime divisor on
X and S is the only LC-place of (X, ∆).
We also have the following global versions for projective varieties:
Definition 2.3. A pair (X, ∆) is called a log Fano pair if:
• X is a normal, projective variety,
• (X, ∆) is KLT and
• the Q-divisor −KX −∆is ample.
A projective variety X is said to be of Fano type if there exists some effective Q-divisor ∆
such that (X, ∆) is a log Fano pair.
