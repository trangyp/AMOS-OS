Observation of Entanglement Enabled Spin-Interference in the Drell-S¨oding Process in
Au+Au Ultraperipheral Collisions at RHIC
(The STAR Collaboration)
We report a measurement of the Drell-S¨oding π+π−production in Au + Au ultraperipheral
collisions at center-of-mass energy per nucleon pair √sNN = 200 GeV using the STAR detector.
For the first time, the Entanglement Enabled Spin-Interference (EESI) effect is observed in the
Drell-S¨oding process through the amplitude A2∆ϕ of the cos(2∆ϕ) modulation.
The measured
A2∆ϕ exhibits no significant dependence on the invariant mass Mπ+π−. An enhancement of three
standard deviations is observed for the Drell-S¨oding process compared to the ρ0 photoproduction.
Furthermore, we measure the |t|-dependence of the Drell–S¨oding process and ρ0 photoproduction.
The Drell–S¨oding spectrum falls more steeply with |t| and exhibits diffractive structures shifted to
lower |t| compared to ρ0 photoproduction, features that are not captured by theoretical calculations.
These results provide new insights into the interplay between quantum interference and photon-
nuclear interactions in ultra-peripheral heavy-ion collisions.
Ultra-strong
electromagnetic
fields
in
relativistic
heavy-ion collisions are manifested as a comoving pho-
ton cloud accompanying the nuclei [1, 2], enabling pho-
tonuclear interactions. In these processes, a photon fluc-
tuates into a virtual vector meson or hadron-antihadron
pair, which scatters via Pomeron exchange (gluonic ex-
changes). Such interactions provide a unique probe of the
photon’s hadronic structure [3–5] and serve as a comple-
mentary tool for studying key nuclear properties, includ-
ing nuclear gluon distributions [6] and the overall size of
the nucleus [7, 8].
A quasi-real spin-1 photon can fluctuate into (i) a vir-
tual vector meson (JP C = 1−−), or (ii) a virtual hadron-
antihadron pair, giving rise to non-resonant photopro-
duction, known as the Drell-S¨oding process. In the case
of scalar mesons such as π+π−, the angular momentum is
encoded in the pair orbital momentum, resulting in quan-
tum entanglement between the π+ and π−in the relative-
motion spatial wave function. A separate quantum phe-
nomenon arises from the collision geometry: since both
nuclei can act as photon emitters or targets, the emis-
sion source (nucleus A or B) becomes indistinguishable,
establishing a “double-slit” interference pattern at the
femtometer scale [7, 9]. This interference induces second-
order cosine oscillations in the asymmetries of the decay
angular ∆ϕ distributions [10]. In this picture, interfer-
ence at the pair level arises from the indistinguishability
of the photon emission sources, while the entanglement
of the π+π−final state allows this interference to mani-
fest in the measured decay angular distributions, giving
rise to EESI effect.
The Drell–S¨oding process is a sensitive probe of the
nuclear gluon structure, nuclear gluon saturation, and
shadowing effects [11, 12].
It has two dominant dia-
grams, corresponding to π+−nucleus and π−−nucleus
scattering [13, 14], as depicted in Fig. 1. A sub-leading
π+π−−nucleus coupling diagram is included to ensure
gauge invariance[13, 15].
In 1960, Drell first proposed
these diagrams as promising contributors to secondary
beams at SLAC [16].
Later in 1966, S¨oding carried
out a full calculation of the γp →π+π−p process and
used it to explain the observed skewing of the ρ0 res-
onance peak [17].
In diffractive photoproduction, the
momentum-transfer |t| distribution primarily reflects the
transverse spatial profile of the target through its form
factor. At the same time, it can also be sensitive to the
transverse size of the interacting dipole via the convolu-
tion of the photon and hadronic wavefunctions with the
scattering amplitude. In particular, a steeper |t| depen-
dence is generally associated with larger effective trans-
verse configurations [18–20]. In this context, comparing
the |t| spectra of the resonant and non-resonant π+π−
contributions within the same mass and rapidity range
provides a way to probe potential differences in their un-
derlying transverse dipole sizes.
Vector meson photoproduction, e.g., ρ0 and J/ψ, has
been measured extensively in ultraperipheral collisions
(UPCs) of heavy ions [21–34]. However, the non-resonant
Drell-S¨oding process remains poorly characterized due
to its small cross section and overlap with resonant ρ0.
The Drell-S¨oding term cannot be isolated on an event-by-
event basis and is extracted statistically after subtract-
ing known contributions, relying on its interference with
the ρ0 photoproduction. In previous ρ0 measurements,
the Drell-S¨oding component is treated with an unknown
amplitude formulated as a constant [23–26, 29] or a pa-
rameterised function [35, 36]. The extracted Drell-S¨oding
contribution depends on the assumed mass dependence of
this term. Therefore, adopting a more realistic spectrum
derived from model calculations would be desirable to
achieve a more precise measurements of the Drell-S¨oding
process. With growing interest in exclusive hadronic pair
production [7, 37–41], precise measurements of the Drell-
S¨oding process offer a new handle to quantify decay-
induced effects in nuclear tomography and EESI via com-
parisons with ρ0 photoproduction. A precise character-
ization of the Drell-S¨oding process is also important for
disentangling excited ρ0 states, e.g., ρ(1450) and ρ(1700),
in exclusive π+π−production [29].

The measurement of exclusive π+π−production was
conducted in Au+Au UPCs at √sNN = 200 GeV at
the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) using the
Solenoidal Tracker at RHIC (STAR) [43].
An inclu-
sive UPC trigger was applied for the pre-selection to the
data [44].
This trigger relied on rapidity gaps defined
by the small beam-beam counter (BBC) tiles on both
sides of the detector (3 < η < 5) [45, 46], low activity in
the mid-rapidity in the barrel time-of-flight (TOF) sys-
tem [47, 48] (TOF multiplicity between 1 and 7), and
the coincidence of neutrons hitting both the east and
west zero degree calorimeters (ZDC) [49, 50]. The inte-
grated luminosities for the triggered data sets recorded
in 2010, 2011, and 2014 are 679, 859, and 1464 µb−1 with
10% uncertainty, respectively. The momenta of charged-

chamber (TPC) covering pseudorapidities |η| < 1.0 for

also provided specific ionization energy loss (dE/dx) mea-
surements for particle identification, which were used to
construct the deviation parameter nσπ by comparing the
measured dE/dx to the value predicted for a pion.
Further offline cuts on the events were required. Events
were required to have a primary vertex reconstructed by
a pair of charged-particle tracks with a longitudinal dis-
tance of |Vz| < 100 cm to the nominal center of STAR.
The associated charged-particle tracks were required to
have at least 15 fit points and 10 dE/dx points to ensure
sufficient momentum and dE/dx resolutions. Charged-
particle tracks were required to match to TOF hits in
order to mitigate the effect of out-of-time pileup. The
distance of closest approach (DCA) for charged-particle
tracks was required to be less than 1.5 cm to the primary
collision vertex to suppress contamination by secondary
decay particles. High purity π+π−pairs were selected by
requiring χ2

also compared with the data and found to underesti-

In conclusion, we report the observation of EESI in
the Drell-S¨oding process and the pair pT and M de-
pendence of the differential cross section for the Drell-
S¨oding process and ρ0 photoproduction in Au+Au UPC
at √sNN = 200 GeV at RHIC. A model-based Drell-
S¨oding shape is employed to separate different processes
involved in exclusive π+π−production.
The observa-
tion of EESI effects demonstrates that the non-resonant
π+π−pairs are entangled in the Drell-S¨oding process.
The amplitude, A2∆ϕ, for the Drell-S¨oding process and
ρ0 photoproduction are found to have similar decreasing
trends along the pair pT . Compared to the ρ0, an en-
hancement is observed in the Drell-S¨oding process in pair
pT in the range 0.05 < pT < 0.1 GeV/c. The measured
A2∆ϕ for these processes is found to have no obvious pair
mass Mπ+π−dependence, and the amplitude A2∆ϕ for
Drell-S¨oding is 3.0 σ higher than for ρ0 photoproduction
over the observed mass regions. Moreover, the observed
Drell-S¨oding |t| spectrum is softer when compared to ρ0
photoproduction. These results advance the exploration
of quantum interference phenomena in photon–nucleus
interactions, providing a basis for future investigations
in ultraperipheral heavy-ion collisions.
