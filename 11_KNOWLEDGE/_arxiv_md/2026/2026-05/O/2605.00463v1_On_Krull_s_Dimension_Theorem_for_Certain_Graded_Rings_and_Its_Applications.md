RIRAI IKEDA
Abstract. This paper explores the dimension theory of non-Noetherian graded rings by intro-
ducing the class of Hilbert-Serre rings. We generalize Krull’s dimension theorem and Smoke’s
dimension theorem by establishing the fundamental inequalities dim(R) ≤GKdimk(R) ≤d(R)
for any Hilbert-Serre ring R, where d(R) is the pole order of its Poincar´e series at t = 1. Fur-
thermore, we apply these results to initial algebras, proving that all these dimensions, including
the transcendence degree, coincide for monomial algebras. Finally, we provide explicit examples
demonstrating that these inequalities can be strict in general, even for integral domains.

1. Introduction
Throughout this paper, all rings are commutative with identity, and all ring homomorphisms
preserve identity.
Dimension theory for Noetherian rings has been one of the central topics in commutative
algebra. One of the crowning achievements in this area is Krull’s dimension theorem [Mat86,
Theorem 13.4], which provides a fundamental understanding of the Krull dimension.
Theorem 1.1 (Krull’s dimension theorem). Let (R, m) be a Noetherian local ring. Then we
have
dim(R) = d(R) = δ(R),
where dim(R) is the Krull dimension of R, d(R) is the order of the pole at t = 1 of the Poincar´e
series of the associated graded ring grm(R), and δ(R) is the minimal number of generators of
m-primary ideals.
In the graded setting, Smoke’s dimension theorem states that for a Noetherian N-graded ring
R, the Krull dimension coincides with the order of the pole at t = 1 of its Poincar´e series,
denoted by d(R) [Smo72, Theorem 5.5].
Theorem 1.2 (Smoke’s dimension theorem). Let R = L
n≥0 Rn be a Noetherian N-graded ring
with R0 a field. Then we have
dim(R) = d(R) = s(R),
where d(R) is the order of the pole at t = 1 of the Poincar´e series of R and s(R) is the minimal
number of homogeneous elements of R such that R is a finitely generated module over the
subalgebra generated by those elements.

However, when the Noetherian condition is dropped, the dimension theory becomes signifi-
cantly more complex. In this paper, we investigate the relationship between various dimension
concepts for non-Noetherian graded rings. To investigate this relationship, we introduce the
class of Hilbert-Serre rings in Definition 3.1, which are graded rings whose Poincar´e series admit
a finite pole order at t = 1.
Our main results concern the relationship among five specific invariants for a graded ring
R = L
n≥0 Rn with R0 = k a field, which are as follows:
• dimgr(R): the graded Krull dimension of R,
• dim(R): the Krull dimension of R,
• tr. degk(R): the transcendence degree of R over k (defined only for integral domains),
• GKdimk(R): the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of R over k,
• d(R): the order of the pole at t = 1 of the Poincar´e series of R.
The Gelfand-Kirillov dimension was originally introduced in [GK66] and is widely used in
non-commutative ring theory and representation theory as an alternative to the Krull dimension.
We note the following properties of the GK dimension of a ring R containing a field k as a
subring:
• If R is a finitely generated algebra over a field k, then GKdimk(R) = dim(R),
• If R is a finitely generated integral domain over k, then GKdimk(R) = tr. degk(R).
For a comprehensive treatment of the GK dimension, we refer the reader to [KL00].
For a Hilbert-Serre domain R, we first establish the fundamental inequalities dim(R) ≤
tr. degk(R) ≤d(R). While the transcendence degree tr. degk(R) is primarily defined for integral
domains, we show that the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension GKdimk(R) can play an analogous role
for general Hilbert-Serre rings. Specifically, we prove that dim(R) ≤GKdimk(R) ≤d(R) holds
for any Hilbert-Serre ring R. Since the GK dimension coincides with the transcendence degree
in the case of integral domains, our result provides a natural generalization of the dimension
inequalities to the non-domain case.
Consequently, we obtain the following main theorem,
Theorem 4.7.
Theorem 1.3. Let R = L
n≥0 Rn be an N-graded ring with R0 = k a field. If R is a Hilbert-
Serre ring, then we have:
dim(R) ≤GKdimk(R) ≤d(R).
Moreover, if R is a domain, then we have:
dim(R) ≤tr. degk(R) = GKdimk(R) ≤d(R).
One of the significant applications of our framework is the dimension theory of initial algebras
and SAGBI (Subalgebra Analogue to Gr¨obner Bases for Ideals) bases.
Initial algebras are
fundamental in the study of subalgebras, yet they have a well-known drawback: the initial
algebra in<(S) of a Noetherian subalgebra S is not necessarily Noetherian. For example, in the
study of invariant rings and initial algebras, Kuroda showed many cases where the initial algebra
is not Noetherian; see [Kur02]. Consequently, our observations and generalization of Smoke’s
dimension theorem to the non-Noetherian case are essential to establish a consistent dimension
theory for initial algebras. For the general theory and various constructions of SAGBI bases, we
refer the reader to [RS90]. Furthermore, SAGBI bases have significant applications in modern
commutative algebra, such as the study of determinantal rings and algebras defined by minors;
see [BCRV22]. From a computational perspective, a package for computing SAGBI bases is
available in Macaulay2, [BCD+] and [BCD+24]. Although computer-assisted calculations are
not directly utilized in the main results of this paper, the author benefited from employing this
package during the research process.

prove our main theorem, Theorem 4.7, regarding the dimension inequalities. In Section 5, we

3. Hilbert-Serre Rings

order at t = 1. If R is Noetherian, the Poincar´e series of R has that invariant by the Hilbert-
Serre theorem; see [AM69, Theorem 11.1]. However, the converse does not hold, i.e., there are
non-Noetherian graded rings whose Poincar´e series has the order of the pole at t = 1. Therefore,
it is worth defining the following class of graded rings.

4. Dimension Inequalities for Hilbert-Serre Rings
In this section, first we show that for a Hilbert-Serre domain, dim(R) ≤tr. degk(R) ≤d(R)
holds, where R = L
n≥0 Rn is an N-graded ring with R0 = k a field and tr. degk(R) is the
transcendence degree of R over k. Then we show that for a Hilbert-Serre ring (not necessarily
a domain), we have dim(R) ≤d(R). Second, we review the definition of the Gelfand-Kirillov
dimension (GKdim). And then, we examine relations between GKdim and the other dimensions.
Finally, we derive the main Theorem 4.7 by combining these results.
Lemma 4.1. Let R be a domain containing a field k as a subring. Then we have dim(R) ≤
tr. degk(R).
Proof. Note that if R is a finitely generated algebra over k, then the equality holds; see [Mat86,

r ≤dim(S) = tr. degk(S) ≤tr. degk(R).
This completes the proof.

5. Application to Initial Algebra
In the previous section, we established the general inequalities dim(R) ≤GKdim(R) ≤d(R)
for Hilbert-Serre rings. In this section, we apply this result to initial algebras. Our main goal
is to demonstrate that, for this specific class of graded rings, all the dimensions coincide.
Let us briefly recall the standard setup and notation for initial algebras. Let S = k[x1, . . . , xn]
be a polynomial ring over a field k. We recall that a monomial order < on S is a total order on
the set of monomials in S that satisfies the following properties:
(1) 1 < xi for all i,
(2) If u < v, then uw < vw for all monomials u, v, w.
In the standard Gr¨obner basis theory, the initial operation is defined for ideals of S with respect
to a fixed monomial order. However, in this paper, we consider the initial ”algebra”, i.e., we
consider the initial algebra of a subalgebra of S instead of an ideal.
Definition 5.1. Let R = k[x1, . . . , xn] be a polynomial ring over a field k and < be a monomial
order on R. Let f1, . . . , fm ∈R be homogeneous polynomials, and let S = k[f1, . . . , fm] be a
homogeneous subalgebra of R. We define the initial algebra in<(S) of S with respect to < as
follows:
in<(S) = k[in<(g) | g ∈S].
If a subset F ⊂S generates the initial algebra in<(S), i.e., in<(S) = k[in<(g) | g ∈F], then we
say F is a SAGBI basis of S with respect to <.
