Abstract
We investigate the vibrational, optical, and electronic properties of insulating YbN thin films using Raman spectroscopy,
Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy, and electrical transport measurements, supported by density functional theory.
Raman spectra reveal the LO(Γ) phonon and a cation-vacancy mode, while the optical conductivity identifies the TO
phonon and an absorption edge corresponding to a 1.7 eV N 2p →Yb 5d transition. The films exhibit thermally activated
resistivity consistent with an insulating ground state. An additional defect induced absorption tail below the intrinsic
band gap is observed, which in combination with the electrical measurements indicates the Fermi energy resides in a
disordered conduction band minimum.
Keywords:
Rare earth nitride, optical conductivity, semiconductor, electronic structure.
The rare earth nitrides (LnNs) are a series of rock-
salt semiconductors which have been subjected to intense
investigation for their potential in a wide array of appli-
cations [1, 2].
They exhibit a broad range of magnetic
behaviors at cryogenic temperatures based on the choice
of Ln due to the difference in localized 4f state occupa-
tion as the series is traversed.
The clearest example of
one finding an application is GdN, due to its combination
of resistivity and large spin angular momentum which is
of interest in the field of superconducting spintronics re-
search [3–10]. ErN, on the other hand has found potential
application in cryocooling based on its large specific heat
at liquid helium temperatures [11, 12].
It is only in the past two decades that almost all mem-
bers of the LnNs have been found to exhibit properties
that resemble an insulating rather than metallic ground
states. This is due to historical confusion originating ex-
perimentally from contamination and unintentional doping
during sample preparation. Advances in preparation con-
ditions, such as powder preparation and characterization
entirely within a glove box, or thin film preparation in an
ultra high vacuum deposition system (≤10−9 mbar) with
an appropriately thick capping layer have been used to
study the LnNs with low levels of contamination [13–16].
Often, theory can provide insight into the ground state of a
compound, however in the LnNs an inappropriate descrip-
tion of the exchange and correlation by use of just the local
density or generalized gradient approximations in density
function theory (DFT) generally predicted metallic ground
states. Employment of self interaction-corrected, Hubbard
U, quasiparticle self-consistent GW, or (screened) exact-

that is generally found to be consistent with experimental
results of the LnN ground states as insulators [17–23].
Ytterbium nitride (YbN), the smallest of the magnetic
LnNs with a lattice constant of 4.78 Å is the only member
with an antiferromagnetic (AFM) ground state [24–26]. A
further study found that YbN obeys a type-III rock salt
antiferromagnetic ordering with long-range ordering along
the ⃗k = [1, 0, 0.5] crystal axis when below the Néel tem-
perature TN = 0.790 ± 0.005 K, similar to YbAs [26, 27].
Moving now to the vibrational and structural properties,
Degiorgi et al. [25] report optical measurements finding a
TO(Γ) mode frequency of 275 cm-1, a plasma frequency of
0.2 eV, and an enhanced optical absorption above 0.8 eV. A
recent investigation using Fourier transform infrared spec-
trometry (FTIR) by Holmes-Hewett et al. [28] on a broad
range of LnN thin films grown by MBE found a similar
TO(Γ) mode frequency in thin-film YbN. The recent stud-
ies on thin film YbN found a variation in lattice constant
(4.78−4.82 Å), band gap (1.5−1.7 eV), mixed evidence for
a multivalent Yb2+/3+ state, and plasma frequency, sug-
gesting that the ground state properties of YbN are not
yet settled [15, 16, 24].
In this work we study and compare the properties of
low-conductivity YbN thin films to previous reports and
compare YbN to the other LnNs in the series.
We in-
vestigate its structural, vibrational, and (opto)electronic
properties. From optical measurements and DFT compu-
tation with Hubbard energy correction, we find that our
low-conductivity YbN has a direct 2p →5d gap of 1.7 eV.
Based on our work we find that the bottom of the conduc-
tion band in YbN is significantly disordered, manifesting
in the transport and optical measurements.

The vibrational properties were studied using Raman
spectroscopy.
A Jobin-Yvon LabRam spectrometer was
employed for the Raman study using a 514.5 nm (2.41 eV)
excitation laser. The Raman spectrum from a YbN thin
film on a Al2O3 substrate is shown in Figure 2 (black),
below which is a Raman spectrum that shows phonon
modes originating from the substrate (blue) from [30]. The
Fröhlich polaron-enabled LO(Γ) phonon mode appears at
580 cm-1. Previous Raman measurements found signals
at Raman shifts at 365 and 575 cm-1 [25].
It is likely
that these signals originate from Yb2O3 and YbN, re-
spectively [31, 32]. We also find and record the presence
of the corresponding second harmonic 2LO(Γ) mode at
1160 cm-1, as well as a Yb cation vacancy-derived mode
(V Yb) at 1347 cm-1 [22]. The latter of these is a highly
polarizable Raman mode has been demonstrated in other
LnNs as a symmetric breathing mode of six nitrogen atoms
neighboring the cation vacancy defect site (previous re-

hash also appears in ion-grown LuN and GdN films, sug-

Together, these results point towards YbN possessing
an insulating ground state with an LO-TO splitting in-
line with other LnNs. Its electronic structure, however,
has only been studied in the ferromagnetic state, omit-
ting its true antiferromagnetic ground state. Experiments
addressing the electronic structure of YbN however have
been necessarily conducted on antiferromagnetic samples.
To connect the experiment with theory this, density func-
tional theory calculations were conducted using Quantum
ESPRESSO 7.3 on the antiferromagnetic state of YbN [37–
39].
The pseudopotentials developed by Dal Corso [40]
for N with 2s and 2p states and Topsakal and Wentzcov-
itch [41] for Yb with 4f, 5s, 5p, 5d, and 6s states in the
valence shell were used for this work. Spin-polarized cal-
culations are essential for describing the 4f 13 occupation
for Yb3+. The calculation was found to be well-converged
using a kinetic energy cutoff of 60 Ry (816 eV) in conjunc-
tion with a 6 × 6 × 3 k-point grid for the 16 atom 1 × 1 × 2
supercell AFM-III extension of the cubic unit cell of the
Fm3m structure.
Rotationally symmetric Hubbard U parameters were
applied to the Yb 4f and 5d states in order to improve
their description over the standard generalized-gradient-
approximation [19, 42]. If not considered, the electronic
structures for most all LnNs are predicted to be met-
als with 4f states partially occupied at the Fermi energy
and/or with N 2p-Ln 5d valence-conduction band overlap.
We identify the appropriate Hubbard U parameter for the
4f states by following the scheme of Larson et al. [19], and
for the 5d states by tuning the band gap to the strong op-
tical absorption onset energy of 1.7 eV at the X point in

The corresponding band structure and density of states

where the thirteen occupied 4f states lay 7 −9 eV below,
and the fourteenth 4f state appears 1 eV above the valence

ous measurements and calculations [19, 25]. Our computa-

rowest direct gap is 2p →4f at Γ with a value of 1.3 eV.
The insulating ground state is in agreement with recent
measurements and calculations [19, 24, 41].
The energy difference in the minimum energy of the un-
occupied 4f and 5d states is rather small, approximately
0.2 eV. Doping YbN with nitrogen vacancies will lift the
Fermi energy into the conduction band, bringing it close
to the unoccupied 4f states, which themselves may con-
tribute to measured transport properties.
Interestingly,
there is 5d-4f hybridization along the Γ −X path, but
a lack thereof for the X −W path. This may cause the
Yb3+ states to transition to Yb2+ for highly doped sam-
ples, therefore providing a mixed Yb2+/3+ valence. This
can be studied by measurement of an enhanced effective
mass or direct observation of Yb2+ using XPS [25]. The
originally-reported properties of what is now understood
to be nonstoichiometric N-poor YbN point towards a car-
rier effective mass much greater than that of ScN, sug-
gesting YbN possesses heavy Fermion or Kondo insula-
tor physics which may be accessible via tuning the car-
rier concentration through control of preparation condi-
tions [25, 43].
Previous XPS measurements on the 4d
states have found mixed evidence of Yb2+ [15, 16], and
measurements accessing the 3d core level clearly identi-
fied the presence of Yb2+[44]. This discrepancy is likely
caused by a combination of surface effects and peak sep-
aration due to the difference in binding energies of those
core levels, and merits further investigation.
Chen et al. [15] suggest a minimum indirect band gap
of 1.64 eV from XPS measurements by assuming that the
Fermi energy resides in the conduction band and extrapo-
lating the drop-off of the density of states at the top of the
valence band. It is known from calculations on Ln3+N3-
species that the valence band energy at Γ is raised relative
to the X point for systems with occupied 4f T2u states,
as was calculated for example in NdN [19].
It remains
possible that the extrapolated VBM that Chen et al. [15]
identify is the sharp drop-off of the N 2p density of states
for energies above the X-point, and the true indirect band
gap is smaller than they estimate. This is because the den-
sity of states reduces significantly at valence band states
toward the Γ-point as we find in our calculation. Their
result for the indirect band gap (1.64 eV) is greater than
our calculated Γ-X band gap of 0.8 eV.
When attempting to identify the presence of Yb2+ in
YbN, it is worth drawing parallels with EuN for which the
compound exhibits a multiple valence (Eu2+/3+) depend-
ing on the sample preparation conditions conditions [20,
45, 46]. This is because as the EuN is doped with nitro-
gen vacancies, Eu3+ (4f 6) converts to Eu2+ (4f 7) with a
distinct difference in magnetic signature converting from
an L = 3, S = 3 and J = 0 state to a L = 0, S = 7/2
and J = 7/2 state by Hund’s rules.
It is possible that
something similar may occur with non-stoichiometric YbN
where Yb3+ (4f 13) may transition to Yb2+ (4f 14) config-
uration, i.e., from L = 3, S = 1/2 and J = 7/2 state to
a L = 0, S = 0 and J = 0 nonmagnetic ground state by
Hund’s rules. There is also direct experimental evidence
that the Kondo effect, i.e., heavy fermion transport, takes
place in EuN [46]. Yb-based multivalent compounds such
as the prototypical YbAl3 can exhibit highly non-standard
Fermi surfaces due to valence fluctuations, giving rise to
heavy fermion transport properties [47, 48]. Doping YbN
with electrons may be a method to access those heavy
fermion transport properties originating from a multiva-
lent Yb2+/3+ state.
In summary, the electronic structure of antiferromag-
netic YbN remains under debate.
We elucidate a few
points by a report on some properties of low-conductivity
YbN deposited using ion-assisted molecular beam epitaxy
which exhibited an exponential temperature-dependence
of resistivity, likely associated with hopping transport of
in-gap defect states, which is in general agreement with our
optical data which suggests a disordered conduction band
minimum. Derived from optical measurements on low con-
ductivity YbN the direct gap at the X point is measured
to be 1.7 eV, with the DFT computation suggesting an
indirect gap of 0.8 eV. The sample shows distinct TO(Γ)
