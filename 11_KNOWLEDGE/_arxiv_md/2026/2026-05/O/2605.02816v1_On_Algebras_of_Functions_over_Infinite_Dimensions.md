ON ALGEBRAS OF FUNCTIONS OVER INFINITE DIMENSIONS
DIMITRIOS GIANNAKIS, MOHAMMAD JAVAD LATIFI JEBELLI, AND MICHAEL MONTGOMERY
Abstract. We introduce a family of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces AΛ of holomorphic functions
defined on an infinite–dimensional domain in a separable Hilbert space, H. The reproducing kernel
of AΛ is constructed using the covariance operator associated with a Gaussian measure on H,
along with a holomorphic function Λ on the unit disk. Under certain conditions on the kernel,
AΛ is closed under pointwise multiplication, giving it the structure of a reproducing kernel Hilbert
algebra (RKHA). We also study twisted canonical commutation relations on these RKHAs, where
the creation and annihilation operators are both bounded.
1. Introduction
We construct and study a family of function spaces on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space that
are simultaneously reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHSs) and Banach algebras with respect
to pointwise multiplication.

Gaussian fields. In the absence of Lebesgue measures, Gaussian measures are natural measures
in the infinite–dimensional setting. Let L2(H, µ) be the space of square-integrable functions on H
with respect to a background Gaussian measure µ on H. We assume that K: H →H is the trace
class covariance operator associated with µ.
Gaussian measures play a crucial rule in the theory of stochastic processes.
The Cameron–
Martin Hilbert space Hµ ⊂H is defined via the covariance structure of the Gaussian measure µ
and the pair (Hµ, H) is called an abstract Wiener space. The interested reader is referred to [6] for
Gaussian measures on infinite dimensions, Cameron–Martin Hilbert spaces, and their application
in stochastic processes. The space L2(H, µ) contains as a closed subspace the infinite-dimensional
Segal–Bargmann space, HL2(H, µ), consisting of holomorphic functions of infinitely many variables
associated with eigenvectors of K. Infinite-dimensional Segal–Bargmann spaces have been studied
extensively by many authors; see [17] for an overview of the subject.
Spaces of square-integrable functions on an infinite-dimensional background, H, also arise natu-
rally in rigorous treatments of quantum field theory. Associated to a Gaussian measure µ on H one
defines a Gaussian field Φ : Hµ →L2(H, µ) as a family of Gaussian random variables in L2(H, µ)
indexed by vectors in Hµ ⊂H with the property that ⟨f, g⟩Hµ = ⟨Φ(f), Φ(g)⟩L2(H,µ). Here, the
map Φ is defined by Φ(h) = ⟨h, ·⟩H ∈L2(H, µ). In the context of Euclidean quantum field theory,
Gaussian fields are known as free fields and they describe systems without interactions. Interacting
systems are modeled as non-Gaussian fields Φ through various regularization techniques. In some
cases, the regularization process can be performed in a mathematically rigorous manner. See [25]
for a treatment of Gaussian processes in constructive quantum field theory.
Given the inclusion Hµ ⊂H, the set B, defined in (1), can be written as the unit ball in the
Cameron-Martin Hilbert space. i.e. B = {ξ ∈Hµ : ∥ξ∥Hµ < 1}. As a result, a function f ∈AΛ
can be considered as a function on the unit ball of Hµ. Below, we show that every such RKHS
AΛ consists of holomorphic functions on this unit ball B. Furthermore, in section 5.1, we show
existence of a Gelfand triple ˆ
AΛ ⊂HL2(H, µ) ⊂AΛ. Here HL2(H, µ) is the holomorphic subspace
of L2(H, µ), and the inclusion HL2(H, µ) ⊂AΛ is given by restricting a holomorphic function on
H to ¯B. In the context of stochastic processes, such a triple defines what are known as generalized
Brownian functionals, where elements of AΛ are the analogs of generalized functions. This traces
back to work of Hida [19] and has applications in stochastic partial differential equation theory
[21,27].
Reproducing kernel Hilbert algebras. In [14], the notion of a reproducing kernel Hilbert
algebra (RKHA) was defined as an RKHS with additional coalgebra structure.
Definition 1. An RKHS A on a set X with reproducing kernel k: X × X →C is an RKHA if it
admits a bounded comultiplication operator ∆: A →A ⊗A satisfying ∆k(x, ·) = k(x, ·) ⊗k(x, ·)
for all x ∈X.
One verifies that the adjoint of ∆from Definition 1 is a (bounded) operator that implements
pointwise function multiplication, ∆∗(f ⊗g) = fg. This makes A a Banach algebra with respect
to a norm induced by the operator norm of multiplication operators on A; see [14, Section 2].

in the family AΛ. We prove that if Λ ∈CW

(a) Elements of AΛ are holomorphic on B ⊂Hµ.
(b) AΛ is invariant under the action of the unitary group of Hµ.
(c) If Λ ∈CW
ess then AΛ is a unital RKHA.

2. Gaussian measures
In this section, we first review basic concepts in the theory of infinite-dimensional Gaussian
measures on H such as the covariance operator and the Cameron–Martin Hilbert space Hµ. We
then study the corresponding space of square-integrable functions and its inner product structure.
In a real setting, many of the statements and definitions of this section can be found in [6].
