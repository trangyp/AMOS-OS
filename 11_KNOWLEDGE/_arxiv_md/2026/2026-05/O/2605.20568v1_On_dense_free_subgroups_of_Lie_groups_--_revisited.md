EMMANUEL BREUILLARD AND TSACHIK GELANDER
Abstract. The main purpose of this note is to correct an erroneous
argument given in the proof of [1, Proposition 2.7]. This does not affect
the validity of any of the statements in [1]. We also provide details which
were missing in the proof of [1, Proposition 3.3], and correct [1, Example
2.2].
In the proof of [1, Proposition 2.7] it is erroneously stated that the com-
mutator subgroup of a finitely generated dense subgroup Γ of a connected
Lie group G contains a finitely generated subgroup, which is dense in the
commutator subgroup [G, G]. This is not true. In fact, there are such exam-
ples where every finitely generated subgroup of [Γ, Γ] is discrete in [G, G],
see [4].
The purpose of this note is to give a valid proof of [1, Proposition 2.7].
Actually, we will prove the following slightly stronger statement:
Proposition 1. Let G be a connected Lie group and Γ a finitely generated
dense subgroup. Then Γ contains a dense subgroup on d+d1 ≤2d generators,
where d = dim(G) and Rd1 is the maximal Euclidean quotient of G.
Remark 2. We remark that [1, Theorem 1.3] and [2, Theorem 5.7] rely
on [1, Proposition 2.7], i.e. on Proposition 1 above. The proofs of these
theorems written in [1, 2] are valid thanks to the correction in the proof of
Proposition 1 provided below.
The problem in the original argument is circumscribed to the second
paragraph of the proof of [1, Proposition 2.7], which deals with solvable
non-abelian Lie groups. For the sake of completeness we shall also review
the reduction to that case.
Proof. Let G be a connected Lie group. Denote G0 = G and Gi+1 = [Gi, Gi]
where [Gi, Gi] is the commutator group of the group Gi. As dim(G) is finite,
this sequence stabilizes at some finite step k. Then the group Gk is connected
and topologically perfect (i.e. has dense commutator subgroup) and G/Gk
is connected and solvable. Note also that as Γ is dense in G its commutator
subgroup [Γ, Γ] is dense in G1 and by induction Γ ∩Gi is dense in Gi for
i = 1, . . . , k. In view of [1, Corollary 2.5] there are t elements in Γ ∩Gk
which generate a dense subgroup in Gk, where t is the minimal number of
generators for the Lie algebra of Gk and in particular t ≤dim(Gk). Thus
we need to show that there are dim(G/Gk)+d1 elements in Γ that generate

Assume first that G is abelian. Then G ∼= Rd1 × Td2, where T denotes a
one-dimensional compact torus T ≃R/Z. We can then find d1 elements in Γ
which generate a discrete cocompact subgroup. Dividing by this subgroup,
we may assume that G is a torus. Then we argue by induction on dim G.
As Γ is finitely generated and dense it contains an element γ of infinite
order. By replacing γ by some power γj if necessary, we may assume that
γ generates a subgroup with connected closure C of positive dimension.
By induction, the proposition holds for G/C. Lift arbitrarily to G a set
of dim G/C generators for a dense subgroup of the image of Γ in G/C.
Together with γ they generate a dense subgroup in G.
We are left with the case where G is solvable and non-abelian.
In a
connected solvable Lie group, density of a subgroup is equivalent to density
of its image modulo the second commutator G2 (cf. [3, Theorem 6.3]). If
G is nilpotent, then density of a subgroup is equivalent to density in G/G1,
which reduces to the abelian case considered above. Thus, we may assume
G is metabelian and not nilpotent.
Since G/G1 is abelian, we may find m := dim(G/G1) + d1 elements
g1, ..., gm in Γ, which, modulo G1, generate a dense subgroup. We will show
that we may pick k ≤dim(G1) additional elements h1, . . . , hk ∈[Γ, Γ] ⊂
Γ, which, together with the gi’s generate a dense subgroup in G.
Since
m + k ≤dim(G) + d1 this will prove the proposition.
The construction
below will work for any subgroup ∆≤Γ which projects densely to G/G1
and produces ⟨∆, h1, . . . , hk⟩which is dense in G. Concretely, below, one
may take ∆= ⟨g1, . . . , gm⟩.
We will make use of the following simple lemma:
Lemma 3. Let G be a connected group, and g ∈G a non-central element.
Then the conjugacy class gG of g is not discrete in G.
Proof. Indeed gG is connected and non-trivial, hence non-discrete.
□
Since Γ is dense and G is not nilpotent, we may pick h1 in [Γ, Γ] which is
not central in G. Set
N := ⟨∆, h1⟩∩G1.
Note that N is normal in G. Indeed, since G1 is both normal in G and
abelian, the normalizer NG(N) contains both ∆and G1 and hence ∆G1
which is dense in G.
Let H be the closure of ⟨∆, h1⟩. Since G1 is abelian and HG1 is dense
in G, the conjugacy class of h1 in H is dense in the conjugacy class of h1
in G, which by the lemma above is non-discrete. But this conjugacy class
is entirely contained in N = H ∩G1. By Cartan’s theorem, being a closed
subgroup, N is a Lie group. Thus, N has positive dimension.
If N = G1 we may stop here, while if N is a proper subgroup of G1 we may
replace G by G/N. Note that the abelianization G/G1 ∼= (G/N)/(G1/N)

Remark 4. The proof above gives a bound which is occasionally better than
the d + d1 bound stated in the Proposition. That bound is
dim(G/G2) + d1 + t,
where, as above, G/G2 is the largest metabelian Lie quotient of G and t is the
minimal number of generators for the Lie algebra of the topologically perfect
radical Gk.
Obviously dim(G/G2) + t ≤dim(G) and in most cases this
inequality is strict. The ‘worst’ case scenario is, of course, the Euclidean
case G ∼= Rd for which the optimal bound is 2d as stated in general in
[1, Proposition 2.7]. In all other cases dim(G/G2) + d1 + t < 2 dim(G).
An additional correction. We take this opportunity to correct an addi-
tional inaccuracy. Example 2.2 in [1] attempts to produce a Lie group G
whose Lie algebra is not 2-generated, but every dense subgroup of G admits
a dense F2. In [1, Example 2.2] we suggested G = ( ^
SL2(R) × T)/⟨(a, α)⟩,
where ^

torus, a is a generator for the center of ^

dense subgroup of T. The problem with this example is that the Lie algebra
Lie(G) ∼= sl2(R) ⊕R is actually 2-generated. However, one can correct the
example by taking G to be
G = ( ^

where T3 is the 3-dimensional torus and α is a generator of a dense subgroup
of T3. The Lie algebra sl2(R) ⊕R3 is not 2-generated.
It is also worth mentioning that although this example shows that the
converse to [1, Theorem 2.1] does not hold for topologically perfect Lie
groups in general, it does hold for perfect Lie groups. Indeed, in a perfect
Lie group elements near the identity generate a dense subgroup if and only
if their logarithms generate the Lie algebra. This follows from the fact that
perfect Lie groups do not have proper dense virtual Lie subgroups (see e.g.
[5, Chapter 2 §5]).
