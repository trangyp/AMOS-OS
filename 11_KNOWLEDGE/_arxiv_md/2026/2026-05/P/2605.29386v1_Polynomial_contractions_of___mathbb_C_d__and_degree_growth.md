Polynomial contractions of Cd and degree growth
Dmitrii Korshunov∗
May 29, 2026
Abstract
We give a simple example of a polynomial contraction automorphism of Cd,
d ≥3, with unbounded degree growth. Combined with Poincar´e–Dulac theorem it
provides an algebraic automorphism of Cd, d ≥3, which is holomorphically but not
algebraically linearizable.
1
Introduction
This note answers a question of Verbitsky and Ornea in connection with their work on
GAGA-type theorems for complex manifolds equipped with a holomorphic contraction
[7].
Definition 1. An automorphism γ ∈Aut Cd is called a contraction if 0 is the unique
fixed point and limn→∞γn(x) = 0 for all x ∈Cn.
Definition 2. Suppose a polynomial automorphism γ of Cn is given by
(x1, . . . xd) 7→(P1(x1, . . . xd), . . . (Pd(x1, . . . xd)). We define the degree of γ to be the largest
among degrees of Pi.
Question 1. Is there a polynomial contraction of Cd, such that degree of γn grows to
infinity with n →∞?
For each d ≥3 we construct an example of such contraction, and show that for d = 2
they do not exist. The property of an algebraic automorphism to be of bounded degree
growth is an algebraic conjugation invariant. On the other hand, Poincar´e-Dulac theorem
applied to our examples guarantees that they are holomorphically conjugate to a linear
map. Thus we obtain examples of automorphisms that are holomorphically conjugate to
a linear map but not algebraically conjugate to any linear map.

Algebraic groups of automorphisms and bounded
degree
We will need the notion of strict algebraicity of a polynomial automorphism, which cap-
tures the property of having bounded degree growth.
Definition 3. Let γ ∈Aut Ad. If there exists an algebraic action of an algebraic group
Φ : G × Ad →Ad, such that γ(·) = Φ(g, ·) for some g ∈G, then γ is called strictly
algebraic.
Proposition 1. A polynomial automorphism γ ∈Aut Ad is strictly algebraic if and only
if supn deg γn < +∞.
Proof. Let Aut ≤e be the (constructible) set of the space of all d-tuples of polynomials
of degree ≤e, naturally identified with Ad(d+e
e ). If deg γn ≤e for all n ≥0, then take
as G the Zariski closure of the set {γn : n ≥0} inside Aut ≤e. It is left to note that a
constructible group over C is actually algebraic.
Another way to see it, is to take the ind-Zariski closure of {γn : n ≥0} in Aut Ad
with its standard structure of an ind-group and see that when degree is bounded it lands
to the finite dimensional ind-subgroup, that is an algebraic group. For a comprehensive
treatment of groups of automorphisms as ind-groups see [5].
To prove the converse, recall that an action of an algebraic group G on the coordinate
ring of an affine variety is locally finite [2, Proposition 1.9]. That is, each polynomial
f ∈C[x1, . . . xd] belongs to a finite dimensional G-invariant subspace G · f (indeed, its
dimension is bounded from above by dim G). Now choose the coordinate polynomials
x1, . . . xd, and take the smallest subspace of the filtration in which all G · x1, . . . G · xd lie.
Remark 1. If γ is strictly algebraic then F ◦γ ◦F −1 is also strictly algebraic for any
polynomial automorphism F ∈Aut Ad.

Polynomial contractions
Theorem 1. Any contraction automorphism γ of C2 has bounded degree growth.
Proof. By results of Milnor and Friedland [4], any γ ∈Aut C2 is conjugate to either
1. Affine, with constant degree-growth.
2. Elementary, that is of the form e(x, y) = (ax + P(y), by + c), with constant degree
growth.
3. Loxodromic, i.e. a finite composition of H´enon automorphisms hi(x, y) = (ay +
Pi(x), x), deg Pi ≥2, with exponential degree growth .
On the other hand, by a theorem of Smillie [8] and Theorem 4.1 in [4] the topological
entropy of a loxodromic automorphism equals the logarithm of its degree. But a contrac-
tion does not have periodic points except the origin, hence its topological entropy must
be zero. Hence a loxodromic automorphism cannot be a contraction.
