Abstract. In this paper, we develop a construction of Poisson n-Lie algebras arising from n-Lie
algebras of Jacobians and establish conditions under which this construction yields a Poisson
n-Lie algebra. We also formulate a general conjecture in the unital case. In addition, we show
that tensor products of Poisson algebras admit natural Poisson n-Lie structures via suitable
quotient constructions. Conversely, we construct a Poisson algebra from a given Poisson n-Lie
algebra, thereby establishing a correspondence between these classes of algebras.
Furthermore, we obtain analogues of Engel’s and Lie’s theorems and provide a character-
ization of solvable and nilpotent Poisson n-Lie algebras in terms of the underlying algebraic
structures. We also introduce the notion of hypo-nilpotent ideals and prove results concerning
maximal hypo-nilpotent ideals in finite-dimensional solvable Poisson n-Lie algebras. Finally, we
show that generalized eigenspaces of multiplication operators form ideals.
1. Introduction
Inspired by the general concept of an Ω-algebra introduced by Kurosh [20], Filippov proposed a
higher-order generalization of Lie algebras, now known as n-Lie algebras [12] (also referred to in the
literature as Filippov algebras). As an example in the infinite-dimensional setting, he constructed
n-Lie structures defined in terms of Jacobians [13].
Another approach to n-Lie algebras originates from Nambu’s pioneering work [26], in which a
generalization of classical Hamiltonian mechanics was proposed. Motivated by Liouville’s theorem,
Nambu formulated an extension of Hamiltonian dynamics to a three-dimensional phase space. In
this framework, the equations of motion are determined by two Hamiltonian functions and depend
on three canonical variables.
Later, Takhtajan [29] systematically developed the foundational principles of a canonical formal-
ism for Nambu mechanics, thereby providing a geometric and algebraic interpretation of Nambu’s
generalization of Hamiltonian mechanics. Observing that the notion of the Nambu bracket leads
naturally to Nambu–Poisson manifolds, he extended the classical Poisson bracket, which is a bi-
nary operation on observables on phase space, to a higher-order multilinear operation of order
n ≥3. In this context, the concept of a Nambu algebra of order n was introduced as a natu-
ral higher-order generalization of a Lie algebra. In fact, a Nambu algebra of order n is another
name for an n-Lie algebra. In addition, the notion of a Nambu–Leibniz algebra of order n was
introduced, and subsequently the same structure was introduced in [6] under the name Leibniz
n-algebra, motivated by the fact that for n = 2 it reduces to a Leibniz algebra [22].
Since then, n-Lie algebras and related algebraic structures have attracted considerable attention,
and their theory has been further developed in a number of works, including [16,19,21,23,27,28],
as well as the references therein.
It is noteworthy that one of the principal examples of an n-Lie algebra, namely the n-Lie algebra
of Jacobians, naturally carries the structure of a Poisson n-Lie algebra. In fact, Cantarini and
Kac [5] proved that for n > 2, any simple linearly compact Poisson n-Lie algebra is isomorphic to
the algebra F[[x1, . . . , xn]] endowed with the n-ary bracket defined by the Jacobian determinant.
Of course, for n = 2, this construction reduces to a Poisson algebra.

An overview of various n-ary generalizations of Lie algebras, including n-Lie algebras, n-Leibniz
algebras, and (generalized) Poisson n-Lie structures, together with their applications in mathe-
matical physics and cohomological aspects, is presented in [8]. We also refer to [24] for notable
results concerning simple Poisson n-Lie algebras.
In this paper, we present a construction based on the n-Lie algebras of Jacobians by adjoining
an arbitrary number m ≥1 of additional columns consisting of fixed elements from an associative
commutative algebra. We show that this construction defines a Poisson n-Lie algebra if and only if
certain algebraic relations are satisfied. This result provides a tool for constructing new examples
of Poisson n-Lie algebras. In the case where associative algebra is unital and m scalar columns are
adjoined, we verify these conditions in low-dimensional cases and formulate a general conjecture
in this setting.
Furthermore, we show that if P is a Poisson n-Lie algebra and B is an associative commutative
algebra, then their tensor product P ⊗B naturally inherits a Poisson n-Lie algebra structure.
For the tensor product of two Poisson algebras, endowed with an n-ary bracket defined via iter-
ated applications of the underlying binary bracket in the way given in [31], we construct a Poisson
n-Lie algebra by factoring out the ideal generated by the elements measuring the failure of skew-
symmetry. In particular, given a Poisson algebra, the corresponding quotient of its tensor product
with an isomorphic copy yields a Poisson n-Lie algebra. Conversely, we present a procedure for
recovering Poisson algebras from Poisson n-Lie algebras using tensor-type constructions. In this
way, a correspondence between n-ary Poisson structures and Poisson algebras is established.
Moreover, we study the structural theory of solvable Poisson n-Lie algebras. In particular,
we establish analogues of Engel’s and Lie’s theorems in this setting, together with several their
consequences. A characterization of solvability in terms of the underlying n-Lie and associative
structures is provided as well. In addition, we introduce hypo-nilpotent ideals of Poisson n-Lie
algebras, demonstrating the significance of maximal hypo-nilpotent ideal in solvable Poisson n-Lie
algebra.
Finally, we show that generalized eigenspaces associated with multiplication operators in as-
sociative commutative algebras form ideals, and we present results on idempotent elements that
extend naturally from Poisson algebras to the n-ary setting.

It is known that classical results on the nilpotency of finite-dimensional Lie algebras admitting
either a non-singular derivation or a fixed-point-free automorphism of prime order remain valid
for Poisson 2-Lie algebras (or simply Poisson algebras) [14, 17]. However, these results do not
extend to n-Lie algebras for n > 2 (see Example 3.1 in [30]), and consequently they fail to hold
for Poisson n-Lie algebras as well.
In the classical setting of 2-Lie algebras (i.e., ordinary Lie algebras), the nilpotency for an ideal
coincides with its nilpotency when regarded as a subalgebra. However, for n-Lie algebras with
n > 2, these two notions no longer agree in general. Examples illustrating this distinction can be
found in [1] and [3]. This discrepancy motivates the introduction of the notion of a hypo-nilpotent
ideal, that is, an ideal which is nilpotent as a subalgebra but not nilpotent as an ideal [3].
As the analogous concept arises naturally in the setting of Poisson n-Lie algebras, we introduce
the notion of a hypo-nilpotent ideal in this context as well.
Definition 5.2. An ideal I of a Poisson n-Lie algebra P is called a hypo-nilpotent ideal if I is
nilpotent as a subalgebra but not nilpotent as an ideal. A hypo-nilpotent ideal that is not properly
contained in any other hypo-nilpotent ideal is called a maximal hypo-nilpotent ideal of P.

Definition 5.3. A torus on a n-Lie algebra L is a commutative subalgebra of the Lie algebra
Der(L) which consists of semisimple endomorphisms. A torus is said to be maximal, if it is not
contained strictly in any other torus.
Over an algebraically closed field, every semisimple endomorphism is diagonalizable. Therefore,
upon choosing an appropriate basis of an n-Lie algebra, any finite family of mutually commuting
diagonalizable derivations can be simultaneously diagonalized.

radical of [Der(L), Der(L)]. Consequently, all maximal tori in an n-Lie algebra L have the same
dimension; this common value is called the rank of L and is denoted by rank(L).

Recall that the classical Peirce decomposition for associative algebras is based on the properties
of idempotent elements. Since the algebra PA is associative, it is natural to investigate the role
of idempotents in the structure theory of Poisson n-Lie algebras. This approach was previously
developed for Poisson algebras (the case n = 2) in [14], and we observe that the corresponding
results extend to arbitrary n with only minor modifications. For this reason, we omit the proofs.
More precisely, assume that the associative component PA of a Poisson n-Lie algebra P contains
a nonzero idempotent element e. Then the following statements hold.
• The element e lies in the center of PL;
• If PL is a solvable n-Lie algebra with trivial center, then PA is nilpotent;
• If PL is solvable, then P is a split Poisson n-Lie algebra and Fe is an annihilator component.
Moreover, if PL is non-split, then PA is nilpotent.
