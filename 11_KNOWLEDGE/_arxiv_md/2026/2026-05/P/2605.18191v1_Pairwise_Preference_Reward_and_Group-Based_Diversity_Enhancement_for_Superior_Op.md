ABSTRACT
Current reinforcement learning(RL) methods are broadly applicable and powerful in verifiable
settings where scalar rewards can be provided. However, in open-ended generation tasks, verifying
the correctness of responses remains challenging, and training reward models incurs substantial
computational and annotation costs. Moreover, reinforcement learning (RLVR) often leads to diversity
collapse and produces stereotypical or rigid outputs — outcomes that are particularly undesirable
in open-domain scenarios. We propose Pairwise Preference Reward and Group-based Diversity
Enhancement (PPR-GDE), a RL method that is more suitable for open-ended generation. PPR-GDE
does not require scalar rewards and incorporates group-level diversity into the reward signal, it
preserves the comparative structure of subjective evaluation through a pairwise preference reward,
mitigates judge position bias via repeated comparisons with swapped response order, and introduces
a group-based diversity reward that explicitly encourages semantic dispersion within a response
group, all of these reward signals are integrated into a unified group-relative policy optimization
objective. We instantiate PPR-GDE on role-playing task, experiments show that PPR-GDE achieves
a better alignment quality as well as expressive diversity than strong RL baselines. Further analysis
shows that pairwise preference is critical for preference alignment in subjective perspective, while
the diversity metric plays an essential role in achieving superior expressive diversity and broader
semantic coverage.

Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) have achieved impressive performance across diverse generation tasks, and reinforce-
ment learning [Christiano et al., 2017, Ouyang et al., 2022] is widely used in reasoning tasks in verifiable settings
such as math and coding[Shao et al., 2024]. However, aligning model with human preferences remains particularly
challenging for open-ended generation tasks, where multiple responses differ along nuanced dimensions such as style,
creativity, aesthetics, persona consistency, and emotional tone. In such settings, the quality of the response is typically
determined by relative human judgments rather than absolute correctness, making both evaluation and optimization
inherently difficult [Xu et al., 2025].
In practice, most RL pipelines rely on scalar rewards provided by automated judges to supervise preference learning
[Christiano et al., 2017, Ouyang et al., 2022]. Pairwise supervision is often more reliable than absolute scoring for
open-ended generation tasks, since it better captures subtle preference differences while reducing evaluator burden.
Nevertheless, most existing methods eventually reduce these pairwise judgments to scalar rewards assigned to individual
responses and optimize them with standard reinforcement learning algorithms such as PPO [Ouyang et al., 2022,
Schulman et al., 2017] and GRPO[Shao et al., 2024]. This design discards the relative structure of subjective evaluation

The second challenge is the entropy collapse or diversity collapse commonly observed during RL. Recent studies show
that policy entropy often decreases rapidly during training, causing the model to concentrate probability mass on a
narrow set of already high-probability outputs and weakening exploratory coverage [Ahmed et al., 2019, Wen et al.,
2024, Cui et al., 2025, Cheng et al., 2025]. This problem is especially harmful for open-ended generation tasks, where a
well-aligned model should not only deliver high-quality responses, but also maintain response diversity under repeated
inputs of an identical prompt to avoid stereotyped outputs. Existing diversity-preserving strategies mainly regularize
token- or policy-level entropy, or promote lexical diversity within a single response [Li et al., 2016, Wen et al., 2024, Cui
et al., 2025]. However, for open-ended generation, a stronger notion of diversity is required: semantic dispersion across
multiple responses generated for the same prompt. This is particularly important for role-playing dialogue [Wang et al.,
2024, Tu et al., 2024], where natural interaction requires not only persona fidelity, but also expressive flexibility—a
good role-playing model should be able to produce diverse yet still character-consistent responses to the same input.
To address these challenges, we propose Pairwise Preference Reward and Group-based Diversity Enhancement
(PPR-GDE), a reinforcement learning method tailored for open-ended generation tasks. Within each group of responses
generated by model, we preserve the comparative nature of subjective evaluation through a pairwise preference
reward formulation, allowing the policy to learn from relative quality distinctions rather than relying on potentially
noisy absolute reward magnitudes. Moreover, our pairwise design can mitigate position bias by performing repeated
comparisons with swapped response order [Shi et al., 2024]. Concurrently, we introduce a group-based diversity
mechanism that explicitly encourages semantic dispersion among responses within the group, thereby preventing
diversity collapse during reinforcement learning. Consequently, PPR-GDE not only improves alignment quality but also
achieves superior expressive diversity and coverage. While we instantiate this framework in the role-playing domain, its
formulation is broadly applicable to various open-ended generation tasks. Figure 1 illustrates the overall framework of
PPR-GDE.
The contributions of this study are summarized as follows:
• We propose Pairwise Preference Reward and Group-based Diversity Enhancement (PPR-GDE), which use
pairwise preference signal rather than scalar reward and novelly unities group-based semantic diversity as the
optimization objection.

comparisons, which aligns with subjective evaluation which differs in style, aesthetics, structure, or semantic realization;
and (2) a group-based diversity mechanism that fosters semantic dispersion to prevent diversity collapse.

3.2
Pairwise Preference Reward
For many open-ended generation tasks, especially subjective tasks such as role-playing, absolute quality scores are
often difficult to define reliably. In contrast, pairwise comparison provides a more stable and interpretable supervision
signal by directly asking which of two responses better satisfies the task requirement [Christiano et al., 2017, Ouyang
et al., 2022, Rafailov et al., 2023b]. Based on this observation, we construct response-level preference rewards from
pairwise judgments within each sampled response group.

4
Experiments
We evaluate the proposed PPR-GDE framework on role-playing generation from three complementary perspectives:
single-turn role-playing quality, multi-turn role consistency, and response diversity.
4.1
Experimental Setup
Base Model and Baselines.
Experiments are conducted based on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct
and Qwen3-0.6B. We compare PPR-GDE against three baselines built on the same backbone: the original Base Model
without further alignment, PPO, and GRPO.
Training Data.
Our training data is constructed from two complementary sources. The first source is RoleBench
[Wang et al., 2024], from which we use role-specific samples to improve the model’s role-playing ability, especially its
understanding of role background and character-specific knowledge. The second source is mCSQA [Sakai et al., 2024],
which is used to strengthen the model’s basic response quality under role-playing settings. For the latter, we assign each
sampled question a role profile and background, and use Qwen3-32B as a compatibility judge to filter out question–role
pairs that conflict with the assigned character setting. After merging the two sources, we obtain a training set of 11,895
examples and a held-out pool of 1,024 examples, which is evenly split into validation and test sets.
Training Protocol.
During training, multiple candidate responses are sampled for each prompt. For PPO and GRPO,
policy updates are driven by scalar reward signals. For PPR-GDE, responses are compared pairwise to construct
preference-based supervision, together with an additional group-based diversity reward. This design encourages the
model to generate responses that are not only preferred, but also semantically diverse.
Hyperparameter Settings.
To ensure fair comparison, all methods share the same backbone model, decoding setup,
optimizer settings, and training budget unless otherwise specified. Thus, the primary difference across methods lies in
the alignment objective rather than implementation details.

4.2
Evaluation Metrics
We evaluate all methods from three complementary perspectives: role-playing quality, multi-turn role consistency, and
response diversity. Unless otherwise specified, higher scores indicate better performance.
Role-Playing Quality.
Following Wang et al. [2024], we report three benchmark-native metrics for single-turn
role-playing quality: CUS, RAW, and SPE. These metrics measure role-style consistency, instruction-following quality,
and role-specific knowledge expression, respectively. Among them, CUS is evaluated on the full test set. RAW is
evaluated on the subset derived from mCSQA, and mainly reflects the model’s basic knowledge and general response
ability under role constraints. SPE is evaluated on the subset derived from RoleBench, and is used to assess the model’s
understanding of role background and character-specific knowledge [Wang et al., 2024, Sakai et al., 2024].
Multi-Turn Role Consistency.
For multi-turn evaluation, we follow Tu et al. [2024] and report Knowledge-Exposure
(KE), Knowledge-Accuracy (KA), and Persona-Utterance (PU) using the official CharacterEval dataset and evaluation
pipeline. Although CharacterEval contains a broader set of evaluation dimensions, we retain these three metrics because
they are the most informative for measuring multi-turn role-playing consistency in our setting [Tu et al., 2024].
Response Diversity.
Beyond role fidelity, we further evaluate response diversity on our test set from both lexical and
semantic perspectives. We report Distinct-2 [Li et al., 2016] as the lexical diversity metric. For semantic diversity, we
use two complementary measures. First, we compute Semantic Nearest-Neighbor Distance (SNND), defined as the

4.3
Overall Results
Table 1 summarizes the main results on three backbone scales: Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, and
Qwen3-0.6B. Overall, RL-based alignment substantially improves role-playing performance over the corresponding
base model across all three scales. Among the aligned methods, PPR-GDE consistently achieves the strongest overall
balance between role-playing quality, multi-turn consistency, and response diversity.
On Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, PPR-GDE achieves the best average score on Role-Playing
Ability, and also obtains the best results on CUS and SPE. On Qwen3-0.6B, PPR-GDE still achieves the best CUS and
SPE scores, but its role-playing average is slightly below PPO and GRPO due to a lower RAW score. This suggests that
the proposed pairwise preference reward is particularly effective for improving the subjective dimensions of role-playing
alignment, especially persona consistency and role-specific knowledge expression.
PPR-GDE also achieves the best average score on Multi-Turn Consistency for all three backbones. In particular, it
consistently performs strongly on PU, while remaining competitive on KE and KA. At the same time, the table reveals
a consistent trade-off: methods with stronger role-playing quality and richer expressive coverage often obtain lower
RAW scores. We defer a more detailed analysis of this phenomenon to Section 4.5, where the ablation study shows that
diversity-oriented optimization can improve expressive coverage while partially sacrificing general response quality
under role constraints.
For response diversity, the unaligned base model remains the most diverse, which is consistent with the general
tendency of RL alignment to narrow the response distribution. However, among the aligned methods, PPR-GDE
achieves the best diversity average on all three backbones. Although it does not dominate every individual diversity
metric at every scale, it consistently preserves broader semantic variation than PPO and GRPO, especially in terms of
NoC and SNND. This shows that the proposed diversity-aware reward effectively mitigates semantic collapse during
RL.

5
Related Work
RL for LLMs.
Reinforcement learning (RL) has become a central paradigm for post-training large language models
(LLMs) beyond supervised fine-tuning. Early work showed that language models can be optimized from human
preference signals for tasks such as stylistic continuation and summarization [Ziegler et al., 2019, Stiennon et al., 2020].
Subsequently, PPO became the standard backbone for RLHF-style alignment when a reward signal can be reliably
specified or learned [Schulman et al., 2017, Ouyang et al., 2022]. More recently, GRPO and its variants have improved
the efficiency and stability of large-scale LLM post-training [Shao et al., 2024, Yu et al., 2025, Zheng et al., 2025].
However, scalar-reward RL is most effective in settings with relatively well-specified or verifiable objectives. For
subjective generation tasks such as open-ended dialogue, creative writing, and role-playing, correctness is difficult
to calibrate with a globally consistent scalar reward, motivating training methods that can exploit richer supervision
signals [Christiano et al., 2017, Wu et al., 2023].
Preference-Based Alignment.
To address the difficulty of specifying explicit rewards for subjective tasks, RLHF
has emerged as a dominant paradigm for aligning language models with human preferences [Christiano et al., 2017,
Ouyang et al., 2022]. In standard RLHF pipelines, annotators compare pairs of model outputs and provide relative
preference judgments, which are then used to train a reward model for subsequent policy optimization [Christiano et al.,
2017, Stiennon et al., 2020, Ouyang et al., 2022]. Recent work has moved toward preserving preference structure more
directly. DPO removes the explicit reward-modeling stage and optimizes the policy directly from pairwise preferences,
while P3O argues that standard PPO is mismatched to comparison-based feedback and proposes a policy objective
tailored to relative supervision [Rafailov et al., 2023b, Wu et al., 2023]. These methods highlight the importance of
preserving comparative information, but they mainly focus on improving the quality of individual responses.
Diversity Preservation and GRPO-style Extensions.
A parallel line of work studies diversity and entropy preserva-
tion in RL-based language generation. Earlier text generation research observed that neural generation systems often
suffer from degeneration and reduced diversity [Li et al., 2016, Holtzman et al., 2020]. More recent analyses of RL for
language models show that policy entropy often decreases rapidly during training, reducing exploration and causing the
policy to collapse onto a narrow set of response modes [Ahmed et al., 2019, Wen et al., 2024, Cui et al., 2025, Cheng
et al., 2025]. This issue is especially problematic for creative and role-playing tasks, where diverse and expressive
responses are essential for natural interaction [Wang et al., 2024, Tu et al., 2024].
Existing solutions mainly follow two directions: entropy regularization and group-wise optimization [Ahmed et al.,
2019, Wen et al., 2024, Cui et al., 2025, Cheng et al., 2025, Shao et al., 2024]. Recent GRPO-style extensions, such as
DAPO and GSPO, further improve training stability by modifying specific components of the optimization procedure,
including clipping strategy, sampling design, and importance-ratio computation [Yu et al., 2025, Zheng et al., 2025].
Our method is fundamentally different from these derivatives. Rather than refining a local computation in GRPO, we
redefine the training signal itself: PPR-GDE preserves pairwise preference information directly in reward construction
and explicitly incorporates a group-level semantic diversity objective. Therefore, PPR-GDE is not another GRPO-style
variant with a small optimization change, but a new training method for subjective generation.
6
Conclusion
In this paper, we propose Pairwise Preference Reward and Group-based Diversity Enhancement (PPR-GDE), a
group-based reinforcement learning framework that synergizes pairwise preference modeling with diversity-oriented
optimization. By extending the GRPO paradigm, our method effectively resolves the discrepancy of scalar reward
10


## Page 11


judgments in open-ended tasks, while simultaneously curbing the diversity collapse prevalent in RL-based alignment,
within the generated response groups, PPR-GDE yields 30% more clusters on average compared to GRPO.
Experiments on role-playing task show that PPR-GDE achieves a superior pareto-frontier between alignment fidelity
and expressive diversity compared to competitive baselines. Our analysis further elucidates a nontrivial trade-off: while
diversity enhancement significantly broadens expressive coverage, it introduces a slight penalty to basic response quality
under constrained settings, as reflected by lower RAW scores. We posit that this tension is an intrinsic property of
open-ended generation alignment, rather than a framework-specific limitation.
Future research will explore several directions: (1) generalizing PPR-GDE to broader preference-sensitive domains,
including but not limited to open-ended generation. (2) developing more granular semantic diversity estimators and
adaptive grouping mechanisms; and (3) investigating the scaling laws of this framework with larger models and more
robust evaluation protocols, including human-in-the-loop assessments.
