Propagation of conditional nonclassical reservoir states during quantum decoherence
Jargalsaikhan Artag,1 Koki Awaya,1 Takumi Kanezashi,1 Haruya
Nagata,1 Daisuke Tsukayama,1 Moe Shimada,1 and Jun-ichi Shirakashi1
1Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science,
Tokyo University of Agriculture and Technology, Koganei, Tokyo, Japan
(Dated: May 18, 2026)
Decoherence is usually described as the loss of local quantum coherence after tracing over en-
vironmental degrees of freedom. This reduced description, however, hides the reservoir state that
carries the lost coherence. Here we show that spin-boson decoherence can write a postselectable
nonclassical imprint into a structured bosonic reservoir. We map zero- and finite-temperature reser-
voirs to one-dimensional chains, evolve the joint qubit-reservoir state with tensor-network dynamics,
and reconstruct the Wigner function of a time-adaptive leading collective reservoir coordinate after
transverse qubit readout. The conditioned mapped-reservoir coordinate develops Wigner negativity
and interference fringes that are strongly suppressed in the unconditional reservoir state. A parame-
ter sweep shows that the spectral exponent and temperature control the visibility of this conditional
nonclassicality, the mapped-chain excitation transport, and the degree to which a single collective
coordinate captures the imprint. These results provide a branch-resolved phase-space picture of
decoherence: the reservoir is not only a sink for qubit coherence, but can carry a measurement-
conditioned nonclassical state in a collective mapped coordinate.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Decoherence converts quantum coherence of a local
system into correlations with surrounding degrees of
freedom [1–4]. This picture is traditionally formulated
through reduced dynamics, influence functionals, or mas-
ter equations, in which the reservoir coordinates are elim-
inated [4–6]. Such approaches are powerful for predicting
relaxation and dephasing, but they leave a complemen-
tary question unanswered: what state is written into the
reservoir while the system decoheres?
Structured reservoirs make this question especially im-
portant. In non-Markovian reservoirs, bath memory can
return information to the system and produce dynamics
that are not captured by a memoryless approximation [7–
10]. The spin-boson model provides a canonical setting
for this problem [3, 11]. It combines a two-level system
with a continuum of harmonic modes and allows the low-
frequency weight of the reservoir to be tuned through the
spectral exponent. Sub-Ohmic reservoirs, in particular,
support slow bath modes and strong memory effects [12–
14].
Nonperturbative methods have made such reservoirs
tractable from several complementary directions, includ-
ing hierarchical equations of motion, path-integral ten-
sor propagators, time-evolving matrix product opera-
tors, and chain-mapping tensor-network approaches [15–
25]. Chain mappings are particularly useful here because
they convert a continuous bosonic bath into a nearest-
neighbor one-dimensional chain while preserving the sys-
tem dynamics [20, 21]. Finite-temperature variants fur-
ther allow thermal reservoirs to be represented within a
pure-state tensor-network formulation [26].
Here we use this mapped-chain representation to re-
construct not only the qubit dynamics, but also the con-
ditional mapped-reservoir coordinate generated during
decoherence. The key distinction is between the uncon-
ditional bath state, obtained by tracing over the qubit,
and the bath state conditioned on a transverse qubit
readout.
Conditional states are standard in quantum
measurement and trajectory descriptions [27–29], and in
the present setting they reveal reservoir branch informa-
tion that is hidden by the unconditional mixture. We
reconstruct the Wigner function of a time-adaptive lead-
ing collective natural orbital of the bath and quantify its
nonclassicality through Wigner negativity [30–34]. This
places the work in direct connection with phase-space re-
construction and Wigner measurement ideas developed
in quantum optics and circuit QED [35–43].
We show that the conditioned leading coordinate de-
velops nonclassical Wigner structure while following ex-
citation through the mapped bath chain.
A param-
eter map identifies an intermediate coupling regime
where conditional nonclassicality, qubit coherence loss,
and finite postselection probability coexist. Four main
cases then compare sub-Ohmic and Ohmic reservoirs at
zero and finite temperature.
This comparison shows
that finite temperature can strengthen the conditional
mapped-coordinate response while suppressing uncondi-
tional Wigner negativity. The resulting picture is branch
resolved: coherence lost by the qubit is imprinted in a
collective mapped-chain coordinate, but the nonclassical
structure is visible only after conditioning on the qubit
readout.

This distinction is the central physical point.
The

reservoir coordinate associated with a specified trans-

selection yield; it is not a numerical diagnostic.
E.
Finite temperature changes conditional and
unconditional visibility
Figure 6 summarizes the finite-temperature effect at
fixed α = 0.2.
For these parameters, Θ/∆= 1 in-
creases the peak conditional nonclassicality in both the
sub-Ohmic and Ohmic cases. At the same time, it re-
duces the peak unconditional Wigner nonclassicality and
suppresses the coherence revival amplitude. This com-
bination is physically consistent within the temperature-
dependent mapped-chain representation: thermal occu-
pation can increase the conditional displacement and ef-
fective chain population, while thermal mixing makes the
unconditioned bath state more classical in phase space.
The sub-Ohmic finite-temperature chain carries much
larger effective occupation than the Ohmic reference. At
Θ/∆= 1, the maximum total mapped-chain occupation
is ∼42 for s = 0.5 and ∼9 for s = 1.0. This increased
occupation is not automatically equivalent to stronger
unconditional nonclassicality, because the unconditional
state also contains thermal and branch-mixing contribu-
tions.

Validity of the leading collective coordinate
The Wigner reconstruction is intentionally not a lo-
cal reaction-coordinate Wigner function. Figure 7(a-f)
shows that the time-adaptive leading natural orbital car-
ries a large fraction of the bath one-body occupation
while spreading over many mapped-chain sites.
The
participation width increases as the conditioned imprint
moves through the mapped coordinate. The heatmaps
of |fk(t)|2 resemble the occupation transport in Fig. 4,
showing that the Wigner coordinate follows the mapped-
chain excitation rather than remaining pinned to a fixed
local mode. This figure also states the limitation of the
reconstruction. A high leading-mode fraction means that
the leading natural orbital is a physically relevant re-
duced coordinate within the reported diagnostics. It does
not mean the entire bath is single-mode. The warmer
Ohmic case is the clearest example: the leading fraction
remains useful but falls at late times, so the Wigner func-
tion should be described as that of a time-adaptive lead-
ing collective coordinate rather than the complete bath
state.

Conditional Wigner snapshots and movies
To visualize the phase-space structure of the collec-
tive reservoir coordinate, Figure 8 shows conditional
Wigner snapshots in the time-adaptive leading mode.
The columns are taken at t = 0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.5, and 5.0,

The Wigner snapshots close the connection between
the scalar metrics and the phase-space object. Condi-
tional nonclassicality is not only a number; it corresponds
to visible interference and branch separation in a time-
adaptive collective coordinate that follows the mapped-
chain dynamics.
III.
DISCUSSION
These results give a conditional phase-space picture of
quantum decoherence. The reservoir is not merely a sink
that absorbs qubit coherence.
In the full system-plus-
reservoir state, decoherence writes a branch-dependent
imprint into structured bath degrees of freedom. A trans-
verse qubit readout exposes this imprint as a conditional
Wigner-nonclassical state of a leading collective mapped-
reservoir coordinate.
The distinction between conditional and unconditional
states is essential.
Tracing over the qubit suppresses
the directly visible Wigner negativity by mixing reservoir
branches. Conditioning on the qubit readout restores a
branch-coherent mapped coordinate and reveals nonclas-
sicality hidden in the unconditional bath. The result is a
conditional phase-space reconstruction of a leading reser-
voir coordinate, not a claim about a fully reconstructed
unconditional bath state.
Finite temperature does not act only as a destruc-
tive perturbation.
In the present data, Θ/∆= 1 in-
creases conditional nonclassical response and effective
mapped-chain population, while reducing unconditional
Wigner negativity and coherence revival. This reflects
the different roles of thermal occupation, conditional dis-
placement, and branch mixing within the temperature-
dependent chain representation. Spectral exponent fur-
ther controls how strongly the mapped chain is occupied
and how concentrated the leading collective coordinate
remains.

in reduced qubit coherence, but conditional nonclassical
states carried by collective modes of the environment.

A.

All main results use a single qubit with ∆= 1, cut-

s ∈{0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 1.0}, and Θ/∆∈{0, 1}.

The mapped chain Hamiltonian in Eq. (3) is evolved

C.
Collective mode and Wigner reconstruction
At each Wigner-evaluation time, the unconditional
bath one-body density matrix is diagonalized and the
leading eigenvector defines the time-adaptive coordinate
cf(t) in Eq. (8).
The same fk(t) is then used for the
unconditional and +x-conditioned Wigner functions. A
continuous phase convention is fixed by maximizing the
overlap with the previous time step. The characteristic
function of cf(t) is evaluated directly as an MPS over-
lap with the corresponding product of chain-site displace-
ment operators, and the Wigner function is obtained by
Fourier transforming this characteristic function on the
finite phase-space grid.
