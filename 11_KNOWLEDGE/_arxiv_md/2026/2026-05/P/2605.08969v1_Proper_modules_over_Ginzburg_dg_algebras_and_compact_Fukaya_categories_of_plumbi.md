PROPER MODULES OVER GINZBURG DG ALGEBRAS AND COMPACT FUKAYA
CATEGORIES OF PLUMBINGS
WONBO JEONG, DOGANCAN KARABAS, AND SANGJIN LEE
Abstract. We study Ginzburg dg algebras which appear at the intersection of representation theory and
symplectic topology. First, we provide a collection of proper modules that generates all proper modules over
a Ginzburg dg algebra, without assuming the Jacobi-finite condition. Using this generation result, we study
the immersed compact Fukaya category of a general plumbing space. In particular, we prove a generation
result for the compact Fukaya category and show that it is equivalent to the category of proper modules over
the wrapped Fukaya category, and hence to the category of microlocal sheaves on the Lagrangian skeleton.

1. Introduction
One of the main objects of study in this paper is the Ginzburg dg algebra, which appears at the intersection
of representation theory, symplectic topology, and related fields such as mathematical physics. A main goal of
this paper is to prove that the (immersed) compact Fukaya category of a plumbing space is equivalent to the
category of proper modules over its wrapped Fukaya category, which in turn is equivalent to the category
of proper modules over the associated Ginzburg dg algebra (see Theorem 1.5). Moreover, this yields an
equivalence between the compact Fukaya category of a plumbing space and the category of microlocal
sheaves on its Lagrangian skeleton (see Corollary 4.6). To prove this, we establish a generation result for
proper modules over Ginzburg dg algebras (see Theorem 1.4).
In representation theory, Ginzburg [Gin06] introduced the notion of Ginzburg dg algebra as an example
of a 3-Calabi–Yau algebra, also defined in [Gin06]. Since then, Ginzburg dg algebras have been studied
extensively in representation theory (for example, see [Gin06, KY11, Kel11a, Her16]). They also appear in
mathematical physics and algebraic geometry: As the term “3-Calabi–Yau algebra” suggests, these algebras
are closely related to the study of Calabi–Yau threefolds that mathematical physicists and algebraic geometers
investigate.
Thus, it is not surprising that Ginzburg dg algebras, as natural examples of 3-Calabi–Yau
algebras, arise in these areas of mathematics.
Ginzburg dg algebras also appear naturally in symplectic topology. Since symplectic topology has strong
connections with mathematical physics and algebraic geometry, one could expect such a relation. However,
it is worth emphasizing that Ginzburg dg algebras arise in a purely symplectic-topological context as well.
More precisely, it is known that Ginzburg dg algebras are equivalent to the wrapped Fukaya categories of
plumbing spaces, see [Asp23, KL25, EL17, EL19, LU21, HLT24]. Thus, a deep understanding of Ginzburg dg
algebras could contribute to the study of mathematical physics, algebraic geometry, and symplectic topology.
In the current paper, we first study proper modules (or equivalently, derived modules of finite total dimen-
sions) over Ginzburg dg algebra, motivated from a symplectic topology problem. Especially, we characterize
a collection of proper modules that generate all proper modules, see Theorem 1.1. (After doing simple linear

The motivating problem concerns the compact Fukaya category of plumbing spaces.
Here, the compact
Fukaya category is the category of compact, exact, (possibly) immersed Lagrangians. (See Definition 4.3 for
more details.) Let us recall that compact Fukaya category is a subcategory of wrapped Fukaya category, as
implied by [JKL26]. Since wrapped Fukaya categories of plumbings are equivalent to Ginzburg dg algebras,
the Yoneda functor sends the compact Fukaya category into the category of modules over Ginzburg dg
algebras. Moreover, compactness implies that the compact Fukaya category embeds into the category of
proper modules. The second main result of the paper is to show that the collection of generating modules is
contained in the Yoneda image of the compact Fukaya category. This implies the equivalence between the
compact Fukaya category of plumbings and the category of proper modules over the associated Ginzburg
dg algebras (Theorem 4.5), as well as a generation result for the compact Fukaya category (Theorem 1.5).
Moreover, it implies the equivalence between the compact Fukaya category of plumbings and the category
of microlocal sheaves on their Lagrangian skeleta (Corollary 4.6).
In the rest of Section 1, we provide more details on our motivations/ideas/results. Let Q be a quiver and
W be a formal sum of cycles of Q. The Ginzburg dg algebra Γ3(Q, W) of a quiver with potential (Q, W)
was first introduced in [Gin06] as an example of 3-Calabi–Yau algebras. It extends to a more general case:
If Q is a graded quiver and W is a formal sum of cycles of degree (3 −n), the same definition provides an
n-Calabi–Yau algebra, which is also called Ginzburg dg algebra Γn(Q, W). See [Kel11a]. (We note that for
n = 3, the ungraded quiver Q can be seen as the graded quiver with a simple graded quiver such that every
edge is of degree 0.)
As mentioned above, Ginzburg dg algebras (especially as 3-Calabi–Yau algebras/categories) have appeared
in many works in representation theory, algebraic geometry, mathematical physics, and related fields. Some
examples are as follows: [Ami09, Kel11b, KY11, DWZ08] studied cluster structures on the category of
proper modules over Γ3(Q, W), denoted by PropΓ3(Q, W), under the assumption that Γ3(Q, W) is Jacobi-
finite.
(Recall that Γ3(Q, W) is Jacobi-finite if and only if its zeroth cohomology is finite-dimensional.)
Bridgeland and Smith [BS15] related Bridgeland stability conditions on Prop Γ3(Q, W) to the geometry of
surfaces and quadratic differentials. Moreover, a Jacobi-finite Γ3(Q, W) and Prop Γ3(Q, W) could provide an
computational example of [KS08, KS10] that studied the wall-crossing phenomena on the space of stability
conditions on 3-Calabi–Yau categories and their generalized Donaldson–Thomas invariants.
We note that in the Jacobi-finite case, Prop Γ3(Q, W) has a known collection of generating objects, i.e.,
a collection of proper modules that generates the category; see [Kin94]. Thus, if a quiver Q is equipped
with a generic potential W, then there exists a generating collection of proper modules over Γ3(Q, W), since
the genericity of W implies the Jacobi-finiteness of Γ3(Q, W). Such generating collections have played an
important role in many works, including some of the examples mentioned above.
However, not every pair (Q, W) satisfies the Jacobi-finite condition. For example, if Q has a cycle and if
W = 0, then Γ3(Q, W = 0) has infinite dimensional zeroth cohomology. Thus, it would be a natural research
direction to seek a specific collection of generating objects for Prop Γ3(Q, W).
The first main result of the current article is the construction of a specific generating collection for
Prop Γn(Q, 0).
Theorem 1.1 (Corollary 3.11). Let n ≥3, and let Q be a non-positively graded quiver. (Roughly speaking,
the non-positively graded condition means that the graded quiver does not contain a positively graded cycle.
See Definition 3.1 for the formal definition.) Then, we provide a specific collection of proper modules over
Γn(Q, 0) that generates Prop Γn(Q, 0).
An example illustrating Theorem 1.1 is given in Section 6.3.

Our main idea for proving Theorem 1.1 originates from symplectic topology. In fact, by employing ideas
from symplectic topology, we prove a more general version of Theorem 1.1, namely Theorem 1.4. Before

As mentioned at the beginning, [KL25] showed that for a graded quiver Q, Γn(Q, 0) is equivalent to the
wrapped Fukaya category of a plumbing space P equipped with a grading structure η, where P is obtained by
plumbing copies of T ∗Sn along Q and η respects the grading on Q. In fact, [KL25] computed the wrapped
Fukaya category in a more general setting, namely for plumbing spaces obtained by plumbing arbitrary
cotangent bundles and equipped with grading structures η. The result is that the wrapped Fukaya category,
denoted by W(P; η), is (Morita equivalent to) an extended/generalized version of a Ginzburg dg algebra.
This generalized version contains additional morphisms associated to each vertex of Q. See Theorem 2.10
and Remark 2.17 for more details. Motivated by this, we refer to W(P; η) as a generalized Ginzburg dg
algebra/category.
Remark 1.3. As stated in Theorem 2.10, the extra morphisms in a generalized Ginzburg dg algebra, which
are added at each vertex, are generators of the chain complexes of based loop spaces.
Thus, the term
“generalized Ginzburg dg algebra” can be replaced by “usual Ginzburg dg algebra with based loop space
coefficients”.
As a general version of Theorem 1.1, we prove Theorem 1.4.
Theorem 1.4 (Theorem 3.10). Let P be a plumbing space of dimension 2n ≥6, obtained by plumbing
cotangent bundles of arbitrary manifolds and equipped with a non-positive grading structure η (see Definition
3.1). For the corresponding generalized Ginzburg dg algebra W(P; η), we provide a specific collection of
proper modules that generates Prop W(P; η).
As mentioned in Remark 1.2 (1), the description of the generating modules we provide can be viewed as
a characterization of indecomposable modules.
Our motivation and main idea for the proof of Theorem 1.4 arise from symplectic topology. In the second
part of the article, namely Sections 4 and 5, we study the motivating symplectic topology problem.
Our motivation from symplectic topology is the study of the compact Fukaya category, especially its
generation. We note that generation of wrapped Fukaya categories is well understood in the literature (see
[CDRGG24, GPS24b]), whereas generation of compact Fukaya categories are not well understood in general;
see [AS12, Li19] for some known cases.
This project originated from the idea of studying (generation of) compact Fukaya categories via wrapped
Fukaya categories. More precisely, we note that the compact Fukaya category of a Weinstein manifold X,
denoted by F(X), can be viewed as a full subcategory of the wrapped Fukaya category W(X). Applying
the Yoneda functor to F(X), compactness implies that the images are proper modules. In other words,
Y (F(X)) ⊂Prop W(X), where Y denotes the Yoneda functor. In particular, for plumbing spaces we have
Y (F(P; η)) ⊂Prop W(P; η).
Theorem 1.4 provides a collection of proper modules {Mi} that generate Prop W(P; η). For each generator
Mi, if there exists a compact Lagrangian Li ∈F(P; η) such that Y(Li) is isomorphic to Mi, then this gives
an explicit collection of generators {Li} of F(P; η). Theorem 1.5 establishes precisely this result.
Theorem 1.5 (Theorems 4.5 and 5.7). For each generating proper module M given in Theorem 1.4, there
exists a compact Lagrangian L in P such that the Yoneda image of L is isomorphic to M. In particular:
(1) The (immersed) compact Fukaya category F(P; η), the category of proper modules Prop W(P; η),
and the category of microlocal sheaves on the Lagrangian skeleton L of P are equivalent:
F(P; η) ≃Prop W(P; η) ≃µSh(L).
(2) The category F(P; η) is generated by a collection of Lagrangians that we describe explicitly.

We should remark that the expected generating Lagrangians could be immersed (if the plumbing quiver

theory. We briefly review the immersed Lagrangian Floer theory in the wrapped setting appearing in the
literature: In [CDRGG24, Section 6.2], the authors defined an immersed version of Lagrangian Floer theory
using Hamiltonian perturbations. This suggests the existence of a Fukaya category whose objects include
exact immersed Lagrangians, but the authors did not verify all details required to establish the full A∞-
structure, so the resulting structure was called a “Fukaya pre-category”. (See [CDRGG24, Remark 8.14]
for more details.) On the other hand, [Gao17] resolved this issue using pearly complexes instead of the
Hamiltonian-perturbation approach. Thus, the remaining problem was to establish the equivalence between
these two constructions: one defined using Hamiltonian perturbations and the other using pearly complexes.
This equivalence is proved in our separate paper [JKL26]. In the same paper, we also extend the generation
theorem of [CDRGG24] to immersed Lagrangians equipped with certain bounding cochains (equivalently,
augmentations) (see Theorem 4.2), showing that such Lagrangians are generated by cocores and hence are
objects of the wrapped Fukaya category considered in this paper.
Using this fact, for each generator M appearing in Theorem 3.10, we construct a (possibly immersed)
Lagrangian in the wrapped Fukaya category whose Yoneda image is isomorphic to M in Section 4.
We also note that Section 5 provides an alternative proof of Theorem 1.5 using a slightly different method.
The argument there uses the setting of [GPS24b]. We include this alternative proof because it gives a more
pictorial explanation of the result. However, the drawback of this approach is that it relies on an ad hoc
argument using a specific Weinstein sectorial covering of the plumbing space.

6.1 we construct a Bridgeland stability condition on the category of interest, namely the category of proper
modules over (generalized) Ginzburg dg algebras. In particular, its stability space is nonempty, and these
categories could provide a useful test ground for studying Bridgeland stability conditions and examining
related theories such as [KS08]. We also give a construction of cluster categories in Section 6.2. Finally, in

to [Kel08], and for a review of A∞-categories, one can consult [Sei08]. We use d for the differential and ◦

2.2. Plumbing space. One of the main purposes of the paper is to study the compact Fukaya category
of plumbing spaces. Thus, it would reasonable to introduce briefly the definition/construction of plumbing
spaces in this preliminary section. In the literature, there exist many good references for more details, for
example, see [AS12]. We refer also the reader to [KL25, Section 3], because we share the same notation.
We construct a plumbing space from a set of data, which we call plumbing data.
Definition 2.3. Let n be a natural number bigger than 1. Plumbing data is a triple (Q, M, sgn) such that
• Q is a (finite) quiver, i.e., a directed graph,
• M is a map from the set of vertices of Q, denoted by V (Q), to the collection of n-dimensional
connected, oriented, closed, smooth manifolds, denoted by On, i.e.,
M : V (Q) →On,
(Note that for simplicity of notation, we set the notation Mv := M(v) for all v ∈V (Q).)
• sgn is a map from the set of arrows, denoted by E(Q), to {1, −1}, i.e.,
sgn : E(Q) →{1, −1}.
We note that even if we allow Mv to be a manifold with boundary, the plumbing space construction given
below (and most of results in the current paper) will still work. However, the resulting plumbing space
should be a Weinstein sector, not a Weinstein manifold. In the paper, for the simplicity of arguments, we
do not allow Mv to be a manifold with boundary.
When plumbing data (Q, M, sgn) is provided, one can construct a Weinstein manifold, called plumbing
space, from the given data.
Especially, the vertex set V (Q) and the map M defined on V (Q) provide
ingredients, and the edge E(Q) and the map sgn on E(Q) specify how to “plumb” the ingredients.
More precisely, the ingredients are cotangent bundles {T ∗Mv|v ∈V (Q)}. If an edge e ∈E(Q) has a
tail v ∈V (Q) and a head w ∈V (Q), then one plumbs T ∗Mv and T ∗Mw at one point. In other words,
