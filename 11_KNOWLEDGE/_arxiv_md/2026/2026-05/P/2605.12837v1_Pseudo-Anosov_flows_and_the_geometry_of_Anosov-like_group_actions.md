THOMAS BARTHELM´E, KATHRYN MANN, NEIGE PAULET,
AND ABDUL ZALLOUM
Abstract. We show that the action on its orbit space induced by a pseudo-
Anosov flow on a closed 3-manifold (and more general Anosov-like actions)
can be seen as an isometric action on a Gromov-hyperbolic space. When
the flow is not R-covered, we show that this action admits elements that are
weakly properly discontinuous and deduce that elements of π1(M) that do
not represent a periodic orbit of the flow are generic for any word metric
coming from a finite generating set. We also give a number of other geo-
metric group-theoretic results for Anosov-like group actions on bifoliated
planes.
1. Introduction
1.1. Motivation and main theorems. There is a rich relationship between the
geometry and topology of a 3-manifold, and the dynamics of flows that mani-
fold supports. An important early result in this direction is Margulis’ theorem
[AS67] that any 3-manifold supporting an Anosov flow must have exponen-
tial growth of its fundamental group. This was extended by Plante–Thurston
to the codimension-1 Anosov case [PT72] and by Paternain for pseudo-Anosov
(expansive) flows on 3-manifolds [Pat93], following Plante and Thurston’s strat-
egy. Exponential growth can also be detected on the level of periodic orbits:
Bowen [Bow72] showed that the number of periodic orbits of bounded length
grows exponentially with the length, and Barthelm´e–Fenley [BF17] showed that
the number of conjugacy classes (thus in particular the number of elements) in
π1(M) representing periodic orbits grows exponentially fast with the length of
the shortest periodic orbit in that class.
We show here that, by contrast, relatively few loops in M are represented by
periodic orbits. To make this precise, for a flow ϕ on a manifold M, denote
by P(ϕ) ⊂π1(M) the set of elements freely homotopic to unoriented periodic
orbits, and P(ϕ)c its complement. We prove:
Theorem A. Let ϕ be a non R-covered pseudo–Anosov flow on a closed 3-
manifold M. Then, for any word metric on π1(M) coming from a finite gener-
ating set, the set P(ϕ)c is generic.
Furthermore, for any finite generating set S, there exists S′ ⊃S for which
the critical exponent of P(ϕ)c is strictly larger than that of P(ϕ). If π1(M) is
Gromov hyperbolic, this holds with S′ = S.

1.2. Connections with CAT(0) cube complexes and dualizable systems. As
some experts may already have noticed, our graph X(P) is highly reminiscent
of the contact graph introduced by Hagen for CAT(0) cube complexes [Hag14],
which has become a very important tool in that theory. Indeed one can see the
graph we consider here as the bifoliated plane counterpart of Hagen’s contact
graph and Theorem C as the counterpart of [Hag14, Theorem 4.1], the main
result of that work.
The key aspect shared by these two apparently unrelated settings is a notion of
walls given by the structure of the space considered: leaves of the foliations for
bifoliated planes, and hyperplanes for CAT(0) cube complexes. The intersection
pattern of these walls then leads to X(P) for bifoliated planes and the contact
graph for CAT(0) cube complexes.
One can in fact formalize this connection by using the framework of dualizable
systems introduced by Petyt–Zalloum [PZ24]: In the abstract setting of a set S
together with a family of walls W (that can formally be defined as bipartitions
of S), [PZ24] builds a metric space from any choice of dualizable system C ⊂2W .
Such a C, is, morally, a choice of what should be“admissible”sequences of walls.
With the correct choice of C, the quasi-treeness of the graph we build as well
as [Hag14, Theorem 4.1] can be seen as instances of [PSZ25, Proposition 2.14].
While we choose not to do the proof of Theorem B using [PSZ25, Proposition
2.14] in order to stay more hands-on, we explain the connection between graphs,
metrics in the plane, and dualizable systems in a little more depth in Section
5.2.

A bifoliated plane is a plane P with two topologically transverse, possibly sin-
gular foliations F+ and F−, whose singularities are k-prongs, with at most

A k-prong is a structure locally isomorphic, as
a bifoliated space, to the image of a neighborhood of 0 in the complex plane
(with the horizontal and vertical or real/imaginary axis foliations), under the
semi-branched cover z 7→zk/2. A face of a leaf l is the boundary of a connected
component of P \ l. Nonsingular leaves have a single face, and k-prongs have
exactly k faces. We define three natural graphs that capture the intersection
pattern of leaves.
Definition 2.1. When (P, F+, F−) is a bifoliated plane, we define the graph
X+(P) by
• Vertices: each leaf of F+ is a vertex.
• Edges: two vertices (leaves) are adjacent whenever they are both inter-
sected by a common non-singular leaf of F−.
The graph X−(P) is defined similarly, reversing the roles of + and −above.
When there is no ambiguity between bifoliated planes, we will simply write X+
or X−.
The definition of edges can be equivalently reformulated in terms of faces, as
follows: two leaves l, l′ in F+ are joined by an edge if and only if they meet a
common face of a leaf of F−.

Definition 2.2. When (P, F+, F−) is a bifoliated plane, define X = X(P) to be
the graph where each leaf of F+∪F−is a vertex, and two vertices are connected
by an edge if and only if they intersect.
Observe that any foliation-preserving homeomorphism of P acts naturally on
each of X, X+ and X−by isometries.
Example 2.3. There are two special cases of bifoliated planes which play a
significant role in Anosov dynamics. These are the trivial plane, isomorphic
to R2 with the horizontal and vertical foliations, and the skew plane, which is
the isomorphism class of the bi-infinite strip {(x, y) : x < y < x + 1} ⊂R2
with the induced horizontal and vertical coordinate foliations. The reader can
easily verify that in the trivial case, X± and X are all complete graphs, i.e., of
diameter 1, and in the skew case, both X± and X are quasi-isometric to R.
In this section, we show that these objects are all coarsely the same, and all
quasi-isometric to trees.
Theorem 2.4. For any bifoliated plane (P, F+, F−), the graphs X+, X−and X
are all quasi-trees and are quasi-isometric to each other.

3. Nonelementary graphs and actions
In this section we show that, in the presence of a group acting with Anosov-like
dynamics, the graph X+ is typically nonelemetary and the action is nonelemen-
tary as well. We further describe the isometries (which are necessarily elliptic or
loxodromic) of X+ induced by such an action. To do this, we begin by recalling
some essential background on Anosov-like actions, more details can be found
in [BM25]. A reader familiar with this basic theory can skip directly to Section
3.2.
3.1. Background: Anosov-like actions on bifoliated planes. The following def-
inition (from [BM25], originally introduced in a slightly different form in
[BFM25]) gives an abstraction of the key properties satisfied by the orbit-space
actions of pseudo-Anosov flows on compact 3-manifolds.
Definition 3.1 (Anosov-like action). An action of a group G by foliation-
preserving homeomorphisms on a bifoliated plane (P, F+, F−) is called Anosov-
like if it satisfies the following:
(A1) If a nontrivial element of G fixes a leaf l ∈F±, then it has a fixed
point x ∈l and is topologically expanding on one leaf through x and
topologically contracting on the other.

(A2) The union of leaves of F+ that are fixed by some element of G is dense
in P, as is the union of leaves of F−that are fixed by some element of
G.
(A3) Each singular point is fixed by some nontrivial element of G.
(A4) If l is nonseparated with another leaf in its leaf space, then some non-
trivial element g ∈G fixes l.
Theorem 3.2 (Theorem 1.4.12, [BM25]). If ϕ is a pseudo-Anosov flow on a
closed 3-manifold, the induced action of π1(M) on its orbit space (Oϕ, Fs, Fu)
is an Anosov-like action.
Admitting an Anosov-like action constrains the topology of the foliations
F+, F−. As one important example we have:
Theorem 3.3 (Trichotomy for Anosov-like actions). Let (P, F+, F−) be a bifo-
liated plane with an Anosov-like action of a group G. Then exactly one of the
following holds:
(i) (P, F+, F−) is trivial,
(ii) (P, F+, F−) is skew (as defined in Example 2.3), or
(iii) There is either a singular point in P, or the leaf spaces of F+ and F−
are both non-Hausdorff with two-sided branching. 1
This was proved for flows by a combination of results of Barbot [Bar95,
Th´eor`eme 4.1] and Fenley [Fen94, Theorem 3.4] and [Fen95, Main Theorem],
see [BM25] for a proof for Anosov-like actions.

Remark 5.9. Notice that the metrics dH, d±, dR± are all roughly geodesic met-
rics, i.e., there exists a constant C such that every pair of points x, y ∈P are
connected by a (1, C)-quasi-geodesic in each of the metrics dH, d±, dR±. To con-
struct such a quasi-geodesic, it is enough to note that any path between x and y
can be isotoped to be in a ”maximally transverse”position in the sense that it is
tangent to either foliations in at most finitely many points and that it intersect
any leaf at most twice. One can show that such a path will automatically be a
(1, C)-quasi-geodesic.
Rough geodesicity is an important property, as it is this class of metric spaces
in which hyperbolicity is a quasi-isometry invariant.

The trivial plane. We first treat the case of a trivial plane.

Proposition 6.4. Let G be a finitely generated group acting Anosov-like on the
