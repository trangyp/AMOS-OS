Abstract
Adverse weather removal (AWR) in real-world images remains challenging due
to heterogeneous and unseen degradations, while distortion-driven training of-
ten yields overly smooth results. We propose PVRF, a unified framework that
integrates zero-shot soft weather perceptions with velocity-constrained rectified-
flow refinement. PVRF introduces an AWR-specific question answering module
(AWR-QA) that uses frozen vision–language models (VLMs) to estimate soft
probabilities of weather types and low-level attribute scores. These perceptions
condition restoration networks via attribute-modulated normalization (AMN) and
weather-weighted adapters (WWA), producing an anchor estimate for refinement.
We then learn a terminal-consistent residual rectified flow with perception-adaptive
source perturbation and a terminal-consistent velocity parameterization to stabi-
lize learning near the terminal regime. Extensive experiments show that PVRF
improves both fidelity and perceptual quality over state-of-the-art baselines, with
strong cross-dataset generalization on single and combined degradations. Code
will be released at https://github.com/dongw22/PVRF.

However, two challenges remain for real-world AWR and photo-realistic restoration. First, it is still
non-trivial to consistently infer the underlying weather condition from degraded inputs and convert it

restorations that are both faithful and photo-realistic remains challenging in AWR.
To address these limitations, we propose PVRF, an all-in-one AWR framework guided by zero-
shot soft weather perceptions from pretrained vision–language models (VLMs). First, our AWR-
specific question answering module (AWR-QA) uses concise, type-specific definitions and quantizes
VLM responses into soft weather-type probabilities and low-level attribute scores. Second, we
use these perceptions to modulate AWR backbones via Attribute-Modulated Normalization (AMN)
and Weather-Weighted Adapters (WWA), enabling degradation-aware posterior estimation under
distortion losses. More importantly, we introduce a perception-guided rectified flow that learns a
transport from a perception-dependent, posterior-anchored source distribution toward the clean-image
distribution. The proposed terminal-consistent residual formulation, together with perception-adaptive
source perturbation, improves photo-realism while maintaining competitive fidelity (Fig. 4).
In summary, PVRF contributes (i) definition-guided, zero-shot VLM perceptions yielding soft type
and attribute priors, (ii) a perception-guided modulation design (AMN/WWA) for degradation-aware
posterior estimation, and (iii) a terminal-consistent residual rectified flow with perception-adaptive
source perturbation for photo-realistic refinement.

Method
Motivation. Although Eq. 1 provides a posterior-mean transport approach for balancing distortion
and perceptual quality, applying it directly to all-in-one adverse weather removal (AWR) is non-trivial
due to the following challenges:
• Mixed degradations yield a compromise posterior mean. Training data in unified AWR spans
multiple degradations D. Without any degradation cue, a single MSE predictor yields E[X | Y ] =
ED∼ptrain(D|Y )

E[X | Y , D]

, i.e., a weighted average of degradation-specific posterior means and
thus a compromise estimate, which is exacerbated under distribution shift.
• Fixed perturbation is suboptimal. Gaussian perturbation is used to mitigate potential singularities
in transport learning, but a fixed global scale σs is ill-suited to all-in-one AWR with varying
degradation severity. This fixed-scale design can over-perturb mild cases and degrade fidelity, while
under-perturbing severe or ambiguous inputs and providing insufficient regularization.
• Missing terminal constraint near t→1. Rectified flow admits an implicit terminal relation: the
optimal velocity field at t = 1 satisfies v∗(X, 1) = X −E[Z0 | Z1 = X]. However, training over
finitely sampled time points does not explicitly enforce this near t→1. Crucially, under Eq. 1 the
term E[Z0 | Z1 = X] is generally intractable, making the terminal regime fragile.
To address these challenges, we first introduce AWR-specific Question Answering module to acquire
reliable weather-type and low-level attribute perceptions from VLMs (Sec. 3.1), then develop
perception-guided conditioning designs for posterior estimation and velocity modeling (Sec. 3.2 and
3.3). Furthermore, in Sec. 3.3, we devise terminal-consistent residual flow with perception-adaptive
source perturbation and terminal-consistent velocity parameterization. The framework of our method
is presented in Fig. 3.
3.1
Zero-shot Soft Perception Prior via VLM Question Answering
In unified AWR, the weather degradation is unknown and may be ambiguous or mixed. A common
way to introduce degradation cues is to predict a weather label and condition restoration on a
one-hot indicator vector [22, 10]. This hard one-hot conditioning has three potential limitations:
training dependence (new types often require retraining), error sensitivity (misclassification
yields mismatched conditioning especially under cross-dataset shifts), and a coarse cue (discarding
uncertainty and severity information).
To address these issues, we extract a zero-shot and soft perception prior from a frozen VLM via
definition-augmented question answering (QA) (Fig. 2), producing a type prior Ptype and an attribute
prior Pattr. For each weather type i ∈{0, . . . , N −1}, we run an independent binary dialogue and
quantify the preference between the canonical answer anchors yes and no using their raw logits:

3.2
Perception-Guided Conditioning Design
This section describes how the extracted soft perception priors are used to modulate network repre-
sentations as conditioning signals.
A key consideration is that ˜Ptype indicates which weather degradations are present, while Pattr
provides continuous cues of how severe the corruption is. Accordingly, we incorporate them through
two complementary mechanisms: (i) attribute-modulated normalization for stable severity modulation,
and (ii) weather-weighted adapters for soft type-aware adaptation.

transformation. Given an input feature F (and similarly an intermediate feature Fmid), AMN predicts
scale and bias from Pattr and modulates the layer-normalized features:

3.3
Terminal-Consistent Residual Flow
Given a degraded input Y and its soft prior c = { ˜Ptype, Pattr}, we first obtain a posterior-mean
anchor µ = Ψ∗(Y, c), where Ψ∗(·) denotes the trained perception-conditioned AWR backbone. We
define the source sample as Z0 = µ + δ ϵ with ϵ ∼N(0, I) and δ controlling the perturbation scale.
In the PMRF setting (Eq. 1), the terminal relation involves E[Z0 | Z1 = X], where X denotes the
clean target image. This term requires averaging the predictor output over the unknown conditional
distribution p(Y | X) and is therefore generally intractable to compute or enforce explicitly in AWR.
To obtain a more tractable formulation and improve stability near t →1, we reparameterize the
transport in a posterior-anchored residual space by defining r0 = Z0 −µ and r1 = Z1 −µ, so that
E[r0 | r1] = 0 holds by construction. This residual formulation removes the content-dependent shift
from the terminal relation and enables a terminal-consistent velocity parameterization. The linear
interpolation path in residual space is then:
rt = (1 −t)r0 + tr1,
t ∈[0, 1].
(6)
Terminal-Consistent Velocity Parameterization.
Instead of directly regressing the residual
displacement r1 −r0, we parameterize the residual-space velocity using a correction network
Φφ(rt, t; ˜Ptype, Pattr), which predicts a correction field in Eq. 7. Since the coefficient t(1 −t)
vanishes at t = 1, the velocity reduces to v(r1, 1) = r1 regardless of Φφ. Thus, the terminal behavior
is guaranteed by the parameterization, rather than being implicitly fitted by the network.
vφ(rt, t; ˜Ptype, Pattr) = trt + t(1 −t) Φφ(rt, t; ˜Ptype, Pattr).
(7)

Conclusion
In this paper, we proposed PVRF, a perception-aware rectified-flow framework for all-in-one adverse
weather removal that injects VLM-extracted soft perceptions into restoration via the plug-and-play
AMN and WWA modules. We further introduce a terminal-consistent residual rectified-flow design
to stabilize terminal learning and improve visual realism. Extensive experiments across multiple
all-in-one settings demonstrate consistent state-of-the-art performance and strong generalization to
unseen and mixed degradations.
