PRINCIPAL GROUPOID MODELS FOR STABLE UCT
KIRCHBERG ALGEBRAS
SAMUEL EVINGTON AND PHILIPP SIBBEL
Abstract. We show that every stable UCT Kirchberg algebra has a principal
´etale groupoid model, and thus contains a C∗-diagonal. Every unital UCT
Kirchberg algebra A for which [1A]0 has infinite order in K0(A) is also covered
by our methods. In particular, we obtain a principal ´etale groupoid model for
the Cuntz algebra O∞.
Introduction
Every simple, separable, amenable C∗-algebra that absorbs the Jiang–Su alge-
bra Z tensorially ([17]) and satisfies the universal coefficient theorem (UCT) of
Rosenberg and Schochet ([34]) is isomorphic to the reduced C∗-algebra of a twisted
groupoid. However, it is an open question whether the twist can be eliminated and
if the groupoid can be taken to be principal (i.e. the groupoid of an equivalence
relation).
In the stably finite case, the existence of a twisted groupoid model was established
by Xin Li ([27]). His strategy was to use the classification machinery of the Elliott
classification programme (see [10, 11, 39, 38, 16, 4] for an overview) to realise
the C∗-algebra A as an inductive limit of simpler building blocks and then build
a Cartan subalgebra in A. By Kumjian–Renault theory, this leads to a twisted
groupoid model for A ([23, 32]). In this case, it is known that the groupoid can
always be taken to be principal, but it is unknown if the twist can be eliminated in
general.
In the purely infinite case, the strategy has been to consider C∗-algebras associ-
ated to graphs and more general graph-like objects such as higher-rank graphs and
topological graphs ([37, 21, 40, 28, 5]). These C∗-algebras have natural groupoid
models, which can be constructed by considering paths in the graph-like object. By
the Kirchberg–Phillips theorem ([22, 29]), K-theory can be used to detect isomor-
phisms, so it suffices to exhaust all possible values for the K-theory. In this case,
it is known that a twist is never needed, but it is unknown if the groupoid can be
taken to be principal in general.
In this paper, we prove that all simple, separable, amenable, purely infinite
C∗-algebras (i.e. Kirchberg algebras) that are stable and satisfy the UCT have a
groupoid model that is both untwisted and principal.

overview) and was used in the construction of groupoid models for the Jiang–Su
algebra Z.

Kirchberg proved that a simple, separable, amenable C∗-algebra A is purely
infinite if and only if A ∼= A ⊗O∞([22]). Moreover, by the Kirchberg–Phillips
theorem ([22, 29]), Kirchberg algebras that satisfy the UCT are classified by their
K-theory. More precisely, two unital UCT Kirchberg algebras A and B are isomor-
phic if and only if ((K0(A), [1A]0), K1(A)) ∼= ((K0(B), [1B]0), K1(B)), and stable
UCT Kirchberg algebras A and B are isomorphic if and only if (K0(A), K1(A)) ∼=
(K0(B), K1(B)); see for example [33, Theorem 8.4.1]. These two results cover all
cases as Kirchberg algebras are either unital or stable ([42]). It is also known that
any pair of countable abelian groups can be realised as the K-theory of a UCT
Kirchberg algebra A and, in the unital case, any group element can be realised as
[1A]0; see for example [33, Section 4.3].

1.3. Kumjian–Renault theory. Cartan subalgebras and C∗-diagonals are spe-
cial classes of abelian subalgebras with close connections to groupoid C∗-algebras
thanks to the work of Kumjian ([23]) and Renault ([32]). We first recall the relevant
definitions.
Definition 1.1 (cf. [32, Definition 5.1]). Let A be a C∗-algebra. A non-degenerate
sub-C∗-algebra D ⊆A is called a Cartan subalgebra if
(i) D is a maximal abelian sub-C∗-algebra of A,
(ii) A is generated by NA(D) = {n ∈A : n∗Dn ⊆D and nDn∗⊆D}, and
(iii) there exists a faithful conditional expectation of A →D.
Moreover, D ⊆A is called a C∗-diagonal if additionally
(iv) every pure state on D has a unique extension to a pure state on A.
We now provide a formal statement of Kumjian–Renault theory.
Theorem 1.2 ([23, 32]). There is a one-to-one correspondence between Cartan
subalgebras and twisted ´etale groupoids. More precisely:
(i) Let G be a topologically principal, second-countable, locally compact, Haus-
dorff, ´etale groupoid and let Σ be a twist over G. Then C∗
r (G, Σ) is separa-
ble and C0(G(0)) ⊆C∗
r (G, Σ) is a Cartan subalgebra. Moreover, C0(G(0)) ⊆
C∗
r (G, Σ) is a C∗-diagonal if and only if G is principal.
(ii) Let A be a separable C∗-algebra and D ⊆A be a Cartan subalgebra. Then
there is a topologically principal, second-countable, locally compact, Haus-
dorff, ´etale groupoid G and a twist Σ such that the inclusion D ⊆A is iso-
morphic to C0(G(0)) ⊆C∗
r (G, Σ). Moreover, the twisted groupoid (G, Σ) is
uniquely determined up to isomorphism by the inclusion D ⊆A.
We will make frequent use of Kumjian–Renault theory to reformulate groupoid
results in the language of Cartan subalgebras and vice versa.

1.7. Minimal dynamics on point-like spaces. A homeomorphism ρ : X →X
from a topological space X to itself is said to be minimal if the only closed ρ-
invariant subsets of X are ∅and X.
In general, there are topological obstructions to the existence of a minimal home-
omorphism on a given space (see [9] for a discussion). In particular, the Lefschetz
fixed point theorem ([25]) implies that no even-dimensional sphere can be equipped
with a minimal homeomorphism. However, minimal homeomorphisms are known
to exist on all odd-dimensional spheres ([15]).
Motivated by the goal of finding a dynamical model for the Jiang–Su algebra Z,
Deeley, Putnam and Strung constructed a minimal homeomorphism on a space Z
that has the same cohomology and K-theory as a point ([8]). The existence of such
a minimal dynamical system is consistent with the Lefschetz fixed point theorem
because the space Z does not satisfy the regularity conditions of the fixed point
theorem.
Loosely speaking, the space Z is constructed by starting with a minimal home-
omorphism ρ : Sd →Sd where d is odd and d ≥3. A suitable ρ-invariant subset
L∞⊆Sd is then removed and the resulting space Sd\L∞is completed with respect
to a suitable metric. The map ρ is then shown to induce a minimal homeomorphism
on Z, and the desired topological properties of Z are established by realising Z as
an inverse limit of contractible spaces.
The following theorem summarises the key properties of the Deeley–Putnam–
Strung construction that we will use in this paper. We write {∗} for the topological
space with a single point.
Theorem 1.11 (cf. [8] and [9, Theorem 2.5]). Let d be an odd number with d ≥3.
There exists an infinite compact metric space Z with covering dimension d or d −1
and a minimal homeomorphism ρ : Z →Z such that, for any continuous generalised
cohomology theory H∗(·), there is an isomorphism of groups H∗(Z) ∼= H∗({∗}). In
particular, this holds for ˇCech cohomology and K-theory.
Translating from topological K-theory to C∗-algebraic K-theory, we observe that
the C∗-algebra C(Z) of continuous functions on the space Z from Theorem 1.11
has the same K-theory as the complex numbers C. In fact, C(Z) is KK-equivalent
to C by [8, Corollary 1.12].
