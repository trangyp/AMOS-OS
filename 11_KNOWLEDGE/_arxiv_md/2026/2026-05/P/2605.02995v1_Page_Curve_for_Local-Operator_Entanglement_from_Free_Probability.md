The local-operator entanglement (LOE) measures the classical simulability of a Heisenberg oper-
ator and is conjectured to witness many-body chaos in locally interacting systems. Using tools from
free probability, we analytically compute its value for Haar random dynamics for all Rényi indices.
We find that it asymptotically reproduces the Page curve for random states in the case of traceless
operators, with exponentially deviating corrections. In contrast to higher-order out-of-time ordered
correlators, which depend on operator correlations via free cumulants, the leading-order LOE is in-
dependent of the initial operator. Guided by our Haar result, we therefore argue that the long-time
value of the LOE entropies in chaotic systems will depend only on autocorrelation functions of the
initial operator up to exponentially small corrections, suggesting that the higher-order structure of
the full Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis is not necessary to describe it.
Introduction.— Recently, there has been an explosion
of interest in properties of operators in many-body sys-
tems. For instance, scrambling of information as mea-
sured by out-of-time ordered correlators (OTOCs) is gen-
erally inaccessible to any single quantum state, quanti-
fying how quantum correlations spread in a many-body
system [1–3].
Indeed, a finer notion of quantum ther-
malization has been developed to explain their behav-
ior at long times in chaotic systems: the full Eigenstate
Thermalization Hypothesis (ETH) predicts correlations
between the matrix elements [4], with a universal struc-
ture depending on free probability [5], the mathematics of
large, non-commutative random variables [6–8]. The full
ETH is naturally organized around the connected higher-
point moments of the operator of interest—the so-called
free cumulants, a ubiquitous quantity in free probability.
Alongside these correlator-based probes, the local-
operator entanglement (LOE) has emerged as a central
tool that simultaneously witnesses quantum chaos while
quantifying the resources required for classical simulation
of Heisenberg operators. It is defined via the state repre-
sentation: any O on a D-dimensional Hilbert space cor-
responds to a state in the doubled Hilbert space [9, 10],
∣OU⟫∶= (OU ⊗1)∣ϕ+⟩,
(1)
with ∣ϕ+⟩∶= D−1/2 ∑i ∣ii⟩∈H⊗2 and OU ∶= U †OU; the
LOE is then the entanglement of ∣OU⟫across a spa-
tial bipartition [11]. Concretely, its scaling sets the cost
of tensor-network representations of O [12], while also
bounding the resources [13, 14] of Pauli propagation [15–
19] and Stabilizer methods [20]. As a probe of chaos, un-
like state entanglement [21], the LOE grows strictly loga-
rithmically under integrable spin chain dynamics [22–27]
and bounds generic four-point OTOCs [28, 29], directly
tying it to the scrambling diagnostics above.
Despite these recent progresses,
questions remain
about which features of the more studied (state) en-
tanglement theory carry over to operator space.

Discussion.— A few comments are in order. First of
all, despite the distinct construction, we emphasize that
the LOE of random operators corresponds to the Page
curve in a doubled Hilbert space [30], at the first two
orders in D.
At higher corrections, Eq. (25) deviates
from the state Page curve with an exponentially small but
operator-dependent value (for which we found an explicit
expression up to k = 4; see App. C).
Secondly, it is informative to compare our result to the
‘magic’ resource of the operator: namely, the amount of
non-Clifford resources it takes to produce from an ini-
tial Pauli, as measured by the operator Stabilizer en-
tropies (OSE) [13]. The OSE is always an upper bound
to the LOE [14], and its value governs simulability ac-
cording to Pauli propagation algorithms [13, 15–19]. For
a Haar-evolved operator, the correction from maximum
is log((2k −1)!!)/(1 −k).
The combinatorial factor of
(2k−1)!! is equal to the number of pairings of 2k elements,
pointing to the fact that free probability is not necessary
to arrive at it, in contrast to our LOE results [56].
Finally, we return to our original motivation: whether
the long-time LOE in chaotic systems depends on higher
moments of the initial operator, and hence the full
ETH. We find that the Haar-averaged LOE depends only
on second cumulants for arbitrary k, and is operator-
independent for traceless operators at leading order.
Since such Haar expressions guide ETH predictions for
ergodic dynamics [4, 53, 57], we can conclude that only
two-point correlations matter at long times—the stan-
dard ETH suffices, unlike higher-point OTOCs whose
leading behavior requires the full ETH [4, 5].
Higher-
point OTOCs and the LOE thus measure fundamentally
different aspects of operator scrambling at long times.
Outlook.— Using free probability, we have analytically
computed the Haar Page curve of the LOE, obtaining an
operator-independent leading-order term and first Page
correction, with exponentially small higher-cumulant cor-
rections.
A natural next step is to formally extend this
to an ETH setting.

dependence [35, 61], cf. Eqs. (2) and (25). Whether an
analogous relation holds for the LOE is an open question;
a first step would be to define a finite-temperature LOE
while preserving its operational properties, where the
energy-window projected operators of Ref. [63] may offer
guidance.
