Introduction
All graphs considered in this article are assumed to be finite, have no self-adjacent vertices, and
no parallel edges. Let N denote the set of positive integers. An additive coloring of a graph G, first
studied in [6] (and originally called a lucky labeling), is a function c : V (G) →N such that whenever
v and w are adjacent vertices in G, P
u∈N(v) c(u) ̸= P
u∈N(w) c(u). If, in addition, c is also a proper
coloring of G, then c is called a proper additive coloring of G. The additive chromatic number of a
graph G, denoted η(G), is the least integer k such that the vertices of G can be additively colored using
labels from the set {1, 2, . . . , k}, and the proper additive chromatic number of G, denoted, ηp(G) is the
least k such that there exists a proper additive coloring of G using labels from the set {1, 2, . . . , k}.
The edge coloring analog of an additive coloring is called an additive edge coloring. An additive edge
coloring is a function c : E(G) →N such that for any two incident edges e1 and e2, P
e∈N′(e1) c(e) ̸=
P
e∈N′(e2) c(e). (Here, N′(e) denotes the set of edges incident to e in G.) Equivalently, one can define
an additive edge coloring of G to be an additive coloring of its line graph, L(G). A proper additive
edge coloring is an additive edge coloring that is also a proper edge coloring. The additive chromatic
index of G, denoted η′(G), is the least k such that there exists an additive edge coloring of G using
labels from the set {1, 2, . . . , k}. The proper additive chromatic index of G, denoted, η′
p(G) is the least
k such that there exists a proper additive edge coloring of G using labels from the set {1, 2, . . . , k}.
Letting χ(G) and χ′(G) denote the traditional chromatic number and chromatic index, respectively,
the following inequalities are immediate from the above definitions: η(G) ≤ηp(G), χ(G) ≤ηp(G),
χ′(G) ≤η′
p(G), η′(G) ≤η′
p(G). And in [6], Czerwi´nski et al. made the following well-known conjecture
regarding additive colorings.
Conjecture 1. [6] For all graphs G, η(G) ≤χ(G).
Conjecture 1 is known as the Additive Coloring Conjecture. This conjecture remains open, but
some progress has been made – for example, it was shown in [3] that if G is planar, η(G) ≤468.
(An improvement from η(G) ≤100280245065 in [6], but still quite far from η(G) ≤χ(G) ≤4, which
must hold if the additive coloring conjecture is true.) Further significant results regarding the additive
chromatic number of planar graphs can be found in [1],[3],[4], and [10]. Progress for some other specific
types of graphs can be found in [13].
There is still plenty of room for progress on the Additive Coloring Conjecture. For example, it
appears to be unknown whether there is a constant k such that for all graphs G, η(G) ≤kχ(G), or
even whether there is a function f : N →R such that for all graphs G, η(G) ≤f(χ(G)).

Concluding Remarks
The reader may have noticed that the proofs of Theorems 2 and 5 differ from those of the other
results in this work in that they provide a set A ⊆N such that every proper edge coloring c : E(G) →A
is an additive edge coloring, as opposed to at least one such proper coloring existing. In light of this,
define the strong proper additive chromatic number of a graph G, denoted ηsp(G), to be the least k ∈N
such that there exists some A ⊆{1, 2, . . . , k} with the property that G can be properly colored using
the elements of A and every proper coloring of G using colors from A is also an additive coloring.
Define the strong proper additive chromatic index, η′
sp(G), analogously for edge colorings. Then
Theorems 2 and 5 also hold true if we replace η′
p(G) with η′
sp(G) in their statements. One course of
future research could be to determine precise relationships between ηp(G) and ηsp(G) or between η′
p(G)
and η′
sp(G) for other classes of graphs. More specifically, one might consider the following question.
Question 17. For which graphs G (other than line graphs of d-regular Vizing-class-1 graphs), does
ηp(G) = ηsp(G)?
Additionally, a question that arises naturally from the results in this work is the following.
Question 18. Is it true that if G is a d-regular graph, then η′
P (G) = χ′(G)?
An affirmative answer to Question 18 would be a strengthening of the theorems in this work and
would also be a strengthening of the additive coloring conjecture for the class of line graphs of d-regular
graphs.
