PROBLEMS IN ADDITIVE NUMBER THEORY, VII:
THE STRUCTURE OF ADDITIVE h-BASES FOR n
MELVYN B. NATHANSON
Abstract. In additive number theory, a finite set A of integers is an h-basis
for n if every integer in {0, 1, 2, . . . , n} can be represented as the sum of exactly
h not necessarily distinct elements of A. This paper introduces a new class of
problems for these and related additive bases. The problems are designed, in
part, to be susceptible to solution by AI.
1. What can humans do?
Today (May 25, 2026), computers can solve mathematical problems sufficiently
difficult to constitute an acceptable Ph.D. thesis and they have even solved a few
significantly more important problems. But computers have not yet been able to
decide what is an “interesting” problem nor to formulate new conjectures or new
areas of research. That is the (likely transitory) advantage that we humans retain.
This paper introduces new questions in additive number theory.
All should be
solvable by humans and some by the AI systems now being applied to questions in
arithmetic. An asterisk (*) identifies problems that AI might have difficulty solving.
2. Four additive number theoretic functions
Let N = {1, 2, 3, . . .} be the set of positive integers, N0 = {0, 1, 2, 3, . . .} the set
of nonnegative integers, and Z the set of all integers. An interval of integers is a
set of the form
[a, b] = {j ∈Z : a ≤j ≤b}
with a, b ∈Z and a < b. Thus, every interval contains at least two integers. The
length of the interval [a, b] is ℓ= b −a. An interval of length ℓcontains ℓ+ 1
integers.
An integer in a subset A of Z is isolated if it is not a member of an
interval contained in A.
The cardinality of the set A is denoted |A|. For every nonempty set X of integers,
we define
AX(k) = {A ⊆X : |A| = k}.
We are interested in infinite sets X and, in particular, the sets X = N0 and X = Z.
The h-fold sumset of a set A of integers is the set hA of all sums of h not
necessarily distinct elements of A:
hA = {a1 + · · · + ah : ai ∈A for all i ∈[1, h]}.

3. A generalization of Sidon sets
The study of additive h-bases for n has consisted mostly of (still unsuccessful)
attempts to compute or to estimate asymptotically the counting function nh(k),
often by constructing explicit families of such sets. Also investigated is the number
of subsets of [0, n] that are h-bases for n (for calculations with h = 2, see OEIS
A066062) and the size, denoted kh(n), of the smallest subset of [0, n] that is an
h-basis for n (for calculations of k2(n), see OEIS A066063). The other counting
functions constructed in Section 2 are largely unstudied.

of A does not include the number ak as a summand, and so A′ = A \ {ak} satisfies
A′ ∈AN0(k −1) and [0, nh(k)] ⊆hA′. Therefore,
nh(k) ≤ℓh(A′) ≤nh(k −1)
which contradicts Theorem 1. This completes the proof.
□

7. Caveat lector
There is an extensive, albeit largely forgotten literature, mostly in German, on
the function nh(k) (e.g. Bloom [1], G¨unt¨urk and Nathanson [2], H¨ammerer and
Hofmeister [3], H¨artter [4], Hofmeister [5, 8, 6, 7], Kohonen [9], J. Marzuola and A.
Miller [10], Moser [11], Moser, Pounder, and J. Riddell [12], Mossige [13], Mrose [14],
Nathanson [15], Rohrbach [16], St¨ohr [17], Weltge and Zyhalko [18], Yu [19, 20])
and it is possible that the problems in this paper have solutions long buried in back
issues of journals, but now resident in and retrievable from LLMs.
We have looked at nh(k) as a function of k for fixed h, but there are also problems
and results when considering nh(k) as a function of h for fixed k, and also symmetry
questions relating nh(k) and nk(h).
There is an important function dual to nh(k). For positive integers h and n,
let kh(n) be the smallest integer k such that there exists a set A of k nonnegative
integers such that A is a basis of order h for n, that is, [0, n] ⊆hA. There are, of
course, many open problems associated to the additive number theoretic function
kh(n).
We have considered only sums of finite sets of integers and sums of finite sets of
nonnegative integers, but there are analogous problems for sums of finite subsets
of the squares, kth powers, primes, and other interesting sets of integers. Mathe-
maticians have also considered not only the longest intervals but also the longest
finite arithmetic progressions, the longest finite geometric progressions, and other
finite configurations contained in h-fold sumsets.
