PRESILTING SEQUENCES FOR 0-AUSLANDER EXTRIANGULATED CATEGORIES
IACOPO NONIS
Abstract. Let C be a reduced 0-Auslander extriangulated category. Motivated by Pan–Zhu silting reduction
for such categories, we introduce the notion of (signed) presilting sequences in C and establish a bijection
between (signed) presilting sequences in C and (signed) τ-exceptional sequences over Λ = EndC (P), where
P is a projective generator of C . This correspondence provides a new perspective on the Buan–Marsh bijection
between signed τ-exceptional sequences and ordered support τ-rigid objects. Furthermore, we introduce a new
category M(C ), called the τ-cluster morphism category of C , whose objects are certain extension-closed sub-
categories of C and whose morphisms are described in terms of signed presilting sequences. As an application,
we recover the τ-cluster morphism category of Λ from M(C ).

1. Introduction
The notion of extriangulated categories, introduced by Nakaoka and Palu in [NP19], provides a unified
generalization of both exact and triangulated categories. Notable instances of extriangulated categories
include extension-closed subcategories of triangulated categories. Moreover, the class of extriangulated
categories is closed under taking ideal quotients formed by projective-injective objects [NP19], as well as
certain relative structures [ZZ18, HLN21, Sak23] and localizations [NOS22].
A fundamental technique in silting theory for triangulated categories [KV88, AI12] is that of reduction
[IY18]. Silting reduction studies collections of silting objects or subcategories that share a common direct
summand, and produces a bijective correspondence with silting objects in a smaller category obtained
by “factoring out” this common part. Recently, this notion has been extended to the broader setting of
extriangulated categories; see [AT22, AT25, LZZZ24, PZ25, Bør24].
Motivated by the additive categorification of cluster algebras [BMR+06], Adachi, Iyama, and Reiten
introduced τ-tilting theory in [AIR14]. Let Λ be a finite dimensional algebra, and let τ denote the Auslan-
der–Reiten translation in mod Λ. Recall that a Λ-module M is called τ-rigid if HomΛ(M, τM) = 0, and

τ-tilting if, in addition, |M| = |Λ|, where |X| denotes the number of isomorphism classes of indecomposable

Reduction techniques for τ-tilting modules were subsequently developed by Jasso [Jas15]. For a Λ-
module M, let M⊥0 denote the full subcategory of mod Λ consisting of all modules X such that HomΛ(M, X) =
0, and define ⊥0M dually. Given a basic τ-rigid Λ-module M, Jasso introduced the τ-perpendicular cat-
egory J(M) = M⊥0 ∩⊥0τM, thereby extending the notion of perpendicular categories of Geigle–Lenzing
from the hereditary setting. He showed that J(M) is equivalent to the module category of a finite dimen-
sional algebra ΓM. The algebra ΓM is known as a τ-tilting reduction. This perspective was subsequently
broadened to the setting of support τ-rigid objects in [DIR+23]. More precisely, for a support τ-rigid object
U = M ⊕P[1] in C(Λ), one defines J(U) = ⊥0τM ∩(P ⊕M)⊥0. In this case, the equivalence between J(U)
and the module category of a finite dimensional algebra ΓU follows by a direct generalization of Jasso’s
result which is the special case P = 0 (see [DIR+23, Thm. 4.12]). We refer to a projective object in a
τ-perpendicular subcategory J(U) as relative projective.
Exceptional sequences were first introduced in algebraic geometry [Bon89, GR87, Gor88], and were
later studied in the representation theory of hereditary algebras by Crawley-Boevey and Ringel [CB92,
Rin94]. An exceptional sequence (M1, · · · , Mt) is a sequence of indecomposable exceptional objects,
where an object is called exceptional if its endomorphism algebra is a division ring and it has no self-
extensions in any degree. Moreover, the sequence is required to satisfy Hom(Mi, Mj) = 0 = Ext≥1(Mi, Mj)
for i > j.
Motivated by τ-tilting theory and Jasso’s reduction for τ-tilting modules, Buan and Marsh [BM21b]
introduced τ-exceptional sequences for a finite dimensional algebra Λ as a generalization of exceptional
sequences in the hereditary setting. A sequence (X1, . . . , Xt) of indecomposable Λ-modules is called τ-
exceptional if Xt is τ-rigid in mod Λ, and (X1, . . . , Xt−1) is a τ-exceptional sequence in J(Xt). If t =
|Λ|, the sequence is said to be complete. Complete τ-exceptional sequences always exist for arbitrary
finite dimensional algebras, in contrast to complete classical exceptional sequences, which in general exist
only in the hereditary case. Moreover, over hereditary algebras, exceptional sequences and τ-exceptional
sequences coincide.
More generally, a signed τ-exceptional sequence, is a τ-exceptional sequence in C(Λ) in which rela-
tive projective objects can be assigned a sign (see Section 3). Signed τ-exceptional sequences generalise
classical signed exceptional sequences introduced by Igusa and Todorov in [IT17]. Signed exceptional
sequences were originally defined to describe morphisms in the cluster morphism category, a category
defined by Igusa–Todorov whose objects are finitely generated wide subcategories of the module cate-
gory of a hereditary algebra. Since then, cluster morphism categories have been actively studied in the
representation theory of finite dimensional algebras. The definition of the cluster morphism category was
generalised by Buan and Marsh [BM21a] to the case of τ-tilting finite algebras, that is, algebras with only
finitely many isoclasses of indecomposable τ-rigid modules [DIJ19], and given the name of τ-cluster mor-
phism category in [HI21b]. The definition was extended to arbitrary finite dimensional algebras by Buan
and Hanson in [BH23]. The objects of the τ-cluster morphism category are τ-perpendicular subcategories
of the module category of a finite dimensional algebra, and morphisms can be described in terms of signed
τ-exceptional sequences. The τ-cluster morphism category exhibits notable connections with topology.
In particular, Hanson and Igusa [HI21b] showed that its classifying space admits the structure of a cube
complex. Furthermore, in many cases, this classifying space is a K(π, 1) space, where π is the picture
group of the algebra [ITW16] (see also [HI21a, BH26]). Finally, the τ-cluster morphism category has also
been studied from both geometric [STTW26, Kai25] and silting-theoretic perspectives [Bør21].
