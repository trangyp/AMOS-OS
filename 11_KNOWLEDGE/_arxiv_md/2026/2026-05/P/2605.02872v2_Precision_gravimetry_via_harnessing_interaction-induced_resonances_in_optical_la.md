By confining a Bose-Einstein condensate in a vertical lattice subjected to a gravitational potential,
we analyze the quantum Fisher information to determine its scaling with respect to time, system size
and particle number. Our results reveal that in the localized phase, on-site interactions U amplify
the quantum Fisher information by a factor with respect to resonance condition U = mh where U is
factor of gradient field amplitude h. This precision enhancement can be employed in gravitational
acceleration measurements with a finite number of particles trapped in optical lattices.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Quantum properties of physical systems potentially
make them a powerful resource for sensing gravita-
tional [1–4], electric [5–13] and magnetic fields [14–23]—
surpassing the sensitivity limits of classical methods [24–
34]. In particular, significant attention has been dedi-
cated for the development of quantum gravimeters [35–
49].
Atomic interferometry, a technique based on the
quantum interference of ultracold atoms, is widely used
in precision gravitational physics. The applications in-
clude Raman interferometry for measuring Earth’s grav-
itational acceleration g [50] and its gradient [35], deter-
mining the gravitational constant [36, 37], introducing a
potential redefinition of the kilogram [51], probing the
quantum nature of gravity [52], and enabling geophys-
ical studies [39].
A central aspect of atomic interfer-
ometry is the spatial delocalization of a large ensemble
of atoms into two distinct vertical paths, which subse-
quently recombine to generate a measurable interference
pattern [2, 53–57].
Bose-Einstein condensates (BECs)
serves as an ideal atomic source for such atomic interfer-
ometers. While atomic interactions in dense BEC clouds
can be beneficial to generate spin squeezing [27, 38, 58–
61], they generally have detrimental effects on the preci-
sion of atom interferometry measurements [62–68]. This
raises a key question: can alternative many-body physics
setups be used to create spatial superposition states in

particles while leveraging the advantageous aspects of
atomic interactions to enhance gravimetry?
Ultracold atoms confined in optical lattices, created
by interfering laser beams [69, 70], have a wide range
of quantum applications such as analyzing strongly cor-
related phases [71–76], determination of forces with mi-
crometer resolution [77–82], quantum information pro-
cessing [83–87] and realizing the quantum walk in the
tilted lattice [88]. The high level of control and measure-
ment precision in ultracold atoms enables detailed studies
of ground-state properties [89, 90] or out-of-equilibrium
dynamics [71, 91] in quantum many-body systems. A key
advantage of optical lattices is the high controllability by
means of external fields and electromagnetic radiation.
By adjusting the depth of the optical potential, one can
adjust the ratio between interaction and kinetic energies
in the system, inducing a quantum phase transition from
a superfluid to a Mott insulator phase [71, 92–94]. Simi-
larly, magnetic and optical Feshbach resonances allow the
dynamics of repulsive and attractive interatomic poten-
tials to be precisely controlled [95–97]. Such systems can
also be served as quantum sensors. While recent studies
have investigated the sensing properties in Wannier-Stark
systems [98–103], a comprehensive analysis in optical lat-
tices is still missing.
In this paper, we investigate the Quantum Fisher In-
formation (QFI), as a figure of merit for sensing gravita-
tional field, for an ultracold gas trapped in an optical lat-
tice. In particular, we focus on QFI scaling with respect
to time, system size, and particle number across vari-
ous range of on-site interaction. Our results demonstrate
that in the localized phase, resonant conditions—where

IV.
GRADIENT FIELD SENSING WITH
BOSE-HUBBARD MODEL
In this section, we use the non-equilibrium quantum
state of a Bose-Hubbard model for sensing the gradient
field h.
In Figs. 2(a)-(c) we plot the normalized QFI,
namely FQ/t2, as a function of time for three different
choices of h/J corresponding to localized, critical and
extended phases, respectively. The exact determination
of the phases will be clarified below. As Fig. 2 shows,
in each phase, the normalized QFI for different choices
of on-site interaction U is depicted. Two observations
are clearly evident. First, After a transient time the QFI
scales quadratically with time which is expected due to
unitary evolution [117]. Second, the on-site interaction
U hardly affects the evolution of the QFI for the choices
of h/J in the extended phase and the transition point,
see Figs. 2(b) and (c). In fact, the on-site interaction U
can only weakly affect the QFI in the localized phase, as
shown in Fig. 2(a). We will investigate the role of U in
the sensing capacity of our probe in more details in the
next section.
Since in the long time limit the QFI scales quadrat-
ically with time, we now focus on extracting the scal-
ing with respect to system size and particle numbers.
To identify the QFI scaling with respect to system, in
Figs. 3(a)-(c), we plot the normalized QFI, FQ/t2 at long
time limit, as a function of h/J for three choices of on-
site interaction U/J keeping the particle numbers fixed to
N=2. As the figures show, by increasing h/J the QFI in-
creases size-dependently in the extended phase (i.e. small
values of h/J) until it reaches its peak at the transition
point hc/J after which it decays size-independently in
the localized phase.
For all interaction strengths U/J, the normalized QFI
scales as FQ/t2 ∼Lβ. This scaling is demonstrated in
Fig. 3(d) for the extended phase with β > 2, transition
point with β = 2 and the localized phase with β = 0.
In the inset of Fig. 3(d), we plot the scaling exponent
β as a function of h/J. The exponent β shows super-
linear scaling of the QFI in the extended phase clearly
identifying quantum-enhanced sensitivity. Interestingly,
exactly at the transition point hc/J the exponent reaches
β = 2 which is identical to a single-particle system in
Ref. [101].

V.
QFI AT RESONANCE CONDITIONS
The occupation of lattice sites by multiple bosons is
constrained by on-site repulsive interactions (U > 0). As
shown in Fig. 2, while the impact of U on sensitivity is
weak in the extended phase and at the transition point,
its effect on sensitivity in the localized phase can be com-
plex and significant. In this section, we systematically
investigate the impact of on-site interaction U on sens-
ing capacity of the Bose-Hubbard model. In Fig. 5(a)
we display the normalized QFI as a function of both U
and h for a system of size L = 11 and boson number
N = 4.
Interestingly, the normalized QFI reveals os-
cillations with consistent local maxima along the lines
of U = mh for m = 1, 2, 3, 4, which are highlighted in
the figure by colored lines.
To quantify the metrolog-
ical advantage along these lines, Fig. 5(b) displays the
normalized QFI enhancement ratio FQ(U)/FQ(U=0) for
different U/J.
One can see at the vicinity of U=mh
the significant QFI amplification, while out of resonance
reads FQ(U̸=mh)/FQ(U=0)=1 indicating that the sens-
ing performance reduces to the non-interacting values.
The above fluctuations are associated with resonances
between different particle configurations in which indi-
vidual particles can move along the lattice despite the
gradient field energy differences at the expense of having
double (or multiple) occupancies at certain lattice sites.
To elucidate the underlying mechanism, starting with the
initial state |...0101010...⟩in Fig. 1(b), the corresponding
configurations for U=mh are illustrated conserving the
initial energy E0. The tunneling of bosons to occupied
sites is suppressed by the repulsive on-site interaction U,
except near these resonant points where energy matches
U=mh.

investigate the expectation value of the following opera-

VII.
CONCLUSION
We study the scaling of the QFI with respect to time,
probe size and the number of bosonic atoms in different
on-site interaction strength regimes. Our analysis shows
that operating at resonance condition, where the inter-
action strength in Stark Bose-Hubbard model relates to
the gradient field as U = mh, significantly enhances mea-
surement sensitivity by amplifying the QFI by a factor
of Ar(U, m) . In contrast, under off-resonance conditions
U ̸= mh, the interaction strength U has no significant
impact on the many-body Bloch oscillations in optical
lattices and the corresponding QFI. This resonance en-
hancement of the QFI can be particularly advantageous
for optical lattice gravimetry, as it allows for higher gravi-
tational field sensitivity with the same number of trapped
particles.
