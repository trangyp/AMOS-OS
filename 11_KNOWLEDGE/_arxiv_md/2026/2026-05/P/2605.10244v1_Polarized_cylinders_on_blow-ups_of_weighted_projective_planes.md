POLARIZED CYLINDERS ON BLOW-UPS OF WEIGHTED PROJECTIVE
PLANES
IN-KYUN KIM, MASATOMO SAWAHARA, AND JOONYEONG WON
Abstract. We study polarized cylinders in certain rational surfaces arising from blow-ups of
weighted projective planes. In particular, we consider the surfaces obtained by blowing up m+4
points in general position on the weighted projective plane P(1, 1, m). These surfaces appear
naturally as weighted hypersurfaces or quasi-smooth complete intersections.
Throughout this article, all considered varieties are assumed to be algebraic and defined over
an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.
1. Introduction.
The existence of polarized cylinders in Fano varieties has attracted considerable attention in
recent years due to its close connection with additive group actions on affine cones and flexibility
properties of affine varieties. In particular, anticanonically polarized cylinders, namely (−KX)-
polar cylinders, play an important role in understanding the geometry of Fano varieties and the
structure of their associated affine cones [3, 4, 5, 6, 13, 14, 15, 9, 10, 17]. The presence of such
cylinders often reflects strong geometric properties of the underlying variety and provides an
effective bridge between birational geometry and affine algebraic geometry.
Let X be a normal projective variety. An open subset U ⊆X is called a cylinder if U ∼= A1×Z
for some variety Z. Let H be an ample Q-divisor on X. We say that a cylinder U is an H-polar
cylinder if there exists an effective Q-divisor D on X such that D ∼Q H and U = X \ Supp(D).
The study of polarized cylinders is strongly motivated by the investigation of additive group
actions on affine cones.
Theorem 1 ([13, Theorem 3.1.9], [15, Theorem 2.1]). Let X be a normal projective variety and
let H be an ample Q-divisor on X. Then the affine cone
Spec

admits a non-trivial Ga-action if and only if X contains an H-polar cylinder.
Moreover, the geometry of polarized cylinders has applications to the study of flexibility
of affine cones and additive group actions on complements of hypersurfaces; see for example
[18, 19, 11, 12, 20, 21, 22].
Despite their importance, (−KX)-polar cylinders are rather rare.
For instance, it follows
from [8] that a quasi-smooth well-formed complete intersection log del Pezzo surface of index
one admits an anticanonical polar cylinder if and only if it is a smooth complete intersection
of two quadrics in P4. On the other hand, smooth complete intersections of two quadrics in P4
admit a large number of cylinders. Similar phenomena also appear in higher dimensions [23].
As a natural generalization of smooth complete intersections of two quadrics in P4, one
may consider complete intersections of two weighted hypersurfaces in the weighted projective
space. These complete intersections are realized as blow-ups Sm of the weighted projective plane
P(1, 1, m) at m + 4 points in general position, where m is odd. More precisely, if m = 2k −1,
then Sm is isomorphic to a quasi-smooth complete intersection of two weighted hypersurfaces of
degree 2k in
P(1, 1, k, k, 2k −1)

Organization of the paper. In §2, we review several notations for the Fujita invariant for log
del Pezzo surfaces, where we follow [5]. Since the surface Sm, which is obtained by blow-up at
m + 4 general points on P(1, 1, m), is a log del Pezzo surface, the Fujita invariant theory applies
in our setting. Hence, we can use the Fujita invariant theory to Sm. On the other hand, we also
construct some examples of cylinders of Sm in this section. These examples will be used in the
next section.
In §3, we prove Theorem 2. The strategy is as follows: Let H be an ample Q-divisor on Sm;
we note that H is of type B or C. In the type B case, we construct an H-polar cylinder by
generalizing arguments in [1]. In the type C case, we observe that special situations reduce to
the former case. In the remaining case, we construct an H-polar cylinder by using P1-fibration
associated with H of type C.
