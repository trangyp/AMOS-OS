Abstract
Each self-similar dendrite K with a finite self-similar boundary defines a finite acyclic edge-
labeled bipartite graph Γ, called the sprout of K. The paper shows that the sprout Γ determines
the combinatorial properties of the dendrite K and its topological structure.

Introduction
Let S = {S1, ..., Sm} be a system of contractive injections of a complete metric space X.
A
compact non-empty set K is called the attractor of a system S if it satisfies the equation K =
S1(K) ∪... ∪Sm(K). The existence and uniqueness of the attractor K was proved by J.Hutchinson
[16]. The subsets Ki1...in = Si1...Sin(K) are called copies of K. The set of those points x ∈K, whose
images are the intersection points of different copies of K, is the self-similar boundary of K. If the
attractor K is connected and does not contain a closed curve, it is called a self-similar dendrite. The
main object of this paper is self-similar dendrites with a finite boundary. These dendrites possess
the single intersection property (SIP) and therefore have a clear combinatorial characterization.
The study of dendrites dates back to papers [21, 23, 24, 31] that appeared in the early 1920-ies
and defined key objectives in dendrite topology. An exhaustive overview of the research in this area
can be found in [8]. Since the inception of the theory of self-similar sets [16], self-similar dendrites
have been considered by many authors in their papers on the subject. In his fundamental work [13]
in 1985, M. Hata proved that the set of endpoints of a non-trivial self-similar dendrite is infinite.
In 1995, J. Kigami [18, 19] investigated the shortest-path metric in post-critically finite self-similar
dendrites and constructed regular Dirichlet forms for such dendrites. Under certain conditions, Julia
sets of complex polynomials may be dendrites. As it follows from results of A.Kameyama [17], if a
self-similar dendrite is the attractor of some invertible system of contractions, it is homeomorphic to
a Julia set of some polynomial. The article [4] by C. Bandt and K. Keller deserves special attention,
as it contains an intersection graph criterion for a finitely branched self-similar continuum to be a
dendrite (Proposition 9). The preprint [5] by C. Bandt and J. Stanke introduces the idea of a ’main
tree’ for post-critically finite self-similar dendrites. The topology automaton approach to the study of
self-similar dendrites was considered in [3, 26, 27]. Self-similar dendrites with non-trivial intersection

A simple and intuitive way to construct self-similar dendrites in the plane and in space is provided

In this paper, we develop a method for defining self-similar dendrites with single point inter-

topological elements of a self-similar dendrite from its sprout.
The main theorems proved in the paper are the following.
First, the isomorphism theorem for self-similar dendrites (Theorem 4.5). It states that if the
systems S = {S1, ..., Sm} and ˜S = { ˜S1, ..., ˜Sm} have isomorphic P-sprouts Γ and ˜Γ, then their
attractors K and ˜K are isomorphic.
The second is Theorem 5.5 that states the conditions under which the set of addresses of a
boundary point p ∈∂K is finite, countable, or uncountable and gives a formula for finding Ord(p, K).
The third is Theorem 6.7 on the order of boundary points with respect to the main tree.
In Section 3 we introduce the P-sprout Γ that defines a self-similar dendrite K with a finite
self-similar boundary and define the index diagram of Γ.
In Section 4 we consider SIP systems of continua and refinement sequences of SIP systems. This

In Section 5 we consider the main tree of a self-similar dendrite, evaluate the orders of the points

In section 6 we consider a semigroup Gϕ of transformations of the set P, and prove the Theorem

If the system S has the finite intersection property, we define its intersection graph Γ(S) [30] as
an edge-labeled bipartite graph (W, B; E) with parts W and B, in which an edge e = (Ki, p) ∈E if

edge from xi to xj iff there is k ∈I such that Sk(xj) = xi. In this case, the edge label is wk.

If K is a dendrite, then the order Ord(x, K) of a point x ∈K is equal to the number of connected
components of K \ {x}. [20, Ch.6,Th. 6].
The points of order 1 are called the end points of K, the points of order ≥2 are the cut points of
K, the points of order ≥3 are the ramification points of K.
Definition 5.1. [29] Let the attractor K of the system S = {S1, ..., Sm} be a self-similar dendrite
with a finite self-similar boundary ∂K. The minimal subdendrite ˆγ⊂K that contains ∂K is called
the main tree of K.
Since ∂K = {p1, ..., pn} is finite and for any two points x, y ∈K there is a unique subarc
γ(x, y)⊂K, the main tree ˆγ is a finite union of all subarcs γ(pi, pj)⊂K. Thus, ˆγ is a finite topological
tree. As was proved in [29, Theorem 15], the arcs γ(pi, pj) are the components of the attractor of
some multizipper, which is a graph-directed system that has the SIP. The arcs γ(pi, pj) are called
the main subarcs of the dendrite K.

Definition 5.3. [6] Let G be a finite digraph and p, p1 be vertices in G.
