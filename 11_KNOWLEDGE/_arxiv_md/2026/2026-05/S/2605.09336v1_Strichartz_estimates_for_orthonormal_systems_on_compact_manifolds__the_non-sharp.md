STRICHARTZ ESTIMATES FOR ORTHONORMAL SYSTEMS ON
COMPACT MANIFOLDS: THE NON-SHARP REGION
HONGZHOU JI, LIPING XU AND AN ZHANG
Abstract. We establish new Strichartz estimates for orthonormal systems on com-
pact Riemannian manifolds in the non-sharp admissible region of exponents, covering
wave, Klein-Gordon, and fractional Schr¨odinger equations. Our approach combines
the result of Wang-Zhang-Zhang [WZZ25] on the sharp admissible line with a Lieb-
Sobolev inequality derived from a recent Cwikel estimate due to Sukochev-Yang-
Zanin [SYZ25], along with an alternative globalization method based on localized
weak Lorentz estimates.
Our results extend the Euclidean results of Bez-Hong-
Lee-Nakamura-Sawano [BHL+19] and Bez-Lee-Nakamura [BLN21], as well as the
classical single-function estimates on manifolds due to Kapitanski [Kap89], Burq-
G´erard-Tzvetkov [BGT04], and Dinh [Din17].

1.1. Some known results. The Euclidean Strichartz estimates are relatively well-
developed. The classical estimate for single functions was first established by Strichartz
[Str77] for the wave equation, and by Ginibre–Velo [GV92] for the Schr¨odinger equa-
tion, in the non-endpoint cases. Keel–Tao [KT98] established a general abstract frame-
work for Strichartz estimates and obtained the full region of admissible exponent pairs,
including the previously inaccessible endpoint cases. Guo–Peng [GP07] obtained an
endpoint Strichartz estimate for the kinetic transport equation.
Guo–Peng–Wang
[GPW08] studied the decay of a class of wave equations in a unified way. See also
[KPV91, COX11, GW14] for fractional or radial cases and applications.
The study of functional inequalities for orthonormal systems dates back to Lieb–
Thirring [LT75], and these inequalities play a crucial role in quantum mechanics. Re-
cently, in the Schr¨odinger setting, Frank–Lewin–Lieb–Seiringer [FLLS14] extended the
classical single-function Strichartz estimates to orthogonal families in L2(Rd). Frank–
Sabin [FS17] later extended these estimates to the full sharp admissible line via a du-
ality principle and complex interpolation for the Schr¨odinger, wave, and Klein–Gordon
equations. Bez–Hong–Lee–Nakamura–Sawano [BHL+19] considered initial data with
Sobolev regularity for non-sharp admissible exponents for the Schr¨odinger propagator.
They developed approaches based on Lorentz norms and localized estimates. Later,
Bez–Lee–Nakamura [BLN21] introduced weighted oscillatory integral estimates and ex-
tended the scope of orthonormal Strichartz estimates to the full admissible region and
to the wave, Klein–Gordon, and fractional Schr¨odinger equations in Euclidean spaces.
Feng–Mondal–Song–Wu [FMSW26] investigated a wide class of dispersive semigroups.
On compact Riemannian manifolds, single-function Strichartz estimates were ob-
tained by Kapitanski [Kap89] for the wave equation, by Burq–G´erard–Tzvetkov [BGT04]
for the Schr¨odinger equation, by Dinh [Din17] for the fractional Schr¨odinger equation,
and by Cacciafesta–Danesi–Meng [CDM24] for the wave, Klein–Gordon and Dirac
equations. See also the interesting works [KPV91, Bou93, GOW14, BD15, DGG17,
DGGRM22] for the periodic setting.

In contrast with the classical single-function case, less is known for orthonormal
systems on compact manifolds. Spectral cluster estimates for orthonormal families
were obtained by Frank–Sabin [FS17], with further refinements on manifolds of non-
positive curvature by Ren–Zhang [RZ24]. For the flat torus, Nakamura [Nak20] derived
Strichartz estimates for orthonormal systems using frequency-global dispersive esti-

Littlewood–Paley theory. Jian–Wang–Xi [JWX25] extended the restriction bounds of
Burq–G´erard–Tzvetkov [BGT07] to arbitrary systems of spectral clusters.
