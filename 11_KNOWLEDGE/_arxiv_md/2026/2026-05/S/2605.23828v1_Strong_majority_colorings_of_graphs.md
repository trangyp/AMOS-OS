Strong majority colorings of graphs
Rafał Kalinowski, Mateusz Kamyczura,
Monika Pilśniak, Mariusz Woźniak
AGH University of Krakow, Poland
Abstract
Motivated by majority vertex-colorings of graphs and digraphs and
majority edge-colorings of graphs, we introduce two concepts of strong
majority colorings. A strong majority vertex-coloring of a graph G =
(V, E) is a mapping c : V →C such that for every vertex v ∈V and
every color α ∈C, at most half of the neighbors of v have color α. The
strong majority number of G, denoted Maj(G), is the least number of
colors in such a coloring. We show that Maj(G) can be arbitrarily large
and prove a tight upper bound Maj(G) ≤2∆(G)+1 for every graph G
without pendant vertices. A strong majority edge-coloring of a graph G
is a mapping c : E →C such that for every edge e ∈E and every color
α ∈C, at most half of the edges adjacent to e have color α. The strong
majority index of G, denoted Maj’(G), is the least number of colors in
such a coloring. It is shown that there is an upper constant bound for
Maj’(G) of all admissible graphs G. We conjecture that this constant
is as small as 4 and confirm this conjecture for numerous graph classes.

Motivation
We introduce two graph invariants related to sizes of color classes in the
neighborhood of a vertex or an edge. To make this paper more readable, we
also give names and notation to analogous invariants previously considered
in the literature, which were an inspiration to our research.
Let G be a simple undirected graph. We call a vertex-coloring of G ma-
jority if for every vertex v at most half its neighbors share the color of v. By
maj(G) we denote the minimum number of colors among all majority vertex-
colorings of the graph G. These colorings were first considered in 1966 by
Lovász [10], who proved the following classical result.

Theorem 1 (Lovász [10]). For every finite graph G,
maj(G) = 2.
Majority vertex-colorings were later investigated for infinite graphs. In
this case, for a subset set S of an infinite set Ω, the condition that the
cardinality of S is at most half the elements of Ωmeans that the comple-
ment of S is of the same cardinality as Ω. Shelah and Milner [12] showed
that maj(G) ≤3 for every graph of arbitrary cardinality, and there exist
uncountable graphs with maj(G) = 3. The renowned Unfriendly Partition
Conjecture states that maj(G) = 2 for every countable graph G. This conjec-
ture is often viewed as one of the most intriguing and difficult open problems
in infinite graph theory. It has been confirmed only for some classes of graphs,
in particular for
– graphs with finitely many vertices of infinite degree (Aharoni, Milner, and
Prikry [1]),
– graphs with finitely many vertices of finite degree (Aharoni, Milner, and
Prikry [1]),
– rayless graphs (Bruhn, Diestel, Georgakopoulos, and Sprüssel [5]),
– graphs without a subdivision of an infinite clique (Berger [3]),
– line graphs (Kalinowski, Pilśniak, and Stawiski [8]).
In 2017, Kreutzer, Oum, Seymour, van der Zyphen, and Woods [9] in-
troduced the following analogous notion for digraphs. A vertex-coloring of
a digraph D is majority if for every vertex v at most half its out-neighbors
share the color of v. Let us denote the minimum number of colors in such
a coloring by −−→
maj(D). In [9] it was shown that −−→
maj(D) ≤4, and it was
conjectured that −−→
maj(D) ≤3 for every finite digraph D. This conjecture is
still open but confirmed in some cases (cf. [2] and references therein).
In 2023, Bock, Kalinowski, Pardey, Pilśniak, and Woźniak [4] defined
majority edge-colorings of graphs. These are edge-colorings such that for
every vertex v and every color α, at most half of the edges incident with v
have the color α. Clearly, a graph having a vertex of degree 1 does not admit
such a coloring. Let us denote the minimum number of colors in a majority
edge-coloring of a graph G by maj′ (G).
The following upper bound for maj′ (G) was established in [4].
Theorem 2 ([4]). Every finite graph G with minimum degree δ(G) ≥2
satisfies
maj′ (G) ≤4.

Consequently, due to Theorem 1 and Theorem 3, two colors suffice for such
an edge-coloring of arbitrary cardinality.

Strong majority vertex-colorings
Throughout, graph colorings need not be proper, and graphs are finite and
undirected, unless otherwise stated. Let c be a vertex-coloring of a graph
G. If for every vertex v and every color α, at most half at most half of the
neighbors of v have color α, then c is a strong majority vertex-coloring of
G. The minimum number of colors in such a coloring is the strong majority
number of G, denoted by Maj(G). Clearly, Maj(G) is defined only for graphs
G without pendant vertices, that is, for graphs G with δ(G) ≥2.
Let us start with two easy examples.
Observation 5. For a cycle Cn, we have Maj(Cn) = 2 if n ≡0 mod 4,
and Maj(Cn) = 3 otherwise.
Proof. In a strong majority vertex-coloring of Cn, two vertices of distance
2 necessarily have two distinct colors. Color subsequent vertices with col-
ors 1, 1, 2, 2, . . .. This yields a strong majority vertex-coloring when n ≡0
mod 4. Otherwise, a third color is needed. If n ≡1 mod 4, then we put 3 at
the end of the above sequence of colors. If n ≡2 mod 4, we end with 3, 3.
For n ≡3 mod 4, we put 1, 3, 3 on the last three vertices.

Strong majority edge-colorings
In this section, we introduce an analog of the strong majority vertex-colorings
for edge-colorings.
Given an edge-coloring c of a graph G = (V, E), we say that an edge
e ∈E is majority colored if there is no color α such that e is adjacent to
more edges of color α than to the other edges. The coloring c is called a
strong majority edge-coloring if every edge of G is majority colored. Clearly,
such a coloring exists only if G does not contain a pendant path of length
more than one, that is, there does not exist a vertex of degree one with a
neighbor of degree two. We call such graphs admissible. On the other hand,
if a graph G is admissible, then assigning every edge a distinct color yields a
strong majority coloring. Hence, from now on in this section we exclusively
consider admissible graphs. The least number of colors in a strong majority
edge-coloring of an admissible graph G is called the strong majority index of
G, denoted by Maj′(G).
Observe that Maj’(G) can be equal to 2 only if the degrees of all vertices of
G are even. Let us start with two simple examples. The first one immediately
follows from Observation 5 and the fact that L(Cn) = Cn.

Conjecture 14. If G is an admissible graph, then

The bound 4 would be best possible because there exist graphs G with
Maj’(G) = 4. Let bS be a subdivision of a snark S which is a cubic graph of
Class 2, that is, with the chromatic index equal to 4. Suppose Maj’( bS) ≤3.
Every edge of bS has three adjacent edges, so they have three distinct colors
in a strong majority edge-coloring. For the same reason, the three edges
incident to a vertex of degree 3 must have three distinct colors. Let x be any
vertex of degree 2 in bS adjacent to vertices v1, v2 of degree 3. Without loss

definition of a snark.
