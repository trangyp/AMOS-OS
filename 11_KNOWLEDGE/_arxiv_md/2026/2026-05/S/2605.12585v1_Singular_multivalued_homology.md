Abstract. Let X be a compact, Hausdorff topological space. Then HM

0 for all n > 0, where HM is the multivalued analogue of singular ho-

1. Introduction
Lefschetz fixed-point theorem is one of the main results in fixed-point
theory. The study of fixed points of continuous multivalued maps between
topological spaces has been an important subject in mathematics since long
ago [2, 5, 7]. Since homology plays an important role in classical fixed-
point theory of continuous maps (for example through Lefschetz or Brouwer
fixed-point theorems), we can ask whether a “multivalued singular homol-
ogy” (that is, the singular n-simplices would be continuous multivalued
maps σ : ∆n →X, where ∆n ⊂Rn+1 is the standard n-simplex) may be
useful to study fixed-points of multivalued maps.
In algebraic geometry, it is usual to find the Lefschetz trace theorem in the
context of correspondences (multivalued maps) [4, 12]. Also in algebraic
geometry, there exists a very important singular homology where the singu-
lar simplices are correspondences between the (algebraic) standard simplex
and X, called Suslin homology [11].
Hence, back in algebraic topology, we can ask if it is possible to construct
a singular homology of topological spaces with multivalued simplices and,
in an affirmative case, what its meaning is. More precisely, let f : X →Y be
a map between two topological spaces. Let gr(f) = {(x, y) ∈X×Y|f(x) = y}
be its graph and τ|gr(f) : gr( f) →X the restriction of the canonic projection
X × Y →X. The following are equivalent:
(1) f is continuous.
(2) the map τ|gr(f) is proper [1, Chapitre I, S. 10].
Thus, we can use this characterization to define a continuous multivalued
map between topological spaces X and Y as a subset T ⊂X × Y, such that
the restriction τ|T : T →X of the canonical projection X ×Y →X is proper,

Definition 2.3. A continuous multivalued map X →Y is a subset T of X×Y
such that the restriction τ|T : T →X of the canonical projection X × Y →X
is proper, surjective and with finite fibers (equivalently τ|T closed, surjective
and with finite fibers, since every finite fiber is compact).

denote the set of continuous multivalued maps from X to Y by M(X, Y). We

5. A final remark in fixed-point theory
Even if multivalued homology does not allow to detect fixed points with
a kind of Lefschetz theorem, we can adapt the proof of [9, Theorem 4] to
detect fixed sets.
Definition 5.1. Let T : X →X be a continuous multivalued map. We say
that a subset A ⊂X is a fixed subset if A = T(A) (T(A) is the projection on
the second factor X of T ∩(A × X)).
We start with an analogue to [9, Lemma 4]:
Lemma 5.2. Let T : X →Y be a continuous multivalued map, with X a
compact space and Y a compact and Hausdorff space. Then, T(A) is closed
for every closed subspace A ⊂X.
Proof. Since τ|T : T →X is proper and X is compact, then T = (τ|T)−1(X)
is compact [1, Chapitre I, S. 10, n. 3, Prop. 6]. Now, if A ⊂X is a closed
subspace, it is compact and hence T ∩(A × Y) is also compact. Then, the
projection of this term on Y under the second projection is also compact and
so it must be closed since Y is Hausdorff.
□
We also have an analogue to [9, Lemma 3]:
Lemma 5.3. Let T : X →Y be a continuous multivalued map with X
a compact Hausdorff space and Y a Hausdorff space. Let {xn}n∈N+ be a
sequence in X that converges to a point x0 and suppose that there is a se-
quence yn ∈T(xn) that converges to a point y0 ∈Y. Then y0 ∈T(x0).
Proof. Since T is closed in X×Y (it is a compact space of a Hausdorff space)
and {(xn, yn)}n∈N+ ⊂T, we conclude that lim(xn, yn) = (x0, y0) ∈T.
□
Now the proof of the next theorem is analogous to the one in [9, Theorem
4].
Theorem 5.4. Every compact Hausdorff space has the fixed set property
for a continuous multivalued map, i.e., there is always a fixed subset.
