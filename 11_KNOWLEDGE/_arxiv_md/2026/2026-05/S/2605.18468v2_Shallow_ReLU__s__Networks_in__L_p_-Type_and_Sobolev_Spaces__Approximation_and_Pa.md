1 Introduction
Deep learning has shown remarkable effectiveness in high-dimensional approximation prob-
lems, particularly in scientific computing, inverse problems, and operator learning (Han
et al., 2018; Adcock et al., 2022; Beck et al., 2023). In many such settings, the ReLUs
activation σs(t) = max{0, t}s (s ∈N0) is especially relevant because it yields piecewise-
polynomial representations that are well suited to smooth targets and derivative-sensitive
tasks (Yang and Zhou, 2025; He et al., 2024).

To obtain meaningful approximation guarantees, one must first identify an appropriate
function class. Reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHSs) are a standard choice, but they
cannot effectively approximate even a single ReLU neuron (Yehudai and Shamir, 2019).
More fundamentally, Steinwart (2024) proved that on an uncountable compact space, no
RKHS contains all continuous functions. This result highlights an intrinsic limitation of
RKHSs: they are naturally adapted to smooth functions and therefore have limited expres-
sive power for nonsmooth targets. Sobolev spaces W α,p, by contrast, accommodate such
nonsmooth functions and are central in approximation theory and the analysis of partial
differential equations (Triebel, 1983). However, Sobolev spaces are also too large from a
statistical perspective: any machine learning method is statistically ineffective over the full
Sobolev class (Wainwright, 2019). This raises a basic theoretical question: which function
classes can neural networks learn efficiently, and how does that answer depend on the acti-
vation function and the regularity of the target space? E et al. (2022) reformulated Barron
spaces (Barron, 1993) and showed that, for shallow ReLU networks, the Barron space is
exactly the largest function class that can be learned statistically efficiently.1
The connection between RKHSs and Barron spaces can be made precise through the
Lp-type spaces studied by Celentano et al. (2021) and Chen et al. (2025). These works

• Optimality of approximation rates in Sobolev spaces. The rates in Theorem 5
for 1 ≤p < 2 are not yet optimal.
The main obstruction is that the currently

available approximation rate over the corresponding spectral Barron space is itself
not sharp. It would be interesting to develop more refined harmonic-analysis tools,
such as Littlewood–Paley decompositions, to analyze approximation in W α,p directly
and possibly obtain optimal rates.
• Approximation under general parameter measures. The approximation results
for Lp-type spaces in Theorem 2 rely on taking the parameter measure ν to be the
uniform distribution τd, which makes the Funk–Hecke formula available. For general
probability measures ν, the present proof no longer applies. Extending the theory to
broader classes of parameter measures remains an open problem.
• Approximation lower bounds. The present work establishes only upper bounds
for approximation over Lp-type spaces. Matching lower bounds, and hence minimax-
optimal approximation results, remain open. Deriving such lower bounds would com-
plete the picture behind Theorem 2.
The techniques developed here may also be useful for studying deeper architectures,
where the interaction between representation norms, approximation, and statistical com-
plexity remains less understood.
