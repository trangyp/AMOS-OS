Sequential Spatiotemporal Magnetic-Field Reconstruction via Quantum Hamiltonian
Learning with NV-Center Spin-1 Hamiltonians
Hiroshi Yamauchi,1, ∗Sophie Colleen Stearn,2, † and Samuel Tovey2, ‡
1SoftBank Corp.
2Quantum Brilliance
We propose a quantum-Hamiltonian-learning-based sequential reconstruction framework for dy-
namic two-dimensional magnetic fields using a local likelihood model derived from a nitrogen-
vacancy center spin-1 Hamiltonian. The latent field is not observed directly; instead, local mea-
surements are generated through nitrogen-vacancy spin dynamics governed by local magnetic-field
values and a shared dipolar coupling parameter. Sequential Bayesian updates over overlapping scan
windows are combined with temporal posterior propagation to reconstruct the evolving field.
Numerical experiments on synthetic maze-like magnetic-field sequences show that the proposed
method recovers the dominant spatial structure, achieving a root-mean-square error of 7.037×10−7 T
at the final frame.
Adaptive diagnostics show decreasing expected information gain and stable
local convergence. Fisher-information and leakage diagnostics reveal a sensitivity–leakage tradeoff
under long-interrogation controls, and the implemented adaptive policy incorporates leakage-aware
control scoring while leaving the likelihood itself unchanged. Combined horizontal and vertical scans
improve reconstruction relative to single-direction acquisition. The shared coupling parameter J is
only partially identifiable: its posterior becomes narrow but remains frame-dependent and biased.
At the final checkpoint, the coupling posterior reaches Jstd = 87.0 Hz, close to a finite-time
product-state reference benchmark of 73.3 Hz, while remaining 3.35× above a gain-extrapolated
ideal-state benchmark. The posterior mean, however, remains biased by 326.9 Hz, indicating that
posterior concentration alone does not imply unbiased coupling identifiability.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Reconstructing spatially distributed magnetic fields
from incomplete and noisy observations is a central prob-
lem in sensing, inverse modeling, and data-driven scien-
tific inference [1–3]. In many practical situations, how-
ever, the target field cannot be measured directly over
the entire domain at once. Instead, observations are ac-
quired locally, sequentially, and under limited measure-
ment resources, a setting naturally related to sequen-
tial Bayesian filtering and particle-based state estimation
[4–6]. Moreover, the field of interest often evolves over
time, so that the estimation problem is inherently spa-
tiotemporal rather than purely spatial. In such settings,
the reconstruction algorithm must recover local structure
from partial observations while propagating information
across successive measurements and maintaining consis-
tency over multiple time frames.
This motivates the
problem of sequential spatiotemporal field reconstruc-
tion, in which local observations are assimilated progres-
sively to recover a global magnetic field that changes over
time.
A particularly natural physical platform for such lo-
cal field-sensitive measurements is provided by nitrogen-
vacancy (NV) centers in diamond [7–9]. Hereafter, we
use NV to denote nitrogen-vacancy centers. NV centers
support coherent spin dynamics that depend sensitively
on the local magnetic environment and therefore provide

does not claim hardware-level deployment, this setting
provides a concrete motivation for studying reconstruc-

measurement models with sequential global inference.
Classical approaches to dynamic field estimation are
typically formulated in terms of direct observation mod-
els, state-space systems, filtering methods, or variational
inverse problems [1–3, 6]. These methods have achieved
broad success in applications ranging from environmen-
tal monitoring and tomography to sequential image re-
construction and sensor fusion. Nevertheless, they usu-
ally assume that the measurement process can be mod-
eled directly in terms of the target variable, or at least
through a comparatively simple observation operator. In
contrast, there are settings in which the relation between
the latent field and the observed data is mediated by
structured physical dynamics, producing nonlinear and
parameter-dependent measurement statistics.
In such
cases, reconstruction is more naturally viewed as a se-
quential parameter-inference problem governed by an un-
derlying dynamical model.
Quantum Hamiltonian Learning (QHL) provides a
principled likelihood-based perspective for this type of
inference [13–15]. In QHL, unknown parameters are es-
timated by comparing observed measurement statistics
with the likelihoods induced by candidate Hamiltonian
models. Rather than treating measurement outcomes as

This viewpoint is partic-

affects system dynamics in a structured way. By embed-
ding unknown quantities into a Hamiltonian and evaluat-

values, one obtains a Bayesian inference pipeline natu-

While QHL has primarily been studied in the con-
text of quantum system identification, calibration, and
parameter estimation [13–15, 17], its broader use as a
modeling principle for spatially distributed inference re-
mains relatively underexplored.
Extending the setting
from a small number of Hamiltonian parameters to a
time-varying spatial field introduces three main chal-
lenges: the unknown quantity becomes high-dimensional
and distributed over space, observations are available
only through local windows, and information must be
propagated over time rather than discarded after each
step. These considerations call for a framework that com-
bines Hamiltonian-based local likelihood modeling with
sequential Bayesian field estimation.
In this work, we develop such a framework and for-
mulate spatiotemporal magnetic-field reconstruction as
a sequential inference problem based on an NV-center
Hamiltonian observation model. The target is a field de-
fined over a spatial grid and indexed by discrete time
frames.
At each step, the algorithm accesses the field
only through local observation windows. Each local win-
dow is associated with a parameterized quantum mea-
surement model in which local magnetic-field values de-
termine the underlying NV Hamiltonian and thereby
modulate the resulting measurement statistics. For each
candidate field hypothesis, the model provides a likeli-
hood through Hamiltonian-induced dynamics and mea-
surement probabilities. These local likelihoods are then
used in a sequential update procedure that progressively
refines the estimate of the field over space and time.
The central idea is to bridge local inference and global
reconstruction. A local window contains only partial in-
formation about the full field, but sequentially accumu-
lated local observations can reveal larger spatial struc-
ture when they are integrated consistently. Accordingly,
the proposed method does not attempt to infer the en-
tire field from a single global measurement model. In-
stead, it decomposes reconstruction into repeated local
likelihood evaluations and update steps, followed by ag-
gregation into a global field map. Repeating this process
across multiple frames also captures temporal evolution,
allowing the reconstructed field to improve progressively
as more information becomes available.
A key feature of the present formulation is that the
field is inferred through measurement statistics induced
by a physically motivated full spin-1 NV Hamiltonian
rather than by direct pixelwise observation. Reconstruc-
tion therefore becomes a problem of identifying latent
field values from the statistical signature of NV spin dy-
namics.
Because the method is sequential, it also be-
comes possible to analyze not only the final reconstructed
field but also the intermediate stages through which the
estimate evolves. This is useful for understanding how
local evidence is incorporated, how spatial structures
emerge over repeated updates, and how temporal propa-
gation influences subsequent reconstruction.
An additional motivation for adopting a QHL-based
formulation, rather than a direct local magnetic-field
readout model, is that the inferred object is not lim-
ited to a reconstructed field map alone. Because infer-
ence is performed through an explicit Hamiltonian model,
the framework can in principle retain information about
latent dynamical structure, including shared interaction
parameters such as the global coupling J. In the present
study, this appears as joint inference of the spatial field
and a shared NV–NV dipolar coupling parameter within
a single Bayesian reconstruction pipeline. More broadly,
such a model-based representation may support down-
stream computation, control, or dynamical processing
beyond reconstruction itself, although such uses are not
investigated in the present work.
The goal of this paper is twofold. First, we establish
a methodological connection between QHL-based likeli-
hood modeling and sequential spatiotemporal magnetic-
field reconstruction under an NV spin-1 Hamiltonian.
Second, we demonstrate through numerical proof-of-
concept experiments that this connection is operationally
meaningful, namely that local Hamiltonian-based obser-
vation models can be integrated into a sequential re-
construction pipeline that progressively recovers dynamic
spatial structure.
The main contributions of this paper are summarized
as follows:
• We formulate sequential spatiotemporal magnetic-
field reconstruction as a Bayesian inference prob-
lem based on local QHL observation models derived
from a full spin-1 NV Hamiltonian.
• We develop a window-based reconstruction frame-
work that combines Hamiltonian-induced likeli-
hood evaluation,
adaptive sequential updating,
global field aggregation, and temporal posterior
propagation.
• We investigate the proposed framework on syn-
thetic dynamic magnetic-field data and evaluate
reconstruction accuracy under a physically moti-
vated rotating-frame NV sensing model that retains
the full spin-1 Hilbert space, transverse strain, and
dipolar interactions, with the dominant zero-field

quency.
• We show that the proposed framework recov-
ers dominant spatial structure stably across time,
while also clarifying that global coupling estimation
remains more challenging than field reconstruction
in the present full spin-1 setting.

RELATED WORK
QHL provides a Bayesian framework for inferring un-
known physical parameters from measurement statistics
generated by Hamiltonian dynamics [13–15].
A repre-
sentative formulation was established by Wiebe et al.,
who showed how Hamiltonian-induced likelihoods can
be incorporated into sequential Bayesian inference even
under imperfect quantum resources [13]. This perspec-
tive is directly relevant to the present work, since our
method likewise infers latent quantities through mea-
surement probabilities generated by a structured Hamil-
tonian model rather than through direct observation.
Closely related Bayesian methodologies have also been
developed in quantum tomography and adaptive esti-
mation. Granade et al. studied practical Bayesian to-
mography from an implementation-oriented perspective
[16], while Husz´ar and Houlsby investigated adaptive
Bayesian quantum tomography [22]. Related Bayesian
and adaptive quantum-estimation ideas also appear in
broader quantum parameter-estimation and tomography
settings [21, 23]. These works are relevant because they
emphasize online posterior updating and adaptive mea-
surement selection, both of which are central to our
reconstruction framework.
The present study differs,
however, in that the unknown quantity is not a low-
dimensional static parameter or a single quantum state,
but a time-varying spatial magnetic field reconstructed
from many local windows. In the terminology of QHL,
the exact-diagonalization backend used in the present
work plays the role of a trusted simulator. Candidate
Hamiltonian parameters are evaluated by a controlled
numerical model, and the resulting Hamiltonian-induced
likelihoods are used to update the Bayesian posterior.
This is distinct from a hardware-in-the-loop implemen-
tation, but it provides a practical route to validating the
reconstruction architecture before experimental deploy-
ment. Thus, the present numerical study should be inter-
preted as a trusted-simulator QHL implementation: can-
didate Hamiltonians are evaluated by a classical exact-
diagonalization model rather than by repeated calls to
a physical quantum device. This choice is appropriate
for validating the reconstruction architecture and for iso-
lating identifiability effects before hardware-level deploy-
ment.
A central methodological principle in Bayesian quan-
tum estimation is that measurement settings should be
chosen according to the current uncertainty state of the
estimator rather than fixed in advance. This principle is
reflected in QHL-based adaptive inference, where candi-
date controls are evaluated according to their expected
utility under the current posterior [13, 14, 16, 22].
In
the present work, the adaptive control mechanism follows
the same information-theoretic philosophy. The measure-
ment schedule is not treated as a static scan pattern
alone; instead, local controls are selected so as to im-
prove uncertainty reduction within each reconstruction
window. Relative to prior adaptive quantum estimation
settings, the main extension here is that adaptive design
is embedded in a field-reconstruction pipeline rather than
a single-system estimation problem.
The physical interpretation of the observation model
used here is closely connected to the broader literature on
quantum sensing. Degen et al. reviewed quantum sens-
ing based on coherent quantum dynamics and showed
how external fields can be inferred through their effect
on measurement statistics [24].
The metrological lim-
its and resource scaling of such sensing protocols are
closely connected to the general theory of quantum pa-
rameter estimation and quantum metrology [18–20, 25].
This likelihood-based sensing viewpoint motivates our
use of Hamiltonian-dependent local measurements as the
primitive observation model. Within this broader con-
text, nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond provide
a prominent example of field-sensitive quantum sensors.
Representative early demonstrations include nanoscale
magnetometry with single NV centers and ensembles, as
well as coherent control of NV spin degrees of freedom
[10–12, 26–28].
Reviews by Schirhagl et al.
[29], Do-
herty et al. [7], Rondin et al. [8], Barry et al. [9], and
Casola et al.
[30] illustrate how local physical quanti-
ties can be probed through coherent quantum dynamics
at small scales. Related non-destructive coherent read-
out strategies have also been demonstrated in trapped-
ion platforms [31], illustrating that high-fidelity quan-
tum measurement principles extend across diverse phys-
ical implementations. The present study does not report
a hardware experiment, but it does instantiate the lo-
cal likelihood model with a full spin-1 NV Hamiltonian.
In this sense, the present work occupies an intermediate
position between abstract Hamiltonian-learning models
and deployment-oriented NV sensing protocols.
Recent work has also explored learning-based strate-
gies for improving quantum sensing protocols and adap-
tive quantum measurement design [14, 32]. Kawaguchi
et al.
proposed a quantum circuit learning approach

that trains parameterized quantum gates to produce a
monotonic sensing response over a target field range,
thereby enhancing the dynamic range of quantum sensing
in dense qubit systems [32].
From the statistical viewpoint, the present work is also
related to particle-based sequential Bayesian inference
and nonlinear Bayesian filtering [4–6, 33–35]. The essen-
tial idea in our method is to maintain a weighted approx-
imation to the posterior over local field candidates and a
shared global coupling parameter, updating these weights
as new local measurements arrive. This is closely aligned
with the sequential Bayesian philosophy underlying QHL
and practical Bayesian tomography [13, 14, 16], while re-
lying on the particle-filtering representation widely used
in nonlinear and non-Gaussian state estimation [5, 6].
In the present setting, this perspective is extended from
parameter estimation to distributed spatiotemporal re-
construction, where posterior information must be prop-
agated not only across repeated measurements but also
across overlapping windows and successive frames.
Another relevant line of research concerns identifica-
tion of quantum systems under limited observational ac-
cess. Kato and Yamamoto studied structure identifica-
tion and state initialization in spin networks with re-
stricted access [17], providing a relevant reference point
for Hamiltonian-structure inference under limited observ-
ability. Their work is relevant because it addresses a set-
ting in which latent interaction structure must be inferred
indirectly through constrained measurements rather than
through full direct access to the system.
Conceptu-
ally, this is close to the present problem: our estimator
does not observe the full spatiotemporal field directly,
but instead infers hidden structure from local measure-
ment windows governed by a parameterized Hamiltonian
model.
The connection is particularly strong for the
treatment of coupling parameters. In spin-network iden-
tification, the goal is often to recover interaction struc-
ture or coupling constants from partial measurements. In
our formulation, a related role is played by the global cou-
pling parameter J, which must be inferred jointly with
local field variables.
A further related research direction concerns reduc-
tion of measurement cost.
In classical signal process-
ing, compressed sensing showed that structured signals
can be reconstructed from substantially fewer measure-
ments than dense sampling would suggest [36–38].
In
quantum information, related efficiency-oriented ideas
appear in compressed quantum tomography and com-
pressive quantum process tomography [39, 40], as well
as in shadow tomography and classical shadow meth-
ods, which estimate many properties of a quantum sys-
tem from relatively few measurements [41, 42].
These
measurement-efficient approaches differ from the present
framework in methodology. Rather than relying on ran-
domized measurement ensembles or compressed recovery
guarantees, our method emphasizes Hamiltonian-induced
local likelihoods, adaptive control selection, and sequen-
tial Bayesian posterior propagation over space and time.
Nevertheless, these works are relevant as neighboring ap-
proaches because they address the same high-level chal-
lenge: how to recover informative structure from limited
measurements in high-dimensional estimation problems.
Taken together, the present study lies at the inter-
section of QHL, adaptive Bayesian inference, quantum
sensing, particle-based sequential estimation, partial-
access inference, and measurement-efficient reconstruc-
tion.
From QHL, it inherits the use of Hamiltonian-
induced likelihoods for parameter inference [13–15].
From adaptive Bayesian tomography and sequential
Bayesian filtering, it adopts the principle of uncertainty-
aware sequential updating [5, 6, 16, 22].
From quan-
tum sensing and NV-center magnetometry, it takes the
physical interpretation that latent quantities can be in-
ferred through their effect on coherent measurement dy-
namics [7–9, 24, 29, 30]. Relative to these prior direc-
tions, the main contribution of this work is to integrate
these ideas into a sequential reconstruction framework
for dynamic magnetic fields using an explicit full spin-
1 NV Hamiltonian.
In contrast to conventional QHL
settings, which primarily address low-dimensional pa-
rameter estimation, the present method targets a time-
evolving two-dimensional field observed only through lo-
cal windows. The resulting formulation therefore extends
Hamiltonian-based Bayesian inference toward sequen-
tial spatiotemporal magnetic-field reconstruction under
a physically motivated NV sensing model.

The experiments were designed to evaluate whether
the proposed sequential reconstruction framework can
recover a dynamic two-dimensional magnetic field from
local noisy observations generated by a full spin-1 NV
Hamiltonian model.
The evaluation focuses on three
aspects: spatial reconstruction accuracy, sequential im-
provement across frames, and joint inference of the
shared global coupling parameter J.

post-processing interpolation; rather, it emerges through

H.
Summary of results
Overall, the experimental results support five main
conclusions:
• The proposed method successfully reconstructs a
coherent large-scale magnetic-field structure from
local NV-based measurements, even though the
structure is not directly observable.
• The adaptive measurement strategy behaves con-
sistently with Bayesian inference, concentrating in-
formation in early steps and converging thereafter.
• The global coupling parameter J is partially iden-
tifiable: its posterior concentrates but remains bi-
ased, indicating limited sensitivity under the cur-
rent sensing configuration.
• Combining horizontal and vertical scan directions
improves reconstruction accuracy by reducing di-
rectional bias and improving the conditioning of the
inverse problem.
• The final coupling posterior reaches an uncertainty
scale close to the product-state reference bench-
mark: Jstd = 87.0 Hz, compared with SQLJ =
73.3 Hz for the finite-time product-state bench-
mark. However, it remains 3.35× above the gain-
extrapolated ideal-state benchmark, and the poste-
rior mean retains a finite bias.
Beyond these observations, a central insight from these
results is that the proposed framework operates in a
regime of asymmetric identifiability. Here, identifiability
refers to the ability to uniquely constrain model parame-
ters from the measurement likelihood. In the present set-
ting, this ability differs significantly between parameters:
the local magnetic field is directly observable through
phase accumulation, whereas the global coupling param-
eter influences the likelihood only indirectly through in-
teraction terms.
Taken together, these results demonstrate that NV-
Hamiltonian-based
local
likelihoods,
combined
with
adaptive sequential Bayesian inference, provide an effec-
tive framework for spatiotemporal magnetic-field recon-
struction, while also revealing fundamental identifiability
limitations for global interaction parameters under the
current sensing configuration.
VII.
DISCUSSION
The results support the central claim of this study:
a local likelihood model derived from a full spin-1 NV
Hamiltonian can be embedded into a sequential recon-
struction pipeline to recover a coherent spatiotemporal
magnetic field from local noisy observations. The signifi-
cance of this result lies not only in the visual recovery of
a meaningful field pattern, but also in the fact that the
recovered structure is not directly visible in the raw lo-
cal measurement statistics. As shown by the comparison
between the random initial prior and the reconstructed
final field, large-scale spatial organization emerges only
after repeated Bayesian updates rather than through di-
rect inspection of measurements. This confirms that the
present method functions as an inference-based recon-
struction framework rather than as a simple local inter-
polation scheme.
A second important observation concerns the adap-
tive measurement mechanism.
The expected informa-
tion gain decreases rapidly across adaptive steps, and
the empirical measurement probabilities become increas-
ingly concentrated.
The representative k-step traces
show the same tendency at the window level: early mea-
surements are the most informative, while later measure-
ments mainly refine an already concentrated posterior.
This behavior is consistent with the intended operation
of adaptive Bayesian inference. If the update procedure
were not effectively reducing uncertainty, one would not
expect the systematic decay of expected information gain
observed in the experiments. The results therefore sup-
port the interpretation that the adaptive control policy
is functioning as intended and that intermediate diag-
nostics such as EIG traces provide meaningful evidence
of posterior concentration.

Another notable feature of the results is the asym-
metry between magnetic-field reconstruction and global
coupling estimation. This asymmetry suggests that the
current likelihood model may be dominated primarily by
single-site phase responses, which would limit sensitivity
to interaction parameters. The field itself is recovered ro-
bustly: reconstruction error decreases across frames, the
final field exhibits the correct maze-like structure, and
the H+V scan yields the best quantitative and qualita-
tive performance. In contrast, the global dipolar coupling
parameter J is not recovered with the same level of ac-
curacy. Although the posterior over J remains bounded
and narrow within each frame, the posterior mean ex-
hibits frame-dependent deviations from the true value,
including substantial overestimation in some intermedi-
ate frames and a smaller positive bias at the final frame.
This indicates a complex identifiability structure rather
than a simple monotonic convergence toward the true
coupling.
In particular, the present sensing configura-
tion provides sufficient information to constrain the spa-
tial field strongly, but does not yet constrain the shared
interaction parameter equally well. In other words, joint
inference is feasible, but the different latent variables are
not equally identifiable under the current control and
readout setting.
This distinction is physically reasonable. In the present
experiment, the local field directly affects the dominant
phase accumulation in the sensing dynamics, whereas
the effect of the shared dipolar coupling is compara-
tively weaker and may be partially confounded with other
Hamiltonian contributions. As a result, the field pattern
can be reconstructed reliably even when the global inter-
action parameter is only partially identified. This sug-
gests that more coupling-sensitive controls, observables,
or experimental schedules will be needed if accurate re-
covery of J is to become a primary objective.
The comparison between H-only, V-only, and H+V
scanning provides one of the clearest practical lessons
of the study. Both the numerical metrics and the recon-
structed field maps show that combining horizontal and
vertical scans improves reconstruction quality relative to
single-direction acquisition. The H+V setting yields the
lowest RMSE and produces the clearest recovery of the
maze-like pattern, while the single-direction scans ex-
hibit more pronounced directional artifacts. This result
shows that the quality of spatiotemporal reconstruction
depends not only on the Hamiltonian likelihood model
itself, but also on the spatial acquisition geometry. Scan
design should therefore be regarded as part of the infer-
ence problem rather than as a secondary implementation
detail.
From the viewpoint of Quantum Hamiltonian Learn-
ing, the present work shifts the role of Hamiltonian in-
ference from isolated parameter learning [13–15] to dis-
tributed spatiotemporal reconstruction under a physi-
cally motivated NV model [7–9]. Conventional QHL for-
mulations typically focus on estimating a small number of
unknown Hamiltonian parameters associated with a sin-
gle system [13–15]. Here, by contrast, the Hamiltonian-
based likelihood is evaluated repeatedly on many local
windows and incorporated into a larger sequential recon-
struction loop. The latent object is therefore no longer
only a low-dimensional parameter vector, but a time-
evolving spatial field together with a shared coupling pa-
rameter. The contribution of the present study is best
understood as an expansion of the scope of QHL: not
a replacement of conventional Hamiltonian learning, but
a demonstration that its likelihood-based logic can be
embedded into a structured field-reconstruction architec-
ture.
The experiments reported here are numerical evalua-
tions of the proposed framework, and this point should be
interpreted carefully. The present study does not claim
hardware-level validation or quantum computational ad-
vantage.
Instead, it establishes that the inference for-
mulation itself is coherent and operationally meaningful
under a full spin-1 NV Hamiltonian model. This is an im-
portant intermediate step, because once the local sensing
dynamics are upgraded from an abstract qubit model to a
more realistic spin-1 NV model [7, 8], issues of computa-
tional cost, parameter identifiability, and control design
become substantially more pronounced.
Several limitations should be stated clearly.
First,
the experiments are based on synthetic field sequences
generated under a controlled model. This is appropri-
ate for methodological validation, but it does not es-
tablish performance under real sensing conditions such
as calibration drift, localization uncertainty, or model
mismatch, which are central concerns in statistical in-
verse problems and practical sensing [1, 3, 9]. Second,
the latent field considered here has a highly structured
maze-like geometry with relatively smooth temporal evo-
lution.
Although useful for controlled evaluation, this
does not exhaust the range of fields relevant to realis-
tic applications. Third, the present control design yields
a narrow coupling posterior, but the posterior mean re-
mains biased. Thus, the main limitation is not only pos-
terior width, but also coupling identifiability and bias
under the current local field–coupling likelihood geom-
etry. Fourth, repeated likelihood evaluation under the
full spin-1 NV Hamiltonian remains computationally ex-
pensive, which will become a serious constraint in larger-
scale or more realistic settings. This motivates approxi-
mate, compressed, or surrogate reconstruction strategies
related in spirit to measurement-efficient reconstruction
and tomography [36, 37, 39, 42]. Fifth, the shot model
used in the present simulation is an idealized textbook
binomial model in which repeated shots are treated as
independent binary measurements. In practical NV de-
vices, nuclear-assisted single-shot readout or related non-
destructive readout schemes may reduce the overhead as-
sociated with repeated measurement and reinitialization.
Such hardware-level readout effects are not included in
the present numerical model. They would primarily mod-
ify the effective measurement-throughput model rather
than the Bayesian likelihood structure itself.

study. A first priority is to improve coupling identifia-

richer evolution-time schedules, nonzero microwave driv-

interaction terms. A related hardware-motivated direc-

measurements.

handled more robustly. A fourth direction concerns scal-

investigate surrogate likelihoods, hierarchical approxima-

CONCLUSION
This paper presented a sequential spatiotemporal
magnetic-field reconstruction framework based on a
nitrogen-vacancy-center spin-1 Hamiltonian likelihood
model [7–9] and adaptive Bayesian inference [6, 13, 16].
The method combines three key elements: local window-
wise likelihood evaluation, sequential posterior updating
over repeated adaptive measurements, and joint infer-
ence of a shared global dipolar coupling parameter to-
gether with the spatial field. In this way, the framework
extends a QHL-based likelihood formulation from low-
dimensional parameter estimation toward dynamic spa-
tial reconstruction under a physically motivated sensing
model.
The experimental results support four main conclu-
sions.
First, the proposed method recovers the dom-
inant large-scale structure of the latent magnetic field
even though that structure is not directly visible in the
raw local measurement statistics. Second, the adaptive
update mechanism behaves coherently as a sequential in-
ference process: expected information gain decreases over
adaptive steps, and representative windows show stable
k-step convergence behavior. Third, combining horizon-
tal and vertical scan directions improves reconstruction
quality relative to single-direction acquisition, both quan-
titatively and qualitatively. Fourth, the framework sup-
ports bounded and stable inference of the shared global
coupling parameter. At the final checkpoint, the coupling
posterior has Jmean = 5326.9 Hz and Jstd = 87.0 Hz. This
uncertainty is close to a finite-time product-state refer-
ence benchmark SQLJ = 73.3 Hz, but the posterior mean
remains biased relative to Jtrue = 5.0 kHz. Thus, accu-
rate field reconstruction is easier than unbiased coupling
recovery in the present full spin-1 NV setting.
At the same time, the present study remains a method-
ological validation under controlled synthetic conditions.
The experiments are conducted as numerical proof-of-
concept studies and do not claim hardware-level imple-
mentation or deployment readiness. The current results
should therefore be interpreted as evidence that the infer-
ence architecture itself is coherent, stable, and technically
meaningful under an NV Hamiltonian sensing model,
rather than as a demonstration of operational sensing
performance.
Overall, the study shows that Hamiltonian-based local
likelihoods derived from NV spin dynamics can serve as
effective primitives for sequential reconstruction of dy-
namic magnetic fields. From a metrological perspective,
the selected controls exhibit a clear Fisher-information–
leakage tradeoff: long-interrogation controls enhance sen-
sitivity, especially to the shared coupling parameter,
while also increasing leakage out of the effective sens-
ing subspace.
The results should be interpreted as
finite-resource adaptive metrological improvement under
standard-quantum-limit-type shot-noise scaling, not as a
demonstration of Heisenberg scaling [19, 20, 43, 45]. The
gain-extrapolated ideal-state quantum Fisher informa-
tion benchmark indicates possible metrological headroom
for J estimation, but the implemented sequential Monte
Carlo estimator yields a posterior uncertainty close to
the product-state reference scale and 3.35× above the
finite-time gain-extrapolated ideal-state benchmark. Es-
tablishing a transition from standard-quantum-limit scal-
ing to Heisenberg-limit scaling would require systematic
resource studies over shot number, interrogation time,
and spin-chain length M. This provides a concrete basis
for future work on richer control design, stronger coupling
identifiability, more realistic sensing conditions, and scal-
able likelihood-evaluation strategies, including possible
hybrid classical–quantum implementations.
