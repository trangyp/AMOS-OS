Micro E-Mini Nasdaq 100 futures (ticker: MNQ) under realistic execution constraints. Using 947 
regular trading hours (RTH) trading days of five-minute bar data spanning 2021 through 2025, 
we construct and systematically test fourteen distinct signal families. These include opening 
range breakouts at multiple horizons, gap-based continuation and fade strategies, volume 
signature signals, cross-session momentum from the Asia session, liquidity grab reversals, 
volatility-regime-conditioned classifiers, and news-event-driven directional strategies. 
Every signal is evaluated against a consistent institutional standard: a minimum T-statistic of 2.0 
on out-of-sample walk-forward results, a minimum of 30 out-of-sample trades, positive net 
return after a fixed two-point round-trip friction cost, and multi-year stability across all available 
years. No tested signal survives these combined criteria simultaneously. The gross edge available 
to next-bar-open systematic execution is structurally constrained to approximately 0.07 to 1.50 
points across all signal families—a ceiling that is insufficient to clear two-point round-trip 
transaction costs. 
One signal family—gap continuation short—produces a T-statistic of 3.23 and a mean net return 
of 14.52 points but on only 22 trades across three years, falling below the minimum sample 
threshold and therefore failing deployment criteria. Two separately validated signals—the RTH 
Confluence Signal (T = 5.83, mean net +15.77 pts, N = 538) and London Session Signal B (T = 
5.15, mean net +5.77 pts, N = 289)—are presented as positive controls confirming the 
methodology is capable of detecting genuine edge when it exists. 
The primary contribution of this paper is methodological: a reproducible, documented 
falsification framework applied consistently across a broad signal universe on a liquid modern 
futures instrument. This contributes to a systematic null result that is underrepresented in both 
retail and academic trading literature, where survivorship bias tends to publish only strategies 
that appear to work. All results are based on strictly out-of-sample walk-forward validation.

1. Introduction 
The hypothesis that short-term price patterns in equity index futures contain exploitable 
directional information is widely held in retail trading communities and appears frequently in 
early academic literature on intraday momentum (Gao, Han, Li, & Zhou, 2018; Heston, 
Korajczyk, & Sadka, 2010). The practical question—whether such patterns survive realistic 
execution costs on modern, highly liquid instruments—receives considerably less rigorous 
treatment. Most published retail strategy research suffers from survivorship bias: strategies that 
appear to work are shared; the far larger population of strategies that do not work is never 
documented. 
This paper addresses that gap. Rather than searching for a strategy to deploy, we designed a 
systematic falsification program: define a signal family, apply the cleanest possible test with 
strict no-lookahead execution assumptions, and report what the data shows—including, 
especially, when the answer is negative. The research was conducted on Micro E-Mini Nasdaq 
100 futures (MNQ), a retail-accessible instrument on one of the world’s most liquid equity index 
futures markets. MNQ was selected because its liquidity and narrow bid-ask spread minimize 
execution confounds, and because its tick-by-tick data is widely available, making results 
reproducible. 
The central finding of this paper is a documented gross edge ceiling: across fourteen signal 
families and multiple parameter configurations, the maximum achievable gross return from 
next-bar-open systematic execution on five-minute MNQ bars is approximately 0.07 to 1.50 
points per trade. A two-point round-trip transaction cost—which we argue is a conservative 
estimate for realistic prop firm or retail futures execution—eliminates this gross edge entirely in 
every case. This result is not a failure of methodology; it is informative about the competitive 
structure of the market itself. 
The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 describes the data and execution framework. Section 3 
presents the validation standards. Section 4 documents each signal family systematically. Section 
5 presents the two positive control signals. Section 6 discusses the structural interpretation of the 
gross edge ceiling. Section 7 covers limitations and extensions. Section 8 concludes.

2.2 Execution Assumptions 
All signals are computed at bar close. All entries are executed at the open of the next bar. This 
bar-close / next-bar-open structure is the minimum realistic assumption for systematic execution 
and eliminates any possibility of same-bar fill bias. No signal is permitted to use any data from 
the bar in which it fires for the entry price calculation. 
A fixed two-point round-trip friction cost is applied to every MNQ trade. This represents 
approximately $4.00 per micro contract and encompasses bid-ask spread, exchange fees, and 
conservative slippage. For high-volatility environments where the bid-ask spread on MNQ can 
widen to two ticks or more, this assumption may understate actual costs. All net return figures 
reported in this paper are after this friction deduction unless explicitly noted as gross. 
2.3 Walk-Forward Validation Structure 
All signal testing uses expanding-window walk-forward validation. The training window grows 
progressively: train on 2022, test on 2023; train on 2022–2023, test on 2024; train on 2022–2024, 
test on 2025. Parameters are selected only on the training window. The test window is never 
touched until the final evaluation step. This structure prevents the most common form of backtest

overfitting—using future data to select parameters—and produces out-of-sample performance 
estimates that are structurally more reliable than in-sample optimization. 
For signals where no parameter selection is required (pure characterization tests), the full dataset 
is used and year-by-year stability is assessed by splitting results by calendar year. A result is 
considered year-stable only if it shows consistent direction and statistical significance across all 
three primary years (2022, 2023, 2024).

These standards are deliberately conservative. In a liquid, highly competitive market such as 
MNQ, the prior probability of finding genuine exploitable edge in publicly available OHLCV 
data is low. Applying a lower bar would increase false positive rates substantially. The two 
positive control signals documented in Section 5 both clear these criteria comfortably, 
confirming the standards are achievable when genuine edge exists. 
All negative results are archived and treated as permanent findings. A signal that fails these 
criteria is logged as rejected and is not revisited without documented new evidence or a 
materially different hypothesis. This prevents the common research failure mode of repeatedly 
adjusting parameters until a signal appears to pass.

4.7 Event Day Trend (News-Driven Signals) 
Following high-impact USD economic releases (FOMC, CPI, NFP, PCE), the hypothesis is that 
forced institutional repositioning creates sustained directional order flow in the 10–60 minutes 
following the release. A ForexFactory calendar providing 993 qualifying high-impact events 
from 2022–2025 was used to identify event days. 
The post-release directional signal fails for a structural reason: the measured drift (mean net 
positive) was entirely contained within bars 1–5 following the release—the news spike itself. 
When correctly measured from bar +6 onward, all tested horizons produce T-statistics between 
0.14 and 0.69. Additionally, the two largest release types by market impact (NFP at 08:30 ET 
and CPI at 08:30 ET) are entirely excluded under Apex Trader Funding prop firm rules that 
restrict trading to RTH hours. The event day signal produced T = 0.38 on the filtered 
RTH-compatible sample. 
An important secondary finding: comparing signal performance on news days versus non-news 
days for the two validated signals (RTH Confluence and London Signal B) shows no significant 
difference. London Signal B actually performs modestly better on news days (T = 0.38, mean 
difference +0.72 points), while RTH Confluence shows no year-stable directional effect from 
news proximity. This means news calendar filters add no value to the validated signal portfolio.

6.2 Why the Ceiling Exists 
The gross edge ceiling has a straightforward market microstructure interpretation. MNQ is 
among the most actively traded futures contracts in the world. Any OHLCV-observable pattern 
that reliably predicts next-bar direction creates an arbitrage opportunity that institutional 
participants with lower transaction costs and faster execution can exploit. The rational 
equilibrium is that such patterns are traded away until their gross edge approximately equals the 
friction cost of the marginal participant. For retail-accessible instruments, this equilibrium is at 
roughly 1–2 points gross per trade on five-minute bars. 
This is distinct from saying the market is perfectly efficient in a strong-form sense. The two 
positive control signals in Section 5 demonstrate that edge above this ceiling exists—but both 
signals use regime classification and multi-bar hold periods that capture structural market state 
transitions rather than single-bar price patterns. The ceiling appears to apply specifically to 
single-bar directional predictions from price and volume features observable by all market 
participants. 
6.3 Implications for OHLCV-Based Research 
Mesfin (2026)  |  13


## Page 14


The finding has a practical implication for anyone designing intraday systematic strategies on 
liquid equity index futures. Strategies that rely on next-bar-open execution following a bar-close 
signal face a structural handicap: the gross edge available from OHLCV features at this 
resolution is too small to survive realistic friction. Strategies that have appeared to work in retail 
literature may have benefited from the following confounds: (1) use of bid-mid or unrealistic fill 
prices, (2) failure to account for full round-trip costs, (3) optimization on in-sample data that 
does not generalize, or (4) reporting only positive results from a larger set of tests (multiple 
comparisons bias). 
The two categories of strategy that avoid this ceiling, based on the positive controls in Section 5, 
share a common feature: they hold positions for 12–15 bars (60–75 minutes) rather than 1–6 
bars. This longer hold period allows the signal’s regime-level information to generate sufficient 
net points to overcome friction.

7. Limitations and Extensions 
7.1 Limitations 
Mesfin (2026)  |  14


## Page 15


The friction assumption of two points round-trip is fixed throughout this study. Actual friction 
varies with session time, volatility, and order size. In high-volatility regimes, MNQ bid-ask 
spreads can widen significantly, making the two-point assumption conservative and potentially 
understating actual costs. Conversely, institutional participants with direct market access can 
execute at substantially lower costs, and signals that are unprofitable at two-point friction may be 
profitable at sub-half-point friction. 
The dataset covers 2021–2025 and does not include the 2018–2020 period or earlier. Market 
microstructure and participant composition in MNQ have changed over time, particularly with 
the growth of retail options and the proliferation of systematic strategies. Results may not 
generalize to earlier or later periods. 
Tick data was not available for this study. All signals are constructed from five-minute OHLCV 
bars. True order flow classification (bid-initiated versus ask-initiated volume) requires tick-level 
data. It is possible—and the positive controls suggest it is likely—that tick data would reveal 
directional information that five-minute aggregation destroys. 
The study tests one instrument (MNQ) as the primary subject. While cross-instrument 
comparison using MGC is included, the generalizability of the gross edge ceiling finding to other 
futures instruments (crude oil, treasury bonds, currency futures) is an open question. 
7.2 Extensions 
The most direct extension of this work is to apply the same falsification framework to tick-level 
data. The positive control signals suggest that regime-level structure at multi-bar resolution 
contains exploitable information. It is plausible that order flow imbalance at tick resolution 
contains bar-level directional information that OHLCV aggregation destroys. Databento provides 
MNQ tick data at approximately $42 per quarter, making this extension feasible at modest cost. 
The gap continuation short signal (T = 3.23, N = 22) is a specific candidate for further 
investigation. If the underlying mechanism—gap-down days with high Kalman velocity tend to 
continue downward within the RTH session—is structural rather than a small-sample artifact, a 
larger dataset (including pre-2021 data) would provide sufficient trade count to validate or reject 
it definitively. 
The VVG classifier identifies days with distinct behavioral characteristics but fails to produce a 
tradable directional edge at entry resolutions tested. A natural extension is to investigate whether 
the classifier has value as a volatility predictor rather than a directional predictor: if 
classifier-positive days reliably show higher realized variance, a volatility strategy (long straddle 
or equivalent) might be more appropriate than a directional entry. 
8. Conclusion

## Page 16


This paper has documented a systematic falsification study of fourteen OHLCV-based intraday 
momentum signal families on MNQ futures. No signal survived the combined criteria of T ≥ 2.0 
out-of-sample, ≥ 30 trades, positive net return after friction, and multi-year consistency. The 
consistent finding across all signal families is a gross edge ceiling of approximately 0.07 to 1.50 
points at five-minute bar resolution—insufficient to clear a two-point round-trip friction cost. 
This result is not presented as a proof that no intraday edge exists in MNQ. The two positive 
control signals demonstrate that edge above the ceiling is achievable through regime 
classification and multi-bar hold structures. Rather, this result establishes a specific, empirically 
documented claim: single-bar directional signals derived from publicly observable OHLCV 
features do not generate economically significant edge at five-minute resolution on this 
instrument under realistic execution assumptions. 
The contribution of this work is methodological as much as empirical. The falsification 
framework—pre-specified criteria, walk-forward validation, systematic negative result 
documentation, and positive control inclusion—is reproducible and applicable to any intraday 
signal research program. The consistent finding that retail OHLCV patterns fail under these 
standards supports the hypothesis that liquid equity index futures markets efficiently arbitrage 
publicly observable price and volume patterns, and that genuine intraday edge in these markets 
requires either proprietary data, structural regime detection, or execution advantages unavailable 
to retail participants.
