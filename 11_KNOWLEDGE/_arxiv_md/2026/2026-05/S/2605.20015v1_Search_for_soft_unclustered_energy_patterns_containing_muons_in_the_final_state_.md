Search for soft unclustered energy patterns
containing muons in the final state in 𝒑𝒑collisions at
√𝒔= 13 TeV with the ATLAS detector
The ATLAS Collaboration
Soft unclustered energy patterns (SUEPs) refer to high-multiplicity, isotropic distributions of
low-momentum particles that could arise in strongly-coupled hidden sector theories. A search
for SUEPs whose decay products contain muons in the final state is presented using 140 fb−1
of proton–proton collision data at √𝑠= 13 TeV collected by the ATLAS detector at the Large
Hadron Collider during 2015–2018. The analysis targets SUEP signatures via gluon–gluon
fusion production of a scalar mediator in Hidden Valley scenarios. No significant excess over
the Standard Model expectation is observed. Exclusion limits are set on the product of the
mediator production cross section and the branching fraction for its decay into a SUEP, down
to 0.05 fb for a mediator mass of 𝑚𝑆= 750 GeV, 0.4 fb for 𝑚𝑆= 400 GeV, and 70 fb for
𝑚𝑆= 125 GeV; the last value, when identifying the mediator with the Standard Model Higgs
boson, translates to an upper limit on the branching fraction of around 0.2%.

1 Introduction
Hidden Valley (HV) models [1, 2] provide a well-motivated extension of the Standard Model (SM) in
which a hidden sector, neutral under SM gauge interactions, is connected to the SM visible sector through
weak, so-called portal interactions. Such scenarios arise naturally in solutions to the hierarchy problem,
which often introduce additional sectors with suppressed couplings to the SM [3]. Strongly interacting
hidden sectors are further motivated by the possibility that dark matter is composed of bound states of a
QCD-like hidden force [4, 5], or that it resides in a dark sector with sizable self-interactions, as suggested
by small-scale structure anomalies in galaxies and their clusters [6–8].
At the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), a range of searches has targeted these scenarios in regimes where the
hidden sector undergoes a shower analogous to a QCD parton shower, giving rise to jet-like signatures once
the hidden-sector particles decay to visible SM particles. These include searches for so-called semi-visible
jets [9–11] and emerging jets [12, 13], which exploit the resulting exotic jet topologies to distinguish such
events from SM backgrounds.
However, if the hidden-sector force remains strongly coupled well above its hadronization scale, corre-
sponding to a quasi-conformal coupling regime, its radiation pattern can differ qualitatively from that of
QCD. In particular, emissions need not be dominated by soft and collinear splittings, and the resulting
shower may instead populate phase space more uniformly [14–17]. For models in which the available
energy is distributed among many particles, this results in a characteristic collider signature known as a
Soft Unclustered Energy Pattern (SUEP), consisting of a high multiplicity of low-momentum particles,
distributed approximately isotropically in the center-of-mass frame [14, 18]. Figure 1 shows a representative
signal diagram. The absence of hard or collimated objects makes such signatures challenging both to
trigger on and to reconstruct. Exploring such unconventional and experimentally challenging signatures is
essential to ensure sensitivity to a broad range of possible extensions of the SM.

Both the existing searches for SUEP signatures performed by the CMS Collaboration [19, 20] and the
analysis presented here are based on the theoretical framework proposed in Ref. [18]. In this framework,
the hidden sector is accessed through a scalar portal, which may be the SM Higgs boson or a heavier
scalar particle. Within the dark sector, particles undergo showering and subsequently hadronize into a
large number of dark mesons 𝜙, which are assumed for simplicity to consist of a single particle species.
A wide range of values for the dark-meson mass 𝑚𝜙is theoretically motivated. Generally, values of
order 1–10 GeV are well suited for SUEP scenarios, as they allow for a rich set of decay channels to SM
particles while still producing the high-multiplicity, soft final states characteristic of this signature, which
are not efficiently targeted by conventional searches. The momenta of the dark mesons are expected to
follow an approximately thermal distribution [21] and are modeled using a Maxwell–Boltzmann spectrum
parameterized by a Hagedorn temperature 𝑇[22], which sets the characteristic momentum scale of the
hadronizing hidden sector and is expected to be of the same order as 𝑚𝜙. The dark mesons are assumed
to decay promptly into pairs of dark photons (𝐴′), which subsequently decay promptly into charged SM
particles via kinetic mixing with the SM photon [23]. While other decay modes of dark mesons to SM
particles are possible [24], this choice provides a convenient and realistic framework to realize a wide range
of final-state particle compositions. For a given dark-photon mass, the branching fractions to SM particles
are fixed, leading to specific mixtures of electrons, muons, and hadrons in the final state. For dark-photon
masses of order 1 GeV, the dominant decay modes are 𝐴′ →𝑒+𝑒−, 𝐴′ →𝜇+𝜇−, and 𝐴′ →𝜋+𝜋−.
A previous search for SUEP signatures by the CMS Collaboration [19] targeted gluon–gluon fusion
production of the mediator and relied on high-transverse-momentum initial-state radiation to trigger events
via large energy deposits in the calorimeters. While this approach provided sensitivity over a broad range
of 𝑇and 𝑚𝜙, it was primarily sensitive to scenarios with large mediator masses. Subsequent searches
targeting associated production of the Higgs boson with a vector boson [20] complemented this result.
However, the requirement of very high particle multiplicities to suppress background limited the sensitivity
to scenarios with low 𝑚𝜙and 𝑇.
The search presented here complements these approaches by targeting final states with a high multiplicity
of prompt muons, which are expected for a broad range of dark-photon masses 𝑚𝐴′. This strategy provides
strong background rejection while maintaining sensitivity to scenarios with moderate particle multiplicities,
thereby extending the coverage of the SUEP parameter space well beyond that of previous searches.

Events are selected using multi-muon triggers and target prompt final states with a high multiplicity
of muons. They are further characterized by relatively low average muon transverse momentum, large
charged-particle track multiplicity associated with the primary interaction, and isotropic spatial distribution
of the muons. The signal region is defined to be sensitive to a broad range of charged-particle multiplicities
and to preferentially select events in which muons are distributed more isotropically than in the dominant
background from QCD multijet production, where muons are produced mainly in heavy-flavor decays, and
which is estimated using a data-driven method.
2 ATLAS detector
The ATLAS detector [25] at the LHC covers nearly the entire solid angle around the collision point.1 It
consists of an inner tracking detector surrounded by a thin superconducting solenoid, electromagnetic
and hadronic calorimeters, and a muon spectrometer incorporating three large superconducting air-core
toroidal magnets.
The inner-detector system (ID) is immersed in a 2 T axial magnetic field and provides charged-particle
tracking in the range |𝜂| < 2.5. The high-granularity silicon pixel detector covers the vertex region and
typically provides four measurements per track, the first hit generally being in the insertable B-layer (IBL)
installed before Run 2 [26, 27]. It is followed by the SemiConductor Tracker (SCT), which usually provides
eight measurements per track. These silicon detectors are complemented by the transition radiation tracker
(TRT), which enables radially extended track reconstruction up to |𝜂| = 2.0. The TRT also provides
electron identification information based on the fraction of hits (typically 30 in total) above a higher
energy-deposit threshold corresponding to transition radiation.
The calorimeter system covers the pseudorapidity range |𝜂| < 4.9.
Within the region |𝜂| < 3.2,
electromagnetic calorimetry is provided by barrel and endcap high-granularity lead/liquid-argon (LAr)
calorimeters, with an additional thin LAr presampler covering |𝜂| < 1.8 to correct for energy loss in material
upstream of the calorimeters. Hadronic calorimetry is provided by the steel/scintillator-tile calorimeter,
segmented into three barrel structures within |𝜂| < 1.7, and two copper/LAr hadronic endcap calorimeters.
The solid angle coverage is completed with forward copper/LAr and tungsten/LAr calorimeter modules
optimised for electromagnetic and hadronic energy measurements, respectively.
The muon spectrometer (MS) comprises separate trigger and high-precision tracking chambers measuring
the deflection of muons in a magnetic field generated by the superconducting air-core toroidal magnets.
The field integral of the toroids ranges between 2.0 and 6.0 T m across most of the detector. Three layers
of precision chambers, each consisting of layers of monitored drift tubes, cover the region |𝜂| < 2.7,
complemented by cathode-strip chambers in the forward region, where the background is highest. The
muon trigger system covers the range |𝜂| < 2.4 with resistive-plate chambers in the barrel, and thin-gap
chambers in the endcap regions.
The luminosity is measured mainly by the LUCID–2 [28] detector that records Cherenkov light produced
in the quartz windows of photomultipliers located close to the beampipe.

3 Data and simulated event samples
The analysis uses 140 fb−1 of proton–proton (𝑝𝑝) collision data collected at √𝑠= 13 TeV by the ATLAS
experiment between 2015 and 2018, corresponding to the full LHC Run 2. Only events recorded under
stable-beam conditions with all detector subsystems operating normally are included, and standard
data-quality requirements are applied to ensure reliable reconstruction of physics objects [31]. The
average number of simultaneous 𝑝𝑝interactions per bunch crossing (pile-up) over the full dataset, ⟨𝜇⟩, is
approximately 34.
The search targets signal models in which a scalar mediator 𝑆is produced via gluon–gluon fusion and
subsequently decays into a quasi-conformal hidden sector, following the framework described in Section 1.

of scenarios in which a larger total energy is injected into the hidden sector. The dark-meson mass is set to
the benchmark values 𝑚𝜙= 1.5, 3, and 5 GeV, chosen as representative points within the range motivated
in Section 1. Consistent with the expectation that 𝑇is of the same order as 𝑚𝜙, it is varied according to

4 Event reconstruction
Events are selected using the multi-muon trigger with the lowest 𝑝T threshold that collected the full
integrated luminosity in each data-taking period. This corresponds to a trigger requiring at least three
muons with 𝑝T > 6 GeV in 2015 and 2016, and at least four muons with 𝑝T > 4 GeV in 2017 and 2018.
Simulated signal samples are corrected to reproduce the trigger efficiency expected in data. A scale factor
is applied to the simulated signal events, defined as the ratio of the trigger efficiency expected in data to
that in simulation. Following Ref. [66], the expected multi-muon trigger efficiency is estimated from the
corresponding single-muon trigger efficiencies with identical transverse momentum thresholds, assuming
that the efficiencies for individual muons are uncorrelated.
The single-muon efficiencies are measured in data and in the 𝑍→𝜇+𝜇−MC sample for muons passing
the offline selection requirements, using a tag-and-probe method similar to that described in Ref. [66].
In addition to the binning in 𝜂, 𝜙, and data-taking period used in Ref. [66], an additional binning in 𝑝T
is introduced to better model the reduced efficiency in the trigger turn-on region. The 𝑝T bin edges are
defined as [4, 6, 8, 10, ∞) GeV. At least three (four) muons reconstructed by the High Level Trigger are
required to be within Δ𝑅= 0.07 of an offline muon in 2015 and 2016 (2017 and 2018). The trigger
efficiency ranges from approximately 1% for all signal models with 𝑚𝜙= 1.5 GeV and 𝑇= 1 GeV to
nearly 100% for the model with 𝑀= 750 GeV, 𝑚𝐴′ = 0.5 GeV, and 𝑚𝜙= 𝑇= 5 GeV. The corresponding
scale factors range between 0.85 and 1. The variations in trigger efficiency and scale factor are driven by
differences in the muon kinematics, in particular the 𝑝T spectrum and multiplicity, between signal models.
Figure 2 illustrates this dependence for muons passing the selection requirements detailed below, before
any event selection is applied.

larger than 500 MeV that are consistent with originating from the beam collision region. The vertex with
the highest sum of squared transverse momenta of associated tracks is considered as the primary vertex
(PV) of the event. The transverse and longitudinal impact parameters of all tracks, denoted by 𝑑0 and 𝑧0,
respectively, are calculated relative to the beamline. For the longitudinal impact parameter, the 𝑧-position
of the primary vertex is used as the reference point.

are required to have 𝑝T > 3 GeV and to satisfy the cut-based LowPt working point defined in Ref. [68].

6 Systematic uncertainties
Experimental uncertainties on the simulated signal yields are evaluated to account for potential differences
between data and simulated signal samples in the event reconstruction and selection. The relative importance
of the various uncertainty sources depends strongly on the signal model parameters. For models with
𝑚𝜙, 𝑇≲3 GeV, where the acceptance is largely constrained by the 𝑝T thresholds of the online muon
reconstruction, the dominant contribution is expected to arise from the trigger efficiency. For models with
𝑚𝜙, 𝑇≳3 GeV, uncertainties associated with ID tracking are expected to become largest. This behavior is
reflected in the ranges quoted for the individual uncertainty sources below.
Two sources of systematic uncertainty affecting the trigger efficiency are evaluated, both arising from the
tag–and–probe procedure used to determine the single-muon trigger efficiencies. The first originates from
the limited statistical precision of the measurement, with its impact on the signal yield in the search region
ranging from about 0.1% to 20%, depending on the model parameters. The second is derived by varying
the selection requirements applied to the probe muon. The set of variations follows Ref. [66], except for the
probe-muon 𝑝T requirement, which is adapted to accommodate the additional 𝑝T binning used. The effect
of this second source on the signal yield in the search region ranges from roughly 0.1% to 4%, depending
on the model parameters.

Offline muon reconstruction is affected by uncertainties in the resolution of the combined muon track
and in the reconstruction efficiency. The impact of the muon track-resolution uncertainty is found to be
negligible. The uncertainty associated with the mismodeling of the muon reconstruction efficiency leads to
variations in the signal yield in the search region ranging from approximately 0.5% to 5%, depending on
the model parameters.
An uncertainty of 100% is assigned to the estimated rate of tracks arising from combinatorial background
in the ID, based on comparisons between data and simulation of the reconstructed track multiplicity as a
function of pile-up [67]. Since the contribution of such tracks is small, its effect on 𝑁tracks ranges from
approximately 1% to 5%, depending on the model parameters. For models predicting very high 𝑁tracks, this
uncertainty is largest in relative terms; however, its impact on the search sensitivity is sub-dominant, since
all events remain within the highest 𝑁tracks bin under any variation. Uncertainties on the ID track-resolution
and reconstruction-efficiency modeling are found to be negligible for all signal models.
A pile-up modeling uncertainty is assigned to account for the difference between the predicted and measured
inelastic cross-sections [72]. Its effect is evaluated to be below 3% on the signal yield in the search region
and below 1% on 𝑁tracks for all signal models.
An uncertainty on 𝑁PU

applied to the expected signal yields in the 𝐷𝑖bins.

LUCID-2 detector [28] for the primary luminosity measurements.

The limits reach values as low as approximately 0.05 fb for 𝑚𝑆= 750 GeV, 0.4 fb for 𝑚𝑆= 400 GeV, and

8 Conclusion
A search for soft unclustered energy patterns is presented using 140 fb−1 of √𝑠= 13 TeV proton–proton
collision data recorded by the ATLAS detector. The analysis targets scenarios in which a scalar mediator
decays into a strongly interacting, quasi-conformal hidden sector, giving rise to high-multiplicity final states
containing low-momentum particles, including muons, that are distributed approximately isotropically in
the mediator center-of-mass frame.
Events are selected using multi-muon triggers, and the search region is defined by requirements on the
muon multiplicity, average muon transverse momentum, and transverse impact-parameter significance,
targeting prompt, high-multiplicity final states with soft muons. The discrimination between signal and the
dominant QCD multijet background is achieved using two observables: the muon sphericity, constructed
in the rest frame of the muon system, and the charged-particle track multiplicity, corrected for pile-up.
The background contribution is estimated by using a likelihood-based ABCD method that exploits the
weak correlation between these observables, with residual correlations accounted for through a data-driven
parameterization.
No significant deviation from the background-only hypothesis is observed in the search region. The results
are therefore interpreted in terms of upper limits at the 95% confidence level on the on the product of the
mediator production cross section and the branching fraction for its decay into a SUEP.
This search extends the sensitivity to SUEP scenarios beyond previous LHC results [19, 20], particularly in
regions of parameter space characterized by moderate particle multiplicities and sizable muon fractions in
the final state. The analysis demonstrates the effectiveness of multi-muon-based strategies and provides a
complementary approach to existing searches targeting primarily hadronic final states.
