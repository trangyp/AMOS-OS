SYSTEMS
ARIJIT MAHATO, TUSHAR KANTA NAIK, AND A RAMESWAR PATRO
Abstract. We provide a complete description of the automorphism group Aut(W) of
a Coxeter group W admitting a star-shaped finite Coxeter diagram. We prove that each
automorphism decomposes as a product of inner and diagram automorphisms, along
with three additional types: transvections and two families of partial conjugations. Fur-
thermore, we investigate the natural short exact sequence 1 →Inn(W) →Aut(W) →
Out(W) →1. Using Moussong’s criteria for hyperbolicity, we show that these groups
possess the R∞-property. Finally, we establish rigidity properties for these groups using
known techniques and provide a solution to the isomorphism problem within the class
of star-shaped Coxeter systems.
1. Introduction
A group W is called a Coxeter group if it admits a presentation W = ⟨S | (sisj)m(si,sj) =
1, si, sj ∈S⟩, where m(si, sj) = m(sj, si) ∈N ∪{∞}, and m(si, sj) = 1 if and only if
si = sj. The pair (W, S) is called a Coxeter system, and the values m(si, sj) for si ̸= sj
are known as the labels (or exponents) of the system. The cardinality of S is defined as
the rank of the system. For each subset I ⊆S, the subgroup WI = ⟨I⟩is called a standard
parabolic subgroup, and its conjugates are called parabolic subgroups, which are themselves
Coxeter groups.
A convenient way to encapsulate the presentation of a Coxeter group is via graphs
or diagrams.
The Coxeter graph Γ(W,S) has vertex set S, where two distinct vertices
si, sj are joined by an edge if and only if m(si, sj) ≥3; with labels for m(si, sj) ≥4.
Parallel to this is the finite (Coxeter) diagram V(W,S), which shares the same vertex set
S. In this diagram, two distinct vertices si, sj ∈S are joined by an edge labelled with
m(si, sj) if and only if m(si, sj) is finite. These graphical representations reveal structural
features at a glance. For instance, if the Coxeter graph Γ(W,S) is disconnected, then W
decomposes as a direct product of the parabolic subgroups associated with the connected
components. When Γ(W,S) is connected, W cannot be expressed as a direct product of
proper parabolic subgroups; accordingly, the Coxeter system (W, S) is termed irreducible.
On the other hand, if the finite diagram V(W,S) is disconnected, then W decomposes as
a free product of the parabolic subgroups. It is worth noting that individually, these
two diagrams determine the underlying Coxeter group uniquely. Consequently, any graph
automorphism of these diagrams extends naturally to an automorphism of the group W,
referred to as a diagram automorphism. The collection of all diagram automorphisms of
W forms a subgroup of Aut(W), denoted by Diag(W).
While a Coxeter system (W, S) uniquely determines the group W, the group itself
does not uniquely determine the system. This motivates the well-known isomorphism
problem for Coxeter groups: determining when two distinct Coxeter presentations (or
equivalently Coxeter graphs or finite diagrams) define isomorphic groups. A classic exam-
ple is the dihedral group D12, which attains two non-isomorphic Coxeter presentations:

A Coxeter group W is called rigid if all Coxeter systems of W induce the same Coxeter
graph up to isomorphism. It is called strongly rigid if any two Coxeter generating sets are
conjugate. The system (W, S) is called reflection rigid, if for every Coxeter generating set
R ⊂SW, there exists an automorphism α ∈Aut(W) such that α(R) = S. Finally, the
system (W, S) is called strongly reflection rigid, if for any Coxeter generating set R ⊂SW,
there exists an element w ∈W such that R = wSw−1.
A Coxeter system (W, S) is said to be even (respectively odd) if all the finite labels are
even (respectively odd). Radcliffe [34] showed that strongly even Coxeter systems (where
all the finite labels are 2 and/or multiples of 4) are rigid. More generally, Bahls [1] proved
that a Coxeter group cannot admit two non-isomorphic even Coxeter systems. He [2]
also gave necessary and sufficient conditions for the strong rigidity of an even Coxeter
group. Large-type Coxeter groups (where no finite labels are 2) are reflection rigid up to
diagram twisting [30]. Infinite irreducible Coxeter groups that do not contain the infinite
dihedral group as a parabolic subgroup are strongly rigid [9, 18]. Charney and Davis [10]
established a significant geometric criterion for a strongly rigid Coxeter group: if a Coxeter
group W acts effectively, properly, and cocompactly on some contractible manifold, then
W is strongly rigid. For general Coxeter groups, an equivalent criterion for strong rigidity
and strong reflection rigidity is given in [19].
The isomorphism problem and the study of automorphisms are deeply intertwined.
While the former seeks the conditions under which different Coxeter systems yield the
same abstract group, the latter investigates the internal symmetries of these groups. This
conceptual bridge naturally leads to a systematic study of automorphisms of Coxeter
groups.
A Coxeter group is called right-angled (in short RACG) if all the finite labels are 2.
If there are no finite labels, it is referred to as a universal Coxeter group. A universal
Coxeter group of rank n is denoted by Un. Let W be an RACG with Γ as its Coxeter
graph. Tits [38] showed that Aut(W) = Spe(W)⋊Aut(F(Γ)), where Spe(W) is the group
of automorphisms of W that preserves the conjugacy classes of involutions of W, and F(Γ)
is the groupoid of co-cliques of the Coxeter graph Γ. Further, he proved that if W does not
contain U3 as a parabolic subgroup, then Spe(W) = Inn(W). For a finite rank irreducible
RACG W, M¨uhlherr [27] explicitly determined Spe(W), and proved that Spe(W) =
Inn(W) if and only if the neighbourhood of each vertex in Γ is an independent subset. For
Un, he [26] proved that Aut(Un) = Spe(Un)⋊Sn. Bahls [4] proved that for an even, large-
type Coxeter group W, whose finite diagram V has no vertex v such that V\{v} consists of
more than two connected components, Aut(W) = Spe(W)⋊Diag(W). Ryan [36] obtained
that for rigid Coxeter groups whose labels are all odd, Aut(W) = Inn(W) ⋊Diag(W).
Caprace and Minasyan [8] studied parabolic-preserving automorphisms, and established
criteria for determining when such automorphisms are inner-by-graph.

The primary aim of this paper is to contribute to the existing literature on the auto-

Although the automorphisms of rank 3 Coxeter groups were described by Franzsen [15, 16],
we include the rank 3 case here to provide a unified treatment of the star-shaped diagram.
While a subfamily of SW consisting of odd Coxeter groups was previously investigated

all members within this broader class, including those with even labels.
We begin by establishing the structural foundations for star-shaped Coxeter groups in
Section 2, where we recall essential results on parabolic subgroups and the centralizers of
generators. We also define three specific types of automorphisms: transvections, and two
type partial conjugations. In Section 3, we focus on the interactions between transvec-
tions and partial conjugations, as well as the subgroups they generate. In Section 4, we
present the explicit structural description of Aut(W) (Theorem 4.4), and derive splitting
criteria for the associated short exact sequence 1 →Inn(W) →Aut(W) →Out(W) →1

for these groups using Moussong’s criteria for hyperbolicity. Finally, Section 5 addresses

Theorem 4.10. All Coxeter groups W ∈SW satisfy the R∞-property.
Proof. Let W ∈SW be a Coxeter group with the generating set S. Note that it does
not contain any parabolic subgroups which is an affine Coxeter group. Furthermore, for
any disjoint subsets T1, T2 ⊆S such that the corresponding parabolic subgroups WT1 and
WT2 are infinite, there necessarily exist vertices s ∈T1 and s′ ∈T2 with m(s, s′) = ∞.

free subgroup of rank 2 (e.g., the subgroup generated by sisj and s0sisjs0 for i ∈I \ I2),
it is a non-elementary hyperbolic group. Applying the result of Fel’shtyn [14, Theorem

Gromov hyperbolic group is infinite, we conclude that W possesses the R∞-property.
