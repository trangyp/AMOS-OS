SCALING LIMITS OF BIVARIATE NEARLY-UNSTABLE HAWKES PROCESSES AND
APPLICATIONS TO ROUGH VOLATILITY
SOHAIB EL KARMI
Abstract. We prove a functional limit theorem for a pair of nearly-unstable Hawkes processes interacting through a
triangular cross-excitation mechanism with kernels with distinct heavy tails. To our knowledge this is the first functional
limit theorem of this type in the heterogeneous case, where the two components have different degrees of roughness.
As the system approaches criticality, the renormalized intensity processes converge weakly to the unique solution
of a coupled stochastic Volterra system driven by two independent Brownian motions. The first component evolves
autonomously as a rough fractional diffusion with Hurst index H1 = α1 −1/2. The second component is driven both
by its own noise and, through a convolution cross-kernel, by the noise of the first: it is this cross-kernel, the convolution
of the two individual Mittag-Leffler kernels, that encodes both Hurst parameters simultaneously and distinguishes the
bivariate limit from any product of independent univariate limits or any classical bivariate Brownian model.
A key consequence is that the two components are decorrelated at short time scales: their functional correlation
ϱ(t) vanishes as Cϱ tα1 as t ↓0, with the exponent governed exclusively by the rougher of the two components and the
constant Cϱ given in closed form. We also show that hypothesis (A5) (scale matching) is not a structural restriction:
dropping it replaces K1 ∗K2 by a time-rescaled version K1 ∗K(ρ12)

with an explicit correction factor.
The proof is self-contained and combines four main ingredients: convergence of the renormalized kernels in the space
of square-integrable functions, tightness of the joint intensity process in the space C([0, 1]; R2), identification of every
limit point as a martingale solution via Rebolledo’s theorem, and uniqueness from the affine Volterra theory of Abi
Jaber, Larsson, and Pulido, which we extend to a new theorem (Theorem 7.3) covering equations with random adapted
drifts. We also identify precisely the three obstructions that prevent extending the proof to the fully symmetric case

1. Introduction
1.1. Background. Hawkes processes [9] are self-exciting point processes whose intensity is driven by the history
of past events through a memory kernel. When this kernel has heavy tails and the process operates near criticality
(branching ratio close to one), the macroscopic fluctuations of the intensity exhibit long memory and fractional scaling.
The univariate limit theory is well developed. Jaisson and Rosenbaum [16] proved that a nearly-unstable Hawkes
process with a power-law kernel of exponent α ∈(1/2, 1) converges, after appropriate renormalization, to a rough
fractional diffusion with Hurst parameter H = α −1/2 ∈(0, 1/2).
This gives a microscopic derivation of rough
volatility models, which are empirically supported by high-frequency equity data [8]. Subsequent work by Horst, Xu,
and Zhang [15] and Horst and Xu [10] extended these results to more general kernels and established C-tightness of
the intensity process in C([0, 1]).
The present paper addresses what happens when two nearly-unstable Hawkes components cross-excite each other.
1.2. The bivariate problem and its difficulty. Bivariate Hawkes processes arise naturally in financial microstruc-
ture, for instance as models of the joint order flow of two related assets or the co-excitation structure of a bid-ask
spread [5, 6, 4]. In such applications the two components typically exhibit different degrees of roughness, reflecting
different autocorrelation structures in the underlying flows.
Empirical calibrations on high-frequency equity data
indicate that the nearly-unstable subcritical regime is essential for reproducing the clustering and autocorrelation pat-
terns observed in order flow [5, 8]; the present paper provides a rigorous functional limit theorem underpinning this
observation in the bivariate heavy-tailed case.
The essential difficulty in the heterogeneous case is the following. When the two self-exciting kernels have distinct
heavy-tail exponents 1/2 < α1 < α2 < 1, the two components must be renormalized on different time scales simul-
taneously: the first by T α1−1, the second by T α2−1. Because these scales are different, the cross-excitation between
the components does not vanish in the limit nor reduce to a simple Brownian correlation. Instead it contributes
a genuinely functional dependence: the limiting correlation structure is carried by a new kernel, the convolution
L12 = b12
∞∥ψ12∥1 · (K1 ∗K2), which encodes both exponents simultaneously. No existing limit theorem for bivariate
Hawkes processes addresses this simultaneous renormalization problem. Multivariate Hawkes processes have been
studied in the finite-dimensional subcritical regime [2, 9], and Bacry–Muzy [5] have calibrated bivariate models to
financial data, but neither paper addresses the near-critical scaling limit with heterogeneous tail indices.
1.3. Main novelty. To our knowledge, this is the first functional limit theorem for a bivariate nearly-unstable Hawkes
process with heterogeneous heavy-tail indices 1/2 < α1 < α2 < 1.
The cross-kernel. The cross-excitation survives renormalization and is captured in the limit by the effective kernel
L12 = b12
∞∥ψ12∥1·(K1∗K2), where Ki are the individual Mittag-Leffler kernels of the two components (Proposition 3.5).
The convolution K1 ∗K2 has Laplace exponent α1 + α2, strictly larger than either individual exponent, so the cross-
kernel is smoother than either self-kernel. The correlation between the two limit processes is therefore not a constant
Brownian correlation but a time-dependent functional dependence whose strength decreases with the lag.
Short-time decorrelation. A direct consequence is that the two components are decorrelated at short time scales (i.e.
as the time lag t ↓0, not in the sense of trading frequency). Their functional correlation satisfies ϱ(t) ∼Cϱ tα1 as
t ↓0, where α1 is the exponent of the rougher component and Cϱ is an explicit constant (Corollary 3.8). The rougher
component governs the rate at which correlation is lost at fine scales. This power-law decay of correlation at short lags
is consistent with the empirical Epps effect [3], which documents decreasing cross-correlations between asset returns
as the sampling frequency increases.
1.4. Main contributions. The core of the paper (Sections 3–7) establishes the following results for the triangular
case (ψ12 ̸= 0, ψ21 = 0), in which component 1 excites component 2 but not conversely.
(1) Functional limit theorem (Theorem 7.8). The bivariate renormalized intensity process (V T,1, V T,2) converges
weakly in C([0, 1]; R2) to the unique weak solution of a coupled stochastic Volterra system in which V 1 is autonomous
and V 2 is driven by both its own noise and the noise of V 1 through the cross-kernel L12. The two components have
distinct Hurst parameters Hi = αi −1/2.

(3) Short-time decorrelation law (Corollary 3.8). The functional correlation satisfies ϱ(t) ∼Cϱ tα1 as t ↓0, with

pre-limit sequence; Obstruction 2 prevents identifying limit points with solutions of the coupled SVE; and Obstruction 3
prevents proving uniqueness of those solutions. These are the points at which the present proof does not extend directly
to the fully symmetric case; a different approach would be needed at each step.
1.5. Relation to the literature. Jaisson–Rosenbaum [16] is the direct predecessor. We borrow their renormal-
ization scheme and extend it to the bivariate setting. The new difficulty is the cross-term, which has no counterpart
in the univariate argument.
Horst–Xu–Zhang [15] provide the C-tightness criterion we apply (Proposition 5.1). We verify its hypotheses for
the cross-term, which requires a separate Hölder bound at a higher exponent than the self-term.
Abi Jaber–Larsson–Pulido [13] provide the uniqueness theory for affine stochastic Volterra equations. We use
their Theorem 4.3 twice: once for V 1 directly, and once for V 2 conditional on V 1 after observing that the resulting
equation has a random but adapted drift.
The extension of their uniqueness result to stochastic adapted drifts
(Theorem 7.3) is new and is not covered by [13].
It is stated as an independent theorem since it applies to any
admissible kernel in the sense of [13] and may find applications beyond the bivariate Hawkes setting.
El Euch–Rosenbaum [7] identify the rough Heston model as the limit of a univariate quadratic Hawkes process.
The bivariate limit obtained here can serve as a starting point for analogous two-asset rough volatility models, though
a fully specified option-pricing model would require additional structure beyond the scope of this paper.
1.6. Organization. Section 2 introduces the model, assumptions, and main notation. Section 3 establishes the cross-
kernel L12, the covariance formula, and the short-time decorrelation law. Section 4 provides analytic illustrations of
the main results, including a calibration to equity market parameters and a discussion of the regularity hierarchy of
the kernels. Sections 5, 6, and 7 contain the proof of the main theorem: C-tightness, identification of limit points via
Rebolledo’s theorem, and uniqueness in law, respectively. Auxiliary lemmas are proved within the section where they
are used.

Remark 2.9 (Interpretation). The structure of (14)–(15) can be read as follows.
The first component V 1 is an
autonomous rough process with Hurst parameter H1; it is driven entirely by β1. The second component V 2 has two

dependent, vanishing correlation found here is a distinctive consequence of the near-critical bivariate microstructure.

4. Analytic illustrations
The main results admit direct analytic illustration without simulation.
4.1. Indicative calibration to equity market parameters. Gatheral, Jaisson, and Rosenbaum [8] estimate the
Hurst parameter of realized volatility for a broad range of equity indices and find H ≈0.1 (α ≈0.6) robustly across
markets. In a bivariate setting, the two components of our model can represent the intensities of buyer-initiated and
seller-initiated trades on a single asset, or the order flows of two correlated assets. Bacry and Muzy [5] calibrate a
bivariate Hawkes model to high-frequency tick data and find asymmetric cross-excitation coefficients, consistent with
the triangular structure ψ21 = 0 of the present paper.
Based on these empirical observations, the parameter regime α1 = 0.6 (H1 = 0.1), α2 = 0.8 (H2 = 0.3), ℓ∞∈
[0.3, 0.6] is representative of equity microstructure. In this regime, Corollary 3.8 gives:
• The functional correlation vanishes as ϱ(t) ≈Cϱ t0.6 for small t, with Cϱ ∈[0.14, 0.28] for ℓ∞∈[0.3, 0.6]

• At a 5-minute horizon (t ≈5/390 ≈0.013 of a trading day), ϱ(0.013) ≈Cϱ × 0.0130.6 ≈0.05–0.10, consistent
with the Epps effect [3].
• The rougher component (α1 = 0.6) governs the short-time decorrelation rate, irrespective of α2.
