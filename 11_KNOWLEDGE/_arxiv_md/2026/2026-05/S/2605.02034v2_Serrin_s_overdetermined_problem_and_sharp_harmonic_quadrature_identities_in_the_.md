SERRIN’S OVERDETERMINED PROBLEM AND SHARP
HARMONIC QUADRATURE IDENTITIES IN THE PLANE
YI RU-YA ZHANG
Abstract. We study a weak formulation of Serrin’s overdetermined boundary value
problem in planar Jordan domains with rectifiable boundary. Our first result estab-
lishes that, within the class of rectifiable Jordan Smirnov domains, the corresponding
harmonic quadrature identity, equivalent to Serrin’s overdetermined problem, nec-
essarily implies that the domain is a disk. Subsequently, we construct a family of
rectifiable, non-Smirnov Jordan domains that nonetheless satisfy the same quadra-
ture identity, thereby demonstrating the sharpness of the Smirnov regularity assump-
tion. Consequently, there exists a nontrivial Jordan domain with rectifiable boundary
satisfying the weak formulation of Serrin’s overdetermined system in R2.

The proof proceeds by combining the quadrature identity with Smirnov’s theory of
analytic functions. Starting from (1.4), we first derive a boundary Cauchy-orthogonality
relation. Within the Smirnov class, this relation yields a bounded holomorphic function
whose boundary trace encodes the overdetermined normal derivative. This construc-
tion enables us to obtain a weak Serrin-type solution and to recover Weinberger’s
P-function method in a low-regularity planar setting. The principal analytical tool is
the theory of nontangential boundary limits of Hardy-class functions in the unit disk;
see also [10].
The Smirnov assumption in Theorem 1.1 is sharp. Our second theorem constructs
non-Smirnov rectifiable Jordan domains satisfying the same quadrature identity.

Theorem 1.2. There exist a0 > 0, a family {Ωa}0<a<a0 of bounded rectifiable Jordan
domains, and constants c(a) > 0, such that none of the domains Ωa is a Smirnov
domain and (1.4) holds. Moreover, each Ωa is invariant under rotation by π/2.

full class of rectifiable Jordan domains.
Equivalently, the Smirnov property is the
exact threshold, within the class considered here, between rigidity and flexibility for
the planar Serrin-type quadrature identity. Moreover, this construction also provides

The construction in Theorem 1.2 is based on a refined Duren–Shapiro–Shields mech-
anism. A purely singular branch is not sufficient for (1.4): Unlike (1.5), the identity
(1.4) couples the boundary arclength density with the interior Jacobian density of the
conformal map. Toward this problem, we first show that such a pure singular con-
struction is impossible in a perturbative regime; see Proposition 4.1. We then correct
the singular factor by solving a nonlinear fixed-point equation for an outer factor.
The four-fold symmetry removes the low Fourier modes corresponding to the M¨obius
degeneracies of the disk, making the linearized operator invertible.
Finally, we briefly address the higher-dimensional setting. The preceding examples
indicate that the uniform upper density condition (1.2) is likely (close to) optimal for
obtaining rough Serrin-type rigidity in dimensions greater than 2, as is particularly
suggested by the results in [28] for (1.5). Nonetheless, the planar construction relies in
an essential way on the inner-outer factorization of functions in the unit disk, and no
analogous factorization theory is currently available in higher dimensions. Moreover,
the proof of Theorem 1.2 also implies the construction would be more complicated that
the one by Lewis–Vogel [26]. Consequently, the question of whether there exist higher-
dimensional, non-spherical domains satisfying the weak Serrin condition remains open
and constitutes a distinct problem, which shall be treated in a separate investigation.
