Security Metrics for Nonlinear Optical Light Sources from Interferometric Field
Reconstruction
Zijian Gan,∗Shuyue Feng,∗Camryn J. Gloor, Wei You, and Andrew M. Moran†
Department of Chemistry, University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, Chapel Hill, NC 27599, USA
Nonlinear optical light sources enable the generation of photons with quantum states that are
intrinsically linked to underlying material dynamics, rather than imposed through external mod-
ulation.
Here we investigate fundamental quantum communication metrics of four-wave-mixing
signal fields generated by the two-dimensional perovskite (PEA)2PbI4. Using polarization-resolved
interferometric measurements together with a microscopic nonlinear response model for the Bloch
vector, we reconstruct effective single-photon polarization density matrices inferred from the ex-
perimental signal fields and evaluate the corresponding Holevo bound and effective secret-bit rates
as a function of the coherence time, population time, and detection wavelength.
We find that
incorporating the coherence-time degree of freedom systematically lowers the Holevo bound by ap-
proximately 2.6–5.8% across the various excitonic resonances, indicating reduced distinguishability
of the polarization states when the full multidimensional parameter space is sampled. To connect
the polarization state indistinguishability with experimentally achievable throughput, we further
introduce an effective secret-bits-per-pulse metric that enables rapid evaluation of secure informa-
tion throughput for candidate materials without requiring photon-number-resolved detection. For
the present system, control of the population time via spin-dependent evolution yields substantially
higher secret-bit rates than manipulation of the coherence time, while spectral regions associated
with single exciton and biexciton resonances define complementary operating regimes for secure
communication. More broadly, this work positions nonlinear spectroscopy as a framework for ex-
ploring how emergent optical materials can generate and structure quantum states in ways that are
advantageous for established quantum communication schemes.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Two-dimensional organic–inorganic hybrid perovskites
(2D-OIHPs) constitute a versatile materials platform in
which optical polarizations can be precisely controlled
across the visible spectrum.
Their high photolumi-
nescence efficiencies, tunable emission wavelengths, and
strong oscillator strengths have enabled applications in-
cluding light-emitting diodes and optically pumped lasers
[1–6].
In addition, the incorporation of chiral organic
components within 2D-OIHPs has been shown to sup-
port circularly polarized luminescence and spin-selective
optical effects, with potential relevance for photonic tech-
nologies [7–14]. While photoluminescence is generally re-
stricted to singly excited states, resonances between sin-
gle excitons and biexcitons contribute to the nonlinear
optical responses of 2D-OIHPs [15–19]. Light emission
induced by higher-order optical interactions involving
biexcitonic states can exhibit relatively high ellipticities,
although these effects are generally short-lived in thin
2D-OIHP quantum wells, where spin relaxation can oc-
cur on the 100 fs timescale [20–26]. These features of the
nonlinear optical responses of 2D-OIHP systems were ex-
ploited in our recent implementation of a BB84-inspired
protocol for quantum key distribution [27, 28], where a
material-intrinsic encoding mechanism for the polariza-
tion states of four-wave-mixing signal photons was used
to transmit an ASCII message [29]. While this prior ef-

among single-exciton fine-structure states.
This ultra-

encoding mechanism in which the polarization basis at
the 2D-OIHP light source is selectively controlled by tog-
gling the pump–probe delay time between values that
are short and long compared to the spin-relaxation time,
thereby permitting communication with efficiencies be-

independent control parameter for the signal field. Be-

dynamic polarization effects; however, we find that biex-

of the 2D-OIHP provide an unexpected and effective

four-wave mixing signal pulses are attenuated to mean
photon numbers of order unity [34–36]. Under these con-

polarization states, which are determined by the material
response and the timing of the optical pulse sequence.

bound and to evaluate secure information throughput di-

requiring photon-number-resolved detection. In the fol-

introducing an effective secret-bits-per-pulse metric that

B.
Four-Wave Mixing Measurements
A detailed description of the four-wave-mixing exper-
imental setup was provided in prior work [41], while a
more concise summary is presented here. The measure-
ments were performed using a Coherent Libra titanium–
sapphire laser system producing 50 fs pulses centered at
800 nm with a pulse energy of 3.5 mJ at a 1 kHz repeti-
tion rate. A visible continuum was generated by focusing
approximately 1.8 mJ of the fundamental output into a
4 m argon-filled cell operated at an absolute pressure of
1.5–2.0 atm. The resulting broadband beam was subse-
quently passed through a fused-silica prism compressor,
where spectral selection of the 490–550 nm region was
achieved using a slit positioned within the spatially dis-
persed beam. Residual dispersion was compensated using
a chirped-mirror compressor (Layertec) providing a total
group-delay dispersion of −880 fs2. At the sample po-
sition, this configuration yielded an instrument response
function of ∼20 fs, consistent with pulse durations below
20 fs.
Following compression, the pump and probe beams
were separated using a 1 mm-thick beam splitter, and
their relative delay time, T, was controlled with a retrore-
flector (Newport UBBR2.5-2UV) mounted on a transla-
tion stage with a 15 cm range (Newport GTS 150). The

III.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A.
Electronic Structure and Nonlinear Optical
Response of 2D-OIHPs
The lead–iodide–based quantum wells employed in the
present experiments incorporate organic layers composed
of phenethylammonium, forming (PEA)2PbI4.
While
our prior characterization of the optical response mo-
tivates this choice of system [40, 41], the experimental
signatures of spin relaxation are consistent with those
observed in other 2D-OIHPs [20–26].
As indicated in
Fig. 3(a), the system exhibits three nearly degener-
ate single-exciton fine-structure states, with transition
dipoles that are either circularly polarized within the
plane of the quantum well (MJ = ±1) or linearly polar-
ized and out-of-plane (MJ = 0). In addition, the relative
stabilities of the biexcitons are governed by the paral-
lel or antiparallel alignment of the single-exciton angular
momentum vectors [18, 40]. Because the linewidths are
much greater than the energy splittings within the exci-
ton fine structure under ambient conditions [46–48], we
have modeled the nonlinear optical response function us-
ing three fundamental spectral line shapes [40, 41].
To illustrate nonlinear optical signatures of the elec-
tronic structure, transient absorption spectra previously
reported in Ref. [40] are overlaid at T = 0 in Fig. 3(b),
where the pump and probe pulses possess either the same
or opposite circular polarizations (SCP and OCP). De-
spite random quantum-well orientations within the poly-
crystalline films [40, 49], the observed difference in SCP
and OCP responses confirms the generation of a short-
lived macroscopic spin polarization. The relative ampli-
tudes of the three classes of resonances reflect the under-
lying sequences of field–matter interactions in the energy-
level diagram, with the parallel and antiparallel biexciton
features being most prominent under SCP and OCP con-
ditions, respectively. Importantly, transient absorption
spectra acquired with SCP and OCP configurations are
commonly compared to determine spin relaxation times,
as the difference between these signals vanishes follow-
ing equilibration within the single-exciton fine structure
[18, 22, 23].

C.
Security Metrics, Holevo Analysis, and
Effective Secret-Bit Rates
The Holevo bound [33] provides a natural metric for as-
sessing whether access to the full multidimensional con-
trol space defined by the coherence time τ, the popu-
lation time T, and the detection wavelength λ yields a
polarization-state ensemble that is systematically more
difficult for an eavesdropper to discriminate than an en-
semble generated by varying only T and λ with τ = 0.
Because the four-wave-mixing polarization states are a
direct manifestation of the underlying coherent dynamics
of the perovskite material, the resulting empirical Holevo
bound quantifies the maximum amount of information
an eavesdropper could extract about the polarization-
encoded quantum states generated by the four-wave-
mixing process. Importantly, the encoded bit values are
inferred from ensemble-averaged polarization statistics
accumulated over many weak pulses, so the security anal-
ysis is based on the collective distribution of polarization
states generated by the material rather than measure-
ments performed on isolated single photons.
A larger
value of the Holevo bound corresponds to a more dis-
tinguishable ensemble of polarization states and a lower
degree of intrinsic security, whereas a smaller value in-
dicates greater overlap among the states produced by
the material and consequently enhanced resistance to dis-
crimination.
To visualize the Holevo bound in a physically transpar-
ent way, it is useful to examine how the experimentally
generated polarization states populate the Bloch sphere
[53]. Because the calculation involves single-qubit density
matrices, each polarization state can be written in terms
of its normalized Bloch vector r = (r1, r2, r3), where each
component is directly extracted from the measured fields,
as detailed in Appendix A. The component r3 represents
the normalized polarization contrast between the hori-
zontal and vertical components, while r1 and r2 describe
the real and imaginary parts of their mutual coherence.
These three numbers collectively encode the full polar-
ization ellipse associated with each point in the delay–
wavelength space. The Holevo bound can then be ex-
pressed in terms of the ensemble-averaged Bloch vector

IV.
CONCLUSIONS
The present work demonstrates that incorporating the
coherence time τ into the polarization-state ensemble sys-
tematically lowers the Holevo bound by approximately
2.6% −5.8%, depending on the wavelength region and
excitonic resonance. Although this reduction is modest
in absolute magnitude, it is significant from the stand-
point of quantum information because the Holevo bound
depends logarithmically on the distinguishability of the
states. Therefore, the observed decrease reflects a mean-
ingful enhancement in intrinsic security: sampling the
full (τ, T, λ) parameter space produces a more broadly
distributed ensemble of polarization states, resulting in a
more mixed ensemble-averaged state ¯ρ and a correspond-
ingly lower information capacity available to an eaves-
dropper. In this sense, the coherent material dynamics
accessed through the coherence time τ provide a natural
mechanism for enhancing the security of polarization-
encoded communication beyond what is achievable by
varying only the population time T.
Complementing the Holevo analysis,
the effective
secret-bits-per-pulse metric provides a practical measure
of communication efficiency by explicitly accounting for
the number of pulses that must be averaged to discrimi-
nate logical states. Whereas the Holevo bound quantifies
intrinsic security, Yeff(λ) incorporates both the empiri-
cally determined error probability and the required pulse
accumulation Nbit(λ), thereby translating polarization-
state distinguishability into an experimentally realizable
secret-bit yield. This formulation enables rapid evalua-
tion of candidate materials directly from reconstructed
signal fields without photon-resolved detection and pro-
vides an immediate criterion for feasibility:
encoding
strategies that require large Nbit(λ) become increas-
ingly impractical due to the extended acquisition time
needed for reliable discrimination.
Within this frame-
work, population-time (T) toggling yields substantially
smaller Nbit(λ) values and higher effective secret-bit rates
across much of the signal spectrum, particularly near the
lower-energy biexciton resonance. In contrast, communi-
cation protocols based on coherence-time (τ) toggling are
feasible only in narrow spectral regions associated with
single-exciton transitions.
More broadly, these results highlight the potential of
nonlinear optical light sources as platforms for material-
intrinsic quantum-state engineering, in which security-
relevant properties of the emitted photons arise directly
from coherent many-body dynamics rather than from
external modulation or active optical elements. In the
present case, the polarization states of attenuated four-
wave-mixing signals encode information through ultra-
fast spin dynamics occurring within a 2D-OIHP system,
illustrating how nonlinear optical responses can access
degrees of freedom that are typically absent from conven-
tional weak coherent sources [51]. The reduced nonlinear-
response analysis further suggests that the strongest po-
larization modulation, and consequently the largest in-
terference contributions to the Bloch-vector magnitude,
occur at intermediate exciton–biexciton spectral overlap
corresponding to ΞX,XXB ≈1, where the combined ef-
fects of absorptive overlap and dispersive phase rotation
are simultaneously optimized.
While the signals stud-

photon regime through attenuation, the extension of such
material-intrinsic encoding strategies to long-range or
free-space quantum communication will ultimately re-
quire protocol-level optimization and integration with es-
tablished quantum key distribution frameworks [38, 54].
Overall, these results highlight a promising interface be-
tween nonlinear spectroscopy, emergent optical materi-
als, and quantum information science.
