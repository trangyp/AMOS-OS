Abstract
Let PG(n −1, q) denote the (n −1)-dimensional projective space over Fq. We investigate
the intersection of two Desarguesian (h −1)-spreads of PG(kh −1, q) and show that it is
determined by a subgeometry over a suitable extension field. Our approach combines a
characterization of subsets of points of PG(k −1, qh) closed under q-order subgeometries
with a matrix model for Desarguesian spreads based on Moore matrices. This leads naturally
to the notion of generalized Segre varieties Sr
kr−1,h−1(q) and a geometric description of their
maximal subspaces. As a main application, we prove that if two distinct Desarguesian (h−1)-
spreads of PG(kh −1, q) contain a common pseudo-arc of size k + 1, then their intersection
is precisely the system Rr
h,q of (h −1)-dimensional subspaces of Sr
kr−1,h−1(q), for some proper
divisor r of h.
Keywords: Segre Varieties; Desarguesian Spreads; Moore Matrices; Subgeometries.

A useful description of a Desarguesian spread is in terms of its director spaces. More precisely,
if PG(kh−1, q) is embedded as a subgeometry of PG(kh−1, qh) and fixed pointwise by a semilinear
collineation Ψ of order h, then a (k−1)-dimensional subspace Θ, such that Θ, ΘΨ, . . . , ΘΨh−1 span
PG(kh −1, qh), determines a spread whose elements are the intersections of PG(kh −1, q) with
the subspaces ⟨P, PΨ, . . . , PΨh−1⟩qh, where P ranges over Θ. The subspaces Θ, ΘΨ, . . . , ΘΨh−1 are
called the director spaces of the spread, and they uniquely determine it [1]. On the other hand,
the Segre variety Sk−1,h−1(q) is the image of the Segre embedding
σk,h : PG(k −1, q) × PG(h −1, q) −→PG(kh −1, q),
and consists of the points corresponding to rank-one k×h matrices. Its geometry is governed by
two families of maximal subspaces, the reguli, whose elements are mutually disjoint within each
family and intersect pairwise in exactly one point across the two families. Segre varieties thus
provide the natural geometric model for rank-one tensors and appear throughout the theory
of field reduction, linear sets, and semifields [5, 8, 9]. A particularly significant link between
these two structures is the following: the elements of a Desarguesian spread corresponding to
the points of a canonical subgeometry inside a director space form precisely the (h −1)-regulus
of a Segre variety, see e.g. [8, Theorem 2.6].
The aim of this paper is to determine the intersection of two Desarguesian spreads of
PG(kh −1, q). We show that such intersections are governed by subfield geometry and can be
described in terms of suitable generalized Segre varieties. Our first result is a characterization
of subsets of points of PG(k −1, qh) that are closed with respect to q-order subgeometries. This
extends a theorem of Rottey and Van de Voorde for the projective line and shows that such
sets necessarily arise from subgeometries defined over a subfield [11]. Next, we introduce the
geometric setting through a matrix model for the Desarguesian spread. More precisely, we
define the ambient projective space over Fqh in terms of the vector space of h × k matrices with
entries in Fqh. We endow this space with a suitable semilinear collineation whose fixed-point
set is a canonical subgeometry isomorphic to PG(kh −1, q). In this model, the points of the
fixed subgeometry are represented by Moore matrices and we describe the relation between
its spread elements and its director spaces. Within this framework, we introduce the notion of
a generalized Segre variety Sr

Preliminaries
In this section, we collect some definitions and known results that will be useful throughout the
paper. In particular, we briefly recall Desarguesian spreads and Segre varieties, together with
some of their basic properties. We also show a characterization result for sets of points having
a closure property with respect to q-order subgeometries.
2.1
Desarguesian spreads
An (h −1)-spread of the projective space PG(n −1, q) is a collection S of pairwise disjoint (h −1)-
dimensional subspaces such that every point of PG(n −1, q) is contained in exactly one element
of S. Equivalently, an (h −1)-spread induces a partition of the point set of PG(n −1, q) into
(h −1)-dimensional subspaces. Two spreads D1 and D2 of PG(n −1, q) are said to be equivalent
if there exists a collineation of PG(n −1, q) mapping D1 onto D2.
A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of an (h −1)-spread in PG(n −1, q) is
that h divides n, as shown by Segre [12]. Accordingly, we write n = hk for some positive integer
k. A relevant class of spreads is given by the Desarguesian spreads, which can be described as
follows; see [12]. Let Σ ≃PG(kh−1, q) be embedded as a q-order subgeometry of PG(kh−1, qh) in
such a way that Σ coincides with the set of fixed points Σ = Fix(Ψ) of a semilinear collineation
Ψ of PG(kh −1, qh) of order h. For any point P ∈PG(kh −1, qh), define
X∗(P) := ⟨P, PΨ, . . . , PΨh−1⟩qh.

Segre varieties
Let k, h ≥1. The Segre map

We may represent the points of PG(kh −1, q) by the homogeneous coordinates
(x11, x12, . . . , x1h; x21, . . . , x2h; . . . ; xk1, . . . , xkh).
In this way, to each point of PG(kh −1, q) we can naturally associate a k × h matrix (xij), with
1 ≤i ≤k and 1 ≤j ≤h. Consider the family of quadrics whose defining equations are given
by the 2 × 2 minors of this matrix. It can be shown that the Segre variety Sk−1,h−1(q) coincides
with the intersection of all quadrics defined in this way, see [5, Theorem 4.94]. Equivalently, the
points of the Segre variety Sk−1,h−1(q) are precisely those whose coordinates form a matrix (xij)
of rank 1, see [5, Theorem 4.101].
The variety Sk−1,h−1(q) contains two distinguished families of maximal subspaces. Fixing a
point of PG(k −1, q) and letting the point of PG(h −1, q) vary, we obtain an (h −1)-dimensional
subspace entirely contained in Sk−1,h−1(q). As the point of PG(k−1, q) varies, the resulting family
of pairwise disjoint (h −1)-dimensional subspaces forms a so called (h −1)-regulus Rh,q of the
Segre variety Sk−1,h−1(q). Analogously, fixing a point of PG(h −1, q) and varying the point in
PG(k −1, q) yields a (k −1)-dimensional subspace of Sk−1,h−1(q). These subspaces form another
family, called a (k−1)-regulus Rk,q of Sk−1,h−1(q). Distinct subspaces belonging to the same regulus
are mutually disjoint, whereas any two subspaces from different reguli intersect in exactly one
point. Moreover, every maximal subspace contained in Sk−1,h−1(q) belongs to exactly one of
these two reguli. For further details, we refer the reader to [4, 5].

2.3
A preliminary result
In this section, we investigate a higher-dimensional analogue of [11, Theorem 1.5]. More pre-
cisely, we study subsets of points of PG(k −1, qh) satisfying a closure property with respect to
q-order subgeometries, and we show that such sets necessarily arise from a subgeometry over
a subfield. In [11] the authors prove the following.
Theorem 2.1 (see [11, Theorem 1.5]). Assume that q > 2. If S is a set of at least three points of
PG(1, qh), such that any three points of S determine a q-order subline entirely contained in S, then S
defines a qr-order subline of PG(1, qh) for some divisor r of h.
A set of k + 1 points of PG(k −1, qh) is said to be in general position if no k of them lie in
a hyperplane. Note that k + 1 points of PG(k −1, qh) in general position determine a unique
q-order subgeometry of PG(k−1, qh). Here, we are interested in a generalization of Theorem 2.1.
Precisely, we consider S to be a set containing k + 1 points of PG(k −1, qh), with q > 2, in general
position, such that for any choice of k + 1 points of S in general position, the unique q-order
subgeometry determined by them is entirely contained in S.
