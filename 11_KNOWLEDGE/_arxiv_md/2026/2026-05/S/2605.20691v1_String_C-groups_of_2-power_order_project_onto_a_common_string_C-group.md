STRING C-GROUPS OF 2-POWER ORDER PROJECT ONTO A
COMMON STRING C-GROUP
DONG-DONG HOU AND
EGON SCHULTE
Abstract. String C-groups are precisely the automorphism groups of abstract regular
polytopes. A certain regular d-polytope Cd with an automorphism group of order 22d−1,
discovered by Conder and shown to have the smallest number of flags among all regular d-
polytopes of high ranks, also has the important extremal property to be the unique minimal
d-polytope, with respect to combinatorial covering, among all finite regular d-polytopes
with 2-power automorphism groups. In other words, the automorphism group of Cd is a
quotient group of every finite string C-group of rank d and 2-power order; and every finite
regular d-polytope with an automorphism groups of 2-power order covers Cd. The existence
of a unique minimal element among string C-groups of 2-power order and given rank is
remarkable in itself.
Key Words: C-group, String C-group, Regular polytope, Automorphism group, 2-Group
MSC Subject Classification (2020): 20B25, 52B15, 51M20
1. Introduction
String C-groups arise naturally as automorphism groups of abstract regular polytopes.
Abstract polytopes are ranked combinatorial structures with distinctive geometric, algebraic,
or topological characteristics. They have attracted considerable attention over the past few
decades (McMullen & Schulte [22], Pellicer [24]).
A large body of research in this area
concerns the classification and analysis of regular, chiral, or other highly symmetric abstract
polytopes, usually with a focus on the polytopes themselves.
A separate direction of research begins with the groups and describes the regular, chiral,
or other symmetric polytopes whose automorphism group is of a given type; for example, a
symmetric group [5], an alternating group [4, 7], or another almost simple group such as a
projective linear group [2, 3, 21].
The present paper investigates string C-groups whose order is a power of 2. These groups
are precisely the automorphism groups of regular polytopes whose number of flags is a power
of 2. Our work continues a line of inquiry, initiated by an open problem suggested in [25] but
properly started only by Hou, Feng, Leemans and Qu [13, 14, 15, 16, 17], as well as Gomi,
Loyola and De Las Penas [12] for special cases, and further developed by Cunningham, Feng,
Hou and Schulte [10].
Our goal is to establish the following two theorems, which are essentially equivalent. Let
Cd denote the small regular d-polytope of type {4, . . . , 4} with an automorphism group of
order 22d−1 discovered in Conder [6]. Our theorems highlight important extremal properties
of Cd.

2. Basic Notions
In this section we briefly review some background about string C-groups and abstract
regular polytopes. For more details the reader is referred to [22, Chs. 2, 3].
String C-groups of rank d are precisely the automorphism groups are abstract regular
polytopes of rank d. An (abstract) polytope of rank d is a ranked partially ordered set P
consisting of faces of ranks −1, 0, . . . , d. The faces of rank j are called j-faces, and the faces
of ranks 0, 1 or d −1 are called vertices, edges or facets, respectively. There are a unique
least face F−1 (of rank −1) and a unique greatest face Fd (of rank d) in P. A flag is a
maximal totally ordered subset (chain) of P. Each flag (maximal totally ordered subset) has
one face for each rank, including F−1 and Fd. Two flags are said to be adjacent if they differ
in exactly one face; they are called j-adjacent if this is a j-face. A polytope P is strongly
flag-connected, in the sense that for any two flags Φ and Ψ there exists a finite sequence of
successively adjacent flags, each containing Φ ∩Ψ. Finally, P has the diamond property:
whenever F ≤G, with F a (j −1)-face and G a (j + 1)-face for some j, there are exactly
two j-faces H with F ≤H ≤G. Abstract 3-polytopes are also called (abstract) polyhedra
and can be realized topologically as maps on closed surfaces.
If F and G are faces with F ≤G, we call
G/F := {H ∈P | F ≤H ≤G}
a section of P and note that this is a (k −j −1)-polytope in its own right if F has rank
j and G has rank k. A face F naturally gives rise to two sections, namely, F/F−1 usually
identified with F, and Fd/F called the co-face of P at F, or vertex-figure of P at F if F is
a vertex.
A polytope P is called regular if its automorphism group is transitive on the flags. Every
regular polytope has regular facets and vertex-figures, and more generally, sections. A regular
d-polytope P is said to be of (Schl¨afli) type {p1, . . . , pd−1} if, for each i = 1, . . . , d −1, each
section lying between an (i −2)-face and an incident (i + 1)-face is isomorphic to a pi-gon
(possibly pi = ∞).

groups of regular polytopes.

universal regular d-polytope {p1, . . . , pd−1} (see [22, Ch. 3D]).

incident with each of its l-faces. In a (0, d −1)-flat polytope, any vertex is incident with any
facet. We simply refer to a (0, d −1)-flat polytope as a flat polytope.
3. The smallest regular polytopes
In this section, we briefly summarize the results of Conder [6] on the smallest regular
polytopes of a given rank.
For a regular d-polytope P of a given Schl¨afli type {p1, . . . , pd−1}, there is a lower bound
for the order of the automorphism group Γ(P) given by
(4)
|Γ(P)| ≥2p1p2 · . . . · pd−1.
As the order of the automorphism group of a regular polytope coincides with the number
of its flags, the term on the right hand side is also a lower bound for the total number of
flags in P. Regular polytopes which attain this lower bound are called tight. Tight regular
polytopes have the smallest number of automorphisms (or flags) among all regular polytopes

4. String C-group of order 2n as extensions
In this section we exploit Frattini subgroups and agemo subgroups of finite p-groups to
establish the remarkable fact that among the finite regular d-polytopes whose automorphism
group are 2-groups there exists a unique minimal element with respect to combinatorial
covering, namely the regular d-polytope Cd of [6] described in the previous section.
Recall that the Frattini subgroup of a finite group G, denoted Φ(G), is defined as the
intersection of all maximal subgroups of G. Clearly, Φ(G) is a proper, characteristic subgroup
of G. Note that Φ(G) consists precisely of the elements x of G that are redundant in any
generating set for G; that is, if X is a generating set of G containing x, then X \ {x} is also
a generating set of G.
