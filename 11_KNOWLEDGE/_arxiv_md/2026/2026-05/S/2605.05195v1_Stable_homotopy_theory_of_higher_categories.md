Abstract. Stable homotopy theory is governed by the principle that after inverting loop spaces,
homotopy types become the representing objects for homology theories. We show that this principle
extends to higher category theory: inverting endomorphism categories leads to a stable homotopy
theory of higher categories, in which higher categories play the role of spaces and categorical spectra
represent homology theories of higher categories. Classical stable homotopy theory is recovered by
inverting morphisms.
While several fundamental features of classical stable homotopy theory persist in this setting,
new phenomena arise from categorical dimension. In particular, stabilization is realized by spectrum
objects, and the passage from unstable to stable homotopy theory is controlled within a stable range.
Our main result is a categorical Brown representability theorem classifying categorical homology
theories by categorical spectra. As a consequence, categorical homology theories give rise to long exact
sequences and support a higher-categorical homological algebra. As an application, we construct the
derived category of a rig, extending homological algebra beyond additive contexts.

1. Introduction
Motivation. Stable homotopy theory is governed by a fundamental principle: after stabilization, ho-
motopy types become the representing objects for homology theories.
We show that this principle is not specific to topology but reflects a structural feature of higher cat-
egory theory: inverting endomorphism categories leads to spectra of higher categories which represent
homology theories of higher categories.
Classically, spectra admit several complementary characterizations: they provide the universal sta-
bilization of spaces, encode stable homotopy controlled within a stable range, are the derived counter-
parts of abelian groups, and represent homology theories. We show that these fundamental aspects of
spectra persist in higher category theory:
(1) spectra of higher categories provide the universal stabilization of higher categories 5.2.49,
(2) they are the derived counterparts of commutative monoids 5.3.9,
(3) they encode stabilized higher categorical information controlled within a stable range (cate-
gorical Freudenthal suspension theorem 5.4.6),
(4) they represent homology theories of higher categories (categorical Brown representability the-
orem 6.5.3), which is the main structural result of this work.
These properties characterize categorical spectra as the higher-categorical counterpart of spectra in
topology.
While several fundamental structures of classical stable homotopy theory persist in higher category
theory, new phenomena arise from categorical dimension, which have no classical analogue. A funda-
mental difference with the classical situation is that delooping increases categorical dimension. As a
consequence, delooping is not a functor enriched in the cartesian product of ∞-categories, which fixes
categorical dimension, but only a functor enriched in the Gray tensor product of ∞-categories, which
behaves additive with respect to dimension. It follows that ∞-categories, which are inherently enriched
in the cartesian product, cannot accommodate a stable homotopy theory of higher categories, and must
be replaced by categories enriched in the Gray tensor product, which we call oriented categories [8].
Stable oriented categories. Oriented categories admit intrinsic notions of fiber and cofiber sequences,
called oriented fiber and cofiber sequences, that play the role of homotopy fiber and cofiber sequences
in topology, and lead to a natural notion of stability in the higher-categorical setting. Following the
principle that stabilization is obtained by inverting endomorphisms, an oriented category is stable if
every morphism admits an oriented fiber and cofiber, and the operation of endomorphisms is invertible.
The prime example is the stable oriented category CatSp of categorical spectra, obtained by sta-
bilizing the (unstable) oriented category ∞Cat of ∞-categories (Definition 5.2.6). Precisely, CatSp is
the limit of the tower of oriented functors
⋯
ΩÐ→∞Catcop ΩÐ→∞Cat.
Here Ωis the oriented functor of endomorphisms and (−)cop reverses morphisms of dimension larger
than one and reflects the non-symmetry of the Gray tensor product. In other words, a categorical
spectrum consists of a sequence of based ∞-categories {Xn}n≥0 equipped with equivalences
Xn ≃Ω(Xn+1).
Stable oriented categories exhibit several characteristic features of classical stable categories in
the higher-categorical setting. For instance, categorical spectra carry a canonical monoidal structure
[23] analogous to the classical smash product, which is the stabilization of the Gray tensor product
(Theorem 5.2.35), and stable oriented categories are uniquely enriched in categorical spectra (Corol-
lary 6.4.17, Theorem 6.7.4).

While classical homology theories depend only on the classifying space of an ∞-category, categorical
homology theories detect genuinely higher-categorical structure. For instance, categorical homology
theories distinguish globes of different dimension whose classifying spaces are contractible.
A fundamental property of classical homology is that cofiber sequences give rise to long exact se-
quences on homology. This property is a key principle in computations: spaces are built by inductively
attaching cells and homology transforms these cell attachments into long exact sequences. We show
an analogue for categorical homology theories.
The present work develops the structural foundations of a stable homotopy theory of higher cate-
gories. In [17] we apply this framework to the categorical homology theory represented by the categor-
ical Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum of the natural numbers. This leads to higher-categorical analogues
of the Dold–Kan correspondence and the Dold–Thom theorem.
Organization of the paper. The paper is organized as follows. Section 3 recalls the foundations of
higher category theory used throughout the paper, including ∞-categories and enriched categories.
Section 4 develops the basic unstable homotopy theory of higher categories, introducing oriented
categories, oriented pullbacks, categorical analogues of spheres and suspension.
Section 5 develops the stable homotopy theory of higher categories. We introduce stable oriented
categories, and show that spectrum objects in oriented categories form the universal stabilization.
Moreover we construct a tensor product of presentable bistable bioriented categories, a generalization
of oriented categories which remedies the antisymmetry of the Gray tensor product. Moreover we
prove a categorical Freudenthal suspension theorem.
Section 6 introduces categorical homology theories, homological algebra in stable oriented categories,
and establishes a categorical Brown representability theorem via a theory of higher-categorical excisive
approximations and a theory of spectrification.

3. Recollection on higher categories
In this section we recall the basic higher category theory needed for our work.
3.1. Enriched categories. We recall the theory of (homotopy coherent) enriched categories [7], [11],
[15], [18].
For every presentably monoidal category V there is a presentable 2-category V−Cat of V-enriched
categories and V-enriched functors and a forgetful functor ι ∶V−Cat →Cat to the presentable 2-
category Cat of categories.
For V = ∞Grp the category of homotopy types, the forgetful functor
ι ∶∞Grp−Cat →Cat is an equivalence by [14, Corollary 3.23.].

5. Stable homotopy theory of higher categories
In this section we develop a stable homotopy theory of higher categories.
We introduce stable
oriented categories, a higher-categorical analogues of classical stable categories, which provide the
natural framework for stability phenomena in higher category theory. Stable oriented categories are
dual to antistable antioriented categories.
The universal example is provided by categorical spectra (Definition 5.2.6), which form a bistable
bioriented category, a compatible stable oriented category and antistable antioriented category. We
show that categorical spectra arise as the stabilization of the bioriented category of ∞-categories
(Proposition 5.2.31). Categorical spectra are an example of spectrum objects in oriented categories
and their variants, which we exhibit as stabilizations in this framework (Theorem 5.2.49).
We construct a monoidal structure on the category of presentable bistable bioriented categories
(Theorem 5.2.35), which is a higher-categorical analogue of Lurie’s tensor product of stable presentable
categories. The tensor unit is the presentable bistable bioriented category of categorical spectra, which
therefore inherits a monoidal structure, the higher-categorical analogue of the smash product, which
was also constructed by Masuda [23, Theorem 4.2.1.].
As the main result of this section (Theorem 5.4.6), we prove a categorical version of the Freudenthal
suspension theorem, which governs the passage from unstable to stable homotopy theory of higher
categories.
5.1. Categorical stability. In the following we introduce stable oriented categories and their variants,

6. A categorical Brown representability theorem
In this section we prove a categorical version of the homological Brown representability theorem:
every categorical homology theory is represented by a unique categorical spectrum, the categorical
coefficient spectrum of the categorical homology theory.
To prove this classification result, we reconstruct a categorical homology theory from its coefficients.
Every reduced oriented functor from based ∞-categories to based ∞-categories, gives rise to a categor-
ical prespectrum of coefficients. The latter is a categorical spectrum if and only if the reduced oriented
functor is excisive.
We reconstruct a categorical homology theory from its coefficients via the following two methods:
(1) a theory of prespectrum objects and spectrification.
(2) a theory of excisive approximation of oriented functors.
In this section we define categorical homology theories, which are a categorical analogue of derived
homology theories.
We define categorical homology theories in terms of a categorical analogue of
excision.
We show that every categorical spectrum represents a categorical homology theory and consider
the categorical homology theory represented by the categorical Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum of a
commutative monoid, which we call categorical homology with coefficients in that commutative monoid.
We introduce oriented exact sequences, a higher-categorical analogue of long exact sequences. We
prove that oriented cofiber sequences give rise to oriented exact sequences in any spectral oriented
category that admits oriented cofibers and oriented fibers (Corollary 6.6.5). We prove that oriented
fibers detect equivalences in any spectral oriented category (Theorem 6.6.11), which makes oriented
exact sequences a useful tool to inductively detect equivalences (Corollary 6.6.15).
We will connect categorical homology with oriented exact sequences: we prove that oriented cofiber
sequences give rise to oriented exact sequences on categorical homology (Corollary 6.6.6).
