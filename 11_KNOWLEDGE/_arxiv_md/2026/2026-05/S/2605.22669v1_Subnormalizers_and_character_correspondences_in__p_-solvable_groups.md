p-SOLVABLE GROUPS
GABRIEL A. L. SOUZA
Abstract. A new family of local-global conjectures in the representation theory
of finite groups has recently been proposed by Moretó. We show that one of the
strongest of these conjectures, the strong subnormalizer conjecture, holds for p-
solvable groups when p is odd, under the condition that the subnormalizer subset
is a subgroup. We also prove it in general when p is odd and the p-length of the
group is 1 and, in the process, obtain new properties related to the Glauberman
correspondence.
1. Introduction
Recently, Moretó [12] has proposed a new family of conjectures which provide
a different framework for viewing some local-global problems in the representation
theory of finite groups. Let G be a finite group, let p be a prime and let x ∈G be a
p-element. We write Irrx(G) for the set of irreducible complex characters of G which
are non-zero when evaluated at x. We call a p-element picky if it lies in a unique
Sylow p-subgroup. One of the formulations of one of the main proposed conjectures
(see [12, Conjecture A]) is as follows:
Conjecture (Picky conjecture). Let G be a finite group, let p be a prime and let
x ∈G be a picky p-element.
Let P ∈Sylp(G) contain x.
Then, there exists a
bijection
f : Irrx(G) →Irrx(NG(P))
such that, for all χ ∈Irrx(G), χ(1)p = f(χ)(1)p and Q(f(χ)(x)) = Q(χ(x)).
In fact, for many classes of groups, we can actually find a bijection f as above
satisfying the stronger condition f(χ)(x) = ϵχχ(x) for a sign ϵχ ∈{1, −1}. In this
case, we say G satisfies the strong picky conjecture.

There is, in fact, a generalization of the picky conjecture, which also appears in [12]
and was studied in [8] and [11]: the subnormalizer conjecture. If x ∈G is a p-element,
we define the subnormalizer subset of x in G as SG(x) = {y ∈G | ⟨x⟩◁◁⟨x, y⟩}. This
is, in general, not a subgroup of G, and so we define the subnormalizer of x in G
as SubG(x) = ⟨SG(x)⟩. It is the case that a p-element x ∈G is picky if and only if
SubG(x) = NG(P), where x ∈P and P ∈Sylp(G). This suggested that the following
conjecture, [12, Conjecture B], could be true:
Conjecture (Subnormalizer conjecture). Let G be a finite group, let p be a prime
and let x ∈G be a p-element. Then, there exists a bijection
f : Irrx(G) →Irrx(SubG(x))
such that, for all χ ∈Irrx(G), χ(1)p = f(χ)(1)p and Q(f(χ)(x)) = Q(χ(x)).
As was the case with the picky conjecture, for many groups, such an f can be
found satisfying the stronger condition f(χ)(x) = ϵχχ(x) for some sign ϵχ ∈{1, −1},
in which case we say that G satisfies the strong subnormalizer conjecture.
This conjecture appears to be very deep, and there are very few classes of groups
for which it is known to hold. By work done in [11], it holds for symmetric groups
for p = 2; it is also known for some elements in groups of Lie type of small rank [8]
or with abelian Sylow p-subgroups [9].
It is on this conjecture that we focus our efforts, in the case of p-solvable groups
for p ̸= 2. Our main result is as follows:
Theorem A. Let G be a p-solvable group, for p ̸= 2, and let x ∈G be a p-element.
Assume that ⟨x⟩◁◁SubG(x). Then, there exist a bijection
f : Irrx(G) →Irrx(SubG(x))
such that f(χ)(1)p = χ(1)p and f(χ)(x) = ϵχχ(x), for some sign ϵχ ∈{1, −1}
The condition ⟨x⟩◁◁SubG(x) is satisfied precisely when SG(x) = SubG(x); that
is, when SG(x) is a subgroup of G. This is because, by definition, SG(x) contains
all subgroups in which ⟨x⟩is subnormal. Notice how the strong picky conjecture for
p-solvable groups with p ̸= 2 follows from Theorem A, since ⟨x⟩is always subnormal
in the normalizer of a Sylow p-subgroup containing it. We also provide a couple
of constructions to obtain elements satisfying this condition. Theorem A is thus a
substantial generalization of [13, Theorem A].
Our general strategy for proving Theorem A follows that of [13]. However, most of
the proof must employ significantly new ideas, adapted to this more general setting.
For p-solvable groups of p-length one – and, in particular, those with a normal
p-complement – we are able to go a step further and completely prove the strong
subnormalizer conjecture for p ̸= 2, under no hypotheses on x.

In order to prove Theorems A and B, we will need to study generalizations of
the Glauberman correspondence and properties thereof. More specifically, we will
need to control inertia groups in contexts where these generalized correspondences
are defined. When returning to the context of the Glauberman correspondence, we
obtain the following result, which, as far as we are aware, has not yet appeared in

Theorem C. Suppose A acts on a finite group G by automorphisms. Let B◁◁A be

4. Normal p-complement

Lemma 4.1. Suppose G has a normal p-complement N, and let x ∈G be a p-element.
