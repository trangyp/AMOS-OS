Superspecial plane quintics with large automorphism groups
Ryo Ohashi
May 29, 2026
Abstract
In this paper, we study plane quintic curves whose automorphism groups have order greater than 10,
as well as those with cyclic automorphism groups of order 8 and 10. The latter two cases are represented
as one-parameter families, where their superspeciality can be explicitly described in terms of a truncation
of certain Gaussian hypergeometric series. Applying this characterization, we determine the exact number
of isomorphism classes of superspecial plane quintic curves with automorphism groups ∼= Z/10Z. We also
provide an efficient algorithm to enumerate such curves with automorphism groups ∼= Z/8Z, and provide
the computational results for the range 13 < p < 10000.

Introduction
Throughout this paper, a curve always means a non-singular projective variety of dimension one defined over
a field of characteristic p > 0. A curve is said to be superspecial if its Jacobian is isomorphic to a product of
supersingular elliptic curves. Superspecial curves remain an important subject of study in algebraic geometry
and number theory, with many open problems regarding their existence and the number of their isomorphism
classes. In recent years, they have also attracted attention because of their applications in cryptography and
coding theory, since they often have many rational points relative to their genus.
It is well known that every plane quartic curve has genus 3 and is non-hyperelliptic, and conversely, every
non-hyperelliptic curve of genus 3 can be realized as a plane quartic curve. Several studies have investigated
the superspeciality of plane quartic curves. Oort [16, Theorem 5.12(1)] showed that there exists a superspecial
plane quartic curve in any characteristic p ≥3. His proof focused on a special family of plane quartic curves
called Ciani curves, whose Jacobians decompose as products of three elliptic curves. Also, Moriya-Kudo [11]
proposed an algorithm for enumerating superspecial Ciani curves. Another characterization of a Ciani curve
is as a plane quartic curve with an automorphism group containing a subgroup isomorphic to (Z/2Z)2. The
possible automorphism groups of plane quartic curves in characteristic p ≥5 are classified into 13 types (the
explicit list of these groups is found in [3, Theorem 6.5.2]). Building on this classification, Brock [2] studied
superspecial plane quartic curves for each of these types. In particular, the superspeciality for the types with
0-dimensional families is determined as follows:
• The Klein quartic X3Y + Y 3Z + Z3X = 0 with an automorphism group of order 168 is superspecial if
and only if p ≡3, 5, 6 (mod 7).
• The Fermat quartic X4 + Y 4 + Z4 = 0 with an automorphism group of order 96 is superspecial if and
only if p ≡3 (mod 4).
• The plane quartic X4 + Y 4 + Y Z3 = 0 with an automorphism group of order 48 is superspecial if and
only if p ≡11 (mod 12).
• The plane quartic X4 + XY 3 + Y Z3 = 0 with cyclic automorphism group of order 9 is superspecial if
and only if p ≡8 (mod 9).
Also, there are 3 types which admit 1-dimensional families. Among them, those whose automorphism groups
have order 24 or 16 are Ciani curves; their superspeciality can be completely described by the supersingularity
of elliptic curves (the explicit equations of them are found in [10, Sections 4–5]). The remaining type consists
of curves with cyclic automorphism groups of order 6, for which it is known (cf. [15]) that there exist exactly
⌊p/12⌋superspecial curves if p ≡5 (mod 6), whereas no such curves exist if p ≡1 (mod 6).

This paper focuses on plane quintic curves, all of which have genus 6 and are non-hyperelliptic. Note that,
in contrast to plane quartic curves, there exist non-hyperelliptic curves of genus 6 that are not isomorphic to
any plane quintic curve (cf. [5, Example IV.5.6]). Badr-Bars [1] classified the possible automorphism groups
of plane quintic curves in characteristic p > 13 into 14 types, and gave an explicit defining equation for each
type. Among these 14 types, those with 0-dimensional families are as follows:
• The Fermat quintic X5 + Y 5 + Z5 = 0 with an automorphism group of order 150.
• The Hurwitz quintic X4Y + Y 4Z + Z4X = 0 with an automorphism group of order 39.
• The plane quintic X5 + Y 4Z + Y Z4 = 0 with an automorphism group of order 30.
• The plane quintic X5 + Y 5 + XZ4 = 0 with cyclic automorphism group of order 20.
• The plane quintic X5 + Y 4Z + XZ4 = 0 with cyclic automorphism group of order 16.

(3.12)
Indeed, by the discussion at the beginning of Section 3.3 and the relation (3.8),
• If λ = −1, then the automorphism group of Cλ is isomorphic to Z/16Z. In this case Cλ corresponds to
the plane quintic of Type 5, which is superspecial if and only if p ≡15 (mod 16) by Proposition 3.2(3).
• Otherwise, the automorphism group of Cλ is isomorphic to Z/8Z.
By Lemma 3.8 and the relation (3.8), we have Cλ ∼= Cλ′ if and only if λ′ ∈{λ, 1/λ}. Therefore, the number
of isomorphism classes of superspecial curves Cλ with Aut(Cλ) ∼= Z/8Z is half of (3.12), as desired.
Thanks to the above theorem, we can determine the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial plane
quintic curves with cyclic automorphism group of order 8 by G(λ), which can be computed in eO(p) operations
over Fp by an argument similar to the proof of [14, Theorem 3.2.9]. Finally, we implemented our algorithm
in Magma for explicitly computing the number. We ran it on a machine equipped with an AMD EPYC 7742
CPU and 2TB of RAM in every characteristic 13 < p < 10000. The experimental results for p ≡7 (mod 8)

From Table 3, we can observe that the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial plane quintic curves
with automorphism groups ∼= Z/8Z seems to be irregular (recall from Theorem 1.1 that they are unlike those
of superspecial plane quintic curves with automorphism groups ∼= Z/10Z). On the other hand, we found that
there is no such a curve in the case where p ̸≡7 (mod 8) and 13 < p < 10000. which completes the proof of
Theorem 1.2.
4
Concluding remarks
The results of Theorem 1.2 for the cases p ≡1, 3, 5 (mod 8) lead us to formulate the following expectation:
Expectation 4.1. If p ̸≡7 (mod 8), then there is no superspecial plane quintic curve with cyclic automor-
phism group of order 8 in characteristic p.
This seems to arise from the fact that, when p ̸≡7 (mod 8), there are more equations that λ must satisfy by
Proposition 3.10, but a theoretical proof remains a topic for future work. It is also an interesting problem to
investigate superspecial plane quintic curves with automorphism groups other than those in Table 1. In that
case, we expect to apply multivariable hypergeometric series, as discussed in [13].
