Gravitational memory provides a distinctive low-frequency probe of gravity, but explicit merger
studies beyond general relativity remain limited. In this work, we investigate memory from binary
black hole mergers in Ricci-coupled scalar-Gauss-Bonnet gravity, a natural extension of scalar-
Gauss-Bonnet theory that admits an additional scalar breathing polarization. Based on numerical-
relativity waveforms of binary black hole coalescences, we show that the change in the scalar charge
of the system across merger generates a significant scalar-memory contribution. For a GW150914-
like system, this effect modifies the memory signal in a gravitational-wave detector on the same
observable timescale and by an amount comparable to the pure scalar-Gauss-Bonnet correction to
tensor memory. Thus, it can substantially enhance the total deviation from the general-relativity
prediction over a broad range of source and detector configurations. We argue that this identifies
a general mechanism: whenever a compact-binary merger changes the asymptotic charge of an
additional gravitational field, and that field sources an observable extra polarization, the resulting
memory can provide a leading low-frequency signature of new gravitational physics.
I.
INTRODUCTION
With the rapid development of gravitational-wave as-
tronomy, compact-binary coalescences have become pre-
cision probes of strong-field gravity [1–3].
Future de-
tectors, including Einstein Telescope [4], Cosmic Ex-
plorer [5], LISA [6] and TianQin [7], will considerably
extend both the number of detectable events and the pre-
cision with which new signatures of gravitational physics
can be tested. Among such signatures, the gravitational
memory effect is especially interesting. Memory corre-
sponds to a permanent displacement of freely falling test
masses after the passage of a burst of gravitational radi-
ation [8–15]. In general relativity, the dominant nonlin-
ear displacement memory is sourced by radiative energy
flux reaching null infinity and is closely related to asymp-
totic Bondi-Metzner-Sachs (BMS) symmetries and soft
theorems [16–23]. Although its low-frequency character
makes detection challenging [24–30], the prospects for a
first measurement in next-generation detectors are en-
couraging. This raises a natural question: how can grav-
itational memory be used to test gravity beyond general
relativity?
A general theoretical framework for memory beyond
GR has recently been developed for Horndeski gravity
and more general scalar-vector-tensor effective field the-

Numerical relativity waveforms

Instead, it changes how
the scalar degree of freedom enters physical observables
through matter interactions when transforming back to
the Jordan frame. Therefore, for vacuum BBH mergers
it is consistent to use the sGB simulations to describe
the underlying tensor and scalar radiation, and to in-

a post-processing step at the level of the detector re-

means that we use the same simulated tensor and scalar

gravity, and isolate the effect of the additional breathing
polarization in RCsGB through the Jordan-frame detec-
tor response in Eq. (27), similar to previous studies of
black hole mergers in Ricci-coupled sGB [65].
In this
way, the comparison between sGB and RCsGB cleanly
captures the impact of the non-minimal matter coupling

underlying strong-field merger dynamics.

V.
A GENERAL BEYOND GR MEMORY
MECHANISM
The RCsGB example discussed above points to a more
general lesson. The observable memory from a compact-
binary coalescence is not controlled only by the modi-
fication of the tensor null-memory kernel. Whenever a
merger changes the asymptotic charge of an additional
gravitational degree of freedom, and whenever that de-
gree of freedom enters the physical detector response
through an extra polarization channel, the merger can
generate an additional memory term that competes di-
rectly with the conventional beyond-GR correction to
tensor memory. After using the RCsGB case to isolate
this mechanism analytically we then formulate in this
section a corresponding conjecture of generality.

VI.
CONCLUSION
Any theory satisfying the conditions (i)–(iii) identi-
fied in Sec. V B should exhibit the same qualitative phe-
nomenon: a merger-induced jump in the charge of an
additional gravitational degree of freedom, when com-
bined with an observable extra polarization channel, can
produce a potentially leading beyond-GR correction to
the memory signal.
In this work, we have demonstrated this mechanism
explicitly in Ricci-coupled scalar-Gauss-Bonnet gravity
which allows for the emission of an additional gravita-
tional scalar polarization in the radiation, namely the
breathing mode, contrary to the pure sGB theory. Us-
ing numerical-relativity waveforms from the correspond-
ing vacuum shift-symmetric sGB theory and incorporat-
ing the non-minimal Ricci coupling at the level of the
physical detector response, we showed that the change in
scalar charge across merger generates an additional scalar
memory contribution in the breathing channel.
For a
GW150914-like system and values of the Ricci coupling
compatible with current weak-field bounds, this contri-
bution alters the observable memory by an amount com-
parable to the pure sGB correction to tensor memory it-
self, and in favorable regions of the parameter space can
substantially enhance the total beyond-GR deviation.
This has two immediate implications. First, beyond-
GR memory from compact-binary coalescences cannot,
in general, be characterized only by modifications of
tensor null memory. Once additional polarizations are
present, the corresponding scalar memory must also be
included in any consistent interpretation of the detector
response in future memory-based tests of gravity. Sec-
ond, through its distinctive dependence on the binary
inclination and on the detector sensitivity to different po-
larizations, beyond-GR memory may provide a valuable
complementary probe of additional gravitational degrees
of freedom.
The scalar-memory mechanism emphasized here is con-
ceptually distinct from the usual tensor-memory effect,
since a direct change in a non-conserved scalar charge
is, at present, not known to arise from an asymptotic
BMS flux-balance law or to be associated with a corre-
sponding soft theorem in the same way as tensor memory.
This makes it interesting not only from the observational
point of view, but also from the perspective of the asymp-
totic structure of gravity beyond GR. It would therefore
be important in future work both to quantify this effect

models, and to clarify its place within the broader in-
