Abstract. The Solomon–Tits theorem says that the poset of proper non-trivial subspaces
of a finite-dimensional vector space has realisation equivalent to a wedge of spheres. In this
paper we prove a variant of this result for collections of geodesic subspaces of Euclidean,
hyperbolic, or spherical geometry, assuming the collection is generated either by points or
by hyperplanes. In the third paper of this series of papers, we will combine this with the
homological stability theorems from the first paper to compute the homology of groups
of scissors automorphisms in these geometries.

1. Introduction
For a field k, the Tits building T(kn) is the geometric realisation of the poset of non-zero
proper linear subspaces of kn, ordered by inclusion. The Solomon–Tits theorem says that
this space is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of (n −2)-spheres and its reduced homology in
degree (n −2) is a free abelian group generated by “apartment” classes (see [Sol69, Theorem
2] and [Bro98, Chapter IV.5, Theorem 2]).
In this paper we prove generalisations of the Solomon–Tits theorem for Tits complexes
TL(Xn) that are constructed using a collection L of complete geodesic subspaces in Euclidean,
hyperbolic, or spherical geometry Xn ∈{En, Hn, Sn}. For k a subfield of R, the Tits building
is an example: there is an isomorphism TL(Sn−1) ∼= T(kn) when we let L be the collection of
geodesic subspaces of Sn−1 that arise as intersections Sn−1 ∩V of the sphere with a non-zero
proper linear subspace V ⊆Rn admitting a basis in kn ⊆Rn. Our interest in such geometric
variants of the Solomon–Tits theorem originates in the study of scissors congruence—this is
the second paper in a series on this topic [Kup+24; Kup+26]—but before explaining this in
Section 1.3 we will state our results precisely.

1.2. Solomon–Tits theorems and polytope groups. Our goal is to give conditions
under which the complexes of Definition 1.1 are equivalent to wedges of n-spheres, and
then give a presentation of the nth reduced homology group—the analogue of the Steinberg
module—equivariantly for the action of subgroups GL ≤I(Xn) (or GL ≤A(En)) preserving
L set-wise. We shall consider two types of conditions on L; to state them, we let Lk ⊆L
denote the subset of geometric subspaces of dimension k, so that L0 ⊆L is the collection of
points in L (or, by convention, antipodal pairs of points in the spherical case), and Ln−1 ⊆L
is the collection of hyperplanes in L.
Definition 1.2.
(i) We say L is generated by points if it is equal to the set of all possible spans of finite
collections of points in L0, other than ∅. (This implies that L0 contains (n + 1)
points in general position, since Xn ∈L.)
(ii) We say L is generated by hyperplanes if it is equal to the set of all possible intersections
of finitely many hyperplanes in Ln−1, other than ∅. (This need not imply that Ln−1
has (n + 1) hyperplanes in general position; Xn ∈L can be obtained as the empty

is the linear span in Rn+1, intersected with Xn to give a geometric subspace of Xn.

1.3. Connection to scissors congruence and scissors automorphism groups. Our
interest in geometric variants of the Solomon–Tits theorem—Theorem A and Theorem B—
originates in the subject of scissors congruence, which considers polytopes in a geometry
Xn up to moves that cut them into finitely many pieces and then rearrange the pieces. For
a fixed polytope P ⊆Xn, these cut-and-paste transformations define a group Aut(P), the
scissors automorphism group of P. Examples include the groups of invertible piecewise
isometries considered previously in the dynamics literature, see e.g. [AG06; BP07; CAR25].

2. Generation by points
In this section we prove Theorem A. This says that STL(Xn) is equivalent to a wedge of
n-spheres and that LsL(Xn) ∼= eHn(STL(Xn)), if L is generated by points—that is, L is equal

if L generated by points, our assumption that Xn ∈L is thus equivalent to assuming that
L0 contains (n + 1) points in general position. We begin with this case because the proof
strategy is well-known, see e.g. [Dup01, Theorem 3.5], [CZ24, Corollary A.7 and Theorem
1.20], [Cal22, Lemma 2.2], and [Mal24, Theorem 2.10].
