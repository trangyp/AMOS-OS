1. Introduction
We work over the field of complex numbers C.
The goal of this paper is to prove the following theorem:
Theorem 1.1 (Shokurov’s global index conjecture for threefold foliations). There exists a pos-
itive integer I satisfying the following. Let X be a normal projective variety of dimension ≤3
and F a foliation on X with log canonical singularities such that KF ≡0. Then IKF ∼0.
Theorem 1.1 has the following log version.
Theorem 1.2 (Shokurov’s global index conjecture for threefold foliations, log version). Let
I ⊂[0, 1]∩Q be a DCC set. Then there exists a positive integer I depending only on I satisfying
the following. Let (X, F, B) be an lc projective foliated triple of dimension ≤3 such that
KF + B ≡0
and
B ∈I.
Then I(KF + B) ∼0.
Theorem 1.2 answers Shokurov’s global index conjecture for foliations [LMX24, Conjecture
5.3] in dimension 3. Previously, Theorem 1.2 was known in the following cases:
(1) When F = TX. This corresponds to the classical Shokurov’s global index conjecture in
dimension ≤3 and was proven in [Xu20, Theorem 1.8.7] based on [Jia21, Corollary 1.7].
(2) When B ̸= 0 [LLM23, Theorem 1.1].
(3) When dim X ≤2 [LLM23, Theorem 1.4] based on [Per05, Theorem 1].

It is also worth mentioning that the non-effective version of Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 was proven
in dimension ≤3 or when rank F = 1 in [CS21, Theorem 12.1] and [CS26, Theorem 1.2], and
for algebraically integrable foliations in [DLM23, Theorem 1.4].
Remark 1.3. By following the proofs of the main results in this paper, we actually have the
stronger criterion: Under the conditions of Theorem 1.1, if F is canonical and not algebraically
integrable, then we may take I ≤30. It is, therefore, interesting to classify non-algebraically
integrable canonical Calabi–Yau foliations in dimension ≤3 based on their indices.
Another interesting question to ask is whether the boundedness of complements conjecture for
foliations [LMX24, Conjecture 5.1] holds in dimension 3, which is known to imply Theorem 1.2
immediately [LMX24, Proposition 5.4]. Yet this is a much harder question and is only partially
known in dimension 2 for Fano foliations [CJV24, Proposition 4.1].
Use of generative AI and some ideas of the proof. A substantial part of the proof was
developed with the assistance of the Rethlas system; see [Ju+26] for an introduction to the
system.
The final argument, however, required significant human input, both in supplying
missing references and in reorganizing the output into a rigorous proof.
The algebraically integrable case was already well understood by the first author: in that
setting, KF+B is crepant to the moduli part of a Calabi–Yau fibration (cf. [ACSS21, Proposition
3.5]), and the relevant torsion and effective base-point-freeness statements are available in low
dimensions. The main difficulty lies in the non-algebraically integrable case, especially in the
purely transcendental case.
When the problem was submitted to Rethlas, the system naturally separated the argument
according to the rank, the algebraic rank, and the dimension. It quickly found the reduction to
dimension three via [Per05], but it did not initially locate [Jia21, Xu20]. It also identified the
Lie-theoretic approach to the rank one non-algebraically integrable case and then to the rank
two case with algebraic rank one. After several prompts, the same ideas were adapted to the
rank two purely transcendental case. At that stage the canonical case without boundary was
essentially settled.
The system was less effective in the algebraically integrable case. It did not find the relevant
references [ACSS21, CHLX23, LMX25] until they were supplied by the first author. This reflects
a limitation of the Lie-theoretic strategy: it detects algebraic subfoliations from invariant sub-
groups of automorphism groups, and is therefore most useful when non-algebraic integrability
imposes strong restrictions on the group. From the viewpoint of the minimal model program,
by contrast, the algebraically integrable case is the more standard one.
Several revisions were needed after the initial output. First, Rethlas treated only the canon-
ical case without boundary; the reduction from the log canonical case is carried out in Propo-
sition 3.1, where the ACC for minimal log discrepancies on surfaces [Ale93] is used to close a
small gap. Second, the original treatment of the rank two purely transcendental case repeatedly
appealed to the rank two algebraic rank one case without recording the necessary finite-cover
and equivariance arguments. These details are supplied below in the Lie-theoretic preliminaries
and in Sections 6 and 7.
Due to the limitation of generative AI, it is possible that we have missed some related refer-
ences in the literature, and we welcome any comments from experts.

2. Preliminaries
We adopt the standard notation and terminology for the minimal model program from [Sho92,
KM98, BCHM10] and use them freely. We shall only consider foliations in dimension ≤3 in
this paper and we shall follow the definitions and notations as in [CS21, CS25] for foliations.
Throughout the paper, for a normal variety X we write TX for the tangent sheaf (Ω1
X)∨;
when X is smooth, this is the usual tangent bundle. We do not use TX as it may have notation
conflicts with the torus in Lie theory, which we shall also use in this paper.
Definition 2.1. Let X be a normal quasi-projective variety. A foliation F on X is a saturated
subsheaf F of TX that is closed under the Lie bracket. The rank of F is the rank of F as a
sheaf and is denoted by rank F. The algebraic rank of F is the maximal dimension of a variety
Z such that there exists a dominant rational map f : X 99K Z and a foliation FZ on Z such
that F = f−1FZ, and is denoted by rankalg F.
We say that F is algebraically integrable if rankalg F = rank F. We say that F is purely
transcendental if rankalg F = 0.
Definition 2.2. A foliated triple (X, F, B) consists of a normal quasi-projective variety X, a
foliation F on X, and an R-divisor B on X such that KF + B is R-Cartier.
We will frequently use the following result on abundance for log Calabi–Yau foliated triples
in dimension ≤3 without citing:
Theorem 2.3 ([CS21, Theorem 1.7],[CS26, Theorem 1.2],[LLM23, Theorem 1.8]). Let (X, F, B)
be a projective foliated triple such that dim X ≤3 and KF + B ≡0. Then KF + B ∼R 0.

3. Reduction
The goal of this section is to prove the following proposition which effectively reduces the
question to the case when X is Q-factorial klt of dimension 3, F is canonical and not algebraically
integrable, and B = 0. Classification results in [Dru21, DO22] can be applied after that.
Proposition 3.1. Assume that Theorem 1.2 holds when all the following conditions hold:
X is Q-factorial klt, dim X = 3, F is canonical, B = 0, and 2 ≥rank F > rankalg F ≥0.
Then Theorem 1.2 holds.
Proof. We divide the proof in several steps.
Step 1.
We reduce to the case when (X, F, B) is Q-factorial dlt in the sense of [LLM23,
Definition 5.2]. In particular, in the rest of the proof, we may assume that X is Q-factorial klt.
We may assume that rank F > 0, otherwise the theorem is trivial. We may assume that
rank F < dim X, otherwise the theorem follows from [Xu20, Theorem 1.8.7].
By [LLM23,
Theorem 1.1], we may assume that I is a finite set. Possibly replacing I with I ∪{1}, we
may assume that 1 ∈I. Possibly replacing (X, F, B) with a Q-factorial dlt modification in the
sense of [LLM23, Theorem 5.8], we may assume that (X, F, B) is Q-factorial dlt in the sense of
[LLM23, Definition 5.2].
Step 2. We reduce to the case when F is not algebraically integrable and dim X = 3. If F is
algebraically integrable, then we let g : (Y, FY , BY ; G)/Z →(X, F, B) be a Q-factorial ACSS
modification associated with contraction f : Y →Z [LMX25, Definition 3.3]. We have
(3.1)
KFY + BY = g∗(KF + B) ≡0.
By [LLM23, Proposition 8.1] there exists a positive integer I depending only on I such that
I(KFY + BY ) ∼0, hence I(KF + B) ∼0 and the proposition follows. In the following, we may
assume that F is not algebraically integrable.
By [LLM23, Lemma 7.2] (based on [Per05, Theorem 1]), we may assume that dim X = 3.
Step 3. We reduce to the case when dim X = 3, rank F = 2, and rankalg F = 1.
Assume that F is purely transcendental.
Then we may assume that B ̸= 0 or F is not
canonical. If B ̸= 0, then KF is not pseudo-effective, which contradicts [CP19, Theorem 1.1].
Thus F is not canonical. Let h : X′ →X be a resolution of F which exists as dim X ≤3
(see [Sei68, Can04, MP13], or [LLM23, Theorem 4.5] for a summary). Then KF′ is not pseudo-

part, which is also a contradiction. Thus we may assume that F is not purely transcendental.
Since dim X ≤3 and F is neither purely transcendental nor algebraically integrable, we have
dim X = 3, rank F = 2, and rankalg F = 1.
Step 4. We reduce to the case when B = 0.

Definition 4.2 (Translation-invariant foliation). Let A be a connected algebraic group, and let
W ⊂Lie A be a linear subspace. We write TA for the tangent bundle of the smooth variety A.
Left-invariant vector fields give the canonical trivialization
TA ∼= Lie A ⊗C OA.
Under this trivialization, W defines a locally free subsheaf
GW := W ⊗C OA ⊂TA.
If W is closed under the Lie bracket, then GW is a foliation on A, called the translation-invariant
foliation induced by W. In particular, if A is commutative, then every linear subspace W ⊂Lie A
induces such a foliation.
Definition 4.3. Let A be a connected algebraic group and let W ⊂Lie A be a linear subspace.
We say that W algebraically generates A if no proper connected algebraic subgroup B ⊊A
satisfies W ⊂Lie B.

connected commutative algebraic group acting on X with dense open orbit U ∼= A. Let W ⊂Lie A
be an r-dimensional linear subspace, and let F be the foliation induced on U by W.
If dim A = r + 1 and F is purely transcendental, then W algebraically generates A.
