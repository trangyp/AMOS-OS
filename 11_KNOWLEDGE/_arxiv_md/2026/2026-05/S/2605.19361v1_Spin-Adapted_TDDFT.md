Spin-Adapted TDDFT
Xiaoyu Zhang1, ∗and Tai Wang1
1College of Chemistry and Molecular Engineering, Peking University, Beijing 100871, China
(Dated: May 20, 2026)
Linear-response TDDFT is widely used for excited states but suffers from spin contamination in
spin-conserving and spin-flip channels. We develop a spin-adapted RPA by using tensor equation-
of-motion and applying the Wigner–Eckart theorem with tensor decoupling. Casting the RPA Fock
matrix and kernels as energy derivatives gives a TDDFT extension. To restore spin-component de-
generacy, we use hybrids combining HF exchange with spin-unpolarized pure XC parts. Benchmarks,
Cr2 dissociation, and phenol O–H conical intersections demonstrate the method.
Linear-response TDDFT is prevalent due to its bal-
ance between efficiency and accuracy.[1] TDDFT formu-
lated on a closed-shell reference state is typically free
from spin contamination. However, when TDDFT is ap-
plied using an open-shell reference state, spin contami-
nation can occur. [2] TDDFT formulated on an open-
shell reference state has been demonstrated to be par-
ticularly valuable for the description of conical intersec-
tions, charge-transfer excited states, nonadiabatic deriva-
tive couplings, and bond dissociation processes.[3] Thus,
eliminating spin contamination in TDDFT, which entails
performing spin adaptation, is of interest to us.
For spin-adapted TDDFT, several early works in-
volved spin-step operators.
[4–8] However, evaluating
the equation-of-motion (EOM) with spin-step operators
in the matrix representation of first quantization is very
complicated, which hinders the derivation of more de-
tailed equations and implementations.
We denote the
total spin of the targeted (final) state as Sf, and the
total spin of the reference (initial) state as Si. Subse-
quently, Sears, Sherrill, and Krylov [9] obtained spin-
pure states corresponding to Sf −Si = −1 with Si = 1
within the configuration-interaction-singles (CIS) frame-
work.
Building on this advancement, Casanova and
Head-Gordon [10] extended the spin purification proce-
dure to access states with Sf −Si = 1, 0, −1 for Si = 1 in
CIS. Later, Li and Liu [11] constructed spin-pure excited
states characterized by Sf −Si = 1, 0 for arbitrary initial
spin Si within the random-phase approximation (RPA)
formalism. Additionally, within the time-dependent den-
sity functional theory (TDDFT) framework, they gener-
ated spin-pure states corresponding to Sf −Si = 1, 0 for
arbitrary Si. However, artifacts regarding accuracy and
spin degeneracy were found by their own paper.
[12]
Zhang and Herbert [13] constructed spin-pure excited
states characterized by Sf −Si = −1 for arbitrary initial
spin Si within the CIS formalism (RPA results are exactly
the same as CIS ones in this case), and they translated it
to TDA by using an ad hoc collinear kernel that follows
the idea of Shao et al. [14]. However, the collinear ex-
change–correlation kernel is intrinsically problematic: it
lacks global spin-rotational invariance and therefore fails
to preserve the degeneracy between the states |10⟩and
|11⟩when |11⟩is chosen as the reference state and a con-
ventional spin-flip TDDFT formalism is employed. [15]
Also, their spin-adapted TDDFT approach sometimes
acquires unphysically negative roots (see table I in ref.
16) and does not have spin degeneracy among different
Sz with the same S. It is noteworthy that applying well-
defined noncollinear pure xc functionals cannot solve this
problem, which will be discussed later in this work.
To address this issue, the X-TDDFT approach is in-
troduced, in which a spin-adapted correction term is ap-
pended to conventional TDDFT [12, 17]. However, this
scheme does not yield fully spin-degenerate states, does
not use the same functional for ground states and ex-
cited states, and incorporates some ad hoc transitions or
parameters. Chibueze and Visscher [16] proposed a par-
tially spin-adapted TDDFT called Q-SF-TDA. However,
Q-SF-TDA is only spin-adapted in OO-OO blocks, where
TDDFT has been spin-adapted. Lee et al. [18] proposed
a mixed-reference scheme and obtained electronic states
that exhibit an increased degree of spin purification for
Sf −Si = −1 with Si = 1, although complete spin purifi-
cation was not achieved. Nonetheless, their approach still
leads to a breaking of spin degeneracy, uses collinear ker-
nels, and incorporates some ad hoc transitions. In short,
spin contamination in TDDFT remains a puzzle.

Intuitively, spin-adapted
TDDFT incorporates spin-flip excitations and, therefore,
necessitates a noncollinear exchange–correlation (xc) ker-
nel, which can be constructed within the multicollinear
formalism.[15, 22–24] However, the introduction of rigor-
ously defined noncollinear pure xc functionals does not,
in general, preserve spin degeneracy.
This limitation
arises because the kernels of noncollinear pure xc func-
tionals still depend explicitly on the spin density, thereby
breaking the spin symmetry that is maintained at the
random-phase approximation (RPA) level, where the ker-
nels are independent of the spin density (cf. eq. 92 in
ref. 11).
To address this issue, we formulate a sufficiently strin-
gent criterion for preserving spin degeneracy, namely the
use of spin-unpolarized pure xc functionals. To retain a
physically meaningful description of spin-dependent in-
teractions under this constraint, we employ hybrid func-
tionals in which the Hartree–Fock (HF) exchange com-
ponent reintroduces the requisite spin interactions.
In
Appendix IV, we present a sanity check of the spin-
adaptation procedure by replacing the electronic Hamil-
tonian operator, ˆH, with the total spin operator, ˆS2,
expressed in second quantization. Equivalently, this cor-
responds to replacing the Fock and kernel with their spin
counterparts. [25] By implementing this consistency as-
sessment, we computationally corroborate the spin purity
of the calculated electronic states.
All calculations were carried out using the aug-cc-
pVTZ basis set.
Our theoretical framework is imple-
mented in our in-house JAX-based software package IQC.
Computational results for the systems listed in Table I of
Ref. 16 are summarized in Table X. We use SA-TDDFT
to obtain that the total electronic spin of the target state
is S −1, while the spin of the chosen reference state is
S. The primary objective of the table is to elucidate the
relationship between unphysically negative excitation en-
ergies and spin degeneracy. All fully spin-adapted meth-
ods employing certain noncollinear exchange–correlation
functionals exhibit such pseudoroots, which we ascribe
to the breaking of spin degeneracy induced by the non-
collinear nature of these functionals.
Another expla-
nation frequently cited in the literature is the multiple
counting of spin correlation in pure xc functionals after
spin-adaptation. However, in the present work, this ar-
tifact is eliminated by the use of spin-unpolarized pure
exchange–correlation functionals.
The excitation energies of the molecules in the
QUEST1 set [26] were computed.
The lowest triplet
state was employed as the reference state for perform-
ing spin-adapted TDDFT calculations, and singlets were
obtained. We compare several spin-adapted approaches,
including X-TDA, MRSF-TDDFT and SA-SF-TDDFT.
X-TDA uses noncollinear kernels provided by the multi-
collinear approach. The corresponding numerical values
are reported in Table XI in the appendix, and the associ-
ated statistical error measures are summarized in Table
II. For X-TDA, the B3LYP functional yields the highest
accuracy, with an RMSE of 0.54 eV. For SA-TDDFT,
the BHHLYP functional provides the best performance,
with an RMSE of 0.50 eV. These two error metrics are
comparable to each other and are also similar to that
obtained with MRSF-TDDFT. Among all methods con-
sidered, SA-SF-TDDFT exhibits the highest overall ac-
curacy, with an RMSE of 0.31.
