TUMOR GROWTH
KALPANA RAWAT AND KUMARASAMY SAKTHIVEL
Abstract. In this work, we analyze a diffuse-interface model for tumor growth, subject to multi-
plicative white noises, posed on a bounded domain O ⊂Rd, d = 2, 3. The model couples a stochastic
incompressible convective Brinkman-Forchheimer (CBF) equation or Navier-Stokes equation with
damping η|v|r−1v for the averaged velocity field v, to a Cahn-Hilliard (CH) equation for the phase
field variable ϕ and to a stochastic reaction-diffusion equation governing the nutrient concentration
σ. The system is endowed with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions for the phase field,
chemical potential µ, and nutrient, while a homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition is prescribed
for the velocity. We establish the existence of local strong solutions corresponding to initial data
(v0, ϕ0, σ0) ∈H1 × H2 × H1, for r ≥1 in d = 2 and r ∈[1, 3] in d = 3. We prove the weak-strong
uniqueness holds in both d = 2 and d = 3. In addition, for d = 2, the uniqueness of weak solutions
is obtained for all η, ν > 0, and r ≥1, while it holds in d = 3 for r ≥3 (with η, ν > 0 when r > 3,
and ην ≥1 when r = 3) under the assumption σ ∈L4(Ω; L4([0, T]; H1)). Moreover, for d = 2 and
r ∈[1, 3], we obtain that the strong solution exists globally in time. The analysis is carried out for
a broad class of smooth potentials (apart from the classical double-well potential), relying on the
Galerkin scheme, projection onto low and high modes, stochastic energy inequalities derived from
Itˆo’s formula, and pairwise comparison argument.
1. Introduction
Many mathematical models of tumor growth are derived from continuum mixture theory, which
treats the tumor as a system of interacting phases-typically representing tumor cells and healthy
cells. Using conservation laws for mass and momentum, various studies formulate models gov-
erned by partial differential equations that describe how these components evolve ([55, 31, 26,
16]). These models often couple equations describing tumor expansion with advection-reaction-
diffusion systems representing the evolution of nutrients such as oxygen and glucose ([25, 9]).
Initially, most of the tumor growth models considered tissue as a porous medium and used Darcy’s
law ([29, 55, 26, 25, 24]) to model flow velocity by treating the cells as a slow-moving viscous
fluid where pressure gradients drive cell motion, rather than inertial forces. Subsequent enhance-
ments involved adopting Stokes or Brinkman flow ([14, 16, 15]) without modeling tissues as a
porous medium ([20, 21]). However, for many cases when the inertial effects are significant ([1]),
the Forchheimer term is used to account for a nonlinear behavior in pressure difference versus flow
rate, which shows Darcy’s law is no longer valid and the nonlinear inertial corrections ([33, 38])
need to be added to Darcy’s law. Recently, in [22], Forchheimer corrections have been suggested
for non-Darcy flow regimes in tumor growth models by incorporating nonlinear terms |v|v and |v|2v
into a time-dependent Darcy-Brinkman’s law. Besides, the Navier–Stokes (NS) system ([37]) was

The deterministic tumor growth models have attracted significant analytical attention, including
the well-posedness of the models. In [25], the authors have analyzed the CH system with chemo-
taxis and active transport mechanisms in bounded domains with a Lipschitz boundary in Rd, d ∈N.
The existence of global weak solutions was shown for all d, and continuous dependence on ini-
tial and boundary data was established for d ≤4. For the Navier–Stokes-Cahn–Hilliard (NS-CH)
system with chemotaxis and singular potentials (e.g., logarithmic type), [32] proves the existence
of a global weak solution in both dimensions d = 2, 3 and continuous dependence and uniqueness
for d = 2. The Darcy–Forchheimer–Brinkman equation extended by incorporating Forchheimer
nonlinearities F1|v|v, and F2|v|2v together with local and nonlocal effects in tumor growth has been
studied in [22] within a bounded Lipschitz domain in Rd, d ≤3. The work establishes the exis-
tence of a global weak solution and includes parameter-sensitivity analyses. Recently, [5] proves
the existence of a weak solution within bounded 2D and 3D domains for a coupled system with
non-constant mobility and convective transport consisting of a CH equation for phase separation
and a generalized quasi-incompressible Forchheimer equation for the velocity field with Forch-
heimer nonlinearity η(ϕ)|v|r−1v for r > 1, and obtains the numerical solutions.
While deterministic models have provided valuable insights into the general dynamics of tumor
growth, they fail to capture the inherent randomness of cellular processes. Tumor proliferation and
differentiation are inherently stochastic, and experimental uncertainties in proliferation and apop-
tosis rates suggest that carcinogenesis should be treated as a stochastic process ([52, 40]). Recent
work has incorporated random fluctuations at the cellular and tissue levels ([39]) and developed sto-
chastic angiogenesis models to better represent capillary network formation ([43, 8, 44]), thereby
addressing key limitations of purely deterministic approaches.

Let us briefly review some of the literature on damped NS (CBF) equations in both determin-
istic and stochastic scenarios. The global solvability of deterministic CBF equations in various
settings, including the whole space, periodic domains, and bounded domains, has been studied in
the literature. The paper [7] studied the NS equations with damping η|v|r−1v in R3, proving global
weak solutions for r ≥1, global strong solutions for r ≥7/2 (see [56] for r ≥3), and uniqueness
of strong solutions for 7/2 ≤r ≤5. The authors in [35] established the global well-posedness of
the 3D CBF equations in bounded domains, proving the existence and uniqueness of weak solu-
tions with energy estimates, as well as higher regularity of strong solutions for all positive times.
The work [27] established the global well-posedness of weak solutions with energy inequality
for the damped Navier-Stokes equations (CBF) in bounded/periodic domains in Rd (d ∈{2, 3, 4})
when r ∈[3, ∞) (with r = 3 provided 2ην ≥1), and the existence of global strong solutions
in periodic domains. The optimal control of the damped Navier-Stokes-Voigt equations has been
explored in [51]. For the case of the stochastic counterpart, [2] investigates the 3D NS equations
with Brinkman-Forchheimer damping η|v|2rv, anisotropic viscosity, and multiplicative stochastic
forcing. They proved the global existence and uniqueness of weak solutions by exploiting the en-
hanced L2r+2 regularity provided by the damping term with r > 1. The stochastic CBF equations
with multiplicative Gaussian noise on domains in Rd, d ∈{2, 3, 4} are analyzed in the paper [36].
The authors established the energy estimates for r ≥1, obtained the pathwise unique strong solu-
tions (probabilistically) on general unbounded domains (r > 3 for any ν and η, r = 3 with 2ην ≥1),
and further demonstrated the existence of a global strong solution (analytically) on the torus.
Although several studies have incorporated stochastic terms into the CH equation ([18, 10]), in
the present work, this component is modeled deterministically. This choice simplifies the analy-
sis caused by the potential term and focuses on the stochastic effects on the other coupled vari-
ables. Beyond these individual analyses, a broader literature has considered pairwise or coupled
interactions among the system variables, emphasizing how random perturbations contribute to the
dynamics of binary fluid mixtures within the diffuse-interface (or phase-field) approach.
In [19], a stochastic Navier-Stokes-Allen-Cahn two-phase flow model is studied in a bounded
domain in R3 in which both equations incorporate white noise. The authors established a stochastic
relative energy inequality for dissipative martingale solutions and proved weak-strong uniqueness
both pathwise and in law. The paper [12] explored unique local strong solutions (H1 × H2 data)
for the stochastic NS-CH system with multiplicative noise (white noise) on a bounded domain in
Rd, d = 2, 3, and obtained the global existence in d = 2. A stochastic phase-field tumor model in
a smooth bounded domain in R3 with additive noise in the CH equation and multiplicative noise
in the reaction-diffusion equation has been considered in [44]. They discussed the well-posedness
(existence of probabilistically strong solutions and continuous dependence) and the optimal con-
trol problem with cytotoxic drug concentrations (u, w) as control variables. More recently, [23]
considered a stochastic variant in a bounded 3D domain with multiplicative Wiener noise in both
the CH and reaction-diffusion equations. The existence of martingale solutions is proved for vari-
able mobility and non-increasing growth (logistic, Gompertzian) functions. Moreover, numerical
approximations are proposed with simulations illustrating the stochastic effects on tumor growth.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to address a stochastic tumor growth model
that incorporates random perturbations in the CH-CBF reaction-diffusion model. Moreover, this
study advances beyond existing works on the well-posedness theory for the system modeling tumor
growth by establishing the local existence of a unique strong solution for d = 3 and the global
existence for d = 2 in the subcritical range r ∈[1, 3].

The study of the proposed model poses several challenges and limitations due to its nonlinear and
coupled structure. A major difficulty stems from the fact that the variable σ is strongly coupled with
the variables v and ϕ. More precisely, the coupling between σ and v given by the advection term
(v · ∇σ) in the stochastic reaction-diffusion equation (3d) behaves similarly to the NS nonlinearity,
making it more difficult to derive uniform estimates and establish existence and uniqueness results
compared to the convective (advection) term (v · ∇ϕ) in (3c), where this term can be handled due
to the higher regularity of ϕ. It is worth noting that due to the presence of v · ∇σ term, the global
existence of strong solutions (in d = 3) of the CH-CBF reaction-diffusion model seems difficult
to establish for any r > 1, even in a periodic domain (or in the whole space). In fact, by virtue
of this nonlinear term, the uniqueness of weak solutions in d = 3 is proven (see Proposition 4.2)
only with an additional regularity assumption for σ ∈L4(Ω; L4([0, T]; H1)). Besides, owing to
the interaction of σ with ϕ (as a proliferation term) in the CH equation (3c), the model does not
have the property of mass conservation for the phase field ϕ, which drives us to adopt the full
norm for ϕ instead of the seminorm, and the use of the full norm makes the analysis significantly
challenging. A further technicality arises when handling the potential ψ with prescribed growth
conditions (see [A1]), particularly in proving the uniqueness (see Propositions 4.1 and 4.2), which
is handled with an additional L4(Ω; L4((0, T); H2)) regularity for ϕ. The inclusion of an additional
source term (proliferation) in (3c) also necessitates a more delicate analysis for several estimates.
Finally, it is worth noting that in the deterministic/stochastic scenario, the Forchheimer term
(|v|r−1v, r ≥3) often acts as a regularizing mechanism to get the strong solution of the CBF equa-
tion in a periodic domain/torus in d = 2, 3 or in the whole space (see [7], [36]), but it does not
seem to work when it comes to a stochastic case in a bounded domain. The commutativity of
the Leray-Helmholtz projector with the Laplace operator, which is used to get the regularity of
the solutions, does not hold in a bounded domain (see [49, Chapter 2]); consequently, we use the
embedding theorems and restrict the absorption exponent r based on the spatial dimensions to get
various results.
The main contributions of this work are the following:
(1) The pth-order energy estimates (Lemma 4.1) have been derived for the stochastic CH-CBF
reaction diffusion system for all r ≥1 in both dimensions d = 2 and d = 3.
(2) Weak-strong uniqueness (Proposition 4.1) for all r ≥1 in d = 2 and for r ∈[1, 3] in d = 3
cases is established. In addition to that, the uniqueness of weak solutions (Proposition 4.2)
is obtained for all η, ν > 0 and r ≥1 in d = 2, and for r ≥3 in d = 3 (with η, ν > 0 when
r > 3, and ην ≥1 when r = 3) under the assumption that σ ∈L4(Ω; L4([0, T]; H1)). It is
evident that in the absence of nutrient concentration σ, that is, for the stochastic damped
NS-CH system in d = 3, the global uniqueness of weak solutions holds for any r ≥3.
(3) The existence of local maximal strong solutions (Theorem 4.1) of the stochastic CH-CBF
reaction diffusion system defined on a bounded domain O ⊂Rd, d = 2, 3 with initial data
(v0, ϕ0, σ0) in H1 × H2 × H1, for r ≥1 in d = 2 and for r ∈[1, 3] in d = 3, is obtained.
(4) In the case O ⊂R2, with initial data (v0, ϕ0, σ0) in H1 × H2 × H1, the global existence of
unique maximal strong solutions (Theorem 5.1) has been established for r ∈[1, 3].
