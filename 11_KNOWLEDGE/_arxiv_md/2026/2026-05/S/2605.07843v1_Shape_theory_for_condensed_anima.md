Shape theory for condensed anima

Abstract
We give different perspectives on the notion of shape for condensed anima. We prove that it
recovers more classical notions of shape for topological spaces in the cases of all paracompact
compactly generated spaces and all locally contractible spaces. These recovering statements
imply and extend comparison results on sheaf and condensed cohomology by Clausen-Scholze
and Haine [Sch19b; Hai22]. Another homotopy-theoretical direction for condensed anima are
their condensed homotopy groups. Connected to this, we give a description of the underlying
topological group functor on condensed groups via quasi-topological groups.

Introduction: Overview and main results
In Algebraic Topology, one studies topological spaces by means of algebraic invariants like ho-
motopy and cohomology. The theory of condensed mathematics suggests that topological spaces
should be replaced by condensed sets or anima. It seems natural to ask how one can translate
classical concepts from algebraic topology developed for topological spaces into this setting.

Notational conventions
Throughout this paper, there appear several (∞-)categories. We fix the following (standard)
notation.
(1) We write Set for the category of sets and Setfin for the category of finite sets.
(2) We write Grp, respectively, AbGrp for the category of (abelian) groups. By TopGrp we
denote the category of topological groups.
(3) We write Top for the category of topological spaces, Comp for the category of compact
Hausdorff spaces, Pro(Setfin) for the category of totally disconnected compact Hausdorff
spaces (=profinite sets) and Extr for the category of extremally disconnected profinite sets.
By CW we denote the category of CW-spaces and by CG the category of compactly generated
spaces using the definition recalled in Remark 2.24.
(4) We write Cat, respectively, Cat∞for the large (∞-)category of small (∞-)categories, and
use Ani ⊂Cat∞for the full subcategory spanned by the anima (∞-groupoids/ spaces). By
Ani<∞⊂Ani we denote the subcategory of truncated anima.
(5) We write Cond(𝒞) for the (∞-)category of condensed objects on some (∞-)category 𝒞.
(6) We write Fun(𝒞, 𝒟) for the ∞-category of functors between ∞-categories 𝒞and 𝒟and

Condensed sets and condensed anima
The basic objects in condensed mathematics are condensed sets. Originally, these are defined as
sheaves of sets on the pro-étale site Pro ́Et∗of a point, i.e., the spectrum of a separably closed field.
See [BS15] for an introduction to the pro-étale topology of schemes. The category of condensed
sets is denoted by
Cond(Set) ≔Sh(Pro ́Et∗, Set) .
Sheaves on Pro ́Et∗can be identified with sheaves on certain subcategories of topological spaces.
The notion of condensed sets extends in the ∞-categorical world to the notion of condensed
anima, denoted by Cond(Ani), by considering hypersheaves with values in the ∞-category Ani
of anima (or spaces).
2.15 Notation. We denote by Pro(Setfin) the category of profinite sets. By Stone duality, the
category Pro(Setfin) can be identified with the full subcategory of totally disconnected spaces in
the category Comp of compact Hausdorff spaces. By a result by Gleason, stated in [Joh86, p. III
3.7], the projective objects in Comp are the extremally disconnected profinite sets Extr ⊂Comp.
2.16 Recollection. There are at least four different defining sites for the ∞-category Cond(Ani)
of condensed anima defining the same ∞-topos of hypercomplete ∞-sheaves:
(i) The pro-étale site Pro ́EtSpec(𝑘) for 𝑘any separably closed field,
(ii) the site Comp of compact Hausdorff spaces,
(iii) the site Pro(Setfin) of profinite sets,
(iv) the site Extr of extremally disconnected profinite sets.
Here, coverings for the sites (ii)-(iv) are given by jointly surjective families of continuous maps
of topological spaces. All of these sites define the same ∞-topos on hypercomplete ∞-sheaves:
Every compact Hausdorff space admits a surjection from an extremally disconnected compact
Hausdorff space, namely from the Čech–Stone compactification of its underlying discrete space.
Moreover, Pro(Setfin) identifies with the subcategory of affine schemes in Pro ́EtSpec(𝑘). Thus
there are hierarchies of bases Extr ⊂Pro(Setfin) ⊂Comp and Pro(Setfin) ⊂Pro ́EtSpec(𝑘). By
[Aok23, Corollary A.7], restriction of sites induces equivalences of hypercomplete ∞-topoi
(2.17)
Shhyp(Pro ́EtSpec(𝑘)) ⥲Shhyp(Pro(Setfin)) ⥲Shhyp(Extr) .
The ∞-topos Cond(Ani) of condensed anima is any of the equivalent ∞-topoi (2.17).
2.18 Recollection. The category Cond(Set) of condensed sets embeds fully faithfully into the ∞-
category Cond(Ani) of condensed anima by post-composition of functors with the fully faithful
embedding 𝑣∶Set ↪Ani. The left adjoint is given by post-composition with the 0-truncation
functor 𝜋0 ∶Ani →Set. In other words, the objects of Cond(Ani) in the essential image of
Cond(Set) are exactly the 0-truncated objects. They are called static condensed anima.
2.19 Recollection. On extremally disconnected profinite sets, hypersheafification can be omit-
ted and the ∞-sheaf condition simplifies. More precisely, a presheaf 𝐹on Extr is a hypersheaf
if and only if 𝐹carries finite disjoint unions to finite products, i.e., there is an identification
Cond(Ani) ≃Shhyp(Extr) ≃Fun×(Extrop, Ani) ,
where the right hand site denotes finite product-preserving functors. It is a consequence of Extr
being a subsite of projective objects and widely used in the literature on condensed mathematics.
A detailed discussion can be, for example, found in [Mai25, Section A.3.5].

4.3 Definition. Quasi-topological groups
A quasi-topological group is a group 𝐺∊Grp with a topology such that
(i) the inversion 𝐺→𝐺, 𝑔↦𝑔−1, is continuous and
(ii) the group operation 𝐺× 𝐺→𝐺is continuous in each variable.
The category of quasi-topological groups with continuous group homomorphisms is qTopGrp.
Note that the second condition in the definition is equivalent to the translations 𝑔↦𝑔ℎand
ℎ↦ℎ𝑔being homeomorphisms for every ℎ∊𝐺.
4.4 Remark. Every topological group is a quasi-topological group and there exists a fully faithful
inclusion functor
TopGrp ↪qTopGrp .
A locally compact Hausdorff quasi-topological group is a topological group [Ell57, Theorem 2].
4.5 Remark. There are interesting examples of topologized groups which are not topological but
quasi-topological. For instance, this phenomenon occurs when equipping fundamental groups
of topological spaces in a canonical way with the quotient topology of the loop space, as for
example studied by Brazas in [Bra11], [Bra13], [BF15]. It is an open question to us whether it is
possible to recover these from a construction internal to condensed mathematics.
