Abstract: This paper extends safety guarantees for multi-task Bayesian optimization with uncertain co-
regionalization matrices from intrinsic co-regionalization models to linear models of co-regionalization.
The latter allows for more flexible modeling of the inter-task correlations by composing multiple
features. We derive uniform error bounds for vector-valued functions sampled from a Gaussian process
with a linear model of co-regionalization kernel. Furthermore, we show the potential performance
gains of linear models of co-regionalization in a numerical comparison on a safe multi-task Bayesian
optimization benchmark.
Keywords: Gaussian Processes, Bayesian Optimization, Safety Guarantees, Multi-Task Gaussian
Processes, Simulation Informed
1. INTRODUCTION
PID controllers are still the most widely used controllers in
industry. However, the tuning of these controllers is often done
by trial and error, which can be time-consuming and costly. To
overcome this issue, the field of Bayesian optimization (BO)
has gained popularity in recent years. BO is a black-box op-
timization technique, i.e., initially almost no prior knowledge
is necessary for setting up the optimization procedure. Then,
by sequential evaluations of the unknown objective function,
the algorithm constructs a probabilistic surrogate model which
is used to predict the function values of the objective at unob-
served points. These predictions are then used to identify new
promising points which are likely to optimize the objective, see
Shahriari et al. (2016) for an overview of BO in general. By
now, this optimization method has been established and is in
practice widely used to automate parameter-tuning processes.
For instance Coutinho et al. (2023); Hajieghrary et al. (2022)
applied BO to automatically tune and adapt PID controllers,
and Shields et al. (2021) used BO to optimize ingredients of
chemical reactions. Furthermore, BO became a cornerstone for
optimizing large scale research infrastructures such as particle
accelerators, see Kirschner et al. (2019); Kaiser et al. (2024).
Despite the great success of BO, the method often becomes
infeasible when applied to high-dimensional problems. In gen-
eral, a Gaussian process (GP), see Williams and Rasmussen
(2006), is utilized to model the unknown objective which suf-
fers from the curse of dimensionality, i.e., the required number
of observations of the objective increases exponentially with
the dimension. In many practical applications there exist first-
principles models of the process to be optimized usually avail-
able in the form of simulations. There are multiple methodolo-
gies to make use of this additional information. Meta learning,
see Vanschoren (2018), can be applied to determine prior dis-
tributions as done by Rothfuss et al. (2021); Pan et al. (2024).
These priors can be seen as a warm start for the optimization
process. Another approach is to apply BO on the simulation
and the real process jointly which is in literature known as
multi-task Bayesian optimization (MTBO), initially proposed
by Swersky et al. (2013) and successfully applied in practice,
see Taylor et al. (2023). The foundation of this approach, multi-
task GP prediction, was originally introduced by Bonilla et al.
(2007). This technique uses correlation matrices to capture the
influence between various tasks, which are learned online from
the available data.
A final, critical consideration for practical application is the
enforcement of safety guarantees. For example, when tuning
controllers, the search space must be constrained to parameters
that yield a stable closed-loop system. Especially if the response
of the system is fast this safety mechanism can barely be han-
dled externally by a fallback mechanism as for the laser-based
optical synchronization (LbSync) at European X-Ray Free-
Electron Laser (European XFEL), see Schulz et al. (2015). The
primary challenge is that the system dynamics, and therefore
the safe parameter region, are initially unknown. This necessi-
tates estimating the safe region from data, for which uniform
error bounds defined as

2.3 Safety Guarantees
The central challenge in ensuring safety for multi-task BO is the
uncertainty surrounding the correlation matrix Σ. Our approach
addresses this by assuming the true matrix is contained within
a known set, Cρ, with a probability of 1 −ρ. In a Bayesian
context, this set can be inferred from data using approximation
methods like Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling,
as demonstrated by L¨ubsen et al. (2024); L¨ubsen and Eichler
(2025a). We will now review the multi-task safety guarantees
for the ICM case from this Bayesian perspective. To this end,
we impose the following assumption.
Assumption 1. The unknown vector-valued function f : X →
Ru is a sample from a GP with zero mean, multi-task kernel
K(x, x′) = Σk(x, x′) and hyper-prior Σ ∼p(Σ) with
compact support, i.e., f(x) ∼GP(0, K(x, x′)). The base
kernel k(x, x′) is at least four times partially differentiable on
X.
The differentiability assumption on the kernel is necessary to
ensure the existence of the Lipschitz constant Lf of the sample
paths of the GP. Moreover, the moduli of continuity ωµ defined
as |µ(x)−µ(y)| ≤ωµ(∥x−y∥p), and ωσ defined as |σ(x)−
σ(y)| ≤ωσ(∥x −y∥p) of the posterior mean and standard
deviation, respectively, are required. Both quantities can be
upper bounded using solely the Lipschitz constant of the kernel,
see L¨ubsen and Eichler (2025a) for details.

5. CONCLUSION
We extended safety guarantees for multi-task Bayesian op-
timization with uncertain correlation matrices from ICMs to
LMCs. This was achieved by extending the uniform error
bounds to LMCs and deriving the necessary components to
compute a robust scaling factor. Finally, a numerical compar-
ison on a synthetic benchmark demonstrated that LMCs can
provide superior performance over ICMs. Both multi-task ap-
proaches can outperform single-task safe BO by transferring
knowledge from the simulation to the real task. This also allows
for an identification of a non-connected safe set. The main
limitations of the LMC as well as the ICM approach is the
increased computational complexity for estimating the set Cρ
and computing the scaling factor ¯β. Future work could focus
on extending the theoretical analysis to other multi-task ker-
nels beyond LMCs, e.g., convolution processes and other non-
separable kernels and merging approximation techniques for
GPs to reduce computational complexity.
