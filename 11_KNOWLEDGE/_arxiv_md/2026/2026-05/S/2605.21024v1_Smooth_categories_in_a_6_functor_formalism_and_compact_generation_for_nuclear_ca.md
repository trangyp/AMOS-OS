SMOOTH CATEGORIES IN A 6 FUNCTOR FORMALISM AND COMPACT
GENERATION FOR NUCLEAR CATEGORIES IN ANALYTIC GEOMETRY
MATTEO MONTAGNANI
Abstract
In this paper, we study the notion of smooth ∞-categories within the framework of a
six-functor formalism. By leveraging the theory of condensed mathematics and analytic
stacks, we apply these results to demonstrate that a rigid analytic variety is smooth if and
only if its associated category of nuclear sheaves is smooth. Furthermore, we relate the
compact generation of the category of nuclear sheaves to the algebraization of the rigid
analytic variety; these results are then employed to obtain an example of a non atomically
generated but internally smooth category.

1. Introduction
Over the last few decades, algebraic geometry has undergone a profound paradigm shift,
evolving toward the intrinsic analysis of varieties via their associated categories. In the
modern development of non-commutative algebraic geometry, highly structured categories
(such as stable ∞-categories ) are treated as geometric spaces. Within this landscape, the
precise translation of geometric properties, such as a variety being smooth or proper, into
purely categorical definitions over QCoh(X) or Perf(X) has become a foundational tool. A
natural ambition is to transport this powerful categorical machinery into the realm of (rigid)
analytic geometry.
Another major direction is the comparison between algebraic and analytic spaces, as
illustrated by GAGA-style theorems and the algebraization problem: understanding the
precise conditions under which a rigid analytic variety X arises as the analytification of a
scheme.
However, attempting to reconcile these algebraic and analytic perspectives at the categor-
ical level reveals a fundamental technical obstacle. As explicitly formalized in Definition 1.2,
the standard notion of smooth and proper ∞-categories crucially relies on the tensor product
of stable presentable ∞-categories introduced in [Lur17]. This tensor product is intrinsi-
cally an “algebraic” operation and behaves poorly in the analytic setting. For example, the
categorical Künneth formula fails for Tate algebras over a non-Archimedean field k, in the
sense that

Consequently, the of smooth and proper ∞-category is essentially algebraic in nature
and ill-suited for the analytic framework.
Tate algebras are the fundamental building blocks of rigid analytic geometry. They
are utilized to construct rigid analytic varieties in much the same way that affine schemes

are used to construct schemes in algebraic geometry (see [Bos14] for a comprehensive
introduction). Throughout this paper, we consider rigid analytic varieties over a complete
non-Archimedean field k of characteristic 0, and we assume all such varieties are locally of
finite type.
Following [Bos14, Section 5.4], it is possible to define the analytification functor (−)an
from the category of schemes locally of finite type over k to the category of rigid analytic
varieties over k. The characterization of the essential image of this functor, obtained in
[Mon25], is a clear manifestation of the fact that the notion of a smooth ∞-category in
Definition 1.2 is intrinsically algebraic. Indeed, the following theorem was established in loc.
cit.
Theorem 1.1 ([Mon25, Theorem 3.1]). Let X be a smooth and proper, quasi-compact, and
separated rigid analytic variety over k. Then Perf(X) is smooth and proper as a k-linear ∞-
category if and only if X is algebraizable (i.e., there exists an algebraic space Y such that
Y an ≃X).
See also [TV07a] for a closely related theorem in complex analytic geometry.
The objective of this paper is threefold. First, based on the theory of condensed mathe-
matics and analytic stacks developed by Clausen–Scholze, we introduce a non-commutative
criterion to detect the smoothness of rigid analytic varieties; in particular, we obtain a notion
of non-commutative smoothness that is well-defined in the analytic setting. Second, we
introduce a non-commutative criterion to detect their algebraizability. Finally, we apply
these results to construct counterexamples to the atomic generation of smooth enriched
categories.
1.1. Categorical smoothness in the algebraic setting: Motivations. The investigation
of an algebraic variety X via its abelian category of quasi-coherent sheaves, QCoh(X), or
its subcategory of coherent sheaves, Coh(X), perfectly captures its essential geometric
properties; classical reconstruction theorems (such as those of Rosenberg [Bra13]) even
demonstrate that these categories can fully recover the underlying space. Nevertheless,
this abelian framework ultimately proved too rigid for broader geometric and categorical
manipulations.
To obtain a more flexible and homologically robust framework, the focus naturally shifted
toward the associated triangulated categories. Following the seminal work of Bondal and
Orlov [BO01], it became evident that the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves,
DbCoh(X), encodes a wealth of deep geometric information about X. However, classical
triangulated categories are known to suffer from technical shortcomings, most notably the
lack of functorial cones and the absence of well-behaved limits and colimits.

These technical limitations necessitated a further conceptual leap: lifting these triangu-
lated structures to appropriate homotopical enhancements. By employing differential graded
(dg) categories or, more broadly, ∞-categories , one can retain the higher homotopical data
that is inevitably lost upon passing to the standard homotopy category. Consequently, the
field has increasingly focused on the ∞-category of quasi-coherent sheaves QCoh(X) and the
category of perfect complexes Perf(X) (the ∞-categorical generalization of vector bundles).
Building on the pioneering work of Orlov [Orl16] in the triangulated setting, the subse-
quent evolution of categorical enhancements paved the way for the modern development of
non-commutative algebraic geometry. In this framework, highly structured categories (such as
dg-categories or stable ∞-categories ) are no longer viewed merely as invariants of spaces,
but are treated as geometric spaces in their own right. Within this landscape, the notion
of a smooth category (respectively, a proper category) emerges as the natural and necessary
generalization of a classical smooth (respectively, proper) algebraic variety.
Formally, following the philosophy introduced by Kontsevich [Kon98], a dg-category C
is defined as smooth if the diagonal bimodule represented by C in C⊗Cop is a compact
object. In the ∞-categorical setting, the definitions of smooth and proper ∞-categories

Similarly, if C is a small, idempotent complete ∞-category , we say that C is smooth
(respectively, proper) if its ind-completion is smooth (respectively, proper) as in Definition 1.2.
When C is the ∞-category of quasi-coherent sheaves associated with an algebraic
variety X, the condition that C is smooth (respectively, proper) is strictly equivalent to
X being a smooth (respectively, proper) variety in the classical sense (see [Lun09] and
[BB02]). We will provide an alternative proof of these known results in a more general
setting in Section A. However, the true power of this definition lies in its applicability to
purely non-commutative contexts, decoupling the condition of regularity from the existence
of an underlying topological space.
Currently, categorical smoothness is not merely a technical artifact, but a foundational
requirement driving several major research programs in algebraic geometry. For instance,
following the seminal works of Lunts [Lun09] and Kuznetsov [KL15], the concept of smooth
categories allows for the introduction of categorical resolutions of singularities. A categorical
resolution consists of a fully faithful embedding of the category of perfect complexes, Perf(X),
into a smooth and proper dg-category.
In the realm of non-commutative algebraic geometry, the concept of smooth and proper
categories is now a central pillar. It facilitates the extension of fundamental algebraic
geometric results to the non-commutative setting, while simultaneously presenting new
challenges at the interface of the commutative and non-commutative worlds (see, for
example, [Per19] and [BP24]).
Motivated by the ambition to develop a parallel non-commutative theory for rigid analytic
spaces, we will work within the setting of derived rigid analytic geometry, a homotopical
generalization of the classical framework detailed in [Bos14]. This theory, developed in
Section 3 and Section 4, is highly inspired by [Mik23]. This approach allows us to establish
a six-functor formalism and obtain a categorical Künneth formula, in stark contrast to the
failure observed in the classical analytic setting (Equation (1)). These are the primary
ingredients we will utilize to relate the categorical notion of smoothness to its geometric
counterpart in the rigid analytic setting.
We emphasize that, because our methods rely on a “categorical analytic tensor product”
and the existence of a six-functor formalism, they can be easily translated to other settings.
For example, they can be adapted to complex analytic geometry or applied to other six-
functor formalisms sharing similar features.
1.2. Categorical smoothness in the analytic setting: A new framework. Derived analytic
geometry was initially developed by Porta and Porta–Yu (see, for example, [PY20; Por18;
PY18b; PY22]), utilizing an approach similar to the one employed by Lurie in [Lur18].
Another approach to derived analytic geometry, closer in style to the framework developed
for derived algebraic geometry in [TV08], has recently been introduced and utilized, for

6
instance, in [BKK24; BBK18]. However, the setting for derived analytic geometry employed
in this paper relies on the recent work of Clausen and Scholze on condensed mathematics
and their theory of analytic stacks, as developed in [Sch; CS19; CS24; CS22]. Within this
framework, one can study rigid analytic varieties, as demonstrated in [Mik23; And21], and
even construct fundamental new analytic stacks in rigid geometry, as explained in [Cam24].
One of the powerful features of this approach is that it admits a robust six-functor formalism
and a “categorical completed tensor product”.
Throughout this paper, we work over a non-Archimedean field k of characteristic zero.
We adopt the definition of animated affinoid algebras introduced in [Mik23, Definition 2.11]
(see Definition 3.7). Derived rigid analytic spaces are then defined by “gluing animated
affinoid algebras along analytic open immersions” (see Definition 4.11). We introduce and
study the sheaf of nuclear ∞-categories over derived rigid analytic varieties; in Section 4,
we will demonstrate its favorable properties.
The notions of nuclear objects and nuclear subcategories were introduced in [CS22]. These
concepts have subsequently been employed—for instance, in conjunction with Efimov’s semi-
nal work on the K-theory of dualizable ∞-categories [Efi24]—to study the algebraic K-theory
of rigid analytic varieties [And23]. We utilize nuclear ∞-categories in this paper because
they are rigid in our setting (see Proposition 4.13). This rigidity, in particular, provides a
simple description of Nuc(k)-atomic objects in Nuc(X) in terms of perfect complexes over X
(see Lemma 5.8). As explained in the following section, this is the key property that allows
us to relate the smoothness of X to the categorical notion of smoothness for Nuc(X) (see
Theorem 5.1). Combined with Theorem 1.1, this property will be crucial in relating the
algebraization of a rigid analytic variety X to the atomic generation of Nuc(X).
To obtain this result, another fundamental step consists of relating the geometric defini-
tion of smoothness for a rigid analytic variety X to a categorical definition. The idea is to
work relative to a base ∞-category C (in this case, Nuc(k)), where the tensor product in
PrL
C satisfies a Künneth formula for rigid varieties. In our setting, this property is proven in
Proposition 4.14. This motivates us to consider presentable ∞-categories that are linear
with respect to the action of a base symmetric monoidal, stable, presentable ∞-category
V. In Section 2, we review the necessary theory to work with these objects, primarily
following [Ben24; Ram24a; Hin21; Hei23; GH15], which provide a rigorous treatment of
the subject. Furthermore, we generalize the notion of smooth ∞-categories in this setting
via the following definition, which is also considered in [Ram24a].
Definition 1.3. Let C be a presentable and dualizable ∞-category over V. We say that
C is internally smooth over V if the coevaluation map admits a V-linear right adjoint that
preserves colimits.

Within the analytic geometry framework described in this paper, we can now relate the
categorical notion of smoothness to its geometric counterpart.
Theorem 1.4 (Theorem 5.1). Let f : X →Sp(k) be a (classical) rigid analytic variety over k.
Then X is smooth if and only if the ∞-category Nuc(X) is internally smooth in PrL
Nuc(k).
This theorem follows as a consequence of Corollary 5.4, which demonstrates that X is
smooth if and only if ∆∗OX is a perfect complex on X b×X, where ∆: X →X b×X is the diagonal
map. These results are obtained by combining the Künneth formula (Proposition 4.14) and
the results for smooth ∞-category in a six-functor formalism developed in Section 2. In our
setting, the construction of an appropriate six-functor formalism for nuclear ∞-categories is
described in Theorem 4.15. Specifically, this six-functor formalism is constructed using the
theory developed in [Man22] and revisited in [HM24]. Furthermore, it is inspired by the
formalism described in [Sch25, Lecture 8] within the setting of derived algebraic geometry.
We observe that, although this theorem ultimately concerns the study of a classical property
of rigid analytic varieties, obtaining the categorical (or non-commutative) characterization
of smoothness requires entering the realm of condensed mathematics and the setting of
derived rigid analytic geometry described herein. Indeed, these methods are also essential
for obtaining the requisite six-functor formalism.
As previously explained, this theorem plays a crucial role in relating the atomic generation
of Nuc(X) to the algebraization of the rigid analytic variety X.
1.3. Applications: atomic generation and algebraization. Together with the notion of
smooth and proper ∞-categories , which are closely related to their geometric counterparts,
one of the main features of non-commutative algebraic geometry is the existence of a single
compact generator for the ∞-category of quasi-coherent sheaves on a quasi-compact and
quasi-separated scheme X over a field k. In particular, there exists a non-commutative
k-algebra A such that we have an equivalence of ∞-categories
QCoh(X) ≃LModA.
These results have been established, for example, in [BB02] and, within the setting of
derived algebraic geometry, in [Toe11]. Moreover, assuming that the variety X is smooth and
proper, one can deduce that the algebra A is also smooth and proper. We will demonstrate
how to reprove a similar result in Section A using the theory of six-functor formalisms.
More generally, the existence of a compact generator was proved by Toën in the setting
of dg-categories; the analogous result in the setting of ∞-categories is the following.


## Page 8

Theorem 1.5 ([Lur18, Proposition 11.3.2.4]). Let R be an E∞-ring specturm and C be a
stable, R-linear, smooth and compactly generated ∞-category. Then C has a single compact
generator.
Without the assumption of compact generation, whether the previous result holds remains
an open conjecture, which can be stated as follows:
Conjecture 1.6 ([Ram24a, Conjecture 0.6]). Let R be an E∞-ring specturm and C be a
smooth, stable, presentable R-linear ∞-category. Then C admits a single compact generator.
This conjecture has been verified in some particular cases in [Ste23]. However, in the
generality presented above, the conjecture remains open to the author’s knowledge.
In [Ram24a; Ram24b], Maxime Ramzi developed the theory of atomic objects and dual-
izable ∞-categories ; see also [Efi24; Efi25] for closely related results and their applications
to the theory of localizing invariants. In this context, atomic objects can be viewed as a
variant of compact objects suitably adapted to the setting of enriched ∞-categories .
Building upon the notion of a V-atomically generated ∞-category , as detailed in
Definition 2.8, Maxime Ramzi proposed a generalization of the preceding conjecture to the
setting of V-enriched ∞-categories .
Conjecture 1.7. Let V be a presentable symmetric monoidal ∞-category and let C be a
V-linear presentable ∞-category , internally smooth over V. Then C is atomically generated.
In this paper, we obtain a counterexample to this conjecture by studying the nuclear
∞-category of a smooth and proper rigid analytic variety. Indeed, we prove the following
result.
Theorem 1.8 (Theorem 5.9). Let p: X →Sp(k) be a smooth and proper, quasi-compact, and
separated rigid analytic variety over Sp(k). Then X is algebraizable if and only if Nuc(X) admits
a single Nuc(k)-atomic (equivalently, compact) generator.
If we assume X to be algebraizable (i.e., X ≃Y an for some scheme Y ), we can prove that
Nuc(X) is Nuc(k)-atomically generated. Building on the relative GAGA theorem of [Wan26],
we establish the following equivalence in Corollary 5.7:
Nuc(Y an) ≃QCoh(Y ) ⊗Modk Nuc(k).
We then deduce our statement regarding the compact generation of QCoh(Y ). Conversely, if
we assume that Nuc(X) admits a single Nuc(k)-atomic generator, we can use Lemma 5.8 to
deduce that this generator must be a perfect complex. Furthermore, applying Theorem 1.4,
we infer that Perf(X) must also be smooth; in this way, we reduce our algebraization result
to Theorem 1.1.

see for example the p-adic Hopf Surface studied in [Mus77; Vos91], combining Theorem 1.4

We have been informed that recently Maxime Ramzi has also constructed a counterex-

We conclude the intruduction observing that Theorem 1.4 implies that it is not, in general,
possible to glue local, relatively compact generators of a sheaf of categories in the analytic (or
fpqc) topology to obtain a global compact generator. Locally in the analytic topology over X,
the sheaf Nuc(−) is of the form ModA(Nuc(k)) for affinoid algebras A. However, Theorem 5.9
demonstrates that, globally, such a generator algebra does not necessarily exist.
This property of gluing local compact generators is one of the foundational features of
Non-Commutative Algebraic Geometry, and it highlights a key distinction between algebraic
and analytic topologies. This gluing procedure for compact generators has been shown to
hold for various topologies utilized in algebraic geometry; see, for example, [TV07b] and
[BB02].

six-functor formalism.
In Section 3 and Section 4, we introduce the definitions of animated affinoid algebras
and derived rigid analytic spaces. We construct a six-functor formalism for nuclear categories
over these spaces and describe the main properties of both the spaces and their associated
nuclear categories.

5. Smooth categories and compact generation in rigid analytic geometry
In this section, we study the compact generation of the ∞-category of nuclear modules
over rigid analytic varieties. In particular, we will show that this ∞-category is compactly
(equivalently, atomically) generated if and only if the rigid analytic variety is algebraizable
(see Theorem 5.9). We then use this result to falsify Conjecture 1.7. Two important steps
in the proof are a version of the GAGA theorem, as described in [Wan26] in the setting of
nuclear ∞-categories , and the comparison between the geometric notion of smooth rigid
varieties and the categorical definition of smoothness (see Theorem 5.1).
5.1. Non-commutative smoothness and classical smoothness. We begin by comparing
the classical notion of smoothness in rigid analytic geometry with the notion of a smooth
∞-category introduced in Section 2. In particular, we will prove the following theorem:
Theorem 5.1. Let f : X →Sp(k) be a (classical) rigid analytic variety over k. Then X is smooth
if and only if the ∞-category Nuc(X) is internally smooth in PrL
Nuc(k).
We observe that an analogous theorem has been proved for schemes in [Orl16] and in
[Lun09] for the category of perfect complexes. However, the generalization to rigid analytic
varieties is not straightforward, and we cannot reproduce the same proof as in [Orl16].


## Page 41


41
Indeed, the analogue of Theorem 5.1 does not hold for the ∞-category of perfect complexes
in rigid analytic geometry. It has been shown in [Mon25] that the notion of smoothness for
the ∞-category of perfect complexes over a rigid analytic variety is intrinsically algebraic
and does not reflect the smoothness of the variety itself. To obtain a correspondence between
smooth “categories of sheaves” and smooth varieties, it is necessary to use a “completed
tensor product of ∞-categories ” with the property that, for a variety X,
Shv(X × X) ≃Shv(X)b⊗Shv(X).
This can be achieved, for example, in the framework of analytic geometry developed by
Clausen and Scholze [CS24], or in the setting developed by Ben-Bassat, Kelly, and Kremnizer
[BKK24]. Here, we have chosen to explore these ideas within the setting of analytic stacks
as in [CS24], focusing on the sheaf of nuclear ∞-categories . However, we believe that it
would not be difficult to adapt some of the ideas presented here to the setting of [BKK24],
utilizing the 6-functor formalism described in [Soo25], or to other “analytic sheaf theories,”
such as the D-solid sheaves described in [Cam24].

This property of gluing local compact generators is one of the foundational features of
Non-Commutative Algebraic Geometry, and it highlights a key distinction between algebraic
and analytic topologies. This gluing procedure for compact generators has been shown to
hold for various topologies utilized in algebraic geometry; see, for example, [TV07b] and
[BB02].
Appendix A. Smooth and proper categories in derived algebraic geometry

as developed in Section 2. In particular, we will prove the following theorem.
Theorem A.1. Let f : X →Y be a morphism of finite Tor-amplitude between quasi-compact,
semi-separated derived schemes of finite type. Then the following hold:
(1) The map f is smooth if and only if QCoh(X) is smooth over QCoh(Y ).
(2) If QCoh(X) is proper over QCoh(Y ), then f is universally closed (i.e., for every derived
scheme W and for every map W →Y , the induced map of topological spaces |X ×Y W| →
|W| is closed).
(3) If f is proper, then QCoh(X) is proper over QCoh(Y ).
Remark A.2. In particular, under the hypotheses of Theorem A.1, if f is separated, then f
is proper if and only if QCoh(X) is proper over QCoh(Y ). Moreover, as explained in [Lur18,
Theorem 11.4.2.1], if we assume QCoh(X) to be smooth, then X is automatically separated
(since the diagonal is a closed immersion; see [Lur18, Lemma 11.3.6.2]). This implies that,
under the hypotheses of Theorem A.1, X is smooth and proper if and only if QCoh(X) is
smooth and proper over QCoh(Y ).

analogue of Theorem 5.1.
Corollary A.3. Let X →Spec(k) be a quasi-compact and separated underived scheme of finite
