Smirnov Decomposition of a Horizontal Vector Charge
in the Heisenberg Group
Zhengyao Huang, Wilhelm Klingenberg
May 14, 2026
Abstract
A divergence-free horizontal vector current in Heisenberg space may be viewed as an
element of the dual space of horizontal test vector fields. By applying a horizontal
Liouville theorem in this setting, the flow lines of such a vector field generate a family
of horizontal curves and an associated measure on this collection. In this paper, we
provide a direct proof of the Smirnov decomposition for a Federer-Fleming current
within the horizontal distribution.

The full scope of this correspondence, however, remains an open question. The space of
flat k-chains Fk(Rn) is defined as the completion of the normal Federer–Fleming k-currents
under the flat norm:
F(T) = inf {M(R) + M(S) : R + ∂S = T} .
While Ambrosio and Kirchheim showed that every flat chain F ∈Fk(Rd) induces a metric
current T ∈Mk(Rn), it is unclear whether the converse holds. In its most concise form, the
k-flat chain conjecture (k-FCC) asserts the natural identification:
Fk(Rd) ∼= Mk(Rd).
Recently, Rabasa and Bouchitté [ AB25 , Theorem A] extended the metric 1-current decom-
position from Lipschitz curves to the class of SBV(I, X) curves, establishing a density char-
acterization of the 1-FCC. In this paper, we study the Smirnov decomposition for horizontal
vector charges in the Heisenberg group Hn. We provide a direct proof adapted from the
arguments given by Arenas and Wengenroth [ RW24 ].
Note that Ambrosio–Kirchheim metric currents of finite mass in the Heisenberg group
coincide with Federer–Fleming horizontal currents of finite mass [ FP25 ] and any horizontal
vector charge of finite total variation corresponds to a metric current. Since the decom-
position is already established at the metric current level by Stepanov and Paolini’s work
[ PS12 ], it can be shown to descend to a classical Federer-Fleming current. The direct proof
presented here offers a self-contained approach and illuminates structural properties of the
decomposition specific to the Heisenberg setting.
