Abstract—Passive multi-target tracking (MTT) aims to infer
the kinematic states of multiple targets from noisy sensor data
in which contributions from unknown target-emitted signals
are superposed. Track-before-detect (TBD) methods improve
robustness to noise by operating directly on raw sensor data
without relying on a preceding detection stage. However, many
existing TBD methods assume that each target’s contribution to
the sensor data is determined solely by its kinematic state. This
assumption limits their applicability to passive MTT, where each
target’s contribution depends on both its kinematic state and the
unknown emitted signal.
We propose subspace TBD, a passive multi-target TBD method
based on a likelihood derived from the complex Bingham distri-
bution that does not require explicit modeling or estimation of the
unknown emitted signals. In a particle filter (PF) framework, each
multi-target hypothesis is mapped to a low-dimensional subspace
spanned by the steering vectors corresponding to the hypothesized
target states. The likelihood is then used to evaluate the alignment
of the normalized multichannel sensor data with this subspace.
Preliminary experiments with simulated acoustic measurements
and a given target activity pattern show that the proposed method
can track two moving targets emitting unknown signals at a
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of −10 dB, whereas a conventional
TBD baseline yields substantially larger tracking errors.
Index Terms—Multi-target tracking, track-before-detect, par-
ticle filter, passive sensing, complex Bingham distribution.
I. INTRODUCTION
This paper addresses MTT, which aims to infer target
states from multichannel noisy sensor data, where these states
typically include kinematic states (e.g., the position and ve-
locity). Many MTT methods first convert the sensor data into
thresholded detections and then perform multi-target inference
using these detections [1]–[7]. This front-end detection step
is a hard, lossy decision-making process. At low SNRs, weak
target evidence can be rejected by thresholding, whereas noise
or clutter can give rise to spurious detections. Because the
tracker only receives the resulting detection set, sub-threshold
evidence is not available to later temporal filtering.
TBD methods avoid this lossy intermediate representation
by evaluating target-state hypotheses directly from the raw
sensor data and by accumulating evidence through the dynam-
ical model [8]–[14]. This makes TBD well suited to low-SNR
tracking. Many existing TBD formulations, especially radar
and sonar TBD with given transmitted waveforms, assume that
each target contribution to the sensor data is fully specified

In this paper, we propose subspace TBD, a multi-target
TBD method designed for passive sensing scenarios involving
superposed target contributions and unknown target-emitted
signals (Fig. 1). The core idea is that the normalized mul-
tichannel sensor data reside near a low-dimensional signal
subspace spanned by the steering vectors associated with the
hypothesized kinematic states. We model this subspace using
an observation likelihood based on the complex Bingham dis-
tribution [15], which characterizes the directional statistics of
normalized complex observations. As a result, the observation
likelihood depends on deviations from the signal subspace,
allowing the likelihood to be evaluated without explicitly
estimating the unknown emitted signals while retaining the
low-SNR advantage of TBD for kinematic tracking under a
given target activity pattern.

II. SUBSPACE TBD: MULTI-TARGET TBD IN THE
PRESENCE OF UNKNOWN EMITTED SIGNALS
We propose subspace TBD, a multi-target TBD method
tailored for passive sensing scenarios with superposed ob-
servations and unknown target-emitted signals. The method
follows the TBD principle of using raw sensor data directly,
thereby avoiding a separate threshold-based detection stage
and exploiting temporal information through a state space
model. Subspace TBD represents each hypothesized target set
as the subspace spanned by its steering vectors. This signal
subspace representation allows the observation likelihood to
be computed without specifying the unknown target spectra
explicitly.

C. Subspace TBD-PF algorithm
We perform inference using an auxiliary PF [16], [17]. At
each time step, particles are propagated by the motion and birth
models, auxiliary weights are computed from the predictive
Bingham subspace likelihood in (3), and ancestor particles are
resampled before the final importance weight update. Thus the
proposed PF retains the TBD advantage of operating directly
on sensor data while using an observation model that avoids
explicit estimation of emitted-signal coefficients for passive
mixtures.
III. RELATION TO PRIOR ART
MTT and superpositional TBD. Classical MTT includes
association-based methods [1]–[3] and random finite set (RFS)
filters [4]–[7]. Earlier direct-measurement MTT/TBD meth-
ods avoided thresholded detections by evaluating multi-target
likelihoods directly from image or sensor data [10], [11]. In
particular, [10] used an association-free likelihood that can
account for multiple targets contributing to the same sensor
cell, whereas [11] developed a RFS-based filter for image
observations under a non-overlap assumption. Superpositional-
sensor RFS filters, in which the observation is modeled as the
additive composition of target contributions and noise, were
further studied in [12]–[14]. Nannuru et al. [14] introduced
a tractable PF approximation of superpositional PHD/CPHD
filters, and Ito and Godsill [8] studied a related PF-based
superpositional TBD model under non-Gaussian noise. These
conventional deterministic-contribution models evaluate the fit
between the unnormalized observation and a sum of target
contributions determined by the hypothesized states. Such
models are appropriate when the target return or intensity
is specified by the kinematic state, but they are mismatched
for passive sensing because the emitted signal coefficients
are unknown nuisance variables. Our model avoids requir-
ing specified emitted-signal coefficients by normalizing the
multichannel observation and scoring only its alignment with
the subspace spanned by the valid targets’ steering vectors.
Compared with the subspace likelihood of Orton and Fitzger-
ald [18], we formulate this alignment as a complex Bingham
likelihood for normalized complex STFT observations and
integrate it into an auxiliary PF for passive multi-target TBD.
Subspace and Bingham modeling. Orton and Fitzgerald
derived a subspace-based likelihood up to a normalizing con-
stant by integrating out unknown emitted signals in multi-target
TBD [18]. Bingham distributions have been used in directional
statistics and tracking [19]–[24], and complex Bingham-type
models have been used for shape analysis, subspace tracking,
and Bayesian subspace estimation [15], [25]–[28]. Our contri-
bution differs in that we use a complex Bingham likelihood
on normalized passive multichannel mixtures so that the filter
estimates kinematic states whose steering-vector subspaces
explain the observed mixtures without explicitly estimating the
emitted target signals.

B. Results
Figures 2 and 3 show the estimated and true x- and y-
positions over time for the proposed and conventional methods,
respectively. For the proposed method (Fig. 2), the estimated
trajectories closely followed the ground truth for both tar-
gets in both x and y dimensions. Even after the second
target appeared at frame t = 100, the filter tracked two
distinct trajectories with only minor deviations from the true
positions. This suggests that the subspace-based observation
likelihood reduced the model mismatch caused by unknown
emitted-signal coefficients under the tested noisy conditions.
In contrast, for the conventional method (Fig. 3), the estimates
deviated significantly from the ground truth. These errors
are attributable to the model mismatch caused by assuming
deterministic target contributions, which does not hold in the
passive sensing scenario. The trajectory run shown in Figs. 2
and 3 had RMSEs of 0.0325 m for the proposed method and
0.9363 m for the conventional baseline.
Figure 4 shows the position RMSE under varying SNR
conditions for both the conventional and proposed methods.
Five independent runs were performed for each condition. The
proposed method consistently achieved a lower median RMSE
than the conventional method across all SNRs. Furthermore,
increasing the number of particles tended to improve the
median RMSE of the proposed method.

V. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we presented subspace TBD for passive MTT
in the presence of unknown emitted signals. The proposed
method addresses a key limitation of conventional TBD algo-
rithms by avoiding deterministic assumptions about the targets’
emitted-signal coefficients Instead of assuming a deterministic
mapping from kinematic state to sensor data, we modeled
normalized multichannel observations in a low-dimensional
signal subspace using the complex Bingham distribution. This
subspace-based observation likelihood depends on how well
the observations align with the span of the valid targets’ steer-
ing vectors, allowing the likelihood to be computed without
explicitly estimating the targets’ spectral content.
