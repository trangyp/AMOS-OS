Abstract
On a mixed-frustration 12-qubit Ising instance run on two D-Wave generations, Advan-
tage2 Zephyr and Advantage system6.4 Pegasus, the late-time subsystem autocorrelation under
cycled reverse annealing sits strictly between two equilibrium reference processes at the device-
calibrated effective temperature: localized parallel tempering, and delocalized equilibrated path-
integral simulated quantum annealing at a fixed Advantage2 pause-point transverse-field scale.
The bracket holds on all three tested schedules and at both hardware calibrations. We obtain this
result through two ingredients: a cycled reverse-anneal protocol (reinitialize state=False,
50 cycles per submission) used as a Markov-chain probe of the device’s pause-point dynam-
ics, and a parallel-tempering falsification framework with bias-corrected and accelerated boot-
strap 95% confidence intervals. Of eighteen tested (instance, schedule) combinations on Advan-
tage2, three are PT-unmatched and correspond to two distinct Ising instances; an independent
native-graph control with no minor embedding on a third mixed-frustration instance repro-
duces the same direction of mismatch.
Among twenty random training instances, schedule
shape modulates basin occupation on six of the thirteen multi-basin-in-readout instances, with
dominant-configuration shifts of up to 38 percentage points including changes of the dominant
configuration. A pre-registered linear predictor of schedule sensitivity from exhaustively com-
putable landscape features fails on ten held-out instances, indicating that schedule sensitivity
is not captured by simple linear functions of the tested landscape moments. The bracketing
result revises an earlier two-pause-enhancement claim and reframes reverse-anneal schedules as
instance-specific basin-occupation probes rather than universal quantum-enhancement knobs.
Introduction
Programmable quantum annealers [1, 2, 3] are routinely used as samplers for statistical-physics
problems [4, 5, 6, 7, 8]. Their forward-anneal output has a reasonably understood interpretation
as biased Boltzmann-like sampling from the programmed Ising Hamiltonian at a device-specific
effective temperature [9, 10], and the open-system dynamics underlying that output have been
characterized within a Schr¨odinger–Lindblad–Markovian hierarchy [11]. The reverse-anneal proto-
col, in which the processor is initialized in a prepared classical state at s = 1, ramped to a pause
point sp < 1 where quantum fluctuations are reintroduced, held for a pause time tp, and returned
to s = 1 for readout, has been proposed as both a local-exploration heuristic and a tool for studying
relaxation dynamics [12, 13, 14, 15, 16].
Two open questions motivate this study. First, does the shape of the reverse-anneal schedule
(the specific trajectory in (t, s) space) control the final sampling distribution, beyond simple tem-
perature rescaling? Second, if the schedule does control the distribution, is that control reproducible

by a classical Monte Carlo process at a matched effective temperature, or does it carry a genuinely

The answers have practical consequences for benchmarking, for the inter-
pretation of QPU samples as thermal-like, and for the experimental reach of quantum-simulation
programs on these devices.
A recent study of subsystem relaxation on D-Wave annealers [17] reported that a two-pause
reverse-anneal schedule (0.45 →0.35) approximately doubled the fraction of mixed-frustration in-
stances for which the subsystem’s final readout fits a quantum reduced-Gibbs target better than its
matched-β classical Boltzmann target. That finding was based on a 40-instance pilot and pointed to-
ward a broader question about schedule engineering. In this work we address the same question with
a fourfold larger dataset, a mixing-time-resolved autocorrelation observable, a parallel-tempering
classical falsification test, a pre-registered structural-predictor validation, and a physical-qubit-set
sensitivity check.
Our results partly support and partly refine the earlier picture. The schedule does modulate
subsystem basin occupation on multi-basin instances, sometimes reassigning the dominant basin
entirely. The larger dataset’s two-pause inversion rate is lower than the baseline single-pause rate
rather than higher: the earlier report’s apparent doubling is consistent with sampling noise in the
smaller pilot (95% CI of the inversion-rate estimate at 40 instances is ≈±15 percentage points),
and in the larger dataset the effect size shrinks to near-zero and reverses direction.
Classical
parallel tempering at the calibrated effective temperature reproduces most schedule-dependent
autocorrelation signals; three of eighteen (seed, schedule) combinations remain unmatched under
a bootstrap-based 95% CI criterion, two of them sharing the same underlying Ising instance, so
the effective number of independent tests among the unmatched cases is closer to two than three.
A pre-registered linear predictor of schedule sensitivity from exhaustively computable landscape
features does not generalise from twenty training instances to ten held-out instances.

Protocol
Subsystem–environment partition.
We work on a D-Wave Advantage2 Zephyr-topology QPU
(solver Advantage2 system1) at a mixed-frustration working point [18]: random 3-regular logical
graphs with N = 12 qubits, a 4-qubit connected subsystem S, an 8-qubit environment E, boundary-
coupling strength λ = 0.5, internal couplings ferromagnetic with bond-frustration probability pS =
0.5 applied only to the S–S edges, and random longitudinal fields hi ∼U[−W, W] with W = 1.0.
These parameters match the mixed-frustration pilot in [17] where the QPU relaxed approximately
70% of instances while matched-β single-spin-flip Glauber dynamics relaxed only 10%.
Schedule families.
Four schedule families were tested (Fig. 1):
• Single-pause reverse anneal, the canonical form: (0, 1) →(τin, sp) →(τin + tp, sp) →(τin +
tp + τout, 1), swept over sp ∈{0.35, 0.40} and tp ∈{100, 500} µs.
• Two-pause reverse anneal with (s1, s2) = (0.45, 0.35) and 50 µs at each depth.
• Quench-and-hold: fast 1 µs ramp to sq = 0.35, 100 µs hold, fast return.
• Forward-anneal control: standard forward anneal, 100 µs total, kept separate from the
reverse-anneal families.
Cycled reverse-anneal protocol.
A key observable is the Markov-chain autocorrelation C(k) of
the subsystem configuration across K repeated reverse anneals. Setting reinitialize state=False
in the sampler [12] causes each of num reads consecutive anneals to start from the previous an-
neal’s final state, so a single submission of K reads produces a K-cycle Markov chain through the
reverse-anneal operator. For each (schedule, seed, initial state) we submit nchains independent jobs
of length K = 50 cycles.
Observables.
We use the memory order parameter M (the maximum pairwise total variation
distance between subsystem marginals obtained from different initial states), two endpoint dis-
tances Dcl and Dq (TVD to the classical conditional Boltzmann and the quantum reduced-Gibbs
target at the pause point, respectively), the pair contrast ∆D = Dcl −Dq, and the Markov-chain
autocorrelation C(k). A basin is defined via steepest descent: the pre-image of each local mini-
mum under the deterministic single-spin-flip descent map [19], computed once per Ising instance
by exhaustive enumeration of the 2N = 4096 configurations.
Effective temperature.
The effective inverse temperature βeff was measured in situ using single-
qubit probes with bias h = 0.5 under reverse annealing at each pause depth, following the protocol
of [10]. At sp ∈{0.30, 0.35, 0.40, 0.45} we measured βeff = 7.07 ± 0.37, 7.26 ± 0.49, 7.22 ± 0.06, and
7.55 ± 0.38, respectively. The variation across the tested pause-depth window is within calibration
uncertainty and is too small to explain the schedule-dependent effects reported below.

Exhaustive basin mapping of the 20 random seeds shows that every instance has multiple classical
local minima (between 8 and 13 minima on the 2N = 4096 configuration hypercube, Fig. 2(a)). In

Schedule-dependent basin occupation
Pooling subsystem samples across gauges, bath states and initial states per (schedule, seed) gives the
end-of-reverse-anneal distribution PS(schedule, seed). On the six responsive multi-basin seeds, the
two-pause schedule measurably redistributes basin weight relative to the single-pause(s=0.4, t=100)
baseline. On seed 4096 the two-pause schedule concentrates PS from 62% to 100% on the same
dominant configuration +--- (a +38 pp concentration shift). On seed 8192 it does the opposite,
redistributing from the dominant -+++ configuration (87% →65%, a −22 pp spreading). On seeds
42 and 12011 the identity of the dominant configuration changes under the two-pause schedule. Two
smaller-shift seeds (1024 and 2048, with dominant-configuration shifts of approximately −10 pp
each) round out the responsive population. The effect is not single-direction: schedule engineering
is not a uniform “sharpen” or “flatten” knob; it reassigns probability weight among competing
near-degenerate minima in a way that depends jointly on the Ising instance and on the schedule
(Fig. 3).

Pathway dynamics probed by cycled reverse annealing
On seed 8192 the cycled reverse-anneal autocorrelation C(k) distinguishes schedules monotonically
with pause depth and duration: the baseline single-pause at sp = 0.4, t = 100 µs has C(k = 49) =
0.256 (BCa 95% CI [0.189, 0.378]), the two-pause schedule has 0.578, and the long-hold single-
pause at sp = 0.35, t = 500 µs has 1.000. Deeper and longer pauses progressively freeze the chain
into a narrower attractor, consistent with schedule-dependent access to transverse-field-mediated
pathways during the pause phase (Fig. 4).
This pathway signature survives on a minority of
multi-basin seeds (roughly 3 of 11 tested) and is absent on the rest, where either the chain trivially
collapses on the single ground-state basin or never mixes under any schedule. The effect is therefore
real but neither universal in the multi-basin population nor monotone in schedule parameters.

Classical falsification
We tested whether classical parallel tempering (PT; replica-exchange Monte Carlo) [20, 21, 22] at a
matched cold-replica temperature β = βeff = 7.22 and a temperature ladder β ∈{1.0, 2.0, 4.0, 7.22}
can reproduce the QPU-observed C(k = 49) for each (seed, QPU schedule) combination. For each
seed we ran PT at four sweep budgets nsweeps ∈{10, 50, 200, 1000} per cycle with 15 chains per
(seed, init, budget). The maximum tested PT budget (1000 sweeps per output cycle, 4000 sweeps
including the temperature ladder) sets the classical reach we can falsify against; budgets beyond
this and stronger baselines such as Houdayer-cluster Monte Carlo [23, 19] or spin-vector Monte
Carlo [24] are not tested here and remain open as future controls. We defined unmatched by a
bootstrap 95% CI criterion: the QPU observation’s CI must not overlap the 95% CI of any tested
PT configuration at the same seed.
Under this criterion, 3 of 18 (seed, QPU schedule) combinations are unmatched (Fig. 5):
• Seed 8192, baseline single pause(sp = 0.4, t = 100 µs): QPU C(49) = 0.256, BCa 95%
CI [0.189, 0.378]; PT envelope across four sweep budgets [0.53, 1.00]. The QPU chain explores
actively; no tested PT budget matches that exploration at the device β.
• Seed 14029, long-hold single pause(sp = 0.35, t = 500 µs):
QPU C(49) = 0.711
[0.633, 0.811]; PT envelope [1.00, 1.00] (PT is fully frozen on this seed at every tested sweep
budget). The QPU remains partially mobile where PT does not.
• Seed 14029, two pause(0.45 →0.35): QPU C(49) = 0.911 [0.856, 0.967]; PT envelope
[1.00, 1.00]. Borderline; the two-pause and long-hold cases on this seed share the underlying
Ising instance.
A physical-qubit-set sensitivity test repeated the three unmatched cases plus two matched
controls on five independent minor-embedding placements [25, 26] on Advantage2. On seed 8192
baseline the unmatched signal is robust: C(49) remains in [0.156, 0.378] across all five placements,
well below the PT envelope. On seed 14029 long-hold all five placements stay strictly below 1.0; on
seed 14029 two pause one of five placements gives C(49) = 0.944, borderline against the tight PT
envelope. The unmatched signal is therefore not a specific-qubit hardware defect on Advantage2,
and the two 14029 cases should be interpreted as sharing one underlying Ising instance with a
borderline second schedule.
A cross-solver check on Advantage system6.4 (Pegasus topology) was performed on the same two
seeds under a controlled protocol: length-1 embeddings on all twelve logical qubits (chain break fraction
= 0 in the returned sample sets), explicit chain strength set to 2.0, and a new PT baseline at
β = 4.29 matched to Advantage system6.4’s own calibrated βeff.

Reconciliation with an earlier pilot claim
A previous study on the same mixed-frustration working point [17] reported that the two-pause
schedule doubled the “quantum-over-classical inversion rate” (the fraction of relaxed instances with
Dcl > Dq) from 17% at the baseline single-pause to 44% at two-pause. Applying the same definition
to our fourfold larger dataset (160 conditions per schedule), we find baseline single-pause(s=0.4,
t=100) at 43% [33, 52%], single-pause(s=0.35, t=100) at 41% [33, 50%], and two-pause at 33%
[24, 41%]. The two-pause rate is the lowest of any reverse schedule tested, not the highest; long-
hold single-pause(s=0.35, t=500) at 52% [44, 60%] is the highest reverse schedule. The pilot’s
40-instance sample size gives a 95% CI of roughly ±15 percentage points on the inversion rate, so
the original “doubling” was within one to two standard errors of the null. In the larger dataset the
doubling is not present.

Discussion
We set out to test whether reverse-anneal schedule shape controls subsystem relaxation beyond
simple temperature rescaling. Four findings.
First, the schedule does modulate endpoint distributions on the subset of multi-basin-in-readout
instances. The effect is instance-specific: it ranges from zero (GS-dominant-in-readout instances)
to ±38 percentage points in dominant-configuration weight (seeds 4096 and 8192), and on two
instances (42, 12011) the schedule changes the identity of the dominant basin.
Across twenty
random seeds, six instances show an effect larger than five percentage points and fourteen do not.
The effect direction is not monotone: some schedules concentrate, others redistribute.
Second, instance-specificity is structural: it is not captured by simple linear functions of the
tested landscape features. On an initial 20-instance training set, mean barrier from the ground
state and gap to the second-lowest local minimum correlated with observed |∆Dgap| at Pearson
r ≈+0.5. We pre-registered linear predictors and tested them on 10 held-out instances; the held-out
correlations (r = −0.01 and +0.22) were not significantly different from zero and the held-out R2
was negative for every pre-registered predictor. The training-set signal was small-sample overfitting,
and a pre-registered a priori predictor using these features is not supported. The held-out failure
is itself informative: it locates schedule sensitivity at a level of basin geometry that is not reducible
to scalar landscape moments such as mean barrier height or gap to the second minimum, and it
motivates pre-registered nonlinear or basin-graph-based predictors as a natural next step.
Third, when we resolve dynamics via cycled reverse annealing, the schedule is visible in the
Markov-chain autocorrelation C(k) on a minority of multi-basin seeds. The cleanest effect is a
monotonic ordering by pause depth on seed 8192, where deeper and longer pauses progressively
freeze the chain, consistent with greater transverse-field influence during the pause, which on this
Hamiltonian correlates with a narrower attractor.
Fourth, classical parallel tempering at the calibrated effective temperature reproduces the
schedule-dependent autocorrelation in 15 of 18 (seed, schedule) combinations tested on Advantage2.
Three QPU observations (seed 8192 baseline, seed 14029 long-hold, and seed 14029 two-pause,
corresponding to two distinct Hamiltonian instances) are not matched by any tested PT configu-
ration’s bootstrap 95% CI. The Advantage2-specific seed 8192 signal survives a physical-qubit-set
sensitivity test across five independent minor-embedding placements but is not replicated on Advan-
tage system6.4 at its own calibrated βeff; we cannot disentangle whether that is a hardware-specific
calibration effect or a β-dependent physical effect. The two seed 14029 cases share an underlying
Ising instance and do reproduce on Advantage system6.4 at its own βeff = 4.29, on all three tested
schedules, under a controlled protocol with length-1 embedding (chain break fraction = 0). Seed
14029’s PT-unmatched signal is therefore a cross-hardware-replicated effect at two substantially
different βeff values and on two distinct QPU topologies (Zephyr and Pegasus).
We do not claim a universal quantum pathway effect. We claim: on a mixed-frustration 12-qubit
Ising instance (seed 14029) the late-time subsystem autocorrelation under cycled reverse annealing
sits strictly between two equilibrium reference processes at the device-calibrated effective temperature
— frozen parallel tempering, and delocalized equilibrated SQA at a fixed Advantage2 pause-point
transverse-field scale — and this bracket holds on two D-Wave generations (Zephyr and Pegasus) at
two substantially different βeff values. Beyond this bracketing instance, reverse-anneal schedules act
as instance-specific basin-occupation probes; on a small subset of multi-basin instances the resulting
dynamics are not matched by parallel tempering at matched effective temperature within the tested
sweep budgets. The subset is narrow, not predictable from the linear structural features tested, and
does not preferentially appear on the two-pause schedule that a previous pilot had proposed would
maximize the quantum signature. The generalizability of this result is limited by the tested sample
12


## Page 13


size: only 6 of 20 logical training seeds show measurable basin shifts, and the PT-unmatched claim
is carried by 2 distinct Hamiltonian instances on Advantage2 plus 1 mixed-frustration native-graph
instance. A cluster-worldline or Houdayer-style Monte Carlo baseline [23], a scaling study at larger
N, and a larger random-instance sample with a pre-registered nonlinear predictor are the natural
next steps.
The practical implication for users of programmable annealers as samplers is twofold. Schedule
engineering is a real knob for basin occupation on multi-basin frustrated instances, but the effect
is instance-specific and not captured by simple linear functions of the landscape features we tested.
For most instances the schedule-plus-QPU combination is indistinguishable from parallel tempering
at matched effective temperature; for sampling applications, the choice of reverse-anneal schedule
is therefore unlikely to give a universal quantum advantage over a well-tuned classical sampler.
Which specific instances do benefit from schedule engineering, and which classical baselines be-
yond matched-β PT can or cannot reproduce the observed QPU dynamics on those instances, are
open questions best addressed by a pre-registered nonlinear structural-predictor study on a larger
random-instance sample together with a stronger classical baseline.
Methods
Hardware and calibration.
Experiments used the D-Wave Advantage2 Zephyr-topology QPU
under the solver identifier Advantage2 system1. Effective temperature βeff was measured in situ by
submitting single-qubit probes with bias h = 0.5 and no couplings at the target pause depth, 5,000
reads per initial state, three probe qubits, with auto scale=False. Schedule coefficients A(sp) and
B(sp) were taken from the solver-specific anneal-schedule tables published by D-Wave [28].
Ising construction.
Random 3-regular logical graphs with N = 12 nodes were generated per
seed. A connected four-node subsystem S was chosen, with the remaining eight nodes forming
E.
Internal S–S edges were sign-flipped with probability pS = 0.5; boundary S–E edges had
strength λ = 0.5 and remained ferromagnetic; E–E edges remained ferromagnetic. Longitudinal
fields hi ∼U[−W, W] with W = 1.0.
Schedules.
Schedule families are defined in the main text. Single-pause schedules used (τin, τout) =
(5, 5) µs. Two-pause schedules used 5 µs between the two hold phases. The quench-and-hold sched-
ule used a 1 µs ramp to sq = 0.35.
Cycled reverse-anneal protocol.
Each Phase 3A job submitted a single sample ising call
with reinitialize state=False and num reads= K = 50, producing a 50-cycle Markov chain.
Each chain was initialized with the specified subsystem state; the environment was fixed to the all-
up configuration. For each (seed, schedule, initial-state) we ran nchains independent chains (default
nchains = 30 in v3 and nchains = 50 in v2). Gauge averaging was not applied in v3 (single gauge).
Parallel-tempering baseline.
The PT implementation uses a fixed temperature ladder β ∈
{1.0, 2.0, 4.0, 7.219} with nearest-neighbor replica exchange every sweep; a second ladder ending at
β = 4.29 is used for the Advantage system6.4 comparison. Each PT chain emits the cold-replica
configuration once per cycle. For each (seed, initial state, sweep budget) we ran 15 independent PT
chains of length K = 50 cycles with nsweeps ∈{10, 50, 200, 1000}. The PT envelope at a given seed
is the union of the 95% bootstrap CIs on C(k = 49) across all four tested sweep budgets; the QPU
observation is called unmatched if the QPU 95% CI does not overlap any of the four PT 95% CIs
13


## Page 14


at the same seed. CIs are bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa) bootstrap [29] over 2000 resamples
across chains (chains treated as independent units; within-chain serial correlation is absorbed into
the chain-level statistic C(k = 49)). BCa uses a bias-correction z0 from the fraction of bootstrap
replicates at or below the point estimate and an acceleration a from the jackknife leave-one-out
replicates; this improves coverage over the percentile bootstrap when C(k = 49) is near the natural
boundary at 0 or 1. For seeds where PT is fully frozen (C = 1.0 with zero across-chain variance,
e.g.
seed 14029 in the baseline PT ladder at β = 7.219), both the jackknife variance and the
bootstrap variance vanish and the CI degenerates to a point [1.0, 1.0]; in such cases the unmatched
verdict is equivalent to the QPU showing strictly less than perfectly frozen behavior under the same
reads-per-cycle budget, and should be read as a comparison against a noiseless classical reference
rather than against a noisy classical baseline.
Basin mapping.
For each seed we enumerated all 2N = 4096 classical spin configurations, com-
puted the Ising energy of each, identified local minima (configurations for which every single-spin
flip raises the energy), and traced every configuration to a local minimum by deterministic single-
spin-flip steepest descent (Gauss–Seidel, lowest-index tie-break). Basin volumes, ground-state basin
fraction, gap to second-lowest local minimum, number of distinct subsystem-projected basin finger-
prints, and minimum-max-energy barriers from the ground state basin to the next-largest basins
(Dijkstra on the hypercube) are extracted per seed.
Pre-registered predictor.
A two-feature linear predictor |∆Dgap| = α+β1·mean barrier from gs+
β2·gap to second minimum was fit on the 20 training seeds (coefficients and per-seed predicted val-
ues for the 10 held-out seeds archived in the supplementary file predictor preregistration.json
prior to the held-out QPU run). Pre-registered success criteria: held-out Pearson r significantly
different from zero (p < 0.05, n = 10) and held-out R2 > 0.
