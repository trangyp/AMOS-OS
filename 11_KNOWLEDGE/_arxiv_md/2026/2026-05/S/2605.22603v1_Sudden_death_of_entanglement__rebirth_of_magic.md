Sudden death of entanglement, rebirth of magic
Chenfeng Cao1, 2, ∗

Local Markovian noise cannot bring entanglement back, but it can bring magic back. Unlike separability,
stabilizer membership is not preserved by local channels, allowing dissipation to push states out of the stabilizer
polytope as well as in. Under local amplitude damping, the n-qubit GHZ family α |0n⟩+ β |1n⟩(0 < α <
β) loses its magic at a lower damping strength γ−and regains it at a higher one γ+, while entanglement is
irreversibly lost at γe. This magic–entanglement complementarity, γe + γ+ = 1 for every n, reflects a system–
environment duality of amplitude damping and persists for a broader class of dissipative channels. For small
α, the reborn magic resides in a fully separable state with all proper marginals stabilizer, yet parity-syndrome
extraction concentrates it onto a single qubit for magic-state distillation. Local dissipation further divides pure
stabilizer states into magic-generators and magic-insulators: at two qubits, the Bell state

Irreversibility under local Markovian noise is resource-
dependent. For entanglement, once a state is separable, every
subsequent local completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP)
map keeps it separable, so entanglement sudden death un-
der amplitude damping is irreversible [1–3]. The canonical
amplitude-damping instance is the Yu–Eberly family α |00⟩+
β |11⟩[4, 5], realized in photonic experiments [6, 7]. Magic
(nonstabilizerness) [8–11] is the non-Clifford resource pro-
moting stabilizer circuits [12, 13] to universal quantum com-
putation [14]. By definition, a non-stabilizer-preserving chan-
nel maps some stabilizer state outside the stabilizer polytope,
hence generates magic [8, 15, 16]. Amplitude damping is an
elementary example: on one qubit it maps |+⟩to a state with
Bloch vector (√1 −γ, 0, γ), outside the stabilizer octahedron
since √1 −γ + γ > 1 for 0 < γ < 1. The stronger question
here is whether, for a multipartite state, magic can disappear
at finite time and return along a deterministic local Markovian
trajectory, possibly after full separability sets in.
Magic can return in this way because the free operations
of magic and entanglement are asymmetric: stabilizer oper-
ations may be entangling, while local CPTP maps preserve
separability without necessarily preserving stabilizer member-
ship [15, 16]. Writing the stabilizer polytope as S, trajectories
ρ(γ) driven by local dissipation can exit S and, since they ter-
minate at the vertex |0n⟩⟨0n| ∈S, must eventually re-enter it.
Whether this happens depends on trajectory shape: an affine
segment ending in S can enter S only once, since its intersec-
tion with S is a single interval containing γ = 1. Under local
amplitude damping, generic inputs give curved trajectories.

Discussion and outlook.—Unlike non-Markovian entangle-
ment revivals or reservoir sudden birth [48, 49], the return
here is Markovian and non-unital, concerning magic rather
than entanglement. In Regimes I and II, the whole reborn
branch lies in fully separable states with stabilizer marginals,
giving a dynamical realization of many-body magic hidden
from local reductions [11, 39, 40]. This is distinct from the
nonlocal nonstabilizerness of Ref. [50], defined via local-
unitary minimization, and from the stronger pure-state notion
of long-range magic [40, 51, 52], which captures magic ro-
bust against shallow circuits. The Bell-state splitting and the
magic–entanglement complementarity identified here com-
plement recent studies of the relation between magic and en-
tanglement structure [53, 54].
The closed-form thresholds also give an exactly solv-
able instance of mixed-state stabilizer-polytope membership,
whose general decision problem requires super-exponential
time [55]. Related tolerant-testing questions for closeness to
pure stabilizer states are addressed in [56]. Inside the stabi-
lizer window the state is a stabilizer mixture, hence efficiently
classically simulable [13]. Outside the window, the robustness
controls the classical-simulation overhead [31, 57–59]. The
simulation overhead is therefore non-monotone in γ, driven
by the channel’s non-unitality.
