Abstract
Remote sensing vision-language models commonly rely on pretrained visual en-
coders to convert images into semantic features before language-model reasoning.
While effective for scene-level understanding, this pipeline may prematurely com-
press local visual evidence, making fine-grained spatial reasoning vulnerable to
language priors, especially in ultra-high-resolution remote sensing imagery. We
present SkyNative, a native multimodal framework for remote sensing that adopts
an encoder-free architecture, removing the pretrained visual backbone to directly
represent images as raw patch tokens in the language-model token space. To
reconcile low-level visual patches with textual tokens, SkyNative introduces a
modality-aware decoupling mechanism that uses modality-specific parameters
within a unified autoregressive backbone. We further introduce a visual reliance
benchmark that diagnoses whether models ground their answers in image evidence
through progressive visual degradation and misleading textual prompts. Across
standard remote sensing understanding tasks and large-format spatial reasoning
evaluations, SkyNative shows stronger image-grounded perception and improved
robustness against prompt-induced language priors. These results suggest that
native patch-level multimodal modeling is a promising direction for reliable remote
sensing vision-language reasoning.

Introduction
Remote sensing (RS) imagery poses a distinctive challenge for vision-language reasoning. Unlike
natural images, RS scenes often cover large spatial extents, contain dense and small objects, and
require models to capture subtle spatial relations across complex backgrounds. Recent remote sensing
vision-language models (RS-VLMs) have achieved promising progress in visual question answering,
scene classification, image captioning, and instruction-following tasks [1, 2]. Most of them adopt
an encoder-based architecture, as shown in Fig. 1(a), where a pre-trained vision encoder [3, 4] first
converts the input image into high-level visual features, which are then projected into the token space
of a large language model (LLM) for cross-modal reasoning [5, 6].
Despite its effectiveness, the encoder-based paradigm may introduce a semantic bottleneck between
raw visual evidence and language-model reasoning. Since the LLM receives encoded visual features
rather than the original image content, fine-grained evidence required for local perception or spatial
reasoning may be weakened before the reasoning stage. This issue is particularly important in remote
sensing, where small objects, local structures, and spatial relations can be decisive for answering a
question. As shown in Fig. 1(b), when asked “What type of vehicle is located behind the blue car on
the road?”, an encoder-based model answers “car”, while the corresponding visual region actually

Our preliminary visual reliance analysis further supports this concern. As shown in Fig. 1(c), encoder-

answers can still be recovered without usable visual evidence, suggesting substantial reliance on
textual or dataset priors. Progressive degradation experiments show a similar trend, and Fig. 1(d)
further indicates that visual tokens receive relatively limited attention during inference. Although
attention alone is not a causal explanation, these observations consistently suggest that existing
encoder-based RS-VLMs may lack a sufficiently image-centric prediction mechanism, leading to
visually unsupported answers in spatial reasoning tasks [8].
We hypothesize that this phenomenon is partly related to the mismatch between pre-trained vision
encoders and autoregressive language models. Vision encoders are typically optimized for image-
level representation learning or contrastive alignment, whereas LLMs are trained with next-token
prediction. As a result, visual evidence may be compressed into high-level semantic features before
entering the LLM, weakening the local details needed for spatial grounding. Rather than assuming
that visual encoders are universally inadequate, we investigate whether removing the pre-trained
semantic bottleneck can improve visual reliance in RS-VLMs.
To this end, we propose SkyNative, a native multimodal framework designed for remote sensing
imagery. To achieve this, SkyNative adopts an encoder-free architecture, which removes the pre-
trained visual backbone commonly used to produce semantic image features. Instead, SkyNative
directly maps raw image patches into the language-model token space and optimizes visual and
textual tokens under a unified autoregressive objective. This design allows patch-level visual evidence
to participate more directly in answer generation. To address the distributional differences between
visual patches and text tokens, SkyNative further introduces a modality-aware decoupling mechanism,
which assigns modality-specific parameters to visual and linguistic inputs while preserving a unified
backbone for cross-modal reasoning.
Beyond model design, we introduce a multi-dimensional visual reliance benchmark to diagnose
whether RS-VLMs ground their predictions in image evidence. The benchmark evaluates models

Related Work
Remote Sensing Vision-Language Models.
Adapting general-purpose VLMs to the geospatial
domain has established robust baselines for RS visual understanding and reasoning, with representa-
tive works including RSGPT [5], GeoChat [6], and VHM [9]. To process complex RS imagery and
extract meaningful visual evidence, current frameworks predominantly rely on heuristic workarounds
to bypass the representational bottlenecks of standard vision encoders. Basic image manipulations,
such as the global rescaling utilized by GeoChat [6] and the tile-and-concatenate operations em-
ployed in GeoPixel [10], inevitably impose a trade-off between fine-grained detail preservation and
computational efficiency. Furthermore, to localize critical visual cues while maintaining tractable
sequence lengths, recent research isolates query-relevant regions through techniques like the token
pruning methods adopted by GeoLLaVA-8K [11] and Coarse-to-fine [12], or the dynamic region
search proposed in ZoomEarth [13]. Despite these gains, the inherent feature compression of vision
encoders inevitably discards fine-grained spatial topologies. Consequently, the downstream language
model cannot directly access raw pixel-level evidence, which often leads it to over-rely on semantic
priors rather than conducting strictly evidence-based visual reasoning in complex scenes.
Visual Reliance and Linguistic Priors.
Recent evaluations have highlighted the “Mirage” effect [7],
“visual sycophancy” [14], and “linguistic biases” [15] in VLMs, underscoring how semantic guessing
often inflates benchmark performance, masking true visual dependence. Stress tests have assessed
visual reliance via noise injections [7, 16] and explored textual susceptibility through deceptive
prompts [17, 18]. Recent robustness assessments in geospatial interpretation have provided a critical
foundation for investigating this phenomenon. While their primary objective was to evaluate general
model stability rather than explicitly disentangle visual reliance from linguistic bias, these prior
studies have provided valuable intervention strategies. These include the distractors utilized in
CHOICE [19], the counterfactuals proposed in RSHallu [20], and the image-blind testing protocols
introduced by RSHR-Bench [21] and OmniEarth [22]. However, these benchmarks typically evaluate
visual and textual factors independently, providing limited insight into the model’s overall visual
reliance. To address this, we propose a specialized remote sensing benchmark that systematically
assesses both visual reliance and susceptibility to misleading textual cues, offering a more reliable
measure of RS-VLMs’ visual understanding and robustness.

Methodology
In this section, we present the overall architecture and training pipeline of the proposed SkyNa-
tive framework, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Unlike conventional RS-VLMs that rely on a standalone
pretrained visual encoder, SkyNative follows a pretrained-vision-encoder-free paradigm. It maps
native RS images into patch-level visual tokens through a shallow convolutional embedding layer,
and feeds them together with textual token embeddings into a unified decoder-only Transformer for
autoregressive reasoning.

3.1
Vision-Language Encoding Layer
To preserve fine-grained textures and spatial structures in RS imagery, SkyNative removes the
standalone pretrained visual encoder and directly constructs visual tokens from native image patches.
Given an input RS image I ∈RH×W ×3, we employ a lightweight convolutional patch embedding
layer consisting of two convolutional layers with a GELU activation in between:
zv = Conv2(GELU(Conv1(I))) ∈RH′×W ′×d,
(1)
where d denotes the hidden dimension of the language model. The resulting 2D feature map is then
flattened into a sequence of visual tokens:
xv = Flatten(zv) + pv,
(2)
where pv denotes the visual positional embedding. This shallow mapping avoids compressing the
image into high-level semantic features too early, thus preserving local visual evidence for subsequent
reasoning.
For the text sequence T, we use the tokenizer and embedding matrix of the base LLM:
xt = Et(Tokenizer(T)),
(3)
where Et is the textual embedding matrix. The visual tokens xv and textual tokens xt are concatenated
into a unified multimodal sequence and processed by the autoregressive Transformer backbone.

3.4
Training Strategy
The training of SkyNative follows a three-stage progressive strategy, moving from visual token
learning to geospatial instruction tuning. Stage I: Vision Perception Learning. We freeze the
LLM parameters and train only the patch embedding layer, enabling raw RS image patches to be
projected into the LLM token space. Stage II: Modality-Aware Alignment. We jointly train the
patch embedding layer and the modality-specific visual parameters, while keeping the core textual
parameters frozen. This stage aligns visual tokens with textual semantics and reduces cross-modal
representation conflicts. Stage III: Geospatial Supervised Fine-Tuning. We unfreeze the full
architecture for supervised fine-tuning on remote sensing instruction datasets. This stage strengthens
the model’s ability to integrate visual evidence with linguistic instructions for geospatial reasoning.
Dataset sources are summarized in Appendix A.1.
3.5
RSME-Bench Construction
To evaluate whether RS-VLMs genuinely rely on visual evidence rather than exhibiting a bias towards
language priors under cross-modal conflicts, we construct RSME-Bench by combining progressive
visual degradation with misleading textual prompts. Detailed data sources, construction procedures,
and examples are provided in Appendix B.
Image Selection and Visual Degradation. We manually select 291 image patches with a resolution
of 512 × 512 from DOTAv2-test [23] and DIOR-val [24]. The selected samples contain visually
recognizable objects and spatial relations that are sufficient for answering the corresponding questions
under the original visual condition.To simulate different degrees of visual evidence loss, we apply
three levels of degradation: L1 uses 70% scale downsampling with mild Gaussian noise, L2 uses
40% scale downsampling with moderate Gaussian noise, and L3 uses 15% scale downsampling with
salt-and-pepper noise. We further introduce a Blind Test setting, where the visual input is replaced
with a zero matrix Inull, forcing the model to answer without valid image evidence.
Annotation with Misleading Text Prompts. We use GPT-4o-mini [25] to generate misleading
textual prompts for 331 selected image samples. Following [26], we construct misleading textual
prompts using three strategies: Repetition, Credibility, and Logical Construction. These prompts
contain false but linguistically plausible claims that conflict with the image evidence. Three domain
experts then manually revise the generated prompts and remove logically flawed instances. Each
instance is expanded into 8 VQA pairs, resulting in 2,648 evaluation pairs.

Experiments
In this section, we comprehensively evaluate SkyNative. Sec. 4.1 and Appendix A introduce the
experimental setup, covering tasks, benchmarks, baselines, and metrics. Subsequently, we report
main quantitative results regarding perception and reasoning across both foundational patch-level and
ultra-high-resolution scales (Sec. 4.2). Finally, we analyze visual reliance and robustness (Secs. 4.3
and 4.4), followed by ablation studies (Sec. 4.5).
4.1
Experiment Setup
Benchmarks and Baselines. We evaluate SkyNative on a diverse suite of remote sensing benchmarks
covering patch-level perception, cross-modal reasoning, ultra-high-resolution interpretation, and

visual reliance stress tests. We compare SkyNative with closed- and open-source general-purpose
VLMs, encoder-free models, RS-VLMs, and large-format RS models. Detailed benchmark and
baseline descriptions are provided in Appendix A.2 and Appendix A.3.
Implementation Details. We train SkyNative on two H200 GPUs using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as
the initialization model. We adopt DeepSpeed ZeRO-3, BF16 mixed precision, and the AdamW
optimizer with a cosine scheduler and a warmup ratio of 0.03. Each training stage is conducted for
one epoch. Stage I uses a maximum learning rate of 5 × 10−5, a maximum sequence length of 2048,
and a total batch size of 256. For Stages II and III, the learning rate is reduced to 5 × 10−6, while the
maximum sequence length and batch size are set to 4096 and 192, respectively. For visual processing,
images are processed with an any-ratio strategy and converted into non-overlapping 16 × 16 patch
tokens with a stride of 16.
Evaluation Metrics. We report Accuracy (Acc.) for classification and VQA tasks, Mean Absolute
Error (MAE) for counting tasks, and MTEM@1 for strict multi-turn dialogue consistency. Higher Acc.
and MTEM@1 indicate better performance, while lower MAE is preferred. For visual degradation
tests, we report the Causal Decay Rate (∆c), where a larger value indicates that more originally
correct predictions fail after visual evidence is degraded. Detailed formulations are provided in
Appendix A.4.
4.2
Main Results and Analysis
In this section, we comprehensively evaluate SkyNative. We first assess its foundational perception

reasoning performance on ultra-high-resolution benchmarks.
Foundational Perception and Reasoning: Patch-Level Benchmarks. To preliminarily verify

mitigating potential reliance on linguistic priors, we comprehensively evaluated it on RSME-Bench

CHOICE [19] as a complementary benchmark to ensure evaluation objectivity and generalizability.
Visual Degradation Test. As shown in Table 3, SkyNative achieves strong original accuracy on
CHOICE (57.02%) and RSME-Bench (79.76%). Under progressive visual degradation, SkyNative

within deeper layers, SkyNative allocates a higher proportion of attention to visual information. This
suggests a more balanced synergy between the LLM’s inherent reasoning capabilities and visual
perception, allowing valid visual evidence to continuously guide the reasoning process.

Conclusion
In this work, we introduce SkyNative, a novel remote sensing encoder-free vision-language model. By
bypassing traditional vision encoders to directly ingest raw visual signals and employing a modality-
aware decoupling mechanism, our approach effectively mitigates the limitation of over-reliance
on linguistic priors during geospatial interpretation tasks. Rigorous experiments demonstrate that
SkyNative achieves state-of-the-art results on comprehensive benchmarks spanning multi-task patch-
level and ultra-high-resolution imagery. Furthermore, comprehensive explorations via progressive
visual degradation and blind tests validate its enhanced ability to focus on and leverage genuine
visual evidence during reasoning. Consequently, its superior performance and robust reasoning
capabilities naturally make our model suitable for future extensions toward more complex remote
sensing reasoning models and geospatial agents.

A
Detailed Experimental Setup
A.1
Train Datasets
Our training pipeline consists of three distinct stages, leveraging a comprehensive mixture of remote
sensing datasets. Stage I utilizes 912k samples sourced from NWPU [40], RSICD [41], RSITMD [42],
Sydney-caption [43], and RSTeller [44]. Stage II incorporates 686k samples from RSVQA-LR [45],
Floodnet [46], and GeoChat-instruction [6]. Stage III is trained on 380k samples derived from the
GeoCoT (w/o CoT) dataset [47].
A.2
Tasks and Benchmarks.
To evaluate the foundational perception, reasoning, and robustness of SkyNative, we conduct ex-
tensive experiments across a diverse suite of remote sensing (RS) benchmarks spanning various
granularities. For foundational perception, we assess global scene understanding on AID [48]
and RS19 [49], while fine-grained local perception is evaluated through dense object counting on
DOTA-val [50], HRRSD [51], VHR [52], and VisDrone [53]. Cross-modal reasoning capabilities are
verified via VQA tasks on RSVQA-HR [45] and VRSBench [54]. To further showcase the model’s
scalability in complex geospatial logic, we utilize ultra-high-resolution multi-task benchmarks in-
cluding XLRS [35], MME-RW-RS [36], LRS-VQA [12], and RSHR [21]. Finally, to investigate
visual evidence reliance and mitigate the “mirage effect”, we conduct stress tests under progressive
degradation and textual interference using CHOICE [19], alongside robustness and hallucination
assessments on the OmniEarth [55] and HnstD [9] benchmarks.
A.3
Detailed Baseline Models.
To comprehensively evaluate SkyNative, we compare it against a diverse suite of state-of-the-art
vision-language models across both general and remote sensing domains. The general-purpose
baselines include closed-source models, namely Gemini-2.0 [27] and Claude-4 [28], alongside
open-source frameworks such as Qwen2.5-VL [30], DeepSeek-VL2 [37], MiniGPT-v2 [31], and
VoCoT [32]. We also encompass general-domain encoder-free architectures, specifically EVEv2 [29],
BREEN [38], and Emu3 [39]. Within the specialized remote sensing domain, our comparisons feature
leading models including GeoChat [6], VHM [9], SkySenseGPT [33], and EarthDial [34]. Finally,
for ultra-high-resolution geospatial imagery, we incorporate specialized large-format remote sensing
models, namely GeoLLaVA-8K [11], Coarse-to-fine [12], and ZoomEarth [13].

D
Limitations
While SkyNative effectively utilizes genuine visual evidence for spatial reasoning, it currently relies
on a standard autoregressive training paradigm. A notable limitation is the absence of advanced
Reinforcement Learning (RL) based reasoning mechanisms. Recent studies have demonstrated that
integrating RL into multimodal models can significantly mitigate confidence miscalibration and
enhance overall reasoning robustness. Therefore, exploring RL-driven reasoning paradigms to further
optimize model reliability and self-evaluation represents a promising direction for our future work.
E
Broader Impacts
This work improves the reliability of remote sensing interpretation by mitigating linguistic bias,
benefiting geospatial applications such as agriculture and urban development. However, pixel-level
processing of large-format imagery increases computational overhead. Additionally, enhanced
automated interpretation of high-resolution data raises inherent privacy concerns. Future research
should address these computational limitations and establish ethical deployment frameworks.
