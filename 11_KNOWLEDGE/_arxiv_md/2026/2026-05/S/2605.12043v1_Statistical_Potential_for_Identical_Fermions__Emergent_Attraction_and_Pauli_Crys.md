Statistical Potential for Identical Fermions:
Emergent Attraction and Pauli Crystal Formation
Kawon Lee, Sangeun Oh, Young Woo Choi,† and Jeong-Hyuck Park∗
Department of Physics, Sogang University, Seoul 04107, Korea
We show that the thermodynamics of N identical fermions maps onto that of distinguishable particles governed by a
collective statistical potential—the microscopic origin of degeneracy pressure. Known to be purely repulsive for N = 2,
this potential develops attractive contributions for N ≥3. Its minima coincide with Pauli crystal configurations, providing
the energetic origin of these structures. For large N, the dominant force is attractive on inner shells and repulsive on
outer ones—not of two-body origin. The global minimum undergoes discrete melting transitions at specific temperatures.
I.
Introduction
Pauli crystals are geometric structures emerging in the
joint probability distribution of identical fermions solely
from the Pauli exclusion principle, even in the absence
of interparticle interactions [1–3]. Unlike Wigner crys-
tals [4], which arise from Coulomb repulsion, Pauli crys-
tals do not break symmetry at the one-body level: the
density remains smooth, and crystalline order appears
only in higher-order correlations.
Recent experimental advances in deterministic few-
fermion preparation, quantum-gas microscopy [5–10],
and Pauli crystal superradiance [11] have enabled their
direct observation, reinforcing the prevailing view that
these structures are purely kinematic consequences of an-
tisymmetrization, without an energetic origin.
In this Letter, we show that this prevailing view is in-
complete: the statistical potential [12, 13] is not purely
repulsive (it is so only for N = 2), Pauli crystal formation
has a precise energetic origin, and its thermal melting
proceeds via discrete structural transitions. We demon-
strate that the thermodynamics of N identical fermions
can be recast as that of distinguishable particles in-
teracting through a collective, configuration-dependent
statistical potential—exactly for harmonic traps and at
leading semiclassical order for generic potentials. Min-
imizing the resulting effective potential—a purely clas-
sical optimization—reproduces Pauli crystal configura-
tions with sub-percent accuracy in particle positions.

Pairwise statistical forces evaluated at the minimum

particle, we identify the partner exerting the strongest
pairwise force. For N = 6, the dominant force on every
particle is repulsive (center ↔outer)—no attractive bond
dominates. For N = 55, a qualitatively different picture
emerges: the dominant pairwise force on inner-shell par-
ticles (3+9+9) is attractive, directed toward neighboring
shells, while that on outer-shell particles (17+17) is re-
pulsive, directed outward—a dual structure characteris-
tic of the Pauli crystal.
The temperature dependence of crystal formation and
melting, studied in [2, 19], can be characterized through
the statistical forces (Fig. 4). For N = 55, the maximum
pairwise force switches from repulsive to attractive be-

The crossover from repulsive to attractive domi-

Dual mechanism.—The pairwise force (10) cannot arise
from any two-body potential: for large N, it organizes
into a dual structure in which the dominant pairwise force
is attractive on inner shells and repulsive on outer shells
(Fig. 3), in contrast to small N (Fig. 2).
Physical reality.—The statistical potential and degener-
acy pressure share a common origin: both arise from
antisymmetrization of the many-fermion wavefunction.
Since degeneracy pressure is an experimentally estab-
lished bulk effect—supporting white-dwarf cores and gov-
erning the incompressibility of trapped Fermi gases—the
configuration-dependent forces encoded in Vstat should
likewise be regarded as physical rather than merely for-
mal. Conversely, the standard view of degeneracy pres-
sure as purely repulsive reflects only its macroscopic av-
erage; microscopically, Vstat contains attractive contribu-
tions for N ≥3, in analogy with Cooper pairing where
effective attraction also requires the many-body Fermi
sea. This dual microstructure should leave experimen-
tally accessible signatures—for instance through anoma-

Outlook.—The statistical potential framework opens sev-
eral directions:
spinful fermions (where antisymmetry

The force-based criterion intro-
duced here—crossover of the globally dominant pairwise
force from repulsive to attractive—may serve as an exper-
imentally accessible diagnostic for quantum crystalline
order, complementing existing single-shot imaging tech-

We anticipate that this dual microstruc-
ture will manifest across systems where antisymmetriza-

where it should be directly observable, to dense astro-
physical objects such as white dwarfs and neutron stars,
whose equilibrium is set by degeneracy pressure.

The minima of Vtotal(X) are consistent with experimentally observed Pauli crystals. Holten et al. [3] measured the

B.
Numerical Methods
All minimizations of Vtotal(X) are performed using the L-BFGS-B algorithm with analytic gradients. Because Vtotal
is a nonconvex function of DN = 2N variables (110 variables for N = 55), we employ a multi-start strategy: for
each (N, φ), the optimizer is run from at least 300 independent random initial configurations using shell-type seeds
(concentric rings with random perturbations). The lowest-energy result is retained. For N = 55 at φ = 2, the best
minimum is reproduced across multiple seeds to within ∼10−3 a0 in particle positions, supporting convergence to a
low-lying minimum. The phase diagram (Fig. 5) uses 1000 seeds per (N, φ) point. The maximization of |Ψ0(X)|2
for the ground-state Slater determinant follows an analogous multi-start L-BFGS-B procedure with 300 seeds and
numerical gradients.
