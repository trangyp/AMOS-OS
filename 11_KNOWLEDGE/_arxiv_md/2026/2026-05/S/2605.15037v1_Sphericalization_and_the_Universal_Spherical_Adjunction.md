SPHERICALIZATION AND THE UNIVERSAL SPHERICAL ADJUNCTION
FERNANDO ABELLÁN AND JONTE GÖDICKE
Abstract. For every adjunction of stable ∞-categories –or more generally, in any locally stable
(∞, 2)-category– we give a simple procedure for inverting the twist and cotwist functors associ-
ated to this adjunction. As a consequence, we obtain an explicit construction for a left and right
adjoint to the inclusion of the (∞, 2)-category of spherical adjunctions of stable ∞-categories
into all adjunctions. We utilize these adjoints to give a description of the walking spherical ad-
junction, a locally stable (∞, 2)-category which classifies spherical adjunctions, and to provide
a synthetic proof of the fact that every spherical functor admits infinitely many left and right
adjoints.
1. Introduction
Suppose that we are given an adjunction of stable ∞-categories L: C −→
←−D: R. To this datum,

say that our adjunction is spherical.
Spherical adjunctions were introduced in [AL17] by Anno and Logvinenko as a generalization
of Seidel and Thomas notion of spherical objects [ST01]. While initially developed as a tool to
construct braid group actions on bounded derived categories, spherical adjunctions have, over the
past decade, become of independent interest [Chr22, DKSS24]. One source of this growing attention
is their connection to the theory of perverse schobers, introduced by Kapranov and Schechtman
[KS14]. Perverse schobers are a proposed categorification of perverse sheaves, and in this context,
spherical adjunctions are expected to provide local models for a schober on a disk with a singularity
at the origin.
In the decategorified setting, classical constructions provide systematic ways to produce perverse
sheaves from more general constructible ones. For spherical adjunctions, however, no comparable
constructions are currently available. The aim of this paper is to develop a (co)free construction
for spherical adjunctions.

which we hope to address in future work is to compute the closed part and provide an interpretation
of this categorified recollement in terms of higher sheaf theory.
