Semi-interlaced polytopes
Fedor Selyanin ∗
Abstract
The Minkowski mixed volume of n subpolytopes D1, . . . , Dn of a polytope P ⊂Rn
clearly does not exceed the normalized volume n! Vol(P). Equality holds if and only if the
subpolytopes are interlaced, i.e., each proper face F ⊊P intersects at least dim(F) + 1 of
the polytopes Di. Efficiently computing mixed volumes for more general collections of sub-
polytopes is crucial for estimating the complexity of numerically solving polynomial systems.
Motivated by relaxing the bound dim(F)+1 to dim(F), we prove a combinatorial formula
for the mixed volume of a broad class of semi-interlaced polytopes. This class includes, in par-
ticular, the off-coordinate polytopes used in computing algebraic degrees – such as Maximum
Likelihood, Euclidean Distance, and Polar degrees – via the Kouchnirenko–Bernshtein the-
ory. We also present applications of our results to the Arnold monotonicity problem [Arn04,
1982-16], which concerns the dependence of Milnor numbers on the Newton polyhedra.
Keywords. Mixed volume, Newton polytope, algebraic degrees, projective toric variety

Introduction
The mixed volume is the polarization of the volume form; thus, the mixed volume MV(P, . . . , P)
of n copies of the polytope P ⊂Rn equals the normalized volume n! Vol(P), see §2.1. The
mixed volume is monotonic in its arguments, meaning that for any collection of subpolytopes
D1, . . . , Dn ⊂P, the inequality MV(D1, . . . , Dn) ≤n! Vol(P) holds. A natural question arises:
when does equality occur?
The answer is as follows. Assume dim(P) = n, since otherwise the equality holds trivially.
Then MV(D1, . . . , Dn) = n! Vol(P) if and only if the polytopes D1, . . . , Dn are interlaced in
P, i.e., for any proper face F ⊊P, at least dim(F) + 1 of the polytopes Di intersect F. A more
general “mixed” version of this statement also exists, where a tuple of polytopes P1, . . . , Pn is
considered instead of a single polytope P (with Di ⊂Pi). The complete answer for rational
polytopes in the mixed case was first given in [Roj94, §4.7, Corollary 9] and later extended to
all convex polytopes in [BS19] (see also [BS19, Remark 3.6]). The conditions for the “unmixed”
case discussed here were first presented in [Es05, Remark 3.9] and later rediscovered in [BS19,
Corollary 3.7].
This problem was initially considered in [Roj94] in the context of theoretical computer sci-
ence. Recall that by the Bernstein–Kouchnirenko theorem (also known as the BKK bound),
the number of solutions of a (sparse) polynomial system equals the mixed volume of the corre-
sponding Newton polytopes in the Newton nondegenerate case (see §2.2). A standard method for
numerically solving a polynomial system f is the homotopy continuation method (see, e.g., [CL]).
Briefly, the method works as follows: first, we consider a start system g with the same Newton
polytopes, whose solutions are known; then, we construct a homotopy from f to g and track the
solutions along the homotopy (using a standard ODE predictor method). The mixed volume
equals the number of solutions and thus serves as a key measure of the method’s computational
complexity.
We now consider the following question: how does the mixed volume change if we weaken
the bound from dim(F) + 1 to dim(F)? We partially answer this question by defining semi-
interlaced polytopes and providing a combinatorial formula for their mixed volume in terms
of the usual volumes of certain associated polytopes (see Theorem 3.6). The answer is partial
because we impose additional restrictions on the polytopes (see Definition 3.1). Strictly speaking,
semi-interlaced polytopes do not generalize interlaced polytopes (see Figure 4). We present two
different proofs of the formula for the mixed volume of semi-interlaced polytopes:
• §4.2: A convex-geometric proof using a formula for the mixed volume;
• §4.4: An algebro-geometric proof based on the geometry of projective toric varieties.
The first proof is purely combinatorial, relying on a formula of Khovanskii for the mixed
volume (see §2.4), and applies to arbitrary convex polytopes. The second proof interprets the
formula in terms of singularities of projective toric varieties (recalled in §2.5 and 2.6) but is
restricted to lattice polytopes. Note that not every convex polytope is combinatorially equivalent
to a convex lattice polytope (see, e.g., [Z08]).
Computing the mixed volume is also an important problem, but is a significantly harder
numerical problem than computing the usual volume (see, e.g., [DGH98] and [Che19]). The
formula for the mixed volume of semi-interlaced polytopes is computationally efficient because it

2.1
Polyhedral geometry and the mixed volume
This subsection recalls basic notions of mixed volumes and support functions. For a comprehen-
sive introduction to convex geometry, we refer to [Ew]. Throughout this paper, we consider only
convex polytopes.
Definition 2.1. A polytope is the convex hull of a finite set of points in Rn. A polytope is
called lattice if all its vertices belong to the lattice Zn.
Definition 2.2. A polyhedron is the intersection of finitely many half-spaces. A bounded
polyhedron is a polytope.
Definition 2.3. The Minkowski sum P1 + P2 of two polytopes is the polytope defined as
{p1 + p2, | p1 ∈P1, p2 ∈P2}. If we consider Rn as an affine space, then the Minkowski sum is
defined up to translation.
Notation 2.4. Let A be an affine space equipped with a full-rank lattice L ⊂A. We use the
lattice volume VolZ, which is normalized so that the volume of a unit simplex of L equals 1.
In particular, the lattice volume of any lattice polytope is an integer. In the standard space Rn,
we consider the standard lattice Zn.
Notation 2.5. Consider an affine space A with a full-rank lattice L. An affine subspace A′ ⊂A
is called rational, if rk(aff(A′) ∩L) = dim(aff(A′)). In this case, we assume that A′ is equipped
with the lattice L′ = L ∩A′, and that the quotient space A/A′ is equipped with the quotient
lattice L/L′.
Notation 2.6. Suppose that F is a subset of an affine space A with a full-rank lattice L ⊂A,
and that its affine span aff(F) is rational (with the corresponding lattice K ⊂aff(F)). Denote
