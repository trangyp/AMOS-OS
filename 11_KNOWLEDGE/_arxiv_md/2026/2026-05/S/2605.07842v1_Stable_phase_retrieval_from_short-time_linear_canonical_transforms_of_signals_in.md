Abstract. Gabor phase retrieval for signals has attracted considerable attention in recent
years. For the more general short-time linear canonical transform (STLCT), which arises
naturally in optical systems and canonical time–frequency analysis, existing work has so
far focused mainly on uniqueness and sampling conditions. Explicit reconstruction formu-
las, quantitative stability estimates, and robust reconstruction algorithms, however, are still
missing. In this paper, we study uniqueness, stability, and robust reconstruction for phase
retrieval from phaseless STLCT measurements in the complex Gaussian shift-invariant space
V ∞
β (φ). We first prove that every signal in V ∞
β (φ) is uniquely determined, up to a global
unimodular constant, by its phaseless STLCT measurements on the semi-discrete set β
2 Z×R,
and we derive an explicit reconstruction formula. We then establish stability on intervals un-

where the reconstruction error is controlled by the discretization parameters, the noise level,
and the conditioning induced by the anchor points. In the Fourier case, our results recover
the corresponding Gabor phase retrieval results of Grohs and Liehr and provide improved
stability constants.
1. Introduction
Phase retrieval concerns the reconstruction of a function f from magnitude-only measure-
ments { |ψ(f)| : ψ ∈Ψ }, where Ψ is a family of linear functionals acting on a function
space. Such problems are inherently nonlinear and arise naturally in many scientific and en-
gineering applications in which detectors record only intensities rather than the underlying
signal, including X-ray crystallography [48], coherent diffractive imaging [13], and optics [55].
From a mathematical point of view, phase retrieval is a nonlinear inverse problem in which
one seeks to recover a function from quadratic measurements. Its central questions concern
injectivity of the associated nonlinear measurement map, stability of the inverse problem,
and the design of constructive reconstruction procedures, see [26, 30] for surveys, as well as
[3, 4, 5, 10, 14, 17, 20, 28, 34, 36, 53] and references therein.
A classical example is Fourier phase retrieval, corresponding to the choice of Ψ as the
Fourier transform. In this setting, the modulus of the Fourier transform does not determine
the signal uniquely. Besides the trivial ambiguities of translation, conjugate reflection, and
multiplication by unimodular constants, there exist infinitely many essentially different func-
tions sharing the same Fourier magnitude [1, 12, 39, 55]. This shows that taking the modulus

numerical experiments illustrating the effectiveness of the proposed method.

The lower bound A(pj) ≥eγ in Step 2 guarantees that each anchor point carries sufficiently
large amplitude, while the spacing constraints in Step 2 ensure that the detected anchor points
are neither too separated nor too dense. The condition pj+1 −pj ≤r ensures that consecutive
anchor points are close enough for stable local reconstruction and phase propagation, whereas
pj+2 −pj ≥r yields a bound on the total number of anchor intervals, which is crucial in the
proof of robustness when passing from local discretization errors to a global reconstruction
bound. Without loss of generality, we may assume that r ≤2s. Indeed, if r > 2s, then at most
two anchor points can be selected in the interval [−s, s]. Otherwise, if three anchor points
p1 < p2 < p3 are selected, then p3 −p1 ≤2s < r, which contradicts the spacing condition
pj+2 −pj ≥r.
Note that the exact constant γ in Condition (P) is typically unknown in practice. The
algorithm therefore works with a threshold eγ extracted from the data. Below we show that,
under suitable assumptions, f satisfies condition (P) at the chosen anchor points with an
effective lower bound inherited from eγ.
