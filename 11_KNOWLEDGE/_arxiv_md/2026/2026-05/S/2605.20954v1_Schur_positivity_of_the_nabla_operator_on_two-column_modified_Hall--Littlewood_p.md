SCHUR POSITIVITY OF THE NABLA OPERATOR ON TWO-COLUMN
MODIFIED HALL–LITTLEWOOD POLYNOMIALS
MENGHAO QU
Abstract. In this paper, we investigate the Schur positivity of modified Hall–Littlewood polynomials
indexed by two-column partitions under the action of the ∇operator.
Specifically, we resolve two
conjectures posed by Bergeron, Garsia, Haiman, and Tesler in the two-column case. Furthermore, our
approach demonstrates that these results can be extended to arbitrary powers ∇k for all integers k ≥1.
1. Introduction
Building upon the foundational work of I. G. Macdonald [Mac88], the modified Macdonald polynomials
˜Hµ[X; q, t], introduced by Garsia and Haiman [GH93], have emerged as a central object of study in
modern algebraic combinatorics. They constitute a remarkable basis of symmetric functions situated at
the intersection of algebraic combinatorics, representation theory, and algebraic geometry. In a celebrated
result, Haiman [Hai01] established that these polynomials arise as the Frobenius characteristic of the
doubly graded Garsia–Haiman module [GH93]. Here, the Frobenius characteristic map serves as a crucial
bridge between symmetric functions and the representation theory of the symmetric group Sn, mapping
the irreducible characters χλ to the Schur functions sλ.
Haiman’s breakthrough ultimately resolved
Macdonald’s positivity conjecture, which asserts that the Schur expansion of Macdonald polynomials
yields coefficients in N[q, t], meaning that they are polynomials in q and t with nonnegative integer
coefficients.
A pivotal tool in navigating this rich algebraic structure is the ∇operator, introduced by Bergeron and
Garsia in [BG99]. By definition, ∇is a linear operator that acts diagonally on the modified Macdonald
basis via the eigenvalue equation ∇˜Hµ[X; q, t] = Tµ ˜Hµ[X; q, t], where Tµ = tn(µ)qn(µ′) with n(µ) =

Over the past few decades, understanding the action of the ∇operator on various
symmetric functions has been a major driving force in the field, as evidenced by several foundational
works [Hai94,GHT99,BGHT99,HHL05,HHL+05,HMZ12]. A central, overarching theme in this pursuit
is the quest for Schur positivity.
In the realm of symmetric functions, establishing Schur positivity
is of profound importance: it typically implies that the function in question is not merely a formal
algebraic sum, but rather the genuine character of a naturally occurring Sn-module, thereby revealing
deep underlying geometric or representation-theoretic phenomena.
A quintessential example of this
phenomenon is the symmetric function ∇en, whose Schur positivity follows naturally from its realization
as the Frobenius characteristic of the module of diagonal harmonics [Hai94]. Beyond its explicit monomial
expansion, which was conjectured in [HHL+05] and established by Carlsson and Mellit [CM18] in their
proof of the shuffle conjecture, the Schur positivity of ∇en can also be gracefully deduced. Namely, the
combinatorial formula for ∇en can be formulated as a weighted sum of LLT polynomials, introduced by
Lascoux, Leclerc, and Thibon [LLT97]. Relying on the foundational result of Grojnowski and Haiman
[GH07], who proved the Schur positivity of all LLT polynomials, this expansion immediately yields the
Schur positivity of ∇en. Inspired by these profound connections, the algebraic combinatorics community
has extensively investigated the action of the ∇operator on other fundamental bases. For instance, it is
well documented that the symmetric functions ∇ωpn [LW07,Ser17], ∇sλ [LW08,BHM+25,KO24], ∇mµ
[Ser18,QX25], ∆eken [HRW18,DM22,BHM+23], and ∆sλen [HRS19] all exhibit Schur positivity (though
some cases remain conjectural), up to a potential global sign.
Within this context, a natural yet formidable problem is to understand the action of ∇on the modified
Hall–Littlewood polynomials. These are defined by specializing the modified Macdonald polynomials at
q = 0, and their Schur positivity is well known, as they arise as the Frobenius characteristics of the
Garsia–Procesi modules [GP92].
In an influential paper [BGHT99], Bergeron, Garsia, Haiman, and
Tesler posed the following two conjectures regarding this highly nontrivial action.

Given partitions λ, µ ⊢n, a semistandard Young tableau (SSYT) of shape λ and weight µ is defined as
a filling of the Ferrers diagram of λ using elements from the multiset {1µ1, 2µ2, . . . } such that the entries
weakly increase across each row and strictly increase down each column. Let SSYT(λ, µ) denote the set
of these fillings, and let Kλ,µ be its cardinality. The integer Kλ,µ is known as the Kostka number.
Among the various combinatorial statistics on SSYTs, the cocharge statistic, a modification of the
charge statistic first introduced in [LS78], is central to our study. We follow the notations in [GG24].
Definition 2.2. Given T ∈SSYT(λ, µ), the reading word σ(T) is obtained by reading the entries row by
row from top to bottom, and from left to right within each row. The first cocharge subword is obtained
by searching the reading word from right to left for a 1, then continuing the search from that position
to locate a 2 (cyclically wrapping around the word as needed), and proceeding in this manner up to the
maximal entry of the word. The cocharge labeling of a permutation is determined by performing a similar
right-to-left cyclic search. Processing the entries 1, 2, . . . , n in increasing order, we assign a label of 0
to the entry 1. The label assigned to i + 1 is incremented if and only if i + 1 appears to the left of i in
the permutation; otherwise, it remains the same. We then similarly find and label the second cocharge
subword among the remaining unlabeled letters. This process is repeated iteratively until all letters have
been assigned a label. The cocharge of T is the sum of the cocharge labels of its reading word.
