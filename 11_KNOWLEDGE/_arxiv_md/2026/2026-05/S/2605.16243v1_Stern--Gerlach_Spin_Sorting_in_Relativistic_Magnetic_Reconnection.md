Evaluating Ξeff across representative space and astrophysical environ-

Magnetic reconnection converts magnetic energy into
bulk flow, heating, and nonthermal particle acceleration
through a localized nonideal region in which field lines
change connectivity [1–4] and in large-scale diamagnetic
cavities, formed in reconnection outflow regions near
cusp-like magnetic field geometries [5–7]. In relativistic
plasmas—especially pair plasmas relevant to compact-
object coronae, magnetar magnetospheres, and some
high-energy laboratory settings—one can ask whether
quantum spin physics ever becomes dynamically relevant
in the diffusion region [8–10]. Spin-fluid and spin-kinetic
plasma theories have existed for years [11–13], but recon-
nection still lacks a compact regime parameter answering
the practical question: when can the SG force compete
with classical orbit dynamics?
This Letter gives such a parameter and keeps two state-
ments distinct.
First, SG deflection in a reconnecting
current sheet can be quantified by a dimensionless ra-
tio. Second, the same force produces a topology-locked,
magnetic-moment-tagged north–south anisotropy in pair
plasmas. Whether this anisotropy modifies tearing onset
or global reconnection dynamics is a separate stability
problem and is left for future work.

magnetic-moment projection are deflected into the same
half-plane, whereas equal spin projections correspond to
opposite µ∥and therefore opposite SG deflections. Fig-

species-tagged.

To verify the branch-sorting prediction, we performed
electron–positron spin-kinetic particle-in-cell simulations
using SpinPIC2D, a 2.5-dimensional code that advances
a Harris current sheet with BMT spin precession, an
optional full-step SG momentum kick, a Boris push,
and a Yee field update [37, 38].
See the Supplemen-
tal Material [18] for the branch-resolved Stern–Gerlach
derivation, pair-plasma sign map, transit-speed closures,
SpinPIC2D numerical parameters, implementation de-
tails, and supplemental diagnostics.
SpinPIC2D pro-
vides two compile-time particle-push paths: a standard
non-relativistic Boris velocity-space rotation [1], valid
when vth ≪c (γ ≈1.01), and an implemented relativis-
tic Boris integrator in momentum space.
The A1/B3
verification runs reported here deliberately use the non-
relativistic path (βtr ≈0.14, γ ≈1.01) because the tested
prediction is the branch-sign relation in Eq. (2), con-
trolled by sgn(µ∥) and ∂Bx/∂z. The coupling constant
χ is chosen so that Eq. (6) gives Ξ0 ≃10 for the sim-
ulation parameters. The astrophysical regime estimates
in Table I are analytical evaluations of Eq. (6); they are
not direct simulations of those environments. These runs
therefore serve as a controlled, kinematic proof of concept
rather than a direct numerical model of any astrophysi-
cal source. Here, the two runs are compared at identical
numerical parameters and the same formal SG-coupling
input χ = 0.0691, which corresponds to Ξ0 ≃10 if the SG
force is enabled. The A1 and B3 runs use the same initial
particle loading and spin random seed; the only difference
between them is whether the SG momentum kick is com-
piled in so any divergence between the two runs at t > 0
is attributable solely to the SG force.
A1 is the clas-
sical null control compiled without the SPIN_SG_FORCE
flag, so the SG momentum kick is disabled; B3 is com-
piled with -DSPIN_SG_FORCE, activating the SG force at
the same parameter set. The initial spin distribution is
isotropic, so the net ensemble spin polarization vanishes;
the diagnostic therefore tests branch-resolved µ∥-sorting
rather than a net center-of-mass force. Figure 2 shows
the resulting moment-tagged spatial asymmetry in the
B3 run. At t = 0 the distribution is consistent with the
isotropic spin initialization.
As reconnection develops,
the µ∥> 0 branch becomes enhanced on the north side
of the sheet, while the µ∥< 0 branch becomes enhanced
on the south side, as predicted by Eq. (2). Figure S2
of the Supplemental Material [18] shows the correspond-
ing A1/B3 time series: the reconnected fluxes overlap to
within 0.1%, while the B3 centroid and baseline-corrected
asymmetry diagnostics develop the predicted signs.

Choice of Peff and its definition
The effective participation factor Peff ∈(0, 1] represents the density-weighted fraction of particles whose µ∥projec-

µ∥branch individually satisfies Eq. (25) regardless of the ensemble polarization. We adopt Peff = 0.1 as a fiducial

SPIN-KINETIC PARTICLE-IN-CELL SIMULATION
SpinPIC2D is a 2.5-dimensional spin-kinetic particle-in-cell code that advances a spin-Vlasov–Maxwell system in
a Harris-sheet geometry; the A1/B3 verification runs reported here use the nonrelativistic Boris path (vth = 0.141c,
γ ≈1.01). The simulation plane is (x, z), with x along the outflow and z along the inflow. Fields are advanced on
a Yee staggered grid with Nx × Nz = 256 × 256, Lx = Lz = 25.6de, and dx = dz = 0.1de. Boundary conditions
are periodic in x and conducting in z. Each timestep applies a half-step BMT spin precession, an optional full-step
SG momentum kick, a full Boris push, a second half-step BMT spin precession, current deposition, and a Yee field
update. The SG kick is compiled out unless the code is built with SPIN_SG_FORCE.
The initial state is a single Harris current sheet centered at z = Lz/2: Bx = B0 tanh[(z −Lz/2)/λ], with density
n(z) = n0sech2[(z −Lz/2)/λ] + nbg.

Vdrift = 0.1c, and vth = 0.141c. Spins are initialized isotropically so ⟨s⟩= 0 and the net ensemble SG force vanishes
initially. Reconnection is seeded by a sinusoidal flux perturbation with amplitude ε = 0.05. Figure 3 verifies the

BMT approximation
The spin precession in Eq. (11) retains only the dominant magnetic contribution to ΩBMT. In the nonrelativistic
limit this is the leading term; electric-field contributions to the full covariant BMT equation are subdominant in the
low-speed validation run (βtr ≈0.14) and are omitted consistently in the controlled A1/B3 comparison. In highly
relativistic environments the electric-field term, the anomalous magnetic-moment correction, and radiation damping
should all be included in a fully covariant extension; providing such an extension is beyond the scope of the present
verification study, which is limited to demonstrating kinematic branch sorting at controllable parameters.
