1. Introduction
Bipartite entanglement embezzlement is a phenomenon in quantum information where
two parties (Alice and Bob) use a shared state ψ on a shared resource space and perform
quantum operations on their resource space and their own register spaces (without commu-
nication) to obtain an entangled state in their shared register space, without disturbing the
original shared state. In a sense, the players have “stolen” entanglement from ψ and now
possess it in their own register spaces, without leaving evidence of this act in the state ψ.
Mathematically, we can describe the process as follows. Suppose the players share a resource
Hilbert space H that is in the state ψ, and that each of their register spaces are dimension
d. Then embezzling entanglement to obtain an entangled state φ = Pd−1
i=0 αiei ⊗ei, where
α0 ≥· · · ≥αd−1 ≥0 and Pd−1

and Bob a unitary V ∈B(H ⊗Cd) for which [U ⊗Id, Id ⊗V ] = 0 and, up to a flip of tensors,
(1.1)
(U ⊗Id)(Id ⊗V )(e0 ⊗ψ ⊗e0) = φ ⊗ψ.
(For φ to actually be entangled and not separable, we require at least two of the αi’s to be
non-zero.) Work of van Dam and Hayden [25] shows that one can always do this procedure
approximately in a finite-dimensional tensor product framework (that is, where H = HA ⊗
HB, dim(H) < ∞, and U only acts non-trivially on Cd⊗HA and V on HB ⊗Cd). It is known
that the dimension of the space H must tend to infinity as the error in the embezzlement
protocol tends to zero [15]. Moreover, even in the case when H = HA ⊗HB and HA and
HB are infinite-dimensional, achieving (1.1) exactly is impossible [3], although it is always
possible in a commuting operator framework (see, for example, [3,11]).

Recent work of van Luijk et. al [17] considers approximate embezzlers. That is, they
consider the setting of a von Neumann algebra M ⊆B(H) and a state (unit vector) ψ ∈H
with the property that, for every entangled state φ ∈Cd ⊗Cd of full Schmidt rank and for
every ε > 0, there exist unitaries Uφ,ε ∈Md⊗M and Vφ,ε ∈M′⊗Md for which (1.1) holds for
Uφ,ε and Vφ,ε within an error of ε. They proved that in such a setting, M is never semifinite
as a von Neumann algebra, and when the state induced by ψ is faithful on both M and
M′, both algebras are Type III. Moreover, they proved that a certain function measuring
how well embezzlement can be done in a separable approximately finite-dimensional Type
III factor M actually classifies the subtype λ ∈[0, 1] of M, and hence the algebra itself up
to isomorphism if λ ̸= 0. They also show that approximate embezzlers exist in all (separable,
approximately finite-dimensional, or AFD) Type III factors with subtype λ where λ ̸= 0.
(In the case of Type III0, some factors do exhibit embezzlement, while others perform as
badly at embezzlement as semifinite factors [17].) One tool used in their study is reducing
bipartite (approximate) embezzlement to an appropriate form of monopartite (approximate)
embezzlement, which in their case is an approximate unitary equivalence of certain states on
Md ⊗M induced by e0 ⊗ψ ⊗e0 and φ ⊗ψ. While some of these techniques can be passed
to exact embezzlement, others rely crucially on approximation arguments that do not hold
in the exact setting.

φ ∈Cd ⊗Cd of full Schmidt rank.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we provide the links
between bipartite exact embezzlement and monopartite exact embezzlement, and show that
these always reduce to Cuntz isometries for the two parties with a certain quasi-free condition
on the state ψ. In section 3, we prove the main self-testing results of the paper, namely,
that up to a compression, bipartite (respectively, monopartite) exact embezzlement protocols
for φ must be unitarily equivalent. In section 4, we examine some of the modular theory
for exact embezzlement when ψ is cyclic and separating (that is, when M is in standard
form) and show that M is never semifinite, recovering a result from [17] in the exact case
(by alternate means). We also determine the type of the algebra generated by the Cuntz
isometries involved.
2. Entanglement embezzlement and Cuntz algebras
In this section, we examine exact embezzlement protocols for an entangled state φ ∈
Cd ⊗Cd of full Schmidt rank, and relate these to Cuntz algebras and quasi-free states in the
sense of [12]. We also formulate a monopartite form of exact embezzlement and show that
one can pass between the two, without changing the observable algebras involved.

4. Type III factors for exact embezzlement
In this section, we will determine the (unique) von Neumann algebra factor that is
generated by the collection of d Cuntz isometries involved in monopartite exact embezzlement
of φ when the state ψ is faithful. We will rely on results in modular theory for von Neumann
algebras to determine the type of Alice’s (respectively, Bob’s) von Neumann algebra. There
are many excellent resources on modular theory of von Neumann algebras (such as [22–24]
to name a few). The reader is directed to these sources and the references therein for more
information; we recall the facts that we need below.
Modular theory was a major tool in the classification program for injective von Neu-
mann algebras, and can be carried out for any faithful normal, semifinite weight on a von
Neumann algebra M. In the case where M is not separable (that is, does not have separable
predual, or equivalently, cannot be represented faithfully and normally on a separable Hilbert
space), there is no faithful normal state on M and weights are necessary. Fortunately, in
our case we are only considering a von Neumann algebra M where the catalyst vector ψ
induces a faithful state on M (which is automatically normal since it is a vector state), so
we may assume that M is separable below.
Modular theory is carried out when the von Neumann algebra M ⊆B(H) is represented
in standard form. We do not need the full strength of standard form of a von Neumann
algebra below, so we refer the reader to the work of U. Haagerup [8] on this matter. One
setting where a von Neumann algebra M ⊆B(H) is in standard form is when there is a
unit vector ψ ∈H that is cyclic for M (that is, Mψ = H) and separating for M (that is,
if X ∈M and Xψ = 0, then X = 0). For bipartite exact embezzlement, after compressing
the space and the algebras by the support projections of the marginals on M and M′,
we may assume that the marginals ω and ω′ are faithful on M and M′, respectively, by
Proposition 2.11, and hence that ψ is cyclic and separating for M. Thus, we may assume
that M ⊆B(H) is in standard form.
