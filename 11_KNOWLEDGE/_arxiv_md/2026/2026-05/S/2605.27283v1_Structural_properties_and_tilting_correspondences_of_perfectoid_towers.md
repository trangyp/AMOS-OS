KAZUKI HAYASHI
Abstract. We prove that every perfectoid tower can be decomposed into a ﬁber product of
perfectoid towers that are either p-torsion free or perfect of characteristic p. As an application,
we show that separated perfectoid towers are reduced. We also establish the tilting invariance
of ´etale cohomology and Koszul homology for perfectoid towers.

1. Introduction
In commutative algebra in mixed characteristic, the theory of perfectoid rings has become
a powerful tool, notably through Y. Andr´e’s breakthrough resolution of the direct summand
conjecture. Recently, S. Ishiro, K. Nakazato and K. Shimomoto [INS25] introduced the notion
of perfectoid towers, which provide an approximation of perfectoid rings by Noetherian rings, as
well a generalization of perfectoid rings. After that, various constructions of perfectoid towers
were found ([Ish26, IS25, HIS26]). However, their intrinsic properties have remained largely
unexplored. In this paper, we therefore study the structure and properties of perfectoid towers
and apply them to a fundamental problem in perfectoid theory, namely, tilting correspondences.
Let us ﬁrst review some of results on structural properties of perfectoid rings, mainly from
[ˇCS24, §2.1]. All typical examples of perfectoid rings are either p-torsion free or entirely p-
torsion (i.e., have characteristic p). In fact, any perfectoid ring is decomposed into perfectoid
rings with either one of these properties:

As in the case of perfectoid rings, we deduce from Theorem A that every ring appearing in a
perfectoid tower is reduced under the separatedness assumption (Corollary 3.12). Moreover, we
establish a gluing theorem for perfectoid towers (Proposition 3.17). This result not only provides
a tower-theoretic analogue of Proposition 1.2, but also yields a new method for constructing
perfectoid towers with possible p-torsion. Prior to this work, the only known construction of
this kind was due to Ishiro–Shimomoto ([IS25, §4.4]), whose examples are recovered as special
cases of our construction (Example 3.19). In contrast to the explicit and laborious calculations
in [IS25], our approach is more conceptual and structural. Furthermore, combining the gluing
theorem with Theorem A, we prove that perfectoid towers are stable under ind-´etale (or, more
adequately, weakly ´ettale) base change (Theorem 3.28).

Corollary 4.15. Let ∆be a simplicial complex, and C(k) the Cohen ring of a ﬁeld k of
characteristic p > 0. The following conditions are equivalent.
(1) The p-Stanley–Reisner ring C(k)[∆] is Cohen–Macaulay.
(2) The Stanley–Reisner ring k[∆] is Cohen–Macaulay.
(3) eHq(lk F; k) = 0 for all F ∈∆and all q < dim lk F.
Proof. (1) ⇔(2): This equivalence follows from Example 2.9 (4) and Theorem 4.13 (2).
(2) ⇔(3): This equivalence is the famous Reisner’s criterion ([BH, Corollary 5.3.9]).
□
Example 4.16. Let ∆be a triangulation of the real projective plane RP2. Then, by Reisner’s
