let λi(G) denote the i-th largest eigenvalue of A(G) (or G). The spectral radius of G
is the largest eigenvalue λ1(G). By Perron-Frobenius Theorem, λ1(G) has non-negative
eigenvectors (called Perron vectors). Moreover, λ1(G) is a simple root and has a positive
eigenvector, when G is connected. Let D(G) denote the degree diagonal matrix of G. The
Laplacian matrix of G is defined as L(G) = D(G) −A(G). The algebraic connectivity
µ2(G) of G is the second smallest eigenvalue of L(G). It is known that G is connected if
and only if µ2(G) > 0.
Let G be a connected graph. A connected factor of G is a connected spanning subgraph.
Clearly, a spanning tree of G is a 1-connected factor. Let τ(G) denote the maximum
number of edge-disjoint spanning trees contained in G. Motivated by Kirchhoff’s matrix
tree theorem (see [22]) and a question of Seymour (see [4]), Cioab˘a and Wong [4] studied
the following problem.
Problem 1. ([4]) Let G be a connected graph. Determine the relationship between τ(G)
and eigenvalues of G.
The crucial tool for Problem 1 is the famous Tree Packing Theorem due to Tutte and
Nash-Williams (see [34, 37]). Cioab˘a and Wong [4] initiated the study of the relationship
between τ(G) and λ2(G) for d-regular graphs G. They [4] proved τ(G) ≥k if λ2(G) <
d −2(2k−1)
d+1 , and conjectured that the condition can be released to λ2(G) < d −2k−1
d+1 . Note
that this conjecture is essentially tight (see [5]). This conjecture was extended in [13],
and partially confirmed in [23, 24] and completely confirmed in[25]. Recently, the spectral
radius (and other eigenvalue) conditions for edge-disjoint spanning trees of graphs were
extensively studied (see [8, 9, 11, 18, 19, 21, 32, 39]).
For the study of edge-disjoint 2-connected factors of graphs, we first introduce the con-
cept of rigidity of graphs. Arising from mechanics, rigidity is the property of a structure
that does not flex, and has been extensively studied in discrete geometry and combina-
torics, and has broad applications in material science, engineering and biological sciences
(see [7, 14, 15, 29, 38]). A d-dimensional bar-and-joint framework (G, p) is the combina-
tion of a graph G = (V (G), E(G)) and a map p : V (G) →Rd that assigns a point in Rd
to each vertex of G. Two frameworks (G, p) and (G, q) are equivalent (or congruent) if
∥p(u) −p(v)∥= ∥q(u) −q(v)∥for any uv ∈E(G) (or for all pairs u, v ∈V (G)), where
∥· ∥denote the Euclidean norm in Rd. A framework (G, p) is generic if the coordinates
of its points are algebraically independent over Q. The framework (G, p) is rigid in Rd
if there exists an ϵ > 0 such that every framework (G, q) equivalent to (G, p) satisfying
∥p(u) −q(u)∥< ϵ for any u ∈V (G) is congruent to (G, p). Asimov and Roth [1] proved
that there is a generic framework (G, p) which is rigid in Rd if and only if every generic
framework of G is rigid in Rd. Thus, the generic rigidity is inherent properties of a graph.
A graph G called rigid in Rd, if there is a generic framework of G which is rigid in Rd.

To prove Theorem 1.3, we first introduce a result from matroid theory. For basic definitions
on matroid, one may refer to the book [35].
Let G be a graph with edge set E = E(G). For any F ⊆E, let G(F) denote the
spanning subgraph of G with edge set F. Let
I = {F ⊆E | G(F) is a forest} .
The circuit matroid M(G) of G is the matroid on the ground set E(G) with independent
set I. The rank function of M(G) is given by
rM(F) = |V (G)| −c(F),
where F ⊆E and c(F) denotes the number of components of G(F). Clearly, G contains
a spanning tree if and only if rM(E) = |V (G)| −1.
Let F be a non-empty subset of E, and let S be the set of vertices incident with some
edge in F. Set H = G(F). The edge set F is called independent in G, if e(H[X]) ≤2|X|−3
for all X ⊆V (G) with |X| ≥2. Let
I = {F ⊆E | F is independent in G} .

with independent set I, which is called the rigid matroid of G. The rank function of R(G)
is given by
