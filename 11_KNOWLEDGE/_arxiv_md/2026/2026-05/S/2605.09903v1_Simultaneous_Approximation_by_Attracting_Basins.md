SIMULTANEOUS APPROXIMATION BY ATTRACTING BASINS
COLTON FISHER, AIDEN HILL, KIRILL LAZEBNIK, PALMER THOMPSON
Abstract. We show that any d ≥3 pairwise-disjoint open sets A1, ..., Ad ⊂bC sharing a
common boundary J can be simultaneously approximated by the d attracting basins A1,
..., Ad of a rational map r having Fatou set F(r) = A1 ⊔... ⊔Ad and so that the Julia set
J (r) approximates J.
1. Introduction
The field of Complex Dynamics centers around the study of dynamical systems of the form
(1.1)
r : bC →bC
where r = r(z) is a rational map in one complex variable (the quotient of two polynomials
r(z) = p(z)/q(z) having no roots in common), and bC := C ∪{∞} is the Riemann sphere,
obtained by adjoining ∞to the complex plane C. The two early 20th century pioneers of the
field, Pierre Fatou and Gaston Julia, both arrived at the following dynamical decomposition
of bC associated to (1.1): the Fatou set, denoted F(r), consists of points in a neighborhood
of which the iterates rn := r ◦...◦r form a normal family in the sense of Montel (see [Zak21],
Chapter 5), and the complement of F(r) is called the Julia set, denoted J (r). This definition
lies at the center of classical and modern work in the field.
A point ζ ∈bC for which r(ζ) = ζ and |r′(ζ)| < 1 is called an attracting fixed point of r.
If ζ is an attracting fixed point of rn for n ≥1, we call ζ, r(ζ), ...., rn(ζ) = ζ an attracting
cycle, and ζ an attracting periodic point. The basin of attraction A(ζ) for an attracting
periodic point ζ consists of points which converge, under iteration, to the attracting cycle.
Systems r for which F(r) consists only of basins of attraction are called hyperbolic; a central
conjecture in the field of complex dynamics is that hyperbolic rational maps are dense among
all rational maps.
If r is hyperbolic, so that F(r) = A(ζ1) ⊔... ⊔A(ζd) for attracting periodic points ζ1,
..., ζd, then each A(ζj) must share a common boundary = J (r) (see [CG93], Chapter 3).
The question studied in this paper is a sort of approximate converse: can any d pairwise-
disjoint open sets sharing a common boundary be approximated by the attracting basins of
a hyperbolic map? More precisely, we study the following question posed to us by Malik

Question 1. Suppose A1, ..., Ad ⊂bC are open sets all sharing a common boundary J. Does
there exist an r with attracting periodic points ζ1, ..., ζd and F(r) = A(ζ1) ⊔... ⊔A(ζd) so
that one has the simultaneous approximation J ≈J (r) and Aj ≈A(ζj) for each j?
In this paper, the notion of approximation ≈we will use is the Hausdorff metric; we recall
the definition.
Notation 1.1. All distances between two points in bC will be measured with respect to the
spherical metric, denoted by d(·, ·). We denote the Hausdorff distance between two sets A,
B ⊂bC by d(A, B), and recall that d(A, B) < ε if and only if A ⊂Nε(B) := {z ∈bC :
d(z, B) < ε} and B ⊂Nε(A).
Our main result is the following answer to Question 1.
Theorem A. Let ε > 0, d ≥2 and A1, ..., Ad be disjoint open sets in bC sharing a common
boundary J. Then there exists a rational map r with attracting fixed points ζ1, ..., ζd so that
F(r) = A(ζ1) ⊔... ⊔A(ζd) and dH(J, J ), dH(A1, A1), ...., dH(Ad, Ad) are all < ε.
Theorem A was influenced by some closely related work in the literature which we now
briefly survey. Under the assumption that d = 2 and J is a Jordan curve, Theorem A is
a result of [Lin15]. When d = 2 and J is a pairwise-disjoint, finite collection of mutually-
exterior Jordan curves, Theorem A is a result of [LY19]. In both [Lin15], [LY19], r may be
taken to be a polynomial (provided J does not contain ∞). When d = 2 and J is a pairwise-
disjoint, finite collection of Jordan curves (not necessarily mutually exterior), Theorem A
is a result of [BEL25], in which case r must be non-polynomial in general (by a result of
[LY19]).
Other recent relevant work includes a study of the rate of approximation in Theorem A
(for d = 2 and J a pairwise-disjoint, finite collection of mutually-exterior Jordan curves) in
[BCKS18]. Approximation by dendrite Julia sets was studied in [BP15]. Related work in
the transcendental setting includes [BT21] and [MPRW25].
Our main contribution is in being able to simultaneously approximate d > 2 open sets as in
Theorem A; this requires some different ideas from the d = 2 case. To highlight the difference
between d = 2 and d > 2, let us remark that while it is not difficult for d = 2 disjoint open
sets to share a common boundary (for instance the two complementary components of any
Jordan curve), the common boundary of d > 2 open sets must be comparatively intricate;
examples include the Lakes of Wada and attracting basins of many Newton maps.

Notation 2.11. We will continue to denote the attracting fixed points of Sn in the conclusion
of Lemma 2.10 by ζ1, ..., ζd.
Remark 2.12. The proof of Proposition 2.13 below relies on the following standard but
deep fact: if all critical values of a rational map r lie in a basin of attraction of an attracting
fixed point, then the Fatou set F(r) consists only of these basins of attraction. Indeed, the
components of F(r) must be pre-periodic by Sullivan’s no wandering domain Theorem, and
pre-periodic components admit a classification (see Chapter 15 of [Mil06]); moreover every
pre-periodic component in this classification must have an associated critical value orbit
(for detailed statements, see Theorems 8.6 and 11.17, Corollary 10.11 and Lemma 15.7 of
[Mil06]). Thus, if all critical values of r lie in a basin of attraction, there can be no other
Fatou components apart from these basins of attraction.
Proposition 2.13. For all sufficiently large n, the Fatou set of the map Sn consists only of
the attracting basins for ζ1, ..., ζd.

for λξ1, ..., λξd. By (2.5), it follows that each critical value of R is contained in one of these
attracting basins for λξ1, ..., λξd. Thus, by taking n large enough, we have that P n
