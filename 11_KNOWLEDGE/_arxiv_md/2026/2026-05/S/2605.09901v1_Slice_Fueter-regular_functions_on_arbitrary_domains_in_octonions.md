SLICE FUETER-REGULAR FUNCTIONS ON ARBITRARY
DOMAINS IN OCTONIONS
XINYUAN DOU, GUANGBIN REN, ZEPING ZHU, AND TING YANG*
Abstract. This paper is concerned with a class of generalized slice Fueter-
regular functions on arbitrary domains in O with local stem functions. Some
classical theorems such as the maximum modulus principle will be generalized
to our setting. Some new phenomena such as the conditional uniqueness of
stem vectors will be discovered by means of new technical tools, e.g., the CCL
equivalence relation and the Bers-Vekua continuation. And a natural connec-
tion between the theory of slice Fueter-regular functions and that of Riemann
domains will be revealed via the quotient space under the CCL equivalence
relation.
1. Introduction
The study of slice Dirac-regular functions, also known as slice Fueter-regular
functions, was originally initiated by M. Jin, G. Ren and I. Sabadini [11], and latter
enriched by R. Ghiloni with a certain type of Bers-Vekua system which characterizes
axially monogenic functions of degree zero [5]. In light of the earlier contributions,
we intend to broaden the current framework and seek for new observations.
More explicitly, this paper focuses on a particular class of generalized slice Fueter-
regular functions, whose stem functions exist locally (but not necessarily globally),
defined on arbitrary domains in O.
Firstly, we notice that the real analyticity
still holds for such functions. Combining the real analyticity and the fact that the
restriction of any such function on every quaternion slice of its definition domain
is harmonic, we are able to give a generalized and enhanced version of maximum
modulus principle, along with its corollary, as follows.
i. (strong maximum modulus principle) Let f be a nonconstant slice Fueter-
regular function on a domain Ω⊂O.
Then the octonion norm of the
restriction of f on any non-empty quaternion slice of Ωhas no local maxi-
mum.
ii. (weak maximum modulus principle) Let f be a nonconstant slice Fueter-
regular function on a domain Ω⊂O. Then the octonion norm of f has no
local maximum.

negative answer to the question about the original version of maximum modulus
principle for slice Fueter-regular functions posed by R. Ghiloni in Sect.

An imaginary unit is an octonion with unit norm and zero real part. The sphere
consisting of all imaginary units is often denoted by S. The symbol CI represents
the subalgebra R + RI (isomorphic to the complex field) generated by any given
imaginary unit I ∈S. It is worth mentioning that every octonion with non-zero

Corollary 5.9. The CCL Riemann domain associated with every domain in O has
at most two connected components.
Proof. The existence of complex structure (see Theorem 5.6) implies that every
CCL Riemann domain is locally path-connected.
We thus know the connected
components coincide with the path-connected components in every CCL Riemann
domain. So it suffices to prove that at least two of any three elements in every CCL
Riemann domain are path-connected.
Assume Ωis a domain in O, and [(zk, Ik)]Ω(k = 1, 2, 3) are three arbitrary points
in DΩ, i.e., the CCL Riemann domain associated with Ω. Then by definition we see
all the three octonions (zk)Ik belong to Ω. It thus follows from Theorem 4.4 that
there exist three circular liftings in Ωconnecting each two of (zk)Ik (k = 1, 2, 3)
respectively. For convenience, we denote the base and the spherical coordinate of
the circular lifting connecting (zi)Ii and (zj)Ij (1 ≤i < j ≤3) by γi,j and Θi,j. We
would like to mention that one can always replace γi,j and Θi,j by γi,j and −Θi,j
without changing the corresponding circular lifting. Hence there are essentially two
possible cases as follows:
Case 1: At least one of γi,j (1 ≤i < j ≤3) satisfies γi,j(0) = zi and γi,j(1) = zj.
Then Theorem 5.8 indicates that for such i, j there exists a path in DΩconnecting
[(zi, Ii)]Ωand [(zj, Ij)]Ω.
Case 2: Every γi,j (1 ≤i < j ≤3) satisfies γi,j(0) = zi and γi,j(1) = zj. Then
invoking Theorem 5.8 again, we see there exist a path in DΩconnecting [(z1, I1)]Ω
and [(z3, −I3)]Ω, and another path in DΩconnecting [(z2, I2)]Ωand [(z3, −I3)]Ω,
implying that [(z1, I1)]Ωand [(z2, I2)]Ωare path-connected.
In conclusion, no matter in which case, at least two of [(zk, Ik)]Ω(k = 1, 2, 3) are
path-connected in DΩ. It completes the proof.
