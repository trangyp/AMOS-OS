Subnormal block Toeplitz operators
Mankunikuzhiyil Abhinand, Ra´ul E. Curto, In Sung Hwang, Woo Young Lee,
and Thankarajan Prasad
Abstract. In this paper we consider the subnormality of block Toeplitz operators TΦ,
where Φ is an n × n matrix-valued function on the unit circle T of the form
Φ = QΦ∗
(Q is a finite Blaschke–Potapov product).
This is related to a matrix-valued version of Halmos’s Problem 5 and Nakazi-Takahashi
Theorem.
We ask whether TΦ is either normal or analytic if TΦ is subnormal, where Φ
is of the above form.
We give answers to this problem for different cases of the symbol.
Moreover, we provide a sufficient condition for the answer to be affirmative when Φ∗is
not of bounded type.

Introduction
Throughout the paper, let H be a complex separable Hilbert space and let B(H) be the
algebra of all bounded linear operators on H. We denote by Ran T, ker T and rank T the
range, the kernel, and the rank of T ∈B(H), respectively. For operators A, B ∈B(H), let
[A, B] := AB −BA.
An operator T ∈B(H) is called normal if [T ∗, T] = 0, hyponormal
if [T ∗, T] ≥0, and subnormal if T has a normal extension N, i.e., T is a restriction of N
to H, where N is a normal operator on some Hilbert space K ⊇H and H is invariant for
N. The notion of subnormal operator was introduced by P.R. Halmos [17] and has been
much-studied in the ensuing decades.
Also, the class of hyponormal operators includes
the classes of normal and subnormal operators [16].
Let T denote the unit circle in the complex plane C. A function θ ∈H∞(T) is called
an inner function if it satisfies |θ| = 1 a.e. on T. For a function φ ∈L∞(T), the Toeplitz
operator Tφ with symbol φ on the Hardy space H2(T) is defined by
Tφf := P(φf),
where f ∈H2(T) and P is the orthogonal projection of L2(T) onto H2(T).
A Toeplitz
operator Tφ is called analytic if φ ∈H∞(T).
A function φ ∈L∞(T) is said to be of
bounded type if φ = ϕ1

By contrast, it seems to be very hard to determine the subnormality of Toeplitz operators

Is every subnormal Toeplitz operator either normal or analytic ?

characterization of subnormal Toeplitz operators by the properties of their symbols is still
unknown. This naturally raised the following problem:
Which Toeplitz operators are subnormal ?
To date, there are two interesting partial answers to the Halmos’ Problem as in the below.
