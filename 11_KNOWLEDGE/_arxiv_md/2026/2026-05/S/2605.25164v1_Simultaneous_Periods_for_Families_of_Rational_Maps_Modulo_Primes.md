Abstract. Let K be a number field, and φ1, . . . , φg ∈K(t) be finitely many rational maps,
each of degree at least 2. We first show that for generic finite sets A1, . . . , Ag consisting entirely
of points that are not φi-periodic, there exists a set of primes p of K of positive density such
that for each Ai and every α ∈Ai, α is not φi-periodic modulo p. The notion of genericity used
here is defined in terms of the associated arboreal fields and is sharper than those previously
used in the literature. Leveraging our proof in the generic case, we then show that the same
conclusion holds for most expected cases of non-generic sets Ai. Finally, we apply our result to
confirm the dynamical Mordell–Lang conjecture for coordinate-wise actions of a class of maps
that includes rational maps that are generic in this sense.
1. Introduction

The forward orbit of a point α ∈P1(K) under φ is the set O+(α) := {φm(α) : m ≥0}. Similarly,
the backward orbit of α is the set O−(α) := {β ∈P1(K) : φm(β) = α for some m ≥0}. We
will say that α is φ-periodic if φm(α) = α for some m ≥1. More generally, we will say that α is
φ-preperiodic if its forward orbit is finite and α is exceptional if its backward orbit is finite.
1.1. Motivation. Given two points α, β ∈P1(K) such that β is not φ-preperiodic and α is not in
the forward orbit of β under φ, one might ask how many primes p of K there are such that α is
in the forward orbit of β under φ modulo p. The fact that there are infinitely many such p unless
α is exceptional for φ follows from [2, Lemma 4.1]. Then, an application of [25, Theorem 2.2])
also shows that there are infinitely many p such that α is not in the forward orbit of β modulo
p. It is known that the set S of primes p such that α is in the forward orbit of β modulo p has
density zero in some cases (see [19] and [14] amongst others). However, there are cases when S has
positive density. More generally, one may expect such examples for exceptional maps (see [11]).
In this context, the set of primes such that α is not in the forward orbit of β always has positive
density - the most general case of this result as established in [4] which we state in Theorem 1.4.

9. Future Directions
There are non-periodic elements α1, α2 for which the methods in this article are currently not
sufficient to establish non-periodicity of both α1, α2 modulo a positive density of primes.
For
example, if we take φ(x) = x2 and α1 = 2, α2 = 8 over K = Q. Then, for every m ≥1, we have
Qm(φ, 8) = Q(µ2m, 81/2m) ⊆Q(µ2m, 21/2m) = Qm(φ, 2).
In the language of derangements, in such cases, one would need to establish an analogue of Propo-

multipliers contained within a given number field (see [13]).
The assumption of almost linear disjointness used in this article should be viewed as an idealized
