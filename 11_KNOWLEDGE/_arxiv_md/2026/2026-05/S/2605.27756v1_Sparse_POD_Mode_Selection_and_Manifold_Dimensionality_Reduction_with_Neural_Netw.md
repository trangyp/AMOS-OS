Abstract.
High-performance computing enables simulation of high-dimensional physical sys-
tems, but downstream analyses such as inverse problems and control remain computationally ex-
pensive, motivating model order reduction (MOR) to construct efficient low-dimensional surrogates.
Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD), a widely adopted data-driven MOR method, projects
dynamics onto linear subspaces spanned by the most energetic modes.
However, POD struggles
for problems with slowly decaying Kolmogorov n-widths, such as advection-dominated and turbu-
lent flows, requiring many modes for accurate reconstruction. Moreover, energy-based selection can
discard crucial low-energy modes needed to capture small-scale features. Recent nonlinear mani-
fold methods using polynomial mappings with alternating or greedy mode selection achieve better
reconstruction with fewer modes. However, these methods fix the nonlinear mapping form a pri-
ori, limiting expressivity. Conversely, neural network (NN) manifolds offer greater expressivity but
employ energy-based selection. We present SparseModesNet, a dimensionality reduction framework
that employs linear encoding via POD modes and nonlinear NN decoding. The decoder leverages
LassoNet, a method enforcing hierarchical sparsity through residual connections with linear skip
layers, to simultaneously select informative POD modes and learn a nonlinear mapping that mini-
mizes reconstruction error. On benchmark advection-dominated and chaotic flows, SparseModesNet
matches or exceeds state-of-the-art performance. For turbulent channel flow at friction Reynolds
number Reτ = 5200, we reduce reconstruction error by 51–78% compared to existing polynomial
manifold methods while maintaining interpretability through physically meaningful mode selection.
Key words. Model order reduction, proper orthogonal decomposition, neural network, autoen-
coder, sparsity-inducing regression

1. Introduction. Many computational tasks in science and engineering require
solving high-dimensional partial differential equations (PDEs), which remains com-
putationally expensive despite advances in high-performance computing. Model or-
der reduction (MOR) methods address this by creating low-dimensional surrogate
models that accurately reconstruct high-dimensional dynamics at significantly re-
duced cost, enabling downstream analyses such as inverse problems, control, and
optimization [7, 51, 40, 2]. Among these, Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD)
has emerged as a widely used approach that identifies high-energy modes in data
and constructs efficient reduced-order models (ROMs) by projecting dynamics onto a
low-dimensional subspace spanned by these modes [33, 56, 8].
However, POD’s limitation to linear subspaces causes it to underperform for
transport-dominated, advection-dominated, or turbulent systems. In such cases, slow
spectral decay, known as the Kolmogorov n-width barrier, requires retaining prohibi-
tively many modes for accuracy, undermining the primary goal of MOR [18]. Recent
work has proposed addressing this barrier by moving beyond linear subspaces to non-
linear manifolds that allow nonlinear combinations of POD modes [1, 48].

Some researchers have explored nonlinear manifold representations that augment
linear POD subspaces with quadratic [50, 69, 35, 57] or higher-order polynomial
mappings [26]. These approaches have successfully constructed ROMs for advection-
dominated problems via manifold projection [5] and nonintrusive inference (i.e., with-
out access to underlying code) [28], demonstrating potential to overcome the Kol-
mogorov barrier with fewer modes. However, many rely on heuristic energy-based
mode selection, where modes are ordered by energy retention and then truncated at
fixed thresholds. This selection can be suboptimal since low-energy modes may be
critical to capture dynamics such as energy dissipation in turbulent flows [4, 12].
Recent works have proposed other mode selection strategies.
In [26, 27], the
authors define a polynomial mapping that decodes a reduced POD representation
and optimize both the linear basis and polynomial coefficients through an alternating
minimization scheme, while [60] proposes a greedy algorithm that iteratively selects
modes based on their contribution to reducing state reconstruction error, later ex-
tended to sparse data in [58]. While advancing beyond energy-based truncation, these
methods face two limitations. First, to capture increasingly complex dynamics with
higher accuracy requires higher-degree polynomials; including all cross-terms among
the reduced variables then incurs combinatorial growth in the number of coefficients,
creating a tradeoff between expressivity and computational tractability. Second, mode
selection is performed separately from the mapping optimization rather than jointly,
which may yield suboptimal mode-mapping combinations.
Importantly, while the
polynomial coefficients are optimized, the functional form of the mapping (e.g., qua-
dratic) is prescribed a priori rather than learned from data, which limits expressivity.
Neural networks (NNs) offer an alternative approach to overcoming the Kol-
mogorov n-width barrier by learning nonlinear manifold representations directly from
data. A prevalent architecture is the autoencoder (AE), which consists of an encoder
that compresses high-dimensional states into a low-dimensional latent representation
and a decoder that reconstructs the original state from this representation. Unlike
polynomial mappings with predetermined functional forms, AEs learn both the com-
pression and reconstruction mappings from data, enabling greater flexibility in cap-
turing complex nonlinear structure. The work [42] first applied deep convolutional
AEs to construct trial manifolds for MOR, demonstrating improved reconstruction
over linear methods for advection-dominated problems.
Subsequent developments
have extended this framework in several directions: [55] incorporates hyper-reduction
techniques to lower online computational costs, [66] generalizes convolutional archi-
tectures to unstructured meshes common in finite element discretizations, and [14]
introduces adaptive decoder filters that evolve with the latent state. Despite these
advances, AE-based methods encode information into latent variables that lack direct
physical meaning, defying interpretability of the learned representations.
In the context of MOR, interpretability can be considered the ability to under-
stand which physical features or modes the reduced model captures and how they con-
tribute to the reconstruction [15]. Several works have pursued this goal by imposing
structure on the learned latent space according to physical considerations: [36] employs
self-supervised learning to produce embeddings distinguishable according to physical
parameters, [63] uses β-variational AEs to learn disentangled, near-orthogonal latent
variables, and [70] encodes frequency information to yield latents corresponding to
distinct temporal scales. While these methods endow the latent space with meaning-
ful structure, the resulting representations remain dense—all latent coordinates con-
tribute to every reconstruction, making it difficult to isolate which modes are essential
for a given state. A complementary principle from interpretable machine learning is

This work bridges interpretable nonlinear manifolds with sparse mode selection

minimal sets of physically interpretable POD modes while leveraging NN expressivity.
The contributions of this work are:
• We propose SparseModesNet, a novel framework that combines the inter-
pretability of POD modes with NN expressivity, enabling sparse mode selec-
tion during NN training and accurate reconstruction of dynamical systems
with slowly decaying Kolmogorov n-widths.
• We demonstrate SparseModesNet’s effectiveness on benchmark problems and
turbulent channel flow, showcasing its ability to select minimal mode sets
while maintaining high state and mode reconstruction accuracy.
• We analyze the interpretability of our NN decoder by computing modes of the
reconstructed data and provide physical insights into selected modes based
on their order and characteristics.
Several recent works have also combined NN expressivity with physically meaning-
ful POD bases for interpretable nonlinear decoders. For example, [20, 6] employ small
linear POD bases augmented with NN correction terms, preserving interpretability
while harnessing nonlinear expressivity. The authors of [64] extend this approach by
applying NN corrections to both encoder and decoder, each weighted with trainable
coefficients for improved fitting. Alternatively, some works augment linear reduction
with nonlinear manifolds derived from probabilistic embeddings [29], user-defined re-
producing kernel Hilbert spaces [21], or Gaussian processes [3]. Although these hybrid
approaches maintain physical interpretability and sparsity by selecting few modes,
they rely on energy-based heuristics rather than simultaneously identifying essential
modes from a candidate set and learning the nonlinear manifold.
Another related work [19] recently introduced Decomposed Sparse Modal Opti-
mization (DESMO), which leverages the Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics
(SINDy) framework [11, 13, 25] to discover interpretable governing equations by pro-
moting sparsity in candidate function libraries. DESMO applies SINDy to POD or
AE-learned bases by constructing a nonlinear candidate library from modes using
predefined basis functions and employing ℓ1-regularization to select the fewest ac-
tive terms. While DESMO promotes sparsity in functional representations of a given
modal basis, our approach identifies which modes themselves should be retained and
simultaneously learns an expressive NN manifold tailored to those selected modes.

on encoder-decoder concepts for dynamical systems. Section 3 presents the Sparse-
ModesNet framework, detailing LassoNet integration for mode selection and the NN
architecture for nonlinear mapping. Section 4 demonstrates SparseModesNet’s per-
formance on benchmark problems and turbulent channel flow.
Finally, Section 5
summarizes our findings and discusses future directions.

3.2.1. Design Choices and Their Theoretical Justification. SparseMod-
esNet differs from the original LassoNet and simple ℓ1-regularization in two key re-
spects, each essential for guaranteeing monotonic mode elimination (i.e., eliminated
modes are never reactivated) when training via the continuation strategy.
Remark 3.1 (Input masking).
Unlike the original LassoNet (2.6), which inputs
the full feature vector ˆz to the NN, we employ ω ⊙ˆz as the NN input such that
the unselected modes are effectively zero.
Here, ω remains a trainable parameter
optimized via (3.2), while ω ⊙ˆz serves as the parametrized input to hNN(·).
Remark 3.2 (Hierarchical constraint). An alternative approach may be to remove
the hierarchical constraint and keep only the ℓ1-penalty, given the latent variable to
both the linear and nonlinear terms of the decoder are already masked with ω.
The input masking yields gradient severance. The gradient of the loss with respect
to the first-layer weights satisfies ∂L/∂W(1)
j
= 0 whenever ωj = 0. The hierarchical
constraint ∥W(1)
j
∥∞≤M|ωj| complements this by enforcing W(1)

= 0 whenever ωj =
0. Together, these properties completely sever mode j’s computational path through
the NN. No gradient signal can flow to update the zeroed-out weights, preventing the
reactivation of an eliminated mode.
Hence, combining gradient severance with the hierarchical constraint guarantees
monotonic mode elimination. That is, under mild regularity conditions on the so-
lution path, modes eliminated at regularization level λk remain eliminated for all
subsequent λℓ> λk. This monotonicity ensures that SparseModesNet produces a
stable, nested sequence of active modes, enabling reliable mode selection. Without
both design choices, the nonzero weights may oscillate and spuriously reactivate pre-
viously eliminated modes as λ increases, forcing the network to repeatedly relearn
mode suppression.
In Section SM2, empirical validations of our design choices are shown through an
ablation study comparing SparseModesNet against variants without input masking
(Remark 3.1) and without the hierarchical constraint (Remark 3.2), demonstrating
how our formulation achieves lower reconstruction error and more reliable mode se-
lection. However, the monotonic mode elimination alone does not explain the better
selection of modes. Specifically, we lack the mathematical machinery to explain how
our method is less affected by the spectral bias [52] as we see in our empirical vali-
dations; while possible future directions would be to explore neural tangent kernels
(NTKs) [34]. Hence, we postpone the formal statement and proof of monotonic mode
elimination, including the precise regularity assumptions to Section SM1.

Data Normalization. We center the data by its temporal mean and apply min-

entries in ω) reaches the target r. However, if the active mode count remains un-
changed for a number of iterations (e.g., 50), we terminate training and select the
r highest weighted modes in ω, ensuring convergence when the model has stabilized
and allowing a fully automated mode selection even when r is unknown a priori.

Neural Network Architecture. For hNN, we employ a hybrid architecture consist-
ing of a single fully connected layer followed by a Deep Polynomial NN (Π-Net) [16].
The fully connected layer transforms input modes into a representation suitable for
the Π-Net to capture polynomial nonlinearities and serves as the gate layer where the
hierarchical sparsity constraint is applied during training. The Π-Net then models
high-order polynomial functions, enabling rich representation of complex relation-
ships. While this layer can be extended to multiple fully connected layers for more
intricate problems, a single layer proved sufficient for the datasets considered.
We experimented with other architectures, including standard multi-layer per-
ceptrons, convolutional neural networks, and U-Nets, but found the hybrid fully
connected-Π-Net architecture provided superior reconstruction accuracy and mode
selection for our problems. Further details on the hybrid Π-Net architecture are pro-
vided in Section SM3.

5. Conclusion. In this work, we introduced SparseModesNet, a novel NN archi-

SM3. Hybrid Neural Network Architecture. In this section, we provide
additional details on the NN architecture employed in our proposed SparseMod-
esNet framework. Specifically, we describe the Deep Polynomial NNs known as Π-
Nets [SM2], and its combination with a linear layer to form the hybrid architecture
used in our experiments.
SM3.1. Deep Polynomial Neural Networks. Deep Polynomial NNs, or Π-
Nets, introduced in [SM2], are a class of function approximators where the output is
a high-order polynomial of the input. This approach contrasts with standard Deep
Convolutional NNs (DCNNs), which typically rely on a composition of linear operators
and nonlinear activation functions.
While any smooth function can be approximated by a polynomial, a direct imple-
mentation faces a combinatorial explosion in the number of parameters, which grows
polynomially with the order of the approximation, i.e., O(oN) for an o-dimensional
input and an N th-order polynomial.
To overcome this challenge, Π-Nets cast the problem of parameter estimation
as a coupled tensor factorization. That is, by decomposing the high-order tensors
that represent the polynomial’s parameters and enforcing factor sharing, the model
significantly reduces the number of learnable parameters and enables an efficient im-
plementation through hierarchical neural network architectures.
A vector-valued function g(y) : Ro →Rp (where o is an arbitrary input dimension)
is modeled as an N th-order multivariate polynomial of the input y. In its vectorized

SM3.2. Hybrid Architecture:

functions through polynomial expansions. However, in certain scenarios, especially
when dealing with reconstructing the data that exhibits both linear and nonlinear

both nonlinear transformations of a standard NN and polynomial approximations.

best in our experiments compared to deeper architectures with multiple fully con-

This suggests that complicated architectures are not necessary when the goal is to
reconstruct data from a limited number of modes, as the polynomial nature of the
Π-Net already provides sufficient expressivity.
