Abstract
Urban decarbonization requires scaling rooftop solar across mil-
lions of fragmented producers, yet cities face a fundamental tension:
energy data is easily manipulated, and economic incentives often re-
ward speculation rather than actual infrastructure deployment. We
present SolarChain, a platform that resolves both problems by an-
choring digital accountability to the thermodynamic limits of solar
energy conversion. Using real-time meteorological data, geospa-
tial coordinates, and first-principles calculations of solar yield, the
system establishes a hard physical boundary for every panel’s max-
imum possible output; any reported generation exceeding this limit
is automatically rejected before entering the shared ledger. This
trustless verification enables a peer-to-peer marketplace with pro-
grammatic reward structures that continuously reinvest value into
equipment maintenance and market liquidity, preventing the spec-
ulative hoarding that typically destabilizes blockchain-based mar-
ketplaces. When electricity is consumed, the corresponding digital
credits are permanently retired in direct proportion to physical
energy dissipation, creating an auditable one-to-one mapping be-
tween urban consumption and carbon accounting. Deployed across
heterogeneous city nodes, the prototype demonstrates resilience
against data injection attacks while lowering capital barriers for
community-level solar expansion. Beyond energy, the framework
offers a general model for coordinating economic activity with

Introduction
The evolution toward sustainable energy has transformed tradi-
tional city power grids from passive networks into active, dis-
tributed microgrids [23]. This shift is important for improving urban
resilience and optimizing urban energy consumption [41]. While
centralized systems have managed these resources, they suffer from
single points of failure, making urban energy networks vulnerable
to disruptions[2, 7]. Consequently, decentralized platforms using
blockchain and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies have emerged
as practical solutions for urban energy management [1, 20, 42].
The reliable operation of such decentralized networks depends
on high-resolution geospatial data [22]. However, bridging macro
urban observations with micro decentralized trading reveals a trust
gap [10, 43]. Existing blockchain energy systems cannot easily ver-
ify the physical authenticity of off-chain IoT data. This vulnerability
exposes the system to False Data Injection Attacks (FDIA), where
malicious actors report fabricated solar generation to extract illicit
token rewards.[31].
To address this challenge, we propose SolarChain, a decentral-
ized platform that integrates blockchain and IoT for urban energy
management. To establish digital trust, the platform tackles FDIA
through cross-domain knowledge fusion. Rather than relying solely
on digital signatures, SolarChain validates data authenticity through
physical laws. Our off-chain engine combines multiple urban data
by correlating GPS coordinates with real-time meteorological con-
ditions. By applying solar geometry [5], the system calculates an
upper limit (𝑃𝑚𝑎𝑥) for power output. Smart contracts automatically
reject any trading requests that exceed this limit, performing anom-
aly detection and securing the network even in areas with limited
sensor coverage.
Beyond anomaly detection, urban energy management also re-
quires economic sustainability. To enhance operational visibility,
SolarChain provides an interactive visual dashboard. It allows urban
planners to monitor spatio-temporal energy flows and supports
effective decision-making. Furthermore, SolarChain utilizes a re-
silient tokenomics architecture to maintain market stability. The
platform prevents token hoarding through a liquidity mechanism
[11]. Additionally, by linking digital assets to physical energy dissi-
pation, the system maps financial incentives to real-world urban
energy consumption.
To advance secure and sustainable urban microgrids, this study
mainly concentrates on:
• Data Fusion: We translate disparate metrics (spatial co-
ordinates, meteorology, IoT states) into a unified physical
boundary via solar geometry.
• Sustainable Incentive Design: We advance the Energy-
as-a-Service paradigm through a resilient tokenomics ar-
chitecture.
• Interactive Decision Support: We enables urban planners
to audit machine-verified microgrid states and cryptograph-
ically authorize transactions prior to on-chain execution.
In the spirit of open science, we release the SolarChain data and
code as open access on GitHub.1

Literature Review
2.1
Spatiotemporal Data Monitoring
In urban computing, resilient Distributed Energy Resources (DERs)
are essential for smart city infrastructure [3, 45]. Grid stability typ-
ically relies on State Estimation, where Bad Data Detection (BDD)
identifies anomalies by evaluating the 𝐿2-norm of measurement
residuals [23, 31].
However, FDIA can bypass these filters. By exploiting the grid’s
topology, attackers can construct a stealthy injection vector that
keeps the measurement residual perfectly invariant[26]. This math-
ematical invisibility renders traditional BDD ineffective, posing a
severe threat to urban IoT-based energy assets [26, 28].
To mitigate these risks, purely data-driven machine learning
models (e.g., spatiotemporal GNNs) have been proposed [33]. Nev-
ertheless, deploying complex AI models on-chain is constrained
by the computational power of smart contracts [8]. Furthermore,
relying on external AI systems could cause centralized trust issues.
This reliance ultimately undermines the resilience of decentralized
networks [39, 43].
To bridge this gap, frontier research utilizes Physics-Informed
Neural Networks (PINNs) to embed thermodynamic laws into data
processing [13, 34]. Following this paradigm, SolarChain establishes
a trustless authentication mechanism bounded by physical laws. By

Decentralized Infrastructure
2.2.1
DePIN Architecture. The Decentralized Physical Infrastruc-
ture Network (DePIN) represents a frontier exploration in integrat-
ing Web3 with urban environments [35]. Traditional centralized
energy systems often suffer from data silos due to commercial con-
fidentiality. The DePIN architecture implements cryptoeconomic
incentives to crowdsource the deployment of physical facilities,
thereby establishing a privacy-preserving Data Commons [14, 44].
Empirical research suggests that sharing spatiotemporal correlation
data via DePIN can reduce urban PV forecasting errors, enhancing
grid resilience in local markets [7, 23]. Consequently, this improved
accuracy leads to better economic efficiency and stability in power
markets. The evolutionary characteristics of such platforms are
summarized in Table 1.

2.2.3
RWA Tokenization and Carbon Auditing. In the context of
Real-World Asset (RWA) tokenization within energy networks, ac-

token burn mechanism to address this challenge is essential for

System Architecture
Existing research has mentioned that decentralized energy systems
often suffer from stealthy FDIA, token hoarding, and unverified
carbon auditing. To address these issues, SolarChain establishes
digital trust based on thermodynamic laws. It bridges physical
constraints with economic incentives to improve data verification
and carbon settlement. This design is implemented through a three-
layer architecture comprising an off-chain physics engine, on-chain
smart contracts, and a decentralized frontend (Figure 1).
3.1
Off-Chain Physics Engine

putational layer, bridging real-world meteorological data with the
on-chain state machine. It utilizes first-principles thermodynamic
modeling to establish an immutable verification boundary for de-
centralized energy assets.

fications, including the surface area (𝐴), conversion efficiency (𝜂),
and temperature coefficient (𝛽). It also gathers local environmental

perature (𝑇𝑚𝑖𝑛). Based on these parameters, the engine calculates
the theoretical maximum power output (𝑃𝑚𝑎𝑥) as predefined in
Equation (1). This boundary is computed and verified before any

3.2
On-chain Market Mechanisms
The on-chain layer of SolarChain establishes a decentralized trust
mechanism to bridge verified energy generation with urban eco-
nomic incentives. To ensure system security and extensibility for
diverse urban deployments, the architecture is modularized, as
summarized in Table 2.
To integrate distributed urban resources, the registry layer es-
tablishes cryptographic digital twins for both energy suppliers and
consumers. Once the off-chain engine verifies a panel’s genera-
tion against its thermodynamic boundary (Eq. 1), the decentralized
market engine executes a forced liquidity distribution mechanism
during the minting process. As formalized in Eq. (2), 75% of the
validated energy value is automatically injected into the energy ex-
change pool to maintain consistent market depth, while the remain-
ing 25% is distributed to asset owners to incentivize infrastructure
maintenance.
Furthermore, the system connects digital asset circulation with
physical laws through a thermodynamic deflationary mechanism.
When urban demand nodes purchase energy from the global supply,
the corresponding native utility tokens (SOLR) are permanently
burned rather than transferred. This programmatic burning cre-
ates an auditable mapping between physical energy dissipation
(thermodynamic entropy increase, Δ𝑆> 0) and digital token defla-
tion, preventing arbitrary inflation and supporting reliable carbon
auditing.
To ensure the system’s vitality, market-clearing mechanisms are
complemented by secondary market protocols and yield distribu-
tions. This approach facilitates P2P ownership transfers through an
offer-approval flow, which updates the asset registry. Furthermore,
a dedicated reward protocol calculates distributions based on the
registered DC power capacity of each panel, subject to a temporal
cooldown to ensure stable long-term participation. By enabling
asset liquidity and connecting financial returns to physical capacity,
the system links incentives with the hardware lifecycle to maintain
economic sustainability.

3.3
Decentralized Frontend
The frontend of SolarChain is designed as an interactive visual
layer for urban energy planning. It connects planners, DER owners,
and industrial consumers with the off-chain engine and the on-
chain contract through a map-centered workspace. Recognizing the
inherent spatial distribution of urban energy systems, the interface
adopts a map-centric design. This spatial layout allows users to
visualize DERs, inspect candidate nodes, and register assets.
To support human-in-the-loop verification, the frontend imple-
ments a planner decision console(Figure 2a) that organizes spa-
tiotemporal DER records into an auditable review. Once a candidate
passes review, a registration modal presents information such as
predicted output, physical residuals, and the planner’s decision
before transaction submission. All operations including asset regis-
tration and energy trading, are then securely executed via Web3
wallet signatures and smart contract calls. Furthermore, the plat-
form provides transparent market visualization, allowing users to
track aggregate supply and local demand to make informed energy
transactions.
Beyond asset registration, the frontend provides market visual
analytics for urban energy coordination. The interface allows users
to track macro data, such as aggregate verified supply and factory
demand, alongside individual participation status. Furthermore,
a dedicated market view compares verified SolarChain liquidity
against a no-split baseline. This visualization demonstrates how ex-
cluding FDIA preserves market depth and reduces trading slippage.
Ultimately, the frontend establishes a decision support system. It
integrates geospatial exploration, physical verification, and human
oversight into a unified workflow for urban microgrid management.
3.4
System Demonstration
The SolarChain platform operationalizes this architecture through
a sequential workflow, guiding users to review, register, and excite
market activities.
The workflow starts with the planner decision console (Fig-
ure 2a). The frontend automatically loads bundled spatiotemporal
DER records upon initialization. It displays their computed bound-
aries, reported generation values, and verification evidence. This
map-centric dashboard organizes candidate DERs and flags genera-
tion claims that deviate from the limits, allowing urban planners to
intuitively review the physical residuals of each asset.
Once a candidate is verified, the process transitions to the solar
panel confirmation interface. The user could check the selected
location and predicted panel outputs before approving the asset.
SolarChain then requests a Web3 wallet signature and submits the
on-chain registration.
For the demand side, the platform facilitates participation through
the energy exchange interface. Energy consumers could utilize this
portal to monitor their current power deficits and execute energy
purchases. To streamline procurement, SolarChain previews costs
and verifies token allowances before executing the token burn and
ledger settlement.
Finally, users can inspect the macro dynamics of these coordi-
nated actions through the market analytics dashboard (Figure 2b).
This panel provides a comparative visualization of system liquidity
against theoretical baselines. By tracking aggregate supply and
local demand, the dashboard provides transparent decision support,
illustrating the relationship between PV generation and industrial
consumption.

System Evaluation
We evaluate SolarChain across three interconnected layers: physical
sensing, data verification, and digital settlement. Specifically, we an-
alyze the spatio-temporal dynamics of distributed PV nodes, assess
the verifier’s robustness against fraudulent records, and quantify
how the forced liquidity distribution improves market depth and
reduces trading slippage.
4.1
Evaluation Benchmark
Our evaluation benchmark simulates a 24-hour cycle (May 1, 2026)
using a fixed random seed. As shown in Figure 3, the data pipeline
integrates urban locations, weather observations, and solar geome-
try to simulate microgrid.
The simulation covers five cities: Beijing, Shanghai, Chengdu,
Shenzhen, and Hangzhou. With 10 PV nodes per city, the dataset
contains 1,200 hourly power records, yielding a verified generation
baseline of 2,028.13 kWh. To test system resilience, we inject 60
FDIA and fault records (a 5% corruption rate). Furthermore, the
market layer simulates 24 hourly liquidity records and 42 P2P trades,
which allows us to evaluate the stability of the market mechanism
over a complete daily cycle.

Conclusion and Future Work
SolarChain is a decentralized energy platform that connects digital
economic settlements to thermodynamic laws. Our system utilizes
real-time meteorological data and solar geometry to establish an
objective verification boundary. Evaluations across a simulated
cities network demonstrate that it successfully intercepts FDIA
without relying on expensive, tamper-proof hardware deployments.
Furthermore, the system stabilizes P2P markets through a forced
liquidity distribution mechanism and provides a reliable foundation
for transparent urban carbon auditing.
To enable reproducible benchmarking and empower future re-
search, the SolarChain dataset and visualization suite are designed
for open reuse by the urban computing community. Researchers
studying spatio-temporal energy analytics can leverage our multi-
city node registry to benchmark clustering algorithms for dis-
tributed energy placement, continuing geospatial analysis tradi-
tions [45]. Scholars investigating anomaly detection in IoT net-
works can test alternative machine learning classifiers against our
physics-bounded ground truth. Furthermore, economists and sys-
tems researchers can simulate alternative tokenomic policies using
our market liquidity logs. Additionally, future work can extend
this framework beyond China. By incorporating global meteoro-
logical datasets, such as ERA5, NOAA, or regional equivalents,
researchers can validate and adapt the system across diverse ge-
ographical boundaries. Ultimately, by releasing these tools and
outlining these research directions, we aim to lower the barrier for
cross-disciplinary collaboration among energy engineers, urban
planners, and data scientists, providing a reusable foundation for
sustainable smart cities.

User Experience Workflow
SolarChain is designed as a human-in-the-loop energy planning
workflow. The user experience begins from a map-centered dash-
board and then moves through verification, registration, settlement,
and audit. Each stage exposes both the user action and the evidence
produced by the system, so that planners can trace how a PV record
becomes an on-chain market state.
Step 1: Wallet and city-state initialization. The planner, PV
owner, or factory owner first connects a Web3 wallet. After the
signature identity is available, the frontend loads the deployed con-
tract addresses, ABIs, city layers, PV nodes, factory nodes, and local
dashboard state. This step binds later approvals and transactions to
a cryptographic account.
Step 2: Spatial inspection of candidate assets. The planner
opens the map workspace and inspects distributed energy resources
across the demonstration cities. For each candidate PV node, the
interface shows location, panel specifications, battery tempera-
ture, DC power, AC power, and predicted generation. This spatial
view is important because SolarChain treats energy generation as a
location-dependent urban process rather than as a generic token
balance.
Step 3: Physics-bounded verification. Before a PV record is
allowed to affect the market, the planner reviews the off-chain
verification evidence. The physics engine combines geospatial coor-
dinates, irradiance, temperature, panel area, efficiency, and tempera-
ture coefficient to compute 𝑃max. If the reported generation satisfies
𝑃reported ≤𝜏𝑃max, the record is marked verified; otherwise it is
labeled rejected. This step blocks FDIA records before they can
create false market supply or invalid token rewards.
Step 4: PV digital-twin registration. After a panel passes veri-
fication, the PV owner submits the asset to the chain. The panel-
registration function records the owner address, location, battery
temperature, DC power, AC power, and creation timestamp. The
resulting panel event provides an audit trail from the planner-
approved physical record to the on-chain digital twin. The con-
firmation view in Figure 8 is the final human checkpoint before
the wallet signature: the user verifies the selected location and
predicted outputs instead of signing a transaction from an abstract
contract call alone.
Figure 8: Solar panel confirmation. User verifies the selected
location and predicted outputs before submitting a Web3
wallet signature.
Step 5: Hourly market-state update. At each simulation step,
verified generation is aggregated together with factory demand.
The market operator calls the market-update function, which ap-
plies SolarChain’s forced distribution rule: 75% of verified value is
added to global market liquidity, while 25% is assigned to personal
producer rewards. This prevents the marketplace from depending
only on voluntary liquidity provision.
Step 6: Reward preview and claiming. PV owners can preview
their pending reward balance before claiming. If the cooldown
interval has passed, the reward contract transfers SOLR to the
owner and updates the last-claim timestamp. This gives owners
a visible maintenance incentive while preventing repeated claims
within the same simulator interval.
Step 7: Factory registration and energy purchase. Factory users
register demand nodes with location and power-consumption meta-
data. When buying verified energy, the factory owner approves
SOLR spending and calls the purchase function. The exchange burns
the corresponding SOLR, decreases global supply, increases the fac-
tory energy balance, and emits an EnergyPurchased event. This
ties digital token deflation to energy consumption. Figure 9 shows
the purchase interface where the user selects a factory, previews
the energy amount, and prepares the SOLR burn transaction before
wallet approval.
Figure 9: Energy exchange interface. The user selects a fac-
tory, previews the energy amount, and prepares the SOLR
burn transaction.
Step 8: P2P panel marketplace. SolarChain also supports sec-
ondary ownership transfer. A PV owner can list a panel in Shop;
buyers place offers after approving token spending; the seller ap-
proves one buyer. The shop transfers SOLR from buyer to seller and
calls the panel ownership-transfer function, so the asset registry
and payment record remain synchronized.
Step 9: Planner audit and analytics. Finally, planners review
charts for verified generation, rejected anomalies, market liquidity,
baseline slippage, token burn, and exergy dissipation. Then dash-
board therefore connects meteorological input, physical verifica-
tion, contract events, and economic settlement into one inspectable
evidence chain.

B
Smart Contracts in SolarChain
The on-chain layer uses a modular design. SolarChain keeps the
physics model off-chain. An off-chain engine verifies energy gener-
ation records, while smart contracts manage state transitions that
require public auditing. These transitions include token issuance,
asset registration, market allocation, and asset transfers. This de-
sign separates expensive physical computations from transparent
market settlements.
The main paper summarizes the six core contracts in Table 2.
This appendix explains how they work together to connect physical
data with digital records. As shown in Code box 2, the panel and
factory contracts form the registry layer. They do not handle energy
trading directly. Instead, they assign stable identities to supply-side
solar panels and demand-side factories. This is important because
urban energy generation and consumption vary by location. The
registry ensures every market event traces back to a specific owner
and physical location.
SolarToken and EnergyExchange form the settlement layer. In
our system, the SOLR token is an accounting tool, not a speculative
asset. Code box 1 outlines its logic, which manages token issuance
and verifiable burns. The exchange contract receives verified energy
data from the off-chain engine. Code box 3 demonstrates how the
system divides this energy into two parts: market liquidity and
owner rewards. When a factory buys energy, it does not simply pay
another user. Instead, the contract burns the spent SOLR tokens.
This reduces the global token supply and increases the factory’s
energy balance. This mechanism directly connects digital token
deflation to physical energy consumption.
Finally, the reward and shop contracts encourage long-term
participation. The reward contract distributes tokens based on panel
capacity. It uses a cooldown timer to prevent multiple claims in the
same simulation step. The shop contract manages the secondary
market for solar panels. It verifies ownership, handles payments,
and updates the registry (Code box 2). Together, these contracts tie
economic incentives to physical infrastructure rather than short-
term token trading.

Dataset Data Dictionary
The released datasets form a linked physical-to-digital benchmark.
The node registry defines each PV asset’s location and hardware
parameters; the hourly generation file expands those nodes into
weather-bounded verification records with FDIA labels. The market-
liquidity file aggregates verified generation into liquidity and slip-
page variables. Additionally, the P2P trade file records factory pur-
chases, SOLR burn, and exergy dissipation. All timestamps use ISO
8601 format in the Asia/Shanghai timezone. To provide a compre-
hensive overview, Table 7 presents the complete data dictionary,
detailing the specific role of each dataset alongside its variable units,
numerical ranges or categorical domains, temporal frequencies, and
sample observations.
