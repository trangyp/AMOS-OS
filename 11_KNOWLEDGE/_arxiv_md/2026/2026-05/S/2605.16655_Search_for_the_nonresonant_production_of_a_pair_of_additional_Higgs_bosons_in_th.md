Abstract
A search is presented for the production of two additional Higgs bosons from an off-
shell Z boson, where both additional particles decay to τ lepton pairs. The search is
performed with a data sample collected with the CMS detector from proton-proton
collisions at the LHC at √s = 13 TeV, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
138 fb−1. No deviation from the standard model background is observed. Exclu-
sion limits are set on the Type-X two-Higgs-doublet model alignment scenario. These
results rule out this model as an explanation to the potential tension between the ex-
perimental and theoretical values of the muon anomalous magnetic moment.

The combined measurement of the muon magnetic moment [1–3] yields a value that differs
by five standard deviations from the standard model (SM) prediction presented by the Muon
g-2 Theory Initiative [4]. Although there are tensions between the different theoretical calcu-
lations [4, 5] that could bring the prediction closer to the experimental value, the discrepancy
could be a hint of beyond-the-SM (BSM) physics. BSM particles can contribute loop corrections
to the calculation of the muon magnetic moment. If present, such particles may be detectable
at the LHC.
The two-Higgs-doublet model (2HDM) is an example of a BSM theory that predicts additional
Higgs bosons, which contribute to the calculation of the muon magnetic moment [6–13]. The
2HDM parameter space required to match this contribution to the observed deviation is as
yet uncovered by experimental measurements. A Charge-Parity (CP)-conserving 2HDM pre-
dicts five Higgs bosons; two neutral scalars (h and H), one neutral pseudoscalar (A), and two
charged scalars (H±) [14]. There are four types of CP-conserving 2HDMs that are dependent on
which Higgs doublet couples to which group of fermions (up-type quarks, down-type quarks,
and charged leptons). These are named Type-I, II, X (“lepton-specific”), and Y (“flipped”). A
renormalizable CP-conserving 2HDM without tree-level flavor-changing neutral currents can
be parametrized by the Higgs boson masses (mh, mH, mA, and mH±), the ratio of the vac-
uum expectation values of the two Higgs doublets (tan β), the mixing angle that determines
the composition of h and H (α), and the mass-squared term in the Higgs potential (m2
12) [14].
The alignment limit matches the 2HDM to the observed Higgs boson (H125) by dictating that
cos(β −α) = 0 for h = H125 (normal scenario) or sin(β −α) = 0 for H = H125 (inverted
scenario) [15]. In this Letter, the neutral CP-even Higgs boson that is not identified as H125 is
denoted ϕ.
Unsuppressed (or enhanced) quark couplings to BSM Higgs bosons are essential for many
searches for extended Higgs sectors, such as that for the Type-II 2HDM of the minimal super-
symmetric SM (MSSM) [14, 16, 17]. In these models, the dominant single Higgs boson pro-
duction modes are gluon fusion and associated production with b quarks, that depend on the
couplings to quarks. However, in the case of Type-X 2HDM, where lepton couplings are en-
hanced and up- and down-type quark couplings are suppressed by large values of tan β, single
Higgs boson production rates are diminished. This results in a parameter space that remains
largely unexplored by existing LHC searches, which typically target “MSSM-like” models.
References [6–13] offer potential explanations to the muon anomalous magnetic moment with a
Type-X 2HDM. In particular, Ref. [9] provides an available parameter space with a mass range
that is accessible for an LHC search. In this space, it is required that tan β ≳90 in the normal
scenario and tan β ≳120 in the inverted scenario. To search in this region, a non-suppressed
production mode is needed. This can be achieved by the simultaneous production of the ϕ and
A bosons from an off-shell Z boson, as shown in Fig. 1. The cross section for this process is
independent of tan β and is predicted to vary between 10–600 fb across the stated mass ranges
at √s = 13 TeV. At high tan β in the Type-X 2HDM, the branching fractions of BSM Higgs
bosons to τ leptons are dominant.
This region of interest lies beyond the sensitivity of single Higgs boson searches such as Ref. [18],
which can only constrain the model at low values of tan β due to suppressed production modes.
LHC di-Higgs searches, sensitive to heavy Higgs bosons, can be broadly categorized into three
types: non-resonant Standard Model Higgs pair production (H125H125), heavy resonance de-
cays to a Higgs and another scalar (X →YH125), and heavy resonance decays to two Higgs
bosons (X →H125H125). Searches targeting final states without τ leptons are not compatible
with the model under consideration, due to the dominant branching fraction of the additional

This Letter reports the search for the Z∗→ϕA →ττττ process, using proton-proton collision
(pp) events at √s = 13 TeV collected with the CMS detector in 2016–2018, corresponding to
an integrated luminosity of 138 fb−1 [19–21]. The search is sensitive to a wide range of masses
of the ϕ and A bosons that incorporate the regions of interest in the models proposed as an
explanation for the muon anomalous magnetic moment measurement. The results obtained
are provided in the HEPData record for this analysis [22].
The CMS apparatus [23] is a multipurpose, nearly hermetic detector, designed to trigger on [24–
26] and identify electrons (e), muons (µ), photons, and (charged and neutral) hadrons [27–29].
A global “particle-flow” algorithm [30] aims to reconstruct all individual particles in an event,
combining information provided by the all-silicon inner tracker and by the crystal electromag-
netic (ECAL) and brass-scintillator hadron calorimeters, operating inside a 3.8 T superconduct-
ing solenoid, with data from the gas-ionization muon detectors embedded in the flux-return
yoke outside the solenoid.

and jets from hadronically decaying τ leptons (τh). The τh discriminators against jets, elec-

Jets originating from the hadronization of b quarks (b jets) are tagged by the DEEPJET algo-

discriminant with a 90% efficiency and a 1% jet misidentification rate [27]. Muons are identi-
fied using the quality of track fitting between the tracker and the muon system, as well as the
consistency of individual track segments with the fitted track, achieving a 99% efficiency with a
0.2% pion misidentification rate [28]. Both electrons and muons are further required to be sep-

Three sources of background are considered: events with one or more jet →τh candidates,
events in which all τh candidates are correctly reconstructed, and a third component encom-
passing all other events that do not fit into these categories. Events with one or more jet →τh
candidates are modeled from data using an improved “FF method” [37] with machine learn-
ing (ML), whilst the remaining contributions are obtained from simulation. In many analysis
categories, the number of predicted background events is small.
The FF method estimates the jet →τh background in the signal regions by selecting events
where τh candidates fail the nominal DEEPTAU identification (ID) requirement but pass a
looser ID selection. Transfer functions (TFs) are then used to define event weights that are
applied to correct the yields and kinematic distributions. The ML FF method derives these
TFs using boosted decision trees as in Ref. [38]. The FF TFs are calculated by comparing re-
gions where the τh candidates fail the ID requirements to regions that pass the tau ID. This is
done in a region of pure jet →τh objects, orthogonal to the signal region, by inverting one or
more of the total charge selection or the ID requirements of other τh candidates in the event.
The reweighting is performed with variables related to the τh candidate that are known to
parametrize the TFs. These include the hadronic decay mode, pT, the ratio pT (jet)/pT (τh)

The remaining backgrounds, dominated by ZZ events in which all τh candidates are recon-

production is simulated with MADGRAPH5 aMC@NLO 2.4.2 at next-to-LO (NLO) precision in
αS [41, 42]. The tt [43] and single t quark production samples [44] are generated at NLO pre-
cision using POWHEG version 2.0 [45–48]. Their cross sections are calculated at next-to-NLO
(NNLO) precision for W+ jets, Z/γ∗→ℓℓ, tt, and single t quark events in the tW channel [49–
51], and at NLO precision or higher for single t quark (t channel) and diboson events [51–53].
Additionally, NNLO/NLO K factors, as derived in Ref. [54], are applied to quark and gluon
fusion initiated ZZ production as a function of the ZZ pair mass at the generator level.
The production of two BSM Higgs bosons from an off-shell Z boson in the alignment limit and
its cross section are modelled in MADGRAPH5 aMC@NLO 2.6.5 at NLO precision in αS [41, 42].
The branching fractions of BSM Higgs bosons are calculated with 2HDECAY [55]. The align-
ment scenario is selected because any differences are minimal in signal generation or branching
fractions out of the alignment scenario but within the experimental constraints from measure-
ments of the H125 couplings. Negligible changes to the signal modelling are observed when
changing the free parameters mH± and m2
12 within their theoretically allowed limits.
The PYTHIA event generator [56] (version 8.230) is used to model parton showering and had-
ronization, as well as for τ lepton decays. The description of the underlying event is parameter-
ized according to the CP5 [57] tune. The NNPDF3.1 [58] parton distribution functions (PDFs)
are used for all simulations. For all simulated events, pileup collisions generated with PYTHIA
are added according to the expected pileup distribution in data. All events are passed through
a GEANT4-based [59] simulation of the CMS detector and reconstructed with the same CMS
event reconstruction software.
Several simulation-to-data corrections are applied to the simulation. The corrections include
adjustments for the light lepton’s trigger efficiency, tracking efficiency, energy scale (electrons
only), identification and isolation requirements, and also for the τh candidate’s trigger effi-
ciency, identification and energy scale, calculated with the “tag-and-probe” scheme [60]. Cor-
rections are also applied to account for discrepancies in pileup profiles, b tagging ID, ⃗p miss
T
, and
jet energies. Finally, simulation-to-data corrections are used to account for differences in the
misidentification rates of electrons and muons as tau candidates, as well as to match dilepton
mass and pT spectra in Drell–Yan simulated events with data. These corrections are performed
in the same way as described in Ref. [18].
A 2% uncertainty is applied to both the ID and trigger efficiencies of electrons and muons,
which is treated as correlated between channels and applied to simulated processes. The elec-
tron energy scale uncertainty is derived from the calibration of ECAL crystals. Uncertainties
ranging from 0–10%, in the ID, energy scale, and trigger efficiencies of hadronic taus in simu-
lated events are determined for separate decay modes and data-taking eras, and utilize a par-
tial correlation scheme. Uncertainties in jet energy calibration and resolution are applied with
different correlations, varying from subpercent to around 10%, depending on the kinematic

The ML FF method has a number of uncertainties. An uncertainty in the non-closure of the
TFs, derived from the reweighted variables, is determined separately in each decay channel.
This has size O(10%) on the total jet →τh background in each channel. An uncertainty is
determined to account for biases in the FF TFs, resulting from inverting the ID and charge
selections to obtain the pure jet →τh regions. This uncertainty is uncorrelated across channels
and is found to be around 5–15%, depending on the channel. Smaller uncertainties, that range
from 0–5% depending on the channel, are included to account for the influence of non jet →τh
objects in the measurement regions.

An exclusion contour at 95% CL in the mA-tan β plane for the Type-X 2HDM alignment scenario

In summary, a search has been presented for beyond-the-standard-model Higgs bosons pro-
duced via off-shell Z bosons that decay into τ leptons in pp collisions at the LHC, using a
data sample collected with the CMS detector at √s = 13 TeV. A good agreement between the
background-only fit and the data is observed. Upper limits on the production cross sections
times the branching fractions range from 0.4 fb at mA = 600 GeV and mϕ = 800 GeV to 190 fb
at mA = 40 GeV and mϕ = 60 GeV. The data, combined with the constraints from previous
searches, exclude a large portion of the Type-X two-Higgs-doublet model’s phase space. This
search rules out this model as an explanation for the muon anomalous magnetic moment, as
described in Ref. [9].
