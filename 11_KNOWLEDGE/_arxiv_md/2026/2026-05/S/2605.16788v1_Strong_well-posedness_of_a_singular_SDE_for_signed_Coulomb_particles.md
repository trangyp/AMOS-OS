Strong well-posedness of a singular SDE for signed Coulomb
particles
Patrick van Meurs∗and Yoan Tardy†
Abstract
We consider an SDE system for signed Coulomb particles moving in R2. Due to the sin-
gular Coulomb interaction force, collisions between particles of opposite sign will happen in
finite time. Upon collision, the colliding particles are removed from the system. Our main
results are the existence and uniqueness of strong global solutions and the characterization
of all possible collisions. The challenge of the proofs is to deal with the singularity of the
interactions.
We overcome this by using scaling invariance of the process and by putting
together several tools from [FT25] developed for the similar Keller–Segel particle system.
Keywords Stochastic particle system, Coulomb interactions, collisions

applied; for γ small enough the equation (1.1) can be interpreted in a weak sense that does not
break down when particles collide. Then, particles instantaneously separate upon collision.
However, for the applications to vortices and dislocations mentioned above, it is more natural to
remove particles upon collision. Indeed, vortices and dislocations can be interpreted as topological
defects that cancel each other out upon collision if their signs are opposite. This motivates us
to apply the following collision rule: upon a collision (possibly between more than two particles),
pairs of particles of opposite sign are instantaneously removed until all particles at the collision
site have the same sign. Then, the remaining particles continue to evolve according to (1.1) (with
N reduced). We refer to this collision rule as the removal rule. Since particles are always removed
in pairs of opposite sign, the ‘net charge’ given by PN
i=1 bi is conserved during collisions.
A second motivation for removing particles upon collision is that for γ ≥1 the drift in (1.1)
is too strong for accommodating separation of particles after collision; see [FJ17, Section 6].
Therefore, [vMPS25] has not considered (SC) for γ ≥1. If one would desire to keep the particles
in the system, then colliding particles would have to stick together. This would mean that their
combined force on a third particle sums to 0 (because they are of opposite sign), and vice versa,
the forces on both particles induced by a third particle cancel each other out. Hence, the collided
particles would have to move as an independent Brownian motion, which is effectively the same
as removing them from the system.
1.2
Aim
Henceforth, we consider (1.1) with the removal rule described above. To the best of our knowledge
there is no global-in-time well-posedness result on (1.1) that can handle collisions. The goal of
this paper is to establish strong well-posedness of (1.1), and to study the kind of collisions that
may occur. The main challenges to overcome are that if a collision happens at τ, then we need to
prove that:
(A) Xτ−:= limt↗τ Xt exists a.s., and
(B) the particles in Xτ are distinct, where Xτ is the configuration obtained after applying the
collision rule to Xτ−.
Property (B) limits the type of collisions that can occur; it only allows for collisions for which
|n+ −n−| ≤1, where n+ (n−) is the number of positive (negative) colliding particles. Property
(B) is not strictly necessary for continuing the evolution of Xτ after τ. However, if Property (B)
holds, then the drift is smooth in an open region around Xτ, for which it is simple to continue the
solution in the strong sense.

1.4
Main results
Our main result, Theorem 7.2, shows that the behavior of the SDE system (1.1) is somewhere
in between the two extremal cases described in Section 1.3. In more detail, for each γ > 0, each
N ≥2, each b and each initial condition X0 of distinct particles,
(a) there exists a strong, global solution,
(b) the solution is path-wise unique,
(c) each collision happens at a distinct time,
(d) each collision is simple, i.e. between two particles of opposite sign,
(e) collisions keep on happening in finite time until only particles of the same sign are left.
Let us compare Properties (a)–(e) with the extremal cases from Section 1.3. With respect to
the extremal case γ = 0 of independent Brownian motions, (a) and (b) are shared, (c) and (d)
become empty statements (because collisions do not occur), but (e) is completely different. With
respect to the extremal case γ = ∞given by the ODE system (1.3), (a) and (b) are shared until
the first multi-particle collision at which two or more particles remain at the collision site after
applying the collision rule. Moreover, each of (c), (d) and (e) are different due to the complicated
collisions that may (or will not) occur in (1.3). In particular, with (c)–(e) on (1.1) established in
this paper, we obtain a precise meaning to the instability of stationary solutions and multi-particle
collisions of (1.3). Indeed, when (1.3) is perturbed with noise, our results show that multi-particle
collisions have to break down into several simple collisions, and that stationary solutions (which
only exist when both signs appear) break down and have a collision in finite time, irrespective of
the size of the noise. As a side result, we also show (Proposition 2.1) that the solution x(t) of the
ODE system (1.3) is left-continuous at its first collision. This was already proved in [SBO07], but
we use a completely different, more direct, ODE-based proof1. This proof serves as a blueprint
for the proof of the left-continuity of the process Xt at collision. The proof is inspired by [Tar24,
Section 5], which we describe below in Sections 1.6 and 2.

The instant separation rule raises two additional challenges with respect to (SC) from (1.1):

based on Dirichlet form theory. Moreover, due to the pairwise attraction between all particles,
the number of colliding particles may exceeds a critical number (which depends on γ), in which
case the colliding particles cannot separate anymore. When this happens, the particle system is
said to explode, and the solution ceases to exist.

of δK does not hold because of the signs b ((1.9) clarifies the dependence).
In our proof of Theorem 6.3, we do not apply the splitting above as done for (KS). Instead, we
rely on the fifth tool given by the space-time scaling invariance. This results in quite a different
proof construction than that in [FT25, Section 9]; see Section 6.3. We consider this the main
technical novelty of our paper.
Actually, the scaling invariance of (SC) also holds for (KS). In fact, the decomposition of
Xt into M(Xt), R(Xt) and U(Xρt) involves a similar space-time scaling. We expect that our
proof method can be adapted to (KS), which potentially results in a simpler proof of the collision
properties for (KS).

1.7
Discussion
Here we address possible extensions of our results. First, the extension from the Coulomb inter-
action force f(x) = x/|x|2 to the Riesz interaction force f(x) = x/|x|α for α > 1 would be very
interesting given the large literature on the Riesz interaction force; see e.g. the review [Lew22].
The main challenge is that the second tool breaks down, i.e. (1.5) will depend on y, and thus the
equation for the dispersion R(Xt) will not decouple.
Second, (1.5) does hold in higher dimensions for f(x) = x/|x|2 with x ∈Rd and d ≥3. Then,
f is sub-Coulomb. The five tools from Section 1.5 will still hold with the modification that the
‘2’ in the formula’s for δ and δK is replaced by d (see the proof of Lemma 4.4 for details). This
suggests that our proofs may be modifiable to the case d ≥3. We do not pursue this here and
leave it for future work.
Third, we address the question on a possible mean-field limit of (1.1), i.e. the limit N →∞
after a proper rescaling is applied. For the collision rule given by instant separation, such mean-
field limits are established in [FJ17, vMPS25] for γ =
1
N ˜γ with ˜γ small enough. For (SC) with
instant separation, the mean-field limit is a coupled set of two equations for the particle densities
ρ± of the positive and the negative particles. Both equations are conservative and both contain a
diffusion term and a nonlocal interaction term. For (SC) with the removal rule, we do not expect
the mean-field limit equations to be conservative. The expected equations are studied for regular
initial conditions in [MZ05, Theorem 3.1] and by a variational approach in [AMS11]. However,
neither of these results provide well-posedness for weak solutions, which poses a second obstacle
for establishing the mean-field limit of (SC).
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 establishes the continuity of solutions to the ODE
system (1.3) at collision. In Section 3 we recall several basic properties of Bessel processes. In
Section 4 we develop the tools mentioned in Section 1.5 for handling (SC). In Section 5 we prove
Proposition 5.1 on the continuity of the solution at the first collision time. In Section 6 we prove
Theorem 6.3 on the characterization of the first collision. In Section 7 we put these results together
to prove our main Theorem 7.2 on the global solution of (1.1).
