Abstract
We consider an inverted harmonic oscillator in the space L2(S) of square-integrable
functions on the circle S and compute its density of states employing the stationary
phase approximation.
Our computation is based on an oscillatory integral repre-
sentation of the Schwartz kernel of the time-evolution operator. This demonstrates
thermalisation as a semi-classical manifestation of the classical Lyapunov instability
— reported earlier in [1, 2] using heuristic analytic continuation. Our spectral anal-
ysis of the Hamiltonian points out and closes the conceptual and mathematical gaps
in the preceding literature.

Introduction
The Hawking temperature [3] is one of the quintessential predictions in semi-classical grav-
ity. A few mechanisms (see the reviews [4–6]) have been offered so far in order to understand
its physical underpinning. Several years ago, Dalui and his collaborators [1, 2] have shown
that this temperature can be understood as the semi-classical manifestation of the Lya-
punov instability of a classical particle around a black hole horizon. To be precise, they
have argued that the classical Hamiltonian for a 1-dimensional chargeless and massless
particle in a neighbourhood of a certain class of (e.g., static spherically symmetric) black
hole horizons is given by h(x, ξ) := κxξ in the leading-order approximation as long as only

There are, however, a few conceptual and computational gaps in their work. As re-

continuing ρ+. The key step in their approach — analytic continuation — is unsatisfactory
on both physical and mathematical grounds for the reasons explained below.

is because an IHO Hamiltonain ˆH−on L2(R) has absolutely continuous real spectrum (17)

because that yields complex eigenvalues (15) for ˆH−. As a consequence, one must consider
ˆH−as an operator on L2(S) instead of L2(R) in order to ensure discrete eigenvalues required
to make sense the notion of density of states.
Furthermore, time must be considered
as a complex parameter τ (in the sense of (2)) for an IHO in order to guarantee closed
trajectories γ−
l (τ) on the phase-space S×R as well as defining the notion of a thermal state.
Physically, this accounts to trade-off between the repulsive nature of an IHO trajectory
and periodicity.
To elucidate the underlying reasons behind the above-mentioned physical and math-
ematical gaps in the work of Dalui and his collaborators [1, 2], we have computed the
density of states ρ+(E) of a SHO employing the stationary phase approximation in Sec-
tion 3.1. It is now evident from (36) and (39) that the urge to infer (39) from (36) via the
analytic continuation ω 7→i ω is not completely unnatural. A naive attempt (as in the
preceding references) will result in an incorrect Maslov factor and miss the summation over
l in (39) but these can be avoided by a careful examination of the phase-space dynamics
under the analytic continuation. It is the spectral theory (Section 2.2) of ˆH−where the
analytic continuation-based approach fails completely and yields physically unreasonable
results like complex eigenvalues (15) of ˆH−.
We close this section by summarising the preceding discussion as follows. Our expres-
sion (39) of the semi-classical density of states of an IHO reproduces the primary result
by Dalui and his collaborators [1, 2], points out and corrects their mistakes in the Maslov
index and energy-degeneracy of periodic orbits γ−
l (τ), and clarifies the gaps in the spectral
properties of an IHO Hamiltonian ˆH−used in their article. In the future, we would like to
propound this picture in a quantum field theoretic setting. At this point, it is not evident
whether the stationary phase lemma is adequate for a field-theoretic generalisation. Also,
the time-evolution operator of the Klein-Gordon equation, for instance, is quite different
from an IHO Mehler kernel (29) used in our present investigation. We plan to address all
these in the forthcoming article.

let D be a topological vector space over the field C. We denote the space of continuous
