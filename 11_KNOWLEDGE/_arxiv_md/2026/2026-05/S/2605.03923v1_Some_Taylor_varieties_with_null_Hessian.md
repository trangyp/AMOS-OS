Abstract. Taylor varieties T n

Therefore, which are these hypersurfaces? If N ≥3, where only cones occur, as proved by P. Gordon and
M. Noether in [GN76] in 1876.
In higher dimensions, the classification of hypersurfaces not cones with vanishing Hessian play a classical
role in the history of algebraic geometry, the first examples being given by P. Gordon and M. Noether in the
same paper [GN76]. In particular, they gave a complete description of the hypersurfaces in P4 and a series
of examples of hypersurfaces with vanishing Hessian not cones for any N ≥5. An interesting observation is
that a null hessian hypersurface can be given as a tangential variety of a subvariety Y , where Y is its focal
loci, that is the locus where the Hessian determinant drops its rank. Note that in general Y ⊂Sing(X)
but not necessarily equality holds ([Rus16]). For its elegance, simplicity and source of further examples the
celebrated Perazzo cubic threefold in P4, given by f(z0, z1, z2, z3, z4) = z0z2
3 + z1z3z4 + z2z2

is worth to be mentioned. It is in fact generalized as the Perazzo cubic hypersurface in PN by the form

is a hypersurface of the form V (f), with
f(z0, . . . , zN) = z0A1(zk, . . . , zn) + · · · + zk−1Ak(zk, . . . , zn) + Ak+1(zk, . . . , zn)
where for i ∈{1, . . . , k} Ai’s are algebraically dependent but linearly independent of degree d −1, while
Ak+1 has degree d. So in particular hf ≡0.
Other known examples are Franchetta’s and Permutti’s, and their generalizations [CRS08, Fra54, Gon17,
GR15, Per57, Per64].
A new interesting family of examples of varieties with null Hessian is given by Gondim, Russo, and
Staglian`o in [GRS20].
Without entering into the picture, we only recall that the study of null Hessian hypersurfaces can also
be approached in a more algebraic setting. In fact, for a given homogeneous polynomial f ∈C[z0, . . . , zN]
of degree d one can consider the associated Artinian Gorenstein algebra A = S/AnnS(f), where S is the
ring of differential operators on C[z0, . . . , zN], and explore the rank of its intern multiplication maps, by
means of the so-called strong (or weak) Lefschetz Property. An interesting literature can be found within
this setting (see [dBW20, FMMR23, Gon17, MW09, MRP24, Wat14] just to cite some of them).
Recently, Conca, Naldi, Ottaviani and Sturmfels [CNOS23] introduced a class of algebraic varieties called
Taylor varieties T n
d,e,m whose definition is related to the Taylor expansion of a rational function in n variables.
In certain cases, these varieties are determinantal hypersurfaces; moreover, when m = d + 1, they have
vanishing Hessian (see [CNOS23, Theorem 4.4]).
Thus, Taylor hypersurfaces provide a new source of
varieties with vanishing Hessian, although some instances recover very well known examples (for example,
when n = 2, d = 1, e = 1, m = 2, one obtains a cone over the Perazzo hypersurface, cf. [CNOS23, Example
4.3]). Motivated by our interest in hypersurfaces with vanishing Hessian, in the following we extend this
result by proving that Taylor hypersurfaces of the form T 2
d,e,d+2 also have vanishing Hessian.
Our main result is the following.
Theorem 1.1. Fix d, e ∈N. If T 2
d,e,d+2 = V (det(PT )) and it is a non-defective hypersurface, then the
Hessian satisfies hf ≡0.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we introduce Taylor varieties, discuss their expected
dimension and explain how they are described via the Pad´e matrix (Section 2.1), an essential tool in our
work. In Section 3, we deal with the case T 2

of the form T n

T 2

The purpose of this section is to prove our main result. We start by proving a necessary condition for a

syzygies relations of the Taylor polynomial.
