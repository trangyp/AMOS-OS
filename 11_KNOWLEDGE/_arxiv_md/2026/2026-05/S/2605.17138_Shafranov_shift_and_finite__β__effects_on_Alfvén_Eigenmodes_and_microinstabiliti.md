Abstract
Future nuclear fusion reactors will have to confine plasma with strong kinetic gradients and
small fractions of fusion-born energetic particles (EP) that are ∼100 times hotter than the ther-
mal ions. In our analysis, we assume the existence of a stable MHD equilibrium and study the
unstable plasma perturbations. In this electromagnetic, kinetic, multi-scale, self-organizing sys-
tem, all species contribute both to the Shafranov shift (equilibrium effect) and to the plasma β
(plasma response). Nonetheless, due to the high complexity of the problem, many works neglect
these effects. We use the global, gyrokinetic code ORB5 to study the plasma stability. Starting
from an electrostatic, thermal plasma with adiabatic electrons in a β = 0 ideal-MHD equilibrium,
we systematically increase the realism of our models.
And study the linear stability and nonlinear fluxes of Toroidal Alfv´en Eigenmodes (TAE), and
the Ion Temperature Gradient (ITG), and Kinetic Ballooning Modes (KBM) microinstabilities
as they arise. Linearly, we find that Shafranov shift effects are a function of the toroidal mode
number, that they are mainly stabilizing, and stronger at longer wavelengths, impacting TAEs the
most with a 90% reduction in growth rate for cases which consistently account for the EP pressure
in the MHD equilibrium. Leading to a law of diminishing returns for the TAE growth rate as a
function of EP fraction. We find that with Shafranov shift asymptotically pushes the ITG frequency
up and the TAE frequency down. Furthermore, we show that KBMs are strongly damped by both
EPs (kinetic) and Shafranov shift (equilibrium) effects.
Nonlinearly we find that the linear TAE stabilization does not effect the saturation levels.
Nonetheless, the heat and particle fluxes carried by the TAE, are are reduced by the Shafranov
shift. While, the ITG fluxes and saturation levels are unaffected by the Shafranov shift.
Introduction
Burning plasmas which confine energetic particles (EPs), are multi-scale, self-organizing, dynamical
systems.
In which sources of free energy, e.g.
gradients in ion temperature and EP density can
destabilize the system resulting in turbulence and degraded confinement.
Axisymmetric toroidal
magnetic geometry (tokamak) is given by the Grad-Shafranov equation [1, 2]. Which is an effective
force balance between the currents, pressure, and magnetic field necessary to confine the plasma. In
real-life applications, for a given magnetic field, higher pressure translates to higher performance per
volume, but a less stable plasma.

Ubiquitous to burning plasmas are energetic particles (EPs), both as fusion-born α-particles
(3.5 MeV for D-T fusion), and as a result of heating and control schemes, such as NBI and ICRH
[3]. Moreover, EPs are essential for the operation of future reactors which will rely on confining the
α-particles to reach sustained burn after ignition. Importantly, the pressure contribution of this low-
density, hot species is often comparable with that of the thermal plasma, βEPs ≈βth, and therefore
changes the internal force balance and the MHD equilibrium reconstruction. Here βj = Pj/(B2
0/2µ0)
is a ratio between the kinetic pressure of the jth species and the plasma magnetic pressure, where
B0 = Bmag is the magnetic field on axis.
The geometrical center of a plasma in a tokamak is determined by last closed flux surface. The
internal pressure in a tokamak pushing against the magnetic flux surfaces displaces the magnetic axis
radially outwards by the Shafranov shift ∆(s) = Rmag −Rgeom(s), where Rmag is the location of the
magnetic axis, and Rgeom(s) = (Rmax(s) + Rmin(s))/2 [4]. We use the flux label s =
p
ψ/ψa, where
ψ is the poloidal magnetic flux, and ψa is the flux at the edge, i.e. minor radius a. A modified MHD
equilibrium with Shafranov shift has reduced effective bad curvature which helps stabilize ideal MHD
ballooning modes, and opens a second stability region beyond Troyon’s β limit [5, 6, 7]. As long as
the Shafranov shift is reasonably small ∆< a, the equilibrium response is generally stabilizing. Not
so is the case for the plasma response, where the finite β > 0 introduces electromagnetic effects and
coupling between the Alfv´en and ion sound spectrum.
Same kinetic gradients in temperature and density can also drive microinstabilities such as toroidal
ion-temperature-gradient (ITG) [8, 9, 10] and kinetic ballooning mode (KBM) [11]. Where the former
is very common in tokamak configurations and the latter being of major concern for high β machines.
Both (KBM and ITG) result in plasma turbulence and anomalous transport which degrades the
confinement and reduces performance [12]. The combined effects on stability are complex and case
dependent, e.g. including finite β (electromagnetic) effects acts to stabilize ITG modes [13], up to the
KBM limit [14], or unless Shafranov shift is accounted for [15].
Moreover, the EPs have large orbit widths that introduce multi-scale wave-particle interactions,
and move with Alfv´enic parallel velocities which can excite Toroidal Alfv´en Eigenmodes (TAEs) [16,
17]. In turn, the unstable TAEs can create strong bursts of energetic particle, expelling them from
the plasma [18].
In this work we aim to gain a deeper understanding of Shafranov shift and finite β effects on
the self-organization and stability of a burning plasma by using the global, gyrokinetic, code ORB5
[19, 20, 21]. We start from a simplified electrostatic cases with adiabatic electrons in a β = 0 magnetic
equilibrium, and systematically build the fully kinetic, electromagnetic picture. We note which cases
are self-consistent, i.e. the magnetic equilibrium accounts for the pressure of all species, and when
consistency matters. Nonlinearly we study the system’s saturation and resulting heat and particles
fluxes, connecting them to the linear effects.
For the nonlinear investigation, in order isolate the Shafranov shift effects we neglect the axisym-
metric n = 0 response of the plasma, including the self-generated zonal flows and structures. These
modes, which govern the nonlinear saturation and self-organization of the system, are considered in
the companion paper ”Nonlinear axisymmetric plasma response in self consistent MHD
equilibria”.

In this work we use ORB5 [19], a global, multi-species, Lagrangian PIC code, which solves the
nonlinear, electromagnetic, gyrokinetic Vlasov–Maxwell system. The fields are represented by the
potentials (ϕ, A∥) using B-splines on a 3D finite element grid in Fourier space. Throughout this work,
except for the internal Kink simulations, we use a correction term to the drift velocity vd, which
accounts for the parallel magnetic field perturbations δB∥to first order [22].
To reduce the Monte Carlo sampling noise ORB5 implements a field-aligned Fourier filter in a
straight-field-line coordinate representation (s, θ∗, φ).
This allows us to keep only the m ∈[nq −
∆m , nq + ∆m] modes. where, ∆m = 5, q is the safety factor, n and m are the toroidal and poloidal
mode numbers. In an axisymmetric geometry the toroidal modes are linearly decoupled, which allows
us to simulate a restricted set of toroidal mode numbers, n, while including a large range of poloidal
mode numbers m.
In the nonlinear simulations we use a modified Krook operator both as noise control and as a
source with a damping/drive of about 5% of the linear growth rate.
As noise control the Krook
operator is designed to conserve flux-surface-averaged moments such as density, energy, parallel flows
and residual zonal flows. In gradient-driven like simulations, the Krook operator acts as a source/sink
on the particles to maintain the original profile, e.g. the temperature gradient in ITG. Being only

A recently introduced ’pullback’ scheme with a mixed - variables representation [25] which deals

We refer to the normalized pressure used to recreate the MHD

The Alfv´en Eigenmodes are excited by energetic particles with either 1% or 3% dilution from the
bulk ions. We take the EP mass to be equal to ion mass mEP = mi, and the effect on bulk density
profiles is considered negligible. The EPs have a constant initial temperature that is 120 times hotter
than the bulk ions τEP (s = 0.5) = TEP /Ti = 120.
Figure 1 presents the species temperature and density logarithmic gradients in standard and peaked
cases. With the resulting (∆′), and the (β) profiles shown on the left. We chose the EP logarithmic
density gradient with several goals in mind. First, to induce the desired instability while avoiding
other instabilities that might exist in the system, e.g. an internal kink due to the q < 1 profile at the
core (s < 0.35). Second, to induce the microinstabilities and the AEs at a similar radial location, and
third, to obtain a system where both the low-n AE and the mid-n ITG have comparable growth rates.
Results and Discussion
Linear physics
In the following section we will use linear simulations to map the dispersion relation of our system.
Typically, ITG instabilities are studied in the electrostatic regime, assuming adiabatic electron re-
sponse, and a circular concentric magnetic geometry. However, this set of assumptions is incompatible
with a burning plasma which includes EPs and AEs that are electromagnetic and kinetic in nature.
Therefore we start from the electrostatic and adiabatic case and build up the complexity of the system
by adding electromagnetic effects, and EPs.

Part I - Thermal plasma
First we focus on the thermal bulk plasma without energetic particles, where gradients in temperature
and pressure drive ITG and KBM instabilities.
Electron models in electrostatic cases
In this section we perform electrostatic simulations, and compare the plasma response with two electron
models: adiabatic and drift kinetic [19]. Figure 2 (left panel) shows that for all cases, the kinetic
electron response results in about double the growth rate found with adiabatic electrons. Thus, we
recover using a global gyrokinetic approach the results of Rewolt and Tang [35] and Dominski et
al [36]. This result is attributed to the non-adiabatic response of the trapped electrons, at least in
collisionless plasmas.
Figure 2:
ITG growth rate γ per toroidal mode number n for adiabatic and kinetic electrons in the
electrostatic limit.
To study the effect of Shafranov shift on the ITG growth rate, we compare in the left panel of
Figure 2 ITG cases driven by peaked gradients with either adiabatic or kinetic electrons in either
self-consistent magnetic geometries with βMHD = βPK or inconsistent βMHD = 0 equilibrium. For
both kinetic and adiabatic electrons the additional Shafranov shift has a stronger stabilization at the
longer wavelengths (lower toroidal mode numbers). As a result the most unstable mode, i.e. the mode
with the highest growth rate, shifts to much shorter wavelengths (higher toroidal mode numbers).
When Shafranov shift is consistently accounted for, the shape and the peak of the dispersion relation
remain independent of the electron model.
Next, in the right panel of Figure 2 we compare between two cases where the Shafranov shift is
self-consistent with the plasma profiles. We find that for the electrostatic ITG, the gradient drive
overcomes the Shafranov shift stabilization with a proportionally stronger response from the kinetic
electrons as before. From this point forward, we will use drift kinetic electrons in all our cases.
Electromagnetic effects
In this section we study finite β effects in cases where the species pressure is self-consistent with the
MHD equilibrium, i.e. βMHD = βST for standard profiles and βMHD = βPK for the peaked profiles.
Figure 3 shows that there are little to no electromagnetic effects on the ITG growth rate for the weaker
standard profiles. While the frequency of the strongly driven ITG is pushed down by Shafranov shift
and pushed up by electromagnetic effects. To what appears to be an upper limit for the self-consistent
system.

Part III - Alfv´en Eigenmodes
In a fluid description, one finds that toroidal magnetic geometry couples the m and m + 1 poloidal
harmonics, leading to the formation of so called gaps in the shear Alfv´en wave (SAW) continuum.
In these gaps, Alfv´en Eigenmodes can resonate undamped, at radial locations where nq = (m + 1/2)
with a typical frequency of ω0 ∼= ωA/2q. Where ωA = vA/R is the Alfv´en frequency, vA = B0/√µ0ρm
is the Alfv´en velocity, and ρm is the mass density. These are global (low-n), electromagnetic modes
called Toroidal Alfv´en Eigenmodes (TAEs).
Energetic particles excite TAEs
The energetic particles in our system have Alfv´enic parallel velocities which match the resonance con-
dition to excite TAEs. In Figure 13 we plot the TAE spectrum in a βMHD = 0 magnetic equilibrium.
With an increase in EP fraction we see a proportional increase in TAE growth rate and therefore a
broadening of the TAE spectrum, where for 3% EPs the TAE spectrum extends to n = 9. The TAEs
driven by 1% EPs show a more complex behavior with a TAE to KBM transition occurring at n = 5.
For all our cases, the higher EP fraction increases the TAE frequency, reaching an asymptotic value
at the shorter wavelengths. An opposite trend to the one found by Biancalani et al [43] (in Figure 6 of
that paper). We find a small kinetic effect of the plasma temperature on the TAE growth rate, that is
proportional to the EP fraction. Additionally, for all cases, the higher plasma temperature decreases
the mode frequency.

We select the TAE with the highest growth rate (n = 2) to represent the TAE system and scan in
EP fraction and βMHD. On the one hand, as we increase the EP fraction we increase the TAE drive

Conclusions
We perform first-principles gyrokinetic numerical simulations to study the effects of plasma pressure on
the plasma stability. On our time scales we assume to have a stable ideal-MHD magnetic equilibrium,
on top of which an unstable plasma perturbation grows. Interested in scenarios where fusion born α
particles heat the plasma, we include small fraction (0%, 1%, 3%) of confined hot energetic particles
which contribute to the internal plasma pressure and introduce large scale kinetic effects. The plasma
pressure pushes on the magnetic field lines, leading to an outwards Shafranov shift of the magnetic
axis from its geometric location. This increase in plasma β introduces electromagnetic modes to the
16


## Page 17


system.
With the help of the global, electromagnetic, PIC code ORB5 [19], and the Grad-Shafranov solver
CHEASE[34], we study the plasma response in circular plasma geometry with a fixed safety factor
profile. Using the pressure profile as our second input, we generate several MHD equilibria which
either account for the thermal plasma pressure, the EP pressure or non of them. Focusing on cases
with standard and peaked temperature and density profiles.
Starting from the commonly used linear, electrostatic plasma with adiabatic electrons to study
the ITG instability, we build our physical picture step-by-step adding complexity at a time. First,
we show that in cases where the Shafranov shift is self-consistent with the thermal profiles, kinetic
electrons contribute to double the microinstability growth rate. While in the case where βMHD = 0
the kinetic effects also change the spectrum, pushing it to the longer wavelengths. Next we solve the
electromagnetic system to find Shafranov shift destabilizing the ITG in the case with standard (ST)
gradients. Here the additional local shear due to Shafranov shift reduces the total shear and thus the
damping. In the case with peaked (PK) gradients we first find an internal Kink unstable in circular
concentric magnetic equilibria (not a solution of the Grad-Shafranov equation). The internal Kink
shows a very strong Shafranov shift stabilization and is stabel in a βMHD = 0 equilibrium. Where
we find an unstable KBM, which are also strongly stabilized by the Shafranov shift in self-consistent
MHD equilibrium.
Next we introduce the energetic particles.
Showing they kinetically stabilize the KBM (when
Shafranov shift is excluded) and indirectly stabilize the ITG via Shafranov shift. The main effect
of EPs is to excite Alfv´en Eigenmodes with frequencies that match the TAE gap.
These low-n
range modes are especially sensitive to Shafranov shift stabilization and are strongly damped in self-
consistent MHD equilibria. Showing a ”saturation” like behavior of the growth rate as a function of
the EP fraction. Overall we find that linear Shafranov shift effects are mainly stabilizing and toroidal
mode n dependent, with a stronger effect on the longer wavelengths.
ITG shows weak Shafranov shift effect on the linear growth rate, on the saturation level and on
the resulting heat and particle fluxes. overall, we would say that it has a smoothening radial effect
which reduces the amplitude of small scale redial structures such as the peak of the flux or a sharp
change in gradient.
TAE linear growth rates are strongly affected by the Shafranov shift, while the nonlinear saturation
level remains relatively independent of by both the Shafranov shift and the linear growth rate. Unlike
the ITG, the resulting heat and particle fluxes are reduced by additional Shafranov shift.
In this work we neglect the axisymmetric response of the plasma, i.e. the n = 0 mode. Nonlinearly,
this mode is responsible for the formation of zonal flows and zonal structures which play a major role
in governing the self-organizing of the system’s impacting both TAE and ITG fluxes and stability.
Due to the high complexity of the phenomena we continue our investigation in a companion paper
”Nonlinear axisymmetric plasma response in self consistent MHD equilibria”.
In conclusion, burning plasmas are complex, self-organizing systems, where consistency with the
magnetic equilibrium matters, especially for the long wavelength, electromagnetic modes.
