Kohei Fukai · Bal´azs Pozsgay · Istv´an Vona

Abstract Free-fermion solvability in quantum spin systems is increasingly understood to be
governed by a graph Clifford algebra defined from the frustration graph of the Hamiltonian.
When the frustration graph belongs to certain classes, such as the even-hole-free and claw-
free (ECF) class, the Hamiltonian is solvable by hidden free fermions: it admits a free-fermion
solution although it does not reduce to a Majorana bilinear under the Jordan-Wigner transfor-
mation. However, unlike in the Jordan-Wigner case, where each mode is a linear combination of
single Majorana fermions, the explicit operator structure of the hidden free-fermion modes—
and that of the local conserved charges—has remained obscure. In this work, we derive a
path-product expansion that expresses each free-fermion mode as a linear combination of path
products along induced paths in the extended frustration graph. The expansion is obtained
from the generating function of the Krylov basis and yields the modes directly, without using
the transfer matrix or the nonlocal conserved charges as input. As an application, the mode
decomposition computes infinite-temperature dynamical correlation functions for arbitrary
ECF frustration graphs. We further obtain explicit expressions for local conserved charges as
linear combinations of path products along induced paths; these charges apply beyond the
free-fermion (ECF) class to more general claw-free frustration graphs. We also identify a uni-
fied family of generalized conserved charges that contains both the previously known nonlocal
conserved charges and these local conserved charges as special cases. For Fendley’s original
FFD chain with homogeneous couplings and periodic boundary conditions, in a suitable basis,
the structure of these local conserved charges exhibits the same Catalan-tree pattern as in the
spin-1/2 XXX chain.

Recently, Fendley introduced a free-fermion spin chain termed “free fermions in disguise”
(FFD) [13]. This model is exactly solvable but lies beyond the JW paradigm [14–21]: the
Hamiltonian maps to a four-Majorana interaction under the Jordan-Wigner transformation,
so the free-fermion solvability is hidden and the underlying algebraic structure is much less
transparent than in the standard JW case. Various multispin and parafermionic extensions have
also been developed in parallel [22–27]. Sufficient conditions for hidden free-fermion solvability
have been formulated graph-theoretically in terms of the frustration graph, which encodes
the anticommutation structure of the Hamiltonian terms [14, 15, 26, 28]: the graph being
even-hole-free and claw-free [14], or, more generally, simplicial and claw-free [15].
Despite these developments, several aspects of the hidden-free-fermion formalism remain
open. The connection between the generalized hidden free fermions and the traditional Jordan-
Wigner solvable models is unclear; the two cases appear to require entirely separate methods.
Although Ref. [15] provides a unified conceptual framework, the concrete construction of the
free-fermion modes still proceeds indirectly through an auxiliary edge operator, the transfer
matrix, and previously known nonlocal conserved charges. This stands in stark contrast to
the Jordan-Wigner case, where one simply diagonalizes a single-particle Hamiltonian matrix.
Moreover, while the procedure for constructing free-fermion modes is known, their explicit
structure has remained a mystery: what operators constitute them, and with what coefficients?
A second unresolved issue concerns the structure of conserved charges in these models.
For claw-free frustration graphs, the independent sets of the frustration graph yield a mutu-
ally commuting family of nonlocal conserved charges [14, 15]. However, the structure of local
conserved charges has remained largely unresolved. This question is significant because the ap-
pearance of higher local conserved charges beyond the Hamiltonian is a standard hallmark of
integrability in spin chains [29–34], and the presence or absence of local conserved charges has
become a useful rigorous diagnostic for integrability and nonintegrability in quantum many-
body systems [35–46]. Even in the free-fermion-solvable ECF case, the explicit form of local
conserved charges has not been pinned down: for Fendley’s original FFD chain, the logarithmic
derivative of the transfer matrix is known to generate them [13], yet even there no closed-form
operator expression has been given. For general claw-free frustration graphs beyond the free-
fermion setting, the situation is more delicate still, and even the existence of local conserved
charges has remained entirely open.
This paper is the second part of a series on graph Clifford algebras and hidden free fermions.
In the first part [47], we studied the algebras behind these models and the defining representa-
tions of these algebras. Furthermore, we constructed few-body eigenstates for specially chosen
antisymmetric Hamiltonians.
In this article, we answer the above questions by deriving an explicit path-product ex-
pansion for the free-fermion modes in generalized free-fermion spin chains, focusing on the
even-hole-free and claw-free case. We show that each free-fermion mode is a linear combina-
tion of path products—ordered products of Hamiltonian terms along induced paths in the
frustration graph extended by an auxiliary edge operator—with coefficients given by indepen-
dence polynomials of suitable residual graphs. This result follows from the Krylov generating
function in operator space, whose path-product expansion yields the free-fermion modes as
residues. The construction gives the modes directly, without using the transfer matrix or the
earlier nonlocal-charge formalism as input. The path-product expansion also produces graph-
theoretic identities for independence polynomials, revealing a concrete link between hidden
free fermions and induced-path combinatorics.
For the inverse problem, we rewrite the edge operator in terms of the free-fermion modes
and compute its infinite-temperature autocorrelation. More generally, this mode-decomposition
method computes infinite-temperature dynamical correlation functions of arbitrary Krylov

familiar: when every path has one vertex, the formula reduces to the nonlocal independent-set
charges of the transfer-matrix formalism [13–15], while when there is only one path, it reduces
to the local conserved charges constructed above. The same range bound follows by extending
the local conserved-charge cancellation mechanism to disconnected families of odd induced
paths.

2.2 Claw-free graphs and simplicial cliques
A graph is claw-free if it contains no claw K1,3 (Fig. 1) as an induced subgraph. A clique is a
subset of vertices K ⊆V such that every pair of vertices in K is adjacent.
The construction of free-fermion modes requires selecting a special clique Ks in the frus-
tration graph and introducing an auxiliary vertex connected to it. Given a clique Ks ⊆V ,
consider adding a new vertex vχ that is adjacent to all vertices in Ks and to no other vertices.
The resulting extended graph Gχ has vertex set V ∪{vχ} and edge set E ∪{(vχ, j) | j ∈Ks}.
When G is claw-free and Gχ also remains claw-free, the clique Ks is called simplicial. A claw-
free graph that contains a simplicial clique is called simplicial claw-free (SCF). This section
sets up the SCF framework, while the main free-fermion mode theorem below specializes to
connected ECF graphs.
Equivalently, a simplicial clique can be characterized intrinsically, without reference to the
extended graph, as follows. For a vertex j ∈V , we denote by Γ(j) its open neighborhood, i.e.,
the set of vertices adjacent to j:
Γ(j) = {j′ ∈V | {j, j′} ∈E}.
(5)
The closed neighborhood Γ[j] includes the vertex itself: Γ[j] = Γ(j) ∪{j}. A clique Ks is
simplicial if and only if, for every vertex j ∈Ks, the open neighborhood of j outside Ks forms
a clique; equivalently, since j ∈Ks, one may write this condition with the closed neighborhood:
Γ[j] \ Ks
is a clique for all j ∈Ks.

A key fact is that every even-hole-free and claw-free (ECF) graph is also SCF [52, 53]:
every connected ECF graph contains a simplicial clique, so the auxiliary edge vertex can
be attached without creating a claw. The ECF condition was introduced in Ref. [14] as a
sufficient condition for the existence of free-fermion modes, and was subsequently generalized
to the SCF condition in Ref. [15]. The present path-product proof of the mode formula uses
the ECF condition, because the even-hole-free condition is used in the loop cancellation in
Theorem 3.6. The conserved-charge construction in Section 5, by contrast, is formulated for
arbitrary claw-free graphs and its range is controlled by the even bubble wand obstruction
introduced there.

5 Conserved charges
We now collect the conserved-charge constructions that follow from the path-product calculus.
Unlike the free-fermion-mode construction above, this section does not require a simplicial
clique to exist or be chosen; that is, no edge operator is part of the conserved-charge setup.
In particular, it is not tied to the even-hole-free setting. The goal is to formulate the charge
constructions in the broader claw-free setting, including claw-free frustration graphs that are
not assumed to be hidden free-fermion solvable. The adjective “local” in this section is used
in the graph-Clifford sense stated in Section 2.
Earlier transfer-matrix constructions produced the nonlocal independent-set charges Q(k)
G
in Eq. (98) [13–15]. The generalized family introduced below contains these nonlocal charges
as one endpoint.
Here we first construct explicit local conserved charges and prove their conservation using
the path-product calculus. We then introduce a unified family of generalized conserved charges
that contains both the known nonlocal conserved charges and the new local conserved charges
as special cases. Finally, we discuss oriented even path operators, a more rigid parity-sensitive
variant whose conservation, within the even-hole range proved below, requires an induced-path
orientation and an additional edge-residual balance condition.

7.5 Remarks
For the Ising chain, each path product reduces to a single Majorana operator because the frus-
tration graph is a path: for each path size, there is a unique induced path from the boundary,
and the Jordan-Wigner string is in one-to-one correspondence with that induced path.
This example illustrates that the path-product expansion (15) generalizes the Jordan-
Wigner transformation. For more complex ECF frustration graphs with higher connectivity,
there can be multiple induced paths connecting the edge vertex χ to a given vertex j, and
individual path products no longer collapse to single Majorana operators. The free-fermion
modes for disguised models beyond the Jordan-Wigner paradigm [13] then become genuine
linear combinations of multiple path products, so the underlying free-fermion structure is
hidden. The next section illustrates this behavior in the Fendley model.

9 Summary and outlook
9.1 Overview
Induced paths are the elementary constituents in our construction. For hidden free fermion
models with even-hole-free and claw-free frustration graphs, Theorem 3.1 expresses each free-
fermion mode Ψk as a linear combination of path products along induced paths in the extended
frustration graph, with coefficients given by independence polynomials of residual graphs.
For arbitrary claw-free frustration graphs, Theorem 5.3 and Theorem 5.7 construct local and
generalized conserved charges from the same induced paths, in ranges controlled by the even-
bubble-wand obstruction. Both lines of result are obtained without using the transfer matrix
or the nonlocal conserved charges of previous approaches [13–15].
9.2 Free-fermion modes
The path-product formula for Ψk is proved through the Krylov generating function ΦG(u)
associated with the added vertex of the extended frustration graph, which represents the edge
operator χ. Theorem 3.6 gives a path-product expansion of this generating function, and the
mode decomposition in Theorem 3.12 then extracts the free-fermion modes as residues at its
poles. The resulting path-product operators are shown to satisfy directly the two defining prop-
erties of a free-fermion mode: the eigenoperator equation [HG, Ψk] = 2ϵkΨk and the canonical
anticommutation relation {Ψk, Ψl} = δk+l,0. Equation (21) and the Jacobi identity yield an
identity for {ΦG(u), ΦG(v)} (Theorem 3.9); residue extraction then gives the anticommutation

identity over induced paths. In Section 6, acting with the modes Ψk on the all-up reference

Ref. [47]. For dynamics, Corollary 3.14 extends the infinite-temperature autocorrelation for-

frustration graphs, a result that may be useful for dissipative extensions of the FFD frame-
work [66]. For the Fendley model, Subsection 8.4 carries out a thermodynamic saddle-point
analysis of the path-product expansion of a free-fermion mode: at fixed range, the dominant
induced paths have a preferred path-vertex density ρ∗(p) tied to the momentum of the mode,
and the optimized operator weight neither grows nor decays exponentially with the distance
from the boundary.
The transverse-field Ising example in Section 7 shows explicitly that the path-product
formula reproduces the Jordan-Wigner strings: when the frustration graph is a path, the
induced path from χ to each vertex j in the extended graph is unique, and its path product
coincides with the Jordan-Wigner string ending at j. For FFD models with a more general
claw-free, even-hole-free frustration graph, the same mechanism persists: each free-fermion
mode is a linear combination of path products along induced paths from χ to the vertices of
the frustration graph, with coefficients given by independence polynomials of residual graphs.
The essential difference from the Ising path graph is that the induced path from χ to a fixed
endpoint is no longer unique, so several path products with the same endpoint can enter the
same mode, a feature that was largely invisible in the earlier transfer-matrix formalism [13–15].
9.3 Local conserved charges
The path-product formalism also gives explicit higher-order local conserved charges. Equa-
tion (120) gives the odd local conserved charges H(2k+1)
G

transfer-matrix nonlocal charges, which are assembled from products over independent sets of

scopic family of odd local conserved charges whenever the smallest even bubble wand grows
with the system size, and in particular whenever no even bubble wand exists, via Corollary 5.4.
When even bubble wands do appear at fixed size, extending the path-product construction
beyond the first obstruction is left for future work; natural directions include coefficients in-
corporating the generalized cycle symmetries of Ref. [15], or a reduction by gauging out cycle
symmetries as in Ref. [19]. The Fendley model is the canonical example: its periodic frustration
graph is C2

already carries the full odd family.
The homogeneous periodic Fendley model has, in addition, a family of oriented even path
charges. Translation symmetry cancels the singleton residual obstruction of Theorem 5.8, so
these even path charges are conserved as well. Equation (253) packages the odd and even fam-
ilies into a single translational path-product formula. Thus the homogeneous periodic Fendley
model has local conserved charges of both path-size parities, whereas for arbitrary inhomo-
geneous couplings the construction proved here retains only the odd local conserved charges.
This one-parity survival is reminiscent of the open spin-1/2 XXX chain, where only the local
conserved charges with even spin-support length remain, giving half as many charges as in the
periodic chain [67]. Lemma 8.2 and Corollary 8.3 show that triangular recombinations of the
homogeneous periodic charges in Eq. (253) produce local conserved charges with a Catalan-tree
coefficient pattern analogous to the XXX and SU(N)-invariant charges [48–50].

This unresolved reconstruction problem does not affect the results proved above, since the

of each Ψk into the right-hand side of Eq. (275) produces a large sum of products of induced-

by single vertices j of the frustration graph. A direct path-product proof would therefore have
to show that the single-vertex part survives, and that all remaining products supported on
longer induced paths, together with products not supported on induced paths, cancel. The first
requirement should follow by combining Bencs-type independence-polynomial identities [55]
with Eq. (47). The second requirement appears to demand higher path-product analogues of
this mechanism, and no such identities are presently known to us. For this reason, a complete
proof of Eq. (275) within the path-product formalism has not yet been obtained. Develop-
ing such a proof would likely require new graph-theoretic input, including generalizations of
Bencs-type identities to products associated with longer induced paths, and constitutes an
interesting direction for future work.
The path-product expansion gives an explicit operator construction of the free-fermion
modes, i.e., the physical spectrum-generating fermion modes Ψk. The Fendley model also has
an exponentially degenerate sector whose Hilbert-space structure was recently clarified in
Ref. [20] in terms of ancilla fermions distinct from the physical fermion modes. It is natural
to ask what operator basis organizes these ancilla zero modes, and whether that basis has a
graph-theoretic interpretation on the frustration graph. A related inverse problem is to express
local operators in the fermionic description. Within the Krylov operator space generated from
a fixed edge operator χ, this inverse problem is solved by the present path-product formula: the
generating function ΦG(u) packages the Krylov basis {ϕj}j≥0, and its mode decomposition
writes these basis elements in terms of the physical fermion modes. The next target is the
corresponding inverse problem for local operators outside this Krylov space. The desired object
is therefore an explicit inverse transform realizing arbitrary local operators inside the fermionic
operator algebra generated jointly by the physical fermion modes and the ancilla zero modes.
Such a transform would extend the edge-operator computation of Corollary 3.14 to infinite-
temperature correlation functions of arbitrary local operators.
The induced paths identified here serve as the constituents of FFD local conserved charges,
in parallel with the model-specific constituents from which local conserved charges are as-
sembled in standard interacting integrable lattice models. In the spin-1/2 XXX chain [68]
and its SU(N)-invariant generalizations, the corresponding constituents are the nested cross
products of Pauli vectors [30, 48–50]. In the XYZ chain [69–75], the corresponding con-
stituents can be described in terms of doubling-product operators and related MPO construc-
tions [59, 60, 65, 76, 77]. In the one-dimensional Hubbard model [78, 79], the local conserved

examples, however, the construction of local conserved charges remains model-dependent, re-

which are defined purely in terms of the frustration graph. This contrast raises the question
whether a comparably abstract graph-theoretic or algebraic framework can organize the local
conserved charges in these standard interacting examples. The Catalan-tree analogy raises a
further concrete question. Since the XXX Catalan-tree pattern admits anisotropic and elliptic
extensions in the XYZ direction, does the homogeneous periodic Catalan-tree pattern of the
Fendley model admit an elliptic or otherwise anisotropic extension of its own?

A complementary program on the absence of local conserved charges is suggested by the
path-product expansion itself. The expansion identifies induced paths as the intrinsic graph-
local constituents from which the local conserved charges are assembled in claw-free frustration
graphs, and thereby singles out induced-path size as the natural notion of locality in the graph
Clifford algebra. With this notion of locality, a sharper question can be posed for frustration
graphs outside the claw-free family, where Theorem 5.3 no longer guarantees a hierarchy of odd
path-product charges: for which graphs does no nontrivial local conserved charge in the graph
Clifford algebra, beyond HG itself, commute with HG for any nonzero choice of couplings?
Adapting absence-of-charge methods developed for spin-chain and lattice integrable models [31,
32, 35–38, 42, 44] to this graph-Clifford notion of locality would provide a systematic route to
graph-theoretic criteria for the absence of local conserved charges.
