Introduction
Polish spaces are central objects of study in classical descriptive set theory, char-
acterized by several fundamental properties: they are exactly the Π0
2 subspaces
of the Hilbert cube, they admit continuous surjections from the Baire space NN,
and they can be constructed as completions of countable dense sets [6]. In the
framework of second-order arithmetic, a Polish space is handyly formalized as a
pair (A, d) consisting of a countable set A and a pseudo-metric d, where points

are represented as eﬀective Cauchy sequences. This formalization has been ex-
tensively utilized in the reverse mathematics of various theorems in analysis
[11].
While the representation of points as equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences
is a standard approach for separable metric spaces, it can be generalized to
second-countable T0 spaces by replacing the metric with a quasi-metric.
In
particular, the class of quasi-Polish spaces, whose structural properties were
extensively investigated by de Brecht [2], serves as a common generalization of
ω-continuous domains and Polish spaces. Notably, quasi-Polish spaces possess
characterizing properties analogous to those of Polish spaces; for instance, they
are precisely the Π0
2 subspaces of the powerset space P(N) (identiﬁed with the
countable product of the Sierpi´nski space) endowed with the Scott topology.
Another approach to formalizing non-metrizable spaces involves the use of
poset spaces (poset is the abbreviation of preorder set).
Mummert/Stephan
[10] investigated the properties of two types of poset spaces: UF spaces and MF
spaces. Regarding the former, it has been observed that the class of UF spaces
is equivalent to that of quasi-Polish spaces by Honda et al. Furthermore, from
the perspective of point-free topology, these spaces are known to be equivalent
to sober spaces whose frames of open sets are countably presented (Heckmann
[5]).
Consequently, the class of quasi-Polish spaces admits several equivalent char-
acterizations, including: (1) quasi-Polish spaces, (2) UF spaces, (3) NP spaces,
(4) Π0
2 subspaces of P(N), and (5) sober spaces associated with countably pre-
sented frames.
While these representations are mathematically equivalent in classical set
theory, switching between them depending on the application—such as descrip-
tive set-theoretic, computational, or point-free topological contexts.
For in-
stance, the quasi-metric framework is indispensable for establishing analytic
results like the generalized Hurewicz theorem [3]. In this study, we investigate
the methods for switching between these representations within the framework
of second-order arithmetic. Such translations are crucial, as they allow for the
“cross-pollination” of results between diverse formalizations; for example, im-
porting metrization theorems developed for poset spaces [9] into the theory of
Π0
2 subspaces of P(N). However, as we demonstrate, the ability to perform
these transitions is not always trivial and often requires signiﬁcant axiomatic
strength beyond RCA0.
In this paper, we introduce several formalizations of quasi-Polish spaces
within second-order arithmetic and conduct a systematic reverse mathematical
analysis of the transitions between these diﬀerent representations. Our main
results are summarized in the following theorems.

2.1
Poset spaces
We ﬁrst review the deﬁnitions of poset spaces studied in [9, 10]. Poset spaces
exist as analogues to ideal spaces; they represent essentially the same structures,
diﬀering only in the direction of the partial order.
Deﬁnition 2.1 (poset spaces). (P, ≤) is a poset if ≤is the preorder on P. A
subset F of a poset P is called a ﬁlter on P if it satisﬁes the following conditions.
(i) ∀p, p′ ∈P((p ∈F ∧p ≤p′) →p′ ∈F)
(ii) ∀p, p′ ∈P((p, p′ ∈F) →∃r ∈F(p, p′ ≤r))
1. A Filter F is unbounded if ¬∃r ∈P(∀p ∈F(r < p)). A UF space of P,
denoted by UF(P) is the set of unbounded ﬁlters on P.
2. A Filter F is maximal if there is no ﬁlter which has F as a proper subset,
so F satisﬁes ∀r ∈P((r /∈F) →∃p ∈F(r⊥p)). A MF space of P, denoted
by MF(P) is the set of maximal ﬁlters on P.
3. A Filter F is non-principal if ∀p ∈F ∃p′ ∈F(p < p′). A space of non-
principle ﬁlters is denoted by NP(P).
A space of ﬁlters is topolized by a basis {Np : p ∈P}. Np = {F ∈F(P) : p ∈F}.
The topologies on UF(P), MF(P) are relative topologies of F(P).
We say a poset P is handy when UF(P) = NP(P).
Every second-countable poset space is a countably based (it can be formed
from a countable poset) [10].

Deﬁnition 2.2 (quasi-Polish spaces). A quasi-metric on a set X is a function
d : X × X →R that satisﬁes the following.
(i)∀x, y ∈X(d(x, y) ≥0)
(ii)∀x, y ∈X(d(x, y) = d(y, x) = 0 →x = y)
(iii)∀x, y, z ∈X(d(x, y) + d(y, z) ≥d(x, z))
A quasi-metric d generates a topology on X with a basis B = {{y ∈X :
d(x, y) < r} : x ∈X, r ∈R>0}. A sequence (an)n in a quasi-metric space X is
called left-Cauchy if:
∀ε > 0 ∃N ∈N ∀m > n > N(d(an, am) < ε)
We say that (X, d) is Smyth-complete if every left-Cauchy sequence in X con-
verges to a point a ∈X in the following sense: ∀ε > 0 ∃N ∈N ∀n > N(d(a, an) <
ε ∧d(an, a) < ε).
A quasi-metric d induces a metric ˆd deﬁned by ˆd(x, y) = max{d(x, y), d(y, x)}.
We say that (X, d) is bicomplete if every ˆd-Cauchy sequence in X converges to
a point a ∈X.
1. A Polish space is a separable and completely metrizable space.
2. A quasi-Polish space is a separable and Smyth-completely quasi-metrizable
