QUIVER VARIETIES AND DUAL CANONICAL BASES
MING LU AND XIAOLONG PAN
Dedicated to Professor Claus Michael Ringel on the occasion of his 80th
birthday
Abstract. We survey some recent developments on the theory of dual canonical bases
for quantum groups and ıquantum groups. The ıquiver algebras were introduced by Wang
and the first author, which are used to give two realizations of quasi-split ıquantum groups
of type ADE: one via the ıHall algebras and the other via the quantum Grothendieck
rings of Nakajima-Keller-Scherotzke quiver varieties.
The geometric construction of the
ıquantum groups produces their dual canonical bases with positivity, generalizing Qin’s
geometric realization of quantum groups of type ADE. Recently, the authors provided a
new construction of the dual canonical basis in the setting of ıHall algebras, and proved
that it is invariant under braid group actions, and obtained the positivity of the transition
matrix coefficients from the Hall basis to the dual canonical basis. As quantum groups can
be regarded as ıquantum groups of diagonal type, we demonstrate that the dual canonical
bases of quantum groups coincide with the double canonical bases defined by Berenstein
and Greenstein, and resolve several conjectures therein.

1.1.2. ıQuantum groups and ıHall algebras. Starting with Satake diagrams, Letzter [Let99,

and ıquantum groups are reviewed in Section 2.
Building upon the framework of semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebras for 1-Gorenstein al-
gebras (see [LP21] and [LW22a, Appendix]), the first author and Wang [LW22a, LW23]
formulated the ıHall algebras of ıquiver algebras associated to ıquivers (Q, ϱ), where ϱ is
an involution of the quiver Q. The ıHall algebras of ıquiver algebras are used to provide a
realization of the universal quasi-split ıquantum groups eUı. For a survey see [LW24]. We
review the ıquiver algebras and ıHall algebras in §4.1–§4.2.
As an application, BGP type reflection functors are constructed for ıquiver algebras, which
induce the relative braid group symmetries of eUı; see [LW21b, LW22b]. The BGP type
reflection functors are reviewed in §5.3.
1.2. Quiver varieties.
1.2.1. Quantum groups and quiver varieties. Lusztig in [Lus90b, Lus91, Lus93] used perverse
sheaves on the varieties of representations of a quiver Q to realize U+. The relation between
Ringel’s realization and Lusztig’s categorification is given by sheaf-function correspondence;
see [Lus98]. Nakajima [Na01, Na04] further developed Lusztig’s construction, and introduced
(graded) Nakajima quiver varieties. We review Lusztig’s geometric realization in §3.2.
Via quantum Grothendieck rings of cyclic quiver varieties, Qin [Q16] provided a geometric
construction of the Drinfeld doubles of quantum groups of type ADE. Qin’s work was built
on the construction of Hernandez–Leclerc [HL15] (who realized half a quantum group using
graded Nakajima quiver varieties) as well as the concept of quantum Grothendieck rings
introduced by Nakajima [Na04] and Varagnolo–Vasserot [VV03]. These constructions are
reviewed in §4.9.

1.2.2. ıQuantum groups and quiver varieties. As a further generalization of graded Nakajima
quiver varieties, motivated by the works [Na01, HL15, LeP13], Keller and Scherotzke [KS16,
Sch19] formulated the notion of regular/singular Nakajima categories R, S for any acyclic
quiver Q. Note that R, S were called the generalized Nakajima categories in [KS16, Sch19],
and they are called Nakajima-Keller-Scherotzke (NKS) categories in [LW21b]. The NKS
varieties are by definition (cf. Definition 4.7) the representation varieties of R and S. The
NKS categories and varieties are reviewed in §4.3.
For a Dynkin ıquiver (Q, ϱ), we find that its ıquiver algebra Λı can be realized as a singular
NKS category Sı, and then use the (dual) quantum Grothendieck ring of perverse sheaves
over Sı to realize quasi-split ıquantum group eUı; see [LW21b]. Qin’s construction [Q16]
of the Drinfeld double eU uses such cyclic quiver varieties, which can be viewed as NKS
varieties of Sı of diagonal type; cf. the ıquiver algebras are used to formulate Bridgeland’s
Hall algebras; cf. [LW22a]. The geometric realization of ıquantum groups is reviewed in
§4.4.
1.3. Dual canonical bases.
1.3.1. Canonical bases of quantum groups. Based on the geometric realization of U+ in
[Lus90b], Lusztig produced the canonical basis of U+ by simple perverse sheaves; cf. also
[Ka91] for the crystal basis. Lusztig [Lus90b] also introduced braid group symmetries on the
quantum group U and used them to define the PBW basis. He also used the PBW basis
to give an elementary construction of the canonical basis of U+; also see [DDPW08, §11.6]
by noting that PBW bases in this case coincide with Hall bases (i.e. natural bases of Hall
algebras). The canonical basis of U+ via perverse sheaves is reviewed in §3.2, and via Hall
basis is reviewed in §3.3.
The dual canonical basis of U+ also has very nice properties and has deep connections to
cluster algebras. We used Hall basis to give it an elementary construction; see [LP25]. This
is reviewed in §5.1.
1.3.2. Dual canonical bases via perverse sheaves. It is a natural question to expand the
canonical basis on U+ to the whole quantum group U. Lusztig found that this is impossible,
and he found a way to solve this question by introducing a modified version ˙U of the quantum
group; see [Lus93]. Inspired by Lusztig’s construction, Bao and Wang [BW18a, BW18b]
found the canonical bases on ˙Uı, the modified ıquantum group.
A bonus of the geometric approach [Q16, LW21b] is the construction of an integral and
positive basis on eU and eUı, which contains as subsets the (mildly rescaled) dual canonical
bases of Lusztig for U+ [Lus90b]. For the quantum group, this positive basis expands the
dual canonical bases of U± to the whole quantum group, and solved the natural question
above in some sense. We call this positive basis the dual canonical basis of eU and eUı, which
corresponds to the perverse sheaves on the NKS quiver varieties.
1.3.3. Dual canonical bases via ıHall algebras. Recently, we [LP25, LP26a, LP26b] used ıHall
algebras to reconstruct the dual canonical bases on quantum groups and ıquantum groups.
This is based on an anti-involution · of eUı (called bar involution) and Lusztig’s Lemma
[BZ14]; note that it is different to Lusztig’s bar involution [Lus90b]. This is reviewed in §5.2.

1.3.4. Double canonical bases of quantum groups. Berenstein and Greenstein [BG17] have
constructed a double canonical basis of eU by combining the dual canonical bases of the
positive and negative halves of eU.
This basis has many properties similar to the dual
canonical basis, for example they are both bar-invariant and contain the dual canonical
bases of the positive and negative halves. We [LP26b] obtained the coincidence of the dual
canonical basis and the double canonical basis for eU, and then solved several conjectures for
double canonical bases therein. We reviewed the double/dual canonical bases of quantum
groups in §5.4.

canonical bases and put them in context.

7.5. Beyond quasi-split type. A Satake diagram consists of a pair (I = I• ∪I◦, ϱ), where
I = I• ∪I◦is a bi-colored partition of the Dynkin diagram I, and ϱ is a diagram involution
(we allow ϱ = Id), subject to suitable conditions. In finite type, the Satake diagrams are
classified by Araki; cf. [BW18b, Table 4]; they are in bijection with real forms of complex
simple Lie algebras.
Problem 7.6. Provide ıHall algebra realization and NKS geometric realization of eUı asso-
ciated to a general Satake diagram of finite type, and construct the dual canonical basis for
eUı.
