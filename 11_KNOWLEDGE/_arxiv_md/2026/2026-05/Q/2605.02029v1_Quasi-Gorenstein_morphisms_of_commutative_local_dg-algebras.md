QUASI-GORENSTEIN MORPHISMS OF COMMUTATIVE LOCAL
DG-ALGEBRAS
ZACHARY NASON, ANDREW J. SOTO LEVINS, AND RYAN WATSON
Abstract. We introduce quasi-Gorenstein morphisms of commutative local dg-algebras
and use a Gorenstein version of the virtually small property to characterize them, a result
which is new even for homomorphisms of local rings. In a different direction, we char-
acterize exact sequences in a noetherian local ring, in the sense of Avramov, Henriques,
and S¸ega, in terms of quasi-Gorenstein morphisms involving Koszul complexes.
1. Introduction
In [3] Avramov and Foxby introduced quasi-Gorenstein homomorphisms of noetherian
local rings. These are the homomorphisms that capture the Gorenstein property: for a
noetherian local ring R with residue field k, R →k is quasi-Gorenstein if and only if R
is Gorenstein. Moreover, if a local homomorphism φ: R →S of noetherian local rings is
quasi-Gorenstein, then R is Gorenstein if and only if S is Gorenstein.
In this paper we introduce quasi-Gorenstein morphisms of commutative local dg-algebras,
extending work of Avramov and Foxby. We first review necessary background for the paper
in Section 2, and in Section 3 we prove dg-analogs of results about quasi-Gorenstein ring
homomorphisms that will be used throughout the paper.
The following is our first main result - it is new even when both R and S are rings.
Theorem A (Proposition 3.4 and Theorem 4.6). Let φ: R →S be a morphism of com-
mutative local dg algebras with S0 a noetherian local ring and GdimR S < ∞. Then the
following are equivalent
(1) φ is quasi-Gorenstein.
(2) Every dg S-module M with bounded and degreewise finite length homology that
has finite Gorenstein dimension over R is virtually G small over S.
This theorem parallels results of Briggs, Iyengar, Letz, and Pollitz [7, Theorem 3.2] and
of Dwyer, Greenlees, and Iyengar [13, Theorem 9.1] that show if φ: R →S is a surjective
homomorphism of noetherian rings of finite flat dimension, then φ is locally complete inter-
section if and only if any S-complex with finite length homology that is proxy small as an
R-complex is also proxy small over S.
The following is our second main result. Recall that a sequence x1, . . . , xn in a noetherian
local ring A is exact if for 1 ≤i ≤n −1, xi is A/(x1, . . . , xi+1)-regular or is an exact zero
divisor on A/(x1, . . . , xi+1).

5. The canonical augmentation of the Koszul complex
In this section, for a dg-algebra R, we study the quasi-Gorensteinness of the canonical
augmentation R →H0(R), focusing on when R is a Koszul complex.
Remark 5.1. Note that for a dg-algebra R, if the canonical augmentation R →H0(R) is
quasi-Gorenstein, then amp RHomR(H0(R), R) = 0. Since sup RHomR(H0(R), R) = sup R
by [25, Proposition 3.3], we have that inf RHomR(H0(R), R) = sup R. Thus, by the definition
of Gorenstein dimension, we have that GdimR H0(R) = −sup R. This unintuitively implies
that the Gorenstein dimension of H0(R) with respect to R is always nonpositive if it is finite.
It is not always the case that R and H0(R) are simultaneously Gorenstein, and so by
Proposition 3.7 it is not always the case that the canonical augmentation is quasi-Gorenstein.
A consequence of [28, Proposition 5] is that it is always possible to write down a dg-algebra
R that is not Gorenstein such that H0(R) is Gorenstein. The next example shows that it is
possible for a dg-algebra R to be Gorenstein without H0(R) being Gorenstein.
Example 5.2. Let A = k[[x, y]] where k is a field and let R be the Koszul complex on x2, xy.
Then A is Gorenstein, and so R is Gorenstein by [15, Theorem 4.9], but H0(R) = A/(x2, xy)
is not Gorenstein. Now by Proposition 3.7 R →H0(R) is not quasi-Gorenstein.
The following gives an example where the canonical augmentation is quasi-Gorenstein.

5.8. Let A be a noetherian local ring. An element x ∈A is an exact zero divisor if it satisfies
