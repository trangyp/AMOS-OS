The evaluation of thermoelectric conversion efficiency remains challenging owing to the lack of in-
ternationally standardized measurement protocols. Commonly used techniques—including the heat
flow, guarded heater, and AC Harman methods—differ fundamentally in their operating principles
and sensitivity to heat losses. In this study, we benchmark three module-level efficiency measurement
techniques—the heat-flow, guarded heater, and AC Harman methods—using commercial Bi2Te3-
based modules with different substrates materials. The conversion efficiencies obtained using the
heat flow and guarded heater methods showed good agreement within experimental uncertainty
for temperature differences up to 70 K. In contrast, the AC Harman method underestimated the
conversion efficiency by approximately 30 %. Through systematic measurements on modules with
different substrates and detailed finite element simulations, this underestimation was attributed to
boundary-condition effects and radiative heat dissipation, which significantly reduce the effective
temperature difference developed across the module in the Harman configuration. These results
highlight the limitations of the AC Harman method for quantitative conversion-efficiency evaluation
under non-ideal thermal environments and emphasize the necessity of accounting for radiative and
substrate-related heat losses. Nevertheless, with appropriate modeling and correction, strategies,
the AC Harman method remains a viable tool for rapid performance screening. Our results provide
a quantitative benchmark of major measurement techniques and contribute to clarify best prac-
tices for module-level thermoelectric metrology and guide method selection, fully supporting future
efforts toward methodological standardization.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Thermoelectric modules play a key role in waste heat
recovery by efficiently converting waste heat into electri-
cal energy. Dissemination of thermoelectric power gen-
eration using waste heat necessitates the establishment
of standardized and accurate measurement techniques to
precisely estimate conversion efficiency[1–5]. The uncer-
tainty in measuring thermoelectric properties is approx-
imately 20 % as per the results of a recent international
round-robin test[6–8]; the results highlight the lack of a
standardized evaluation protocol or sample modules in
the field of thermoelectrics[9–12].
The performance of thermoelectric power generation
modules is typically evaluated by assessing their conver-
sion efficiency, η, defined as the ratio of the input heat
flow to the high-temperature side of the module, Qin, to

B.
Heat flow and guarded heater methods
In HFM, a calibrated Cu-block heat flow meter was
employed to measure heat flow from the cold side of the
module Qflow[13, 21].
Embedded thermocouples along
the Cu block accurately measured the temperature gra-
dient.
The heat flow was calculated using the Fourier
equation. The input heat flow, Qin, into the module can
be determined along with the maximum output power,
Pout, generated by the module using an external electri-
cal load device as: Qin = Qflow + Pout. The combined
standard uncertainty of heat flux evaluation in the HFM
ranges from 10 % to 13 % (373 K to 1023 K) at a cov-
erage factor k = 2, corresponding to a confidence level
of 95 %[13]. As shown in Fig. 1(b), the HFM involves
placing a thermoelectric module in series with a refer-
ence block of known thermal conductivity. Multiple ther-
mometers are embedded in the reference block, and heat
flow is evaluated based on the temperature gradient.

C.
AC Harman method
The AC Harman method is a valuable tool for esti-
mating the ZT, enabling rapid assessment of module-
level performance (Fig. 1(d)). The experimental setup
used for the AC Harman method, as shown in Fig. 2(b),
employed a heater block placed beneath the module to
control its temperature. Silicone grease was applied be-
tween the module and the heater block to ensure good
thermal contact. Unlike the other two methods, one side
of the module was exposed to the environment, resulting
in a temperature difference caused by heat loss due to

In previous studies, we evaluated BiTe-based thermo-
electric materials using a comparable approach and found
that the (RDC −RAC)/RAC value measured at ambient
pressure was underestimated by up to 40 % compared
with measurements performed under high-vacuum con-
ditions, owing to increased convective heat loss[24]. This
behavior is attributed to the transition from the viscous

In the present apparatus, when operated under vacuum
conditions with pressures P ≤10−1 Pa, the effect of con-

For the time-domain voltage measurements, AC or DC
currents with identical root-mean-square values were ap-

the frequency-domain measurements, an LCR meter
(HIOKI IM3590 impedance analyzer) operated in ana-
lyzer mode was used to perform frequency-sweep mea-
surements, with the integration time set to one cycle cor-
responding to a single period of the applied signal.

B.
Numerical simulation
To clarify the origin of this discrepancy, we examine
the factors contributing to the difference in conversion
efficiency evaluated using the AC Harman method. A
key distinction lies in the boundary conditions employed
in the measurements. In the AC Harman method, one
side of the module is effectively open: heater on the high-
temperature side is positioned beneath the module, while
the opposite side is exposed to a vacuum, resulting in an
uncontrolled temperature on the low-temperatures side.
In addition, radiative heat transfer from surfaces not in
contact with the heater block induces multidirectional
heat flow, thereby reducing the effective temperature dif-
ference within the module.

dissipation to the surrounding environment, whereas the
thicker alumina substrate tends to retain heat within the
module.
To further confirm this interpretation, finite element
analysis was performed using COMSOL under the same
conditions as those applied to the skeleton-type module,
with a focus on simulating the temperature difference
in the alumina-type module.
The calculated hot-side
temperature dependence of the temperature difference
within the thermoelectric material region of the alumina-
substrate module is shown in Fig. 8. Red circles and blue
squares represent the calculated results for the alumina
and polyimide substrates, respectively. As the tempera-
ture of the high-temperature heat source increases, the
temperature difference generated within the module also
increases.
Moreover, the disparity in the temperature
difference between the two module types becomes more
pronounced, indicating the significant impact of radiative
heat transfer arising from the use of different substrate
materials.
These observations suggest that external heat-loss
paths, especially through the substrate in the open-
boundary Harman configuration, reduce the effective
temperature difference and lead to a lower apparent ef-
ficiency. This highlights careful boundary-condition con-
trol is essential for precise conversion-efficiency measure-
ments.
First, the temperature distribution within the module
must be accurately evaluated by considering the thermal
contact resistances[34–37] and electrical contact resis-
tances of the electrodes[25, 30, 38–42], as well as heat loss
due to thermal radiation and conduction[43, 44]. In ad-
dition, other factors, such as geometric shape effects[45–
49], and inhomogeneous temperature distribution within
the module[50], should also be taken into account.
In the present analysis, the conversion efficiency was
calculated under the assumption that the material prop-
erties were temperature independent. Incorporating for-
mulations that explicitly consider the temperature de-
pendence of Z would further improve the accuracy of the
efficiency estimation. Recent studies have reported dis-
crepancies in the theoretical evaluation of conversion effi-
ciency based on Eq. (3)[51–53]. Fundamentally, the ma-
terial figure of merit, zT = (S2/ρκ)T, is not equivalent
to the effective ZT of a thermoelectric module[54–56].
Although the AC Harman method is widely used for ma-
terial evaluation and its reliability has been extensively
discussed, the present study demonstrates that substrate
properties and radiative transfer can introduce signifi-
cant discrepancies in module-level conversion-efficiency
estimates.
V.
CONCLUSION
A comparative study was conducted to evaluate three
measurement methods for characterizing thermoelectric
modules using a commercial Bi2Te3-based modules with
different substrates. An experimental apparatus was de-
veloped to estimate the thermoelectric conversion effi-
ciency using these methods.
From a standardization
viewpoint, our findings offer several important insights.
First, the heat flow method (HFM) and the guarded
heater method (GHM) yielded close conversion efficiency
values within comparable uncertainty ranges when mea-
suring temperature gradients between the hot and cold
sides of the modules. Second, the AC Harman method
was effective for evaluating small temperature differences;
however, an analytical approach was required to ac-
curately assess conversion efficiency at larger tempera-
ture differences, which presents a challenge for module-
level evaluation under such conditions. This result in-
dicates the necessary of correcting for radiative heat
loss in the AC Harman method, which becomes a dom-
inant source of error at high temperatures, as well as
accounting for heat loss through the substrates. Impor-
tantly, our methodology using identical modules, system-
atic boundary condition control, and coupled numerical-
experimental validation, contributes to the development
of practical measurement protocols, selection guidelines
for appropriate techniques, and design principles for ref-
erence modules, thereby accelerating the progress toward
internationally thermoelectric module metrology.
