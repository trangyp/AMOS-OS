Quandle presentations of surface knots in 4-manifolds and
bridge numbers
Zhou Xiaozhou∗
Abstract
The fundamental quandle is an invariant for distinguishing surface knots, yet com-
putable presentations have traditionally been limited to surfaces embedded in the 4-
sphere. Building on the framework of banded unlink diagrams introduced by Hughes,
Kim, and Miller, we give a Wirtinger type presentation of the fundamental quandle
of surface links in arbitrary 4-manifolds. As applications, we extend the work of Sato
and Tanaka to show that for any b ≥4 and m ≥0, there exist infinitely many pair-
wise non-local surface knots with bridge number b in CP 2#mCP 2, and we distinguish
infinite families of surface knots with isomorphic knot groups, extending results of
Tanaka and Taniguchi.

Introduction
The knot group, defined as the fundamental group of the complement, has traditionally
served as a fundamental invariant in the study of surface knots. However, the knot group
is not a complete invariant, as there exist non-isotopic surface knots with isomorphic
knot groups. To capture finer topological information, Joyce and Matveev independently
introduced the algebraic structure of a quandle in the 1980s [Joy82, Mat84]. This theory
was further developed by Fenn and Rourke, who defined racks and augmented quandles,
establishing them as essential invariants for codimension-two knots [FR92].
In the case of surface knots embedded in the 4-sphere S4, the theory is well-established.
These surfaces can be effectively described by various diagrammatic methods, such as bro-
ken surface diagrams, motion pictures, and marked graph diagrams (ch-diagrams) [CKS13].
Based on these descriptions, combinatorial algorithms for deriving Wirtinger-type presen-
tations of the fundamental quandle have been extensively developed. For instance, Ka-
mada, Kim, and Lee provided a method for computing quandle invariants from marked
graph diagrams [KKL15], and Ashihara developed algorithms to calculate fundamental
biquandles from ch-diagrams [Ash12]. These computational tools enable the extraction of
algebraic data directly from the geometric descriptions of surfaces in S4.
However, for surface knots embedded in general 4-manifolds, such as the complex
projective plane CP 2, classical diagrammatic methods are often insuﬀicient or diﬀicult to
apply. To investigate surface knots in arbitrary 4-manifolds, two powerful and complemen-
tary frameworks have recently emerged: banded unlink diagrams and bridge trisections.

Hughes, Kim, and Miller introduced banded unlink diagrams, which represent surfaces
by using Kirby diagram of 4-manifold [HKM20], providing a framework to extract algebraic
invariants directly from diagrams. Complementing this, Meier and Zupan developed the
theory of bridge trisections [MZ18], extending the work of Gay and Kirby [GK16].
In this paper, we establish a diagrammatic method to compute the fundamental quan-
dle of a surface link S embedded in an arbitrary 4-manifold. For a banded unlink diagram
D of S, we define a diagrammatic quandle Q(D) by encoding the crossing information
and the band data of the diagram. We prove that this algebraically defined quandle is
isomorphic to the topological fundamental quandle Q(S), thereby providing a computable
Wirtinger type presentation for surface knots in general 4-manifolds. Our main result is
stated as follows:
Theorem 3.11. Let S be a surface link and D be a banded unlink diagram of S. Then
Q(S) and Q(D) are isomorphic.
As a consequence of Theorem 3.11, the fundamental kei of S#Cd can be described
explicitly in terms of the parity of d (see Corollary 3.19).
As the first application, we use kei colorings, namely quandle homomorphisms into
finite kei, to study the bridge number of surface knots. This extends the work of Sato and
Tanaka on surface knots in S4 [ST22] to arbitrary 4-manifolds without 1-handles for local
surface knots.
Corollary 4.4. Let X be a closed 4-manifold which admits a handle decomposition without
1-handle. For any integer b ≥4, there exist infinitely many pairwise non-isotopic local
embedded surfaces with bridge number b in X.
Furthermore, by constructing examples of surfaces which are not null-homologous, we
obtain the following result for the non-local case:
Theorem 4.5. In CP 2#mCP
2, for any integer b ≥4 and m ≥0, there exist infinitely
many pairwise homologous non-isotopic embedded surfaces with bridge number b.
As the second application, we generalize known phenomena in S4 concerning surface
knots with identical knot groups but distinct fundamental quandles [TT26] to arbitrary
4-manifolds without 1-handles for local surface knots.
Corollary 4.12. Let X be a 4-manifold without 1-handle. There exist infinitely many
triples of pairwise non-isotopic local surfaces {S1, S2, S3} embedded in X such that
(1) the knot groups G(S1), G(S2), G(S3) are isomorphic to each other,
(2) the knot quandles Q(S1), Q(S2), Q(S3) are pairwise non-isomorphic.
Furthermore, by constructing examples of surfaces which are not null-homologous, we
obtain the following result for the non-local case:
Theorem 4.13. For every integer m ≥0, there exist infinitely many triples of pairwise
homologous surfaces {S1, S2, S3} embedded in CP 2#mCP
2 which are not local, such that
(1) the knot groups G(S1), G(S2), G(S3) are isomorphic;
(2) the knot quandles Q(S1), Q(S2), Q(S3) are pairwise non-isomorphic.

Organization of the paper
In section 2, we review the basic definitions of quandle, augmented quandle, and the
fundamental quandle of a surface knot. Section 3 establishes a diagrammatic method for
computing the fundamental quandle. We recall the notion of banded unlink diagram due
to [HKM20] and define the diagrammatic quandle Q(D) associated with such a diagram.
The main result of this section, Theorem 3.11, assert that Q(D) is isomorphic to the
topological fundamental quandle Q(S). In Section 4, we apply these invariants to study
the bridge numbers of surface knots and to distinguish surface knots with isomorphic knot
groups.

Preliminary
2.1
Quandles
Let us begin by recalling the definitions of quandles and augmented quandles, following
[FR92].
Definition 2.1. A quandle is a set Q equipped with a binary operation (x, y) 7→xy which
satisfies the following three axioms:
(1) For any x in Q, we have xx = x.
(2) For any x, y in Q, there exists a unique element z in Q such that x = zy.
(3) For any x, y, z in Q, the identity (xy)z = (xz)yz holds.
For simplicity, we denote (xy)z by xyz, and denote x(yz) by xyz. A quandle Q is called a
kei if xyy = x for all x, y in Q.
Example 2.2. A dihedral quandle Rn is the set Rn = Z/nZ equipped with the operation
xy := 2y −x. A direct computation shows that xyy = x for all x, y in Rn. Hence, Rn is a
kei.
By the second axiom, for each y ∈Q, we can define a bijection fy : Q →Q by
fy(t) = ty. We denote its inverse by f−1
y . We shall write x¯y = f−1
y (x). Note that ¯y is
not an element of Q. We just consider the mapping x 7→x¯y, which is called an operator.
With this notation, the third axiom can be rewritten in the following equivalent form:
xyz = x¯zyz,
∀x, y, z ∈Q.
This leads us to regard each element of a quandle as an operator.
In expressions such as xy¯z, we refer to x as being at the primary level, and to y, ¯z
as being at the operator level. By the third axiom, any expression obtained by iterated
quandle operations can be written in the form xw, where x in Q is at the primary level
and w is a word in the free group F(Q) on Q, at the operator level. From this viewpoint,
the operation of quandle could be interpreted into an action of F(Q) on Q, written as
(x, g) 7→x · g.
Two elements x, y ∈Q are said to be operator equivalent if fx = fy, and we write
x ≡y. The corresponding equivalence classes define the operator group Op(Q) of the

quandle. More precisely, Op(Q) is defined as the quotient F(Q)/N, where F(Q) is the
free group generated by the element of Q, and N is the normal subgroup
N = {w ∈F(Q) | w ≡1}.
This operator interpretation motivates a natural variant of quandles, in which the
operator group is made explicit.
Definition 2.3. An augmented quandle is a pair (X, G) together with the following data:
• a right action of the group G on the set X, written as (x, g) 7→xg;
• the conjugation action of G on itself, given by gh = h−1gh;
• a map ∂: X →G, called the augmentation map,
such that the following augmentation identity holds:
∂(xg) = g−1∂(x)g
for all x ∈X and g ∈G.
Given an augmented quandle (X, G), the induced quandle operation on X is defined
by letting xy be the result of acting on x by ∂(y) ∈G. The augmentation identity then
implies that
∂(xy) = ∂(y)−1∂(x)∂(y),
which shows that the quandle operation is compatible with the conjugation action in G.
From now on, we shall use the notation xg to denote the action of g ∈G on x ∈X,
and, whenever an element y ∈X appears at the operator level, identify it with its image
∂(y) ∈G.
We introduce the general quandle presentations, following the definition in [FR92].
Definition 2.4. Let Q be a quandle. An equivalence relation ∼on Q is called a congruence
if, for all x, y, z, w ∈Q, the condition x ∼y, z ∼w implies xz ∼yw.
Definition 2.5. Let S and T be two sets, and denote by F(S ∪T) the free group on S ∪T.
The extended free quandle, denoted by FQ(S, T), is the augmented quandle defined on
the set S × F(S ∪T) with the acting group F(S ∪T).
Following standard notation, we denote the element (x, w) in S × F(S ∪T) by xw.
The augmentation map ∂: FQ(S, T) →F(S ∪T) is given by ∂(xw) := w−1xw. The
associated quandle operation is given by
(xw)(yz) = (xw)∂(yz) = xwz−1yz
for any x, y ∈S and w, z ∈F(S ∪T).
Definition 2.6. A general augmented quandle presentation is a quadruple [SP , SO |
RP , RO], consisting of the following four sets:
• SP : a set of primary generators;
• SO: a set of operator generators;

• RP : a set of primary relations, which are equations of the form x = y with x, y are
elements of FQ(SP , SO);
• RO: a set of operator relations, which are relations in the group F(SP ∪SO).
The augmented quandle defined by such a presentation is the quotient
Q := FQ(SP , SO)/ ∼,
where ∼is the smallest congruence on FQ(SP , SO) satisfying:
1. x ∼y whenever (x = y) is in RP ;
2. z∂(x) ∼z∂(y) (or simply zx ∼zy) for any z in FQ(SP , SO), whenever (x = y) is in
RP ;
3. zu ∼zv for any z in FQ(SP , SO), whenever (u = v) is in RO.
If SO = ∅, the presentation is called a primary quandle presentation.
Remark 2.7. When the operator group is finitely presented, the operator relations RO
often arise from a presentation of the operator group. More precisely, suppose that the
operator group has a presentation ⟨SO | RG⟩. Via the augmentation map ∂: Q →G, each
relation in RG induces an operator relation u ≡v in RO.
2.2
Fundamental augmented quandles of surface knots in 4-manifolds
The definition of fundamental quandle of a link is introduced in [FR92]. Let S be a surface
link embedded in the 4-manifold X. Let ν(S) be the tubular neighborhood of S. Define
E(S) = X \ ν(S) which is called the exterior of S. Fix a base point ∗∈E(S). We denote
the fundamental group π1(E(S)) by G(S) and call it the link group or knot group.
Let N = (D, ξ) be a pair consisting of a meridian disk D of S and a path ξ in E(S)
starting at a point on the boundary ∂D and terminating at the basepoint x0. Such a pair
is called a noose. We denote the homotopy class of N by [N] = [(D, ξ)], and let Q(S)
denote the set of all such homotopy classes.
We define an action of G(S) on Q(S). Let x ∈Q(S) be represented by the path ξ,
and let g ∈G(S) be represented by a loop γ based at ∗. We define
x · g := [ξ · γ]
where the right-hand · denotes the concatenation of paths.
The set Q(S) may equipped with a structure of an augmented quandle by using the
action of G(S).
Definition 2.8. The fundamental augmented quandle of a surface link S is an augmented
quandle (Q(S), G(S)). Let x, y in Q(S) be represented by the Nx = (Dx, ξ) and Ny =
(Dy, η), respectively. The augmentation map ∂: Q(S) →G(S) is defined by the product
of paths
∂(x) := ¯ξ mx ξ
where mx = ∂Dx is the boundary loop of the meridian disk Dx.
This augmentation
induces a quandle operation on Q(S): the operation xy is defined as the action of ∂(y) on
x, given explicitly by
xy = [(Dx, ξ ¯η my η)].

3.1
Banded unlink diagrams of surface links in 4-manifolds
We first recall the definition of the banded unlink diagram, following [HKM20].

A Wirtinger type presentation of the surface knot group
For a surface link S in a 4-manifold X, the surface knot group G(S) := π1(E(S)) is the
fundamental group of the exterior E(S) = X \ int(N(S)). To compute this group, we can
view the exterior E(S) itself as a compact 4-manifold with boundary. Its handle decompo-
sition (and thus its Kirby diagram) can be derived from the diagram of (X, S) by treating
the meridians of the surface S as new 1-handles. We first state the general proposition
for determining the fundamental group of any connected 4-manifold X represented by a
Kirby diagram K. We will then apply this result to our specific case of the surface exterior.

4.1
Trisections and surface links
We first recall the definition of trisection and bridge trisection, following [GK16] and
[MZ18]. In particular, bridge trisections provide a framework that generalizes the notion
of bridge number for classical knots to surfaces embedded in 4-manifolds.
Definition 4.1. Let X be a closed 4-manifold. A (g, k1, k2, k3)-trisection T of X is a
decomposition X = X1 ∪X2 ∪X3 where
• Xi ∼= #kiS1 × D3 for each i in {1, 2, 3};
• Xij = Xi ∩Xj = ∂Xi ∩∂Xj ∼= ♮gS1 × D2 for each i, j in {1, 2, 3} and i ̸= j;
• Σ = X1 ∩X2 ∩X3 ∼= #gS1 × S1.
Definition 4.2. Let X be a 4-manifold with a trisection T , and let S be a closed surface
embedded in X. We say S is in (g, k1, k2, k3; b, c1, c2, c3)-bridge position if
• Di = S ∩Xi is a collection of ci embedded boundary parallel disjoint disks in Xi;
• τij = S ∩Xij is a collection of b boundary parallel disjoint arcs in Xij;
• S ∩Σ consists of 2b points.

In this section, we study surface knots with isomorphic knot groups but distinct funda-

(2) the knot quandles Q(S1), Q(S2), Q(S3) are pairwise non-isomorphic.
