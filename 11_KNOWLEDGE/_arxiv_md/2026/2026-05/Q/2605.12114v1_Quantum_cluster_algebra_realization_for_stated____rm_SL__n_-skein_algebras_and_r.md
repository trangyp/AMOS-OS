QUANTUM CLUSTER ALGEBRA REALIZATION FOR STATED SLn-SKEIN
ALGEBRAS AND ROTATION-INVARIANT BASES FOR POLYGONS
PEIGEN CAO, MIN HUANG, AND ZHIHAO WANG

1.1. (Quantum) cluster algebras. Cluster algebras, introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky [FZ02],
are a class of commutative algebras equipped with a distinguished set of generators known as cluster
variables. These variables are grouped into collections called clusters, which are related by certain
birational transformations known as mutations.
In [BFZ05], Berenstein, Fomin, and Zelevinsky
introduced the notion of upper cluster algebras, defined as the intersection of the Laurent polynomial
rings associated with each cluster. From a geometric perspective, upper cluster algebras are often
more natural objects to consider. Cluster variables are rational functions in initial cluster variables


## Page 3


3
by construction. The remarkable Laurent phenomenon [FZ02] says that all the cluster variables are
actually Laurent polynomials in initial cluster variables. Moreover, the coefficients of these Laurent
polynomials are non-negative, cf. [LS15, GHKK18, Dav18, Hua23, Hua24].
The quantum (upper) cluster algebras (Definition 3.3) were introduced by Berenstein and Zelevin-
sky in [BZ05] and it is proved that the Laurent phenomenon also holds in the quantum setting.
For a (quantum) cluster algebra A and its upper cluster algebra U , the Laurent phenomenon
ensures that A ⊆U . However, in general, A ̸= U . The problem of whether a cluster algebra
coincides with its upper cluster algebra was posed by Berenstein, Fomin, and Zelevinsky and has
been studied in [BFZ05].
This question has attracted considerable attention since its inception;
see, for example, [Mul13, Mul14, cLS15, GY20, SW21, Lad13, MW24, IOS23, CGG+25, QY25] and
references therein. If A = U , then the cluster algebra A enjoys several desirable properties, including
the existence of a generic basis and a theta basis [CKQ24, Qin24b, GLFS20, GHKK18, GLS20].
The search for (quantum) cluster algebra and upper (quantum) cluster algebra structures on
important algebraic and geometric objects has drawn significant interest; see, for example, [FZ02,
Sco06, SW21, CGG+25, GLSB26, QY25] and the references therein. Cluster algebras and cluster
varieties have also been constructed in the context of Teichm¨uller theory [FG06, FST08, GSV10,
GS19], and are closely related to skein theory [Mul16, IY23, LY22, LY23, KW24, Kim25, IY25].
1.2. (Reduced) stated SLn-skein algebras and quantum trace maps. A pb surface S is ob-
tained from a compact oriented surface S by removing finitely many points, which are called punc-
tures, such that every boundary component of S is diffeomorphic to an open interval. An embedding
c : (0, 1) →S is called an ideal arc if both ¯c(0) and ¯c(1) are punctures, where ¯c: [0, 1] →S is the
‘closure’ of c. For any positive integer k, we use Pk to denote the pb surface obtained from the closed
disk by removing k punctures from the boundary.
The stated SLn–skein algebra Sω(S) of a pb surface S is the quotient of the R–module freely
generated by the set of isotopy classes of stated n–webs (Definition 2.1) in S × (−1, 1), subject to
the relations (15)–(22). For two stated n-webs α, β, their product αβ is defined by stacking α over
β. The stated SLn–skein algebra was introduced by Lˆe and Sikora [LS24] as a generalization of the
SLn–skein algebra [Sik05] to the stated setting, extending the stated SL2– and SL3–skein algebras
[Lˆe18, Hig23] to the SLn case. The reduced stated SLn–skein algebra S ω(S) [LY23] is defined as the
quotient of Sω(S) by the two–sided ideal generated by all bad arcs (see Figure 1).

1.5. Rotation-invariant bases for the reduced and localized stated SLn-skein algebras of
a polygon. One important motivation for studying the equivalence between the quantum cluster
algebra and the (reduced) stated SLn-skein algebra is the construction of “good” bases for the latter.
The existence of such good bases is crucial for the study of the (reduced) stated SLn-skein algebra,
with applications including the injectivity of the quantum trace map, the description of the image of
general webs under the Frobenius map constructed in [KLW25] (see Remark 6.19), and the structure
of the center of the algebra.
At present, a “good” basis is known only in limited cases. For SL2, the bracelet basis provides
a “good” basis of the (reduced) stated SL2-skein algebra [Qin24b, Thu14].
For SL3, a so-called
reduced non-elliptic basis was constructed in [FS22, Hig23]. This basis behaves well with respect to
the quantum trace map [DS24, Kim20], but it does not satisfy a positivity property. For n > 3,
the existence of “good” bases of the (reduced) stated SLn-skein algebra remains an important open
problem. When n = 4 and the surface is a polygon, a rotation-invariant basis was constructed in
[GPP+25]; however, as far as we know, its further structural properties are not yet understood.
As an application of Theorem 1.4, we show that the theta basis (see [GHKK18, DM21]) provides a

2.1. Stated SLn-skein algebras. Let S be a pb surface (see §1.2). Consider the 3-manifold S ×
(−1, 1), the thickened surface. For a point (x, t) ∈S × (−1, 1), the value t is called the height of
this point.
Definition 2.1 ([LS24]). An n-web α in S × (−1, 1) is a disjoint union of oriented closed curves
and a directed finite graph properly embedded into S × (−1, 1), satisfying the following requirements:


## Page 10


10
(1) α only contains 1-valent or n-valent vertices. Each n-valent vertex is a source or a sink. The
set of 1-valent vertices is denoted as ∂α, which are called endpoints of α. For any boundary
component c of S, we require that the points of ∂α ∩(c × (−1, 1)) have mutually distinct
heights.
(2) Every edge of the graph is an embedded oriented closed interval in S × (−1, 1).
(3) α is equipped with a transversal framing.
(4) The set of half-edges at each n-valent vertex is equipped with a cyclic order.
(5) ∂α is contained in ∂S × (−1, 1) and the framing at these endpoints is given by the positive
direction of (−1, 1).
We will consider n-webs up to (ambient) isotopy which are continuous deformations of n-webs in
their class. The empty n-web, denoted by ∅, is also considered as an n-web, with the convention that
∅is only isotopic to itself.
A state for α is a map s: ∂α →{1, 2, · · · , n}. A stated n-web in S × (−1, 1) is an n-web
equipped with a state.
By regarding S as S × {0}, there is a projection pr: S × (−1, 1) →S. We say the (stated) n-web
α is in vertical position if
(1) the framing at everywhere is given by the positive direction of (−1, 1),
(2) α is in general position with respect to the projection pr: S × (−1, 1) →S × {0},
(3) at every n-valent vertex, the cyclic order of half-edges as the image of pr is given by the
positive orientation of S (drawn counter-clockwise in pictures).
For every (stated) n-web α, we can isotope α to be in vertical position.
For each boundary
component c of S, the heights of ∂α ∩(c × (−1, 1)) determine a linear order on c ∩pr(α). Then a
(stated) n-web diagram of α is pr(α) equipped with the usual over/underpassing information at
each double point (called a crossing) and a linear order on c ∩pr(α) for each boundary component c
of S.
The orientation of ∂S induced by the orientation of S is called the positive orientation of ∂S.
The one opposite to the positive orientation of ∂S is called the negative orientation of ∂S. In
this paper, we always assume that the orientations of depicted surfaces point to the readers, i.e.,
the surfaces are equipped with the orientations in counterclockwise. The orientations of boundary
components of a surface also have positive orientations, i.e., these are induced from the orientation
of the surface.
A stated n-web diagram α is called negatively ordered if the linear order on α ∩c, for each
boundary component c of S, is indicated by the negative orientation of c.
