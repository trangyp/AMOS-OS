Quasiisometric embeddings between right-angled Artin groups:
flexibility
Shaked Bader, Oussama Bensaid, and Harry Petyt
Abstract
We give a complete characterisation of when the right-angled Artin group on one cycle
graph can be quasiisometrically embedded in the right-angled Artin group on another
cycle graph. In particular, we find infinitely many instances of quasiisometric embeddings
where there is no subgroup relation. This contrasts with the fact that such groups are
quasiisometrically rigid.
More generally, we construct quasiisometric embeddings between graph products of
finite or cyclic groups whose underlying graphs are cycles. As a special case, we obtain
exotic quasiisometric embeddings of the hyperbolic plane in all right-angled Artin groups
whose defining graph contains an induced cycle of length greater than four.

Introduction
A right-angled Artin group is a group whose only relations are that some of its generators
commute. At one extreme of this we have free groups and at the other we have free-abelian
groups, and one can view the class of right-angled Artin groups as an interpolation between
these two extremes.
Accordingly, right-angled Artin groups have many nice algebraic and combinatorial prop-
erties reminiscent of free and free-abelian groups. For example, they admit simple normal
forms for elements [HM95], their conjugacy classes are well understood [Ser89], and they are
linear [DJ00, HW99]. See [Cha07] for a survey.
In many ways, though, thinking of right-angled Artin groups as an interpolation between
free and free-abelian groups undersells the richness and complexity of the class. This is perhaps

most glaring when one considers their subgroup structure. As an early example, Bestvina–
Brady famously used subgroups of right-angled Artin groups to show the inconsistency of
the Eilenberg–Ganea and Whitehead conjectures [BB97]. Furthermore, the compact special
groups of Haglund–Wise can be characterised as the convex cocompact subgroups of right-
angled Artin groups [HW08]. Specialness has been an extremely influential notion, for instance
playing a key role in the resolution of the virtual Haken conjecture [Ago13, Wis21]. Aside
from subgroups, Croke–Kleiner gave proper, cocompact actions of a four-generator right-
angled Artin group on two CAT(0) spaces with non-homeomorphic visual boundaries [CK00],
and more recently Fioravanti has shown that understanding the growth of automorphisms
of right-angled Artin groups requires understanding automorphisms of all compact special
groups [Fio25].
One can ask when two right-angled Artin groups are isomorphic, and it turns out that
this has a simple answer.
A right-angled Artin group A can be encoded by a simplicial
graph Γ that has a vertex for each (standard) generator and an edge whenever two generators
commute.
Clearly if two right-angled Artin groups have the same associated graph then
they are isomorphic, and Droms proved the converse [Dro87]. Thus there is a one-to-one
correspondence between right-angled Artin groups and simplicial graphs, so we can write
A = AΓ without ambiguity.
A more subtle question is that of when one right-angled Artin group is a subgroup of
another. The most obvious subgroups of AΓ are the parabolic subgroups AΛ, where Λ is an
induced subgraph of Γ. This can be pushed further: if one doubles Γ (see Definition 2.6) then
one obtains an index-two subgroup of AΓ, so one can obtain right-angled Artin subgroups by
repeatedly doubling and taking an induced subgraph. This naturally leads to the extension
graph Γext, introduced by Kim–Koberda [KK13]. If Λ is an induced subgraph of Γext, then AΛ
is a subgroup of AΓ [KK13, Thm 1.3] - we give an account of a refinement of this in Section 3.
Kim–Koberda also studied the converse, and showed that the existence of a monomorphism
AΛ →AΓ puts restrictions on Λext and Γext. Their results are strongest in the case where
Γ has no triangles, in which case they show that it is necessary for Λ to be an induced
subgraph of Γext [KK13, Thm 1.11]. This completely classifies which right-angled Artin groups
are subgroups of AΓ when Γ is triangle-free, and their theorem can be viewed as a natural
extension of Droms’ theorem.
By studying the extension graphs of cycles, Kim–Koberda
deduce the following.
Theorem 1.1 ([KK13, Thm 1.12]). Let m, n ≥4. There is a monomorphism ACm →ACn if
and only if m = n + p(n −4) for some p ≥0.
From the perspective of large-scale geometry, the two ends of our spectrum are rather
different: free-abelian groups are absolutely quasiisometrically rigid, in the sense that every
group quasiisometric to Zn is commensurable with Zn, but any two nonabelian free groups
are quasiisometric. This suggests that both quasiisometric rigidity and flexibility should be
displayed by right-angled Artin groups.
On the flexibility side, Behrstock–Neumann showed that if Γ and Λ are finite trees of di-
ameter at least three, then AΓ and AΛ are quasiisometric [BN12]. More generally, Behrstock–
Januszkiewicz–Neumann classified which right-angled Artin groups defined by “trees of tri-
angles” are quasiisometric [BJN10].
The first examples on the rigidity side were the atomic right-angled Artin groups, due to
Bestvina–Kleiner–Sageev [BKS08]. This was vastly generalised by Huang, who showed that

if AΓ and AΛ both have finite outer automorphism group, then AΓ and AΛ are quasiisometric
if and only if they are isomorphic [Hua17]. He moreover handled various cases with infinite
outer automorphism group [Hua25], and gave a large class of right-angled Artin groups AΓ
that are absolutely quasiisometrically rigid [Hua18].
Extension graphs also play a role in Huang’s work [Hua17], which involves passing from
a quasiisometry AΓ →AΛ to an isomorphism Γext →Λext. This mirrors the situation in
mapping class groups, where quasiisometric rigidity is established by inducing an automor-
phism of the curve graph [BKMM12] and applying Ivanov’s theorem [Iva97, Kor99, Luo00].
Extension graphs can often be thought of as acting as curve graphs for right-angled Artin
groups [KK14].
In this article, we are interested in quasiisometric embeddings between right-angled Artin
groups. Passing from quasiisometries to quasiisometric embeddings is the geometric analogue
of passing from isomorphisms to monomorphisms, which was already discussed. In the setting
of mapping class groups (of surfaces with complexity at least four), Bowditch proved an
extremely strong rigidity result for quasiisometric embeddings: any quasiisometric embedding
between mapping class groups is actually a self-quasiisometry at bounded distance from a left-
multiplication map [Bow18]. There are also very strong results for homomorphisms when the
genera do not differ too much [AS12, De 25] (which is a necessary condition [ALS09]).
For the absolutely quasiisometrically rigid right-angled Artin groups considered in [Hua18],
all self-quasiisometries were completely classified in [BBP26b, Thm 9.23]. Comparing with
the mapping class group case, one may hope that quasiisometric embeddings between such
right-angled Artin groups are all close to self-quasiisometries. Failing that, one may hope for
a correspondence between quasiisometric embeddings and monomorphisms.
Our main result is the following, which shows that these hopes are misplaced, even in the
very simplest cases.
Theorem A. Let m, n ≥4. There is a quasiisometric embedding ACm →ACn if and only if
m = n or there exist p ≥1, q ≥0 such that m = n + p(n −4) + q(n −2).
By [Hua17], the groups in Theorem A are pairwise non-quasiisometric, so for m ̸= n
the quasiisometric embeddings in Theorem A are not close to quasiisometries. The theorem
also shows that the spectrum of quasiisometric embeddings is richer than the spectrum of
subgroups: comparing with Theorem 1.1 gives the following.
Corollary B. For each n > 6, there are infinitely many values of m for which there is a
quasiisometric embedding ACm →ACn but ACm is not a subgroup of ACn.
Proof. If 2q ̸≡0 modulo n −4, then m = n + p(n −4) + q(n −2) cannot be expressed
as n + p′(n −4) with p′ an integer. By Theorem A, there is a quasiisometric embedding
ACm →ACn, but Theorem 1.1 shows that ACm is not a subgroup of ACn. Since n > 6, there
are infinitely many such q.
In the case where m = n + p(n −4), the subgroup embeddings in Theorem 1.1 can
actually be taken to be quasiisometric embeddings [KK13, Cor. 1.15] (see also Proposition 3.1).
We show in Proposition 7.1 that, even in this case, there are quasiisometric embeddings
that are not at finite distance from any composition of self–quasiisometric-embeddings and a
homomorphism (that is, not quasiconjugate to a homomorphism).

As mentioned, the transition from quasiisometries to quasiisometric embeddings is analo-
gous to the transition from isomorphisms to subgroups. Since the latter situation corresponded
to passing from isomorphisms of defining graphs to a more complex situation involving ex-
tension graphs, it is reasonable to wonder whether, at least in quasiisometrically rigid cases,
one can relate quasiisometric embeddings to maps between extension graphs.
We established one direction of this for many right-angled Artin groups in [BBP26b,
Cor. 6.6]: in many situations, a quasiisometric embedding AΓ →AΛ induces a graph embed-
ding Γext →Λext, but the image need not be induced. The existence part of Theorem A goes
in the converse direction, by first building a nice embedding of extension graphs. This raises
the following natural question.
Question. Are there natural conditions on an embedding f : Γext →Λext of extension graphs
under which one can construct a quasiisometric embedding F : AΓ →AΛ?
It would seem that any reasonable construction of F from the data of f would require
it to then induce the map f, which already puts restrictions on what f can look like: see
[BBP26b]. A more ambitious question is the following.
Question. If there exists an embedding Γext →Λext, does that mean there exists a quasiiso-
metric embedding AΓ →AΛ?
We point out an important subtlety in this question: it is not true that the existence of
an embedding Γ →Λext implies that AΓ can be quasiisometrically embedded in AΛ. Indeed,
it is not difficult to see that C2n−2 is a (non-induced) subgraph of Cext
n , but Theorem A shows
that AC2n−2 cannot be quasiisometrically embedded in ACn for any n > 6. By comparison,
note that if Γ embeds as an induced subgraph of Λext, then Γext does as well.
The construction of quasiisometric embeddings that we employ in order to prove Theo-
rem A is robust enough that we can also use it to build quasiisometric embeddings between
graph products where the underlying graphs are cycles and the vertex groups are finite or
cyclic. The following is a simplified version of Corollary 8.4, which is itself a straightforward
consequence of Theorem 8.3.
Theorem C. Suppose that m, n > 6 satisfy m = n + p(n −4) + q(n −2) for some integers
p ≥1, q ≥0. Let G and H be graph products of finite or cyclic groups with underlying graphs
Cm and Cn, respectively. If every vertex group of G has smaller cardinality than every vertex
group of H, then there is a quasiisometric embedding G →H.
Given an integer N ≥2 and a simplicial graph Γ, let AΓ(N) denote the graph product with
underlying graph Γ and with all vertex groups being Z/NZ. If N = 2, then AΓ(2) is also known
as the right-angled Coxeter group WΓ. If n > 4, then the hyperbolic plane can be tiled by
regular right-angled n-gons, and a regular such tiling can be generated by the reflection group
ACn(2), which is thus a virtual surface group. Hence Theorem C yields exotic quasiisometric
embeddings of H2 as a special case.
Corollary D. If Γ contains an induced cycle of length greater than four, then there are
quasiisometric embeddings f : H2 →AΓ such that f(H2) is not contained in any finite neigh-
bourhood of any finite union of surface subgroups of AΓ.

By using a larger finite cyclic group Z/NZ as edge stabilisers, one can generate a thick

Just like how right-angled Artin groups defined on cycles are quasiisometrically rigid
[Hua17, Hua18], if n > 4 and N > 2 then the buildings In,N are quasiisometrically rigid,
in the sense that every self-quasiisometry is at finite distance from a self-isometry [BP00] (in
fact this holds for all Fuchsian buildings [Xie06]). Moreover, if n > 5 and N > 2, then any two
uniform lattices in In,N are commensurable [Hag06]. In contrast to this rigidity, Theorem C
shows that there are interesting quasiisometric embeddings between Bourdon buildings.

Remark 2.5. The group AΓ has a natural action on Γext. With our convention of using left
conjugation, this is a left action given by g(h·v) = gh·v. (Note that this differs from [KK13].)
The action is transitive on copies of Γ.
As noted above, in order for copies g1 · Γ and g2 · Γ that make up Γext to intersect, it is
sufficient that g1 and g2 differ by a suffix that is a power of a single letter. In general, these
two copies of Γ are not translates of one another by powers of a single generator, because the
action is a left action: consider g1 = s1s2 and g2 = s1s2s1, where s1 and s2 are non-commuting
standard generators of AΓ. This is the same behaviour as the edges of a Cayley graph.
With this comparison to Cayley graphs in mind, one can think of building a “path of copies
of Γ” inside Γext by considering increasingly long suffixes. This idea will be fundamental to
the construction of Section 4.
According to [KKL17], the extension graph of a graph Γ can be obtained from Γ by
repeatedly doubling.
Definition 2.6 (Double). Let Γ be a graph, and let v be a vertex of Γ. The double of Γ
along v is the graph obtained from two disjoint copies (Γ, 0) and (Γ, 1) of Γ by identifying
(w, 0) ∼(w, 1) for all w ∈Star(v).
The following theorem provides a connection between extension graphs and quasiisometric
embeddings. It is a special case of [BBP26b, Cor. 6.6], which itself relies on the results of
[BBP26a]. A leaf of a graph is a vertex of valence at most one. A graph is square-free if it
does not contain the cycle C4 as an induced subgraph.
Theorem 2.7. Let Γ and Λ be triangle-free graphs. If Γ has no leaves and Λ is square-free,
then every quasiisometric embedding AΓ →AΛ induces a combinatorial embedding Γext →

Note that the conditions of Theorem 2.7 are satisfied if Γ and Λ are both cycles of length

From extension graphs to homomorphisms
In [KK13, Thm 1.3], Kim–Koberda show that if Γ is an induced subgraph of Λext, then AΓ
can be found as a subgroup of AΛ. Moreover, the proof of [KK13, Cor 1.15] shows that AΓ can
be found as an undistorted subgroup of AΛ. They give three proofs for this, with two going
through mapping class groups. The most elementary argument, though, is a combination of
two observations:
• If Λ′ is the double of Λ along some vertex w, then AΛ′ is isomorphic to the kernel of
the map AΛ →Z/2Z sending the generator tw to 1 and all other generators to 0 [BKS08,
Ex. 1.4].
• Every finite induced subgraph of Λext is contained in an induced subgraph of Λext
obtained by a finite sequence of doubles [KK13, Lem. 3.1].
Together, these observations show that AΓ is isomorphic to a parabolic subgroup of a finite-
index subgroup of AΛ, and hence is undistorted.
Note, however, that this AΓ-subgroup of AΛ may a priori have nothing to do with the Γ-
subgraph of Λext that we began with. We address this with the following refinement of [KK13,
Thm 1.3]. Let us call the inclusion homomorphism of an undistorted subgroup non-distorting.

In this short section, we combine the results of Sections 4 and 5 to prove Theorem A, which

Theorem A. Let m, n ≥4. There is a quasiisometric embedding ACm →ACn if and only if
