Abstract: We analyze a quantum Maxwell demon operating a Szilard engine in free fall
near a black hole horizon, where quantum information, thermodynamics, and spacetime
causality intersect. The demon is modeled as a coherent two-level system, and the working
substance is a single particle in a one-dimensional chamber crossing the event horizon.
As the chamber crosses the horizon, the particle’s Hilbert space splits into accessible and
inaccessible sectors, leading to non-unitary reduced dynamics for an external demon due
to tracing over interior degrees of freedom. We construct explicit measurement, expan-
sion, and wall-removal protocols for demons located outside or inside the horizon. Our
results show that an external demon experiences degraded measurement correlations and
reduced work extraction due to horizon-induced information loss, yet still obeys local ther-
modynamics and Landauer’s principle. For an internal demon, the protocol reduces locally
to the flat-spacetime case, preserving the equivalence principle at the level of dynamics.
While the equivalence principle holds dynamically, quantum information processing pro-
vides an operational signature of the horizon through reduced accessibility and irreversible
open-system behavior, clarifying how information, causality, and thermodynamics coexist
in black-hole spacetimes.

1
Introduction
Black hole complementarity plays a central role in modern black hole physics from the per-
spective of the information paradox [1]. It asserts that a complete spacetime description
of a black hole cannot be obtained by either a stationary external observer or an infalling
observer alone; rather, both perspectives are necessary to capture the full quantum informa-
tion. However, a profound paradox challenges the principle of complementarity, suggesting
the existence of a high-energy barrier—a “firewall”—at the event horizon, which would
violate the equivalence principle of general relativity [1, 3].
To resolve this tension while preserving both complementarity and equivalence, the
ER=EPR correspondence has been proposed [2], identifying Einstein–Rosen bridges with
entangled Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen pairs. This approach also addresses the AMPS (Almheiri–Marolf–Polchin
paradox [1, 3], which demonstrates that accepting black hole complementarity together
with three natural assumptions—(I) unitary evolution of black hole evaporation, (II) the
– 1 –


## Page 3


equivalence principle for infalling observers, and (III) the validity of effective quantum field
theory for external observers—leads to a violation of the monogamy of entanglement [6, 7].
Specifically, late radiation B from an old black hole [4, 5], would need to be entan-
gled simultaneously with early radiation RB and interior modes A, which is quantum-
mechanically impossible. The AMPS proposal—that the entanglement between B and A
breaks down, producing a firewall—highlights the tension between quantum mechanics,
gravity, and thermodynamics near the horizon.
The possible breakdown of the equivalence principle in the near-horizon region, and
efforts to preserve it, lie at the core of the black hole information paradox. Studying this
region and its peculiar features is therefore of fundamental importance [8–11].
In this
paper, we investigate these issues using a quantum thermodynamic approach. We propose
a quantum Szilard heat engine (SHE) [12] assisted by a Maxwell demon (MD) that is in
free fall near the black hole horizon, with a temperature different from that of the working
substance. Our model provides a consistent, fully quantum treatment of the measurement
process without invoking the von Neumann projection postulate [13], allowing us to probe
horizon physics through quantum information accessibility.
Maxwell’s demon has long been a provocative subject because its apparent ability to
sort molecules seems to violate the second law of thermodynamics (SLOT) [14, 15]. In
1929, Szilard introduced a simplified “one-molecule heat engine” (the Szilard engine) that
concretely embodies this paradox [12].
The demon measures which side of a chamber
contains a single molecule and then uses that information to extract work kBT ln 2 from a
single heat bath, seemingly defying the SLOT. The paradox was resolved by recognizing
the thermodynamic cost of information erasure: Landauer’s principle [16] establishes that
erasing one bit of memory dissipates at least kBT ln 2 of heat into the environment. When
the demon is treated as part of the physical system, erasing its memory at the end of each
cycle restores consistency with the second law [17].
The demon has been studied in the context of quantum information science. Quantum
thermodynamics now studies quantum heat engines (QHEs) that use coherent working
substances operating away from the thermodynamic limit [18, 21]. When the demon itself is
quantum mechanical [22–24], the combined system (working substance plus demon) retains
quantum coherence, opening new possibilities for work extraction. Several attempts have
been made to quantum-generalize the SHE—for example, by quantizing the measurement
process [23], the control of motion [22], or the insertion and expansion steps [25]. However, a
fully quantum description of the entire Szilard cycle, with an intrinsically quantum demon,
remains lacking.
Hybrid quantum–classical models can lead to unphysical predictions,
underscoring the need for a consistent quantum treatment of MD-assisted thermodynamics.
Our work studies MD by embedding a fully quantum Maxwell demon within the curved
spacetime near a black hole horizon. We develop a coherent two-level demon model coupled
to a Szilard engine in free fall, enabling a first-principles analysis of how causal horizons
affect quantum measurement, information gain, and thermodynamic performance. This
approach reveals a subtle but operational distinction between internal and external ob-
servers: while the equivalence principle ensures local dynamics remain unchanged for an
infalling demon, the horizon nonetheless imposes an information-theoretic boundary that

degrades measurement fidelity and work extraction for an external demon. This provides a
concrete, protocol-based realization of black hole complementarity, where the same physical
process yields two distinct thermodynamic descriptions without contradiction.
We construct measurement, controlled-expansion, and wall-removal protocols, distin-
guishing the cases in which the demon is outside or inside the black hole. Our analysis shows
that when the demon is outside, horizon-induced information loss degrades measurement
correlations and reduces extractable work, while still respecting local thermodynamics and
Landauer’s principle. When the demon is inside, the horizon lies entirely in its causal past,
and the protocol reduces locally to the flat-spacetime case. Thus, while the equivalence
principle holds at the level of local dynamics, quantum information processing provides an
operational signature of the horizon through reduced accessibility and irreversible open-
system behavior.

2
Maxwell demon equipped with a particle in a chamber falling into the
black hole
We now examine the physics of the near-horizon region from the perspective of an observer
falling freely into it. To frame the discussion, we divide spacetime into two distinct domains:
region A, the black hole interior, and region B, its exterior. The equivalence principle
of general relativity ensures that for an observer in free fall, the crossing of the event
horizon is not marked by any local detectable signal. As a result, such an observer cannot
operationally pinpoint the horizon’s location through purely classical, local measurements
Nevertheless, a compelling conceptual question arises: is it possible for an infalling ob-
server to operationally discern the horizon, or to acquire information that meaningfully cor-
relates with the spacetime’s interior-exterior split? To explore this, we must move beyond
purely classical descriptions and instead consider quantum degrees of freedom alongside
information-theoretic principles.

Guided by this aim, we construct a quantum-mechanical model inspired by the Szilard
engine and Maxwell’s demon. Our setup consists of a single quantum particle confined
to a one-dimensional chamber, together with a Maxwell demon capable of performing
measurements and storing information. This controlled framework allows us to explicitly
analyze the interplay of measurement, information, and work extraction, while maintaining
a direct conceptual link to the physics of horizons.
In this model, the chamber is represented by an infinite square potential well of width
L, as shown schematically in Fig. 2. The particle’s position within the well serves as the
spatial degree of freedom, distinguishing regions A and B, analogous to the black hole’s
interior and exterior. The Maxwell demon is realized as a single two-level quantum system,
with ground state |g⟩and excited state |e⟩, separated by an energy gap ∆. This two-level
system serves as the demon’s memory and measurement apparatus, allowing it to record
the particle’s position and to participate dynamically in the system’s thermodynamic and
quantum evolution.
By mapping the near-horizon physics onto this quantum information–theoretic model,
we create a concrete setting where the interplay between the equivalence principle, quan-
tum measurement, and information processing becomes quantitatively accessible. In what
follows, we will analyze how the demon’s capacity to store and manipulate information
shapes the system’s effective thermodynamics, and what this reveals about the operational
meaning of horizon detection for an observer in free fall.
At the start of the protocol, the engine’s working substance—a particle confined in the
chamber—is taken to be in a state of local thermal equilibrium, described by an inverse
temperature β. This temperature is defined locally in the reference frame of the freely
falling observer, who in our setup is the Maxwell demon itself. Throughout this paper, we
therefore use the terms freely falling observer and freely falling demon interchangeably.
The equivalence principle of general relativity tells us that, for a freely falling observer,
the near-horizon region of a black hole is locally indistinguishable from flat Minkowski
spacetime.
Within a sufficiently small region, such an observer feels no local gravita-
tional field, measures no gravitational redshift, and cannot detect the presence of the
event horizon through any local experiment. As a result, all physical quantities measured
locally—including thermodynamic observables—are governed by the laws of special rela-
tivity. In particular, the inverse temperature β is precisely the same flat-spacetime inverse
temperature that defines a thermal state in Minkowski space.
Meanwhile, the Maxwell demon is taken to be in contact with its own low-temperature
bath, characterized by an inverse temperature βD.
Both the chamber and the demon
are in free fall within region B outside the event horizon before crossing (see Fig. 2).
Consequently, βD must also be understood as a locally defined inverse temperature in the
same Minkowski-like frame associated with the infalling observer.

4
Quantum-Coherent Memory and Measurement Protocol
In the standard “chamber” picture of Maxwell’s demon, the demon’s memory is usually
treated as a classical bistable degree of freedom—two perfectly distinguishable, energeti-
cally degenerate states. While adequate for describing classical information processing, this
picture inherently excludes quantum coherence, because superpositions of memory states
are neither defined nor meaningful in a purely classical framework.
To capture genuinely quantum effects, we instead model the demon’s memory as a
two-level quantum system (TLS). This choice allows the memory to maintain quantum
coherence, opening the way to study how quantum correlations influence measurement,
feedback, and thermodynamic performance. The TLS offers a minimal but fully quantum
description of the demon’s memory, providing a natural setting to explore the thermody-
namic consequences of quantum information processing.
Before turning to the horizon, it is useful to recall how the measurement protocol works
in flat (Minkowski) spacetime. Once the partition is inserted, the particle is confined either
to the left or to the right subdomain. Measurement is carried out via a controlled-NOT
(CNOT)-type interaction, where the particle’s spatial location acts as the control and the
demon’s TLS as the target. If the particle is in the left subdomain, the demon’s internal
state remains unchanged; if it is in the right subdomain, the demon’s state is flipped.

sentially. While the inserted wall still divides the chamber into left and right subdomains,
the horizon introduces a further division into accessible and inaccessible regions. In gen-
eral, one of the wall-defined subdomains will intersect the horizon, so that only part of it
remains causally connected to the demon.

At finite demon temperature, thermal fluctuations further degrade the correlation be-

A particularly unfavorable situation arises if the demon’s two-level system is made
degenerate before being cooled. The demon then remains maximally mixed and establishes
no correlations with the accessible degrees of freedom of the particle; the measurement
becomes completely ineffective, even though global evolution is still unitary.
Conversely, if the demon is first cooled to zero temperature and the degeneracy limit
is taken afterward, symmetry breaking selects a definite memory state and an effective
measurement becomes possible. This sensitivity to the order of limiting procedures reflects
the interplay between quantum measurement, thermodynamics, and causal structure.

6
The wall and the demon are inside of the black hole
We now examine the scenario depicted in Fig.3(c) and (d), where both the Maxwell de-
mon and the inserted wall lie completely inside the black hole. In this configuration, no
additional conceptual or technical subtleties emerge beyond those already encountered in
flat spacetime.
From the demon’s viewpoint, the event horizon now resides entirely in the causal past
of its worldline.
After crossing, all exterior events that occurred prior to the crossing
are either already inside its past light cone or forever causally disconnected. Crucially,
the horizon acts as a one-way causal boundary: signals emitted from the exterior before
crossing can still reach the demon, but the demon cannot send any signal back to affect
the exterior future. This causal asymmetry, however, has no bearing on the local dynamics
experienced by the demon.
Because the demon is in free fall, the equivalence principle guarantees that its immedi-
ate neighbourhood is locally indistinguishable from Minkowski spacetime. There is no lo-
cally detectable gravitational field, no singularity, and no operational signature of the hori-
zon crossing itself. Consequently, every physical quantity relevant to the Maxwell-demon
protocol—energies, temperatures, entropies, work, and information—can be defined locally
in the demon’s proper frame, exactly as in flat spacetime.
All observables are defined on spacelike hypersurfaces orthogonal to the demon’s
four-velocity. These hypersurfaces furnish a natural local notion of simultaneity and ensure
that the particle, the wall, and the demon remain in mutual causal contact throughout the
protocol. Unlike the situation in which the demon stays outside the black hole, no part of
the chamber becomes causally inaccessible once the demon has crossed the horizon.
– 15 –


## Page 17


Hence, the particle’s Hilbert space does not split into accessible and inaccessible sectors.
There is no need to trace over hidden degrees of freedom, and the reduced dynamics of
the demon-particle system remain unitary. Measurement, feedback, controlled expansion,
and wall removal can all be implemented using the same quantum operations as in flat
spacetime, with proper lengths and energies replacing global coordinate quantities.
Thus, for an infalling demon located inside the black hole, the complete Szilard–Maxwell-demon
cycle proceeds locally exactly as it would in Minkowski spacetime. The presence of the hori-
zon leaves no operational imprint on the demon’s thermodynamic or information-processing
capabilities. This explicitly demonstrates that the equivalence principle holds at the level
of local quantum thermodynamics, even though the global causal structure differs radically
from that of flat spacetime.
To underscore the contrast, we compare this situation with the case in which the demon
and wall remain outside the black hole. When the demon is outside, the horizon splits the
chamber into accessible and inaccessible parts, forcing a factorization of the Hilbert space,
a partial trace over interior modes, and leading to non-unitary reduced dynamics. When
both the demon and the wall are inside, none of these features occur. This conclusion is
not obvious unless one carefully respects the causal structure: although the horizon lies
in the demon’s past, it remains globally relevant. Once inside, the demon encounters the
horizon only as a past boundary—not as a dynamical object with which it can interact.
The demon’s inability to send signals to the exterior does not impede any local protocol,
because thermodynamics requires only local control and memory, not future causal access.
Signals emitted earlier from the exterior are irrelevant; they are already part of the
demon’s past light cone and introduce no inaccessible degrees of freedom, so no tracing is
required. The difficulty arises only when part of the system lies outside the demon’s causal
past—a situation that never occurs here.
Because spacelike hypersurfaces are orthogonal to the four-velocity, all quantities are
defined on such hypersurfaces in the demon’s frame.
This allows a local definition of
simultaneity (via Fermi normal coordinates) without ever invoking Schwarzschild time.
The Hamiltonian is well-defined, and the partition function, entropy, and work are local
scalars—precisely as required by relativistic thermodynamics.
Note that unitarity is restored inside the horizon. This follows from the absence of any
hidden subsystem or “lost” Hilbert space from the interior demon’s perspective. In other
words, there is no effective decoherence of the Lindblad type. Consequently, the protocol
reduces to the familiar unitary evolution ρ(t) = U(t)ρ(0)U†(t), just as in flat spacetime. It
is crucial to recognize that the horizon does not violate the equivalence principle; rather,
it is the accessibility of information that distinguishes the two observers.
An external
demon requires an open-quantum-system description, while an internal demon can treat
the system as closed. We have the same underlying spacetime, but the operational physics
differs according to the observer’s causal relation to the horizon.

7
Relation to black hole information and complementarity
This work engages directly with the long-standing discussion surrounding black hole com-
plementarity, the AMPS firewall paradox, and the ER=EPR conjecture. Those debates are
usually conducted in the language of quantum field theory and holography; here we take a
different approach. By constructing a clean, quantum-thermodynamic toy model, we show
that the core features of complementarity need not remain abstract principles—they can
emerge as concrete, operational constraints on what an observer can actually do.
We build on familiar results—for instance, that horizons force causally restricted ob-
servers into mixed states, or that Unruh-DeWitt detectors model local responses to curva-
ture—but we shift the focus away from field-theoretic entanglement and toward the oper-
ational limits of a tangible thermodynamic protocol. Concretely, we place a fully quantum
Maxwell demon next to a black hole horizon and ask what it can and cannot achieve. The
answer comes in the form of explicit, quantitative bounds on its measurement fidelity, the
mutual information it acquires, and the work it can extract. By partitioning the Hilbert
space according to causal contact and linking the resulting noise to the spatial support of
the wavefunction, we obtain an operational picture in which spacetime geometry directly
regulates thermodynamic efficiency, enforces a form of complementarity, and remains per-
fectly consistent with the equivalence principle—all without sacrificing global unitarity.
ER=EPR correspondence: The ER=EPR conjecture [2] proposes that entangled states
are connected by Einstein-Rosen bridges, giving quantum correlations a geometric face.
Our model does not rely on wormhole geometries, but it offers a clean operational analog:
the splitting of the Hilbert space into Hacc and Hlost.
The lost subspace provides an
operational analogy, not a literal ER bridge construction, in the global description, yet it
is completely inaccessible to the external demon. This provides a concrete, calculationally
simple illustration of how entanglement and causal structure together constrain what can
be done with quantum information in a curved background.
Black hole complementarity: What we have here is complementarity made operational.
The same physical event—the chamber crossing the horizon—admits two equally valid but
mutually incompatible effective descriptions. For the external demon, the dynamics are
open and noisy; for the infalling one they are unitary. There is no contradiction, because
the two observers have access to different sets of degrees of freedom. Complementarity,
in this picture, is not a mysterious duality but a direct consequence of causal information
flow. The reduced dynamics we have derived thus provide an information-theoretic handle
on a debate that often remains purely conceptual.
Operational form of entanglement monogamy: Globally, the evolution is unitary, and
the von Neumann entropy of the closed total system is conserved. Locally, however, the
external demon is forced to discard everything that happens behind the horizon. (For some
explicit calculation about the role of the horizon in the density matrix, refer to Appendix
A). One can, for example, model the price of the mentioned discard as a mixed demon

Szilard engine, and it fits perfectly with the requirement of global unitarity.
8
Conclusion and Outlook
We have presented a fully quantum model of a Maxwell demon operating a Szilard heat
engine in the near-horizon region of a black hole, providing a controlled setting in which
quantum information, thermodynamics, and spacetime causality are explicitly intertwined.
By treating the demon as a coherent two-level system and the working substance as a single
particle in a freely falling chamber that is crossing the event horizon, we have derived the
operational consequences of horizon crossing for quantum measurement, feedback, and
work extraction.
Our analysis reveals a sharp distinction between the experiences of an external demon
and an infalling demon. For an external demon, the horizon partitions the particle’s Hilbert
space into accessible and inaccessible sectors, necessitating a partial trace over interior
degrees of freedom. This leads to non-unitary reduced dynamics, described by a Lindblad-
type master equation, which reduces the extractable work in the feedback cycle.
– 18 –


## Page 20


In contrast, for an infalling demon located inside the horizon, no such partitioning
occurs. The demon retains full causal access to the entire chamber, and the reduced dy-
namics remains unitary, indistinguishable from the flat-spacetime Szilard cycle. This result
upholds the equivalence principle at the level of local quantum dynamics, demonstrating
that no operational signature of the horizon exists for a freely falling observer performing
local thermodynamic operations.
The model provides a concrete, protocol-based realization of black hole complementar-
ity. The same physical process—chamber crossing the horizon—yields two distinct effective
descriptions: an open-system, information-limited dynamics for the external demon, and
a closed-system, unitary dynamics for the internal demon. This observer-dependent ther-
modynamics illustrates how these tensions (complementarity, monogamy of entanglement,
...) can be consistently represented in an operational toy model. Furthermore, it opera-
tionalizes the ER=EPR intuition by showing that the inaccessible interior modes act as an
“entangled partner” that is geometrically disconnected from the external observer, thereby
limiting accessible correlations and thermodynamic performance.
From a quantum thermodynamic perspective, our work extends Maxwell demon proto-
cols to curved spacetime with causal horizons, establishing that thermodynamic operations
remain well-defined locally but are constrained by information accessibility. The horizon-
induced depolarizing channel, with noise strength 1 −η, directly links spacetime geometry
to information channel capacity and work extraction limits. This offers a new lens through
which to study quantum engines in gravitational settings and provides a platform for
tabletop simulations using quantum-optical or superconducting circuit analogs of horizon
physics.
Future work could extend this framework to more realistic field-theoretic settings,
incorporate back-reaction effects, or explore connections to holographic complexity and
tensor network models. Experimentally, the reduced dynamics derived here may be tested
in analog gravity systems where effective horizons are engineered. By bridging quantum in-
formation, thermodynamics, and general relativity, this model advances our understanding
of how quantum information shapes—and is shaped by—the causal structure of spacetime,
offering a step toward a consistent operational physics of black holes.
