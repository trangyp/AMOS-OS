Quantum Desynchronization of Limit Cycles
Hans Christiansen and Jens Paaske
Center for Quantum Devices, Niels Bohr Institute,
University of Copenhagen, 2100 Copenhagen, Denmark
It is well known from classical physics that weakly coupled self-sustained oscillators may spon-
taneously lock their phases.
Just like classical synchronization is known to break down due to
noise induced phase slips, we show here how the synchronization of continuous variable quantum
systems breaks down by proliferation of quantum phase slips. Within a Keldysh path integral for-
mulation of limit cycles, we analyze the phase dynamics and show how, in spite of strong phase
correlations, quantum phase slips degrade the actual phase locking. This approach also allows us to
address non-Markovian effects on the synchronization of limit cycles, which we illustrate explicitly
for superconducting resonators coupled via a voltage biased double quantum dot.
Introduction.
Driven-dissipative nonlinear oscillators
can sustain stable oscillations whose amplitude and fre-
quency are set by the dynamics, rather than by initial
conditions. These are known as limit-cycle oscillators [1].
When two limit cycle oscillators interact and their respec-
tive frequencies are sufficiently close, they tend to syn-
chronize their motion, meaning that their relative phase
locks at a constant value. Inside an Arnold tongue where
their frequency detuning is smaller than the interaction
strength, this phase locking is stable under weak pertur-
bations. Numerous examples of synchronization of limit
cycles can be found in nature, and provides, for example,
the all-important stability of numerous biological func-
tions under noisy conditions [2–4].
Noise smoothens out the Arnold tongue and the desyn-
chronization transition from locked to free-running rel-
ative phase turns into a crossover rather than a sharp
transition. Physically, this happens because the relative
phase starts slipping by sudden full revolutions of 2π due
to the noise. These phase slips cause diffusion of the rel-
ative phase which inhibits synchronization in the strict
sense and renders the degree of synchronization a ques-
tion of timescales [1, 5, 6].
Improving the quality of synchronization by lowering
the thermal fluctuations, one eventually reaches the lower
bound set by the Heisenberg uncertainty principle. Re-
cent theoretical and experimental works have addressed
limit-cycle oscillators and their potential synchronization
in the presence of quantum fluctuations using a number
of different observables [7–11]. Here we extend the anal-
ysis of synchronization to include the diffusive phase-slip
dynamics induced by the quantum fluctuations, using a
coherent state Keldysh path integral. To the best of our
knowledge, with the exception of Ref. 12, this key feature
has so far not been considered in the context of quantum
synchronization of limit cycles.
To illustrate how quantum fluctuations degrade phase
locking, we first revisit the quantum Stuart-Landau equa-
tion [13], which has been defined in terms of a Lind-
blad master equation (LME) for a harmonic oscillator
with jump operators describing single-photon gain and
two-photon loss [9, 14]. Here we demonstrate how quan-
tum phase slips degrade the synchronization, even in the
presence of pronounced phase correlations. Second, we
analyze a microscopic model for a non-Markovian reso-
nant gain medium responsible for the synchronization of
two superconducting microwave resonators. Here we find
that the synchronization frequency adjusts to increase
the effective gain rate, essentially entraining to the reso-
nance frequency of the environment.

Summary and Outlook. In this letter, we have analyzed
the synchronization of continuous variable limit-cycle os-
cillators in the presence of quantum fluctuations.
We
show that the proliferation of phase slips degrades the
synchronization to an imperfect (mod-2π) synchroniza-
tion characterized by epochs of phase locking interrupted
by sudden phase slips. The duration of these epochs, set
by the inverse of the phase-difference diffusion constant,
provides a measure for the quality of synchronization.
This measure allows to distinguish genuine phase-locking
from the more trivial dissipative mode coupling, which
does not require a limit cycle. To illustrate this quan-
tum desynchronization mechanism, we have analyzed the
diffusive phase dynamics for the paradigmatic model of
two coupled Stuart-Landau oscillators, as well as for a
full microscopic and non-Markovian model of two super-
conducting microwave resonators coupled via a voltage-
biased DQD. In both cases we find that the quantum
fluctuations reduce the quality of synchronization, while
frequency, and phase correlations remain.
