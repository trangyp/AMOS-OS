Quantum Troesch complexes
Th´eo Deturck
Abstract
We study the eﬀect of quantum Frobenius twist on Ext-groups in the category of quan-
tum polynomial, and prove that the existence of type of complexes, called quantum Troesch
complexes, enables the construction of a spectral sequence computing the Ext groups of
twisted functors from the knowledge of Ext-groups of the original functors. We then con-
struct quantum Troesch complexes in the special case where the parameters of the quantum
deformation is a root of unity of order 3.
1
Introduction
Let K be a ﬁeld, and q ∈K× be a non-zero scalar. In [5], Friedlander and Suslin introduced the
category of strict polynomial functors, a category particularly useful for studying the represen-
tation theory of GLn(K), and of Schur algebras. This category was deformed in [6] to obtain
the category of quantum polynomial functors. The latter is related to q-Schur algebras Sq(n; d)
in the same way that strict polynomial functors are related to Schur algebras. More speciﬁcally,
a quantum polynomial functor F of degree d can be seen as a family F(n) of Sq(n; d)-modules
with some compatibilities between them. The evaluation F 7→F(n) then deﬁnes an equivalence
of category between the category of quantum polynomial functors of degree d and the category
of ﬁnite-dimensional Sq(n; d)-modules, provided that n ≥d. In particular, it gives isomorphisms

When q is a root of unity of odd order, a quantum polynomial functor F (1)q can be constructed
from a strict polynomial functor F, using the quantum Frobenius twist. The representations of
the q-Schur algebra obtained in this way play an important role in the representation theory of
quantum Schur algebras. For example, each irreducible representation of the q-Schur algebra
decomposes as a tensor product of the form M ⊗N (1)q, where M belongs to a ﬁnite family of
representations of the q-Schur algebra, and N is a representation of the classical Schur algebra.

greater than the degrees of F and G. The purpose of this article is to present some advances

Troesch complexes when q3 = 1. This is achevied in theorem 3.15. In particular, this shows
that theorem 1.2 holds in this case. Actually, we prove a bit more than what is required in
the deﬁnition of quantum Troesch complex, namely we endow the quantum Troesch complexes
with a structure of graded diﬀerential Pq-algebra, and prove that it is an exponential functor
(see deﬁnition 3.4). This enables us to simplify the computation of the homology of the Troesch
complexes. The main diﬃculty is constructing an appropriate diﬀerential and product. Whereas
the diﬀerential itself is quite natural to consider, the product needed is an ad-hoc deformation
of a more natural product. More precisely, the diﬀerential is a linear combination of the form
δ1+λδ2, with δ1 and δ2 being two degree-1 maps which are natural to consider, and λ (which is not
actually a constant) may not be the most natural choice, but it is not diﬃcult to determine. We
observe that when qℓ= 1, the most natural product on quantum Troesch complexes comes from
identifying this complex with the algebra of quantum ℓ× n matrices, and taking the associated
product. But contrarily to the ad-hoc product we discovered when ℓ= 3, this most natural
product does not seem to be compatible with the diﬀerential of deﬁnition 3.7, nor with any
diﬀerentials the author has considered. This leaves the following problem open for the moment.
Problem 1.3. Assume that qℓ= 1, for ℓ> 3. Does there exist a Troesch complex in the sense
of deﬁnition 2.19?
