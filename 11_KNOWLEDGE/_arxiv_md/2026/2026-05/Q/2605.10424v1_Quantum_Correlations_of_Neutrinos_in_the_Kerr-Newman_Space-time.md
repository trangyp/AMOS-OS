Abstract
Thanks to feeble interactions, neutrinos show special advantages
in the field of quantum information (QM). The properties of quan-
tum correlations (QCs) are fundamental for neutrino-based QM. In
this paper, we investigate the influence of the Kerr–Newman metric
on QCs by varying the metric parameters, namely the mass M, an-
gular momentum per unit mass a, and charge Q. Both radial and
non-radial neutrino propagation are considered under the weak-field
approximation. The results show that, for inward propagation in the
Kerr–Newman metric, the oscillation probabilities and QCs differ sig-
nificantly from those obtained in the Schwarzschild metric. In the case
of radial outward propagation, the angular momentum a increases
the oscillation period of the neutrino survival probability Pee, entan-
glement, and nonlocality, whereas the charge Q decreases the corre-
sponding periods. For non-radial propagation, the modulation effects
of M and a on the oscillation patterns of both probabilities and QCs
become more pronounced. As M increases, the oscillation probability
remains within a higher-value range, whereas tripartite entanglement
exhibits the opposite trend. Furthermore, our results reveal that, de-
spite differences in their variation ranges, entanglement and coherence
exhibit highly consistent oscillation behaviors in both radial and non-
radial propagation cases. These findings provide broader quantitative
support for the potential use of neutrinos as quantum information re-
sources.

Introduction
Neutrinos, as the most elusive particles in the standard model, can oscillate
between flavor states during propagation due to their non-zero masses [1–8].
Thanks to extremely weak interactions in ordinary matter, the decoherence
of neutrino state is much feebler than those of electrons and photons widely
used in quantum information processing. Thus, neutrinos may be employed
as a resource for quantum information. In this context, a series of studies
have been conducted on the quantum correlations (QCs) of neutrinos, such
as entanglement, coherence, and discord [9–20]. Notably, the quantification
of QCs for neutrinos can usually be expressed as functions of oscillation
probabilities, which lay experimental bases for the theoretical researches.
As is known, there is more than one approach to quantifying QCs. For in-
stance, coherence can be quantified by the l1-norm as a quantitative measure[21,
22], and by the Leggett-Garg inequality as a qualitative criterion[16]. For bi-
partite entanglement, it can be quantified by concurrence[23], negativity[24],
and entanglement of formation[25]. Hence, one can characterize the proper-
ties of a correlation from different perspectives. In light of these definitions,
investigations on the QCs of neutrinos consider special oscillation modes such
as spin oscillations [26], collective oscillations[27] and effects of environments
like matter [28], and gravitation[29].
As for the effects of gravity on neutrino flavor oscillations, they are shown
by modification of the propagation phase of mass eigenstates[30–36]. A co-
variant method to express the phase of neutrino in a general curved space-
time was obtained by Fornengo and et al.[30] The WKB approximation to
the phases in the case of radial propagation was figured out by Visinelli
[35]. Regarding the case of non-radial propagation, the most intriguing phe-
nomenon is the gravitational lensing effect. It describes neutrino oscillation
including not only the phase differences between mass eigenstates but also
those between different paths of each neutrino [37–42].
For simplicity, recent studies on gravity-impacted QCS of neutrinos mainly
focus on the Schwarzschild metric and radial propagations. In the Schwarzschild
metric, Wang and and et al. found that[29], the entanglement of the neutri-
nos is suppressed under special mass parameters, and the genuine tripartite
non-locality for neutrinos always exists since the Svetlichny parameter used
to quantify non-locality is always greater than 4. Thus, neutrinos under a
classical gravitational field can still show non-trivial properties on QCs.
In this work, we investigate the properties of QCs in a more realistic

Conclusions
In this paper, we investigate the properties of three-flavor neutrino oscillation
probabilities and QCs, including entanglement, nonlocality, and coherence,
in different space-time metrics. In particular, we examine how variations in
the Kerr–Newman metric parameters affect these quantities. Two propa-
gation scenarios are considered: radial propagation, including both inward
and outward directions, and non-radial propagation. For the non-radial case,
we mainly focus on the gravitational lensing effect. In both scenarios, the
weak-field approximation is adopted.
For radial propagation, the oscillation probability and QCs exhibit dis-
tinct behaviors depending on the propagation direction. Compared with out-
ward propagation, inward propagation in the Kerr–Newman metric leads to
oscillation probabilities and QCs that differ significantly from those obtained
in the Schwarzschild metric. Moreover, the oscillation profiles are more sen-
sitive to parameter variations in the inward radial case. Regarding the effects
of individual parameters, we find that, for radial outward propagation, the
oscillation period of Pee increases with the angular momentum per unit mass
a, whereas it decreases with increasing electric charge Q. Meanwhile, the
mass M induces a clear phase shift in the oscillation. Similar features are
also observed in the three QCs, indicating their intrinsic dependence on the
oscillation wavelength. Notably, for both radial propagation directions, the
parameter a has a pronounced influence on the oscillation probabilities and
QCs. For example, as a increases, the oscillation patterns are completely
distorted at a = 1 × 1011 km for outward radial propagation. In the inward
radial case, the oscillation patterns corresponding to a = 1 × 105 km and
a = 1 × 107 km are nearly identical. These results provide a theoretical ba-
sis for further understanding how the angular momentum per unit mass of
massive celestial bodies affects neutrino oscillations and their QCs.
For the gravitational lensing effect in non-radial propagation, the metric
parameters also exert a significant influence on the oscillation probabilities
and QCs. In particular, the modulation effects of M and a on the oscilla-
tion patterns become more pronounced. The results show that increasing M
causes the oscillation probability to vary within a higher-value range, whereas
tripartite entanglement exhibits the opposite trend. In addition, the oscilla-
tion range of monogamy at M = 1 × 105 m is substantially larger than those
obtained for the other two smaller values. Regarding the angular momentum
parameter a, its modulation effect on the oscillation patterns of both proba-

## Page 23


bilities and QCs is most evident at a = 1 × 107 m, compared with the other
two smaller values. Specifically, entanglement oscillates within a higher-value
range at a = 1×107 m, whereas monogamy of nonlocality displays the oppo-
site behavior. These findings provide useful references for investigating the
influence of gravitational lensing on neutrino QCs.
Overall, the space-time geometry described by the Kerr–Newman metric
differs from that of static and spherically symmetric metric. The present
analysis provides a more comprehensive reference for understanding the in-
fluence of compact celestial objects on neutrino oscillation probabilities and
QCs. In addition, by extending the investigation to non-radial neutrino prop-
agation under the light-ray approximation, this study offers a new perspective
for exploring other QCs of neutrinos.
