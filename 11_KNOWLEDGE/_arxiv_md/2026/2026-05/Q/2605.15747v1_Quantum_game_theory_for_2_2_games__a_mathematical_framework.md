Quantum game theory for 2×2 games: a mathematical framework
Gloria Ferraris(1) and Veronica Umanit`a(2)
May 18, 2026
Abstract
We develop a rigorous mathematical framework for quantum game theory applied to static 2×2
games, extending classical concepts to the quantum setting where players may employ arbitrary
unitary operations (pure strategies) or probability measures over the continuous group SU(2) (mixed
strategies). The Eisert-Wilkens-Lewenstein protocol is introduced as the standard implementation
of quantum 2 × 2 games. We prove the existence of Nash equilibria for continuous quantum mixed
strategies via a ﬁxed-point argument, generalising the classical Nash theorem to the quantum case.
Keywords: Quantum Games, Nash equilibria.

Introduction
Game theory is a fundamental mathematical tool for studying strategic interactions among rational
agents, with applications ranging from economics to evolutionary biology, and from political science
to computer science. First formalized by von Neumann and Morgenstern [9] and subsequently ex-
panded by the concept of the Nash equilibrium [8], classical game theory provides a solid framework
for analyzing competitive and cooperative situations in which an agent’s decisions inﬂuence and are
inﬂuenced by those of others.
However, since the fundamental laws governing our world are quantum in nature, one naturally
wonders what would happen if players had access to quantum resources. Classical theory, in fact,
has certain inherent limitations that have led, since the late 1990s, to the development of a theory of
quantum games, in which players’ strategies are represented by unitary transformations on quantum
states and the correlations between them can be enhanced by two fundamental resources: superposition
and entanglement (see e.g.the seminal works [7], [2], [3],[4], [6]).
The aim of this paper is to provide a uniﬁed and rigorous treatment of quantum game theory for
static two-player 2×2 games, with a particular focus on mathematical formalization and the search for
strategic equilibria. We introduce the notion of continuous mixed strategies and some recent analytical
tools, such as the representation of payoﬀs in quadratic form (see [5]), for the analysis of equilibria.
An original contribution of this work is the proof of the existence of Nash equilibria in continuous
quantum mixed-strategy games for 2 × 2 games (Theorems 2 and 3).
While existence results for
discrete mixed strategies were already known in the literature, the continuous case - where players
can choose according to arbitrary probability measures on the entire group SU(2) - requires more
sophisticated tools and provides a natural generalization of Nash’s theorem to the quantum case.
The structure of the paper is as follows. Section 2 outlines the formalism of classical 2 × 2 game
theory and introduces the concept of a Nash equilibrium. Section 3 shows how this classical framework
admits a natural quantum generalization, in which the extension of the strategy space from discrete
sets to the unitary group SU(2). We then deﬁne quantum mixed strategies as probability measures on
SU(2) and introduce the corresponding notion of quantum equilibrium. Section 4 provides an existence
theorem for Nash equilibria in continuous quantum mixed strategies, generalising the classical result.

Classical framework for 2 × 2 games
Game theory is the branch of mathematics that studies strategic interactions between rational agents,
called players.
The possible actions of players are called moves, and a prescription that speciﬁes
the particular move to be made in all possible game situations is a strategy. A pure strategy is a
deterministic choice of a single action. In particular, in a static game, i.e., where each player makes a
single, simultaneous decision without knowing others’ choices, a pure strategy coincides with a single
move.

pure strategies (2 × 2 game). Let S = {s1, s2} be the strategy set of player A and T = {t1, t2} that of

The most widely used framework for implementing quantum versions of simultaneous 2 × 2 games is
