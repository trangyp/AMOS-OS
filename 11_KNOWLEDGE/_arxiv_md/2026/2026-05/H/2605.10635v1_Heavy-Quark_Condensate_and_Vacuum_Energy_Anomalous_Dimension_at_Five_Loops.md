Introduction
The quark condensate ⟨¯qq⟩plays a central role in the dynamics of the strong interactions.
Its nonvanishing value in the limit of massless quarks, mq →0, signals the spontaneous
breaking of chiral symmetry. For physical up- and down-quark masses mu and md, the
pion can be identified as the associated pseudo Goldstone boson. As shown by Gell-Mann,
Oakes, and Renner [1], the mass Mπ and decay constant Fπ of the pion are closely related
to the up-quark condensate ⟨¯uu⟩via

In practice, the value of the quark condensate is often required in applications of the
operator product expansion (OPE) [2]. Indeed, it is the leading non-trivial local oper-
ator contributing to the short-distance expansion of a gauge-invariant operator product

condensate as an effective long-distance quark mass [3].
In many cases, the OPE is used at low energies, where only the non-perturbative light-
quark condensates are relevant. Conversely, at sufficiently high energies non-perturbative
effects become negligible and only perturbative heavy-quark condensates need to be con-
sidered. In this scenario, the OPE is equivalent to the asymptotic small-mass expansion

One approach for bridging the gap between the low-energy and high-energy regimes

method has been used to extrapolate from perturbative heavy-quark condensates in the
weakly-coupled regime to strongly coupled light-quark condensates [12, 13].

known connection to the vacuum anomalous dimension γ0 [14]. Assuming nh quark flavours

An RGOPT determination based on the four-loop heavy-quark condensate [15] and the five-

results [12], which could be rectified by incorporating partial higher-order contributions [13].
A completion of this analysis requires the five-loop condensate and the six-loop anomalous
dimension.
In this note, we present the first five-loop QCD result including non-vanishing quark
masses. We compute the perturbative heavy-quark condensate at five loops and derive
the vacuum anomalous dimension at the same order. The computation of the anomalous
dimension constitutes a completely independent cross check of the results by Baikov and
Chetyrkin [16], which unlike other five-loop anomalous dimensions in QCD [17–24] had not
been confirmed by a second calculation so far.

Calculational Strategy
To compute the quark condensate at five loops, we first generate the 3451 contributing
massive vacuum diagrams with the help of QGRAF [25]. In order to verify the gauge inde-
pendence of our results we insert the Feynman rules in a general covariant gauge, expanding
around Feynman gauge to linear order in the gauge parameter.
We simplify the resulting expressions using FORM [26] code. In particular, we adopt
the color [27] package to determine colour factors. The resulting single-mass-scale scalar
vacuum integrals can be mapped onto 34 integral families, which correspond to different
mass colourings of the four five-loop sector templates depicted in figure 1 as well as their
subsectors that form when contracting propagator lines to a point.
The scalar integrals can be reduced to a set of 142 master integrals by exploiting
integration-by-parts relations [28].2 To this end, we employ crusher [30], a custom im-
plementation of Laporta’s algorithm [31], together with tinbox [32] for reduction over

Conclusions
We have presented the five-loop result for the heavy-quark condensate. This is the first
time that non-vanishing quark masses have been incorporated in a QCD calculation at this
order. We have further derived the five-loop vacuum anomalous dimension, independently
confirming a result by Baikov and Chetyrkin [16].
Applications of our result include asymptotic small-mass expansions and RGOPT de-
terminations [12, 13] of the light-quark condensate. Furthermore, the present work is an
important milestone on the path towards future five-loop QCD calculations involving mas-
sive vacuum diagrams, in particular decoupling of heavy quarks, quark-mass determinations
via sum rules, and Higgs boson production and decay.
