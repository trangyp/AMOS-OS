HECKE MONOIDS, THEIR HOMOMORPHISMS AND PARABOLICITY
ARKADY BERENSTEIN, JACOB GREENSTEIN AND JIAN-RONG LI
Abstract. We study homomorphisms of Hecke monoids, notably parabolic homomor-
phisms, which map parabolic elements to parabolic elements, and injective ones. The impor-
tance of the first class stems from the fact that parabolic elements form a rather mysterious
submonoid of the Hecke monoid, and we found a plethora of parabolic homomorphisms.
Concerning injective ones, as a first step towards their classification, we classified all locally
injective connected homomorphisms between Hecke monoids of classical types and expect
all of them to be injective. As a surprising byproduct of our study of parabolic and injective
homomorphisms we described, to some extent, all homomorphisms between Hecke monoids.

1. Introduction and main results
The aim of the present work is to systematically study homomorphisms of Hecke monoids
and thus to develop their comprehensive theory. Homomorphisms, especially injective ones,
are instrumental in representation theory of these monoids and Coxeter groups as well as
corresponding unipotent algebraic groups.
We start with parabolic homomorphisms of Hecke monoids, also referred to as 0-Hecke
monoids, Demazure monoids and even Coxeter monoids in the literature. Our interest in
them was motivated by the following surprising observation. Let M be a Coxeter matrix
over a finite set I and let W(M) = ⟨si : i ∈I⟩(respectively, (W(M), ⋆)) be the corre-
sponding Coxeter group (respectively, the Hecke monoid generated by simple idempotents
also denoted by si, i ∈I, see §§2.3, 2.5 for details). Given J ⊂I, let WJ(M) = ⟨sj : j ∈J⟩
be the parabolic subgroup of W(M) corresponding to J (§2.4). It is well-known that the
assignments sj 7→sj, j ∈J and sj 7→1, j ∈I \ J, define a homomorphism of monoids
pJ : (W(M), ⋆) →(WJ(M), ⋆), which we refer to as a parabolic projection. It should be
noted that such homomorphisms of Coxeter groups are very rare (see Remark 3.17).
We say that K ⊂I is of finite type (in the literature they are also called spherical)
if WK(M) is finite. For any K ⊂I of finite type, there is a distinguished family of elements
wJ;K ∈WK(M), J ⊂K called parabolic (cf. §2.8). In particular, wK
◦:= w∅;K is the longest
element in WK(M). Our first main result is the following
Theorem 1.1. Each parabolic projection maps parabolic elements to parabolic elements.
Moreover, all light homomorphisms of Hecke monoids, i.e. those homomorphisms sending
generators to generators or 1, also map all parabolic elements to parabolic elements.
This theorem is highly non-trivial and is proven in §4.2. The argument relies very heavily
on the structure of the rather mysterious monoid PK(M) = {wJ;K : J ⊂K}.
These
monoids for Weyl groups appeared for the first time in the joint work of the first author
with David Kazhdan ([1]).
In particular, it was shown, by a geometric argument, that
PK(M) is an abelian submonoid of the Hecke monoid (WK(M), ⋆). For non-crystallographic
finite Coxeter groups, we provide a different proof for non-crystallographic ones here for the
reader’s convenience (Proposition 3.29).
This result was a byproduct of geometric considerations, namely of our study of homomor-
phisms of nilpotent Lie algebras and the corresponding (unipotent) Lie groups. We show
(Theorem 4.38) that any light homomorphism of Hecke monoids corresponding to Weyl
groups W(M) →W(M ′), finite or infinite, gives rise to a homomorphism of nilpotent Lie
algebras n(A) →n(A′) and their (unipotent) Lie groups U(A) →U(A′), where A and A′
are (generalized) Cartan matrices associated with M and M ′, respectively (see §4.4 for the
details).
Motivated by Theorem 1.1, we pose the following
Problem 1. Classify all parabolic homomorphisms of Hecke monoids, that is all those
homomorphisms which map all parabolic elements in the domain to parabolic elements of
the codomain.

same as homogeneous homomorphisms (see §3.2). For finite W(M) they are classified in
Theorem 3.23 and turn out to be, essentially, the unfoldings, and are expected to be inde-

injective, it is natural to pose the following
Problem 2. Classify injective homomorphisms of Hecke monoids.
Like parabolic ones, this class of homomorphisms is also closed under compositions. This

As the first step, we pose the following (see Definitions 3.1 and 3.12 for the terms in italic)
Problem 3. Classify all locally injective connected homomorphisms of Hecke monoids.

like those of parabolic and injective homomorphisms. We expect that all (locally) injec-

Lemma 3.18) or by taking compositions with homogeneous homomorphisms (cf. Conjec-
ture 5.21). We hope that this can be used in representation theory of Hecke monoids and
instrumental for solving Problem 2 for infinite Hecke monoids.
We completely solved Problem 3 for all classical series of Coxeter monoids in Section 5.
