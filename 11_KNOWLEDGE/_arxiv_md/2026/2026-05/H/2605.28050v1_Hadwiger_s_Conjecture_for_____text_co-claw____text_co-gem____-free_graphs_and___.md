Hadwiger’s Conjecture for {co-claw, co-gem}-free graphs
and {fork, antifork}-free graphs
Daniel Carter∗
Jung Hon Yip†
May 28, 2026
Abstract
We prove Hadwiger’s Conjecture for {co-claw, co-gem}-free graphs and {fork, antifork}-free graphs,
where the co-claw is the disjoint union of a triangle and a vertex, the co-gem is the disjoint union of
a 4-vertex path and a vertex, the fork is obtained from K1,3 by subdividing one of the edges, and the
antifork is the complement of the fork. The {co-claw, co-gem}-free graphs include the complements of line
graphs of triangle-free multigraphs, and thus our results imply Hadwiger’s Conjecture for these graphs.
In fact, we prove a stronger result: every {co-claw, co-gem}-free graph G has a Kχ(G)-model where each
branch set has size at most 2, and every {fork, antifork}-free graph G has a Kχ(G)-model where at most
one branch set has size greater than 2.

Introduction
Graphs in this paper are finite, loopless and with no parallel edges; graphs allowing parallel edges will be
called multigraphs. A graph H is a minor of a graph G if H can be obtained from G by deleting vertices and
edges, and by contracting edges. The maximum integer n such that the complete graph Kn is a minor of
G is called the Hadwiger number of G, denoted had(G). Recall that ω(G) is the size of the largest clique
in G, and χ(G) is the chromatic number of G. We use the notation A ⊔B to denote the disjoint union of
two graphs A and B, and G for the complement of G. For a graph H, we say that G is H-free if no induced
subgraph of G is isomorphic to H. Likewise for a set S of graphs, G is S-free if G is H-free for all H ∈S. A
class of graphs is hereditary if it is closed under taking induced subgraphs. For any undefined graph theory
notation, see Diestel [12].
One of the deepest unsolved problems in graph theory is Hadwiger’s Conjecture:
Conjecture 1.1 ([14]). For all graphs G, had(G) ≥χ(G).
See [15, 22, 25] for surveys on Hadwiger’s Conjecture in general. Recently, there has been interest in
proving Hadwiger’s Conjecture for various hereditary classes; see [2, 10] for surveys and [3, 19, 21, 24] for
other results on Hadwiger’s Conjecture in hereditary classes.
The claw is the complete bipartite graph K1,3, and the co-claw is the complement of the claw, which is
K3 ⊔K1. The gem, also known as the 4-fan, is the graph on five vertices formed by adding a vertex adjacent
to all of the vertices in a 4-vertex path; the co-gem is the complement of the gem, which is P4 ⊔K1. The
fork is obtained from the claw by subdividing one of the edges, and the antifork is the complement of the
fork (see Figure 1). In this paper, we prove Hadwiger’s Conjecture for {co-claw, co-gem}-free graphs and
{fork, antifork}-free graphs. Proving Hadwiger’s Conjecture for {fork, antifork}-free graphs was a problem
proposed by the first author at the 2025 Barbados Graph Theory Workshop [23].
Theorem 1.2. If G is {fork, antifork}-free, then had(G) ≥χ(G).
Theorem 1.3. If G is {co-claw, co-gem}-free, then had(G) ≥χ(G).

We now motivate Theorems 1.2 and 1.3. Since Hadwiger’s Conjecture has been proven for line graphs
of multigraphs [5, 21], it is natural to ask if it also holds for complements of line graphs. As a first step,
our results imply Hadwiger’s Conjecture for complements of line graphs of triangle-free multigraphs (see
Corollary 1.7).
Hadwiger’s Conjecture remains difficult for hereditary classes characterized by a single forbidden induced
subgraph. For example, despite having a structure theorem for claw-free graphs [8], Hadwiger’s Conjecture
remains open for these graphs. In fact, even the special case of Hadwiger’s Conjecture for K3-free graphs
(these are graphs with independence number at most 2, and every K3-free graph is claw-free) is open despite
significant attention [1, 8–10, 18, 19, 22].1 To date, the best partial result by Chudnovsky and Fradkin [6] is
that had(G) ≥(2/3)χ(G) if G is claw-free. As another example, Hadwiger’s Conjecture remains open for
triangle-free graphs [11, 13, 17]. There are some encouraging signs for triangle-free graphs: a breakthrough
result of Delcourt and Postle [11] shows that there is a constant c > 0 such that every triangle-free graph G
satisfies had(G) ≥c·χ(G), improving on previous work by Dvoˇr´ak and Yepremyan [13]. This demonstrates the
difficulty of proving Hadwiger’s Conjecture in classes characterized by a single forbidden induced subgraph.
However, what if we forbid two induced subgraphs? In this case there are several positive results in the
literature [3, 19, 21, 24]. We mention a few results about fork-free graphs, which are a natural generalization of
claw-free graphs. Hadwiger’s Conjecture holds for {fork, K5 −e}-free graphs and {fork, HVN}-free graphs (see
Figure 2). In these two classes, every graph G satisfies χ(G) ≤ω(G) + 1 [20], and more generally, Hadwiger’s
Conjecture holds for hereditary classes C in which χ(G) ≤ω(G) + 1 for all G ∈C [2]. However, there are
{fork, antifork}-free graphs with χ(G) > ω(G) + 1. Thus, other techniques are required to prove Hadwiger’s
Conjecture for {fork, antifork}-free graphs. We remark that any strengthening of Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 by
dropping one forbidden induced subgraph would imply a known hard case of Hadwiger’s Conjecture. For
example, Hadwiger’s Conjecture for fork-free or co-gem-free graphs would imply it for K3-free graphs, while
Hadwiger’s Conjecture for antifork-free or co-claw-free graphs would imply it for triangle-free graphs.
Our primary tool for proving Theorem 1.2 is the structure theorem for {fork, antifork}-free graphs by
Chudnovsky, Cook, and Seymour (Theorem 3.1), which states that every {fork, antifork}-free graph satisfies
one of eight outcomes. It is straightforward to see that a minimal {fork, antifork}-free counterexample
to Hadwiger’s Conjecture cannot satisfy seven of the eight outcomes; we prove this in Section 3. The
eighth outcome, when G is the complement of a line graph of a simple triangle-free graph, is handled in
Section 2. In fact, we prove a strengthening. We prove Hadwiger’s Conjecture for {co-claw, co-gem}-free
graphs (Theorem 1.3), which implies Hadwiger’s Conjecture for the complements of line graphs of triangle-free
multigraphs. Our proof uses the Strong Perfect Graph Theorem [7].

Proof of Theorem 1.5
In this section we prove Theorem 1.5. A hole is a cycle of length at least 4; an antihole is the complement of
a hole. A hole or antihole is odd if it has an odd number of vertices. A graph G is perfect if ω(H) = χ(H) for
all induced subgraphs H of G (including G itself). The celebrated Strong Perfect Graph Theorem states that
the perfect graphs are precisely the {odd hole, odd antihole}-free graphs [7].
A minimal counterexample to Theorem 1.5 is a {co-claw, co-gem}-free graph G such that had2(G) < χ(G)
but had2(H) ≥χ(H) for all proper induced subgraphs H of G. We prove Theorem 1.5 by showing that any
minimal counterexample must be odd hole and odd antihole-free, and therefore perfect.

Then G is {odd hole, odd antihole}-free, so G is perfect and had2(G) ≥ω(G) = χ(G).

Proof of Theorem 1.4
In this section we prove Theorem 1.4. We need the following structure theorem of {fork, antifork}-free graphs
by Chudnovsky, Cook, and Seymour (definitions to follow):
Theorem 3.1 ([4]). Let G be a {fork, antifork}-free graph. Then at least one of the following is true:
(i), (ii) G or G is disconnected.
(iii), (iv) G or G has a pair of adjacent simplicial twins.
(v), (vi) G or G is candled.
(vii), (viii) G or G is the line graph of a simple triangle-free graph.
Chudnovsky, Cook, and Seymour used Theorem 3.1 to prove that χ(G) ≤2ω(G) if G is {fork, antifork}-free.
We now explain the unfamiliar terms appearing in this structure theorem.
A vertex v in a graph G is simplicial if its neighborhood NG(v) induces a clique. Two vertices u, v ∈V (G)
are twins if NG(u) ∩(V (G) \ {u, v}) = NG(v) ∩(V (G) \ {u, v}).
Two sets of vertices are complete if every edge between them exists, and anticomplete if there are no edges
between them. A candelabrum is a graph formed by 2k non-empty disjoint sets of vertices Y1, . . . , Yk and
Z1, . . . , Zk such that:
• Each Yi is a clique and each Zi is a stable set.
• The sets Yi are pairwise anticomplete and the sets Zi are pairwise complete.
• Yi is complete to Zj if i = j and anticomplete to Zj if i ̸= j.
We call the sets Yi the candle sets and Zi the base sets.
A graph G is candled if it contains a non-empty candelabrum with candle sets Y1, . . . , Yk and base sets
Z1, . . . , Zk such that every vertex of G not in the candelabrum is adjacent to every vertex in Z1, . . . , Zk and
non-adjacent to every vertex in Y1, . . . , Yk (see Figure 3).

had2(G) ≤∆(H) = ω(G), where ∆(H) is the maximum degree in H, and so had2(G) = χ(G) −1.
An example of such H is a d-regular graph of girth at least 7 with |V (H)| odd, which guarantees
χ(G) = ω(G) + 1 (to see this, note that each color class of a coloring of G corresponds to a matching in
H and therefore covers at most (|V (H)| −1)/2 edges of H; there are d|V (H)|/2 edges in H, so at least
d + 1 = ω(G) + 1 color classes are required).
4
Further results and open problems
One can easily improve the {co-claw, co-gem}-free result to a slightly more general class. Note that if C
is a hereditary class such that had2(G) ≥χ(G) for all G ∈C, then the closure of C under disjoint unions
is another class with the same property. The {co-claw, co-gem}-free graphs are not closed under disjoint
unions. Let C′ denote the closure of {co-claw, co-gem}-free graphs under disjoint unions, which corresponds
to graphs whose connected components are {co-claw, co-gem}-free. We may obtain a forbidden induced
subgraph characterization of C′ as follows. Note that the co-claw with the isolated vertex removed is K3,
and the co-gem with the isolated vertex removed is the 4-vertex path P4. Let G be a connected graph that
contains the co-claw or co-gem as an induced subgraph, say on vertex set Y . Define X ⊆Y as follows: if Y
induces the co-claw, then X induces K3, and if Y induces the co-gem, then X induces P4. Then there is at
least one vertex v ∈V (G) anticomplete to X (for instance, the unique vertex in Y \ X). Choose such a v as
close to X as possible; then v must be distance 2 from X. Let w be a neighbor of v that is adjacent to a
vertex of X. By considering the neighborhood of w in X, we deduce that G induces one of the graphs in
Figure 4. Hence:
Proposition 4.1. Every connected component of G is {co-claw, co-gem}-free if and only if G is S-free, where
S is the set of graphs depicted in Figure 4. Therefore if G is S-free, then had2(G) ≥χ(G).
There are yet stronger results that can be easily obtained. For instance, let S′ be S minus the third and
tenth graphs in Figure 4. Then one can prove that if G is S′-free, then had2(G) ≥χ(G). This can be done by
closing the class of {co-claw, co-gem} graphs under both disjoint unions and complete joins (disjoint unions
in the complement) simultaneously. Here, we note that C′ is not closed complete joins; a complete join of two
graphs in C′ might contain the third or tenth graph in Figure 4.
We conclude with some further problems. Corollary 1.7 proves Hadwiger’s Conjecture for complements of
line graphs of triangle-free multigraphs. The first author has extended this result to all complements of line
graphs of multigraphs. The proof uses some similar techniques but with substantial computer assistance; the
details will appear in a future paper. Proposition 3.3 suggests the following broad problem:
Problem 4.2. For which hereditary classes C is it true that had2(G) ≥χ(G) for every graph G ∈C?

Such a class C must at least avoid the construction in Proposition 3.3, so for instance C cannot contain
odd cycles of length at least 7. Perhaps the converse holds:
Problem 4.3. Does every {C2k+1}k≥3-free graph G satisfy had2(G) ≥χ(G)?
An affirmative answer would be a substantial strengthening of the conjecture of Seymour that had2(G) ≥
χ(G) if G is K3-free [22]. Here is an even stronger conjecture. For integer m ≥1, let hadm(G) be the largest
t such that G contains a Kt-model using branch sets of size at most m. Let Cm be the class of graphs
G such that hadm(H) ≥χ(H) for all induced subgraphs H of G. Then C1 is the class of perfect graphs,
which we know is the class of {odd hole, odd antihole}-free graphs. It is easy to see that Cm is a subset of
{C2k+1}k≥3m/2-free graphs, as hadm(Cℓ) is equal to 2 if 3m > ℓand 3 if 3m ≤ℓ.
Problem 4.4. Is Cm the class of {C2k+1}k≥3m/2-free graphs for all m ≥2?
