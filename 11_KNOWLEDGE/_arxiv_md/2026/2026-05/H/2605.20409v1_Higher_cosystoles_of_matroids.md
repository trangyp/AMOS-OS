HIGHER COSYSTOLES OF MATROIDS
JAMES DYLAN DOUTHITT, ELANA ISRAEL, AND LEE KENNARD
Abstract. We define a matroid invariant called the three-cosystole that is related
to higher notions of cogirth for weighted matroids, and we prove an optimal upper
bound for it in the class of regular matroids of rank at most six. To accomplish this,
we show that it is increasing under matroid extensions and then estimate it for each
of the maximal simple regular matroids of rank at most six.
A weighted matroid is a matroid M equipped with a non-negative and non-zero function
µ : E →R≥0 on its ground set E. We define a normalized, weighted girth by the formula
sys(M, µ) = min µ(C)/µ(E),
where the minimum runs over circuits C, and where µ(X) for a subset X ⊆E denotes
the sum of µ(e) over e ∈X. The systole of M is the matroid invariant
sys(M) = max sys(M, µ)
obtained by maximizing over admissible weight functions. The cosystole sys∗(M) is
defined similarly using cocircuits in place of circuits. Matroid cosystoles have been
studied in [CCD07] in general, [CO23] in the binary case, and [KWW25] in the regular
case. An advantage of (co)systoles over (co)girths is their invariance under scalings of
the weight function. In particular, we may restrict to the case where µ(E) = 1.
The main results in [KWW25] are in Riemannian geometry, but the main computation
to prove them is to show sharp upper bounds of the form sys∗(M) ≤s∗(d) for regular
matroids of rank d ≤9. For rank six, the estimate states that sys∗(M) ≤1/3. Moreover,
Theorem 3.14 in that paper shows that, for any µ with total weight one, there exist
six pairwise distinct cocircuits C∗
i whose µ-weights sum to at most two. In addition
to implying sys∗(M) ≤1/3, it follows using that M is binary that there exist three
linearly independent cocircuits whose µ-weights sum to at most one.
In this paper, we reprove the second of these estimates using a different strategy. This
has three advantages. The first is the following, formal strengthening of the estimate
on linearly independent cocircuits.

1. Regular matroids of rank at most six
Regular matroids play an important role in matroid theory and have been characterized
in multiple ways. A deep and constructive characterization of regular matroids is due
to Seymour [Sey80]: Regular matroids are built from 1-sums, 2-sums, and 3-sums of
graphic matroids, cographic matroids, and the sporadic matroid R10. In small ranks, it is
possible to use Seymour’s classification to construct an explicit list of regular matroids.
We do this in ranks up to six in this section.
We first note that adding loops or elements in parallel to existing elements of the
ground set makes the ground set larger while preserving the rank and regularity of
the matroid. As these extensions are trivial and lead to an infinite set of objects, we
restrict to simple matroids to avoid loops or parallel elements.
In rank one, the uniform matroid U1,1 = M(K2) is the only simple matroid, and
therefore the only simple regular matroid.
In rank two, there are two simple regular matroids: the direct sum U2,2 = M(K2) ⊕
M(K2) with two elements and the graphic matroid U2,3 = M(K3) with three. Note
that the first of these is isomorphic to M(P2) where P2 is the two-edge path and is
therefore isomorphic to a restriction of M(K3).
In rank three, there are five simple regular matroids. All of them are graphic and
representable as the graphic matroid M(G) for some subgraph G ⊆K4.
