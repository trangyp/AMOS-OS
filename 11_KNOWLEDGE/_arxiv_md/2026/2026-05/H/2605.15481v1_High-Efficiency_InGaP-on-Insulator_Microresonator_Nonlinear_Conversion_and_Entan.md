Abstract: InGaP-on-insulator, with its intrinsically high 𝐿(2) optical nonlinearity, has emerged
as an e!cient and bright integrated photonic platform for frequency conversion and on-chip
entanglement generation, but high waveguide propagation loss in the visible wavelength range
has limited its overall performance. Here, we identify the dominant loss mechanism through
mode-proﬁle analysis and e"ectively mitigate the loss using a surface treatment method. Statistical
analysis of the resonator quality factor and propagation loss reveals the optimal ring radius
that maintains a strong nonlinear interaction while suppressing signiﬁcant bending related loss,
resulting in loss as low as 0.49 dB/cm (4.31 dB/cm) at 1560 nm (780 nm). The method provides
a 3.5–4→linear performance enhancement, enabling a second-harmonic generation e!ciency
of 3.01 →105 %/W and a photon-pair generation rate of 11.7 MHz/𝑀W and coincidence-to-
accidental ratio as high as 10,000. The quasi-phase matching condition is experimentally veriﬁed,
and nonlinear conversion is systematically characterized across the entire parameter space. This
work establishes a scalable pathway for classical and quantum photonics in a low-loss, highly
nonlinear, and wafer-scale integration platform.
1.
Introduction
Integrated nonlinear photonics plays an important role in scalable classical and quantum optical
technologies. On-chip second-order (𝐿(2)) nonlinear processes enable e!cient frequency con-
version [1–7], entangled photon-pair generation [3,4,8,9], and optical information processing,
such as squeezed light [10], through di"erence-frequency generation (DFG), second-harmonic
generation (SHG), and spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC). A nonlinear photonic
platform that simultaneously o"ers high nonlinear e!ciency, low propagation loss, and compati-
bility with large-scale heterogeneous integration remains in high demand for practical classical
and quantum photonic systems.
Several 𝐿(2) systems have produced promising results. Thin-ﬁlm lithium niobate (TFLN) is one
leading platform due to its low propagation loss and high electro-optic e!ciency [11]. However,
challenges remain in heterogeneous integration and monolithic incorporation with active photonic
components. Wide-bandgap III–V semiconductors provide a compelling alternative. In particular,
indium gallium phosphide (InGaP) is attractive due to its large second-order nonlinear coe!cient
(𝐿(2) ↑220 pm/V [7, 12]) and wide bandgap (↓1.9 eV), which enables visible wavelength
operation and eliminates two-photon absorption at infrared wavelengths. Its high refractive index
allows tight modal conﬁnement and strong nonlinear interaction, enhancing the e"ective nonlinear
interaction strength. Furthermore, wafer-scale heterogeneous bonding has been demonstrated on
a 4-inch InGaP epitaxial wafer, providing a pathway toward scalable InGaP-on-insulator photonic
integration [13].

Cavity enhancement in microresonators dramatically increases the nonlinear interaction
strength by increasing intracavity ﬁeld intensity and enabling resonant buildup of the interacting
modes. In 𝐿(2) systems, doubly resonant microresonators signiﬁcantly reduce pump power
requirements and enable e!cient SHG and SPDC in ten-micron-scale compact devices [3,6].
However, a major challenge in III–V platforms, including InGaP, is the signiﬁcant propagation loss
at visible wavelengths [3,4]. Scattering loss from epitaxial surfaces and sidewalls, bulk defects,
and surface-state absorption can severely limit cavity quality factors, particularly at shorter
wavelengths where scattering loss scales strongly [14,15]. Although e!cient SHG and SPDC
have been demonstrated in small-radius, suspended InGaP microresonators, mechanical stability,
thermal stability, and large-scale photonic integration remain fundamentally challenging [3,4].
Therefore, realizing low-loss, high-performance classical and quantum nonlinear photonics in
heterogeneously integrated InGaP-on-insulator platforms remains a signiﬁcant challenge.
In this work, we demonstrate high-e!ciency nonlinear conversion and entanglement generation
in low-loss InGaP-on-insulator microresonators. Through all-optical wafer-scale fabrication,
systematic identiﬁcation and mitigation of dominant loss mechanisms, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a),
and dispersion-engineered resonator design, we achieve an SHG e!ciency of 3.01 →105 %/W
and a photon-pair generation rate of 11.7 MHz/𝑀W in 20 𝑀m-radius microresonators. These
results establish simultaneous high-performance classical and quantum nonlinear processes
within a monolithically integrated InGaP-on-insulator platform. Our work positions InGaP-on-
insulator microresonators as scalable building blocks for next-generation frequency converters
and integrated quantum photonic circuits.

ﬁelds and carriers through absorption and defect-assisted recombination processes [23–25]. This
phenomenon, commonly referred to as surface recombination, has been extensively studied in
electronic and optoelectronic devices and is strongly associated with the surface-to-volume ratio
of the active region [26].

3.
Low-Loss InGaP-on-Insulator

atoms bond to the III–V surface to suppress oxidation and terminate dangling bonds. We
investigate its impact on surface states, surface morphology, propagation loss, and resonator
quality factor. The top surfaces of three samples were studied: untreated material (“as-grown”),
samples subjected to a 30 s dip in 10% NH4OH (“OH treatment”), and samples treated with
a 10 min rapid thermal annealing (RTA) in N2 forming gas followed by a 10 min dip in 20%
(NH4)2S at 60 ↘C (“S treatment”).

leaked VIS pump photons. The photon-pair generation rate (PGR) and coincidence-to-accidental

11.7 MHz/𝑀W is obtained, reaching 30 MHz PGR at a CAR of 70. These results demonstrate
highly e!cient quantum light generation in compact, low-loss InGaP-on-insulator integrated
waveguides enabled by cavity-enhanced nonlinear interactions.
6.
Second Harmonic Generation
Although the add-drop structures are not optimized for SHG due to the strong escape e!ciency
in the IR range, signiﬁcant SHG is still observed in the 20 𝑀m resonator conﬁgurations. Here,
we use an add-drop structure with a similar conﬁguration but with a 200 nm coupling gap
for the VIS wavelength to increase the coupling e!ciency of the VIS photons. For the IR
resonance at ↓1535.47 nm, 𝑊𝑆= 33.6k, 𝑊𝑂= 367.9k, 𝑋esc = 90.9%, and propagation loss
= 1.35 dB/cm. For the VIS resonance at ↓767.74 nm, 𝑊𝑆= 73.7k, 𝑊𝑂= 102.7k, 𝑋esc = 28.2%,
and propagation loss = 12.6 dB/cm. The resonator is pumped using a mode-hopping-free CW IR
laser (Toptica DLC Pro), and the current applied to the microheaters is tuned until the maximum
SHG intensity is achieved. The shorter-wavelength peak corresponds to the VIS resonance, while
the longer-wavelength peak corresponds to the IR resonance when no thermal current is applied.
As the thermal current increases, a clear trend is observed where improved modal overlap is
achieved and the VIS resonance redshifts faster than the IR resonance, as shown in Fig. 6(a).
When the thermal current is further increased, the modal overlap and SHG intensity decrease.
With the optimal thermal current applied, the SHG signal is collected and measured using a
power meter (Newport 2936-R). The relationship between SHG intensity and IR pump power is
shown in Fig. 6(b). A ﬁtted slope of 1.98 is obtained, conﬁrming the quadratic dependence of
SHG on pump power, and an on-chip SHG e!ciency of 3.01 →105 %/W is achieved, which is
comparable to suspended InGaP rings with smaller radii [3,4]. Furthermore, a peak conversion
e!ciency of 46.3% is observed at 0.48 mW on-chip pump power, as shown in Fig. 6(c). With
optimized coupling conditions (critical coupling for both resonances) and achievable quality
factors of ↓969 k and ↓229 k for the IR and VIS modes, respectively, an on-chip SHG e!ciency
of 2.20→107 %/W and a photon-pair generation rate (PGR) of 163.33 MHz/𝑀W can be achieved
[8,33].
