We use bosonization to analyze the ground state Hall response of interacting bosonic N-leg ladders
threaded by a flux. We derive an explicit expression of the Hall imbalance in a perturbative expansion
in the band curvature, retaining fully the interactions. For small magnetic field the Hall resistance
is proportional to the derivative of the logarithm of the charge stiffness with respect to density,
generalizing the result obtained in the two leg case. We also consider the effect of temperature, and
establish that at low temperature, corrections to the Hall resistance are exponentially small in the
Meissner phase.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The Hall effect[1] and its quantum version[2] are key
concepts in modern condensed matter physics, with ap-
plications in metrology[3] and sensors[4]. In the case of
non-interacting electrons, the quantum Hall effect has
been understood in terms of topological invariants of the
ground state wavefunction[5–8]. For low magnetic fields,
the Hall conductance obtained from the Boltzmann equa-
tion is related to the curvature of the Fermi surface
by Tsuji’s formula[9] in three dimensions, and to the
area swept by the scattering path by Ong’s formula[10]
in two dimensions. However, with strongly interacting
particles, the theory of the Hall effect remains chal-
lenging, even for low field[11–14].
In the realm of in-
teracting quantum particles, an important issue is the
Hall effect of interacting bosonic particles.
Indeed for
bosons interactions are usually needed from the start
to avoid pathological cases. A Hall effect with charged
bosonic particles can be observed in Josephson junction
arrays[15], and using ultracold atoms in artificial gauge
field[16–23]. The simplest interacting bosonic system in
which the Hall effect can be studied is a planar lad-
der formed of one-dimensional interacting bosons cou-
pled by interchain hopping, since such a system allows a
nonzero flux per plaquette while being amenable to pow-
erful analytic[24–26] and numeric[27–29] methods. The
ground state phase diagram of bosonic ladders in a flux
has been considered using analytic methods[30–33] and
numerical methods[34–44]. At low flux, a Meissner-like
phase, with current circulating on the edge is found,
while at higher flux, a Vortex phase-like is obtained[31].
At finite temperature, the crossover between the Meiss-
ner and the Vortex phase was found to persist.[44–46]
Moreover, for fluxes commensurate with the particle den-
sity, analogues of the quantized Hall effect have been
predicted.[47–49] Recently a numerical study of the Hall
coefficient in a quasi-one dimensional system, has pre-
dicted a universal behavior for the Hall coefficient above
an interaction threshold [50, 51]. In the case of N−leg
ladder systems with SU(N) symmetry, the Hall imbal-
ance, i.e. the difference of particle number between up-
per and lower legs, was shown to take the classical value
RH = 1/n [50], a prediction confirmed in a quantum
simulation with strongly interacting ultracold fermions
[52]. The possibility to reliably measure the Hall effect
in strongly correlated systems has prompted both fur-
ther analytical calculation of the Hall polarization and
voltage[53] and their measurement[52, 54]. In a previ-
ous Letter, we used a bosonization approach[55] prop-
erly taking into account band curvature [11, 56–58] and
predicted analytically that the ground state Hall resis-
tance of a two-leg bosonic ladder is proportional to the
derivative of the logarithm of the Kohn stiffness[59] with
respect to particle density. This result showed a remark-
able connection between two important transport coef-
ficients that encode the many-body effects triggered by
interactions in the response to external fluxes. Recent ex-
periments with ultracold bosons have been able to mea-
sure the Kohn stiffness [60]. Beyond the clear potential
of our formula to predict Hall voltages from single chain
response to Aharonov-Bohm fluxes, it clarified the exotic
Hall response of charged bosonic two-leg ladder systems,
in particular in the vicinity of a Mott transition[61, 62].
Given the persistence of a Vortex-Meissner crossover at
finite temperature[46], it is also worthwhile to consider
the effect of temperature on the Hall resistance in the
two-leg case.
In the present paper, we first generalize our previous
ground state results to the N-leg ladder with N ≥3 and
then calculate in the two-leg case the temperature de-
pendence of the Hall imbalance. In the high tempera-
ture limit, the Hall imbalance decays as a power law in
temperature, while at low temperature, the ground state
result is asymptotically recovered. The low temperature

The plan of the paper is as follows. In Sec. II, we start
by introducing the three-leg ladder model and then, using
bosonization approach with an appropriate basis change,
we derive an analytic expression for the Hall voltage and
Hall imbalance by taking into account band curvature

polynomials in the chain index n, we derive analytic ex-
pression of Hall voltage and Hall imbalance. The result
shows that the proportionality of the Hall resistance to
the derivative of the logarithm of the Kohn stiffness is
present in all N-leg bosonic ladder. In Sec. IV, we con-
sider the two- and three-leg bosonic ladder at positive
temperature in a solvable limit, and nd establish that at
low temperature, corrections to the Hall resistance are
exponentially small in inverse temperature in the Meiss-

V.
CONCLUSION
We have analyzed an N-leg bosonic ladder in the
ground state and in the presence of flux, and found that
at low flux, in the Meissner phase, the Hall resistance
was proportional to the derivative of the logarithm of the
Kohn stiffness with respect to the particle density, gener-
alizing the result obtained in the two-leg case [53]. In the
Vortex phase, the Hall voltage stops being proportional
to the applied flux, so it can be used as a marker of the
transition. We have also considered the finite tempera-
ture case for the two-leg ladder in the exactly solvable
Luther-Emery limit, and we found that the deviations
from the zero temperature results were suppressed ex-
ponentially in the Meissner phase, while they were only
suppressed as power laws on the Vortex phase. A similar
result holds also for the three-leg ladder with appropri-
ate transformation.
This shows that the result of [53]
is robust, both with respect to temperature and num-
ber of chains. Those predictions can be compared with
numerical simulations [51] and experiments on ultracold
atoms [82]. Finally, it is worthwhile considering also the
generalization of the results for two-leg fermionic ladder
in [53] to the case of many legs. An interesting issue is
the limit of the number of coupled chains going to in-
finity, realizing an anisotropic two dimensional array. If
our formula remained applicable in that limit, it would
predict a divergent Hall resistance changing sign across
the Mott transition in the two-dimensional case. In [53],
we had predicted that the Hall resistance of a two-leg
fermionic ladder would also behave as a derivative of the
logarithm of the Kohn stiffness with respect to the par-
ticle density. Obviously, it would be interesting to see
if, as in the bosonic case, this result is also valid with
any number of coupled chains. Another open question
is the role of interchain interactions.
When those in-
teractions dominate, they impose at zero flux a density
wave ordering that competes with the Meissner phase
[83]. Such transition could have a signature in the behav-
ior of the Hall resistance. So far, we have only discussed
cases where the charge mode could be treated as a per-
fect Tomonaga-Luttinger liquid, with a nonzero charge
stiffness even at nonzero temperatures. However, in non-
integrable models, the charge stiffness vanishes as soon
as the temperature is nonzero [84], and the Drude peak
in the conductivity is replaced by a Lorentzian. It would
be worthwhile to investigate whether a relation between
the weight of the Lorentzian and the Hall resistance can
be established.
Models containing coupling to a bath
[85–87] in the action or obeying a Lindbladian dynamics
[88] could help clarifying that issue. Finally, let us stress
that our results provide a bosonic multileg ladder per-
spective on Hall responses, establishing a bridge to the
recent experimental observations[52, 54].
