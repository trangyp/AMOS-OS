HIGHER-LEVEL AFFINE WREATH PRODUCT ALGEBRAS
THOMAS MORAN
Abstract. We define and study two new classes of algebras, called higher-level affine wreath
product algebras and higher-level affine Frobenius Hecke algebras.
They depend on a Frobenius
superalgebra and are defined, respectively, as path algebras of the higher-level affine wreath product
category and higher-level affine Frobenius Hecke category. Our constructions produce a broad range
of new higher-level algebras under a unified framework. Special cases include higher-level analogues
of the degenerate affine Hecke algebra and affine Sergeev algebras, both of which appear to be new.

1. Introduction
1.1. Quantum wreath products. Affine Hecke algebras and their degenerate counterparts play
an important role in the representation theory of Lie algebras. In recent years, several modified
versions of these algebras have appeared in the literature. For example, in [Sav20], Savage defined
the affine wreath product algebras, which are generalizations of degenerate affine Hecke algebras.
They depend on a Frobenius superalgebra, and have been used in the categorification of lattice
Heisenberg algebras in [Sav19]. Affine wreath product algebras provide a simultaneous unification
and generalization of existing constructions in the literature that were previously treated separately.
For example, special cases include the degenerate affine Hecke algebras, affine Sergeev algebras
[Naz97], wreath Hecke algebras [WW08], and affine zigzag algebras [KM19].
A quantum analogue of the affine wreath product algebras was defined by Rosso and Savage
in [RS20]. These are called affine Frobenius Hecke algebras (or quantum affine wreath algebras),
and depend on a symmetric Frobenius superalgebra. Special cases of the affine Frobenius Hecke
algebras include affine Yokonuma–Hecke algebras [CPd14] and affine Hecke algebras. Both affine
wreath product algebras and affine Frobenius Hecke algebras are special cases of quantum wreath
products, in the sense of [LNX24].

1.2. Higher-level Hecke algebras. In recent years, several new classes of higher-level Hecke-
type algebras have appeared in the literature. Loosely speaking, these algebras are obtained by
introducing red strands, which interact with the black strands corresponding to the original Hecke
algebra. They are closely related to the cyclotomic quotients of Hecke algebras, and have been used
to categorify tensor products of simple modules over a quantum group (see [Web17]). Examples of
such higher-level Hecke algebras include the tensor product algebras from [Web17, Def. 4.7], and
the higher-level affine Hecke algebras (or the type F affine Hecke algebras) from [MS21, Def. 2.7]
and [Web20, Def. 5.5]. Another recent example is the affine Schur category from [SW24].
It is natural to ask whether one can combine the above-mentioned approaches by defining higher-
level versions of the affine wreath product algebras and affine Frobenius Hecke algebras. In this
paper, we answer this question in the affirmative by constructing such algebras. We expect that
these constructions will lead to new developments in categorification and representation theory.
1.3. Higher-level affine wreath product algebras. Fix a commutative ground ring k. To every
Frobenius superalgebra A, we associate the higher-level affine wreath product category LAW(A) and
the higher-level affine wreath product algebra W aff

framework. For example, by specializing A, we obtain the following:
• When A = k, the higher-level affine wreath product algebra is a higher-level version of the

appear as a path algebra of the affine Schur category from [SW24].
• If A = Cl is the two-dimensional Clifford superalgebra (see Example 3.3), then the higher-

to be new.

[WW08, Def. 2.4] for the definition of the wreath Hecke algebra.) This construction appears
to be new.

2.3. Monoids. For a given set X, we define F(X) to be the free monoid on X. That is, F(X) is
the set of all words in X, and the product of any two words is their concatenation. We denote the
length of a word w ∈F(X) by |w|. A subword of w is an element in F(X) obtained by deleting
some (not necessarily consecutive) letters in w.
Definition 2.4. Let u, v ∈F(X). Then a (u, v)-shuffle is a word w ∈F(X) satisfying the following
two properties:
• The length of w is |w| = |u| + |v|.
• The element w contains both u and v as subwords.
We define F(X)u,v to be the set of all (u, v)-shuffles.
Example 2.5. Let X = {a, b, c}. Then the word abcb is an (ac, bb)-shuffle. The words cbab and
acbbc are not (ac, bb)-shuffles.
