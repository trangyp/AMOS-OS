Abstract:
In this work, we investigate non-interacting holographic dark energy (HDE)
with the Hubble radius as the infrared cutoff in Einstein-Cartan gravity. We derive the
Einstein-Cartan equations from the action principle and obtain Friedmann-like equations
by introducing a torsion scalar. Considering a Weyssenhoff spin fluid, we determine the
scaling behavior of the torsion scalar as Φ ∼a−3 without introducing an ad hoc ansatz,
resolving the ansatz problem of previous torsion scalar scenarios. In the absence of in-
teractions between dark matter and dark energy, the torsion scalar shifts the equation of
state for holographic dark energy toward negative values from the dust-like value obtained
in HDE without torsion, making cosmic acceleration possible. In particular, the resulting
equation of state can approach ωX ≃−1 and cross the phantom divide within the weak
torsion regime |Φ/H| < 1. The model predicts a dynamical equation of state in which cos-
mic acceleration gradually weakens, potentially consistent with recent DESI observations.
In spacetimes with torsion, the cosmic distance duality relation between the luminosity
distance dL and the angular diameter distance dA is modified as dL = dA(1 + z)2(1 + η).

Introduction
Modern cosmology demonstrates that the universe is currently undergoing accelerated ex-
pansion through the analysis of various observational data, such as Type Ia supernovae
(SNIa) [1, 2], the cosmic microwave background (CMB) [3, 4], baryon acoustic oscillations
(BAO) [5, 6], and large-scale structure (LSS) [7, 8]. This phenomenon is commonly at-
tributed to dark energy, which is modeled as an effective energy component with negative
pressure. Dark energy is one of the most important and challenging problems in particle
physics and modern cosmology [9]. The simplest explanation for dark energy is provided by
the ΛCDM model, which includes the cosmological constant Λ and has served as the refer-
ence model for the standard cosmology to date. However, the cosmological constant model
has clear limitations in that it does not fundamentally resolve the extreme fine-tuning
problem of vacuum energy [10, 11] and the cosmic coincidence problem [12].
To address the limitations of the cosmological constant model, several alternative dark
energy models have been proposed. These approaches can be broadly classified into two
categories. The first category modifies the geometric sector on the left-hand side of the
Einstein equations in general relativity [13–16]. The second category extends the matter
sector on the right-hand side through the energy-momentum tensor [17–22].
Although

such models can reproduce late-time acceleration, their fundamental physical origin re-
mains unclear. Moreover, recent observations have reported the Hubble tension [23–25]
and the S8 tension [26]. The Hubble tension refers to the discrepancy between the current
Hubble parameter inferred from the CMB within the ΛCDM model and that measured
from the local distance ladder observations. Similarly, the S8 tension reflects a mismatch
in the current amplitude of matter clustering inferred from high-redshift CMB data and
that measured from low-redshift LSS observations. Models with interactions between dark
matter and dark energy are known to exhibit structural constraints in fully resolving both
tensions simultaneously [27, 28]. In addition, recent results from the Dark Energy Spectro-
scopic Instrument (DESI) suggest that the equation of state for dark energy is dynamic and
that cosmic acceleration is gradually weakening [29]. Hence, such models require further
modifications or new physical interpretations.
We approach the aforementioned challenges from the perspective of a new physical
interpretation. While general relativity is widely regarded as the classical limit of quantum
gravity, a consistent theory of quantum gravity has not yet been established. Nevertheless,
its classical limit is expected to pass through the Einstein-Cartan theory before restoring
general relativity, as shown in Fig. 1. In this limit, spacetime and matter emerge simul-
taneously. Since matter possesses intrinsic properties such as mass, charge, and spin, it
is physically motivated to consider a gravitational framework that incorporates all these
degrees of freedom. Such a framework is provided by the Einstein-Cartan theory. Fur-
thermore, we apply the holographic principle to this theory because this principle has a
quantum nature. Ultimately, we aim to analyze the dark energy problem using a model
that is as close as possible to quantum gravity.

The Einstein-Cartan theory relaxes the torsion-free condition of general relativity,
introducing torsion as a macroscopic manifestation of matter’s intrinsic spin [30]. In this
framework, torsion is treated as an independent dynamical variable in addition to the
metric, so that spacetime geometry is characterized by both metric and torsion.
The
dynamics of spacetimes with torsion are governed by the Einstein-Cartan equations, while
torsion is algebraically related to the spin density of matter by the Cartan equations [31–33].
This theory prevents the formation of singularities [34, 35] and provides new insights into
cosmological problems such as inflation [36, 37], dark matter [38], and dark energy [39–42].
In spacetimes with torsion, torsion affects light propagation and modifies the cosmological

distance duality relation [43]. Nevertheless, the contribution of torsion is expected to be
small in the current universe, and thus the cosmological observations are still not sufficient
to demonstrate an irrefutable discrepancy with the predictions of general relativity [44].
Cohen et al. [45] suggested that vacuum energy in a region of finite size L should
not exceed the mass of a black hole of the same size, leading to a correlation between an
ultraviolet (UV) cutoff and an infrared (IR) cutoff. This is known as UV/IR mixing or
holographic principle. In general relativity, non-interacting holographic dark energy model
with the future event horizon as the IR cutoff can account for cosmic acceleration [46].
However, such models suffer from causality problem and circular logic problem caused by
the future event horizon. Alternative choices such as the particle horizon and the Hubble
radius fail to produce cosmic acceleration in the non-interacting case [47, 48]. Interacting
holographic dark energy models with the Hubble radius as the IR cutoff can achieve cos-
mic acceleration, but they do not admit a non-interacting limit [49]. To overcome these
difficulties, non-interacting holographic dark energy models with the Hubble radius as the
IR cutoff have been considered in Einstein-Cartan theory, leading to cosmic acceleration.
However, axial torsion scenario violates the cosmological principle [50], whereas torsion
scalar scenario suffers from an ansatz problem [51]. Since both scenarios rely on assumed
forms of the Einstein-Cartan equations, their theoretical completeness remains limited.
In this work, we derive the Einstein-Cartan equations from the action principle and
obtain Friedmann-like equations by introducing a torsion scalar.
Based on this setup,
we analyze the scaling behavior of the torsion scalar and the dynamics of non-interacting
holographic dark energy with the Hubble radius as the IR cutoff. This resolves the ansatz
problem for the torsion scalar and shows that cosmic acceleration can be achieved. Further-
more, we investigate light propagation in spacetimes with torsion and examine the modified
relation between the luminosity distance and the angular diameter distance. Based on the
derived evolution of the torsion scalar, we determine the scaling behavior of the deviation
parameter η, which quantifies torsion-induced deviations from the standard cosmic dis-
tance duality relation. This provides a theoretical framework for performing a likelihood
analysis of the torsion scalar.
The structure of this paper is as follows. In Sec. 2, we discuss the weak equivalence
principle in spacetimes with torsion and derive equations of motion from the action prin-
ciple. In Sec. 3, we obtain Friedmann-like equations by introducing the torsion scalar and
determine its scaling behavior by considering a Weyssenhoff spin fluid. In particular, we
analyze the dynamics of non-interacting holographic dark energy with the Hubble radius
as the IR cutoff. In Sec. 4, we examine the cosmological distance duality relation and
determine the scaling relation of the deviation parameter. Throughout this work, we use
natural units c = ℏ= 1.

Conclusions
We have demonstrated the feasibility of the non-interacting holographic dark energy with
the Hubble radius as the IR cutoff within the framework of Einstein-Cartan gravity. The
torsion scalar plays an important role in the equation of state of holographic dark energy,
enabling cosmic acceleration even in the weak torsion regime, while remaining consistent
with the cosmological principle. Furthermore, we found that the torsion scalar preserves the
standard relation between redshift and the scale factor but modifies the relation between
the luminosity distance and the angular diameter distance. This suggests that the subtle
effect of the torsion scalar could be encoded in direct observations such as SNIa and BAO.
In spacetimes with torsion, the weak equivalence principle is not always satisfied be-
cause geodesics, autoparallels, and null curves are generally distinct. Totally antisymmet-
ric torsion preserves the weak equivalence principle, whereas general torsion violates it.
Since the physical origin of torsion is the spin of matter, which is intrinsically quantum
in nature, this violation may be inevitable. Torsion does not propagate and is directly
encoded in the spin of matter through the algebraic Cartan equations. In this work, we
have adopted the Einstein-like form of the Einstein-Cartan equations because the canonical
energy-momentum tensor naturally incorporates the spin density of matter.

To utilize the results of the standard Friedmann equations, the Einstein-like form can
be recast into the effective form, from which Friedmann-like equations follow under the
cosmological principle. The torsion scalar is linked to the spin density of matter through
the Cartan equations. Assuming the Weyssenhoff fluid of fermions without spin polariza-
tion, together with adiabatic expansion and particle number conservation, the behavior
of the torsion scalar is determined as Φ ∼a−3. Interestingly, the torsion scalar exhibits
matter-like scaling behavior, which is attributed to the non-propagating nature of torsion.
This shows that the ansatz problem for the torsion scalar has been resolved. For the non-
interacting holographic dark energy with the Hubble radius as the IR cutoff, the torsion
scalar shifts the equation of state ωX toward the negative values from the dust-like value
obtained in the non-interacting holographic dark without torsion, making cosmic accelera-
tion possible even in the weak torsion regime |Φ/H| < 1. Specifically, the phantom divide
ωX = −1 is reached at |Φ/H| ≃0.667 for d = 0.837 and at |Φ/H| ≃0.381 for d = 0.95.
Furthermore, we have found that ˙ωX > 0, which indicates that cosmic acceleration is grad-
ually weakening. This suggests that our model may provide a feasible cosmological scenario
consistent with recent DESI observations.
To prepare for the likelihood analysis, we have investigated light propagation in space-
times with torsion under the geometric optics approximation. Considering both a bundle
of null curves diverging from the source and a bundle converging to the observer, we
have shown that torsion affects light propagation, modifying the standard relation between
redshift and the scale factor and the reciprocity theorem. As a result, the relation be-
tween the luminosity distance dL and the angular diameter distance dA is modified as
dL = dA(1 + z)2(1 + η), where η is the deviation parameter arising from torsion effects.

cutoff. Moreover, a non-interacting limit exists. Overall, our results support the feasibility
