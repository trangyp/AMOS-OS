Hot spots in convex hyperbolic planar domains with
small eigenvalues
Lawford Hatcher
Abstract
We prove a variant of Rauch’s hot spots conjecture for hyperbolic planar
domains with small Neumann or mixed Dirichlet-Neumann eigenvalues. We
conclude, for instance, that on bounded convex domains in the hyperbolic plane
with sufficiently large area, second Neumann Laplace eigenfunctions have no
interior critical points.
1
Introduction
Let H2 denote the hyperbolic plane. Given a domain Ω⊆H2, let −∆denote the
Neumann Laplacian acting on L2(Ω).
Theorem 1.1. Suppose that Ω⊆H2 is a finite-area, geodesically convex domain. If
µ ∈(0, 1/4] is the least element of the positive spectrum of −∆, then any Neumann
eigenfunction corresponding to µ has no interior critical points.
The threshold 1/4 arises naturally as the bottom of the spectrum of the Laplacian
acting on the hyperbolic plane. Below this threshold, there exist smooth solutions
to −∆u = µu on H2 that are globally radially decreasing. We use these functions to
construct test functions that allow us to prove Theorem 1.1 via contradiction.
We explain in Section 3 below that an ideal n-gon Ωin the hyperbolic plane
with at least 18 vertices has a positive Neumann eigenvalue under 1/4. Theorem
1.1 implies that an eigenfunction corresponding to the smallest such eigenvalue has
critical points only in ∂Ω.
Another application of Theorem 1.1 is that large but bounded convex hyperbolic
planar domains have no interior critical points.
Corollary 1.2. There exists A > 0 such that if Ω⊆H2 is a bounded convex domain
with area at least A, then a second Neumann eigenfunction on Ωhas no interior
critical points. The constant A may be taken to be the unique value for which the
hyperbolic disk of area A has second Neumann eigenvalue equal to 1/4.

We also prove a variant of Theorem 1.1 for the mixed problem. Let D ⊆∂Ωbe a
connected subarc of the boundary. Let −∆M denote the mixed Laplacian on Ωwith
Dirichlet boundary conditions on D and Neumann boundary conditions on ∂Ω\ D.
The bottom of the spectrum of −∆M is strictly positive. We also prove
Theorem 1.4. Suppose that Ω⊆H2 is geodesically convex with finite area. If λ ∈
(0, 1/4] is the least element of the spectrum of −∆M, then any mixed eigenfunction
corresponding to λ has no interior critical points.
In particular, as we show in Section 3 below, for any bounded convex domain Ω⊆H2,
there exists ϵ > 0 depending on Ωsuch that if D is connected and has diameter at
most ϵ, then a corresponding first mixed eigenfunction has no interior critical points.
Rauch conjectured in 1974 that a second Neumann eigenfunction on a bounded
Lipschitz domain in Euclidean space has extrema only on the boundary.
This is
widely known as the hot spots conjecture. See [Rau75] and [BB99] for a discussion of
this problem. There have been many recent advancements made on this problem; for
instance [JM20; Roh24; Dio24; CWY26; CGY26].
We recently addressed the hyperbolic version of this conjecture for non-acute
geodesic triangles in H2 [Hat25b]. However, as we point out in [Hat25b], hyperbolic
triangles always have second Neumann eigenvalue exceeding 1/4, so Theorem 1.1 does
not imply the result in this setting.
To our knowledge, Miyamoto [Miy09] was the first to apply the method of proof we
employ here to partially address the hot spots conjecture. We also used this method
to prove the Euclidean version of Theorem 1.4 in [Hat25a]. Rohleder [Roh26] also
recently made use of this sort of argument to prove a result related to the hot spots
conjecture.

The proof of Theorem 1.1 primarily consists of showing that a second Neumann
eigenfunction cannot have an interior critical point at which the function is nonzero.
That the eigenfunction has no nodal critical points is well known, but we provide a
proof in Lemma 2.2 below. Recall that, by the work of Cheng [Che76], the nodal (i.e.
zero-level) set of an eigenfunction is a union of smooth arcs that intersect each other
transversely, and these intersections occur exactly at the set of nodal critical points
(i.e. points at which u = 0 and ∇u = 0 simultaneously) of the eigenfunction.
Lemma 2.2. If u is a Neumann eigenfunction on a simply connected domain Ω⊆H2
whose eigenvalue µ ∈(0, 1/4] is the least element of the positive spectrum, then u has
no nodal critical points.
Proof. We claim that the nodal set of u does not contain a loop. Indeed, suppose to
the contrary that Ω′ is a subdomain of Ωbounded by a loop in the nodal set. Then
the function equal to u in Ω′ and equal to 0 in H2 \ Ω′ defines a test function that
contradicts (1), proving the claim.
If u has a nodal critical point, then near this point, the nodal set of u consists of at
least two transversely intersecting arcs. By the claim, these arcs cannot form a loop
and thus have at least four distinct endpoints in ∂Ω. Since Ωis simply connected, u
has at least four nodal domains, contradicting Courant’s nodal domain theorem.

3
Examples
Here we explain the two examples mentioned in Section 1.
3.1
Ideal polygons with many vertices
Let Ωdenote the interior of the convex hull of n < ∞points in the ideal boundary
∂∞H2. We call Ωan ideal polygon, and we refer to the n points in ∂∞H2 as the
vertices of Ω. We claim that ideal polygons have discrete Neumann spectrum in the
interval (0, 1/4) and, if n ≥18, then Ωhas a Neumann eigenvalue in (0, 1/4). Since Ω
is convex, Theorem 1.1 implies that a second Neumann eigenfunction has no interior
critical points.
First, note that the bottom of the spectrum of the Dirichlet Laplacian on Ωis
greater than 1/4. Otherwise, we could extend a corresponding eigenfunction to equal
0 in H2 \ Ωto create a test function that contradicts (1).
Let S equal the hyperbolic surface obtained by gluing two copies of Ωtogether
along their boundary. Then S is a hyperbolic n-punctured sphere. This surface has
discrete spectrum in the interval [0, 1/4) consisting only of eigenvalues. By Theorem
2 [Zog87], S has a Laplace eigenvalue in (0, 1/4). The eigenfunctions on S can be
decomposed into sums of eigenfunctions that are all even or odd with respect to the
symmetry exchanging the two copies of Ω. The even functions restrict to Neumann
eigenfunctions on Ω, and the odd functions restrict to Dirichlet eigenfunctions. Since
Ωhas no Dirichlet eigenvalues in (0, 1/4), it follows that Ωhas a Neumann eigenvalue
in this interval.
3.2
Large bounded convex domains
Corollary 1.2 is a consequence of Theorem 1.1, Chavel’s extension of the Szeg¨o–
Weinberger inequality to hyperbolic planar domains, and the fact that the second
Neumann eigenvalue of hyperbolic disks tends to 0 as the radius tends to infinity.
Proof of Corollary 1.2. By Theorem 1.1, it suffices to show that there exists A > 0
such that for all bounded convex domains of area at least A, the second Neumann
eigenvalue is at most 1/4. To our knowledge, Chavel was the first to point out in
[Cha80] that Weinberger’s proof [Wei56] of the Szeg¨o-Weinberger inequality can be
extended in a simple way to prove that among simply connected hyperbolic planar
domains of the same area, the disk maximizes the second Neumann eigenvalue. Thus,
it suffices to find a sufficient condition for a hyperbolic disk to have second Neumann
eigenvalue at most 1/4.
Theorem 2 of [LL23] implies that the second Neumann
eigenvalue of a hyperbolic disk is strictly decreasing to 0 in the radius. As the radius
tends to 0, the eigenvalue approaches positive infinity, so there exists a unique r0 ∈
(0, ∞) for which the hyperbolic disk of radius r0 has second Neumann eigenvalue
