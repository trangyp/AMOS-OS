JORDAN PROPERTY OF BIRATIONAL AUTOMORPHISM GROUPS
OF SURFACES AND BIRATIONAL PERMUTATIONS
ALEXANDR ZAITSEV
Abstract. We prove that the group of birational automorphisms of a geometrically
irreducible algebraic surface over a finite field is Jordan. We show that the analogous
statement fails in higher dimensions. Finally, we prove that groups of birational permuta-
tions over finite fields have bounded finite p′-subgroups; in particular, they are p-Jordan.

1. Introduction
Groups of birational automorphisms of algebraic varieties are often complicated. One of
the possible ways to study such groups is to look at their finite subgroups. In particular,
one can study the Jordan property of infinite groups.
Recall that a group Γ is called Jordan if there exists a constant J = J(Γ) such that
every finite subgroup G ⊂Γ contains a normal abelian subgroup of index at most J. The
minimal such J is called a Jordan constant of Γ.
Over fields of characteristic zero, the Jordan property for groups of birational auto-
morphisms is by now well understood in many cases.
In particular, the group of bi-
rational automorphisms of projective plane Bir(P2) is Jordan (see [10, Theor´em`e 3.1]).
More generally, in [9] Prokhorov and Shramov proved that Bir(X) is Jordan for every
non-uniruled variety X, and also showed that birational automorphism groups of ra-
tionally connected varieties of dimension n are uniformly Jordan.
In particular, this
gives the Jordan property for Bir(Pn) over fields of characteristic zero.
The situa-
tion in positive characteristic is different. Already over an algebraically closed field K
of characteristic p > 0 the group PGL2(K) contains simple subgroups isomorphic
to PSL2(Fpr) of arbitrarily large order, so the usual Jordan property fails already for
the group Bir(P1) ≃Aut(P1) ≃PGL2(K). On the other hand, this argument does not
apply over finite fields.
In [8], Prokhorov and Shramov proved that the group Bir(P2
Fq) over a finite field Fq is
Jordan. A natural question here is whether over finite fields one can strengthen this result
and prove the Jordan property for birational automorphism groups of all geometrically
irreducible surfaces. The purpose of the first part of this paper is to answer this question.
Namely, the main result of the paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1. Let S be a geometrically irreducible algebraic surface over Fq. Then the

The key point is to treat surfaces of Kodaira dimension −∞that are not geometrically

In the second part, we establish Jordan-type properties for groups of birational permu-
tations BBir(X) of the variety X over a finite field Fq. Recall that the group BBir(X) is
a subgroup of Bir(X), which consists of birational automorphisms f such that f and f−1
have no Fq-points of indeterminacy. Over finite fields this group was studied by Cantat
in [2] and by Asgarli, Lai, Nakahara, Zimmermann in [1]. In positive characteristic there
are natural analogies for Jordan property and for boundedness of finite subgroups (for
definitions see Section 5). For example, Brauer and Feit, and later Larsen and Pink,
proved that over any field K of positive characteristic p the groups GLn(K) are p-Jordan;
see [7, Theorem 0.4]. Also, p-Jordan property was established for algebraic groups by Hu
in [6] and for Bir(P2) by Chen and Shramov in [3]. We prove the following theorem.
Theorem 1.4. Let Fq be a finite field of characteristic p. Let X be an irreducible variety
over Fq with a Fq-point. Then BBir(X) has bounded p′-subgroups. In particular, BBir(X)
is p-Jordan.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we prove a general statement on bira-
tional automorphism groups of two-dimensional conic bundles over finite fields. In Sec-
tion 3 we apply this result together with bounds on gonality and automorphism groups
of curves to prove Theorem 1.1. In Section 4 we show that the analogous statement does
not extend to higher dimensions by proving Theorem 1.3. Finally, in Section 5 we turn
to groups of birational permutations over finite fields and prove Theorem 1.4.

5. Birational permutations
In this section, we establish boundedness of finite subgroups in groups of birational per-

Definition 5.1. Let X be a variety over a field K. For f ∈Bir(X) denote by Ind(f)
the locus of indeterminacy. Then the group of birational permutations BBir(X) is the
following subgroup
BBir(X) = {f ∈Bir(X) | Ind(f)(K) = Ind(f −1)(K) = ∅}.

Proof (of Theorem 1.4). Denote by N ⩾1 the number of Fq-points on X and denote one
of these points by P. The group BBir(X) acts on Fq-points by permutations. Consider
the stabilizer subgroup BBirP(X) ⊂BBir(X) of P. Clearly, we have
[BBir(X) : BBirP(X)] ⩽N.
Consider a finite p′-subgroup G ⊂BBir(X). Consider the intersection
GP = G ∩BBirP(X).
Then we have [G : GP] ⩽N and GP is a finite p′-subgroup acting on X with a fixed
point P. By Theorem 5.5 we have GP ⊂GL(TP(X)) ≃GLd(Fq), where d is the dimension
of the tangent space at P and
|G| = [G : GP] · |GP| ⩽N · | GLd(Fq)|.
This proves that BBir(X) has bounded finite p′-subgroups.
Also, therefore, BBir(X)
is p-Jordan by Lemma 5.4.
□

ness. For example, one can show that even the finite subgroups of BBir(P1

Fq) are
not bounded.
