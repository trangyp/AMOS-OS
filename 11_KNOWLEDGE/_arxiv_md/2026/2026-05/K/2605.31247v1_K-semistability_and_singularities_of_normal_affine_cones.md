TRAN-TRUNG NGHIEM
Abstract. We provide a complete and optimal characterization of Q-
Gorenstein normal affine singularities in terms of their K-stability in the
sense of Collins–Székelyhidi.
1. Introduction
1.1. Main result. Let Y be a T-variety, i.e. a complex normal affine variety
of dimension n + 1 endowed with the effective action of a torus T [AH06].
When the action is good, we say that (Y, T) is a normal affine cone. In con-
crete terms, this is the cone over a projective variety with a given embedding
in some projective space. In the last 20 years, a notion of K-stability for Y
has been well developed and refined notably in the works [MSY06; MSY08;
RT11; CS18; CS19; LWX21; Wu22]. This condition is expected to charac-
terize the existence of constant scalar curvature Sasaki metrics on the cone’s
base. The metrics have positive scalar curvature if and only if it lifts to a
scalar-flat Kähler cone metric on Y , and conversely any cscK cone metric on
Y must be scalar-flat. In complex geometry, affine cones appear, for exam-
ple, as local tangent cones of isolated singularities; or asymptotic cones of
complete Kähler metrics. Thus, understanding how K-(semi)stability affects
cone singularities would be the first step to understand more general cscK
metrics near local singularities.
On the other hand, seminal results [Oda12; Oda13b; OS15] in the compact
cases show that the K-stability notion, defined in [Tia97; Don02], must sur-
prisingly restrict singularities of a scheme (with Q-Cartier canonical divisor)
to the classes originally introduced to run Mori’s MMP; cf. [KS88; Ale96;
KM98] for a detailed account.
Recall from [Oda13b] that if a normal compact polarized variety (X, L)
is K-semistable, then X must have log canonical singularities (or semi-log
canonical if X is not normal); and Kawamata log terminal singularities (klt)
when L = −KX. Namely, let KX be the canonical divisor on X such that
mKX is Cartier for some m > 0; then X is said to be log canonical if for
one (hence any) resolution X′ →X,

rational singularities [Kol13, Lemma 3.6], hence not log terminal.
Thus, K-semistability for a normal affine cone would at best allow us to
deduce log canonical singularities outside of the apex. On the other hand,
when the variety is an affine cone over a log Fano variety, then K-semistability
should also imply that the apex is a klt singularity as well; that is, any
exceptional divisor dominating the apex must have log discrepancy > −1.
A Q-Gorenstein normal affine cone Y with klt singularities also goes under
the name of Fano cone singularity [LWX21].
This type of singularity is
assumed to be the right category for Calabi–Yau metrics on normal affine
cones [MSY08; CS19].
Our main theorem confirms this expectation and
provides an answer to [Oda24, Problem 2.19].
Theorem A. Let (Y, T) be a Q-Gorenstein normal affine cone. Then if
(Y, T, ξ) is K-semistable, the K-semistable Reeb vector is unique up to scaling
and Y \ {0} has log canonical singularities. More precisely, let a1(ξ) be the
slope of the K-semistable pair (Y, ξ) (cf.
Proposition 2.12), we have the
following trichotomy:
(1) If a1(ξ) < 0 then (Y, T, ξ) is K-stable iff Y \ {0} is log canonical.
(2) If a1(ξ) = 0 then (Y, T, ξ) is K-stable iff Y \ {0} is klt.
(3) If a1(ξ) > 0 and (Y, T, ξ) is K-semistable, then Y is itself klt; i.e.,
(Y, T) is a Fano cone singularity.
In case 1 (resp. 2), (Y, T) is in fact the cone over a log pair of general type
(resp. log Calabi–Yau polarized variety). The Reeb cone is one-dimensional
in both cases.
Of course a similar result also holds for a (non-normal) affine cone pair
(Y, D), where D is an effective Weil divisor such that KY + D is Q-Cartier.
Since the proof can be repeated without any extra difficulty, we omit it for
convenience and only note here some metric interpretation.
When (Y, D) is a pair of log Fano cone singularity, K-stability is equiva-
lent to (Y, D) admitting Ricci-flat Kähler cone metrics on Y \D with cone
singularities over D. This has been known from the works [MSY08; CS19;
Li21; Hua22] (see also [BL22; Ngh25] when the log pair has a spherical ac-
tion, and [GP16; OS15] for the compact case). Moreover, it is also known
that it suffices to test K-stability of log Fano cone singularities on special
test configurations [LW25].

When the cone is not a log Fano singularity but still Q-Gorenstein, our
result implies that K-stability is equivalent to the (singular) Sasaki base
admitting Sasaki–Einstein metrics of the same Einstein constant as the slope
sign. This has been known when Y \ {0} is smooth, in which case the Reeb
cone is one-dimensional [BG08, Theorem 8.1.14], hence K-stability of Y boils
down to K-stability of a log Calabi–Yau pair or a log pair of general type.
In general, the stability of a cone pair (Y, D)—not assumed to be Q-
Gorenstein, should be related to existence of constant scalar curvature Sasaki
metrics on the (singular) Sasaki link of Y with conical singularities along the
Sasaki base of D; see [CS18; ACL21] for some partial results in the metric-
to-stability direction. The K-stability notion for a pair also goes under the
name of log K-stability [OS15; AHZ25].
1.2. Towards singularities of cscK cones. Throughout the proof of The-
orem A, we have crucially used the Q-Gorenstein condition to define discrep-
ancy [KM98]. As it turns out, K-semistability can still be defined without the
Q-Gorenstein assumption [CS18] (see also [Wu22] for a definition of Monge–
Ampère energy for an arbitrary polarized cone). Moreover, K-semistability
is implied by the existence of cscK cone metrics on Y \ {0} [CS18, Corollary
1.1]. Examples of non-Q-Gorenstein cscK toric cones have been built by E.
Legendre [Leg11]. An interesting question is to see whether K-stability of a
general cone imposes restrictions on singularities in a more general sense.
Note that when Y is a Ricci-flat cone in the sense of [EGZ09], then Q-
Gorenstein follows from van Coevering’s theorem when Y has an isolated
singularity [Coe10]. A recent work of Hallgren–Székelyhidi shows that this
also holds when the singularities are defined in a reasonable sense that in-
cludes the klt case [HS25].
In contrast with these results, our following
remark suggests that a K-stable cone with cscK but not Ricci-flat metric is
never Q-Gorenstein, no matter how we define a priori singular cscK metrics
so that they coincide with the klt notion in [EGZ09].
Theorem 1.2. Assume that (Y, T) is a K-stable Q-Gorenstein cone with
K-stable Reeb vector ξ.
Assume also that any pair of cscK cone metrics
with the same Reeb vector ξ is related by an automorphism preserving the
Reeb vector. Then any cscK cone metric on Y \ {0} with Reeb vector ξ is
Ricci-flat.
Remark 1.3. Here, our arguments rely on a strong uniqueness modulo
automorphisms assumption. The latter holds when the Sasaki link is smooth
(i.e., the cone has an isolated singularity) [BB17; HL21]. We expect this
uniqueness property to also hold in the singular case (details will appear
elsewhere).
In the toric examples built in [Leg11], KY is not Q-Cartier but KY +D is
always Q-Cartier for a suitable choice of an effective boundary D, which can
be modified so that (Y, D) is log canonical [CLS11]. Based on this evidence,

Definition 2.2.
• (Normal affine cone) Given a normal affine variety Y with a good T-
action, we call the pair (Y, T) a normal affine cone. A pair decorated
with a Reeb vector (Y, T, ξ) is a polarized cone.
• (Q-Gorenstein cone) The normal affine cone is said to be Q-Gorenstein
if KY is Q-Cartier; namely there is m ∈N such that mKY is a
Cartier divisor. A Q-Gorenstein cone with klt singularities is called
a Fano cone singularity.
• We say that ξ is quasi-regular if there is c ∈R such that cξ ∈Nk;
otherwise ξ is said to be irregular. Equivalently, ξ is quasi-regular if
it generates a S1-action of rank 1; if this action is moreover free, we
say that ξ is regular.
Remark 2.3.
(1) In [Oda24, Definition 2.9 (iii)], a Q-Gorenstein cone is defined to be a
reduced scheme which is normal crossing in codimension 1, satisfying
Serre’s S2 condition, with Q-Cartier canonical bundle. The Serre’s
S2 condition is implied by our normality assumption ; see [Ser65,
Chap. IV.4].
(2) We distinguish between the conditions Gorenstein and Q-Gorenstein
of index 1. The former means that KY is Cartier and Y is Cohen–
Macaulay (CM) (cf. [BH93, Definition 2.1.1]); while the latter only
means that KY is Cartier. A Q-Gorenstein klt singularity is CM (in
particular a Fano cone singularity is CM); but a cone over a polarized
abelian variety of dimension at least 2 is not CM, although it is Q-
Gorenstein of index 1. We refer the reader to [Kol13, Corollary 3.4]
for several CM criteria and examples.
