Klein tunneling of the laser coherence
Konstantin Manannikov,1 Sagie Gadasi,1 Ekaterina Mironova,1 Eran
Bernstein,1 Andrei Poliakov,1 Nir Davidson,1 and Alexander N. Poddubny1, ∗
1Department of Physics of Complex Systems, Weizmann Institute of Science, Israel, Rehovot 7610001
We study theoretically the lasing synchronization of the two arrays of lasers with the complex
mode dispersion, separated by a spectrally detuned barrier. We demonstrate that for lasing at the
Dirac point, the synchronization persists for an order of magnitude higher barriers than in the arrays
with a usual parabolic dispersion or a purely dissipative coupling. We interpret this effect as the
Klein tunneling of the laser coherence through the barrier. Our numerical findings are supported
by an analysis of the delocalization of the linearized eigenmodes of the arrays, which enhances the
synchronization.
Understanding whether multiple lasers can synchro-
nize despite the inevitable noise, such as quenched dis-
order in laser resonant frequencies, is an essential prob-
lem both for basic nonlinear physics [1] and for practical
applications [2].
For nearest-neighbor dissipative cou-
pling between the lasers, the synchronization condition
is determined by the competition between noise and the
coupling strength [3]. Synchronization can be enhanced
when the coupling between the lasers has both disper-
sive and dissipative components and is characterized by a
non-Hermitian effective Hamiltonian H ̸= H†[4–6]. This
complex coupling can now be realized in practice, for ex-
ample, in a degenerate cavity laser setup [7–9] or coupled
polariton condensates [10, 11].
Laser synchronization with complex coupling has been
studied so far only for laser arrays with a simple mode dis-
persion [4–6]. More complex band structures, potentially
useful for lasing applications, can be inspired by topolog-
ical photonics, which uses mode dispersion as a tool to
engineer edge states of light. Topological photonics has
now matured from initial demonstrations of propagating
electromagnetic edge states [12–15] to advanced studies
of quantum [16, 17], nonlinear [18], non-Hermitian[19],
and active structures [20–22]. Lasing of topological pho-
tonic edge states was demonstrated [20] and later shown
to be robust to disorder [23, 24]. A Dirac band structure
with tailored photon loss has been put forward to allow
scalable single-mode surface-emitting lasing [25, 26].
Despite this progress, one phenomenon intrinsic to
Dirac mode dispersion has not been investigated in the
context of lasing – chiral Klein tunneling [27, 28].
In
Klein tunneling, originally proposed for relativistic quan-
tum particles, particles with linear dispersion can cross a
potential barrier with a tunneling amplitude that is inde-
pendent of the barrier thickness. While photonic coun-
terparts of Klein tunneling have been recently studied
[29–32], its impact on active lasing structures and their
coherence remains unclear.
In this Letter, we theoretically investigate an analog
of the Klein tunneling between two coupled laser arrays
separated by a frequency-detuned barrier. We consider
a one-dimensional ring geometry [Fig. 1(a)] and a two
dimensional hexagonal lattice geometry [Fig. 4(a)]. We
tune the complex coupling between lasers such that both
geometries lase at the K point of the Brillouin zone with
gapless Dirac (linear) dispersion. We then use rigorous

Laser synchronization through a barrier.
In the absence of disorder, any finite dissipative cou-
pling τ1 ensures that the lasers in each region of Fig. 1(a)
are always synchronized. However, lasers in region L will
synchronize with lasers in region R only if the coher-
ence can tunnel through the frequency-detuned barrier
separating them. We study this coherence tunneling for
gapless linear dispersion and for parabolic dispersion in
search of an analog to the Klein tunneling effect.

Fig. 2(c) shows that for wide barriers the critical barrier

Similar to the one-dimensional case of Fig. 1(b), we
have performed a detailed analysis of the linearized prob-
lem. This analysis, detailed in Sec. S4 in Ref. [34], con-
firms that the underlying mechanism for synchronization
is the delocalization of the lowest-loss eigenmodes. The
lasing field profile is determined by the competition be-
tween the sample anisotropy and the nonlinearity. The
anisotropy aims to split the modes with different sym-
metries inside the unit cell (which plays the role of the
mode polarization), and the nonlinearity favors the chiral
modes px ±ipy, d2xy ±idx2−y2 with the same intensity at
all the lasers. An example of the near field is shown in
Fig. 3(c) and Fig. S3 in Ref. [34]. The synchronized laser
mode has a chiral phase pattern with a vortex of charge
1 in each unit cell.

Summary.
We demonstrated that frequency syn-
chronization between two laser arrays, separated by
a frequency-detuned barrier, is significantly enhanced
when lasing at the gapless linear dispersion point (in
1D) and at the Dirac point (in 2D). The lasing synchro-
nization persists as long as the eigenmodes of the linear
system remain coupled through the barrier, resulting in
Klein tunneling of the laser coherence. Our findings could
open new avenues to investigate the impact of the com-
plex photonic band structure on laser synchronization.
An interesting future research direction is the interplay
between disorder, artificial gauge fields, and spin-orbit
interactions [9]. Our results could be experimentally ver-
ified using a degenerate cavity laser setup [37].

S2.
PROBABILITY OF DESYNCHRONIZATION
AND CRITICAL BARRIER HEIGHT ∆∗
B
In this section, we describe the procedure for quantify-
ing frequency synchronization in numerical calculations.
We take a laser with the index i from the right side of
the array and another one with the index j from the left
side, and denote their phase difference as ∆ij. We are
interested in their phase fluctuations over time (practi-
cally over the 1000 cavity roundtrip times) σt[∆ij]. We
denote the results of averaging over different pairs (i,
j) as ⟨σt[∆ij]⟩pairs. We define the lasers to be synchro-
nized if ⟨σt[∆ij]⟩pairs < 0.2. To calculate the probabil-
ity of desynchronization Pdesync, we average over differ-
ent random initial conditions (typically around 200 re-
alizations).
To calculate critical barrier height ∆∗
B we
first fix some initial barrier height ∆B and calculate
⟨σt[∆ij]⟩pairs,init_cond. If this value is above 0.2, it means
that ∆B > ∆∗
B, and vice versa. Then we perform a bi-
nary search while |⟨σt[∆ij]⟩pairs,init_cond −0.2| > 0.02.

split modes. It is this competition that determines the
complicated profile of the lasing mode.
