1. INTRODUCTION
1.1. ADHM spaces and Gieseker varieties. So-called ADHM spaces [AHDM78] form a fun-
damental class of algebraic varieties that were used to describe framed U(r)-instantons on
S4 = R4 ∪{∞} by means of linear algebra. The ADHM space has a natural partial compactifi-
cation which is a non-singular algebraic variety. Following [Nek03], we will call it the Gieseker
space or the Gieseker variety and denote G(n, r). It also admits a description as a moduli space of
framed torsion-free sheaves on P2(C) of rank r and second Chern class n [NY03]. This variety
has been used extensively by physicists. For instance, [Nek03] defined instanton partition
functions as equivariant localizations on the Gieseker space. Nakajima quiver varieties [Nak94]
are a natural generalization of these spaces.
1.2. Relation to Higgs/Coulomb branches and symplectic duality. The Gieseker space is a
symplectic resolution of the singularities of the Higgs branch of the 3d N = 4 quiver gauge
theory corresponding to the Jordan quiver. In [KS25], the first and third authors proposed
a general approach that allowed them to identify equivariant cohomology of the Gieseker
space with the algebra of functions on the schematic C×-fixed points of the corresponding
BFN Coulomb branch ([BFN18]) thus proving the non-quantized equivariant Hikita conjecture
relating Higgs and Coulomb branches (see, for example, [Kam22, Section 5.6], where the
quantized version is formulated). In [KS25], first and third authors used a similar approach to
identify the equivariant cohomology of the Gieseker space with the center of the degenerate
cyclotomic Hecke algebra resulting in a new simple proof of the main result of [SVV17], see
also [Web15].

1.3. Generalization to K-theory. It’s natural to replace the equivariant cohomology of the
Gieseker space by the equivariant K-theory. A K-theoretic version of the Hikita conjecture was
formulated in [DK25] (see also [Zho23, Appendix B]) and one could ask whether it holds for
the Gieseker space and whether the equivariant K-theory of this space allows for a description
in terms of cyclotomic Hecke algebras, similar to [SVV17], [Web15] and [KS25].
1.4. Main result. The main goal of this paper is to apply the approach of [KS25] to obtain an
algebraic description of the equivariant K-theory of the Gieseker space (see Theorem A below)
which is precisely the K-theoretic analog of the main theorem of [SVV17].
Initially, the Iwahori-Hecke algebra is a deformation of the group algebra of the symmetric
group. The cyclotomic Hecke algebra is a quotient of the affine Hecke algebra of the symmetric
group introduced for all complex reflection groups by Brou´e and Malle [BM93]. At the same
time, Ariki and Koike defined a Hecke algebra for the complex reflection group G(r, 1, n) that
generalizes the Iwahori-Hecke algebra of types A and B [AK94]. This algebra turned out to
be the cyclotomic Hecke algebra for affine type A. In [Pae25], the second author described
combinatorial correspondences between a fixed point locus of the Gieseker space and blocks of
a certain specialized Ariki-Koike algebra.
Here we propose a common framework to generalize these observations. The main theorem
of this paper is:
Theorem A (Theorem 3.15). The equivariant K-theory of the Gieseker space is isomorphic to the
Jucys-Murphy’s center of the cyclotomic Hecke algebra, as an algebra over the equivariant K-theory of a
point.
In particular, we obtain a basis in the Jucys-Murphy’s center coming from the geometry (see
Remark 3.16).
1.5. Relation to the center conjecture. The following conjecture was formulated in [McG12,
Conjecture 3.1] and is still open in general (see Section 4.3 for the list of cases when the
conjecture is known).
Conjecture 1.1. For q ̸= 1, the Jucys-Murphy’s center of the cyclotomic Hecke algebra coincides with
its center.
In [GP00, Lemma 7.1.7] (see also [HS26, Equation (2.5)]) it’s proven that the center of the
cyclotomic Hecke algebra is dual to the cocenter under a certain natural pairing. Note that
the cocenter is a “simpler” object than the JM-center from the algebraic point of view as, for
example, it is known to have an explicit basis (see [HS26, Theorem 1.4.1]). Note now that the
equivariant K-theory of the Gieseker space pairs perfectly with the equivariant K-theory of a
certain closed projective subvariety of the Gieseker space that we will denote by L(n, r) (this
is the general feature of Nakajima quiver varieties, see [Nak01, Theorem 7.3.5]). So, Theorem
A implies that the Jucys-Murphy’s center is dual to the equivariant K-theory of L(n, r). We
conclude that Conjecture 1.1 is equivalent to the following conjecture (compare with [SVV17,
Theorem 3.37]).

Conjecture 1.2. There exists and isomorphism between the equivariant K-theory of L(n, r) and the
cocenter of the cyclotomic Hecke algebra compatible with the natural pairings.
So, in a sense, one can reduce Conjecture 1.1 to the statement similar to the main theorem
we prove in this paper. Alternatively, if one proves Conjecture 1.1 then Conjecture 1.2 would
follow immediately. At the moment, we do not know how to prove Conjecture 1.2 (see Section
4 for more details).
1.6. Corollaries of the main result. In [Pae25] second author identifies components of fixed
points in Gieseker space at roots of unity (also described as affine type A quiver varieties)
with blocks of the cyclotomic Hecke algebra (at the same roots of unity). After specializing the
parameters to roots of unity, we naturally recover the aforementioned bijection observed in
[Pae25] as a corollary of Theorem A. Moreover, up to Conjecture 1.1, we obtain the following
upgrade of [Pae25].
Theorem B (Theorem 5.4). The K-theory of an affine type A quiver variety is isomorphic, as a
C-algebra, to the center of the corresponding block of the specialized cyclotomic Hecke algebra.
Remark 1.3. A related result with K-theory being replaced by cohomology was obtained by Webster in [Web15,
Proposition 3.9] by using different methods. Webster identifies (equivariant) cohomology of affine type A quiver
varieties with centers of certain KLR-algebras.
For r = 1, the Gieseker space G(n, r) becomes isomorphic to the Hilbert scheme Hilbn(A2)
of n points on A2. In this case, the affine cyclotomic Hecke algebra becomes equal to the Hecke
algebra of type A. Moreover, by [FG06, Theorem 7.2] for q ̸= 1 (i.e., after localizing q −1) the
Jucys-Murphy’s-center of the type A Hecke algebra identifies with its center. So, Theorem A in
this case claims the following.
Corollary C (Corollary 5.1). The equivariant K-theory of the Hilbert scheme Hilbn(A2) is isomorphic
to the Jucys-Murphy’s-center of the type A Hecke algebra. For q ̸= 1, this is also the center.
Warning 1.4. What we call Jucys-Murphy’s center of the type A Hecke algebra is not equal to what
is called by the same name in [FG06, Theorem 7.2]. In their notation, our Jucys-Murphy’s center is
generated by Li’s while their Jucys-Murphy’s center is generated by Li such that Li = (q −1)Li + 1.
They coincide for q ̸= 1 but their specializations to q = 1 are different.
1.7. Structure of the paper. This paper is structured as follows. In §2 we recall the definition
and some facts about Gieseker space. In the §3 we prove Theorem A. In §4 we reformulate
Conjecture 1.1 in terms of the identification between the cocenter Tr(Hn,r) of Hn,r and the
equivariant K-theory of L(n, r) and prove that the former embeds into the latter. Finally, in
§5 we obtain various corollaries of the Theorem A. Namely, in the special case of r = 1, we
identify the equivariant K-theory of Hilbn(A2) with the Jucys-Murphy’s center of the type A
Hecke algebra. For general r we then proceed to study the special cases q = 1 and q begin a
root of unity. For the latter we prove Theorem 5.4.
