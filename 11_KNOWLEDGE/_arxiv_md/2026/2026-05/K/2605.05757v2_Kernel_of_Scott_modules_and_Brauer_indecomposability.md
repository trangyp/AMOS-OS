Kernel of Scott modules and Brauer indecomposability
Lin Wua
a12431015@mail.sustech.edu.cn
Abstract
Let k be an algebraically closed field of a prime characteristic p. Let G be a finite
group. We investigate the Brauer indecomposability of Scott kG-modules in relation
to the kernel of modules. We generalize a criterion for Brauer indecomposability. We
also prove that, in certain cases, Brauer indecomposability of a Scott kG-module can be
lifted from that of a Scott module over a p-local subgroup.

Introduction
Let G be a finite group and let p be a prime number. Let k be an algebraically closed field
with char(k) = p.
Let P ≤G be a p-subgroup.
For any finite dimensional kG-module
M, the Brauer construction BrP(M) = M(P) is a kNG(P)-module (for backgroud of Brauer
construction, we refer the reader to [16]). If for any p-subgroup Q ≤G, ResNG(Q)
QCG(Q)M(Q) is
indecomposable or equals to 0, we say M is Brauer indecomposable.
In 2011, Kessar, Kunugi and Mitsuhashi introduced the concept of Bruaer indecompos-
ability in [5]. They gave this definition for the purpose of studying gluing processes, which is
a method for studying categorical equivalence between p-blocks of finite groups (see [6, 10]).
Broué ’s gluing method [3, Theorem 6.3] gives us a suﬀicient condition for S(G × H, ∆P) to
induce a stable equivalence of Morita type between two principal blocks. If this is the case,
we always have that S(G × H, ∆P) is Brauer indecomposable, and no exceptions are known
so far. For reference, one could see Koshitani and Lassueur’s works [7–9]. So it is important
to know whether the Scott module is Brauer indecomposable or not.
Regarding the Brauer indecomposability of Scott modules, one may refer to [4,5,15]. Our
present paper primarily builds upon Ishioka and Kunugi’s work [4], Koshitani and Tuvay’s
work [11,15] to establish a series of relationships between the kernel of a Scott module and its
Brauer indecomposability.
For subgroups Q, R ≤G, we let
HomG(Q, R) = {ϕ : Q →R | ϕ is induced by a conjugation}.
(1)
For a p-subgroup P ≤G, the fusion system FP(G) is the category whose objects are the
subgroups of P and whose morphism set from Q to R is HomG(Q, R).
In [12–14], Koshitani and Tuvay study the Brauer indecomposability of Scott modules with
an explict vertex P, where they make use of [4, Theorem 1.3], and we introduce this theorem
here.

From the conclusions in [4, 15], we observe the following fact: in certain cases, when we
want to determine the Brauer indecomposability of the Scott module S(G, P), the subgroups
of core(P) are not important; what matters are the subgroups between core(P) and P. The
following two theorems are intended to illustrate this fact.
There is a generalization of [4, Theorem 1.3].
Theorem 1.2. Let G be a finite group and P a p-subgroup of G. Let M = S(G, P) and
suppose that FP(G) is saturated. TFAE:
(1) M is Brauer indecomposable.
(2) ResNG(Q)
QCG(Q)S(NG(Q), NP(Q)) is indecomposable for each fully normalized subgroup Q
satisfying core(P) ≤Q ≤P.
We introduce a reformulation of [15, Corollary 1.2].
Theorem 1.3. Let G be a finite group and P a p-subgroup. Let M = S(G, P). We assume
Fp(G) is saturated. Let R ◁G be a normal subgroup such that R ≤P ∩ker(M). Let
A = {T ≤P | R ≤T and T is a maximal subgroup of P}.
(3)
If for any T ∈A we have ResG
CG(T)M is indecomposable, then M is Brauer indecomposable.
In particular, core(P) ≤P ∩ker(M), and any subgroup R satisfying the above conditions is
contained in core(P).
We will see that core(P) ≤ker(S(G, P)) is always true. Now we impose an additional
condition on the kernel of Scott module: P ≤ker(S(G, P)).
Koshitani and Tuvay investigate the lifting property of Brauer indecomposability of Scott
modules from a subgroup to the whole group in their preprint [11].
We use the method
of [11, Theorem 1.1], but change the conditions, and finally obtain the following result:
Theorem 1.4. Let G be a finite group and let P be a p-subgroup. Assume P ≤ker(S(G, P))
and NG(P) is a p-group. If S(NG(P), P) is Brauer indecomposable, then S(G, P) is Brauer
indecomposable.
