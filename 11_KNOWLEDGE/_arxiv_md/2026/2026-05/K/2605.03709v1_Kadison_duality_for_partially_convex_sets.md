KADISON DUALITY FOR PARTIALLY CONVEX SETS
TEA ˇSTREKELJ
Abstract. This paper extends the Kadison duality between compact convex sets and function
systems to the setting of partial convexity.
A partially convex set is a set that is convex in a
designated set of “convex” variables when the others are held fixed.
We introduce the notion
of a regular partially convex set and identify its dual as a finitely generated free module over a
commutative C∗-algebra endowed with a compatible Archimedean order unit structure. We call
such spaces free order unit modules. We prove that for any compact regular partially convex set K,
the space of continuous functions that are affine in the convex variables is the canonical example of
such a module. Conversely, we show that the partially convex state space of a free order unit module
is a compact regular partially convex set. Our main result establishes a categorical duality between
compact regular partially convex sets and free order unit modules.
We also establish a Stone-
Weierstrass-type theorem, demonstrating that partially affine polynomials are dense in the space of
continuous partially affine functions on any compact regular partially convex set. Finally, we prove
a Hahn-Banach-type separation theorem of compact partially convex sets from their outer points.

Key words and phrases. convex set, affine function, Archimedean order unit space, state space, Kadison duality,
partially convex set, partially affine polynomial, continuous partially affine function, free order unit module.

1. Introduction
Convexity is a simple but powerful concept originating from geometry.
It gives theoretical
background to many research areas in mathematics, such as linear programming, computational
geometry and probability. A subset of a vector space is convex if it contains the line segment joining
any two of its points, or equivalently, if it is closed under convex combinations. In modern analysis,
the importance of convexity often lies in its dual relationship with the space of affine functions
defined upon the set.
The cornerstone result in this direction is Kadison’s duality [Alf71]. Specifically, this duality
establishes a canonical bijection between compact convex sets and function systems, which are
unital, closed, self-adjoint subspaces of continuous functions. For a compact convex set K, the
associated function system is the space of continuous affine functions on K. Conversely, any abstract
function system R finds a geometric realization in its state space S(R), the set of all unital positive
functionals on R.
This correspondence allows for a rich interplay: the properties of the affine
functions on K provide insight into the structure of K itself, and in turn, the geometric properties
of K can be reflected in the behavior of the corresponding affine functions.
Building on this classical foundation, there have been many generalizations of convexity devel-
oped for more complex geometric contexts [MM96, Nai04]. A well-established noncommutative
notion of convexity is matrix convexity [Wit84, EW97, WW99, HM12, HKM17, HL21, DK25]. In
this setting, single convex sets are replaced by families of matrix sets across all dimensions, con-
nected via direct sums and isometric conjugations. Just as classical convexity is linked to function
systems via the Kadison duality, matrix convexity finds its natural noncommutative counterpart in
operator systems via the Webster-Winkler duality [WW99]. Hence, by studying matrix convexity
one introduces several convex-geometric ideas and tools to understand operator spaces, systems
and algebras [Pau02, DM05, Arv08, DK15].
Recently, generalized notions of noncommutative convexity have been studied [HHLM08, DHM17,
JKMMP21, JKMMP22, KMˇS+], where the underlying sets are convex only in a subset of variables.
Formally, the geometry of these sets is encoded by a tuple Γ of noncommutative polynomials. The
core idea is that while classical convex sets are intersections of half-planes, these Γ-convex sets are
defined by systems of polynomial inequalities involving the components of Γ.
The aim of this paper is to establish an analogue of Kadison’s duality for partially convex sets,
a prominent instance of Γ-convex sets.
1.1. Partial convexity. Let V = Rn+m be a finite-dimensional real vector space. An element of
V is denoted by z = (x, y) with x ∈Rn and y ∈Rm. For a subset K ⊆V let πY (K) = {y ∈
Rm | ∃x ∈Rn : (x, y) ∈K} be the projection of K onto Rm. The projection πX(K) on Rn is defined
similarly.
Definition 1.1. A set K ⊆Rn+m is partially convex in x if for any fixed y0 ∈πY (K), the slice
Ky0 = {x ∈X | (x, y0) ∈K} is a convex set.
Example 1.2. All convex sets are partially convex. Similarly, a disjoint union of convex sets is
partially convex in x as long as the sets do not overlap vertically (i.e., they share no y-coordinates).

2x2 = y2 + 1.
We will consider spaces of functions on a partially convex set K endowed with the supremum

5.8). Finally, Section 6 provides the proof of Theorem 1.5.
