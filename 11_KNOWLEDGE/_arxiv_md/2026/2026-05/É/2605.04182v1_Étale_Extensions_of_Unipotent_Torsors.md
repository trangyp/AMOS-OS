ÉTALE EXTENSIONS OF UNIPOTENT TORSORS
GABRIEL BASSAN
Abstract. In this paper we study extension problems for torsors in positive
characteristic. Let F be a field of characteristic p > 0 and U/F be a unipotent
algebraic group. As our first main result, we prove that every U-torsor defined
over the generic point of a discrete valuation ring OK, containing a field F,
extends to the normalization of OK in some finite separable extension of its
fraction field. We then globalize this result and prove that for X/F a normal
integral curve over an algebraically closed field F, every U-torsor over an open
set X◦⊆X extends to some ramified cover of X which is étale over X◦. As an
application, we are able to find isomorphisms between certain unipotent variants
of Nori’s fundamental group scheme for curves.

Introduction
Let X be a normal integral scheme, X◦⊆X a dense open subscheme, and G a
group scheme over X. A basic question that appears in several guises (compactifi-
cations of torsors, ramification theory, and “tame/wild” variants of fundamental
groups for example) is the following:
Question. Given a G-torsor P →X◦, can one extend it across the boundary to
all of X?
Although on nice situations (X/F a normal integral variety over a field and
G/F a smooth connected algebraic group for example), one can extend torsors by
normalizing X in the function field of P, the resulting X-scheme usually cease to
be a torsor due to ramification behavior on the boundary.

Example. Let n > 1 be an integer. Let F be a field such that char(F) ∤n and
F contain the n-th roots of unity. Consider X := P1, X◦:= X \ {0, ∞} and the
µn-torsor
P := Spec F[t, t−1, T]/(T n −t) →X◦≃Spec F[t, t−1].
Taking the normalization of X in the function field of P, we obtain (up to isomor-
phism) the n-th power morphism P1 →P1, t 7→tn. This map is ramified at 0 and
∞and therefore, cannot be a µn-torsor.
Nonetheless, for a finite étale group G, one can bypass the ramification issue by
passing to some finite étale cover V →X◦and show that every G-torsor extends
to the normalization of X in (the fraction field of) some finite étale cover V (see
Proposition 1.12). This suggests the following refined question.
Question. Given a G-torsor P →X◦, can one extend it to some finite cover of
X ramified only across the boundary X \ X◦?
In characteristic 0, since every finite group scheme is étale, the answer is always
affirmative for finite groups. In characteristic p > 0, where finite non-étale group
schemes can occur naturally, the question becomes more delicate, even for finite
group schemes. Our main goal is to show that, nevertheless, torsors under unipotent
groups over curves do admit extensions after passing to some cover ramified outside
of X◦. Our first result answers a local version of this question for unipotent groups.
Theorem A (Theorem 3.14). Let OK be a DVR, containing a field F of character-
istic p > 0, with fraction field K and perfect residue field. Let U/F be a unipotent
group and P →Spec K be a U-torsor. Then, there exists a finite separable exten-
sion L/K such that the base change PL →Spec L extends to the normalization of
OK in L.
We then globalize this result and obtain the following theorem.
Theorem B (Theorem 4.4). Let X/F be a normal integral curve over an al-
gebraically closed field F of characteristic p > 0 and U/F be a unipotent group
scheme. Let X◦⊆X be a nonempty open set and P →X◦be a U-torsor. Then,
there exists a finite surjective morphism Y →X which is étale over X◦such that
P extends to Y .
As an application, we explain how to define a natural intermediate fundamental
group scheme πn(X◦) (which has been previously defined in [BKP22]), sitting
between the Nori fundamental group of X◦and X, i.e
πN(X◦) ↠πn(X◦) ↠πN(X)
and prove that the “gap” between πN(X◦) and πn(X◦) has “no unipotent part”.
More precisely, we prove the following result.
Theorem C (Theorem 5.12). If X/F is a normal separated integral curve over
an algebraically closed field F of characteristic p > 0 and X◦⊆X is a nonempty
open set, then the morphism
πN(X◦) ↠πn(X◦)
becomes an isomorphism after passing to the maximal pro-unipotent quotients.

Organization of the paper.
• In Section 1 we go through some preliminary notions. In 1.1 we give a review
on nonabelian cohomology, with an important emphasis on the obstruction
to lifting torsors in an exact sequence.
This gives the cohomological
backbone of our extension arguments in the following sections. In 1.2
we precisely define and obtain some simple results on étale extensions
of torsors. In 1.3 we recall the ramification behavior of Artin–Schreier
extensions.
• Section 2 is devoted to proving a local version of our extension result
for discrete valuation rings and the group αp. This is mostly done by
explicit calculations involving uniformizers of Artin–Schreier extensions.
This section provides the initial results needed to start the more abstract
machinery used in the following sections.
• In Section 3 we extend the results of Section 2 to unipotent groups, but
still over a DVR (Theorem A). For this, we make a “dévissage” argument
using the machinery of nonabelian cohomology to reduce the theorems to
statements of the preceding section.
• In Section 4 we are able to extend our results to curves and prove Theorem
B by using the results of Section 3 to find local extensions of a torsor along
the boundary of an open set and then use a Riemann–Roch argument to
find a suitable cover which allows us to glue the local extensions.
• In the last section, Section 5, we show some applications (Theorem C) of
our results to the Nori fundamental group scheme of open curves.

Notation and conventions
Throughout this paper, a ring R will always be unital commutative. A variety
over a field F means a separated F-scheme of finite type. A curve over a field F is
a variety of dimension 1. Every group scheme G/X is supposed to be fppf over its
base scheme X. An algebraic group G over a field F is an F-group scheme. An
algebraic group G/F is said to be unipotent if it admits a composition series over
F with successive quotients isomorphic to subgroups of Ga.
All torsors and cohomology groups are taken in the fppf topology. A G-torsor
P →X means a right G-torsor.
Given a field K, a valuation on K will always be assumed to be discrete (with
valuation group a discrete subgroup of R). In this setup, we usually denote the
valuation ring of v by OK and its residue field by k. For a finite extension L/K we
usually denote an extension of v to L by vL and we write e(vL|v) for the ramification
index and f(vL|v) for the residue degree. By a normalized valuation we mean a
valuation with image equal to Z ⊆R. Since every discrete subgroup of R is of the
form αZ ⊆R, we can always normalize (non trivial) valuations in a unique way.
We will sometimes say that a normalized valuation vL extends some normalized

1. Preliminaries and auxiliary results
We start by covering some preliminaries. We will not necessarily give full proofs
of propositions, but sketches and references will be given.
1.1. Nonabelian cohomology and twists.
Notation 1.1. Let G be a group scheme over a scheme X, we denote by
H1(X, G)
the set of isomorphism classes of (right) G-torsors (not necessarily representable).
This set has a preferred point given by the trivial torsor. With this, we will usually
look at H1(X, G) as a pointed set.
Remark 1.2. If G is commutative, this group agrees with the first cohomology of
G as an abelian sheaf on the fppf site of X.
Definition 1.3. Let G be a group scheme over X. Let P be a (right) G-torsor,
and let Y →X be an X-scheme equipped with a (left) G-action a: G ×X Y →Y .
Define the twist (or contracted product) of Y by P to be the fppf sheaf
P,aY := P ×G,a Y := (P ×X Y )/G,
where G acts (on the left) on P ×X Y by
g · (p, y) := (p · g−1, a(g) · y).
When no confusion arises, we will omit the action a in the notation and denote
the twist simply by P Y or P ×G Y .
If f : G →H is a morphism of X-group schemes, and P →X is a G-torsor,
the same construction yields an H-torsor PH := f∗P := P ×G H, where G acts
on H through f. We call this torsor the induced torsor. Twisting is compatible
with morphisms of X-group schemes H →G, functorial with respect to Y and
compatible with base change X′ →X by an fppf morphism.
Remark 1.4. Observe that given a class α ∈H1(X, G), two different represen-
tatives P and Q of α, give rise to isomorphic twists, but not in a unique way.
In this sense, the construction really depends on the torsor and not only in its
cohomology class. Nonetheless, we will sometimes write α Y to mean a twist by
some representative of α.

1.2. Étale extensions of torsors. We begin by recalling the notion of étale
extensions of torsors.
Definition 1.10. Let X be a normal integral scheme, X◦⊆X a dense open
subscheme and G/X an X-group scheme. Given P →X◦a G-torsor, we say
that P étale extends to X (or is étale extendable) if there exists a connected
normal scheme Y and a finite surjective morphism f : Y →X étale over X◦
together with a GY -torsor PY →Y whose restriction to f −1(X◦) is (isomorphic
to) f ∗P →f −1(X◦). We say that any such PY is an étale extension of P over Y .
Remark 1.11. Let me point out that if X is a normal integral variety over a field,
this definition is equivalent to asking the following: there exists a connected étale
cover f : V →X◦such that the torsor f ∗P →X◦extends to the normalization of
X in K(X◦). This is the definition of a torsor “étale locally extending” given in
[BKP22, Definition 3.2.]. Indeed, this definition implies ours by taking f : Y →X
as the normalization of X in the function field K(V ) (by [GW20, Corollary 12.52]
f : Y →X will be finite) and observing that f −1(X◦) = V . On the other hand,
given our f : Y →X, setting V = f −1(X◦) gives us a connected finite étale
cover V →X◦and moreover, since Y is normal, it is the normalization of X in
K(V ) = K(Y ) is Y .
From now on, let us fix a field F. We will be interested in understanding which
torsors admit étale extensions. First of all, recall that given an algebraic group
G/F, we have an exact sequence
1 →G◦→G →π0(G) →1 ,
where π0(G) is the the group of connected components of G, which is finite étale,
and G◦is the identity component of G.
For finite étale groups, it is easy to see that all torsors étale extend.
Proposition 1.12. Let G/F be a finite étale group scheme, X/F a normal integral
F-scheme and X◦⊆X a dense open subscheme. Then every G-torsor over X◦
étale extends to X.
Proof. Given a fixed nontrivial G-torsor p: P →X◦, any connected component
of it will be a finite étale cover of X◦. Fix a connected component T. Since
P is trivial after pullback by itself, it will also be trivial after pullback to T.
Therefore, PT ∼= GT extends trivially to the normalization of X in K(T), proving
the claim.
■
In particular, since every finite group scheme is étale in characteristic 0, we have
the following obvious corollary.
Corollary 1.13. If F is a field of characteristic 0, then every torsor for any finite
group scheme étale extends.
