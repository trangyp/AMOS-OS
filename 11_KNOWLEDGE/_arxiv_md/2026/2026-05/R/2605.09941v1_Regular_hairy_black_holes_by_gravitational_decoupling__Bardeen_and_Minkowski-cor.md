Regular hairy black holes by gravitational decoupling: Bardeen and Minkowski-core
seeds
Daulet Berkimbayev1, ∗
1Al-Farabi Kazakh National University, 71 Al-Farabi Ave., Almaty 050040, Kazakhstan
We construct two families of regular hairy black holes within gravitational decoupling using a fixed
exponential deformation profile for an effective tensor-vacuum sector. The first family is generated
from a Bardeen-type seed and produces a de Sitter-like core. The second family is generated from
a hollow seed with an asymptotically Minkowski core so that the central density vanishes and
no de Sitter core is produced. For each branch we determine the critical deformation strengths
separating horizonless, extremal, and two-horizon geometries in the static case, and we obtain the
corresponding Kerr-like rotating extension by promoting the mass parameter to the deformed mass
function. Representative parameter choices are used to illustrate the horizon structure and to verify
the weak energy condition in the exterior region.
Keywords: Regular black holes; hairy black holes; gravitational decoupling; Bardeen black hole; Simpson–
Visser geometry; weak energy condition; rotating black holes
I.
INTRODUCTION
In classical General Relativity (GR), black-hole (BH)
solutions typically contain curvature singularities, and
the Penrose–Hawking singularity theorems clarify why
geodesic incompleteness arises under broad assumptions
[1, 2]. The appearance of singularities is therefore closely
tied to predictability and to the limits of classical evo-
lution. In this context the weak cosmic censorship con-
jecture (WCC) proposes that physically formed singular-
ities are generically hidden behind event horizons, while
the strong cosmic censorship conjecture (SCC) addresses
deterministic evolution by excluding the development of
Cauchy horizons for generic initial data (see, e.g., [3, 4]).
Although these conjectures remain open, they provide
a useful guide:
regular BH models can be viewed as
effective spacetimes in which central incompleteness is
avoided at the price of introducing nontrivial effective
sources and, frequently, violations of some energy condi-
tions.
A standard way to soften the central pathology is to
introduce corrections to GR or to use effective matter
sectors that become important at high curvature. This
has motivated many regular BH proposals and systematic
discussions of their causal structure and energy condi-
tions [5–17]. Energy conditions provide a useful diagnos-
tic for the effective sources needed to sustain regularity
[18].
A complementary problem is the existence of BH hair.
Many models generate additional structure beyond mass,
charge, and angular momentum, including scalar hair
and generalized interactions [19–30].
One of the most
practical frameworks to engineer hairy configurations,
while keeping control of the field equations, is gravita-
tional decoupling (GD) [31–42].
Ref. [43] introduced a transparent GD implementation,

In this paper we keep the same strategy and the same
physical filter, but we broaden the set of seeds:
• A Bardeen-type seed is regular from the start, but
it comes with a de Sitter-like core.
It is a use-
ful benchmark for how the deformation modifies a
known regular interior.
• A Simpson–Visser (SV) hollow seed has an asymp-
totically Minkowski core and therefore no de Sitter
core [46].
These two branches allow a direct comparison between
regularization by a de Sitter core and by a Minkowski-
like core, within the same deformation profile.

This is the perspective adopted in the present

VI.
CONCLUSIONS
In this work we applied gravitational decoupling with
a fixed exponential deformation profile to construct
two sets of regular hairy black holes, extending the
Minkowski-deformation setup of Ref. [43] to alterna-
tive seeds.
Starting from a Bardeen-type seed we ob-
tained regular hairy geometries with a de Sitter-like core,
whereas a Simpson–Visser “hollow” seed leads to regu-
lar solutions with a Minkowski-like core and vanishing
central density. For both branches we mapped the pa-
rameter space of the deformation strength and identified
the critical values that separate horizonless configura-
tions, extremal solutions, and two-horizon black holes. A
Kerr-like rotating extension follows from the same mass-
function prescription, and the horizon analysis carries
over straightforwardly to the rotating case through the
corresponding ∆(r) function.
For representative parameter choices we verified that
the effective sources satisfy the weak energy condition
in the exterior region, outside the event horizon, while
maintaining regular behavior at the center.
Overall,
these results enlarge the set of regular Kerr-like space-
times obtainable within gravitational decoupling and
provide a useful starting point for further investigations
of thermodynamics, geodesic structure, including lens-
ing/shadows, and dynamical stability in both core real-
izations.
