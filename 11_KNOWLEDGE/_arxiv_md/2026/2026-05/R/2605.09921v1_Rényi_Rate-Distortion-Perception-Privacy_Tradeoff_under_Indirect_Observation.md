R´enyi Rate-Distortion-Perception-Privacy Tradeoff
under Indirect Observation
Jiahui Wei and Marios Kountouris
DaSCI, Department of Computer Science and AI, University of Granada, Spain
Email: {jiahui.wei, mariosk}@ugr.es
Abstract—We introduce a R´enyi Rate-Distortion-Perception-
Privacy (R-RDPP) framework for indirect source coding. A latent
source S is correlated with a private attribute U, and the encoder
observes only a noisy view X such that (S, U) −X −Y holds at
the decoder output Y . The communication cost is measured by
Sibson’s α-mutual information IS
α, the privacy leakage by IS
β, the
semantic distortion between S and Y , and the realism constraint
at the semantic marginal PS. We characterize the scalar Gaussian
RDPP tradeoff, revealing that standard privacy metrics inher-
ently penalize legitimate semantic recovery. To resolve this, we
introduce a conditional privacy measure that quantifies only the
residual leakage. In addition, we refine the achievability bounds
for α > 1 via the Poisson functional representation. By deriving
the exact geometric-mixture distribution of the Poisson index,
we obtain exact closed-form expressions for integer-order R´enyi
entropies and sharper computable bounds in regimes where the
resulting expression improves the logarithmic-moment approach.
Index Terms—Privacy, R´enyi entropy, Poisson functional
representation, Sibson’s mutual information, Rate-distortion-
perception
I. INTRODUCTION
Modern compression systems must do far more than min-
imise distortion. In semantic and goal-oriented communication
for 6G and machine-to-machine networks [1]–[3], the encoder
is asked to preserve a latent quantity of interest S rather than
the raw observation X. Recent studies on perception further
show that reconstructions should be statistically realistic, i.e.,
they match a target marginal [4]. And with the development
of generative models, users care more about their privacy
and seek to reveal as little as possible about their personal
information (e.g., a sensitive attribute U that is correlated
with S [5]–[7]). These three demands interact strongly, realism
and faithful semantic recovery pull in opposite directions,
and the operationally meaningful distortion-perception-privacy
tradeoff should be quantified jointly.
Most rate–distortion–perception (RDP) work [4], [8]–[10]
treats the direct setting in which the encoder sees the very
source it must reconstruct. Conventional communication sys-
tem treats the setting where the encoder observes exactly the
source that it must reconstruct. In semantic communication
this assumption is rarely valid, and the latent target S (e.g.
the spoken content of an audio, the underlying anatomy of a
medical image, the state of a remote process) is considered.

The encoder accesses only a noisy or transformed view X
(a recorded waveform, a reconstructed CT slice, sensor mea-
surements) related to S through some physical or pre-encoding
channel PX|SU. The classical indirect or remote source coding
problem [11]–[13] captures exactly this asymmetry between
the observation and the reconstruction target. Recent works
have also extended it to other network problems and finite-
blocklength analysis [14], [15]
Blau and Michaeli’s perceptual constraint dp(PX, PY ) ≤
P [4] is meaningful when the encoder sees the same object that
has to look real to the user. In the indirect setting this would
be self-defeating, matching PX would make the reconstruction
look like the noise-corrupted observation rather than the clean
semantic source. The perceptual quality the user cares about
is that Y ∼PS in distribution; e.g. the de-noised image must
look like a clean medical image, not like a noisy scan. We
therefore impose the perception constraint Dα(PS∥PY ) ≤P,
which generalises [4] both to the indirect template and to the
R´enyi family. Authors in [16] investigated this RDP problem
under the indirect setting.
The integration of privacy into this framework adds a
critical layer of complexity. In information-theoretic privacy,
standard mutual information has long been used to quantify
average privacy leakage [6]. To capture worst-case privacy
risks, the maximal leakage framework was introduced [7].
Bridging the gap between average and worst-case limits,
recent literature has increasingly adopted R´enyi divergence
and Sibson’s α-mutual information as tunable, robust metrics
for α-leakage [17], [18]. In the indirect semantic paradigm,
a natural setup is that the noisy observation X contains a
mixture of the semantic target S and the private attribute U
that has to be protected. Therefore, we generalize our recent
formulation of the direct R´enyi Rate-Distortion-Perception (R-
RDP) problem [19] by extending it to the indirect setting
and provide a Gaussian characterization to illustrate the key
tradeoff.
On the achievability side, simulating the required stochastic
test channel relies heavily on the Poisson functional repre-
sentation, conceptually grounded in the Stochastic Functional
Representation Lemma (SFRL) [20]. Especially, for order
α > 1 R´enyi extension, the coding cost is investigated
through a possibly loose logarithmic-moment bound because
of the convexity of uα [19]. In this work, we improve it by
characterizing the exact probability distribution of the Poisson
selection index, and provide a sharper and more intrinsic

Our contributions are threefold. First, we formulate the indi-
rect R-RDPP problem, replacing the standard direct perception
constraint with a semantic realism metric and introducing
a Sibson’s mutual information criterion to track the private
leakage. Second, we provide the exact optimal test channel for
the scalar Gaussian regime, analytically proving the three-way
tradeoff and an extension to a conditional criterion to isolate
residual privacy leakage without penalizing semantic utility.
Third, we refine the achievability bounds for the Poisson func-
tional representation in the α > 1 regime by establishing the
exact geometric-mixture distribution of the Poisson index that
improves upon existing logarithmic-moment approximations.

V. CONCLUSION AND OUTLOOK
In this paper, we established the R´enyi Rate-Distortion-
Perception-Privacy (R-RDPP) framework for indirect source
coding. Our Gaussian analysis demonstrated that unconditional
leakage metrics penalize legitimate semantic recovery, high-
lighting the potential utility of conditional Sibson informa-
tion to measure true privacy breaches. On the achievability
part, our exact distributional characterization of the Poisson
functional representation yields sharper R´enyi entropy bounds
for α > 1, offering a deeper understanding of the search
complexity inherent in stochastic channel simulation. Future
work will explore the formal definition of the conditional
leakage and investigate its application in modern machine
learning problems like generative models.
