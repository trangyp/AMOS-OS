RATIONAL HOMOLOGY DISK DEGENERATIONS OF ELLIPTIC
SURFACES
MARCOS CANEDO AND GIANCARLO URZÚA
Abstract. In this paper, a QHD singularity is a weighted homogeneous normal surface
singularity admitting a rational homology disk (QHD) smoothing. These singularities
are rational but often not log canonical. We classify all QHD degenerations of nonsin-
gular projective elliptic surfaces, extending Kawamata’s classification of the case with
only Wahl singularities (i.e., log terminal QHD singularities). We also realize all QHD
degenerations of Dolgachev surfaces Da,b with one QHD singularity, for every pair of
integers a, b. For each such degeneration, we construct a minimal slc birational model
via a Seifert partial resolution in the sense of Wahl followed by semistable flips. Fi-
nally, we prove that these minimal slc models are unobstructed and deform to the recent
degenerations of Dolgachev surfaces constructed by D. Lee and Y. Lee.

1. Introduction
Broadly speaking, a QHD singularity is a normal complex surface singularity admit-
ting a smoothing whose Milnor fiber is a rational homology disk (i.e., with Milnor number
zero). (See Section 2 for some basic facts, and QHD discrepancies [C] for some compu-
tations.) They were originally studied by J. Wahl in the 1980s [W1,W2]. By [SSW] and
[BS], there is an explicit classification of such singularities when, in addition, they are
weighted homogeneous. Their minimal resolution graphs are represented in Figures 7 and
8. Wahl conjectured [W3, W5] that QHD singularities are weighted homogeneous (see
[PSS, B, BPS]). In this paper, the term QHD singularity will always refer to a weighted
homogeneous QHD singularity. These singularities are rational, but typically not log
canonical. In fact, the log canonical QHD singularities are precisely Wahl singularities

2. Basics on QHD singularities
For us, a surface singularity (p ∈W) is an analytic germ of a normal complex surface W
at a singular point p. A resolution of (p ∈W) is a proper birational morphism ϕ: X →W
such that X is a nonsingular surface. The pre-image of the singular point p, seen as a
reduced divisor, is called exceptional divisor. A resolution is minimal if there are no (−1)-
curves in its exceptional divisor. Every surface singularity has a minimal resolution. On
the other hand, if a nonsingular projective surface X contains disjoint exceptional divisors
of rational singularities pi, then there is a birational morphism ϕ: X →W that precisely
contracts them, and so W has the singularities pi and it is projective. This is the Artin
Contraction Theorem [A, Thm. 2.3].

Definition 2.8. Let (p ∈W) a normal surface singularity. A smoothing of (p ∈W) is a
flat surjective morphism π: (W ⊂W) →(0 ∈D), where W is an irreducible 3-fold and
(0 ∈D) is an analytical germ of a smooth curve, such that the fiber over 0 is isomorphic
to W, and the generic fiber is non-singular.
For a smoothing one can choose a local embedding W ⊂CN and a small ϵ-ball Bϵ
so that W intersects Sϵ = ∂Bϵ transversally, then the smooth 4-manifold M = Bϵ ∩Wt
is independent of small ϵ and t ̸= 0, and of the embedding of W [W2]. We call M the
Milnor fiber of the smoothing. The connected 4-manifold M has the homotopy type of
a two-dimensional CW complex, thus we have Hi(M, Z) = 0 for i > 2. Furthermore, by
Greuel–Steenbrink [GS, Thm. 2], we have that the rank of H1(M, Z) is zero. We also
have that H2(M, Z) is a finitely generated free abelian group. The Milnor number of the
smoothing is the rank of H2(M, Z). For example, for Du Val singularities of type An, Dn,
or En we have only one type of smoothing and the Milnor number is n.
Definition 2.9. A smoothing of (p ∈W) with Milnor number equal to 0 is called a
rational homology disk smoothing (QHD smoothing for short). Singularities that admit a
QHD smoothing are called QHD singularities.

Remark 2.12. We have the following facts for an arbitrary QHD singularity (p ∈W).
(1) It is a rational singularity [SSW, Prop.2.4].
(2) Resolution graphs of all QHD WHS were classified in [BS] after [SSW]. The classifica-
tion distinguishes three families: Wahl singularities, valency 3 (Figure 7), and valency
4 (Figure 8). For valency 4 there is a cross ratio moduli in the central curve; the one
having a QHD has one fixed cross ratio [F].
(3) (Local) K2 of (0 ∈X) is equal to (local) K2 of its minimal good resolution plus the
number of curves in this resolution [SSW, Prop. 2.4].
(4) The only log canonical QHD singularities are Wahl singularities and three quotients
of simple elliptic singularities (valency 3).
(5) By Wahl [W4], QHD smoothings occur over 1-dimensional smoothing components of
the deformation space of (p ∈W), which we call QHD components. There can be one

We end this section by defining an operation that will help transit between generic and

Definition 3.17. Let W be a QHD surface, and let p ∈W be a singularity. Let ϕ: X →
W be the minimal resolution of W. Assume there is (−1)-curve Γ in X intersecting one
curve of the star graph over p transversally at one point. Let X →Z be the contraction
of Γ. We have two situations (See Figure 6):
• Γ intersects the central curve of the star graph, and so we choose a leg [ei,1, . . . , ei,ri].
• Γ intersects a curve in the leg [ei,1, . . . , ei,ri] with position j < ri.
The sliding of Γ with respect to the leg [ei,1, . . . , ei,ri] is the existence of a nonsingular
surface X′ containing a chain of nonsingular rational curves
[Wahl chain, 1, ei,ri, . . . , ei,1]
such that the contraction of the (−1)-curve and all consecutive ones from this chain is Z.
We say that the result of the sliding is the surface W ′, which is defined as the contraction
in X′ of all star graphs coming from W and the new Wahl chain. The (−1)-curve between
the Wahl chain and the star graph will be denoted by Γ′. We call the inverse process of
going from W ′ to W an unslide.
