REMARKS ON BASEPOINT-FREENESS THRESHOLDS OF POLARIZED
ABELIAN SURFACES
ATSUSHI ITO
Abstract. We determine the basepoint-freeness threshold of a very general polarized abelian
surface over the field of complex numbers. We also give the first example of a polarized abelian
surface whose basepoint-freeness threshold is irrational.
1. Introduction
Throughout this paper, we work over the field of complex numbers C. Let (X, l) be a po-
larized abelian variety. In [JP20], Jiang and Pareschi introduce the basepoint-freeness thresh-
old β(X, l). This invariant turns out to be a powerful tool in the study of linear series on
abelian varieties, such as syzygies and jet separation [JP20, Cau20, Cau24, Ito22b]. While
several bounds on β(X, l) have been established under various geometric assumptions (e.g.
[Ito22a, Ito23a, Ito23b, Jia22, Jia23, Roj22]), there are still only a few examples where the
exact value is known.
When X is an abelian surface, building on earlier joint work with Lahoz [LR23], Rojas
[Roj22] investigates β(X, l) using Bridgeland stability conditions and obtains the following
result.

We comment on Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4 in relation to the Seshadri constant ε(X, l),
another invariant measuring the positivity of the polarization l. In [Ito22a, §3], we observe
some similarities between β(X, l) and ε(X, l). A similar phenomenon appears in Theorem
1.3, as Bauer [Bau99, Theorem 6.1] determines ε(X, l) using the minimal solution to Pell’s
equation x2 −2dy2 = 1 for abelian surfaces with Picard number one.
Seshadri constants are defined for any polarized variety, and it is a famous open problem
whether they can be irrational. In the case of abelian surfaces, Bauer and Szemberg [Bau98b,
Corollary A.2] show that ε(X, l) is always rational. On the other hand, Caucci suggested that
basepoint-freeness thresholds of polarized abelian varieties could be irrational, even though
no such example was known at the time [Cau20, Remark 2.3]. Following Theorem 1.1, Rojas
raised the question of whether β(X, l) is rational for every polarized abelian surface [Roj22,
Remark 4.2]. Corollary 1.4 provides the first example of a polarized abelian surface with
irrational basepoint-freeness threshold.
After completing the draft of this paper, the author was informed through private com-
munication that Chunyi Li, Lei Song, and Xiao Wang had also independently discovered the
example in Corollary 1.4.
This paper is organized as follows. In §2, we recall some notation on cohomological rank
functions, basepoint-freeness thresholds, and semihomogeneous bundles. In §3, we determine
β(X, l) under the assumption of Theorem 1.1. In §4 and §5, we give proofs of Theorem 1.3
and Corollary 1.4, respectively.
