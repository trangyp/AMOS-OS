Abstract
We introduce the notions of rgs and irgs as properties of a
Keisler measure µ, and prove that they are respectively equiv-
alent to the existence of a generically stable random type that
extends µ and to the fact that its canonical extension, namely
the random type rµ, is generically stable. We compare these no-
tions with the known concepts of fim, fam, and self-averaging,
and in particular we show that every irgs measure is dependent
in the sense of [10].

Introduction
Generic stability has a long historical background, dating back to the early
developments of stability theory by classical model theorists, notably Bruno
Poizat. Its roots can even be traced further back to ideas in algebraic geom-
etry, particularly in the work of Andr´e Weil.
Most of the initial systematic investigations of this notion were carried
out first in stable theories and later extended to NIP theories. Pillay and
Tentovic [13] introduced the notion of generically stable types in arbitrary
theories, providing a correct generalization of definable/finitely satisfiable
types in stable context.

On the other hand, the study of Keisler measures in model theory has
seen significant progress in recent years, driven by connections to probabil-
ity, analysis, and learning theory. For Keisler measures in NIP theories, the
notion of generic stability has been appropriately generalized and has been
extensively studied. However, despite this historical development, a satis-
factory notion that serves as a correct generalization of generic stability for
measures in arbitrary theories has not yet been identified, or at least there
is no consensus on it.
The difficulty is twofold. First, unlike the NIP setting, various different
notions such as definability, finite satisfiability, fam, and fim (see, e.g., [4])
are no longer equivalent in general. Second, there is no analogue of Morley
sequences for measures that plays the same canonical role as in the case of
types.
A reasonable idea to address the second issue is to study random types,
in analogy with Keisler measures, in the randomization of the theory, as de-
veloped in several works (see, e.g., [1, 12, 5, 7]). Since it is a well-established
belief that many model-theoretic properties are preserved under randomiza-
tion, it is natural to define a measure as generically stable if its randomization
is a generically stable type.
With the above discussion and historical background in mind, the goal
of this paper is to introduce a notion that satisfies the desired properties. In
this sense, our work continues and is intended to be a completion of the ideas
developed in previous works (see, e.g., [11, 12, 10, 5]).
In particular, in [5] the notion of self-averaging was proposed as a can-
didate for generic stability of measures. This notion captures several of the
expected desirable properties of generic stability. However, it still does not
fully meet the anticipated requirements, and we believe that it fails to ade-
quately reflect the fundamental distinction between types and measures.
We argue that the notion of dependence (see [10]), which has been less
emphasized in the literature , more accurately captures this distinction. The
goal of this paper is therefore to propose suitable notions of generic stability
for measures, in such a way that, on the one hand, they are equivalent to
generic stability of types in the randomization, and on the other hand, they
satisfy the dependence property.
In this paper we are concerned with two “new” classes of Keisler measures,
which we call randomly generically stable (rgs) and linearly randomly generi-
cally stable (irgs). These notions are defined purely in the classical language,
without reference to continuous logic, but they are designed to capture the

Dependent measures and irgs
The concept of dependent Keisler measure was introduced in [10], based
on the Fremlin–Talagrand notion of stability for families of functions with
respect to a measure. The latter notion is of great importance in measure
theory, one of its key features being the characterization of Glivenko–Cantelli
classes of measurable functions. At first glance, this concept may seem dif-
ficult and unnatural from a model-theoretic perspective. However, its nat-
ural properties and applications, partly presented in [10], together with the
comparison of dependent measures with well-behaved measures such as fim
carried out in the recent paper highlight the necessity of studying this con-
cept in model theory and in future research. We will prove that every irgs
measure is dependent, which improves the fact that every fim is dependent
(see Proposition 30 in [10]).

Remark 4.15. In Theorem 4 of [8], it is shown that if a measure µ is
fim, then a probabilistic form of tameness holds for Morley product mea-
sures. More precisely, events such as Oφ, Iφ, Lφ, corresponding respectively

to the order property, independence property (NIP failure), and strict order
property, have measure zero with respect to the corresponding Morley prod-

It
is not difficult to see that the same phenomenon should also hold for irgs
measures. The idea of the proof is to combine the argument of Theorem 6.6
from [8], applied to the measure rµ (after suitably adapting the definitions to
the present setting), together with the argument used in the proof of Theo-
rem 4.11 above. Indeed, since rµ is fim, an argument analogous to the proof
of Theorem 6.6 shows that the corresponding Morley measure associated to rµ
assigns measure zero (or at least measure strictly less than 1{2) to the events

sion fails for µ, one may then perform a transfer argument analogous to the
one appearing in the proof of Theorem 4.11 above, obtaining a contradiction.
Consequently, the corresponding probabilistic tameness results from [8] also
extend to irgs measures. This provides further evidence that irgs is a robust
candidate for a notion of generic stability.
