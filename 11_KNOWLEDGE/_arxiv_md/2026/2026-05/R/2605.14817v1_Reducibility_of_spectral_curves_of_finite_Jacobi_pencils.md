REDUCIBILITY OF SPECTRAL CURVES OF FINITE JACOBI
PENCILS
BORIS SHAPIRO
Abstract. We consider finite pencils of Jacobi matrices
Jn(w) = A + wB,
where A is diagonal and B is tridiagonal with zero diagonal.
The spectral
curve is the affine plane curve
χn(λ, w) = det(λI + Jn(w)) = 0.
The main question is to describe when this curve is reducible. We prove generic
irreducibility for fixed pairwise distinct diagonal entries and discuss several
elementary reducibility mechanisms. Besides disconnected Jacobi chains, con-
stant eigenvalue branches, and reflection-symmetric components, one must also
take into account reducibility caused by scalar diagonal blocks. We formulate a
reducibility conjecture and record low-dimensional evidence and counterexam-
ples to several overly optimistic classifications. A central point of the picture
is a codimension-growth principle: apart from the cutting divisors bi = 0,
genuinely connected primitive reducibility should move to higher and higher
codimension as the size of the chain grows.

There are several elementary mechanisms. First, if some bi = 0, the chain splits
into two smaller Jacobi chains. Secondly, χn can have a constant eigenvalue branch,
i.e. a factor λ + a independent of w. Thirdly, a component of the chain may be
invariant under reversal. Then the Jacobi matrix decomposes into symmetric and
anti-symmetric subspaces for the reversal, giving a factorization of the characteristic
polynomial. Finally, if a connected block has scalar diagonal part, say ar = · · · = as,
then its characteristic polynomial is the characteristic polynomial of a constant
tridiagonal matrix evaluated at λ + ar and w; over C this usually splits into linear
factors.
The goal of the paper is to collect these elementary mechanisms, prove generic
irreducibility, and formulate a conjectural picture.
This finite-dimensional problem was motivated by the work of Djakov and Mitya-
gin on spectral Riemann surfaces and trace formulae for infinite tridiagonal operator
pencils. In that setting one studies operators of the form L + zB, where L is di-
agonal and B is tridiagonal, and the spectral Riemann surface records the analytic
continuation and monodromy of the eigenvalue branches. The present paper studies
a finite algebraic analogue: for a finite Jacobi pencil we ask when the polynomial
spectral curve χn(λ, w) = 0 is reducible. Thus the reducibility problem consid-
ered below may be viewed as a finite-dimensional counterpart of the irreducibility
questions for spectral Riemann surfaces in [1]; the subsequent work on convergence
radii and related tridiagonal spectral problems gives additional background for this
point of view [2, 3].

5. Low-dimensional evidence and first test cases
The purpose of this section is not to give a complete case-by-case classification
in every low degree. Such a classification quickly becomes uneven unless one first
stratifies the parameter space by the equality pattern of the diagonal entries. In-
stead we record the clean facts which are used in the sequel and separate them from
computational evidence. Throughout this section we put
t = w2,
Pn(λ, t) = χn(λ, w).
Two qualifications should be kept in mind.
First, if some bi = 0, the chain
splits and reducibility is immediate. We therefore emphasize the connected case
b1 · · · bn−1 ̸= 0. Secondly, if some diagonal entries coincide, extra reducibility may
occur; the scalar-diagonal block ar = · · · = as is the simplest example. Thus the
most transparent classification statements are those made either on the distinct-
diagonal stratum or after explicitly allowing scalar and partially scalar blocks.

5.3. Degrees five, six and seven. For degrees five, six and seven the coefficient-
comparison method gives many useful restrictions, but a complete printed classifi-
cation would require a separate discussion of all equality patterns among a1, . . . , an.
We therefore record the uniform conclusion which is safe and useful for the main
conjecture.
Proposition 5.6. In degrees 5 ≤n ≤7, the elementary connected reducibility
mechanisms listed in Proposition 3.1 have codimension at least two in the connected
parameter space. More precisely, a full palindromic chain has codimension n −1,
a full scalar-diagonal chain has codimension n −1, and a generic constant branch
has expected codimension ⌊n/2⌋.
Proof. The palindromic equations identify the diagonal entries in opposite pairs and
also identify the squared couplings in opposite pairs; this gives n −1 independent
equations for the full chain. The scalar-diagonal condition is a1 = · · · = an, again
of codimension n−1. Finally, Pn(−aj, t) has zero constant term and degree at most
⌊n/2⌋in t. Vanishing of its non-constant coefficients gives at most ⌊n/2⌋equations,
and on the generic constant-branch stratum these equations are independent.
□
Remark 5.7. This proposition is deliberately not a classification theorem. Its role
is to record the feature relevant for Conjecture 4.3: within the connected stratum
none of the currently visible mechanisms gives a hypersurface for n ≥4. Scalar
blocks give many connected reducible examples, but they lie on high-codimension
coincidence strata.

5.6. Summary of the low-dimensional picture. The revised low-dimensional
evidence is therefore as follows. Degrees two and three admit short complete state-
ments. Degree four already shows that palindromy is not the only connected mecha-
nism, because scalar diagonal blocks give non-palindromic factorizations. In degrees
five through seven, the useful robust statement is codimensional: all visible con-
nected mechanisms have codimension at least two. Degree eight is the first serious
primitive test case; the known connected factorizations are palindromic or scalar,
and the remaining possible primitive splittings can be organized by the Hensel-
lifting test above.
6. Final remarks
Theorem 2.1 shows that reducibility is exceptional for fixed pairwise distinct
diagonal entries.
The classification in degrees at most seven suggests that the
exceptional locus is generated by four concrete mechanisms: disconnection, constant
branches, reflection symmetry, and scalar diagonal blocks. The first open case of
Conjecture 4.2 is n = 8. In that case any new phenomenon would have to appear
as a primitive factorization of type 4 + 4 or 3 + 5 in λ, or as a paired factorization
over w not forced by an involution of the chain.
Conjecture 4.3 predicts that
such a phenomenon, if it exists, cannot form a hypersurface component, while
Conjecture 4.4 predicts that primitive connected reducibility becomes increasingly
rare.
