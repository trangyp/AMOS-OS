Abstract
RED-2400 is a public benchmark of algorithmically-rejected trading events from a live Solana
decentralised-exchange filter stack. I logged the data continuously between 2026-04-10 and 2026-05-02.
The benchmark contains 6,659 rejection events linked to 169,122 post-rejection price and liquidity
observations and 1,836 graveyard-tracker snapshots. Outcome labels follow the five-tier classification of
Kamat (2026c): saved (windowed), saved (early-death), missed, flat, and unclassifiable. Thresholds use the
trough-to-reference and peak-to-reference price ratios within a 24-hour window. Most filter-design datasets
cover the accept side only. That gap leaves reject-side outcomes unmeasured and biases filter validation.
RED-2400 lets researchers replicate filter-precision claims directly. RED-2400 is the first window in a
planned dataset series; subsequent windows will extend the time horizon and enable regime-stratified
analysis.
Keywords. filter design; counterfactual evaluation; reject-inference; algorithmic decision systems;
cryptocurrency markets; benchmark dataset.

Background
Filter-gated decision systems run in quantitative finance and in regulatory compliance. A fixed rule set
decides which events proceed to action and which are rejected. Outcome evaluation has traditionally relied
on accept-side data alone — profit on filled trades, recovery on approved loans. Reject-side outcomes are
unobserved.
Heckman (1979) on sample-selection bias is the foundational reference. The standard treatment models the
missing data probabilistically rather than measuring it. Reject-inference in consumer credit (Crook and
Banasik, 2004) and counterfactual-learning frameworks (Beygelzimer and Langford, 2009) inherit the same
limitation. The post-rejection trajectory is reconstructed by assumption, not observed.
Comparison to existing resources. No public dataset combines rejection events with linked post-rejection
outcomes. The Lending Club credit archive covers rejected loan applicants but offers no observable outcome
trace; the rejected applicant disappears from the system after refusal. Solana on-chain transaction dumps
record every transfer but contain no filter-rejection structure: a researcher cannot tell which tokens were
considered and rejected by an algorithmic decision system. DexScreener API archives provide live token
telemetry without rejection labels. Public anti-money-laundering benchmarks such as the Elliptic Bitcoin set
release accepted-side labels only. RED-2400 fills the gap. Each event has a known rejection timestamp, a
continuous post-rejection price and liquidity trace, and a deterministic outcome label.
DEX markets are different. Rejected tokens stay observable on-chain after the filter rejects them. Price,
liquidity, and trade volume keep recording for every token. I measure the post-rejection trajectory directly
instead of inferring it. RED-2400 captures this at scale: 6,659 rejection events and 169,122 outcome
observations. The data is enough for filter-precision auditing and classifier training.
The dataset accompanies Kamat (2026c). The rule is reproducible from the dataset alone.
Data Description
The dataset comprises three linked tables.
rejections.csv — 6,659 rows. One row per rejected decision event observed between 2026-04-10 and
2026-05-02. Fields: timestamp (ISO 8601 UTC), source (anonymised collector identifier: source_a or
source_b), mint (Solana mint address), symbol (token ticker), reason (anonymised filter label: filter_1 to
filter_8 in descending order of rejection volume), timeSlot (categorical: normal or strong).
rejection_outcomes.csv — 169,122 rows. Per-token outcome time series linked to rejections by mint
address. Fields: sampleTs, mint, symbol, rejectReason (filter_1 to filter_8), rejectTs, ageMin (minutes since
rejection), priceUsd, liquidity (USD, log2-binned), volume24h (USD, log2-binned), dexId, pairAddress.
graveyard_lifecycle.csv — 1,836 rows. Run-level snapshots of the rejected-token tracker, recorded
between 2026-04-23 and 2026-05-02. Fields: ts (snapshot timestamp), mint, symbol, from (previous state), to
(new state, e.g. alive_active, dormant, de-indexed), liquidity (USD, log2-binned), ageDays. Each row
captures the cohort state at one tracker pass. The data lets researchers reconstruct the timestamp at which
each token left the price oracle, supporting replication of the saved (early-death) label.

The classifiable subset under the rule of Kamat (2026c) comprises rejection events with at least two outcome
observations within the 24-hour window (n ≥ 2), plus events with exactly one observation of age ≤ 60
minutes followed by oracle absence (the n = 1 early-death tier). Researchers should derive this subset from
rejection_outcomes.csv rather than from the broader rejection log.
Filter category labels are anonymised (filter_1 through filter_8, in descending order of rejection volume) to
protect operational confidentiality of the live decision system. Numerical fields (liquidity, volume24h) are
quantised to the nearest power of two to preserve distributional shape while blocking threshold inference.
Filter-internal model outputs (per-event signal scores, weight vectors, calibration thresholds) and the
priceChange* derivatives are removed from the published release. Source-collector identifiers are
anonymised to source_a and source_b.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods
I logged decision events from public Solana DEX venues continuously between 2026-04-10 and
2026-05-02. Every event passing through the filter stack was recorded. Rejected events were retained
alongside their post-decision price and liquidity trajectory for the full observation horizon. Sampling
intervals are non-uniform; they vary with venue activity. Researchers should treat the time series as
event-driven rather than fixed-interval.
The classification rule follows Kamat (2026c, Section III.C, "Classification Tiers") and is reproduced below
for completeness. Let n denote the number of outcome samples for a given rejection event within the
24-hour window, peak the maximum observed price, trough the minimum observed price, and reference the
rejection-time price.
Saved (windowed): n ≥ 2 and trough/reference ≤ 0.5. The asset declined by at least half within the
outcome window.

Saved (early-death): n = 1 and the single sample has age ≤ 60 minutes. The asset was present in the
oracle shortly after rejection but absent on all subsequent sampling attempts within the window, the
observable signature of de-listing or rug-pull.
Missed: n ≥ 2 and peak/reference ≥ 2.0. The asset appreciated by at least a factor of two within the
outcome window.
Flat: n ≥ 2 and neither of the preceding thresholds is met.
Unclassifiable: n = 1 with sample age > 60 minutes; or n = 0; or the reference price is missing or
invalid.
I chose 60 minutes for the early-death threshold after looking at the histogram in Kamat (2026c, Figure 1).
The gap between 60 minutes and the next single-sample observation was effectively infinite. A different cut
would change the labels at the margin but not the population structure.
The rule is deterministic and depends only on observable post-rejection features. It does not require access
to the original filter logic, does not use any redacted field, and is reproducible from the published dataset
alone. The five tiers are mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive over rejection events with valid reference
prices.
Data curation, anonymisation, and CSV export were performed by the open-source lib/datasetCurator.js
utility, released alongside the dataset on Zenodo. Field redaction is enforced at the curation stage and is
verifiable by inspection of the published code.
How to Use This Dataset
To replicate Kamat (2026c) on RED-2400: load rejection_outcomes.csv, filter to events with n ≥ 2 OR (n =
1 AND ageMin ≤ 60), apply the five-tier rule to each event, group by rejectReason, count labels. The rule is
small enough to implement in roughly twenty lines of pandas. Expected runtime: under two minutes on a
laptop.
Limitations
• 23 days (2026-04-10 to 2026-05-02) is the observation window. Longer-horizon drift in filter precision
is unaddressed in this release; extension to 60+ day windows is planned for subsequent releases in the
dataset series.
• Single asset class only — Solana cryptocurrency DEX. Cross-asset and cross-venue generalisation is
out of scope.
• Per-event signal scores, weight vectors, and threshold values are redacted. The redaction protects
operational confidentiality of the live decision system. Rejection-reason category names are retained
because they describe interpretable observable conditions, not calibration parameters.
• The unclassifiable label class covers events with too short an observation horizon to apply the n ≥ 2
windowed rule. The early-death tier (n = 1, age ≤ 60 minutes) catches some of these but not all.
Researchers should treat the remainder as missing-at-random or apply right-censoring survival methods.
• Reject-side outcomes only. Accept-side realised performance is out of scope; a two-sided audit is a
separate paper.
