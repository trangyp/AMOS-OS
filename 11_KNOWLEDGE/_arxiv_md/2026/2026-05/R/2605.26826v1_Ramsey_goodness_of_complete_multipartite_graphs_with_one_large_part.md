Ramsey goodness of complete multipartite graphs
with one large part
Shaonan Mi,∗
Ye Wang†
Abstract
For graph G, a connected graph H of order n is said to be G-good if r(G, H) = (χ(G) −
1)(n −1) + s(G), where χ(G) is the chromatic number of G and s(G) is the minimum size
of a color class in a χ(G)-coloring of G. Let Kp+1(α; n) denote the complete (p + 1)-partite
graph with p partite sets of size α and one partite set of size n. We determine all graphs G
for which Kp+1(α; n) is G-good for large n. The characterization depends on the parameter
snd(α), the smallest non-divisor of α.
Keywords: Ramsey number; Ramsey goodness; Regularity method

Introduction
Given graphs G and H, the Ramsey number r(G, H) is the smallest N such that every
red-blue edge coloring of KN contains either a red copy of G or a blue copy of H. Let χ(G)
denote the chromatic number of G, and let s(G) be the minimum size of a color class in a
proper χ(G)-coloring of G. We denote by v(G) and e(G) the numbers of vertices and edges of
G, respectively.
For vertex-disjoint graphs G and H, we write G ∪H for their union and G + H for their
join.
We denote by nG the union of n disjoint copies of G.
Let Kα1,...,αp be the complete
p-partite graph with partite sets of sizes α1, . . . , αp. In particular, we write Kp(α) for Kα,...,α,
and Kp+1(α; n) for Kα,...,α,n, where p is the multiplicity of α. Let Pn be a path on n vertices.
Burr [2] showed that for every connected graph H with v(H) ≥s(G),
r(G, H) ≥(χ(G) −1)(v(H) −1) + s(G).
(1)
Following Burr and Erd˝os [4], a connected graph H is G-good if the equality holds in (1).
A fundamental result of Chv´atal [6] shows that trees are Km-good, which initiated systematic
research on Ramsey goodness; see, for example, the survey of Conlon, Fox and Sudakov [7]. Book
graphs Kp +nK1 are bridges connecting small cliques and large complete multipartite graphs in
Ramsey theory, see [5,17,18]. Burr, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp [3] showed that the complete
multipartite graphs with one large part are (K2 + mK1)-good.

Theorem 1.1 (Burr, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp [3]). Let m ≥1, p ≥1 and α1, . . . , αp ≥1
be integers. Then Kα1,...,αp,n is (K2 + mK1)-good for large n.
Nikiforov and Rousseau [20] proved that Kp + nK1 is Km-good for large n. By the stability-
supersaturation lemma instead of the regularity lemma, Fox, He and Wigderson [13] proved that
Kp + nK1 is Km-good if n ≥2p10m. More recently, Liu and Li [19] characterized all graphs G
such that Kp + nH is G-good for large n as follows.
Theorem 1.2 (Liu and Li [19]). Let p ≥2 be an integer, and let G and H be graphs with
v(G) = m, χ(G) = k + 1 ≥2 and s(G) = 1. Then Kp + nH is G-good for large n if and only if
G is a subgraph of mK2 + Kk−1(m).
These results naturally raise the question of whether similar Ramsey goodness phenomena
persist for more general multipartite graphs. In this paper, we characterize all graphs G for
which Kp+1(α; n) is G-good for large n. Let snd(α) = min{k ∈N+ : k ∤α}, the smallest
non-divisor of α. This parameter determines the structure of the extremal graphs in our result.
Theorem 1.3. For integers α ≥1 and p ≥snd(α), if G is a graph with v(G) = m, χ(G) =
k+1 ≥2 and s(G) = 1, then Kp+1(α; n) is G-good for large n if and only if G ⊆mT +Kk−1(m)
for every tree T with v(T) = snd(α).
Note that for graphs G and H, we write G ⊆H if G is a subgraph of H, which means there
is a graph embedding from G to H. Namely, there is an injective mapping φ : V (G) →V (H)
such that the adjacency is preserved: for every edge uv ∈E(G), φ(u)φ(v) ∈E(H). By G ⊆
mT + Kk−1(m) for every tree T with v(T) = snd(α), we mean that there is a graph embedding
φT from G to mT + Kk−1(m) for each T with v(T) = snd(α), where φT may differ for distinct
choices of T. Let us look at some simple cases. In the case snd(α) = 2 for odd α, the graph G in
Theorem 1.3 is a subgraph of mK2 +Kk−1(m). When snd(α) = 3, for example α = 2, the graph
G in Theorem 1.3 is a subgraph of mK1,2 + Kk−1(m). When snd(α) = 4, for example α = 6,
the graph G in Theorem 1.3 is a subgraph of both mK1,3 + Kk−1(m) and mP4 + Kk−1(m).
Remark.
The condition that G ⊆mT + Kk−1(m) for every tree T with v(T) = snd(α)
in Theorem 1.3 does not mean that there exist G1 and G2 such that G ⊆G1 + G2 with

either a path or contains a K1,3 as a subgraph. If the tree on seven vertices is a path u1u2 . . . u7,
then there is a graph embedding

5
Extensions and generalized multipartite graphs
Our result covers some well-known results. For example, we can prove the following result by
the similar proof of Theorem 4.1. In the following corollary, if we take G = K2 + mK1 with
v(G) = m + 2 and χ(G) = 3, then G must be a subgraph of (m + 2)K2 + (m + 2)K1, and hence
a subgraph of (m+2)T +(m+2)K1 for any tree T with v(T) = min{snd(α1), . . . , snd(αp)} ≥2,
which coincides with Theorem 1.1 in Burr, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp [3].
Corollary 5.1. Let α1, . . . , αp ≥1 be integers, and let G be a graph with v(G) = m, χ(G) =
k + 1 ≥2 and s(G) = 1. If G ⊆mT + Kk−1(m) for every tree T with v(T) = min{snd(α1), . . . ,
snd(αp)}, then Kα1,...,αp,n is G-good for large n.
More generally, the graph Kp+1(α; n) in Theorem 1.3 can be replaced by any spanning
subgraph of Kp(α) + nKq+1(α; β) for any q ≥1 and β ≥0. If we take α = 1 and β = 0, the
following corollary shows that Kp+nKq is G-good for large n if and only if G ⊆mK2+Kk−1(m),
which reduces to Theorem 1.2 in Liu and Li [19].
Corollary 5.2. Let α ≥1 and p ≥snd(α) be integers. Let G be a graph with v(G) = m,
χ(G) = k + 1 ≥2 and s(G) = 1, and let graph H = Kq+1(α; β) for any q ≥1 and β ≥0.
Then Kp(α) + nH is G-good for large n if and only if G ⊆mT + Kk−1(m) for every tree T with
v(T) = snd(α).
Proof. By Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 4.1, it suffices to show that if Kp(α) + nK1 is G-good for
all large n, then Kp(α) + nH is G-good for all large n.
Let N = (χ(G) −1)(pα + nh −1) + s(G), where h = v(H). For any red-blue edge coloring
of KN, we suppose that it contains no red G, then we will find a blue Kp(α) + nH. For any
integer w with wα ≥pα + (h −1)(r(G, H) −1), since Kw(α) + (pα + nh −wα)K1 is G-good
for sufficiently large n from the assumption, there must be a blue Kw(α) + (pα + nh −wα)K1
in KN. Let W be the vertex set of Kw(α) and X the set of other pα + nh −wα vertices. Since
12


## Page 13


r(G, H) ≤|X|, we can find a blue H induced by X. Then we remove such blue H’s one by one.
The number of the remaining vertices in X is at most r(G, H) −1.
By deleting a vertex from the remaining vertices in X and h −1 vertices from W, we can
remove another blue H. When deleting vertices from W, we adopt the following strategy. We
first remove vertices by deleting entire vertex classes whenever possible. Any remaining vertices
to be deleted are then chosen preferentially from an incomplete vertex class. As a consequence,
after the removal of each copy of H, at most one vertex class in W is incomplete. This step can
be repeated for at most r(G, H) −1 times. There are at least wα −(h −1)(r(G, H) −1) ≥pα
vertices left in W. Note that the difference between |W ∪X| and pα is a multiple of h. So
we can remove some H from W until there is a blue Kp(α) left in W, which yields a blue
Kp(α) + nH.
