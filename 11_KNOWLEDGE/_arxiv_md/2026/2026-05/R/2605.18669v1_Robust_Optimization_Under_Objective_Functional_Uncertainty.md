Abstract—This paper proposes a new robust optimization (RO)
formulation namely the RO under objective functional uncertainty
(ObRO). The ObRO adopts a min-max structure where the inner
problem finds the worst-case objective function in a continuous
function space to maximize the cost, and the outer problem
finds the optimal decision in a Euclidean space to minimize the
cost. A solution algorithm is designed to alternately generate
the worst-case objective function at the current decision and the
optimal decision for the current collection of objective functions.
Using operator theory, we prove that this algorithm converges
to the defined “semi-global” saddle point of the ObRO problem.
In addition, we propose a numerical solver based on the piece-
wise linearization (PWL) approximation of objective functions.
The PWL approximate problem is proved to be numerically
consistent with the original ObRO problem. The obtained results
are applied to the degradation-aware battery charging scheduling
in distribution networks.
Index Terms—robust optimization, functional uncertainty,
minimax optimization, saddle point, operator
I. INTRODUCTION
Robust optimization (RO) serves as a fundamental decision-
making tool for mitigating the impacts of ubiquitous uncer-
tainties in the real world. RO formulations have been evolved
into a diverse family of variants with widespread engineering
applications, such as state estimation [1], robotics [2], traffic
control [3], power system dispatch [4], and adversarial training
[5]. Representative RO models include the static single-stage
RO that seeks a here-and-now decision robust to all possible
realizations of uncertainty, and adaptive multi-stage RO that
coordinates here-and-now decisions and recourse decisions in
response to the actual realization of uncertainty [6].
Historically, the studies of RO have predominantly focused
on uncertainties associated with the input parameters of
optimization models. To date, there are two types of RO
formulations involving uncertainties embedded in the objec-
tive/constraint function expressions. The first is distributionally
robust optimization (DRO) where the objective/constraint
functions contain undetermined coefficients governed by some
probability distribution functions (PDFs) within an ambiguity
set [7–9]. DRO handles parametric uncertainty in the original
problem and considers PDF-type functional uncertainty in the
parameter space by leveraging statistical information of the
uncertainties such as moment, divergence or optimal transport
[10]. The second stream is preference robust optimization

are leveraged in the PRO analysis [13, 14]. The functional
uncertainty in PRO captures the partial unknown of a utility
function as the stakeholder’s behaviors are not entirely random.
Nevertheless, in some engineering problems the form of
an actual function may be fully unknown due to the lack of
knowledge about the underlying theory. For instance, battery
degradation is a critical issue in energy storage technologies,
which is closely linked to the depth of discharge (DoD) [15]. As

The ObRO problem has a min-max structure. Given a vector
x, the inner problem finds the worst-case objective function
that leads to the maximum cost while not deviating from the
reference function too much (regulated by the penalty Di(fi)).
The outer problem finds the optimal vector x that achieves the
lowest cost under the worst-case uncertain objective function.
An ObRO optimum is a saddle point (x∗, f ∗) such that
x∗is the minimizer of minx∈Sx V (f ∗, x) and f ∗is the
maximizer of maxf∈N (f 0) V (f, x∗). Due to the allowed non-
convexity of f, it is impractical to find the global saddle point.
Alternatively, it is of interest to introduce the definition below.
Definition 1 (Semi-global saddle point): For the ObRO
problem (5), a solution (x∗, f ∗) is a semi-global saddle point
if x∗is a local minimizer of minx∈Sx V (f ∗, x) and f ∗is a
global maximizer of maxf∈N (f 0) V (f, x∗).
A semi-global saddle point is stronger than a standard
local saddle point where the outer minimization and inner
maximization problems are both locally optimal. A semi-global
saddle point is more desirable in practice as it figures out the
global worst-case objective and provides a locally optimal
countermeasure at least. This paper will focus on finding a
semi-global saddle point of the ObRO problem.

IV. PWL NUMERICAL SOLVER
The master problem (8) is a nonlinear program with respect
to the n-dimensional decision variable x, while the subproblem
(7) is an infinite-dimensional optimization problem with respect
to the objective function fi. In general, the difficulty in (8)
stems from the nonlinearity of fi acting as an input, and
the challenge in (7) arises from the infinite dimensionality of
fi acting as a decision variable. Therefore, the core of the
numerical solver is to work out a proper finite-dimensional
approximation of fi to mitigate the intractability in (7) and (8).
In this section, we propose to handle such difficulty by PWL
approximation. In addition, the property of PWL approximation
also helps to establish the consistency between the original
problems and corresponding approximate formulations.

VI. CONCLUSION
We have formulated the ObRO problem that finds the
decision with the minimum cost under the worst-case objective
function. We have designed an alternate iterative algorithm
which converges to a semi-global saddle node of the ObRO
problem. A PWL-based numerical solver has also been pro-
posed, which is consistent with the original ObRO problem.
The obtained results have been applied to the degradation-aware
BESS charging scheduling in distribution networks.
