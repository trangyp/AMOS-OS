RESIDUAL PROPERTIES OF FINITELY GENERATED GROUPS
IN THE WEIHRAUCH LATTICE
EMMANUEL RAUZY
Abstract. Consider, on the space of marked groups, the map ResC which
associates to a marked group its greatest residually-C quotient, for diﬀerent
sets C of groups.
Except for trivial cases, this map is discontinuous.
We
use the Weihrauch lattice to quantify how discontinuous it is. We show that
equational noetherianity of C and whether the set of residually-C groups is a
quasivariety both can be characterized in terms of the position of ResC within
the Weihrauch lattice. We give exact classiﬁcations of ResC, for C one of: the
set of ﬁnite groups, of nilpotent groups, of k-nilpotent groups, k ≥1, of ﬁnitely
presentable groups, of LEF groups, of torsion free groups.
1. Introduction
Let C be a class of groups.
A group G is residually-C if for every non-identity element g of G there is a
quotient of G in C in which g has a non-identity image.
Every group G has a greatest residually-C quotient, obtained by taking the quo-
tient of G by the intersection of all kernels of onto homeomorphisms from G to
groups of C, i.e., by the following normal subgroup:
\
f : G↠H, H∈C
ker(f).
We denote by ResC(G) the group deﬁned this way, and by ResC the map sending
G to this quotient.
Note that, even though proving the existence of the group ResC(G) is trivial,
the deﬁnition given above provides us with very little information. Indeed, “the set
of all kernels of onto homomorphisms from G to groups in C” always exists, but it
may be very hard to characterize or understand this set.
In some sense, the existence of the group ResC(G) is non-constructive. (This
statement could be made precise in various ways. For instance [Bau00] provides a
constructive framework which coincides with the Weihrauch analysis of problems.)
In the present paper, we provide a topological analysis of the map ResC that
gives a way to quantify how non-constructive is the existence of ResC(G).
One of our motivations comes from the study of computability. We will not insist
on this aspect, but we quote here a result of Slobodskoi.
The present work stems from a question raised by Vincent Guirardel during the defense of
my PhD thesis. It took me several years to understand the question and its relevance. Thanks
are also due to Vasco Brattka, whose explanations have greatly improved my understanding of
Weihrauch reducibility.
Most of this work was conducted while the author was supported by an Alexander von Humboldt
fellowship.

Theorem 1.1 (Slobodskoi, [Slo81]). There exists a ﬁnitely presented group whose
greatest residually ﬁnite quotient is not recursively presented.
This result, and other related results from [Rau22, BPR25], all revolve around
leveraging the discontinuity of the map ResC to obtain computability theoretical
results. Our hope is that the present study in turn provides a dichotomy between
classes C which give rise to “a suﬃciently discontinuous ResC”, so that certain
computability theoretical phenomena occur, and the other ones, for which the map
ResC is too tame to permit these computability theoretical phenomena.
In order to have a nice topological framework to work with, we will study the
map ResC on the set of marked groups. A k-marked group is a pair (G, S), where G
is a ﬁnitely generated group and S ∈Gk is a generating tuple of G. A morphism of
k-marked groups (G, (s1, ..., sk)) →(H, (s′

The marked quotient relation
(G, S) ⪰(H, S′) ⇐⇒∃f : (G, S) →(H, S′)
is easily seen to be an order relation. The set of k-marked groups forms a poset
which is in fact a complete lattice isomorphic to the lattice of normal subgroups of

ResC(G, S) = sup {(H, S′) ∈C | (G, S) ⪰(H, S′)} .
The set of marked groups is naturally equipped with a Polish topology, the
resulting topological space is called the space of marked groups.
The main problem of this paper is the following one:
Problem A. What properties of C can be read oﬀof the topological properties of
ResC?
We could ask for instance: when is ResC continuous, when are preimages of open

In order to obtain ﬁner classiﬁcations than these, we use the notion of contin-

1.1. The logical point of view on the map ResC. Another point of view on
the map ResC follows the lines of [Cha].
A relation in a marked group (G, S) is an element of the group FS, the free group
over S, which deﬁnes the identity in G. A subset of FS is the set of relations of a
marked group if and only if it is a normal subgroup of FS.
Thus we can say that a set R ⊆FS of relations implies another relation w ∈FS
when w belongs to the normal subgroup generated by R.
When focusing on groups in a certain class C, it is natural to consider implication
modulo C:
Deﬁnition 1.2. Let R be a subset of a free group FS and w an element of FS. We
say that the relations of R imply w relative to C if every marked group in C which
satisﬁes the relations of R also satisﬁes w = 1.
The marked group ResC(G, S) is the marked sgroup whose relations are exactly
those that are consequences relative to C of the relations of (G, S).

1.2. Weihrauch reduction. An informal deﬁnition of continuous Weihrauch re-
ducibility is the following:
f ≤W g
if and only if f can be computed by “doing something continuous, applying g, and
then doing something continuous again”. What makes this informal statement non-
trivial to formalize is the fact that what we mean by “something continuous” is in
fact a continuous multi-function, instead of being simply a function, and that the
notion of continuous multi-function used to deﬁne Weihrauch reduction is set in
the category of represented spaces instead of the category of topological spaces.
We will explain this in more details below.
Let us ﬁrst give some examples to motivate the introduction of Weihrauch re-
duction.
For X and Y Polish spaces, let us call a function f : X →Y Σ0
n-measurable if
the preimage of an open set by f is Σ0
n.

Quoting from [BGP21],
“Weihrauch reducibility oﬀers a reﬁnement of the Borel hierarchy very much in
the same way as many-one reducibility yields a reﬁnement of the Kleene hierarchy”.
The Weihrauch lattice has a rich structure, with algebraic operations on prob-
lems, closure operators that capture other reducibility notions [BGP21].
Many
types of behaviors of discontinuous functions can be described thanks to a classiﬁ-
cation in the Weihrauch lattice.
Let us come back to a formal deﬁnition of Weihrauch reduction.
A represented space is a set X equipped with a partial surjection from Baire space
δ :⊆NN →X. When δ(p) = x, we say that p is a δ-name of x. Let f : X →Y be a
function between represented spaces (X, δ) and (Y, τ). A realizer of f is a partial
function F :⊆NN →NN such that
∀p ∈dom(δ), f(δ(p)) = τ(F(p)).
In words: F maps the name of a point in X to a name of its image.
A multi-function f :⊆X ⇒Y is simply a relation R ⊆X × Y which we treat
as a multi-valued function via the formula f(x) = {y ∈Y | R(x, y)}. A realizer of
a multi-function f is a partial function F :⊆NN →NN such that
∀p ∈dom(δ) ∩δ−1(dom(f)), τ(F(p)) ∈f(δ(p)).

Deﬁnition 1.4 (Continuous Weihrauch reducibility). Let f :⊆X ⇒Y and g :⊆
Z ⇒W be multi-functions between represented spaces. We say that f continuously
Weihrauch reduces to g, and write
f ≤W g,
if there is a represented space V and two continuously realizable multi-functions

If f ≤W g and g ≤W f, we say that f and g are Weihrauch equivalent, and write

1.3. Admissible representations of topological spaces. The deﬁnition of Weih-
rauch reduction given above is set in the category of represented spaces. We can
express Weihrauch reduction for functions between topological spaces by establish-
ing a correspondence between some topological spaces (here it will be suﬃcient to
deal with T0 second countable spaces) and some representations (the admissible
representations).
First, to any representation ρ of a set X we can associate a topology, the ﬁnal
topology of the representation, given by
U ⊆X is open ⇐⇒ρ−1(U) is open in Baire space.
Secondly, we associate representations to topological spaces.
Let ρ and τ be two representations of a set X. We say that ρ (continuously)
translates to τ if the function
idX : (X, ρ) →(X, τ)
has a continuous realizer. Two representations of a set X are called equivalent if
each one translates to the other one.
Deﬁnition 1.5 ([KW85, Sch02]). Let X be a topological space. A representation
ρ :⊆NN →X of X is admissible if and only if
• It is continuous,
• Any continuous representation of X translates to τ.
Any second countable T0 space can be equipped with a standard representation
(Deﬁnition 3.5), which is always admissible, and admissible representations are
unique up to equivalence. And we have:
Theorem 1.6 ([KW85]). Let X and Y be second countable T0 spaces. Then a
function f : X →Y is continuous if and only if it is continuously realizable with
respect to any admissible representations of X and Y .
In other words, we have an equivalence of categories between second countable
T0 spaces with continuous functions as morphisms on the one hand, and represented
spaces equipped with equivalence classes of standard representations on the other
hand.

1.4. Representations of marked groups. The back and forth between repre-
sented spaces and topological spaces can be though of in the following way: each
type of description of the elements of a set X yields a topology on X (the sets of
open sets being the set of “recognizable properties”), and conversely each (second
countable T0) topology yields a way to describe the elements of X (an element is
described by the basic open sets to which it belongs).
In this setting, the topology of the space of marked groups arises in a very natural
way: it is the topology associated to marked groups deﬁned by their word problem.
In Section 5, we deﬁne a representation of the set of marked group by saying that
“the name of a marked group is a sequence which encodes the characteristic function
of its set of relations”, and the ﬁnal topology of this representation is precisely the
topology of the space of marked groups.
Another topology that is used several times in the present paper is the Scott
topology on the set of marked groups, which admits as a base the set of basic
sets of the form “those marked groups which satisfy the relations {r1, ..., rn}”, for
any ﬁnite set of relations {r1, ..., rn}. This topology is the ﬁnal topology of the
representation associated to group presentations: a marked group is described by a
(countably inﬁnite) presentation.

of the form 1 = 1 =⇒r = 1. Such quasi-identities are called identities. Equiv-

• abelian groups,
• k-nilpotent groups,

Example 2.2. The set of residually free groups is a quasivariety, because free

2.3. Equational Noetherianity. For n in N, consider a tuple (X1, ..., Xn) of
unknowns, and let Fn be the free group on (X1, ..., Xn).
An equation is an element W ∈Fn.
If (g1, ..., gn) are elements in a group G, let W(g1, ..., gn) be the element of G
obtained by substituting in W each element Xi by the corresponding gi. We say
that (g1, ..., gn) is a solution to W if W(g1, ..., gn) = 1.
A set Sys ⊆Fn is called a system of equations. For a system Sys of equations
and a set of groups C, we deﬁne the algebraic set VSys(C) associated to Sys as
VSys(C) = {(g1, ..., gn) ∈G | G ∈C, W(g1, ..., gn) = 1 ∀W ∈Sys}.
Deﬁnition 2.3 ([BMR99]). A group G is equationally noetherian if for every n
and every tuple (X1, ..., Xn) of unknown, every system of equations with unknowns
(X1, ..., Xn) over G is equivalent to a ﬁnite subsystem:
∀Sys ⊆Fn, ∃Sys0 ⊆Sys, Sys0 is ﬁnite & VSys({G}) = VSys0({G}).
The above deﬁnition was extended to families in [GH19].

A multi-function between sets X and Y is a relation R ⊆X × Y which we treat

Let ρ and τ be representations of a set X. Say that ρ (continuously) translates

We denote the fact that ρ translates to τ by ρ ≤τ. If ρ ≤τ and τ ≤ρ, then ρ and
τ are called (continuously) equivalent. When studying the continuous Weihrauch
degrees of functions between represented spaces, representations can be considered

Deﬁnition 3.2 ([KW85, Sch02]). Let X be a topological space. A representation
ρ :⊆NN →X is called admissible for X if the following hold:
(1) The representation ρ is continuous,
(2) For every continuous representation τ :⊆NN →X we have τ ≤ρ.
It is possible for a representation ρ of a set X to be admissible for a topology
T diﬀerent from its ﬁnal topology. But this arises only if this topology T is not
sequential while having as sequentialization the ﬁnal topology of ρ [Sch02, Sch21],
and in all cases that we will consider, represented spaces are systematically equipped
with the ﬁnal topologies of their representations.
An admissible representation is a representation admissible with respect to its
ﬁnal topology. If ρ is admissible for X, (X, ρ) is an admissibly represented space.
A space that admits an admissible representation is necessarily T0 (non T0 spaces
can be handled by using multi-representations [Sch02]).

□

NN

and thus in particular not not Π0
3-measurable.

above Lim′
NN. We leave the following as a problem.
Problem 11.1. Prove that, if C is one of: the set of ﬁnitely generated solvable
groups, the set of ﬁnitely generated torsion groups, then ResC is not Π0
