REFINED OBSTRUCTIONS TO LOCAL-GLOBAL PRINCIPLES FOR 0-CYCLES
FRANCESCA BALESTRIERI, ANOUK GREVEN, RACHEL NEWTON, SOUMYA SANKAR, KATERINA SANTICOLA,
AND MANOY TRIP
Abstract. We introduce new ‘refined’ obstructions to local-global principles for 0-cycles on algebraic vari-
eties over number fields. Assuming finiteness of relevant Tate–Shafarevich groups, we show that the Hasse
principle and weak approximation for 0-cycles on generalised Kummer varieties and bielliptic surfaces are
controlled by obstructions of this new type. As an additional application of our refined obstructions, we
answer a question of Zhang about the relationship between the Brauer–Manin and connected descent ob-
structions for 0-cycles. We also show that a Corwin–Schlank style refined obstruction set coincides with the
set of global 0-cycles, conditionally on the Section Conjecture.

1.1. Outline of the paper. Section 2 contains preliminaries on obstruction sets for 0-cycles. In Section 3
we construct refined obstruction sets for 0-cycles, which are a priori finer than those in the existing literature.
In Section 4 we recall the definitions of the Hasse principle and weak approximation for 0-cycles, and prove
a bridging result between obstructions for rational points over all finite extensions and obstructions for 0-
cycles. In Section 5 we introduce various subgroups of the Brauer group consisting of locally constant Brauer
classes, and recall some results on the Hasse principle and weak approximation for torsors under abelian
varieties. In Section 6 we define generalised Kummer varieties and the refined obstruction sets appearing in
Theorems 1.2 and 1.6. Theorem 1.6 is proved in Section 7.1. Section 7.2 concerns the proof of Theorem 1.2,
noting that this proof relies on a theorem on rank jumps for abelian varieties (Theorem 1.4), which we prove
in Section 8. In Section 9 we give an application of our refined obstructions to bielliptic surfaces, proving
Theorem 1.7 and Corollary 9.2. In Section 10 we apply our new machinery to answer a question of Zhang
on weak approximation for varieties. Finally, in Section 11 we generalise work of Corwin and Schlank to
0-cycles, proving that a certain refined obstruction set is the only obstruction to the Hasse principle for
0-cycles on smooth, quasi-projective, geometrically integral varieties over number fields (Theorem 1.10).

8.1. Bertini Theorems. Recall that a linear system V on a nonsingular projective variety X is a subspace
of the space of global sections Γ(X, L ), where L is an invertible sheaf on X. By [Har13, II, Proposition

called the base points of V . The base locus is the scheme-theoretic intersection of the vanishing loci of all
the s ∈V . It forms a closed subscheme of X.
A useful tool for us will be a strong version of Bertini’s theorem, which asserts that a generic divisor in
a linear system V on X inherits nice properties of X. We recall the classical version first.

smooth, geometrically integral k-variety. Let L be an invertible sheaf, and V ⊂Γ(X, L ) a base-point-free
linear system. Then a generic member of the linear system V is smooth, geometrically reduced, and of
dimension dim X −1.
