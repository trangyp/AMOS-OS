Abstract—Recommender systems often rely on observational user–item
interaction data, which is prone to selection bias due to users’ selective
interactions with items. Inverse propensity weighting and doubly robust
estimators effectively mitigate selection bias under observed confounding,
but are unreliable in the presence of hidden confounders. Existing
approaches relying on randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or global
sensitivity bounds are constrained in practice: RCTs demand costly
experimental data, while global sensitivity bounds presume a uniformly
bounded effect of unmeasured confounders on propensities through
sensitivity analysis, thereby neglecting heterogeneity across user–item
interactions. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel framework,
which estimates user–item level sensitivity bounds, thereby substantially
relaxing the homogeneity assumption inherent in global sensitivity bounds
named Personalized Unobserved-Confounding-aware Interaction Decon-
founder (PUID). To ensure both robustness and predictive accuracy,
we further develop an adversarial optimization strategy and propose a
benchmark-guided variant (BPUID) that incorporates pre-trained models
as stabilizing references. Extensive experiments on three real-world
datasets demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms global
methods under hidden confounding, without requiring RCT data.
Index Terms—Recommendation Systems, Selection bias, Hidden con-
founding, Sensitivity analysis
I. INTRODUCTION
Recommender systems are widely used to deliver personalized
content across e-commerce, social media, and lifestyle services [1].
Users typically interact with only a small portion of available items,
preferring items they already like. As a result, interactions with less-
preferred items are rarely observed.
In recommender systems, missing data often follows a Missing
Not At Random (MNAR) pattern, as user rating propensity depends
on unobserved preferences [2]. This leads to bias when averaging
observed ratings, since users more often rate liked items [3, 4].
Similarly, ad-placement systems favor popular items due to selection
bias [5]. Modeling MNAR mechanisms helps improve recommenda-
tion quality and reduce bias [6].
To address selection bias, standard methods include error imputa-
tion [7–9], inverse propensity weighting (IPW) [10–13], and doubly
robust estimation [12, 14, 15]. These methods assume all confounders
are observed. However, unobserved confounders such as user intent,
income, or demographics jointly affect exposure and feedback, mak-
ing these methods ineffective. This hidden confounding poses a major
challenge for unbiased recommendation evaluation [16–19].
Existing solutions to hidden confounding fall into two classes.
Sensitivity-analysis methods bound the influence of hidden con-
founders by restricting propensity scores to a plausible range via
worst-case optimization [16, 20]. A leading approach, the Robust
Deconfounder (RD), embeds such bounds into adversarial training to
maintain stability under confounding [21, 22].

Data-fusion methods use auxiliary data from randomized controlled
trials (RCTs) or A/B tests to calibrate propensity scores and impu-
tation models [18, 23, 24]. Some balancing techniques incorporate
unbiased RCT ratings to correct for unobserved confounders [18].
However, these approaches often require costly or ethically sensitive
data collection [25, 26]. Recent advances in sequential recommen-
dation and large-language-model integration show promise for ro-
bustness under MNAR, yet challenges remain in handling correlated
latent variables and adaptive causal structure [27–30].
To address these challenges, we propose a novel framework
called Personalized Unobserved-Confounding-aware Interaction De-
confounder (PUID), which quantifies hidden confounding strength
via user-item-specific sensitivity bounds. PUID employs a sensi-
tivity analysis model to estimate personalized bounds on nominal
propensity scores using observational data. To infer confounding
strength, we introduce an entropy-based assumption that measures the
contribution of unobserved confounders to reductions in observable
entropy. Furthermore, we extend the model to a Benchmark-guided
PUID (BPUID) that integrates a pre-trained model as a stabilizing
reference, alleviating the trade-off between robustness and accuracy
typical in worst-case optimization. The main contributions of this
paper are summarized as follows:
• To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to
model personalized hidden confounding strength via user-item-
specific bounds, enabling personalized estimation of confound-
ing strength.
• We develop an entropy-based sensitivity estimator using dis-
cretized information gain to infer confounding from observa-
tional data, and design an adversarial training strategy with a
benchmark-guided extension (BPUID) to improve both robust-
ness and prediction accuracy.
• We conduct extensive experiments on three real-world datasets,
demonstrating that the personalized approach significantly out-
performs global methods and validates its effectiveness.
II. METHODOLOGY
We propose the Personalized Unobserved-confounding-aware In-
teraction Deconfounder (PUID), a method that addresses hidden con-
founding in recommender systems by estimating user–item-specific
sensitivity bounds via entropy-based constraints while enhancing
feature representation through matrix factorization.

C. Proposed Algorithms
We propose two novel algorithms to mitigate confounding bias:
• PUID (Personalized Unobserved-Confounding-aware Inter-
action Deconfounder): Incorporates personalized sensitivity
bounds into IPS and DR estimators via robust optimization.
• BPUID (Benchmark-guided PUID): Enhances PUID with
benchmark-guided regularization to stabilize training and bal-
ance robustness with accuracy.

III. EXPERIMENTS
We conduct experiments to answer following research questions:
• RQ1: Do the proposed PUID and BPUID methods outperform
existing methods under conditions of hidden confounding?
• RQ2: Do our methods stably perform well under scenarios with
different levels of hidden confounding?
A. Experimental Settings
Dataset and Preprocessing. We evaluate PUID and BPUID on
three public datasets: KuaiRec, Coat, and Yahoo!R3. Each contains
both biased and unbiased interactions. KuaiRec has 1.3M biased and
0.27M unbiased interactions from 7,176 users and 10,729 items; Coat
includes 6,960/4,640 interactions; Yahoo!R3 has 311,704/54,000 in-
teractions. For KuaiRec, we perform correlation-based feature selec-
tion and discretization, retaining 19 item and 14 user features. For
ID-only datasets (Coat and Yahoo!R3), we generate pseudo-features
using matrix factorization and clustering. To simulate exposure bias,
we downsample unbiased interactions based on estimated propensities
and create masked subsets with 10–80% removal to test robustness
under sparsity.

B. Performance Comparison (RQ1)
As shown in Table I, PUID and BPUID consistently outperform
baselines across all datasets. PUID-IPS uses personalized bounds to
reweight each user–item pair, achieving stable gains under varying
sparsity. PUID-DR attains the highest accuracy among doubly-robust
estimators through personalized bounds and error imputation, effec-
tively mitigating propensity bias. BPUID variants further improve ro-
bustness in sparse or noisy settings via benchmark-guided regulariza-
tion, delivering substantial gains on Coat and Yahoo!R3. While PUID
excels with sufficient data, BPUID handles sparsity more effectively.
Both significantly surpass global-bound and shared-representation
methods, confirming that personalized robust optimization success-
fully alleviates hidden confounding.
C. Robustness Under Different Levels of hidden Confounding (RQ2)
Table II shows that PUID and BPUID outperform RD and BRD
across different mask ratios and datasets. By using instance-level
sensitivity bounds, PUID variants consistently surpass global-bound
baselines, with PUID-IPS and PUID-DR achieving high accuracy
in data-rich settings. BPUID further improves robustness under
sparsity or noise via benchmark-guided regularization. While PUID
excels with sufficient data, BPUID provides stability under high
missingness—both confirming that personalized robust optimization
mitigates hidden confounding effectively.

across user–item interactions, while RCT-based solutions suffer from
costly data collection. To overcome these limitations, we propose

under hidden confounding, its efficacy may still affected by some
potential issues. The estimation of entropy-based sensitivity may be
influenced by the chosen discretization strategy, while the robustness
of personalized bounds relies on score range normalization and
interval calibration. Furthermore, the framework’s generalizability is
connected to the capacity of the underlying representation model, and
hyperparameter selection inherently balances the trade off between
user and item side impacts. Future work will explore more flexible
discretization approaches, optimized calibration protocols, integration
with advanced representation learning paradigms, and systematic
hyperparameter sensitivity analysis to enhance the framework’s adapt-
ability and robustness to diverse data scenarios.
