REAL 2-BLOCKS IN QUASI-SIMPLE GROUPS
JOHN REVERE MCHUGH AND A. A. SCHAEFFER FRY
Abstract. We determine which quasi-simple groups have a non-principal 2-block that is stable
under complex conjugation. As a corollary, we determine that the Mathieu group M22 is the only
simple group not possessing a nontrivial irreducible Brauer character of quadratic type, answering
a recent question of Gow and Murray.
1. Introduction
The study of reality properties of representations and characters of finite groups has been a prolific
area of research since the early years of character theory. The characters and Brauer characters
corresponding to irreducible self-dual modules, which we will call self-dual (Brauer) characters, are
exactly those irreducible (Brauer) characters that are stable under complex conjugation, relating
this study to that of self-dual modules.
Motivated by the computation of decomposition numbers, Fong showed in [Fon74, Lem. 1]
that any nontrivial irreducible self-dual FG-module for a finite group G and perfect field F of
characteristic 2 necessarily admits a nondegenerate alternating bilinear form.
This began the
study of whether, further, for such a V there is a nondegenerate G-invariant quadratic form. If
V is equipped with a nondegenerate G-invariant quadratic form, V is said to be of quadratic
type. Modules and Brauer characters of quadratic type have been highly studied, for example in
[GW93, GW95, GW97, MN16, GM21].
This paper is motivated by a question of Gow and Murray on the topic. Namely, in [GM21,
Sec. 5], Gow and Murray ask whether each nonabelian simple group aside from the Mathieu group
M22 has some nontrivial, quadratic-type Brauer character. By [GW93, Prop 1.4], any real 2-block
B (that is, one stable under complex conjugation - see 2.2.3 below) of a finite group contains a
self-dual Brauer character ψ. Then by [GM21, Lem. 17], ψ is of quadratic type unless possibly if
B is the principal block. Hence if G has a non-principal real 2-block, then there exists a nontrivial
Brauer character of quadratic type.
This naturally leads to the question of which groups possess a non-principal real 2-block. We
answer this question for nonabelian (quasi)-simple groups:
Theorem 1.1. Let G be a finite quasi-simple group. Then G has a non-principal real 2-block if
and only if G/Z(G) is not one of M11, M22, M23, M24, PSL3(3), or PSU3(3).
In [GM21], it is shown that the sporadic simple groups noted in Theorem 1.1 contain an irre-
ducible Brauer character of quadratic type except for M22. As a corollary, we may answer the
question of Gow and Murray:
Corollary 1.2. Let G be a finite nonabelian simple group. Then G has a nontrivial irreducible
Brauer character of quadratic type if and only if G is not M22.
Some of the key results we use are those of Fong–Srinivasan [FS82] describing the blocks of
general linear and unitary groups, which were extended in [Bro86], as well as those of Srinivasan–
Vinroot [SV15, SV20] on the compatibility of Lusztig’s Jordan decomposition of characters with
respect to Galois action, which were extended in [SFTV25].
We thank John Murray for suggesting this question and for valuable discussions on the topic. The second author
is grateful for support from the U.S. National Science Foundation, Award No. DMS-2439897.

2. Preliminaries
We begin with some preliminaries that will be useful throughout.
2.1. Group-Theoretic Notation. Let G be a group. If g, h ∈G then we set gh := ghg−1, and
if H ≤G is a subgroup of G then gH := gHg−1. We write H ⊴G if H is a normal subgroup of
G. The center of G is denoted Z(G), and the derived subgroup is denoted Gder. The normalizer
(respectively, centralizer) of a subgroup H of G is denoted NG(H) (resp., CG(H)).
Let g ∈G be an element of finite order. If ℓis a prime number, then g is an ℓ′-element if
(|g|, ℓ) = 1, i.e., if the order of g is prime to ℓ. If |g| is a power of ℓthen g is an ℓ-element. So, for
example, the identity of G is the only element of G which is both an ℓ-element and an ℓ′-element.
An arbitrary element g ∈G of finite order can be expressed uniquely as a product of commuting
elements g = gℓgℓ′ = gℓ′gℓsuch that gℓ∈G is an ℓ-element and gℓ′ ∈G is an ℓ′-element.
Recall that an element g ∈G is real if g is G-conjugate to g−1.
2.2. Characters and Blocks. We next recall some generalities on the character and block theories
of finite groups. Let G be a finite group, let ℓbe a prime number, and let O be a complete discrete
valuation ring whose field of fractions K has characteristic 0 and whose residue field O/J(O) has
characteristic ℓ. Assume that K is “large enough” for G, which occurs e.g. when K contains a root
of unity of order equal to the exponent of G. Also, let K0 denote the algebraic closure of Q in K
and set O0 := K0 ∩O.
2.2.1. Irreducible Characters. Let IrrK(G) denote the set of irreducible K-characters of G. If χ ∈
IrrK(G) then χ takes values in K0. After fixing an identification of K0 with a subfield of the complex
numbers, we may thus view χ as a C-character of G. Because K is large enough, in this way we
can and will identify IrrK(G) with the set Irr(G) of irreducible (complex) characters of G.
We use standard character-theoretic notation throughout this note. The principal character of G
is denoted 1G. The group of linear characters of G, which are the characters λ ∈Irr(G) satisfying
λ(1) = 1, is denoted Lin(G). Recall that Lin(G) acts on Irr(G) by pointwise multiplication. If
H ≤G and χ is a character of G, then ResG
H(χ) denotes the restriction of χ to H. If g ∈G and θ
is a character of H, then gθ denotes the character of gH defined by gθ(gh) = θ(h) for all h ∈H.
2.2.2. Real Irreducible Characters. If ξ ∈C, then ξ denotes the complex conjugate of ξ. We may
assume without loss that the subfield K0 ⊆C is closed under complex conjugation. Then the
complex conjugate of an irreducible character χ of G is the irreducible character χ defined by
χ(g) = χ(g) for all g ∈G. It is well-known that χ(g) = χ(g−1) for any χ ∈Irr(G) and any g ∈G.
A character χ ∈Irr(G) is called real if it satisfies χ = χ, which occurs if and only if χ is real-
valued. We remark that if M is an irreducible KG-module affording the character χ, then χ is
afforded by the dual KG-module HomK(M, K). For this reason, real characters of G are also known
in the literature as self-dual characters.

3. Initial Results
Lemma 3.1. Let G be a sporadic simple group, an alternating group, or the Tits group 2 F4(2)′.
Then OG possesses a non-principal real 2-block if and only if G is not one of M11, M22, M23, M24.
Proof. If G is a sporadic group, 2 F4(2)′, or an alternating group An with 5 ≤n ≤7, the statement
can be seen using GAP [GAP25]; this was already noted for sporadic groups in [GM21, Sec. 5].
Now let G = An be an alternating group with n ≥8. A character of the symmetric group Sn
lies in the principal block if and only if it corresponds to a partition with empty 2-core for n even,
resp. 2-core (1) if n is odd. Then the character corresponding to the partition (1, 2, n −3) lies
in a non-principal 2-block of Sn if n is even and the character corresponding to (1, n −1) lies in
a non-principal 2-block of Sn if n is odd. Further, in each case the partition is not self-conjugate
(recall here that n ≥8), so restricts irreducibly to a (necessarily real-valued) character χ ∈Irr(An).
As [Sn : An] = 2, the principal 2-block of Sn is the unique block above the principal block of An,
and hence χ lies in a real, non-principal 2-block of An.
□

Proof. First assume that GF = F4(q). Note that in this case, (G, F) is self-dual, Z(G) is trivial,
and GF = GF /Z(GF ). Further, GF contains subgroups isomorphic to Sp6(q). Then by Lemma
4.5, there is a semisimple 2′-element s ∈(G∗)F ∗satisfying condition (A) of Definition 4.1. (Note
that CG∗(s) is necessarily connected since Z(G) is.) Then the statement follows from Proposition
4.2(a).
Now suppose G = GF = E6(ϵq)sc, and write S := GF /Z(GF ). Further, let H = E6,ad be the
corresponding simple algebraic group of adjoint type, with H = HF = E6(ϵq)ad. Then H∗= G
contains a subgroup isomorphic to F4(q), so from above there exists a real semisimple 2′-element of
H∗. Further, CH∗(s) is connected since H is of adjoint type, so H has a non-principal real 2-block
B by Proposition 4.2(a).
Now, the simple group S is isomorphic to a normal subgroup of H with index dividing 3, and we
identify S with this subgroup. Here B cannot lie above the principal block B0(S) of S, as then by
Lemma 2.2, Irr(B) = Irr(B0(H))·β for some β ∈Irr(H/S) of order 3, where B0(H) is the principal
block of H, contradicting that B is real. Then B lies above either one or three non-principal blocks
of S, and since B must also lie above the complex conjugates, at least one of these blocks of S
is real. Then O[S] contains a real non-principal 2-block. But this must be dominated by a real
non-principal 2-block of O[G] using Lemma 2.3, completing the proof.
□
Lemma 4.12. Let G be a simply connected simple algebraic group and let F be a Frobenius endo-
morphism of G such that GF ∈{E7(q)sc, E8(q)}. Then O[GF ] and O[GF /Z(GF )] each possess a
non-principal real 2-block.
Proof. Let (G∗, F ∗) be dual to (G, F). Then G∗is a simple algebraic group of adjoint type with
root datum of Cartan type E7, respectively E8, and G∗F ∗= E7(q)ad, respectively E8(q). (Note that
E8(q) is self-dual.) By [ST08, Thm. 2.3.1], every semisimple element in G∗F ∗is real. Therefore,

5. Proof of the Main Results
Finally, we end with the proofs of Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2.
Proof of Theorem 1.1. If G is simple, we may assume by Lemma 3.1 that G = GF /Z(GF ) for G
a simple, simply connected algebraic group and F : G →G a Steinberg endomorphism. Then the
result follows from Lemmas 3.2, 4.10, 4.11, and 4.12 together with Corollaries 4.4, 4.7, and 4.9.
So, assume that Z(G) ̸= 1. If O[G/Z(G)] has a non-principal real 2-block b, then so does O[G]
using Lemma 2.3, by considering a block dominating b. It suffices to see that the covering groups
of the exceptions listed in the theorem do not have non-principal real 2-blocks.
The groups PSL3(3), PSU3(3), M11, M23, and M24 each have trivial Schur multiplier, so it further
suffices to check the covering groups of M22, which has a cyclic Schur multiplier of size 12. Again
using [GAP25], we see that these covers have no non-principal real 2-block, and this completes the
proof.
□
Proof of Corollary 1.2. First, if G is not one of the simple groups excluded in Theorem 1.1, then
G has a real non-principal 2-block B. By [GW93, Prop 1.4], B then contains a self-dual Brauer
character ψ, which by [GM21, Lem. 17] must be of quadratic type.
Now suppose that G is one of the simple groups excluded in Theorem 1.1. The statement for
sporadic groups is shown in [GM21, Sec. 5]. So, we are left to consider the groups PSL3(ϵ3). Here
we argue similarly to the case of sporadic groups in [GM21, Sec. 5] - namely, we see in GAP [GAP25]
that there is an orthogonal character (of degree 12 when ϵ = 1, respectively degree 14 when ϵ = −1)
that restricts to a self-dual irreducible Brauer character when considered on 2-regular elements.
□
