1. Introduction
1.1.
An explicit construction, including a dimension formula, for the irreducible repre-
sentations of symmetric groups Sn in module case over an algebraically closed field F of
characteristic p > 0 is still an open important problem. A landmark result in this direction
was obtained by Mathieu [M], who computed the dimensions of the irreducible modules
associated to partitions λ = (λ1, . . . , λl) of n with l = ℓ(λ) satisfying λ1 −λl + l ≤p,
by means of the classical Schur-Weyl duality. Kleshchev [K1] later characterized these
modules representation theoretically by showing they coincide precisely with those whose
restriction to every subgroup Sk for all 1 ≤k < n is semisimple, or equivalently, on
which the Jucys-Murphy elements of FSn act semisimply. Following [K1], such modules
are called completely splittable (also called calibrated or homogeneous in literature). Us-
ing the modular branching rules for Sn (cf. [K2]), one then obtains a dimension formula
in terms of paths in Young modular graphs, which recovers Mathieu’s result.

The theory of completely splittable representations has been extended to various alge-
bras beyond Sn. Generalizing previous work [K1, M], Ruff [Ru] classified the irreducible
completely splittable modules for degenerate affine Hecke algebras. Earlier constructions
and classifications over the complex field were given by Cherednik [C1, C2] and Ram [Ra]
for related algebras. Analogous theories have since been developed for degenerate affine
Hecke-Clifford superalgebras [W, HKS], for Khovanov-Lauda-Rouquier algebras [KR], and
for quiver Hecke superalgebras [KL].
1.2.
In order to study q-Young symmetrizers arising in the projective representation
theory of Sn initialed by [Sch], Jones and Nazarov [JN] introduced the notion of non-
degenerate affine Hecke-Clifford superalgebras Haff
n (q). The study of Haff
n (q) and its as-
sociated cyclotomic quotient algebras has made substantial progress recently (cf. [BK1,
KKT, KL, KMS, LS, Mo, N1, N2, Ol, Ts, SW, Sh]).

solve two interrelated problems concerning representations of Haff
n (q) over F. The first is
to classify and explicitly construct all irreducible completely splittable Haff

when the parameter q is a root of unity. The second is to derive necessary and suffi-

1.4.
Let Hn(q) be the finite Hecke-Clifford superalgebra. A Hn(q)-module is called com-
pletely splittable if the Jucys-Murphy elements L1, . . . , Ln (see (5.1)) act semisimply on
it. According to [N2], there is a surjective algebra homomorphism Haff
n (q) ↠Hn(q) send-
ing Xk to the Jucys-Murphy element Lk for each 1 ≤k ≤n. Via this homomorphism,
we classify all irreducible completely splittable Hn(q)-modules and derive an explicit di-
mension formula for them. Recall that the irreducible representations of Hn(q) over F
are parameterized by the set RPh(n) of h-restricted h-strict partitions of n when h is
odd [BK1], and by the set DRPh(n) of doubly restricted h
2-strict partitions of n when h
is even [Ts, Mo]. One of our main results identifies the subset CSPh(n) ⊆RPh(n) (resp.
CSPh(n) ⊆DRPh(n)) in the case h is odd (resp. h is even) consisting of exactly those
partitions that label irreducible completely splittable Hn(q)-modules.
As an application of the classification, we show that when n = h with h odd, or when
n = 2h with h even, every irreducible representation of Hn(q) is completely splittable. For
these critical values of n, this provides an explicit construction of all irreducible Hn(q)-
modules together with closed dimension formulas. A comparison of total dimensions then
shows that Hn(q) is not semisimple at these values. To extend non-semisimplicity to
larger n, we establish that Hn+1(q) fails to be semisimple whenever Hn(q) does; the proof
exploits the identification of the branching graph for Hn(q) with the crystal graph of
type A(2)
h−1 (when h is odd) and D(2)
h/2 (when h is even). Taken together, these arguments
establish that the semisimplicity of Hn(q) is equivalent to the known sufficient condition
given in [SW], thereby recovering and strengthening theorem of [Sh] for Hn(q) by removing
an additional hypothesis that was assumed there.

2.1. Some basics about superalgebras. We shall recall some basic notions of superal-
gebras, referring the reader to [BK1, §2-b]. By a superspace over F, we mean a Z2-graded
vector space. Let us denote by ¯v ∈Z2 the parity of a homogeneous vector v of a vector
superspace. By a superalgebra, we mean a Z2-graded associative algebra. Let A be a
superalgebra. By an A-module, we mean a Z2-graded left A-module. A homomorphism
f : V →W of A-modules V and W means a linear map such that f(av) = (−1) ¯f¯aaf(v).
It is common in superalgebra to write expressions that are initially defined only for ho-
mogeneous elements. Their intended meaning for arbitrary elements is then obtained by
extending the definition linearly from the homogeneous case. Let V be a finite dimen-
sional A-module. Let ΠV be the same underlying vector space but with the opposite
Z2-grading. The new action of a ∈A on v ∈ΠV is defined in terms of the old action by
a·v := (−1)¯aav. Note that the identity map on V defines an isomorphism from V to ΠV .
By forgeting the grading we may consider any superalgebra A as the usual algebra which
will be denoted by |A|. Similarly, any A-supermodule V can be considered as a usual
|A|-module denoted by |V |. A superalgebra analog of Schur’s Lemma (cf. [K2]) states
that the endomorphism algebra EndA(V ) of a finite dimensional irreducible A-module V
is either one dimensional or two dimensional. In the former case, we call the module V
of type M while in the latter case the module V is called of type Q. A superalgebra A is
said to be semisimple if the usual algebra |A| is semisimple.
