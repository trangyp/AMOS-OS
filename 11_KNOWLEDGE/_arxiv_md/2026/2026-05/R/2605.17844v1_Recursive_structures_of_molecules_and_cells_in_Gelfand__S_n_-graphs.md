Abstract
W-graphs, representing the multiplication action of the standard basis on the canonical
basis in the Iwahori-Hecke algebra are introduced by Kazhdan and Lusztig. Marberg defined a
generalized W-graph, the Gelfand W-graph, corresponding to the Hecke algebra modules instead
of Hecke algebras. To classify the molecules and cells of the Gelfand Sn-graphs, in this paper,
we introduce a recursive structure of Sn and then discuss the action of the recursive structure
on the molecules. Using this struction, we show that a specific molecule is indeed a cell.

Introduction
For any Coxeter system pW, Sq, the associated Iwahori-Hecke algebra H is equipped with two
distinct bases: a standard basis tHw : w P Wu and Kazhdan–Lusztig basis tHw : w P Wu. The
action of left multiplication by elements of the standard basis on the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis can
be visualized as a directed graph, i.e., the left Kazhan-Lusztig graphs of W. These graphs serve as
canonical examples of W-graphs, weighted directed graphs that encode H-module structures with
canonical bases analogous to tHw : w P Wu.

A crucial combinatorial challenge in studying W-graphs is the classification of their cells, defined
as the strongly connected components of the graphs. This task is foundational because the cells
inherits the W-graph structure from the original graph by restriction. A parallel research goal
focuses on characterizing molecules within W-graphs, which are the connected components of the
undirected graph formed by retaining only bidirectional edges.
Among the W-graphs, we concentrated on the Gelfand W-graphs defined by Marberg and Zhang
in [11]. These graphs have fixed-point-free(FPF) involutions as vertices and they are generated by
perfect models, which is the sum of all irreducible representations without repetition.
In this paper, we focus on the type A case, when W is the symmetric group Sn for even n.
Let Fn be the set of FPF involutions in Sn for even n. In this case, Marberg and Zhang classified
the molecules using combinatorial methods in [13]. They defined a Robinson-Schensted-Knuth-like
insertion, which maps the FPF involutions to special tableaus. Then the molecules are just the
set of FPF involutions corresponding to tableaus with the same shape. Zhang later proved that
all molecules are exactly cells in [18] using same insertion with similar case analysis. However,
their proofs are somewhat cumbersome, raising a natural question: Can we develop an alternative
approach to verify this theorem?
The FPF involutions can be partitioned into n subsets according to the other element in the
2-cycle containing 1. The subset of Fn, whose elements begin with p1, 2q, can correspond to Fn´2
by an operator ρ. Therefore, we can represent Fn by some copies of Fn´2, and call it the recursive
structure of Fn.
Applying the recursive structure, we analyze its action on the molecules and
employ this algebraic approach to demonstrate that a specific molecule is indeed a cell.
This paper is organized as follows.
Section 2 contains some preliminaries on W-graph and
molecules. Section 3 proposes the recursive structure and its properties. Section 5 gives a criterion
of the boundaries between two different molecules. In Section mole-sect, we study the actions of
recursive structures on the molecules. Finally, in section 7, we prove certain molecules are indeed
cells.
