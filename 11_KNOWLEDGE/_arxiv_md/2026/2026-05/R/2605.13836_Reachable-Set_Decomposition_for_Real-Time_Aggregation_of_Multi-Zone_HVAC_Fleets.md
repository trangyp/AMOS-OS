Reachable-Set Decomposition for Real-Time Aggregation of
Multi-Zone HVAC Fleets
Jingguan Liu, Xiaomeng Ai, Cong Chen, Shaoze Li, Shichang Cui, Jiakun Fang, and Jinyu Wen
Abstract—Aggregating building heating, ventilation, and air-
conditioning (HVAC) fleets provides substantial real-time flexibil-
ity to power system operations. However, real-time aggregation
of multi-zone HVAC fleets faces two key challenges: (i) strong
coupling across zones and time makes flexibility characteriza-
tion high-dimensional and computationally demanding, and (ii)
the sequential revelation of temperature states and exogenous
conditions requires that decisions made at each period preserve
feasibility over the remaining horizon using only currently
realized information. To address these challenges, this paper
proposes a reachable-set decomposition framework comprising
an offline decomposition stage and a real-time policy. In the
offline stage, backward reachable sets are formulated to encode
remaining-horizon feasibility into per-period state constraints,
so that any state within the current reachable set is guaranteed
to sustain feasible operation over the entire remaining horizon.
A tailored inner approximation is then developed for tractable
calculation in multi-zone-coupled HVAC settings. In the real-time
stage, aggregate flexibility is computed efficiently via building-
level parallel linear programs followed by closed-form Minkowski
summation of power intervals, and any regulation signal within
the reported flexibility interval admits a recursively feasible
disaggregation. Case studies demonstrate the effectiveness of
the proposed framework in aggregate flexibility characterization,
disaggregation feasibility, and scalable computation.
Index Terms—Building HVAC systems, reachable set, demand-
side aggregation, time-causal operation.

Mathematically, each building’s flexibility can be character-

the fleet-level aggregate flexibility is given by the Minkowski
sum of these building-level power sets [7]. However, real-time
aggregation of multi-period, multi-zone-coupled HVAC fleets
faces two fundamental challenges. First, HVAC flexibility is
strongly coupled across both time and zones: thermal dynam-
ics induce inter-temporal coupling, while heat transfer among
zones creates cross-zone coupling [8]. As a result, explicitly
projecting the building-level multi-zone-coupled feasible set
into the power subspace is difficult, and computing fleet-level
aggregate flexibility through direct Minkowski-sum operations
in a high-dimensional multi-period space is computationally
prohibitive [9], [10]. Second, real-time operation is a sequen-
tial decision process in which, at each period, the aggregator
observes only the current thermal state and the exogenous
conditions realized up to that time, while future realizations
remain unknown [2], [11], [12]. We refer to this information
constraint as time-causal operation. Under such an information
structure, the aggregation and disaggregation policy must
further be recursively feasible: each current decision must
ensure feasibility not only at the current period but also over
the remaining horizon, regardless of how future exogenous
conditions unfold [3], [13].
These challenges call for an aggregation framework that
is both computationally efficient for large multi-zone HVAC
fleets and amenable to time-causal, recursively feasible real-
time operation.
A. Related Work
Existing methods for HVAC flexibility aggregation can be
broadly classified into three categories.

HVAC systems as thermal batteries based on power and
thermal-state limits, and then sum these limits to obtain aggre-
gate flexibility. These methods are simple and naturally time-
causal. However, they usually rely on homogeneous model
structures, which practical multi-zone HVAC systems with het-
erogeneous coupling topologies and varying state dimensions
rarely satisfy. Moreover, the resulting aggregate sets are often
outer approximations, so some trajectories within the reported
flexibility region may not admit feasible disaggregation [17].

ibility by reformulating Minkowski-sum computation as a

assumed to be available simultaneously. Therefore, their full-
horizon formulation violates time causality and limits direct
applicability in real-time operation.
Overall, existing methods provide useful tools for HVAC ag-
gregation, but none simultaneously satisfies the key real-time
requirements summarized in Table I. Specifically, a practical
real-time framework should handle high-dimensional multi-
zone coupling, preserve time causality under sequential infor-
mation revelation, guarantee recursive feasibility, and remain
scalable for large fleets. To bridge this gap, we draw inspiration
from backward reachability analysis in control theory [24]–
[26], which is typically used to identify safe and optimal
control actions for single dynamic systems. Here, however, we
use it to characterize aggregation feasibility rather than control
optimality. Building on this idea, we develop a reachable-set
decomposition framework for real-time aggregation of multi-
zone HVAC fleets. To the best of our knowledge, this is the
first work to adapt backward reachability analysis to the real-
time aggregation of multi-zone HVAC fleets and to use it
to establish a time-causal, recursively feasible, and scalable
aggregation framework.

III. AGGREGATION FRAMEWORK
This section presents the proposed reachable-set decompo-
sition framework. A high-level framework overview is given
first, followed by a detailed introduction to each component.
A. Framework Overview
Our framework consists of four components:
1) We first develop a reachable-set decomposition that con-
verts full-horizon coupling into a time-causal per-period
feasibility description, addressing the sequential decision
requirement in real-time operation (Section III-B).
2) We then construct a tailored inner approximation of the
reachable sets to obtain a safe and tractable polyhe-
dral representation, addressing the computational chal-
lenge caused by high-dimensional zone coupling (Sec-
tion III-C).
3) Building on the offline-computed reachable sets, we next
develop a scalable real-time aggregation and disaggrega-
tion policy that uses only currently realized information
while preserving recursive feasibility over the remaining
horizon (Section III-D).
4) Finally, we summarize the offline–online implementa-
tion procedure of the proposed framework for real-time
HVAC aggregation (Section III-E).

remaining-horizon feasibility as a per-period state constraint,

C. Tractable Approximation
In general, exactly computing the reachable sets in (9) is
computationally intractable for two reasons. First, (9) defines
the reachable sets only implicitly through nested universal and
existential quantifiers, and therefore does not admit a closed-
form characterization in general. Second, due to multi-zone
thermal coupling, T rch
b,t−1 lies in a high-dimensional space and
can be viewed as the projection of a lifted polytope involving
the one-step decision variables Tin
b,t and Pac
b,t. Eliminating
these variables requires high-dimensional projection and is
computationally prohibitive [10].
Although many classical reachability-analysis methods have
been developed for set approximation, they are not directly
suitable for real-time aggregation of HVAC fleets. Nonlinear
set approximations [24] can be computationally burdensome
for real-time aggregation, while sampling-based methods [25]
may become inefficient in high-dimensional multi-zone set-
tings. Moreover, zonotope-based methods [26] are not closed
under intersection, which makes it difficult to efficiently
enforce the repeated comfort, input, and future-feasibility
constraints arising in our setting. We therefore develop an
efficient polytope-based inner approximation method tailored
for multi-zone HVAC systems. The proposed approximation
preserves recursive-feasibility guarantees through an inner
construction while remaining computationally tractable and
readily embeddable into the subsequent real-time aggregation
and disaggregation procedures.

E. Implementation Procedure
The implementation procedure of the proposed framework
is illustrated in Fig. 3. It consists of an offline stage that
precomputes remaining-horizon feasibility information and a
real-time stage that performs time-causal aggregation and
disaggregation using only current observations.
1) Offline stage. In the offline stage, the approximate reach-
able sets are computed backward in time for each building.
Specifically, for each period t and building b, the linear
program in (21) is solved to obtain the affine parameters
of the approximate reachable set, which yields the explicit
representation in (22). The resulting set is then used in the
backward recursion for the preceding period. Repeating this
procedure over the scheduling horizon yields the approximate
reachable sets for all periods and encodes remaining-horizon
feasibility into per-period state constraints for real-time use.
2) Real-time stage. In the real-time stage, aggregation
and disaggregation are performed sequentially in a forward
manner over the scheduling horizon. At the beginning of
each period t, each building observes its current temperature
state Tin

D. Computational Efficiency
We close by examining the computational efficiency of the
proposed framework. As described in Section III-E, both the
offline reachable-set computation and the real-time aggrega-
tion can be parallelized across buildings. Consequently, the
dominant computational burden lies in the two per-building
optimization subproblems, while fleet-level interval summa-
tion and the closed-form disaggregation policy introduce neg-
ligible overhead.
Table III reports the solver time of these per-building
subproblems as the number of zones per building increases:
the total offline decomposition time across all periods for one
building and the mean online aggregation time per period for
one building. As the number of zones grows from 4 to 24, the
offline reachable-set computation remains below 9 s, while
the online per-period aggregation time stays below 0.2 s. This
efficiency arises because the offline reachable set effectively
decouples the multi-period problem into per-period subprob-
lems, thereby shifting most of the computational burden from
real-time aggregation to offline computation. Although both
offline and online computation times increase with the number
of zones, they remain within an acceptable range for practical
applications. This moderate scaling is mainly due to the fact
that the two core optimization problems in the proposed
framework, namely the reachable-set computation in (21) and
the building-level interval computation in (27), are both linear
programs that can be solved efficiently by off-the-shelf solvers.
These results confirm that the proposed framework remains
computationally tractable for multi-zone buildings.

V. CONCLUSION
This paper proposes a reachable-set decomposition frame-
work for the real-time aggregation of multi-zone HVAC fleets,
addressing the practical need for scalable and implementable
demand-side flexibility utilization by building aggregators.
Backward reachable sets encode remaining-horizon feasibility
into per-period state constraints, a tailored inner approximation
provides a tractable set representation, and a scalable real-time
policy is built on the offline-computed reachable sets.
Numerical results provide practical insights for HVAC
aggregation. For aggregators, the key is not only to report
greater flexibility, but also to ensure that it remains feasible
in real time. In this regard, backward reachable sets are
essential because they prevent over-promising and preserve
feasible continuation over the operating horizon. Moreover, the
proposed decomposition shifts most computation offline and
leaves only lightweight online subproblems, enabling scalable
implementation for large aggregations. The results also show
that conservative methods may reduce infeasibility risk, but
often at the cost of underutilizing available flexibility. By
contrast, the proposed framework achieves a better balance
among flexibility utilization, feasibility, and scalability.
