Abstract. We develop a Kubo–Ando theory on order intervals of completely positive
maps. Using Arveson’s Radon–Nikodym theorem as a structural tool, we define relative
Kubo–Ando means ΦσΩΨ for completely positive maps dominated by a common ambient
map Ω. The special choice Ω= Φ+Ψ yields an intrinsic mean of two completely positive
maps.
We prove that these means are independent of the chosen Stinespring representation
and satisfy the expected order-theoretic properties, including monotonicity, transformer
inequalities, Jensen-type inequalities, data processing, and monotonicity with respect to
the ambient map. For the geometric mean, we obtain a block-positivity characterization
and show that the intrinsic geometric mean vanishes exactly when the two maps have
no nonzero common completely positive submap. Finally, we compare the construction
with existing finite-dimensional and form-theoretic approaches: for maps between matrix
algebras it agrees with the Choi-matrix mean, and in the geometric case it agrees with
Okayasu’s Pusz–Woronowicz mean on their common domain.
1. Introduction
Kubo–Ando theory characterizes binary operations on positive operators by order-
theoretic axioms, and provides the natural framework for arithmetic, harmonic, and geo-
metric means. In their foundational paper [7], Kubo and Ando developed this work by
considering a few number of order-theoretic properties, including monotonicity and the
transformer inequality.
The cone of completely positive maps is an equally natural ordered object, but it does
not carry a canonical Kubo–Ando calculus by itself. They arise naturally in the study
of C∗-algebras, dilation theory, and quantum information.
Recent work has also used
Kubo–Ando techniques in the study of operator-algebraic quantum channel divergences;
see, for example, [6].
There are also finite-dimensional ways of describing operator notions to completely pos-
itive maps. For maps between matrix algebras, the Choi–Jamio lkowski correspondence
identifies completely positive maps with positive Choi matrices [3, 4]. Applying this iden-
tification, one may define means, and more generally perspective-type constructions, for
completely positive maps by using the corresponding operator constructions to their Choi

show that the intrinsic geometric mean vanishes precisely when the two maps have no

is the setting of Frenkel–Mosonyi–Vrana–Weiner [8].
Another approach, based on the Pusz–Woronowicz theory of positive sesquilinear forms,
was developed by Okayasu [9] for completely positive maps between von Neumann al-
gebras. In that framework, Okayasu studies geometric means, parallel sums, harmonic
means, and Lebesgue-type decompositions, and also formulates Kubo–Ando type connec-
tions for completely positive maps.
The purpose of this section is to clarify how the present Radon–Nikodym construction
fits into this picture. In particular, we show that in the matrix-algebra case it agrees with
the Choi-matrix means, and we explain its relation both to Okayasu’s Pusz–Woronowicz
geometric mean.
5.1. Agreement with the Choi-matrix mean. We first compare the relative mean
with the Choi-matrix construction in the finite-dimensional case. Let
Φ : Md −→Mm
be linear, and let J(Φ) ∈Md ⊗Mm denote its Choi matrix. By Choi’s theorem, Φ is
completely positive if and only if J(Φ) ≥0, and the completely positive order corresponds
to the usual order of Choi matrices.
Thus, for completely positive maps Φ, Ψ : Md →Mm, one may define a Choi-matrix
mean by
J(ΦσChΨ) = J(Φ)σJ(Ψ).
