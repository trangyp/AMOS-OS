Abstract. This is a review of progress on the question whether noetherian Hopf algebras
always have finite injective dimension and related good homological properties. As well as
discussing in detail the main results giving positive answers for particular classes of Hopf
algebras, some consequences of such positive answers are also described.
Full definitions
and references are included, also sketches of some proofs. A considerable number of open
questions are listed, additional to the original question, which itself remains open after 30
years.

The primary purpose of this paper is to report on the current status of the following
question, which is a more precise version of one first asked in [12, §3.1, Question A and
remark following] and [15, §1.14]1. The terms used in the question are explained in Definition

2. Basic definitions and their interplay
We’ll focus thoughout on noetherian Hopf K-algebras. It will frequently be necessary to
assume that such an algebra is also affine, meaning finitely generated as a K-algebra, but it
is a moot point whether this additional hypothesis is necessary. That is, it is currently not
known whether the answer to the following question is “yes”.
Question 2.1. Is every noetherian Hopf K-algebra affine?
The answer is “yes” for a Hopf algebra which is pointed, commutative or cocommutative,
thanks to Molnar [56] for the last 2 cases and to Goodearl and Zhang [35] for the first2.
Definition 2.2. Let A be a noetherian K-algebra.
(i) If the injective dimensions of AA and AA are finite then A is said to have finite injective
dimension. In this case these integers are equal by [80], and are denoted by injdim(A).
(ii) A is regular if it has finite global dimension; that is, gldim(A) < ∞. Right global
dimension always equals left global dimension [74, Exercise 4.1.1]; and when gldim(A)
is finite, gldim(A) = injdim(A).
(iii) A is Artin–Schelter Gorenstein, usually abbreviated to AS–Gorenstein, if
(AS1) injdim(AA) = d < ∞;
(AS2) for every simple left A-module V with dimK(V ) < ∞, Extd

(AS3) the right A-module versions of (AS1) and (AS2) hold.
(iv) If A is AS-Gorenstein and gldim(A) < ∞, then A is called Artin–Schelter regular,
usually shortened to AS-regular.

3. Classes of Gorenstein and GK-Macaulay Hopf algebras
After a couple of preliminary lemmas, we record in §3.2 the progress which has been made
to date towards settling Question 1.1. Then, in §3.3 we focus on a particular class of examples,
namely the affine noetherian Hopf K-algebras which satisfy a PI, since the progress achieved
in that setting may serve as an imperfect template for more general future results.
3.1. Filtered-graded equipment. Initiated by Bjork and Ekstrom [8–10,30]), the analysis
of relations between the possession by a filtered K-algebra A of the various homological
properties listed in Definition 2.2 and by its associated graded algebra gr(A) appeared in a
number of papers from the mid-1980s onwards - most notably, see [45]. The following two
lemmas build on this earlier work.

In this section we review a number of consequences flowing from the possession by a noe-

4.1. The antipode (I). Takeuchi [71] showed that in general the antipode S of a Hopf
algebra H is not bijective. Nevertheless it has been conjectured, for example by Skryabin

bijective:
Theorem 4.1. (Lu, Oh, Wang, Yu, [51, Corollary 0.3]) If H is a noetherian AS-Gorenstein
Hopf algebra then its antipode S is bijective.

result that the antipode of an affine noetherian PI Hopf algebra is bijective.
