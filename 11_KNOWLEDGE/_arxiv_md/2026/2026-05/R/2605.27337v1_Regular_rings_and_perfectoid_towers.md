REGULAR RINGS AND PERFECTOID TOWERS
KAZUKI HAYASHI
Abstract. We prove a mixed-characteristic analogue of Kunz’s theorem in terms of perfectoid
towers: a Noetherian local ring of residue characteristic p is regular if and only if it admits a
ﬂat map to a Noetherian ring that extends to a perfectoid tower. This result is deduced from
another mixed-characteristic analogue due to O. Gabber and J. Lurie. We also characterize
regularity for perfectoid towers via vanishing of single higher Tor-module of the residue ﬁeld
with a perfectoid algebra.

Theorem A (Theorem 3.11). Let R be a commutative Noetherian local ring of residue charac-
teristic p. Then the following conditions are equivalent.
(1) The local ring R is regular.
(2) There exists a perfectoid tower (1.2) consisting of Noetherian rings such that for any
(or, equivalently, some) i ≥0 the transition map ti : Ri →Ri+1 is ﬂat.
Although this theorem—except for the parenthetical assertion—can be deduced from the
p-adic Kunz theorem of B. Bhatt, S. B. Iyengar, and L. Ma (see Remark 3.12), we present an
independent proof. We emphasize that our proof does not rely on the Auslander–Buchsbaum–
Serre regularity criterion. Thus we can apply Theorem A to obtain a new proof of the stability
of regularity under localization at prime ideals containing p (see Proposition 3.13).
On the other hand, Bhatt–Iyengar–Ma gave a sharper characterization of regularity via per-
fect(oid) algebras. For instance, if (R, m, k) is an excellent reduced Noetherian local Fp-algebra,
it suﬃces to assume the vanishing of a single Tor-module TorR
q (Rperf, k) ([BIM19, Theorem 4.13
(1)]).1 A key input in their proof is that systems of parameters for R are weakly proregular on
Rperf. In this paper, we establish such a rigidity result for more general perfect(oid) algebras:

regular if any one of the following conditions is satisﬁed.

Notations and conventions.
• We consistently ﬁx a prime number p > 0.
• All rings are assumed to be commutative and unital (unless otherwise stated).
• For an Fp-algebra R, let ϕ = ϕR : R →R denote the absolute Frobenius.
• By a pair we simply mean a couple (A, I) consisting of a ring A and an ideal I of A.
When the ideal I is principal, say I = (a), then we often write (A, a) in place of (A, I).
• For a pair (A, I) and an A-module M, we say that an element x ∈M is I-torsion if
for all a ∈I there exists an integer n > 0 such that anx = 0. Let MI-tor denote the
A-submodule of M consisting of all I-torsion elements in M. We say that M is I-torsion
free if MI-tor = 0. Note that we follow the terminologies in [FKI].
• For a pair (A, I), when we say an A-module M is I-adically complete, we always mean
that M is Hausdorﬀcomplete with respect to the I-adic topology.
