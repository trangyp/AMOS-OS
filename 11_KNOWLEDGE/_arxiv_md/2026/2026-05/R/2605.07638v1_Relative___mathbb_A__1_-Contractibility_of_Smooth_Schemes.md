RELATIVE A1-CONTRACTIBILITY OF SMOOTH SCHEMES
ADRIEN DUBOULOZ, KRISHNA KUMAR MADHAVAN VIJAYALAKSHMI, AND PAUL ARNE ØSTVÆR
ABSTRACT. We study smooth morphisms f : X →S that are A1-contractible in the unstable A1-
homotopy category H(S). For base schemes S of finite Krull dimension, we show that A1-contractibility
is a fiberwise property: such a morphism is A1-contractible if and only if all its geometric fibers are
A1-contractible. We apply this criterion to An-fiber spaces, obtaining a geometric description of their
A1-contractibility in terms of local factorizations as towers of torsors under vector bundles, building
on results of Asanuma. In low relative dimensions, we establish rigidity results. In relative dimension
1, A1-contractible morphisms over normal bases are precisely Zariski locally trivial A1-bundles. In
relative dimension 2, we show that over bases with characteristic zero residue fields, A1-contractible
morphisms are A2-fiber spaces, and we obtain Zariski local triviality under additional hypotheses on
the base. We also exhibit counterexamples in positive and mixed characteristic, and formulate open
problems concerning the existence of exotic A1-contractible surfaces.
INTRODUCTION
A central theme in A1-homotopy theory is to understand how motivic homotopy types reflect
the geometry of algebraic varieties. Although A1-contractible varieties are indistinguishable from
a point in the unstable A1-homotopy category, they can exhibit rich and subtle geometry, especially
in families. This leads to natural questions: is A1-contractibility detected fiberwise for smooth
morphisms? What geometric constraints does it impose? And how does it interact with classical
structures such as affine fibrations and vector bundle torsors?
While these questions have been extensively studied over fields, their relative counterparts re-
main largely unexplored. In particular, A1-contractibility over higher-dimensional bases—such as
Dedekind schemes—has not been systematically investigated. One aim of this paper is to develop
a structural theory of A1-contractible morphisms over a general base.
A guiding analogy comes from topology: fiber bundles with contractible fibers are typically
homotopy equivalent to the base, and often trivial under mild hypotheses. Thus contractibility
tends to eliminate obstructions and impose strong local structure. We show that a similar principle
holds in A1-homotopy theory: for smooth morphisms, A1-contractibility is a fiberwise property.
Algebraic varieties that are weakly contractible in the sense of Morel–Voevodsky A1-homotopy
theory have been intensively studied in recent years. In dimensions d ≥3, this has led to the con-
struction of many smooth A1-contractible varieties not isomorphic to affine space, including fami-
lies with positive-dimensional moduli (see [AØ21] for a survey). In low dimensions, however, A1-
contractibility exhibits strong rigidity. The affine line A1 is the only A1-contractible quasi-projective
curve. In dimension 2, it was only recently shown that, over fields of characteristic zero, the affine
plane A2 is the unique smooth A1-contractible affine surface [CR24]. In positive characteristic, the
existence of exotic A1-contractible surfaces remains open.

The first result refines the analogous criterion in stable motivic homotopy theory and relies on
a combination of base change techniques and a gluing argument originating in [MV99]. It shows
that H(S) does not detect variation in the isomorphism types of the fibers of a smooth family of A1-
contractible varieties. Consequently, all known smooth non-isotrivial families of A1-contractible
varieties of relative dimension d ≥3 over positive-dimensional bases S (see, for example, [DF18,
DG25, MV25]) are A1-contractible over S. The second result builds on Asanuma’s work on quasi-
polynomial algebras [Asa82, Asa87] and provides a geometric explanation for the ubiquity of A1-
contractible morphisms arising from affine fibrations. The remaining problems concern, on the
one hand, the classification of relatively A1-contractible morphisms of relative dimension at most
2, and, on the other hand, the local structure of such morphisms in the Zariski and Nisnevich
topologies.
A central theme of the paper is the contrast between low and high dimensions. While dimension
≥3 admits abundant nontrivial families of A1-contractible varieties, dimensions ≤2 exhibit strong
rigidity. This dichotomy parallels classical phenomena in topology and affine algebraic geometry.

2. RELATIVE A1-CONTRACTIBILITY OF An-FIBER SPACES

3.3. A1-contractibility of smooth schemes of relative dimension 2. Relative dimension 2 is the
first setting in which A1-contractibility exhibits genuinely new phenomena. Unlike the case of
curves, where a complete classification is available, the interaction between A1-homotopy theory
and the birational geometry of surfaces leads to a much richer and only partially understood pic-
ture.
Over fields of characteristic zero, recent advances show that A1-contractibility is rigid: smooth
A1-contractible surfaces are isomorphic to A2. This result combines techniques from A1-homotopy
theory with deep structure theorems for affine surfaces. In positive characteristic, however, several
key ingredients break down. In particular, separability issues obstruct the use of A1-curves, and
existing methods do not rule out the existence of exotic A1-contractible surfaces.
In the relative setting, new phenomena already appear over one-dimensional bases. While A1-
contractibility forces the structure of an A2-fiber space under the assumption that the residue fields
have characteristic zero, the problem of Zariski local triviality remains widely open beyond special
cases (e.g. Sathaye’s theorem for Dedekind schemes in characteristic zero). Moreover, counterex-
amples in mixed characteristic show that naive generalizations fail. These considerations lead to
the following guiding questions:
» Are there smooth affine A1-contractible surfaces in positive characteristic that are not iso-
morphic to A2?
» When is an A2-fiber space locally trivial in the Zariski topology?
» To what extent do cancellation and rigidity phenomena persist in relative dimension 2?
