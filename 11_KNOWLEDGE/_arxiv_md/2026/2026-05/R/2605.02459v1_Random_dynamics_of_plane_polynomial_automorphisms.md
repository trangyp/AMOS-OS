Convention. Throughout this paper, a measure on C2 will always mean a Borel
probability measure.
Our main goal is to classify µ-stationary measures when the group Γµ := ⟨Σµ⟩
generated by the support of µ is non-elementary. In this framework, we expect that
the existence of a µ-stationary measure is a rare phenomenon.
Jung’s theorem [32] asserts that the group G of polynomial automorphisms of
the plane is the amalgamated product of its affine and elementary subgroups. Any
automorphism which is not conjugate to any elementary map is called loxodromic.
A H´enon map h(x, y) = (y, p(y)−ax) with a ∈C\{0} and deg(p) ≥2 is loxodromic.
By a theorem of Friedland and Milnor [25], every loxodromic automorphism is con-
jugate to a product of H´enon maps. H´enon maps have a rich dynamical behaviour
and loxodromic automorphisms are precisely the elements of G that have positive
topological entropy. We say that µ is non-elementary if Γµ contains two loxo-
dromic elements generating a free group of rank two, and purely loxodromic if
Γµ \ {1} contains only loxodromic elements.
Our first result shows that non-trivial recurrence can only appear in compact
subsets of the plane.
Theorem 1.1. Let µ be a finitely supported, non-elementary and purely loxodromic
measure on G. If ν is a µ-stationary measure on C2, then ν is compactly supported.
The assumptions in the above theorem are not very restrictive. Indeed, by a
theorem of Lamy [36], if f and g are two loxodromic elements that do not satisfy
any relation of the form fn = gm for some non-zero n, m ∈Z, then, there exists
k ≥1 such that f k and gk generate a non-elementary, purely loxodromic subgroup
of G. For any d > 1, we denote by Gd the variety of automorphisms of degree at
most d. We have that, outside a countable union of hypersurfaces in Gd × Gd, two
automorphisms generate a free, purely loxodromic subgroup. See Section 2.2.

1.4. Related results. Stiffness property was introduced by Furstenberg [26] in the
framework of homogeneous dynamics. In a seminal work, Benoist–Quint [10] have
given a classification of stationary measures in this context. Since then, their ideas
have been extended to increasingly more general frameworks: Eskin–Mirzakhani
[23] for SL2(R)-actions on moduli space of translation surfaces, Brown–Rodriguez
Hertz [12] for smooth actions on real surfaces, and Brown–Eskin–Filip–Rodriguez
Hertz [11] for smooth actions in any dimension. See the introduction of [13] for a
more detailed historical account.
Inspired by the work of Brown and Rodriguez Hertz, Cantat–Dujardin [13] have
studied random dynamics on compact complex surfaces. They establish stiffness
on the real part X(R) of a K3 surface X defined over the reals when µ is a finitely
supported measure on the group of automorphisms of X such that Γµ does not
preserve any curve. Building on the results of Cantat–Dujardin and Brown–Eskin–
Filip–Rodriguez Hertz, Roda [44] has extended the stiffness property for groups
of automorphisms of complex K3 surfaces that do not preserve any curve, that is,
stiffness holds also on X(C). If a µ-stationary measure ν is hyperbolic, then the
stable manifolds W s(ω, q) are almost surely complex one-dimensional submanifolds
biholomorphic to C. When Γµ does not preserve any curve, these stable manifolds
depend non-trivially on the itinerary ω. This property is crucial in order to apply

We now define the Bass-Serre tree of G which is a simplicial tree T on which
G acts faithfully by isometries [36, 45]. The set of vertices of T is G/A ∪G/E,
and there is an edge between the left classes gA and g′E if and only if there exists
h ∈G such that hA = gA and hE = g′E. Notice that if h′ satisfies the same
property, then h′ = hs for some s ∈S. Thus, the set of edges of T is represented
by G/S. There are no cycles thanks to the amalgamated product property. We
first endow T with the natural distance dT of a graph and we then consider the
geometric realization of T where an edge is isometric to [0, 1] with the euclidean
distance. Thus, T is path-connected, and there is a unique geodesic between two
points. Notice that T is not locally compact: there is an uncountable set of edges
starting from one vertex, see [36, Section 2] for a description of representatives of
G/A and G/E.
The action by left translations f(gS) := fgS defines a representation of G in the
isometry group of T . The action is faithful by a theorem of Lamy [36, Remarque
3.5]. An element g ∈G is elliptic if g fixes a point in T . Otherwise, g is called
loxodromic. In this case, g acts on T by translation along an infinite geodesic,
called the axis of g. An automorphism is elliptic precisely when it is conjugate
to an elementary map. Thus, for an automorphism g, the following are equivalent

(1) g is loxodromic,
(2) g is conjugate to a product of H´enon maps,

2.3. Random walks on hyperbolic spaces. Random walks on locally compact
trees are well understood, and under reasonable assumptions on the law, we obtain
classical properties such as transience, convergence to the boundary at linear speed
and identification of the Poisson boundary. Here, we cannot use these results as
we are working with the Bass-Serre tree which is not locally compact. We state a
theorem of Maher and Tiozzo which combines results from [40, 41] that concerns

5.1. Examples. We can construct an example of a non-elementary and purely
loxodromic µ with no µ-stationary measures as follows. If f is a loxodromic auto-
morphism, we denote by Kf ⊂C2 the compact set of points with bounded back-
ward and forward orbit. Recall that if Σµ is symmetric, then the support of any
µ-stationary measure is a Γµ-invariant compact set. Thus, if f and g are two loxo-
dromic automorphisms generating a non-elementary, purely loxodromic subgroup,
such that Kf and Kg do not intersect, then for any measure µ supported on f, g
and their inverses, there exists no µ-stationary measure. If f and g are loxodromic
automorphisms generating a non-elementary subgroup, it suffices to conjugate g by
a large translation g′ := τgτ −1 to obtain that Kf and Kg′ are disjoint.
It is also not difficult to construct examples of non-elementary, purely loxodromic
measures having a finite orbit. Indeed, it suffices to take a ping-pong group as in
Section 2.2, with f a H´enon map having a fixed point at the origin.
To our knowledge, there is no example of a non-elementary, purely loxodromic
and non-dissipative measure µ that admits a non-atomic µ-stationary measure.
