Rainbow panconnectivity in a graph collection
Menghan Ma∗
Lihua You†
Xiaoxue Zhang‡
Abstract
Let G = {G1, . . . , Gn−1} be a collection of not necessarily distinct n-vertex
graphs with the same vertex set V . A path P with V (P) ⊆V and |E(P)| ≤n−1 is
rainbow in G, if there exists an injection ϕ: E(P) →[n −1] such that e ∈E(Gϕ(e))
for each e ∈E(P). The graph collection G is said to be rainbow panconnected if for
every pair of vertices x, y ∈V , there exists a rainbow path of k vertices joining x
and y in G for every integer k ∈[dG(x, y) + 1, n], where dG(x, y) is the length of a
shortest rainbow path between x and y in G. In this paper, we study the rainbow
panconnectivity of G under the minimum degree condition. Our result improves
upon the corresponding results of [J. Graph Theory, 104(2)(2023), 341–359] and
[Electron. J. Combin., 32(4)(2025), #P4.17].

Introduction
In recent years, transversals in graph collections have become a popular research
topic.
This concept was first formally introduced by Joos and Kim [7] in 2020.
Let
G = {G1, . . . , Gs} be a collection of not necessarily distinct graphs with the same vertex
set V .
For a graph H with V (H) ⊆V and |E(H)| ≤s, if there exists an injection
ϕ: E(H) →[s] such that e ∈E(Gϕ(e)) for each e ∈E(H), then we say that H is a partial
G-transversal if |E(H)| < s, H is a G-transversal if |E(H)| = s. That is to say, each
edge of H comes from a distinct graph Gi with i ∈[s], we further say that H is rainbow
in G. The following question was proposed by Joos and Kim in [7].
Problem 1.1 ([7]). Let H be a graph with m edges and G = {G1, . . . , Gs} be a collection
of not necessarily distinct graphs with the same vertex set V , where m ≤s.
Which
properties imposed on G yield a rainbow copy of H?

Focusing on Problem 1.1, there has been a great deal of work done on it. Scholars
have imposed a variety of constraints on graph collections, including Dirac-type condition
[4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 14, 16], Ore-type condition [8, 10, 11, 12], edge number condition [1, 2,
13, 18] and so on. For more results, we refer the readers to a survey [15].
A path (cycle) on l vertices is referred to as an l-path (a l-cycle), respectively. A
path (cycle) which contains all vertices of a graph is called a Hamiltonian path (cycle). A
graph G is said to be panconnected, if for every pair of vertices x, y ∈V (G), there exists a
k-path joining x and y in G for every integer k ∈[dG(x, y) + 1, n]. The minimum degree
condition that guarantees a graph is panconnected was given by Williamson [17].
Theorem 1.1 ([17]). If G is a graph of order n ≥4 such that δ(G) ≥n+2
2 , then G is
panconnected.
In 2023, Li et al. [8] generalized Theorem 1.1 to the setting of graph transversals, i.e.
the rainbow version of Theorem 1.1. A collection G of n-vertex graphs with the same
vertex set V is said to be rainbow panconnected if for every pair of vertices x, y ∈V , there
exists a rainbow k-path joining x and y in G for every integer k ∈[dG(x, y) + 1, n], where
dG(x, y) is the length of a shortest rainbow path between x and y in G. In [8], they also
defined a special graph collection as follows, and obtained Theorem 1.2.
Definition 1.1 ([8]). Let n be odd, Q1 be an empty graph with n−1
2
vertices and Q2 be
a graph on n+1
2
vertices with δ(Q2) ≥1 such that one component of Q2 is a single edge.
Define Fm = {F1, . . . , Fm} as a collection of n-vertex graphs with the same vertex set such
that Fi = Q1 ∨Q2 for each i ∈[m].
Theorem 1.2 ([8]). Let G = {G1, . . . , Gn} be a collection of not necessarily distinct n-
vertex graphs with the same vertex set V . If δ(Gi) ≥n+1
2
for each i ∈[n], then either G
is rainbow panconnected or G = Fn.
Clearly, the edges of a rainbow path in Theorem 1.2 only come from at most n −1
graphs in the collection G = {G1, . . . , Gn}. In 2025, Sun et al. [16] established the same
minimum degree condition for a collection of n −1 graphs to be rainbow panconnected.
Theorem 1.3 ([16]). Let n ≥4 be an even integer or a sufficiently large odd integer, G =
{G1, . . . , Gn−1} be a collection of not necessarily distinct n-vertex graphs with the same
vertex set V . If δ(Gi) ≥n+1
2
for each i ∈[n −1], then either G is rainbow panconnected
or G = Fn−1.
However, Theorem 1.3 requires n to be sufficiently large when n is an odd integer.
The authors in [16] conjectured that Theorem 1.3 holds for all n. Motivated by this, we
confirm the conjecture and obtain the following result.
Theorem 1.4. Let n ≥3 and G = {G1, . . . , Gn−1} be a collection of not necessarily
distinct n-vertex graphs with the same vertex set V . If δ(Gi) ≥n+1
2
for each i ∈[n −1],
then either G is rainbow panconnected or G = Fn−1.

Here, we introduce some basic notation and concepts in graph theory. In this paper,
we only consider finite and simple graphs. Let G = (V (G), E(G)) be a simple graph
with vertex set V (G) and edge set E(G). For a vertex u ∈V (G), the set of neighbours
of a vertex u in G is denoted by NG(u), and dG(u) = |NG(u)| is the degree of u in G.
Let δ(G) = min{dG(u) : u ∈V (G)} denote the minimum degree of G and σ2(G) =
min{dG(u) + dG(v) : u, v ∈V (G), uv /∈E(G)} denote the minimum degree sum of
any pair of nonadjacent vertices in G.
For any two vertices u, v ∈V (G), dG(u, v) is
defined as the length of a shortest path between u and v. For two disjoint graphs G1
and G2, the join of G1 and G2, denoted by G1 ∨G2, is the graph obtained from G1
and G2 by adding all edges between V (G1) and V (G2). For U ⊆V (G), the induced
subgraph G[U] satisfies V (G[U]) = U and E(G[U]) = {uv ∈E(G) : u, v ∈U}. For two
disjoint subsets U, W ⊂V (G), G[U, W] denotes the bipartite subgraph of G with vertex
bipartition (U, W) and edge set E(G[U, W]) = {uw ∈E(G) : u ∈U, w ∈W}. We use
EG[U, W] for E(G[U, W]) for short. For positive integers a < b, let [a] = {1, 2, . . . , a} and
[a, b] = {a, a+1, . . . , b}. For any terminology and notation that are not explicitly defined,
we refer the reader to [3].

Preliminaries
In this section, we present some necessary definitions and known results that will be
used later.
For a path P = u1u2 · · · us, we denote the subpath of P between ui and uj by uiPuj,
where i, j ∈[s]. We use |P| to represent the number of vertices of path P. For two
distinct vertices vi, vj on a cycle C = v1v2 · · · vsv1 with i < j, we denote the two paths
between them as the forward segment viCvj = vivi+1 · · · vj and the backward segment
viC−vj = vivi−1 · · · vj, where subscripts are taken modulo s. For a subgraph H of G and
a vertex u ∈V (G) \ V (H), let NG(u, H) = NG(u) ∩V (H) and dG(u, H) = |NG(u, H)|.

then G is rainbow Hamiltonian connected.
