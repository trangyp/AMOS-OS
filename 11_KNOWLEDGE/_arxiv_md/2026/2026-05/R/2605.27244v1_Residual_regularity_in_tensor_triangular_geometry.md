RESIDUAL REGULARITY IN TENSOR TRIANGULAR GEOMETRY
EMMY VAN ROOY
Abstract. We investigate a new notion of regularity for tensor triangulated categories,
called residual regularity. We show that residual regularity descends and ascends via
finite separable extensions and we classify all finite groups whose derived category of
permutation modules is residually regular.

Introduction
In [Bal05], Balmer associates to any essentially small tensor triangulated category K a
geometric space Spc(K) in a universal way. This space Spc(K) has since been referred to as
the Balmer spectrum of K, and the resulting theory is named tensor triangular geometry
— tt-geometry for short. Since then, tt-geometry has become a well-established technique
to study tensor triangulated categories (abbreviated similarly as tt-categories). Among
many other things, tt-geometry allows one to classify objects in the essentially small tt-
category K that are the same up to the basic operations available in K: taking suspensions,
cones, direct sums and summands or tensoring with objects. In fact, this classification is
equivalent to the computation of the Balmer spectrum Spc(K). Furthermore, the theory
covers a large class of specialised, and remarkably distinct, areas of mathematics, ranging
from algebra to analysis. To name a few, it applies to algebraic geometry, stable homotopy
theory, modular representation theory, motivic theory and noncommutative topology. In
this way, tt-geometry gives a unifying approach to many fields of mathematics. We refer
to the surveys [Bal10; Bal20a; Ste18] for an overview of the basic theory, its virtues and
its applications.
Often, the essentially small tt-category K sits inside a larger, but still orderly, tt-
category T. For example, think of perfect complexes Dperf(R) over a commutative ring
R inside the unbounded derived category D(R); or the Spanier-Whitehead category of
finite pointed CW-complexes SHfin inside the stable homotopy category of topological
spectra SH. More precisely, T is a rigidly-compactly generated tt-category and K =: Tc is
its subcategory of rigid-compact objects. Such a tt-category T is referred to as a ‘big’ tt-
category. The subcategory Tc is always an essentially small tt-category, so we can consider
its Balmer spectrum Spc(Tc). Approaching the study of T through the tt-geometry of Tc
is a rich topic, and this is precisely the context that we will be in today.

A common theme in tt-geometry is to transpose ideas and techniques between the
various fields it brings together. Taking inspiration from algebraic geometry in particular
has often proved fruitful.
In algebraic geometry, regularity is a well-studied condition
on Noetherian schemes, and regular points are, in a precise sense, better behaved than
non-regular (i.e. singular) ones. Let us, with the power of foresight, recall the following
characterisation of regularity for a commutative Noetherian ring R: a point p ∈Spec(R)
is regular if and only if the residue field Rp/pRp is perfect over the local ring Rp, i.e. it is a
compact object in the category D(Rp). In this work we investigate a notion of regularity
for ‘big’ tt-categories, dubbed residual regularity, which mirrors this characterisation of
classical regularity in commutative algebra.
The road to residual regularity is paved by a type of residue objects that are afforded
to us through the framework of homological residue fields [BKS19]. These homological
residue fields are homological symmetric monoidal functors
hH : T →A(T)H,
from our ‘big’ tt-category T to various tensor abelian categories A(T)H. The parameter
H ranges over a set called the homological spectrum of T, which is denoted by Spch(Tc).
The homological spectrum is very close to the Balmer spectrum in the sense that there is
a canonical map
πT : Spch(Tc) →Spc(Tc),
that is always surjective and is known to be bijective for many standard tt-categories across
algebraic geometry, modular representation theory and stable homotopy theory. However,
it should be noted that it can fail to be injective, as was recently shown in [BHR26]. The
theory of homological residue fields has already shown its mettle: they give rise to a tensor
triangular nilpotence theorem [Bal20c] and a support theory for non-compact objects of
‘big’ tt-categories [Bal20b].
The homological residue fields furnish us with the aforementioned residue objects as
follows: in A(T)H the tensor unit 1 admits an injective hull hH(κ(H)) that comes from a
unique object κ(H) in T via the functor hH. These residue objects κ(H) are in general
non-compact objects of T. They play the role of the classical residue fields in commut-
ative algebra and indeed recapture exactly those in the case of the derived category of a
commutative ring. The tt-analogue of regularity presents itself:
Definition. A ‘big’ tt-category T is called residually regular if for every point P ∈Spc(Tc),
there exists an H ∈π−1
T ({P}) such that the residue object κ(H) is a compact object in
the local category TP := T/ Loc(P).
This definition was proposed by Paul Balmer in private communication.
It follows
immediately that residual regularity can be checked locally on the Balmer spectrum. Fur-
thermore, by construction, a Noetherian scheme X is regular if and only if its ‘big’ derived
category D(X) = DQcoh(X) is residually regular. Using the extensive theory of homolo-
gical residue fields, one can show that residual regularity of T implies that the map πT is
in fact a bijection and that the Balmer spectrum Spc(Tc) is a weakly Noetherian topolo-
gical space. This already excludes the stable homotopy category of spectra SH from being
residually regular, as its Balmer spectrum is not weakly Noetherian; see Example 2.13.
There have been various efforts in the literature to generalise regularity to other settings,
such as e.g. [BCRPZ24; BIKP24; Kra20; KPV25; Nee26; Orl16; Rou08] for triangulated
categories and [BL14; GS20] for differential graded algebras and structured ring spectra.
This is also intimately related to the search for an analogue of the bounded derived category
of a Noetherian scheme for more general triangulated categories; see [Nee26; Rou08]. We
return to this topic in Remark 2.6, but the definitive relationship of these other notions
to residual regularity will require further investigation.

In modular representation theory, the most well-known ‘big’ tt-categories are arguably
the stable module category Stab(kG) and the homotopy category of injectives K Inj(kG),
for a finite group G and a field k. In recent years, another actor has entered the scene, the
derived category of permutation kG-modules, denoted by DPerm(G; k). Permutation kG-
modules are easy to grasp: they are simply the k-linearisations of G-sets. Informally then,
the ‘big’ tt-category DPerm(G; k) is a certain localisation of the homotopy category of all
permutation kG-modules, and it admits the categories K Inj(kG) and Stab(kG) as further
localisations. Permutation modules are closely related to equivariant homotopy theory,
Mackey functors and Artin motives; see [BG23].
The tt-geometry of these categories
DPerm(G; k) has been studied extensively by Balmer–Gallauer in [BG25a].
Our main result classifies all finite groups whose derived category of permutation mod-
ules is residually regular:
Theorem (Theorem 4.20). Let G be a finite group and k a field of positive characteristic
p dividing the order of G. The derived category of permutation kG-modules DPerm(G; k)
is residually regular if and only if p = 2 and all Sylow p-subgroups of G are cyclic.
For the worried reader, we mention that none of the points at which we prove that resid-
ual regularity of DPerm(G; k) fails ‘come from’ Stab(kG) (or K Inj(kG) for that matter);
all points of Stab(kG) are either residually regular or unknown. Indeed, as Stab(kG)c is a
strongly generated triangulated category, it is the expectation that Stab(kG) is residually
regular for any choice of finite group G and field k.
A core part of the proof of this classification is a technical result concerning separable
extensions of tt-categories, as introduced in [Bal11].
These are special kinds of func-
tors between tt-categories which can be thought of as an analogue of ´etale extensions in
algebraic geometry. Separable extensions are ubiquitous in tt-geometry. In modular rep-
resentation theory, the restriction to any subgroup gives rise to a separable extension at
the level of the derived category of permutation modules. The above classification follows
grosso modo by doing some hands-on computations for specific small groups and then
applying the following result to the separable extensions induced by restriction to these
groups:
Theorem (Corollary 3.18). Let F : T →S be a finite separable extension of finite degree
and let Q ∈Spc(Sc). Suppose that πS : Spch(Sc) →Spc(Sc) is a bijection and that Spc(Sc)
is Noetherian. Then S is residually regular at the point Q if and only if T is residually
regular at the point P := φF (Q), where φF : Spc(Sc) →Spc(Tc).
We refer to the body of the paper for the unexplained hypotheses in this result, and
simply remark that these are relatively mild conditions. Heuristically, the result consti-
tutes a descent-ascent statement for residual regularity that can be viewed as a tt-analogue
of (classical) regularity being an ´etale local property in algebraic geometry. In fact, the
descent part (the left-to-right direction) in this descent-ascent statement already holds
under weaker conditions; see Proposition 2.21.
Finally, we also point out another result with a counterpart in algebraic geometry. When
R is a commutative Noetherian ring that is regular at a closed point m ∈Spec(R) (i.e. m
is a maximal ideal of R), then the residue field Rm/mRm is a perfect complex already in
D(R), not just in the stalk category D(Rm). In Proposition 2.16, we show that the same is
true in tt-geometry for the residue objects of closed points, under the condition that the
closed point satisfies an additional topological hypothesis, which is automatically satisfied
when the spectrum Spc(Tc) is Noetherian.
The outline of the paper is relatively straightforward. In Section 1 we recall the relevant
details of the theory of homological residue fields. Thereafter, Section 2 is dedicated to
the definition of residual regularity and its first consequences, among which is the descent
statement of the above descent-ascent result. Next, in Section 3, we start by recalling the

Conventions
We often write ‘tt’ to abbreviate ‘tensor triangulated’ or ‘tensor triangular’.
Throughout, a ‘big’ tt-category refers to a rigidly-compactly generated tensor triangu-
lated category T, as in [BF11]. This means that T admits all set-indexed coproducts, its
compact objects and its rigid objects coincide, and the essentially small subcategory Tc
of compact-rigid objects generates T as a localising triangulated subcategory. The tensor
product of T will be denoted by ⊗, the unit by 1 and the suspension functor by Σ. The
tt-category Tc is essentially small, so we can consider its Balmer spectrum Spc(Tc). We
will write suppT(−) for the universal support theory on Tc, i.e.
suppT(a) = {P ∈Spc(Tc) | a /∈P},
for a ∈Tc.
A tensor triangulated functor F : T →S between two ‘big’ tt-categories is called geo-
metric if it preserves all coproducts. By Brown–Neeman Representability [Nee96], any
geometric tt-functor admits a right adjoint U : S →T, which is triangulated, lax-monoidal
and preserves coproducts. Moreover, the adjunction F ⊣U satisfies a projection formula:
U(X ⊗F(Y )) ∼= U(X) ⊗Y for objects X ∈S and Y ∈T. Since F is symmetric monoidal,
it preserves rigid objects and therefore restricts to a tt-functor F : Tc →Sc. Thus, F
induces a continuous map on Balmer spectra that we denote by φF : Spc(Sc) →Spc(Tc).
For a collection of objects S ⊆T, we denote by thick(S), respectively Loc(S), the thick
triangulated subcategory generated by S, respectively localising triangulated subcategory
generated by S. By contrast, the thick ⊗-ideal generated by S is denoted by ⟨S⟩.
Analogously, but by abuse of notation, when A is a Grothendieck abelian category with
colimit-preserving symmetric monoidal structure and S ⊆A is a collection of objects, we
denote by Loc(S), respectively ⟨S⟩, the localising Serre subcategory, respectively Serre
⊗-ideal generated by S.

2. Residual regularity
The following definition was proposed by Paul Balmer in private communication.
Definition 2.1. A ‘big’ tt-category T is residually regular at the point P ∈Spc(Tc) if there
exists a homological prime H ∈π−1
T ({P}) such that the residue object κ(H) is a compact
object in the stalk category TP = T/ Loc(P).
A ‘big’ tt-category T is residually regular if it is residually regular at all points of Spc(Tc).
Any reasonable definition of regularity in tt-geometry should recapture the classical
concept of regularity in algebraic geometry, when applied to the derived category of a
Noetherian scheme. The following example shows that this is indeed the case for residual
regularity, while simultaneously further justifying the terminology for the residue objects.
Example 2.2. Let R be a commutative ring with derived category D(R). Then T = D(R)
is a ‘big’ tt-category with Tc ∼= Dperf(R) = Kb(proj(R)). As a consequence of Thomason’s
work [Tho97], its Balmer spectrum is homeomorphic to the classical Zariski spectrum
Spec(R); see [Bal05, Corollary 5.6]. Moreover, if P = P(p) ∈Spc(Tc) is the point corres-
ponding to a prime ideal p ∈Spec(R), then the stalk category TP is naturally equivalent to
the derived category D(Rp). By [Bal20c, Corollary 5.11], T = D(R) satisfies the steel con-
dition. Take H the homological prime corresponding to the Zariski prime p ∈Spec(R) ∼=
Spc(Tc). In [BC21, Corollary 3.3], Balmer and Cameron show that the residue object
κ(H) is isomorphic to the (classical) residue field κ(p) = Rp/pRp ∈D(R).
Altogether, if R is a Noetherian ring, it follows immediately that D(R) is residually

Remark 2.6. As explained in the introduction, the definition of residual regularity takes

any sufficiently general conditions to ensure that the residue objects are cohomologically
locally finitely presented. In a similar fashion, in [BIKP24], regularity is defined for certain

Proposition 4.16. Let k be a field of characteristic 2. The category DPerm(C2n; k) is
residually regular.
Proof. We prove this by induction on n. In the base case, Spc(T(C2)c) has three points.
The rightmost point is M(1) which is always residually regular by Proposition 4.7. The
leftmost point is M(C2) and it is residually regular as observed in Example 4.12. Finally,
the stalk category at the bottom point is precisely Stab(kC2), which is a tt-field, and
therefore we also have residual regularity there by Example 2.7.
Now suppose that n > 1. The induction hypothesis states that T(C2n−1) is residually
regular at every point. The restriction functor T(C2n) →T(C2n−1) catches the rightmost
2n −1 points of Spc(T(C2n)c), see [BG25a, Lemma 8.5]. By Proposition 4.10, we know
that restriction ‘reflects’ residual regularity and thus these 2n −1 points are all residually
regular.
The remaining two points are taken care of by Proposition 4.14: The stalk
category of T(C2n) at M(C2n) is equivalent to the stalk category of DPerm(C2) at M(C2),
of which all points are residually regular by the base case.

Lemma 4.19. If G is a finite 2-group, then at least one of the following things are true:
• G is cyclic.
• G has a subgroup isomorphic to C2 × C2.
• G has a subgroup isomorphic to Q8.
Proof. If G does not contain a subgroup isomorphic to C2 × C2, then G must have a
unique subgroup of order 2 (since its centre is nontrivial). Then, by application of [Hal76,
Theorem 12.5.2] (originally in [Bur11, Section 105]), the group G must either be cyclic
or be a generalised quaternion group. In the latter case, G certainly contains a subgroup
isomorphic to Q8.
□
Theorem 4.20. Let G be a finite group and k a field of positive characteristic p dividing
the order of G. Then DPerm(G; k) is residually regular if and only if p = 2 and all Sylow
p-subgroups of G are cyclic.
Proof. For any p-Sylow subgroup P of G, the map

residually regular by Proposition 4.16. So, suppose that G is a non-cyclic 2-group. By
Lemma 4.19, G must have a subgroup H with H ∼= C2 × C2 or H ∼= Q8. In Example 4.18,
we found that T(C2×C2) is not residually regular at its closed point M(C2×C2). Now, note
