Abstract
Learning from implicit feedback in recommender systems is fundamentally challenged by per-
vasive label noise. While conventional denoising approaches often discard noisy instances to ensure
robustness, this strategy inevitably suffers from low data utilization. Alternative methods that
employ a Bayes-label transition matrix (BLTM) can leverage all available data, but their estimates
tend to be biased in practical recommendation scenarios. To address these limitations, this paper
proposes a Robust GMM-weighted Bayes-label Transition Matrix framework (RGBT). Our solu-
tion utilizes a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to derive instance-specific reliability scores, which
systematically calibrate the BLTM estimation to mitigate bias. Theoretical analysis confirms that
our approach, by leveraging the BLTM framework with GMM calibration, simultaneously ensures
full sample utilization, delivers consistent estimation, and critically, achieves a significant reduc-
tion in estimation variance. Extensive experiments on multiple real-world and synthetically flipped
datasets demonstrate that RGBT not only utilizes noisy samples more effectively than mainstream
reliable sample-based denoising methods, but also achieves significantly superior calibration ca-
pability of the transition matrix compared to state-of-the-art transition matrix-based denoising
approaches.

Introduction
The field of Recommender Systems (RS) has witnessed significant progress in recent years [LHX+24,
WZL+23, LLS+24, YQC+25]. This approach primarily leverages implicit feedback, such as clicks and
purchase records, based on the premise that these signals reflect users’ underlying preferences to model
interests and generate personalized suggestions [BHW+24, CHC+24, SWZ+24, LWZ+24, WSM+24,
KWJ25]. However, notable exceptions exist. For instance, a user might purchase an item only to
return it later, or click on a video but exit almost immediately. In such cases, the interactions do
not reflect genuine user interest and are thus characterized as noisy interactions in recent literature
[WFH+21, WXM+22, GDH+22, WGL+23, HWY+24].
Existing denoising methods typically address noisy implicit feedback by directly discarding un-
reliable samples to ensure robustness, yet this strategy inevitably leads to suboptimal data utiliza-
tion [WFH+21, WXM+22, GDH+22, WGL+23, HWY+24]. For instance, ADT [WFH+21] adaptively

ity of these approaches are heuristic in nature, lacking the theoretical foundations to guarantee that
the denoised model converges to the optimal cleaner.
Accurately estimating the noise transition matrix remains a fundamental challenge in learning from
noisy implicit feedback. Although Bayesian label transition matrix (BLTM) [YYH+22] methods theo-
retically enable full sample utilization, their practical application in recommendation scenarios is sub-
stantially constrained by estimation bias. The research community has pursued multiple strategies to
enhance transition matrix estimation, including statistically consistent modeling in RRFN [YL24], ma-
trix factorization in Dual-T [YLH+20], anchor point-free estimation in T-Revision [XLW+19], volume
minimization in VolMinNet [LLH+21], cycle-consistency regularization in CCR [CNW+22], instance-
dependent estimation in IDNT [WPQ+25], and self-supervised learning in CoNL [LYW+25]. Despite
these methodological advances, current approaches remain ill-suited for the complex, dynamic noise of
real-world systems. This inadequacy fundamentally manifests as irremediable estimation bias in the
transition matrix, which then propagates through the learning process, systematically distorting the
final recommendation model.
To address these interconnected limitations, we propose a novel Robust GMM-Weighted Bayes-label
Transition Framework (RGBT) that integrates GMM-based sample weighting with Bayesian transition
matrix correction. The main contributions of this work are threefold:
1) We develop a robust recommendation framework that effectively leverages noisy samples through
Bayesian label transition matrix (BLTM) modeling, while achieving more accurate bias correction in
transition matrix estimation via Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)-based calibration.
2) We provide theoretical guarantees demonstrating that our framework maintains full sample
utilization through the BLTM and delivers consistent, low-variance estimation via GMM-based cali-
bration, outperforming other transition-matrix methods in statistical efficiency.
3) Extensive experiments on multiple real-world and synthetic flip datasets show that RGBT not
only utilizes noisy samples more effectively than mainstream reliable sample-based denoising meth-
ods, but also achieves significantly superior transition matrix calibration compared to state-of-the-art
transition matrix-based denoising approaches.

Experiments
To demonstrate the effectiveness and generalization of our proposed framework, RGBT, we compare
the results of our method with state-of-the-art recommendation models on four backbones and three
real-world datasets. We aim to answer the following research questions:
• RQ1: How does our proposed RGBT framework perform in terms of noise robustness and sample
efficiency compared to standard training and state-of-the-art denoising methods?
• RQ2: How accurate is the transition matrix estimated by our calibration method on synthetic
flip datasets compared to existing matrix-based approaches, and how does this accuracy translate
to debiasing performance?
• RQ3: How sensitive is the framework’s performance to its key components (e.g., reliability
weighting and BLTM calibration) and hyperparameters? What is their respective impact on both
synthetic (flip) and real-world noisy datasets in terms of sample utilization and final accuracy?
• RQ4: Does our iterative distillation with progressive reliability thresholding lead to more ef-
fective sample incorporation and better matrix estimation than strategies with fixed confidence
thresholds?
4.1
Experimental Settings
4.1.1
Datasets
We conduct extensive comparative experiments on three publicly available datasets: Adressa [GDH+22,
WFH+21, HWY+24, WXM+22], MovieLens [GDH+22, WXM+22, HWY+24], and Yelp [GDH+22,
WFH+21, HWY+24].
• Adressa: A news reading dataset. We strictly filter the test set to include only interactions
with a dwell time of at least 10 seconds, treating them as reliable positive signals.
• MovieLens: A widely used movie rating dataset. The test set includes only interactions with a
rating of five.
• Yelp: A business recommendation dataset. The test set comprises only interactions with ratings
higher than three.
Note that these filters are applied exclusively to the test set. The training and validation sets remain
noisy and unfiltered to simulate real-world scenarios.
4.1.2
Noise Simulation for RQ2
To rigorously evaluate the transition matrix estimation in RQ2, we follow the synthetic noise genera-
tion protocol proposed in RRFN [YL24]. Since real-world noise distributions are often unknown, we
construct synthetic datasets using the clean MovieLens data by injecting two representative types of
rating flip noise to simulate diverse noisy environments:
• Symmetric Flip Noise: This setting simulates random noise where user ratings are corrupted
without a specific pattern. For a clean rating y, the noisy label ˜y is generated such that the label
flips to any other rating class k ̸= y with a uniform probability of
η
K−1, where K is the total
number of rating classes and η is the noise rate.
• Pairwise Flip Noise: This setting simulates adjacent confusion, which is common in rating
systems (e.g., confusing a 4-star rating with a 5-star rating). For a clean rating y, the noisy label
˜y is flipped to a specific adjacent class (e.g., y →y −1) with probability η.
We vary the noise rate η within the range of {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4} to assess the robustness of our matrix
estimation under varying noise intensities.

4.1.3
Evaluation protocols
Following the protocol established in prior research, we split all datasets into training, validation, and
clean test sets with an 8:1:1 ratio to ensure consistency in experimental setup [WFH+21, WXM+22,
GDH+22, HWY+24].
For performance evaluation, we adopt the two widely used ranking metrics
common in denoising recommendation literature: NDCG@K and Recall@K. Higher scores on both
metrics indicate better model performance. To enable a comprehensive comparison, we report results
for K = 5, K= 10, K= 20, and K = 50 across all datasets.
To quantify the accuracy of noise estimation in our method, we employ the evaluation metric used
in related work, which calculates the L1 norm to measure the discrepancy between the ground-truth
and the estimated instance-dependent transition matrices [CLN+22, LYW+25].
4.1.4
Baselines
To evaluate the effectiveness of our proposed method in mitigating the adverse impact of noisy interac-
tions, we conduct comparisons against a comprehensive set of baselines. Our experimental framework
encompasses four widely used recommendation model backbones that operate on implicit feedback:
• GMF [HC17]: A generalized matrix factorization model that captures the latent factors of users
and items.
• NeuMF [HC17]: A neural collaborative filtering model that combines the linearity of matrix
factorization with the non-linearity of neural networks.
• NGCF [WHW+19]: A graph neural network model that leverages high-order connectivity in the
user-item interaction graph for enhanced representations.
• LightGCN [HDW+20]: A streamlined and effective graph convolutional network specifically de-
signed for recommendation, which propagates user and item embeddings on the interaction graph.
Each backbone model is trained using the following representative learning paradigms and denoising
strategies:
• Normal [HC17]: The standard training paradigm using the binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss.
• WBPR [GDFST12]: A sampling-based method that treats popular but non-interacted items as
hard negatives.
• WRMF [HKV08]: A weighted matrix factorization approach with fixed, pre-defined weights to
down-weight potential noise.
• T-CE [WFH+21]: A loss correction method that truncates high-loss samples in each training
iteration using a dynamic threshold.
• DeCA [WXM+22]: A co-teaching inspired method that uses two models to identify and account
for disagreements caused by noisy samples.
Furthermore, to benchmark the core noise estimation component of our method, we compare it
against several state-of-the-art transition matrix estimation techniques.
• BLTM[YYH+22]: A Bayesian framework for label transition matrix estimation that theoretically

Related Work
5.1
Denoising in Recommender Systems
Research in recommender systems has progressed from foundational matrix factorization and graph
models [KBV09, HKV08, WDZE16, HDW+20, WHC+19, YCWT18] to advanced denoising techniques,
including adaptive pruning [WFH+21, TXL+22], Gaussian mixture-based label correction [SYM25],
meta-learning frameworks [GDH+22, WGL+23, GRI+23], and diffusion models [WXF+23, ZWX+24],
with additional contributions in exposure bias correction [LZW+24, CXY+24, HWY+24], contextual
noise modeling [KHWZ14, LZM18], and enhanced negative sampling [DQH+19, WXH+20, CZW+19].
While conventional denoising methods typically discard noisy instances to ensure robustness, this
strategy inevitably leads to low data utilization.
5.2
Denoising in Statistical Methods
Statistical methods provide theoretical foundations through PAC learning [AL88], consistent loss cor-
rection [LT15], Bayesian ranking [RFGST12, GDFST12, DYH+19, DFH+18], and extended matrix
factorization [HZKC16], while transition matrix-based approaches enable sophisticated noise modeling
via volume minimization [LLH+21], part-dependent [XLH+20b] and dual-T [YLH+20] approximations,
instance-dependent techniques [BHN+21, LYW+25, WPQ+25, MVRN18, ZCY+24], Bayesian correc-
tion [YQ20], transition-revision methods [XLW+19], semi-supervised separation [LSH20], bounded
noise frameworks [CLRT20], causal learning [YLG+21], Bayes-optimal estimation [YYH+22, YWY+23],
and cycle consistency [CNW+22]. While alternative approaches employing Bayes-label transition ma-
trix (BLTM) can leverage all available data, their estimation often suffers from significant bias in
practical recommendation scenarios.

## Page 15


5.3
Denoising in Machine Learning
Machine learning offers noise-tolerant classification theories [NDRT13, DPNS14] complemented by deep
learning advances in loss correction [PRKM+17], optimized training [YXH+18], and robust learning
strategies [SS19, XLH+20a, NMN+20, BYW+22, ZZH+21, BYH+21, WHW+19], supported by bench-
mark studies [WZC+22] and causal analysis [YGD+23].
While these techniques provide practical
robustness, they often lack theoretical guarantees.
6
Conclusion
This study addresses the challenge of instance-dependent noise in recommendation systems by propos-
ing the Robust GMM-weighted Bayes-label Transition Matrix framework.
Our RGBT framework
advances the field through its integration of Gaussian Mixture Model-based reliability weighting with
Bayes-label transition matrix estimation. The GMM mechanism systematically calibrates transition
matrix estimation by assigning instance-specific reliability scores, effectively mitigating bias while uti-
lizing the complete noisy dataset. Theoretical analysis demonstrates that the Bayes-label transition
matrix formulation conditions noise transitions on the Bayes-optimal label rather than the latent true
label, achieving substantial variance reduction over conventional approaches while ensuring full sam-
ple utilization and consistent estimation. Extensive experiments on real-world and synthetic datasets
demonstrate RGBT’s superior performance, outperforming both reliable sample-based denoising meth-
ods in data utilization and state-of-the-art transition matrix-based approaches in calibration accuracy.
This work establishes a robust foundation for Bayesian noise modeling in recommendation systems,
opening avenues for future research to incorporate causal inference in modeling real-world recommen-
dation noise feedback mechanisms for developing more robust and unbiased recommendation systems.
