Real approximation for homogeneous spaces with
finite stabilizers
David Harari, Nguy˜ên Ma.nh Linh, and Giancarlo Lucchini Arteche
Abstract
We prove some new cases of real appoximation for homogeneous spaces with
finite stabilizers and describe the state of the art around this question, giving
proofs that are well-known to experts but that, to our knowledge, cannot be found
in the literature. Our main new result needs the latest advances in the topic of the
Brauer–Manin obstruction for homogeneous spaces with supersolvable stabilizers.
It states that any finite k-group that is split by a 2-primary extension satisfies real
approximation.
Keywords : Real approximation, homogeneous spaces, Brauer–Manin obstruction.
MSC classes: 14G12, 14M17, 12G05.

Introduction
Let k be a number field and let ΩR be the set of real places of k. For v ∈ΩR, denote
by kv the corresponding completion, which is isomorphic to R. The different inclusions

These questions are a particular case of the property known as weak approximation
for homogeneous spaces, which considers an arbitrary finite set of places of k instead of
just the real places and an arbitrary homogeneous space X = G/H of a linear algebraic
k-group G. It is well-known that there exists an obstruction to this property, known
as the Brauer–Manin obstruction.
A famous question/conjecture by Colliot-Thélène
[CT03, Introduction] asks whether this obstruction is the only one. On the other hand,
the third author proved in [LA19] that the Brauer–Manin obstruction does not interact
with real places, so that the conjecture (which hasn’t been settled in general) does imply
a positive answer to Questions 1.1 and 1.2.
The goal of this note is to settle some cases of this question with a positive answer and
describe the state of the art around this question. Some cases can be dealt with using
very elementary arguments, stemming essentially from basic nonabelian cohomology and
classical Number Theory. Others need the latest advances in the topic of the Brauer–
Manin obstruction for homogeneous spaces, in particular the powerful results by Harpaz
and Wittenberg in [HW20]. Our main result (Theorem 4.1) states that any k-group that
is split by a 2-primary extension satisfies real approximation. We also give a result for
certain antisolvable groups (Theorem 4.6).
It is worth noting that Julian Demeio recently announced a proof of Colliot-Thélène’s
conjecture for all homogeneous spaces SLn/H with H a solvable k-group [Dem26,
Theorem 1.1], which would imply a positive answer to Question 1.2 for all solvable
A, with no assumption on the extension splitting the group. Such a result would also
shorten some of the proofs here below, but we make no use of it in this note.

The following result gives a condition under which such Sylow subgroups can exist.
Proposition 3.2. Let p be a prime number and A be a k-group.
Assume that the
action of Γ on A factors through a finite quotient which is a p-group. Then there exists
a Γ-stable p-Sylow subgroup S of A
Proof. Let Γ′ be a finite quotient of Γ that acts on A and is a p-group. Then this
quotient acts on the set of p-Sylow subgroups of A. Since Γ′ is a p-group, every orbit
of this action must have a cardinal that is a power of p. On the other hand, since the
number of Sylow subgroups is coprime to p, the classical orbit formula applied to this
action tells us that at least one of these powers must be 1, which yields an invariant
Sylow subgroup.

When we restrict the definition to constant groups, supersolvable groups include

alternating group A4 is not supersolvable, even with a trivial Γ-action.
Note also that not every abelian k-group is supersolvable.

order 3 that permutes the three nontrivial elements in A. Then the k-group does not
admit any nontrivial Γ-stable (normal) subgroup, so it is impossible to obtain cyclic
quotients.
The following result provides a family of non-constant supersolvable groups.
Proposition 3.3. Let p be a prime number and A be a k-group of p-primary order.
Assume that the action of Γ on A factors through a finite quotient that is also a p-
group. Then the k-group A is supersolvable.
Proof. Assume that A admits a central, Γ-stable cyclic subgroup C and consider the
quotient A/C. We claim that if A/C is supersolvable, then so is A. Indeed, by definition,
there exists normal subgroups
{1} = B0 ⊆B1 ⊆· · · ⊆Bn−1 ⊆Bn = A/C,
such that Bi/Bi−1 is cyclic for every 1 ≤i ≤n. Write π : A →A/C the canonical
projection and define
• A0 := {1};
• Ai := π−1(Bi−1) for 1 ≤i ≤n + 1.
Then each Ai is Γ-invariant and is normal in A as it is the preimage of a normal subgroup
of A/C via a Γ-equivariant morphism. Moreover, we have A1 = C, An+1 = A and we
have inclusions
{1} = A0 ⊆A1 ⊆· · · ⊆An ⊆An+1 = A,

By induction on the cardinal of A and since a trivial group is obviously supersolvable,
we are reduced to proving that, when A satisfies the hypotheses of the proposition, there
exists a central, Γ-stable cyclic subgroup.
Now, since A is a p-group, its center Z is nontrivial and characteristic in A, hence
Γ-stable. As in the previous proof, let Γ′ be a finite quotient of Γ that acts on A and is
a p-group. Then this quotient acts on Z and hence on the set of nonzero elements in Z.
But the cardinal of this set is coprime to p, so the same classical argument tells us that
this action has at least one invariant element. The cyclic subgroup C generated by this
element is then normal (since it is central) and clearly Γ-stable.

More advanced results
4.1
Groups split by 2-extensions
We present now the main result of this note, which depends on the elementary results
from the previous section and on a deep result by Harpaz and Wittenberg in [HW20].
Theorem 4.1. Let A be a finite k-group on which the action of Γ factors through a
finite quotient which is a 2-group. Then A satisfies real approximation.
Note that this result includes all constant k-groups.
Proof. By Proposition 3.2, we know that there exists a Γ-stable 2-Sylow subgroup S of
A. By Proposition 3.1, it will suffice then to prove the result for S. Now, by Proposition
3.3, this group is supersolvable. Then we know by [HW20, Thm. B] that the Brauer–
Manin obstruction to weak approximation is the only obstruction. By [LA19, Thm. 6.1],
this implies that X = SLn/S satisfies real approximation. This concludes the proof.
¨⌣
This is as far as we can go without imposing any conditions on the structure of
the abstract group A. In the following sections we present some results that relax the
hypothesis on the action of Γ, but at the price of imposing conditions on the group A.

Lemma 4.5. Let A be a characteristically simple nonabelian finite k-group. Assume
that the simple factors of A are almost complete. Then A is almost complete.
In particular, if we assume moreover that the outer action of Γ on A factors through
a finite quotient which is a 2-group, then real approximation holds for A.
Proof. By [Gor80, Chap. 2, Thm. 1.4], A = F n (as an abstract group) for some finite

exists a section σ : Out(F) →Aut(F). Now, one has Aut(A) = Aut(F)n ⋊Sn by

Example 4.8. The group An for n = 5 or n ≥7 is a simple, almost complete group
whose outer automorphism group is a 2-group. This is also true for all 26 sporadic
groups, so that the hypotheses of Theorem 4.6 always hold and we may deduce that
these groups satisfy real approximation regardless of the Galois action.
A slightly more general example would be any direct product A × B of two such
groups. Indeed, if A and B are not isomorphic, then Aut(A × B) ≃Aut(A) × Aut(B).
On the other hand, we already mentioned in the proof of Lemma 4.5 that Aut(A2) ≃
Aut(A)2 ⋊S2. In both cases we see then that the outer automorphism group would be
a 2-group.
In order to construct more involved examples, it is worth noting that in [Ber67]
Bercov characterizes anti-solvable finite groups whose composition factors are almost
complete as iterated twisted wreath products of their simple factors.
