ROWMOTION ON HOOK AND TWO-ROW ALT ν-TAMARI LATTICES
SEN-PENG EU, VEI-CHENG HIOE, AND YI-LIN LEE
Abstract. In 2024, Ceballos and Chenevi`ere introduced alt ν-Tamari lattices, parameterized by
a lattice path ν and an increment vector δ, as a common generalization of ν-Tamari and ν-Dyck
lattices.
We study rowmotion on two families: the alt hook-Tamari lattice Hδ(a,b) (where ν =
EN a−1Eb−1N) and the alt 2-row-Tamari lattice Tδ(a,b) (where ν = EaNEbN).
We explicitly determine the orbit structures of Hδ(a,b) and Tδ(a,b) under rowmotion, and prove
that their orbit structures are independent of the increment vector δ. As a consequence, we show
that rowmotion on Hδ(a,b) exhibits the cyclic sieving phenomenon. We also compute orbit sums
for several natural statistics. In the hook case, we evaluate the down-degree, peak, valley, and area
statistics; in the 2-row case, we focus on the down-degree statistic. All of these—except for the area
statistic—are homometric under rowmotion.
Regarding the methodology of this paper, our results in the hook case are obtained by applying
a simple local modiﬁcation to their Hasse diagrams. In the 2-row case, we introduce a switching
property for semidistributive lattices, which allows us to compare the orbit structures arising from
diﬀerent increment vectors.

INTRODUCTION
The rapidly developing ﬁeld of dynamical algebraic combinatorics aims to study the structure
of operators acting on objects in algebraic combinatorics.
One of the most extensively studied
operators is rowmotion on various important objects, such as Boolean lattices, chains, root posets,
minuscule posets, Tamari lattices, and Cambrian lattices; see [16, Section 7] for a comprehensive
review. Recently, rowmotion on 321-avoiding permutations [2], fence posets [10], rooted trees [7],
m-Tamari lattices and biCambrian lattices [8] have been explored, and these works produced many
interesting results.
Rowmotion is an operator, denoted by Row, acting on the set of order ideals J(P) of a ﬁnite poset
P. According to the fundamental theorem of ﬁnite distributive lattices, J(P) is isomorphic to a ﬁnite
distributive lattice when ordered by inclusion (see [14, Theorem 3.4.1] for example). The concept
of rowmotion has since been extended to various broader classes of lattices. In 2019, Barnard [3]
deﬁned rowmotion on semidistributive lattices, while Thomas and Williams [16] introduced a version
for trim lattices, both of which generalize the distributive case. In 2023, Defant and Williams [9]
uniﬁed and further generalized these deﬁnitions to the class of semidistrim lattices.
The Tamari lattice, a central object in algebraic combinatorics, provides a partial ordering on
the set of Dyck paths. Since its introduction by Tamari [15], this lattice has received signiﬁcant
attention, and it has been generalized from various mathematical perspectives. In 2012, Bergeron
and Pr´eville-Ratelle [4] introduced m-Tamari lattices to provide conjectural combinatorial interpre-
tations for the dimensions of certain trivariate diagonal harmonic spaces. Subsequently, in 2017,
Pr´eville-Ratelle and Viennot [11] extended these to ν-Tamari lattices Tam(ν), where ν is a given
lattice path on the square lattice that starts at the origin and consists of unit north steps N and
unit east steps E. Throughout the paper, we may identify such a path with a ﬁnite word over the
alphabet {N,E}, and use exponents to denote repeated concatenation of a word with itself; see
Figure 1 for examples. The nth m-Tamari lattice is a special case of ν-Tamari lattices by taking
ν = (NEm)n; for m = 1, this further reduces to the classical nth Tamari lattice.
Defant and Lin [8] have investigated rowmotion on m-Tamari lattices. They fully determined
the orbit structure of m-Tamari lattices under rowmotion, and proved that it exhibits the cyclic

2.1
The ν-Dyck, ν-Tamari, and alt ν-Tamari lattices
Let ν be a lattice path on the square lattice. A ν-path µ is a lattice path consisting of N and E
steps that shares the same endpoints as ν and stays weakly above ν. Let P(ν) denote the collection
of all ν-paths. By equipping P(ν) with diﬀerent partial orders, one obtains the ν-Dyck lattice, the
ν-Tamari lattice, and their common generalization, the alt ν-Tamari lattices. These structures are
deﬁned as follows.
The ν-Dyck lattice, denoted by Dyck(ν), is the poset on P(ν) where p ≤q if q stays weakly above
p. The cover relation p ⋖q in Dyck(ν) corresponds to the operation of replacing a valley EN with a
peak NE at some speciﬁc position in the ν-path p. In the speciﬁc case where ν = (NEm)n, Dyck(ν)
reduces to the Dyck lattice on the set of m-Dyck paths. Moreover, when m = 1, Dyck(ν) reduces
further to the classical Dyck lattice on the set of usual Dyck paths. The Hasse diagram of Dyck(ν)
with ν = EN 2E2N is shown in Figure 2(a).
The ν-Tamari lattice, denoted by Tam(ν), was introduced by Pr´eville-Ratelle and Viennot [11]
using the set of ν-paths P(ν). For any lattice point x on a path µ ∈P(ν), deﬁne its altitude2
alt(ν,x) to be the maximum number of E steps that can be added to the right of x without crossing
ν. Consider a valley EN of µ, and let x be the lattice point between these E and N steps. Let y be
the ﬁrst lattice point following x such that alt(ν,y) = alt(ν,x), and let µ(x,y) be the subpath of µ
from x to y. We deﬁne µ′ to be the path obtained from µ by interchanging the subpath µ(x,y) with
the E step immediately preceding x. This operation, denoted by µ ⋖µ′, is called the ν-rotation
of µ at x. The ν-Tamari lattice Tam(ν) is the poset on P(ν) whose cover relations are given by
these ν-rotations; it was shown in [11] that Tam(ν) is a lattice. The Hasse diagram of Tam(ν) with
ν = EN 2E2N is shown in Figure 2(c). Similarly, when specializing ν = (NEm)n, Tam(ν) is the
m-Tamari lattices on the set of m-Dyck paths; when m = 1, Tam(ν) reduces to the classical Tamari
lattices on the set of Dyck paths.

The cyclic sieving phenomenon

CONCLUDING REMARKS
In this paper, we study rowmotion on two families of alt ν-Tamari lattices: the alt hook-Tamari
lattice Hδ(a,b), corresponding to the path ν = EN a−1Eb−1N; and the alt 2-row-Tamari lattice
Tδ(a,b), corresponding to the path ν = EaNEbN. Our results show that these two families support
Conjecture 1.1, which states that the rowmotion orbit structure of an alt ν-Tamari lattice depends
only on the path ν and is independent of the increment vector δ. Furthermore, these two families
also support Conjecture 2.5 regarding the homometry of the down-degree statistic. It is natural to
extend Conjecture 2.5 from ν-Tamari lattices to alt ν-Tamari lattices, the statement is given below:
Conjecture 5.1. Let ν be a lattice path. The down-degree statistic is homometric for rowmotion
on alt ν-Tamari lattices Tamδ(ν) and is independent of the increment vector δ.
It would be interesting to ﬁnd a direct explanation of the invariance of the rowmotion orbit
structure and the orbit sum of the down-degree statistic for general alt ν-Tamari lattices. Another
possible direction is to study whether the switching property (Theorem 4.8) used in the 2-row case
can be applied to other families of semidistributive lattices.
