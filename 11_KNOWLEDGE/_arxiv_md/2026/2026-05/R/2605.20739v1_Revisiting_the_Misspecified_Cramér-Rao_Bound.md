Revisiting the Misspeciﬁed Cram´er-Rao Bound
Malaak Khatib, Student Member, IEEE, Nadav Harel, Member, IEEE, Joseph Tabrikian, Fellow, IEEE, and Tirza
Routtenberg, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—Estimation under model misspeciﬁcation arises in
many signal processing problems, where the assumed observation
model deviates from the true data-generating mechanism due to
errors or simpliﬁcations. The misspeciﬁed Cram´er-Rao bound
(MCRB) is a widely recognized mean-squared-error (MSE) lower
bound for this case, which has originally been used to describe
the asymptotic behavior of the misspeciﬁed maximum likelihood
(MML) estimator. Despite its widespread use, the MCRB lacks a
rigorous characterization of the class of estimators for which it is
valid. In this paper, we revisit the theory of parameter estimation
under model misspeciﬁcation and re-examine the foundations of
the MCRB. We ﬁrst demonstrate these limitations and examine
a naive version of the MCRB, which relies only on local
misspeciﬁed unbiasedness. We show that this bound is generally
not tight and may be unattainable. To obtain a meaningful
bound, we develop a new derivation based on the concept of
pointwise equivalent models. By maximizing the naive bound for
these models, we recover the classical MCRB, now supported
by a constructive derivation, an explicit characterization of
the associated estimator class, and an equality condition. This
formulation establishes a formal link between local unbiasedness
conditions and achievable bounds, offering new insights into the
MCRB structure and its relevance to practical estimators. Finally,
we deﬁne the notion of an efﬁcient misspeciﬁed estimator and
show that if it exists, it is achieved by the MML estimator.
Index Terms—Non-Bayesian misspeciﬁed parameter estima-
tion, misspeciﬁed Cram´er-Rao bound (MCRB), performance
bounds, local unbiasedness, pointwise equivalent models
I. INTRODUCTION
Performance bounds play a central role in estimation theory
by characterizing the achievable mean-squared-error (MSE),
providing benchmarks for practical estimators, serving as
valuable tools for system design, and guiding the design of
cognitive and adaptive systems in various applications [1]–
[3]. In classical settings, the Cram´er-Rao bound (CRB) is the
most widely used benchmark, mainly due to its simplicity and
its asymptotic relation with the maximum likelihood (ML)
estimator. However, the CRB relies on the assumption that
the statistical/observation model is correctly speciﬁed. This
assumption is often not valid in practice due to modeling
errors, unknown effects, or deliberate model simpliﬁcations. In
such cases, estimators operate under an assumed misspeciﬁed
model, while their bias and MSE are determined by the true
data-generating mechanism, rendering classical performance
bounds, such as the CRB, inapplicable. Thus, there is a need
for rigorous performance bounds that account for misspeciﬁ-
cations for system design and performance analysis.
The asymptotic behavior of the ML estimator under model
misspeciﬁcation, commonly referred to as the misspeciﬁed
maximum likelihood (MML) estimator, has been extensively
studied in the statistical literature since the seminal works
of [4]–[6]. In this setting, the MML estimator maximizes

a principled tool for model selection [18], [19] and for the
analysis of estimation after model selection [20]. All these
developments have enabled the derivation of tractable MCRBs
in a wide range of signal-processing applications, such as
in radars [21]–[23], channel estimation [24]–[26], integrated
sensing and communication [27], fake features estimation [28],
direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation [17], [29], and time-
delay and Doppler estimation [30].
Despite these advances, the existing MCRB framework
exhibits several fundamental limitations that hinder its theo-
retical clarity and practical applicability. In particular, existing
formulations do not explicitly specify the class of estimators
for which the MCRB constitutes a valid lower bound. In
practice, it is often implicitly assumed that the MCRB applies
to all locally unbiased estimators with respect to (w.r.t.) the
pseudo-true parameter; however, this condition does not, in
general, deﬁne the appropriate estimators’ class, and as shown
in this paper, the MCRB fails to bound the MSE of such
estimators. In addition, the MCRB differs substantially from
a naive extension of the classical CRB to the misspeciﬁed
setting that relies solely on local misspeciﬁed unbiasedness
[7], [10]. This naive bound is known to be generally not
tight and may fail to properly capture the misspeciﬁcation
effect. Yet, a clear characterization of the limitations of the
naive MCRB and its relation with the MCRB remains lacking.
Moreover, the original foundational derivation of the MCRB
in [7] relies on least-favorable distribution arguments that
are not fully transparent from a signal-processing perspective
and do not readily extend to other classes of performance
bounds, such as the Barankin bound. The state-of-the-art
covariance-inequality-based derivation in [8] is closely aligned
with classical estimation theory, but depends on a ﬁrst-order
expansion of the log-likelihood derivative around the MML
estimator and its asymptotic properties [6], rendering the
resulting bound inherently asymptotic and estimator-speciﬁc.
Finally, unlike the CRB, the relationship between the MCRB
and efﬁciency under model misspeciﬁcation and the bound

In this paper, we present a uniﬁed, estimator-agnostic,
and non-asymptotic derivation of the MCRB that explicitly
identiﬁes the class of estimators for which the bound is valid.
We ﬁrst discuss the limitations of the existing MCRB theory.
Then, we introduce a naive MCRB, obtained by directly
extending classical CRB arguments under local misspeciﬁed
unbiasedness, and show that this bound is generally not tight
and may be unattainable by estimators operating solely under
the assumed model. To resolve these limitations, we introduce
the concept of pointwise equivalent models, an auxiliary family
of probability density functions (PDFs) that coincides with
the true model only at the operating point. By maximizing
the naive MCRB over this family, we recover the tightest
achievable lower bound and show that it coincides with the
classical MCRB. Importantly, this construction yields an ex-
plicit characterization of the class of estimators for which the
MCRB is a valid MSE lower bound and clariﬁes the conditions
under which the bound is attainable. Finally, we deﬁne a
principled notion of efﬁciency under model misspeciﬁcation
and show that under standard regularity conditions, the MML
estimator is efﬁcient in this generalized sense.
The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
• Limitations of existing MCRB formulations: We identify
fundamental limitations of current MCRB formulations,
including non-transparent derivations and the lack of a well-
deﬁned class of estimators for which the bound holds. We
demonstrate via a simple counterexample that commonly
used misspeciﬁed unbiasedness conditions do not character-
ize the appropriate estimator class.
• Analysis of the naive MCRB: We derive the naive MCRB
[7] by solving a constrained MSE minimization problem
that enforces only local misspeciﬁed unbiasedness. We show
that this bound is always looser than or equal to the MCRB,
reduces to the oracle CRB when the pseudo-true and true
parameters coincide, and is generally unattainable due to its
equality condition.
• New derivation of the MCRB via pointwise equivalent
models: We introduce the concept of pointwise equivalent
models and show that maximizing the naive misspeciﬁed
bound over this class recovers the classical MCRB. This
yields a transparent, estimator-agnostic derivation, together
with an explicit equality condition and an estimator-class
characterization.
• Misspeciﬁed efﬁciency: We introduce a principled notion
of efﬁciency under model misspeciﬁcation via the equality
conditions of the MCRB. We show that, under standard
regularity conditions, the MML estimator is efﬁcient in this
sense, establishing a direct analogue of the classical ML–
CRB efﬁciency relationship for misspeciﬁed models.

II. PROBLEM FORMULATION AND BACKGROUND
In this section, we formulate the problem of non-Bayesian
estimation under model misspeciﬁcation. In Subsection II-A,
the problem formulation is presented, and in Subsection II-B,
the MCRB is introduced. Finally, in Subsection II-C we
present the limitations of the current version of the MCRB
framework and the crucial need for a new bound derivation.

formulated as follows: Let (Ωx, F, Pϑ) denote a probability

III. NAIVE MCRB UNDER LOCAL MISSPECIFIED
UNBIASEDNESS
As described in Section II, there is a need for a rigorous
derivation of the MCRB that clearly states the conditions
under which the bound holds and speciﬁes the relevant class
of estimators. In this section, we develop an MSE lower
bound based solely on local misspeciﬁed unbiasedness. To this
end, in Subsection III-A, we present the local unbiasedness
under model misspeciﬁcation. Then, in Subsection III-B, we
develop the corresponding MSE bound as the solution to
a minimization problem. Finally, in Subsection III-C, we
demonstrate that, in the general case, this development may
lead to a non-informative bound. Nevertheless, understanding
why this naive approach of using only local misspeciﬁed
unbiasedness is inadequate, provides essential background that
motivates the derivation of the MCRB in Section IV.

IV. NEW DERIVATION OF THE MCRB
In Subsection III-C, it was demonstrated that a misspeciﬁed
bound based solely on local misspeciﬁed unbiasedness leads to
an overly optimistic and generally unattainable bound, named
here the naive MCRB, since the unbiasedness condition char-
acterizes a set of estimators that is unrealistic in a misspeciﬁed
setting. While knowledge of the true PDF p(x; ϑ0) is nec-
essary for performance analysis, the unbiasedness conditions
placed on the estimator must not imply a dependence on the
true PDF. This balance will lead to a proper deﬁnition of
local unbiasedness, enabling the derivation of a tighter and
appropriate misspeciﬁed Cram´er-Rao type bound.
In this section, we introduce a new derivation of the MCRB
that aims to achieve this balance. The key idea is to embed
the true PDF p(x; ϑ0) into a broader family of auxiliary PDFs
that preserve only its pointwise properties. This approach
limits the information that can be used by the bound and by
the associated unbiasedness conditions. We derive the naive
MCRB from Subsection III-B for the different auxiliary PDFs.
By maximizing over this family, we identify the pointwise
equivalent PDF that yields the tightest MSE lower bound. We
then show that the resulting bound coincides with the classical
MCRB from Deﬁnition 3, thereby justifying its use.
The steps for the proposed MCRB derivation are as follows:
1) Introduce pointwise equivalent models by constructing an
embedded family of PDFs that are pointwise equivalent
to p(x; ϑ0) (Subsection IV-A).
2) Present the naive MCRB for a generic pointwise equiva-
lent model (Subsection IV-B).
3) Maximize the bound over the family from Step 1) to
obtain the tightest bound, and show that it coincides with
the MCRB (Subsection IV-C).

VII. CONCLUSIONS
This paper revisited the MCRB and addressed fundamental
gaps in its theoretical foundation. By introducing a principled
notion of local misspeciﬁed unbiasedness and a family of
pointwise equivalent models, we derived the MCRB through
an estimator-agnostic procedure that clariﬁes both its attain-
ability and its structural properties. The resulting theory ex-
plicitly identiﬁes the class of estimators for which the MCRB
is valid, and establishes a clear notion of efﬁciency under
model misspeciﬁcation, as well as its relation with the MML
estimator. Beyond providing a uniﬁed derivation, the proposed
framework offers insight into the limitations of naive MCRB
and shows that the MCRB can be interpreted as the tightest
achievable naive MCRB with minimal information about the
true model. Future research directions include extensions to
cyclic [29] and constrained [37] parameter spaces, and devel-
opment of large error bounds, such as the Barankin bound.
