Refocusing spacetimes need not be strongly refocusing
Friedrich Bauermeistera
aDepartment of Mathematics, Dartmouth College
Abstract
We prove that there are globally hyperbolic spacetimes (X, g) which are refo-
cusing but not strongly refocusing. In fact, every globally hyperbolic strongly
refocusing spacetime of dimension at least 3 admits globally hyperbolic met-
rics which are refocusing but not strongly refocusing. This answers a question
by Chernov, Kinlaw, and Sadykov [12]. We then prove that globally hyper-
bolic spacetimes which are Legendrian refocusing (a notion introduced in this
paper) admit globally hyperbolic strongly refocusing metrics.
1. Introduction
Unless otherwise stated, any manifold in this paper is smooth and with-
out boundary, and geodesics of Riemannian and Lorentzian manifolds are
assumed to be maximal.
1.1 Definition (strongly refocusing spacetime). Let (X, g) be a spacetime
and let p, q ∈X with p ̸= q. We say that (X, g) is strongly refocusing with
respect to p and q if every null-geodesic through p also passes through q. In
this case we also have that all null-geodesics through q go through p, see [20,
Proposition 3.3]. We say that (X, g) is strongly refocusing if there exist p ̸= q
in X such that (X, g) is strongly refocusing with respect to p and q.
A weaker notion was introduced earlier by Low [21, 22, 23], who gave three
different definitions, later proved to be equivalent by Kinlaw [20, Proposi-
tion 3.7].

1.2 Definition-Theorem (refocusing spacetime). Let (X, g) be a strongly
causal spacetime. The following three conditions, corresponding to Low’s
three definitions of refocusing, are equivalent:
(i) There exist p ∈X and an open U ∋p such that for every open V
with p ∈V ⊆U there is q ∈X \ V with every null-geodesic through q
entering V .
(ii) There exist p ∈X and an open U ∋p such that for every open V
with p ∈V ⊆U there is q ∈X \ U with every null-geodesic through q
entering V .
(iii) There exist p ∈X and a sequence (qn)n∈N which does not have p as an
accumulation point such that for every open neighborhood V of p there
is some N ∈N such that every null-geodesic through qn goes through
V for n ≥N. In this case we say that (X, g) is refocusing with respect
to the point p ∈X and the sequence (qn)n∈N in X.
We say that (X, g) is a refocusing spacetime if any, hence every, of these
three equivalent conditions is fulfilled. The implications (iii)⇔(ii)⇒(i) hold
for arbitrary spacetimes. We use formulation (iii) as our working definition
throughout.
Every strongly refocusing spacetime is refocusing. The following question
is due to Chernov, Kinlaw, and Sadykov [12].
1.3 Question ([12]). Are there globally hyperbolic refocusing spacetimes
which are not strongly refocusing?
A weaker form of Question 1.3 was answered affirmatively by Kinlaw [20],
who exhibited a globally hyperbolic spacetime that is refocusing at a point
but fails to be strongly refocusing at that same point.
1.4 Example ([20]). Let h be the round metric on S2 and let (X, g) =
(S2 × (−π, π), h −dt2). Fix x ∈S2 and let −x be its antipodal point. The
sequence qn = (−x, π −1
n) shows that (X, g) is refocusing with respect to
p = (x, 0), but it is not strongly refocusing with respect to p. Note however
that (X, g) is strongly refocusing at other points.
We resolve Question 1.3 in the affirmative, proving the following, stronger
theorem.

1.5 Result (Theorem 3.16). Every globally hyperbolic strongly refocusing

To place this in the context of Low’s reconstruction program, let (X, g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime, let N denote its space of lightrays, and
let Σ denote its set of skies.
For an event p ∈X, its sky is the subset

Proof. The fact that (X, g) being non-refocusing implies that the map X →
Σc is a homeomorphism is first stated by Low in [22] and proved by Kinlaw in

In many ways the most natural way to view Σ as a subspace of a larger
space is to embed Σ into the space L of smooth Legendrian submanifolds of
N which are Legendrian isotopic to one (hence every) sky. This is motivated
for example by the Legendrian Low conjecture, which states that points
in a globally hyperbolic spacetime (X, g) are causally related if and only
if their skies are Legendrian linked in N. The Legendrian Low conjecture
was proved for a broad class of spacetimes by Chernov and Nemirovski [13].
Strong refocusing is an obstruction to the Legendrian Low conjecture. We
equip L with the C∞topology, and use this to define a finer topology on the
space of skies.
1.9 Definition (fine topology on the space of skies). Let (X, g) be a globally
hyperbolic spacetime, let N be the space of lightrays of (X, g) and let Σ be
the space of skies. Let L be the space of smooth Legendrian submanifolds
of N Legendrian isotopic to a sky, equipped with the C∞topology. We call
the subspace topology on Σ as a subset of L the fine topology. We denote
the set Σ equipped with this topology by Σf.
1.10 Definition. Let (X, g) be a globally hyperbolic spacetime. We say that
(X, g) is Legendrian refocusing with respect to a point p ∈X and a sequence
(qn)n∈N in X if p is not an accumulation point of {qn}n∈N but skyqn →skyp in
L. We say that (X, g) is Legendrian refocusing if it is Legendrian refocusing
with respect to some point p ∈X and some sequence (qn)n∈N in X.
The condition of a spacetime being Legendrian refocusing sits between
being strongly refocusing and being refocusing. The benefit of this definition
is that it fits nicely into the Legendrian picture.
1.11 Remark. Let (X, g) be a globally hyperbolic spacetime. Then (X, g)
is Legendrian refocusing if and only if the sky map S : X →Σf fails to be a
homeomorphism, equivalently if and only if the sky map S : X ,→L fails to
be an embedding.

1.14 Remark. Throughout this paper, whenever we say that a globally

A priori this assumption is not justified;

including the definition of globally hyperbolic spacetimes and a discussion

We quickly review the definition and some key properties of globally hy-

2.1 Definition (globally hyperbolic spacetime, Cauchy surface). A space-
time (X, g) is causal if it contains no closed causal curve, and globally hy-
perbolic if it is causal and if J+(p) ∩J−(q) is compact for all p, q ∈X. A
subset M ⊂X is a topological Cauchy surface of (X, g) if it is a topological
hypersurface meeting every inextendible timelike curve in exactly one point,
and an acausal Cauchy surface if it meets every inextendible causal curve in
exactly one point.
A spacetime (X, g) is globally hyperbolic if and only if it admits an acausal
topological Cauchy surface M.
In that case any two topological acausal
Cauchy surfaces are homeomorphic and X is homeomorphic to M × R; see
Geroch [16] and [19]. Smooth refinements of all the above are due to Bernal
and Sánchez. In particular: (X, g) is globally hyperbolic if and only if it ad-
mits a smooth, spacelike Cauchy surface M; any two smooth Cauchy surfaces
of (X, g) are diffeomorphic; and X is diffeomorphic to M × R [4, 6]. Until
Bernal and Sánchez [8] showed that globally hyperbolic spacetimes can be de-
fined as causal spacetimes with compact causal diamonds, they were defined
by requiring them to be strongly causal. Globally hyperbolic spacetimes are
the ones compatible with Penrose’s strong cosmic censorship hypothesis [25],
and are generally regarded as the physically reasonable spacetimes.
2.2 Definition (temporal functions). For a spacetime (X, g), a smooth func-
tion T : X →R is called a temporal function if its gradient is a past-pointing
timelike vectorfield. A surjective temporal function T : X →R is called
Cauchy if its level sets are Cauchy surfaces, i.e. if for all t ∈R we have that
T −1(t) ⊂X is a Cauchy surface. Every globally hyperbolic spacetime (X, g)
admits a surjective Cauchy temporal function T : X →R. This was proved
by Bernal and Sánchez [4, 5, 6].
Temporal functions are strictly increasing along future-pointing causal
curves.
Low showed in [23] that the set N of future-directed, unparametrized,
inextendible null geodesics of a strongly causal spacetime (X, g) carries a
natural smooth structure and a natural contact structure. When (X, g) is
globally hyperbolic, this contact manifold can be identified with the spherical
cotangent bundle of any spacelike Cauchy surface. To see this, let M ⊂X
be a spacelike Cauchy surface. Every future-directed null geodesic α meets
M in a unique point, say α(t) = p. Let π : TpX →TpM be the orthog-
onal projection and set v = π(α′(t)) ∈TpM. Note that ker(π) ⊆TpX is

A. Topological consequences of Refocusing and Strong Refocusing
There are various topological consequences of (or, equivalently, obstruc-
tions to) refocusing phenomena. In this section we give a brief overview. Low
proved that any Cauchy surface of a globally hyperbolic refocusing spacetime
must be compact [23].
Chernov and Rudyak then proved that the semi-
Riemannian universal cover of a globally hyperbolic refocusing spacetime of
dimension at least 3 is again globally hyperbolic and refocusing [14, Theo-
rem 11.5]. Combining this with Low’s theorem gives the following.
A.1 Proposition. Let (X, g) be a refocusing, globally hyperbolic spacetime
of dimension at least 3. Then any Cauchy surface of (X, g) is compact with
finite fundamental group.
In the strongly refocusing case one obtains information about the coho-
mology as well. The author proved the following Bott-Samelson type result
for strongly refocusing spacetimes [1, Proposition 3.13].
A.2 Proposition. Let (X, g) be a globally hyperbolic, strongly refocusing
spacetime of dimension at least 3. Then any Cauchy surface of (X, g) is
compact with finite fundamental group, and its universal cover has the integral
cohomology ring of a CROSS.
The result on cohomology is chiefly a consequence of a more general
contact-theoretic statement proved by Frauenfelder, Labrousse, and Schlenk
[15, Theorem 1.13].
For refocusing spacetimes, the analogous cohomological statement re-
mains open.
A.3 Question. For every globally hyperbolic, refocusing spacetime of di-
mension at least 3, does any Cauchy surface of its universal cover have the
integral cohomology ring of a CROSS?
