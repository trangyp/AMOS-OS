RATIONAL POINTS ON SMOOTH SURFACES IN P3 OVER FINITE
FIELDS
YVES AUBRY AND JOS´E FELIPE VOLOCH
Abstract. We improve a bound due to the second author on number of rational points on
smooth surfaces in P3 over finite fields and look at families of surfaces that achieve or nearly
achieve this bound, for which we compute their exact number of rational points. These
computations may have independent interest.
1. Introduction
Let S be a smooth surface in P3 of degree d defined over the finite field Fp with p prime.
We are interested in the number ♯S(Fp) of rational points of S over Fp. Deligne has proved,
as a consequence of his proof of the Weil conjectures in [3], that
♯S(Fp) ≤p2 + 1 + (d3 −4d2 + 6d −2)p.
There are, in addition, several bounds that can be obtained by elementary methods. See
[4,5] and the references therein. An example of such bound was proved by the second author
in Proposition 2 of [10], namely
♯S(Fp) ≤(d −1)(p + 1)2 + p + 1
which is better than Deligne’s bound if d >
p
p/3 approximately. Moreover, if we suppose
that S does not contain a line defined over Fp, then the above bound can be improved to
♯S(Fp) ≤(d −1)p2 + (d −2)(p + 1) + 1.
By a different method, inspired by [8], the second author proved in Theorem 2 of [10] that
if 2 < d < p and if m denotes the number of Fp-lines contained in S, then we have
(1)
♯S(Fp) ≤d(d + p −1)(d + 2p −2)

(2)

surfaces for which we are able to determine its number of rational points together the number
of Fp-lines contained in it and that achieve or get close to this bound.
