1.2. Discrete densities and dimensions. Dimension theory plays a fundamental role in
fractal geometry and dynamical systems. In this subsection, we recall the essential notions of
densities and dimensions that will be employed in our analysis.

These notions of density and dimension play a fundamental role in elucidating the structure

of the attractor of the dual contractive system. Moreover, employing renewal theory in the

Motivation and scope of the present work. Despite the important developments in
discrete fractals that followed Strichartz’s original introduction of RIFS [45], several gaps and
limitations persist in the existing literature. Earlier works presuppose discreteness either by
defining the system directly on a discrete space or by assuming that invariant sets are uniformly
discrete. Moreover, restrictive conditions have been imposed on the starting points of orbits
and strong non-overlapping hypotheses have been heavily relied upon. As a result, the finer
dimensional theory and the precise distributional properties of these macroscopic sets have
remained largely incomplete.
Our work broadens the scope of RIFS theory by removing these historical restrictions.
We begin in the general setting of complete metric spaces and, within our newly introduced
framework, revisit the fact that any locally finite non-empty invariant set of an RIFS is
essentially a finite union of forward orbits. This structural insight motivates a deeper study
of individual forward orbits. Because the expanding mappings in an RIFS are generally not
surjective, we introduce the concept of admissible words to capture valid iterative sequences and
obtain an equivalent characterisation for the existence of non-empty invariant sets. Specialising
to the expanding systems F = {rix + bi}m
i=1 on R, we systematically examine the topological
properties of forward orbits starting from arbitrary points. Discreteness is treated as a property
to be proved rather than as an a priori assumption, and we determine the exact dimensions of
both the orbits and the associated invariant sets under significantly weakened overlap conditions.
A crucial step is the foundational connection we establish between the mass and Beurling
dimensions of these expansive orbits and the classical Hausdorff, box-counting and similarity
