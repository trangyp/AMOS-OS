Abstract. Reeb spaces of continuous real-valued functions on topological
spaces are fundamental and strong tools in investigating the spaces. The Reeb
space is the natural quotient space of the space of the domain represented
by connected components of its level sets.
They have appeared in theory
of Morse functions in the last century and as important topological objects,
they are shown to be graphs for tame functions on (compact) manifolds such as
Morse(-Bott) functions and naturally generalized ones. Related general theory
develops actively, recently, mainly by Gelbukh and Saeki. For nice Haudorff
spaces and continuous functions there, they are ”1-dimensional”.
We concentrate on Reeb spaces which are not CW complexes and study
their representations by graphs and nice examples. Reconstructing nice smooth
functions with given Reeb graphs is of related studies and pioneered by Sharko
and followed by Masumoto, Michalak, Saeki, and so on. The author has also
contributed to it.
1. Introduction.
The Reeb spaces Rc of continuous real-valued functions c : X →R on topological
spaces X are fundamental and strong tools in investigating the spaces. They have
appeared in theory of Morse functions in the last century. [21] is one of related
pioneering papers. More rigorously, we can consider the equivalence relation ∼c on
X and Rc := X/∼c. For x1, x2 ∈X, x1∼cx2 if and only if they are in a same
connected component of a level set c−1(y). We define the notion of level set of the
function in this way. A contour of c is a connected component of a level set of of
c. We have the quotient map qc : X →Rc and the unique continuous function
¯c : Rc →R with c = ¯c ◦qc.
They have been shown to be graphs for tame functions on (compact) manifolds
such as Morse(-Bott) functions and naturally generalized ones, according to [5, 18],
and general studies by [22, 23].
We give exposition on singularity of smooth maps. A singular point of a differen-
tiable map is a point of the manifold of the domain where the rank of the differential
is smaller than both the dimension of the manifold of the domain and that of the
target. We use ”critical” instead of ”singular” in the case where the manifold of
the target is 1-dimensional. Let S(c) denote the set of all singular points of c.

2.1. Cell complexes and 1-dimensional cases. Hereafter, we use D0 := R0
for the one-point set and S−1 for the empty set ∅. A cell complex is a pair of a
Hausdorff space X and a family {ej,λj : Dj −Sj−1 →X} of homeomorphisms
onto subsets of X whose images are mutually disjoint and the union of all of whose
images is X, and which satisfying the condition that the set ej,λj
X −ej,λj is always
the union of the images of some of maps ej′,λj′ with j′ < j. Each map is a cell of
the cell complex and the image of the map is also a cell of this. As a specific class,
a CW complex is a cell complex which satisfies the following.
• For each cell of its, its closure in X contains finitely many cells.
• Each subset O ⊂X is open in X if and only if for each cell ej,λj(Dj −Sj−1)
of it, the intersection ej,λj(Dj −Sj−1) T O is open in ej,λj(Dj −Sj−1).
It is well-known that the dimensions of CW complexes yield a topological invariant
for topological spaces regarded as CW complexes. In our paper, a cell complex is
of dimension 1 and its dimension is a topological invariant and we call this a weakly
almost graph with ends and loops. In this case, a 1-cell is called an edge of the cell
complex and a 0-cell is called a vertex of this.
A weakly almost graph with ends and loops the closures of some of whose edges
are not homeomorphic to S1 is a weakly almost graph with ends. It is a weakly
almost graph if the closures of some of its edges are always homeomorphic to D1.
For a weakly almost graph with ends and loops, we use the terminology ”graph”
instead of ”weakly almost graph” if the 1-dimensional CW complex is locally finite.
For a weakly almost graph with ends, consider a continuous real-valued function
which is injective on each of its edges and we call the pair a digraph, here.
2.2. Pre-digraphs. A pre-digraph is defined first in the present paper. This is
defined in the following way.
Definition 1. A pre-digraph is a triplet (G0, SG0, cG0) of a Hausdorff space G0, its
discrete subset SG0 (which may not be closed in G0) and a continuous function
cG0 : G0 →R satisfying the following.
• G0−SG0 is homeomorphic to a disjoint union ⊔e∈EG0,SG0 e of copies of D1−
S0. Note that each of the copies is homeomorphic to R. By decomposing
G0 into the disjoint union of these copies and all elements of SG0, G0 is
regarded as a 1-dimensional cell complex.
• On each copy before, the function cG0 is injective.
Definition 2. Let (G0, SG0, cG0) be a pre-digraph. A point s ∈G0 is non-finite or
NF if we cannot choose a neighborhood Ns,G0,sG0 of G0 which is homeomorphic to

points there.
Definition 3. Two pre-digraphs (G0,1, SG0,1, cG0,1) and (G0,2, SG0,2, cG0,2) are iso-
morphic or equivalently, a pre-digraph is isomorphic to the other, if there exists

yields the relation ϕG ◦cG0,1 = cG0,2 ◦ΦG with some homeomorphism ϕG : R →R

is said to be an isomorphism of the pre-digraphs.
