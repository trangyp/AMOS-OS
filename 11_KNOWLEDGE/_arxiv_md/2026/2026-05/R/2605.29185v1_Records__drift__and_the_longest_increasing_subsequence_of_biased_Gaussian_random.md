Records, drift, and the longest increasing subsequence of biased Gaussian random walks
J. Ricardo G. Mendonc¸a
∗
Escola de Artes, Ciˆencias e Humanidades, Universidade de S˜ao Paulo, 03828-000 S˜ao Paulo, Brazil
The longest increasing subsequence (LIS) of a random walk has so far been studied mainly for zero-mean,
symmetric step increments. We numerically investigate the LIS of biased Gaussian random walks, with unit-
variance increments and positive drift µp = Φ−1(p), where p = P(ξ > 0). In contrast with the symmetric case,
we find that for every fixed p > 1/2 the mean LIS length grows linearly, ⟨Ln(p)⟩∼a(p)n, with a(p) increasing
from 0 at p = 1/2 to 1 as p →1. The record count is also linear, with coefficient λ(p) given by Spitzer’s formula
for the mean ascending ladder epoch, and the LIS becomes increasingly aligned with this record skeleton as p
grows. At the symmetric point p = 1/2, the record skeleton collapses to the Sparre Andersen √n scale, while
the LIS returns to the symmetric finite-variance √n logn regime. Near this limit, the excess a(µp) −λ(µp)
vanishes more slowly than linearly in the drift, although our data do not resolve a single power law. The empirical
distribution of Ln also changes across the singular point, from lognormal-like at p = 1/2 to fluctuations consistent
with Gaussian behavior for every sampled p > 1/2.
Keywords: Longest increasing subsequence; biased random walk; drift; record statistics; correlated stochastic processes
I.
INTRODUCTION
The classic longest increasing subsequence (LIS) prob-
lem, first proposed by Ulam in the early 1960s, asks for
the properties of the increasing subsequences πi1 < ··· < πik,
1 ≤i1 < ··· < ik ≤n, of maximal length k in random n-
permutations π [1, 2]. The LIS problem for random permuta-
tions proved very rich, and its study led to developments across
subjects ranging from group representation theory to random
matrix theory, algebraic combinatorics, determinantal point
processes, and exactly solvable models of statistical mechanics.
We refer the reader to [3–7] for comprehensive expositions and
further references.
Recently, the LIS problem for random walks started also
to receive attention [8, 9]. The LIS finds applications in the
analysis of real-time data streams and large-scale correlated
data [10–13]. Several recent numerical studies have inves-
tigated the properties of the LIS of random walks [14–19].
These studies confirmed the few existing rigorous results and
identified candidate scaling forms for the leading asymptotic
behavior of the length of the LIS of random walks for different
distributions of step increments, short and heavy tailed, the
possible universal properties of its full distribution function
and its large deviation rate function. All results obtained so
far, however, refer to the LIS of symmetric random walks. The
characterization of the LIS of biased random walks remained
unexplored. Biased random walks model time series with a
directional trend; the LIS then probes monotone structure in
a correlated, nonstationary sequence. While analytical results
are not available, the relevant observables are well-defined, and
a numerical investigation along the lines of the symmetric case
[14–19] offers a first step.
We study the LIS of biased random walks, in which the
step increments are Gaussian with unit variance and a positive
mean µp set by the bias parameter p = P(ξ > 0) ≥1/2. The
choice of Gaussian increments isolates the effect of asymmetry

without introducing heavy-tail effects, in a setting where the
symmetric case is well-understood. As we shall see, the lead-
ing asymptotic behavior of the length of the LIS is qualitatively
different from the symmetric one already at vanishing asym-
metry, and a notion of an asymptotic exponent in the sense
of the symmetric scaling laws no longer captures the relevant
mechanisms. In this case one should appeal to the theory of
records of biased random walks, in which the universal Sparre
Andersen behavior at p = 1/2 is replaced by a linearly growing
record skeleton with which the LIS aligns [21–24].
We find that any positive bias produces θ(p) = 1 throughout
1/2 < p < 1, so the question becomes the form of the leading
coefficient a(p) rather than of any exponent. This contrasts
with the heavy-tailed case [19], where θ(α) varies continu-
ously with the tail index. The symmetric point p = 1/2 is
a singular limit of the linear regime; the data show that the
small-drift lift-off a(µp)−λ(µp) vanishes more slowly than
linearly in µp, so that (a(µp)−λ(µp))/µp increases as µp ↓0;
however, the local log-log slope drifts across the sampled range
and no single power law captures the behavior. The LIS distri-
bution itself undergoes a sharp transition across this singular
point: lognormal-like at p = 1/2, as reported for the symmetric
case [19], and consistent with Gaussian fluctuations for every
sampled p > 1/2, in line with the central-limit picture applied
to the extensive sum-like structure of Ln in the linear regime.
This paper is organized as follows. In Section II we review
the main facts about the LIS of symmetric random walks and
the role of records, fixing notation. Section III defines the
biased Gaussian walk and the observables we measure. Sec-
tion IV characterizes the linear regime, reports the coefficient
a(p), identifies the record-density coefficient λ(p) with the
known closed form [24], and documents the empirical tran-
sition in the distribution of Ln across p = 1/2. In Section V
we interpret the gap a(p)−λ(p) as the LIS contribution from
non-record interpolations between consecutive records of the
walk. Section VI treats p = 1/2 as a singular limit of the linear
regime and identifies the drift crossover scale µc(n) at which
the symmetric prediction breaks down. Finally, in Section VII
we summarize and discuss our results and indicate directions
for further research involving distributions of infinite variance.

II.
BACKGROUND
A.
The LIS of random walks
An n-step random walk is a sequence (X1,...,Xn) of integer
or real numbers given by
X0 = 0,
Xk = Xk−1 +ξk, 1 ≤k ≤n,
(1)
where the step increments ξk are independent and identically
distributed (i.i.d.) random variables according to some proba-
bility distribution φ(x|β). The walk positions are correlated
according to
Cov(Xs,Xt) = min(s,t)Var(ξ);
(2)
the LIS problem for random walks differs from the permutation
case because the entries of the sequence are not independent.
Given a random walk (X1,...,Xn), we are interested in the
behavior of its longest possible strictly increasing subsequence
Xi1 < ··· < Xik,
1 ≤i1 < ··· < ik ≤n.
(3)
A sequence can have more than one LIS, with different ele-
ments but the same maximal length k. Note that for continuous
distributions of step increments, ties occur with probability
zero, so the distinction between weakly (Xi1 ≤··· ≤Xik) and
strictly increasing subsequences is immaterial. We therefore
refer to the LIS without qualification throughout. The LIS of
a sequence of n numbers can be calculated in O(n) space and
O(nlogn) time by the patience sorting algorithm [5, 25].
B.
Known facts
In the mathematical literature, we can find the following two
main rigorous results about the LIS of zero-mean (E(ξ) = 0),
symmetric random walks. First, when φ(x|β) is of finite
variance, it has been proved that for all ε > 0 and sufficiently
large n the expected length Ln of the LIS is bounded by
c√n ≤E(Ln) ≤n1/2+ε
(4)
for some c > 0 [8]; the lower bound follows from the Erd˝os–
Szekeres theorem [2, 4]. For the simple, discrete random
walk on Z with step increments ±1, where repeated visits to
lattice levels create plateaus that admit weakly but not strictly
increasing extensions, the lower bound on the expected length
of the weak LIS can be improved to c√n logn ≤E(Ln) [8].
Second, when the distribution of step increments φ(x|β) has
infinite variance, the bounds on E(Ln) become
nβ0−ε ≤E(Ln) ≤nβ1+ε,
(5)
where β0 ≃0.690 is the positive solution of x + 2−1−x = 1
and β1 ≃0.815 is obtained from the numerical solution of an
implicit equation involving a non-elementary integral [9]; in
this case, the exponents are not sharp.
Numerical simulations show that the behavior of Ln depends
on the increment distribution. When φ(x|β) is of finite vari-
ance, it has been found that the LIS of the random walk behaves
asymptotically as
Ln ∼a√n logn+b√n
(6)
with a ≃b ≃0.36 [14–16]. When φ(x|β) is heavy tailed,
otherwise, it has been found that
Ln ∼nθ
(7)
with a varying exponent in the range 0.5 < θ ≲0.716 depend-
ing on the tail index α according to which φ(x|β) ∼|x|−1−α
as |x| →∞[14–18]. The largest exponent θ ≃0.716 was found
for the ultra-fat tailed random walk, which has the heaviest
possible distribution of step increments [9, 17]. More recently,
a study of discrete heavy-tailed random walks on the integers
[19] discriminated between (6) and (7) by combining weighted
nonlinear least squares with ANOVA-based model selection,
confirming the two-regime picture. The same study reports
that the bulk of the distribution of Ln is well-approximated by
a lognormal across all α and n examined, supplanting earlier
Gumbel-collapse conjectures [14, 16]. The logn correction in
(6) is rigorously established only for the weak LIS of discrete
random walks; whether it reflects an actual property of the LIS
for continuous distributions of step increments or is an artifact
of the intrinsic discreteness of simulations remains unclear.

E.
Distribution of Ln
The linear regime also has a distributional signature. In the
linear regime p > 1/2, the LIS length Ln(p) is an extensive
functional of n approximately-independent contributions (mod-
ulo the global monotonicity constraint), and a central-limit-
type argument suggests that the standardized Ln(p) should
converge to a Gaussian at fixed p for large n. The symmetric
case p = 1/2 falls outside the linear regime and the same ar-
gument does not apply; for the sublinear-LIS regime, the bulk
of the Ln distribution is believed to be well-approximated by a
lognormal [19].

Ln at n = 106 against the Gaussian quantile, for four represen-
tative values of p. At p = 1/2 the data depart visibly from
the y = x reference: the right tail of Ln is fatter than Gaussian,

The numerical evidence therefore separates the two regimes
clearly: lognormal-like at p = 1/2, and consistent with Gaus-
sian fluctuations for every sampled p > 1/2, with the central-
limit picture already in effect at the smallest asymmetry tested.

VII.
SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
We have studied the longest increasing subsequence of the
biased Gaussian random walk, with drift parametrized by p =
P(ξ > 0) ≥1/2 or, equivalently, µp = Φ−1(p). For every fixed
p > 1/2, the mean LIS length, the mean record count, and the
mean LIS-record overlap grow linearly with the walk length,
⟨Ln(p)⟩∼a(p)n,
⟨Rn(p)⟩∼λ(p)n,
⟨RLIS
n (p)⟩∼r(p)n.
(35)
Thus the exponent question familiar from the symmetric and
heavy-tailed settings ceases to be informative here; any positive
drift gives θ(p) = 1, and the nontrivial object is the coefficient
a(p). The record coefficient λ(p) is quantitatively accounted
for by the known record-rate formula [24], while no analogous
closed-form expression is presently available for a(p).
The linear regime has a simple mechanism interpretation.
The positive drift creates an extensive record skeleton, and
the LIS increasingly aligns with this skeleton as p →1. The
difference a(p)−λ(p) measures the non-record contribution
to the LIS: it vanishes at both endpoints, peaks near p ≃0.60
(µp ≃0.25), and reaches about 10% at its maximum. Near
the symmetric point, the limit is singular. The record density
satisfies λ(µp) ≃1.39µp, whereas the data indicate that a(µp)
vanishes more slowly than linearly: a(µp)/µp increases as
µp ↓0. The local log-log slope of a(µp)−λ(µp) drifts across
the sampled range, so no single power law is resolved; in partic-
ular, the naive inter-record excursion estimate does not capture
the observed small-drift behavior. The finite-size crossover
between the symmetric and linear regimes is controlled by the
drift scale µc(n) ∼n−1/2.
The distribution of Ln(p) also changes across the singular
point. At p = 1/2, the standardized LIS length remains right-
skewed and is consistent with the lognormal-like bulk observed
in previous work on symmetric walks. For every sampled
p > 1/2, the standardized fluctuations are instead consistent
with a Gaussian, in agreement with a central-limit picture for an
extensive observable in the linear regime. These observations
suggest that Ln/n is self-averaging for fixed positive drift.
The main open problem is to derive the LIS coefficient
a(p). Any candidate theory must reproduce the lower bound
a(p) ≥λ(p), the high-drift limit a(p) →p, and the singular
small-drift behavior reported here. Such a derivation would
have to combine the renewal structure of the record skeleton
with the global monotonicity constraint that governs the inser-
tion of non-record LIS elements between records. The biased
Cauchy random walk is an infinite-variance counterpart, since
it belongs to a different record-statistical regime in which the
record exponent depends continuously on the drift.
