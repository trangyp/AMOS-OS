ZEROS OF CHARACTERS AND ORDERS
OF ELEMENTS IN FINITE GROUPS
GUNTER MALLE, GABRIEL NAVARRO, AND PHAM HUU TIEP
Abstract. We investigate a beautiful conjecture of T. Wilde on character val-
ues and element orders of finite groups. We reduce it to a statement on nearly
simple groups that can be checked “prime by prime”. For these groups, we show
that a strong form of Wilde’s conjecture holds in many important cases, and for
primes p > 5 we are able to show the required statement for most classes of nearly
simple groups. The few remaining cases, however, seem to require information on
extensions of irreducible characters that are not available at the present time.
1. Introduction
The first theorem on zeros of characters of finite groups is due to W. Burnside
and asserts that every non-linear irreducible character χ of a finite group G takes
the value zero on some element (see [16, Thm 3.15]). (Much later on, it was proved
in [21], using the classification of finite simple groups, that this zero can be chosen
to occur on an element of prime power order.) R. Brauer and C. Nesbitt proved the
most celebrated connection between the order of elements and the codegree |G|/χ(1)
of χ: if a prime p does not divide the integer |G|/χ(1), then χ(g) = 0 for all g ∈G
of order divisible by p (see [16, Thm 8.17]). A more sophisticated version of this
involving defect groups and blocks is a classical consequence of Brauer’s second main
theorem, see below.
This paper investigates an intriguing conjecture of T. Wilde [29], which relates
orders of elements, zeros, and codegrees, implies the result of Brauer and Nesbitt,
and whose statement looks innocent.
Conjecture A (Wilde). Let G be a finite group and let χ ∈Irr(G). If χ(g) ̸= 0 for
some g ∈G, then the order o(g) of g divides |G|/χ(1).
Our first main result is the reduction of Conjecture A to a stronger version of the
problem for nearly simple groups. Let us say that (H, h, χ) satisfies (∗) if H is a
finite group, h ∈H, the derived subgroup L = [H, H] is quasi-simple, Z(L) = Z(H)
is cyclic, H = L⟨h⟩, and χ ∈Irr(H) is faithful with χ(h) ̸= 0.
