UNDECIDABILITY PROBLEMS FOR SEMIFREE DG ALGEBRAS
CIPRIAN MANOLESCU AND NICK ROZENBLYUM
Abstract. We prove that the stable tame isomorphism, quasi-isomorphism, and derived Morita
equivalence problems for semifree noncommutative differential graded algebras (DGAs) are all un-
decidable. This resolves half of Problem 5.16 from the K3 Problem List in Low-Dimensional Topol-
ogy. We present two solutions, both obtained (essentially autonomously) by Gemini Deep Think /
Aletheia.
1. Introduction
Differential graded algebras play an important role in numerous branches of mathematics, in-
cluding algebraic geometry, representation theory, homotopy theory, symplectic geometry, and
low-dimensional topology.
Some of the DGAs that appear in practice are graded commutative, and some are not.
In
particular, in contact geometry, the Chekanov-Eliashberg Legendrian contact DGA [5, 7] associates
to a Legendrian submanifold a noncommutative DGA that is semifree (i.e., its underlying graded
algebra is free). A Legendrian isotopy induces a stable tame isomorphism of the Legendrian contact
DGAs.
It is thus a natural question whether we can tell semifree DGAs apart. There are several vari-
ants of this question, because there exist different notions of equivalence for DGAs: stable tame
isomorphism, quasi-isomorphism, and derived Morita equivalence (ordered here from the strongest
notion to the weakest). We refer to Section 2 for the exact definitions.
Let R be a nontrivial, unital commutative ring. For the decidability questions below to make
sense, we assume that R is Turing computable.1
Theorem 1. Let A and B be semifree differential graded algebras over R. The following questions
are undecidable:
(a) whether A and B are stable tame isomorphic;
(b) whether A and B are quasi-isomorphic;
(c) whether A and B are derived Morita equivalent.
This answers one half of Problem 5.16 from [2]. The other half consists of the analogous problems
for semifree graded commutative DGAs, which remain open.
We give two proofs of Theorem 1. The first is based on constructing two DGAs that are equivalent
(in any of the above senses) if and only if a certain associative algebra is trivial. We then apply the
undecidability of the triviality problem for such algebras. The second solution is based on a similar
reduction to another known undecidable problem: the triviality of finitely presented groups.
The first proof is shorter than the second. However, the second has the advantage that it explicitly
reduces to the better-known Adyan-Rabin theorem about groups, and that it proves undecidability
of a few other related problems. (See Remarks 1 and 2.)

2. Background
A differential graded algebra A = ⊕i∈ZAi over R is a Z-graded unital algebra together with a
differential ∂: A →A of degree −1 satisfying ∂2 = 0 and the graded Leibniz rule. We say that A is
semifree if, as a graded algebra, A is isomorphic to the free, noncommutative R-algebra on finitely
many homogeneous generators xi, i.e., to the tensor algebra T(x1, . . . , xn) on the free R-module
generated by x1, . . . , xn.
An elementary automorphism of a free tensor algebra T(x1, . . . , xn) is one that maps exactly one
generator xi 7→αxi + P (where α ∈R× and P is a homogeneous polynomial exclusively in the
remaining generators) and leaves all other generators fixed. A tame isomorphism between tensor
algebras T(x1, . . . , xn) and T(y1, . . . , yn) is the composition of some elementary automorphisms of
T(x1, . . . , xn) followed by the isomorphism sending xi 7→yi or all i.
A stabilization of a semifree DGA A is obtained from A by adjoining two free generators e and
f of degrees k + 1 and k (for some k ∈Z) such that ∂(e) = f and ∂(f) = 0.
Following [5, 8], two semifree DGAs A and B are called tame isomorphic if they are related by a
tame isomorphism (of the underlying tensor algebras) that commutes with the differentials. They
are called stable tame isomorphic if they become tame isomorphic after applying finitely many
stabilizations to each.
A quasi-isomorphism between DGAs A and B is a DG homomorphism A →B inducing an
isomorphism on homology; two DGAs are called quasi-isomorphic if they are related by a zigzag of
quasi-isomorphisms. If two semifree DGAs are stable tame isomorphic, they are quasi-isomorphic;
see [8, Corollary 3.11].
Given a DGA A, we denote by D(A) the derived category of left DG modules over A. Two
semifree DGAs A and B are derived Morita equivalent if D(A) and D(B) are equivalent as trian-
gulated categories. Quasi-isomorphism implies derived Morita equivalence. We refer to [10, 11] for
more on the Morita theory for DGAs.
3. Reduction to algebras
For the first proof of Theorem 1, we use the following result:
Theorem 2 (Bokut’ [4]). Let A be a finitely presented (noncommutative, unital) algebra. The
problem of whether A = 0 is undecidable.

In other words, if
S = {f1, . . . , fm} ⊂R⟨x1, . . . , xn⟩
is a finite set of non-commutative polynomials, then it is undecidable whether it generates the unit

A = R⟨x1, . . . , xn⟩/I
being trivial.
Starting from a set S as above (where we may as well assume n, m ≥1) we construct a semifree
non-commutative DGA A equipped with the following generators:
• Degree 0 generators: x1, . . . , xn with differential ∂A(xi) = 0.
• Degree 1 generators: r1, . . . , rm with differential ∂A(rj) = fj.
The degree 0 module A0 is the free algebra R⟨x1, . . . , xn⟩, and the image of the differential ∂A :
A1 →A0 is the two-sided ideal I. Consequently, the 0-th homology is given by
H0(A) = A0/I = A.
Next, let B be the semifree non-commutative DGA with the same generators xi and rj as before,
with differential
∂B(xi) = 0,
i = 1, . . . , n;
∂B(rj) = 1,
j = 1, . . . , m.
Because 1 is a boundary, we deduce that 1 = 0 in the ring H∗(B), so B is acyclic: H∗(B) = 0.
Proposition 3. For the algebras A and B described above, the following are equivalent:
(a) A and B are stable tame isomorphic;
(b) A and B are quasi-isomorphic;
(c) A and B are derived Morita equivalent;
(d) The algebra A is trivial: A = 0.
Proof. As mentioned in Section 2, we have (a) ⇒(b) ⇒(c). It suffices to prove (c) ⇒(d) and
(d) ⇒(a).
(c) ⇒(d): Since B is acyclic, it is quasi-isomorphic (and hence derived Morita equivalent) to
0. Thus, the derived category D(B) is equivalent to the zero category. Since we assumed (c), we
deduce that D(A) is also equivalent to the zero category. The DGA A (viewed as a free module
over itself) is a compact generator for D(A) (see [10]), with natural abelian group isomorphisms
HomD(A)(A, A) ∼= H0(A).
Therefore, we must have that A = H0(A) is trivial.
(d) ⇒(a): Since H0(A) = 0, we have 1 = 0 in H∗(A), so A is acyclic. By [6, Corollary 2.2],
because A and B are acyclic and have the same number of generators and relations, they are stable
tame isomorphic.
□
Theorem 1 follows by combining Theorem 2 with Proposition 3.

G is trivial is undecidable.

Proposition 5. For the algebras A and B described above, the following are equivalent:
(a) A and B are tame isomorphic;
(b) A and B are stable tame isomorphic;
(c) A and B are quasi-isomorphic;
(d) A and B are derived Morita equivalent;
(e) The group G is trivial: G ∼= 1.
Proof. We have (a) ⇒(b) ⇒(c) ⇒(d). It suffices to prove (d) ⇒(e) and (e) ⇒(a).
(d) ⇒(e): Let us compute the 0-th homology H0 for both DGAs. Because there are no generators
in negative degrees, the 0-cycles consist of the entire free algebra R⟨X⟩. The 0-th homology is
therefore obtained by taking the quotient of this algebra by the two-sided ideal generated by the
boundaries of the degree 1 elements.
For A, the boundaries are generated exactly by the relation polynomials fj. Hence,
H0(A) = R⟨X⟩/⟨f1, . . . , fN⟩∼= R[G],
the group ring of G over R.
For B, the boundaries are generated by the ideal ⟨f1, . . . , fN, {x −1}x∈X⟩. Setting x = 1 for all
generators collapses all group elements to the identity, which automatically satisfies the relations
fj = 1 −1 = 0. The algebra therefore retracts to the base ring:
H0(B) = R⟨X⟩/⟨f1, . . . , fN, {x −1}x∈X⟩∼= R.

Remark 1. The argument in this second proof also shows that the tame isomorphism problem for
semifree DGAs is undecidable.
Remark 2. An augmentation of a DGA A is a DG map ϵ : A →R (such that ϵ(1) = 1). One can ask
about the decidability of the three kinds of equivalence questions for augmented DGAs. The proof
of Theorem 1 in Section 3 does not immediately apply because it involves the zero DGA, which
cannot be augmented. The proof in this section does apply though, because the tame isomorphism
in (e) ⇒(a) in Proposition 5 is compatible with the natural augmentations of A and B (which send
all the x’s to 1 and the other generators to 0).
