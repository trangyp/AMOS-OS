Abstract: We investigate the description of quark confinement in terms of confining strings or flux
tubes. We show that compact QED with a topological θ-term, in the dyon condensation phase, is
described by a massive two-form field Bµν that gives rise to a string theory with an IR Brazovskii-
Lifshitz fixed point at strong coupling. This corresponds to a quantum consistent “free string” in (3+1)
dimensions, representing the dual of asymptotic freedom in the UV. Contrary to critical strings, which
correspond to trivial Gaussian fixed points, this string is stabilized by a finite thickness, determined by
the mass of the Bµν field, instead of living in a higher-dimensional space. It correspondingly contains
a massive world-sheet resonance, in addition to the Nambu-Goto phonons, that improves fitting with
data. We compute the confining potential and show that it reproduces a generalized Arvis potential

Introduction
Understanding the mechanism of confinement in the strong interaction remains one of the central open
problems of the Standard Model of particle physics. Over the past decades, using theoretical insights
as well as lattice gauge theory calculations, a coherent theoretical picture has emerged, identifying
the essential ingredients that such a mechanism is expected to involve [1, 2].
A key element of
this picture is the condensation of magnetic monopoles, which realises a dual analogue of the Higgs
mechanism familiar from superconductivity [3]. This leads to the electric-magnetic dual of the Meissner
effect, whereby electric, rather than magnetic, flux is expelled from the vacuum and confined into flux
tubes.
These flux tubes manifest themselves as confining strings connecting, for instance, quark-
antiquark pairs, thereby preventing the existence of free coloured particles at low energies. Note that
confinement is not predicated on non-Abelian gauge fields, compactness of the gauge group is enough.
Abelian confinement of Cooper pairs in condensed matter systems in the superinsulating state [4, 5]
has been recently detected in many materials (for a review see [6]). Furthermore, Polyakov’s proof of
confinement in 3D was precisely for a compact U(1) [7]. This shows that the non-Abelian character
of gauge fields is not the main driver of confinement.
While the potential role of strings in understanding confinement is intuitively appealing and widely
accepted, a concrete and fully developed implementation remains elusive. It is therefore somewhat
surprising that, despite the extensive development of string theory over the past 40-50 years, relatively
little effort has been devoted to this challenge (see [8, 9] for a review of recent work). The standard
Nambu-Goto string, in addition to requiring the wrong spacetime dimensionality, fails to reproduce

key phenomenological features of hadronic physics, such as particle masses and the intercept of Regge
trajectories.
We know that QCD provides a meaningful description of strong interactions only at short scales,
where asymptotic freedom renders the coupling weak and the theory perturbative. At large scales,
however, the coupling becomes strong and color is confined, leading to hadrons as the only observable
states. In this confinement regime one expects strings, formed by color flux tubes to correctly describe
the strong interactions. In the prevailing view, these are effective strings, since it is believed that
a quantum consistent string theory with Nambu-Goto behaviour at large distances is impossible in
(3+1) dimensions. Duality, however, suggests that strings are dual mirrors to gauge fields. If duality
is realized, there should exist an infrared string fixed point at strong gauge coupling. Such a string
fixed point should be universal and independent of color, representing a genuine infrared string theory
of confinement rather than a microscopic property of the gauge fields themselves, a dual mirror to
asymptotic freedom. As emphasized by Polyakov [10], if such a string fixed point can be found in
any confining gauge theory, due to universality it should be the correct dual theory of QCD. At large
scales, colour should play essentially no role. Polyakov’s conjecture is even more compelling, given
that, contrary to a widespread belief, a quantum consistent string theory with long distance Nambu-
Goto behaviour does exist in (3+1) dimensions [11, 12], where it realizes a Brazovskii-Lifshitz fixed
point [13–15].
In this work we take Polyakov’s proposal seriously and analyze compact QED in (3+1) dimensions
as a paradigmatic example of a confining gauge theory in the strong-coupling regime, one that finds
a direct physical realization in superinsulators.
It is known that compact QED in the monopole
confinement phase gives rise to a massive antisymmetric tensor Bµν field theory which, after proper
integration of the massive field, admits an exact dual representation as a string theory [16–18]. Here
we show that, when a θ-term is added to the action (and the condensate is thus dyonic) this string
formulation reduces exactly to the previously mentioned quantum consistent Brazovskii-Lifshitz string.
In presence of a θ-term the cutoff can be removed and the model flows to the Brazovskii-Lifshitz point
describing a string with a finite thickness. The thickness may be determined by the mass of the Bµν
field and is all that remains of the original gauge fields. Strings much longer than their thickness
approach c = 1 (per transverse coordinate) Nambu-Goto behaviour.

coordinates through a Gaussian map such that only quantum effects of transverse deformations ϕi(σ)

The Brazovskii-Lifshitz string can be said to be dual to the Polyakov string. There is a crucial
difference though. Even if we introduce a Lagrange multiplier for an independent world-sheet metric
`a la Polyakov, here this metric survives also inside the powers of the Laplacian in the higher-order
terms. Therefore there is no Weyl symmetry, even at the classical level. The only symmetries are
reparametrization invariances on the world sheet, which can be completely fixed in transverse gauge
by choosing one space coordinate along the string and writing an action for the remaining transverse
coordinates alone. Therefore, there is also no need to cancel a Weyl anomaly at quantum level which
implies the absence of any constraint on the allowed space-time dimensions: 3+1 dimensions are
perfectly fine. The absence of Weyl symmetry has another important consequence. The Virasoro
constraints of vanishing energy-momentum tensor are also absent. Therefore, also the usual mass
formula following from these constraints is absent and, correspondingly there is no tachyon in the
target space spectrum.
Both the Polyakov string and the Brazovskii-Lifshitz string introduced in
[11] are quantum consistent strings. The Polyakov string is stabilized by a critical dimension 26, the
Brazovskii-Lifshitz string by a finite thickness.

that will turn out to be exactly the Brazovskii-Lifshitz confining string.

Compact QED with θ Term and Confining Strings

Conclusions
We have investigated the description of confinement in terms of a quantum consistent confining string
arising from a gauge theory. Starting from compact QED in (3 + 1) dimensions with a θ–term, we
showed that, in the dyon condensation phase, the theory admits a dual formulation in terms of a
– 21 –


## Page 23


massive two-form field Bµν leading to a string theory that flows to a Brazovskii–Lifshitz infrared fixed
point. This fixed point describes a quantum consistent string of finite thickness whose long-distance
behaviour approaches that of the Nambu–Goto string. But, contrary to the Nambu-Goto string, this
confining string can be consistently defined in 3 + 1 dimensions. The non-vanishing θ plays a crucial
role in making the two-dimensional theory quantum mechanically consistent in the sense that the
cutoff can be removed leading to finite couplings.
Our main results are:
• IR string fixed point: compact QED with a θ–term flows to a Brazovskii–Lifshitz fixed point
describing a confining string with an intrinsic thickness.
• Additional massive world-sheet resonance: Brazovskii-Lifshitz strings contain a massive world-
sheet resonance that can be considered as an alternative to the worldsheet axion [19] to improve
fitting with the data. Lattice data support negative stiffness also for SU(N).
• Quark potential: at one-loop the theory predicts a generalized Arvis [38] potential V (L) =
a(L)L
p
1 −2b(L)/a(L)L2 with running coefficients entirely determined by the string dynamics.
• Regge intercept: finite thickness tends to raise the intercept from the Nambu–Goto value α0 =
1/12 to higher values.
• Phenomenology: solving the Schr¨odinger equation for the heaviest quarkonium states Υ(nS)
yields mass–splitting ratios in 2.5% agreement with experiment and point to the exclusive stringy
origin of these states.
In close analogy with critical strings, which couple naturally to a massless two-form, the confining
strings described here couple to a massive antisymmetric tensor field Bµν. The mass introduces the
physical scale associated with the thickness of the confining string.
Dualities among massive antisymmetric tensor theories play a central role in our analysis. In
particular, the 2d sine–Gordon model admits a dual description as a higher-derivative theory of the
Brazovskii–Lifshitz type, while compact QED in four dimensions, after dyon condensation, can be
formulated in terms of a massive antisymmetric tensor field Bµν.
Recent developments in generalized and non-invertible symmetries touch on similar properties of
massive antisymmetric tensor symmetries and may also be important for a more complete formulation
of the dynamics of confining strings [36]. Likewise, T ¯T and J ¯T deformations could play a role in a
full description of the effective two-dimensional theory (see for instance [37]). These directions provide
promising avenues for future work. Also, here we have mainly studied the physics of the IR fixed
point. Further investigations of the consequences of a non-zero stiffness for QCD will be the subject
of forthcoming publications.
Overall, our results provide concrete support for Polyakov’s conjecture that confining gauge theo-
ries admit a universal infrared string description. In this picture the Brazovskii–Lifshitz string repre-
sents, in some way, the infrared dual of asymptotically free gauge dynamics, capturing the universal
long-distance behaviour of confining flux tubes independently of the microscopic gauge group. Be-
yond supporting the string picture of confinement, this framework opens several directions for further
investigation, including the role of relevant perturbations around the fixed point, the extension to
dynamical quarks, and detailed comparisons with lattice results for flux-tube spectra and potentials.
We hope to address some of these questions in the near future.
