Abstract
We decompose the ambient Bochner Laplacian acting on tangential vector fields on a
thin shell around an arbitrary smooth hypersurface Mn ,→Rn+1 into an intrinsic piece
and a radial boundary-shear piece. The intrinsic piece is the deformation Laplacian ∆(n)
B +
Ric(n) on every hypersurface, regardless of extrinsic geometry. The boundary-shear piece
is determined entirely by the normal profile of the velocity field.
We prove that stress-
free (Navier slip) boundary conditions yield the deformation Laplacian universally, and that
Hodge (zero tangential vorticity) boundary conditions yield the Hodge Laplacian universally.
Both results hold on any smooth hypersurface, not only on surfaces of constant curvature.
This extends the sphere-specific results of Temam-Ziane and Miura to the general case and
explains the extension-dependence found by Chan, Czubak, and Yoneda on the ellipsoid
as a physical boundary-condition dependence. We also derive a continuous one-parameter
family of boundary conditions interpolating between the two limits, producing an effective
viscous operator ∆α = ∆Def −2α Ric −4α(1 −α)S2 that couples to the extrinsic geometry
(through the shape operator squared) only in the intermediate partial-slip regime.

Introduction
What viscous operator should appear in the Navier-Stokes equations for a fluid confined to a
thin layer around a curved surface? This question arises naturally in the thin-shell limit: a
three-dimensional incompressible fluid, governed by the standard Navier-Stokes equations in
flat R3 (where there is no ambiguity about the viscous operator), is confined to a thin shell
of thickness ϵ around a smooth surface M2 ,→R3, and one takes ϵ →0 to derive effective
two-dimensional equations on M.
The answer, as discovered independently by Temam and Ziane [1] and Miura [2], depends
on the boundary condition imposed at the walls of the shell. Temam and Ziane, working on
the sphere S2 with Hodge boundary conditions (vanishing tangential vorticity at the walls),
obtained the Hodge Laplacian. Miura, working on general curved thin domains with Navier slip
(stress-free) boundary conditions, obtained the deformation Laplacian ∆Def = ∆B + Ric. On
the sphere, these two operators differ by the Weitzenb¨ock gap 2 Ric.
In a companion paper [3], we showed that the deformation Laplacian is the unique viscous
operator consistent with the Lagrangian kinematics of intrinsic fluids on a Riemannian manifold,
and we carried out an explicit decomposition of the ambient 3D Bochner Laplacian on a spherical
shell that recovers both the Temam-Ziane and Miura results as the two natural endpoints of a
boundary-condition family. The question left open was whether this decomposition is specific
to the sphere (a surface of constant curvature) or extends to general hypersurfaces.

Chan and Czubak [4] derived a general Gauss formula for the Laplacian on hypersurfaces,

Discussion
5.1
The universality mechanism
The key to both universality results is the Gauss equation. The ambient Bochner Laplacian,
restricted to tangential fields, produces zero-order terms involving nH S and S2 (the first and
second powers of the shape operator). These are extrinsic quantities. But the combination
nH S −S2 is precisely the intrinsic Ricci tensor, by the Gauss equation for hypersurfaces in flat
space. This identity holds on every smooth hypersurface without any curvature assumptions.
The boundary condition then determines a radial contribution Frad that is also built from
nH S and S2.
For Navier-slip, Frad = 0: no radial contribution, and the operator is the
deformation Laplacian. For the Hodge condition, Frad = −2 Ric(n): the radial contribution is
again intrinsic (another application of the Gauss equation), and the operator shifts from the
deformation to the Hodge Laplacian. For intermediate conditions, the Gauss equation absorbs
most of the extrinsic terms, leaving only the S2 remainder in (22).
5.2
Relationship to previous results
Temam and Ziane [1] derived the Hodge Laplacian on S2 with Hodge boundary conditions.
Miura [2] derived the deformation Laplacian on general curved thin domains with Navier-slip
conditions. Our results show that both are instances of a single universal structure: the bound-
ary condition selects the operator, and the selection is independent of the extrinsic geometry.
The Temam-Ziane result is not an artefact of the sphere’s constant curvature but a universal
principle.

Chan and Czubak [4] derived a general Gauss formula for the Laplacian on hypersurfaces,
leaving the extension (and hence the normal profile) unspecified. Chan, Czubak, and Yoneda [5]
found that the projected operator on the ellipsoid depends on the extension. Our results explain
this dependence physically: different extensions correspond to different boundary conditions,
and the two natural fluid-mechanical choices (stress-free and zero-vorticity) yield the two in-
trinsic Laplacians regardless of the surface.
Most recently, Chan, Czubak, and Fuster Aguilera [6] derived four viscosity operators on
the ellipsoid by taking the thin-shell limit along different expansion directions (the scaling di-
rection versus the normal direction) and with different averaging methods. Their finding that
the result depends on the expansion method is consistent with the present work: our Fermi-
coordinate (normal direction) decomposition yields the universal intrinsic operators of Theo-
rems 1.1 and 1.2, while expansions along non-normal directions introduce additional extrinsic
dependence analogous to the intermediate regime of Theorem 1.3.
In a companion paper [3], we carried out this analysis for the sphere and derived the kine-
matic selection of the deformation Laplacian for intrinsic fluids. The present results reinforce
the kinematic selection from a different direction: even in the thin-shell limit, the deforma-
tion Laplacian is the universal outcome when the boundary condition respects the intrinsic
kinematics (zero normal shear).
5.3
The interpolating family and partial-slip membranes
The operator (22) for intermediate boundary conditions has a physical interpretation. A real
membrane (biological, industrial) is neither perfectly stress-free nor perfectly vorticity-free; its
boundary condition is determined by its mechanical properties and lies somewhere between
the two limits. The parameter α measures the degree to which the membrane constrains the
tangential vorticity.
For intermediate α, the effective viscous operator depends on the extrinsic geometry through
S2. This means the viscous behaviour of a fluid near a curved membrane is genuinely sensitive
to the curvature of the membrane, in a way that is absent for the two limiting cases. The
coefficient 4α(1 −α) is maximal at α = 1/2 (the “half-slip” condition), where the extrinsic
contribution is strongest.
On a surface of constant curvature (S = c Id), S2 = c2 Id is a scalar multiple of the identity
and acts as an additional zeroth-order potential.
On a surface with non-constant principal
curvatures (such as an ellipsoid), S2 is a non-trivial endomorphism that couples the different
tangential directions anisotropically. This anisotropy is a genuine physical effect: a fluid in
partial slip on an ellipsoid would experience direction-dependent effective viscosity, with different
dissipation rates along the directions of maximum and minimum curvature.
5.4
Implications for the viscous operator question
Czubak [7] surveyed the question of which Laplacian should appear in the Navier-Stokes equa-
tions on Riemannian manifolds and concluded that “the exact form of the equations will depend
on the physical problem at hand.” Our results give a precise version of this statement for the
thin-shell setting: the operator depends on the boundary condition, the two natural choices give
the two standard intrinsic operators, and intermediate choices produce a one-parameter family
that interpolates between them with an extrinsic correction.
Together with the kinematic selection of the deformation Laplacian for intrinsic fluids [3],
this provides a complete account of the viscous operator question in both the intrinsic and
thin-shell settings.
