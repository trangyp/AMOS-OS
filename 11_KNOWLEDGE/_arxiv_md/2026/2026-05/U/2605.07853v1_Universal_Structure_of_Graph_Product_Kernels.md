Dedicated to Mike Davis on the occasion of his 75th birthday.
Abstract. Let GΓ be a graph product over a finite simplicial graph Γ, and let KΓ
denote the kernel of the canonical homomorphism from GΓ to the direct product
of its vertex groups. It is known that, up to isomorphism, KΓ depends only on the
underlying graph Γ and the cardinalities of the vertex groups.
In this paper we establish a functorial refinement of this fact. We show that any
collection of set maps between the vertex groups naturally induces a homomorphism
between the corresponding kernels, and that this construction is functorial. Several
applications are discussed.
1. Introduction
Graph products interpolate between free and direct products of groups and have
played a central role in geometric group theory. Given a simplicial graph GΓ with
vertex set V pΓq and a collection of vertex groups tGiuiPV pΓq, the graph product GΓ is
obtained by taking the free product of the Gi’s and adding commutation relations
corresponding to the edges of Γ. Its structure reflects both the algebra of the vertex
groups and the combinatorics of Γ.

Corollary 1.4. Let Γ be a finite simplicial graph and let GΓ be the graph product of
groups tGiuiPV pΓq.
(i) If each Gi is infinite countable, then KΓ is isomorphic to the commutator subgroup
of the right-angled Artin group AΓ on Γ.
(ii) If each Gi is countable, then KΓ is a retract of the commutator subgroup of the
right-angled Artin group AΓ on Γ.
(iii) If each Gi is nontrivial, then the commutator subgroup of the right-angled Coxeter
group WΓ on Γ is a retract of KΓ.
Corollary 1.4 (i) and (ii) is a strengthening of Corollary 14 of [Sh12]. Part (iii) has
also been established in Lemma 2.7 of [Li23].
Corollary 1.4 (ii) is useful in investigating the hereditary properties of graph products.
For instance, it is well-known that finitely generated right-angled Artin groups are
poly-free (e.g. [Hv07]). One may extend this result as follows:
Corollary 1.5. Let Γ be a finite simplicial graph and let GΓ be the graph product of
countable groups tGiuiPV pΓq. Then KΓ is poly-free. In addition, if each vertex group is
poly-free, then GΓ is poly-free.
Let C be a class of groups satisfying the following properties:
(P1) Every subgroup of any finitely generated right-angled Artin group is in C;
(P2) If B is a group that has a normal subgroup A P C such that B{A P C, then B P C.
Corollary 1.6. Let C be a class of groups satisfying properties pP1q and pP2q. Let Γ be
a finite simplicial graph and let GΓ be the graph product of countable groups tGiuiPV pΓq.
If each Gi P C, then GΓ P C.
There is an application of Corollary 1.6 to the Baum–Connes conjecture.
Definition 1.7 (Baum–Connes Conjecture with Coefficients and Finite Wreath Prod-
ucts, [SP25]). A countable discrete group G is said to satisfy the Baum–Connes
conjecture with coefficients with finite wreath products (abbreviated BCC with finite
wreath products) if, for every finite group F, the wreath product G ≀F satisfies the
Baum–Connes conjecture with coefficients.
In particular, any group satisfying this conjecture also satisfies the usual Baum–
Connes conjecture with coefficients. An advantage of this strengthened version of the
conjecture is that it is stable under group extensions [SP25, Theorem 4.3(6)].
It is known that the Baum–Connes conjecture with coefficients is closed under graph
products, since it is stable under both finite direct products and amalgamated products
along subgroups [OO01a, OO01b].
Corollary 1.8. Let Γ be a countable simplicial graph and let GΓ be the graph product
of countable groups tGiuiPV pΓq. If each vertex group Gi satisfies BCC with finite wreath
products, then GΓ also satisfies BCC with finite wreath products.

with stabilisers precisely the conjugates of the vertex group Gi [Dav98]. This building
is a CAT(0) cube complex where cubes of maximal dimension are homeomorphic to
the cone on the first barycentric subdivision of L. Therefore, X has dimension n ` 1.
The action of Q on L combines with action of GL on X to give an action of GL ¸ Q
on X. This is evident from the panel structure of X (see for example [BH99, II.12]
and also [PP24, §3]). Since KL acts freely on X, the restriction of this action to G is
proper. Since X is CAT(0), it follows that it is a model for EG of dimension n ` 1.
Therefore, gdG “ n ` 1.
By Corollary 5.14, vcdG ď n. If L is a barycentric subdivision or Lsing is a full
subcomplex of L, then by Theorem 1.1 of [LP17], vcdG1 “ n ` 1, which implies that
vcdG “ n ` 1.
□

5.6. Eilenberg–Ganea conjecture. A well-known conjecture of Eilenberg and Ganea
predicts the following.
Conjecture 5.20 (Eilenberg–Ganea, 1957). If a group G has cdG “ 2, then there
exists a 2-dimensional KpG, 1q-complex.
In [BB97, Theorem 8.7], Bestvina and Brady proved that if L is a flag triangulation
of a spine of the Poincar´e homology sphere (see also Example 5.16), then BL is a
counterexample to either the Eilenberg–Ganea Conjecture or the Whitehead Conjecture
[Whi41].
In [LP17], we showed that for the same acyclic flag 2-complex L, vcdWL “ 2 but
WL does not act properly and cellularly on any contractible 2-complex. If rWL, WLs
was a counterexample to the Eilenberg–Ganea Conjecture, then it would also be a
counterexample to Wall’s D(2) Problem [Wal79, D3].
In his discussion of the Eilenberg–Ganea Conjecture, Davis remarks that the Bestvina–
Brady group BL is a “more convincing candidate for a counterexample” than rWL, WLs
(see [Dav08, p. 156]). The following formalizes this relation.
Corollary 5.21. Let L be a flag triangulation of a spine of the Poincar´e homology
sphere. If rWL, WLs is a counterexample to the Eilenberg–Ganea Conjecture, then BL
is a counterexample as well.
Proof. By Corollary 1.4 (iii), rWL, WLs embeds as a subgroup in rAL, ALs which in
turn is a subgroup of BL. Since cdrWL, WLs “ cdBL “ 2, the claim follows.
□
