Abstract— This paper introduces a signal strength-based direc-
tion of arrival (DOA) estimation approach for directional sen-
sors that explicitly accounts for missed detections. In traditional
phase-based DOA estimation frameworks, negative information
from expected emitters that fall below the detection threshold
fall outside the scope of standard measurement models. Unlike
phase-based DOA estimation methods, the proposed approach
relies only on received signal strength measurements. As a
result, missed detections arise naturally from the sensing and
detection process and convey valuable information via the known
detection thresholds. By incorporating both detected signals and
missed detections into the likelihood function, we develop a
probabilistic estimation method that fully leverages the underlying measurement and detection models. Simulation results
show that the proposed method significantly improves DOA estimation accuracy compared to baseline techniques, partic-
ularly in challenging scenarios with high missed-detection rates. Real-world experiments using Bluetooth Low Energy
(BLE) signals and directional antennas further validate the effectiveness of the approach, demonstrating substantial
performance gains. These findings highlight the value of modeling missed detections in sensor array processing and
open new avenues for enhancing localization performance in wireless communication systems.
Index Terms— Direction of Arrival Estimation, Missed Detections, Directional Sensors, Directional Sensitivity, Wireless
Localization, BLE
I. INTRODUCTION
Accurate estimation of the direction of arrival (DOA) of
a signal source is fundamental in many signal processing and
communication systems, supporting tasks such as localization,
tracking, and beamforming [1]–[3]. The problem has been
extensively studied [4]–[6], with recent advances summarized
in [7].
Classical DOA methods rely on sensor arrays and exploit
time delays (time-difference of arrival (TDOA)) or spatial
correlations, such as multiple signal classification (MUSIC) [8]
and estimation of signal parameters via rotational invariance
techniques (ESPRIT) [9]. More recent work improves robust-
ness and performance in challenging conditions, including
impulsive noise and limited array configurations [10], [11].
In many practical systems, such as Bluetooth Low Energy
(BLE), sensors provide only scalar received signal strength
indicator (RSSI) measurements without phase coherence, mak-
ing classical methods inapplicable. This has motivated RSSI-

Several such methods have been proposed. For example,

Machine learning approaches [17]–[19] learn relationships
from RSSI to location or direction using data-driven models,
capturing complex propagation effects but requiring extensive
training data and observed measurements during estimation.
Despite these advances, existing methods generally assume
that measurements are available and rely on aggregating ob-
servations. They do not explicitly model missed detections,
where sensors fail to report measurements due to low signal
strength, interference, or hardware limitations.
Missed detections are common in wireless systems [20],
[21] and can provide useful information when detection thresh-
olds are known. However, they are typically ignored in existing
DOA estimation approaches.

In this paper, we propose a probabilistic framework for
RSSI-based DOA estimation that incorporates both detected
signals and missed detections into the likelihood function. By
jointly modeling detection probability and RSSI measurements,
the method exploits information from both detections and non-
detections.
This constitutes a modeling advancement for RSSI-based
localization, improving estimation accuracy in scenarios with
frequent missed detections caused by low signal strength,
noise, or propagation effects.
The method is evaluated through simulations and real-
world experiments using BLE signals and directional antennas,
demonstrating improved performance in challenging condi-
tions.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: In
Section II, we formulate the problem and present the mea-
surement model. This is followed by Section III, where we
describe the proposed state estimation method that accounts
for missed detections. In Section IV, we derive the specific
application of the proposed method to DOA estimation with
directional sensors. In Section V, we describe the simulation
setup used to evaluate the proposed method, and present the
simulation results. Followed by Section VI, where we detail
the real-world experimental study, and present the results from
the real-world experiments. Finally, Section VII concludes the
paper and discusses future work.
II. PROBLEM FORMULATION
Throughout this paper, we consider a setting with N sensor
measurements, each originating from a sensing element char-
acterized by a known measurement model hm(x), where x
is the state to be estimated and m = 1, 2, . . . , N indexes the
sensors. The index m may correspond to different sensors in
an array of directional elements or to multiple measurement
instances from a single sensor. The sensors may share a
common measurement model or each may have its own.
Each sensor measurement is associated with a known de-
tection threshold γ. Our objective is to estimate the state x of
a signal source using the n ≤N measurements that result in
detections. The state x represents any parameter that influences
the measurement model—such as DOA, position, or velocity—
and is assumed to remain constant over the N measurements.
We further assume that a signal source is always present,
so the state x always exists, and that no false alarms occur;
i.e., any detection made by a sensor is guaranteed to originate
from the signal source whose state we aim to estimate.

missed detections can be applied to various estimation prob-

systems.

DOA estimation accuracy in practical scenarios with sensor

In practical applications, the constant detection efficiency
term pc,m is typically less than 1 due to hardware imperfec-
tions, environmental conditions, and other loss mechanisms.
To evaluate the robustness of the proposed method under
such conditions, simulations are conducted for pc,m ∈[0.7, 1],
assuming identical values across all sensors. The results are
shown in Fig. 8, which presents the mean RMSE of the DOA

The results show that the proposed method remains robust

lower values of α. However, the performance degrades for
higher values of α compared to the case with pc,m = 1, which
is expected since reduced detection efficiency leads to more
missed detections even at higher signal strengths.

B. Experimental Results
Using the real BLE data with missed detections, we can
further evaluate the performance of the proposed method. By
applying the same optimization framework to the real data,
we can assess the impact of missed detections on the DOA
estimates and the overall system performance.
1) Real BLE Data and Challenges: When working with real
data, it is important to consider environmental factors that
affect the RSSI measurements, such as multipath propagation,
interference from other devices, and obstacles in the signal
path. These factors introduce additional variability into the
measurements, making accurate DOA estimation more chal-
lenging.
One such effect is the occurrence of false negatives, where
a sensor fails to detect a signal that is actually present. This
can arise for various reasons, such as low signal strength,
interference, or hardware limitations. Within the proposed
framework, this is captured by the detection efficiency pc,m,
which models additional losses due to practical effects such as
interference, hardware limitations, or unmodeled propagation
effects. The resulting probability of detection is given by
pD,m(x) = pc,m pα,m(x),
(16)
where pα,m(x) denotes the nominal detection probability due
to thresholding. The resulting optimization problem remains
the same as in (15). Unlike in Section V, where a fixed value
of pc,m was used for all sensors, the detection efficiency in
real data is estimated from observed missed detection rates
and may vary across sensors, channels, and time. In the real
BLE experiments, we observed such variations in false negative
probabilities, with channel 37 exhibiting a higher rate than
the other channels. These estimates of pc,m are then used to
compensate for missed detections in the proposed method.
The measurement noise variance σ2 is also estimated from
the real data by examining the variability of the RSSI mea-
surements when the source is known to be present.
2) DOA Estimation with Particle Filtering: By incorporat-
ing this compensation for false negatives into the proposed
method, we can improve the robustness of the DOA estimates
in the presence of missed detections. The resulting DOA
estimates can be compared to ground truth measurements
obtained from GPS data, allowing us to evaluate the accuracy
and reliability of the proposed method in real-world scenarios.
Furthermore, it was observed that simply taking the maxi-
mum of the likelihood function for DOA estimation could lead
to incorrect peak selection due to the presence of multiple
peaks in the cost function, especially in scenarios with high
missed detection rates. To address this issue, we implemented
a constant velocity (CV) particle filter (PF) that utilizes the
likelihood function as measurements. This approach allows us
to track the correct peak over time, improving the accuracy of
the DOA estimates.
The results of applying the proposed method with false
negative compensation and the PF to the real BLE data are
presented in Fig. 13. The figure shows the DOA estimates from
the PF. The ground truth DOA measurements from the GPS data
are also included for comparison, as well as the likelihood
function used in the estimation process.
It is clearly seen in the likelihood function that there are
multiple peaks present around certain angles, more specifically
symmetric around ψ = 0◦, 90◦, 180◦, −90◦, similar to the
simulation results. This creates challenges for the estimation
process, as the PF needs to choose the correct peak to track
over time. We can see that there is two distinct regions
where the
DOA estimate deviates from the ground truth,
which corresponds to the regions where the likelihood function
exhibits multiple peaks. Firstly around 13:49:40 to 13:49:50,
and secondly around 13:51:10 to 13:51:20. It is challenging to
choose the process noise parameters for the PF to effectively
track the correct peak in these scenarios, as a higher process
noise can lead to rapid switching between peaks, while a
lower process noise may result in the filter getting stuck on
an incorrect peak.
Looking at the estimation error in Fig. 14a, we can see that
the proposed method with the PF manages to track the DOA
quite accurately, even in the presence of missed detections
and false negatives, with RMSE of 22.6◦over the entire
experiment. The baseline method that does not account for
missed detections performs similarly in this case, with RMSE
of 24.7◦, as the missed detection rates are not very high in
this dataset.

VII. CONCLUSIONS
This work demonstrates that missed detections carry valu-
able information that can enhance DOA estimation in systems
with a known measurement model. By incorporating missed
detections into a probabilistic framework, the proposed method
improves robustness under challenging signal conditions and
high missed-detection rates.
Simulation results and real-world experiments using BLE
Yagi antennas confirm improved estimation accuracy. The
approach exploits both detected and missed signals, yielding
more reliable DOA estimates in scenarios with limited detec-
tions and low signal strength.
Despite these improvements, several limitations remain.
The method is sensitive to the detection threshold, which
can affect performance if not properly chosen. Environmen-
tal effects such as multipath propagation and interference
may degrade RSSI quality and reduce accuracy. Incorporating
missed detections also increases computational complexity,
potentially limiting real-time applicability. Performance may
