Universal Design and Physical Applications of Non-Uniform Cellular Automata on
Translationally Invariant Lattices
Xiang-You Huang,∗Jie-Yu Zhang,∗and Peng Ye
†
Guangdong Provincial Key Laboratory of Magnetoelectric Physics and Devices,
State Key Laboratory of Optoelectronic Materials and Technologies,
and School of Physics, Sun Yat-sen University, Guangzhou, 510275, China
(Dated: May 14, 2026)
Lattice geometry profoundly shapes physical phenomena such as subsystem symmetry and di-
rected percolation (DP). Among various lattice geometries, hyperbolic lattices are characterized
by constant negative curvature and non-Abelian translation symmetry, offering a rich platform for
investigating this geometry-physics interplay. However, the exponentially growing lattice size and
nontrivial translation symmetry make approaches developed for Euclidean lattices incompatible, a
limitation particularly evident in uniform cellular automata (CA). To resolve this, we develop a
higher-order non-uniform cellular automata (NUCA) algorithm applicable to both translationally
invariant regular Euclidean and hyperbolic lattices. In the algorithm, the non-uniform update rules
incorporate nontrivial geometric data through a lattice-deforming procedure. We demonstrate the
broad applicability of our algorithm to hyperbolic lattices through several applications on the hyper-
bolic {5, 4} lattice. By applying a linear NUCA, we generate subsystem symmetry-protected topo-
logical (SSPT) states and spontaneous subsystem symmetry-breaking states associated with regular
or irregular subsystem symmetries unattainable on Euclidean lattices. We design the multi-point
strange correlators to detect nontrivial SSPT states and derive a sufficient condition for non-Abelian
translationally invariant NUCA-generated models. Furthermore, by generalizing the NUCA to non-
uniform Clifford quantum cellular automata (CQCA), we generate subsystem symmetries of the
hyperbolic cluster state, extending the established correspondence between translationally invariant
CQCA and subsystem symmetries. Moreover, we simulate the DP process via a probabilistic NUCA
that inherits the treelike structure of the lattice, and numerically estimate percolation thresholds
and the phase diagram. Our NUCA algorithm overcomes the limitations of uniform CA for physical
systems on hyperbolic lattices and can be extended to other non-Euclidean lattice geometries. The
applications pave the way for a systematic study of (i) the design, classification and detection of
phases of matter with subsystem symmetries, and (ii) physics related to directed percolation on
hyperbolic lattices.

I.
INTRODUCTION
Among the factors influencing the properties of physi-
cal systems, lattice geometry plays a crucial role, govern-
ing behaviors from non-interacting fermions to strongly
correlated systems.
The lattice geometry imposes lo-
cal constraints on the system, which can profoundly
shape its macroscopic properties.
A prominent mani-
festation of this interplay is subsystem symmetry.
As
a generalization of global symmetry, subsystem symme-
try is particularly sensitive to lattice geometry through
their action on rigid, non-deformable and subextensive
subsystems that are constrained by the underlying lat-
tice [1, 2]. Research shows a deep connection between
these symmetries and subsystem symmetry-protected
topological (SSPT) order [1–10], fracton topological or-
der [11, 12] and symmetry-enriched topological order [13–
16].
For instance, cluster states protected by subsys-
tem symmetries can serve as universal resource states
for measurement-based quantum computation [5–8] as
experimentally demonstrated in various platforms [17–
19].
Similarly, the influence of lattice geometry is ev-
ident in non-equilibrium statistical physics, such as di-
rected percolation (DP), where the local directed perco-
lating rules dictated by the lattice geometry determine
the global phase transition.
DP is important for the
Janssen-Grassberger conjecture on the DP universality
class [20–23]. DP critical behavior has been experimen-
tally verified in turbulent liquid crystals [24, 25] and var-
ious other turbulent phenomena [26, 27]. Furthermore,
the application of DP extends to modern physical sys-
tems ranging from measurement-induced phase transi-
tions to discrete time crystals [28, 29]. Therefore, investi-
gating these geometry-dependent systems on diverse lat-
tice geometries facilitates a deeper understanding of the
interplay between physical phenomena and the geometry.
Among various lattice geometries, translationally in-
variant lattices are particularly important because they
host well-defined crystal momenta, providing the founda-
tion for physics ranging from band theory to the renor-
malization group. The hyperbolic lattice can be viewed
as a discretization of anti-de Sitter space with constant
negative curvature [30, 31].
Hyperbolic lattices have
nontrivial geometric properties, most notably the non-
Abelian translation symmetry that generalizes the no-
tion of translation invariance beyond the conventional
Abelian picture [32–36]. In recent years, hyperbolic lat-
tices have attracted broad interest in condensed-matter
physics due to the experimental advances in the simula-
tion of quantum systems on them [37–39]. On the theo-
retical side, hyperbolic geometry has been shown to host
phenomena beyond the scope of Euclidean lattice models,
including non-interacting fermions [34–36, 40–43], many-
body physics [44–49], quantum codes [50–52] and holog-
raphy [53–57]. Therefore, the hyperbolic lattice opens a
new avenue for geometry-dependent systems, including
subsystem symmetries of quantum states and directed
percolation. However, the non-Abelian translation sym-
metry and the finite surface-to-volume ratio in the ther-
modynamic limit prevent the direct application of ana-
lytical and numerical approaches developed for Euclidean
lattices [32, 58–60]. To date, known subsystem symme-
tries are restricted to specific spin models [47–49], and
the absence of a comprehensive scheme for their genera-
tion impedes the understanding of quantum states with
subsystem symmetries on hyperbolic lattices. Moreover,
numerical simulations of percolation on the hyperbolic
lattice suffer from the need to label lattice sites and store
lattice data as the system size grows exponentially [58–
60]. As DP is an important model in non-equilibrium
statistical physics, developing a method for efficient sim-
ulation is crucial to understand physics related to DP on
the hyperbolic lattice.
Therefore, it is essential to develop new methods to
investigate physical phenomena inherent in hyperbolic
lattice geometries.
To overcome these geometric con-
straints, one can generalize the framework of cellular au-
tomata (CA), which are discrete dynamical models for
Euclidean systems by generating rich global behaviors
from simple local update rules [61–65].
The local-to-
global structure makes CA and their variants powerful
tools for investigating physics, particularly for studying
the subsystem symmetry of quantum states [3–7, 10, 16,
66] and directed percolation [23, 28, 29]. Furthermore,
quantum cellular automata as the quantum generaliza-
tion of CA provide frameworks for quantum simulation
and universal quantum computation [67–70].
Conven-
tionally, CA are uniformly defined with synchronous evo-
lution under an identical update rule across all sites [62–
64], making them naturally suited for translationally in-
variant Euclidean lattices. However, to model physical

Non-uniform cellular automata (NUCA) are variants of
CA that relax the requirement of uniformity to generate
complex dynamics [71–75] for diverse applications [76–

during the evolution, but lack the mathematical struc-
tures necessary to capture non-Euclidean geometries.
Therefore, they remain incapable of modeling hyperbolic
physics by merely breaking Abelian translation invari-
ance. The construction of NUCA for hyperbolic lattices
is challenging as the underlying geometry imposes con-
straints on the physical locality and the dynamics. Due
to the absence of a distortion-free two-dimensional Eu-
clidean model of the hyperbolic plane [80], the design
of NUCA reflecting hyperbolic geometry presents a chal-
lenge that remains unaddressed.
In this paper, we develop a higher-order non-uniform
cellular automata algorithm capable of modeling physi-
cal systems on both translationally invariant regular Eu-
clidean and hyperbolic lattices as illustrated in Fig. 1.
These regular Euclidean and hyperbolic lattices are de-
noted by the Schläfli symbol {p, q}, where q regular p-
gons meet at each vertex. Figure 2 illustrates the Eu-
clidean {4, 4} lattice and the hyperbolic {5, 4} and {6, 6}
lattices, and we denote NUCA(p, q) for the application
of our algorithm to the {p, q} lattice. The algorithm de-
rives the non-uniform update rules directly from the ge-
ometric data induced by embedding a hyperbolic lattice
into the Euclidean square lattice. We explicitly construct
linear NUCA with deterministic updates and probabilis-
tic NUCA with stochastic dynamics. Both constructions
are broadly applicable to diverse physical systems de-
fined on hyperbolic lattices. Here, we demonstrate that
the NUCA algorithm is a powerful tool for investigating
quantum states with subsystem symmetries [1, 2], design-
ing Clifford quantum cellular automata for the hyperbolic
cluster state, and simulating directed percolation [22, 23].
Through these successful applications, we explicitly show
the capability of the NUCA algorithm to uncover a wide
variety of physical phenomena inherent in the hyperbolic
lattice as demonstrated in Fig. 1. Furthermore, our al-
gorithm can in principle be generalized to other complex
geometries, such as self-similar fractal lattices [81–84].
Concretely, this work is composed of the following parts.
First, we formalize the NUCA algorithm that incor-
porates nontrivial geometric data into non-uniform up-
date rules Eq. (6) and Eq. (10). Through evolution un-
der these update rules, the discrete dynamics of a one-
dimensional NUCA(p, q) are mapped to spatial config-
urations on the two-dimensional {p, q} lattice.

these translationally invariant CQCA are known to cor-

correspondence to hyperbolic geometries, we first for-

the cluster model on the {5, 4} lattice, and then deter-

translationally invariant CQCA and subsystem symme-
tries on Euclidean lattices to the hyperbolic case.
Furthermore, we apply our NUCA to non-equilibrium
statistical physics, specifically in the study of directed
percolation.
On the Euclidean square lattice, the DP
process can be simulated by the uniform probabilistic
CA [22, 23]. To generalize these methods to simulate the
DP process on the hyperbolic lattice, we design a prob-
abilistic NUCA(5, 4) for the {5, 4} lattice whose update
rules Eq. (73) explicitly capture the treelike structure
of the hyperbolic lattice. By simulating the dynamical
evolution of the NUCA and conducting scaling analysis,
we numerically estimate several DP thresholds including
those for bond DP and site DP, and obtain the phase
diagram in Fig. 17 through interpolation.
Our proba-
bilistic NUCA overcomes the difficulties in labelling sites
and storing lattice data in the numerical study of per-
colation on hyperbolic lattices [58].
Consequently, our
probabilistic NUCA may be applied to explore physical
phenomena associated with DP.

II.
DESIGN OF NON-UNIFORM CELLULAR
AUTOMATA ALGORITHM
To begin with, we introduce the general construction
of our higher-order NUCA algorithm.
Conventionally,
CA are defined by uniformity, where the states of all
sites evolve according to an identical and local rule syn-
chronously [61–63]. Non-uniform cellular automata as-
sume spatially or temporally varying rules for sites to
update [71–75], thereby relaxing the constraint of trans-
lation invariance. With these properties, we expect that
the NUCA can model physics on exotic lattice geome-
tries, e.g., a hyperbolic lattice without Abelian transla-
tion symmetry.
In this work, we focus on one-dimensional higher-order
NUCA. Here, one-dimensional means that the NUCA
configuration at each time step is supported on a line,
while higher-order means that the NUCA have memory
of multiple preceding time steps [62, 63].
Our NUCA
algorithm provides a unified framework for constructing
both deterministic and probabilistic NUCA. For deter-
ministic and linear NUCA, the update rules uniquely
determine the states of sites from preceding configura-
tions. For probabilistic NUCA, the states are updated
from probability distributions specified by preceding con-
figurations. All notation used for NUCA throughout this
paper is summarized in Table I for convenience.

Conventional CA, based on synchronous evolution and

As the lattice-deforming proce-
dure for hyperbolic lattices induces distortion, the trans-
lation symmetry is not manifest on the deformed lat-
tice. Therefore, a NUCA-generated Hamiltonian may or
may not be translationally invariant and we construct
a sufficient condition to ensure this symmetry.
As re-
viewed in Appendix A, translations in hyperbolic space
are non-Abelian, leading to nontrivial periodic boundary
conditions (PBC) of the hyperbolic lattice [32–36].
A
PBC lattice is constructed by the factor group Γ/ΓPBC,
where ΓPBC is a normal subgroup of the non-Abelian
translation group Γ.
The specific lattice topology de-
pends on ΓPBC. We denote the translation operator for
[ga] ∈Γ/ΓPBC as T[ga], and the translation symmetry of
the Hamiltonian is defined as T[ga]H T −1
[ga] = H , ∀[ga] ∈
Γ/ΓPBC.
We first consider the general SSPT Hamiltonian
Eq. (37), and recall that it can be transformed to a uni-
form form by applying Hadamard gates on one sublat-
tice. In the Hamiltonian Eq. (37), we identify the center
of a term hij defined by ¯fj as site xi−u(j)ym(j) which is
the unique nontrivial support on the complementary sub-
lattice. Under a translation [ga] mapping this center to
xi′−u(j′)ym(j′), the Hamiltonian term hi′j′ does not nec-
essarily coincide with T[ga]hijT −1
[ga] as it is defined by the
update rule ¯fj′ at site xi′yj′. A sufficient condition for
hi′j′ = T[ga]hijT −1
[ga] is that the action of the Hamiltonian
term must be isotropic with respect to the geodesic dis-
tance from its center. Specifically, for qubits with the
same geodesic distance from the center, the term has
either nontrivial or trivial action on all of them simul-
taneously. This condition can be incorporated into the
design of update rules, and is not satisfied by a uniform
¯f. The SSPT models we designed satisfy this sufficient
condition and thereby are translationally invariant.
The analysis for SSSB models Eq. (41) is more com-
plex as it does not rely on a sublattice structure. In this
case, the center of a Hamiltonian term can be a poly-
gon or a vertex. For a Hamiltonian term centered on a
polygon such as Eq. (59), the condition is similar to the
SSPT case. For qubits with the same geodesic distance
from the center, the term has either nontrivial or trivial
action on all of them simultaneously. For a Hamiltonian
term centered on a vertex such as Eq. (56), a similar con-
dition holds. For qubits with the same geodesic distance
from the central vertex, the term has either nontrivial or
trivial action on all of them simultaneously. These con-
ditions are both incorporated into the design of update
rules.
A NUCA-generated Hamiltonian is translation-
ally invariant on the hyperbolic lattice if it satisfies this
sufficient condition. In Appendix C, we provide a more
comprehensive discussion of translational invariance on
the hyperbolic lattice.
IV.
DIVERSITY OF NUCA-GENERATED
SUBSYSTEM SYMMETRIES
Subsystem symmetries are intimately related to the
physical properties of quantum systems.
The geomet-
ric constraints of subsystem symmetries make the de-
termination and classification of them challenging even
for the Euclidean lattice [3–7, 10].
To date, only a

sical spin models on hyperbolic lattices have been dis-
covered in the literature [47–49], without a systematic
algorithm for designing and classifying them. In the pre-

by NUCA(5, 4), showing that the hyperbolic geometry
leads to new types of subsystem symmetries. In this sec-
tion, we take NUCA(5, 4)-generated symmetries as ex-
amples to classify subsystem symmetries on the hyper-
bolic lattice into regular and irregular symmetries. This
classification goes beyond the classification on Euclidean
lattices.

VI.
PROBABILISTIC NUCA(5, 4) FOR
SIMULATING DIRECTED PERCOLATION ON
THE HYPERBOLIC LATTICE
Directed percolation (DP) is a fundamental model in
non-equilibrium statistical physics particularly for the
conjectured universality of DP [20–22], whose critical be-
havior has been experimentally verified [24–27]. In a DP
process, sites are either occupied or empty, and occu-
pation spreads only along a specific direction through a
stochastic process.
On Euclidean lattices, DP can be
studied through probabilistic cellular automata [23, 64,
65] which are variants of CA with stochastic update rules
as introduced in Sec. II. Moreover, physics related to DP
can also be simulated by using probabilistic CA, e.g., the
study of measurement-induced phase transitions and the
design of discrete time crystals [28, 29]. However, the DP
process on the hyperbolic lattice cannot be simulated by
the dynamics of uniform probabilistic CA as they can-
not preserve the neighboring relation. Additionally, the
numerical studies of undirected percolation on the hy-
perbolic lattice suffer from the locating problem and the
need to store the whole lattice [58, 59], which are also
challenges for directed percolation. In this section, we de-
velop a probabilistic NUCA(5, 4) which overcomes these
challenges to numerically simulate directed percolation
on the {5, 4} lattice, showing the power of our NUCA
algorithm to model physics on the hyperbolic lattice.

SUMMARY AND OUTLOOK

tomata algorithm capable of investigating physical sys-
tems across both translationally invariant Euclidean and
hyperbolic lattices as summarized in Fig. 1. By embed-

tortion of hyperbolic geometry.

is capable of investigating physical systems on hyper-
bolic lattices, generalizing the conventional uniform CA
for Euclidean lattices. By using linear NUCA(5, 4), we
systematically design SSPT and SSSB models associated

acterize these subsystem symmetries into regular [e.g.,
Eq. (59)] and irregular [e.g., Eq. (44)] symmetries in
Sec. IV, reflecting the exponential expansion of the hy-
perbolic lattice.
We design nonlocal MPSC to detect
a nontrivial NUCA-generated SSPT state, and derive
a sufficient condition for NUCA-generated Hamiltonians
to be invariant under non-Abelian translation symmetry.
We also generalize our classical NUCA algorithm to the
non-uniform CQCA. Based on the update rule Eq. (44)

dimensional lattices, where two-dimensional CA can be
applied. Another direction is to apply the NUCA frame-
work to other quantum states with NUCA-generated sub-

strated in the Hamiltonian terms visualized in the corre-
sponding figures, and can be checked by the neighboring
relation of polygons summarized in Table III as the lat-
tice is regular.
Additionally, uniformity implies translation symmetry
on the Euclidean lattice. We discuss the application of
NUCA(4, 4) on the Euclidean square lattice in Sec. II
and Appendix B with uniform update rules. On the Eu-
clidean plane E2 the discrete normal translation subgroup
corresponding to Iso(E2) = R2 ⋊O(2) is given by Z × Z,
with normal subgroup NxZ × NyZ. Both of these groups
are Abelian, so the factor group is also Abelian and iso-
morphic to ZNx ×ZNy. This defines an Nx ×Ny-unit-cell
PBC lattice with 2-torus topology. Therefore, a sufficient
condition for a translation-invariant Hamiltonian is that
the term defined by the update rule Eq. (B4) is invariant
under translations in ZNx × ZNy. Therefore, the term
need not be isotropic.
This is intrinsically guaranteed
by a uniform update rule ¯f(x). Thus the condition for
translation invariance in the Euclidean case is trivial.
