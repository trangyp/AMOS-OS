ABSTRACT
We present a study of intergalactic medium (IGM) transmission at 4.5 < z < 6.5 using high-signal-to-
noise JWST/NIRSpec prism spectroscopy of 143 galaxies at 5 < z < 7 from the CAPERS and JADES
surveys. By comparing the observed flux blueward of Lyα emission line to the prediction of spectral
energy distribution modeling, we directly measure the IGM transmission along the individual galaxy
sightlines. The average transmission measured from these galaxy sightlines is consistent with previous
measurements based on luminous quasars. Current NIRSpec spectroscopy is sufficiently deep to probe
IGM transmission on single sightlines. We find evidence for a highly ionized structure, GS-z6IS, at
z ∼5.75 −6 in the GOODS-S field based on the analysis of a high-S/N spectrum of one galaxy,
GS-18846, at z = 6.335. The IGM transmission of GS-z6IS is 0.17 ± 0.02, an order of magnitude
higher than the average of previous measurements at this redshift. This structure has a line-of-sight
scale of ∼110 cMpc and spatially extends over at least 21 × 17 cMpc2. GS-z6IS is associated with
a known large-scale galaxy overdensity at the same redshift, whose member galaxies show enhanced
Lyα visibility and a broader Lyα equivalent width distribution compared to field galaxies at similar
redshift. This result supports the interpretation that Lyα overdensity can trace bubbles of increased
IGM transmission, although environmental effects on galaxy properties may also contribute. Our study

1. INTRODUCTION
The reionization of neutral hydrogen in the intergalac-
tic medium (IGM) is a milestone in the history of the
Universe. During this epoch, the ionizing photons gen-
erated from the first stars, galaxies, and active galactic
nuclei (AGNs) start impacting the surrounding IGM,
transitioning the IGM from a neutral to a mostly ion-
ized state (e.g., R. Barkana & A. Loeb 2001; X. Fan
et al. 2006a). There has been much progress in narrow-
ing down the timeline of cosmic reionization: measure-
ment of the Thompson optical depth by the polariza-
tion of cosmic microwave background (CMB) constrains
that the cosmic reionization occurs before z ∼7.7 ± 0.7
( Planck Collaboration et al. 2020); observations of
quasars show that the cosmic reionization ends at z ∼5
– 6 (e.g., X. Fan et al. 2006b; G. D. Becker et al. 2015;
A.-C. Eilers et al. 2018; J. Yang et al. 2020; Y. Zhu
et al. 2021); statistic studies of Lyα emitting galaxies
(LAEs) and Lyα break galaxies (LBGs) also show that
the neutral hydrogen fraction in IGM decreases rapidly
from z > 8 to 5 (e.g., S. Malhotra & J. E. Rhoads 2004;
V. Tilvi et al. 2014; M. Ouchi et al. 2010; Z.-Y. Zheng
et al. 2017; C. A. Mason et al. 2018; W. Hu et al. 2019;
A. M. Morales et al. 2021; I. G. B. Wold et al. 2022; P.
Bolan et al. 2022; Y. Ning et al. 2022; M. Tang et al.
2024a; L. Napolitano et al. 2024).
One of the most direct and model-independent meth-
ods to constrain cosmic reionization is by measuring the
IGM transmission from the transmitted flux in the re-
gion between rest-frame wavelength the Lyα and Lyβ
transitions (rest-frame λ0 = 1027 – 1216 ˚A; we refer
to this region as the “Lyα transmitted spectrum” here-
after). Neutral hydrogen in the IGM resonantly scat-
ters these redshifted photons, strongly suppressing the
transmitted intensity and even resulting in complete ab-
sorption (i.e., the Gunn-Peterson trough; J. E. Gunn &
B. A. Peterson 1965) when the neutral hydrogen frac-
tion is < 0.01%. Thanks to the extreme luminosities
of high-redshift quasars, this method has been effec-
tively applied to the quasar spectra over the last three
decades, providing precise measurements of IGM trans-
mission at 5 ≲z ≲6 (X. Fan et al. 2006a; G. D.
Becker et al. 2015; A.-C. Eilers et al. 2018; J. Yang et al.
2020; S. E. I. Bosman et al. 2022). The differences in
IGM transmission between the quasar sightlines at a
fixed redshift also reveal that reionization does not pro-
ceed isotropically, but rather follows a patchy topology
(G. D. Becker et al. 2015). However, two problems oc-
cur when using quasars. First, the sparse distribution of
quasars across the sky limits our ability to sample the
three-dimensional structure of the nearby IGM. Second,
quasars are bright themselves, prone to ionizing large
regions around them (this is the well-known “proximity
effect”; C.-A. Faucher-Gigu`ere et al. 2008).
Galaxies, though much fainter, are far more numerous
and densely distributed, allowing us to map the local
patchiness of the IGM and directly trace how reioniza-
tion proceeds across different environments. R. Thomas
et al. (2017) conducted the first such test using galaxy
spectra from the VLT/VIMOS Ultra Deep Survey (O.
Le F`evre et al. 2015) and measured the IGM transmis-
sion up to z ∼5 (see also R. Thomas et al. 2020, 2021).
More recently, K. Kakiichi et al. (2023) utilized ultra-
deep narrowband imaging to probe Lyα transmission at
z ∼4.9 using background galaxies and cross-correlated
it with the distribution of LAEs at the same redshift.
However, the strong atmospheric OH emission in the
near-infrared and the rapidly increasing IGM attenua-
tion at z > 5 make it extremely challenging to push IGM
transmission studies to higher redshifts. The advent of
JWST, with its high sensitivity and freedom from atmo-
spheric emission, now enables IGM transmission studies
to reach into the reionization era. R. A. Meyer et al.
(2025) demonstrated this capability by combining NIR-
Spec/prism spectra of galaxies at 5 < z < 11, finding
IGM transmission measurements consistent with those
derived from quasar sightlines.
In this work, we present a study of IGM transmis-
sion using JWST/NIRSpec prism spectra of UV-bright
galaxies from the CAPERS and JADES surveys and ex-
plore high signal-to-noise (S/N) galaxy spectra to probe
ionized structures. In Section 2, we describe the data
used in this work.
Section 3 outlines our method for
measuring IGM transmission and presents the results.
We identify three high-transmission regions at z ≳5.3
using the individual high S/N galaxy spectra, including
a region at z ∼5.9 previously reported in R. A. Meyer
et al. (2025). In Section 4, we confirm this region to
be a large ionized structure at z ∼5.9 in the GS field
associated with a galaxy overdensity, and then explore
Lyα emission of galaxies within this structure. Finally,
Section 5 summarizes our results.

2. DATA AND SAMPLE
The objects investigated in this work are drawn from
the JWST CANDELS-Area Prism Epoch of Reioniza-
tion Survey (CAPERS) and JWST Advanced Deep Ex-
tragalactic Survey (JADES). In Section 2.1 and 2.2, we
briefly introduce the observation and data reduction for
CAPERS and JADES, respectively. In Section 2.3, we
describe the selection of the galaxy sample.
2.1. CAPERS
CAPERS is targeting seven NIRSpec pointings in each
of the EGS, COSMOS, and UDS fields (21 fields in to-
tal) using the PRISM/CLEAR spectroscopic element
which provides low resolution, R ∼100, wavelength
coverage from 0.6 to 5.3 µm. For each pointing, CA-
PERS used three NIRSpec MSA configurations with the
lower-priority objects being replaced in each observa-
tion. Therefore, this setup enables high-priority objects
to be observed multiple times to achieve a high S/N,
while observing a large sample of lower-priority objects
to maximize scientific yield. Each MSA configuration
is carried out using a three-shutter slitlet with nodding
between the three shutters. The effective exposure time
of each MSA configuration is 5,690 s. Targets may be
observed on one to three MSA configurations, depend-
ing on their priority, such that total effective exposure
time per target ranges from 5,609 s to 17,079 s. At the
time of this study, CAPERS had completed the obser-
vations in the EGS and COSMOS fields, as well as 38%
of the planned observations for the UDS. Therefore, this
work focuses on CAPERS observations from the avail-
able data in these fields.
The NIRSpec data were reduced using the JWST Cal-
ibration Pipeline v1.17.1 (H. Bushouse et al. 2025) with
several customized steps similar to P. Arrabal Haro et al.
(2023). A detailed description of data reduction will be
presented in a dedicated paper. The 1D spectra are then
extracted using an optimal extraction (K. Horne 1986).
The galaxies’ redshifts are measured using a variety of
software, including a modified version of Marz, a mod-
ified version of MSAEXP, Bagpipes, Cigale, etc. (S. R.
Hinton et al. 2016; G. Brammer 2023; A. C. Carnall
et al. 2018; M. Boquien et al. 2019). The results are
then combined and vetted by the CAPERS team.
The photometric catalogs in the three fields are ob-
tained from the UNICORN project (S. Finkelstein et
al., in prep), which provides robust photometry mea-
surements (including accurate colors, total fluxes, and
noise estimates) in a similar method as used in S. L.
Finkelstein et al. (2024). The IDs of CAPERS galaxies
are from the UNICORN catalog.
2.2. JADES
JADES (D. J. Eisenstein et al. 2023a) is a joint pro-
gram between the JWST NIRCam and NIRSpec GTO
teams, targeting the Great Observatories Origins Deep
Survey (GOODS; M. Giavalisco et al. 2004) South and
North fields (GS and GN, respectively). The program
includes multi-wavelength imaging with NIRCam and
MIRI, along with low- and medium-resolution spec-
troscopy with NIRSpec. The MSA configurations are
also designed to be a three-shutter slitlet with nodding
between the three shutters. In this work, we use the low-
resolution spectroscopy obtained with the NIRSpec dis-
perser/filter configuration of PRISM/CLEAR (R ∼100)
from the JADES DR3. The exposure times of JADES
spectra span a wide range, from ∼0.9 hrs to as much
as ∼62 hrs. The JADES NIRSpec data were retrieved
from the MAST HLSP archive22. We refer the readers
to A. J. Bunker et al. (2024) for details.
We utilize the published JWST images from the
JADES DR3 (D. J. Eisenstein et al. 2023a,b; F.
D’Eugenio et al. 2024).
JADES NIRCam observa-
tion provides 14 broad- or medium-bands (broad- and
medium-band imaging from F090W, F115W, F150W,
F182M, etc. filters from JADES, K. N. Hainline et al.
2024, combined with medium-band imaging in F430M,
F460M, and F480M from JEMS, C. C. Williams et al.
2023) covering wavelengths from 0.8 to 5.0 µm.
The
JADES photometric catalog provides matched-aperture
flux densities for the galaxies, and photometric red-
shifts measured from the SEDs (M. J. Rieke et al. 2023;
D. J. Eisenstein et al. 2023b; K. N. Hainline et al.
2024). In addition to the JWST images, we also uti-
lize the archival Hubble Space Telescope (HST) images
to complement the JWST images in the UV-to-optical
wavelength range. The archival HST images were ob-
tained from several HST observation programs, includ-
ing CANDELS, HUDF, UVCANDELS, etc (N. A. Gro-
gin et al. 2011; A. M. Koekemoer et al. 2011; X. Wang
et al. 2025; R. J. Bouwens et al. 2011).
The IDs of
JADES galaxies are from the JADES NIRSpec IDs.

3. IGM TRANSMISSION
To measure the IGM transmission, we need to nor-
malize the observed spectrum by an intrinsic, IGM-
unattenuated spectrum appropriate for each galaxy. To
achieve this, for each galaxy we perform a joint fitting
to the galaxy’s spectral energy distribution (SED) us-
ing all available photometry and the NIRSpec/PRISM
spectrum to obtain a model spectrum. We describe our
method in Section 3.1 and then describe the IGM trans-
mission measurements in Sections 3.2 and 3.3.
3.1. SED fitting
We use the Python package BAGPIPES (A. C. Carnall
et al. 2018) to jointly fit the photometry and spectra of
the objects. We employ Binary Population and Spectral
Synthesis v2.2.1 (BPASS; J. J. Eldridge et al. 2017; E. R.
Stanway & J. J. Eldridge 2018) with a broken power-law
initial mass function with slopes of α1 = −1.3 for stars
with 0.1 – 0.5 M⊙and α2 = −2.35 for 0.5 – 100 M⊙
(model ‘135 100’). For the star formation history pa-
rameterization, we use the Gaussian Process model from
dense basis (K. G. Iyer et al. 2019), where the star for-
mation history is split into 4 dynamically adjusted time
bins, and during each time bin, 25% of the total stellar
mass is formed. The stellar metallicity is allowed to vary
between 0.001 and 1 Z⊙. We include nebular emission
with the metallicity of the gas equal to that of the stellar
populations, and an ionization parameter, log U, in the
range −4 to −1.
The slope of the UV continuum and its extrapolation
into the wavelength range blueward of Lyα are sensitive
to the choice of dust attenuation law. The Calzetti dust
law, derived from local star-forming galaxies (D. Calzetti
et al. 2000), has been frequently applied in high-redshift
studies. However, recent analysis (e.g., V. Markov et al.
2025) have revealed that the dust law of high-redshift
galaxies may deviate from this empirical dust law, po-
tentially introducing systematic biases in the estimation
of IGM transmission. To account for this uncertainty, we
adopt a flexible modification of the Calzetti dust law, as
formulated by S. Salim et al. (2018). This parametriza-
tion has two free parameters: δ, quantifying the devia-
tion from the slope of the Calzetti law, and B, represent-
ing the strength of the 2175 ˚A absorption feature. When
δ = 0, the Salim dust law is equivalent to the Calzetti
dust law. In this study, we fix B = 0, as the wavelength
range of interest is minimally affected by this feature.
We allow AV and δ to vary in the range of 0 – 8 and
-1.6 – 0.4, respectively.
To account for the slitlosses and calibration uncertain-
ties of NIRSpec spectroscopy, we adopt a multiplicative
scaling factor (assumed to be a second-order Chebyshev
polynomial) in BAGPIPES to estimate the wavelength-
dependent flux calibration to the broadband photome-
try. We allow the zeroth order to vary from 0.1 to 10,
while the first and second orders are allowed to vary from
−0.5 to 0.5. To account for the variable resolution of
the NIRSpec spectrum, we utilize the resolution model

BAGPIPES adopts the IGM attenuation model of A. K.
Inoue et al. (2014) as default to account for the IGM at-
tenuation. A. K. Inoue et al. (2014) derived the distribu-
tion functions of intergalactic absorbers from the obser-
vational statistics of Lyman Limit systems, Lyα Forest
systems, and Damped Lyα systems, and then integrated
the distribution function to derive the mean transmis-
sion function of IGM. However, as shown in the quasar
studies, the IGM transmission contains a large scatter,
and the scatter increases with increasing redshift (for
example, the effect optical depth ranges from ∼3 to 6
at 5.7 < z < 5.9 in J. Yang et al. 2020; see also G. D.
Becker et al. 2015; A.-C. Eilers et al. 2018). Including
the IGM attenuation model in the SED fitting could
bias our estimation of IGM transmission. To avoid this,
in the SED fitting, we mask the wavelength range blue-
ward of the Lyα break. Additionally, since the Lyα line
cannot be reliably modeled with the SED-fitting codes
and the Lyα break could be affected by strong Damped
Lyα absorption (W. Hu et al. 2023; K. E. Heintz et al.
2024, 2025), we mask both the Lyα emission line and
the Lyα break region (rest-frame < 1270 ˚A).

3.2. IGM Transmission Measurements

observed Lyα transmitted spectrum to the predicted
IGM-unattenuated spectra, T = fobs/fpred.

To improve the S/N, we combine the Lyα transmission
from all sightlines. We compute the average transmis-

∆z = 0.25. Within each redshift bin, we adopt inverse-

the associated uncertainties.

Because we are probing very weak signals that are
typically undetectable in the spectrum of an individ-
ual galaxy, even small systematic effects, such as back-
ground residuals, could bias our measurements.
It is
therefore important to verify that our uncertainties are
not affected by systematic offsets. To test this, we exam-
ine the distribution of noise-normalized fluxes in spec-
tral regions where no intrinsic galaxy flux is expected.
Specifically, we select galaxies at z > 7.5 and extract
spectral pixels in the wavelength range 7000 – 7500 ˚A,
corresponding to rest-frame wavelengths beyond the Ly-

4. A HIGH TRANSMISSION REGION AT
5.75 < z < 6 IN GS
A high transmission region with a size of tens of cMpc
can enable Lyα photons being shifted out of resonance
before they encounter neutral IGM, and therefore, can
substantially reduce the IGM attenuation and enhance
the Lyα visibility of galaxies within the ionized region
(M. Dijkstra 2014; I. Jung et al. 2022; R. L. Larson et al.
2022; M. Yue et al. 2025). This has led to the use of
overdensity of Lyα-emitting galaxies as tracers of ion-
ized bubbles and reionization (V. Tilvi et al. 2020; W.
Hu et al. 2021; R. Endsley et al. 2021; I. Jung et al. 2022;
L. Whitler et al. 2025; Z. Chen et al. 2025). Here, we
show that the high IGM transmission at 5.75 < z < 6
in the sightline of GS-18446 and the average of other
GS galaxies corresponds to a galaxy overdensity at this
redshift. In Section 4.1, we first explore the origin of
the high-transmission region in the sightline of GS-18846
and its connection with the underlying galaxies. In Sec-
tions 4.2 and 4.3, we examine the Lyα properties of the
galaxies in the high transmission region. Our conclusion
is that this overdensity of Lyα-emitters in the GS field

4.1. An ionized bubble GS-z6IS associated with an
overdensity
The low neutral hydrogen density like that observed in
GS-18846 at 5.75 < z < 6 could be driven by an intrin-
sically underdense region–a large void of neutral IGM.
However, this case does not seem sufficient to produce
the low optical depth observed for this galaxy. Using the
CROC cosmological hydrodynamic simulations, H. Zhu
et al. (2024) investigated the origin of Lyα transmis-
sion spikes in quasar spectra and found that such spikes
can be explained by the underdense regions. To explore
this possibility, we compare the optical depth along the
sightline of GS-18846 with the CDF of optical depths
at 5.75 < z < 6 from the CROC simulation in Figure
6. The CROC CDF is calculated based on integrating
the Lyα transmission along 45 simulated sightlines over
this redshift range. The CROC CDF agrees well with
quasar-based measurements, but yields optical depths
that are much larger than the optical depth measured
in the spectrum of GS-18846. This discrepancy suggests
that a low IGM density alone cannot explain the unusu-
ally high transmission along this sightline.
Rather, the high Lyα transmission is likely due to an
increase in the number of ionizing photons impacting
the IGM at 5.75 < z < 6 in the GS field, leading to
a higher ionized hydrogen fraction. This suggests the
presence of an overdensity of ionizing sources at z ∼5.9
in this field, producing a “bubble”. Hereafter, we name
this ionized structure at z ∼6 in the GS field: GS-z6IS.
Producing such a large ionized structure when the
IGM was still highly neutral requires a large-scale over-
density of star-forming galaxies and/or AGNs to gener-
ate substantial ionizing photons, such as expected from
galaxy overdensities (e.g., J. Witstok et al. 2024; C. L.
Martin et al. 2025). To identify such a structure, we first
use the JADES-GS spectroscopic catalog.
The trans-
verse scale of the NIRSpec field of JADES in GS is ∼21
cMpc × 17 cMpc, much smaller than the line-of-sight
extent of the apparent ionized structure (109 cMpc).
Given the uncertain location of the center of the galaxy
overdensity, we select all galaxies with secure redshifts
(flag < D) in this field with no restriction on sky loca-
tion. The top right panel of Figure 7 shows the redshift
distribution of the galaxies between z = 5 and 6.75 in
the JADES-GS field, binned in ∆z = 0.25. A clear spike
can be seen at the redshift bin 5.75 – 6 with 64 galaxies,
exceeding the other redshift bins with 10 – 27 galaxies.
This implies a galaxy overdensity at 5.75 – 6 with an
overdensity factor of δ ∼4, after correcting for volume
differences.
This suggests that GS-z6IS is associated
with an overdensity at z ∼5.9.
We note that JADES spectroscopy observation was
optimized to observe the most distant galaxies (A. J.
Bunker et al. 2024; F. D’Eugenio et al. 2024). The com-
plex selection functions could bias the redshift distribu-
tion of targets. Particularly, the galaxies at 5.7 < z < 8
were assigned slightly higher priority than the galaxies
4 < z < 5.7. However, we do not expect the observed
spike arising from these selection effects, given the high
significance of the excess compared to that from the
higher and lower redshift galaxies.
Furthermore, the
JADES-GN spectroscopy observation was designed sim-
ilarly, and it shows no such comparable redshift over-
density at 5.75 < z < 6.
We also explore for any evidence of a galaxy overden-
sity at this redshift in the GS field using archival data
from the VLT/MUSE Hubble Ultra Deep field (HUDF)
survey (R. Bacon et al. 2017). The MUSE HUDF sur-
vey is an integral field spectroscopy survey of the HUDF
field, covering the wavelength range from 4650 – 9300
˚A. The high-redshift galaxies in the MUSE HUDF sur-
vey are primarily identified through their Lyα emission
lines, i.e., LAEs. In the bottom right panel of Figure
7, we present the redshift distribution of MUSE HUDF
LAEs. The number of LAEs gradually decreases with
the redshift due to the decreasing instrument sensitivity
for longer wavelengths and increasing IGM attenuation
for higher redshifts.
Despite this, a peak remains at
5.75 < z < 6, with twice the number of LAEs compared
to the declining trend, supporting the presence of an
overdensity.
Indeed, several independent studies over the past two

et al. (2004) confirmed 3 LAEs with Gemini/GMOS-

possible overdensity at z ∼5.8 in the GS field. Based
on the ground-based, wide-field (31′ × 33′) narrowband

identified an overdensity at z ∼5.7 – 5.8 with 17 LAEs.
In addition, S. Malhotra et al. (2005) analyzed the
HST/ACS grism spectroscopic observation of the GS
field and identified an overdensity at z = 5.9 ± 0.2 con-

factor of 4. More recently, J. M. Helton et al. (2024) re-

4.2. GS-9422: A Galaxy with Most of Its Escaped Lyα
Photons Observed?
Among the member galaxies of GS-z6IS, we first ex-
amine an extreme Lyα emitting galaxy at z = 5.936,
GS-9422, which shows strong UV and optical high-
ionization lines and a remarkable rest-frame UV con-
tinuum turnover at 1400 ˚A. The 1400 ˚A turnover could
be explained as caused by the partial-covering damped
Lyα absorber system (DLA) or the two-photon emission
from transition 2s →1s in hydrogen (A. J. Cameron
et al. 2024; S. Tacchella et al. 2025). In this section, we
argue that the 1400 ˚A turnover is caused by a partial-
covering damped Lyα absorber based on its Lyα trans-
mitted spectrum, and we argue that the escaped Lyα
photons of GS-9422 experience very little IGM attenu-
ation.
The high-ionization UV lines of GS-9422, such as
C IV, He II+O III], and C III], suggest a highly ionized
ISM, which is not readily modeled by the standard SED
fitting packages.
However, the extra-galactic objects,
such as AGNs or star-forming galaxies, usually exhibit
a power-law UV continuum (e.g., G. R. Meurer et al.
1999; J. S. Dunlop 2013; Z.-Y. Cai & J.-X. Wang 2023;
S. Tacchella et al. 2025).
Therefore, we alternatively
adopt a power law (fλ ∝λβ) to model the UV contin-
uum. We mask the emission lines, including Lyα, C IV,
He II+O III], and C III], to avoid their contribution to
the continuum fitting. We model the 1400 ˚A turnover
using a modified Voigt profile, which is defined by a
Doppler parameter, a neutral hydrogen column density
NHI, a velocity offset of the DLA system, and a cover-
ing factor fc, similar to W. Hu et al. (2023). Because
the DLA absorption of GS-9422 is very wide and the
spectral resolution is considerably low (R ∼40), we fix
the velocity offset to be 0 and adopt a typical Doppler
parameter 30 km s−1 (J. X. Prochaska et al. 2015). We
also consider the IGM attenuation based on the A. K.
Inoue et al. (2014) model. Furthermore, we utilize the
Python package SpectRes (A. C. Carnall 2017) to ac-
count for the resolution model for the prism spectra.

Taken together, our analysis shows that the LAEs
in GS-z6IS exhibit enhanced Lyα EWs relative to the
field galaxies at similar redshift, but are comparable to
galaxies observed at later epochs when the IGM was
fully ionized.
This provides additional evidence that
galaxies in GS-z6IS experience reduced IGM attenua-
tion, consistent with the presence of a highly ionized
bubble. Nevertheless, as GS-z6IS is associated with a
galaxy overdensity and Lyα escape is known to corre-
late with galaxy properties such as star formation rate
and outflow kinematics (J. Matthee et al. 2016; H. Yang
et al. 2017), environmental effects on galaxy properties
may also contribute to the enhanced Lyα EWs.
5. SUMMARY
In this work, we present direct measurements of
IGM transmission at 4.5 < z < 6.5 using high-S/N
JWST/NIRSpec prism spectroscopy of 143 galaxies at
5 < z < 7 from the CAPERS and JADES surveys, and
compare our measurements with previous works.
We
also explore the high transmission regions at z ≳5.5
revealed by the individual high-S/N spectra. The main
results are summarized as follows:
• By comparing the observed Lyα transmitted spec-
trum to the prediction of SED modeling, we mea-
sure the IGM transmission and effective optical
depth along individual galaxy sightlines. We use
the inverse variance weighting method to average
the transmission measurements and find that our
measurements at 4.50 < z < 5.75 are consistent
with those reported in previous studies. We find
that the effective optical depth at 5.75 < z < 6.00
is significantly lower than previous studies, which
is driven by an ionized structure at z ∼5.75 –
6.00. At z > 6.00, the current data is not deep
enough to provide robust constraints.
• We identify an ionized structure GS-z6IS at z ∼
5.75 – 6.00 in the GS field, independently con-
firming the earlier findings in R. A. Meyer et al.
(2025).
We detect GS-z6IS from the high-S/N
spectrum of GS-18846 at z = 6.335. This struc-
ture has a scale of 109 cMpc and an IGM trans-
mission of 0.17 ± 0.02, an order of magnitude
higher than the empirical relation at this redshift.
The average IGM transmission of other galaxies
at 6.3 < z < 7 in GS (excluding GS-18846) shows
a tentative transmission excess at z ∼5.75 – 6,
indicating GS-z6IS spatially extends over at least
21 × 17 cMpc2. Our direct measurement of the
scale of GS-z6IS can serve as a useful comparison
for reionization simulations, such as T.-Y. Lu et al.
(2024); M. Neyer et al. (2025).
• GS-z6IS is associated with a known overdensity of
LAEs and LBGs discovered two decades ago (E. R.
Stanway et al. 2004; J. X. Wang et al. 2005; S.
Malhotra et al. 2005).
This, for the first time,
provides direct evidence that the overdensity of
LAEs can trace the highly ionized structures in
the epoch of reionization.

Interestingly, the IGM transmission within GS-z6IS,
as probed by GS-18846, is still below unity. This sug-
gests that residual neutral hydrogen remains within this
ionized structure. One possible explanation is that the
sightline of GS-18846 passes through dense, self-shielded
neutral hydrogen clouds, such as filaments in the cosmic
web (J. Chardin et al. 2018; E. Garaldi et al. 2022).
Alternatively, the structure may consist of multiple ion-
ized bubbles that have not yet completely merged, leav-
ing large neutral islands in between.
Owing to the
limited spectral resolution of the NIRSpec prism data,
we are unable to distinguish between these two scenar-
ios. Future medium- and high-resolution NIRSpec spec-
troscopy will be essential for resolving the small-scale
kinematics and transmitted features necessary to dis-
criminate between these possibilities.
