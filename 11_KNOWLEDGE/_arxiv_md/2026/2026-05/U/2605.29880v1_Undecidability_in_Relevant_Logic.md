UNDECIDABILITY IN RELEVANT LOGIC
SØREN BRINCK KNUDSTORP
Abstract. We prove undecidability for every positive relevant logic extending the sys-
tem axiomatized by hypothetical syllogism, prefixing, and suffixing and contained in the
logic of the semilattice frame (Pfin(N), ∪, ∅).
This settles the longstanding decision problem for the semilattice relevant logic S in
the negative, contrary to prevailing expectations of decidability. It also provides a new
proof of Urquhart’s undecidability theorem for R, E, and T [26], now by reduction from
the Wang tiling problem for arbitrarily large finite isosceles right triangular regions of the
plane.
§1. Introduction. Initially presented proof-theoretically, the problem of pro-
viding a model theory for relevant logics led Urquhart [24, 25] to develop semi-
lattice semantics. In the signature {→, ∧}, the resulting semilattice logic S→,∧
enjoys the finite model property, is decidable, and coincides with R→,∧, the
implication-conjunction fragment of the relevant logic R. In the presence of dis-
junction, the logic S = S→,∧,∨properly extends its R-counterpart R+ = R→,∧,∨.
While S is known to be recursively enumerable and was axiomatized by Fine [9]
and Charlwood [5], it has been an enduring open problem whether it is decidable.
More broadly, decision problems for relevant logics have been studied since
the beginnings of the field, with an early mention in Curry and Craig’s re-
view [7] of Church’s weak theory of implication [6], which corresponds to the
implication fragment R→of R. Kripke [18] proved R→decidable, and Meyer [20]
extended the result to the implication-conjunction fragment R→,∧. Still, the de-
cision problem for R+ (let alone for R) remained open for another two decades,
until Urquhart [26] settled the matter. Using a Lipshitz–Hutchinson-style [19, 13]
reduction that passes via a coordinatization theorem of von Neumann [30], he
showed that many relevant logics, including R+, E+ and T+, are undecidable.
Intriguingly, as noted in [26], S eluded these techniques, and its decision problem
remained unresolved. Eventually, this led experts, including Urquhart [28], to
conjecture that S is decidable. Contrary to this conjecture, we show that S is
undecidable. In doing so, we prove undecidability for a broad class of relevant
logics, among which are R+, E+ and T+ (to the author’s knowledge, the proof
of [26] remains the only prior undecidability proof for these systems).
The weakest system we prove undecidable is the relevant logic TWJ+.

The proof is by reduction from the Wang tiling problem, introduced by Wang [31,

proven undecidable by Berger [3]. A Wang tile is a square with coloured sides
and a fixed orientation (no rotation or reflection allowed). Given a finite set
of tiles W, the problem asks whether the plane can be covered with translated
copies of tiles from W placed side-by-side so that adjacent tiles have matching
colours on shared sides.
To code the tiling problem, for each finite tile set W, we construct a formula
ψW and prove that
(i) if TWJ+ ⊬ψW, then W tiles the plane, and
(ii) if W tiles the plane, then S ⊬ψW.
We shall in fact prove a stronger statement than (ii), but (ii) as stated suffices
to conclude that every logic L with TWJ+ ⊆L ⊆S is undecidable. It is worth
noting that we do not prove (i) directly. Instead, we show that if TWJ+ ⊬ψW,
then W tiles isosceles right triangular regions of the plane of arbitrarily large
finite size, which, by a K˝onig’s Lemma argument, implies (i).
An earlier version of this work appeared as the conference paper [14]; the
present journal version simplifies the proof, generalizes the result, and explains
the tiling intuitions in greater depth.
The latter is also relevant in view of
the apparent breadth of the methods developed here and elsewhere, including

Remark 3.3 (Sources of (un)decidability). This highlights the role of Hypo-
thetical syllogism, Prefixing and Suffixing in driving undecidability.
In com-
parison, recall that omitting Hypothetical syllogism from TWJ+ gives the de-
cidable logic TW+. This reflects a more general phenomenon: its (Hypothetical
syllogism-free) extension RW+ = TW+ ⊕Assertion [11] is likewise decidable, and
as are related Hypothetical syllogism-free (usually presented as Contraction-free)
variants of S [12]. Similarly, when Hypothetical syllogism is retained but the af-
fixing axioms are omitted, the resulting logic B+ ⊕Hypothetical syllogism is
decidable [4].
We should also mention that while the combination of these three axioms
drives undecidability modulo the decidable basic relevant logic B+, further con-
tributions, of course, come from axioms already present in B+. For example,
omitting distributivity from R+ yields Lattice-R+, which is decidable [20] (al-
though it has no primitive recursive decision procedure [27]).
The focus so far has been on the lower bound of the undecidable interval
[TWJ+, Log(Pfin(N), ∪, ∅)]. But instead of leaving out theorems of TWJ+, de-
cidability may also be brought about by extending with formulas not valid
in (Pfin(N), ∪, ∅).
For instance, adding φ →(ψ →φ) to R+ results in the
{∧, ∨, →}-fragment of intuitionistic propositional logic, while adding Peirce’s
law ((φ →ψ) →φ) →φ results in the corresponding fragment of classical
propositional logic. If, however, we add to R, in the language with negation, an-
other paradox of material implication, namely explosion φ ∧¬φ →ψ, we obtain
the logic KR, which remains undecidable [26] (in fact already in its four-variable
fragment, but not in its three-variable fragment [29]).

Within axiomatic theories such as ZFC, we can formalize statements of the
form ‘TWJ+ ⊢φ’ and ‘TWJ+ ⊬φ’. We may therefore ask, for a given φ ∈
L, whether the derivability of φ in TWJ+ is settled by ZFC. When presented
with the axiomatization of TWJ+ in §2.1, one might, at least initially, expect
this question to be trivial: surely ZFC, powerful enough to formalize modern
mathematics, should settle whether a formula φ ∈L is derivable from the simple
axioms and rules of TWJ+. This expectation, however, is far too optimistic.
Corollary 6.5. Assume that ZFC is sound for statements of the form ‘TWJ+ ⊢
φ’, i.e. that
ZFC ⊢‘TWJ+ ⊢φ’
implies
TWJ+ ⊢φ.
Then there are infinitely many φ ∈L for which the statement ‘TWJ+ ⊢φ’ is
independent of ZFC.
Proof. Since ZFC is recursively enumerable, we can define a Turing machine
that, on input φ, searches for a ZFC-proof of ‘TWJ+ ⊢φ’ or of its negation
‘TWJ+ ⊬φ’.
From this machine, for each finite set of formulas Φ ⊆L, we can define 2|Φ|
Turing machines that, outside Φ, run the preceding Turing machine, but, within
Φ, hard-code the 2|Φ| possible YES/NO answer patterns. One of these is correct
for inputs φ ∈Φ. It is also correct for inputs φ /∈Φ provided that ‘TWJ+ ⊢φ’
is not independent of ZFC. For if there is a ZFC-proof of ‘TWJ+ ⊢φ’, then
by soundness, TWJ+ ⊢φ. And if there is a ZFC-proof of ‘TWJ+ ⊬φ’, then
by consistency (implied by soundness), TWJ+ ⊬φ, since otherwise ZFC would
prove ‘TWJ+ ⊢φ’ by formalizing the corresponding finite derivation.
