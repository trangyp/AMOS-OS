UNBOUNDED MEAN CONVEX DOMAINS IN
EUCLIDEAN SPACE
JIAN GE
Abstract. In this note, we prove that the infimum of the mean curva-
ture on any disconnected boundary component of an unbounded mean
convex domain in Rn must be zero.
0. Introduction
The splitting theorem of Cheeger-Gromoll plays an essential role in the
study of the open Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature.
For manifold with boundary, [Kas83] proved that if M is complete Riemann-
ian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature and weakly mean-convex
boundary, and if ∂M is disconnected with at least one boundary compo-
nent, then M splits isometrically as a product:
Σn−1 × [0, ℓ].
In particular the boundary components are totally geodesic and parallel.
See also [CK92] for a warped product splitting. The compactness of one
boundary component is used in an essential way, it traps the shortest geo-
desic connecting two connected components of the boundary. If all boundary
components are non-compact, it is more complicated even for flat manifolds.
Classical minimal surface theory in R3 has a strong geometric property:
The only mean-convex domains in R3 with disconnected boundary are slabs
between parallel planes.
In particular the boundary has mean curvature
≡0, cf. [FCS80] and [HM90]. For higher dimensions, generalized catenoids
give non-flat minimal hypersurfaces trapped in a slab, you can stack many
catenoids one by one, the domain that these surfaces bounds is mean convex
with many boundary components. Therefore one cannot expect a product
structure. In fact, Gromov made the following conjecture:
Conjecture 0.1 (Section 3, Conjecture 1 in [Gro19]). Let X be an infinite
mean convex domain ⊂Rn. If the boundary ∂X is disconnected, then non
of the connected component of ∂X may have its mean curvature separated
away from 0, i.e. infimum of the mean curvatures of all components are
zero.

Theorem 0.2. Let X ⊂Rn be a connected unbounded domain with C2
smooth boundary.
Let ν be the outward unit normal of X.
Assume X

In particular, no connected component of ∂X has mean curvature separated

The idea of the proof is simple. We introduce a penalized distance func-
tion. The penalty forces the minimum to occur at a bounded pair of points
on the two connected components of the boundary, while becoming negligi-
ble as the penalty parameter tends to zero. At such a minimizing pair, the
first variation shows that the segment meets the two boundary components
almost perpendicularly. The second variation of the distance along matched
tangent directions then compares the two mean curvatures. Uniform mean
convexity makes a definite positive contribution, while the penalty terms
vanish in the limit. Hence a contradiction.
