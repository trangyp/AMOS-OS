Universal Approximation of Nonlinear Operators and Their Derivatives
Filippo de Feo∗

Abstract
Derivative-Informed Operator Learning (DIOL), i.e. learning a (nonlinear) operator and its derivatives, is an
open research frontier at the foundations of the influential field of Operator Learning (OL). In particular, Universal
Approximation Theorems (UATs) of nonlinear operators and their derivatives are foundational open questions
and delicate problems in nonlinear functional analysis. In this manuscript, we prove the first UATs of non-linear
k-times differentiable operators between Banach spaces and their derivatives, uniformly on compact sets and in
weighted Sobolev norms for general finite input measures, via OL architectures. Our results are the first complete
generalizations of the corresponding influential classical results in [Hornik, 1991] to infinite-dimensional settings
and OL.
We discuss several open areas where DIOL and our UATs find applications: high-order accuracy in OL,
fast constrained optimization in Banach spaces (e.g. optimal control of PDEs, inverse problems) and numerical
methods for infinite-dimensional PDEs (e.g. HJB PDEs on Banach spaces from optimal control of PDEs, SPDEs,
path-dependent systems, partially observed systems, mean-field control).
We parameterize nonlinear operators via Encoder-Decoder Architectures (EDAs), renowned classes of OL
architectures due to their generality, including classical architectures, such as DeepONets, Deep-H-ONets, PCA-
Nets. Our results are based on four key features that allow us to prove UATs in full generality: (i) Approximation
Properties of Banach spaces. (ii) k-times continuous differentiability in the sense of Bastiani (weaker than k-times
continuous Fréchet differentiability). (iii) Natural compact-open topologies for UA; indeed, we show that UA
in standard compact-open topologies induced by operator norms is violated even for Fréchet derivatives. (iv)
Construction of novel weighted Sobolev spaces for the UA.
Keywords: Operator Learning, Derivative-Informed Operator Learning, Sobolev spaces on Banach spaces, Learn-
then-Optimize, Optimize-then-Learn, Constrained Optimization on Banach spaces, Infinite-dimensional PDEs, Infinite-
Dimensional Optimal Control

Introduction
1.1
Model Problem and Goals
Let X, Y be Banach spaces and let F : X →Y be an unknown k-times differentiable, possibly nonlinear, operator.
For many crucial tasks (see Section 2 for extensive motivations and concrete examples), it is fundamental to
Simultaneously Learn F and Its Derivatives DiF, ∀i ≤k, accurately.
Finite-dimensional case.
If X, Y are finite dimensional, e.g. X = RN, Y = RN′, then F is a function, DiF(x)
are finite-dimensional multilinear functions, and the problem is classical in Machine Learning (ML). Typically, F is
parametrized by a ML architecture, such as neural network, which are able to learn F with very high accuracy. From
a theoretical perspective, this is supported by influential classical results called Universal Approximation Theorems
(UATs). These state that neural networks can simultaneously approximate k-times differentiable functions and their
derivatives, uniformly on compact sets and in Sobolev norms [61, 62, 103]. These are very powerful approximation
statements in mathematics.
Infinite-Dimensional case.
Let now X, Y be infinite-dimensional. When k = 0 this is now a standard task in the
influential field of Operator Learning (OL). Here nonlinear operator F is parametrized by suitable OL architectures
(see detailed literature review in Subsection 1.2). When k ≥1, the goal is stronger: one aims to learn F together
with its derivatives DiF, 1 ≤i ≤k. We refer to this problem as Derivative-Informed Operator Learning (DIOL).
However, as we will see, this problem is substantially more delicate and current investigations are mainly limited to
separable Hilbert spaces and k = 1. Indeed, from the literature review, we will infer that
Derivative-Informed Operator Learning is an open research frontier at the foundations of Operator Learning,
spanning theoretical, computational, and application-driven challenges, with possible implications in all applied
sciences. In particular:
Universal Approximation Theorems of Nonlinear Operators and Their Derivatives are foundational open questions.

functional analysis and raises the fundamental question that we will tackle in this manuscript:
how can we recover Universal Approximation in infinite dimensions?

The formulation (2) is especially well suited to UA: it leads naturally to compact-open topologies on the product

we prove the first UATs for nonlinear k-times differentiable operators and all their derivatives, both in
compact-open topologies and in weighted Sobolev norms for general finite input measures.

results in Hornik, 1991 [61] to infinite-dimensional settings and Operator Learning.
These results launch Derivative-Informed Operator Learning on Banach spaces and raise new theoretical and com-
putational challenges for Operator Learning.

1.2
State of the Art
Operator Learning.
Over roughly the last seven years, operator learning has emerged as a central paradigm
for learning maps between infinite-dimensional function spaces. OL has been extensively used to learn maps from
coefficient fields, forcing terms, initial or boundary data, and other functional inputs to the corresponding par-
tial differential equation (PDE) solutions or observables, providing fast surrogate models for many-query scientific
computing. Beyond classical PDE solution surrogates, OL has been used for scientific discovery and data-driven
modeling, nonlinear reduced-order modeling, Koopman/operator-inference methods, solver acceleration, data-driven
closure and constitutive modeling, turbulence and continuum-model learning, computer-graphics shape and surface
maps, and weather-style forecasting operators. Standard architectures include the Encoder-Decoder Architectures
[75], DeepONet [23, 83], Fourier Neural Operator [74], PCA-Net [8], Deep-H-ONet/Hilbert–Galerkin Neural Op-
erator [20] (see also [25]), Laplace Neural Operator [17], Spectral Neural Operator [41], and Convolutional Neural
Operator [109]. Their use for these tasks is theoretically supported by a series of influential UATs for nonlinear
operators on infinite-dimensional spaces [23, 74, 8, 64, 83, 20, 21, 65, 73, 67, 77, 131]. We refer to [75] for a detailed
review of OL. We also refer to [31, 22, 56, 86] for signature learning and UATs there, to [78, 102, 90] for OL on
Wasserstein spaces.
Derivative-Informed Operator Learning.
Even more recently, DIOL has been rapidly growing [15, 16, 25,
52, 53, 84, 55, 98, 97, 107, 129]. Due to the theoretical and computational challenges, numerical results are mostly
restricted to the separable Hilbert setting and to the first derivative case1. Here, similarly to the finite dimensional
case, the goal is to learn an operator and its (infinite-dimensional) derivatives. This offers, at least, three practical
applications (we refer to Section 2 for extensive examples):
(i) higher-order accuracy than standard OL with a significantly lower training sample size and generation cost
[97, 16, 107, 15, 85, 52].
(ii) Applications of OL to constrained optimization in infinite-dimensional spaces (such as optimal control of PDEs,
as inverse problems, and optimal design) [129, 85, 52, 53], where derivatives encode fundamental information
on minimizers, thereby allowing for fast online optimization.
(iii) Development of numerical methods for infinite-dimensional PDEs [25], such as functional differential equations
in Physics and Kolmogorov and Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman PDEs from infinite-dimensional optimal control
and games (controlled PDEs, SPDEs, path-dependent systems, mean-field control, and partially observed
stochastic systems), where the (infinite-dimensional) derivatives of the solution appear naturally in the infinite-
dimensional PDE.
However, the literature on Derivative-Informed Operator learning is mainly empirical and, unfortunately, UATs
for the simultaneous approximation of nonlinear operators and their infinite-dimensional derivatives are not under-
stood. Indeed, to the best of our knowledge, the only available results are proven in the following very special cases
(all appeared within about one year with respect to the first appearance of the present manuscript on arXiv):

1.3
Our Contributions
It is evident from the literature review above that Derivative-Informed Operator Learning is an open DIOL research
frontier at the foundations of OL, spanning theoretical, computational, and application-driven challenges, with
possible implications in all applied sciences. In particular, UATs of nonlinear operators and their derivatives are
foundational open questions. Therefore, the goal of the present manuscript is to provide the influential field of OL
with the theoretical foundations for the simultaneous learning of operators and their derivatives. We summarize the
structure of the paper and our contributions as follows.
Motivations for Derivative-Informed Operator Learning (Section 2).
As promised at the beginning of the
introduction, we start by presenting several motivations showing the broad relevance of DIOL and, consequently, of
UATs of nonlinear operators and their derivatives. While, in this section, we mostly avoid mathematical rigor, we
think that the presentation is to be considered an equally important contribution of the paper, as we launch several
new challenges and applications of DIOL. Motivations for DIOL typically come from query tasks (see Definition
2.1), requiring fast evaluation of a target k-times differentiable operator F : X →Y and, possibly, its derivatives.
This leads to higher-order accuracy in OL, as well as to Learn-then-Optimize methods for fast online constrained
optimization in Banach spaces (such as optimal control of PDEs, inverse problems, or optimal design) and to nu-
merical methods (conjectured as Deep Banach–Galerkin Methods) for infinite-dimensional PDEs on Banach spaces,
such as functional differential equations in physics (e.g. the celebrated Hopf equation in turbulence theory and
Schwinger–Dyson equation in quantum field theory) and HJB PDEs from infinite-dimensional optimal control (in-
cluding problems of PDEs, SPDEs, path-dependent systems, partially observed stochastic systems, and mean-field
SDEs). We refer to Section 2 for extensive bibliographic references.

B Compact-open Topologies (Section 4.2).
Our first goal is now to address the question above
and prove UATs in suitable compact-open topologies. We reason as follows:
(a) in general Banach spaces, possibly non-separable, we cannot rely on dense Hilbert–Schmidt (or compact)
embeddings as in [129], making such a non-intrinsic approach inapplicable in general settings. We might require
DiF(x) to be, e.g., compact operators, for all i and try to achieve UA in operator norms ∥·∥Li(X,Y ). However,
these are severe restrictions in infinite-dimensions, leading to unsatisfactory UATs, for our goals: for instance,
we could not represent even one of the most basic operators, namely the identity map F : X →X, F(x) := x,
since DF(x) ≡I is not a compact operator unless X is finite-dimensional, and it is not Hilbert–Schmidt when

Motivations for Derivative-Informed Operator Learning
In this section, we present several motivations showing the broad relevance of Derivative-Informed Operator Learning
and, consequently, of universal approximation theorems of nonlinear operators and their derivatives. Mostly, we avoid
developing applications with mathematical rigor, preferring a broad exposition and leaving them open as challenges
for future research. However, in Example 2.2 we will apply Theorem 4.4 to obtain UA via HGNOs (Subsection 3.3)
to a nonlinear C∞operator mapping forcing functions to solutions of the Navier–Stokes equations in 2D.
We will use the following terminology:
Definition 2.1 (Query task). A query task is a task where fast evaluation of a target operator F : X →Y , and
possibly, its derivatives DiF, i ≤k, at a given input x ∈X is required, while direct computation of F(x), DiF(x),
i ≤k is expensive. The aim is to obtain a fast surrogate in the form of an Operator Learning architecture.

Basic notations
In this manuscript, we often denote by C > 0 a generic constant, which may change from line to line.
A.1
Moore–Smith sequences and sequences
In this manuscript we will work with Moore–Smith sequences (also called nets) and sequences in topological spaces.
We advise the reader not to confuse nets in topological spaces with neural nets, as abbreviations of neural networks,
frequently used in the manuscript. Therefore, we prefer to call them Moore–Smith sequences.
Definition A.1 (Directed set). A directed set is an index set A equipped with ⪯such that
(i) ⪯is a pre-order relation (i.e. reflexive and transitive binary relation);
(ii) for all α, β ∈A, there exists γ ∈A so that α ⪯γ and β ⪯γ.
Definition A.2 (Moore–Smith sequences and sequences). A Moore–Smith sequence (or net) in a topological space
X is a mapping from a directed set A to X; we denote it by {xα}α∈A. When A = N, a Moore–Smith sequence is
called a sequence, and we denote it by {xn}n∈N. In this manuscript, to distinguish Moore–Smith sequences from
sequences we will use Greek letters (typically α, β) for indexes of Moore–Smith sequences and roman letters (typically
n, d, m) for indexes of sequences.
Definition A.3 (Convergence). A Moore–Smith sequence {xα}α∈A in a topological space X is said to converge to
a point x ∈X, denoted xα →x, if for any neighborhood N of x, there is a α0 ∈A so that xα ∈N if α ⪰α0. If X
is Hausdorff, the limit is unique.
A.2
Locally convex topological vector spaces
Definition A.4 (Seminorms and norms). A seminorm on a vector space W is a map ρ : W →[0, ∞) such that (i)
ρ(x + y) ≤ρ(x) + ρ(y); (ii) ρ(λx) = |λ|ρ(x) for λ ∈R. A seminorm | · | is a norm if (iii) |x| = 0 implies x = 0. Let
I be an index set. A family of seminorms {ρi}i∈I is said to separate points if ρi(x) = 0 for all i ∈I implies x = 0.
Definition A.5 (Locally convex space). A locally convex space is a vector space W with a family {ρi}i∈I of semi-
norms. The induced topology is the weakest topology in which all ρi, i ∈I are continuous and in which the operation
of addition is continuous. If the seminorms separate points the induced topology is Hausdorff. A Moore–Smith
sequence wα →w if and only if ρi(wα −w) →0 for all i ∈I.
The family {ρi}i∈I is called directed (or fundamental) if, for all i1, . . . , in ∈I, n ∈N there is a γ ∈I and C > 0
so that max(ρik(x), k = 1, . . . , n) ≤Cργ(x), for all x ∈W, or equivalently, by induction, restricting n = 2. The
existence of such a family can be assumed without loss of generality [110, p. 126]. If {ρi}i∈I is a directed family,
then {{x ∈W : ρi(x) < ε} : i ∈I, ε > 0} is a neighborhood base at 0 and {{x ∈W : ρi(x −w) < ε} : i ∈I, ε > 0} is
a neighborhood base at p ∈W. In this case, a subset G is dense in W if and only if for all w ∈W, i ∈I, ϵ > 0, there
exists g ∈G such that ρi(g −w) < ϵ. Let k ∈N and W = W1 × . . . Wk, where each Wj, j ≤k is a locally convex
topological vector space whose topology is generated by a family of directed seminorms {ρj
ij}ij∈Ij. We endow W with
the product topology generated by the family of directed seminorms {ρi}i∈I, where ρi(w) := max(ρj
ij(wj), j ≤k),
w = (w1, . . . , wk), i = (i1, . . . , ik) ∈I := I1 × . . . × Ik.
Definition A.6 (Equivalent seminorms). Two families of seminorms {ρi}i∈I and {νβ}β∈B on a vector space X are
called equivalent if one of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:
(i) they generate the same topology.
(ii) Each ρi is continuous in the d-natural topology and each dβ is continuous in the ρ-natural topology.
(iii) For each i ∈I, there are β1, . . . , βn ∈B and C > 0 so that for all x ∈X, it holds ρi(x) ≤C (νβ1(x) + · · · + νβn(x))
