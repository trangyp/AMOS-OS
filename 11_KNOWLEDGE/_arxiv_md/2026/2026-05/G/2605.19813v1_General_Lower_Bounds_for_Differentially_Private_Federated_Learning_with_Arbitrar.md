General Lower Bounds for Differentially Private Federated Learning
with Arbitrary Public-Transcript Interactions∗
Yicheng Li†
May 20, 2026
Abstract
We prove a general lower bound for differentially private federated learning protocols with arbitrary
public-transcript interactions. The protocol may use any number of adaptive rounds, and each client’s local
samples may be reused across rounds. For parameter estimation under squared ℓ2 loss, we establish a feder-
ated van Trees lower bound for every estimator satisfying a total clientwise sample-level zero-concentrated
differential privacy (zCDP) constraint. The main technical ingredient is a privacy-information contraction
inequality for complete public transcripts. We illustrate the bound through applications to mean estimation,
linear regression, and nonparametric regression.

Introduction
Differential privacy (DP) [27, 25] is a widely adopted rigorous mathematical framework for quantifying the pri-
vacy guarantees of randomized algorithms. A common setting is the central DP model, where a trusted curator
has access to the entire dataset and produces a differentially private output. In many applications, however, the
data are distributed across multiple clients with different sample sizes and various privacy requirements, while
the local datasets cannot be directly shared due to privacy concerns or communication constraints. This setting
is often referred to as federated DP or federated learning. A fundamental question in this area is to understand
the trade-offs [16] between privacy and utility: how much accuracy must be sacrificed to achieve a certain level
of privacy?
A line of work has been devoted to establishing lower bounds for the minimax risk of central DP estimation.
Early minimax formulations and testing reductions were followed by private analogues of Le Cam’s method,
Fano’s inequality, and Assouad’s lemma [6, 1]. For high-dimensional and approximate-DP problems, finger-
printing and tracing arguments provide another powerful route [11, 28, 30, 37], and the score-attack framework
turns these ideas into a general likelihood-score method [16, 20]. More recently, Fisher-information contraction
and private van Trees inequalities have yielded smooth Bayesian lower-bound tools under zCDP [15]. These
results are all focused on the central DP model.
However, federated learning protocols often allow repeated and adaptive communication between a server and
many clients. In lower-bound arguments, this creates essential differences: the same local sample may influence
many messages, and the server’s later queries may depend on all earlier messages. Recent work has studied lower
bounds for federated DP in several concrete statistical problems [17, 34, 18, 5, 38, 29, 19]. These results are
sharp for their target problems, but their lower-bound arguments are tailored to specific models and typically
restricted to non-interactive releases, one-pass or chained sequential transcripts, or fresh-batch decompositions
to separate communication rounds.
To our knowledge, establishing general lower bounds for federated DP
protocols with arbitrary public-transcript interactions and local sample reuse across rounds remains open.
In this paper, we give a general lower-bound inequality for federated DP estimation of arbitrary parameters
in general statistical models, with error measured by squared ℓ2 loss. The protocol may have arbitrary public-
transcript interactions: each client message can depend on the client’s entire local dataset and on all previously
public messages. Local samples may be reused across rounds, and the privacy budget is not necessarily split by
round. The only privacy requirement is that, for each client, the complete ordered public transcript satisfies a
total sample-level zCDP budget.
As applications, our results easily provide lower bounds for mean estimation, linear regression, nonparametric
regression, and other problems under federated DP. As special cases of federated DP, our results also recover
known lower bounds under central or local DP.

1.1
Related work
Lower bounds in central DP
The central DP lower-bound literature adapts several classical minimax
techniques to privacy-constrained estimation. Private versions of Le Cam, Fano, and Assouad inequalities give
general testing-based tools for central DP statistical estimation [1]. These reductions are conceptually close to
classical packing and hypercube arguments, and they are especially useful when the private difficulty can be
encoded through pairwise or coordinatewise testing instances.
Another major line is based on fingerprinting, tracing, and score attacks. Fingerprinting codes were first used
to prove lower bounds for approximate-DP query release and empirical-risk problems [11, 9], and robust tracing
arguments later removed some distributional restrictions [28]. For statistical estimation, generalized fingerprint-
ing lemmas have produced sharp private lower bounds for Gaussian covariance and other exponential-family
problems [30, 37]. The score attack of Cai et al. [20] abstracts this approach through likelihood scores, recover-
ing and extending sharp lower bounds for generalized linear models, ranking, sparse models, and nonparametric
regression; the earlier “cost of privacy” analysis of Cai et al. [16] used related tracing ideas for mean estimation
and linear regression. More recently, a Fisher information contraction was developed in Cai and Li [15] to give
a general van Trees lower bound for central DP estimation, which is especially well suited to dependent priors
and matrix parameters. All of these results are central-DP results: the statistic is a single private output of a
pooled sample, so they do not address clientwise privacy budgets or adaptive public transcripts.
Lower bounds in local DP
The local DP model is more restrictive than central DP: each observation, user,
or client must privatize its own message before it is observed by any curator. The minimax theory of local
privacy was initiated by Duchi et al. [22, 23, 24], who developed private data-processing inequalities and sharp
minimax rates for locally private estimation.
Subsequent work has refined the information-theoretic and interactive aspects of local privacy. Acharya
et al. [3] give unified lower bounds for interactive high-dimensional estimation under information constraints,
a framework that includes local privacy and communication constraints. Fisher-information viewpoints have
also been developed for distributed estimation under blackboard protocols and for local DP channels [7, 8, 21].
Recent user-level local-DP work allows each user to hold multiple observations and protects the entire local
collection, establishing tight distribution-estimation rates and broader phase-transition lower bounds [2, 31].
Other work studies testing and communication-limited local privacy [36], nonparametric density and functional
estimation under local privacy [12, 13, 14, 32], and high-dimensional regression under local privacy [40].
However, the techniques used in local DP lower bounds are not directly applicable to the central or federated
DP setting here. If applied naively to the central DP setting, similar approaches in these works often lose a
factor of n in the effective sample size due to a rough conversion through group privacy.
Lower bounds in federated DP
A growing literature studies statistical and optimization lower bounds
under federated or distributed DP constraints. For convex federated learning without a trusted server, Lowy
and Razaviyayn [35] establish nearly tight excess-risk bounds under inter-silo record-level privacy and compare
this trust model with local, shuffle, and central DP. Zhang et al. [39] study how server trust affects private
federated estimation and inference, while related distributed-learning work considers robustness and privacy
trade-offs under distributed mechanisms [4]. These works clarify how the privacy model and trust assumptions
affect achievable risk, but their lower-bound tools are tailored to convex optimization or specific inferential
tasks.
In nonparametric and transfer-learning problems, recent papers have shown that heterogeneous client sam-
ple sizes and privacy budgets change both the optimal aggregation rule and the minimax rate. Results in-
clude federated nonparametric regression and testing under heterogeneous distributed DP [18, 17], federated
transfer learning and nonparametric classification under distributed DP [34, 5], adaptive federated density es-
timation [19], private distributed functional data analysis [38], federated Cox regression and cumulative-hazard
estimation [29], and federated PCA/spiked covariance estimation [33].
The present work is closest in spirit to the van Trees lower-bound arguments in heterogeneous federated
nonparametric and matrix estimation [18, 33, 38]. However, existing proofs either work with non-interactive local
releases, use one-pass, chained sequential, or fresh-batch decompositions to separate communication rounds, or
exploit problem-specific reductions. Our contribution is a general lower bound: each client is charged only its
total sample-level zCDP budget, arbitrary public-transcript interactions are allowed, and the same local dataset
may be reused across arbitrary adaptive rounds.
