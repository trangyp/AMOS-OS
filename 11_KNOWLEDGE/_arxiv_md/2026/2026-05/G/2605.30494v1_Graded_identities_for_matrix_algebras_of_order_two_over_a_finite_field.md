GRADED IDENTITIES FOR MATRIX ALGEBRAS OF ORDER
TWO OVER A FINITE FIELD
DIOGO DINIZ, EDUARDO PINTO DA FONSˆECA, AND LUIS FILIPE RAMOS
Abstract. Let G be an arbitrary group and let F be a finite field. In this
paper, we determine bases for the TG-ideals of graded polynomial identities of
the algebra M2(F) for all possible G-gradings. The bases obtained consist of
finitely many non-trivial graded identities, and are finite whenever G is finite.
1. Introduction
A fundamental development in the theory of PI-algebras is A. Kemer’s solution
to the Specht Problem for algebras over a field of characteristic zero.
Given a
concrete algebra, however, it is a very difficult problem to determine a finite basis
for its polynomial identities. Let M2(K) be the matrix algebra of order two over
the field K. When K has characteristic zero Yu. P. Razmyslov provided a finite
basis for the identities of M2(K) in [19] and V. Drensky provided a minimal basis,
consisting of two identities, in [7]. Over an infinite field of characteristic different
from two, analogous results were obtained by P. Koshlukov in [12]. A basis for the
identities of the matrix algebras of order two over a finite field was determined by
Yu. N. Mal’tsev and E. N. Kuz’min in [16].
An important concept in A. Kemer’s theory of T-ideals in characteristic zero
is the notion of graded polynomial identities for graded algebras. It is natural to
extend Specht’s Problem to the context of graded algebras. In the case of algebras
over fields of characteristic zero a positive answer has been given by E. Aljadeff and
A. Kanel-Belov in [10] and by I. Sviridova in [20]. O. M. Di Vincenzo in [6] and P.
Koshlukov and S. S. Azevedo in [14] determined bases for the graded identities of
M2(K) in characteristic zero and when K is infinite, respectively. When K is a finite
field of characteristic different from two the Z2-gradings on M2(K) were described
in [13] and finite bases for the graded polynomial identities were determined.
Gradings on matrix algebras over an algebraically closed field were classified by
Yu. A. Bahturin and M. Zaicev in [2]. Over an arbitrary field the group gradings
on matrix algebras of order two were classified in [11]. More generally, analogous
results concerning matrix algebras of prime order were obtained in [3] and [5]. In the
present paper we determine bases for the graded identities for the matrix algebra of
order two with an arbitrary group grading. We also remark that the bases obtained
are finite when the grading group is finite.

2.1. Varieties of Graded Algebras. Let XG = ∪g∈GXg, where Xg = {xi,g | i ≥

Let A = ⊕g∈GAg be a G-graded algebra. Any map ψ : XG →A such that
ψ(Xg) ⊆Ag for all g ∈G can be uniquely extended to a homomorphism Ψ :
K⟨XG⟩→A of graded algebras. Given f = f(x1,g1, . . . , xn,gn) ∈K⟨XG⟩the image
of f under Ψ depends only on the elements ai = ψ(xi,gi), for i = 1, . . . , n and
is denoted f(a1, . . . , an). Moreover f = 0, or simply f, is a graded polynomial
identity for A if f(a1, . . . , an) = 0 for all a1 ∈Ag1, . . . , an ∈Agn. The set IdG(A)
of all graded polynomial identities for A is a TG-ideal of K⟨XG⟩, i. e., it is an ideal
invariant under all endomorphisms of K⟨XG⟩as a graded algebra. We remark that
if G = {e} is the trivial group then we are speaking of the ordinary identities of A,
in this case we omit the symbols if G and e in the notation, hence the free algebra
is denoted K⟨X⟩and X = {x1, x2, . . . }.
Let S ⊆K⟨XG⟩, the variety V determined by S is the class of all graded algebras
A such that S ⊆IdG(A). Thus by a variety of graded algebras we understand the
class of all algebras determined by some given set of graded polynomial identities.
Given a class A of algebras we denote var(A) the smallest variety that contains A.
If A = {A} we denote it simply by var(A). A set S ⊆K⟨XG⟩is a basis for IdG(A)
if this is the smallest TG-ideal of K⟨XG⟩that contains S. This occurs if and only
if var(A) is the variety determined by S.
The following concept will be important for the proofs of our main results:
Definition 8. A G-graded algebra A is graded subdirectly irreducible if the inter-
section of all non-zero graded ideals is different from zero.
In [1] the authors prove that graded algebras can be seen as universal algebras,
therefore Birkhoff’s Theorem, see [9, Theorem 2.6], implies the following proposi-
tion:
Proposition 9. Every G-graded algebra is a subdirect product of graded subdirectly
irreducible algebras.
A variety V of graded algebras is locally finite if every algebra in V is locally
finite. The index of V is the supremum of the nilpotency indices of the nilpotent
algebras in V. Next we prove that a variety of algebras over a finite field that has
finite index is generated by its finite graded subdirectly irreducible algebras. First
we prove that such varieties are locally finite.
