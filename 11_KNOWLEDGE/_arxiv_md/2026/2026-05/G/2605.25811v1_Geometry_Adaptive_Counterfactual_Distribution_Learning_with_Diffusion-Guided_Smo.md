Geometry Adaptive Counterfactual Distribution
Learning with Diffusion-Guided Smoothing
Kwangho Kim*
Department of Statistics, Korea University
May 26, 2026
Abstract
We study counterfactual distribution learning for high-dimensional outcomes whose
counterfactual law may concentrate near lower-dimensional structure. Standard isotropic
smoothing treats all ambient directions equally, leading to unfavorable scaling and unstable
local inference. We propose two diffusion-guided estimators based on semiparametric debias-
ing: diffusion-informed smoothing for counterfactual densities and diffusion-informed score
smoothing for counterfactual scores. The estimators combine causal nuisance adjustment with
geometry-adaptive localization driven by diffusion score information, removing first-order
nuisance bias while aligning smoothing with local outcome geometry. We establish asymptotic
expansions, risk bounds, and inference procedures for smoothed density and score-based
targets, with ambient density inference obtained under additional approximation conditions.
Under structural geometry conditions, the leading stochastic error is governed by an effective
dimension induced by the diffusion-guided kernel, rather than by the ambient dimension.
Semi-synthetic experiments based on CelebA show steeper error decay for geometry-adaptive
methods, supporting the proposed effective-dimension theory.
Keywords: causal inference; counterfactual density; semiparametric inference; influence functions;
effective dimension; score estimation

Introduction
Counterfactuals ask what would have happened under a specified intervention, whether or not
it was actually taken, and are foundational for scientific explanation and data driven decision
making. Originating in causal inference, this perspective has motivated a growing literature on
predicting outcomes and evaluating policies in deployment environments that differ from the
training distribution (e.g., Chernozhukov et al. 2013, Dickerman et al. 2022, Kim et al. 2022, Kim
2025). In many applications, however, stakeholders need counterfactual distributional information
rather than only summaries such as means. Distributional targets are needed to quantify tail risk,
characterize heterogeneity, and assess policy trade offs: an intervention may leave the mean nearly
unchanged while reducing catastrophic events, or it may induce multimodality that changes which
subpopulations benefit or are harmed.
Recent work has begun to learn unobserved counterfactual distributions and general functionals
directly. Some approaches rely on explicit structural or graphical causal models, often requiring
a specified directed acyclic graph, and prioritize mechanistic generation over inference and
uncertainty quantification (e.g., Sanchez & Tsaftaris 2022, Pawlowski et al. 2020). Other recent
generative approaches enable flexible counterfactual sampling, but typically focus less on statistical
efficiency, robustness, local density or score inference, and confidence bands (e.g., Wu et al. 2024,
Ma et al. 2024). Semiparametric approaches address part of this problem by targeting smoothed
counterfactual functionals and using influence function based correction to reduce first-order
sensitivity to nuisance estimation (e.g., Kim et al. 2018, Kennedy et al. 2023, Martinez-Taboada &
Kennedy 2023). Yet they do not resolve a central challenge in modern applications: outcomes
may be ultrahigh dimensional while concentrating near lower dimensional structure, as in images,
trajectories, or rich clinical measurements. In such settings, isotropic smoothing can impose a
severe ambient dimensional bias variance trade off.
In parallel, score-based diffusion models have become a powerful tool for learning complex
high dimensional distributions (e.g., Ho et al. 2020, Song et al. 2021, Vincent 2011, Song et
al. 2021). These methods learn the score, the gradient of the log density, and use it to guide reverse
dynamics for sampling. Beyond generation, score fields provide a useful geometric primitive:
they encode local directional information and can induce data adaptive neighborhoods without
requiring explicit density evaluation (Hyvarinen 2005, Song et al. 2021, Song & Ermon 2019).

Recent work further suggests that score-based diffusion models can exploit low dimensional
structure in high dimensional representations (e.g., Pidstrigach 2022, Tang & Yang 2024, Liang
et al. 2024).
Motivated by this connection, recent counterfactual generative methods have begun to incor-
porate modern generators, including diffusion models. A representative example is Luedtke &
Fukumizu (2025), which debiases a generator training objective using doubly robust corrections to
enable counterfactual sampling under confounding. However, this line of work is primarily geared
toward sample generation and global distributional discrepancy control, rather than inference for
local distributional objects or downstream functionals. Moreover, their theoretical guarantees
are largely framed in ambient dimensional terms, rather than in terms of intrinsic dimension
adaptivity for local density and score targets. This raises a natural question: can we design
semiparametric counterfactual learning methods that target local distributional objects, support
confidence intervals or bands, and adapt to intrinsic rather than ambient dimension?
This paper takes a step toward this goal by developing diffusion-guided methods for coun-
terfactual distribution learning and inference. The core idea is to replace isotropic smoothing
with score constructed smoothing operators whose local shape is guided by diffusion geometry.
When outcomes concentrate near lower dimensional structure or exhibit anisotropic curvature,
these operators can localize along directions that govern effective variation, potentially reducing
the smoothing complexity relative to the ambient dimension. We embed this geometry guided
smoothing inside semiparametric debiased estimators, so causal nuisance errors enter through
second order product remainders. The resulting framework supports inference for smoothed
counterfactual densities, score fields, and downstream functionals, while explicitly separating the
oracle effective dimension gain from the additional drift introduced by learning the geometry.
Our contributions are threefold. First, we formalize well posed smoothed counterfactual
targets, including density and score targets, that remain meaningful for ultrahigh dimensional or
nearly singular outcome laws. Second, we develop two influence function based estimators for
these targets. Both use diffusion-guided kernels to incorporate outcome geometry and one step
corrections to remove first-order causal nuisance bias. Third, we establish asymptotic expansions,
risk bounds, and confidence procedures for density and score based targets. Our theory is among
the first to formalize geometry adaptive counterfactual distribution learning and counterfactual
inference, showing when diffusion-guided smoothing yields oracle stochastic error governed by

motivates our diffusion-guided construction, but it also introduces several challenges.
Three challenges arise. First, the ambient outcome dimension d can be very large even when
counterfactual variation lies near a lower dimensional structure, so the smoothing design should
avoid treating irrelevant ambient directions as equally informative. Second, the relevant geometry
is unknown: although modern diffusion and score-based models provide tools for learning local
geometry in high dimensional spaces (Vincent 2011, Ho et al. 2020, Song et al. 2021, Song et
al. 2021), their use in counterfactual learning is nontrivial because the target counterfactual law
is not directly observed and must be recovered under confounding. Third, once the diffusion
geometry is learned, the smoothing kernel itself is perturbed, creating a geometry-induced target
drift that is not removed by the causal nuisance correction for a fixed kernel. Our approach
addresses these challenges by combining influence function based counterfactual estimation with
diffusion-guided smoothing, while explicitly separating causal nuisance error from geometry
learning error.

Conclusion
We take a first step toward counterfactual distribution learning with valid inference and geometry
adaptive smoothing grounded in diffusion models. Several limitations remain. The theory relies
on high level drift control and intrinsic geometry learning conditions, thus sharper primitive
guarantees linking these conditions to score accuracy, noise schedules, and finite sample geometry
learning remain an important next step. Computation may also be challenging for ultrahigh
dimensional outcomes, where learning diffusion geometry and evaluating transported kernels can
be expensive. These limitations motivate end to end diffusion based implementations, principled
selection of h, and tuning procedures that preserve valid inference under adaptive smoothing.

The experiment is designed to isolate the variance behavior predicted by the effective dimension
theory. When the outcome embedding concentrates near lower dimensional structure, geometry-
adaptive smoothing should exhibit steeper log-log error decay than isotropic smoothing. The one

Data source and representation
We follow the CelebA observational design used in the counterfactual faces experiment of Luedtke
& Fukumizu (2025). Each unit is an image with binary treatment A ∈{0, 1}, where A = 1
