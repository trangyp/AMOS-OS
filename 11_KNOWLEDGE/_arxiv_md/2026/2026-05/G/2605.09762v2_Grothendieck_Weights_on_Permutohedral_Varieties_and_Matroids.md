Abstract. Grothendieck weights, introduced by Shah, are K-theoretic analogues of Minkowski weights on smooth
toric varieties. We study Grothendieck weights on the permutohedral fan and prove two main results: a K-balancing
condition that characterizes Grothendieck weights by a finite system of linear equations, and an explicit product rule
for the ring structure. We apply this framework to matroids, giving a combinatorial characterization of Grothendieck
weights onmatroidal fans. As the mainapplication, we compute the motivic Chern class of the hyperplane arrangement
complement in its wonderful compactification and show that the result depends only on the matroid, not on the
realization. This allows us to extend the definition of the motivic Chern class to all loopless matroids.

1. Introduction
Let X be a complete smooth toric variety with fan Σ. In [FS97], Fulton and Sturmfels introduce the notion
of Minkowski weights and show that the Chow ring of a complete smooth toric variety is isomorphic to the ring
of Minkowski weights. A Minkowski weight is a Z-valued function on Σ satisfying a balancing condition. They
also give a “fan displacement rule” for computing the product of two Minkowski weights.
In [Sha20], Shah introduces the K-theoretic analogue of this theory. For a cone σ ∈Σ, let xσ = [OV (σ)] ∈
K(X) denote the K-class of the structure sheaf of the orbit closure V (σ). A Grothendieck weight is a Z-valued
function g : Σ →Z such that P
σ cσg(σ) = 0 whenever P
σ cσxσ = 0. The K-ring of X is isomorphic to the
group GW(Σ) of all Grothendieck weights via the map α 7→gα, where gα(σ) = χ(α · xσ) and χ : K(X) →Z is
the Euler characteristic; this map is in fact a ring isomorphism. Shah also proves a product rule for computing
the product of two Grothendieck weights. Both results are obtained by applying the Riemann–Roch transform
to lift the corresponding results for Minkowski weights to K-theory.
However, Shah’s approach has two drawbacks. Because it proceeds via the Riemann–Roch transform, which
is an isomorphism only over Q, the resulting coefficients are rational rather than integral. Moreover, these

3. Strongly unimodular fan
Although the main focus of this paper is the permutohedron, our arguments work for the larger class defined
below.
Definition 3.1. A fan Σ ⊆NR is called strongly unimodular if it is unimodular and, for any two cones σ, τ ∈Σ,
[N : Nσ + Nτ] = 1 or ∞.
Here unimodular means that each cone is generated by part of a Z-basis of N. Thus, in addition, if (Nσ)R and
(Nτ)R generate the whole NR, then the index of Nσ + Nτ is one.
We collect some useful properties of strongly unimodular fans in the following proposition.
Proposition 3.2. Let Σ ⊆NR be strongly unimodular.
(1) Every subfan Σ′ ⊆Σ is strongly unimodular.
