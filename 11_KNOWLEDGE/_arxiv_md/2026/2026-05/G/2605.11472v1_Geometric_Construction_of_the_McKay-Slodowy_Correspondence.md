Preliminary
This section contains some basic theory, most of which is well known.
Throughout this paper, a variety is an integral, separated scheme of finite
type over C, a curve is a variety of dimension 1, and a surface is a variety of
dimension 2.
Grothendieck groups
Let A be an abelian category. The Grothendieck group K(A) is defined as
the quotient of the free abelian group generated by objects of A, by the sub-
group generated by relations of the form A −B + C whenever there exists an
exact sequence 0 →A →B →C →0 in A.
For a finite group G, we denote by R(G) the Grothendieck group of (finite-
dimensional complex) representations of G. In fact, since C[G] is a semi-simple
ring, we have R(G) = L
ρ∈Irr(G) Z · ρ, where Irr(G) is the set of all irreducible
representations of G. The inner product of characters of representations gives a
bilinear form R(G) × R(G) 7→Z, (ρ1, ρ2) 7→⟨χρ1, χρ2⟩. Moreover, after being
equipped with multiplication defined by the tensor product ⊗, R(G) becomes
a ring, called the Grothendieck ring of representations of G.

Quotients by finite groups
Let G be a finite group, C a category, and X an object in C with automor-
phism group Aut(X). We say G acts on X, denoted G ↷X, if there exists a
group homomorphism G →Aut(X). Under this homomorphism, each g ∈G
is identified with an automorphism g : X →X. A morphism f : X →Y is
called G-invariant if G acts on X and f ◦g = f for all g ∈G. A morphism
f : X →Y is called G-equivariant if G acts on both X and Y, and f ◦g = g ◦f
for all g ∈G. A morphism π : X →X/G is called the (categorical) quotient
of X by G if for every G-invariant morphism f : X →Y, there exists a unique
(up to isomorphism) morphism ¯f : X/G →Y such that f = ¯f ◦π. It follows
immediately that the quotient is unique (up to isomorphism) if it exists.
In the category of finite sets, the quotient of a finite set by a finite group
exists and is precisely the set of its orbits.
In the category of quasi-projective varieties, [Liu, Chapter 3, Exercise 3.3.23]
shows that for any quasi-projective variety X acted by a finite group G, the
quotient morphism π : X →X/G always exists, and it is a finite surjective
morphism. Furthermore, [Liu, Chapter 4, Exercise 1.10] shows that if X is nor-
mal, then so is X/G, and [KM98, Lemma 5.16] shows that if X is Q-factorial,
then so is X/G.
