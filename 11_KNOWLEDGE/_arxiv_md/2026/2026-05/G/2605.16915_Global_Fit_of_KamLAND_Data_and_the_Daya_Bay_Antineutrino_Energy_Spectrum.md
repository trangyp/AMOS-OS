Global Fit of KamLAND Data and the Daya Bay Antineutrino Energy Spectrum
Guihong Huang
Wuyi University, Jiangmen 529020, China
Abstract
Recently, the JUNO experiment published its measurement of the solar neutrino oscillation parameters ∆m2
21 and sin2 θ12 based on
59 days of data, with central values differing by 0.2σ from those released by the KamLAND experiment in 2013. Meanwhile, short-
baseline reactor neutrino oscillation experiments such as Daya Bay, RENO, and Double Chooz have observed significant deviations
between the measured antineutrino spectrum and the Huber-Müller model prediction around 5 MeV. To further investigate the
impact of these deviations on the measurement of reactor neutrino oscillation parameters, we construct a global analysis framework
that is weakly dependent on the reactor antineutrino flux model. This framework is based on the independently measured 235U and
239Pu fission antineutrino spectra from the Daya Bay experiment, combined with the public data from KamLAND. First, using the
Huber-Müller model, we successfully reproduce the KamLAND 2013 results to within 0.1σ. Then, replacing the Huber-Müller
model with the Daya Bay measured antineutrino spectra in a combined analysis, we find that the best-fit value of the mass-squared
difference ∆m2
21 decreases from 7.53+0.17
−0.16 × 10−5 eV2 to 7.50+0.19
−0.18 × 10−5 eV2, while the best-fit value of the mixing angle tan2 θ12
also shows a decreasing trend. This result is in better agreement with the latest JUNO measurement, suggesting that differences in
the predicted reactor antineutrino spectra may be an important cause of the tension between the two experiments.
Keywords: Neutrino oscillation parameters; Reactor antineutrino spectrum; Global analysis
1. Introduction
The great success of neutrino oscillation experiments and
theories in explaining the solar and atmospheric neutrino anoma-
lies provides strong evidence that neutrinos have non-zero mass.
The solar neutrino experiment SNO measured the mass-squared
difference ∆m2
21 = 5.6+1.9
−1.4×10−5 eV2 and mixing angle tan2 θ12 =
0.427+0.030
−0.029 [1]. In contrast, the long-baseline reactor neutrino
oscillation experiment KamLAND reported ∆m2
21 = 7.54+0.19
−0.18 ×
10−5 eV2 and tan2 θ12 = 0.481+0.092
−0.080 [2]. With the constraint on
θ13, the KamLAND results become ∆m2
21 = 7.54+0.18
−0.18×10−5 eV2
and tan2 θ12 = 0.460+0.063
−0.056 (from Fig. 4 in [2]). Hence, there is
some tension between KamLAND and SNO in both ∆m2
21 and
tan2 θ12, especially for ∆m2
21. Very recently, the JUNO experi-
ment published the most precise measurement of solar neutrino
oscillation parameters to date, with ∆m2
21 = 7.50+0.12
−0.12×10−5 eV2
and tan2 θ12 = 0.4476+0.0182
−0.0182 [3]. This differs from the Kam-
LAND result by 0.2σ, indicating a mild tension.
On the other hand, short-baseline reactor neutrino oscilla-
tion experiments have revealed differences between the mea-
sured reactor antineutrino spectrum and the Huber-Müller model
prediction, both in shape and absolute flux. These differences
manifest as an approximately 6% deficit in the total reactor
neutrino flux and the so-called “5 MeV bump” [4–9]. Given
the central role of the reactor antineutrino spectrum in reac-
tor neutrino oscillation analyses, it is of great interest to under-
stand how the spectral anomaly affects the fitted neutrino mass

7.34+0.17
−0.16 × 10−5 eV2 and tan2 θ12 = 0.437+0.029

study used the overall energy-dependent ratio f(E) between
data and the Huber-Müller expectation [10], and subsequent
work adopted the Daya Bay unfolded antineutrino spectrum
[9], but without a quantitative discussion of its impact on so-

the reactor neutrino flux expectation influences the KamLAND
fit, we construct in this paper a global analysis framework that
is weakly dependent on the reactor neutrino flux model.
In this study, we first reproduce the latest KamLAND anal-
ysis results. Then, by treating the 235U and 239Pu antineutrino
spectra as free parameters and imposing constraints from the
Daya Bay experiment, we perform a combined fit of KamLAND
data and Daya Bay antineutrino spectra. For completeness, we
also investigate a simplified spectral correction method similar
to that in Ref. [10]. The final results show that in the combined
fit, ∆m2
21 decreases by about 0.19σ, which is slightly smaller
than the 0.25σ decrease obtained with the simplified correc-
tion. The global analysis framework developed here will be
valuable for future uses of reactor antineutrino spectra, such as
the unfolded spectrum from JUNO-TAO.

of the independent antineutrino spectra of 235U and 239Pu [8].
They found that spectral deviations from theory appear at dif-
ferent confidence levels for the two isotopes. This provides the
motivation and input for our combined analysis of KamLAND
data and Daya Bay antineutrino spectra.

treated as free parameters, uniformly binned into 25 neutrino
energy bins from 2 to 8 MeV. This introduces 50 new spectral
free parameters, which are constrained by the unfolded reactor
antineutrino spectra from Daya Bay. The bin-to-bin uncertainty

In addition, we perform a simplified combined analysis that
directly uses the unfolded antineutrino spectrum from Daya Bay
to correct the KamLAND expectation via the ratio of data to
theoretical prediction.
This simplification is justified by the
fact that the average fission fractions of the two experiments
are quite close. For simplicity and conservativeness, the covari-
ance matrix and shape uncertainties of the Huber-Müller model
are kept unchanged in this simplified analysis.

4. Conclusion
Based on the KamLAND 2013 long-baseline reactor neutrino
oscillation data and the Daya Bay 2019 short-baseline antineu-
trino spectra of 235U and 239Pu, we have constructed a detailed
combined analysis framework that incorporates spectral shape
constraints and systematic error correlations. This framework
systematically investigates the impact of reactor antineutrino
spectrum anomalies on the neutrino oscillation parameters ∆m2
21
and tan2 θ12.
In the constrained θ13 mode, our best-fit solar
oscillation parameters are ∆m2
21 = 7.50+0.19
−0.18 × 10−5 eV2 and
tan2 θ12 = 0.439+0.064
−0.053. Compared to the KamLAND standalone
fit using the Huber-Müller theoretical spectrum, the central val-
ues of ∆m2
21 and tan2 θ12 decrease by about 0.2σ and 0.4σ,
respectively, bringing the measurements into better agreement
with the latest high-precision results from JUNO.
This study demonstrates that measurements of the reactor
antineutrino spectrum can effectively break the dependence of
reactor neutrino oscillation experiments on spectral models and
partially alleviate the long-standing tension between KamLAND
and solar neutrino experiments.
Furthermore, the combined
KamLAND–Daya Bay analysis method developed here can serve
as a reference for systematic uncertainty assessments and spec-
tral model corrections in future large-scale neutrino experiments
such as JUNO and Hyper-K.
