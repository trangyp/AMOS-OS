Guitar Nebula: extreme accelerator in extreme environment
Nikonorov Igor Nikolaevicha,b, Barkov Maxim Vladimirovicha, Lyutikov Maximc
aDepartment of stellar physics and evolution, Institute of Astronomy of the Russian Academy of Sciences, 48 Pyatnitskaya str., Moscow, 119017, Moscow, Russia

Abstract
Guitar nebula is a prime example of a class of bow-shock pulsar wind nebulae (PWNe), powered by a wind of a supersonically
moving neutron star. Bow-shock PWNe can probe particle acceleration processes in relativistic pulsar winds, as well as the structure
of the interstellar medium (ISM). We demonstrate that the Guitar is an exceptional object in a number of ways. First, particles
escaping the PWN and forming the X-ray “kinetic jet” need to be accelerated to the energies corresponding to the maximal electric
potential of the neutron star ηacc ≳3/4 : it is another example of the class of extreme accelerators. Second, exceptionally bright
Hα emission requires that the central pulsar PSR J2225+6535 passes through a dense, low ionization ISM region. Bright X-ray
emission of the “kinetic jet” then also requires exceptionally high magnetic field, ∼100 µG. We hypothesize that Guitar passes
through the one of long-predicted, narrow dense shells of an old supernova remnant, currently in the “pressure-driven snowplow”
regime.
Keywords: supernova remnants, galactic cosmic rays, pulsars, interstellar medium
1. Introduction
Pulsars produce ultrarelativistic winds; their interaction with
surrounding media forms Pulsar Wind Nebulae (PWNe), which
shine from radio to hard gamma-rays band (Aharonian, 2004;
Gaensler and Slane, 2006).
A particular type of PWNe is formed when a fast moving
pulsar escapes the preceding supernova ejecta. Interaction of
the pulsar wind with the interstellar medium (ISM) then forms
bow-shock PWNe (Barkov et al., 2019a; Olmi and Bucciantini,
2019). Bow-shock PWNe (BSPWNe) can be used to probe the
structure of ISM on scales down to the the stand-off distance (a
distance between a pulsar and a bow shock apex, ∼1016 cm,
Equation (1)) (Nikonorov et al., 2025; Barkov et al., 2020,
2019b).
In modeling BSPWNe, various, seemingly unrelated phe-
nomena should be taken into account: overall morphology (in-
cluding possible density variations in the external medium),
internal geometry of the pulsar (relative direction of the spin
and velocity, magnetic inclination angle - bullet, frisbee and
cartwheel geometries, magnetic inclination angle and magnetic
hoop stresses in the pulsar wind) (Barkov et al., 2019a), parti-
cle acceleration in PWNe, kinetic jets produced by BSPWNe
(Bandiera, 2008; Barkov et al., 2019b; Barkov and Lyutikov,
2019), and, specifically, dynamic (time and spatially depen-
dent) Hα emission (Nikonorov et al., 2025). The resulting self-
consistent model should allow us to assess parameters at the
bow-shock apex and the properties of pulsar wind.
In this paper, we argue that Guitar PWN, associated with
PSR J2225+6535, is exceptional in not one, but two ways:

(i) it accelerates particles up to the theoretical maximum (it is
an example of an extreme accelerator); (ii) it currently passes
through a highly unusual ISM phase (high total density, low
ionization, high magnetic field), produced by a previous super-
nova explosion, unrelated to the pulsar powering the Guitar.
The plan of the work is as follows. In §2 we review the struc-
ture of BSPWN, and expected high-energy and Hα signatures
and outline connections between them. In §3 we show, that
all of the observed PWNe, except Guitar, fall in line with ex-
pectations: Guitar Nebula stands out both in X-rays and in Hα
emission. In §4 we offer a plausible explanation (that the cen-
tral pulsar passes through a special layer in the ISM, formed by
an old supernova remnant (SNR) in the snowplow regime), and
explain the tension between expected and observed dispersion
measure of pulsar with our scenario and give predictions on the
rotation measure. §5 concludes the manuscript.

4.1. Pulsar inside the unrelated SNR
The general picture is introduced in Figure 3. We suggest
that the pulsar is now moving through an old SNR, which is
currently in the pressure-driven snowplow regime (Cox, 1972;
Chevalier, 1974). At the current SNR age, approximately 30-50
kyrs, the hot tenuous central part sustains relatively high pres-
sure, approximately at the Sedov level, while the denser shell is
in the cooling snowplow regime. This regime is usually referred
to as “pressure-driven snowplow” (Chevalier, 1977).
In particular,
• A supernova unrelated to PSR J2225+6535 exploded ∼
30 −50 kyrs ago, in a common warm neutral medium
(nISM ∼1 cm−3, BISM ∼1µG, βISM ∼30, ionization degree
χISM ∼10−6). SNR expanded to Rfront ∼20 −50 pc;
• At the SNR forward shock (hereafter denoted by index s),
the medium is ionized, magnetic field is compressed (βs ∼
103, χs ∼1);
• The shocked shell is in a strongly cooling regime, losing
thermal pressure to radiation. At the same time, a hot and
rarified central part keeps high pressure. As a result, shell
material is compressed, together with the frozen-in mag-
netic field;
• The cooled "compressed zone" (CZ) becomes cold, mostly
neutral and sandwiched between hot interior and hot
shocked exterior zones. It is the pressure of the magnetic
field that stabilizes the compression. The thickness of the
shell is (1 −2) × 1017 cm;
• PSR J2225+6535 happens to traverse now the high den-
sity, high magnetic field CZ.

In regards to extreme acceleration by pulsars and PWNe, they
were proposed to be sources of very-high-energy (VHE; ≳100
GeV) gamma-rays (both extended halos and point sources). Ex-
amples include 1LHAASO J1740+0948u with spectrum ex-
tending up to 300 TeV, which is offset from the middle-aged
pulsar PSR J1740+1000, the possible source of particles, in
the direction of the tail of its X-ray nebula (Cao et al., 2025b;
Gagnon et al., 2026). Highly aged millisecond pulsar (MSP)
J0218+4232 can accelerate particles to no more than ∼1 PeV
(1015 eV) and is the only known potential source of them for
“Peanut” nebula, which was detected by LHAASO with pho-
ton energies up to 760 TeV and does not fit into halo templates
(Cao et al., 2025a). If this association is confirmed, not only
would “Peanut” have a huge size (250 pc × 25 pc), but the
particle acceleration efficiency in terms of maximum possible
energy would be extreme. The most striking example of ex-
treme acceleration is PSR J1849-0001 ( ˙E ∼1037 erg/s), known
to power its’ PWN visible in X-rays and VHE (The LHAASO
Collaboration, 2026; Alfaro et al., 2026; H. E. S. S. Collabora-
tion et al., 2023). The highest energy photon registered from it
is 2.3 PeV, which suggest acceleration efficiency close to 1 and
can shed light on the origin of the particles more energetic than
the cosmic ray “knee”. Our research adds PSR J2225+6535
(with ηacc ≳3/4) to the list of the sources with extremely ef-
ficient acceleration and suggests that the latter may be a usual
property of pulsars.
The escape of energetic particles from PWNe can occur in
the form other than kinetic jets, namely pulsar halos. They
are extended VHE emissions surrounding middle-aged pulsars
(τ ∼105 yr).
These structures, first unambiguously identi-
fied by the High-Altitude Water Cherenkov (HAWC) observa-
tory around the Geminga and Monogem pulsars (Abeysekara,
2017), exhibit gamma-ray emission extending up to PeV en-
ergies (Cao, 2021). Their discovery challenges conventional
models of particle acceleration and diffusion in pulsar wind
nebulae (PWNe), while offering new insights into the origin of
cosmic-ray (CR) electrons and positrons (e±).
In relation to the structure of ISM, the transition to the radia-
tive phase in supernova remnants has long been hypothesized
to lead to the catastrophic collapse of the postshock gas, and
formation of a thin, dense shell (e.g. Falle, 1981; Blondin et al.,
1998). It is expected that a shell would then be highly magne-
tized (we are not aware of any relevant MHD calculations). We
suggest that Guitar is currently traversing such a shell. The
presence of magnetic field may also resolve the puzzle: the
cooled shells are expected to be RT unstable (Vishniac, 1983;
Mac Low and Norman, 1993). We suggest that the compressed
magnetic field suppresses RT instabilities.
