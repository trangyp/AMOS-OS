GenAI-FDIA: Physics-Informed Generative Models
for False Data Injection Attacks
Mohammad A. Razzaque, Senior Member, IEEE, and Muta Tah Hira
Abstract—Training and evaluating false data injection attack
(FDIA) detectors for power systems is constrained by data
scarcity. Operational grid measurements are commercially sen-
sitive, and hand-crafted attacks fail to capture the distributional
structures imposed by network physics. We present GENAI-
FDIA, a novel framework benchmarking a pool of P=20
architectures for physics-compliant FDIA synthesis, spanning
Wasserstein GANs (Generative Adversarial Networks), MMD-
VAEs (Maximum Mean Discrepancy Variational Autoencoders),
normalising flows, diffusion models, and cross-family hybrids.
These are evaluated across three IEEE testbeds (14-bus DC, 30-
bus DC, and 14-bus AC) under a 60/20/20 chronological split us-
ing data-driven Bad Data Detection (BDD) threshold calibration.
All architectures achieve evasion rates of ϵBDD ≥86.6% on the
14-bus network, and limiting an attacker’s topological knowledge
induces a measurable degradation in stealthiness (p ≤0.0022).
We identify a previously unreported failure mode; applying
affine physics projections in normalised feature spaces critically
displaces the attack vector, collapsing BDD evasion from ∼55% to
<2% on the 30-bus testbed. We resolve this via an inference-time
harmoniser, restoring full stealthiness (ϵBDD=100%) across all
physics-informed variants without retraining. We further isolate
a covariance-collapse phenomenon (κ ≈−0.076) within hybrid
architectures and rectify it through 50-epoch warm-up schedules
(κ →0.785, ∆MMD = −3.1%). GENAI-FDIA delivers a recov-
ery blueprint applicable to any physics-constrained generative
model deployed for power-system security.
Index Terms—False data injection attacks, generative mod-
els, variational autoencoders, diffusion models, physics-informed
generation, power-system security, explainability.
I. INTRODUCTION
Modern power grids are simultaneously decarbonising, dig-
italising, and decentralising. Renewable generation now con-
tributes a significant fraction of electricity supply across major
regions [1] and is supported by distributed energy resources,
storage, and vehicle-to-grid infrastructure. The resulting cyber-
physical interdependencies have widened the attack surface
beyond what conventional security systems were designed to
monitor. Recent incidents, the BlackEnergy-driven distribution
outages in Ukraine in 2015 [2], the INDUSTROYER2 de-
ployment against Ukrainian high-voltage substations in 2022
[3], and the Volt Typhoon pre-positioning operations disclosed
inside US critical infrastructure [4], demonstrate that energy
infrastructure is now an explicit, well-resourced target.
State-estimation pipelines are particularly exposed. False
Data Injection Attacks (FDIAs) [5] exploit the algebraic

Undetected, such manipulation can drive suboptimal gener-

Detector quality depends directly on the diversity of attack
data on which detectors are trained. Real attacks are rare
and concealed; controlled testbed experiments cover only a
small fraction of the feasible attack space, and the formal
enumeration of all BDD-bypassing vectors scales poorly be-
yond small bus systems [7]. Generative AI models address
this scarcity by learning the statistical distribution of feasible
attacks. Conditional Wasserstein GANs trained on formally
stealthy corpora [7] reach >90% BDD evasion on the 14-bus
benchmark. Subsequent work specialised the GAN paradigm
with sparse adversarial and ResNet–FCN generators [8], least-
squares GANs [9], autoencoder-initialised generators for time-
varying topologies [10], time-series-coherent GANs [11], and
attention–diffusion hybrids [12]. Another set of work em-
beds AC or DC power-flow constraints directly into the syn-
thesis mechanism, including extrapolative adversarial VAEs
[13], physics-constrained GANs [14], and physics-informed
denoising diffusion [15]. ADMM-based synthesisers jointly
evade multi-label locational detectors [16], and high-renewable
scenarios expand the feasible attack space [17]. On the de-
tection side, sequential LSTM-autoencoders [18], [19], graph-
convolutional attention networks [20], transformer–LSTM hy-
brids [21], and adversarial anomaly transformers [22] now
raise the empirical evasion bar.
Despite this progress, three compounding limitations (L)
restrict the practical utility of existing pipelines. (L1) Each
published framework commits to a single generative archi-
tecture, with no mechanism for selecting the architecture
best suited to a given topology, dataset size, or measure-
ment representation; the across-family variance has not been
characterised on a common protocol. Nawaz et al. [23] re-
port the only prior multi-architecture comparison, but only
across two GAN variants on a single testbed. (L2) Attacker
topology knowledge is treated as a binary condition rather
than the continuous spectrum along which real adversaries
move [24]. (L3) Generated attacks are rarely subjected to
attribution analysis that would identify which sensors are
preferentially manipulated, information that is operationally
actionable for sensor hardening. Furthermore, prior physics-
informed approaches apply the projection in normalised fea-
ture space without analysing the geometric interaction with

II. METHODOLOGY
A. State estimation, BDD, and threat model
Power-system state estimation recovers bus-voltage angles
ˆx ∈RN−1 from measurements z ∈RM using the linear DC
model z = Hx+ε, ε∼N(0, R), with weighted least-squares
solution ˆx = (H⊤R−1H)−1H⊤R−1z [28]. BDD accepts the
measurement set when the weighted residual
J(z) = (z −Hˆx)⊤R−1(z −Hˆx)
(1)
falls below a χ2 threshold τ. Following [5], an FDIA c =
Hδ ∈col(H) biases the state estimate by δ while leaving
the residual unchanged. Components in col(H)⊥= ker(H⊤)
excite non-zero residual energy and are detectable. For 14-
bus DC, H ∈R54×13 partitions the measurement space into
a 13-dimensional stealthy subspace and a 41-dimensional
detectable complement; for 30-bus DC, H ∈R71×29 yields
a 29-dimensional stealthy subspace.
The adversary issues an injection c ∈RM producing
za

k∈[0, 1] controlling the fraction of H-rows revealed, assigned
to MITRE ATT&CK ICS sub-techniques as k=0 →T0817
(drive-by compromise), k=0.5 →T0842 (network sniffing),
k=1 →T0843 (program download) [25]. The objective is
to learn a conditional generator Gθ : Z × Rcdim →RM
approximating p(c | Hk), where Hk encodes the k-fraction of
H that the adversary observes.
B. Block-wise framework and chronological partition
The evaluation is divided into three blocks (Fig. 1). Block A
benchmarks the entire pool and applies Pareto-front selection.
Block B characterises the effect of attacker knowledge k on
a representative per-family triplet (one base, one PI- variant,
one cross-family PI- hybrid) drawn from each testbed; this de-
sign isolates knowledge-induced variance from family-induced
variance, which a strict Pareto-optimal subset (typically dom-
inated by a single family per testbed) cannot. Block C applies
cross-level XAI attribution to Pareto-optimal configurations.
To prevent the inflation of evasion rates that occurs when
generative training, detector training, and evasion evalua-
tion share data, every dataset is partitioned chronologically
(shuffle=false, seed=42) into three non-overlapping
segments: 60% Dgen, 20% Ddet, and 20% Deval.

E. Conditioning, Pareto selection, and warm-up
Continuous conditioning: The conditioning vector Hk ∈
Rcdim is constructed by flattening the first ⌊kM⌋rows of
H and zero-padding to cdim=M. Block B evaluates k ∈
{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}. Conditioning is injected by direct con-
catenation: the encoder and discriminator receive [a; Hk], the
generator receives [z; Hk], and the DDPM denoising network
receives Hk as an additional channel at every reverse-diffusion
step.
Pareto selection: Models are ranked over four objectives:
minimise MMD, maximise ϵBDD, maximise ϕ, and maximise
∆ˆxnorm (normalised attack impact). The fourth axis protects
against magnitude collapse: a generator with ∆ˆxnorm ≪1 is
statistically stealthy but operationally inconsequential.
1) TC β-warm-up schedule: For RealNVP-TC-MMD-VAE
the total-correlation weight β is linearly annealed from 0
to its target over the first 50 epochs. Applying full β from
epoch zero produces the covariance-collapse mode reported
in Section III-B; the warm-up restores κ to 0.785 at no
measurable cost in MMD.

B. Block A: 30-bus DC and physics-projection failure
On the 30-bus, FlowTS achieves the lowest MMD (0.0015±
0.0019) with ϵBDD=0.972 ± 0.016, making it the dominant
Pareto point under the broken PHYSICSWRAPPER; the base
DDPM follows (MMD=0.0113, ϵBDD=0.912). The headline
finding on this testbed is the physics-projection failure mode of
Lemma 1. Eight of the ten PI variants collapse to ϵBDD <30%
(mean 2.4%); the two TC-based PI variants remain at 100%
because their latent TC structure incidentally generates sam-
ples close to col(H) in physical units. After the harmoniser
is applied (Table III), all ten PI- variants reach ϵBDD=1.000
and the Pareto frontier is reshaped, PI-FlowTS (MMD 0.0016)
and PI-LSTM-WGAN (MMD 0.0032) join the non-dominated
set alongside FlowTS. The collapse is absent in the 14-bus
DC because that testbed’s calibrated τ is more permissive
relative to residual inflation. LSTM-AE evasion is zero for
all 20 models, indicating that the 30-bus LSTM-AE has learnt
temporal patterns that no model evades without retraining.
The 14-bus DC testbed masks the failure because its cal-
ibrated τ is permissive enough that imperfect projections
remain below it, and LSTM-bearing architectures benefit from
temporal modelling that independently improves evasion. The

F. Ablation summary
The four ablation conditions (no physics, no selection, no
conditioning, no temporal) were trained from scratch with
three seeds for 200 epochs on three PI-probes across all
testbeds. The two non-trivial conditions are summarised here;
no-selection and no conditioning produce ∆MMD ≈0 at
the k=0 default. No-physics on the DC testbeds increases
MMD by 26% (14-bus) and 18% (30-bus) for PI-LSTM-
MMD-VAE-WGAN, while leaving the TC and flow probes
nearly unchanged. On 14-bus AC, the sign reverses: removing
the physics projection reduces MMD by 78% for PI-LSTM-
MMD-VAE-WGAN and by 32% for PI-RealNVP. The DC-
derived H embedded in PHYSICSWRAPPER introduces a
systematic mismatch under 68-dimensional AC phasor mea-
surements, and disabling it improves fidelity. The no-temporal
effect produces the largest single-component impact: remov-
ing the LSTM encoder increases MMD by 294% on 14-
bus DC and 591% on 30-bus DC for PI-LSTM-MMD-VAE-
WGAN. The same removal is negligible on 14-bus AC, where
the 35,130-sample corpus provides sufficient signal for non-
temporal architectures (Fig. 6).

I. Limitations
PHYSICSWRAPPER uses the DC power-flow linearisation,
which ignores reactive power, voltage-magnitude variation,
and transformer tap positions; the 14-bus AC testbed already
shows that the DC projection corrupts imaginary phasor com-
ponents. All three testbeds use simulator output; field PMU
recordings were unavailable due to commercial confidentiality.
LSTM-AE evasion is zero for all 20 models on 30-bus DC, re-

IV. CONCLUSION
GENAI-FDIA is a unified framework for benchmark-
ing physics-informed generative FDIA synthesis. A pool
of P=20 architectures spanning Wasserstein GANs, MMD-
VAE, normalising flows, and score-based diffusion, includ-
ing four novel cross-family hybrids (MMD-VAE-WGAN,
LSTM-MMD-VAE-WGAN, TC-MMD-VAE, RealNVP-TC-
MMD-VAE), is evaluated on three IEEE benchmarks (14-bus
DC, 30-bus DC, 14-bus AC with 68-dimensional phasor mea-
surements) under a strict 60/20/20 chronological partition with
data-driven detector calibration. The four hybrids each yield
a measurable gain over their parents; MMD-VAE-WGAN
(MMD 0.035 on 14-bus DC) is 6.7× tighter than the MMD-
VAE base (0.236) and 3.5× tighter than WGAN (0.124), con-
firming that the adversarial discriminator sharpens the encoder
posterior; LSTM-MMD-VAE-WGAN is Pareto-optimal on 14-
bus DC at MMD 0.023, a 4.2× improvement over LSTM-
MMD-VAE (0.097); RealNVP-TC-MMD-VAE leads the 14-
bus AC frontier (W1=0.0104, the lowest of all 20 models)
ahead of TC-MMD-VAE (0.0107) and the MMD-VAE parent
(0.0108), and the addition of total-correlation disentanglement
to the latent yields κ=1.000 cross-level agreement on 30-bus
DC after β-warm-up.
The main contributions are: (i) a multi-model Pareto
pool whose non-dominated subset varies across testbeds (PI-
DDPM, LSTM-WGAN, and LSTM-MMD-VAE-WGAN on
14-bus DC; FlowTS, PI-FlowTS, and PI-LSTM-WGAN on
30-bus DC after the harmoniser; RealNVP-TC-MMD-VAE,
TC-MMD-VAE, and MMD-VAE on 14-bus AC), supporting
the rejection of a single architectural choice; (ii) a formal
geometric characterisation of the µ /∈col(H) failure mode
that displaces inverse-scaled physics-projected attacks outside
the stealthy subspace, together with an inference-time har-
moniser that restores ϵBDD=100% for all ten PI- variants
without retraining; (iii) the identification of a total-correlation
covariance-collapse mode and its resolution via a 50-epoch
β-warm-up schedule that lifts cross-level attribution agree-
ment from below-chance to substantial; and (iv) a continu-
ous attacker-knowledge axis k ∈[0, 1] mapped to MITRE
ATT&CK ICS sub-techniques, under which W1 increases
monotonically by 1.6–49.2× from k=0 to k=1 at strong effect
sizes (η2 ≥0.636).
Empirically, every pool member achieves ϵBDD ≥86.6% on
the 14-bus DC; the residual test can be defeated by every
generative family. Physics-informed wrapping raises ϕ by
2.6× on the 14-bus, but under the broken normalised-space
projection collapses BDD evasion to < 30% for eight of ten
PI-variants on the 30-bus; the harmoniser restores 100% for
all ten on every testbed. Temporal modelling is the dominant
ablation driver (∆MMD up to +591% on the 30-bus DC
when LSTM layers are removed). Cross-level XAI agreement
reaches κ=1.000 on both DC testbeds with the harmoniser
and β-warm-up applied. For practitioners, we recommend PI-
LSTM-WGAN (ϵBDD=100%, best physics consistency) or
PI-DDPM (lowest MMD) as primary candidates on the 14-
bus DC. For larger networks, PI-variants must apply the
harmoniser in physical units rather than in normalised feature
space. Three directions follow directly: a differentiable AC
power-flow constraint layer to remove the DC approximation,
an end-to-end evasion-training scheme via a differentiable
surrogate BDD loss, and validation on field PMU recordings.
