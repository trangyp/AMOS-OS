Geometry of weak contact conics to irreducible quartics
with 2 nodes and 1 cusp via rational elliptic surfaces and
Zariski pairs
Khulan TUMENBAYAR
Abstract. Let Q be an irreducible quartic with two nodes and one cusp as its singularities and
let C be a conic such that the intersection multiplicity at each point of C ∩Q is even and C ∩Q
contain at least one smooth point zo of Q. In this paper, for every Q we ﬁnd all possible conics C as
above via studying geometry of C and Q through that of integral sections of a rational elliptic surface
which canonically arises from Q and zo ∈C ∩Q. As an application, we construct Zariski pairs of
degree 7 and degree 8, whose irreducible components consist of Q, C and line passing through two
of the singular points of Q .
Keywords: Elliptic surface, section, weak contact conic, Zariski pair

Introduction
Let B be a reduced plane curve and let C be a smooth conic in P2. Let Ix(B, C) denote the
intersection multiplicity at x ∈B ∩C. We ﬁrst deﬁne a weak contact conic as follows:
Deﬁnition 1.1 If Ix(B, C) is even for ∀x ∈B ∩C, then C is called a weak contact conic to
B. Moreover if ∀x ∈B ∩C is a smooth point of B and Ix(B, C) is even, then C is called a
contact conic to B.
In [9] and [10], we studied contact conics to 3-nodal and 3-cuspidal quartics via geometry
of rational elliptic surfaces. In this article, we continue to study geometry of contact conics
to an irreducible quartic Q along this line. More precisely, we study weak contact conics to
an irreducible quartic Q with 2 nodes and 1 cusp only as its singularities. In this article, we
restrict ourselves to the case when C is a weak contact conic to Q such that Q ∩C contains
at least one smooth point zo of Q . Even under such restriction, we still obtain non-trivial
examples of Zariski pairs, which we explain later. We ﬁrst consider the following problem:

Preliminaries
2.1
Construction of irreducible quartics with 2 nodes and 1 cusp
Let [T, X, Z] be homogeneous coordinates of P2. Let Q be the standard quadratic trans-
formation or the standard Cremona transformation with respect to {T = 0}, {X = 0} and
{Z = 0}. We call [0, 0, 1], [0, 1, 0] and [1, 0, 0], the fundamental points with respect to Q.
Lemma 2.1
(i) Let C be a conic not passing through any of the fundamental points and
tangent to {Z = 0}, but not tangent to {T = 0} and {X = 0} in P2. Then Q(C)
is a quartic whose singularities are only 1 cusp at [0, 0, 1] and 2 nodes at [0, 1, 0] and
[1, 0, 0].
(ii) Let L be the line tangent to C at a point P = [T0, X0, Z0] ∈C, where T0X0Z0 ̸= 0.
If L does not contain any of the fundamental points, then Q(L) is a conic tangent to
Q(C) at Q(P) = [X0Z0, T0Z0, T0X0] and passes through the fundamental points.
(iii) Let L be the line tangent to C at a point P = [T0, X0, Z0] ∈C, where T0X0Z0 ̸= 0. If
L pass through one of the fundamental points, then Q(L) is a line passing through that
fundamental point.
(iv) Let L be conic, that contains the fundamental points, then Q(L) is a line.
(v) If x ∈P2 \ {fundamental points}, then Ix(C, L) = IQ(x)(Q(C), Q(L)).
Since all of these statements are well-known, we omit their proofs.
We make use of
Lemma 2.1 when we consider explicit examples.

Elliptic Surfaces

a ﬁbration ϕ : S →C over a smooth projective curve, C, as follows:
