Close hyperbolic encounters between black holes produce distinctive bursts of gravitational radiation with
a time-frequency morphology that is qualitatively different from that of quasi-circular inspirals. Expected to
arise in dense stellar environments through dynamical interactions, these encounters probe formation channels
and mass ranges inaccessible to isolated binary evolution, making them a compelling target for current and
next-generation detectors. In this work, we reanalyze 38 high-mass events from the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA
catalogs using the hyperbolic configuration of the TEOBResumS-Dal´ı waveform model. We compare these
with analyses using the quasi-circular, precessing configuration of the same model, computing Bayes factors
to evaluate which description is favored by the data. We find that most events strongly to mildly favor the
quasi-circular, precessing scenario, except for GW190521. For this event, we find that the signal is best fit by a
dynamical capture waveform, with Bayes factor ln Bhyp
prec = 3.71+0.11
−0.11. We confirm this preference via further
analyses with TEOBResumS-Dal´ı in different configurations (quasi-circular, non-precessing; eccentric, non-
precessing; and eccentric, precessing), as well as one using the quasi-circular, precessing numerical relativity
surrogate model NRSur7dq4. We also highlight the results we obtain for GW231123, another high-mass signal
linked to evidence of strong precession, for which we find strong preference for the quasi-circular, precessing
scenario, with ln Bhyp

corresponding scenario, the GW190521-like signals did so only marginally.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Over 200 gravitational wave (GW) signals have been re-
ported so far by the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA Collaboration
(LVK) [1].
The majority of these astrophysical events are
associated with binary black hole (BBH) mergers in bound
systems; several binaries involving neutron stars have also
been detected [2, 3]. Pulsar timing observations have led to
the detection of a stochastic gravitational-wave (GW) back-
ground, widely interpreted as originating from an ensemble of
supermassive black hole (BH) binaries [4]. GW observations
by current observatories provide insight into the strong-field
regime of gravity, allowing tests of general relativity[5, 6],
observation of the population mass profile [5, 6], and con-
straints on the neutron star equation of state [2], among other
pursuits [7–9].
Binary bound systems that culminate in merger, collec-
tively known as compact binary coalescences (CBCs), are
ideal sources for ground-based laser interferometers [10] be-
cause the characteristic frequencies of lighter binaries typi-
cally fall within their 10–1000 Hz sensitivity band. However,
current observatories have limited sensitivity at the lower end
of this range, creating ambiguity in detections unless system-
atics are properly modeled [11]. This low-frequency regime
is nonetheless important, as it opens the possibility of ob-
serving heavier-mass binaries, such as GW190521 [12] and
GW231123 [13]. In particular, in the sub-hertz (< 1 Hz) band,
one may even detect binaries on highly eccentric (e > 0.8) or
fly-by orbits, including those involving BHs. These events
may result in several outcomes: they may be hyperbolic en-
counters, meaning they scatter once without subsequent inter-
action, or become bound in a highly eccentric orbit, result-
ing in multiple fly-bys and subsequent merger (zoom-whirl
orbit), or they may collapse directly into a plunge.
These
systems release gravitational radiation during close encoun-
ters as GW bremsstrahlung [14–17]. These dynamical sys-
tems have a rich morphological structure [18–20] which be-
comes even richer with the inclusion of higher-order modes.
Moreover, the time-frequency content of GW signals in this
scenario is morphologically distinct compared to those from
quasi-circular (QC) systems [21–24], manifesting in laser in-
terferometers as characteristic blip-like profiles in time, with
broadband frequency.
Although no detections have been confirmed to date, ec-
centric binaries may be relevant sources in densely packed
clusters or near active galactic nuclei; it has been estimated
that at least BBH hyperbolic encounters could be common
given simulations and event rate estimates [25–27]. If de-
tected, BBHs in hyperbolic or zoom-whirl orbits could there-
fore provide key insight into the population of BHs and other
compact objects, and help detail a more complete picture of
the stochastic gravitational-wave background [28, 29]. GW
bremsstrahlung, detection rates peaking in the 1Hz range,
is therefore relevant for A# [30] and next generation detec-
tors like Cosmic Explorer (CE) [31] and Einstein Telescope
(ET) [32].

Dense stellar environments naturally assemble binaries,
trigger close encounters, and produce hierarchical mergers
that populate BH masses inaccessible to single-star evolution
[33–36], accumulating BH mergers within the pair-instability
mass gap, whose boundaries are set by the physical stability
of very massive stars (∼50 −120M⊙). In this range, the heat
inside the core can lead to photon conversion into electron-
postitron pairs, reducing radiation pressure within the star.
This can in turn cause the violent ejection of much of the mass
through oxygen burning, sometimes leading to complete dis-
ruption through pair-instability supernovae [37, 38]. Below
this range of masses, stars are too small to trigger pair pro-
duction and collapse normally, while above it, the cores are
so massive that pair production only speeds up their direct
collapse into BHs. Dynamical formation channels are natural
factories of binaries that may populate the otherwise forbid-
den pair-instability mass gap, making GW bremsstrahlung a
potentially important probe of mass-gap mergers [33–36] as
well as a source of information on the BBH formation chan-
nels in dense environments [39, 40]. [41, 42] present the first
computation of the power emitted from close hyperbolic en-
counters to first order.
Parameter estimation (PE) techniques for gravitational-
wave sources have almost exclusively focused on BBH merg-
ers in bound orbits, with a few exceptions. The first analy-
sis of a GW signal to consider unbound orbits was presented
in [43], who used the TEOBResumS-Dal´ı waveform model
in its hyperbolic configuration. Assuming non-spinning BHs
and using only the dominant quadrupolar modes (2, ±2), the
authors there analyzed the high-mass GW190521 event, find-
ing the hyperbolic description to be favored by the data over a
QC, precessing one by a Bayes factor (BF) of 4300 to 1, with-
out taking astrophysical prior odds into account. Additionally,
Fontbut´e et al. [44] produced an accurate numerical relativ-
ity (NR) surrogate for hyperbolic encounters of equal mass
spin-aligned BBHs with higher order modes. However, they
struggled to correctly recover the parameters of synthetic sig-
nals in PE. More recently, some of the present authors showed
in [23] accurate recovery of the parameters of synthetic sig-
nals from direct plunges and scattering encounters analyzed
with RIFT [45–48], an algorithm that performs Rapid param-
eter inference on gravitational wave sources via Iterative FiT-
ting, and modeled via TEOBResumS-Dal´ı [49–56].
Motivated by these two previous studies on hyperbolic
PE, we present in this work the re-analyses of 38
GW
signals sourced by BHs in the pair-instability mass gap
using the hyperbolic and precessing, QC configurations
of TEOBResumS-Dal´ı. For the each event, we include non-
precessing spins in the hyperbolic analysis, as well as higher-
order modes (specifically, the (2, ±2), (2, ±1), (3, ±3), and
(4, ±4) modes in the co-precessing frame).
To strengthen
our results, for two highlighted events, GW190521 and
GW231123, we present additional analyses including eccen-
tricity and/or spin-precession using TEOBResumS-Dal´ı,
and precessing runs with the NRSur7dq4 [57] model.
This paper is organized as follows. In Section II we intro-
duce the parameter space of BBHs on unbound orbits consid-
ered in this work, the TEOBResumS-Dal´ı GW model, the
PE code used RIFT, and detail the settings we employ in the
analyses. In Section III, we describe the results of our analy-
ses, presenting our catalog of 38 events, including the deeper
study of the two notable cases mentioned above. We conclude
in Section IV with a summary and interpretations of our find-
ings.

F.
Follow-up maximum likelihood synthetic injections
To investigate our ability to actually measure evidence of
hyperbolic systems, we carry out analyses of two synthetic
signals for each of GW190521 and GW231123: the maxi-
mum likelihood waveform from both the hyperbolic and the
precessing analyses.
Table III lists the parameters of the
four waveforms. We project the GW190521-like signals into
a three detector network comprising LIGO-Hanford, LIGO-
Livingston, and Virgo; we consider only the LIGO detectors
for the GW231123-like signals, to match the real event. We
add no noise when projecting each signal onto the detectors,
so as to focus on studying actual biases and systematics, with-
out random noise fluctuations. For all these injections, we
perform PE with TEOBResumS-Dal´ı in the hyperbolic and
QC, precessing configurations.
The results of these analyses are presented in Figures 5
and 6, for the GW190521-like and GW231123-like signals,
respectively. For all four of the injections, the matched analy-
ses (meaning, those carried out with the same BBH configura-
tion as the injected signal) correctly recovered the true values
of most of the parameters, with only the χeff posterior of the
matched hyperbolic GW231123-like run lying just outside the
90% CI. Due to the extremal spins of the injection and moder-
ate prior assumptions used in the analysis, the shift of the pos-
terior away from the true value is unsurprising. As expected,
the mismatched analyses’ results (i.e., hyperbolic analyses of
a precessing signal and vice versa) are instead biased from the
true values, in some cases severely. For χeff, the mass ratio,
and the total mass, QC, precessing analyses of hyperbolic sig-
nals consistently return posteriors shifted toward lower values
than the true. Mirroring this, results from hyperbolic analy-
ses of QC, precessing waveforms are biased to higher values
for the cases of χeff and M. Estimates of q in these analyses
do not follow a similarly consistent pattern; the multimodal,
highly biased posterior obtained in the mismatched analysis
of the precessing GW231123-like injection particularly stands
out.

of the correct scenario, we calculate the BFs contrasting
each pair of analyses; these are listed in Table II. For the
GW231123-like signals, we find in each case strong prefer-
ence for the correct scenarios, suggesting these waveforms
are significantly different from each other.

in Section III E, a strong preference for precessing systems

only marginal preference instead for the matched analyses of
