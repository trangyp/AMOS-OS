GAUSSIAN PROCESSES WITH SAMPLE PATHS IN
REPRODUCING KERNEL BANACH SPACES
TONI KARVONEN AND RASMUS K. H. SØRENSEN
Abstract. We investigate the connection between Gaussian processes and
Gaussian random elements in reproducing kernel Banach spaces. We show that
the covariance operator of a weak second-order Radon probability measure on
such a space is uniquely determined by a positive definite function. In the
Gaussian case, we characterize those positive definite functions that arise from
covariance operators in terms of γ-radonifying operators. Building on these
results, we extend the classical Driscoll theorem to the Banach space setting.
1. Introduction
Let (Ω, A, µ) be a complete probability space and ξ : T × Ω→R a centered
Gaussian process with covariance function K. For such a random process, questions
concerning the regularity of its sample paths arise immediately. A classical result
asserts that the sample paths of ξ belong to the reproducing kernel Hilbert space
H(K) of K with probability zero whenever H(K) is infinite-dimensional.
This
result was originally stated without proof by Parzen [17, eq. (34)], and later proved
by Kallianpur [12] and Le Page [14].
This motivated Driscoll [7] to ask whether there exists another reproducing kernel
Hilbert space H(R) that contains the sample paths of ξ almost surely.
Under
appropriate assumptions, Driscoll proved the zero–one law
(1.1)
µ(ξ ∈H(R)) = 0
or
µ(ξ ∈H(R)) = 1,
and established a necessary and sufficient condition for µ(ξ ∈H(R)) = 1 to hold.
The condition given by Driscoll is equivalent to requiring that H(K) ⊆H(R) and
that the operator L : H(R) →H(R), defined by LR(·, t) = K(·, t) for all t ∈T, is
nuclear. Fortet [9] subsequently introduced the terminology that R dominates K if
H(K) ⊆H(R), and that R n-dominates K if, in addition, L is nuclear. In modern
terminology, this is commonly referred to as nuclear dominance.
The assumptions made by Driscoll in the original paper are that T is a separable
metric space and that the sample paths of ξ are continuous with respect to the
metric topology on T. Driscoll describes the latter condition as “a rather restrictive
assumption, even for Gaussian processes” [7, p. 309]. Nevertheless, this assumption
allows ξ to be regarded as a random element taking values in the space C(T) of

Luki´c and Beder [15] later proved that none of Driscoll’s additional assumptions

The crux of their analysis is the canonical metric on T induced

the nuclear dominance assumption, H(R) may be assumed to be separable, and
hence T admits a countable dense subset. Random elements in H(R) can therefore

random process ξ taking values in H(R). Thereby the measurability difficulties
that motivated Driscoll’s additional assumptions can be resolved by reducing the
problem to this countable dense subset of T, where these difficulties do not arise.
In this paper, we extend the analysis of Luki´c and Beder [15] to the Banach space
setting and generalize Driscoll’s theorem to separable reproducing kernel Banach
spaces. Our main results are Theorems 5.6 and 5.7, which establish the “one” and
“zero” parts of Driscoll’s theorem, respectively. These theorems are summarized
in the following theorem using γ-radonifying operators [11, 21]. The γ-radonifying
operators can be thought of as operators that map “white noise” in a Hilbert space
into a well-defined Gaussian random element in a Banach space (see Theorem 3.14).
Theorem 1.1. Let (Ω, A, µ) be a complete probability space, let X be a separable
reproducing kernel Banach space, and let ξ : T × Ω→R be a centered Gaussian
process with covariance function K and associated Hilbert space H(K).
(1) If H(K) ⊆X and the embedding ι : H(K) →X is γ-radonifying, then there
exists a centered Gaussian random element η : Ω→X such that ξt = ηt
almost surely for all t ∈T.
(2) If H(K) ̸⊆X or if the embedding ι : H(K) →X is not γ-radonifying, then
µ({ω ∈Ω: ξ(·, ω) ∈X}) = 0.
Remark 1.2. Due to subtle measurability issues discussed in Section 5 we purposely
avoid statements such as “ξ has sample paths almost surely in X”.
Similar to Luki´c and Beder [15], a cornerstone of our analysis is the connection
between random elements in reproducing kernel Banach spaces and the associated
stochastic processes. We show that the covariance operator of a (weak) second-order
Radon probability measure on such a space is uniquely determined by a positive def-
inite function. In particular, we characterize those positive definite functions that
arise as covariance functions of Radon Gaussian measures. This result replaces the
Prokhorov theorem used in Driscoll’s original paper [7, Theorem 2]. Moreover, our
analysis provides a perspective on kernel-based methods that is more natural when
considering pushforwards under measurable mappings between Banach spaces.
Several authors have established γ-radonifying embeddings of reproducing kernel
Hilbert spaces associated with Gaussian processes into classical function spaces.
A fundamental result due to Brze´zniak [5, Theorem 3.6] shows that the indefinite
integration operator is γ-radonifying as an operator from L2(0, 1) into the fractional
Sobolev space W α,p(0, 1) for p ∈(2, ∞) and α ∈(1/p, 1/2). This yields fractional
Sobolev regularity, and hence H¨older regularity, of Brownian motion. The results
in [5] are formulated in terms of almost sure convergence of Gaussian series rather
than in the language of γ-radonifying operators. Further details, refinements, and
results on Besov regularity can be found in [5] and the references therein.

4. Reproducing Kernel Banach Spaces
Natural candidates for sample path spaces of random processes are function
spaces that are continuously embedded into RT . Such spaces are characterized by
the continuity of pointwise evaluation.
Definition 4.1. A Banach space X ⊆RT is called a reproducing kernel Banach
space if for every t ∈T, the functional δt : X →R, x 7→x(t) is continuous.
If X is a Hilbert space it is called a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. Throughout
this section, X ⊆RT denotes a reproducing kernel Banach space with norm ∥· ∥,
and δt : X →R denotes the evaluation functional at t ∈T.
We show that covariance operators are uniquely determined by positive definite
functions and characterize those positive definite functions that arise as kernels of
Gaussian covariance operators. For a discussion of the role of reproducing kernel
Banach spaces in machine learning, we refer to [3] and the references therein.

dimensional Hilbert spaces. As an immediate consequence, we obtain the following
classical result, originally stated by Parzen [17, eq. 34] and subsequently proved by
Kallianpur [12, Theorem 5.1] and Le Page [14].
Corollary 5.9 (Parzen). Let ξ : T × Ω→R be a centered Gaussian process with
covariance function K. If H(K) is infinite-dimensional then
(5.2)
µ({ω ∈Ω: ξ(·, ω) ∈H(K)}) = 0.
