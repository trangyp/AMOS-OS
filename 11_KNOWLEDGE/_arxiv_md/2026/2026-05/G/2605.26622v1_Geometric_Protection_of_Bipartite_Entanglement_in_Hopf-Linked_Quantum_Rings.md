Geometric Protection of Bipartite Entanglement in Hopf-Linked Quantum Rings
V. Yogesh1, ∗and Prosenjit Maity2, †
1Department of Physics, Government Arts College, Nandanam, Chennai - 600 035, India
2Department of Physics, Ramakrishna Mission Residential College, Narendrapur, Kolkata - 700 103, India
We determine the bipartite entanglement bounds of two interacting electrons in deeply interlocked
Hopf-linked quantum rings via exact diagonalization of the unexpanded 3D Coulomb interaction.
This identifies an exact continuous spatial symmetry that geometrically isolates the positive-parity
Bell state, preventing classical interaction-driven localization. A non-coplanar geometric tilt (α > 0)
is essential to lift the exchange degeneracy and maintain this maximally entangled manifold as a
state of frozen entanglement. However, a higher-order Schrieffer-Wolff transformation demonstrates
this geometric protection is fundamentally bounded; uncancelled inter-orbital momentum transitions
inevitably induce dynamical parity mixing. This defines a critical interaction threshold (λcrit) for
irreversible entanglement collapse. Our analysis shows that the resulting bounding conditions reveal
scaling limitations in mesoscopic semiconductor architectures, dictating the necessity of synthetic
macroscopic platforms to achieve robust topological protection.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Spatial asymmetries in solid-state architectures in-
evitably break kinetic degeneracies, causing highly en-
tangled superpositions to collapse into classical localized
states [1–4]. While topological invariants [5, 6] can en-
gineer decoherence-resistant manifolds, utilizing them
for bipartite entanglement remains severely restricted by
inter-particle Coulomb repulsion [7, 8]. To map the fun-
damental boundaries of interaction-driven entanglement
collapse without relying on perturbative constraints, we
introduce a minimal geometric model on a Hopf-linked
topology: two interacting electrons confined to spatially
separated, non-coplanar 1D quantum rings.
Classical Aharonov-Bohm setups rely on macroscopic
magnetic solenoids that obstruct the ring’s interior, heav-
ily limiting physical proximity to the weakly interlocked
regime (β > 1). To overcome this, we employ a local non-
Abelian Rashba spin-orbit field projected into an effective
1D synthetic gauge field [9]. This isolates orthogonal spins,
lifting the identical-fermion restriction and restoring the
positive-parity manifold. Significantly, this intrinsic geo-
metric mechanism leaves the ring’s interior as an absolute
vacuum, enabling access to the deeply interlocked topo-
logical regime (0 < β < 1) without physical collision.
By comprehensively diagonalizing the unexpanded 3D
Coulomb interaction in this strongly coupled near-field
limit, we uncover a generalized anti-unitary symmetry
that protects the entangled ground state. We show that
this geometric protection is bounded by a critical inter-
action strength (λcrit), beyond which unavoidable parity
mixing induces Wigner localization.

VI.
PHYSICAL IMPLEMENTATION AND
PHASE DIAGRAM
To translate the dimensionless parameters (λ, β, α) into
physical metrics, we evaluate a mesoscopic GaAs archi-
tecture (m∗≈0.067me). Survival of the geometric entan-
glement requires λ ≪λcrit. Furthermore, validating the
independent local gauge assumption at extreme near-field
proximity requires exponential suppression of evanescent
tunneling (Jdirect ≪2|W|) [23–25]. This necessitates an
absolute hard-wall barrier via direct vacuum suspension
to exponentially suppress the overlap-induced Heisenberg
exchange Jdirect ≈Ec exp(−2κdgap) (Appendix H).
In a bare GaAs environment (ϵr ≈12.9), a ring of
R = 15 nm yields λ ≈1.47. This starkly exceeds the criti-
cal threshold (λ > λcrit) driven by near-field amplification
of ucross, causing immediate parity collapse and explic-
itly violating the perturbative assumption (Ec ≪∆Ekin)
necessary for the Schrieffer-Wolff transformation. To re-
store the validity of the weakly mixed topological domain,
strong dielectric screening is mandatory. Utilizing highly
proximate high-κ top-gate screening [26, 27] elevates the
local dielectric constant (ϵeff ≈50). For a scaled R = 10
nm ring, the parameter drops to λ ≈0.25, safely satisfy-
ing the perturbative bounds and pushing the system deep
into the protected quantum domain mapped in Fig. 5(b).
Notably, as the system is pushed to extreme geometric in-
terlocking (β < 0.2), maintaining the protected entangled
phase dictates that the geometric tilt (α) must be corre-
spondingly increased to counteract heightened Coulomb
interactions.
Isolating the kinetic degeneracy necessitates a Rashba
coupling of λR =
√
3ℏ2/(2m∗R) ≈9.85×10−11 eV·m. As
this requisite magnitude vastly exceeds typical intrinsic
spin-orbit strengths in standard GaAs heterostructures
[28], extreme structural inversion asymmetry is required.
Concurrently, a locally compensated non-collinear anti-
ferromagnetic texture must provide an exchange splitting
2J ≫Ec without inducing macroscopic dipole cross-talk
[29–31].
Given that realizing these tailored magnetic tex-
tures atop vacuum-suspended, non-coplanar architectures
strains the absolute limits of current solid-state lithogra-
phy, this framework presents a minimal geometric model
to describe the foundational bounds of parity mixing
caused by the Schrieffer-Wolff transformation.
These
bounding conditions reinforce the transition toward syn-
thetic platforms, such as superconducting circuit QED
resonators [32] or ultracold atoms in optical tweezers [33],
where interaction distances and synthetic gauge fields can
be robustly engineered to preserve geometric protection.
VII.
CONCLUSION
By quantifying the bipartite entanglement limits in
continuously parameterized Hopf-linked geometries, we
established a precise boundary for interaction-driven en-
tanglement collapse. We demonstrated that static, non-
perturbative geometry-induced protection of this continu-
ous bipartite state is intrinsically bounded. Perfect spatial
symmetry suppresses classical localizing asymmetries by
spectrally isolating the ground state. However, the gen-
eralized anti-unitary symmetry group governing the full
interaction prohibits the cancellation of odd inter-orbital
momentum transfers. This defines a quantum-to-classical
phase boundary (λcrit) where the symmetry protection
fails. Rather than acting as an absolute entanglement
switch, a mechanical tilt (α > 0) actively isolates the pre-
existing Bell states into a gapped subspace that survives
up to the critical interaction threshold. Ultimately, by
determining the bounding conditions for Schrieffer-Wolff
transformation derived parity mixing in deeply interlocked
regimes, this framework exposes the fundamental scaling
limits of conventional semiconductors, validating the ne-
cessity of synthetic macroscopic platforms for topological
protection.
