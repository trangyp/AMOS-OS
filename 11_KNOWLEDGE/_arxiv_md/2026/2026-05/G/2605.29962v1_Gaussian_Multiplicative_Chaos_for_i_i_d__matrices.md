Convergence to the GMC via [34]
In this section we will show how to use the methodology of [34] to deduce convergence to
the GMC for Gaussian divisible ensembles. In particular, [34] provides a general framework
for deducing GMC convergence; i.e., a list of assumptions (see [34, Assumptions 2.5]) on
an asymptotically Gaussian field Xpzq under which one has convergence to the GMC, [34,
Theorem 2.6].
For the most part, the framework of [34] applies without major changes to our set-up.
However, there are a few differences which we now outline. We will be working with the
matrices Xt defined in (4.1) and quantities such as ΦNpz, η, tq as defined in Section 4.1. For
the discussion that follows we recall also the parameter ηm “ N´1´δm, which has appeared
previously in several sections.
(i) The work [34] regularizes the field Xpzq by convolving it with a smooth test function on
some scale ν, obtaining smoother fields Xνpzq. As can be seen by our methods, this is
inconvenient for us, as our regularization parameter is the η (actually in the language
of [34] ν “ η1{2) in ΦNpz, η, tq.
Instead, we will take (modulo some cut-offs and a rescaling, see Section 8.1 below)
Xpzq “ ΦNpz, N´1´δm, t1q and Xνpzq “ ΦNpz, ν2, 0q for ν2 " N´1{2. At this point note
that Xpzq is evaluated at η “ ηm instead of η “ 0 and at t “ t1. On the other hand,
Xνpzq is evaluated at t “ 0. The proof would also work with η “ 0 and then t “ t1
everywhere, but the various computations would be more tedious.
We will prove asymptotics for the joint Laplace transforms of these fields which are
sufficient to apply the method of [34]. For the most part, [34] makes no explicit use
of the fact that the regularizations come from convolutions. In Section 8.3 below we
explain why the parts of [34] that do rely on this are still applicable to our set-up.
(ii) We cannot quite verify every assumption of [34, Assumptions 2.5], as they were some-
times written in a notationally convenient way, that turns out to be slightly stronger
than what was necessary. Below we present our joint Laplace transform asymptotics in
Proposition 8.5. By reading the proof of [34, Theorem 2.6], one can easily see that these
asymptotics are sufficient for the proofs to work. Details are given below in Section 8.3.
(iii) Part of the method of [34] is based on a modified L2 argument, which leads to controlling
the joint moments of Xpzq and Xpwq. In particular, one needs to cut-off a contribution
where |z ´ w| ĺ N´1{2`b; the assumptions in [34] to control this contribution are stated
in way that we cannot quite verify them. Instead, we provide an upper bound (Lemma
8.6) and a further argument (Lemma 8.8) that shows this upper bound is sufficient to
remove this contribution.
Essentially, we are losing a small Nε factor over [34, Eq.
(2.15)] which can instead be absorbed into [34, Eq. (2.16)].
