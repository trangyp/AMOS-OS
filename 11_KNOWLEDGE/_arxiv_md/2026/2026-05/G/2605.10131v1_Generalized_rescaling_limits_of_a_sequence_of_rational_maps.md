GENERALIZED RESCALING LIMITS OF A SEQUENCE OF RATIONAL
MAPS
CHARLES FAVRE AND CHEN GONG
Abstract. We consider a sequence of complex rational maps (fn) of a fixed degree d ≥
2.
Building on the seminal work of Kiwi, we introduce the notion of generalized rescaling
limits. These are rational maps possibly defined over a non-Archimedean field obtained by
renormalizing at some scale a fixed iterate of the sequence (fn). We explain that the set of all
generalized rescaling limits is naturally organized as a tree, and bound the size of this tree in
term of the degree d.
We apply our theory to quadratic rational maps. Using Kiwi’s classification, we describe
all possible trees in this case, and prove a uniform bound on the number of cycles with small
multipliers.

Luo’s method uses ultralimits of the hyperbolic 3-space, following the approach developed
by Bestvina [Bes88] and Paulin [Pau88] in the context of Kleinian groups. He deduced from
his construction the appearance of monomial rescaling limits under suitable conditions, and
gave a characterization of hyperbolic components containing a diverging sequence with bounded
multipliers. The authors [FG25] and more recently Poineau [Poi25b] and Roy [Roy25] revisited
his construction using Berkovich techniques in the spirit of Morgan-Shalen approach [MS85]
allowing for more flexibility. Note that these degeneration techniques have already proven to be
a powerful tool in addressing uniformity problems in arithmetic dynamics, see, e.g., [DKY20,
Poi25a, Yap25] and Diophantine geometry, see [LY26]. We also refer the reader to [Hul26] for a
similar approach from the perspective of model theory.
Our aim is to define a notion of g-rescaling limit1 encompassing all these limiting procedures,
and to develop a general framework in order to take into account all possible limits of the iterates
of (fn) whether defined over an Archimedean or a non-Archimedean field. We prove that these
limits can be naturally organized into a simplicial tree.
⋄
Let us now explain in more details our main results.
The first difficulty to overcome is that (fn) could be dense in the space Ratd of rational map
of degree d. It is thus necessary to extract a subsequence in order to have a chance to get
limits. Furthermore, we want the limit to capture non trivial dynamical contents. In order to
do so, one needs to extract a subsequence of (fn) that converges simultaneously in all projective
compactifications of Ratd. Ultrafilters turn out to be the adequate tool to canonically extract
converging subsequences in all compact set at the same time in a compatible way, see [Gol22,
§3].
We thus shall fix once and for all a non-principal ultrafilter ω of the set of integers. It is an
element of the power set of N whose elements are called ω-big, and allows one to take ultralimits.
In particular, the sequence (fn) has a limit along ω in any projective compactification of Ratd.
A crucial aspect of our theory is the analysis of the degeneration of (fn) at multiple scales.
This fact was noticed by Luo, see [Luo21, Remark, p.3] and the discussion on [Luo24, p.3805], and
naturally appears in studying degenerations of Riemann surfaces, see [BCG+24, AN22, AN25].
Formally, a scale2 is determined by a bounded sequence of positive real numbers, and we
identify two sequences εn, ηn when the limit along ω of their quotients εn/ηn is finite and
non-zero. The set of scales is naturally totally ordered so that for instance (e−n) < (1/n) <
(1/ log n) < (1). It admits a unique maximal element (1), which we refer to as the trivial scale.
It is convenient to include the sequence (0) as an extended scale which becomes a minimum
among all scales.
An (extended) scale ε determines an algebraically closed field Hε defined by a variation
on the construction of ultraproducts (see [Sch10] for a formal definition of this notion and its
extensive applications to commutative algebra). By definition, Hε is the quotient of the set of
sequences of complex numbers (zn) such that limω |zn|εn < ∞by the ideal of sequences such
that limω |zn|εn = 0. The function |(zn)| := limω |zn|εn defines a norm for which Hε is complete.
When ε = (1), then Hε is isomorphic to C; when (0) < ε < (1), then Hε is a spherically
complete non-Archimedean field; and when ε = (0), then it is a trivially valued complex analog
of a model of the Robinson field of hyperreals.
Let us write fn = [Pn : Qn] in projective coordinates where Pn, Qn are homogeneous poly-
nomials normalized so that the maximum of their coefficients have norm 1. Given a scale ε,
each coefficient of Pn and Qn determines an element in Hε, and in this way we get a limit fε
which is a (possibly constant) rational map of degree ≤d defined over Hε. We can now define
a workable notion of g-rescaling.

Definition. A restricted g-rescaling adapted to (fn) is a pair of a sequence of Möbius transfor-
mations M = (Mn) and a scale ε > (0) such that (M · f · M−1)ε is a rational map of positive
topological entropy.
When ε = (1), positive entropy is equivalent to have degree at least 2. When (0) < ε < (1),
the condition is stronger and is equivalent to say that the map has degree ≥2 and does not have
potential good reduction, see [FRL26]. A rescaling in the sense of Kiwi is a restricted g-rescaling
with ε = (1). We shall say that two restricted g-rescalings (M, ε) and (L, η) are equivalent iff
ε = η and (L−1 · M)ε has degree 1.
It is also important to consider g-rescalings possibly having degree 1 limits, but the definition
is more technical. We refer to §4 for the general definition.
With this terminology, we prove the basic fact that there exists a unique adapted g-rescaling
whose limit has degree d. We call it the fundamental g-rescaling of (fn). One can obtain it either
through Luo’s construction or using Berkovich theory [FG25, Roy25]. The associated scale is
obtained by minimizing the normalized resultant of fn on its PGL(2)-orbit in Ratd, see §3.
The following example features a fundamental rescaling, a Kiwi rescaling, and a g-rescaling
that belongs to neither category, see Example 4.15 for details.
Example. Consider fn(z) = e−nz4 + n−1z3 + z2. Here, (id, (1/n)) is the fundamental rescaling
and (id, (1)) is a Kiwi rescaling whose limit is z2. In contrast, (id, (1/ log n)) is a g-rescaling
that is neither fundamental nor Kiwi: its limit is a degree 3 polynomial az3 + z2 defined over a
non-Archimedean field with |a| < 1.

Our previous result can be seen through the lenses of renormalization theory. Recall that
renormalization is an essential tool in dynamics, see [Avi11] and [BGH25], and more specifically
in the dynamics of complex polynomials. We refer to the books of McMullen [McM94], to the
notes by Lyubich, [Lyu23], or to the recent survey by Dudko [Dud25] for a thorough discussion
of this theory and its far-reaching applications in holomorphic dynamics.
An important observation is that we can encapsulate the degeneration of a sequence of rational
map (fn) along an ultrafilter ω into a single degree d rational map f defined over the ultraproduct
H0 of the field of complex numbers mentioned above.
Cycles of g-rescalings adapted to (fn) are obtained by iterating and zooming f at some scale,
so that we can loosely interpret them as a form of renormalization of f. Since renormalization
theory has proved to be an essential tool in understanding bifurcations, we expect the notion
of g-rescalings to play an analogous role in the study of bifurcation phenomena at infinity in
the moduli space of rational maps. Results by Luo [Luo22a, Luo24] and Kiwi [Kiw25] are first
instances of this idea.
⋄

1.2.1. Non-principal ultrafilters. Let ω be a non-principal ultrafilter in N. Recall that this is

this is a function on the power set mω : 2N →{0, 1} such that mω(E) ≤mω(F) if E ⊂F;

existence of a non-principal ultrafilter relies on the axiom of choice.
It is customary to say that E is ω-big when mω(E) = 1. The ultrafilter technique allows one
to extract a generalized subsequence in any compact Hausdorff space K, even when K is not
sequentially compact. More precisely, for any sequence x = (xn) ∈KN, there exists a (unique)
point xω ∈K such that for every neighborhood V of xω, the set {n ∈N : xn ∈V } is ω-big. We
call xω the limit of x along ω. Note that if (xn) converges in the usual sense, then xω coincides
with the standard limit of a sequence.

shall drop the reference to ω and simply write lim x = xω.
