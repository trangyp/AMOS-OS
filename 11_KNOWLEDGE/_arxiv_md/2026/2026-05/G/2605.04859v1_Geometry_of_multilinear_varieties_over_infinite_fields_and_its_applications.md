GEOMETRY OF MULTILINEAR VARIETIES OVER INFINITE FIELDS AND
ITS APPLICATIONS
QIYUAN CHEN AND KE YE
Abstract. Multilinear varieties, defined as the sets of rational points of varieties cut out by mul-
tilinear functions, were first introduced and studied by Gowers and Mili´cevi´c [Proc. Edinb. Math.
Soc., 2021] for finite K. In this paper, we investigate multilinear varieties over infinite fields from
a geometric perspective.
We establish two fundamental results: a codimension formula for the
Zariski closure of a multilinear variety, and the existence of a high-dimensional irreducible subvari-
ety passing through any given K-rational point. These results serve as a geometric foundation for
analyzing various ranks of tensors and homogeneous polynomials, including partition rank, analytic
rank, geometric rank, (collective) strength and (collective) Birch rank. As applications, we resolve
the Adiprasito-Kazhdan-Ziegler conjecture [arXiv:2102.03659, 2021] on the stability of partition
rank for perfect infinite fields. We thereby settle the stability conjecture for collective strength [Se-
lecta Math., 2024], as well as the conjecture on the linear equivalence between strength and Birch
rank [arXiv:2410.00248, 2024] for such fields. Moreover, our results immediately yield a strength-
ening of the theorems of Bik-Draisma-Snowden [arXiv:2401.02067, 2024] and Lampert-Snowden
[arXiv:2406.18498, 2024], for multilinear varieties over infinite fields.

1.2.1. Stability of ranks. The first application concerns the stability of partition rank and strength
under field extensions. Given f ∈Hom(Kn1 × · · · × Knd, K) and a field extension F/K, we denote
by fF ∈Hom(Fn1 × · · · × Fnd, F) the F-multilinear function induced by f. For convenience, we
simply write RF(f) := RF(fF). We say that R has the stability if RK(f) ≍d RK(f) for all f ∈
Hom(Kn1 × · · · × Knd, K).
Here “≍d” means the linear equivalence between two functions (cf.
Definition 4.2). We recall the following key conjecture on the stability of partition rank, proposed
by Adiprasito, Kazhdan and Ziegler.
Conjecture 1.3 (Stability of partition rank). [1, Conjecture 1.7] Let K be a field. For any integer
d ≥2 and f ∈Hom(Kn1 × · · · × Knd, K), we have PRK(f) ≍d PRK(f).
Over finite fields, Conjecture 1.3 is equivalent to Conjecture 1.6, together with two other conjec-
tures [14, Theorem 5.6]. By [28, Claim 3.2], Conjecture 1.3 implies the following:
Conjecture 1.4 (Stability of collective strength). [28, Conjecture 1.6] Let K be a field with
char(K) = 0 or char(K) > d. Then strK(P) ≍d strK(P) for any P ∈K[x1, . . . , xn]d.
Note that if char(K) = p ≤d for some prime p, then [8, Example 1.2] shows that strK(P) can be
arbitrarily larger than strK(P), implying the necessity of char(K) > d in Conjecture 1.4. On the
other hand, since f ∈Hom(Kn1×· · ·×Knd, K) can be viewed as a polynomial in K[xj,s]j∈[d], s∈[nj], we
have PRK(f) = Od(strK(f)). In particular, Conjectures 1.3 and 1.4 are equivalent over any field K
with char(K) = 0 or char(K) > d. However, Conjecture 1.4 is slightly weaker than Conjecture 1.3
because of the additional assumption that char(K) > d.
In Theorems 4.9 and 4.18, we prove
the stability of partition rank and collective strength over perfect infinite fields, respectively. As
before, all constants are determined explicitly. For convenience, we summarize the two results in
the following theorem without presenting the explicit constants.

Here ARK(f) denotes the analytic rank [22] of f over the finite field K. It is proved in [31] that
Conjecture 1.6 is true up to a log-factor. According to [5], the validity of Conjecture 1.6 implies
that of the following conjecture over finite fields.
Conjecture 1.7 (Strength vs. Birch rank). [5, Conjecture 1.3] Let K be a field and let d ≥2 be

(b) If K is perfect and infinite, then PRK(f) ≍d GR(f).
(c) If either char(K) = 0 or K is perfect and infinite with char(K) > d, then strK(P1, . . . , Pm) ≍d,m
