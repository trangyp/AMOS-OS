Graceful Labeling of Two Families of Spiders
Songling Shan∗
Yucheng Zhong†
May 18, 2026
Abstract
A graceful labeling of a graph G is an injective function f : V (G) →{0, . . . , |E(G)|}
such that { |f(u) −f(v)| : uv ∈E(G) } = {1, . . . , |E(G)|}. If such a labeling exists,
then we call G graceful. Introduced by Rosa in 1967, graceful labeling has been widely
studied, and the Graceful Tree Conjecture asserts that every tree is graceful.
The
conjecture is known to hold for several classes of trees, including caterpillars, trees with
at most four leaves, trees of diameter at most five, and certain spiders. An important
subclass is that of α-labelings, where a graceful labeling f admits an integer α such
that each edge joins a vertex with label at most α to one with label greater than α. A
result from 1982 by Huang, Kotzig, and Rosa shows that if H has an α-labeling with a
vertex u labeled 0 or α, and G has a graceful labeling with a vertex v labeled 0, then
identifying u and v yields a graceful graph, though this requires a 0-labeled vertex in
G. We prove a related result that relaxes this condition: if G has a graceful labeling f
such that f(u) + ⌊n/2⌋+ 1 ≤n and n ̸≡1 (mod 4), where u ∈V (G) and n ≥2 is an
integer, then joining u to an end vertex of the vertex-disjoint n-vertex path Pn yields a
graceful graph. As an application, we show that any spider with legs L1, . . . , Ls (s ≥1)
satisfying |E(L2)| ≥2|E(L1)| + 4 and |E(Li+1)| ≥2|E(Li)| + 2 for i ∈{2, . . . , s −1} is
graceful. Furthermore, we give an explicit graceful labeling for spiders with one leg of
arbitrary length and all others of length at most two such that the center is labeled by
0. This labeling enables the construction of larger graceful spiders by attaching paths
at the center.
Keywords: Graceful labeling; α-labeling; Graceful Tree Conjecture.

Graceful labelings were first introduced by Rosa [10] under the name of β-valuations,
and the term “graceful labeling” was due to Golomb [4].
The major open problem on
graceful labelings is the Graceful Tree Conjecture, proposed by Rosa in 1967 [10]. It is
also known as the Kotzig–Ringel–Rosa Conjecture, reflecting its close connection to the
Ringel Conjecture [9] and Kotzig’s earlier work on related graph decomposition and labeling
problems, including special cases of the conjecture.
Conjecture 1 (Graceful Tree Conjecture). Every tree is graceful.
While Conjecture 1 has attracted considerable attention, it has been verified only for
certain special classes of trees, including caterpillars [10] (a caterpillar is a tree with the
property that the removal of all leaves leaves a path), trees with at most four end-vertices [6],
and trees with diameter at most five [5]. For additional classes of trees known to admit
graceful labelings, we refer the reader to Gallian’s dynamic survey [3]. In this paper, our
focus is on graceful spiders.
A spider is a tree with at most one vertex (called the center) of degree greater than two.
Let S be a spider such that the degree of the center is at least three (otherwise, the spider
is simply a path). Each path in S connecting the center and a leaf is called a leg of S. The
general question whether all spiders are graceful is still open, but some classes of spiders
have been shown to be graceful. Bahls, Lake, and Wertheim [1] proved that spiders whose
leg lengths differ by at most one are graceful. Panpa and Poomsa-ard [8] showed that all
spiders with at most four legs of length greater than one admit a graceful labeling, and this
result was extended by Panpa, Imnang, and Wasuanankul [7] to spiders with at most five
such legs. For additional classes of spiders known to admit graceful labelings, we refer the
reader to Gallian’s dynamic survey [3].
A special type of graceful labeling, called an α-labeling, has been used as a constructive
tool; see, for example, Panpa and Poomsa-ard [8]. A graceful labeling f is said to be an
α-labeling if there exists an integer α such that f(u) ≤α < f(v) for each edge uv with
f(u) < f(v). The integer α is called the index of f. Clearly, a graph must be bipartite in
order to admit an α-labeling. The following lemma of Huang, Kotzig, and Rosa [6] provides
a useful method for constructing larger graceful graphs from smaller ones.
Lemma 1 ([6]). Let G and H be vertex-disjoint graphs with u ∈V (G) and v ∈V (H). If G
admits an α-labeling with index α such that u is labeled by α or 0, and H admits a graceful
labeling such that v is labeled by 0, then the graph obtained from G and H by identifying u
and v is graceful.
The graceful labeling in Lemma 1 is obtained by shifting the vertex labels of H upward
by α, and, when u is labeled α, shifting the labels in the upper part of the α-labeling of G
upward by |E(H)|. If instead u is labeled 0, one first applies the reverse α-labeling of G,
thereby obtaining another α-labeling in which u receives the label α. The same construction
then applies. In either case, the vertex formed by identifying u and v is assigned the label
α.

For n ≥1, let Pn denote a path on n vertices. Cattell [2] proved that every path Pn
admits an α-labeling in which a prescribed vertex can be assigned any label in [0, n −1],
provided that n ̸≡1 (mod 4). Consequently, if a spider admits a graceful labeling in which
its center is labeled by 0, then one can construct a larger graceful spider by attaching a
suitable path at its center via Lemma 1. However, after this operation, the center of the
resulting spider is no longer labeled by 0; it is labeled by α.

the center of a graceful spider, provided the path length is relatively large. Let G and H
