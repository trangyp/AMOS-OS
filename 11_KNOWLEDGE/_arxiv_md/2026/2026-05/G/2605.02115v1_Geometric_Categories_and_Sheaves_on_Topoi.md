Geometric Categories and Sheaves on Topoi
Connor Bass
May 1, 2026
Abstract
We introduce the notion of a geometric (∞, 1)-category, the protopyical example of which is an (∞, 1)-
topos. We study (hyper)sheaves on geometric (∞, 1)-categories, proving that these are characterized
by a form of Čech (hyper)descent. As an application we study (hyper)sheaves on (n, 1)-topoi for all
n ∈Z≥1∪{∞}, and prove that the effective epimorphism topology on an (n, 1)-topos X may be identified
as the canonical topology on X. Moreover, we show that for finite n ∈Z≥1 the study of sheaves on an
(n, 1)-topos X is equivalent to the study of (n −1)-truncated sheaves on certain (∞, 1)-topoi. We then
globalize our study to consider sheaves on ∞T op. In the appendix, we study the behavior of modules
under a reflective monoidal (∞, 1)-functor L⊗: C⊗→D⊗, and study (hyper)sheafification under a
change of universe.

2.1
Topoi, Sheaves, and Sites
We begin with our most essential definitions.
Definition 2.1.1. Let C be a presentable (∞, 1)-category, and let S be a (possibly large) collection of arrows
in C. Write S for the strongly saturated class of morphisms in C generated by S (HTT 5.5.4.7), and suppose
that S is of small generation (HTT 5.5.4.7). Writing C′ ⊆C for the full subcategory of C spanned by the
S-local objects, one calls a map L : C →C′ left adjoint to the inclusion C′ ,→C ([Ker] 06VH) a Bousfield
localization of C at S.
Remark 2.1.2. Under the assumptions of 2.1.1, the map L : C →C′ always exists, C′ is presentable, and
C′ ,→C is accessible (HTT 5.5.4.15).
Definition 2.1.1 is not entirely standard: in HTT, such L : C →C′ are simply referred to as localizations,
which creates a notational conflict with the following:
Definition 2.1.3. Let C be an (∞, 1)-category, and let W be any collection of morphisms in C. A localization
of C at W is an (∞, 1)-category W −1C and an (∞, 1)-functor F : C →W −1C satisfying the property that
for all (∞, 1)-categories E, the map F ∗: Fun(W −1C, E) →Fun(C, E) is fully faithful, and the essential image
of F ∗is comprised of those maps G : C →E which carry each w ∈W to an isomorphism in E.
Under the notation of 2.1.1, it is not true that L : C →C′ is a localization at S. However, it is true that such
L are localizations at S, and we will (somewhat abusively) use the naked term localization to refer to such
a Bousfield localization. Moreover, the map L : C →C′ does still satisfy the following universal property:
Lemma 2.1.4. Under the notation of 2.1.1, let E be a cocomplete (∞, 1)-category. Write Funcoℓim(C′, E)
for the full subcategory of Fun(C′, E) spanned by those maps C′ →E which preserve small colimits, and define
Funcoℓim(C, E) ⊆Fun(C, E) similarly. Then, pulling-back along L yields a fully faithful map Funcoℓim(C′, E) →
Funcoℓim(C, E), whose essential image is those (∞, 1)-functors carrying every edge of S to an isomorphism
in E.
Proof. Combining HTT 5.5.4.15 with [Ker] 02G3, we deduce that L is a localization at S, in the sense
of 2.1.3. Thus, L∗: Fun(C′, E) →Fun(C, E) is fully faithful, and the essential image of L∗is the full
subcategory of Fun(C, E) spanned by those arrows G : C →E satisfying the property that, for all s ∈S,
G(s) is an isomorphism in E. Since L preserves small colimits, it is immediately apparent that L∗restricts
to an arrow Funcoℓim(C′, E) →Funcoℓim(C, E). It remains to show that a cococontinuous map G : C →E
carries each s ∈S to an isomorphism precisely when G carries each s′ ∈S to an isomorphism, which
follows from HTT 5.5.4.10.
□
Remark 2.1.5. In the case that E is also locally small, 2.1.4 coincides with HTT 5.5.4.20. Moreover, under

this relies on HTT 5.5.4.20). The more general case may be deduced from 2.1.4.

Definition 3.1.1. Let κ be a regular cardinal. We call an (∞, 1)-category C κ-geometric if:
(1) C has pullbacks,
(2) C admits κ-small coproducts, and
(3) Said κ-small coproducts in C are disjoint and universal.
In the case that κ = ℵ0, we will call an ℵ0-geometric (∞, 1)-catgeory C a finitary (∞, 1)-category. In the
case that κ = Ωis the bounding cardinal of our implicitly chosen universe, we will call C a geometric
(∞, 1)-category.
Remark 3.1.2. Let C be a finitary (∞, 1)-category. Then, C admits finite limits if and only if C is disjunctive
in the sense of [Bar14] 4.2.
Remark 3.1.3. Let C be an (∞, 1)-category, and let κ and κ′ be regular cardinals satisfying κ ≤κ′. If C is
κ′-geometric, then C is κ-geometric.
