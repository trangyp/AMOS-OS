Gravitational wave interactions with matter: beyond quadrupolar perturbations
Ulrich K. Beckering Vinckers1, ∗and Nigel T. Bishop1, 2, †
1Department of Mathematics, Rhodes University, Makhanda, 6140, South Africa
2National Institute for Theoretical and Computational Sciences (NITheCS), Stellenbosch, South Africa
Previous work has developed the theory of linearized gravitational wave (GW) interactions with
matter using the Bondi–Sachs formalism, but with the perturbations restricted to be quadrupolar,
i.e., the angular dependence is spherical harmonic with ℓ= 2. Here, the theory is extended to the
case of GWs on a Minkowski background with general ℓ. Formulas for the GW damping and heating
effects are obtained for arbitrary ℓ≥2. It is found that the effects are, generally, enhanced, and this
suggests that it is unlikely that higher ℓ-modes will be seen in GW observations of the post-merger
signal of a binary neutron star merger, or of a core collapse supernova.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Since the detection of gravitational waves (GWs) [1], there has been recent interest in the interaction between GWs
and matter [2–6]. Such investigations are typically carried out by studying the linearized field equations of General
Relativity (GR) through the use of the Bondi–Sachs metric [7, 8], together with the so-called eth formalism [9, 10]. In
particular, a procedure for solving the linearized field equations in such a context, and for a Minkowski background,
was described in [11]. This procedure was subsequently used in [2] to study the interaction of GWs with a spherical
dust shell, and those results were applied to astrophysical scenarios in [3]. The damping of GWs propagating through
a matter shell was studied in [4], with the results of such a study being used in [5] to determine the temperature
distribution inside the shell.
A further study of this temperature distribution for astrophysical and cosmological
scenarios was performed in [12].
The results presented in [4] were specific to the case where a Minkowski background is considered, and the results
were extended to a Schwarzschild background in [6]. While analytical results were obtained in [4, 11] for the Minkowski
case, the Schwarzschild cases studied in [6, 13] required the use of numerical methods. It is worth noting that [2–
6, 11–13] only considered quadrupolar contributions to the GWs.
While the studies of GW interaction with matter discussed above are specific to quadrupolar (ℓ= 2) contributions,
octupolar (ℓ= 3) contributions to the metric components on a Minkowski background have been studied previ-
ously [14]. In the present work, we consider a solution for the case of a Minkowski background for an arbitrary ℓ≥2
mode. We also apply this solution to the consideration of GW interaction with a matter shell, which was carried
out for the ℓ= 2 case in [4]. Such an application involves the consideration of a congruence of timelike geodesics,
and analyzing the shear tensor associated with it. To this end, we find analytical expressions for the shear tensor
components for a general value of ℓ, and use these to study GW damping and heating effects. Evaluation of the
general formulas in the ℓ= 2 case provides a consistency check against previous results, and evaluation in the ℓ= 3
and 4 cases is used to consider the astrophysical implications of this work.
This manuscript is organized as follows.
In Section II we briefly review the Bondi–Sachs metric, and the eth
formalism which have been used in [11] to construct a solution procedure for obtaining fixed ℓ-mode solutions that
are perturbations around a Minkowski background. We also briefly review such a solution procedure in Section II. In
Section III, we give the metric variables for a general ℓ≥2 mode solution. In Section IV, we consider a matter shell
that is treated as having no back-reaction onto the metric. There, and in Section IV A, we discuss how the velocity field
perturbations associated with the matter field are related to the metric perturbations through the geodesic equation
as well as the normalization condition for the velocity field. We then make use of these expressions for the velocity
field components to obtain expressions for the shear tensor in Section IV B, which is associated with a congruence
of geodesics whose tangent vector field is given by the velocity field. Section IV C examines the GW damping and
heating effects in the matter shell for the ℓ= 2, 3, and 4 cases. The damping effects for these different ℓ-modes
are then compared in the context of astrophysically motivated scenarios in Section V. We discuss our conclusions
in Section VI. We provide explicit expressions for the metric variables, velocity field components, and shear tensor
components for the ℓ= 2, 3, and 4 cases in the appendices. The computer algebra code used in this work is also
described in the appendices, and is available as supplementary material.

VI.
SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION
Previous work on GWs on a Minkowski background within the Bondi–Sachs framework was mainly limited to
quadrupolar perturbations, i.e., to the case that angular dependence of the GWs is described by an ℓ= 2 spherical
harmonic. This work has extended these results to perturbations with an arbitrary value of ℓ≥2 for the spherical
harmonic, and expressions have been obtained for the metric components, as well as for the velocity and shear of a
matter field around the GW source. These expressions have then been used to evaluate the GW damping and heating
effects when the matter is viscous. It was found that, for constant frequency, the effects are enhanced as ℓincreases.
Allowing the frequency to increase with ℓ, as is expected in astrophysical scenarios, the effect is still enhanced for
long wavelengths, i.e., when ψℓ=2 = λℓ=2/(2πri) is somewhat greater than unity.
When ℓ= 2, the GW damping and heating effects are expected to be significant for the astrophysical scenarios of
BNS and CCSNe. We investigated these scenarios when ℓ= 3 and 4, and found that the effects are always enhanced,
perhaps substantially so depending on the actual values of some uncertain astrophysical parameters. In particular, it
was found that the damping of the ℓ= 3, 4 modes is almost complete when the frequency is towards the lower end
of the expected range. If a nearby BNS or CCSNe occurs, then this work indicates that higher order ℓmodes would
not be present in the GW signal, except possibly at frequencies towards the top of the expected range; further, in the
BNS case, such a signal would be outside the frequency sensitivity of current detectors and so would not be observed.
It has been shown when ℓ= 2 that use of the more physically appropriate Schwarzschild background can produce
GW damping and heating effects larger by a factor O(10) than on a Minkowski background. To what extent this
applies to higher ℓmodes is unknown; this would need to be investigated numerically, and is deferred to further work.

Appendix D: Computer codes
The computer algebra codes used in this work are available as supplementary material, and make use of sympy [25].
We have also made use of numpy [26], and matplotlib [27] for producing Figs. 1 and 2. These computer algebra
and plotting codes are contained in the anc directory, and are organized into four packages, as well as an additional
notebooks directory which contains the Jupyter notebook: beyond quadrupolar notebook.ipynb.
The Jupyter
notebook is used to execute the code contained in the four aforementioned packages. Below, we describe the four
packages.
• geometric: This package is used for computing the Christoffel symbols as well as the Riemann tensor, Ricci
tensor, and Einstein tensor components for a given metric tensor. The package also contains functions that are
used for linearizing expressions. In this manuscript, we use this package for the case where the metric is the
Bondi–Sachs one given in Eq. (1).
• eth formalism: This package is used for the implementation of eth operations specific to the case where the
complex dyad is qA = (1, i/ sin θ). In addition, the package contains substitution rules which we use to rewrite
certain expressions in terms of eth operations rather than explicit θ and ϕ derivatives. We also use this package
for generating sZℓ,m components.

• eth field equations: This package makes use of the Ricci tensor components generated using the geometric
package, and rewrites the expressions in terms of eth operations by making use of the substitution rules contained
in the eth formalism package. We note that these field equations are obtained for the case where the background
fields for β and W are arbitrary functions of r, and in the present manuscript we have set both of these to zero,
which corresponds to considering perturbations around a Minkowski background. As was done in [2], we have
made use of the following field in our computer algebra
RJ = (qAqB −r2JhAB)RAB ,

the aforementioned substitution rules are also implemented to obtain the matter Eqs. (12)–(16).

expression, and reducing the field equations to differential equations for the radial parts. The final ℓ-mode ex-
pressions for the metric variables and shear tensor components given in Eqs. (4)–(9), and (20)–(22), respectively,
