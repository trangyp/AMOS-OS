Generalized Killing spinors
associated with the Ricci tensor
Natsuki Imada
May 21, 2026
Abstract
In this paper, we introduce the notion of Ricci Killing spinors on Riemannian spin manifolds,
which form a class between generalized Killing spinors and standard Killing spinors. We prove an
existence theorem for Ricci Killing spinors that are not Killing spinors on a certain class of Sasakian
manifolds. This yields new examples of manifolds admitting generalized Killing spinors.
Keywords: Generalized Killing spinors; Einstein manifolds; Sasakian manifolds.
2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 53C27; Secondary 53C25, 58J60.

Introduction
Killing spinors are special sections of the spinor bundle on a spin manifold that satisfy a certain
differential equation. They have been extensively studied in differential geometry and mathematical
physics due to their connections with special holonomy, supersymmetry, and Einstein metrics. In this
paper, we introduce a new class of spinors called Ricci Killing spinors, which generalize the notion of
Killing spinors by incorporating the Ricci curvature of the manifold.
A generalized Killing spinor or Cauchy spinor on a spin manifold (M, g) is a non-zero spinor field
ψ ∈Γ(ΣM) that satisfies the equation
∇Xψ = A(X) · ψ
for all X ∈TM,
where A is a symmetric endomorphism of the tangent bundle TM and · denotes the Clifford multipli-
cation. When A is a constant multiple of the identity map, ψ is called a Killing spinor [BFGK91].
Generalized Killing spinors are strongly related to hypersurfaces in Riemannian manifolds admit-
ting parallel spinors.

In the search for examples of generalized Killing spinors that are not Killing spinors, several
important results have been obtained. For 3- and 7-dimensional spheres, explicit examples of such
generalized Killing spinors were given in [MS14b] (see also [MS14c]). Furthermore, they proved that
on spheres of all other dimensions between 2 and 8, every generalized Killing spinor is necessarily a
Killing spinor. The properties of generalized Killing spinors on the 3-dimensional sphere were further

The existence and construction of generalized Killing spinors have also been studied on other spe-

In the present paper, we introduce the notion of Ricci Killing spinors as a special class of gener-
alized Killing spinors where the endomorphism A is related to the Ricci curvature of the manifold.
Specifically, we define a Ricci Killing spinor as a non-zero spinor field ψ that satisfies
∇Xψ = λ Ric(X) · ψ
for all X ∈TM,
for some non-zero constant λ ∈R \ {0}. Here, Ric is the Ricci curvature tensor viewed as an endo-
morphism of the tangent bundle. The class of Ricci Killing spinors includes that of Killing spinors.
Then a natural question arises: under what conditions does a Ricci Killing spinor reduce to a Killing
spinor? In this paper, we first answer this question by establishing a geometric inequality relating the
constant λ and the scalar curvature. Specifically, we prove the following theorem:
Theorem A. Let (M, g) be an n-dimensional Riemannian spin manifold (n ≥2) admitting a Ricci
Killing spinor with a constant λ. If Scal = 0 then (M, g) is Ricci-flat and the Ricci Killing spinor is
parallel. If Scal ̸= 0 then the following inequality holds:

The equality holds if and only if (M, g) is Einstein, namely, the Ricci Killing spinor is a Killing spinor.
Next, we investigate the local structure of manifolds admitting Ricci Killing spinors. It is a classical
result that Riemannian spin manifolds admitting non-parallel Killing spinors are locally irreducible.
However, as we will see in Section 2, generalized Killing spinors can exist on locally reducible manifolds.
For Ricci Killing spinors, we prove that the existence on product manifolds imposes a strong restriction
on the geometry of the factors:
Theorem B. Let (M, g) be a Riemannian spin manifold. Suppose that there exists an open subset
U ⊂M isometric to a Riemannian product U1 × U2 of two non-Ricci-flat Riemannian spin manifolds.
Then (M, g) does not admit a Ricci Killing spinor.
By taking one of the factors of a product manifold to be Ricci-flat, one can easily construct Ricci
Killing spinors that are not Killing spinors.
Therefore, it is natural to ask whether one can find
examples of Ricci Killing spinors on non-product manifolds. As the final main result of this paper, we
show the existence of Ricci Killing spinors on non-product manifolds using Sasakian geometry.
Theorem C. Let (M2m+1, g, ξ, η, φ) be a simply connected null Sasakian η-Einstein spin manifold of
dimension at least 5. Then M admits a Ricci Killing spinor which is not a Killing spinor.
This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we briefly review the basic properties of gener-
alized Killing spinors and discuss their behavior on product manifolds. In Section 3, we derive the
fundamental equations for Ricci Killing spinors and give the proofs of Theorem A and Theorem B.
Finally, in Section 4, we focus on Sasakian manifolds, and prove Theorem C to provide non-trivial
examples.

Generalized Killing spinors
We first review the basic properties of generalized Killing spinors, following [MS14b]. For the basics
of spin geometry, we refer the reader to [LM89,BHM+15].

Definition 2.1. A non-zero spinor field ψ ∈Γ(ΣM) on a Riemannian spin manifold (M, g) is called
a generalized Killing spinor if there exists a symmetric endomorphism A of the tangent bundle TM
such that
∇Xψ = A(X) · ψ
for all X ∈TM.
Here, an endomorphism A is said to be symmetric if g(A(X), Y ) = g(X, A(Y )) for all X, Y ∈TM.
If A is a constant multiple of the identity map, then ψ is called a Killing spinor. In particular,
if A = 0, then ψ is called a parallel spinor. In these cases, it is well-known that the manifold is
Einstein. However, in general, a Riemannian spin manifold admitting a generalized Killing spinor is
not necessarily Einstein. We give an example of such a manifold in Section 4.
By the same argument as in the case of Killing spinors, we can show that the following lemma
holds for generalized Killing spinors as well.
Lemma 2.2. The norm of a generalized Killing spinor is constant. In particular, a generalized Killing
spinor has no zeroes.

Finally, we briefly remark on the irreducibility of manifolds admitting generalized Killing spinors.
It is known that if a Riemannian spin manifold admits a non-parallel Killing spinor, then it is locally
irreducible (i.e., it cannot be decomposed locally as a Riemannian product of two or more manifolds)
[BFGK91]. However, this is not the case for generalized Killing spinors. For example, we have the
following proposition.
Proposition 2.5. Let M, N be Riemannian spin manifolds and M is even-dimensional. If M admits
a generalized Killing spinor and N admits a parallel spinor, then the product manifold M × N admits
a generalized Killing spinor.
Proof. If M is even-dimensional, then the spinor bundle of M ×N is isomorphic to the tensor product
ΣM ⊗ΣN of the spinor bundles of M and N (see [B¨ar98]). Hence, for a spinor field ψM ∈Γ(ΣM)

3
Ricci Killing spinors
In this section, we introduce the notion of Ricci Killing spinors and show some basic properties of
them. We also characterize when a Riemannian spin manifold admitting a Ricci Killing spinor becomes
Einstein.
Definition 3.1. A non-zero spinor field ψ ∈Γ(ΣM) on a Riemannian spin manifold (M, g) is called
a Ricci Killing spinor if there exists a non-zero constant λ ∈R \ {0} such that
∇Xψ = λ Ric(X) · ψ
for all X ∈TM,
where Ric is the Ricci curvature tensor viewed as an endomorphism of the tangent bundle.
Real Killing spinors and parallel spinors are special cases of a Ricci Killing spinor. Indeed, if ψ is
a Killing spinor satisfying ∇Xψ = µX · ψ for some constant µ ∈R \ {0}, then the manifold is Einstein
with Ric = 4(n−1)µ2 Id. The Killing spinor equation implies that ψ is also a Ricci Killing spinor with
λ = (4(n −1)µ)−1. On the other hand, if ψ satisfies ∇Xψ = 0 for all X ∈TM, then the manifold is
Ricci-flat. In this case, ψ is also a Ricci Killing spinor with any non-zero constant λ.
Remark 3.2. In the definition of Ricci Killing spinors, we exclude λ = 0 because including or
excluding it does not make a difference. In fact, when λ = 0, ψ is a parallel spinor, and parallel
spinors are a special case of Ricci Killing spinors as we have seen.

locally reducible. Proposition 2.5 is also true for Ricci Killing spinors because the Ricci curvature of
such a product manifold M × N is given by Ric(X + Y ) = RicM(X) for X ∈TM and Y ∈TN. In
particular, if M admits a Killing spinor, then M × N admits a Ricci Killing spinor which is not a
Killing spinor.

Ricci Killing spinors on Sasakian manifolds
In the previous section, we provided examples of product manifolds admitting a Ricci Killing spinor
which is not a Killing spinor. In this section, we present examples of such manifolds that are not
product manifolds. For this purpose, we consider Sasakian manifolds, which are Riemannian manifolds
equipped with a special structure, often regarded as the odd-dimensional analogue of K¨ahler manifolds.
Definition 4.1. Let (M, g) be a Riemannian manifold. A Sasakian structure on M is a triple (ξ, η, φ)
consisting of a vector field ξ, a 1-form η, and a (1,1)-tensor field φ such that the following conditions
hold:
1. η(ξ) = 1,
2. φ2 = −Id +η ⊗ξ,
3. g(φ(X), φ(Y )) = g(X, Y ) −η(X)η(Y )
for all X, Y ∈TM,
4. (∇Xφ)(Y ) = g(X, Y )ξ −η(Y )X
for all X, Y ∈TM.
A Riemannian manifold (M, g) equipped with a Sasakian structure is called a Sasakian manifold.
Kim and Friedrich introduced the notion of Sasakian quasi-Killing spinors, which are a special
class of generalized Killing spinors on Sasakian manifolds [KF00].
Definition 4.2. A non-zero spinor field ψ ∈Γ(ΣM) on a Sasakian manifold (M, g, ξ, η, φ) is called a
Sasakian quasi-Killing spinor of type (a, b) if it satisfies
∇Xψ = aX · ψ + bη(X)ξ · ψ
for all X ∈TM,
where a, b ∈R are constants.
Regarding the existence of Sasakian quasi-Killing spinors, the following result is known.
Proposition 4.3 ([KF00]). Let (M 2m+1, g, ξ, η, φ) be a simply connected Sasakian spin manifold
(m ≥2). Then M admits a Sasakian quasi-Killing spinor of type (−1/2, b) if and only if Ric =
(2m −4b) Id +4bη ⊗ξ.
Now we have the following result on the existence of Ricci Killing spinors on Sasakian manifolds.
Theorem C. Let (M2m+1, g, ξ, η, φ) be a simply connected null Sasakian η-Einstein spin manifold of
dimension at least 5. Then M admits a Ricci Killing spinor which is not a Killing spinor.
