YOSHIDA ALGEBRA FOR GROUPOIDS
KEITARO SHIIZUKA
Abstract. In this paper, we extend the notion of the Yoshida
algebra of a finite group introduced in [Yos83] to finite groupoids
and investigate its fundamental properties. Our main results show
that the center of the Yoshida algebra of a finite groupoid is iso-
morphic to the center of the corresponding groupoid algebra, and
that there exists a surjective ring homomorphism from the crossed
Burnside ring of a finite groupoid, introduced in [Shi26+], onto the
center of the Yoshida algebra of a finite groupoid.
Key words and phrases : Groupoid, groupoid action, Yoshida
algebra, crossed Burnside ring, center, linear representation.

1. Introduction
The concept of a groupoid was introduced by H. Brandt in 1926 (see
[Bra26]). It generalizes the notion of a group, the main deference being
that composition is not necessarily defined for every pair of elements.
Various equivalent definitions of groupoids and their actions exist. In
this manuscript, from a categorical perspective, we treat a groupoid
G as a category in which every morphisms is invertible. Under this
viewpoint, an action is given by a functor G →set.

2. Preliminaries
2.1. Notations and conventions. Throughout this manuscript, all
groupoids are assumed to be finite. Let k be a commutative ring, and
denote by k-mod the category of finite generated k-modules. For a
ring A, we write Z(A) for its center. Other notation and conventions
follow [Shi26+].
2.2. Groupoids. In this subsection, we prepare the groundwork for
the subsequent sections. Throughout this paper, we treat groupoids
within categorical framework.
Definition 2.1. [Shi26+]
(1) A category G is a groupoid if every morphism in G is invertible.
(2) For each object x ∈G0, the set G(x, x), denoted by Gx, is called the
isotropy group at x ∈G0.
(3) A subcategory H of a groupoid G that is itself a groupoid is called
a subgroupoid of G. If H is a subgroupoid of G, then the inclusion
functor H →G is denoted by InclG
H.
(4) If X is a finite set, then the groupoid Pair(X) is the category
defined by the following conditions:
(1) Pair(X)0 := X,
(2) Pair(X)(x, y) := {(x, y)}, for (x, y) ∈X × X,
(3) The composition of f = (x, y) and g = (y, z) is defined by
gf := (x, z).
The groupoid Pair(X) is called the groupoid of pairs.
(5) Let G be a groupoid. For x, y ∈G0, define x ∼y if there exists a
morphism g : x →y. Then ∼is an equivalence relation on G0. The ele-
ments of the quotient set G0/∼are called the connected components
of G, and we denote this set by π0(G). In particular, If |π0(G)| = 1,
then G is called a connected groupoid.
