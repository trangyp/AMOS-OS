Q-difference analogue of the Stothers-Mason theorem
Jian-Tang Lu, Xing-Xing Lu∗and Zhi-Tao Wen†
Abstract
In this paper, we give a new definition of the q-weight of zeros, which reduces
to the multiplicity of zeros as q →1. Furthermore, we obtain a q-difference version
of the Stothers-Mason theorem by means of the new definition of the q-difference
radical, which covers the classical Stothers-Mason theorem as q →1. As applications,
we study the polynomial solutions of q-difference Fermat type functional equations.
Keyword: q-weight of zeros, q-difference radical, Stothers–Mason theorem, Fermat
type functional equation

Introduction
Let P be a polynomial. The radical rad(P) is the product of distinct linear factors of P.
Let a, b and c be relatively prime polynomials such that not all of them are identically zero.
The Stothers–Mason theorem [12], [14], see also [13], states that if they satisfy a + b = c,
then
max{deg(a), deg(b), deg(c)} ≤deg(rad(abc)) −1.
A difference analogue of the Stothers-Mason theorem or difference abc theorem for

prime polynomials in C[z], which are not all constants, satisfying a + b = c. Then,
max{deg(a), deg(b), deg(c)} ≤˜nκ(a) + ˜nκ(b) + ˜nκ(c) −1,

One of the purposes of this paper is to introduce a q-difference analogue of the radical of
polynomials and a q-difference analogue of the Stothers-Mason theorem, which are different
from the results in [3]. These q-difference counterparts reduce to the classical radical of
polynomials and Stothers-Mason theorem, respectively, as q →1. As applications, we

In Section 2, we define the q-weight of a zero of an analytic function in a domain G,
and investigate its properties via q-difference calculus. We then introduce the q-difference
radical by means of the q-weight of zeros and focus on the q-difference analogue of the
Stothers-Mason theorem by using the q-difference radical in Section 3. Section 4 is con-
cerned with an extension of the aforementioned q-difference Stothers-Mason theorem. Fi-
nally, in Section 5 we study the polynomial solutions of q-difference version of the Fermat
type functional equations.
