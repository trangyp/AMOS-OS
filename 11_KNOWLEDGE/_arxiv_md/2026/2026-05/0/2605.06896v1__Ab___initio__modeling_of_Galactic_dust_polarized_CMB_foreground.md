Ab initio modeling of Galactic dust polarized CMB foreground
Alexei G. Kritsuk1, Ka Wai Ho2, Ka Ho Yuen3, and Raphael Flauger1
1Physics Department, University of California, San Diego, La Jolla CA 92093, USA
2Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics, University of California, Santa Barbara CA 93106, USA
3Institute of Science and Technology for Deep Space Exploration, Nanjing University, Suzhou, Jiangsu
Province, Peoples Republic of China
We present the analysis of high-resolution synthetic dust polarization maps derived from
large-scale simulations of magnetized multiphase interstellar turbulence carried out with the
AthenaK code on the Frontier exascale supercomputer at the Oak Ridge National Laboratory.
Our turbulence model accurately captures spectral properties of the E- and B-modes measured
by Planck at 353 GHz. The simulations provide new insights into the physical origins of the
observed E/B asymmetry and positive TE signal, facilitating the development of advanced
models of Galactic foreground emission for current and future CMB experiments.
1
Introduction
Polarized emission from Galactic dust is one of the dominant foregrounds for cosmic microwave
background (CMB) polarization and a major challenge for extracting the primordial CMB signal
on large angular scales.
In this short communication we report results from a high-resolution (40963) numerical
simulation of turbulence in the multiphase interstellar medium (ISM). Our approach employs
a homogeneous multiphase MHD turbulence model 1, which successfully reproduced a number
of statistics for the local ISM, to generate synthetic dust polarization maps 2 of the I, Q,
and U Stokes parameters. The high-fidelity maps are then used to decompose the E and B
modes and compute the TT, EE, BB, TE, and TB spectra, EE/BB and TE/EE spectral
ratios, and rTE 

concerning the physical origins of the observed E/B asymmetry and positive TE correlation
with an aid of two complementary simulations of highly supersonic, trans- and sub-Alfv´enic
MHD turbulence with an isothermal equation of state.
This work further solidifies conclusions based on our previous lower resolution simulations2,4
by achieving more realistic effective Reynolds numbers, approaching the characteristic values for
the local ISM at the solar radius in the Milky Way disk.

supernova feedback. To make the model tractable, we adopt a simplified approach and apply
a random external large-scale solenoidal forcing to induce energy cascade. An artificial kinetic

up a specific form of spatio-temporal intermittency in MHD turbulence, leading to the devel-

The 40963 multiphase simulation was carried out with a version of the AthenaK code 8,
using the PPM-4 reconstruction, HLLD Riemann solver, IMEX2 time integration, and constrained
transport (CT) 9. Our production runs covered 75 Myr of evolution from static, uniform initial
conditions and utilized 4096 nodes (32k GPU equivalents) of the Frontier system at ORNL.
In total 228 sets of synthetic maps were generated on the fly for statistically stationary fully
developed MHD turbulence (uniformly sampled in time for t ∈[30, 75] Myr; the large eddy
turnover time τd = 5 Myr and the forcing auto-correlation time τa = 2 Myr). The two archival
10243 isothermal simulations10,11 were carried out with the PPML solver12,13, which also employed
HLLD and CT, and covered about 6τd of stationary evolution.

Results
A sample synthetic polarization map taken at t = 38 Myr is shown in Fig. 1. As expected,
the thermal dust polarization direction is mostly perpendicular to the direction of plane-of-
sky (POS) magnetic field shown by the drapery pattern. Likewise, the POS field direction is
predominantly parallel to the dense filaments in this map, as expected for lower-density, diffuse
regions of the ISM, where gravity does not play a significant role.

Figure 2 shows time-averaged spectra and spectral ratios derived from the synthetic maps.
Spectral slopes αEE = −2.45, αBB = −2.44, and αTE = −2.54 obtained in the range of scales
where approximate equipartition of magnetic and kinetic energy is reproduced (shaded band in
the figure) resemble Planck PR3 scaling best measured at 353 GHz in the largest sky region
LR71 3: −2.42 ± 0.02, −2.54 ± 0.02, and −2.50 ± 0.02, respectively.
The EE/BB power ratio measured in the same interval of scales, EE/BB = 1.88, is very
close to Planck’s EE/BB = 1.89 ± 0.03, although in the simulation there are clear hints of
scale-dependence with a tendency of decreasing EE/BB ratios toward smaller scales. While
higher-resolution multiphase simulations are certainly needed to confirm the trend, we note that
our isothermal simulations (Fig. 3) in both sub- and trans-Alfv´enic regimes do not show the
same scale-dependence in the far-inertial range. We argue that, keeping in mind the ‘shocking’
origin of the E/B asymmetry14, one can explain the difference as due to substantially lower sonic
Mach numbers, Ms, in a more realistic multiphase model (Ms ≈4) compared to the strongly
supersonic isothermal cases B1 (Ms ≈10) and B2 (Ms ≈9), in which the sonic scale cannot be
resolved on a 10243 grid.
In contrast, the TE/EE and rTE ratios are both approximately constant across the inertial
range in the multiphase simulation (Fig. 2, right panels). Comparing our TE/EE = 0.71/p0
with Planck’s measurement TE/EE = 2.77±0.05, we estimate the value of intrinsic polarization,
p0 = 0.26. Note that our model prediction for the TE correlation ratio, rTE = 0.33, is pretty
close to Planck’s result rTE = 0.357 ± 0.003. Finally, we find rTB = −0.0015 ± 0.0006, which is
consistent with the parity-preserving design of our turbulence model and predictably inconsistent
with Planck’s measurement rTB ≈0.05.
