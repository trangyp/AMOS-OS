(r, s)-sets from Desarguesian ovoids
Francesco Pavese
Abstract
An (r, s)-set in PG(n, q) is a set of points, say X, such that each s-dimensional projective
subspace contains at most r points of X. We investigate (n, n−2)-sets and (n−2, n−3)-sets in
PG(n, q), n ≤6. We show that the trivial upper bounds on (n, n−2)-sets in PG(n, q), 4 ≤n ≤6,
(4, 3)-sets in PG(6, q) and (3, 2)-sets in PG(5, q) are essentially sharp. A (3, 2)-set in PG(13, q)
of size q6−1
q−1 is also constructed.
Keywords: finite geometry; points in general position; (r, s)-set; evasive set.

Introduction
Let q be a prime power and let PG(n, q) or AG(n, q) denote the n-dimensional projective or affine
space over the finite field Fq. A cap is a set of points in PG(n, q) such that at most two of them are
on a line, whereas a set of points in PG(n, q) such that at most n in a hyperplane is known as an
arc. These objects have been extensively studied due to their connections to coding theory; see,
for instance, [13]. More generally, following [9], a pointset X in PG(n, q) is called (|X|; r, s, n, q)-set
or simply (r, s)-set if the properties below are satisfied:
i) each s-dimensional projective subspace contains at most r points of X;
ii) X spans the whole PG(n, q);
iii) there is an (s + 1)-dimensional projective subspace containing r + 2 points of X.
The term (r + 2)-general set, 1 ≤r ≤n −1, is also used to denote a (r + 1, r)-set, see [2, 22, 19].
Indeed, X is an (r+2)-general set if any r+2 distinct points of X are in general position. Hence a
cap is a 3-general set and an arc is an (n + 1)-general set. An (r, s)-set can be similarly defined in
AG(n, q). In particular, an (r, s)-set of AG(n, q) is also an (r, s)-set of PG(n, q). On the other hand,
(r, s)-sets in AG(n, q) are also known as (s, r)-subspace evasive sets [21]. Recently these sets gained
renewed interest due to their connections with bipartite Ramsey graphs [20] and list-decodable
codes [8]. Nonetheless, few explicit constructions of large (r, s)-sets are known in literature. In

Conclusion
Here we have presented constructions of the following:
i) a (4, 3)-set and a (6, 4)-set in AG(6, q), q ≥4, of size q2 −q + 1;
ii) a (3, 2)-set and a (5, 3)-set in AG(5, q), q ≥4, of size q2 −q;
iii) a (3, 2)-set in PG(5, q), q ≥4, of size q2 −q + 2;
iv) a (3, 2)-set in PG(13, q) of size q6−1
q−1 .
Except for iv), all these examples have cardinalities matching the theoretical upper bound up to
a constant factor. An (r, s)-set in PG(n, q) is said to be complete if it is not contained in a larger
(r, s)-set. It would be interesting to determine whether the constructed sets are complete or not.
