Abstract. We consider a natural filtration Badpδq Ă Badpδ1q for δ ě δ1 ą 0 on the
set of badly approximable numbers to complement the filtration of the well approximable
numbers by the τ-well approximable numbers. We show that the set Badpδq is a p1{3, 18δq-
winning set and give a lower bound on its Hausdorff dimension. We introduce the notion
of pα, βq-ubiquitously losing sets to the theory of Schmidt games, give an upper bound on
the Hausdorff dimension of an pα, βq-ubiquitously losing set that is strictly less than full
Hausdorff dimension, show that Badpδq is a p1{2, 18{δq-ubiquitously losing set, and give an
upper bound on the Hausdorff dimension of Badpδq that is strictly less than one. Combined
with a finite intersection property and a bilipschitz transfer property, we obtain results for
finite intersections of translates of Badpδq.

1. Introduction
Classically, Diophantine approximation is the study of the approximation properties of the
real numbers by the rational numbers. The set of real numbers is partitioned into the set
of badly approximable numbers, which we denote Bad, and the set of well approximable
numbers. There is a (non-exhaustive) filtration on the set of well approximable numbers by
the τ-well approximable numbers, a filtration indexed by the real numbers τ. The natural
tool to study the well approximable numbers and the τ-well approximable numbers is the
mass transference principle. For details, see, for example, the survey [6, Sections 1.1–1.3].
Conversely, a natural tool to study the set of badly approximable numbers Bad is the theory
of Schmidt games. In this paper, our first goal is to consider a theory analogous to that of the
τ-well approximable numbers for the badly approximable numbers, namely the filtration on
Bad whose subobjects are the sets Badpδq (Definition 1.1) and which is indexed by the real
numbers 1{
?
5 ą δ ą 0. To study these sets, we introduce the notion of pα, βq-ubiquitously
losing sets in Definition 1.5 to the theory of Schmidt games for real numbers 0 ă α ă 1 and
0 ă β ă 1, further reinforcing the utility of the theory of Schmidt games for studying Bad.
The benefit of the notion of pα, βq-ubiquitously losing sets is that it enables us to bound
the upper Hausdorff dimension by a bound that is strictly less than full Hausdorff dimension
(see Theorem 1.12 for the precise statement) and to also retain a version of the key property
associated with Schmidt games, namely a finite intersection property (see Theorem 1.13 as
an example and Theorem 7.2 for the finite intersection property).
More generally, the introduction of pα, βq-ubiquitously losing sets (for Rd where d P N
but even more generally is possible) into the theory of Schmidt games enables us to achieve
our second goal for this paper, namely extending the study of sets that satisfy the countable
intersection property and have full Hausdorff dimension to the study of sets that satisfy the
finite intersection property but need not have full Hausdorff dimension. This may enable the
theory of Schmidt games to become a more versatile tool in fractal geometry itself.
Finally, the ideas in this paper may have greater applicability in studying the finer structure
of other pα, βq-winning sets and amplifying the utility of other games motivated by Schmidt
games (see Remark 1.14).

Remark 1.14. The theory of Schmidt games is a natural tool to study not only Bad but also
various generalizations and related sets in Diophantine approximation and also in ergodic
