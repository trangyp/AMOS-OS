Abstract. We construct a surjective homomorphism from the (suitably inter-

1. Introduction
1.1. Branches of quiver gauge theories. Consider a quiver Q whose arrows are
endowed with equivariant parameters as in Subsection 2.1. To this data, one can
associate a quiver gauge theory, which comes with two algebraic varieties called
the Higgs and Coulomb branches respectively. These two varieties are expected
to be closely related to each other via a phenomenon called 3-dimensional mirror
symmetry, made mathematically precise by the Hikita-Nakajima conjecture [Hik17]
(see also [Bra+16; KMP21], also [Zho23] in K-theory, and numerous other works).

the two branches, as follows:
• the “Higgs branch algebra” refers to a certain incarnation of the K-theoretic Hall
algebra of Q, see [KS11; P˘ad20; SV13].
• the “Coulomb branch algebra” refers to a certain deformed algebra of functions
on the space defined in [Nak16; BFN24].

1.4. Relation to previous work. The existence of a homomorphism (4) has been
suggested by numerous mathematicians and physicists, see for instance [Rap+23]
and [CL26], as well as the seminal paper [Nak16]. In the present paper, we make
this homomorphism precise by
• interpreting the specific K-HA which appears in the homomorphism (4) as the
loop-nilpotent version of this construction;
• identifying the loop-nilpotent K-HA with a specific shuffle algebra by new wheel
conditions (defined over R instead of over F = Frac(R));
• finding generators for the aforementioned shuffle algebra over the ring R. While
the shuffle algebra is spherically generated over F ([Neg23]) it is not so over R;
• using the shuffle algebra incarnation to define the shifted double loop-nilpotent
K-HA in (7). It would be very interesting to define a double K-HA geometrically.
The loop-nilpotent CoHA and K-HA have been studied previously, see for instance
[Dav23; Dav+26; VV22; Jin26]. Meanwhile, integral forms of shuffle algebras have
been studied by [FT19; Tsy21] in type A and by the second-named author in [Neg24]
for the Jordan quiver. In fact, the particular integral form of the shuffle algebra
we consider in the present paper is the natural generalization of the construction
of loc. cit. to arbitrary quivers. The presentation of Coulomb branch algebras by
generators and difference operators, which plays a key role in the present paper, has
been studied in [BFN24; DK25; FT19; Kam+14; Wee19] and many other works.
We would also like to mention the upcoming paper [MW26], developed indepen-
dently from ours. Among other things, the authors of loc. cit. construct a injective
homomorphism from a rational shuffle algebra to a suitably defined limit of (the
