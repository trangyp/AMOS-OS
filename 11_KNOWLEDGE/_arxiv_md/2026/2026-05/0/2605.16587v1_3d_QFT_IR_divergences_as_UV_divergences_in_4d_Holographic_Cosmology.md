Abstract
In this paper we consider IR divergences in a 3d toy model field theory for 4d
holographic cosmology, and we analyze them by introducing a mass term in a way
that preserves a certain form of the generalized conformal structure. This allows
us to compute 2- and 3-point functions at 2-loops and study their IR structure
below the mass scale, from which we argue for a possible IR finiteness beyond
perturbation theory, consistent with lattice results. In the holographically dual
4d cosmology, this corresponds to UV finiteness, i.e., the absence of cosmological
singularities. The 3d IR field theory methods could be extended beyond this
specific application.

Introduction and main results
Holographic cosmology was born out of the observation that gravity is holographic, first
put forward [1, 2], but made concrete by the AdS/CFT correspondence [3] (see the books
[4, 5] for more information). The first applications of holography to cosmology were started
by [6–9], and the first application to usual weakly coupled inflation via holography, from a
strongly coupled QFT, was started by Maldacena in [10].
But a true alternative cosmology, one in which we have a strongly coupled 4-dimensional
gravity phase, dual to a 3-dimensional QFT, was defined in [11, 12]. Namely, the space
is not approximately AdS or dS (not even in its strong coupling form), so one considers
instead a phenomenologically defined QFT3 with ”generalized conformal structure”. This
holographic model fits the parameters to CMBR observations, as was then shown in [13–
15], with the same number of parameters as for Λ-CDM plus inflation, obtaining a result
that is indistinguishable from it. This is what we will call holographic cosmology from now
on.
These theories with generalized conformal structure can be understood as the dimen-
sional reduction of a 4-dimensional theory with conformal invariance to 3 dimensions. The
resulting theory has no mass scales, except for an overall scale of the coupling, and is also
super-renormalizable. In the early 80’s, it was conjectured that such theories are IR finite
[16, 17]. Recently, it was shown that this conjecture seems to hold on the lattice, at least
for the case of a theory of an adjoint scalar with ϕ4 interaction [18]. The two-loop IR
logarithmic divergence of a theory that is massless at the quantum level, but has a bare
mass, is gone non-perturbatively, and the coupling constant becomes an IR mass regulator.
In this paper, we consider further the IR perturbative structure of the 2- and 3-point
functions of these super-renormalizable 3-dimensional QFTs, also using a specific toy model
for holographic cosmology, the one used in previous works [19–22]. Here we also consider
scalar fields with a finite mass m, which is not just an IR regulator, meaning that we also
consider momenta less than it, p < m.
The technical feature that allows us to do consistent calculations with the massive
theory is that the addition of the mass, if viewed as a type of field while we perform
the symmetry transformations, does not break the generalized conformal structure, but
rather leads to more general Ward identities. We will study these IR divergences, drawing
conclusions that can be applied more generally for 3-dimensional super-renormalizable
theories, and then apply these methods to holographic cosmology.

Observations on the result
The regime ˆ¯q ≪1 (¯q ≪m) would be non-perturbative in the massless theory (using m
just as regulator in the divergent diagrams) if ¯q < λ. In the full massive theory, however,

IR (¯q →0), cancels in the presence of a (finite) mass term, for ¯q ≪2m. Moreover, we see
from (3.95) that the IR expansion has no dependence on the scale ¯q at leading order in ˜λeff:

Conclusions and discussion
In this paper, we have considered the IR behaviour of three-dimensional quantum field
theories with generalized conformal structure, which is used in the phenomenological def-
