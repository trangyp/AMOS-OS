TIMOFEY KRASIKOV AND KIRILL RASSOLOV
Abstract. A trinomial algebra is a commutative finitely generated algebra given by a system
of compatible relations each of which is a polynomial with three terms. Such algebras arise
as the Cox rings of varieties admitting a complexity one torus action.
We describe locally
nilpotent derivations of a trinomial algebra that are homogeneous under a natural torus action
of complexity one.
Introduction
A trinomial variety is an affine variety given by a system of equations of the form aMi +
bMi+1+cMi+2 = 0, compatible in a special way. Here the Mi denote fixed monomials in disjoint
sets of variables; see Section 1 for details. Following [15], we distinguish two types of trinomial
varieties depending on whether the monomial corresponding to the empty set of variables (i.e.,
equal to 1) is allowed. The coordinate algebra of a trinomial variety is called a trinomial algebra.
The ground field k is assumed algebraically closed and of characteristic zero.
Recall that the complexity of an action of a reductive group G on a variety X is the codi-
mension of a general orbit of a Borel subgroup B ⊆G. For an effective action of an algebraic
torus T, the complexity equals dim X −dim T. A trinomial variety is endowed with a natural
torus action of complexity one, which comes from the standard torus action on affine space
by scaling the variables. Moreover, there is a close connection between trinomial algebras and
arbitrary varieties with a torus action of complexity one via Cox rigns.
The Cox construction (see, e.g., [4]) is fruitfully applied to toric varieties, that is to say, normal
varieties with a torus action of complexity zero. The Cox ring of a toric variety is a polynomial
ring, which allows one to realize, say, an affine toric variety as the categorical quotient of an
affine space by an action of a diagonalizable group [6]. In turn, trinomial algebras are the Cox
rings of normal rational semiprojective varieties with a torus action of complexity one, finitely
generated divisor class group, and with no nonconstant invertible functions. In particular, every
affine variety satisfying these conditions is the categorical quotient of a trinomial variety by an
action of a diagonalizable group [14, 15].

A derivation δ is called locally nilpotent, or an LND, if for every f ∈R, there exists an n ∈Z>0
such that δn(f) = 0. The exponential map establishes a bijection between locally nilpotent
derivations up to scaling and Ga-subgroups in the automorphism group of a given algebra.
By a Ga-subgroup we mean a one-parameter algebraic subgroup isomorphic to the additive
group of the ground field k. Namely, an LND δ corresponds to the subgroup {exp(tδ) | t ∈k}.
Furthermore, LNDs homogeneous with respect to the action of a quasitorus H correspond to
H-normalized Ga-subgroups.
Homogeneous LNDs of trinomial algebras were studied in [20, 13, 19]. Explicit formulae
for the homogeneous LNDs were obtained in the case of complexity one action of a special
quasitorus H coming from the standard torus action on affine space by scaling the variables.
The H-homogeneous LNDs were called elementary. In the present paper, we consider LNDs
homogeneous with respect to the torus T which is the neutral component of the group H. For
factorial varieties, the quasitorus H is connected and therefore coincides with T. However in
non-factorial case we have the strict inclusion T ⊂H, and there may appear T-homogeneous
LNDs which are not H-homogeneus; see Remark 5.7 for specific example.
Our interest in LNDs homogeneous with respect to a torus is motivated by their applications
to study of arbitrary LNDs. Given a derivation of a finitely generated algebra, one can decom-
pose it into a sum of homogeneous derivations, which are called the homogeneous components.
However, the homogeneous components of a locally nilpotent derivation are, generally speaking,
not locally nilpotent. The importance of free gradings is that for certain homogeneous com-
ponents of a given LND, local nilpotency can be guaranteed. To be more precise, one should
consider the Newton polytop of the degrees of the non-zero homogeneous components; then the
homogeneous components corresponding to the vertices turn out to be locally nilpotent. Thus
the study of homogeneous LNDs for free gradings provides more information about an arbitrary
LND than for gradings by a non-free abelian group. In particular, we give a new proof of the
rigidity criterion obtained earlier in [12].
Recall that an algebra R is called rigid if its automorphism group contains no Ga-subgroups,
i.e., R admits no non-zero LNDs. Two LNDs are called equivalent if their kernels, i.e., the
algebras of invariants of the corresponding Ga-subgroups, coincide. R is called semirigid if all
nonzero LNDs are equivalent.

elementary LNDs, and we provide explicit formulae (Theorem 2.2). As an application, besides

Type 1, see Corollary 3.2.
For a trinomial algebra of Type 2, we obtain a description of the equivalence classes of T-
homogeneous LNDs (Theorem 4.1). This implies a semirigidity criterion, see Corollary 4.2.

Locally nilpotent derivations. The following lemma gathers some basic properties of locally
nilpotent derivations. We refer the reader to [10, Principles 1, 5, and 7 and Corollary 1.23] for
the proof.
Lemma 1.8. Let R be a k-domain, δ an LND of R and f, g ∈R. Then:
(i) fδ is a derivation; it is locally nilpotent if and only if f ∈ker δ.
(ii) ker δ is factorially closed, meaning that fg ∈ker δ implies f, g ∈ker δ.
(iii) Suppose that f | δ(f); then δ(f) = 0.
(iv) Suppose that f | δ(g) and g | δ(f); then either δ(f) = 0 or δ(g) = 0.
