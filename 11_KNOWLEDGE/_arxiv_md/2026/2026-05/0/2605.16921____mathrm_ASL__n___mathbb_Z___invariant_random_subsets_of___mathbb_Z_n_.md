Abstract. We classify measures on {0, 1}Zd, d ≥3, the space of subsets of
Zd, which are invariant under all affine special linear transformations. In other
words, we classify simple point processes on Zd whose law is invariant under
affine special linear transformations.
We show that every such process is built from a random equivariant poly-
nomial together with independent random sampling, a higher-order general-
isation of the cut-and-project method: a random polynomial map is drawn
from a distribution invariant under a natural action of SLd(Z), each site is
then retained independently with a probability determined by a measurable
function of the polynomial’s value, and the classical cut-and-project construc-
tion is recovered in the degree-one case. As a corollary, when the underlying
Zd-action is weakly mixing the measure must be a convex combination of
Bernoulli shifts, in the spirit of de Finetti’s theorem on exchangeable pro-
cesses. Our theorem also makes precise how the Howe–Moore theorem fails for
the pair (ASLd(Z), SLd(Z)).
Motivated by this classification, we formulate a conjecture for ASLd(R)-
invariant point processes on Rd, predicting that any such set decomposes into
a Poisson part and a quasicrystal part.
The proofs rely on the interaction
between the Host–Kra theory of characteristic factors, Zimmer’s theory of
dynamical cocycles of simple Lie groups, and the dynamics of SLd(Z)-actions
on homogeneous spaces.
1. Introduction
1.1. Invariant random subsets. An ASLd(Z)-invariant random subset of Zd
is a random subset of Zd whose distribution is an ASLd(Z)-invariant probabil-
ity measure on the space {0, 1}Zd, where ASLd(Z) acts by translations. Our goal
in this work is to understand and possibly classify all such sets. For d = 1, the
only condition is invariance under translations, so the problem is tantamount to
understanding shift-invariant measures on {0, 1}Z, which form a vast, unclassi-
fiable space [FRW11, Hjo01].
Similarly, it is hopeless to expect any clean de-
scription of translation-invariant measures on {0, 1}Zd for d ≥2. Invariance un-
der a richer group of transformations of Zd, such as ASLd(Z), should in princi-
ple force some rigidity of invariant measures. In the extreme case, by a classical
theorem of de Finetti [DF37], the only ergodic measures on {0, 1}Zd invariant un-
der all permutations of Zd correspond to Bernoulli random subsets. We refer to
[Dia77, DF80, Kir19, GK21] for a more modern treatment of de Finetti’s theorem.

1.2. Discrete Howe-Moore theorem and invariant random subsets of coset

1.3. Dreams about the continuous analogue. A point process on Rd can be
viewed either as a random discrete subset of Rd or a random locally finite sum of
Dirac masses [DVJ03]. These points of view are largely equivalent, but working
with measures is technically slightly more convenient. Let us write M(Rd) for the
space of locally finite sums of Dirac masses on Rd with the topology of weak-*
convergence on compact subsets.
An ASLd(R)-invariant point process on Rd is a process whose distribution is an
ASLd(R)-invariant probability measure on M(Rd). To our knowledge, it was Jens
Marklof who first asked for the classification of ASLd(R)-invariant point processes
on Rd.
This problem was our initial motivation to work on ASLd(Z)-invariant
subsets of Zd, in hopes that the solution of the discrete analogue will shed some
light on the continuous version.
We present two examples of ASLd(R)-invariant point processes on Rd. Their
constructions can then be combined to produce many more examples. The first is
a Poisson point process [DVJ03, Chapter 3], which is the continuous analogue of
a Bernoulli random set. Given η > 0, the Poisson point process Πη on Rd is the
unique random subset Πη of Rd satisfying the following properties:
(1) For any measurable subset A ⊂Rd, the expected number of points in Πη∩A
is ηvol(A).
(2) For any disjoint measurable subsets A, B ⊂Rd, the numbers of points in
Πη ∩A and Πη ∩B are independent.
The Poisson point process Πη depends only on the Lebesgue measure and η; it
is therefore invariant under any measure-preserving map. In particular, Πη is an
example of an ASLd(R)-invariant point process on Rd.
The second family of examples comes from cut-and-project sets, which were
studied and classified in [RSW23]. Pick m ≥d and fix a decomposition Rm =
Rd ⊕Rm−d. We select a compact window W ⊂Rm−d of positive Lebesgue measure.
Let L be a translate of a lattice in Rm and define Λ(L, W) as the projection of
L ∩(Rd × W) to Rd. The sets Λ(L, W) are always discrete; we call them cut-and-
project sets. Cut-and-project sets are also known as quasi-crystals or model sets
[BG13, Sen95].

Rm−d in such a way that the action on Rd is the standard one. Then, taking the
intersection with Rd × W and the projection to Rd are equivariant with respect

2. Notation, conventions and preliminaries
Throughout, given a group Γ, we say that (X, µ) is a Γ-system if X is a standard
Borel space equipped with a measurable Γ-action preserving the Borel probabil-
ity measure µ. A factor π : (X, µ) →(Y, ν) is a Γ-equivariant measurable map
between Γ-systems such that ν = π∗µ. Given a factor π, we naturally identify
L∞(Y ) with a sub-algebra of L∞(X) via f 7→f ◦π. By Mackey’s point realisation
theorem [Mac62], every Γ-invariant von Neumann sub-algebra of L∞(X) closed
under complex conjugation arises this way. Given two factors (X, µ) →(Yi, νi)
for i = 1, 2, the intersection L∞(Y1) ∩L∞(Y2) is again a Γ-invariant sub-algebra
and therefore defines a factor of X, which we denote Y1 ∧Y2. By construction,
L∞(Y1 ∧Y2) = L∞(Y1) ∩L∞(Y2).

2.4. Host–Kra–Ziegler theory for non-ergodic actions. Rather than nilpo-
tent Lie groups themselves, it is their homogeneous spaces that will be instrumental
in this work.
A nilmanifold is the quotient N/Γ of a nilpotent Lie group N by a lattice Γ ⊂N.

measure. If ∆⊂Γ is normal in N, we may identify N/Γ with (N/∆)/(Γ/∆). We

equivalently, Γ ∩Z(N) = {e}, where Z(N) denotes the centre of N.
