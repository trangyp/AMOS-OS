4HWC J2029+3641: a Pulsar Wind Nebula Powered by PSR J2030+3641?
Ziwei Ou,1 Jie Wang,2 and Songpeng Pei3
1Tsung-Dao Lee Institute, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai 201210, China

ABSTRACT
4HWC J2029+3641 is a newly discovered point source detected by HAWC, with no previously
identified TeV counterpart. The gamma-ray pulsar PSR J2030+3641, located 0.1◦from the source
center, is a middle-aged pulsar showing spin parameters similar to Geminga. Using Fermi-LAT data
spanning from August 2008 to February 2026, we performed binned maximum likelihood spectral
analysis in the energy range from 300 MeV to 1 PeV. A phase-resolved analysis was conducted to
separate the off-peak and on-peak emissions. No significant spatial extension was found for the off-peak
component. The off-peak spectrum exhibits strong curvature and is best described by an exponentially
cutoff power-law model. The observed radio-to-gamma phase lag and narrow peak separation favor an
outer-gap model for the gamma-ray emission.
Keywords: High Energy astrophysics (739) — Interstellar medium (847)
1. INTRODUCTION
The discovery of very-high-energy (VHE) and ultra-
high-energy (UHE) γ-ray sources has advanced signifi-
cantly with the operation of ground-based observatories
such as the High Altitude Water Cherenkov (HAWC)
(A. Albert et al. 2020) observatory and the Large High
Altitude Air Shower Observatory (LHAASO) (Z. Cao
et al. 2024). These facilities have opened a new win-
dow into the non-thermal universe, revealing numerous
Galactic sources that emit photons up to PeV energies
(Z. Cao et al. 2023). 4HWC J2029+3641 (R.A.=307.39◦
Dec.=36.68◦) is a new point source discovery by HAWC
(R. Alfaro et al. 2026). No identified TeV counterpart
was found in TeVCat.
A γ-ray pulsar J2030+3641 locates 0.1◦away from
the center of 4HWC J2029+3641 (D. A. Smith et al.
2023). With a spin period of 0.2 s, a spin-down lumi-
nosity of 3 × 1034 erg s−1, and a characteristic age τc of
500 kyr, PSR J2030+3641 is a middle-aged neutron star
with spin parameters similar to those of the Geminga.
The distance is about 1.5 to 3 kpc (F. Camilo et al.
2012).
Given the spatial coincidence between 4HWC
J2029+3641 and PSR J2030+3641, it is natural to inves-
tigate whether the TeV emission originates from a pulsar
wind nebula (PWN) powered by this pulsar. Middle-

aged pulsars are often associated with PWNe, which
form when the relativistic wind of particles from the pul-
sar interacts with the surrounding interstellar medium
or the supernova remnant ejecta (A. R. Bell 1992; Y. E.
Lyubarsky 2003).
PWNe comprise a highly magnetized relativistic
plasma, with particles being accelerated at the termina-
tion shock where the ram pressure of the pulsar wind
is balanced by the pressure of the highly relativistic
plasma (E. Amato & J. Arons 2006; B. Cerutti & G. Gi-
acinti 2020). These nebulae are efficient particle acceler-
ators, producing synchrotron emission from radio to X-
rays and inverse-Compton (IC) scattering of low-energy
photons (e.g., cosmic microwave background (CMB), in-
frared, optical) into the γ-ray band ( Lhaaso Collabora-
tion et al. 2021). Recently, the LHAASO source cata-
log reported 43 UHE γ-ray sources, most of which were
identified as PWNe or PWN candidates (Z. Cao et al.
2024). PWN are detected by Fermi-LAT at high-energy
(R. Rousseau et al. 2012; M. H. Grondin et al. 2013) and
ground-based telescopes at VHE ( H. E. S. S. Collabo-
ration et al. 2018). Particles from pulsar wind are ac-
celerated at the termination shock, which makes PWN
as important γ-ray sources. These are generated via IC
scattering of high-energy electrons.

and divide the pulsar phase into off-pulse and on pulse
(Section 3). The off-pulse and on-pulse analysis are pre-

2. DATA ANALYSIS
The Fermi-LAT data used in our study span from
2008 August 4 to 2026 February 24. We selected events
from the Pass 8 event class and used P8 R3 V3 Source
instrument response functions (IRFs). All γ-ray photons
within an energy range of 300 MeV to 1 PeV and within
a circular region of interest (ROI) of 15◦radius centered
on PSR J2030+3641 were considered. To minimize γ-
ray contamination from the Earth’s limb, γ-ray photons
with a zenith angle < 95◦were selected.
The spectral results presented in this work were
obtained by performing a binned maximum likeli-
hood fit with 40 logarithmically spaced bins in the
0.3 GeV to 1 PeV using the Science Tool gtlike.
Spectral and spatial models were constructed to in-
clude Galactic and isotropic diffuse emission compo-
nents (“gll iem v06.fits,”and “iso P8R3 V v06.txt,” re-
spectively) as well as known γ-ray sources within 15◦
of the pulsar, based on the Fermi-LAT Forth Source
Catalog (4FGL-DR4) (J. Ballet et al. 2023). The spec-
tral parameters of the sources within 4◦of our target
were left free. The spectral parameters of other sources
included were fixed at the 4FGL-DR4 values.
In the
pulsar phase-resolved analysis, photons within a specific
spin phase interval were selected.

6. DISCUSSIONS
6.1. The Magnetospheric Emission from PSR
J2030+3641
The derived γ-ray conversion efficiency η for PSR
J2030+3641 is about 142 ± 6 ± 100%. This seemingly
unphysical result is most likely driven by the system-
atic uncertainty in the pulsar distance as discussed in F.
Camilo et al. (2012), because Lγ scales as the square of
the distance and the nominal value of ˙E itself depends
on distance. The second, much larger error bar is de-
liberately included to accommodate such systematic ef-
fects, notably the uncertainty in the distance (DM-based
distance ∼460 pc versus dust-scattering halo distance
∼400) and possibly also the beaming factor.
The
lower part of the allowed range already places this pul-
sar among the most efficient γ-ray emitters, comparable
to the “Geminga-like” pulsars. Thus, the measurement
indicates that PSR J2030+3641 converts a very large
fraction of its spin-down power into high-energy radi-
ation, highlighting the need for an accurate geometric
distance to pin down the true conversion efficiency.

The observed γ-ray peak separation of ∆= 0.32 in
phase is relatively narrow compared to the canonical
value of ∼0.4-0.5 seen in many young γ-ray pulsars
(A. A. Abdo et al. 2013). This indicates a viewing geom-
etry where the line of sight cuts the emission cone close
to its center (K. P. Watters et al. 2009). Modeling of the
light curve and polarization profile for PSR J2030+3641
has constrained the magnetic inclination angle α to the
range of about 38◦to 54◦, implying a nearly orthogonal
rotator (α ∼50◦) (F. Camilo et al. 2012). Such a ge-
ometry is crucial for determining the three-dimensional
structure of the magnetosphere and the location of par-
ticle acceleration regions.
The measured phase lag of radio/γ phase gives δ ≈
0.3.
The positive lag (γ-ray arriving later) favors an
outer-magnetospheric origin for the high-energy emis-
sion, with the γ-ray production site located at a larger
altitude than the radio emission region.
This phase
lag may translate into an emission height for the ra-
dio pulses. The consistency of this lag with predictions
of the outer gap (OG) model further supports the sce-
nario where the γ-ray are produced in the outer magne-
tosphere via curvature radiation from relativistic parti-
cles accelerated in a vacuum gap (C. Venter et al. 2012;
C. Kalapotharakos et al. 2014).
The combination of a moderate peak separation and
a clear phase lag provides a discriminating test be-
tween competing emission models (M. Pierbattista et al.
2016). While the OG model naturally accommodates
both the narrow peak separation and the positive lag,
the two-pole caustic (TPC) model (A. G. Muslimov &
A. K. Harding 2004) would require extreme line-of-sight

6.3. Hadronic Scenario
The acceleration of protons in PWN and the subse-
quent production of hadronic γ-ray signals are theoreti-
cally feasible (M. Lemoine et al. 2015; A. M. W. Mitchell
& S. T. Spencer 2026). Hadrons can originate from three
main scenarios: (1) ions stripped from the pulsar surface
and accelerated in the magnetosphere, particularly if the
pulsar was born with a millisecond period; (2) mass-
loading of the pulsar wind by ions from the surrounding
interstellar medium, which are then accelerated in the
wind or at the termination shock (M. Lyutikov 2003;
G. Morlino et al. 2015); and (3) shock mixing or re-
acceleration of pre-existing material from the supernova
remnant or ambient medium, for example through re-
verse shock interactions or diffusion back into the PWN
(J. M. Blondin et al. 2001; Y. Ohira et al. 2018). In
these scenarios, accelerated protons can interact with
ambient gas via pp collisions, producing neutral pions
that decay into high-energy γ-ray , as well as charged
pions that ultimately yield neutrinos. Thus, from a the-
oretical perspective, PWNe are viable sites for hadronic
particle acceleration and the emission of hadronic sig-
nals.
However, despite this theoretical viability, current ob-
servational evidence suggests that hadronic emission
is at most sub-dominant in most PWNe.
In addi-
tion, stacking analyses by the IceCube collaboration
have found no significant neutrino excess from a sam-
ple of TeV-bright PWNe, placing upper limits on the
hadronic fraction (M. G. Aartsen et al. 2020). For in-
dividual sources like the Crab Nebula, lepto-hadronic
models remain speculative ( Lhaaso Collaboration et al.
2021; F. Aharonian et al. 2024), and constraints from
both γ-ray spectral features (e.g., the absence of a clear
pion-decay bump) and neutrino non-detections indicate
that any hadronic component must be energetically sub-
dominant.
Therefore, while hadronic acceleration in
PWNe is not ruled out and remains an exciting pos-
sibility ( Lhaaso Collaboration et al. 2021; Z. Cao et al.
2024).
6.4. Pulsar Halo
G. Giacinti et al. (2020) investigates the conditions
under which TeV γ-ray halos form around pulsars, dis-
tinguishing them from PWN. A key factor is the en-
ergy density of relativistic electrons ϵe relative to that
of the interstellar medium ϵISM.
Using two estima-
tors—one based on pulsar spin-down properties and the
other on TeV γ-ray luminosity—the authors find that
most TeV-bright sources have ϵe ≳ϵISM indicating that
their emission originates from within the PWN. Only
when ϵe ≲ϵISM (e.g., for Geminga and PSR B0656+14
(A. U. Abeysekara et al. 2017; M. Di Mauro et al. 2019))
can the emission be classified as a true halo, where elec-
trons have escaped into the unperturbed ISM. Consider-
ing ϵe = Einj/V and Einj = ˙E/τc, we obtain R > 27.1 pc

The evolutionary stage of the pulsar system also plays
a critical role in halo formation.
Halos are expected
only at late times (t ≳100 kyr), after the pulsar has
escaped its SNR and electrons diffuse into the ISM (D.
Hooper & T. Linden 2018). PSR J2030+3641 satisfies
such age condition of being halo. In contrast, younger
systems still confine most high-energy electrons within
the PWN or surrounding SNR. Although old, low-power
pulsars are numerous, their lower surface brightness and
rapid cooling of high-energy electrons mean that halos
contribute little to the total TeV γ-ray luminosity from
pulsars.
7. CONCLUSIONS
Based on the Fermi-LAT data, we have performed a
detailed phase-resolved spectral analysis of the region
around 4HWC J2029+3641 and its spatial coincidence
with the γ-ray pulsar PSR J2030+3641. Our main find-
ings are as follows. The off-pulse emission (which iso-
lates the putative pulsar wind nebula from the magne-
tospheric contribution) shows no significant spatial ex-
tension, with TSext = 2.1. This yields an upper limit on
the source radius of < 0.1◦, consistent with a compact
PWN rather than a diffuse halo.
The off-pulse spec-
trum exhibits strong curvature and is best described by
a PLSC model. The on-pulse spectrum is harder.
The curved off-pulse spectrum can be adequately re-
produced by IC scattering of relativistic electrons off the
CMB and infrared/optical photon fields, with a neb-
ular magnetic field of a few µG. However, a hadronic
scenario can not be ruled out. PSR J2030+3641 con-
verts a very large fraction of its spin-down power into
gamma rays, with an apparent efficiency η > 100%
when using the nominal DM distance.
This paradox
likely originates from systematic distance uncertainties
and/or a small beaming correction. The observed radio-
to-gamma phase lag δ ≈0.3 and narrow peak sepa-
ration (∆= 0.32) favour an outer-gap model for the
high-energy emission, placing the γ-ray production re-
gion near the light cylinder.
