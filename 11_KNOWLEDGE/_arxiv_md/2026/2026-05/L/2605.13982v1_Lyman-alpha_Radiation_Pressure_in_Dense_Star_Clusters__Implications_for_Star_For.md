Implications for Star Formation and Winds at Cosmic Dawn
Shyam H. Menon

ABSTRACT
Observations with the JWST in lensed fields have revealed that galaxies at cosmic dawn may con-
centrate their star formation in highly dense, compact, star clusters. The high columns and low metal-
licities encountered in their birth environments suggest that Lyman-alpha (Lyα) radiation pressure
may be crucial to their formation and evolution. In this study, we address this question by post-
processing snapshots from radiation hydrodynamic simulations of dense star cluster-forming clouds
(Σ∗≳103 M⊙pc−2) with a range of dust abundances (Zd = 0–0.1 Zd,⊙) using the colt Monte Carlo
code. We infer that Lyα is likely to have mild (∼10%) effects on the gas-to-star conversion efficiencies
(ϵ∗≳60%) for Zd ≳0.01 Zd,⊙, and even in dust-free environments, ϵ∗≳25% – much higher than
the ≲10% values typical of star-forming regions in the local Universe. This is because the densest
filaments dominating stellar mass assembly (n ≳104 cm−3) remain sub-Eddington (fEdd < 1). On
the other hand, the bulk of the gas volume (n ≲103 cm−3) has fEdd > 1, with noticeable fractions
having fEdd ≳10, implying that Lyα can launch dynamically significant winds from these systems
rapidly (≲4 Myr), with possible implications for ionizing photon escape and galactic outflows. The
Lyα force multiplier MF is highly sensitive to Zd, with MF ≲3 (≲500) for 0.1 Zd,⊙(dust-free) envi-
ronments respectively. Nevertheless, Lyα dominates over UV and IR radiation pressure at all values
of Zd ≲0.1 Zd,⊙, by factors of ∼3–500. Our results suggest that Lyα radiation pressure reinforces the
emerging picture of locally efficient, bursty star formation accompanied by rapid outflows in galaxies
at cosmic dawn.

1. INTRODUCTION
The epoch of cosmic dawn (z ≳6) marks the phase
of cosmic history when galaxies have begun assembling
stars and black holes within their host dark matter ha-
los, which subsequently drive the reionization of the in-
tergalactic medium.
The launch of the James Webb
Space Telescope (JWST; J. P. Gardner et al. 2023) has
revolutionized our view of this epoch, revealing galaxies
that are highly compact (C. M. Casey et al. 2023; T.
Morishita et al. 2025), dense (Y. Isobe et al. 2023; Ab-
durro’uf et al. 2024; M. W. Topping et al. 2025), and
metal/dust-poor (K. Nakajima et al. 2023; M. Curti
et al. 2023; T. M. Stanton et al. 2026), with resolved
views offered by gravitationally-lensed fields indicating
that the bulk of their star formation is concentrated
in massive, compact stellar clusters with stellar sur-
face densities (Σ∗) 2–3 orders of magnitude higher than
those in the local Universe (M. Pascale et al. 2023; L.
Mowla et al. 2024; A. Adamo et al. 2024; M. Messa et al.
2026; A. Claeyssens et al. 2026). The extreme physical
conditions realized in the interstellar medium (ISM) of
these galaxies has emerged as a stress-test for our un-
derstanding of star and galaxy formation regulated by
stellar/AGN feedback across cosmic time.
The accumulated observational evidence since the
launch of the JWST points to possible tensions with
pre-launch predictions. For instance, the discovery of
an overabundant population of UV-bright galaxies at
z ≳10, with respect to empirical and model-based
predictions, suggests modifications to the mapping be-
tween emergent luminosity and dark matter halo masses
at these epochs (e.g., S. L. Finkelstein et al. 2023; M.
Castellano et al. 2024; S. Carniani et al. 2025; R. P.
Naidu et al. 2026).

mation models incorporating these bottom-up insights
have yielded UV luminosity and stellar mass functions
that are in better agreement with observations than pre-
JWST models at z ≳10 (R. S. Somerville et al. 2025).
However, none of these studies fully consider the con-
tribution of Lyα radiation pressure.
The oversight is
related to its high dimensionality and complexity, as
well as the inherently demanding computational require-
ments for modeling it self-consistently. Since Lyα pho-
tons have extremely high cross-sections to abundantly
present hydrogen atoms, they undergo resonant scatter-
ing, and impart a cumulative radially-outward momen-
tum that can drastically exceed the energy carried by
each photon (T. F. Adams 1975; D. A. Neufeld 1990;
M. Bithell 1990; M. Dijkstra & A. Loeb 2008). These
considerations have motivated semi-analytic and Monte
Carlo radiative transfer calculations quantifying the mo-
mentum injection by Lyα radiation pressure in ideal-
ized slab/spherical cloud geometries and source distri-
butions (A. Smith et al. 2017b; L. Michel-Dansac et al.
2020; B.-X. Lao & A. Smith 2020; A. Smith et al. 2025),
finding that it can dominate over the momentum in-
put from other pre-supernovae mechanisms by factors
of ∼10–1000 (T. Kimm et al. 2018; O. Nebrin et al.
2025), depending sensitively on the dust optical depth at
Lyα wavelengths (G. M. Tomaselli & A. Ferrara 2021).
Attempts at incorporating these insights into subgrid
models for Lyα feedback in (low-mass/metallicity) sim-
ulations (T. Kimm et al. 2018; D. Han et al. 2022)
and semi-analytic models (O. Nebrin et al. 2023; A. U.

impacted by including Lyα feedback. Most recently, O.

Despite these

D. Manzoni & A. Ferrara
(2025) built on these analytical results with a 1D semi-
analytic shell model to explore the implications for the
star formation efficiency of clouds. They estimate that
Lyα radiation pressure precludes efficient star formation
(≳50%) in dense star clusters except for the most ex-
treme cases (Σ∗≳105 M⊙pc−2) and/or near-solar dust
abundances. Collectively, these results introduce com-
plications for interpretations of JWST observations that
invoke elevated star formation efficiencies.
It is important to note that neutral hydrogen (H i) gas
in star-forming clouds are highly turbulent and filamen-
tary, with the sources of stellar radiation and the ion-
ized hydrogen (H ii) regions they form being distributed
in space rather than point sources—complications that
cannot be accounted for fully in semi-analytic calcula-
tions, yet are likely to have implications for Lyα mo-
mentum injection. For instance, in supersonic turbulent
media across scales, stars form and accrete through fil-
aments with densities/columns that exceed the average
in the cloud by factors ∼10–100, remaining relatively
unaffected by feedback as gravity dominates in them;
however, lower density columns are susceptible to ejec-
tion by radiation pressure, with the transition column
where gravity wins out being dependent on the momen-
tum injection rate per unit stellar mass (T. A. Thomp-
son & M. R. Krumholz 2016). This explains why 3D nu-
merical simulations quantifying the impact of radiation
pressure from stellar UV and dust-reprocessed IR pho-
tons on dusty gas reveal that the outcomes can depart
from simple analytical expectations (M. R. Krumholz &
T. A. Thompson 2012; M. R. Krumholz & T. A. Thomp-
son 2013; A. L. Rosen et al. 2016; S. Raskutti et al. 2017;
J.-G. Kim et al. 2018; S. H. Menon et al. 2023). These
differences are aggravated by additional 3D effects that
diminish the impact of radiation pressure: such as the
tendency for the photon flux to preferentially be higher
in low density gas (i.e. the matter–radiation anticorrela-
tion) (M. A. Skinner & E. C. Ostriker 2015; S. H. Menon
et al. 2022a), and the vector cancellation of fluxes in
stellar populations with multiple radiation sources (J.-
G. Kim et al. 2018; S. H. Menon et al. 2023). It remains
to be seen to what extent the aforementioned impacts
of Lyα radiation pressure in regulating star formation
and driving winds are affected by similar mechanisms in
realistic star-forming environments.
In this paper, we address this question directly by
computing the Lyα radiation pressure force in post-
processing on snapshots from a suite of radiation-
hydrodynamic (RHD) simulations of the formation
of dense star clusters from turbulent, self-gravitating
clouds (S. H. Menon et al. 2025), using the Monte Carlo
Lyα radiative transfer code colt (A. Smith et al. 2015;
W. McClymont et al. 2025). We span a range of dust
abundances representative of metal-poor environments
at the epoch of reionization (Zd ∼0–0.1 Zd,⊙) allow-
ing us to quantify the sensitivity of Lyα feedback on
dust opacity in a fully 3D, turbulent medium with ra-
diation sources formed self-consistently through turbu-
lent fragmentation. We focus on two central questions:
(i) whether Lyα radiation pressure can prevent efficient
star formation during the assembly of dense star clus-
ters at cosmic dawn, and (ii) whether it can drive dy-
namically significant winds once star formation has sat-
urated. The remainder of this paper is organized as fol-
lows. In Section 2 we describe our RHD simulations, the
Lyα radiative transfer post-processing, and our treat-
ment of dust.
Section 3 presents our results for the
Lyα force-gravity competition in our simulations, and
the momentum budget relative to other radiative feed-
back mechanisms. We discuss broader implications in
Section 5 and summarize our conclusions in Section 6.
Finally in Appendix A and B we quantify the sensitiv-
ity of our results to our analysis approach and numerical
choices.
2. METHODS
2.1. Simulations of Dense Star Cluster Formation
For this work, we use the gas and stellar properties ob-
tained with radiation hydrodynamic simulations of the
formation of a young star clusters from turbulent, self-
gravitating clouds. The simulation setups are similar to
that described in S. H. Menon et al. (2025), with ad-
ditional physics implemented and different choices for
the subgrid stellar population as described below. We
briefly summarize the most relevant aspects, focusing on
the differences, and refer the reader to the earlier works
for more detailed information.
The simulations are initialized with a turbulent, self-
gravitating molecular cloud of mass 106 M⊙and ra-
dius 10 pc, corresponding to a surface density of ∼
3000 M⊙pc−2. The cloud is evolved in a adaptive mesh

refinement (AMR) domain of size 40 pc × 40 pc × 40 pc,
with a maximum effective resolution of 10243 cells (min-
imum cell size ∼0.03 pc), with regions outside the cloud
a 100 times less dense. Turbulence is driven using the
method described in C. Federrath et al. (2010b, 2022), to
achieve a velocity dispersion σv ∼23 km/s sufficient for
the cloud to be marginally stable (αvir = 2Ekin/Egrav =
2), with a power spectrum E(k) ∝k−2 containing a nat-
ural mixture of solenoidal and compressive modes. The
turbulence is driven for 5 eddy turnover times (5L/σv)
without self-gravity to achieve a steady state for the
turbulence+thermochemistry, using the TURBSPHERE ap-
proach of using a static potential to confine the gas in
this phase (H. B. Lane et al. 2022). This serves as the
initial conditions for the subsequent phase of turbulent
collapse where self-gravity is turned on. Unresolved star
formation is represented by sink particles using the im-
plementation described in C. Federrath et al. (2010a,
2011), which we interpret as unresolved subclusters; for
the purpose of assigning radiative feedback, each sink
is assumed to be fully sampled in the IMF. We com-
pute the corresponding stellar radiative output with the
BPASS v2.2 model (J. J. Eldridge et al. 2017; E. R.
Stanway & J. J. Eldridge 2018).
Radiation hydrodynamics is performed with the Vari-
able Eddington Tensor–closed VETTAM implementation
(S. H. Menon et al. 2022b) in the FLASH code (B. Fryxell
et al. 2000; A. Dubey et al. 2008). We solve the radia-
tive transfer in four bands (Lyman continuum, Lyman–
Werner, photoelectric, and a gray infrared band), which
together provide the heating rates, and photoioniza-
tion and photodissociation rates for different chemical
species and dust, with dust cooling via IR emission
treated self-consistently in the IR band assuming dy-
namical coupling to the gas (S. H. Menon et al. 2022a).
These rates are included in a minimal chemical network
(i.e. as described in J.-G. Kim et al. 2023) using the im-
plementation described in an upcoming paper (Menon
et al., 2026, in prep). The chemical network solves for
the non-equilibrium abundances of H, H2, H+ and e−,
and adopts a steady-state approximation for the C/C+
ionization balance, and charge transfer dictated O/O+
abundances; the CO abundance is based on fits to PDR
models of M. Gong et al. (2017).
While the network
is highly idealized, we find that it can capture the ther-
mal evolution of the gas as well as an extensive chemical
network, and therefore suffices for the purposes of this
study. We refer the reader to earlier work (S. H. Menon
et al. 2023, 2025) for the gas/dust opacities and numer-
ical choices for evolving the hydrodynamics/radiation
transport.
2.2. Post-processing Lyα Radiative Transfer
We post-process snapshots of our RHD simulations
with the Cosmic Lyα Transfer (colt) code (A. Smith
et al. 2015, 2019, 2022; W. McClymont et al. 2025). We
construct the gas distribution in colt using the simu-
lated gas densities and temperatures, and we use the po-
sitions, masses, and ages of sink particles to define the
sources of UV radiation. We neglect any contribution
from a intragalactic and metagalactic UV background,
which is expected to be subdominant compared to the
radiation field produced by the massive clusters in our
simulated volumes. For each sink, we compute the emer-
gent source spectrum with BPASS, consistent with the
stellar-population model used in the on-the-fly RHD cal-
culations. We however, increase the spectral resolution
well beyond the three discrete bands captured on-the-fly
in the simulations.
We then use colt to compute the ionization state
of H, He, and metals by propagating 108 Monte Carlo
(MC) photon packets, iterating until the total H+ re-
combination rate in the simulation volume is converged
to 0.1%. Although our RHD simulations evolve H ion-
ization on the fly, we adopt the colt solution for the
subsequent Lyα transfer to be more consistent, and as
the MCRT approach in post-processing can potentially
achieve higher angular and spectral resolution.
That
being said, we find that the ionization states with VET-
TAM are in good agreement with colt for the spatial
structure of H ii regions and the distribution of H ion-
ization fractions.
Given the converged ionization structure, colt per-
forms a ray-based transfer of individual Lyα photon
packets through the medium, including contributions
from (i) radiative recombination emission, and (ii) col-
lisionally excited Lyα emission. The code follows res-
onant scattering in neutral hydrogen and includes ab-
sorption and scattering by dust.
For the purposes of
this paper, our primary diagnostic is the momentum
imparted to the gas by Lyα photons. We estimate the
local momentum imparted to each gas cell by computing
a vector-sum over the momentum increments deposited
by the ensemble of photon packets undergoing scatter-
ing events within that cell. We note that this converges
faster than path-based momentum estimators.
To reduce the computational cost of the resonant-
scattering random walk, we employ moderate core skip-
ping, i.e. a random excursion into the wings of the line
facilitated by an interaction with an atom of sufficient
velocity, preventing repetitive scatterings with negligi-
ble spatial diffusion (S.-H. Ahn et al. 2002). While this
technique can significantly speed up calculations with-
out affecting emergent Lyα photon statistics/profiles, it

relevant to core skipping is xcrit, which is in units of the
dimensionless Doppler frequency x ≡(ν −ν0)/∆νD for

Since the photon optical depth and therefore
the number of scatterings drop further away from the
core, higher values of xcrit will be computationally faster
but represent the internal radiation field less accurately.
We adopt a constant value of xcrit = 2 in our cal-
culations as a conservative balance between computa-
tional efficiency and physical accuracy. Another numer-
ical choice of relevance here is the number of photon
packets launched (nph), which is important for ensur-
ing convergence of the Lyα radiation field, especially at
large optical depths (P. Camps & M. Baes 2018). We
adopt a total of nph = 106 photon packets in this study.
In Appendix B, we quantify the dependence of our re-
sults on these choices by repeating the calculations for
xcrit ∈{1, 2, 3} and nph ∈{105, 106, 107}, and demon-
strate that the conclusions of this study are insensitive
to these choices.
2.3. Treatment of Dust
Dust can act as a source of continuum opacity for
Lyα photons, destroying Lyα photons and thereby set-
ting an upper limit on the Lyα radiation pressure force
(e.g., S. P. Oh & Z. Haiman 2002; G. M. Tomaselli &
A. Ferrara 2021; O. Nebrin et al. 2025). The choice of
dust opacity used in the colt Lyα radiation transport
step is therefore likely to be critical to the inferred dy-
namical impact of Lyα radiation pressure. In the sim-
ulations, we assume a uniform dust-to-gas ratio (Zd)
that scales linearly with the initial metallicity (Z) and
does not evolve; i.e. we do not include metal enrich-
ment or dust injection, so both Z and Zd are fixed in
space and time. We focus on two metallicities—0.1 Z⊙
and 0.01 Z⊙—chosen to probe the parameter regime rel-
evant for observed galaxies with the JWST at the Epoch
of Reionization. We adopt values of Z⊙= 0.0134 and
Zd,⊙= 1/162 in this work and the J. C. Weingartner &
B. T. Draine (2001) grain optical properties using the
Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC) dust size distribution
model. We repeat these choices for their corresponding
colt post-processing calculations.
In addition to these, we repeat the colt post-
processing steps (ionization and Lyα transport) for the
0.01 Z⊙simulation outputs, but with (i) an assumed
dust abundance of 4×10−4 Z⊙and (ii) a dust-free case6.
The former case is motivated by empirical findings of a
super-linear relation between Z and Zd at Z ≲0.1 Z⊙
(A. R´emy-Ruyer et al. 2014), as the the empirical fit
for Z = 0.01 Z⊙corresponds to Zd = 4 × 10−4 Zd,⊙.
However, as we will discuss in Section 5.4, it is the dust
opacity at Lyα wavelengths that is relevant here, and an
alternate grain size distribution can correspond to this
range of values even at higher Z. On the other hand, the
dust-free case represents an upper limit on the impact
of Lyα radiation pressure.

3.3. Effectiveness of Lyman α radiation pressure
In this section we quantify the momentum injection
by Lyα radiation pressure, and compare it to other rel-
evant radiative feedback mechanisms that are included
in the simulation on-the-fly (i.e. UV and IR radiation
pressure on dust/gas). We compute the so-called Lyα
force multiplier MF (M. Dijkstra & A. Loeb 2008; T.
Kimm et al. 2018; A. Smith et al. 2019; G. M. Tomaselli
& A. Ferrara 2021), which represents the boost in the
total momentum imparted by Lyα photons over the ini-
tial photon momentum (i.e. the single-scattering mo-
mentum ∼LLyα/c), akin to the force-multiplication
factor used for metal absorption lines in stellar atmo-
spheres (J. I. Castor et al. 1975) or the trapping fac-
tor (ftrap) used for dust-reprocessed IR radiation (e.g.,
T. A. Thompson et al. 2005; N. Murray et al. 2010;
S. H. Menon et al. 2022a).

tional to the ratio ttrap/tlight, where ttrap represents
the time Lyα photons remain trapped in a cloud due

2020) where τcl is the Lyα line center optical depth

4. SEMI-ANALYTIC MODELS TO QUANTIFY
THE EFFECTS OF Lyα RADIATION PRESSURE
Our results demonstrate that the inclusion of Lyα ra-
diation pressure can render noticeable fractions of gas in
star-cluster forming environments super-Eddington—up
to ∼40% (3%) for dust-free (0.1 Z⊙) cases (Figure 3).
However, we also find that there is a time evolution in
the super-Eddington fraction, with fEdd substantially
lower at the early stages of the evolution when the bulk
of the stellar mass is assembled. This complicates our
attempts to quantify the potential impacts of Lyα ra-
diation pressure on the star formation efficiency, as one
needs to examine the competition between gravity and
radiation pressure simultaneously with the stellar assem-
bly. This necessitates modeling Lyα feedback on-the-fly
in 3D RHD simulations, which remains beyond the ca-
pabilities of existing codes. Nevertheless, we can em-
ploy semi-analytic models of star formation, which have
been tested on similar numerical simulations of radia-
tion pressure-regulated star formation, to explore the
relative impact of Lyα radiation pressure on the star
formation efficiency.

5. DISCUSSION
Our results suggest a consistent picture in which Lyα
radiation pressure is a critical source of early stellar feed-
back in dense star cluster-forming regions at dust abun-
dances typical of cosmic dawn (≲0.1 Z⊙), dominating
over UV and IR radiation pressure on dust/gas. How-
ever, at the same time, the effective impact of Lyα radi-
ation pressure depends on its competition with gravita-
tional forces (fEdd), for which we find a diverse range of
outcomes depending on the density of the gas: lower
density (n ≲103 cm−3) gas has fEdd > 1, whereas
fEdd < 1 for the high-density (n > 104 cm−3) filaments
through which most of the stellar growth occurs. There-
fore our results suggest that the star formation efficiency
is likely to remain high in the dense star clusters form-
ing at high-z. On the other hand, a significant fraction
of the volume-filling gas can be ejected by Lyα radia-
tion pressure, suggesting implications for the visibility
of light from the cluster, ionizing photon escape, and
launching outflows from the galaxy.

5.2. Ionizing photon escape and wind launching
While we find that most of the high-density gas—
which carries substantial fractions of the total mass—is
sub-Eddington to Lyα radiation pressure, most of the
volume is in lower density gas which is super-Eddington
(Figure 4). This can be confirmed visually in Figure 5,
where a large fraction of the solid angles around the
(central) stellar population has fEdd > 1, suggesting
that they would be ejected by Lyα radiation pressure.
Moreover, our semi-analytical star formation model in-
dicates that progressively larger fractions of the gas mass
is ejected on shorter timescales as Lyα becomes more ef-
fective (Figure 7). Such rapid, efficient dispersal of dense
gas around dense cluster-forming regions likely has im-
plications for the escape of UV photons from the stellar
populations and the launching of outflows by the star-
burst.
For instance, clearing the dense gas surrounding young
stellar populations remains the most prominent bottle-
neck to the escape of LyC photons from galaxies typ-
ical of the high-z Universe (J.-P. Paardekooper et al.
2015; M. Trebitsch et al. 2017). The rapid drop of the
LyC emission rate of young stellar populations as mas-
sive stars die in supernovae explosions (≳3 Myr) make
models that allude to pre-supernova feedback in clearing
sightlines for LyC escape attractive for facilitating the
growth of ionized bubbles (C. A. Carr et al. 2024; A. E.
Jaskot 2025). Clearing dusty gas in the centers of galax-
ies has also been invoked to explain the extremely bright
and blue spectrum of galaxies with minimal evidence of
dust attenuation (A. Ferrara et al. 2023; A. Ferrara 2023;
R. Marques-Chaves et al. 2026). On these fronts, numer-
ical simulations of star cluster-forming clumps indicate
that LyC and UV radiative feedback may already evac-

5.3. Nuances imposed by clumpy gas distributions
Turbulence driven by a combination of sources in
the ISM induces clumpy, anisotropic gas distributions
and disordered velocity fields (e.g., C. Federrath et al.
2008) that influence the impact of radiation pressure on
dust/gas in H ii regions (S. Raskutti et al. 2016; J.-G.
Kim et al. 2017; S. H. Menon et al. 2022a, 2023). For
the case of Lyα radiation pressure, the impact of turbu-
lence is borne out in several ways. For instance, turbu-
lent density fluctuations can produce channels with low
τLyα through which photons can escape without signifi-
cant momentum boosts. O. Nebrin et al. (2025) estimate
that the Lyα force multiplier MF,Lyα is suppressed by
a factor ∼10 for gas with turbulent Mach numbers of
M ∼10 with a scaling MF,Lyα ∝M−8/9. Preliminary
comparisons of the MF,Lyα we deduce in our calculations
with analytic uniform density sphere/slab solutions sug-
gest similar levels of suppression, although we defer a
systematic comparison of models to a follow-up paper.
Suppression of MF,Lyα by factors ∼few may also be in-
troduced by velocity fluctuations in the turbulent flow
or in outflowing gas (O. Nebrin et al. 2025), although
only for high relative velocities.
Our calculations on the other hand highlight yet an-
other effect introduced by turbulent density distribu-
tions: namely, the arrangement of gas into filamentary
structures, with gas densities much higher (and lower)
than the average (C. Federrath et al. 2008; C. Federrath
& R. S. Klessen 2012).
This has implications on the
competition between Lyα radiation pressure and grav-
ity: i.e. even for a given (average) value of MF,Lyα and
Lyα momentum injection rates, higher column density
filamentary structures have lower values of fEdd, with
fEdd < 1 for sufficiently high Σ > Σcrit where Σcrit is
given by Equation (8) for Lyα. This implies that even if
the average column of gas is ≲Σcrit—which is the case
for the clouds modeled in this work—a noticeable frac-
tion of gas mass that are at > Σcrit remain resistant to
Lyα radiation pressure, necessitating larger efficiencies
than implied by assuming a uniform density. In fact,
this nuance is true for all forms of radiation pressure (S.
Raskutti et al. 2017; S. H. Menon et al. 2023). A corol-
lary of this point is that if numerical simulations do not
resolve the density substructure in H ii regions, where
the competition between radiation pressure and gravity
play out, they may overestimate the impact of feedback
even if they model the radiative transfer with a perfect
method. Moreover, there is the added concern raised by
M. R. Krumholz (2018) that it matters at what scale the
radiation pressure–gravity competition plays out, and
applying momentum at larger scales necessitated by the
numerical resolution may further bias towards feedback.
The aforementioned points likely influence the strong
effects on bound cluster formation found in the galaxy-
scale simulations with a subgrid Lyα feedback model in
T. Kimm et al. (2018), where they impart a force of
∼MFLLyα/c uniformly around star particles based on
the local gas column of the cell. The values of MF they
use are likely overestimated as it does not consider sup-
pression by turbulence and velocity gradients (O. Nebrin
et al. 2025), and the lack of resolved substructure pre-
cludes the survival of high-density filaments that con-
tinue to feed star formation15. These challenges moti-
vate the development of subgrid models of Lyα feedback
calibrated to high-resolution turbulent clouds in the fu-
ture.

6. CONCLUSIONS
We quantify the dynamical importance of Lyα radia-
tion pressure in dense star cluster-forming environments
to interpret its potential to regulate star formation and
eject gas in galaxies at cosmic dawn. We post-process
snapshots of radiation-hydrodynamic simulations of the
collapse of turbulent GMCs forming systems with prop-
erties resembling observed lensed star clusters (M⋆≳
106 M⊙, Σ∗≳103 M⊙pc−2 A. Adamo et al. 2024; A.
Claeyssens et al. 2026) with the Monte Carlo Lyα radia-
tive transfer code colt. We span a range of dust abun-
dances, Zd = {0, 4 × 10−4, 0.01, 0.1} Zd,⊙, representa-
tive of the parameter space relevant to galaxies observed
with the JWST at the Epoch of Reionization. We quan-
tify the competition between Lyα radiation pressure and
gravity, its dependence on gas properties and dust abun-

dance, the momentum injected by Lyα photons, and its
relative importance to other radiative feedback mecha-
nisms. We incorporate these insights into semi-analytic
star formation models to infer the potential effects of
Lyα were it included on-the-fly. Our key findings are:
1. Lyα radiation pressure is unlikely to preclude
efficient star formation: The fraction of the ini-
tial cloud mass that is super-Eddington (fEdd > 1)
to Lyα radiation pressure remains low at all times,
≲3% (≲35%) for 0.1 Zd,⊙(dust-free limit) respec-
tively.
Especially at early times when stellar mass
is being assembled. This is because the densest gas
that dominates stellar mass assembly (n ≳104 cm−3)
has fEdd < 1 for all dust cases, with fEdd > 1
predominantly for the bulk of the volume-filling gas
(n ≲103 cm−3). Our semi-analytic model incorpo-
rating these insights find that the gas-to-star conver-
sion efficiency ϵ∗would remain ≳50% with the in-
clusion of Lyα in environments with non-zero dust
abundances. Even in the dust-free limit ϵ∗≳25%,
much higher than values observed for typical clouds
in the local Universe (∼10%).
2. Lyα radiation pressure can drive dynamically
significant winds once star formation satu-
rates: Once the SFE has saturated, MfEdd>1/Mcloud
peaks for all dust cases, with the super-Eddington
mass fractions comparable to the total remaining gas
mass, suggesting that Lyα can eject a significant frac-
tion of the residual gas. Furthermore, ≳5%Mcloud ∼
5 × 104 M⊙has fEdd > 10 in the dust-poor environ-
ments, implying that a substantial amount of gas is
susceptible to acceleration to large velocities.
Our
semi-analytic model indicates that Lyα-induced gas
ejection can remove obscuration around the stellar
sources on timescales quicker by up to factors ∼2 to
values ≲4 Myr, with implications for ionizing pho-
ton escape and the attenuation of radiation from the
stellar population.
3. Lyα radiation pressure dominates over UV
and IR radiation pressure at all dust abun-
dances: The Lyα force multiplier MF is highly sen-
sitive to the dust abundance, spanning nearly four or-
ders of magnitude across our parameter space, from
MF ≲10 for Zd ≳0.1 Zd,⊙to MF ≳100 at low
dust abundances. Nevertheless, even at the highest
dust abundance considered, Lyα radiation pressure
exceeds the UV and IR radiation pressure forces by a
factor of ∼10, rising to ≳500 in the dust-free limit.
This highlights Lyα radiation pressure as the dom-
inant radiative feedback mechanism in dense, low-
metallicity star-forming systems.
4. Accurate dust opacities at Lyα wavelengths
are essential for robust feedback predictions:
Our results confirm that continuum absorption of
Lyα photons by dust is a critical factor in regulat-
ing its dynamical impact, with dust-poor environ-
ments (Zd < 0.01 Zd,⊙) highly susceptible to its ef-
fects.
Given empirical evidence for a super-linear
relation between metallicity and dust-to-gas ratio at
Z ≲0.1 Z⊙(A. R´emy-Ruyer et al. 2014), and the pos-
sibility of distinct grain size distributions in the early
Universe that could further reduce the dust opacity
at Lyα wavelengths, the effective dust opacity may be
considerably lower than a simple linear scaling with
metallicity would suggest, underscoring the impor-
tance of careful dust modeling when assessing the role
of Lyα feedback at cosmic dawn. In addition to mak-
ing Lyα feedback more effective, lower dust opacities
would also boost the visibility of high-z Lyα emitting
galaxies.
We also discuss implications on star formation (Sec-
tion 5.1), photon escape and winds (Section 5.2), nu-
ances imposed by clumpy gas distributions (Section 5.3)
and our assumptions on the dust opacity (Section 5.4).
Overall, we infer that Lyα would likely reinforce the
emerging picture of (locally) efficient bursts of star for-
mation and rapid outflows in galaxies at cosmic dawn.
In follow-up work we will systematically explore similar
quantities across a range of star cluster-forming environ-
ments, compare more closely to predictions from ideal-
ized models of Lyα feedback, and use these to build im-
proved subgrid models to incorporate Lyα feedback in
cosmological galaxy formation simulations.
Our work
demonstrates that Lyα radiation pressure is a critical
feedback mechanism to capture on-the-fly in the high-z
Universe, which we anticipate will be realized as efficient
numerical methods are developed toward this end.
