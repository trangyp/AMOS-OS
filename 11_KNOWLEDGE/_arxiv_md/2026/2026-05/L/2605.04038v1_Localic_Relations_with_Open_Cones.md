ABSTRACT. Localic relations are relations internal to the category of locales,
forming the point-free analogues of set-theoretic relations, and providing the
general backdrop of localic order theory. This work studies open cone localic
relations, whose source and target maps are open, and provides a frame-theoretic
description via point-free up and down closure operators, called cones.
The cones arising from open cone localic relations form join-preserving and
parallel pairs of maps on the underlying frame. Axiomatising this structure, a
frame equipped with such a pair of cones is called a conic frame. The main
construction shows that, conversely, any conic frame induces a localic relation
with open cones, whose cones are exactly the given ones. The main result is an
adjunction with identity counit between the category of locales equipped with
open cone localic relations, and the opposite of the category of conic frames.
The unit gives a strongly dense inclusion of an open cone localic relation into
the relation induced by its own cones.
Fixed points of the adjunction are those relations recovered by their cones,
and include kernel pairs of open maps and all weakly closed localic relations with
open cones. As a special case we recover Kock’s Godement theorem for locales.
Moreover, for fixed points, internal reflexivity and transitivity are completely
characterised in terms of the cones.

1. INTRODUCTION
Localic relations are relations internal to the category Loc of locales (recalled
in Section 2), and therefore serve as point-free analogues of set-theoretic relations.
As such, they form the general backdrop of localic order theory, providing the
foundations for point-free analogues of topological order theory [Nac65], as well
as localic lattice theory [Joh85].
Localic relations also form a natural starting point for (constructive) Stone-type
dualities for spaces with additional structure [Pri72; Esa74] or lattices with ad-
ditional structure [JT51; Pet96]. For instance, localic partial orders are used by
Townsend in [Tow96; Tow97] to provide a constructive Priestley duality for dis-
tributive lattices (see also [KL07]). Localic preorders specifically could provide a
localic counterpart to other frame-theoretic treatments of ordered spaces such as bi-
frames [BBH83], d-frames [JA10], and the recently introduced ad-frames [Gou26].
The present work grew out of similar considerations, in an attempt to refine the
duality between ordered topological spaces and ‘ordered locales’ in [HS24] (dis-
cussed more in Section 11). One of the general themes in duality theory is to find
topological representations of algebraic structures. Here we approach the question
from the localic side: admitting locales as the point-free counterparts of topolo-
gical spaces, then structurally we can define localic relations and localic preorders
as those internal to the category Loc. The question then becomes what these struc-
tures correspond to algebraically. This paper provides one such ‘externalisation’
procedure, explained further below.
Localic equivalence relations are important within locale theory itself for des-
cent theory, and to describe quotients and proper maps [Ver94; Ple97; Man23].
For example, a sufficient condition for effectiveness of localic equivalence rela-
tions was found in [Koc89], which we generalise in the present work (Section 9.3).
Subsequently, localic relations appear in topos theory [Joh02], where they are for
instance used to represent étendues (‘locally localic’ toposes) via certain sheaves
of localic equivalence relations [KM92]. Since localic equivalence relations may
be regarded as special types of localic groupoids, this also connects to the more
general Joyal-Tierney representation theorem of Grothendieck toposes [JT84], and
the more recent work using quantale theory [Res07; QR19].
Connecting to logic, a localic relation could be seen as a point-free analogue of
a Kripke frame, providing the relational semantics for modal logic [Kri63; Zac25].
Thus localic relations could provide a natural setting for intuitionistic [Sim94],
(point-free) topological [AvBB03], or positive modal logics [Dun95; CJ97]. In
turn, this might also connect to transition systems and model checking in computer
science [BK08]. Finally, we mention that localic preorders could find application
in the mathematical foundations of physics, in particular for localic or constructive
treatments of Lorentzian causality theory [Pen72] or causal set theory [Bom+87].
All that being said, working internally we lose the convenient point-wise de-
scription of relations in terms of pairs xRy. Instead, a localic relation R on X is
a sublocale R

concepts that can be technically difficult to handle: sublocales and product locales.

frame-theoretic description of the class of localic relations whose source and target
maps are open, called open cone relations, in terms of localic up and down closure
operators. While strong, the open cone assumption appears also in the literature

In this section we recall the necessary background theory on frames and loc-

complete lattice L is a partial order that has all meets (greatest lower bounds) and
joins (least upper bounds). We denote the corresponding order of a lattice by ⊑, to
be thought of as a generalised inclusion relation of regions. A frame is a complete
lattice L in which the infinite distributivity law holds:

Denoting by Top the category of topological spaces and continuous maps, (gen-

2.1. Sublocales. Sublocales are the appropriate localic analogue of subspaces in
point-set topology. There are several equivalent ways of presenting them, each with
their own advantage in different contexts. We briefly describe the most important
ones here, but leave full details and their precise translations to [PP12, §III]. First
recall the following basic terminology, which will also be used later in the paper.
Definition 2.2. A function c: P →P on a partially ordered set is called:
• inflationary if x ⩽c(x) for all x ∈P;
• subidempotent if c(c(x)) ⩽c(x) for all x ∈P;
• idempotent if c(c(x)) = c(x) for all x ∈P;
• a closure operator if it is monotone, inflationary and subidempotent.
Fix a frame L. A sublocale set is a subset S ⊆L that is closed under all meets
of L, and closed under the Heyting implication in the sense that for all s ∈S
and x ∈L we have x →s ∈S. A frame congruence is an equivalence relation ≡
on L such that ≡⊆L × L is a subframe (closed under finite meets and all joins).
A nucleus is a closure operator ν : L →L that preserves binary meets.
Definition 2.3. A sublocale of a locale X is equivalently one of the following:
• an (isomorphism class of an) extremal monomorphism A
X;
• a sublocale set S of OX;
• a frame congruence ≡on OX;
• a nucleus ν on OX.
Inclusion of sublocales is denoted ⊆, which is structurally just a subobject in-
clusion of extremal monomorphisms, but can be more concretely represented by
literal subset inclusions of sublocale sets, or as reverse inclusion of frame congru-
ences or nuclei. For fixed X, this gives a lattice Sℓ(X) of sublocales, which is in
fact a coframe.

12. DISCUSSION
We finish the paper with some general comments, discussion, and directions for
future research.
12.1. Non-open cones. A natural next step would be to attempt a similar recon-
struction for arbitrary localic relations R on X in terms of their generalised internal
cones ↑, ↓: Sℓ(X) →Sℓ(X). At face value, these cones will capture more of the
structure of the initial relation. It is yet unknown if there is a counterexample
similar to 9.4, where distinct localic relations produce the same internal cones.
Moreover, since the proofs in the present work rely deeply on openness and the
frame theoretic techniques of generating sublocales, the construction would likely
look quite different.
12.2. Internal cones. Continuing that thought, as briefly mentioned at the start
of Section 4, since epimorphisms in Loc are not preserved under pullback [Pit83;
Ban90], the general theory of relations with respect to orthogonal factorisation sys-
tems [Kle70] shows that composition of localic relations is not associative. Picking
a non-pullback-stable epimorphism e, an explicit non-associative triple of relations
can be constructed from its graph. As a consequence, there exists a transitive rela-
tion R whose internal cones ↑, ↓are not subidempotent (cf. Counterexample 10.10).
In fact, this counterexample works generally for any category with an orthogonal
factorisation system (E, M) where E is not pullback stable.
An explicit example in the spatial world may be illustrative. Equip Top with
the orthogonal factorisation system (dense image, closed embedding) [Kle70, §2],
which is not pullback stable. Subobjects are just closed subsets, and so internal

internal transitivity corresponds to set-theoretic transitivity. On the other hand,

the specialisation order of a topological space, the relation has open cones in only

raises the question of whether the present adjunction Cone ⊣Rel could be gen-
