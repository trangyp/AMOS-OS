The global well-posedness of (NS) for initial velocity in L2(R2) has been known since
Leray’s classical work [24]. In [22], Kato proved that the d-dimensional Navier–Stokes equa-
tions are locally well-posed for arbitrary initial data in Ld(Rd), and globally well-posed for
sufficiently small data. On the vorticity side, the Cauchy problem on R2 with initial vorticity
in L1 was studied in [6, 7, 23], where analogues of Leray’s and Kato’s results were established.
Cottet [10] and, independently, Giga–Miyakawa–Osada [17] extended the existence theory to
finite measure initial data in M(R2). Uniqueness in M(R2) was later proved by Gallagher
and Gallay [12], building on the partial results in [17, 23, 15, 13].
For the long-time behavior of (NS) on R2, many works have shown that vorticity solutions
with initial data in L1 are asymptotically governed by the linear heat equation with the same
total mass. This was first established by Giga and Kambe [16]; a dynamical-systems approach
was later developed in [14]. In [8], Carpio observed a deep connection between this asymptotic
behavior and the uniqueness of the fundamental solution for the vorticity equation. Finally,
Gallay and Wayne [15] proved the asymptotic approximation for arbitrary initial data in
L1. More precisely, if ω0 ∈L1(R2), then the corresponding vorticity converges to the Oseen
vortex with mass M =
R
R2 ω0 dx dy.
For the Navier–Stokes system (NS) on R2
+ with Navier boundary conditions, one may
extend the vorticity oddly across the boundary by setting ω(t, x, −y) = −ω(t, x, y). The
extended function then solves the vorticity equation on the whole plane and satisfies ω|y=0 =
0. Consequently, the half-plane problem with Navier boundary condition remains globally
well posed for initial data in M. Moreover, the refined asymptotic expansions on R2 obtained
in [15] imply the corresponding long-time behavior on R2

refer to [1, 11, 32] and the references therein for related studies of (NS) on the half plane
with no-slip boundary conditions.

Near monotone shear flows such as the Couette flow, the dynamics are known to enjoy
important stabilizing effects, including enhanced dissipation and inviscid damping, see [2, 4,
9, 20, 21, 29, 33] and references therein. In this sense, the flow is expected to be more stable,
and this feature is also reflected in the long-time behavior obtained in [26]. We refer the
reader to that work for further discussion on the relation between such asymptotic behavior
and other stability phenomena [5, 28, 27, 3, 25] near Couette flow.
On the other hand, in many physical experiments, Couette-type flows are produced by
boundary forcing rather than posed in the whole space. This naturally leads us to investigate
the problem in domains with boundaries. As a first step, the half-plane provides the simplest
setting in which one can isolate and understand the effect of the boundary on the large-time
dynamics.
The purpose of the present paper is to extend these results to (1.2) on the half-plane R2
+.

whole-space one, because the term y∂xω changes parity in y. Another essential difference is
