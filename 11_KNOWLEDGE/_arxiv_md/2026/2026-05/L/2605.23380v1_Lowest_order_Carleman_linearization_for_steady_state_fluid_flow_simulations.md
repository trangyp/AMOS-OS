Lowest order Carleman linearization for steady state fluid flow simulations
Luca Cappelli
1 and Sauro Succi
1

00161 Roma, Italy
(*Electronic mail: luca.cappelli@iit.it)
(Dated: 25 May 2026)
It is shown that the lowest (second) order truncation of the Carleman linearization of the fluid equations (C2) recov-
ers not only the initial transient of the time evolution but also its late stage, namely the steady-state solution. This
asymptotic property is first proved analytically for the decaying logistic with external forcing and then shown to hold
to a significant degree of accuracy also for the fairly more complex case of two-dimensional fluid flows at moderate
Reynolds number. This time-asymptotic property opens interesting prospects for the simulation of steady-state solu-
tions of the fluid equations on quantum computers.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The development of quantum algorithms for the simula-
tion of fluid equations and transport phenomena has attracted
considerable interest in recent years1–9. A key obstacle in
this direction arises from the inherently nonlinear structure
of the Navier–Stokes equations, which is incompatible with
the linear framework underlying quantum simulation. Dif-
ferent strategies have been proposed to circumvent this ob-
stacle10–15; among them, Carleman linearization has emerged
as the prominent approach16–22, providing a systematic way
of transforming a finite-dimensional nonlinear system into an
infinite-dimensional linear one23. For all practical purposes,
the infinite hierarchy is truncated (or closed) at a finite level
K.
Increasing the truncation order generally improves the
approximation accuracy and extends the time interval over
which the truncated dynamics remains close to the exact non-
linear solution24,25. Such convergence horizon decreases with
increasing Reynolds number, owing to the growth of nonlin-
ear interactions and small-scale activity26–28. Beyond such
time horizon, the Carleman procedure is no longer expected
to yield an accurate solution, and sometimes may even di-
verge altogether29–31. In this paper we show that the low-
est order Carleman truncation (K = 2), of the Navier-Stokes
equations proves capable of recovering the correct asymptotic
steady state, at least for flows at moderate Reynolds num-
bers below hundred. This property is first discussed in the
context of a highly simplified but representative toy model,
the logistic decay equation with external forcing, and then
demonstrated via actual simulations of the two-dimensional
Kolmogorov flow. This opens up interesting prospects for the
simulations of steady-state flows on quantum computers. It is
also shown that C2 performs a coarse-graining in time, thus
pointing to the potential prospect of quantum-centric simula-
tions of fluids, whereby quantum computers would handle the
largest eddies, leaving the short ones for classical simulation.
The paper is structured as follows. In Sec. II, we revisit the
forced logistic equation and show that the stationary state can
be recovered using a second–order Carleman approximation.
Moreover, we provide a statistical interpretation of the Carle-
man embedding, relating it to a sort of temporal filtering. This
naturally motivates the physically relevant test case of a forced
fluid system, linearized via the Carleman approach, which is
introduced in Sec. III. Finally, in Sec. IV, we present numer-
ical simulations demonstrating that the second–order Carle-
man approximation (C2 hereafter) correctly recovers the sta-
tionary state in three distinct regimes.

V.
COMMENT ON QUANTUM-CENTRIC TURBULENCE
MODELLING
In essence, the C2 approximation captures the initial stage
of the fluid evolution as well as the slow modes that dom-
inate the long–term solution. Referring again to the logis-
tic analogy, the point is that Carleman linearization is basi-
cally a multiscale expansion in a properly rescaled time scale
˜t = 1−e−at. The short-time limit, at ≪1, ˜t ∼at, controls the
convergence of the early-stage of the evolution, but it is clearly
inadequate to describe the long-term evolution at ≫1 due to
secular terms. This is where the rescaled timescale ˜t comes
into play: since ˜t remains in the range 0 ≤˜t < 1 all along the
evolution, including the asymptotic limit t →∞, the low–order
C2 approximant never diverges and proves able to capture the
time asymptotic behaviour. As to time filtering, this may open
up the possibility to employ C2 in future quantum-centric sim-
ulations of turbulent flows, whereby the quantum computer
would be in charge of the large (and slow) eddies, leaving the
smaller scales to a classical computer. This is essentially the
spirit of so-called Large-Eddy-Simulations, where the dynam-
ics of large eddies results form an explicit filtering of the small
ones 35. Such practices are ubiquitous in fluid dynamics and
other nonlinear transport phenomena36–38. At this stage this
is a mere speculation which needs systematic work in order to

VI.
SUMMARY
Building on analytical insights from the decay logistic
equation with external forcing, we have shown that a second–
order Carleman approximation recovers the stationary state of
fluid flows at moderate Reynolds numbers up to about 100.
This setting also suggests an interpretation of the Carleman
embedding from the perspective of statistical physics, where
the k-th order Carleman variable evolves according to a tem-
poral coarse–graining of the power–replica xk. The resulting
errors can thus be interpreted as filtered time fluctuations. We
then extended these ideas to a physically relevant case of a
two-dimensional fluid flow under periodic forcing. Using the
second–order Carleman approximation of the Navier–Stokes–
Hamilton–Jacobi equations34, we studied the asymptotic be-
haviour of a two-dimensional forced Kolmogorov-like flow,
for various Reynolds numbers up to Re ∼100. Across all the
examples considered in this work, the second–order Carleman
approximation accurately captures the stationary state of the
2D incompressible, viscous fluid moving under the effect of
the external field. Moreover, C2 exhibits a coarse-graining
mechanism in time which proves capable of smoothing out
flow oscillations but misses nevertheless sudden changes of
the flow configuration as the Reynolds number is increased.
These findings suggest a potentially significant shift in per-
spective: the second–order Carleman approximation may be
used to compute stationary states of fluid flows, a goal of sig-
nificant practical relevance, for instance in aerodynamic de-
sign.
The inherent time-filtering of the Carleman linearization
may also open up the perspective of quantum-centric fluid
simulations, whereby quantum computers would handle
the very large eddies, leaving the small ones to classical
simulations. To date, this is however only a speculation which
needs systematic inspection to be placed on solid quantitative
grounds.
Future developments will focus on a more systematic anal-
ysis of the correspondence between temporal coarse-graining
and Carleman linearization, possibly beyond C2, as well as
numerical investigations with different initial conditions and
higher values of Reynolds numbers using larger grids.
