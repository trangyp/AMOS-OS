VERONICA ARENA AND TERRY DEKUN SONG, WITH AN APPENDIX BY DHRUV RANGANATHAN
ABSTRACT. The logarithmic Hilbert scheme of a logarithmic curve parametrizes subschemes on the expanded
degenerations of the curve that are transverse to the boundary. We prove that the logarithmic Hilbert scheme of
points on a smooth pointed curve is an iterated weighted blow-up of the symmetric product of the underlying
curve. In doing so, we explicitly identify the blow-up centers, weights, and give them modular interpretations. As
applications, we calculate their integral Chow rings in terms of those of the symmetric products. Key ingredients
in our work include two recent results: the integral Chow ring formula of weighted blow-ups and a weighted ana-
logue of Castelnuovo’s criterion for blow-downs. We recover the folklore result the logarithmic Hilbert scheme
of toric P1 is a toric stack, and the Appendix by Dhruv Ranganathan outlines a complementary approach using
Chow quotients.

1.1. Toric geometry on P1. We note that the geometry of the logarithmic Hilbert stacks is particularly con-
crete when C = P1 and D ≤0 + ∞. In this case,
Corollary C. The logarithmic Hilbert stacks Hilbn(P1|0) and Hilbn(P1|0 + ∞) are toric stacks.
Indeed, the above logarithmic Hilbert stacks are weighted blow-ups of Pn ∼= Symn(P1) along torus-
invariant strata. Tracking the weights in the blow-ups, we identify the toric fan of Hilbn(P1|0) as the succes-

1.4. Perspectives. While logarithmic moduli spaces are primarily motivated by applications in enumer-
ative geometry, it is of independent interest to relate their geometry to the well-studied non-logarithmic
moduli spaces. Our work gives a concrete first example in this direction.
Let C →Exp(C|D) be the universal curve over the stack Exp(C|D) of expanded degenerations, and
let Hilbn(C ) be the relative Hilbert scheme of length n, so that the morphism Hilbn(C ) →Exp(C|D) is
representable. While being universally closed, the stack Hilbn(C ) is neither quasi-compact nor separated
and has positive dimensional stabilizers.

The intermediate moduli stacks Hilbn(C|D)≤i are open substacks of Hilbn(C ) that are smooth, proper,
and contain Hilbn(C \ D) as a dense open substack. In other words, they are candidates to produce well-
behaved moduli spaces from the stack Hilbn(C ). From this point of view, the iterated weighted blow-up
descriptions in Theorem A set up a VGIT-style wall-crossing between logarithmic and classical Hilbert
scheme of points on a smooth marked curve, in which the Gm-quotients arise from action by the rubber
torus. Instances of weighted blow-ups as wall-crossing morphisms include Shah’s work on degree-2 K3
surfaces [Sha80] and its application to intersection theory [COP24], K-moduli of plane curves [ADL24], and
moduli of stable genus one curves [ALI+23, §7].
More generally, given a moduli stack M over (fine and saturated) logarithmic schemes, there often ex-
ists a piecewise linear space S –this is a stack over fs log schemes that admit log modifications by Artin
fans [Ken23]–and a morphism M →S that is representable by an algebaric stack. Producing proper
open substacks of M and studying their wall-crossing morphisms is a systematic way to relate logarithmic
moduli spaces and their non-logarithmic parallels.
1.5. Related work. The general moduli problem of logarithmic Hilbert schemes is motivated by approach-
ing enumerative geometry via degenerations: they are constructed for smooth pairs by Li–Wu [LW15], for
curves in simple normal crossings pair three-folds by Maulik–Ranganathan [MR24] and in full generality
by Kennedy-Hunt [Ken23]. Examples and their concrete geometric descriptions point to connections to the
toric geometry of secondary polytope by work of Kennedy-Hunt on linear systems of curves in toric sur-
faces [KH24] and geometric invariant theory by work of Tschanz and Shafi–Tschanz on logarithmic Hilbert
schemes of points on degenerations of K3 surfaces [Tsc23, ST25]. We also acknowledge the forthcoming
work of Suraj Dash, which gives a combinatorial classification of GIT quotients arising from the relative
Hilbert schemes of points over the stack of expansions in a more general setting.
1.6. Future directions. As logarithmic Hilbert schemes are motivated by the study of moduli spaces via
degeneration, it is natural to investigate logarithmic Hilbert and quot schemes of nodal curves as well as
the relative logarithmic moduli spaces of the universal curve over Mg,n. Analogous wall-crossing mor-
phisms relating them to the ordinary moduli spaces can potentially lead to comparison formulas between
enumerative invariants coming from the logarithmic and non-logarithmic moduli spaces.
The weighted blow-up picture explored in this work seems to be applicable to general logarithmic sym-
metric products and has application to the theory of logarithmic 0-cycles. In the surfaces case, combining
this approach with [MR25, §4] should produce a complete description of the cohomology and Chow ring
of the logarithmic Hilbert schemes of points on surfaces.
Viewing smooth points as divisors on curves, it will be worthwhile to study the moduli of logarithmic
hypersurfaces from the perspective of wall-crossing. For instance, it will be interesting to produce inter-
mediate moduli spaces that interpolate between the logarithmic linear system of curves on toric surfaces
studied by Kennedy-Hunt [KH24] and the ordinary linear system.

5. LOGARITHMIC HILBERT SCHEMES VIA WEIGHTED BLOW-UPS
5.1. Relevant results for weighted blow-ups. In this subsection we will assume the basic definitions of
weighted blow-ups following the work of Quek–Rydh [QR21]. A shorter introduction to weighted blow-
ups can be found in [ALI+23].
Intuitively, weighted blow-ups can be thought of as birational transformations replacing a chosen center
Z by a divisor E, where E →Z has the structure of a bundle of weighted projective stacks P(a1, . . . , ad).
Choosing the (strictly positive integer) weights a1, . . . , ad can be a subtle task, as it requires to assign Gm-
weights to local trivializations of the normal bundle of Z in X that are compatible across the open cover.
In this paper we will restrict our attention to weighted blow-ups with extra regularity properties, namely
what Quek–Rydh defined as regular weighted blow-ups.
Remark 5.1. As a weighted blow-up is obtained as a quotient of the deformation to the weighted nor-
mal cone via the action of Gm, the weighted normal cone is at the heart of understanding the geometry
and intersection theory of weighted blow-ups. In our setting, the weighted blow-up centers are regularly
embedded, and we will study their weighted normal bundles both in this section (to prove the weighted
blow-up construction) and in Section 6.1, where we compute its top Chern class to apply the weighted ana-
logue of Keel’s formula. It is important to remark that the weighted normal bundle is a twisted weighted
vector bundle (using the definition of [QR21]) and it is not necessarily isomorphic to the normal bundle.

APPENDIX A. TORIC STRUCTURE OF Hilbn(P1|0 + ∞) VIA CHOW QUOTIENTS
by Dhruv Ranganathan
Corollary C of this paper states that that the logarithmic Hilbert scheme Hilbn(P1|0 + ∞) is toric; indeed,
it is presented as a sequence of toric blowups of a toric variety. A closely related variant of this space is a
well-known toric variety: the toric variety associated with the secondary polytope of the length-n interval.
See results of Kennedy-Hunt [KH24] and also [MR24, §6]. Using this, one can check that the logarithmic
Hilbert scheme Hilbn(P1|0 + ∞) is toric. Since Hilbn(P1) is Pn, which is also toric, one might guess the
intermediate case Hilbn(P1|0). We briefly sketch this and leave many details to the reader.
Consider the “rubber” logarithmic Hilbert scheme Hilbn
rub(P1|0 + ∞), parameterizing cycles on expan-
sions of P1 along 0 and ∞, but where two points are identified if they differ by C∗-scaling in the main
component. The moduli problem is represented by a Deligne–Mumford stack with logarithmic structure,
by the same methods as [MR24].
Separately, the linear system Pn of degree-n divisors on P1. There is a C∗-scaling action on Pn induced
by the standard C∗-action on P1. Let H denote the (normalized) Chow quotient of Pn by this action. The
Chow quotient parameterizes isomorphism classes of C∗-equivariant degenerations of P1 with a degree-
n divisor. This follows from, for instance, the “finite case” of Alexeev’s work on complete semi-abelian
moduli [Ale02, §2.10-12], or by work of Ascher–Molcho [AM16]. As such, there is a moduli map:
h : Hilbn
rub(P1|0 + ∞) →H
Proposition A.1. The morphism h is an isomorphism on coarse moduli spaces.
Proof. By the moduli interpretation, one easily checks that h is finite. It is also clearly birational, since the
interior parameterizes n points on C∗. The Chow quotient is normal by construction, so the map is an
isomorphism by Zariski’s main theorem.
□
As mentioned above, the Chow quotient of the linear system is the toric variety associated with the
secondary polytope of the length-n interval [KSZ91]. Therefore,
Corollary A.2. The coarse moduli space of Hilbn
rub(P1|0 + ∞) is a toric variety.
One can adapt this argument to the non-rubber moduli space using the approach of Kennedy-Hunt
[KH24]; we do not pursue this here. Nevertheless, inspecting the map
