Abstract
We study ℓ-colourings of (v, k, λ)-BIBDs (balanced incomplete block designs) where,
within each block, one colour is absent and each of the ℓ−1 other colours appears exactly
k
ℓ−1 times. We establish several necessary conditions for such colourings to exist. We
also use these coloured BIBDs to provide new necessary conditions for the existence of
Hadamard matrices, affine planes, and twin prime powers.
Keywords: colouring; balanced incomplete block design; Hadamard matrices; affine
planes; twin prime powers

Introduction
If v, k, λ ∈Z+ with 2 ≤k < v, then a (v, k, λ)-BIBD (balanced incomplete block design) is
a pair (V, B) where V is a set of v points and B is a collection of k-element subsets of V ,
called blocks, such that for any pair of distinct x, y ∈V , there are exactly λ blocks containing
both x and y. The value λ is called the index of the design. If C = {c1, . . . , cℓ}, then an
ℓ-colouring of a BIBD (V, B) is a surjective mapping f : V →C, where the elements of
C are called colours. The colour class of ci ∈C is the set of all points in V coloured ci.
A locally equitable colouring of (V, B) is a colouring such that for any B ∈B and any two
colour classes C1 and C2,
|C1 ∩B| −|C2 ∩B|
 ≤1.

were considered in [1, 2, 8, 9, 18, 35, 36]. Locally equitable colourings of group divisible de-
signs and maximum packing designs are studied in [7]. Luther and Pike [19, 20] investigated
such colourings on BIBDs and characterized all BIBDs which admit a locally equitable

future work in Section 7.

Necessary Conditions for 0-ULSE Coloured BIBDs
Since k < v by the definition of a BIBD, every BIBD on v points admits a 0-LSE v-colouring.
We call 0-LSE v-colourings of a (v, k, λ)-BIBD trivial and when ℓ< v, we call 0-LSE ℓ-
colourings nontrivial. It follows immediately from the definition of 0-LSE ℓ-colourings that
ℓ> 1. We now show that 0-LSE 2-colourings do not exist.
Lemma 3.1. If there exists a 0-LSE ℓ-coloured BIBD, then ℓ> 2.

Proof. If a 0-LSE 2-coloured BIBD exists, then every block contains points that are all the
same colour. Suppose for a contradiction, we have a 0-LSE 2-coloured BIBD. Recalling that
a colouring is surjective, there must be two points, x and y, that are coloured differently.
By the definition of a BIBD, there exists a block containing both x and y and so we have a
block containing both colours. This is a contradiction and so there are no 0-LSE 2-coloured
BIBDs.
In [19, 20], it was shown that if ℓ≥k, then a locally equitable ℓ-coloured (v, k, λ)-BIBD
exists if and only if ℓ= v. We use an analogous argument to give a similar result for 0-LSE

Lemma 3.2. Let D be a (v, k, λ)-BIBD and let ℓ≥k + 1. A 0-LSE ℓ-colouring of D exists
if and only if ℓ= v.
Proof. If ℓ= v, then assigning every vertex its own unique colour will result in a 0-LSE
ℓ-colouring of D.
Suppose instead that k + 1 ≤ℓ< v and suppose for a contradiction there exists a 0-LSE
ℓ-colouring of D. Since there are fewer colours than points, there exist two points, say x
and y, such that x and y are assigned the same colour, say c1. Let B be a block containing
the pair xy. By the definition of a 0-LSE colouring, one colour, say c2, is absent from B.
Since |B| = k, this leaves at least k −1 colours to be assigned to k −2 elements in B that

the colour classes for the colours c1 and c3 respectively, then |C1 ∩B| −|C3 ∩B| > 1. This
contradicts our assumption that the BIBD is 0-LSE ℓ-coloured.

A General Construction
In this section we give a framework for constructing 0-ULSE coloured BIBDs. This frame-
work requires two designs as input ingredients: resolvable BIBDs and transversal designs.
A BIBD (V, B) is resolvable if its blocks can be partitioned into sets Π1, . . . , Πr such that
for each 1 ≤i ≤r, ∪B∈ΠiB = V and B1 ∩B2 = ∅for any distinct B1, B2 ∈Πi. The sets
Π1, . . . , Πr are called resolution classes. We abbreviate resolvable BIBD to RBIBD.
If k, n, λ ∈Z+ and n > 2, then a transversal design TDλ(k, n) is a triple (V, G, B) where
V is a set of kn points, G is a partition of V into k n-element subsets called groups, B is
a collection of k-element subsets, called blocks, that contain exactly one point from each
group, and for every pair of distinct points x, y ∈V that are not in the same group, there
are exactly λ blocks containing both x and y. In particular, no two points in the same group
are in the same block. If λ = 1, then we write TD(k, n) instead of TD1(k, n). The following
result follows from simple counting arguments.
Lemma 4.1. If a TDλ(k, n) exists, then each point occurs in exactly λn blocks and there
are exactly λn2 blocks.

An affine plane of order n is an (n2, n, 1)-BIBD. Using well known results on affine planes,

proof. It was originally proven in [34] that there exists a projective plane of order n when n
is a power of a prime. Consequently, we have the following existence result for affine planes.
Lemma 5.5. If n ≥2 is a prime power, then there exists an affine plane of order n.
Determining if there exists a projective plane of an order that is not a power of a prime

credited to Tarry [32] and proving that there is no projective plane of order 10 was done
by Lam, Thiel, and Swiercz [17]. It remains open whether there exists a projective plane of
order 12, which is the smallest open case.

resolvable.

Question 7.6. Are there infinitely many twin prime powers?

By Theorems 5.3, 5.7, and 6.4, if a Hadamard matrix, an affine plane, or a pair of twin
prime powers is given, then a 0-ULSE coloured BIBD can be constructed. Consequently,
the existences of the families of 0-ULSE coloured BIBDs stated in Theorems 5.3 and 5.7
are necessary conditions for the existences of Hadamard matrices and affine planes respec-
tively. Hence, proving the nonexistence of these 0-ULSE coloured BIBDs of specific orders
would prove the nonexistence of the corresponding Hadamard matrices and affine planes.
Furthermore, proving that there are only finitely many of the 0-ULSE coloured BIBDs from
Theorem 6.4 would prove that there are only finitely many twin prime powers.
