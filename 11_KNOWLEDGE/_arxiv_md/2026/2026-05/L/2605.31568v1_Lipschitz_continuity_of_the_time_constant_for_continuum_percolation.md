Abstract. We consider the Boolean model of continuum percolation, where points are placed in Rd by
a Poisson point process and pairs of points with distance at most 1 are connected by an edge. The time
constant is the limiting ratio of the chemical distance (i.e. graph distance) to the Euclidean distance for pairs
of distant connected points. Yao, Chen, and Guo [27] established the existence of a time constant in the
supercritical regime. We show that above the critical intensity, the time constant is a Lipschitz continuous
function of the intensity. The proof adapts a recent argument of Can, Nakajima, and Nguyen [3] to the
continuous setting.
1. Introduction
Given a point set X ⊂Rd, we denote by G(X) the graph with vertex set X and edge set consisting of
{x, y} ⊂X such that ∥x −y∥≤1. Let Xλ denote the homogeneous Poisson point process of intensity λ
on Rd. We consider the infinite random geometric graph G(Xλ), which we abbreviate by Gλ. The critical
intensity λc = λc(d) is defined by
λc = inf {λ > 0 : P(Gλ contains an infinite component) > 0} .

infinite component, which we denote by C∞.

number of edges in a path between them. For all x, y ∈Gλ, the chemical distance between x and y is

2. Previous Work
The first results on time constants appear in the context of First Passage Percolation (FPP) on graphs,
where edges of a graph are given i.i.d. random weights, and the random metric considered is the minimum
total weight of a path between two points; this metric is called the passage time. The chemical distance of
percolation can be viewed as a special case of the passage time where all weights lie in {1, ∞} (although many
results in FPP assume that the weights are almost surely finite, or an even stronger integrability condition).
The first regularity result for the time constant for FPP on Z2 appeared in Cox [6] under a uniform
integrability assumption on the weight distribution. Still restricted to dimension two, Cox and Kesten [8]
removed the integrability condition by considering geodesics for truncated passage times. They introduced
the idea of bypassing edges with weights that are too large, and established control over the length of these
bypasses. Kesten [17] extended this result to Zd, d ≥2 by allowing the truncation to be large enough to
control the size of the clusters of closed edges. However, this technique still does not allow for edges with
infinite weight. A new approach by Garet, Marchand, Procaccia, and Th´eret [13] established continuity
of the map from the underlying distribution to the time constant for general laws on [0, +∞] without any
moment assumption. Dembin [10] improved the regularity result for a generalized percolation model in which
first-passage percolation occurs in a random environment to a log-Lipschitz result. Building upon this work,
Cerf and Dembin [4] refined the approach by considering a multi-scale renormalization and proved Lipschitz
continuity of the time constant in generalized first-passage percolation. The main ideas were to prove the
average size of a bypass is small and to properly choose the different scales of the renormalization process.
Keeping the same approach of looking at bypasses in “good” boxes under the same model and coupling,
Can, Nakajima, and Nguyen [3] also established Lipschitz continuity of the time constant in generalized FPP.
The insight here is to use lattice animals and a one-step renormalization with Russo’s formula to bound the
length of the detour caused by removing an edge in the geodesic.

4. Approximating the chemical distance
Recall that for v ∈Rd, we denote by ˚v the (random) point of the infinite cluster of Gλ which is closest in
