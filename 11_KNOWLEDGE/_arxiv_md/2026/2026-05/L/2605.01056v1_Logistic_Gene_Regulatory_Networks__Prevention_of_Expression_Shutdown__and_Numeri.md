A B S T R A C T
Hill functions, the standard tool for modelling gene regulatory networks, carry three structural
flaws when the cooperativity exponent is non-integer: loss of global smoothness, silent complex-
valued arithmetic corruption of ODE trajectories, and an identically zero basal production rate
that traps bistable models in off-states. Logistic functions 𝑓±, being globally 𝐶∞, real-valued
for all arguments, and strictly positive at zero, resolve all three simultaneously. For a two-gene
negative-feedback oscillator, local asymptotic stability is established for all positive parameters
via the Routh–Hurwitz criterion, and no Hopf bifurcation is possible without time delays.
For bistable positive autoregulation, saddle-node thresholds are characterised through explicit
transcendental equations; with biophysically grounded E. coli parameters, basal logistic produc-
tion drives off-state escape in ≈44 min while the Hill model remains permanently trapped.
The 11-gene Traynard cell-cycle Boolean network is translated automatically via the product-
of-logistics De Morgan formalism and integrated without warnings, all variables remaining
bounded and non-negative. The De Morgan framework places every repressor threshold at a
positive measurable concentration, whereas the weighted-sum formulation of Samuilik et al.
places repressor critical points at negative concentrations, rendering them biologically inert. On
an 80-gene Boolean-derived ODE system with 𝑛= 3.509, the Hill solver entered silent complex-
valued contamination at 𝑡≈52.64 and terminated near 𝑡≈63–65; the logistic formulation
completed 𝑡∈[0, 200] without a single warning. The always-positive production rate ensures full
controllability, enabling sliding mode, model predictive, and feedback-linearisation strategies
where Hill-based formulations fail.

1. Introduction
Gene regulatory networks constitute the fundamental control architecture of living cells, orchestrating the
spatiotemporal patterns of gene expression that underlie development, homeostasis, and adaptation to environmental
change. Mathematical modelling of these networks has become an indispensable tool for understanding the dynamical
behaviours that emerge from transcriptional interactions—oscillations, bistability, multistability, and chaos [36, 12]—
and for guiding the rational design of synthetic gene circuits in biotechnology and medicine. At the heart of nearly every
such model sits the Hill function, a formulation introduced over a century ago to describe cooperative ligand binding
and subsequently adopted as the near-universal representation of sigmoidal gene regulatory responses. Its activation
form ℎ+(𝑥, 𝜃, 𝑛) = 𝑥𝑛∕(𝑥𝑛+ 𝜃𝑛) and repression counterpart ℎ−(𝑥, 𝜃, 𝑛) = 𝜃𝑛∕(𝑥𝑛+ 𝜃𝑛) are intuitive, mechanistically
grounded, and have been applied successfully to models of the lambda phage lysis–lysogeny decision, the lac and
gal operons in Escherichia coli, developmental patterning in Drosophila, and mammalian cell cycle control, among
many others. The Hill coefficient 𝑛quantifies cooperativity, 𝜃denotes the half-maximal concentration, both carry clear
physical interpretations, and the sigmoidal shape faithfully captures the switch-like regulatory transitions characteristic
of biological decision-making.
Yet this success conceals three substantial hidden costs. The most immediately consequential—and least often
acknowledged—is that the activation Hill function vanishes identically at zero input, ℎ+(0, 𝜃, 𝑛) = 0. This is math-
ematically convenient but biologically wrong. Experimental studies across bacterial operons, eukaryotic promoters,
and synthetic circuits consistently show that genes are never fully silent; minimal transcription persists at 0.1–10%
of maximal expression due to stochastic promoter binding, incomplete repressor occupancy, nucleosome dynamics,
and intrinsic chromatin accessibility. This basal activity is not a nuisance to be corrected but a functional feature: it
prevents irreversible shutdown in bistable systems, maintains transcriptional machinery in a primed state for rapid
induction, and shifts expression distributions from multimodal to unimodal, reducing phenotypic noise. Models built
on Hill functions either ignore this reality entirely or introduce ad hoc additive offsets of the form ℎ+ + 𝜀, which add
an arbitrary parameter, disrupt the normalised [0, 1] response range, and have no grounding in molecular mechanism.
A second set of limitations concerns mathematical structure and numerical behaviour. When the Hill coefficient
takes a non-integer value—as it routinely does when fitting experimental dose-response curves, yielding values such
as 𝑛≈1.39, 2.73, or 3.52—the function loses global smoothness. For 𝑛∈(𝑘, 𝑘+ 1), derivatives of order greater than
𝑘diverge at the origin, restricting the function to 𝐶𝑘smoothness and rendering unavailable the higher-order analytical
tools—centre manifold reduction, normal form analysis, Hessian-based optimisation—that modern bifurcation and
parameter estimation workflows depend upon. The power-law form 𝑥𝑛is also numerically problematic for non-integer
exponents: it requires evaluating the transcendental composition 𝑒𝑛ln 𝑥, which accumulates floating-point error near
zero and, critically, becomes complex-valued whenever any trajectory component transiently overshoots to a negative
concentration, as every adaptive ODE solver will produce from rounding errors alone. The result is silent corruption: the
solver continues integrating a complex-valued surrogate system, producing smooth and visually plausible trajectories
that are not solutions to the true biological model, with no visual artefact to alert the modeller. Beyond smoothness, the
maximum slope of ℎ+ at its inflection point equals 𝑛∕(4𝜃), coupling threshold position and transition steepness into a
single expression; the two cannot be adjusted independently, which obscures biological parameter interpretation and
complicates the rational tuning of synthetic circuits where sensitivity and decision threshold must be set as separate
design variables. Finally, the rational form 𝑥𝑛∕(𝑥𝑛+ 𝜃𝑛) has no closed-form inverse for general 𝑛, blocking feedback
linearisation and gradient-based model predictive control, while the zero production rate at 𝑥= 0 creates fundamental
controllability gaps from which Hill-based dynamics cannot recover without external intervention.
The logistic function resolves all three limitations simultaneously. Its activation form 𝑓+(𝑥, 𝜃, 𝜆) = 1∕(1+𝑒−𝜆(𝑥−𝜃))
and repression form 𝑓−(𝑥, 𝜃, 𝜆) = 1∕(1 + 𝑒𝜆(𝑥−𝜃)) are globally 𝐶∞, real-valued for all arguments including negative
ones, and strictly positive at zero concentration for all finite 𝜆and 𝜃. The self-referential derivative identity 𝑓′ =
𝜆𝑓(1 −𝑓) reduces Jacobian entries to products of function values, eliminating fractional exponents from stability
analysis entirely. The closed-form logit inverse 𝑓−1(𝑦) = 𝜃+ 𝜆−1 ln(𝑦∕(1 −𝑦)) enables exact feedback linearisation.
The parameters 𝜃and 𝜆are fully decoupled: the threshold can be repositioned without altering the transition slope,
and vice versa; both map directly to biologically measurable quantities—𝜃to dissociation constants or half-maximal
effective concentrations, and 𝜆to effective cooperativity. Most importantly, the non-zero output at 𝑥= 0 means that
basal expression is built into the function’s shape rather than appended to it, with its magnitude controlled by the product
𝜆𝜃through parameters that already define the regulatory response, requiring no additional degrees of freedom.

Logistic functions have a long and productive history in statistics, machine learning, and neural network approxi-
mation theory, and have recently appeared in GRN modelling through the work of Samuilik and colleagues [73], who
used a single increasing sigmoid for all regulatory interactions, with signed weights encoding direction. While this
provides a unified formalism with attractive mathematical properties, it forces both activation and repression through
the same functional form. For repression in particular, this leads to critical points at biologically meaningless negative
concentrations and a systematic failure to approach unity under unrepressed conditions—pathologies that arise not from
biological necessity but from the modelling choice itself. The present paper takes a fundamentally different approach:
by deploying increasing logistic functions for activation and decreasing logistic functions for repression, each precisely
where it is biologically appropriate, we preserve the distinct sigmoidal dynamics of each regulatory mode. We show
that increasing and decreasing sigmoids can be combined in a product that naturally encodes AND combinatorial logic,
and we compare this formulation in detail with the Samuilik weighted-sum alternative.
The present work focuses on practical advances across three interconnected themes: model formulation, biological
realism at low expression, and numerical reliability for downstream control. The first theme establishes the logistic
framework on two canonical small-network motifs and on one large-scale network. For a two-gene oscillator with
negative feedback, Jacobian analysis establishes local asymptotic stability at the unique equilibrium for all positive
parameter values, and we prove that the two-dimensional ODE system cannot undergo a Hopf bifurcation; sustained
oscillations require the introduction of time delays. For bistable positive autoregulation, we derive the saddle-node
bifurcation conditions for both Hill and logistic formulations through explicit transcendental equations, characterise
the bistable parameter range as a function of steepness 𝜆, and compare the models’ behaviour under low initial
conditions using biophysically grounded E. coli parameters. The Traynard mammalian cell cycle Boolean network [78],
comprising 11 genes (Cdc20, Cdh1, CycA, CycB, CycD, CycE, E2F, p27, Rb, Skp2, and UbcH10), is translated
automatically into a continuous ODE system through the product-of-logistics De Morgan formalism introduced in
Section 2 and integrated numerically; all variables remain strictly bounded and non-negative throughout, confirming
that the framework scales to the complexity of established cell-cycle regulatory networks.
The second theme concerns biological realism at low expression. Using experimentally measured E. coli parameters
for mRNA synthesis and degradation, translation efficiency, and protein dilution, we demonstrate that the logistic
model’s intrinsic basal production rate drives noise-driven escape from the off-state in approximately 44 minutes
(numerical simulation; ≈58 minutes by conservative linear analytical estimate), consistent with galactose operon
observations. The Hill model, lacking any basal production, cannot sustain the protein accumulation required to cross
the unstable separatrix: mRNA decays exponentially from initial conditions, protein reaches only a transient peak well
below the separatrix threshold, and the trajectory remains permanently confined near zero.
The third theme concerns numerical reliability and downstream control. A dedicated experiment on an 80-gene
Boolean-derived ODE system with non-integer Hill exponent 𝑛= 3.509 provides direct empirical evidence of the
reliability gap. The Hill-based solver entered silent complex-valued arithmetic contamination from the first moment
any state variable overshot zero, produced smooth but corrupted trajectories that terminated near 𝑡≈63–65 rather
than the intended 𝑡= 200, and returned biologically meaningless extrapolated values at later observation times—all
without any visually detectable artefact in the trajectory plot. The logistic formulation, with identical parameters and
initial conditions, completed the full integration without a single warning, with all 80 state variables non-negative and
bounded throughout, and yielded physically consistent state extractions at all five requested observation times.
Building on this numerical foundation, we then analyse how the always-positive production rate of logistic models
eliminates the controllability gaps that arise in Hill-based models at zero concentration, and show that multiplicative
control, steepness modulation, sliding-mode control, and model-predictive control strategies are directly applicable at
expression levels where Hill-based formulations lose controllability or require substantial modification. The closed-
form logit inverse enables exact feedback linearisation, and the bounded, globally Lipschitz structure of the logistic
right-hand side provides well-conditioned Jacobians throughout the state space.
Taken together, these results establish logistic functions not as a minor variation on an established theme but as
a principled, computationally robust, and biologically faithful foundation for the next generation of GRN models,
immediately deployable through standard numerical libraries and compatible with automatic differentiation tools.

2.4. Extension to Multi-Gene Regulatory Networks
The logistic-based formulation extends naturally to arbitrarily large networks, preserving its analytical and
computational advantages even in high-dimensional settings. A distinguishing feature of our modelling philosophy
is the explicit differentiation between increasing and decreasing logistic functions: we deploy each form precisely
where it is biologically appropriate, thereby maintaining clear regulatory interpretability. This stands in contrast to
some alternative formulations that attempt to unify all regulatory interactions through a single increasing functional
form [73], potentially obscuring biological meaning.
Our framework accommodates logical rules governing how multiple regulators collectively orchestrate gene
expression, following the Boolean network formalism pioneered by Albert and Othmer [5] for the Drosophila segment
polarity network. These rules capture combinatorial effects that arise naturally in transcriptional regulation. We denote
by Φ∶→[0, 1] the map that translates a Boolean regulatory rule into its continuous logistic approximation.

2.6.1. Structural Distinctions Between the Two Formulations
The two formulations differ along four interrelated axes that we summarise here before pursuing the gate-by-gate
analysis. First, with respect to the encoding of regulatory direction, using a negative weight inside an increasing sigmoid
(the Samuilik convention) versus a positive weight inside a decreasing sigmoid (our convention) are not equivalent
representations of repression. A negative weight inside an increasing sigmoid places the sigmoid’s critical point at
𝑥𝑐= 𝜃∕𝑤< 0 (with 𝜃> 0 and 𝑤< 0), strictly outside the physically admissible domain 𝑥≥0, so the function never
undergoes its sigmoidal transition over any biologically realisable concentration and remains nearly flat and close to
zero throughout. The decreasing-sigmoid convention places the critical point at 𝑥𝑐= 𝜃∕𝑤> 0, a positive, biologically
interpretable inhibition midpoint (IC50) that can be measured directly from dose-response data. This is a structural
pathology of the Samuilik encoding, not a consequence of any particular parameter choice, and it persists for every
repressor in every network regardless of size.
Second, with respect to the regulatory architecture, the Samuilik model aggregates all regulatory inputs into a
single weighted sum ∑𝑤𝑖𝑗𝑥𝑗passed through one increasing logistic per gene, modelling additive or competitive
effects, whereas our product-of-logistics approach multiplies individual sigmoidal terms for each regulator. The latter
naturally captures multiplicative (AND) interactions, in which multiple conditions must be satisfied simultaneously, as
well as OR interactions via the De Morgan product formula, while keeping each regulator’s contribution analytically
separable.
Third, with respect to the threshold structure, the Samuilik model uses a single threshold 𝜃𝑖pooled across all
regulators of gene 𝑖, prescribed as 𝜃𝑖= ∑

to every interaction independently; this quantity is context-free, network-size-invariant, and directly identifiable from
individual dose-response measurements.
Fourth, with respect to biological interpretability, each factor in our product represents the probability-like
occupancy of an independent binding site, and the effective threshold 𝜃𝑖𝑗∕𝑤𝑖𝑗> 0 maps directly to a dissociation
constant 𝐾𝑑or half-maximal effective concentration 𝐸𝐶50, measurable from single-regulator dose-response experi-

regulatory influences manifestly consistent within a single mathematical framework.
3. Biological Realism Through Low-Expression Modelling
Real gene regulatory systems never fully shut down. Even under strong repression, promoters exhibit low-level
transcription, often referred to as “promoter leakiness” or basal activity, driven by factors such as nucleosome

systems from trapping in irreversible off-states during stochastic fluctuations. The logistic function naturally captures
this imperfect inhibition. Unlike Hill functions, which drop to zero at low input and can lock systems into unresponsive
states in bistable or feedback motifs, logistic models maintain a small but nonzero production rate, allowing noise-
driven escapes and rapid recovery. In resource-limited cellular environments, where maximal expression may be
unattainable even in the absence of repressors, the logistic’s saturation reflects capacity constraints rather than binding
cooperativity, thereby enhancing biological realism in large-scale GRNs.

The biological significance of bistability in positive autoregulation cannot be overstated. In cellular environments
characterised by stochasticity arising from low molecule counts, typically on the order of 1 to 10 transcription factor
molecules per cell, the system faces a critical challenge: under low initial conditions or in the presence of stochastic
fluctuations, the regulatory circuit can become irreversibly trapped in an “off” state [8]. This phenomenon has profound
implications for cellular decision-making, metabolic switching, and developmental processes. Without intrinsic
recovery mechanisms, such as basal (leaky) transcription, cells may fail to respond appropriately to environmental
cues, potentially leading to metabolic failure or developmental arrest.
The choice of mathematical model used to describe transcriptional regulation profoundly impacts our ability to
capture these dynamics. Hill functions, widely employed for their sigmoidal shape that mimics cooperative binding of
transcription factors to DNA, make a critical simplifying assumption: they posit zero basal expression in the absence
of an activator. This assumption, while mathematically convenient and appropriate for describing sharp, switch-like
responses, introduces both numerical instability and a fundamental failure to capture noise-driven escape from low-
expression states. In stark contrast, logistic functions possess an inherent non-zero basal production rate, which more
accurately reflects in vivo conditions where leaky transcription occurs due to incomplete repression, constitutive (albeit
weak) promoter activity, or stochastic binding events [57]. This distinction becomes particularly critical in high-
degradation or noisy cellular environments, where Hill-based models require the ad hoc addition of leakage terms
to match experimental observations, a limitation repeatedly documented in both synthetic gene circuits and natural
operons such as the gal system [8].

negligible degradation relative to cellular division (dilution-dominated decay).

4.1.1. Boolean Network Construction
The Boolean network consists of 𝑁= 80 variables 𝐱= (𝑥1, 𝑥2, … , 𝑥80), each governed by a propositional

variables such as 𝑥1, 𝑥3, and 𝑥4 are regulated by 15, 21, and 22 distinct conjunctive clauses respectively, each clause

BooleanMinimize in DNF mode. This preprocessing step eliminates redundant and duplicate conjunctive clauses,
such as absorbed terms of the form 𝐶∨(𝐶∧𝐷) →𝐶, or tautologies 𝐶∨¬𝐶→TRUE, and propagates constants arising
from the seven fixed variables. Without this step, duplicate clauses surviving into the ODE translation would introduce
artificial integer coefficients (2×, 3×, ...) in the production terms, inflating Φ𝑖beyond its biologically meaningful [0, 1]
bound.

Two further properties of the Hill function amplify the impact of the mechanisms above. First, the maximum slope

4.4. Hill Functions with Real Exponent: A Generically Unreliable Framework
The experiment reported above is not an isolated unlucky draw. The failures documented in Section 4.2 are
structural consequences of the mathematical definition of ℎ±, and they manifest inevitably whenever 𝑛∉ℕ. Fitted
Hill coefficients in the systems biology literature consistently report non-integer values (see, e.g., [6, 45, 15, 13]):
transcription-factor binding curves typically yield 𝑛∈[1.2, 3.8]; cooperative enzyme kinetics give 𝑛≈1.7–2.4; and
synthetic toggle switches and repressilators are fitted with 𝑛≈2.1–4.6. Our simulation draws 𝑛from U(1, 5), so the
probability of drawing an integer is exactly zero, and the fixed value 𝑛= 3.50918 is representative of the generic
situation encountered in practice.
The various failure modes are manifestations of a single underlying cause: the ODE system derived from Hill
functions with real-valued 𝑛is numerically unstable in a precise sense. A numerical integration method applied to

The positive-orthant boundary is precisely the region that low-expression states and transient dynamics explore.
As a consequence, standard convergence and stability theorems for Runge–Kutta and multistep methods do not apply;
the computed solution has no guaranteed accuracy bound, even in exact arithmetic; and in finite-precision arithmetic
the simulation will fail or produce incorrect results with near-certainty for any trajectory that passes through a
neighbourhood of zero, which, for a randomly initialised 80-dimensional system, is virtually inevitable. This pathology

The pathology compounds with network size and observation horizon. In a small two- or three-variable system,
solver failure is easy to detect visually; but in a high-dimensional network (𝑁= 80 here, with realistic GRN models

Practitioners sometimes attempt to circumvent these problems through ad hoc patches, but none of the standard
remedies resolves the underlying issue. Clamping 𝑥to [0, ∞) at each step introduces a discontinuity in the right-hand

determined by experimental data. Finally, tightening solver tolerances (reducing AccuracyGoal and PrecisionGoal)

5. Control Advantages of Logistic Functions in Gene Regulatory Networks
5.1. Always-Positive Production and Decoupled Parameters
The logistic function provides fundamental advantages over Hill functions for controlling biological networks,
stemming from its non-zero response at minimal expression levels and analytical tractability. The logistic function
𝑓+(𝑥, 𝜃, 𝜆) = 1∕(1 + 𝑒−𝜆(𝑥−𝜃)) maintains 𝑓+(0, 𝜃, 𝜆) = 1∕(1 + 𝑒𝜆𝜃) > 0, ensuring continuous regulatory control even
when gene expression drops to zero, whereas the Hill function ℎ+(𝑥, 𝜃, 𝑛) = 𝑥𝑛∕(𝑥𝑛+ 𝜃𝑛) vanishes identically at
𝑥= 0, creating controllability gaps that compromise feedback regulation. Biologically, genes exhibit persistent non-
zero basal (leaky) expression even without activators, stabilising low-expression states across multiple cell divisions
as observed in the GAL network in yeast and promoter leakage in auto-regulatory circuits [3, 44]. Without repressor
proteins, transcription proceeds at sustained high rates as seen in bacterial operons (e.g., the lac system) and phage

expression via the product 𝜆𝜃, whereas Hill functions rigidly fix ℎ−(0) = 1, offering no intrinsic parameter-based
modulation. Similarly, the activation function 𝑓+(0, 𝜃, 𝜆) = 1∕(1+𝑒𝜆𝜃) provides tunable control of the basal expression
rate via the same product 𝜆𝜃.
The parameters 𝜆(steepness) and 𝜃(threshold) map directly to tunable molecular properties: 𝜃represents the
dissociation constant for binding, adjustable through operator mutations or protein engineering [82, 34], while 𝜆
governs cooperativity, modifiable via multimeric repressors or auxiliary binding sites [74]. These parameters are
experimentally accessible through synthetic biology techniques such as promoter libraries, directed evolution, and
optogenetics [54, 67]. The full parameter independence inherent in the logistic formulation, where threshold position
𝜃and transition steepness 𝜆are decoupled, proves particularly advantageous for control design, enabling independent
tuning of the decision threshold and response sensitivity without compensatory parameter adjustments. This contrasts
sharply with Hill functions, where the maximum slope 𝑛∕(4𝜃) couples both parameters, requiring simultaneous
readjustment of cooperativity and threshold to maintain desired control characteristics.

6. Conclusion
This paper has argued, through systematic theoretical analysis and extensive numerical case studies, that logistic
functions constitute a mathematically principled, computationally robust, and biologically faithful alternative to Hill
functions for modelling gene regulatory networks [11, 20, 14, 16, 17]. The advantages are not incidental: they arise
from deep structural properties of the logistic form and manifest simultaneously across the mathematical, numerical,
and biological dimensions that matter most in practice.
The global 𝐶∞regularity of logistic functions removes the origin singularities that afflict Hill functions whenever
the cooperativity exponent is non-integer—a ubiquitous occurrence when fitting experimental dose-response data. The
self-referential derivative identity 𝑓′ = 𝜆𝑓(1−𝑓) simplifies Jacobian computations substantially: derivatives reduce to
products of function values, eliminating the fractional power evaluations that make Hill-function Jacobians expensive
and ill-conditioned near zero. The closed-form logit inverse 𝑓−1(𝑦) = 𝜃+ 𝜆−1 ln(𝑦∕(1 −𝑦)) enables exact feedback
linearisation and rapid threshold calculations that Hill functions cannot offer for general non-integer exponents. The
full decoupling of steepness 𝜆and threshold 𝜃—whose maximum slope 𝜆∕4 depends on 𝜆alone, in contrast to the Hill
maximum slope 𝑛∕(4𝜃) which entangles both design variables—allows independent tuning of decision threshold and
response sensitivity in circuit design and parameter estimation alike.
Three canonical network motifs were analysed. For the two-gene negative feedback oscillator, Jacobian analysis
and the Routh–Hurwitz criterion establish local asymptotic stability at the unique equilibrium for all biologically
meaningful parameter values. The trace of the Jacobian is −(𝛾1 + 𝛾2) < 0 and the determinant is strictly positive
for all logistic parameter choices, guaranteeing stability without further conditions. A key structural result is that this
two-dimensional ODE system cannot undergo a Hopf bifurcation: the trace cannot be made to vanish by any choice of
𝜆, 𝜅𝑖, or 𝜃𝑖, so sustained limit cycles require the introduction of time delays [12, 36].
For bistable positive autoregulation, saddle-node bifurcation thresholds were characterised through explicit
transcendental equations for both the Hill and logistic formulations. For the Hill model with 𝑛= 3 and 𝑐= 1, the
critical amplification is 𝛼crit ≈1.89; since the experimentally grounded E. coli parameters yield 𝛼≈600 ≫1.89, the
Hill model is well within the bistable regime and possesses an absorbing state at the origin from which no intrinsic
dynamics can escape. For the logistic model with 𝜆= 3 and 𝜃= 1, the bistable range is 1.821 < 𝛼< 2.823; at 𝛼≈600
the system is monostable at the high-expression state, and basal production alone drives escape. Table 4 documents
how this bistable window widens with increasing steepness 𝜆, with the lower threshold approaching unity and the
upper threshold diverging as 𝜆→∞, in direct analogy with higher cooperativity in Hill functions. Using biophysically
grounded E. coli parameters (Table 3), numerical simulation demonstrates that the logistic model escapes the off-state
in approximately 44 minutes through basal production alone, consistent with a conservative linear analytical estimate
of ≈58 minutes and with galactose operon observations. The Hill model produces mRNA synthesis four orders of
magnitude below the degradation rate at the initial condition; mRNA decays exponentially, protein reaches a transient

order 10−69 appearing in 𝑥4 well before the visible NDSolve::ndsz warning fired at 𝑡≈52.64—and produced
smooth, visually plausible trajectories that were solutions to a corrupted surrogate system rather than the true biological
model. Integration terminated near 𝑡≈63–65, leaving approximately 67–68% of the intended horizon covered only
by unconstrained polynomial extrapolation; the extrapolated values included biologically impossible concentrations. A
modeller inspecting only the trajectory plot would find no visual artefact to indicate that the simulation had failed. Under
identical parameters and initial conditions, the logistic formulation completed the full integration over 𝑡∈[0, 200]
without a single warning, with all 80 state variables strictly non-negative and bounded throughout, and yielded
physically consistent state extractions at all five requested observation times.
Our product-of-logistics formulation for multi-input regulation offers biological interpretability that weighted-sum
alternatives cannot match. Each threshold 𝜃𝑖𝑗is directly interpretable as a dissociation constant determinable from
independent experimental measurements, enabling decomposed validation that is structurally impossible in shared-
threshold formulations. The product structure naturally encodes AND logic, while the alternative single-sigmoid
approach of Samuilik et al. [73] produces critical points at biologically meaningless negative concentrations and fails to
approach unity under unrepressed conditions. Section 2.5 establishes formally that incorporating explicit real-valued
interaction weights into the product-of-logistics formulation is equivalent to the fixed-weight formulation after the
parameter rescaling 𝜆′
𝑖𝑗= 𝜆𝑖𝑗𝑤𝑖𝑗and 𝜃′
𝑖𝑗= 𝜃𝑖𝑗∕𝑤𝑖𝑗; the two parameterisations produce identical dynamics and differ
only in how steepness and threshold information is distributed across parameters.
The repression side of the formulation is equally well-served. The decreasing logistic function naturally approaches,
but need not exactly reach, unity in the absence of repressor—capturing biological realities such as polymerase
saturation, resource competition, and stochastic promoter switching that the Hill function’s fixed maximum cannot
represent. For applications requiring exact normalisation, the scaled variant 𝑓−
scaled(𝑥, 𝜃, 𝜆) = (1 + 𝑒−𝜆𝜃) ⋅𝑓−(𝑥, 𝜃, 𝜆)
restores unit value at zero repressor concentration; under typical parameter regimes (𝜆𝜃≥4) the scaling factor is
negligible, so the choice between scaled and unscaled forms is guided by biological context rather than mathematical
necessity.
The always-positive production rate of logistic models eliminates the controllability gaps that Hill functions
create at zero concentration, where zero production precludes recovery through intrinsic dynamics alone. We showed
theoretically that multiplicative control, steepness modulation, sliding mode control, and model predictive control
strategies are all directly applicable at expression levels where Hill-based formulations lose controllability or require
substantial modification. The full decoupling of threshold 𝜃and steepness 𝜆allows independent tuning of the decision
threshold and response sensitivity—an advantage that Hill functions, where both properties are entangled in the
expression 𝑛∕(4𝜃), cannot provide. These control strategies are theoretically grounded; experimental validation in
optogenetic platforms is a natural direction for future work.
