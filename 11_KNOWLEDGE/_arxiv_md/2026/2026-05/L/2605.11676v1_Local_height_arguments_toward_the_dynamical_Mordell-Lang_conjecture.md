Introduction
In this article, the base field is C unless otherwise stated. As a matter of convention, every variety
is assumed to be integral, but the closed subvarieties can be reducible. Let X be a variety and let
f be an endomorphism of X. For a point x ∈X(K), the orbit Of(x) is the set {f n(x)| n ∈N}.
We denote N = Z+ ∪{0}. An arithmetic progression is a set of the form {mk + l| k ∈N} for some
m, l ∈N.
The dynamical Mordell–Lang conjecture is one of the core problems in the field of arithmetic
dynamics. It was proposed by Ghioca and Tucker in [GT09] and can be stated as follows.

Dynamical Mordell–Lang Conjecture (DML Conjecture). Let f be an endomorphism of a
quasi-projective variety X. Let V be a closed subvariety of X and let x ∈X(C) be a point. Then
the return set {n ∈N| f n(x) ∈V (C)} is a finite union of arithmetic progressions.
There is an extensive literature on various cases of the DML conjecture. Two significant cases
are as follows.
(i) If f is an ´etale endomorphism of X, then the DML conjecture holds. See [Bel06] and [BGT10,
Theorem 1.3].
(ii) If X = A2, then the DML conjecture holds. See [Xie17] and [Xie, Theorem 3.2].
One can consult [BGT16, Xie] and the references therein for further known results.
In this paper, we mainly study the DML conjecture for curves. So we recall the following notion
introduced in [Xie, Definition 1.3].
Definition 1.1. For a quasi-projective variety X and an endomorphism f of X, we say (X, f)
satisfies the DML(1) property, if for any closed subcurve C ⊆X and any point x ∈X(C), the
return set {n ∈N| f n(x) ∈C(C)} is a finite union of arithmetic progressions.
Here “1” stands for the dimension of the closed subvariety.

We will investigate regular endomorphisms of AN in this paper. We firstly introduce some notions

Setting 1.2. A regular endomorphism of AN is a dominant endomorphism f that can be extended
to an endomorphism of PN. Here, we fix the standard coordinate system [x0, . . . , xN] on PN and

1.2
Discussions
We make some discussions about the conditions of our main results, and describe the proof strategy.
The idea of using heights to deal with the DML Conjecture dates back to almost 20 years ago.
For example, see [BGKT10] and [GTZ12, Theorem 8.1]. Philosophically, this work is a successor
of [XYb], in which the authors use a height argument to investigate the dynamical Mordell–Lang
conjecture.
As the authors mentioned in the introduction of [XYb], the key point of such an
argument is to find two different speeds of growth. We recall a main result in [XYb] to further
illustrate this issue.
Theorem 1.9. ([XYb, Theorem 1.1]) Let X be a projective variety over an algebraically closed
field of arbitrary characteristic and let f be a surjective endomorphism of X. Suppose that any
cohomological Lyapunov multiplier of any iterate of f is not a positive integer. Let C ⊆X be a
closed subcurve and let x ∈X(K) be a point. If Of(x) = X, then Of(x) ∩C(K) is a finite set.
We will not recall the definition of cohomological Lyapunov multipliers here (see [XYb]). In-
stead, we remark that philosophically the condition of Theorem 1.9 means that the dynamical
system is “at the opposite of polarized systems”. Hence the system is not “isotropic”, and we are
managed to find two speeds of height growth in there. However, the dynamical systems studied
in this paper are polarized. Thus, the “height argument” fails and we need to use a “local height

argument” instead. Also, this explains the philosophy behind Condition 1.5 — “break down the
isotropy”. To this end, we naturally consider a regular endomorphism f which has a visible degree

The article [XYZ] is a baby forerunner of this paper. The main result that the authors proved in
[XYZ] can be summarized in the following proposition. By combining with some previous results,
the authors solved the dynamical Mordell–Lang conjecture for split self-maps of affine curve times
projective curve in [XYZ]. The base field of [XYZ] is Q.
Proposition 1.10. Let f be an endomorphism of A1 and g be an endomorphism of P1. Suppose
deg(f) = deg(g) ≥2 and g has no exceptional point. Let C ⊆A1 × P1 be an irreducible closed
subcurve which is not a fiber of A1 or P1. Then for every x0 ∈A1(Q) and y0 ∈P1(Q), the set
C ∩Of×g(x0, y0) is finite.
The proof of Proposition 1.10 is by using a local height argument, which we briefly recall as
follows. Let C be the closure of C in P1 × P1 and let L∞be the infinity line in P1 × P1. Then
for an appropriate place, the points in Of×g(x0, y0) tend to L∞with the maximal speed. So a
subsequence of the points in C ∩Of×g(x0, y0) shall tend to a point in P ∈C ∩L∞, for which
the P1-component of the points must also tend to P on L∞with the maximal speed. However,
Silverman’s result [Sil93, Theorem E] guarantees that this cannot happen.
One can see that the key point in the procedure above is finding two different speeds of local
height growth at a certain place. Then Silverman’s result controls the speed of the slower one. By
looking into the proof of [Sil93, Theorem E], one finds that Roth’s theorem lies at the core. As
a result, Roth’s theorem will also play a central role in this article. Matsuzawa has used Roth’s
theorem to study problems in high-dimensional arithmetic dynamics [Mat23, Mat25]. Inspired by
his work, we try to use the local height argument to study the dynamical Mordell–Lang conjecture.

In this section, we introduce a basic notion of local heights (i.e. Weil functions) on PN. Over
number fields, the theory of local heights is standard, which measures the degree of closeness of a
point with a fixed closed subscheme in a projective variety. For example, see [Lan83, Chapter 10]
and [Sil87]. In this paper, for our purpose, we need this notion for rational points in PN as well as
the infinity hyperplane in PN over arithmetic function fields. In the setting of arithmetic function
fields, the theory of local heights for Cartier divisors in projective varieties was developed in [Voj21,
Subsection 3D]. But it seems that the theory for closed subschemes of higher codimension has not
been developed yet. We will also not try to develop a general theory in this article. Instead, we
will only give some definitions and prove some inequalities that will be used in the sequel. We
restrict ourselves in PN and work with its natural coordinates in order to maintain an elementary
and concrete flavor of our article.

Further discussions: the positive characteristic case
It is also interesting to study the dynamical Mordell–Lang conjecture over fields of positive char-
acteristic. In this setting, we still concern about the return set of an orbit into a closed subvariety,
but the conclusion needs to be modified. See, for example, [BGT16, Example 3.4.5.1]. Indeed, the
examples in [XYb, Section 5] show that the return set can be very complicated, instead of a finite
union of arithmetic progressions. Although such complicated return sets often appear for endo-
morphisms of zero entropy, there also exist polarized counterexamples in positive characteristic.
See [XYb, Example 5.4]. The interested reader may have a glance at [CGSZ21, XY25, XYb, XYa]
and the references therein for recent progress on the dynamical Mordell–Lang problem in positive
characteristic.
As we have mentioned in Introduction, the authors studied the dynamical Mordell–Lang con-
jecture in [XYb] by using a height argument.
The results in loc.
cit.
are characteristic-free.
Therefore, we would like to know about how much we can extend the results in this work to the
setting of positive characteristic. Nevertheless, there is neither Siegel’s theorem nor Roth’s theorem
in positive characteristic. Hence our approach should fail.
Our main result Theorem 1.7 can be divided into two parts. Firstly, we prove the DML(1)
property under Condition 1.5; and secondly, we characterize the periodic curves in this setting —
all of them are vertical lines. The following example shows that we cannot expect the second part
in positive characteristic. Indeed, it shows that in the setting of Theorem 1.3, there are invariant
curves which are not straight lines while the degree gap is arbitrarily large.
Example 5.1. Let K be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p > 0. Let d be a positive
integer prime to p and let q > d be a power of p. Consider the endomorphism
f(x, y) = (xq + (xd −yd −1)yq−d, yq)
of A2. It is a regular endomorphism with degree gap d.
Let C be the irreducible affine curve {xd = yd + 1}. Then C is an f-invariant curve which has
degree d. We can see that C ∩H∞⊆{xy ̸= 0}. Hence f∞is unramified at the points in C ∩H∞.
This shows that neither of the two parts of Theorem 1.3 is valid in positive characteristic.
This example illustrates that not only Roth’s theorem, but also Siegel’s theorem play an im-
portant role in our approach.
However, the DML part of Theorem 1.7 may remain valid in positive characteristic. As [XYb,
Example 5.4] suggests, for regular endomorphisms in positive characteristic, it seems that f∞should
have some purely inseparable components in order to let the return sets be more complicated than
finite unions of arithmetic progressions. But then the ramification status of f∞is formidable.
These thoughts propel us to ask the following question.
