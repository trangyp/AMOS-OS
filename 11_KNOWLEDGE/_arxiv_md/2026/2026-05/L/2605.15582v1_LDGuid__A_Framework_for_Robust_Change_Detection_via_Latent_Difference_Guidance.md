Abstract—Modern deep learning models for change detection
(CD) often struggle to explicitly represent task-relevant semantic
differences. This paper proposes the Latent Difference Guidance
(LDGuid) framework that explicitly learns and injects semantic
differences into CD models. LDGuid deploys adversarial autoen-
coding to implement a difference embedding (DE) module. The
DE module is pretrained via the information bottleneck method,
restricting it to learn only task-relevant differences between pre-
and post-event samples. The learned latent difference is then
used as an explicit guidance signal in the CD model. We validate
LDGuid by integrating it into U-Net, BIT, and AERNet baselines
for CD and evaluating it on LEVIR-CD, WHU-CD, SVCD,
and CaBuAr datasets. Experimental results show that LDGuid
enhances segmentation performance across all benchmarks, with
particularly remarkable gains in challenging settings affected by
spectral noise. The results further highlight the ability of LDGuid
in incorporating domain knowledge, such as task-specific spectral
indices. Our findings suggest that semantic difference learning
can drastically enhance the robustness of CD in remote sensing.
Index Terms—Change detection, adversarial networks, autoen-
coding, latent representation, image segmentation.
I. INTRODUCTION
Change detection (CD) in bitemporal satellite imagery is
a fundamental task in remote sensing. It plays a critical role
in various applications such as urban expansion monitoring
[1], disaster assessment [2], and environmental surveillance
[3]. Due to their expressive power, deep learning models have
become dominant components for CD [4]. These models learn
from the data a mapping from a pair of co-registered images,
i.e., pre- and post-event images, to a binary change map.
Among various proposals, sequence-to-sequence architectures
with skip connections, e.g. U-Net [5], and Transformer-based
models such as the bitemporal image transformer (BIT) [6]
can achieve state-of-the-art performance due to their ability to
efficiently encode long-range spatiotemporal correlations.
Although state-of-the-art CD models can efficiently learn
the binary change map, a fundamental task remains chal-
lenging for them: determining effective semantic differences
with minimal background noise. This task arises in several
applications where soft information on scene changes is re-
quired rather than a change map, e.g., wildfire segmentation
[7]. Naive approaches, such as feature concatenation [8] or
internal difference enhancement [9], often fail to fully capture
the change of interest from complex static backgrounds. This
issue is starkly illustrated in a recent wildfire segmentation

result on the California Burned Area (CaBuAr) dataset [7],
where training a U-Net over both pre- and post-fire images
surprisingly degrades performance compared to a model that
is trained on post-fire images alone. This result indicates that
while the model is capable of learning binary change maps, it
struggles to efficiently represent semantic differences.
This work proposes a novel framework, named Latent
Difference Guidance (LDGuid), to explicitly extract semantic
differences from co-registered inputs and inject them into
segmentation models. Instead of relying on the segmentation
model to implicitly learn the changes, LDGuid employs a
pre-trained module to learn a latent representation of semantic
differences, which can be efficiently incorporated into the CD
model. Experiments across various datasets demonstrate the
superiority of LDGuid over state-of-the-art CD approaches.
A. Contributions
In this paper, we propose LDGuid as a generic framework
to enhance CD models by extracting semantic differences. Our
main contributions are as follows: (i) We design a difference
embedding (DE) module, which uses an autoencoder with an
adversarial decoder to learn a latent representation of differ-
ence with minimal background noise. (ii) Invoking the infor-
mation bottleneck method, we develop a pre-training loop that
trains the DE module. The pre-trained DE is then utilized to
guide the main CD model. (iii) We demonstrate the versatility
of LDGuid by deploying it into three benchmark architectures
for CD, namely U-Net, BIT, and AERNet. (iv) We conduct
comprehensive experiments on several urban building datasets,
i.e., LEVIR-CD [10], WHU-CD [11], and SVCD [12], as
well as the recent CaBuAr dataset for wildfire segmentation
[7]. Our results depict that LDGuid consistently outperforms
baseline approaches, particularly in complex scenarios with
high spectral noise such as burn scar detection.
B. Related Work
Deep CD Models: Due to natural spatial correlation in
data, earlier deep learning architectures for CD rely on CNN-
based sequence-to-sequence models such as U-Net [13, 14].
These models often capture local correlations. To capture
global dependencies, Transformers are widely used in the
CD literature. Examples of Transformer-based CD models are
BIT [6] and SwinSUNet [15]. Recent studies focus on hybrid
architectures, which combine local and global dependencies
in the data. For instance, AERNet [16] combines CNNs with

models (SSMs), e.g., ChangeMamba [18], which model spatio-

Semantic Difference Learning: In various applications
of CD, semantic differences, i.e., soft information on scene
changes, are to be learned explicitly. Traditional approaches
employ difference enhancement modules (DEMs), e.g., WNet
[19] and TransUNetCD [20]. These modules extract differ-
ence features by combining the latent representations of pre-
and post-event samples. The semantic differences learned by
DEMs often contain considerable background noise. Recent
studies such as [21] and [22] propose using generative mod-
els, e.g., denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs)
and generative adversarial networks (GANs), to extract more
robust features. Although these approaches can improve the
performance of the end-to-end model, they fail to reduce the
background noise in semantic differences. This follows from
the fact that DEMs do not incorporate the target difference
in their training objectives. Unlike these approaches, LDGuid
explicitly learns a latent representation of semantic differences
by directly incorporating it in the pre-training objective.
II. PROBLEM SETUP
We consider CD in a supervised setting. Let (X, ˜X) ∈X ×
X be a pair of co-registered samples, where X and ˜X are the
pre-event and post-event samples, respectively. The goal is to
design a model Fθ : X × X 7→M, which predicts a mask
M ∈M that depends on semantic changes of the samples,
e.g., detecting burned areas from pre-fire and post-fire images.
The model Fθ is trained on a dataset D = {(Xi, ˜Xi, Mi)}n
i=1.
Remark 1: Note that M does not necessarily include all
changes. In wildfire segmentation, for instance, some differ-
ences between pre- and post-fire images are not fire-related
and hence excluded from the mask.
The main objective is to develop a framework that captures
the related differences between the samples X and ˜X with
minimal background noise, i.e., minimal information on the
differences between X and ˜X that are not related to M.
III. METHODOLOGY AND DESIGN
LDGuid is illustrated in Fig. 1. The framework consists of
two components: (i) a difference embedding (DE) module that
deploys adversarial autoencoding to learn a latent representa-
tion Z for semantic differences, and (ii) a feature injection
mechanism that incorporates Z into the segmentation model.
A. Difference Embedding Module
The DE module is shown in the dashed box in Fig. 1. DE de-

ing. Namely, the module consists of
1) an autoencoder (AE) with encoder Cϕ and decoder Dφ
that computes a latent representation Z. The decoder Dφ
estimates the post-event sample conditioned on the pre-
event sample, i.e., it takes Z and X as input.

Intuition on DE Design: In the absence of the adversarial
decoder Aψ, the AE computes a latent representation Z, which
together with X recovers ˆX ≈˜X. Intuitively, Z contains
semantic differences between X and ˜X. Nevertheless, it is
not guaranteed that Z does not contain background noise, i.e.,
irrelevant information. The role of adversarial decoder is to
minimize background noise by decoding ˘X, which is highly
different from ˜X. This promotes Z to contain only relevant
semantic differences required for recovery of ˜X from X and
suppress other irrelevant information in X and ˜X.
B. Training DE Module
Following the intuition on DE, we develop a mechanism
based on the information bottleneck method [23] to train
this module. To this end, let us formally define the features
computed in this module: (1) the encoder Cϕ with learnable
parameter ϕ computes the latent variable as Z = Cϕ(X, ˜X),
(2) the decoder Dφ with learnable parameter φ computes an
estimate ˆX of the post-event sample as ˆX = Dφ(X, Z), and
(3) the adversarial decoder Aψ with learnable parameters ψ
computes the adversarial estimate ˘X from the latent represen-
tation Z as ˘X = Aψ(Z). As mentioned, DE aims to estimate
the post-event sample ˜X at the output of the decoder, i.e.,
ˆX ≈˜X, while the adversarial estimate has minimal correlation
with ˜X. This can be obtained via the following loss.

recovery error with the fidelity of the adversarial estimate. Let

samples, e.g., mean squared error (MSE). We then define the

C. Latent Difference Injection
Once the latent difference Z is computed, it serves as an
explicit guidance signal for the downstream CD task. As
shown in Fig. 1, we inject Z into the main segmentation
model Gθ by treating it as input features. Depending on the
architecture, Z is resized and concatenated with the post-event
image features or the backbone’s feature maps. We discuss this
procedure for U-Net, BIT, and AERNet in Section IV.

The fine-tuning enables better suppression of background

which the DE module is updated less frequently than the
main model. Since the pre-trained DE module solely extracts
features during CD, LDGuid’s added computational overhead
(both parameters and inference time) is negligible.
IV. EXPERIMENTS
We next validate LDGuid by adapting it to different CD
models and evaluating it on several CD datasets.
A. Experimental Setup
Datasets: We evaluate LDGuid on four remote sensing
datasets. The first three are LEVIR-CD [10], WHU-CD [11],
and SVCD [12], which capture structural and scene-level
changes in urban environments. For wildfire application, we
use CaBuAr [7], which consists of multi-spectral Sentinel-2
imagery of burn scars. Furthermore, consistent gains across
these datasets (e.g., LEVIR-CD, CaBuAr) prove LDGuid’s
efficiency and scalability for large-region applications.
Baselines and Metrics: We compare LDGuid against
three baselines: (i) U-Net [5], (ii) the Transformer-based
architecture BIT [6], and (iii) the hybrid architecture AERNet
[16]. Unless otherwise stated, all models are trained for 200
epochs on an NVIDIA H100 GPU using Adam optimizer with
learning rate 1 × 10−4 and cross-entropy loss. Performance is
evaluated using F1-score and Intersection over Union (IoU).
DE and Feature Injection: LDGuid is adapted to all three
baselines. As the DE module, we pre-train an adversarial AE
considering a dual-branch encoder with two-layer CNNs to
separately extract change and context latent features. These
features are fused and fed into a three-layer transposed con-
volutional decoder, which computes both reconstruction and
adversarial output. The latent difference Z computed by the
pre-trained DE is then injected into each baseline architecture
as follows: (i) for U-Net, Z is concatenated with the input
post-event image. (ii) for BIT and AERNet, Z is aligned and
injected into the bottleneck, i.e., the features computed before
the Transformer or GCFAM module. An example of LDGuid
adaptation to BIT is shown in Fig. 2.
B. Results and Discussion
Table I summarizes the test results for LDGuid and base-
lines. The results are averaged over multiple seeds, with
statistical significance assessed at p < 0.05. As observed, our
framework yields consistent performance improvements across
all datasets. Remarkably, on WHU-CD, LDGuid boosts the
U-Net’s IoU by 23.36% over the baseline. It further improves
the IoU of the state-of-the-art BIT architecture by 9%, reach-
ing 97.40%. The results confirm that the explicitly-learned
semantic differences by DE compensate for the lack of global
context in baseline architectures. Among the three baselines,
AERNet depicts minimal gains. This is due to its internal edge
refinement, which provides implicit information on semantic
differences. Nevertheless, LDGuid still enhances performance
in this case, which indicates that our latent difference guidance
still provides complementary semantic information that cannot
be fully captured from raw inputs alone.
Results on CaBuAr further depict the role of explicit se-
mantic difference learning in more complex CD tasks, e.g.,
applications with multi-spectral data samples. As shown in
Table I, LDGuid outperforms all baselines with IoU 86.63%
compared to the state-of-the-art 84.04% achieved by BIT.
Impact of Domain Knowledge: Our experiments with
CaBuAr revealed that feeding raw 12-band Sentinel-2 data to
DE can degrade performance (IoU 84.58%). This behavior can
be explained by the fact that the high-dimensional data in this
dataset contain significant noise, e.g., clouds and vegetation
types. This can cause the DE module to quickly overfit. To
overcome this issue, we pretrained DE with normalized burn
ratios (NBRs), which is a specific index for combustion [24].
This forces the latent representation to focus on the burn
signal. This demonstrates that for complex data, LDGuid can
be efficiently deployed when the DE module is designed and
pretrained with domain-specific knowledge.

observe this trade-off, we swept β across a wide range from

V. CONCLUSION
We proposed LDGuid, a novel CD framework that explic-
itly learns semantic differences via a DE module. Invoking the
information bottleneck method, we designed an efficient pre-
training loop. We validated LDGuid across diverse datasets,
including LEVIR-CD, WHU-CD, SVCD, and CaBuAr. The
results demonstrate that LDGuid consistently outperforms the
benchmark CD approaches, revealing the ability of LDGuid
to capture domain-specific knowledge. Extending LDGuid to
multi-modal CD tasks, e.g., Optical-SAR, is a natural direction
for future work and is currently ongoing.
