Longitudinal beam dynamics with active cavity systems
Chao Li,∗Yong-Chul Chae,† Sven Pfeiffer, and Chris Christou
Deutsches Elektronen Synchrotron, Notkestrasse 85, 22607 Hamburg, Germany
(Dated: May 28, 2026)
In storage-ring-based light sources, harmonic cavities are commonly employed to
lengthen the bunch, thereby mitigating collective effects and increasing beam life-
time. While this dual-RF configuration provides important benefits, it also intro-
duces additional challenges. In particular, the impedance of the fundamental cavity
modes can drive the beam into a longitudinal coupled-bunch unstable regime. To
mitigate this effect, low-level RF (LLRF) feedback is introduced to reduce the ef-
fective impedance experienced by the beam.
This work investigates longitudinal
beam dynamics in the PETRA-IV dual-RF system with normal-conducting cavities,
explicitly accounting for the LLRF feedback loop. Both analytical modeling and nu-
merical simulations are used to characterize the onset and growth of coupled-bunch
instabilities. The results show that, with appropriately chosen LLRF parameters,
the destabilizing effect of the cavity fundamental mode can be effectively suppressed,
enabling stable operation of the storage ring at the design beam current. This work
highlights the critical role of RF feedback systems in ensuring robust longitudinal
stability, thereby supporting the realization of PETRA-IV design goals and con-
tributing to the development of next-generation synchrotron light sources, where
high brilliance and operational reliability are essential.

I.
INTRODUCTION
In high-intensity circular accelerators, various longitudinal beam instabilities
can be caused by interactions among circulating beams and accelerating cavities
impedance modes. If the High-Order-Modes (HOMs) are well-damped in the cavity
design, then the fundamental mode will be the main concern. The cavity funda-
mental mode, besides interacting with beam, also connects two other terms at least.
One term is the generator current, which is applied to excite the generator field to
act on the beam. The other term is the Low-Level-RF (LLRF) feedback control
loop, which regulates the electron field that the beam can sample. To have a reliable
beam performance assessment, one must study these coupled dynamics consistently.
In the synchrotron light source, many machines are updated to the fourth gen-
eration, which features 10−11 meter level natural emittance, 10−3 meter level bunch
length, 10−1 level Ampere beam current. One of the biggest concerns is to maintain
a reasonable Touschek lifetime. To ensure the Touschek lifetime with different beam
conditions, extra active/passive, normal-conducting/superconducting harmonic RF
cavities can be introduced to provide a longer natural bunch length and a lower elec-
tron density to reduce the Toushck effect. The harmonic cavities also introduce a
significant synchrotron tune spread to stabilize the collective beam motion through
the Landau damping mechanism. However, the fundamental modes of the harmonic
cavities are significant impedance sources as well, which conversely might increase
the chance to excite the collective instabilities.

based 4th generation light source capable of delivering a beam with emittance as low
as 20 pm. Tab. I gives the nominal design parameters of the storage rings. There
are two operation schemes proposed in PETRA-IV storage ring: 1) the timing mode;
2) the brightness mode. In the timing mode scheme, there are 80 bunches evenly

IV.
LOW-LEVEL RF FEEDBACK
Low-level RF feedback is indispensable and reacts in a shorter time, compared
to the synchrotron period 1/fs, for maintaining precise amplitude and phase in the
accelerating cavities so that proper longitudinal bucket area is preserved. The proper
LLRF feedback can compensate imperfections such as detuning from microphonics,


## Page 21


21
Lorentz-force effects, and beam-induced voltage, among others, to ensure a stable
machine operation. The LLRF feedback can mitigate the longitudinal coupled bunch
instabilities as well by significantly reducing the effective impedance (closed-loop)
beam sampled. According to the signal delay time in the feedback loop, the LLRF
feedback is grouped into short-delay, or direct RF feedback (DRF-FB), and long-
delay feedback.
In general, the DRF-FB reduces the impedance in a broadband
sense, and the long-delay feedback reduces impedance at the revolution frequency
harmonics. In PETRA-IV, both the DRF-FB and long delay feedback are included
in the nominal design for both main and harmonic cavities. The DRF-FB is with
PID controller type and the delay time of the long-delay feedback is set as one turn.
In this section, we introduce the LLRF-RF feedback in general and the description
in the Laplace s-domain is applied for convenience, which can be converted to the
frequency domain by setting s = iω.

SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
The coupled beam–cavity–feedback system exhibits complex dynamics, and care-
ful attention must be paid to controlling beam instabilities. In this paper, using the
nominal double-RF system in PETRA-IV as an example, we discuss the coupled lon-
gitudinal dynamics in general. The nominal PETRA-IV design includes both main
and harmonic cavities, which are normal-conducting and actively powered. Due to
the large shunt impedance and low quality factor, the harmonic cavity drives unsta-

This work further emphasizes the essential role of low-level RF (LLRF) systems in
future synchrotron light sources where active RF system are planned. As beam sta-
