LOCAL TOPOLOGICAL ORDER, HAAG DUALITY, AND REFLECTION
POSITIVITY
PIETER NAAIJKENS1, DAVID PENNEYS2, AND DANIEL WALLICK2
Abstract. In our previous article [arXiv:2307.12552], we introduced local topological order
(LTO) axioms for abstract quantum spin systems which allow one to access topological order via a
boundary algebra construction. Using the LTO axioms, we produced a canonical pure state on the
quasi-local algebra, which gives a net of von Neumann algebras associated to a poset of cones in Rn.
In this article, motivated by [arXiv:2509.23734], we introduce an axiom for LTOs which ensures
Haag duality for cone-like regions using Tomita-Takesaki theory. We prove this axiom is satisfied for
all known topologically ordered commuting projector models. We thus get an independent proof of
Haag duality for the Levin-Wen string net models originally proved in [arXiv:2509.23734]. We also
give a reflection positivity axiom for LTOs, connecting to the recent article [arXiv:2510.20662].
We again prove this axiom is satisfied for all known topologically ordered commuting projector
models about some Z/2-reflection symmetry.

lattices [Naa11, Naa13, FN15, Oga22, BKM24, BV25, BHNV26, BK25, BK26]. The approach used

one uses a technical condition called Haag duality, or a generalization thereof [Oga22], which states
that the von Neumann algebras for each cone and its complement are each other’s commutants.
Haag duality also has a quantum information theoretic interpretation [vLSW26]. In [OPGRdA25],
it was shown that a large class of models, specifically string-net models [LW05], satisfy Haag duality,
building on previous work showing Haag duality for the Toric Code [Naa12] and abelian Quantum
Double Model [FN15].
In this article, we provide a sufficient condition for Haag duality that is easy to check for all known
topologically ordered commuting projector models. To do so, we employ the local topological order
(LTO) axioms of [JNPW25], which characterize topological order locally using operator algebraic
conditions. The setup of the LTO axioms is a net of local algebras R 7→A(R) equipped with a net of
projections pR ∈A(R) satisfying pS ≤pR whenever R ⊆S. In 2D, we obtain a 1D net of boundary
algebras from the LTO axioms after cutting the system using a hyperplane, which can be used
to implement a topological holographical principle in the sense of [CW23, IW23]. For the known
commuting projector models, the net of boundary algebras is identical to the net of interaction
algebras [Zan01], and the DHR bimodules recover the bulk topological order [Jon24, JNPW25].
We give a Haag duality axiom for LTOs inspired by [OPGRdA25], which relates the bound-
ary algebras on each side of the cut, which is again satisfied by all known topologically ordered
commuting projector models. The axiom, in the setting of a quantum spin system, can be loosely
summarized as follows. (We refer the reader to (LTO-HD) below for the precise statement.)

The faithfulness assumption in Theorem A above allows us to show that both boundary algebras
B(Λ±) are in standard form on pΛ+pΛ−L2(A, ψ), so we may employ Tomita–Takesaki theory to get
the desired result. Again, this faithfulness has been verified for the known topologically ordered

assumption that pΛ± have central support 1 in A(Λ±) is automatic for quantum spin systems by
purity of ψ, which ensures A(Λ±) are factors. However, our treatment also applies to abstract spin
systems satisfying our assumptions, where the cone algebras are not necessarily factors [JNP25].
Additionally, we show that LTOs satisfy a mutual information condition that, together with
Haag duality, is used in [BGNW25] to show that the approximate split property is satisfied for the
cone von Neumann algebras. We also remark what can be said when the cone algebras are not
factors, in which case the proof in [BGNW25] does not work without some additional assumptions.

2. Background
In this section, we recall the basic objects of study in this note, and we prove some new results in
§2.5 below. Our basic setup is that of a net of algebras: a unital quasilocal C∗-algebra A together
with an assignment of unital C∗-subalgebras A(R) of A to a class of subregions R in a lattice L.
In [JNPW25], we worked with rectangular subsets in a Zn lattice, which we extend to ‘disk-like’
regions in §2.1 below. These C∗-subalgebras are required to satisfy the following axioms:
(N1) (vacuum) A(∅) = C · 1A,
(N2) (isotony) R ⊂S implies A(R) ⊂A(S),
(N3) (locality) R ∩S = ∅implies [A(R), A(S)] = 0 inside A, and
(N4) (nondegeneracy) S
R⊂L A(R) is dense in L.
A typical example is a quantum spin system on a lattice, where for each site x of the lattice we
have A({x}) ∼= Md(C), and for R ⊂L finite, A(R) is defined by taking the tensor product of the
algebras for each site in R. We will however not assume our local algebras are tensor products of
single-site algebras unless explicitly stated. Such a system is sometimes called an abstract quantum
spin system and we reserve the terminology ‘quantum spin system’ for the case where the local
algebras are tensor products of full matrix algebras as above.

2.1. Local topological order axioms. We recall the local topological order axioms introduced
in [JNPW25]. The axioms are a generalization of the LTQO axioms introduced in [BHM10, BH11]
that allow us to study what happens at the boundary of a region (rather than the bulk considered
in the LTQO axioms).
A net of projections for the net of algebras A is an assignment of an
orthogonal projection pR ∈A(R) for every region R, such that R ⊂S implies pS ≤pR in A(S). In
applications the projections pS are typically the local ground state projections of a frustration-free
Hamiltonian [FNW92, BHM10, NSY24].
Notation 2.1. For two rectangles R, S and s > 0, we say R is completely surrounded by S, denoted
R ≪s S, if for every point ℓ∈R, the s-ball in the ℓ∞metric is contained in S. Observe that R ≪s S
if and only if both R ⊂S and the distance from ∂R to ∂S is at least s, where ∂denotes taking the
boundary.
We say R is (weakly) surrounded by S, denoted by R ⋐s S, if R ⊆S, I := ∂R ∩∂S consists
of a single non-empty face of R, and every point of S \ R is contained in some s-cube which is
completely contained in S \ R. This means for every other face F of R except I, the distance from
F to ∂S in the direction of a normal vector pointing away from S is at least s.

Definition 2.2. A local topological order (LTO) is a pair (A, p) where A is a net of algebras
equipped with a net of projections that satisfies the following axioms, for sufficiently large regions
R, S,2 where s > 0 is some fixed global constant.
(LTO1) Whenever R ≪s S, pSA(R)pS = C · pS.
(LTO2) Whenever R ⋐s S, pSA(R)pS = B(R ⋐s S) · pS.
(LTO3) Whenever R1 ⊂R2 ⋐s S with ∂R1 ∩∂S = ∂R2 ∩∂S, B(R1 ⋐s S) = B(R2 ⋐s S).
(LTO4) Whenever R ⋐s S1 ⊂S2 with ∂R∩∂S1 = ∂R∩∂S2, if x ∈B(∂R∩∂S1) such that xpS2 = 0,
then x = 0.
The final three axioms may be loosely summarized by saying that B(R ⋐s S) really only depends
on the sites in L near I, and that there is a single algebra B(I) that plays the role of C in (LTO1)
when R ⋐s S instead of R ≪s S. For a rigorous version of this summary, we refer the reader to
[JNPW25, (2.14) and Prop. 2.15].

3. A Haag duality axiom for LTOs
We continue our assumption here that (A, p) is an LTO with projections assigned to disk-like
regions R, which are extended to cone-like regions by looking at the net of von Neumann algebras
Λ 7→A(Λ) := A(Λ)′′ acting on L2(A, ψ). As before, we write B(Λ) := pΛA(Λ)pΛ, which is the

The Haag duality axiom for LTOs (LTO-HD) is a compatibility condition for the boundary
algebra of a cone-shaped region Λ and its complement Λ′. As our axiom treats Λ and Λ′ with equal

pS+A(R+)pS = pS+pS−A(R)pS = pS−A(R−)pS, i.e.,
B(R+ ⋐S+)pS = pS+pS−A(R)pS = B(R−⋐S−)pS.
