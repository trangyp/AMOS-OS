Abstract
In this paper, we solve exit problems for a Lévy process that resets proportionally to its
current position at independent Poisson epochs times. This resetting causes an additional
(proportional to its current level) downward (upward) jump when the current position of the
process is on the positive (negative) domain. Such a process can be expressed as an SDE,
whose existence and uniqueness it discussed. All identities are given in terms of new family
of scale functions. To obtain the new family of scale functions, we reduce the problem of the
LT of the exit times into integral equations that are solve in terms of resolvent series.
Keywords: Partial stochastic resetting Lévy process, fluctuation theory, scale functions, partial
resetting integral equation.

Introduction
This paper studies a spectrally negative Lévy process (SNLP) which experiences, at some (inde-
pendent) Poisson epochs, a random jump whose size is random proportion of the state just before
the jump. Stochastic processes exhibiting this type of mechanism are referred as processes with
partial stochastic resetting or processes with additive-increase and multiplicative-decreased (AIMD).
In the special case where the process resets to the origin (the proportional component is absent,
one obtains a process with total stochastic resetting, a class of models originating in [14]. Due
to the theoretical and practical significance, such processes have attracted a lot of attention and
various results for the total resetting can be found in [3, 6, 7, 10, 12, 15, 16, 26, 27, 28] and the
references therein, whilst for the partial resetting the results are limited and can be found in
[10, 27]. There is no doubt the aforementioned processes have been motivated from different
fields. Following the thorough and extensive discussion in [8, 10], the total or partial resetting
process can be found in population genetics (see for e.g. in [1]), in physics (see for e.g. [11]), in
the transmission control protocol (TCP) networks (see in [13, 16, 25]), in cash management (see
[8]), in stochastic thermodynamics (see [10]), in queuing (see [2]), in finance (see [17]).
To formulate mathematically our problem, we let X ≡{Xt}t≥0 be a SNLP and N := {Nt}t≥0 be
a Poisson process with epochs {Ti}i∈N of intensity λ. The collapses are modelled by multiplying
the present process position by a fixed proportion p ∈(0,1), i.e. −∆XTi = (1−p)XTi−for ∆Xt = Xt −
Xt−. Therefore, we can define the partially stochastic resetting Lévy process (PSR-LP) U ≡{Ut}t≥0,
starting at U0 = x0 with a resetting coefficient p ∈(0,1) as the solution to the stochastic differential
equation (SDE)
dUt = dXt −(1 −p)Ut−dNt.
(1)
Clearly N models the number of partial resetting events in the time interval [0,t] (and for which
we have used the convention that Pn

the negative jumps due to the process X, U experiences downward jumps, due to resetting, when
UTi−> 0 and upward jumps, due to resetting, when UTi−< 0. It is also clear that the Brownian
motion component and the Lévy jump components now provide mechanisms for U to move
above and below 0, however, it is still not possible to move (spatially) between R+ to R−(or
vice-versa) by a partial stochastic reset. Clearly, when p = 1 the process reduces to dynamics of
the underlying process X and when p = 0 we get the total resetting SNLP. We note that in the
presence of partial stochastic resetting, the resulting trajectories tend to be closer to the origin,
while they experience long excursions in the unperturbed case. This hints at the existence of a
stationary state which was further investigated in [11]. A typical sample path of the process U is

The main aim of the paper is to develop fluctuation theory for the PSR-LP, which is achieved
by construction a new class of scale functions (based on potential measures rather the classical
scale functions) that fits the PSR-LP set up. Within this framework, the paper addresses two main
difficulties arising in the partial resetting or AIMD processes: (a) the fluctuation theory of Lévy
processes typically rely on two key properties, namely the Markov property and the skip-free
property. However, as pointed out in [27] the key feature distinguishing the AIMD processes
from classical Lévy models is that the distribution of each jump is depend on the position of X
prior to this jump. This dependence substantially complicates the study of associated exit identi-
ties and prevents the direct application of the standard machinery of Lévy fluctuation theory, for
eg. techniques based on Wiener–Hopf factorization or Itô excursion. To overcome these difficul-
ties, we develop an alternative methodology, based on potential densities and integral equations.
(b) As will be seen in the upcoming sections SPR-LP exit identities are derived based on an in-
tegral type equations, which the unknown function is evaluated at a contracted spatial point
rather than at the integration variable itself. This destroys the classical convolution or symmetric
structure typically exploited in integral equations and complicates the direct application of stan-
dard spectral or compactness arguments. To overcome this we develop a solution based on the
resolvent series.
The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2 we provide some basic theory of scale func-
tions and we provide useful identities that will be used in the rest of the paper. In Section 3, the
existence of the solution of the SDE in Eq. (1) is discussed and we present our main results (two
and one-sided exit identities) with their proofs. In the same section we show how these results
can reduce to the total stochastic resetting Lévy process, by means of a limiting argument (p →0).
