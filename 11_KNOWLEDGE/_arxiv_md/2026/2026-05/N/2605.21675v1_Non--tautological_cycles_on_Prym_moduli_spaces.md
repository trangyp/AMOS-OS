Abstract. We denote by Rg;m the moduli space of m–pointed Prym curves of genus g, that
is, tuples [ eC/C; x1, . . . , xm] where [C, x1, . . . , xm] is an m–pointed curve of genus g and eC/C is
an ´etale double cover of C. In this paper, we address the problem of the non–tautology of the
Chow ring of Rg;m. The locus which allows us to achieve earlier bounds for the non–tautology
of CH•(Rg) compared to Mg is the component RB0
g of the locus of bi–elliptic Prym curves.
This parametrises covers [ eC/C] such that, if C →E is the bi–elliptic structure, the composition
eC →E factors through an elliptic cover of E. Our main contribution is thus the non–tautology
of the class [RB0
8] ∈CH∗(R8). In the course of establishing this theorem, a similar result for
the compact moduli spaces Rg;2m for g + m ≥8 is proven.
1. Introduction
In the landmark paper [Mum83], Mumford raised for the first time the question whether the
Chow ring of Mg could be generated by tautological classes. Recall that the tautological ring
R•(Mg) is the Q–subalgebra of CH•(Mg) generated by the κ–classes κi := f∗(ci(ωf)i+1), where
f : Cg →Mg is the universal curve over Mg and ωf is the relative dualising sheaf of f.
Since then, significant strides towards a better understanding of the Chow rings CH•(Mg),
for low g, have been made:
– Mumford determines the Chow ring CH•(M 2) in [Mum83].
– Faber determines the Chow rings CH•(M 3) and CH•(M4) in [Fab90a] and [Fab90b].
– Izadi determines the Chow ring CH•(M5) in [Iza95].
– Vakil and Penev determine CH•(M6) in [PV15].
– Canning and H. Larson determine CH•(Mg) for g = 7, 8, 9 in [CL24].
In all the cases above, the Chow ring is generated by tautological classes and, using work by
Faber in [Fab99], the ideal of relations is fully determined.
The natural question is whether this pattern comes to an end as the genus g increases. The
answer, as expected, is affirmative. This expectation is confirmed for the first time in [GP03]
where, for g = 2(2h + 1), the Chow ring CH•(Mg) is shown to be non–tautological for h large
enough. Furthermore, van Zelm showed in [vZ18] that the class of the bi–elliptic locus in M12
is non–tautological. By [CL24] and [CL25], this is the first instance of [Bg] /∈R•(Mg).
Another moduli space whose Chow ring has been heavily investigated in recent years is Ag, the
moduli space of principally polarised abelian varieties of dimension g. In [COP25] and building
on methods from [CMOP25], they show that the class of the product locus [A1 × A5] does not
lie in the tautological ring R•(A6). Recall from [vdG99] that R•(Ag) is the Q–subalgebra of
CH∗(Ag) generated by the classes λi = ci(Eg) where Eg →Ag is the Hodge bundle.
In this work, we focus on the Prym moduli spaces Rg which parametrise ´etale double covers
of curves of genus g. These moduli spaces were classically used to uniformise moduli of abelian
varieties via the Prym map Pg : Rg →Ag−1. The morphism Pg is particularly effective in genus
g ≤6, when it is dominant. Studying the Chow ring of Rg, as well as the Chow ring of moduli
spaces of pointed Prym curves Rg;m := Rg ×Mg Mg,m, is therefore very natural. Our results
are the following.
Theorem 1.1. Assume g ≥2 and g + m ≥8. The even cohomology of Rg;2m contains non–
tautological algebraic cycles.

Let us now recall some considerations which give context to our theorems. From [FL10],
[FV12], [FV24], and [Bru16], we now know that Rg is unirational for g ≤7, uniruled for g ≤9,
and of general type for g ≥13, g ̸= 16. Seeing that the birational complexity of Rg increases
with the genus, we expect the Chow ring of Rg to become more complicated. In a way, this
paper confirms this heuristic reasoning. We also point out that, despite the fact that R8 is
uniruled, the Chow ring CH•(R8) is not tautological.
Another aspect of the geometry of Rg which motivated our work is the following observation.
It often happens the preimage u−1(Z) ⊂Rg of a subvariety Z of Mg under the forgetful map
u : Rg →Mg, is reducible. For example, this is the case when Z is the hyperlliptic or the bi–
elliptic locus in Mg. This last locus is instrumental in the proofs of Theorem 1.1 and Theorem
1.2. For a reference on the bi–elliptic locus in Rg, see [Nar92]. Since, as we have mentioned,
the bi–elliptic locus in Mg is non–tautological for g ≥12, any component of u−1(Bg) has non–
tautological class for g ≥12. The question now is whether this could happen at an earlier stage.
As it is transparent from the proofs of Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2, this strategy proves to
be successful.
1.1. Structure of the paper. The paper is organised as follows. Section 2 discusses some
basic notions and results foundational to the proof of Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2. The main
focus of this section are the boundary strata of Rg;m. Section 3 is dedicated to the cohomology
of R1;m. Finally, in Section 4, the results gathered so far are used to prove our main theorems.
