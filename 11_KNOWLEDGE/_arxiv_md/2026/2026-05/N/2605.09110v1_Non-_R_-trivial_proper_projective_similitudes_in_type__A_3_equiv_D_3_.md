NON-R-TRIVIAL PROPER PROJECTIVE SIMILITUDES
IN TYPE A3 ≡D3
M. ARCHITA AND KARIM JOHANNES BECHER
Abstract. Over an arbitrary field of characteristic different from 2 ad-
mitting an anisotropic torsion 3-fold Pfister form, we apply a construction
due to Merkurjev to produce an algebra with orthogonal involution of de-
gree 6 which admits proper projective similitudes that are not R-trivial. In
particular, such examples exist over every finitely generated transcendental
extension of a local or global number field, as well as over every finitely
generated extension of transcendence degree 3 of R.
Keywords: Classical adjoint linear algebraic group, stably rational, R-
equivalence, quadratic form, algebra with involution, quaternion algebra,
hyperbolic, Pfister form, cohomological dimension
Classification (MSC 2020): 11E04, 11E57, 11E81, 14E08, 20G15
1. Introduction
Let K be a field of characteristic different from 2. In [10], A. Merkurjev
studied R-equivalence for classical groups G of adjoint type over K. Presenting
G as the group of proper projective similitudes of a K-algebra with involution
(A, σ), he described the group of R-equivalence classes G(K′)/R for field exten-
sions K′/K in terms of a quotient of two subgroups of K′× related to factors
of similitude of σ and norms from finite extensions of K′ where the involution
becomes hyperbolic. (See Theorem 3.1 below.)
Merkurjev’s work had been followed up in various directions. Firstly, us-
ing Merkurjev’s criterion, R-triviality of G was established for certain types of
groups; see e.g. [13] and [1]. Secondly, many examples of non-R-trivial groups
G were constructed; see e.g. [6], [5], [4] and [1]. The wealth of examples sug-
gest that R-triviality might be rather the exception than the default. Thirdly,
sufficient conditions on the base field K were considered for having triviality
of G(K)/R for adjoint classical groups of given types; see e.g. [8], [11], [12], [3]
and [2].
In [10, Theorem 3], R-triviality is completely characterized in terms of in-
variants for adjoint groups of type A3 ≡D3. In this article we exhibit sufficient
conditions on the field K for the presence of adjoint groups G of type A3 ≡D3
over K such that G(K)/R is nontrivial. This is stronger than having that G is
not R-trivial, since for the latter, the existence of a (possibly transcendental)
extension K′/K with G(K′)/R ̸= {1} suffices. We apply a construction in [10]
to obtain examples of such groups G of type A3 ≡D3 with G(K)/R ̸= {1} over
any field K that admits an anisotropic torsion 3-fold Pfister form.

2. Algebras with involution and similitudes
By a K-algebra with involution we mean a pair (A, σ) where A is a finite-
dimensional K-algebra endowed with an K-linear involution σ such that A
has no nontrivial two-sided ideals I with σ(I) = I and, denoting by Z(A)
is the center of A, we have K = {x ∈Z(A) | σ(x) = x}.
It follows that
dimKA = [Z(A) : K] · n2 for some integer n ∈N, denoted by degA and called
the degree of A. Algebras with involution are distinguished into three types:
orthogonal, symplectic and unitary; we refer to [7, §2] for details.
Let (A, σ) be a K-algebra with an involution. An element a ∈A× is said
to be a similitude if σ(a)a ∈K×. The similitudes of (A, σ) form a subgroup
of A×, which is denoted by Sim(A, σ). We refer to [7, §12] for the basics on
similitudes. We obtain a group homomorphism
µ : Sim(A, σ) →K×,
a 7→σ(a)a ,
called the multiplier map. The image of this map is denoted by G(A, σ). Hence
G(A, σ) = {x ∈K× | x = σ(a)a for some a ∈A×} .
We set PSim(A, σ) = Sim(A, σ)/K× and call this the group of projective simil-
itudes of (A, σ). Note that PSim(A, σ) is naturally isomorphic to the group of
automorphisms of (A, σ), in view of the Skolem-Noether Theorem.
If degA = 2m with m ∈N and σ is orthogonal then, for a ∈Sim(A, σ), we
have NrdA(a) = ±am, and we set
Sim+(A, σ) = {a ∈Sim(A, σ) | NrdA(a) = am} .
If degA is odd or σ is not orthogonal, then we set Sim+(A, σ) = Sim(A, σ).
Hence Sim+(A, σ) is always a subgroup of Sim(A, σ) of index 1 or 2. We ob-
tain corresponding subgroups PSim+(A, σ) = Sim+(A, σ)/K× of Sim(A, σ) and
G+(A, σ) = µ(Sim+(A, σ)) of G(A, σ).
For a field extension K′/K, we obtain from (A, σ) by scalar extension an
K′-algebra with involution (AK′, σK′). One obtains naturally a group scheme
PSim+(A, σ) such that, for any field extension K′/K, the set of K′-rational
points PSim+(A, σ)(K′) is given by PSim+(AK′, σK′). Note that PSim+(A, σ)
is an adjoint, semi-simple, connected linear algebraic group over K.
Assume that (A, σ) is a K-algebra with orthogonal involution of even degree.
We denote by C(A, σ) the Clifford algebra of (A, σ); see [7, §8] for the definition.
The center of C(A, σ) is a quadratic ´etale extension of K, hence of the form
K[X]/(X2 −d) for some d ∈K×, and the square-class dK×2 ∈K×/K×2 is
uniquely determined by σ, denoted by disc(σ) and called the discriminant of σ.

3. R-triviality in groups of type A3 ≡D3
The study of R-equivalence for semisimple adjoint classical groups can be
reduced to such ones that are absolutely simple. As a consequence of A. Weil’s
classification results in [14], any absolutely simple adjoint classical group over
K is given by PSim+(A, σ) for some K-algebra with involution (A, σ). If A
is of even degree 2n and σ is an orthogonal involution, then this group is of
Dynkin type Dn.
For a finite field extension L/K, we write NL/K : L →K for the norm
map, and we abbreviate N∗
L/K = NL/K(L×). Similarly, for a central simple
K-algebra A, we write NrdA : A →K for the reduced norm map, and we
abbreviate Nrd∗
A = NrdA(A×).
Let (A, σ) be a K-algebra with orthogonal or symplectic involution.
We
denote by Hyp(A, σ) the subgroup of K× generated by the subsets N∗
L/K where
L/K ranges over the finite field extensions for which σL is hyperbolic. We set
H(A, σ) = K×2 · Hyp(A, σ) .
Merkurjev studies the group of R-equivalence classes of PSim+(A, σ) by means
of the following translation.
Theorem 3.1 (Merkurjev [10, Theorem 1]). We have H(A, σ) ⊆G+(A, σ) and
PSim+(A, σ)/R ≃G+(A, σ)/H(A, σ).
In particular, PSim+(σ) is R-trivial if and only if G+(AK′, σK′) = H(AK′, σK′)
holds for every field extension K′/K.
This provides tool to compute R-equivalence on semisimple adjoint classical
groups and to construct examples of nonrational adjoint groups. The simplest
examples occur for algebras with orthogonal involutions of degree 6. In the
split case, where (A, σ) is adjoint to a regular 6-dimensional quadratic form
η over K, [10, Theorem 2] yields that PSim+(A, σ) is R-trivial if and only if
C(A, σ) (the even Clifford algebra of η) is not a division algebra. Using this one
obtains examples over fields of cohomological dimension 3 where PSim+(A, σ)
is not R-trivial. However, there seems to be no such example known where
PSim+(A, σ)(K) ̸= {1} over a field of cohomological dimension 3 with A split.
We now turn our attention to the nonsplit case.
Proposition 3.2. Let (A, σ) be a K-algebra with orthogonal involution of degree
6 and of nontrivial discriminant. Let C = C(A, σ) and L = Z(C). Assume that
ind(C) = 2 and A is not split. Then there exist K-quaternion algebras Q, Q1, Q2
and an orthogonal involution γ on Q such that L is contained in Q and γ extends
the nontrivial automorphism of L/K and such that A ∼Q ∼Q1 ⊗Q2 and
(A, σ) ∈(Q1, canQ1) ⊗(Q2, canQ2) ⊞(Q, γ) .
Proof. This is explained in [10, p. 207-208].

In the case where K is nonreal, we can now characterize when exactly all
proper projective similitudes are R-trivial.
Corollary 3.8. Assume that K is nonreal. Then PSim+(A, σ)(K)/R = {1}
for every central simple K-algebra with orthogonal involution (A, σ) of degree 6
if and only if I3K = 0.
Proof. Note that I3K is torsion, because K is nonreal. If I3K ̸= 0, then by
Theorem 3.7 there exists a central simple F-algebra with orthogonal involution
(A, σ) of degree 6 such that PSim+(A, σ)(K)/R ̸= {1}. If I3K = 0, then by
[8, Prop. 3.2], every central simple K-algebra with orthogonal involution (A, σ)
satisfies G(A, σ) = H(A, σ) and consequently PSim+(A, σ)(K)/R = {1}.
□
