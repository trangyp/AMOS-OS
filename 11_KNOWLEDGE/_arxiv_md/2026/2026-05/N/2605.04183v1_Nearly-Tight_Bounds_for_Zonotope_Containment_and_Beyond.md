Abstract
We investigate the convex-body containment problem max{s > 0 : sZ ⊆Q}, where the outer body
Q ⊆Rd is described by a membership oracle and the inner body Z ⊆Rd is a zonotope. Our main result
is a sampling-based O(
√
d)-approximation algorithm for this problem that almost matches the lower
bound of Ω(
p
d/log d) by Khot and Naor in the oracle model. Assuming zonotopes can be sparsified
by a linear number of generators, which is referred to as Talagrand conjecture, our approach attains the
optimal approximation factor of Θ(
p
d/log d). Our second main result is a proof of Talagrand’s conjecture
for ∆-modular zonotopes whenever ∆is constant. Those zonotopes are of the form Z = {Wx: ∥x∥∞≤
1} where the non-zero d × d sub-determinants of W are between 1 and ∆. This result establishes a
connection between zonoid sparsification and spectral sparsification of Batson, Spielman and Srivastava.
We complement these results with a universal Ω(
p
d/log d) lower bound holding for all zonotopes.
Finally, we consider containment problems max{s > 0 : sK ⊆Q}, for general convex bodies K ⊆

∆-Modular Zonotopes.
A zonotope Z = {Wx: x ∈[−1, 1]n} is ∆-modular, if W ∈Rd×n has full

General Convex Bodies and Polytope Approximation.
Naszódi [48] provided a sampling based al-
gorithm to approximate any convex body K with a polytope P spanned by a polynomial number of ver-
tices, such that, if P is scaled by O(d/log d), then it contains K (see also [47]). One can further note that,
for s(d) = O(d/log d), Gap-Containment is a Yes-instance if all vertices of P are in Q, otherwise it is a
No-instance. Since sampling from a convex body can be done in polynomial time [23, 44, 19], then for
s(d) = O(d/log d), Gap-Containment can also be solved in randomized polynomial-time. A recent re-
sult by Huang and Rudelson [35] shows that this O(d/log d) factor is optimal for general (non-symmetric)
convex bodies. This is achieved by constructing a specific convex body such that no polytope with a poly-
nomial number of vertices can improve upon the O(d/log d) factor. We show a matching lower bound of
s(d) = Ω(d/log d) for any polynomial-time algorithm for Gap-Containment that holds even for symmetric
convex bodies. Consequently, Naszódi’s method is an optimal algorithm for containment. For symmetric
convex bodies K ⊆Rd, Barvinok [6] showed that there exists a polytope P ⊆K with a polynomial number
of vertices such that sP ⊇K with s = O(
√
d). Our lower bound proves that this approximation cannot be
computed in polynomial time, if K is given by a membership oracle.
1.2
Related Work
The zonotope containment problem — the question of whether one zonotope is fully contained in a second
zonotope — arises naturally in the context of complexity theory [57, 8, 9, 34], control theory [1, 39, 41],
and neural network verification [26, 27, 28]. Despite its significance, the algorithmic literature on zono-
tope containment remains sparse. To the best of our knowledge, most existing algorithms focus on exact
containment and, thus, require exponential time relative to the number of generators, or some other re-
strictive structural assumption [51, 40]. Furthermore, the scenario where a zonotope is contained within a
convex body accessible only via an oracle remains largely unexplored, unlike the computation of inradius,
circumradius, diameter, volume and other geometric functionals [15, 23, 45, 44, 36, 46, 19].
