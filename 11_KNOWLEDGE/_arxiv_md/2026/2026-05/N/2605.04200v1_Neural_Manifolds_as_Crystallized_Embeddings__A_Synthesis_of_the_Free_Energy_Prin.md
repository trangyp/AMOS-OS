Abstract
The free energy principle casts perception as variational inference, but its biological implemen-
tation remains underspecified. In particular, the generalized-coordinate formalism should not
be read as a literal claim that neurons compute arbitrary Taylor expansions. This paper argues
that generalized synchronization provides the missing bottom-up mechanism.
A contractive
recurrent circuit driven by structured sensory input can synchronize to the driving dynamics.
Under generic embedding conditions developed in the reservoir-computing literature, the re-
sulting synchronization map can embed the low-dimensional sensory manifold into neural state
space. Thus, the geometry predicted by the free energy principle need not be imposed from
above by an explicitly Bayesian neural calculus; it can arise from ordinary recurrent dynamics
driven by the world.
I then propose a developmental extension.
Hebbian plasticity acting on the correlations
generated by sensory-driven synchronization may crystallize the embedded manifold into re-
current connectivity, yielding an autonomous continuous attractor network when the required
fixed point exists. On this view, mature head-direction, grid-cell, and stimulus-driven visual
manifolds are not genetically prespecified templates, but developmental products of three in-
teracting processes: dynamical contraction, generalized synchronization, and correlation-based
plasticity. The synthesis links the free energy principle, reservoir-computing embedding theo-
rems, and contraction-theoretic models of Hebbian recurrent networks. It also yields testable
predictions about dimensional thresholds for topological recovery, developmental sensitivity to
plasticity, and the dependence of attractor geometry on input statistics.
The central open
problem is whether the Hebbian fixed point exists and preserves the embedding quality of the
synchronization manifold.

Introduction: The Bridge to Biological Mechanism
The free energy principle (FEP), developed by Friston and colleagues across two decades, casts
cortical computation as variational Bayesian inference in generalized coordinates of motion — a
Taylor-series-style decomposition of sensory trajectories on which approximate posterior beliefs
evolve via gradient descent on variational free energy [14, 19]. The framework is mathematically

rigorous and computationally productive. Its biological implementation, however, is not a literal
correspondence: the generalized-coordinate formalism should not be read as a literal claim that neu-
rons explicitly compute arbitrary Taylor expansions, and gradient flows in generalized-coordinate
space do not have direct correlates in identifiable neural mechanism.
Friston and colleagues have addressed this gap primarily through process theory. Predictive
coding maps variational inference onto hierarchical message passing, with descending predictions
and ascending prediction errors.
Precision weighting is linked to synaptic gain and attention.
Active inference connects perception to action through the fulfillment of sensory and proprioceptive
predictions. Learning is treated as parameter optimization in a generative model, while Markov-
blanket formulations place perception, action, and self-organization within a broader account of
biological systems at non-equilibrium steady state [14, 19, 38, 17, 16]. Within this biological bridge,
generalized synchronization appears as the dynamical relation between internal and external states
under free-energy minimization.
A parallel thread makes this synchronization picture naturally adjacent to embedding theory.
Kiebel, Daunizeau, and Friston [30] compared generalized coordinates with Takens-style temporal
embedding. Friston, Breakspear, and Deco [15] formalized free-energy minimization as inducing
generalized synchronization between brain and environment. Palacios et al. [38] extended this
picture to coupled networks of mutually inferring neurons, and Friston et al. [17] developed the
Markov-blanket version in stochastic dynamical systems. Most recently, Friston et al. [18] invoked
Takens’ theorem directly in a renormalizing active-inference setting, with an explicit appeal to
Haken’s synergetics and the slaving principle. These papers make an embedding-theoretic reading
of FEP natural, but they do not supply the quantitative embedding theory or the developmental
mechanism.
This paper makes that step, as well as the motivation for making it. I argue that generalized
synchronization provides a bottom-up mechanism for the biological implementation of FEP. A
contractive recurrent circuit driven by structured sensory input can synchronize to the driving
dynamics; under generic embedding conditions, the resulting synchronization map can embed the
sensory manifold into neural state space. On this view, the geometry described by FEP need not be
imposed by an explicitly Bayesian neural calculus. It can arise from recurrent dynamics driven by
the world, and may then be stabilized developmentally by Hebbian plasticity acting on the activity
correlations generated by that synchronization.
Their developmental endpoint is the continuous attractor network whose attractor manifold is
the embedded sensory manifold, sculpted developmentally by the conjunction of dynamical contrac-
tion and correlation-based plasticity rather than genetically prespecified. The empirical regularity
that motivates the claim is a striking finding of twenty-first-century systems neuroscience: when
the brain represents a sensory variable that lives on a low-dimensional geometric object, the cor-
responding neural population activity traces out that same geometric object. Head direction cells
in the rodent thalamic and cortical head-direction circuit form a ring [9, 40]. Grid cells in entorhi-
nal cortex form a torus [20, 11]. Population activity in mouse visual cortex can trace a circular
manifold under densely sampled stimulus rotations [4]; Stringer et al. [48] provide complementary
evidence on the high-dimensional geometry of V1 population responses. Across these and other
systems, neural population activity recovers low-dimensional geometric structure corresponding to
the represented variable, even when thousands of neurons participate.
Despite the regularity of the phenomenon, no unified mechanistic account exists for why cortical
representation should take this geometric form. The continuous attractor network (CAN) literature
[29, 5] describes these manifolds as autonomous attractors of structured recurrent connectivity, but
typically takes that connectivity as given by hand-designed templates or treats its developmental
origin as a separate problem.

The free energy principle (FEP) [14] and predictive processing frameworks explain that cortex
performs inference but, on their own, do not specify why the inference produces this particular
geometry rather than some other. The neural manifold literature characterizes the geometry em-
pirically but does not derive it from principle. Each literature describes part of the picture; none
provides the whole.
In this paper, I argue that the pieces of a unified account exist and have not been assem-
bled. Motivated by the potential bottom-up advantages of an embedding-theoretic view [35, 36]
and our other work in this space [37], the argument proceeds in five steps. Section 2 traces the
points at which Friston and colleagues connect generalized coordinates, generalized synchronization,
Takens-style embedding, Markov blankets, and self-organization. Section 3 reviews the parallel de-
velopment in reservoir computing and dynamical systems theory, where it has been formalized that
generalized synchronization is a topological embedding of the source attractor under generic Whit-
ney conditions, with isometric embedding under Nash dimensional bounds. Section 4 reviews the
contraction-theoretic study of Hebbian-plastic recurrent networks. Section 5 proposes the synthe-
sis: Hebbian plasticity acting on the activity correlations produced by sensory-driven generalized
synchronization crystallizes the embedded synchronization manifold into autonomous CAN connec-
tivity. Section 6 derives empirical predictions and identifies the open mathematical problem at the
heart of the claim. Section 7 discusses relations to neighboring frameworks — Haken’s synergetics,
slow feature analysis, self-organizing maps, and population coding theory.
The synthesis is novel in its integrated form, although each ingredient appears in prior work.
The contribution of the present paper is to make the integration explicit, to identify what has been
claimed, what has been merely gestured at, and what remains genuinely open.

The Free Energy Principle’s Embedding Trajectory (2009–2025)
The first move toward an embedding-theoretic account of cortical representation under the free
energy principle was made in Kiebel, Daunizeau, and Friston [30]. In a discussion of how varia-
tional filtering tracks fast sensory dynamics through generalized coordinates of motion, the authors
wrote: “The use of generalised coordinates is formally similar to temporal embedding in the char-
acterisation of dynamical systems: Takens’ theorem [49] states that it is possible to embed (i.e.,
geometrically represent) the structure of a vector-field in a higher dimensional space. This means
that one can reconstruct the structure of the manifold, on which dynamics unfold, by using a Taylor
expansion of the vector-field. This is very close to the idea of projecting the system into generalized
coordinates.” In the same paper, the authors speculated on synchronization: “Coupled nonlinear
systems naturally evolve towards a synchronous state, even with relatively weak coupling. It would
be very interesting if these synchronised states could be associated with optimised free-energy states
that are mandated by perception in particular and the free-energy principle in general.”
Friston, Breakspear, and Deco [15] developed this connection formally. Treating perceptual
dynamics as a skew-product system in which environmental hidden states drive neuronal response
states, they framed the resulting coupling in terms of generalized synchronization. They distin-
guished weak from strong synchronization based on the smoothness of the synchronization map:
weak synchronization corresponds to a continuous C0 but non-smooth map with fractal synchroniza-
tion manifold and global dimension exceeding the driver dimension, while strong synchronization
corresponds to a smooth C1 map preserving dimensions. They used conditional Lyapunov expo-
nents to characterize the stability of the synchronization manifold and partitioned the exponents
into tangential and transverse components. They demonstrated through simulations of avian song
perception that free-energy minimization produces fluctuations of conditional Lyapunov exponents

around small near-zero values, a regime they termed self-organized critical slowing. The formal
foundations cited were [28, 1].
Palacios, Isomura, Parr, and Friston [38] extended the framework to coupled networks of mu-
tually inferring neurons, demonstrating that emergent generalized synchronization arises directly
from local free-energy minimization. The 2019 work also showed that synaptic plasticity in this
setting produces structure learning that reflects the causal structure of exogenous input, although
the plasticity rule used was free-energy gradient descent rather than Hebbian correlation. In a
parallel paper, Palacios, Razi, Parr, Kirchhoff, and Friston [39] developed the Markov-blanket and
hierarchical self-organisation framework.
Friston, Heins, Ueltzh¨offer, Da Costa, and Parr [17] took the framework into Markov-blanket
territory. Using coupled stochastic Lorenz systems, they showed that conditional independencies
at non-equilibrium steady-state induce a particular partition of states into internal, blanket, and
external sets, and that the coupling between internal and external states across the blanket can
be characterized as a generalized synchrony or synchronization of chaos. They cited [28, 1] for the
formal grounding of the synchronization claim.
Friston, Da Costa, Sakthivadivel, Heins, Pavliotis, Ramstead, and Parr [16] provided the path-
integral formulation. Encoding the dynamics of any random dynamical system by a Lagrangian
playing the role of self-information, they showed that the most-likely internal path under free-energy
minimization encodes a Bayesian belief about the most-likely external path. The synchronization
between internal and external paths is interpreted as inference, in the sense that the internal path
parameterizes a conditional density over external paths.
Most recently, Friston, Heins, Verbelen, Da Costa, Salvatori, Markovic, Tschantz, Koudahl,
Buckley, and Parr [18] returned to embedding directly. In a section addressing how renormalizing
generative models can recover stochastic chaos from sequences of static images, the authors wrote:
“The answer lies in Takens’ embedding theorem [49, 10], which means that any (chaotic) attractor
can be reconstructed from a time-delay embedding, which is implicit in the temporal RG operators
used in renormalization.” They cited [34] on coupled-map-lattice spatiotemporal embedding. The
continuous-time link to the renormalization-group framework, they noted, is generalized coordinates
of motion [19], with the renormalization-group formulation as the discrete homologue.
Taken together, these papers articulate a framework in which (i) free-energy minimization
induces generalized synchronization between brain and environment; (ii) generalized coordinates of
motion and renormalization-group operators implement a Takens-style temporal embedding; (iii)
the synchronization manifold has the formal properties of a Bayesian belief about the world. They
make the bridge to embedding theory natural, but they do not supply the quantitative dimensional
content from the reservoir-computing line or the developmental mechanism proposed here.
What this trajectory has not done is quantify the embedding-theoretic content of the synchro-
nization claim, nor leverage it for explanatory power. The C0/C1 distinction is invoked quali-
tatively. The dimension counting is done via Kaplan-Yorke on conditional Lyapunov exponents
rather than via Whitney’s bound. The Nash isometric embedding is not invoked. Although Friston
et al. [18] cite Ørstavik and Stark [34] on coupled-map-lattice embedding, the specific forced and
stochastic delay-embedding theorems of Stark [46] and Stark et al. [47] — the formal results that
handle exactly the Langevin-driven skew-product structure on which the FEP rests — have not
been developed as part of the FEP’s perception and generalized-synchronization account. These
are precisely the ingredients that the parallel reservoir-computing literature has developed.

The Reservoir-Computing Formalization of GS as Embedding
Independently of the FEP literature, the reservoir computing community has developed a quantita-
tive formalization of generalized synchronization as an embedding. The relevant theorems are due
to Hart and collaborators, building on prior work by Stark, Sauer, Yorke, and Casdagli on delay
embeddings for forced systems.
Stark [46] and Stark, Broomhead, Davies, and Huke [47] extended the classical Takens em-
bedding theorem to forced systems. The key result is that for a skew-product system in which a
driver state x evolves autonomously and a response state y is forced by the driver, the time-delay
embedding constructed from observations of the response generically yields a topological embed-
ding of the driver attractor into the reconstructed state space, provided the embedding dimension
exceeds twice the dimension of the driver attractor and certain genericity conditions on the periodic
orbits of the forcing are satisfied. The 2003 paper extends the result to stochastic forcing, which is
precisely the Langevin-driven structure that the FEP invokes.
Hart, Hook, and Dawes [27] showed that for echo state networks driven by structured input, the
network admits an embedding of the input system’s attractor under conditions on the contraction
and observation function. Grigoryeva, Hart, and Ortega [21] proved a more general result: “The
celebrated embedding theorem of Takens is a particular case of a much more general statement ac-
cording to which, randomly generated linear state-space representations of generic observations of
an invertible dynamical system carry in their wake an embedding of the phase space dynamics into
the chosen Euclidean state space. This embedding coincides with a natural generalized synchro-
nization that arises in this setup and that yields a topological conjugacy between the state-space
dynamics driven by the generic observations of the dynamical system and the dynamical system
itself.” Hart [25] extended the result to continuous-time reservoir computers, providing a closed-
form integral representation of the synchronization function in the linear case and establishing C1
regularity under explicit contraction and observation conditions.
Hart [26] proved the Whitney/Nash version. For a generic state-update map F — among all
smooth maps satisfying the echo state property and bunching — of reservoir dimension N > 2q,
where q is the dimension of the source attractor, the generalized synchronization function f : M →
RN is a C1 embedding of the source attractor. The genericity is over the full space of smooth
contracting maps, not restricted to linear reservoirs; this is the result that makes the framework
applicable to nonlinear biological circuits.
Separately, for sufficiently high dimension satisfying
Nash’s bound, an isometric embedding generalized synchronization exists; the isometric case can
be constructed explicitly when both the reservoir system and source dynamics are linear.
Wong, Martin, and Eckhardt [54] identified the contraction conditions on continuous-time leaky
reservoirs that guarantee the existence of generalized synchronization. The key result is that a
continuous-time leaky reservoir of the form ˙x = −Cx + σ(Ax + Bu) is globally contracting when
the connectivity matrix A satisfies sparse, diagonally-dominant-in-columns structure with nega-
tive diagonals — specifically, when the logarithmic norm µ1(A) < 1 or µ∞(A) < 1. These are
biologically interpretable conditions on the structure of cortical microcircuits.
The composite picture from this literature is the following. A driven recurrent network with ap-
propriate contraction structure admits a unique synchronization function from environmental states
to neural states. Under generic Whitney conditions, the synchronization function is a topological
embedding of the environmental attractor into the neural state space. Under sufficient dimension-
ality, the synchronization function can be made an isometric embedding in the Nash sense. The
contraction conditions required are biologically plausible.
The key papers canvassed here show little explicit bibliographic contact between the reservoir-
computing and FEP literatures. The two traditions have developed in parallel, with substantial

Hebbian Plasticity and Contraction Structure in Cortical Mi-
crocircuits
A third literature, partially separate from both the FEP and reservoir-computing lines, has studied
how Hebbian and Hebbian-like correlation-based plasticity acts in recurrent networks with contrac-
tion structure. The relevant results combine biological plausibility with mathematical tractability.
Three results from two literatures form an almost-complete chain from plasticity to embedding.
Kozachkov, Lundqvist, Slotine, and Miller [32] showed that anti-Hebbian plasticity drives the recur-
rent weight matrix toward symmetry — the anti-symmetric part of W decays to zero. Centorrino,
Bullo, and Russo [8] proved that symmetric weight matrices admit sufficient conditions for Eu-
clidean (ℓ2) contraction in Hopfield and firing-rate networks, with log-optimal rates. Euclidean
ℓ2 contraction provides one natural route to the contraction and bunching hypotheses used in the
GS/embedding machinery of Hart [25] and Wong, Martin, and Eckhardt [54] — the operator-norm
contraction regime under which the synchronization function is C1 and the embedding theorems
apply. In a separate paper, Centorrino, Bullo, and Russo [7] extended the analysis to the joint
neural-synaptic system with dynamic Hebbian weights, proving non-Euclidean contractivity of the
coupled (h, W) dynamics in weighted ℓ1 norms.
The chain assembles as: (1) biological plasticity drives W toward symmetry; (2) symmetric
W admits Euclidean contraction conditions; (3) Euclidean contraction plus regular driving yields
smooth embedding. The remaining gap is a timescale separation argument. Kozachkov’s sym-
metrization is dynamic — W is changing. Centorrino 2023’s Euclidean result is for fixed sym-
metric W. The connection requires that after plasticity has approximately converged and W is
approximately symmetric and stationary, the Centorrino 2023 conditions apply and the embedding
theorems activate. This is biologically reasonable: it is the difference between learning a sensory
representation (plasticity during development) and using it (approximately frozen weights during
steady-state perception). The timescale separation converts the dynamic non-Euclidean contraction
results into the static Euclidean contraction regime the embedding theorems need.
A separate strand of work has shown that Hebbian-like plasticity on driven recurrent net-
works can produce continuous attractor structure. Bernacchia [3] (preprint 2007 [2]) analyzed a
binary recurrent network with Hebbian plasticity and showed that a continuous attractor emerges
when the input distribution matches the tuning-curve distribution: experience matches sensory
coding.
Sch¨onsberg, Giana, Chopra, Diamond, and Goldt [45] studied perceptual biases that
emerge from continuous Hebbian plasticity in a recurrent network, showing that diverse perceptual
biases emerge from continuous Hebbian plasticity in a recurrent network model. The Hebbian-
architecture-generation literature in reservoir computing [6] has shown that unsupervised Hebbian
adaptation can reshape reservoir architecture from input correlations, improving downstream pro-
cessing.
A fourth strand has shown that Hebbian-like learning rules can produce structured recurrent
circuitry.
Vafidis, Owald, D’Albis, and Kempter [50] showed that path-integration accuracy in
head-direction ring attractor models can emerge from Hebbian learning. More broadly, Eckmann,
Young, and Gjorgjieva [12] demonstrated that synapse-type-specific competitive Hebbian learning
forms functional structured recurrent networks, illustrating the capacity of biological plasticity rules
to generate organized connectivity.
These results, taken together, establish that biologically realistic Hebbian plasticity operating
on driven recurrent networks can produce autonomous continuous attractor structure. What has

Synthesis
The synthesis proposed here is the following. The mathematical chain has three links, drawing
on published results from three literatures. First, biological plasticity mechanisms — specifically,
anti-Hebbian excitatory plasticity, inhibitory Hebbian plasticity, and excitatory-inhibitory balance
— drive the recurrent weight matrix toward symmetry and, once symmetric, produce contraction
in the Euclidean (ℓ2) norm [32, 8]. Second, Euclidean contraction in a driven recurrent network
guarantees the existence of a unique, smooth synchronization function f : M →RN under the
bunching condition [27, 25, 46]. Third, for generic observation functions and generic contracting
networks with N > 2 dim(M), the synchronization function is a C1 embedding [26]. Each link is
established in the literature; the composition has not been stated.
Consider a recurrent cortical circuit whose weights have approximately converged under Hebbian
plasticity to a symmetric matrix satisfying the Euclidean contraction conditions of [8], driven by
sensory input that lives on a low-dimensional environmental manifold. By Hart [25, 26], the network
admits a unique generalized synchronization function f : M →RN that is a C1 embedding of the
environmental manifold M into the neural state space RN, provided N > 2 dim(M), and an
isometric embedding under sufficient additional dimensionality. On the timescale of fast neural
dynamics, the network state tracks f(xt) where xt is the current environmental state.
Now superimpose Hebbian plasticity on the recurrent connectivity. The activity correlations
produced by the synchronization regime, integrated over experience, drive the recurrent weight
matrix toward a fixed point. I conjecture that the Hebbian fixed point is the recurrent connectivity
matrix whose autonomous attractor manifold is the image f(M) of the synchronization function,
i.e., the embedded environmental manifold.
At this fixed point, the circuit no longer requires
sensory drive to maintain the manifold; the manifold has been ’crystallized’ into connectivity. The
trained circuit is a continuous attractor network of the canonical neural type, whose attractor
geometry is the embedded sensory manifold, constructed developmentally from the conjunction of
generalized synchronization and Hebbian plasticity.
A subtlety deserves explicit statement. The contraction that licenses generalized synchroniza-
tion and the marginal stability that defines a continuous attractor are, locally, opposite dynamical
conditions. During the driven phase, the network contracts — all trajectories converge toward
the synchronization-function image f(M). In the autonomous CAN phase, the network must be
marginally stable along f(M) (permitting persistent activity at any point on the manifold) while
contracting transverse to f(M) (so that perturbations off the manifold decay). The crystallization
conjecture therefore requires that Hebbian plasticity, acting on the correlations of the GS-driven
activity, weakens contraction specifically in the directions tangent to f(M) while preserving con-
traction in the transverse directions. Whether the fixed point of such a plasticity rule has this
structure is the core of the open mathematical problem stated in Section 6.
The synthesis is novel in its integrated form. Each ingredient appears in prior work: Bernac-
chia [3] showed Hebbian-induced continuous attractors but without a synchronization-function
framing; Lu and Bassett [33] showed that invertible generalized synchronization combined with
output-feedback training produces autonomous attractors matching source dynamics, but with

FORCE training rather than Hebbian dynamics on internal weights; Sch¨onsberg et al. [45] showed
plastic-attractor formation under continuous Hebbian dynamics in driven recurrent networks; the
Hebbian-architecture-generation literature [6] showed Hebbian sculpting of reservoir connectivity
from input correlations. Palacios, Isomura, Parr, and Friston [38] combined synaptic plasticity with
FEP-induced generalized synchrony, but with free-energy gradient plasticity rather than Hebbian
correlation, and without the embedding-theoretic framing.
The three-way synthesis: Hebbian plasticity acting on the activity correlations produced by
sensory-driven generalized synchronization yields a CAN whose attractor manifold is the topologi-
cally (and under sufficient dimension, isometrically) embedded sensory manifold.
The synthesis has biological content distinct from its predecessors. It does not require genetic
prespecification of ring or toroidal connectivity; the connectivity is the developmental product of
sensory experience filtered through generic contraction structure and Hebbian plasticity. It predicts
that disrupting correlation-based plasticity during a developmental sensitive period should prevent
CAN formation despite normal sensory input, and that altering input statistics while preserving
plasticity should produce CANs whose geometry matches the altered statistics. It predicts that
the CAN attractor manifold should match not the bare sensory variable but the synchronization-
function image of that variable, which under generic observation conditions is a faithful topological
embedding but may differ metrically.
And it predicts a specific Whitney lower bound on the
dimensionality of the neural population that participates in the CAN: N > 2 dim(M).

Empirical Predictions and the Open Mathematical Problem
Five empirical predictions follow directly from the synthesis.
Prediction 1 (Generic embedding threshold).
For a d-dimensional sensory manifold, the
reservoir-embedding results predict a generic sufficient ambient dimension threshold of N > 2d for
faithful embedding by generic observation/reservoir maps. Since N is integer-valued, this corre-
sponds to N ≥2d+1. This is not an upper bound on the intrinsic dimension of the represented man-
ifold, which remains d, nor is it a topological lower bound: special embeddings can occur in lower
ambient dimensions, such as S1 ⊂R2 and T 2 ⊂R3. Rather, it is the generic Whitney/Takens-style
threshold at which faithful recovery should become robust under generic projections and adequate
sampling.
Prediction 2 (Topological recovery across projection dimension).
When persistent ho-
mology is applied to population activity projected into increasing ambient dimension, the predicted
Betti numbers should become more reliably recoverable once the projection dimension reaches
the generic embedding threshold N > 2d, assuming adequate sampling, signal-to-noise, and met-
ric choice.
Below this threshold, recovery may fail or become unstable, although special low-
dimensional embeddings can preserve topology. For head-direction systems (d = 1), β1 = 1 should
stabilize at N ≥3; for grid systems (d = 2), β1 = 2 and β2 = 1 at N ≥5; for V1 orientation
(d = 1), β1 = 1 at N ≥3.
Prediction 3 (Discrimination tracks embedding resolution).
If the synchronization-function
image is approximate, states whose images diverge slowly in neural state space should be percep-
tually confusable, and states whose images diverge quickly should be discriminable.
The psy-
chometric function on any stimulus dimension should track the local separation modulus of the
synchronization function image. Categorical perception, metameric equivalence, and the structure

of psychophysical thresholds emerge as consequences of embedding quality rather than requiring
separate explanations.
Prediction 4 (Developmental sensitivity).
Disrupting correlation-based recurrent plasticity
during the formation of a continuous attractor network should prevent autonomous attractor forma-
tion despite preserved sensory drive. Early sensory experience should determine attractor geometry
in cases where sensory statistics are manipulable.
Prediction 5 (Geometry tracks input statistics).
If sensory statistics are altered while plas-
ticity is preserved, the resulting CAN attractor manifold should match the altered statistics. This
is a sharper version of Bernacchia’s matching condition [3].
The published empirical record on Predictions 1 and 2 is consistent with the synthesis. The
Betti numbers found in Chaudhuri et al. [9], Gardner et al. [20], and di Sarra et al. [11] are consistent
with those the embedding framework predicts, at dimensions consistent with the Whitney bound.
Sansford, Whiteley, and Rubin-Delanchy [44] revisit the Gardner et al. toroidal topology finding
and establish that ambient intrinsic dimension much greater than log n suffices for persistence
diagrams to reveal latent homology — a result consistent with, but not directly establishing, the
Whitney-threshold prediction. A formal mathematical treatment of the smooth-embedding regime
in driven recurrent networks is available as a companion preprint [37].
The open mathematical problem.
The central claim of the synthesis — the existence of a
Hebbian fixed point for the induced weight dynamics on the synchronization-driven activity, and
the embedding-quality of the resulting crystallized circuit — has not been proved. The technical
ingredients exist: plasticity-driven symmetrization [32], Euclidean contraction under symmetric
weights [8], non-Euclidean contraction of the joint neural-synaptic system [7], the existence of
synchronization functions under contraction [25, 54], and generic embedding under Whitney bounds
[26, 21].
The question is whether the Hebbian fixed point yields symmetric weights satisfying the Eu-
clidean (ℓ2) contraction conditions of [8] on the neural subsystem after timescale separation, with
the resulting crystallized circuit supporting the synchronization-function image as an autonomous
attractor. Whether the fixed point exists, whether it is unique, whether it preserves the embed-
ding quality, and whether the autonomous attractor coincides exactly with the embedded sensory
manifold or only approximates it — these are open questions. The composition of three separately-
proved results is not obvious, precisely because of the metric mismatch between Centorrino 2024’s
non-Euclidean contraction of the dynamic system and Centorrino 2023’s Euclidean contraction of
the frozen system.
I conjecture that under: (i) timescale separation between fast neural dynamics and slow plas-
ticity, so that the embedding analysis applies to approximately frozen weights; (ii) an anti-Hebbian
or mixed Hebbian rule that drives the weight matrix toward symmetry [32]; (iii) the resulting sym-
metric weights satisfying the Euclidean contraction conditions of [8] during the driven phase; and
(iv) positive-definite time-averaged activity correlations C = ⟨f(ϕt(x)) f(ϕt(x))⊤⟩t — the Hebbian
fixed point W ∗exists and supports f(M) as an autonomous attractor of the undriven dynamics,
with marginal stability along f(M) and contraction transverse to it. The metric distortion between
the autonomous attractor and the embedded sensory manifold is bounded by the local condition
number of f. This is a well-posed mathematical question with accessible technical ingredients from
three literatures. A proof, or a counterexample, would settle the central technical question of the
synthesis.

Technical gaps in the supporting chain.
Several gaps remain beyond the central conjecture.
First, the embedding theorems of Hart [27, 26] are proved in discrete time; the FEP’s generalized
filtering operates in continuous time. Hart [25] and Wong et al. [54] provide continuous-time GS
results, but the full chain from contraction through smoothness to generic embedding has not been
assembled in continuous time; Hart [26] explicitly notes this as open. Second, the FEP involves
stochastic (Langevin) dynamics; Stark et al. [47] prove delay embeddings under stochastic forcing,
but the GS-to-embedding chain under noise is less developed. Third, the genericity in Hart [26]
— “generic F among all smooth contracting maps” — does not directly imply that any specific
parameterized family of networks (such as tanh RNNs or biological firing-rate models) lies in the
generic set, although the richness of these families makes this overwhelmingly likely. Fourth, the
Euclidean contraction conditions of [8] are proved for autonomous networks; in the driven case the
h-Jacobian is unaffected by scalar input (the driving term enters additively), but this needs careful
statement for vector-valued sensory input. Fifth, whether Friston’s generalized filtering dynamics
satisfy the contraction/echo-state conditions that license the embedding chain has not been proved;
the precision-weighting mechanism may provide sufficient contraction, but this is an open question
connecting the FEP’s variational machinery to the dynamical-systems framework invoked here.
7
Relation to Other Frameworks
The synthesis has natural connections to several adjacent traditions.
Haken’s synergetics.
The synchronization function is the dynamical-systems heir of Haken’s
slaving principle [22, 23], with fast neural dynamics enslaved by slow sensory order parameters on
a low-dimensional manifold. The HKB model [24] is the classical coordination-dynamics instance
of slaving on S1. Friston’s 2025 paper [18] makes this lineage explicit, dedicating itself to Haken’s
memory and noting that recursive application of the slaving principle is the synergetic foundation
of FEP renormalizability. The crystallization claim adds a developmental dimension to the slaving
lineage: the slaved configuration is not merely a state of the joint system but is sculpted into
autonomous connectivity by Hebbian plasticity, so that the slaved manifold survives even when the
driver is removed.
Self-organizing maps and slow feature analysis.
Self-organizing maps [31, 51] share the
intuition that competitive plasticity from driven activity yields a topology-preserving map from
input space into neural space. Slow feature analysis [53, 13] produces place-cell- and head-direction-
cell-like representations from temporal slowness. The crystallization synthesis differs in three ways:
the synchronization function arises from the recurrent dynamics of the driven circuit itself rather
than from a hand-imposed competitive lattice; the post-Hebbian attractor manifold is autonomous
— the trained circuit no longer needs sensory drive to maintain the manifold; and the dimensional
content is set by Whitney/Nash bounds rather than by lattice geometry.
Population coding theory.
Rate coding, spike-timing coding, and population coding emerge
as projections of the synchronization function onto different measurement axes. The recent work of
Walker et al. [52] and Pohl et al. [41] on the conceptual dimensions of representation — sensitivity,
specificity, invariance, and functionality — maps directly onto properties of the synchronization
function: continuity, injectivity, low-dimensional domain, and the prediction-separation link.
10


## Page 11


Predictive processing.
The synthesis is consistent with the predictive processing framework
but adds geometric content the framework does not contain on its own. Predictive processing is a
story about the inferential structure of cortical computation; the crystallization synthesis is a story
about how that inferential structure is built developmentally and what geometry it produces. Active
inference, which closes the sensorimotor loop, requires an extension of the embedding theorems
beyond the strict skew-product setting. Although Friston et al. [18] cite Ørstavik and Stark [34]
in an embedding context, the specific forced and stochastic delay-embedding theorems [46, 47] —
which handle exactly the Langevin-driven skew-product structure that the FEP invokes — have
not been developed as part of the FEP’s perception and generalized-synchronization account; this
remains fertile ground.
Bayesian mechanics.
The recent Bayesian-mechanics formulations of the FEP [43, 42] provide
axiomatic foundations for the variational structure of self-organizing systems. The crystallization
synthesis is compatible with these formulations and may provide a route to a more concrete bio-
physical interpretation of Bayesian mechanics in cortical circuits, where the abstract variational
structure is realized through the conjunction of contraction-induced synchronization and Hebbian-
induced crystallization.
8
Conclusion
The geometry of cortical representation has been one of the most striking empirical findings of
twenty-first-century systems neuroscience, and it has lacked a principled mechanistic account. I
have argued that the pieces of such an account exist across three currently-fragmented literatures:
the free energy principle, the reservoir-computing formalization of generalized synchronization as
embedding, and the contraction-theoretic study of Hebbian plastic recurrent networks. The syn-
thesis is that Hebbian plasticity acting on the activity correlations produced by sensory-driven
generalized synchronization crystallizes the embedded synchronization manifold into autonomous
continuous attractor network connectivity. When the required fixed point exists, the mature circuit
would be a CAN whose attractor manifold approximates the embedded sensory manifold.
The synthesis is testable.
It predicts specific Whitney dimension thresholds for persistent-
homology recovery of manifold topology, a developmental sensitivity of CAN formation to correlation-
based plasticity, and a sensitivity of attractor geometry to input statistics. The published empirical
record on the dimensional thresholds is consistent with the synthesis. The central remaining math-
ematical question — the existence and embedding-quality of the Hebbian fixed point — is identified
as an open problem.
Friston’s free-energy program has repeatedly linked perception, synchronization, generalized
coordinates, Markov blankets, and self-organization, but it has not supplied a bottom-up account
of how cortical circuits acquire the geometry attributed to them by the theory.
The synthesis
developed here identifies generalized synchronization as that missing dynamical bridge. Structured
sensory drive can embed environmental dynamics into recurrent neural state space, and Hebbian
plasticity may crystallize the resulting synchronization manifold into autonomous connectivity.
The result is a falsifiable developmental claim: neural manifolds should depend on dimensional
sufficiency, contraction, sensory statistics, and intact correlation-based plasticity.
