Abstract. We study 3-dimensional trans-Sasakian manifolds using the Newman–Penrose for-
malism. In this framework, the geometry of the structure vector field is encoded by scalar spin
coefficients: acceleration, shear, expansion, and twist. A central observation is that, in dimen-
sion 3, the trans-Sasakian condition is equivalent to the characteristic vector field defining a
shear-free geodesic congruence, or equivalently a conformal foliation by geodesics. Thus, the
Newman–Penrose equations provide a direct scalar formulation of the conformal foliations stud-
ied by Baird and Wood in the theory of harmonic morphisms. Within this framework, we derive
curvature and Laplacian identities for trans-Sasakian manifolds and their main subclasses, in-
cluding formulae for the Ricci tensor, scalar curvature, Einstein condition, rough Laplacian,
divergence and harmonicity of the characteristic vector field, together with several illustrative
examples. As an application, we consider trans-Sasakian structures compatible with fixed ho-
mogeneous metrics of type E(κ, τ). We prove a rigidity result: in the non-space-form cases, the
Newman–Penrose equations force the characteristic vector field to be vertical. Hence, for τ ̸= 0
and κ ̸= 4τ 2, every compatible trans-Sasakian structure is the canonical vertical α-Sasakian
structure, while for τ = 0 and κ ̸= 0, it is vertical and cosymplectic.
In particular, these
non-space-form homogeneous metrics admit no proper compatible trans-Sasakian structures.

Introduction
The Newman–Penrose formalism, introduced by Ezra T. Newman and Roger Penrose [19, Newman and
Penrose 1962], provides a powerful framework in general relativity for studying spacetime geometry through
a complex null tetrad. Its essential feature is the replacement of the tensorial description of the Levi–Civita

connection by complex scalar invariants, the spin coefficients, which encode the geometry of the chosen
frame. In subsequent work [20, Newman and Penrose 1965], Newman and Penrose derived five complex,
equivalently ten real, constants of motion for Bondi-type solutions of the Einstein field equations. This
programme was further extended in [13, Exton et al. 1969] to Einstein–Maxwell spacetimes.
A refinement of the Newman–Penrose formalism was later developed by Geroch et al. [14, Geroch et
al. 1973]. Their approach keeps only a pair of null directions, together with spin- and boost-weighted
quantities and differential operators preserving these weights. This gives a more invariant version of the
spin-coefficient formalism, lying between the original Newman–Penrose formalism and a fully covariant
tensorial description.
The Newman–Penrose formalism has also been adapted to Riemannian three-manifolds. Aazami [1,
Aazami 2015] introduced the formalism in this setting, and Matsuno [18, Matsuno 2025] recently applied
it to 3-dimensional almost contact metric manifolds, characterizing several standard classes, including
contact, Sasakian, cosymplectic, Kenmotsu, trans-Sasakian, and (κ, µ)-contact metric manifolds. In the
original Lorentzian setting, the Newman–Penrose formalism is particularly well suited to the study of
shear-free geodesic congruences.
In dimension three, the same language is natural for almost contact
metric geometry: normal almost contact metric structures are characterized by the property that the
structure vector field generates a shear-free geodesic congruence. Thus trans-Sasakian geometry can be
studied effectively through the twist, expansion, and shear of this congruence.
It is well known that every orientable Riemannian 3-manifold admits almost contact metric structures
compatible with the given metric; see [8, Cabrerizo et al. 2009]. Hence, once g and an orientation are
fixed, compatible almost contact metric structures are abundant. The geometry becomes restrictive only
after imposing additional conditions, such as normality, the contact metric condition, the Sasakian or
trans-Sasakian equations, homogeneity, or compatibility with a prescribed fibration.
In this paper we study the trans-Sasakian condition for the homogeneous metrics of the spaces E(κ, τ).

By a trans-Sasakian structure compatible with a metric of type E(κ, τ), we mean an almost contact
metric structure (φ, ξ, η, g) satisfying the trans-Sasakian equation, where the metric g is fixed to be the
prescribed homogeneous metric. This is not the same as classifying homogeneous almost contact metric
or trans-Sasakian structures, where (φ, ξ, η, g) itself is assumed to be invariant under a chosen transitive
group action. Here no homogeneity assumption is imposed on φ, ξ, η, or on the functions α and β. The
problem is therefore a fixed-metric rigidity problem: given a homogeneous metric, determine whether any
compatible trans-Sasakian structure can occur, homogeneous or not.
Our main rigidity result is that, in the non-space-form E(κ, τ) cases, the trans-Sasakian equations force
the structure vector field to be the canonical vertical field. More precisely, if τ ̸= 0 and κ ̸= 4τ 2, then
every compatible trans-Sasakian structure is vertical and hence reduces to the canonical α-Sasakian case.
If τ = 0 and κ ̸= 0, corresponding to M 3 = M 2
κ × R, then every compatible trans-Sasakian structure is
again vertical and hence cosymplectic. In particular, no proper trans-Sasakian structures are compatible
with these fixed homogeneous metrics.
A second important theme of the paper is the precise equivalence, in dimension three, between compat-
ible trans-Sasakian structures, shear-free geodesic congruences, and conformal foliations by geodesics. We
express this equivalence in Newman–Penrose spin coefficients and thereby relate trans-Sasakian geometry
to the conformal foliations considered by Baird and Wood in the theory of harmonic morphisms. From
this viewpoint, the trans-Sasakian equation becomes a scalar system governing the congruence generated
by the structure vector field. This equivalence gives a practical advantage beyond terminology. The Baird–
Wood theory studies conformal foliations by geodesics through harmonic morphisms and quotient foliations,
whereas the Newman–Penrose formalism detects the same condition directly through spin coefficients: in
dimension three a unit vector field generates a conformal foliation by geodesics if and only if

σnp = 0.

Thus the existence problem becomes a system of local scalar differential equations attached to a congruence.
For the homogeneous metrics E(κ, τ), this makes the rigidity mechanism especially transparent: starting
from an arbitrary compatible structure, the Newman–Penrose equations themselves force the structure

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review trans-Sasakian geometry and the Newman–
Penrose formalism. In Section 3, we prove the equivalence between trans-Sasakian structures, shear-free
geodesic congruences, and conformal foliations by geodesics, and formulate it in Newman–Penrose notation.
In Section 4, we derive curvature formulae in the Newman–Penrose framework, including expressions for
the Ricci tensor, the scalar curvature, the Riemann curvature tensor, and the Einstein condition for trans-
Sasakian manifolds and their main subclasses, together with several illustrative examples. In Section 5,
we study the rough Laplacian and divergence of the structure vector field, including the conditions for
parallelity, harmonicity, pointwise collinearity with the rough Laplacian, and divergence-freeness. Finally,
in Section 6, we apply the formalism to the homogeneous metrics E(κ, τ) and prove the rigidity results
described above.

Trans-Sasakian structures, shear-free geodesic congruences, and
conformal foliations by geodesics
In dimension 3, the Newman–Penrose formalism admits a particularly geometric interpretation of the
trans-Sasakian condition. Once the metric and orientation are fixed, an almost contact metric structure is
completely determined by its structure vector field ξ. The covariant derivatives of ξ are then described by
the congruence formed by its integral curves, through the associated acceleration, shear, expansion, and
twist.

definitions, that in dimension 3 the following three conditions are equivalent:
(a) a compatible trans-Sasakian almost contact metric structure;
(b) a geodesic shear-free congruence generated by the structure vector field; and
(c) a conformal foliation by geodesics.

the trans-Sasakian condition.
Proposition 3.3 moreover reveals an unexpected bridge between trans-Sasakian geometry and the theory
of conformal foliations by geodesics developed by Baird and Wood in the context of harmonic morphisms.

an almost contact metric structure and a one-dimensional foliation, and Proposition 3.3 shows that
conformal and geodesic foliation

trans-Sasakian structure.
Thus the classification of conformal foliations by geodesics in 3-dimensional space forms in [2, Baird and

structures on those fixed metrics. The isolated singularities appearing in their work correspond, in our

Newman–Penrose computation for trans-Sasakian structures
on homogeneous metrics of type E(κ, τ)
The spaces E(κ, τ) form the standard two-parameter family of simply connected, oriented, homogeneous
Riemannian 3-manifolds with a 4-dimensional isometry group. Geometrically, E(κ, τ) is characterized by a
Riemannian submersion
π : E(κ, τ) −→M 2(κ),
where M 2(κ) is the simply connected surface of constant curvature κ, whose fibres are the integral curves of
a unit vertical Killing vector field ξ; the constant τ is the bundle curvature of this fibration. The condition
κ ̸= 4τ 2 gives the non-space-form members of the family. In particular, when τ = 0, one obtains the
product spaces S2(κ) × R and H2(κ) × R, while for τ ̸= 0, the family includes important examples such
as Heisenberg group Nil3, Berger spheres, and the universal cover of PSL2(R) (see [10, Daniel 2007]). The
purpose of this section is to present, in a completely explicit and verifiable form, the computation showing
how the Newman–Penrose formalism can be used on the homogeneous metrics of type E(κ, τ). The main
conclusions are:

What is striking in this argument is that the obstruction appears directly at the level of the Newman–
Penrose equations. The combination of the shear-free condition with a single Sachs equation immediately
detects the incompatibility between the Sol Ricci tensor and the trans-Sasakian equations. In particular,
the Newman–Penrose formalism turns the problem into a very short rigidity argument expressed entirely
through scalar quantities associated with the congruence.
Remark 6.9 The remaining space-form cases, which complete the list of Thurston geometries, are not
treated separately here, since they do not exhibit the rigidity phenomenon considered in this section. In the
family E(κ, τ), the round sphere corresponds to κ = 4τ 2; in this case the Ricci tensor is isotropic and the
Newman–Penrose equations no longer force the structure vector field to be vertical. Hyperbolic space H3
also falls outside the vertical-fibration mechanism used above. A Newman–Penrose study of these space-
form cases, and more generally of warped product geometries, would be natural: there the method would not
primarily give vertical rigidity, but rather an efficient way to analyse shear-free geodesic congruences and
the corresponding trans-Sasakian structures.
