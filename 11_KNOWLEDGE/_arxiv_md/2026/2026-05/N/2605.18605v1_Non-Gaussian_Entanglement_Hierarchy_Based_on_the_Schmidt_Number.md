Non-Gaussian entanglement is a promising resource in various quantum tasks. A recently defined class iden-
tifies entanglement that cannot be generated by applying Gaussian operations to separable inputs. To further
explore the entanglement in this context, we introduce a quantitative witness ENG in bipartite bosonic systems,
which satisfies ENG = 1 for all Gaussian-entanglable states, while ENG > 1 certifies non-Gaussian entanglement.
Its ceiling d = ⌈ENG⌉provides a lower bound on the Schmidt number irreducible by Gaussian transformations,
thereby defining a natural hierarchy of non-Gaussian entanglement. For pure states, the condition is sharp
and the hierarchy reflects the complexity of state learning. We benchmark the framework with some paradig-
matic non-Gaussian states, such as NOON states and squeezed Kerr states, and analyze its robustness against
loss. Moreover, we construct an experimentally economical NOON-type witness requiring only four projec-
tive measurements, where an analytical Gaussian-entanglable threshold is derived. These results establish an
operationally meaningful and experimentally accessible framework for identifying non-Gaussian entanglement
resources in continuous-variable quantum platforms.
Entanglement is a key resource for quantum communica-
tion, computation, sensing, and error correction [1–3].
In
bosonic systems, such as optical modes, microwave cavities,
and mechanical oscillators, entanglement takes on a particu-
larly rich structure, owing to the natural division of states and
operations into Gaussian and non-Gaussian classes [4–6]. Al-
though Gaussian operations can generate entanglement from
separable inputs, they are fundamentally limited in the type
and depth of entanglement they can create. Indeed, Gaussian
resources alone cannot achieve entanglement distillation [7–
9], loophole-free Bell inequality violations [10], or univer-
sal quantum computation [11, 12], making non-Gaussian en-
tanglement an indispensable ingredient for next-generation
bosonic quantum technologies.
Characterizing entanglement beyond the Gaussian regime
is substantially more intricate. For Gaussian states, the co-
variance matrix encodes all entanglement properties, and
well-established criteria, such as the Peres-Horodecki posi-
tive partial transpose (PPT) condition [13, 14], Simon’s cri-
terion [15], and the logarithmic negativity [16, 17], pro-
vide necessary and sufficient conditions for detection and
quantification.
For non-Gaussian states, however, second-
moment-based tools are generally incomplete: entanglement
may reside in higher-order correlations, requiring more pow-
erful strategies, such as witnesses involving higher-order mo-
ments [18, 19], phase-space criteria [20–23] or full state to-
mography [13, 14, 16].
In the finite-dimensional setting,
the Schmidt number [24, 25] provides a hierarchy of entan-
glement dimensionality with direct operational meaning for
quantum information tasks [26, 27]. Extending an analogous
quantitative hierarchy to non-Gaussian bosonic entanglement
has remained an open challenge.

In this Letter, we introduce a witness ENG for non-Gaussian
entanglement characterization. This quantity generalizes the
standard negativity-based entanglement witness [16] to the
non-Gaussian setting. It satisfies ENG = 1 for all GE states,
while ENG > 1 certifies non-Gaussian entanglement. Its ceil-
ing d B ⌈ENG⌉witnesses the minimum Schmidt number that
remains after Gaussian transformations, thereby inducing a hi-
erarchy of non-Gaussian entanglement dimensions. For pure
states, the criterion is sharp and the hierarchy is connected to
state-learning complexity. We benchmark our framework on
some paradigmatic non-Gaussian states, and further evaluate
its robustness against loss. Finally, to circumvent the cost of
full state tomography, we derive a practical witness tailored
to NOON states requiring only four projective measurements,
with an analytical threshold achievable by any GE state.

Mixed states.– In realistic scenarios, inevitable experimen-

examine the robustness of our criteria in the presence of noise.
The Hamiltonian Eq. (13) can be realized in state-of-the-art

As time accumulates, E and ENG increase and

As expected, loss reduces both entanglement witnesses. For

Application to entanglement distillation.– It is known that
Gaussian entangled states cannot be distilled in the Gaussian
settings [52]. We show that the NGE states detected by ENG >
1 provides a promising resource in entanglement distillation.
We consider a two-copy distillation protocol as illustrated in
SM [39] Sec. II, and use the lossy two-mode squeezed Kerr
states ρ as the input. With appropriate choices of Gaussian
unitaries and homodyne measurements, the entanglement dis-
tillation can be successfully achieved, where the final state ρd
exhibits enhanced entanglement than the input, E(ρd) > E(ρ).
In Fig. 3, we plot the distilled entanglement E(ρd) (blue) as a
function of time, and observe E(ρd) > E(ρ) within the whole
time period considered. For short times (t < 0.4), the non-
Gaussian entanglement ENG(ρ) is quite small, so that the cor-
responding distillation gain E(ρd) is low. For larger times, a
significant enhancement is shown when the non-Gaussian en-
tanglement becomes more pronounced.

Conclusions.– In this work, we develop witnesses for
the certification and characterization of Gaussian-entanglable
(GE), non-Gaussian entangled (NGE), and genuinely non-
Gaussian entangled (GNGE) states in bipartite continuous-
variable systems.
Our main witness of certifying NGE is
based on the partial transpose of the density matrix, mini-
mized over all two-mode Gaussian unitaries. This quantity
provides a lower bound on the Schmidt number irreducible
by Gaussian transformations, thus defines a natural hierarchy
of witnessing non-Gaussian entanglement dimensions. For
pure states, the condition becomes sharp for certifying GNGE
states, and the hierarchy is connected to the complexity of
the state learning. We have benchmarked the framework on
paradigmatic non-Gaussian states, and examined its robust-
ness against loss. To simplify the experimental implementa-
tion, we further complemented our criteria with a practical
witness tailored to NOON states, where only four density-
matrix elements are required for detection.
Our results extend the Schmidt-number paradigm to the CV
non-Gaussian entanglement regime and provide a practical
route to identifying entanglement resources. As an example,
we have shown that the certified non-Gaussian entanglement
in squeezed-Kerr states are effective resource for entangle-
ment distillation, illustrating the witnessed non-Gaussian en-
tanglement have promising applications. More broadly, Non-
Gaussian entangled states are emerging candidates in vari-
ous quantum information fields, such as quantum metrology,
quantum computing and quantum communication. Our work
opens new avenues for entanglement characterization and for
identifying resources that are essential for quantum technolo-
gies.
