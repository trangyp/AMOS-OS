NON-EXTERNAL PROOFS OF LAGRANGE INVERSION FORMULA
DOMINIK BECK∗AND PIOTR MA´CKOWIAK
Abstract. The goal of the paper is to present two simple proofs of the Lagrange Inversion Formula
for formal power series. Both proofs are non-external in the sense that they use concepts that do
not go beyond the scope of formal power series analysis, e.g. we do not refer to the notion of formal
Laurent series while proving the formula.
1. Introduction
In a recent paper of Surya and Warnke [5], a simple inductive proof of the Lagrange Inversion
Formula (LIF) is presented. In that proof the first step of induction (n = 0) is implicitly based
on a simple property of formal Laurent series - so it uses a more general notion than the notion
of a formal power series. This motivated us to write relatively simple proofs of LIF without going
beyond the scope of formal power series calculus. The aforementioned paper [5] contains a very nice
introduction to the Lagrange Inversion Formula, basic definitions and properties of formal power
series calculus so we refer the reader to that paper for applications, motivation and literature on
LIF.
We are going to prove the following theorem which is an equivalent formulation of Theorem 1
from [5].

that this is somewhat excessive (see [5] for a discussion on this).

2. Notation, basic facts and assumptions
Let N denote the set of positive integers and set N0 := N ∪{0}. For a formal power series f(x),
its n-th coefficient is fn or [xn]f(x), n ∈N0.
The product of two formal power series f(x) and g(x) is the formal power series h(x) such that

paper we consider formal power series with coefficients in an integral domain K (e.g. K ∈{Q, R, C});
this assumption guarantees that the product is associative and commutative and for any pair of
formal power series f(x) and g(x) their product is zero formal power series θ (every coefficient of θ
equals 0 ∈K) if and only if either g(x) = θ or f(x) = θ. The order of a non-zero formal power series
f(x) is the number ordf := min{n ∈N0 : fn ̸= 0}; ordθ := +∞. It is known that the order of the
product f(x)g(x) equals the sum of orders of f and g.
For any l ∈N0, xl denotes a formal power series whose all coefficients are 0, except the coefficient
l which is 1 ∈K. The product of k copies of a formal power series f(x) (k-th power of f) is denoted
by f k(x), k ∈N; f 0(x) := x0. A formal power series g(x) possesses multiplicative inverse, that is,
there exists a formal power series f(x) such that f(x)g(x) = x0 if and only if g0 ̸= 0. Any formal
power series f(x) with f0 = 0 and f1 ̸= 0 is called almost unit. Any almost unit formal power
series f(x) possesses its compositional inverse ¯f(x) (which is also almost unit), that is, it holds
f( ¯f(x)) = ¯f(f(x)) = x. Here by g(f(x)) or, equivalently, (g ◦f)(x), we mean the composition of a
formal power series g(x) with a formal power series f(x), where f(x) is a nonunit, f0 = 0, that is, a
formal power series (g◦f)(x) which is defined by [xn](g◦f)(x) := P∞

The derivative of a formal power series f(x) is f ′(x) (or, equivalently, (f(x))′) defined by
[xn]f ′(x) := (n + 1)[xn+1]f(x), n ∈N0. In our proofs we shall use the following properties of
formal power series calculus. Let f(x) and g(x) be formal power series. Then, for any nonunit
formal power series h(x),
(1) product rule: (fg)′(x) = f ′(x)g(x) + f(x)g′(x),
(2) power rule: (f k+1)′(x) = (k + 1)f k(x)f ′(x), k ∈N0,
(3) chain rule: (f ◦h)′(x) = (f ′ ◦h)(x)h′(x) ,
(4) term-by-term differentiation: [xn](f ◦h)′(x) = [xn](f0(h0)′(x) + f1(h1)′(x) + f2(h2)′(x) + . . .),
n ∈N0,
(5) right distributive law: ((fg) ◦h)(x) = (f ◦h)(x)(g ◦h)(x).
The notions and properties of formal power series presented in this section can be found in the
classic work [3] or in [2, Chapter 1] or in a recent book [1].

3.2. Calculus of formal power series approach to LIF. The below proof uses the right
distributive law, basic formulas of formal power series calculus and properties of order of a formal
power series we introduced in section 2 of the paper. However, it also needs a bit technical (though
simple) lemma.
Lemma 3.1. Let g = P∞
n=0 gnxn be a multiplicatively invertible formal power series, that is, g0 ̸= 0.
For every j, s ∈N0, it holds
[xs](xjg−s+j(x) + xj+1g−s+(j−1)(x)g′(x)) = δj,s,
where δj,s denotes the Kronecker delta.
Proof. Since the order of the formal power series xjg−s+j(x) is j and the order of xj+1g−s+(j−1)(x)g′(x)
is not less than j + 1 (it may be +∞), the claim is true for j ≥s.
Suppose that j < s. We have [xs−j−1](g−s+j)′(x) = (s −j)[xs−j]g−s+j(x) (by definition of the
formal derivative) and, at the same time, [xs−j−1](g−s+j)′(x) = (−s + j)[xs−j−1](g−s+j−1(x)g′(x))
(by power rule).
Hence, [xs−j]g−s+j(x) + [xs−j−1](g−s+j−1(x)g′(x)) = 0 which is equivalent to
[xs](xjg−s+j(x) + xj+1g−s+j−1(x)g′(x)) = 0. The lemma is proved.
