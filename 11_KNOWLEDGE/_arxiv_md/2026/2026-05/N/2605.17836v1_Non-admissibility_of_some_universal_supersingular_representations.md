NON-ADMISSIBILITY OF SOME UNIVERSAL SUPERSINGULAR REPRESENTATIONS
ZACHARY FENG, HEEJONG LEE, RAY LI, VAUGHAN MCDONALD, AND NISCHAY REDDY
ABSTRACT. Let K/Qp be an unramified extension of degree f with residue field k. Let σ be an irreducible representation
of GLn(k) over Fp. For n ≥3, we prove that the universal supersingular representation of weight σ is non-admissible
and of infinite length when σ is sufficiently generic and satisfies certain technical conditions. This generalizes the previous
results for n = 2 and a non-trivial finite extension K/Qp. Our method employs a weight cycling argument together with
recent progress on the Serre weight conjectures.
1. INTRODUCTION
1.1. Background. The mod p local Langlands correspondence for GL2(Qp) gives a tight connection between 2-
dimensional Galois representations of Gal(Qp/Qp) over Fp and admissible smooth representations of GL2(Qp)
over Fp (see [Col10, Paˇs13]). It is desirable to extend such a correspondence to GLn(K) where K/Qp is a finite
extension. When either n ≥3 or K ̸= Qp, mod p representations of GLn(K) exhibit surprisingly complicated
behaviors compared to the case of GL2(Qp). To explain such behaviors, we introduce the notion of supersingular
representations.
Let G := GLn(K), K := GLn(OK), and Z be the center of G. For an irreducible Fp-representation σ of KZ, we
consider the universal supersingular representation of weight σ
SS(σ) := c-IndG
KZ σ/(T1, . . . , Tn−1)
where Ti is the Hecke operator of σ associated to the ith fundamental cocharacter. Then a supersingular representation
of G is equivalent to an irreducible admissible quotient of SS(σ) for some σ1. [AHHV17] classified irreducible
admissible mod p representations of p-adic reductive groups in terms of supersingular representations, analogous to
the case of complex representations. However, the classification of supersingular representations remains mysterious.
Let n = 2. For K = Qp, [Bre03] showed that SS(σ) is already irreducible and admissible and thus classified
supersingular representations of GL2(Qp). In stark contrast, when K/Qp is a non-trivial unramified extension, [BP12]
showed that SS(σ) admits infinitely many non-isomorphic irreducible quotients under a mild assumption on σ. [Sch14,
Hen19] showed that SS(σ) is non-admissible if and only if K ̸= Qp. Moreover, [Sch15, Wu21] showed that any
irreducible quotient π of SS(σ) is not of finite presentation when n = 2 and K ̸= Qp, meaning that the kernel of
SS(σ) ↠π is not finitely generated as a Fp[G]-module. They also proved the non-admissibility of SS(σ). In a
different direction, [Le25] showed there exists an irreducible smooth mod p representation of GLn(K) that is non-
admissible and does not have a central character for n ≥2 and K whose residue field is a non-trivial extension of Fp
(see also, [Le19, GS20, GLS23]).

The methods in previous works are restricted to GL2. [BP12] extensively used the theory of diagrams (first in-
troduced in [Pas04]) which is currently not available for groups other than GL2. [Sch14, Hen19] proved the non-
admissibility of SS(σ) by explicitly computing the pro-p Iwahori invariant subspace of SS(σ) for GL2 (for K/Qp
unramified, this was first observed by Breuil; see [Bre03, Remarque 4.2.6]). [Sch15, Wu21] used a similar argument
and a result of Hu [Hu12] relying on diagrams. [GLS23, Le25], which studied different problems, also used the theory
of diagrams to prove the result for n = 2 and extended it to general n using parabolic induction. This is why the
residue field is assumed to be a non-trivial extension of Fp in loc. cit.
Our method is distinct from the previous ones in the sense that:
(1) we employ a weight cycling argument which applies to any split reductive group over any finite extension
K/Qp, and
(2) we make use of the moduli space of mod p local Galois representations (the reduced part of the Emerton–Gee
stack [EG23]) and the connection between mod p local Galois representations and mod p representations of
p-adic groups given by globalization and the space of mod p algebraic automorphic forms.
Even though the proof of Theorem 5.1 works only when n ≥3, a similar argument can prove the non-admissibility
and the infinite length property of SS(σ) for n = 2 and f ≥2. This is explained in Remark 5.3, which gives a concise
illustration of our method. We view our method as a showcase for how Galois representations can guide the mod p
representation theory of p-adic groups.
We explain our method in more detail. The weight cycling argument was first discovered by Buzzard for GL2(Qp).
We use its most general form due to [EGH13]. In fact, we reinterpret their result in the following form (Proposition

What remains is to find (a family of) ρ satisfying the above conditions. Let Xn,red be the reduced Emerton–Gee
stack constructed in [EG23]. It can be viewed as the moduli space of n-dimensional mod p representations of GK. It is
known that there is a bijection between irreducible representations of GLn(k) and irreducible components in Xn,red.
[LLHM+24] studied explicit local charts of certain subspaces in Xn,red. The regular colength one local charts in
loc. cit. have two irreducible components corresponding to σ and σ′. Then ρ in the intersection of the two components
has σ and σ′ as its modular weights (Proposition 4.4). For the ordinarity of ρ with respect to σ′, we further intersect
with the ordinary locus in the component corresponding to σ′. Showing the non-emptiness of this triple intersection is
the most technical part of our work (Proposition 3.14). Using local model theory developed in [LLHLM23], we reduce
this to computing intersections of certain translated affine Schubert cells. Then the specialness of the p-alcove ensures
that our ρ is supersingular with respect to σ. Our result also shows that the supersingular locus in the irreducible
component for σ is of codimension one when the (p-restricted) highest weight of σ belongs to a special p-alcove
(Corollary 3.28). This exhibits a striking contrast compared to the lowest alcove case.
