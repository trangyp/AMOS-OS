Abstract
Let f(t, x), M(t, x) ∈C([0, 1]2) with M(t, x) > 0. We consider differential equations of the form
∂f
∂t (t, x) = M(t, x)f(t, x) −M(t, 0)f(t, 0)
x

x > 0.
For a fixed positive weight M, we ask whether the condition f(0, x) = 0 forces f ≡0. We show the answer
is negative for smooth functions: there exist f(t, x), M(t, x) ∈C∞([0, 1]2) with f(0, x) = 0, f(t, 0) ̸≡0,
and M(t, x) > 0 satisfying the above equation. However, we show that for a large class of M(t, x), the
equation does have uniqueness. We relate this to uniqueness/non-uniqueness theorems for weighted
Laplace transforms. A key example originated in an output by ChatGPT-5.5-Pro, and we include a
discussion of its output as well as a complete proof.

We were originally motivated to study differential equations of the form (1.6) by the famous anisotropic
Calder´on problem [Cal80, LU01]: does the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map on a compact Riemannian manifold
with boundary, of dimension ≥3, uniquely determine the Riemannian manifold structure (up to the natural
boundary-fixing diffeomorphism obstruction)? There has been a considerable amount of work on this problem,
and we do not attempt any sort of history here; see [Uhl09, Uhl14] for surveys.
Roughly speaking, the connection to the Calder´on problem is the following. Consider a smooth, Riemannian
manifold with boundary, M, and let t denote boundary normal coordinates near the boundary. On a
neighborhood of the boundary, this decomposes the manifold into layers, one for each small t ≥0, which
are diffeomorphic to ∂M, each of which inherit a metric g(t) and Laplace-Beltrami operator △g(t). Lee and
Uhlmann [LU89] showed that the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map is the initial condition for a differential equation
similar to the following equation:
∂
∂tΛ(t) = Λ(t)2 −△g(t),
(1.7)
where Λ(t) is a pseudodifferential operator of order 1 on ∂M, depending smoothly on t (see [Str17, (9.1)] for
the precise differential equation which arises, which is a bit more complicated). If this differential equation
had uniqueness, then the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map would uniquely determine the metric near the boundary.
In fact, [LU89] use this idea in the real analytic setting, where the differential equations automatically have
uniqueness. Because the left-hand side of (1.7) is a pseudodifferential operator of order 1, and the right-hand
side is a pseudodifferential operator of order 2, the principal symbols on the right-hand side must cancel.
This shows that the principal symbol of △g(t) is the square of the principal symbol of Λ(t). Thinking of the
principal symbol as the limit of the symbol as some parameter goes to 0, this sees (1.7) as a non-commutative
analog of (1.6). See [Str17, Section 9] for a more detailed discussion and more references. We do not know if
the methods of this paper have any application to the Calder´on problem.
