Non-Parametric Equation of State Reveals Non-Conformal Behavior Beyond Neutron Star Densities
Yong-Jia Huang,1, 2, ∗Shao-Peng Tang,1 and Yi-Zhong Fan1, 3
1Key Laboratory of Dark Matter and Space Astronomy,

3School of Astronomy and Space Sciences, University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei, Anhui 230026, China.
We propose a non-parametric approach to construct the statistical equation of state (EOS) continuously from
the nuclear crust to the asymptotic-freedom regime. Driven by the observationally required stiffening to support
two-solar-mass neutron stars (NSs) with relatively small radii for low-mass NSs, this global thermodynamic con-
straint suggests a clear peak of squared sound speed (c2
s) in massive NSs. To prevent overshooting perturbative
QCD (pQCD) energy-density bounds, this early stiffening must be actively compensated by an extended density
range of softening, with c2
s not approaching 1/3 until ∼30 nsat. Consistently, the trace anomaly ∆≡1/3 −p/ϵ
becomes positive beyond NS densities and approaches the pQCD limit from above. This natural emergence
of ∆> 0 organically aligns with some anticipated microphysics, likely arising from the pressure dilution in
a quark-hadron mixed phase, non-conformal pQCD corrections to quark-gluon interactions, or the symmetry-
breaking effects of finite strange quark mass. By measuring the degree of this non-monotonic behavior in the
posterior, we find evidence for a hadron-quark phase transition in the cores of the most massive neutron stars.
This indicates that the non-perturbative quark matter is intrinsically soft, fundamentally distinguishing it from
the stiff scenarios associated with the quark-star picture.
Introduction. Nuclear interactions at densities far above sat-
uration are difficult to probe in terrestrial experiments, but
such extreme conditions occur naturally in the Universe in
neutron stars. Observations of neutron stars constrain their
global structure and, in turn, the thermodynamic properties
of cold, dense matter in their interiors, commonly encoded in
the equation of state (EOS), i.e., the relation between pres-
sure p and energy density ϵ. In the past few years, multi-
messenger measurements—most notably the tidal deformabil-
ity inferred from the binary-neutron-star merger GW170817
[1, 2] and mass–radius constraints [3–7] from X-ray pulse-
profile modeling by the Neutron Star Interior Composition
Explorer (NICER)—have substantially improved the accuracy
with which neutron-star properties can be determined.
At supranuclear densities, additional degrees of freedom
(e.g., hyperons or deconfined quarks) may appear [8, 9].
These unknown degrees of freedom, together with uncertain-
ties in dense-matter many-body dynamics, limit the reliability
of purely ab initio extrapolations. As a result, microphysical
inferences from NS observations are often drawn within pa-
rameterized EOS families or specific phenomenological mod-
els [10–12] and remain conditional on modeling assumptions.
Motivated by this limitation, non-parametric EOS inference
[13–16], has been developed to provide flexible EOS rep-
resentations. These approaches combine astrophysical con-
straints with robust low-density nuclear input from chiral ef-
fective field theory (χEFT) [17, 18] and with high-density in-
formation informed by perturbative QCD (pQCD) [19, 20],
thereby bounding the set of admissible EOSs [21–24].
Asymptotic freedom makes pQCD a rigorous constraint
on the cold-matter EOS, but this perturbative regime lies far
above typical NS densities. Standard non-parametric meth-
ods, such as Gaussian Processes (GPs)[13, 24], often struggle

to connect these widely separated density regimes. In par-

suppress the sustained structural change needed to connect a
stiff intermediate-density EOS to the near-conformal pQCD
limit. To incorporate high-density information, recent stud-
ies have extrapolated the pQCD constraints down to lower
densities by enforcing causality and thermodynamic stability
[20]. However, this has led to a debate regarding the den-
sity of pQCD implementation [25–27]. The inferred proper-
ties of the NS core—such as whether the EOS systematically
soften—depend sensitively on the density at which the pQCD
constraint is imposed [28].
In this work, we remove this ambiguity by constructing a
non-parametric EOS ensemble that extends continuously from
the nuclear crust to the pQCD limit at µB = 2.6 GeV. To over-
come the limitations of standard GP priors, we introduce an
action-optimized intermediate segment: candidate c2
s(n) paths
are assigned an action based on their mismatch from a linear
interpolation between the endpoint states, and the path min-
imizing this action is selected from several physically moti-
vated classes and then projected onto the exact endpoint con-
straints via the GP covariance. This yields a continuous EOS
from the crust to the pQCD anchor and allows the impact of
the asymptotic QCD boundary on neutron-star interiors to be
determined without introducing an arbitrary implementation
density.
Methods.

of magnitude, connecting strongly coupled hadronic matter

boundary-value problem. Achieving an unbiased prior that
exactly matches both the pressure P and the energy density ϵ
at the high-density limit is mathematically challenging. Since
the thermodynamic integration follows the differential rela-
tion ∂nϵ = (ϵ + P)/n, even small variations in the squared

In traditional non-parametric constructions, connecting to
pQCD relies on the integral constraints proposed by Komolt-
sev and Kurkela [20]. To evaluate the viability of a gener-
ated EOS, this approach extrapolates the strict thermodynamic
conditions backwards from high densities down to a chosen
intermediate matching density. If the matching is imposed
only near the nTOV, the EOS inside the observable massive
star can remain comparatively stiff, while the EOS becomes
extremely soft at higher densities [26]. In our framework,
the posterior EOS is evaluated as a global continuous trajec-
tory up to µB = 2.6 GeV without introducing an artificial im-
plementation interface. Our approach confirms that the non-
monotonic structure of c2
s truly manifests within the cores of
the most massive NSs.
The physical origin of this non-monotonic behavior is fun-
damentally driven by the constraints of the global boundary-
value problem. At low density, the trace anomaly is initially
positive because hadrons are massive and nuclear interactions
are attractive. As density increases, astrophysical observa-
tions (M ≥2 M⊙) demand a prominent early stiffening of
the EOS to prevent gravitational collapse, inherently forcing
∆to become negative (c2
s > 1/3). However, the thermody-
namic relation ∂nϵ = (ϵ+p)/n dictates that this early stiffening
rapidly accumulates energy density. To successfully reach the
exact pQCD boundary (ppQCD, ϵpQCD) without systematically
overshooting the permissible energy-density limit, this early
sound-speed peak must be actively compensated by a signifi-
cant subsequent softening (see also Appendix A.3).
This thermodynamically required softening naturally drives
the trace anomaly back to positive values (∆> 0) at higher
densities. This macroscopic structural requirement perfectly
uncovers the microscopic QCD physics: at sufficiently high
chemical potentials (e.g., µB around 2.6 GeV), residual gluon-
exchange interactions characterized by the strong coupling αs
provide a negative interaction correction to the pressure (p <
ϵ/3) [43, 44]. This mandates that the system must approach
absolute asymptotic freedom (∆→0+) strictly from above
[19, 45]. Our data-driven inference thus naturally recovers
this highly non-trivial behavior, confirming that the peak-to-
soften structure in c2
s is an intrinsic thermodynamic necessity.

at high densities.

Summary and discussion. In this work, we establish a non-
parametric framework that continuously connects the low-
density nuclear EOS to the asymptotic pQCD limit and our
global inference resolves the ambiguity regarding pQCD im-
plementation. We demonstrate that a non-monotonic sound-
speed profile—peaking well above the conformal limit and
subsequently softening—naturally emerges and manifests di-
rectly within the interiors of massive NSs.
We introduced
a novel integrated softening index to distinguish a hadron-
quark transition from the softening induced merely by ex-
otic hadronic degrees of freedom. Measured against purely
hadronic models, our posterior confirms the presence of a
hadron-quark transition inside massive neutron stars.
Crucially, the global structural evolution we infer highlights
a pronounced and extended softening regime following the
intermediate-density c2
s peak. This extended softening indi-
cates that the non-perturbative quark EOS within the NS pic-
ture is inherently soft.
Furthermore, our global thermody-
namic boundary-value inference reveals an intrinsic correla-
tion: accommodating a larger MTOV necessitates a more ex-
treme early stiffening, which in turn inevitably forces the high-
density quark EOS to become even softer to safely converge
with the asymptotic pQCD limits without overshooting the
energy-density bounds (See Appendix C for the discussion).
This required stiffening-then-softening construction provides
a clear phenomenological distinction from the conventional
quark-star picture[55], which conversely relies on an intrinsi-
cally stiff quark EOS to support massive compact objects.
The extended softening beyond the NS density might arise
from several different microphysics, such as the pressure di-
lution in a extended quark-hadron mixed phase [33], non-
conformal pQCD corrections to quark-gluon interactions [56],
or the symmetry-breaking effects of finite strange quark mass
[57, 58]. Disentangling these possibilities is difficult with only
cold, isolated neutron stars. This strongly motivates extend-
ing the analysis to finite temperatures, as the thermal proper-
ties of dense matter can directly probe the underlying QCD
phase structure.
Because cold EOSs originating from dif-
ferent physical backgrounds are expected to exhibit distinct
finite-temperature behaviors, numerical simulations of binary
neutron star mergers [59–67] provide a critical opportunity to
differentiate them. The extreme thermodynamic conditions
reached during these highly dynamical events could expose
distinct multi-messenger signatures, ultimately allowing us to
identify the specific microphysics driving the transition to de-
confined quark matter.

3.
How constraints shape the structure of c2
s
The inclusion of high-density pQCD limits imposes strin-
gent restrictions on the EOS. While it is mathematically
straightforward to construct arbitrary continuous paths in P–n
space that connect the pressure between hadronic and pQCD
segment, these paths can lead to vastly different accumulated
energy densities ϵ.
To illustrate this systematically in Fig. 5, we construct test
pressure trajectories by using the same sample as in Fig. 1 us-
ing a modified cubic Hermite spline (for simplicity, we main-
tain the continuity of c2
s here, but not that of its first deriva-
tive.):
P(n) = PHermite(n) + A · B(x),
(A24)
where x = (n −n1)/(n2 −n1) is the normalized density, n1
and n2 represent the boundary density for hadronic and pQCD
segment. PHermite(n) is the standard cubic Hermite interpola-
tion constrained by the boundary pressures and c2
s. The shape
function B(x) = x2(1 −x)2 vanishes at the endpoints together
with its first derivative. By tuning the amplitude A, we gener-
ate three representative paths that exactly match the P bound-
aries:
• Hermite path (A = 0, green dashed): A standard in-
terpolation.
• Soft-then-stiff path (A < 0, blue): The EOS is initially
softer than the standard interpolation, requiring a rapid
stiffening near the high-density end.
• Stiff-then-soft path (A > 0, red): The EOS exhibits
pronounced early stiffening, followed by a much softer
state to meet the final pressure target.
As dictated by the thermodynamic relation dϵ/dn = (ϵ +
P)/n, these three paths yield drastically different energy-
density accumulations even though they share the same final
pressure.
To accommodate the existence of massive pulsars (M ≥
2.0 M⊙), astrophysical constraints demand that the EOS un-
dergo prominent early stiffening, pushing its behavior toward
the stiff-then-soft (A > 0) trajectory. However, this early stiff-
ening also builds up ϵ very rapidly. If it persisted to higher
densities, ϵ would systematically overshoot the tight pQCD
bound.
Therefore, in order to simultaneously provide structural
support for massive neutron stars and land precisely on the
coupled (PpQCD, ϵpQCD) point, any early stiffening in c2
s must
be followed by a significantly softer state. This subsequent
softening is a thermodynamic necessity that slows the accu-
mulation of ϵ. The non-monotonic structure of c2
s is not a
mathematical artifact of a non-parametric method, but a fun-
damental global requirement that emerges when macroscopic
astrophysical constraints are unified with microscopic pQCD
limits.
