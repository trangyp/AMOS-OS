1. Introduction
The study of volumes of three-dimensional hyperbolic manifolds and hyperbolic polyhedra is an
important direction in the development of modern geometry. It is of interest to describe sets of
volumes and their properties for various classes of polyhedra, see, for example, [8, 15]. On the one
hand, one can fix the combinatorial structure of a polyhedron and ask for which dihedral angles
the polyhedron (with finite or ideal vertices) has maximal volume. For example, by the well-known
result of Milnor [25], the greatest volume of a hyperbolic 3-simplex is attained on the regular ideal
3-simplex, that is, on such a simplex whose all vertices are ideal and all dihedral angles are equal
to each other, and hence are equal to π/3. An analogous result also holds in the n-dimensional
case. As shown in [19], the greatest volume of a hyperbolic n-simplex is attained on the regular
ideal n-simplex. On the other hand, one can fix the dihedral angles and ask which polyhedron
has maximal volume for a given number of faces or vertices. A hyperbolic polyhedron is called
right-angled if all its dihedral angles are equal to π/2. For the construction of three-dimensional
hyperbolic manifolds corresponding to torsion-free subgroups in reflection groups of right-angled
polyhedra, see [34]. The aim of this paper is to study volumes of right-angled polyhedra normalized
by the number of vertices of the polyhedra.

Let us note the connection of the obtained results with the theory of hyperbolic knots and links.
For a hyperbolic link L ⊂S3, consider the volume of its complement endowed with the complete
metric of constant negative curvature and denote it by vol(L). Let c(L) be the minimal number of
crossings among all diagrams of the link L. In [14], Champanerkar, Kofman, and Purcell defined
the volume density of a link as follows: vd(L) = vol(L)/c(L). It is known that vd(L) ≤voct. A
sequence of links Ln such that c(Ln) →∞is called geometrically maximal if limn→∞vd(Ln) = voct.
Examples of geometrically maximal sequences of links were constructed in [14].
Subsequently, Burton [12] showed that the set of volume densities of all hyperbolic links is
everywhere dense in the interval [0, voct]. Adams, Calderon, and Mayer showed in [1] that the set of
volume densities of hyperbolic links in thickened surfaces Sg × I, where g ≥2, is everywhere dense
in the interval [0, 2voct].
In [23], Kwon and Tham studied a special class of hyperbolic links, which are called fully aug-
mented links (or FAL for short). For them, the natural measure of complexity is the number of
augmentations (they are also called vertical components of the link). For this class, Kwon and
Tham defined the volume density as the ratio of the volume of the link complement to the number
of its vertical components and showed that the set of densities lies in the interval [voct, 10vtet], with
both bounds sharp. In addition, they established that the spectrum is discrete in [voct, 2voct) and
everywhere dense in [2voct, 10vtet].

There is a natural geometric connection between fully augmented links and ideal right-angled
polyhedra: the complement of each such link decomposes into two mutually isometric ideal right-
angled polyhedra [24, 28, 36] (for an analogous decomposition of complements of links in the thick-
ened torus, see [22]). At the same time, the volume of the link is equal to twice the volume of
the polyhedron, and the number of vertical components of the link is related to the number of
vertices of the polyhedron. Consequently, using a suitable normalization, the spectrum of volume
densities of links can be embedded in the spectrum of normalized volumes of polyhedra. However,
the class of ideal right-angled polyhedra is substantially broader than the subclass of polyhedra
arising from links, and therefore the question of describing the volume spectrum of the class of all
ideal right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra naturally arises.
The paper has the following structure. Section 2 gives the main definitions and properties of
ideal right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra. Section 3 is devoted to compact right-angled hyperbolic
polyhedra, in particular, to L¨obell polyhedra. Sections 4 and 5 contain the proofs of Theorem 1.1
and Theorem 1.2, respectively. Section 6 compares the obtained results for normalized volumes of
ideal and compact right-angled polyhedra with the results of Kwon and Tham on the spectrum
of volume density of augmented links. In conclusion we formulate some open questions and one
conjecture.
2. Ideal right-angled polyhedra
By an abstract polyhedron we mean such a cell complex on S2 that can be realized as a convex
polyhedron in three-dimensional Euclidean space. According to the classical theorem of Steinitz,
see, for example, [27, Ch. 1], realizability of a cell complex as a convex Euclidean polyhedron is
equivalent to its one-dimensional skeleton being a 3-connected graph without loops and multiple
edges. Recall that a graph is called k-connected if it contains at least k + 1 vertices and any two
of its distinct vertices can be joined by at least k independent paths. By the Menger-Whitney
theorem, a graph containing at least k + 1 vertices is k-connected if and only if, after deleting any
of its k −1 vertices (and the edges incident to them), the resulting graph is connected. Denote
by E(P) the set of edges of an abstract polyhedron P. A labeling of an abstract graph P is a
map E : E(P) →(0, π/2]. The pair (P, E) will be called a labeled abstract polyhedron. We will say
that a labeled abstract polyhedron is realized as a hyperbolic polyhedron if there is a polyhedron
P in hyperbolic space H3 such that there exists a label-preserving graph isomorphism sending the
one-dimensional skeleton P(1), with its edges labeled by dihedral angles, to the labeled abstract
polyhedron (P, E). Below we will consider such polyhedra in H3 for which all dihedral angles are
equal to π/2, and we will call them right-angled. A vertex of a polyhedron in H3 is called ideal if
it lies on the absolute ∂H3. For more details on polyhedra in the space H3, see [3].
Let G be a plane graph and let G∗be the graph dual to G. A simple closed curve consisting of
k ≥3 edges in G∗will be called a k-circuit. A prismatic k-circuit will mean such a k-circuit γ that
no two edges of the graph G crossing γ have a common vertex.
Necessary and sufficient conditions for the realization in H3 of a labeled abstract polyhedron of
finite volume were obtained in the works of Andreev [5, 6]; see also [29]. We will give the result
for the special case of right-angled polyhedra following [7]. In this case the necessary and sufficient
conditions are purely combinatorial.
Theorem 2.1. A right-angled abstract polyhedron (P, E) is realized as a hyperbolic polyhedron,
which we denote by P, if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:
(1) P has at least six faces;
