NON-COMBINATORIAL INVOLUTIVE BRAIDINGS: THE QUANTUM
ALGEBRA glk,m
ANASTASIA DOIKOU
Abstract. We investigate involutive, non-combinatorial solutions of the braid equation viewed as
special deformations of the permutation map. By employing these solutions, we identify the associ-
ated quantum algebra, which we introduce as the glk,m Yangian. The algebra glk,m is also recognized
as a subalgebra of the Yangian. Furthermore, we construct specific highest-weight modules of glk,m,
which simultaneously yield the eigenstates of certain quantum spin-chain-like Hamiltonians. In the
special case of the algebra gl1,1 the spin chain Hamiltonian reduces to a variant of the Heisenberg XX
model. Furthermore, we present a comprehensive analysis of combinatorial bases of highest weight
representations of gl1,1, explicitly linking them to specific shapes of Young tableaux.
1. Introduction
Involutive, combinatorial solutions to both the braid and Yang-Baxter equations [1, 27] have been
widely studied (see, for example, [10, 13, 14, 24, 25]), especially since Drinfel’d proposed the set-
theoretic version [9] of the Yang-Baxter equation. In the present investigation however we focus
on a specific class of non-combinatorial involutive solutions of the braid equation (see also [19])
and derive the associated quantum algebras [8, 15] using the Faddeev-Reshetikhin-Takhtajan (FRT)
construction [11]. Rigorous definitions distinguishing combinatorial and non-combinatorial solutions
are provided later in Definition 1.1. Associated with this specific class of non-combinatorial solutions
is a quantum algebra (or Yang–Baxter algebra) called the glk,m Yangian, denoted by Y (glk,m).
This Yangian admits glk,m as a subalgebra, which itself is a newly derived algebra. The primary
novelty of our approach lies in treating both the braid equation solution and its associated quantum
algebra as special, non-parametric deformations of the permutation map and the glk+m Yangian
[23], respectively. These constitute primary findings in this study and are formulated in Theorem
2.3, Proposition 2.8.
Furthermore, centralizers of the braid group are identified as coproducts of representations of the
algebra glk,m, allowing us to construct distinct combinatorial bases for the irreducible representations
of the associated quantum algebra. These bases also provide eigenstates for a quantum spin-chain-
like “Hamiltonian” constructed from the sum of all generators of the A-type Artin braid group. These
are also main results in our analysis and are detailed in Theorem 3.3. Finally, the representation
theory of the algebra gl1,1 is analyzed thoroughly (Propositions 4.2 and 4.3) in connection also to
specific shapes of Young tableaux (Subsection 4.1).

4.1. Irreducible representations as Young tableaux. We recall basic definitions of Young
tableaux as these are essential combinatorial objects that play central role in representation the-
ory [12]. We denote λ ⊣N a partition λ = (λ1, λ2, . . . , λp) of the positive integer N, where λi are
weakly decreasing positive integers and P
i∈[p]
λi = N. The size of λ is denoted |λ|, and in general
|λ| = N.
Definition 4.4. (Young diagram) Suppose λ = (λ1, λ2, . . . , λp), λ ⊣N where p ≥1. The Young
(or Ferrers) diagram of shape λ is an array of N squares having p rows with row i containing λi
squares.
Definition 4.5. A filling (or weight) of a Young diagram is any way of putting a positive integer
in each box of the diagram. Let µ = (µ1, µ2, ..., µl) be a filling of a Young diagram. Each µi is the
number of times the integer i appears in the diagram
In order for the diagram to be completely filled, it is necessary for |λ| = |µ|. It is possible to fill
diagrams arbitrarily in this manner, however we impose certain restrictions on the filling µ. These
restrictions lead to the definition of a Young tableaux.
Definition 4.6. (Standard Young tableaux) Suppose λ ⊣N. A Young tableau T is obtained by
filling in the boxes of the Young diagram with symbols taken from some alphabet, which is usually
required to be a totally ordered set. A Young tableau of shape λ is also called a λ-tableau. A Young
tableau is standard if the rows and columns of T are increasing sequences. That is, T is filled with
the numbers 1, 2, . . . , N bijectively.
We consider the set [n] with the standard ordering 1 < 2 < . . . k < k + 1 < . . . < n. There are
various definitions for semi-standard Young tableaux depending on the variation of the associated
Schur functions [21]. We use here the following definition of a semi-standard Young tableaux.
Definition 4.7. A Young tableau is semi-standard if the filling is:
(1) weakly increasing across each row and strictly increasing down each column for numbers
