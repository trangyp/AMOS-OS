2.2
Our Method
Our starting point is Fastfood, which improves the dependence on d, ε at the expense of introducing
the dependence on diameter in the term ∆2/ε2. A natural strategy for improvement is to add a
preprocessing step that controls the diameter. At the same time, it must not distort point distances
in a way that would distort the kernel estimate.
A useful observation is that while we may need to accurately preserve “small” distances, we
can afford to only loosely preserve “large” ones. If x, y ∈Rd are at distance ∥x −y∥≥
p
log(1/ε),
then e−∥x−y∥2

any smaller.
Thus, we need preprocessing that (i) controls the diameter, (ii) preserves “small” distances,
and (iii) keeps “large” distances from collapsing. Fortunately, this type of embedding has been
introduced by [BRS11] for a different set of applications. Their result embeds points in the unit
sphere while preserving distances smaller than √ε up to (1 ± ε) and preventing larger distances
from collapsing below Ω(√ϵ). By scaling the “small” distance threshold, their embedding can be
shown to preserve the Gaussian KDE.
Unfortunately, applying [BRS11]’s embedding costs the same O(d/ε2) running time as RFF.
In fact, their embedding is identical to RFF: it also has the form x 7→ψ(Wx) where W is a full
Gaussian matrix of order dε × d. Therefore, it cannot be used to improve the running time of KDE
queries beyond what RFF already gives us.
This discussion suggests the strategy of proving a result similar to [BRS11] but with a shorter
running time. This is precisely the main ingredient in our approach, Theorem 1.3. Since their
embedding has the form ψ(Wx), a natural candidate is to replace the Gaussian matrix W with an
RHT-based proxy. We show that (a variant of) the Fastfood transform, ψ(HD2HD1x), forms an
embedding into the unit sphere with the properties we need.

2.3
Additional Related Work
Orthogonal Random Features (ORF) [YSC+16, CRW17, CRS+18], Quasi Monte-Carlo features
(QMC) [ASYM16, HSH24] and quadrature-based features [Bac17, MKBO18] offer extensions and
alternatives to RFF for kernel approximation with reduced variance. These lines of work are
complementary to the worst-case query time bounds we study and yield the same O(d/ε2) bound as
RFF in this context. Notably, [YSC+16] proposed Structured ORF (SORF), a triple-iterated RHT
method (ψ(SHD3HD2HD1x) in the notation from Section 2), as a heuristic alternative to ORF,
and showed it performs well empirically. The triple-RHT heuristic was also proposed in [AIL+15]
for locality sensitive hashing (LSH). Kernel Nystr¨om methods [RCR15, GM16, MM17] produce
data-dependent features that control the error based on properties of the kernel matrix, though not
in the worst case.
KDE coresets are a well-studied tool for reducing the data size |X| to a size independent of its
original value [LPMST15, LJLB15, KL19, PT20, DM24]. As mentioned earlier, they are useful as
preprocessing in our method to avoid any dependence on |X| in the query time.
KDE data structures with relative error were pioneered by [CS17] and widely studied since
[SRB+19, BCIS18, BIW19, CKNS20, KSW26]. This stronger error guarantee entails longer query
times than those in Table 1. [BLM+24] proposed hybrid additive-relative error KDE data structures
via preprocessing relative error KDEs with FJLT. The same can be done with our spherical
embedding, Theorem 1.3, in place of FJLT, for improved hybrid running times.
KDE data structures have also recently been used for approximate kernel matrix-vector multi-
plication, with applications to efficient Attention in long-context transformer-based deep learning
architectures [CLD+21, BIMW21, ZHDK23, BIK+23, SSX25, CGS+25, IKSW25].

7
Discussion, Limitations and Open Questions
We proved a new bound on the time complexity of kernel mean queries, improving in some cases
over classical algorithms. Our techniques introduce a new fast spherical embedding theorem and
involve a new chaos-based analysis of randomized Hadamard transforms.
Our spherical embedding result Theorem 1.3 may be of independent interest. [BRS11]’s embed-
ding it is based on has been used in a range of other applications including local (cardinality-based)
dimension reduction, Euclidean snowflake dimension reduction, private near neighbor counting in
high dimensions, Euclidean graph spanners, and efficient algorithms for the Earth Mover Distance
[BRS11, GK15, AIMN23, AZ23]. Theorem 1.3 could possibly be used to improve running time
bounds in these areas.
21


## Page 22


The KDE query times in Table 1 paint a rather complex mosaic of techniques and parameter
regimes. The main open question is to establish optimal bounds for this fundamental problem. In
particular, it is interesting whether other trade-offs between the error ε and the effective diameter
∆σ are possible and what is the optimal trade-off between them. Is a eO(d + 1/ε2) bound possible
that depends on ∆σ only polylogarithmically, or even not at all?
Another open question is extensions to other radial kernels (i.e., which are a function of the
Euclidean distance). Theorem 1.4 extends our Gaussian kernel result to IMQ kernels with essentially
the same bound, through a substantive function approximation result due to [BM10]. Extensions to
other kernels may be handled along similar lines, though they might require specialized work and
the adapted bound might degrade according to the specific kernel. This limitation of our method
is shared by Fastfood and FJLT+RFF, which also necessitate per-kernel treatment, and they are
similarly specialized to radial kernels. RFF on its own is less sensitive to the specific kernel and is
not specialized to radial kernels. It yields the same O(d/ε2) bound as long as the kernel is positive
definite, shift-invariant, and its spectral measure is efficient to sample from.
Our results are primarily theoretical, and we do not include an empirical evaluation. This is partly
because structured matrix methods like RHTs are highly sensitive to hardware and implementation,
and require specialized care in practice (see, e.g., [AK26]), whereas full matrix multiplication is
typically already highly optimized and parallelized. We leave this undertaking for future work. Prior
work (e.g., [YSC+16]) has reported that heuristic methods for kernel approximation can match or
outperform provable methods empirically (see, e.g., their comparison of SORF and Fastfood), and
it remains an open problem to bridge the gap between them.
