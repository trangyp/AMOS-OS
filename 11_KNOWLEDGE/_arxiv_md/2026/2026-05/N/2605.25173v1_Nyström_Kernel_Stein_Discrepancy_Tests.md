Abstract
Kernel Stein discrepancy (KSD) is among the most popular goodness-of-fit (GoF) measures
on general domains with a large number of successful deployments.
One of the main
applications of KSD is in constructing powerful GoF tests. However, tests relying on the
classical U-/V-statistic-based KSD estimators have two major drawbacks. (i) Their runtime
scales quadratically in the number of samples. (ii) Their asymptotic null distribution is
computationally intractable in most cases, typically handled by bootstrapping. While it
is known that the Nystr¨om method permits accelerating KSD estimation with no loss of
statistical accuracy under mild conditions, to the best of our knowledge, the fundamental
question of its impact on bootstrap-based GoF testing is open; resolving this question is
the focus of the current paper.
In particular, we prove that the key properties of the
quadratic-time bootstrapped KSD-based GoF test (asymptotic level and local consistency)
are preserved by its Nystr¨om acceleration. We numerically demonstrate the efficiency of
the accelerated KSD estimator and bootstrap in the context of GoF testing of spherical and
functional data. Our numerical results show that the Nystr¨om-accelerated method performs
statistically on-par with the quadratic-time approach, while requiring substantially smaller
runtime.
Keywords:
kernel Stein discrepancy, goodness-of-fit testing, Nystr¨om method, local
consistency, accelerated bootstrap
1 Introduction
Testing for goodness-of-fit (GoF) is a fundamental problem in data science and statistics
(Ingster and Suslina, 2003; Lehmann and Romano, 2021). Given a (fixed known) target
distribution P0 and samples of an (unknown) sampling distribution P, the aim is to decide if
the samples come from the target, that is, to test H0 : P = P0 versus H1 : P ̸= P0. While fa-
mously tackled by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test (Kolmogorov, 1933; Smirnov, 1948) using
the empirical distribution function, more recent GoF tests—using, for example, nearest-
neighbor-based statistics (Bickel and Breiman, 1983; Schilling, 1983a,b), innovation pro-
cesses (Khmaladze, 1988, 1993), or optimal transport (Hallin et al., 2021)—exist. However,

existing tests share at least one of the following drawbacks (Hagrass et al., 2026): (i) P0 is
subject to restrictive distributional assumptions, for example, P0 is assumed to belong to a
parametric family. (ii) They assume data in Rd. (iii) Full knowledge of P0 is required; they
do not allow distributions known only up to a normalizing factor. Such partially known dis-
tributions typically arise in Bayesian settings or in restricted Boltzmann machines (RBMs).
(iv) They are challenging to compute efficiently.
The highly flexible framework of kernel methods (Aronszajn, 1950; Steinwart and Christ-
mann, 2008), in particular, the associated kernel mean embedding (Berlinet and Thomas-
Agnan, 2004; Smola et al., 2007; Gretton et al., 2012; Muandet et al., 2017), permits map-
ping probability distributions into a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) and allows
comparing two measures by considering the distance of their embeddings in RKHS norm,
giving rise to the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD; Gretton et al. 2012). MMD also
arises as an integral probability metric (Zolotarev, 1983; M¨uller, 1997) when the underlying
function class is chosen to be the unit ball in an RKHS; it is known to be equivalent (Se-
jdinovic et al., 2013b) to energy distance (Baringhaus and Franz, 2004; Sz´ekely and Rizzo,
2004, 2005) (respectively known as N-distance; Zinger et al. 1992; Klebanov 2005). If the
kernel associated with the RKHS is characteristic (Fukumizu et al., 2007; Sriperumbudur
et al., 2010), the mean embedding is injective, and MMD is a metric, which allows designing
non-parametric tests that are consistent against all alternatives.
Indeed, kernel mean embeddings allow designing powerful GoF tests (Balasubramanian
et al., 2021; Hagrass et al., 2024b), addressing (i) and (ii). A novel approach to tackle
GoF testing builds on kernel Stein discrepancies (KSDs; Chwialkowski et al. 2016; Liu
et al. 2016), which combine Stein’s method (Stein, 1972; Chen, 2021; Anastasiou et al.,
2023) with the expressivity and computational tractability of RKHSs. These tests build
on a Stein operator—associated to the known P0—that acts on functions in an RKHS
and consider the RKHS-norm of the mean embedding (w.r.t. the sampling distribution
P) of functions mapped by this operator. One particular Stein operator, the Langevin-
Stein operator (Gorham and Mackey, 2015; Chwialkowski et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2016;
Oates et al., 2017; Gorham and Mackey, 2017), allows handling not necessarily normalized
densities, thereby addressing (i) and (iii).
Since the inception of KSDs, several other GoF tests with corresponding kernel Stein
operators have been designed, additionally addressing (ii); for example, for spherical and
Riemannian manifolds (Xu and Matsuda, 2020, 2021), (infinite-dimensional) functional data
(Wynne et al., 2025), discrete distributions (Yang et al., 2018), point processes (Yang et al.,
2019), random graphs (Xu and Reinert, 2021), sequential models (Baum et al., 2023), and
time-to-event data (Fernandez et al., 2020). Related works independent of the underlying
domain include a sequential GoF testing approach (Martinez-Taboada and Ramdas, 2025),
minimax-optimal KSD-based GoF tests (Hagrass et al., 2026), aggregating KSD-based tests
over different kernel choices to maximize test power (Schrab et al., 2022a), and an analysis
of the robustness of KSD-based GoF tests (Liu and Briol, 2025).
These tests enjoy a broad set of real-world applications. They permit model validation
in challenging settings, for example, deciding whether a Markov chain was drawn from a
given distribution, or improving sample quality (Chwialkowski et al., 2016; Gorham and
Mackey, 2017; Liu and Lee, 2017).
They have successfully been applied in the context
of the Ising model (Yang et al., 2018), on network data (Yang et al., 2018, 2019; Fatima

Despite their success, KSD-based GoF tests suffer from two major drawbacks. First,
the classical U- (Liu et al., 2016) and V-statistic-based (V-KSD; Chwialkowski et al. 2016)
estimators have a runtime that scales quadratically with the number of observations, which
hinders their application in large-scale settings, violating (iv).
Second, the asymptotic
distribution of these estimators under the null hypothesis is often analytically intractable.
To alleviate this analytical intractability, the limiting distribution is typically approximated
using the wild (also known as i.i.d. weighted if the samples are i.i.d.) bootstrap (Arcones
and Gin´e, 1992; Dehling and Mikosch, 1994), which, however, further increases the total
runtime.
Some mitigations reducing the runtime and targeting (iv) exist.
If the kernel func-
tion is analytic, an option is using a linear-time estimator (Chwialkowski et al., 2016, Ap-
pendix 5.1), but this approach may lead to a loss in test power when compared to the
quadratic-time counterpart (Chwialkowski et al., 2015). An independent approach consists
of replacing the RKHS-norm by an Lp-norm (p = 2: Jitkrittum et al. 2017; p ≥2: Huggins
and Mackey 2018), which leads to a different statistic and also requires a good choice of
parameters to work successfully. A special case of Huggins and Mackey (2018) yields a
random Fourier feature-based (RFF; Rahimi and Recht 2007; Sriperumbudur and Szab´o
2015; Szab´o and Sriperumbudur 2019; Chamakh et al. 2020) approximation of KSD. But,
as elaborated by Huggins and Mackey (2018), if RFF is applied to KSD-based GoF tests, it
is known (Chwialkowski et al., 2015, Proposition 1) that the resulting statistic can not dis-
tinguish a large class of measures. An alternative to the RFF-based approach not requiring
the sampling and the target distribution to live on Rd is the Nystr¨om method (Nystr¨om,
1930; Williams and Seeger, 2001; Rudi et al., 2015; Chatalic et al., 2022; Kalinke and Szab´o,
2023); it permits the acceleration of the estimation of KSD and KSD-based tests on general
domains (N-KSD; Kalinke et al. 2025). The analysis of Nystr¨om-accelerated KSD-based
GoF tests is the main focus of this work.
