Abstract. We revisit congruence zeta functions of smooth projective varieties over finite fields in the framework of Scholze’s
Berkovich motives. To use it, we define the notion of motivic congruence zeta functions via categorical traces as an analogue of
Kahn’s approach. With this motivic setting, we prove that our new zeta functions agree with the classical one.

A Brief History of Congruence Zeta Functions. The congruence zeta function of a variety over a finite field is one of the
most classical and important objects in arithmetic geometry. Let X be a variety over a finite field Fq (i.e. separated Fq-scheme
of finite type). Its congruence zeta function is defined by

For smooth projective curves (i.e. algebraic curves, in particular, elliptic curves), Hasse and Weil showed that Z(X, t) is a rational
function and identified its numerator as the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius acting on the Jacobian. Grothendieck et
al. have developed the theory of ℓ-adic ´etale cohomology, and proved the Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula ([SGA5]) for
higher dimensional smooth projective varieties, which have contributed to the progression of Weil’s conjecture ([Wei49]) and
algebraic/arithmetic geometry.
A Motivic Approach. Kahn has defined categorical zeta functions via a “motivic” interpretation of an (ℓ-adic) ´etale cohomology
theory, and compared with the classical (congruence) zeta function in [Kah24]. His strategy is to use general (categorical) traces
of triangulated categories and (classical) six-functor formalism. In fact, he has challenged not only congruence zeta functions
over a finite field, but also L-functions over a global field by the machinery of traces and six-functor formalism above. Similarly,
our strategy can be also expected to extend to the cases of global fields.
Berkovich Motives. On the other hand, Scholze has recently constructed a motivic six-functor formalism on small arc-stacks
associated with Banach rings / Berkovich spaces in [Sch24]. This theory gives a unified construction of “motives” of geometric
points, in particular, it recovers Voevodsky’s ´etale motives over algebraically closed fields (see also [Sch24, Proposition 1.14]).
Note that, over a discrete ground field, we can consider varieties as small arc-stacks via trivial norms.

Main Theorem. The aim of this paper is to give a new description of congruence zeta functions of smooth projective varieties
over finite fields. We can view the ∞-category ]
Dmot(X) of “modified” motivic sheaves as a dualizable object in the (∞, 2)-
category ModDmot(k)(PrL) where X is a smooth projective variety over k. Via categorical trace formalisms, we define a motivic
congruence zeta function of X in the spirit of Kahn’s construction (,however, without explicitly using Chow motives). Our main
result identifies this new motivic congruence zeta function with the classical one defined above:
Theorem 1.1 (Theorem 4.6). For smooth projective variety X over a finite field, we obtain Zmot(X, t) = Z(X, t).
Structure of this paper. In section 2, we recall the basic notions of Berkovich spaces and motivic sheaves according to
[Sch24]. Since Berkovich motives include still quite new notions, we allow ourselves a slightly redundant recollection (e.g.
Banach C-algebras via Gelfand duality).
In section 3, we review the formalism of categorical traces in a presentably symmetric monoidal stable ∞-category and in
its (∞, 2)-category of module categories. Our strategy is inspired by Clausen-Scholze’s lecture notes ([CC]) (and more general
discussion by [HSS17]).
Finally, in section 4, we define a “Berkovich motivic” version of congruence zeta functions for smooth projective varieties over
a finite field, and we analyze our new motivic congruence zeta function via the categorical trace method and the Grothendieck–
Lefschetz trace formula. Moreover, we want to emphasize that the same strategy can be applied, with little change, to suitable
“varieties” over algebraically closed Banach fields.
Notation. In this paper, we fix a prime number p > 0. We assume that rings are commutative rings with unit. Basically, we
will work in (stable) ∞-categories.

2. Recollection of Berkovich motives
In this section, we will review the notions of Berkovich spaces and motivic sheaves.
Definition 2.1 ([Sch24, Definition 2.1,2.2,2.10], Banach rings/fields). A ring R is Banach if R is equipped with a map
| −|R : R →R≥0 satisfying the following conditions:
(1) We have |0|R = 0 and | −1|R ≤1.
(2) For any elements a, b ∈R, we have |ab|R ≤|a|R|b|R.
(3) For any elements a, b ∈R, we have |a + b|R ≤|a|R + |b|R.
(4) The ring R is complete with respect to the induced topology by | −|R.
Moreover, a Banach ring (K, | −|K) is a Banach field if K is a field, and for any elements a, b ∈K, we have |ab|K = |a|K|b|K.
Furthermore, we let Ban denote the category of Banach rings with non-expanding maps (i.e. maps f : R →S of rings
satisfying |f(a)|S ≤|a|R for any element a ∈R). Note that Ban has all colimits ([Sch24, Proposition 2.4]).
♦
Definition 2.2 ([Sch24, Definition 2.13], Berkovich spectra). The Berkovich spectrum of a Banach ring R is the closed subspace
M(R) ⊂Q
a∈R[0, |a|R] of maps ∥−∥: R →R≥0 satisfying the following conditions:
(1) We have ∥0∥= 0 and ∥1∥= 1.
(2) For any element a ∈R, we have ∥a∥≤|a|R.
(3) For any elements a, b ∈R, we have ∥ab∥= ∥a∥∥b∥.
(4) For any elements a, b ∈R, we have ∥a + b∥≤∥a∥+ ∥b∥.
For any point x ∈M(R) (which corresponds to the map ∥−∥x), we let K(x) denote the completion of the fraction field of
the Banach ring (R/ker(∥−∥x), ∥−∥x), which is a Banach field.
♦
