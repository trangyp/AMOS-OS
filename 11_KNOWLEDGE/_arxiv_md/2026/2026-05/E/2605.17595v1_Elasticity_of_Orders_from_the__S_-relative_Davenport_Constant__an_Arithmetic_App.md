ELASTICITY OF ORDERS FROM THE S-RELATIVE DAVENPORT
CONSTANT: AN ARITHMETIC APPLICATION OF A
NUMBER-THEORETIC INVESTIGATION
JARED KETTINGER AND GRANT MOLES
Abstract. Orders in algebraic number fields have long been objects of central interest in
algebraic number theory. Despite non-maximal orders failing to be Dedekind, the present
authors have previously shown that the structure of the ideal class group may still contain
enough information to determine elasticity. In this paper, we develop the S-relative Dav-
enport constant, which builds on previous work by M. Ska lba. Although Ska lba’s original
construction was defined to aid in the study of binary quadratic forms, we show that this
related invariant is the exact tool needed to tackle the question of elasticity in non-integrally
closed orders. In particular, we investigate the elasticity of orders O whose conductor ideal
I = (O : OK) is prime as an ideal of O, as well as orders in quadratic number fields with
primary conductor. We also give conditions under which O will have the same elasticity as
the full ring of integers OK.
1. Introduction
Let G be a finite abelian group. A G-sequence {g1, g2, . . . , gn} of (not necessarily distinct)
elements of G is called a zero-sum sequence if g1 + g2 + · · · + gn = 0. We use D(G) to
denote the smallest positive integer m such that any G-sequence of length m must have a
zero-sum subsequence. Equivalently, we may define D(G) as the largest positive integer m
such that there exists a zero-sum sequence of length m with no proper zero-sum subsequence.
This group invariant is known as the Davenport constant of G.
The Davenport constant was first introduced by Rogers ([24]) and later popularized by
Davenport ([7]) in connection with the arithmetic of rings of integers. In particular, when G
is taken to be the class group of a ring of integers OK, D(G) is a sharp upper bound for the
length of the prime factorization (counting multiplicity) of the ideal (α) for an irreducible
element α ∈Irr(OK). More generally, the Davenport constant is the precise tool we need
to take advantage of uniqueness of ideal factorization—allowing us to discern arithmetic
information about an element α from the ideal (α). Consider the following definition.

For a non-maximal order O of a number field K, the relationship between D(Cl(O)) and
ρ(O) is more tenuous as O fails to be Dedekind, since O = OK. As we will see, this results in
the generalized class group Cl(O) containing only information about those ideals relatively
prime to the conductor ideal (O : OK). However, recent work by the present authors in
[14] has shown that, in the case when (O : OK) is a prime ideal of OK, the elasticity of the
order is still completely determined by the structure of Cl(O). In this paper, we consider the
more general case when (O : OK) is a prime ideal of O. In order to do so, we will introduce
a variant of the Davenport constant which not only informs our present inquiry, but also
provides a natural language for the results of [14].
The Davenport constant has long been studies as a purely combinatorial or number-
theoretic object (see [21] or [3], respectively). It has also inspired numerous variants and
generalizations in the area of factorization theory. Interestingly, multiple generalizations have
been defined and studied from a strictly number-theoretic point of view which were later
found to have profound arithmetic implications. For example, Adhikari and Rath introduced
weighted Davenport constants in 2006 ([1]), after which they were widely studied. It was not
until 2014 that Halter-Koch gave an arithmetic interpretation of these Davenport constants
in [11]. Furthermore, in 2022, Boukheche et al. demonstrated in [2] that these invariants
completely determine the arithmetic of the normset of a ring of integers OK for a Galois
number field K.
Similarly, in 1993, Ska lba introduced the relative Davenport constant as part of his inves-
tigation of numbers with a unique representation by a binary quadratic form ([25]). It turns
out, when we generalize this invariant from elements to sets, we find precisely the tool we
need to study the arithmetic of non-maximal orders.
In Section 2, we will focus on developing this group invariant and proving results which will
inform our arithmetic inquiries. In Section 3, we will study orders whose conductor ideal is
prime in the order, culminating in a characterization of their elasticities in Theorem 3.14. In
Section 4, we briefly apply these results to settle prior conjectures on orders whose elasticity
is equal to that of its integral closure. Finally, in Section 5, we restrict our attention to
orders in quadratic number fields, allowing for a more complete characterization of elasticity
in Theorem 5.8. We conclude with Theorem 5.16, which settles a particular case of a well-
known conjecture on the elasticity of intermediate orders.
To remain consistent with previous work on this topic and to emphasize the relationship
between a (non-maximal) order and the full ring of integers, we will adopt the notation R
to refer to an order in a number field K and R to refer to its integral closure OK.

2. The S-Relative Davenport Constant
A natural generalization of the Davenport constant was first introduced by Ska lba in [25]
to answer a question on binary quadratic forms. He further explored this invariant in its
own right in [26]. Before defining it, we introduce some relevant terms. For a finite abelian
group G, a G-sequence {g1, g2, . . . , gn} is called irreducible if it has no proper zero-sum
subsequence. Furthermore, if {g1, g2, . . . , gn} also fails to be a zero-sum sequence, we call
it primitive.
The relative Davenport constant of G with respect to a, denoted
Da(G), is the largest positive integer m such that there exists an irreducible G-sequence
{g1, g2, . . . , gm} with g1 + g2 + · · · + gm = a. We will presently develop an analogue of the
relative Davenport constant replacing the element a with a subset. Taking advantage of the
groundwork done in [25], we are able to give explicit formulas for this constant for certain
subsets of arbitrary cyclic groups which will translate to strong arithmetic results in later
sections.
Definition 2.1. Let G be a finite abelian group and S a nonempty subset of G. We call
the sequence of elements {g1, . . . , gn} an S-sum sequence in G if g1 + · · · + gn ∈S. If S
is the singleton set S = {g} for some g ∈G, we may use the term g-sum sequence rather
than {g}-sum sequence for convenience. Note that this agrees with the existing definition of
a zero-sum sequence.
Definition 2.2. Let G be a finite abelian group and S a nonempty subset of G. We define
the S-relative Davenport constant, denoted DS(G), to be the minimal natural number
n such that any S-sum sequence {g1, . . . , gn} of length n in G must contain a zero-sum
subsequence.
It should be observed that this definition differs from the generalization introduced in
[13] which uses similar notation. The following proposition demonstrates the relationship
between the S-relative Davenport constant and the original invariant.
Proposition 2.3. Let G be a finite abelian group. Then DG(G) = D(G).
Note that the S-relative Davenport constant and Ska lba’s original construction generalize
the two equivalent definitions of D(G)—in terms of sequences of minimal and maximal
lengths respectively. With this in mind, we introduce a “small” version of our invariant.
Definition 2.4. Let G be a finite abelian group and S a nonempty subset of G.
We
define the small S-relative Davenport constant, denoted dS(G), to be the largest whole
number n such that there exists an S-sum sequence in G of length n which has no zero-sum
subsequence. If every S-sum sequence in G has a zero-sum subsequence, we define dS(G) = 0.
The terms small and large Davenport constant are used analogously throughout the lit-
erature. Namely, d(G) denotes the largest possible integer ℓsuch that there exists a G-
sequence of length ℓwith no 0-subsequence—proper or otherwise. For abelian groups, we
have d(G) + 1 = D(G), but this fails in general for non-abelian groups. For details, see [8].
The following results demonstrate that we avoid this bifurcation for the S-relative Davenport
constant of abelian groups as well.
Lemma 2.5. Let G be a finite abelian group and S a nonempty subset of G. Then dS(G) = 0

As a final note, we turn our attention to a pair of conjectures. The first asks whether,
given a half-factorial order R in a number field K, any order T with R ⊆T ⊆R must also
be an HFD. This conjecture seems to have first appeared (albeit indirectly) in [12] in 2005.
In 2023, [23] suggested that a counterexample should exist, though an immediate corollary
to [9, Theorem 6] shows that such an example could not appear in any quadratic number
field. The question was also of central interest in [4]. In this paper, it was shown that the
conjecture holds whenever the conductor ideal I of R is a radical ideal of R.
The second, far stronger statement was conjectured in [16]. It asks whether, given any
order R in a number field K, any order T with R ⊆T ⊆R must satisfy ρ(R) ≥ρ(T) ≥ρ(R).
Although this question is likely far from being settled in full generality, it follows from
Theorem 5.8 and a simple set-theoretic lemma that this conjecture must hold when R is a
quadratic order with primary conductor. Moreover, we can find a characterization of when
the elasticity of R and T must be equal; namely, when the elasticity of R (and of T) is
achieved by an element relatively prime to the conductor.
