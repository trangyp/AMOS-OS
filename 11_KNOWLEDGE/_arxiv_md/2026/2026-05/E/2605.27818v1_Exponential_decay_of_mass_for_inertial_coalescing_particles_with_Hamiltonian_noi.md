Abstract. We study a system of N inertial particles on a two-dimensional torus T2, evolving
under a second-order stochastic dynamics with position-dependent friction λ and noise amplitude
σ, and undergoing coalescence at rate R0 when their distance falls below a threshold δ. In the joint
small-mass / small-correlation limit µ(ε) →0, µ(ε)/ε →α ∈(0, ∞), the empirical measure of the
surviving particles converges to a stochastic continuity equation with inertial drift gα. Assuming
that σ is tangent to the level sets of a Hamiltonian H = h1(x1) h2(x2) satisfying mild non-degeneracy
and convexity-type conditions, and that λ and the amplitude ρ of σ along ξ = ∇⊥H are aligned
with H, we prove that the expected total mass decays exponentially in time, with an explicit rate
depending on α and on the values of λ and ρ on the separatrix {H = 0}. The proof rests on a
cell-by-cell analysis of the sign of div gα on the level sets of H, showing that the inertial drift pushes
trajectories toward the separatrix at a quantitative rate.
1. Introduction
1.1. Inertial particles and the small-mass limit. The motion of small inertial particles trans-
ported by a fluid is a classical and physically rich problem at the interface of fluid dynamics, kinetic
theory, and stochastic analysis. A particle of mass µ > 0 immersed in a fluid with velocity field
u(t, x) is, to a first approximation, governed by a balance between its inertia and the drag exerted
by the surrounding fluid, leading to a second-order Newton-type equation in which the small pa-
rameter µ multiplies the acceleration. When µ is small, the particle is closely entrained by the flow,
and one expects the dynamics to reduce to a first-order equation for the position alone. Quantifying
this reduction, and identifying the residual effects of inertia that survive the limit µ →0, is the
basic problem of small-mass asymptotics.

turbulent or otherwise irregular environment. The two small parameters µ and ε then compete, and
the limit (µ, ε) →(0, 0) depends qualitatively on the ratio µ/ε. In the language of fluid mechanics,
this ratio is essentially the Stokes number

a fluctuating flow.
For the purpose of a simplified description, we divide the range of Stokes numbers in three
regimes. When St ≫1, particles are very inertial, large and heavy compared to the turbulent
eddies; particles receive several small random kicks from the eddies and perform a sort of inertial
Brownian Motion. This is the so-called Abrahamson regime [1], well understood also from the
viewpoint of stochastic modeling of turbulence [15] (the approach further developed here). When
St ≪1, particles behave like traces, they follow the fluid streamlines and the coalescence rate has
been understood by means of the Saffman-Turner theory [18]; also that case is subsceptible of a
rigorous analysis by a stochastic model of turbulence [9], based on the approach to coagulation
developed in [10], [8]. But, when the values of the Stokes number are intermediate, say St = O(1),
particles and turbulent eddies are roughly comparable, and the behavior is much more difficult.
Several contributions in the Physics literature are devoted to this case, see for instance [2], [3], [5],
[6], [11], [17], [19]. Many heuristic and physically deep ideas have been presented in these works, in
some cases concerned with concentration effects produced by turbulence eddies, a fact also at the
core of the proof given here. Our work deals rigorously with a concentration property and controls
the quantitative consequence of the exponential decay of particle density due to coalescence.

9. Beyond factorization and alignment
In this section we discuss possible extensions of Theorem 2.2 beyond the two main structural
assumptions used in the proof: the factorized form (2.1) of the Hamiltonian, and the double align-
ment condition (A) on λ and ρ. The two extensions have very different difficulty profiles, and we
treat them separately.
9.1. Beyond double alignment. When λ or ρ is not aligned with H, several structural facts
break down. In dimension 2, condition (T) (tangency σ · ∇H = 0) automatically gives σ = ρ ξ for
a scalar amplitude ρ, so monotonicity of |H| along the flow still holds without aligning ρ.
However
- without alignment of λ, ∇βα = −α ∇λ/(2(λ+α)2) is no longer parallel to ∇H, and ξ·∇βα ̸=
0. The formula for gα in Proposition 2.1 then picks up an extra term βα ρ2 (ξ · ∇λ)/λ3 · ξ
that does not vanish on S;
- without alignment of ρ, ξ · ∇ν ̸= 0, and a similar extra term βα ν (ξ · ∇ν) ξ appears.

not vanish on S in general, so the sign-control argument of Section 5 fails. A natural perturbative
approach, that is writing λ = l(H) + ε l1, ρ = r(H) + ε r1 for small ε, could represent a possible
direction for future work.

concentrated in a small number of places, and identifying them is the starting point for a future
extension.
