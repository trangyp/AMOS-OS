Introduction
1.1
mm-entropy: definition and history
Various kinds of entropy are used for complexity evaluation of mathematical
objects since Clausius classical works in termodynamics [2]. In this article,
entropy is considered as a complexity measure for metric spaces. The most
known notion used for this purpose is metric entropy some times also called
ε-entropy. Let us recall the corresponding definition.
Let (X, d) be a metric space and let A ⊂X be a totally bounded subset
of X. Let N(A, ε) denote the minimal number of closed balls of radius ε
sufficient to cover the set A. Then
H(A, ε) := ln N(A, ε)

The idea of using metric entropy for measuring the ”complexity” and
”massiveness” of the sets in metric spaces dates back to the works of L.S. Pont-
ryagin and L.G. Shnirelman from the thirties years of the XX century. A huge
progress in the study of metric entropy of various sets in functional spaces
was achieved by A.N. Kolmogorov’s school, see [10] and the famous surveys
[11, 19, 20, 21].
A bit later, the metric entropy found unexpected and remarkable applica-
tions in Probability Theory where R. Dudley, V.N. Sudakov and X. Fernique
used it for investigation of the sample path properties of Gaussian processes,
cf. [12, 13].
The notion of metric entropy can be naturally extended to other classes
of objects. For a metric space (X, ρ) equipped with a finite Borel measure P
let us define the mm-entropy by

definition uses the well known fact that a measure P is concentrated on
a compact up to an arbitrarily small mass [9]. The index ”mm” stands for
”measure metric” by analogy with M. Gromov’s mm-spaces [8]. The quantity
Hmm(·, ·) is called mm-entropy of the triplet (X, ρ, P). See also [24] for a
more detailed analysis of the structure ”distance plus measure”.
The interest to the study of mm-entropy is, in particular, based on the
fact that it enables to define a non-trivial invariant of mm-spaces with respect
to measure preserving isometries. For example, one can state that there is
no such isomorphism of two mm-spaces if their mm-entropies are different.
Let us say some words about the curious history of this notion. In fact,
it was introduced by C. Shannon in his classical work [16, Appendix 7] but
passed completely unappreciated on the background of other aspects of this
work which made a revolution in information transmission theory.
It is
enough to say that in the first Russian translation of [16] the related Ap-
pendix (as well as some others) was omitted as being of no interest. There-
fore, the notion of mm-entropy, unlike that of metric entropy, has been almost
not studied. We are only aware of the work [15] and some other works of the
same authors where this object appears in the context of signal transmission
theory. Some researchers, apparently unaware of [16], rediscovered it.

In the literature, several well studied notions can be found that are close
to mm-entropy as tools for measuring an ε-discretization error but have an
integral form. In a vast literature devoted to the dictionary based informa-
tion transmission, the moments of the discretization error are used, see, for
example the works of G. Luschgy and G. Pages [14], S. Dereich with coauthors
[3]–[6]. On the other hand, A.M. Vershik used the Kantorovich distance in
his works from ergodic theory [22].
In recent years, with A.M. Vershik’s initiative, the interest to mm-entropy
has been renewed due to potential applications in ergodic theory. Here, one
of the most natural objects of study are the distributions of random processes
in the corresponding spaces of trajectories. In this direction, A.M. Vershik
and M.A. Lifshits considered in [23] the distributions of Gaussian processes.
They showed that for a wide class of Banach spaces equipped with a Gaus-
sian measure mm-entropy is tightly related to the metric entropy of the cor-
responding dispersion ellipsoid (the unit ball of the measure’s kernel, RKHS)
and to the small ball measures. Interestingly, the result of [23] is new even
for the distribution of a Wiener process.
The present work is the first one where the mm-entropy is evaluated for
an important class of non-Gaussian processes.

5
Further research directions
5.1
α-stable L´evy processes with α ⩾1
Our upper bound for mm-entropy from (2) actually extends to the case α ⩾1.
The proof is identical to the provided one up to the standard minor changes
in decomposition (12). However, we are not at all sure that the order of this
bound remains optimal for α ∈[1, 2].
As for the upper bound in (2), our proof depends heavily on the assump-
tion α < 1. It is certainly too crude for possible extension to a larger range
of α.
As suggests a result from [23] concerning mm-entropy of Wiener process
(i.e. α = 2) in C[0, 1], the true order of mm-entropy is somewhere between
ε−α and ε−α| ln ε|.
This problem remains open so far.
5.2
Two-parametric setting
The results provided in this article concern the case ε →0 with δ fixed. It is
quite natural to consider the behavior of Hmm(ε, δ) when both ε, δ →0. As
the findings of [23] suggest, there should be several zones defined by relations
between ε and δ with different asymptotics of Hmm(ε, δ) in each zone.
Our findings in this direction will be published elsewhere.
