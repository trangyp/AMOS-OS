ENRICHED CATEGORIES, REAL METRICS AND
LORENTZ MANIFOLDS
MARCO GRANDIS
Abstract. This expository article brings together two subjects:
generalised metrics based on enriched categories, on the one hand,
and Lorentz manifolds, on the other, at the price of dealing with
details that are well known either in category theory or in relativity.
The spacetime of relativity can be given a real valued metric
ρ(x, y), with values in the extended real line R = [−∞, ∞], or
better (if equivalently) a real valued ‘antimetric’ γ(x, y) = −ρ(x, y)
(satisfying a reverse triangle inequality); the latter, as a function
of y, is positive on the timecone of x, annihilates on its lightcone,
and is −∞on all events which cannot be influenced by x.
All this can be given a well-established base in category theory,
extending Lawvere’s notion of a metric space. In fact, a space with
a real valued metric can be viewed as an enriched category on the
extended real line R, structured as a symmetric monoidal closed
category.
Introduction
0.1. Real valued metrics. Lawvere’s article [L1] presented, in 1974,
a (generalised) metric space X as a small category enriched on the
positive extended half-line w+ = R
+ = [0, ∞]; the latter is viewed as
a category (with morphisms λ ⩾µ) endowed with a strict symmetric
monoidal closed structure given by the (extended) sum λ + µ.
Concretely, X is a set equipped with a generalised metric δ: X×X
→w+ (the enriched hom) satisfying the triangular inequality (enriched
composition) and a second condition (enriched units)
(1) δ(x, y) + δ(y, z) ⩾δ(x, z),
0 ⩾δ(x, x)
(x, y, z ∈X).
The latter amounts to δ(x, x) = 0, for all x ∈X. Symmetry and
‘separation’ are not required.
Although various similar extensions have been studied in the theory
of metric spaces, Lawvere’s structure combines a formal elegance (or
even ‘necessity’) within category theory, with concrete advantages, e.g.
with respect to the existence of infinite products and coproducts –
which obviously fails for metrics with finite values.

Lorentz manifold, can be given a real valued antimetric which forbids

influence the latter.

1. Enriched categories on a strict base
Categories enriched over monoidal categories are a classical topic
of category theory [EK, Ma, Ky]. The following simplified version is
sufficient for our goals.
1.1. Reviewing enriched categories. Informally, we recall that a
category C consists of objects X, Y, ... and morphisms, or arrows, f :
X →Y, ... between objects. The latter have a (partial) composition
law: for each pair of consecutive morphisms f : X →Y and g: Y →Z

there is a composed morphism gf : X →Z. This partial operation is
associative (whenever composition is possible) and every object X has
an identity, written as id X : X →X, or 1X, which acts as a unit for
legitimate compositions.
We always assume that a category is locally small: the objects can form
a class Ob C, but the morphisms X →Y between two given objects always
form a set C(X, Y ), called a hom-set. C is small if the class Ob C is a set.
We begin by recalling, or presenting, some elementary cases of ‘en-
riched categories’.
(a) In a preadditive category C, each hom-set C(X, Y ) has a structure
of abelian group, and the composition gf is additive in each variable
(3)
g(f + f ′) = gf + gf ′,
(g + g′)f = gf + g′f,
(always assuming that these compositions are legitimate, of course).
For instance, the category of real vector spaces (and linear mappings)
has such a structure.
(b) In an ordered category C, each hom-set C(X, Y ) has an order rela-
tion (reflexive, transitive and antisymmetric), and the composition is
monotone in each variable
(4)
f ⩽f ′ =⇒gf ⩽gf ′,
g ⩽g′ =⇒gf ⩽g′f.
Let us note that here, because of transitivity, the previous condition
is equivalent to global monotony: if f ⩽f ′ and g ⩽g′, then gf ⩽
gf ′ ⩽g′f ′.
(c) Terminology. In case (a) we say that C is a category enriched over
Ab, the category of abelian groups (and homomorphisms), equipped
with the usual tensor product A ⊗B. We need the latter to express
condition (3) by a homomorphism C(X, Y ) ⊗C(Y, Z) →C(X, Z).
In case (b) we say that C is a category enriched over Ord, the category
of ordered sets (and monotone mappings), equipped with the cartesian
product A×B. As we already remarked, condition (4) can be sim-
ply expressed by a monotone mapping C(X, Y ) × C(Y, Z) →C(X, Z)
defined on the cartesian product.

2. Metric spaces as enriched categories
We now review Lawvere’s generalised metric spaces, and their exten-
sion to real valued metrics, as enriched categories over a suitable strict
base, studied in [G4]. In both cases, the associated forgetful functor is
far from being faithful, and of no interest.
2.1. Lawvere’s base. The strict monoidal category w+ = R
+ =
[0, ∞] was introduced by Lawvere [L1], in 1974 (the original notation
is R). Its objects are the extended positive numbers λ ∈[0, ∞], with
a unique morphism λ →µ when λ ⩾µ (for the natural order), and
a ‘tensor product’ λ + µ given by the extended sum, with unit 0. All
diagrams in R
+ commute (as in any category based on an ordered set).
A small category X enriched over R
+ amounts to a generalised met-
ric space, in the following sense: a set X equipped with hom-values
X(x, y) ∈[0, ∞], which we rewrite as δ(x, y), satisfying the triangle
inequality and a ‘reflexivity axiom’
(15)
δ(x, y) + δ(y, z) ⩾δ(x, z),
0 ⩾δ(x, x)
(for x, y, z ∈X).
Again, these laws represent the composition arrow X(x, y)+X(y, z)
→X(x, z) and the identity arrow 0 →X(x, x) of the enrichment.
Note that the latter amounts here to δ(x, x) = 0. The symmetry and
separation axioms of classical metrics are not required.
The forgetful functor U = w+(0, −): w+ →Set takes only two values: a
singleton (at 0) and the empty set (elsewhere); the associated category X0
is discrete, and the simplified view of enrichment does not work.
2.2. Positive metrics and weighted algebraic topology. An in-
fluential part of the article [L1], developed by several authors, extends
part of the theory of metric spaces to enriched categories, for instance
Cauchy completeness.

property, such a generalised metric space X can also express privileged
directions. It was used in this sense by the present author, under the
name of δ-metric space, or δ-space, as a basic structure for an enriched
version of Directed Algebraic Topology [G1, G3], where the paths are
measured and the forbidden ones are penalised with an infinite cost.

δ-metric meant to hinder any backward move

3. Real antimetrics and Minkowski spacetime
Reversing inequalities, we briefly consider ‘antimetrics’. The theory
is obviously equivalent to the previous one, but more natural in Rela-
tivity. The future timecone of a Lorentz vector space has a well-known
‘antinorm’, which we use in 3.4 to define an antimetric γ(x, y) of the
whole spacetime; the latter is ⩾0 if the event x can influence y, and ∞
otherwise. The geodesic paths have thus a maximal valuation with re-
spect to γ (the longest elapsed proper time) and minimal (if negative)
with respect to the associated real metric ρ(x, y) = −γ(x, y).
We essentially follow the terminology of O’Neill’s book on semi-
Riemannian manifolds [On], although here it would be more natural to
use the opposite signature of the metric tensor.

3.6. Real valued metrics and topology. All this supports a claim
of [G4]: there is no sensible way of deriving a definite topology from a

ferentiable structure and the metric tensor (or the associated ρ-metric);
they are continuous for the natural topology, while all the topologies
defined on M by general procedures working on its ρ-metric (see [G4])
