Altermagnets are a class of materials with compensated magnetic moments, in which spin sub-
lattices are related by specific symmetries other than inversion or translation. This allows time-
reversal symmetry to be broken without a net magnetization. Here, we synthesize single crystals
of Fe1−xCrxSb2 and investigate their electrical transport and magnetic properties, with a focus on
Fe0.85Cr0.15Sb2. Magnetization measurements suggest spin-compensated ordering below ∼3.5 K,
where magnetic moments align along the crystallographic b-direction. Transport measurements re-
veal a crossover from large positive to negative magnetoresistance, while an anomalous Hall response
emerges below 3.5 K, indicating time-reversal symmetry breaking below TN. Muon spin relaxation
measurements demonstrate bulk magnetic order below 3.5 K, confirming that the low-temperature
ordering is intrinsic rather than due to an impurity phase. These results support a potential al-
termagnetic ground state in Cr-doped FeSb2 with time-reversal symmetry breaking without net
magnetization.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Altermagnetism (AM) has recently emerged as a dis-
tinct class of collinear magnetism, extending beyond
the conventional ferromagnetism (FM) and antiferromag-
netism (AFM) [1, 2]. These systems feature compensated
magnetic moments, analogous to antiferromagnets, while
global time-reversal symmetry is broken, as in ferromag-
nets.
This behavior arises when spin sublattices with
opposite, compensated moments are related by specific
rotational (proper or improper and symmorphic or non-
symmorphic) operations, rather than simple translation
or inversion [2]. As a consequence, altermagnets can ex-
hibit non-relativistic spin-split electronic band structures
along non-nodal planes with alternating spin polarization
[3, 4].
This symmetry-induced spin-splitting can yield
nontrivial Berry curvature effects and can give rise to an
anomalous Hall effect (AHE) [5–7]. These unique prop-
erties position altermagnets as promising candidates for
spintronics and next-generation electronic devices [8].
Progress in discovery and understanding of AM sys-
tems has been driven largely by symmetry-based theoret-
ical analyses and first-principles predictions, which were
later followed by experimental efforts [2, 9, 10]. For ex-
ample, the semiconducting altermagnet MnTe has been
shown to exhibit spin-split electronic [11] and magnonic
bands [12], as well as a strain-tunable AHE [13–17]. The
altermagnetic ground state has also been experimentally
confirmed in other magnetic systems [18, 19], includ-
ing CrSb [20, 21], MnF2 [22], Co1/4NbSe2 [23, 24], and

oretical predictions with experimental realization in ma-
terials continues to pose significant challenges for alter-
magnetic systems [26, 27].
A fundamental distinction between AM and conven-
tional AFM is that, in altermagnets, rotational/mirror
symmetry (arising from spatial anisotropy in the crys-
tal structure or electronic correlations) must be coupled
with time-reversal symmetry at specific magnetic sites
[5, 28]. The compounds Fe1−xCrxSb2, which crystallize
in the marcasite-structure type, serve as illustrative ex-
amples [see Fig. 1 A]. The marcasite lattice hosts two
distinct transition metal sites that are linked by a rota-
tional symmetry operator. However, FeSb2 has a weakly
diamagnetic ground state [29, 30] and has been further
described as a “correlated d-electron topological Kondo-
insulator” [31, 32]. In contrast, CrSb2 displays collinear
antiferromagnetic ordering with k = (1, 0, 0). However,
the global coupling between rotational and time-reversal
symmetries is not satisfied in CrSb2 [33], resulting in con-
ventional antiferromagnetism rather than altermagnetic
order. Recently, Mazin et al. proposed that substitution
on the Fe site could stabilize compensated magnetic con-
figurations compatible with altermagnetic symmetry [see
Fig.
1 A] [28], generating significant interest [34, 35].
In particular, Cr substitution has been suggested as a
route to realizing such states, potentially accompanied by
anomalous Hall response. Prior experimental work has
shown that Cr doping induces magnetic order in FeSb2
[36]. However, despite these predictions, the magnetic
ground state of Cr-doped FeSb2 has remained unresolved.
In particular, it is unclear whether the magnetic struc-
ture is both compensated and symmetry-related in the
manner required for altermagnetism, and whether such

RESULTS
Crystal structure and sample morphology
The marcasite crystal structure consists of corner-
sharing Sb octahedra surrounding the transition metal
site.
The two metal-Sb octahedra are connected by a
combination of rotation (180◦around the b-axis) and
translation (0, 1
2, 1
2). Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD)
patterns, collected on crushed crystals with varying x in
Fe1−xCrxSb2, confirm single-phase marcasite structure
at each composition [see Fig. 1 B]. Only minor contri-
butions were observed from Sb (from flux; see Methods
for details on the growth procedure) and Si (from sample
holder). With increasing Cr-content, the peak positions
shift to lower 2θ angles, consistent with the larger ionic
radius of Cr compared to Fe. Lattice parameters were
extracted using Rietveld refinement of the PXRD pat-
terns, which are shown in Fig. 1 C. The parameters a
and b follow Vegard’s law, exhibiting a linear increase
with increasing Cr-content, consistent with the observa-
tions of Hu et al. [36]. However, the lattice parameter
c shows a non-monotonic trend; incorporation of small
amounts of Cr leads to a decrease in c, followed by an
increase at higher Cr concentrations. This behavior was
observed in both single-crystal XRD and powder diffrac-
tion results. Overall, the increasing lattice parameters
and shifting peak positions confirm the successful doping
of Cr at the Fe-site. The crystal compositions were fur-
ther confirmed using energy-dispersive spectroscopy [see
Table S1]. In this report, we will focus on the electrical
and magnetic properties of the Fe0.85Cr0.15Sb2 samples,
while the properties of all other Fe1−xCrxSb2 samples are
shown in the Supplemental Information file.

Local magnetic order using muon spin relaxation
To further investigate the magnetic ordering, muon
spin relaxation (µSR) measurements were performed on
these samples. µSR has been successfully used to probe
magnetic structures in other altermagnetic materials [44].
In µSR, spin-polarized positive muons with a mean life-
time of ∼2.2 µs are implanted into the sample, and
their decay positrons are counted over time.
The im-
planted muon spin (spin- 1
2) precesses at a frequency of
ωµ = γµBinternal, where γµ is the muon gyromagnetic ra-
tio and Binternal is the internal magnetic field of the sam-
ple. The decay positrons are preferentially emitted in the
direction of the muon spin at the time of decay, allowing
for a time-dependent measurement of the local magnetic
field. In the weak transverse field (TF) configuration, a
small magnetic field (Bexternal) is applied perpendicular
to the muon polarization, causing the muon ensemble to
precess symmetrically in the absence of local magnetic
order (T > Tc). If long-range magnetic order is present
(T ≤Tc), a net Binternal leads to a loss of precessional
symmetry in the TF signal when intentionally measured
with a low time resolution.
Fig.
3 A shows representative time-dependent µSR
spectra collected under a weak TF of 3 mT on co-aligned
Fe0.85Cr0.15Sb2 samples. At 30 K, the samples exhibit
complete muon polarization, suggesting the absence of
long-range magnetic ordering consistent with paramag-
netism. At 7.5 K, a partial polarization loss is observed,
and by 3.6 K, a significant polarization loss is evident.

equation,

III.
DISCUSSION
While the exact magnetic structure in Fe0.85Cr0.15Sb2
remains elusive, a combination of complementary ex-
perimental observations tightly constrains the magnetic
ground state in this sample. µSR results, specifically un-
der a weak TF, establish that the magnetic order is bulk
in nature, ruling out impurity-driven or minority-phase
magnetism.
Magnetization measurements demonstrate
that the ordered moment is small and non-saturating
up to 14 T, consistent with a compensated spin struc-
ture. χ(T) exhibits saturation along [001] and a cusp-
like feature along [010], indicating that [010] is the mag-
netic easy axis in a spin-compensated system.
These
constraints restrict the set of viable magnetic states to
a spin-compensated magnetic structure, although weak
spin canting cannot be ruled out.
In parallel, the observation of a spontaneous anoma-
lous Hall response demonstrates that time-reversal sym-
metry is broken in the electronic structure. The two mag-
netic sublattices occupy crystallographic sites related by
a 180◦rotation about the b-axis. These results, therefore,
provide evidence supporting altermagnetism in Cr-doped
FeSb2 as proposed by Mazin et al. [28]. A symmetry-
driven compensated magnetic state with non-zero Berry
curvature provides the most natural and internally con-
sistent interpretation of the data presented here.

IV.
CONCLUSION
In summary, we report on an experimental investiga-
tion of the magnetic ground state in Fe1−xCrxSb2 (x =
0.09, 0.15, and 0.60) single crystals using several comple-
mentary probes. Among them, the Fe0.85Cr0.15Sb2 sin-
gle crystals exhibit bulk magnetic order below 3.5 K,
as confirmed using muon spin relaxation and magnetic
susceptibility.
Magnetization measurements show that
the magnetic moments align along the crystallographic
b-direction.
The samples also exhibit a large, non-
saturated positive MR, which switches to negative MR
near TN. In addition, an anomalous Hall effect is ob-
served in Fe0.85Cr0.15Sb2 samples in the magnetically or-
dered regime, consistent with a non-zero Berry curva-
ture. The combination of time-reversal symmetry break-
ing and spin-compensated magnetic ordering provides ex-
perimental evidence supporting the prior theoretical pre-
diction of altermagnetism in Cr-doped FeSb2. Predicted
to host a d-wave altermagnetic state, further investiga-
tions using neutron diffraction, inelastic neutron scat-
tering, and scanning tunneling microscopy below 3.5 K
could directly probe the magnetic structure, spin-split
excitations, and signatures of multipolar ordering in this
system.

METHODS
Crystal growth and structural characterization
Single crystals of Fe1−xCrxSb2 were grown using a
conventional solution method.
High purity Fe (pieces,
99.98%, Sigma Aldrich), Cr (pieces, 99.997%, Sigma
Aldrich), and Sb (shots, 99.9999%, Thermo Scientific)
were loaded into Canfield crucibles, sealed in quartz tubes
under 1/4 atmosphere of argon, and heated to 1000 ◦C
for 4 hours. The melt was cooled to 800 ◦C over 10 h,
followed by slow cooling to 650 ◦C, where excess flux was
decanted. A reduced cooling rate of 0.4 ◦C/h was neces-
sary to obtain large crystals. Compositions were tuned to
account for Fe-rich incorporation tendencies, with nom-
inal ratios of 5.1:0.9:94 and 4.2:1.8:94 for x = 0.09 and
0.15, respectively, and 2.4:3.6:94 for x = 0.60. Final com-
positions were verified by energy-dispersive spectroscopy
(SEM, TESCAN MIRA-3).
Crystal morphology depends on composition: Fe-rich
samples (x ≤0.09) grow along [010] with [101] facets,
while Cr-rich samples (x ≥0.60) favor [001] growth
with [110] facets. Crystals with composition x = 0.15
displayed a mixture of both growth tendencies.
Crys-
tal orientation was determined using single-crystal X-
ray diffraction (Rigaku XtaLAB Synergy, Mo Kα, λ =
0.71 Å). Data were collected via ω scans and processed
with CrysAlisPro (version 1.171.42.101a, Rigaku OD,
2023), including Lorentz and polarization corrections.
Powder X-ray diffraction (Rigaku MiniFlex, Cu Kα,
λ = 1.54 Å) was performed on crushed crystals, and lat-
tice parameters were obtained via Rietveld refinement
using GSAS-II[50].
Structural visualization employed
VESTA[51].
Electrical and magnetic property measurement
Electrical transport measurements were performed us-
ing a Quantum Design Physical Property Measurement
System (PPMS). Crystals were shaped into rectangular
bars (∼700 × 500 × 200 µm), and Pt wires were at-
tached using H20E silver epoxy in standard four-probe
and Hall configurations on [101] and [110] facets. Resis-
tivity (ρ) was measured with currents (I) of 1–3 mA along
the [010] or [001] direction, under magnetic fields (µ0H)
up to 14 T. Magnetoresistance and Hall measurements
were conducted under field-cooled conditions, and the
antisymmetric Hall component was extracted following
standard procedures.[52]. Magnetization measurements
were performed using the PPMS Vibrating Sample Mag-
netometer (VSM) and Magnetic Property Measurement
System (MPMS). Crystals (1.5–45 mg) were mounted
using GE varnish. Temperature-dependent susceptibil-
ity was measured from 2-300 K under field-cooled (FC)
conditions (0.1–1 T), and field-dependent magnetization
(M )was recorded isothermally on FC samples.
Muon spin relaxation
Muon spin relaxation experiments were performed on
the General Purpose Surface-Muon (GPS) instrument
[45] at the Swiss Muon Source (PSI). Single-crystal sam-
ples (∼3×2×0.7 mm) with aligned crystallographic axes
were arranged in a mosaic configuration within a 7 × 7
mm grid. The muon spin polarization was rotated by 45◦
to ensure sensitivity to all directions. Measurements were
conducted between 1.5–200 K in zero field, weak trans-
verse field (3 mT), and longitudinal fields up to 0.2 T.
Data were analyzed using the MUSRFIT package [53].

Electrical transport properties of Fe1−xCrxSb2 samples
Electrical transport properties were measured with current and voltage contacts placed on the well-
identified [101] and [110] facets. Fig. S2 A shows the temperature-dependent ρxx (normalized to
resistivity at 300 K) where I was applied along [010] under zero applied field (solid lines) and 14 T
magnetic field (dashed lines) along [101] for x = 0.00, 0.09, and 0.15 samples. Normalized ρxx(T)
was measured with I along [001] and µ0H along [110] in samples with x = 0.15, 0.60, and 1.00, as
shown in Fig. S2 B. With decreasing temperature from 300-2 K, ρxx(T) increases by two to four
orders of magnitude. Two distinct cusps are observed in samples with x = 0.00, 0.09, and 1.00,
consistent with literature reports [6, 4, 3, 5]. Only the high temperature cusp at ∼100 K is visible
in samples with x = 0.15. Both cusps are heavily suppressed in the heavily doped sample with
x = 0.60.
MR and Hall resistivity were also measured in the doped samples. Hall voltage was measured
perpendicular to both current and magnetic field directions.
The antisymmetric component of
Hall resistivity was extracted using the procedure outlined in Ref [1]. Crystals with x = 0.09 show
positive, non-saturated MR at 130 K, which increases modestly at 110 K, as seen in Fig. S3 A. Upon
further cooling, MR drops close to zero at 30 K and turns negative at even lower temperatures. A
sizable, non-saturated negative MR of ∼33 % is observed at 5 K under a 14 T magnetic field. In
the Hall configuration, the antisymmetric component shows a linear field-dependence, as shown in
Fig. S3 B. The negative slope decreases with increasing temperature, suggesting thermal excitation
of n-type carriers, consistent with the ρxx(T) behavior.
In the Cr-rich samples with composition x = 0.60, negative MR appears below 90 K, with the
magnitude of negative MR increasing linearly with decreasing temperature. As shown in Fig. S3

a lower concentration of charge carriers at 2 K than at 20 K. A slight field-dependent curvature
is observed in addition to a linear ordinary Hall effect, which could be due to could be associated

Magnetic properties in Cr-doped FeSb2
χ(T) is shown in Fig. S4 A for samples with x = 0.09 along [001], [010], and [101]. Noticeable
anisotropy is exhibited in these samples, and a Curie-Weiss paramagnetic behavior is observed.
Curie fit shown in the inset warrants µeff = 0.7µB per f.u.. To probe magnetic properties further,
µSR measurements were performed. No symmetry loss was observed under a weak TF of 3 mT
down to 1.5 K, as shown in Fig. S4 B. This finding is consistent with the paramagnetic behavior
observed in magnetization measurements.
For the Cr-rich sample (x = 0.60), χ(T) is shown in Fig. S4 C. Curie-Weiss behavior is observed
in χ(T), with discontinuities at 68 K marking the magnetic ordering temperature TN. At TN,
a cusp-like feature is observed χ(T) along [001], while a further increase is observed when χ(T)
is measured perpendicular to [001]. Field-dependent magnetization, measured along [001], shows
linear behavior both above and below TN, reaching a maximum of 0.1 µB per f.u at 10 T (see
inset of Fig. S4 C). The plateau in χ(T) and low M(H) supports a spin-compensated structure,
consistent with the prior literature reports [2].
µSR data collected in the TF configuration are shown in Fig. S4 D for representative tempera-
tures. Complete polarization oscillations are visible in the paramagnetic regime at 100 K, while
polarization loss is observed at 70 K and below. These spectra were fitted using equation (1) [see
Main manuscript] to extract the magnetic volume fraction and decay rates, as shown in Fig. S4
E. Magnetic ordering occurs over a broad temperature range of 60-80 K, below which the entire
sample orders magnetically. The decay rate exhibits a temperature-dependent peak well below TN,
suggesting only the slow (dynamic component) is observed. No fast decay component was observed
within the resolution limit of muSR. In ZF, the slow component is detected down to 5 K [see Fig.
S4 F]. No time-domain oscillations were observed in the ZF spectra, consistent with a non-uniform
field distribution at the muon sites.
