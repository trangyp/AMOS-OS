Abstract. In this paper, we study maximal Cohen-Macaulay sheaves on closures of minimal nilpotent
orbits in simple Lie algebras. For singularities of type An, we first classify vector bundles on their sym-
plectic resolutions whose pushforwards are maximal Cohen-Macaulay. We then construct equivariant
maximal Cohen-Macaulay sheaves via irreducible representations of the stabilizer group. We compare
these two approaches in the case of maximal Cohen-Macaulay Weil divisors, and extend the equivariant
construction to the classical types Bn, Cn, and Dn.
Finally, we formulate the construction for an
arbitrary simple Lie algebra and carry it out explicitly in the exceptional cases.

1. Introduction
Let G be a simply connected complex Lie group with simple Lie algebra g. Under the adjoint action
of G on g, the set of nilpotent elements N decomposes into finitely many G-orbits. Among the nonzero
G-orbits in N, there is a unique orbit of minimal dimension.
This is called the minimal nilpotent
orbit, usually denoted by Omin. Its closure is Omin = Omin ∪{0}, where 0 is an isolated singularity.
With Kostant–Kirillov symplectic form on Omin, this singularity is a symplectic singularity in Beauville’s
sense in [Bea00]. It’s also mentioned in this paper that those minimal orbit closures are indeed analytic
prototypes for isolated symplectic singularities with smooth projective tangent cones.
Maximal Cohen-Macaulay sheaves play a central role in the study of singular algebraic varieties. They
arise naturally as higher syzygies in free resolutions and may be regarded as the sheaves that are, in a
precise sense, closest to vector bundles on smooth spaces. Maximal Cohen-Macaulay sheaves on surface
symplectic singularities, also known as ADE singularities, were classified in [AVE85].
In [Xu26], we
constructed many families of maximal Cohen-Macaulay sheaves on Nn+1, the minimal nilpotent orbit
closure of type An, showing the complexity of this classification problem in higher symplectic singularities.
In the first part of this paper, we further develop that method and classify vector bundles on symplectic
resolutions of isolated symplectic singularities whose pushforwards are maximal Cohen-Macaulay sheaves.
The main result of this part, stated in Section 2.3, is the following:

2. Singularities and Maximal Cohen-Macaulay sheaves
In this chapter, we work on constructing maximal Cohen-Macaulay sheaves on some singular varieties.
Our main interest is in those varieties with isolated singularities. If the singularity is also symplectic,
while some methods are developed in [Xu26] for constructing purpose, in section 3 we will state a criterion
to determine if a vector bundle on the resolution of an isolated symplectic singularity pushes forward to
a maximal Cohen-Macaulay sheaf.
As all symplectic isolated singularities with symplectic resolutions are very close to An singularities (we
explain this point in 2.2), special treatment should be applied to these nilpotent orbit singularities. If one
again considers the equivariant sheaves with respect to conjugation action of SL(n), a full classification
is possible, at least for those sheaves induced by an irreducible representation of the stabilizer, which is
the main result in the following chapters. This also generalizes to those minimal orbits in other types
of semisimple Lie algebras, and therefore it is worth briefly discussing the structures of those isolated
singularities in the minimal orbit closures here too. One should notice that although all minimal orbits
have symplectic singularities, only An’s minimal orbits admit symplectic resolutions (cf. [Fu03]).
2.1. Symplectic Singularities. We start with the definition of symplectic singularity.
Definition 2.1 (Symplectic singularity). A variety X has a symplectic singularity at a point p ∈X if
there exists an open neighborhood U ⊂X of p such that:
(a) U is normal;
(b) The smooth locus Ureg admits an algebraic symplectic 2-form φ;
(c) For any resolution of singularities f : eU →U, the pullback f ∗φ defined on f −1(Ureg) extends to
an algebraic 2-form on eU.
In this section, we assume X = Spec O is an affine singular variety with only symplectic singularities
and π : e
X →X is a resolution of X. We assume this resolution is symplectic, i.e., there is an algebraic
symplectic form on Xreg which extends to a global symplectic form on e

X is affine this is the same as saying π : e

2.2. Minimal nilpotent orbit closures. Minimal orbits closures Omin in simple Lie algebras are
important examples and standard analytic model among all isolated symplectic singularities with smooth
projective cones, as the following theorem from [Bea00] shows,
Proposition 2.3. Let (X, p) be an isolated symplectic singularity whose projective tangent cone is
smooth. Then (X, p) is analytically isomorphic to (Omin, 0) for some simple complex Lie algebra.

for all nilpotent orbit closures in sln+1. In particular, the minimal orbits Nn+1,1, which consists of all

3. Equivariant Maximal Cohen-Macaulay sheaves on minimal orbit closure of sln+1
In [Xu26], we construct some maximal Cohen-Macaulay sheaves on N3,1, the variety of 3×3 nilpotent
matrices with rank ≤1, but little classification work is done here. One can see the reason why the
classification is so difficult: even for rank 2 sheaves, there are indecomposable maximal Cohen-Macaulay
sheaves π∗f ∗V where π : T ∗P2 →N3,1 is the symplectic resolution, f : T ∗P2 →P2 is the projection map
and V is a vector bundle on P2 belonging to one of the following families
(1) TP2(−1), TP2(−2).
(2) Nontrivial extension of Ix by OP2, where Ix is the ideal sheaf of a closed point x on P2.
(3) Stable 2-bundles with c1 = 0 and c2 = 1.

Steiner bundles on P2. In this way one can also construct maximal Cohen-Macaulay sheaves on Nn+1,1
