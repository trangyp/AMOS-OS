Abstract. Extending Enriques’ characterization to algebraically closed fields of char-
acteristic p ≥7, we show that every smooth projective surface X with h1(X, OX) = 2
and p1(X) = p2(X) = 1 is birational to an Abelian surface. This characterization fails
if p ≤5 and we give a sharp alternative.

1. INTRODUCTION
In [Enr05], Enriques characterized Abelian surfaces birationally among smooth projec-
tive complex surfaces X as those with irregularity h1(X, OX) = 2 and first and second
plurigenus p1(X) = p2(X) = 1.
This has been generalized to higher dimensions by
Chen–Hacon [CH01] (see also [Jia11,Par12]).
First steps towards such a characterization of Abelian surfaces in positive character-
istics have been made by Ferrari [Fer19] (see also [Bau25a, HPZ19] for statements in
higher dimensions). The problem is particularly subtle in characteristic 2 and 3, because
some (quasi-)bielliptic surfaces with trivial canonical bundle cannot be distinguished from
Abelian surfaces using only Hodge numbers and plurigenera. Thus, the following formu-
lation of our main theorem is natural:
Theorem 1.1. Let X be a smooth projective surface over an algebraically closed field of
characteristic p > 0. Then, X is birational to an Abelian or (quasi-)bielliptic surface S
with ωS ∼= OS if and only if h1(X, OX) = 2 and pi(X) = 1 for

3. SURFACES OF MAXIMAL ALBANESE DIMENSION
In this section, we prove a characteristic-free version of [Kol86, Proposition 4.5]:
Theorem 3.1. Let X be a surface of maximal Albanese dimension. Then, either X is
birational to an Abelian surface or p2(X) ≥2.
Proof. By rigidity, we may assume that X is minimal. As X is of maximal Albanese
dimension, it is not birationally uniruled by Lemma 2.1, hence κ(X) ≥0 (one could also
deduce this from [Bau25b, Theorem C]).
Now, seeking a contradiction, assume that X is not an Abelian surface and p2(X) ≤1.
By [Eke88, Corollary 1.8], X is not of general type. Note also that κ(X) ̸= 0 by either
classification of surfaces [BM77] or by [HPZ19, Theorem 0.2]. We therefore have κ(X) =
1, so X admits a canonical genus 1 fibration g: X →B. As X has maximal Albanese
dimension, the general fiber of g is not rational by Lemma 2.1, so g is generically smooth.
In fact, again because X has maximal Albanese dimension, no fiber of g is contracted
by f. As the underlying reduced scheme of a fiber of g is either smooth or a union of
rational curves, Lemma 2.1 implies that it is smooth. Thus, by [LLR04, Theorem 6.6], the
associated Jacobian fibration J(g): J(X) →B is smooth and hence χ(OJ(X)) = 0. By
[LLR04, Theorem 3.1] and the Leray spectral sequence for OX with respect to g, we have
χ(OJ(X)) = χ(OX). As p1(X) ≤1 and h1(X, OX) ≥2, we deduce that h1(X, OX) = 2,
p1(X) = 1, b1(X) = 4 and b2(X) = 6. By [KU85, Lemma 3.4], b1(X) = 4 and the fact
that the Albanese map of X does not contract fibers of g implies that g(B) = 1.
