EQUIVARIANT NONLINEAR PARTIAL DIFFERENTIAL OPERATORS ON
CONSTANT CURVATURE SPACES
FRANCESCO BALLERIN AND ERLEND GRONG
Abstract. Motivated by PDE-learning, we give a classifying space for nonlinear operators on
simply connected spaces with constant curvature which are also equivariant under the action of
the isometry group. The nonlinear operators we are considering are those that can be written
as a polynomial in linear operators. We show that the classifying space for such operators
can be realized as the vector space spanned by equivalence-classes of multigraphs. We also
illustrate how this realization can help us discover non-trivial linear dependence relations
between nonlinear differential operators relative to the dimension of the manifold. We also
give some comments on operators equivariant under the identity component of the isometry
group and under isometry groups of sub-Riemannian model spaces.
1. Introduction
Characterizing the class of differential operators that are equivariant for the action induced by
the isometry group of a Riemannian manifold is a fundamental problem in differential geometry,
with direct implications wherever symmetry is a modeling constraint. In this paper we construct
a universal object for nonlinear differential operators on the Riemannian model spaces that are
equivariant with respect to all isometries.
By a result of Helgason [11] from 1959, all isometry-equivariant linear differential operators
are polynomials in the Laplace-Beltrami operator ∆, giving a one-to-one correspondence between
such operators and polynomials in one variable. Helgason proved this statement for the class of
two-point homogeneous spaces, which includes the constant curvature, simply connected spaces.
For the latter, we will show that the classifying space for nonlinear polynomial operators is the
real vector space spanned over equivalence classes of multigraphs.

Our motivation for this work comes in part from the growing field of PDE learning, where
the goal is to identify or parametrize differential operators from data while enforcing physical
symmetries. Equivariance under isometries is a natural and desirable constraint in this context,
reducing the effective complexity of the learning problem. This has motivated the development
of equivariant neural architectures for PDE-related tasks [5, 10, 14, 1, 17]. Theorem 1.1 gives a
complete and explicit basis for the space of nonlinear polynomial isometry-equivariant operators
on model spaces with multigraphs serving as an explicit encoding. In particular, if one limits the
consideration of candidate terms by polynomial degree and total order, then Theorem 1.1 shows
that the number of equivariant candidate terms are asymptotically constant with respect to the
dimension d. For example, Theorem 1.1 shows that the number of equivariant candidate terms
up to total order 4 is the same for every d ≥2, while Theorem 1.2 shows the difference between
d = 2 and higher dimensions in the case of total order 6.
Structure of the paper. Section 2 collects the necessary background on multigraphs and their
encoding, the theory of invariant tensors and Iwahori’s theorem for linear tensors invariant under
orthogonal transformations. Section 3 develops the theory of nonlinear polynomial differential
operators on Riemannian manifolds, introducing symmetrized covariant derivatives and the con-
traction machinery. Section 4 constructs the operators Nγ associated to multigraphs in detail
and proves that they are well-defined. This section also contains several examples. Section 5
introduces frame bundles, and connects the concepts of equivariant maps on a manifold and
invariant maps on the frame bundle. Section 6 describes Riemannian model spaces and contains
the proof of the main result Theorem 1.1. Section 7 finally looks at several related problems to
the main result. We look in detail at the kernel of the map N when p = 6 and d = 2, giving
us in the end Theorem 1.2. This section also gives a classifying space for operators that are
invariant under the connected component of the isometry group; a result which is valid for real
projective spaces in addition to the Riemannian model spaces. Finally, we give some brief details
on non-linear operators invariant under isometry groups of sub-Riemannian model spaces. The
appendix contains tables with more examples.

2. Preliminaries
2.1. Multigraphs. In this paper, multigraphs arise naturally as combinatorial objects encoding
invariant contraction patterns between symmetric covariant derivatives of a function. We begin
by reviewing the pertinent definitions and establishing the notation.
A multigraph γ consists of a finite set of vertices V [γ] = {v1, . . . , vn} together with a finite
multiset of edges E[γ] = {{vi1, vj1}, . . . , {vip, vjp}}, where each edge e ∈E[γ] is an unordered
pair {v1, v2} of vertices in V [γ]. In particular, multiple edges between the same pair of vertices
are allowed, and we also allow loops, i.e., edges of the form {v, v}. The reader should take note
that in some of the literature multigraphs do not allow loops, and the term pseudographs is used
instead [4, 9]. Two multigraphs are said to be isomorphic if there exists a bijection between their
vertex sets inducing a bijection between edge multisets.
The degree deg(v) of a vertex v ∈V [γ] is defined as the number of edges incident to v, where
each loop contributes 2 to the degree of the vertex to which it is attached.

7.3. Sub-Riemannian model spaces. A sub-Riemannian manifold (M, E, g) is a triple, where M
is a connected manifold, E ⊆TM is a subbundle of TM and g is a metric tensor defined only
on E. In this section, we will assume that E is bracket-generating, equiregular, meaning that
there exists a flag of subbundles
E = E1 ⊊E2 ⊊· · · ⊊Es = TM,
with Ei+1 = span{Ei, [E, Ei]}. We write d1 for the rank of E. Note that the case s = 1 equals a
Riemannian manifold. By the bracket-generating condition, we can write any linear differential
operators using only vector fields with values in E. We say that a linear differential operator L
has nonholonomic order k if k is the minimal number of derivatives in the direction of E needed
for L, see [2, Section 4.2] for details.

the sub-Riemannian cometric. Let ♯: TM →E be the map ♯α(ξ) = ⟨ξ, ♯α⟩g for α ∈T ∗M,
