EllipseLIO: Adaptive LiDAR Inertial
Odometry with an Ellipsoid Representation
Rowan Border 1 and Margarita Chli 1
Abstract— LiDAR Inertial Odometry (LIO) is a critical
component for many mobile robots that need to navigate
without relying on external positioning (e.g., GPS). Platforms
that operate autonomously in different environments and with
heterogeneous LiDAR sensors require a LIO approach that can
adapt to these different scenarios without human intervention.
Existing LIO approaches can typically provide reliable and
accurate odometry in scenarios with similar environments
and sensors when suitably tuned. However, many approaches
struggle to retain robust odometry across heterogeneous envi-
ronments and sensors while using a consistent configuration.
This paper presents EllipseLIO, a real-time LIO approach
that generalises between scenarios by using methods for Li-
DAR scan filtering and registration that adapt to the sensor
capabilities and environment without requiring scenario-specific
tuning. Experiments with EllipseLIO and state-of-the-art LIO
approaches on five datasets with diverse and challenging
scenarios demonstrate that EllipseLIO is the best-performing
approach overall. It achieves a 38% lower odometry error on
average than the second-best approach and is the only approach
that does not diverge in any experiment. An open-source version
of EllipseLIO will be available at github.com/v4rl-ucy/ellipselio.
I. INTRODUCTION
Mobile robots require a positioning system to safely
navigate through their surrounding environment. Platforms
that operate outdoors often use an external positioning sys-
tem (e.g., GPS/GNSS), but this is not possible when the
signals are blocked by structures (e.g., indoors) or experience
interference (i.e., jamming). LiDAR Inertial Odometry (LIO)
approaches enable robots to navigate using onboard odom-
etry computed from LiDAR and Inertial Measurement Unit
(IMU) measurements. The reliability of the LiDAR odometry
used is particularly critical for autonomous platforms that
need to operate in diverse environments and with different
sensors without human intervention.
Existing LIO approaches can provide highly accurate and
robust odometry in a wide range of scenarios, with different
environments and sensors, when they are tuned to operate
reliably in each scenario. However, many approaches do not
generalise well between different scenarios without tuning
as their methods for scan filtering and registration do not
adapt online to the sensor capabilities and environment
structure. This is substantiated by the variation in relative
performance of the compared LIO approaches on different
datasets (Fig. 1). Scan filtering methods typically downsam-
ple LiDAR scans to a fixed resolution without considering
the sensor resolution or the range of measurements. This can
produce sparse scans with insufficient geometric fidelity for

The approach presented in this paper, EllipseLIO, ad-

• An ellipsoid-based scan registration method that pro-

point match to account for the local surface geometry.
• A drift correction method that is directly integrated
into the scan registration pipeline and does not require
separate loop closure handling. Point matches are adap-
tively weighted to prioritise those with older ellipsoids
in revisited locations and correct accumulated drift.

II. RELATED WORK
LIO approaches aim to provide accurate and robust odom-
etry by combining low-frequency but high-fidelity geometric
information from LiDAR scans with high-frequency but
noisy motion measurements from an IMU. They consist of
three key stages: preprocessing, odometry, and mapping.
The preprocessing stage applies filtering and in some cases
motion-undistortion to the raw LiDAR scans. These scans
can contain over a hundred thousand points and are too
computationally expensive to process in real time. Some
approaches [6–8] reduce this complexity by extracting ge-
ometric features (e.g., lines and planes), but these features
often do not generalise between different sensors.
Other approaches [1–3] downsample the points to a uni-
form resolution using a voxel grid filter. This is an efficient
method for reducing the computation cost, but using a fixed
downsampling resolution does not generalise well between
different environment scales. AdaLIO [9] presents a method
for switching between resolutions in small- and large-scale
environments, but it requires heuristic parameter tuning. This
paper presents a range-based filtering method that adaptively
varies the downsampling resolution used at different ranges
based on the LiDAR field-of-view and resolution.
The odometry stage estimates the sensor motion from the
LiDAR scans and IMU measurements. In tightly coupled
approaches, the IMU measurements propagate the odometry
estimate between LiDAR scans. An estimate from the LiDAR
is obtained by matching features or points between scans or
against an incrementally updated map. Feature matching [6–
8] can be computationally efficient, but its performance
depends on the saliency of features extracted from a scan.
Most approaches [1–3] perform scan registration using
variants of the Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm. ICP
associates points in the current scan with points in the
previous scan or map and computes an alignment between
them by minimizing the point-to-point, point-to-plane, or
plane-to-plane distance. Point-to-point ICP works well in
unstructured environments but takes longer to converge and
is sensitive to noise. Point-to-plane ICP works well in struc-
tured environments and converges more quickly but is less
accurate in unstructured environments. Generalized ICP [10]
uses a plane-to-plane error metric that is more robust to
sensor noise in unstructured environments but still makes
planar assumptions about the surface geometry.
Recent work has focused on reducing drift and preventing
divergence when using these ICP variants. X-ICP [11] at-
tempts to detect degenerate scenarios and prevent divergence
by constraining the registration optimisation along degener-
ate axes. Super Odometry [12] and GenZ-ICP [13] combine
point-to-point, point-to-plane, and point-to-line (only [12])
metrics with weights derived from threshold parameters. The
ellipsoid-based registration method presented in this paper
adapts the error metric used for each map point based on
weights obtained directly from its associated ellipsoid.
The mapping stage aggregates points from LiDAR scans
into a map with a common reference frame that can be used
for scan-to-map registration. The map is stored in a data
structure that supports efficient searching and point insertion
(e.g., an ikd-tree [2, 8], Octree [14] or voxel hash map [3]).
Many approaches use this data structure to directly represent
their map but some also build a higher-level representation
to encode additional geometric information (e.g., with sur-
fels [14, 15]). EllipseLIO stores map points in a dynamic
iOctree data structure [16] and builds an ellipsoid map
representation to encode high-fidelity geometric information.
III. ELLIPSELIO
EllipseLIO aims to provide reliable odometry across di-
verse scenarios with different environments and sensors by
adapting to the environment and sensor capabilities. It applies
adaptive range-based filtering to LiDAR scans instead of
uniform-resolution filtering to preserve sufficiently high-
fidelity geometric information on surfaces at every range. It
adapts the error metric used for each point match during the
scan-to-map registration based on the structure of an ellipsoid
that represents the local surface geometry around the map
point. Accumulated drift is corrected when the sensor returns
to a previously visited location by adaptively weighting point
matches to prioritise those with older map points that were
captured when the location was previously observed.
Figure 2 presents an overview of EllipseLIO. LiDAR scans
are preprocessed by applying range-based filtering and mo-
tion undistortion. The range-based filtering uses an efficient
iOctree [16] data structure to downsample points in the
LiDAR scan to different resolutions based on their distance
from the sensor. The undistortion compensates for the sensor
motion during a scan using state estimates obtained from the
high-frequency IMU measurements.
The odometry pipeline performs state estimation using the
iterated Extended Kalman Filter (iEKF) presented in FAST-
LIO [8]. The state is updated by registering a processed
LiDAR scan with the map using ellipsoid-based registra-
tion metrics and adaptive weights for point matches. State
predictions propagate the latest updated state forwards using
high-frequency IMU measurements. These predicted states
are used for motion undistortion of the LiDAR scans and
provide an initial estimate for the next state update.
Mapping is performed by aggregating processed LiDAR
scans into a globally consistent map with a common ref-
erence frame. Map points are stored in an iOctree [16] to
enable fast insertion and searching. This allows an ellipsoid
to be estimated for each point in real time. These ellipsoids
represent the local surface geometry around each point and
are obtained using the method presented in [17].
A. Preprocessing
LiDAR scans typically contain over a hundred thousand
points and are captured at a frequency of 10 Hz. Reducing
the number of scan points is necessary because performing
odometry and mapping with every point in real time is often
computationally infeasible.

The registration error metric used for each match is
determined by the structure of the ellipsoid associated with
the map point. This structure is decomposed into geometric
primitives representing lines, planes, and balls. Line primi-
tives denote surfaces with a single dominant axis of variation
(e.g., a pole), plane primitives denote surfaces with minimal
variation along a single axis (i.e., the surface normal), and
ball primitives denote surfaces with similar variation along
each axis. These primitives are assigned saliency scores
based on how closely they match the ellipsoid structure.

{v1, v2, v3}, and their corresponding magnitudes m =
{m1, m2, m3}, where m1 ≤m2 ≤m3. The saliency
scores for the associated geometric primitives are g =
[gline, gplane, gball]. The registration error between a scan
point Gp, and its matched map point Gq, is computed for
each geometric primitive.

map in the global frame using the updated odometry states.

is achieved by varying the density of points added to the map
and the size of the ellipsoids created based on their distance
from the sensor, such that the ellipsoids for nearby points

IV. EVALUATION
EllipseLIO is evaluated on five datasets: Newer Col-
lege [21, 22], Oxford Spires [4], Botanic Garden [5],
GRACO [23], and GEODE [24]. These cover a diverse range
of environments, platforms, and sensors. The Newer College
and Oxford Spires datasets consist of mostly structured urban
scenes, captured by handheld platforms with high-resolution
Ouster OS1-64 and OS0-128 LiDARs. The Botanic Garden
dataset captures an unstructured park environment from a
wheeled platform with a lower-resolution Velodyne VLP-
16. The GRACO dataset captures an urban area from an
aerial platform at altitudes of 20 to 40 m with a VLP-16. The
GEODE dataset contains challenging sequences from indoor
and outdoor environments, captured by handheld, wheeled,
and waterborne platforms with an OS1-64 and VLP-16.
The performance of EllipseLIO is compared with state-
of-the-art approaches: DLIO [1], FAST-LIO2 [2], LIO-SAM
[7], and iG-LIO [3], using their open-source implementa-
tions. All approaches were run with ROS2 Humble and
Ubuntu 22.04 on an Intel i9-13905H CPU with 32 GB RAM.

V. DISCUSSION
The odometry results (Table 1) show that EllipseLIO is
the best-performing approach overall. It consistently attains
the lowest or second-lowest odometry error across all of
the dataset sequences and achieves a 38% lower APE on
average than the second-best-performing approach, iG-LIO
0.5 m. EllipseLIO is also the only approach that runs on every
dataset sequence without diverging.

EllipseLIO is able to provide reliable odometry on all
of the dataset sequences without diverging by adapting
the resolution used for scan filtering and registration to
preserve sufficient geometric information at every range.
On sequences in narrow enclosed environments (e.g., stairs,
stairs-alpha and stairs-beta), the compared approaches using
their tuned resolutions either diverge or produce an APE
over 2× greater than EllipseLIO, except for DLIO on the
stairs sequence, as their scan filtering does not retain enough
measurements. When the compared approaches use a 0.1 m
voxel resolution on these sequences many of them still
diverge as their scan registration methods do not adapt the
number of neighbouring points or voxels used for surface
estimation to account for the greater point density.
On sequences in large-scale open environments where
structures are far from the sensor (e.g., the aerial-* and
water-* sequences) there is a large separation between the
LiDAR scan lines. The point density along the scan lines in
these environments is much greater than between them, even
after filtering. This can result in degenerate sets of nearest
points being used for surface estimation (e.g., in DLIO and
FAST-LIO2) and produce invalid point matches (e.g., in LIO-
SAM). It causes the compared approaches, except for iG-
LIO, to diverge on many of these sequences. iG-LIO is more
robust to divergence as it uses voxel-based surface estimation
to ensure a better spatial distribution of the points.
EllipseLIO is able to provide accurate odometry esti-
mates for all of the sequences regardless of their structure
by adapting the scan registration metrics used to the lo-
cal surface geometry. The ellipsoid representation captures
the saliency of different geometric primitives (Fig. 4): in
structured environments (e.g., observatory-01 and aerial-04)
over 70% of the ellipsoids are primarily planar, while in
natural environments (e.g., parkland-mound and 1006-01)
these account for less than 55%. The adaptivity of EllipseLIO
enables it to retain more consistent performance between
different environments than the compared approaches. Its
mean APE is 2× larger on the natural Botanic Garden dataset
than on the structured Oxford Spires dataset, while the mean
APE values for DLIO, FAST-LIO2, and iG-LIO, which make
planar surface assumptions, are 3× larger.

A. Ablation Studies
Ablation studies evaluate the impact of key components
on the performance of EllipseLIO (Fig. 6). Replacing the
adaptive match weighting with constant weighting increases
the relative mean APE by 3.6×. This variant accumulates
drift over a trajectory without correction, which increases the
APE for longer sequences with large loops (e.g., 1006-01 and
bodleian-02). Replacing the ellipsoid-based scan registration
with point-to-point registration increases the relative mean
APE by 5.2×. This variant has a larger APE in structured
environments (e.g., christchurch-03 and keble-02) where the
plane and line registration metrics provide valuable con-
straints. Replacing the range-based filtering with uniform
filtering at 0.1 m increases the relative mean APE by 20×.
This variant diverges in large-scale environments (e.g., the
aerial-* and water-* sequences) because the uniform filtering
does not provide a suitable spatial distribution of points.
VI. CONCLUSION
EllipseLIO is able to provide reliable odometry that gen-
eralises between different environments and sensors with-
out requiring scenario-specific tuning. Its range-based scan
filtering method adapts to the sensor and environment to
retain sufficient geometric information on every surface. Its
ellipsoid-based scan registration method adapts the error
metric used for point matches based on the local surface ge-
ometry. Odometry drift is corrected during scan registration
by adaptively weighting point matches. Experiments with El-
lipseLIO and state-of-the-art LIO approaches on five diverse
and challenging datasets show that it is the only approach
that does not diverge on any sequence and it achieves at least
29% lower APE on average for every dataset than the second-
best approach. Future work will integrate visual information
into EllipseLIO so that the resulting approach can provide
reliable odometry in environments with significant geometric
degeneracy where LIO approaches consistently fail.
