We present a non-perturbative holographic dual description for the O(N) vector model in d-
dimensional Euclidean space within the functional renormalization group (FRG) framework. By
continuously iterating Wilsonian RG transformations, the extra-dimensional scale coordinate is
identified as the radial direction of an emergent (d + 1)-dimensional bulk spacetime.
We con-
struct a bidirectional holographic dictionary that maps non-perturbative fluctuations directly into
the emergent bulk metric warping factors. Under the massless critical configuration, the emergent
gravitational vacuum spontaneously organizes into a stable, regular Anti-de Sitter (AdSd+1) geom-
etry without coordinate singularities, satisfying all foundational local energy conditions. Near the
thermal horizon, by systematically eliminating the conical deficit singularity, we rigorously prove
that the semiclassical Hawking temperature identically matches the boundary field theory temper-

the First Law of Black Hole Thermodynamics, spontaneously generating the Bekenstein-Hawking
area law (Shorizon = N

I.
INTRODUCTION
The holographic duality, or the Anti-de Sitter/Conformal Field Theory (AdS/CFT) correspondence, has established
a profound paradigm shift in modern theoretical physics by mapping strongly coupled boundary quantum field theories
to dual gravitational dynamics in a higher-dimensional bulk spacetime [1–4].
Despite its monumental success, a
core conceptual challenge remains unresolved: constructing a rigorous, first-principles derivation that dynamically
translates the non-perturbative renormalization group (RG) flow of a boundary theory into the Einstein equations
of bulk gravitation [5–8].
A promising avenue to bridge this gap is the functional renormalization group (FRG)
framework [9, 10]. By identifying the continuous Wilsonian RG scale with the radial coordinate of an emergent bulk
space, the FRG naturally introduces an extra spatial dimension directly from the boundary degree of freedom [11–15].
In the literature, various foundational approaches have attempted to construct bulk gravity from field-theoretic RG
flows, yet each retains critical theoretical limitations when moving beyond vacuum geometries [5, 7]. Early pioneering
frameworks, such as Polchinski-type exact RG equations, successfully mapped scale evolution to a bulk field equation
but were strictly confined to a kinematic, fixed background [16–18]. Subsequent formalisms tried to introduce dynam-
ical bulk gravity via collective fields in large-N theories [8, 19]. While these methods have achieved notable success
in deriving AdS background geometries near quantum criticality, their extension to finite-temperature environments
introduces non-trivial theoretical challenges. Specifically, black hole geometries and their corresponding black hole
thermodynamics have never been systematically derived from a first-principles RG framework. Without a continuous,
dynamic backreaction capable of capturing the thermal horizon, previous holographic RG models inevitably intro-
duce mathematical ambiguities near the infrared (IR) regime, preventing a rigorous statistical-mechanical derivation
of horizon thermodynamics [11, 12].
Parallel to these approaches, modern quantum information perspectives utilizing Quantum Error Correcting codes
and tensor networks—most notably the Multi-scale Entanglement Renormalization Ansatz (MERA)—have provided
an elegant spatial discretization of AdS geometry [20–23]. However, these information-theoretic frameworks remain
predominantly kinematic; they construct a static spatial slice of AdS through entanglement structure but lack the
capacity to dynamically generate black hole geometries from boundary thermal states [24, 25]. Consequently, they
cannot provide a first-principles statistical-mechanical definition of the horizon’s local thermodynamics or explain the
internal energy’s dynamic evolution [26, 27].
In this work, we address these foundational challenges by developing a continuous and self-consistent holographic
