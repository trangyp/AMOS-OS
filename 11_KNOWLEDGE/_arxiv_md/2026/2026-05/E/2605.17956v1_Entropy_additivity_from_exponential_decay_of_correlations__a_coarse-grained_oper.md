Abstract
Thermodynamic extensivity is commonly introduced as a postulate — the homogeneity of degree one
of thermodynamic potentials. We provide a constructive derivation of this property from microscopic
conditions on the pair potential, without assuming it. Working with the one- and two-particle reduced
densities of the N-body canonical Gibbs state, we introduce a combined coarse-graining operator C on
single-particle phase space M = Λ × R3 , producing dimensionless mesoscopic probabilities {Vi × Πα}
over spatial–momentum cells. Under three conditions on the pair potential — stability, temperedness,
and exponential cluster decomposition with correlation length ξ. We show using the Ursell cluster
expansion that the coarse-grained entropy satisfies

Introduction
The distinction between intensive and extensive thermodynamic variables occupies a central position in
classical thermodynamics, yet it is not primitive. We argue that the distinction emerges from the interplay
of three structural ingredients: the existence of a thermodynamic limit, the short-range character of
microscopic interactions, and the decay of correlations. Standard treatments [1, 5, 22] identify extensivity
with first-degree homogeneity of thermodynamic potentials—a postulate that implicitly encodes all three
ingredients without making them explicit. Our goal in this paper is to make the emergence of extensivity
constructive. We introduce a combined coarse-graining operator C acting on the single-particle phase space
M = Λ × R3 of an N-body canonical ensemble, building on the one-particle reduced density f (1) and
the Ursell cluster expansion for the reduced s-particle densities. Within this framework, we demonstrate
that extensivity is equivalent to the statistical factorisation of f (1) across spatial cells, and that the
coarse-grained entropy is additive up to a correction that is exponentially small in ℓ/ξ (the cell diameter
in units of the correlation length). It follows that when the pair potential violates temperedness, and
correlations do not decay exponentially, this factorisation fails, and the entropy is genuinely non-additive,
with the departure quantified by inter-cell mutual information.
The connection between extensivity, decay of correlations, and factorisation of the partition function
is implicit in the foundational work of Ruelle [3], Fisher [4], and Lebowitz–Penrose [7]. It is worth stating
upfront what we consider to be the contribution in our paper:
(i) A self-contained operator formalism based on reduced densities that makes the factorisation mecha-
nism explicit and quantitative via a joint coarse-graining operator acting on single-particle phase
space—a step not taken in the classical literature.
(ii) A proof of entropy additivity at higher orders of the cluster expansion (pairs, triples, and all higher
cumulants), yielding a clean bound on the total deviation from extensivity.
(iii) Although we do not provide a rigorous proof in the present work, we identify the non-commutativity
of nonlinear thermodynamic functionals with spatial averaging as the structural source of non-
extensivity, and connect it to the Buchert–Ostermann commutation problem [14–17] in relativistic
cosmology. The details of this are reserved for future work focusing on cosmology.
Coarse-graining procedures appear throughout statistical mechanics as phase-space partitioning [5, 6, 22]
or as spatial averaging in kinetic theory [18, 19]. Projection-operator techniques [20, 21] reduce microscopic
dynamics to relevant variables. The rigorous foundations of the thermodynamic limit are in Ruelle [3, 8],
Fisher [4], and Lebowitz–Penrose [7]; the Gibbs measure framework is treated rigorously in Georgii [9] and
Dobrushin [10]. Non-extensive statistical mechanics is studied systematically in Tsallis [11] and reviewed

The N-Body Framework and Reduced Densities

2.1
Two Levels of the Extensivity Question
We distinguish two logically independent levels:
(i) Phenomenological level. A state function X(S, V, N) is extensive if
X(λS, λV, λN) = λ X(S, V, N)
for all λ > 0.
(1)
Equivalently, X is homogeneous of degree one in (S, V, N). At this level, extensivity is defined by
postulate [1, 2]. We note that S, V , and N retain their conventional interpretations as entropy,
volume, and particle number, respectively.
(ii) Structural level. The present paper derives (1) from microscopic conditions on the pair potential
(Definitions 2.3–2.5 below), without appealing to (1) as an assumption. The two levels are logically
independent; the phenomenological classification motivates but does not answer the structural
question.

Discussion
Summary of the framework.
The operator we defined, C = Cx ◦Cp, coarse-grains the one-particle
reduced density on single-particle phase space M = Λ × Rd. By working on M rather than on the 2dN-
dimensional N-body phase space ΓN, the joint mesoscopic probabilities (Definition 3.8) are well-defined
via the reduced two-particle density f (2)—a function on M × M and the ill-definedness that arises when
one tries to integrate a single N-body density over two disjoint phase-space cells simultaneously is avoided.
Separation of two distinct corrections.
Our propositions theorem 4.1 and theorem 4.8 distinguish
between two physically independent sources of deviation from strict extensivity:
(a) Bulk correlation correction: O(|Λ| e−ℓ/ξ). This arises from inter-cell statistical dependence in the
bulk and is exponentially suppressed for ℓ≫ξ.
(b) Surface correction: O(|∂Λ|). This arises from the breaking of translation invariance at the boundary,
and is present even for non-interacting systems.
Standard textbook treatments conflate these two corrections under a single O(|∂Λ|) term, which is incorrect
in general.
Long-range interactions.
For gravitational systems (e.g. self-gravitating gas clusters [3]) or unscreened
Coulomb plasmas, ϕ(r) ∼r−1 in d = 3 and temperedness fails(for a survey of such systems see [27, 42]).
theorem 4.6 shows that I(i, j) decays only as |xi −xj|−1, whose sum over all pairs diverges, and SCG ̸=
P
i Si. This is consistent with the known non-extensivity of self-gravitating systems studied in the Tsallis
framework [11, 12].
Non-commutativity and the Buchert problem.
theorem 3.2 demonstrates that the spatial average
of a nonlinear thermodynamic functional differs from the functional of the spatial average by a Jensen
correction term. In the context of general relativity, this hints at the origin of the Buchert backreaction [14–
16]: spatial averaging of the Einstein equations produces extra terms (backreaction) because the equations
are nonlinear in the metric. theorem 3.2 is the flat-space, non-relativistic analogue of this statement,
providing a thermodynamic counterpart to the cosmological averaging problem.
Scope and limitations.
The results are established for classical systems in equilibrium. Extensions to
quantum systems, non-equilibrium steady states, or relativistic kinematics would require corresponding
generalisations of the Ursell expansion, and are not addressed here. The cluster decomposition condition
(theorem 2.5) restricts the results to the single-phase region; at phase transitions the correlation length ξ
diverges and the bounds weaken.

Conclusion
We have provided a constructive, operator-based derivation of extensivity in classical statistical mechanics.
The main contributions are:
(i) Consistent framework. By working with reduced s-particle densities on single-particle phase
space M rather than the full N-body phase space, all joint mesoscopic probabilities are well-defined,
dimensionless, and properly normalised.
14


## Page 15


(ii)
proof of entropy additivity at higher orders. theorem 4.1 proves that the coarse-grained
entropy satisfies SCG = P
i Si + O(|Λ| e−ℓ/ξ/ℓd) by bounding the entire multi-information series
using the Ursell cluster expansion, including all s-body terms.
(iii) Non-commutativity of nonlinear functionals. theorem 3.2 demonstrates via Jensen’s inequality
that spatial averaging does not commute with nonlinear thermodynamic functionals such as the
entropy density. This is the structural source of non-extensivity and connects to the Buchert–
Ostermann commutation problem in relativistic cosmology.
(iv) Quantified non-extensivity. For systems with long-range interactions, theorem 4.6 identifies
inter-cell mutual information as the precise measure of departure from extensivity, providing a bridge
between thermodynamics and information theory.
(v) Generalised Euler relation. theorem 4.8 derives the standard Euler relation U = TS −PV + µN
as a corollary of the thermodynamic limit, and gives the explicit surface correction E∂= O(|∂Λ|)
for finite systems.
These results are consistent with the classical work of Ruelle [3], Fisher [4], and Lebowitz–Penrose [7],
and with the Gibbs-measure framework of Georgii [9]. The contribution of the present paper is an explicit
operator formalism that makes the factorisation mechanism transparent, quantifies all correction terms,
and connects thermodynamic extensivity to the broader problem of spatial averaging in nonlinear theories.
We reserve the question of perturbation theory, in particular higher order perturbation theory in the
cosmological context [23–25], for future work.

B
The Overall Narrative
These plots together tell a single coherent story.
1. The coarse-grained framework is consistent with standard Gibbs statistics in the fine-graining
limit, with a controlled O(M −2) convergence rate (Plot 3).
2. The mesoscopic probabilities πi,α (Plot 2) are the natural objects for a reduced description, inheriting
the structure of f (1)(x, p) (Plot 1) while discarding sub-cell fluctuations.
3. Entropy is additive—and thermodynamics is extensive—if and only if inter-cell mutual information
decays fast enough (Plots 4–5). Short-range interactions guarantee exponential decay; long-range
interactions produce algebraic decay and permanent non-additivity.
4. The failure of extensivity for long-range interactions is not merely a correction: it is a qualitatively
different regime visible as a rising S/N curve in Plot 6.
5. Spatial averaging does not commute with nonlinear thermodynamic functionals (Plot 7). This is a
real, quantifiable effect connected to the cosmological backreaction problem.
6. Surface corrections to the Euler relation vanish relative to bulk quantities in the thermodynamic limit
(Plot 8), and they are physically distinct from bulk correlation corrections.
The overall conclusion is that extensivity is not a postulate: it is a derived consequence of microscopic
stability, temperedness, and the exponential decay of correlations. When any of these conditions fails, the
entropy is non-additive, and the degree of non-additivity is quantified by the inter-cell mutual information—
a bridge between classical thermodynamics and information theory made explicit by the coarse-graining
framework of this paper.
