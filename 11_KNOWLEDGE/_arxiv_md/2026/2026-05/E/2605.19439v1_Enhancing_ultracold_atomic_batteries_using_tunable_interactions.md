Enhancing ultracold atomic batteries using tunable interactions
Duc Tuan Hoang
,∗Thomas Busch
, and Thom´as Fogarty
Quantum Systems Unit, Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology Graduate University, Onna, Okinawa 904-0495, Japan.
(Dated: May 20, 2026)
We study the charging performance of a one-dimensional, many-body bosonic quantum battery
driven by a harmonic-oscillator charger, focusing on how many-body effects and intra-species in-
teractions influence the energy-transfer dynamics. We show that by tuning the charger frequency,
the system can reach a resonance condition where perfect energy transfer and maximal extractable
work are achieved. In the weak-coupling limit this can be understood by approximating the bat-
tery–charger dynamics using an effective two-level model, which accurately predicts the maximum
stored work, ergotropy, and optimal charging time. In this regime, many-body batteries exhibit
enhanced charging power, reduced quantum speed limit (QSL) times, and comparable or lower
irreversible work relative to single-particle batteries. We further examine the role of intra-species
interactions: repulsive interactions inside the battery medium suppress performance, whereas attrac-
tive interactions can significantly enhance it, with both types of interactions generating additional
charging resonances. Our results show that particle number and interaction control provide pow-
erful tools for designing fast, efficient, and scalable quantum batteries, and point toward a feasible
experimental implementation in ultracold-atom platforms.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Quantum technologies, based on bona fide quantum
resources such as coherence, entanglement, many-body
correlations, and statistics, are rapidly advancing with
the potential to surpass their classical counterparts [1–
3]. Within this landscape, quantum batteries (QBs) have
emerged as a promising paradigm for how to store and de-
liver energy at the quantum scale. They are usually mod-
eled as finite-dimensional or continuous-variable quan-
tum systems that are charged through a unitary process
and later deliver work via controlled extraction protocols
[4–14]. A central objective in this field is to understand
how genuine quantum effects can enhance the power and
the maximum extractable work, the so-called ergotropy
[15–18].
Over the past decade, many theoretical studies have
established that collective charging and correlations
can yield superextensive enhancements of the charging
power, particularly under global operations that gen-
erate multipartite entanglement between battery cells
[16, 19–21].
Furthermore, many-body phases can sta-
bilize energy storage, while proximity to quantum criti-
cality can boost work extraction due to enhanced suscep-
tibilities [10, 22]. While correlations among battery cells
can boost the battery performance, they come with the
trade-off that the energy can be locked in correlations be-
tween the battery and charger, reducing ergotropy and
creating a gap between stored and extractable energy
[12, 13, 23, 24]. A wide variety of QB architectures have
been proposed and partially realized, including Dicke-
type light–matter models [6, 25–29], spin and spin-chain
batteries [8–10, 22, 30–32], ultracold atom systems [7]
and solid-state platforms [33, 34]. Yet, none have been
fully realized currently in experimental settings [5].
∗t.hoang@oist.jp
Here we study the suitability of mixtures of ultra-
cold few-body systems in low dimensions in the contin-
uum as a setting for QBs [35–38], where the interplay
between many-body interactions and quantum statis-
tics can lead to many new intriguing phenomena [39–
41] which may be advantageous for increasing the charg-
ing power. Low dimensional few-body ultracold quantum
systems have already been identified as interesting plat-
forms for quantum simulation and technologies [42, 43],
in particular for the exploration of non-equilibrium ther-
modynamics of quantum systems [44, 45], as they of-
fer highly-controllable experimental settings [46–48]. We
will show that many-body interactions effects and the
infinite Hilbert space of the continuum system can lead
to significant enhancements and interesting features with
respect to energy storage and work extraction compared
to spin-inspired QBs.
In particular, we propose a one-dimensional, bosonic
quantum battery model coupled to a harmonic-oscillator
charger. This coupling is realised by a sudden quench of
the two-body inter-species contact interaction between
the two systems, which can be tuned experimentally
using Feshbach resonances [48, 49].
Our focus is on
the interplay between the interaction strength and the
charger’s trap frequency, which together determine the
resonance condition under which maximum energy trans-
fer and the highest ergotropy can be achieved. In ad-
dition to numerically simulating the complex quantum
many-body process, we also derive an effective two-level
model that provides an approximate description of the
battery dynamics in the weak-interaction regime. This
approximation gives analytical expressions that predict
the resonance conditions, the scaling of the optimal
charging power and the Mandelstam–Tamm bound on
the QSL time [50, 51], which sets the characteristic time
for optimal charging in this regime. A central outcome
of our analysis is a many-body speedup: increasing the
particle number shortens the time required to reach the

This behavior can be understood in terms of
the overlap defined in Eq. (29).
The overlap involves
four single-particle states: the initial states consist of the
battery ground state ϕ0(x), which has even parity, and
the first excited state of the charger φ1(x), which has
odd parity; the final states are the charger ground state
φ0(x), with even parity, and the nth excited state of the
battery ϕn(x). For energy transfer from the charger to
the battery to occur, the overlap integral must be finite.
This requires the product of the four involved wave func-
tions to be an overall even function. Since the product
ϕ0(x)φ1(x)φ0(x) is odd under parity, the battery excited
state ϕn(x) must also be odd parity in order to render
the entire integrand even. Consequently, only odd par-
ity battery excited states can participate in the resonant
energy-transfer process. At resonance, the ratio of the
ergotropy to the charger initial energy also reaches unity,
indicating maximum extractable energy from a battery.
The resonance spectra for NB = 1 and NB = 2 batteries
do not differ significantly when there are no intra-species
interactions among particles in the battery.

Interacting battery
In this section, we investigate the many-body quan-
tum battery with finite intra-species interaction gB ̸= 0,
and examine how this interaction modifies the reso-
nance spectrum and affect the power relative to the non-
interacting scenario. When we introduce a finite intra-
species interaction among the battery particles, one can
no longer use the analytical expression derived for the
non-interacting case to predict the resonance conditions
or the overall performance of the battery. Therefore, the
new resonance conditions must be calculated numerically,
first by finding degeneracies between the decoupled en-
ergy spectra of the battery and charger systems, and then
fine-tuning ωC to ensure complete energy transfer.
1.
Modification of the resonance spectrum
In Fig. 8, we display the resonance spectrum for dif-
ferent values of the intra-species interaction gB in the
battery. Analogous to Fig. 2(g), we plot the maximum
stored work rescaled by the initial energy of the charger.
In the absence of intra-species interactions, the spectrum
exhibits a single resonance peak near the odd values of
WC, consistent with the behavior of a single-particle bat-
tery. Adding more bosons to the battery shifts this peak
to slightly larger values of WC and simultaneously broad-
ens its width.
When intra-species interactions are present, the dy-
namics and the resulting spectrum become significantly
more complex. While the non-interacting battery has a
harmonic energy spectrum which can have degeneracies,
finite intra-species interactions break these degeneracies
and introduce multiple new channels for energy trans-
fer. This is shown in Fig. 8(a) where two distinct peaks
emerge when gB = 3, and for higher n, more than two
peaks appear across all values of gB. This behavior may
be advantageous from the perspective of optimal control
and engineering: by tuning the intra-species interaction,
one can modify the resonance structure and achieve ef-
fective energy transfer for a wider range of initial energies
in the charger.
We also note that in the weak interaction regime,
|gB| < 1 repulsive interactions will generally shift the
resonance peak toward smaller values of WC, whereas
attractive interactions move the resonance toward larger
values of WC. This is due to the fact that interactions
affect lower energy states more than higher ones. Repul-
sive interactions increase the ground state energy more
than the nth excited state, which reduces their energy dif-
ference and thereby reduces the amount of energy it can
store. On the other hand, with attractive interactions the
groundstate energy decreases more than the nth excited
state, widening their energy difference and increasing the
amount of energy it can store. For large attractive inter-
actions the groundstate can form a bound state with neg-
ative energy that is technically unbounded from below,
while repulsively interactions bosons are bounded from
above by the Tonks-Girardeau limit.
This shows that
much more work can be transferred from the charger to
the battery in the attractive regime.
2.
Effect of the interaction on the charging power
Next, we compare the charging power of the battery
in the presence of finite intra-species interaction gB with
that of the non-interacting battery, as shown in Fig. 9
(a) for NB = 2 and (c) for NB = 3. For each value of
gB, we select the resonance peak that yields the highest
charging power at the time the battery reaches its first
maximum of stored work tmax.
Repulsive interactions
(gB > 0) lead to a reduction in charging power when
compared to the single-particle performance. This be-
havior can be attributed to the increased time required to
reach the maximum energy transfer compared to the non-
interacting case. This effect is evident in Fig. 9(b) and
(d), which show the QSL time for NB = 2 and NB = 3,
respectively, computed numerically using Eq. (34). For
repulsive interactions, the quantum speed-limit time is
noticeably larger than in the non-interacting case (cir-
cles). Together with the shift of the resonance values of
ωC toward smaller energies, the net effect is a significant
degradation of the charging power. This trend suggests
that as the system approaches the Tonks–Girardeau limit
[53, 54], where strongly repulsive bosons behave like non-
interacting fermions, the charging power becomes signifi-
cantly suppressed relative to the non-interacting bosonic
system.

IV.
CONCLUSIONS
We have studied the charging performance and the er-
gotropy of a bosonic many-body quantum battery, which
is charged by a single particle in a 1D harmonic oscilla-
tor. We have shown that at weak coupling gBC to the
charger, the non-interacting battery (gB = 0) can be well
described by the approximated two-level model. In other
words, in the weak coupling limit the essential features
of the energy-transfer dynamics, such as the scaling of
the charging power, the quantum speed limit time, the
maximal stored work and the resonance frequency can
be captured analytically.
We have compared the ana-
lytical expression with the numerical results using Exact
Diagonalization, confirming that the two-level approxi-
mation provides reliable predictions as long as excitations
to higher-energy states remain suppressed. As the inter-
species interaction becomes stronger, the system can be
no longer described by the two-level model due to the
larger amount of irreversible work production, as shown
in Appendix. C.
We have shown that many-body batteries (NB > 1)
exhibit a clear advantage over their single-particle coun-
terparts.
The charging time decreases with increasing
particle number, leading to an enhancement of the charg-
ing power that scales as √NB.
This improvement is
reflected in the quantum speed limit, whose reduction
for larger NB directly explains the observed speedup
in energy transfer. Furthermore, in the weak coupling
regime, many-body batteries maintain a comparable or
even smaller amount of irreversible work generated dur-
ing the sudden interaction quench, indicating that faster
charging does not come at the cost of significantly in-
creased energetic overhead. The reduced von Neumann
entropy at the time of maximal charging for larger NB
further reveals that correlations between the battery and
charger build up more efficiently in the many-body set-
ting.
When finite intra-species interactions are introduced,
the resonance structure becomes richer and more tun-
able. Repulsive interactions shift the resonance to lower
charger energies and generally suppress the charging
power, with performance decreasing as the system ap-
proaches the Tonks–Girardeau limit.
In contrast, suf-
ficiently strong attractive interactions shift the reso-
nance to higher energies and can reduce the characteristic
charging time, thereby enhancing the charging power be-
yond the non-interacting case. However, for large initial
charger energies, this enhancement may be offset by the
faster increased quantum speed limit time. Additionally,
strong interactions lead to the appearance of multiple

resonance peaks, offering further opportunities for engi-
neering optimal operation points.
Overall,
our results highlight several mechanisms
through which many-body effects and interparticle in-
teractions can be harnessed to enhance quantum bat-
tery performance. The optimal charging strategies can
be achieved from a careful balance between interaction
strength, particle number, and charger energy to reach
the resonance conditions and decrease the QSL time.
These findings open paths for the experimental realiza-
tion in ultracold atom platforms, where precise control
of inter-species and intra-species interactions is readily
available, and suggest new directions for designing scal-
able and high-power quantum energy-storage devices.

Appendix C: Stronger interaction quench
In the following, we study the performance of the bat-
tery charged under strong inter-species interaction gBC.
Figure 10(a) shows the time-dependent injected work and
ergotropy of a two-boson battery for gBC = 1.4.
The
value of ωC is chosen by solving the resonance equation
Eq. (B12).
As shown in the figure, the charging time
required to reach the first maximum of transferred en-
ergy is significantly shorter than in the weak-coupling
regime [see Fig. 5(a)]. Moreover, the charging dynamics
become considerably more complex, as neither the to-
tal work nor the ergotropy exhibits the simple two-level
oscillations observed in the weak-coupling case. This in-
creased complexity is further illustrated in Figs. 10(b)
and (c), where the dynamics of the passive-state proba-
bilities reveal that multiple excited states become popu-
lated, especially at longer charging times. As the charg-
ing duration increases, more excitations are generated
during the quench, leading to a higher degree of mixing
in the battery state. Notably, even though the dynam-
ics are more intricate in the strong-coupling regime, the
trap frequency ωC can still be tuned to achieve substan-
tial energy transfer and nearly optimal ergotropy.
