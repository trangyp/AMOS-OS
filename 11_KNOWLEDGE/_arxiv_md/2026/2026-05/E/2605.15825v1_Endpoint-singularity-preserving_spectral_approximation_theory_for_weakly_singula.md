Abstract
We introduce a fractional approximation framework for functions with limited regularity near the terminal point. The
proposed basis is constructed by composing classical Jacobi polynomials with an endpoint algebraic mapping, thereby
incorporating the terminal singular structure directly into the approximation space. The main structural properties
of the fractional polynomials are established, including orthogonality relations, derivative identities, and a singular
Sturm–Liouville eigenvalue formulation. We then introduce the associated weighted Sobolev spaces and prove pro-
jection and Gauss-type interpolation error estimates in weighted norms. Inverse inequalities and weighted Sobolev
embedding estimates are also derived. The resulting theory provides a rigorous foundation for high-order spectral and
collocation approximations of endpoint-singular and weakly regular problems, including terminal value problems,
fractional diﬀerential equations, and weakly singular Volterra integral equations.
Keywords: orthogonal polynomials, endpoint singularities, spectral approximation, weighted Sobolev spaces,
weakly regular solutions
1. Introduction
Orthogonal polynomial approximations constitute one of the fundamental pillars of modern numerical analysis due
to their central role in spectral methods, high-order interpolation, quadrature theory, and approximation in weighted
Sobolev spaces [12, 13]. Classical polynomial systems such as Legendre, Chebyshev, and Jacobi bases have been ex-
tensively studied and successfully employed in the numerical treatment of diﬀerential, integral, and integro-diﬀerential
equations [6]. Their remarkable approximation properties for smooth functions, together with precise projection and
interpolation estimates, have made them indispensable tools in the development of high-order numerical schemes [5].
Despite this success, classical smooth polynomial bases often encounter substantial diﬃculties when applied to
functions possessing endpoint singularities or limited regularity. In many important applications, including terminal
value problems, weakly singular Volterra integral equations, and fractional diﬀerential equations, the exact solution
typically exhibits reduced smoothness near one endpoint of the computational interval [1]. Such singular behavior
frequently leads to a severe deterioration in the convergence rate of standard polynomial approximations, even when
global smoothness is only mildly violated. This issue becomes particularly pronounced in spectral and collocation
methods, where the theoretical exponential convergence is strongly tied to solution regularity.
The numerical treatment of endpoint-singular functions has therefore attracted considerable attention in recent
years [14]. Various singularity-resolving strategies have been proposed, including graded meshes, singular basis
enrichment, coordinate transformations, adaptive polynomial spaces, and weighted approximation frameworks [16].
Among these approaches, variable transformations and singularity-adapted basis constructions have proved particu-
larly eﬀective, since they preserve the global high-order structure of spectral approximations while incorporating the
intrinsic asymptotic behavior of weakly regular solutions.
In the context of fractional diﬀerential equations and weakly singular models, several important developments have
recently been made. In particular, generalized Jacobi-type functions and transformed orthogonal systems have been

Motivated by these challenges, the present work introduces the Zaky–Jacobi polynomials as a class of endpoint-

the terminal point. The proposed polynomial system is constructed through an endpoint transformation that naturally
preserves the singular structure arising in terminal-point phenomena. In contrast to classical smooth polynomial
bases, the Zaky–Jacobi framework intrinsically captures weak regularity at the endpoint and therefore provides a
substantially more eﬀective approximation mechanism for singular terminal problems.
The main objective of this paper is to establish the approximation theory associated with the Zaky–Jacobi polyno-
mial family. To this end, we ﬁrst develop the fundamental structural properties of the basis, including orthogonality
relations, recurrence formulas, and the associated singular Sturm–Liouville eigenstructure. These properties provide
the mathematical foundation required for the rigorous analysis of spectral approximations in weighted spaces. We
then introduce the corresponding weighted Sobolev spaces adapted to the endpoint transformation and derive approx-
imation estimates for the associated L2-orthogonal projection and Gauss-type interpolation operators. In particular, we
establish weighted norm error bounds for both the approximation error and its transformed derivatives, together with
inverse inequalities and weighted Sobolev embedding estimates. These results show that the Zaky–Jacobi framework
achieves spectral-order convergence even for weakly regular functions whose singular structure is concentrated near
the terminal point.
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 is devoted to the analytical properties of the
Zaky–Jacobi polynomial basis, where the fundamental structural results, including orthogonality relations, recurrence
formulae, derivative identities, and the associated singular Sturm–Liouville framework, are established. Section 3 de-
velops the weighted orthogonal projection theory and derives the corresponding approximation estimates in suitable
weighted Sobolev spaces generated by the transformed derivative Dρ. Section 4 focuses on the Zaky–Jacobi–Gauss
interpolation framework and its associated error analysis, including stability estimates, inverse inequalities, weighted
Sobolev inequalities, and Hölder-type estimates for the right-sided weakly singular integral operator. Section 5 con-
structs the Zaky–Jacobi spectral-collocation method for weakly singular Volterra integral equations and establishes
convergence estimates in both the L∞(J)-norm and the weighted L2
κµ,υ,ρ(J)-norm. Section 6 presents numerical exper-
iments that illustrate the accuracy and convergence behavior of the proposed method. Finally, Section 7 summarizes
the main ﬁndings and outlines the signiﬁcance of the proposed Zaky–Jacobi framework for endpoint-singular and
weakly regular problems.

A rigorous approximation theory was established for the Zaky–Jacobi polynomials. We derived their main struc-
tural properties, including orthogonality relations, recurrence formulas, derivative identities, and the associated singu-
lar Sturm–Liouville eigenvalue problem. We also introduced the corresponding weighted Sobolev spaces generated

operators. Error estimates were proved in weighted norms for both the approximation error and its transformed
derivatives. In addition, inverse inequalities, weighted Sobolev inequalities, and Hölder-type estimates for the right-
sided weakly singular integral operator were obtained. These results form the analytical foundation for the stability
and convergence analysis of the proposed spectral-collocation method.
The developed theory was then applied to a right-sided weakly singular Volterra integral equation. A fully discrete
spectral-collocation scheme was constructed by combining the Zaky–Jacobi interpolation operator with a Jacobi–
Gauss quadrature rule adapted to the weak singularity of the kernel. The resulting error analysis established conver-
gence in both the L∞(J)-norm and the weighted L2
κµ,υ,ρ(J)-norm. The numerical experiments conﬁrmed the theoretical
results and demonstrated the eﬀectiveness of the proposed method for problems whose solutions exhibit terminal
singularities.
Overall, the framework provides a robust and analytically justiﬁed basis for high-order approximation of endpoint-
singular functions and for the construction of accurate spectral and collocation methods for weakly singular integral
