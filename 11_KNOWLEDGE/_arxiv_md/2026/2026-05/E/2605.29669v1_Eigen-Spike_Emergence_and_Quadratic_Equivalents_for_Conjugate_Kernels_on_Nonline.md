Abstract
Recent work in random matrix theory (RMT) has developed the notion of deterministic equivalents:
typically linear surrogate models that approximate the spectral behavior of large nonlinear random
matrices, such as nonlinear feature maps in neural networks (NNs). On the one hand, these deterministic
equivalents make theoretical predictions tractable by reducing a complex model to a simpler model with
properties that fall under the umbrella of classical RMT tools. However, this leaves open the question
of whether this idealized linear equivalence remains meaningful when dealing with high-dimensional
nonlinearly separable data, such as performing clssification on nonlinearly separable data. Motivated by
this, we consider the conjugate kernel (CK), which is the nonlinear feature map of a feedforward NN,
under a canonical nonlinearly separable dataset, the XOR problem; and we use the study of informative
outlier eigenvalues in the CK and whether their corresponding eigenvectors asymptotically align with
XOR labels as a proxy for nonlinear learnability. We develop a robust quadratic equivalent to the spiked
CK matrix that enables a precise analysis of emergent informative spikes, as one modifies various knobs
common in ML practice: sample complexity, signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), nonlinear activation choice, and
pretrained features. In each of these scenarios, we derive a precise BBP-type phase transition in which
linear classification via the CK eigenvectors becomes possible. Our analysis helps translate the power of
deterministic equivalence tools in RMT to study problems of practical relevance in ML.

Introduction
Spectral information—from weight matrices, kernel matrices, and Hessians—provides a quantitative window
into representation learning and efficient optimization in neural networks (NNs): e.g., eigenvalues in weight
matrices of NNs capture the strength of learned directions, while corresponding eigenvectors indicate which
features in the data are amplified or extracted during training [MM21, MM19, MM20, MPM21, YTH+22,
LM21, WES+23, BES+22, SBL16, GKX19]. A major direction in deep learning theory is to use spectral
tools to understand when a network can propagate, preserve, transform, and amplify task-relevant structure
that may be hidden in high-dimensional datasets.
A concrete and widely used spectral mechanism is the emergence of spikes (outlier eigen/singular values)
and the alignment of their eigen/singular vectors with signal directions in the dataset. Random matrix

This dataset is a balanced four-component Gaussian mixture whose two classes are unions
of opposite components [RGKZ21]. The key property is that XOR is not linearly separable in first-order
statistics: the class means vanish, and any method that only “sees” linear correlations cannot recover labels.
However, XOR is quadratically separable, so a second-order feature can potentially make the task linearly
solvable. Heuristically, the eigenvalues of K describe the spectrum of learned similarities, while the alignment
of leading eigenvectors with task structure (e.g., labels or cluster indicators) may lead to spectral methods
solving the task. Therefore, this paper aims to answer the following spectral question for K:
When do outlier eigenvalues emerge in the CK spectrum, and when do their spiked eigenvectors
align with the XOR labels and enable linear classification?
In practice, NN performance is affected by hyperparameters, dataset structure, and architecture; and,
as a practical matter, machine learning (ML) practitioners typically fiddle (quite aggressively) with various
“knobs” of the ML training process in order to improve model performance. Motivated by this, we consider
properties of the CK matrix for the XOR problem, as various “knobs” for K are varied; and, depending
on their values, we observe very different spectral behavior. Concretely, we vary the following knobs (which
are among the standard parameters that are tuned in ML practice): sampling size scaling n with input
features d and NN width N; signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) in the data X; the pretrained weight matrix W ;
and the choice of nonlinear activation σ. We show that, depending on the values of these “knobs,” one can
obtain qualitatively different spectral properties, various phase transitions, and the emergence of quadratic
informative spikes in the CK matrix. Importantly, the emergence of these quadratically informative spikes
makes the nonlinear XOR linearly classifiable.

on the nonlinear XOR problem. We refer to such spikes as quadratic informative spikes. Addressing these
questions will clarify the regimes in which NNs, and in particular various choices for training knobs of the

1.2
Our Contributions
We develop a RMT framework for the CK that is based on a QE and that captures when nonlinear learnability
emerges for XOR, in regimes where the LE behavior is insufficient. We are particularly interested in this
emergence as a function of the following four “knobs” that are widely-used by ML practitioners.
1. Finite-SNR proportional limit (n ≍d): two negative results. In the proportional regime with
SNR = Θ(1), CK can exhibit outlier eigenvalues, but their eigenvectors do not align with XOR labels;
hence spectral methods with linear readouts on CK features fail to classify XOR (Theorem 3; Figure 1).
We also prove failure for Euclidean distance kernel spectral clustering.
2. Large-SNR proportional limit: emergence of quadratic informative spike. We show that with
increasing SNR scaling and with proper activation choice, a pair of informative outliers separates and
aligns with the XOR class direction, enabling linear spectral clustering/classification (Theorem 5).
3. Pretrained/spiked features: test-time BBP transition. A low-rank perturbation of a random W
can yield the emergence of an outlier whose eigenvector aligns with the induced task direction (Theorem 6)
even for finite-SNR and proportional limit. Then, empirically, we show that linear spectral clustering
occurs for CK matrix with weights from a pretrained NN on CIFAR-2.
4. Quadratic sample-size regime (n ≍d2): a quadratic kernel. We prove an operator-norm reduction
of the CK to a quadratic polynomial kernel in this regime for ultra-wide NNs; with proper activation
choice, the reduced model has an informative outlier direction to recover XOR labels (Theorem 7).

Additional Literature Review
Simplicity bias and low-rank structure.
Recent work suggests that deep networks and transformers
exhibit an implicit distributional simplicity bias, learning simpler (lower-order) statistical structure of the
data earlier in training [BPQ+24, RGLG24]. Complementarily, [HMZ+23] report a low-rank simplicity bias
in deep networks, arguing that depth/over-parameterization can implicitly favor low effective-rank feature
representations.
From a broader perspective, [Wil25] argues that many generalization phenomena often
viewed as “mysterious” in deep learning can be reconciled with classical theory via soft inductive biases.
Spectrum of kernel random matrices.
The study of kernel random matrices and their spectral distri-
butions has become a major topic in RMT. Global convergence of the empirical spectral distribution (ESD)
is known for broad classes of nonlinear kernels [EK10, CS13, DV13]. In the proportional regime, [CS13] and
[DV13] characterize limiting spectra of inner-product kernels. The CK and NTK can be viewed as random
kernels, whose spectra were further studied [FW20, AP20, P´ec19, PW17, DM24, GP26] and connected to
generalization in random feature regression [MM22, AP20] and memorization of spurious feature [BM24] .
Nonlinear spiked covariance matrices.
Spiked models exhibit phase transitions where informative
eigenvalues separate from the bulk [BAP05, P´ec06]. Recent work extends this to nonlinear random matrices,
i.e., matrices whose entries are nonlinear functions of high-dimensional random projections (such as σ(W X)),
leading to nonlinear analogues of BBP transitions and alignment formulas [WZ24, Fel23, BES+23, WWF24,
4

GKK+23, BP22]. Outlier eigen-structure of CK and NTK have been studied [LHY25], using the deterministic
equivalence from [GDZ+22].
Although they can deal with random deep networks and general Gaussian
mixture datasets, their CKs and NTKs are expected kernels (namely first take width N →∞) and only
equivalent to linear kernels.
Polynomial sample-size regimes.
In the proportional regime (n ≍d), LE/Gaussian-equivalence phe-
nomena often imply linear-kernel behavior for broad families of kernels. Moving to polynomial regimes (n ≍
dk), several works show that many kernel matrices behave like polynomial kernels of degree k (with rigorous
deterministic equivalents and sharp asymptotics) [XHM+22, LY25, CM24, MS24]. In the quadratic regime
(k = 2), nonlinear kernels can reduce in operator norm to quadratic polynomial kernels [GMMM19, PWZ24],
enabling classification of quadratically separable data such as XOR [RGKZ21]. [WHL+25] recently proved
a conditional Gaussian equivalence for random feature model at quadratic regime to show the asymptotic
generalization error in this case.
Gaussian mixtures and XOR problem.
Gaussian mixture classification, specifically the XOR mix-
ture has become an important testbed for statistics and ML to evaluate various algorithms.
[RGKZ21]
documented a sharp contrast between trained two-layer networks and kernel methods on such mixtures,
motivating a spectral analysis of when (and why) kernels can succeed. Related RMT analyses for mixture
classification with kernels appear in [LC19, Cou19]. [Gla23] showed how training two layer NNs with SGD
can learn this XOR problem even with a sample complexity ˜O(d). Recently, [TGM25] analyzed continual
learning by studying one-hidden-layer quadratic NNs trained with gradient descent on an XOR dataset.
[DD25] analyzed two-layer networks after one gradient step on W with Gaussian-mixture data using higher-
order polynomial equivalence. This motivates our spiked feature case in Section 4.3, while our result focuses
on label-aligned outliers for linear classification on XOR, unlike trained feature regression tasks in [DD25].

Main Results
In this section, we present our main results on the outlier eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the CK K across
several regimes. In the first three regimes, we consider proportional limit, where d, n, N →∞with fixed
aspect ratios, while varying other “knobs” discussed in Section 1. In particular: Section 4.1 treats the finite
SNR = Θ(1) regime; Section 4.2 analyzes large SNR = Θ(n1/4); and Section 4.3 replaces the random weight
matrix W with a “pretrained” weight matrix modeled as a rank-one perturbation, still with SNR = Θ(1).
Then, in Section 4.4, we consider the quadratic sample-size regime n ≍d2, with finite SNR and random
weights. In each of these cases, we predict the emergence of outlier eigenvalues and whether their associated
eigenvectors enable linear classification.

Conclusion
We have introduced a quadratic equivalent framework and illustrated its use by characterizing when nonlinear
structure becomes spectrally visible in the CK matrix. For the nonlinear XOR problem, we show that in the
proportional limit with finite SNR, the CK can exhibit outliers, but their eigenvectors are asymptotically
unaligned with labels. In contrast, by increasing SNR, sample complexity, or adding pretrained structure
in weight, the quadratic nonlinearity contributes emergence of quadratic informative outliers that enable
linear spectral classification. Our results highlight the complex and subtle relationship between common ML
training “knobs” and the emergence of spikes in the spectrum of ML models. Extending these quadratic
equivalents to more general scalings, deeper networks, and other forms of structured data is an interesting
direction.

CIFAR Pretrained Weights Experiment Details
A recent line of works [MM21, MM19] have tracked the evolution of the Empirical Spectral Distribution
(ESD) of weight matrices throughout the training process of Deep Neural Networks. We focus on two main
phases of ESD during training: the so-called ”Bulk+Spikes” model, and Bulk-Decay model. The emergence
of spikes in the weight spectra is strongly related to the feature-learning ability of neural networks [FCB22],
and suggests the spikes carry task-relevant information.
In order to study the spectra of the CK under pretrained weights, we trained a L = 20-layer fully
connected Neural Network on the CIFAR image dataset, with input dimension d0 = 3072 and hidden
dimensions Nℓ= 512, for ℓ= 1, · · · , L. To extract binary-classification relevant features from the trained
weights, we restrict the CIFAR-10 dataset to two classes. The model was trained using stochastic gradient
descent (SGD) with momentum, with a learning rate lr = 0.05 and batch size b = 128. Our model achieved
approximately 88% accuracy on an independent test set after 40 epochs of training. Figure 9 depicts the
spectra of FC1 layer weights, tracked over each pass of the training dataset. Early phases of training exhibit
the ”Bulk+Spikes” model: the bulk distribution remains close to random MP initialization, with several
outlier spikes beginning to emerge past the right edge of the MP distribution. In later training phases, the
bulk begins to bleed out from the MP distribution, with significant outlier spikes emerging.
Our results empirically suggest that the LE approximation of the CK breaks down even during the early
stages of training. Indeed, XOR becomes almost linearly separable in the CK component space after just one
epoch of training. After further training, a spiked eigenvalue emerges with XOR-label aligned eigenvector.
Figure 10 depicts the fourth leading eigenvector of the CK under weights frozen after each epoch of training,
with a phase transition occurring between Epoch 20 and 30.
The results of these experiments suggests
that under certain training regimes and parameter tuning, there exists a critical phase transition in the
learning process for which the emergence of spikes in weights becomes sufficient for quadratic channels in
the CK to become non-negligible. One possible explanation is that training the neural network induces
spiked eigenvalues with eigenvectors that align with XOR labels, even though the model is not trained on
the XOR problem directly. Theorem 6 models this by artificially introducing a large spike in the weights,
which induces a spiked CK eigenvalue with XOR-label aligned eigenvector.
