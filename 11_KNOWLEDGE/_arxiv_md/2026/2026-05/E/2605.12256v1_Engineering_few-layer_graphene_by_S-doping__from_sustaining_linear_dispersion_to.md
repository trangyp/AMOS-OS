Engineering few-layer graphene by S-doping: from sustaining linear dispersion to flat bands
Armin Sahinovic1 and Rossitza Pentcheva1, ∗
1Department of Physics and Center for Nanointegration (CENIDE),
Universität Duisburg-Essen, Lotharstr. 1, 47057 Duisburg, Germany
(Dated: May 13, 2026)
Motivated by the technological relevance of S-doped few-layer graphene (FLG) in battery applications and
in the oxygen reduction reaction, we systematically explore the effect of basal plane S-doping on the elec-
tronic properties of mono-, bi-, and four-layer graphene, using first-principles calculations with van der Waals
corrections. In the monolayer we find a variety of effects ranging from a sustained Dirac cone with localized
impurity bands away from the Fermi level in thiophenic doping (2V1S) to a band gap opening of 0.4 eV and
flat bands close to the Fermi-level in graphitic doping (1V1S) and an additional n-type doping together with
spin-polarization, when three S-atoms are adsorbed in a four-site vacancy (4V3S). Incorporation in FLG leads to
modification of the Dirac cone into a set of hyperbolic touching bands in 2V1S; reduction (bilayer) and closing
of the band gap with additional hyperbolic touching bands in conjunction with the flat band at the Fermi level in
1V1S and 4V3S and a reduction of spin polarization in the latter. Overall, S-doping enables design of the band
structure and tuning the electronic behavior of FLG from metallic to insulating and from linear dispersive to flat
bands that makes S-doped FLG a promising material for versatile technological applications.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Since the initial discovery of graphene [1] a substantial ef-
fort has focused on tailoring the exceptional properties of 2D
materials for device applications [2] by defects, doping, ad-
sorbates, interaction with the substrate or application of a gate
voltage [3, 4]. Vacancies may occur naturally, or be artifi-
cially introduced using chemical processing or electron, ion
and laser irradiation [5–12]. Unsaturated bonds either at edge
sites or at vacancies promote different incorporation mecha-
nisms into the graphene structure. For example, N-dopants
are preferentially found in a pyridinic and pyrrolic configura-
tion [13]. Intensive research has been focused on dopants like
nitrogen or boron that modify the number of charge carriers
leading to n- and p-doping, respectively [4, 14, 15].
In contrast to N or B doping, the electronic structure of S-
doped graphene is much less explored despite its technolog-
ical relevance for applications in energy storage such as su-
percapacitors and batteries [16–21] or as a metal-free-cathode
catalyst in the oxygen reduction reaction [22–27]. Moreover,
it was shown that S-doping promotes the stronger adsorption
of Pt in graphene [24], improving the ORR activity compared
to commercially available Pt/C cathodes [24, 28]. S-doping
introduces two additional electrons and has a similar electron-
negativity compared C [24, 27, 29], which is expected to lead
to less localized effects compared to N-doping, introducing
one additional electron [30]. Experimental results indicate
incorporation of S-dopants in the basal plane [22, 31] and a
homogeneous distribution in the graphene sheet [32]. Differ-
ent basal-plane incorporation geometries were proposed, e.g.,
graphitic or thiophenic [21, 22, 24, 31–33], the latter is con-
sidered promising for catalytic activity [23]. The different
dopant configurations can have distinct effect on the electronic
and transport properties requiring a detailed understanding of
their influence. A further unsettled question refers to the role

of S in the emergence or quenching of spin-polarization in
graphene: while some studies report spin-polarization in S-
doped graphene [29], others suggest that S quenches the spin
polarization of defective graphene [34].
Scalable plasma-based growth [32, 35, 36] or liquid exfo-
liation [37] often leads to few-layer graphene for applications
in opto-electronics and energy storage devices [38–40]. Mul-
tilayer graphene, in particular twisted [41–43] and rhombo-
hedrally stacked [44, 45] has attracted a lot of interest due to
the correlated physics and emergence of flat bands and van
Hove singularities. Bernal-stacked multilayer graphene ex-
hibits touching hyperbolic bands at the Fermi level [40, 46–
52] and can also host a broad set of intriguing phases, e.g., as
a bilayer with applied displacement electric field [53–55].
While the effect of doping and defects has been studied
thoroughly in single layer graphene for a series of dopants [4,
56–63], it is unclear how the effect of S-doping is modified by
the few-layer structure used in scalable device fabrication, and
how the interlayer interactions influence the electronic prop-
erties. To gain insight into the mechanism of S-incorporation
and its influence on the electronic properties of monolayer,
bilayer and 4L-FLG, we carried out density functional theory
(DFT) calculations including van der Waals corrections. As a
starting point we address the energetic stability and electronic
properties of vacancies in graphene and FLG (Section III).
Subsequently, the stability and structure of a wide range of
S-doping configurations and an S-concentration varying be-
tween 2.00-6.25 % in the monolayer, bilayer and 4L-FLG
is explored in Section IV. Lastly, we analyze the electronic
structure of the S-doped monolayer and multilayer graphene
in Section V and demonstrate a wide set of intriguing effects,
ranging from preserved Dirac cones, e.g., for thiophenic in-
corporation to Dirac-cone splitting and band gap opening in
conjunction with the emergence of flat bands for graphitic
incorporation. Moreover, spin polarization is predicted for
S-doping in more extended vacancies with important distinc-
tions concerning spatial localization to the one emerging due
to vacancies.

one to superimpose the graphene/graphite-like band structure

II.
METHODS AND COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS
We performed first-principles simulations on pristine and
sulfur-doped graphene and FLG with the projected augmented
wave (PAW) method in the framework of the Vienna Ab initio
Simulation Package (VASP) [64, 65], using the generalized
gradient approximation as parameterized by Perdew, Burke,
and Ernzerhof [66].
For accurate description of the long-
range van der Waals interactions we use the Grimme DFT-
D3 correction with Becke-Johnson damping [67, 68]. The
wave functions were expanded into plane waves up to a cut-
off energy of 400 eV. A Gaussian smearing of 0.075 eV was
applied to the Kohn-Sham states. The density of states was
calculated using the tetrahedron method with Blöchl correc-
tions [69]. To minimize the interaction of dopants in neigh-
boring unit cells and avoid the downfolding of K and K’ to
Γ [14] we considered a 5 × 5 supercell for the monolayer and
multilayer graphene systems. The integration over the Bril-
louin zone was performed using a 5 × 5 × 1 and a 24 × 24 × 1
Γ-centered ⃗k-point grid for relaxation and density of states,
respectively. The systems were separated by 20 Å of vacuum
to avoid spurious interaction with their periodic images and
the positions of all atoms were fully relaxed until the forces
were below 0.01 eVÅ−1. Band unfolding into the primitive
cell of graphene was carried out using the method introduced
by Popescu and Zunger as implemented in VASPKIT [70–72].
Besides graphitic incorporation, we have explored addi-
tional binding configurations in defective graphene which we
denote by NVVNSS, where NV and NS represent the number
of C-vacancies and S-dopants incorporated in the vacancy, re-
spectively. The vacancy formation energy is defined as:

III.
VACANCIES IN MONOLAYER AND FEW-LAYER
GRAPHENE
Prior to exploring S-doping, we address the properties of
different vacancies in graphene. The formation of a mono-
vacancy (1V) is associated with a high energy cost of Ef =
7.49 eV (cf. Fig. 1), close to the experimental value of 7.3±1
eV in graphite [74]. Previous theoretical results range be-
tween 7 and 8 eV, depending on the method used [75–77].
Interestingly, we find only a minor enhancement of the va-
cancy formation energy in few-layer graphene: 7.73 eV/ 7.87
eV in the surface/subsurface layer of 4L-FLG, respectively.
Top views of the relaxed structures are shown in Fig. 1a.
A significant distortion in 1V lowers the symmetry from D3h
to C2v forming a 5-9 ring defect [78].The corresponding band
structure of 1V in the monolayer is displayed in Fig. 1d. The
Dirac cone is split, opening a band gap of 0.34 eV. The valence
band (VB) of predominantly C pπ origin crosses the Fermi
level around K. Its maximum (VBM) lies 0.52 eV above the
Fermi level, indicating pronounced p-type doping related to
the two-electron deficiency due to the missing C. The valence
and conduction bands show nearly linear dispersion close to
K and flatten towards Γ and M. Away from K the VB exhibits
a stronger spin splitting with the minority/majority spin band
at/0.1 eV below the Fermi level. Additionally, two pairs of
flat bands of C pσ-orbital character emerge, an occupied one
at −0.62 eV and an empty one at 3.4 eV in the majority spin
channel and two empty ones at 1.6 eV and 3.5 eV (not shown
here) in the minority spin channel. The splitting of the pσ-
bands has been attributed to a Jahn-Teller effect [79–81].
As a result of the exchange splitting of bands, the single
vacancy exhibits a substantial spin-polarization with a total
magnetic moment of 1.5 µB, in agreement with previous re-
sults [78, 79, 82, 83]. The largest contribution to the spin den-
sity of 1V, shown in Fig. 1a, is localized at the exposed carbon
atom in the 9-ring and has an in-plane pπ-orbital character, in-
dicative of a dangling bond, with smaller contribution of the
remaining C-sites surrounding the vacancy. The spin polariza-
tion of the single vacancy is interesting in view of spintronics
applications, e.g., the pπ-band occupation and thus the mag-
netism can be tuned using a gate voltage [84] or a scanning
tunneling microscope tip [82, 85].
Before discussing the 1V incorporated in the multilayer, we
elaborate briefly on the main features of the undoped graphene
multilayer band structure. The ones of the undoped bilayer
and 4L-FLG are displayed in Fig. 5 in Appendix A. The inter-
layer coupling and the altered vertical pπ bonds lead to a mod-
ification of the linear dispersion of the Dirac-cone into two
(four) pairs of valence and conduction hyperbolic bands, one
pair (two pairs) touching at the Fermi-level and the remain-
ing split apart in the bilayer (4L-FLG). For further discussion
of the band structure of multilayer Bernal stacked graphene,
see [46]. The splitting and dispersion varies with the layer
number N and position in the layer stacking sequence, where
surface layers exhibit a smaller energy splitting.
We find a strong modification of the 1V bilayer band struc-
ture, presented in Fig. 1e. Two pairs of hyperbolic bands are
separated by a band gap of 0.42 eV. The minima of the two

S-atom intercalated between the sheets of 4L-FLG is 2.08 eV.

VI.
SUMMARY
We present a systematic investigation of the effect of S-
doping on the structure, stability and electronic properties of
mono-, bi-, and four-layer graphene using density functional
theory including van der Waals corrections.
As a starting
point, we considered the properties of vacancies in single and
FL graphene. We found that the formation energy per site is
reduced from 7.49 eV (1V) to 3.32 eV (2V) and, finally, to
2.65 eV (5V), making the formation of larger vacancies fea-
sible. Moreover, spin polarization of 1.0-1.5 µB was found in
1V, 3V and 5V localized at the sites with unsaturated bonds
and their neighbors, in contrast to 2V and 4V due to saturated
dangling bonds that also leaf to a higher stability.
A variety of doping configurations was considered includ-
ing graphitic, thiophenic, as well as incorporation in larger va-
cancy clusters. We showcase here the most stable ones, 1V1S,
2V1S and 4V3S, which exhibit distinct features in the band
structure.
Except for 2V1S, we find a strong out-of-plane
relaxation of the dopant that impacts the electronic proper-
ties. For example, while a strong n-doping doping of the split
Dirac-cone is found for the metastable planar 1V1S config-
uration, the band structure of the 2.09 eV more stable non-
planar graphitic 1V1S features a split Dirac-cone with a band
gap of 0.4 eV, in conjunction with a flat band of S- and C-
pπ character at the Fermi-level. The p-type doping and the
spin-polarization of the 1V vacancy are quenched due to the
additional charge carriers introduced by S, that saturate the
dangling bond.
A similar band structure with a split Dirac cone and ad-
ditional nearly flat bands around the Fermi level with sub-
stantial S pσ and neighboring C pπ contribution is found in
4V3S. Most importantly, the low-dispersion bands at EF show
n-type doping and a notable exchange splitting, leading to a


## Page 8


8
magnetic moment of 0.99 µB and a spin-density that is much
more delocalized than the one in vacancies.
Finally, thiophenic 2V1S represents a configuration where
the Dirac cone is sustained. Additional empty and occupied
impurity flat bands emerge, contributed by S and the neigh-
boring C, respectively. Here the S-dopant renders two pπ elec-
trons to graphene, whereas the remaining four electrons form
a tetravalent bond with four neighboring C.
A central aspect of our study is to explore how the proper-
ties of the defective monolayer are altered in the multilayer.
For 1V instead of the split Dirac cone of the monolayer, two
pairs of hyperbolic bands with VBM and CBM shifted away
from K emerge in the bilayer, whereas 4L-FLG features four
pairs of hyperbolic bands, one touching at EF. Moreover, the
top valence band becomes flatter and the p-type doping is re-
duced with increasing thickness. Similarly, a reduction of the
band gap with a smaller shift of the conduction band minima
away from K is observed in the 1V1S bilayer and a closing
of the band gap in 4L-FLG. Concomitantly, the dispersion of
the flat band at the Fermi level is further reduced in 4L-FLG.
Remarkably, in the 4V3S bilayer a Dirac-cone with linear dis-
persion originating from the intact graphene layer is overlaid
on the band structure of the the doped layer. For 4V3S in 4L-
FLG, the undoped layers renders hyperbolic touching bands
that show n-doping. This is coupled with a reduced exchange
splitting of the flat bands that decreases the magnetic moment
to 0.39 µB and 0.27 µB in surface and subsurface doping,
respectively. Finally, in 2V1S the Dirac-cone is altered into
two/four pairs of hyperbolic bands pairs, one/two touching at
EF, reminiscent of undoped few-layer graphene [46], while
the splitting of the occupied and empty impurity flat bands is
reduced due to the screening.
Overall, our results demonstrate that a wide range of
features can be realized in the band structure of S-doped
graphene depending on the configuration, ranging from sus-
tained Dirac cone (2V1S) to split Dirac cone in conjunc-
tion with the emergence of flat bands at the Fermi level in
graphitic 1V1S that are also exchange split in 4V3S. In par-
ticular, the emergence of flat bands in S-doped graphene is
reminiscent of similar features in twisted bilayer and rhom-
bohedral graphene [42, 44], as well as Bernal-stacked bilayer
graphene manipulated by a displacement field [53] which ren-
der a variety of intriguing correlated phenomena. The broad
variation and tunability of the electronic properties of S-doped
graphene allows to design the band structure by selective in-
corporation of S and has potential for a wide array of applica-
tions in catalysis, energy storage and spintronics.
