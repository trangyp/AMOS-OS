Abstract
For a finite group Γ, acting on a finite group G, we find necessary conditions for
which the first Γ0-equivariant Hochschild cohomology of the group algebra kG
is non-trivial, where k is a field of characteristic p dividing the order of G and
Γ0 is the stabilizer subgroup in Γ of some element in G. For any field k we show
that the Γ-equivariant Hochschild cohomology of Γ-algebras with coefficients in
a Γ-equivariant bimodule [1] is isomorphic with some kΓ-relative Ext, in the
context of relative homological algebra.
Keywords: group, Hochschild, cohomology, equivariant, algebra
MSC Classification: 16E40 , 20J06
1 Introduction
When there is a group action on an algebra, this algebra is not studied in isolation
but together with symmetries and then equivariant Hochschild cohomology (homol-
ogy) shows up. There is a recent growing interest in studying equivariant Hochschild
(co)homology [2], [3], [4].

Let Γ be a finite group and G be a finite Γ-group, which is a group (G, ·)
endowed with a Γ-action by group automorphisms. This means that there is a group
homomorphism Γ →Aut(G, ·) defining the Γ-action, that is denoted by
Γ × G →G, (σ, g) 7→σg,
satisfying some well-known axioms. Sometimes, like in [5], these groups are called
”groups with operators”. Let k be a field and n ∈Z, n ≥0. By a Γ-equivariant kG-
module N we understand a kG-module that is also a kΓ-module and the action of G
and Γ are related to each other by the equality
σ(gm) = σg(σm),
for any σ ∈Γ, g ∈G, m ∈N. A structure of Γ-equivariant kG-module on N is the
same as a structure of left k(G ⋊Γ)-module on N, where G ⋊Γ is the semi-direct
product of G by Γ, see [5].
In Subsection 2.1 we revisit equivariant group cohomology following [6, Section
1]. For N a Γ-equivariant kG-module we denote by Hn
Γ(G, N) the Γ-equivariant n-
th cohomology of G with coefficients in N. Inspired by [7, Section 2], we present
in Subsection 2.2 a definition of Γ-equivariant Hochschild cohomology. Let A be a
finite dimensional Γ-algebra (see the first paragraphs of Subsection 2.2) and let M
be a Γ-equivariant (A −A)-bimodule. Equivalently, M is an (A −A)-bimodule such
that the linear action of Γ on M is compatible with the left, respectively right, A-
module structure of M. The Γ-equivariant n-th Hochschild cohomology will be denoted
by HHn
Γ(A, M). Historically, it seems that this variant of Γ-equivariant Hochschild
cohomology of an algebra with coefficients in a bimodule was previously defined by K.
K. Jensen in the context of Banach algebras, [1, Definition I.1.7]. In a recent article
[2], the authors develop another variant still called equivariant Hochschild cohomology
which, in the above context, is denoted by H∗
Γ(A, M). This is the homology of the
total complex of a bicomplex, obtained by mixing the Hochschild cochain complex
and the cochain complex of the Γ-group cohomology [2, Definition 5]. The objective
of Subsection 2.3 is to recall some basic aspects of the relative homological algebra,
which shall be need it for proving the second main result of this paper.
The first Hochschild cohomology of an algebra is the same as the space of outer
derivations on this algebra. Studying the triviality, or non-triviality, of outer deriva-
tions on algebraic structures is an important topic in a variety of areas: derivations
on Lie algebras [8], derivations on group algebras [9], derivations on block algebras of
finite groups [10, Section 7], [11], etc. Let x ∈G and denote by Γx the stabilizer sub-
group of x in Γ. In the first main result we study the above problem for the space of
Γx-equivariant outer derivations of the group algebra kG. In Section 3, as a particu-
lar case of Proposition 4 we obtain a k-linear injective map from H1
Γx(CG(x), k) into
the first Γx-equivariant Hochschild cohomology of kG. Using this, in the first main
theorem of this paper we obtain some necessary conditions for the non-triviality of
the first Γx-equivariant Hochschild cohomology of kG. In Section 4 we introduce these
conditions, by defining CΓ,G(p)-good elements (Definition 1), which are applied for
a finite Γ-group G, when p divides |G| and k is a field of characteristic p. We begin
