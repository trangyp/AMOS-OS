PIERO GIACOMELLI
Abstract. We introduce expander evolution algebras (EEAs), a
class of nonassociative algebras defined over an arbitrary field K
in which the underlying undirected loopless graph of the algebra—
in the sense of Kowalski—is an expander graph in the classical
sense of Cheeger. Starting from the formal graph definition of
Kowalski and the algebraic framework of Tian, we establish a
dictionary between combinatorial expansion and algebraic structure:
the Cheeger constant of the associated graph governs connectivity,
the subalgebra lattice, the growth of the evolution sequence, and—
over R and C—the spectral gap of the evolution operator. Over a
general field K we prove that EEAs are always connected and simple
(as evolution algebras), carry no proper large evolution subalgebras,
and that every generator of a symmetric EEA is algebraically
persistent. Over C we obtain the sharp Alon–Boppana lower bound
for the second eigenvalue of the evolution operator, leading to the
definition of Ramanujan evolution algebras as optimal expanders.
We also construct families of EEAs from Cayley graphs of finite
groups. We close with open problems.
1. Introduction
Evolution algebras, introduced by Tian and Vojtechovsky [15] and
developed systematically in the monograph [14], are nonassociative,
commutative, and non-power-associative algebras over a field K. Their
defining feature is a distinguished natural basis {e1, . . . , en} in which
distinct basis elements multiply to zero, while e2

tural constants aik ∈K. Behind this rule lies varied structure: discrete
dynamical systems, Markov chains, non-Mendelian genetics, and graph
combinatorics all admit natural descriptions within this framework.
Expander graphs, in contrast, are finite graphs that are simultane-
ously sparse (bounded degree) and highly connected (large Cheeger
constant). Introduced implicitly by Pinsker [13] and formalized by Alon
and Milman [1], they have become a central object in combinatorics,
computer science, and number theory; see Kowalski’s comprehensive
treatment [8].

The present paper asks: What algebraic properties does an evolution
algebra inherit when its associated graph is an expander? The answer
turns out to connect expansion to connectivity, the subalgebra lattice,
evolution dynamics, and the spectral theory of the algebra.
Main contributions.
(1) We define the underlying undirected loopless graph Γ(E, B) of

4. Structural Properties of EEAs
Section 4 develops the structural consequences of the expansion hy-
pothesis introduced in Section 3. The guiding question is the following:
what does it mean, algebraically, for an evolution algebra to satisfy a
uniform isoperimetric inequality on its underlying undirected graph?
The expansion condition h(E, B) ≥h > 0 is intrinsically combinatorial;
it bounds, from below, the ratio |E(W)|/|W| over every admissible
subset W of natural-basis indices. In an evolution algebra, however,
subsets of indices correspond to evolution subalgebras, plenary powers
act on the basis through neighbourhood enlargement, and the persis-
tency dichotomy of Tian [14] classifies generators according to their
long-time return behaviour. The aim of this section is to transport the
combinatorial content of the expansion inequality into each of these
algebraic settings, producing four structural conclusions: a quantita-
tive impossibility of small almost-isolated subalgebras and of small
direct summands, a logarithmic diameter bound, a near-exponential
support growth law for plenary powers, and a triviality theorem for the
hierarchical decomposition in the symmetric case.
The first observation is that the very definition of h(E, B) already
constrains the lattice of evolution subalgebras generated by subsets of
the natural basis. The minimum defining the expansion constant ranges
over every nonempty S ⊆{1, . . . , n} with |S| ≤n/2; consequently,
for every evolution subalgebra E′ = spanK{ei : i ∈S} of dimension
m ≤n/2, the boundary E(S) in Γ(E, B) contains at least h · m edges.
Each such edge is a pair {i, j} with i ∈S and j /∈S, and at least one
of the structural constants aij or aji is nonzero. Two consequences
are immediate. First, no proper evolution subalgebra of dimension
at most n/2 can be isolated from its complement: it must be linked
to it through at least hm nonzero structural constants. Second, the
algebra cannot decompose as an internal direct sum E = E1 ⊕E2 with
dim E1 ≤n/2, since such a decomposition would force the boundary
edge set to be empty. These two facts should be read as structural

Theorem 6.3 (Simplicity and irreducibility). Let E be a Markov h-

(i) The Markov chain with transition matrix A is irreducible.
(ii) E is a simple evolution algebra.

11. Open Problems
We close with problems that remain open.
Open Problem 1 (Algebraic characterisation of EEAs over finite
fields). For a field K = Fq (finite field with q elements), characterise
the h-EEAs over K purely in terms of the algebra structure (without
reference to real eigenvalues). In particular, does the combinatorial
expansion condition h(E, B) ≥h have an algebraic analogue in the
representation theory of E over Fq?
Open Problem 2 (Non-symmetric EEAs and algebraic persistency).
Theorem 4.5 requires the symmetry assumption aij = aji. Is algebraic
persistency equivalent to some directed expansion condition on D(E, B)?
More precisely: is there a directed analogue of the Cheeger constant
for evolution algebras such that the directed expansion condition is
equivalent to algebraic persistency?
Open Problem 3 (Expansion constant as algebraic invariant). Theo-
rem 3.10 shows that for nonsingular EEAs, the isomorphism class of
Γ(E, B) is an algebraic invariant. Is the Cheeger constant h(E, B) itself
an algebraic invariant (not just the graph up to isomorphism)? Is there
an algebraic formula for h in terms of the structural matrix A?
Open Problem 4 (Ihara–Selberg zeta function of EEAs). Tian [14,
Section 6.2.3] notes a connection between evolution algebras and the
Ihara–Selberg zeta function. For an EEA E, the Ihara zeta function
Z(E, u) = Q
[C](1 −u|C|)−1 (product over prime cycles in Γ(E, B)) is
known to satisfy a Riemann Hypothesis if E is a Ramanujan evolution
algebra. Develop the analogy between the Riemann Hypothesis for
EEAs and the classical Riemann Hypothesis.
Open Problem 5 (EEAs and 3-manifolds). Following Tian’s pro-
gramme [14, Section 6.2.6], a triangulation of a 3-manifold M defines
an evolution algebra A(M, t). When is the family (A(M, tk)) of evo-
lution algebras (over successive barycentric subdivisions tk) an EEA
family? Is this related to geometric or topological properties of M (e.g.,
hyperbolicity)?
Open Problem 6 (Automorphisms and symmetry of EEAs). For a
Ramanujan evolution algebra E, compute the automorphism group
Aut(E) (as described by Elduque–Labra [7]). Are Ramanujan EEAs
more “rigid” (smaller automorphism group) than non-Ramanujan EEAs
of the same dimension and degree?
