EXAMPLES OF INFINITELY MANY (−1)-CURVES WITH HIGHER
GENUS ON ELLIPTIC SURFACES
SICHEN LI AND JIHAO LIU
Abstract. In this article, we construct the first example of an elliptic surface with
infinitely many smooth (−1)-curves of genus g > 1, settling an open question of Bauer
et al. [Duke Math. J. 162 (10) (2013), 1877-1894].
1. Introduction
It is well known that there exist infinitely many (−1)-curves of genus zero on the
rational surface obtained by blowing up P2 at nine points in very general position. Along
similar lines, Bauer et al. proved that there exist surfaces with infinitely many negative
curves of any given negative self-intersection, as follows.
Theorem 1.1. [Bauer et al. 13, Theorems 4.1 and 4.3] For (i) each m > 0 and g = 0
or (ii) each m > 1 and each g ≥0, there are smooth projective surfaces with infinitely
many (−m)-curves with genus g.
The case of m = 1 and g > 1 is left open by Theorem 1.1. More precisely, Bauer et al.
raised the following question, which has been open for more than a decade.
Question 1.2.
[Bauer et al. 13, Question 4.4]
Is there, for each g > 1, a smooth
projective surface with infinitely many (−1)-curves of genus g?
As a first step towards Question 1.2, the first author showed in [Li19, Proposition
2.5] that it has an affirmative answer for an elliptic surface X, provided that X has
infinitely many sections and every pseudoeffective divisor on X admits an integral Zariski
decomposition. Moreover, as shown in [Li26, Proposition 4.1], to answer Question 1.2, it
suffices to construct an example of an elliptic surface X over a smooth projective curve
B of genus g > 1 such that X has a non-torsion section and χ(OX) = 1 as follows.
Question 1.3. [Li26, Question 4.2] For each g > 1, is there an example of an elliptic
surface X over a smooth projective curve B of genus g such that X has a non-torsion
section and χ(OX) = 1?

In this article, we give a complete affirmative answer to Question 1.3 by constructing
the first such example. We thus prove the following theorem.
Theorem 1.4. There exists an elliptic surface X with infinitely many (−1)-curves of
genus g > 1.
Remark 1.5. The surface X is given by an explicit Weierstrass equation over an explicit
hyperelliptic curve B of genus g; see Construction 3.1. The point of the construction is to
keep χ(OX) = 1 while raising the genus of the base, and to produce a section whose non-
torsionness can be checked on a single fiber by the Lutz-Nagell theorem [Lut37,Nag35].
As observed by Yi Gu, our elliptic surface is non-isotrivial, since the j-invariant of the
generic fiber is a non-constant function of the parameter t.

2. Preliminaries
Notation and Terminology. Let X be a smooth projective surface over C.
• By a curve on X, we mean a reduced and irreducible curve.
• A negative curve on X is a curve with negative self-intersection.
• A (−k)-curve on X is a curve C with C2 = −k < 0.
In this section, we collect some standard facts about elliptic surfaces from [SS19, Chap-
ter 5].
Definition 2.1. [SS19, Theorem 5.19] Let E be an elliptic curve over the function field
K of a smooth projective curve C. Then there exists an elliptic surface S over C whose
generic fiber is isomorphic to E/K. In this case, we say that S is the Kodaira-Néron
model of E/K. We also call S the elliptic surface associated with E/K.
Proposition 2.2.
[SS19, Proposition 5.4] The global sections of f : S →C are in a
natural one-to-one correspondence with the K-rational points of E.

Lemma 3.1. Let X be as in Construction 3.1. Then there exists a relatively minimal
elliptic fibration f : X →B whose singular fibers are exactly twelve fibers of Kodaira type
I1. Moreover χ(OX) = 1.
Proof. The discriminant of the Weierstrass equation is
∆= −16 (4(t2 + 1)3 + 27) = −16 D(t) ∈H0(B, L⊗12).
We have D′(t) = 24 t(t2 + 1)2, while D(0) = 31 and D(±i) = 27; hence D and D′ have
no common root, so the six roots of D are simple. The branch points of π : B →P1 are
λ1, . . . , λ2g+1 and ∞. By the choice of the λi, no root of D is a branch point, so each
of the six simple roots of D has two distinct, unramified preimages on B. Thus ∆has
twelve simple zeros on the affine part t ̸= ∞. As deg L⊗12 = 12, these account for all the
zeros of ∆; in particular ∆(∞) ̸= 0. Therefore the singular fibers Fi of f are exactly the
twelve fibers of type I1 over these twelve points, i = 1, · · · , 12. The data (L, a4, a6) is
minimal: a6 = 1 vanishes only at ∞, where a4 = t2 + 1 does not vanish. Thus, a4 and
a6 never vanish to orders at least 4 and 6 respectively. By Remark 2.4, f : X →B is a
relatively minimal elliptic fibration. For each singular fiber Fi (i = 1, · · · , 12), the Euler
number e(Fi) = 1. Therefore, χ(OX) = 1 by the Noether formula (cf. [SS19, Corollary
5.50]).
