Weight of the De Rham-Betti Structures of Abelian Varieties
Zekun Ji
Abstract
In this note, we prove that for any abelian variety A defined over Q, its de Rham-Betti (dRB)
group GdRB(A) necessarily contains the group of homotheties in GL(H1
B(A, Q)). Consequently,
this rules out the existence of non-zero dRB classes in odd-degree cohomology groups of abelian
varieties over Q.

Unlike the case of Hodge structures, where the notion of weight is built into the definitions, the
general formalism of de Rham-Betti structures and groups lacks such a notion (see [8, p. 3] for a
more detailed explanation). This is the main obstruction to directly extending [3, Theorem 4.1]
to odd-degree cohomology groups of abelian varieties. The main goal of this note is to show the
following property of the dRB groups of abelian varieties, which is a generalization of [6, Proposition
4.7].
Theorem 1.4. [=Theorem 4.2] For any abelian variety A defined over Q, its de Rham-Betti group
GdRB(A) contains the group of homotheties in GL(H1
B(A, Q)).
Combining this theorem with the Kuga-Satake correspondence (see, for example, [1, Proposition
6.4.3] and [8, Theorem 6.1]), we have the following observation.
Corollary 1.5 (=Corollary 4.3). Let X be a hyper-K¨ahler variety defined over Q. Then the dRB
group of H2
dRB(X, Q) contains the subgroup of homotheties in GL(H2

The proof of Theorem 1.4 uses the interaction between the de Rham-Betti group and the
Mumford-Tate group of an abelian variety (see Section 2 for a summary of properties of these
two groups) as well as properties of CM-fields.

De Rham-Betti Groups and Mumford-Tate Groups
In this section, we summarize some properties of de Rham-Betti groups and Mumford-Tate groups.
For de Rham-Betti theory, more details can be found in [2, Section 7.5.3], [3] and [8, Section 2].
For the theory of Mumford-Tate groups, more details can be found in [13].
A de Rham-Betti structure is a triple of the form (VdR, VB, ρm) such that VB is a finite-
dimensional Q-vector space, VdR is a finite-dimensional Q-vector space, and ρm is an isomorphism
of C-vector spaces ρm : VB ⊗Q C ∼= VdR ⊗Q C. An element α ∈VB is a de Rham-Betti class if
ρm(α ⊗1) ∈VdR ⊗1. In the sequel, we sometimes abbreviate the adjective ‘de Rham-Betti’ as
‘dRB’. Given a one-dimensional dRB structure (VdR, VB, ρm), if we can find a Q-basis for VB and
a Q-basis for VdR such that ρm is scalar multiplication by (2π√−1)k, then we denote this dRB
structure by QdRB(k).

In [2, Section 7.1.6] and [8, Section 2], it is explained that the category of all dRB structures,
denoted by CdRB, has a natural tensor structure. Together with the forgetful functor ωB to the
category of Q-vector spaces which remembers only VB of a dRB structure, it forms a neutral
Tannakian category.
Given a specific dRB structure V , we denote the Tannakian category generated by V in CdRB
by ⟨V ⟩⊗. Then the de Rham-Betti group of V is defined as Aut(ωB|⟨V ⟩⊗) (see [4, Page 20] for a
definition). We denote it by GdRB(V ). It is an algebraic subgroup of GL(VB).

Remark 2.3. The first property follows from the Tannakian formalism of dRB structures while
the second is a corollary of the deep “Analytic Subgroup Theorem” of W¨ustholtz ([16]). See [8,
Section 5] for a more detailed exposition.
Another key property concerning the dRB group of an abelian variety is the following, which is
also a corollary of the Analytic Subgroup Theorem.
Theorem 2.4 ([3], Theorem 3.8). Given an abelian variety A defined over Q, we have that
EnddRB(H1(A, Q)) = EndHdg(H1(A, Q)) ∼= End◦(A).
The Mumford-Tate group is the fundamental symmetry group associated with a Hodge structure.
We refer the reader to [13] for more details.
Given a Q-Hodge structure V of weight k, i.e.,
V ⊗Q C = L
p+q=k V p,q such that V p,q = V q,p, one can associate V with an R-representation of
the Deligne torus µ : S := ResC/RGm →GL(VR) which is defined as follows. Given z ∈S(R) = C×,
its image under µ acts via scalar multiplication by z−pz−q on the summand V p,q.
The Mumford-Tate group MT(V ) of a Q-Hodge structure V is the smallest Q-algebraic subgroup
of GL(V ) whose set of R-points contains the image of µ. The Hodge group of V , denoted by Hdg(V ),
is the smallest Q-algebraic subgroup of GL(V ) whose set of R-points contains the image of the set
{z ∈C×|zz = 1} under µ. These definitions yield the following well-known lemma.
Lemma 2.5. Let V be a Hodge structure of weight k where k ̸= 0. Then MT(V ) contains the
subgroup of homotheties in GL(V ). Moreover, there is an isogeny Φ : Gm × Hdg(V ) →MT(V )
which takes (t, g) ∈Gm × Hdg(V ) to t−1g ∈MT(V ).

Proof of the Theorem
Certain properties of simple abelian varieties of type IV are needed. Recall that an abelian variety
A is of type IV if its endomorphism algebra R = End◦(A) is a type IV division algebra (see [14,
Ch. 19] for the precise definition); in particular, its centre E is a CM-field. Let V = H1(A, Q)
and fix a polarization form ϕ. Then the associated Rosati involution † on R satisfies ϕ(f(v), w) =
ϕ(v, f †(w)). Crucially, for abelian varieties of type IV, the restriction of † to E coincides with
complex conjugation on the CM-field E [14, pp. 201-202].
Lemma 4.1. Assuming the above setup and notation, we have Z(Hdg(A)) ⊂UE.
Proof. This is well known.
We include the proof for completeness of exposition.

Hdg(A), hence Z(Hdg(A)) is naturally a subgroup of Z(R)× = E×. Now note that for any element
g ∈Z(Hdg(A)) and any v, v′ ∈V , the polarization form ϕ satisfies
