Worst-Case Sample Complexity Bounds for
Distributed Inner Product Estimation with Local Randomized Measurements
Zhenyuan Huang,1 Kun Wang,1, ∗and Ping Xu1, †
1College of Computer Science and Technology, National University of Defense Technology, Changsha 410073, China
(Dated: May 15, 2026)
We study distributed inner product estimation for n-qubit states using local randomized measure-
ments, for which rigorous worst-case guarantees are less understood. We first reduce the minimax
kernel optimization to Hamming-distance kernels. Within this class, unbiasedness fixes a unique
kernel. For this kernel under local Clifford sampling, we prove a sharp fourth-moment bound using
the single-qubit Clifford commutant. This yields worst-case sample complexity O(
√
4.5n), attained
by identical pure product stabilizer states. For the same kernel under local Haar sampling, we prove
a local twirling identity that compares its fourth moment with the Clifford fourth moment. This
gives the same rigorous upper bound as in the Clifford case, but the comparison is lossy. This mo-
tivates the conjectured sharper Haar scaling O(
√
3.6n) attained by product states, and verify it for
several important classes of states. We also show that independent single-qubit Pauli shadows have
worst-case scaling O(
√
7.5n) for large n.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Distributed inner product estimation (DIPE) [1] asks
two remote parties, given copies of unknown n-qubit
states ρ and σ, to estimate the Hilbert–Schmidt inner
product Tr [ρσ] using only local operations and classi-
cal communication (LOCC). This task lies at the core of
cross-platform verification, distributed benchmarking,
and certification of quantum devices prepared on sepa-
rate platforms [2–4], and has motivated extensive theo-
retical and experimental work [5–13]. The optimal sam-
ple complexity of DIPE was established in [14], where
it is achieved by a protocol based on global random-
ized measurements [15]. The problem addressed in this
work is to establish rigorous worst-case sample com-
plexity bounds for experimentally feasible DIPE proto-
cols based on local randomized measurements (LRM).
LRM is a practically significant variant of the ran-
domized measurement toolbox [15]: one samples an
n-qubit unitary U = Nn
ℓ=1 Uℓin product form from
single-qubit ensembles and then performs computa-
tional basis measurements [1, 16]. This differs signifi-
cantly from global randomized measurements, where
one samples a single n-qubit unitary, which may be
highly entangling across the full system. LRM is there-
fore the experimentally favorable setting for distributed
architectures: it is compatible with local control and
avoids the need for global random circuits across the
full system [15, 17, 18]. Despite this practical appeal, the
theoretical picture remains incomplete. Elben et al. [1]
proposed the standard local Haar protocol, and Zhu et
al. [5] experimentally validated it, but neither work pro-
vides a sample complexity analysis. Zheng et al. [7] ob-
tained average-case and state-dependent sample com-

Bell-dimer states can exceed the pure
product benchmark at the level of Bn,H, illustrating that
the Haar fourth moment alone is not maximized by
product states. The Haar-random pure state curve is
a typical state benchmark. As the Hilbert-space dimen-
sion grows exponentially with n, random sampling is
unlikely to hit the structured extremal regions; typical
n-qubit Haar-random states are highly entangled and
have strongly suppressed local correlations.
This be-
havior is consistent with the discussion in Appx. D,
where entanglement can enhance Bn,H though it may
simultaneously reduce second-moment contributions.
VI.
CONCLUSIONS
We established worst-case sample complexity bounds
for distributed inner product estimation with local ran-
domized measurements and identified the kernel se-
lected by the worst-case framework.
The kernel op-
timization can be reduced to Hamming distance ker-
nels, and unbiasedness in this class fixes the unique
kernel. The Clifford and Haar analyses share the same
first three variance terms because both single-qubit en-
sembles are unitary 3-designs, but their fourth-moment
structures are different and must be treated separately.
In the single-qubit Clifford case, the Clifford commu-
tant gives a sharp fourth-moment bound and hence
worst-case scaling O(

identical pure product stabilizer states. In the single-
qubit Haar case, a local twirling relation gives the same
rigorous upper bound as the Clifford case, but this com-
parison bound is not attainable. Identical pure prod-
uct states instead yield the sharper conjectured sample
complexity scaling O(
√
3.6n). We proved the conjecture
on several supporting classes: arbitrary product state
