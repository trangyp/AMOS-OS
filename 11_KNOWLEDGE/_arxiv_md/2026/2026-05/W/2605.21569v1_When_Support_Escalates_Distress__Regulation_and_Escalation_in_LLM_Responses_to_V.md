ABSTRACT
Large language models are increasingly used for mental health support, yet little is known about
whether their responses are psychologically safe across different help-seeking styles. We examine
a foundational distinction in emotional disclosure, venting vs. advice-seeking, and whether LLMs
respond in ways that regulate or amplify distress. Using 178,800 Reddit posts, we first show the
two help-seeking styles are linguistically distinguishable at scale. We then introduce a measurement
framework grounded in interpersonal emotion regulation theory that captures Regulation and Escala-
tion as empirically independent dimensions. Across persona conditions (default, friend, therapist),
GPT-5.3 responses systematically mirror help-seeking style: venting elicits more regulation, but
also more escalation. Therapist personas reduce escalation while maintaining regulation, whereas
friend personas increase both. A crowdsourced human study finds no user experience penalty for the
safer therapist condition, but reveals that lay raters cannot reliably detect escalation without expert
knowledge. Responses that feel supportive may simultaneously intensify distress in ways standard
safety evaluation cannot see, and empathy metrics alone cannot replace a framework that measures
both.

Introduction
Mental health support is among the most consequential and fastest-growing use cases for large language models
(LLMs). Available, low-stigma, and increasingly empathic, these systems are already used by more than 1 in 8 U.S.
adolescents and young adults for mental health advice (McBain et al., 2025), many of whom receive no professional
care otherwise. Among adults with diagnosed mental health conditions, nearly half report using LLMs for mental health

support (Rousmaniere et al., 2025), and recent survey suggests that nearly a quarter of U.S. adults use LLMs for mental
health overall (Stade et al., 2026). LLMs have become de facto emotion regulation partners for a large and vulnerable
population, and how LLMs respond to emotional distress has direct consequences for mental health outcomes.
Ensuring these interactions are safe requires more than preventing overtly harmful outputs. A more latent risk is that
LLM responses can appear empathetic and supportive while functionally worsening emotional outcomes—amplifying
distress, reinforcing maladaptive appraisals, or encouraging rumination. This failure mode is invisible to refusal-based
safety frameworks (Zhao et al., 2025), which evaluate whether a model refuses a harmful request rather than whether
it harms through the dynamics of an ordinary supportive exchange. It is, however, consistent with the sycophantic
tendencies of LLMs: optimizing for affective alignment and perceived helpfulness over responses that genuinely serve
the user (Sharma et al., 2023b; Fanous et al., 2025). In factual domains, sycophancy produces misinformation (Chen
et al., 2025); In emotional support contexts, this tendency has a clinical analog. Co-rumination describes a pattern in
which validation co-occurs with sustained focus on negative experiences and affect intensification, producing poorer
psychological outcomes despite appearing supportive (Rose, 2002). Whether LLM responses exhibit this structure is
unknown, because existing safety tools are not designed to detect it.
To investigate this risk, we study a foundational distinction in help-seeking discourse: venting versus advice-seeking.
Venting is an open-ended and emotion-focused disclosure strategy (Tran et al., 2023; Parlamis, 2012), while advice-
seeking is more regulated and solution-oriented (Lazarus and Folkman, 1984). Both involve distress disclosure, but they
differ in communicative intent in ways that should shape a well-calibrated response. Because venting is oriented toward
validation and emotional acknowledgment, it provides a sensitive test case for whether LLMs prioritize agreement over
emotional regulation, and whether that prioritization comes at a cost.
We address this question through a combination of large-scale Reddit data and a theory-grounded measurement
framework capturing regulatory and escalatory dynamics in LLM responses. Concretely, we contribute: (1) A linguistic
characterization of venting and advice-seeking behavior, derived from a large-scale, person-matched Reddit corpus of
178,800 posts from 14,040 users, showing that the two help-seeking styles are robustly distinguishable at scale, and that
LLMs accommodate the emotional register of input discourse in their responses. (2) A theory-grounded measurement
framework capturing Regulation and Escalation as two validated, largely independent response dimensions, grounded
in interpersonal emotion regulation theory. (3) Empirical findings showing that regulatory and escalatory behaviors
co-occur rather than trade off in response to venting, and that persona conditioning shifts this balance in theoretically
coherent ways. (4) A crowdsourced human annotation study demonstrating that automated annotation underestimates
escalatory behaviors relative to human observers, and that safer therapist-persona responses carry no user experience
penalty.
2
Related Works
2.1
LLMs for Mental Health Support
Automated support for mental health has a history that predates current LLMs. Early work demonstrated that even
scripted, rule-based conversational agents could reduce depression and anxiety symptoms under controlled conditions:
Fitzpatrick et al. (2017) showed that Woebot, a CBT-based chatbot, produced clinically meaningful reductions over two
weeks in a randomized trial. The arrival of large, general-purpose language models has substantially expanded both
the reach and the ambiguity of this use case. LLMs are now among the most commonly used mental health resources
for adolescents, young adults, and diagnosed adults in the United States (McBain et al., 2025; Rousmaniere et al.,
2025; Stade et al., 2026), and users routinely assign relational personas to them when seeking support, most commonly
framing the model as a close friend or a therapist (Li et al., 2025).
Evaluating whether these interactions are safe has proven difficult. Most safety research focuses on refusal of overtly
harmful requests (Zhao et al., 2025), a framework that is well-suited for preventing explicit harms but cannot detect
subtler risks. More recent behavioral work offers a starting point: Chiu et al. (2024) proposed BOLT, a computational
framework for assessing LLM behavior across therapeutic approaches, finding that LLMs more closely resemble low-
quality therapy than high-quality therapy on several dimensions. Our work complements this by focusing specifically
on the interactional dynamics triggered by different help-seeking styles, and by grounding the measurement framework
in interpersonal emotion regulation theory rather than therapeutic technique taxonomies.
2.2
Venting and Advice-Seeking in Online Communities
Reddit has become a central data source for computational research on emotional disclosure and mental health.
De Choudhury and De (2014) provided an early characterization of mental health discourse across Reddit communities,
showing that the platform attracts a distinctive quality of social support, ranging from emotional to prescriptive,

particularly in response to self-disclosing posts. This body of work has established the value of Reddit as a naturalistic
site for studying emotional support at scale, but it has largely treated help-seeking as a homogeneous category.
In the psychology literature, venting and advice-seeking are theoretically distinct. Venting is an emotion-focused,
open-ended disclosure strategy oriented toward emotional release and social acknowledgment (Rimé, 2009; Parlamis,
2012), whereas advice-seeking is more regulated, solution-oriented, and future-directed (Lazarus and Folkman, 1984).
These differences in communicative intent have real consequences for how responses should be calibrated: Tran et al.
(2023) showed that the effect of venting on emotional outcomes depends heavily on the quality of the social response
received. Despite the theoretical maturity of this distinction, no prior computational work has examined venting and
advice-seeking in direct contrast using a within-user design that controls for individual differences in language and
distress expression. Our dataset addresses this gap.
2.3
Accommodation, Sycophancy, and Co-Rumination
Conversational accommodation, the tendency of interlocutors to align with each other’s linguistic register, provides
the theoretical basis for expecting LLMs to mirror the emotional tone of their inputs (Pickering and Garrod, 2004;
Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil et al., 2011) In the LLM literature, this mirroring tendency has been studied primarily under
the label of sycophancy: a disposition to affirm the user’s expressed views and validate their emotional frame regardless
of accuracy (Sharma et al., 2023a; Fanous et al., 2025; Rehani et al., 2026). Sycophancy research has primarily examined
factual domains, but in emotional support contexts the relevant risk is different. Rose (2002) identified co-rumination
in peer relationships as a pattern in which validation and focused engagement with negative experiences co-occur
with affect intensification and appraisal reinforcement, producing poorer mental health outcomes despite appearing
supportive. Zaki and Williams (2013) and Hofmann et al. (2016) formalized the distinction between interpersonal
responses that down-regulate distress and those that escalate it. Whether LLM accommodation in emotional contexts
resembles co-rumination, producing supportive and escalatory responses simultaneously, is a question prior work has
not addressed. Our measurement framework is designed to make this testable.
Prior computational work has primarily operationalized response quality as empathy (Sharma et al., 2020), establishing
that theoretically grounded, multidimensional annotation is both tractable and meaningfully linked to user outcomes.
Our framework builds on this tradition while identifying a key limitation: measuring only supportive qualities like
empathy leaves escalatory response features unmeasured. A response can score high on empathy while simultaneously
amplifying distress, reinforcing negative appraisals, or endorsing blame attributions. Capturing this requires dimensions
grounded not just in empathy theory but in co-rumination research and interpersonal emotion regulation theory
(Rose, 2002; Zaki and Williams, 2013). We use GPT-5.3 as annotator at scale, supported by evidence that LLM
annotators approach human reliability on safety-sensitive tasks (Boji´c et al., 2025)—whether this holds across escalatory
dimensions specifically is tested in our human study.

4
LLM Response Elicitation
To ensure help-seeking intent is conveyed implicitly via linguistic style, we excluded posts containing surface forms
of vent, venting, advice, or advise. From the filtered corpus we sampled 1,500 posts per category. We used GPT-
5.3, the default ChatGPT model as of March 2026 (OpenAI, 2026), reflecting the LLM most likely encountered in
real-world help-seeking. Drawing on Li et al. (2025), who documented that users routinely assign relational personas
(most commonly a close friend or therapist) when prompting LLMs for mental health support, we implemented three
conditions: default (no persona; i.e., helpful assistant), friend persona, and therapist persona (see persona prompts in
Appendix A). This yields a 2 (venting vs. advice-seeking) × 3 (persona) design, producing 9,000 total LLM responses.
We analyze only first responses; multi-turn dynamics are left for future work.
5
LLM Accommodation of the Venting Register
The linguistic profile characterizing venting—absolutist and high-certitude, morally outward-directed, high-arousal,
and negative-affect-dominant—identifies the specific register features that a mirroring LLM response would reinforce
rather than regulate. Using the same DLA framework, we ask whether LLMs accommodate this register and whether
persona conditioning shapes that pattern.
Default responses to venting mirror emotional distress while endorsing its absolutist and moral framing. Venting
responses showed elevated feeling language (d = 0.66), negative tone (d = 0.54), and present-moment focus (d = 0.50),
with unigrams including sounds, feel, listen, yeah and burden-intensifying language (really, lot, carry, heavy, incredibly;
Table 2). Crucially, emotional accommodation co-occurred with cognitive endorsement: certitude was the strongest
cognitive effect across all personas (d = 0.56), accompanied by absolutist framing (ALLNONE d = 0.27) and moral
alignment (MORAL d = 0.19), echoing the register of the venting posts themselves. Responses to advice-seeking
reversed this profile, rising on third-person reference (d = −0.29) and practical-instructional language (key, simple, tips,
answer, if, example, suggest, approach), consistent with the deliberate, tractable framing of advice posts (Appendix
Figures 8 & 9).
All three personas mirror negative affect, but diverge on certitude, absolutism, and moral alignment. The friend
persona showed the highest absolutism (ALLNONE d = 0.36) and moral alignment (MORAL d = 0.29), suggesting
the greatest risk of endorsing rather than reframing distress. The therapist, despite the strongest emotional attunement

6
A Theory-Grounded Framework for Measuring Regulatory and Escalatory Response
Behaviors
6.1
Dimension development
To move beyond binary safety metrics toward interactionally meaningful constructs, we developed a six-dimension
ordinal annotation framework grounded in two complementary sources. Theoretically, dimensions were derived
from interpersonal emotion regulation research distinguishing regulatory from escalatory interpersonal responses
(Zaki and Williams, 2013) and co-rumination research documenting how validation and affect intensification co-
occur in peer support (Rose, 2002). Empirically, the choice of dimensions was anchored in the specific linguistic
features of the venting register characterized in the Linguistic Registers section:Appraisal Endorsement captures
the risk of mirroring absolutism and certitude; Moral Alignment, the risk of mirroring accusatory and conflict-laden
framing; Emotional Amplification, the risk of mirroring high-arousal negative affect; while Cognitive Reappraisal
(cognitive reframing), Emotional Validation (acknowledgment of the user’s affective state), and Regulatory Containment
(emotional stabilization) capture the stabilizing behaviors that register structurally elicits but a co-ruminative response
withholds. Each dimension was operationalized as a 0-4 ordinal scale with behaviorally anchored rubric descriptors.

Finally, substantial variation across user messages (random intercept variance = 1.11) indicates that the content of the
user message strongly influences how models respond, reinforcing the importance of evaluating LLMs in context.
8
Human Studies
We conducted two complementary human studies. The first used expert annotators to validate the measurement
framework. The second used crowdsourced workers to examine how lay users evaluate LLM responses across persona
conditions.
8.1
Expert Annotation: Framework Validity
8.1.1
Method
The original rubric requires PhD-level expertise in clinical psychology, consistent with evidence that frontier LLMs
achieve annotation reliability comparable to domain experts on subjective tasks (Boji´c et al., 2025; Rehani et al.,
2026). We developed a simplified rubric that preserves the six-dimensional structure and 0-4 ordinal scales but replaces
technical terminology with plain-language descriptions and everyday anchor examples (See Appendix B). Two PhD
researchers with expertise in psychology independently annotated 20 LLM responses using this rubric.
8.1.2
Results
We computed quadratic weighted Cohen’s κ between the mean expert scores and GPT annotations. Agreement
between expert means and GPT annotations was substantial for the regulation composite (κ = 0.47) and near-
perfect for the escalation composite (κ = 0.81), exceeding conventional thresholds for moderate and almost perfect
agreement respectively (Landis and Koch, 1977; McHugh, 2012). This confirms that the six-dimensional framework,
as operationalized by the LLM annotator, captures constructs that domain-expert human raters recognize and apply
consistently.

8.2
Crowdsourced Lay Evaluation
8.2.1
Participants and design
We recruited 68 annotators via Prolific who were U.S. adults with at least a bachelor’s degree and English as their
first language. Annotators evaluated responses to help-seeking Reddit posts using a custom interface that supported
side-by-side comparison and optional text highlighting.
Each annotator was assigned one help-seeking message from a pool of 34 Reddit posts (17 venting, 17 advice-seeking)
and rated all three corresponding LLM responses (default, friend, therapist). This within-annotator design controlled for
individual rating tendencies when comparing personas. Across the study, each of the 102 responses received ratings
from two independent annotators. Annotators were not informed that the responses were LLM-generated or that the
source messages originated from Reddit.
8.2.2
Measures
For each response, annotators rated the six framework dimensions on 0-4 ordinal scales. They also answered two
separate response evaluation questions: “Imagine you are the person who wrote this message. How much would you
want to receive this reply?”(desirability) and “How helpful do you think this reply would be?” (helpfulness).

General Discussion
9.1
Regulation and escalation increase together
Our central finding is that LLM responses do not trade off between support and risk. They increase both simultaneously,
especially in response to venting. This coupling resembles co-rumination, where emotional validation co-occurs with
sustained focus on negative experiences and affect intensification (Rose, 2002). The mismatch between perceived
empathy and functional emotional outcome is precisely what makes it a latent safety risk: responses that feel supportive
may still reinforce distress.
9.2
Persona conditioning steers the balance
Persona conditioning reveals that this alignment can be steered. The friend persona increases both regulation and
escalation, producing more emotionally engaged but also more amplifying responses. In contrast, the therapist persona
reduces escalation while maintaining high levels of regulation. This is encouraging: it shows the balance can be shifted
through simple prompt-level interventions, without architectural changes or fine-tuning. Given that users, especially
adolescents, often frame LLMs as friends when seeking mental health support (Common Sense Media, 2025; Internet
Matters, 2025), the friend persona’s amplification effect raises a safety concern: the model’s tendency to prioritize
relational warmth and agreement may contribute to co-ruminative dynamics, reinforcing maladaptive appraisals under
the guise of empathy.
9.3
Users do not penalize the safer persona
Across 102 responses and 68 crowdsourced evaluators, the therapist persona received ratings of desirability and
helpfulness indistinguishable from the friend and default conditions. This null held within both venting and advice-
seeking contexts. In venting posts, where escalation risk is highest, therapist responses trended toward higher helpfulness
than friend responses, though this did not reach significance. The result suggests that the UX cost of adding a therapist-
style system prompt to emotional support applications may be low. Whether this generalizes to real-world deployment,
including extended multi-turn interactions and users in genuine distress, warrants further study. With that caveat, the
present evidence gives product designers little basis for omitting a therapist-style prompt on user experience grounds
alone.
9.4
Escalation is harder to measure than regulation
The human studies reveal an important asymmetry in construct accessibility. Regulation evaluation overlaps with
everyday empathy perception, and domain experts and lay crowdworkers agree with the LLM almost equally well on
default responses (κ = 0.47 vs. 0.55). Evaluating escalation, on the other hand, requires clinical concepts that are not
part of lay intuition, and the expert-prolific gap is large (κ = 0.81 vs. 0.22). The friend persona further reveals a specific
blind spot in LLM annotation: the model over-credits relational warmth as genuine regulatory support. If the LLM
systematically inflates regulation scores for friend responses, the safety gap between friend and therapist personas may
be even larger than what was reported.
9.5
Empathy and sycophancy may be intertwined
Our findings also speak to the relationship between empathy and sycophancy in high-affect contexts (Rehani et al.,
2026). Rather than treating empathy as uniformly desirable and sycophancy as a distinct failure mode, the co-occurrence
of validation and escalation suggests they may be intertwined. While we do not directly measure user-perceived empathy
or independently validate sycophancy, this should be interpreted as a hypothesis-generating observation, highlighting
the challenge of disentangling supportive validation from escalatory agreement in model design and evaluation.
9.6
Limitations
Our results should be interpreted with several limitations in mind. First, our annotation relies primarily on GPT-5.3 as
annotator, using the same model family that generated the responses. The expert validation study provides independent
support for the framework, but the risk of systematic bias remains. Second, our analysis is limited to first-turn
interactions. It is therefore unclear whether the observed regulatory–escalatory coupling would persist, attenuate, or
intensify over longer multi-turn exchanges. Third, our Reddit corpus provides naturalistic discourse, but LLM mental
health tool users may differ from Reddit populations in communication style and need. In addition, Reddit populations
are disproportionately young, English-speaking, and Western, and subreddit self-selection may exaggerate stylistic

Finally, we do not measure real-world mental health outcomes. The framework identifies response properties associated

longitudinal and clinical validation.
9.7
Ethical Considerations
This work examines LLM use in sensitive mental health contexts, and several ethical considerations warrant explicit
attention. Our findings highlight that seemingly empathetic responses may reinforce distress, underscoring that LLMs
should not replace professional care. Persona prompting that frames a model as a friend or therapist may encourage
users to attribute expertise and relational commitment to systems that cannot provide sustained or accountable support.
Over time, this could discourage help-seeking from qualified professionals, particularly among younger or socially
isolated users who are also most likely to use friend-framed interactions and most susceptible to escalatory responses.
Risks are unlikely to be evenly distributed. Adolescents, highly distressed individuals, and those lacking support
networks may be disproportionately harmed by over-validating or escalatory responses. Safety evaluation must therefore
attend to high-risk subpopulations, not only average behavior.
We also note the dual-use potential of the annotation framework. Detailed rubrics for escalatory behavior could in
principle guide optimization toward those same behaviors. Responsible use requires that the framework be applied in
its intended evaluative context. Reddit data was collected under public API terms; all analyses are conducted at the
aggregate level without identifying individuals. The corpus contains offensive language and descriptions of distress
consistent with the naturalistic venting register; no personally identifiable information is retained or reported.

Conclusion
LLMs actively shape emotional exchanges in ways that refusal-based safety frameworks cannot detect. When responding
to venting, they increase both regulatory and escalatory behaviors simultaneously, mirroring the structure of co-
rumination: responses that feel empathetic may still reinforce distress. Our framework makes this testable by treating
Regulation and Escalation as independent dimensions rather than poles of a single scale. Our human studies show that
escalation is harder for lay raters to detect than regulation, underscoring the need for expert-informed annotation in
safety evaluation. Persona conditioning shifts this balance meaningfully: therapist-style prompts reduce escalation
while maintaining regulation, at no measurable cost to user experience. Given that friend framing is the most common
real-world persona for mental health help-seeking, its elevated escalation is a population-level concern. Our framework
provides a theory-informed, empirically validated lens for evaluating LLM safety in high-stakes emotional contexts,
one that empathy metrics alone cannot replace.

Appendix C: Corpus Construction and Linguistic Analysis Methods
C.1
Corpus Construction
We collected original posts (submissions) and applied two filters: users must have (a) posted in at least one venting and
one advice-seeking forum, and (b) maintained a posting rate of no more than one post per day on average, removing
single-post users and high-frequency spam accounts. This yielded 14,040 users with 92,217 posts in venting forums
(mean = 6.56, SD = 12.78) and 86,641 posts in advice-seeking forums (mean = 6.17, SD = 9.28); descriptive statistics
and corpus-level Cohen’s d values are reported in Table 1.
Each record contains the post timestamp, subreddit, username, a unique post identifier, and the concatenated title
and body text. We derived two analysis variables: is_venting, a boolean flag indicating membership in a venting
subreddit, and userid_isventing, a concatenation of username and is_venting that serves as the unit of
analysis for within-user feature aggregation.

Analysis (DLA; Schwartz and Ungar 2015), a framework for measuring associations between linguistic features and an
outcome variable at the user level. For each user, features are aggregated separately over their venting posts and their
advice-seeking posts at the user-forum level. Group differences are then quantified using Cohen’s d:

Appendix F: Analysis of LLM Response
Therapist and friend personas operationalize distinct support registers, not degree shifts along a shared warmth
scale. The therapist persona is marked by canonical reflective-listening vocabulary — sounds (d = 1.52), curious (d =
1.09), feel/feeling/feelings (d = 1.14–0.66), hearing (d = 0.92), yourself (d = 0.77), notice (d = 0.66), experiences

correspond to distinct therapeutic traditions rather than a single warmth continuum.
