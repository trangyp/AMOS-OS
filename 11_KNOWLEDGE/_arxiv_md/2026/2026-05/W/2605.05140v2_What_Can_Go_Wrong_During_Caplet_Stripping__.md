What Can Go Wrong During Caplet Stripping ?
Fabien Le Floc’h
*
Correspondence: fabien@2ipi.com
Received: May 2026
Abstract: We study exact and near exact extraction of caplet volatilities from market cap quotes and
identify why some common choices produce extreme oscillations or negative vols. Interpolation scheme
and node placement are shown to be the primary drivers of instability, which can be amplified by isolated
bad quotes. We propose practical, production ready remedies: continuous flat-linear and C1 flat-smooth
kernels that preserve bootstrap equivalence, midpoint node placement with a global solver, positivity
enforcement via an exponential reparametrization or Hyman non-negative C1 splines. We also introduce
simple data quality checks. Numerical experiments demonstrate substantially reduced oscillations, robust
positive caplet curves, and negligible repricing error, delivering a fast and stable caplet stripping workflow
suitable for real-world use.
Keywords: cap; floor; caplet; stripping; interpolation
1. Introduction
Interest rate caps and floors are widely used financial instruments that provide protection against
interest rate fluctuations. A cap is a series of European call options on interest rates, while a floor is a series
of European put options. Caplet stripping is the process of extracting the implied volatilities of individual
caplets from the market prices of caps and floors. This process is crucial for pricing and risk management in
the interest rate derivatives market.
Caps are typically quoted in terms of their implied volatilities. There are three conventions: the
Black-Scholes implied volatility, which was the historical choice before negative interest rates became
more widespread (also known as lognormal volatility), the Bachelier implied volatility (also called normal
volatility or b.p. vol because it is quoted in basis points) and the shifted lognormal volatility. For the sake of
simplicity, we will focus here on the Bachelier quotes, and the stripping will consist of finding the Bachelier
implied volatilities of the caplets that are consistent with the market prices of the caps. Our observations
also apply to the other conventions.

of interpolation and node placement can lead to strong oscillations in the caplet volatilities, and we will
propose remedies to alleviate this issue.

3. Smoother Interpolations
3.1. Node Placement and Interpolation
Being overly enthusiastic about joining the dots, one may consider to directly interpolate linearly
between the nodes, placed at the cap maturities. There is no dependency towards the next caplet (the matrix
is lower triangular) and thus bootstrapping is still applicable. However, this approach can lead to strong
oscillations in the caplet volatilities, especially for longer maturities. We will show that using midpoint
nodes instead of at-maturity nodes can help reduce these oscillations. We will thus consider two kinds of
node set:
• At-maturity nodes set 𝜏𝑘= 𝑇𝑘−𝛿.
• Midpoint nodes set 𝜏1 = 𝑇1 −𝛿and 𝜏𝑘= (𝑇𝑘−1 + 𝑇𝑘)∕2 −𝛿for 𝑘≥2.
The shift 𝛿is typically one month for a forward looking rate with 1 month tenor, and zero for a backward
looking rate.
The introduction of midpoint nodes leads to a non lower triangular matrix, and thus requires a global
solver to find the caplet volatilities. We use the Levenberg-Marquardt method with backtracking line search
as global solver [4]. With the global solver, we are not limited to piecewise linear interpolation, and we can
also use a cubic spline interpolation.
3.2. Enforcing Positivity
A priori, there is no guarantee that the linear interpolation at midpoints will lead to positive caplet
volatilities. In our example, the linear interpolation at mid-points actually leads to negative caplet volatilities

5.1. Modified Z-Score
Given a series of observations 𝑥1, … , 𝑥𝑛, the median absolute deviation (MAD) is

An observation 𝑥𝑖is flagged as an outlier when

the classical mean and standard deviation.

6. Conclusion
When using a simple bootstrap algorithm to strip the caplet volatilities, there is no good reason to use
a pure piecewise-constant interpolation. A flat-linear interpolation is always preferable as it produces a
continuous vol curve. For smoother interpolations, the use of midpoint nodes instead of at-maturity nodes
help to reduce oscillations significantly. In particular, we found the Hyman non-negative C1 spline to work
well. Finally, it is important to check the quality of the cap quotes before stripping, as outliers can lead to
strong oscillations in the caplet volatilities and we have provided a practical way of doing so.
