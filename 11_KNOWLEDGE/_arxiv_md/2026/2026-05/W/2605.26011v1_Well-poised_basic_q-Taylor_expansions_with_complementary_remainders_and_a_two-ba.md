WELL-POISED BASIC q-TAYLOR EXPANSIONS WITH
COMPLEMENTARY REMAINDERS AND A TWO-BASIS KERNEL
ABDULHAFEEZ A. ABDULSALAM AND MICHAEL J. SCHLOSSER
Abstract. We prove a nonterminating well-poised basic q-Taylor expansion with com-
plementary remainder for a two-basis infinite-product kernel. The finite theory is the
well-poised p = 0 specialization of the elliptic Askey–Wilson Taylor framework: the pa-
rameter c produces the rational well-poised basis, while the nome p gives the elliptic
deformation. In the full elliptic setting with nome p, the Taylor expansions considered are
usually finite for convergence reasons; the nonterminating expansions studied here are a
distinctive feature of the basic well-poised case and do not extend directly to the elliptic
setting. For the kernel proposed in the second author’s earlier outlook [18, Sec. 5], we
compute the two Taylor coefficient families and prove the complementary limiting remain-
der by a Taylor-theoretic annular argument. The proof avoids Bailey’s nonterminating
8ϕ7 summation; it uses instead the well-poised Cooper formula, Jackson’s terminating
8ϕ7 summation, Rogers’ nonterminating 6ϕ5 summation, and theta-function addition and
interpolation. As a consequence, Bailey’s nonterminating 8ϕ7 summation is recovered from
the two-basis Taylor expansion. We also include a one-family quadratic example and a
multi-kernel outlook governed by Weierstrass’ elliptic partial-fraction identity.
1. Introduction
Taylor expansions associated with the Askey–Wilson divided-difference operator [1] are
built not from ordinary monomials, but from basis functions adapted to the multiplicative
q-grid. In the polynomial case this point of view goes back to Ismail’s q-Taylor theorem
for the Askey–Wilson operator [6]. It was developed further in Ismail’s operator calculus
and monograph [7, 8], extended by Ismail and Stanton [9, 10], and complemented by the
convergence and summation work of L´opez, Marco and Parcet [12] and Marco and Parcet
[13].
The second author’s elliptic Taylor theorem [18], in turn, showed that the same idea
extends to well-poised rational bases and to an elliptic deformation of the Askey–Wilson
operator. The broader elliptic framework comes from Rains’ BCn-symmetric difference-
operator theory [15, 16]; further elliptic Taylor and interpolation consequences, including
an elliptic Cooper formula, were developed by the second author and Yoo [19].
We use the following terminology throughout. The extra denominator parameter c in the
operator Dc,q and in the rational monomials Φk(z; a, c) is what makes the theory well-poised:
it turns the ordinary Askey–Wilson monomial basis into a rational basis on which the
modified operator still lowers degree. The nome p is a separate elliptic deformation. Thus
the specialization p = 0 of the elliptic theory should not be regarded as merely a degenerate
elliptic case; it is the basic well-poised case in its own right. This distinction is not only
terminological. In the elliptic theory with p retained, the Taylor expansions have normally
been formulated as finite expansions, essentially because convergence of nonterminating
elliptic expansions is too restrictive for the applications at hand; compare the discussion of

13. Further examples and multi-kernel outlook
The quadratic product in Theorem 10.3 is a genuinely one-family example: its Taylor
remainder is the tail of an absolutely convergent well-poised basis expansion and tends
to zero. No two-grid annular cancellation is needed. In this sense the quadratic example
is independent of the nonterminating 8ϕ7 issue of this paper; its summation input is the
quadratic Watson-type identity attached to the chosen basis, and Bailey’s nonterminating
8ϕ7 summation is not used.

The two-basis kernel theorem itself is the main nontrivial example of the complementary-
remainder mechanism. By Corollary 11.26, it recovers Bailey’s nonterminating 8ϕ7 summa-
tion from the Taylor expansion. It is natural to ask for a transformation analogue. The finite
elliptic Taylor calculation in [18] applies the same philosophy to the Spiridonov–Zhedanov
elliptic extension [20] of Rahman’s rational functions [14] and yields Frenkel–Turaev’s 12V11
transformation [4] after a specialization of the free Taylor parameter. At p = 0, the analo-
gous target is Bailey’s four-term nonterminating 10ϕ9 transformation; standard references
are Bailey [2] and Gasper–Rahman [5].
The present two-kernel theorem does not by itself contain enough freedom to prove the
four-term transformation. It is a summation-level result: a single kernel is decomposed into
two complementary flat contributions. A nonterminating 10ϕ9 transformation should instead
arise from a connection-coefficient identity with an additional free parameter, and more
naturally from a theory with several complementary kernels. In such a theory, the three-term
Weierstrass addition formula used in Section 11 should be replaced by the appropriate
specialization of Weierstrass’ general elliptic partial-fraction identity. For Bailey’s four-term
transformation one expects the r = 4 specialization, whose four summands would play the
role of the complementary pieces. In multiplicative theta notation, the general identity
gives the natural r-term replacement for the two-kernel theta interpolation used here; see
Whittaker and Watson [21, Ch. 20] for the classical theory and Gasper–Rahman [5, Ch. 11]
for the theta notation used in the hypergeometric literature.
Thus the natural continuation of the present work is not merely to append another
parameter to the two-basis kernel, but to develop a multi-kernel well-poised q-Taylor
expansion. The expected structure is a finite family of complementary flat prefactors,
a corresponding family of normalized kernels, and a residual cancellation governed by
Weierstrass’ elliptic partial-fraction identity rather than by the three-term addition formula.
Such a result would provide the appropriate basic counterpart of the elliptic 12V11 derivation
and is the setting in which Bailey’s four-term nonterminating 10ϕ9 transformation should
naturally appear.
14. Concluding remarks
The paper separates four phenomena which are easily conflated. First, the finite p = 0
well-poised q-Taylor theorem is inherited from the second author’s elliptic operator theory.
Second, unlike the usual elliptic theory with nome p, the basic well-poised specialization
supports nonterminating expansions; these infinite expansions are the main reason to study
the p = 0 case as an independent theory rather than only as a degeneration. Third, exact
one-family Taylor remainders do not by themselves give uniqueness in broad meromorphic
classes, because flat functions vanish on the Taylor grid. Fourth, the two-basis kernel
identity is precisely a complementary-remainder theorem: the one-family Taylor remainder
generally converges to a nonzero second-basis contribution.
