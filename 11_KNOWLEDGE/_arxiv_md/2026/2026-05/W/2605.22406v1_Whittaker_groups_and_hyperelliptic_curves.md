Abstract. Let K be a complete, non-archimedean valued field with a residue field of characteristic
different from 2. A Whittaker group Γ is a discontinuous subgroup of PGL(2, K), freely generated
by elements s0, . . . , sg of order two, each defined by a pair of fixed points {a0, b0}, . . . , {ag, bg}.
These fixed points are called “in good position”.
A subgroup W ⊆Γ of index 2 is a Schottky group and produces a hyperelliptic Mumford curve
Ω/W →Ω/Γ ∼= P1, called Whittaker curve, of genus g and with branch locus B ⊆P1(K).
An explicit parametrization of Whittaker curves in terms of theta functions for W and Γ and the
data of the fixed points, is developed. In particular, this allows one to express the branched points
(and other data such as p-adic periods and p-adic heights) in terms of values of theta functions.
A central theme of this paper is the relation between the fixed points and the branch locus. For
a given configuration (P, m) of g + 1 pairs of points in P1, one defines a rigid space FixP,m of fixed
points in good position with that configuration and a rigid space of branched points BranchP,m
in that configuration. A main result is that the natural morphism FB : FixP,m →BranchP,m is a
rigid étale covering with Galois group {±1}d−1 for some d ≥1.
For all cases of genus g = 2, 3 (and for some more), an approximation of FB is computed which
confirms the main result.
Classification of Whittaker groups and analytic reductions of Whittaker curves is another im-
portant issue of this paper.
The background material in this paper complements the work of
L. Gerritzen, G. Van Steen, F. Herrlich and others. It involves re-examination of some proofs,
the derivation of properties of semi-stable analytic reductions and studying good position of fixed
points.
1. Introduction and Summary
E.T. Whittaker (1899 and 1929), [Wh1, Wh2], studied the uniformization of hyperelliptic
curves over C with the aim of finding curves having an explicit uniformizing Fuchsian subgroup of
PGL2(R) and an explicit equation as well. This topic is still active.
Whittaker groups Γ ⊆PGL2(K), the analogues over a non-archimedean valued field K, were
introduced in [P, G-vdP]. They are natural examples for Schottky groups and Mumford curves.
There is a renewed interest in Whittaker groups because the uniformization of hyperelliptic Mum-
ford curves (here called Whittaker curves) is of importance for the explicit computations of p-adic
periods, p-adic heights and a p-adic BSD conjecture (see also [T]). The aim of the group of re-
searchers E. Kaya, M. Masdeu and J.S. Müller [KMM] is to develop algorithms and examples for
these p-adic heights. This is the direct inspiration for the current manuscript.
In the present paper we also revisit [P, G-vdP, F-vdP] in order to complete proofs, to correct
mistakes and to answer old and new questions. Proofs of statements which can easily be found
in [G-vdP] and/or [F-vdP] are not reproduced. Beyond that, the important themes are: explicit
formulas (Propositions 6.1 and 6.2 and 6.4) for the uniformization of Whittaker curves; a result
(Theorem 5.8) explaining the relation between fixed points and branch points and finally (Sec-
tions 8 and 9) explicit examples for genus 2 and 3. We now describe in more detail the contents

2.3. Semi-stable reductions of Mumford curves.
Theorem 2.1. (Stable reduction theorem).
Let X/K be a smooth, connected, projective curve of
genus ≥2. There is a finite, separable extension K′ ⊃K such that K′×K X has a stable reduction.
This reduction is unique.
The existence of a semi-stable reduction is a highly nontrivial result and has various proofs in
the literature. It can be seen that the stable reduction is unique. Any semi-stable reduction is
obtained by blowing up (successively) points on the stable reduction.
We will review an explicit proof of the stable reduction theorem for hyperelliptic curves over K,
with residue characteristic ̸= 2. A useful extension of the stable reduction theorem is the following.
Corollary 2.2. Let X/K be a smooth, connected, projective curve of genus ≥2 and S ⊆X(K) a
finite set. There is a finite, separable extension K′ ⊃K such that X′ := K′ ×K X has a minimal
semi-stable reduction Red: X′ →(X′, S) which separates the points of S. This reduction is unique.
As before, separation means that the images under Red, of the points in S, are distinct and
are distinct from the nodes of (X′, S). Minimality means that there is no irreducible component,
isomorphic to the projective line over k which has ≤2 special points. Here, special point means a
node or the image of a point of S. The proof is obtained by “blowing up” points on the given stable
reduction in order to “separate” S. In case the valuation of K is discrete, the stable reduction can
be seen as a model of the curve over the valuation ring Ko. This is a surface and “blowing up”
is indeed blowing up points on the surface. In the general case “blowing up” can be realized by
refining the pure affinoid covering associated to the given reduction.
For a Mumford curve, the uniformization by a Schottky group provides its stable reduction.

7.1.3. Realizing finite trees of finite groups. A finite tree of finite groups (T, G) consists of a finite

morphism Ge →Gv is given if v is a vertex of the edge e.

K
and morphisms of Gv and Ge to the stabilizers of τ(v) and τ(e) in PGL2,K. One requires that the

those defined by the object (T, G).
From a configuration (P, m) one obtains a rather special finite tree of finite groups (T, G). Here
T is the tree of (P, m). For a vertex v one defines Gv = {±1} if v is odd and Gv = {1} if v is
even. For an edge e one defines Ge = {±1} if e is odd and Ge = {1} is e is even. Further, each
morphism Ge →Gv is injective. A B-tree with configuration (P, m) can be seen as a realization τ
of (T, G).
