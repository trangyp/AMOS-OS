Abstract. We introduce the vertical bA-cowaist, a codimension-one invariant for parti-
tioned manifolds. It extends the concept of infinite vertical bA-cowaist for bands to arbitrary
partitioned manifolds, which may be noncompact and have compact boundary. We estab-
lish a sharp inequality relating the scalar curvature, the bottom spectrum of the Laplacian,
and this invariant. As an application, we obtain a high-dimensional analogue of Munteanu-
Wang’s bottom spectrum estimate. We also prove a quantitative strengthening of Anghel’s
theorem together with a boundary version, as well as a Calabi-Yau type theorem that goes
beyond the dimensional restrictions of the earlier µ-bubble method. Our approach is based
on deformed Dirac operators.
1. Introduction
The study of scalar curvature is a central topic in differential geometry, closely linked to
topology, index theory, and global analysis. A key question is how scalar curvature bounds
interact with large-scale invariants on complete manifolds — particularly on noncompact
ones, where topology and spectrum combine in subtle ways.
A particularly powerful obstruction to positive scalar curvature comes from the Dirac
operator. Lichnerowicz [28] showed that a closed spin manifold with positive scalar curvature
must have vanishing bA-genus. Gromov and Lawson [20] then introduced enlargeability and
used index-theoretic methods to obtain far-reaching obstructions. These results reveal a
deep connection between scalar curvature and topology; for a comprehensive overview, we
refer to Gromov’s Four lectures on scalar curvature [19].

invariants. For a complete Riemannian manifold (M, gM), the Laplacian ∆is self-adjoint
(see [16]). The spectrum σ(M) := σ(−∆) of M is a closed subset of [0, ∞). The bottom

We end this section with the proof of the Calabi-Yau type theorem in higher dimensions.
Proof of Theorem D. We argue by contradiction. Suppose that (M, gM) is noncompact and
has sublinear volume growth.
Let K0 ⊂M be a compact set outside which the scalar
curvature is nonnegative.
By Lemma 1.14 applied to K0, there exists a bounded region Ω⊃K0 such that ∂Ωis
mean-concave with respect to the outer unit normal of Ω. Consequently, ∂Ωis mean-convex
when regarded as the boundary of the complement M \ Ω.
Since M is partitioned at infinity by a closed hypersurface with nonzero bA-genus, the
definition applied to the compact set Ωyields a compact set L ⊃Ωand a separating closed
hypersurface N ⊂M \ L with bA(N) ̸= 0.
Now set W := M \ Ω. The hypersurface N lies in M \ L and is disjoint from the compact
collar L \ Ω; hence N also separates W. Thus W is a complete noncompact manifold with
compact mean-convex boundary ∂W = ∂Ω, nonnegative scalar curvature, and it is separated
by a closed hypersurface with nonzero bA-genus.
Applying Theorem 1.11 to W shows that W is isometric to the product
(∂W × [0, ∞), g∂W + dt2),
where (∂W, g∂W) is a compact Ricci-flat manifold. In particular, W has linear volume growth.
Because Ωis bounded, the volume growth of M coincides with that of W, contradicting
the assumption that M has sublinear volume growth.
