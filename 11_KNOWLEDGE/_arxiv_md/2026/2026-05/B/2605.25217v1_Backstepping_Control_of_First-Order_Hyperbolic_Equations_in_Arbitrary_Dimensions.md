Backstepping Control of First-Order Hyperbolic Equations in
Arbitrary Dimensions with Non-Trapping Characteristics
Mohamed Camil Belhadjoudja
Department of Applied Mathematics, University of Waterloo, 200 University Avenue West, Waterloo, ON, Canada, N2L 3G1
Abstract
This paper presents a backstepping approach for the boundary control of first-order hyperbolic equations with spatially
varying coefficients posed on domains of arbitrary dimension. The method is based on a change of variables induced by
the characteristic flow of the time-invariant transport operator, transforming the original multidimensional system into a
continuum of decoupled one-dimensional hyperbolic equations evolving along individual characteristic curves. A backstepping
controller is then designed for each equation in the decomposition, and the resulting control laws are reassembled in the original
coordinates to achieve finite-time stabilization of the full system. The framework relies on the existence of characteristic curves
foliating the spatial domain, with uniformly bounded transit times (non-trapping).
Key words: Hyperbolic PDEs; Backstepping; Finite-Time Stability; Characteristic Methods

The key idea to design U is to first straighten out the
characteristics of (6): rather than working directly on Ω
with the spatial coordinates x = (x1, x2, . . . , xn), we in-

Conclusion
This paper extended the backstepping approach of [21] to
first-order hyperbolic equations defined on n-dimensional
domains, for any n ≥1, by decomposing the multidi-
mensional equation into a continuum of one-dimensional
equations along characteristic curves and applying the
approach of [21] to each equation. In a forthcoming work,
we reveal how the proposed framework can be extended
to interconnections of multidimensional first-order hy-
perbolic equations with velocities in opposing directions.

It is worth emphasizing that the main contribution of this
paper is not the backstepping controller itself, but rather
the identification of an equivalence between the control
of multidimensional hyperbolic equations and the control
of a continuum of one-dimensional hyperbolic equations.
This equivalence suggests that other control approaches
developed in the one-dimensional setting might also ex-
tend to the multidimensional case through the same re-
duction. Whether this is indeed the case for specific meth-
ods remains to be investigated.
Finally, the approach developed here appears difficult to
extend to parabolic equations, as diffusion couples all
characteristics. For parabolic equations defined on gen-
eral domains, writing down the backstepping kernel equa-
tions is not difficult once the geometry has been absorbed
into the PDE coefficients. However, establishing well-
posedness of these kernel equations does not appear to be
straightforward, at least to the author’s current knowl-
edge.
