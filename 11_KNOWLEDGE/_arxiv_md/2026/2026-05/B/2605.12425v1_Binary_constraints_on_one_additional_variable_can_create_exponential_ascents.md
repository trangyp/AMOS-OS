Abstract
Local search in combinatorial optimisation can be viewed as an uphill climb on a corresponding
fitness landscape, where the assignments visited by a strict local search follow an ascent in the
landscape. This hill-climbing is sometimes surprisingly efficient, but not always. Since fitness
landscapes can be succinctly represented by valued constraint satisfaction problems (VCSPs), it is
natural to ask: what properties of VCSPs ensure that all ascents are polynomial? Or alternatively,
what are the “simplest” VCSPs with exponential ascents? Prior examples of VCSPs with long
ascents were built up as a chain of gadgets of constraints. Here we give a simpler star of gadgets
construction by gluing 2n triangles of constraints at a common centre variable. We obtain a binary
VCSP on 4n + 1 Boolean variables with an exponential ascent of length 10 · 2n −9. The variable at
the centre of our construction intertwines two sublandscapes with only linear ascents into one with
exponential ascents. The VCSP that we construct is significantly simpler than prior constructions
in terms of treedepth (reducing Ω(log n) to 3) and feedback vertex set number (reducing Ω(n) to 1).
We discuss the consequences of this simplicity for the parameterized complexity of local search.

Introduction
Local search heuristics are popular methods for combinatorial optimisation [1,8,11,26,30],
and ascents in fitness landscapes are proving to be a good lens on the efficiency or inefficiency
of these methods [7, 12, 18, 21, 27]. Given a finite set of Boolean variables V , a space of
assignments {0, 1}V , and an objective function f : {0, 1}V →Z (that we call the fitness
function) to be maximized, local search starts with some initial assignment x0 ∈{0, 1}V
and follows some update rule to proceed to another assignment until it eventually finds a
good one. We say that two assignments x, y ∈{0, 1}V are adjacent if they differ on a single
variable (i.e., ∃u ∈V such that xu ̸= yu and ∀v ∈V \{u}, xv = yv) and x is a local peak if
for all adjacent y, f(x) ≥f(y). Based on this, we say the method is a strict local search if
the update rule always returns an adjacent assignment of strictly better objective value, or
terminates at a local peak if a better adjacent assignment is not available. The sequence of
such assignments x0, x1, . . . , xT is an ascent: each xt+1 is adjacent to xt, f(xt+1) > f(xt),
and xT is a local peak. A fitness landscape is the pair consisting of the fitness function and
an adjacency structure on the assignments [16,18,32].

Binary constraints on one additional variable can create exponential ascents
For researchers wanting to understand the efficiency or inefficiency of local search, this
has set clear research agendas. On the one hand, identify families of fitness landscapes that
ensure short ascents to their peaks [18,21,29]. On the other hand, construct “simple” families
of fitness landscapes that have exponentially long ascents [12,20,22,27]. The latter approach
is at work as early as the 1970s, with Klee and Minty [23] constructing a d-cube where
an ascent taken by a simplex pivot rules visits all 2d assignments on the way to the only
peak. This construction recursively took a (d −1)-cube with a long ascent, made a “reversed”
version of it, and then added the dth variable such that fixing xd = 0 induces the “original”
(d −1)-cube and fixing xd = 1 induces the “reversed” cube. In this way, Klee and Minty [23]
doubled the long ascent with each new variable. This Klee-Minty recursive construction has
served as a model for many later constructions squeezing exponential ascents into fitness
landscapes [2,9,10,13,14,16,24]. But given the recursive nature of these constructions, are
they really that “simple”?

1.2
Prior chain of gadgets constructions
Early recursive constructions like the Klee-Minty cube cannot be represented by sparse
valued constraint graphs [3,7]. This motivated more recent work to built up VCSP-instances
as a chain of iteratively connected gadgets. Each gadget is a constant-sized set of variables
and ‘internal’ constraints involving only those variables. Some variables in the kth gadget
are designated as ‘connectors’ and these are the only variables involved in constraints to
variables from the (k + 1)th gadget. Repeating this process builds up a chain of m gadgets.
Since both the number of variables and the number of edges is linear in m, this results in
sparse constraint graphs with the max-degree and pathwidth bounded by a constant.
Broadly, there have been three kinds of local search intractability results for sparse VCSPs:
chain constructions where (1) some ascent is exponential, (2) the steepest ascent taken by
greedy local search is exponential, or (3) all ascents from a designated initial assignment are
exponential. We give examples of some of these chain constructions in Figure 1. For results of
kind (1), Monien and Tscheuschner [27] built Max-Cut-instances with an exponential ascent
by chaining together gadgets on five variables with the connector variable participating in
three internal constraints and one constraint to the next gadget (see Figure 1a). This resulted
in a chain construction of a Max-Cut-Instance of maximum degree four. By generalising from
Max-Cut to general VCSPs, Kaznatcheev, Cohen, and Jeavons [18] showed that the fifth
variable in each gadget could be eliminated, reducing the maximum degree of the construction
to three while preserving the exponential ascent (see Figure 1b).4 Both of these constructions
had pathwidth two. For results of kind (2) of the particular ascent followed by greedy local

and van Marle [31] reduced the pathwidth of a construction with an exponential steepest

1.3
Our contribution
Although the chain construction VCSPs are simple, the fitness landscapes that they represent
are built up from sublandscapes that are complicated along every dimension. No matter
how you divide these landscapes into a constant number of induced sublandscapes, at least
one of those sublandscapes will have an exponential ascent. Is this an essential feature of
exponential ascents? We show here this recursive complexity is not an essential feature: it is
possible to take two simple sublandscapes and combine them into a complex one.
Instead of building a chain of gadgets, in this paper, we build a star of gadgets with an
exponential ascent. Specifically, in Section 2, we build a VCSP-instance on 4n + 1 Boolean
variables that represents a fitness landscapes with an ascent of length 10· 2n −9 (Theorem 2).

Consequences for the parameterized complexity of local search
Our star of gadgets construction and the prior chain of gadgets constructions (that we
overviewed in Section 1.2 and Figure 1) can be seen as part of a broader research goal of
uncovering the parameterized complexity of local search. Prior work has reduced both the
max-degree and the pathwidth to (more or less) as low as they can go, giving us a (close to)
tight characterization of how simple VCSPs can be in terms of max-degree and pathwidth
while still having some ascents, the steepest ascent, or all ascents, exponentially long. The

earliest results on long ascents focused on max degree, but it proved itself to be too coarse-
grained as a complexity parameter. Just by going from max degree two to three, Boolean
VCSPs go from P to NP [33] and longest ascents go from quadratic to exponential [18,20,31].
Treewidth and pathwidth are more fine-grained and can be used to separate local search
from non-local search since bounded treewidth VCSPs are tractable for non-local dynamic
programming algorithms [4, 6] but are still expressive enough to encode the exponential
ascents that stop strict local search from being efficient [22]. But these measures are still too
coarse-grained for discriminating between different strict local search algorithms for Boolean
VCSPs: at treewidth one, all strict local search is quadratic or better [18], by pathwidth
two exponential ascents exist for popular algorithms like greedy local search [20,31], and by
pathwidth three no strict local search algorithms are guaranteed to be efficient [22]. For a
more fine-grained parameterized complexity analysis of local search, we are forced to look for
more subtle properties of constraint graphs.
Given that our construction showed that a star of gadgets can work where only a
chain of gadgets was known before, it makes sense to move from a sparsity parameter like
pathwidth – that measures how how far the constraint graph is from a path/chain – to
a sparsity parameter like treedepth – that measures how far the constraint graph is from
a star. Given a tree T = (V, P) rooted at r0, let ≺T be the descendant relationship for
T. In other words, r ≺T s if the path from r to r0 in T passes through s and r ̸= s.
We say that T is a elimination-tree of binary VCSP-instance C = (V, S, w) if for every
{u, v} ∈S either u ≺T v or v ≺T u. The treedepth(G) is the minimal height(T) over all
elimination-trees of C. Treedepth is closely related to treewidth and pathwidth: any graph G
has treewidth(G) ≤pathwidth(G) ≤treedepth(G) −1 ≤treewidth(G) log n [5,15,28].
The only constraint graphs that have treedepth ≤1 are VCSPs with only unary constraints.
Unary VCSPs represent so-called smooth fitness landscapes. In a smooth fitness landscape,
each variable has a preferred assignment independent of all other variables. Thus, there is
a single fitness peak and all ascents to that peak are (very) short: all ascents have length
equal to the number of variables on which the initial assignment and the peak differ. For
treedepth one, the longest ascent, steepest ascent, and shortest ascent all have the same
length, so there is no separation in the linear bound.
The only constraint graphs that have treedepth ≤2 are disjoint unions of stars. In terms
of bounds on all ascents, stars are trees and trees have at most quadratic ascents [18]. Van
Marle [31] showed that for Boolean VCSPs whose constraint graph is a star the longest
possible ascent can have length at most n2/4, and gave an example that achieves this bound.
Van Marle [31] also showed that the steepest ascents in such problems can have length at
most 2(n −1), and gave an example that achieves this bound. In other words, for treedepth
two, or stars, we see a separation between bounds on some ascents versus steepest ascents.6
Before our paper, the big gap in treedepth was between all ascents being quadratic and
some ascents being exponential – we closed this gap. As we outlined in Section 1.2, all of the
prior constructions of exponential ascents with sparse constraint graphs are based around
chains of gadgets. Their constraint graphs therefore all contain a path of constraints of
length linear in the number n of variables. Any graph with a path of length Ω(n), must have
treedepth of Ω(log n) [5,28]. Thus, all prior chain constructions have treedepth of Ω(log n).
In contrast, we provided an exponential ascent from a star of gadgets construction that has

initial assignment. We expect that the answer to both questions is no, but to a different
extent. In our star of gadgets construction, given the initial assignment 0n, the steepest
ascent is a single step flipping the central variable to reach the peak 1(0)n−1. There are
prior tricks for converting an arbitrary long ascent into a steepest ascent [19,31], but these
tricks increase treedepth by at least one. So we expect that treedepth at least four will be
required for an exponential steepest ascent. As for all ascents exponential from some initial
assignment, we expect a more substantial gap, which we state in the positive form:
▶Conjecture 4. If a family of VCSPs has its treedepth bounded by a constant then given
any initial assignment, there exists an ascent of polynomial length to some local peak.
In other words, we do not expect our star of gadgets approach to provide a significant
improvement over existing chain of gadgets constructions of logarithmic treedepth with all
ascents exponentially long from some initial assignment. An essential feature of current chain
of gadget constructions is that at any given step along the long ascents, only one gadget is
“active” at a time, where a gadget is “active” if it has some fitness increasing potential change
in its variables. This “activation” of the gadget is moved left or right along the chain as the
ascent proceeds. For a “star” of gadgets, there is no similar concept of left or right and so
we expect the “centre” of the “star” to require on the order of log(n) bits to know which
gadget is “activated”. To accommodate this large “centre” (or implement it as a chain), we
expect that any construction with all ascents exponential will require logarithmic treedepth.
This also highlights one of the downsides of treedepth: “simple” classes like trees also
have logarithmic treedepth. Thus, the class of tree-structured binary Boolean VCSPs where
all ascents are provably quadratic [18] has the same logarithmic complexity in terms of
treedepth as chain constructions where all ascents are exponential [22,25,27]. It is awkward
to have a complexity parameter where a very natural class of easy instances (trees) has the
same high parameter values as the hardest instances. Thus, an alternative is to start at
trees and look for the smallest modification of them that drastically increases the length of
ascents. This was Kaznatcheev, Cohen, and Jeavons [18]’s initial motivation for looking at
treewidth – as a way of highlighting the fragility of local search: increase treewidth from 1
to 2 and quadratic transforms to exponential. Our results push this approach to stricter,
more fine-grained, sparsity parameters.
Our star of gadgets construction creates a sharp transition from quadratic to exponential
ascents under the more fine-grained sparsity parameter of feedback vertex set number. The
feedback vertex set number fvsn(G) is the fewest number of vertexes that one needs to remove
from a graph G so that only a forest remains. It is easy to see that the feedback vertex
set number is stricter than treewidth and pathwidth, with treewidth(G) ≤pathwidth(G) ≤
fvsn(G) + 1 for any graph G. For a tree (or forest), the feedback vertex set number is
zero and all ascents are quadratic [18]. For our construction, we add a single variable to a
forest structured VCSP, so the feedback vertex set number is one but there now exist some
exponential ascents. This is a stark contrast to the prior chain of gadgets constructions,
