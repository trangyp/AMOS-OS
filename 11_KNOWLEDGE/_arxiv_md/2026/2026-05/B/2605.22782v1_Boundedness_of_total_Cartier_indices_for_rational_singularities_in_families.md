1. Introduction
We work over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic 0.
A result of Greb–Kebekus–Peternell [GKP16, Theorem 1.10 and Remark 1.11] shows that,
for any variety of klt type X, there exists a positive integer I such that ID is Cartier for every
Q-Cartier Weil divisor D on X. Such a definition can be generalized beyond the category of klt
type varieties. More precisely, we may define the following.
Definition 1.1 (Total Cartier index). Let X be a normal noetherian scheme. For any Q-Cartier
Weil divisor D on X, the Cartier index of D is the minimal positive integer I such that ID is
Cartier. The total Cartier index of X is
tci(X) := lcm{Cartier indices of all Q-Cartier Weil divisors on X},
when this least common multiple is finite, and +∞otherwise.
A more general question to ask is whether in a bounded family of varieties X →B, we have
that tci(Xb) is bounded for any fiber. This is of course too optimistic as it is possible that
tci(Xb) = +∞for some b by considering a cone over an elliptic curve. Nevertheless, [HJ26,
Theorem 1.2] proved that, for a bounded family of varieties, the total Cartier indices of the
fibers that are of klt type are bounded. With this in mind, Han and Jiang asked the following:
Question 1.2 ([HJ26, Problem 4.5]). Are the total Cartier indices bounded for a bounded
family of varieties with rational singularities?

The goal of this paper is to provide an affirmative answer to Question 1.2:
Theorem 1.3. Let π : X →B be a projective morphism to a finite type k-scheme. Then there
exists a positive integer I satisfying the following. For any closed point b ∈B such that Xb is
normal projective of pure dimension with at most rational singularities, tci(Xb) divides I.
Strategy of the proof. We remark that [HJ26, Theorem 1.2] proved the case for klt type
fibers, which is a weaker condition than rational singularities. The proof of [HJ26, Theorem 1.2]
heavily relies on the minimal model program in families. In this paper, we choose an alternative
approach.
The proof has a local core and a global reduction. The local core is the algebra theorem,
namely Theorem 5.1: if a normal rational local ring is as a prime localization of a complex affine
scheme cut out in AN by a bounded number of equations of bounded degree, then the Cartier
index of every closed point is bounded in terms only of those numerical bounds.
The proof of Theorem 5.1 is by induction on the local dimension. Dimension ≤1 is immediate.
In every higher dimension, the induction first provides a uniform multiple of the divisor which
is Cartier on the punctured spectrum. The remaining index at closed points is then controlled
by the torsion in H2 of the topological link. For surfaces, we use Brieskorn’s computation of the
class group of rational complex surface singularities and Poincar´e duality for the link [Bri68].
In dimension ≥3, we use Koll´ar’s exponential sequence argument to inject the punctured local
Picard group into H2 of the link [Kol16]. A semialgebraic boundedness theorem, which comes
from real algebraic geometry [BPR06], gives a uniform bound for this torsion in bounded degree.
It remains to pass from closed complex points to the prime localizations which occur in the
induction. This is done by a finite residue field Noether normalization argument. After adjoining
an algebraic closure of the residue field, the prime localization becomes a faithfully flat local
base change of a closed point local ring over an algebraically closed field isomorphic to C. Flat
divisorial base change transports the divisor to this closed point model, and faithfully flat descent
then brings the resulting Cartierness back to the original local ring.
The global theorem follows by covering a projective family by finitely many affine charts of
bounded degree, applying the universal local theorem on each chart, and reducing global tci to
closed point local Cartier indices. The final passage from complex coefficients to an arbitrary
algebraically closed field of characteristic zero is by spreading out the family, the point, and the
divisor over a finitely generated field, embedding that field into C, applying the complex result,
and descending Cartierness by faithfully flat local base change.
Remark 1.4. Although out of the topic of this paper, it is worthy to mention that since our
approach does not rely on the minimal model program, a lot of questions in moduli theory of
structures with rational singularities (including some already solved questions in KSBA moduli
and K-moduli theory) might be able to be solved in an easier and more straightforward way.
We leave the applications to readers of potential interest.
Organization. Section 2 recalls some preliminaries and algebraic tools that will be used in the
rest of the paper. Section 3 contains the topological input from semialgebraic topology, surface
rational singularities, and higher-dimensional local Picard groups. Section 4 proves the bounded
degree local residual estimates, including the Noether normalization reduction from arbitrary
prime localizations to closed points. Section 5 proves the universal local theorem. Section 6
globalizes the local theorem, makes field descent, and proves Theorem 1.3.
