Abstract. The notion of a semitransitive binary action of a group
G on a topological space is introduced. A duality theorem is proved,
establishing a bijective correspondence between semitransitive distribu-
tive binary G-spaces and topological fields whose multiplicative group
is isomorphic to G. This result yields an equivalence between the cate-
gory of semitransitive distributive binary G-spaces and the category of
topological fields with multiplicative group G.
As applications of the duality theorem, two important results are
established.
It is shown that a finite group can act semitransitively,
distributively, and binarily only on finite sets whose cardinality is a
power of a prime number. A complete characterization of those groups
that can appear as multiplicative groups of topological fields is also
obtained.
1. Introduction
Representations of groups by unary operations (permutations, bijections,
transformations) on sets play an important role in many branches of math-
ematics and its applications. Similarly, representations of groups by binary
operations (binary transformations) on sets occupy an important place in
algebra and other areas of mathematics.
The set C(X) of all continuous maps from a topological space X to it-
self, endowed with the compact-open topology, is a topological monoid under
composition of maps, with the identity map as the unit. All invertible el-
ements of this monoid form the homeomorphism group H(X) of the space
X.
To construct the group of binary transformations of a topological space
X, one should consider the set C2(X) of all continuous maps from X2 to X
and define a natural group operation on it. There are various ways to define
the product (composition) of two binary operations φ and ψ belonging to
C2(X). However, the most natural products are those given by the formulas
φψ(x, y) = φ(x, ψ(x, y)),

group of binary transformations of the space X. The group H(X) embeds
(algebraically and topologically) into the group H2(X) [4]. Thus, the group
of binary transformations H2(X) is a natural extension of the homeomor-

Any topological group that acts effectively on a space X is isomorphic
to some topological subgroup of the group H(X) [5].
Consequently, the
homeomorphism group H(X) is a universal object for topological groups
acting on the space X. Similarly, the group of binary transformations H2(X)
serves as a universal object for topological groups acting binarily on X.
The foundation of the theory of binary G-spaces, or groups of binary
transformations, was initiated in [3].
This theory was further developed
in [4,6–11]. In the theory of binary G-spaces, a special role is played by dis-
tributive binary G-spaces, because important concepts and results of equi-
variant topology extend to them [7,8,10]. In particular, a complete classifica-
tion of transitive binary actions has been obtained for the class of distributive
binary G-spaces [11].
In the present paper, we introduce the notion of a semitransitive binary
G-space and solve the problem of classifying semitransitive binary actions in
the class of distributive binary G-spaces. It is proved that every semitran-
sitive distributive binary G-space admits a structure of a topological field
compatible with the given binary action (Theorem 3.11). Thus, we estab-
lish a one-to-one correspondence between semitransitive distributive binary
G-spaces and topological fields with the multiplicative group G (the dual-
ity theorem 3.13). This correspondence is compatible with isomorphisms of
the corresponding objects. Hence, the category of semitransitive distribu-
tive binary G-spaces is equivalent to the category of topological fields whose
multiplicative group is isomorphic to G. It should be noted that the proof of
Theorem 3.11 relies on a number of ideas presented in [12], which is devoted
to the study of distributive systems of operations.
Theorem 3.13, like many duality theorems, has important applications.
One of them concerns the description of possible underlying sets of semi-
transitive distributive binary actions of finite groups: if there exists a semi-
transitive distributive binary action of a finite group G on a space X, then
the cardinality of this space is a power of a prime number (Corollary 3.15).

Another important application of Theorem 3.13 is related to the problem
of describing multiplicative groups of topological fields. This problem has
deep algebraic roots. As early as 1960, A. I. Maltsev formulated the hypoth-
esis that it is impossible to describe multiplicative groups of fields by means
of first-order formulas. This hypothesis was subsequently confirmed. Never-
theless, the problem is solvable using second-order formulas. The following
assertion holds: a topological group G is the multiplicative group of some
topological field if and only if it admits a binary representation possessing
the properties of semitransitivity and distributivity (Theorem 3.16).
2. Binary G-spaces and Transitivity
In this section, we recall the necessary definitions and present results from
biequivariant topology [3,4,6–8,11] that will be used in what follows.
Let X be a topological space, and let C2(X) be the set of all continuous
binary operations on X. On the set C2(X), define the multiplication of two
elements by the formula
(2.1)
φψ(x, y) = φ(x, ψ(x, y)),
where φ, ψ ∈C2(X) and x, y ∈X.
Proposition 2.1 ( [3]). The space C2(X), endowed with the compact-open
topology, is a topological monoid under the operation (2.1), whose unit is
e(x, y) = y.
The invertible elements of the monoid C2(X) are called binary transforma-
tions of the space X. Denote by H2(X) the group of all invertible elements
of the monoid C2(X), that is, the group of binary transformations of the
space X.
The following assertion holds [4].
Theorem 2.2. Let f ∈H2(X) be a binary transformation of the space X.
Then, for every a ∈X, the map fa : X →X defined by
(2.2)
fa(x) = f(a, x)
is a homeomorphism of the space X.
A binary operation φ ∈C2(X) is called a continuous left quasigroup oper-
ation on X if the following condition holds: for any elements a and b in X,
the equation φ(a, x) = b has a unique solution x ∈X.
Theorem 2.2 immediately implies the following assertion.
Proposition 2.3. Let f ∈H2(X) be a binary transformation of the space
X. Then f is a left quasigroup operation on X.
Thus, all elements of the group H2(X) are continuous left quasigroup
operations on X.
Let G be a topological group. A continuous map α : G×X2 →X is called
a binary action of the topological group G on the space X if the following
conditions hold:

It is easy to verify that any topological group G acts binarily on itself by
means of the conjugate left translation

θ : G →H2(X)
from the group G to the group H2(X) of all binary transformations of the
space X.
The kernel of this homomorphism θ is called the kernel of the binary action
α and is denoted by Ker α. Thus,
Ker α = {g ∈G | g(x, y) = y for all x, y ∈X}.
This set is a closed normal subgroup of the group G.
The binary action α is called effective if Ker α = {e}. Since any binary
action of the group G on X with kernel Ker α induces an effective binary
action of the group G/Ker α on X, we will henceforth consider only effective
binary actions.
A binary representation of a topological group G is its continuous homo-
morphism into the group H2(X), where X is some topological space. Since
every binary action of a group G on a space X generates a binary repre-
sentation G →H2(X) and, conversely, every such representation defines a
binary action on X, a binary representation of the group G may be regarded
as its binary action on the corresponding space X.
Let (G, X, α) and (G, Y, β) be binary G-spaces. A continuous map f :
X →Y is called biequivariant if
f(α(g, x, y)) = β(g, f(x), f(y)),
or, equivalently,
f(g(x, y)) = g(f(x), f(y)),
for all g ∈G and x, y ∈X.

A biequivariant map f : X →Y that is a homeomorphism is called an
equivalence of binary G-spaces, or a biequimorphism. All binary G-spaces
together with biequivariant maps form a category.
A binary G-space (G, X, α) is called distributive if
g(x, h(y, z)) = h(g(x, y), g(x, z))
for all g, h ∈G and x, y, z ∈X. In this case, α is called a distributive binary
action of the group G on X.
The binary action of a group G on itself by means of the conjugate left
translation
g(x, y) = xgx−1y
is a distributive binary action.
For arbitrary g ∈G and x ∈X, consider the map gx : X →X defined by
gx(y) = g(x, y),
y ∈X.
The following criterion for distributivity of a binary action of a group G on
X holds.
Theorem 2.4 ( [11]). A binary G-space X is distributive if and only if one
of the following conditions holds:
1) the map gx : X →X is a biequivariant homeomorphism for all x ∈X;
2) the equality gxhy = hgx(y)gx holds for all g, h ∈G and x, y ∈X.
The set
G(x,y) = {g ∈G | g(x, y) = y},
where (x, y) is a fixed pair of points of the binary G-space X, is a closed
subgroup of the group G. This subgroup is called the stationary subgroup of
the pair (x, y). The subgroup G(x,x) is called the stationary subgroup of the
point x.
In general, the stationary subgroup G(x,y) is not a normal subgroup of the
group G. However, the following assertion holds.
Proposition 2.5 ( [11]). Let X be a distributive binary G-space, and let
x ∈X be an arbitrary point. Then the stationary subgroup G(x,x) is a closed
normal subgroup of the group G.
A binary action of a group G on X is called transitive if G(x, x) = X for
every x ∈X. In this case, X is called a transitive binary G-space [11]. In
fact, transitivity of a binary action implies that G(x, y) = X for all x, y ∈X.
Let H be a closed normal subgroup of the group G. The map
α : G × G/H × G/H →G/H,
defined by
(2.4)
g(kH, lH) = (kgk−1l)H,
where g, k, l ∈G and g(kH, lH) = α(g, kH, lH), is a transitive distributive
binary action of the group G on G/H.

From Theorem 3.11 and Proposition 3.2 the following important theorem
follows directly.
Theorem 3.13 (Duality theorem). There exists a one-to-one correspon-
dence between the following objects:
1) semitransitive distributive binary G-spaces;
2) topological fields whose multiplicative group is isomorphic to G.
Remark 3.14. The correspondence established in the last theorem is com-
patible with isomorphisms of the corresponding objects. Thus, Theorem 3.13
establishes an equivalence between the category of semitransitive distributive
binary G-spaces and the category of topological fields whose multiplicative
group is isomorphic to G.
Theorem 3.13, like any duality theorem, opens the way to a wide range
of applications. One of them is related to the classification of semitransitive
distributive binary actions of finite groups. In particular, from Theorem 3.13,
taking into account the structure of finite fields, the following statement
follows directly.
Corollary 3.15. Let G be a finite group, and let X be a semitransitive
distributive binary G-space. Then |X| = pn, where p is a prime number and
n is a natural number.
Not every topological group admits a semitransitive distributive binary
action on a topological space. By Theorem 3.13, the problem of describing
such groups is equivalent to the problem of classifying groups that can be
multiplicative groups of topological fields.
The classical problem of describing multiplicative groups of fields is one of
the important and difficult problems in algebra (see [2,13,14]). In 1960, at
the Third Colloquium on General Algebra in Moscow, A. I. Maltsev conjec-
tured that the class of multiplicative groups of fields cannot be characterized
elementarily, i.e., by first-order formulas [15]. This conjecture was proved
in [16] (see also [17]). However, multiplicative groups of fields can be char-
acterized by second-order formulas or hyperidentities [2].
From Theorem 3.13 the following characterization of multiplicative groups
of topological fields follows directly.
Theorem 3.16. A group G is the multiplicative group of some topological
field if and only if there exists a semitransitive and distributive binary repre-
sentation of G.
