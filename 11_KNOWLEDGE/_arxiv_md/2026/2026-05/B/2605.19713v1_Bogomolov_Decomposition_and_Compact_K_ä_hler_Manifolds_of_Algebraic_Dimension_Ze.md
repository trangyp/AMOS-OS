1. Some notation and terminology.
X is an n-dimensional compact connected complex manifold (some-
times just normal if explicitly said).
A subvariety is an irreducible
compact analytic subset Z ⊂X. The ‘general’ (resp. ‘generic’) point
of X is a point lying outside countably (resp.
ﬁnitely) many strict
subvarieties of X depending on the situation considered. A subvariety
is ‘trivial’ if either a point or X.
A ﬁbration f : X →Y is a surjective holomorphic map (regular
model of a meromorphic ﬁbration) with connected ﬁbres. Its general
ﬁbre Xy is the one lying over a general point y of Y .
We denote by BC(X) the Barlet-Chow cycle space of X, by BC(X/Y )
its Zariski-closed subset consisting of cycles contained in some ﬁbre of
f : X →Y , equipped with its ﬁbre map: f∗: BC(X/Y ) →Y . We
refer to [3] for the construction, properties with proofs of this space.
Especially for the compactness of its components when X is Kähler.

2. Introduction: Bimeromorphic classification of
compact Kähler manifolds.
Let Xn be a compact complex manifold of dimension n. Let 0 ≤
a(X) ≤n be its ‘algebraic dimension’, that is the transcendence de-
gree over C of its ﬁeld of meromorphic functions M(X). If a(X) = n, X
is ‘Moishezon’, that is: bimeromorphic to a projective manifold. At the
other extreme, a(X) = 0, and the number of irreducible divisors of X is
ﬁnite ([37]). In the intermediate case, there is a unique (up to bimero-
morphic equivalence) ﬁbration aX : X →AX, called the ‘algebraic
reduction’ of X, which induces a ﬁeld isomorphism aX : M(AX) →
M(X), and so dim(AX) = a(X). In the non-Kähler case, from dimen-
sion 3 on, no classiﬁcation scheme emerges, by the extreme ﬂexibility
of twistor spaces of anti self dual riemannian fourfolds. The main rea-
son being that covering analytic families of cycles of codimension 2 or
more do not produce ﬁbrations, these families building non-compact
components of the Barlet-Chow space BC(X).
When X is Kähler, or in the class C (i.e: bimeromorphic to Kähler1),
the situation is opposite: the space BC(X) has compact components2,
and covering families of cycles produce ﬁbrations. In particular, al-
gebraic reductions ([13], [29]), as well as Albanese maps ([14]) can be
constructed in the relative setting of ﬁbrations.
• These two tools permit to essentially reduce the structure of any X
in C to the special case where a(X) = 0 as follows: Iterating the relative
algebraic reduction, one gets a ﬁbration f : X →Y with general ﬁbre
Xy such that a(Xy) = 0, Y not containing any nontrivial subvariety
V ⊂Y with a(V ) = 0 ([13], [29]). Moreover, the structure of Y is well
understood. See §8 for some more details.
• The remaining task is then to describe the compact Kähler man-
ifolds of algebraic dimension zero. We will give a conditional answer
(in Theorem 7 below) to this question, in terms of the special cases
of ‘Kummer’ and ‘simple non-Kummer’ manifolds (introduced in [31]),
the latter ones being conjecturally ‘bimeromorphically symplectic’.
In short: assuming conjecture 3 below, all compact Kähler manifolds
are constructed from familiar ones: projective, tori and hyperkähler.
See Theorem 7 for the case a(X) = 0, and §8 for the general case.
This conjecture and decomposition was conceived in the mid 1980’s,
inspired by [8], [9], [10], esp. Remark 6.1, and partly by [30].
