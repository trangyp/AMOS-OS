Abstract
This paper studies the zero-divisor graphs attached to several finite chain-ring fam-
ilies and computes the homological invariants of their edge ideals by using cochordal
constructible systems. We begin with a general layered graph C(q, L), whose vertices
are arranged according to valuation layers and whose adjacency is governed by the
single rule k + ℓ≥L, form some integers k and ℓ. This graph models the zero-divisor
structure of a finite chain ring with residue field of order q and nilpotency index L. We
prove that C(q, L) is cochordal, determine its type sequence, then correct and refine the
Betti formula of its edge ideal [Dung and Vu, Cochordal zero divisor graphs and Betti
numbers of their edge ideals, Comm. Algebra 54(2) (2026) 736–744]. The results are
then specialized to the Gaussian quotient rings Z2m[i] and to the truncated polynomial
rings Zp[x]/(xc). We compute projective dimension, regularity, independence number,
height, Hilbert series, and Cohen–Macaulay behavior. The computations show that
these quotient rings have 2-linear resolutions, while Cohen–Macaulayness occurs only
in the expected degenerate or complete-graph cases.

Introduction
The zero-divisor graph of a commutative ring records multiplication-to-zero by a simple
graph. Since Beck’s original study of colorings of commutative rings [7] and the subsequent
structural formulation of Anderson and Livingston [3], this graph has served as a useful
interface between ring theory and finite graph theory. For the residue class ring Z/nZ, the
graph is especially concrete, as its vertices are the nonzero residues whose greatest common
divisor with n is not one, and two distinct vertices x, y are adjacent precisely when n divides
xy.
The study of zero-divisor graphs began with Beck’s work on coloring problems for
commutative rings [7], and was later developed systematically by Anderson and Livingston,
who introduced the standard zero-divisor graph associated with a commutative ring [3].
Subsequent work investigated structural properties of these graphs, for example, Akbari,
Maimani and Yassemi characterized important cases in which zero-divisor graphs are planar
[1], while Anderson and Badawi further refined the theory by studying zero-divisor graphs
from the perspective of ring-theoretic annihilation and adjacency relations [2]. In [4], the
authors showed that zero divisor graphs are universal graphs.
The graph-theoretic simplicity of Γ(Z/nZ) hides a rather rich homological problem. If
G is a finite simple graph on vertices x1, . . . , xs, its edge ideal is the squarefree quadratic

monomial ideal I(G) = (xixj : {xi, xj} ∈E(G)) in a standard graded polynomial ring. The
minimal free resolution of S/I(G) measures the arrangement of edges, induced subgraphs,
and vertex covers of G in a way that is sensitive enough to detect induced matchings and
chordality, see the standard references [24, 46].
A decisive point is Fröberg’s theorem: the edge ideal I(G) has a 2-linear resolution if and
only if the complement graph G is chordal [19]. Thus, a cochordal graph is not only a graph
with a forbidden-cycle condition in its complement, but also a graph whose edge ideal has
the cleanest possible homological shape. For such a graph all nonzero graded Betti numbers
of S/I(G) lie on the linear strand βi,i+1, and hence the total Betti number βi is already the
relevant graded invariant.
The residue class graphs Γ(Z/nZ) therefore provide a natural test family for the interac-
tion between arithmetic and homological algebra. The arithmetic supplies valuation classes,
and the edge relation becomes an inequality on the exponents of prime factors. The homo-
logical task is to convert this valuation data into a free resolution. Earlier work by Rather,
Imran and Diene [41] and by Rather, Imran and Pirzada [42] computed parts of the linear
strand in several small arithmetic families, including cases such as p4, p2q, and pqr. Pirzada
and Rather [34] studied the linear strand of edge ideals of some zero divisor graphs.
A recent manuscript of Dung and Vu [15] proposed a broader method based on cochordal
constructible systems and type sequences. The manuscript proves, in essence, that Γ(Z/nZ)
is cochordal exactly when n has one of the forms pa, paq, or pqr, where the primes appearing
are distinct. It also proposes closed Betti-number formulae for the corresponding edge ideals.

The purpose of this study is to carry out an analogous program for other zero divisor
graphs: (1) Γ(Rm) of the Gaussian integer ring modulo powers of 2, that is, Rm = Z2m[i] ∼=
Z[i]/(2m), where m is a positive integer, and (2) for zero divisor graph Γ(Rp,c) of the truncated
polynomial rings Rp,c = Zp[x]/⟨xc⟩, where p is prime and c ≥2 is a positive integer. Our
goals are the following:
(1) Determine the type sequence of Γ(Rm) explicitly and, compute all Betti numbers of
the edge ideal I(Γ(Rm)), which gives a closed formulas for the projective dimension and
regularity.
(2) We study the Cohen–Macaulay property and the Hilbert series of S/I(Γ(Rm)). We show
that S/I(Γ(Rm)) is Cohen–Macaulay only for m = 1, while for m ≥2 the depth is 1 and
the dimension is at least 2. We further give a combinatorial description of the Hilbert series
of S/I(Γ(Rm)) in terms of independent sets in Γ(Rm), viewed as the Stanley–Reisner ring
of the independence complex, in the spirit of Stanley [45]. A similar type of study is carried
for the zero divisor graph Γ(Rp,c).

We now recall the external results used later. The following known result characterizes
graphs whose edge ideals have a linear resolution.
Theorem 2.1 (Fröberg, see [19]). For a finite simple graph G, the edge ideal I(G) has a
2-linear resolution if and only if G is chordal. Equivalently, G is cochordal if and only if
I(G) has a 2-linear resolution.
The following known form of Dirac’s theorem is used only through its consequence for
chordal elimination.
Theorem 2.2 (Dirac, see [13, 25]). Every noncomplete chordal graph has at least two nonad-
jacent simplicial vertices. Consequently, chordal graphs admit perfect elimination orderings.
The following result is the standard Betti-splitting principle for monomial ideals.
Theorem 2.3 (Francisco–Hà–Van Tuyl, see [18]). Let I = J+K be a splitting of a monomial
ideal satisfying the usual splitting-function hypotheses. Then, for all i, j,
βi,j(I) = βi,j(J) + βi,j(K) + βi−1,j(J ∩K).
In particular, the total Betti numbers satisfy the corresponding ungraded equality.
The following known specialized splitting result is the form needed for quadratic edge
ideals.
Lemma 2.4 (Nguyen–Vu, see [33]). Let I be a quadratic monomial ideal and let x be a
variable. If I = xP + J is the x-partition of I, where P is generated by variables and J is
generated by the quadratic monomials not divisible by x, then this decomposition is a Betti
splitting whenever the hypotheses of the quadratic splitting criterion hold; in particular, it
applies in the cochordal elimination steps used below.
The following known observation identifies a useful colon ideal at a cochordal elimination
step.
Proposition 2.5 (Jaramillo–Villarreal, see [28]). If G is cochordal, then the edge ideal I(G)
admits an elimination step I(G) = xP + J in which P = I(G) : x is a monomial prime

Cohen–Macaulay properties
The quotient rings considered in this paper have linear resolutions, but they are almost never
Cohen–Macaulay. The result below separates linearity from Cohen–Macaulayness and gives
a complete classification inside the cochordal residue-class families.


## Page 37

37
We use two standard facts from Stanley–Reisner theory. First, S/I(G) is Cohen–Macaulay
only if the independence complex of G is pure, or equivalently, G must be well-covered.
Second, by the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula, depth(S/I(G)) = N −pd(S/I(G)) for
N = |V (G)|, see [8, 46].
The following theorem gives the complete Cohen–Macaulay classification for the cochordal
families.
Theorem 9.1. Let n ≥2 and suppose that Γ(Z/nZ) is cochordal. Then S/I(Γ(Z/nZ)) is
Cohen–Macaulay if and only if n is prime or n = p2 for some prime p.
Proof. In order to prove that S/I(G) is not Cohen–Macaulay, it is enough to exhibit two
maximal independent sets of different sizes.
First suppose that n = p is prime.
Then
Z/pZ is a field, so it has no nonzero zero-divisors.
Hence Γ(Z/pZ) has no vertices and
I(Γ(Z/pZ)) = 0. The quotient is just the ground field K, and is therefore Cohen–Macaulay.
Now suppose that n = p2. The nonzero zero-divisors modulo p2 are precisely the nonzero
multiples of p, and therefore Γ(Z/p2Z) = Kp−1. For N = p −1, the minimal vertex covers of
KN are exactly the sets obtained by deleting one vertex from V (KN). Hence every minimal
vertex cover has size N −1 = p −2, so ht I(KN) = p −2. By Corollary 5.3, we also have
pd(S/I(KN)) = p −2. The Auslander–Buchsbaum formula gives
depth(S/I(KN)) = N −pd(S/I(KN)) = (p −1) −(p −2) = 1.
On the other hand,
dim(S/I(KN)) = N −ht I(KN) = (p −1) −(p −2) = 1.
Thus depth(S/I(KN)) = dim(S/I(KN)), and S/I(KN) is Cohen–Macaulay.
Now assume n = pa with a ≥3. Any vertex in Va−1 is adjacent to every other vertex of
Γ(Z/paZ), so a singleton from Va−1 is a maximal independent set. On the other hand, V1
contains at least two vertices and is independent, since 2 < a. Extending V1 to a maximal
independent set gives a maximal independent set of size at least two. Thus the graph is not
well-covered, and S/I is not Cohen–Macaulay.
Next let n = paq with distinct primes. If a = 1, then Γ(Z/pqZ) = Kq−1,p−1, whose two
bipartition classes are maximal independent sets of different sizes because p ̸= q. Hence
the graph is not well-covered. If a ≥2, the union A1 ∪· · · ∪Aa is a maximal independent
set of size (q −1)pa−1. Also, B0 ∪{b}, where b is one fixed vertex of Ba−1, is a maximal
independent set of size pa−1(p −1) + 1. These two sizes are different, since equality would
imply (q −p)pa−1 = 1, impossible for a ≥2 and distinct primes. Therefore S/I is not
Cohen–Macaulay.
Finally let n = pqr with p < q < r. The set Ap ∪Aq ∪Ar is a maximal independent set.
The set Bqr ∪Aq ∪Ar is also maximal independent, as every excluded vertex in Ap, Bpq, or
Bpr is adjacent to an included vertex. Their sizes differ by
|Ap| −|Bqr| = (q −1)(r −1) −(p −1) > 0.

Conclusion and future work
In this paper, we developed a uniform chain-ring framework for studying zero-divisor graphs
through their valuation layers. The central object was the layered graph C(q, L), whose
adjacency relation is determined by the inequality k + ℓ≥L. This simple rule captures the
zero-divisor graph of a finite chain ring with residue field of order q and nilpotency index L,
and it allows the graph-theoretic and homological computations to be carried out without
treating each ring element separately.
Using the cochordal constructible-system method, we proved that C(q, L) is cochordal
and obtained an explicit type sequence. Substituting this type sequence into the corrected
Dung and Vu [15] formula gave a closed expression for all graded Betti numbers of the
quotient by the edge ideal. In particular, for I = I(C(q, L)) ⊆S, we obtained a 2-linear
resolution whenever I ̸= 0, together with the projective dimension pdS(S/I) = qL−1 −2.
The correction term in the Betti formula is essential, as it records the global overlap created
by the ordered star construction and prevents overcounting.
The general theory was applied first to the Gaussian quotient Rm = Z2m[i] ∼= Z[i]/(1 +
i)2m. Since Rm is a finite chain ring with residue field of size 2 and nilpotency index
2m, its zero-divisor graph is precisely C(2, 2m).
This identification gives the type se-
quence, the complete Betti table, the projective dimension, and the regularity of S/I(Γ(Rm)).
We also computed the independence number α(Γ(Rm)) = 22m−1 −2m + 1 and height
height I(Γ(Rm)) = 2m −2. Consequently, S/I(Γ(Rm)) is Cohen–Macaulay only for m = 1.
The same method was then applied to the truncated polynomial rings Rp,c = Zp[x]/(xc).
Here the valuation layers are the sets mk \ mk+1, and their sizes are pc−k−1(p −1). The
adjacency condition is again exactly k +ℓ≥c, so Γ(Rp,c) ∼= C(p, c). This gives closed formu-
lae for the graded Betti numbers, projective dimension, independence number, height, and
Hilbert series. The Cohen–Macaulay classification is especially clean: for c ≥2, the quotient
S/I(Γ(Rp,c)) is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if c = 2. The graph Γ(Z2m[i]) is C(2, 2m), while
Γ(Zp[x]/(xc)) is C(p, c). Thus both are threshold graphs with valuation weights, and their
previously computed invariants are threshold-graph invariants as well.

lae. On one hand, the Hilbert series is obtained from the independence polynomial of the

Several directions remain open.
First, it would be useful to extend the method be-
yond chain rings to finite principal ideal rings with more than one maximal ideal, where
the valuation-layer picture is replaced by a multi-indexed divisor structure. Second, one
may study finer invariants of the independence complexes arising here, such as shellability,
sequential Cohen–Macaulayness, and levelness. Third, the explicit type sequences suggest
possible algorithms for computing Betti tables of larger classes of cochordal zero-divisor
graphs without using Hochster’s formula directly. Finally, the comparison between graph
isomorphism classes of zero-divisor graphs and ring-theoretic isomorphism classes remains a
natural problem: the examples in this paper show that different rings may produce the same
zero-divisor graph, but a systematic description of this phenomenon is still incomplete.
