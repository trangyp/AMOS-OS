Boundary Geometry Turns Entanglement into Steering
Yu-Xuan Zhang1 and Jing-Ling Chen2, ∗
1School of Physics, Nankai University, Tianjin 300071, People’s Republic of China
2Theoretical Physics Division, Chern Institute of Mathematics,
Nankai University, Tianjin 300071, People’s Republic of China
(Dated: May 21, 2026)
Entanglement does not in general imply Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen steering.
We identify a
boundary-geometric mechanism that closes this gap on product-null boundary strata of two-qubit
state space, where Bob’s conditional states touch the boundary of the Bloch ball.
The key ob-
struction is local: if a projective assemblage approaches a Bloch-sphere boundary contact with a
first-order tangential displacement but only a second-order inward defect, then no finite-measure
local-hidden-state model can reproduce it. For two-qubit states with a product vector in the kernel,
this boundary contact is exactly the tangency of Bob’s steering ellipsoid to the Bloch sphere. At such
a product-null tangency, a single tangential coherence controls both partial-transpose negativity and
the boundary-contact scaling obstruction. The same boundary minor gives a compact experimental
witness: once the product-null contact is verified or guaranteed, the tangential coherence supplies
the steering signal. Consequently, every entangled two-qubit rank-two state, and every entangled
rank-three state whose null space is spanned by a product vector, is two-way projectively steerable.
The same boundary idea extends to arbitrary steering cuts: the Bloch-sphere contact is replaced
by a rank-deficient trusted conditional state, and support-kernel first-order coherence implies both
NPT entanglement and projective steering.
Introduction.— Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen steering is a
form of quantum nonlocality in which measurements per-
formed by an untrusted party remotely prepare condi-
tional states for a trusted party in a way that cannot be
explained by a local-hidden-state (LHS) model [2, 3, 6–8].
It occupies an intermediate position in the hierarchy of
quantum correlations: Bell nonlocality =⇒steering =⇒
entanglement, while the reverse implications fail in gen-
eral [4–8]. In particular, there exist entangled states ad-
mitting classical hidden-variable or local-hidden-state de-
scriptions for relevant measurement classes, and steering
itself may be directional [5, 9]. Thus entanglement alone
is not usually a steering certificate. The question we ad-
dress is when this gap can close.
Rather than optimizing a steering inequality for a pre-
scribed set of measurements, we ask whether the geom-
etry of the state itself can remove the freedom available
to an LHS model. Boundary points are natural places
to look for such a mechanism: near the boundary of the
trusted state space, a hidden-state decomposition has less
room to absorb small coherent perturbations than it has
in the interior. The answer developed here follows this
boundary route and is tied to the null space of the bipar-
tite state. A product vector in the kernel is not merely
a rank defect. It has an operational meaning for steer-
ing: it can force a trusted conditional state to acquire
a zero direction. For a trusted qubit, this means that
the conditional state touches the boundary of the Bloch
ball. In the steering-ellipsoid picture, Bob’s conditional
states form an ellipsoid inside the Bloch ball, and such a
product-null condition produces a tangency between this
ellipsoid and the Bloch sphere [11]. We show that this
boundary contact is the place where entanglement can
become steering.

Conclusion.— We have shown that a product vector
in the kernel of a two-qubit state can turn entanglement
into projective steering. The mechanism is local and ge-
ometric. The product-null condition creates a boundary
contact of Bob’s conditional states with the Bloch sphere,
while a nonzero tangential coherence makes this contact
nondegenerate. At such a contact, the assemblage has
first-order tangential motion but only second-order in-
ward defect. A finite-measure LHS model cannot repro-
duce this scaling, because it would have to keep a uniform
positive hidden measure in punctured caps shrinking to
the contact point.
For two-qubit product-null states, the same tangential
coherence has an algebraic meaning: it is the off-diagonal
entry of a negative partial-transpose minor.
Thus the
coefficient that certifies NPT entanglement is also the
coefficient that triggers the boundary-contact scaling ob-
struction. This gives an exact collapse of the hierarchy
on the product-null boundary, Ent ⇐⇒TPS, and in par-
ticular yields the rank-three product-null branch, with
rank-two and pure entangled states as lower-rank conse-
quences. Operationally, the same observation turns the
boundary partial-transpose minor into a minimal steer-
ing certificate once the product-null contact is exact or
guaranteed by the source.
Finally, the same idea extends beyond two qubits. In
a general cut X|Y , a rank-deficient trusted conditional
state together with a first-order coupling between its oc-
cupied part and its zero direction produces the same
two-dimensional boundary scaling after restriction. This
yields both projective steering across the fixed cut and
an NPT minor. The result clarifies why the product-null
boundary is special: in the interior of the trusted state
space, an LHS model has enough geometric freedom to
absorb small coherences, while at a boundary contact
the zero direction removes this freedom. The mechanism
therefore points to a broader principle: steering can be
forced not only by how much entanglement a state con-
tains, but by how that entanglement meets the boundary
of the trusted state space.
