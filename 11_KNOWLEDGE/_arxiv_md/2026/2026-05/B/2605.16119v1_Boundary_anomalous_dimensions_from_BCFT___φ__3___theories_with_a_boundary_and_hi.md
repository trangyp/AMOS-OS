Boundary anomalous dimensions from BCFT:
ϕ3 theories with a boundary and higher-derivative
generalizations
Yongwei Guoa,b and Wenliang Lia
aSchool of Physics, Sun Yat-sen University, Guangzhou 510275, China
bShing-Tung Yau Center and School of Physics, Southeast University, Nanjing, 210096, China
E-mail: liwliang3@mail.sysu.edu.cn
Abstract: We consider the bulk ϕ3 deformation of the free boundary conformal field
theory in the ϵ expansion. We determine the leading corrections to the scaling dimensions
of boundary fundamental operators and some boundary operator expansion coefficients.
Our procedure combines the conformal multiplet recombination with the boundary crossing
symmetry. The results cover both the single field case and the multi-field case with SN+1
global symmetry, which are associated with the Yang-Lee model and the (N +1)-state Potts
model respectively. These semi-infinite models describe branched polymers, percolation,
and spanning forest at a surface. We generalize these results to some higher derivative
theories.
In addition, we study the ϕ2n+1 theories with n > 1, but only obtain some
boundary operator expansion coefficients.

The conformal field theory (CFT) with a ϕ3 interaction has many interesting physical
applications.
For the single field case, the ϕ3 theory describes the critical behaviors of
Yang-Lee edge singularity, as well as branched polymers 1 in two dimensions higher due
to the Parisi-Sourlas dimensional reduction [1–5].2

Our goal is to determine the anomalous dimension ˆγΦ of the boundary fundamental
operator Φ. Let us explain the problems of using only the multiplet recombination method
or boundary crossing symmetry. A naive application of the multiplet recombination does
not fully determine ˆγΦ. More specifically, according to the previous work on the ϕ2n case
[24], one may try to study the bulk-boundary two-point function ⟨ϕ(x)Φ(ˆy)⟩. (The hat
indicates coordinates on the boundary.) This does not work because the multiplet recombi-
nation relates it to the vanishing free correlator ⟨ϕ2(x)Φ(ˆy)⟩f. In other words, the multiplet
recombination only leads to a constraint on the order of the anomalous dimension. On the
other hand, if one considers the crossing symmetry for ⟨ϕ(x)ϕ(y)⟩, the boundary crossing
equation involves infinite numbers of contributions in both channels. As a result, a direct
analysis of the crossing equation is more challenging than those in the ϕ2n cases.
In fact, the combination of the two methods leads to a relatively simple derivation
of the leading term of ˆγΦ in the ϵ expansion. In more details, there are infinitely many
unknown BOE coefficients in the boundary channel expansion of ⟨ϕ(x)ϕ(y)⟩. Most of them
can be fixed using the multiplet recombination. Then, ⟨ϕ(x)ϕ(y)⟩is reduced to a function
with a finite number of unknown parameters, and thus can be directly expanded in the
bulk OPE limit. By matching with the bulk channel expansion, the unknown parameters
in the boundary channel are determined, which include the anomalous dimension and the
BOE coefficient of Φ.

Discussion
In this paper, we considered the ϕ3 theory with a boundary and their higher-derivative
generalizations. We used the multiplet recombination in conjunction with the boundary
crossing symmetry, and we determined the anomalous dimensions of the boundary funda-
mental operators. For the ϕ2n+1 theories with n > 1, we only determined some leading
BOE coefficients.
For the ϕ3 theory, both the single field and SN+1-symmetric multi-field cases are stud-
ied. In the CFT without a boundary, the large N limit of the multi-field case corresponds
to N + 1 decoupled theories with a single field [76]. This is no longer the case in the pres-
ence of a boundary. In some sense, the boundary acts as a nonlocal probe, allowing us to
distinguish CFTs that local probes alone cannot tell apart.
In ϕ2n theories with a boundary, the anomalous dimensions of the boundary composite
operators are found in [24, 36]. This is achieved using the analyticity of bulk-boundary-
boundary three-point functions ⟨ϕ Φp Φp+1⟩. The direct generalization to the ϕ3 theory fails
for the following reasons. In the conformal block expansion, only Φ contributes in the free
theory. There are infinitely many new contributions in the interacting theory, but we do not
know the new contributions associated with the ϕ3 interaction. The correlator ⟨ϕ Φp Φp+2⟩
does not help either because it vanishes in the free theory and the anomalous dimensions
do not appear in the leading correction. Perhaps one needs to examine bulk-bulk-boundary
three-point functions, but their conformal block expansions are more complicated.
We did not fix the anomalous dimension of the boundary fundamental operator in the
ϕ2n+1 theory for n > 1. Perhaps it could be determined using input from the Lagrangian.
More broadly, it would be interesting to incorporate Lagrangians in the bootstrap studies.
This might help sharpen the bootstrap predictions.
As mentioned in the introduction, the motivation for studying the single field ϕ3 CFT
was its connection to branched polymers.
The corresponding boundary CFT naturally
describes branched polymers at a surface. But the single field ϕ3 theory is also related to
the Yang-Lee theory. Here, we briefly discuss this relation in the presence of a boundary. In
the thermodynamic limit, the Yang-Lee zeros are associated with a line of singularities in
the free energy as a function of the bulk external magnetic field. Near the edge of the line,
the scaling dimension of ϕ controls the singular behavior of the free energy, i.e., the density
of Yang-Lee zeros. If another external magnetic field is imposed on the boundary, then
the free energy should exhibit certain singular behavior controlled by the scaling dimension
of Φ. In other words, the scaling dimension of Φ should characterize the behavior of the
density of zeros as we vary the boundary external magnetic field.
The coefficients in the bulk channel expansion are products of bulk OPE and one-
point function coefficients. These products are not necessarily positive, so the numerical
bootstrap that relies on positivity conditions would not work. It is interesting to explore
the bootstrap methods that apply to this scenario [77–79]. Moreover, it would also be useful
to develop novel bootstrap methods for CFTs without a boundary [80–91].
Interface CFTs can be represented by boundary CFTs using the folding trick [92]. See
e.g.
[58, 77, 93, 94] for more recent studies.
It would be interesting to generalize the

walls, which can be used to study the relation between UV and IR CFTs.
