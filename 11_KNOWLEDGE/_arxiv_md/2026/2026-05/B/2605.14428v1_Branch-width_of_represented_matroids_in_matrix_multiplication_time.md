Abstract
For an 𝑛-element matroid 𝑀given by an 𝑛× 𝑛matrix representation over a ﬁnite ﬁeld F
and an integer 𝑘, we present an (𝑂𝑘,F(𝑛2) +𝑂(𝑛𝜔))-time algorithm that either ﬁnds a branch-
decomposition of 𝑀of width at most𝑘, or conﬁrms that the branch-width of 𝑀is more than𝑘,
where 𝜔< 2.3714 is the matrix multiplication exponent, and the𝑂𝑘,F(·)-notation hides factors
that depend on 𝑘and F in a computable manner. All previous algorithms including Hliněný
and Oum [SIAM J. Comput. (2008)] and Jeong, Kim, and Oum [SIAM J. Discrete Math.
(2021)] run in at least Ω(𝑛3) time. Moreover, if the input matrix representation is given by
a standard form, our algorithm runs in 𝑂𝑘,F(𝑛2)-time, since 𝑂(𝑛𝜔)-time is only needed for
ﬁnding a standard form of the input matrix. When 𝑀is given by an𝑚×𝑛matrix, the overhead
for ﬁnding a standard form is 𝑂(𝑚𝑛min(𝑚,𝑛)𝜔−2).
As corollaries, we obtain faster algorithms for rank-width of directed graphs and path-
width of matroids represented over a ﬁxed ﬁnite ﬁeld. Furthermore, we also present an ap-
proximation algorithm for ﬁnding branch-width that works on inﬁnite ﬁelds provided that
the input matrix is of a standard form and contains a bounded number of distinct values of
entries.
To suggest that our algorithm is optimal, we observe that for every ﬁeld F, deciding
whether the branch-width of a matroid represented over F is 0 is as hard as deciding whether
a square matrix over F is singular. Under the assumption that singularity testing requires
Ω(𝑛𝜔)-time, this implies that the overhead of 𝑂(𝑛𝜔) is unavoidable. We also show strength-
enings of this observation to rule out some approximations under this assumption.

Branch-width was introduced by Robertson and Seymour [24] for graphs, hypergraphs, ma-
troids, and more generally, for connectivity functions. Here we will describe its deﬁnition for
matroids. Let 𝑀be a matroid on a ﬁnite set 𝐸with the rank function 𝑟. The connectivity function
of a matroid 𝑀is deﬁned as 𝜆𝑀(𝑋) := 𝑟(𝑋) +𝑟(𝐸\𝑋) −𝑟(𝐸). It is well known that 𝜆𝑀is symmetric,

for all 𝑋,𝑌⊆𝐸. A tree is subcubic if every node has degree 1 or 3. A branch-decomposition of a
matroid 𝑀is a pair (𝑇, L) of a subcubic tree 𝑇and a bijection L : 𝐸→{𝑡: 𝑡is a leaf of 𝑇}.
The width of a branch-decomposition is measured as follows. First, for each edge 𝑒of 𝑇, the
connected components of 𝑇\ 𝑒induce a partition (𝑋𝑒,𝑌𝑒) of 𝐸, and the width of an edge 𝑒is
deﬁned by 𝜆𝑀(𝑋𝑒). Note that 𝜆𝑀(𝑋𝑒) = 𝜆𝑀(𝑌𝑒) since 𝜆𝑀is symmetric. Next, the width of a
branch-decomposition (𝑇, L) is deﬁned by the maximum width among all edges of 𝑇. Lastly, the
branch-width of 𝑀, denoted by bw(𝑀), is the minimum width of all its branch-decompositions. If
|𝐸| ≤1, then 𝑀admits no branch-decomposition and we deﬁne bw(𝑀) = 0. It is known that it is
NP-hard to compute branch-width of a matroid represented over a ﬁxed ﬁeld [8, Corollary 2].
Branch-width of graphs was introduced as a tool to study tree-width of graphs. Many al-
gorithmic problems on graphs, when restricted to graphs of bounded tree-width, admit eﬃcient
algorithms. Here are two ingredients for such a framework for graphs of bounded tree-width.
(a) Eﬃcient algorithms to ﬁnd a tree-decomposition of small width when the input graph has
tree-width 𝑘.
(b) Dynamic programming algorithms to solve given problems based on tree-decompositions
of graphs.
For (a), Bodlaender and Kloks [2] presented a linear-time algorithm to ﬁnd a tree-decomposition
of width at most 𝑘for graphs of tree-width at most 𝑘. For (b), there are many interesting algo-
rithms known for graphs of bounded tree-width based on a tree decomposition. One important
theorem here is the classic theorem of Courcelle [4], stating that any graph property that can be
described by a monadic second-order formula can be decided in linear time for graphs of bounded
tree-width if the input graph is given with its tree decomposition of width at most 𝑘.
To extend this framework to matroids, the deﬁnition of branch-width is more convenient,
because it is diﬃcult to imagine an analog of vertices in matroids. We remark that Hliněný and
Whittle [12] have successfully deﬁned the tree-width of matroids but their deﬁnition is more
complicated than the deﬁnition of branch-width. We now have analogs for both (a) and (b).

element matroids of branch-width at most 𝑘.

1.3
Main idea
Our key idea is to convert our problem into a problem on the rank-width of graphs. Rank-width
of a graph was introduced by Oum and Seymour [22]. Given a standard representation 𝐴of a
matroid 𝑀, we construct a graph 𝐺such that the rank-width of 𝐺is bounded if and only if the
branch-width of 𝑀is bounded. Our bound depends on the number of distinct values of entries
in a standard representation. Then, we use the following algorithm of Fomin and Korhonen [7],
which provides a quadratic-time algorithm for approximating rank-width.
Theorem 1.2 (Fomin and Korhonen [7]). Let 𝑛≥2. For an integer 𝑘, in time 𝑂𝑘(𝑛2), we can either
ﬁnd a rank-decomposition of the input 𝑛-vertex graph 𝐺of width at most 𝑘, or correctly conclude that the
rank-width of 𝐺is more than 𝑘.
We could also use the almost-linear-time algorithm of Korhonen and Sokołowski [20] for
computing rank-width, but it would not change the running time we end up with.
Interestingly, previous algorithms due to Hliněný and Oum [11] and Jeong, Kim, and Oum [16]
for ﬁnding a rank-decomposition of small width convert the graph rank-width problem into a
problem on matroid branch-width. However, we convert the matroid branch-width problem
back to rank-width problems with explicit bounds. A similar transformation appears in the book
of Courcelle and Engelfriet [5, page 438] without explicit bounds.

1.6
Hardness
Lastly, we give evidence that the factor of 𝑛𝜔in Corollary 5.8 is unavoidable when the matroid
is given by a general representation. In particular, we make the simple observation that deciding
whether the branch-width of a matroid represented as an 𝑛×𝑛matrix over a ﬁeld F is equivalent
to deciding whether an 𝑛× 𝑛matrix over F is singular.

Lemma 7.1. Let 𝐴be a matrix over a ﬁeld F without a zero column. Then bw(𝑀(𝐴)) = 0 if and only

rithms. We show that for a ﬁxed ﬁeld F with at least 4 elements, computing the branch-width of

whether a square matrix over F is singular. Note that in the following statement, each of 𝑘, 𝛼,
and F are (arbitrarily) ﬁxed constants.

1.7
Overview
This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 will introduce several deﬁnitions and lemmas we will
use throughout the paper. In Section 3, we will ﬁnd a symmetric matrix whose rank-width is
equal to the branch-width of a given represented matroid. Section 4 will discuss the key method
for the paper, which constructs a graph whose rank-width is functionally equivalent to the rank-
width of a 𝜎-symmetric matrix. In Section 5, we will use the algorithm of Jeong, Kim, and
Oum [16] to convert our approximation algorithm to an exact ﬁxed-parameter tractable algo-
rithm. In Section 6, we will apply our result to present an algorithm ﬁnding a path-decomposition
of a represented matroid if one exists. Finally, in Section 7, we will prove the hardness result on
approximating the branch-width of matroids.

Preliminaries
All graphs and matroids are ﬁnite in this paper. For a non-negative integer 𝑛, let [𝑛] denote the
set of positive integers less than or equal to 𝑛.
An 𝑋×𝑌matrix, for sets 𝑋and 𝑌, means a matrix whose rows are indexed by 𝑋and columns
