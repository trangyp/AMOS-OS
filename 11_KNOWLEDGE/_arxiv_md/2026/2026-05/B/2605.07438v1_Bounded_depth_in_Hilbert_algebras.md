BOUNDED DEPTH IN HILBERT ALGEBRAS
LUCA CARAI, MIRIAM KURTZHALS, AND TOMMASO MORASCHINI
Abstract. Hilbert algebras are the implicative subreducts of Heyting algebras. It is shown
that having depth ⩽n is an equational condition in Hilbert algebras. This generalizes an
analogous well-known result in the setting of Heyting algebras.
1. Hilbert algebras and implicative filters
An algebra ⟨A; →⟩is said to be a Hilbert algebra when it is an implicative subreduct of
a Heyting algebra (see, e.g., [5, 6, 7]). In every Hilbert algebra A we have a →a = b →b
for all a, b ∈A. Therefore, we can define a constant 1 = a →a for a ∈A. In addition, the
binary relation ⩽on A defined by the rule
a ⩽b ⇐⇒a →b = 1
is a partial order on A with maximum 1.
Let A be a Hilbert algebra. A set F ⊆A is an implicative filter of A when 1 ∈F and for
all a, b ∈A,
if (a ∈F and a →b ∈F), then b ∈F.
When ordered under inclusion, the set of implicative filters of A forms a distributive lattice
that we denote by Fi(A) (see, e.g., [3, Prop. 2.9]). Every implicative filter of A is an upset
(see, e.g., [3, Lem. 2.8(1)]). Moreover, for every a ∈A the principal upset
↑a = {b ∈A : a ⩽b}
is an implicative filter of A (see, e.g., [3, p. 192]). We denote by FgA(−) the closure operator
of implicative filter generation on A (see [2, Lem. 2.3]).
Proposition 1.1. Let A be a Hilbert algebra and X ⊆A. Then
FgA(X) = {a ∈A : a = 1 or b1 →(b2 →(. . . (bn →a) . . . )) = 1 for some b1, . . . , bn ∈X}.
From Proposition 1.1 and the fact that Hilbert algebras are subreducts of Heyting algebras
we deduce the following.
Corollary 1.2. Let A be a Hilbert algebra and X ∪{c} ⊆A. Then
FgA(X ∪{c}) = {a ∈A : a = 1 or b1 →(b2 →(. . . (bn →(c →a)) . . . )) = 1
for some b1, . . . , bn ∈X}.

Let A be a Hilbert algebra. With every F ∈Fi(A) we associate a congruence of A as
follows:
θF = {⟨a, b⟩∈A × A : a →b, b →a ∈F}.
The quotient algebra A/θF is a Hilbert algebra that we denote by A/F. Similarly, we denote
the equivalence class of an element a of A under θF by a/F. The map F 7−→θF is a lattice
isomorphism from Fi(A) to the congruence lattice of A. Because of this, the following is a
consequence of the Correspondence Theorem (see, e.g., [1, Thm. II.6.20]).
Theorem 1.3. Let A be a Hilbert algebra and F ∈Fi(A). Then there exists an isomorphism
h: ↑F →Fi(A/F), where ↑F is the subposet of Fi(A) with universe {G ∈Fi(A) : F ⊆G}.
We recall that an element of lattice A is said to be meet irreducible when it is neither the
maximum of A nor it can be written as the meet of two larger elements. Given a Hilbert
algebra A, we denote the poset of meet irreducible elements of Fi(A) by A∗. Owing to the
distributivity of Fi(A), the poset A∗coincides with that of meet prime elements of Fi(A) (see,
e.g., [3, p. 192]). We will make use of the following observation (see, e.g., [3, Prop. 2.10(1)]).
Proposition 1.4. Let A be a Hilbert algebra, a ∈A, and F ∈Fi(A). If a /∈F, there exists
G ∈A∗such that F ⊆G and a /∈G.
Since principal upsets of Hilbert algebras are always implicative filters, the following is an
immediate consequence of Proposition 1.4.
Corollary 1.5. Let A be a Hilbert algebra, a ∈A, and a, b ∈A. If a ⩽̸ b, there exists
F ∈A∗such that a ∈F and b /∈F.
Remark 1.6. An algebra ⟨A; ∧, →⟩is said to be an implicative semilattice when it is a ⟨∧, →⟩-
subreduct of a Heyting algebra (see, e.g., [8]). Let A be an implicative semilattice (resp. a
Heyting algebra) and A→its Hilbert algebra reduct. Then Fi(A→) coincides with the lattice
of filters of A and, consequently, (A→)∗coincides with the poset of meet irreducible filters of
A. We recall that the meet irreducible filters of a Heyting algebra are precisely its prime
filters. Therefore, in the case where A is a Heyting algebra, (A→)∗is the poset of prime
filters of A.
⊠
2. The main result
We say that a Hilbert algebra A has depth ⩽n for n ∈N when A∗does not contain any
chain of length n + 1. We will show that this condition can be described equationally.1 To
this end, for every n ∈N we define recursively a formula dn(x0, . . . , xn) as follows:
d0(x0) = x0;
dn+1(x0, . . . , xn+1) = ((xn+1 →dn(x0, . . . , xn)) →xn+1) →xn+1.
Theorem 2.1. Let A be a Hilbert algebra and n ∈N. Then A has depth ⩽n iff A ⊨dn ≈1.

1 ∈F and a0 /∈F. Consequently, A∗is nonempty. It follows that A is not of depth ⩽0, as

We will show that ↑b ∩↑(b →an−1) ⊆G. Consider c ∈↑b ∩↑(b →an−1). Then b ⩽c and

F0 ∈A∗, we know that F0 is meet prime. Consequently, either ↑b ⊆F0 or ↑(b →an−1) ⊆F0,

That b →an−1 ∈F0 is impossible as well. Indeed, b →an−1 ∈F0 ⊆F1 and b ∈F1 imply

The claim above implies that there exists an ∈(F1 ∩G) −F0. As an /∈F0, we have
an < 1. From an ∈G it follows that an−1 ⩽an and an →an−1 = an−1. Then an−1 < an

By what we have observed above, an →an−1 = an−1, so we can assume i < n −1. Since

To finish the proof, we need to show that if A is not of depth ⩽n, then A ⊭dn ≈1.
Assume that A∗contains a chain of size n + 1. By what we have shown above there is a
subalgebra A of the form a0 < · · · < an < 1. Moreover, this subalgebra does not validate
dn ≈1 because di(a0, . . . , ai) = ai for every i ⩽n. Hence, we conclude that A ⊭dn ≈1.

contain any chain of length n + 1.
Corollary 2.4. Let A be an implicative semilattice or a Heyting algebra and n ∈N. Then
A has depth ⩽n iff A ⊨dn ≈1.
