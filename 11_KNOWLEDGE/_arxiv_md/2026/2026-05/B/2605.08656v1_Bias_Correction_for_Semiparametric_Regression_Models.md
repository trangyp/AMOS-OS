Bias Correction for Semiparametric Regression Models
Yuming Zhang1, Yanyuan Ma2, Xuming He3, St´ephane Guerrier4,5
1Department of Biostatistics, Harvard University, USA

Abstract
We consider a broad class of semiparametric regression models in which the condi-
tional distribution of the response takes the form ftY |xTβ ` mpzq, ϕu, which is known
up to a parametric component β of diverging dimension p, a smooth function mp¨q,
and a dispersion parameter ϕ. Existing semiparametric literature on such models has
primarily focused on semiparametric efficiency for β, typically treating ϕ and mp¨q as
nuisances and largely ignoring their finite-sample bias. However, the finite-sample bias
of standard estimators can be substantial (especially when p is large relatively to n
and/or dispersion is high) and can seriously undermine inference for β. Moreover, ϕ
is often of direct scientific interest and requires accurate estimation. To address this
gap, we propose SABRE, a simulation-based bias correction framework for this broad
semiparametric model class. We establish asymptotic properties of SABRE for the
subclass of generalized partially linear models, where bias reduction for β and ϕ can
be achieved without inflating variance, and we outline how the underlying principle
may be adapted more generally. Comprehensive simulation studies and a real-data
application on early-stage diabetes demonstrate the empirical effectiveness of SABRE
in reducing bias and improving inference.
Keywords Bias reduction, B-splines, dispersion parameter, generalized partially
linear models, misclassified data.

Introduction
1.1
Background
Statistical analyses in modern applications often require flexible regression models that can
capture complex relationships between responses and covariates, while retaining a finite-
dimensional parameter that yields a clear interpretation of the covariate-response associ-
ation.
Motivated by this, we consider a broad class of semiparametric models in which
the conditional distribution of Y |x, z takes the form ftY |xTβ ` mpzq, ϕu, with β, mp¨q,
and ϕ all unknown, yielding mean EpY |x, zq “ gtxTβ ` mpzqu and variance varpY |x, zq “
vtxTβ ` mpzq, ϕu for known functions gp¨q and vp¨, ¨q. The covariates px, zq are determin-
istic, with x P IRp and z P r0, 1s. The parametric component β has dimension p, which is
allowed to grow with the sample size n. The nonparametric component mp¨q : r0, 1s Ñ M is
a smooth function taking values in a bounded set M Ă IR; for identifiability, the intercept
is absorbed into mp¨q. Finally, ϕ P Φ Ă p0, 8q is a dispersion parameter. This model class
includes the generalized partially linear models (GPLMs; see e.g., Boente et al. 2006).
Our model class encompasses many important modern settings. A first example arises in
studies based on electronic health records (EHRs), such as EHR-based phenotyping and
surrogate-outcome analyses. In these settings, the covariates may include large collections of
diagnosis codes, procedures, medications, so the ratio p{n is typically not small. It is natural
to model a latent binary disease status Y ˝ via a binary regression with linear predictor
xTβ ` mpzq, such as a logistic regression. In practice, researchers often use an EHR-derived
phenotype (e.g., Lu et al. 2024) or a thresholded biomarker (e.g., Cai and Moskowitz 2004)
as an observed surrogate Y , which is a misclassified or noisy version of Y ˝. The surrogate
mechanism is typically treated as known, either based on domain knowledge or estimated
from external data and then regarded as fixed in the analysis. The observed outcome Y then
follows a distribution of the form ftY |xTβ ` mpzq, ϕu, where f is determined jointly by the

original model for Y ˝ and the known surrogate mechanism.
A second example is taken from the recent literature on label-only differential privacy (label-
DP). In applications such as online advertising, medical outcomes, and surveys, the covariates
may encode numerous user-, item-, or context-specific features, making it more realistic to
assume diverging parameter dimension. Moreover, the response is often private. A common
practice is to obtain an observed label Y by adding a known but stochastic label mechanism
(e.g., Warner 1965; Ghazi et al. 2021) to the latent true response Y ˝, which follows an
exponential-family-type regression model with linear predictor xTβ ` mpzq. The observed
label Y again falls within our model class, with the distribution determined jointly by the
original model for Y ˝ and the known stochastic label mechanism.
Beyond EHRs and label-LP applications, our model class also arises in a wide range of
important settings, including epidemiologic studies with misclassified disease status or ex-
posures (e.g., Chen et al. 2013), genetic analyses with noisy case-control or genotype labels
(e.g., Biffani et al. 2017), and public health surveys where data are subject to self-report
bias (e.g., Preston et al. 2015).
Across these examples, β is the primary target for estimation and inference. In EHR appli-
cations, β quantifies the association between risk factors and latent disease status, while in
label-DP settings it captures the underlying relationship between covariates and the unob-
served true outcome prior to privacy randomization. The dispersion ϕ is also often of direct
scientific interest and plays a key role in studies across biomedical research (e.g., Breslow
1984), genomics (e.g., Anders and Huber 2010), insurance and actuarial science (e.g., Smyth
and Jorgensen 2002), as well as in sample size calculations based on preliminary analyses
such as pilot studies.
The semiparametric literature on related models has focused primarily on developing estima-
tors for β that achieve semiparametric efficiency, while treating ϕ and mp¨q as nuisances (see

e.g., Severini and Staniswalis 1994; Murphy and Van der Vaart 2000; Ruppert et al. 2003;
H¨ardle et al. 2004; Jankova and Van De Geer 2018). The bias of these estimators is typically
overlooked because it is asymptotically negligible compared to their standard errors. How-
ever, in finite samples this bias can be substantial and seriously undermine the accuracy of
downstream inference. This phenomenon is well documented in parametric models, where
maximum likelihood estimators (MLEs), though asymptotically unbiased and efficient, may
exhibit pronounced finite-sample bias that distorts confidence intervals (CIs) and hypothe-
sis tests (see e.g., Wang and Basu 1999; Mittelhammer et al. 2005; Sur and Cand`es 2019).
Related work has also shown that biased estimation of ϕ can lead to unreliable inference on
β, even when β itself is estimated with no bias (see e.g., Kosmidis 2014; Heller et al. 2019).
Despite these practical concerns, there is a striking lack of bias correction methodology for
semiparametric models of the form ftY |xTβ ` mpzq, ϕu. Existing work either studies bias
correction in low-dimensional parametric models, or emphasizes semiparametric efficiency
without addressing bias. Our objective is therefore to obtain bias-reduced estimation of β
and ϕ in this broad model class, while retaining the same variance for β and allowing p to
diverge with n.
In this work, we propose a unified bias correction framework called SemipArametric Bias-
Reduced Estimation (SABRE) for semiparametric models of the form ftY |xTβ`mpzq, ϕu. It
simultaneously (i) yields bias-reduced estimators for β and ϕ, (ii) retains the variablity for β,
and (iii) remains valid when p2logpnqn´1 Ñ 0. SABRE effectively mitigates finite-sample bias
to deliver more accurate and reliable inference in a wide range of modern applications where
flexible modeling of covariate effects, surrogate or privatized responses, and finite-sample
performance are all of central importance.

1.2
Related Works
A substantial semiparametric literature studies models closely related to ftY |xTβ`mpzq, ϕu.
Earlier work on partially linear models considers the special case with Gaussian errors and
identity link function (see e.g., Heckman 1986; Chen 1988; Speckman 1988), with focus
on estimation of β. Generalized partially linear and single-index extensions (see e.g., Sev-
erini and Staniswalis 1994; Carroll et al. 1997) place our model into a quasi-likelihood or
exponential-family setting, deriving profile or quasi-likelihood estimators for β. Robust esti-
mation for GPLMs has also been proposed, for example Boente et al. (2006), who constructs
M-type and quasi-likelihood-type estimators for β and mp¨q that resist outliers. All of these
works focus on ?n-consistency, asymptotic normality, and semiparametric efficiency for low-
dimensional β. However, they do not address bias correction, and typically treat ϕ as a
nuisance parameter.
While bias correction methods for semiparametric models remain scarce, a rich literature
exists for parametric models. One line adopts a preventive approach, analytically modifying
score equations to remove leading-order bias (e.g., Firth 1993; Kosmidis and Firth 2009).
These adjustments are typically model-specific and must be derived separately for each likeli-
hood, making them difficult to generalize across a broad class of models. A second line uses a
corrective approach, starting from an initial estimator and adjusting it via an estimated bias
function, often through simulation-based schemes such as the bootstrap (Efron and Tibshi-
rani, 1994), classical minimum distance (Newey and McFadden, 1994), or indirect inference
(Gouri´eroux et al., 1993), whose bias properties have been studied in various parametric
settings (e.g., MacKinnon and Smith 1998; Gouri´eroux et al. 2000; Guerrier et al. 2019).
Importantly, these methods are developed for low-dimensional parametric models; they do
not cover semiparametric models with a functional component mp¨q, a dispersion ϕ, or a di-
verging parameter dimension. Moreover, all these corrective methods require approximating
expectations of the initial estimator by Monte Carlo averages under the exact parametric

model, which makes them fundamentally parametric tools and renders their extension (with
theoretical guarantees) to semiparametric models with diverging parameter dimension highly
challenging.
Another strand of related work concerns high-dimensional inference. For penalized estima-
tors such as the Lasso (Tibshirani, 1996), a large literature has developed debiased estima-
tors to correct shrinkage bias and enable valid inference for low-dimensional components of
β when p can exceed n (e.g., Zhang and Zhang 2014; Van de Geer et al. 2014; Ning and Liu
2017). These methods, however, are typically formulated for parametric generalized linear
models (GLMs). In general, they do not include a functional component mp¨q, do not tar-
get bias in the dispersion parameter ϕ, and do not cover more complex settings where the
observed response is a noisy manifestation of a latent truth, as considered in our framework.
1.3
Contributions
In this paper, we propose SABRE as a corrective bias correction framework for semipara-
metric models of the form ftY |xTβ ` mpzq, ϕu, known up to β, mp¨q, and ϕ. We begin from
a B-spline estimator (obtained under a parametric approximating model that approximates
mp¨q with a B-spline expansion) as an initial estimator, and then construct a bias-corrected
SABRE estimator as the solution to an estimating equation that equates this initial esti-
mator to its simulation-based expectation under the same parametric approximating model.
We summarize our main contributions below.
To the best of our knowledge, SABRE is the first bias correction framework for the broad
semiparametric model class we consider.
Its key device is a parametric approximating
model that is “close” to the target semiparametric model, allowing expectations of an ini-
tial estimator to be approximated by simulation despite the unknown mp¨q. This enables
simulation-based bias correction but also substantially complicates the theory because the
6


## Page 7


approximating-vs-true model discrepancy must be carefully controlled. Since our model class
is very broad and the technical details highly depend on the specific distribution of Y |x, z,
we develop the asymptotic theory within the GPLM subclass, which already covers many
common models (e.g., logistic and Poisson regressions).
We also provide a roadmap for
extending the theory beyond GPLMs. The SABRE methodology itself applies to the full
model class, and our numerical experiments show promising performance beyond GPLMs; a
complete formal theory for the full class is yet to be developed in future work.
Our second contribution is to provide bias correction for both β and ϕ while allowing p to
diverge at a rate of p2logpnqn´1 Ñ 0, without relying on sparsity assumptions or penalized
methods. Existing bias reduction methods typically assume fixed p (e.g., Firth 1993; Guerrier
et al. 2019), or treat ϕ only in very simple parametric models (e.g., Saha and Paul 2005 for
two-parameter negative binomial models). In contrast, SABRE achieves bias correction for
both β and ϕ in a broad semiparametric model class, while allowing diverging p.
Third, we establish asymptotic normality for the SABRE estimator of β, showing that bias
correction is achieved without sacrificing efficiency. This result directly supports subsequent
inference such as to construct CIs for β.
Although our primary targets are β and ϕ, SABRE also yields a bias-reduced estimator of
mp¨q as a byproduct, with an established asymptotic distribution that supports improved
inference for mp¨q.
We conduct simulation studies and a real data application to demonstrate substantial finite-sample
gains of SABRE. Across all empirical experiments, SABRE markedly reduces bias in β, ϕ,
and mp¨q relative to alternative methods, with the improvements most pronounced when p{n
is large and/or dispersion is high. These bias reductions translate into CIs for β with more
accurate empirical coverage and typically shorter length.

Diabetes is a rapidly growing chronic disease, and many cases remain undiagnosed because
of a prolonged asymptomatic phase, making outcome misclassification plausible in clinical
practice. In this section, we analyze an early-stage diabetes dataset from Islam et al. (2020).

delayed healing. Some diabetic individuals may be diagnosed as non-diabetic, whereas false
positives are unlikely once a diagnosis follows confirmatory evaluation. Therefore, we model
the binary diabetes indicator using a partially linear logistic regression with misclassified

Conclusions
Bias plays a central role in finite-sample inference, yet the semiparametric literature on mod-
els of the form ftY |xTβ ` mpzq, ϕu has largely focused on semiparametric efficiency for β
in specific model subclasses, treating ϕ and mp¨q as nuisances and overlooking finite-sample
bias. To address this gap, we have proposed SABRE, a simulation-based bias correction
framework for this broad semiparametric model class that also allows a diverging parameter
dimension. SABRE leverages a parametric approximating model built from a B-spline ap-
proximation of mp¨q: starting from the B-spline sMLE, SABRE is defined by matching this
initial estimator to its simulation-based expectation under the same approximating model.
Within the GPLM subclass, we developed a comprehensive asymptotic theory, including
convergence rates and smaller bias orders for β and ϕ, and asymptotic normality for β with
semiparametric efficiency. Our analysis also yielded asymptotic properties for the induced
estimator of mp¨q. We further provide a roadmap indicating how the theory could, in prin-
ciple, be extended to the broader model class, but do not pursue the full generality here, as
the derivations are highly distribution-specific.
Simulation studies demonstrated substantial finite-sample gains of SABRE over existing ap-
proaches. Across partially linear logistic, inverse Gaussian, and negative binomial regression
models, SABRE markedly reduced bias in β and ϕ, with the most pronounced gains when
p{n is large, misclassification is severe, or dispersion is high. These bias reductions yielded
CIs for β with more accurate empirical coverage and shorter length. We also applied SABRE
to an early-stage diabetes dataset with an accompanying emulation study, where it delivered
