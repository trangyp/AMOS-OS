ANNA JOV´E AND  LUKASZ PAWELEC
Abstract. We consider transcendental entire functions having doubly parabolic Baker do-
mains, such that the Denjoy-Wolff point of the associated inner function is not a singularity.
We describe in a very precise way the dynamics on the boundary from a measure-theoretical
point of view. Applications of such results lead to a better understanding of the topology and
the dynamics on the boundaries. In particular, we improve some of the results in [FJ23] for
the Baker domain of z + e−z. In fact, our conclusions are obtained by applying new results
established here on the dynamics of the radial extension of one component doubly parabolic
inner functions, which strengthen those of [IU23].
1. Introduction
Let f : C →C be a transcendental entire function and denote by {fn}n∈N its iterates, which
generate a discrete dynamical system in C. Then, the complex plane is divided into two totally
invariant sets: the Fatou set F(f), defined to be the set of points z ∈C such that {fn}n∈N
forms a normal family in some neighbourhood of z; and the Julia set J (f), its complement.
The connected components of the Fatou set are called Fatou components.
For entire functions, invariant Fatou components are always simply connected [Bak84], so
the Riemann map can be used as a uniformization. More precisely, let U be a invariant Fatou
component of f and let φ be a Riemann map from the open unit disk D onto U. Then,
g: D −→D,
g := φ−1 ◦f ◦φ
is an analytic self-map of D. The function g is called the associated inner function. Since f|U
and g|D are conformally conjugate by φ, the study of holomorphic self-maps of D is a good
approach to analyse the dynamics of f|U. Moreover, we can extend the conjugacy by using
radial limits for both g and φ, i.e.

Let us focus on doubly parabolic Baker domains, i.e. invariant Fatou components in which
iterates converge locally uniformly to ∞, and for which the hyperbolic distance between iterates
converges to zero. Baker domains are exclusive to transcendental functions, and present richer
dynamics (both in the interior and on the boundary), affected by the presence of the essential
singularity.
Moreover, if U is a doubly parabolic Baker domain, there are infinitely many accesses from
U [BD99] (see also [BFJK17, JF25]), which form a natural partition of the boundary ∂U, in
the following way.
Definition. (Target) Let f : C →C be a transcendental entire function, let U be a Baker
domain, and let φ: D →U be a Riemann map. Let I ⊂∂D be a closed arc, delimited by
ξ1 ̸= ξ2 ∈∂D such that φ∗(ξ1) = φ∗(ξ2) = ∞. We say that φ∗(I) ⊂∂U is a target on ∂U.

A first step on this direction are the results of Doering and Ma˜n´e [DM91] (and also [BFJK19,
Thms. B and C]), which give a description of f|∂U from a measure-theoretical point of view.
More precisely, if U is a doubly parabolic Baker domain, f|∂U is always ergodic with respect to
any harmonic measure ωU. In the case when f|∂U has finite degree or the Denjoy-Wolff point
of the associated inner function is not a singularity, then f|∂U is also conservative, and thus
ωU-almost every orbit is dense on ∂U.
In contrast with attracting basins, no harmonic measure supported on ∂U is f-invariant, so
one cannot do a straightforward analysis applying classical results from ergodic theory, such as
Birkhoff Ergodic Theorem or Kac’s Lemma (compare with the results given in Appendix A).
Nevertheless, there exists an infinite σ-finite measure µ which is f-invariant and absolutely
continous with respect to ωU [DM91]. This measure µ allows us to prove more precise statements
on the ergodic properties of f|∂U and the distribution of orbits with respect to targets, although
the complexity of the arguments is much greater than in the case where an invariant probability
measure exists.
Statement of results. For a Baker domain, the convergence point of the interior orbits is
placed on the boundary. In terms of the associated inner function, this means that the Denjoy-
Wolff point (the point of convergence of the interior orbits) is on the unit circle (and can be
assumed to be 1, by precomposing the Riemann map φ: D →U with an appropriate rotation).

measure λ on ∂D under φ∗.

Doubly parabolic inner functions. The main theorems stated above are in fact obtained by
first proving more general results for the radial extension of the associated inner functions, and
then transfering them to the Baker domains, exploiting the compatibility between the targets
and the Riemann map.
Inner functions associated to doubly parabolic Baker domains are themselves doubly parabolic
(Def. 3.4). For these inner functions we develop a very precise measure-theoretic description of
their boundary dynamics, including the following.
• We give an asymptotic description of occupation times, first return times and waiting
times (Thm. 4.1), in the same spirit as in Theorem A.
• We consider a specific class of inner functions, namely one component doubly parabolic
inner functions (Def. 3.2). We prove that the boundary of such inner functions fits
into the framework of AFN-systems (Lemma 4.2), which form a class of interval maps

theory. Thus, we will be able to apply results on AFN-systems concerning the asymptotic

Applications. Even though the previous results are interesting on their own, we apply them
to a specific class of Baker domains to deduce some additional properties, which are interesting
from a topological and dynamical point of view, and not necessarily related with ergodic theory.
Let us start by defining this specific class of Baker domains, which we call expanding.
Definition. (Expanding Baker domains) Let f be a transcendental entire function, and U
be a Baker domain. We say that U is an expanding Baker domain if there exists r > 0 such
that the following properties are satisfied.
(a) U is doubly parabolic Baker domain, and the Denjoy-Wolff point of the associated inner
function is not a singularity.
(b) For every x, y ∈∂U such that f(y) = x there exists a branch Fy of f−1 which is
well-defined in D(x, r), and Fy(D(x, r) ∩U) ⊂U.
(c) |f′(z)| > 1, z ∈D(x, r), for all x ∈∂U; and for every finite target E, |f′(z)| ≥KE > 1,
z ∈D(x, r), for all x ∈E.

any finite degree satisfying the previous properties, inspired by [FH06]. In particular, the Baker
domains of degree two of the function f(z) = z+e−z, previously studied in [BD99, FH06, FJ23],
are expanding.

Finally, we apply the obtained results to describe the boundary of the Baker domain of the
function f(z) = z + e−z. The boundary of such Baker domain has been significantly studied in
[FJ23], giving a detailed description of both the topology and the dynamics. More precisely, the
boundary of such Baker domain is made out of hairs (curves of escaping points), some of them
land at a point and others accumulate in indecomposable continua, and that the dynamics of
the hairs is encoded by some symbolic dynamics. With the previously developed techniques
we are able to prove that almost every of such hairs land, and we prove some estimates on the
measure of codes satisfying some specific dynamical properties (see Theorem E for a precise
statement).
Remark. We restricted ourselves to entire functions, since we need Fatou components to have
infinitely many accesses to infinity (and preimages of infinity being dense in the unit circle),
so that the concept of target makes sense and partititions the boundary in an interesting way.
For meromorphic functions, this is no longer the case: there exist doubly parabolic Baker
domains with a unique access to infinity, and their boundary may even be a Jordan curve
[BFJK17, BFJK25].
Structure of the paper. We collect some needed background on Infinite Ergodic Theory
and AFN-systems in Section 2, which will serve as a framework for the ergodic study of our
maps. In Section 3, we study doubly parabolic inner functions (the inner functions that can
be associated to the Baker domains considered) and their mapping properties. In Section 4 we
prove a finer measure-theoretic description of the boundary dynamics of doubly parabolic inner
functions (including estimates on the asymptotic distribution of orbits and periodic points).
Finally, in Section 5 we transfer all the results for doubly parabolic inner functions to the
boundaries of Baker domains, using the targets and their compatibility with the Riemann map.
The proof of the main theorems can be found in this section, even though they follow as direct
applications of the work in the previous sections.
As mentioned earlier, the previous results have analogues in the case of basins of attraction
(and, therefore, for centered inner functions), which can be obtained quite easily from classical
results in ergodic theory (such as the Birkhoff Ergodic Theorem). We collect these results in
Appendix A, and invite the reader to consult them in order to appreciate the difficulties that
arise intrinsically when an invariant probability measure is not available.

2. Background on Infinite Ergodic Theory
Let (X, A, µ) be a measure space, and T : X →X be a measure-preserving transformation.
We are interested in the case when µ is an infinite σ-finite measure.
The main difficulty when dealing with measure-preserving transformations for which the
preserved measure is not finite, but just σ-finite, is that the standard results in ergodic theory

here only the results that we need.
