While standard minimal coupling works well for Dirac fermions, its application
to scalar fields features a known “peculiarity”: the term linear in Aµ does not co-
incide with the conserved Noether current of the interacting theory. We recently
proposed choosing a different principle for electromagnetic interactions, namely a
linear coupling AµJµ with Jµ a (globally) conserved current, accepting the conse-
quence that one must abandon full local gauge invariance in the electromagnetic
sector and adopt an extended electrodynamics (of Aharonov–Bohm type) that can
couple consistently to non-locally-conserved currents. We first review the standard
theory (scalar QED and the role of the A2|ϕ|2 term), then present the physical moti-
vations offered for proposing the modified coupling and discuss general consequences
of reducing gauge invariance. We then focus on the central condensed-matter claim:
for bosonic charged condensates, the modified framework predicts a rescaled mag-
netic penetration depth λ →λ/
√
2, while leaving other key qualitative features of
superconducting electrodynamics and the type-I/type-II distinction unchanged (up
to an equivalent rescaling of the GL parameter). Finally, we analyze experimental
data for a London-length consistency check based on independent measurements of
the ratio ns/m∗between carrier density and effective mass. We compare for five ma-
terials an “optical” penetration depth λopt inferred from IR/THz superfluid spectral
weight with a “magnetic” depth λmag obtained independently (LE-µSR, TF-µSR,
microwave methods, etc.). Data for Nb, YBCO and Ba(Fe,Co)2As2 confirm the hy-
pothesis λopt > λmag, with a ratio not far from 1.4; data for Pb are inconclusive
while data for MgB2 indicate λopt ≃λmag as predicted by the standard theory.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Local gauge invariance (LGI) and minimal coupling are cornerstone principles of modern
electrodynamics and of the Standard Model. In the familiar fermionic case (Dirac QED),
replacing ∂µ →∂µ + i(q/ℏ)Aµ produces an interaction term of the form −JµAµ with Jµ =
q ¯ψγµψ, which coincides with the conserved Noether current associated with global phase
symmetry. This harmony among (i) LGI, (ii) strict local charge conservation, and (iii) the
physical meaning of JµAµ as interaction energy is aesthetically and technically compelling.

A.
Why insist on a linear AµJµ coupling with conserved Jµ?
The proposal in [1] can be read as an attempt to elevate to a principle what is commonly
true in fermionic QED and in classical electrodynamics: the interaction energy density is
JµAµ, and gauge invariance of this interaction term in a transformation Aµ →Aµ + ∂µf is
closely tied to current conservation (since, by integration by parts, Jµ∂µf →−f ∂µJµ).
The fact that the interaction energy is not gauge invariant in standard scalar QED appears
as a weak point of the theory in a semiclassical limit, where a proper expression for this
energy is expected to be gauge-invariant and independent from whether, given a current, it
is made of fermions or bosons.
In ordinary Maxwell electrodynamics with strict local charge conservation, gauge trans-
formations are allowed and the potentials are not unique. However, Aharonov and Bohm
famously argued that potentials can have physical significance even in regions where Fµν = 0,
as evidenced by the (gauge invariant) AB phase. In extended electrodynamics frameworks
that reduce gauge freedom, Aµ can be treated more directly as a physical field (modulo
residual gauge transformations), and then JµAµ becomes a natural candidate for a physi-
cally meaningful interaction energy density. Ref. [1] emphasizes that if one wants to treat
JµAµ as a well-defined physical quantity, one is led to link it to a conserved Jµ.
Maxwell theory with gauge invariance supports two transverse propagating photon po-

The extended Aharonov-Bohm electrodynamics with reduced gauge invariance can also
be applied to effective theories with apparent local nonconservation. In many-body quan-
tum mechanics and effective condensed-matter models, one sometimes encounters nonlocal
potentials, coarse-graining, or open-system descriptions where the usual local continuity
equation for an effective current can appear violated (even if the underlying microscopic
theory is conservative). Ref. [1] connects this to the possibility that strict local charge con-
servation could fail in certain effective quantum models, and that an electromagnetic theory
able to couple to such currents would be required (or at least meaningful as an effective de-
scription). Related discussions appear in our broader program on Aharonov–Bohm extended
electrodynamics and “gauge waves” [2–4].
A recent complementary perspective is provided by Lenzi et al., who study a gener-
alized (fractional) Schr¨odinger dynamics combining (i) a temporal memory kernel, (ii) a
fractional (Riesz) spatial operator, and (iii) an explicitly nonlocal interaction term written
as an integral operator acting on the wavefunction [5]. In this framework, the quantity
ρ(x, t) = |Ψ(x, t)|2 obeys a generalized continuity equation rather than the standard local
form ∂tρ + ∇·J = 0. Lenzi et al. explicitly show that (a) the memory kernel introduces
additional terms that act as sources/sinks for the local density, and (b) the spatially nonlocal
term generates a redistribution that further invalidates a strictly local continuity equation
[5].
Lenzi et al. connect this effective nonconservation to a standard physical mechanism:
openness of the reduced system. They emphasize that when the dynamics is non-Markovian
(with memory), probability can be temporarily stored in “memory channels” and later flow
back from the environment to the system, leading to an effective nonunitary evolution at the
level of the reduced description [5]. From the viewpoint adopted in Ref. [1], such examples are
relevant because they illustrate concrete, mathematically controlled situations in which the
effective current associated with a reduced model does not satisfy the naive local continuity
equation even when the underlying microscopic theory is conservative. This supports the
motivation for considering (at least as an effective theory) an electromagnetic sector capable

III.
RENOUNCING FULL GAUGE INVARIANCE: POSSIBLE
CONSEQUENCES
If full local gauge invariance is abandoned (or restricted), the electromagnetic field theory
is no longer uniquely Maxwellian, and several general consequences follow.
As it was explicitely stated in Ref. [1], the “alternative coupling” is intended as an
effective model for bosonic condensed-matter systems, while some crucial scattering-theory
properties (soft-photon factorization, KLN cancellations) fail in this framework, placing
particle-physics scattering outside scope. In this sense, the proposal is not a competitor to
the Standard Model as a UV-complete theory, but an effective phenomenological framework
for certain macroscopic bosonic systems.

B.
Possible astrophysical and cosmological implications
Once one modifies electrodynamics and its coupling, one naturally asks: could compact
astrophysical objects or cosmological environments provide constraints or signatures?
Impact is likely limited for standard cosmological scalar fields. Many cosmological scalar
fields invoked in inflation, quintessence, axion-like models, or fuzzy dark matter are electri-
cally neutral; the modified electromagnetic coupling would not apply directly. Thus, broad
cosmological consequences are not automatic.
A more relevant arena is charged scalar configurations coupled to electromagnetism and
gravity, notably charged boson stars described by the Einstein–Maxwell–Klein–Gordon sys-
tem. These models are explicitly locally U(1) gauge invariant, and they involve a conserved
Noether current that includes the −2qAµ|ϕ|2 term, as in Eq. (5). See refs. [6, 7].
From the viewpoint of the modified-coupling proposal, such systems would be altered at
a basic level if one replaced Maxwell electrodynamics by AB extended electrodynamics and
enforced a different coupling principle. Possible changes would be the following: (i) equilib-
rium configurations (field profiles, charge distributions) would shift; (ii) stability boundaries
and maximal masses could change due to altered electromagnetic energy and stress; (iii)
radiative properties could change if additional modes propagate or couple.

likely come from laboratory condensed-matter tests, because superconductors provide con-
trolled environments where the relevant bosonic condensate is real and tunable.
We will address this point in the next sections.

VI.
CONCLUSIONS
We have revisited the long-standing observation that, for charged scalar fields, the term
linear in Aµ obtained by expanding the minimally coupled Lagrangian does not coincide
with the fully conserved electromagnetic source current of the interacting theory. In stan-
dard scalar QED this is not an inconsistency: the conserved source current is obtained by
varying the full action with respect to Aµ and necessarily includes the “seagull” contribu-
tion proportional to A2|ϕ|2. The alternative viewpoint advocated in Ref. [1] is to elevate to
a guiding principle the requirement that electromagnetic interaction energy be expressible


## Page 11


11
as JµAµ with Jµ the (conserved) Noether current of the full matter sector, even in effec-
tive models where a naive local continuity equation may fail. This choice leads naturally
to a reduced-gauge electromagnetic sector (AB-type extended electrodynamics) that can
couple consistently to currents which are not locally conserved at the level of the reduced
description.
On the phenomenological side, the most concrete consequence for superconductors is
a simple and testable scaling: in the modified bosonic framework the magnetic penetra-
tion depth is predicted to satisfy λmag = λL/
√
2, while the qualitative structure of AC
electrodynamics and the type-I/type-II classification remain unchanged up to an equiva-
lent parameter mapping. The Landau-level analysis in Appendix A provides an additional
quantum-mechanical signature of the doubled diamagnetic term, but we have emphasized
that for integer-spin bosons of atomic mass the associated energy shifts are far too small to
be experimentally accessible.
We have then implemented a London-length “consistency check” by comparing indepen-
dent determinations of the same superfluid stiffness ns/m∗: optical/THz measurements pro-
vide an effective length λopt defined exactly as λL, while magnetic probes (LE-µSR Meissner
profiling, TF-µSR in the vortex state, or calibrated microwave methods) provide λmag. For
Nb, YBCO and Ba(Fe,Co)2As2, currently available datasets support the trend λopt > λmag,
with ratios ranging from ∼1.2–1.3 (YBCO) up to ∼1.4 (Ba(Fe,Co)2As2) depending on
sample matching and definitions. This is intriguing, since there are many possible parasitic
effects which can weaken the response of supercurrents to an external field and so increase
the effective penetration depth, but a decrease needs a more fundamental explanation. For
Pb the situation remains inconclusive because type-I materials require sizable nonlocal cor-
rections and different experimental routes yield a spread of effective values, while MgB2
provides the standard result λopt ≃λmag.
The main methodological conclusion is that future progress hinges on improved consistent
comparisons: (i) optical and magnetic determinations should be performed on the same
specimen (or on specimens from the same growth batch with tightly controlled doping and
disorder), (ii) tensor components must be matched directly in anisotropic materials, and
(iii) uncertainty budgets should be propagated uniformly so that the significance of any
observed offset can be assessed quantitatively. A definitive verification (or refutation) of
