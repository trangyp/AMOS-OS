Abstract
Treewidth is a well-studied decompositional parameter to measure the tree-likeness of a graph. While
the propositional satisfiability problem (Sat) is known to be tractable when parameterized by the
treewidth of the underlying primal graph, the evaluation of quantified Boolean formulas (QBFs)
remains PSPACE-complete even on formulas of constant treewidth. Intuitively, this is because
ordinary treewidth does not take into account the prefix of the QBF: it neither distinguishes between
existential and universal variables, nor accounts for the order in which they are quantified. In
the past, several weaker variants of treewidth have been devised to incorporate prefix-sensitive
information. To establish tractability for QBFs under these notions, prior work has employed either
strategy- or resolution-based techniques, thereby dividing the parameterized complexity landscape of
QBF into two regimes that are incomparable in strength. We establish fixed-parameter tractability
with respect to bilateral treewidth, a novel and strictly more powerful decompositional parameter
that combines these rivaling approaches by simultaneously allowing for branching on strategies
and performing Q-resolution. As in previous works in this direction, our algorithm assumes that a
suitable tree decomposition is provided on the input.

Introduction
The language of quantified Boolean formulas (QBFs) [10] extends propositional logic with
existential and universal quantification over variables. While this enables succincter formaliz-
ations, which is highly desirable for tasks in planning and verification [6, 12, 52, 20]—see
also [46] for a recent survey on further applications—it comes at the price of increased
complexity: the satisfiability problem for QBFs (QSat) is PSPACE-complete [49].
When tasked with solving QSat, existential variables can often be handled analogously
to the propositional setting (Sat), whereas genuinely new approaches are needed for their
universal counterparts. Over the past decades, two conceptually different paradigms have
emerged, operating either top-down or bottom-up, respectively. In the top-down approach,
universal expansion explicitly branches over all (partial) assignments to universal variables,
creating a (possibly exponentially large) conjunction of formulas in which existential variables
are replaced by annotated copies to indicate the respective branch. In practical solving, the
(partial) propositional formula that is obtained by universal expansion can then be handed to
a Sat solver [3, 11, 36, 29]. From a proof-theoretic perspective, calculi like ∀Exp + Res [30]
and further generalizations [8] combine universal expansion with well-known propositional
proof systems such as resolution [42].

Universal reduction, the second approach towards eliminating universal variables, processes
QBFs in a bottom-up fashion. Here, universal variables can be dropped from a clause if they
are not blocked by a co-occurring existential variable that is quantified later. Combining
universal reduction with different variants of resolution gave rise to proof systems like
Q-Res [32] and further generalizations [27, 4, 47]. The system Q-Res in particular models
practical QSat solving based on conflict-driven clause learning (CDCL) [51, 28, 37]. Research
in proof complexity has revealed that the paradigms of universal expansion and universal
reduction are incomparable [30, 7], that is, their underlying proof systems do not simulate
each other. As a consequence, the more sophisticated systems IR-calc and IRM-calc [9] have
been introduced to incorporate both paradigms simultaneously.
The PSPACE-hardness of QSat has motivated the question of whether structural proper-
ties of instances could be employed to achieve tractability. At the foundational level, this
study is typically pursued through the lens of parameterized complexity [19, 17]. There, one
analyzes the running time of algorithms not only in terms of the input size |I|, but also
with respect to a specified numerical parameter k, which can be seen as a measure of how
“well-structured” an instance is. The standard notion of tractability used in this setting is
tied to algorithms that run in time f(k) · |I|O(1) for some computable function f; problems
admitting such algorithms are called fixed-parameter tractable (FPT).
The most ubiquitous structural measure employed for such investigations is the treewidth of
the instance, typically measured in terms of its primal graph, thus capturing the tree-likeness of
its variable-to-variable interactions. But while treewidth has been instrumental in the design
of fixed-parameter algorithms for Sat [18, 26, 50], CSP [44], Integer Programming [31, 21]
and many other related problems, QSat is known to remain PSPACE-complete even on
instances which are “path-like” in the sense of having not only constant treewidth but also
constant pathwidth [2]—see also the related works [40, 25, 24] for refined lower bounds.
Intuitively, this is because treewidth does not take into account the prefix of a QBF: it
neither distinguishes between existential and universal variables, nor accounts for the order in
which they are quantified. Consequently, several weaker variants of pathwidth and treewidth
have been proposed to incorporate prefix-sensitive information. These notions split into two
competing realms that remarkably echo the two paradigms encountered in proof complexity
and practical solving.
The first realm is represented by prefix pathwidth [23], a variant of pathwidth that places
an additional restriction on the order in which variables appear in a path decomposition.
More specifically, a variable can only be forgotten when all variables on which it depends have
already been introduced in the decomposition. Fixed-parameter tractability with respect
to prefix pathwidth is then established in a top-down fashion that essentially eliminates
variables from left to right, maintaining at each time step the sets of QBFs that an existential
strategy could have produced under all universal plays so far. This procedure closely resembles
incremental universal expansion with a subsequent explicit instantiation of some existential
variables, as will be discussed in Subsection 3.3. In contrast, respectful treewidth [16, 2]
enforces variables to be forgotten in an order that reverses their occurrence in the prefix. Fixed-
parameter tractability with respect to this parameter is proved bottom-up by producing
variants of Q-Res proofs. Dependency treewidth [22] generalizes respectful treewidth by
incorporating dependency schemes [43, 37, 47, 48], thereby allowing greater flexibility in the
order of variable elimination. Here, the produced derivations are certain types of Q(D)-Res
proofs, where D is the deployed dependency scheme. Prior work has shown that respectful
treewidth and dependency treewidth are incomparable to prefix pathwidth [22], see Figure 1.

For a positive integer i, we use [i] to denote the set {1, . . . , i}. We assume familiarity with

Quantified Boolean Formulas.
Let Z be a countable set of Boolean variables. A literal

An assignment to a set Z of Boolean variables is a function δ : Z →{0, 1}. Given
some Z′ ⊆Z we denote by δ↾Z′ the restriction of δ to Z′. The set of all assignments to Z is
denoted by ⟨Z⟩. Given a QBF Φ = Q.ϕ and an assignment δ ∈⟨Z⟩for some Z ⊆Var(Φ),
we denote by ϕ|δ the matrix that is obtained when δ is applied to ϕ.
The semantics of a QBF Φ = Q.ϕ are neatly described by a two-player game. Over
the course of a game, the variables of Φ = Q.ϕ are assigned to 0 or 1 in the order they
appear in the prefix, with the existential player (universal player, respectively) choosing
the value for Var∃(Φ) (Var∀(Φ), resp.). When the game ends, the players have constructed
a total assignment δ ∈⟨Var(Φ)⟩. The existential player wins if and only if ϕ|δ ≡⊤. For
every x ∈Var∃(Φ), let LQ(x) be the set of all universal variables that are quantified to the
left of x in Q. An existential strategy for Φ is a set τ = {τx | x ∈Var∃(Φ)} of functions
τx : ⟨LQ(x)⟩→{0, 1} that specify the assignment to be used for each existential variable x
based on the assignment to all universal variables preceding x. An existential strategy τ
is a winning existential strategy if for every universal assignment β ∈⟨Var∀(Φ)⟩, we have
ϕ|β∪{x7→τx(β↾LQ(x))|x∈Var∃(Φ)} ≡⊤. A QBF is true if and only if it admits a winning existential
strategy; otherwise we say the QBF is false. If a QBF contains no variables, it is considered
to be true if its matrix is empty, and false if its matrix contains the empty clause. Two QBFs
are equisatisfiable if and only if they are either both true or both false.
We remark that the notion of existential strategies defined above can also be lifted to
account for the presence of dependency schemes; we elaborate on this in the next subsection.
Dependency Schemes and Posets for QBFs.
A partially ordered set (poset) D is a
pair (V, ⪯D) where V is a set and ⪯D is a reflexive, antisymmetric, and transitive binary
relation over V . A linear extension of a partially ordered set D = (V, ⪯D) is a relation ≤O
over V such that x ≤O y whenever x ⪯D y and (V, ≤O) is a linear order. We use dependency
posets [22, 23] to provide a general and formal way to speak about the various dependency
schemes that have been devised for QBFs [43, 37, 47, 48]. Not all dependency schemes give
rise to sound proof systems, and not all are easy to compute. Consequently, we focus solely
on the class of polynomial-time computable permutation dependency schemes [48], which
can be efficiently deployed in resolution-style proof systems without compromising their
soundness. Informally, a permutation dependency scheme assigns to each QBF Φ = Q.ϕ a
binary relation R on its variables such that permuting Q with respect to any order that is
consistent with R yields an equisatisfiable QBF. Based on these dependency schemes, we
define the following notion.
Given a QBF Φ = Q.ϕ, a (permutation) dependency poset D = (Var(Φ), ⪯D) of Φ is a
partially ordered set over Var(Φ) with the following properties:
1. for all x, y ∈Var(Φ), if x ⪯D y, then x = y or x is quantified to the left of y in Q and
2. for every linear extension ≤O of D, Φ = Q.ϕ is true if and only if Q≤O.ϕ is true, where
Q≤O is obtained from Q by rearranging variables together with their respective quantifiers
such that their order agrees with ≤O.
Given a polynomial-time computable permutation dependency scheme, the respective de-
pendency poset can be obtained in polynomial time by computing the transitive closure.
The trivial dependency scheme gives rise to the trivial dependency poset Dtrv of Φ = Q.ϕ, a
special type of poset that for all x, y ∈Var(Φ), enforces x ⪯Dtrv y, whenever x = y or x is
quantified to the left of y in Q. Other examples of polynomial-time computable permutation
dependency schemes include Dtree, Dres, Drrs, and Dstd [35, 48].
Given a QBF Φ = Q.ϕ with dependency poset D we define for each v ∈Var(Φ) the
set D⪯(v) = {v′ | v′ ⪯D v and v′ ∈Var(Φ)} of variables that v depends on. Due to the

Derivation Rules: Q(D)-Resolution and Strategy Extension
In this section, we introduce the derivation rules that will be used in Section 5 to prove
fixed-parameter tractability of QSat with respect to bilateral treewidth. Our first two rules,
Resolution and Reduction, are tightly linked to the proof system Q-Res that was introduced
by Kleine Büning [32] and its dependency-aware variants [47]. Our adaptation of these rules,
however, eliminates variables in an exhaustive way that is more akin to multi-resolution
proofs [15, 39, 2]. Crucially, in contrast to these prior works, we handle tautologies differently
to allow for the sound application of an additional rule that we call Strategy Extension.

Eliminating Universal Variables via Reduction
Let Φ = Q.ϕ be a QBF that does not contain any tautological clauses. We define the
following rule.
ϕ
Reduction of u ∈Var∀(Φ) :
Red(ϕ, u) = {C \ {u, u} | C ∈ϕ}.
We say that we reduce u from ϕ whenever we compute Red(ϕ, u). Note that if ϕ contains
the clause {u} or {u}, then Red(ϕ, u) contains the empty clause, indicating a false instance.
▶Lemma 2. Let Φ = Q.ϕ be a QBF that contains no tautological clause, let u ∈Var∀(Φ),
and D be a dependency poset of Φ. Suppose that for all x ∈Var∃(Φ) with u ⪯D x, there is
no clause C ∈ϕ such that {x, u} ⊆Var(C). Then Φ is true if and only if Q \ {u}.Red(ϕ, u)
is true. Moreover, no clause in Red(ϕ, u) is tautological or contains a literal of u.

and that both u and u are exhaustively eliminated.
Towards the former claim, suppose that Φ is true, that is, there exists a winning existential
strategy τ for Φ. Let τ ′ be the existential strategy that copies τ on all x ∈Var∃(Φ) for
which u ̸∈D⪯(x) and for all remaining x copies τ on the partial assignment u = 0. We claim
that τ ′ is a winning existential strategy for Q \ {u}.Red(ϕ, u). Suppose not, that is, there
exists a universal assignment β ∈⟨Var∀(Φ) \ {u}⟩such that some clause C ∈Red(ϕ, u) is

3.3
Eliminating Variables via Strategy Extension
Strategy Extension is a rule based on a branching technique deployed in [23]. In contrast to
resolution and reduction, this rule operates on multiple QBFs simultaneously. Intuitively,
these QBFs correspond to every possible formula that a specific partial existential strategy
could have produced when responding to every possible universal play thus far. We will show

4.1
Trunk-Aligned Tree Decompositions and Bilateral Treewidth
Let Φ = Q.ϕ be a QBF with dependency poset D and let T = (T, χ) be a tree decomposition
of Φ. For some ℓ∈N, let Tr<T = (t0, . . . , tℓ) be a designated sequence of nodes t0, . . . , tℓ
such that t0 is a leaf, tℓ= root(T), and ti−1 is the child of ti for all i ∈[ℓ], that is, Tr<T
is a leaf-to-root path. We refer to Tr<T as the trunk of T . We call T a trunk-aligned tree
decomposition of Φ with respect to D if it satisfies the following property imposed on the
order in which variables appear in bags: For all u ∈Var(Φ), we have that
(P1)
for all v ∈Var(Φ) with u ∈D⪯(v), it is the case that v /∈χ(forget(u)) or
(P2)
forget(u) ∈Tr<T and for all v ∈D⪯(u), it is the case that v ∈T≤forget(u).
Informally, Property (P1) requires all variables that depend on u to avoid the bag where
u is forgotten—that is, they are forgotten inside Tu or only occur outside of that subtree.
Inside the trunk, Property (P2) allows for an alternative: all variables that u depends on
are introduced “before” u is forgotten. The width of a trunk-aligned tree decomposition
is defined analogously as for standard tree decompositions. The bilateral treewidth of Φ
with respect to D, denoted by btw(Φ, D), is the minimal width over all trunk-aligned tree
decompositions of Φ with respect to D.
In Section 5, we will use trunk-aligned tree decompositions to construct proof-like
derivation sequences. Intuitively, whenever a variable is forgotten in a trunk-aligned tree
decomposition, we will eliminate it from all QBFs that we currently maintain in our derivation
sequence by using one of the rules from Section 3. As we will prove in Section 5, Property (P1)
ensures that we invoke the reduction rule (the resolution rule, respectively) only on universal
(existential, resp.) variables that are not blocked by a co-occurring existential (universal,
resp.) variable that is quantified later. Similarly, Property (P2) guarantees that eliminating
variables via strategy extension is tractable. As a result, our approach not only solves QSat,
but also produces concise proofs; previously, dependency treewidth was known to admit such
proof extractions, whereas prefix pathwidth was not. Before proceeding to Section 5, we
briefly discuss these measures and their relationship to bilateral treewidth.

Concluding Remarks
Our work closes the previous rift between prefix pathwidth and dependency treewidth,
two incomparable measures for the tree-likeness of QBFs. Bilateral treewidth provides
not only a unified explanation for why prefix pathwidth and dependency treewidth enable
fixed-parameter QBF evaluation, but genuinely extends them by covering instances that are
beyond the reach of any of the previously developed width parameters for QBF. Moreover, our
evaluation algorithm produces fixed-parameter sized proofs as a witness and thus preserves a
key advantage of dependency treewidth. In future work, it would be interesting to investigate
whether the asymptotic running time of the presented QBF evaluation algorithm can be
improved, or whether it is tight under the Exponential Time Hypothesis.
From the viewpoint of proof complexity, it may be of interest to formally analyze these
proofs and, if possible, link them to existing proof systems. From an empirical perspective,
it may be interesting to see whether any of the insights employed in our proofs can be
adapted to speed up the solution of instances which are expected to be “tree-like”. We remark
that—similarly as for the preceding work on prefix pathwidth [23]—the theoretical upper
bound in the function f is triple-exponential in the bilateral treewidth (cf. Lemma 14), and
hence is not of practical significance.
Moreover, a major question left for future work is whether the decompositions underlying
bilateral treewidth can be computed in fixed-parameter time. None of the known techniques
for computing or approximating treewidth [1, 41, 13, 14, 34] seem directly applicable to
bilateral treewidth, and in fact the question remains open even for the previously studied
notions of prefix pathwidth and dependency treewidth. Nevertheless, as illustrated in the
