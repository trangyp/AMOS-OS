3. The Three-Filter Closure Certificate
For an impostor kernel K and a representative a in its seed congruence class, define the candidate
(K, a). We exhibit a rigorous obstruction for each candidate via one of three filters.
3.1. Filter Z: Zsigmondy / Higgs exponent. By Zsigmondy’s theorem [5], every primitive
prime divisor r of pe + 1 (with e > 1) satisfies r ≡1 (mod 2e). For r to be 3-Higgs, every prime
factor of 2e must be 3-Higgs and occur in 2e with exponent at most 3. Applied to the seed exponent
a itself, certain values of a are immediately incompatible.
3.2. Filter N: Seed-divisor non-3-Higgs witness. If m | a and a/m is odd, then 2m+1 | 2a+1.
If 2m+1 contains a non-3-Higgs prime factor, then any UPN with 2a ∥n would inherit a non-3-Higgs
prime divisor, a contradiction. We precompute (full or partial) factorizations of 2m + 1 for m ≤M
and reject (K, a) whenever any factored proper divisor exposes a non-3-Higgs prime.

a, a/m odd). Iterate: for each pe ∈targets, factor pe +1 and accumulate the resulting odd needs; if
any prime’s incoming valuation exceeds its current target, raise the target. At each step, compute

Lemma 1. The lower-bound closure is monotone in the seed. If v2 > a + 1 at any step using a
subset S of factor(2a + 1), the same overshoot survives when more primes from factor(2a + 1) are
added. Hence no UPN with 2a ∥n and odd-component structure refining K exists.
Proof. Adding a prime r to targets contributes v2(rer + 1) ≥1 to the running v2 sum and adds the
prime factorization of rer +1 to the odd needs, which can only raise other targets. Both operations
are non-decreasing in v2. Hence if any closure state reaches v2 > a + 1, the same state remains
reachable from larger seeds and the budget is exceeded.
A UPN with 2a ∥n would satisfy P

components of n. If targets is a subset of the actual odd components, the sum v2 in the closure is
a lower bound for a + 1. Overshoot contradicts this.

5. The Set Heven
Let H := {m ≥1 : every prime factor of 2m + 1 is 3-Higgs} and Heven := H ∩2Z.
Proposition 4 (Structural lemma). Suppose m = 2k ∈Heven with k odd and k ≥1. Then
(1) Every prime factor of k is a 3-Higgs prime.
(2) For every prime q | k, vq(k) ≤3.
(3) For every odd divisor d | k, 2d ∈Heven.
The first two conditions say k is Higgs-cubefree: a product of 3-Higgs primes each with multiplicity
at most 3. The third condition is a multiplicative closure: writing S = {k odd : 2k ∈Heven}, S is
closed under taking odd divisors.
Proof. By Zsigmondy’s theorem applied to {2n + 1}n≥1, the number 22k + 1 has a primitive prime
divisor r for every k ≥1 odd (the only Zsigmondy exception for the sequence 2n +1 is n = 3, which
does not arise since 2k is even). The order of 2 modulo r is exactly 4k, so r ≡1 (mod 4k) and
r −1 = 4k · s for some positive integer s.
By hypothesis r is 3-Higgs, so every prime q | r −1 is 3-Higgs and vq(r −1) ≤3.
(1) For each prime q | k, q | 4k | r −1, so q is 3-Higgs.
(2) For each odd prime q | k, vq(r −1) = vq(4k) + vq(s) = vq(k) + vq(s) ≥vq(k). Combined with
vq(r −1) ≤3, we get vq(k) ≤3. (The prime 2 does not constrain k here since k is odd.)
(3) If d | k with d odd and d < k, then 2d | 2k with (2k)/(2d) = k/d odd. Therefore 22d+1 | 22k +1,
so every prime divisor of 22d +1 is a prime divisor of 22k +1 and hence 3-Higgs. So 2d ∈Heven.
□

Status of the hypotheses.
(H1) is a quantitative form of the equidistribution heuristic for
primitive prime divisors of cyclotomic values Φn(2). The natural expectation is that, for n = 4p,
the primes r | Φn(2) should be asymptotically equidistributed among the residue classes mod 16
that are compatible with the constraint r ≡1 (mod 4p), so a 1
4-proportion would satisfy r ≡1
(mod 16). (H1) demands only that this expectation eventually produces at least one such prime.

not, on their own, govern the distribution within the prime-divisor set of a single cyclotomic value
