Introduction
Following the Scott-Strachey approach [1] to giving programming languages denotational seman-
tics, many efforts have been discussed in the literature to give a process language a full abstract
denotational semantics, i.e., the denotational model of a process language is in agreement with
its operational intuition. Several bisimulation preorders have been developed, among those the
famous one is prebisimulation [2] [3], in which the divergence [4] [5] is considered.
To develop a full abstract denotational model of a process language based on prebisimulation
preorder, its behavioural semantics has two problems: (1) Two processes related by a standard
denotational interpretation afford the same finite observations. (2) Prebisimulation can make
distinctions between the behaviours of two processes based on infinite observations. So, finitary
part of prebisimulation is needed to obtain full abstract results.
There existed two main results on finitary bisimulation: the logical form [6] and the behavioural
form [7]. Following the latter one [7], we give the definitions of truly concurrent prebisimulations
and their finitary ones.
