Abstract
Fall, Iggidr, Sallet, and Bonzi—see also Earn and McCluskey (2025) and [AAHK25]—have
studied a class of bilinear epidemic models with rank-one next-generation matrix (NGM), in which
the right Perron eigenvector yields an explicit endemic equilibrium (EE), while the left Perron
eigenvector provides Lyapunov functions at both the disease-free equilibrium (DFE) and the EE.
Together with the graph-theoretic method of Shuai and van den Driessche (2013), these results
establish strong threshold theorems: when the DFE loses stability, a unique EE emerges and be-
comes globally asymptotically stable, with the transition certified by an explicit global Lyapunov
function.
A striking extension was obtained by [RZ12], who proved analogous results for a two-strain
rational rates (saturating) model with multiple boundary equilibria. In this setting, stability is re-
layed across equilibria with increasing infection levels, inducing a partition of parameter space
into regions where exactly one equilibrium is certified to be globally stable by an explicit Lya-
punov function. We refer to this as the competitive exclusion partition property (CEPP).
We show that these results are governed by a common Perron–Volterra mechanism: the Lya-
punov functions are sums of a Volterra entropy on resident variables and a linear functional on
invaders, weighted by left Perron eigenvectors of transversal Jacobians. A balance identity elim-
inates higher-order coupling terms, reducing stability certification to the recursive evaluation of
invasion numbers on the siphon lattice. For a two-strain model with concave increasing incidence,
we prove the CEPP completely, providing explicit global Lyapunov functions at all four equilibria
(Theorem 4). For n ≥3 strains, the same mechanism works, as long as all the strains involve
only one compartment. Beyond that, we also obtain global Lyapunov functions at all equilibria
for models with one irreducible rank one strain, and one (or more) scalar strains in Theorem 5;
whether further extensions are possible remains an open problem.
We have provided an algorithmic implementation in the Mathematica package EPIDCRN
(https://github.com/florinav/EpidCRNmodels). The method organizes the dy-
namics via the minimal siphon lattice, recursively computes Perron eigenvectors of transversal
Jacobians, and constructs candidate Lyapunov functions for all equilibria, producing a parame-
ter partition into regions with a unique locally stable equilibrium and, for n = 2, explicit global
stability certificates.

Why CRN methods.
The first characteristic of most models in mathematical epidemiology (RS),
ecology, and immuno-virology is that they are positive ODEs (or stochastic models whose mean field
is a positive ODE). This means that important phenomena occur when trajectories reach the boundary
of the positive orthant, and that the notion of invariant boundary faces is crucial. While boundary faces
have been studied in classical ODE theory, the systematic exploitation of simplifications arising for
positive ODEs was initiated by CRN works, see notably [HT81, ET89], who showed that all positive
polynomial models admit (non-unique) mass-action representations.
Beyond the polynomial case, but assuming a stoichiometric representation, crucial results relating
boundary ω-limit points to invariant boundary faces and to the Petri-net concept of siphons were
obtained in [ADLS07]. A recent proof of the folklore result that the Jacobian has a triangular block
form on siphon faces was given in [AAH26, Avr26], and [AAHK25] noted that transversal Jacobians
on siphons have the Metzler/cooperativity property. This property further ensures the existence of left
and right Perron eigenvectors which, in all cases we have studied, provide Lyapunov functions and
escape directions from siphon faces respectively.

Conditional GAS in mathematical epidemiology models.
[FIST07, IKST06, BFIS11, EM25] es-

In [SvdD13], Shuai and van den Driessche presented two methods for constructing Lyapunov
functions which establish the global asymptotic stability (GAS) of equilibria in infectious disease
models. The first method, for proving the global stability of the disease-free equilibrium (DFE), used
a Lyapunov function which combined a Volterra function in the variable(s) which are uncondition-
ally positive, and a linear function in the “infection/siphon/possibly zero” variables [AABH25], with
weights given by the left Perron eigenvector of the next generation matrix (NGM).
Note that [SvdD13, Thm 2.2(ii)] stated that instability of the DFE implies persistence — a par-
ticular case of a famous, unresolved persistence conjecture in mathematical epidemiology. The proof
was not given, being referred to as known; see [AABH25] for a recent discussion of the state of this
conjecture and its relation to siphon theory.
A second result of [SvdD13] pertained to the more difficult problem of establishing conditional
GAS for a selected endemic equilibrium (EE). This had been tackled previously only for particular
cases of balanced bilinear positive ODEs of rank one, in papers like [FIST07, IKST06, BFIS11] –see
also [EM25] for more recent work and see [AAHK25] for a review.
[SvdD13]’s result for the EE was proved using Kirchhoff’s matrix tree theorem and some com-
binatorial identities, and became known as the “graph method of [SvdD13]”. This graph method is
similar in spirit to the Metzler transversal Jacobian method, both relying essentially on the possibility
of computing Perron eigenvectors.

Below, we extend the results of [FIST07, IKST06, BFIS11, EM25, SvdD13, RZ12] by dropping
the rank one assumption, and by examining “instability escape paths” which visit several invariant
faces on their way from the DFE to the EE. The global stability mechanism revealed is that at each
boundary equilibrium indexed by a siphon face, the Lyapunov function is the sum of a Volterra entropy
on the resident variables and a linear functional on the invaders weighted by the left Perron eigenvector
of the transversal Jacobian. In the models considered, a balance identity eliminates higher-order cou-
pling terms, so that the derivative of the Lyapunov function splits into a dissipative entropy part and
linear invasion terms governed by the recursive reproduction functions. This yields global Lyapunov
functions on all faces and, consequently, a competitive exclusion partition of the parameter space.
Contents.
This paper provides:
1. A unified proof of the competitive exclusion partition property (CEPP) for two-strain concave
incidence models (Section 2), which generalizes [RZ12]. We provide explicit global Lyapunov
functions at all four equilibria via a single Perron–Volterra ansatz (this extends easily to n-strain
models where each strain/siphon is a singleton).
2. An extension to two-strain models with one scalar strain and one irreducible rank-one n × n-
block is provided in Section 3.
3. Section 4 illustrates via the example of the symmetric two-block rank-one model that our
Perron–Volterra ansatz does not extend further.
4. Section 5 provides an algorithmic implementation of our Perron–Volterra ansatz in the Mathe-
matica package EpidCRN (https://github.com/florinav/EpidCRNmodels), which
includes symbolic verification of the balance identity, a partition of parameter space into CEP
regions, and numerical orbit checks. A local Lyapunov theorem (Theorem 6) near an arbitrary
siphon face of a general chemical ODEs is provided here as well.

5.2
Perron-based construction of the CEP partition
The same spectral object, the left Perron eigenvector of the transversal Jacobian, has two roles:
1. it determines invasion or exclusion through the sign of the dominant transversal eigenvalue;
2. it supplies the linear invader coefficients in the Lyapunov function.
The lattice walk starts at the DFE node and descends by removing unstable invaders. At each node
Σ it computes the face equilibrium, transversal Perron data, invasion quantities, and the candidate
Perron–Volterra function.

The CEP walk is:
1. start with the DFE node;
2. compute all outgoing invasion numbers;

5.6
Conclusion and open directions
The algorithmic CEP picture rests on:
1. entropy contraction on resident variables;
2. Perron weights for transversal invaders;
3. recursive relay on the siphon lattice.
These ingredients yield explicit global Lyapunov functions in the proved cases. The first obstruction
appears when two non-scalar blocks interact through the same susceptible equation, producing the
mixed remainder isolated in (12). Controlling that remainder, or replacing the ansatz by a cross-
equilibrium Lyapunov function, is the main open direction.
