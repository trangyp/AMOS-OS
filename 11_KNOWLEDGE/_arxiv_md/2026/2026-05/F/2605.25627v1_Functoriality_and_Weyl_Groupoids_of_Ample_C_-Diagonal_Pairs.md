Abstract. We initiate a functorial study of ample C∗-diagonal pairs and their Weyl groupoids,
focusing on how certain well-behaved ∗-homomorphisms induce geometric maps between the as-
sociated groupoids. Given a morphism between diagonal pairs satisfying compatibility conditions
with the diagonal and the canonical conditional expectations, we construct an induced partial mor-
phism between the associated Weyl groupoids and analyze its properties. This provides a way to
transfer certain structural information between Cartan-type inclusions. As applications, we study
the behaviour of expectation-compatible ideals, faithful conditional expectations, and dynamical
comparison under diagonal-preserving morphisms. We further investigate tensor products of ample
C∗-diagonal pairs and prove that the Weyl groupoid of a tensor product is naturally identified with
the product of the corresponding Weyl groupoids. Under suitable hypotheses, we obtain a subad-
ditivity result for diagonal dimension via dynamic asymptotic dimension. We also prove that the
Weyl functor is faithful on a natural subcategory of untwisted pairs, providing a concrete invari-
ant that distinguishes non-isomorphic diagonal pairs. The theory is illustrated through examples
arising from AF algebras, graph C∗-algebras, crossed products, and recent constructions of exotic
diagonals in UHF and Cuntz algebras.
1. Introduction
Cartan subalgebras of C∗-algebras, introduced by Kumjian and refined by Renault, play a role in
the structure and classification of C∗-algebras. A unital inclusion (D ⊂A) is called a C∗-diagonal
pair if D is a maximal abelian, regular subalgebra of A possessing the unique extension property
(UEP). Renault’s celebrated reconstruction theorem shows that every such pair arises from a twisted
étale groupoid (G, Σ) with topologically principal G, and that the pair is ample precisely when the
unit space is totally disconnected.
In recent years, Kopsacheilis and Winter developed a systematic theory of ample C∗-diagonal
pairs, establishing deep results on dynamical comparison, tracial almost divisibility, and the equiv-
alence between diagonal comparison and the conjunction of dynamical comparison and strict com-
parison [10]. However, the functorial behavior of such pairs under ∗-homomorphisms — particularly
those that respect the Cartan structure has remained largely unexplored. Understanding this be-
havior is essential for applications to classification, rigidity, and the construction of exotic diagonals
in tensor products and inductive limits.
Very recently, Sibbel and Winter [17] constructed an explicit C*-diagonal with Cantor spectrum
in the Cuntz algebra O2, answering a long-standing question.
Their construction proceeds via
crossed products by minimal homeomorphisms on Cantor spaces and infinite tensor products, ulti-
mately relying on Kirchberg’s O2-absorption theorem. The present work provides a complementary
perspective: the functorial framework developed here (specifically, Theorem 5.8 and Theorem 4.6)
offers a systematic language for understanding how such diagonals behave under homomorphisms
and quotients.

Remark 2.1. For a Weyl groupoid G arising from an ample C*-diagonal pair, the UEP forces

the structure of a C*-diagonal pair. Condition (D) is the minimal requirement that the diagonal

3. Transfer of the faithful conditional expectation and dynamical comparison
The purpose of this section is to connect the functorial behaviour of C*-diagonal pairs with
dynamical properties of their Weyl groupoids. We show that, under suitable hypotheses, faithful
conditional expectations and dynamical comparison can be transferred along diagonal-preserving
morphisms, thereby linking operator-algebraic structures with the underlying groupoid geometry.
Proposition 3.1. Let Φ : (A, D, EA) →(B, C, EB) be a morphism of C∗-diagonal pairs. Assume
that:
(i) Φ is surjective,
(ii) there exists a ∗-homomorphism Ψ : B →A such that Φ ◦Ψ = idB,
(iii) EA ◦Ψ = Ψ ◦EB.
If EA is faithful, then EB is faithful.
Proof. If EB(b) = 0 for a positive b ∈B, then Ψ(b) ≥0 and EA(Ψ(b)) = Ψ(EB(b)) = 0. Since EA
is faithful, Ψ(b) = 0, and applying Φ yields b = 0.
□

5. Partial groupoid morphisms from diagonal isomorphisms
5.1. Why partial groupoid morphisms? A naive approach to functoriality would be to seek an
ordinary continuous groupoid homomorphism ρ : GB →GA extending the unit space homeomor-
phism h. However, this fails for two fundamental reasons.
First, a ∗-homomorphism Φ : A →B that is an isomorphism on diagonals need not map every
normalizer of C to a normalizer of D. Only the normalizers in the image Φ(NA(D)) are guaranteed
to lift. Consequently, only those germs in GB represented by this specific subset of normalizers can
be mapped back to GA. This inevitably produces an open subgroupoid HΦ ⊆GB as the natural
domain of the induced map.
Second, this phenomenon is already familiar in Renault’s theory: a Cartan subalgebra inclusion
(C ⊂B) corresponds to a twisted groupoid (G, Σ), but a morphism of Cartan pairs does not
generally yield a morphism of the underlying groupoids. Instead, it yields a partial morphism or
a morphism of extensions, which is precisely captured by an open subgroupoid of the target. Our
construction ρΦ : HΦ →GA is the concrete manifestation of this principle, bridging the gap between
the algebraic map Φ and the geometric groupoid models. It aligns with the philosophy of partial
actions and inverse semigroups, where the normalizer semigroup N(D) naturally acts partially on
the spectrum of D.

Remark 5.9. Theorem 5.8 has the following geometric interpretation.
Why partiality appears?
The ∗-homomorphism Φ : A →B lifts to a map on normalizers: Φ(NA(D)) ⊆NB(C). However,
the converse inclusion fails in general: not every normalizer of C lies in the image of Φ. Conse-
quently, only those germs in GB represented by normalizers that do lift can be mapped back to GA.
This subset HΦ ⊆GB is exactly the domain of the induced map.
Why subgroupoids are inevitable?
The set HΦ is not arbitrary: it is an open subgroupoid. This follows from the fact that liftable
normalizers are closed under multiplication (if n1, n2 lift, so does n1n2) and under taking adjoints.
Moreover, every unit lifts because 1B = Φ(1A). Thus HΦ inherits the groupoid structure from GB.
Contrast with Kumjian’s equivalence?
When Φ is an isomorphism, NΦ = NB(C) and HΦ = GB. In that case, ρΦ is an ordinary groupoid
isomorphism, recovering Kumjian’s equivalence of categories between twists and C∗-diagonals. For
non-isomorphisms, the domain must shrink to the largest subgroupoid on which a well-defined map
exists.
This is analogous to passing from global actions to partial actions in inverse semigroup
theory.

Remark 6.6. It is expected that if the Weyl groupoid G of an ample C*-diagonal pair is non-

Example 8.2. Let X be a compact totally disconnected Hausdorff space, and let α : Z ↷X be
a free minimal action. Consider the crossed product A = C(X) ⋊α Z with its canonical diagonal
D = C(X). The transformation groupoid associated to the action is G = X ⋊α Z, whose elements
are pairs (x, n) ∈X × Z with
s(x, n) = x,
r(x, n) = αn(x).
Since X is totally disconnected, G is an ample Hausdorff étale groupoid. Moreover, freeness of the
action implies that G is principal: indeed, if (x, n) ∈Gx

Hence every isotropy group is trivial. By Kumjian’s reconstruction theorem [12, Theorem 3.1],

9. Further questions
We conclude with several questions suggested by the preceding results.
Question 9.1. Characterise exactly which inverse semigroup homomorphisms between normaliser
semigroups arise from a morphism Φ with Φ|D an isomorphism.
Question 9.2. Given a continuous open groupoid homomorphism ρ : H →GA where H is an open
subgroupoid of an effective ample Weyl groupoid GB, find conditions under which there exists a
morphism Φ : A →B inducing ρ.
Question 9.3. Give a complete characterisation of expectation-compatible ideals in terms of the
geometry of the groupoid, allowing for possibly twisted groupoids.
Question 9.4. Kopsacheilis and Winter [10] characterized diagonal comparison for a single ample
C*-diagonal pair. Can the functorial map ρΦ of Theorem 5.8 and the functor W of Theorem 5.12
be used to prove that diagonal comparison is preserved under surjective morphisms satisfying (D),
(E), (N)? More precisely, if Φ : (A, D) →(B, C) is a surjective morphism and (A, D) has diagonal
comparison, does (B, C) also have diagonal comparison?
