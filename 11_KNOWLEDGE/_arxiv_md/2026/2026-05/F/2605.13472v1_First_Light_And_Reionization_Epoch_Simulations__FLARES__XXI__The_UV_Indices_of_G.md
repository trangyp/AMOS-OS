Abstract
UV absorption line indices trace both chemical enrichment and star formation histories of high-
redshift galaxies. Yet, the reliability of UV absorption line indices as quantitative tracers of stellar
metallicity (Z⋆) remains uncertain. In this study, we combine synthetic spectral modelling and cos-
mological simulations to establish a theoretical foundation for interpreting these features in the early
Universe. Using the forward modelling package Synthesizer, we compute equivalent widths for a
suite of UV indices based on BPASS stellar population synthesis models and assess their sensitivity
to intrinsic parameters, including metallicity, star formation history (SFH), and model assumptions.
Certain indices, such as 1719 ˚A, are found to exhibit strong and consistent correlations with stellar
metallicity, while others show varying levels of dependence on SFH. To evaluate the role of complex
galaxy formation histories, we apply these models to galaxies drawn from the First Light and Reion-
ization Epoch Simulations (Flares). The simulations provide a diverse population of galaxies with
realistic distributions of stellar metallicity and SFH, enabling a detailed examination of how UV in-
dices evolve under different enrichment scenarios. We explore the correlation of physical properties in
Flares with the metallicity distribution and reproduce a synthetic mass–metallicity relation (MZR).
We then turn to predictions of equivalent widths for the UV indices using Flares. Across the major-
ity of indices, equivalent widths are found to increase monotonically with metallicity, consistent with
theoretical expectations from simple stellar population models. The 1719 ˚A index emerges as one
of the most stable and reliable tracers of stellar metallicity, while the 1460 ˚A feature exhibits strong
sensitivity to nebular emission and bursty star formation. Although increased scatter is introduced in
the cosmological simulations due to composite stellar populations and stochastic enrichment histories,
the overall metallicity trends remain preserved. These predictions serve as a theoretical benchmark
for interpreting rest-frame UV spectra from upcoming and ongoing surveys of high-redshift galaxies,
including those from the JWST. By linking absorption features to stellar chemical properties, this
work supports the development of UV spectral tracers for probing metallicity in the early Universe.

1. INTRODUCTION
A key goal of extragalactic astrophysics is the chart-
ing of the build-up of elements over the Universe’s his-
tory. Such measurements provide important constraints
on both stellar evolution and galaxy formation models.
Since its deployment, the James Webb Space Telescope
(JWST, Gardner et al. 2023) has provided invaluable
data, particularly in the study of high redshift galax-
ies (Bunker et al. 2024; Bagley et al. 2024; Holwerda
et al. 2024). Being positioned at the second Lagrange
point (L2), approximately 1.5 million kilometres from
Earth, it is situated in a stable environment for observing
the Universe. In the infrared wavelength regime, JWST
has successfully identified galaxies that existed less than

The high-resolution capabilities of JWST have allowed

particularly the strong oxygen transitions such as [O iii]
and [O ii], provide valuable constraints on the chemical
composition and physical conditions of the ionised gas
(Shapley et al. 2023).
The chemical composition of a galaxy is inherently
non-uniform, reflecting a distribution across its compo-
nents, including stars, cold (i.e. star-forming) gas, and
hot gas phases. Each of these components traces distinct
enrichment histories and is probed through different ob-
servational and modelling techniques. In this work, we
focus on stellar metallicity.
Stellar metallicities, on the other hand, can be con-
strained from more subtle continuum features, including
UV absorption features (Rix et al. 2004; Sommariva et al.
2012; Calabr`o et al. 2021; Byrne et al. 2022), the contin-
uum slope (Cullen et al. 2019), spectral breaks (Morales
et al. 2025), and flux ratios (Senchyna et al. 2017), among
other methodologies. In this work we focus on the UV
continuum indices.
In the rest-frame UV, specific continuum indices have
been identified as indicators of stellar metallicity. These
features, spanning 1370 ≤λ(˚A) ≤1853 were initially de-
fined by Fanelli et al. (1992) and most recently revised by
Calabr`o et al. (2021). By exploring the strengths of these
UV absorption lines, it is possible to infer the chemical
abundance of specific elements in stars, offering a means
to trace metallicity evolution within the framework of
galaxy evolution.
The initial set of UV indices compiled by Fanelli et al.
(1992) was further investigated by Rix et al. (2004) and
Leitherer et al. (2011), who focused on absorption in-
dices around 1370 ˚A, 1425 ˚A, and 1978 ˚A. Early find-
ings demonstrated that rest-frame UV indices hold sub-
stantial potential for revealing the properties of young,
high-redshift galaxies within the observer frame. By fo-
cusing on indices that display high sensitivity to metal-
licity, these studies established the groundwork for ap-
plying UV indices to examine the chemical composition
of stellar populations.
However, over the past two decades, the reliability of
UV indices for determining metallicity has been ques-
tioned, primarily due to contamination from Interstel-
lar Medium (ISM) absorption (Rix et al. 2004; Leitherer
et al. 2011). In addition, early investigations often suf-
fered from limitations, including small sample sizes, nar-
row Full Width Half Maximum (FWHM) resolutions,
and restricted redshift ranges. Notably, the 1978 ˚A index
was found to be sensitive to variations in the Initial Mass
Function (IMF) (Sommariva et al. 2012). Conversely, in-
dices at 1460 ˚A, 1501 ˚A, and 1533 ˚A, which correspond
to absorption features such as Ni II, Si II, and S V from
the photospheres of young, hot stars, demonstrated in-
dependence from stellar age and IMF variations, offering
a more reliable approach for assessing stellar metallicity
(Calabr`o et al. 2021).
Recent advancements in high-redshift spectroscopic
observations, particularly through instruments such as
JWST/NIRSpec, presents an opportunity to determine
whether these UV indices remain reliable as metallicity
indicators for studying the latest spectroscopic observa-
tions of distant, young galaxies.
In this paper, UV indices covered in the most recent
literature will be re-evaluated in the context of the syn-
thetic observations pipeline Synthesizer (Lovell et al.
2025; Roper et al. 2026) and the First Light And Reion-
ization Epoch Simulations (Flares, Lovell et al. 2021;
Vijayan et al. 2021).
Building on previous investigations (Rix et al. 2004;
Sommariva et al. 2012; Calabr`o et al. 2021), we use
the Binary Population and Spectral Synthesis model
(BPASS) (Stanway & Eldridge 2018) to produce theo-
retical simple stellar populations. Each modelled popula-
tion presented in this work investigates how variations in
intrinsic physical parameters influence the behaviour of
the UV absorption indices at fixed metallicity. By exam-
ining the equivalent width response to changes in these
parameters, we quantify the impact of physical processes.
The parameters explored include star formation history
(SFH), initial mass function (IMF) variations, and the
inclusion or exclusion of nebular emission. Furthermore,
we assess the role of spectral grid resolution, both within
the underlying BPASS models and in comparison with
the resolving power of JWST/NIRSpec, to contextualise
its impact on the accurate measurement and interpreta-
tion of UV indices.
Following the results obtained using BPASS with
Synthesizer, we extend our analysis to the Flares
suite of cosmological simulations to predict the observ-
able properties of galaxies with realistic star forma-
tion and chemical enrichment histories. We present the
physical properties of Flares galaxies, with particu-
lar emphasis on the mass–metallicity relation and the
behaviour of the simulations under different metallicity
weighting schemes. This provides the necessary context
for interpreting how the increased complexity of compos-
ite stellar populations in Flares modifies the behaviour
of UV indices relative to idealised simple stellar popula-
tion models.
The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 will ex-
plore the impact of physical properties on the equivalent
widths of UV indices for synthetic observations obtained
with Synthesizer. In Section 3, we present predictions
from the Flares project, defining the metallicity and its
correlation with star formation, stellar mass, and select
UV indices. Finally, we summarise our findings in this
work and present our conclusions in Section 4.
2. THEORETICAL BACKGROUND
In this section, we explore, using a simplified star for-
mation and metal enrichment history, how each UV in-
dex correlates with metallicity, and the impact of varia-
tions in star formation history, initial mass function, and
nebular emission. Furthermore, we emphasise the role
of spectral grid resolution in enabling comparisons be-
tween model predictions and observational data, partic-
ularly for forthcoming high-resolution spectroscopic sur-
veys. Here, we employ the Synthesizer synthetic obser-
vations pipeline (Lovell et al. 2025; Roper et al. 2026).

These grids are used to examine the relationship be-
tween equivalent width and metallicity across the defined
UV indices assuming a continuous star formation history
of 100 Myr. Figure 2 demonstrates a consistent mono-
tonic trend for the majority of indices, with equivalent
widths increasing systematically with metallicity. This
behaviour is physically well motivated, as higher metal-
licities enhance line blanketing and opacity in stellar at-
mospheres, leading to stronger absorption features in the
emergent UV spectra.
These results confirm that the UV indices implemented
within Synthesizer retain strong sensitivity to stellar
metallicity, providing a robust foundation for their appli-
cation as metallicity diagnostics in subsequent analysis.
However, not all indices follow a monotonic trend with
metallicity. Figure 2 shows that notable departures are
evident for the 1400 ˚A and, more prominently, the 1460 ˚A
indices. The 1400 ˚A feature, typically associated with
Si iv, exhibits a mild dip in equivalent width at Z ≈
10−2. This change in behaviour may reflect the transition
between dominant ionisation stages of silicon, effects of
outflows, or the onset of line saturation effects within the
stellar photospheres. Such behaviour is commonly seen
in regions where temperature and ionisation balance shift
rapidly with increasing metallicity.

2.3. Star Formation History
To quantify how star-formation activity impacts the
UV indices, we explore three parametrisations of a single
spectral grid with Synthesizer. Each grid is constructed
from BPASS single-metallicity stellar populations and in-
tegrated over a different star formation history of 10 Myr,
100 Myr, and 1 Gyr, following the approach of Byrne
et al. (2022). These varying star formation episodes al-
low for a direct comparison of the equivalent widths pre-
dicted for different star formation timescales, exploring
the impact of burstiness on the absorption lines of a stel-
lar population.
Figure 3 shows that across most indices, a pattern
emerges, with spectra corresponding to the youngest,
10 Myr populations generally exhibiting stronger absorp-
tion than those formed under extended (≥100 Myr) star
formation histories. However, this response is not uni-
form across all features, revealing dependencies between
ionisation state, metallicity, and the temporal distribu-
tion of stellar ages.

10 Myr bursts producing slightly weaker absorption than
100 Myr populations at metallicities below Z ≈10−2,

The 1425 ˚A index shows a mild offset, with 10 Myr his-

1 Gyr population slightly lower, suggesting weak sensi-
tivity to stellar age distribution.

2.4. Initial Mass Function
The Initial Mass Function (IMF) describes how the
stellar mass is distributed throughout a stellar popula-
tion during formation, with star formation and subse-
quent feedback processes being inherently dependent on
the mass distribution of stars. Consequently, IMF varia-
tions can therefore lead to measurable differences in the
equivalent widths of UV indices.
In this study, two widely used IMF parametrisations
are considered: the Chabrier IMF (Chabrier 2003) and
a broken power-law (BPL) IMF (Kroupa 2001).
The
Chabrier IMF is a commonly adopted empirical form,
characterised by a log-normal distribution at low stellar
masses and transitioning to a power-law decline at higher
masses (typically above ∼1 M⊙), extending up to an
assumed upper stellar mass limit of ∼100–300 M⊙de-
pending on the model implementation (Chabrier 2003).
In contrast, the broken power-law IMF allows for dif-
ferent power-law slopes across distinct stellar mass in-
tervals. For BPASS, the default BPL IMF follows the
prescription of Kroupa (2001), with a power-law slope
of −1.30 over the mass range 0.1–0.5 M⊙, steepening to
−2.35 at higher masses. For comparison, the Chabrier
IMF adopts a log-normal form below ∼1 M⊙and tran-
sitions to a high-mass power-law slope of −2.3.
BPL
extends to a maximum initial stellar mass of 300 M⊙.
The broken power law IMF introduces greater flexibility,
being frequently used to probe IMF variations in extreme
environments, such as high-redshift galaxies or starburst
regions, where deviations from the canonical IMF may
occur.
The divergence between the Chabrier and BPL initial
mass functions in Figure 4 is most pronounced at in-
dices 1370 ˚A and 1460 ˚A, corresponding to spectral fea-
tures dominated by Si iii and Ni ii, respectively. These
deviations arise from the differing treatment of high-
mass stars between the two IMF formulations. The BPL
IMF, with its flatter slope at the high-mass end, yields
a greater proportion of massive stars compared to stan-
dard IMFs. The primary differences between these IMFs
arise at lower stellar masses, whereas deviations in the
high-mass slope, as in the BPL IMF, directly impact the
relative contribution of massive stars to the integrated
spectrum. As a result, spectral regions sensitive to the

2.5. Emission Modelling
To assess the impact of photoionisation modelling on
equivalent width estimations, it is necessary to distin-
guish between the pure stellar incident spectrum and
the nebular-processed spectrum that includes emission
from ionised gas.
Comparing these two cases enables
a direct evaluation of how nebular emission influences
the measured UV indices.
Intrinsic spectra integrate
the emission from ionized gas, which can significantly
modify the equivalent width of spectral features.
By
comparing these emission models, it becomes possible
to verify whether purely incident emission spectra suf-
ficiently represent observational conditions or if nebular
emission must be considered to capture realistic spectral
behaviour.
To incorporate nebular emission within the BPASS
model grids, a radiative transfer model was implemented
using the CLOUDY photo-ionisation code (Gunasekera
et al. 2023). This addition enables continuous star forma-

3.1. The First Light and Reionization Epoch
Simulations
In this work we utilise the First Light and Reioniza-
tion Epoch Simulation (Lovell et al. 2021; Vijayan et al.
2021) suite to study the UV indices. In short, Flares is
a suite of zoom re-simulations employing the AGNdT9
variant of the Eagle reference model (Schaye et al. 2015;
Crain et al. 2015), including adopting the same particle
mass. The core Flares suite consists of 40 14/h cMpc
radius re-simulations, using regions selected from a large
(3.2 cGpc)3 low-resolution dark matter only simulation
(Barnes et al. 2017). The chosen regions cover a wide
range in over-density, δ + 1 ≈−1 →1 (at z ≈4.7), with
an intentional over-representation of the highest-density
environments. This selection strategy enables the sim-
ulation of galaxies spanning a broader range in stellar
mass, luminosity, and environment than would be achiev-
able using a periodic volume with comparable computa-
tional resources. To ensure that this environmental bias
does not distort statistical inferences, each region is as-
signed a weight corresponding to its cosmological volume,
allowing the combined sample to recover representative
population-level properties. All subsequent analyses and
distributions presented in this work are computed using
these weights.
The resulting galaxy sample, covering stellar masses in
the range M⋆= 108–1011 M⊙(at z = 5), aligns well with
all but the most sensitive JWST observations (Wilkins
et al. 2022b). Stellar properties are typically measured
within a fixed spherical 30 pkpc aperture, centred on the
potential minimum of each galaxy, ensuring consistency
in derived quantities such as stellar mass and metallicity
while minimising contamination from surrounding ma-
terial. For a more detailed introduction to Flares see
Lovell et al. (2021), and for the underlying physics model,
Schaye et al. (2015) and Crain et al. (2015).
3.1.1. Spectral Energy Distribution modelling
The utilisation of synthetic spectral energy distribu-
tions (SEDs) generated for the Flares galaxies is central
to this work. The original SED modelling produced for
Flares is described in Vijayan et al. (2021) and builds
upon the efforts of Wilkins et al. (2016b) and Wilkins
et al. (2018). Recently, we have updated this modelling
to utilise the new Synthesizer synthetic observations
pipeline. This is identical to our previous approach in
Vijayan et al. (2021), now carried out with the publicly
available Synthesizer (Lovell et al. 2025; Roper et al.
2026) package.
In brief, the generation of Flares galaxy spectra re-
quires the construction of synthetic spectral energy dis-
tributions (SEDs) using the BPASS v2.3 binary stellar
population synthesis model.
The adopted grid corre-
sponds to a binary BPASS model with a broken power-
law IMF over 0.1–300 M⊙, and slopes −1.3 and −2.35.
Each star particle within a galaxy is assigned an SED
based on its mass, age, and metallicity, with the com-
bined contribution from all star particles representing
the integrated stellar emission of the system.
In real
galaxies, however, this stellar radiation is subsequently
reprocessed through interactions with gas and dust in
the interstellar medium (ISM), modifying the emergent
spectrum. To account for this, ionisation-bounded H ii
regions are modelled using CLOUDY, which is coupled to
each star particle to simulate nebular emission (Gunasek-
era et al. 2023).
Active galactic nucleus (AGN) emission is not included
in this framework. This assumption is justified by the
relatively low stellar masses and high redshifts of the
galaxies considered, for which AGN activity is expected
to be either absent or sub-dominant to stellar processes
in the UV range. As such, the spectral features analysed
in this work are assumed to be dominated by stellar and
nebular contributions.
Assuming a spherical geometry and a nebular metallic-
ity that matches the total metallicity of the correspond-
ing star particle, the pure stellar spectrum is used as the
incident radiation field.
Additionally, a covering frac-
tion of 1 is assumed, which corresponds to a LyC escape
fraction of approximately zero for an ionization-bound
nebula. The hydrogen density is set at 102.5 cm−3, and
the ionization parameter is volume-averaged.
To model the impact of dust on the emergent spec-
tra, we adopt a two-component attenuation prescription.
First, a birth-cloud component is applied to all stars
younger than 10 Myr, accounting for the dense, undis-
persed material that surrounds newly formed stellar pop-
ulations. Second, attenuation by the diffuse interstellar
medium is evaluated for every galaxy by computing the
surface density of metals, ΣZ, along the line of sight
to each individual star particle.1
The resulting metal
column is converted to a wavelength-dependent optical
depth, τλ, using a dust–to–metal scaling fitted to the
dust model implemented in L-Galaxies (Vijayan et al.
2019), which reproduces the lower dust-to-metal ratios
inferred for high-redshift systems.
This
approach
yields
a
strongly
environment-
dependent UV luminosity function in which attenuation
removes an average of ∼40% of the intrinsic UV light
at z = 7 and grows increasingly dominant below z ≈6.2
Together, the birth-cloud and diffuse ISM components
provide a self-consistent, particle-by-particle dust model
that captures the heterogeneous obscuration expected in
early galaxies while remaining consistent with empirical
constraints on the dust content of the high-redshift
Universe.
This process culminates in generating synthetic spec-
tra for each galaxy, where the continuum of one such
galaxy from the Flares simulation is illustrated in Fig-

absorption and emission features arising from the under-

interstellar medium. In the simulations, each galaxy is
constructed from a physically motivated set of stellar and
gas properties assuming a continuous star formation his-
tory of 100 Myr, ensuring that the resulting spectra re-
flect realistic galactic conditions and evolutionary states.

flattening, and in some cases a decline in equivalent
width at Z ≳10−2, indicating a departure from sim-
ple monotonic behaviour. This trend is most evident in
the highest-mass Flares galaxies at z = 5, where a dip
in equivalent width is observed at the metal-rich end.
This behaviour suggests that the absorption strength of
Fe-peak elements becomes increasingly influenced by sec-
ondary effects at high metallicity, including burst-driven
changes in the stellar population, variations in the ioni-
sation structure, and partial filling of absorption features
by emission.
The 1501 ˚A index broadly follows the simple stel-

small number of outliers at the high-mass end. A mild

to further metal enrichment, as the line approaches satu-

The 1533 ˚A and 1550 ˚A indices recover the expected

C iv feature at 1550 ˚A exhibits mild flattening, consistent
with the increasing influence of stellar winds and circum-
stellar absorption, which act to reduce the sensitivity of
the line to metallicity alone.
At longer wavelengths, the 1719 ˚A and 1853 ˚A in-
dices show good agreement with simple stellar popula-
tion predictions, with increased scatter observed toward
higher metallicities. No significant flattening is evident,
however, higher-mass systems occupy a broader distribu-
tion in equivalent width. This dispersion likely reflects
the blended nature of these indices, where contributions
from multiple transitions introduce increased sensitiv-

spectral effects in Flares galaxies at z = 5.
Overall, the close agreement between Flares and sim-

While all
indices retain sensitivity to metallicity, their robustness
varies. The 1425 ˚A and 1550 ˚A features emerge as the
most stable diagnostics, whereas all other indices provide
additional sensitivity to star formation variability and
enrichment complexity within high-redshift galaxies.
3.3.1. The redshift evolution of UV indices in Flares.
The epoch of reionization spans a broad redshift inter-
val from approximately z ∼10 to z ∼6, encapsulating a
period of intense and rapid chemical evolution. Within
this epoch, the Flares simulations provide an opportu-
nity to trace the evolution of rest-frame UV absorption
features in response to time-dependent star formation
and enrichment processes. By examining the behaviour
of UV index equivalent widths across discrete redshift
snapshots 5 ≤z ≤10, an exploration into the stability
and reliability of these UV indices in Flares is pos-
sible under increasingly dynamic conditions.
For each
redshift slice, the median equivalent width is derived us-
ing a binned, weighted quantile estimation, ensuring that
the resulting trends reflect the underlying distribution of
galaxy properties rather than being dominated by out-
liers or sampling fluctuations.
Across the suite of UV indices, the 1370 ˚A and 1400 ˚A
features are notably stable across redshift, exhibiting
minimal deviation.
This behaviour is consistent with
the theoretical trends established in Section 2, where
these Si-dominated absorption features were shown to be
largely insensitive to variations in star formation history
and stellar population age distributions. Although mod-
est changes were identified under extreme star formation
histories, the overall response remained weak compared
to other indices. The strong alignment observed across
the full redshift range therefore reinforces the resilience
of these features, indicating that Si absorption is only
weakly affected by time-variable environmental condi-
tions, including fluctuations in star formation rate and
ISM properties.
The 1425 ˚A index displays a modest but noticeable
increase in equivalent width variance with redshift, re-
flecting its composite nature involving contributions from
Fe V, C III, and Si III. This blend introduces a more
complex sensitivity to chemical enrichment, especially in
galaxies where iron-peak enrichment proceeds on diver-
gent timescales.
Conversely, the 1460 ˚A and 1501 ˚A indices exhibit the
highest degree of redshift sensitivity within the sample.
These features show marked increases in variability be-
tween redshifts, indicating that both Ni II and S V are
particularly susceptible to environmental and temporal
variance. Given that both ions arise in regions sensitive
to stellar wind dynamics, ionizing radiation fields, and
population age, their equivalent widths are more strongly
modulated by short-term changes in star formation his-
tory and feedback. This makes these indices less reliable
for use as stand-alone metallicity tracers at the high-
est redshifts without careful modelling of galaxy-specific
conditions.
Notably, the level of deviation decreases again for the
1533 ˚A and 1550 ˚A indices, suggesting that Si II and
C IV transitions are marginally more resilient to red-
shift driven variation, despite their known associations
with interstellar and wind sensitive regions. This points
to a partial averaging out of burst driven variability or
possibly a convergence of the physical conditions under
which these lines form by z ∼5. Nonetheless, care must
be taken when interpreting these features at the highest
redshifts, particularly given the broader dynamic range
of the 1550 ˚A index and its sensitivity to stellar feedback.
Two of the most consistent indices across redshift are
1719 ˚A and 1853 ˚A, which show minimal deviation from
their respective metallicity trends. The 1719 ˚A blend,
in particular, stands out for its exceptionally tight align-
ment across all redshifts. Comprising contributions from
N IV, Si IV, Al II, and Fe IV, the index appears to bene-
fit from an intrinsic averaging effect, wherein fluctuations
in one component may be compensated by the relative
stability of others. This results in a line profile that is less
susceptible to stochastic deviations and more represen-
tative of the integrated stellar population, even under
bursty conditions. The 1853 ˚A index, while less sensi-
tive overall, displays similarly stable behaviour, further
reinforcing its reliability in chemically mature systems.
However, its interpretation should be approached with
caution due to prior evidence of potential line contam-
ination within the index window, which may introduce
systematic uncertainties, particularly in high-resolution
analyses.
A notable feature in the redshift evolution of the UV
indices is the apparent deviation at z = 9, particularly
at metallicities near Z ∼10−2. However, this behaviour
is most plausibly attributed to small-number statistics

Collectively, these results highlight the nuanced red-
shift dependence of UV indices in realistic high-redshift
galaxies. The stability of indices like 1370 ˚A, 1425 ˚A,
1719 ˚A, and to some extent 1853 ˚A, positions them as
promising tools for tracking metallicity evolution during
the epoch of reionization. However, the observed vari-

careful treatment of temporal effects, especially when in-
terpreting data from galaxies in the earliest stages of star
formation and enrichment.

and have yet to be applied to constraining metallicity

such as JWST have provided an abundances of high-z

to these predictions of serve to complement the use of
UV indices as metallicity tracers of the high-z Universe.
At present, direct measurements of stellar metallic-
ity in the reionization era remain observationally chal-
lenging, and constraints are typically inferred indirectly
or through simulations and forward-modelled synthetic
spectra. However, the rapid expansion of high-quality
rest-frame ultraviolet spectroscopy from recent JWST
surveys at 5 ≲z ≲10 now provides an opportunity to
directly test and apply physically motivated metallicity
diagnostics at early cosmic times.
We demonstrate that rest-frame UV absorption in-
dices constitute a robust set of stellar metallicity tracers
when analysed within a fully forward-modelled frame-
work.
Our analysis shows that metallicity constraints
derived from UV indices remain stable even when in-
dividual features are weakened or partially obscured.
This may be further progressed by explicitly accounting
for observational effects, including spectral resolution,
signal-to-noise degradation, and stochastic sampling to
combine multiple indices within a joint inference frame-
work mitigates the limitations of any single diagnostic,
enabling reliable metallicity recovery under conditions
representative of realistic high-redshift observations.
Using both controlled stellar population models and
the cosmological Flares simulations, we systematically
characterise the sensitivity of each UV index to metal-
licity, abundance ratios, star formation history, nebu-
lar emission, and redshift evolution. This mapping re-
veals that several indices, most notably 1370 ˚A, 1425 ˚A,
1533 ˚A, and 1719 ˚A, exhibit monotonic, low-scatter be-
haviour across a broad range of galaxy properties, estab-
lishing them as particularly robust metallicity tracers.
While other indices show enhanced sensitivity to bursts,
nebular contamination, or high-metallicity regimes..
Taken together, these results establish a clear, index-
specific framework for stellar metallicity inference in the
early Universe. By providing a physically grounded inter-
pretation of UV absorption features and demonstrating
their resilience to parametric variations, this study vali-
dates UV indices as a strong technique for probing chem-
ical enrichment, star formation, and feedback in faint
high-redshift galaxies.
A notable omission in the present investigation of sim-
ple stellar populations in Section2, is the explicit inclu-
sion of α-enhancement. Variations in the abundance ra-
tio [α/Fe] provide an important probe of chemical enrich-
ment histories, particularly within high-redshift galaxies.
The inclusion of non-solar abundance patterns therefore
offers an opportunity to further investigate the sensitivity
of UV indices to the chemical evolution of early galaxies.
Future work will extend this analysis by examining the
behaviour of α-enhancement within both idealised sim-
ple stellar populations and hydrodynamical simulations,
enabling a more detailed exploration of how abundance
variations influence the physical and observable proper-
ties of galaxies in simple stellar populations and Flares
during the early Universe.
The framework developed here presents a promising
opportunity for application to JWST/NIRSpec obser-
vations in order to constrain stellar metallicities.
Al-
though this remains a task that is strongly dependent
on both sample size and sufficient signal-to-noise ra-
tios, future work will aim to potentially complement
the present study with a dedicated publication.
This
forthcoming work will systematically assess the current
state of the art and provide direct comparisons be-
tween noise-modelled cosmological simulations and high-
redshift JWST/NIRSpec observations.
