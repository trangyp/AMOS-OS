Flavour Changing Neutral Current decays at LHCb
C. Langenbruch on behalf of the LHCb collaboration
Physikalisches Institut, Heidelberg University, INF 226, 69120 Heidelberg, Germany
Flavour Changing Neutral Current (FCNC) decays are forbidden at lowest perturbative or-
der in the Standard Model (SM) and only allowed via quantum loops. These transitions are
therefore heavily suppressed in the SM, and New Physics (NP) can give significant contribu-
tions through virtual corrections. Of particular interest are semileptonic b →s(d)ℓ+ℓ−and
radiative b →s(d)γ decays that allow to not only to search for the presence of NP, but also
probe its potential operator structure through a multitude of observables. These observables
include measurements of branching fractions, angular observables, CP-asymmetries and tests
of lepton flavour universality. Long-standing tensions of data with SM predictions have been
observed in b→sµ+µ−decays, consistently in measurements of branching fractions and angu-
lar analyses. However, the significance of these tensions depends on assumptions on hadronic
uncertainties in the SM prediction. These proceedings summarise the most recent results on
FCNC decays from the LHCb experiment, including the legacy measurement of the key decay
B0 →K∗0µ+µ−, using 8.4 fb−1 of data from the LHC Run 1 and 2.
1
Introduction
Rare Flavour Changing Neutral Current (FCNC) decays are sensitive probes for New Physics
(NP) as they are strongly (loop-)suppressed in the Standard Model (SM). Heavy new particles
can significantly contribute to these processes via virtual corrections. Precision measurements
of semileptonic b→s(d)ℓ+ℓ−and radiative b→s(d)γ decays therefore allow to perform model-
agnostic searches for NP. Depending on the flavour-violating couplings of NP these searches can
be sensitive to mass scales of O(100 TeV), well beyond those accessible through direct searches.
The large b¯b production cross-section at the LHC of around 500 µb at √s = 13 TeV results in
enormous samples of all b-hadron species, including B0, B±, and B0
s-mesons, as well as Λ0
b-
baryons. As illustrated in Fig. 1 (left) this results in a multitude of decay modes with different
Lorentz structure, allowing both cross-checks and complementary measurements that can probe
the operator structure of potential NP.
The LHCb experiment is ideally suited for the analysis of rare FCNC decays due to its high
efficiency for the reconstruction of secondary vertices from b-hadron decays provided by the
LHCb Vertex Locator (VeLo), which exhibits an impact parameter resolution of around 20 µm.

Branching Fractions
The LHCb experiment performs precision determinations of the branching fractions of rare
b→sµ+µ−processes by measuring them relative to the corresponding tree-level b→c¯cs decays,
which result in the same final states via J/ψ(→µ+µ−) transitions. The SM predictions for the
branching fractions of FCNC decays are unfortunately affected by significant hadronic uncer-
tainties, from both non-perturbative form factors, sketched in Fig. 1 (middle), and charm-loop
contributions, shown in Fig. 1 (right). While there has been recent progress on form factor
uncertainties e. g. from lattice calculations 1 the contributions from the charm-loop are very
challenging to estimate and the corresponding uncertainties are currently under discussion.
The measurements are performed in bins of q2 = m2(µ+µ−), which gives some information
on the relative contributions from different effective operators. The results for the decay B0
s →
ϕµ+µ−are shown in Fig. 2 (left). At intermediate q2 values the measurement 2 lies around
3 standard deviations (σ) below a SM prediction 3,4. Consistent tensions with SM predictions
have also been observed in the decays B+ →K+µ+µ−, B0 →K0µ+µ−, B+ →K∗+µ+µ−,
and Λ0
b →Λ0µ+µ−5,6. The branching fraction of the normalisation mode for Λ0
b →Λ0µ+µ−,
Λ0
b →J/ψΛ0, was recently updated by LHCb in Ref. 7. This lead to a significant reduction
of the branching fraction of the rare mode, illustrated in Fig. 2 (right), where the updated
measurement is compared with two SM predictions 8,9. Low branching fractions have also been
reported by the CMS experiment for B0 →K∗0µ+µ−10 and for B0
s →ϕµ+µ−11. While the
tensions with the SM predictions are consistent, their significance depends on the mentioned
assumptions on hadronic uncertainties.
Most recently, the LHCb experiment has found first evidence for the b →sµ+µ−decay
B+ →¯Λ0pµ+µ−, a meson decay with a baryonic final state, with 3.5 σ significance 12. The decay
is studied in two regions of m(¯Λ0p), and its branching fraction in the region m(¯Λ0p) < 2.8 GeV
is measured to be B = (1.70+0.65
−0.56 ± 0.17 ± 0.14) × 10−8, around 2 σ below a SM prediction 12.
Furthermore, LHCb has provided measurements of two radiative b→dγ decays, that, when
normalising to the b →sγ decay B0 →K∗0γ, exhibit sensitivity to the ratio of CKM matrix
elements |Vtd/Vts|. Figure 3 (left) shows the b →dγ decay B0 →ρ0γ, where the photon is
reconstructed in the electromagnetic calorimeter. The branching fraction (ratio) is determined to
be B(B0 →ρ0γ) = (7.9±0.3±0.2±0.2)×10−7 (B(B0 →ρ0γ)/B(B0 →K∗0γ) = 0.0189±0.0007±
0.0005) 13. These are the world’s most precise measurements and consistent with the world
average. In addition, LHCb has found first evidence for the rare b →dγ decay B0
s →K−π+γ
with a significance of 3.5 σ.

CP-Asymmetries
CP-asymmetries in rare b →sµ+µ−decays, such as ACP (B0 →K∗0µ+µ−) shown in Fig. 4
(bottom right) constitute clean observables, as they are sensitive to weak phases that are not
induced by QCD effects.
LHCb has recently measured the time-dependent CP-asymmetry
ACP = S sin(∆mdt) −C cos(∆mdt) using the rare decay B0 →K0
Sµ+µ−, where S is mixing-
induced and C from potential direct CP-violation. In the SM, the expectation is S = sin 2βd and
C = 0. Integrated over the full q2 range 941 ± 55 signal events are found and the CP-violating
parameters are determined to be S = 0.82 ± 0.29 ± 0.05 and C = −0.13 ± 0.32 ± 0.04 23, in good
agreement with the SM expectation, as shown in Fig. 5 (left).

5
Run 3 and Future Data Samples
Since the start of Run 3 the LHCb experiment has collected pp collision data corresponding
to an integrated luminosity of more than 22 fb−1, as shown in Fig. 6 (left). For analyses of
rare decays that are statistically dominated this large data sample is extremely valuable, but a
good understanding of the data taken by the new detector is required. To this end, LHCb has
performed an angular analysis of the decay B+ →J/ψ(→µ+µ−)K+ using 1 fb−1 of 2024 data.
The analysis determines the forward-backward asymmetry AFB = 0.00019 ± 0.00048 ± 0.00033
and the flat parameter FH = 0.0005 ± 0.0011 ± 0.0014 32, in excellent agreement with the
expectation of zero. In addition, these parameters are measured differential in bins of 17 different
variables, and no significant deviations from the expectation are found, giving great confidence
in the quality of the data.

Lepton Flavour Universality tests
Lepton flavour universality (LFU) can be tested in rare b→sℓ+ℓ−decays by measuring branch-
ing fraction ratios of b →sµ+µ−and b →se+e−decays. If lepton masses can be neglected,
these ratios are unity in the SM and uncertainties from QED effects are at most of the or-
der of 1%. Crucially, the SM prediction is not affected by hadronic uncertainties that cancel
in the ratio. Most recently, the LHCb experiment has measured RK∗in the high q2 region
above 14 GeV2/c4 using 9 fb−1 of Run 1 and 2 data. The analysis is validated using the charmo-

Conclusions
These proceedings summarise recent results on FCNC decays from the LHCb collaboration.
Most observables are in good agreement with the SM predictions, but consistent tensions persist
in measurements of branching fractions and angular analyses. These tensions are particularly
prominent in the angular analysis of the key decay mode B0 →K∗0µ+µ−, where the tensions
are found to correspond to around 4 σ in a fit of the vector coupling ReC9. However, the SM
predictions for branching fractions and angular observables are affected by significant hadronic
uncertainties. In particular contributions from the so-called charm loop are currently under
discussion. The clarification of these tensions will therefore require additional work, both in
theory as well as in experiment. On the experimental side the large Run 3 data sample will
allow for measurements of unprecedented precision in rare decays that are largely statistically
dominated. First control measurements using Run 3 data illustrate the excellent quality of these
data samples, that already correspond to an integrated luminosity of more than 22 fb−1.
