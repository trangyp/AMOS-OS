FUNCTORIALITY OF THE KSGNS CONSTRUCTION FOR
INTERTWINERS OF STRICT POSITIVE C∗-CORRESPONDENCES
LUCUS BRADY AND RYAN GRADY
Abstract. We prove that the KSGNS construction can be viewed as an endofunctor on a
category whose objects are positive C∗-correspondences from a fixed C∗-algebra and mor-
phisms are given by intertwiners which account for automorphisms of the fixed C∗-algebra.
Using this perspective, we provide a functorial perspective for strict positive equivariant
C∗-correspondences of C∗-dynamical systems and show every strict positive equivariant
C∗-correspondence of C∗-dynamical systems unitarily uniquely dilates under the KSGNS
construction to an equivariant C∗-correspondence of the dynamical systems.

1. Introduction
The KSGNS construction1 provides a generalization of both the GNS construction and
Stinespring Dilation Theorem to the setting of Hilbert modules, and enables one to form C∗-
correspondences from suitable completely positive maps. Abstractly, the KSGNS construc-
tion provides an assignment of strict positive C∗-correspondences to C∗-correspondences.
Due to this transformation of data, we seek to investigate in what sense is this assignment
functorial.
To begin understanding functorality of the KSGNS construction, we first need a cate-
gory encoding C∗-correspondences.
Work regarding categorizing C∗-correspondences has
been already taken up.
In the work of [EKQR05], the authors construct a category for
C∗-correspondences, later called the Enchilada category [EKQ23], to obtain a categori-
cal view of the imprimitivity theorems for C∗-dynamical systems. Briefly, the Enchilada
category has objects as C∗-algebras, morphisms given by unitary isomorphism classes of
C∗-correspondences, and composition given by the balance/interior tensor product. A mod-
ification of the Enchilada category can be made to keep track of the unitary isomorphism
as 2-morphisms [BMZ12]. In the work of [BMR24], the Enchilada category was generalized
to the semi-category called Quesadilla by taking morphisms as unitary isomorphism classes
of strict positive C∗-correspondences. The Enchilada category is contained as a subcategory
of Quesadilla, and the KSGNS construction can be viewed as a retraction of Quesadilla to
Enchilada [BMR24].
In this work, we take an alternative perspective by viewing the correspondences as objects
instead of morphisms. One way of achieving this perspective is to consider the under cat-
egory of either Enchilada or Quesadilla for a fixed C∗-algebra; however, we want to study
the intertwiners (not necessarily unitary) of the correspondences which consider potential
automorphisms of the fixed C∗-algebra. This point of view is not readily available from
taking the under category of either Enchilada or Quesadilla, or by encoding the intertwining
data as 2-morphisms due to a lack of a well-defined composition of automorphisms between
different C∗-algebras.

Traditionally, the automorphism data is not taken into account when discussing intertwin-
ers (such as for representations of groups); however, such automorphisms play an important
role when viewing these algebras through the lens of quantum mechanics. To emphasize this
point, let us suppose we have a C∗-algebra A representing the algebra of observables for a
quantum system. As states are examples of non-degenerate completely positive maps, then
every state ϕ ∈S(A) defines a strict positive C∗-correspondence from A to C. Applying
the GNS construction yields a triple (Hϕ, πϕ, xϕ) where Hϕ is a complex Hilbert space and
πϕ : A →B(Hϕ) is a cyclic representation of A with unit cyclic vector xϕ. Trivially, the
identity map on C intertwines ϕ with itself. From the GNS construction, the identity map
on C lifts to the identity map on Hϕ which intertwines πϕ with itself.
Now suppose we have additional knowledge of a ∗-automorphism α : A →A for which
ϕ(α(a)) = ϕ(a) for all a ∈A. If we take into account both α and 1C, we can construct
a non-trivial unitary U ∈B(Hϕ) for which U ◦πϕ(a) = πϕ(α(a)) ◦U for all a ∈A. As
the GNS construction provides a localization of the C∗-algebra framework of quantum me-
chanics to the Hilbert space framework, the dependence of the intertwiner for the localized
system on automorphisms of A suggest that we account for this information when discussing
intertwiners for states.
As the KSGNS construction is a generalization of the GNS construction, we find it appro-
priate to take automorphism information into account for intertwiners of strict positive C∗-
correspondences. To this end, we build a category for a fixed C∗-algebra A called PosCor(A)
whose objects are strict positive C∗-correspondences from A to any other C∗-algebra and
whose morphisms are intertwiners which account for automorphisms of A. As we take ob-
jects to be correspondences from A to any other C∗-algebra, the composition rule of the
intertwiners becomes a bit tricky. Therefore, we build PosCor(A) in two stages.
In the first stage, we define a category for fixed C∗-algebras A and B called PosCor(A, B)
where objects are given by strict positive C∗-correspondences from A to B and morphisms are
given by intertwiners which take into account automorphisms of A. Inside PosCor(A, B) is a
full subcategory, called Cor(A, B), were objects are given by C∗-correspondences from A to
B. Using PosCor(A, B), we can view the KSGNS construction as an idempotent endofunctor
on PosCor(A, B) (Theorem 3.5, Proposition 3.6).
For fixed C∗-algebras A, B, and C, we can use the interior tensor product to construct
functors from PosCor(A, B) to PosCor(A, C) which commute with the KSGNS endofunctor
(Theorem 3.10). Using these functors and the morphisms of PosCor(A, B), we can ”com-
bine” together all the categories PosCor(A, B) for a fixed C∗-algebra A to form our desired
category PosCor(A). Using the KSGNS endofunctor on PosCor(A, B) and its properties

As we build the categories PosCor(A, B) and PosCor(A), we define topologies on the hom-

every strict positive equivariant C∗-correspondence between C∗-dynamical systems unitarily
uniquely dilates under the KSGNS construction to an equivariant C∗-correspondence of the
C∗-dynamical systems (Theorem 5.6). In the case the group for the C∗-dynamical systems is
trivial, Theorem 5.6 is simply the KSGNS construction. Therefore Theorem 5.6 can viewed
as a generalization of the KSGNS construction.
Let us outline the layout of this paper. In section 2, we provide background related to
completely positive maps, Hilbert modules, as well as the KSGNS construction. We also
provide a comment in the last subsection regarding notation for equivalence classes of simple
tensors in both the interior tensor product and KSGNS construction.
In section 3, we
build the category PosCor(A, B) and prove the KSGNS construction defines an idempotent
endofunctor on this category. We additionally construct functors from the interior tensor
product, and we verify such functors commute with the KSGNS endofunctor. We conclude
the section by endowing the hom-sets for the category PosCor(A, B) with topologies and
consider the extent for which composition, the KSGNS endofunctor, and the functors from
the interior tensor product are continuous.
In section 4, we utilize the categories PosCor(A, B) and the results of the functors on this
category to bootstrap our way to the category PosCor(A). In addition, we show the KSGNS
construction defines an idempotent endofunctor on PosCor(A). We also define topologies on
the hom-sets for PosCor(A) and consider the extent of which composition and the KSGNS
endofuctor are continuous. Finally, in section 5, we apply the framework and results to strict
positive equivariant C∗-correspondences of C∗-dynamical systems.

2.1. Completely positive maps. For a C∗-algebra A, we have for each n ∈N the ∗-algebra
of n-by-n matrices with entries in A, denote by Mn(A). Using any faithful representation
of A, the ∗-algebra Mn(A) can be equipped with a norm making it into a C∗-algebra.
Given a linear map ϕ : A →B between C∗-algebras, we obtain an induced linear map
ϕn : Mn(A) →Mn(B) for each n ∈N; in particular, we have

For any two C∗-algebras A and B, a positive map from A to B is a linear function
f : A →B such that for all a ∈A, f(a∗a) ∈B+. As every element of A+ is of the form
a∗a for some a ∈A, then we equivalently have that f is positive if f(A+) ⊂B+. While
continuity of f is not assumed, every positive map is bounded; in fact, if A and B are unital
C∗-algebras, then ||f|| = ||f(1)|| [Pau02]. Furthermore, as positive maps preserve positive
elements, it follows that every positive map preserves the ∗-operation.
For any two C∗-algebras A and B, a completely positive map from A to B is a linear
function f : A →B such that fn : Mn(A) →Mn(B) is positive for every n ∈N. Examples
of completely positive maps include ∗-algebra homomorphisms and positive maps whenever
the domain or codomain is a unital commutative C∗-algebra [Pau02]. Not every positive
map is completely positive; for example, the transpose map on M2(C) defines a positive map
which is not completely positive.
2.2. Hilbert modules. For a C∗-algebra A, an inner product A-module is a complex vector

(3) for all x, y ∈E, ⟨x, y⟩= ⟨y, x⟩∗

5.2. Strict positive equivariant C∗-correspondences of C∗-dynamical systems. The
notion of a C∗-correspondence is a generalization of a non-degenerate ∗-algebra homomor-
phism between the algebras. In a similar manner, an equivariant C∗-correspondence between
C∗-dynamical systems is viewed as a generalization of equivariant ∗-algebra maps between
C∗-dynamical systems. To understand the data we take in the definition of such a corre-
spondence, let us look at an equivariant ∗-algebra homomorphism between two C∗-dynamical
systems.
Let (A, G, α) and (B, G, β) be C∗-dynamical systems, and let π : A →B be an equivariant
∗-algebra homomorphism; that is, for all g ∈G and a ∈A, π(αg(a)) = βg(π(a)). Let E = B
viewed as a Hilbert B-module. For each g ∈G, let Ug = βg which is βg-linear as well as
adjointable with adjoint β−1

x ∈E, a ∈A,
Ug(π(a)x) = Ug(π(a))Ug(x) = π(αg(a))Ug(x)
Generalizing the maps Ug and generalizing E to a general Hilbert B-module yields the
appropriate notion of an equivariant C∗-correspondence.
Definition 5.4.
Let (A, G, α) and (B, G, β) be C∗-dynamical systems.
A strict positive equivariant C∗-
correspondence from (A, G, α) to (B, G, β) is the data ((E, ϕ), U) where (E, ϕ) is a strict
positive C∗-correspondence from A to B and U is a strongly continuous group homomorphism
U : G →B(E), and the data satisfies the following conditions:
(1) For all g ∈G, Ug is a βg-adjointable unitary.
(2) For all g ∈G and a ∈A, Ug ◦ϕ(a) = ϕ(αg(a)) ◦Ug.
In the case (E, ϕ) is a C∗-correspondence from A to B, we call ((E, ϕ), U) an equivariant
correspondence from (A, G, α) to (B, G, β).
We remark that the notion of an equivariant correspondence is not something new. In
section 2.2 of [EKQR05], the authors refer to the actions of α and β as (α, β)-compatible
actions on E, and the representation U as the coefficients for the actions α and β.

setting, we can forgo all continuity assumptions on α, β and U to still have the statement

assumes U is continuous with respect to the strong operator topology, then the statement
of the theorem is again still true with ˜U also being continuous with respect to the strong
operator topology.
For proving the most general statement, one would drop the continuity assumption on the
functor F; that is, we take a unitary valued functor F : BG →PosCor∗-alg(A). Given such
a functor, we can post compose by the KSGNS endofunctor to obtain the appropriate data
for the quadruple in a similar manner as in the proof above.
