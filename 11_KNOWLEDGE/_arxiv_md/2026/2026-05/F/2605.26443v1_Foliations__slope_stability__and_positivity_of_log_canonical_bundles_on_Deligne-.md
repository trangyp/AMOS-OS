FOLIATIONS, SLOPE STABILITY, AND POSITIVITY OF LOG CANONICAL BUNDLES
ON DELIGNE–MUMFORD STACKS
SEBASTIAN CASALAINA-MARTIN AND SHEND ZHJEQI
ABSTRACT. We generalize some results of Campana–P˘aun regarding foliations, slope stability, and
positivity of log canonical bundles on smooth projective varieties to the case of smooth proper DM
stacks admitting projective coarse moduli spaces. This paper is the third in a series aiming to gener-
alize results of Popa–Schnell and Wei–Wu on Viehweg hyperbolicity to the setting of DM stacks, and
in particular, to certain KSBA moduli spaces.
INTRODUCTION
In [CP19], Campana–P˘aun showed that for a smooth projective variety X with a reduced normal
crossing divisor ∆⊆X, if some positive tensor power of Ω1
X(log ∆) contains a subsheaf with big
determinant, then the first Chern class of every torsion-free quotient sheaf of every positive tensor

the setting of smooth proper Deligne–Mumford (DM) stacks over C with projective coarse moduli

a stack X is the quotient of a smooth projective variety by the action of a finite group; i.e., X =
[V/G] where V is a smooth projective variety and G is a finite group acting on V.
In this paper, we extend [CP19, Thm. 7.6, Thm. 1.2] to the more general class of DM stacks
mentioned above:
Theorem A. Let X be a smooth proper integral DM stack over C with projective coarse moduli space, and
let ∆⊆X be a reduced divisor with at worst normal crossing singularities. Suppose that some positive
tensor power of Ω1

torsion-free coherent quotient sheaf of every positive tensor power of Ω1

Theorem C. Let X be a smooth proper integral DM stack over C with projective coarse moduli space, and
let F ⊆TX be a foliation. Suppose that there exists a movable class α ∈N1(X )R such that every nonzero
quotient sheaf of F has positive α-slope. Then F is an algebraic foliation.
We prove Theorem C in Theorem 2.7. We recall that the case where X = [V/G] for a smooth
projective variety V with a finite group G acting on V was proven in [CP19, Thm. 1.1], and in
fact follows also from the case of smooth projective varieties. In the case where X is a smooth
projective variety and the class α is the class of a curve obtained as the complete intersection of
(very) ample hypersurfaces, the result is due to Bogomolov–McQuillan [BM16].
Our strategy of proof for Theorem 2.7 is to use the approach of Campana–P˘aun for [CP19,
Thm. 1.1], and can be summarized as follows. One considers the family of analytic leaves of the
foliation F, viewed as a family of analytic stacks with total space contained in X an × X an (param-
eterized by the first projection), and then one takes the Zariski closure. In general, one does not
expect to have much control over this Zariski closure, but under the hypotheses on the α-slope of
F and its quotients, together with results about α-slope and vanishing of global sections that we
establish in [CMZ26a], extending result in the literature for projective varieties, one can make an
asymptotic Riemann–Roch calculation to show that the dimension of the Zariski closure in ques-
tion is minimal (i.e., dimension of X plus the rank of the foliation). From this one obtains that the
Zariski closure of the general analytic leaf is algebraic of the same dimension, and one argues that
one obtains a rational map X 99K Z to the resolution of singularities of an appropriate parameter

Returning to the proof of Theorem A, once one has the rational map p : X 99K Z to a smooth
projective variety Z whose fibers generically induce the foliation F, in Section 4, we turn to the

by contradiction, and are simply streamlining the discussion here for brevity). The strategy is to
consider the divisor KX /Z + ∆hor + R(p), where ∆hor is the horizontal part of ∆with respect to p,
and R(p) is the ramification divisor for p (see Section 3 where the terminology is reviewed). On
the one hand, one shows from a direct computation that, given the set-up, one has the inequality
(KX /Z + ∆hor + R(p)) · α < 0, so that KX /Z + ∆hor + R(p) is not pseudo-effective. On the other
hand, inductively, considering the fibers, one can deduce that KX /Z + ∆hor + R(p) is pseudo-
effective, giving the contradiction.
The latter argument, reducing to the fibers to obtain that KX /Z + ∆hor + R(p) is pseudo-effective,
relies, in the case of smooth projective varieties, on results of Claudon–Kebekus–Taji [CKT21],

Campana–P˘aun [CP15], Fujino [Fuj17], and P˘aun–Takayama [PT18]. We generalize a special case
of these results to the case of DM stacks in Theorem 3.2 and Theorem 3.5; as the results in the
literature are made for singular varieties, unlike most of the other arguments in this paper, we
prove these results by reducing to statements on the coarse moduli spaces.

1. PRELIMINARIES
1.1. Terminology. We work over the complex numbers C.
A variety is an integral separated
scheme of finite type over C. An alteration X′ →X is a surjective projective generically finite
morphism of schemes over C. We use the definition of a Deligne–Mumford (DM) stack in [LMB00,
Def. 4.1]. Note that this differs from the definition in [Sta26] in that there is the additional hy-
pothesis in [LMB00, Def. 4.1] that the diagonal be representable, separated, and quasi-compact.
We direct the reader to [CMW18, App. B] for a discussion of the relationship among various def-
initions of DM stacks in the literature (see in particular [CMW18, Fig. 1]). We emphasize that,
with the definition of DM stack that we are using, a morphism from a scheme to a DM stack is
schematic (representable by schemes); see e.g., [CMW18, Lem. B.20 and Lem. B.12].
The general set-up in this paper will be a smooth proper (resp. separated) integral DM stack X
of finite type over C with coarse moduli space π : X →X, with the added assumption that the
algebraic space X be a projective (resp. quasi-projective) variety. We refer the reader to [CMZ26a,
CMZ26b], as well, where we discuss certain properties of such DM stacks.

Proof. We closely follow the proof by [CP19] and make necessary adjustments along the way.
While the proof is fairly lengthy, the strategy of the proof is easy to describe: One considers the
analytic family Λ of leaves of the foliation, parameterized by the points of X an where the leaves
are defined, as a family with total space contained in the product X an × X an. One then takes the
Zariski closure of Λ in X an × X an to obtain an algebraic substack W of X × X . The assumptions

the first projection W →X is also irreducible, so that the general fiber is the Zariski closure of
an analytic leaf. Finally, viewing the morphism W →X as a family of substacks of X , one uses
Chow varieties to obtain a rational map X 99K Z to a smooth projective variety with general fiber
given by leaves of the foliation, which are algebraic.
