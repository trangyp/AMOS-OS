ABSTRACT
Context. With the increasing precision of asteroseismic observations, it becomes possible to reliably measure oscillation properties of
an increasing number of stars. Interpreting these measurements requires a good theoretical understanding of their link to fundamental
stellar properties.
Aims. In this study, we focus on the phase offset in gravity(g)-mode frequencies, which is imprinted in the asymptotic eigenfrequency
pattern of mixed dipole modes observed in red–giant branch stars. We aim to unravel its physical origin and thus enable an informed
interpretation of observations.
Methods. Using stellar models, we empirically test the contribution of the g-mode offset εg (which is related to the wave reflection at
cavity boundaries and commonly considered to be the dominant phase term) and glitches to the total observable phase.
Results. We find that, additionally to εg, buoyancy glitches play an important role in the correct interpretation of the g–mode frequency
phase. We further find that glitches in the evanescent zone also contribute to the phase, and we present a formalism to quantify this
contribution. Finally, we propose a modification to the widely used formula for εg.
Conclusions. The g–mode frequency phase carries more information than previously considered. It has large analytic potential to
study not only the reflection properties of the buoyancy cavity, but also the properties of glitches in the Brunt-Väisälä frequency.
Key words. asteroseismology – stars: interiors – stars: low-mass
1. Introduction
The space-borne observation campaigns Kepler and TESS have
delivered long, high-precision timeseries of a large number of
stars in the Milky Way. Among these are several ten thou-
sand red-giant stars (e.g. Hekker et al. 2011; Mosser et al. 2012;
Yu et al. 2018; Pinsonneault et al. 2014, 2018, 2025). Thanks to
their high luminosity, the sample of red giants bright enough to
achieve a good signal-to-noise ratio in the light curves is also
large, which makes statistical studies of their properties possible.
Due to their large size and therefore cool surface temperatures,
red-giant stars have convective envelopes, which stochastically
drive solar-like oscillations: Energy from the turbulent convec-
tive motions is transferred to resonant global oscillation modes
of the stars. This stochastic excitation leads to observable os-
cillations within a Gaussian power envelope centered around a
frequency of maximum oscillation power νmax, defined by the
typical time scale of convection (Mathur et al. 2012). It scales
with a star’s surface gravity g and effective temperature Teff as
(e.g. Kjeldsen & Bedding 1995)

4. Glitches in the Brunt-Väisälä frequency
Glitches are sharp variations in the stellar structure compared
to the local wavelength of the oscillations. In RGB stars, the
spike in the Brunt-Väisälä frequency (cf. Fig. 1) representing the
chemical discontinuity at the deepest extent of the convective
envelope during the first dredge-up is the most prominent sharp
feature that can act as a glitch. During evolution, it first appears
in the evanescent zone of excited mixed modes and moves into
the buoyancy cavity as νmax decreases (cf. also the discussion by
van Lier et al. 2025).

agreement between fit and numerical calculation, however there
are still discrepancies. The agreement for the most evolved mod-
els with νmax <∼40 µHz can be improved using the Gaussian
glitch prescription ΦG (Cunha et al. 2019, see also Sect. 4.3), as
demonstrated by the ocher points in Fig. 6. We discuss these find-
ings in the following section.
7. Discussion
From the theoretical perspective on the g-mode offset, the re-
sults presented in Fig. 4 clearly show that the weak-coupling
limit Ψ →−π
4 for the evanescent-zone term is not applica-
ble on the early RGB. This is to be expected since the evanes-
cent zone is relatively narrow during this evolutionary stage
(van Rossem et al. 2024). The difference between this limit and
the numerical evaluation of Ψ given by Eq. (17) (∼0.06) is
significant compared to some of the observational uncertainties
achieved for Φ−1
2 by Mosser et al. (2018); with future advances
in observational techniques the significance is likely to increase.
It will therefore become necessary to correctly account for the
evanescent-zone contribution to the g–mode frequency phase.
Our model computations suggest that the formulation we de-
rived in Eq. (17) is promising to match observationally obtained
phases. The fact that the three prescriptions for Ψ shown in Fig. 4
converge as evolution progresses can be explained by the fact
that the evanescent zone widens (van Rossem et al. 2024) and
therefore the phase contribution by the coupling to the p-mode
cavity decreases.
The results presented in Fig. 5 suggest that the analytical
model might reproduce the morphology of the time evolution of
the g–mode frequency phase better. This is in line with the find-
ings by Pinçon et al. (2019), who compared similarly obtained
εg,ana to an ensemble of observed g-mode offsets. However, the
results shown in Fig. 6 demonstrate that this leads to a misin-
terpretation of the observed phases. While Pinçon et al. (2019)
concluded that the kink as the phase starts to drop is inherent to
the g-mode offset produced by the behavior of the wave function
at the outer boundary of the (smooth) cavity (Eq. 10), we were
able to show that this feature is more likely linked to the spike in
the Brunt-Väisälä frequency acting as a glitch. We were further
able to support this hypothesis by calculating an additional set of
stellar models where we artificially removed the spike. For these
models, the phase Φ−1
2 did not show a kink and closely followed
the respective εg,num, while εg,ana still had a similar shape to the
models including the spike (see Fig. B.1).
While the glitch is in the evanescent zone, our calculations
support that subtracting the phase term derived in Sect. 4.1 eval-
uated at νmax from the glitch-free observed phase Φ −1
2 (Eq. 53),
is suitable to isolate a g-mode offset consistent with εg,num.

8. Conclusions
By comparing theoretical contributions to the phase offset of
g-mode eigenfrequencies to the phases observed from models,
we constrained the interpretation of the g-mode offset. Firstly,
we showed that the common wide–evanescent zone expansion
Ψ →−π
4 for the evanescent-zone contribution to εg is not appli-
cable on the early RGB. However, it seems to be valid as a limit
as the evanescent zone widens with evolution. We get results
most consistent with the observed phase when using Eq. (17) to
calculate the evanescent-zone term, rather than the term origi-
nally derived by Takata (2016a), given in Eq. (14). The assump-
tion we made when deriving the modified version ˆΨ will also
affect the wave function in the evanescent zone, further studies
would be required to investigate how this affects other results de-
rived by Takata (2016a), including the coupling strength in the
strong-coupling limit.
Secondly, the frequency dependence of εg as calculated from
our models is significant on the late RGB. It becomes compara-
ble with observational uncertainties and therefore more elaborate
fitting procedures might need to take it into account.
Thirdly, the analytical model of the buoyancy cavity intro-
duced by Pinçon et al. (2019) is suitable to estimate values of
the g-mode offset within observational uncertainties on the late
RGB. However, it predicts a trend in the evolution of εg with
νmax that is not consistent with the numerical evaluation of the
full expression for the g-mode offset. The trend in the analyt-
ically estimated g-mode offset partly reproduces the effect of
the Brunt-Väisälä spike acting as a glitch in the evanescent zone
and therefore leads to a misinterpretation of the observed phases.
While Pinçon et al. (2019) suggest that the kink in Φ −1
2 at the
transition from rb being frequency-dependent to rb ≡rBCZ is in-
herent to Eq. (10), we were able to demonstrate that it is instead
produced by the phase contribution from the glitch.
Lastly, we were able to show that the Brunt-Väisälä spike
contributes significantly to the phase offset of g-mode frequen-
cies. When it acts as a glitch in the buoyancy cavity, not only
the additive phase Φcavity but also the changed pattern of the
frequency spectrum lead to a shift in the observed Φ −1
2 rela-
tive to the g-mode offset εg given by the (smooth) shape of the
buoyancy cavity. It becomes clear that in this way the g-mode
phase is an observable to investigate both, the shape of the Brunt-
Väisälä spike and the treatment of convective boundaries in stel-
lar models by the remaining discrepancies in a model-to-model
and model-to-observation comparison, respectively.
