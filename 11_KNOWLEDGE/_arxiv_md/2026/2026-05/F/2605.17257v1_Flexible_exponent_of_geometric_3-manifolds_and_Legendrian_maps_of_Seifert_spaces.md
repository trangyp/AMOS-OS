1. Introduction
Let M, N be two closed, connected, oriented manifolds of the same dimension. The
degree of a map f : M →N, denoted by deg(f), is probably one of the oldest concept in
topology.
The study of various properties of mapping degrees for various classes of manifolds
M and N, in particular when M = N, has a long history. Influenced by the work of
Thurston and Gromov [Thu22, Gro82], the topic became very active and make many

For a closed orientable manifold M, we use D(M) to denote the set of degrees of all
self-maps of M. Following Gromov, a closed orientable manifold M is called flexible if and
only if D(M) is infinite. Equivalently, M is flexible if and only if M admits a self-map of
degree greater than 1.
Definition 1.1. Suppose M is a closed connected orientable Riemannian n-manifolds.
The flexible exponent of M is defined as
α(M) := inf{α ⩾0 | ∃C s.t. | deg(f)| ⩽C Lip(f)α for all f : M →M}.
We will soon see that α(M) only depends on the homotopy type of a differentiable
manifold M and α(M) ∈[0, dim M]. Moreover, α(M) > 0 if and only if M is flexible
(see Corollary 2.6 and Corollary 2.8). The flexible exponent measures how effectively
a self-map can wrap itself. For a simply connected closed manifold M it is known by
[BGM24, Theorem A, Theorem D] that α(M) = dim M if and only if M is formal.
We study α(M) for 3-manifolds in this paper. By Thurston’s geometrization picture,
geometric 3-manifolds are building blocks of general 3-manifolds. Since α(M) connects
the geometry and topology of M, it is natural to ask about α(M) for geometric M. Our
main result Theorem 1.2 gives a complete answer to this question.

Motivated by Theorem 1.2, one may ask the following questions.
Question 1.4. Suppose M and N are closed orientable smooth manifolds.
(1) Does α(M × N) = α(M) + α(N)?
(2) If M and N have a common finite cover, does α(M) = α(N)?
(3) If M and N supports the same homogeneous geometry, does α(M) = α(N)?
(4) If there exists a map f : M →N of non-zero degree, does α(M) ⩽α(N)?
For geometric 3-manifolds, all questions above have positive answers by Theorem 1.2
and other known facts in topology, geometry, and non-zero degree maps of 3-manifolds.
The answer appears to be unknown in higher dimensions.
1.2. Legendrian maps on Seifert manifolds. It is often expected that if a homotopy
class of maps contains a covering, the best choice of a representative in this class should
be a covering.
In our study of α(M), this is true, as we will see, when M supports
H2 × E1, E3, and Sol geometries. However, when M supports the Nil geometry, our map
realizing α(M) = 8/3 is not a covering, but a composition of coverings and the so-called
“Legendrian maps”. We now give the definition of these maps, which is of independent
interest.
