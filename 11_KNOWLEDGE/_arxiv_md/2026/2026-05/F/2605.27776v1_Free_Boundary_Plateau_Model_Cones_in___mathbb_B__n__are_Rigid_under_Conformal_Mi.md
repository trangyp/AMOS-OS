ELHAM MATINPOUR
Abstract. The classical theorem of Nitsche [4] asserts that every free-boundary
minimal disk in the unit ball B3 is an equatorial plane disk. Fraser and Schoen [3]
extended this rigidity result to all dimensions and to spaces of constant sectional
curvature, showing in particular that such disks are totally geodesic. In a previous
work [5], the author established an analogue for singular Y -type surfaces: any con-
formal minimal immersion of the flat Y -cone into Bn that meets the boundary sphere
orthogonally must coincide with the flat Y -cone itself (up to orthogonal transforma-
tion).
In the present paper we push this program one step further by treating the more
intricate tetrahedral singularity. We prove that every conformal and minimal immer-
sion of the flat T-cone into the unit ball Bn, subject to the free-boundary orthogo-
nality condition, is necessarily congruent to the flat T-cone. Combining this with the
smooth-disk result of Nitsche–Fraser–Schoen and the Y -cone result of [5], we obtain
a unified uniqueness theorem: any free-boundary minimal Plateau surface in Bn that
is conformal to one of the reference model domains (a disk in the plane, the Y -cone,
or the T-cone) must itself be congruent to that model.
1. Introduction
This paper continues the line of investigation initiated in [5], where the uniqueness
of the flat Y -cone was established among all free-boundary minimal Y -surfaces that are
conformal to the model Y -cone. Here we address the next natural singular model—the
tetrahedral T-cone—and thereby complete the picture for all admissible Plateau-type
singularities under the conformality assumption.
In the smooth (non-singular) setting the foundational rigidity result is due to Nitsche [4],
who proved that any free-boundary minimal disk in B3 is an equatorial flat disk. Fraser
and Schoen [3] later generalized this statement to arbitrary dimension and to am-
bient manifolds of constant curvature showing that such disks are totally geodesic
(and, under an additional parallel mean-curvature assumption, totally umbilic in a
three-dimensional constant-curvature submanifold). These theorems provide the regu-
lar building block of our program.
For Plateau surfaces the situation is substantially more delicate because of the presence
of the classical Y - and T-junction singularities. The treatment of singular cases is set-
tled by exploiting the conformal structure on each sector together with the minimality

– P is a 2-dimensional affine plane in Rn (the local model for boundary
points and free-boundary arcs),

3. Rigidity of Tetrahedral Stationary Geodesic Networks
In this section we show that a stationary geodesic network on the sphere with tetra-
hedral combinatorics must coincide with the regular tetrahedral network.
Definition 3.1. Let N be a finite union of arcs on the unit sphere Sn−1 meeting at
vertices. We say that N is a stationary geodesic network if the following conditions
hold:
(1) Each edge of N is a geodesic arc of Sn−1.
(2) At each vertex exactly three edges meet.
(3) (120◦equilibrium condition) If v is a vertex and τ1, τ2, τ3 are the unit tangent
