Finite Sentence-Interface Control for Learning Bounded-Fan-Out
Linear MCFGs under Fixed Monoid Typing
Takayuki Kuriyama
Abstract
We study positive-data learning of bounded-fan-out linear multiple context-free gram-
mars under a fixed explicit finite monoid homomorphism h. The main obstacle beyond the
context-free case is that an MCFG nonterminal derives a tuple whose components may be
placed in a surrounding sentence in different orders. We introduce sentence-interface types
as finite external control objects for such tuple occurrences. A type records the permuta-
tion of tuple components in the final sentence together with the h-values of the boundary
intervals between them. For reduced working binary linear nondeleting MCFG presentations
whose string languages satisfy (f, h)-tuple substitutability, we build a typed refinement, a
finite characteristic sample, and a canonical positive-data learner. Once the sample contains
this characteristic sample and remains contained in the target language, the learner recon-
structs the language exactly. Consequently, for fixed fan-out bound f and fixed explicit h,
the resulting class is identifiable in the limit from positive data. Moreover, the hypothesis
associated with any given finite sample is constructible in polynomial time for fixed f and
fixed h, including output size. Thus sentence-interface control is the finite mechanism that
lifts fixed-h distributional reconstruction from context-free grammars to bounded-fan-out
linear MCFGs.
Keywords. grammatical inference, multiple context-free grammars, linear multiple context-
free grammars, finite monoids, sentence-interface types, identification in the limit

Introduction
Learning grammars from positive data alone requires finite information that can eventually be
observed in every complete text for the target language. Gold’s theorem rules out broad language
classes in this setting [9], while query-learning models such as Angluin’s regular-set algorithm
obtain power from active information [3].
A standard positive-data strategy is therefore to
restrict the distributional behavior of substrings or tuples so that finitely many observed contexts
are enough to determine the whole language.
This perspective underlies the Clark–Eyraud
line for context-free grammars [4], Yoshinaka’s substitutability-based work for context-free and
mildly context-sensitive languages [16, 17, 18], and later developments for multiple context-
free grammars with Clark [5, 6].
Kanazawa’s monograph on learnable classes of categorial
grammars provides another background example in which restrictions on grammatical structure
lead to positive-data learnability through finite witnessing information, compatible with the
characteristic-sample viewpoint used here [11].
The present paper asks what finite local information is needed when the grammar formalism
is a bounded-fan-out linear MCFG. MCFGs are a standard mildly context-sensitive formalism
[14, 15, 13, 10]; their nonterminals derive tuples rather than single strings. Thus the external
environment of a nonterminal occurrence is not just a two-sided context. The tuple components
may be scattered through the final sentence, and their left-to-right placement can change from
one occurrence to another. Componentwise information such as h(w1), . . . , h(wd) is therefore
not enough to describe the role of a d-tuple occurrence in a sentence.
Our finite control object is the sentence-interface type. For an arity-d sentence context, it
records the permutation in which the d holes appear and the h-values of the d + 1 boundary

The semantic distributional assumption is (f, h)-tuple substitutability. It compares tuples
only when they have the same componentwise h-type; under that equality, sharing one accepting
sentence context forces equality of all accepting sentence contexts. Yoshinaka’s multidimensional
substitutability is stronger as a distributional implication because it does not require equality of
componentwise h-types. The fixed-h setting therefore gives a different reconstruction problem:
the learner is not trying to infer the finite monoid, but to use the prescribed finite monoid as
external control for typed reconstruction.
Technically, the paper proceeds from finite interface control to exact reconstruction. Given
a reduced working binary linear nondeleting MCFG presentation of fan-out at most f, we
refine each nonterminal by both its componentwise output h-type and its sentence-interface
type. The transport maps induced by binary rule templates determine the interface of each
child occurrence from the parent interface and the sibling output type.
After trimming to
productive and reachable typed copies, we extract a finite characteristic sample exposing every
surviving typed state and every realized typed rule. The canonical learner rebuilds rules from
observed typed tuples and concrete binary witnesses.
Soundness is supplied by (f, h)-tuple
substitutability; completeness is supplied by the exposed typed refinement.
This paper is related to the author’s fixed-h reconstruction for CFGs [1, 2], but the present
contribution is not merely a notational lift. In the CFG case an occurrence has an ordinary two-
sided context. In the MCFG case the external state must additionally control the sentence-level
placement of tuple components. Sentence-interface types are introduced to isolate precisely this
new finite object.
The theorem should be read as a fixed-parameter positive-data reconstruction result. The
fan-out bound f, the explicit finite homomorphism h: Σ∗→M, and the working binary linear
nondeleting presentation discipline are fixed in advance. Within this regime, the learner recon-
structs the target language once a finite characteristic sample has appeared and constructs the
current hypothesis in polynomial time from the finite sample at hand. The point is therefore
not to infer the finite monoid or to normalize arbitrary MCFG presentations, but to show that
sentence-interface types provide enough finite external control for exact reconstruction under
the fixed-h discipline.
The contributions are as follows.
(i) We introduce sentence-interface types Sd × Md+1 as finite external control states for tuple
occurrences in bounded-fan-out linear MCFGs under fixed monoid typing.
(ii) We define rule-template transport maps that propagate these types from parent occur-
rences to child occurrences using the sibling componentwise h-type.
(iii) We construct the typed refinement and prove the associated typing invariants and language
equivalence.
(iv) We extract a finite characteristic sample exposing typed states and realized typed rules,
and define a canonical positive-data learner.
(v) We prove exact reconstruction, identification in the limit, and polynomial-time construc-
tion of the hypothesis from a given finite sample for fixed f and fixed explicit h.

Main Results
This section records the theorem package before the technical development. The definitions
needed for the fully formal statements are introduced in the next sections, but the hypotheses
and the strength of the conclusions are already fixed here.
Theorem 2.1 (Finite sentence-interface control, informal). Fix a fan-out bound f and an ex-
plicit finite monoid homomorphism h: Σ∗→M. For every reduced working binary linear non-
deleting MCFG G of fan-out at most f, each successful tuple occurrence admits a finite type
consisting of its componentwise output h-type and its sentence-interface type. These types are
propagated through binary rules by finite transport maps depending only on the rule template, the
parent interface, and the sibling componentwise h-type. The resulting trimmed typed refinement
is language-equivalent to G.
The formal form of this statement is Proposition 7.11 after the transport maps have been
defined. It is the structural part of the paper: it explains why a finite typed reconstruction is
possible at all.

Related Work and Positioning
The present paper belongs to the positive-data branch of grammatical inference. Gold’s theorem
shows that broad language classes cannot be identified from positive data alone [9], while An-
gluin’s regular-set algorithm illustrates the additional power obtained from active queries [3]. In
the positive-data setting, a common way to recover learnability is to impose a distributional or

structural restriction that yields finite tell-tales or characteristic samples; see also de la Higuera’s
general treatment of this viewpoint [7, 8].
For context-free grammars, the distributional-learning line of Clark and Eyraud [4] and
Yoshinaka’s substitutability-based work [16] show how restrictions on contexts can support
positive-data reconstruction. Yoshinaka later developed multidimensional substitutability for
mildly context-sensitive formalisms [17, 18], and Clark–Yoshinaka studied related learning prob-
lems for multiple context-free grammars [5, 6]. The comparison with these works is a comparison
of both distributional assumptions and reconstruction mechanisms. Yoshinaka’s untyped mul-
tidimensional implication compares any two tuples sharing an accepting context. The present
(f, h)-tuple substitutability condition compares tuples only after equality of their component-
wise h-types has been fixed, and the learner uses the prescribed finite monoid as external control
rather than attempting to infer it.
The main new object introduced here is the sentence-interface type. In the CFG case, the
exterior of an occurrence is an ordinary two-sided context; under a fixed homomorphism h, its
finite record is essentially the pair of boundary values (h(u), h(v)). In an MCFG derivation,
however, a nonterminal derives a tuple, and the tuple components may appear in the final
sentence in different left-to-right orders.
The sentence-interface type records this placement
permutation together with the boundary h-values. This finite placement record is not present
in the standard untyped substitutability framework and is the additional control needed for the
typed refinement and rule-transport construction below.
Kanazawa’s monograph on learnable classes of categorial grammars [11] is relevant as another
example in which restrictions on grammatical structure lead to positive-data learnability through
finite witnessing information. The present paper follows the same broad tell-tale/characteristic-
sample pattern, but applies it to fixed-h interface-controlled reconstruction for bounded-fan-out
linear MCFGs.
Multiple context-free grammars and their equivalents are standard mildly context-sensitive
formalisms [14, 15, 13, 10].
The theorem below is not a normal-form theorem for arbitrary
MCFGs. It is a reconstruction theorem for languages having a reduced working binary linear
nondeleting presentation and satisfying the language-level (f, h)-tuple substitutability condition.

Learning from positive data
Definition 4.1 (Text and learner). A text for L ⊆Σ∗is an infinite sequence (w1, w2, . . . )

4.3
The grammar formalism
Definition 4.7 (Working binary linear nondeleting MCFG). A working binary linear nondelet-
ing multiple context-free grammar is a tuple G = (V, Σ, P, S, µ), where V is a finite nonterminal
set, Σ a finite terminal alphabet, S ∈V the start symbol with µ(S) = 1, and µ: V →N>0 the
fan-out map. Every rule in P has one of the following forms: (1) a start rule S →A with
A ̸= S and µ(A) = 1; (2) a terminal rule A →(a) with A ̸= S, a ∈Σ, and µ(A) = 1; (3)
a binary rule ρ: A →(α1, . . . , αe)(B, C), where A ̸= S, e = µ(A), µ(B) = dB, µ(C) = dC,
each αi is a word over Σ ∪{x1, . . . , xdB, y1, . . . , ydC}, and every variable x1, . . . , xdB, y1, . . . , ydC
occurs exactly once in the whole tuple (α1, . . . , αe). In addition, the presentation is assumed to
be start-separated: no terminal rule or binary rule has left-hand side S, and S does not occur
as a child nonterminal on the right-hand side of any binary rule. Equivalently, the only rules
with left-hand side S are start rules S →A, and the start symbol is never used as a non-start
child.
Remark 4.8 (Start-separated convention). The start-separated convention makes the external
start step explicit: every successful derivation begins with one rule S →A. In the typed refine-
ment this step is represented by eS →Aτst
(p), where τst = (id1; 1M, 1M). If S were also allowed
as a child of non-start rules, typed copies of S with interfaces other than τst would have to be

introducing a fresh start nonterminal S′ with the single rule S′ →S0, where S0 is the original

The semantics of a binary rule ρ is simultaneous substitution: if ⃗u ∈LB(G) and ⃗v ∈

xi and vj for yj in the template tuple (α1, . . . , αe).

Remark 4.10 (Scope of the working-form assumption). The working form is an assumption

5
Why Sentence Interfaces Are Needed
5.1
From two-sided contexts to tuple placement
For CFG occurrences, a fixed finite monoid type can record the two exterior pieces of a context:
if a nonterminal derives a string w inside uwv, the boundary data (h(u), h(v)) are the natural
finite record. For an MCFG occurrence of arity d, the derived object is a tuple (w1, . . . , wd). A
surrounding sentence context has d named holes, and those holes can occur in any left-to-right
order. Thus the external state of a tuple occurrence must remember not only the monoid values
of the boundary intervals, but also the placement order of the tuple components in the final
sentence.
5.2
Why componentwise type is not enough
Componentwise h-type records (h(w1), . . . , h(wd)), but it does not record whether w1 appears
before w2, after w2, or separated by other material in a sentence. This distinction is invisible
in the tuple itself and in its componentwise type, yet it matters for typed rule incidence: a
binary rule can route the same child tuple into different positions of the parent sentence. The
permutation component of a sentence-interface type is introduced exactly to keep this finite
incidence information available for the typed refinement. Section 6.1 gives the formal definition,
and Example 6.3 gives the minimal fan-out-two pattern.
Remark 5.1 (Role of the permutation component). The permutation component is not intro-
duced to classify concrete contexts up to equality. It is used to make typed rule incidence finite
and stable: for a fixed linear nondeleting binary rule, the parent sentence-interface and the sib-
ling output type determine the child sentence-interface. This is what allows the typed refinement
to route each child occurrence through a finite state space.
6
Sentence-Interface Types and Transport
The purpose of this section is to construct the finite state space that will later drive the typed
refinement. A state is not merely a componentwise h-type of a tuple. It must also remember
how the tuple is placed in a surrounding sentence. Sentence-interface types provide a finite
external state sufficient for the typed-refinement and reconstruction argument developed here,
and the transport maps below show how binary MCFG templates propagate such states from
parent occurrences to child occurrences.
Throughout this section, let G = (V, Σ, P, S, µ) be a reduced working binary linear nondelet-
ing MCFG and let h: Σ∗→M be an explicit finite monoid homomorphism.

assigned to the same cut position, a tie-order is a total order on the corresponding slot labels at
that cut. The occurrence data include these tie-orders. Thus two tuple occurrences are identical
only when they have the same positive-length intervals, the same cut positions for empty slots, the
same named slot labels, and the same tie-orders at every cut. This convention is used unchanged
for zero-length variable intervals in binary witnesses.

11
Discussion and Scope
The result proved here is a fixed-parameter reconstruction theorem. The fan-out bound f, the
explicit finite homomorphism h: Σ∗→M, and the working binary linear nondeleting presen-
tation discipline are part of the setting; they are not inferred from positive data. The theorem
34


## Page 35


is not a normal-form theorem for arbitrary MCFG presentations. It applies to languages ad-
mitting a reduced working presentation of the specified kind and satisfying the language-level
(f, h)-tuple substitutability condition.
The typed refinement and the characteristic sample are constructed from a chosen witnessing
presentation. This presentation dependence is harmless for identification in the limit, because
the characteristic sample is only an existence witness for convergence and is not supplied to
the learner. The polynomial result of Section 10 likewise has a specific scope: for fixed f and
fixed explicit h, it constructs ˆG(K) from a given finite sample K in polynomial time, including
output size. It is not a polynomial time-and-data theorem; such a statement would require
uniform polynomial bounds on characteristic samples, as indicated by Proposition 10.7.
The comparison with Yoshinaka’s multidimensional substitutability and the Clark–Yoshinaka
MCFG line is a comparison of assumptions and reconstruction mechanisms, not a simulation
or subsumption claim. Yoshinaka’s condition is an untyped implication, whereas the present
theorem compares tuples only after their componentwise h-types agree and uses the fixed monoid
together with sentence-interface transport as finite external control. Natural extensions left open
here include inferring a suitable finite congruence instead of fixing h, treating copied or deleted
variables beyond the linear nondeleting discipline, and identifying subclasses with polynomial-
size characteristic samples.
12
Conclusion
This paper introduced sentence-interface types as finite external control objects for tuple occur-
rences in bounded-fan-out linear MCFGs under a fixed explicit finite monoid homomorphism.
The point beyond the CFG case is that componentwise h-type alone does not record how tuple
components are placed in the surrounding sentence. By recording both the placement permu-
tation and the boundary h-values, sentence-interface types make interface transport through
binary rule templates finite and computable. Using this control, we constructed a typed re-
finement, a finite characteristic sample, and a canonical positive-data learner. For every target
satisfying (f, h)-tuple substitutability, the learner reconstructs the language exactly once the
sample contains the characteristic sample and remains inside the target language. Hence Cmcf
f,h
is identifiable in the limit from positive data for fixed f and fixed explicit h, and ˆG(K) is
constructible in polynomial time from each finite sample K. Thus sentence-interface control
isolates the finite mechanism by which fixed-h distributional reconstruction extends from CFGs
to bounded-fan-out linear MCFGs.
