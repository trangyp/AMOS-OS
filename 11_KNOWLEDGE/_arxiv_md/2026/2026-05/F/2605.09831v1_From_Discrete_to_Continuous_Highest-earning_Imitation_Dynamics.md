From Discrete to Continuous Highest-earning
Imitation Dynamics
Azadeh Aghaeeyan and Pouria Ramazi
Abstract—Decision-making by imitating the highest earners
has been observed in experimental studies. In two-strategy
decision-making problems, this behavior may result in perpetual
fluctuations in the population proportions of the two strategies.
How these fluctuations evolve for large population sizes remains
unclear. This paper addresses this question for a heterogeneous
population of players imitating the highest earners. We show that
the family of Markov chains describing the discrete population
dynamics forms a generalized stochastic approximation process
for a good upper semicontinuous differential inclusion—the mean
dynamics. Furthermore, we prove that the mean dynamics always
equilibrate. Then, by using results from stochastic approximation
theory, we show that the amplitudes of fluctuations in the
population proportions of the two strategies diminish to zero with
probability one, as the population size approaches infinity. Our
results suggest that in a well-mixed, large population, imitating
the highest earners is unlikely to generate large-scale, perpetual
fluctuations.
Index Terms—decision-making dynamics, imitation, evolution-
ary game theory, differential inclusion.
I. INTRODUCTION
Individuals regularly face decisions, such as whether to
receive a flu shot or purchase a new product. In these contexts,
it is often assumed or observed that individuals chiefly behave
as either best-responders or imitators [2]–[5]. Best-responders
choose the option that maximizes their immediate benefit,
while imitators pay attention to others’ decisions and how
satisfied they are with those decisions [6], [7]. In the long
term, individuals may either settle on a satisfactory decision,
i.e., reach an equilibrium, or repeatedly switch their choices.
A finite population of best-responders who benefit from
agreeing with the majority equilibrates [8], whereas a finite
population of best-responders benefiting from disagreeing
with the majority either reaches an equilibrium or fluctuates
between two adjacent states [9], [10]. A mixed population
of these two types may either equilibrate or fluctuate [11].
The same holds true for a population of individuals imitating
the highest earners [12]. Obtaining the conditions for the
existence of fluctuations and characterizing them appear to
be challenging.

namics can be considered an approximation that simplifies

“imitate-the-best” rule used in evolutionary game theory [31]
and can be interpreted as a simplified form of success- or
payoff-biased social learning, according to which agents use
others’ success to guide their actions when they lack sufficient
information about their own payoff functions. Examples of
such learning have been reported in both humans [32] and
other animals [33].
We start off by showing that the family of Markov chains
corresponding to the discrete imitation population dynamics
is a generalized stochastic approximation process (GSAP)
for a good upper semicontinuous differential inclusion–the
associated mean dynamics. We obtain the equilibrium points
of the mean dynamics and find that the mean dynamics always
equilibrate. Building on the results of Roth and Sandholm [28],
we show that the amplitude of the fluctuations in population
proportions reported in [12] converges to zero with probability
one.
Our contribution is hence threefold: First, we derive the
mean dynamics associated with the discrete imitation popula-
tion dynamics, where individuals imitate the highest earners.
The mean dynamics take the form of differential inclusions
rather than differential equations. Second, we show that the
Markov chain associated with the population dynamics, in-
dexed by population size, is a GSAP for the mean dynamics–
Lemma 1. Building on the results in [28], we show that
the mean dynamics both in finite horizon–Proposition 2–
and in the long term approximate the evolution of the dis-
crete imitation population dynamics when the population size
approaches infinity–Theorem 2 and Corollary 1. Third, we
find the equilibria and analyze the convergence of the mean
dynamics. This is achieved by leveraging the property of the

Compared with [30], we use a similar analytical framework
rooted in [28]. However, important differences arise because of
the imitation update rule: the abstract state no longer directly
coincides with the proportion of A-players in the population,
and that trajectories starting from boundary points require sep-
arate analysis. To make the boundary analysis tractable, on the
boundary and on the set where the maximum A-utility equals
the maximum B-utility, we formulate the differential inclusion
describing the continuous-time dynamics as the convex hull of
two vector fields. This construction is not a direct corollary of
[28]; it requires model-specific analysis and is not provided in
[12]. In particular, compared with [12], we extend the result
to large population sizes by developing the mean dynamics.
In agreement with [12], we find that the equilibria are either
extreme points or points at which both strategies yield the
same payoff. By conducting a global convergence analysis for
the corresponding continuous-time dynamics, we further show
that the fluctuations reported in [12] are unlikely to persist for
large population sizes. A preliminary version of this paper
studied two special cases [1]: a population of imitators with
antidiagonal anti-coordination payoff matrices and a popula-
tion of imitators with diagonal coordination payoff matrices. In
that version, the analysis also relied on a restrictive assumption
on the population proportions and payoff-matrix entries, under
which the preferred strategy depended only on the proportion
of A-players. This substantially simplified the analysis.

only finitely many intersections on the unit interval, strategy

the highest-earning strategy at x = ρ.

VII. CONCLUDING REMARKS
We studied the behavior of a heterogeneous population of
individuals imitating the highest earners when the population
size approaches infinity. Leveraging the available results in the
stochastic approximation theory and through formulating and
analyzing the asymptotic behavior of the mean dynamics, it
was shown that the amplitudes of the reported perpetual fluc-
tuations in the population proportions of A-players converge to
zero with probability one. Whether the perpetual fluctuations
also diminish in games with more than two available strategies
remains a subject for future investigation.
