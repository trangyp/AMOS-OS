FINITE GROUPS WITH HIGH COMMUTING
PROBABILITY FOR SYLOW SUBGROUPS
ELOISA DETOMI, D´EBORA SENISE, AND PAVEL SHUMYATSKY
Abstract
Given two subsets X, Y of a finite group G, we write Pr(X, Y ) for the
probability that random elements x ∈X and y ∈Y commute. If X, Y are
subgroups, we denote by Pr∗(X, Y ) the maximum real number ϵ with the
property that for every pair of distinct primes p ∈π(X) and q ∈π(Y ) there
is a Sylow p-subgroup P of X and a Sylow q-subgroup Q of Y such that
Pr(P, Q) ≥ϵ.
In this paper we handle, among other things, finite groups G with high
probabilities Pr∗(T, G), where T is either a term of the lower central series
of G or the generalized Fitting subgroup F ∗
i (G). Our main results show
that the structure of such groups is similar, in some precise sense, to that
of nilpotent groups.
1. Introduction
Given two subsets X, Y of a finite group G, we write Pr(X, Y ) for
the probability that random elements x ∈X and y ∈Y commute. The
number Pr(G, G) is called the commuting probability of G. There is an
extensive recent literature dedicated to this subject. It is well-known
that Pr(G, G) ≤5/8 for any nonabelian group G. Another important
result is the theorem of P. M. Neumann [10], which states that if G is
a finite group and ϵ is a positive number such that Pr(G, G) ≥ϵ, then
G has a normal subgroup R such that both the index |G : R| and the
order of the commutator subgroup [R, R] are ϵ-bounded. Throughout
the article we use the expression “(a, b, . . .)-bounded” to mean that a
quantity is bounded from above by a number depending only on the
parameters a, b, . . .. A number of further results on the commuting
probability in finite groups can be found in [2, 6, 4, 7, 8, 9].
Given a finite group G and a prime p ∈π(G), we write Sylp(G) for
the set of Sylow p-subgroups of G. If X, Y are subgroups of G, we
denote by Pr∗(X, Y ) the maximum real number ϵ with the property
that for every pair of distinct primes p ∈π(X) and q ∈π(Y ) there is
P ∈Sylp(X) and Q ∈Sylq(Y ) such that Pr(P, Q) ≥ϵ.

It is easy to see that G is nilpotent if and only if Pr∗(G, G) = 1,
that is, if and only if coprime Sylow subgroups of G commute. The
paper [4] handles finite groups G in which the condition holds with
high probability.
It is shown that if Pr∗(G, G) ≥ϵ > 0, then the
index |G : F2(G)| is ϵ-bounded. Moreover, if for any p ∈π(G) there is
P ∈Sylp(G) and a Hall p′-subgroup H ≤G such that Pr(P, H) ≥ϵ,
then |G : F(G)| is ϵ-bounded. Here and throughout F(G) stands for
the Fitting subgroup and Fi(G) for the ith term of the upper Fitting
series of G.
Let γ∞(G) be the intersection of the lower central series of a group
G. The present paper grew out of the observation that a finite group
G is nilpotent if and only if Pr∗(γ∞(G), G) = 1. Here we explore finite
groups with high commuting probability Pr∗(γ∞(G), G) or Pr(P, G),
where P runs over the set of Sylow subgroups of γ∞(G). Recall that
the exponent of a finite group G is the minimum number e such that
xe = 1 for all x ∈G.
Theorem 1.1. Let ϵ > 0. Let G be a finite group and T = γ∞(G).
Assume that Pr∗(T, G) ≥ϵ. Then
(1) The index |G : F2(G)| is ϵ-bounded;
(2) The exponent of G/F(G) is ϵ-bounded.
Theorem 1.2. Let ϵ > 0. Let G be a finite soluble group and T =
γ∞(G). Suppose that for any prime p ∈π(G) there is P ∈Sylp(G)
and a Hall p′-subgroup H of T such that Pr(H, P) ≥ϵ. Then the index
|G : F(G)| is ϵ-bounded.
Somewhat surprisingly, it is not true that if for each p ∈π(T) there
is P ∈Sylp(T) and a Hall p′-subgroup H of G such that Pr(P, H) ≥ϵ,
then |G : F(G)| is ϵ-bounded (see Example 3.3). On the other hand,
it will be shown that under this hypothesis the exponent of G/F(G) is
ϵ-bounded.
The following result was obtained in [6]: Suppose that Pr(P, G) ≥ϵ
whenever P is a Sylow subgroup of G. Then G has a normal subgroup
R such that both the index |G : R| and the order of the commutator
subgroup [R, R] are ϵ-bounded. Let k ≥1, and let γk(G) be the kth
term of the lower central series of a group G. Here we prove
Theorem 1.3. Let G be a finite group and T = γk(G) for some k ≥1.
Suppose that Pr(P, G) ≥ϵ > 0 whenever P is a Sylow subgroup of T.
Then G has a nilpotent normal subgroup R of nilpotency class at most
k + 1 such that both the exponent of G/R and the order of γk+1(R) are
ϵ-bounded.

2. Preliminaries
All groups considered in this paper are finite and this will be tacitly
assumed throughout. We slightly abuse the standard terminology and
say that a group G has trivial Sylow p-subgroup whenever p does not
divide |G|.
Let a group A act by automorphisms on a group G. We say for short
that the group A acts on a group G coprimely if the orders of A and G
are coprime, that is, (|A|, |G|) = 1. The following lemma records some
standard results on coprime action. In the sequel the lemma will often
be used without explicit references.
