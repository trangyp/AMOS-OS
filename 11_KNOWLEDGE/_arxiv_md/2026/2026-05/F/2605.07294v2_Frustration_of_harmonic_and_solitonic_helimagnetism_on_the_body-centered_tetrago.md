The triangular lattice antiferromagnet (TLAF) with nearest-neighbor exchange interaction is a
model platform in the field of frustrated magnetism. Here, anharmonic (‘up-up-down’) and harmonic
(‘120 degree’) magnetic states compete, because the fundamental helimagnetic wave and its higher
harmonic are degenerate in energy. We show that a body-centered tetragonal lattice (BCTL) can
realize a similar frustration of harmonic and anharmonic helimagnetic states, and that the tetragonal
magnetic Weyl semimetal GdAlSi realizes this scenario.
In an applied magnetic field, resonant
elastic X-ray scattering reveals a competition of harmonic cycloidal and solitonic double-Q states,
well consistent with mean-field calculations.
Our work provides a new paradigm for frustration
physics in BCTL materials.
Introduction – Real-world materials with crystal lat-
tices that harbor geometric frustration of magnetic in-
teractions have attracted significant attention [1–8]. In
frustrated systems, competing interactions or lattice ge-
ometry prevent the simultaneous minimization of all local
exchange energies, leading to degeneracy and enhanced
spin fluctuations.
As frustration provides a versatile mechanism for gen-
erating unconventional ground states [2–4], extending its
concepts beyond canonical lattice geometries is impor-
tant.
So far, studies of frustration-induced competing
magnetic orders have been centered primarily on lattices
with triangle plaquettes, such as the triangular, kagome,
and pyrochlore lattices [9–11]. In the next paragraphs, we
first revisit the essential frustration physics of the trian-
gular lattice antiferromagnet (TLAF) before demonstrat-
ing analogous frustrated behavior on the body-centered
tetragonal lattice (BCTL), which is the focus of this Let-
ter.
On the TLAF, nearest-neighbor antiferromagnetic
interactions cannot be simultaneously satisfied by a
collinear configuration of magnetic moments [Fig. 1(a)].
As a consequence, frustration gives rise to a variety
of competing ordered states, including noncollinear and
noncoplanar spin textures, as well as superpositions of
multiple magnetic waves: multi-Q states. The ground
state is a three-sublattice structure in real space, corre-
sponding to the ordering vector Q = (1/3, 1/3, 0), which
coincides with the K point in reciprocal space. At K and
symmetry equivalent points, the Fourier-transformed ex-
change interaction Jk is maximized, see Fig. 1(b). Impor-
tantly, the TLAF with nearest-neighbor interactions sat-
isfies JQ = J2Q, which underlies its rich magnetic phase
diagram.
Under an applied magnetic field, the degeneracy of the
TLAF leads to multiple competing states, including the
umbrella (cone) and Y-states shown in Fig. 1(c,d). The
former (latter) corresponds to adding a uniform (nonuni-
form) magnetization component to the 120◦noncollinear
ground state. With increasing field, the Y-state contin-
uously evolves into an up-up-down configuration, giving
rise to the characteristic 1/3-magnetization plateau of
TLAF magnets [12–14]. Importantly, these states differ
in their harmonic content: the umbrella state is described
by a single-Q (harmonic) modulation plus a net magne-
tization. Meanwhile, the Y- and up-up-down states re-
quire additional higher harmonic modulations, including
components at twice the primary ordering vector of the
helimagnetic wave (2Q). The formation of anharmonic,
or solitonic, states is enabled by the condition JQ = J2Q
for the exchange interaction in reciprocal space, which
allows spin modulations at both Q and 2Q to coexist
without additional energy cost.
In this Letter, we generalize the concept of competing
harmonic and anharmonic magnetic orders, induced by
frustration, to the BCTL by combined theoretical and
experimental evidence. Within an appropriate range of
exchange parameters, the condition JQ ∼J2Q is real-

Solitonic state and exchange interactions – We use
a BCTL model with intralayer nearest (J1) and next-
nearest-neighbor (J2), and interlayer (Jc) exchange in-
teractions, as shown in Fig. 1(e). When only ordering
vectors in the basal plane are considered, we may project
all the ions onto a single plane. As shown in Fig. 1(f), the
Fourier-transformed exchange interaction Jk has maxima
at Q = (2/3, 2/3, 0) for reasonable model parameters
J1 = −1.0, Jc = 1.5, J2 = −0.25. This Jk naturally sat-
isfies the condition JQ = J2Q, similar to the case of a
TLAF. In a finite magnetic field, two possible distortions
of helimagnetic order are conical and solitonic, as shown
in Fig. 1(g,h). Both have a finite net magnetization, but
the latter is realized by adding higher harmonic content
to the helimagnetic wave. The energies of these two dis-
torted helimagnetic states, Econe and Esol, in the BCTL
model are described as

Note that a solitonic state, termed ‘chiral soliton lat-
tice’, has been reported in monoaxial chiral helimagnets,
where the ordering vector Q is fixed along a single direc-
tion. In this chiral material platform, the spin rotation
plane is fixed by the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction
and a magnetic field applied within the plane of spin ro-
tation results in the formation of a solitonic state. The
present geometry is different: Q can point along multiple
directions in the basal plane, the magnetic field is also
applied within the plane, and naively the conical state
should be favored [18–21]. Nevertheless, the conical and
solitonic states become degenerate, when the frustration
condition JQ = J2Q is relevant in the BCTL.

Figure 2(a) shows
GdAlSi’s noncentrosymmetric crystal structure, in which
the polar axis is oriented along the c-axis. In GdAlSi’s
electronic structure, topological Weyl fermions emerge
along the directions equivalent to [110] in the kc = 0
plane, as schematically illustrated in Fig. 2(b) [22–24].
The magnetic Gd moments begin to order at 32 K, below
which a cycloidal helimagnetic order is realized in zero
magnetic field, shown in Fig. 2(c).
The zero-field magnetic cycloid in GdAlSi is under-
stood in two steps. First, the intralayer (J1) and inter-
layer (Jc) nearest-neighbor interactions compete on the
Gd sublattice [Fig. 2(a)]. Like the case of Fig. 1(a), the
Fourier-transform Jk is maximized at Q = (2/3, 2/3, 0)
and yields JQ = J2Q in the parameter regime appropriate
for GdAlSi, Jc = 2J1. Indeed, an almost commensurate
magnetic ordering vector, Q = (q, q, 0) with q = 0.673,
has been observed experimentally in GdAlSi [24].
Once the ordering vector Q is selected, the DM inter-
action, allowed by the polar crystal structure, becomes
important to determine the direction of the magnetic mo-
ments — or the rotation plane of the helimagnetic wave.
In polar GdAlSi, the DM vectors are perpendicular to
both the polar axis and Q, favoring the spin-rotation
plane to be cycloidal, as shown in Fig. 2(c).

Competing harmonic and solitonic states in GdAlSi –
A magnetic field applied along the [110] direction trans-
forms the ground state of GdAlSi into one of two com-
peting, distorted helimagnetic states: one of conical, and
one of solitonic character. Both these states are super-
positions of two magnetic waves termed double-Q states:
an additional, collinear modulation appears normal to
the main helimagnetic wave [17].
Figure 2(d) shows the magnetic ground state of GdAlSi
at low temperatures and high magnetic fields. This is the
conical double-Q state [24]. The cycloidal Qcyc remains
the dominant ordering vector, being oriented perpendicu-
lar to B. However, this cycloid is distorted into staggered
cones, corresponding to a secondary, commensurate mod-
ulation with an up-up-down spin configuration. Despite
this secondary component, the primary cycloid remains
nearly harmonic.
In this work, we identify a solitonic double-Q state
emerging in a magnetic field at elevated temperatures,
which has not been reported so far [24].
This state

X-ray diffraction experiments – Experimentally, the
anharmonicity of the solitonic texture manifests itself as
second-harmonic reflections in magnetic diffraction mea-
surements. We perform resonant elastic X-ray scattering
(REXS) experiments at the Gd L2 absorption edge, de-
tails of which are given in the Supplemental Material [17].
Figure 3(a,b) shows a REXS line scan parallel to the di-
rection of the applied magnetic field. The main peak vis-
ible here is the magnetic reflection (−1 −q, 3 −q, 10),
which is a magnetic satellite of the Bragg reflection
(−1, 3, 10). Right next to it, a weaker magnetic peak
is observed at (−3 + 2q, 1 + 2q, 10), and this is identified
as a second-harmonic satellite of the (−3, 1, 10) Bragg
reflection. The observation of this second-harmonic re-
flection is the main experimental result of our paper, and
an incisive proof for the field-induced solitonic texture in
GdAlSi. In the End Matter, we provide more details of
the experiments, including polarization analysis of the
scattered REXS intensity.

Modeling the phase diagram – We consider the mag-
netic phase diagram of GdAlSi with B ∥[110].
This
phase diagram prominently features the competing
double-Q states discussed above:
a conical double-Q
and a solitonic double-Q state. The phase boundaries
in Fig. 4(a) are determined by magnetization measure-
ments in pulsed magnetic fields up to 60 T [17].
The
harmonic and anharmonic double-Q states compete at
finite fields, and there is a pocket-shaped region for the
conical double-Q state at low temperature.
The magnetic phase diagram of GdAlSi is well re-
produced by calculations in mean-field theory, shown in
Fig. 4(b). The key ingredients of the model are tetrag-
onal symmetry — allowing for two symmetry-equivalent
ordering vectors in the basal plane — and JQ ≈J2Q.
Both of these ingredients are a natural consequence of
Q ≈(2/3, 2/3, 0) on the BCTL. The details of the re-
sulting phase diagram, with its subtle balance of har-
monic and solitonic phases in a magnetic field, are also
affected by anisotropic interactions: the Dzyaloshinskii-
Moriya interaction (DMI) and the anisotropic exchange
interaction [17]. At the highest temperature, a single-Q
harmonic conical state is favored by DMI (phase IV). As
compared to Ref. [24], the present model incorporates
the effect of J2Q and consequently realizes solitonic dis-
tortions at finite temperature.
We confirmed that the
solitonic state is absent in the model of Ref. [24].
Conclusions – By a combination of theory and exper-
iments we demonstrate that concepts from frustration
physics on the triangular lattice can be applied to the
BCTL, which is little explored to date [25–27].
The
presence of degenerate ordering vectors, specifically Q
and 2Q, provides a fresh perspective for complex mag-
netic orders on the square lattice [28].
In the case of
GdAlSi, this frustration condition plays an essential role
in stabilizing the anharmonic magnetic state.
The re-
lationship between harmonic and anharmonic helimag-
netic structures realized in GdAlSi, and Weyl electrons
in its band structure, remains to be clarified.
In par-
ticular, future studies should address whether and how
much Weyl electrons contribute to the Dzyaloshinskii-
Moriya and anisotropic exchange interactions in this class
of topological semimetal.

Polarization analysis in resonant X-ray diffraction for
the solitonic double-Q state – To provide a detailed mag-
netic structure model in the solitonic double-Q state, we
perform polarization analysis of the resonantly scattered
X-rays in Fig. 5(a-d). In our experiments, the incident
X-rays are linearly polarized parallel to the scattering
plane (π-polarization). The scattered X-ray beam con-
tains both components parallel (π′) and perpendicular
(σ′) to the scattering plane, which are separated using
an analyzer plate [17].
In our experimental geometry,
the intensity Iπ-π′ corresponds to the Fourier component
of magnetic moments along the [110]-direction, whereas
Iπ-σ′ probes the component within the (H-HL) plane.
For ordering vectors along the two directions equivalent
to [110] in the tetragonal structure of GdAlSi, we select
two representative reflections such that the Fourier com-
ponents along c and [1-10] are mapped onto the Iπ-σ′
channel of each reflection [17]. Figure 5(a,b) shows the
polarization analysis of the dominant ordering vector Q,
which is along the direction of the external magnetic field.
Sharp peaks in the π–π′ channel for both reflections in-
dicate a finite [110]-component of the ordered magnetic
moment, while a pronounced peak in Iπ-σ′ — observed
only for (−1 −q, 3 −q, 10) — demonstrates the pres-
ence of a c-component. In combination, these results are
characteristic of a cycloidal magnetic structure for this
Q.
Along the direction orthogonal to the external mag-
netic field, we observe an incommensurate modulation
