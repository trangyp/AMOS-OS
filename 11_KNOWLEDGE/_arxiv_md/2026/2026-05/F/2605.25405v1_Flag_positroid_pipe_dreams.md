FLAG POSITROID PIPE DREAMS
WILLIEM RIZER AND MARTHA YIP
Abstract. We introduce flag positroid pipe dreams (FPPs), whose role in the study of
complete flag positroids is analogous to the role of
L
-diagrams in the study of positroids.
We develop the combinatorics of these diagrams and highlight some of their properties. FPPs
are in bijection with intervals in the Bruhat order of the symmetric group, and the number of
elbows in an FPP is the dimension of the corresponding Richardson cell in the decomposition
of the nonnegative flag variety. We show how complete flag positroids can be built rank
by rank via FPPs, and how the
L
-diagrams of the positroid constituents of the flag can
be obtained from the FPP via a simple standardization operation. Using partial FPPs, we
give an alternative proof of a conjecture of Benedetti, Chavez, and Tamayo on the problem
of characterizing elementary positroid quotients via cyclic shifts of decorated permutations,
in the nonnegatively representable case. Our proof partially addresses a problem of Chen
et al. regarding an explicit characterization of the cyclic shift operators purely in terms of
decorated permutations. We show that the poset of nonnegatively representable elementary
positroid quotients is self-dual. The maximal chains of this poset are in bijection with FPPs.

2. Background
In this section, we provide some background on positroids, flag positroids, and their com-
binatorics. See [19], [9], [4] for more details.
2.1. Positroids and flag positroids. Let [n] = {1, . . . , n}. A matroid M = (E, B) consists
of a ground set E = [n] and a nonempty collection B of subsets of E satisfying the basis
exchange property: for any B, B′ ∈B and b ∈B\B′, there exists b′ ∈B′\B such that
B\{b} ∪{b′} ∈B.
An element in B is a basis of M and every basis of M has the same size; this is the rank
of M. The dual of the matroid M is the matroid M ∗whose bases are the complements of
the bases of M in E. Given two matroids M and M ′ on the ground set E, M is a quotient
of M ′ and we write M ≤q M ′ if every basis of M is contained in a basis of M ′, and every
basis of M ′ contains a basis of M. A matroid quotient is elementary if the matroids are of
consecutive ranks.

Two matroids (E, B) and (E′, B′) are isomorphic if there exists a bijection φ : E →E′
that preserves independent sets. A linear matroid of rank k is a matroid arising from a k ×n
matrix A over a field k, where E is the index set of the columns of A and B consists of
the k-subsets of E whose corresponding columns are linearly independent. A matroid M is
realizable or representable over a field k if it is isomorphic to a linear matroid over k.
Definition 2.1. A positroid is a matroid that is representable by a matrix whose maximal
minors are nonnegative.
Benedetti and Knauer note in [4, Example 1] that being a positroid is not preserved under
matroid isomorphism, and that the ordering of the ground set of a positroid is a part of the
definition.
Let r = (r1, . . . , rt) be an increasing sequence of integers 1 ≤r1 < · · · < rt ≤n −1. A
flag matroid of ranks r is a sequence of matroids M• = (M1, . . . , Mt) such that rank Mi = ri
and Mi ≤q Mi+1 for all i = 1, . . . , t −1. A flag matroid M• = (M1, . . . , Mt) of ranks r is
representable over k if there exists an rt ×n matrix A such that the ri ×n submatrix formed
by the first ri rows realizes Mi, for each i = 1, . . . , t. The flag minors of A are the minors
formed by the first ri rows of A, for i = 1, . . . , t.
Definition 2.2. A flag positroid is a flag matroid that is representable over R by a rt × n
matrix A whose flag minors are nonnegative.
In [4], the authors call such a flag matroid nonnegatively representable. Equivalently, this
means that the flag positroid is a point in the nonnegative flag variety Fl≥0
n , see Section 2.2.
For positroids P, Q on [n], we write P ⊴q Q if P is a nonnegatively representable quotient
of Q. That is, (P, Q) is a two-step flag positroid. If P and Q are of consecutive rank, then
(P, Q) is an elementary two-step flag positroid.
Contrast the definition of a flag positroid with that of a positroid flag matroid, which is
a flag matroid whose constituents are all positroids. By definition, every flag positroid is a
positroid flag matroid, but not every positroid flag matroid is a flag positroid, as we discuss
in the next example.
Example 2.3. The partial order of positroid quotients (Πn, ≤q) was introduced by Benedetti,
Chavez, and Tamayo [3]. For a fixed positive integer n, the elements of the poset are the
positroids on [n], and P is covered by Q in the poset if and only if P is an elementary
quotient of Q. Maximal chains in (Πn, ≤q) are complete positroid flag matroids.

Definition 3.3. Let u = u1 · · · un ∈Sn. A Rothe pipe dream of u is a tiling of an n × n
array of boxes by the six tiles
(respectively empty, horizontal pipe, vertical pipe, pivot elbow, cross, and elbow) such that:
(i) there are n pipes that begin at the top of the array and end at the right boundary
of the array,
(ii) a pivot (i, ui) is tiled by a pivot elbow,
(iii) a box that is below some pivot of u and left of some pivot of u is an empty tile,
(iv) a box (i, j) with j > ui that is below some pivot of u is a horizontal pipe, and
(v) a box (i, j) with j < ui that is above some pivot of u is a vertical pipe.
As a consequence of these conditions, u completely determines the placements of pivot
elbows, horizontal pipes, vertical pipes, and empty tiles, and each box in the Rothe diagram
of u can be tiled by a cross or an elbow.
A Rothe pipe dream is reduced if in addition to the above five conditions it also satisfies:
(vi) two pipes may not cross more than once.
Given a pipe in a reduced Rothe pipe dream, we consider it directed from the northwest to
the southeast. The exit permutation of a reduced Rothe pipe dream R is the order in which
the pipes exit the array on the right boundary. That is, if the pipe beginning in column j
exits in row i, then i is the exit row of pipe j.
If every box in the Rothe diagram of u is tiled by a cross, then the reduced Rothe pipe
dream has exit permutation u. At the other extreme, if every box in the Rothe diagram of u is
tiled by an elbow, then the exit permutation is the longest permutation w0 = n(n−1) · · · 321.

5. Elementary quotients via decorated permutations
Using flag positroid pipe dreams, we give a new proof in Theorem 5.12 of the theorem of
Chen et al. [10, Theorem 1.5] in the case of nonnegatively representable elementary quotients.
A strength of our approach is that we can explicitly characterize the “frozen” subsets A which
appear in the theorem. This partially addresses [10, Remark 3.6], which asked for “a concise
characterization - based solely on π - of the sets A”.
We conclude this article by introducing the poset (Πn, ⊴q) of nonnegatively representable
elementary positroid quotients and showing some of the properties it possesses.
5.1. Decorated permutations. The following definition of a decorated permutation is
equivalent to the standard ones in the literature (see [2, Definition 4.5] or [19, Definition
13.3] for example), where for our purposes it is convenient to extend the colouring/decorating
notation to all of [n] and not just defined on the fixed points of [n].
Definition 5.1. A decorated permutation on [n] is a bijection π : [n] →[n] together with a
colouring function c : [n] →{1, 2} satisfying
(i) c(j) = 1 if π(j) < j, and
(ii) c(j) = 2 if π(j) > j.
In other words, if we regard fixed points j such that c(j) = 2 as weak excedances of π, and
fixed points j such that c(j) = 1 as non-weak excedances of π, then the colouring function
is given by c(j) = 2 if and only if j is a weak excedance of π. If c(j) = i, we say that j is
an i-coloured position and π(j) is an i-coloured value of π. In one-line notation, we notate
the colouring function by decorating π(j) with an underline if j is a 1-coloured position, and
π(j) with an overline if j is a 2-coloured position. Let Dn denote the set of all decorated
permutations on [n].
