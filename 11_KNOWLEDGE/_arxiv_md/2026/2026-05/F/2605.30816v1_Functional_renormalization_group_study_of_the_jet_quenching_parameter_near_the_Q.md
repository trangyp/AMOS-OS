Functional renormalization group study of the jet quenching parameter near the QCD
critical end point
Yi-zhen Huang,1 Shi Yin,2 Sheng-nan Han,1, 3 Jing Wu,1, ∗Feng Li,4, † and Wei-jie Fu1
1School of Physics, Dalian University of Technology, Dalian, 116024, P.R. China

We investigate the jet quenching parameter ˆq in the QCD phase diagram within a QCD-assisted
low-energy effective theory using the functional renormalization group (fRG). Following the formal-
ism that relates ˆq to the spectral functions of the chiral order-parameter field, we compute the σ and
π meson contributions to ˆq at finite temperature and baryon chemical potential from analytically
continued mesonic two-point functions. We find that ˆq receives appreciable contributions mainly
above the chiral phase boundary and exhibits a pronounced enhancement at large baryon chemical
potential as the chiral crossover sharpens toward the critical end point (CEP), a behavior consis-
tent with the picture of partonic critical opalescence (PCO) – a pronounced enhancement of jet
transverse momentum broadening induced by the critical σ field fluctuations.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Whether the QCD phase diagram possesses a critical
end point (CEP), i.e., the end point of a first-order phase
transition line in the regime of large baryon chemical
potential, and if so, where it is located, remains one of
the most fundamental open questions in strong interac-
tion physics. At small baryon chemical potential, it is
found that the transition from the quark-gluon plasma
(QGP) to hadronic matter is a smooth crossover [1, 2],
while at large baryon chemical potential, the existence
and location of a CEP are still under active investiga-
tions [3–7], see also [8–12] for relevant reviews. A widely
adopted strategy for probing QCD criticality is through
the higher-order cumulants of conserved charges [13–17]
such as the baryon number [18–23]. In the heavy-ion col-
lision experiments, the beam energy scan program of sec-
ond phase (BES-II) at RHIC has delivered high-statistics
measurements of net-proton fluctuations [17], revealing
suggestive deviations from non-critical baselines at cer-
tain collision energies.
Specifically, recently it is pre-
dicted from theoretical calculations that there is a peak
structure in the kurtosis of baryon number fluctuations
as a function of the collision energy in the fixed-target
energy range [21, 23]. In addition, the temperature fluc-
tuations [24], spin fluctuations and correlations [25], light
nuclei yield ratios [26, 27] have also been proposed as pos-
sible complementary probes of the CEP.
The probes mentioned above are all formed in the late
stage of the collision, around the chemical and kinetic
freeze-outs, when the system has already cooled down
close to the hadronization temperature.
As a conse-
quence, they primarily reflect the state of the medium
at these freeze-out surfaces, which lie near or below the
chiral phase boundary in the QCD phase diagram. Even

these soft probes can faithfully reflect the direct infor-

at the earlier stage of the collision and sensitive to the
medium throughout its entire evolution.
Jets are the
natural candidates: originating from hard scatterings in
the initial impact, they traverse the QGP while it ex-
pands and cools, and lose energy through interactions
with the local medium.
The resulting transverse mo-
mentum broadening is characterized by the jet quench-
ing parameter ˆq [28, 29], which directly probes the lo-
cal transport properties of the medium. Experimentally,
a hint along this direction has already been seen: the
nuclear modification factor RAA for hard hadrons mea-
sured in the BES program shows an intriguing additional
suppression at certain centralities in Au+Au collisions
at √sNN = 14.5 GeV [30], an observation whose the-
oretical implications remain to be fully explored. In a
recent study, Wu et al. computed ˆq within the quark-
meson model in a framework that relates ˆq to the spec-
tral function of the chiral order parameter.
They ob-
tained this spectral function by mapping to the Ising
model and identified a pronounced enhancement of ˆq
near the CEP, which they interpreted as the partonic
critical opalescence (PCO), in analogy to the enhance-
ment of light scattering near a classical liquid-gas critical
point. The critical behavior was thus captured through
an external universality-class input:
as also shown in
their work, no such enhancement appears when the chi-
ral order-parameter spectral function is instead obtained
from a one-loop perturbative calculation.

the situation at µB = 0.

rather than from the explicit temperature dependence
of hk(T) in the final expression.

from the CEP. This means that the signal of enhanced
jet quenching is not restricted to a narrow window of
collision energies but could be observable over a broader
range of the beam energy scan.
It is worth noting that in the mean-field calculation
of Ref. [31], no enhancement of ˆq near the CEP was ob-
served without coupling the quark-meson model to the
Ising model. In contrast, the fRG approach employed in
this work systematically incorporates quantum and ther-
mal fluctuations through the Wetterich flow equation,
which naturally generates the critical behavior without
resorting to an external universality-class mapping. As
a result, the enhancement of ˆq near the CEP emerges
directly from the fRG calculation itself.
IV.
SUMMARY AND OUTLOOK
We have computed the jet quenching parameter ˆq at
finite temperature and baryon chemical potential within
a QCD-assisted PQM model. Unlike the previous mean-
field treatment in Ref. [31], which required an external
mapping to the Ising model to capture critical behavior,
the fRG approach employed here generates the enhance-
ment of ˆq near the CEP directly through the Wetterich
flow equation.
The resulting ˆq takes appreciable values mainly above
the chiral phase boundary, indicating that energetic jets
primarily interact with the deconfined QGP medium, and
both the σ- and π-meson contributions exhibit a pro-
nounced enhancement at large baryon chemical potential
as the chiral crossover sharpens toward the CEP. Notably,
this enhancement extends along the phase boundary to-
ward smaller µB, which suggests that the signal of en-
hanced jet quenching could be accessible over a broader
range of beam energies, rather than being confined to a
narrow window near the CEP.
Several extensions of this work are warranted. Most
importantly,
the
present
calculation
includes
only
mesonic contributions to ˆq from quark–meson scattering;
it is necessary for a quantitative comparison with phe-
nomenologically extracted values to incorporate quark-
gluon scattering channels [53, 54].
Moreover, connect-
ing to the nuclear modification factor RAA will require
realistic hydrodynamic modeling and a careful treat-
ment of initial-state effects, in particular the Cronin en-
hancement [55–58], whose interplay with the critical en-
hancement of ˆq at BES energies deserves a dedicated
study [59, 60].
