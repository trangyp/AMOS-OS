FIBRATIONS, THE FIRST BETTI NUMBER, AND ALMOST
NONNEGATIVE RICCI CURVATURE
HONGZHI HUANG, XIAN-TAO HUANG, JIKANG WANG, AND XINGYU ZHU
Abstract. In this paper, we prove fibration theorems for manifolds with almost non-
negative Ricci curvature and certain extra regularity assumptions. We show that a closed
n-manifold M satisfying
diam(M)2 secM ≥−κ
and
diam(M)2RicM ≥−δ,
where δ > 0 is sufficiently small depending only on n and κ, fibers over a b1(M)-torus.
This removes the upper sectional curvature bound required in the earlier result of Yam-
aguchi [Yam88]. As a corollary, we obtain a refinement of Yamaguchi’s smooth fibration
theorem ([Yam91]), showing that the fiber itself (rather than a finite cover of it) fibers
over a b1-torus.
Our results extend to manifolds satisfying a generalized Reifenberg
condition introduced in [HH24], which encompasses both a lower bound on sectional
curvature and the local rewinding Reifenberg condition. In the nonsmooth setting, a
similar result also holds for a non-collapsed RCD(−ϵ(D, r, n), n) space whose diameter
is bounded by D and which satisfies the (r, δ(n))-local rewinding Reifenberg condition.
The proofs rely on an equivariant regularity theorem for almost submetries under a lower
Ricci curvature bound. In addition, we study the stability of rank of Abelian actions
along equivariant Gromov-Hausdorff convergence in this paper.

1. Introduction
A closed manifold M is said to have almost nonnegative Ricci curvature if there exists a
small constant δ(n) > 0, depending only on the dimension n of M, such that its diameter
and the minimal eigenvalue of its Ricci tensor satisfy
diam(M)2RicM ≥−δ(n).
(1.1)
Gromov [Gro81] (see also Gallot [Gal82]) proved that if M satisfies (1.1) for a sufficiently
small δ(n), then the first Betti number b1(M) ≤n. Furthermore, Gromov conjectured that
the equality b1(M) = n implies that M is diffeomorphic to the flat n-torus, a conjecture
that was finally resolved by Colding [Col97].
A natural question arises: under the pinching condition (1.1), what specific geometric
structure is guaranteed when b1(M) < n? Given an upper bound on sectional curvature,
Yamaguchi proved the following fibration theorem.
Theorem 1.1 ([Yam88]). For any n ∈Z+ and κ > 0, there exists δ(n, κ) > 0 such that
the following holds. Let M be an n-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold satisfying
(1.1) and
diam(M)2 secM ≤κ.
Then M fibers over a b1(M)-dimensional torus.
Yamaguchi ([Yam88]) further conjectured that the conclusion remains true even without
the upper bound on sectional curvature.
However, Anderson ([And92]) constructed a
counterexample as follows: for n ≥4, there exists a sequence of closed n-manifolds {Mi}
such that |RicMi| →0, b1(Mi) = k (where k ≤n −1), and Mi converges in the Gromov–
Hausdorff sense to a flat torus T k, but no cover of Mi fibers over S1.
When the pinching condition (1.1) is strengthened to the case of almost nonnegative
sectional curvature, Yamaguchi established the following result.
Theorem 1.2 ([Yam91]). There exists a constant δ(n) > 0, depending only on n, such
that any compact n-manifold M satisfying
diam(M)2 secM ≥−δ(n)
(1.2)
has the following properties:
(a) A finite cover of M fibers over a torus of dimension b1(M);
(b) If b1(M) = n, then M is diffeomorphic to the n-torus.
In fact, this theorem is a special case of Yamaguchi’s general smooth fibration theorem
in [Yam91]. The general theorem states that if a manifold with uniformly lower bound
of sectional curvature collapses in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense to a lower-dimensional
smooth manifold, then the collapsing yields a smooth fibration whose fiber satisfies the
topological properties described above.
Note that under the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1, the manifold M admits a uniform
lower bound on its sectional curvature, which depends only on n and κ.
In [Yam88],
this two-sided bound on sectional curvature is crucial for establishing regularity estimates
for exponential maps and for carrying out a certain smoothing procedure of Riemannian
metrics.
Recent advances in metric geometry under lower Ricci curvature bounds allow us to
remove the upper sectional curvature assumption in Theorem 1.1 and to avoid passing to
a finite cover in Theorem 1.2. A main result of this paper unifies Theorems 1.1 and 1.2
as follows.
