First-Principles Explanation of the Drift Configuration Dependence of the Radial
Electric Field and High-Confinement Access in Tokamaks
B. J. Frei1, R. Bilato1, O. Grover1, W. Zholobenko1, C. Angioni1,
M. Bergmann1, P. Ulbl1, F. Jenko1, and the ASDEX Upgrade Team2
1 Max-Planck Institute for Plasma Physics, Boltzmannstr. 2, Garching, D-85748, Germany
and
2 See author list of H. Zohm et al 2024 Nucl. Fusion https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/ad249d
(Dated: May 25, 2026)
The origin of the difference in the high-confinement (H-mode) power threshold between favorable
and unfavorable drift configurations in tokamaks—experimentally linked to a deeper radial electric
field (Er) well in the former—remains unresolved. Using first-principles gyrokinetic simulations of
edge and scrape-off-layer turbulence in ASDEX Upgrade, we show that turbulence-driven poloidal
flows generate this deeper Er well in the favorable configuration through enhanced nonlinear tur-
bulence–mean flow energy transfer. This transfer is significantly weaker in the unfavorable case,
yielding a shallower Er well, while turbulence intensity is simultaneously higher. Within the turbu-
lence–flow shear suppression paradigm, the combination of stronger shear and reduced turbulence
facilitates H-mode access in the favorable configuration. These results provide the first validated,
self-consistent full-f gyrokinetic explanation of how drift configuration controls the nonlinear dy-
namics of profiles, Er, flows, and turbulence, thereby setting the H-mode power threshold.
In tokamaks, the direction of the ion ∇B drift (relative
to the active X-point) defines favorable (fav) and unfa-
vorable (unfav) configurations. This change of direction
significantly influences the H-mode power threshold, with
unfav discharges generally requiring more than twice the
threshold power than fav ones [1–5]. This difference ob-
served in multiple devices [5–9] correlates with a deeper
radial electric field (Er) well near the separatrix in fav
before the transition from low-confinement (L-mode) to
H-mode. A threshold in Er at the L-H transition has
been identified in fav at ASDEX Upgrade (AUG) [10],
while it remains unclear in unfav.
Understanding the
mechanism underlying this difference in Er is crucial for
ITER and future fusion reactors, as it determines the
heating power required and limits operational flexibility.
Recent experiments at AUG comparing fav and un-
fav discharges at matched parameters [5] show that
neoclassical (NC) effects alone cannot explain the ob-
served Er differences, highlighting a significant role for
non-NC physics. Proposed mechanisms include ion or-
bit losses [11–13], scrape-off-layer flows [14], and non-
linear turbulence–mean flow interactions driven by the
Reynolds stress (RS) [4, 15–20].
In particular, turbu-
lence–mean flow energy transfer is highly sensitive to
poloidal asymmetries in the RS, which can be driven
by X-point geometry, flow shear, and radial transport
[19, 21].
Reduced models incorporating such asymme-
tries capture qualitative experimental trends [20], but
a predictive, self-consistent, and nonlinear description
of the interplay between equilibrium profiles, Er, flows,
and turbulence remains to be established.
Existing
studies—based on linear, simplified or quasi-linear ap-
proaches in simplified geometries—are not able to cap-
ture correctly the non-local coupling and nonlinear feed-
back between turbulence, mean flows, and Er in real-
istic diverted configurations.
In addition, edge turbu-
lence requires a GK treatment. Developing such a self-
consistent and GK description is fundamental to deter-
mining whether these coupled effects fully account for
the differences between fav and unfav and to quantifying
their respective contributions.
In this Letter, we present the first gyrokinetic (GK)
study of fav and unfav discharges at AUG [5] using
validated first-principles simulations of edge-SOL turbu-
lence.
We show that stronger nonlinear generation of
poloidal flows near the separatrix explains the deeper
Er well in fav.
An evolution equation for the flux-
surface–averaged (FSA) poloidal kinetic energy reveals
that these poloidal flows are driven by radially localized
asymmetries in the total velocity stress Π, a full-f gener-
alization of RS. These asymmetries arise from the orien-
tation of E × B flow relative to the X-point on the low-
field side (LFS), producing a radial gradient of Π that
enhances turbulence–flow energy transfer in fav. Mean-
while, turbulence intensity is stronger in unfav. Overall,
the higher H-mode power threshold in unfav might result
from both weaker turbulence–mean flow energy transfer
and stronger turbulence levels.
Experiments at AUG. Dedicated deuterium experi-
ments at AUG [5] compared a pair of lower single-null
(LSN) discharges, #36983 (fav) and #37375 (unfav), to
investigate the effect of the ion ∇B drift direction on Er.
Both discharges were performed under matched condi-
tions: line-averaged electron density ne ≃2.7×1019 m−3
(lower density branch), 600 kW of ECRH in L-mode. The
on-axis toroidal magnetic field and plasma current were
|Bϕ| = 2.5 T and |Ip| = 0.8 MA. Measurements of Er
were obtained from Doppler back-scattering (DBS), while
the electron temperature (Te) and ne were reconstructed
via integrated data analysis (IDA) [22]. The main ion
temperature (Ti) was assumed to be equal to that of the
impurity species measured by charge-exchange recom-
bination spectroscopy (CXRS). The deuterium toroidal
(Uϕi) and poloidal (Uθi) velocities were inferred from im-
purity velocity measurements using the NC code NEOART
[23].

Turbulence Gyrokinetic Modelling.
We model these
two discharges in the L-mode phase using the spectral
version of the GENE-X code [24, 25], which solves the full-
f, electromagnetic, long-wavelength, and collisional GK
Vlasov equation with X-points. The details of the numer-
ical setup is reported in A. The magnetic equilibrium at
t = 2.7 s is considered for the fav discharge (#36983) in
the L-mode phase (prior to the L-H transition triggered
at 2.78 s with a power threshold of 1.1 MW). To minimize
differences between the two configurations in the simu-
lations, the unfav equilibrium (#37375) is generated by
reversing the direction of Bϕ from #36983, changing the
direction of the ion ∇B drift and the magnetic helicity. A
density source is introduced near the separatrix to mimic
neutral ionization. To reach a quasi-steady state, both
simulations were run until t ∼2.5 ms. In quasi-steady
state, the total power crossing the separatrix is approxi-
matively 0.5 MW in both cases. A time window of 0.1 ms
is employed to perform the statistics.

The simulations reproduce the overall experimental Er
behavior in both the edge and SOL, with good agree-

(GAM) [31] activity is also found around the Er well in

The OMP profiles and velocities (Fig. 1) structures in-
fluence Er through the radial force balance constraint.
Thus, to identify the origin of the difference between Er
observed in Fig. 2, we consider the ion radial force bal-
ance, which holds in the full-f GK formalism [28, 30].
Assuming isotropic ion pressure, the radial force bal-
ance reads [29]: Er = ∇rPi/(qini) + UiϕBθ −UiθBϕ,
with Er = −∇ϕ1 · ˆeψ. As shown in Fig. 1, the diamag-
netic (∇rPi/[qini]) and toroidal rotation (UiϕBθ) terms
are nearly identical in both configurations and domi-
nate Er for ρpol ≲0.98, implying a small contribution
from the poloidal flow (−UiθBϕ). However, within the
Er well (ρpol ∼0.995), they nearly cancel out, leaving
Er ≃−UiθBϕ. Hence, the depth and the minimum value
of the Er well (Er,min) are set by the amplitude and shape
of the poloidal ion flow Uiθ, which is much larger in am-
plitude in fav. With the same magnetic field strength, the
resulting inner E × B shear is approximately 1.6 times
stronger in fav than in unfav [20].
The SOL Er agree well with experiments [5] and
conduction-limited GK [30], with only minor differences
between fav and unfav.
As a consequence, the outer
E × B shear layer is 1.6 times larger in fav and exceeds
the inner shear in amplitude in both fav and unfav. Alto-
gether, Figs. 1 and 2 therefore indicate that the observed
difference in the depth of the Er well (and its shear) can-
not originate from NC effects, but instead arises from
nonlinear interactions between turbulence and poloidal
flow, as we demonstrate below.

Conclusions. First-principles GK simulations of edge
and SOL turbulence in realistic diverted geometry repro-
duce the experimentally observed difference in Er well
depth between the fav and unfav configurations at AUG
[5].
The simulations show that this difference arises
from nonlinear turbulence-driven poloidal flows, which
set both the depth and shear of the Er well. We demon-
strate that the efficiency of this nonlinear energy transfer
is enhanced (fav) or reduced (unfav) depending on the di-
rection of localized E×B shear near the LFS separatrix.
From the evolution equation of the mean poloidal kinetic
energy, the strength of the turbulence-mean flow energy
transfer is identified to be driven by the radial gradient
of the full-f velocity stress Π. Finally, turbulence activ-
ity is found to be larger in unfav. These results provide
the first self-consistent GK explanation of the drift con-
figuration dependence of the Er well depth, identifying
turbulence-mean flow energy transfer (mediated by the
gradient of Π) as the key nonlinear mechanism yielding
the deeper Er well in fav. With the stronger turbulence
in unfav, this nonlinear mechanism driven by the E × B
drift direction between the OMP and X-point on the LFS
may underlie the observed configuration dependence of
the H-mode (and I-mode) power threshold [16, 37]. Fi-
nally, flux-driven (fixed input power) simulations are re-
quired to further test this mechanism against experimen-
tal power thresholds. Finally, we note that similar trends
are also observed in simulations of other devices, such as
TCV [38], indicating the universality of the present find-
ings.

Simulation Setup.
We summarize here the numeri-
cal setup employed in GENE-X. While a comprehensive
description of the numerical methods implemented in
GENE-X can be found in [24, 25], only the most relevant
aspects are highlighted here. At the boundaries of the
numerical domain (extending from ρpol = 0.9 to 1.03),
the distribution functions are assumed to be Maxwellian
[25], with density and temperatures fixed at values close
to experiments.
The electromagnetic fields are set to
zero. To avoid artificial flow shear near the edge bound-
ary, Neumann boundary conditions on flux variables are
applied. For the numerical resolution, we use 6 parallel
(Hermite) and 4 perpendicular (Laguerre) spectral coef-
ficients in velocity space [30], 32 poloidal planes, and a
constant grid spacing of ∼1.4 mm (about 1.5 ion ther-
mal gyroradius ρi near the separatrix) within the poloidal
plane.
To mimic the effects of neutral ionization in the edge,
we introduce a poloidally homogeneous, axisymmetric,
radially localized Gaussian energy-conserving density
source centered at ρpol = 0.99, with a constant amplitude
of 1.5 × 1022s−1 for both fav and unfav. In the present
setup, particle and energy sources and sinks arise from
a combination of Dirichlet boundary conditions at the
edge and SOL boundaries—where the profiles are fixed
to values close to experiments—and perpendicular hy-
perdiffusion applied within a narrow buffer region be-
tween ghost points and the numerical domain. Upon dis-
cretization, this approach is equivalent to a Krook-like
operator that relaxes the distribution function toward

Radial Current Contributions. We briefly discuss the
contributions from the radial electrostatic (Jes = Jes ·
ˆeψ) and diamagnetic (JB = JB · ˆeψ) currents. These
currents can either drive or damp the mean vorticity ω
(mean poloidal flow) depending on their sign (relative to
the poloidal flow velocity).
Jes typically acts as driv-
ing term through nonlinear interactions with turbulence,
whereas JB generally damps poloidal flows. In partic-
ular, in the absence of turbulence, JB damps poloidal
flows via parallel ion viscosity (NC damping [23, 26]).
In Fig. 6, the FSA and time-averaged radial profiles of
JB and Jes are shown in fav and unfav. Here, the signs
are defined relative to uθ such that positive (negative)
values indicate drive (damping). The contribution from
JB is negative in both cases, but its amplitude is larger
in unfav around the Er well, indicating stronger damp-
ing. In contrast, the electrostatic radial current Jes is
positive and stronger in the fav case near the Er well,
providing a stronger drive in fav.
The combination of
JB and Jes therefore suggests a stronger net drive of the
poloidal flow in fav. However, we note that the present
analysis is qualitative since a rigorous balance analysis of
Eq. (A1) is hindered by the separation of characteristic
time scales, with JB evolving on longer collisional time
scales and Jes on shorter turbulence time scales. Conse-
quently, we focus here on the role of Jes in driving mean
poloidal flows.
GAM Oscillations.
The coupling between mean
poloidal flows and diamagnetic currents can transfer en-
ergy to pressure sidebands via the excitation of GAMs
[39–41]. These pressure sidebands can in turn couple to
parallel currents and dissipate energy through electron
resistivity or can be nonlinearly depleted [39], thereby
limiting the growth of mean (zonal) poloidal flows. In
the present simulations, the GAM activity, which is in-
ferred from the GAM amplitude AGAM, is found to be
stronger in unfav around the Er well and reaches a min-
imum at the location of Er,min, as shown in Fig. 7. In
contrast, AGAM is much smaller in fav. The difference
in the GAM amplitude between fav and unfav, also evi-
denced in the standard deviations in Fig. 5, arises from
nonlinear interactions with turbulence via the velocity
stress Π. This is confirmed by a detailed Fourier analy-
sis of Π (not shown here) revealing a clear peak at the
linear GAM frequency in unfav. This enhanced GAM ac-
tivity suggests a larger energy transfer through geodesic
coupling in unfav, which may further inhibit the buildup
of mean poloidal flows and contribute to a shallower Er
well in unfav. A more quantitative analysis of GAM drive
and interaction with turbulence [41] is deferred to future
work.
