Abstract. The existence of stationary finitely dependent processes on combinatorial models like
Zd subshifts can be quite mysterious. For instance, Holroyd and Liggett constructed such processes
on proper 4-colorings of Zd for all d while Holroyd, Schramm and Wilson showed that there are no
such processes on proper 3-colorings of Zd for d > 1. In this paper, we take inspiration from these
results and investigate them further. On the positive side, we show that there exists a dense set of
stationary finitely dependent processes supported on subshifts with strong mixing properties like
the finite extension property. On the negative side, we see that the cohomology of the subshifts
can form an obstruction to the existence of such processes. In particular we use Conway-Lagarias-
Thurston height functions to characterise when there exists a finitely dependent process on the
space of tilings by boxes of Z2 answering the tiling problem posed by Gao, Jackson, Krohne and
Seward in dimension 2. The ideas also apply to many other models, such as graph homomorphisms
and ribbon tilings.
On the way, we also show that continuous cocycles on strongly irreducible
subshifts valued in a special class of groups (including torsion free Gromov hyperbolic groups and
free product of cyclic groups) are perturbations of group homomorphisms.

1. Introduction
A finitely dependent process on a graph G = (V, E) is a stochastic process (Xv)v∈V such that
there is a constant k for which given any two sets A, B ⊂V separated by a distance k, we have that
(Xv)v∈A is independent of (Xv)v∈B. The support of a process (Xv)v∈V taking values in a finite set
A is the set of configurations x ∈AV such that for all finite sets B ⊂V ,
P(Xv = xv for all v ∈B) > 0.
In this paper, we study the support of shift-invariant (stationary) finitely dependent processes on
the Zd lattice. The support X ⊂AZd of any shift-invariant process with state space A satisfies
two natural properties: Firstly it is compact. Secondly, it is invariant under translations by Zd.
These two properties define a subshift X ⊂AZd: A closed translational invariant subset of AZd.
Additionally, if the process is finitely dependent (for the constant k as above), then the support
X satisfies the property: If x, y ∈X and A, B ⊂Zd are separated by distance k then there exists
z ∈X such that z|A = x|A and z|B = y|B. Indeed, if the probabilities of Xv = xv for all v ∈A
and Xw = yw for all w ∈B are each individually positive then so is the joint probability. Any
subshift which satisfies above property is said to be strongly irreducible. Thus, if there is a finitely
dependent process whose support is contained in a subshift X, then X must contain a strongly
irreducible subshift. This leads us to one of the main questions motivating our research:
Question 1.1. Are there general conditions on the subshift which imply the existence or non-
existence of shift-invariant finitely dependent processes supported on them?
One of the first results in this direction comes from the papers by Holroyd and Liggett [HL16]
and Holroyd, Wilson and Schramm [HSW17]. It was proved in [HL16] that if there is a finitely
dependent process on the space of proper colorings of Z, then it is not a (so-called) block factor of

an iid process. Using this, they went on to construct shift-invariant finitely dependent processes on
proper colorings of Z which are not block-factors of an iid process. Although examples of finitely
dependent processes which are not block factors of an iid were known [BGM93], these formed a
somewhat more natural set of such examples. An a priori surprising result is that while there
is a shift-invariant finitely dependent process on the space of proper 4-colorings of Zd for all d
[HL16], there are none on the space of proper 3-colorings [HSW17] of Zd for d ≥2. To prove

Schramm and Wilson analyzed the variance of the height function associated to proper 3-colorings
of Zd. A somewhat different argument was found in [CM21] to show that proper 3-colorings do not
contain a strongly irreducible subshift, bypassing the probabilistic argument by Holroyd, Schramm
and Wilson. Although the two arguments seem very different at first, both of them have a strong
common thread: The existence of height functions for the model and their rigid behavior for
strongly irreducible subshifts. Taking inspiration from these ideas, we find a large class of subshifts
for which there are no finitely dependent processes on them.
The main novel idea comes from the analysis of cocycles (following [Sch95]) taking values in some
special groups. Specifically, a cocycle on a subshift X taking values in a group G is a continuous
function c : X×Zd →G such that c(x, i+j) = c(x, i)c(σi(x), j) where σi(x) is a configuration y ∈X
such that yj = xi+j for all i ∈Zd. Many subshifts of interest have interesting and useful cocycles:
Tilings of R2 by 1 × m and m′ × 1 boxes for integers m, m′ > 1 ([KK92] following constructions by
Conway-Lagarias [CL90] and Thurston [Thu90]), graph homomorphisms from Zd to a fixed finite
undirected graph H [CGdMO25a] and ribbon tilings [She02]. For many of these subshifts, there
are natural cocycles which take values in groups (or their direct products) with a very special
property: The centraliser of any infinite order element in the group is cyclic. Such groups are said
to satisfy the CIC property. This condition is satisfied by several classes of groups arising naturally
in geometric group theory. For example, torsion-free Gromov hyperbolic groups [BH99, Chapter
III.Γ, Corollary 3.10] (in particular free groups), free products of cyclic groups [MKS04, Chapter
4, exercise 28] satisfy this property.

denote by |g| the minimum length of the representation of g in terms of S. We extensively use the
following rigidity result, which is also the main technical innovation of this paper.

There is a remarkable relationship between questions about finitely dependent processes and ques-
tions in Borel/continuous combinatorics [Ber23, GR23] which is still not completely understood.
However, it is true that if there is a continuous equivariant map from the free part of the full shift
to a given subshift X, then there is also a shift-invariant finitely dependent process on X. By the
above results it follows that there is no continuous equivariant map from the free part of the full
shift to the space of tilings by m × 1 and 1 × m′ boxes of R2 when m, m′ > 1 are integers. This
answers a question raised in [GJKS25, Problem 2, Section 4.5] in the negative which asked if there
is such a map into the space of tilings by m × m and (m + 1) × (m + 1) boxes for m ≥2.

boxes in Rd, there exists a continuous equivariant map from the free part of the full shift to the
space of tilings by boxes in R?

characterisation in the case of tiling by boxes of R2.

(1) There exist m, n > 1 such that the m × 1 and 1 × n boxes tile each element of R.

One of the most interesting questions which we still struggle with is coming up with novel
methods of constructing finitely dependent processes (see Question 9.7). Beyond the block factors
of iid, one of the main ways to construct stationary finitely dependent processes on subshifts still
relies on constructing one [HSW17] on the so-called m-net processes on Zd (which in turn uses
the ‘mysterious’ shift-invariant finitely dependent proper 4-colorings of Z constructed in [HL16] -
see the remark in [HHL20, beginning of Page 2]). Questions of this form point more towards the
second direction of our research. As observed earlier, the support of every shift-invariant finitely
dependent process is a strongly irreducible subshift. We do not know whether the converse is true.
Question 1.4. Given a strongly irreducible Zd subshift X, does there exist a shift-invariant finitely
dependent process on X? Is there a fully supported one?
We have some partial answers though.
Theorem 1.5. Let X be a strongly irreducible subshift of AZ. The set of stationary finitely depen-
dent processes supported on X is weak-∗dense in the set of shift-invariant measures on X.
The results we have for higher dimensional subshifts seem to require a stronger mixing property
called the finite extension property. The finite extension property for subshifts of finite type was
introduced in [BnMP18] for a completely different purpose. Several classes of subshifts like the
space of proper d + 2 colorings of Zd [ABnC+21] and the space of graph homomorphisms from Zd
to finite dismantleable graphs ([BW00, Lemma 5.2]) satisfy the finite extension property. We prove
that any subshift X with the finite extension property admits a shift-invariant finitely dependent
process with full support (i.e. support equal to X).

our main results. In Section 3 we discuss the existence of shift-invariant finitely dependent m-

2. Background and main results
2.1. Finitely dependent processes.
Here onwards, we will assume that G is a countable group. We will abuse the notation and let G
also denote right Cayley graph of G with respect to a fixed set of generators.
Definition 2.1. A process (Xg)g∈G is k-dependent if for any subsets A, B ⊂V with d(A, B) > k,
the restrictions XA = (Xg)g∈A and XB = (Xg)g∈B are independent. A process (Xg)g∈G is finitely
dependent if it is k-dependent for some k ∈N ∪{0}.
A stochastic process (Xg)g∈G is shift-invariant if (Xg)g∈G has the same distribution as (Xh−1·g)g∈G
for all h ∈G. If G = Zd, shift-invariant processes are commonly called stationary processes. But

for uniformity, we will refer to them as shift-invariant processes. In this paper, we will only be
interested in shift-invariant processes.
Clearly iid processes are shift-invariant finitely dependent processes. One natural way to con-
struct shift-invariant finitely dependent processes is by applying locally defined maps called block
factors to iid processes, which we now introduce.
Let (Xg)g∈G and (Yg)g∈G be shift-invariant processes where Xg ∈A and Yg ∈B. Then,
Definition 2.2. A process (Yg)g∈G is an r-block factor of (Xg)g∈G, if there exists a measurable
function ψ : AB(r) →B such that Yg = ψ((Xh)h∈g·B(r)) for all g ∈G. (Yg)g∈G is a block factor
of (Xg)g∈G if it is r-block factor of (Xg)g∈G for some r ∈N.
It is easy to see that block factors of iid processes are shift-invariant finitely dependent processes.
Lemma 2.3. If (Xg)g∈G is an iid process and if (Yg)g∈G is a r-block factor of (Xg)g∈G for some
r ∈N, then (Yg)g∈G is a shift-invariant (2r + 1)-dependent process.
Proof. If d(S, T) > 2r +1, then B(S, r) and B(T, r) are disjoint subsets of G. But (Yg)g∈S depends
only on (Xh)h∈B(S,r) while (Yg)g∈T depends only on (Xh)h∈B(T,r).
□
It was an open question for long time whether all shift-invariant finitely dependent processes
arise in this way. Aaronson, Gilat, Keane and de Valk ([AGKdV89]) constructed a shift-invariant
1-dependent process on Z which is not a 2-block factor of any iid process. Aaronson, Gilat and
Keane [AGK92] constructed a 1-dependent five-state stationary Markov chain example which is
not a 2-block factor of an iid. Burton, Goulet and Meester [BGM93] constructed a 1-dependent
hidden Markov process on Z which is not a r-block factor of any iid. More references and a history
of this problem can be found in [HL16].
Holroyd and Liggett constructed more examples of shift-invariant finitely dependent processes
which are not block factors of any iid in [HL16]. Their processes are supported on proper q−colorings
of Z for q ≥3 (see Example 2.8). In [HSW17], it was proved that there does not exist a block-factor
of iid supported on proper q-colorings of Z for any q ≥2. They also proved that there does not
exist a shift-invariant one-dependent proper 3-coloring of Z. An easy argument [HL16, Section
5] showed that any shift-invariant finitely dependent proper q-coloring cannot be a Markov chain.
However, Holroyd and Liggett proved the following.

7. Cocycles on strongly irreducible subshifts
In this section, we prove that cocycles defined on strongly irreducible subshifts valued in certain
groups must be small perturbations of a homomorphism in some sense. In the next section, we will
use this to prove that there are no strongly irreducible subshifts on certain subshifts.
In general, the expectation is that the 1-cohomology of strongly irreducible subshifts should not
be too complicated. In Theorem 1.2, we prove that this is indeed the case for cocycles taking values
in a certain class of groups.

9.6. Finitely dependent processes and periodic points.
An element x ∈AZd is called periodic if the subgroup {v ∈Zd : σvx = x} has a finite index in Zd.
The twelve tiles theorem in [GJKS25] implies that if there is a continuous coloring of F(2Zd) by a
subshift of finite type X, then X must contain periodic points.
Question 9.6. If a subshift X ⊂AZd is the support of a shift-invariant finitely dependent process,
then does X contain periodic points? Are periodic points dense in X?
This is true in one dimension since any one dimensional strongly irreducible subshift has dense
set of periodic points. Given that there are strongly irreducible Z2-subshifts without any fully
periodic points ([Hoc24]), an affirmative answer to this question will negatively answer Question
1.4.
9.7. Finitely dependent processes invariant under the subactions.
We learned about this question from Deepak Dhar. While in this paper we focus on shift-invariant
finitely dependent processes, the question of finitely dependent processes invariant under a sub-
action is largely ignored.
For instance, though there are no shift-invariant finitely dependent
processes on the space of domino tilings, there are plenty of finitely dependent processes which
are invariant under translations by the 2Z × 2Z. As an example, note that the delta measure on a
periodic configuration invariant under 2Z×2Z is finitely dependent. It follows in fact from standard
well-known results (see [CKP01]) that the space of finitely dependent processes on domino tilings
of Z2, invariant under full rank subgroups is dense in the space of Z2-ergodic probability measures.
It is however unclear whether for any k ∈N, the finitely dependent processes on domino tilings
invariant under the kZ × kZ sub-actions are dense in the space of ergodic measures.
9.8. Structure of finitely dependent processes.
While we know ways to construct shift-invariant finitely dependent processes, we know very little
about their structure. As discussed in the introduction, it was earlier conjectured and later refuted
that they are block factors of iid processes. We suggest the following modification.
