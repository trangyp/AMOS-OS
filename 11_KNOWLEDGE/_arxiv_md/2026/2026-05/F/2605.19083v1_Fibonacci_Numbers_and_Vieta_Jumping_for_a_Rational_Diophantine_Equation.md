A central tool in our analysis is the method of Vieta jumping, which constructs new
integer solutions of a quadratic Diophantine equation from existing ones by replacing one
root with the other root determined by Vieta’s formulas. Starting from a positive integer
solution, one “jumps” to another solution sharing one coordinate with the original pair,
hence the name Vieta jumping. For further background on Vieta jumping, see [1, 2, 7, 8].
We first define two operations to solutions (a, b) to (1.1) that generate new solutions:
flipping and Vieta jumping; it is trivial to see these are solutions by direct substitution.
• Flipping: The flip of (a, b) is the pair (b, a), and is also a solution.
• Vieta Jumping: The Vieta jumps of (a, b) are (kb −1 −a, b) and (a, ka −1 −b),
and both are also solutions.
