Functional Renormalization Group as a Ricci Flow: An F-Entropy Perspective on
Information Metric Dynamics
Ki-Seok Kim
Department of Physics, POSTECH, Pohang, Gyeongbuk 37673, Korea
Asia Pacific Center for Theoretical Physics (APCTP), Pohang, Gyeongbuk 37673, Korea∗

We establish an equivalence between the Functional Renormalization Group (FRG) and the
Ricci flow modified by a diffeomorphism. By reformulating the Polchinski exact renormalization
group equation into an infinite-dimensional Fokker-Planck framework, we show that the evolution
of the Fisher information metric on the coupling constant space is a geometric optimization process.
Central to this mapping is our construction of a field-theoretic F-entropy functional—an infinite-
dimensional analogue of Perelman’s F-entropy functional—which acts as a Lyapunov potential for
the theory.
We prove that the continuous scale evolution of the field distribution constitutes a
Riemannian gradient flow of this F-entropy, which in turn deforms the information metric on the
coupling constant space via the parametric Hessian of the entropic landscape. Crucially, the log of
the effective action serves as a scalar potential Φ that generates the diffeomorphisms required to
ensure the tensorial consistency of the flow. Our framework demonstrates that successive integration
of high-energy degrees of freedom effectively smooths out the curvature of the information manifold,
driving the system toward a Ricci soliton equilibrium. These results provide a novel foundation for
characterizing the stability of RG fixed points and offer a first-principles bridge connecting quantum
field theory, information geometry, and Perelman’s theory of geometric evolution.

I.
INTRODUCTION
The Renormalization Group (RG) framework, since its inception by Wilson and Wegner [1, 2], has served as
a cornerstone of modern theoretical physics, providing a systematic paradigm for understanding how macroscopic
phenomena emerge from microscopic degrees of freedom. Central to this framework is the Functional Renormalization
Group (FRG), which offers a powerful non-perturbative approach via the Wetterich equation [3] to describe the
scale-dependence of the effective average action ΓΛ. Despite its profound success in modern physics, a comprehensive
geometric principle governing the trajectory of the RG flow in the space of couplings remains an open and fundamental
question.
In parallel, Information Geometry has established that the parameter space of statistical models can be endowed
with a Riemannian structure via the Fisher information metric [4–9]. This information-theoretic lens has inspired
various attempts to characterize RG flows as geodesic trajectories or as entropy-driven gradient flows within such
metric spaces [10–19]. A major breakthrough in geometric analysis occurred with Perelman’s proof of the Poincar´e
conjecture, where he introduced the F-entropy functional to demonstrate that the Ricci flow—a parabolic evolution
of the Riemannian metric—is a gradient flow that monotonically increases a specific entropy [20–25].
The possibility of a deep connection between RG flows and Ricci flows has been hinted at in the context of non-linear
sigma models, where the one-loop beta function of the target space metric takes the form of the Ricci tensor [26–32].
However, a formal and exact mapping that transcends specific model assumptions and directly embeds the exact
FRG framework within the canonical Ricci flow has remained elusive. Previous approaches often remained restricted
to specific fixed points or relied on the assumption of a purely gradient flow in a flat parameter space, frequently
neglecting the non-trivial curvature of the information manifold and the gauge-like redundancy inherent in the choice
of coordinates.
In this paper, we bridge this critical gap by explicitly deriving the modified Ricci flow from the exact FRG framework
without relying on the weak-coupling expansion. By representing the FRG as a Fokker-Planck type evolution of field
distributions, we demonstrate that the metric’s rate of change naturally decomposes into the Ricci curvature and the
Hessian of a scalar potential, Φ. Crucially, we rigorously identify this potential as the log of the effective average action,
which acts as an emergent geometric gauge-fixing term that absorbs non-tensorial coordinate artifacts and restores
