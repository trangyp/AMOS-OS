1School of Mathematics, Zhejiang Normal University, Jinhua, Zhejiang 321004, China.

Abstract. Graph coloring with preferences offers a powerful framework for constraint satisfaction
problems in which fulfilling every request is impossible but satisfying a guaranteed positive fraction
is highly desirable. A request on a graph G equipped with a list assignment L assigns to each vertex
of some subset dom(r) ⊆V (G) a preferred color from its list. Following Dvoˇr´ak, Norin, and Postle
(2019), G is ε-flexibly k-choosable if, for every k-list assignment L and every request r, there is an
L-coloring of G that agrees with r on at least ε|dom(r)| vertices. The corresponding notion for
DP-coloring (correspondence coloring) was formalized by Bradshaw, Choi, and Kostochka (2025).
Choi, Clemen, Ferrara, Horn, Ma, and Masaˇr´ık (2022) proved that every planar graph without
4-cycles and with 3-cycle distance at least 2 is ε-flexibly 4-choosable. We improve the result in
two respects: weakening the hypothesis from 3-cycle distance ≥2 to vertex-disjoint triangles, and
strengthening the conclusion from list flexibility to weighted DP-flexibility: Every simple planar
graph without 4-cycles and without intersecting triangles is weighted ε-flexibly DP-4-colorable. The
list size 4 is sharp: Montassier, Raspaud, and Wang constructed a planar graph without 4-cycles,
5-cycles, and intersecting triangles that is not 3-choosable.
1. Introduction
The flexibility framework of Dvoˇr´ak, Norin, and Postle [8] interpolates between list coloring
and precoloring extension. Rather than asking whether a partial precoloring extends to a full L-
coloring, which is typically a hard question with a binary answer, one asks for an L-coloring that
honors a constant fraction of a set of vertex-color preferences. This is the natural relaxation when
preferences come from soft constraints, and it has driven a productive line of research in recent
years.

a set L(v) of colors; an L-coloring is a proper coloring ϕ with ϕ(v) ∈L(v) for all v. The graph G
is k-choosable if it is L-colorable for every L with |L(v)| ≥k. A request for (G, L) is a function r
with domain dom(r) ⊆V (G) and r(v) ∈L(v). For ε > 0, r is ε-satisfiable if there is an L-coloring
ϕ of G with ϕ(v) = r(v) for at least ε|dom(r)| vertices v ∈dom(r). We say (G, L) is ε-flexible if

DP-flexibility. DP-coloring (correspondence coloring), introduced by Dvoˇr´ak and Postle [9], gen-
eralizes list coloring. Given G and h : V (G) →N, an h-cover (H, L) of G is a graph H with
• a clique L(v) ⊆V (H) of size h(v) for each v ∈V (G),
• edges between L(u) and L(v) forming a matching whenever uv ∈E(G),
• no edges between L(u) and L(v) whenever uv /∈E(G).

for all xy ∈E(G). The graph G is DP-k-colorable if every k-cover admits an (H, L)-coloring.

Main result. Lam, Xu, and Liu [11] and Wang and Lih [14] proved 4-choosability for the planar
subclasses excluding C4 and excluding intersecting triangles, respectively. The following natural
flexibility question remains open: does list size 4 suffice for ε-flexibility under either hypothesis
alone? Under the conjunction, [5] obtained ε-flexibility at list size 4, but only under the stronger
requirement that triangles lie at distance ≥2 (which forbids triangles even when joined by an edge).
We weaken this to vertex-disjointness and simultaneously upgrade to weighted DP-flexibility.
Theorem 1.1. There exists ε > 0 such that every simple planar graph without 4-cycles and without
intersecting triangles is weighted ε-flexibly DP-4-colorable.
Two triangles are intersecting if they share a vertex; equivalently, the hypothesis says that the
distance between 3-cycles is at least 1.
The list size 4 cannot be reduced: Montassier, Raspaud, and Wang [13] constructed a planar
graph without 4-cycles, 5-cycles, and intersecting triangles that is not 3-choosable. So Theorem 1.1
is best possible with respect to the list size.
2. Preliminaries
Here we fix the terminology used throughout the paper. For a vertex v, we call v a k-vertex, a k+-
vertex, or a k−-vertex if d(v) = k, d(v) ≥k, or d(v) ≤k, respectively. Similarly, we define a k-face,
a k+-face, and a k−-face, and likewise k-, k+-, and k−-paths and cycles. A k-face f = [v1v2 · · · vk]
is an (x1, x2, . . . , xk)-face if d(vi) = xi for each i ∈[k]. If a k-vertex v is adjacent to a vertex u,
then we call v a k-neighbor of u; similarly, we define a k+-neighbor and a k−-neighbor. A face f is
a pendent 3-face of a vertex v if v is not on f but is adjacent to some 3-vertex u on f; in this case
we call u a pendent 3-neighbor of v. Let dist(u, v) denote the distance between u and v in G.
Given a function f : V (S) →Z and a vertex v ∈V (S), let f ↓v denote the function with
(f ↓v)(w) = f(w) for w ̸= v and (f ↓v)(v) = 1. A list assignment L is an f-assignment if
|L(v)| ≥f(v) for all v ∈V (S). Given a set of graphs F and a graph H, a set I ⊂V (S) is F-
forbidding if the graph H together with one additional vertex adjacent to all vertices in I contains
no graph from F. Let S be an induced subgraph of G and v ∈V (S). We define dS(v) as the degree
of v in the induced subgraph S. The following two definitions and two lemmas are from [5] in terms
