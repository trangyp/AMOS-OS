FAITHFUL LINEAR AND RELATIONAL REPRESENTATIONS OF
DIAGRAM CATEGORIES AND MONOIDS
JAMES EAST, MARIANNE JOHNSON, AND MARK KAMBITES
Abstract. We study representations of diagram categories by binary relations and
matrices over rings and semirings. Our main result is a faithful involutive tensor repre-
sentation of the partition category P (and consequently of each partition monoid Pn)
by zero-one matrices over an arbitrary (additively) idempotent semiring. The dimen-
sions of the matrices involved are powers of 2, and we show that these are minimal
with respect to faithful involutive tensor representations by matrices over any semiring.
Intriguingly, these matrices encode the number of floating components formed when
composing partitions, and can therefore be used to construct faithful representations
of (d-)twisted partition categories PΦ and PΦ,d (and the respective twisted partition
monoids PΦ
n and PΦ,d
n
) over rings of appropriate characteristic. We also give lower-
dimensional involutive representations of the Brauer and Temperley–Lieb categories B
and TL. In the case of TL, the dimensions are given by Fibonacci numbers.

linear categories, linear monoids, representations.

1. Introduction
Diagram algebras arise naturally in many areas of mathematics and science, and
play important roles in statistical mechanics, biology, computer science, representation
theory, logic, combinatorics, knot theory and more. These algebras have bases consisting
of various kinds of set partitions, which are represented and multiplied diagrammatically.
Key examples include Brauer algebras [8], Temperley–Lieb algebras [63] and partition
algebras [40,56]; for more on the background and applications, see the survey [57]. Early
on, Jones and Kauffman recognised the importance of underlying diagram monoids
[38–44], which underpin the structure of the algebras, and the link was made precise by
Wilcox in his work on twisted semigroup algebras [64]; see also [35]. Diagram monoids
and algebras occur as endomorphism monoids/algebras of associated diagram categories,
studied for example in [2,11,31,45,54,56,57].
Diagram monoids have also become a major theme in modern semigroup theory,
due to their intrinsically intriguing algebraic and combinatorial properties, and striking
parallels with transformation semigroups. For example, there are studies on Green’s re-
lations [33,64], presentations by generators and relations [17–19,49,53], characterisation
and enumeration of idempotents [13,14], idempotent-generation [12,21,22,24], congru-
ences [26, 28–30], Krohn–Rhodes complexity [3], semigroup identities [4, 5, 47, 48], and
connections to category theory [23,27].
In a different direction, the recent article [9] studied transformation representations of
diagram monoids, in each case giving an explicit formula for the transformation degree,
i.e. the minimum number of points on which the monoid acts faithfully by functions. For
example, the partition monoid Pn has degree 1+ B(n+2)−B(n−1)+B(n)
2
, where B(n) is the
nth Bell number. Any transformation representation leads to a linear representation of
the same degree, over any unital (semi)ring. In general, however, there can be faithful
linear representations whose dimension is substantially lower than the transformation
degree, and this is one of the main motivations for the present work. As a sample result,
we show that there is a faithful 2n-dimensional representation of Pn by matrices over
any (additively) idempotent semiring. This representation also models the well-known
(product-reversing) involution of Pn as transposition of matrices, thereby retaining in-
formation that is lost by transformation representations (as full transformation monoids
have no involutions). Since matrices over the Boolean semiring can be interpreted as
binary relations, we also obtain a faithful representation of Pn by relations of degree 2n.
In fact, all of these monoid representations can be packaged into a single faithful rep-
resentation of the partition category P in the full matrix category M. This category
representation contains much more information, as it is defined also on hom-sets Pm,n
with m ̸= n. It is additionally compatible with natural tensor structures on P and M,
and has order-theoretic connections as well. We remark that the relation-theoretic in-
terpretation of our representation P →M seems to be closely related to a special case
of a functor considered briefly in [16].
Let us now give a fuller summary of the paper. We begin in Section 2 by recalling
the preliminary material we need, including the definitions of the partition category P,
the Brauer category B and the Temperley–Lieb category TL, as well as their twisted
counterparts.

We now gather the definitions and results we need on general categories and semirings,
as well as specific categories of matrices and diagrams/partitions. For further details and
background, see for example [20, 30, 36, 37, 52, 62]. We denote the sets of integers and

number n ∈N, we write [n] = {1, . . . , n}, interpreting this to be empty when n = 0.

2.1. Categories. All categories we consider are small, meaning that their morphisms
form a set. Formally, a category is a tuple (C, O, d, r, ◦), where:
• C is the set of morphisms,
• O is the set of objects,
• d : C →O and r : C →O are the domain and range maps, and
• ◦is the composition operation of morphisms, where a ◦b is defined (for a, b ∈C)
if and only if r(a) = d(b), in which case d(a ◦b) = d(a) and r(a ◦b) = r(b),
with composition satisfying the associative law where defined, and where for each object
s ∈O there exists an identity morphism at s denoted ιs with d(ιs) = r(ιs) = s and
such that ιs ◦a = a and b ◦ιs = b wherever defined. We typically identify the category
(C, O, d, r, ◦) with the underlying (morphism) set C, and use juxtaposition to denote
composition: ab = a ◦b. For objects s, t ∈O, we write
Cs,t = {a ∈C : d(a) = s, r(a) = t}
for the set of all morphisms s →t, and refer to such sets as hom-sets. Note that if s
and t are distinct there is no requirement that Cs,t be non-empty; for s = t we have
ιs ∈Cs,s. We write Cs = Cs,s for the endomorphism monoid of the object s ∈O.

The categories of interest in this paper are strict tensor categories, meaning that

• faithful if it is injective on objects and morphisms,
• involutive if C and D are both involutive categories, and ϕ preserves the involu-
tions,
• a tensor representation, if C and D are both tensor categories, and ϕ preserves
the tensor operations.
2.2. Semirings. A (unital) semiring is a tuple (K, +, ·, 0, 1), such that:
• (K, +, 0) is a commutative monoid, with + referred to as addition,
• (K, ·, 1) is a monoid, with · referred to as multiplication,
• multiplication distributes over addition, and
• 0 is a multiplicative zero element.
We typically identify a semiring with its underlying set K, and we always assume that K
is non-trivial, in the sense that 0 ̸= 1. We say that a semiring K:
• is commutative if the multiplication of K is commutative,
• is (additively) idempotent if 1+1 = 1 (or equivalently if α+α = α for all α ∈K),
• has characteristic 0 if 1 generates an infinite subsemigroup under addition.
The prototypical example of an idempotent semiring is the Boolean semiring, B = {0, 1},
in which 1 + 1 = 1 (and all other additions and multiplications follow from the axioms).
Another important and widely studied example is the tropical (or max-plus) semiring
(see for example [51]). The natural numbers under ordinary addition and multiplication
is a semiring of characteristic 0.

7. Representations of diagram algebras
In this section we relate our main results to known results on the (classical) repre-
sentation theory of diagram algebras. The latter are endomorphism algebras in natural
linear versions of our diagram categories, whose definitions we now recall. These can
be seen as special instances of a categorical version of the twisted semigroup algebras
considered for example in [64].
Definition 7.1. Let K be a commutative unital ring, and δ ∈K a fixed element. The
linear partition category (with respect to K and δ), denoted P(K, δ), has:
• object set N,
• morphism sets Pm,n(K, δ) consisting of all formal K-linear combinations of par-
titions from Pm,n,
• composition ⋆defined to be the K-linear extension of the rule:
a ⋆b = δΦ(a,b)ab
for composable partitions a, b ∈P.
Here ab is the product in P, and Φ(a, b) ∈N is the twisting parameter from
Definition 2.3.

8. Concluding remarks and open questions
Our main results give faithful linear representations of the diagram categories P, B
and TL, as well as their (d-)twisted counterparts, over various kinds of semiring. Where
these representations preserve the involution and tensor operations, we have seen that
the corresponding sequence of dimensions of matrices grows exponentially and is min-
imum possible for such a representation.
However, it remains open whether smaller
representations are possible if one does not insist on preserving these operations.
Question 8.1. Does there exist a faithful matrix representation of P with sequence of
dimensions less than 2n, or even growing sub-exponentially?
Note that our reduced (non-tensor-preserving) representations of B and TL from Sec-

Question 8.2. Does there exist a faithful matrix representation of TL with sequence

One can also ask whether it is possible to move beyond idempotent semirings. Given
Remark 3.13, a positive answer to the next question would involve building a completely
different representation.
Question 8.3. Do there exist ‘natural’ faithful (involutive tensor category) representa-
tions of P, B or TL in M(K) where K is a ring, or even a field?
Analogous questions can also be asked for the corresponding twisted categories.
Of course minimum-dimension representations for individual diagram monoids will
not necessarily arise as restrictions of representations of the corresponding categories, so
there also arise separate questions about their effective dimension (2.2). (As we noted
in the introduction, any finite monoid—such as Pn, Bn or TLn—embeds in some trans-
formation monoid Td, and hence in the matrix monoid Md(K) over any semiring K.)

diagram monoids Pn, Bn and TLn (and their (d-)twisted counterparts) over K?
