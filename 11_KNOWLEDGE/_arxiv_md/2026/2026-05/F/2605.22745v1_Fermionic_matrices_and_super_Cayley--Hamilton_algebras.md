2. Grassmann matrices
2.1. Superalgebras. We use the language of superspaces and Z/(2) graded
algebras or superalgebras.
Definition 2.2.
(1) A superspace is a Z/(2) graded space W = W0 ⊕W1.
(2) A superalgebra is an associative algebra A with a Z/(2) grading
A = A0 ⊕A1 so that AjAj ⊂Ai+j, i + j mod 2.
(3) We denote by d(a) ∈{0, 1} the degree of an homogeneous element
of A.
(4) The supercommutator of two homogeneous elements a, b is
[a, b] := ab −(−1)d(a)d(b)ba.
(5) An algebra is supercommutative if the supercommutator of two
homogeneous elements is always zero,
(6) The supercenter of A is the set of elements of A which super com-
mute with all elements of A. It is a supercommutative subalgebra.
One has the notion of graded tensor product Ab⊗B of superspaces, when
they are both superalgebras this is also a superalgebra which as vector
space is A ⊗B but for multiplication of graded elements one defines:
a1 ⊗b1 · a2 ⊗b2 = (−1)d(a2)d(b1)a1a2 ⊗b1b2.
If A = A0 or B = B0 the multiplication in the graded tensor product
equals that of usual tensor product.
Proposition 2.3. The graded tensor product Ab⊗B of two supercommuta-
tive superalgebras is supercommutative.

3. Superalgebras with trace
In order to explain the SFT for Grassmann matrices we develop a lan-
guage which is a natural evolution of the one developed in the previous
paragraphs, at least in characteristic 0, the language of superalgebras with
trace and their identities (cf. [1]).
Definition 3.1. An associative superalgebra with trace, also called SAT
over a field F is an associative F–superalgebra R with a 1-ary operation
t : R →R
which is assumed to satisfy the following axioms:
(1) t is F–linear and compatible with the Z/(2) grading.
(2) t(a)b = (−1)d(a)d(b)b · t(a),
∀a, b ∈R homogeneous.
(3) t(ab) = (−1)d(a)d(b)t(ba),
∀a, b ∈R.
(4) t(t(a)b) = t(a)t(b),
∀a, b ∈R.

This operation is called a formal trace.
We denote t(R) := {t(a), a ∈R} the image of t.
Remark 3.2. We have the following implications:
Axiom 1) implies that t(R) is an F–submodule.
Axiom 2) implies that t(R) is contained in the supercenter of R.
Axiom 3) implies that t is 0 on the space of supercommutators [R, R].
Axiom 4) implies that t(R) is an F–subalgebra and that t is t(R)–linear.
We call t(R) the trace algebra of R.
The basic example of algebra with trace is of course the algebra of m×m
matrices over a supercommutative algebra B with the usual trace.
We have made no special requirements on the value of t(1). Of course in
many important examples this is a positive integer.
SAT form a category where objects are SAT and morphisms algebra
homomorphisms φ which commute with the trace and grading.
d(φ(a)) = d(a),
t(φ(a)) = φ(t(a)).
Recall that in an algebra R with some extra operations, an ideal I must be
stable under these operations so that R/I can inherit the structure.
In a SAT a superideal is an Z/(2) graded ideal I stable under the trace,
that is if a ∈I then t(a) ∈I. The usual homomorphism theorems are then
valid for SAT.
Given a SAT R, if we forget the trace we just have an associative super-
algebra.
On the other hand, there is a simple formal construction which associates
to any F superalgebra R a superalgebra with trace Rt.
Denote by [R, R] the linear span of supercommutators, a graded subspace
of R.
We then define Rt := R⊗K[R/[R, R]], with K[R/[R, R]] the superalgebra
of Formula (2).
Given an element r ∈R, its trace t(r) is the class of r in R/[R, R] (this is
a functor adjoint to the previous forgetful functor). This extends to a trace
on Rt with trace algebra K[R/[R, R]].
Proposition 3.3. A graded homomorphism f : R →A with A a trace
superalgebra extends uniquely to a trace preserving map f : Rt →A.

3.13. Trace identites.
Using evaluations allows us to introduce the
notion of trace identity for a trace superalgebra R.
Definition 3.14. A trace identity for a trace superalgebra R is a element
f ∈FT = F⟨X, Y ⟩[t(M)] of the free algebra such that f vanishes under all
evaluations in R.
Let us now remark that the set of all trace identities of a trace algebra
R is a special ideal of FT , called a T–ideal, that is an ideal in FT closed
under trace and under all substitutions of variables.
By definition a substitution of a variable x must be an element of degree
1 in FT , while a substitution of a variable y must be an element of degree
0 in FT .
A basic question for any algebra R is to describe a set of generators of
its trace identities as T–ideal,
The theory is really effective only when F is a field of characteristic 0,
