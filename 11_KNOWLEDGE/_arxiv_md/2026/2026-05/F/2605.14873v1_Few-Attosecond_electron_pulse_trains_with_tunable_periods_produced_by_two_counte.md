Abstract
Attosecond electron pulses enable real-time probing of ultrafast matter dynamics, yet conven-
tional modulation schemes suffer from drastically shortened longitudinal focal lengths when target-
ing sub-attosecond durations. To address this bottleneck, we propose and demonstrate a compact
scheme utilizing two counter-propagating lasers that reveals a previously unidentified stable modu-
lation regime. Contrary to established models of ponderomotive forces and stochastic acceleration
in dual-laser fields, we show that a specific parametric resonance condition permits the electron
beam to be stably modulated into highly periodic attosecond trains with rapid energy gain. Us-
ing a sub-relativistic electron beam, simulations confirm the generation of ∼1 as pulses with a
Lorentz factor up to 15 and a relative energy spread below 0.02%, extending the focal length by
three orders of magnitude compared with conventional approaches. This work identifies the critical
transition from ordered modulation to stochastic acceleration, offering a viable route to overcoming
the focal-length barrier in attosecond electron-pulse applications.
The need for attosecond electron pulses stems from their capability to probe electron dy-
namics within atoms, molecules, liquids, solids, and nanoscale materials with unprecedented
temporal resolution [1–7]. By enabling real-time tracking of such ultrafast phenomena, at-
tosecond electron pulses open revolutionary opportunities in ultrafast electron dynamics [8],
chemistry [9], materials science [10–12], ultrafast microscopy [13, 14], free-electron quantum
optics [15], and the generation of high-power attosecond light sources [16].
Existing methods[17–20] for generating attosecond electron pulse trains aimed at these
applications typically operate by imprinting an energy modulation onto the electron beam,
which gradually converts into temporal modulation during drift [21–23]. For an attosecond
electron pulse, Liouville’s theorem dictates that the minimum pulse duration τmin is inversely
proportional to the energy spread ∆W. The longitudinal focal length Ld—which character-
izes both the distance required for attosecond pulse formation and the characteristic length
over which the attosecond pulse duration broadens—can be related to τmin and the Lorentz
factor γ by a simple scaling relation: Ld ∝γ2p

For a given pulse duration, a higher γ enables the attosecond structure to persist over a
significantly longer drift distance. Leveraging recent advances in high-power laser technology
[25, 26], we demonstrate such a mechanism: a compact, laser-based scheme that simultane-
ously modulates an electron beam into attosecond pulse trains—substantially shorter than
previously achievable—while rapidly boosting its Lorentz factor, thereby extending the focal
length by orders of magnitude. Crucially, contrary to established models of ponderomotive
force [18, 20] or stochastic heating [27, 28] in dual-laser fields, we identify a specific para-
metric resonance condition under which the beam enters a previously unidentified stable
modulation regime. This regime combine direct laser acceleration (DLA) [29] with para-
metric resonance [30], enabling both longitudinal modulation and rapid energy gain of the
electron beam.

In practical experiments, the attosecond electron pulse train must be separated from

the modulation region of the two lasers for diagnostic purposes. Based on our previous
analysis, the parallel-polarization configuration imposes lower requirements on laser power.
Moreover, as long as the resonance condition is satisfied at some point during the dual-laser
modulation, an energy modulation is imprinted onto the electron beam. By leveraging the
transverse ponderomotive force of the pump laser [33], the electron beam can be deflected
away from the optical axis while preserving its longitudinal momentum modulation. When
the deflection angle exceeds the laser divergence angle, the beam can be effectively extracted
from the laser modulation region, and subsequently converts into an attosecond pulse train
during drift propagation.
To demonstrate the feasibility of this separation scheme, we perform a final simulation
incorporating realistic laser and beam parameters. Thermionic cathode electron guns typ-
ically operate at currents on the order of µA to mA; therefore, space-charge effects can be
safely neglected in the modulation process for such sources. We consider both the pump
and probe lasers as x-polarized Gaussian beams with identical divergence angles, focused at
the origin. Both lasers and the electron beam are modeled as having finite pulse durations.
Detection is performed at a distance of approximately 1.6 mm from the origin.
The simulation results are shown in Fig. 5. Owing to the transverse ponderomotive
force of the pump laser, the electron beam acquires a transverse momentum, resulting in an
exit angle of approximately 0.35 rad relative to the optical axis—substantially larger than
the laser divergence angles of 0.03 rad, as illustrated in Fig. 5(a). The modulated pulse
train maintains a minimum duration of around 7 as, as shown in Fig. 5(a2). Furthermore,
as the pump laser continuously overtakes the electron beam, the spatial variation of the
laser beam waist leads to position-dependent ponderomotive forces. This gives rise to slight
differences in the average energy and exit angle among individual attosecond pulses after
their formation, as depicted in the momentum-space and energy distributions of Fig. 5(b)
and (c). Nevertheless, the Lorentz factor of each pulse reaches 7.7, corresponding to Ld =
1.6 mm, which is sufficient for subsequent experimental manipulation.
In practice, the required laser sources could be a 20 TW femtosecond laser for the pump
and a 100 kW picosecond laser for the probe. Given that modern advanced laser facilities
already exceed 10 PW in output power [34], our scheme presents very modest requirements
in terms of laser intensity, highlighting its experimental feasibility. Compared to previous
approaches, this method extends Ld by more than two orders of magnitude.
Moreover,

unlike plasma wakefield configurations [35–37], our scheme requires at most two laser beams
and operates without any plasma instability. This significantly reduces the experimental
complexity and unpredictable situations. Beyond its effectiveness for low-γ electron beams,
our analysis demonstrates that the proposed scheme remains viable for beams with higher
γ0. For instance, using a pump wavelength of 1 µm and a probe wavelength of 10 mm, the
parallel configuration of our scheme can modulate electron beams with γ0 ≈100.
In summary, we have proposed a novel scheme that simultaneously generates and ac-
celerates few-attosecond electron pulse trains with tunable periods by using two colliding
laser fields to modulate an electron beam. The mechanism relies on parametric resonance
within the dual-laser system to longitudinally modulate the electron beam, leading to the
formation of attosecond pulse train on a sub-pump-wavelength scale after a specific dura-
tion, while DLA boosts the pulse train to relativistic energies to extend longitudinal focal
length. Simulations with realistic sub-relativistic electron beam parameters confirm the gen-
eration of attosecond electron pulses with durations as short as 1 as, a maximum Lorentz
factor of 15, and a relative energy spread of 0.02%, thereby extending Ld by up to three
orders of magnitude. This work elucidates the critical transition from ordered modulation to
stochastic acceleration in dual-laser systems and provides a viable experimental pathway to
overcoming the long-standing focal-length barrier in attosecond electron-pulse applications.
