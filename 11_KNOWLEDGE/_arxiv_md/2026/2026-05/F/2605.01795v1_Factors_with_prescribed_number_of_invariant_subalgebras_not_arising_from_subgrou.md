YONGLE JIANG AND QINXUAN XU*
Abstract. For any given integer n ≥1, we construct i.c.c. groups G such that the II1
factors L(G) have exactly n-many G-invariant von Neumann subalgebras not arising from
subgroups.
1. Introduction
The study of invariant von Neumann subalgebras in group von Neumann algebras has
received considerable attention in recent years. Motivated by the pioneering works [1,12,24],
Amrutam and the first named author introduced the invariant von Neumann subalgebras
rigidity (ISR) property [5]. A countable discrete group G is said to satisfy the ISR property if
every G-invariant von Neumann subalgebra M ⊆L(G) is of the form L(H) for some normal
subgroup H ◁G, where G acts on L(G) by conjugation. In other words, for groups with
the ISR property, the lattice of G-invariant von Neumann subalgebras of L(G) is completely
determined by the lattice of normal subgroups of G, hence precluding the existence of any
“exotic” invariant subalgebras that do not arise from underlying group-theoretic structure.
It can also be thought of as an ingredient to classify the simplest type, i.e. the Dirac type
of invariant random von Neumann subalgebras, a new concept introduced in [4].
Since its introduction, the ISR property has been established for a broad array of
groups. Amrutam-Jiang originally proved that many “negatively curved” groups possess the
ISR property [5], including all torsion-free non-amenable hyperbolic groups and torsion-free
groups with positive first ℓ2-Betti number under mild assumptions, as well as certain finite
direct products thereof. Subsequent works have extended these results in several directions.
For instance, Chifan-Das-Sun proved that all acylindrically hyperbolic groups with trivial
amenable radical have this ISR property [13]. The first example of an infinite amenable
group with the ISR property was constructed by Jiang-Zhou [23], showing that the finitary
permutation group S∞enjoys this rigidity property. More recently, Dudko-Jiang developed a
character approach to the ISR property [15], which has been proven to be effective in treating

• The case of exactly one exotic invariant subalgebra: In [3], Amrutam-Dudko-Jiang-

ISR property, yet admits precisely one G-invariant von Neumann subalgebra beyond
those arising from normal subgroups. This provides an interesting example where
the failure of rigidity is minimal.
• The case of infinitely many exotic invariant subalgebras: This phenomenon appears
in the works of Jiang-Liu [22] and Jiang-Li [21], who completely classified all G-
invariant von Neumann subalgebras in L(G) for G = Zn ⋊SL(n, Z) with n ≥2.
These examples raise a natural and intriguing question:
Question 1.1. For any given integer n ≥2, can one construct a countable discrete group G
that does not satisfy the ISR property, such that the G-invariant von Neumann subalgebras
in L(G) can be completely classified, and the number of such subalgebras not arising from
subgroups is exactly n?
While it is not hard to construct groups G with 2n-many such exotic invariant subal-
gebras for any n ≥1 using known results (see Remark 4.11), the general case needs a new
construction. In fact, this question can be viewed as an analogue, in the setting of invariant
von Neumann subalgebras, of a classical and important problem in the theory of II1 factors:
Question 1.2. Given any integer n ≥2, can one construct a II1 factor M possessing
exactly n Cartan subalgebras, or exactly n group measure space Cartan subalgebras, up to
automorphism or unitary conjugacy?
This question was largely resolved by Krogager-Vaes [26], who constructed, for every
positive integer n, a class of II1 factors M that admit exactly n group measure space Cartan

Our construction draws inspiration from the works of [3,21,22]. Indeed, the group Gn is
also constructed to be certain semi-direct product group A⋊Γ, where A is abelian, so that we
can explore the algebraic action Γ ↷( bA, Haar) that it induces. But the translation of their
ideas to attack Question 1.1 requires several new technical ingredients from ergodic theory,
field theory, character theory and arithmetic observations. For example, we expect all exotic
invariant subalgebras arise precisely from non-trivial factors of the above algebraic action,
hence we do need a complete classification of all non-trivial factors such that the number of
such factors equals a prescribed number. The starting point is to understand [11, Remark
1.4], see Lemma 3.1 for a detailed proof, which provides us with a natural candidate, i.e.
k ⋊k∗for a suitable chosen field k, to work with.
However, even with the above mentioned candidate at hand, new challenges arise while
trying to classify invariant subalgebras. In particular, we highlight that there exists a new
rigid phenomenon, i.e. Lemma 4.7 that does not seem to arise in all previous works, see Re-
mark 4.8 for more discussion on this. We anticipate that the techniques developed here may
find further applications in the study of intermediate subalgebras and rigidity phenomena
in operator algebras.
The paper is organized as follows: besides this section, there are three more sections. In
Section 2, we collect facts on algebraic actions, torsion subgroups of multiplicative groups

algebraic action k∗↷bk in Section 3. The proof of Theorem A will be given in Section 4, see
Corollary 4.14 and Corollary 4.16. In fact, both of them follow from the full classification of
invariant subalgebras in L(k ⋊k∗) as in Theorem 4.9.
