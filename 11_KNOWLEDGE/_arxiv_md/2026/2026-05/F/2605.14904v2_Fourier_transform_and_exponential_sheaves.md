1. Introduction.
1.1.
This note concerns exponential sheaves, their realizations, and the “universal”
Fourier transform on them. For concreteness, it is written in the De Rham setting of
Hodge modules on complex varieties. However, the arguments apply with minimal
change to ℓ-adic sheaves. In fact, up till (but excluding) Proposition 6.10, the
arguments can be transcribed over to any “reasonable sheaf formalism” (see [GL]
and [CHS] for an independent development of related ideas in the motivic sheaves
formalism).1 The core ideas go at least as far back as N. Katz (see the discussion of
the “over-world” in the tail end of [K-ESDEI, Fourth Lecture]).
1.2.
The definitions of the relevant categories E(X) and the basic tools for working
with them are contained in §3. In §4, the exponential kernel is introduced. Fourier
transform is defined in §5. Invertibility is demonstrated in Theorem 5.3; the Fourier
miracle is Theorem 5.5. Realization functors are discussed in §6. Theorem 6.7 justifies
the “universal” label for our Fourier transform by showing compatibility with its
classical counterparts under realizations. In §7, t-structures are discussed. Theorem
7.2 asserts that realizations are t-exact. Theorem 7.4 demonstrates that realizations
are faithful on the heart.
1.3.
Over a point, the categories considered coincide with those in [KS, Chapter
4]. So the reader may choose to see the E(X) as generalizing the framework of
[KS, Chapter 4] to the relative setting. In particular, one gets the formalism of
Grothendieck’s operations, notions of exponential/rapid decay cohomology with
coefficients, intersection cohomology, etc. However, I have not pursued this.
1.4.
My motivation comes from attempting to understand the cross characteristic
“analogies” between various exponential sums, as in [K]. Informally, objects in the
categories E(X) defined below are classes of the usual objects in the derived category
of sheaves D(X × Ga). Given a classical object K ∈D(X × Ga), its class in E(X) should
be thought of as representing the classical object:
πX!(K ⊗µ∗et) ∈D(X).

available standard exponential sheaf. The categories E(X) are equipped with a
natural “exponential kernel” (the object E in §4) that always exists and maps to the
exponential sheaf defining any particular realization. The point is that the exponential
kernel E and the Fourier transform on the categories E(−) exists even when a classical
canonical exponential sheaf does not: Artin-Schreier sheaves are char p objects and
there are p −1 of them over the prime field Fp with no reasonable way to choose
amongst them compatibly across p; the exponential D-module is irregular and does
not underlie a mixed Hodge module; no adequate local system on A1(C) exists in
the classical topology.
Most of these constructions can be made essentially verbatim for ℓ-adic sheaves
on schemes over Z[1/ℓ].2 The objects of E(X) and the Fourier transform below
are then “universal” versions of the classical notions, specializing to them under
realizations. The E(X) are thus vehicles for cross-characteristic comparisons: a direct
comparison via classical categories and the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence is
generally not possible because the relevant ℓ-adic sheaves/D-modules usually have
wild ramification/irregular singularities.
