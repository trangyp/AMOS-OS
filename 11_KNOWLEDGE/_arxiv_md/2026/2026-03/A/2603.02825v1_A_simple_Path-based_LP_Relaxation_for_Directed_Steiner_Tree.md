A simple Path-based LP Relaxation for Directed Steiner Tree
Kanstantsin Pashkovicha, Marta Pozzib, Laura Sanitàb
aUniversity of Waterloo, Department of Combinatorics & Optimization,
bBocconi University, Department of Computing Sciences,
Abstract
We study the Directed Steiner Tree (DST) problem in layered graphs through a simple path-based linear pro-
gramming relaxation. This relaxation achieves an integrality gap of O(ℓlog k), where k is the number of ter-
minals and ℓis the number of layers, which matches the best known bounds for DST previously obtained via
lift-and-project hierarchies. Our formulation bypasses hierarchy machinery, offering a more transparent route to
the state-of-the-art bound, and it can be exploited to provide an alternative simpler proof that O(ℓ) rounds of the
Sherali–Adams hierarchy suffice for reducing the integrality gap on layered instances of DST.
Keywords:
Directed Steiner Tree, LP Relaxation, Sheraly-Adams hierarchy
1. Introduction
We study the Directed Steiner Tree (DST) problem,
where we are given a directed graph G = (V, E) with
nonnegative edge costs ce, e ∈E, a root node r ∈V
and a collection of terminals T ⊆V. The goal is to
find the cheapest collection of edges such that all the
terminals are connected to the root, i.e., there is a path
from r to every terminal in T. The nodes in V \(T ∪{r})
are called Steiner nodes. Here, we let n = |V| , m = |E|,
and k = |T|.
Our work aligns with prior progress on linear pro-
gramming formulations for the Directed Steiner Tree
(DST) problem. There is a natural edge-formulation
for DST that has integrality gap Ω(
√

of works has developed increasingly stronger formula-
tions that, for so-called ℓ-layered instances of DST, re-
duce this gap from Ω(
√

in this series is due to Rothvoss [6], who studied the
application of the Lasserre hierarchy [4]. He showed
that O(ℓ) rounds of Lasserre hierachy suffice to achieve
the integrality gap O(ℓlog k). Later Friggstad et al.[2]
proved that ℓ-rounds of the Sherali-Adams hierarchy
[7] and 2ℓ-rounds of the Lovasz-Schriver hierarchy
[5] suffice to reduce the integrality gap to O(ℓlog k).
Note that the Sherali-Adams hierarchy [7] and Lovasz-
Schriver hierarchy [5] are substantially simpler than
the Lasserre hierarchy [4]. Thus the resulting formu-
lations in [2] can be seen as substantially simpler than
the one in [6].
In this work, we present a very simple formulation
for DST with O(nℓ) variables that achieves an integral-
ity gap of O(ℓlog k), showing that the use of hierarchy-
based frameworks is not necessary to attain the cur-
rent state-of-the-art bound. Our approach builds on
ideas from [2], in particular the reduction of DST to
instances of the Group Steiner Tree (GST) problem,
but leads to a significantly more direct formulation and
analysis. Rather than invoking hierarchy machinery,
we map our relaxation for DST into a compact ex-
tended formulation for the resulting GST instance.
Using known results for GST [3], we immediately
obtain the same O(ℓlog k) integrality gap for our for-
mulation. We then provide an elementary proof of the
fact that this relaxation is also an implicit relaxation for
O(ℓ) rounds of the Sherali–Adams hierarchy. As a con-
sequence, our work yields an alternative and substan-
tially more straightforward proof that Sherali–Adams
hierarchies suffice to achieve the O(ℓlog k) integrality
gap for ℓ-layered instances of DST.
1.1. Problem Definition and Notations.
We focus on layered instances of the Directed
Steiner Tree (DST) problem. We say that an instance
G = (V, E) of DST with terminals T is ℓ-layered if V
can be partitioned into V0, V1, ..., Vℓsuch that
(i) V0 = {r} and Vℓ= T
(ii) for every edge uv ∈E we have u ∈Vi and v ∈
Vi+1 for some 0 ≤i < ℓ.
