An antichain condition for infinite groups ∗
Mattia Brescia, Bernardo Di Siena, Alessio Russo
Abstract
Let χ be a subgroup-theoretical property. We introduce an antichain condition ACχ
which forbids the existence of infinite antichains of mutually permutable non-χ subgroups
whose infinite joins remain non-χ. This is a “width” analogue of the real chain condition
on non-χ subgroups, and it extends the usual hierarchy of weak chain conditions (double
chain condition, deviation, and RCC).
Our main results show that, within the universe of generalized radical groups, the
antichain condition is as rigid as the corresponding chain conditions. For the properties χ of
normality, almost normality, near normality, permutability, modularity, and pronormality,
we prove that a generalized radical group satisfies ACχ if and only if it satisfies RCC on
non-χ subgroups; equivalently, it satisfies any of the standard weak chain conditions on
non-χ subgroups. In particular, we obtain minimax-type dichotomies: either the group is
minimax, or every subgroup satisfies χ. This yields characterizations in terms of Dedekind
groups, quasi-Hamiltonian groups, groups with modular subgroup lattice, and T-groups.
In the pronormal case, one has to deal with locally finite simple groups and a use of the
Classification of Finite Simple Groups seems unavoidable.
Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): Primary 20E15, 20F22; Secondary 20F19, 20F24.
Keywords: antichain condition; subgroup lattice; chain conditions; FC-groups; locally finite
simple groups.

Introduction
Finiteness conditions on lattices of subgroups have long been a driving force in infinite group
theory. Beyond the classical maximal and minimal conditions, several weak chain conditions
have proved particularly effective in forcing strong structural constraints on soluble and gener-
alized soluble groups. Among the most studied are the double chain condition, the weak double
chain condition DCC −∞, deviation, and the real chain condition RCC.
Recall that a poset Λ satisfies (weak) double chain condition if it contains no subposet with
the order type of Z (where each subgroup has infinite index in its successor). It has deviation
if it contains no subposet with the order type of Q (see, for instance, [24, 6.1.13] and [19]),
and it satisfies RCC if it contains no subposet with the order type of R. These conditions can
be imposed not only on the full subgroup lattice, but also on the family of subgroups failing a

A systematic investigation of weak chain conditions on restricted systems of subgroups
has been recently carried out in a series of papers. Beyond the double chain condition on
subnormal subgroups and on non-subnormal subgroups, several natural classes of χ-subgroups
were treated, including abelian and non-abelian subgroups [1, 2], and non-pronormal subgroups
[9]. These results highlight a recurring phenomenon in generalized radical groups: weak chain
conditions typically collapse to minimax-type alternatives, often forcing the group to belong
to an extremal class where every subgroup satisfies the relevant property. Related “extremal”
subgroup constraints involving pronormality (for instance, metahamiltonian and abelian-or-
pronormal groups) are studied in [7, 8].
More recently, Dardano and De Mari introduced and studied the real chain condition on
non-χ subgroups, showing that it provides a flexible framework that simultaneously generalizes
DCC −∞and deviation, yet remains strong enough to yield sharp structure theorems for
generalized radical groups [13, 14]. The present paper complements this “depth” perspective
by introducing a “width” analogue based on antichains, generalizing and sharpening most of
their theorems.
An antichain condition. Let χ be a subgroup property. We say that a group G satisfies
ACχ if it does not contain an infinite antichain (Hλ)λ∈Λ of mutually permutable subgroups
such that
1. for every λ ∈Λ, Hλ ̸≤W

2. for every infinite subfamily {Hλ | λ ∈Λ0}, the join W

By construction, ACχ is weaker than RCC on non-χ subgroups; in fact, we prove in Lemma 2.2
that RCC implies ACχ.
In this paper, we want to give the broadest setup in which all the aforementioned chain
condition live, showing how lattice “width” is indeed a new, more general way to look at lattice
“depth” in infinite groups.
Scope and main results. We work throughout in the class of generalized radical groups,
i.e. groups possessing an ascending normal series whose factors are locally finite or locally
nilpotent. This class is large enough to cover most of the classical classes of groups satisfying
finiteness conditions, yet rigid enough for the standard pathological classes of groups (e.g.
Tarski monsters) to be avoided.
Our first set of applications concerns the properties of being almost normal and nearly nor-
mal. In Section 3 we prove that, for generalized radical groups, ACan and ACnn are equivalent
to DCC −∞on non-(almost normal) and non-(nearly normal) subgroups, respectively, and
hence to RCC and deviation on the same systems; equivalently, every non-minimax subgroup
must be almost normal (resp. nearly normal). This is proved in Theorem 3.4 and recovers and
sharpens the minimax alternative established for the corresponding chain conditions, and it
connects with the study of groups having few subnormal non-normal subgroups .
We then specialize to normality. As an application of the result just stated, in the same
section we obtain that, for generalized radical groups, ACn is equivalent to DCC −∞on non-
normal subgroups and to the other weak chain conditions on non-normal subgroups; moreover,
it forces a strong dichotomy: the group is either minimax or Dedekind (Theorem 3.5).

Section 4 is devoted to the properties of being permutable (quasinormal) and modular. Here

Finally, Section 5 treats pronormal subgroups. Besides the same minimax-versus-extremal

is valid, the proof given there contain gaps in the passage to the infinite locally finite case.
Our Proposition 5.6 fills this gap using detailed information on families of finite simple groups;
accordingly, the Classification of Finite Simple Groups enters the argument.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 establishes general tools for extracting “good”
subgroups from infinite direct products under ACχ, and records the basic implications holding
among the different chain conditions. Sections 3–5 apply this tools to the subgroup properties
listed above and provide the corresponding structure theorems.

Preliminaries
We begin with the following remark about the hierarchy of chain condition classes. Its straight-
forward proof can be found in [13].
Remark 2.1. Let Λ be a poset of subgroups of a group G. The following hold:
1) If Λ satisfies DCC −∞, then Λ has deviation.
2) If Λ has deviation, then Λ satisfies RCC.
3) If Λ satisfies RCC and is complete, then Λ has deviation.
The RCC is the weakest of these conditions. However, the following lemma shows that the
antichain conditions are a further generalization of the real chain conditions.
Lemma 2.2. Let χ be a subgroup property. Then the class of groups satisfying the RCC on
χ-subgroups is a subclass of that of groups satisfying ACχ.
Proof. Let (Hλ)λ∈Λ be an infinite antichain of mutually permutable subgroups of G such that:
1. For every λ ∈Λ, Hλ ̸≤W
γ∈Λ\{λ} Hγ;
2. The join of every infinite subfamily of (Hλ)λ∈Λ is not a χ-subgroup of G.
Assume without loss of generality that it is countable, and index it as (Hq)q∈Q. For all r ∈R,
define Kr = ⟨Hq | q < r, q ∈Λ⟩.
Due to the properties of (Hλ)λ∈Λ, the family (Kr)r∈R
constitutes a real chain of non-χ-subgroups of G.

We aim to apply these results to subgroup properties that generalize normality within the
universe of radical groups and generalized radical groups. Recall that a group G is radical if it
possesses a (normal) ascending series with locally nilpotent factors. A group G is generalized
radical if it possesses a (normal) ascending series with factors that are locally finite or locally
nilpotent. In most cases, we can reduce the study of generalized radical groups with ACχ to
the radical-by-finite case, as in [13].
Proposition 2.6. Let X be a class of groups closed under taking subgroups and homomorphic
images, such that any locally finite X-group is soluble-by-finite. Then any generalized radical
X-group is a radical-by-finite X-group.
3
Almost normal and nearly normal subgroups
We now apply the general strategy from the preliminaries to the posets of almost normal and
nearly normal subgroups. Let G be a group and H a subgroup of G. The subgroup H is said
to be almost normal in G if the normalizer NG(H) has finite index in G. We denote this by
H an G. On the other hand, the subgroup H is said to be nearly normal in G if it has finite
index in its normal closure HG. We denote this by H nn G. One obvious, yet important fact
is that if a cyclic subgroup of G is either almost normal or nearly normal, then it is contained
in the FC-centre of G.
It is easy to verify that the properties an and nn satisfy conditions (i) and (ii) of Lemma
2.4.
Let G be a group. The set of all FC-elements of G is a subgroup and is usually denoted by
FC(G). If G is abelian, the number r = r0(G) + P
p∈P rp(G) will be said to be the total rank
of G, where r0 and rp are the usual Pr¨ufer torsion-free rank and p-rank of G, respectively.
Proposition 3.1. Let G be a group satisfying ACan (resp. ACnn). If G is an FC-group, then
G is central-by-finite (resp. finite-by-abelian).
Proof. Since G′ and G/Z(G) are periodic, we can assume without loss of generality that G is
periodic. Let A be an abelian subgroup of G. If A is not ˇCernikov, then A has a homomorphic
image of infinite total rank. Thus, by Lemma 2.3(i) with L = 1, A is almost normal (resp.
nearly normal) in G. If on the other hand A is a ˇCernikov group, then its finite residual is
contained in Z(G) and hence A/AG is finite, so by Dietzmann’s Lemma, AG/AG is finite, too,

implying that A is both almost normal and nearly normal in G. The result then follows from
the well-known results of Eremin (see [18]) and Tomkinson (see [29]).
We begin by isolating a structural obstruction to the antichain conditions, namely the fact
that the centre of the FC-centre has infinite total rank. This will be used repeatedly to force
finiteness conditions on suitable subgroups and on sections of G.
Theorem 3.2. Let G be a group satisfying ACan (resp. ACnn). If Z(FC(G)) has infinite
total rank, then G is central-by-finite (resp. finite-by-abelian).
Proof. Let Z = Z(FC(G)), let T be the torsion subgroup of Z and let D = Dri∈N Ci be a
direct product of cyclic subgroups of Z, which exists by hypothesis.
Case 1: G satisfies ACan.
By Lemma 2.5, every subgroup of D is almost normal in G. In particular, for every finite
subgroup F of D, F G is finitely generated and, writing D = F G × E, EG has still infinite
total rank; then D is the direct product of infinitely many G-invariant subgroups. Then every
cyclic subgroup of G is almost normal by Lemma 2.3(iii) and hence G is an FC-group. The
conclusion now follows from Proposition 3.1.
Case 2: G satisfies ACnn.
By Lemma 2.5, every subgroup of D is nearly normal in G. Let TD be the torsion subgroup
of D. If D/TD has infinite rank, one easily gets that every subgroup of D/TD is normal in
G, so G/TD is an FC-group by Lemma 2.5, and G′TD/TD is finite by Proposition 3.1. In
particular, if TD is finite, we are done, so we may assume that TD is infinite. In this case,
[11, Lemma 2.7] yields the existence of a finite G-invariant subgroup N of T such that every
subgroup of T/N is normal in G, so, as before, G′ is finite.
The next theorem is the first global step: it shows that, in the presence of ACan (or ACnn),
generalized radical groups are forced into a minimax-type alternative.
This will be found
frequently in the rest of the paper.
Theorem 3.3. Let G be a group satisfying ACan (resp. ACnn).
Proof. Let A be an abelian non-minimax subgroup of G, which we may find by [25, Theorem
10.35], and let B be a free subgroup of A such that A/B is periodic. If B has infinite total rank,
then by 2.5 it is contained in FC(G). Since FC-groups are central-by-periodic, Z(FC(G)) has
infinite total rank and the thesis follows from Theorem 3.2. Thus, we can suppose that B is
finitely generated, so that A/B has an infinite socle S/B. Now, Lemma 2.4(i) yields that B is
almost normal (resp. nearly normal). If B is nearly normal, |BG : B| = n, say, we have that
(BG)n! ≤B, so BG/BG has finite exponent and hence it is finite. Therefore, in any case B is
almost normal. In particular, C = BG is finitely generated and NG(B)/B is finite-by-abelian
by Theorem 3.2. Then (C ∩NG(B))/B is polycyclic-by-finite and so is C. However, since C
is finitely generated, SC/C is still infinite and hence by Theorem 3.2 we have that G′C/C is
finite and so G′ is polycyclic-by-finite. In particular, every non-minimax subgroup H of G has
polycyclic-by-finite commutator subgroup and hence H/H′ contains a homomorphic image of
infinite total rank. Therefore, H is almost normal in G by Lemma 2.3(i) with L = 1.
We now prove the main theorem of this section.
Theorem 3.4. Let G be a generalized radical group. The following conditions are equivalent:

(ii) G satisfies Max-∞on non-almost normal (resp. non-nearly normal) subgroups;
(iii) G satisfies DCC −∞on non-almost normal (resp. non-nearly normal) subgroups;

(v) G satisfies RCC on non-almost normal (resp. non-nearly normal) subgroups;

We now obtain the corresponding result for normal subgroups.
Theorem 3.5. Let G be a generalized radical group. The following conditions are equivalent:
(i) G satisfies Min-∞on non-normal subgroups;
(ii) G satisfies Max-∞on non-normal subgroups;
(iii) G satisfies DCC −∞on non-normal subgroups;
(iv) G has normal deviation;
(v) G satisfies RCC on non-normal subgroups;
(vi) G is minimax or Dedekind;
(vii) G satisfies ACn.
Proof. By [13, Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 3.11], the first 6 conditions are equivalent, and
they all imply (vii) by Lemma 2.2. Let us suppose (vii) holds. Certainly, ACn implies ACnn,
so G is radical-by-finite by the previous theorem. Suppose that G is not minimax, so that we
may find an abelian non-minimax subgroup A of G. Let B be a torsion-free subgroup of A
such that A/B is periodic. If B has infinite rank, by Lemma 2.4(i) and Lemma 2.5 all cyclic
subgroups of G are normal, so G is a Dedekind group, so we may assume that B is finitely
generated. However, this means that for every integer k > 2, A/B2k has an infinite socle.
Again by Lemma 2.4(i) and Lemma 2.5, G/B2k is a Dedekind group for every k > 2 and hence
G is abelian.

Permutable and modular subgroups
We next consider the antichain conditions restricted to permutable and modular subgroups.
Although these classes are not, in general, subgroup-closed, the relevant posets still interact
well with subgroups in generalized radical groups, so that one can push the minimax alternative
obtained earlier to these settings.
Let G be a group and K a subgroup of G. The subgroup K is said to be permutable (or
quasinormal) in G (denoted K per G) if for every subgroup H of G, HK = KH. Recall that
a group is called quasi-Hamiltonian if all its subgroups are permutable.
Proposition 4.1. Let G be a group satisfying ACper. If G is radical-by-finite, then every
non-minimax subgroup of G is permutable in G.
Proof. Let H be a non-minimax subgroup of G. Since H is radical-by-finite, H contains an
abelian non-minimax subgroup A by [25, Theorem 10.35]. Then A has a homomorphic image
A/B that is the direct product of an infinite collection of non-trivial subgroups. Write now
A/B = Dri∈I Ai/B, where each Ai/B is infinite. Then by Lemma 2.4, every Ai is permutable
in G. Let x be an element of H \ B. We can select an infinite sub-collection of (Ai/B)i∈I
generating a subgroup Ax/B such that ⟨x⟩∩Ax ≤B. By Lemma 2.3(ii), there exists a subgroup
bAx of Ax containing B which is permutable in G, such that ⟨x⟩bAx is permutable in G. Since
H is generated by the join of such subgroups, H is permutable in G.
The following characterization sharpens the previous equivalence by isolating the exact role
played by the Hirsch–Plotkin radical and by minimax sections. It will be the key input when
we turn to permutable and modular subgroups.
Theorem 4.2. Let G be a generalized radical group. The following conditions are equivalent:
(i) G satisfies Min-∞on non-permutable subgroups;
(ii) G satisfies Max-∞on non-permutable subgroups;
(iii) G satisfies DCC −∞on non-permutable subgroups;
(iv) G has permutable deviation;
(v) G satisfies RCC on non-permutable subgroups;
(vi) G is minimax or quasi-Hamiltonian;
(vii) Every non-minimax subgroup of G is permutable in G;
(viii) G satisfies ACper.
Proof. By [13, Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 4.6], the first 6 conditions are equivalent and
clearly (i) and (vii) are equivalent. By Lemma 2.2, they all imply (viii). Assume (viii) holds
and suppose first that G is locally finite and not ˇCernikov, so that we may find a subgroup S
which is the direct product of infinitely many non-trivial cyclic subgroups. Write S = Dri∈Λ Si,
where each Si is infinite. Then by Lemma 2.4, every Si is permutable in G. Let now x and
y be two elements of G and assume without loss of generality that ⟨x, y⟩∩S = 1. Lemma
2.3(ii) makes it possible to find a subgroup bS = ⟨Sγ⟩γ∈Γ of S for some Γ ⊂Λ such that ⟨x⟩bS
is permutable in G. Clearly, s every Si is permutable in G, also bS is such. Now it easily

We now move to modular subgroups. Let G be a group and K a subgroup of G. The
subgroup K is said to be modular in G (denoted K mod G) if for all subgroups H, L of G:
• If H ≤L, then L ∩⟨H, K⟩= ⟨H, K ∩L⟩;
• If K ≤H, then L ∩⟨H, K⟩= ⟨K, L ∩H⟩.
Recall that the property of being modular is join-closed (see for instance [27, Theorem
2.1.6(d)]). In the following, we will use Theorem 2.4.11 and Theorem 2.4.14 in [27] to refer to
the classification of quasi-Hamiltonian groups made by Iwasawa. Recall also that a subgroup
is permutable if and only if it is modular and ascendant.
Theorem 4.3. Let G be a generalized radical group. The following conditions are equivalent:
(i) G satisfies Min-∞on non-modular subgroups;
(ii) G satisfies Max-∞on non-modular subgroups;
(iii) G satisfies DCC −∞on non-modular subgroups;
(iv) G has modular deviation;
(v) G satisfies RCC on non-modular subgroups;
(vi) Every non-minimax subgroup of G is modular in G;
(vii) G is minimax or has a modular subgroup lattice;
(viii) G satisfies ACmod.
Proof. By [13] and [16], the first 7 conditions are equivalent. By Lemma 2.2, they all imply
(viii). Suppose (viii) holds and that G is not minimax. Assume first that G is locally finite,
let X be a finite subgroup of G and let x be an element of X. A result of Zaiˇcev (see [30])
ensures that we may find an abelian X-invariant subgroup E of G which is the direct product
of countably many cyclic groups. Clearly, we assume without loss of generality that E ∩X = 1
Since X is finite, we may write E = Dri∈N Ei, where each Ei is X-invariant. Then we may
apply Lemma 2.3(ii) to find an X-invariant subgroup bE of E which is modular in G and such
that bE⟨x⟩is modular in G. Then bE⟨x⟩∩X = ⟨bE ∩X, x⟩= ⟨x⟩. This shows that every cyclic
subgroup of X is modular in X and hence L(X) is modular. By the generality of X, it follows
that L(G) is modular, too, and this means that G is metabelian by the already quoted result
by Iwasawa. An application of Proposition 2.6 yields that in general G is radical-by-finite.
Let now H be a non-minimax subgroup of G, and let A be a non-minimax subgroup of H.
Then we may find a subgroup B of A such that A/B is the direct product of infinitely many
non-trivial subgroups. Let now h be an element of H \ B. Clearly, B can be taken in a way
that A/B has still infinite total rank and that ⟨h⟩∩A ≤B. Now, like before, we may apply
Lemma 2.3(ii) to find an ⟨h⟩-invariant subgroup bAh of A which is modular in G and such that
bAh⟨h⟩is modular in G. From this we have that H = ⟨⟨bAh⟨h⟩⟩| h ∈H \ B⟩is modular in G
and we have that L(G) is modular by [15].

Pronormal subgroups
Finally we deal with pronormal subgroups. Here the poset is typically more rigid, and the
antichain condition forces strong restrictions on the Hirsch–Plotkin radical. One main obstacle
will be in fact dealing with groups with trivial Hirsch-Plotkin radical, but in that case it will
be shown that on can always produce forbidden antichains, provided that the group is locally
finite and not soluble-by-finite.
Let G be a group and H a subgroup of G. The subgroup H is said to be pronormal in G if,
for every element g ∈G, the subgroups H and Hg are conjugate in their join ⟨H, Hg⟩. Recall
here that a group G is said to be a T-group if it lacks in subnormal non-normal subgroups;
moreover, G is called a T-group if every subgroup of G is a T-group. Recall also that a locally
(soluble-by-finite) group G is a T-group if and only if every cyclic subgroup of G is pronormal
(see for instance [9, Corollary 2.1]).
We first show that ACpr rules out infinite total rank in a natural family of sections of
the Hirsch–Plotkin radical. This is a crucial step towards the final minimax alternative for
radical-by-finite groups under ACpr.
Theorem 5.1. Let G be a group satisfying ACpr. If the Hirsch-Plotkin radical R of G has
infinite total rank, then G is a T-group.
Proof. Since R is locally nilpotent, it satisfies ACn. Thus, by Theorem 3.5, R is a Dedekind
group.
So, R contains a direct product D of infinitely many non-trivial cyclic subgroups
and hence by Lemma 2.4(ii) every cyclic subgroup of R is normal in G. In particular, every
subgroup of R is normal in G. Let H and K two subgroups of G such that H is subnormal in
K and assume first that H ∩D is finitely generated. Then we can assume that D ∩H = 1.
Write now D = D1 × D2, where D1 and D2 are both of infinite total rank. Then by Lemma
2.3(ii) there exist subgroups bD1 ≤D1 and bD2 ≤D2 which are pronormal in G. This implies
that bD1H and bD2H are pronormal in bD1K and bD2K, respectively. However, since they are
also subnormal in those groups, they are even normal. From this it follows that K normalizes
both bD1H and bD2H and hence also H = ( bD1H) ∩( bD2H) is normal in K.
Assume now that H ∩D is infinitely generated and let x be an element of G\R. By Lemma
2.3(ii), there exists a subgroup Dx of H ∩D such that Dx⟨x⟩is pronormal in G. It follows that
in the quotient G/(H ∩D), all cyclic subgroups are pronormal. Thus, G/(H ∩D) is a T-group
(see [9, Corollary 2.1]). Since H/(H ∩D) is subnormal in K/(H ∩D), it is normal, and also
in this case H is normal in K.
Lemma 5.2. Let G be a locally radical T-group satisfying ACpr. Then every subgroup of G is
pronormal in G.
Proof. Let H be a finitely generated subgroup of G. Since a locally nilpotent T-group is a
Dedekind group, any section of H contains a normal abelian subgroup, so H is hyperabelian.
Then G is locally soluble and hence even metabelian, since soluble T-groups are metabelian
(see [26]). If G is non-periodic, then G is abelian, so we can assume that G is periodic. Let
L = [G′, G]. Then π(L)∩π(G/L) = ∅and 2 /∈π(L). Let π = π(G/L), and let P be a Sylow π-
subgroup of G. Note that PL is a normal subgroup of G and P ∼= PL/L. Since P is nilpotent,
it satisfies ACn. If P is not a ˇCernikov group, it is a Dedekind group, so, by Lemma 2.3(i), P
is pronormal in G and hence G = NG(P)L, which is in contradiction to the fact that obviously
NG(P)/P is a π′-group. Therefore, P is a ˇCernikov group. Let now F/PL be a non-trivial

We can now combine the previous structural restrictions with the radical-by-finite hypoth-
esis to obtain a clean dichotomy. This mirrors the pattern already met for the other classes
of subgroups, and completes the parallel between the different antichain conditions studied in
the paper.
Theorem 5.3. Let G be a radical-by-finite group satisfying ACpr. Then G is minimax or all
its subgroups are pronormal in G.
Proof. By Lemma 5.2, it suffices to prove that if G is not minimax, then G is a T-group.
Assume G is not minimax, so that the Hirsch-Plotkin radical R of G is not minimax, too.
Since in a locally nilpotent group pronormality and normality coincide, R is a Dedekind group
by Theorem 3.5. If R has infinite total rank, then by Theorem 5.1, G is a T-group, we can
suppose that R has finite total rank. In particular, R is abelian, R = T × A, where T is the
torsion subgroup of R, which is a ˇCernikov group, and A is not minimax.
Let x be an element of infinite order in R and let F be a free abelian subgroup of R
containing x such that R/F is periodic. Let L be a subgroup of finite index in F containing
x. Then R/L has infinite total rank, so L is normal in G by Lemma 2.4(ii). Since R has
finite total rank, F is finitely generated, so ⟨x⟩equals the intersection of all the subgroups of
finite index of F containing it. In particular, ⟨x⟩is normal in G. Since R is generated by its
aperiodic elements, it follows that every subgroup of R is normal in G.
Suppose now for a contradiction that G is not abelian. Then CG(R) = R has index 2 in G,
and every element outside R acts as the inversion on R. Write G = ⟨g⟩R where g2 belongs to
R. Let V be a free abelian subgroup of R such that R/V is periodic, and let W = V 4. Then
R/W has infinite total rank, so G/W is a T-group again by Theorem 5.1. However, ⟨g⟩W is
clearly subnormal and not normal in G/W and this gives a contradiction.
Corollary 5.4. Let G be a locally radical group satisfying ACpr. If G has a finitely generated
subgroup that is not minimax, then all subgroups of G are pronormal in G.
Proof. Let X be a finitely generated subgroup of G that is not minimax. By Theorem 5.3, X
is a T-group. Since being a T-group is a local property, every finitely generated subgroup of G
is a T-group, implying G is a T-group. By Lemma 5.2, every subgroup of G is pronormal.
Corollary 5.5. Let G be a locally radical group of finite total rank satisfying ACpr. Then G
is minimax or all its subgroups are pronormal in G.
Proof. If there exists a finitely generated subgroup of G that is not minimax, the result follows
from Corollary 5.4. Suppose all finitely generated subgroups are minimax. Since G has finite
total rank, by Lemma 10.39 of [25], G is hyperabelian (and thus radical). The result follows
from Theorem 5.3.

Suppose now that G is an infinite locally finite group and let L be the locally soluble radical
of G. We claim that L is soluble. Assuming that L does not satisfy the minimal condition,
a theorem of ˇCernikov (see, for instance, [25, Theorem 3.45]) yields that L does not satisfy
the minimal condition on abelian subgroups. Let E be a finite subgroup of L. Then, by a
result of Zaicev ([30]) we may find an E-invariant abelian subgroup A of L not satisfying the
minimal condition. Exchanging A with its socle, we may assume that A is the direct product of
infinitely many non-trivial cyclic subgroups. Since E is finite, we may take A∩E = 1 and write
A as the direct product of infinitely many E-invariant subgroups. Let now F be a subnormal
subgroup of E. An application of Lemma 2.3 (ii) makes it possible to find an E-invariant
subgroup bA of A such that F bA is pronormal in G. In particular, F = (F bA) ∩E is pronormal
in E and hence normal in it. This shows that E is a T-group, so that E is metabelian and
hence L itself it such.
Finally, assume for a contradiction that G/L is infinite and let S be any Sylow 2-subgroup
of G, which does not satisfy the minimal condition by the first part of the proof and [21, Lemma

shows that G is locally soluble, which leads to the contradiction G = L.
