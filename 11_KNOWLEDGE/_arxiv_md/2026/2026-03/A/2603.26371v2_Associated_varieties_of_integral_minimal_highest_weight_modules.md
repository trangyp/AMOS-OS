ASSOCIATED VARIETIES OF INTEGRAL MINIMAL
HIGHEST WEIGHT MODULES
ZHANQIANG BAI, JING JIANG*, AND RUI WANG
Abstract. Let g be a complex simple Lie algebra and L(λ) be a highest
weight module of g with highest weight λ−ρ, where ρ is half the sum of
positive roots. A simple g-module Lw := L(−wρ) is called integral min-
imal if the associated variety of its annihilator ideal equals the closure
of the minimal special nilpotent orbit. In this paper, we find that the
associated variety of any integral minimal module Lw is irreducible and
equal to the orbital variety corresponding to the minimal length element
in the Kazhdan–Lusztig right cell containing w.
Keywords: Highest weight module; associated variety; nilpotent orbit;
Schubert variety; Kazhdan–Lusztig cell.

Introduction
Let G be a simple Lie Group. Let g be its simple complex Lie algebra
and h be a Cartan subalgebra.
For any finitely generated U(g)-module
M, Bernstein [10] defined the associated variety V (M) in the dual space
g∗, whose dimension coincides with the Gelfand–Kirillov (GK) dimension
of M [36]. This variety is a useful invariant in the study of U(g)-modules,
especially for highest weight modules, see for example [15, 35, 30, 29, 38].
But in general, computing this invariant for an irreducible module is a highly
nontrivial problem.
Let Φ+ ⊆Φ be the set of positive roots determined by a Borel subalgebra b
of g. Denote by Π the set of simple roots in Φ+. Fix a Borel subgroup B ⊆G
corresponding to b. We have a triangular decomposition g = n ⊕h ⊕n−.
Choose a subset J ⊆Π. Then it generates a subsystem ΦJ ⊆Φ. Let pJ be the
standard parabolic subalgebra corresponding to J with Levi decomposition
pJ = lJ ⊕uJ. For a Φ+(lJ)-dominant integral weight λ ∈h∗, let F(λ) be the
irreducible lJ-module with highest weight λ −ρ, where ρ is half the sum of
positive roots. Viewing F(λ) as a pJ = lJ ⊕u+
J -module (with the nilradical
acting trivially), we define the generalized Verma module by
NJ(λ) := U(g) ⊗U(pJ) F(λ).
Let L(λ) denote the irreducible quotient of NJ(λ), which is a highest weight
module of g with highest weight λ −ρ.
Denote by W the Weyl group of Φ. Let Lw denote the simple highest
weight g-module with highest weight −wρ −ρ, where w ∈W. Joseph [21]
found that the associated variety V (Lw) is a union of orbital varieties, which
are defined as follows. For a nilpotent G-orbit O ⊆g, the irreducible com-
ponents of O ∩n are called the orbital varieties of O. Each such component
can be expressed as V(w) = B(n ∩wn) for some w ∈W. The associated
variety V (Lw) is said to be irreducible if it consists of a single orbital vari-
ety. For highest weight Harish-Chandra modules L(λ), the associated variety
V (L(λ)) has been characterized in Bai–Xiao–Xie [6] and Bai–Hunziker–Xie–
Zierau [3]. In general, however, the structure of V (Lw) or V (L(λ)) remains
unknown; see, for example, [15, 35, 38].
As noted in [15], the associated variety V (Lw) is intimately connected to
Schubert varieties. In the following, we recall some of these relations.
The flag variety G/B decomposes into a disjoint union of Schubert cells
BwB/B, where w is an element in the corresponding Weyl group W. The
Schubert varieties X(w) = BwB/B are the closures of the Schubert cells.
Schubert varieties are fundamental objects in algebraic geometry, represen-
tation theory, and combinatorics. X(w) is said to be smooth if it is a smooth
algebraic variety at every point, and rationally smooth if the Poincar´e poly-
nomial pw(t) = P

Investigations into the smoothness and rational smoothness of Schubert
varieties have been a major focus since the seminal work of Lakshmibai and
Sandhya [26], who provided a combinatorial characterization for smoothness
in type A: a Schubert variety X(w) ⊆SL(n)/B is smooth precisely when
the permutation w ∈Sn avoids the patterns 3412 and 4231. This result was
subsequently generalized to smoothness and rational smoothness in other
classical types by Billey [11]. Then Billey and Postnikov [12] developed a uni-
fied method based on root subsystems and embeddings. They demonstrated
that, for any semisimple G, the (rational) smoothness of X(w) ⊆G/B is
determined by pattern avoidance with respect to a finite list of patterns
originating from stellar root subsystems (e.g., B2, G2, A3, B3, C3, D4). From
now on, the corresponding Weyl group element w for a smooth Schubert
variety X(w) is called a smooth element.
We refer to [23] or §2.3 for the definition of Kazhdan–Lusztig right (resp.
left and two-sided) cell equivalence relation and use ∼R (resp. ∼L and ∼LR)
to denote the right (resp. left and two-sided) cell equivalence relation. We
use CR(w) (resp. CL(w) and CLR(w)) to denote a KL right (resp. left and
two-sided) cell which contains w.
From [21], we know that the associated variety V (Lw) is constant on each
KL right cell. It is also known that the associated variety V (Lw) = V(w)
if the Schubert variety X(w) is smooth, see for example [15, Cor. 4.3.2].
Therefore, the associated variety V (Lw) will be irreducible if the KL right
cell contains a smooth element. For type A, Bai and Chen [1] had given an
efficient algorithm to find out all the smooth elements in a given KL right
cell.

For a highest weight g-module L(λ), the annihilator Ann(L(λ)) is a prim-
itive ideal in the enveloping algebra U(g).
The associated graded ideal
gr(Ann(L(λ))) is an ideal inside the symmetric algebra S(g), which is natu-
rally isomorphic to gr(U(g)). The associated variety of the quotient module
U(g)/Ann(L(λ)) is defined as the zero locus of gr(Ann(L(λ))) in g∗; this
set is called the annihilator variety of L(λ) and is known to be the Zariski
closure of a nilpotent orbit OAnn(L(λ)) in g∗[22]. In [4] and [2], the anni-
hilator variety of a highest weight module had been given in a simple way
by using the Robinson–Schensted algorithm and Sommers duality. But the
associated variety of a highest weight module is still a challenging problem.
In the case where OAnn(L(λ)) coincides with Omin, the unique minimal
nilpotent orbit of g∗, the associated variety V (L(λ)) had been given in a
simple and uniform characterization by Bai–Ma–Xiao–Xie [5]. From [5], we
know that there exists a unique minimal special nilpotent orbit (see [18, §6.3]
