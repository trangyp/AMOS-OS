Abstract.
The diﬀerential λ-calculus studies how the quantitative aspects of programs
correspond to diﬀerentiation and to Taylor expansion inside models of linear logic. Recent
work has generalized the axioms of Taylor expansion so they apply to many models that
only feature partial sums. However, that work does not cover the classic web based models
of Köthe spaces and ﬁniteness spaces.
First, we provide a generic construction of web based models with partial sums.
It
captures models, ranging from coherence spaces to probabilistic coherence spaces, ﬁniteness
spaces and Köthe spaces. Second, we generalize the theory of Taylor expansion to models
in which coeﬃcients can be non-positive. We then use our generic web model construction
to provide a uniﬁed proof that all the aforementioned web based models feature such Taylor
expansion.
Keywords.
Categorical semantics, Linear Logic, Quantitative semantics, Taylor expan-
sion.

Introduction
Quantitative semantics was introduced with linear logic after Girard noticed that the in-
terpretation of programs in mathematical models, were analytic maps [19]. They can be
written as an inﬁnite sum of multilinear maps through a formula called the Taylor expansion:

The n-th derivative at 0 captures the part of the program that uses its input exactly n-times
during execution. In this way, quantitative semantics helps us understand how computation
interacts with non-determinism [32], probabilities [11, 15] or quantum primitives [40].
The diﬀerential λ-calculus [16] and the syntactic Taylor expansion [17] extend quanti-
tative semantics. They give a purely syntactic way to track resources by building deriva-
tive and Taylor expansion inside the language itself. These theories also uncover a strong
link between the world of logic and computation, and the world of topology and analysis
[12, 13, 26, 27].
The diﬀerential λ-calculus [6] and the syntactical Taylor expansion, and their model [36]
have a fatal drawback: they allow any countable sums. The only way to interpret such sums

computationally is as non-determinism. From a syntactic viewpoint, this is puzzling. Indeed,
Taylor expansion can be applied to deterministic [17] or probabilistic [10] programs, and en-
joys uniformity properties that are needed to prove normalization [17, 44]. From a semantic
viewpoint, allowing countable sums rules out most quantitative semantics. Fortunately, re-
cent works show that the diﬀerential calculus [14, 18] and the Taylor expansion [18] can be
axiomatized using only partial sums. Partial sums match computational behaviours such as
determinism in coherence spaces [19], or randomness in probabilistic coherence spaces [11].
One limitation remains. The inﬁnite partial sums used in the coherent theory of Taylor
expansion [18] rely on Σ-monoids [21]. Σ-monoids only allow positive sums: if x+y = 0 then
x = y = 0. For programming language semantics, this is not a problem as programs never
cancel each other out. However, this restriction becomes a serious issue when developing
a theory of Taylor expansion that also works for quantitative semantics built on analysis
and topology.
In particular, Köthe spaces [12] and ﬁniteness spaces [13] are important
quantitative models that use analytic maps. Because they involve negative coeﬃcients, they
are not covered by the coherent Taylor expansion described in [18].
Contributions.
Our main contribution (Part II) is to generalize the coherent Taylor
expansion [18] to a more general notion of partial sums called PCMs [22] that includes
negative coeﬃcients. This axiomatization of partial sum is more subtle to work with, in
the same way that summability over real numbers is more subtle than summability over
non-negative real numbers. In order to prove in a uniﬁed way that both Köthe spaces and
ﬁniteness spaces feature a coherent Taylor expansion, we introduce a generic construction of
web models (Part I), which is another contribution. We ﬁrst consider models in which the
coeﬃcient of the vectors and matrices range over an arbitrary Σ-monoid in which objects are
sets of vectors and morphisms are matrices. We then generalize our construction to arbitrary
PCMs whose summability is given by a notion of absolute convergence. This describes web
models where coeﬃcients can be negative, as in Köthe spaces [12] and ﬁniteness spaces [13].
Related work.
The use of partial sums in semantics is rather common, and can be
traced back to algebraic programming semantics [35]. Various notions of positive sums have
been used in the literature, such as in geometry of interaction [21] and quantum programming
semantics [42, 7]. Our results on web models provide a uniﬁed point of view on various proof
techniques that appear throughout the literature on web based semantics [19, 12, 13, 11, 32].
Under some light additional assumptions (see remark 30), our web models based on Σ-
monoids can be described as categories of modules over Σ-semirings [43] with an orthogonal
basis reminiscent of tight orthogonality in double glueing [23].
However, [43] does not
describe Taylor expansion in that setting, and even though they prove that their model
have a Lafont exponential, they do not provide any explicit deﬁnition. They also do not
provide a deﬁnition for more general PCMs based on absolute convergence. Our theory
of coherent Taylor expansion is an adaptation of the coherent Taylor expansion of [18]
to a setting with possibly negative coeﬃcients. Our work lies in the ﬁeld of categorical
diﬀerentiation, whose aim is to study the categorical properties of diﬀerentiation [2, 3, 8]
and of Taylor expansion [25, 33, 45].

Conclusion and future work
In part I, we have built a generic construction of web models, based on strong and absolute
PCM. It uniﬁes various models of the literature. In part II, we have described a theory
of Taylor expansion in categories enriched over PCMs. Web models given by our generic
construction feature such Taylor expansion as proved in section 7.
Thus, Taylor expan-
sion is compatible with the computational properties described by partial sums, such as
determinism, ﬁnite or countable non-determinism, randomness, and absolute convergence.
Future work. We conjecture that the exponential of our web models is always Lafont
(see remark 23). Our theory of coherent Taylor expansion captures a wide class of web mod-
els, but there are no known web-free examples. We conjecture that the analytic version [26]
of the category of convenient vector spaces [4] is not representable but features a coherent
16


## Page 17


Taylor expansion. Finally, although we described our web models with a strong emphasis
on the algebraic point of view of partial sums, it should be possible to adopt a topological
viewpoint on absolute PCMs, as it is done in Köthe spaces [12] and ﬁniteness spaces [13].
