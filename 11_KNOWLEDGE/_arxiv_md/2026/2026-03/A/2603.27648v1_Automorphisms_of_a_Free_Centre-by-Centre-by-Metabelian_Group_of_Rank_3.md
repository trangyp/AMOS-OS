AUTOMORPHISMS OF A FREE
CENTRE-BY-CENTRE-BY-METABELIAN GROUP OF RANK 3
C. E. KOFINAS
Abstract. Let F3 be the free group of rank 3 and let G3 = F3/[F ′′
3 , F3, F3],
that is, G3 is a free centre-by-centre-by-metabelian group of rank 3. We show
that Aut(G3) contains a proper finitely generated subgroup that is dense with
respect to the formal power series topology.
Keywords: free centre-by-centre-by-metabelian groups, automorphisms of rel-
atively free groups, formal power series topology, dense subgroups of automor-
phism groups.
Mathematics Subject Classification 2010: 20F28, 20E36.
1. Introduction
Let F3 be the free group of rank 3 freely generated by the set {f1, f2, f3}. We
write G3 = F3/[F ′′
3 , F3, F3] and xi = fi[F ′′
3 , F3, F3] for i = 1, 2, 3.
Thus, G3 is
a free centre-by-centre-by-metabelian group of rank 3, freely generated by the set
{x1, x2, x3}. The natural mapping from F3 onto G3 induces a group homomorphism
from Aut(F3) into Aut(G3). The image of this homomorphism is denoted by T3,
and the automorphisms of G3 that belong to T3 are called tame; otherwise they are
called non-tame (or wild).
It was shown in [12] that the intersection of the lower central series of G3 is trivial.
Hence, we may consider the topology on G3 corresponding to the lower central
series G3 ⊇γ2(G3) ⊇γ3(G3) ⊇· · · . For c ≥2, the natural mapping from G3 onto
G3/γc(G3) induces a group homomorphism from Aut(G3) into Aut(G3/γc(G3)).
Write IcAG3 for the kernel of this homomorphism.
By [1], T
i≥2 IiAG3 = {1}.
Therefore, the topology corresponding to the lower central series induces a topology
on Aut(G3) that corresponds to the series
Aut(G3) ⊃I2AG3 ⊃I3AG3 ⊃. . . .
This topology is called the formal power series topology.
The motivation for studying Aut(G3) comes from recent progress on automor-
phism groups of relatively free groups. For a free centre-by-metabelian group of
rank 3, it was shown in [13] that its automorphism group contains a finitely gen-
erated subgroup that is dense with respect to the formal power series topology. In
rank 2, it remains open whether a finitely generated dense subgroup exists. In rank
n ≥4, the automorphism group of a free centre-by-centre-by-metabelian group of
rank n is finitely generated (see [14]) and hence the problem of finding a finitely
generated dense subgroup is trivial. It is therefore natural to ask whether a finitely
generated dense subgroup exists in Aut(G3). Our aim is to extend the correspond-
ing result from [13] for a free centre-by-metabelian group of rank 3 and prove the
following result.

2. Preliminaries and Background
2.1. Notation. Let G be a group.
For a, b ∈G, we write ab = b−1ab and
[a, b] = a−1b−1ab for the commutator of a and b. We adopt the left-normed con-
vention for commutators: for g1, . . . , gc ∈G, with c ≥3, we write [g1, . . . , gc] =
[[g1, . . . , gc−1], gc]. For subgroups A and B of G, we denote by [A, B] the subgroup
of G generated by all commutators [a, b], where a ∈A and b ∈B. We adopt the
left-normed convention for subgroups as well, so that for subgroups A, B and C of
G, we write [A, B, C] = [[A, B], C]. For a subset X of G, ⟨X⟩denotes the subgroup
of G generated by X. For a subgroup H of G, ⟨X⟩H denotes the subgroup of G
generated by all the elements y−1xy, where y ∈H and x ∈X. We write G′ for the
derived subgroup of G, that is, G′ = [G, G], and G′′ = (G′)′. For a positive integer
c, let γc(G) be the c-th term of the lower central series of G. Note that γ2(G) = G′.
We say that G is residually nilpotent if T
i≥1 γi(G) = {1}.
Let Aut(G) be the automorphism group of G. For c ≥2, the natural group
epimorphism from G onto G/γc(G) induces a group homomorphism from Aut(G)
into Aut(G/γc(G)). We denote the kernel of this homomorphism by IcAG. The
elements of I2AG, that is, the automorphisms of G that induce the identity mapping
on the abelianization of G, are called IA-automorphisms of G. It was proved in
[1, Theorem 1] that the series I2AG ⊃I3AG ⊃. . . is central. Furthermore, if G is
residually nilpotent, then by [1, Theorem 2], T
i≥2 IiAG = {1}.
2.2. Automorphisms of Relatively Free Groups. Let Fn be the free group of
finite rank n, with n ≥2, freely generated by the set {f1, . . . , fn}. Let V be a
variety of groups and let V (Fn) be the verbal subgroup of Fn corresponding to V .
Thus, the group Fn(V ) = Fn/V (Fn) is a relatively free group of rank n in V . For
background on varieties of groups and relatively free groups, see [15]. The natural
mapping from Fn onto Fn(V ) induces a group homomorphism from Aut(Fn) into
Aut(Fn(V )).
The image of this homomorphism is denoted by TFn(V ), and the
automorphisms of Fn(V ) which belong to TFn(V ) are called tame; otherwise they
are called non-tame (or wild).

2.3. The Formal Power Series Topology. For our terminology and notation,
we follow [7] (see also [4]). Let G be a relatively free residually nilpotent group of
finite rank n, with n ≥2. For an integer k ≥2 and for a subgroup N of Aut(G),
we write IkN = N ∩IkAG (that is, IkN is the set of elements of N that induce the
identity mapping on G/γk(G)). Note that IkN is a normal subgroup of I2N. The
following result is a special case of [7, Proposition 2.1].
Proposition 2. For a positive integer n ≥2, let G be a relatively free residually
nilpotent group of finite rank n. Let N be a subgroup of Aut(G) such that TG ⊆N.
Then, N is dense in Aut(G) with respect to the formal power series topology, if and
only if IkAG = (IkN)Ik+1AG for all k ≥2.
Note that M3 is residually nilpotent by [8]. Hurley [12, Proposition 7.11 and
its Corollary] claimed that the quotient F ′′

Aut(M3), Aut(C3) and Aut(G3) all admit the formal power series topology induced
by their respective lower central series.
