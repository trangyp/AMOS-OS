1. Introduction
The Cremona group Crnpkq is the group of birational transformations of the projective
space Pn over the field k. This group is known to be very large, see e.g. [8]. In fact, if
n ą 1, then Crnpkq is not an ind-algebraic group, see [6].
This motivates the study of algebraic subgroups of Crnpkq. The connected algebraic
subgroups of Cr2pCq were classified by Enriques in [15]: any such subgroup is contained in a
maximal connected algebraic subgroup, and every maximal connected algebraic subgroup
is conjugate to either AutpP2q » PGL3pCq or Aut˝pFnq, where n ‰ 1 and Fn denotes the
n-th Hirzebruch surface. The classification of maximal connected algebraic subgroups of
Cr3pCq was later stated by Enriques and Fano in [16], and a first classification was given
by Umemura in [45, 46, 47, 48]. In this case, it again follows from the classification that
every connected algebraic subgroup of Cr3pCq is contained in a maximal one.
Recently, in [17], Fanelli-Floris-Zimmermann proved that the analogous property does
not hold for CrnpCq when n ě 5: there exist connected algebraic subgroups that are not
contained in any maximal one. Their result was extended to the case n “ 4 by Kollár in
[35].

Umemura’s proof has been revisited and generalised to any algebraically closed field
of characteristic zero by Blanc-Fanelli-Terpereau in [4, 5]. In these two articles, they use
only techniques from algebraic geometry, such as the theory of algebraic groups and the
Minimal Model Program (MMP), whereas Umemura used a result of Lie that classifies
analytic actions on complex threefolds ([45, Theorem 1.12]).
Throughout this article, we work over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic
zero. Following the strategy of Blanc-Fanelli-Terpereau, we pursue an alternative direc-
tion, namely the classification of maximal connected algebraic subgroups of BirpC ˆ P2q,
where C denotes a smooth projective curve of genus gpCq ě 1. Running an MMP from
a variety birationally equivalent to C ˆ P2, we obtain two types of Mori fibre spaces
π: X ÝÑ B, according to the dimension of B:
(1) Mori conic bundles over a projective surface B birationally equivalent to C ˆ P1,
see Lemma 9.6(1),
(2) Mori Del Pezzo fibrations over B “ C, see Lemma 2.13.
Since C is not rational, the case where B “ Specpkq (or equivalently, X is a Fano
variety), does not occur. The first case was studied mainly in [22], where the first author
classified connected algebraic subgroups that are maximal in BirpX{Bq, with B being a
ruled surface. Such a subgroup is called relatively maximal.
In this article, we focus on the automorphism groups of Mori Del Pezzo fibrations
π: X ÝÑ C and determine whether Aut˝pXq is a maximal connected algebraic subgroup
of BirpXq. This leads to our Theorem A and Theorem B. From these results, we deduce
the classification of maximal connected algebraic subgroups of BirpC ˆ P2q, which gives
Theorem C.

1.2. Plan of the article. This article is structured as follows.
In Section 2, we recall generalities on Del Pezzo surfaces and Mori Del Pezzo fibrations.
We also analyse the Sarkisov links starting from Mori Del Pezzo fibrations. The Sarkisov
Program is an algorithm that decomposes every birational map between Mori fibre spaces
into Sarkisov links; it was established by Corti in dimension two and three, by Hacon and
McKernan in higher dimensions and by Floris for the equivariant version [14, 27, 19]. This
program serves as our main tool to determine the maximality of the automorphism group
of a Mori fibre space.
Let π: X ÝÑ C be a Mori Del Pezzo fibration, where C is a smooth projective curve
of genus gpCq ě 1. The integer d “ K2

Remark 2.1. Let C be a smooth projective curve of genus gpCq ě 1 and let X be
birational to a Mori fibre space π: Y ÝÑ C. Then X is not Fano. Indeed, any Fano
variety is rationally connected [34, Theorem 0.1], and rational connectedness is invariant
under birational maps. So, if X is Fano, then Y is rationally connected and hence C is
rationally connected, against gpCq ě 1.

dominant morphism to a smooth projective curve B such that the general fibre is rationally
connected. Then π has a rational section.
