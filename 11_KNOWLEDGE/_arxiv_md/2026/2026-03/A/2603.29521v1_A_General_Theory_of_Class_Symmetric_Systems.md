A GENERAL THEORY OF CLASS SYMMETRIC SYSTEMS
PETER HOLY, EMMA PALMER, AND JONATHAN SCHILHAN
Abstract. We develop a general theory for class-sized symmetric systems as
a natural extension of symmetric systems with respect to class forcing.
In
particular, adapting the usual notions of pretameness and tameness for class
forcing, we present sufficient conditions for the preservation of the axioms of
G¨odel-Bernays set theory (without the axiom of choice), and for the forcing
theorem to hold for class-sized symmetric systems.
1. Introduction and Basic Definitions
Class-sized symmetric systems have been used to verify a number of important
set theoretic results, for instance in the construction of the Gitik model [6], in which
all uncountable cardinals are singular. Other important examples are given by the
Morris model [19] (see also [13]) or the Bristol model [12], [14]. The basic properties
of these symmetric systems – whether they satisfy the forcing theorem and preserve
the relevant axioms – have been established on an ad hoc case-by-case basis for these
particular systems. The main point of our paper is to provide a general theory of
class-sized symmetric systems: we provide useful sufficient conditions, which are
based around the well-known notions of pretameness and tameness for class forcing,
for when class-sized symmetric systems admit forcing relations and satisfy the truth
lemma (these properties are commonly jointly referred to as the forcing theorem),
and for when they preserve the axioms of G¨odel-Bernays set theory (except for the
axiom of choice). Our results also provide a possible answer to the second question
in [17, Question 7.14], where Richard Matthews asks for a combinatorial condition
on class-sized symmetric systems that ensures the preservation of the axioms of
G¨odel-Bernays set theory other than the axiom of choice and the powerset axiom.
We denote G¨odel-Bernays set theory without the axiom of choice by GB, and
unless otherwise noted, this will be our base theory in this paper. We present GB
as a two-sorted theory1, that talks about a domain of sets and a domain of classes;
we follow the convention that set variables are given by lowercase letters x, y, a, b . . .
while class variables are given by uppercase letters X, Y, A, B . . . .
The axioms of GB are:
• The following set axioms: Extensionality, Regularity, Pairing, Union, Infin-
ity, and Powerset.
• Extensionality for classes: for any classes A, B, if (∀x)(x ∈A ↔x ∈B),
then A = B.

In this section, we define forcing relations for atomic formulas (Definition 4) and

ensures that a system admits forcing relations for atomic formulas (Theorem 10).

When does S admit a forcing relation for atomic formulas? Given second-order
class comprehension, the answer is always.
Lemma 8. Assume second-order class comprehension11. Then S admits a forcing
relation for atomic formulas.
Roughly, this follows because second-order class comprehension allows us to do
recursion on classes. See [1, Lemma 15] for a proof in the context of usual class
forcing; applying this to P and then restricting the result to HS gives us a forcing
relation for atomic formulas for S.
However, when we only assume GB, this argument no longer works (and in fact
there can be class forcing notions that do not admit forcing relations for atomic
formulas, cf. [10, Corollary 7.6]). Instead, we give a condition on S, based on the
idea of pretameness, which ends up ensuring that S admits forcing relations for
atomic formulas.

The above should be contrasted with a result of Matthews ([16, Section 4.1]
or [17, Theorem 7.13]), where he shows that one can have a pretame notion of
class forcing P with a symmetric system S = ⟨P, G, F⟩– which is thus combinato-
rially pretame – that fails to force Replacement, which tells us in particular that
only assuming combinatorial pretameness of S is not sufficient for the pretameness
of S. It also answers the second question posed in [17, Question 7.14], by provid-
ing stratification as a very natural combinatorial property of a symmetric system
S = ⟨P, G, F⟩that ensures, together with the pretameness of P, the preservation of
GB−and thus the pretameness of S.27 Unlike for the case of class forcing (where
pretameness is a combinatorial requirement on class partial orders), we do not know
of a combinatorial property of class symmetric systems that is equivalent to their
pretameness.

6. Preserving the powerset axiom

preserves the powerset axiom. In complete analogy, we say that a class symmetric
system S is tame if it is pretame and preserves the powerset axiom.

7. Final remarks and open questions
We end the paper with some observations and open questions. The following
remains open:
Question 38. Is there a combinatorial characterization of the pretameness of class
symmetric systems?
We would like to observe that there are important class symmetric systems that
necessarily use class forcing notions which are not tame. In order to formalize this,
we again work in an ambient universe V with a transitive model M of GB. We
may even assume, for the sake of simplicity, that V |= ZFC and M is countable
in V .28
Definition 39. We say that two class symmetric systems S and S′ of M are weakly
equivalent if they produce the same set of symmetric extensions (in V ).
Let us consider the question whether, if S = ⟨P, G, F⟩is a tame class symmetric
system in M, there is a weakly equivalent symmetric system S′ = ⟨P′, G′, F′⟩in
M with a tame notion of class forcing P. This has a negative answer, letting S be
the class symmetric system to obtain Gitik’s model [6] in which every uncountable
cardinal is singular. If a weakly equivalent S′ with a tame notion of class forcing P′
existed, let G be P′-generic over M. Then, M[G] has a submodel that is an S-
generic extension of M, in which every uncountable cardinal is singular. By upward
absoluteness of this property, this is clearly a contradiction. Another example that
does not use large cardinals is the Morris model (see [19], and [13] for a modern
exposition).
Regarding pretameness, the analogous question seems to be open:
Question 40. If S is pretame in a model of GB, does this model have a weakly
equivalent symmetric system S′ = ⟨P, G, F⟩with P pretame?
A positive answer to the following question would imply that stratification is, in
a certain sense, essential for tame symmetric systems:
Question 41. If S is a tame symmetric system in a model of GB, does this model
have a weakly equivalent tame symmetric system S′ that is stratified?29
