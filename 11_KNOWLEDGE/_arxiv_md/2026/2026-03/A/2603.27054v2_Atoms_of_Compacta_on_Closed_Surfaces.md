ATOMS OF COMPACTA ON CLOSED SURFACES
JUN LUO, J¨ORG THUSWALDNER, XIAO-TING YAO, AND SHUQIN ZHANG
Abstract. For any compact set K lying on a closed surface S we introduce
a closed equivalence relation ∼, called the Sch¨onflies equivalence on K. We
show that every class [x]∼of ∼is a continuum and that the resulting quotient
space K/ ∼is a Peano compactum. By definition, all components of a Peano
compactum are locally connected and for any ε > 0 only finitely many of
them have diameter greater than ε. The decomposition DK = {[x]∼: x ∈K}
refines every other upper semicontinuous decomposition of K into subcontinua
that has a Peano compactum as its quotient space. In other words, DK is the
core decomposition of K with Peano quotient. The elements of DK are called
atoms of K. We also show that for any branched covering f : S∗→S from
a closed surface S∗to S, every atom of f −1(K) is sent into an atom of K. If
f is even a covering, it sends every atom of f −1(K) onto an atom of K. We
illustrate our theory with examples and show that it cannot be generalized to
n-manifolds with n ≥3 by providing a detailed counterexample in R3.

1. Introduction
In the present paper we are concerned with upper semi-continuous decomposi-
tions of compact subsets of a closed surface S whose associated quotient spaces have
certain topological properties. Recall that an upper semi-continuous decomposition
(usc decomposition, for short) D of a topological space X is a partition of X into
closed subsets with the following property: For each δ ∈D and each open set U
containing δ there is an open set V such that δ ⊂V ⊂U and V is a union of
elements of D. If each element of D is connected, we call D a usc decomposition

A classical application of such decompositions comes from Moore’s decomposi-
tion theorem (cf. [15, Theorem 22]), which gives rise to usc decompositions D of the
extended complex plane bC into nonseparating subcontinua. The resulting quotient
space for such a decomposition is known to be homeomorphic to bC. This theorem
forms the starting point of the well-developed theory of decomposition of manifolds,
see e.g. [6].
We recall some basic notions and relevant background. Let D be an usc decom-
position into subcontinua of a compactum K. For any x ∈K, denote the unique
element of D that contains x by D(x). The map πD : K →D sending every x ∈K
to D(x) will be called the natural projection on D. Therefore, πD(x) has two inter-
pretations. It can be regarded as a point in the quotient space under the quotient
topology, still denoted by D without risk of confusion, or it can be regarded as the
subcontinuum D(x) of K. If D is clear from the context, we just write π instead of
πD. For an usc decomposition D of a compact metric space K (a compactum, for
short), the resulting quotient space D is again a compactum; see for instance [8,
Theorem 20] and its corollary [8, p. 149].
Recall that a Peano continuum is the image of the unit interval [0, 1] under a
continuous map. Due to the Hahn-Mazurkiewicz-Sierpi´nski Theorem, a continuum
is locally connected if and only if it is a Peano continuum. The following notion of
Peano compactum provides a natural generalization of Peano continuum.
Definition 1.1 (F-compactum and Peano compactum; cf. [11, Theorem 3]). A com-
pact metric space is called an F-compactum if its nondegenerate components form
a null sequence, in the sense that for any ε > 0 there are at most finitely many
components of diameter greater than ε. A compact metric space is called Peano
compactum if it is an F-compactum each of whose nondegenerate components is a
Peano continuum.
Definition 1.2 (Some kinds of decompositions). Let D be an usc decomposition into
subcontinua of a compact metric space K.
(1) If the resulting quotient space is a Peano compactum we call D a Peano
decomposition.
(2) If D is a Peano decomposition and no other Peano decomposition refines D
then D is called an atomic decomposition of K (with Peano quotient).
(3) If D is a Peano decomposition that refines every other Peano decomposition
we call it the core decomposition of K (with Peano quotient) and denote it
by DP C
K .
Here we defined the core decomposition w.r.t. the property of being a Peano
compactum.
As can be seen in [7, Definition 1.1], core decompositions can be
defined in a meaningful way also w.r.t. other properties. For instance, the existence
of the core decomposition, w.r.t. the property of being semi-locally connected is
established in [7, Theorem 2.7 (3)]. In [7, Theorem 5.4], the authors treat a special
case where the core decomposition of a continuum w.r.t. local connectedness exists
and coincides with its core decomposition w.r.t. semi-local connectedness. In the
present paper we will use the term “core decomposition” exclusively in the sense of
in Definition 1.2.

Definition 2.1 (The Sch¨onflies relation on surfaces). Let K be a compactum on a
closed surface S. The Sch¨onflies relation on K is a set RK ⊂K×K consisting of all
points (x, y) ∈K ×K such that either x = y or one can find a marked quadrilateral
(Q, I1, I2) satisfying each of the following three conditions:
(a) x ∈I1, y ∈I2 and K ∩∂Q ⊂I1 ∪I2.
(b) K ∩Q has infinitely many components Qn (n ≥1), satisfying Qn ∩Ii ̸= ∅
for each i ∈{1, 2}.
(c) (Qn)n≥1 converges w.r.t. Hausdorff distance to a continuum Q∞satisfying
x, y ∈Q∞.
When S is a sphere, the relation RK coincides with the Sch¨onflies relation given
in [11, Definition 4]; see Lemma 3.7. Thus there is no harm in calling our new rela-
tion Sch¨onflies relation again. Following [11], we also give the following definition.
Definition 2.2 (Sch¨onflies equivalence on surfaces). Let K be a compactum on a
closed surface S. The smallest closed equivalence relation containing RK is called
Sch¨onflies equivalence. We denote this equivalence relation by ∼K or, if K is clear
from the context, just by ∼.
The collection DK of the classes of ∼K forms a partition of K into closed sets.
By compactness of K it is routine to verify that closedness of RK implies that DK
forms an usc decomposition of K.
Our first main theorem shows that DK is the core decomposition of K.
Theorem 2.3. Let K ⊂S. Then the core decomposition of K exists and is given
by DK.
The following corollary, which could also be proved directly with less effort than
Theorem 2.3, is an immediate consequence of Theorem 2.3. Recall that, for x ∈K,
DK(x) is the unique element of DK containing x.

related works. Second, f is a polynomial, considered as a self-map of the extended
complex plane onto itself. See for instance [14].

(1) If f is a branched covering then f(δ) is a subset of an atom of K.
(2) If f is a covering then f(δ) is an atom of K.

An open annulus is just an elementary region bounded by two disjoint Jordan

Definition 3.9 (Planar and strictly planar). A subset of S is called planar, if it is
