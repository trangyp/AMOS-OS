A set theoretic version of equations on groups
Mihai-Silviu Lazorec
February 28, 2026
Abstract
Let G be a finite group. The aim of this paper is to study the number of solutions S ⊆G
of the equation 0{n}(S) = L, where L is a non-empty subset of G, n is a positive integer and
0{n}(S) = {sn | s ∈S}. Besides our findings obtained in this general frame, we also outline
some results which hold for some particular cases such as: i) L is a normal subset of G; ii) G
is abelian; iii) G is an extraspecial p-group.
MSC (2020): Primary 20D60; Secondary 20K01, 20D15, 20E99.
Key words: group equations, normal subsets, abelian groups, extraspecial groups, element orders,
cyclic subgroups

Introduction
Let G be a finite group, n be a positive integer and l be an element of G. We denote by Nn,l the
set of solutions x ∈G of the equation xn = l. One of the best-known results concerning such an
equation is due to Frobenius (see [2, 4]):
Theorem 1.1. Let G be a finite group, n be a positive integer and l ∈G. Then |Nn,l| is a
multiple of (n, |CG(l)|).
While the most popular version of Theorem 1.1 is the one corresponding to the case l = 1 (check
[11] for a proof and [9] for some applications), the literature also includes various generalizations of
this result (see [6]). We recall the following two results which outline what happens if l ranges over
a subgroup or a normal subset of G.
Theorem 1.2. Let G be a finite group and n be a positive integer.
i) If L is a subgroup of G, then P
