Abstract
The wave–particle duality of massive macromolecules—such as the fullerene C60—is a well-
established quantum phenomenon.
However, whether the quantum behavior of large organic
molecules actively dictates the macroscopic structure and function of synthetic materials remains
unknown. In organic semiconductors, crystal polymorphism fundamentally determines optoelec-
tronic performance, yet classical thermodynamic models consistently fail to resolve the microscopic
origins of phase selection. This includes the long-standing anomaly of divergent polymorph forma-
tion under identical thermodynamic parameters across different reactor scales. Here we show that
the polymorphism of copper phthalocyanine (CuPc, 576 Da)—a planar macromolecule compara-
ble in mass to C60—is governed by quantum coherence during atmospheric-pressure organic vapor
phase deposition (OVPD). We establish the Dissipative structure field-Induced Multipartite Entan-
glement (DIME) framework, which integrates ambient blackbody radiation, molecular de Broglie
wavelengths, and frontier orbital directionality to model field-driven quantum entanglement. We
demonstrate that exceptionally weak environmental decoherence at room temperature preserves
the coherence of molecular matter waves, enabling the self-assembly of ultralong (> 1 cm) single-
crystalline η-CuPc nanowires. By leveraging the DIME framework to manipulate environmental
decoherence, we rationally designed an OVPD reactor to synthesize a previously undiscovered poly-
morph, designated ω-CuPc. Our findings reveal that multipartite quantum entanglement acts as
the decisive regulator of organic crystal assembly, opening a deterministic, quantum-level pathway
for engineering organic semiconductor polymorphism.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Wave-particle duality, the foundational cornerstone of quantum mechanics, has trans-
formed our understanding of the natural world across every scale of matter. Following the
experimental verification of this phenomenon for elementary particles, a landmark study
extended this paradigm to macromolecules by demonstrating quantum interference for the
fullerene C60, a 720 Da spherical aromatic molecule[1]. This breakthrough proved that mas-
sive organic molecules can exhibit quantum wave-like behavior, challenging the assumption

that quantum coherence is confined to atomic-scale systems in ultra-isolated laboratory con-
ditions. Yet, in the decades since this discovery, a critical research question has remained:
can the quantum wave-particle duality of large organic molecules directly dictate the macro-
scopic structure and functional performance of synthetic organic materials?
Addressing this question is particularly urgent for organic semiconductor crystal engineer-
ing, where crystal polymorphism is the primary determinant of optoelectronic performance,
governing charge carrier mobility, exciton diffusion length, and device stability. Copper ph-
thalocyanine (CuPc, 576 Da) is a planar aromatic macrocycle with a molecular weight com-
parable to that of fullerene C60 (720 Da), serving as the workhorse organic semiconductor for
field-effect transistors and photovoltaics. CuPc possesses well-characterized polymorphs—
such as the η, α, and β phases—that exhibit drastically different functional properties. While
atmospheric-pressure organic vapor phase deposition (OVPD) is the standard method for
growing these crystals, classical models relying exclusively on macroscopic thermal and ki-
netic parameters fail to explain phase selection or the subtle lattice contractions observed
under varying conditions. This exposes a fundamental unresolved challenge in organic semi-
conductor science: polymorph design and control have long been confined to empirical trial-
and-error screening, lacking a first-principles, quantum-mechanical rational framework that
enables deterministic, predictive control of phase formation during vapor-phase materials
synthesis.
A potential resolution to this gap lies at the unprecedented intersection of non-equilibrium
thermodynamics and quantum biology. Ilya Prigogine’s dissipative structure theory estab-
lished that highly ordered structures emerge spontaneously from chaos in open systems
driven far from thermodynamic equilibrium[2]. Concurrently, studies in quantum biology
have rewritten the rules for where quantum phenomena can persist, proving that ambient,
dissipative environments can actually support and tune quantum coherence. For example,
excitation energy in photosynthetic light-harvesting systems is transported with > 95%
efficiency.
Direct observations of long-lived electronic quantum coherence in the Fenna-
Matthews-Olson complex and marine cryptophyte algae confirm that delocalized, multi-
partite entangled states survive at ambient temperatures[3]. Through environment-assisted
quantum transport (ENAQT), the dynamic biological environment acts as an active regu-
lator of quantum entanglement, suppressing destructive interference and enhancing optimal
transport pathways.

These biological findings directly map to the synthetic chemistry of OVPD-grown CuPc
crystals.
An OVPD reactor is an open, dissipative system defined by blackbody radi-
ation, carrier gas collisions, and temperature gradients—precisely the conditions where
environment-tunable quantum entanglement can persist and direct molecular behavior.
Here, we bridge this gap by introducing a theoretical framework: Dissipative struc-
ture field-Induced Multipartite Entanglement (DIME). The DIME framework unifies Pri-
gogine’s dissipative structure theory with the quantum mechanics of macromolecular wave-
particle duality to explain CuPc polymorph selection during atmospheric-pressure OVPD.
We demonstrate that exceptionally weak environmental decoherence at room temperature
preserves the coherence of CuPc molecular matter waves, resolving the long-standing puzzle
of how ultralong (> 1 cm) single-crystalline η-CuPc nanowires self-assemble. Furthermore,
leveraging the predictive power of the DIME framework, we rationally designed a modified
OVPD reactor to successfully synthesize a previously unreported polymorph, designated
ω-CuPc.
The significance of this study lies in its fundamental paradigm shift: it establishes dissi-
pative structure field-induced multipartite quantum entanglement as the decisive, previously
unrecognized regulator of organic crystal engineering. By proving that macroscopic quan-
tum coherence governs phase selection, this work opens entirely new, rational routes for the
quantum-level manipulation of organic semiconductor polymorphism, paving the way for
next-generation, high-performance optoelectronic devices.
II.
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP AND METHODS
A.
Atmospheric-Pressure Organic Vapor Phase Deposition (OVPD)
The synthesis of copper phthalocyanine (CuPc) polymorphs was conducted using a
custom-built, pilot-scale atmospheric-pressure organic vapor phase deposition (OVPD) sys-
tem. This system is designed for continuous synthesis and comprises four core functional
units. First, the inert gas supply and flow control unit delivers high-purity nitrogen (N2)
carrier gas, with flow rates precisely regulated between 0–5000 standard cubic centimeters
per minute (sccm) via a high-precision mass flow controller. Second, the high-temperature
vapor reaction unit utilizes a single-zone horizontal tube furnace (maximum operating tem-
4


## Page 5


perature of 1200 ◦C; control accuracy of ±1 ◦C). The organic source material (high-purity
CuPc powder) sublimates within the constant-temperature zone of the fused silica reaction
tube, with the source temperature held constant at the experimentally determined sublima-
tion temperature of CuPc under atmospheric pressure vapor deposition conditions. Third,
the product collection and tail-gas treatment unit capture the downstream flow. This unit
includes a fused silica product collector sealed with vacuum flanges, which transitions into
a Polyurethane (PU) transmission tube (7 mm inner diameter, 500 mm length). The PU
tube feeds into a series of three two-port glass gas washing bottles (connected by identical
PU tubing) to collect the nucleated ω-CuPc phase, terminating in a final gas washing bottle
for tail-gas purification. Finally, a horizontal operation platform ensures the coaxiality and
mechanical stability of the entire system during continuous operation.
B.
Spectroscopic Characterization
Ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) absorption spectra were recorded to evaluate the electronic
transition properties and Davydov splitting of the resulting crystalline phases.
Fourier-
transform infrared (FTIR) transmission spectroscopy was performed over a range of 400–
1800 cm−1 to map the structural homology, intermolecular packing, and skeletal vibration
characteristics of the newly synthesized ω-CuPc phase against the thermodynamically stable
β-CuPc phase.
III.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

thermodynamic variables such as temperature gradients and substrate supersaturation.

thermodynamic minimums and successfully synthesized a novel polymorph, designated ω-
CuPc. Fig. 1 presents the comparative morphological analysis between the CuPc starting
material and the ω-phase CuPc, a new polymorph identified for the first time in this work.

C.
Universal Quantum Signatures in Phthalocyanine Polymorphs
To understand the microscopic origins of this structural assembly, we evaluated the com-
parative polymorphism of metal phthalocyanines (MPcs)[4–9].
Under identical ambient
OVPD conditions (450 ◦C source, 400–500 sccm N2, 25 ◦C deposition), multiple MPc sys-
tems—including CuPc, CoPc, and F16CuPc—spontaneously self-assemble into ultralong,
centimeter-scale η-phase nanowires.
This universal morphology is fundamentally underpinned by highly conserved crystallo-
graphic parameters, primarily a uniquely short b-axis representing the molecular stacking
direction (3.77 ˚A for η-CuPc; 3.78 ˚A for J-CoPc; 3.758 ˚A for η-F16CuPc). The massive Davy-
dov splitting universally observed in these η-phases (136–164 nm) directly reflects increased
intermolecular transfer integrals resulting from this tight b-axis herringbone geometry.
Crucially, the metal center variations act as a direct probe of the underlying quantum

as an open, dissipative system driven far from thermodynamic equilibrium, we reframe the

IV.
CONCLUSIONS
We have demonstrated that the wave–particle duality of heavy organic macromolecules—
specifically their capacity to sustain delocalized quantum wavepackets under ambient
conditions—actively directs polymorph selection during organic crystal growth. By iden-
tifying the critical failures of classical thermodynamic models to explain the temperature-
dependent assembly of the η, α, and β phases of CuPc, we established the Dissipative
structure field-Induced Multipartite Entanglement (DIME) framework. This model proves
that the environmental quantum field, specifically the spectral distribution of background
radiation, acts as a tunable regulator of molecular entanglement rather than an absolute
source of decoherence.
Leveraging this framework, we rationally manipulated the environmental measurement
strength within a pilot-scale OVPD reactor to direct the quantum-coherent self-assembly of
a previously unreported crystal structure: the monoclinic ω-CuPc phase. The structural val-
idation of this double-layer stacking motif confirms that reactor geometry and environmental
shielding can override global thermodynamic minimums.
This work represents a fundamental paradigm shift for materials science, establishing
quantum crystal engineering. By redefining polymorphism from a macroscopic thermody-
namic phenomenon to a localized quantum control problem, we open entirely new, determin-
istic pathways for the design of functional materials. Optoelectronic devices leveraging the
tightly coupled architectures of these quantum-directed phases—such as the broadband near-
infrared absorption and ultrafast photoresponse inherent to the η and ω phases—promise to
