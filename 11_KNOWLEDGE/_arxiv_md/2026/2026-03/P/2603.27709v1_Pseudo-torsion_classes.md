PSEUDO-TORSION CLASSES
KIYOSHI IGUSA AND RAY MARESCA
Abstract. For a finite dimensional algebra Λ, we consider a torsion class G in mod-Λ,
which is not necessarily finitely generated. We construct a wall-and-chamber structure
for G where the chambers are the connected components of the complement of the union
of walls. We also consider “infinitesimal chambers”. To each chamber we associate
a “pseudo-torsion class” and a “pseudo-torsionfree class” and show that they are all
distinct. We consider “green paths” in the stability space and associate to them Harder-
Narasimhan stratifications of G. This paper is part of a series of papers whose goal is
to study the “ghosts” which are remnants of the indecomposable Λ-modules which do
not lie in G.
In the special case when our torsion class is all of mod-Λ, we are in the classical well-
known setting. All of our results apply to this classical setting. The “pseudo-torsion
classes” are torsion classes. We point out that we do not take the closure of the set of
walls. So, we get more chambers and our results are new even in this classical setting.

Introduction
Given a torsion class G in mod-Λ [BR] we consider a subcategory of G with the same
objects but with only “strict” morphisms. Strict morphisms, along with other properties,
have kernels in G and, as with all morphisms in G, they have image and cokernel in G. In
G we define pseudo-torsion classes to be classes of objects of G closed under what we call
“strict” extensions, given by short exact sequences where both morphisms are strict, and
having all strict quotients of its objects. Pseudo-torsionfree classes are defined similarly.
They are closed under strict extension and contain all strict subobjects of its objects.
We also consider pseudo-wide subcategories of G. These are the full subcategories of G
with its strict morphisms which are closed under strict extension and contain the kernel,
image and cokernel of every strict morphism between its objects. When G = mod-Λ
these notions correspond to the usual notions of torsion classes, torsionfree classes and
wide subcategories since all morphisms are strict in that case.
We consider the stability diagram for the torsion class G in which we take only
the walls labeled with objects of G.
These diagrams appeared in the first author’s
research in algebraic K-theory and in this paper, we provide a representation theoretic
explanation. Some walls in this diagram appear as they do in the stability diagram
for mod-Λ; however, other walls become larger due to our strict subobject and strict
quotient conditions.
More precisely, the stability space is V = Hom(K0Λ, R) ∼= Rn
where n is the number of simple Λ-modules. Every finitely generated module M, has
dimension vector [M] ∈K0Λ and, for every θ ∈V , we have θ([M]) ∈R which we denote
simply as θ(M).
For every M ∈G we have the “pseudo-wall” DG(M) labeled with M. This is the set
of all θ ∈V so that θ(M) = 0 and θ(M ′) ≤0 for all “strict subjects” M ′ of M. We
define the “pseudo-chambers” of G to be the connected components of the complement
of the union of these pseudo-walls. One of our main results is that each pseudo-chamber
has an associated pseudo-torsion class and these pseudo-torsion classes are distinct. In
the classical case [BST], the chambers are the components of the complement of the
closure of the set of walls. This misses all the “thin” chambers which we also consider.
We define a “green path” in V to be a smooth path θt so that, whenever this path
goes through a wall, say θt0 ∈DG(M) it does so in the positive direction:
d
dt(θt(M))|t=t0 > 0.
We also require that, for t << 0, θt should start in the negative octant where θt(M) < 0
and, for t >> 0, we should have θ(M) > 0 for all nonzero M ∈mod-Λ. This is a
variation of Bridgelands’s construction [Br] which, together with [Ki], is the origin of
the study of stability diagrams.
In the main part of the paper, we show that green paths give “strict” Harder-
Narasimhan (HN) stratifications of G. We also obtain maximal “strict” forward Hom-
orthogonal (FHO) sequences.

This process works exceptionally well when there are only “thin” walls. The situation
becomes considerably more unwieldy when there are thick walls. An example of a thick
wall is the “null wall” in the tame hereditary case. In our next paper [IM] we will deal
with thick walls, which we call “Level 1” walls by “refining” them. Details will be given
in the next paper, but we outline the first steps of the refinement process in the last
section. We observe that mod-Λ is a torsion class. So, our constructions apply to this
classical case.
Section 1 gives the basic definitions of strict morphisms, pseudo-torsion classes,
pseudo-torsionfree classes and pseudo-wide subcategories. Analogous to the classical
case [BR], we prove the basic theorem (Theorem 1.22) that pseudo-torsion classes and
pseudo-torsionfree classes are dual in the sense that they are perpendicular under strict
morphisms, i.e., there are no nonzero strict morphisms from a pseudo-torsion class to
the corresponding pseudo-torsionfree class.
Section 2 constructs the wall and chamber structure in the stability diagram of our
torsion class G. We assign a pseudo-torsion class to each pseudo-chamber, and show
these are different for different chambers (Theorem 2.21). We also assign two pseudo-
torsion classes to each wall, one for each side of the wall, the “positive” side and the
negative side. When the wall is “thin” we show that the pseudo-torsion class on the
positive side of the wall is a minimal extension of the one on the negative side of the
wall (Corollary 2.29). The minimal extending module is the pseudo-brick which labels
the wall. (Compare with the classical result in [BCZ].) The main theorem here is that
the pseudo-torsion class assigned to each point in the stability space Rn is constant
on each pseudo-chamber (Theorem 2.20) and on each green component of the union of
pseudo-walls (Definition 2.31).
We also consider “relative” pseudo-torsion classes in the pseudo-wide subcategory
which labels each thick wall. Fortunately, “relative” pseudo-wide subcategories are again
pseudo-wide subcategories which helps with induction on deeper “layers” of refinements
of thick walls.
We give several examples in this section. In one of these, “ghosts” and their corre-
sponding “derived objects” will appear. Ghosts are the remnants of objects of mod-Λ
which do not lie in the torsion class G. If A is one of these missing objects, we embed
A into an object B ∈G with quotient C = B/A. The “ghost” is the map B →C
considered as an object of the “pseudo-derived category” of G. The same missing object
can have several ghosts. Ghosts originated in [I2] and were further explained in [I3].
In Section 3, given a “green path”, we construct a “strict” Harder-Narasimhan strat-
ification of G. Each object of G will have a unique strict filtration with subquotients in
the “slices” of the HN-stratification. The objects in the slices can be arranged to form a
“strict forward Hom-orthogonal sequence” (FHO) (Definition 3.5). In the presentation of
this idea, it will be easier to construct the FHO-sequence first and the HN-stratification
as a consequence. This discussion is bases on [Br] and [I1].
Section 4 gives a preview of our next paper in which we explain how “thick” walls
create an issue that we solve by “resolving” each thick wall into “quasi-thin” layers and
obtain an infinite dimensional stability diagram. We show that this (in general) infinite

1. Basic definitions
Let Λ be a finite dimensional algebra over any field. Let n be the rank of Λ. Then,
up to isomorphisms, there are n simple modules S1, S2, · · · , Sn covered by n projective
modules P1, · · · , Pn. We take only finitely generated right Λ-modules. The category of
such modules is usually denoted mod-Λ.
We consider a fixed torsion class G in mod-Λ. This is a class of finitely generated
Λ-modules which is closed under extension and under taking of quotients. This means
that for any short exact sequence of Λ-modules
0 →A →B →C →0
if A, C are in G then so is B. And if B ∈G then C ∈G.
The kernel A of a morphism B →C in G might not be in G and this is a problem
that we need to get around. We will construct a new category: the “strict category G”
with the same objects as G but with only “strict morphisms”. This new category will
have good properties. Every morphism will have kernel, image and cokernel in G. Using
these new morphisms we will construct “pseudo-torsion classes” and pseudo-torsion free
classes.
1.1. Strict morphisms and strict extensions.
Definition 1.1. Given an object B in a torsion class G, a subobject is a submodule
B′ of B which lies in G. This includes 0 and B. By a strict subobject we mean a
subobject A ⊆B so that A ∩B′ also lies in G for any other subobject B′ of B. We use
the notation A ↣B to indicate a monomorphism whose image is a strict subobject of
B. We call such a monomorphism a cofibration.
Note that any simple subobject of an object B ∈G will be a strict subobject for
trivial reasons.
Lemma 1.2. If A is a strict subobject of B and B′ is another subobject of B. Then
A ∩B′ is a strict subobject of B′.
Proof. Any subobject X of B′ is also a subobject of B.
So, A ∩X lies in G.
But
(A ∩B′) ∩X = A ∩X. So, A ∩B′ ↣B′.
□
Theorem 1.3. Any composition of cofibrations is a cofibration.
Proof. Suppose that A ↣B ↣C and C′ is a subobject of C. Then B∩C′ is a subobject
of B. So, A ∩(B ∩C′) = A ∩C′ is a subobject of A. So, A ↣C.

1.2. Pseudo-torsion pairs. Now we define “pseudo-torsion classes” and “pseudo-torsionfree
classes” and show that they form strict Hom-perpendicular pairs.
Definition 1.18. A nonempty set of objects P ⊆G is called a pseudo-torsion class
if the following hold.
(1) P is closed under strict quotients, i.e., if A ↠B is a fibration and A ∈P then
so is B. In particular, 0 ∈P.
(2) P is closed under strict extensions.
Definition 1.19. A nonempty class Q ⊆G is called a pseudo-torsionfree class if the
following hold.
(1) Q is closed under strict subobjects, i.e., if A ↣B is a cofibration and B ∈Q
then so is A. In particular, 0 ∈Q.
(2) Q is closed under strict extensions.
Definition 1.20. For any X ⊆G, its right perpendicular class X ⊥is defined to be
the class of all Y ∈G so that there are no nonzero strict morphisms X →Y for any
X ∈X. Similarly, for any Y ⊆G, the left perpendicular class ⊥Y is the class of all
X ∈G so that there are no nonzero strict morphisms from X to any object in Y.
Lemma 1.21. Let X ⊂G be a subclass of objects in a torsion class G.
(1)
⊥X is a pseudo-torsion class.
(2) X ⊥is a pseudo-torsionfree class.
Proof. We prove (1) as the proof of (2) is analogous. Let M ∈⊥X and f : M ↠N be
a strict quotient of M. Suppose that g : N →X is a nonzero strict morphism where
X ∈X. Then, the composition M ↠N →X is a nonzero strict morphism contradicting
the assumption that M ∈⊥X. Therefore, ⊥X is closed under strict quotients.

4. Resolution of thick walls
This is a preview of our next paper [IM] in which we discuss maximal green sequences
given by green paths and resolve the problem that arises when the green path goes
through thick walls.
The poset of pseudo-torsion classes in a torsion class G forms a lattice since any
intersection of pseudo-torsion classes is a pseudo-torsion class and any collection of
pseudo-torsion classes generates a pseudo-torsion class. A maximal green sequence
in G is defined to be a maximal linearly order tower in this lattice. Given a green path θt
which only goes through thin walls DG(Bβ) at times tβ, we obtain a FHO-sequence {Bβ}
with π(Bβ) = tβ. This is a possibly countably infinite collection of pseudo-bricks. This
gives a possibly continuum number of pseudo-torsion classes P(θδ) where δ ∈R ranges
though the Dedekind cuts in the countable family of real numbers tβ. This includes both
the components of the complement of the set of tβ and the infinitesmal green and red
side of each pseudo-wall, namely P(θtβ) and P(θtβ).
When a green path goes through a thick wall, such as the null domain for a tame
hereditary algebra, it cannot distinguish between the objects in W(θ) since they all have
the same (or proportional) dimension vectors. To separate these objects we need a new
stability space which may have uncountable dimension. We will assume the cardinality
of the underlying field K is at most that of R. Then the set of isomorphism classes of
finitely generated Λ-modules will have cardinality at most the continuum. Then we can
find smooth green paths which pass through a possibly continuum number of walls at
different times.
