POLYNOMIAL INTERPOLATION OF PARTIAL FUNCTIONS
IN FINITE ALGEBRAS WITH A MAL’CEV TERM
ERHARD AICHINGER, MARIO KAPL, AND BERNARDO ROSSI
Abstract. We provide polynomial completeness results for finite algebras in
congruence permutable varieties. In 2001, Idziak and Słomczyńska introduced
the completeness concept of being polynomially rich: a finite algebra is polyno-
mially rich if every function preserving congruences and the Tame Congruence
Theory labelling of prime quotients in the congruence lattice is a polynomial
function of the algebra. We call a finite algebra strictly polynomially rich if
every partial congruence and type preserving function is a polynomial function,
and we describe strictly polynomially rich algebras in congruence permutable
varieties.
1. Introduction
The guiding question of this paper is the characterization of those partial
functions on an algebraic structure that can be interpolated by polynomials.
We seek for a description of those algebras in which the interpolable functions
admit a concise description.
Here, we adopt the viewpoint of universal alge-
bra [BS81, MMT87] and consider an algebraic structure as a universe together
with a set of operations.
For instance, the symmetric group S3 := (S3, ◦) is
an algebraic structure. Its polynomial functions are the functions of the form
(x1, . . . , xn) 7→t(x1, . . . , xn, s1, . . . , sn), where n ∈N and t(x1, . . . , xn, s1, . . . , sn)
is a well formed expression using the operations of the algebra and constants
s1, . . . , sn taken from the algebra. For example, f(x1, x2) := x1 ◦((1 3 2) ◦x2) is
a binary polynomial function of S3.
Let A be any algebraic structure, let k ∈N, let T be a subset of Ak, and sup-
pose that a function f : T →A is given by a table of its values. We would like to
determine whether f can be interpolated by a polynomial function of A; in other
words, we ask whether there exists a k-ary polynomial function p of A such that
the restriction p|T of p to T satisfies p|T = f. When the answer is “yes”, an expres-
sion representing p witnesses this answer. But how could we witness the answer

Another completeness property has been introduced in [IS01]. It starts from the
observation that on an algebra A = (A, F), a function f : Ak →A is congruence
preserving if the expanded algebra A+f := (A, F ∪{f}) has the same congruence
relations as A. Denoting the congruences of A by Con(A), we can express that A
is affine complete by saying that every function f with Con(A + f) = Con(A) is
polynomial. In the 1980’s, D. Hobby and R. McKenzie provided another invariant
of polynomial functions: a pair of congruences (α, β) ∈Con(A) with α < β and
no γ ∈Con(A) with α < γ < β is called a prime section of Con(A), and we write
α ≺β. With each prime section, [HM88] associates a type which, sloppily put,
describes how polynomial functions behave on β-classes modulo α. The labelled
congruence lattice of A is denoted by Con∗(A): It is the digraph with vertex
set V := Con(A), edges E := {(α, β) ∈V 2 | α ≺β}, together with a labelling
function t with domain E that associates to each edge its type. P. Idziak and
K. Słomczyńska [IS01] addressed the following question: which algebras have
the property that every function f : Ak →A with Con∗(A) = Con∗(A + f) is
polynomial? They called such algebras polynomially rich and characterized those
finite algebras in congruence permutable varieties that have the property that all
their homomorphic images are polynomially rich [IS01, Theorem 24].

3. Characterization of strictly polynomially rich Mal’cev
algebras
Our description of strictly polynomially rich algebras will involve certain con-
cepts from universal algebra [BS81]. In particular, we will use congruence re-
lations and their (binary) commutator as introduced, e.g., in [MMT87]. Sev-
eral useful properties of polynomial functions with respect to commutators are
listed in [Aic06, Section 2]. In a Mal’cev algebra, the largest congruence γ with
[γ, β] ≤α is called the centralizer of β modulo α and denoted by (α : β). In [IS01],
the following condition has been used to describe polynomially rich algebras.
Definition 3.1 (cf. [IS01, AI04, AM09]). A Mal’cev algebra A satisfies the con-
dition (SC1) if for every strictly meet irreducible congruence µ of A we have
(µ : µ+) ≤µ+.
We also need the following condition on prime quotients; this condition is a
generalization of the condition (AB2) used in [AI04].
Definition 3.2 (cf. [Ros24a, Definition 1.2]). Let A be a Mal’cev algebra, let p
be a prime number, and let γ ∈Con A. We say that (A, γ) has property (ABp) if
for all α, β ∈I[0A, γ] with α ≺β and [β, β] ≤α, and for each a ∈A we have that
|(a/α)/(β/α)| ∈{1, p}. In other words, for each abelian prime quotient ⟨α, β⟩in
the interval I[0A, γ], each β-class is the union of either 1 or p distinct α-classes.
We say that A has property (ABp) if (A, 1A) has property (ABp).
The notion of type-preserving function was first introduced in [IS01].
The
premise is that polynomial functions preserve the labelling of prime quotients
with the TCT-types {1, . . . 5}: Let A be a finite algebra and let f : Ak →A
be a polynomial function. Then the algebra A + f and the algebra A have the
same congruence lattice and for each prime quotient ⟨η, θ⟩the type of ⟨η, θ⟩is
the same in A and A + f. Hence for finite algebras, we could define that f
preserves the types of A if for each prime quotient ⟨η, θ⟩the type of ⟨η, θ⟩is
the same in A and A + f. In the case of Mal’cev algebras, the types of prime
quotients are determined by commutators, and commutators can be expressed by
the preservation of certain 4-ary relations [AM07, Lemma 2.4]. Thus we rather
use the following definition (cf. [AM09, Section 5]):

Proof of Theorem 3.10. The equivalence of (1), (2) and (5) follows from [Aic00,

that µ1 is not abelian. Hence Proposition 8.2 implies that µ1 is an atom. By
the definition of homogeneous series µi/µi−1 is homogeneous in A/µi−1 for each
i ∈{1, . . . , n}. Hence, since A/µi−1 is strictly 4-polynomially rich, Lemma 12.6
implies that µi/µi−1 is not abelian and Proposition 8.2 implies that µi/µi−1 is an
atom. Thus, (µ0, . . . , µn) is a maximal chain in Con A and each interval is not
abelian. Let ⟨α, β⟩be a prime quotient in Con A, and let γ0, . . . , γn be a maximal

j ∈{1, . . . , n} such that I[γi−1, γi] ↭I[µj−1, µj], and therefore, by Lemma 6.4,
