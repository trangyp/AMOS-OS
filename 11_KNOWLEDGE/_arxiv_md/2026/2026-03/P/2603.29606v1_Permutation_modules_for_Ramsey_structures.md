Abstract. Suppose R is a commutative ring and G is a group
acting on a set W. We consider the RG-module RW in the case
where G is the automorphism group of an ω-categorical structure
M and W is, for example, M n (for n ∈N). We develop methods
which may provide information about two questions in the case
where R is a field F: whether FW has a.c.c. on submodules; and
in the case where M is finitely homogeneous, whether FW is of
finite composition length. In the case where M is a Ramsey struc-
ture and so G is extremely amenable, we give a simple ‘decision
procedure’ for membership in a submodule of RW specified by a
given generating set. If F is a field, we show that there is a dual-
ity between submodules of FW and the topological FG-module of
definable functions from W to F.
2020 MSC: Primary 20B27; Secondary 20B07, 03C15.
Keywords: Oligomorphic permutation groups; ω-categorical struc-
tures; structural Ramsey theory; extreme amenability.

1.2. Ramsey structures and a decision procedure. The aim of
this paper is to initiate a consideration of Questions 1.1 and 1.2 in
the case where M is a (reduct of a) ω-categorical Ramsey structure.
Although there are ω-categorical structures which do not have this
property (see [9]), those that do have it form a broad and robust class
of interesting examples (see [13], for example) which is conjectured to
include all finitely homogeneous structures. This approach is clearly
motivated by work on reducts and infinite domain CSP’s (see [4], for
example).
If M is a (ordered) Ramsey structure, then by the theorem of Kechris,
Pestov and Todorˇcevi´c ([16]), the group G = Aut(M), considered as
a topological group with the usual topology on a permutation group,
is extremely amenable: meaning that every non-empty compact space
on which it acts continuously has a G-fixed point (see 1.3.3). Under
this hypothesis, we can develop a duality between the module FW and
the topological module F[W] of definable functions W →F. These
are functions which are constant on the parts of a partition of W into

1.3. Background definitions and notation. In this section we col-
lect together some facts, terminology and notation about permutation
groups and model theory. The reference [17] gives a much more com-
prehensive account of the background.
1.3.1. Permutation groups. Suppose G is a group acting (on the left)
on a set X and A ⊆X. We denote by G(A) the pointwise stabiliser
{g ∈G : g(a) = a for all a ∈A}. If the action is faithful, then we call
G a permutation group (on X) and we may regard G as a subgroup of
Sym(X), the symmetric group of all permutations of X. In this case it
is useful to think of Sym(X) as a topological group and give G the rela-
tive topology where basic open sets are left cosets of subgroups G(A) for
finite A ⊆X. Note that if g ∈G, then gG(A) = {h ∈G : h|A = g|A},
so the topology here is that of G ⊆XX with the product topology
where X has the discrete topology (here and elsewhere, g|A denotes the
restriction of the function g to A). The topology on Sym(X) is some-
times called the topology of pointwise convergence, and for countable

With this topology, a subgroup of Sym(X) is closed iff it is the auto-

iff it is closed and all of its orbits on X are finite (in which case, it is
profinite).
A subgroup H of G ≤Sym(X) is open iff there is a finite set A ⊆X
with G(A) ≤H. Let a be a tuple enumerating the elements of A and
let D be the G-orbit containing a. For g, h ∈G write E(ga, ha) iff
g−1h ∈H. This is a well defined, G-invariant equivalence relation on
D and the stabilizer of the E-class containing a is H.
Note that if R is any commutative ring and G ≤Sym(X), then the
action of G on the permutation module RX, considered as a discrete
space, is continuous.
1.3.2. Model theory. The notation (and abuse thereof) is fairly stan-
dard. Suppose L is a first-order language and M an L-structure. We
usually do not distinguish notationally between the structure and its
domain (underlying set): thus M will also denote the latter. We denote
by Aut(M) the automorphism group of M. If M is a countably infi-
nite ω-categorical structure (we usually just say ‘ω-categorical’ here)
then by the Ryll-Nardzewski Theorem G = Aut(M) is oligomorphic on
M: it has finitely many orbits on M n for all n ∈ω (and conversely).
Equivalently, G(A) has finitely many orbits on M for all finite A ⊆M.
In this case a subset of M n is A-definable iff it is G(A)-invariant and
n-tuples b, c ∈M n are in the same G(A)-orbit iff they have the same
(complete) type over A. We denote the latter condition by the usual
model-theoretic notation tp(b/A) = tp(c/A), or b ≡A c. We also use
this notation (with some care) when a, b are elements of other sets on
which G is acting continuously, such as a permutation module FW or
the module of definable functions F[W].

If D is a ∅-definable subset of M n and E is a ∅-definable equivalence
relation on D, we refer to the equivalence classes as imaginary elements
of M (of sort D/E). We denote the set of these (varying D, E, and
including M) as M eq. If G = Aut(M), then G acts continuously on M eq
and if e ∈M eq, then its stabilizer Ge is open in G. If M is (countable)
ω-categorical then the converse is true: any open subgroup of G is of
the form Ge for some e ∈M eq. Up to interdefinability, M eq is closed
under finite unions of sorts. So being a sort in M eq is the same thing
as being a finite union of G-orbits on M eq.
If M is ω-categorical, a ∈M and B is a finite subset of M, the model-
theoretic notions of a being in the algebraic (resp. definable) closure
of B correspond to a being in a finite G(B)-orbit (resp. of size 1). This
extends to M eq in an obvious way.
Suppose that L is a (relational) language and M is a countable L-
structure. The class of finite L-structures which can be embedded into
M is referred to as the age of M, denoted by Age(M). We say that M
is a Fra¨ıss´e structure if every isomorphism between finite substructures
of M extends to an automorphism of M (other terminology used is
that M is homogeneous, or ultrahomogeneous). We can make the same
definition for arbitrary structures M (where function symbols are in the
language), but for the purposes here, we should assume local finiteness
of M: every finite subset is contained in a finite substructure. This
holds automatically if M is ω-categorical. The classical result of Fra¨ıss´e
says that M is a Fra¨ıss´e structure iff Age(M) is an amalgamation class.
Moreover, every amalgamation class of finite structures with countably
many isomorphism types is the age of a (unique up to isomorphism)
countable Fra¨ıss´e structure, referred to as the Fra¨ıss´e limit of the class.
Note that if G a closed permutation group on a countable set X, then
the canonical structure associated with (G; X) is a Fra¨ıss´e structure
with automorphism group G. So any general result about automor-
phism groups of Fra¨ıss´e structures is a general result about closed per-
mutation groups on a countable set.
