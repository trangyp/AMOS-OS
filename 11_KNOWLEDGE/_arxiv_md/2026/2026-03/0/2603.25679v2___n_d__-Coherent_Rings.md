(n, d)-COHERENT RINGS
RAFAEL PARRA
Abstract. We investigate finiteness conditions on modules of bounded projective dimension
and their connection with generalized notions of coherence. For a ring R, we consider the class
FP≤d
n (R) of finitely n-presented modules of projective dimension at most d and develop the
corresponding relative homological theory. We establish several characterizations of left (n, d)-
coherent rings in the sense of Mao and Ding [44], in terms of FP≤d
n (R) and the associated classes
of FP≤d
n -injective, FP≤d
n -projective, FP≤d
n -flat, and FP≤d
n -cotorsion modules. As a consequence,
when d ≥gl.D(R) or d = ∞, we recover Costa’s n-coherence [17] and obtain new characterizations
of regularly coherent rings.
1. Introduction
It is well known that finitely generated and finitely presented modules over a ring R play a
central role in homological algebra. Many homological properties of modules can be expressed
functorially in terms of these classes. A significant development in this direction is due to Lee
[40], who introduced a generalization of the classical notion of coherence. A ring R is said to be
Lee d-coherent (for d > 0 or d = ∞) if every finitely generated submodule of a free R-module
whose projective dimension is at most d −1 is finitely presented. Equivalently, R is Lee d-coherent
if every finitely 1-presented R-module of projective dimension at most d is finitely 2-presented.
Motivated by these ideas, in this paper we investigate a refinement of the class of finitely n-
presented modules. Recall that an R-module M is said to be finitely n-presented if there exists
an exact sequence Fn →Fn−1 →· · · →F1 →F0 →M →0, where each Fi is a finitely generated
projective (equivalently, free) R-module for 0 ≤i ≤n. For n = 1, this recovers the classical notion
of a finitely presented module, while for n = 0 it coincides with that of a finitely generated module.
We concentrate on the subclass consisting of finitely n-presented modules of bounded projective
dimension, and we denote by FP≤d
n (R) the class of finitely n-presented R-modules whose projective
dimension is at most d.
Our approach is further motivated by the notion of (n, d)-coherent rings introduced by Mao and
Ding [44], which provides a common framework encompassing both Lee’s d-coherence and Costa’s
n-coherence. Recall that, according to Costa, a ring R is n-coherent if every n-presented R-module
is (n + 1)-presented. In particular, 0-coherent (resp. 1-coherent) rings coincide with Noetherian
(resp. coherent) rings. For n ∈N∗and d ∈N∗∪{∞}, a ring R is called (n, d)-coherent if every
finitely (n −1)-presented submodule of a free R-module with projective dimension at most d −1
is finitely n-presented.
The main contribution of this work is to provide a unified approach to finiteness conditions
in homological algebra via the classes FP≤d
n (R).
These classes naturally induce the notions of
FP≤d
n -injective and FP≤d
n -flat modules, defined by the vanishing of Ext1
R(M, −) and TorR
1 (−, M),
respectively, for all M ∈FP≤d
n (R). Using this framework, we characterize (n, d)-coherent rings in
terms of the associated injective, projective, flat, and cotorsion modules. Moreover, if d ≥gl.D(R)
or d = ∞, then (n, d)-coherence coincides with Costa’s n-coherence, and the corresponding relative
notions recover those previously studied in the literature; see [58, 59, 11, 61, 33]. In addition,
the study of the classes FP≤d
n (R) is motivated by their potential applications as a tool in the
investigation of algebraic K-theory of rings.

2. Finitely n-presented modules of bounded projective dimension and
(n, d)-coherent modules
For each d ∈N, let P≤d(R) denote the class of R-modules of projective dimension at most
d. This class is closed under direct summands and is resolving; that is, it contains all projective
modules and is closed under extensions and kernels of epimorphisms in R-Mod. Recall that a class
C of R-modules is said to be thick if it is closed under direct summands and satisfies the following
condition: for every short exact sequence A ↣B ↠C, whenever two of the modules A, B and C,
belong to C, so does the third. In particular, the class of modules of finite projective dimension,
P<∞(R) := S
d∈N P≤d(R), is thick.
Let n ∈N. An R-module M is called finitely n-presented if there exists an exact sequence
Fn →Fn−1 →· · · →F1 →F0 ↠M, where each Fi is a finitely generated projective (equivalently,
free) R-module for 0 ≤i ≤n. Such a sequence is referred to as a finite n-presentation of M.
The class of all finitely n-presented R-modules is denoted by FPn(R). In particular, FP0(R) is
the class of all finitely generated R-modules, and FP1(R) is the class of all finitely presented R-
modules.
Moreover, the class FP∞(R) := T
n≥0 FPn(R) consists of all R-modules admitting a
projective resolution by finitely generated modules. Modules in FP∞(R) will be referred to as
finitely ∞-presented R-modules. Clearly, proj(R) ⊆FP∞(R), where proj(R) denotes the class of
finitely generated projective R-modules. For convenience, we also set FP−1(R) := R-Mod. These
classes form the chain
proj(R) ⊆FP∞(R) ⊆· · · ⊆FPn(R) ⊆· · · ⊆FP1(R) ⊆FP0(R) ⊆FP−1(R) = R-Mod.

The classes FPn(R) satisfy several closure properties; see [27, Thm. 2.1.2]. Given a short exact

(a) If A ∈FPn(R) and B ∈FPn+1(R), then C ∈FPn+1(R).

3. (n, d)-coherent rings
The notion of (n, d)-coherence was introduced by Mao and Ding [44, Def. 4.1]. A ring R is said
to be left (n, d)-coherent, where n ∈N∗and d ∈N∗∪∞, if every finitely n-presented R-module
of projective dimension at most d is finitely (n + 1)-presented. On the other hand, following Lee
[40], a ring R is said to be left Lee d-coherent, where d > 0 or d = ∞, if every finitely generated
submodule of a free R-module with projective dimension at most d −1 is finitely presented. A
natural extension of this condition to n ≥2 is given by requiring that every finitely (n−1)-presented
submodule of a free R-module with projective dimension at most d −1 be finitely n-presented.
The following proposition shows that this condition is equivalent to (n, d)-coherence.
Proposition 3.1. Let R be a ring, n ∈N∗, and d ∈N∗∪{∞}. Then the following statements are
equivalent:
(1) R is left (n, d)-coherent.
(2) Every finitely (n −1)-presented submodule of a free R-module with projective dimension at
most d −1 is finitely n-presented.
Proof. (1)⇒(2). Let K be a finitely (n −1)-presented submodule of a free R-module F (without
loss of generality, finitely generated) with pdR(K) ≤d −1. Then the quotient F/K is finitely
n-presented and satisfies pdR(F/K) ≤d. By assumption, F/K is finitely (n + 1)-presented, which
implies that K is finitely n-presented.
(2)⇒(1). Let M be a finitely n-presented R-module with pdR(M) ≤d. Then there exists a
short exact sequence K ↣F ↠M, where F is a finitely generated free R-module and K is finitely
(n −1)-presented. Since pdR(M) ≤d, we have pdR(K) ≤d −1. By (2), K is finitely n-presented,
and hence M is finitely (n + 1)-presented. Therefore, R is left (n, d)-coherent.
□
In particular, R is left Lee d-coherent if and only if it is left (1, d)-coherent. One of the aims of
this paper is to extend the results of [40] to the case n ≥2.

Remark 3.7. Let R be a ring, n ∈N∗, and d ∈N∗∪{∞}.

is (n, d)-coherent). Let n ≥2, and consider the ring R of [41, Ex. 5.12]. Although modules

necessary adjustments. This ring has the following properties: every finitely n-presented

d ∈N∗.
(f) The property of being left (n, d)-coherent is not, in general, preserved under quotient

the ring of integers, and let X be a (possibly infinite) set of commuting indeterminates.

to a quotient of some ring Z[X]. Consequently, a quotient of a left (1, d)-coherent ring
need not be left (1, d)-coherent.
