G.X. Dong,1 X.B. Wang,1 N. Michel,2, 3 and M. Płoszajczak4, ∗

(Dated: March 10, 2026)
In stellar hydrogen burning, the CNO cycle dominates, with the 14N(p,γ)15O reaction being
the slowest process. Consequently, this reaction critically inﬂuences the solar composition, CNO
neutrino ﬂuxes, and the evolution of star clusters and galaxies.
Recent direct measurements of
14N(p,γ)15O have reported an enhanced astrophysical S-factor. This work presents a microscopic
theoretical study of the 14N(p,γ)15O reaction using the Gamow shell model in the coupled-channel
representation (GSM-CC). The calculations achieve good agreement with experimental data for
both the total S-factors and the separate contributions from transitions to the ground state and
excited states of 15O.
However, the predicted S-factor at zero energy exceeds the experimental
value. Based on the computed S-factors, the derived carbon and nitrogen abundances align closely
with predictions from recent 14N(p,γ)15O cross-section measurements, yet remain signiﬁcantly lower
than the latest solar neutrino observation values.
Introduction – The key input of the standard solar
model is the solar metallicity, which is evaluated by the
ratio of elements heavier than helium to hydrogen in the
Sun [1, 2]. The early estimation of solar metallicity was
derived from the spectra of the solar photosphere, which
agreed with helioseimology measurements [3]. Later, us-
ing more advanced techniques [4], a 30%–40% reduc-
tion [5–7] of solar metallicity has been found as com-
pared to the traditional modeling [8].
This contradic-
tion, the so-called “solar composition problem”, is the
long-standing puzzle for over twenty years.
In the Sun, ∼2% of the energy is produced by the
carbon-nitrogen-oxygen (CNO) cycle [9]. For stars about
1.3 times more massive than the Sun, this process domi-
nates energy production [10], and its importance depends
on metallicity. 14N(p,γ)15O is the slowest process in the
CNO cycle.
Therefore, a precise determination of the
14N(p,γ)15O astrophysical S factor is important not only
for studying the composition of the Sun and CNO neutri-
nos, but also for understanding the evolution and age of
star clusters and galaxies. Solar neutrinos provide direct
information about the interior of the Sun, making them
an eﬀective tool for solving the problem of the Sun’s com-
position.
Recently, the solar neutrinos from the CNO
cycle were measured by Borexino collaboration [11, 12]
providing important insight about the solar metallicity.
The observed ﬂux of CNO neutrinos from the decay of
15O, which is directly related with the rate of the reac-
tion 14N(p,γ)15O and, hence, the astrophysical S factor,
suggests the enhanced production rate of 15O.
A lot of eﬀorts have been made to measure the
14N(p,γ)15O reaction cross section.
Despite that, still
the signiﬁcant diﬀerences remain between various exper-

iments concerning both the total zero energy astrophys-
ical factor S(0) and the individual contributions to the
S(0) of the transitions to the ground state and excited
states of 15O. Several measurements, extending to lower
energies, have been performed at LUNA underground
laboratory [13–17].
Direct measurement at LENA fa-
cility [18] found an almost twice as large astrophysical
factor at zero energy for the transition to the ground
state Sg.s.(0) than previously obtained at LUNA. Five
angles of diﬀerential cross sections were measured by Li
et al. at Notre Dame for Ep = 0.7 to 3.6 MeV [19]. Other
experiments done at the CASPAR underground accelera-
tor [20] and the HZDR tandem accelerator [21], provided
additional constraints on the R-matrix ﬁtting. The to-
tal non-resonant S factors were also measured with the
activation method in the range of center-of-mass (c.m.)
energies from 0.55 to 1.4 MeV [22]. Last year, the new
Solar Fusion III Compilation (SF-III) has recommended
the value S(0)=1.68 ± 0.14 keV b [23] However, the recent
measurement at HINEG facility in Hefei of the S-factors
for all transitions in the energy range Ep = 0.11 −0.26
MeV [24] gave a value of S(0)=1.92±0.08 keV b, which is
14% higher than the recommended value. This value of
S(0) implies a high metallicity composition for the Sun,
although it is also consistent with a low metallicity com-
position with a conﬁdence level of 1σ.
At energies corresponding to solar temperatures, di-
rect measurement of the 14N(p,γ)15O reaction cross sec-
tion is impossible under present experimental conditions.
In this case, theoretical studies are necessary. Theoret-
ical studies of 14N(p,γ)15O reaction were done mainly
using the R-matrix method [14, 15, 18–21, 24–28], or the
modiﬁed potential cluster model [29]. In this work, we
present the ﬁrst microscopic study of this reaction us-
ing the Gamow shell model in the coupled-channels rep-
resentation (GSM-CC) [30].

The astrophysical factor at zero energy S(0) plays a
crucial role in nuclear astrophysics. In the Sun, tempera-
tures in the solar core range from 0.020 to 0.035 MeV [22].
As shown in Fig. 2(a), values of the S factor at Ec.m. ≥0
are nearly constant and match the zero-energy S factor.
Therefore, the comparison of calculated and experimen-
tal S factors in Fig. 3 is done at Ec.m. = 0. The experi-
mental astrophysical factors in this ﬁgure are derived by
extrapolating the measured cross sections to zero energy
using the R-matrix approach.
It can be seen that the GSM-CC(1) and GSM-CC(2)
calculations give an astrophysical factor S(0) that is
larger than the value recommended by SF-III [23] and
previous experimental data. However, the GSM-CC(2)
calculation yields the S(0) factor (1.996 keV b) which is
compatible within error bars with a recent measurement
at the HINEG facility (1.92 ± 0.08 keV b) [24].
It is worth noticing that GSM-CC(1) and GSM-CC(2)
calculations, which yield quite similar energy spectrum
of 15O, predict signiﬁcantly diﬀerent S factor at the zero
energy. They are: S(0)=2.263 keV b and S(0)=1.996 keV
b for GSM-CC(1) and GSM-CC(2), respectively.

respect, energies of 5/2+

source of uncertainties in our calculations.
To further investigate impact of the astrophysical S
factor calculated in GSM-CC on the composition of the
Sun, we determine the abundances of C and N nuclei
relative to hydrogen (NCN) in the Sun’s photosphere us-
ing the method described in Ref. [12].
Based on the
B23-GS98 analysis combined with recent global neutrino
measurements and the GSM-CC astrophysical factor, the
derived NCN values are 3.61 × 10−4 and 4.15 × 10−4 for
GSM-CC(1) and GSM-CC(2), respectively.
Current predictions of NCN still exhibit signiﬁcant un-
certainties.
The measurements based on spectroscopy
of the photosphere yield NCN = 4.143 × 10−4 according
to the GS98 compilations [8].
For comparison, solar
neutrino measurements give NCN = 5.81+1.22
−0.94 × 10−4 [55],
while the latest measurement of the cross section of the
14N(p,γ)15O reaction at HINEG gives NCN = 4.45+0.69
−0.61 ×
10−4 [24].
GSM-CC(1) calculation seems to underes-
timate the NCN compared to both the spectroscopy
measurement and neutrino data, whereas GSM-CC(2)
demonstrates improved consistency with the data.
Conclusions – The 14N(p,γ)15O reaction cross section
has been studied here using the microscopic GSM-CC
approach which provides a uniﬁed description of spec-
tra and reaction cross-sections. As the slowest process
in the CNO cycle, this reaction is crucial for address-
ing the solar metallicity problem. Notably, recent direct
measurements [24] report the value of S factor that is sig-
niﬁcantly higher than the currently recommended value
in the SF-III compilation [23]. To demonstrate theoret-

ical uncertainties, we implemented two variants of the
GSM-CC calculation: the one from ﬁtting the energies
of low-lying states of 14N and 15O, and the other one
with a slight increase of the depth of the Woods-Saxon
potential for the sd shells. After adjusting the correc-
tive factors in the GSM-CC calculation, both approaches
yield a similar spectroscopy of 15O and the calculated
astrophysical factors show good agreement with exper-
imental data.
The S factors at zero energy calculated
in GSM-CC are higher than most previous experimen-
tal extrapolations. The GSM-CC(2) calculation reduces
S(0) as compared with GSM-CC(1), and is compatible
with the value provided in the recent measurement at
HINEG [24].
The microscopic calculations presented in this work
yield C and N abundances NCN ∼3.61–4.15×10−4, consis-
tent within 1σ conﬁdence level with recent cross-section
measurements at HINEG facility [24], but signiﬁcantly
lower than NCN values derived from the solar neutrino
measurements [55].
