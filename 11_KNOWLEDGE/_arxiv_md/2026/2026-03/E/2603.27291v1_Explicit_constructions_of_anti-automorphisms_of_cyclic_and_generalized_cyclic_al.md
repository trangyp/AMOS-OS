SUSANNE PUMPL¨UN
Abstract. While involutions on central simple algebras have been studied extensively and are
well understood, much less is known about general anti-automorphisms.
We present norm criteria for the existence of anti-automorphisms as well as explicit construc-
tions of anti-automorphisms, on cyclic and generalized cyclic algebras. Our approach describes
anti-automorphisms as polynomial maps and unifies existing approaches.
It recovers classical
criteria for the existence of involutions as special cases.
We obtain norm conditions for the existence of anti-automorphisms of the second kind and of
infinite order on the ring of twisted Laurent series K((t; σ)) over a field K and the ring of twisted
Laurent series D((t; σ)) over a division algebra D that is finite-dimensional over its center.
Our construction produces all possible anti-automorphisms of proper nonassociative cyclic or
generalized cyclic algebras, i.e. for special classes of Petit algebras which can be viewed as canonical
generalizations of associative cyclic and generalized cyclic central simple algebras.
1. Introduction
The existence and classification of involutions is a classical question in the theory of central simple
algebras. Involutions on central simple algebras either preserve the center (involutions of the first
kind) or act on the center nontrivially (involutions of the second kind). Their study involves for
instance Brauer groups, and quadratic and hermitian forms [KMRT1998].
It is well-known that a central simple algebra over F admits an involution of the second kind which
leaves a subfield F0 of F fixed, if and only if its Brauer class lies in the kernel of the corestriction
map CorF/F0 : Br(F) →Br(F0). For cyclic algebras (K/F, σ, a) in particular, we thus know that
CorF/F0([(K/F, σ, a)]) = [(K/F, σ, aτ(a))], where τ generates the automorphism group of F/F0, and
consequently, (K/F, σ, a) admits an involution of the second kind, if and only if aτ(a) ∈NK/F (K×).
We call such a condition a norm condition.
While the existence of involutions (anti-automorphisms of order two) on central simple algebras is
well understood, much less is known about general anti-automorphisms of central simple algebras,
see [Lew2006, Lew2003, LewTig1999] for some results. Explicit examples of central simple division
algebras over algebraic number fields F which have anti-automorphisms of the second kind, but no
involution can be found in [MorST2005].
We will distinguish between anti-automorphisms of a central simple algebra A over a field F which
are F-linear (of the first kind), and F0-linear (of the second kind), where F0 is a proper subfield of F.
More precisely, we will investigate anti-automorphisms of the second kind that are τ-semilinear for
some τ ∈Aut(F) with fixed field F0 = F τ. Unlike in the case of involutions, for anti-automorphisms
of the second kind the extension F/F0 will generally not be quadratic ´etale. Also note that our
definition of anti-automorphisms of the second kind differs from the one presented in [Lew2003].
We will focus on anti-automorphisms both of cyclic algebras and of generalized cyclic algebras as
defined in [Jac1996] (note also the slightly different approach described in [Tignol1987]). Generalized
cyclic algebras do not need to be crossed products, e.g. see [Hanke2001, Hanke2004, Hanke2005].

Our approach is based on describing cyclic algebras as quotient rings K[t; σ]/K[t; σ](tn −a), respec-
tively generalized cyclic algebras as quotient rings D[t; σ]/D[t; σ](tm −d) with D a central simple
algebra, employing skew polynomials.
We show that all anti-automorphisms of a cyclic algebra
(K/F, σ, a) that restrict to some anti-automorphism on K, and under some mild conditions also
all anti-automorphisms of a generalized cyclic division algebra (D, σ, d) that restrict to some anti-
automorphism on D, are canonically induced by anti-automorphisms of the skew polynomial rings
K[t; σ], respectively D[t; σ]. They map t to αtk for some suitable positive integer k and α ∈F ×
(respectively in C×). We call an anti-automorphisms (resp., an isomorphism) mapping t to αtk a
monomial anti-automorphism (resp., isomorphism) of degree k.
In order to describe the anti-automorphisms we obtain new observations also on isomorphisms
between cyclic and between nonassociative cyclic algebras. Monomial isomorphism of degree k > 1
that extend τ can only exist between associative cyclic algebras (Theorem 4.2), and all isomorphism
between proper nonassociative cyclic algebras that extend τ will be monomial of degree one and
require that σ and τ commute.
More precisely, suppose that τ ∈Aut(K) and τστ −1 = σk for some k ∈{1, . . . , n −1} such that
gcd(k, n) = 1. Let α ∈K×. Then there exists a ring isomorphism extending τ that maps t to αtk
for k > 1 if and only if (K/F, σ, a) is associative and
NK/F (α)bk = τ(a)
(Corollary 4.4). Monomial anti-automorphisms of degree ℓ> 1 greater than one that extend τ do
not exist on cyclic algebras when τ ∈Aut(K) commutes with σ (Theorem 3.2).

one exist that extend τ, assuming there is some α ∈K× such that NK/F (α) = τ(a)a (Theorem 3.2

there exist τ ∈Aut(K), such that gcd(k, n) = 1 and τστ −1 = σk for some integer k ∈{1, . . . , n −1}
such that gcd(k, n) = 1. If additionally NK/F (α) = τ(a)ak then there exists an anti-automorphism
extending τ that is monomial of degree k > 2 (Theorem 4.8).
The situation for generalized cyclic algebras is similar, with some small modifications (e.g., Theo-
rem 6.5, Theorem 7.5). Most notably, when σ has order m, monomial anti-automorphisms of degree
greater than one can only exist on associative generalized cyclic algebras, and all proper nonasso-
ciative algebras (D, σ, d) only admit monomial anti-automorphisms of degree one. The conditions
for the existence of anti-automorphisms again involve a norm condition (Theorem 7.5).
This paper grew out of our attempt to understand what turned out to be an error in the proof
of [MorST2005, Proposition 3.4]. This proposition displays an elegant example of a cyclic division
algebra over a number field, that admits no involution but admits an anti-automorphism of the
second kind and lays the groundwork for the main result [MorST2005, Theorem 4.4] which contains
yet another elegant construction of such a scenario, this time for a twisted Laurent series ring
K((t; σ)). Both proofs explicitly write down some anti-automorphisms of the second kind, which
however, are not well-defined. The proofs for both results are repaired in Section 5 and show that
[MorST2005, Theorem 4.4] indeed is correct (with a different proof) (stated in Theorem 5.5) and that
[MorST2005, Proposition 3.4] has to be slightly adjusted (Proposition 5.1). Indeed, we give criteria
for the existence of anti-automorphisms of infinite order on the twisted ring of Laurent polynomials
K((t; σ)); itself a central simple algebra over F((tn)) (Theorem 5.2), and for the existence of anti-
automorphisms of infinite order on the twisted ring of Laurent polynomials D((t; σ)) for a division
algebra D (Theorem 7.10).
We also correct the norm criteria presented in the Special Case 1 and the General Case in [Hanke2007,
Section 3] (Remark 7.8).

2. Preliminaries
2.1. Nonassociative rings and algebras. Let R be a unital commutative ring and let A be an
R-module. We call A a (nonassociative) algebra over R if there exists an R-bilinear map A×A 7→A,
(x, y) 7→x · y, the multiplication of A. We will just denote this multiplication by juxtaposition. We
will only consider unital algebras, which possess an element denoted by 1, such that 1x = x1 = x
for all x ∈A.
The associativity in A is measured by the left nucleus defined as Nucl(A) = {x ∈A | [x, A, A] = 0},
the middle nucleus Nucm(A) = {x ∈A | [A, x, A] = 0} and the right nucleus Nucr(A) = {x ∈
A | [A, A, x] = 0}, where [x, y, z] = (xy)z −x(yz). Their intersection Nuc(A) = {x ∈A | [x, A, A] =
[A, x, A] = [A, A, x] = 0} is the nucleus of A.
The commuter of A is defined as Comm(A) =
{x ∈A | xy = yx for all y ∈A} and the center of a nonassociative algebra A is defined as C(A) =
Nuc(A) ∩Comm(A).
Every unital nonassociative algebra is a unital nonassociative ring (A, +, ·) with the distributivity
laws satisfied by definition of its multiplication. Conversely, every nonassociative ring can be viewed
as a nonassociative algebra over its center, or any subring of it.
A nonassociative ring (or algebra) A is called a proper nonassociative ring (or algebra) if it is not
associative.
Write Aop = {xop | x ∈A} for the opposite algebra of A. We recall that Aop has the same addition
as A and multiplication given by xop ◦yop = (yx)op.
Let R = D[t; σ] be the ring of skew polynomials of a central simple algebra D over some field. We
will look at the algebras D[t; σ]/D[t; σ](tm −a). These algebras are well-known associative quotient
algebras when tm −a generates a two-sided ideal, and are the lesser known proper nonassocative
Petit algebras otherwise. Their multiplicative structure is given by
g ◦h = gh modr(tm −a)
for every g, h of degree less than m, where the right-hand side denotes the remainder of gh after
right division with tm −a [Petit1966].
2.2. Anti-automorphisms. Let A be an R-algebra or a ring. An anti-homomorphism on A is
an additive map F : A →A such that f(xy) = f(y)f(x) for all x, y ∈.
If f is bijective it is
called an anti-automorphism on A. The restriction of an anti-automorphism f : A →A to R is
an automorphism τ = f|R ∈Aut(R). An anti-automorphism f is called of the first kind if it is
F-linear, and of the second kind otherwise. (This deviates from the definition in [Lew2006, p. 254],
where anti-automorphisms of the second kind are defined to have order two when restricted to R.)
Put R0 = Fix(τ) = Rτ, then R0 is a subring of R. If f|R is a non-trivial τ ∈Aut(R) we call f
a τ-semilinear
anti-automorphism, if we want to make clear which τ is involved. A τ-semilinear
anti-automorphism is an R0-linear anti-automorphism.
We note that odd powers of an anti-automorphism f are anti-automorphisms and even powers are
isomorphisms.
When A is a central simple (associative) algebra over a field F and f is an involution of the second
kind, we know that F/F0 is always a quadratic ´etale field extension with Galois group generated by
τ. When F/F0 is a Galois field extension and D a central division F-algebra such that D ̸∼= Dop
we also know that if every element of order two in Gal(F/F0) extends to an automorphism of D,
then D has no involution which restricts to the identity on F0. In particular, if F0 has no nontrivial
automorphism (e.g. F0 = Q), then D has no involution at all [MorST2005, Proposition 3.2.].

both f and g are F-linear involutions, then u satisfies f(u) = ±u, whereas if both are involutions of
the second type we may chose u ∈A such that f(u) = u.
For an associative cyclic R-algebra A, we write Aτ = {xτ | x ∈A} for the conjugate algebra Aτ, for
τ ∈Aut R. We recall that Aτ has the same addition and multiplication as A and scalar multiplication
given by twisting the scalar multiplication via axτ = (τ −1(a)x)τ for all a ∈R.
It is well-known that a map ef : A →A is a ring anti-automorphism of an associative F-algebra
which restricts to some τ ∈Aut(F), if and only if the map f : Aτ →Aop given by f(xτ) = ef(x)op is
an isomorphism of F-algebras [MorST2005, p. 2]. This holds for algebras defined over rings a well,
but we will not use this point of view.
We will take a slightly different approach. Given some τ ∈AutR0(R), there is an obvious one-one
correspondence between τ-semilinear anti-automorphisms ef : A →A and τ-linear ring isomorphisms
f : A →Aop (which can be also viewed as algebra isomorphisms between R0-algebras) defined via
f(x) = ef(x)op.
2.3. Cyclic algebras. Let K/F be a cyclic Galois field extension of degree n with Gal(K/F) = ⟨σ⟩.
Our approach will be based on viewing cyclic algebras as quotient algebras of skew polynomials. Let
(K/F, σ, a) be an associative cyclic algebra over F, then (K/F, σ, a) = K[t; σ]/K[t; σ](tn −a), where
tn −a ∈K[t; σ] is a skew polynomial that is two-sided, that is generates a two-sided ideal we factor

6. Monomial anti-automorphisms of degree one on generalized cyclic algebras
6.1. Nonassociative generalized cyclic algebras. Generalized cyclic algebras are special exam-
ples of central simple algebras which do not have to be crossed products (for interesting examples,
see [Hanke2001, Hanke2004, Hanke2005]).
Let D be a finite-dimensional central division algebra over C = Cent(D) of degree r and σ ∈Aut(D)
such that σ|C has finite order m and fixed field F = Fix(σ). In this setup, C/F is automatically
a cyclic Galois field extension of degree m and Gal(C/F) = ⟨σ|C⟩. Then there exists u ∈D× such
that σm = iu and σ(u) = u. These two relations determine u up to multiplication with elements in
F ×.
Let f(t) = tm −d ∈D[t; σ] be a two-sided skew polynomial, that means d ∈F ×. The associative
quotient algebra D[t; σ]/D[t; σ](tm −d) is called a generalized cyclic algebra and denoted by (D, σ, d)
following Jacobson [Jac1996, p. 19].
The generalized cyclic algebra (D, σ, d) is a central simple algebra over F of degree mr. Generalized
cyclic algebras are special examples of crossed product algebras where the crossed product is taken
using D and the cyclic group ⟨σ⟩. Every solvable crossed product division algebra is a generalized
cyclic algebra by Albert.
When D = C and f(t) = tm −d ∈F[t; σ], we obtain the cyclic algebra (F/F0, σ, d) of degree m as
special case.
The classical definition of associative generalized cyclic algebras naturally generalizes to nonassocia-
tive algebras. Let f(t) = tm−d ∈D[t; σ], d ∈D×. The nonassociative algebra D[t; σ]/D[t; σ](tm−d)
over F is called a (nonassociative) generalized cyclic algebra of degree mn. We denote this algebra
also by (D, σ, d) and include both the associative (d ∈F) and the nonassociative (d ∈D \ F)
generalized cyclic algebras in this notation.

8. Outlook
Since the theory we developed is bases on skew polynomials it can be canonically extended to cyclic
and generalized cyclic Azumaya algebras over rings.
Let D be an Azumaya algebra of constant rank r with center C.
Let σ ∈Aut(D) be a ring
automorphism, such that σ|C has finite order m and fixed ring S = Fix(σ) ∩C. We will assume
that C/S is a cyclic Galois ring extension of degree m with Galois group Gal(C/S) = ⟨σ|C⟩. This
implies that C has constant rank m as an S-module.
Let f(t) = tm −d ∈D[t; σ] with d ∈D× invertible. Then (D, σ, d) = D[t; σ]/D[t; σ](tm −d) is a
nonassociative (Petit) algebra over its center S = C ∩Fix(σ); and an associative Azumaya algebra
over S of constant rank rm if and only if d ∈S× which is called a nonassociative generalized cyclic
Azumaya algebra when d ∈S×.
If D = C is a commutative ring, C/S is a cyclic Galois extension of rings of degree m with Galois
group generated by σ, and d ∈S. In this case (C/S0, σ, d) = C[t, σ]/C[t; σ](tm −d) is an Azumaya
algebra which is called an associative cyclic Azumaya algebra of rank m2 [Pum2021, Szeto1982].
It is straightforward to check that the results from the last section also hold for (generalized) cyclic
Azumaya algebras, since for the right choice of α ∈C× and τ ∈Aut(D), the correspondingly
defined maps Gτ,α,k and eGττ,α,k are also isomorphism/anti-automorphisms for generalized Azumaya
algebras.
This broadly ties in with problems studied in [First2015], where Azumaya algebras without involution
are constructed; and examples like the one of an an Azumaya algebra B over a Dedekind domain C
