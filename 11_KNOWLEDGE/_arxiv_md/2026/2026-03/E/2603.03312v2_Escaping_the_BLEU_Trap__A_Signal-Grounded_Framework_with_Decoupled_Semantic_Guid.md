Abstract
Decoding natural language from non-invasive
EEG signals is a promising yet challenging task.
However, current state-of-the-art models remain
constrained by three fundamental limitations: Se-
mantic Bias (mode collapse into generic tem-
plates), Signal Neglect (hallucination based on
linguistic priors rather than neural inputs), and
the BLEU Trap, where evaluation metrics are ar-
tificially inflated by high-frequency stopwords,
masking a lack of true semantic fidelity. To ad-
dress these challenges, we propose SEMKEY, a
novel multi-stage framework that enforces signal-
grounded generation through four decoupled se-
mantic objectives: sentiment, topic, length, and
surprisal. We redesign the interaction between the
neural encoder and the Large Language Model
(LLM) by injecting semantic prompts as Queries
and EEG embeddings as Key-Value pairs, strictly
forcing the model to attend to neural inputs. Fur-
thermore, we move beyond standard translation
metrics by adopting N-way Retrieval Accuracy
and Fr´echet Distance to rigorously assess di-
versity and alignment. Extensive experiments
demonstrate that our approach effectively elimi-
nates hallucinations on noise inputs and achieves
SOTA performance on these robust protocols.

//github.com/xmed-lab/SemKey.

2. Related Work
EEG-to-Text Decoding: Evolution and Frontiers. Early
approaches relied on handcrafted features like Mel-
frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs) (Cooney et al.,
2018) and wavelet transforms (Panachakel et al., 2020), ne-
cessitating extensive domain expertise. The advent of deep
learning introduced automated feature extraction via CNNs
and LSTMs (Zhang et al., 2018) to capture spatio-temporal
dynamics. A paradigm shift occurred with Transformer-
based pipelines: (Wang & Ji, 2022) proposed projecting
EEG signals into a pre-trained BART latent space, and
building on this, DeWave (Duan et al., 2023) introduced
an end-to-end framework processing raw brainwaves for
direct translation.
More recently, the integration of Large Language Models
has spurred new directions. Thought2Text (Mishra et al.,
2025) explores leveraging LLMs for EEG encoding, while
WaveMind (Zeng et al., 2025) proposes a conversational
foundation model aligned across textual and visual modali-
ties. However, our work differs from these distinct streams:
unlike WaveMind, which relies on image stimuli that offer
richer and more direct semantic grounding, we tackle the
different task of pure EEG-to-Text decoding from abstract
textual stimuli without such visual aids.
The Validity Crisis and Methodological Gap. Notwith-
standing architectural advancements, the field faces a crisis
of validity. The pivotal study “Are EEG-to-Text Models
Working” (Jo et al., 2024) exposed that high BLEU scores
in prior arts are often artifacts of Teacher Forcing, where
models ignore the noisy EEG encoder in favor of the LLM’s
linguistic priors. While GLIM (Liu et al., 2025) recently ad-
dressed this by establishing the first effective pipeline with-
out teacher forcing, it—along with its predecessors—still
suffers from “signal neglect,” leading to semantic collapse
in open-ended generation (as discussed in Section 1).
To bridge this gap between open-ended generation and faith-
ful neural decoding, SEMKEY departs from the prevailing
reliance on implicit latent alignment. We propose a Coarse-
to-Fine decoding paradigm to enforce validity: First, we
introduce an explicit, attribute-driven mechanism where
high-level semantic anchors (e.g., Sentiment, Topic) ac-
tively steer the decoding trajectory. This serves as a hard
constraint to prevent semantic collapse, ensuring adherence
to the global semantic intent. Complementing this high-
level guidance, we reformulate the fine-grained LLM inter-
action via a Q-K-V Injection mechanism. By forcing EEG
embeddings to act as Keys and Values, we enforce a strict
token-level dependency on neural signals.

3.1. Stage 1: Parallel Multi-Task Attribute Extraction
Based on (Liu et al., 2025), we employ a multi-task learning
strategy to explicitly decouple x-level semantic labels. The
EEG encoder transforms signals into latent embeddings,
inheriting alignment and reconstruction tasks via a frozen
Flan-T5 model (Chung et al., 2024). Concurrently, we in-
troduce four auxiliary decoding heads to predict semantic
attributes: Sentiment, Topic, Length, and Surprisal. These
predictions serve as a robust semantic skeleton to guide the
subsequent generation. As shown in Figure 2, this stage is
structured into three key components: a prior-guided encod-
ing stream, a parallel multi-task learning framework, and a
unified training objective.
Prior-Guided EEG Encoding Stream. To mitigate the
severe inter-subject variability and domain shifts inherent
in raw EEG signals (Jiang et al., 2024), we adopt the Prior
Label Injection strategy utilized in GLIM (Liu et al., 2025).
Let Xeeg ∈RT ×C denote the input EEG signals. Following
this protocol, discrete priors Lprior (including Subject ID,
Dataset source, and Task type) are aggregated into a global
context vector eprior. This vector is then employed to dy-
namically modulate the intermediate features within the
hierarchical Conformer encoder (Gulati et al., 2020; Jiang
et al., 2024), ensuring the feature extraction process remains
robust against individual noise and distribution shifts. The
implementation details can be found in Appendix B.
After that, an Aligner projects the encoded states into the
shared semantic dimension dmodel, yielding distinct dual
representations to serve different decoding granularities:
(1) Sequence Embeddings (Eeeg ∈RL×dmodel), which
preserve fine-grained temporal details for word-level recon-
struction; and (2) Global Embedding (veeg ∈Rdmodel),
obtained via attention pooling (Lee et al., 2019), which
summarizes the holistic semantic information for high-level
attribute prediction. Following GLIM (Liu et al., 2025),
we adopt a reconstruction loss Lrecon (standard autoregres-
sive cross-entropy) to enforce precise token generation from
the sequence embeddings Eeeg, alongside an alignment ob-
jective Lalign (comprising Contrastive and Commitment
losses) to synchronize the EEG embeddings (Contrastive is

3.2. Stage2: Multi-Perspective Active Retrieval
Decoding
Prediction heads from Stage 1 are frozen, and the aligned
EEG features are injected into the LLM via a learnable
projector. To ensure fidelity, we introduce two key mecha-
nisms: (1) Prompt Guidance, where the predicted attributes
are structured into a natural language constraint; and (2)
Q-K-V Injection, which re-routes the attention mechanism:
the text prompt serves as the Query, interacting strictly with
EEG embeddings provided as Keys and Values. This ar-
chitecture transforms passive conditioning into an active
retrieval process, compelling the LLM to actively query
neural signals for every token generated. This stage follows
an end-to-end paradigm, meaning that the EEG Encoder
keeps trainable while the LLM Decoder keeps frozen. To
address the “signal neglect” and “semantic bias” issue, we
introduce a Guidance-Generation paradigm: a lightweight
projector is imported, with a novel Q-K-V injection mecha-
nism.

3.3. Escaping the “BLEU Trap”: A Holistic Evaluation
Protocol
Standard evaluations in EEG-to-Text decoding heavily rely
on n-gram matching metrics such as BLEU. However, our
experiment shows that these metrics can be misleading in
this domain. A model generating generic sentences can
achieve high BLEU scores without extracting any real se-
mantic information from the EEG, refer to Section 4.1. To
avoid this “BLEU trap” and strictly evaluate both seman-
tic alignment and generative diversity, we establish a com-
prehensive evaluation protocol consisting of three distinct
categories:
Retrieval-based Alignment Metrics (N-Way Accuracy).
To measure the pure semantic alignment capability of the
EEG Encoder, we employ N-way Retrieval Accuracy, a
standard protocol for evaluating modality alignment (Rad-
ford et al., 2021; Li et al., 2024). This metric evaluates
whether the predicted EEG embedding can successfully re-
trieve the correct text from a pool of distractors. Specifically,
we utilize the sentence transformers (Reimers & Gurevych,
2019) library to encode the ground-truth text and N −1
randomly selected negative samples into dense vectors. We
then calculate the Cosine Similarity between the aligned
EEG embeddings and these candidate text vectors.
To mitigate the variance from the random selection of nega-
tive samples, we conducted multiple experimental runs and
reported the average accuracy to ensure stability. We report
accuracy at four difficulty levels: 2-Way, 4-Way, 10-Way,
and 24-Way Accuracy.

Semantic Substance Metric (Content Recall).
High
BLEU scores can be achieved by matching stopwords, while
missing the core meaning. To evaluate the recovery of
important information, we introduce Content Recall. We
first filter out stopwords from both the reference and gen-
erated text, retaining only content-bearing words (nouns,
verbs, adjectives). Content Recall calculates the percent-
age of ground-truth content words that successfully appear
in the generated hypothesis. This metric penalizes “fluent
but empty” hallucinations and rewards semantic fidelity (Ji
et al., 2023; Maynez et al., 2020).
Generative Diversity & Quality Metrics. A major failure
mode of previous EEG-to-Text models is semantic bias,
where the model repeats a few safe sentences. To quantify
the richness and non-repetitiveness of our generation, we
utilize four key metrics.
First, to evaluate vocabulary usage and uniqueness, we em-
ploy Dist-1 & Dist-2 and Self-BLEU. Dist-n metrics calcu-
late the ratio of distinct unigrams and bigrams to the total
number of generated tokens (Li et al., 2016), where higher
values indicate broader vocabulary usage. Complementarily,
Self-BLEU (Zhu et al., 2018) measures the BLEU score of
each generated sentence against all others in the test set; a
lower score indicates that the model generates unique sam-
ples for different EEG inputs rather than regurgitating mem-
orized templates. Additionally, to assess structural diversity,
we compute Head Entropy (Prefix Diversity). Following
(Holtzman et al., 2019), we calculate the Shannon entropy
of the initial bigrams (first two tokens) across all gener-
ated sentences. A higher Head Entropy signifies greater
diversity in sentence initiations, preventing the model from
collapsing into fixed opening templates. Finally, to evaluate
semantic realism at the distribution level, we compute the
Fr´echet Distance (FD) (Semeniuta et al., 2018). We map
all sentences to a high-dimensional semantic space using
a pre-trained Sentence Transformer and calculate the dis-
tance between the multivariate Gaussian distributions of the
generated (µg, Σg) and reference (µr, Σr) embeddings:
FD = ||µr −µg||2 + Tr(Σr + Σg −2(ΣrΣg)1/2)

Quantitative Results. Table 1 and Figure 3 present the
comprehensive performance comparison. SEMKEY demon-

First, regarding Alignment & Semantic Substance, SEMKEY
consistently outperforms baselines across all retrieval set-
tings (N = 2, 4, 10, 24). Notably, the performance advan-
tage widens as task difficulty increases: while achieving a
8.0% relative gain in the 2-Way setting, this margin expands
significantly to +34.4% in 10-Way and +51.2% in 24-Way

nuances against a large pool of distractors.
Second, in terms of Generative Diversity, SEMKEY exhibits

achieves a +179.0% increase in Dist-2 and reaches a Head
Entropy of 6.21 (vs. 2.18 for GLIM). This high entropy

produces diverse sentence openings. Finally, for Generative
Quality, SEMKEY records the best scores with a 42.7%
improvement in Self-BLEU and a 54.4% improvement in
FD, indicating that our generated semantic distribution is
significantly closer to the ground truth manifold.
It is worth noting that while the EEG-to-Text baseline suf-
fers from the most severe semantic bias and signal neglect
(with almost 0 diversity), it paradoxically achieves a higher
Content Recall than GLIM (2.1% vs. 1.2%). This is largely
an artifact of the model memorizing high-frequency content
words from the training corpus rather than performing true
decoding. This anomaly underscores the necessity of a holis-
tic evaluation: high recall alone is insufficient for validity
and must be interpreted alongside retrieval accuracy and
generative diversity to rule out overfitting and hallucination.

B. Implementation Details
B.1. Model Architecture & Hyperparameters
The SEMKEY framework is implemented in PyTorch, utilizing the PyTorch-Lightning library for modularity. Our architecture
builds upon the GLIM (Liu et al., 2025) backbone, employing Flan-T5-Large (via HuggingFace Transformers) as the
pre-trained language model. The EEG Encoder consists of 6 encoder-decoder blocks with 8 attention heads each, projecting
signals into a high-dimensional space (dmodel = 1024). The resulting sequence embeddings (Eeeg) possess a temporal
dimension of L = 96. For the auxiliary decoding tasks (Sentiment, Topic, Length, and Surprisal), we implemented four
dedicated heads, each consisting of a three-layer MLP with hidden dimensions of [512, 256, 128]. The total parameter count
of the model is approximately 799M.
Our framework employs a reconstruction loss (Lrecon), implemented as the standard autoregressive cross-entropy objective
derived from the frozen Flan-T5. To enforce latent space consistency, we incorporate an alignment objective (Lalign)
comprising both commitment and contrastive terms. Specifically, the commitment loss minimizes the Mean Squared Error
(MSE) between the generated sequence embeddings (Eeeg) and the target text representations from Flan-T5. Meanwhile,
the contrastive loss optimizes the cosine similarity between the global EEG embedding (veeg) and the corresponding text
embedding. Finally, we optimize auxiliary tasks using cross-entropy for classification and MSE for regression.
B.2. Training Protocol
The training process consists of two distinct stages followed by an end-to-end (E2E) fine-tuning phase. All experiments
were conducted on a single NVIDIA H100 (80GB HBM3) GPU.

C. Semantic Bias in MTV Augmentation
In this section, we analyze the quality of the Multiple Text Variants (MTVs) dataset strategy adopted from GLIM (Liu et al.,
2025). While intended to enhance supervision by providing paraphrased references, a critical inspection reveals a severe
semantic pattern bias.
As detailed in Table e, the augmentation algorithms (e.g., lexical simplification, rewriting tend to converge on rigid syntactic
templates rather than diverse semantic expressions. Specifically:
• Sentiment Task: A disproportionate number of variants begin with static headers like “The movie...” or “It...”.
• Topic Task: The biography descriptions are dominated by the “He was...” pattern.
This structural homogeneity artificially reduces the perplexity of the target distribution, potentially encouraging models to
overfit to these static headers rather than decoding the unique neural semantics of the input.
D. Showcase of the “BLEU Trap”
The “BLEU Trap” refers to the phenomenon in EEG-to-Text generation where models achieve high n-gram matching scores
(BLEU) by memorizing high-frequency templates, despite failing to decode the correct semantic content (Hallucination).
Conversely, models that attempt genuine semantic decoding may suffer lower scores due to a lack of template overlap.

E. Qualitative Samples: Signal Dependency Verification
To further validate the signal dependency discussed in the main experiments, we present a qualitative comparison of model
outputs when conditioned on pure Gaussian noise (Eeeg ∼N(0, I)) instead of real EEG signals.
Ideally, an EEG-to-Text model should not generate coherent narrative text from random noise. As shown in Table h, the
baseline GLIM model suffers from severe hallucination, producing grammatically fluent but factually nonsensical sentences
(e.g., repeating “Charles” or “He was the wife of...”). This confirms that GLIM relies heavily on internal language priors
rather than the input signal.
In contrast, SEMKEY generates completely unstructured, chaotic tokens (gibberish). This “collapse” is a positive indicator
in this context, demonstrating that our decoder strictly respects the input constraints and does not default to memorized
language templates when the signal is absent.
F. Limitations and Future Work
Despite the advancements discussed, decoding fidelity at the fine-grained word level remains a significant bottleneck.
We attribute this limitation largely to the inherent characteristics of the ZuCo benchmark, which contains a high density
of low-frequency proper nouns—such as specific names and locations—that are notoriously difficult to resolve from
coarse-grained EEG signals (Huth et al., 2016).
The Challenge of Inter-Subject Variability. Moreover, the dataset’s collection paradigm, characterized by a multi-subject
setting with limited data per individual, necessitates a cross-subject training strategy (Zhou et al., 2025). However, given
the substantial inter-subject variability in brain topology and cognitive processing (Finn et al., 2015; Wang et al., 2025a),
effective generalization across subjects remains intrinsically challenging (Zheng et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2026). As illustrated
in Table i, while our method significantly reduces the rigid template hallucinations seen in baselines (e.g., repeating ”He was
a...” for every biography), it still struggles with precise proper noun retrieval.
