Experimental Evidence for Increased Particle Fluxes Due to a Change in Transport at
the Separatrix near Density Limits on Alcator C-Mod
M.A. Miller1, J.W. Hughes1, T. Eich2, G.R. Tynan3, P. Manz4, A.E. Hubbard1, B. LaBombard1, J. Dunsmore1

(Dated: March 26, 2026)
Experimental inferences of cross-field particle flux at the separatrix, Γsep
⊥, show rapid growth near
H-mode and L-mode density limits at high magnetic field on Alcator C-Mod. Increases in Γsep
⊥
correlate well with proximity to high density operational boundaries as proposed by the separatrix
operational space model. Γsep
⊥
grows as the L-mode density limit and the H-L-mode back transition
boundaries are approached, consistent with expectations of plasma instability-driven turbulence
suggested by theory, confirming the power dependence of density limits. Γsep
⊥
is well-organized by
the characteristic wavenumber for resistive ballooning mode turbulence, kRBM, from interchange-
drift-Alfv´en fluid turbulence theory, with additional dependence on the cylindrical safety factor,
ˆqcyl, yielding an empirical limit to plasma operation of k2
RBMˆqcyl = 1. This limit corresponds to
the point where the perpendicular heat flux, Q⊥, reaches the level of the parallel heat flux, Q∥, i.e.
Q⊥≈Q∥, beyond which point thermal equilibrium is not satisfied, resulting in a fold catastrophe.
There is a strong need to develop a physics-based un-
derstanding of mechanisms that limit the edge density
profile of tokamaks operating at high density.
Fusion
reactors will need to operate with high edge densities [1–
4] to ensure that power fluxes to the device remain be-
low material limits [5–8]. Most importantly, operation at
high density puts plasmas at risk of density-limit-driven
disruptions [9, 10], the triggering physical mechanism of
which remains contested. A growing body of evidence
exists suggesting that at high density, the role of plasma
transport will eclipse that of ionizing neutrals in deter-
mining the edge density [11–13]. In these reactor-relevant
scenarios, use of external actuators to modify the plasma
density may be limited. It is therefore imperative to un-
derstand particle transport at high density, its implica-
tions for core performance, and crucially, its influence in
bringing a plasma toward high density operational limits.
Using novel experimental analysis, this Letter finds
that particle transport plays a key role in the limit to
plasma operation at high density on the Alcator C-Mod
tokamak.
Unique diagnostic capability on C-Mod of
neutral Lyα emission in the edge enabled inference of
the cross-field particle flux at the separatrix, Γsep
⊥. In-
ferences of Γsep
⊥
are collated into databases and com-
pared against key parameters describing drift-Alfv´en tur-
bulence (DALF) [14–17], codified by boundaries from the
recently proposed separatrix operational space (SepOS)
model [18]. Large increases in Γsep
⊥
are found at high-
density turbulent transition boundaries from the SepOS
model.
These changes track αt, a parameter mediat-
ing the influence of interchange modes over drift-wave
(DW) modes [19]. Across both the low-confinement mode
(L-mode) and the high-confinement mode (H-mode),
Γsep
⊥
organizes best with the normalized characteristic
wavenumber for the resistive ballooning mode (RBM),
kRBM, derived from the SepOS model. The data suggest
that particle transport prior to the H- and L-mode den-
sity limits has a common physical origin rooted in the
interchange-driven RBM instability [20–22].
Limits to
operation in both regimes and across a range of plasma
current, IP , are well-described by an approach to a crit-
ical value k2
RBMˆqcyl = 1, with ˆq the cylindrical safety
factor.
Under reasonable assumptions, this empirical limit can
be expressed as a scaling for the pressure gradient scale
length, λp, of the form λp ∼a, a key assumption in
the density limit scaling proposed in the recent work by
Giacomin et al. [23]. The limit also suggests that the
transport scales stronger-than-linearly with αt, consis-
tent with recent experimental observations [19, 24]. Fur-
ther, this empirical limit yields a scaling on power, P for
the density limit of the form n ∼P 4/7, similar to that
from a model for radiative collapse [25].
The primary
scaling on power of the threshold limit suggests that ulti-
mate thermal collapse is driven by atomic physics. Prior
to this limit, large particle fluxes drive strong perpendic-
ular heat fluxes, Q⊥, on the order of the parallel heat flux,
Q∥, directly preceding thermal catastrophe as proposed
in a theory for the density limit related to thermal insta-
bility [26]. The transport analysis shown in this Letter
experimentally affirms the role of turbulence in reaching
thermal catastrophe via power balance, in line with ear-
lier conjectures [11, 27–29] and bridging the gap between
disparate paradigms for which to understand the density
limit.

the ionization source via measurements of line-integrated
Lyα emissivity, ϵLyα, from the population of neutral
atoms in the edge plasma. A collisional-radiative model,
ADAS [30], allows inference of Γ⊥, under the assumption
of insignificant poloidal asymmetry and the imposition of

This Letter provides experimental evidence for the im-
pact of turbulent cross-field particle transport in setting
operational limits at high density in both H- and L-modes
on Alcator C-Mod. It has clarified the local physics pa-
rameter determining the cross-field particle flux, acting
as a powerful extension of a large body of work studying
the role of dimensionless edge turbulence control param-
eters in limiting plasma operation [14, 38, 39, 42, 47, 50–
52]. The new findings presented here are enabled by neu-
tral measurements allowing experimental inference of the
particle flux. The presence of both ETS and LYMID en-
ables particle balance calculations and the comparison of
particle transport data with parameters from edge tur-
bulence and stability theory. The empirical limit found
in this work suggest that the final step in the chain of
events associated with the density limit is the collapse
of a power-starved plasma via atomic physics. Scalings
of Γsep
⊥
and Q⊥, however, confirm that it is enhanced
perpendicular transport across relative to parallel trans-
port along the separatrix, that is fundamentally respon-
sible for the edge plasma cooling leading to the density
limit. Power-dependent transport phenomena preceding
high density limits and their impact on edge profiles may
well be important for next-step devices and power plants
like SPARC [4, 49] and ARC [53], which plan to oper-
ate at higher densities and magnetic fields, and for which
understanding limits to edge density profiles will be of ut-
most importance for sustainable and efficient operation.
