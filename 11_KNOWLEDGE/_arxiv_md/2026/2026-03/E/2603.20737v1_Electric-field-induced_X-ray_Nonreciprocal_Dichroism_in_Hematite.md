Hematite (α-Fe2O3) is a prototypical room-temperature antiferromagnet whose time-reversal-odd
magnetic structure has recently attracted renewed attention. While such magnetic symmetry can
be characterized in terms of higher-order multipoles beyond the magnetic dipole, their manifesta-
tion in measurable physical phenomena has remained largely elusive. In this work, we investigate
x-ray absorption near the Fe K-edge of hematite under an applied electric field, which explicitly
breaks space-inversion symmetry. We observe an electric-field-induced x-ray nonreciprocal linear
dichroism (E-induced XNLD) that reflects the time-reversal-odd nature of the magnetic order. Nu-
merical simulations based on ab-initio density functional theory reproduce the observed spectra,
including their dependence on the antiferromagnetic domain and x-ray polarization. Furthermore,
a symmetry-resolved multipole analysis reveals that this response originates from the magnetic
quadrupole and the magnetic toroidal octupole induced by the applied electric field. These results
demonstrate that electric-field-modulated x-ray absorption provides direct access to the antifer-
roic order of higher-order multipoles in time-reversal-odd antiferromagnets, thereby establishing a
general framework to uncover hidden symmetry properties in magnetic materials.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Hematite (α-Fe2O3) is a ubiquitous, yet prototypical
antiferromagnetic (AFM) material that has been stud-
ied for decades.
In earlier studies, its weak ferromag-
netism originating from the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya (DM)
interaction [1–5] and the Morin transition [6] were ex-
tensively investigated [7].
More recently, various dis-
tinct physical properties and functionalities have been
reported, including electric switching of magnetization
[8, 9], anomalous Hall electrical transport [10], magneto-
optical Kerr effect [11–13] and chiral magnon band split-
ting [14]. These phenomena arise from the characteris-
tic symmetry of hematite, namely, its time-reversal-odd
(T -odd) magnetic structure. This symmetry of hematite
has been discussed in the context of both g-wave alter-
magnetism [10, 14–16] and multipolar order parameters
[17, 18]. Consequently, hematite serves as an excellent
platform for exploring phenomena associated with the
breaking of T -symmetry.
Although the magnetic structures of hematite below
and above the Morin transition temperature TM are
(nearly) collinear, the interplay with the crystal sym-
metry leads to a highly nontrivial order parameter.
Specifically, high-rank magnetic multipoles—including
octupoles (rank 3), hexadecapoles (rank 4), and triakon-
tadipoles (rank 5) —have been proposed as the relevant

degrees of freedom [18]. This implies that the underlying
magnetic ordering is significantly more complex than a
simple collinear picture suggests. Such hidden complex-
ity is expected to be common in T -odd antiferromagnets
of sufficiently high point-group symmetry [19–21]. There-
fore, clarifying how such higher-order multipoles manifest
in measurable physical quantities is a key to bridging the
gap between symmetry-based classifications and experi-
mentally observable phenomena.
An effective approach to accessing T -odd multipole de-
grees of freedom is the observation of x-ray dichroism.
There are different types of x-ray dichroism, including
x-ray magnetic circular dichroism (XMCD), x-ray mag-
netochiral dichroism (XMChD), and x-ray nonreciprocal
linear dichroism (XNLD). Each x-ray dichroism is sen-
sitive to specific magnetic multipole orderings [22–25]:
XMCD primarily probes magnetic dipole [26, 27] and
magnetic octupole [28, 29], XMChD is sensitive to mag-
netic toroidal dipole [22, 30], and XNLD reflects magnetic
quadrupole [22, 31]. In addition to this selectivity, mea-
surements of x-ray dichroism offer two key advantages
over visible and near-infrared optical approaches: ele-
ment specificity and the capability to make direct com-
parisons with theoretical calculations.
However, the aforementioned x-ray dichroism measure-
ments suffer from a limitation. When multipoles are or-
dered in a compensated antiferroic manner, local dichroic
signals cancel each other out, preventing the observation
of macroscopic dichroism. To overcome such a limitation,
we propose x-ray dichroism measurements of T -odd anti-
ferromagnets under an applied electric field (E) which ex-
plicitly breaks space-inversion (P) symmetry. By driving

A.
Magnetic structure of hematite
Hematite crystallizes in the corundum structure with
the space group R¯3c [7, 36]. Below TN ≈950 K, it ex-
hibits a weakly canted AFM order, where spins lie in the
basal (001)h plane (h in the subscript denotes the hexag-
onal setting) [see right panel of Fig. 1(a)] [6, 7]. The N´eel
vector is defined as L = M1 −M2 −M3 + M4, where
Mi (i = 1 - 4) are Fe spins of site i [for the site labels,
see Fig. 1(a)]. When L is perpendicular to the ah axis
(termed as L⊥state), the magnetic point group is 2/m
[37, 38]. In the L⊥state, the Fe spins are slightly canted
due to the DM interaction, resulting in a net magneti-
zation along the ah axis. The two domain states related
by the T operation, corresponding to a reversal of all
spins, are termed as L⊥+ and L⊥−. L can freely rotate
in the basal plane by applying an in-plane magnetic field
due to the spin canting. When L is parallel to the ah
axis, the magnetic point group is 2′/m′ (termed as L∥
state) [38]. As for the L⊥state, the two domain states
related by the T operation are termed as L∥+ and L∥−.
At TM ≈255 K, hematite undergoes the Morin transi-
tion, where the Fe spins are aligned parallel to the ch axis
with a down-up-up-down configuration [see left panel of
Fig. 1(a)] [6].

B.
X-ray nonreciprocal dichroism under an electric
field
Before discussing x-ray dichroism under E, we briefly
summarize previous x-ray measurements in the high-
T phases of hematite.
Because of in-plane magnetic
anisotropy, x-ray linear dichroism (XLD) is expected,
and has been observed experimentally at the Fe L-edge
[39, 40]. We note that XLD is sensitive to the anisotropy
of the square of magnetization and therefore cannot dis-
tinguish the domain states of L+ and L−. Since there is
a net magnetization, XMCD is also expected, although
it has been reported as a very small effect [40]. However,
very recently, XMCD originating from altermagnetic or-

der has been reported [41, 42]. Magnetic properties of
hematite have also been investigated by the resonant x-
ray scattering, and the presence of local P-odd/T -even
[43] and P-odd/T -odd multipoles has been suggested
[44, 45].
We now turn to the E-induced x-ray dichroism in a
general framework, specifically focusing on the compo-
nent that is linear in E. In this case, P-symmetry is ex-
plicitly broken by E. Combined with the intrinsic break-
ing of T -symmetry, the induced x-ray dichroism becomes
P-odd and T -odd. Among different types of x-ray dichro-
ism, XMChD and XNLD belong to this class [23]. Both
XMChD and XNLD correspond to x-ray nonreciprocal
directional dichroism (XNDD), in which absorption dif-
fers for counter-propagating beams. XMChD refers to
the XNDD for unpolarized light, whereas XNLD de-
scribes XNDD for linearly polarized light. Note that al-
though MChD is often used to denote dichroism in mag-
netized chiral materials, here we use this term more gen-
erally to describe NDD observed with unpolarized light.
In XNLD, the linear polarization dependence reverses
upon inversion of the propagation direction (+k →−k).
To classify the symmetry conditions that host XNDD,
it is convenient to introduce the linear magnetoelectric
(ME) tensor, because the linear ME effect also occurs in
P-odd and T -odd materials [46–48]. The linear ME effect
is described as Pi = αijHj, where Pi is polarization,
Hj is a magnetic field, and αij is the linear ME tensor
that depends on the magnetic point group of the material
[49]. Here, we consider the case where the x-ray beam
propagates along the z axis. In this geometry, XNDD
can appear when the off-diagonal ME tensor elements
αxy and αyx are nonzero. When the tensor is symmetric,
i.e., αxy = αyx, XNLD can be nonzero, whereas when
it is antisymmetric, i.e., αxy = −αyx, XMChD can be
nonzero. Further details of the relationship between the
ME tensor and XNDD are discussed in Appendix A.
Finally, we discuss E-induced NDD in hematite from a
symmetry perspective. To classify the possible responses,
we consider the quadratic (second-order) ME effect, de-
scribed as Pi = βijkHjEk. Here, βijk is a third-rank T -
odd axial tensor that belongs to the same tensor class as
the piezomagnetic tensor [49, 50]. This can be regarded
as an induced linear ME tensor, with αij = βijkEk. In
the L⊥+ state with point group 2/m, when the x-ray
beam propagates along the z (∥ch) axis and E is ap-
plied along the same axis, the induced linear ME tensor
can contain finite off-diagonal components αxy and αyx
(with x ∥ah and y ∥ah × ch), where αxy ̸= αyx. This
indicates that, in the configuration E ∥k ∥ch, both
XMChD and XNLD are allowed.
On the other hand,
in the L∥state with the magnetic point group is 2′/m′,
the ME tensor induced by Ez has vanishing off-diagonal
components (αxy = αyx = 0), suggesting both XMChD
and XNLD for x- or y- polarized beam are forbidden.
However, XNLD is expected to be allowed, and in fact
maximized, for linearly polarized x-ray beams oriented
at ±45◦with respect to the original x axis when the co-
ordinate system is rotated by 45◦to new (x′, y′), where
the tensor exhibits symmetric off-diagonal components
αx′y′ = αy′x′ = (αxx −αyy)/2.
In the low-T phase
with point group ¯3m, the induced ME tensor compo-
nents under the same condition satisfy αxy = –αyx, sug-
gesting that only XMChD is allowed. We emphasize that
this ME tensor analysis determines which types of x-ray
dichroism can appear but does not provide information
on their magnitude.

A.
Experiments
A natural single crystal of hematite, with the widest
face parallel to the (001)h plane, was purchased from Sur-
faceNet GmbH. Magnetization measurements confirmed
that the crystal exhibits a spontaneous magnetization at
room temperature and undergoes the Morin transition at
TM ≈253 K [see Fig. 6(a) in Appendix B]. The sample
was polished down to a thickness of 50 µm.
To apply
an electric field along the thickness direction, indium-

A.
Electric-field dependence
Figure 2(a) shows the x-ray absorption spectrum
around the Fe K-edge. The inset displays the spectrum
over a wide energy range including both the pre-edge and
main-edge regions, while the main panel presents a mag-
nified view of the pre-edge peak. The obtained absorp-
tion spectrum agrees well with those in previous studies
[43, 53]. The pre-edge peak is attributed to the 1s →
3d E2 transition [43], which provides the dominant con-
tribution to XNDD [23, 25]. Therefore, we focus on this
specific energy range.
We now present the results of the x-ray transmittance
measurements under E. During the measurements, mag-
netic fields of 0.1 T were applied along the x axis to
prepare a monodomain state of L⊥+ [see Fig. 6(b) in
Appendix B]. The incident x-ray beam was linearly po-
larized along the x axis. An AC voltage with a peak am-
plitude of 200 V was applied, and the transmitted x-ray
intensity modulated at the same frequency was detected.
Figure 2(b) shows the resulting spectra of the AC com-
ponent (∆I) normalized by the DC one (IDC), ∆I/IDC.
Although the obtained changes were small (∆I/IDC is on
the order of 10−6), the spectra exhibit a clear dispersive
line shape. This shape is characterized by two peaks of
opposite signs at 7.1105 keV and 7.1120 keV, with a zero
crossing between them.
Figure 2(b) also shows the ∆I/IDC spectra measured
under AC voltages with peak amplitudes of 150, 100,
and 50 V. The ∆I/IDC spectrum systematically increases
with increasing voltage. Figure 2(c) displays the voltage
dependence of ∆I/IDC integrated in the energy range of
7.1095 keV - 7.1110 keV. The integrated signal is linear to
the applied voltage. These results demonstrate that the
high-T phase of hematite exhibits the E-induced XNDD
expected from its symmetry.

What does multipole analysis imply?
Although the
matter tensor Tαβγ must satisfy the symmetry con-
straints of the material, it is defined by the matrix ele-
ments connecting the ground and excited states. Thus, it
primarily characterizes the response channel of the x-ray
absorption process, rather than being identified directly
with static multipoles of the entire system.
Nevertheless, for the Fe K-edge considered here, the
relevant transitions involve an isotropic 1s core state and
E2 (1s →3d) and E1 (1s →4p) channels.
Conse-
quently, the symmetry information encoded in the mat-
ter tensor Tαβγ is largely governed by the 3d states,
which also define the symmetry of the Fe2O3 system
under an applied electric field.
From this perspective,
the tensor/multipole analysis goes beyond mere clas-
sification of x-ray response channels and strongly sug-
gests that the magnetic toroidal octupole and magnetic
quadrupole constitute the dominant multipolar compo-
nents characterizing the E-induced state of hematite.
Surprisingly, these E-induced multipoles originate from
extremely small displacements of Fe3+ ions. Our precise
measurements, together with detailed numerical analysis,
reveal this subtle yet important property of the system.
We also note that, since the K-edge transition from the
spinless 1s initial state, the T -odd properties appearing
in Eq. (4) are introduced purely through the orbital de-
grees of freedom. Accordingly, the magnetic multipoles
in Eq. (4) can be physically interpreted in terms of orbital
currents (see Appendix G).

Finally, we comment on the possible multipole ordering
in the absence of an electric field. Because P-symmetry is
preserved, the P-odd and T -odd multipoles vanish upon
averaging over the unit cell. However, the local site sym-
metry of individual Fe atoms is 3, which is P-odd and
T -odd, allowing for the P-odd and T -odd multipoles,
such as the magnetic toroidal octupole.
The Fe sites
form inversion-related pairs [sites 1 and 2, or 3 and 4
in Fig. 1(a)], in which the P-odd and T -odd multipoles
appear with opposite signs, leading to a compensated
arrangement at zero electric field. Applying an electric
field breaks this compensation and gives rise to a finite
P-odd and T -odd multipolar response. From this view-
point, the present study of E-induced XNDD suggests
that the high-T phases of hematite inherently supports
an antiferroic arrangement of magnetic toroidal octupoles
and magnetic quadrupoles, which becomes macroscopi-
cally observable when the inversion-related compensation
is lifted by an external electric field.
VI.
SUMMARY
In conclusion, we have demonstrated that the AFM
state of hematite above TM exhibits E-induced XNDD,
primarily E-induced XNLD. The observed spectra, in-
cluding their polarization angle dependence and T do-
main sign reversal, are in excellent agreement with DFT-
based simulations. Furthermore, our multipole analysis
reveals that an electric field induces a magnetic toroidal
octupole, suggesting an antiferroic ordering of these oc-
tupoles in the absence of an electric field. These results
highlight that probing x-ray absorption as a response to
an applied electric field provides a powerful means to
access hidden symmetry information, surpassing the ca-
pabilities of conventional zero-field measurements.
Finally, the explicit identification of the magnetic
toroidal octupole paves a pathway to exploring its as-
sociated physical consequences, including nonreciprocal
transport phenomena arising from asymmetric electronic
band structures [55–57].
