Eliminating Persistent Boundary Residence via
Matrosov-Type Auxiliary Functions
Tianyu Han, Guangwei Wang, and Bo Wang
Abstract— Control barrier functions enforce safety by
guaranteeing forward invariance of an admissible set. Un-
der standard (non-strict) barrier conditions, however, for-
ward invariance alone does not prevent trajectories from
remaining on the boundary of the safe set for arbitrarily
long time intervals, potentially leading to boundary stick-
ing or deadlock phenomena. This paper studies the elim-
ination of persistent boundary residence under forward-
invariant barrier conditions. Inspired by Matrosov-type ar-
guments, we introduce an auxiliary function framework
that preserves forward invariance while excluding infinite-
time residence within boundary layers. Sufficient condi-
tions are established under which any trajectory can only
remain in a prescribed neighborhood of the boundary for
finite time, thereby restoring boundary-level liveness with-
out altering forward invariance. The proposed construction
does not rely on singular barrier formulations or controller-
specific modifications, and can be incorporated into stan-
dard safety-critical control architectures. Numerical exam-
ples illustrate the removal of boundary sticking behaviors
while maintaining safety across representative systems.
Index Terms— Control barrier functions, safety, liveness,
boundary residence, Matrosov-type auxiliary functions.
I. INTRODUCTION
C
ONTROL barrier functions (CBFs) have become a cen-
tral framework for enforcing safety in control systems
by guaranteeing forward invariance of an admissible set [1]–
[4]. By imposing inequality constraints on the derivative of a
barrier function, CBFs enable systematic controller synthesis
that ensures constraint satisfaction for systems with inputs. As
a result, CBF-based approaches have been successfully applied
across a wide range of safety-critical systems, including non-
holonomic mobile robotics [5], [6], robotic manipulations [7],
and connected autonomous vehicles [8], [9], to name a few.
While forward invariance guarantees that trajectories remain
within the safe set, it does not, under standard non-strict
barrier conditions, ˙h ≥−αh(h), preclude trajectories from
residing on the boundary for arbitrarily long time intervals.
Such boundary residence may lead to undesirable sticking,
sliding, or deadlock behaviors in safety-critical control im-
plementations [10], [11]. In practical implementations, barrier

conditions are often enforced through quadratic-programming
(QP)-based controllers. Although these formulations can ef-
ficiently combine safety and performance objectives in a
unified optimization framework at the same time, the resulting
closed-loop dynamics may inherit the non-strict nature of the
underlying barrier condition. In particular, when the barrier
constraint becomes active, the QP may admit equilibria on the
boundary of the safe set. More importantly, under certain geo-
metric alignments between performance and safety objectives,
the QP-based controller can render such undesired boundary
equilibria asymptotically stable, leading to persistent boundary
residence despite preserved safety [11].
When such boundary equilibria are asymptotically stable,
the resulting behavior cannot, in general, be eliminated by
arbitrarily small perturbations or implementation noise. In
particular, asymptotic stability entails Malkin’s total stability,
reflecting a form of local input-to-state robustness [12]. As
a consequence, trajectories near the equilibrium continue to
be attracted toward the boundary, rendering the associated
deadlock behavior structurally persistent rather than incidental.
A natural way to avoid this phenomenon is to enforce a
strict barrier condition, ˙h > −αh(h), which induces repelling
behavior at the boundary of the safe set [13]. Intuitively, such
a condition ensures that trajectories cannot remain on the
boundary and are instead driven back toward the interior of
the admissible set. However, enforcing strict barrier conditions
may be structurally incompatible with certain control systems.
In particular, for driftless systems (i.e., f(x) ≡0) such as
the unicycle model, there may exist boundary configurations
where Lgh(x) = 0, meaning that the control input cannot
influence the derivative of the barrier function. At such points
the barrier derivative becomes ˙h = 0 regardless of the chosen
control input, making the strict inequality ˙h > 0 infeasible
on the boundary. From an implementation standpoint, strict
barrier conditions also pose challenges for QP-based con-
trollers. Standard QP formulations operate on closed constraint
sets and cannot directly enforce strict inequalities [14]. As
a result, strict barrier conditions must be approximated by
introducing small margins or relaxation parameters, which
may compromise feasibility or alter the effective safety bound-
ary. Consequently, even when strict barrier conditions are
theoretically desirable to avoid boundary equilibria, their prac-
tical implementation within standard QP-based safety filters
remains nontrivial.
Several approaches have been proposed to mitigate the un-
desired equilibria and deadlock phenomena induced by CBF-
based controllers. One line of work analyzes the equilibrium

structure of CBF-based QP controllers and modifies the opti-

For example, an additional CBF constraint is introduced in
[11] to remove boundary equilibria; however, the feasibility of
the resulting QP cannot be guaranteed in general. The work
in [15] provides a detailed characterization of the equilibrium
points induced by CBF-based QP controllers and proposes
a modified formulation that eliminates certain interior and
boundary equilibria while preserving forward invariance. A
penalty-based optimization framework is developed in [16] to
confine or eliminate undesired equilibria via appropriate tuning
of a penalty parameter, while providing an inner approximation
of the closed-loop region of attraction. Another line of research
investigates how barrier functions and controller structures
influence the closed-loop dynamical behavior. In [17], the
relationship between the choice of the CBF and the resulting
closed-loop equilibria is analyzed, showing that the number
and locations of undesired equilibria are largely independent
of the particular barrier function selected. The work in [18]
studies the phenomenon from a dynamical perspective and
demonstrates, through Monte Carlo simulations, that liveness
can be associated with the instability of undesired equilibria.
This paper investigates how to prevent persistent boundary
residence while preserving the standard CBF safety frame-
work. Instead of enforcing strict barrier inequalities, we intro-
duce an auxiliary function formulation inspired by Matrosov
theorem in stability theory. Unlike the above-mentioned works
that address undesired equilibria by modifying the optimiza-
tion problem, penalty structure, or controller formulation to
remove certain undesired equilibria, our method is designed
to rule out persistent residence near the boundary, which is a
trajectory-level liveness property distinct from, and generally
not implied by, equilibrium elimination results alone. By
augmenting the barrier dynamics with an auxiliary function,
the proposed framework preserves forward invariance while
ensuring that trajectories cannot remain on the boundary of
the safe set for arbitrarily long time intervals. The main
contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: i) Finite
boundary residence under non-strict barrier conditions: Unlike
strict barrier conditions, which enforce instantaneous repulsion
from the boundary, the proposed formulation allows trajec-
tories to temporarily evolve on the boundary but guarantees
that such residence cannot persist indefinitely. This provides
a weaker yet practically meaningful sufficient condition that
restores boundary liveness without requiring strict barrier
inequalities; and ii) Auxiliary-function-based framework: We
propose an auxiliary barrier formulation that guarantees finite-
time escape from boundary neighborhoods while preserving
forward invariance. The resulting conditions depend only on
the barrier dynamics and can be directly incorporated into
standard CBF-based controllers.
Conceptually, the proposed framework lies between clas-
sical non-strict and strict CBF conditions: it preserves the
flexibility of non-strict barriers while excluding persistent
boundary residence. We demonstrate the proposed framework
through representative examples and simulations, illustrating
its effectiveness across different system dynamics and control
implementations.

in stability theory concerning strict and non-strict Lyapunov

that trajectories converge to an invariant set rather than to
the equilibrium itself. In contrast, a strict Lyapunov function
ensures asymptotic convergence to the equilibrium. When
only a non-strict Lyapunov function is available, asymptotic
stability can still be established by introducing additional
auxiliary functions. Matrosov’s theorem [12] provides a sys-
tematic framework for this purpose: by augmenting the Lya-

convergence to the equilibrium can be recovered even in the
absence of strict decay conditions.

V1(t, x(t)) is non-increasing along system trajectories and

derivative possesses a non-zero definiteness property on the set
{x : W(x) = 0}. This additional property prevents trajectories
from remaining on or near this invariant set and ensures
that trajectories eventually leave its neighborhood. Matrosov’s
theorem therefore allows asymptotic convergence to be estab-
lished even when only a non-strict Lyapunov function V1 is

developed in this paper: by introducing a Matrosov-type
auxiliary function, persistent residence of trajectories on the
boundary of the safe set can be excluded even when the barrier
condition itself is non-strict.
Under standard barrier condition (3), trajectories may con-
verge to or remain on the boundary of the safe set ∂C for
arbitrarily long time intervals. Although reciprocal or strict
barrier conditions can theoretically prevent such behavior, they
are often incompatible with optimization-based implementa-
tions, where constraints must be expressed as non-strict linear
inequalities. This paper develops conditions that guarantee
safety while excluding persistent residence of trajectories on
the boundary of the safe set.
III. MAIN RESULTS
The key idea underlying the following result is to recast
persistent residence near the boundary as a boundedness
contradiction argument in the spirit of Matrosov theorem. If
a trajectory were to stay in a neighborhood of the boundary
for arbitrarily long times, then the auxiliary function would
accumulate a strictly monotone variation over such intervals.
Since the auxiliary function is bounded, this is impossible. Un-
like strict barrier conditions, which directly impose a stronger
differential inequality on the barrier function, the present
argument excludes persistent boundary residence indirectly
through an auxiliary-function contradiction mechanism.

V. CONCLUSION
This paper investigated the elimination of persistent bound-
ary residence under standard non-strict barrier conditions. By


## Page 7


introducing a bounded Matrosov-type auxiliary function with a
derivative bounded away from zero near the boundary, we ob-
tained a trajectory-level criterion ensuring finite-time exit from
sufficiently thin boundary layers, while preserving forward
invariance. The result does not rely on strict barrier inequalities
or singular barrier formulations, and was illustrated through a
CLF-CBF-QP implementation and representative systems.
