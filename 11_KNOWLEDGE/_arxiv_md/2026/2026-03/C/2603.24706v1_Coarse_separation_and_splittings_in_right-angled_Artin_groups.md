Abstract
In this article, we characterise geometrically when a right-angled Artin group splits
over an abelian subgroup. More precisely, given a finite graph Γ, we show that A(Γ) splits
over an abelian subgroup if and only if it is coarsely separable by a family of subexponential
growth, which amounts to saying that Γ is complete or separated by a complete subgraph.

Introduction
Separation phenomena in groups have long been central in geometric group theory, as
they frequently signal the existence of algebraic splittings.
A foundational result is
Stallings’ theorem [Sta71]: a finitely generated group has more than one end if and only
if it splits over a finite subgroup. Equivalently, the Cayley graph of such a group can
be divided into at least two deep components by removing a finite set precisely when
the group admits a splitting over a finite subgroup. Thus, a coarse separation property
already reveals a nontrivial splitting, and therefore an algebraic feature that remains in-
variant under quasi-isometry. This perspective has been extended in several directions.
Dunwoody and Swenson [DS00] provided an initial generalization by broadening the
subgroup-theoretic aspect of Stallings’ theorem from finite subgroups to finitely gener-
ated virtually polycyclic codimension one subgroups. In particular, if G is one-ended
and not virtually a surface group, then G splits over a two-ended subgroup if and only
if it contains an infinite cyclic codimension-one subgroup. Here, a subgroup H ≤G is
called codimension-one if some (or equivalenlty, any) Schreier graph Sch(G, H) (with
respect to a finite generating set) has at least two ends. We refer the reader to [Wal03]
for more background on the connection between codimension-one subgroups and split-
tings. A further generalization, due to Papasoglu [Pap05], addresses the geometric side
of Stallings’ theorem by replacing finite separating sets with quasi-lines. He proved that
a one-ended finitely presented group that is not commensurable to a surface group splits
over a two-ended subgroup if and only if its Cayley graph is separated by a quasi-line.
In particular, the existence of such a splitting is invariant under quasi-isometry.
In the same spirit, and building on our previous work [BGT24], we investigate coarse
separation by families of subsets rather than individual ones. Our approach is quantita-
tive, focusing on the volume growth of the separating sets instead of their quasi-isometry
type. We refer to §2 for our definition of coarse separation.
In the present paper, we consider the class of right angled Artin groups. Recall that,
given a finite simple graph Γ (that is, a graph with no loops nor multiple edges), the
associated right-angled Artin group A(Γ) is defined by the following presentation: its
generators correspond to the vertices of Γ, and two generators commute if and only if
the corresponding vertices are connected by an edge. Our main result is the following.
Theorem 1.1. Let Γ be a finite graph. The following assertions are equivalent:
(i) A(Γ) is coarsely separable by a family of subexponential growth;
(ii) A(Γ) splits over an abelian subgroup;
(iii) Γ is complete or separated by a complete subgraph.
The classification of right-angled Artin groups up to quasi-isometry is a longstanding
problem that has only been resolved in certain special cases. It is clear though that
right-angled Artin groups both exhibit flexibility and rigidity features depending on
the properties of the defining graph. For instance, on the one hand, Behrstock and
Neumann [BN08] showed that all groups A(Γ), where Γ is a tree of diameter at least
3, are quasi-isometric (see [BJN10] for a higher-dimensional analogue of this result).
On the other hand, widely generalising an earlier result of Bestvina, Kleiner and Sagev
[BKS09], Huang [Hua17] proved that if A(Γ1) and A(Γ2) both have finite automorphism
groups, then they are quasi-isometric if and only if they are isomorphic. The property
of having a finite automorphism group can be detected directly from the graph Γ (see
[Hua17]). In particular, such graphs are not separated by complete subgraphs. We have
the following non-trivial consequence of Theorem 1.1.

Corollary 1.2. Let G and H be two quasi-isometric right-angled Artin groups. If G
splits over an abelian subgroup, then so does H.
We now present the strategy of the proof of Theorem 1.1. We first present the main
ingredients, which are of independent interest, and then explain how to combine them
to obtain the final result.
Ingredient 1: From right-angled Artin groups to graph products of finite
groups. Given a graph Γ and a weight ν : V (Γ) →N≥2, we denote by Γ(ν) the graph
product Γ{Zν(u) | u ∈V (Γ)}.
The following theorem plays a central role in the proof of Theorem 1.1. (See Theorem 5.1
for a more general statement.)
Theorem 1.3. Let Γ be a finite graph that is not a join and that contains at least two
vertices. If A(Γ) is coarsely separable by a family of subexponential growth, then, for
every k ≥0, there exists a weight ν ≥k such that Γ(ν) is coarsely separable by a family
of subexponential growth.
The assumptions on Γ amounts to saying that A(Γ) be acylindrically hyperbolic. We
show that, in any finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic group, a coarsely separating
family with subexponential growth must coarsely separate a bi-infinite Morse geodesic
(see Theorem 3.1). We then observe that, in the right-angled Artin group A(Γ), every
bi-infinite Morse geodesic lies inside a quasi-isometrically embedded copy of Γ(ν), for
some weight ν whose minimum is sufficiently large compared to the Morse gauge of the
geodesic (see Theorem 5.2). This reduces the problem of coarse separation in a right-
angled Artin group to that of coarse separation in a graph product of finite groups.
Ingredient 2: Coarse separation in hyperbolic groups. Despite the fact that
right-angled Artin groups are usually not hyperbolic, many graph products of finite
groups are hyperbolic, which will allow us to deduce some valuable information on
coarse separation in right-angled Artin groups thanks to Theorem 1.3. The hyperbolic
case follows from the main result of a companion paper [BGT26b], where we prove the
following result.
Theorem 1.4. Let G be a hyperbolic group.
Assume that G is one-ended and not
virtually a surface group. Then G is coarsely separable by a family of subexponential
growth if and only if it splits over a virtually cyclic subgroup.
Combining Theorem 1.4 and Theorem 1.3 we deduce the following special case of The-
orem 1.1.
Corollary 1.5. Let Γ be a finite graph. If Γ is not complete, without cut-point, and
□-free, then A(Γ) is not coarsely separable by a family of subexponential growth.
Ingredient 3: Thickness and combinatorial reduction. Roughly speaking, a space
X is thick relative to a pair (A, B) of two collections of subspaces if any two points of
X can be connected by a chain of subspaces belonging to A such that the intersection
between any two consecutive subspace along this chain belong to B. A rather elementary
but useful observation is:
Proposition 1.6. Let X be an (A, B)-thick metric space. Assume that the subspaces in
A are uniformly not coarsely separable by families of subexponential growths and that the
subspaces in B have exponential growth. Then X is not coarsely separable by a family
of subexponential growth.

It is worth mentioning that Proposition 1.6 has applications to others groups than

In §6.1, we establish a graph-theoretic decomposition for triangle-free graphs that are
not separated by a complete subgraph. As a consequence, Theorem 6.7 shows that,
if Γ is triangle-free and not separated by a complete subgraph, then A(Γ) is (A, B)-
thick, where A consists of uniform neighborhoods of left cosets of parabolic subgroups
associated to cycles in Γ, and B consists of left cosets of parabolic subgroups associated
to pairs of non-adjacent vertices. This reduces the proof of Theorem 1.1 to the case
where Γ is a cycle of length at least 4, and provides the main technical application of
theorem 1.6 in the setting of right-angled Artin groups: under suitable assumptions,
if a family of subexponential growth coarsely separates an amalgamated product, then
it also coarsely separates one of the factor groups. Finally, in §6.3, we show that it is
enough to restrict to right-angled Artin groups defined by triangle-free graphs.
Recipe. If Γ is complete (and non-empty), then A(Γ) is free abelian and is clearly
coarsely separable by a subspace of polynomial growth.
If Γ contains a separating
complete subgraph, then A(Γ) splits over a free abelian subgroup, and consequently is
coarsely separable by a subspace of polynomial growth. Conversely, assuming that Γ is
not complete and cannot be separated by a complete subgraph, we need to show that
A(Γ) is not coarsely separable by a family of subexponential growth. By ingredient 3, it
suffices to verify that A(Cn) cannot be coarsely separated by a family of subexponential
growth for every n ≥4. For n = 4, A(Cn) is a product of two free group of rank two,
so the desired conclusion follows from [BGT24, Theorem 1.3]. For n ≥5, the desired
conclusion follows from Corollary 1.5.
Discussion.
Beyond its conceptual interest, and the new quasi-isometric invariants
it provides, it is worth mentioning that Theorem 1.1 imposes restrictions on coarse
embedding between right-angled Artin groups. Roughly speaking, it implies that, with
respect to some JSJ decompositions above abelian subgroups, rigid pieces are sent into
neighbourhoods of rigid pieces. See Section 7.1 for a precise statement and for an explicit
example for which we can show the non-existence of coarse embeddings. This connection
between JSJ decomposition and coarse embeddings remains to be further investigated.
(See [Mar20] for some information on the cyclic case.)
As another application, we will use Theorem 1.1 in [BGT26a] in order to characterise
algebraically which right-angled Artin groups virtually split over Zn, for a fixed n ≥0,
solving a problem from [Zar19].
In view of Theorem 1.1, two questions can be naturally asked. Right-angled Artin groups
belong to two more general family of groups: graph products of groups and cocompact
special groups. On the one hand, we expect that:
Conjecture 1.7. Let Γ be a finite graph and G a collection of infinite finitely generated
virtually nilpotent groups. The graph product ΓG is coarsely separable by a family of
subexponential growth if and only if Γ is complete or separated by a complete subgraph.
A proof should follow by combining a generalisation of Theorem 1.4 to relatively hyper-
bolic groups (conjectured in [BGT26b]) with the thickness of non-relatively hyperbolic
graph products that can be deduced from [Gen17, Theorem 8.35].
On the other hand, we ask:
Question 1.8. When is a cocompact special group (e.g. a right-angled Coxeter group,
or more generally a graph product of finite groups), coarsely separable by a family of
subexponential growth?

Preliminaries
In this section, we recall some basic definitions and notations related to coarse separa-
tion, following [BGT24].
If (X, d) is a metric space, we denote by B(x, r) (resp. S(x, r)) the closed ball (resp. the
sphere) of radius r, namely:
B(x, r) := {z ∈X | d(x, z) ≤r},
S(x, r) := {z ∈X | d(x, z) = r}.
If A ⊆X is a subset, we denote by |A| ∈N ∪{+∞} its cardinality; and by A+r its
r-neighborhood, namely:
A+r := {x ∈X | d(x, A) ≤r}.
Our definition of coarse separation is the following:
Definition 2.1. Let X be a metric space and A, Z two collections of subspaces. Then
A is coarsely separated by Z if there exist k, L ≥0 such that, for every D ≥0, one can
find some A ∈A and Z ∈Z satisfying:
• A is k-connected, and
• A\Z+L has at least two k-coarsely connected components with points at distance
≥D from Z.
Notice that, in the case where X is a graph and Z a collection of subgraphs, a connected
subgraph Y ⊂X is coarsely separated by Z if there exists L ≥0 such that, for every
D ≥0, there is some Z ∈Z such that Y \ Z+L has at least two connected components
with points at distance ≥D from Z.
Example 2.2. Note that, if Z coarsely separates some A ∈A, then it coarsely separates
A. The converse, however, is not true. For instance, if X = R, if Z = {{0}}, and if A
is the family of segments [−n, n] with n ∈N, then Z coarsely separates A but does not
coarsely separate any segment [−n, n].
In this paper, we focus our attention to coarse (non-)separation by families with slow
growth. More precisely:
Definition 2.3. Let X be a graph of bounded degree, and let S be a family of subgraphs
of X. We define its relative growth by

Coarse separation in acylindrically hyperbolic groups
This section is dedicated to the proof of the following statement. We refer the reader to
Section 3.1 for the definitions of acylindrically hyperbolic groups and Morse geodesics.
Roughly speaking, our theorem claims that, in a finitely generated hyperbolic-like
group, a coarsely separating family of subexponential growth must coarsely separate
two hyperbolic-like directions.
Theorem 3.1. Let G be a finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic group and let Z
a family of subspaces. If Z has subexponential growth and coarsely separates G, then
there exists a bi-infinite Morse geodesic in G that is coarsely separated by Z.
Section 3.1 records some basic definitions and properties related to Morse geodesics, and
Section 3.2 contains the proof of Theorem 3.1.
3.1
Morse geodesics
First, let us recall the definition of Morse subspaces.
Definition 3.2. Let X be a geodesic metric space. Given a map M : [0, +∞)×[0, +∞),
a subspace Y ⊂X is M-Morse, or Morse with Morse gauge M, if, for all A > 0 and
B ≥0, every (A, B)-quasigeodesic connecting two points of Y remains in the M(A, B)-
neighbourhood of Y .
It is worth mentioning that a Morse subspace is always coarsely connected and quasi-
isometrically embedded.
Morse subspaces include quasiconvex subspaces in hyperbolic spaces, but they can also
be easily found in acylindrically hyperbolic groups, which is why they interest us here.
Recall that:
Definition 3.3. A group G is acylindrically hyperbolic if it admits an action on some
hyperbolic space X that is non-elementary and acylindrical, i.e. for every D ≥0, we can
find N, L ≥0 such that
∀x, y ∈X, d(x, y) ≥L ⇒#{g ∈G | d(g · x, g · y) ≤D} ≤N.
The following observation records the fact that, in an acylindrically hyperbolic, there
are Morse rooted quasi-trees starting from every point.
Lemma 3.4. Let G be acylindrically hyperbolic group. There exists a Morse gauge M
such that, for every g ∈G, there exists an M-Morse quasi-tree (T2, o) ,→(G, g).
In our lemma, (T2, o) denotes a 2-regular tree rooted at o.
Proof of Lemma 3.4. According to [DGO17, Theorem 2.24] and [Sis16, Theorem 2], our
acylindrically hyperbolic group G contains a Morse free subgroup, hence a Morse rooted
tree based at 1. By translating our Morse tree, by g, we get a Morse rooted tree based
at g, as desired.

Coarse separation in thick spaces
In this section, we introduce a notion thickness which we will use as an obstruction to
coarse separation, thanks to Propositions 4.6 and 4.7 below. It is reminiscent of thick
metric spaces introduced in [BDM09] as an obstruction to relative hyperbolicity.
Definition 4.1. Let X be a metric space and A, B two collections of subspaces. A
subset E ⊂X is (A, B)-thick if, for all x, y ∈E, there exist A1, . . . , An ∈A such that
x ∈A1, y ∈An, and Ai ∩Ai+1 contains some B ∈B for every 1 ≤i ≤n −1.

This section is dedicated to the proof of the following statement:

Quasi-median graphs.
There are many possible equivalent definitions of quasi-median
graphs. A short one is the following. First, recall that a Hamming graph is a (connected
component of a) product of complete graphs. Then, quasi-median graphs are retracts
of Hamming graphs. This definition motivates the analogy with median graphs, also
known as one-skeletons of CAT(0) cube complexes, which can be characterised as re-
tracts of hypercubes. However, it may not be the easiest definition to manipulate. In
practice, it may be more convenient to define quasi-median graphs as weakly modular
graphs with no induced copy of K2,3 or K−
4 , but this may sound more artificial and may
be more difficult to digest. This characterisation can be found in [BMW94], and this is
the perspective taken in [Gen17]. Since we will not use this definition here, we do not
recall it. However, let us mention one piece of the definition of weakly modular graphs
for future use:
Lemma 5.3. A quasi-median graph X satisfies the triangle condition, i.e. for all vertices
o, x, y ∈V (X) satisfying d(o, x) = d(o, y) and d(x, y) = 1, there exists a common
neighbour z of x and y such that d(o, z) = d(o, x) −1.
Another rather easy consequence of the definition of quasi-median graphs as specific
weakly modular graphs is:
Lemma 5.4 ([Gen22, Lemma 3.3]). Let X be a quasi-median graph. If γ1 and γ2 are
two geodesics with the same endpoints, then γ2 can be obtained from γ1 by bypassing
finitely many squares.
We refer the reader to [BMW94] for other characterisations of quasi-median graphs. As
for median graphs, it is more informative to introduce hyperplanes and to describe how
they are related to the geometry of quasi-median graphs.
Definition 5.5. Let X be a quasi-median graph. Two edges a, b ∈E(X) are
• parallel if there exists e1 = a, . . . , en = b ∈E(X) such that ei and ei+1 are opposite
sides of a square in X for every 1 ≤i ≤n −1;
• in the same hyperplane if there exists e1 = a, . . . , en = b ∈E(X) such that ei and
ei+1 are parallel or belong to the same triangle for every 1 ≤i ≤n −1.
A hyperplane is a maximal collection of edges pairwise all in the same hyperplane. We
denote by Hyp(X) the set of the hyperplanes of X.
See Figure 1 for some examples of hyperplanes. Our next result motivates the idea that
the geometry of a quasi-median graph is encoded in the combinatorics of its hyperplanes.
Theorem 5.6 ([Gen17]). Let X be a quasi-median graph.
• Every hyperplane J separates, i.e. the graph X\\J obtained from X by removing
all the edges of J is disconnected. Its connected components are sectors. Sectors
are gated in X.
• For every hyperplane J, its carrier N(J) (i.e. the subgraph in X induced by J)
and its fibres (i.e. the connected components of N(J)\\J) are gated.
• For every hyperplane J, its carrier N(J) decomposes as C × F for all clique C
and fibre F of J.
• A path is a geodesic if and only if it crosses each hyperplane at most once. Conse-
quently, the distance between two vertices coincides with the number of hyperplanes
that separate them.

7.1
Complete-cut-decompositions
As a consequence of Theorem 1.1, there exist strong restrictions on possible coarse
embeddings between right-angled Artin groups. In order to motivate this assertion, we
need the following definition:
Definition 7.1. A complete-cut-decomposition (T, (Vs)s∈V (T)) of a graph X is the data
of a tree T and a collection of induced subgraphs Vs ≤X indexed by V (T) such that:
• {Vs | s ∈V (T)} covers X, i.e. E(X) = S
s∈V (T) E(Vs);
• for every s ∈V (T), Vs has no complete cut;
• for all adjacent r, s ∈V (T), Vr ∩Vs is a complete cut of X properly contained in
both Vr and Vs.
According to [BGT26a], every finite graph admits a complete-cut-decomposition. Then:
Corollary 7.2. Let Φ, Ψ be two finite graphs.
Fix two complete-cut-decompositions
(R, (Us)s∈V (R)) and (S, (Vs)s∈V (S)) respectively of Φ and Ψ.
If there exists a coarse
embedding φ : A(Φ) →A(Ψ), then, for every r ∈V (R) such that Ur is not complete,
there exists some s ∈V (S) such that φ sends ⟨Ur⟩in a neighbourhood of a coset of ⟨Vs⟩.
Proof. On the one hand, because Ur is not complete and has no complete cut, it follows
from Theorem 1.1 that ⟨Ur⟩is not coarsely separable by a family of subexponential
growth. On the other hand, to the complete-cut-decomposition (S, (Vs)s∈V (S)) of Ψ,
corresponds a graph-of-groups decomposition of A(Ψ) whose edge-groups are free abelian

As a concrete application of Corollary 7.2, let us mention a case where we can prove that
there does not exist a coarse embedding between two given right-angled Artin groups.
