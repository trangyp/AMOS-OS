ABSTRACT
An efficient way to get implicit equations of conics on five points and quadrics on nine, using pencils
of conics and quadrics, is revealed. Parallel axis right cones intersect on a conic. An example, to
show how to place five coplanar points on a cone, using kinematic mapping with dual quaternions is
presented. A second congruent cone is found as a translation of the first. Cone symmetry helps to
explain how mapping produces eight real solutions, apparently all different, belong to a unique cone
pair intersection. Future extension of this, pertaining to a nine point quadric, can be contrived if a
way to map planar points to parallel axis cones is formulated.
Keywords points, conics, quadrics, kinematic mapping, cones

Nine Point Quadric
Replacing Eq. 1 with a 10×10 matrix and evaluating ten 9×9 determinants incurs at least an order of magnitude greater
number of FLOPs than the conic. Now quadric pencils replace conic pencils. An octahedron replaces the quadrilateral
in Fig. 1. Six points on its vertices and eight planes, each defined by three of the points, together with three remaining
points together form a system of four plane pairs. Two plane pairs provide a double covering of the vertices. Combining
the remaining points requires four multipliers α, β, γ, δ as opposed to the two, λ, µ, for conics.
• Summarizing, three octahedron vertices among ABCDEF, e.g., ABC, define a plane,
• And, automatically, its partner that effects complete covering. Upper/lower case and Greek letters apply to
point/plane in the same convention as point/line was applied to conics in Section 1.1.
• Four of these are chosen from the available 10 plane pairs shown below. The choice is 10C4 = 210.
• From these
ABC ↔DEF, ABD ↔CEF, ABE ↔CDF, ABF ↔CDE,
ACD ↔BEF, ACE ↔BDF, ACF ↔BDE, ADE ↔BCF,
ADF ↔BCE, AEF ↔BCD
• Four plane pairs
ABF ↔CDE ≡pv, ADE ↔BCF ≡ru,
ADF ↔BCE ≡ts, ABE ↔CDF ≡qw
are chosen.
• Like the two of three line pairs in Eq. 2 the four plane pairs, arbitrarily selected, are assembled into the sum of
four plane products and multipliers, Eq. 6,
pvα + ruβ + tsγ + qwδ = 0.
(6)

ABF ↔CDF, AED ↔EBC, ADF ↔BCF, ECD ↔AEB,
substituted into Eq. 6 yields the same quadric.

Five Points Placed on a Conic Section
Given five coplanar points ABCDE –different point names for a different numerical example to exploit advantage
of their ideal placement without too much sacrifice of generality– that define a unique conic according to Section 2,
above, what is the relation of µ : λ in Eq. 2 to a planar section of an right cone on the five points, chosen to simplify
algebra, with apex on origin, angle π/2 and axis on the z-axis? A possible approach, but as yet without answering the
question, might be to find the disposition of that plane and the five points by mapping them to an actual cone using dual
quaternion (DQ) algebra as outlined below.
• Define a planar rigid body in the end effector frame EE containing
A(0, 0, 0), B(b1, 0, 0), C(c1, c2, 0), D(d1, d2, 0), E(e1, e2, 0).
• Define the fixed frame FF with cone apex and axis as specified above.
• Map the five points in EE to FF as follows.
• Place A on the cone generator defined by the intersection of planes y = 0 and x −z = 0.
• Map the remaining four points to the cone x2 + y2 −z2 = 0 in FF with a triple DQ product.
• Placement of A provides two linear constraint equations.
• Mapping of BCDE entails four quadratic constraints.

has 16 complex roots. They are artifacts of absolute points on intersecting coplanar circles ruling two congruent cones

Conclusion
• Efficient ways to compute implicit equations of conics and quadrics on points and the underlying geometry
were described.
• Kinematic mapping was used to move planar point arrays onto conic sections, relating these to their equations.
• How the section is shared on the intersection curve between two congruent cones was shown.
• Multipliers extract the unique conic or quadric using two singular members of the pencil in which it resides.
• Future work is intended to connect multipliers with the displacement between intersecting cone pair.
• To do this for quadrics can Zyklographie of planar points and cones be extended to comprehend spatial points
and four dimensional cones?
