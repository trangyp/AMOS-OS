Virtual splittings of right-angled Artin groups
Oussama Bensaid, Anthony Genevois, and Romain Tessera
April 1, 2026
Abstract
In this article, we determine, given a finite graph Γ and an integer n ≥1, when a
right-angled Artin group A(Γ) virtually splits over an abelian subgroup of rank n. More
precisely, we show that the following assertions are equivalent: (1) A(Γ) admits Zn as a
codimension-one subgroup, (2) A(Γ) virtually splits over Zn, (3) A(Γ) splits over Zn, and
(4) Γ either is a complete graph with n + 1 vertices or contains a complete subgraph of
size n that has a subgraph separating Γ. Our result improves the main result of [Zar19].

Introduction
Given a graph Γ, the right-angled Artin group A(Γ) is given by the presentation
⟨u vertex of Γ | [u, v] = 1 whenever u and v are adjacent in Γ⟩.
Right-angled Artin groups interpolate between free groups (when “nothing commutes”,
i.e. Γ has no edges) and free abelian groups (when “everything commutes”, i.e. Γ is a com-
plete graph). Despite the early appearance of similar interpolations for other algebraic
structures, such as monoids and algebras (see for instance [CF69, KMLNR80, Ber76]), as
well as a few mentions of the groups in some articles [HR72, KR80, Dic81], the study of
right-angled Artin groups started in earnest with the work of Baudisch [Bau77, Bau81]
and Droms [Dro83, Dro87a, Dro87b, Dro87c]. Since then, many articles have been ded-
icated to right-angled Artin groups from various perspectives. In addition to being an
instructive source of examples, right-angled Artin groups also led to interesting applica-
tions. Two major contributions in this direction include the Morse theory developed in
[BB97] and the theory of special cube complexes introduced in [HW08].
Despite the fact that right-angled Artin groups have been extensively studied, a few
very natural questions remain completely open:

(Commensurability Problem) Given two graphs Γ1 and Γ2, when are A(Γ1) and

(Surface Subgroup Problem) Given a graph Γ, when does A(Γ) have a subgroup

In this article, we are mainly interested in the Commensurability Problem, which is
of course closely related to the Quasi-isometric Problem since commensurable finitely
generated groups are automatically quasi-isometric. The Commensurability Problem
has been solved only for a few families of graphs. An interesting playground is given
by trees, because right-angled Artin groups defined by finite trees of diameter ≥3 are
known to be all quasi-isometric [BN08].
So far, the Commensurability Problem has
been solved only for trees of diameter ≤4 [CRKZ19] and paths [CRKZ21]. Aside trees,
notable examples are given by results of quasi-isometric rigidity [BKS08, Hua17, Hua16],
including right-angled Artin groups with finite outer automorphism groups.
Several
quasi-isometric and commensurability invariants are also worth mentioning, including
divergence [BC12, ABD+13], cut-points in asymptotic cones [BC12], extension graphs
for some graphs of large girth [KK14, Corollary 8], Morse boundaries [CCS23], and some
homotopy types of simplicial complexes [AGMP25].
More precisely, we are interested in the following question: when does a right-angled
Artin group virtually split over an abelian subgroup, i.e. when does it contain a finite-
index subgroup that decomposes non-trivially as an amalgamated product or an HNN
extension over an abelian subgroup? For splittings over trivial subgroups, i.e. free prod-
uct decompositions, one knows from Stallings’ theorem that admitting such a splitting is
preserved by quasi-isometry, and in particular by commensurability, and it is known that
a given right-angled Artin group splits non-trivially as a free product if and only if the
graph that defines it is disconnected. For splittings over infinite cyclic subgroups, one
also knows from [Pap05] that admitting such a splitting is preserved by quasi-isometry,
and in particular by commensurability, and [Cla14] shows that a given right-angled Artin
group splits non-trivially over an infinite cyclic group if and only if the graph that defines
it has a cut-vertex. For splittings over abelian subgroups of arbitrary rank, it follows
from [BGT26] that, among finitely generated right-angled Artin groups, admitting such
a splitting is again preserved by quasi-isometry, and in particular by commensurability,
which happens precisely when the graph defining the right-angled Artin group under
consideration has a separating complete subgraph [GH17].
But what about virtual
splittings over an abelian subgroup with a fixed rank ≥2?

The first work to investigate virtual splittings over abelian subgroups is [Zar19], where
it is proved that, if a right-angled Artin group A(Γ) virtually splits over an abelian
subgroup of rank n, then Γ must be separated by some complete subgraph with ≤n
vertices. The main result of our article is the following characterisation:
Theorem 1.1. Let Γ be a finite graph and n ≥0 an integer. The following assertions
are equivalent:
(i) A(Γ) contains a coarsely separating abelian subgroup of rank n;
(ii) A(Γ) virtually splits over an abelian subgroup of rank n;
(iii) A(Γ) splits over an abelian subgroup of rank n;
(iv) Γ either is a complete graph with n + 1 vertices or contains a complete subgraph
of size n that has a subgraph separating Γ.
Here, given a finitely generated group G, a subgroup H ≤G is coarsely separating if
some neighbourhood of H in (a Cayley graph of) G has at least two deep connected
components (i.e. two components with points arbitrarily far away from H). Examples of
coarsely separating include codimension-one subgroups, i.e. subgroups with ≥2 relative
ends (as defined in [Hou74]). In fact, since a subgroup on which there is a splitting always
has codimension-one, the conditions (i) −(iv) from Theorem 1.1 are also equivalent to
A(Γ) containing a codimension-one abelian subgroup of rank n. (Actually, it turns out
that an abelian subgroup is coarsely separating if and only if it is virtually a codimension-
one subgroup. We refer the reader to [Gen26] and references therein for more information
on the similarities and differences between codimension-one and coarsely separating
subgroups.)
As an immediate consequence of Theorem 1.1, one obtains the following commensura-
bility invariant:
Corollary 1.2. Let Γ1 and Γ2 be two finite graphs such that A(Γ1) and A(Γ2) are com-
mensurable. If Γ1 contains a complete subgraph of size n that has a subgraph separating
Γ1, then Γ2 must also contain such a subgraph.
Notice that, since the condition (iv) from Theorem 1.1 implies that our graph Γ must
contain a separating complete subgraph with ≤n vertices, one recovers the main result
of [Zar19] from Theorem 1.1.

Right-angled Artin groups
This section is dedicated to the following observation about right-angled Artin groups,
which will play an important role in the proof of Theorem 1.1.
Proposition 2.1. Let Γ be a graph and Λ ≤Γ a complete subgraph of size m. If ⟨Λ⟩is
virtually contained in an abelian group of rank n ≥m, then Λ is contained in a complete
subgraph of size n.
Here, given a graph Γ and a subgraph Λ ≤Γ, we denote by:
• link(Λ) the subgrapb of Γ induced by the vertices that are adjacent to all the
vertices of Λ;
• star(Λ) the subgraph of Γ induced by Λ ∪link(Λ);
• ⟨Λ⟩the subgroup of A(Γ) generated by the vertex-set V (Λ) of Λ (thought of as
generators of A(Γ)).
Our proposition will be an easy consequence of the next two elementary facts about
right-angled Artin groups.
Lemma 2.2. Let Γ be a graph and Λ ≤Γ a subgraph. The commensurator of ⟨Λ⟩in
A(Γ) coincides with its normaliser, namely ⟨star(Λ)⟩.
Proof. Let g ∈Comm(⟨Λ⟩), i.e. g⟨Λ⟩g−1 ∩⟨Λ⟩has finite index in ⟨Λ⟩. We know from
[AM15, Proposition 3.4] that there exist h ∈⟨Λ⟩and Ξ ≤Λ such that g⟨Λ⟩g−1 ∩⟨Λ⟩=
h⟨Ξ⟩h−1. Under the abelianisation map A(Γ) →ZV (Γ), h⟨Ξ⟩h−1 is sent to ZV (Ξ), which
must have finite index in the image ZV (Λ) of ⟨Λ⟩. The only possibility is that Ξ = Λ.
Hence
g⟨Λ⟩g−1 ∩⟨Λ⟩= h⟨Ξ⟩h−1 = h⟨Λ⟩h−1 = ⟨Λ⟩,
i.e. ⟨Λ⟩≤g⟨Λ⟩g−1. By applying the same argument to g−1 ∈Comm(⟨Λ⟩) shows that

Thus, we have proved that the commensurator of ⟨Λ⟩in A(Γ) coincides with its nor-
maliser. The description of this normaliser is given by [AM15, Proposition 3.13].
Lemma 2.3. Let Γ be a graph and n ≥0 an integer. The right-angled Artin group A(Γ)
contains an abelian subgroup of rank n if and only if Γ contains a complete subgraph of
size n.
Proof. If Γ contains a complete subgraph Λ with n vertices, then ⟨Λ⟩provides an abelian
subgroup of rank n of A(Γ). Conversely, if Γ does not contain a complete subgraph with
n vertices, then its Salvetti complex has dimension ≤n−1, which implies that A(Γ) has
cohomological dimension ≤n−1, and consequently cannot contain an abelian subgroup
of rank n.

Coarse separation
In this section, we record basic definitions and properties about coarse separation that
will be useful for the proof of Theorem 1.1. We start by recalling the definition of coarse
separation used in [BGT24] (restricted to connected graphs for simplicity).
Definition 4.1. Let X be a connected graph and Z a collection of subgraphs.
A
connected subgraph Y ⊂X is coarsely separated by Z if there exists L ≥0 such that,
for every D ≥0, there is some Z ∈Z such that Y \ Z+L has at least two connected
components with points at distance ≥D from Z.
Understanding coarse separation in Euclidean spaces will be important in order to prove
Theorem 1.1. We start by recording two elementary observations.

Lemma 4.2. For every n ≥1, a subgroup of Zn is coarsely separating if and only if its
rank is n −1.
Proof. Let H ≤Zn be a subgroup. First, assume that H has rank n. In other words, H
has finite index in Zn. As a quasi-dense subspace, it cannot be coarsely separating. Next,
assume that H has rank n−1. Then, Zn/H is virtually infinite cyclic, hence two-ended.
The preimage under the quotient map Zn ↠Zn/H of a ball separating Zn/H into at
least two unbounded connected components yields a neighbourhood of H that separates
Zn into at least two deep connected components (i.e. components with point arbitrarily
far away from H). Thus, H is coarsely separating. Finally, assume that H has rank
≤n −2. Fix a basis {a1, . . . , ak} of H. Because coarse separation is preserved under
quasi-isometry, H coarsely separates Zn if and only if the vector subspace V spanned
by {a1, . . . , ak} coarsely separates Rn. But V has dimension ≤n −2, so such a coarse
separation cannot occur. Thus, H is not coarsely separating.

If Y coarsely separates Zn, then it must be quasi-dense in Zn−1 × {0}.

In order to prove Theorem 1.1, we start by extracting from [BGT26] a weak version of
our theorem:
Theorem 5.1. Let Γ be a finite graph. The following assertions are equivalent:
(i) A(Γ) contains a coarsely separating abelian subgroup;
(ii) A(Γ) virtually splits over an abelian subgroup;
(iii) A(Γ) splits over an abelian subgroup;
(iv) Γ contains a separating complete subgraph or is complete.

Proof of Theorem 1.1. If Γ is a complete graph with n + 1 vertices, then A(Γ) is a free

separates Γ, then we show that A(Γ) splits over Zn as follows. Because Λ0 separates Γ,
