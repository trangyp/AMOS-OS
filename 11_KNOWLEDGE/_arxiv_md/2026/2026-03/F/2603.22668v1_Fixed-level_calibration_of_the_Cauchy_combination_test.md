FIXED-LEVEL CALIBRATION OF THE CAUCHY COMBINATION

HIROFUMI OTA
Abstract. The Cauchy combination test (CCT) is widely used because it gives
a closed-form combined p-value and is known to be asymptotically valid as the
nominal level α ↓0 under broad dependence structures. We study a different
asymptotic question: whether the usual Cauchy cutoff remains accurate at an
ordinary fixed level when the number K of combined p-values grows under de-
pendence. Under a canonical one-factor equicorrelated Gaussian copula model,
we show that the raw CCT is generally not asymptotically exact at fixed α. With
fixed positive correlation, the statistic converges to a random latent-factor limit,
so there is no universal fixed-level reference law. When the common correla-
tion ρK weakens with K, fixed-level behaviour is governed by the boundary-
layer scale sK = √ρK(log K)3/2, and the raw CCT is asymptotically exact if
and only if ρK(log K)3 →0. Because the size distortion arises entirely from
the reference law and not from the statistic, it can be corrected without mod-
ifying the test statistic itself. We propose the boundary-layer calibrated CCT
(BL-CCT), which replaces the standard Cauchy reference by a one-parameter
Gaussian-smoothed Cauchy family while keeping the statistic unchanged. This
reference-law correction is fundamentally different from existing approaches that
modify the test statistic. BL-CCT is asymptotically exact under the weaker con-
dition ρK log K →0 and provides a useful finite-K approximation on bounded
boundary layers. Numerical experiments support the theory.
1. Introduction
1.1. Overview. Combining many p-values into a single global test is a classical
problem in mathematical statistics. Among analytic combination rules, the Cauchy
combination test (CCT) of Liu and Xie [LX20] is attractive because it yields a
closed-form combined p-value and remains numerically stable at very small sig-
nificance levels. Its standard asymptotic justification, however, is a vanishing-level
result: under broad conditions on the null dependence structure, the CCT statistic
is asymptotically standard Cauchy as the nominal level α ↓0. In practice the test
is routinely applied at ordinary levels such as 0.05 or 0.01 with a large number
K of component tests; see, for example, [LLZ+20, ZBZ+22]. This creates a gap
between theory and practice: does the usual Cauchy cutoff remain asymptotically
exact when α is fixed and K →∞under dependence? Recent numerical work
indicates that the answer can be negative [LLZL23, OLZX24, GJW25, AL25].
Key words and phrases. p-value combination; analytic correction; dependence; fixed-level cali-
bration; Gaussian copula.

We study this question under the one-factor equicorrelated Gaussian copula
model, in which all dependence among the p-values is generated by a single shared
Gaussian factor. This model is widely adopted in genomic applications and is ana-
lytically tractable because conditioning on the latent factor renders the component
tests independent. The key finding is that, although the conditional Cauchy limit
familiar from vanishing-level theory still holds, a deterministic centring term in-
duced by the latent factor grows with K and ρK and displaces the null distribution
of the statistic. This centring is the source of the fixed-level size distortion. It is
of lower order than the statistic itself and therefore vanishes under the rescaling
implicit in α ↓0, which is why vanishing-level analysis does not detect it.
1.2. Main contributions. Our first result concerns the raw CCT developed by
[LX20]. When the common correlation ρ is fixed and positive, the CCT statistic
converges to a random latent-factor limit, so there is no universal fixed-level ref-
erence law. When instead ρK ↓0 as K →∞, the transition between exact and
inexact behaviour is governed by the boundary-layer scale
sK = √ρK(log K)3/2.
When sK is bounded, the conditional 1-stable fluctuation reduces to a standard
Cauchy law, but a deterministic centring term of order sK persists in the null dis-
tribution. The raw CCT is therefore asymptotically exact at fixed α if and only if
ρK(log K)3 →0.
A key consequence is that the size distortion originates from a mismatch be-
tween the standard Cauchy reference law and the actual null distribution of TK,
not from the heavy-tailed fluctuation of the statistic. The statistic TK itself does
not need to be modified. We exploit this by introducing the boundary-layer cali-
brated CCT (BL-CCT), which replaces only the standard Cauchy cutoff by a one-
parameter Gaussian-smoothed Cauchy family indexed by sK. Under this correc-
tion, exactness holds under the weaker condition ρK log K →0, and on bounded
boundary layers the reference family already provides a useful finite-K approxima-
tion. On the broader scale cK = ρK log K, the remaining size distortion is explicit
and strictly conservative at conventional levels such as 0.05 and 0.1.
1.3. Related works. Existing asymptotic theory for the CCT and its extensions [LX20,
LLZL23, FCPT23, GJW25] operates in the regime α ↓0 with K fixed or growing.
The present paper instead fixes α at a conventional level and lets K →∞, which
reveals a latent-factor drift mechanism that vanishing-level analysis does not de-
tect.
Several recent studies address the finite-K size distortion of the CCT by modify-
ing the test statistic: a positive adjustment to the combination weights [OLZX24],
a right-tail-weighted combination encompassing Cauchy-type statistics [LMP25],
a truncated variant [CXG25], and a stepwise procedure [BLS25]. In contrast, BL-
CCT keeps the statistic unchanged and corrects only the reference law, preserving
the computational simplicity of the original CCT. The present paper gives a pre-
cise characterisation of the fixed-level transition and identifies the boundary-layer
scale.

6. Conclusion
This paper has studied the fixed-level calibration of the Cauchy combination test
under dependence. In the one-factor Gaussian copula model, the size distortion of
the raw CCT is driven by a latent-factor centring term, yielding the sharp threshold
ρK(log K)3 →0. Because this distortion arises from the inadequacy of the standard
Cauchy reference law rather than from the statistic itself, it can be corrected by
replacing the Cauchy reference with a Gaussian-smoothed Cauchy family, leading
to BL-CCT with the weaker exactness condition ρK log K →0.

Several directions remain open. The equicorrelated model relies on a single
scalar ρK. Under a multi-factor model, each latent factor contributes its own cen-
tring term, and whether the one-parameter BL-CCT family suffices or a multi-
parameter reference is needed remains unclear. Heterogeneous or block-structured
correlations would require a different decomposition of the truncated conditional
mean. The present analysis is confined to null calibration; the power properties of
BL-CCT under specific alternatives remain to be investigated.
The fixed-level mechanism is also not specific to the Cauchy transform. Any
combination rule based on a regularly varying score function with tail index 1,
including the harmonic mean p-value [Wil19] and the classes studied by [FCPT23]
and [GJW25], will produce a similar centring effect under the same copula model.
A unified treatment covering general regularly varying scores is a natural next step.
