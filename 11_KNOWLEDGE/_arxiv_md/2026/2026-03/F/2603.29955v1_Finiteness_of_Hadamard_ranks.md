FINITENESS OF HADAMARD RANKS
DARIO ANTOLINI, EDOARDO BALLICO, AND ALESSANDRO ONETO
Abstract. The Hadamard rank of a point with respect to a projective variety is, if it exists, the minimum
number of points of the variety whose coordinate-wise product is the given point. We classify the projective
varieties for which the Hadamard rank is finite for any point. As a by-product we obtain the finiteness of
the Hadamard rank with respect to varieties of tensors, such as Grassmannians, Chow varieties, varieties
of reducible forms and their secant varieties, complementing previous known results on secant varieties of
Segre-Veronese varieties. We prove sharp upper bounds on the maximum Hadamard rank for certain families
of algebraic varieties: this is a consequence of a result on the lower semi-continuity of the Hadamard rank
for curves that do not contain points with at least two zero coordinates.
1. Introduction
In [CMS10], the authors introduced the definition of Hadamard product of algebraic varieties to study
statistical models known as Restricted Boltzmann Machines by means of algebraic and tropical geometry.
Such approach was continued and generalized in [CTY10, MM15, Mon18].
Tensors encoding the joint probabilities that lie on Discrete Restricted Boltzmann Machines can be expressed
as entry-wise product of low-rank tensors. This interpretation has been considered in [SM18, OV26]. A
general geometric framework for such decompositions of tensors was formalized in [AMO25] where the
definition of Hadamard ranks was introduced with respect to any algebraic variety. This notion can be
regarded as a multiplicative version of the classical additive notion of rank with respect to any algebraic
variety which is geometrically studied through secant varieties, see [OV25] for a recent survey.
Notation. Let V be a (N + 1)-dimensional C-vector space and fix a basis {e0, . . . , eN}. For any vector
p = p0e0 + · · · + pNeN, we denote by (p0 : · · · : pN) ∈PV = PN the corresponding projective point.
Definition 1.1. Let p = (p0 : · · · : pN), q = (q0 : · · · : qN) ∈PV . Their Hadamard product is defined, if it
exists, as the coordinate-wise product p ⋆q = (p0q0 : · · · : pNqN).
Let X ⊂PV be any irreducible projective variety.
Definition 1.2. The Hadamard-X-rank of a point q ∈PV is defined as
HrkX(q) = min{m | q = p1 ⋆· · · ⋆pm, pi ∈X},
with the convention that HrkX(q) = ∞if the set of such decompositions is empty.
The question that we address in this paper, which naturally arises from this definition, is the following.
Question. For which X ⊂PV and q ∈PV the Hadamard-X-rank of q is finite? For which X ⊂PV the
Hadamard-X-rank is finite for every point of PV ?
Having in mind applications to tensor decompositions, in [AMO25], the authors focused on toric varieties,
such as Segre-Veronese varieties, and their secant varieties. Since toric varieties are Hadamard-idempotent,
see e.g., [FOW17, Proposition 4.7], the Hadamard-rank with respect to toric varieties is infinite for every
point outside the variety: in particular, that is the case for Segre-Veronese varieties. At the same time, it was
proven in [AMO25, Corollary 3.8] that the Hadamard-rank with respect to secant varieties of toric varieties

Contrarily to the classical notion of matrix rank, it is well known that the additive notion of X-rank fails
often to be lower semi-continuous: a first example is given by Rational Normal Curves in P3.
In Example 3.1 it is given a family of points contained in a Hadamard power X⋆m but with infinite Hadamard-
X-rank. That is not the only pathology against lower semi-continuity of Hadamard ranks: in the following
example, we give a point contained in X⋆2 but with Hadamard-X-rank equal to 3.
Example 4.1. Consider the conic C = {x0(x1 + x2) + (x1 −x2)2 = 0} ⊂P2. Note that C is strongly
concise, thus the Hadamard rank is finite for every point by Theorem A. Also, note that C is not binomial,
thus C⋆2 = P2 by Theorem 2.6. The conic C is tangent to the line H0 = {x0 = 0} at the point (0 : 1 : 1).
We compute the Hadamard-C-rank of all points in H0.
• (0 : 1 : 1) has Hadamard-C-rank equal to 1. This is the only one in H0 since C ∩H0 = {(0 : 1 : 1)}.
• (0 : s : t) with (s : t) ̸∈{(1 : 1), (1 : −1)} have Hadamard-C-rank equal to 2. Indeed, we can write
(0 : s : t) = (0 : 1 : 1) ⋆(−(s −t)2 : s(s + t) : t(s + t)).

Definition 4.2. Let X ⊂PV be an algebraic variety and p ∈PV . The border Hadamard-X-rank of p is
HrkX(p) = min{m | p ∈X⋆m}.
with the convention that if p ̸∈X⋆m for all m ≥1, then HrkX(p) = ∞.
We now show that for varieties whose points do not contain too many zeroes, border rank equals rank in the
low-rank regime.
We recall the following notation from [BC24].
Notation. For all i = 0, . . . , N, we denote by ∆i ⊂PN the variety of points with at most i + 1 non-zero
coordinates. Hence
∆0 = {(1 : 0 : · · · : 0), . . . , (0 : · · · : 0 : 1)} ⊂∆1 ⊂· · · ⊂∆N−1 = H0 ∪· · · ∪HN ⊂∆N = PN.
In particular, ∆N−k ⊂PN is the variety of points having at least k coordinates equal to zero.
Remark 4.3. Let X ⊂PN be an irreducible variety of dimension n. Since a linear space of dimension at

Lemma 4.5. Let X ⊂PN be an irreducible variety of dimension n ≤N/2 such that X ∩∆N−n−1 = ∅.

Proof. Since X ∩∆N−n−1 = ∅, the points of X have at most n coordinates equal to zero. A product of r

□

Proof. Since (1) implies that ηN(C) = PN, (2) follows from (1) and Lemma 4.5. We prove (1).
