Abstract. Let k be a nonarchimedean local field. For any n ě 3, we construct the first
examples of robust quasi-isometric embeddings of non-elementary free groups into GLnpkq
which are not limits of Anosov representations. If K “ R, C, we exhibit examples of
non-locally rigid, robust quasi-isometric embeddings of virtually free groups into GLnpKq,
n ě 3, which are not limits of Anosov representations. Moreover, we exhibit a non-Anosov
robust quasi-isometric embedding of the free semigroup Z ˚ Z` into GL3pCq, which is a
limit of Anosov representations.
1. Introduction
Let k be a local field. A representation ρ : Γ Ñ GLnpkq, n ě 2, of a finitely generated group
Γ is called a quasi-isometric embedding if the orbit map of ρ is a quasi-isometric embedding
of Γ into the Riemannian symmetric space (resp. Bruhat–Tits building) of GLnpkq, where k is
Archimedean (resp. nonarchimedean). The representation ρ is called a robust quasi-isometric
embedding1 (resp. robustly discrete-faithful) if there exists an open neighborhood of ρ, in
the space of representations HompΓ, GLnpkqq, containing entirely quasi-isometric embeddings
(resp. discrete-faithful representations).
Anosov representations of Gromov hyperbolic groups comprise a rich class of robust quasi-
isometric embeddings into reductive groups. Anosov representations into real reductive Lie
groups were introduced by Labourie [22] and further developed by Guichard–Wienhard [16];
the definition extends for representations of hyperbolic groups into the k-points of reductive
groups defined over a nonarchimedean local field k, see [19, Def. 5.31], [26, Def. 3.1], and [15,
Rmk. 1.6]. Sullivan in [28] established that a robustly faithful2 non-locally rigid representation
of a finitely generated group into PSL2pCq is convex cocompact and hence Anosov. Motivated
by Sullivan’s structural stability theorem, R. Potrie and F. Kassel independently asked in [25,
Q.5] and [20, Q.1] whether a non-locally rigid robust quasi-isometric embedding (resp. robustly
discrete-faithful representation) of a hyperbolic group into a higher rank real semisimple Lie
group G is Anosov. A negative answer to the previous questions is known when G “ SL˘
5 pRq
[12, Ex. 8.5], G “ SLnpCq, n ě 30 [31, Cor. 1.2] and G “ SLnpRq for n ě 756 [31, Thm. 1.1] (see
also Remark 4.3). A positive answer to [25, Q.5] was given in [9] when G “ SL2pRqr ˆSL2pCqs,
r ` s ě 1 and G “ SL3pRq for reducible representations of non-elementary free groups.
In the present paper, we construct examples of robust quasi-isometric embeddings of virtu-
ally free groups which fail to be limits of Anosov representations. The first examples of Zariski
dense, quasi-isometric embeddings of (non-elementary) free groups into real semisimple Lie
groups, which are not limits of Anosov representations, were constructed by Carvajales–Lessa–
Potrie in [9, Thm. 1.5]. Such examples are limits of representations of free groups whose images
contain unipotent elements, and hence they are not robust quasi-isometric embeddings. Other

subset of j-Anosov representations of Γ into GLnpkq (see also Definition 2.1). A representation

a negative answer to the nonarchimedean analogue of [9, Prob. 1.1].
Theorem 1.1. Let k be a nonarchimedean local field and integers n ě 3, m ě 2. There exists
a robust quasi-isometric embedding of the free group of rank m into GLnpkq which is not a
limit of Anosov representations into GLnpkq.
Theorem 1.1 shows that nonarchimedean analogues of Sullivan’s stability theorem [28] and
of the density theorem of Kleinian groups [5, 6, 23, 24] fail for Anosov representations of free
groups into GLnpkq, n ě 3. To our knowledge, these are the first examples of robustly discrete
free subgroups of reductive k-groups that fail to be Anosov. The examples of Theorem 1.1
are constructed as the ping-pong combination of a non-proximal3 diagonal matrix with an
Anosov free subgroup of rank m ´ 1, following the point of view of the construction in [9,
Thm. 1.5] in dimension 3. For the proof of robustness of the examples of Theorem 1.1 we
crucially use the ultrametric property of the nonarchimedean absolute value on k.
In addition, we also exhibit examples of robust quasi-isometric embeddings of virtually free
groups into real and complex general linear groups which fail to be Anosov.
Theorem 1.2. Let d ě 2 be an even integer. There exists a virtually free group Γ, depending
only on d, with the property: for every n ě d ` 1, there is a representation ψn : Γ Ñ GLnpRq
and an open neighborhood On of ψn in HompΓ, GLnpCqq satisfying the following:
(1) Every representation in On is a quasi-isometric embedding and non-locally rigid;
(2) On X Anosovj pΓ, GLnpCqq is empty for any j “ 1, . . . , d ´ 1; and
(3) On X Anosovr pΓ, GLnpRqq is empty for any r “ 1, . . . , n ´ 1.
If n P td`1, . . . , 2d´1u the representation ψn in Theorem 1.2 is not in the closure of Anosov
representations of Γ into GLnpCq. The representation ψn, as well as any representation in the
open set On, from Theorem 1.2 is obtained as a ping-pong combination of a virtually cyclic
group, with non-trivial torsion, with infinite cyclic groups. As a consequence, this provides
more flexible (in terms of the dimension of the space of deformations) examples of non-Anosov
robust quasi-isometric embeddings into any dimension ě 3. While the proof of the robustness
of the examples in [31] relies crucially on Corlette’s Archimedean superrigidity [11], the proof
of Theorem 1.2 uses the local rigidity of the finite subgroups of Γ. We refer to Example 6.2
for an explicit non-Anosov robust quasi-isometric embedding of the free product pZ ˆ D8q ˚ Z
into GL3pCq, where D8 is the dihedral group of order 8.
Anosov representations of semigroups into a real semisimple Lie group were introduced by
Kassel and Potrie in [21, §5]. We exhibit examples of robust quasi-isometric embeddings of
free semigroups which are not Anosov, thus providing a negative answer to an analogue of [9,
Prob. 1.1] for free semigroups. We denote by Z` the semigroup of natural numbers equipped
with multiplication.
