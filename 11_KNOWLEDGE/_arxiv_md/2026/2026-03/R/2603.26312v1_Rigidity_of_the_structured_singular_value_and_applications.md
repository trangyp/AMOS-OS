ABSTRACT. The structured singular value µE for a linear subspace E of Mn(C) is defined by
µE(A) = 1/inf{∥X∥: X ∈E, det(In −AX) = 0}
(A ∈Mn(C)),
and µE(A) = 0 if there is no X ∈E with det(In −AX) = 0. It is well-known that µE(A) coincides
with the spectral radius r(A) when E = {cIn : c ∈C} and µE(A) = ∥A∥when E = Mn(C), for
all A ∈Mn(C). Also, for any linear subspace E satisfying {cIn : c ∈C} ⊆E ⊆Mn(C), we have
r(A) ≤µE(A) ≤∥A∥. We prove that if E = {cIn : c ∈C} and F is any linear subspace of Mn(C)
containing E, then µE = µF if and only if E = F. We prove the exact same rigidity theorem for the
linear subspace consisting of the diagonal matrices of order n. On the contrary, when E = Mn(C),
we show that there is a proper subspace F of Mn(C), viz. the space of symmetric matrices such that
µE = µF = operator norm. Further, we characterize all linear subspaces F ⊆Mn(C) such that µF
coincides with the operator norm. Next, we show that in general there is no subspace E of Mn(C)
such that µE = the numerical radius, not even for M2(C). Then, we prove that except for the spectral
radius and operator norm, µE cannot be a convex combination of the spectral radius, numerical
radius and operator norm for any E ⊆M2(C). The unit ball of the function µE induces various
domains in Cd depending on E, e.g., the symmetrized bidisc G2, the tetrablock E, the pentablock
P and the hexablock H. The domains G2 and E correspond to the linear subspaces of scalar and
diagonal matrices in M2(C) respectively, whereas P and H arise from certain linear subspaces of
upper triangular matrices in M2(C). For each of these linear subspaces, we establish the rigidity of
the corresponding structured singular value.
1. INTRODUCTION
The structured singular value is one of the central notions in the theory of robust control, where it
plays a key role in analyzing the stability of systems under structured uncertainties. Given a linear
subspace E of the space of complex matrices Mn(C), the structured singular value µE : Mn(C) →R
is defined as
µE(A) :=

A ∈Mn(C).

In case there is no X ∈E with det(In −AX) = 0, µE(A) is defined to be equal to 0. The linear
subspace E is referred to as the ‘structure’. The structured singular value can be defined in a more
general setting of m×n matrices, e.g., see the last section of [1]. However, we restrict our attention
to Mn(C) in this article. It is well-known that for all A ∈Mn(C), µE(A) = r(A) when E is the space
of scalar matrices {cIn : c ∈C} and µE(A) = ∥A∥when E = Mn(C). Here, r(A), ∥A∥are the
spectral radius and operator norm of a matrix A respectively and In is the identity matrix in Mn(C).

any A ∈Mn(C). See [27] for proofs to these results. Most of the classical literature on structured
singular value (e.g., see [18, 19, 27] and the references therein), especially in robust control theory,
focuses on the structure E comprising of block diagonal matrices whose diagonal blocks are either

2. RIGIDITY RESULTS FOR CLASSICAL STRUCTURED SUBSPACES
The main aim of this section is to establish the rigidity of structured singular value µE when
E ⊆Mn(C) is the subspace of scalar matrices or diagonal matrices, which is to say that for linear
subspaces E,E′ of Mn(C) with Escalar ⊆E,E′, we prove that
(1) µE(A) = r(A) for all A ∈Mn(C) if and only if E = Escalar ;
(2) µE′(A) = µEdiag(A) for all A ∈Mn(C) if and only if E′ = Ediag.
Also, following the discussion of the previous section on the domains G2,E,P and H, we prove the
rigidity of the associated structured singular values r(·),µtetra,µpenta, and µhexa, respectively. More
precisely, we study the following problems: to classify all linear subspaces E ⊆M2(C) containing
the identity matrix for which, for every A ∈M2(C), one of the following holds:
µE(A) = r(A),
µE(A) = µtetra(A),
µE(A) = µpenta(A),
or
µE(A) = µhexa(A).
We provide a complete solution to these problems and show that each one admits a unique solution.
