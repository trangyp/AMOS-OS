NEW EXAMPLES OF NON-UNIQUE ENHANCEMENTS FOR
TRIANGULATED CATEGORIES
A. RIZZARDO, J. SYMONS, AND M. VAN DEN BERGH
Abstract. We present a general procedure for constructing triangulated cate-
gories, linear over a field, with distinct enhancements. Some of our examples
can be equipped with a (non-degenerate) t-structure, thereby showing that the
existence of a t-structure does not imply uniqueness of enhancements, whether
in the strong or weak sense (depending on the example).

categories (even linear over a field) for which uniqueness fails [RV19]. The examples
in [RV19] are derived categories of graded fields and are, in particular, semi-simple.
The main point of this paper is to generalise the construction in [RV19] in such a

non-degenerate t-structure (in contrast to derived categories), which may suggest
that it is the presence of such a t-structure that causes enhancements to be unique.
The more general construction developed in the current paper, however, easily yields
triangulated categories equipped with a non-degenerate t-structure that admit more
than one enhancement.

2.5. Enhancements. We follow [LO10, Definition 2.1] except that we place our-
selves in the A∞-context throughout. This is justified by the results in [COS19].
For an introduction to the required A∞-notions, see § 3.
An A∞-enhancement (or simply enhancement) of a triangulated category T is
a pair (C,E), where C is a pretriangulated A∞-category and E ∶H0(C) →T is an
equivalence of triangulated categories. Two enhancements (C,E) and (C′,E′) are
said to be equivalent if there is an A∞-functor F ∶C →C′ such that H0(F) is an
equivalence. If, in addition, we can choose F in such a way that there exists an
isomorphism of exact functors E ≅E′ ○H0(F), then we say that (C,E) and (C′,E′)
are strongly equivalent.
As in [LO10, Definition 2.2], an enhancement (C,E) is (strongly) unique if it is
(strongly) equivalent to any other enhancement.
We note that all triangulated categories that occur “in nature” come with a
standard enhancement.
E.g.
if A is an A∞-category, then C∞(A) provides a
standard enhancement for D(A). If A is a dg-category, then we can consider both
a standard dg-enhancement of the derived category, like h-proj(A) or semi-free(A),
and C∞(A). The two are strongly equivalent by [Pos11, Bottom of page 93].

4. Perfect dimension for A∞-categories
4.1. Perfect dimension. It will be convenient to make the following definition.
Definition 4.1. An A∞-category A has perfect dimension ≤q if the inclusion
⟨A⟩q+1 ↪Perf(A)
is an equivalence, where
●⟨A⟩q+1 (see [BV03, §2.2]) is the full subcategory of D(A) spanned by objects

●Perf(A) (see [BV03, §2.2]) is the category of perfect A-modules.
Remark 4.2. Note that the perfect dimension is not a Morita invariant of A since
it depends on the choice of the specific generator A of Perf(A).
Lemma 4.3. If A has perfect dimension ≤q, then the totalization functor, i.e. the
