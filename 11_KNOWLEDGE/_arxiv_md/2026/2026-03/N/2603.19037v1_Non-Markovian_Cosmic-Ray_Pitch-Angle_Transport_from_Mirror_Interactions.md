ABSTRACT
Cosmic-ray pitch-angle transport in magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence is governed by the
interplay between magnetic mirroring and gyroresonant scattering. We develop a guiding-center (GC)
Langevin model with explicit mirror drift and gyroresonant diffusion to describe the pitch angle evo-
lution. This model accurately captures our test-particle simulation results in three-dimensional MHD
turbulence, driven both solenoidally and compressively. We find that magnetic mirroring can drive
anomalous pitch-angle diffusion at large pitch angles (including 90◦) with non-Markovian memory ef-
fects, which arises from trapping of particles in magnetic wells. Gyroresonant scattering controls the
escape rate from these wells. Across MA, large-pitch-angle particles are jointly regulated by mirror
trapping and gyroresonant escape, exhibiting a transition from anomalous to normal diffusive pitch-
angle transport as scattering strengthens, whereas small-pitch-angle particles remain gyroresonance-
dominated and diffusive throughout.
The pitch angle transport is found to be dominated by the
compressible perturbations with marginal influence from Alfv´en modes. In compressible turbulence
with realistic damping accounted for, transit time damping (TTD) treatment fully recovers mirror
interactions.
1. INTRODUCTION
Cosmic-ray (CR) transport in magnetized turbulence
underpins particle acceleration and nonthermal emission
across astrophysical and space-plasma environments. In
practice, CR propagation is often described as a diffusive
process: it is assumed that pitch-angle scattering rapidly
decorrelates particle directions, allowing transport to be
modeled by a Markovian diffusion equation with well-
defined coefficients(J. R. Jokipii 1966; R. Schlickeiser
2002). However, when scattering is intermittent or in-
sufficient, particle trajectories retain memory of the un-
derlying magnetic structure, rendering a diffusive de-
scription invalid(G. Zimbardo & S. Perri 2020; S. Maiti
et al. 2022; S. Zhao et al. 2025; J. L¨ubke et al. 2025).
A long-standing warning sign is the “90◦problem”.
Quasilinear theory (QLT) provides a baseline descrip-
tion of resonant wave-particle interactions, yet its δ-
function resonance yields a zero diffusion coefficient
of pitch angles (Dµµ = 0 for µ ≃0) (J. R. Jokipii
1966; R. Kulsrud & W. P. Pearce 1969; G. Qin & A.
Shalchi 2014).
As a result, QLT-based closures may
mischaracterize confinement in regimes where near-90◦

Despite broad consensus on the importance of compres-

ing rates within a Markovian transport picture.
In this work we demonstrate that mirror structures
can drive a distinct regime, namely, mirror-induced
anomalous diffusion that lies beyond both QLT and its
nonlinear extensions.

3. RESULTS
3.1. Separating mirror interactions from gyroresonance
We characterize pitch-angle transport using the en-
semble spread of the pitch-angle cosine, ⟨|∆µ(t)|⟩=
⟨|µ(t + t0) −µ(t0)|⟩, where t0 is the reference start time
(by default t0 = 0; µ0 ≡µ(0)). We quantify its scaling
⟨∆µ⟩∝tα. Here α = 0.5 corresponds to normal diffu-
sion, α < 0.5 to subdiffusion, and α > 0.5 to superdif-
fusion (with α = 1 indicating ballistic growth). Time is
normalized to the gyroperiod tg. To isolate the physical
channels that drive µ evolution, we exploit the orthogo-
nality of Alfv´enic and compressible perturbations in k-
space and decompose the turbulence into Alfv´enic (A)
and compressible (C) components(J. Cho & A. Lazarian
2002, 2003).
In the C component, mirror interactions and gyrores-
onance coexist. Mirror is controlled by ∇|B| and is in-
herently broadband across the inertial range, whereas
gyroresonance is concentrated near cyclotron-resonant
scales. Notably, mirror interactions do not rely on a spe-
cific resonant scale and exhibit no intrinsic rigidity de-
pendence (Eq.1), so varying the Larmor radius primar-
ily affects gyroresonant scattering. To construct mirror-
dominated reference cases, we suppress gyroresonance
by artificially reducing the Lamor radius to a sufficiently
small value (e.g. rL = 1 lgrid), thereby shifting the reso-
nant scale into the dissipation range. Comparing results
from the full turbulence, the mode-decomposed fields
and these gyroresonace-suppressed runs allows us to dis-
entangle deterministic mirror confinement from stochas-
tic resonant decorrelation.
In the A component, the perturbations are predomi-
nantly transverse to the mean field B0. As a result, the
mirror-relevant gradients ∇|B| enter only at higher or-
der. Mirror/TTD effects therefore vanish at leading or-
der and the A-modes contribution to pitch-angle scatter-
ing is dominated by gyroresonance. However, gyroreso-
nant scattering by Alfv´enic turbulence is inefficient near
µ ≃0. Indeed, we see that in large pitch angle cases
(small µ0) the µ evolution resulted from the C compo-
nent is nearly identical to that from the full turbulence
(Fig. 1a,b).5 This implies that the A component con-
tributes negligibly in this regime. This behavior is fur-
ther strengthened by the scale-dependent anisotropy of
Alfv´enic turbulence, which suppresses power at the par-
allel wavenumbers k∥required for the resonance with
particles in realistic astrophysical environment(P. Gol-
dreich & S. Sridhar 1995; H. Yan & A. Lazarian 2002,
2004).
Another question is whether field-line curvature or
Richardson superdiffusion of A modes could provide an
additional contribution (A. Lazarian & H. Yan 2014; S.
Maiti et al. 2022). To assess the combined effect of field-
line curvature and mirror interactions, we performed a
simulation using the solenoidally driven datacube with
rL = 1 lgrid. We see the C modes and whole datacube
result in nearly indentical ⟨|∆µ|⟩behaviour (Fig. 2a,b),
indicating that A modes field line curvatures are also
negligible.

Memory effects are probed directly by “reset” exper-

changed, and then re-measure the increment statistics
∆µ = µ(∆t + tc) −µ0. For µ0 = 0, the PDF depends
strongly on tc at early times (Fig. 3e) and relaxes only
after long evolution (tc ∼103 tg). This behavior reflects
mirror-induced spatial focusing: particles initially dis-

gyroresonant scattering to restore spatial randomness.
In contrast, for µ0 = 0.8, the PDFs collapse rapidly for
different tc (Fig. 3f), demonstrating fast decorrelation.
Together, these diagnostics identify a non-Markovian,
anomalous diffusion regime at low MA.

4. DISCUSSION
4.1. Negligence of damping effects
In MHD turbulence generated by Athena++, artifi-
cial isotropic viscosity is added at small-scale dissipa-
tion ranges to maintain numerical stability and to pre-
vent energy pile up at large k (J. M. Stone et al. 2020).
In real plasma environment, multiple physical damping
channels operate and their relative importance depends
on both wavelength scale and plasma parameters. Be-
cause interstellar phases differ in density, ionization frac-
tion, and plasma β, they have different dominant damp-
ing mechanisms (H. Yan & A. Lazarian 2004; H. Yan
et al. 2004).
Low density rarefied media such as the
solar wind and Galactic halo tend to be governed by
collisionless damping. In warm ionized or neutral me-
dia, collisional viscosity and thermal conduction gain
importance.
In partially ionized phases, ion–neutral
friction preferentially damps currents transverse to B.
In all cases, damping preferentially suppresses large-k
power.
Moreover, for compressive (mostly fast-mode)
fluctuations in low-β plasmas, both theory and recent
simulations and observations show stronger damping at
oblique/quasi-perpendicular angles than in quasi-slab
geometries (V. Petrosian et al. 2006; H. Yan & A. Lazar-
ian 2008; S. Zhao et al. 2024; C. Hou et al. 2025; S. Zhao
et al. 2026). Including physical damping therefore fur-
ther suppresses the high-k portion of the spectrum and
quasi-perpendicular components, and gradually restores
the equivalence between TTD and mirror interactions in
the diffusive limit.
4.2. Anisotropic particle distribution and
gyroresonance instability
In this paper, the possible kinetic feedback is not in-
cluded. In the case of low MA turbulence, for instance,
the insufficient scattering is bound to drive anisotropy
in the particle distribution.
The pressure anisotropy,
in turn, can excite gyroresonance instability, which can
increase the scattering rate, in turn (H. Yan & A. Lazar-
ian 2011; O. Lebiga et al. 2018). As the result, particle
transport can restore normal diffusion through the self-
excited Alfven waves (see R. M. Kulsrud 2005; H. Yan &
A. Lazarian 2012; A. M. Bykov et al. 2013). The inter-
play between mirror interaction and the gyroresonance
counting for the feedback will be studied elsewhere.
5. SUMMARY
In this work, we showed that mirror structures in
turbulent magnetic fields drive non-Markovian pitch-
angle diffusion at large pitch angles, which influences
the transport picture of CRs. By combining full-orbit
test-particle simulations in 3D MHD turbulence with a
guiding-center (GC) Langevin model, we disentangle the
roles of gyroresonant scattering and mirror interactions.
We find that mirror interactions act multiscale: they
are local in real space, generate non-Markovian memory
through trapping in mirror potential wells, and statisti-
cally produce anomalous pitch angle transport. At large
Alfv´enic Mach number MA, gyroresonant kicks become
strong enough to let particles hop across many local-
ized mirror structures, causing decoherence and restor-
ing to normal diffusion. The coupling between these two
processes governs the transport of particles. Our main
results can be summarized as follows:
1. Mirror structures, generated primarily by com-
pressive modes (and by pseudo-Alfv´en fluctuations
in the incompressible limit), introduce a determin-
istic drift in µ which naturally breaks the QLT
prediction of pitch-angle scattering and bring the
particles out of the 90◦barrier.
2. Gyroresonant scattering modulates the escape
from mirror wells and is essential for establish-
ing normal diffusion. The higher the Alfv´en Mach
number MA is, the faster normal diffusion restores.
3. The mirror effect leads to non-Markovian, anoma-
lous pitch-angle diffusion with memory, character-
ized by leptokurtic distributions of pitch angle in-
crement and heavy-tailed PDF of waiting time.
4. The pitch angle transport is dominated by the
interaction with compressible fluctuations.

Our results suggest that CR propagation experiences

medium, MA may vary over a wide range. Consequently,
the balance between compressive mirror trapping and
