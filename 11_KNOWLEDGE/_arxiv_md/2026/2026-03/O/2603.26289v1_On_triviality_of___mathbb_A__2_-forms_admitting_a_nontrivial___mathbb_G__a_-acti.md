On triviality of A2-forms admitting a nontrivial
Ga-action
Debojyoti Saha
Theoretical Statistics and Mathematics Unit, Indian Statistical Institute,
203 B.T.Road, Kolkata-700108, India
e-mail : debojyotisaha19@gmail.com
Abstract. T. Kambayashi had shown that A2-forms over separable field exten-
sions are necessarily polynomial rings. However, there exist inseparable A2-forms
which are not necessarily polynomial rings. In this paper, we give a structure the-
orem for A2-forms over arbitrary field extensions admitting a nontrivial Ga-action.
From this structure theorem we derive some conditions under which an A2-form
becomes trivial. In particular, we prove that over a field k, a factorial A2-form
having a k-rational point and a non-trivial Ga-action is trivial and we also give
examples demonstrating that none of these hypotheses can be discarded. As a
consequence of the structure theorem, we obtain a generalization of the Zariski
Cancellation Theorem for the affine plane over an arbitrary field.
Keywords.
A2-form, Exponential Map (Ga-action), Makar-Limanov Invariant, Cancellation.
2020 MSC. Primary: 14R10, 13A50; Secondary: 13B25.

Introduction
All rings in this article are assumed to be commutative and unital. For an algebra B over a ring R,
we write “B = R[n]” for some n ≥1 if B = R[t1, t2, ..., tn] for some t1, t2, ..., tn in B algebraically
independent over R, i.e, if B is isomorphic to a polynomial ring in n indeterminates over R.
Let k be any field. A k-algebra A is said to be an An-form with respect to a field extension L |k
if A ⊗k L = L[n]. T. Kambayashi had shown [9, Theorem 3] that every separable A2-form over k is
trivial. However, for inseparable field extensions, there exist examples of nontrivial An-forms even
for n = 1 ([2], [10]).
In this paper we investigate the structure of A2-forms that admit a nontrivial Ga-action. A k-
algebra admitting nontrivial Ga-actions is called non-rigid, otherwise it is called rigid. In particular
k[2] is non-rigid. We establish the following structure theorem for nonrigid A2-forms (Theorem 3.1).

Theorem A. Let A be a non-rigid A2-form with respect to a field extension L|k. Then A = B[1],
where B is an A1-form.
The following result on A1-forms follows from a theorem of T. Asanuma [2, Theorem 4.8]. A self
contained proof can be found in [6, Corollary 4], where P. Das proves a more general result quoted
in Remark 3.10 below.
Theorem 1.1. Let k be any field. Let A be an A1-form with respect to a field extension L|k. Suppose
the following hold:
(i) A is a U.F.D
(ii) A has a k-rational point.
Then A = k[1].
As an application of Theorem A we deduce the following analogue of Theorem 1.1 for A2-forms
(Theorem 3.5).
Theorem B. Let A be a non-rigid factorial A2-form over a field k with respect to a field extension
L |k having a k-rational point. Then A = k[2].
We also give examples demonstrating that none of the assumptions in Theorem B can be dis-
carded; in particular, the hypotheses in the theorem are minimal.
Recall that a k-algebra A is said to be cancellative if for any k-algebra B, A[1] ∼=k B[1] ⇒A ∼=k B.
The Zariski Cancellation Theorem for affine plane states that when A = k[2], A is cancellative. To
be more precise, we have the following:
Theorem 1.2 (Zariski Cancellation Theorem). Let k be any field. Suppose B is any k-algebra such
that B[1] ∼=k k[3]. Then B ∼=k k[2].
Theorem 1.2 was established by Fujita, Miyanishi and Sugie ([7], [11]) in characteristic 0, by
Russell [12] over perfect fields and later by Bhatwadekar-Gupta [3] over arbitrary fields.
As an
application of Theorem A, we prove the following generalization (Theorem 3.11).
Theorem C. Let A be an A2-form over a field k with respect to a field extension L|k. Let B be a
k-algebra such that A[1] ∼=k B[1]. Then A ∼=k B.

Preliminaries
Let A be an affine k-domain. We first recall the definition of an exponential map, the equivalent
notion of a Ga-action, on A.
Definition 2.1. Let ϕ : A −→A[1] be a k-algebra homomorphism. For any indeterminate U over A,
let us denote the map ϕ : A −→A[U] by the notation ϕU. The map ϕ is said to be an exponential
map on A if:
(i) ϵ0 ◦ϕU is identity on A, where ϵ0 : A[U] −→A is the evaluation map at U = 0.

Given an exponential map ϕ : A −→A[U], define, for each n ≥0 , k-linear functions ϕn : A −→A
by ϕn(a) = the coefficient of Un in the polynomial ϕ(a). For each a ∈A denote degU(ϕ(a)) by

then Aϕ is also a U.F.D.

Theorem 2.4. Let k be any field. Then any one dimensional normal k-subalgebra A of a polynomial

Theorem 2.5. Let k be any field. Let A, B be k-domains of transcendence degree 1 over k such that
A[n] = B[n] for some n ≥1. Then A = B if A is not a polynomial ring.

Lemma 2.6. For an algebraically closed field k, the ring of invariants of any nontrivial exponential
map ϕ on k[X, Y ](= k[2]) is k[f] = k[1] for some f ∈k[X, Y ] such that k[X, Y ] = k[f][1].

Corollary 3.2. Let k be a field and A be a non-rigid A2-form with respect to some field extension
L|k. If A ⊂k[n] for some n ≥2, then A = k[2].
Proof. By Theorem 3.1, A = B[1], where B is an A1-form with respect to L|k. Now as B ⊆A ⊆k[n],
by Theorem 2.4, we get B = k[1]. Hence A = k[2].
Corollary 3.3. Let A be an A2-form over k. Then the following are equivalent:
(i) ML(A)=k.
(ii) A = k[2].
Proof. (ii) ⇒(i) : This is trivial.
(i) ⇒(ii) : As ML(A) = k, A is nonrigid. By Theorem 3.1, A = B[1] for some k-subalgebra
B of A. If B were rigid, then, by Theorem 2.3, ML(A) = B ̸= k. Thus B is also nonrigid and
as k = ML(A) is inert in A, it is algebraically closed in A, it follows from [5, Lemma 2.2 (d)] that
B = k[1]. Hence A = k[2].
Corollary 3.4. Let k be a field of characteristic p > 2. Let A be a non-rigid A2-form with respect
to k|k, where k is the algebraic closure of k. Then there exist indeterminates X, Y over L such that
A ⊗k L = L[X, Y ] and
A = k[Xpe, α2β, αβλ][Y ]
for some e ≥0 and α, β ∈k1/pe[X] and λ = (pe + 1)/2.
Proof. Follows from Theorem 3.1 and [2, Theorem 8.1].
We now deduce Theorem B.
Theorem 3.5. Let A be an A2-form over a field k with respect to any field extension L |k. Suppose
the following hold
(i) A is a U.F.D
(ii) A has a k-rational point (or equivalently A has a retraction to k) and
(iii) A is non-rigid.
Then A = k[2].
Proof. By Theorem 3.1, we have that A = B[1] for some A1-form B. The rest follows from Theorem

Remark 3.10. Let k be a field. Let R be a k-algebra and A an R-algebra such that A is a U.F.D
with a retraction to R. In [6], P. Das had shown that if A is an A1-form over R with respect to L|k,
i.e, if A ⊗k L = (R ⊗k L)[1] for some field extension L|k, then A = R[1]. Example 3.9 shows that even
when R is a P.I.D like k[1], an analogous result for Theorem 3.5, does not hold for A2-forms over R,
i.e, Theorem 3.5 does not extend to A2-forms over P.I.D’s.
