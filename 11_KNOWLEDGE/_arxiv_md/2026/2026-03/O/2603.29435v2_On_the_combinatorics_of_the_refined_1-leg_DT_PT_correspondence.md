DAVIDE ACCADIA, DANILO LEWA ´NSKI, SERGEJ MONAVARI
ABSTRACT. We provide a new proof of a result of Bessenrodt on the relation among the generat-
ing series of reversed plane partitions and skew plane partitions, motivated by the geometric
DT/PT wallcrossing formula for local curves recently proved by the third author. This also
recovers a result of Sagan.
We moreover establish various new closed formulas for the weighted enumeration of reversed
and skew plane partitions, proving a result dual to a theorem by Gansner, we find a new identity
on the generating series counting internal and external hooks of a given Young diagram, and
we combine the latter with Bessenrodt’s theorem. Finally, we interpret our results as identities
in the Fock space via the bosonic/fermionic formalism.

1. INTRODUCTION
1.1. Overview. The study of plane partitions and their various generalisations is a central
theme in combinatorics, with deep connections to representation theory and enumerative
geometry, in particular for enumerative problems concerning Hilbert schemes, see e.g. [8, 9, 17].
Motivated by the work of the third author on refined Donaldson-Thomas invariants of local
curves and topological string partition functions [16], we offer a new perspective on classical
results of Bessenrodt [2] and Gansner [5], which relate the enumeration problems of reverse
plane partitions and skew plane partitions.
Historically, Gansner’s work [5] refined the algorithm of Hillman and Grassl [10] describing
the correspondence between hook-lengths and reverse plane partitions.

1.2. Bessenrodt’s result and thin partitions. Let λ be a Young diagram. Denote by H(λ) the
set of internal hooks of λ, and by H′(λ) the set of external hooks of λ. We remark that an
internal (resp. external) hook of λ is equivalent to a □∈λ (resp. □/∈λ) where the arm and
leg lengths of the hooks (i.e. their hook type) are uniquely determined by the boundary of λ.
Bessenrodt [2] established the following bijection.
Theorem 1.1 ([2, Thm. 3.2]). Let λ be a Young diagram. There is a bijection of sets
H′(λ) ←→H′(∅) ∪H(λ),
which preserves hook types.
Bessenrodt’s original strategy for proving Theorem 1.1 is to carefully keeping track of the
relation between addable and removable hooks in λ of given hook and arm length. The proof
exploits the representation of partitions as Maya diagrams and it admits a natural interpretation
in terms of bosonic operators in the Fock space (cf. also [6]).
Our main result is an alternative proof of Theorem 1.1 for the case of thin partitions (cf. The-
orem 3.5), a class of partitions which we introduce in Definition 3.1. Our argument is based
on a certain subdivision of the Young diagram, in which the constituent rectangles, called
tectonic plates, are shifted in a careful and controlled way, that we call tectonic movement. This
tectonic movement preserves hook types and makes the underlying combinatorial structure
transparent, allowing the desired equality to emerge naturally.

1.5. Relations to the DT / PT correspondence. The enumeration problem of reverse and skew
plane partitions naturally arises in the context of Donaldson-Thomas (DT) and Pandharipande-
Thomas (PT) theory of local curves [7, 15, 16] and of surfaces [4]. In fact, as the third author
showed in [15, 16], the generating series of topological Euler characteristics of Hilbert schemes
(the DT side) and the moduli spaces of stable pairs (the PT side) of a local curve are controlled by
the generating series of skew and reverse plane partitions, respectively. From this perspective,
the DT/PT wallcrossing formula [16, Thm. 1.9] is the geometric counterpart of the combinato-
rial bijection originally proved by Sagan [19] (see also [12]). Under this dictionary, Theorem 1.1
is the refined combinatorial counterpart of the refined DT/PT wallcrossing formula proved in
[16], defined by virtual integration on the corresponding moduli space. See also the recent
works [13, 14] for the refined DT/PT correspondences for general Calabi-Yau threefolds, and
[1, 3, 11] for their relation with the refined topological vertex.
