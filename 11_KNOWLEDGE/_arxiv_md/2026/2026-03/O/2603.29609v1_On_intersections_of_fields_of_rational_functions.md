ON INTERSECTIONS OF FIELDS OF RATIONAL FUNCTIONS
FEDOR PAKOVICH
Abstract. Let X and Y be rational functions of degree at least two with
complex coefficients such that C(X, Y ) = C(z).
We study the problem of
determining when the field extension [C(z) : C(X) ∩C(Y )] is finite and attains
the minimal possible degree deg X ·deg Y . We give a complete characterization
in the case where X is a Galois covering. We also establish several related
results concerning the functional equation A◦X = Y ◦B in rational functions,
in the case where one of the functions involved is a Galois covering. Finally,
we consider an analogous problem for holomorphic maps between compact
Riemann surfaces.
1. Introduction
This paper is concerned with the following problem: given rational functions X
and Y of degree at least two with complex coefficients such that
(1)
C(X, Y ) = C(z),
under what conditions
(2)
C(X) ∩C(Y ) ̸= C ?
By the L¨uroth theorem, this condition is equivalent to the existence of a non-
constant rational function H such that
(3)
C(X) ∩C(Y ) = C(H),
which in turn is equivalent to the existence of non-constant rational functions A
and B satisfying
(4)
A ◦X = B ◦Y.
Thus, the problem under consideration can be viewed as a special case of the more
general problem of describing solutions of (4) in rational functions.
The last problem is connected with many areas of mathematics and has been
studied intensively (see, e.g., the recent paper [30] and the references therein).
Nevertheless, it remains largely open. Furthermore, known approaches to equation
(4) focus on the study of the “separated variable” curves
(5)
A(x) −B(y) = 0,
where A and B are rational functions, and on conditions ensuring that such a curve
has a factor of genus zero. However, these conditions usually do not directly yield
any information about the rational functions X and Y parametrizing (5) or its
components.
Consequently, very little is known about general conditions under
which (2) holds, except in the case when both X and Y are polynomials.

(4) was solved under the assumption that A and B are continuous functions on
CP1. Finally, in [29], a description of the solutions of (4) was obtained in the case
where A, B, and X, Y are formal power series of order at least two, that is, series
of the form P
n≥2 cnzn.
For non-polynomial rational functions X and Y , condition (2) can sometimes be
decided in the presence of symmetries. Specifically, let GX denote the finite group
of deck transformations Aut(CP1, X) associated with X. Then (2) implies that
⟨GX, GY ⟩is a subgroup of GH, and hence finite. Thus, finiteness of ⟨GX, GY ⟩is a
necessary condition for (2). Moreover, if both X and Y are Galois coverings, then
(3) holds if and only if ⟨GX, GY ⟩is finite, in which case (2) holds for some Galois
covering H with GH = ⟨GX, GY ⟩. In particular, it applies to the case where X and
Y are of degree two, since any rational function of degree two is a Galois covering.
Considering only rational functions of degree two, we already see that (2) does
not generally imply (8) in the non-polynomial case.
Indeed, since the dihedral
group of any order can be generated by two involutions, the left-hand side of (8)
can be arbitrarily large, even for X and Y of degree two. Although these examples
are somewhat specialized, it is unclear whether, for X and Y satisfying (1) and (2),
there exist any general upper bounds for the degree [C(z) : C(X) ∩C(Y )]. Thus,
a natural first step toward the general problem is to focus on the case in which
this quantity attains its minimal possible value, or equivalently, when (3) holds for
some rational function H of degree deg X · deg Y .

Theorem 1.1. Let X and Y be rational functions of degree at least two satisfying
C(X, Y ) = C(z). Assume that X is a Galois covering. Then the equality
[C(z) : C(X) ∩C(Y )] = deg X · deg Y
holds if and only if Y admits a factorization Y = U◦V , where V is a GX-equivariant
rational function and U is a rational Galois covering such that the group ⟨GX, GU⟩
is finite of order deg X · deg U, with GX ∩GU = {e}.
Notice that in the case where Y is indecomposable, that is, cannot be represented
as a composition of two rational functions of degree at least two, Theorem 1.1 im-
plies that Y is either GX-equivariant or a Galois covering. However, once condi-
tion (8) is weakened to (2), the latter conclusion no longer holds in general (see
Section 3.3).
If condition (2) holds for rational functions X and Y , and
X = X1 ◦X2 ◦· · · ◦Xl,
Y = Y1 ◦Y2 ◦· · · ◦Yk
are decompositions of X and Y into indecomposable rational functions of degree at
least two, then (2) remains true for the rightmost factors Xl and Yk. Consequently,
the case where X and Y are indecomposable, along with the case when (8) holds,
can be regarded as a particularly interesting case of the problem of describing
rational functions satisfying (1) and (2). On the other hand, it follows from the
classification of rational Galois coverings going back to Klein that such a covering
X is indecomposable if and only if it has the form X = µ1 ◦zp ◦µ2, where p is
prime and µ1, µ2 are M¨obius transformations.
For the Galois coverings X = zn, n ≥2, our second result provides a condi-
tion—distinct from (8)—which ensures that an indecomposable rational function
Y satisfying (1) and (2) is GX-equivariant. For such X, the group GX is cyclic, gen-
erated by z 7→εz, where ε is a primitive nth root of unity, and the GX-equivariance
of Y takes an explicit form that generalizes (6). Namely, Y is GX-equivariant if
and only if Y = σ ◦zsR(zn), where σ is a M¨obius transformation, R is a rational
function, and s ≥0 is an integer such that gcd(s, n) = 1.

that has a zero of order at least two at 0, and let n ≥2 be an integer. Then the
