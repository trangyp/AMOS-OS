Abstract
The center of distances of a metric space (X, d) is the set C(X) of all t ∈R+
for which the equation d(x, p) = t has a solution for each p ∈X. We prove the
inequality |C(X)| ≤1 + ⌊log2 n⌋for all finite ultrametric spaces (X, d) which
have exactly n points. It is also shown that for every integer n ≥1 there exists
a finite ultrametric space (Y, ρ) such that |Y | = n and |C(Y )| = 1+log2⌊n⌋.
Keywords: center of distances, diametrical graph, finite ultrametric space, labeled
rooted tree.
MSC Classification: 54E35
1 Introduction
The concept of the center of distances was introduced by Wojciech Bielaś, Szymon
Plewik and Marta Walczyńska in paper [5] as follows.
Definition 1.1 Let (X, d) be a metric space and let D(X) be the distance set of (X, d),
D(X) := {d(x, y) : x, y ∈X}.

The center of distances of (X, d) is a subset C(X) of the distance set D(X) defined as
C(X) := {t ∈D(X) : ∀p ∈X ∃x ∈X d(p, x) = t}.
(1)
This concept was used in [5] for a generalization of John von Neumann Theorem
on permutations of two sequences with the same set of cluster points [23]. The centers
of distances of ultrametric spaces generated by labeled trees are found in [15]. Some
interesting properties of the center of distances are proved in [1, 3, 4, 17, 19], but the
authors are not aware of any results describing the properties of these centers in the
case of arbitrary ultrametric spaces.
In the present paper we investigate the center of distances of finite ultrametric
spaces. The main purpose of the paper is to provide a solution to the following problem.
Problem 1.2 Let n be a positive integer number. Find the smallest integer M(n) satisfying
the inequality
|C(X)| ≤M(n)
for each finite ultrametric spaces (X, d) which have at most n points.
The paper is organized as follows. The next section contains some definitions and
facts from the theory of metric spaces and graph theory. Theorem 3.10 of Section 3
gives a solution of Problem 1.2 and it is the main result of the paper. In the final
Section 4 we formulate three conjectures connected with the center of distances of
finite ultrametric spaces.
2 Preliminaries
Let us start from the basic concept of metric space.
In what follows we will use the symbol R+ to denote the set [0, +∞).
Definition 2.1 Let X be a non-empty set. A metric on X is a symmetric function d: X ×
X →R+ satisfying the equality d(x, x) = 0 for each x ∈X, the inequality d(x, y) > 0 for all
distinct x, y ∈X, and the triangle inequality
d(x, y) ≤d(x, z) + d(z, y)
for all x, y, z ∈X.
A metric space (X, d) is called an ultrametric space if the strong triangle inequality
d(x, y) ≤max{d(x, z), d(z, y)}
holds for all x, y, z ∈X. In this case, the metric d is called an ultrametric on X.
Definition 2.2 Let (X, d) and (Y, ρ) be metric spaces. A mapping Φ: X →Y is said to be
an isometry if Φ is bijective and the equality
d(x, y) = ρ(Φ(x), Φ(y))

Using the concept of direct successor, we can define the notion of the level of the
