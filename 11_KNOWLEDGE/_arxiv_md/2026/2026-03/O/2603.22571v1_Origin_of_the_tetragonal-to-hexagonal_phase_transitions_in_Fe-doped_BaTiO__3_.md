Origin of the tetragonal-to-hexagonal phase transitions in Fe-doped BaTiO3
Zhiyuan Li, Ruiwen Xie, and Hongbin Zhang∗
Institute of Materials Science, Technische Universit¨at Darmstadt, 64287 Darmstadt, Germany
(Dated: March 25, 2026)
Based on detailed first-principles calculations, we investigate the tetragonal-to-hexagonal phase
transition in Fe-doped BaTiO3. Total energy calculations confirm a crossover from the tetragonal
to hexagonal phases around 4% Fe, in agreement with experimental observations, where compara-
tive calculations show that neither CaTiO3 nor SrTiO3 exhibits similar behavior under equivalent
substitution. Furthermore, three possible mechanisms are quantified: oxygen vacancies shift the
crossover concentration from ∼4% to ∼2% through charge compensation, Jahn-Teller distortions
impose a larger elastic penalty, both favoring tetragonal-to-hexagonal phase transitions; whereas
the tolerance factor is reduced in comparison with that of pristine BaTiO3 for reasonable Fe valence
states, disfavoring the occurrence of the hexagonal phases. Detailed analysis on the electronic struc-
ture reveals that the charge redistribution induced by oxygen vacancy is strongly orbital dependent
due to the local crystal structure distortions.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Structural phase transitions in ABO3 perovskite ox-
ides give rise to diverse functional properties, enabling
tunable ferroelectricity [1–4], piezoelectricity [3, 5], and
electro-optic responses [3, 5] through symmetry-breaking
distortions.
This underscores the critical potential of
tailoring phase transitions in perovskites for enabling
enhanced functionalities. For instance, BaTiO3 (BTO)
stands as an archetypal system for investigating such
structure-property relationships, distinguished by its rich
polymorphism and exceptional sensitivity to external
perturbations. Upon heating, BTO undergoes a defini-
tive structural evolution: rhombohedral (R3m) →or-
thorhombic (Amm2) →tetragonal (P4mm) →cubic
(Pm¯3m) at approximately 183 K, 278 K, and 403 K,
respectively, while the hexagonal structure (P63/mmc)
stabilizes only above 1733 K [6]. Strategically engineering
these phase transitions yields exceptional performance
metrics. For example, constructing a morphotropic phase
boundary (MPB) in (Ba,Ca)(Zr,Ti)O3 minimizes the po-
larization anisotropy energy, achieving a giant piezoelec-
tric coefficient (d33 ∼620 pC/N) comparable to lead-
based ceramics [7]. Similarly, stabilizing the tetragonal
symmetry via epitaxial strain in BTO thin films has en-
abled effective Pockels coefficients as high as 923 pm/V
for integrated photonics [8].
To tailor the desired phases with enhanced proper-
ties, three most common strategies are usually lever-
aged to manipulate the energetic landscape: epitaxial
strain, dynamic field excitation, and chemical substi-
tution.
Epitaxial strain engineering utilizes substrate-
induced lattice mismatch to stabilize non-equilibrium
phases [9, 10], e.g.
imparting a compressive strain of
∼1.7% in BTO thin films elevates the Curie tempera-
ture to nearly 500◦C and enhances the remanent polar-
ization to ∼70 µC/cm2, far exceeding bulk values [11].

Dynamic excitation offers transient access to metastable
structures, where optical pulses or intense terahertz elec-
tric fields can coherently drive soft phonon modes to
induce ferroelectricity in quantum paraelectrics on pi-
cosecond timescales [12, 13]. Complementing these phys-
ical stimuli that target metastable or transient states,
chemical substitution, distinguished by the concentration
regime into doping versus alloying (low vs. high concen-
tration of extrinsic contents), provides a distinct pathway
for intrinsic bulk phase control. This approach enables
the tailoring of phase stability through precise modifi-
cations of local structure, electronic configuration, and
defect chemistry [14–17]. For instance, isovalent alloying
(e.g., Sr2+ for Ba2+) is widely utilized to shift transi-
tion temperatures for tunable dielectrics [18], while alio-
valent doping/alloying causes more fundamental changes
depending on the possible charge compensation mecha-
nisms [19].
Among chemical substitution strategies, transition
metal (TM) doping in BTO has revealed complex and
concentration-dependent tailoring behaviors that remain
elusive. Morrison et al. [20] demonstrated that Mn, Co,
Ni, and Zn substitutions primarily modify the sequence
of temperature-driven phase transitions between conven-
tional perovskite polymorphs. For instance, Fe substi-
tution stabilizes the high-temperature hexagonal phase
(space group P63/mmc) at room temperature [20–22].
The hexagonal polymorph exhibits fundamentally differ-
ent coordination environments characterized by the co-
existence of corner-sharing and face-sharing octahedral
sites, the latter forming distinct M2O9 dimers. This con-
nectivity contrasts sharply with the exclusively corner-
sharing framework of the tetragonal phase, leading to
distinct electronic and magnetic properties [23, 24]. Sys-
tematic experimental investigations have identified crit-
ical concentration thresholds for hexagonal phase emer-
gence in Fe-substituted BTO. X-ray diffraction studies
reveal tetragonal-hexagonal phase coexistence beginning
at Fe concentrations of 2–4 at.%, with hexagonal frac-
tion progressively increasing to dominance (>90%) at
10 at.% [25, 26].

(5 at.%) reduces the hexagonal phase fraction from
98.8% to 18.6%, while even isovalent substitutions (Sr2+,
Ca2+) produce similar phase transitions despite main-
taining charge neutrality[31]. These findings imply Gold-
schmidt’s tolerance factor as a critical control parame-
ter, with small variations (∼0.3%) producing dispropor-
tionately large structural responses.
However, a com-
prehensive theoretical understanding of the microscopic
mechanisms, particularly the interplay between tolerance
factor variations, local structural distortions, and oxy-
gen vacancy formation, remains elusive[4]. While previ-
ous density functional theory (DFT) studies have exam-
ined individual dopant effects in transition metal-doped
BTO[32–36], they provide limited insight into the origin
of tetragonal to hexagonal phase transitions. The quan-
titative evaluation of defect-induced phase stability and
establishment of design principles for phase transition
control remain largely unexplored from a first-principles
perspective[37–40].
In this work, we employ comprehensive DFT calcu-
lations to investigate phase stability in Fe-substituted
BTO to elucidate the underlying mechanisms. System-
atic DFT calculations have been performed to evaluate
the energetics of tetragonal-hexagonal phase transitions
in Fe-substituted BTO, using the cubic phases as a ref-
erence, in comparison with CTO and STO. Furthermore,
three fundamental mechanicsms, i.e., oxygen vacancies,
Jahn-Teller distortions, and tolerance factor, are scru-
tinized based on detailed DFT-informed analysis. Un-
folded band structure analysis, Fe 3d occupation matrix
decomposition, and charge-density difference calculations
are applied to quantify the orbital-resolved charge com-
pensation at the Fe site induced by oxygen vacancies and
reveal a symmetry-lowering orbital reconstruction of the
FeTi–VO defect complex.
II.
COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS
The first-principles calculations were performed using
the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP)[41].
The electron-ion interactions were described using the
projector-augmented wave (PAW) method[42], with the
Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient ap-
proximation (GGA) functional[43] for the exchange-
correlation potential.
To simulate the dilute substitu-
tion, supercells containing 270 atoms were used, corre-
sponding to 3 × 3 × 6 (3 × 3 × 1) supercells for the cu-
bic/tetragonal (hexagonal) BTO structures. To ensure
structural consistency across the different host materials,
the initial atomic configurations for the hexagonal STO,
hexagonal CTO, and tetragonal CTO phases were con-
structed by directly substituting the Ba atoms with Sr
or Ca atoms within the corresponding BTO lattices. For
such BTO supercells, one, two, and three substitutional
Fe atoms on the Ti sites are considered, corresponding
to nominal doping concentrations of 1.85, 3.7, and 5.6
at.%, respectively. These theoretical concentrations ap-
proximate the experimental molar percentages of 2, 4,
and 6 mol.% like a previous theoretical study[35]. For
each Fe concentration, multiple symmetry-inequivalent
substitution sites were systematically generated using the
bsym Python package [44], for all considered phases (cu-
bic, tetragonal, hexagonal) at each substitution concen-
tration. To investigate the effects of oxygen vacancies,
we created oxygen-deficient structures by systematically
removing one oxygen atom next to Fe sites. Again, only
symmetry-inequivalent vacancy sites were considered to
avoid redundant calculations.

ergetics for phase stability analysis. The first approach

To avoid arbitrary choices of magnetic ordering

4.0 eV was applied to the Fe 3d orbitals, consistent with

studies[46, 47]. All structures were optimized until forces

L¨owdin orthogonalized atomic orbitals with fine energy
resolution to accurately resolve the Fe 3d and O 2p or-
bital features. Regarding the determination of the va-
lence state required for tolerance factor calculations, we
adopted the high-spin Fe2+ and Fe3+ configuration. This
assignment is robustly supported by extensive experi-
mental XANES and EPR characterizations[48–50]. Con-
sequently, we utilized the corresponding Shannon ionic
radii [51] to quantify the geometric tolerance factor. To
investigate the electronic structure modifications upon
Fe doping, band structure calculations were performed
along high-symmetry k-point paths in the Brillouin zone.
For direct comparison between the pristine BTO and
Fe-substituted systems, band unfolding analysis, which
maps the band structure of the doped supercell back to
the primitive cell Brillouin zone[52], was employed using
the Pyprocar package [53].
III.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A.
Total energies for Fe-substituted
BTO/CTO/STO
Our total energy calculations reveal a definitive ther-
modynamic crossover from the tetragonal to the hexag-
onal phase driven by increasing Fe concentration. Fig-
ure 1(a) shows the total energies of Fe-substituted BTO
as a function of Fe-concentration.
For the pristine
case, the tetragonal phase is lower in energy by 18.5
meV/f.u. in comparison to the hexagonal phase. The
tetragonal-hexagonal energy difference decreases from
∼19 meV/f.u. (pure BTO) to ∼5 meV/f.u. at ∼2% Fe,
then changes sign where the hexagonal phase is lower
by ∼13 meV/f.u. at ∼6% Fe. The computed crossover
(∼4%) agrees with experiments that report a 4–8% win-
dow where hexagonal fractions become predominant [26].
Crucially, this crossover trend proves robust against sta-
tistical methodologies; as detailed in the Supplemental
Material, the phase transition threshold remains con-
sistent across simple numerical averaging, Boltzmann
weighting, and degeneracy-weighted statistics. For com-
pleteness, the cubic phase is also considered, and it re-
mains consistently higher in energy than both tetragonal
and hexagonal polymorphs across the entire concentra-
tion range. In the pristine limit, this high internal energy
at 0 K is expected because the ideal cubic aristotype
corresponds to a dynamically unstable saddle point on
the potential energy surface, characterized by imaginary
soft phonon modes at the Γ-point [54]. Upon Fe substi-
tution, this energetic disadvantage persists, confirming
that the cubic phase is not a competing ground state
against the Jahn-Teller driven hexagonal stabilization.
While the stoichiometric model predicts a crossover near
4%, experimental observations often detect phase coexis-
tence starting as early as x ≈0.02 [25]. This discrepancy
points to extrinsic factors: oxygen-deficient samples ex-
hibit a broader hexagonal window. Consequently, in the
following section (Sec. III B 1), we quantify how oxygen
vacancies (VO) fundamentally shift this energy landscape
to bridge the gap between the stoichiometric trend and
the earlier experimental onset.
The same Fe substitution does not stabilize the hexag-
onal polymorph in CaTiO3 or SrTiO3 (Figs. 1(b) and
(c)). In CaTiO3, which exhibits orthorhombic →tetrag-
onal →cubic phase transitions with temperature, the
hexagonal structure remains higher by around 200–
300 meV/f.u. across all tested concentrations. Our cal-
culations also show that the cubic phase lies slightly
above the tetragonal phase in energy, consistent with
the temperature-driven transition sequence. Fe doping
in CaTiO3 primarily shifts these transition temperatures
rather than stabilizing hexagonal coordination [29]. In
SrTiO3, where only the cubic phase is observed experi-
mentally, our calculations confirm that the cubic struc-
ture is energetically favorable and the hexagonal phase
remains ∼200–300 meV/f.u. higher in energy even at high
Fe contents [30]. Therefore, only BaTiO3 shows compe-
tition between tetragonal and hexagonal phases upon Fe
doping.
B.
Origins of phase transitions
As suggested in [31], there are three possible mecha-
nisms for the tetragonal-to-hexagonal transition in Fe-
substituted BaTiO3, i.e., oxygen vacancies, Jahn–Teller
(JT) distortions, and changes in the tolerance factor.
Based on the total-energy trends in Sec. III A, we show
below that VO lowers the crossover concentration, while
JT-related local anisotropy adds more elastic cost in
the tetragonal lattice than in the hexagonal one. The
tolerance-factor part is discussed in Sec. III B 3.

1.
Influence of O-vacancies
Figure 1(d) presents the energy differences between the
tetragonal and hexagonal phases when oxygen vacancies
(VO) are introduced to the Fe dopants. Obviously, af-
ter considering VO, the hexagonal phase becomes signif-
icantly more energetically favored compared to the sto-
ichiometric case. For instance, at 2% Fe concentration,
the energy difference inverts from −5.6 meV/f.u. (sto-
ichiometric) to +5.6 meV/f.u. (oxygen-deficient), indi-
cating a stabilization of the hexagonal structure even
at this low doping level.
Similarly, for the 4% Fe-
substituted case, the hexagonal stability margin ex-
pands substantially from 7.4 meV/f.u. to 20.4 meV/f.u.
Overall, the tetragonal-to-hexagonal phase transition
crossover is shifted from ∼4% down to ∼2% Fe. This
matches the observed coexistence starting at x=0.02 in
BaTi1−xFexO3−δ [25] and is consistent with larger hexag-
onal fractions in quenched samples, where more VO are
expected [31].It is noted again that this trend is robust
against statistical methodology. Mechanistically, VO fa-
cilitates charge compensation and relaxes the local Fe–O
coordination, thereby reducing the elastic penalty of Fe-
centered distortions in the hexagonal network relative to
the tetragonal one.
2.
Role of Jahn-Teller Distortions
To estimate the JT contribution separately from other
relaxations, we performed constrained relaxations that
suppress first-order octahedral anisotropy around Fe
while allowing the rest of the structure to relax: (I) from
fully relaxed 2% Fe-substituted cells, extract the average
Fe–O bond length ¯dFe-O; (II) build “JT-suppressed” trial
structures with all six Fe–O bonds set to dFe-O ≡¯dFe-O;
(III) partially relax with Fe and its first O shell con-
strained to keep a uniform octahedron; all other ions
and the cell are free.
The energy increase relative to
the fully relaxed structure defines the JT stabilization
energy per Fe. At 2% Fe, suppressing JT raises the en-
ergy by 14.8 meV/f.u. in the tetragonal phase but only
5.6 meV/f.u. in the hexagonal phase [Fig. 2(a)].
This
shows that the tetragonal environment relies more on lo-
cal octahedral anisotropy for energy lowering.

3.
Local tolerance factor
There has been historically conflicting literature re-
garding the defect chemistry of Fe in BaTiO3, in par-
ticular the valence state of Fe. Calculations and spec-
troscopic characterizations suggest that Fe dopants in
BaTiO3 predominantly adopt the high-spin Fe3+ oxida-
tion state [48–50, 58–60], contrasting with the intuitive
Fe4+ state. Recent hybrid functional DFT calculations
reveal that Fe substitutional defects (FeTi) maintain a
high-spin configuration (S = 5/2) across a wide range of
Fermi levels in the rhombohedral limit [58]. This compu-
tational finding is strongly corroborated by experimental
characterization. X-ray absorption near-edge structure
(XANES) spectroscopy consistently identifies the oxida-
tion state as +3 (> 90% purity) in conventionally sin-
tered ceramics [48, 49]. Furthermore, Electron Paramag-
netic Resonance (EPR) studies unequivocally detect the
characteristic g ≈2 signal of high-spin Fe3+ centers [50],
and M¨ossbauer spectroscopy confirms the isomer shifts
typical of high-spin coordination [59, 60].
In addition, it is reported that reduced species (Fe2+)
can be artificially induced under strongly reducing con-
ditions [61, 62]. In addition, in oxygen-deficient regimes,
microscopic evidence indicates that oxygen vacancies can
locally reduce Fe3+ to Fe2+, with vacancies clustering
around the reduced centers [63]. Mixed Fe2+/Fe3+ va-
lence states have also been reported in reduced sam-
ples [64].
Furthermore, both high-spin and low-spin
Fe2+ are regarded as EPR-silent, rendering EPR evi-
dence insufficient to exclude meaningful Fe2+ contribu-
tions in realistic synthesis conditions. Consequently, Fe
incorporation, whether predominantly as high-spin Fe3+
or with non-negligible Fe2+ contributions, invariably re-
duces the local Goldschmidt tolerance factor relative to
pristine BaTiO3. We evaluate the local tolerance factor
(t) using Shannon’s ionic radii [51] for 6-fold coordination
(Ba2+: 1.61 ˚A, O2−: 1.40 ˚A). For dominant high-spin
Fe3+ (r = 0.645 ˚A) versus host Ti4+ (0.605 ˚A),

tolerance-factor predictions, indicating that the thermo-
dynamic gain from FeTi–VO defect complexes overrides
this geometric penalty through orbital interactions spe-
cific to the face-sharing hexagonal framework [35, 65].

IV.
CONCLUSION
We perform systematic first-principles investigations
on the tetragonal-to-hexagonal phase transitions in Fe-
substituted BaTiO3. Total energy calculations on 270-
atom supercells establish a thermodynamic crossover
near 4% Fe concentration in BaTiO3, consistent with
previous experiments, while neither CaTiO3 nor SrTiO3
exhibits similar behavior. Three possible mechanisms are
quantified. It is observed that oxygen vacancies shift the
crossover concentration from ∼4% to ∼2% by facilitating
charge compensation. Moreover, Jahn-Teller distortions
impose a systematically larger elastic cost in the tetrag-
onal phase (14.8 meV/f.u.) than in the hexagonal one
(5.6 meV/f.u.), with the distortion index λ consistently
higher in the tetragonal host across all Fe concentrations,
rationalizing the sharpening of the phase crossover at el-
evated dopant densities. However, local tolerance-factor
analysis demonstrates that Fe incorporation does not fa-
vor the occurrence of the hexagonal phase.
Orbital-resolved analysis of the Fe 3d occupation ma-
trix reveals an orbital-selective charge redistribution
caused by the VO formation. The net gain of 0.27 e on
the Fe atoms is equally distributed in the spin-up and
spin-down channels, whereas the number of electrons in
the dxy orbitals is increase by 0.66 by depleting the oc-
cupation of the other spin-down orbitals.
