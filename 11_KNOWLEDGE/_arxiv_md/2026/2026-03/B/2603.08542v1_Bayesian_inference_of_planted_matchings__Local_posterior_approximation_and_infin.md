Zhou Fan∗

Abstract
We study Bayesian inference of an unknown matching π∗between two correlated random
point sets {Xi}n
i=1 and {Yi}n
i=1 in [0, 1]d, under a critical scaling ∥Xi −Yπ∗(i)∥2 ≍n−1/d, in
both an exact matching model where all points are observed and a partial matching model
where a fraction of points may be missing. Restricting to the simplest setting of d = 1, in
this work, we address the questions of (1) whether the posterior distribution over matchings is
approximable by a local algorithm, and (2) whether marginal statistics of this posterior have
a well-defined limit as n →∞. We answer both questions affirmatively for partial matching,
where a decay-of-correlations arises for large n. For exact matching, we show that the posterior
is approximable locally only after a global sorting of the points, and that defining a large-n limit
of marginal statistics requires a careful indexing of points in the Poisson point process limit of
the data, based on a notion of flow. We leave as an open question the extensions of such results
to dimensions d ≥2.

Introduction
Identifying an unknown matching or alignment between two collections of objects is a fundamental
task across many domains of science and engineering, arising in batch correction and data integra-
tion for single-cell genomics [37], particle tracking and trajectory inference [27], image matching
[25], record linkage and database alignment [32], and network alignment [5]. Many methods for
estimating latent matchings have been developed in these applications, often using combinations of
ideas around local or nearest neighbor featurization [21], PCA/CCA/NMF for dimension reduction
[35, 22], linear assignment or MAP estimation [10, 40], and/or optimal transport [33, 17]. However,
while all such methods return an estimate of a “most likely” matching, most do not provide a
calibrated quantification of uncertainty for the underlying match.
Motivated by a need for better theoretical understanding of the algorithmic feasibility of uncer-
tainty quantification in these problems, in this work we introduce and study two simple Bayesian
models for matching point sets in Rd. In an exact matching model, we will assume complete obser-
vations of two point sets X1, . . . , Xn ∈Rd and Y1, . . . , Yn ∈Rd, related through a latent bijection
π∗: [n] →[n] by
Xi = ¯Xπ∗(i)

where subsets of { ¯X1, . . . , ¯Xn} and { ¯Y1, . . . , ¯Yn} may be independently unobserved. We will study,
both statistically and computationally, posterior inference for π∗under a uniform prior distribution
over all candidate matchings (or equivalently, a uniform random ordering of the given observations
X1, . . . , Xn and Y1, . . . , Yn).
A Gaussian setting of exact matching where ¯Xi ∼N(0, Id) and ¯Yi−¯Xi ∼N(0, σ2Id) was studied
in depth in [13, 23], who obtained precise characterizations of information-theoretic limits for perfect
and almost-perfect recovery of π∗, and of estimation error by the MLE. In the simplest setting of
fixed dimension d, results of these works imply that the threshold for almost-perfect recovery of π∗
is σ ≪n−1/d. In this work, we will study the critical scaling regime ∥¯Yi −¯Xi∥2 ≍n−1/d, where each

point Xi will typically match to more than one point Yj with non-vanishing posterior probability
as n →∞. In this regime, we ask two basic questions:
1. Algorithmically, can one approximate the “marginals” of the posterior law P(π | X, Y ) —
i.e. the posterior probability vector for the correct match to each point Xi or Yj — using an
efficient local algorithm that sees only a neighborhood of O(1) closest points to Xi or Yj?
2. Statistically, as n →∞, can one characterize the limiting empirical distribution of these
posterior marginals, and hence understand properties of Bayes-optimal inference of π∗such
as the fraction of mismatched points in a Bayes estimate or the average size of Bayes credible
sets for uncertainty quantification?
It is evident that these questions relate to the nature of decay-of-correlations and the existence
of an infinite-volume limit for the posterior measure P(π | X, Y ). We will introduce our models for a
general dimension d, where these questions are naturally posed, although the results of our current
paper will pertain only to dimension d = 1 (where the answers are already somewhat nuanced,
especially for the exact matching model). We discuss below the additional challenges that may
arise in extending our analyses to dimensions d ≥2.
1.1
Summary of results
Partial matching.
In the partial matching model, we will assume that each point ¯X1, . . . , ¯XN
and ¯Y1, . . . , ¯YN for a latent sample size N is independently and randomly observed with a fixed
probability p. We study a posterior distribution over partial bijections π∗that match each observed
point X1, . . . , XNX to a corresponding point in the opposite set Y1, . . . , YNY , or to the empty label
∅.
This imposes a softer bijectivity constraint for π∗than in the exact matching model.
We
establish that for dimension d = 1, under mild assumptions for the joint law of ( ¯Xi, ¯Yi) where
| ¯Yi −¯Xi| ≍n−1/d = n−1, the posterior law P(π | X, Y ) exhibits decay-of-correlations in a suitable
sense.
This allows us to provide an affirmative answer to Question 1, showing that posterior marginal
match probabilities for each point Xi can be approximated by computing a local posterior over
partial bijections between points in a window of size O(n−1) around Xi. As n →∞, the empirical
distribution of posterior marginals converges weakly to a natural infinite-volume limit defined over
the Poisson point process limit of the observed data, providing also a simple answer to Question 2.
Exact matching.
In the exact matching model where all points ¯X1, . . . , ¯Xn and ¯Y1, . . . , ¯Yn are
observed and inference for an exact bijection is required, we provide a partially affirmative answer
to Question 1, showing that the posterior marginals are well-approximated by an algorithm that
first performs a global step of sorting {Xi}n
i=1 and {Yi}n
i=1, and then computing a local posterior
over matchings of O(1) points having the same sorted indices. Our analyses imply that a more
naive approach of computing a local posterior over, say, bijections between the k points closest
to Xi and the k points closest to its nearest neighbor Yj would fail to correctly approximate the
posterior law, even in the limit k →∞.
The need for this global sorting is related to an incomplete decay-of-correlations of Gibbs
measures in the infinite-volume limit. In a related model of matching a countably infinite point set
on R to itself, [8] showed that there is an infinite set of extremal infinite-volume Gibbs measures,
one corresponding to each integer value of a conserved quantity called the flow that induces long-
range dependence. In our model, we define an analogous notion of flow relative to π∗(c.f. Definition
2.13), and we give an answer to Question 2 by showing that the empirical distribution of posterior

marginals converges to an infinite-volume limit corresponding to matchings over the limiting Poisson
point process that have 0 flow relative to π∗.
Thus the answers to these questions are already different in dimension d = 1 for the partial and
exact matching models, where a conserved flow variable in exact matching presents an obstruction
to correlation decay. Extending our analyses to dimensions d ≥2 will carry additional challenges:
• For exact matching, there is no direct analogue of sorting or ordering of points when d ≥2,
raising a question of how global information may be used as an input for local approximation
of the posterior marginals to then succeed.
• For both partial and exact matching, analogues of the boundary variables Γx(π) in our anal-
yses, which form a Markov chain in dimension d = 1, constitute a more general Markov
random field in dimensions d ≥2, raising the question of whether there are additional phase
transition phenomena for the long-range correlations of the Gibbs measure.
We believe these are interesting open questions, which we leave to future work.
1.2
Related work
Statistical inference of planted matchings.
MLE or MAP estimation in the exact matching
model (1) may be understood as a permutation optimization problem over a complete weighted
bipartite graph, with a random adjacency matrix whose entries are the costs of matching Xi to Yj.
Variants of this problem where the edge weights are independent have also been studied in [28, 18],
with remarkably precise results.
An early work studying our setting of dependent edge weights in a context of particle tracking
was [11], followed by [13, 23] who gave sharp results on perfect and near-perfect recovery in a
Gaussian model for various noise variances σ2 and dimensions d. Permutation recovery in linear
regression with shuffled responses was studied in [30]. Variations of a partial matching model were
also studied in [14], motivated by database alignment problems with imperfect overlap.
A related problem is that of matching geometric random graphs, where one observes only
pairwise distances or similarities k(Xi, Xj) and k(Yi, Yj) between data points. We refer to [38] for
pointers to this literature and recent results.
Gibbs measures on permutations.
The classical Mallows model falls under this framework
and remains an active area of research [29, 39, 26, 2]. Asymptotics and large deviations of the
Gibbs measure for a matching model in a different graphon-type scaling limit were studied in [9].
Our model scaling and posterior measures are instead more closely related to models of spatial
random permutations studied in a context of Bose-Einstein condensates in mathematical physics
[19, 36, 7]. In dimension d = 1, [8] gave a characterization of infinite-volume Gibbs measures for
a wide class of such models with convex noise potential V (·), showing that the extremal Gibbs
states are in correspondence with an integer parameter called the flow. The infinite-volume limit
of our exact matching model may be viewed as an extension of this setting to non-convex noise
potentials and two different point sets. To our knowledge, an analogous complete characterization
in dimensions d ≥2 seems to be unresolved. Existence of an infinite-volume limit for permutations
on a regular lattice was shown for periodic boundary conditions by [6] and in high temperature by
[4]. The band structure in some such models has been studied in [20].

B.2.1
Local weak convergence to a limiting point process
We recall some definitions about weak convergence of point processes; for a fuller discussion, we
refer to [31, Chapter 3], or [24, 15, 16].
Consider a metric space E with its Borel σ-algebra E. A point measure on E is a measure m
such that there exists a countable collection of points {xi ∈E : i ∈I ⊆N} with m = P
i∈I δxi. Let
M = M(E) be the set of all point measures on E. Let M = M(E) be the smallest σ-algebra making
all evaluation maps m 7→m(A) measurable for all A ∈E. A point process η is a random element of
(M, M) (i.e. measurable map from some underlying probability space (Ω, F) to (M, M)). We may
identify η with a countable collection of random points {Xi : i ∈I} in E, so that η = P
i∈I δXi.
The intensity measure µ of a point process η is the measure on E given by µ(A) = Eη(A) for all
A ∈E. We say that η is locally-finite if P (η(A) < ∞) = 1 for every bounded A ∈E.
Definition B.9. Let µ be a non-atomic measure on E. A Poisson point process with intensity
measure µ is a point process η on E so that for any collection of pairwise disjoint sets A1, . . . , Ak ⊆
E, the variables η(A1), . . . , η(Ak) are independent with η(Aℓ) ∼Poisson(µ(Aℓ)). We write η ∼
PPP(µ).
In the case when E is Euclidean and the intensity measure is of the form µ(·) = λ·Leb(·), where
λ ≥0 is a rate parameter and Leb is Lebesgue measure, we say that η is a homogeneous Poisson
point process with rate λ and write η ∼PPP(λ).
