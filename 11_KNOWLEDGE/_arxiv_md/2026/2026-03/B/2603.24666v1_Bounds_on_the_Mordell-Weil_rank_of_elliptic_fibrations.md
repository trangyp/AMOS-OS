Abstract. We present two proofs for a bound on the rank of the
Mordell-Weil group of some elliptic fibrations.
The bounds apply to
Calabi-Yau varieties, which are also of interest to the physics of string
theory.
We prove explicit bounds for Calabi-Yau threefolds, as pre-
dicted by physics, and give new explicit bounds for fourfolds under mild
assumptions.
These results motivate a conjecture for bounds in any
dimensions.
1. Introduction
We prove bounds on the rank of the Mordell-Weil group MW(X/B) of
an elliptically fibered variety X over a base B. We present two types of
arguments, which have parallels, but differ in some details and emphasis.
Each method leads to possible generalizations in different directions.
For X a smooth K3 surface, it is known that the rank of the Mordell-
Weil group can take any value 0 ≤rkMW(X/B) ≤18 [30, 28]. In higher
dimensions, the existence of a bound on rkMW(X/B) is guaranteed, for
Calabi-Yau varieties, by the effective boundedness of families of elliptic fi-
brations [12, 3].
The currently highest known Mordell-Weil rank for el-
liptic Calabi-Yau threefolds is obtained for special Schoen manifolds with
rkMW(X/B) = 10 [20, 11], but based on expectations from physics, higher
ranks should be possible.
In this work, we prove three structure theorems for bounds on elliptic
fibrations: Theorem 3.1, Theorem 4.16 and Theorem 4.18..
Based on these, we furthermore prove the following explicit bounds:
Theorem. Let X →B be an elliptic Calabi-Yau threefold with sections and
singular fibers. Then the Mordell-Weil group of X satisfies rank MW(X/B) ≤
28.
Theorem. Let X →B be an elliptic Calabi-Yau fourfold with sections and
singular fibers. Then under mild assumptions the Mordell-Weil group of X
satisfies rank MW(X/B) ≤38.
The precise statements and hypotheses are given in Theorem 5.1 and The-
orem 6.3. These theorems and their proofs formalise and generalise previous
results from physics [35, 38, 33].

1.1. Bounds from physics. To every elliptic Calabi-Yau 3-fold X one can
associate a six-dimensional N = 1 supergravity theory obtained as the low-
energy limit of F-theory [52, 43, 44] compactified on X. In this context, the
free and torsion part of the Mordell-Weil group of X encode the continuous
and, respectively, discrete abelian gauge symmetries in the effective action
(see, for example, the reviews [54, 7] for details and references). Bounds on
the rank of the Mordell-Weil group, or in physics terms on the rank rU(1)
of the abelian gauge group, are motivated by general arguments suggesting
that the number of light degrees of freedom and their interactions in any
consistent quantum gravitational theory are severely constrained.
While
gauge and gravitational anomalies alone do admit infinite families of theo-
ries with arbitrarily high abelian gauge rank [47], taking into consideration
constraints from quantum consistency of probe strings [26] sets tight bounds
on the ranks [35, 38, 33, 27].
In [35] bounds on rU(1) have been obtained for general N = 1 supergravity
theories in six dimensions by analysing probe consistency of certain solitonic
strings coupling to gravity. These bounds are given by rU(1) ≤22 for theories
with T ≥1 tensor multiplets (subsequently improved to rU(1) ≤20 in [27])
and rU(1) ≤32 for T = 0. In explicit realisations of the supergraviy via
F-theory, the solitonic strings arise from D3-branes wrapping suitable curves
on B. If the curve C is rational, movable and without base points on B,
the associated string couples to all abelian gauge groups because C has a
non-zero intersection number with the height pairing divisor associated with
the Shioda map.
This follows by extending arguments in [6] to Calabi-
Yau threefolds [34] and led, for smooth base B, to the conservative bounds
rk(MW(X/B)) ≤32 for B = P2 and rk(MW(X/B)) ≤20 otherwise in [35].
In [38] these bounds were sharpened to
(1)
rk(MW(X/B)) ≤10 C · (−KB) −2 ,
which also holds for elliptic Calabi-Yau fourfolds with a smooth base B
and C rational, smooth and in a movable class without base points. For
elliptic threefolds, (1) implies the stronger bound rk(MW(X/B)) ≤28 if
B = P2 (leading to T = 0) and rk(MW(X/B)) ≤18 otherwise. The physics
derivation of (1) relies on a specific interpretation of the zero modes on the
probe strings.
For X a threefold and B a rational fibration, [33] argues
for the bound rk(MW(X/B)) ≤18 geometrically by relating MW(X/B) to
the Mordell-Weil group of the K3 surface obtained by restricting the elliptic
fibration to the rational fiber of B.

In Section 3, we prove the structure theorem 3.1. While philosophically

of the elliptic fibration, the Shioda pairing, or heights. This approach uses
only elements of the classical minimal model theory for rational surfaces,
and avoids difficult results in higher dimensional birational geometry. It is
formulated in the scheme language. At the current state this method does
not provide an explicit effective bound for fourfolds.
In Section 4 we develop and prove the two structure theorems 4.16 and
4.18 for elliptic fibrations (not necessarily Calabi-Yau). The proof formalises
and generalises the physics approach of [34, 35, 38, 33] by proving an injection
from MW(X/B) to MW(Z) for a suitable surface Z (smooth or birationally
a K3 surface) obtained by restricting the elliptic fibration to a smooth curve
C in the smooth locus of the base B; in particular, if the Picard rank of B
exceeds one, C must be movable. The result uses key properties of the Shioda
map of rational sections and its height-pairing (see in particular Lemma 4.2).
We present the two different approaches, as developed in Section 3 and
in Section 4, to bound the Mordell-Weil rank since each of them has the
possibility of generalization in different ways, which could be useful for future
work.
In Section 5, we apply Theorems 3.1 and 4.16 to prove a universal bound,
formulated as Theorem 5.1, on the Mordell-Weil rank of elliptic Calabi-Yau
threefolds.
In Section 6, we apply the structure theorems 4.16 and 4.18 to obtain
an explicit bound for the Mordell-Weil rank of elliptic Calabi-Yau fourfolds,
Theorem 6.3. The proof makes use of general results from the Minimal Model
Program (in particular Theorem 2.11). For the proof we have to make the
assumption that the base of the fourfold, in case its rank is one, can be taken
to be smooth. Under this hypothesis, we can use the classification of smooth
rank-one Fano threefolds [22] to establish the bound by proving the existence
of a curve C as required by Theorems 4.16 and 4.18. Note that unlike for
Calabi-Yau threefolds, in some of the cases the curves giving the bound are
not movable and are not rational.
Our results naturally motivate a generalisation to any dimension, which
we present in the form of a conjecture in Section 7.
