Abstract. We give a structure result on the set of locally constant stability conditions, Stab(D/R),
defined by Halpern-Leistner–Robotis [HLR25] showing that it has the structure of a complex mani-
fold, in total analogy with Bridgeland’s work. As a consequence, we show that the property of having
relative mass-hom bounds and the existence of good moduli spaces depends only on the connected
components of Stab(D/R). Lastly, we observe that the datum of a locally constant stability condi-
tion is equivalent to that of a flat family of stability conditions, as described in [BLM+21], in the
context of noncommutative algebraic geometry.

1. Introduction
Since the introduction of stability conditions by Bridgeland in [Bri07], a major problem in the
field has been the construction of moduli spaces of semistable objects. Needless to say, a great deal
of very deep mathematics has emerged from it. In a first approximation, there are two natural
questions to ask given a triangulated category D together with a stability condition σ ∈Stab(D):
(1) Under which hypotheses on σ is the substack of semistable objects Mss
σ (v) ,→Mσ(ϕ, ϕ + 1]
algebraic and admits a proper good moduli space Mss
σ (v)?
(2) Which of these conditions are preserved as σ ∈Stab(D) varies?
It turns out that even defining the moduli functor, a question tackled first in [AP06] and later
in [Tod08], is quite challenging and nontrivial. To complicate things, unlike the case of coherent
sheaves, these moduli stacks do not admit any meaningful group action, making the techniques
from GIT unavailable in this context. As a result, the best approach so far has been considering the
problem as that of building moduli of objects in abelian categories, a topic first considered in [AZ01],

Theorem 1.1 ([AHLH23], Theorem 7.23). Let k be an excellent ring of characteristic 0 and A
be a cocomplete k-linear abelian category that is locally noetherian. Assume that MA, the stack
parametrizing flat objects in A, is algebraic and locally of finite type over k. Then any quasi-compact
closed substack X ⊂MA admits a proper good moduli space.
Lately, there has been a great amount of interest surrounding these moduli problems ([HLM25])
with applications ranging from moduli of perverse sheaves [Lam25] to cohomological applications
[Dav24], [BDN+25]. In the context of Bridgeland stability, one of the more striking applications of
[AHLH23] is the following existence theorem. Let X be a smooth and projective scheme over an
algebraically closed field. A stability condition on X, σ = (A, Z) ∈Stab(X), consist of the heart of
a t-structure A ⊂Db(X) and a central charge homomorphism Z : K0(Db(X)) →C together with
some compatibility properties. Assume that σ is algebraic, meaning that Im(Z) ⊂Q[i] (hence the
heart A is a noetherian abelian category). Assume also that:
(1) the heart A satisfies openness of flatness,
(2) A is bounded with respect to the standard t-structure on Db(X),
(3) Mss
σ (v) is bounded for all v ∈K0(Db(X))
then the Harder–Narasimhan filtration gives a Θ-stratification on Mσ(v) whose open stratum is
exactly Mss
σ (v), hence it admits a proper good moduli space by [AHLH23, Theorem 6.5]. This,
together with the deformation argument by Piyaratne–Toda ([Tod08], [PT19, Proposition 4.12])
implies that the same holds for every algebraic stability condition τ in the same connected compo-
nent of Stab(X) containing σ. This gives a bittersweet answer to questions 1 and 2: the hypotheses
which grant the existence of moduli spaces are well understood, yet there is no general technique
available to tackle the problem in full generality.
The issue of the existence of stability conditions has been solved in the recent breakthrough
[Li26], where C. Li manages to prove nonemptiness of Stab(X) for every smooth projective variety
X. Proving that the newly built stability conditions verify the conditions above would allow for a
huge source of new examples of moduli spaces.
In the relative setting, the quest of constructing stability conditions for smooth and proper
families X →S, allowing for a rich moduli theory, has been addressed in [BLM+21]. Notably, it
does not suffice to have a collection {σs}s∈S of stability conditions on Db(Xs) for all s ∈S and
impose a local constancy property for the central charges. The right requirement turns out to be
a global condition on Db(XC) for every smooth curve with a morphism C →S, called a Harder–
Narsimhan structure. The extension of such formalism to the noncommutative setting has been
written recently in [Per26].
On the other hand, the first extension of the theory of stability conditions in the enhanced setting
appeared in the work of Halpern-Leistner–Robotis [HLR25]. For a smooth and proper category,
linear over an excellent base algebra, they define a new structure, called a locally constant stability
condition (Definition 2.39) with very convenient properties for the existence of good moduli spaces.
Specifically, in their approach there are two major contributions:
• the notion of a sluicing (Definition 2.34) overcomes the noetherianity assumption on the
t-structures necessary to apply Theorem 1.1,
• the generic flatness property in 1 is shown to be equivalent to an explicit bound on the
dimension of hom-spaces in the category, called a mass-hom bound (see Theorem 2.42).
We say that a locally constant stability condition is proper if, loosely speaking, it satisfies open-
ness of the semistable locus and boundedness of semistable objects. Our main contribution is the
following.

Conventions. Although the theory of Bridgeland stability conditions does not have any restrictions
coming from the characteristic of the ground field k, that of good moduli spaces does. In fact,
even the ´etale local structure of algebraic stacks in [AHR20], necessary for the existence results in
[AHLH23], works only in characteristic zero. The extension of such a theory in positive characteristic
is a deep problem in the field (see [AHL26]). To overcome these pathologies, throughout the paper
we will assume char(k) = 0 for every field k. For t-structures we use the cohomological notation;
all functors are derived unless specified.

Definition 2.1. A derived algebraic stack S is perfect if
• the diagonal of S is affine,
• Dqc(S) is compactly generated,
• compact and perfect objects in Dqc(S) coincide.
For a perfect derived algebraic stack S, the category Perf(S) may be regarded as a commutative
algebra object in the category of small, idempotent-complete, stable ∞-categories Catst.
Definition 2.2. An S-linear category is a Perf(S)-module object in Catst.
The collection of S-linear categories has the structure of an ∞-category
CatS := ModPerf(S)(Catst),
together with a symmetric monoidal structure denoted C ⊗Perf(S) D. An S-linear functor is a
morphism C →D in CatS, and the collection of such forms another S-linear category, FunS(C, D).
To be more precise, given C, D, F ∈CatS, the S-linear functors C ⊗Perf(S) D →F classify the
bilinear maps C × D →F. There is a canonical functor
C × D →C ⊗Perf(S) D,
and in particular, given objects C ∈C and D ∈D, we denote C ⊠D ∈C ⊗Perf(S) D their image
under this functor.
Lemma 2.3 ([Per19], Lemma 2.7). Let C and D be S-linear categories. The category C ⊗Perf(S) D
is thickly generated by objects of the form C ⊠D, with C ∈C and D ∈D.
Fix a morphism T →S of perfect derived algebraic stacks. The product
DT := D ⊗Perf(S) Perf(T)
is called the base change of D along T →S: it is naturally a T-linear category. Moreover, for every
S-linear functor Φ : C →D there is a T-linear functor ΦT : CT →DT obtained from base-change.
For any point s ∈S, the base change along Spec(κ(s)) →S gives a κ(s)-linear category Dκ(s),
called the fibre of D over s.
Let D be an S-linear category. For objects E, F ∈D there is a mapping object HomS(E, F) ∈
Dqc(S) defined by the property
MapDqc(S)(G, HomS(E, F)) ∼= MapD(E ⊠G, F), for G ∈Perf(S).
Specifically, the functor
MapD(E ⊗(−), F) : Dqc(S) →(Spaces)
is representable by [Lur09, Proposition 5.5.2.2] and HomS(E, F) ∈Dqc(S) is the representing object
by definition.
2.1.1. Presentable linear categories. Often, for applications it is more suited to work with larger cat-
egories. In the geometric setting, this usually translates to working with unbounded, quasi-coherent
categories. In general, this corresponds to enlarge the category considered to the ∞-category PrCatst
of presentable stable ∞-categories. Once again, the category Dqc(S) is a commutative algebra object
in PrCatst. As above, a presentable S-linear category is a Dqc(S)-module object in PrCatst. The
collection of such forms an ∞-category
PrCatS := ModDqc(S)(PrCatst).
If D ∈CatS there is a corresponding category Ind(D) ∈PrCatS called the ind-completion of D,
obtained roughly as freely adjoining all filtered colimits to D. This correspondence is functorial and
induces
Ind : CatS →PrCatS .

2.2. Smooth and proper categories.
Definition 2.4. Let D be an S-linear category.
• D is proper if HomS(E, F) ∈Perf(S) for all E, F ∈D,
• D is smooth if idInd(D) ∈FunS(Ind(D), Ind(D)) is representable by a compact object.
These notions are stable under base change, as shown below.
Lemma 2.5 ([Per19], Lemma 4.10). Let D be an S-linear category, and let T →S be a morphism
of perfect derived algebraic stacks.
• If D is smooth over S then DT is smooth over T,
• if D is proper over S then DT is proper over T.

Definition 2.14. An accessible t-structure on a compactly generated, presentable, stable ∞-

the induced t-structure on Cω is bounded and noetherian.
