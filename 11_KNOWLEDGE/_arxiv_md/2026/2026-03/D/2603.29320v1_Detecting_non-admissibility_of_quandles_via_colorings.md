Detecting non-admissibility of quandles via colorings
Katsunori Arai∗and Ryoya Kai †
Abstract
A quandle is an algebraic system whose axioms are motivated by Reidemeister moves in
knot theory. A typical example is a conjugation quandle arising from a group. A quandle is
said to be admissible if it is isomorphic to a conjugation quandle. Admissible quandles often
yield knot invariants that coincide with those derived from the knot group, whereas non-
admissible quandles may produce genuinely new invariants. In this sense, it is important to
construct non-admissible quandles. In this paper, we provide criteria for determining whether
given quandles are admissible by colorings of (1, 1)-tangles. As an application, we construct
numerous examples of non-admissible quandles by analyzing simple tangles obtained from
the Hopf link and the trefoil knot.
1
Introduction
Quandles were introduced as algebraic structures for constructing link invariants; their axioms
correspond to Reidemeister moves in knot theory. Beyond knot theory, they can be regarded as a
generalization of conjugation in groups and are closely related to the algebraic properties of point
symmetries of symmetric spaces. These connections have led to developments and applications
in various areas of mathematics. In particular, the fundamental quandle of an oriented link is a
remarkably strong link invariant.
However, the algebraic structure of the fundamental quandle of an oriented link is often too
complicated to analyze directly. Therefore, it is common to study quandle homomorphisms from
the fundamental quandle to a fixed, explicitly described quandle. Such homomorphisms can be
described combinatorially as assignments of elements of the quandle to the arcs of an oriented link
diagram satisfying certain conditions. These assignments are called colorings of the diagram by the
quandle, and are in bijection with homomorphisms from the fundamental quandle to the quandle.
Moreover, using the functor that associates a group to a quandle, quandle colorings can be related
to group homomorphisms of the knot group into an appropriate group. From this perspective,
quandle colorings refine the notion of group homomorphisms from knot groups.
Every group gives rise to a quandle via conjugation, and its subquandles are called conjugation
quandles. A quandle is said to be admissible if it is isomorphic to a conjugation quandle. In this
case, quandle homomorphisms from the fundamental quandle correspond to group homomorphisms
from the knot group. Consequently, to extract genuinely new information beyond invariants derived

Problem 1.1 (cf. [2, Question 3.1]). Determine whether a given quandle is admissible or not.
In general, it is difficult to determine whether a given quandle is admissible. In fact, to solve
this problem, we need to solve the word problem for certain elements in the associated group of
the quandle.
In this paper, we introduce the notion of (L, p)-admissibility for quandles, where L is an oriented
link, and p is a base point on L. This notion is described in terms of colorings of a diagram of
the associated (1, 1)-tangle, which is obtained by cutting L at p. As an application, we provide
explicit examples of non-admissible quandles by analyzing simple (1, 1)-tangles obtained from the
Hopf link and the trefoil knot.

Preliminaries
In this section, we review basic definitions and examples of quandles used throughout the paper.
Definition 2.1. Let X be a nonempty set and ◁: X × X →X a binary operation on X. The
pair (X, ◁) is called a quandle if it satisfies the following conditions:
• For any x ∈X, we have x ◁x = x.
• For any y ∈X, the map Sy : X →X, defined by Sy(x) = x ◁y, is bijective.
• For any x, y, z ∈X, the identity (x ◁y) ◁z = (x ◁z) ◁(y ◁z) holds.
For x, y ∈X, we denote S−1
y (x) by x◁y. For simplicity, we often write X for the quandle (X, ◁)
when no confusion can occur.
Let X and Y be quandles. A map f : X →Y is a quandle homomorphism if it preserves the
quandle operations. A quandle isomorphism is a bijective quandle homomorphism. Two quandles
are said to be isomorphic if there exists a quandle isomorphism from one to the other.
A subquandle of a quandle (X, ◁) is a subset A ⊂X that is closed under the operations ◁
and ◁. If A is a subquandle of X, then the inclusion map i : A ,→X is an injective quandle
homomorphism. More generally, for a quandle homomorphism f : X →Y , the image f(X) is
a subquandle of Y . In addition, if the homomorphism f is injective, then the image f(X) is
isomorphic to X.
Example 2.2. A group G becomes a quandle with the operation g ◁h := h−1gh for g, h ∈G,
denoted by Conj(G). A subquandle of Conj(G) is called a conjugation quandle. We note that any
subquandle of Conj(G) consists of some conjugacy classes of a subgroup of the group G.
Example 2.3. Given a group G and an automorphism σ ∈Aut(G), define the binary operation g◁
h := σ(gh−1)h for g, h ∈G. Then G becomes a quandle. This quandle is denoted by GAlex(G, σ),
and is called a generalized Alexander quandle.
Definition 2.4. A quandle X is said to be admissible if there exists an injective quandle homo-
morphism X →Conj(G) for a group G. Otherwise, X is said to be non-admissible. In other
words, an admissible quandle is isomorphic to a subquandle of Conj(G) for some group G.

(L, p)-admissibility for quandles

is to study colorings of a (1, 1)-tangle obtained from an oriented link with a base point.

Definition 3.2. Let L be an oriented link with a base point p, and let ˜L be the (1, 1)-tangle

Remark 3.3. The (L, p)-admissibility generally depends on the choices of the orientation of L
and of the base point p. In particular, if K is an oriented knot and p ∈K is a base point, then the
(1, 1)-tangle ˜K obtained from (K, p) is independent of the choice of base point on K. A quandle
X is said to be L-admissible if it is (L, p)-admissible for every base point p ∈L. Hence, for an
oriented knot K, we simply say that a quandle is K-admissible.
The following proposition shows that (L, p)-admissibility is a necessary condition for admissi-
bility. This can be viewed as a refinement of [3,9].
Proposition 3.4. Any admissible quandle is (L, p)-admissible for any link L with a base point
p ∈L.
A proof of Proposition 3.4 is given in Section 5. As a consequence, we obtain the following
criterion:
Corollary 3.5. If a quandle is not (L, p)-admissible for some link L with a base point p, then the
quandle is non-admissible.

Remark 4.3. The contrapositive of Corollary 4.2 also follows from a group-theoretic calculation.
Indeed, for elements x, y in a group, the identity y−1xy = x holds if and only if x−1yx = y. Thus, in
any conjugation quandle, if x◁y = x, then y ◁x = y. Since every admissible quandle is isomorphic
to a conjugation quandle, the same implication holds in every admissible quandle.
