GALOIS SUBSPACES FOR COMPACT RIEMANN SURFACES OF
GENUS 2
JUAN-PABLO LLERENA-C´ORDOVA
Abstract. Let X be a compact Riemann surface of genus 2 and D a very
ample divisor with ϕD its associated embedding into Pn. We consider the set
GX,D of linear subspaces W of Pn of codimension 2 with projection πW such
that fW = πW ◦ϕD is Galois, i.e. f∗
W k(P1) ⊆k(X) is a Galois extension. It is
known that GX,D is isomorphic to a disjoint union of projective spaces. In this
article, we calculate the dimension of projective spaces in the decomposition
of GX,D, when D is induced by a subgroup of Aut(X).
Introduction
Let X be a compact Riemann surface of genus g, k(X) the function field of X,
D a very ample divisor on X, and ϕD : X →PN the embedding induced by D. If
W ∈G(N −2, N), we denote by πW : PN →P1 the projection away from W.
Definition 0.1. We say that ϕD is a Galois embedding if there exists a subspace
W ∈G(N −2, N), such that fW = πW ◦ϕD is a Galois cover; that is, the field
extension f ∗
W k(P1) ⊆k(X) is Galois.
In this case, we say that W is a Galois
subspace for D. Moreover, if W is disjoint from ϕD(X), then we say that W is a
disjoint Galois subspace.
The general concept of Galois embeddings and Galois subspaces was introduced
by Yoshihara in [12]. The particular case when W is a point was introduced by
Fukasawa in [7]. In recent years, multiple authors have expanded on the idea of
Galois subspaces, e.g., [2], [8], [9], [10], and [1]. We build on the work of Auffarth
and Rahausen [2], in which the authors study the following set
GX,D := {W ∈G(N −2, N); πW ◦ϕD is Galois}.
They prove (see [2, Theorem 1.1]) that if g ≥2, then

The set GX,D has been studied when X has genus 0 or 1 in [2], and [3], re-
spectively.
In this article, we will study the set GX,D when g = 2, using the
decomposition of Equation (1). Because XD,πH is a projective space of dimension
ℓ(D −DπH) −1 and Aut(X) is finite, to study the components of the decomposi-
tion of GX,D, we only need to focus on calculating the dimension of each projective
space. This is possible because the isomorphism class of Aut(X) is known for genus
2 (see [5] and [4]).
We mainly focus on disjoint Galois subspaces, which lie in the 0-dimensional com-
ponents of the decomposition of Equation (1). Moreover, GX,D has a 0-dimensional
component if and only if there exists a subgroup H ≤Aut(X) with X/H ∼= P1 such
that D ∼DH, where DH := DH,p := P
h∈H h[p] for some p ∈X (see Lemma 2.1).
Note that, because X/H ∼= P1, the definition of DH is independent of the choice of
p, up to linear equivalence (see Lemma 1.1).
The main result of this article describes the components of the decomposition of
GX,D in Equation (1) when D is induced by a subgroup of Aut(X), to guarantee
the existence of a 0-dimensional component.
Theorem 0.2. Let X be a compact Riemann surface of genus 2 and let H ≤G =
Aut(X). The following assertions hold:
(1) Assume G ̸∼= C10. Two very ample divisors induced by subgroups of G differ
by a multiple of the canonical divisor.
(2) In GX,DH, there is a one-to-one correspondence between disjoint Galois
subspaces and subgroups of order |H|.
The structure of the article is as follows:
In Section 1, we prove some general results on compact Riemann surfaces of
genus g ≥2. We prove Theorem 0.2 in Section 2. Lastly, in Section 3, we present
a table describing the dimension of the components of GX,DH.
Remark 1. Even though the techniques used to prove Theorem 0.2 hold for arbi-
trary genus, that is, Lemma 1.2 and Lemma 1.4, we are unable to prove an analog of
Theorem 0.2 for genus greater than 2. The main reason is that for a Riemann sur-
face X of genus 2, there exist many subgroups H ≤Aut(X) such that X/H ∼= P1.
This is not the case when the genus is greater than 2. Although the structure of
Aut(X) is well-known for low genus, it is not clear whether the techniques used in
the proof of Theorem 0.2 can be used to give a similar result for higher genus.
Finally, we note that Yoshihara and Fukasawa have a list of current open prob-
lems of Galois subspaces [13], in which they also summarize what is currently known
regarding each problem.

In this section, we will prove the lemmas necessary to prove Theorem 0.2. For
this section, unless stated otherwise, X will be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g ≥2, and G = Aut(X).

Now, if X has genus 2, then we have an equivalent characterization of a very
ample divisor.
Lemma 1.5. Let X be of genus 2 and D a divisor on X. We have that D is a very
ample divisor if and only if deg(D) ≥5.
Proof. The “if” part of the lemma follows from the Riemann-Roch formula and the
fact that deg(D) > deg(K) + 2 (see [11, Chapter VII Proposition 1.2]). The “only
if” part of the lemma is a consequence of the fact that there is no immersion from
X into Pn with n < 3; the case n = 1 follows from Hurwitz’s Formula and the case
n = 2 contradicts the Genus-Degree Formula.
□
Finally, we have the following lemma.
Lemma 1.6. If X has genus 2 and D1, D2 are two divisors on X with deg(D1) −
deg(D2) > 2, then ℓ(D1 −D2) = deg(D1) −deg(D2) −1.
Proof. This can be proven using the Riemann-Roch formula and the fact that
deg(D1) −deg(D2) > deg(K).
□
2. Proof of main result
We will use the notation of the previous section. As mentioned in the intro-
duction, to study the structure of the 0-dimensional component of GX,D, we only
need to consider the case where D is a very ample divisor induced by a subgroup
H ≤Aut(X). Indeed, this follows from the following lemma.
Lemma 2.1. Let X be a compact Riemann surface of genus g ≥2 and G =
Aut(X). Then, GX,D has a 0-dimensional component if and only if D ∼DH for
some H ≤G with X/H ∼= P1.
Proof. Assume that GX,D has a 0-dimensional component, then by the decomposi-
tion of Equation (1) we have that there exists a subgroup H ≤G with X/H ∼= P1
such that ℓ(D −DH) = 1. Therefore, D ∼DH. Conversely, if there exists a sub-
group H ≤G with X/H ∼= P1 such that D ∼DH, then ℓ(D−DH) = ℓ(DH −DH) =
1, so GX,DH has a 0-dimensional component.
□
So, if we want to study the disjoint Galois subspaces we can assume that D is
induced by a subgroup H ≤G with X/H ∼= P1. Thus, we only need to calculate
ℓ(DH −DN) where N ≤Aut(X) and X/N ∼= P1. Using Lemma 1.5, we know
that DH is very ample if and only if deg(DH) ≥5. Furthermore, we have that
deg(DH) = |H|. Thus, we only consider DH when |H| ≥5.
It is known that if X is a compact Riemann surface of genus 2, then Aut(X) is
isomorphic to one of the following groups (see [5, Table 1]):
