GEOMETRIC INVERSE SEMIGROUP THEORY: A NOTE ON THE
MILNOR-SCHWARZ LEMMA FOR INVERSE MONOIDS
GIORGIO MANGIONI AND FRANCESCO TESOLIN
Abstract. We generalise the Milnor-Schwarz lemma to inverse monoids acting on presheaves of
geodesic metric spaces. We provide two proofs of this fact: one only uses elementary techniques,
inspired by the arguments for group actions on metric spaces; the other involves a version of the
Vietoris-Rips complex, and builds on work of Chung-Mart´ınez-Szak´acs.

Introduction
Where groups can be thought of as sets of symmetries, an inverse semigroup S is a set of partial
symmetries, that is, each element has a “local” inverse and their composition produces an idempotent
element (that is, one such that ee “ e). The set of all idempotents EpSq forms a meet-semilattice, with
the meet operation corresponding to multiplication. The fundamental example of an inverse semigroup
is the collection of partial bijections on a set, with each idempotent being the identity on some subset;
see Example 1.5 for details.
There has been a recent growing interest in geometric inverse semigroup theory [GK13, Mag21,
GSS22, CMS25], where the authors study inverse semigroups as extended metric spaces. For example,
given a semigroup S, together with a quasi-generating set M Ď S (that is, a subset which generates
S together with EpSq), one can equip S with the Cayley metric dM, which is the direct analogue of
the word metric for groups. However, up to this point, there has been no “proper” analogue of the
renowned Schwarz-Milnor lemma from geometric group theory, which allows one to study the geometry
of a group via the geometry of a metric space on which the group acts “nicely” [Sch55, Mil68].
To fill this gap, we first need the correct notion of action of an inverse semigroup, which is that of an
´etale action introduced in [Ste11] and studied further in [Tes25]. Namely, given a semigroup S, with
idempotents EpSq, we consider an action of S on a presheaf over EpSq: see Definition 1.9 for further
details. This approach of acting on a presheaf is an effective tool to generalise ideas from group theory,
as it recovers the usual definition of group action when S is a group (seen as an inverse monoid with
a unique idempotent).
In order to do geometric inverse semigroup theory, we then restrict to actions by 1-Lipschitz maps
on presheaves of geodesic metric spaces (see Definition 1.10). In this framework there are natural
analogues of the notions of properness and coboundedness, which we defer to Definition 2.1 below. We
now state our main theorem, which recovers the usual Schwarz-Milnor lemma for groups:
Theorem A. Let S be an inverse monoid, with identity element 1, and pX, EpSq, pq be a presheaf of
geodesic metric spaces. If S acts on X properly and coboundedly then:
(1) There exists a finite, quasi-generating set M Ď S.
(2) There exists x1 P X1 such that, if dM is the Cayley metric associated to M, the orbit map
pS, dMq Ñ X sending s P S to x1 ¨ s is an order-preserving quasi-isometry.
Note that, in general, one cannot hope to get a genuinely finite generating set. This is because any
meet-semilattice E with a top element is itself an inverse monoid, and if E is uncountable then it
cannot be finitely generated (see Remark 2.6).

Outline of the paper. In Section 1 we give the background on inverse semigroups and how they
act on presheaves of metric spaces. In Definition 1.14 we reinterpret the Cayley graph of an inverse
semigroup as a presheaf of geodesic metric spaces (the Sch¨utzenberger graphs).
In the two following sections, we give two proofs of Theorem A. The first is an elementary argument
adapting the proof of the usual Schwarz-Milnor lemma for groups. The second uses a theorem of
Chung-Mart´ınez-Szak´acs [CMS25], which roughly states that any two “Cayley-like” metrics on an
inverse semigroup are coarsely equivalent. In order to produce such a metric, we introduce an analogue
of the Vietoris-Rips complex for group actions, which we believe to be of independent interest.
Further Questions. This work provides further evidence that presheaves (of metric spaces) are the
natural objects on which inverse semigroups act.
Using our generalisation of the Schwarz-Milnor
lemma, we hope that more examples of quasi-finitely generated inverse monoids with interesting geo-
metric properties may be produced. In this direction, [GSS22] considered the properties of finitely
generated inverse monoids with hyperbolic Sch¨utzenberger graphs, so we ask the following vague ques-
tion:
Question B. What are the properties of a (finitely generated?) inverse monoid that acts properly and
coboundedly on a presheaf of CAT(0) (resp. systolic, injective, median) spaces?
The main results of [GSS22] show that, while finitely presented inverse monoids whose Sch¨utzenberger
graphs are quasitrees enjoy several nice algorithmic and language-theoretic properties, just requiring
hyperbolicity of the Sch¨utzenberger graphs is not even enough to get a decidable word problem.
However, besides the metric spaces appearing as fibers, we expect that the presheaf structure itself
might provide another element of flexibility in studying the geometry of inverse semigroups. In other
words, it might be possible to infer better properties of an inverse monoid by looking at actions
where not only the fibers are restricted to specific geometries (for example hyperbolicity), but also the
restriction maps of the presheaf satisfy suitable properties.

1. Background on inverse semigroups
Definition 1.1 (Inverse semigroup, domain and range). An inverse semigroup is a semigroup S such
that for all s P S there exists a unique s´1 P S such that ss´1s “ s and s´1ss´1 “ s´1. An inverse
monoid is an inverse semigroup S with an element 1 P S such that 1s “ s1 “ s for all s P S. For each
element s P S we call the element s´1s the domain of s and denote it by dom psq, and call ss´1 the
range of s and denote it by ran psq.
Definition 1.2 (Idempotents). An idempotent of an inverse semigroup S is an element e P S such
that ee “ e. The set of all idempotents in S is denoted by EpSq. Notice that, for every s P S, both
dom psq and ran psq are idempotents by construction. Furthermore, an immediate corollary of the
definition, as shown in [Law98], is that any two idempotents commute.
Remark 1.3. Note that a group is therefore an inverse semigroup with a unique idempotent element.
Definition 1.4 (Partial order). Let S be an inverse semigroup. Set s ď t if and only if s “ et for
some idempotent e P EpSq. As shown in [Law98], this is a partial order, termed the natural partial
order of S. With respect to the natural partial order, the idempotents EpSq form a meet-semilattice,
with meet operation e ^ f “ ef.
Example 1.5 (Partial bijections). Let X be a set, and let S be the collection of all partial bijections,
that is, bijections between subsets of X. S is an inverse monoid with the following multiplication:
given A, B, C, D subsets of X and partial bijections f : A Ñ B and g: C Ñ D, the product gf is the
map pg ˝ fq|f ´1pBXCq : f ´1pB X Cq Ñ gpB X Cq. Furthermore, given f : A Ñ B, the domain and range
of f are the identity maps on A and B, respectively. If e P S is an idempotent, then e is the identity
on some subset A Ď X; hence, given f, g P S, f ď g if and only if f is a restriction of g.

Remark 2.6. Notice that, in general, one cannot hope to get finite generation from an action on
a presheaf, without further assumptions on the structure of the poset EpSq. Indeed, let E be any
uncountable meet-semilattice with a top element 1e, and let G be any finitely generated group. Then
S “ E ˆ G is an inverse monoid, with multiplication pe, gq ¨ pf, hq “ pef, ghq and EpSq “ E. Hence
S is quasi-finitely generated, but cannot be finitely generated as it is uncountable. Since G can be
any finitely generated group, and since the restriction maps teu ˆ G Ñ tefu ˆ G are isometries, this
example also shows that no geometric properties of the fibers, nor of the restriction maps of a presheaf,
is enough to ensure finite generation.
