Light-Tunable Giant Anomalous Hall Effect in the Flat-Band Magnetic Weyl Semimetal AlFe2O4
Tingyan Chen,1, 2 Shengpu Huang,1, 2 Jing Fan,3 Dong-Hui Xu,1, 2 Rui Wang,1, 2, ∗and Da-Shuai Ma1, 2, †

2Center of Quantum materials and devices, Chongqing University, Chongqing 400044, People’s Republic of China
3Center for Computational Science and Engineering, Southern University of Science and Technology, Shenzhen 518055, China
Achieving a giant anomalous Hall effect (AHE) and enabling its effective tuning are fundamental goals for
topological spintronics. Magnetic Weyl semimetals hosting flat bands offer a promising route to maximize
the AHE. However, while theoretical models are well-established, realistic material candidates remain scarce.
Since the intrinsic anomalous Hall conductivity (AHC) is topologically dictated by the momentum separation
(κ) between Weyl nodes, actively manipulating remains a key challenge. Here, through comprehensive first-
principles calculations, we establish the inverse spinel AlFe2O4 as a realistic ferromagnetic half-metallic
platform integrating three-dimensional flat bands and Weyl physics.
Spin-orbit coupling induces a single
pair of Weyl nodes, yielding a giant intrinsic AHC of 398 S · cm−1. By constructing a symmetry-constrained
tight-binding model, we uncover a deterministic relationship between microscopic electronic couplings and the
macroscopic AHE. Exploiting this via Floquet engineering with circularly polarized light, we demonstrate that
the effective couplings are dynamically suppressed. This optical modulation controllably enlarges κ, shortens
the topological Fermi arcs, and drives a dramatic, quantitative suppression of the AHC, providing a practical
blueprint for ultrafast, light-controlled topological transport.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The anomalous Hall effect (AHE) remains one of the
most captivating transport phenomena in condensed matter
physics, fundamentally linking macroscopic charge currents
to the microscopic quantum geometry of Bloch electrons [1,
2].
Originating intrinsically from the momentum-space
Berry curvature, which acts as a fictitious magnetic field
induced by the interplay of time-reversal symmetry breaking
and spin-orbit coupling (SOC), the AHE enables transverse
current generation without the need for external magnetic
fields [3–6].
This dissipationless transport characteristic
makes the AHE highly desirable for next-generation low-
power spintronics and topological memory devices [7–12].
Recently, magnetic Weyl semimetals (WSMs) have emerged
as a fertile playground for exploring the AHE, as their defining
Weyl nodes act as singular magnetic monopoles of Berry
curvature [13–18]. In a magnetic WSM, the intrinsic AHC
is topologically constrained by the distribution of nodes with
opposite chirality. Theoretical studies have established that
for a minimal WSM with a single pair of Weyl nodes, the
intrinsic AHC peak is given as

topological currents. Although static tuning strategies such

been explored, they fundamentally lack the reversibility and
ultrafast speed required for practical device operation.
In
this context, Floquet engineering with periodic driving of a
light field emerges as a fascinating avenue [27–35].
As a
time-periodic perturbation, the light field provides a robust
optical mechanism to dynamically reconstruct electronic
bands and precisely tailor κ. Despite recent progress in light-
driven topological phase transitions [36–46], applying Floquet
engineering to realistic 3D flat band materials to dynamically
modulate the AHE remains an unexplored frontier.
This
raises a pivotal question:
Beyond bridging the gap from
theoretical models to physical realization, how can we
uncover the microscopic origins of to dynamically manipulate
macroscopic topological transport?

AlFe2O4 serves as a ferromagnetic half-metallic material

tunable AHC. In the absence of spin-orbit coupling (SOC),
its electronic structure features a three-component fermion

close proximity to the Fermi level. The inclusion of SOC lifts
this degeneracy, opening a gap at the Γ point and inducing
a single pair of Weyl nodes located at high-symmetry lines
−K ↔Γ ↔K with κ ≈0.292.
Consequently, the system
hosts a giant anomalous Hall conductivity (AHC) peak of
∼398 S · cm−1 near the Fermi level.
More interestingly,
by constructing a symmetry-allowed minimum tight-binding
model, we find that κ, which is linearly correlated with
the AHC, is highly sensitive to nearest-neighbor couplings.
Building on this mechanism, we employ Floquet engineering
via circularly polarized light (CPL) to dynamically modulate
the topological responses. This optical manipulation directly
results in a significant shortening of the Fermi arcs on
the (010) surface and a corresponding suppression of the
AHC. These results establish AlFe2O4 as a realistic material
platform for investigating the interplay between 3D flat bands
and Weyl physics, and provide a viable strategy for the optical
tuning of topological transport.
II.
COMPUTATIONAL METHODS
First-principles calculations were performed using the
Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [47, 48]
within the framework of density functional theory (DFT)
[49, 50].
The electron-ion interactions were described by
the projector-augmented-wave (PAW) method [51].
The
generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with the Perdew-
Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) parametrization was employed for the
exchange-correlation functional [52]. To properly account for
the strong electron correlation effects of the Fe 3d electrons,
the GGA+U method [53] was adopted with an effective
Hubbard U = 4 eV, consistent with previous theoretical
studies on AlFe2O4. The experimental lattice constants were
adopted [54], and the internal atomic positions were fully
relaxed until the residual forces on each atom were less than
0.02 eV/ ˚A.
The energy cutoff for the plane-wave basis
was set to 560 eV, and a 9 × 9 × 9 Monkhorst-Pack k-point
grid [55] was used for the Brillouin zone sampling. Spin-
orbit coupling (SOC) was included self-consistently where
specified.
To investigate the topological properties, we constructed
a tight-binding Hamiltonian using the maximally localized
Wannier functions (MLWFs) method [56–58].
Based on
the orbital-projected band structure (fat-band analysis), the
five 3d orbitals of the Fe atoms were selected as the
initial projectors to construct the MLWFs [59].
Starting
from this Wannier-based tight-binding (WFTB) Hamiltonian,
we introduced the light-matter interaction via the Peierls
substitution to derive a time-dependent Hamiltonian [60–62].
Here, the dipole approximation was applied, neglecting the
spatial variation of the light field due to its much longer
wavelength compared to the lattice constant [27]. In the high-
frequency (off-resonant) regime, where the Floquet bands are
well separated by a large photon energy ¯hω, an effective
static Hamiltonian can be derived using the high-frequency
expansion [60, 61].
We truncated this expansion to the
first order to accurately describe the photon-dressed band
structures. Finally, the topological surface states and Fermi
arcs were calculated from this effective Floquet Hamiltonian
using the WannierTools package [63].
III.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
In this work, we focus on aluminum ferrite, AlFe2O4,
which crystallizes in an inverse spinel structure [64, 65].
In a general normal spinel (AB2O4), the A and B cations
strictly occupy tetrahedral and octahedral sites, respectively.
However, an inverse spinel features a structural inversion
where the A cations and half of the B cations co-occupy the
octahedral network [66, 67].
Following this configuration,
as illustrated in Fig. 1(a), the Fe3+ ions in AlFe2O4 occupy
the tetrahedral A-sites (Wyckoff position 8a), while the Fe2+
and Al3+ ions jointly occupy the octahedral B-sites (Wyckoff
position 16d) within a face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice
(space group Fd¯3m, No. 227, with a = 5.67 ˚A) [67, 68].
Crucially, the octahedral B-sites in the spinel framework form
a three-dimensional pyrochlore network of corner-sharing
tetrahedra.
This unique geometric configuration is highly
conducive to destructive quantum interference, naturally
providing a structural basis for the emergence of electronic
flat bands [69].
Magnetically, AlFe2O4 exhibits a robust
ferromagnetic ground state.

Specifically, two quasi-flat bands are

quasi-flat bands.

(SOC) in this magnetic system, the low-energy electronic

Specifically,
as illustrated in Fig.

along the L ↔Γ ↔X ↔W path completely lose their
degeneracy under SOC, even though they largely retain their
flat dispersion profiles. Simultaneously, as shown in Fig. 2(b),
the high-fold degeneracy of the three-component fermion at
the Γ point is lifted, resulting in the opening of a distinct
band gap. To carefully examine the topological consequences
of this SOC-induced gap, we evaluated the energy difference
between the highest valence band and the lowest conduction
band. This analysis reveals gapless points corresponding to
a pair of Weyl nodes located at the fractional coordinates of
(0,0,±0.146) along the −K ↔Γ ↔K path. As highlighted
in Fig. 2(b), these nodes carry opposite chiral charges of +1
and −1, denoted by yellow and blue dots, respectively.
To visualize the topologically protected surface states, we
calculated the surface state spectrum on the semi-infinite
(010) surface, as presented in Fig.

topologically trivial (Ck3=0 = 0), whereas the k3 = π plane
is non-trivial (Ck3=π = 1).

While achieving a giant static AHC is highly desirable,
the ultimate goal for topological applications lies in its active
manipulation. As established in Eq. (1), dynamically tuning
the AHC requires uncovering the microscopic parameters that
dictate the Weyl node separation κ. To reveal the dominant
factors governing κ in the ferromagnetic Weyl semimetal
AlFe2O4, we construct a symmetry-allowed effective minimal
tight-binding (TB) model. Rather than employing a full multi-
orbital description, we capture the essential band dispersion
characteristics and topological features, particularly the quasi-
flat bands and Weyl nodes, by defining an effective s-orbital
model on the pyrochlore sublattice, which preserves the
crucial crystalline symmetries of the space group Fd¯3m (No.
227). The schematic of this lattice model and the considered
hopping pathways (t1 ∼t4) are illustrated in Fig. 3(a). The
primitive cell contains four equivalent sites (corresponding to
the Wyckoff position 16c), located at fractional coordinates
r1 = (0,0,0), r2 = (1/2,0,0), r3 = (0,1/2,0), and r4 =
(0,0,1/2).

IV.
SUMMARY AND OUTLOOK
In summary,
we have established the inverse spinel
AlFe2O4 as a realistic and ideal material platform that
beautifully integrates three-dimensional flat bands, Weyl
physics, and dynamically tunable anomalous Hall transport.
Through
comprehensive
first-principles
calculations
and
symmetry-constrained
tight-binding
modeling,
we
demonstrated that the interplay between the pyrochlore-
network-induced flat bands and spin-orbit coupling generates
a single pair of Weyl nodes, yielding a giant intrinsic
AHC of 398 S · cm−1 near the Fermi level.
Crucially,
we uncovered a deterministic relationship between the

From an experimental perspective, the physical realization
of our theoretical proposal is highly feasible.
First, high-
quality AlFe2O4 bulk single crystals have already been
successfully synthesized in experiments [64–66], providing
a solid material foundation for our predictions.
Second,
the proposed Floquet engineering relies on light intensities
(up to eA0/¯h = 0.10 ˚A−1) that are readily achievable in
current ultrafast laser experiments[27, 72].
The predicted
dynamic band reconstruction,
specifically the evolution
of the Weyl nodes and the shortening of the Fermi
arcs, can be directly visualized using time- and angle-
resolved photoemission spectroscopy (TrARPES)[27, 72].
Concurrently, the ultrafast suppression of the macroscopic
AHC can be precisely detected through ultrafast terahertz
transport measurements[31, 44, 73]. Ultimately, our findings
not only bridge the gap between abstract flat-band Weyl
models and realistic materials but also provide a practical
blueprint for developing ultrafast, light-controlled topological
spintronic devices.
