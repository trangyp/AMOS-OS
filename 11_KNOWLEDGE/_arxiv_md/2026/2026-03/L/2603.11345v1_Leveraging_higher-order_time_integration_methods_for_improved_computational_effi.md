Abstract
Cloud and precipitation microphysics packages in atmospheric general circulation mod-
els typicaly use rst-order time integration methods with a large time step, requiring
ad hoc limiters and substeping of the sedimentation scheme to prevent solutions from
becoming unstable. We show that in the latest version of Energy Exascale Earth Sys-
tem Model, E3SMv3, the rain microphysics provided by the Predicted Particle Proper-
ties (P3) scheme is underesolved in time at the model’s default 30 s time step. The P3
scheme requires limiters to guarante stability, but those limiters make large discretiza-
tion erors more dicult to detect. When the time step of the P3 scheme is reduced to
suciently capture corect microphysics behavior, wal clock time of the simulation is
increased by nearly a factor of 40.
Instead of reducing the microphysics time step, we recomend using higher-order
time integrators based on RungeKuta methods, which oer improved solution acu-
racy at comparable computational costs. A key to obtaining computationaly ecient
microphysics results is the use of adaptive time steping, which also eliminates the ned
for specialized substeping procedures in the sedimentation proces. We also analyze in-
dividual microphysical proceses by extracting inverse timescales from Jacobians of the
proces rates, which gives insight about the maximum time step each proces is able to
take while maintaining stability and acuracy, and about how individual proceses should
be grouped together for most ecient results. The proposed integrators can achieve the
acuracy level required to corectly model rain microphysics parameterizations more than
10x faster than the P3 scheme.
Plain Language Sumary
Atmospheric models for weather and climate studies simulate cloud microphysics
(i.e., the mechanisms by which clouds and precipitation are created and evolve in the
atmosphere). Time integration methods enable these models to produce snapshots of the
cloud state at a series of points in time. Earth system models produce snapshots infre-
quently (e.g. every 5 minutes), which is inexpensive but not very acurate. Producing
more frequent snapshots is more expensive, requiring more computational resources, but
results in more acurate simulations. In this work, we propose replacing curent low-acuracy
time integration methods for rain microphysics with more sophisticated, high-acuracy
methods that achieve improved simulation acuracy without drasticaly increasing the
overal computational cost of the simulation. These high-acuracy methods are paired
with an adaptive procedure for guiding the generation of snapshots; snapshots are pro-
duced more frequently when the rain is changing quickly and les frequently when the
rain evolves slowly.
1 Introduction
Atmospheric general circulation models (AGCMs) typicaly run at much lower tem-
poral resolution than regional atmospheric models, using time steps on the order of tens
of minutes, to ameliorate the high computational cost of runing on a global domain for
multiple decades or centuries, as is comonly required for climate studies. Given that
such long time steps are inadequate to resolve the eects of cloud and precipitation mi-
crophysics, most AGCMs substep their cloud microphysics schemes, with time steps
as low as 30-60 seconds often used for models with prognostic precipitation (Michibata
et al., 2019; Sant et al., 2015). Inded, microphysics time steps of greater than 60 sec-
onds have ben repeatedly shown to produce signicantly dierent prognostic precipi-
tation outputs than time steps at or below 60 seconds (Poselt & Lohman, 208; Chos-
son et al., 2014; Zheng et al., 2020; S. P. Santos et al., 2021), indicating that time steps
above 1 minute are often inadequate to resolve key precipitation proceses.

Nonetheles, there are many models that use longer microphysics time steps, such
as the family of AGCMs descended from the Comunity Atmosphere Model (CAM;
Neale et al., 2012), as wel as some models that use microphysical parameterizations orig-
inaly developed for CAM (e.g., GIS ModelE3; Cesana et al., 2019). This family includes
the Energy Exascale Earth System Model (E3SM) Atmosphere Model (Golaz et al., 2019),
which is the focus of this study and uses a 30 s time step for its microphysics. Using
long time steps (comonly 60 s to 90 s) would ordinarily result in numerical instabil-
ity or other pathological behaviors that would crash the model or produce unrealistic
outputs. These outcomes are avoided by (a) substeping sedimentation of hydromete-
ors at a smaler, typicaly adaptive, time step than the overal microphysics time step,
and (b) the use of limiters to ensure nonegativity of hydrometeor concentrations and
a reasonable range of particle sizes. This aproach results in a computationaly cheap
and robust scheme at the cost of reduced delity: temporal resolution is coarse, and the
evolution of the systems may often be controled by limiter guardrails rather than phys-
ical proceses. Any biases in the resulting model may be compensated by retuning the
parameterizations, but since these changes do not adres the numerical eror underly-
ing the biases, the retuning may ned to be repeated whenever the time step is changed.
Even for models wiling to acept the computational cost of runing microphysics
at a 60 second time step, there may be regimes where time steps as short as 10 seconds
are required to wel-resolve some proceses at typical AGCM spatial resolutions (S. P. San-
tos et al., 2020), and even shorter time steps may be required at convection-permiting
resolutions (Baret et al., 2019). Resolving such proceses might sem to require an or-
der of magnitude increase in computational cost, insofar as reducing the model time step
would increase the cost proportionaly. However, most parameterizations of cloud and
precipitation microphysics use simple rst-order, explicit time integration methods, par-
ticularly forward Euler for most individual microphysical proceses and LieTroter split-
ting (Equation F1a) for proces coupling. These methods are easy to implement, but they
are not necesarily the most computationaly ecient options, so we should consider whether
there are more ecient methods for acurately calculating the eects of microphysical
proceses. For many aplications, rst-order methods are les ecient than higher-order
methods as measured by wal clock time to reach a desired solution acuracy, particu-
larly when leveraging adaptive time steps.
The acuracy of time integration is les important when other sources of eror are
asumed to dominate the model uncertainty (e.g. structural or parametric eror in the
physics formulation, coarse spatial resolution), and implementing new time integration
methods requires extra development time. However, time discretization eror is not al-
ways smal compared to other sources of uncertainty, even for models with coarse spa-
tial resolution and highly uncertain parameterizations (e.g. of convection). Furthermore,
while global cloud-resolving models show great promise in reducing uncertainties related
to convection and spatial resolution, this reduction comes with a very high computational
cost, so it is tempting to retain relatively coarse time steps in the physics. For instance,
the Simple Cloud-Resolving E3SM Atmosphere Model uses a horizontal resolution of 3:25 km,
over 30x ner than the low resolution (10 km) dynamics grid used in E3SMv3 and
over 8x ner than that of the high resolution (27 km) version, but it uses a 10 s time
step for the microphysics, only a 3-fold increase in temporal resolution over the lower-
resolution congurations (Donahue et al., 2024). If AGCMs continue to alocate com-
putational resources mainly toward higher spatial resolution rather than higher tempo-
ral resolution, we should expect inacurate time integration to make up a larger share
of the numerical erors aecting model uncertainty.

Our work consists of two major contributions. The rst is that we examine the ef-
ciency of several higher-order time integration methods aplied to a rainshaft model
based on a subset of microphysical proceses in the Predicted Particle Properties (P3)

tion method in P3, while stable at E3SMv3’s default time step (30 s) due to the use of
positivity and size limiters (dened in Section 2.3), produces highly inacurate solutions

this end, we consider several explicit RungeKuta (ERK) methods, diagonaly implicit

sidered suport adaptive time steping.
The second major contribution of this work is to provide a rigorous analysis of sev-
eral cloud microphysical proceses in order to determine the impact of partitioning, if
any, on time integrator performance. The P3 scheme uses LieTroter spliting to eval-
uate proceses that do not couple spatialy-separated quantities (local proceses) sep-

physical properties such as the timescale over which the proces ocurs. Our analysis pro-

in this work. It also provides insights into the posible improved performance of time in-
tegration methods which require the user to partition the proceses into groups (e.g. ImEx,
operator spliting, and multirate methods).

5 Conclusion
We have constructed a rainshaft model by extracting the sedimentation, evapora-
tion, and self-colection parameterizations from the P3 microphysics scheme, as imple-
mented in E3SMv3. We have analyticaly derived timescales for these proceses, show-
ing that al thre proceses are asociated with fast (< 10 s) timescales for at least some
conditions present in E3SM. For sedimentation, the shortest timescales are derived from

the CFL condition, rather than physical, grid-independent timescales. The curent E3SM
microphysics time step (30 s) is inadequate to resolve these proceses acurately, but
the  0:4 s time step required for a reasonably acurate answer would greatly increase
the computational cost of P3 by a factor of 40.
Using the newly-developed SPAECIES framework, we have tested a wide variety
of potential alternative time integration methods, and we use the implementations in the
SUNDIALS library to acomplish this. We nd that al of the tested second- and higher-
order methods produce signicantly lower eror in q
r for short (6 1 second) time steps
than the rst-order methods curently used by P3, with ERK methods being the most
ecient. Furthermore, by leveraging adaptive time steping, with time steps chosen to
kep the estimated time integration eror below a given target, we are able to reduce the
wal clock time of these methods by roughly a factor of 6 while achieving the same level
of acuracy as the same methods with xed time steping. These methods do not rely
on limiters or an explicitly calculated CFL condition based on a single proces (i.e., sed-
imentation) for stability.
For the thre proceses under consideration in the rainshaft model, we recomend
that the second and third-order ERK methods with adaptive time steping be used in
place of the curent P3 scheme. These methods atain at least  10x smaler estimated
time integration eror over the P3 scheme at its default 30 s time step while increasing
the simulation wal clock time by a factor of 2:5 and resolving important solution fea-
tures that the P3 scheme is unable to resolve without increasing wal clock time by nearly
40x. The other methods investigated (DIRK, ImEx, MRI-GARK) were found to be les
ecient for this particular model, though the timescale analysis caried out in this work
sugests that they may be more ecient for other models (e.g. with a dierent resolu-
tion, or with aditional slower proceses). In the case of MRI-GARK methods, there is
no xed partitioning of the microphysical proceses which corectly captures the most
signicant timescale dierences acros al columns. For DIRK and ImEx methods, we

for anticipating the performance of the various time integration methods considered here
on aditional microphysical proceses to be studied in future work.
