UNEXPECTED TORIC RICHARDSON VARIETIES
EUGENE GORSKY, SOYEON KIM, AND MELISSA SHERMAN-BENNETT
Abstract. We prove that an open Richardson variety in the complete flag variety for GLn is isomorphic
to a torus if and only if the corresponding closed Richardson variety is toric. Such toric varieties can
be classified in terms of the combinatorics of Bruhat intervals, and include many varieties of dimension
larger than n −1. We give a combinatorial description of the corresponding polytopes, and compute
several explicit examples.
1. Introduction
Richardson varieties arise in various contexts in algebraic geometry and combinatorics, including Schu-
bert calculus, total positivity, and cluster algebras. Given two permutations v, w ∈Sn such that v ≤w
in Bruhat order, the open Richardson variety Rv,w is the intersection of the opposite Schubert cell for
v and the Schubert cell for w. Open Richardson varieties are smooth, affine, irreducible varieties, and
dim Rv,w = ℓ(w)−ℓ(v). The closed Richardson variety Rv,w is the Zariski closure of Rv,w in the complete
flag variety Fl(n). This paper is concerned with the closed Richardson varieties which are toric varieties.
Recall that Rv,w is T-toric for some torus T if there is a T-action on Rv,w with an open orbit. We say
Rv,w is toric if it is T-toric for some T.
The (n −1)-dimensional torus T ⊂SL(n) of diagonal matrices with determinant 1 acts on the flag
variety and on all Richardson varieties. It is well-known (see [2, 8, 15, 20, 30, 37] and references therein)
that Rv,w is T-toric if and only if the roots associated to any maximal chain from v to w are linearly
independent. By construction, all T-toric Richardson varieties have dimension at most n −1.
It is also possible for a Richardson variety Rv,w to be toric with respect to some other torus T. This
situation has been much less studied. As shown in [8, Corollary 3.3], if Rv,w is T-toric, then the interval
[v, w] is a lattice, or, equivalently, has no subintervals which are 2-crowns (a 2-crown is the Bruhat graph
of S3 shown below).

The first main result of this paper is that, if the open Richardson variety Rv,w is a torus T, then Rv,w
is a toric variety.
Theorem 1.1. Suppose the open Richardson variety Rv,w is isomorphic to a torus T. Then the action
of T on Rv,w extends to the closed Richardson variety Rv,w and Rv,w is a toric variety with respect to T.
We then show that this is in fact a characterization of toric Richardsons, and also provide various
equivalent combinatorial characterizations.
Theorem 1.2. The following conditions are equivalent:
(a) The open Richardson variety Rv,w is a torus.
(b) The closed Richardson variety Rv,w is a toric variety.
(c) The Bruhat interval [v, w] does not contain a subinterval isomorphic to S3.
(d) The Bruhat interval [v, w] is a lattice.
(e) The interval poset {[v′, w′] : [v′, w′] ⊂[v, w]} ∪{∅}, ordered by containment, is a lattice.

exercise in poset theory; we include a proof in Lemma 2.17 for completeness. The implication (d) =⇒
(c) is straightforward, as S3 is not a lattice. The reverse implication (c) =⇒(d) is an unpublished result

We call toric Richardson varieties unexpected if dim Rv,w = d > n−1, since in this case they are not
T-toric. Unexpected toric Richardsons are toric with respect to a torus action that does not extend to the
entire flag variety. We give a number of explicit examples of unexpected toric Richardsons in Section 5.
One nice infinite family is given by the “large hypercube” intervals recently found in [14]; in this case,
d = O(n log n), which shows the dimensions of unexpected toric Richardsons can grow quite large. As
explained in [14, Section 3.4], under the assumptions of Theorem 1.2 the closed Richardson variety Rv,w
is smooth if and only if [v, w] is a hypercube.
One way to understand a toric Richardson Rv,w is to study its moment polytope bP(v, w). For T-toric
Richardsons, the moment polytope is the Bruhat interval polytope P(v, w) of [28], see [37]. For T-toric
Richardsons, the face lattice of P(v, w) is the interval poset Int[v, w], which consists of the subintervals
of [v, w] and ∅, ordered by containment. In particular, the Hasse diagram of [v, w] is the 1-skeleton of
P(v, w). A similar result holds for arbitrary toric Richardsons.
Theorem 1.3. The moment polytope bP(v, w) of the toric Richardson Rv,w has face lattice isomorphic
to Int[v, w]. That is, the s-dimensional faces of bP(v, w) are in bijection with subintervals [v′, w′] ⊂[v, w]
with ℓ(w′) −ℓ(v′) = s, and this bijection respects containment.
In particular, the 1-skeleton of bP(v, w) is the Hasse diagram of [v, w].
One nice consequence of our results is a characterization of when Int[v, w] is the face lattice of a
polytope. We note that if [v, w] is a 2-crown, then Int[v, w] is not a lattice so cannot be the face lattice
of a polytope. In fact, 2-crowns are the only obstacle for Int[v, w] being a face lattice.
Corollary 1.4. The following are equivalent:
(a) The interval poset Int[v, w] of sub-intervals of [v, w] is the face lattice of a polytope.
(b) The Bruhat interval [v, w] does not have any 2-crowns.
(c) The Bruhat interval [v, w] is a lattice.
Another way to understand toric Richardson varieties is to study projections to Grassmannians. By
[25, 26], the projection of Rv,w to Gr(k, n) is a positroid variety, which is indexed by, among other things,
a positroid and a plabic graph. We show that all projections of toric Rv,w are T-toric positroid varieties,
whose corresponding plabic graphs Gk(v, w) are forests. We can also understand the moment polytope
bP(v, w) using positroid polytopes, which are T-moment polytopes of positroid varieties.
Theorem 1.5. Assume that Rv,w is toric. The moment polytope bP(v, w) is the Minkowski sum of affine
transformations of positroid polytopes indexed by Gk(v, w).
Theorem 1.5 generalizes the fact that Bruhat interval polytopes are Minkowski sums of positroid
polytopes. Note that the affine transformations in Theorem 1.5 in general depend on k, and map the
positroid polytopes contained in Rn−1 to the space Rd corresponding to T.
Though we do not pursue this direction here, the motivation for this paper arose from torus actions
from cluster structures. In recent years, it was proved [9, 19, 18] that the open Richardson variety Rv,w
has a cluster structure. As a consequence, one can construct [29, 24] an action of a potentially larger
torus T on Rv,w, where the dimension of T equals the number of frozen variables in the cluster structure1.
This leads to the following question.
Question 1.6. Does the action of T on Rv,w extend to the closed Richardson variety Rv,w?
Theorem 1.1 answers Question 1.6 in the positive in the case when T has maximal dimension.
The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we give background on Richardson varieties,
positroid varieties, and related combinatorics. In Section 3, we characterize the closed Richardson varieties
which are toric and investigate their projections to the Grassmannian. In Section 4 we discuss the moment
polytopes of toric Richardson varieties. Finally, Section 5 has a number of examples, including two infinite
families of unexpected toric Richardson varieties.

2.6. A lemma on posets. Let P be a poset and let Int(P) be the set of intervals of P, together with
the empty set. We consider Int(P) to be a poset with respect to containment, so ∅is the minimal element
of Int(P). The following lemma will be useful to us.
Lemma 2.17. A finite poset P is a lattice if and only if Int(P) is a lattice.
Proof. Suppose P is a lattice with meet ∧and join ∨. Consider [x, p], [y, q] ∈Int(P). By the definition
of meet and join, [a, b] contains [x, p] and [y, q] if and only if [a, b] contains [x ∧y, p ∨q]. So [x ∧y, p ∨q] is
the least upper bound of [x, p] and [y, q]. A similar argument shows that [x∨y, p∧q] is the greatest lower
bound of [x, y] and [p, q]. The least upper bound of ∅and [x, y] is [x, y], and the greatest lower bound is
∅. Thus, Int(P) is a lattice.
If Int(P) is a lattice, then it has a maximal element. This maximal element contains all intervals [x, x]
so must be the poset P. That is, P is itself an interval and so must have a minimal element ˆ0 and a
maximal element ˆ1. The poset P is isomorphic to the interval [[ˆ0, ˆ0], [ˆ0, ˆ1]] in Int(P). Since subintervals
of lattices are lattices, P is a lattice.
□
