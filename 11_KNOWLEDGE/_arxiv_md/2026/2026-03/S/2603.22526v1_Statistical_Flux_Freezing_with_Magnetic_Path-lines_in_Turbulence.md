Statistical Flux Freezing with Magnetic Path-lines in Turbulence
Amir Jafari∗
The classical Alfv´en flux–freezing theorem states that, in ideal magnetohydrodynamics, magnetic
flux is transported by the flow and magnetic field lines remain frozen into the plasma. In turbulent
plasmas, however, the velocity and magnetic fields are spatially rough, invalidating the regularity
assumptions underlying the classical theorem. Previous work has shown that Lagrangian trajectories
in such rough flows can become nonunique in the limit of small magnetic diffusivity, leading to
stochastic formulations of magnetic flux freezing based on magnetic field lines. Field lines, however,
are instantaneous geometric objects that do not possess a natural time evolution and do not preserve
any identity in turbulent flows.
Recent work has proposed an alternative description based on
magnetic path-lines, which are time-parametrized trajectories generated by an effective transport
field constructed from the Eulerian magnetic field. This formulation provides dynamical objects in
spacetime and has been shown to recover known reconnection rates and to define magnetic topology
change within a mathematically well-defined framework. In this work we use path-lines to formulate
a statistical flux-freezing theorem in turbulent plasmas.
Introducing a stochastic regularization
of the path-line equation ˙X(t) = B(X(t), t), we study the zero–diffusivity limit of the resulting
stochastic dynamics. We show that if backward pair dispersion persists in this limit, the transition
kernel of the stochastic path-line dynamics cannot collapse to a Dirac measure. Magnetic path-lines
therefore remain intrinsically stochastic in the ideal limit, implying that magnetic flux is conserved
only in the statistical sense over ensembles of backward-advected path-line surfaces. This yields
a statistical formulation of Alfv´en’s theorem in terms of magnetic path-lines and shows that the
classical deterministic form of flux freezing cannot hold in sufficiently rough turbulent magnetic
fields. While closely related in physical content to earlier stochastic flux-freezing approaches based
on magnetic field lines, the path-line formulation provides time-evolving dynamical trajectories that
retain identity in spacetime and offer a simpler and more transparent framework for analyzing
stochastic magnetic transport.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The classical Alfv´en flux–freezing theorem [1] in ideal
magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), describing the transport
of magnetic flux by the flow, has played a central role in
plasma physics. However, the presumed ideal regime is
extremely sensitive to initial conditions and can quickly
give rise to turbulence. The classical derivation of flux
freezing relies on implicit regularity assumptions for the
velocity and magnetic fields. In particular, it assumes
that Lagrangian trajectories of the flow are uniquely de-
fined. In turbulence this assumption can easily fail, as
the velocity field may become only H¨older continuous
with exponent h = 1/3 < 1, well below the Lipschitz
regularity required by the Picard–Lindel¨of theorem for
uniqueness of trajectories [2, 3]. This lack of regularity is
closely related to the Onsager conjecture [3–5] concerning
anomalous dissipation in turbulent flows.
When the advecting field is sufficiently rough, La-
grangian trajectories can become non-unique in the
limit of vanishing viscosity or diffusivity.
This phe-
nomenon is known as spontaneous stochasticity [6]. In-
stead of converging to a single deterministic trajectory,
the vanishing–noise limit can produce a probability dis-
tribution of possible particle histories. In the context of
magnetized turbulence, this behavior has important im-
plications for magnetic transport and flux freezing [7].

Field lines, however, are geometric objects defined only

plasmas, field lines do not preserve any identity as the
magnetic field evolves [8].
In this paper we adopt a different viewpoint and for-

proposed in [9]. These are trajectories X(t) generated by

The central question is whether the regularized stochas-

spread of possible histories persists.
A useful diagnostic of this behavior is backward pair

in time. If the dynamics were deterministic in the ideal

natural indicator of spontaneous stochasticity.

C.
Stochastic path-line freezing
Theorem II.1 implies that persistent pair dispersion
prevents collapse of stochastic magnetic path-lines to
unique deterministic curves in the ideal limit. Instead,
the limiting dynamics must be described statistically by
probability measures on path space.
We refer to this
phenomenon as stochastic path-line freezing.
Corollary II.2 (Stochastic path-line freezing). When-
ever the pair–dispersion condition (7) holds, magnetic
path-lines are not frozen as unique deterministic curves.
Instead the dynamics induces an ensemble of admissible
path histories in the zero–noise limit.
This stochasticity provides a kinematic Lagrangian
mechanism by which magnetic connectivity and trans-
port can evolve in highly conducting turbulent flows. A
full dynamical theory of reconnection, however, requires
coupling to the induction equation and energy balance.

III.
DISCUSSION
The formulation developed in the present work ap-
proaches flux freezing from a different perspective than
previous treatments. Instead of using magnetic field lines
as the primary objects, we consider time–parametrized
magnetic path-lines generated by an effective transport
field B(x, t). Because these curves evolve in time, they
can be analyzed using the standard tools of Lagrangian
dynamics and stochastic processes. In ideal incompress-
ible MHD with negligible bulk flow (u ≈0 and ρ =
const), the equation ˙x = B coincides with propagation of
Alfv´enic disturbances at the Alfv´en speed, so the path-
lines describe genuine signal transport along the mag-
netic field. In general non-ideal, turbulent, or advecting
plasmas (u ̸= 0), these curves should not be interpreted
as trajectories of material carriers nor as exact character-
istics of the full MHD system (whose propagation direc-
tions are given by the Els¨asser velocities u±B). Rather,
they represent geometrically preferred pathways along
which magnetic tension communicates disturbances and
along which magnetic structure reorganizes in space-
time. Their physical significance is therefore operational:
the stochastic divergence of nearby path-lines quantifies
the rate at which magnetic configurations decorrelate.
In particular, this dispersion reproduces both laminar
Sweet–Parker reconnection and the turbulent Lazarian–
Vishniac scaling, analogously to Richardson dispersion
in stochastic flux-freezing theories. In fact, the coarse-
grained induction equation implies that the filtered Eu-
lerian field Bℓdoes not evolve as if it were simply frozen
into uℓ, nor necessarily as if it propagated purely with
uℓ± Bℓ.
Within this framework the key diagnostic of determin-
istic behavior is the collapse of backward trajectories ar-
riving at the same spacetime point. If the classical flux–
freezing picture were valid, two such trajectories would
coincide at earlier times in the limit of vanishing diffu-
sivity. The analysis presented above shows that this col-
lapse fails whenever backward pair dispersion persists.
In that situation the transition kernel of the stochastic
path-line dynamics cannot converge to a Dirac measure,
and the ideal-limit transport process remains intrinsically
stochastic.
As a consequence, magnetic transport cannot in gen-
eral be described by a unique deterministic mapping of
path-lines in sufficiently rough turbulent magnetic fields.
Instead, magnetic transport must be described statis-
tically through probability distributions on path histo-
ries. In this setting the classical deterministic form of
the Alfv´en flux–freezing theorem breaks down. Never-
theless, a statistical form of flux freezing survives: mag-
netic flux is conserved in the ensemble sense over ran-
dom surfaces obtained by backward advection along the
stochastic path-line flow (see Sec. II and Appendix B).
This statistical flux-freezing law arises naturally from
the stochastic path-line formulation.
When backward
trajectories fail to collapse, the preimage of a surface
under the path-line flow becomes a random ensemble of
surfaces. Magnetic flux is therefore not preserved along a
single deterministic mapping, but only in the mean over
the ensemble of stochastic path-line histories.
The mechanism underlying this behavior is sponta-
neous stochasticity. Persistent pair dispersion prevents
the stochastic regularization of the path-line equation
from collapsing to a single deterministic trajectory in the
zero–diffusivity limit. The resulting stochastic transport
of magnetic structures provides a natural Lagrangian
mechanism by which magnetic connectivity can evolve
even when the microscopic nonideal effects are arbitrar-
ily small. Importantly, this evolution should not be un-
derstood as motion of individual field lines or flux tubes.
The stochastic path-lines encode transport of magnetic
pattern rather than transport of a localized physical ob-
ject. In this sense they are analogous to quasiparticles
such as phonons or photons in field theory: asking “what
exactly moves” along these trajectories is no more mean-

The stochastic path-line picture also has implications
for magnetic reconnection.
Classical reconnection the-
ories often assume that magnetic connectivity evolves
through localized nonideal effects, while the large-scale
magnetic field remains approximately frozen into the
flow. In turbulent plasmas, however, the stochastic trans-
port implied by spontaneous stochasticity allows mag-
netic connectivity to evolve through turbulent disper-
sion of magnetic structures. Recent work based on the
path-line formulation has derived expressions for rates
of magnetic connectivity change in turbulent flows [9].
The present results provide a complementary foundation
for such approaches by establishing that the underlying
path-line dynamics may remain intrinsically stochastic
in the ideal limit. Within this viewpoint, reconnection is
associated with rapid reorganization of magnetic pattern
rather than with the identity of individual field lines. Ex-
ponential separation of nearby path-lines quantifies the
rate at which magnetic configurations decorrelate, while
loss of continuity of the bundle flow corresponds to gen-
uine topological change.
A quantitative theory of reconnection rates requires
coupling the present path-line framework to the full in-
duction equation and the dynamics of the magnetic and
velocity fields. The analysis presented here establishes
the kinematic ingredient of this picture by showing that
the ideal-limit path-line dynamics can remain intrinsi-
cally stochastic.

The path-line viewpoint also highlights the difference
between time-evolving trajectories and instantaneous ge-
ometric objects. Magnetic field lines are defined only at
a fixed time and require an auxiliary curve parameter,
whereas path-lines are ordinary dynamical trajectories
in spacetime. Treating these trajectories as the basic ob-
jects provides a direct way to analyze stochastic transport
mechanisms arising from rough advecting fields. Math-
ematically, the construction defines a closed dynamical
system on the tangent bundle with base coordinates x
and fiber coordinates B, since ˙B is determined by the
induction equation. This base–fiber structure admits a
natural Riemannian geometry given by the Sasaki met-
ric, providing a canonical notion of distance, continuity,
and topology for evolving magnetic configurations.
It is also important to emphasize that the present anal-
ysis is purely kinematic. It establishes the noncollapse of
path-line dynamics under persistent pair dispersion, but
does not determine reconnection rates or energy conver-
sion. Addressing those questions requires coupling the
stochastic path-line description to the induction equa-
tion and to the dynamics of the magnetic and velocity
fields. In such an extension the effective transport field
B(x, t) would evolve consistently with the MHD equa-
tions, allowing the stochastic path-line dynamics to be
embedded in a fully dynamical model.
