Abstract
We present a study of minimal unsatisfiable subsets (MUSs) of 2-CNF Boolean formulas, building on
the Abbasizanjani-Kullmann classification of minimally unsatisfiable 2-CNFs (2-MUs). First we give
a linear-time procedure for recognising 2-MUs. Then we study the problem of finding one simple
MUS. On the one hand we extend the results by Kleine Büning et al, which showed NP-completeness
of the decision, whether a deficiency-1 MUS exists. On the other hand we show that deciding/finding
an MUS containing one or two unit-clauses (which are special deficiency-1 MUSs) can be done in
polynomial time. Finally we present an incremental polynomial time algorithm for some special
type of MUSs, namely those MUSs containing at least one unit-clause.
We conclude by discussing the main open problem, developing a deeper understanding of the
landscape of easy/hard MUSs of 2-CNFs.

Introduction
An unsatisfiable CNF formula in general contains many “sources” of unsatisfiability, and the
main approach is to consider minimally unsatisfiable subsets (MUSs for short). MUSs serve
as root causes of infeasibility, and find applications in diagnosis, error localisation, product
configuration, model checking (CEGAR), and redundancy removal.
Considering complete enumeration of all MUSs, an early paper is [16]. An important
milestone was given by [15], which captures much of the progress in the field, and presents
the MARCO framework. A general tool set is provided in [4], while a probabilistic approach
to approximate counting is introduced in [5].
Since we are focusing on the special input class 2-CNF, enumeration of MUSs for special
classes is of importance to us. It appears the only work available here is on Horn CNFs (at
most one positive literal in each clause). [18, Theorem 5] shows MUS enumeration with
polynomial delay for Horn CNFs. They also make a remark on a special case of Horn CNFs,
which is worth discussing here. Namely they consider “core KBs”, which are 2-CNFs with only
mixed clauses, containing one positive and one negative literal (which are always satisfiable).
On [18, Page 282] they make the remark, that core KBs allow finding minimal axiom sets with
polynomial delay, by enumerating paths (see [19] for a recent paper on enumerating paths).
Our situation is more complex, since we are considering general 2-CNFs, where one has to
consider the problem of complementary literals occurring on a path (“contradictory paths”).
So even in the simplest case of fixing two unit-clauses (the situation we call “Family I”), a
more elaborate algorithm for finding one MUS is needed, and enumeration with polynomial
delay is not known. And if we relax the condition, and allow more general forms of MUSs

Deciding 2-MU
Our first result is that 2-MU can be decided in linear time (while the trivial algorithm,
using SAT-decision for 2-CNF in linear time, needs quadratic time). Our main tool is
checked singular DP-reduction, and the algorithm for deciding 2-MU runs checked singular
DP-reduction, and if the reduction was successful, checks the final result (depending on the
deficiency 1 or ≥2). Singular DP-reduction has been applied in the literature to reduce MUs,
see for example [13] for a fundamental study. [13, Lemma 9] indeed spells out the conditions
such that we can check for MU, and this is our basis for “checked” sDP-reduction (which
may fail):
▶Definition 4.1. Recall that for F ∈CLS a variable v ∈var(F) is called singular if
ldF (v) = 1 or ldF (v) = 1. A main clause is the clause C ∈F containing the chosen
literal v resp. v occurring only once (there is a choice iff ldF (v) = ldF (v) = 1), while the
side clauses D1, . . . , Dm ∈F for m ≥0 are the clauses (without duplication) containing
the complemented literal v resp. v. The sDP-check for v, F fails if one of the following
conditions holds:
(i) m = 0;
(ii) there is i ∈{1, . . . , m} such that C and Di clash in a variable other than v;
(iii) there are i, j ∈{1, . . . , m}, i ̸= j, such that Di \ C = Dj \ C;
(iv) there is i ∈{1, . . . , m} and E ∈F with v /∈var(E) and Di \ C = E \ C.4
For F ∈CLS and v ∈VA a checked sDP-reduction step (csDP-reduction) results in
FAIL, if the sDP-check fails, and otherwise results in
F ′ := (F \ {C, D1, . . . , Dm}) ∪{(C ∪Di) \ {v, v} : i ∈{1, . . . , m}} ∈CLS.
A full csDP-reduction for F ∈CLS applies csDP-reduction (non-deterministically) as
long as either a failure is reached, in which case FAIL is the result, or otherwise until no
singular variable exists anymore, in which case the result is the final F ′ ∈CLS.
The following fundamental property of csDP-reduction follows directly from [13, Lemma 9]
(and is also easy to check directly):
▶Lemma 4.2. For F ∈CLS holds:
1. If full csDP-reduction fails, then F /∈MU.
2. Otherwise for the resulting F ′ holds F ∈MU ⇔F ′ ∈MU.

NP-completeness of finding simple MUSs
We now start investigating “simple” MUSs of 2-CNFs F.
Considering the complexity
parameter deficiency for MU, the simplest MUSs of F have deficiency one. [6] showed that
deciding the existence of an MUS of deficiency one, that is, whether MU(F) ∩MUδ=1 ̸= ∅
holds, is NP-complete, based on the NP-completeness of deciding whether a digraph contains
two vertex-disjoint paths between two given pairs of vertices. We can give now a direct proof
of this (not using “read-once resolution” as in [6]), directly using that Families III, IV (recall
Subsection 3.1) contain two “independent” paths. This allows also to pinpoint the source
of hardness more precisely, and we will later see, that Families I, II, containing only one
“independent” path, can be found efficiently.

having to handle these trivial cases. Expanding on [6, Definition 4.2]:
▶Definition 5.3. A C-DPP instance is an st-digraph G:
G has a special closed walk if there is a path P1 from s to t and a path P2 from t to s.
If such paths exist with V (P1) ∩V (P2) = {s, t}, then we say that G has a special cycle.
Here “DPP” stands for “disjoint path problem”, while “C” stands for “cyclic”. For examples
see Section A.
We are ready to prove that FC(G) encodes the two special paths from a special cycle
into the two implication chains constituting the MUs of Family III (recall Subsection 3.1):
▶Theorem 5.4. Consider a C-DPP instance G:
1. Without both vertices s, t having incoming as well as outgoing arcs, FC(G) is satisfiable.
2. Every F ′ ∈MU(FC(G)) contains four occurrences of variable x0 (i.e., vdF ′(x0) = 4).
3. G has a special closed walk iff FC(G) is unsatisfiable.
4. G has a special cycle iff MU(FC(G)) ∩FIII ̸= ∅.
5. If G does not have a special cycle, then for each F ′ ∈MU(FC(G)) we have δ(F ′) ≥2.
Proof. For Part 1, the satisfying assignments for the four cases are as follows, using V :=
V (G) \ {s, t}, and mapping all elements of V to the same value as indicated, and stating the
missing clauses of FC(G) which would be violated by the assignment for any v ∈V :
no outgoing arc from s: x0 7→1, V 7→0; violates x0 →v .= {x0, v};
no incoming arc to s: x0 7→0, V 7→1; violates v →x0 .= {x0, v};
no outgoing arc from t: x0 7→0, V 7→0; violates x0 →v .= {x0, v};
no incoming arc to t: x0 7→1, V 7→1; violates v →x0 .= {x0, v}.
Part 2 follows from Part 1 and the fact, that all four (violated) clause-types must be present.
For Part 3 recall that any F ∈2–CLS∗is unsatisfiable iff idg(F) has a contradictory walk (a
walk in the implication digraph from some literal x to x and back). If there is a special closed
walk in G, then this yields immediately a contradictory walk in idg(FC(G)) from x0 to x0 and
back, and thus FC(G) is unsatisfiable. For the other direction, by Part 1, every contradictory
walk in idg(FC(G)) must contain x0 as well as x0, and thus contains a contradictory walk
P1 from x0 to x0 and back via P2. If P1 or P2 contains some complemented literal v for

▶Theorem 5.5. Consider any class FIII ⊆C ⊆MUδ=1. Deciding whether F ∈2–CLS has
an MUS belonging to C, that is, whether MU(F) ∩C ̸= ∅holds, is NP-complete.
Proof. Given an C-DPP instance G as input, we transform it to FC(G) in polynomial time
(indeed in linear time). Now G has a special cycle iff FC(G) ∈C, by Theorem 5.4, Parts 4
and 5. The assertion follows now by the NP-completeness of the decision-problem, whether
a C-DPP instance has a special cycle ([6, Lemma 4.1]).

By small modifications, we can show that Family IV is similarly hard to find:
▶Theorem 5.6. Consider any class FIV ⊆C ⊆MUδ=1. Deciding whether F ∈2–CLS has
an MUS belonging to C, that is, whether MU(F) ∩C ̸= ∅holds, is NP-complete.
Proof. We introduce a new variable y0, and use the modified translation FC′(G), which adds
the new binary clause x0 →y0, and for outgoing arcs (t, v) uses the clause y0 →v (instead
of x0 →v), and for incoming arcs (v, s) uses the clause v →y0 (instead of v →x0).
While with Theorem 5.4 we obtain that every MUS of FC(G) contains four clauses of the
types x0 →v, v →x0, v →x0, x0 →v, now every MUS of FC′(G) contains x0 →y0 (yielding
also the contraposed arc y0 →x0) plus the four clauses of the types x0 →v, v →y0, v →x0,
y0 →v. Thus x0 occurs positively once and negatively twice, while y0 occurs positively twice
and negatively once, that is, we have two degree-3-variables. With the similar proofs as for
Theorem 5.4 we obtain now, that G has a special cycle iff MU(FC′(G)) ∩FIV ̸= ∅, and if G
does not have a special cycle, then for each F ′ ∈MU(FC′(G)) we have δ(F ′) ≥2. So we can
adapt the proof of Theorem 5.5.

Summary and Outlook
We started by showing 2-MU decision in linear time (Section 4), based on singular DP-
reduction (sDP), with Question 4.3 asking what the general complexity of sDP is. We then
started considering MUSs of 2-CNFs. Question 4.6 asked whether finding some MUS can be
done in linear time. Then in Section 5 we turned to hardness results, showing that deciding
the existence of Family-III or Family-IV MUSs is NP-complete. Family-I and Family-II

MUSs (those with two resp. one unit-clause) on the other hand can be found efficiently, as
shown in Section 6. Question 6.6 considers the problem of finding shortest Family-II-MUSs
(which can be done efficiently for Family-I-MUSs). Finally we showed that Families I and
II together can be enumerated in incremental polynomial time; Question 7.5 asks whether
polynomial delay is possible, while Question 7.6 discusses quickly more practical algorithms.
An interesting open research direction is to establish a more complete complexity map
for finding MUSs of 2-CNFs. The same question about a more complete map arises concerning
further efficient enumerations of MUSs.
▶Conjecture 8.1. Regular paths in digraphs with given skew-symmetry can be enumerated
with polynomial delay.

containing two given unit-clauses.
