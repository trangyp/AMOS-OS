Abstract. Drawing on the theory of Minimal Model Program singularities
for foliations, we define relative canonical and log-canonical singularities for
algebraic stacks with finite generic stabilisers. We show that if a point has log-
canonical singularities, its stabiliser group is a finite extension of an algebraic
torus, thus, ´etale locally, the good moduli space exists. If the singularity is
canonical, we further show that the locus of stable points is non-empty.

Introduction
There is an intimate relation between Birational Geometry and the construction
of quotient spaces. In both endeavours, one strives to find a distinguished quotient
space in a fixed birational equivalence class. In this paper, we further investigate
this relation by studying how the singularities of the Minimal Model Program affect
the local construction of quotient spaces of algebraic stacks.
Main Theorem. Let X be a normal algebraic stack of finite type over an alge-
braically closed field k of characteristic zero with affine diagonal and finite generic
stabilisers. Let x ∈X(k) be a point. Then,
(1) if X has relative log-canonical singularities at x, the stabiliser group of x is
a finite extension of an algebraic torus and a good moduli space exists ´etale
locally on X, and
(2) if X has relative canonical singularities at x, the locus of stable points with
respect to the local good moduli space is non-empty.

We say that an algebraic stack X has relative (log-)canonical singularities if there
exists a smooth presentation X →X such that the relative tangent sheaf TX/X has
(log-)canonical singularities as a foliation on X. Similarly, we say X is relatively
regular if TX/X is a regular foliation on X. Since singularities of the Minimal Model
Program are insensitive to smooth morphisms, these notions are independent of the
choice of presentation (see Definition 2.11).
When X admits a good moduli space q ∶X →Q, we say that x ∈X is stable if
q−1(q(x)) = {x} (see [ER21, Definition 2.5]).
Recall that, ´etale locally on X, a coarse moduli space exists when the stabiliser
is finite ([KM97, Theorem 1.1]), and a good moduli space exists when the stabiliser
is reductive ([AHR20, Theorem 1.1]). Thus our Main Theorem yields the following.

We deduce two corollaries.
Corollary A. Let G be an affine algebraic group acting with finite generic sta-
bilisers on a normal scheme X of finite type over an algebraically closed field k of
characteristic zero. Let x ∈X be a closed point and suppose that the foliation in-
duced by G is log-canonical at x. Then the stabiliser group of x is a finite extension
of an algebraic torus.
Corollary B. Let X →X be a smooth presentation of a normal algebraic stack
of finite type over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic zero with affine
diagonal and finite generic stabilisers. Let x ∈X be a closed point and suppose
that the induced foliation F ∶= TX/X is canonical at x. Then there exists an ´etale
neighbourhood U →X of x and a morphism U →Q to an affine scheme Q of finite
type over k, such that F∣U = TU/Q over the generic point of U.
Motivation. Our main interest is algebraic foliations. Notably, we would like to
generalise the following ([MP13, Fact I.ii.4]) to higher rank locally free foliations.
Theorem (McQuillan–Panazzolo). Let X be a Noetherian normal scheme over C
and let x ∈X be a closed point. Suppose that ∂is a local derivation of X such that x
is ∂-invariant. Then ∂is log-canonical at x if and only if the induced endomorphism
of the tangent space TX,x is non-nilpotent.
In higher rank, in order to formulate a generalisation, we have to consider the
stabiliser Lie algebras of the foliation and their normal representations (see §1.8).
If we then assume that F is induced by a smooth presentation of an algebraic
stack, i.e. F is algebraically integrable in a strong sense, then part (1) of our Main
Theorem immediately implies that F is ´etale locally induced by an algebraic torus
action, thus, in particular, it is non-nilpotent. Corollary A is an instance where we
can apply our result.
Another significant motivating conjecture is [CS25a, Conjecture 6.4.2], which,
from our perspective, states that canonical foliations admit a stable quotient space.

Conjecture (Cascini–Spicer). Let X be a klt Q-factorial projective variety over C
and let F be an algebraically integrable foliation on X with canonical singularities.
Then there exists a projective morphism f ∶X →Y to a projective variety Y such
that F = Tf over the generic point of X.
If we again assume that F is induced by a smooth presentation of an algebraic
stack, then Corollary B states that the conjecture is true ´etale locally on X. More
precisely, the foliation is ´etale locally induced by a stable good moduli space. We
may hope, perhaps under further global assumptions, to be able to glue these local
moduli spaces, as in [AHR20], in order to obtain a global algebraic space quotient.
We also want to mention that, in [CHLX23, Theorem 2.1.9], the authors provide
an answer to the Cascini–Spicer conjecture when the foliated pair (X,F) is F-dlt.
Our methods are different. In loc. cit., the authors resolve an indeterminacy locus
(as we also do in §3.2) and then run a Minimal Model Program for foliations in
order to construct the morphism f. On the other hand, we show that a local good
moduli space exists and prove it is of maximal dimension.

Main Ideas. The main ingredient is semistable reduction. Variants of this tool,
such as [AK00, Theorem 0.3], have already been successfully employed in the study
of algebraically integrable foliations in [ACSS22].
We use functorial semistable
reduction ([ATW20, Theorem 1.2.17]) and proceed in several steps.
Let X →X be a minimal presentation around a closed point x and let F be the
induced locally free foliation on X.
(I) We first observe that an algebraic stack X with finite generic stabilisers is
generically a smooth quotient stack by a finite group. As a result, there
exists a rational map f ∶X ⇢B to a smooth projective scheme B (§3.1).
(II) We can then functorially resolve the indeterminacy locus of f in order
to obtain a modification σ ∶Z →X represented by σX ∶Z →X, and a
morphism g ∶Z →B. We deduce that σ∗
XF is a foliation on Z (§3.2).
(III) We now use functorial semistable reduction to find an alteration τZ ∶Zss →
Z and a semistable morphism gss ∶Zss →Bss such that F ss ∶= π∗
XF is a
foliation on Zss, where πX = σX ○τZ (§3.3).
(IV) By construction F ss is a subsheaf of the logarithmic tangent sheaf Tgss of
gss. We show that, when F is log-canonical, F ss is log-canonical (Lemma
1.30).
It follows that F ss = Tgss, else we could extract a divisor with
negative log-discrepancy (§3.4).
(V) Since gss is semistable, it induces local torus actions. The differentials of
these actions are captured by the normal representations of Tgss, which are
faithful and semisimple representations of Abelian Lie algebras (§1.9).
(VI) Now suppose G is the stabiliser group of x. Then G acts on a proper space
V ∶= π−1(x) ⊆Zss. Suppose it has a fixed point v ∈V , then, infinitesimally
around v, G acts as F ss. But F ss = Tgss (Step (IV)), and Tgss acts as a
torus (Step (V)). We deduce that G must be a torus (§4.1).
(VII) If F is canonical at x, we show that the image of the non-regular locus
of σ∗
XF on Z via the morphism g is not dense (Proposition 3.6). Using
properties of good moduli spaces, this is enough to conclude (§4.2).
We encounter some technical difficulties which are worth pointint out.
(i) We need to keep track of the locus where F is not regular. We do so by
adding a boundary divisor and using logarithmic geometry.
(ii) We work with foliations which are not necessarily saturated in TX. We
take ample space to thoroughly discuss foliations in this setting (§1).
(iii) The functorial semistable reduction only produces a Deligne–Mumford stack
Zss. For this reason, we must work with foliations on such spaces (§2.1).
(iv) If we merely required gss to be logarithmically smooth, we could take τZ to
be a modification ([ATW20, Theorem 1.2.12]). However, we really do need
semistability to ensure that Tgss acts faithfully.
(v) The action of G on V need not have a fixed point. For this reason, we work
with additive subgroups of G, which always have a fixed point (§4.3).
(vi) There is no foundational material on formal stacks. As a result, our treat-
ment may at times be overly laborious, especially in §1.8, §2.2 and §3.4.

1. Singularities of Foliations on Schemes
We start by defining foliations on schemes (§1.1) and logarithmic schemes (§1.2).
We then work towards defining singularities of foliations (§1.4 and §1.5) by first
studying foliations on discrete valuation rings (§1.3). After describing the behaviour
of singularities under smooth morphisms (§1.6) and alterations (§1.7), we define the
normal representations of stabiliser Lie algebras (§1.8) and compute them in the
case of semistable morphisms (§1.9).

In this subsection, X is a locally Noetherian normal scheme over a field k of

morphism of foliations is a morphism of subsheaves of TX.

Lemma 4.1. Let V be a proper Deligne–Mumford stack over k and let the additive
group Ga act on V. Then there exists a point v ∈V(k) fixed by Ga.
Proof. By the Keel–Mori theorem, V admits a coarse moduli space q ∶V →V as
a proper algebraic space ([KM97, Theorem 1.1]). Since V is a uniform categorical
quotient, the Ga action on V descends to a Ga action on V . By Lemma 4.2, there
exists a closed point v ∈V fixed by Ga. But q induces a bijection between closed
points of V and V , thus q−1(v) ⊆V(k) is a singleton fixed by Ga.
□
Lemma 4.2. Let V be a proper algebraic space over k and let the additive group
Ga act on V . Then there exists a closed point v ∈V fixed by Ga.
Proof. Pick a closed point v ∈V whose orbit has minimal dimension.
We first
show that its orbit O is closed. Let ¯O ⊆V be the set-theoretic closure of the orbit
morphism σv ∶Ga →V of v. Note that ¯O is Ga-invariant. Indeed, Ga is dense in
¯O, thus Ga × Ga is dense in Ga × ¯O, so that the image of Ga × ¯O →V is ¯O. If the
orbit were not closed, there would exist a closed point v′ ∈V not contained in the
image of σv whose orbit would necessarily have strictly lower dimension than the
orbit of v. Now, suppose v is not fixed, then its stabiliser is trivial, for Ga does
not have non-trivial algebraic subgroups. It follows that O is a Ga-torsor, hence O
is affine. On the other hand, O is a closed subspace of a proper algebraic space,
hence is proper. We deduce that O is finite, thus O = {v}, for Ga is connected.
□

Lemma 4.3. Let G be an affine connected algebraic group over k. Suppose there
exists no additive subgroup Ga ⊆G, then G is an algebraic torus.
Proof. If the unipotent radical of G were not trivial, it would necessarily contain a
copy of Ga. It follows that G is reductive, thus its derived subgroup G′ is semisim-
ple. If G′ were not trivial, a Borel subgroup would necessarily contain a copy of Ga.
It follows that G is Abelian, reductive and connected, i.e. an algebraic torus.
