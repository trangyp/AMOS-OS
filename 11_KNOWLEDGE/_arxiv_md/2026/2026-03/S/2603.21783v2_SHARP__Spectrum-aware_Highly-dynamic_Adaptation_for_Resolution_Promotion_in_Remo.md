Abstract
Text-to-image synthesis for remote sensing (RS) holds immense
potential to advance Earth observation tasks, yet the community
lacks an accessible, high-performance generative framework. A
critical bottleneck is the inherent demand for large-scale, high-
resolution imagery, where directly training foundational models
incurs prohibitive computational and temporal costs. While achiev-
ing training-free resolution promotion via Rotary Position Embed-
ding (RoPE) extrapolation offers a cost-effective alternative, current
algorithms rely on static scaling rules that are heavily biased to-
ward natural scenes. Natural images typically feature single, salient
subjects, whereas RS imagery is characterized by dense, scattered,
and minute instances that are highly dependent on high-frequency
structural integrity. To overcome this fundamental domain mis-
match, we present a comprehensive framework for large-scale RS
image synthesis. First, we establish a robust domain-specific gen-
erative prior by training on a curated dataset of over 100,000 RS
images. Second, to accomplish high-fidelity resolution promotion,
we propose a novel training-free positional extrapolation algorithm,
named SHARP. At its core, SHARP introduces a rational decay
scheduler, 𝜅𝑟𝑠(𝑡), to continuously modulate RoPE frequencies step-
by-step throughout the entire denoising process. By explicitly cou-
pling the extrapolation strength to the evolving spectral state of
diffusion—enforcing strong global layout constraints early on and
progressively relaxing them to recover dense high-frequency details
later—SHARP preserves crisp instance features while seamlessly ex-
panding the spatial footprint. Extensive experiments demonstrate
that our approach consistently achieves state-of-the-art perfor-
mance across multiple promoted resolutions with negligible over-
head, providing a highly efficient and structurally faithful solution
for large-scale RS generation. Our model weights and code are fully
open-sourced at https://github.com/bxuanz/SHARP.

Crucially, existing training-free algorithms rely on a static ex-
trapolation rule that remains fixed throughout the entire denoising
process. This rigid assumption fundamentally conflicts with the
inherent generative physics of diffusion models [7, 11, 20, 21, 27, 36].
Diffusion-based image synthesis follows a coarse-to-fine, frequency-
progressive paradigm: during the initial high-noise phase, the model
prioritizes converging on the low-frequency global layout (e.g.,
broad coastlines, primary road networks); subsequently, in the
late low-noise phase, the generative focus shifts to crystallizing
high-frequency texture details (e.g., dense vehicles, minute building
edges).
Current algorithms apply a uniform, unyielding positional com-
pression from beginning to end (red dashed line in Fig. 1). While this
static extrapolation strength is necessary early on to establish an
expanded layout, it acts as a severe spectral bottleneck during the
later detail-refinement phase. By continuously compressing the spa-
tial frequencies precisely when delicate micro-textures are trying to
emerge, static methods irreparably suppress high-frequency com-
ponents. For RS imagery, this leads to catastrophic over-smoothing,
washing out the critical structural integrity of fine instances.
To overcome this fundamental domain mismatch and compu-
tational bottleneck, we present a comprehensive framework for
large-scale RS text-to-image synthesis. First, we establish a ro-
bust domain-specialized prior (RS-FLUX) by fine-tuning FLUX
[3] on a curated dataset of over 100,000 high-quality RS images.
Second, to accomplish high-fidelity resolution promotion, we pro-
pose SHARP (Spectrum-aware Highly-dynamic Adaptation for
Resolution Promotion).
Unlike static baselines, SHARP achieves highly-dynamic adap-
tation by introducing a rational decay scheduler 𝜅𝑟𝑠(𝑡) (blue solid
line in Fig. 1) that continuously modulates RoPE frequencies step-
by-step. Our core insight is to explicitly synchronize the positional
extrapolation strength with the natural spectral evolution of the
diffusion process. During the early global-layout generation phase,
SHARP applies strong positional extrapolation to guarantee macro-
layout coherence across the expanded spatial canvas. As the genera-
tion transitions into the texture-refinement phase, SHARP smoothly
and continuously decays the extrapolation strength. By deliberately
reducing the positional compression when precise textures are form-
ing, SHARP liberates the model to render crisp micro-structures
without spatial distortion. This dynamic decoupling seamlessly ex-
pands the image footprint while faithfully preserving intricate RS
details.
In summary, our main contributions are:
• Domain-Specialized Generative Prior: We construct a
102,952-pair RS text-to-image dataset and fine-tune FLUX to
build RS-FLUX, providing a powerful foundational DiT for
the RS community.
• Identifying the Static Bottleneck: We identify and the-
oretically analyze the fatal conflict between existing static
positional extrapolation and the frequency-progressive na-
ture of diffusion, revealing the root cause of over-smoothing
in high-frequency RS generation.
• Dynamic Extrapolation Algorithm (SHARP): We pro-
pose SHARP, a novel training-free algorithm that dynami-
cally couples RoPE frequencies with the denoising spectrum.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that SHARP effectively
eliminates over-smoothing, achieving state-of-the-art perfor-
mance across multiple promoted resolutions with negligible
overhead.

Related Work
2.1
Remote Sensing Image Generation
Generative modeling for RS imagery has evolved significantly, tran-
sitioning from GAN-based methods to highly expressive diffusion
models [9, 11]. While early RS generative research demonstrated
the value of diffusion priors, they predominantly targeted specific
restoration or conditional tasks (e.g., super-resolution [22], cloud re-
moval [29]). For open-ended text-to-image synthesis, most existing
RS adaptations continue to rely on U-Net backbones [24], which are
inherently biased toward patch-level synthesis rather than holistic,
complex scene generation. The recent success of Diffusion Trans-
formers (DiTs) [3, 18] in producing photorealistic natural images
by scaling self-attention motivates our development of RS-FLUX,
a DiT-based generative foundation model tailored for the dense and
complex structural statistics of RS imagery.
2.2

Methodology
This section presents our two-component framework. Section 3.1
describes RS-FLUX, the domain-specialized DiT prior obtained by
fine-tuning FLUX [3] on curated RS data. Section 3.2 establishes
the spectral motivation that directly informs the design of SHARP
(Sec. 3.3), our training-free dynamic resolution promotion strategy.
The overall pipeline is illustrated in Fig. 4.
3.1
Domain-Specialized Prior: RS-FLUX
Open-source DiT checkpoints [18] are optimized for natural images
and fail to capture the semantics and spectral statistics of aerial
scenes. RS imagery contains dense man-made structures, small ob-
jects, and repetitive textures under-represented in natural-image
priors [6]. Parameter-efficient approaches such as LoRA [12] con-
strain updates to a low-rank subspace that may be insufficient for
bridging the large distribution gap between natural and RS imagery.
We therefore perform full fine-tuning of FLUX [3] on over 100,000
high-quality RS images, updating all transformer parameters so that
attention heads, feed-forward layers, and the final projection jointly
adapt to the spectral and geometric statistics unique to overhead
imagery. The resulting RS-FLUX serves as the base generator in all
experiments. Because SHARP operates exclusively at inference time
by modifying the RoPE frequency table, it requires no additional
high-resolution data or retraining—the two components are fully
decoupled.

4
Experiments
4.1
Experimental Setup
4.1.1
Training Data Construction. We construct a domain-specialized
corpus from the GeoChat dataset [13], which provides RS images
with multi-turn dialogues. Since the question-answer format is
poorly suited to caption-style supervision, we use Qwen-VL [2] to
convert each sample into a compact prompt-image pair (Fig. 5b),
yielding 102,952 pairs for RS-FLUX fine-tuning. The corpus spans
multiple resolutions (Fig. 5a), promoting robustness to varying
spatial extents.
4.1.2
Evaluation Protocol. We construct a benchmark of 100 GPT-
5.4 generated prompts [25] covering diverse RS scenes. We evaluate
generation quality using three reference-free metrics: CLIP Score
[10] (semantic consistency), Aesthetic Score [23] (perceptual qual-
ity), and HPSv2 [32] (human preference).
4.1.3
Multi-Scale Promotion Setting. To evaluate generation be-
yond the 1024 × 1024 training resolution, we test on three isotropic
targets (15362, 20482, 25602) and three anisotropic targets (2048 ×
1536, 2560 × 1920, 3072 × 2048). All methods generate images from
the same 100 prompts under identical random seeds.
4.1.4
Implementation Details. RS-FLUX is fine-tuned from the
10242 FLUX checkpoint using AdamW (𝛽1=0.9, 𝛽2=0.999, weight
decay 0.01) with a 10−5 learning rate and cosine annealing. Train-
ing spans 10K steps with bf16 precision and an effective batch size
of 64 (8×NVIDIA A6000 GPUs). For fair comparison, all inference
methods share the same backbone, Euler sampler (28 steps, guid-
ance scale 3.5), and seeds; only the RoPE adaptation strategy varies.
SHARP hyperparameters (𝛼𝑠=3, 𝛼=1, 𝛽=32) follow the ablation re-
sults in Sec. 4.5.
4.2
RS-FLUX Validation at Native Resolution
Before evaluating resolution promotion strategies, we verify that
domain-specialized fine-tuning improves generation quality at the
native 1024 × 1024 resolution. Table 1 compares vanilla FLUX with
RS-FLUX on the same 100-prompt benchmark.
RS-FLUX improves over vanilla FLUX by +3.09 CLIP, +0.79 Aes,
and +0.047 HPSv2. The largest gain appears on CLIP Score (+12.1%
relative), indicating that full fine-tuning substantially narrows the
semantic gap between generated outputs and RS-specific prompts—
vanilla FLUX frequently misinterprets aerial terminology. The Aes-
thetic Score and HPSv2 improvements further confirm that RS-
FLUX produces visually coherent aerial scenes with realistic color
palettes and spatial layouts, establishing a stronger starting point
for resolution promotion.

Quantitative Comparison
Table 2 compares SHARP with four baselines across six resolutions.
The results show a consistent ranking: RS-FLUX (no RoPE adap-
tation) < PI < NTK-aware < YaRN < SHARP. Although PI, NTK-
aware, and YaRN progressively improve the frequency treatment,
they all remain static during denoising. By introducing dynamic
scheduling, SHARP consistently outperforms the strongest static
baseline.
Overall performance. SHARP achieves 27.54 CLIP, 5.78 Aes,
and 0.270 HPSv2 on average, surpassing the strongest baseline YaRN
by +0.39 / +0.13 / +0.008 and RS-FLUX by +1.47 / +0.44 / +0.029.
Isotropic promotion. SHARP improves over YaRN from 27.27
/ 5.68 / 0.263 to 27.68 / 5.81 / 0.271. The margin widens with the
promotion factor: +0.32 / +0.11 / +0.007 at 15362 versus +0.50 /
+0.14 / +0.009 at 25602. This widening corroborates the spectrum-
aware design: as the promotion factor grows, static methods suffer
increasingly severe high-frequency suppression, whereas SHARP’s
dynamic scheduling compensates accordingly. From 15362 to 25602,
SHARP’s metrics degrade by 0.75 / 0.18 / 0.010 versus 0.93 / 0.21 /
0.012 for YaRN, indicating slower quality decay under aggressive
promotion.
Anisotropic promotion. SHARP leads at all anisotropic set-
tings, with the largest margins at the most challenging 3072 × 2048
(26.93 / 5.64 / 0.263 vs. 26.50 / 5.50 / 0.254). Despite differing pro-
motion factors along the two spatial axes, SHARP handles this
gracefully through axis-independent RoPE rescaling via the dimen-
sionless frequency ratio 𝑟(𝑖), consistent with its resolution-agnostic
formulation.

4.6
Computational Efficiency
SHARP modifies only the RoPE frequency computation at each
step and introduces no additional parameters. Table 4 reports in-
ference time on a single A6000 GPU. SHARP adds less than 1.5%
overhead relative to vanilla RS-FLUX across all tested resolutions.
The extra cost comes only from evaluating Eq. 8–12 per step, involv-
ing element-wise arithmetic over the RoPE dimensions—negligible
compared with the self-attention and feed-forward computations
dominating each transformer pass. Peak GPU memory is unchanged
because SHARP introduces no additional parameters or intermedi-
ate activations.
5
Conclusion
We present SHARP, a training-free resolution promotion frame-
work for large-scale RS text-to-image synthesis. By replacing static
extrapolation with a rational decay scheduler, 𝜅𝑟𝑠(𝑡), SHARP pre-
serves both global layouts and dense high-frequency details. SHARP
consistently outperforms static baselines across diverse scales with
negligible overhead (< 1.5%). Future work includes content-adaptive
schedules and sparse attention for extreme resolutions.
