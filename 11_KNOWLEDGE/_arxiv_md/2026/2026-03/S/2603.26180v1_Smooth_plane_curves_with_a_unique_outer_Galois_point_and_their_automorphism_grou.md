SMOOTH PLANE CURVES WITH A UNIQUE OUTER GALOIS
POINT AND THEIR AUTOMORPHISM GROUPS
ESLAM BADR AND TAKESHI HARUI
Abstract. We consider smooth plane curves X of degree d ≥4, defined
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, that possess a unique
outer Galois point. This geometric condition forces the curve to be a cyclic
covering of the projective line, and ensures that its automorphism group fits
into a specific theoretical framework.
For each possible non-cyclic reduced
automorphism group Autred(X), we fully characterize the defining equation of
X and the precise structure of its full automorphism group Aut(X).
This comprehensive analysis not only identifies the exact form of the equa-
tion for each automorphism type but also establishes the detailed criteria under
which these scenarios can occur, thereby offering a complete classification of
defining equations for smooth plane curves with a unique outer Galois point
and a non-cyclic reduced automorphism group.
1. Introduction and main results
Let K be an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, and let X, Y, Z be the
homogeneous coordinates of the projective plane P2(K).
Suppose that X is a smooth plane curve of degree d ≥4 with the property that
its full automorphism group Aut(X) fixes a point P ∈P2(K) that does not lie on
X. Consequently, X cannot be the Fermat curve Fd : Xd + Y d + Zd = 0, whose
automorphism group is well explored in the literature (see [12, 15]), as it does not
leave invariant any point in the plane P2(K). The existence of such a fixed point
structures the automorphism group in a very specific way, which can be understood

A key additional hypothesis is that the point P serves as a unique outer Galois
point for X, a notion that has been extensively studied in the literature (see, for
example, [5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10]). This condition has profound implications for the curve’s
algebraic structure. As established in these works, the presence of such a Galois
point forces the curve to be a cyclic covering of the projective line, branched at a
set of points that includes the intersection of X with the line fixed by P’s stabilizer.
In practical terms, this means that X is a superelliptic curve. In our coordinates,
the action of the cyclic group N (the kernel of the map to Autred(X)) on X can be
viewed as the superelliptic automorphism:
(X : Y : Z) 7→(X : Y : ζdZ),
where ζd is a fixed primitive dth root of unity inside K.
In their recent paper [16], the second author, together with K. Miura and
A. Ohbuchi, examined the situation in which the reduced automorphism group
Autred(X) of X (as defined above) is A5. Precisely, they provided necessary con-
ditions for the defining equation of X and analyzed the structure of the full auto-
morphism group Aut(X). Inspired by their groundbreaking work, we extend their
investigation to the case where the reduced automorphism group Autred(X) is non-
cyclic. It is worth noting that the cyclic case has already been notably addressed in
[12], and our work aims to deepen the understanding of non-cyclic automorphism
groups, providing new insights into the broader structure and classification of plane
curves with reduced automorphism groups.
Superelliptic curves and the existing literature. The curves under consid-
eration in this paper belong to the broader family of superelliptic curves, defined
as cyclic covers of the projective line. Superelliptic curves have been extensively
studied, with significant contributions to the understanding of their automorphism
groups. Kontogeorgis [1] investigated the group of automorphisms of cyclic exten-
sions of rational function fields. Shaska and collaborators [2, 3] developed invariant-
theoretic approaches to hyperelliptic and superelliptic curves with extra automor-
phisms. More recently, Beshaj, Hoxha, and Shaska [4] initiated a systematic study
of superelliptic curves of level n, focusing on their moduli spaces, invariant the-
ory, and computational aspects. Their work provides a framework for classifying
superelliptic curves with prescribed automorphism groups and includes parametric
equations for various families. However, as they note, determining explicit equa-
tions for a curve given its automorphism group remains a challenging problem in
general.
Given this context, one might ask: what new information can the present paper
contribute? The answer lies in several fundamental distinctions.

Distinction from prior work. The key differences between our work and the
existing literature on superelliptic curves are the following:
1. Geometric context: abstract curves versus plane embeddings with
Galois points. The works cited above treat superelliptic curves as abstract alge-
braic curves, given by affine equations of the form yn = f(x). Their automorphism
groups are studied up to birational equivalence, without reference to any specific
embedding into projective space. In contrast, the curves in our investigation are
smooth plane curves X ⊂P2 of degree d ≥4 that possess a unique outer Galois
point.
This geometric condition, which has no analogue in the abstract theory,
forces a specific homogeneous equation:
Zd + Ld,Z(X, Y ) = 0,
where Ld,Z(X, Y ) is a homogeneous polynomial of degree d in X and Y , of degree
at least d −1 in each variable separately. The unique outer Galois point condi-
tion further ensures that the cyclic group N = ⟨diag(1, 1, ζd)⟩is the kernel of the
map Λ from Aut(X) to Autred(X). This normal form reflects both the superel-
liptic structure and the specific embedding into P2, with the point P fixed by the
full automorphism group. Passing from the affine model yn = f(x) to this plane
model is not a trivial change of coordinates; the embedding introduces additional
geometric structure—the line at infinity, the fixed point P, and the Galois point
condition—that has no analogue in the abstract theory.
2.
The Galois point condition as a new geometric constraint.
The
existence of a unique outer Galois point is a strong geometric condition that selects
a very special subclass of superelliptic curves. Not every superelliptic curve admits
a Galois point; in fact, the Galois point condition imposes explicit restrictions
on the geometry of the curve: the branch locus must lie on the line Z = 0, the
ramification indices must divide the degree d in a prescribed manner, and the lifts
of automorphisms from Autred(X) to Aut(X) must respect both the cyclic cover
structure and the fixed point P. These constraints have no analogue in the abstract
classification, which considers all superelliptic curves regardless of whether they
admit a Galois point or a plane embedding.
3. The reduced automorphism group and the exact sequence. A central
feature of our work is the focus on the reduced automorphism group Autred(X),
which fits into the exact sequence:
1 →N →Aut(X) →Autred(X) →1,
where N is cyclic of order d and Autred(X) is a finite subgroup of PGL2(K). This
decomposition, which arises from the existence of the fixed point P, is specific to
plane curves with this property. The abstract theory of superelliptic curves typically
studies the full automorphism group directly, without this canonical decomposition
into a cyclic kernel and a reduced part acting on the base.
4. Complementary, not redundant. The existing literature and our work are
therefore complementary. The abstract theory provides a general framework and
identifies possible group-theoretic structures. Our work addresses a more refined
geometric question: which of these structures can be realized by a smooth plane
curve with a unique outer Galois point, and what is the explicit equation in each
case?
The two approaches enrich each other: the abstract theory informs the
possible groups, while our work provides concrete geometric realizations that can
serve as test cases for further study.
In summary, while the curves we study are superelliptic in nature, they form
a geometrically distinguished subclass characterized by the presence of a Galois
point and a fixed point in the plane. The classification we obtain is not a subset of

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls essential preliminaries, includ-
ing smoothness criteria for curves of the form Zd+F(X, Y ) = 0, the classification of
finite subgroups of PGL2(K), and the characterization of minimal invariant binary
forms for each such group. The remaining sections are devoted to the detailed anal-
ysis of the four possible reduced automorphism groups. Section 3 treats the dihedral
case Dm, establishing necessary and sufficient conditions on the defining equation
and providing a complete description of the full automorphism group structure.
Section 4 handles the symmetric group S4, while Section 5 addresses the icosahe-
dral case A5. Section 6 deals with the alternating group A4, which exhibits features
that are intermediate between the dihedral and larger polyhedral cases. Each of
these sections follows a uniform pattern: we first determine the possible invariant
binary forms that can appear, then we verify sufficiency and analyze the resulting
automorphism group structure. Finally, Section 7 presents a computational verifi-
cation of our classification through systematic MAGMA implementations, including
tables summarizing all admissible configurations for low degrees.

7. MAGMA Implementation
All theorems presented in this work are constructive: they not only establish the
existence of smooth plane curves with prescribed reduced automorphism groups,
but also provide explicit defining equations and concrete generators for the full auto-
morphism group. Moreover, every classification result is fully algorithmic, allowing
for systematic, computer-assisted generation of all admissible configurations.


## Page 25


25
To make these constructions tangible and reproducible, we have implemented
each of the four cases—dihedral Dm, symmetric S4, alternating A4, and icosahedral
A5—as standalone, theorem-compliant MAGMA V2.12–15 packages. Each package
mirrors the precise statements of Theorems 1.4, 1.2, 1.3, and 1.1, respectively, and
is designed with a uniform, user-friendly interface. The packages are available from
the authors upon request.
A typical package provides the following functionality:
• Display the full theorem statement, including all binary forms and gener-
ators.
• Enumerate all valid parameter configurations (ϵ, ϵ′, ϵ′′, t, . . . ) for a given
degree d.
• Construct the symbolic equation of the curve in minimal form.
• Output the exact structure of Aut(X) together with explicit projective
generators.
To demonstrate the scope and correctness of our implementation, we executed
systematic searches for each of the four families over a range of degrees:
• Dm: 4 ≤d ≤15,
• S4: 4 ≤d ≤30,
• A4: 4 ≤d ≤60,
• A5: 4 ≤d ≤32.
For each degree, our code automatically retrieves all admissible configurations,
eliminates isomorphic duplicates, and outputs the corresponding curve equation,
automorphism group, and generators in symbolic form. The complete results are
summarized in Tables 4, 1, 3, and 2.
Crucially, our computational experiments validate the theoretical results against
the existing literature:
For d = 4, the A4 package recovers the unique smooth quartic with automor-
phism group eA4 ⋊C2, in perfect agreement with Henn’s classification of plane quar-
tics [17]. For d = 5, 7, the Dm package produces the curve X : Zd +XY ·Td−2,−= 0
with automorphism group Cd × Dd−2, matching the classification of smooth quin-
tics by Badr and Bars [11] and the classification of septics by Badr, El-Guindy, and
Kamel [14]. For d = 6, both the S4 and A4 packages yield the sextic X : Z6+S6 = 0
with automorphism group C6◦GL2(F3), confirming the results of Badr and Bars on
sextic curves [13]. For d = 12, 20, 30, 32, the A5 package gives examples equivalent
to those examples described by Harui, Miura, and Ohbuchi [16].
These coincidences are not isolated: every configuration produced by our pack-
ages for low degrees aligns with known classifications, providing strong computa-
tional evidence for the completeness and correctness of our theorems.
