SUPPORTS OF INTERSECTIONS OF THICK SUBCATEGORIES
AND PROXY SMALLNESS
KIRIKO KATO AND RYO TAKAHASHI
Abstract. Let R be a commutative noetherian ring. Let Db(R) be the bounded derived category of finitely
generated R-modules. Let X and Y be thick subcategories of Db(R). In this paper, we consider the question
asking when the equality Supp(X ∩Y) = Supp X ∩Supp Y holds, and give several answers. As applications, we
obtain a characterization of the proxy small subcategories, and classifications of certain thick subcategories.
1. Introduction
Let R be a commutative noetherian ring. Let Db(R) be the bounded derived category of finitely generated
R-modules, and Dpf(R) the full subcategory of Db(R) consisting of perfect complexes. In the present paper,
we raise and deal with the following quite natural question.
Question 1.1. Let X, Y be thick subcategories of Db(R). When does the following equality hold true?
(1.1.1)
Supp(X ∩Y) = Supp X ∩Supp Y.
Following [10], we say that a thick subcategory Z of Db(R) is proxy small if there is an equality Supp Z =
Supp(Z ∩Dpf(R)). A typical example of a proxy small subcategory of Db(R) is a thick subcategory of Dpf(R).
The notion of proxy small subcategories corresponds to that of a proxy small complex introduced by Dwyer,
Greenlees and Iyengar [2] in the way that the proxy small complex is the complex whose generating thick
subcategory is a proxy small subcategory. Note by definition that, for Y = Dpf(R), those thick subcategories
X of Db(R) which satisfy the equality (1.1.1) are precisely the proxy small subcategories of Db(R).
Applying the classical Hopkins–Neeman theorem [3] and the relatively recent theorem of Pollitz [7], we
are able to get several answers to Question 1.1.
Theorem 1.2 (Theorem 3.6). (1) The equality (1.1.1) holds provided that both X and Y are proxy small.
(2) Suppose that R is a local ring. Then the following three conditions are equivalent.
• The ring R is a complete intersection.
• The equality (1.1.1) holds for all thick subcategories X and Y of Db(R).
• The equality (1.1.1) holds for all thick subcategories X of Db(R) and Y = Dpf(R).
In the case where Y is contained in Dpf(R), we obtain a criterion for the equality (1.1.1) to hold.
Theorem 1.3 (Theorem 4.2). Let X and Y be thick subcategories of Db(R). Suppose that Y is contained in
Dpf(R). Then the equality (1.1.1) holds if and only if the following full subcategory of Db(R) is triangulated.

2. Preliminaries
This section is devoted to preliminary definitions and results, which will be used in the later sections. Let
us begin with stating our convention, which is valid throughout the present paper.
Convention 2.1. Throughout this paper, we assume that all subcategories are strictly full subcategories,
and that all rings are commutative noetherian rings with identity. Let R be a (commutative noetherian) ring.
We denote by Dsg(R) the singularity category of R, that is to say, the Verdier quotient of the triangulated
category Db(R) by the triangulated subcategory Dpf(R). Also, Sing R stands for the singular locus of R,
namely, the set of prime ideals p of R such that the localization Rp of R at p is not a regular local ring.
We recall some notions of subcategories of a triangulated category.
Definition 2.2. (1) A thick subcategory of a triangulated category T is by definition a triangulated subcat-
egory of T closed under direct summands. For an object T ∈T , we denote by thickT T the thick closure
of T in T , that is, the smallest thick subcategory of T containing X. Note that thickDb(R) R = Dpf(R).
(2) Let F : T →U be an exact functor of triangulated categories.
Let X, Y be subcategories of T , U
respectively. The essential image FX of X by F and the inverse image F −1Y of Y by F are defined by:
FX = {FX | X ∈X},
F −1Y = {T ∈T | FT ∈Y}.
The essential image FX is a subcategory of U, while the inverse image F −1Y is a subcategory of T . Note
that FX is closed under shifts when so is X, and that F −1Y is thick when so is Y.
(3) Let T be a triangulated category. Let X and Y be two subcategories of T . We define the extension of X
and Y by the following equality; it is a subcategory of T .
X ∗Y = {T ∈T | there exists an exact triangle X →T →Y ⇝in T with X ∈X and Y ∈Y}.

Definition 2.5. A thick subcategory X of Db(R) is called proxy small if Supp X = Supp(X ∩Dpf(R)).

Definition 2.8. Let X be a topological space. A subset A of X is called specialization-closed if for every

that the specialization-closed subsets of X are precisely the (possibly infinite) unions of closed subsets of X.

Definition 2.9. (1) Suppose that R is a local ring. We say that R is dominant if, for every nonzero object

(2) We say that R is locally dominant if for every prime ideal p of R the local ring Rp is dominant.

5.3]. In particular, every local ring that is a hypersurface is a locally dominant ring.

3. Cases where the support equality holds or not
In this section, we seek answers to Question 1.1, applying results from the literature. We present examples
where the equality (1.1.1) holds and ones where it fails. We begin with remarking that the definition of proxy
smallness is interpreted as a special case of the equality (1.1.1).
Remark 3.1. In the case where Y = Dpf(R), the equality (1.1.1) holds if and only if the subcategory X is
proxy small. This follows from the definition of proxy smallness and the fact that Supp Dpf(R) = Spec R.

The following proposition is an immediate consequence of Remark 3.1 and Theorem 2.7.
Proposition 3.2. Let R be a local ring, and let Y = Dpf(R). Then R is a complete intersection if and only
if the equality (1.1.1) holds for all thick subcategories X of the derived category Db(R).
The proposition below gives rise to a condition for (1.1.1) not to be satisfied.
Proposition 3.3. Let R be a non-Gorenstein local ring. Then the following two statements hold true.
(1) Let X, Y be nonzero thick subcategories of the derived category Db(R). Assume that every object in X
has finite injective dimension and every object in Y has finite projective dimension. Then (1.1.1) fails.
(2) Let X be the subcategory of Db(R) consisting of complexes of finite injective dimension. Then the equality
(1.1.1) fails for every nonzero thick subcategory Y of Dpf(R).
Proof. (1) As X and Y are nonzero, the maximal ideal m of R belongs to both Supp X and Supp Y. Suppose
that (1.1.1) holds. Then m belongs to Supp(X ∩Y), which means X ∩Y is nonzero. We can choose a nonzero
object Z ∈X ∩Y. By assumption, Z has finite projective dimension and finite injective dimension. It follows
from [2, Theorem 5.3] that the local ring R is Gorenstein. This contradiction completes the proof.
(2) In view of (1), it is enough to verify that X is nonzero. Let K be the Koszul complex of a system of
generators of the maximal ideal m of R. Let I be the Matlis dual of K. Then each component of the bounded
complex I is an injective R-module, so I has finite injective dimension. Each cohomology of the complex I
is a finite-dimensional vector space over the residue field R/m, and in particular, it is a finitely generated
R-module. Thus I is isomorphic to an object J of Db(R). Therefore X contains the nonzero object J.

So far, whenever we consider a pair of thick subcategories of Db(R) that does not satisfy (1.1.1), either of
those two subcategories is contained in Dpf(R). Let us construct an example of a pair of thick subcategories
of Db(R) which neither satisfies (1.1.1) nor fits inside Dpf(R).
Example 3.4. Let R = k[[x, y, z]]/(x2, xy, y2) with k a field. Then R is a complete Cohen–Macaulay non-
Gorenstein local ring of dimension one, and in particular, it possesses a canonical module ω. Let m = (x, y, z)
be the maximal ideal of R, and let p = (x, y) be the minimal prime ideal of R. Put X = thickDb(R) ω and
Y = thickDb(R) m. As the R-modules ω and m have infinite projective dimension, it is observed that neither X
nor Y is contained in Dpf(R). The localization ωp is the injective hull of the residue field of the artinian local
ring Rp, while mp = Rp. In particular, neither ωp nor Rp is zero, and hence p belongs to Supp X ∩Supp Y.
Suppose that p belongs to Supp(X ∩Y). Then there exists an R-complex Z ∈X ∩Y such that Zp ≇0.
As the Rp-module ωp is injective, every object in thickDb(Rp) ωp has finite injective dimension over Rp. Also,
every object in thickDb(Rp) Rp has finite projective dimension over Rp. It is observed that the Rp-complex Zp
has finite projective dimension and finite injective dimension, which forces the local ring Rp to be Gorenstein
by [2, Theorem 5.3]. However, it is easy to see that Rp ∼= k[[x, y, z]](x,y)/(x2, xy, y2) is not Gorenstein. This
contradiction shows that p is not in Supp(X ∩Y), and we conclude that Supp(X ∩Y) ̸= Supp X ∩Supp Y.
Now we apply the Hopkins–Neeman theorem to get an answer to Question 1.1.
Proposition 3.5. The equality (1.1.1) holds if both the subcategories X, Y of Db(R) are contained in Dpf(R).
Proof. Pick any element p ∈Supp X ∩Supp Y. Let K be the Koszul complex of a system of generators of the
prime ideal p. Using [5, Proposition 2.3(3)], we see that Supp K = V(p) ⊆Supp X ∩Supp Y. As K belongs
to Dpf(R) and X, Y are contained in Dpf(R), Theorem 2.4 implies that K belongs to X ∩Y. It follows that
p ∈V(p) = Supp K ⊆Supp(X ∩Y). We conclude the equality Supp(X ∩Y) = Supp X ∩Supp Y holds.
■
Using the above proposition, we obtain a sufficient condition for the equality (1.1.1) to hold, which leads
us to a characterization of the complete intersection local rings in terms of the equality (1.1.1).
Theorem 3.6. (1) The equality (1.1.1) holds whenever the thick subcategories X, Y are proxy small.
(2) Suppose that R is a local ring. Then the following three conditions are equivalent.
(a) The local ring R is a complete intersection.
(b) The equality (1.1.1) holds for all thick subcategories X, Y of Db(R).
(c) The equality (1.1.1) holds for all thick subcategories X of Db(R) and Y = Dpf(R).
Proof. (1) As X ∩Dpf(R) and Y ∩Dpf(R) are thick subcategories contained in Dpf(R), Proposition 3.5 yields
Supp(X ∩Dpf(R)) ∩Supp(Y ∩Dpf(R)) = Supp((X ∩Dpf(R)) ∩(Y ∩Dpf(R))) = Supp(X ∩Y ∩Dpf(R)).

It is natural to consider the singularity category version of Question 1.1, i.e., the question asking when
(3.6.1)

holds for thick subcategories X, Y of Dsg(R). The question admits a clean answer, when R is locally dominant.
Proposition 3.7. Suppose that the ring R is locally dominant (e.g., R is locally a hypersurface). Then the
equality (3.6.1) holds true for all thick subcategories X, Y of the singularity category Dsg(R).
Proof. For all thick subcategories X, Y of Db(R) containing R, we shall prove the equality
IPD(X ∩Y) = IPD(X) ∩IPD(Y).
Clearly, the right-hand side contains the left-hand side. Pick any prime ideal p belonging to IPD(X)∩IPD(Y).
Note that p is in Sing R, and that IPD(X), IPD(Y) are specialization-closed. We see that IPD(R/p) = V(p),
and this is contained in both IPD(X) and IPD(Y). Since the ring R is locally dominant, R/p belongs to both
X and Y by Theorem 2.11(1). It follows that p ∈V(p) = IPD(R/p) ⊆IPD(X ∩Y), and we are done.
■
Using the above proposition, we get the corollary below, which says that if R is a local complete intersection
of codimension at least two, there exist thick subcategories X, Y of Dsg(R) such that (3.6.1) does not hold.
Corollary 3.8. Suppose that R is a local ring which is a complete intersection. Then the equality (3.6.1)
holds true for all thick subcategories X and Y of Dsg(R) if and only if the local ring R is a hypersurface.
Proof. By Proposition 3.7, the “if” part holds. We now prove the “only if” part. This is trivial in the case
where R is regular, so we may assume that R is singular. Take two nonzero thick subcategories X, Y of Dsg(R).
Then the maximal ideal m of R belongs to the singular supports Suppsg X and Suppsg Y. By assumption,
the equality Suppsg(X ∩Y) = Suppsg X ∩Suppsg Y holds. Hence m belongs to Suppsg(X ∩Y), and therefore,
the intersection X ∩Y is nonzero. It is observed from [8, Theorem 3.7] that R is a hypersurface.
■
We close the section by posing the question below, which asks whether the converse of Proposition 3.7 is
true. Note that Corollary 3.8 supports this question in the affirmative, since a local complete intersection is
dominant if and only if it is a hypersurface; see [9, Theorem 1.1(4)].
Question 3.9. Suppose (3.6.1) holds for all thick subcategories X, Y of Dsg(R). Is then R locally dominant?
4. Characterizing thick subcategories satisfying the support equality
The purpose of this short section is to consider characterizing those thick subcategories X, Y of Db(R)
which satisfy the equality (1.1.1), and then restrict it to Y = Dpf(R). We begin with stating a lemma.
Lemma 4.1. Let R be local. Let X, Y ∈Db(R) with RHomR(X, Y ) = 0. Then one has X = 0 or Y = 0.
Proof. It follows from [1, Proposition 16.4.19] that pdR RHomR(X, Y ) = pdR Y + sup X, where sup X :=
sup{i ∈Z | Hi(X) ̸= 0}. Since RHomR(X, Y ) = 0 and pdR 0 = −∞by the definition of projective dimension,
either pdR Y or sup X is equal to −∞. We have Y = 0 in the former case, and X = 0 in the latter case.

(2) The extension X ∗Y is a triangulated subcategory of Db(R).
(3) Any morphism f : X →Y in Db(R) with X ∈X and Y ∈Y factors through an object in X ∩Y.

Applying Theorem 4.2 to Y = Dpf(R), we get a characterization of the proxy small subcategories.
Corollary 4.3. Let X be a thick subcategory of Db(R). The following statements are equivalent.
(1) The thick subcategory X of Db(R) is proxy small.
(2) The extension X ∗Dpf(R) is a triangulated subcategory of Db(R).
(3) Any morphism f : X →P in Db(R) with X ∈X and P ∈Dpf(R) factors through an object in X ∩Dpf(R).
Remark 4.4. The implication (1)⇒(3) in Corollary 4.3 is none other than [10, Lemma 4.2(1)].
5. Applications of Corollary 4.3
In this section, we shall provide applications of Corollary 4.3. For this purpose, we introduce a notation.
Definition 5.1. Let E be a thick subcategory of Dpf(R). Let π : Db(R) →Db(R)/E be the canonical functor.
We define the subcategory eE as follows; note that eE contains E.
eE = {T ∈Db(R) | HomDb(R)/E(πT, πDpf(R)) = 0}

(1) eE is a proxy small subcategory of Db(R) with eE ∩Dpf(R) = E and Supp eE = Supp E.
(2) The following are equivalent for each thick subcategory X of Db(R).
(a) There are inclusions E ⊆X ⊆eE.

Proof. Let π : Db(R) →Db(R)/E be the canonical functor.
(1) As we saw in Remark 5.2, the subcategory eE of Db(R) is thick and contains E. Pick any object P ∈eE ∩

(2) (a)⇒(b): If E ⊆X ⊆eE, then Supp E ⊆Supp X ⊆Supp eE, so that Supp X = Supp eE = Supp E by (1).
(b)⇒(c): Suppose that X contains E and satisfies Supp X = Supp E. The inclusions E ⊆X ∩Dpf(R) ⊆X

Supp X = Supp E. Thus X is proxy small, and X ∩Dpf(R) = E by Theorem 2.4.
(c)⇒(a): Suppose that X is proxy small with X ∩Dpf(R) = E. Then X contains E. Corollary 4.3 and [4,
