STABLE HOMOLOGY OF STRATA OF ABELIAN DIFFERENTIALS
PHILIP TOSTESON
Abstract. We show that the homology of strata of abelian differentials stabilizes in a range
where the number of simple zeros is large relative to the homological degree. In this range,
we show that the rational cohomology agrees with the restriction of the tautological classes
to the stratum, and that the rational Picard group is trivial for unprojectivized strata. Our
proof method is to develop an h-principle for these strata, valid in a range of homological
degrees that increases with the number of simple zeros. The same approach also applies to
higher order differentials.
1. Introduction
The Hodge bundle over the moduli space of genus g curves Hg = {C ∈Mg, ω ∈
Γ(C, Ω1
C)} admits a natural stratification into loci according to the multiplicities of the zeros
of ω. These strata of abelian differentials play an important role in intersection theory of
Mg and in Teichm¨uller dynamics. Their connected components were classified in a landmark
paper of Kontsevich–Zorich [KZ03], but little has been known about their higher homology
groups.
The homology of moduli spaces of curves stabilizes in the genus, as shown by Harer
[Har85], and the stable rational homology was determined by Madsen–Weiss [MW07] by
identifying the stable homology with the homology of an infinite loop space. The purpose
of this paper is to develop analogous results for strata of abelian differentials.

Higher order differentials. Our h-principle also applies to strata of higher order differ-
entials (Ω1
C)⊗k. In fact, the case k = 1 is significantly more difficult than the other cases:
because (Ω1
C)⊗k is (k −1)(2g −2)-jet ample for all curves C ∈Mg, it is possible to quickly
obtain the case k > 1 using the method of Aumonier-Das [AD23].
1.1. Proof method and structure of paper. Our argument for Theorem 1.10 follows the
typical structure of applications of Vassiliev’s method to h-principles. We construct a finite
dimensional approximation to the space of continuous sections and use Poincar´e duality
to reduce to proving that a Gysin map of compactly supported cohomology groups is an
isomorphism in a range. Then, using a simplicial resolution of a discriminant locus (in this
case sections of Ω1 that have zeros to higher order than prescribed), we construct spectral
sequences converging to the compactly supported cohomology of both sides, such that the
natural map between these spectral sequences an isomorphism in a range of degrees. The
main difficulty we face is in constructing a partial resolution of the space of algebraic sections
that both converges and has computable cohomology in a stable range.
In §2, we consider the poset of divisors in the universal curve of multiplicity ≥2 and
associated Bar constructions and spectral sequences. In §3, we state the geometric bounds
that give a spectral sequence converging to the compactly supported cohomology of Hg,µ in
a range. In §4 we prove Theorem 1.10, and in §5 we apply it to establish the other results
of this paper.
1.2. Relation to other work. Theorem 1.10 is in the spirit of recent results of Aumonier
[Aum25], Aumonier–Das [AD23], and Das–Tosteson [DT24] on comparing spaces of holo-
morphic maps and holomorphic sections to spaces of continuous sections.
A major obstacle to proving Theorem 1.10 is that it cannot be reduced to a fiberwise
statement (relative to the projection to Mg,n−m1(µ)/Sµ). Indeed, there are choices of C, D′
such that the space of nonsingular holomorphic sections of Γ(C, Ω1(−D′)) fails to approx-
imate the homology of Γtop(C, J1(Ω1(−D′))). Any proof must establish that the locus of
“obstructed fibers” does not contribute to the stable cohomology. In particular, the strategy
of Aumonier–Das [AD23] does not apply. Instead, we use a variant of Das–Tosteson’s frame-
work, working globally over Mg,n−m1(µ)/Sµ. We formulate a criterion for convergence of the
resolution in terms of certain dimension bounds on the locus of bad fibers. To obtain the
range in Theorem 1.10, we use geometric input: Clifford’s theorem, and the fact codimension
of the locus of d-gonal curves grows in g (see Proposition 3.2).
As mentioned in Remark 1.2, simultaneous independent results of Chen–Larson [CL26]
also give Theorem 1.1. Chen–Larson’s stable range is g/6 larger, and their method of proof is
more direct. On the other hand, our approach via the h-principle (Theorem 1.10) applies to
integral cohomology and allows us to exhibit stabilization maps that induce isomorphisms on
homology. Substituting a dimension bound that Chen–Larson establish into our argument
improves our stable range (see Remark 3.5).

Zorich [KZ03] have computed the connected components of all strata and shown that there
can be up to three connected components. Still, their answer displays uniformity as the

Full computation of stable integral cohomology. As mentioned above, our methods reduce

2. Posets of divisors of multiplicity ≥2
There are several possible choices for which algebraic object to use to build the resolution
of the discriminant locus (various topological posets, or topological categories). We choose
to adopt certain subposets of the Hilbert schemes of curves consisting of divisors whose
multiplicity at every point is ≥2.
In this section, we define the relevant posets, their
combinatorial stratifications, the associated spectral sequence for simplicial resolutions, and
a convergence criterion for simplicial resolutions. This material is closely analogous to §3,
§4, §5 of Das–Tosteson, with slightly different (in fact simpler) combinatorics.
We also
strengthen the key convergence criterion, Theorem 4.5, allowing for certain fibers to be
