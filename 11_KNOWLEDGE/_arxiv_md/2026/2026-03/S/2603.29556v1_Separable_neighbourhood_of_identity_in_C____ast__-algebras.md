Abstract
We study the structure of separable elements in bipartite C∗-algebras, focusing on the ex-
istence and size of a separable neighbourhood around the identity element. While this phe-
nomenon is well understood in the finite-dimensional setting, its extension to general C∗-
algebras presents additional challenges. We show that the problem of determining such a
neighbourhood can be reduced to estimating the completely bounded norm of contractive pos-
itive maps. This approach allows us to characterize the size of such neighbourhoods in terms
of structural properties of the algebra, notably its rank. As a consequence, we also resolve a
recent conjecture of Musat and Rørdam.
1. Introduction
Understanding various aspects of separability and entanglement is a central theme in quantum
information theory. In finite dimensional systems investigating the structure of separable states is
still an active area of research ([LMW24], [CÐ15]). In particular, the geometry of separable states
around the identity (maximally mixed state) has been well studied and plays an important role in
entanglement detection and robustness questions. However, exploring this phenomenon beyond
matrix algebras to general C∗-algebras is both natural and technically subtle. More concretely, it is
known ([GB02]) that in the finite dimensional setting, there is a nontrivial neighbourhood of the
bipartite identity element consisting entirely of separable elements. In other words, sufficiently
small perturbations of the identity cannot generate entanglement. The main question is then,
what happens in an arbitrary C∗-algebra setting?
In this paper we undertake this task, namely, given two C∗-algebras A and B, we explore
the problem of finding an optimal neighbourhood of separable elements of the bipartite identity
1A ⊗1B. By “separable elements” we mean the closure of the set of positive elements x ∈A ⊗min B
that admit the decomposition x = ∑i ai ⊗bi, where ai ∈A+, bi ∈B+. The identity element is the
natural reference point of the cone of positive elements, and in matrix algberas it is the dual of the
trace functional, so it makes sense to investigate this phenomenon based on the identity element.
In matrix algebras, separability questions can often be reformulated using positive maps via
the well known Horodecki criterion ([HHH96]). And using this connection, Gurvits and Burnum

result on the matrix algebra case (see Theorem 3.6).
There has been a long effort in understanding the theory of entanglement and separability
in operator algebras pioneered by Størmer ([Stø08]), Arveson ([Arv09]), Paulsen ([Pau]), and
other researchers ([PTT11, JKPP11]). In particular the Horodecki criterion has been generalized
by Størmer ([Stø09]) to the setting where one of the algebras is nuclear and the other is an UHF
algebra. Later Miller and Olkiewicz ([MO14]) extended this to nuclear C∗algebras. Note that
these generalizations were done to characterize "separable states" as opposed to “separable ele-
ments" in a C∗-algebra and there is a subtle difference in these two concepts if one goes beyond
the matrix algebra case. Since we are dealing with separable elements, in Section 4 we establish
the Horodecki criterion for for separable elements in nuclear C∗-algebras. Having this at our dis-
posal, we identify the problem of finding the separable neighbourhood of the bipartite identity
element 1A ⊗1B is equivalent to the problem of computing how large the cb norm of a positive
map can be from the algebra A to B. Now computing cb norms of completely bounded maps
is a fundamental problem in operator algebras and underpins many interesting concepts of the
underlying spaces ([Pau, Smi83, Tom83]). In particular, the rank of a C∗-algebra, i.e. the largest
dimension of an irreducible representation, plays a crucial role in determining how large the cb
norm of a linear map acting on it can be. We have our main result as follows

γ(A, B) := sup{r ∈R+ : 1A ⊗1B −x is separable for all x = x∗with ∥x∥⩽r}.

2. Preliminaries
We will study (completely) positive maps between C∗-algebras.
Definition 2.1. Let Φ : A →B be a linear map between C∗-algebras. It is called positive if Φ(a∗a) ⩾0 for
all a ∈A. It is called completely positive if for all n ∈N the amplification Φn := Idn ⊗Φ : Mn ⊗A →
Mn ⊗B is positive.
A good overview of completely positive maps can be found in [Pau] and [BO08]. An important
thing to note is that either the domain or the codomain is commutative then positive maps are
automatically completely positive. There are also useful criteria for complete positivity if either
the domain or the codomain is the matrix algebra.
Lemma 2.2 ([BO08, Chapter 1: Proposition 5.11, Proposition 5.13]). Let A be a C∗-algebra. The linear
map Φ : Mn →A is completely positive if and only if its Choi matrix Choi(A) := ∑n
i,j=1 eij ⊗Φ(eij) ∈
Mn ⊗A is positive.
The linear map Ψ : A →Mn is completely positive if and only if the functional bΨ : Mn ⊗A →C is
positive, where bΨ(∑i,j eij ⊗aij) = ∑n
i,j=1 Ψ(aij)ij.
Another important concept will be that of a completely bounded map, i.e. a map Φ : A →B
such that the amplifications Φn : Mn(A) →Mn(B) are uniformly bounded, i.e. supn∈N ∥Φn∥< ∞;
this quantity will be called the cb norm of Φ. Similarly to the case of positivity, bounded maps
into commutative C∗-algebras are automatically completely bounded, but it is not the case that
bounded maps with commutative domains are automatically completely bounded.
We will mostly study the maps between C∗-algebras by going to the induced maps between
biduals, their universal enveloping von Neumann algebras. The reason for utility of this construc-
tion stems from the fact that it is easier to study maps between von Neumann algebras, and in our
specific setting we will mostly have to deal with particularly nice von Neumann algebras. A lot
of information on this topic can be found in [Tak79, Chapter III.2]; a basic introduction can also be
found in [BO08, Chapter 1.4].
We will sometimes use a certain approximation property: nuclearity for C∗-algebras and injec-
tivity for von Neumann algebras. An important relationship between the two is that a C∗-algebra
is nuclear if and only if its bidual A∗∗is injective. A lot of information about these properties can
be found in the book [BO08].
3. The matrix algebra case
In this section we will clarify the relationship between bounds on the cb norms of positive maps
and the size of the neighbourhood consisting of separable elements.
Definition 3.1. A bipartite element X ∈(Mn ⊗Mm)+ is called separable if it admits a decomposition
X = ∑i Pi ⊗Qi, where 0 ≤Pi ∈Mn and 0 ≤Qi ∈Mm. This is a closed convex subset of Mn ⊗Mm and
it will be denoted by Sep(Mn ⊗Mm).
Definition 3.2. We define the following quantities:
• η(Mn, Mm) := sup{∥Φ∥cb : Φ : Mn →Mm, contractive positive map}.
• ηu(Mn, Mm) := sup{∥Φ∥cb : Φ : Mn →Mm, unital positive map}.

4. Horodeckis criterion for von Neumann algebras
An important generalization of the Horodeckis criterion appeared in [MO14]. However their re-
sults concern separable states and we work with separable elements in the C∗-algebras; unlike the
finite dimensional case, you cannot automatically translate between the two frameworks. Fortu-
nately their proof can be adapted to our setting and we will provide some details for the conve-
nience of the reader. Before that, let us formally define our sets of separable elements, as we will
have to distinguish the cases of C∗-algebras and von Neumann algebras.
Definition 4.1. If A and B are C∗-algebras. We denote by Sep(A ⊗B) the set of separable elements of
A ⊗B, i.e. the closure of the finite sums of the form ∑i ai ⊗bi, where the ai and the bi are positive.
For von Neumann algebras M and N we will use the notation Sepσ(M⊗N) to define an analogous set,
this time taking the closure in the weak∗-topology; elements of this set will be called separable, other positive
elements will be called entangled.
Remark 4.2. In the definition of Sep(A ⊗B) we might as well take the closure in the weak topology,
because the set is convex and the closure of a convex set is the same with respect to the weak and the norm
topologies, as a consequence of the Hahn-Banach separation theorem. This will be useful later, because the
weak topology on A is equal to the topology induced from the weak∗topology of A∗∗.
The main goal of this section is the following theorem, analogous to the main result of [MO14].
Theorem 4.3. Let x ∈M⊗N, where M is an injective von Neumann algebra. Then x ∈Sepσ(M⊗N) if
and only if (Id ⊗ϕ)(x) ∈M⊗M is positive for all positive, normal, finite rank maps ϕ : N →M.
Note that one direction is clear, so we only have to show that if applying positive maps to one
leg of the tensor preserves positivity then the element has to be separable. The crucial first step,
just like in [MO14], is the case M = Md and then we will use structural results for von Neumann
algebras to conclude the general case.

We will start our analysis by establishing a correspondence between the η and γ parameters

Proposition 5.2. We have γ(A, B) = γσ(A∗∗, B∗∗).
Proof. Suppose that γ(A, B) = r, i.e. for any Hermitian element H ∈A ⊗B of norm at most
r the element 1A ⊗1B −H is separable. Let now K = K∗∈A∗∗⊗B∗∗be of norm at most r. By
Kaplansky’s density theorem, we can approximate it in the strong operator topology by Hermitian
elements Ki from A ⊗B of norm at most r. By our assumption the elements 1A ⊗1B −Ki are
separable in A ⊗B. Since they converge strongly, in particular weak∗, to 1A ⊗1B −K ∈A∗∗⊗B∗∗,
these elements are separable as well. It follows that γ(A∗∗, B∗∗) ⩾r = γ(A, B).
Now assume that γ(A∗∗, B∗∗) = r and let H = H∗∈A ⊗B be a Hermitian element of norm at
most r. It follows that the element 1A ⊗1B −H is separable in A∗∗⊗B∗∗. Therefore 1A ⊗1B −H
is a weak∗-limit of finite sums of the form ∑i ai ⊗bi, where 0 ⩽ai ∈A∗∗and 0 ⩽bi ∈B∗∗.
We can use Kaplansky’s theorem to approximate each ai and bi by positive elements in A and B,
respectively. Therefore 1A ⊗1B −H can be approximated in the weak∗topology of A∗∗⊗B∗∗by
finite sums of the form ∑i Pi ⊗Qi, with 0 ⩽Pi ∈A and 0 ⩽Qi ∈B. But this means that these
finite sums converge to 1A ⊗1B −H in the weak topology of A ⊗B. As we already mentioned,
the cone of separable elements is closed and convex, therefore weakly closed, hence we conclude
that the elements 1A ⊗1B −H are separable. As a consequence γ(A, B) ⩾r = γ(A∗∗, B∗∗), which
finishes the proof.
Under extra assumptions, we will now obtain a similar result for η(A, B).
Proposition 5.3. Assume that B is a nuclear C∗-algebra. Then η(A, B) = ησ(A∗∗, B∗∗).
Proof. Let ϕ : A →B be a positive map. We can always consider the bidual map ϕ∗∗: A∗∗→B∗∗,
which has the same norm and cb norm as ϕ. It follows that η(A, B) ⩽ησ(A∗∗, B∗∗).
To go the other way, the issue is that not all normal maps from A∗∗to B∗∗arise from maps
from A to B. Let ψ : A∗∗→B∗∗be a normal positive map. We can consider the restriction
ψ|A, which will have the same norm and cb norm as ψ, by using Kaplansky’s density theorem.
Let us verify it for the norm; the proof for the cb norm will be the same as we can run the same
argument for amplifications. Indeed, suppose that for all contractions x ∈A we have ∥ψ(x)∥⩽C.
For a contraction x ∈A∗∗we can find a net of contractions xi ∈A converging strongly to x, in
particular in the weak∗-topology. Therefore limi ψ(xi) = ψ(x) in the weak∗topology. As we have
∥ψ(xi)∥⩽C and the balls are weak∗closed, it follows that ∥ψ(x)∥⩽C, therefore ∥ψ∥⩽∥ψ|A|∥,
and the other inequality is clear.
Now we have the map ψ|A : A →B∗∗and we have to construct maps going into B. Here we
will use nuclearity. Indeed, nuclearity of B forces B∗∗to be injective, therefore we can approxi-
mate the identity map by finite rank, normal completely positive maps. We will also use the fact

Remark 5.4. An alternative argument to finding maps from B∗∗into B can be given using Choi matrices.
Indeed, injectivity of B∗∗is also equivalent to the existence of approximate factorizations ϕλ : B∗∗→Mnλ
and ψλ : Mnλ →B∗∗such that ψλ ◦ϕλ converges to IdB∗∗in the point-weak∗topology. As the domain
of ψλ is a matrix algebra, we can use Lemma 2.2 to view it as an element of Mnλ(B∗∗). By Kaplansky’s
density theorem, this element can be approximated by positive elements in Mnλ(B), i.e. completely positive
maps from Mnλ into B. By composing with ϕλ, we obtain maps from B∗∗to B ⊂B∗∗, which converge to
the identity on B∗∗in the point-weak∗topology.

6. A question of Musat-Rørdam in [MR25]
In this section we answer a question that Musat-Rørdam asked in their recent article on entangle-
ment theory in C∗algebras.
Let κ(A, B) = sup{∥ρ∥max : ρ ∈S(A) ⊗∗S(B)}, where S(A) ⊗∗S(B) is the set of functionals
ρ on the algebraic tensor product A ⊙B such that ρ(1A ⊗1B) = 1 and ρ is positive on separable
elements. We would like to show the following
Proposition 6.1. We have κ(A, B) = min(rank(A), rank(B)).
Proof. By [MR25, Proposition 3.14 (iii)] this quantity is infinite if neither A nor B is subhomoge-
neous.
So we may assume that one of them is subhomogeneous. It was already noted in [MR25,
Proposition 3.4, Theorem 3.15] that the parameter κ(A, B) is related to η(A, B), at least in the case
of matrix algebras. We will show that it is still the case in our setup and we will conclude by our
results on η(A, B).
Without loss of generality, we can assume that A is subhomogeneous, because κ(A, B) =
κ(B, A). Note that any functional on A ⊗B can be viewed as a weak∗-continuous functional on
the bidual A∗∗⊗B∗∗. As A∗∗is a direct sum of matrix algebras tensored with commutative alge-
bras and separable elements split with respect to direct sums (Lemma 4.5), we may replace A∗∗
by L∞(X) ⊗Md, where d ⩽rank(A). As L∞(X) is commutative, it is easy to see that separable
elements in (L∞(X) ⊗Md)⊗B∗∗are the same as the ones in Md ⊗(L∞(X)⊗B∗∗), as in both cases
they can be viewed as functions on X valued in separable elements in Md ⊗B∗∗. In this case we
have a one-to-one correspondence between functionals bϕ positive on separable elements and pos-
itive maps ϕ from L∞(X)⊗B∗∗to Md given by bϕ(∑i ai ⊗bi) = tr(∑i aT
i ϕ(bi)). Moreover, unitality
of bϕ translates to tr(ϕ(1)) = 1.
We can recover bϕ using the formula bϕ(x ⊗y) = tr((∑i,j eij ⊗eij)(x ⊗ϕ(y))). Now we can
proceed just like in [MR25, Proposition 3.4], to show that any Φ ∈S(Md) ⊗∗S(L∞(X)⊗B∗∗) is of
the form Φ = ρ ◦(Id ⊗ϕ), where ϕ is a unital positive map from L∞(X)⊗B∗∗→Md and ρ is a
