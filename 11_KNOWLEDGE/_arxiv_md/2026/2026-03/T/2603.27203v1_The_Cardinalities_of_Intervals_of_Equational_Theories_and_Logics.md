Abstract
We study the cardinality of classes of equational theories (varieties) and logics
by applying descriptive set theory. We affirmatively solve open problems raised by
Jackson and Lee [Trans. Am. Math. Soc. 370 (2018), pp. 4785-4812] regarding
the cardinalities of subvariety lattices, and by Bezhanishvili et al. [J. Math. Log.
(2025), in press] regarding the degrees of the finite model property (fmp). By coding
equations and formulas by natural numbers, and theories and logics by real numbers,
we examine their position in the Borel hierarchy. We prove that every interval of
equational theories in a countable language corresponds to a Π0
1 set, and every fmp
span of a normal modal logic to a Π0
2 set. It follows that they have cardinality
either ≤ℵ0 or 2ℵ0, provably in ZFC. In the same manner, we observe that the set
of pretabular extensions of a tense logic is a Π0
2 set, so its cardinality is either ≤ℵ0
or 2ℵ0. We also point out a negative solution to another open problem raised by
Jackson and Lee [Trans. Am. Math. Soc. 370 (2018), pp. 4785-4812] regarding the
existence of independent systems, which relies on Jeˇzek et al. [Bull. Aust. Math.
Soc. 42 (1990), pp. 57-70].

Introduction
An important theme in the studies of universal algebra and non-classical logics involves
determining the cardinality of equational theories (equivalently, varieties) and logics. A
variety is an equationally definable class of algebras [4]. Finite monoids that generate
varieties with 2ℵ0 many subvarieties were constructed in [13], and later a finite monoid
that generates a variety with ℵ0 many subvarieties was found in [12]. In [12], Jackson and
Lee raised the question of whether the subvariety lattice of every variety has cardinality
either ≤ℵ0 or 2ℵ0 [12, Question 6.4 (ii)].
In the logical context, Jankov [14] showed that there are 2ℵ0 many distinct superin-
tuitionistic logics, and consequently 2ℵ0 many modal logics in extensions of K, K4, S4,
etc.. This laid a foundation for the study of cardinalities of classes of modal and super-
intuitionistic logics. Fine [11] showed that every normal extension of the modal logic
S4.3 is finitely axiomatizable. As a consequence, he obtained that S4.3 has countably

and superintuitionistic logics, we refer to [5].
For a superintuitionistic or modal logic L, the degree of fmp of L refers to the car-
dinality of the fmp span of L, that is, the set of logics that define the same class of
finite Kripke frames as L. Bezhanishvili et al. [2] proved that every cardinal κ ≤ℵ0
and κ = 2ℵ0 is realized as the degree of fmp for some superintuitionistic and transitive
modal logic. They also asked the question whether it is provable without the Continuum
Hypothesis (CH) that the degree of fmp can (only) be either ≤ℵ0 or 2ℵ0 [2, Section 8
(1)]. This question is closely connected to the one posed by Jackson and Lee. A logic is
tabular if it is characterized by a single finite Kripke frame, and is pretabular if it is not
tabular while all its consistent extensions are tabular. Recently, Chen [7] showed that
for every cardinal κ ≤ℵ0 and κ = 2ℵ0, there is a tense logic that has κ many pretabular
extensions. Even though not explicitly asked in the paper, the question of the cardinals
ℵ0 < κ < 2ℵ0 naturally arises in the same spirit as the above two open questions.
The common theme of these questions is the cardinality of classes of equational theo-
ries and logics between countable and continuum, hence they are inherently set-theoretic.
It is not to be expected that standard tool kits in the field of universal algebra or modal
and superintuitionistic logics could be useful for answering these kinds of questions, as
they fall in the scope of ZFC, of which CH is independent. An explicit construction of un-
countably many equational theories or logics typically results in constructing 2ℵ0-many
of such equational theories or logics.
In this paper, we apply descriptive set theory via coding to address the aforemen-
tioned three questions. The coding method aligns with the standard technique of G¨odel
numbers in computability theory. The idea behind the proofs is to effectively code equa-
tions (resp. formulas) by natural numbers, so that varieties (resp. logics) correspond to
subsets of ω, which in turn are identified with elements of the Cantor space 2ω, namely,
real numbers. Thus, instead of varieties or logics, we can count the corresponding sets
of reals. That descriptive set theory applies in this context is no surprise, as it is exactly
the theory of sets of reals. In descriptive set theory, sets of reals are classified in several
hierarchies in terms of their “complexity” (see Section 2), and their properties are stud-
ied with respect to their complexity. The main result that we rely on in this paper is
that Borel sets have cardinality either ≤ℵ0 or 2ℵ0.
Therefore, all we have to do is verify that the sets of reals corresponding to the sets
of equational theories and logics in question are Borel. Specifically, we show that an
interval of equational theories in a countable language corresponds to a Π0
1 set, and the
fmp span of a modal logic to a Π0
2 set. This implies that their cardinality is either ≤ℵ0
or 2ℵ0, provably in ZFC, answering the open questions by Jackson and Lee [12, Question
6.4 (ii)] and by Bezhanishvili et al. [2, Section 8 (1)] in the positive. On the other hand,
we also point out that it follows from [16] that there is an equational theory such that
its extensions form a continuum chain, which thus admits no independent system. This
example provides a negative solution to [12, Question 6.4 (i)]. Moreover, it implies that
the cardinality result of intervals of equational theories cannot be shown by constructing
independent systems, which explains why we need to employ the toolkit of descriptive set

2.2
Equational theories and logics
We refer to [4, 1] for basic facts of universal algebra. A language, or signature, is a set of
function symbols with each arity specified. Throughout the paper, we assume that the
language is countable. Given a language and a set X of variables, we define terms over
X in the language in the standard way. An equation, or identity, over X is an expression
of the form s ≈t where s and t are terms over X. Following the convention, we assume
X to be a countably infinite set and omit it if no confusion arises. A set Φ of equations
is an equational theory if Φ is the set of equations valid in a variety V, where a variety
is a class of algebras closed under homomorphic images, subalgebras, and products. We
can take the following syntactic characterization of equational theories as our working
definition of equational theories in this paper (see, e.g., [4, Chapter 2, Definition 14.16
and Theorem 14.17]).

Main results
The core idea behind the proofs in this section is coding. If the language is countable,
there are only countably many equations, which can be effectively coded by natural num-
bers. For instance, one may employ a standard G¨odel numbering based on prime factor-
ization or a pairing function. Then, the set Eq = {i ∈ω : i is a code of an equation} is

recursive. Let φi denote the equation with code i. Equational theories Φ then correspond
to particular kinds of elements A ∈2ω, i.e., infinite 0-1 sequences, in such a way that
A(i) = 1 iff φi ∈Φ. These will be the reals coding set of sentences satisfying some basic
properties, such as the inclusion of all identities s ≈s and closure under substitutions.
In that case, we also call A the code of Φ. We will be loose on the distinction between
an equational theory and its code if there is no confusion. As every equational theory
has a unique code, instead of counting equational theories, we can count elements of
2ω, i.e., reals, where descriptive set theory applies. If we can show that the set of reals
corresponding to a class of equational theories is in a specific complexity class, such as
Borel, we can draw from descriptive set theory consequences on the cardinality of that
class of equational theories. Since in modal logic the language is finite, it falls under a
special case of the above observation.
We start with intervals of equational theories. Let T and Eq respectively be the sets
of codes of all terms and equations. Let Φ0 and Φ1 be equational theories. Recall that
the interval between Φ0 and Φ1 is the set
[Φ1, Φ2] = {Φ : Φ is an equational theory such that Φ1 ⊆Φ ⊆Φ2}.
An interval in this sense may not be linearly ordered.

Lemma 3.3. Every equational theory has ≤ℵ0 or 2ℵ0 many extensions.
Proof. This follows from Lemma 3.2 by taking Φ1 to be the contradictory equational

Theorem 3.4. Every variety in a countable language has ≤ℵ0 or 2ℵ0 many subvarieties.
Proof. By Birkhoff’s theorem (see, e.g., [4, Theorem 11.9] and [1, Theorem 4.11]) that
a class of algebras is a variety iff it is equationally definable, there is a one-to-one
correspondence between varieties and equational theories.
Thus, we can reformulate
Lemma 3.2 and Lemma 3.3 in terms of varieties: if the language is countable, then for
any varieties V0 and V1, the interval [V0, V1] has cardinality ≤ℵ0 or 2ℵ0; in particular,
every variety has ≤ℵ0 or 2ℵ0 many subvarieties.
This answers the open question [12, Question 6.4 (ii)] in the affirmative.
Remark 3.5. Jackson and Lee also raised a second problem: Does every variety with
uncountably many subvarieties have an independent system extending its equational
theory [12, Question 6.4 (i)]? An independent system extending an equational theory
Φ is an infinite set Ψ of equations such that every subset Ψ′ ⊆Φ induces a distinct
equational theory Th(Φ ∪Ψ′). Note that a positive answer to this question would also
yield the conclusion of Theorem 3.4, as we can construct 2ℵ0 many equational theories
from 2ℵ0 many subsets of Ψ. However, we provide a negative answer to this question as
follows.

Next, we present an application of our method to logics and study the degrees of the
finite model property (degrees of fmp for short). The degrees of fmp were introduced in
[2] as a modified version of the degrees of Kripke incompleteness [10] using finite Kripke
frames. We first recall the definition for modal logics; the case of superintuitionistic
logics is analogous. Let L0 be a logic.
Definition 3.6. Let FFr be the set of all finite Kripke frames. For L ∈NExt L0, let
FFr(L) = {F ∈FFr : F ⊨L},
and the fmp span of L (in NExt L0) be the set
fmpL0(L) = {L′ ∈NExt L0 : FFr(L′) = FFr(L)}.
The degree of fmp of L (in NExt L0) is the cardinality of the set fmpL0(L).
The condition FFr(L) = FFr(L′) induces an equivalence relation on the lattice
NExt L0, and the fmp span of L refers to the equivalence class that L belongs to.
Thus, intuitively, the degree of fmp of L measures the extent to which L cannot be
distinguished from other logics by the means of finite Kripke frames. It was proved
in [2] that every cardinal 0 < κ ≤ℵ0 or κ = 2ℵ0 is realized as the degree of fmp for
superintuitionistic logics and transitive modal logics. So, the question naturally arises
whether any cardinal between ℵ0 and 2ℵ0 is also realized as the degree of fmp for some
logic. We show that this cannot be the case.

4
Conclusion and future research
In this paper, we applied descriptive set theory to study the cardinality of classes of
equational theories and logics via coding. The cardinality results were formulated for
equational theories, varieties, modal logics, and tense logics to match each specific set-
ting. However, the idea of coding applies to any formal system with a reasonable syntax
such as Theorem 2.3. For instance, the cardinality result of intervals (Lemma 3.2) also
holds for quasivarieties and universal classes. In practice, such results are of interest
only if there is some sort of antidichotomy result for cardinals ≤ℵ0 and 2ℵ0.
A concrete open question is discussed in Remark 3.5, namely [12, Question 6.4 (i)]
in the setting of finite languages, and specifically in the setting of monoids. We also
mention two limitations of our method. An obvious one is that it heavily relies on coding.
Therefore, the method works only if everything involved (e.g., equations, formulas, finite
Kripke frames) can be effectively and uniquely coded. For example, this is not the case
for Kripke frames, which form a proper class. We leave it for future research to apply
this method to count Kripke complete logics or the degree of Kripke incompleteness [10].
Another point is that the perfect set property is just one of the regular properties
that Borel sets enjoy. Other regular properties include Lebesgue measurability and the
Baire property. But these do not have immediate consequences for equational theories or
logics. We leave it for future research to obtain results for classes of equational theories
or logics, other than determining their cardinalities, by using the complexity of their
corresponding sets of reals.
