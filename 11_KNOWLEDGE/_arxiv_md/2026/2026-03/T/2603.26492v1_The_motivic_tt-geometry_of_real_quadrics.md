THE MOTIVIC TT-GEOMETRY OF REAL QUADRICS
JEAN PAUL SCHEMEIL
Abstract. We study the tensor-triangular geometry of the category of Voevodsky motives gen-
erated by real quadrics. At the prime 2, we determine its Balmer spectrum, and find that it is a
countably infinite, non-Noetherian space of Krull dimension 2. We detail the relationship between
this space, the real Artin-Tate spectrum computed by Balmer–Gallauer, and Vishik’s isotropic
points. We conclude by combining our computation with Balmer–Gallauer’s results on Artin-Tate
motives to obtain a full description of the spectrum of integral motives of quadrics over real algebraic
numbers.

1. Introduction
Tensor-triangular geometry (tt-geometry for short) has found fruitful applications in many settings
[Bal10]. A particularly active direction is motivic tt-geometry, whose long-term goal is to classify
motives up to the tensor-triangulated structure of their ambient category
DM(k) := DM(k; Z),
introduced by Voevodsky [Voe00].
For geometric motives (the compact objects of DM(k)), this
amounts to describing the Balmer spectrum [Bal05] of the tt-category DMgm(k).
Although the
current status of the problem remains largely open, recent work of Vishik [Vis24; Vis25] has provided
profound insights into the complexity of this space. When the characteristic of the base field k is zero,
Vishik defined for any prime number p the category of isotropic motives DM(k/k; Fp) by localising
DM(k; Fp) at motives of p-anisotropic varieties (those whose closed points have degree divisible by
p). Using this framework, Vishik constructed a family of isotropic realisation functors
ψE,p : DM(k) →DM(E(P∞)/E(P∞); Fp)
indexed over all primes p and field extensions E/k.
When restricted to geometric motives, the
kernels of these functors detect the largest currently known family of closed points of the spectrum

Vishik to establish that Spc(DMgm(R)) has cardinality 2c. This bound provides a first measure of

A sensible approach to navigate this complexity is to study the spectrum of the motivic category
"from below": rather than confronting DMgm(k) all at once, one considers the tt-geometry of carefully
chosen full tt-subcategories of the motivic category. The relevance of this strategy relies on a general
theorem of Balmer [Bal18] who showed that the inclusion of a full tt-subcategory
L ,→K
induces a continuous surjective restriction map on spectra
Spc(K) ↠Spc(L).
This surjection effectively provides a lower bound on the complexity of the ambient spectrum. In
the motivic setting, manifestations of this idea are exemplified by the work of Peter [Pet13] and
Gallauer [Gal19] who initiated the study of the spectrum of Tate motives DTMgm(k); Balmer-Gallauer
[BG22; BG25] who introduced the study of the spectrum of Artin motives DAMgm(k) and Artin-Tate
motives DATMgm(k); and Sparks [Spa25] who studied the tt-geometry of these categories in isotropic
contexts. The relevance of restricting to such full tt-subcategories is that their tt-geometry can often
be described explicitly in terms of specific algebraic invariants of the base field. For example, over
algebraically closed and real closed fields, Gallauer [Gal19] and Balmer-Gallauer [BG22] demonstrated
that the spectrum of Tate motives admits a description in terms of the motivic cohomology of the
base field; for Artin motives, the recent breakthrough of Balmer-Gallauer [BG25] has shown that,
over any field, the spectrum can be expressed in terms of the absolute Galois group of the base
field. Furthermore, Artin-Tate motives over real closed fields, Balmer-Gallauer [BG22] have obtained
a corresponding description involving a combination of the above invariants. Finally, in isotropic
contexts, Sparks [Spa25] has shown that at the prime 2, the tt-geometry of isotropic (Artin-)Tate
motives over flexible fields can be described in terms of the isotropic motivic cohomology of the base
field.
Following the themes outlined above, in this article we propose to initiate the study of the Balmer
spectrum of the full tt-subcategory generated by motives of smooth projective quadrics
DQMgm(k; R).
Originally introduced by Bachmann [Bac17] to study the Picard group of the motivic category, this
subcategory provides a natural first candidate to incorporate motives of higher dimensional varieties
into the landscape of motivic tt-geometry. To set the stage, we briefly address the case when the base
field k is algebraically closed. Because all quadratic forms over such a field are isotropic, the motives
of quadrics completely decompose into Tate motives; consequently, DQMgm(k, R) = DTMgm(k, R).
Thanks to the work of Peter [Pet13] and Gallauer [Gal19], the tt-geometry of this category is already
well understood. However, if k is not algebraically closed, DTMgm(k; R) forms a proper tt-subcategory
of DQMgm(k; R). In general, it is expected that the tt-geometry of DQMgm(k; R) reflects the com-
plexity of the theory of quadratic forms over k: this is because DQMgm(k; R) is generated by Tate
motives and by motives of anisotropic quadrics, which correspond to anisotropic quadratic forms over
k. This naturally identifies the case k = R (or more generally, any real closed field) as the natural
first step beyond the algebraically closed fields, and it will represent the primary focus of this article.
Indeed, we have very good control over the theory of quadratic forms over R; moreover, if R = Fp
for p an odd prime (or more generally, 2 is invertible in R), then motives of quadrics decompose into
Tate motives and motives of quadratic extensions (i.e. M(C)), in particular we have an equivalence
DATMgm(R; Fp) ≃DQMgm(R; Fp).
