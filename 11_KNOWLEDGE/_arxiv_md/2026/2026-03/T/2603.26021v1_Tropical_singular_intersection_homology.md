ABSTRACT. We introduce tropical singular intersection homologies (non-GM and GM) with the
tropical coefficients on rational polyhedral spaces using their filtrations. We investigate their fun-
damental properties. In the non-GM case, we give a Poincaré duality and two bilinear pairings
analogous to the cup and cap products under some assumptions. We compute the homologies in
some cases.

1.4. Background and related works. Intersection homology was first introduced by Goresky and
MacPherson in [GM80] in order to generalize some salient features of manifold theory to spaces
with singularities. They used the language of piecewise linear (PL) chain complexes and worked
over PL pseudomanifolds without codimension-one strata. The intersection homology was soon
reformulated in terms of sheaves in derived categories, as suggested by Deligne [GM83], where
the functorial machinery of derived categories could be applied. King [Kin85] introduced the sin-
gular chain intersection homology, and Friedman and McClure [FM13] defined the singular chain
intersection homology with possibly codimension-one strata. Friedman introduced two types of
singular intersection homologies: the GM intersection homology and the non-GM intersection ho-
mology (see [Fri20]). The GM and non-GM intersection homology groups coincide under the
classical assumption (that is, without codimension-one strata and with an adequate perversity), but
they differ in general. Furthermore, Friedman and McClure [FM13] proved the Künneth formula
and the Poincaré duality for the non-GM intersection homology, while these formulas do not gener-
ally hold for the GM intersection homology. The textbook by Maxim [Max19] covers the theory of
intersection (co)homology from the view point of algebraic geometry. The textbook by Friedman
[Fri20] treats the general theory of intersection (co)homology.
Tropical geometry is a relatively new field at the intersection of algebraic geometry and combi-
natorics. Itenberg, Mikhalkin, Katzarkov, and Zharkov [IKMZ19] defined the tropical homology
for certain polyhedral complexes in tropical projective space. Jell, Shaw, and Smacka [JSS19] and
Jell, Rau, and Shaw [JRS18] generalized this definition to rational polyhedral spaces with face
structures and proved the Poincaré duality. Gross and Shokrieh [GS23] provided an alternative

Our work is related to that of Mikami [Mik25] in that both papers focus on intersection (co)homology

[Mik25].
1.5. Structure of the paper. In §2, we recall the theory of rational polyhedral spaces following
[MZ14, JRS18, GS23] and the theory of singular intersection homology following [Fri20]. In §3,
we first show that the singular tropical intersection homology satisfies basic properties analogous
to those of the ordinary singular intersection homology (cf. [Fri20]). We then introduce a sheaf-
theoretic definition of the tropical intersection homology. In §4, we show a Poincaré duality for
the non-GM case by using the generalized Deligne sheaf theory from [Fri10]. In §5, we present
several examples and computations. In §5.1, we provide a counterexample to the Poincaré duality
in the GM case similar to that in the ordinary singular intersection homology. In §5.2, we compute
tropical intersection homology groups for the 1-dimensional case. In §5.3, we give examples of
tropical intersection homology groups using various filtrations.
