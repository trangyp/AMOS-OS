1. Introduction
The study of the Strong and Weak Lefschetz Properties of graded Artinian rings is an eclectic
mix of many areas of mathematics, including commutative algebra, algebraic geometry, linear
algebra, combinatorics, and representation theory. The origins of this topic can be traced back
to work of Stanley [20], Watanabe [24], and Reid, Roberts and Roitman [22]. For introductions
to this area, see [9, 16], and for a sample of recent research, see [1]. In this paper, we consider the
Lefschetz properties for a family of graded Artinian rings constructed from the van der Waerden
simplicial complexes vdw(n, k), a family of complexes introduced by Ehrenborg, Govindaiah,
Park, and Readdy[7] within the context of combinatorics and number theory.
Let R = K[x1, . . . , xn] be a polynomial ring over a field K, and suppose I is a homogeneous
ideal of R such that

the WLP or SLP. For example, it has long been conjectured that all graded Artinian complete
intersections have the SLP in characteristic zero [22].

2. Background
Let R = K[x1, . . . , xn] be a polynomial ring over an infinite field K. At this point, we make
no additional assumptions on the characteristic of K, although certain results will require the
characteristic to be zero. We continue to use the terminology defined in the introduction.
2.1. Lefschetz Properties. We review some of the needed results about the WLP and SLP.
As noted in the introduction, an Artinian ring has the the WLP if there is a general linear form
ℓsuch that the map ×ℓ: Ai →Ai+1 has maximal rank for all i ≥0.
Gaining access to this general form is sometimes difficult for an arbitrary Artinian ring.
However, since the Artinian rings we consider have the form R/I where I is a monomial ideal,
Migliore, Mir´o-Roig, and Nagel [15, Proposition 2.2] (which assumes the field is infinite) and
Lundqvist and Nicklasson [14, Proposition 4.3] (which does not assume any condition on the
cardinality of the field), showed that to check if R/I has the WLP, it suffices to check if the map
given by multiplication by a specific linear form has maximal rank. Precisely,
Theorem 2.1. Suppose I ⊆R = K[x1, . . . , xn] is a monomial ideal such that A = R/I is an
Artinian ring. Then A has the WLP if and only if the map ×ℓ: Ai →Ai+1 where
ℓ= x1 + · · · + xn
has maximal rank for all i ≥0.
The next result was first shown by Stanley [20], using the Hard Lefschetz Theorem. Watanabe
[24], and Reid, Roberts, and Roitman [22] gave alternative proofs to this foundational result.
Theorem 2.2. Let n ≥1 be an integer, and suppose a1, . . . , an are positive integers with ai ≥2
for i = 1, . . . , n. If the characteristic of K is zero, then the Artinian ring
K[x1, x2, . . . xn]/⟨xa1
1 , xa2
2 , . . . , xan
n ⟩
is a complete intersection that has the SLP.
We use this result for the special case that a1 = · · · = an = 2.
2.2. Simplicial complexes and the van der Waerden simplicial complexes. A simplicial
complex on a finite set of vertices X = {x1, x2, , . . . , xn}, denoted by ∆, is a collection of subsets
of X such that: (i) {xi} ∈∆for all xi ∈X, and (ii) if S ∈∆and A ⊆S, then A ∈∆. Note that
we often omit the empty set when listing out the elements of ∆. While some authors sometimes
omit (i) when defining simplicial complexes, for our purpose we include this requirement; this
prevents us from considering degenerate cases.
The elements of ∆are called faces. The faces that are maximal with respect to containment
are called the facets of ∆. If F1, F2, . . . , Fs are the facets of ∆, then we write ∆= ⟨F1, F2, . . . , Fs⟩,
and say ∆is generated by F1, F2, . . . , Fs.
The dimension of a face F ∈∆is dim(F) = |F|−1. Accordingly, the dimension of the empty
set is −1. The dimension of ∆, denoted dim ∆, is the maximal dimension of all of its faces. If
d = dim ∆, then the f-vector of ∆is the (d+2)-tuple f(∆) = (f−1, f0, f1, . . . , fd) where fi is the
number of faces of dimension i of ∆. We sometimes write fi(∆) for fi if we want to distinguish
the simplicial complex to which we are referring. Note that f−1 = 1.
The Stanley-Reisner ideal allows one to study ∆via commutative algebra.

3. Families of A(vdw(n, k)) with the Strong and Weak Lefschetz Properties
In this section, we identify some families of rings A(vdw(n, k)) that have either the SLP or
WLP. Informally, this will happen if either k is small or large, when compared to n.
We begin by showing that the ring A(vdw(n, k)) always has the WLP in the first two degrees
for any van der Waerden complex vdw(n, k).

5. Pseudo-manifolds
In this section we classify when vdw(n, k) is a pseudo-manifold (with or without boundary).
Our classification allows us to use results of Dao and Nair [6] to deduce when our algebras
A(vdw(n, k)) have some Lefschetz properties. Recall the definition of a pseudo-manifold.
Definition 5.1. A k-dimensional simplicial complex is a pseudo-manifold if
(1) all the facets have the same dimension;
(2) all k −1 dimensional faces are contained in a maximum of two facets; and
(3) given any two facets, Fa, Fb of the simplicial complex, there exists a sequence of facets
Fa = G0, G1, G2,. . . , Gn = Fb such that the dimension of Gi ∩Gi+1 is k −1 for all
0 ≤i ≤n −1.
A k-dimensional pseudo-manifold has a boundary if it has at least one k −1 dimensional face
that belongs to exactly one facet.
