The local geometry of the stack of Ar-stable curves
Davide Gori, Ludvig Modin and Michele Pernice
April 1, 2026
Abstract
In this paper we study the local geometry of the stack of pointed Ar-stable curves. In particular,
we analyze the deformation theory of Ar-stable curves and their automorphism groups in order
to study the combinatorics of families of curves over [A1/Gm], and use this to classify all closed
points of the stack of Ar-stable curves. As a byproduct, we also classify all open substacks of the
moduli stack of degree 2 cyclic covers of P1 that admit a separated good moduli space. This is the
first in a series of three papers aimed at studying obstructions for the existence of good moduli
spaces for stacks of curves with A-type singularities, and using these to find an open substack of
the stack of Ar-stable curves that admits a proper non-projective good moduli space when r = 5.

Introduction
The moduli space of smooth curves is one of the most studied moduli spaces in algebraic geometry, going
back to Riemann. In [DM69], Deligne and Mumford constructed the first modular compactification
using algebraic methods. In later years, new modular compactifications appeared in the literature.
Among the most prominent examples are the Schubert compactification [Sch91] and those arising in
the Hassett–Keel program, which investigates the modularity of the log-Minimal Model Program of
Mg,n (see for instance [HH09,HH13,AFSvdW17,AFS17a,AFS17b]), all of these involve curves with A-
type singularities. Further interplay between the log-MMP of Mg,n and alternative compactifications
can be found in [CTV23,CTV21,Gor26].
The first compactifications were constructed using Geometric Invariant Theory (GIT for short), via
an analysis of stability in the Hilbert and Chow schemes. The series of papers [AFSvdW17,AFS17a,

AFS17b] introduce a more intrinsic approach, using what is called local Variation of GIT (VGIT for
short). New compactifications constructed later in [CTV23] use the same technique.
This paper is the first in a series dedicated to the problem of constructing modular compactifications us-
ing the intrinsic techniques within the framework of the “Beyond GIT” program introduced in [HL14].
Namely, we aim to use the criteria introduced in [AHLH23] and verify Θ- and S-completeness to con-
struct new modular compactifications that admit a proper good moduli space, which is not projective.
This modular compactification will be contained inside the moduli stack Mr
g,n parametrizing Ar-stable
curves, i.e. curves with at worst Ar-singularities and such that the log-dualizing line bundle is ample.
We work over a field of characteristic 0, the results generalize in characteristic big enough with respect
to (g, n), similarly to what is done in [Gor26, Definition 4.1.1].
This paper aims at providing a combinatorial classification of isotrivial degenerations of Ar-stable
curves, equivalently morphisms Θ := [A1/Gm] →Mr
g,n. This is achieved by:
1) classifying all the curves with positive-dimensional stabilizers (see Corollary 3.26);
2) studying their equivariant deformation theory, which allows us to control how the combinatorics
of the curve changes along Gm-equivariant deformations (see Proposition 3.38 and Proposi-
tion 3.41);
3) globalize the classification, which allows us to determine whether an isotrivial degeneration be-
tween two curves exists or not, only based on the combinatorics of the curves (see the results in
Section 3.4).
An important byproduct of the study of isotrivial degenerations is Theorem 3.52, where we describe
the closed points of Mr
g,n. As a corollary, we get the following result.

In other words, if g is sufficiently large relative to r, then the good moduli space of our modular
compactification descends to a compactification of the coarse moduli space of smooth curves. Es-
sentially, the restriction on r comes from smooth hyperelliptic curves admitting nontrivial isotrivial
degenerations to curves with one A2g-singularity (respectively one A2g+1-singularity).
The combinatorial classification of isotrivial degenerations will also be crucial to deal with Θ-completeness
and S-completeness in the future papers of the series, and it relies heavily on the strong connection
between A-singularities and (reduced) 2:1 covers of the projective line, which we call honestly hyper-
elliptic curves (following [Cat82]). More precisely, given a generically smooth family of curves over a
dvr whose special fiber contains an A-singularity, we can locally modify the family by replacing such
singularity with an hyperelliptic curve (up to a dvr extension). This process is called semistabilization
and can be done explicitly with ADE singularities; see for instance [Fed13] and [CML13]. In the case
of an Ar-singularity, the semistabilization procedure can be described depending on the parity of r:
(e) if r is even, then a blowup in a smoothing family of the singularity replaces the singularity with
an honestly hyperelliptic curve of genus r/2 (with at worst Ar−1-singularities) attached to the
rest of the curve in a Weierstrass point;
(o) if r is odd, then an appropriate blowup (described in loc. cit.) in a smoothing family of the
singularity replaces the singularity with an honestly hyperelliptic curve of genus (r −1)/2 (with
at worst Ar−1-singularities) attached to the rest of the curve in two points exchanged by the
hyperelliptic involution.
The semistable replacement can be obtained by repeating the process in order to end up with only
nodal singularities. This strong connection is also reflected in the deformation space of Ar-singularities

degenerations to the Gm-equivariant geometry of the deformation spaces of Ar-stable curves. In the

1.1
Local structure and good moduli spaces
Let us start by recalling the notion of good moduli spaces and the ´etale local structure for algebraic
stacks.
Good moduli spaces were introduced in [Alp13] as a characterization of the key properties of morphisms
[Spec A/G] →Spec A//G, where G is a linearly reductive group acting on Spec A and Spec A//G is
the quotient constructed by geometric invariant theory (i.e. the spectrum of the G-invariants of A).
The definition that captures these properties is surprisingly sparse.
Definition 1.1. A quasi-compact quasi-separated morphism π : X →X to an algebraic space X is a
good moduli space if the following holds.
1. the canonical morphism OX →π∗OX is an isomorphism,
2. the functor π∗: QCoh(X) →QCoh(X) is exact.
Other than geometric invariant theory quotients, a key example to keep in mind is that a classifying
stack BG admits a good moduli space if and only if G is a linearly reductive group scheme. Fur-
thermore, if X admits a good moduli space then so does every closed substack of X, which has the
consequence that the automorphism group schemes of the closed points of X are linearly reductive.
When a stack is not constructed as a quotient of a quasi-projective scheme by a reductive action,
one cannot use the methods of geometric invariant theory directly to study whether it admits a good
moduli space: there are however strong local structure results that allow one to do this ´etale locally.
We state the first version of the theorem that appeared, which is for stacks locally of finite type over
an algebraically closed field; there are also versions over general bases with weaker assumptions than
here, namely [AHR25, Theorem A] and [AHHLR24, Theorems 1.3,1.4,1.5 and 1.6].
Theorem 1.2 [AHR20]. Let X be an algebraic stack, locally of finite type and quasi-separated over an
algebraically closed field κ, with affine stabilizers. Let x ∈X(κ) be a point and H ⊂Gx a subgroup
of the stabilizer of x such that H is linearly reductive and Gx/H is ´etale. Then there exists an affine
scheme Spec A with an action of H, a κ-point w ∈Spec A fixed by H and an ´etale morphism of pointed
stacks
f : ([Spec A/H], w) →(X, x)
such that f −1(BGx) = BH, in particular f induces the given inclusion H ⊂Gx on stabilizer group
schemes at w. Moreover, if X has affine diagonal, then f can be arranged to be affine.
In particular, this implies that every point of a stack with linearly reductive stabilizer has an ´etale
neighborhood that admits a good moduli space, namely [Spec A/Gx] →Spec AGx. With this in hand,
one would like to glue these local good moduli spaces to a global one: for instance, the linearization

This section is dedicated to the introductory material regarding curves with singularities of type A.

Definition 3.49. We say that a n-pointed Ar-stable curve of genus g over an algebraically closed field

1. every Ah-singularity, with h ≥2, is atomic, that is it lies in an Ai- or Ai/Aj-attached atom
