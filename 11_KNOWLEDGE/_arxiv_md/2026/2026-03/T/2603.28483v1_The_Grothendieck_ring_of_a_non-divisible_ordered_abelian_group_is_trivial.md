THE GROTHENDIECK RING OF A NON-DIVISIBLE
ORDERED ABELIAN GROUP IS TRIVIAL
BLAISE BOISSONNEAU, MATHIAS STOUT, AND FLORIS VERMEULEN
Abstract. We consider the model-theoretic Grothendieck ring
of definable sets in ordered abelian groups. It is well-known that
KQ ∼= Z[T]/(T 2 + T) and KZ = 0, but surprisingly little is known
about other cases. We present a short computation which shows
that they all collapse: KG = 0, unless G is divisible.
We consider an ordered abelian group G, equipped with the natural
Loag = {0, +, −, <}-structure. By definable in G we mean Loag(G)-
definable (i.e. with parameters). Write Def(G) for the collection of all
definable sets in Gn, for n ∈N.
We study the model-theoretic Grothendieck ring KG of G, as in-
troduced by Krajiˇcek-Scanlon in [9]. The underlying group is given
by
• Generators: [A] for all A ∈Def(G).
• Relations:
(1) [A] = [B] if there exists a definable bijection A
∼−→B,
(2) [A \ B] + [B] = [A] for all A, B ∈Def(G) with B ⊆A.
Then KG is naturally a ring for the multiplication induced by the
cartesian product: [A] · [B] = [A × B]. It has a zero element [∅] = 0,
and for any a ∈G it holds that [{a}] = 1. Note that this construction
works more generally for any first-order structure.
It is almost immediate to see that KZ = 0. Indeed, we have that
[Z>0] + [{0}] = [Z>0], by using the translation x 7→x + 1. More gen-
erally, this argument works for any discretely ordered abelian group.
On the other hand, it is known that if G is divisible then KG =
Z[T]/(T 2 + T) by results of Kageyama-Fujita [8].

Somewhat surprisingly, not much is known about the intermediate
case, despite the interest in the Grothendieck rings of adjacent struc-
tures, such as valued fields [4, 2, 7, 5, 10] (e.g. for the purposes of
motivic integration). To the best of our knowledge, the only available
result in this setting is due to Bhardwaj and Moonen [1], proving that
KG is always a quotient of (Z/qZ)[T]/(T 2 + T), where q is the largest
odd prime dividing p −1 for all primes p for which G is not p-divisible.
In fact, more is true:
Theorem 1. The Grothendieck ring of definable sets of any non-divisible
abelian ordered group is trivial: KG = 0.
This theorem follows from the calculations in Lemma 2 below, which
builds upon the work in [8] and [1]. We present these calculations in
full to keep this note self-contained.

Remark 1. Although the Grothendieck rings of all ordered abelian
groups are now known, many interesting challenges remain. One could
rather attempt to compute the Grothendieck semiring, which amounts
to classifying all definable sets up to definable bijections. This seems

out of reach for general ordered abelian groups as it is already nontriv-
ial in the discrete [3] and divisible case [6]. A more reasonable challenge
would be to consider dp-minimal groups of rank 1.
In another direction, there are many variants of KG that come up
naturally, e.g. in the context of motivic integration ([7, 10]) whose full
structure is not yet known. Essentially, these variants are given by
restricting the notion of morphism. We mention three cases of interest:
(1) The graded Grothendieck semiring KG[∗], where one only con-
siders definable maps between subsets of the same ambient di-
mension.
(2) The ring obtained by further restricting to piecewise GLn(Z) ⋉
Gn-maps. More generally, one may replace Z by any subring of
Q generated by inverting some set of primes over Z.
(3) Measured versions of the above rings: the basic objects are
now pairs (X, µ) consisting of a definable set and a definable
map µ: X →G. A morphism f : (X, µ) →(Y, ω) consists of a
definable bijection f : X →Y which is piecewise GLn(Z) ⋉Gn
and which is measure-preserving, meaning that
x1 + · · · + xn + µ(x) = f1(x) + · · · + fn(x) + ω(x).
