COMPACT SETS IN LOCALLY COMPACT GROUPS
CHUCK AKEMANN
Abstract. Let G be a non-discrete, locally compact group with
Haar measure m. We prove that there exists a compact set K ⊂
G with m(K) = 0 such that KK−1 contains a neighborhood of
the identity. Moreover, such a set may be constructed inside any
prescribed neighborhood of the identity.
1. Introduction
Let G be a non-discrete, locally compact group with left Haar mea-
sure m. The classical Steinhaus–Weil theorem asserts that if A ⊂G has
positive measure, then AA−1 contains a neighborhood of the identity
(see, e.g., Hewitt and Ross [1, Corollary 20.17]).
That the converse fails is well known in abelian groups (for example
on the torus), but the general nonabelian case does not appear to be
explicitly recorded.
We prove that the converse fails in a strong form for all non-discrete
locally compact groups.

5. Proof of the main theorem
Proof. Let G be a non-discrete, locally compact group and let O be an
open neighborhood of the identity.
By Yamabe’s theorem (see, e.g., Montgomery and Zippin [4, Sec-
tion 4.6]), there exists an open subgroup G′ of G and a compact normal
subgroup N ⊂O ∩G′ of G′ such that H = G′/N is a Lie group. Let
ρ : G′ →H be the canonical quotient map.
Case 1:
dim H > 0.
Choose an open neighborhood W of the
identity in H such that ρ−1(W) ⊂O ∩G′. By the Lie case, there exists
a compact set KH ⊂W with mH(KH) = 0 such that KHK−1
H contains
a neighborhood V of the identity in H. Set K := ρ−1(KH). Then K is
compact and K ⊂O. By the pullback lemma, mG(K) = 0. Moreover,
KK−1 = ρ−1(KHK−1
H ) ⊃ρ−1(V ), which is open in G′ and hence in G.
Case 2: dim H = 0. Then H is discrete, so N is open in G′ (hence
open in G) and compact. Since G is non-discrete, N is infinite. By
the structure theory of compact groups (Hofmann–Morris [2, Chapter
9]), either N is totally disconnected or there exists a closed normal
subgroup M ⊂N such that N/M is a compact Lie group of positive
dimension.
- If N is totally disconnected, apply the profinite-case lemma inside
N to obtain the desired K ⊂N ⊂O. Since N is open, any neighbor-
hood in N is a neighborhood in G. - If N/M has positive dimension,
repeat Case 1 inside N: the resulting set K ⊂N is compact, null in N
(hence in G), and KK−1 contains a neighborhood of the identity in N
(hence in G).
This completes the proof.
□
