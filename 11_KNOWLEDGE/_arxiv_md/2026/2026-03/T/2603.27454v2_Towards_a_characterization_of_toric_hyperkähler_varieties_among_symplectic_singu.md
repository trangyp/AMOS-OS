In this article we prove that, when wt(ω) = 2, after replacing the original conical
C∗-action on (X, ω) by another (algebraic) conical C∗-action on (X, ω), we can take
a new isomorphism φ′ in a C∗-equivariant way. In particular, such φ′ is an algebraic
isomorphism. This answers Question 5.10 posed in [Part I].
The following is a basic strategy for the proof. In the situation of Theorem 5.8 of [Part
I], we pull back the standard conical C∗-action on (Y (A, 0), ωY (A,0)) by φ and get a conical
C∗-action on (X, ω). However, this is a complex analytic C∗-action. Here we replace the
original C∗-action on X with a new algebraic C∗-action on X by using the Hamiltonian
T n-action (cf. (2.2)) and compare it with the complex analytic C∗-action. Then we can
construct a complex analytic T n-equivariant automorphism ϕ : (X, ω) →(X, ω) so that
the complex analytic C∗-action on the lefthand side is transformed to the new C∗-action
on the righthand side. Now the composite φ′ := φ ◦ϕ−1 is the desired one.
