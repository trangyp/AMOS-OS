In this paper, we prove an explicit formula for the motivic local density
of isolated surfaces singularities. We use an additional data, the inner
rate related to the bilipschitz geometry of the singularity. Those inner
rates were first introduced in [1] by L. Birbrair, W. D. Neumann and
A. Pichon, and were studied in [14] by A. Belotto da Silva, L. Fantini
and A. Pichon. They study the metric structure of the germ of an iso-
lated complex surface singularity (X, 0) by means of an infinite family of
numerical analytic invariants, called inner rates. A resolution of (X, 0)
is good if its exceptional locus is a normal crossing divisor.
Given a
good resolution π : Y −→X of (X, 0) that factors through the blowup
of the maximal ideal and through the Nash modification of (X, 0), an
irreducible component Ei of the exceptional divisor π−1(0) of π, and a
small neighborhood N(Ei) of Ei in Y with a neighborhood of each dou-
ble point of π−1(0) removed, the inner rate qi of Ei is a rational number
that measures how fast the subset π(N(Ei)) of (X, 0) shrinks when ap-
proaching the origin (see Section 3). The inner rate qi is independent of
the choice of an embedding of (X, 0) into a smooth germ, only depending
on the analytic type of (X, 0) and on the divisor Ei. The finite set of
inner rates which are bilipschitz invariants for the inner metric has been
described explicitly in [1]. It is worth noting that an inner rate equal to
1 characterizes a metrically conical region of the surface, it corresponds
to linear inner contact between two complex curve germs on (X, 0).

2. Recap on motivic integration
Historically, Batyrev showed the equality of Betti Numbers of two
complex proper smooth birational Calabi-Yau varities over C using p-
adic integration and Weil conjectures for zeta functions. Kontsevich con-
structed the motivic integral in order to generalize this result and showed
the equality of Hodge Numbers. Several motivic integration theories had
been developped in the last thirty years. The sets we want to measure
are the semi-algebraic in valued fields, they are seen as subsets of the arc
space of variety over the residue field in Kontsevich/Denef-Loeser the-
ory, and as definable sets in a first order theory in the Cluckers-Loeser
version.
We are considering the theory of Cluckers-Loeser (See [4]), specialized
to the case of algebraically closed residue field. Let us recall the setting.
Fix k a field of characteristic zero. Consider the three sorted language
of Denef-Pas, with one sort for the valued field, with the language of
rings {0, 1, +, −, ·}, one sort for the residue field with the language of
rings and one sort for the value group with the Presburger language
{0, +, ≤, {≡n}n≥1} where ≡n is a binary relation for congruence modulo
n.
