The nonlinear chaotic nature of the Navier-Stokes equations and the practical limits
32
of real-world measurements mean that turbulence is practically unpredictable from a
33
deterministic point of view. Any tiny measurement error in the velocity ﬁeld will rapidly
34
grow and propagate, leading to a large mismatch between a computational prediction
35
and a later measurement of the ﬂow ﬁeld. Due to this fact, and the engineering pressure
36
to concentrate on averaged ﬂow ﬁeld quantities, the only experimentally reproducible,
37
measurable, and (in principle) predictable quantities are statistical (Monin & Yaglom
38
1971,1975; Hopf 1952). In this formalism, the velocity ﬁeld 푢(푥) is treated as a random
39
variable that is fully characterized by its probability measure. 푁-point statistics are deﬁned
40
as statistical measures captured by the probability measures (PDFs) of the velocity ﬁeld
41
at 푁distinct spacetime points. Due to the computationally demanding nature of direct
42
numerical simulations of the Navier-Stokes equations (DNS), many questions about high-
43
order velocity moments of multipoint statistics involving many points have been practically
44
inaccessible. Current methods for estimating averaged quantities, RANS and LES, focus
45
mainly on single-point and low-order moments (such as the mean velocity ﬁeld) and are
46
typically inaccurate for multipoint and high-order velocity moments (Pope 2000). One
47
major challenge of computing multipoint statistics is the closure problem. The governing
48
equation for the 푁-point PDF has terms that depend on the (푁+ 1)-point PDF. Although
49
the governing equation for the (푁→∞)-point probability measure is known to be closed
50
(Hopf 1952; Monin & Yaglom 1971,1975; Kolmogorov 1991), the solutions are beyond
51
the current technical reach.
52
Recent progress has been made by Sreenivasan & Yakhot (2021) where they created
53

54
This paper obtains the nontrivial pressure closure in the 2-point velocity increment Hopf
55
equation that is equivalent to the closure of the 푛th-order velocity moment equation from
56
Sreenivasan & Yakhot (2021). This allows the closure to be further generalized to the 푁-
57
point velocity increment Hopf equation containing complete information of the 푁-point
58
probability measure shown in section 2. Then, in section 3, an example of the closure’s
59
capability is demonstrated by determining the functional form of the transition function
60
between the 2-point structure function and the 3-point fusion rules for the 3-point structure
61
function.
