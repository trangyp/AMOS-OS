Abstract
To every ω-categorical structure M one can associate two spaces of symmetries which
determine the structure up to first-order bi-interpretability: the topological group AutpMq
of its automorphisms and the topological monoid EEmbpMq of its elementary embed-
dings, both equipped with the topology of pointwise convergence τpw. We investigate
the relation of τpw to other topologies on these spaces: in particular, when τpw is mini-
mal, i.e. does not admit any strictly coarser Hausdorff semigroup topology.
A common method to prove minimality of τpw on EEmbpMq is to show that it coin-
cides with the algebraically defined semigroup Zariski topology τZ. We show that τpw
differs from τZ on EEmbpMq whenever AutpMq has non-trivial centre. We then provide
general conditions on the behaviour of algebraic closure on M that imply minimality of
τpw. These condition cover, for example, countable vector spaces and projective spaces
over finite fields. Turning to AutpMq, we describe the minimal T1 semigroup topolo-
gies on the automorphism groups of model-theoretically simple one-based ω-categorical
structures with weak elimination of imaginaries. We conclude by proving that the met-
ric pointwise topology τmp is minimal, equals τZ, and is strictly coarser than τpw, on
EEmbpMq for the real and the rational Urysohn space and sphere.
Keywords: Pointwise convergence topology, Zariski topology, elementary embedding,
oligomorphic permutation group, saturated model, model-theoretic stability, model-theoretic
simplicity, base monotonicity, modularity, lovely pairs, Urysohn spaces.
MSC classes: 20M20, 54H15, 22A15, 03C45, 03C15.

Introduction
1.1
Spaces of symmetries and their topologies
Given a countable structure M, there are two natural spaces of symmetries preserving its
structure as given by first-order logic: its automorphism group AutpMq (if we require
the symmetries to be bijections), and its monoid of elementary embeddings EEmbpMq.1
From these objects, considered respectively as a permutation group and a transformation
monoid, one can draw back conclusions about the original structure M. For example, we
can tell whether or not M is ω-categorical, i.e. up to isomorphism the only countable model
of its first-order theory; by the theorem of Ryll-Nardzewski, Engeler, and Svenonius (see the
textbook [60]), this is the case if and only if AutpMq is oligomorphic, that is, AutpMq ñ Mn
has only finitely many orbits for all n ě 1 in its diagonal action. In the ω-categorical
case, we can even measure exactly “how well” AutpMq and EEmbpMq still know M: they
determine M up to first-order interdefinability, i.e. the automorphism group/monoid of el-
ementary embeddings of any other structure acts the same way if and only if that structure
has a first-order definition without parameters in M and vice-versa.
One can then abstract further: AutpMq and EEmbpMq are, respectively, a topological group
and a topological monoid when equipped with the topology τpw of pointwise conver-
gence, for which a subbasis of open sets is given by sets of all maps which send a partic-
ular point x P M to another specific point y P M. Moreover, AutpMq is τpw-closed in the
group of all permutations of M and EEmbpMq is τpw-closed in the full monoid MM. This
implies that τpw is Polish on these spaces, i.e., separable and induced by a complete metric.

From these topological algebraic structures, we can still extract considerable information
about M: in the ω-categorical case both the topological group AutpMq and the topologi-
cal monoid EEmbpMq determine M up to first-order bi-interpretations, a more generous
equivalence than the above-mentioned interdefinability (Coquand; see [3], and [43]). To
provide another example, famously from AutpMq we can tell whether or not M has a cer-
tain combinatorial property called the Ramsey property: this is the case if and only if the
topological group AutpMq is extremely amenable, i.e. any continuous action of it on a com-
pact Hausdorff space has a fixed point [37].
This invites a further step of abstraction where we view these spaces of symmetries only as
algebraic structures by forgetting the topology, giving rise to two natural questions:
Question 1.1. How much information about M is still encoded in the pure group AutpMq
or the pure monoid EEmbpMq?
Question 1.2. What is the relation of τpw to the other (Hausdorff/Polish) topologies com-
patible with the algebraic structure of AutpMq or EEmbpMq?
1.2
The case of groups
Over the past four decades, a rich literature has emerged on these questions in the con-
text of automorphism groups. Regarding Question 1.1, in the ω-categorical case, we often
have that the pointwise convergence topology is the same in all possible actions of AutpMq
as a closed oligomorphic permutation group; this then implies that the algebraic struc-
ture of AutpMq encodes M up to bi-interpretability. As exemplary references, we point
to [25, 61] for the notion of the small index property; to [55] for forall-exists-interpretations;
and to [36, 38] for ample generics. In fact, when AutpMq has ample generics, then τpw is
even the unique Polish group topology on it (which is a stronger statement than the point-
wise topology being the same in all actions); this then provides a complete simple answer
to Question 1.2 (in the Polish case). The survey [45] contains many further references to no-
table works. Counterexamples to non-uniqueness of τpw amongst Polish group topologies,
or even to M not being determined up to bi-interpretability by the group AutpMq, such as
that given in [30], are hard to construct; indeed, it is consistent with ZF that τpw is always
the unique Polish group topology on AutpMq [43] (cf. [59, 56]). Not only the topology τpw,
but even the action AutpMq ñ M can be reconstructed from the pure group structure in
many cases under the additional assumption that M has no algebraicity (i.e., for each finite
A Ď M every element in MzA has an infinite orbit under the action of the stabiliser of
A) [55].
Looking at Question 1.2, in many interesting special circumstances τpw is such that there
are no strictly coarser Hausdorff group topologies on AutpMq. Topological groups with
this property are known as minimal, and they are intensively studied [24]. Examples of
minimal topological groups arising from automorphism groups of countable structures are
S8 :“ AutpN; “q by [32], and more generally the automorphism groups of ω-categorical
stable structures [7]. The latter is a rich class of model-theoretically “tame” structures, in-
cluding, for example, infinite-dimensional vector spaces and projective spaces over finite
fields. Indeed, the automorphism groups of stable ω-categorical structures are not only
minimal but totally minimal: every continuous surjective homomorphism to a Hausdorff
topological group is open. In their survey of minimal topological groups, Dikranjan and
Megrelishvili explicitly ask which oligomorphic permutation group are minimal [24, Ques-
tion 2.3].
On the positive side, from [33] we have some additional results under the model-theoretic
weakening of stability known as simplicity: the automorphism groups of simple transitive
ω-categorical one-based structures with weak elimination of imaginaries are minimal [33].
This includes, for example, the automorphism group of a vector space of infinite dimension
over a finite field with a symplectic or unitary bilinear form [19]. On the negative side, we

can identify three potential sources of non-minimality: non-transitivity, the presence of
intervals of some form, and the presence of a metric structure. The first source can lead
to failures of minimality by “localising” the topology of pointwise convergence to an orbit
of AutpMq (Example 3.22). From the the second source, an interval topology τint can be
defined, for example on AutpQ; ăq, with the basic open sets consisting of maps sending a
particular point x P M into a specific interval (rather than another point); in this example,
τint is minimal and strictly coarser than τpw [16, 33]. The third source happens outside of
the ω-categorical setting, and allows to define a “metric pointwise topology” τmp coarser
than τpw; an example is the real Urysohn sphere [63].
1.3
The case of monoids
Let us now move to the context of monoids of elementary embeddings. With the notable
exception of some early works by Lascar [43, 44], the study of the behaviour of τpw on
EEmbpMq (or other more general spaces of symmetries) is much younger: it was only ini-
tiated about 15 years ago [13]. Since then, a considerable amount of results has emerged
on the topic, as recently surveyed in [46]. The motivation for this line of research is three-
fold. Firstly, studying monoids of elementary embeddings sheds light also on automor-
phism groups: this perspective can already be found in Lascar’s work [43, 44]. Secondly,
such study can help us develop tools for the general study of topological semigroups, as
shown in the seminal paper [27]; compared to groups, substantially different methods to
show, say, minimality are required, and results differ: for example, every monoid of ele-
mentary embeddings of an ω-categorical structure has a Polish topology strictly finer than
τpw [28]. Finally, the study of EEmbpMq often serves as an initial step to understanding
even richer spaces of symmetries [11] such as endomorphism monoids (maps preserving
positive quantifier-free formulas) and polymorphism clones (a generalization of endomor-
phisms monoids to higher arity functions), with the latter having important applications
even in theoretical computer science [12, 31].
Moving from AutpMq to EEmbpMq there are surprising differences in topological behaviour
that we explore in this paper. At a first glance, EEmbpMq may look extremely close to
AutpMq in the contexts we study. In fact, for a saturated structure M (which includes
ω-categorical ones), it coincides with the τpw-closure of AutpMq in MM. Yet, there is a
fascinating tension between the weaker algebraic structure of a monoid (which destroys
the possibility of several techniques coming from groups) and the fact that we have a much
richer space of maps (making it potentially easier to see the first-order behaviour of M from
algebraic structure).
Arguably, as mentioned in [46], EEmbpMq often behaves better than AutpMq: already Las-
car gives an extremely satisfying answer to Question 1.1 for EEmbpMq by proving that any
isomorphism between the monoids of elementary embeddings of two countable saturated
G-finite structures is continuous when restricted to their automorphism groups. In fact, it
is even a topological isomorphism between the monoids as shown recently in [46]. No re-
sult to this level of generality is known for isomorphisms between automorphism groups.
This result seems to be close to optimal since the non-G-finite counterexamples to topolog-
ical reconstruction [30] lift to monoids [10]. As another example, by [52, Theorem 3.2], if
two ω-categorical structures with no algebraicity have isomorphic monoids of elementary
self-embeddings, then this isomorphism is automatically a topological one; no such result
is known for groups, with various special cases proved in work following [55].
Another context where EEmbpMq seems to behave better than AutpMq is that of minimality,
which is the main topic of the present paper. From [52], we know that τpw is minimal on
the monoid of elementary embeddings of any ω-categorical structure with no algebraicity.
This already shows that standard sources of failure of minimality on AutpMq do not seem
to work for EEmbpMq, since both pQ; ăq (where one can define an interval topology on
AutpMq) and the random bipartite graph (where one can define a localized topology) have

1.4
The Zariski topology
Not only τpw seems to be more easily minimal in the context of EEmbpMq, but this can
often be shown by proving that τpw has a purely algebraic description in terms of the
semigroup Zariski topology, a topology that is known to be contained in every Hausdorff
semigroup topology on a given semigroup [27]. In this topology τZ, introduced in [27]
(for semigroups), closed sets are solution sets of equations over the semigroup in one free
variable with parameters from the semigroup. Since it is defined in purely algebraic terms,
any algebraic isomorphism between monoids is a homeomorphism of their Zariski topolo-
gies. However, τZ is, a priori, not particularly well-behaved or expressive: it need not be
Hausdorff or a semigroup topology. Indeed, on AutpMq, τZ is hyperconnected (and hence
not Hausdorff) whenever M has no algebraicity [6], and also the more intensively studied
group Zariski topology [14] (where inversion may be used in the definition of closed sets) is
often not a semigroup topology [33] (with notable exceptions including pN; “q and pQ; ăq).
Contrary to the group case, as demonstrated by [52], in many cases the Zariski and the
pointwise topologies coincide in EEmbpMq. This was already observed in [28], where it
was then asked whether there exists an ω-categorical structure M at all where τpw ‰ τZ
on its endomorphism monoid EndpMq. The question can be answered affirmatively even
for EEmbpMq (which is an endomorphism monoid by adequately expanding the language
of M) by the results of [10], and in [52] another more natural and tangible example was
provided; yet these two examples remain the only known ones up to this day. In fact,
showing that τpw “ τZ is the standard path to prove minimality of τpw on EndpMq. This
approach was taken, in particular, in [27, 28, 53], where then other methods are used to
show that τpw is also maximal, and hence unique, among Polish semigroup topologies on
various natural endomorphism monoids.

Results
We now present our main results; all relevant definitions can be found in Section 3. We
present our results in terms of permutation groups G acting on a countable set Ω. To con-
nect this with the previous discussion, we equip ΩΩwith the topology of pointwise con-
vergence τpw. Note that G ñ Ωis closed in the set of all permutations with respect to τpw
if and only if it equals AutpMq for some first-order structure M on Ω. As mentioned above,
the closure G of G in ΩΩis precisely the monoid EEmbpMq of elementary self-embeddings
of M whenever M is saturated; in particular, this is the case whenever M is ω-categorical.
Throughout the paper we work with the assumption that G ñ Ωhas locally finite alge-
braic closure, by which we mean that for any finite A Ď Ωthere are only finitely many
b P Ωwith finite orbit under the action of the pointwise stabiliser of A, GA. If all such b
are elements of A for any A Ď Ω, then we say there is no algebraicity. The automorphism
groups of ω-categorical structures have locally finite algebraic closure.
Our first theorem provides, for the first time, general conditions that imply τpw ‰ τZ on
monoids of elementary embeddings; cf. Section 1.4.
Theorem A (Theorem 4.8). Let G ñ Ωbe a closed permutation group with locally finite
algebraic closure. Suppose that G has non-trivial centre. Let S be either G or G. Then, the
semigroup Zariski topology τZ on S is not Hausdorff. In particular, it is properly contained
in τpw.
Several interesting structures have automorphism groups satisfying the above assumptions
on G: countable vector spaces over finite fields (with bilinear or quadratic forms), and in-
finitely many disjoint copies of the complete graph Kn (which are essentially the source of

Theorem A tells us that the standard method to prove minimality of τpw on EEmbpMq (by
showing τpw “ τZ) cannot work in many natural ω-categorical structures with algebraicity
(the latter is implied by a non-trivial center). We thus develop alternative techniques to
establish minimality as long as the algebraic behaviour of a structure is sufficiently tame:
Theorem B (Theorem 7.19). Let G ñ Ωbe the automorphism group of a countable satu-
rated structure with locally finite algebraic closure such that algebraic independence satis-
fies base monotonicity. Then, τpw is minimal amongst Hausdorff semigroup topologies on
G.
In this theorem, algebraic independence is a natural notion of independence on subsets of
a saturated structure, which, for example, in infinite vector spaces over finite fields corre-
sponds to linear independence. Base monotonicity is a natural condition on a notion of in-
dependence which, roughly, tells us that the algebraic behaviour of a structure is not more
complex than that of a vector space. Theorem B subsumes the results of [52] since every
ω-categorical structure with no algebraicity is such that algebraic independence satisfies
base monotonicity. Moreover, it yields minimality of τpw for the automorphism group of
an infinite dimensional vector space over a finite field, in spite of τpw ‰ τZ (by Theorem A).
Theorem B applies to a wide range of cases. In particular, G “ AutpMq also acts on any
expansion M‹ of M by finitely many imaginary sorts (that is, quotients of M by AutpMq-
invariant equivalence relations) with τpw giving rise to the same topological group (and
the same topological monoid on G); and when M is ω-categorical, algebraic closure is lo-
cally finite also on the action of G on M‹, meaning that Theorem B still applies. Often,
one needs to move to these actions for algebraic independence to satisfy base monotonic-
ity. From this, Theorem B applies to all (model-theoretically) simple one-based structures
with very weak elimination of imaginaries (see Remark 7.20). Simple one-based structures
include all smoothly approximable structures [19], which contain all ω-categorical ω-stable
structures [18], and all simple finitely homogeneous structures [4]. The condition of very
weak elimination of imaginaries roughly means that the behaviour of algebraic closure on
imaginary elements can be described by only looking at finitely many imaginary sorts. We
do not know any examples from the aforementioned classes that do not have this property
(cf. [19, 17]). Finally, algebraic independence easily satisfies base monotonicity also outside
the context of model-theoretic simplicity. For example, our theorem applies to the monoids
of elementary embeddings of countably infinite densely ordered vector spaces over finite
fields.
Going back to the context of groups, we improve the main result of [33] (Theorem B therein)
by describing the T1 semigroup topologies on G coarser than τpw under general conditions:
firstly, we consider all T1 semigroup topologies rather than Hausdorff group topologies,
and moreover we remove the assumption from [33, Theorem B] that points are algebraically
closed in the non-transitive setting.
Theorem C (Theorem 6.8). Let G ñ Ωbe the automorphism group of a simple one-based
countable saturated structure with weak elimination of imaginaries and locally finite alge-
braic closure. Then, every T1 semigroup topology on G coarser than τpw is a generalized
pointwise topology. Moreover, if G is transitive, then τpw is minimal amongst T1 semigroup
topologies on G.
We define generalised pointwise topologies in Definition 3.26. In the non-transitive con-
text, minimality of τpw can fail: this is witnessed by the random bipartite graph. On top of
our theorem applying to the aforementioned examples of simple one-based ω-categorical
structures with weak elimination of imaginaries, Theorem 6.8 works under more general
assumptions which include, for example, simple (not one-based) ω-categorical Hrushovski
constructions [65, 33]. Since the original argument in [33] heavily relied on taking conju-
gates of pointwise stabilizers, our proof follows a substantially different route, with the

We conclude by studying topologies on the semigroup of elementary embeddings of Ury-
sohn spaces. Recall that that in the group case, the presence of a metric is a source of failure
of minimality for τpw. By adapting techniques from [52] to the metric setting, we show that
under general conditions, the Zariski topology is a Hausdorff semigroup topology and
hence minimal amongst all such topologies on the monoid of elementary embeddings; that
it agrees with the metric pointwise topology τmp; and that it is strictly coarser than τpw. In
particular, we obtain the following (for the mentioned spaces the isometric embeddings are
precisely the elementary embeddings):
Theorem D (Theorem 8.24). Let U be any of the following Urysohn spaces:
• the rational Urysohn space;
• the rational Urysohn sphere;

• the real Urysohn sphere;
• the rational ultrametric Urysohn space.
Then, the semigroup Zariski topology τZ on the space IEpUq of isometric embeddings of

In this section we define a natural way to obtain sinks through the existence of a sufficiently
“generic” copy of M within itself, which we shall call a universally embedded model. In
Section 7.2.1 we define and give examples of universally embedded models. We show that
in most cases of interest to us these are just what is commonly known in model theory as
a lovely pair [8]. Most of our discussion is to give the reader context and intuitions for
the definition. We prove such universally embedded models exist whenever |!

yield sinks. This concludes the proof of Theorem 7.19.

Minimal semigroup topologies in Urysohn spaces
So far, we obtained results showing that τpw is minimal amongst Hausdorff semigroup
topologies in several monoids of elementary embeddings. Indeed, various sources of fail-
ures of minimality of τpw for groups do not work in the context of monoids. In this sec-
tion, we study semigroup topologies on the monoids of isometric embeddings of Urysohn
spaces. These are homogeneous metric spaces which will yield examples of semigroup
topologies for which τpw is not minimal (amongst Hausdorff semigroup topologies). In-
deed, the minimal Hausdorff semigroup topology for these structures naturally arises from
the presence of a metric and is known as the metric topology τmp. We will show minimal-
ity of τmp for the spaces of isometric embeddings of various Urysohn spaces by showing
these coincide with the Zariski topology τZ. Our techiques can be seen as adapting the
ideas of [52] to a metric context.

The Urysohn universal metric space UR, also known as the real Urysohn space, constructed
by Urysohn in 1925, is a complete separable metric space uniquely characterized by uni-
versality (every separable metric space embeds isometrically into UR) and homogeneity
(every partial isometry between finite subspaces extends to a global isometry). It was orig-
inally constructed by taking the completion of what we now call the rational Urysohn space
UQ . This countable rational metric space, although it can be identified today as the Fraïssé
limit of the class of finite metric spaces with rational distances, was obtained by Urysohn
through a direct constructive method. The real Urysohn space UR then arises as its met-
ric completion. There is also a bounded version of the Urysohn space, known as the real
Urysohn sphere Ur0,1s, which is universal and homogeneous with respect to the class of
finite metric spaces of diameter at most 1. Again, this can be obtained as the completion
of the rational Urysohn sphere UQ1, which is the Fraïssé limit of finite metric spaces with
rational distances bounded by 1. It is easy to see that the spaces of isometries AutpUq and
isometric embeddings IpUq “ AutpUq of a Urysohn space U are naturally endowed with
a metric topology induced by the product topology on U U, where U is endowed with the
topology arising from its metric.
The isometry groups IpURq and IpUr0,1sq, equipped with τmp, are Polish groups whose al-
gebraic and dynamical properties have been extensively studied. Notably, every Polish
group embeds as a closed subgroup of IpURq [63], and IpURq is extremely amenable [50].
The spaces of isometric embeddings have also been studied in a topological context, with,
for example, [42] showing that every transformation monoid on a countable set embeds in
the space of isometric embeddings of UQ.
The model-theoretic construction of UR and Ur0,1s extends to a broader framework based
on so called distance monoids: for any such monoid, the associated class of finite metric
structures admits a Fraïssé limit, yielding a universal and homogeneous structure. Details
of this setting are provided below and may also be found in [22].
The minimality of the τmp of IpUr0,1sq was established in [62] by proving the fact that the
isometry group of the Urysohn sphere is Roelcke precompact. In [34]the minimality of τmp
of IsompURq and for other generalized Urysohn spaces was obtained through a different
approach. In what follows, we study the monoids of isometric embeddings of generalized
Urysohn spaces. These are also the monoids of elementary embeddings of these struc-
tures. We prove that τmp “ τZ Ĺ τpw for the monoids of isometric embeddings of UQ, UR,
Ur0,1s, and UQ1. We will give most proofs in the broader context of generalised Urysohn
spaces [22].
