Machine Learning Based Mesh Movement for
Non-Hydrostatic Tsunami Simulation
Yezhang Li, Stephan C. Kramer, Matthew D. Piggott
Department of Earth Science and Engineering, Imperial College London, South
Kensington Campus, London, SW7 2AZ, United Kingdom
Abstract
This study investigates the use of machine learning based mesh adaptivity,
specifically mesh movement methods (UM2N), with depth integrated non-
hydrostatic shallow water models. Motivation for this comes from the need
for models which balance efficiency and accuracy for use in probabilistic
coastal hazard assessment.
Implementations are built on the discontinu-
ous Galerkin finite-element (DG-FE) based software, Thetis, which leverages
the partial differential equation (PDE) framework Firedrake for automated
code generation. Verification on benchmark test cases and validation against
laboratory measurements of coastal hazards, focusing on tsunami propaga-
tion, run-up, and inundation is performed. In these tests, the UM2N-driven
meshes help resolve key non-hydrostatic dynamics and yield numerical so-
lutions in close agreement with reference computations and measured data.
Numerical results indicate that the UM2N surrogate based approach sig-
nificantly accelerates conventional mesh movement techniques and has high
robustness over long integration periods and under strongly nonlinear wave
conditions.
Keywords:
Shallow Water Equations; Non-Hydrostatic; Discontinuous Galerkin; Finite
Element Method, Mesh Movement; Monge–Ampère; Deep Learning; Neural
Network

3. Discretization methods and implementation in Thetis and Fire-
201
drake
202
The non-hydrostatic model is solved using the unstructured-mesh finite
203
element coastal ocean modelling system, Thetis (Kärnä et al., 2018), which
204
is built upon the automated code generation framework, Firedrake (Ham
205
et al., 2023). Following the pioneering work and relevant extensions of layer-
206
averaged non-hydrostatic approach (Stelling and Zijlema, 2003; Wei and Jia,
207
2013) and non-hydrostatic pressure correction (Marshall et al., 1997; Stansby
208
and Zhou, 1998; Lai et al., 2010), Pan et al. (2019) integrated the layer-
209
averaged non-hydrostatic approach into DG finite element implementation in
210
Thetis. In this formulation the 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes (NS) equa-
211
tions are vertically integrated to obtain a depth-averaged continuity equation
212
for total water depth H, and a horizontal momentum equation for the depth-
213
averaged velocity ¯u. Mentioned in Sect. 2, non-hydrostatic effects are rep-
214
resented by decomposing the pressure into hydrostatic and non-hydrostatic
215
parts and retaining a depth-averaged non-hydrostatic pressure q in the hori-
216
zontal momentum balance. The vertical momentum and continuity equations
217
are combined to yield a Poisson problem in the depth-integrated (Pan et al.,
218
2019).
219
In the spatial discretisation, the depth-averaged variables (free-surface
220
elevation and horizontal velocity) are discretised on unstructured triangu-
221
lar meshes with a discontinuous Galerkin (DG) finite element formulation,
222
typically piecewise linear IPDG
1
−IPDG
1
spaces. Numerical fluxes across el-
223
ement interfaces are treated using linearised approximate Riemann solvers,
224
and viscosity is stabilised by an interior-penalty formulation (Kärnä et al.,
225

226

227

6. Conclusion
620
In this paper, the use of machine learning based mesh movement for non-
621
hydrostatic shallow water modelling has been investigated for the simulation
622
of complex wave interactions and tsunami run-up on irregular bathymetries
623
and changeable boundary conditions.
624
Results indicate that mesh movement via the UM2N network not only
625
offers improved accuracy but also displays more effective convergence be-
626
haviour, particularly during later stages of wave propagation, underscoring
627
its suitability for accurately capturing complex dynamic wave phenomena
628
using adaptive mesh refinement techniques. The solitary wave based sce-
629
narios also indicates the strong robustness of UM2N adaptation in handling
630
the rapid changes of free surface elevation caused by water refraction. This
631
means that UM2N is an effective surrogate model for costly MA equation-
632
based mesh movement and that tight coupling of machine learning based
633
mesh movement with a modern PDE framework is both feasible and prac-
634
tical.
The approach offers an alternative way to faster, adaptive coastal
635
modelling without losing accuracy in tsunami simulation. Moreover, new
636
monitor functions are designed for these scenarios including a wetting-drying
637
interface in order to track wave propagation correctly and enhance refinement
638
in these areas.
639
Future research in this area will involve the extension of our framework
640
to 3D mesh geometries, based on the work including Li et al. (2005) and
641
Rowbottom et al. (2025).
Mesh adaptive techniques based on unsuper-
642
vised learning, such as G-adaptivity (Rowbottom et al., 2025), and Unsuper-
643
vised and Generalizable Mesh Movement Network (UGM2N) (Wang et al.,
644
2025) provide alternative movement strategies that could also be applied to
645
non-hydrostatic models. Considering mesh regularity and uniformity during
646
movement, the mesh adaptation algorithms can be further improved (e.g.,
647
allowing boundary node movement), thus helping to generate meshes with
648
