MORSE AND STABLE SUBGROUPS VIA THE COSET INTERSECTION COMPLEX
TOMOHIRO FUKAYA, HAOYANG HE, EDUARDO MARTÍNEZ-PEDROZA, AND TAKUMI MATSUKA
Abstract. In this note, we study the equivalence of Morse and stable subgroups in the framework
of the coset intersection complex. Under certain conditions on a coset intersection complex of a
group, we prove that infinite-index Morse subgroups are stable. Our main theorem recovers results
in the literature on right-angled Artin groups and graph products. As an application, we show that
for the genus-two handlebody group, any infinite-index Morse subgroup is stable.
1. Introduction
The notion of stable subgroups was introduced by Durham–Taylor [DT15].
The notion of
Morse (or strongly quasiconvex) subgroups was introduced independently by Tran [Tra19] and
Genevois [Gen19].
Definition 1.1. Let G be a finitely generated group and H a subgroup.
(1) The subgroup H is stable if H is finitely generated undistorted subgroup such that for some
(equivalently, every) finite generating set S of G and for every k ≥1 and c ≥0, there is
L = L(S, k, c) such that any two (k, c)-quasigeodesics in G with the same endpoints on
H are at Hausdorff distance at most L.
(2) The subgroup H is Morse or strongly quasiconvex if for some (equivalently, every) finite
generating set S of G and for every k ≥1 and c ≥0, there is m = m(S, k, c) such that
any (k, c)-quasigeodesic in G with endpoints on H is contained in the M-neighbourhood
of H.
The two notions are equivalent if G is hyperbolic, but they are not equivalent in general.
For example, right-angled Coxeter groups contain subgroups that are Morse but not stable [Tra19,
Section 7], and non-hyperbolic peripheral subgroups of relatively hyperbolic groups are also Morse
but not stable. It is, therefore, natural to ask when these two notions are equivalent. The following
characterisation is due to Tran.
Theorem 1.2 ([Tra19, Theorem 4.8]). Let G be a finitely generated group. Let H be an infinite
subgroup of G. Then H is stable if and only if H is Morse and hyperbolic.
The two notions have shown to be equivalent for particular classes of groups, as the following
results in the literature illustrate. Note that (2) is a special case of (3) in Theorem 1.3.
Theorem 1.3. If H is a non-trivial infinite-index Morse subgroup of one of the following groups,
then H is stable.
(1) [Kim19, Corollary B] A mapping class group of an oriented, connected, finite-type surface
with complexity at least two.
(2) [Tra19, Theorem 8.19] A right-angled Artin group whose defining graph is finite.
(3) [BCK+26, Theorem 1.5] A graph product whose defining graph is finite, connected with
at least two vertices, and whose vertex groups are infinite.
Some of these results are particular instances of a more general statement, which this note
presents. We study the equivalence of Morse and stable in the framework of the coset intersection
complex, introduced by Abbott–Martínez-Pedroza [AMP26].

The main result of this note is the following.
Theorem 2.6. Let (G, P) be a group pair such that the following three conditions hold:
(1) Each P ∈P is undistorted in G.
(2) For each P ∈P, if Q ⩽P is Morse in P, then Q is finite or finite-index in P.
(3) The coset intersection complex K(G, P) is quasi-isometric to a simplicial tree.
If H is a non-trivial infinite-index Morse subgroup of G, then H is a stable subgroup. Moreover
H is virtually free.
Our proof is inspired by the work of Tran [Tra19]. We remark that a similar strategy in the con-
text of graph products is adapted by Balasubramanya–Chesser–Kerr–Mangahas–Trin [BCK+26,
Section 6]. Our theorem leads to the following corollary.
Corollary 1.5. Let G be one of the following groups. Then infinite-index Morse subgroups of G
are stable and virtually free.
(1) [BCK+26, Theorem 1.5 & Corollary 1.7] A graph product whose defining graph is finite,
connected with at least two vertices, and whose vertex groups are infinite.
(2) A group acting cocompactly and acylindrically on a simplicial tree, with infinite, undistor-
ted edge stabilisers that contain only finite or finite-index Morse subgroups.
(3) The genus-two handlebody group.
Note that Corollary 1.5(3) does not immediately follow from Theorem 1.3(1), since the handle-
body group is exponentially distorted in the mapping class group of the boundary of the handle-
body [HH12].
Whilst we are able to recover (2) and (3) of Theorem 1.3, we are unable to recover (1). Therefore,
we raise the following question.
Question 1.6. Let (G, P) be a group pair such that K(G, P) is hyperbolic, each P ∈P is
undistorted and has only finite or finite-index Morse subgroups. If H ⩽G is an infinite-index
Morse subgroup, is H stable?
The main result of this note provides a positive answer in the case that K(G, P) is a quasi-tree.
In the more general case, the mapping class group provides positive evidence as a consequence
of [Kim19, Corollary B], cited as Theorem 1.3(1). Specifically, for the mapping class group
Mod(S) of a closed surface of genus g ≥2, let P be a collection of representatives of conjugacy
classes of maximal free abelian subgroups generated by Dehn twists. The pair (G, P) satisfies
the hypotheses of the question, as we now explain. In [HMP26, Proposition 5.4], it is shown
that K(Mod(S), P) is quasi-isometric to the curve complex, which is hyperbolic [MM99]. Every
group in P is undistorted in Mod(S) (see [Ham09, Corollary 4] or [FLM01]). Since each group
P ∈P is isomorphic to Z3g−3, any Morse subgroup of P is either finite or finite-index (see
Proposition 3.1).
Organisation. We prove the main result in Section 2 and the corollary in Section 3.

Lemma 2.5. Let G be a finitely generated group that acts properly on a geodesic metric space X
that is quasi-isometric to a simplicial tree. Then G is virtually free.
Proof. To see that G is quasi-isometric to a simplicial tree, choose v ∈X and a finite generating
set {s1, · · · , sn} of G, let K be the union of geodesics [v, siv] for all 1 ≤i ≤n. Then if Y is the
union of gK for all g ∈G, then Y is a connected subspace of X quasi-isometric to G. One can
show that any connected subspace of X is quasi-isometric to a simplicial tree.
It is a well-known consequence of Stallings’ theorem on ends [Sta68, Sta71] and Dunwoody’s
accessibility [Dun85] that every group that quasi-isometrically embeds to a tree is virtually free
(see [DK18, Chapter 20] or [But23, Corollary 10.7] for a proof).
□
We are now ready to prove the main theorem.
Theorem 2.6. Let (G, P) be a group pair such that the following three conditions hold:
(1) Each P ∈P is undistorted in G.
(2) For each P ∈P, if Q ⩽P is Morse in P, then Q is finite or finite-index in P.
(3) The coset intersection complex K(G, P) is quasi-tree.
If H is an infinite-index Morse subgroup of G, then H is a stable subgroup. Moreover H is
virtually free.
Proof. By Proposition 2.1, the action of H on K(G, P) is proper. It follows from Lemma 2.5 that
H is virtually free. In particular, H is hyperbolic, so it is stable by Theorem 1.2.
□

Remark 3.5. If the coset intersection complex K(G, P) is a tree, then it corresponds to a Bass–Serre
tree of a graph of groups decomposition of G where the underlying graph is connected and finite,
the vertex groups are given by the collection P, and all edge groups are infinite. Conversely, given a
finite graph of groups decomposition of G with finite connected underlying graph and infinite edge
groups, the Bass–Serre tree is the coset intersection complex K(G, P) where P is the collection
of vertex groups.
3.3. The genus-two handlebody group. We recall some relevant notions. A handlebody Vg
of genus g ≥2 is the three-manifold obtained by attaching g one-handles to a three-ball. The
handlebody group Hg of Vg is the group of isotopy classes of orientation-preserving homeomorph-
isms Vg →Vg. The group Hg is finitely presented [Waj98, Theorem 18]. A meridian is an
isotopy class of essential simple closed curves in ∂Vg that bounds an embedded disc in Vg. The
disc complex D(Vg) is the flag complex whose r-simplex corresponds to r + 1 distinct disjoint
meridians. The mapping class group Mod(S) of a surface S is the group of isotopy classes of
orientation-preserving homeomorphisms S →S. The curve complex C(S) of a oriented, closed,
connected surface S of genus at least two is the flag complex whose r-simplices are collections of
r + 1 isotopy classes of disjoint essential simple closed curves.
We refer to [Hen20, FM12] for basic facts about these notions, some of which we will use
below. Elements of Hg map meridians to meridians. The simplicial action of Hg on the collection
of maximal simplices of D(Vg) has finitely many orbits. Choose representatives Q1, · · · , Qn of
these orbits. The Dehn twist Tα about a meridian α is an element of Hg, and more generally, a
composition of Dehn twists about disjoint meridians is an element of Hg. For each 1 ≤i ≤n, let
Pi = ⟨Tα | α ∈Qi⟩and P = {Pi | 1 ≤i ≤n}. The group Hg can be regarded as a subgroup
of Mod(∂Vg). There is also a natural simplicial inclusion from D(Vg) to the complex of curves
C(∂Vg).
