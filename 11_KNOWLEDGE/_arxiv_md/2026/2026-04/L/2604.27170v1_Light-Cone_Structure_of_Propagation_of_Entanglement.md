LIGHT-CONE STRUCTURE OF PROPAGATION OF
ENTANGLEMENT
I. M. SIGAL
Abstract. For a wide class of bipartite systems with localized couplings, we
establish existence of an effective light-cone for propagation of entanglement.
This result yields a hard lower bound on the time it takes, under ideal conditions
(no loss, no decoherence), to transport entanglement to a distant location (say,
a node of a graph-structured quantum network), or to maintain it there.
1. Introduction
1.1. Background and informal discussion. Entanglement is a striking quan-
tum phenomenon and a key resource in quantum information science, enabling
quantum communication (see [1,2,3,15,22,24,26,32,35,41,51,63,64,65,66,68,72,
80,81,87,91,92], for reviews, and [31,61,62,67,85,89], for books and lecture notes).
It is also responsible for the main obstacle in quantum information processing and
quantum computing - the decoherence (see [78] for a review).
Only entangled states might have nonclassical correlations, as statistical charac-
teristics of the complementary - separable - states can be reproduced by classical
means ([88]). As a result, the genuine quantum communication is possible only
through (distributed) entanglement.
A theoretical analysis of such a communication would be grounded in the finite-
ness of entanglement propagation speed (modulo exponentially small localization
leakage). However, the latter property was not established until now. The reason
for this disconnect goes to the root of this subject: the Lieb-Robinson bounds - the
common tool in proving the finiteness of speed of propagation in quantum infor-
mation theory - estimate commutators of propagating observables, while there is
no known characterization of entanglement in terms of any system of observables.
In this paper, we establish the finiteness of speed of propagation of entanglement
for bipartite systems with localized couplings between their subsystems yielding
the entanglement light-cone structure. Our approach for analysis the space-time

4. Conclusion
In this paper, we establish the entanglement light-cone structure in bipartite
quantum systems with localized interactions offering a rigorous, general framework
for analysis of the entanglement propagation. This structure is complementary to
the Lieb–Robinson causal structure for correlations of observables, which does
apply to entanglement.
To quantify the spatial distribution of entanglement, we introduced the notion
of local entanglement, which could be especially useful in analysis of the distributed
entanglement used in quantum communication. Using this notion, we investigated
how this distribution changes with time.
Our bounds are applicable to continuum (including semi-relativistic) as well to
short-range lattice quantum systems.
