LATTICES DETERMINED BY THEIR COMMENSURATOR
ADRIEN LE BOUDEC AND COLIN REID
Abstract. Let Γ be a finitely generated cocompact lattice of a totally disconnected
locally compact group G, and C a dense subgroup of G that contains and commen-
surates Γ. We study the problem of describing all finitely generated commensurated
subgroups of C. We establish general rigidity results ensuring every finitely generated
commensurated subgroup of C is virtually contained in Γ. In more concrete situations,
in fact we conclude that up to commensurability, Γ is the only infinite finitely gener-
ated commensurated subgroup of C. For instance this last conclusion holds when G
is the automorphism group of a tree. This settles in particular the problem whether
two non-commensurable cocompact tree lattices may have the same commensurator.
Further applications include commensurators of cocompact lattices in other groups of
automorphisms of trees, as well as commensurators of graph product of finite groups
in automorphism groups of right-angled building.
1. Introduction
Let G be a locally compact group, and Γ a discrete subgroup of G. The commensura-
tor CommG(Γ) of Γ in G is the set of g ∈G such that Γ and gΓg−1 are commensurable.
Recall that two subgroups are commensurable if their intersection has finite index in
each of them. A general problem consists in relating properties of Γ with those of its
commensurator CommG(Γ). One key result in this realm is Margulis’ arithmeticity cri-
terion, which asserts that when G is a connected semisimple Lie group with trivial center
and no compact factor and Γ is an irreducible lattice, then Γ is arithmetic if and only if
CommG(Γ) is a dense subgroup of G [Mar91, Chap. IX (1.9)]. Another closely related
result of Margulis in this setting is the commensurator superrigidity theorem, which
asserts that a Zariski dense linear representation of CommG(Γ) in a simple algebraic
group under which the image of Γ is unbounded extends to a continuous representation
of G [Mar91, Chap. VII Th. 5.4].
Those results and the wealth of techniques employed had a vast influence in sev-
eral other contexts, notably in situations where the ambient group G is no longer a
connected Lie group or an algebraic group over a local field, especially in the general
setting of non-positively curved metric spaces and their isometry groups. One instance
of such a situation is where G = Aut(T) is the automorphism group of a locally fi-
nite tree.
A remarkable fact in that setting is that when Aut(T) acts cocompactly
on T, any two cocompact lattices of Aut(T) are always conjugate up to commensu-
rability [Lei82, BK90]. In particular any two cocompact lattices of Aut(T) have their
commensurator that are conjugate. Bass–Kulkarni for bi-regular trees and Liu in gen-
eral showed that the commensurator of a cocompact lattice of Aut(T) is always dense
[BK90, Liu94]. Mozes [Moz99] and Abramenko-Rémy [AR09] showed the same result for

In the present paper we are concerned with the general problem of understanding
when a lattice Γ in a locally compact group G is determined by its commensurator.
Question 1. Let G be a locally compact group, and Γ a lattice. Suppose Γ′ is a lattice
in G such that CommG(Γ) = CommG(Γ′). Is Γ′ necessarily commensurable with Γ ?
Any subgroup of G commensurable with Γ is also a lattice in G with the same
commensurator as Γ, so recovering Γ up to commensurability is the best one can hope
for. When CommG(Γ) is a discrete subgroup of G, then necessarily Γ has finite index in
its commensurator, and the answer is always positive. Hence Question 1 is interesting for
lattices with a non-discrete commensurator. Actually it is natural to assume CommG(Γ)
is dense in G, as the natural locally compact group that encapsulates the context is
the closure of CommG(Γ) in G. When G is a connected semisimple Lie group with
trivial center and no compact factor, the aforementioned arithmeticity criterion and
superrigidity theorem imply a positive answer to Question 1. In the case where G =
Aut(T) and the lattices Γ, Γ′ are cocompact, Question 1 is a long-standing open problem.
It was originally asked in [BK90], and also appeared in [LMZ94, Moz99, BL01, FMT].
We will be working in the context where G is a totally disconnected locally compact
(tdlc) group, and lattices are finitely generated and cocompact. In that situation we ac-
tually consider a more general problem than Question 1. First we consider the situation
where C is any dense subgroup of G such that Γ ≤C ≤CommG(Γ). Second, and more
significantly, we consider the problem of studying all finitely generated commensurated
subgroups H of C (without assuming H is a lattice in G). The group C comes with a
finitely generated commensurated subgroup given by the context (namely Γ), and the
first question one can ask is whether there are other ones. That problem shares some
similarities with the S-arithmetic version of the Margulis–Zimmer problem [SW13].
Results. In this general setting with Γ a finitely generated cocompact lattice of a
tdlc group G, and C a dense subgroup of G such that Γ ≤C ≤CommG(Γ), our first
theorem provides sufficient conditions ensuring every finitely generated commensurated
subgroup H of C is virtually contained in Γ (i.e. some finite index subgroup of H
is contained in Γ). It is worth pointing out there is another natural commensurated
subgroup of C other than Γ, namely the intersection C ∩U between C and a compact
open subgroup U of G. Since Γ∩U is finite and C ∩U is dense in U, the subgroup C ∩U
is never virtually contained in Γ (provided we are not in the trivial situation where G
is discrete). Hence the conclusion of the theorem cannot encompass all commensurated
subgroups of C.

We recall some terminology. A profinite group is finitely generated if it has a dense
finitely generated subgroup. A tdlc group G is locally finitely generated if some
compact open subgroup of G is finitely generated (since all compact open subgroups are
commensurable, this is equivalent to ask that every compact open subgroup is finitely
generated). The normal core of a subgroup L of G is CoreG(L) = T
G gLg−1. The
following statement is a simple version of Theorem 4.4 below.
Theorem A. Let Γ be a finitely generated cocompact lattice of a tdlc group G, and C
a dense subgroup of G such that Γ ≤C ≤CommG(Γ). Suppose:
(1) G admits a compact open subgroup with trivial normal core;
(2) Every closed normal subgroup of G is discrete or open;
(3) G is not locally finitely generated.
Then every finitely generated commensurated subgroup of C is virtually contained in Γ.
Making an assumption on normal subgroups of G is quite natural in this setting.
Clearly assumption (2) is satisfied if G is topologically simple, but (2) is much less
stringent than topologically simplicity. Assumption (1) is very mild, especially in the
presence of (2). Assumption (3) is the most restrictive one. It is worth noting Theorem
A fails without this assumption, see Example 1 below.
When G ≤Aut(T) is a closed subgroup acting cocompactly on T, (1) is always
true, and there are several known conditions ensuring (2) is true, at least when G acts
minimally on T. One is Tits’ independence property [Tit70], or some of its variations.
Another one is that the local action of G is primitive and G has no finite quotient
[BM00, (1.7)]. Deciding when (3) is true is delicate, but it is at least true for the entire
group Aut(T). From Theorem A together with an independent result allowing for the
the existence of compact normal subgroups (see Proposition 4.2 below) we obtain:
Theorem B. Let T be a locally finite tree such that Aut(T) acts cocompactly on T. Let
Γ be a cocompact lattice of G = Aut(T). Then, up to commensurability, Γ is the only
infinite finitely generated commensurated subgroup of CommG(Γ).
That theorem in particular yields a positive answer to the aforementioned open prob-
lem of Question 1 for G = Aut(T) and Γ, Γ′ cocompact:
Corollary C. Any two cocompact lattices of Aut(T) with the same commensurator are
commensurable.
Another situation where Theorem A (or rather Theorem 4.4) can be applied concerns
groups of automorphisms of right angled buildings. Every graph product ΓP , associ-
ated to a right-angled Coxeter system (W, S) and a family P = (Ps)s∈S of finite groups,
naturally sits as a cocompact lattice in the automorphism group of a right-angled build-

X (i.e. automorphisms of the chamber graph of X), and by Aut0(X) the group of
type-preserving automorphisms of X. Aut0(X) is a closed and cocompact subgroup of
Aut(X), which in general is of infinite index in Aut(X). This setting yields an extensive
family of tdlc groups and cocompact lattices which fall into the general setting we con-
sider. Caprace showed that for X thick, irreducible and of non-spherical type, Aut0(X)
is abstractly simple [Cap14] (see also Haglund–Paulin [HP98] for earlier results in the
related context of CAT(0) cube complexes, as well as Lazarovich [Laz18]). Haglund
[Hag08] and Kubena–Thomas [KT12] showed the commensurator of ΓP in Aut(X) is

As discussed right after the statement, the assumption that the ambient locally com-
pact group G is not locally finitely generated is the most restrictive one in Theorem A.
We develop a complementary approach that no longer relies on this local condition of
G. Instead, this second approach is based on local properties of another tdlc group that
naturally appears in the present context. Associated to a group C and a commensurated
subgroup Γ, there is a tdlc group C//Γ and a homomorphism τC,Γ : C →C//Γ with dense
image, such that there exists a compact open subgroup UΓ such that τ −1
C,Γ(UΓ) = Γ and
the normal core of UΓ in C//Γ is trivial. Those properties actually characterize C//Γ
[SW13, Lemma 3.6]. The group C//Γ is called the Schlichting completion of C with re-
spect to Γ. If NC,Γ is the collection of finite index normal subgroups N of Γ such that N
is the intersection of finitely many C-conjugates of Γ, the C-congruence completion
bΓC of Γ is defined as the inverse limit of {Γ/N} where N ranges over NC,Γ. Then the
closure of τC,Γ(Γ) in C//Γ is a compact open subgroup of C//Γ isomorphic to bΓC.
We recall some terminology. The quasi-center QZ(G) of a tdlc group G is the set
of elements of G whose centralizer is open. The subgroup QZ(G) is normal in G, and
QZ(G) contains every discrete normal subgroup of G. The discrete residual Res(G)
of G is the intersection of all open normal subgroups of G. The local prime content
of a profinite group U is the set of prime numbers p such that p divides the order of
every open subgroup of U. Equivalently, U contains an infinite pro-p subgroup.
Theorem E. Let Γ be a finitely generated cocompact lattice of a tdlc group G, and C a
dense subgroup of G such that Γ ≤C ≤CommG(Γ). Suppose QZ(G) is discrete, Res(G)
is open, and every abstract normal subgroup of G is contained in QZ(G) or contains
Res(G). Suppose also the C-congruence completion bΓC of Γ verifies:
(1) bΓC has infinite local prime content;
(2) Any two infinite closed normal subgroups of bΓC have infinite intersection;
(3) There is no infinite closed normal subgroup M of bΓC such that M embeds as a
closed subgroup of F I for some finite group F and set I.
Then every finitely generated commensurated subgroup of C is virtually contained in Γ.

Identifying the profinite group bΓC is a delicate problem. We say that a commensu-
rated subgroup Γ of a group C has the congruence subgroup property (CSP) in C if
every finite index subgroup of Γ contains an element of NC,Γ. Equivalently, the natural
surjective homomorphism bΓ →bΓC is an isomorphism, where bΓ is the profinite comple-
tion of Γ. The idea of considering CSP in a setting where Γ is a lattice in a group G
that is not necessarily algebraic was suggested by Lubotzky [Moz98a]. When Γ is an
arithmetic lattice in a simple algebraic group and C = CommG(Γ), this notion coincides
with the classical one [Moz98a, Proposition 1.1]. The interest of this notion in the con-
text of Theorem E is that in certain situations, enough is known on Γ (as an abstract
group, independently on the way Γ sits inside G) to ensure that its profinite completion
bΓ satisfies the conditions (1), (2), (3) from Theorem E. This is for instance the case if G
is a closed and cocompact subgroup of the automorphism group of an infinitely ended
tree. In that situation Γ admits a finite index subgroup that is a non-abelian free group,
and bΓ admits a finite index subgroup that is a non-abelian free profinite group. This
guarantees (1), (2), (3) hold. More generally, if Γ is Gromov-hyperbolic and virtually
special, then bΓ satisfies the conditions needed in Theorem 5.11 (see Proposition 8.1).
So for Γ, C, G as in Theorem E and for Γ hyperbolic and virtually special, we therefore
obtain a criterion (conditionally to the fact that Γ has CSP in C) ensuring every finitely
generated commensurated subgroup of C is virtually contained in Γ (see Corollary 8.2).
We effectively manage to implement this criterion for certain closed subgroups of
automorphism groups of trees. Mozes showed that when G = Aut(Td) is the full au-
tomorphism group of a regular tree Td and Γ is a cocompact lattice in G, then Γ has
the CSP in CommG(Γ) [Moz98a, Theorem 1.2]. Mozes’ argument can be generalized

2. Preliminaries
2.1. On the discrete residual. The relevance of the discrete residual Res(G) of a
tdlc group G will appear repeatedly in the paper, notably (but not only) through the
following result, the main contribution of which is the equivalence between (2) and (3)
due to Caprace–Monod.
Theorem 2.1. For G a compactly generated tdlc group, the following are equivalent:
(1) Res(G) is a discrete subgroup of G;
(2) Res(G) is trivial;
(3) The compact open normal subgroups form a basis of neighborhoods of 1 in G.
Proof. See [Rei20a, Theorem G] or [CLB19, Proposition 2.2] for (1) ⇔(2), and [CM11,
Corollary 4.1] for (2) ⇔(3).
□
Although very simple, the following observation will be particularly useful.
Lemma 2.2. Let G be a tdlc group, and L a closed subgroup of G. Then:
(1) If L1 is a finite index open subgroup of L, then Res(L1) = Res(L).
(2) The commensurator of L in G normalizes Res(L).
Proof. The inclusion Res(L1) ≤Res(L) follows from the definition. For the converse,
let O be an open normal subgroup of L1. Then O is open in L, and has finitely many L-
conjugates. Their intersection O′ is an open normal subgroup of L, so Res(L) ≤O′ ≤O.
Hence Res(L) ≤Res(L1).
For g in the commensurator of L in G, we have Res(g−1Lg ∩L) = Res(L ∩gLg−1) =
Res(L) by the first point. Hence conjugation by g stabilizes Res(L).

3. A join operation for commensurated subgroups
The goal of this section is to prove Theorem 3.5. As a consequence of it, we derive
that in any group, in the poset of commensurability classes of subgroups, any pair of
finitely generated commensurated subgroups has a unique least upper bound; which is
also a finitely generated commensurated subgroup (Corollary 3.6).
Definition 3.1. Let G be a tdlc group and H a subgroup of G. An open envelope
for H in G is an open subgroup E of G such that H ≤E, and for every open subgroup
O of G such that H is virtually contained in O, E is virtually contained in O.
It is easy to see that if H1 and H2 are commensurable subgroups of G with open
envelopes E1 and E2 in G, then E1 and E2 are also commensurable. In particular, an
open envelope, if it exists, is unique up to commensurability. Note also that E is an
open envelope for H if and only if E is an open envelope for the closure H: this follows
from observing that for any open subgroup O, we have H virtually contained in O if
and only if H is.
The following result, which will be used as a tool in the proof of Theorem 3.5, ensures
that when H is generated by a relatively compact subset of G, there is always an open
envelope for H in G.

rather long, and relies on an auxiliary result from [Rei20b]. We provide a short and

G. The subgroup H = ΓP ∩O is therefore commensurated in ΓP , and finitely generated
because it is cocompact in the compactly generated group O. By Proposition 7.1 it
follows that H is finite or finite index in ΓP . In the first case we infer O is compact, and
therefore N is compact. In the second case we infer O has finite index in G, and therefore
N is cocompact. Since any closed cocompact subgroup contains Res(G), we have shown
every closed normal subgroup of G is either compact or contains Res(G). When applied
to the LE-radical RadLE(G), which cannot contain Res(G) because Aut0(X) is not
locally elliptic, we infer RadLE(G) is compact. K = RadLE(G) is therefore a maximal
compact normal subgroup, and we have shown the conclusion.
□

7.3. The proof of Theorem D. We will invoke the following result proven by Haglund
[Hag08, Theorem 4.30] and Kubena–Thomas [KT12, Density Theorem].
Theorem 7.7. Let X be the semi-regular right angled building associated to a graph
product of finite groups ΓP . Then the commensurator of ΓP in Aut(X) is dense in
Aut(X).
Proof of Theorem D. In case X is of spherical type, then Aut(X) is a finite group and
the statement is trivially true. So we assume that X is of non-spherical type. We then
have to see all the requirements of Theorem 4.4 are met. Density of the commensurator
is provided by Theorem 7.7. The stabilizer of a chamber is a compact open subgroup
of Aut(X) with trivial normal core. Theorem 7.3 says every closed normal subgroup
of G is either compact or contains Res(G), and Corollary 7.6 ensures closed subgroups
containing Res(G) are not locally finitely generated (because those contain Aut0(X)).
Therefore all the assumptions of Theorem 4.4 are verified, and we deduce that any
infinite finitely generated commensurated subgroup H of CommG(ΓP ) is virtually con-
tained in ΓP . In other words that reduces the problem to the situation where H is
contained in ΓP . The latter is treated by Proposition 7.1.
□
