BO HOU AND YUANCHANG LIN
Abstract. There is a Lie algebra structure on the tensor product of a Leibniz algebra and a Zinbiel
algebra for the operads of Leibniz algebras and Zinbiel algebras are Koszul dual. In this paper, we
extend such conclusion to the context of bialgebras. We show that there is a Lie bialgebra struc-
ture on the tensor product of a Leibniz bialgebra and a quadratic Zinbiel algebra, while there is an
inﬁnite-dimensional Lie bialgebra structure on the tensor product of a Zinbiel bialgebra and a qua-
dratic Z-graded Leibniz algebra. For a special quadratic Z-graded Leibniz algebra, the property that
its tensor product with a Zinbiel bialgebra forms a Lie bialgebra characterizes the Zinbiel bialgebra.
By examining the relationship between solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation in a Zinbiel
algebra (resp. a Leibniz algebra) and solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation in the induced
Lie algebra, we prove that the induced Lie bialgebra is quasi-triangular (resp. triangular, factorizable)
whenever the original Zinbiel bialgebra (resp. Leibniz bialgebra) is quasi-triangular (resp. triangu-
lar, factorizable). Finally, we present a construction of a quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra on the tensor
product of a quasi-Frobenius Zinbiel algebra and a quadratic Leibniz algebra.

1.1. Lie bialgebras and their construction. A bialgebra structure on a given algebra is obtained
as a coalgebra structure that induces the same algebra structure on the dual space, subject to a set
of compatibility conditions between the product and coproduct. One of the most famous examples
of bialgebra is the Lie bialgebra [17]. Lie bialgebras can be viewed as an inﬁnitesimalization of
Poisson-Lie groups. This concept was ﬁrst introduced by Drinfeld in the context of the Yang-
Baxter equations and quantum groups. Since then, it has found applications in many other areas
of mathematics and mathematical physics, such as the theory of Hopf algebra deformations of
universal enveloping algebras [20], string topology and symplectic ﬁeld theory [13], the Goldman-
Turaev theory of free loops in Riemann surfaces with boundaries [24], and beyond. There have
been many bialgebra theories for other algebra structures that essentially follow the approach of
Lie bialgebras, including: antisymmetric inﬁnitesimal bialgebras [3, 7], left-symmetric bialgebras
[6], Jordan bialgebras [45], Novikov bialgebras [26], perm bialgebras [28, 35], Leibniz bialgebras
[42, 8], anti-pre-Lie Poisson bialgebras [36], Zinbiel bialgebras [44], and so on. Recently, the
extension problem of Lie bialgebras [27] and factorizability of Lie bialgebras [34] have been further
studied.
A well-known phenomenon from operadic theory states that if A and B are algebras over bi-
nary quadratic operads that are Koszul dual to each other, then their tensor product A ⊗B can be
equipped with a bracket [−, −] such that (A⊗B, [−, −]) forms a Lie algebra [23]. Based on this fact,
Hong, Bai, and Guo introduced the notion of a Novikov bialgebra to construct inﬁnite-dimensional
Lie bialgebras via Novikov bialgebra aﬃnization [26]. Lin, Zhou, and Bai later showed that the
tensor product of a perm bialgebra and a quadratic pre-Lie algebra (resp. a pre-Lie bialgebra and
a quadratic perm algebra) admits a Lie bialgebra structure [35]. Notably, the operads of perm
algebras and pre-Lie algebras are Koszul dual to each other [38]. In this paper, we construct
quasi-triangular (resp. triangular, factorizable) Lie bialgebras using Leibniz bialgebras, and we
also construct inﬁnite-dimensional Lie bialgebras via the aﬃnization of Zinbiel bialgebras.
1.2. Constructions of Lie bialgebras from Leibniz bialgebras. Leibniz algebras were ﬁrst dis-
covered by Bloh under the name of D-algebras [9] and later introduced by Loday [39] with the
motivation of studying periodicity in algebraic K-theory. The (co)homology and homotopy theo-
ries of Leibniz algebras were established in [22, 39]. Recently, Leibniz algebras were studied from
diﬀerent aspects due to applications in both mathematics and physics, such as integration, defor-
mation quantization, rational homotopy theory, and higher gauge theories [10, 14, 16, 37]. In [42],
the bialgebra theory and the classical Yang-Baxter equation for Leibniz algebras were introduced,
where Tang and Sheng established the equivalence between Leibniz bialgebras and Manin triples
of Leibniz algebras, and subsequently applied twisting theories and relative Rota-Baxter operators
to study triangular Leibniz bialgebras. A one-to-one correspondence between factorizable Leib-
niz bialgebras and quadratic Rota-Baxter Leibniz algebras of nonzero weights was established by
Bai, Liu, Sheng, and Tang [8], who also introduced the notions of quasi-triangular and factorizable
Leibniz bialgebras. Here, we use quasi-triangular (resp. triangular, factorizable) Leibniz bialgebras
to construct quasi-triangular (resp. triangular, factorizable) Lie bialgebras.
By showing that the tensor product of a Leibniz (co)algebra and a Zinbiel (co)algebra admits a
Lie (co)algebra structure, we are able to construct Lie bialgebras on the tensor product of Leibniz
bialgebras and quadratic Zinbiel algebras. Manin triples of Lie algebras (resp. Leibniz algebras)
provide an equivalent description of Lie bialgebras (resp. Leibniz bialgebras). We present a natural
construction of Manin triples of Lie algebras from those of Leibniz algebras. By establishing
the relationship between solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation (CYBE) in the induced
Lie algebra and solutions of the Leibniz Yang-Baxter equation (LYBE) in the original Leibniz
algebra, we can construct quasi-triangular (resp. triangular, factorizable) Lie bialgebras from the

1.3. Zinbiel bialgebra and their aﬃnization constructions of Lie bialgebras. Zinbiel algebras
were introduced by Loday in [38]. Under Koszul duality, the operad of Zinbiel algebras is dual to
that of Leibniz algebras. Zinbiel algebras are also known as pre-commutative algebras or chrono-
logical algebras [32], and they are equivalent to commutative dendriform algebras [3]. They play
an important role in the deﬁnition of pre-Gerstenhaber algebras [4]. Recently, Zinbiel algebras
have appeared in the study of rack cohomology [15], number theory [11], and in the construction
of Cartesian diﬀerential categories [31]. Moreover, Wang developed a bialgebra theory for Zinbiel
algebras and studied quasi-triangular and factorizable Zinbiel bialgebras [44].
A crucial construction of inﬁnite-dimensional Lie algebras is known as the process of aﬃniza-
tion. Roughly speaking, the aﬃnization of a given algebra structure consists of deﬁning an inﬁnite-
dimensional algebra structure and then obtaining another algebra structure on the tensor product of
the original algebra with the inﬁnite-dimensional one, which in turn can resolve the original algebra
structure. The aﬃnization of Novikov bialgebras, perm bialgebras, and dendriform D-bialgebras
has been studied in [26], [35], and [29], respectively, using an inﬁnite-dimensional algebra structure
on the vector space of Laurent polynomials. In this paper, we deﬁne a Z-graded Leibniz algebra bV4
in Example 2.7, and obtain an aﬃnization characterization of Zinbiel algebras by (bV4, ◦). Moreover,
we show that there exists a completed Lie coalgebra structure on the tensor product of a Zinbiel
coalgebra and a completed Leibniz coalgebra, which could give a characterization of the Zinbiel
coalgebra by its aﬃnization with a completed Leibniz coalgebra structure on the vector space of bV4.
Therefore, we show that there is a natural completed Lie bialgebra structure on the tensor product
of a Zinbiel bialgebra and a quadratic Z-graded Leibniz algebra. By the quadratic Z-graded Leibniz
algebra (bV4, ◦, ω), an aﬃnization characterization of a Leibniz bialgebra is given.
Theorem [Theorems 4.14 and 4.17] Let (A, ·, ∆) be a Zinbiel bialgebra, (B = ⊕i∈ZBi, ◦, ω) be a
quadratic Z-graded Leibniz algebra and (g := A ⊗B, [−, −]) be the induced Lie algebra from (A, ·)
and (B, ◦). Deﬁne two linear maps ϑ : B →B ˆ⊗B by Eq. (4.4) and δ : g →g ˆ⊗g by Eq. (2.2).
Then (g, [−, −], δ) is a completed Lie bialgebra. In particular, if (B = ⊕i∈ZBi, ◦, ω) is the quadratic

In addition, we use quasi-Frobenius Zinbiel algebras to construct quasi-Frobenius Z-graded Lie
algebras, thereby providing many examples of quasi-Frobenius Lie algebras.

2. Lie (co)algebras on tensor product of Zinbiel (co)algebras and Leibniz (co)algebras
In this section, we recall the notions of Zinbiel (co)algebras and Leibniz (co)algebras, and show
that there are Lie (co)algebra structures on the tensor product of Zinbiel (co)algebras and Leibniz
(co)algebras.
2.1. Zinbiel algebras and Leibniz algebras.
Deﬁnition 2.1. A (left) Zinbiel algebra (A, ·) is a vector space A together with a binary operation
· : A ⊗A →A satisfying the following Zinbiel identity:
a1 · (a2 · a3) = (a1 · a2) · a3 + (a2 · a1) · a3, ∀a1, a2, a3 ∈A.
A Zinbiel algebra A is left commutative, i.e., a1 · (a2 · a3) = a2 · (a1 · a3) for all a1, a2, a3 ∈A.
For the classiﬁcation of low-dimensional Zinbiel algebras, see [2, 5, 18]. Below we present some
examples of Zinbiel algebras.
Example 2.2. (i) (A, ·) is a 2-dimensional Zinbiel algebra with a basis {e1, e2} whose non-zero
product · is given by e1 · e1 = e2.
(ii) (A, ·) is a 3-dimensional Zinbiel algebra with a basis {e1, e2, e3} whose non-zero product · is
given by e1 · e1 = e1 · e2 = e2 · e1 = e3.
(iii) (k[x], ·) is a Zinbiel algebra, where k[x] denotes the set of polynomials in one indeterminate
over k and the product · is given by

There are close connections between Zinbiel algebras and various types of algebras. A Zinbiel
algebra is equivalent to a commutative dendriform algebra. Consequently, if (A, ·) is a Zinbiel
algebra, then (A, ⋄) is a commutative associative algebra, where a1 ⋄a2 := a1 · a2 + a2 · a1 for
all a1, a2 ∈A. Moreover, every Zinbiel algebra under the commutator product yields a Tortkara
algebra [19, 25].
Deﬁnition 2.3. A representation of a Zinbiel algebra (A, ·) is a triple (V, l, r), where V is a vector
space, l, r : A →End(V) are linear maps such that the following equations hold for all a1, a2 ∈A:
l(a2)l(a1) = l(a2 · a1) + l(a1 · a2),
r(a1 · a2) = r(a2)r(a1) + r(a2)l(a1) = l(a1)r(a2).
Two representations (V, l, r) and (V′, l′, r′) are called equivalent if there exists a linear isomorphism
f : V →V′ such that f(l(a)(v)) = l′(a)(f(v)) and f(r(a)(v)) = r′(a)( f(v)) for all a ∈A and v ∈V.

Then (A ⊗B, [−, −]) is a Lie algebra, called the induced Lie algebra from (A, ·) and (B, ◦).
