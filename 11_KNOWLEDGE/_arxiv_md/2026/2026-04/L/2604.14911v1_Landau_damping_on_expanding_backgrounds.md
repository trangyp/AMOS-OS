LANDAU DAMPING ON EXPANDING BACKGROUNDS
DAVID FAJMAN, LIAM URBAN
Abstract
We analyse the effect of expansion in Newtonian cosmology on the asymptotic behaviour of charged
self-interacting plasmas close to Poisson equilibria. To this end, we study the Vlasov-Poisson system on
the phase space of a 3-torus which is expanding with respect to the scale factor a(t). We show that, for
a(t) = tq with q ∈(0, 1
2), solutions to this system exhibit nonlinear Landau damping for initial data that is
small with respect to a suitably strong Gevrey class, i.e., the charge density contrast of the plasma decays
superpolynomially. For larger choices of q within this range, the initial data requirements become stricter
while the decay weakens. To our knowledge, this is the first result showing Landau damping in a cosmological
setting.

Landau damping describes a phenomenon for collisionless gases, first truly demonstrated by
Landau in [Lan46; TH65], improving upon initial observations by Vlasov, see [Vla45]. In short, it
refers to strong decay of the electromagnetic field generated by the perturbation of a charged plasma
near an equilibrium, or equivalently of non-zero modes of the charge density of a plasma close to
an equilibrium. From the modern mathematical viewpoint discussed below, it is best understood
as a specific phase mixing effect for the Vlasov-Poisson system, and not necessarily restricted to
plasmas, see [MV11; BMM16]. The most general condition for Landau damping to occur is if the
background µ satisfies the Penrose stability condition, originating from [Pen60], namely

to [Vil10; Bed22].
The first full proof of nonlinear Landau damping is due to Mouhot and Villani in their break-
through work [MV11] for analytic perturbations, assuming the Penrose condition (1.4) to hold. In
particular, in [MV11, Proposition 2.1], it is shown that the Penrose condition unifies various other
stability conditions previously present in the literature. That this result extends to (γ−1)-Gevrey
regular perturbations with γ > 1
3, as conjectured in [MV11, Section 13] via a heuristic nonlinear
analysis, has since been rigorously established over the course of multiple works that also greatly
simplify the original proof, such as [BMM16; GNR21]. In addition, the effect has recently even been
demonstrated to hold in the Gevrey-critical regime γ = 1
3 in [Ion+24a]. This still rather strong
regularity requirement seems necessary as Sobolev regularity is generally insufficient to control
nonlinear resonances, often referred to as plasma echoes, see [Bed21]. However, these resonances
can be controlled on fixed time scales or for certain specific solution families, see [GNR22; Zil25].
Finally, the explicit structure of the Poisson equilibrium has proven to be especially useful to study
more difficult settings, as it was used in [Ion+24b] to show Landau damping on R3×R3. Therein, it
provided a direct method to analyse the effects of dispersion for low frequencies where an analogue
of the Penrose condition would necessarily be violated.
1.1.2. Newtonian gravity and cosmology. To the extent that phase mixing effects have been studied
in Newtonian gravity, this has largely been in the context of isolated bodies, where the Vlasov-
Poisson system is coupled to an additional external field that simulates the gravitational effect of
a large body, e.g., a Keplerian potential modeling an isolated star in [CL24; HS25] or describing
the potential well of a spherically symmetric black hole in [RS20]. We also refer to the textbook
[BT08, Chapters 4f.] for a broader overview on the Vlasov-Poisson system in stellar dynamics.
Models arising from Newtonian cosmology often consider the special case of (1.2) where the
scale factor is determined by the rate of expansion, with a linearized stability analysis similar
but not identical to that of its macroscopic analogue, the expanding Euler-Poisson system; see
for example [Pee80, Chapter II]. Both the Vlasov- and Euler-Poisson systems admit power law
solutions with a(t) = C t
2
3 which matches the behaviour of the scale factor in a dust-filled FLRW
universe, and one expects the so-called Jeans instability to lead to gravitational collapse.
For
the Euler-Poisson system, rigorous results appear to match this expectation: In the recent work
[Faj+25] including the first author, the expanding Euler-Poisson system was studied, where the

Less is known for the expanding Vlasov-Poisson system. Again in the case of gravitational

existence for compactly supported data and backgrounds with compact momentum support was
shown by Rein and Rendall in [RR94]. The scale factors admissible therein either cause recollapse
in finite time, exhibit dust-like power law expansion or grow linearly as t →∞. In the latter
case, these equilibria were also shown to be nonlinearly stable in [Rei97], in the sense that dilution
ensures that the density contrast remains small if it is small initially, while the power-law solution
needs to be excluded explicitly. These results have been extended to the Boltzmann-Poisson system,
in particular with positive cosmological constant modeled by an external field, in [Lee10; Lee13;
Lee16]. In all of these cases, this extension essentially works since admissible scale factors behave
asymptotically similarly with and without collisions, and in particular remain strong enough to
make collisions asymptotically negligible toward the future.
If one wants to precisely study the effect of expansion on the damping rate as we intend, one
needs to decouple it from the matter conent itself and has to consider the Vlasov-Poisson system
subject to an external field driving expansion, as discussed in Appendix A. In particular, we note
that such an external field is time-dependent, as opposed to the time-independent field caused by
a single static stellar body discussed at the start of this section.
Having chosen such a field, Theorem 2.8 shows that repulsive particle interaction leads to nonlinear
stability, and actively homogenizes due to the occurance of Landau damping, if the expansion rate
is sufficiently weak rather than sufficiently strong as in [Rei97; Lee16; Faj+25].
One has to expect this to break down for self-gravitating matter and in three spatial dimensions,
even for slow expansion rates and the Poisson equilibrium, as discussed in Appendix B.2. Curiously,
for d ≥4, Landau damping actually extends to the self-interacting regime, which we formulate and
prove more precisely in Section B.1.
1.1.3. Relativistic equations. While phase mixing results in cosmologically motivated settings are
still rather sparse, we note that such effects have been observed for relativistic kinetic equations
near black hole exteriors in a similar vein to the Newtonian phenomena discussed at the start of
the previous section, see [RS18; RS24].
For the relativistic Vlasov-Poisson system with repulsive particle interaction, which arises as
a special case of relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell system, Landau damping was shown for the linearised
equations on T3 and R3 by Young in [You15], and then partially extended to the nonlinear setting
in [You16]. For both of these results, the fact that information cannot travel faster than the speed
of light imposes limitations compared to the classical setting: [You15, Theorem 2.1] states that, in
most settings, exponential decay of the density modes is prohibited, and thus any damping effect
one can establish must be weaker than in the classical setting. Indeed, in [You15, Theorem 2.2], the
modes decay as exp(−ctγ) for γ < 1. In [You16], the background is taken to be (γ−1)-Gevrey regu-
lar with γ < 1, and nevertheless, one can only obtain damping in (γ′)−1-Gevrey classes with γ′ < γ.
In their recent work [TVR25], Taylor and Velozo Ruiz studied phase mixing for the relativis-
tic massive Vlasov equation on a fixed FLRW background with power law expansion and without
particle interaction. Therein, they were able to show that phase mixing occurs if the background
expands with tq for q ∈(0, 1
2) via a vector field method.

When comparing this work to our present results, it is useful to view (1.2) as the formal nonrela-
tivistic limit of the relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell system on an FLRW background after transforming
the system into coordinates comoving with respect to expansion. We discuss this more detail in Re-
mark A.2. From this viewpoint, Theorem 2.8 confirms that the critical threshold for phase mixing
of the free transport equation is also the critical threshold of Landau damping when introducing re-
pulsive Newtonian particle interaction. In fact, we even obtain stronger decay rates for the density
contrast in Theorem 2.8 than in [TVR25], as discussed after Theorem 2.8. It is not clear whether
the decay bounds obtained in [TVR25] are sharp, especially since Gevrey regularity is not its main
focus. Nevertheless, this difference in decay rates may be due to differences between the relativis-
tic system and the Newtonian model as in the works by Young on the relativistic Vlasov-Poisson
system, but also due to the presence of a nontrivial background aiding decay of the perturbation,
leading to Landau damping that is truly stronger than phase mixing for the free transport equation
alone.
For the Einstein-Vlasov system, while homogenization of the matter content itself is not well
understood, expansion does cause many cosmological solutions to dilute toward the future when
the expansion rate is sufficiently strong, and thus the geometry on spatial slices in a suitable gauge
converges to a spatially homogeneous limit. This includes accelerated de-Sitter-like expansion in
[Rin13; AR16] and linear expansion in [AF20; BF22] as well as the 2+1-dimensional results [Faj17;
Faj18]. To obtain results for weaker expansion rates, including the FLRW solution on the torus
with zero cosmological constant, one likely would need to invoke stronger effects than dilution, such
as phase mixing in compact spatial topology or dispersion for open topologies. Regarding the lat-
ter, Taylor showed in [Tay24] that spherically symmetric perturbations of the FLRW solution with
spatial topology R3 within the Einstein-massless Vlasov system, which exhibit power law expansion
with q = 1
2, are stable due to dispersion.
Finally, we note that vector-field methods originally developed to show stability of Minkowski
spacetime within the Einstein-Vlasov system, such as in [LT20; FJS21; Big+21]2, can also be useful
in studying the Vlasov-Poisson and Vlasov-Maxwell systems, see for example [Smu16; Big25].
1.2. Organisation. In Section 2, we provide the necessary tools to precisely state the main result
in Theorem 2.8 and to outline the remainder of the proof. This splits into a linear analysis of
charge density modes in Section 3, and a bootstrap argument in Section 4 to deal with nonlinear
terms. We also provide three appendices: In Appendix A, we derive the expanding Vlasov-Poisson
system from a classical one for general dimensions and including gravitational particle interaction,
and elaborate on the relationship between (1.2) and charged relativistic equations. In Appendix
B, we discuss Landau damping in high spatial dimensions and some heuristics on why this fails for
d = 3. Finally, we collect tools regarding the Liouville-Green approximation in Appendix C, which
we use in Section 3.
