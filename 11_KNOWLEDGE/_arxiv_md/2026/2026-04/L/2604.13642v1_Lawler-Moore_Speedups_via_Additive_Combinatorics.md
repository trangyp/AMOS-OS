Abstract. The Lawler-Moore dynamic programming framework is a cornerstone of scheduling theory,
providing a robust approach for solving various parallel machine problems. The framework relies on two
essential properties: a regular objective function that is monotone in the completion times of jobs, and a
fixed priority ordering, such as Smith’s Rule or Jackson’s Rule, which dictates the processing sequence
on each machine. For fundamental objectives, including minimizing total weighted completion time
(Pm|| P wjCj), maximum lateness (Pm||Lmax), and the weighted number of tardy jobs (Pm|| P wjUj),
this framework yields running times of O(P m−1 · n) for the first two problems and O(P m · n) for the
third, where P denotes the total processing time of all jobs. Recent conditional lower bounds under the
Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis (SETH) suggest that the dependence on P is essentially optimal,
but they still leave open the possibility of improving the dependence on the maximum processing time
pmax of any single job.
In this paper, we present the first major speedup of the Lawler-Moore recurrence by introducing
a new state-pruning technique and a novel swapping argument. Our approach centers on a key lemma
from additive combinatorics, which we use to define a specific exchange of job subsets between machines.
We establish a new structural property showing that, whenever our specific swap does not increase the
objective value, there is an optimal schedule in which the load difference between any two machines is
bounded by 4p2

Introduction
The Lawler-Moore algorithm [LM69] stands as a cornerstone of scheduling theory, offering a robust
dynamic programming framework for solving a wide range of parallel machine problems in pseudo-
polynomial time. This framework is particularly effective for problems characterized by regular
objective functions that are monotone in the completion times of jobs. Additionally, it requires an
easily computed priority ordering that dictates the processing sequence once jobs are assigned to
their respective machines. By leveraging these two properties, the algorithm formulates the problem
as a structured set of recursive subproblems, yielding an efficient dynamic program.
To illustrate the Lawler-Moore approach, consider the problem of minimizing the total weighted
completion time on a set of m = O(1) identical parallel machines, denoted by Pm|| P wjCj in
standard scheduling notation [Gra69]. We are given a set of jobs J = {1, . . . , n}, where job j has an
integer processing time pj ∈N and an integer weight wj ∈N representing its relative importance
or holding cost. The goal is to schedule the jobs on parallel identical machines M1, . . . , Mm so as
to minimize their total weighted completion time. By Smith’s Rule [Smi56], there exists an optimal
schedule in which the jobs on each machine are processed in non-increasing order of their efficiencies
ej := wj/pj. Consequently, a schedule σ for J is fully defined by a partition J = J1(σ)∪. . .∪Jm(σ)
(i.e., a family of m subsets of jobs such that each job appears in exactly one subset), where Ji(σ) is
the set of jobs assigned to machine Mi. The objective is to find a schedule that minimizes P
j wjCj,
where Cj is the completion time of job j in the Smith-ordered sequence on its assigned machine.
In this context, the Lawler-Moore framework leverages Smith’s Rule [Smi56], which says that
the jobs on each machine are processed in non-increasing order of efficiency ej := wj/pj in any
optimal schedule. The dynamic program then defines a state Tj[P1, . . . , Pm−1] as the minimum cost
of scheduling the first j jobs, presorted by efficiency, where Pi represents the total processing time
(load) assigned to machine Mi. Consider some state Tj[P1, . . . , Pm−1]. Since the jobs are processed
according to Smith’s Rule, we can assume without loss of optimality that no job in {1, . . . , j −1} is
scheduled after job j. Consequently, if j is assigned to machine Mi, it completes at time Cj = Pi; if it
is assigned to Mm, it completes at time Cj = P(Jj)−(P1 +· · ·+Pm−1), where P(Jj) := p1 +· · ·+pj
is the total processing time of the first j jobs. This directly leads to the Lawler-Moore recurrence
relation:

With slight modifications, the Lawler-Moore framework also applies to several other classic
scheduling problems, most notably:
– The Pm||Lmax problem, which asks to minimize the maximum lateness. Here each job j ∈J
has a due date dj ∈N, and the lateness of job j is defined as Lj = Cj −dj.
– The Pm|| P wjUj problem, which asks to minimize the total weighted number of tardy jobs. A
job j ∈J is tardy if its completion time Cj exceeds its due date; this is represented by a binary
variable Uj that equals 1 iff Cj > dj.
For both objectives, Jackson’s Rule [Jac55] provides the necessary priority ordering, establishing
that there exists an optimal schedule in Earliest Due Date (EDD) order. While the recurrence for
Pm||Lmax mirrors the (m −1)-dimensional state space of the weighted completion time problem,

Despite its foundational status, the Lawler-Moore framework has remained essentially stagnant
for almost sixty years. Since its introduction, the O(P m−1 · n) time complexity has served as an
unchallenged baseline for pseudo-polynomial algorithms in parallel machine environments. Recent
conditional lower bounds reinforce this baseline, showing that, assuming the Strong Exponential
Time Hypothesis (SETH), the running time in Theorem 1 is essentially optimal for all three problems
above.
Theorem 2 ([BDW26]). Assuming SETH, for any ε > 0, the Pm|| P wjCj and Pm||Lmax prob-
lems cannot be solved in P m−1−ε · 2o(n) time, and the Pm|| P wjUj problem cannot be solved in
P m−ε · 2o(n) time.
Since the total processing time P can be as large as pmax · n, where pmax is the maximum pro-
cessing time of any single job, the most natural path toward algorithmic improvement is to refine
the dependence on pmax. While such improvements exist, they are often restricted: n-fold integer
programming [KK18] provides powerful FPT results for arbitrary m but lacks efficient pseudo-
polynomial runtimes when m = O(1). More recently, techniques based on integer programming
with thin constraint matrices have been used to efficiently solve makespan minimization and re-
lated variants [HS26]. However, this approach does not easily apply to Pm|| P wjCj because of its
non-linear objective, and the running times obtained for Pm||Lmax and Pm|| P wjUj involve a mul-
tiplicative factor of pd#
max, where d# is the number of distinct due dates in the instance. This renders
the approach impractical for instances with many distinct due dates. In this paper, we introduce
the first major speedup technique designed to optimize the Lawler-Moore recurrence directly.
1.1
Our results
The main contribution of this paper is a new framework for accelerating the classical Lawler-Moore
algorithm, particularly in the regime where the maximum processing time pmax is small relative
to the total processing time P. By identifying and pruning redundant states within the dynamic
program, our framework replaces the dependence on the total processing time P with a dependence
on pmax, yielding the following results:
Theorem 3. The Pm|| P wjCj and Pm||Lmax problems can be solved in O(p2m−2
max

the Pm|| P wjUj problem can be solved in time O(p2m−2

Consequently, the running time depends on pmax rather than on the total processing time P. This
makes the resulting algorithms significantly more efficient in scenarios characterized by a large
number of relatively short tasks.
A central component of our algorithm is a new swapping argument that yields structural the-
orems for all three problems in Theorem 3. Swapping arguments that exchange the positions of
jobs in a schedule have long been used to prove the optimality of priority rules such as Smith’s
Rule and Jackson’s Rule. However, whereas these traditional arguments typically focus on pairs of
jobs, our approach swaps sets of jobs using a lemma from additive combinatorics. Specifically, the
lemma states that sufficiently large multisets over a common set of integers {1, . . . , U} must have
submultisets whose elements have equal sum.

1.2
Related work
We briefly review the state-of-the-art results for the Pm|| P wjCj, Pm||Lmax, and Pm|| P wjUj
problems, as well as their generalizations to arbitrary m. Our discussion focuses exclusively on exact
algorithms and hardness results for these three problems.
While the single-machine 1|| P wjCj problem is efficiently solved by Smith’s Rule in O(n log n)
time, this result only extends to the parallel machine unweighted counterpart for an arbitrary
number of machines (the P|| P Cj problem) [CMM67]. The weighted version Pm|| P wjCj is NP-
hard for m ≥2, even when pj = wj for all jobs j ∈J [BJS74], while P|| P wjCj (where m is
arbitrary) is strongly NP-hard [GJ90]. On the other hand, the problem is only weakly NP-hard
for fixed m, due to Lawler and Moore’s pseudo-polynomial algorithm [LM69]. Furthermore, Knop
and Koutecký [KK18] showed that P|| P wjCj is fixed-parameter tractable (FPT) for parameter
k = max{pmax, wmax}, by presenting an algorithm for the problem running in kO(k2) · nO(1) time.
The single-machine 1||Lmax problem is optimally solved by Jackson’s rule in O(n log n)
time [Jac55]. However, the problem is significantly more difficult on identical parallel machines,
as P||Lmax generalizes the classical makespan minimization problem P||Cmax, even when jobs have
precedence constraints and non-trivial release times [LLRK76]. Since P||Cmax is NP-hard for any
fixed m ≥2 [LRKB77], and strongly NP-hard for arbitrary m, this also holds for P||Lmax. On
the positive side, the problem remains solvable in O(P m−1 · n) time via the Lawler-Moore frame-
work [LM69]. More recently, Hermelin and Shabtay [HS26] utilized thin ILPs to provide an algorithm

Preliminaries
We consider a set of jobs J = {1, . . . , n}, given offline, to be scheduled non-preemptively on a set
of m identical parallel machines M1, . . . , Mm, for some fixed constant m = O(1). The processing
time, weight, and due date of job j ∈J are denoted by pj, wj, and dj, respectively, and the efficiency
of job j is defined by ej := wj/pj. We denote the total processing time of all jobs by P := P
j pj,
and the maximum processing time by pmax = maxj pj. For a subset of jobs J′ ⊆J, we write
P(J′) = P
j∈J′ pj for the total processing time of the jobs in J′ (so, in particular, P = P(J)).
Throughout this paper, we assume that the jobs in J are preordered according to a fixed priority
ordering consistent with the specific objective function being minimized.
– For Pm|| P wjCj, jobs are ordered such that e1 ≥e2 ≥· · · ≥en.
– For Pm|| P wjUj and Pm||Lmax, jobs are ordered d1 ≤d2 ≤· · · ≤dn.
Under this assumption, whenever two jobs j1 < j2 are assigned to the same machine in an optimal
schedule, there exists an optimal schedule in which j1 is processed before j2. We refer to such
schedules as proper schedules.
Since the processing order is predetermined in a proper schedule, any such schedule σ is deter-
mined by a partition σ = {J1(σ), . . . , Jm(σ)}, where Ji(σ) ⊆J is the set of jobs assigned to machine
Mi for all i ∈{1, . . . , m}. Here, we say that J1(σ), . . . , Jm(σ) ⊆J is a partition of J if each job in
J appears in exactly one subset Ji(σ). For j ∈J, we let Jj = {1, . . . , j} denote the set of all jobs
that precede j in the priority ordering, and define Ji,j(σ) := Ji(σ) ∩Jj. The load on Mi at step j
of σ is the total processing time of jobs in Ji,j(σ), namely P(Ji,j(σ)). Because the total load on all
machines at step j equals P(Jj), the load of the m-th machine is implicitly determined by the loads
of the first m −1 machines:

Swapping via Additive Combinatorics
We now describe a general swapping argument that can be applied to various scheduling problems on
parallel machines. Let f be an arbitrary regular objective function that admits a priority ordering.
Broadly speaking, given any proper schedule σ for J, the swap defined below, when admissible,
produces an alternative proper schedule σ′′ for J (the notation σ′ is reserved for an intermediate
schedule produced by the swap). We say that the swap is optimal for f if f(σ′′) ≤f(σ) for every
proper schedule σ. The goal of this swapping argument is the following generic structure theorem,
which allows us to prune the state space of the Lawler-Moore dynamic program.
Theorem 4. Let f be a regular objective function that admits a priority ordering. Suppose that the
swap described below is optimal for f. Then any Pm||f instance admits an optimal proper schedule σ
for f with
|P(Jh,j(σ)) −P(Ji,j(σ))| ≤4p2

3.1
Additive combinatorics lemma
Our swapping argument relies on the following lemma regarding the existence of submultisets with
equal sums. A similar argument was used by Polak, Rohwedder, and Węgrzycki [PRW21, Lemma
2.1] to obtain a fast algorithm for the Knapsack problem. For a multiset of integers A, we write
Σ(A) for the sum of its elements, counted with multiplicity.
Lemma 1. Let U ∈N, and let A, B be multisets whose elements lie in {1, 2, . . . , U}. If |A|, |B| ≥2U
then there exist non-empty A′ ⊆A and B′ ⊆B such that Σ(A′) = Σ(B′).
Proof. Let {a1, .., a2U} ⊆A and {b1, .., b2U} ⊆B be arbitrary 2U elements in A and B. We construct
sets Ai ⊆A and Bi ⊆B for each i ∈{0, 1, .., 2U} as follows:
– For i = 0, we set A0 := B0 := ∅.
– For i > 0, if Σ(Ai−1) ≤Σ(Bi−1) then we set Ai := Ai−1 ∪{ai} and Bi := Bi−1, otherwise we
set Ai := Ai−1 and Bi := Bi−1 ∪{bi}.
Observe that −U < Σ(Ai) −Σ(Bi) ≤U for all i ∈{0, 1, .., 2U}. By the pigeonhole principle, since
there are 2U integers x with −U < x ≤U and 2U + 1 many indices i, there exist i < j with
Σ(Ai) −Σ(Bi) = Σ(Aj) −Σ(Bj). Rearranging yields Σ(Aj) −Σ(Ai) = Σ(Bj) −Σ(Bi), and so
Σ(Aj \ Ai) = Σ(Bj \ Bi). Set A′ := Aj \ Ai and B′ := Bj \ Bi. Then Σ(A′) = Σ(B′), and since
j > i at least one of A′ and B′ is nonempty. As Σ(A′) = Σ(B′), actually both A′, B′ ̸= ∅.

Discussion
In this paper, we refine the Lawler-Moore framework by replacing the traditional pseudo-polynomial
dependence on the total processing time P with a dependence on the maximum job size pmax. Our
results provide significant algorithmic speedups for fundamental parallel-machine problems and show
that the SETH-based lower bounds previously associated with P can be bypassed through a more
fine-grained parameterization. Moreover, we obtain a generic structural result on optimal solutions
that may be useful for variants and special cases of these problems. We believe that our approach is
12


## Page 14


general enough to accelerate a wide range of other dynamic programming recurrences in scheduling
and combinatorial optimization.
Several interesting directions for future research remain. Below we provide a partial list of those
we believe are the most important (in no particular order):
1. Can one solve Pm|| P wjCj or Pm|| P wjUj in wO(1)
max ·n time? Our entire framework is based on
swapping sets of jobs with equal processing time, so a positive answer would most likely require
a fundamentally different approach.
2. Can P2|| P wjCj, P2||Lmax, or 1|| P wjUj be solved in eO(p2
max + n) time? Such a running time
exists for P2||Cmax by the recent Knapsack algorithms [Bri24,Jin24].
3. In [ABHS22], a lower bound of eO(n + p1−ε
max · n) is shown for several scheduling problems on one
or two machines, including P2||Lmax and 1|| P wjUj. Can one improve this bound, or extend it
to problems with more than two machines?
4. What about the Rm machine model, where the pi,j’s are arbitrary. Can one get algorithms with
running times of the form pO(1)
max · n in this environment as well?
