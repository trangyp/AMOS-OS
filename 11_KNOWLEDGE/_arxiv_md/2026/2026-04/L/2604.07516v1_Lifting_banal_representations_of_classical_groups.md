Lifting banal representations of classical groups
Johannes Droschl
April 10, 2026
Abstract
Let G be a symplectic or a split orthogonal group over a local non-
archimedean field F. A prime ℓis called banal with respect to G if it does
not divide the cardinality of the k-points of G, where k is the residue field
of F. In this paper we show that for every banal prime ℓ, any smooth
irreducible Fℓ-representation of G(F) admits a lift to Qℓ. We also state
similar results for more general classical groups of symplectic, orthogonal
or unitary type. As an application we prove Howe-duality in the strongly
banal case for symplectic-orthogonal or unitary dual pairs.

Introduction
Let F be a local non-archimedean field of characteristic different from 2 with ring
of integers O and residue field k of cardinality q. Let ℓbe a prime not dividing q
and G a reductive group over F which we assume in this introduction to be split.
The study of representations of G(F) over various fields of coefficients lies at
the heart of the automorphic side of the Local Langlands program. Depending
on the chosen field and the group G, several classification results have been
achieved. For example, if G is a general linear group, the work of Bernstein and
Zelevinsky [5] settles the case of representations with complex coefficients, and
the work of Vignéras, see for example [30], and Mínguez and Sécherre, cf. [26],
the case of Fℓ-coefficients. In the case of classical groups, the theory of Arthur
[1] provides us with a good understanding of the tempered representations with
complex coefficients. However, unlike in the case of the general linear group,
the behavior of representations of classical groups with coefficients in fields of
non-zero characteristic remains largely a mystery.
Taking inspiration from finite groups, the Fℓ-representation theory should be
reminiscent of the C-representation theory, as long as ℓis large in comparison
to the size of G. Such ℓare called banal. Over a finite group W, such a size
condition is usually given by ℓ∤|W|. In this case the Fℓ-representations of W
behave exactly as their complex counterparts. In particular, one can realize
every irreducible representation in a family over Zℓ, i.e. there exists a Zℓ[W]-
stable lattice in any irreducible Qℓ-representation, and tensoring this lattice with
Fℓyields an irreducible representation. Moreover, any irreducible representation
over Fℓcan be constructed in this way.

The main goal of this note is to establish a similar result for symplectic, orthog-
onal, or unitary groups over non-archimedean local fields. We will assume in
this introduction that all groups are split, however we will treat in the paper
also the non-split case. Note that for general linear groups and their inner forms
this has been achieved in [24], however our methods are quite different and in
fact can also be adapted to give a new proof in the general linear case. To be
more precise, we call a smooth irreducible Qℓ-representation ˜π of G(F) a lift of
an irreducible Fℓ-representation π of G(F) if there exists a Zℓ-lattice o inside ˜π,
in the sense of [30], such that o ⊗ZℓFℓ∼= π.
We assume that G is unramified over O. In this case we can take [10, Lemma
5.22] as a definition of a banal prime.
Definition 1. A prime ℓis called banal with respect to G if it does not divide
|G(k)|.
The main result of the paper is the following, see Corollary 6.1 and Corol-
lary 6.3.
Theorem 1. Let G be a symplectic or split orthogonal group and ℓa banal prime
with respect to G. Any smooth irreducible Fℓ-representation π of G admits a lift
to Qℓ.
Moreover, we will also explain how a similar result can be achieved for general
linear groups and more general classical groups of symplectic, orthogonal, or
unitary type.
In Corollary 6.3 we also give a sufficient condition involving
the cuspidal support when an integral representation over Qℓreduces to an
irreducible representation over Fℓ. The starting point of our explorations will
be the following theorem.
Theorem 2 ([9, Proposition 4.15]). Theorem 1 is true for cuspidal representa-

In the last section, we give a proof of the modular Howe-duality conjecture in
the strongly banal case for symplectic-orthogonal and unitary dual pairs. The
modular local theta correspondence has been studied and developed in great
detail in [29], where the author managed to prove the respective statements of
Howe-duality under a certain hypothesis which he calls Hypothesis (H) and in
turn boils down to the question whether a certain principal series representation
is irreducible. Note that in [29] Hypothesis (H) was proven for all but finitely
many primes ℓ, however no explicit descriptions of those that satisfy it was
known. Thanks to the work of [29] it will thus suffice to prove that the reduction
mod ℓof a certain integral irreducible representation is irreducible. Using the
above results, we can achieve this without much effort and hence complete
the last missing step in the proof of the modular theta correspondence in the
strongly banal case as laid out in [29]. For more details see Section 7.
Let us finally quickly note that in the non-split case, we will define a class of banal
representations over Fℓand show in Corollary 6.4 that any banal representation
admits a lift to Qℓ. To be more precise, a representation is called banal if its
cuspidal support can be lifted in a banal way, a notion that is inspired by the
methods of [24], for more details see Section 2.2. In particular, if ℓis banal with
respect to G, all irreducible representations of G are banal by Lemma 2.7.
