LOCALLY FINITE SOLVABLE LIE ALGEBRAS OF DERIVATIONS
MIKHAIL ZAIDENBERG
Abstract. Let X be an affine variety and h ⊂Lie(Aut(X)) be a solvable Lie subalgebra
generated by a finite collection of locally finite Lie subalgebras. The authors of [3] wondered
whether h is itself locally finite. Here we present some criteria for the local finiteness of
h. Under a certain additional assumption, we answer this question in the affirmative in the
particular case where X is the affine plane A2.

1. Introduction
All the algebraic varieties in this paper are defined over an algebraically closed field k of
characteristic zero. Let X be an affine variety over k, and h a Lie subalgebra of Lie(Aut(X)).
We say that h is locally finite if every f ∈O(X) belongs to a finite-dimensional vector subspace
of O(X) invariant under h, see, e.g., [10, Definition 1.5.1]. A derivation ∂∈Der(O(X)) is said
to be locally finite if the Lie subalgebra k∂is locally finite. Recall the following questions.
Questions 1.1 ([3, Question 1]). Let h = ⟨h1, . . . , hk⟩Lie be a solvable Lie subalgebra of
Lie(Aut(X)) generated by the locally finite Lie subalgebras hi ⊂Lie(Aut(X)), i = 1, . . . , k. Is
it true that h is locally finite?
The author knows of no example where h as above were not locally finite.
Recall that a locally finite Lie subalgebra h ⊂Lie(Aut(X)) is finite-dimensional, see [10,
Lemma 1.6.2]. Question 1.1 is related to the following one.

Questions 1.2 (see [10, Question 2]). Let {hi}i be a family of locally finite Lie subalgebras of
Der(O(X)). Is is true that the Lie subalgebra h ⊂Lie(Aut(X)) generated by the hi is locally
finite provided that it is finite-dimensional?
Here, we only consider the case of a solvable Lie algebra h. In Section 2, after recalling
some results from [3] and [10], we give the following finite-dimensionality criterion for such a
Lie algebra h.
Proposition 1.3 (see Proposition 2.11). Let h ⊂Lie(Aut(X)) be a Lie subalgebra as in
Question 1.1. Then h is finite-dimensional if and only if the derived ideal [h, h] is nilpotent.
In Section 3 we show that, in the case where X = A2, the answer to Question 1.1 is
affirmative under an additional assumption.
Theorem 1.4 (cf. Theorem 3.1). A solvable Lie subalgebra
h = ⟨∂1, . . . , ∂k⟩Lie ⊂Lie(Aut(A2))
generated by locally finite derivations ∂1, . . . , ∂k is locally finite and triangulable, provided h
contains a locally finite derivation ∂that is not locally nilpotent.
We believe that an analogue of this theorem, with a similar proof, also remains valid for
normal affine toric surfaces and more general affine toric varieties. On the other hand, we
do not know the answer to Question 1.1 in the case where X = A2 and all locally finite
derivations in h are locally nilpotent.
The content of the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we consider Question 1.1 for general
affine varieties, and we perform several successive reductions. In particular, we prove Propo-
sition 1.3. In Section 3 we turn to the case of the affine plane. After recalling necessary
preliminaries on the structure of Lie(Aut(A2)) as a bigraded Lie algebra, we prove Theorem
1.4. In Lemma 3.21 we treat the case, where h is of rank 2, and in Proposition 3.23 the case,
where h is of rank 1. Besides, we show that every solvable subalgebra h ⊂Lie(Aut(A2))
as in Question 1.1 is triangulable, see Lemma 3.4. The latter does not hold, in general, for
solvable locally finite Lie subalgebras of Lie(Aut(An)) if n ≥3, see Remark 3.5.2. Answering
a question proposed by A. Regeta1, we show that a solvable subalgebra h ⊂Lie(Aut(A2))
consisting of locally finite derivations is filtered by a sequence of locally finite Lie subalgebras
and is triangulable, see Corollary 3.24.
2. General affine varieties
2.1. First reduction. We can consider that every hi in Question 1.1 is one-dimensional.
Indeed, being locally finite, hi is finite-dimensional and consists of locally finite derivations.
Thus, every hi is generated by a finite set of locally finite derivations, and the same is true
for h.

∂s and ∂n, see, e.g., [12, Theorems 9.4.2 and 9.4.3]. Furthermore, ad∂= ad∂s + ad∂n is a Jordan

2.3. Third reduction. We use the following terminology, see for example [10, Sec. 1.8].
Definition 2.4. A Lie subalgebra t ⊂Lie(Aut(X)) is said to be toral if it consists of semisim-
ple derivations. The rank rk(h) is the maximal dimension of toral subalgebras of h.
We will need the following lemma.
Lemma 2.5 ([10, Lemma 1.8.2]). For a toral Lie subalgebra t ⊂Der(O(X)), there exists
a unique smallest torus Tmin = Tmin(t) ⊂Aut(X) such that t ⊂Lie(Tmin). Every subspace
E ⊂Der(O(X)) invariant under adt is also invariant under Tmin. Furthermore, t is locally
finite and dim(t) ≤dim(X). If dim(t) = dim(X), then X is a toric variety and t is algebraic.
Definition 2.6 (see, e.g., [9]). A Lie subalgebra h ⊂Der(O(X)) is said to be algebraic (resp.
integrable) if h = Lie(G) (resp. h ⊂Lie(G)) for an algebraic subgroup G ⊂Aut(X).
A derivation ∂∈Der(O(X)) is called algebraic (resp. integrable) if the Lie subalgebra k∂
is algebraic (resp. integrable).
We know that h is integrable if and only if it is locally finite, see [10, Theorem E]. In the
latter case, h ⊂Lie(Aut(X)). For example, every locally nilpotent derivation is algebraic,
and every semisimple derivation is integrable. Indeed, if s ∈Der(O(X)) is semisimple, then s
is contained in a unique minimal algebraic torus Tmin(s) ⊂Aut(X), see [7, Proposition 7.6.1].

2.4. Nilpotency of the derived ideal. Recall that the derived ideal g(1) = [g, g] of a
solvable finite-dimensional Lie algebra g is nilpotent. This is no longer true, in general, if g
is infinite-dimentional, see, e.g., Example 2.12 below. We have the following criterion for the
finite-dimensionality of a solvable Lie algebra.
Lemma 2.10. Let h be a finitely generated solvable Lie algebra. Then h is finite-dimensional
if and only if the derived ideal h(1) is nilpotent and finitely generated.

dimensional. Thus, h is finite-dimensional if and only if h(1) is. If h is finite-dimensional, then
h(1) is nilpotent and finitely generated.

Remarks 3.5.
