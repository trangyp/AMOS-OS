Abstract
We study the optimal transport problem on globally hyperbolic spacetimes associ-
ated with Orlicz-type Lorentzian cost functions of the form u ◦ℓ, where u is a suitable
monotonically increasing and concave function, and ℓis the time separation. Our work
encompasses and generalises the case u(x) = up(x) = p−1xp for p ∈(0, 1), as well as the
more recent p < 0, which have been the only examples considered so far in the litera-
ture. A fundamental notion for our purposes is the property of u-separation for a pair of
measures, which generalises McCann’s p-separation and for which we are able to obtain
strong duality to the full Orlicz-type optimisation problem. In our main results, we char-
acterise timelike Ricci curvature lower bounds via the convexity of the relative entropy
along geodesics arising from the Orlicz-type optimal transport with cost u ◦ℓ, which is a
far-reaching generalisation of McCann’s seminal work in the case u = up, p ∈(0, 1).
Keywords: Lorentzian optimal transport, Orlicz–Wasserstein, entropic convexity, timelike
Ricci curvature bounds

Orlicz spaces are a generalisation of Lp-spaces, allowing for more general convex functions u
(usually called Young functions in the literature) in the definition of the norm, with u(x) := xp
reproducing the well-known Lp-norm. We refer to the textbooks of Rao–Ren [27, 28] for a
detailed treatment of this classical topic in analysis.

As far as Lorentzian geometry and signature are concerned, methods of optimal transport have
seen a significant rise in popularity over the last decade (see for example the works of Eckstein–
Miller [11], Suhr [32], Kell–Suhr [15]). The seminal works of McCann [22] and Mondino–Suhr
[25] have established the equivalent characterisation of timelike Ricci curvature lower (and,
in the case of [25], also upper) bounds via convexity properties of the Boltzmann–Shannon
entropy, extended later to the R´enyi entropy by Braun [4] and to Finsler spacetimes by Braun–
Ohta [8]. This has led to a fruitful study of non-smooth Lorentzian spaces satisfying timelike
Ricci curvature bounds, where powerful theorems from classical smooth spacetime geometry
have been established in extremely low regularity (see e.g. Cavalletti–Mondino [9], Braun
[4, 5], Braun–McCann [7], Beran–Braun–Calisti–Gigli–McCann–Ohanyan–Rott–S¨amann [2]).

order-theoretic notions of completeness considered recently in the literature [2, 5, 6, 12, 24]
and global hyperbolicity on a (finite-dimensional) smooth spacetime. Under the assumption
of closedness of the causal relation ≤, it turns out that all of these notions are equivalent
to global hyperbolicity (see Theorem A.4), mirroring the well-known equivalence between
completeness and properness of a finite-dimensional Riemannian manifold as a consequence
of the Hopf–Rinow theorem. Given the indispensability of the assumption that the causal
relation be closed in the synthetic literature, our compatibility result justifies the restriction
to the globally hyperbolic setting whenever one deals with problems of optimal transport on
smooth spacetimes.

Outlook
Our work opens up the possibility of investigations in several different directions. First, one
could study the connection between timelike Ricci curvature upper bounds as considered by
Mondino–Suhr [25] in the general Orlicz setting introduced here, as well as broader ranges of
synthetic dimensions N and the convexity of R´enyi entropies in the Lorentz–Finsler context
50


## Page 51


(cf. Braun–Ohta [8]). Moreover, natural questions of stability of concepts we studied in this
work arise, such as optimal couplings, Kantorovich potentials, or the u-separation condition,
under suitable perturbations of the function u.
Second, just like the results of McCann [22] and Mondino–Suhr [25], our work motivates
a study of nonsmooth Lorentzian spaces satisfying synthetic Ricci curvature bounds below,
which we may term TCDu (timelike curvature-dimension) or TMCPu (timelike measure con-
traction property) spaces. One may ask whether these conditions are stable, not just un-
der suitable convergence of Lorentzian spaces, but also with respect to certain convergences
un →u of admissible functions. Results which consider the stability under perturbations
of u could help generalise the characterisation of timelike Ricci lower bounds via entropic
convexity, as presented in this work, to hold for a more general class of functions u, such
as the interesting edge case u(x) = u1(x) = x. Further topics worth looking into include a
study of the degenerate elliptic u-d’Alembertian and relevant comparison results in analogy
with [2, 5], as well as hyperbolic Orlicz spaces Lu which should fit into Gigli’s [12] setting of
hyperbolic Banach spaces.

Global hyperbolicity and order-theoretic completeness
In this appendix, we clarify the relationship between certain notions of order-theoretic com-
pleteness recently studied for the chronological [6] and causal [2, 12] relations, and global
hyperbolicity. Assuming closedness of the causal relation, these turn out to be equivalent
notions for (finite-dimensional) smooth spacetimes, mirroring the well-known equivalence
between completeness and properness for a Riemannian manifold as a consequence of the
Hopf–Rinow theorem. Recall that ˜g is a background complete Riemannian metric on M.
Definition A.1 (Causal forward/backward completeness). A spacetime (M, g) is called
causally forward complete if every sequence (xn) ⊆M with xn ≤xn+1 ≤z for all n ∈N
and some z ∈M, is convergent in M. Similarly, we call (M, g) causally backward complete if
it is forward complete when considered with the opposite time orientation. Analogously, we
say (M, g) is chronologically forward complete if every sequence xn ≪xn+1 ≪z converges,
and chronologically backward complete if its time-reversal is chronologically forward complete.
Lemma A.2 (Chronological completeness = causal completeness). A spacetime (M, g) is
chronologically forward (resp. backward) complete if and only if it is causally forward (resp.
backward) complete.
Proof. We only show the claim for the forward completeness notions. It is trivial that every
causally forward complete spacetime is chronologically forward complete. For the converse
direction, suppose (M, g) is chronologically forward complete and let pn ≤pn+1 ≤z be a
causally bounded ordered sequence. We claim that we can construct a sequence (xn) in such
a way that xn ≪xn+1 ≤z, xn ≪pn, d˜g(xn, pn) < n−1. Once we have done this, picking any
˜z ∈I+(z) shows the necessary chronological boundedness of xn, from which convergence of
(xn) and thus of (pn) follows, establishing causal forward completeness.
To construct xn, we proceed by induction. Pick x1 ∈I−(p1) such that d˜g(x1, p1) < 1. Suppose
x1, . . . , xn have been constructed, then by assumption xn ≪pn ≤pn+1 ≤z. By push-up,

(n + 1)−1. Thus we have constructed the desired sequence (xn).

(i) ⇔(iv), the equivalence (i) ⇔(v) is analogous.
(i) ⇒(iv) : Suppose (M, g) is globally hyperbolic, and let pn ≤pn+1 ≤y. Letting x := p1, we
have that pn ∈J(x, y) for every n. By compactness of the latter, every subsequence of (pn)
has a further subsequence that converges to some point in J(x, y). All of these sublimits have
to agree: Indeed, if pnk and pml are two subsequences of (pn) with limits p, q, then for fixed
k, for large enough l we have that pnk ≤pml, so taking l →∞gives pnk ≤q, hence taking
k →∞we get p ≤q, which yields p = q by symmetry. From this, it is easy to conclude that
in fact the sequence (pn) itself has to converge, showing (iv).
(iv) ⇒(i) : By Lemma A.3, (M, g) is causal, and by assumption the causal relation is closed,
so it suffices to show that causal diamonds J(x, y) are bounded for every x, y ∈M. Indeed,
suppose not and take pn ∈J(x, y) such that d˜g(x, pn) > n. Connect x to pn by ˜g-arclength
parametrised causal curves γn : [0, bn] →M. We may apply the limit curve theorem (see e.g.
Minguzzi [23, Thm. 3.1]) to conclude the subsequential existence of an inextendible causal
curve γ : [0, ∞) →M starting at x. Since γ is the locally ˜g-uniform limit of the γn, all of
which lie in J(x, y), by closedness of the latter also γ([0, ∞)) ⊆J(x, y). Defining zn := γ(n)
gives a sequence zn ≤zn+1 ≤y which does not converge, contradicting (iv).
It would be interesting to examine whether the closedness assumption on the causal order
can be removed from the above result, and if not, to construct an example demostrating this.

Remark A.5 (On the generality of the equivalence). The only tool we used to prove the
equivalence of chronological/causal forward/backward completeness and global hyperbolicity
in this section was the limit curve theorem for inextendible causal curves, as well as approxima-
bility by points in their timelike futures/pasts in conjunction with the openness of the timelike
relation. In particular, Theorem A.4 holds verbatim for Lorentz-Finsler spacetimes. More-
over, in the generality of Lorentzian pre-length spaces as studied by Kunzinger-S¨amann [16],
its proof yields the equivalence of compactness of causal diamonds with chronological/causal
forward/backward completeness provided the Lorentzian pre-length space is causally closed,
localisable, and its background positive definite metric is proper (cf. [16, Thm. 3.14]). It is
unclear in this generality whether causality and compactness of causal diamonds are enough
to recover global hyperbolicity in the sense of [16, Def. 2.35(v)].
