Abstract
Muon-electron universality is tested in B0 →K∗0ℓ+ℓ−decays, in the dilepton-
invariant-mass region above the ψ(2S) resonance. The analysis uses beauty mesons
produced in proton-proton collisions recorded by the LHCb detector at center-of-
mass energies of 7, 8, and 13 TeV, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
9 fb−1. The ratio of branching fractions between the muon and electron channels,
RK∗0, is measured to be 1.08 +0.14
−0.12 (stat) ± 0.07 (syst) for a dilepton-invariant-mass
squared above 14.0 GeV2/c4, consistent with the Standard Model prediction. This
result represents the most precise measurement of RK∗0 in this region and the first
such measurement performed at a hadron collider.

Lepton flavor universality (LFU) is a key aspect of the Standard Model (SM), whereby
the electroweak gauge interactions couple identically to the three charged lepton flavors, up
to effects proportional to their masses. The flavor-changing neutral-current (FCNC) quark
transition b→sℓ+ℓ−, where ℓrepresents an electron, muon or tau lepton, can be used
to precisely test LFU by comparing the rates for different lepton flavors. It occurs only
through loop diagrams in the SM, leading to typical branching fractions of O(10−7). This
suppression makes these decays sensitive probes of new physics (NP), whose contributions
to the decay amplitudes can be comparable to those of the SM. Measurements of b→sℓ+ℓ−
processes are sensitive to NP particles with masses up to O(50 TeV/c2), depending on the
model and the nature of the couplings to SM particles, well beyond the direct reach of
current colliders [1].
The universality of electroweak couplings to the three lepton families is confirmed to the
level of 0.1% by precise studies of SM properties, particularly W- and Z-boson decays [2,3].
As a consequence, ratios of b→sℓ+ℓ−decays involving different lepton flavors are expected
to differ from unity only through mass-suppressed effects, which are negligible for electrons
and muons. This makes the ratios particularly sensitive to possible differences between
transitions involving muons and electrons. As the strong interaction does not couple
to leptons, hadronic uncertainties largely cancel in these ratios. Recent measurements
of b→sµ+µ−processes show intriguing deviations from SM predictions in branching
ratios and angular distributions, ranging between 2 and 4 standard deviations (σ) [4–9].
However, interpreting these measurements remains challenging due to uncertainties in
hadronic form-factor calculations [10–12] and nonperturbative effects to charm-loop
contributions [13–15].
Several phenomenological models offer NP interpretations to
explain these deviations [16–18]. This has motivated experimentalists to investigate these
anomalous results from different perspectives, aiming to reduce theoretical uncertainties
or to test similar effects in the electron decay channel through complementary observables.
In this regard, ratios between different lepton flavors provide theoretically clean tests of
LFU.
Decays involving b→sℓ+ℓ−transitions are typically modeled as functions of the
dilepton-invariant-mass squared, q2. The dominant uncertainties on the ratio of muon
and electron branching fractions across the relevant q2 range are related to the modeling
of radiative effects and have been estimated to be ≲1% [19,20]. In contrast, NP could
introduce effects to these transitions depending on the lepton flavor, potentially at
levels exceeding 20% variation in the ratios [21–25], while remaining consistent with
other experimental constraints.
Any significant observation of LFU breaking would
therefore provide unambiguous evidence for NP. Depending on the specific nature of NP
contributions, different q2 dependence of flavor-dependent couplings could emerge, making
precise tests of LFU across the q2 phase space imperative.
Numerous LFU tests in b→sℓ+ℓ−transitions, focusing on electron-muon ratios, have
been performed in recent years [26–35]. Among these efforts, the most precise result to
date is the combined measurement of the ratios in B+ →K+ℓ+ℓ−and B0 →K∗0ℓ+ℓ−
(throughout this Letter, K∗0 indicates the K∗(892)0 meson, the K∗0 is reconstructed via
its dominant decay mode K∗0 →K+π−) decays in a q2 region below the J/ψ resonance
(q2 < 6 GeV2/c4), which validated LFU with an uncertainty of ≃4% [31]. For more than a
decade, the q2 region above the ψ(2S) resonance had been investigated only by the Belle
collaboration [28]. This region has been further explored by the LHCb collaboration with
the recent measurements of the ratios in B0
s →ϕ(1020)ℓ+ℓ−[33] and B+ →K+ℓ+ℓ−[35]

The analysis uses proton-proton (pp) collision data recorded by the LHCb detector
during the years 2011, 2012 (Run 1), and 2015–2018 (Run 2), corresponding to integrated

systematic uncertainties related to reconstruction differences between electrons and muons.

The LHCb detector [37,38] is a single-arm forward spectrometer covering the pseudo-
rapidity range 2 < η < 5, designed for the study of particles containing b or c quarks. The
online event selection is performed by a trigger [39], which consists of a hardware stage
based on information from the calorimeter and muon systems, followed by a software
stage which applies a full event reconstruction. At the hardware trigger stage, events with
large detector occupancy are rejected. For the muon mode, candidates are required to
have a muon with high transverse momentum (pT), and, for the electron mode, have an
electron with high transverse energy in the calorimeters. The software trigger requires
a two- or three-track secondary vertex with significant displacement from any primary
pp interaction vertex (PV). At least one charged particle must have significant pT and
be inconsistent with originating from a PV. A multivariate algorithm [40,41] based on
kinematic, geometric, and lepton identification criteria is used for the identification of
secondary vertices consistent with the decay of a b hadron. Triggered data further undergo
a centralized, offline processing step to deliver physics-analysis-ready data across the entire
LHCb physics program [42].
Simulated samples are used to model the effects of the detector acceptance, resolution,
and the imposed selection requirements, which are needed to evaluate the reconstruction

simulation, pp collisions are generated using Pythia [43] with an LHCb specific configura-
tion [44]. Decays of unstable particles are described by EvtGen [45], in which final-state
radiation is generated using Photos [46]. The interaction of the generated particles
with the detector, and its response, are implemented using the Geant4 toolkit [47] as
described in Ref. [48].
The signal simulation samples are generated using differential decay rates according
to a dispersion model detailed in Ref. [49], where local and nonlocal amplitudes are fitted
simultaneously from the decay B0 →K∗0 (→K+π−)µ+µ−. Separate samples are used for
each data-taking period. Better consistency with data is achieved by weighting selected
signal candidates [30] to account for known discrepancies between data and simulation in
B0 production kinematics, event occupancy, track reconstruction, particle identification
(PID), and trigger efficiencies.
Additional corrections are applied to the simulation of the electron channel [35].
They account for differences in q2 resolution between data and simulation caused by
bremsstrahlung emission. The analysis is sensitive to energy losses due to the interactions
of the electrons with the detector material. While the muon q2 resolution is essentially
negligible at the level of sensitivity of this analysis, the electron resolution must be
accurately modeled when computing the efficiency.

than 4%.
Selection requirements are applied offline to improve signal purity. Signal candidates
are reconstructed by combining two oppositely charged electrons or muons with a K∗0
candidate, whose reconstructed mass is required to be within ±100 MeV of the known K∗0
mass [36]. All final-state particles are required to be within the geometric acceptance of
the detectors providing particle-identification information (Cherenkov detectors, electro-
magnetic calorimeter, and muon detector [38]) and in the momentum range covered by the
PID calibration samples. They must also form a vertex that is significantly displaced from
the PV that fits best to the flight direction of the signal B candidate. Final-state particles
are identified using a combination of multivariate classifiers that exploit information from
all particle identification subsystems, together with additional PID selection criteria that
ensure tracks are consistently identified according to the relevant particle hypotheses.
To recover the B0 mass resolution degraded by emission of bremsstrahlung photons, the
electron momentum is corrected to include the energy of any reconstructed clusters in the
ECAL that match the position of the extrapolated electron track before the magnet and
are not associated with any other track in the event. The requirements apply to both
signal and control modes.
Backgrounds from resonant B0 →K∗0ψ(2S) (→µ+µ−) decays contribute only
marginally in the high-q2 range. On the contrary, the measured dielectron mass squared

of the resonant decay B0 →K∗0ψ(2S) (→e+e−) can erroneously leak into the selected
region if unrelated neutral clusters are incorrectly identified as bremsstrahlung photons
and their energy added to the energy of the electron tracks. While such bremsstrahlung
misidentification concerns only 1% of the candidates, the resonant decay is almost two
orders of magnitude more abundant than the rare process of interest, and would therefore
become the dominant background and significantly degrade the experimental sensitivity.
For this reason the electron q2 is always calculated with electron momenta before any
bremsstrahlung photon recovery, while the bremsstrahlung-corrected electron momenta
are employed only when reconstructing the invariant mass of B0 used for the measurement
of the ratio of interest. With these considerations, the analysis is restricted to the region
14 < q2 < 22 GeV2/c4 for the measurement of RK∗0, where the upper limit is extended
slightly above the kinematic limit of the decay to stabilize the parameterization of the
background. Within this range, the leakage from B0 →K∗0ψ(2S) (→e+e−) decays is
negligible.
To further reduce background contamination from combinations of unrelated tracks
(combinatorial background) and misreconstructed decays due to missed or misidentified
particles, two sets of optimizations are performed. Combinatorial background is suppressed
using two distinct multivariate classifiers for the muon and electron modes, which share
kinematic, vertex, and geometric features of the signal candidate as input. The classifiers
are adapted from those in Ref. [30], with the working points of both modes optimized
separately. They are trained using the simulated signal samples as signal proxy, and
candidates with both leptons of the same charge sign in data (the same-sign sample)
as background proxy. Backgrounds from specific misidentified decays are suppressed
with dedicated mass vetoes and tight PID requirements [30]. Residual misidentified
backgrounds are modeled in the fit.
The lepton-universality ratios are extracted from simultaneous unbinned maximum-
likelihood fits to the B0 invariant-mass distributions of B0 →K∗0ℓ+ℓ−candidates. The
fits incorporate the measured efficiency values according to Eq. 2 and are performed
jointly for the muon and electron modes, as well as across the high-q2 (signal) and
J/ψ (normalization) regions. The analysis strategy is first validated by measuring rJ/ψ,
the ratio of the branching fractions B0 →K∗0J/ψ (→µ+µ−) to B0 →K∗0J/ψ (→e+e−).
For this measurement, the selected range is 9.0 < q2 < 10.2 GeV2/c4 for the muon
mode and 7.6 < q2 < 9.8 GeV2/c4 for the electron mode. In addition, Rψ(2S), defined
analogously to RK∗0 using the B0 →K∗0ψ(2S) (→ℓ+ℓ−) decay, is validated in the range
12.9 < q2 < 14.0 GeV2/c4 for the muon mode and 11.4 < q2 < 14.0 GeV2/c4 for the
electron mode. The q2 region definitions here guarantee a consistent selection efficiency
between the control and signal modes [35], rather than consistency between the muon
and electron modes. In these validation studies, the B0 mass resolution of the control
modes is improved by constraining the dilepton invariant mass to that of the J/ψ or
ψ(2S) meson [36]. The fit configuration matches that used in Ref. [30], yielding measured
values of rJ/ψ = 1.035 ± 0.013 ± 0.035 and Rψ(2S) = 1.034 ± 0.033 ± 0.012, where the first
uncertainty is statistical and the second is from efficiency-related systematic.
In the fit in the high-q2 region, weights are applied to both the data and simulated
samples of the muon channel, to reduce differences in the selection efficiency of the electron
and muon modes which are q2 dependent. This reduces bias in the efficiency ratio caused
by an imperfect modeling of the form factors used to generate the simulated samples. The
weight is defined as weff(q2) ≡εee(q2)/εµµ(q2), where εee(q2) and εµµ(q2) are the efficiencies

of the electron and muon modes, respectively, obtained from efficiency-corrected signal
simulation samples [35]. The muon channel is chosen for reweighting owing to its higher
statistical precision and better q2 resolution. The weights are evaluated in q2 intervals of
0.2 GeV2/c4 separately for each data-taking period, with statistical uncertainties evaluated
with a bootstrapping method.
The B0-candidate mass distribution is modeled separately for the electron and muon
channels. For the muon channel, the signal shape is described by the sum of a modified
Crystal Ball function [50] with tails on both sides of the peak (double-sided Crystal Ball)
and a Gaussian function sharing the same peak position, with all shape parameters fixed
from simulation. For the electron channel, the model parameters depend on the number
of bremsstrahlung photons associated with the electron tracks. Each category is described
by a double-sided Crystal Ball function, with shape parameters and relative fractions fixed
from simulation. To account for minor data-simulation discrepancies, the peak position is
shifted and its width scaled by parameters allowed to vary in the fit and shared between
the control and signal modes, in order to maximize statistical power and reduce systematic
uncertainties.

In summary, the first hadron-collider measurement of the ratio of branching ratios of
B0 →K∗0e+e−(µ+µ−) decays is performed in the high-q2 region using the LHCb Run 1
and Run 2 dataset, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 9 fb−1. The result is
RK∗0 = 1.08 +0.14
−0.12 (stat) ± 0.07 (syst), which is consistent with the previous result reported
by Belle [28] and achieves a threefold improvement in precision. The present Letter
therefore provides the most precise test to date of the LFU ratio RK∗0 in the high-q2
region. A summary of the results of RK∗0 from this work, the LHCb measurement in the
lower q2 region [31] and the SM prediction is presented in Fig. 3 in the End Matter. All
existing measurements of RK∗0 are in agreement with the SM. These findings provide a
stringent test of LFU in b→sℓ+ℓ−transitions at high-q2, complementing early studies at
lower dilepton invariant masses. Full exploitation of future larger datasets [55] will be
essential to further clarify the observations and probe potential new physics effects in
FCNC processes.
