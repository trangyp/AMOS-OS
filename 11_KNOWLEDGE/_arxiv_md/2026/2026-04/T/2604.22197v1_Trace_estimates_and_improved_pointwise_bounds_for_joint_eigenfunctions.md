1.1. Quantum completely integrable (QCI) system. A semiclassical pseudodifferential
operator P1(h) is called quantum completely integrable (QCI) if there exist functionally
independent semiclassical pseudodifferential operators P2(h), · · · , Pn(h) such that
[Pi(h), Pj(h)] = 0,
i, j = 1, · · · , n.
Given real numbers E1(h), · · · , En(h), we want to study the L2 normalised functions uh
that solves Pj(h)uh = Ej(h)uh for all j = 1, · · · , n.
We call them joint eigenfunctions
corresponding to the joint eigenvalues E(h) := (E1(h), · · · , En(h)) ∈Rn of the system
ˆP := (P1(h), · · · , Pn(h)).
The principal symbols pj ∈C∞(T ∗M) of each operator Pj(h) give rise to a moment map
that defines an associated classical integrable system. The moment map is denoted by
P := (p1, · · · , pn) : T ∗M →Rn.
The regular set of P, denoted by (T ∗M)reg, consists of points (x, ξ) ∈T ∗M such that
dim span{dp1(x, ξ), · · · , dpn(x, ξ)} = n.
As a global assumption, the classical system P is Liouville integrable, i.e. the set (T ∗M)reg
is an open dense subset of T ∗M.
The above construction induces a Lagrangian torus fibration of T ∗M. If E = (E1, · · · , En)
is a regular value of P, then by the Arnold-Liouville theorem, P−1(E) consists of a finite
disjoint union of Lagrangian tori:
