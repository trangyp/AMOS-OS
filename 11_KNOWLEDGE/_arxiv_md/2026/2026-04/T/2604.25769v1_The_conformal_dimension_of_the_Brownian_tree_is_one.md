THE CONFORMAL DIMENSION OF THE BROWNIAN TREE IS ONE
JASON MILLER AND YI TIAN
Abstract. The Brownian tree, also known as the continuum random tree, is a canonical random
compact, geodesic R-tree that arises as the universal scaling limit for numerous models of discrete
random trees. A key quasisymmetric invariant of a metric space is its conformal dimension, defined
as the infimum of the Hausdorff dimensions over all quasisymmetrically equivalent spaces. This
value is always bounded below by the space’s topological dimension and above by its Hausdorff
dimension. In the present paper, we prove that the conformal dimension of the Brownian tree is 1,
matching its topological dimension.

1. Introduction
The Brownian tree (a.k.a. the continuum random tree), introduced by Aldous [Ald91a, Ald91b,
Ald93], is a canonical random compact, geodesic R-tree that arises as the universal scaling limit
for numerous models of discrete random trees, e.g., critical Galton–Watson trees, critical multi-
type Galton–Watson trees [Mie08], uniform unordered binary trees [MM11], uniform unordered
trees [HM12], uniform unlabeled unrooted trees [Stu19], critical random graphs [ABBG12], and the
minimum spanning tree [ABBGM17]. It is also the scaling limit of random dissections [CHK15],
random planar maps with a unique large face [JS15], random outerplanar maps [Car16], and random
graphs from subcritical classes [PSW16]. The Brownian tree also has connections to branching pro-
cesses, superprocesses [LG99], and random geometry [CS04, LG13, Mie13, She16, LG19, DMS21].
There are many other examples in the literature where the Brownian tree arises that we have not
listed here because they are far too numerous.

The conformal dimension, introduced by Pansu [Pan89], is a quasisymmetric invariant that plays an
important role in the classification of metric spaces up to quasisymmetric equivalence. It is defined
as the infimal Hausdorff dimension among all spaces within a given quasisymmetric equivalence
class. By definition, this dimension must lie between the topological dimension of the space and
its original Hausdorff dimension.
The problem of computing or estimating conformal dimension has been studied for many gen-
eral metric spaces and concrete deterministic fractal spaces [Tys00, BT01, KL04, Kov06, TW06,
Mac11, CP14, Kig14, KOR18, Kwa20]. Since many naturally occurring fractals arise from ran-
dom processes, it is very natural and interesting to study their conformal dimension. In [RS21],
it was shown that the conformal dimension of fractal percolation is almost surely strictly smaller
than its Hausdorff dimension, although explicit values for the conformal dimension have not yet
been determined. The conformal dimension of the graph of a one-dimensional Brownian motion
is almost surely equal to its Hausdorff dimension 3/2 [BHL25]. The authors proved in a previous
paper [MT26] that the conformal dimension of the Brownian sphere is almost surely equal to its
topological dimension 2.
The main result of the present paper is that the conformal dimension of the Brownian tree matches
its topological dimension.
Theorem 1.1. Almost surely, the Brownian tree has conformal dimension 1.
A metric space whose conformal dimension is equal to its Hausdorff dimension is referred to as
minimal.
In contrast to [BHL25], to the best of our knowledge, the Brownian sphere [MT26]
and the Brownian tree studied in the present paper are the first random fractals whose conformal
dimensions are shown to be equal to their topological dimensions.
The quasisymmetric uniformization problem asks for natural conditions under which a given metric
space is quasisymmetrically equivalent to a model space. For results regarding standard model
spaces, see [TV80] for S1; [BK02] alongside [Raj17, LW20, NR23, MW25] for S2; and [Bon11]
for round carpets. Several recent papers have explored these types of questions in the context of
trees. For instance, [BT21] introduced the continuum self-similar tree (CSST) as a deterministic
model space for a compact metric R-tree, possessing the property that the Brownian tree is almost
surely homeomorphic (but not quasisymmetrically equivalent) to it.
Moreover, [BM22] proved
that a quasiconformal tree (a compact metric R-tree that is doubling and of bounded turning) is
quasisymmetrically equivalent to the CSST if and only if it is trivalent and uniformly branching.
Furthermore, [BM20] proved that any quasiconformal tree is quasisymmetrically equivalent to a
geodesic R-tree, and [Kin17] proved that its conformal dimension is 1.
R-trees also play an important role in the study of Gromov hyperbolic groups. For example, by
using the theory of group actions on R-trees and Rips’ machine [BF95], it was proven in [Bow98]
that if the boundary of a one-ended hyperbolic group contains a local cut point, the group splits
over a two-ended subgroup.
We remark that the Brownian tree almost surely cannot be embedded quasisymmetrically into Rn,
or any doubling space [Tro21]. In particular, the Ahlfors regular conformal dimension and the
conformal Assouad dimension (variants of the conformal dimension) of the Brownian tree are almost
surely infinite.
As in [MT26], the arguments in the present paper are motivated by [CP13], where the authors
constructed metrics quasisymmetrically equivalent to a prescribed one using the technique of hy-
perbolic fillings and admissible weight functions. Roughly speaking, an admissible weight function
assigns a non-negative real number to each metric ball in the original metric space. This number
corresponds to the “relative size” of the ball compared to its “parent” ball under the new metric
