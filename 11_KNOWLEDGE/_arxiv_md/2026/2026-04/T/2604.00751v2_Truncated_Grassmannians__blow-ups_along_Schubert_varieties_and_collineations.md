TRUNCATED GRASSMANNIANS, BLOW-UPS ALONG
SCHUBERT VARIETIES AND COLLINEATIONS
EVGENY FEIGIN
Abstract. Truncated Grassmannians are defined as closures of orbits
of abelian unipotent groups acting on the degree truncations of projec-
tivized wedge powers. We show that such truncations in a more general
setup show up in the description of the blow-ups of general flag varieties
along Schubert subvarieties. We work out the case of Grassmannians
in detail. In particular, we show that our blow-ups are members of a
larger family of varieties projecting onto Grassmannians, and describe
the fibers of these projections via the spaces of collineations.
Introduction
Let g be a simple Lie algebra over the filed of complex numbers and let
G be the corresponding simple simply connected Lie group. For a standard
parabolic subgroup P, let G/P be a generalized flag variety. We denote by
Sσ the Schubert variety in G/P attached to the Weyl group element σ. In
this paper we describe the blow-ups along Sσ for certain Schubert varieties
in the complex Grassmannians via truncated Grassmannians.
Not much
is known about the blow-ups of flag varieties along Schubert subvarieties
in general, although some partial results (mostly in the smooth case) are
available [BS25, HKLS25, KP21, LSi21]. We formulate a general approach,
based on the truncated flag varieties, and work out the construction in detail
for the Grassmannians. We provide more details below.
Let V be a vector space of dimension n and let us fix d < n; to simplify the
notation, we assume that d ≤n −d. We fix a decomposition V = Vd ⊕Vn−d
fixing two subspaces of V of dimensions d and n −d. Let Grd(V ) be the
Grassmannian of d-dimensional subspaces in V , which can be realized as the
quotient SLn/Pd, where Pd is the maximal parabolic subgroup correspond-
ing to the d-th simple root; in particular, Pd preserves Vd. Let pd be the
Lie algebra of Pd and let ad be the opposite abelian unipotent radical, i.e.
ad ⊕pd = sl(V ). The group exp(ad) acts on the Grassmannian and Grd(V )
is the closure of the orbit exp(ad)Vd.
The Grassmannian Grd(V ) admits the Pl¨ucker embedding Grd(V ) ⊂
P(ΛdV ) [Fu97].

vectors form a basis of Vd and the last n −d form a basis of Vn−d. The
wedge power ΛdV admits the standard basis of the form vI consisting of
wedge monomials, where I is a cardinality d subset of the set {1, . . . , n}.

Before passing to a more general situation, let us mention two similar
constructions found in the literature. First, in [Fa26, FW25, FZ21] the au-
thors consider the blow-ups of Grassmannians along zero schemes of Pl¨ucker
coordinates of fixed degree. They define the generalized Kausz compactifi-
cations [Kaus00] and describe the link to the spaces of collineations. Second,
in [FO24, FGNP26, Sv25] the authors study truncated Grassmannians for
certain allowed sets of degrees. Our setup is different, since we want to keep
the exp(ad) (and P −

Finally, let us describe the general case.
Let G be a simple simply-
connected Lie group with the flag variety G/B (the case of arbitrary G/P
is similar). Let λ be a regular dominant integral weight and let L(λ) be the
corresponding irreducible highest weight g module with the highest weight

We fix the projective embedding G/B ⊂P(L(λ)) with the
image being the closure of the B−orbit through the highest weight line
[v(λ)].
Let O(λ) be the line bundle on G/B obtained as a pull back of
O(1) on P(L(λ)).
For a Weyl group element σ let v(σλ) ∈L(λ) be the
corresponding extremal weight vector. The (opposite) Demazure module
D(σλ) ⊂L(λ) is the U(b−) span of v(σλ). The (opposite) Schubert variety
Sσ sits inside P(D(σλ)) as the closure of the B−orbit of the line [v(σλ)].
We define the truncated b−module Lσ(λ) as the quotient L(λ)/D(σλ)
and denote the image of v(λ) as vσ(λ); note that the truncated module
Lσ(λ) is cyclic with cyclic vector vσ(λ). We also define the truncated flag
variety Xσ(λ) ⊂P(Lσ(λ)) as the closure of B−[vσ(λ)].
Theorem C. The blow-up of G/B along the Schubert variety Sσ admits a
closed embedding into G/B × Xσ(λ) as the closure of the B−orbit through
the product [v(λ)] × [vσ(λ)].

5. The general case
In this section we describe the general picture for the blow-ups of flag vari-

Schubert varieties inside partial flags. In particular, in the previous sections
we considered the case of Grassmannians.
