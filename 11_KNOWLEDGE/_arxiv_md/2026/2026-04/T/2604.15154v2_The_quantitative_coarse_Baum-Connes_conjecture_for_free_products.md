The quantitative coarse Baum–Connes conjecture for free products
Jintao Deng∗
Ryo Toyota†
Abstract
Let G and H be finitely generated groups. In this paper, we prove the quantitative coarse
Baum–Connes conjecture for the free product G ∗H under the assumption that the conjecture
holds for both G and H.
1
Introduction
Higher index theory is a generalization of the Atiyah–Singer index theorem to noncompact settings via
K-theory of C∗-algebras. One central problem is the coarse Baum–Connes conjecture, which provides
a framework for computing higher indices of elliptic operators on non-compact manifolds. The coarse
Baum–Connes conjecture asserts that a certain coarse assembly map
µ : KX∗(X) →K∗(C∗(X))
is an isomorphism for a metric space X with bounded geometry. This conjecture has many significant
applications in analysis, geometry, and topology, including the Novikov conjecture on the homotopy
invariance of higher signatures and the Gromov–Lawson conjecture on positive scalar curvature. It
has been verified for a large class of metric spaces for example, spaces with finite asymptotic dimen-
sion [Yu98] and spaces admitting coarse embedding into Hilbert space [Yu00].
In this paper, we study the higher index theory for the free product of a pair of groups. Let G
and H be a pair of finitely generated groups and G ∗H their free product. Several coarse geometric
properties are known to be stable under free products. Bell and Dranishnikov [BD01] showed that
the asymptotic dimension of G ∗H is controlled by that of G and H. Chen, Dadarlat, Guentner, and
Yu [CDGY03] proved that G∗H has property (A) (resp. admits a coarse embedding into Hilbert space)
if and only if G and H do. Also, Fukaya and Matsuka [FM23] proved that coarse convexity is preserved
by free products. All these properties are important sufficient conditions for the coarse Baum–Connes
conjecture. Regarding Baum–Connes conjecture with coefficients, Oyono-Oyono [OO01] showed that
for a group G acting on a tree, it satisfies Baum–Connes conjecture with coefficients if and only if
all stabilizers of the vertices do. These results naturally lead to the following question regarding the
coarse Baum–Connes conjecture:
Question 1.1. Let G and H be finitely generated groups. If G and H satisfy the coarse Baum–Connes
conjecture, does G ∗H also satisfy it?
In this paper, we study the quantitative coarse Baum–Connes conjecture for free product. The
following result is our main result.

The quantitative method has played an important role in Baum–Connes type conjectures. In [Yu98],
Yu introduced quantitative K-theory (also known as controlled K-theory). Roughly speaking, quanti-
tative K-theory of Roe algebras Kε,r
0 (C∗(X)) (respectively Kε,r
1 (C∗(X))) is defined as the homotopy
classes of quasi-projections (respectively quasi-unitaries) in matrices over C∗(X) with propagation less
than a fixed parameter r (see Definition 3.3 and Definition 3.5). This theory approximates ordinary
K-theory and admits a controlled Mayer–Vietoris sequence, which reduces the problem to simpler
geometric situations.
Oyono-Oyono and Yu [OOY15, OOY19] systematically developed quantitative K-theory. Guent-
ner, Willett, and Yu [GWY24] introduced dynamical complexity and applied these techniques to the
Baum–Connes conjecture for dynamical systems. Since these approaches establish that assembly maps
are isomorphisms via quantitative assembly maps, it is natural to formulate quantitative versions of
the assembly map (cf. [OOY15, Section 6], [Zha24], and [OOY24]).
As an application of Theorem 1.2, we obtain new examples of groups satisfying the coarse Baum–
Connes conjecture. Arzhantseva and Tessera [AT19] constructed a group G which is an extension
of groups coarsely embeddable into Hilbert space, but which itself does not admit a coarse embed-
ding. It is known that such an extension satisfies the quantitative coarse Baum–Connes conjecture
(Remark 3.17). By our main theorem, the free product G ∗G′ of any two such groups also satisfies
the quantitative coarse Baum–Connes conjecture, providing new examples of finitely generated groups
satisfying the coarse Baum–Connes conjecture, especially in cases where G or G′ does not coarsely
embed into Hilbert space.

plexity) introduced in [GTY12]. Let X and Y be families of metric spaces and let r > 0. We say that

We exploit the geometric decomposition of the Bass–Serre tree T associated with the free product
G ∗H. However, since T typically has infinite valence, a subset of G ∗H corresponding to a bounded
subset of T consists of infinitely many copies of G and H glued together in a highly intricate way.
Another difficulty arises from the fact that the coarse Baum–Connes conjecture does not, in general,
pass to subspaces. In [OOY24, Theorem 2.13], it is shown that the quantitative coarse Baum–Connes
conjecture for a metric family can be recovered from a geometric decomposition provided that all
subspaces of the pieces satisfy the conjecture uniformly. However, our main theorem does not assume
that subspaces of G or H satisfy the quantitative coarse Baum–Connes conjecture. To overcome this
issue, we construct a decomposition of G ∗H that avoids such permanence properties. The detailed
analysis in Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2, which is specific to free products, plays a key role in the proof.

The Roe algebras and localization algebras
In this section, we shall briefly recall the basic concepts of the coarse Baum–Connes conjecture.
Let Z be a proper metric space. Recall that a metric space is said to be proper if every bounded
subset is precompact.
Let H be an infinite-dimensional, separable Hilbert space. We choose a countable dense subset
Z0 ⊂Z. Now consider the Hilbert space ℓ2(Z0, H). Every element T ∈B(ℓ2(Z0, H)) can be expressed
as an (Z0 × Z0)-matrix T = (Tx,y)x,y∈Z0.
Definition 2.1. Let T = (Tx,y)x,y∈Z0 ∈B(ℓ2(Z0, H)).
(1) The propagation of T is defined as
propagation(T) := sup{d(x, y) : Tx,y ̸= 0}.

(2) T is said to be locally compact, if for every compact subset B ⊂Z, the operators T ·χB and χB ·T

is invariant under coarse equivalences (c.f. [WY20, Theorem 5.1.15]).
