PETER CHOLAK, NATASHA DOBRINEN, AND CHARLES MCCOY
Abstract. By recent work of Dobrinen [6] and Balko et al. [2] we know that every
finite 𝐺in the Henson graph H𝑛+1 (the universal ultrahomogeneous (𝑛+ 1)-clique
free graph) has exact finite big Ramsey degree 𝑘(𝐺, 𝑛). That is, there is a positive
integer 𝑘(𝐺, 𝑛) such that for each finite coloring 𝐶of the copies of 𝐺in H𝑛+1, there
is ˜H, a substructure of H𝑛+1 and isomorphic to H𝑛+1, such that in ˜H at most 𝑘(𝐺, 𝑛)
colors are used on the copies of 𝐺in ˜H. Moreover, for exactness, for some coloring
and all corresponding ˜H, all 𝑘(𝐺, 𝑛) colors are needed. The ultimate result here is
that if |𝐺| ≥2, then there is a finite computable coloring 𝐶such that, for all such ˜H,
we have that ˜H computes ∅(|𝐺|−1) (and hence the halting set).
1. Introduction
Definition 1. Given a countable infinite structure B and a finite substructure A of
B. Let ℛA,B(𝐶, ˜H) be the statement: “𝐶is a finite coloring of all copies of A in B, ˜H
is a substructure of B, isomorphic to B, and all copies of B within ˜H have the same
fixed color." We say A has the Ramsey Property in B iff, for all 𝐶, there is an ˜H such
that ℛA,B(𝐶, ˜H).
Here we will use various versions of Henson graphs for B. Recall, for each
𝑛≥2, the (𝑛+ 1)-st Henson graph, H𝑛+1, is the universal, ultrahomogeneous,
(𝑛+1)-clique free, graph (for details, see Section 4). It is also the Fraïssé limit of the
class of all finite (𝑛+ 1)-clique free graphs. It is well-known that nodes have the
Ramsey Property in any Henson graph; this is due to work of Komjath and Rödl for
𝑛= 2 [13], and El-Zahar and Sauer for all 𝑛≥3 [8] (see Theorem 13). Throughout
the paper ˜H will always be a copy of some H𝑛+1 and all colorings considered are
finite colorings.
The theorem that nodes have the Ramsey Property in H𝑛+1 is often stated as that
H𝑛+1 is indivisible. If we divide the nodes of H𝑛+1 into finitely many pieces, then one
of the pieces must contain a copy of H𝑛+1. Indivisibility is very important to our
work; it provides the needed local structure of H𝑛+1 and is discussed in Section 5.
A proof of indivisibility for H𝑛+1 appears in Section 6. This proof is done in the
style of a forcing construction and hence yields computability theoretic corollaries
and in addition might provide more clarity than other published proofs.
