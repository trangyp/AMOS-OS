TWO REMARKS ON TRANSCENDENTAL SHIFT-LIKE MAPS ON CN
RAMANPREET KAUR
Abstract. In [4], the dynamics of a particular polynomial diffeomorphism of CN, called
a polynomial shift-like map of type ν, has been studied as a higher dimensional analog
of H´enon maps. In this note, we prove that the Julia set of their transcendental coun-
terpart is non-empty. In addition, an example of a transcendental shift-like map with an
escaping wandering domain has been provided which, in fact, showcases a contrast with
the dynamics of a polynomial shift map.
1. Introduction
Bedford and Pambuccian, in their paper titled,“Dynamics of shift-like polynomial dif-
feomorphisms on CN”, initiated the study of the dynamics of a polynomial shift map of
type ν on CN, where N ≥2 [4]. In the spirit of H´enon maps, they constructed filtration
for a polynomial shift-like map of type ν which are indeed their higher dimensional analog.
Loosely speaking, for R > 0 (large enough) they constructed the sets V +
R (a forward in-
variant set in which the orbit of every point tends to infinity in forward time), V −
R (a set in
which the forward orbit of any point can remain in it for a finite positive time), and VR (a
compact set in which forward orbit of every point is bounded). In this article, we study the
dynamics of their transcendental counterpart, that is, a transcendental shift-like map of
type ν on CN, where 1 ≤ν ≤N −1. In particular, we prove that its Julia set is non-empty.
This indicates that it is worth studying the dynamics of a transcendental shift-like map.
To study the dynamics of a transcendental diffeomorphism in higher dimensions, one may
pursue the following three approaches to understand its dynamics or to develop techniques
to study its dynamics.
(1) Extending techniques from the transcendental dynamics for a map in several vari-
ables. Specifically, to extend the techniques from the dynamics of a transcendental
H´enon map.
(2) Attempt to investigate from the perspective of a polynomial shift-like map in the
setting of a transcendental shift-like map.
(3) Extend the known results for a transcendental entire function in one complex vari-
able to their higher dimensional analogs.
As mentioned earlier, our primary focus is to show that the dynamics of a transcendental
shift-like map is not linear, that is, its Julia set is non-empty. To achieve this, we shall

To obtain that the Julia set of a transcendental shift-like map is non-empty, it is sufficient
to show that its topological entropy is infinite. For this, we shall use the method which
Markus Wendt used in [8] to show that any transcendental entire function f has an infinite
topological entropy. In fact, he divided the proof into two cases: when the sequence {fn},
given by fn(z) = f(nz)
n , is quasi-normal and when it is not. In quasi-normal case, it was
obtained that each fn is a polynomial-like map of arbitrarily large degree, and in non-
quasinormal case, by using Ahlfors Five Islands Theorem, Wendt worked with arbitrary
large number of disks with pairwise disjoint closures such that each of these disks contained
a univalent preimage of all but atmost 2 disks.
It is to be mentioned that a similar
approach was later used by Arosio et al. in order to achieve that the topological entropy
of a transcendental H´enon map is infinite [3].
2. Preliminaries
A diffeomorphism F : CN →CN, with N ≥2 is said to be shift-like if the orbit of a
point z ∈CN under F determines a bi-infinite sequence {ζj}j∈Z such that
F k(z) = (ζk+1, . . . , ζk+N) ∈CN.
Now, given a shift-like map F, it is said to have type ν for some 1 ≤ν ≤N −1, if F has
the following form. For every (z1, z2 . . . , zN) ∈CN,
F(z1, z2, . . . , zN) = (z2, z3, . . . , zN, f(zN−ν+1) −az1),
where a ∈C∗and f is a holomorphic function. If f is a polynomial of degree d, then F is
said to be a polynomial shift-like map of type ν (see [4]). We say that F is a transcendental
shift-like map of type ν if f is a transcendental entire function.
