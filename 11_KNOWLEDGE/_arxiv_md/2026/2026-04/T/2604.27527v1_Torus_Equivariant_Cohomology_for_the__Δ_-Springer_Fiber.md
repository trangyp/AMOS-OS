Abstract. We define a torus U ⊂T = (C×)K which acts on the ∆-Springer varieties Yn,λ,s defined
by Griffin-Levinson-Woo [10] and give a Borel-style presentation for the equivariant cohomology
ring H∗
U(Yn,λ,s). Our presentation arises from the orbit harmonics deformation technique, and uses
methods of Chou-Matsumura-Rhoades [5] and Abe-Horiguchi [1].
1. Introduction
A central theme in Schubert calculus is to describe cohomology rings of naturally occurring
varieties by explicit generators and relations [1, 2, 5, 12]. Borel’s presentation of the cohomology ring
of the complete flag variety Fln(Cn) as the coinvariant algebra Rn is the foundational example [3],
and analogous presentations for Springer fibers and their generalizations have played a central role
in geometric representation theory and algebraic combinatorics. Equivariant cohomology enhances
these rings by accounting for the action of a torus, and equivariant Borel-style presentations often
reveal structure that is invisible in ordinary cohomology.
The varieties studied in this paper are the ∆-Springer fibers Yn,λ,s introduced by Griffin-Levinson-
Woo [10]. These are subvarieties of partial flag varieties depending on a partition λ ⊢k ≤n and an
integer s ≥ℓ(λ). Their ordinary cohomology is described in [10] in terms of the ∆-Springer modules
Rn,λ,s introduced by Griffin [9], which simultaneously generalize the Garsia-Procesi modules [6] and
the generalized coinvariant rings Rn,k of Haglund-Rhoades-Shimozono [11]. Thus the geometry of
Yn,λ,s lies at the intersection of Springer theory and the algebraic combinatorics surrounding Delta-
type coinvariant constructions.
In the classical Springer fiber setting, Kumar-Procesi [13] gave a coordinate ring presentation
of the torus-equivariant cohomology of the Springer fiber. Abe-Horiguchi [1] upgraded this to a
Borel presentation with explicit generators and relations by extending the argument of Tanisaki [16]
for ordinary cohomology. The generalized coinvariant rings Rn,k were realized as the cohomology
ring of the moduli space of spanning line configurations defined by Pawlowski-Rhoades [14], whose
equivariant cohomology was computed by Chou-Matsumura-Rhoades [5] as a Borel-style presenta-
tion. The varieties Xn,k are smooth but not compact; Griffin-Levinson-Woo [10] give an alternate
geometric model as a special case of the varieties Yn,λ,s, which are compact yet singular.
The goal of this paper is to establish the analogous Borel presentation for the torus-equivariant
cohomology for the full ∆-Springer family. To do so, we define a natural subtorus
U ⊂T = (C×)K
which preserves Yn,λ,s, and we compute the equivariant cohomology ring H∗
U(Yn,λ,s) explicitly.
Our presentation takes the form of a quotient of the polynomial ring Q[xn, us]. The defining
ideal is generated by two families of relations. The first family,

equivariant Schubert classes on Grassmannians. Writing Jn,λ,s for the ideal generated by these two

2.6. Commutative algebra and orbit harmonics. We now recall the orbit harmonics philos-
ophy in the form needed for this paper. Let X ⊂Qn be a finite set, and let
I(X) := {f ∈Q[xn] : f(x) = 0 for all x ∈X}
be its vanishing ideal. Since X is finite, evaluation identifies the quotient Q[xn]/I(X) with the ring
of Q-valued functions on X. In particular,
dimQ Q[xn]/I(X) = |X|.
The key observation is that one may pass from the generally inhomogeneous ideal I(X) to a
homogeneous ideal without changing the vector space dimension of the quotient. For a nonzero
polynomial
f = fd + fd−1 + · · · + f0
with each fi homogeneous of degree i and fd ̸= 0, define the top-degree part of f by τ(f) := fd.
We then set
T(X) := ⟨τ(f) : f ∈I(X), f ̸= 0⟩.
Equivalently, T(X) = grI(X) is the associated graded ideal of I(X) with respect to the total-degree
filtration. The quotient Q[xn]/T(X) is therefore graded, and one has
dimQ Q[xn]/T(X) = dimQ Q[xn]/I(X) = |X|.
When the point locus X is stable under the action of a finite group G acting linearly on Qn, both
I(X) and T(X) are G-stable. It follows that the graded quotient Q[xn]/T(X) inherits a graded
G-module structure. The passage
X ⇝Q[xn]/T(X)
is often referred to as orbit harmonics. One may also think of taking gr of the ideal I(X) as a flat
deformation of the point locus X to a nonreduced scheme supported at the origin.
In many examples, the ideal T(X) admits an explicit description by generators, and this turns
a concrete point set into a graded quotient ring with representation-theoretic or geometric signif-
icance. In our setting, Griffin showed that the ordinary ∆-Springer ring Rn,λ,s may be obtained
from a finite point locus Xn,λ,s(αs) ⊂Qn by the rule
Rn,λ,s ∼= Q[xn]/grI(Xn,λ,s(αs)).
This point-locus description should be viewed as the nonequivariant shadow of the constructions
used later in this paper.

5. Some Remarks on Localization
We record here a localization statement for the U-action on Yn,λ,s. Since Yn,λ,s admits a U-stable
affine paving, its equivariant cohomology is a free H∗
U(pt)-module. Therefore the Chang-Skjelbred
sequence [4] gives an injection
H∗

U(Y U
n,λ,s).
Thus the ring H∗

locus.
At present, however, we do not have a satisfactory combinatorial description of the image of this
restriction map. Unlike the classical GKM setting, the U-fixed locus is not expected to be finite:
the torus U acts with repeated characters along the rows of Λ, so localization does not reduce to
a finite direct sum of copies of Q[us]. A more precise description would require understanding the
geometry of the fixed components Y U
n,λ,s together with the closures of the one-dimensional U-orbits.
We leave such a non-isolated GKM description for future work.
