TSCHIRNHAUSEN BUNDLES OF SEXTIC COVERS OF P1
SAM FRENGLEY AND SAMEERA VEMULAPALLI
Abstract. A degree d genus g cover of the complex projective line by a smooth irreducible curve
C yields a vector bundle on the projective line by pushforward of the structure sheaf. We classify
the bundles that arise this way when d = 6. Interestingly, our methods show that all constraints on
the pushforward are “explained” by multiplication in an algebra. Finally, we show that all possible
pushforwards are realized by covers with a nontrivial proper subcover.
Figure 1. A cartoon drawing of the geography of degree 6 covers of P1. Given a
sextic cover π: C →P1, its scrollar invariants (e1, . . . , e5) yield an integer point of
this region. We prove that every integer point of this region arises as the scrollar
invariants of a sextic curve. The covers in the pink region P3 \ P2 necessarily factor
through cubic subcovers. The covers in the blue and green regions Q necessarily
factor through quadratic subcovers.
The covers which do not factor through a
nontrivial proper subcover are contained in the purple region P2 ∩P3.

1. Introduction
Let C be a smooth irreducible projective curve of genus g over C equipped with a finite morphism
π: C →P1 of degree d. The restriction of functions gives an inclusion OP1 →π∗OC. Define the
Tschirnhausen bundle of π to be Eπ := (π∗OC/OP1)∨.
By the Birkhoff-Grothendieck theorem,
vector bundles on P1 split completely, so we may write (uniquely up to non-unique isomorphism)
Eπ ∼= OP1(e1) ⊕· · · ⊕OP1(ed−1)
with e1 ≤e2 ≤· · · ≤ed−1. Because h0(P1, π∗OC) = h0(C, OC) = 1, we have e1 ≥1. The integers
⃗eπ = (e1, . . . , ed−1) are called the scrollar invariants of π, and encode a good deal of information
about the curve C. For the rest of this article, assume 1 ≤e1 ≤· · · ≤ed−1.
Question 1.1 (Tschirnhausen realization problem). Which (d −1)-tuples ⃗e ∈Zd−1 arise as the
scrollar invariants of a smooth irreducible degree d cover π: C →P1?
For 2 ≤d ≤5, the answer to the Tschirnhausen realization problem is known completely (com-
bining [Mar46], [KO93], [VV24, Theorem 3.1], and [FV25, Theorem 1.4]) using the parametrizations
of low degree covers [CE96, Cas96]. Moreover, when d is prime there is a conjectural answer to the
Tschirnhausen realization problem [VV24, Conjecture 1.3]; no such conjecture exists for composite
d > 5. In Theorem 1.3 we give a complete answer to the Tschirnhausen realization problem in the
case d = 6.

Theorem 1.3. A quintuple ⃗e ∈Z5 arises as the scrollar invariants of a smooth irreducible sextic
cover if and only if ⃗e ∈Q ∪P3.
1.1. Constraints on scrollar invariants. The scrollar invariants of a degree d cover π: C →P1
must satisfy constraints arising from multiplication. More precisely, π∗OC is an OP1-algebra and
this forces ⃗eπ to satisfy some linear inequalities [Vem22, Section 1.3] (e.g., see Example 2.1 for a
proof that e2 ≤2e1 when d = 3). One significant feature is that the linear inequalities imposed on

⃗eπ depend on the degrees of the subcovers of π. In the case d ≤5, the necessary constraints arising
from multiplication in [Vem22, Section 1.3] are known to be sufficient [Mar46, KO93, VV24, FV25].
Moreover, when π is primitive (that is, if π does not factor through a nontrivial proper subcover)
it is known from [Vem22, Section 1.3] that ei+j ≤ei + ej for all i and j. A conjecture of the second
author [VV24, Conjecture 1.3] asserts that this is a sufficient condition, i.e., every ⃗e satisfying
ei+j ≤ei +ej arises as the scrollar invariants of a smooth primitive cover. This conjecture is known
for each d ≤5 (combining [Mar46, VV24, FV25]).
In the imprimitive case, the constraints in [Vem22, Section 1.3] are significantly more complicated.
Regardless, they are worked out explicitly for all d < 18 [Vem22, Theorem 1.26]. One might wonder
if these are sufficient conditions, but this turns out to be false (as we show in the case d = 6).
There is a good reason for this insufficiency: if π: C →P1 factors through a subcover as τ ◦γ, then
γ∗OC is an algebra over the intermediate cover and this forces the slopes of γ∗OC to satisfy some
linear inequalities. This in turn imposes constraints on ⃗eπ.
Indeed, in the case d = 6, if π is a sextic cover factoring as τ ◦γ through a degree 2 cover
τ : X →P1, then ⃗eπ satisfies additional linear inequalities coming from the multiplicative structure
of γ∗OC as a cubic OX-algebra. This constraint is truly new in the sense that it is not implied
by those in [Vem22, Section 1.3].
We prove via explicit construction that this new constraint,
combined with the constraints of [Vem22, Section 1.3], are necessary and sufficient conditions for a
quintuple ⃗e to arise as the scrollar invariants of a smooth sextic cover.
1.2. Main ideas of the proof of Theorem 1.3. If π: C →P1 is a degree 6 cover [Vem22,

constraints of [Vem22, Section 1.3] but which do not arise as the scrollar invariants of a sextic curve.

1.3. Future directions. We now pose several open questions for interested reader which may be
tractable via the methods of this paper.
1.3.1. Tschirnhausen bundles of primitive degree 6 covers. A conjecture of the second author [VV24,
Conjecture 1.3] asserts that every ⃗e satisfying ei+j ≤ei + ej arises as the scrollar invariants of a

smooth primitive cover. The case d = 6 is the smallest degree in which this conjecture is not
known. In [VV24, Theorem 1.4] it is shown that if ⃗eπ is the scrollar invariants of a primitive cover,
then ei+j ≤ei + ej for all i and j. Thus the difficulty of proving [VV24, Conjecture 1.3] lies in

1.3.2. Maroni loci of larger than expected dimension. Let Hd,g be the moduli stack of degree d
covers of P1 by genus g smooth irreducible curves. Define the Maroni locus
M(⃗e) = {π : C →P1 : ⃗eπ = ⃗e} ⊆Hd,g.
An application of Serre duality shows that P ei = d + g −1, so henceforth assume this. If the
Tschirnhausen bundle of a cover behaves like a “random” vector bundle on P1 of rank d −1 and
degree d + g −1, one might expect that:
(1) M(⃗e) is nonempty precisely when h1(P1, End(O(⃗e))) ≤dim(Hd,g); and
(2) when M(⃗e) is nonempty, we have codim M(⃗e) = h1(P1, End(O(⃗e))).
Unfortunately, both of these guesses are false in general. Regarding (1), the algebra structure on
π∗OC sometimes forces M(⃗e) to be empty when h1(P1, End(O(⃗e))) ≤dim(Hd,g) (for example take
d = 3 and ⃗e = (1, 3)). Regarding (2), in the cases d = 4, 5 it is known that the dimension of M(⃗e)
can be larger than the expected dimension when the elements of ⃗e are spread out; see [VV24, FV25].
Let Mfac(⃗e) ⊆M(⃗e) be the locus of covers factoring through a nontrivial proper subcover.
In Section 3 and Section 4, we explicitly construct elements of Mfac(⃗e) when possible. We do
not compute dim Mfac(⃗e), but in many cases such a computation should be possible. Now, the
inequality
dim M(⃗e) ≥dim Mfac(⃗e)
yields a lower bound on the dimension of M(⃗e). In cases where the elements of ⃗e are spread out,
we expect that
dim Mfac(⃗e) ≥dim(H6,g) −h1(P1, End(O(⃗e))).
Combining these two inequalities, one may be able to show that when d = 6 many Maroni loci have
dimension larger than the expected dimension.
1.3.3. Making a conjecture for the Tschirnhausen realization problem for all d. It is natural to
wonder if, for general degree d, all constraints on the scrollar invariants ⃗eπ “come from” multipli-
cation over some (possibly trivial) subcover of π. If this is true then it may be possible to combine
the ideas of Section 2 with [Vem22, Section 1.3] to generalize [VV24, Conjecture 1.3] to give a
conjectural answer to the Tschirnhausen realization problem for composite d > 6. This would be a
substantial step forward towards the Tschirnhausen realization problem.
1.3.4. Solving the Tschirnhausen realization problem in degrees 8 and 9. Degrees 8 and 9 provide
excellent test cases for examining when all constraints on the Tschirnhausen bundle “come from”
multiplication. Using the ideas of Section 2 and [Vem22], one can extract a number of constraints

on the Tschirnhausen bundles of degree 8 and 9 covers explicitly. If these conditions are sufficient
for the existence of a cover, the natural question is now to construct all possibilities.
We now present two mechanisms by which one might construct degree 8 covers with specified
scrollar invariants. Let τ : X →P1 be a hyperelliptic curve. First, one may specify a quartic cover
of X by specifying a double cover γ : C →X along with a line bundle L on C and a squarefree
section s ∈L⊗−2. A theorem of Wood [Woo14, Theorem 1.4] describes1 the moduli space of pairs
(γ : C →X, L) where γ is a double cover, L is a line bundle on C, and the pushforwards γ∗OC,
γ∗L and γ∗(L⊗−2) are specified. The moduli space is described as a quotient of an open locus of
sections of a vector bundle on X by a group. Upon fixing the pushforwards, one needs to check the
nonemptiness of this open locus. Alternatively, one could try to use the parametrization of quartic
algebras [CE96] over a hyperelliptic curve.
Remark 1.4. A related application of Wood’s parametrization to covers of P1 is given in [LV25],
which uses Wood’s parametrization to study the Brill–Noether loci of covers in Hirzebruch surfaces.
In degree 9, one may construct examples as triple covers of triple covers. Let τ : X →P1 be
a triple cover of P1 and let V be a rank 2 vector bundle on X. It is well-known that the moduli
stack of triple covers γ : C →X with (γ∗OC/OX)∨≃V is given by the quotient of an open locus
of Sym3 V ⊗det(V )∨by Aut(V ). One may construct examples where V is a direct sum of line
bundles; see [Lar21] for a description of line bundles on trigonal curves and their pushforwards to
P1.
1.4. History. There has been a great deal of work on the Tschirnhausen realization problem. We
highlight here two ways (out of many) of constructing curves with prescribed scrollar invariants. In
[VV24, Theorem 1.5] a construction is given for a cover with specified scrollar invariants whenever ⃗e
is concave; the proof involves desingularizing curves in Hirzebruch surfaces and keeping track of how
the scrollar invariants change. In [DP22, Theorem 1.1] it is shown that for any ⃗e = (e1, . . . , ed−1),
there exists an integer k ∈Z≥0 such that (e1 + k, . . . , ed−1 + k) is realized as the scrollar invariants
of some cover; the proof involves deforming curves in projective bundles. For further constructions
of curves with specified scrollar invariants, see [KO93, Cop99, Bal02, Cop21, CVZ23, Red26]. For
constraints on scrollar invariants of curves in general degree, see [Ohb97, Vem22, VV24].
1.5. Conventions. We work over the complex numbers, but all results in this paper hold over an
algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. All curves are assumed to be smooth, projective,
and irreducible unless otherwise specified. Throughout we drop the subscript and write O = OP1
for the structure sheaf of P1. If X is a curve, then a degree d cover of X means a finite morphism
C →X of degree d, where C is smooth and irreducible.

2. Constraints on the Tschirnhausen bundle
We begin with a warm-up showing how multiplication imposes constraints on the Tschirnhausen
bundle of a trigonal cover.
Example 2.1. Suppose that π: C →P1 has degree d = 3. We claim that e2 ≤2e1. Let
m: π∗OC ⊗π∗OC →π∗OC
denote the multiplication map. Fix a splitting π∗OC = O ⊕O(−e1) ⊕O(−e2) and write A =
O⊕O(−e1). If m(A⊗A) ⊆A then A is a quadratic subalgebra of π∗OC, and therefore the trigonal
cover π: C →P1 factors through the degree 2 morphism SpecP1(A) →P1, which is a contradiction.
Therefore, the multiplication map m induces some nonzero morphism O(−e1)⊗O(−e1) →O(−e2)
(composing m with the projection onto O(−e2)) and thus e2 ≤2e1.
In the remainder of this section, we will prove the following theorem (which is the “only if”
direction of Theorem 1.3).
Theorem 2.2. Let π : C →P1 be a sextic cover with scrollar invariants ⃗eπ = (e1, . . . , e5). Then
⃗eπ ∈Q ∪P3.
The bounds in [Vem22, Theorem 1.26] imply that ⃗eπ ∈P2 ∪P3. Therefore to prove Theorem 2.2
it is sufficient to prove the following proposition.
Proposition 2.3. If ⃗eπ ∈P2 \ P3, then ⃗eπ ∈Q.
We first give a brief outline of the proof of Proposition 2.3 before giving the proof in Section 2.1.
Let π: C →P1 be a sextic cover with ⃗eπ ∈P2 \ P3. Lemma 2.4 shows that π factors as τ ◦γ
through a degree 2 cover τ : X →P1; consider the vector bundle γ∗OC. This vector bundle splits
as OX ⊕V for some rank 2 vector bundle V on X. The vector bundle V is either semistable or
unstable.
If V is semistable, Lemma 2.5 bounds the difference of summands of τ∗V . In Lemma 2.7 we give
an explicit combinatorial construction of an admissible partition to conclude that ⃗eπ ∈Q.
If V is unstable then its Harder–Narasimhan filtration has the form 0 ⊆L ⊆V for a line bundle
L on X. In this case, the exact sequence of vector bundles 0 →L →V →V/L →0 pushes forward
to an exact sequence of vector bundles
(2.1)
0 →τ∗L →τ∗V →τ∗(V/L) →0.
It is possible that sequence (2.1) does not split.
Nevertheless, using properties of the Harder–
Narasimhan filtration and the OX-algebra structure of γ∗OC, we directly prove several linear
inequalities on the summands of τ∗L and τ∗(V/L).
Now, explicit manipulation of these linear
inequalities (Lemma 2.8) shows that ⃗eπ ∈Q.

Theorem 3.2 (Maroni [Mar46]). Let 1 ≤e1 ≤e2 be integers. The pair (e1, e2) arises as the
scrollar invariants of a cubic cover if and only if e2 ≤2e1.

(x1, y1) ⊗(x2, y2) 7→(x1x2 + sy1y2, x1y2 + x2y1).
