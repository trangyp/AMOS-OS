THE SIMILARITY OF IRREDUCIBLE OPERATORS IN FACTORS
MINGHUI MA, RUI SHI, AND SHANSHAN YANG
Abstract. An operator T in a separable factor M is said to be irreducible in M if
the von Neumann subalgebra W ∗(T) generated by T is an irreducible subfactor of
M, i.e., W ∗(T)′∩M = CI. We say that T is a single generator of M if W ∗(T) = M.
In this paper, we study generators of separable factors related to maximal abelian
self-adjoint subalgebras. As an application, we obtain a complete characterization
of normal operators in separable factors which are similar to irreducible operators.
1. Introduction
Let H be a separable complex Hilbert space and B(H) the algebra of all bounded
linear operators on H. An operator T in B(H) is said to be irreducible if there is no
nontrivial projection commuting with T. Otherwise, T is called reducible. In 1968,
P. Halmos [14] introduced the concept of irreducibility of operators and proved that
the set of irreducible operators is a norm-dense Gδ subset of B(H). The irreducibility
of operators is clearly a unitary invariant, and it is natural to seek necessary and
sufficient conditions for an operator to be similar to an irreducible operator. Along
this line, F. Gilfeather [11, Theorem 2] proved that, on a separable Hilbert space, every
normal operator with empty point spectrum is similar to an irreducible operator.
Subsequent to Gilfeather’s result, C. Fong and C. Jiang [9, Main theorem] gave a
complete characterization of normal operators in B(H) that are similar to irreducible
operators. The similarity of irreducible operators in matrix algebras was completely
characterized by C. Hsin [15]. The reader is referred to [16, Section 2.2] for more
results on the similarity of irreducible operators. Noting that B(H) is a type I factor,
and some related operator-theoretic results [1, 2, 4–6, 12, 13, 20, 23, 24] have been
established in factors recently, it is natural to investigate the similarity of irreducible
operators in factors. As the proof of [9, Main theorem] makes essential use of specific
features of B(H), obtaining an analog in general von Neumann algebras requires the
development of new methods.

Recall that an operator T in a separable factor M is said to be irreducible if
the von Neumann algebra W ∗(T) generated by T is an irreducible subfactor of M,
i.e., W ∗(T)′ ∩M = CI. Otherwise, T is called reducible in M. By definition, every
scalar in C is irreducible. Moreover, it is straightforward to prove that an operator
in M2(C) is similar to an irreducible operator if and only if it is not a scalar multiple
of the identity. We present the main result of this paper as follows, where by R(T)
we denote the range projection of T.
Theorem 1.2. Let M be a separable factor with dim M > 4. Then a normal operator
N in M is similar to an irreducible operator if and only if the following two conditions
are satisfied:
(1) R(λI −N) ≿I −R(λI −N) for every scalar λ in C;
(2) the set {I, N, N 2} is linearly independent over C.
In 1954, N. Dunford [3] introduced the concept of spectral operators on a Banach
space and proved that every spectral operator on a Hilbert space is similar to the sum
of a normal operator and a commuting quasinilpotent operator. By replacing B(H)
with a factor M, K. Dykema and A. Krishnaswamy-Usha [4, Section 3] showed that
if T is a spectral operator in M, then there exists an invertible operator X in M

operators. In addition, we prove in Proposition 4.7 that if the relative commutant
{T}′ ∩M is abelian, then T is similar to an irreducible operator in M. We conclude
the introduction with the following question.
Question 1.3. In a separable factor, what are the necessary and sufficient conditions
for an operator to be similar to an irreducible operator? What are the necessary and
sufficient conditions for an operator to be similar to a generator?
