THE INTEGRAL CHOW RING OF M0(Pr, 2)
RENZO CAVALIERI AND DAMIANO FULGHESU
Abstract. We compute a presentation for the integral Chow rings of the moduli stacks of degree 2 maps from
smooth rational curves to projective space Pr, as a quotient of a three-variable polynomial ring. The relations as r
varies have rich combinatorial structure: all non-trivial relations are encoded by two generating functions which are
rational functions.
1. Introduction
This article is the second step in the authors’ program to compute the Chow rings with integral coefficients of the
stacks M0(Pr, d). We refer to the introduction of [CF23] for history, context and relevance of the broader problem.
Here we focus on the work presented in this manuscript, and explain why it is a natural self-contained step in the
program. We begin by stating the result.

The broad strategy for computing the presentation parallels [CF23]: present M0(Pr, 2) as a global quotient
stack by a special group, thus identifying the Chow ring of the quotient with the group equivariant cohomology
of the space being quotiented [EG98a, Tot99]; show M0(Pr, 2) is an open substack of some manageable ambient
space, which provides a set of generators; construct an equivariant envelope for the complement and compute
the pushforward of the Chow rings of the components of the envelope to obtain the relations. The localization
computation on the equivariant envelope produces the presentation in the closed form version of Theorem 1. We
verify the equivalence of the two forms of Theorem 1 in Section 5.
The stacks M0(Pr, d) admit a natural presentation as global quotients by PGL2: informally, considering the source
of a map to be a projective line with homogeneous coordinates (s : t), a point of M0(Pr, d) can be represented
by (r + 1) homogeneous polynomials in s, t of degree d. All other representatives of the given moduli point are
obtained as the orbit of the PGL2 action reparameterizing the source curve, thus exhibiting M0(Pr, d) as the global
quotient of an open subset U of projective space (where the homogeneous coordinates are the coefficients of the
(r + 1) polynomials) by PGL2.
For degree d odd, a GL2 action on the affine cone (minus the zero section) over U lifts the PGL2 action and
presents M0(Pr, d) as a global quotient stack by the special group GL2 ([CF23, Prop. 2.4]).
When d is even such a lift does not exist. We make use of a general construction from [DL21] to exhibit M0(Pr, d)
as a global quotient stack by the special group GL3. Informally, for a moduli point [f : C →Pr] ∈M0(Pr, d), we
view the source curve not as a parameterized P1, but as a smooth conic in P2. The map is then determined by
(r + 1) sections of the restriction to the conic of the line bundle OP2(1), with all other representatives obtained as
the orbit of a GL3 action lifting the standard linear action on P2.
We give a slicker, albeit less intuitive, description of this construction: denoting by S ⊂P(H0(OP2(2))) ∼= P5 the
locus of smooth conics, the map S →[Spec(k)/PGL2] is a GL3 torsor. The pull-back Y of [U/PGL2] ∼= M0(Pr, d) →
[Spec(k)/PGL2] via this morphism is therefore also a GL3 torsor, implying [Y/GL3] ∼= M0(Pr, d).
We thus need to compute the GL3 equivariant Chow ring of Y . One can observe that Y is an open subset
of a projective bundle over S, and we can therefore compute a presentation for its Chow ring by constructing an
equivariant envelope for the complement of Y . For this task the case d = 2 stands out with respect to all other
even degrees for its simplicity, which led us to write its treatment as a standalone paper. In this case, there are two
components of the envelope: one is a projective bundle over the universal conic, which is itself a projective bundle
over P2. The second is isomorphic to a product of projective spaces. The localization computation, which can in
both cases be performed relative to a morphism to P2, is then extremely simple and elegant.
We conclude with a brief discussion of our observations and expectations for the remaining cases: when d ≥4,
the components of an equivariant envelope should be describable in terms of the geometry of either a subvariety of
the absolute Hilbert scheme of points in Hilbn(P2), or the relative Hilbert scheme of the universal smooth conic
over its base Hilbn
U/S. Atiyah-Bott localization is not immediately available in these cases, either because the
space is non-proper or too singular, but we are exploring an approach through virtual intersection theory which we
believe might allow for applying the localization theorem again; if this approach succeeds, then the combinatorics
of localization and of trying to treat uniformly all even cases should make the computations an interesting challenge
for future work.
The paper is organized as follows: in Section 2.1 we provide some background on GL3 counterparts ([DL21]) to
make this work more self-contained and set notation. In Section 3 we apply this construction to give a presentation
of the stack M0(Pr, 2) as a global GL3 quotient stack. In Section 4 we set up a GL3 equivariant envelope and
compute the pushforward of the Chow rings of its components. In Section 5 we show how these computations give
rise to the efficient generating function form of Theorem 1. We conclude by showing some explicit computations in
low dimensions.
