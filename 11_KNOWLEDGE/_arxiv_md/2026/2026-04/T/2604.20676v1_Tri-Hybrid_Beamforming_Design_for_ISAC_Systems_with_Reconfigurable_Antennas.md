Tri-Hybrid Beamforming Design for ISAC Systems
with Reconﬁgurable Antennas
Jiangong Chen, Graduate Student Member, IEEE, Xia Lei, Yuchen Zhang, Member, IEEE, Kaitao Meng,
Member, IEEE, and Christos Masouros, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) sys-
tems require efﬁcient beamforming architectures to jointly
support communication and sensing functionalities. To reduce
hardware overhead, Hybrid Beamforming (HBF) has been widely
studied and shown to achieve performance close to fully digital
beamforming under practical hardware constraints. As a promis-
ing evolution, Reconﬁgurable Antenna (RA) technologies have
recently emerged to further enhance beamforming Degrees of
Freedom (DoFs) by dynamically reconﬁguring antenna Electro-
magnetic (EM) characteristics, yet their integration into ISAC
systems remains largely unexplored. In this paper, we investigate
an RA-assisted ISAC system and develop a decoupled Triple-
Hybrid Beamforming (Tri-HBF) framework that alternatively
optimizes digital, analog, and EM beamformers to maximize
the communication rate and sensing Signal-to-Clutter-plus-Noise
Ratio (SCNR). For both Single-user Single-target (SUST) and
Multiple-user Multiple-target (MUMT) scenarios, we ﬁrst trans-
form the original fractional objectives into fraction-free ones via
methods tailored to their respective structures. The resulting
problems are then solved via alternating optimization over
different variable blocks. Closed-form updates are derived for
all variables except the EM beamforming subproblem in the
MUMT scenario. To further reduce complexity introduced by
Semideﬁnite Relaxation (SDR) in EM beamforming, we propose
a low-complexity iterative approach across antennas with closed-
form updates. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed
scheme signiﬁcantly outperforms benchmark designs with con-
ventional omnidirectional and directional antennas, achieving
almost 100% improvement in spectrum efﬁciency and 62.5%
reduction in antenna overhead, thereby unveiling the potential
of RA in enhancing ISAC performance towards 6G.
Index Terms—Integrated sensing and communication, recon-
ﬁgurable antenna, tri-hybrid beamforming.
I. INTRODUCTION
As one of the six usage scenarios of IMT-2030 [1], In-
tegrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) has become
a cornerstone technology for the forthcoming 6G networks,
offering signiﬁcant advantages in efﬁciently combining com-
munication and sensing functionalities [2], [3]. By integrating
both capabilities, ISAC systems make more efﬁcient use of
hardware and spectrum resources, signiﬁcantly enhancing the
sustainability of wireless networks.

A key enabler of ISAC is Multiple-input Multiple-output
(MIMO) technology, which offers abundant Degree of Free-
doms (DoFs) for both communication and sensing. On the
communication side, MIMO techniques such as beamforming,
space-time coding, and Multi-user (MU) precoding provide
high spectral efﬁciency, spatial diversity, and interference sup-
pression. On the sensing side, MIMO processing also enables
high-resolution Direction-of-Arrival (DoA) estimation, active
sensing, and robust target detection. Motivated by these advan-
tages, a large body of literature has investigated beamforming
design for ISAC systems. Representative formulations directly
optimize communication and sensing Signal-to-Interference-
plus-Noise Ratio (SINR)/Signal-to-Clutter-plus-Noise Ratio
(SCNR) [4], [5], [6], adopt transmit beampattern matching
[7], [8], [9], exploit estimation-theoretic criteria such as the
Cram´er–Rao Bound (CRB) [10], or employ sensing mutual
information as an information-theoretic metric [11], [12], [13].
These studies have established the fundamental algorithmic
framework of ISAC beamforming, but most of them are mainly
developed under conventional fully digital array architectures.
As wireless systems continue to migrate toward mmWave
and higher frequency bands, large-scale arrays become in-
creasingly attractive for ISAC, since narrow beams and large
apertures are beneﬁcial to both communication and sensing.
However, fully digital beamforming requires one dedicated
Radio Frequency (RF) chain per antenna, leading to prohibitive
hardware cost and power consumption in such regimes. To
address this issue, Hybrid Beamforming (HBF) has been
widely recognized as a practical solution [14]. Existing ISAC
works have investigated fully-connected HBF architectures
[15], sub-connected architectures [16], and improved sub-
connected implementations with enhanced phase-shifter ﬂex-
ibility [17]. These studies show that HBF can approach the
performance of fully digital beamforming under favorable
hardware conﬁgurations. Nevertheless, its achievable DoFs
are still fundamentally limited by ﬁxed antenna positions
and ﬁxed radiation characteristics, which becomes particularly
restrictive in ISAC systems requiring simultaneous support for
MU communication and multi-target sensing.
To further enlarge the design space, recent ISAC research
has moved beyond conventional HBF toward antenna-domain
reconﬁgurability. Among the emerging architectures, ﬂuid
[18], movable [19], and pinching [20] antennas belong to the
category of position-reconﬁgurable antennas, while rotatable
antennas [21] provide orientation reconﬁgurability. Building
on these hardware concepts, recent works have started to in-
corporate such reconﬁgurable antennas into ISAC systems. For
ﬂuid antennas, early studies considered FA-assisted ISAC and
showed that jointly optimizing antenna positions and transmit
beamforming can improve both communication and sensing

performance [22]. This line has quickly evolved toward more
explicit trade-off-oriented designs, where ﬂuid antennas are
shown to enlarge the achievable communication–sensing re-
gion compared with conventional ﬁxed-position arrays [23].
For movable antennas, recent ISAC designs have investigated
bistatic multi-user systems in which the antenna coefﬁcients
and antenna positions are jointly optimized to enhance ﬂexible
beamforming performance [24]. For pinching antennas, very
recent works have introduced PASS-enabled ISAC frame-
works, where pinching beamforming is exploited to establish
reliable communication and sensing links, and the correspond-
ing communication–sensing rate region has also been char-
acterized [25], [26]. However, these position-reconﬁgurable
architectures mainly enhance performance through geometry
adaptation in the spatial domain, rather than electromagnetic
control at the element level.
Another emerging direction is Electromagnetic (EM)-
domain reconﬁgurability for ISAC. In our earlier confer-
ence work [27], a Reconﬁgurable Antenna (RA)-aided Triple-
Hybrid Beamforming (Tri-HBF) framework was introduced,
where digital, analog, and EM beamforming are jointly de-
signed to enlarge the achievable communication-and-sensing
trade-off region. More recently, compound reconﬁgurable an-
tenna arrays were investigated for ISAC clutter suppression
[28], where radiation pattern and polarization states are jointly
exploited in the EM domain to enhance sensing performance.
Reconﬁgurable pixel antennas have also been incorporated
into ISAC systems, where binary-controlled pixel states pro-
vide additional EM-domain ﬂexibility for joint communica-
tion and sensing optimization [29]. In parallel, polarization-
reconﬁgurable antennas have recently been introduced into
polarimetric ISAC, where polarization agility is exploited to
improve fairness while reducing RF-chain cost [30]. These
works collectively indicate that recent progress in ISAC is
increasingly driven by new antenna architectures that introduce
additional EM-domain Degrees of Freedom (DoFs). Never-
theless, the existing literature still has several limitations.
Some works focus on highly specialized hardware freedoms,
such as pixel-state or polarization reconﬁguration, instead of
more general pattern-domain beamforming. Some studies are
developed under simpliﬁed sensing settings, such as single-
target scenarios. In addition, certain works emphasize speciﬁc
functionalities, such as clutter suppression or fairness, rather
than pursuing a more general communication-and-sensing
trade-off design. These limitations motivate a more systematic
RA-enabled ISAC framework that can accommodate different
levels of EM-domain reconﬁgurability under richer sensing
environments.
Motivated by the above observations, and following the RA
modeling frameworks established in communication-oriented
studies in [31], [32], [33], we consider two complementary
RA models for ISAC systems, where Tri-HBF is exploited
for both communication and sensing components in terms
of SINR enhancement, improved directional energy focusing,
and interference mitigation between users and sensing targets.
Speciﬁcally, the main contributions of this work can be sum-
marized as follows
• We present one of the early systematic investigations of
RA-enabled ISAC with Tri-HBF. Speciﬁcally, we extend
two representative EM-domain RA modelings, as in [31],
[32], to a monostatic ISAC system. The ﬁrst one adopts a
continuous spherical-harmonic-based radiation represen-
tation for arbitrary pattern generation, which establishes
an ideal upper bound on EM-domain beamforming ﬂex-
ibility. The second one utilizes radiation patterns data
from realistic measurements in [34], yielding a physically
realizable discrete pattern selection design. Furthermore,
we adopt SCNR and sum-rate as the objective for sensing
and communication in subsequent optimization process.
• For the Single-user Single-target (SUST) scenario, we
formulate a Fractional Programming (FP) optimization
problem and apply Dinkelbach’s transform to convert it
into a convex form. A closed-form solution is derived for
the fully digital beamforming case, and the corresponding
HBF solutions are obtained via Manifold Optimization
(MO). For the EM-domain beamforming of RA, we ﬁrst
perform joint optimization across all antenna elements
using Semideﬁnite Relaxation (SDR), and rigorously
prove the tightness of the SDR solution. To further
reduce computational complexity, we propose an antenna-
wise Alternating Optimization (AO) strategy and derive
corresponding closed-form updates for the arbitrary pat-
tern generation model. For the discrete pattern selection
model, we traverse all candidate radiation patterns per
antenna to obtain the optimal pattern conﬁguration.
• Without altering the fundamental optimization frame-
work, we extend the proposed design to Multiple-user
Multiple-target (MUMT) scenarios. Since the objective
function becomes a sum-of-ratios form and is no longer
directly amenable to Dinkelbach’s method, we adopt
a Lagrangian dual transform combined with a modi-
ﬁed quadratic transform to decouple communication and
sensing fractional components. These transforms yield
solvable ﬁrst-order optimality conditions for each user’s
beamformer, enabling closed-form fully digital solutions
and hybrid approximations similar to the SUST case. The
EM beamforming for RA can be handled in a similar
manner. For the arbitrary pattern generation model, either
SDR-based joint optimization or low-complexity alter-
nating closed-form updates can be employed, while for
discrete pattern selection model, only alternating traversal
over the ﬁnite radiation-pattern set is considered.
Our simulation results demonstrate that, compared to tra-
ditional HBF schemes with non-reconﬁgurable antennas, the
proposed RA-based Tri-HBF approaches, including both arbi-
trary pattern generation and discrete pattern selection models,
achieve signiﬁcantly better objective function values in both
SUST and MUMT scenarios. From the communication sum
rate and sensing SCNR trade-off curves, it can be seen that
the proposed method strikes a better trade-off relationship
between communication and sensing, substantially enhancing
the ﬂexibility of ISAC systems. Furthermore, the provided
transmit patterns clearly illustrate that, both at the single-
antenna radiation pattern level and the array beampattern level,
the RA-based schemes designed in this paper are more focused

To more clearly illustrate the advantage of RA, the single-
antenna radiation patterns and the array transmit beampatterns
of different schemes are shown in Fig. 6. The transmit power
is set to -30 dBm, and the weighting factor ˜β is set to
5 × 10−3. From the single-antenna perspective, both OA and
CosA exhibit ﬁxed radiation coverage. The OA radiates energy
uniformly in all directions, whereas the CosA concentrates its
radiation around θ = 90o. In contrast, after Tri-HBF opti-
mization, the RA is capable of concentrating radiated energy
toward the intended CUs and sensing targets. Furthermore,
owing to the more ﬂexible spherical harmonics representation
adopted in Model I, it achieves stronger directivity than
Model II, which manifested by reduced power leakage toward
other regions and a higher main-lobe peak gain. Intuitively,
better single-antenna directivity leads to improved array-level
transmit performance, which is validated by the transmit beam-
patterns shown in Fig. 6. Speciﬁcally, CosA outperforms OA
in suppressing energy at scatterer locations. Additionally, the
array beampatterns corresponding to the two RA models show
less energy leakage, with Model I demonstrating particularly
reduced sidelobe levels, thus enhancing power concentration
in the main beam.
From a hardware reduction perspective, the objective values
versus the number of RF chains and antennas are shown in
Figs. 7 and 8, where the transmit power is set to 0 dBm and
the communication–sensing trade-off factor is ﬁxed at 0.5. In
general, all schemes exhibit improved performance with an
increasing number of RF chains and antennas, with the RA-
based Tri-HBF consistently outperforming the conventional
OA and CosA HBF schemes. Notably, as shown in Fig. 7,
the RA AO Brute-force Model II scheme, despite its rela-
tively lower performance, outperforms the conventional fully
digital beamforming schemes (OA and CosA). Furthermore,
compared to CosA HBF scheme, RA AO Brute-force Model II
scheme achieves a reduction of about one RF chain. Thanks to
the arbitrary pattern generation ability of RA Model I, both RA
Joint SDR Model I and RA AO Closed-form Model I achieve
signiﬁcantly better performance gains. With only two RF
chains, they outperform the OA HBF and CosA HBF schemes
by a considerable margin, showcasing the future opportunities
for advanced RA design and development. As illustrated
in Fig. 8, for the same number of RF chains, a limited
number of RAs can replicate the performance of large antenna
arrays in conventional OA and CosA schemes. Speciﬁcally,
Model II can save around 10 antennas compared to the CosA
scheme, while Model I results in a saving of approximately
30 antennas. This clearly highlights the advantages of RA in
environments with limited space for large antenna array.
