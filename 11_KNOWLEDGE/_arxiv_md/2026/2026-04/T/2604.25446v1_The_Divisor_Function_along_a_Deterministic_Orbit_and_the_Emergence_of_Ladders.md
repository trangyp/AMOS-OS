Abstract
We study the deterministic recursion nj+1 = nj −τ(nj), where τ(n)
is the divisor function, and the associated orbit length a(x). Heuristics
based on the average order of τ(n) suggest that a(x) ≍x/ log x, but
the strong dependence along the orbit makes this difficult to analyze.
We develop a deterministic framework based on energy identities
and dyadic decomposition, reducing the problem to the study of the
distribution of τ(n) along the orbit. Our main conceptual contribu-
tion is to reduce this endogenous multiplicative problem to a single
structural obstruction, captured by a phase-rigidity mechanism for the
orbit. Within this framework we prove several unconditional structural
results: large values of τ(n) contribute negligibly on dyadic scales, any
energy-concentrating obstruction must occur at a single divisor scale,
and small variance of τ(nj) along the orbit forces the emergence of a
long near-arithmetic progression on which τ(n) is essentially constant.
The only potential obstruction is the formation of energy-saturating
divisor ladders. This is addressed via two explicit hypotheses: a phase-
rigidity principle for the endogenous orbit, and an anti-concentration
statement ruling out such configurations. Under these hypotheses we
obtain
a(x) ≍
x
log x.
Keywords: Divisor function; deterministic recursion; multiplicative func-
tions; short intervals; orbit dynamics; energy concentration; inverse theorem;
near-arithmetic progressions.

Introduction
The study of arithmetic functions along deterministic sequences lies at the
interface of analytic number theory, probability, and dynamical systems.
Classical results provide detailed information on the average behavior of
multiplicative functions such as the divisor function
τ(n),
but these results rely on exogenous sampling, for instance averaging over
intervals or arithmetic progressions. In contrast, far less is understood when
the sampling is generated endogenously by the function itself.
In this paper we study such a setting for the divisor function. Starting
from an initial value x, consider the deterministic orbit
n0 = x,
nj+1 = nj −τ(nj),
and define the orbit length
a(x) := min{k ≥0 : nk ≤0}.
The sequence a(x) appears in the OEIS as A155043 [6].
A heuristic based on the average order
X
n≤x
τ(n) = x log x + O(x)
suggests that typical step sizes are of order log n, leading to the prediction
a(x) ≍
x
log x.
However, this heuristic relies on independence assumptions that fail in the
present setting, where the sequence (nj) is highly dependent.
Endogenous sampling and the main difficulty
The orbit (nj) constitutes an endogenous sample of the divisor function. In
particular:
• the values τ(nj) determine future sampling locations,
• standard results for multiplicative functions in short intervals or arith-
metic progressions [4, 5] do not apply directly,
• independence heuristics break down due to the recursive structure.
This places the problem outside the scope of existing techniques.

Relation to existing results
Classical results on the distribution of τ(n), as well as recent work on mul-
tiplicative functions in short intervals and arithmetic progressions [4, 3],
suggest that such strong concentration should not occur under exogenous
sampling.
However, these results rely on averaging over independent or weakly cor-
related samples and do not apply to the present setting, where the sampling
is generated endogenously and exhibits strong dependence.
Toward resolving the conjecture
A natural approach is to establish anti-concentration estimates of the form

for all sufficiently long progressions with T ≍log N.
Even a weak bound of this type would suffice to rule out such ladders
and hence imply the main conjecture.

Beyond the first-order asymptotic, it is natural to ask whether a finer de-
scription of the orbit length holds.
