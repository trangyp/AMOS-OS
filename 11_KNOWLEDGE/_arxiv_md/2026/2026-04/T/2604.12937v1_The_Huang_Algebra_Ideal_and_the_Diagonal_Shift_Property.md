THE HUANG ALGEBRA IDEAL AND THE DIAGONAL SHIFT
PROPERTY
DARLAYNE ADDABBO
Abstract. Let V be a grading-restricted vertex algebra and let A∞(V ) = U ∞(V )/Q∞(V )
be the associative algebra constructed by Huang, where U ∞(V ) is the space of column-finite
infinite matrices with entries in V and Q∞(V ) is an ideal of a (nonassociative) algebra structure
on U ∞(V ) defined by Huang. Huang introduced families of elements in Q∞(V ) and conjectured
that these elements generate Q∞(V ). We discover and prove that Huang’s elements all satisfy
what we call “the diagonal shift property”.
On the other hand, in the case that V is the
rank one Heisenberg vertex operator algebra, we construct infinitely many linearly independent
elements in Q∞(V ) that do not satisfy the diagonal shift property. As a corollary, we disprove
Huang’s conjecture.
1. Introduction
Given a vertex operator algebra (VOA) V Zhu defined an associative algebra A(V ) such
that there is a one-to-one correspondence between irreducible admissible V -modules and A(V )-
modules [Z].
Frenkel and Zhu proved that in the case that V is rational, the category of
admissible V -modules and the category of A(V )-modules are equivalent [FZ]. These algebras
were crucial in Zhu’s proof of the modular invariance of correlation functions for elements in
rational C2-cofinite VOAs.
While Zhu algebras encode the essential information about modules of rational VOAs, if one
wants to study irrational VOAs, Zhu algebras are in general insufficient. For this reason, Dong,
Li, and Mason introduced “higher level Zhu algebras”. Given a VOA V one has an infinite
family of associative algebras An(V ), n ∈N. The level zero Zhu algebra A0(V ) is Zhu’s original
algebra A(V ). Dong, Li, and Mason proved several results about the relationship between V -
modules and An(V )-modules, and this relationship was further studied in [BVY1]. Miyamoto
introduced pseudo-traces for C2-cofinite irrational VOAs and proved that the space of these
pseudo-traces is invariant under the action of SL2(Z) [Miy].
In [H], Huang proved the modular invariance of intertwining operators for rational VOAs sat-
isfying the same conditions as those in [Z]. He subsequently conjectured the modular invariance
of logarithmic intertwining operators for C2-cofinite, CFT-type VOAs.
In [H4], given a grading-restricted vertex algebra, Huang introduced an associative algebra
A∞(V ) and proved that the set of equivalence classes of lower-bounded generalized V -modules
are in bijection with the set of equivalence classes of nondegenerate graded A∞(V )-modules. He
also defined a family of subalgebras An(V ), n ∈N, of A∞(V ) and proved that there is a bijection
between the set of equivalence classes of irreducible lower-bounded generalized V -modules and
the set of equivalence classes of irreducible nondegenerate graded An(V )-modules. Given n ∈N,
the higher level Zhu algebra An(V ) is a subalgebra of An(V ), and hence A∞(V ) contains all
of the higher level Zhu algebras as subalgebras. Huang further studied the algebras A∞(V )
and An(V ) n ∈N in [H3], and in [H5] he used them to prove his conjecture on the modular
invariance of logarithmic intertwining operators, a result which subsumed the earlier modular
invariance results of Huang, Miyamoto, and Zhu, and filled some gaps in [Miy].
Given a VOA V and n ∈N the higher level Zhu algebra An(V ) is a quotient of V by an ideal
denoted On(V ). One has an explicit description of On(V ) in terms of VOA elements [DLM],
which we review below in Section 2.2. This explicit description is useful in describing higher

Given a VOA V , n ∈N, and an admissible V -module M, Dong, Li, and Mason [DLM] defined
Ωn(M) := {w ∈M|vkw = 0 if wt v −k −1 < −n for v ∈V homogeneous weight}.
The subspace Ωn(M) of M is an An(V ) module and the action of An(V ) is given by zero modes:
Any element v + On(V ) in An(V ) acts on Ωn(M) by the zero mode o(v) of v. The ideal On(V )
consists precisely of every element in V whose zero mode acts as 0 on Ωn(M) for all admissible
V -modules M [DLM].
In contrast to the higher level Zhu algebras which encode information about the action of
VOAs on their admissible modules by zero modes, Huang algebras encode the action by all
modes. Given a grading-restricted vertex algebra, the Huang algebra is a quotient not of V
but of the space U∞(V ) of column-finite infinite matrices, doubly indexed by N, with entries
in V . The associative algebra A∞(V ) is then defined to be U∞(V )/Q∞(V ), where Q∞(V ) is
a certain ideal that is defined abstractly: Given a lower-bounded generalized V -module M,
Huang constructs an N-graded space Gr(M) = ⊕n∈N Grn(M) and defines an action of U∞(V )
on this space. Then Q∞(V ) consists of all elements of U ∞(V ) that act as zero on Gr(M) for
all lower-bounded generalized V -modules M [H4].
To date, there is no explicit description of the ideal Q∞(V ) in terms of vertex algebra elements.
In [H4], given a grading-restricted vertex algebra V , Huang defined a subspace O∞(V ) of U∞(V )
analogous to the subspaces On(V ) of V and proved that O∞(V ) is a subspace of Q∞(V ). He also
defined elements of U∞(V ) arising from the Jacobi identity and proved that these elements are
in Q∞(V ) [H3]. He conjectured in [H4] that O∞(V ) and these Jacobi identity elements generate
all of Q∞(V ). In this paper, we introduce a property called the “diagonal shift property” and
prove that elements of O∞(V ) and the Jacobi identity elements constructed by Huang satisfy
this property. In the case that V is the rank one Heisenberg VOA we prove that there exist
elements of Q∞(V ) that do not satisfy the diagonal shift property, and as a corollary, we find
that Huang’s conjecture is not in general true.
We briefly summarize the structure of this paper. In Section 2 we review the definitions of
grading-restricted vertex algebras and their lower-bounded generalized V -modules. We then
review higher level Zhu algebras and some facts about them which will be used in the paper.
Following this, we provide background on Huang algebras. In Section 3 we define the diagonal
shift property and prove that for a grading-restricted vertex algebra, elements of O∞(V ) and the
Jacobi identity elements constructed by Huang satisfy the diagonal shift property. In Section
4 we review the construction of the rank one Heisenberg VOA M(1) and construct an infinite
family of linearly independent elements in Q∞(M(1)) that do not satisfy the diagonal shift
property. All vector spaces in this paper are assumed to be complex, N denotes the nonnegative
integers, and Z+ denotes the strictly positive integers.
