Trapping Regions for Quadratic Systems with
Generalized Lossless Nonlinearities
Diganta Bhattacharjee, Shih-Chi Liao, Peter J. Seiler, and Maziar S. Hemati
Abstract— A trapping region is a compact set that is
forward invariant with respect to the dynamics. Existence
of a trapping region certifies boundedness of trajectories,
and the size of the set provides an estimate of the ultimate
bound. Prior work on trapping region analysis has focused
on quadratic systems with energy-preserving (lossless) non-
linearities. In this work, we focus on a generalization of the
lossless property and present an efficient parameterization
that enables optimal trapping region computation for a
broader class of quadratic systems than afforded by exist-
ing methods. We also formulate conditions for ellipsoidal
trapping regions, whereas spherical regions have been
the focus of prior works. Three numerical examples are
used to demonstrate the proposed framework: (1) a four
dimensional system for which the prior state-of-the art is
incapable of identifying a trapping region; (2) a low-order
unsteady aerodynamics model for which the proposed
approach yields trapping regions approximately an order
of magnitude smaller than prevailing methods; and (3) a
two-state academic example in which the proposed ap-
proach correctly identifies a globally asymptotically stable
equilibrium point.
I. INTRODUCTION
Trapping region analysis, developed in the context of
reduced-order modeling for incompressible fluid flows
[1], characterizes a compact, invariant set of a quadratic
system. Under appropriate conditions, all trajectories
starting outside the trapping region are guaranteed to
eventually converge to it, thereby establishing bound-
edness of the system responses [1]–[4] and providing
an estimate of the ultimate bound [5] in the process.
The analysis also utilizes physics-based constraints on
energy conservation of the quadratic nonlinearity, termed
the ‘lossless property’ [1]–[4], [6], [7]. Thus, a trapping
region analysis can be used to embed constraints into
a model synthesis framework such that the resulting
models admit trapping regions [6], [8], [9]. Prior results
in the literature establish global boundedness guarantees
under constraints due to the aforementioned lossless
property. However, those results hinge upon a ‘standard’
form of the lossless constraint that limits applicability

Furthermore, the standard form restricts prior results to
spherical trapping regions, which can be conservative.
A convex optimization-based architecture has recently
been put forward in Liao et al. [2] for refining spherical
trapping regions. Despite the refinements and optimal-
ity guarantees originating from convexity, conservatism
might be inevitable with a spherical shape for certain
applications [2], [6]; these observations showcase the
need for an alternative characterization of the invariant
set to reduce conservatism.
A generalized lossless property was recently put for-
ward by Goyal et al. [9] in the context of data-driven
modeling of dynamical systems, opening applicability of
trapping region analysis to a broader class of quadratic
systems. The generalization helps facilitate analysis of
a broader class of quadratic systems than the standard
lossless setting. A sufficient condition for global bound-
edness under the generalized lossless constraint was
provided and a spherical trapping region was estimated;
however, all results in the study reverted to using the
standard lossless condition within the proposed opti-
mization framework [9]. Indeed, as noted by Goyal et
al., the generalized lossless condition “makes the corre-
sponding optimization problem even more challenging
to solve” and future work will “require developing novel
strategies to efficiently handle the additional complexity
introduced by” the generalized lossless condition [9].
The focus of our letter is on formulating the generalized
lossless constraint in a manner that is convenient to
embed within an optimization framework. However,
unlike Goyal et al. [9] whose study focused on data-
driven modeling, our focus is on optimization within
the trapping region analysis itself.
In this letter, we propose an optimization framework
for global boundedness analysis using ellipsoidal trap-
ping regions (i.e., trapping ellipsoids) under the afore-
mentioned generalized lossless constraint. There are
two main contributions to our work. First, we develop
conditions for the generalized lossless property, building
on the work in [9]. We cast the generalized lossless
constraint as a linear equality constraint. We establish
sufficient conditions for the existence of a globally at-

Definition 3 ( [2], [3]): A trapping region D ⊆Rn
is a compact set that is forward invariant, i.e., if y(t0) ∈
D then y(t) ∈D, ∀t ≥t0. A trapping region D ⊆Rn is
called globally attracting if there exists an open set O ⊃
D such that every trajectory enters O for sufficiently
large t.
By definition, if a trapping region is globally attracting
then every trajectory will eventually enter this set, pos-
sibly in the limit as t →∞. Moreover, if a trajectory
enters the trapping region, then it remains in the trapping
region for all future time. As one special case, consider
D := {0}. In this case, D is a trapping region if and only
if x = 0 is an equilibrium point. In addition, D := {0} is
a globally attracting trapping region if and only if x = 0
is a globally asymptotically stable equilibrium point.
Previous work on quadratic systems [1], [2], [4]
has focused on spherical trapping regions B(r) with
“energy” functions W(y) := yTy. Suppose W is mono-
tonically decreasing outside a given ball B(r). Then it
can be shown, via a Lyapunov-like analysis, that the
ball is a trapping region. In this special case, the ball
B(r) is known as a globally monotonically attracting
trapping region [2], [4]. In this letter, we will consider
more general (ellipsoidal) trapping regions and more
general classes of Lyapunov-like (“energy”) functions
of the form V (y) := yTPy where P ≻0. We focus
on globally attracting trapping regions in this work.
Throughout the remainder of the letter, we will be
using the terms trapping region and trapping ellipsoid
in reference to a globally attracting trapping region.

A quadratic nonlinearity Q satisfies the generalized
lossless constraint in Definition 1 for many possible S
(see, for example, the discussion on the Lorenz attractor
in Section II). We utilize a polynomial approach for
characterizing all of these possible generalized lossless
matrices for a given quadratic nonlinearity. This involves
recasting the generalized lossless constraint into a form
that depends only on the matrices defining the quadratic
nonlinearity (see (2)) and the generalized lossless matrix
S. First, we note that the inner product yTSQ(y) is a
cubic polynomial in y but is linear in S. Hence this
expression can be written as g(S)h(y) where g(S) is a
row vector that is linear in S and h(y) is a column vector

matrix G such that g(S) = GTvec(S) since g is a linear
function of S. This yields the necessary constraint for

V. CONCLUSIONS
We propose an ellipsoidal framework for trapping
region analysis of quadratic systems that allow efficient
parameterization and optimization of the lossless prop-
erty. The numerical routine, which requires solutions to
SDPs, includes a shift coordinate computation that is
directly linked with the existence of a trapping ellipsoid;
hence, this step alone can be useful for establishing
global boundedness of complex models in applications.
Illustrative numerical results are provided that highlight
key features like reduced conservatism in the ultimate
bound and broader applicability than the prior methods.
