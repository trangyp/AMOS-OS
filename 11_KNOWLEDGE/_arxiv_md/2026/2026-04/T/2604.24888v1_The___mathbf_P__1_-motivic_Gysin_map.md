Abstract. We develop a P1-unstable non-A1-invariant theory of motivic
spaces and spectra, and construct the Gysin map therein for regular immer-

This in particular gives the Gysin map in the Annala–Hoyois–Iwasa
P1-motivic spectra, and thus gives a uniform construction for the Gysin maps
of various cohomology theories.

1. Introduction
The P1-motivic homotopy theory is a version of motivic homotopy theory re-
cently developed by Annala–Hoyois–Iwasa [AHI25]. It is a generalization of Vo-
evodsky’s P1-stable A1-homotopy theory [Dun+07], in that it keeps P1-stability
but replaces A1-invariance with the weaker condition called smooth blowup exci-
sion [AHI25, §2], cf. Definition 2.3.
This makes it suitable for studying many
non-A1-invariant cohomology theories, e.g. Hodge cohomology [Stacks, 0FM4], de
Rham cohomology [Stacks, 0FL6] away from characteristic 0, crystalline cohomol-
ogy [Stacks, 07GI], and the recently developed prismatic cohomology [BS22], as
they all satisfy the smooth blowup excision [BL22, §9.4].
Unlike the study of A1-homotopy theory which has been carried out for decades
and successfully applied to solve conjectures including Bloch–Kato [Voe11], the
study of P1-motivic homotopy theory has just got started: there are many basic
constructions yet to be done and many structures yet to be revealed, among which
stands the Gysin map, the topic of this paper.

study of the Gysin map, we notice that we have only used blowup excision and
P1-homotopy, but not P1-stability anywhere, so we propose a P1-unstable theory
of non-A1-invariant motives here:

1.4. Notation and convention. We summarize our global notation and conven-
tion here, mainly regarding derived algebraic geometry.
In short, everything is
animated or derived unless otherwise stated, and readers familiar with the setup
can safely skip this subsection. Precisely:
(1) A category means an (∞, 1)-category. For n ∈N, we use [n] to denote the
poset ({0, 1, . . . , n}, ≤), often viewed as a category.
(2) A ring is an animated ring.
(3) For a ring R, an R-module is an animated R-module, or in other words
an object in D≥0(R). The category D≥0(R) is left complete prestable and
canonically presentably symmetric monoidal under ⊗R. We denote the pre-
sentable stabilization of D≥0(R) by D(R) and call its objects R-complexes.
(4) For a ring R, an R-algebra is a ring along with a map from R to it. We let
Alg(R) denote the category of R-algebras, which is canonically presentably
symmetric monoidal under ⊗R.

2. P1-unstable motivic spectra
In this section, we introduce a minimal version of motivic spectra where the
Gysin map is available. It will be very similar to but a little more general than the
version in [AI23] and [AHI25], and will clarify the structures that we actually use
in constructing the Gysin map. Impatient readers who know the theory in [AHI25]
can skip this section and do everything with their motivic spectra, i.e. read all the
P as Sm and read all the PS(PS) as the Nisnevich version of MSS.
Here, all our schemes are quasicompact and quasiseparated.
2.1. Basic definitions.
Definition 2.1 (motivic setup). A qbu-motivic setup, or simply a motivic setup,
is a wide subcategory P ⊂Ring, i.e. a class P of ring maps closed under finite
composition, such that:
(1) P is closed under base change;
(2) P contains open immersions;
(3) P is Zariski local on both the source and the target; accordingly, we can
talk about a scheme map being in P, meaning Zariski locally so;
(4) for any ring R, the full subcategory PR of P-algebras over R is small, and
moreover the Zariski sheaf of categories R 7→PR is accessible;
(5) P contains projective bundles, i.e. for any scheme Y and any vector bundle
E/Y , the projective bundle map P(E) →Y lies in P;

A.4. Possible directions of further generalization.
Remark A.32 (more functoriality). The blowups introduced in this section should
have more functoriality than in excessive maps, just as the Proj construction of
graded rings has more functoriality than in certain graded maps: it is also functorial
in certain maps that scale the grading. We plan to pursue such functoriality in
future work, as we may need to use it in comparing the MS of [AHI25] and the
logSH of [BPØ22].
It will probably involve the Proj construction of Nn-graded
rings, i.e. an animated version of the theory in [BS03] and [MR25].
Remark A.33 (pushout-blowup for cubes). One can define pushout-blowups for
excessive [1]n-diagrams similar to Definition A.25, with descriptions similar to The-
orem A.31, consisting of 2n −1 steps of blowups. This will be needed for composing
n Gysin maps, generalizing Theorem 3.17 that composes two Gysin maps. Ideally,
the author expects a common generalization of such pushout-blowups and poset
blowups as in Definition A.6, but he has no concrete idea on this yet.
