Thermodynamic Identiﬁcation of the Internal Superconducting Phase Boundary in
UTe2 for H ∥b
Michal Valiˇska,∗Tetiana Haidamak, Andrej Cabala, Petr Proschek, and Vladim´ır Sechovsk´y
Charles University, Faculty of Mathematics and Physics,
Department of Condensed Matter Physics, Ke Karlovu 5, 121 16 Prague 2, Czech Republic

(Dated: April 29, 2026)
The H–T phase diagram of UTe2 for magnetic ﬁeld along the hard b axis contains an unresolved
internal boundary near µ0H ∼14–15 T, previously inferred from ac susceptibility and transport
experiments but lacking thermodynamic evidence. We report ultrasound results for several elastic
modes in an ultraclean UTe2 single crystal with Tc > 2 K for H ∥b down to 0.33 K and up to
18 T. A pronounced anomaly in the longitudinal C33 mode, with a weaker response in C44 and no
resolvable anomaly in C55, establishes this feature as a bulk thermodynamic phase boundary and
reveals a symmetry-selective coupling to lattice strain. The phase line remains nearly constant in
ﬁeld near 14 T and terminates near 13.5 T and 1.25 K at a tetracritical point, providing the thermo-
dynamic evidence for the fourth phase boundary in the H–T phase diagram. The results constrain
the order-parameter structure of the high-ﬁeld phase and support ﬁeld-induced multicomponent
superconductivity in UTe2.
Since the discovery of superconductivity in UTe2 by
Ran et al., this heavy-fermion paramagnet has become
one of the central platforms for the study of spin-triplet
and potentially topological superconductivity [1]. Early
thermodynamic and transport studies rapidly estab-
lished UTe2 as an unconventional superconductor with a
large and highly anisotropic upper critical ﬁeld, strong-
coupling behavior, and a striking sensitivity to magnetic
ﬁeld, pressure, and sample quality [2–4]. High-ﬁeld and
high-pressure measurements subsequently revealed a re-
markably rich superconducting phase diagram whose de-
tailed topology remains intensely debated [5–12].
The interpretation of UTe2 is strongly aﬀected by
sample quality.
Molten-salt-ﬂux growth and related
liquid-transport methods enabled ultraclean crystals
with Tc > 2 K [13–15], while uranium deﬁciency and
uranium-site defects were identiﬁed as key sources of
suppressed or split transitions [16, 17].
The same ul-
traclean generation of crystals also enabled quantum-
oscillation and quantum-interference studies of the quasi-
two-dimensional Fermi surface [18–20], and revealed the
enhancement of superconductivity in intense magnetic
ﬁelds [21]. This issue is crucial for the high-ﬁeld phase
diagram for H ∥b, where ultraclean crystals revealed
additional anomalies near µ0H ∼14–15 T in ac suscepti-
bility and transport studies [11, 22, 23]. These anomalies
were interpreted as an internal superconducting phase
boundary, but a bulk thermodynamic signature has re-
mained missing. A multicomponent spin-triplet scenario
has further predicted a nearly horizontal internal line ter-
minating at a tetracritical point near 15 T [24].
This question has acquired additional interest through
recent progress on the p–T phase diagram. In the broader
H–p–T phase space, two locations have emerged where
only three second-order phase boundaries appeared to
meet, implying an incomplete and therefore thermo-
dynamically problematic topology unless an additional
phase line is present.
One of these cases, in the p–T
plane, has now been resolved by recent ultrasound mea-
surements that uncovered the missing thermodynamic
boundary and established a tetracritical point (TetCP)
[25]. At the same time, the high-ﬁeld and high-pressure
superconducting phases were shown to be continuously
connected [12], and the corresponding H–p–T construc-
tion implies that the pressure-induced TetCP evolves into
a tetracritical line extending toward ambient pressure
and the high-ﬁeld superconducting regime [25]. Within
this picture, the long-suspected anomaly near 14–15 T
for H ∥b is naturally expected to represent the miss-
ing internal phase boundary whose endpoint at ambient
pressure forms the high-ﬁeld TetCP.
Here we address this issue using pulse-echo ultrasound
measurements of the elastic constants Cij on an ultr-
aclean UTe2 crystal with Tc > 2 K for magnetic ﬁeld
applied along the b axis. We resolve a distinct anomaly
in C33(H) within the superconducting state that traces a
nearly temperature-independent line near 14 T between
0.33 and 1.1 K. We show that this anomaly constitutes
bulk thermodynamic evidence for the fourth phase line in
the H–T phase diagram. The line terminates near 13.5 T
and 1.25 K at a TetCP, consistent with the tetracritical-
line scenario recently established in the p–T phase dia-
gram [25]. Our results therefore place the high-ﬁeld H–T
phase diagram of UTe2 on ﬁrm thermodynamic footing
and provide further support for multicomponent super-
conductivity in the ultraclean limit.

ined through its coupling to lattice strain εij.

A clear
softening anomaly, marked by arrows, is resolved in-
side the superconducting state at a nearly temperature-
independent ﬁeld of about 14 T. Since C33 is a bulk
elastic constant, this anomaly establishes the missing
thermodynamic signature of the internal high-ﬁeld phase
boundary previously inferred only from ac susceptibility
and transport measurements [11, 22, 23]. The anomaly
is visible from the lowest measured temperature up to
1.1 K, and its amplitude increases substantially on ap-
proaching the multicritical region where the line termi-
nates at the TetCP. At higher temperatures, the feature
weakens and shifts to lower ﬁelds as the system moves
away from the tetracritical regime. For the 0.9 and 1.1 K
isotherms, an additional kink is observed at higher ﬁeld,
consistent with the transition into the upper high-ﬁeld
superconducting phase [6, 9, 21].
The enhancement of the C33 anomaly on approach-
ing the TetCP is consistent with the known sensitivity of
elastic constants to critical and multicritical ﬂuctuations
in correlated uranium systems, including metamagnetic
critical endpoints in UTe2 and UCoAl [30, 31] and tri-
critical ﬂuctuations in UIrSi3 [32]. Although a TetCP is
distinct from a conventional critical endpoint, the same
enhanced lattice response appears to be operative here.
A weaker anomaly is also observed in the transverse
mode C44 [Fig. 1(b)]. It has the opposite sign, appearing
as a hardening near 14 T, but its ﬁeld position tracks the
C33 anomaly, indicating that both modes detect the same
thermodynamic boundary. By contrast, no correspond-
ing anomaly is resolved in C55 within our experimental
resolution [Fig. 1(c)]. This mode selectivity mirrors the
pressure-induced tetracritical case, where the newly iden-
tiﬁed internal boundary under pressure was prominent in
the C33 elastic response but not comparably visible in C55
[25]. The combination of strong C33, weaker but ﬁnite
C44, and absent C55 response shows that the high-ﬁeld
phase beyond the internal boundary couples anisotropi-
cally to lattice strain and provides a symmetry-sensitive
constraint on its order-parameter structure.
The inset of Fig. 1(a) shows the corresponding trans-
verse magnetostriction ∆lc/lc for H ∥b. A kink is ob-
served at essentially the same ﬁeld as the anomaly in
C33, conﬁrming that the ﬁeld-induced boundary aﬀects
not only the dynamic elastic stiﬀness but also the static
equilibrium lattice distortion. Because both elastic con-
stants and magnetostriction derive from the same strain-
dependent free energy, the anomaly cannot be attributed
merely to vortex dynamics or to a transport-speciﬁc fea-
ture.

The mode selectivity also has symmetry implications.
In orthorhombic D2h symmetry, diﬀerent shear modes
probe diﬀerent strain channels: C44 couples to εbc, C55
to εac, and C66 to εab [27, 28]. Recent ultrasound work
argued that the low-ﬁeld superconducting state is single-
component, with an odd-parity B2u state as the most
compatible candidate, based on the absence of shear-
modulus discontinuities at Tc [28]. In this context, the
ﬁnite anomaly in C44, together with the absence of a
resolvable C55 response, shows that the phase beyond
the internal boundary is not a simple continuation of the
low-ﬁeld state but rather couples to strain in a symmetry-
selective manner. The present data do not uniquely de-
termine the secondary component, particularly without
C66, but they provide thermodynamic evidence that the
high-ﬁeld phase carries additional order-parameter struc-
ture, consistent with multicomponent scenarios for the
tetracritical region [24, 25, 33, 34].
The anomaly positions extracted from the thermody-
namic data are summarized in Fig. 3. Following the no-
tation of the pressure-dependent phase diagram [12, 25],
we denote the low-ﬁeld superconducting state as SC1 and
the upper high-ﬁeld state as SC2. The newly resolved,
nearly ﬁeld-constant internal boundary near 14 T sepa-
rates SC1 from an intermediate region naturally identi-
ﬁed as SC1+SC2 and terminates near 13.5 T and 1.25 K
at a tetracritical point (TetCP), where four phase bound-
aries meet. Thus, the ultrasound anomaly supplies the
missing thermodynamic phase line required to complete
the H ∥b phase-diagram topology and provides direct
support for ﬁeld-induced multicomponent superconduc-
tivity. It is consistent with the tetracritical-line scenario
advanced from the pressure-dependent phase diagram
[12, 24, 25], and echoes the role of sound velocity in sens-
ing tetracritical topology in UPt3 [35].
