THE REAL AND STABLE RANK OF TRACIALLY COMPLETE
C*-ALGEBRAS
SAMUEL EVINGTON AND AARON TIKUISIS
Abstract. We prove that a factorial tracially complete C∗-algebra with CPoU
has real rank zero and stable rank one. This leads to an essentially complete
description of the Cuntz semigroup of these algebras. In particular, the results
of this paper hold for the uniform tracial completions of Z-stable C∗-algebras.
Introduction
The real rank of a C∗-algebra was introduced by Brown and Pedersen ([4]), based
on the observation that a compact Hausdorff space X has covering dimension at
most n if and only if every continuous function f : X →Rn+1 can be perturbed so
that the range does not contain the origin. Brown and Pedersen’s work builds on
Rieffel’s earlier notion of topological stable rank ([23]), which was later shown to
coincide with the Bass stable rank ([16]).
The minimal values of the real and stable rank are of particular interest as they
relate to the relative abundance or scarcity of invertible elements in a given C∗-
algebra A. Indeed, A has real rank zero if the set of invertible self-adjoint elements
Inv(A) ∩Asa is dense in set of self-adjoint elements Asa, and A has stable rank one
if the set of invertible elements Inv(A) is dense in A.
Every von Neumann algebra has real rank zero (by Borel functional calculus),
and every finite von Neumann algebra has stable rank one (by using unitary polar
form). Moreover, there are close connections between these notations of rank and
the Elliott classification programme for simple nuclear C∗-algebras (see the intro-
duction to [6] for a survey). Indeed, every approximately finite dimensional algebra
([4]) and every Kirchberg algebra has real rank zero ([29]), and every stably finite
classifiable C∗-algebra has stable rank one ([25]). In particular every classifiable
C∗-algebra has either real rank zero or stable rank one, and often both.
In this paper, we shall consider the real rank and the stable rank of factorial
tracially complete C∗-algebras (see Definition 1.1 below). These C∗-algebras arise
naturally when one completes the unit ball of a C∗-algebra A with respect to the
uniform 2-seminorm ∥a∥2,T (A) = supτ∈T (A) τ(a∗a)1/2.
Here, T(A) denotes the
space of tracial states on A, which is assumed to be non-empty. Provided T(A) is
compact (which is automatic when A is unital), the result of this construction is a
C∗-algebra AT (A) with a bundle-like structure over T(A), where the fibres are the
GNS-representations πτ with respect to each τ ∈T(A). This viewpoint emerged
from Ozawa’s work on W∗-bundles ([21], see also [13]), and was subsequently gen-
eralised to arbitrary trace simplices by the authors along with Carri´on, Castillejos,
Gabe, Schafhauser, and White ([6]).

Tracially complete C∗-algebras provide a general framework for carrying out
tracial gluing arguments, which allow one to utilise properties of the von Neumann
algebras {πτ(A)′′ : τ ∈T(A)} to understand the structure of the tracial completion
AT (A).
Through techniques such as Matui and Sato’s property (SI) ([19, 20]),
one can in turn use the structure of AT (A) to prove results about the underlying
C∗-algebra A. Tracial gluing arguments have underpinned recent progress on the
Toms–Winter regularity conjecture ([11, 8, 10]), the abstract approach to the C∗-
classification ([7, 9]), and dynamical notations of regularity ([18, 26]).
The technical machinery for implementing tracial gluing arguments is known
as complemented partitions of unity (CPoU). Informally, a tracially complete C∗-
algebra has CPoU provided there are (approximately) projection-valued partitions
of unity over the trace space (see Definition 1.2). Under mild regularity conditions
on A, which are satisfied when A is classifiable in the sense of the Elliott classifica-
tion programme, the uniform tracial completion of A will have CPoU ([11, 6]).
This brings us to the first main theorem of this paper.
Theorem A. Let (M, X) be a factorial tracially complete C∗-algebra with CPoU.
Then M has real rank zero.
The conclusion that M has real rank zero is stronger than previously known
results giving real rank zero of the tracial ultrapower Mω ([6, 15]). Indeed, to
prove that M has real rank zero, one must achieve approximations in operator
norm ∥· ∥, whereas for proving that the tracial ultrapower Mω has real rank zero
approximations with respect to the weaker uniform 2-norm ∥· ∥2,X are sufficient.
Accordingly, the proof of Theorem A is substantially harder than typical tracial
gluing arguments based on CPoU (such as those in [6, Section 7]).
Our approach is to show that every hereditary C∗-subalgebra B of M contains
a (not necessarily increasing) approximate unit consisting of projections.
This
property, first studied under the name property (HP) in [22], was shown to be
equivalent to real rank zero in Brown and Pedersen’s original paper on real rank
zero ([4]). In fact, it is enough to show that for any x ∈B and ϵ > 0 there is a
projection p ∈B such that ∥px −x∥< ϵ, using a standard argument invoking the
C∗-identity (see (2.19) below).
The strategy is to construct a ∥· ∥2,X-Cauchy sequence of ∥· ∥2,X-approximate
projections using CPoU, whose error tends to zero. By completeness, such a se-
quence must have a ∥·∥2,X-norm limit in our tracially complete C∗-algebra (M, X),
and it is not hard to show that the limit must be a projection. The difficulty is to
ensure that this projection lies in a desired hereditary C∗-subalgebra B (which is
hard as B need not be ∥· ∥2,X-closed) and ensure it acts like an approximate unit
(which is also a ∥·∥-norm condition). The key idea is to use the “acts as a unit” re-
lation a ◁b (see Section 1.1) as an interlocutor between the ∥· ∥2,X-world of CPoU
arguments and the ∥· ∥-world of hereditary C∗-sublagberas. This builds on the
techniques developed in [14] for constructing projections in hereditary subalgebras.
Motivated by this success, one might hope that similar techniques could be used
to prove that factoriall tracially complete C∗-algebra with CPoU have stable rank
one. This is indeed the case.
Theorem B. Let (M, X) be a factorial tracially complete C∗-algebra with CPoU.
Then M has stable rank one.
This time the focus is on unitaries, rather than on projections. More precisely,
the aim is to find elements a with a unitary polar form a = u|a| for some unitary
u. Any such element a is then ϵ-close to the invertible element u(|a| + ϵ).
In a tracial von Neumann algebra (M, τ), every element a ∈M has a (not
necessarily unique) unitary polar form. However, there is no ∥· ∥2,τ-continuous

4. Applications
In this section, we explore some applications of Theorems A and B. We begin
with a discussion of the trace problem for a factorial tracially complete C∗-algebra
(M, X). This problem first appeared in the literature as [6, Question 1.1] and asks
whether the inclusion X ⊆T(M) is in fact an equality. The problem was solved
by the first-named author in [14] when (M, X) is type II1 and has CPoU. Using
Theorem A, we can provide a simplified proof of this result.
Theorem 4.1 (Theorem C). Let (M, X) be a type II1 factorial tracially complete
C∗-algebra with CPoU. Then T(M) = X.
Proof. Let τ ∈T(M).
Let τ∗: K0(M) →R be the the induced map on K0.
By [6, Corollary 7.19], we have (K0(M), K0(M)+) ∼= (Aff(X, R), Aff(X, R+
0 )) with
the isomorphism given by the pairing of K0-classes with tracial states in X. In
particular, the K0-class of the unit 1M is mapped to the constant function 1Aff(X,R).
We may therefore view τ∗as an order-preserving group homomorphism τ∗:
Aff(X, R) →R such that τ∗(1Aff(X,R)) = 1.
As τ∗is a group homomorphism
between R-vector spaces, it is automatically Q-linear. Since τ∗is order preserving
and satisfies τ∗(1Aff(X,R)) = 1, it follows that τ∗is R-linear and we have |τ∗(f)| ≤
∥f∥∞for all f ∈Aff(X, R).

unit space Aff(X, R). By Kadison duality ([17]), τ∗is given by evaluation at some

rank zero by Theorem A, the linear span of its projections is ∥· ∥-dense in M by
[22, Proposition 14]. Therefore, τ(a) = τ0(a) for all a ∈M. Hence, τ ∈X.
