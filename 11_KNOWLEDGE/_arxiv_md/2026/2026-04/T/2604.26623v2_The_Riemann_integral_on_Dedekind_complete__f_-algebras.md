Abstract
In this paper we develop a theory of integration for locally band preserving functions,
introduced by Ercan and Wickstead, on Dedekind complete f-algebras. Specifically, we
construct Darboux and Riemann integrals and show that they are equal. We then connect
the theory of integrable functions to the theory of order differentiable functions, intro-
duced by the third and fourth authors, by proving a Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
Furthermore, we show that a Mean Value Theorem for Integrals holds and that we can
integrate by parts and substitutions.
Keywords: Riemann integral, vector lattice, Φ-algebra, differentiation, locally band
preserving, Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Introduction
Recently, an order-theoretic approach to complex analysis was developed
in [RS19, RS25] for complex Φ-algebras (unital f-algebras). The first pa-
per focused on series and power series in universally complete complex Φ-
algebras, and the second paper introduced two distinct, but both natural,
notions of differentiability using order convergence in Dedekind complete Φ-
algebras: order differentiability and super order differentiability. This theory
allows one to do analysis on certain spaces which do not have a natural norm,
such as C∞(K), and it illustrates how order-theoretic notions play a crucial
role in the development of the theory of complex analysis.
A natural continuation of the work done in [RS19, RS25] is to develop
a theory of differential and integral calculus in real Dedekind complete Φ-
algebras. The work done on order derivatives in [RS25] naturally carries over
to the real case, and this paper mainly focuses on the construction and the
properties of the Riemann integral.
While developing the theory of Riemann integration, locally band preserv-
ing functions, first introduced in [EW98], became central to the theory. We
further developed their properties in [KNRS26]. Generalisations of classical
results were proved for locally band preserving functions in real Dedekind
complete Φ-algebras, such as the Intermediate Value Theorem, the Extreme
Value Theorem, and the Mean Value Theorem—the last of which uses the
notion of order differentiability introduced in [RS25].
The structure of the paper is as follows. Some preliminary results are
given in Section 2.
Primarily, these are results from [KNRS26] that are
needed in the remainder of the paper.
The Riemann integral for locally band preserving and order bounded
functions f : [a, b] →E is constructed in Section 3, where E is a Dedekind
complete f-algebra and [a, b] is an order closed interval in E.
As in the
classical case, we show that the Darboux and Riemann constructions yield
the same integral.
In Section 4, we prove analogues of the basic classical properties of the
integral.
We also show that a locally band preserving and uniformly or-
der continuous function is necessarily integrable. It is known that an order
continuous function need not be uniformly order continuous in this setting,
so it is an open question whether order continuity is sufficient to guarantee
integrability.
Section 5 is devoted to defining

Order intervals
We introduce the notation we use for order intervals in E.
It is natural

2.3
Order continuity and uniform order continuity
In [KNRS26, Proposition 2.13], order continuity was characterised in a way
that resembles the classical ε −δ definition of continuity. A similar argu-
ment can be used for uniformly order continuous functions. We give these
characterisations below in Proposition 2.9.
Definition 2.7. Consider f : dom(f) →E. We say f is order continuous
at c ∈dom(f) if whenever xα →c in dom(f), we have f(xα) →f(c). The
function f is called order continuous if it is order continuous at every point in
its domain. We say f is uniformly order continuous if whenever xα −yα →0
in dom(f), we have f(xα) −f(yα) →0.
Notation 2.8. The notation ∆↓0 means that ∆is a downwards directed
set in E with infimum 0, whereas E ↘0 means that E is a subset of E with
infimum 0 that needs not be directed.
In the following characterisations of order continuity and uniform order
continuity, and later in Definitions 2.15 and 2.16, we use ∆↓0 and E ↘0. It
is possible to replace E ↘0 with E ↓0, but ∆must be downwards directed.
Therefore, the different arrows indicate when directedness is required

2.4
Locally band preserving functions
The notion of a locally band preserving function was first introduced in
[EW98], and was investigated further in [KNRS26]. In Section 3, we will
construct a Riemann integral for order bounded functions f : [a, b] →E, and
we will investigate the integrability of f primarily when f is locally band
preserving. What follows is some of the properties of locally band preserving
functions that will be needed throughout the remainder of the paper. We use
a definition that is equivalent to the one used in [EW98] by [KNRS26, Propo-
sition 4.4.] for vector lattices with the projection property.
Definition 2.10. Let E be a vector lattice with the projection property.
A function f : dom(f) →E is locally band preserving if for every band
projection P and for all x, y ∈dom(f), we have that P(x) = P(y) implies
P(f(x)) = P(f(y)).
Example 2.11.
(i) A linear function f : E →E is locally band preserving if and only if it
is band preserving.
(ii) Polynomials and power series, as in [RS19], defined on E are locally
band preserving.
(iii) In ℓ∞, a function is locally band preserving if and only if it is defined
coordinatewise.

Theorem 2.13 (Intermediate Value Theorem). Let E be a Dedekind com-
plete vector lattice. If f : [a, b] →E is order continuous and locally band
preserving, and y ∈[f(a) ∧f(b), f(a) ∨f(b)], then there exists a c ∈[a, b]
such that f(c) = y.
Theorem 2.14 (Extreme Value Theorem). Let E be a Dedekind complete
vector lattice.
If f : [a, b] →E is order continuous and locally band pre-
serving, then there exist c, d ∈[a, b] such that for all x ∈[a, b], we have
f(c) ≤f(x) ≤f(d).

2.5
Order differentiability and super order differentia-
bility
There are two natural ways of defining derivatives in the vector lattice setting
that turn out to be related, but not equivalent. These derivatives were first
studied in [RS25], and then further investigated in [KNRS26]. Between those
two papers, there is a minor difference in whether r ≫0 means r is a positive
invertible element or a weak order unit—we use the latter, and note that the
results in [RS25] are still true. To obtain uniqueness of order derivatives with
this change, it was necessary to assume E satisfies the projection property
in [KNRS26, Proposition 3.7].
Definition 2.15. Let E be a Φ-algebra with the projection property. A
function f : dom(f) →E is called order differentiable at c ∈dom(f) with
derivative f ′(c) if there exists an r ≫0 such that (c−r, c+r) ⊆dom(f), and
for every ∆↓0 there exists an E ↘0 with the property that for all ε ∈E
there exists a δ ∈∆satisfying
x ∈(c −r, c + r) and |x −c| ≤δ =⇒|f(x) −f(c) −(x −c)f ′(c)| ≤|x −c|ε.
Definition 2.16. Let E be a Φ-algebra with the projection property. A
function f : dom(f) →E is called super order differentiable at c ∈dom(f)
with derivative f ′(c) if there exists an r ≫0 such that (c−r, c+r) ⊆dom(f),
and for every ∆↓0 there exists an E ↘0 with the property that for all ε ∈E
there exists a δ ∈∆satisfying
x ∈dom(f) and |x −c| ≤δ =⇒|f(x) −f(c) −(x −c)f ′(c)| ≤|x −c|ε.
Remark 2.17. The above definitions differ only in whether the implication is
required to hold only for x ∈(c−r, c+r) or for all x ∈dom(f). These are not
equivalent as it is possible for a net (xα) to converge to c without ever entering
(c−r, c+r). It is immediate from the definitions that f is order differentiable
at c if and only if there exists an r ≫0 such that (c −r, c + r) ⊆dom(f)
and the restriction f|(c−r,c+r) is super order differentiable at c.
Order derivatives are always unique, see [RS25, Remark 3.3] and [KNRS26,
Proposition 3.7]. We also have available the sum and product rules for order
differentiability from [RS25, Theorems 3.15 and 4.3], where the reader may
also find chain and quotient rules.
