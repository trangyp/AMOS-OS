1. Introduction
Since the 1970s, Whittaker modules have been introduced and have attracted
considerable attention. As a class of modules that are not weight modules, they
play a significant role both in the representation theory of Lie algebras and in
physics. For a finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra g = n−⊕h⊕n+ and
Lie algebra homomorphism η : n+ →C, a g-module is called a Whittaker module
if x −η(x) acts locally nilpotently on it for any x ∈n+. Whittaker modules
over sl2(C) were constructed by Arnal and Pinzcon [1]. Kostant introduced and
studied Whittaker modules for all finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebras
g.
The map η is called non-singular if η(xα) ̸= 0 for all simple root vectors
of g.
He showed that simple non-singular Whittaker modules are in one-to-
one correspondence with the maximal ideals of the center Z(g) of the universal
enveloping algebra of g [17]. Further work related to singular Whittaker modules
has been carried out by E. Backelin [2] and E. McDowell [26]. In [10], it was
proved that the category of Whittaker modules, for any reductive g, is extension
full in the category of all g-modules. Whittaker modules have been extensively
studied across various algebraic structures, including the Virasoro algebra and
its related algebras [21, 22, 29, 30, 38], quantum groups [28, 35, 39], generalized
Weyl algebras[6], affine Lie algebras [8, 9, 11], and Lie superalgebras [7]. For Lie
algebras lacking triangular decomposition, P. Batra and V. Mazorchuk provided a
