Translation Monoids and Recursive Evaluation
in Finite Binary Algebras
Volkan Yildiz
vo1kan@hotmail.co.uk
April 3, 2026
Abstract
Let A = (A, ⋆) be a finite binary algebra, not necessarily associative. For each n ≥1,
every full binary bracketing on x1, . . . , xn determines an n-ary term operation on A, and
hence an evaluation word obtained by listing its values on An in lexicographic order. This
produces an mn × Cn−1 array, where m = |A| and Cn−1 is the (n −1)st Catalan number.
We show that the recursive structure of these arrays is governed by the translation monoid
T(A) = ⟨La, Ra : a ∈A⟩≤AA,
La(x) = a ⋆x,
Ra(x) = x ⋆a.
More precisely, context maps arising from subterms are exactly the elements of T(A), so
every element of the translation monoid occurs as a recursive block map. We also prove that
rank defines a natural chain of two-sided ideals in T(A), that the minimum-rank elements
form a minimal nonempty two-sided ideal, and that Green’s J -classes are contained in rank
layers. Finally, we show by example that equal rank does not determine the J -class in
general.
Keywords: finite binary algebras, Catalan bracketings, term operations, evaluation words,
translation monoids, context maps, recursive blocks, transformation semigroups, Green’s rela-
tions, minimal ideals.

Introduction
Binary bracketings form one of the most classical Catalan families in algebra. For each n ≥1,
the set of full binary bracketings on x1, . . . , xn has cardinality Cn−1, the (n −1)st Catalan
number. Once a binary operation is fixed, every such bracketing determines an n-ary term
operation, and hence an associated evaluation word on the underlying algebra.
The present paper studies these evaluation words for a finite binary algebra A = (A, ⋆).
Fixing an ordering of A, each bracketing determines a word obtained by listing the values of
the induced term operation on An in lexicographic order. In this way one obtains, for each
n, a family of words indexed by the Catalan set of bracketings. Our aim is to understand the
recursive structure of these words.
The present paper is motivated in part by our earlier study of bracketed Kleene implications,
[6], where the Catalan family of bracketings was analysed at the level of three-valued truth tables
and their associated generating functions. Here we replace that special setting by a general finite
binary algebra and focus on the translation monoid governing recursive context maps.
The key observation is that when a bracketing is decomposed at a subterm, the corresponding
evaluation word decomposes into blocks, and these blocks are controlled by unary maps obtained
by repeatedly combining with fixed constants along the chain of enclosing binary operations.
For example, consider the bracketing
t = (x1 ⋆x2) ⋆x3
and the subterm u = x2. After fixing the variables outside u, say
x1 = 0,
x3 = 1,
the whole term becomes a unary function of the value of u, namely
x 7−→(0 ⋆x) ⋆1.
This is a composition of a left translation and a right translation:
x 7−→(0 ⋆x) ⋆1 = R1 ◦L0(x).
Indeed,
(R1 ◦L0)(x) = R1(0 ⋆x) = (0 ⋆x) ⋆1.
Hence the block of the evaluation word of t obtained by fixing
x1 = 0,
x3 = 1
is obtained from the evaluation word of u by coordinatewise action of the map R1 ◦L0 ∈T(A).
This leads naturally to the transformation monoid
T(A) = ⟨La, Ra : a ∈A⟩≤AA,
La(x) = a ⋆x,
Ra(x) = x ⋆a,
which we call the translation monoid of A.
The present paper lies between two established lines of research. The first is the study of
Catalan bracketings and the term operations they determine; see, for example, the literature
on associative spectra and its later developments [1, 4]. The second is the study of translation

monoids and context-induced unary maps of algebras [2, 3]. Our contribution is to connect these
two viewpoints through the recursive evaluation arrays of finite binary algebras. We show that
recursive blocks in bracketing-evaluation arrays are governed by the translation monoid, thereby
turning a Catalan bracketing problem into a natural transformation-semigroup problem.
Our main structural result shows that if u is a subterm of a bracketing t, then after fixing all
variables outside u, the induced unary map from the value of u to the value of t belongs to T(A).
More precisely, it is a composition of left and right translations determined by the enclosing
chain from u to t. As a consequence, every recursive block in every bracketing-evaluation array
is obtained by coordinatewise action of an element of T(A) on the evaluation word of a subterm.
This identifies the translation monoid as the natural algebraic object governing recursive
evaluation in finite binary algebras.
It also opens a semigroup-theoretic perspective on the
problem: one may ask how the internal structure of T(A), including its ideals and Green classes,
reflects the recursive complexity of the associated evaluation arrays. In the present paper we
focus on the foundational step, namely the passage from subterms to translations.
The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2 we introduce bracketing evaluation arrays,
give a concrete example, and define the translation monoid. We then formalise subterm con-
text maps and prove that they all belong to T(A). Section 3 shows that the realised context
maps coincide with the whole translation monoid and develops the first semigroup-theoretic
consequences of this construction.

Bracketings, evaluation words, and translation monoids
For each integer n ≥1, let Bn denote the set of all full binary bracketings on the variables
x1, . . . , xn,
with the order of variables preserved. Thus Bn may be identified with the set of all binary terms
obtained from x1, . . . , xn by inserting parentheses in all possible ways, and
|Bn| = Cn−1,
where Cn−1 is the (n −1)st Catalan number.
Definition 2.1. Let A = (A, ⋆) be a finite binary algebra, (not assumed to be associative), and
fix once and for all an ordering
A = {a1, . . . , am},
m = |A|.
For t ∈Bn, let
tA : An →A
denote the n-ary term operation determined by t in A.
The evaluation word of t over A, denoted wt, is the word of length mn obtained by listing
the values of tA on all tuples of An in lexicographic order relative to the fixed ordering of A.
The family
En(A) = (wt)t∈Bn
will be called the nth bracketing-evaluation array of A.
Thus En(A) may be viewed either as an indexed family of words over the alphabet A, or as
an mn × Cn−1 array whose columns are indexed by binary bracketings. The combinatorics of
bracketings supplies the Catalan indexing, while the algebra A determines the resulting words
and the relations among them.
To understand the recursive structure of the words wt, it is useful to look at what happens
when one focuses on a subterm u inside a bracketing t. After fixing values for all variables

outside u, the whole term t becomes a unary function of the value of u. Thus each occurrence
of a subterm gives rise to a map A →A. The simplest such maps are obtained by multiplying
a variable value on the left or on the right by a fixed element of A, namely
La(x) = a ⋆x,
Ra(x) = x ⋆a.
Definition 2.2. For each a ∈A, define maps La, Ra : A →A by
La(x) = a ⋆x,
Ra(x) = x ⋆a
(x ∈A).
The submonoid of the full transformation monoid AA generated by these maps will be denoted
by
T(A) = ⟨La, Ra : a ∈A⟩≤AA
and called the translation monoid of A.
We next formalise the unary maps arising from subterm occurrences.
Definition 2.3. Let t ∈Bn, and let u be a subterm occurrence of t. Let the variables occurring
in u be regarded as internal to u, and all other variables of t as external to u. After fixing values
of the external variables, the term t determines a unary map
κt,u,a : A →A,
where a records the chosen values of the external variables, by evaluating u as a single input
variable and then evaluating the whole term t. We call κt,u,a the context map of the occurrence
u in t relative to the external assignment a.
For a subterm occurrence u of a bracketing t, let xi1, . . . , xik be the variables occurring in u,
in their inherited left-to-right order. By relabelling these variables as x1, . . . , xk, the occurrence
u determines a bracketing eu ∈Bk. We define the evaluation word of the occurrence u to be
wu := weu.
Equivalently, wu is obtained by listing the values of the induced term operation of u as its
internal variables vary over Ak in lexicographic order.

The main point is that these context maps do not range arbitrarily over all unary transfor-
mations of A. They are already forced to lie in the translation monoid.
Theorem 2.5. Let A = (A, ⋆) be a finite binary algebra, let t ∈Bn, and let u be a subterm
occurrence of t. For every choice of values of the variables external to u, the associated context
map
κt,u,a : A →A
belongs to the translation monoid T(A). More precisely, κt,u,a is a composition of left and right
translations, with one factor for each enclosing binary operation on the chain from u to t.

Proof. We argue by induction on the distance from u to t in the chain of enclosing subterm
occurrences.
If this distance is 0, then u = t. In that case the context map is simply the identity map on
A, since the value inserted at u is already the value of the whole term t. As T(A) is a monoid,
it contains the identity transformation, so the claim holds.
Now assume the statement holds whenever the distance is at most d, and let u be at distance
d + 1 from t. Let v be the parent subterm occurrence of u. Then exactly one of the following
two cases occurs:
v = s ⋆u
or
v = u ⋆s,
where s is the sibling subterm occurrence of u inside v.
Fix an assignment of values to all variables external to u. In particular, all variables occurring
in s are fixed, so the value of s under this assignment is a constant c ∈A.
Suppose first that v = s⋆u. Then whenever the variable input to the context map has value
x ∈A, the value at v is
c ⋆x = Lc(x).
From v upward to the whole term t, the remaining enclosing context has distance d. By the
induction hypothesis, the induced map from the value at v to the value of t is some element
g ∈T(A). Hence the total context map is
κt,u,a = g ◦Lc ∈T(A).
The case v = u ⋆s is analogous. The value at v is then
x ⋆c = Rc(x),
and by the induction hypothesis the remaining enclosing context contributes some map g ∈
T(A). Therefore
κt,u,a = g ◦Rc ∈T(A).
In both cases the context map lies in T(A), and each step from u to t contributes exactly
one left or right translation. This completes the induction.
⋆
The preceding theorem shows that the recursive structure of bracketing evaluations is me-
diated by the translation monoid. To express the immediate consequence for evaluation words,
we let unary maps act coordinatewise on words: if f : A →A and v = v1v2 · · · vr ∈Ar, then
f(v) := f(v1)f(v2) · · · f(vr).
Corollary 2.6. Let t ∈Bn, and let u be a subterm occurrence of t. Every block of the evaluation
word wt obtained by fixing all variables external to u has the form
f(wu)
for some f ∈T(A). In particular, every recursive block arising in any bracketing-evaluation
array En(A) is obtained from the evaluation word of a subterm by the coordinatewise action of
an element of the translation monoid.
Proof. Fix values of all variables external to u.
As the internal variables of u run through
all possible values in lexicographic order, the values of the subterm u form the word wu. By
Theorem 2.5, the corresponding context map from the value of u to the value of t is some
transformation f ∈T(A). Applying this transformation coordinatewise to the word wu produces
exactly the corresponding block of wt. The final statement follows immediately.
⋆
Remark 2.7. Corollary 2.6 identifies T(A) as the basic algebraic object underlying recursive
evaluation in finite binary algebras. It is therefore natural to ask how the semigroup-theoretic
structure of T(A), including its ideals and Green classes, reflects the recursive complexity of the
families En(A).

Realised context maps and first semigroup consequences
We now make precise the relation between recursive subterm contexts and the translation
monoid. The main point is that no gap remains: every element of the translation monoid is
realised by a context map.
Definition 3.1. Let A = (A, ⋆) be a finite binary algebra. Denote by C(A) the set of all unary
maps A →A arising as context maps
κt,u,a : A →A
from subterm occurrences u in bracketings t, under arbitrary external assignments a.
By Theorem 2.5, one already has
C(A) ⊆T(A).
The reverse inclusion also holds.
Proposition 3.2. For every finite binary algebra A,
C(A) = T(A).
Proof. The inclusion C(A) ⊆T(A) is exactly Theorem 2.5.
Conversely, let f ∈T(A). Then f is a composition of left and right translations, say
f = φk ◦· · · ◦φ1,
φi ∈{La, Ra : a ∈A}.
We construct a one-hole context Q[z] recursively. Set
Q0[z] = z.
If φi = La, define
Qi[z] = a ⋆Qi−1[z].
If φi = Ra, define
Qi[z] = Qi−1[z] ⋆a.
Then, by induction on i, the unary map induced by Qi[z] is
φi ◦· · · ◦φ1.
Hence Qk[z] induces f.
Finally, replace each constant occurring in Qk[z] by a fresh external variable, and let u be a
single variable in place of z. Under the external assignment sending those fresh variables back
to the chosen constants, the resulting subterm context map is exactly f. Therefore
f ∈C(A).
So T(A) ⊆C(A), and equality follows.
⋆
Thus the recursive bracketing process sees the whole translation monoid, and not merely a
distinguished subset of it.

Corollary 3.3. For every f ∈T(A), there exist an integer n ≥1, a bracketing t ∈Bn, and a
subterm occurrence u of t such that a recursive block of wt is equal to
f(wu).
In particular, every element of T(A) occurs as a recursive block map in some bracketing-
evaluation array.
Proof. Let f ∈T(A). By Proposition 3.2, there exist a bracketing t, a subterm occurrence
u of t, and an external assignment such that the associated context map is exactly f.
By
Corollary 2.6, the corresponding recursive block of wt is therefore
f(wu).
Hence f occurs as a recursive block map in the bracketing-evaluation array containing wt.
⋆
Corollary 3.4. Every nonempty ideal of T(A) and every J -class of T(A) contains an element
that occurs as a recursive block map in some bracketing-evaluation array.
Proof. Let X be either a nonempty ideal of T(A) or a J -class of T(A). Choose f ∈X. By
Corollary 3.3, the element f occurs as a recursive block map in some bracketing-evaluation
array. Therefore X contains such an element.
⋆
We now record the first semigroup-theoretic consequences.
Definition 3.5. For f ∈T(A), define the rank of f by
ρ(f) = |f(A)|.
Proposition 3.6. For all f, g, h ∈T(A),
ρ(g ◦f ◦h) ≤ρ(f).
Proof. Since h(A) ⊆A, we have
f(h(A)) ⊆f(A).
Applying g to both sides yields
g(f(h(A))) ⊆g(f(A)).
Hence
(g ◦f ◦h)(A) = g(f(h(A))) ⊆g(f(A)).
Therefore
ρ(g ◦f ◦h) = |(g ◦f ◦h)(A)| ≤|f(A)| = ρ(f).
⋆
Corollary 3.7. For each integer r with 1 ≤r ≤|A|, the set
Ir = {f ∈T(A) : ρ(f) ≤r}
is a two-sided ideal of T(A).
Proof. Let f ∈Ir and g, h ∈T(A). By Proposition 3.6,
ρ(gfh) ≤ρ(f) ≤r.
So gfh ∈Ir, proving that Ir is a two-sided ideal.

Proposition 3.12. There is no general theorem forcing deeper recursive levels to enter lower-
rank ideals of T(A).
Proof. Let A = G be a finite group, written multiplicatively, and let ⋆be the group multiplica-
tion. For each a ∈G, both La and Ra are permutations of G. Hence every element of T(G) is
a permutation, so every element has rank |G|. Thus T(G) has no proper nonempty rank ideal,
and no recursive block map can be forced into a lower-rank layer by increasing depth.
⋆
For the translation-monoid programme developed here, the basic structural questions are
resolved as follows. The realised context maps are exactly the elements of T(A), and hence
every element, every nonempty ideal, and every J -class of T(A) is realised by recursive block
maps. The rank filtration
Ir = {f ∈T(A) : ρ(f) ≤r}
consists of two-sided ideals, and the minimum-rank layer forms a minimal nonempty two-sided
ideal of T(A). Moreover,
f J g =⇒ρ(f) = ρ(g),
although the converse fails in general: equal rank need not determine the J -class. Finally, there
is no general eventual descent of recursive block maps into lower-rank ideals.
