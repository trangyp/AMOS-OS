Abstract
Let G = (V, E) be a finite connected graph with vertex set V and edge set E,
and let U(G) be the set of all ultrametric spaces (V, dl) generated by vertex
labelings l: V →R+. We prove that the inequality
|D(V )| ≤|E| + 1
holds for all (V, dl) ∈U(G), where D(V ) is the distance set of (V, dl). The
necessary and sufficient conditions under which the above inequality turns to
an equality are found. Moreover, we prove that each connected graph with
non-negative vertex labeling generates a pseudoultrametric space and find some
sufficient conditions under which this space is ultrametric.
Keywords: distance set, pseudoultrametric space, tree, ultrametric space, vertex
labeling.
MSC Classification: Primary 54E35. Secondary 05C05.

1 Introduction
The theory of ultrametric spaces is closely connected with problems of applied math-
ematics, physics, linguistics, psychology, and computer science, see, for example,
[1–7].
By studying the flows in networks, R. Gomory and T. Hu [8] deduced a statement
that can be formulated as follows: If (X, d) is a finite ultrametric space with distance
set
D(X) := {d(x, y) : x, y ∈X},
then the inequality
|D(X)| ≤|X|
(1)
holds.
We will call inequality (1) the Gomory–Hu inequality.
Definition 1.1 A finite ultrametric space (X, d) is said to be a GH-space if the equality
|D(X)| = |X| holds. In what follows we denote by GH the class of all GH-spaces.
Two descriptions of the class GH were obtained in terms of the representing trees
of finite ultrametric spaces and, respectively, so-called diametrical graphs of such
spaces (see [9], Theorem 2.3 and Theorem 3.1). A criterion of (X, d) ∈GH in terms of
weighted Hamiltonian cycles and weighted Hamiltonian paths are found in Theorem
2.5 of paper [10]. The number of non-isometric spaces (X, d) ∈GH are also calculated
in [10].
The finite ultrametric spaces (X, d) with |X| ≥3 for which every self-isometry
X →X has at least |X|−2 fixed points give us an interesting example of GH-spaces.
(See Theorem 2.3 of [9] and Theorem 3.4 of [11].)
At the present paper we investigate the ultrametric spaces generated by connected
graphs G with given vertex labelings l : V (G) →R+.
The ultrametric spaces generated by labeled trees were introduced in [12]. Some
results describing the metric and topological properties of such spaces can be found
in [13–20].
The present paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we collect together some
definitions and facts related to ultrametric spaces and graphs.
The main results of the paper are presented in Section 3. Theorem 3.3, the
first theorem of Section 3, shows that every connected labeled graph G generates
a pseudoultrametric on V (G). Moreover, we show that this pseudoultrametric is an
ultrametric if G is locally finite. The necessary and sufficient conditions under which
an ultrametric space generated by finite labeled tree belongs to the class GH are given
in Theorem 3.8. Theorem 3.10 contains a partial generalization of Theorem 3.8 to the
case of ultrametric spaces generated by arbitrary finite connected labeled graphs. In
Proposition 3.12 we prove that every finite connected labeled graph G(l) generates
a GH-space for some suitable vertex labeling l. Example 3.13 describes the vertex
labelings on complete finite graphs which generate GH-spaces.
The final Section 4 contains new conjectures and some open problems.

2 Preliminaries
Recall some necessary definitions from the theory of metric spaces and the graph
theory.
Let X be a non-empty set. An ultrametric on X is a function d : X × X →R+,
R+ := [0, ∞), such that for all x, y, z ∈X:
(i) d(x, y) = d(y, x),
(ii) d(x, y) = 0 ⇐⇒x = y,
(iii) d(x, y) ≤max{d(x, z), d(z, y)}.
Inequality (iii) is often called the strong triangle inequality.
If we replace condition (ii) by the weaker condition d(x, x) = 0, then d is called a
pseudoultrametric on X.
Just as in the case of an ultrametric space, we introduce the distance set D(X) of
a pseudoultrametric space (X, d) as
D(X) := {d(x, y) : x, y ∈X}.
The following proposition directly follows from Theorem 14 in Chapter 4 of book
[21].
Proposition 2.1 Let (X, d) be a pseudoultrametric space. Then there exists Y ⊆X such
that the function
ρ := d|Y ×Y
is an ultrametric on Y and the equality
D(X) = D(Y )
holds, where D(X) and, respectively, D(Y ) are the distance sets of the pseudoultrametric
space (X, d) and the ultrametric space (Y, ρ).
Let us turn now to the graph theory. Recall that a graph is a pair (V, E) consisting
of a non-empty set V and a (possibly empty) set E whose elements are unordered
pairs of distinct points from V . For a graph G = (V, E), the sets V = V (G) and
E = E(G) are called the set of vertices and the set of edges, respectively. If x and y
are vertices of G and {x, y} ∈E(G), then we say that x and y are adjacent in G. A
graph G is said to be empty if the equality E(G) = ∅holds. A graph H is called a
subgraph of a graph G, H ⊆G, if the inclusions
V (H) ⊆V (G)
and

4 Conclusion — Expected Results
We believe that the following hypothesis is correct.
Conjecture 4.1 Let i = 1, 2 and Ti = Ti(li) be finite labeled trees with non-degenerate
labelings li : V (Ti) →R+. If (V (T1), dl1) and (V (T2), dl2) are GH-spaces, then the following
statements are equivalent:
(i) The equality
D(V (T1)) = D(V (T2))
holds, where D(V (T1)) and D(V (T2)) are the distance set of the space (V (T1), dl1) and
(V (T2), dl2) respectively.
(ii) The spaces (V (T1), dl1) and (V (T2), dl2) are isometric.
We note that the implication (ii) ⇒(i) is evidently true and the validity of (i) ⇒
(ii) follows from the strong triangle inequality if T1 and T2 contain at most three
vertices.
The following problem naturally arises.
Problem 4.2 Describe, up to isometry, all finite GH-spaces generated by labeled trees.
It was shown in Theorem 3.3 that every locally finite connected tree with non-
degenerate labeling generates an ultrametric space.
Conjecture 4.3 The following statements are equivalent for every connected graph G:
(i) The ultrametric space (V (G), dl) is discrete for every non-degenerate labeling l : V (G) →
R+.
(ii) The graph G is locally finite.
Recall that an ultrametric space (X, d) is called discrete if for every p ∈X there
exists ε > 0 such that
d(p, x) > ε
for every x ∈X \ {p}.
We note that Conjecture 4.3 is valid if G is a tree (see Corollary 5 in [15]).
In Example 3.7 we describe a connected graph H that is not locally finite but
generates an ultrametric for each non-degenerate labeling l : V (H) →R+.
Problem 4.4 Describe, up to graph isomorphism, all connected graphs G which generate
ultrametric spaces for each non-degenerate labeling l : V (G) →R+.
Conjecture 4.5 Let G be a connected graph. Then the following statements are equivalent:
(i) There exists a non-degenerate labeling l : V (G) →R+ such that the pseudoultrametric
space (V (G), dl) is not ultrametric.
