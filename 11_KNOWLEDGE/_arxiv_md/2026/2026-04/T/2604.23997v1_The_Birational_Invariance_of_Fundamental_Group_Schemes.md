1. Introduction
Fundamental group schemes provide an algebro-geometric refinement of the classical fundamental
group. For a connected scheme X proper over a field k endowed with a rational point x ∈X(k), a neutral
Tannakian subcategory of Vect(X) determines an affine k-group scheme through the fibre functor at x.
In this way, different classes of vector bundles give rise to different fundamental group schemes. This
Tannakian formalism is effective in positive characteristic and over non-algebraically closed fields.
The fundamental group schemes considered in this paper are the following. Nori introduced the funda-
mental group scheme πN(X, x), defined as the Tannaka dual of the category of essentially finite bundles,
and also the unipotent fundamental group scheme πuni(X, x), attached to the category of unipotent vector
bundles [13]. In positive characteristic, Mehta and Subramanian introduced the local fundamental group
scheme πLoc(X, x) by means of Frobenius trivial vector bundles [12], Dos Santos introduced the stratified
fundamental group scheme πstr(X, x), defined as the Tannaka dual of the category of stratified bundles
(equivalently, F-divisible bundles) [6], Amrutiya and Biswas introduced the F-fundamental group scheme
πF (X, x) from the Tannakian subcategory generated by Frobenius finite bundles [2]. Langer introduced
the S-fundamental group scheme πS(X, x), defined by the category of numerically flat vector bundles
[9]. In characteristic zero, Otabe introduced the EN-fundamental group scheme πEN(X, x) through the
category of semi-essentially finite bundles [14]. More recently, Adroja and Amrutiya introduced the ex-
tended local fundamental group scheme πELoc(X, x) by using Frobenius semi-trivial vector bundles [1].
The ´etale fundamental group scheme π´et(X, x), associated with the category of ´etale trivializable vector
bundles, has its geometric origin in Grothendieck’s theory of finite ´etale coverings and the classical ´etale
fundamental group [7].
A basic problem is whether these fundamental group schemes are invariant under birational modifica-
tions. For the S-fundamental group scheme, Hogadi and Mehta proved birational invariance for smooth
projective varieties over an algebraically closed field [8]. For the F-fundamental group scheme, Amrutiya
proved birational invariance for smooth projective varieties over a perfect field of positive characteristic
[3]. Adroja and Amrutiya also obtained birational invariance results for the local, Nori and extended
Nori fundamental group schemes [1]. The aim of this paper is to establish a uniform Tannakian criterion
for a birational morphism to induce an isomorphism on the corresponding fundamental group schemes,
and then apply this criterion to prove birational invariance for all the fundamental group schemes listed
above over an arbitrary perfect field.

Theorem 1.1 (Corollary 3.3). Let k be a field, f : X →Y a birational morphism of integral schemes
proper over k with Y normal, x ∈X(k) lying over y ∈Y (k). Then the following conditions are equivalent:
(1) f ∗induces an isomorphism π(CX, x) ∼= π(CY , y).
(2) f ∗and f∗are quasi-inverse exact tensor functors between CX and CY .
(3) For any E ∈CX, we have f∗E ∈CY .
In particular, when f : X →Y is a birational morphism (or birational map) between smooth projective
varieties over k, using this theorem we can prove the birational invariance of certain fundamental group
schemes.
Theorem 1.2 (Theorem 4.7 and Corollary 4.8). Let k be a perfect field, f : X 99K Y be a birational map
between smooth projective varieties over k, x ∈X(k) lying over y ∈Y (k). Then there exists a natural
isomorphism π∗(X, x) ∼= π∗(Y, y) for any ∗∈{S, N, EN, F, EF, Loc, ELoc, ´et, E´et, uni}. Moreover, if f
is a birational morphism, then the induced homomorphism πstr(X, x) →πstr(Y, y) is also an isomorphism.
The proof proceeds in two steps. In Section 3 we establish the general criterion above for a birational
morphism to induce an isomorphism of Tannaka group schemes. In Section 4 we verify its hypotheses for
the Tannakian categories defining the S, Nori, extended Nori, F, extended F, local, extended local, ´etale,
extended ´etale and unipotent fundamental group schemes. For numerically flat vector bundles, the key
input is the base change result and the argument of Hogadi–Mehta [8]. The remaining cases are then
deduced from the numerically flat case and from the defining properties of the corresponding Tannakian
subcategories and their saturation behaviors.
