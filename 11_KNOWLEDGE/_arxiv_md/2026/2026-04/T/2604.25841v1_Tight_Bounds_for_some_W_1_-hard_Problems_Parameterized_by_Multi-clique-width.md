Abstract
In this work we contribute to the study of the fine-grained complexity of problems parameterized by
multi-clique-width, which was initiated by Fürer [ITCS 2017] and pursued further by Chekan and
Kratsch [MFCS 2023]. Multi-clique-width is a parameter defined analogously to clique-width but
every vertex is allowed to hold multiple labels simultaneously. This parameter is upper-bounded by
both clique-width and treewidth (plus a constant), hence it generalizes both of them without an
exponential blow-up. Conversely, graphs of multi-clique-width k have clique-width at most 2k, and
there exist graphs with clique-width at least 2Ω(k). Thus, while the two parameters are functionally
equivalent, the fine-grained complexity of problems may differ relative to them.
As our first and main result we show that under ETH the Max Cut problem cannot be solved in
time n2o(k) ·f(k) on graphs of multi-clique-width k for any computable function f. For clique-width k
an nO(k) algorithm by Fomin et al. [SIAM J. Comput. 2014] is tight under ETH. This makes Max
Cut the first known problem for which the tight running times differ for parameterization by
clique-width and multi-clique-width and it contributes to the short list of known lower bounds
of form n2o(k) · f(k). As our second contribution we show that Hamiltonian Cycle and Edge
Dominating Set can be solved in time nO(k) on graphs of multi-clique-width k matching the
tight running time for clique-width. These results answer three questions left open by Chekan and
Kratsch [MFCS 2023].

Introduction
The framework of parameterized complexity allows a more detailed study of computational
complexity by expressing problem complexity as a function of both input size n and one or
more parameters. A problem with parameter k is fixed-parameter tractable (FPT) if it can
be solved in time f(k)·nO(1), and slice-wise polynomial (XP) if it can be solved in time ng(k).
While these definitions allow any dependence on k, it is natural to seek to make this as low
as possible. Moreover, there has been much research activity on fine-grained (parameterized)
complexity, that is to find FPT and XP algorithms with optimal dependence on k subject to
hypotheses such as the (Strong) Exponential-Time Hypothesis (ETH and SETH).1
This has been most successful relative to structural parameters of graphs such as
treewidth (tw) and clique-width. For example, many standard problems (e.g., Vertex
Cover, Dominating Set, 3-Coloring) cannot be solved in subexponential FPT time,
i.e., in 2o(tw)nO(1), under ETH [62, 56]. On the other hand, for these problems standard
dynamic-programming algorithms on tree decompositions achieve those bounds implying their
tightness. Similar single-exponential tightness results for treewidth were shown for several
problems, e.g., Feedback Vertex Set, Hamiltonian Cycle, Treewidth [4, 16, 10].
The results of this flavor are not restricted to single-exponential running times. It was
shown that for some problems (e.g., Planar Vertex Cover, Planar Hamiltonian
Cycle) 2Θ(√tw)nO(1) is optimal [56], for some other problems (e.g., Vertex Disjoint Paths,
Chromatic Number) 2Θ(tw log tw)nO(1) is optimal [58, 16, 38, 21, 50], and sometimes (e.g.,
for Metric Dimension, Geodetic Set) even double-exponential running time 22Θ(tw)nO(1)
is required [59, 51, 3, 29, 37]. ETH was also used to prove some tight bounds for XP
algorithms for W[1]-hard problems. For example, List Coloring and CSP (with the
treewidth of the primal graph) cannot be solved in time no(tw)f(tw) for any computable
function f [23].
For problems solvable in single-exponential time, even finer bounds can be achieved under
the stronger hypothesis, namely SETH. This line of research was initiated by Lokshtanov et
al. [57] who showed that for several standard problems (e.g., Independent Set, Dominating
Set, Odd Cycle Transversal), folklore dynamic-programming algorithms on tree
decompositions are optimal under SETH. For instance, Independent Set cannot be
solved in time (2−ε)twnO(1) for any ε > 0 unless SETH fails. Since then, many results of this
flavor were shown for treewidth (see e.g., [64, 15, 60, 16, 17, 20, 26, 53, 21, 25, 24, 33, 34, 55]).
One of the limitations of treewidth is that only sparse graphs have bounded treewidth,
more precisely, a graph of treewidth k with n vertices contains at most kn edges. To overcome
this limitation, a variety of parameters generalizing treewidth was studied, most importantly
clique-width (cw). The clique-width of a graph is at most k if it can be constructed using
at most k labels where the operations, informally speaking, are such that vertices assigned
the same label may only get the same neighbors in the future, and relabeling is possible
to allow reusing the labels. For clique-width, many (S)ETH-tight results are also known
(e.g., [45, 5, 27, 14, 28, 1, 46, 41, 8, 32, 9, 55]). Even though bounded clique-width generalizes
bounded treewidth, an exponential blow-up is necessary sometimes: there exist graphs of
treewidth k and clique-width 2Θ(k) [13]. Thus, being FPT or XP relative to clique-width
transfers to parameter treewidth, but the same is not true for fine-grained bounds.
With this motivation, Martin Fürer introduced the two parameters fusion-width (fw) [30]

Fusion-width generalizes clique-width by allowing an additional operation which merges
all vertices of a certain label, while multi-clique-width essentially uses the same operations
as clique-width but it allows every vertex to hold multiple labels simultaneously. Even
though the three parameters are functionally equivalent [30, 31], in the context of fine-grained
complexity, the precise choice of the parameter matters. Fürer [30, 31] initiated the study of
the complexity of problems parameterized by these two parameters by considering variants
of the Independent Set problem. Chekan and Kratsch [11] continued this line of research
by first, establishing the relation mcw ⩽fw + 1 between the two parameters.
Second,
they showed that several problems (e.g., q-Coloring, Chromatic Number, Connected
Vertex Cover) can be solved in the same running time when parameterized by clique-
width and its generalization multi-clique-width. The existing matching lower bounds for
clique-width then imply the tightness of their algorithms under (S)ETH. They left open
though whether Hamiltonian Cycle, Max Cut, and Edge Dominating Set can also be
solved in the same running time as for clique-width. For those problems, they only managed
to achieve the same running time for the weaker parameter fusion-width.
In this work, we answer these three questions. First, we show that Max Cut, known to
be solvable in nO(k) for clique-width, cannot be solved in time n2o(k) ·f(k) for any computable
function f for multi-clique-width unless ETH fails. An algorithm with matching running
time can be derived from the nO(k) algorithm for clique-width by Fomin et al. [27] using
the fact that a multi-k-expression can trivially be transformed into a 2k-expression [31].
This makes Max Cut the first and only problem known to separate clique-width and
multi-clique-width in terms of fine-grained parameterized complexity of problems. Also
it contributes to the short list of ETH-based lower bounds of form n2o(k): to the best of
our knowledge, for structural graph parameters such a lower bound is only known for the
Chromatic Number, b-Coloring, and Fall Coloring problems parameterized by clique-
width (and therefore, of course, also for smaller parameters) [28, 43]. Our construction relies
on cuts forming anti-matchings. Crucially, we find out that “paths of anti-matchings” have
logarithmic multi-clique-width leading to the desired lower bound.
▶Theorem 1. Unless ETH fails, there is no algorithm solving Max Cut in time n2o(k) ·f(k)
when the input graph G on n vertices is provided with a multi-k-expression of G for any
computable function f.
Second, we show that Hamiltonian Cycle and Edge Dominating Set can both be
solved in time nO(k) for multi-clique-width, i.e., in the same running time as for clique-width,
matching the lower bounds by Fomin et al. [27, 28].
▶Theorem 2. Given a graph G on n vertices and a multi-k-expression of G, in time nO(k)
we can decide whether G admits a Hamiltonian cycle.
▶Theorem 3. Given a graph G on n vertices, a multi-k-expression of G, and integer size,
in time nO(k) we can decide whether G admits an edge dominating set of size at most size.
To obtain those algorithms, we rely on the fact that for every vertex, only a constant
number of labels can participate in the creation of incident edges used by a solution.

Related work
(S)ETH-tight bounds have also been actively studied for various further
structural parameters. For example, going below treewidth, size of the modulator to constant
treewidth and related parameters were considered [40, 19]. For cutwidth many standard
problems [44, 63, 36, 6], connectivity problems [7], and several more [60, 61, 35] were studied.
Parameterizations by vertex-cover number [51, 18, 22, 50, 54], treedepth [53, 39, 50], vertex
integrity [52], and feedback-vertex-set number [61] were also studied. As for parameters
for dense graphs, some results are known for rank-width [2] and modular-treewidth [42].
Recently, Lampis [48, 49, 47] also initiated a line of research devoted to conjectures weaker
than SETH from which the same tight bounds follow, for instance, pw-SETH.

Lower bound for Max Cut
In the Max Cut problem, given a graph G and a value cut, we are asked whether there is a
partition (V1, V2) of G satisfying |EG(V1, V2)| ⩾cut. In this section, we prove the following.
▶Theorem 1. Unless ETH fails, there is no algorithm solving Max Cut in time n2o(k) ·f(k)
when the input graph G on n vertices is provided with a multi-k-expression of G for any
computable function f.
As a starting point of our reduction we use the Multicolored Independent Set problem.
In this problem, we are given a graph G = (U1 ∪· · · ∪Uk′, E) where U1, . . . , Uk′ are pairwise
disjoint equal-sized independent sets, each of size n′. The goal is to find a multicolored
set X—a set that contains exactly one vertex from each of the sets U1, . . . , Uk′—that is an
independent set in G. The following lower bound for this problem is well-known: 2
▶Theorem 5 (Theorem 5.2 in [56]). Unless ETH fails, there is no f(k′) · (n′)o(k′) time
algorithm for Multicolored Independent Set for any computable function f.
So we now fix an instance G = (U1 ∪· · · ∪Uk′, E) of Multicolored Independent
Set. We may assume that n′ > 1 holds as the instance is trivial otherwise. We define the
value n = n′ −1. In the following, we introduce some basic gadgets used in our reduction,
then we present a high-level overview of the reduction itself, after that we formalize the
construction, and finally we prove its correctness.

Algorithm for Hamiltonian Cycle
A Hamiltonian cycle in a graph G is a cycle visiting each vertex of G precisely once. In
the Hamiltonian Cycle problem, given a graph G, we are asked whether G contains a
Hamiltonian cycle.
In this section, we show that Hamiltonian Cycle can be solved in time nO(k) on
graphs provided with multi-k-expressions. The crux behind the algorithm can be sketched as
follows. A partial solution of Hamiltonian Cycle is usually a path packing of a subgraph
created in the expression so far. Next, every vertex is incident with precisely two edges in
any Hamiltonian cycle of the final graph G. So for every endpoint of a path in such a path
packing, it suffices to guess one label “responsible” for the creation of the missing incident
edge of the sought Hamiltonian cycle later. Here, for a path consisting of a single vertex, we
treat this vertex as two independent endpoints. With this idea in hand, we are able to adapt
the algorithm by Bergougnoux et al. [1] working on clique-expressions to multi-expressions.
Let us remind the reader that clique-expressions are essentially multi-expressions where
no vertex holds multiple labels simultaneously. To solve the problem, Bergougnoux et
al. [1] study the unique auxiliary multigraph of a partial solution, i.e., a path packing of
the current graph. In this multigraph, the vertex set is equal to the label set [k] of the
provided clique-expression. And for every path, say with endpoints u and v, of the path
packing, there is one edge in this multigraph between the labels of u and v—this edge may,
in general, be a loop. Even though the number of possible auxiliary multigraphs can be as
large as nΘ(k2)—indeed, for every pair of labels the multiplicity of the corresponding edge
can vary between 0 and n—Bergougnoux et al. [1] apply the technique of representative sets
to argue that what really matters about such an auxiliary multigraph is its degree sequence,
i.e., the degree of every label, and the partition of labels into connected components. This
reduces the number of relevant partial solutions to be at most nO(k) · kO(k) = nO(k).

multiple auxiliary graphs. Namely, for every path and each of its endpoints, any of its up

on this walk, in particular, the first and the last edge of this walk have different colors. Note
that if C admits a red-blue Eulerian trail, then it contains at least one blue and at least one
red edge. In our applications, red edges will represent paths of partial solutions created so
far while blue edges correspond to hypothetic paths possibly created later in the expression.
For two multigraphs A and B, we use A⊎B to denote the edge-disjoint union of A and B,
i.e., the multigraph such that for all labels i, j ∈[k], the multiplicity of {i, j} in A ⊎B is
equal to the sum of the multiplicities of {i, j} in A and B.
Now let A and B be two families of multigraphs such that the edges of those multigraphs
are colored red. We write A ≲B if for every multigraph M on the vertex set [k] whose
edges are colored blue, the following holds: if there is a multigraph B ∈B such that B ⊎M
admits a red-blue Eulerian trail, then there also exists a multigraph A ∈A such that A ⊎M
admits a red-blue Eulerian trail. In this case, we also say that the family A represents the
family B. Observe that ≲is a transitive relation. Bergougnoux et al. [1] showed that the

of the same graph [31], the lower bound transfers to multi-clique-width showing that our
algorithm is tight under ETH.
▶Theorem 39. Let G be an n-vertex graph given together with a multi-k-expression of G
and let size be an integer. Then the Edge Dominating Set problem cannot be solved in
time f(k) · no(k) for any computable function f unless the ETH fails.
6
Conclusion
We have answered three open questions left by Chekan and Kratsch [11] about the fine-
grained complexity of Max Cut, Hamiltonian Cycle, and Edge Dominating Set with
respect to multi-clique-width. For Max Cut we ruled out time n2o(k) assuming ETH, while a
matching algorithm can be obtained by using that multi-clique-width k implies clique-width
is at most 2k. In contrast, for Hamiltonian Cycle and Edge Dominating Set we
were able to give algorithms with the same time bound nO(k) as for clique-width, which
matches known lower bounds that extend to multi-clique-width. It would be interesting
to study further problems that are XP (but likely not FPT) and have tight bounds with
respect to clique-width and to determine such tight bounds relative to multi-clique-width and
fusion-width. Are there problems that separate two or even all three of these parameters?
The same question can also be posed for problems that are FPT for clique-width. So
far, for a small selection of problems, the same tight bounds were obtained for all three
parameters [30, 31, 11]. It would similarly be interesting to find separations between them.
The fact that multi-clique-width generalizes treewidth (and clique-width) with only
a constant increase in value arguably makes it an attractive parameter for fine-grained
parameterized complexity. This would be much strengthened if there were a better way to
approximate it, say in FPT time. So far, only a doubly-exponential approximation follows
from its relation to clique-with. Can this be improved?
