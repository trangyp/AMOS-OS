Abstract. Let us consider a sphere Sn−1 of radius r in Rn, where we have fixed poles N
and S. Suppose that K is a set in Rn containing a translated copy of each meridian (that
is an Sn−2-sphere) of Sn−1. Then the Hausdorff dimension of K must be bigger than or
equal to n −1.
Given a circle C in R3 as the boundary of a two-dimensional planar disc D, i.e., C =
∂D, we define the normal direction of C to be the same of D.
Let S2(r) be a sphere of radius r > 0 in R3 to which we have assigned two diametri-
cally opposed points, N and S, as its North and South poles, respectively. And we have
coordinates such that N = (0, 0, +r), S = (0, 0, −r).
