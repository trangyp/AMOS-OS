Abstract
We develop the Ruelle transfer operator theory for Axiom A diffeomorphisms and
construct Sinai-Ruelle-Bowen measures, carrying the symbolic spectral results
of Part I [66] over to smooth dynamics through the Markov partition coding of
Part III [68]. This Part contains four Main Theorems. The first proves struc-
tural stability of Axiom A diffeomorphisms satisfying the strong transversality
condition, with an explicit H¨older exponent for the conjugating homeomorphism
in terms of the hyperbolicity data, refining the classical results of Robbin and
Robinson. The second establishes quasi-compactness of the transfer operator on
H¨older spaces with a quantitative spectral gap bound; as consequences we obtain
exponential decay of correlations with explicit rate, the central limit theorem
for H¨older observables via the Nagaev-Guivarc’h spectral perturbation method,
real-analyticity of the pressure, and meromorphic continuation of the Ruelle
dynamical zeta function. The third constructs SRB measures on mixing basic
sets as the unique equilibrium states for the geometric potential, proves abso-
lute continuity of the unstable foliation, and derives an explicit product-formula
for the conditional densities along unstable manifolds. The fourth establishes
the Pesin entropy formula identifying the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy of the SRB
measure with the sum of its positive Lyapunov exponents. The Gibbs Equiv-
alence Theorem, assembling the symbolic, variational, spectral, and geometric
characterizations of the equilibrium state on a mixing basic set, follows from
these four Main Theorems together with the imported results of Parts I and III
[66, 68]. This Part constitutes Part IV of a six-part series on the thermodynamic
formalism for hyperbolic dynamical systems.

1 Introduction
This Part applies the spectral theory of Part I [66] and the coding map of Part III [68]
to develop the transfer operator theory and construct Sinai-Ruelle-Bowen measures
for Axiom A diffeomorphisms. This Part contains four Main Theorems:
1. Main Theorem 3.2 (Structural Stability of Axiom A Diffeomorphisms): every
Axiom A diffeomorphism satisfying strong transversality is structurally stable,
with a H¨older-continuous conjugating homeomorphism whose exponent is computed
explicitly from the hyperbolicity data. This refines the results of Robbin [53] and
Robinson [55].
2. Main Theorem 4.3 (Quasi-compactness and Spectral Gap of the Transfer Operator):
the Ruelle transfer operator Lϕ acting on Cα(Ω) is quasi-compact, with simple dom-
inant eigenvalue eP (ϕ) and essential spectral radius bounded explicitly in terms of
the hyperbolicity data. Exponential decay of correlations, the central limit theorem,
and real-analyticity of the pressure follow from this spectral structure.
3. Main Theorem 5.3 (Existence and Characterization of SRB Measures): on each
mixing basic set, the SRB measure exists uniquely and coincides with the equi-
librium state for the geometric potential ϕu = −log | det Df|Eu|, and has explicit
conditional densities on unstable manifolds with respect to the Riemannian measure.
4. Main Theorem 5.7 (Pesin Entropy Formula): the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy of the
SRB measure equals the sum of its positive Lyapunov exponents.
Together with the three theorems imported from Parts I [66] and III [68] (Theo-
rems 2.1, 2.2, 2.3), these four Main Theorems yield the Gibbs Equivalence Theorem,
assembling characterizations of Gibbs measures individually established by Sinai [64],
Ruelle [58], and Bowen [14] into a single quantitative statement.
The Gibbs Equivalence Theorem (Section 5) states that on each mixing basic
set, the symbolic Gibbs measure (transferred via the coding map), the variational
equilibrium state, the eigenmeasure of the transfer operator, and the SRB measure
for the geometric potential all coincide. The individual equivalences are known: the
symbolic Gibbs property and the eigenmeasure characterization are in Bowen [14,
Theorems 1.4 and 1.7]; the SRB characterization as equilibrium state for the geometric
potential is in Bowen-Ruelle [16]; and the equivalence of Gibbs and equilibrium states
under expansiveness and specification is in Haydn-Ruelle [33]. Our contribution is the
self-contained proof with explicit constants, not the individual characterizations. The
spectral gap bound gap(Lϕ) ≥α log λ−1 (Section 4) holds on the standard H¨older
space Cα(Λ); sharper bounds on anisotropic Banach spaces are available from Gou¨ezel-
Liverani [31] but require a more sophisticated functional setting.

The transfer operator approach we develop here obtains the spectral gap via the
Birkhoff cone contraction of Part I [66]. A parallel functional-analytic program reaches
the same conclusions through direct arguments on specifically-designed Banach spaces.
Liverani [42] proved exponential decay of correlations for smooth uniformly hyperbolic
systems using Birkhoff cone contraction, and Liverani [43] organized the functional-
analytic approach to invariant measures for piecewise hyperbolic systems. Keller and
Liverani [41] proved stability of isolated eigenvalues of transfer operators under per-
turbation, which is the spectral input required for the Nagaev-Guivarc’h method used
in our CLT argument; the present Part tracks the stability constants in the Keller-
Liverani theorem explicitly in terms of the hyperbolicity data, which is not done in
their original statement. The anisotropic Banach space approach of Gou¨ezel-Liverani
[31], already mentioned, extends these methods to general Anosov systems.
The construction of SRB measures beyond uniform hyperbolicity has been a central

extended the Young tower construction to surface diffeomorphisms with non-uniform
hyperbolicity and proved polynomial decay of correlations for the SRB measure. Luz-
zatto [44] gives a comprehensive Handbook survey of the non-uniformly expanding
theory to which our uniformly hyperbolic construction provides the anchor case with
explicit constants. Viana and Oliveira [72], Chapter 12, Section 12.3, proved expo-

with a homeomorphism between them. The construction uses the cone criterion from

3.2 Structural Stability
Persistence provides local conjugacies on each basic set; in this subsection we assemble
them into a global topological conjugacy between f and a nearby diffeomorphism g,
under the strong transversality condition. This is Main Theorem 3.2. The result is used
in Section 5 to show that SRB measures vary continuously with the diffeomorphism.
Definition 3.1 (Structural Stability) A diffeomorphism f : M →M is structurally stable
if there exists ε > 0 such that every diffeomorphism g with ∥f −g∥C1 < ε is topologically
conjugate to f: there exists a homeomorphism h : M →M with h ◦f = g ◦h.

7 Conclusion
This Part transfers the symbolic spectral theory of Part I [66] and the variational
theory of Part II [67] to smooth Axiom A dynamics through the coding map of
Part III [68], with four Main Theorems proved with explicit constants throughout.
Main Theorem 3.2 establishes structural stability of Axiom A diffeomorphisms under
the strong transversality condition, with a quantitative H¨older exponent for the con-
jugating homeomorphism computed in terms of the hyperbolicity data, refining the
classical results of Robbin [53] and Robinson [55]. Main Theorem 4.3 establishes quasi-
compactness of the Ruelle transfer operator on H¨older spaces, with a quantitative
spectral gap bound, and this single spectral statement yields exponential decay of
correlations with explicit rate, the central limit theorem via the Nagaev-Guivarc’h
spectral perturbation method, real-analyticity of the pressure with explicit first- and
second-derivative formulas, and meromorphic continuation of the Ruelle dynamical
zeta function. Main Theorem 5.3 constructs the SRB measure on each mixing basic set
as the unique equilibrium state for the geometric potential, proves absolute continuity
of the unstable foliation, and produces an explicit product-formula for the conditional
densities along unstable manifolds. Main Theorem 5.7 establishes the Pesin entropy
formula, identifying the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy of the SRB measure with the sum
of its positive Lyapunov exponents.
Together with the three imported results of Parts I and III [66, 68] (Theorems 2.1,
2.2, and 2.3), these four Main Theorems yield the Gibbs Equivalence Theorem: on
each mixing basic set, the symbolic Gibbs measure transported via the coding map,
the variational equilibrium state, the eigenmeasure of the transfer operator, and the
SRB measure for the geometric potential all coincide as a single probability measure
characterized simultaneously by four different properties. The individual equivalences
are due to Sinai [64], Ruelle [58], Bowen [14], and Haydn-Ruelle [33]; our contribution
is the self-contained assembly with explicit constants. All constants in this Part are
expressed in terms of the contraction rate λ, the H¨older exponent α of the potential,
the dimension d of the manifold, the norms of Df and Df−1, and the diameter of the
Markov partition inherited from Part III [68], so the full chain of estimates can be
tracked through subsequent Parts.

logarithm, local limit theorems, and large deviations principles with explicit rate
functions. Part VI [70] develops multifractal analysis of pointwise-dimension and

Open Problems
Spectral gap on anisotropic spaces. Our bound gap(Lϕ) ≥α log λ−1 holds
on Cα(Λ). The optimal spectral gap on anisotropic Banach spaces, as studied by
Gou¨ezel-Liverani [31], may be larger. Can the explicit constants of this Part be
transferred to that setting?
Partially hyperbolic extensions. The extension of thermodynamic formalism
to partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms is a major open direction. The coding and
spectral techniques of this series can be adapted under appropriate conditions (dom-
inated splitting, center bunching, accessibility), but a full treatment requires the
machinery of Burns-Wilkinson [19] and Hirsch et al. [36].
SRB measures beyond uniform hyperbolicity. The SRB construction uses
uniform hyperbolicity through absolute continuity of the unstable foliation. For
partially hyperbolic systems with mostly contracting center (Bonatti et al. [9]),
SRB measures exist but explicit conditional density formulas are not available.
Non-uniformly hyperbolic systems. Young’s tower construction [75, 76]
extends several of our results to systems with non-uniform hyperbolicity (H´enon
maps, certain unimodal maps). Can the explicit spectral gap and SRB-measure
constants be computed in the tower setting?
Optimal H¨older exponent for the conjugacy. Our H¨older exponent γ =
log λ/(log λ −log ∥Dg∥∞) for the structural-stability conjugacy is explicit but not
known to be sharp. What is the best-possible exponent in terms of the hyperbolicity
data?
Computational aspects. The explicit spectral gap and conjugacy bounds in this
Part are formulas, not numbers. Can they be evaluated rigorously using interval
arithmetic for a specific Axiom A system?
