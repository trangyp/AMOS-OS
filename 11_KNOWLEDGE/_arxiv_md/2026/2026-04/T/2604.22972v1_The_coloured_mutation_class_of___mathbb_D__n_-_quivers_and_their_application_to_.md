THE COLOURED MUTATION CLASS OF Dn- QUIVERS AND THEIR
APPLICATION TO m-CLUSTER TILTED ALGEBRAS
VIVIANA GUBITOSI AND CLAUDIO QURESHI
Abstract. In this paper, we present an explicit and purely combinatorial characterization of the
m-coloured quivers that appear within the m-coloured mutation class of a quiver of type Dn. The
m-coloured mutation, as defined by Buan and Thomas in [6], generalises the well-known quiver
mutation introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky [9].
Consequently, we derive a comprehensive
description of the Gabriel quivers associated with m-cluster-tilted algebras of type Dn. Notably,
our characterization extends a result by Vatne, [16], which we recover when m = 1.
Introduction
For a given integer m ≥1, m-coloured quivers, or simply coloured quivers, were introduced by
Buan and Thomas in 2008 [6]. A coloured quiver Q is defined as a finite quiver where each arrow is
assigned a colour c from the set {0, . . . , m}, subject to the conditions that Q contains no loops and
satisfies two additional properties: monochromaticity and skew-symmetry. Notably, any acyclic
quiver Q can be interpreted as a coloured quiver by assigning to each arrow of Q the colour 0 and
subsequently adding an arrow of colour m in the reverse direction.
Buan and Thomas also defined an operation, termed coloured quiver mutation, on these coloured
quivers. They demonstrated that this operation is compatible with the mutation of m-cluster tilt-
ing objects. In the specific case of m = 1, the coloured mutation of the induced coloured quiver
corresponds to the mutation defined by Fomin and Zelevinsky in [9], commonly referred to as FZ-
mutation. Since its introduction, quiver mutation has been the subject of extensive research, as
evidenced by works such as [2, 3, 4, 5]. The problem of characterizing FZ-mutation classes for
quivers of various types has been addressed by Vatne for type Dn [16], Bastian for type ˜An [1], and
Buan and Vatne for type An [7]. Notably, a comparable study focusing on coloured mutations has
only been conducted for type An in [10] and for type ˜An in [11].
Two coloured quivers, Q1 and Q2, are considered mutation equivalent if Q1 can be derived from
Q2 through a sequence of coloured mutations, and vice versa. An equivalence class defined by this
relation is termed a coloured mutation class. Torkildsen demonstrated in [14] that the coloured
mutation class of a connected acyclic quiver Q is finite if and only if Q is either of Dynkin or
extended Dynkin type, or possesses at most two vertices.
From an algebraic perspective, coloured quiver mutation provides insights into m-cluster tilted
algebras, specifically algebras of the form End(T), where T represents an m-cluster tilting object
