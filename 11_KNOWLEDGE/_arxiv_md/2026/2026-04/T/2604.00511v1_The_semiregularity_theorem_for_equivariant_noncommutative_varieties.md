Abstract. We generalize the classical semiregularity theorem of Buchweitz and Flenner
to the setting of noncommutative algebraic geometry, with group actions. This applies in
particular to twisted derived categories, in which case it answers a question of Markman and
streamlines part of his proof of the Hodge conjecture for abelian fourfolds. Along the way,
we prove that for many finite group actions on derived categories of varieties, the invariant
category is of geometric origin.

1. Introduction
A coherent sheaf E on a variety X is semiregular if the obstruction space Ext2(E, E) for
its deformations is controlled by Hodge theory, in the sense that the semiregularity map to
the Hochschild homology group HH−2(X) is injective. The classical semiregularity theorem
of Buchweitz and Flenner [BF03] says that a coherent sheaf E0 on the special fiber X0 of
a smooth proper family of complex varieties X →S can be deformed to the generic fiber,
provided its Chern character remains of Hodge type along S.
The goal of this paper is to prove a generalization of this theorem in which X is allowed
to be a “noncommutative variety”, i.e. a semiorthogonal component of the derived category
of a smooth proper family of varieties, and the condition on E0 is relaxed to “equivariant
semiregularity” in the presence of a suitable group action. For simplicitly, we first state our
result in the motivating case of twisted derived categories, without group actions.

can be used to simplify some of the arguments in [Mar25], which was the main motivation for
this work; we explain this in §1.1 below, after describing some other relevant results.
Theorem 1.1 is a special case of a much more general semiregularity theorem (Theorem 6.1)
proved in the body of the paper, where the role of X is replaced by a smooth proper S-linear
category C of geometric origin, meaning that C admits a realization as a semiorthogonal com-
ponent of a smooth proper S-scheme. This also yields, for example, a version of Theorem 1.1
where the given complex E0 may itself be twisted. In turn, our general semiregularity theorem
is based on a smoothness result for the moduli space of objects in C at a semiregular point
(Theorem 4.1), which generalizes the smoothness of moduli spaces of objects in CY2 cate-
gories proved in [Per22] (see Remark 4.2(1)). The key ingredient in the proof of our smoothness
result is Pridham’s work on semiregularity [Pri24].
Finally, we prove an equivariant version of our general semiregularity result, extending the
scope of its applications. The semiregularity of an object E ∈C in a C-linear category C is a
strong condition which is difficult to check in practice. However, in the presence of a suitable
finite group of symmetries G acting on C, it turns out that a more tractable condition suffices.
Namely, we introduce two notions of equivariant semiregularity for an object E ∈C:
• E is G-semiregular if it admits a G-equivariant structure and the semiregularity
map Ext2(E, E) →HH−2(C) is injective when restricted to the G-invariant subspace
Ext2(E, E)G.
• E is weakly G-semiregular if it admits a G-equivariant structure such that the corre-
sponding object eE ∈CG of the invariant category is semiregular.
As the names suggest, G-semiregularity implies weak G-semiregularity (Lemma 2.7). In prac-
tice, these conditions may be significantly easier to verify than semiregularity, especially when
the symmetry group G is large.
In Theorem 6.3 we deduce a G-equivariant version of our semiregularity theorem, which
says that for suitable group actions, weak G-semiregularity has all of the same consequences as
ordinary semiregularity. The precise general statement is somewhat technical, but for abelian
varieties we obtain a clean statement:

1.1. Relation to Markman’s work. The key argument in Markman’s work [Mar25] can be
summarized as follows. He constructs a certain complex E0 ∈Dperf(X0) on the special fiber
of a family X/S of abelian sixfolds together with an algebraic class B0 ∈H2(X0, Q(1)) such
that:
(1) By construction, there exists an abelian sixfold Y0 (the square of the Jacobian of a
genus 3 curve), a coherent sheaf F0 on Y0 which is G-semiregular for a finite subgroup
G ⊂Y0 (acting by translations), and a derived equivalence Φ: Dperf(Y0) ≃Dperf(X0)
such that E0 = Φ(F0) (see [Mar25, Lemma 8.4.1]).
(2) The class w0 = exp(B0) · ch(E0) remains Hodge along S.
In these terms, the main result of [Mar25] boils down to proving that w0 remains algebraic
along S.
Markman’s version of the semiregularity theorem does not directly apply in the above
setting, because it requires the object E0 to be a coherent sheaf (whereas E0 above is a perfect
complex) and semiregular (not only G-semiregular). To surmount these issues, Markman
devises two clever solutions that occupy [Mar25, §9]. For the first issue, he shows that up to
a shift, E0 is the derived dual of a coherent sheaf A0 on X0. For the second, the idea is to
descend E0 (and hence A0) to another abelian sixfold X′
0 on which it becomes semiregular;
naively, one would like to take X′
0 to be the quotient of X0 by G, but G only naturally acts
on Dperf(X0), so this requires some maneuvering. The details of these arguments are ad hoc
and quite involved.
Our Theorem 1.2, on the other hand, applies directly in the situation of (1) and (2) above,
and thus renders such gymnastics unnecessary. Indeed, by a result of Rouquier [Rou11], the
identity component of the group of autoequivalences of the abelian variety X0 is identified
with X0 × X∨
0 . Hence the subgroup G ⊂Y0 translates via the equivalence Φ to a subgroup
G ⊂X0×X∨
0 acting on Dperf(X0) for which E0 is G-semiregular. Moreover Ext<0(E0, E0) = 0
because E0 is the image of a coherent sheaf under a derived equivalence. Therefore, we may
apply Theorem 1.2 to conclude that w0 remains algebraic along S.

1.2. Organization of the paper. In §2 we define (equivariant) semiregularity in our context.
In §3 we review some preliminaries about the Hodge theory of categories that are needed for
the formulation of our results. In §4 we prove our result on the smoothness of the moduli
space of objects at a semiregular point. In §5 we prove our geometrization results for group
actions on derived categories. Finally, in §6 we combine everything to prove our general
noncommutative semiregularity results, as well as Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 from above for twisted
derived categories.
1.3. Conventions. A variety over a field k is a scheme over k which is geometrically integral,
separated, and finite type over k. For a scheme S, Dqc(S) denotes the unbounded derived
category of quasi-coherent sheaves, and Dperf(S) ⊂Dqc(S) denotes the subcategory of perfect
complexes. All such categories are considered in the enhanced sense, as stable ∞-categories.
All functors are derived.
We freely use the language of “categories linear over a base” described in [Per19] and
summarized in [HP24, §4] and [Per22, §2]. Briefly, if S is a base scheme, then an S-linear
category C is a (suitably enhanced) triangulated category with an action of the monoidal
category Dperf(S) of perfect complexes on S. The key example of such a category is an S-
linear semiorthogonal component C ⊂Dperf(X), where X is an S-scheme.

consider an S-linear category C which supports a reasonable version of Hodge theory. Such
a theory was developed in [Per22] when C is a smooth proper S-linear category of geometric
origin, meaning that C admits an embedding as an S-linear semiorthogonal component into
Dperf(X) for a smooth proper S-scheme X.2

5. Geometrizing group actions on derived categories
Let C is a smooth proper S-linear category of geometric origin, where S is a variety
over a field of characteristic 0, and assume that C is equipped with an action by a finite
group G. In this setting, the invariant category CG is a smooth proper S-linear category
[BP23, Proposition 3.15], but it is unknown whether CG is necessarily of geometric origin,
even if C = Dperf(X). Our goal here is to explain that the situation is better when G acts
through the identity component of the group of autoequivalences, focusing on the case when
C is the (twisted) derived category of a variety.
The key observation, due to Olsson [Ols25] and explained to us by James Hotchkiss, is that
many autoequivalences of Dperf(X) can be lifted to automorphisms of a suitable gerbe. When
a group action on Dperf(X) lifts to an action on a gerbe in this way, the invariant category
is a semiorthogonal component of the quotient stack of the gerbe; in good cases, this implies
that the invariant category is of geometric origin.
