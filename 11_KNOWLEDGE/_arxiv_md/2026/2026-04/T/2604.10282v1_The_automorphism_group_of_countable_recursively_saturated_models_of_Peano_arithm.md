Abstract
In this paper, we extend the concept of a Lascar generic automorphism in the setting
of models of Peano arithmetic (PA) to the subgroup of the automorphism group of a
countable recursively saturated model M of PA that fixes pointwise a strong cut I of
M, denoted by (Aut(M))(I). Then, we prove that:
(1) (Aut(M))(I) has the small index property.
(2) The cofinality of (Aut(M))(I) is uncountable.
(3) Any nontrivial normal subgroup of (Aut(M))(I) is meagre in it. In particular, the
infinite cyclic group Z is not a homomorphic image of (Aut(M))(I).

Introduction
In early 1991, J. K. Truss introduced [22] the concept of a generic1 automorphism, aiming
to identify an element of a permutation group which ’almost’ intersects with all the finite
partial isomorphisms of the underlying set.
To be more precise, if G is a permutation
group endowed with a Polish topological space (i.e., a separable complete metric space), an
element g of G is called generic if its conjugacy class is comeagre in G—that is, its conjugacy
class is equal to the intersection of a countable family of open dense subsets of G. It is well-
established that the automorphism group of any structure M, denoted by Aut(M), forms
a topological group. Furthermore, when M is countable, Aut(M) is a Polish group [9]. In

Concurrently, D. Lascar examined the automorphism group of various structures in a series

of a generic automorphism introduced by Truss. Lascar’s notion is based on strong amal-
gamation properties and plays an important role in showing the small index property in
a variety of structures (e.g., see [5] or [16]); where a topological group of cardinality κ is
said to have the small index property if every subgroup whose index is less than κ is open
in the ground space. So, the small index property guarantees that the topological struc-
ture of a topological group can be reduced to its abstract group structure. Moreover, it
is intimately connected to the extent to which a structure can be recovered from its auto-
morphism groups. In [16], Lascar and S. Shelah showed that the automorphism group of
any uncountable saturated structure have the small index property. Then in [15], Lascar
modified their method to show that the automorphism group of every countable arithmeti-
cally saturated model of Peano arithmetic (PA) has the small index property; where an
arithmetically saturated model of PA is a recursively saturated model in which the standard
cut is strong (for precise definitions and details, see the Preliminaries section below).
In section 3 of this paper, we first generalize the method Lascar used in [15]. Then, we
show that the Lascar’s result on countable arithmetically saturated models of PA can be
generalized to every automorphism group of a countable recursively saturated model of PA
which pointwise stabilizes a strong cut of the ground model:
Theorem A. Suppose M is a countable recursively saturated model of PA and I is a strong
cut of M. Then the subgroup of Aut(M) which fixes I pointwise, denoted by (Aut(M))(I),
has the small index property.
Section 4 of this paper is devoted to some observations concerning the introduced notion of
a Lascar generic automorphism for (Aut(M))(I), which we call a Lascar I-generic automor-
phism. We begin by examining the set of fixed points of a Lascar I-generic automorphism.
Then, we generalize a theorem by R. Kaye [6] which demonstrates a Galois-like correspon-
dence between closed normal subgroups of the automorphism group of a countable recur-
sively saturated model of PA and its invariant cuts which are closed under exponentiation.
We show that if I is a strong cut of M, then Kaye’s result also holds for (Aut(M))(I).
As a consequence, we use this generalization along with the notion of Lascar I-generic
automorphisms to derive a property for the normal subgroups of (Aut(M))(I):
Theorem B. Suppose M is a countable recursively saturated model of PA and I is a strong
cut of M. Then for every normal subgroup N of (Aut(M))(I) either (Aut(M))(I) = N or
N is meagre in (Aut(M))(I).
In section 5, we investigate how (Aut(M))(I) can be covered by a chain of its proper
subgroups. In other words, we compute the cofinality of (Aut(M))(I); where the cofinality

Preliminaries
Throughout this paper, we mostly work in the language of Peano arithmetic, that is
LA := {0, 1, +, ., <}. For every language L extending LA, we denote PA(L) by PA∗. More-
over, we use M, K, L, and similar notations to refer to L-structures (for a given language
L) where the universes of these structures are represented by M, K, L, and etc.., respec-
tively. A given L-structure M is called recursively saturated if it realizes every recursive
type with a finite number of parameters from M. In [2], J. Barwise and J. Schlipf showed
that any countable model M of PA is recursively saturated iff it carries an inductive satis-
faction class; here an inductive satisfaction class S of M is a subset of M which contains
pairs ⟨φ, a⟩such that (1) M |= Form(φ), (2) (M; S) |= PA∗, and (3) (M; S) satisfies
Tarski’s inductive conditions for satisfaction (for a more precise definition see [7]). In [21],
C. Smory´nski by generalizing Barwise-Ressayre expandability result, proved that for every
countable recursively saturated model M of PA there exists some inductive satisfaction class
S such that (M; S) is also recursively saturated. Moreover, For every model M of PA, we
define the standard system of M as below:
SSy(M) := {X ∩N : X is definable in M}.
Theorem 2.1 (Smory´nski [21]). Every two recursively saturated models of PA are isomor-
phic iff they have the same theories and the same standard systems.
A model M is called arithmetically saturated if for every finite tuple ¯a ∈M, each arithmetic
type in tp(¯a) is realizable in M. It is well-known that a recursively saturated model M of
PA is arithmetically saturated iff the standard cut N is strong in M (e.g., see [23]); where
a strong cut is defined as follows: a cut I of a model M is called strong if for every coded
function f in M whose domain contains I there exists some e > I such that f(i) ∈I iff
f(i) < e for all i ∈I.
For every X ⊆M, by K(M; X) we mean the set of all elements of M which are definable
in M with parameters from X. If X = {a} for some a ∈M, we simply denote K(M; X)
by K(M; a).

As mentioned in the Introduction, the cofinality of a group G, denoted by cf(G), is the
least cardinal number κ such that G is the union of an increasing chain of κ many proper
subgroups. In [10], Kossak and Schmerl proved that for a countable recursively saturated
