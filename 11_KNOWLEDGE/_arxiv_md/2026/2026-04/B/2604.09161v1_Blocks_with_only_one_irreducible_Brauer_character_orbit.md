Abstract In this paper, we prove that a p-block with abelian defect group is inertial if it covers a
p-block of a normal subgroup of p-power index having only one irreducible Brauer character orbit.
Keywords block; irreducible Brauer character orbit; inertial

Introduction
Througuout this paper, let O be a complete discrete valuation ring with a field K of frac-
tions of characteristic 0 and with an algebraically closed residue field k of prime characteristic
p. In addition, we always assume that K is large enough for all finite groups considered below.
A Morita equivalence between two block algebras is basic if it is induced by an endoper-
mutation source bimodule (see [32]). Following [30], a block is called inertial if it is basically
Morita equivalent to a block with normal defect group.
Let G be a finite group and let b be a block of G over O, i.e. a primitive central idempotent
of OG. We denote by Aut(G) the automorphism group of G, by Aut(G)b the stabilizer of b
in Aut(G) and by IBr(b) the set of irreducible Brauer characters of b. If IBr(b) forms a single
orbit under the action of Aut(G)b, we say that IBr(b) is a single Aut(G)b-orbit.
The block b is nilpotent-covered if there exists a nilpotent block ˜b of a finite group ˜G such
that ˜G contains G as a normal subgroup and ˜b covers b. Nilpotent-covered blocks are inertial
(see [30]) and provide canonical examples of blocks with only one irreducible Brauer character
orbit. It is natural to ask whether blocks with only one irreducible Brauer character orbit
are inertial. In this paper, we try to answer this question in the abelian defect group case.
Actually, we prove the following theorem.
Theorem 1.1. Let ˜G be a finite group containing G as a normal subgroup of p-power index,
˜b a block of ˜G with abelian defect groups, and b a block of G covered by ˜b. If IBr(b) is a single
Aut(G)b-orbit, then ˜b is inertial.
However, in the case of nonabelian defect group, the problem above is not true in general.
