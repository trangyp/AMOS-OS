Abstract. Given a curve C that is a degree k cover C →P1 totally ramified at two
points p and q, we can seek to understand the space of degree d line bundles on C with
prescribed ramification at p and q. The corresponding subschemes of Picd(C) are called
transmission loci and are parameterized via elements of the (extended) k-affine symmetric
group eΣk. Transmission loci provide a refinement of the splitting loci that have recently
been extensively studied for k-gonal curves. Pflueger has conjectured analogues of the classic
Brill-Noether theorem should hold for transmission loci. In this paper we prove Pflueger’s
conjectures.
1. Introduction
A fundamental problem in algebraic geometry is to understand the space of maps from an
algebraic curve C to projective space and the study of this problem has led to the rich area
of Brill-Noether theory. Given the correspondence between maps C →Pr of degree d and
degree d line bundles L ∈Picd(C) along with an (r +1)-dimensional basepoint-free subspace
V ⊆H0(C, L) of global sections, the main object of study in classical Brill-Noether theory
is
W r
d (C) = {L ∈Picd(C) : h0(C, L) ≥r + 1}.
When C is a general curve of genus g, several papers from the 1980s give us insight into
the core geometry of W r
d (C). Griffiths−Harris [11] proved that W r
d (C) has the expected
dimension ρ(g, r, d) := g −(r + 1)(g + r −d); Gieseker [10] proved that W r

away from W r+1

These results together form the celebrated Brill-Noether theorem.
Curves arising in natural contexts are rarely general, often admitting explicit descriptions
due to special maps to projective space.
For example, when C is a k-gonal curve, the
covering map f : C →P1 is special when k < ⌊(g + 3)/2⌋, as the general Brill–Noether
theorem predicts W 1

of P1. The analysis is challenging because W r

class of f∗L, equivalently by its splitting type ⃗e ∈Zk [4, 13]. The splitting type refines the
data (r, d) and defines the splitting locus
W ⃗e(C) := {L ∈Picd(C) : f∗L ∼= OP1(⃗e) or a specialization thereof}.
Recently, E. Larson−H. Larson−Vogt [12] prove complete analogues of the general Brill-
Noether theorem for W ⃗e(C) for any general k-gonal curve.

In this paper, we study the Brill-Noether theory of covers of P1 totally ramified at two points.
The results about W ⃗e(C) proved in [12] extend to this setting, since their degeneration argu-
ment already uses such covers [12, Remark 2]. However, when the covering map f : C →P1
has two points p and q of total ramification, i.e., OC(kp) ∼= f ∗OP1(1) ∼= OC(kq), we can seek
to understand the more refined locus of degree d line bundles on C with prescribed ramifica-
tion at the special points p and q, i.e., line bundles with specified values of h0(C, L(ap + bq))
for all a, b ∈Z. By work of Pflueger [15], we can prescribe ramification at p and q via a
choice of a permutation τ in the (extended) k-affine symmetric group eΣk, which means that
τ is a bijection Z →Z such that τ(n + k) = τ(n) + k for all n. Such a permutation τ defines
a τ-transmission locus
W τ(C, p, q) = {L ∈Picd(C) : h0(C, L(ap + bq)) ≥#{n ≥−b : τ(n) ≤a} for all a, b ∈Z}.
Every splitting locus W ⃗e(C) is a transmission locus W τ(⃗e) for some permutation τ(⃗e) (since
a splitting type ⃗e can be prescribed using k inequalities for a ∈{−e1k, . . . , −ekk} and b = 0
above), but the converse is false. Indeed, up to a pre- and post-translation, τ(⃗e) is the same
as the permutation w(⃗e) constructed in [12, Thm. 1.4] (see Remark 2.5 for more details).
Studying W τ(C, p, q) thus leverages the full combinatorics of eΣk.
Pflueger [16] has conjectured that analogues of Brill-Noether theorem should hold for the
locus W τ(C, p, q). The expected codimension of W τ(C, p, q) in Picd(C) is invk(τ), the num-
ber of k-inversions in τ modulo k (Definition 2.4). Pflueger has shown that the expected
dimension serves as an upper bound for dim W τ(C, p, q) [16, Thm. 1.7]. We establish that
the expected dimension also serves as a lower bound by proving a new regeneration theorem
along with smoothness results, thus proving part of the conjectured analogues:
Theorem 1.1. Fix τ ∈eΣk and let C →P1 be a general degree k, genus g cover totally
ramified at two points p and q such that k is the smallest positive integer satisfying OC(k(p−
q)) ∼= OC.
(1) The variety W τ(C, p, q) is non-empty if and only if g ≥invk(τ). If W τ(C, p, q) is
non-empty, then dim W τ(C, p, q) = g −invk(τ).
(2) If g = invk(τ), then the number of points in W τ(C, p, q) is the number of reduced
words for τ + d −g in Σk.
(3) W τ(C, p, q) is normal and Cohen–Macaulay, and is smooth away from the union of
transmission loci W τ ′(C, p, q) ⊂W τ(C, p, q) having codimension 2 or more.
(4) When g > invk(τ), W τ(C, p, q) is irreducible.
(5) When the characteristic of the ground field does not divide k and g ≥invk(τ), the
universal Wτ has a unique component dominating a component of Hk,g,2 that consists
of twice-marked curves (C, p, q) such that k is the smallest positive integer satisfying
OC(k(p −q)) ∼= OC.

1.1. Proof technique and outline of the paper. We approach the problem using degen-
eration, wherein a general degree k cover of P1 degenerates to a singular curve of compact
type, namely a chain of elliptic curves, on the special fiber. Pflueger’s work [16] supplies
a concrete combinatorial description of transmission loci on the special fiber of our family.
Combined with upper-semicontinuity, this combinatorial description enables an upper bound
on the dimension of transmission loci for the generic fiber of our family. The key technical
result of this paper is a regeneration theorem (Theorem 4.4), which shows that limit line
bundles in transmission loci on the central fiber smooth out to the generic fiber in at least
the expected dimension. With regeneration in hand, the proof of enumeration is immediate
and Cohen-Macaulayness (Theorem 4.7) and connectedness (Theorem 4.8) are shown by
establishing them on the central fiber, since those properties are open in flat families. To
prove smoothness of W τ(C, p, q) on the open locus consisting of line bundles of type precisely
τ (Corollary 5.4), we define a pointed Petri map whose injectivity is equivalent to smooth-
ness at a line bundle of type τ, and show the map is injective on the central fiber of our
family. We finally prove smoothness of W τ(C, p, q) along transmission loci of codimension
1 (Theorem 5.6), by showing such loci are Cartier divisors of W τ(C, p, q), which also yields
normality and irreduciblity.
In Section 2, we discuss the connection between prescribing ramification at p and q and
permutations in eΣk and reduce the problem of checking whether a line bundle L lies in
W τ(C, p, q) to verifying for the existence of k “new” sections in k distinct twists L(aip+biq)
for certain ai, bi ∈Z (Proposition 2.13). In Section 3, we discuss our degeneration along
with the definition and combinatorial stratification of transmission loci on the special fiber.
We then carry out set-theoretic regeneration in Section 4 by carefully choosing non-colliding
ramification indices for our so-called τ-positive limit linear series and showing they spread
out in the expected dimension to the generic fiber of our family. Because the transmission
loci on the special fiber are reduced, we obtain scheme-theoretic regeneration. In Section 5,
we prove the smoothness of transmission loci away from codimension 2 transmission loci.
Finally, in Section 6, we outline the proof of Theorem 1.1(5), as we are able to re-use the
arguments of [12].
1.2. Conventions. We work over an algebraically closed field. For n ∈Z, we will use [n] to
denote the set {0, 1, . . . , |n| −2, |n| −1}. Throughout, curve will mean a connected proper
nodal curve and C will refer to a smooth curve. We will use g to refer to the genus of a
curve and d to refer to the degree of a line bundle.
