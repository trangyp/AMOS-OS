BICOMPACT TORSION CLASSES AND
CONJECTURES ON BRICK INFINITE ALGEBRAS
SOTA ASAI
Abstract. A torsion class T of the module category mod A of a finite dimensional algebra A over a
field K is said to be compact if there exists a module M ∈mod A such that T is the smallest torsion
class containing M. If a torsion class satisfies this and the dual condition, then we call it a bicompact
torsion class.
We conjecture that bicompact torsion classes are precisely functorially finite torsion
classes, and prove it for hereditary algebras and also for semistable torsion classes. This gives that
Demonet Conjecture implies Enomoto Conjecture, both of which are important conjectures on brick
infiniteness.
1. Introduction
For a finite dimensional algebra A over a field K, classification of full subcategories satisfying certain
conditions of the category mod A of finitely generated modules is one of the most important problems.
Especially, researchers actively have investigated torsion classes T ⊂mod A, that is, full subcategories
closed under taking factor modules and extensions. For each torsion class T , we uniquely have the
corresponding torsion-free class F which forms a torsion pair (T , F) in mod A.
We write tors A for the set of torsion classes. In general, it is a very hard problem to classify all
T ∈tors A. The most fundamental family of torsion classes are functorially finite torsion classes. By
[Sm], T ∈tors A is functorially finite if and only if there exists some module M ∈mod A such that
T = Fac M, where Fac M is the full subcategory of factor modules of M⊕s (s ≥0).
There are several kinds of generalizations of functorially finite torsion classes. In this paper, we
focus on the following families of torsion classes:
(1) compact (cocompact, bicompact) torsion classes;
(2) semistable torsion classes.
We first give the definitions of compact torsion classes and related notions. For each M ∈mod A,
we write T(M) (resp. F(M)) for the smallest torsion class (resp. torsion-free class) containing M.

As an application of Theorem 1.4, we show a certain implication between open questions on brick
infiniteness.
If the endomorphism algebra EndA(M) of M ∈mod A is a division K-algebra, then M is called
a brick. A semibrick is defined as a set of isoclasses of bricks which are pairwisely Hom-orthogonal.
Semibricks are in bijection with wide subcategories of mod A [R1], and their relationship with torsion
classes are studied in many works including [AP, BCZ, BH, DIRRT, H, IT, J, MŠ].
An algebra A is said to be brick infinite if there are infinitely many isoclasses of bricks. Brick
(in)finiteness of algebras are characterized in many ways by using torsion classes and 2-term silting
complexes; see Proposition 5.2 and Corollary 5.6 in this paper by [AS, A2, DIJ, R2, Se] for example.
Moreover, there are many conjectures for brick infinite algebras [MP, STV, Pf]. In this paper, we
consider the following two conjectures.
The first one was proposed by Enomoto [E]. A semibrick consisting of infinitely many isoclasses of
bricks is called an infinite semibrick. If there exists an infinite semibrick in mod A, then A is clearly
brick infinite. Enomoto conjectured that the converse also holds, and gave a partial answer.
Conjecture 1.6 (Enomoto Conjecture, Conjecture 5.1). Let A be brick infinite. Then there exists an
infinite semibrick in mod A.

The other conjecture was suggested by Demonet [D]. In [A2], we proved that A is brick infinite
if and only if there exists θ ∈K0(proj A)R which is not rigid. He strengthened this to the following
conjecture.
Conjecture 1.7 (Demonet Conjecture, Conjecture 5.3). Let A be brick infinite. Then there exists a
lattice point θ ∈K0(proj A) which is not rigid.
One of the reasons why lattice points are important is that the presentation space Hom(θ) for
θ ∈K0(proj A) is available. There are many studies including [AsI, DF, F, HY, Pl, PYK] in this
direction.
Another advantage of lattice points is that the torsion class T θ admits a semibrick S such that
T θ = T(S) if θ ∈K0(proj A) [A2]. By this and Theorem 1.4, we prove the following relationship
between the two conjectures.
Theorem 1.8 (Theorem 5.4). Assume that the base field K is algebraically closed. Then Demonet
Conjecture 1.7 implies Enomoto Conjecture 1.6.
The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we define bicompact torsion classes, and
recall some basic properties on functorially finite torsion classes and semistable torsion classes. Section
3 is devoted to the proof of Theorem 1.4. In Section 4, we show Theorem 1.5 by using numerically
disjoint subcategories, and deduce Theorem 1.3. In Section 5, Theorem 1.8 is shown.
Convention. In this paper, K is a field, and A is a finite dimensional K-algebra. We write mod A

2. Preliminary
For a full subcategory C ⊂mod A, we set
⊥C := {X ∈mod A | HomA(X, C) = 0},
C⊥:= {X ∈mod A | HomA(C, X) = 0}.
Let T , F be full subcategories of mod A. Then the pair (T , F) is called a torsion pair if F = T ⊥
and T = ⊥F. A full subcategory T ⊂tors A is called a torsion class in mod A if there exists a full
subcategory F ⊂mod A such that (T , F) is a torsion pair (in this case, F = T ⊥). This is equivalent
to that T is closed under taking factor modules and extensions in mod A. We write tors A for the set
of all torsion classes in mod A. Dually, torsion-free classes are defined, and torf A denotes the set of
all torsion-free classes in mod A.
Let C ⊂mod A be a full subcategory. We write T(C) (resp. F(C)) for the smallest torsion class
(resp. torsion-free class) containing C. Under this notation, we have two torsion pairs (T(C), C⊥) and
(⊥C, F(C)). In the case C = {M}, we simply write T(M) and F(M). We set
Filt C = {X ∈mod A | there exists 0 = X0 ⊂X1 ⊂· · · ⊂Xl = X with Xi/Xi−1 ∈C},
Fac C = {X ∈mod A | there exists a surjection C⊕s →X with C ∈C and s ≥0},
Sub C = {X ∈mod A | there exists an injection X →C⊕s with C ∈C and s ≥0}.
Then we have T(C) = Filt(Fac C) and F(C) = Filt(Sub C); see [MŠ, Lemma 3.1].
We are mainly interested in the case C = {M} for M ∈mod A. The following notions are the main
topics of this paper.
Definition 2.1. Let T ∈tors A. Then T is said to be compact if there exists M ∈mod A such that
T = T(M), and said to be cocompact if there exists M ∈mod A such that T ⊥= F(M), and said to
be bicompact if T is both compact and cocompact.

An additive full subcategory C ⊂mod A is said to be functorially finite if every M ∈mod A admits
a left C-approximation and a right C-approximation. We write f-tors A for the set of functorially finite
torsion classes. The following property called the Smalø symmetry is our start point. In particular,
the “if” part of Conjecture 1.2 holds true.
Proposition 2.3. [Sm, Theorem] Let (T , F) be a torsion pair in mod A. Then the following conditions
are equivalent.
(a) The torsion class T is functorially finite.
(b) The torsion-free class F is functorially finite.
(c) There exists M ∈mod A such that T = Fac M.
(d) There exists N ∈mod A such that F = Sub N.
In this case, T is bicompact.
We say that a torsion pair (T , F) is functorially finite if both T and F are functorially finite. It is
equivalent to that T or F is functorially finite by Proposition 2.3.
As a notion related to functorially finite torsion classes, we explain 2-term presiltng complexes
introduced in [KV] in the perfect derived category Kb(proj A).
Let U be a complex in Kb(proj A). Then U is said to be presilting if HomKb(proj A)(U, U[> 0]) = 0.

form U−1 →U0 whose terms except the −1st and the 0th ones vanish.

X ∈Tθ holds. Therefore we obtain T ⊂Tθ. Dually, F ⊂Fθ follows.
□
Now we are able to show Theorem 4.3.
Proof of Theorem 4.3. Since T is bicompact, Lemma 4.5 and its dual imply that both T and T ⊥
are polyhedral. Then Lemma 4.6 (a)⇒(d) allows us to take θ ∈K0(proj A)R such that T ⊂Tθ and
F ⊂Fθ ⊂Fθ. Since (T , T ⊥) and (Tθ, Fθ) are torsion pairs in mod A, we have (T , T ⊥) = (Tθ, Fθ).
Thus T = Tθ is bicompact, so Theorem 3.1 (b)⇒(d) implies that T = Tθ is functorially finite.
□

5. Conjectures on brick infiniteness
In this section, we apply our results so far to two conjectures by Enomoto [E] and Demonet [D] on
brick infiniteness. We here recall some terminology.
A module M ∈mod A is called a brick if EndA(M) is a division K-algebra (it is equivalent to
EndA(M) ≃K if K is algebraically closed), and a semibrick S is the set of isoclasses of bricks such
that HomA(M, N) = 0 for any M ̸= N ∈S. A semibrick is said to be finite (resp. infinite) if it is a
finite (resp. infinite) set of isoclasses of bricks.
We say that A is brick finite if there are only finitely many isoclasses of bricks in mod A, and
otherwise, A is said to be brick infinite.
By definition, if there exists an infinite semibrick in mod A, then A is brick infinite.
Enomoto
proposed that the converse also holds.
Conjecture 5.1 (Enomoto Conjecture). [E, Conjecture 5.12] Let A be brick infinite. Then there
exists an infinite semibrick in mod A.
This is one of the two conjectures we consider. The other conjecture by Demonet is based on the
following known characterizations of brick infinite algebras.
Proposition 5.2. [DIJ, Theorem 4.2] [A2, Theorem 4.7] For a finite dimensional algebra A, the
following conditions are equivalent.
(a) The algebra A is brick infinite.
(b) The set 2-silt A is infinite.
(c) There exists θ ∈K0(proj A)R which is not rigid.
Strengthen this, Demonet suggested the following conjecture.
Conjecture 5.3. [D, Question 3.49] Let A be brick infinite. Then there exists a lattice point θ ∈
K0(proj A) which is not rigid.
We prove that these two conjectures have the following relationship.
Theorem 5.4. Assume that the base field K is algebraically closed.
(1) Let θ ∈K0(proj A) be a lattice point. If θ is not rigid, then there exists an infinite semibrick
S such that T θ = T(S).
(2) Demonet Conjecture 5.3 implies Enomoto Conjecture 5.1.
To show this, we use the following property.
Proposition 5.5. [A2, Theorem 2.23] Let θ ∈K0(proj A) be a lattice point. Then the torsion class
T θ has a semibrick S such that T θ = T(S).
Theorem 5.4 is proved as follows.
Proof of Theorem 5.4. (1) By Proposition 5.5, there exists a semibrick S such that T θ = T(S). Assume
that the semibrick S is finite. Then T θ is compact. Since K is algebraically closed and θ ∈K0(proj A),
Theorem 3.6 implies that θ is rigid. This contradicts our assumption. Thus S must be infinite.
(2) is immediate from (1).
□
We end this paper with explaining some more relationship between semibricks and torsion classes.
There exist a bijection from sbrick A of semibricks to the set wide A of wide subcategories of mod A,
that is, full subcategories closed under kernels, cokernels and extensions [R1, 1.2]. The map sends
each semibrick S ∈sbrick A to Filt S ∈wide A.

To each W ∈wide A, associating the smallest torsion class T(W) containing W defines a map
T: wide A →tors A. Marks-Šťovíček showed that T is injective [MŠ, Proposition 3.3].
As in [AP], T ∈tors A is said to be widely generated if there exists W ∈wide A such that T = T(W);
or equivalently, there exists S ∈sbrick A such that T = T(S). By [AS, Proposition 5.4] based on [Se,
Corollary 4.10], A is brick finite if and only if every T is widely generated.
Recently, Ringel showed that, for any module M ∈mod A, there exists a finite semibrick S such
that T(M) = T(S) in [R2, Proposition 5.5]. Thus a compact torsion class is always widely generated.
As a consequence of these studies and [DIJ], we have the following characterization of brick finite
algebras.
Corollary 5.6. For a finite dimensional algebra A, the following conditions are equivalent.
(a) The algebra A is brick finite.
(b) The set tors A is finite.
(c) Every T ∈tors A is functorially finite.
(d) Every T ∈tors A is bicompact.
(e) Every T ∈tors A is compact.
(f) Every T ∈tors A is widely generated.
Proof. (a), (b) and (c) are equivalent by [DIJ, Corollary 2.9, Theorems 3.8, 4.2]. They are equivalent
to (f) by [AS, Proposition 5.4]. (c)⇒(d) is Proposition 2.3, (d)⇒(e) is clear, and (e)⇒(f) is [R2,
Proposition 5.5].
□
