Abstract
We compute exact finite-rank BPS generating functions for the fermionic matrix model
with single-trace supercharge Qp = Tr(Ψp) at (p, N) = (5, 3), (5, 4), (5, 5), (7, 4), together
with partial data at (7, 5). In all complete computed cases, the charge-resolved spectrum
exhibits an overdetermined factorization — a power of p, times an onset monomial xqmin,
times (1 + x)N, times a palindromic reduced polynomial — despite the loss of Casimir
solvability at p ≥5. We prove rank palindromicity rR = rN2−p−R from the exterior top-
degree pairing; at (5, 5), the ten low-charge ranks and the minimal divisibility condition
(1 + x) | R5,5 determine the remaining middle rank, and direct computation confirms the
full generating function. For fixed p, the mod-p Witten indices give a closed-form index floor;
together with the trivial Hilbert-space upper bound, this places any accumulation point of

Introduction
Supercharge cohomology at finite rank draws a sharp distinction between monotone and for-
tuitous BPS states [1]. Monotone states are those that persist as the rank N increases, while
fortuitous states exist only in finite-N windows.
In holographic examples these two classes
were conjecturally related to smooth horizonless geometries and black-hole microstates, respec-
tively [1], but the cohomological definitions make sense more generally. The model we study is
the quantum mechanics of a single N ×N matrix of complex fermions Ψij, with odd supercharge
Qp = Tr(Ψp),
p ≥3 odd,
(1)
where {Ψij, Ψ†
kl} = δikδjl and H = {Qp, Q†
p} [2, 3]. The Hilbert space is the exterior algebra
Λ•(CN×N), graded by fermion number. The basic problem is to determine the exact1 charge-
resolved BPS spectrum as a function of p and N.
For p = 3, this problem was solved exactly in Ref. [4]. In the cubic model, the traceless
Hamiltonian is the Kostant Laplacian [5] and acts on each SU(N) irreducible summand through
the quadratic Casimir operator ˆC2, so the model admits the fortuity phenomena analyzed in
Refs. [1,4,6]. For p ≥5, that Casimir solvability is lost even though, as we show below, the U(1)
trace mode still decouples exactly. Identical SU(N) irreducible representations can then occur
at different energies, and the p = 3 maximal-family mechanism no longer applies.
In this paper we use that framework in the structural sense: although the present model is
not proposed as holographic, it provides a controlled setting in which one can compare exact
BPS counts, index floors, and the N-dependence of the cohomology. We take care to distinguish

This picture has been explored in N = 4 super Yang–Mills (SYM) and in supersymmet-

duality [19]. In the finite-N cohomology computations among these works [1, 6–8, 10], the su-
percharge complex is built sector by sector and the cohomology is extracted at small N. That
is exactly the strategy we use here.
The loss of Casimir solvability places the higher-p models outside the class of previously
solved or exactly structured fermionic quantum mechanical models, such as the quartic Grass-
mann matrix models solved in Refs. [20–22], the fermionic tensor-matrix reductions discussed in
Refs. [23–25], the recent disorder-free melonic model of Ref. [26], and the BMN-type construc-
tions of Ref. [27]. The comparison with the disorder-free SYK literature [26,28,29] is especially
natural: both that model and ours replace disorder by a fixed non-random supercharge, but the
melonic model has a controlled large-j (large-number-of-fermions) melonic limit and SYK-like
infrared physics, whereas the present system has exact SU(N) symmetry, broad charge support,
and no evident melonic simplification.
The underlying Lie-algebraic framework — primitive cocycles, spectral sequences, and the
associated cohomology — goes back to Refs. [30,31]; see Refs. [32,33] for earlier work on fermionic
matrix models and Ref. [3] for a recent review in the same context.

Hamiltonian and Casimir structure
For p = 3, the supercharge Q3 on the traceless sector is wedge multiplication by the cubic
primitive cocycle on Λsu(N), and its Laplacian H = {Q3, Q†

Every state in a given irrep therefore has the same energy, and the BPS spectrum is completely

For p ≥5, the U(1) trace mode still decouples exactly (§2.4 below), but the traceless super-
charge is the differential associated to the degree-p primitive cocycle of su(N) [30,31], which for

sociated to degree-p cocycles are not in general expressible purely in terms of Casimir–Racah
operators [34,35].
Our data confirm that the full set of central Casimir–Racah operators is insufficient to control
the spectrum.

Cartan generators do not resolve the degeneracy.
A natural refinement is whether the
Cartan generators Ha of SU(N) — which commute with H automatically, by SU(N)-invariance
— supply enough additional conserved quantities to resolve the spectrum beyond what the
Casimirs already see. They do not, and for a structural reason. Within an irreducible SU(N)
representation, the simultaneous spectrum of the Cartan generators is exactly the set of weights
of that irrep. Hence the Cartans distinguish weight spaces within a single copy of an irrep, but
they cannot distinguish between two distinct copies of the same irrep: both copies carry identical
weight diagrams. In the decisive case (N, p) = (4, 5) at R = 4, the E = 0 and E = 2100 blocks
are both copies of the same SU(4) irrep with identical (C2, C3, C4), so the full Cartan set adds no
resolving power on top of the Casimirs. Resolving such multiplicities would require additional
commuting operators that act nontrivially on multiplicity spaces, not merely the central Casimirs
or the Cartan generators [36]. The (1+x)N factor in ZBPS is therefore unlikely to reflect an extra
Cartan-type conservation at the level of the Hamiltonian. Its natural target, as discussed in §5,
is instead an image-rank/Cartan-creator structure — and, at the cohomology level, a possible
freeness property of the reduced cohomology over the exterior algebra of the traceless diagonal
creators — rather than extra conserved charges of H.
The U(1) decoupling survives exactly; what fails at p ≥5 is Casimir solvability inside the
traceless sector. This is what makes the higher-p BPS spectrum nontrivial: no single represen-
tation characterizes the zero-energy sector, and the factorization of ZBPS into (1 + x)N × T(x)
emerges from a Hamiltonian not controlled by the central Casimir algebra.

Discussion and open problems
The exact formulas (19), (20), (24), and (27) are the main concrete results. They show that

higher-p spectra are genuinely new: the U(1) trace mode still decouples, the Hamiltonian is no
longer controlled by a single Casimir, and the BPS generating function still factorizes rigidly.

Appendix B
Sector-by-sector data
All computations at N = 3 and N = 4 were performed in double-precision floating-point arith-
metic using dense linear algebra (SVD of the stacked matrix AR in (14)). Fock states are ordered
lexicographically by bit pattern, and Q matrices are built by explicit enumeration of all closed
index chains in Tr(Ap). Ranks were determined by singular-value thresholds of 10−10 relative
to the largest singular value; at N = 3, 4 the spectral gaps between zero and nonzero singular
values exceed 104, making rank determinations unambiguous.
The N = 5 computations used sparse matrix assembly with Numba-accelerated inner loops
to build each sector map QR as a compressed sparse row matrix. The Gram matrix Q⊤
RQR was
then decomposed into connected components via its sparsity graph. Each block was diagonalized
independently using dense eigvalsh or LDL⊤factorization, with spectral gaps between zero
and nonzero eigenvalues exceeding 102 in every block. At R = 9, the block families exhibit
the structure of Weyl orbits of SU(5): the most common block multiplicities are divisors of
|S5| = 120. All N = 5 results were independently verified by a second implementation using
union-find block decomposition and blockwise LDL⊤pivot counting.
