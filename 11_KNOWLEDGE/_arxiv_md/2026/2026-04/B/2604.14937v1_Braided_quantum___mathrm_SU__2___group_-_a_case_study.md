JACEK KRAJCZOK AND PIOTR M. SO LTAN
Abstract. We continue the study of the braided compact quantum group SUqp2q for
complex q satisfying 0 ă |q| ă 1 introduced by Kasprzak, Meyer, Roy and Woronowicz
([19]). We address such aspects as existence of the Haar measure, construct the scaling
group, the antipode and its polar decomposition and describe the related braided Hopf
algebra. We also study when the braided flip extends to a completely bounded map and
establish equivalence between the two approaches to bosonization and braided tensor
product taken in the literature ([19] vs. [30, 36, 11]).

In the first paper [42] dealing with braided tensor products on C˚-algebra level (referred
to “crossed products” in that article) written by S.L. Woronowicz, a central role was
played by the inclusion morphisms of the factor C˚-algebras into the braided product.
The notation used in that paper was j1 P MorpA, A bCBq and j2 P MorpB, A bCBq. The
theory of braided tensor products of C˚-algebras was further developed in [19, 30, 31]
and [11], where the notation for the braided product utilizes the symbol b (or bζ in
[19], see Section A.1) and variously ι1, ι2 etc. or ιA, ιB for the inclusion morphisms. The
useful convention of denoting the inclusion morphism with a symbol which contains a
symbol designating the C˚-algebra in question, like ιA, adopted e.g. in [30, 31, 11] is not
sufficiently precise when we are dealing with the braided tensor product of a given C˚-
algebra A with itself: A b A. In that case the notation j1 and j2 seems more appropriate,
but it becomes increasingly clumsy when multiple braided products are considered. The
solution of using notation of the form ιA,1, ιB,3 etc. (e.g. to denote inclusion morphisms
from A and B into A b B b B) solves the notational dilemmas, but is rather heavy and
most of the time unnecessary. We propose to pass seamlessly between the convention of
including the symbol for the C˚-algebra in the notation and only indicating its position in
the braided product depending on the context. Although this is not a coherent notational
scheme, we feel it will not cause any confusion to the reader, as the context always clearly
explains which algebra is being included at what position in the braided tensor product.
Sometimes (e.g. in the proof of Proposition A.9) we will use a combination of these two
variants of notation simultaneously.

A. Appendices
A.1. Braided tensor products. In this section we will show that the definitions of
braided tensor product given in [19] and [30, 31] are equivalent in the situation when
both constructions apply. In the end, we want to be compatible with the general von
Neumann algebraic approach to braided tensor product introduced in [11]. It is very
close to the conventions of [31], however [11] uses left actions, whereas [30, 31] use right
actions. This difference is not essential, see [11, Section 9.2], where it is explained how to
translate construction of [11] to the setting of right actions. We note that [19] also uses
left actions.
