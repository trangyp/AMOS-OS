REMUS FLORICEL, SARAH PLOSKER, AND AVNER SADIKOV
Abstract. Let A be a unital C∗-algebra and H a Hilbert space. The cone CP(A, B(H)) of
completely positive maps carries the Bures metric β, closely related to the cb-norm.
We introduce a family of Bures–Kuratowski (BK) metrics on CB(A, B(H)) that extend β
exactly on CP(A, B(H)). The construction combines a Kuratowski embedding of the Bures
cone, based at an anchor θ ∈CP(A, B(H)), with a regular-representation Hausdorff coordinate
arising from universal regular models. Each BK metric admits an ℓp-wedge decomposition,
splitting CB(A, B(H)) into the Bures cone and a non-CP component attached at θ.
We then study Vietoris–Rips and ˇCech complexes of BK metric spaces. The wedge formula
yields explicit criteria for mixed simplices, a join-type description of the mixed Rips complex,
and ball-intersection criteria for mixed ˇCech simplices.
For finite point clouds, this makes
the mixed simplicial geometry computable from the two component metrics and reveals new
homological features arising from the interaction between the CP and non-CP sectors.

1. Introduction
Let A be a unital C∗-algebra and H a Hilbert space. We consider
X := CB(A, B(H)),
C := CP(A, B(H)) ⊆X,
the spaces of completely bounded and completely positive maps, respectively; see [13,14] for a
comprehensive treatment of the subject.
The cone C carries a natural metric geometry given by the Bures distance β, defined through
common Stinespring representations [10]. This metric is quantitatively linked to the cb-norm
by the Kretschmann–Schlingemann–Werner inequalities [10], and therefore provides a canonical
geometric structure on the completely positive cone. In particular, when H = C, it reduces to
the classical Bures distance on positive functionals [4].
A basic question is whether this geometry extends in a meaningful way from C to the full space
X. A direct extension compatible with the cb-norm is too much to expect globally. Instead, the
point of view of this paper is that the geometry of X should be understood relative to the CP

The final section turns from metric geometry to simplicial constructions. Once a BK space
is identified as an ℓp-wedge, it becomes natural to ask how Vietoris–Rips and ˇCech complexes
detect the interaction between the CP and non-CP sectors. For Vietoris–Rips complexes, the
wedge structure yields a complete and explicit description of mixed simplices: they are governed
by the two radial functions measuring distance to the glued basepoints, leading to a join-
type decomposition of the mixed part of VRt(X, dθ) (Theorem 6.5). In the case p = ∞, this
description becomes exact on finite point clouds, where the mixed simplices form a genuine
simplicial join of sublevel-set complexes from the two components.
For ˇCech complexes, the situation is more delicate. Mixed simplices are no longer determined
purely by pairwise radial bounds; instead, they reduce to ball-intersection problems within one
component subject to a radial constraint imposed by the other (Proposition 6.24). This leads to
a natural distinction between intrinsic and ambient ˇCech complexes in the BK setting: ambient
complexes can detect the glued basepoint and become contractible at relatively small scales,
while intrinsic complexes retain the combinatorial geometry of the point cloud. In particular,
for finite BK clouds, the ambient ˇCech complex exhibits a “cone effect” driven by the basepoint,
which can collapse homology earlier than in the corresponding Vietoris–Rips filtration. These
results show that the wedge geometry produces new and explicitly computable interactions
between the CP and non-CP sectors at the level of simplicial topology.
