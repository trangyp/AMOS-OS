Bond-Length–Driven Magnetic Transition
in Quasi-One-Dimensional CrSbX3 (X=S, Se)
Kang Lee,1 Hong-Suk Choi,1 and Kwan-Woo Lee1, 2, ∗
1Department of Applied Physics, Graduate School, Korea University, Sejong 30019, Korea
2Division of Semiconductor Physics, Korea University, Sejong 30019, Korea
(Dated: April 3, 2026)
Using ab initio calculations, we investigate the magnetic ground states of quasi-one-dimensional
insulating CrSbX3 (X = S, Se) with infinite double-rutile chains. Within conventional band theory,
without explicit Coulomb correlations (U), we obtain band gaps in close agreement with experi-
ment. Remarkably, we find that the magnetic order is highly sensitive to the Cr–Cr bond length
dCr−Cr: increasing the bond length induces a transition from antiferromagnetic to ferromagnetic
order at a critical distance dc
Cr−Cr ≈3.53(±0.05) ˚A. Accordingly, CrSbS3 lies near the transition
boundary, whereas CrSbSe3 is robustly ferromagnetic, in good agreement with experiment. Analy-
sis of the exchange interactions reveals that the first-order phase transition is dominated by a sign
reversal of the intrachain nearest-neighbor superexchange J1 mediated by chalcogen ions, while the
intrachain direct exchange J2 remains ferromagnetic and changes only gradually. This behavior re-
flects an emergent Bethe–Slater-like behavior driven by competing exchange pathways in a quasi-1D
transition-metal system, where the competition between J1 and J2 dictates the magnetic ground
state. Besides, the electronic structures of the ground states of each compound are investigated.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Low-dimensional systems host a variety of intrigu-
ing phenomena, including Peierls instabilities, spin-
or charge-density waves, exotic magnetism, and su-
perconductivity. In particular, according to the Mer-
min–Wagner
theorem
[1],
a
purely
one-
or
two-
dimensional (1D/2D) system cannot sustain spontaneous
magnetization at finite temperature. However, quasi-
1D and -2D systems often manifest magnetic states
due to weak inter-chain interactions or coupling along
other crystallographic directions, often called the van der
Waals magnet [2–4]. Interestingly, the quasi-1D ternary
chromium trichalcogenides CrSbX3 (X=S, Se), which
consist of the double-rutile chain structure of CrX6 octa-
hedra extending along the ˆb-axis [5, 6], show distinct, yet
sometimes controversial, magnetic states, i.e., insulating
ferromagnetic (FM) versus antiferromagnetic (AFM) or-
ders [5–8].
CrSbSe3 is a rare insulating FM of an optical en-
ergy gap Eg ∼0.7 eV and the Curie temperature TC=71
K, showing the saturated moment 3µB/Cr [5, 7, 9, 10],
consistent with a Cr3+ (3d3) ionic configuration of spin
S = 3
2. This compound shows a large anisotropic magne-
tocaloric effect [7, 9, 11], with the easy axis ˆa perpendicu-
lar to the double-rutile chain, as theoretically confirmed
[12, 13]. The magnetocrystalline anisotropy constant is
Ku ∼145 kJ/m3 at 10 K and decreases by ∼73% at TC
for the hard axis ˆb [11]. In contrast, the T dependence of
Ku along the other crystallographic directions is compar-
atively weak. Based on ab initio calculations, a very low
thermal conductivity of 0.56 W/m·K was suggested, lead-
ing to a high thermoelectric figure of merit 0.46 at 900

gation suggested that, based on the calculated moderate
cleavage energy, a CrSbSe3 ladder structure could be ex-
foliated, with a predicted TC more than twice that of the
bulk [12]. Besides, applying pressure P, the insulating
FM CrSbSe3 undergoes a gradual transition into a su-
perconducting state, with the onset occurring at Pc ≈33
GPa. The superconductivity reaches the maximum criti-
cal temperature of Tc=7.7 K at Pc ≈58 GPa [15].
In contrast, although CrSbS3 was initially reported to
be an insulating FM [5, 6], a recent experiment shows
an AFM order [8], implying that its magnetic state is
sensitive. The resistivity measurement indicates an insu-
lating state of Eg ≈0.9 eV, confirmed by the heat capac-
ity measurement [16]. The susceptibility data show two
peaks at TCT =94 K and the N´eel temperature TN=43
K, accompanying a kink and a hump in the specific heat
measurements, respectively. The measurements of neu-
tron powder diffraction indicate that CrSbS3 shows a C-
type AFM, featuring a zigzag spin alignment along each
double rutile chain, the easy axis ˆa as in CrSbSe3, and no
spin canting. The long-range spin order was confirmed by
asymmetric features in the electron spin resonance (ESR)
spectra below TN. The observed saturated local moment
of the Cr ion is 2.37 µB, much smaller than the nominal
value of S = 3
2, implying substantial p −d hybridization.
Besides, seemingly at around 50 GPa, an insulator-to-
metal transition appeared, but no superconductivity was
observed.

II.
CRYSTAL STRUCTURE AND
CALCULATION APPROACHES
Figure 1 shows the crystal structure of the ternary
chromium trichalcogenides CrSbX3 (space group: Pnma,
No. 62), containing 4 formula units (f.u.) in the unit
cell. In these systems, the edge-shared octahedra CrX6
form infinite double-rutile chains that are linked by SbX3
pyramids and extend along the ˆb-axis. The double-rutile
chains are weakly connected by the van der Waals inter-
action in the ac plane. The chains are oriented obliquely
in this plane.

mining stable structures in such quasi-1D systems, we
optimized the crystal structures of these compounds us-

D3) method [21], as implemented in the Vienna ab ini-

crucial factor for determining the magnetic ground state

when dCr−Cr was varied (see below). Note that our re-

III.
RESULTS
A.
Energetics
To find the magnetic ground state for these com-
pounds, we first investigated changes in energy, as vary-
ing dCr−Cr in the range of 3.2 ˚A to 4.0 ˚A, encompassing
all possible values in these systems. Throughout the en-
tire range, in both of the compounds the nonmagnetic
state is energetically unfavorable, lying approximately
1 eV/f.u. above the AFM and FM states, indicating a
strong tendency toward spin-orders.
Over the dCr−Cr range investigated here, for both sys-
tems the magnetic moments remain nearly unchanged,
reflecting the insulating character: the total moment of 3
µB in the FM and the Cr local moment of 2.7-2.8 µB in
the AFM, corresponding to a S = 3
2 state. The changes
in energy of each magnetic state can be well fitted by
a function including terms up to cubic order, reflecting
the van der Waals interaction for the quasi-1D systems,
but some deviations near the equilibrium positions. As
usual in the systems, these changes show an asymmetric
behavior about each equilibrium position. The compres-
sion case is stiffer than the stretching case, being about 6
times stiffer in CrSbS3 and twice stiffer in CrSbSe3 (for
details, see SM).
From these energy changes, we calculated the varia-
tions in the energy difference ∆E between the AFM and
FM states for both compounds, as varying dCr−Cr. As
shown in the top panel of Fig. 2, these variations show
an approximately cubic decrease with decreasing dCr−Cr,
analogous to the energy changes of each magnetic state,
but pronounced discontinuities at the boundaries of the
transition between AFM and FM. This indicates a first-

in
CrSbSe3, earlier calculations within the local spin den-

also found an insulating ground state, but with a
Eg significantly smaller than the experimental value
[12, 13]. To reproduce the observed Eg, those stud-

Heyd–Scuseria–Ernzerhof (HSE) hybrid functional [12],
and consequently interpreted the system as a Mott insu-
lator [13]. (Note that no previous results for CrSbS3 ex-
ist.) In contrast, our calculations for both compounds
using wien2k, and independently confirmed with fplo,
yield Eg’s already at the GGA level that are in close
agreement with the experiment measurements [7, 8].
Both wien2k and fplo codes are all-electron full-
potential methods, allowing an accurate description of
the anisotropic potential and the localized 3d orbitals
without relying on shape or pseudopotential approxima-
tions. This indicates that the insulating behavior in these
compounds can be understood within a band-insulating
feature, without invoking strong correlation effects be-
yond GGA.

IV.
DISCUSSION
As mentioned in the Introduction, superconductivity
emerges at P ∼33 GPa in CrSbSe3 that is an insu-
lating FM at ambient pressure. Using the experimental
structure including the internal parameters at p = 22.4
GPa [15], we calculated the magnetic ground state. At
the pressure (dCr−Cr = 3.18 ˚A), AFM has a lower energy
than in FM by 12.7 meV/f.u., following the Bethe-Slater-
curve-like behavior observed in Sec. III A. At the AFM
state, the Cr local moment of 0.9 µB, whereas those of
the other ions are minor, indicating a pressure-induced
spin-state transition from nominally 3d3↑to 3d2↑,1↓. This
state is itinerant, resulting from a substantial volume col-
lapse of approximately 22% relative to ambient pressure.
This indicates a transition of an insulating FM→itinerant
AFM→superconductivity with increasing pressure, sug-
gesting that the superconductivity is likely associated
with AFM fluctuations rather than FM ones. At the su-
perconducting phase, the Fermi surfaces (see SM) show
very strong 1D character, implying pronounced nesting
instabilities along the chain direction, reminiscent of the
high Tc cuprate Ba2CuO3+δ [37]. These issues require
further study.
Another notable feature is the presence of a second
sharp peak at T2 = 94 K in the magnetic susceptibility
of CrSbS3, accompanied by a corresponding peak in the
heat capacity data, in addition to the peak observed at
TN = 43 K [8], as mentioned in the Introduction. Li et
al. fitted the susceptibility data and obtained the effec-
tive moments of 3.74 and 2.76 (in units of µB/Cr) at the
region of T ≥T2 and TN ≤T ≤T2 [8], respectively.
Each value is close to the theoretical value for Cr3+ (3d3,
S =
3
2) and Cr4+ (3d2, S = 1). Additionally, the g-
factor derived from the ESR spectra is 1.96 at T2, shows
a jump of 0.02 at T2, and then increases smoothly to 1.99
at TN upon cooling. The latter g-factor corresponds to
Cr3+ (t3
2g), for which only a tiny spin–orbit coupling is
expected. Therefore, they concluded that this system un-
dergoes a charge-transfer transition from Cr3+ (3d3) to
Cr4+ (3d2) at TCT (= T2), rather than a spin-state tran-
sition, returning to Cr3+ at TN. Although no change in
crystallographic symmetry is observed at TCT and TN,
the T-dependent variations show discontinuities at TCT
in the lattice parameters of a and c, whereas the b param-
eter remains unchanged. Remarkably, the a and c show
opposite thermal expansion behaviors, positive and neg-
ative, respectively, in the range of between TCT and TN,
accompanying small discontinuities in the average Cr-S
bond length at both temperatures. In the T range, these
variations lead to a little volume collapse and a more
sudden drop in the ratio of c
a, but less than 1% in both.
Below TN, the variations for all lattice parameters are
negligible.
To investigate the charge transfer transition, the
atomic internal parameters were optimized while keeping
the experimentally observed lattice parameters at around
TCT , with a unit cell volume less than 1% smaller than

for the charge-transfer transition are found within our

elucidate the unusual T-dependent behaviors in the inter-

beyond the conventional DFT would be necessary. In
addition, for ab initio theoretical investigations, comple-

the T-dependence of local structural parameters seem
to be essential. More fundamentally, direct experimen-
tal measurements are necessary to confirm the previously
proposed charge-transfer transition. Therefore, these is-
sues warrant further investigation from both theoretical
and experimental viewpoints.
V.
SUMMARY
Throughout the ab initio calculations performed with
the accurate all-electron full-potential code wien2k, we
investigated the origin of the distinct magnetic ground
states for the quasi-1D ternary chromium trichalco-
genides CrSbX3 (X=S, Se), composed of infinite double-
rutile chains of edge-sharing CrX6 octahedra. In contrast
to previous reports showing substantially underestimated
Eg [12, 13], our calculations within the conventional band
theory (GGA) well reproduce insulating states with Eg’s
very close to the experimental values, indicating that
these are band insulators rather than Mott insulators.
Remarkably, our results show that changes in the Cr-
Cr bond length dCr−Cr lead to variations in the intra-
chain magnetic interactions: the shortest Cr-Cr direct
exchange interaction J2 along the chain direction ˆb and
even more pronounced variations in the intra-chain NN
superexchange interaction J1 along an oblique in-plane
direction. At the critical value dc
Cr−Cr ≈3.53(±0.05) ˚A,
evident discontinuities emerge in both the energy differ-
ence between AFM and FM and in J1, indicating the
first-order magnetic phase transition. These findings sug-
gest that CrSbS3 lies in close proximity to the critical
region of the phase transition, in apparent agreement
with previously reported, yet controversial, experimental
observations of its magnetic state [5, 6, 8]. In contrast,
CrSbSe3 remains robustly ferromagnetic, consistent with
the experiments [5, 6].
Taken together, the observed bond-length dependence–
extending to a broader class of quasi-1D Cr-based
magnets–reveals an emergent Bethe–Slater-like behavior
driven by competing exchange pathways in a quasi-1D
transition-metal system, in particular with the critical
value dc
Cr−Cr. This establishes bond length as a decisive
control parameter for magnetic order in these systems,
as often observed in transition metal-based compounds.
However, for these systems it would be much easier to ac-
cess and tune multiple magnetic phases by bond-length
engineering, since these have (quasi)-one-dimensionality
and lie in close proximity to the critical region.
