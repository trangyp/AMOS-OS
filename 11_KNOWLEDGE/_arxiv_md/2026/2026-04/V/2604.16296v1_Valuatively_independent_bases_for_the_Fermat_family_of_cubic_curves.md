Valuatively independent bases for the Fermat family of cubic curves
Jakob Hultgren and Sohaib Khalid
April 20, 2026
Abstract
Let π : (X, L) →D∗be the Fermat family of cubic curves in P2. For each k ≥1, we construct a
valuatively independent basis for H0(X, Lk). The construction uses a canonical cost function determined
by a Hessian structure on the essential skeleton Sk(X, π).

Introduction
Given a polarised degenerating family π : (X, L) →D∗of Calabi-Yau manifolds which is meromorphic over
the punctured unit disc D∗, a central problem in complex geometry is to determine the limiting behaviour
of the canonical Ricci-flat Calabi-Yau metrics ωt ∈c1(Lt) on the fibres Xt as t →0. A very important
special case is when the degeneration is a large complex structure limit point, which is the subject of the
Strominger–Yau–Zaslow (SYZ) picture of mirror symmetry. A key expectation in this picture is the metric
SYZ conjecture, which states that, given any δ > 0, there should exist ε > 0 such that whenever 0 < |t| < ε,
the fibre Xt admits an open subset Ut ⊆X whose Calabi-Yau volume satisfies |Ut| ≥(1 −δ)|Xt| and which
admits a smooth map Ut →B whose fibres are special Lagrangian tori of (Xt, ωt).
A program initiated by Yang Li uses techniques from non-Archimedean geometry together with local
and global stability estimates for real and complex Monge-Amp`ere equations to prove this, and under this
program, the metric SYZ conjecture has been verified in several cases [Li22a, PS22, HJMM22, Li24b, AH23].
The main obstacle in carrying out this program in full generality is producing solutions to certain real
Monge-Amp`ere equations. Crucially, [Li23a] shows that the metric SYZ conjecture holds for π : X →D∗if
the solution to the non-Archimedean Monge-Amp`ere equation simultaneously solves a real Monge-Amp`ere
equation, in an appropriate sense.
Due to the close connections between the theory of real Monge-Amp`ere equations and the theory of
optimal transport, it is natural to attempt to formulate an optimal transport problem whose optimiser can
be shown to solve both the non-Archimedean and the real Monge-Amp`ere equation.
This is indeed the
approach taken in [HJMM22, Li24b, AH23]. More generally, the picture that emerges from this is that such
an optimal transport problem should be formulated on Sk(X, π) × B where B is to be thought of as the
essential skeleton Sk(X∨, π∨) of a degenerating family π∨: X∨→D∗mirror to π : X →D∗. In [Li25], Yang
Li has proposed a conjectural picture where canonical bases of H0(X, Lk), k ≥1 are identified with lattice
points in the top-dimensional faces of B and the correct cost function for the optimal transport problem
appears as the limit of straightforward valuations in these basis elements
ck(x, s) = k−1 valx(s), x ∈Sk(X∨, π∨), s ∈H0(X, Lk) ⊂B.
(1)
By the main result in [Li25], the resulting optimal transport problem would produce the solution to the non-
Archimedean Monge-Amp`ere equation if the canonical bases satisfy the property of valuative independence
(see Section 3) globally on Sk(X, π).
However, showing existence of such bases seems like a challenging
problem.
The purpose of this note is to provide an explicit construction of a sequence of bases H0(X, Lk), k ≥1
when (X, L) is given by the Fermat family of cubic curves in P2.
Theorem 1. Let X be the Fermat family of curves in P2
xyz −t(x3 + y3 + z3) = 0,
polarized by the restriction L of −KP2 and let k ≥1. Then H0(X, Lk) has a valuatively independent basis.

In Theorem 1, the natural candidate for B is given by the boundary of the (anti-canonical) moment
polytope of P2 and monomial sections of H0(X, Lk), k ≥1, are naturally identified with its lattice points.
Moreover, Sk(X, π) carries a natural Hessian structure induced by the toric data of P2 (see Section 2). We
recall that a basis θ1, . . . , θr of H0(X, Lk) is valuatively independent if for any n ∈SK(X, π) and functions
rational functions ai,
valn((a1θ1 + · · · + arθr)/τ) = min{valt(ai) + valn(θi/τ) : ai ̸= 0}.
(2)
As explained in [Li25], constructing a basis which is locally valuatively independent, i.e. satisfies (2) for all
n in a small open set of Sk(X, π), is a relatively straightforward task. The key difficulty is to produce a
basis such that (2) holds globally on Sk(X, π). A central idea in our method of proof is to use a canonical
cost function c : Sk(X, π) × B →R induced by the Hessian structure on Sk(X∨, π∨), and look for a basis
satisfying (1).
The canonical cost function c, which was introduced in [HO19], is explicitly computable
using the monodromy data of Sk(X, π) as an affine manifold (see Section 2). Combined with the ansatz (1)
proposed in [Li25], it provides, for each basis element, an a priori indication of the vanishing orders along
each strata of the central fiber, and thus a heuristic for how to construct such a global basis. From the
explicit expression for c, we can inductively construct the valuatively independent basis for H0(X, Lk) from
valuatively independent bases for H0(X, rL) with r < k by adding correction terms to monomials of the
form λσ · S where σ is a section given by an appropriate monomial term, S is an appropriate member of the
valuatively independent basis of H0(X, rL) and λ is an appropriate constant.
