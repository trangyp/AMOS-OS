VARIETIES OF MINIMAL DEGREE IN WEIGHTED PROJECTIVE SPACE
MAYA BANKS AND RITVIK RAMKUMAR
ABSTRACT. We initiate a study of varieties of minimal degree in weighted projective spaces. We call a
weighted projective space P(w0,...,wn) divisible if wi | wi+1 for all i. We provide sharp bounds for
when a non-degenerate subvariety of a divisible weighted projective space has minimal degree. We define a
weighted notion of 1-generic matrices and, in analogy with the classical theory, show that there is a theory
of weighted determinantal scrolls. Moreover, we characterize precisely when these have minimal degree
and determine their weighted Np properties, and tie this to two weighted notions of regularity. Finally,
we propose conjectural bounds for more general weighted threefolds and pose several natural questions.
Throughout, we highlight the differences between this theory and the classical case.
1. INTRODUCTION
A subvariety X ⊆Pn of codimension c that is not contained in a hyperplane satisfies deg(X) ⩾1 +c.
If equality is attained, then X is called a variety of minimal degree. The celebrated results of del Pezzo
and Bertini [Ber23,dP86,EH87] provide a complete classification of these varieties: X is either a quadric
hypersurface, a rational normal scroll, or a cone over the Veronese surface P2 ,→P5. Eisenbud and Goto
[EG84] showed that varieties of minimal degree are completely characterized by their homological prop-
erties; namely, X is of minimal degree if and only if IX is 2-regular. This syzygetic perspective culminated
in the theory of small schemes, vastly generalizing the results of del Pezzo and Bertini [EGHP06].
These results fit into a larger program devoted to understanding the geometry of syzygies in projec-
tive space, beginning with Green’s theorem on linear syzygies of smooth curves [Gre84a, Gre84b]. A
recent trend has focused on extending the “geometry of syzygies” to a multigraded setting, exchang-
ing embeddings into Pn for embeddings into more general toric varieties, including weighted projective
spaces. For some examples, see [MS04,HSS06,BES20,BHS22,Cob23,HHL24,BE24b,BHSY25,CH25].
These extensions have seen applications beyond the realm of toric varieties; for example, the weighted
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity introduced in [Ben04] has been applied to the study of group cohomol-
ogy and invariant rings [Sym10,Sym11].
Of particular relevance to us are [BE24a, BE25], in which the authors develop analogues of Green’s
Np conditions for weighted projective spaces P(w0,...,wn), discuss several notions of Castelnuovo-
Mumford regularity in the weighted setting, and prove an analogue of Green’s theorem for curves in
P(1,...,1,m,...,m). These results show that the interplay between geometry and syzygies remains quite
robust in the weighted setting. We further explore these connections by initiating a study of minimal
degree varieties in weighted projective spaces. A theory of varieties of minimal degree in P(w), beyond
being interesting in its own right, could pave the way for a tighter connection between the geometry of
subvarieties in P(w) and their free resolutions. For instance, as suggested in [BE25, Section 7.2], such a
theory could lead toward a weighted analogue of the Green–Lazarsfeld gonality conjecture, relating the
syzygies of a curve to its special embeddings into varieties of minimal degree.

The primary goal of this paper is to initiate a study of varieties of minimal degree in weighted projective
space, including the development of a notion of weighted scrolls, and to explore the relationship between
degree and syzygies of weighted projective varieties. To this end, we consider the following questions:
(Question 1) What is the minimal degree of a non-degenerate subvariety of a weighted projective space?
(Question 2) Which subvarieties attain this minimal degree?
(Question 3) Do minimal degree varieties in weighted projective space have simple syzygies?

The answer to Question 2 is more complicated. Varieties of minimal degree need not be arithmetically
Cohen–Macaulay. Furthermore, there are in general infinitely many varieties of minimal degree up to
projective equivalence. These pathologies are already present for curves in simple weighted projective
spaces; P(1,1,2,2) contains a curve of minimal degree that is not arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay, and
P(12,24) contains infinitely many projective equivalence classes of minimal degree curves (Example 3.7
and Example 3.8).
In projective space, every variety of minimal degree is cut out by the 2×2 minors of a matrix of linear
forms. In light of this and the challenges to the classification problem discussed above, our approach to
Question (2) is to understand analogues of linear determinantal varieties for weighted projective spaces.
To this end, we introduce what we believe to be a robust analogue of rational normal scrolls in the weighted
setting. Our theory of weighted scrolls is rooted in the determinantal presentation in the classical case:
we define a class of weighted projective varieties cut out by the minors of matrices satisfying a weighted
version of the classical 1-genericity condition.

Definitions 4.1 and 4.3. Let M be a matrix with homogeneous entries in S(w) = k[x0,...,xn] where
wi = deg(xi). Assume that all entries in a given column have the same degree. The profile of M is the
sequence of degrees of the entries in its first row. A generalized row of a matrix M is a nontrivial k-linear
combination of the rows of M, and a generalized entry is a nontrivial k-linear combination of the entries
of a generalized row. We say that the matrix M is pseudo 1-generic if the entries of any generalized
row are a regular sequence in S(w)(x0,...,xn). If M is pseudo 1-generic and its profile can be obtained by
deleting entries from (w0,...,wn), then we say M is 1-generic.
When S is standard graded, pseudo 1-genericity is equivalent to the usual 1-genericity [Eis05, Section
6B]. In this case, all the entries of M must be linear and pseudo 1-genericity is equivalent to the nonva-
nishing of any generalized entry. Just as in the classical case, the vanishing locus of the maximal minors
of a p×q pseudo 1-generic matrix over S is an arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay scheme of codimension
q−p+1 (Proposition 4.5). However, we cannot ensure integrality in general (Example 4.11).
Definition 4.10. Let M be a 1-generic matrix in S(w) with two rows. If X = V(I2(M)) ⊆P(w) is integral
we call X a (weighted) determinantal scroll.
This definition is not restrictive; we show in Proposition 4.9 that if X is integral and defined by the
minors of a matrix M with two rows, then M must be pseudo 1-generic. Furthermore, for divisible
spaces, one can always find 1-generic matrices. If an entry of the profile increases (for instance to be
larger than a weight in the ambient weighted projective space), then the degree of the scroll increases as
well; thus, if we are interested in varieties of minimal degree, 1-genericity is a natural condition.

We turn finally to Question 3 for determinantal weighted scrolls. Classically, the simplicity of syzygies
is measured either via the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity or the Np properties. In weighted projective
space, there are several distinct notions of regularity, of which we focus on two: weighted regularity and
Koszul regularity (see Section 5.2). We explicitly compute the Betti numbers of the determinantal scrolls
and analyze their weighted and Koszul regularity as well as their weighted Np properties (Definition 5.5).
Theorem D (Corollary 5.12 and Theorem 5.8).
Let X be a d-dimensional determinantal scroll in a
divisible weighted projective space.
(1) X is a variety of minimal degree if and only if its weighted (resp. Koszul) regularity is minimal
among all the d-dimensional determinantal scrolls.
(2) X is weighted Np for all p ⩾0.
This result provides a notable juxtaposition to projective space, where the linearity of the resolution
exactly captures whether a variety has minimal degree. In contrast, there is no reasonable notion of a

“linear resolution” that detects minimal degree in the weighted setting. In particular, we can construct
varieties whose resolutions are “Koszul linear” in the sense of [BE25], yet whose degree is much larger

In Section 6, we explore non-divisible weighted projective spaces, leveraging our results for divisible
spaces to obtain degree bounds even in the absence of divisibility. We work this out for curves in weighted
projective threefolds P(1,1,m,n) for arbitrary m,n. We also study determinantal curves in a general
P(w0,w1,w2,w3) and give an example of a weighted projective threefold in which a curve of minimal
degree cannot be determinantal. Finally, we conclude with a number of natural follow-up questions.

Before defining the weighted versions of these measurements, we first recall the definitions in the
standard case. Let M be a module over S with homogeneous maximal ideal m, and let F• →M be its

In addition to Conjecture 6.4, we conclude with several natural questions regarding divisible weighted
projective spaces.
We have seen that not all varieties of minimal degree are arithmetically Cohen–
Macaulay; as a starting point, it would be useful to characterize this class.
Question 6.8. Are all arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay varieties of minimal degree in P(w) determinantal?
We know that all curves of minimal degree are rational and smooth.
Question 6.9. Determine which maps from stacky projective lines to P(w) correspond to curves of mini-
mal degree in P(w). Which have images that are arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay?
Question 6.10. Geometrically classify the curves of minimal degree in P(w). Let Q(t) be the Hilbert
quasi-polynomial of a curve of minimal degree in P(w). To answer this question, one can try to understand
the components of the Hilbert scheme HilbQ(t)(P(w)). Is it irreducible? If not, what are its components,
and what do their general members look like? Are the components generically smooth? A good starting
case would be the curves in P(1,1,2,2).
We showed in Corollary 5.12 that scrolls of minimal degree have minimal Koszul and weighted regu-
larity, but saw in Theorem 5.15 that in the non Cohen–Macaulay case the weighted regularity at least may
increase. How general is this phenomenon?
Question 6.11. Let X,Y ⊆P(w) be two varieties of minimal degree, and assume that X is arithmetically
Cohen–Macaulay and Y is not. How do the weighted and Koszul regularities of X and Y compare?
As seen in Remark 4.19, the Kronecker–Weierstrass form is not determined up to projective equiva-
lence.
Question 6.12. Describe the Aut(P(w))-orbits of the weighted 1-generic matrices; that is, determine
when two Kronecker–Weierstrass forms are projectively equivalent. As a first step, describe the GL-orbits
of 2×n matrices of linear forms.
