MATEUSZ MICHA LEK, LEONID MONIN, AND BOTONG WANG
Abstract. Let P Ă Rn be a convex polytope and let ℓbe a linear functional which is nonconstant
on every edge of P. The induced acyclic orientation determines positive and negative Bia lynicki-
Birula type partitions of P into unions of relative interiors of faces. Our first result establishes a
duality: the positive partition is a stratification if and only if the negative one is a stratification.
Our second result connects poset invariants with monotone path polytopes. Assuming the in-
duced vertex relation admits the structure of a graded poset, we prove that the Chow polynomial
of the resulting vertex poset agrees with the h-polynomial of a (dual) monotone path polytope.
1. Introduction
Let P Ă Rn be a convex polytope and let ℓ: Rn Ñ R be a linear functional which is nonconstant
on every edge of P. Then ℓinduces an acyclic orientation of the 1-skeleton of P by directing each
edge from the endpoint of smaller ℓ-value to the endpoint of larger ℓ-value. We denote by ˆ0 (resp. ˆ1)
the unique vertex minimizing (resp. maximizing) ℓon P.
A basic object attached to pP, ℓq is a Bia lynicki-Birula type partition of P into subsets indexed
by the vertices. For a vertex v, let O´pvq (resp. O`pvq) be the union of relative interiors of faces G
of P on which ℓattains its maximum (resp. minimum) at v (see Definition 2.1). Intuitively, O´pvq
collects points whose “sink” (within the minimal face containing the point) is v, while O`pvq collects
points whose “source” is v.
Although tO´pvquv and tO`pvquv are always partitions of P, they need not be stratifications in the
topological sense: it may happen that the closure of two strata intersect without one being contained
in the other. Our first goal is to understand the combinatorial structure on the vertex set which
determines whether the BB-type partitions are stratifications.
There are several natural relations on the vertices induced by ℓ. First, the directed 1-skeleton
defines the chain order C by declaring Cpv, wq if there exists a directed chain v Ñ ¨ ¨ ¨ Ñ w. Second,
we consider the witness relation O, where Opv, wq holds if there exists a face G of P such that ℓ
attains its minimum on G at v and its maximum on G at w.
Third, we introduce positive and
negative Bruhat-type relations B˘ (see Section 2). In general, O need not be transitively closed, so
it may fail to be a partial order, even though its transitive closure is always C.
Our first main result is a symmetry statement that is not apparent from the definitions.

The structure becomes significantly simpler after imposing an irreducibility condition motivated
by geometry. Let F `pvq and F ´pvq be the closure of O`pvq and O´pvq, respectively. In general

Irreducibility Assumption. For every vertex v, both F ´pvq and F `pvq are faces of P.
Under Irreducibility Assumption we obtain a collection of equivalent characterizations of the strat-

The final section connects these polyhedral structures back to poset invariants. Motivated by poset
theory—specifically Stanley’s theory of kernels and Kazhdan–Lusztig–Stanley polynomials [Sta92]
and the recent notion of Chow functions/polynomials of posets [FMV24, Pro18]—we obtain a concrete
bridge between combinatorics and geometry in the simple CHpPq setting: we exhibit a natural kernel

polynomial of the Chow quotient, identified as the h-polynomial of a dual monotone path polytope

Geometric motivations. Bia lynicki-Birula in his fundamental work [BB73] introduced canonical
decompositions of algebraic varieties endowed with a C˚–action. When the fixed point set is finite,
each fixed point v has an attracting cell consisting of those x for which limtÑ8 t¨x “ v, and a repelling
cell defined using t Ñ 0. For a projective toric variety XP associated to a polytope P, choosing such
a one–parameter subgroup is equivalent to choosing a linear functional ℓthat is non–constant on
edges. In this case the torus fixed points are the vertices of P, and the Bia lynicki-Birula cells coincide
(via the moment map) with our polyhedral cells O´pvq and O`pvq. Thus the stratification problem
for O˘ is the toric avatar of the question when BB decompositions behave well.
The witness relation O and its transitive closure C have a direct geometric meaning: Opv, wq holds
precisely when there exists a C˚–orbit whose closure connects the fixed points v and w, and C records
chains of such orbits. In this sense our main symmetry theorem O´ stratifies ô O` stratifies reflects
a duality between attracting and repelling BB cells.
Beyond toric varieties, C˚–actions on flag varieties and Grassmannians lead to Bruhat and Richard-
son stratifications. For suitable actions the BB cells coincide with Schubert cells, motivating our
introduction of Bruhat–type relations on vertices. Classically, Chow quotients of Grassmannians by
C˚–actions produce important spaces such as the variety of complete collineations [Tha99]. Its toric
counterpart is the fact that the Chow quotient of pP1qn by the diagonal C˚–action is the permutohe-
dral variety [BS92], i.e. the toric variety of the monotone path polytope of the cube. Via the natural
embedding pP1qn ãÑ Grpn, 2nq this identifies the permutohedral variety as a subvariety of the variety
of complete collineations [Mic23, DMS25].
These ideas play a central role in recent work on compactifications of linear spaces. In particu-
lar, closures of linear subspaces in pP1qn—arrangement Schubert varieties [AB16]—may be singular,
but their Chow quotients by suitable C˚–actions are the smooth wonderful models of matroids, a
phenomenon highlighted in the groundbreaking work of Huh [Huh14]. This striking improvement
of singularities motivated our study of when the monotone path polytope CHpPq is simple. More
generally, C˚–actions and their Chow quotients have been intensively studied in recent years, see for
instance [Kap93, KT04, GM11, BHK15, OSCRW19, MMW21, MMM`23, ORCW24, Knu25].
Organization of the paper. Section 2 introduces the relations on vertices and proves various

consequences culminating in Theorem 4.13.

Given a face F of P, a directed edge v Ñ w in F is called saturated if there does not exist a chain
of at least two directed edges from v to w in F. Moreover, we call a chain of directed edges saturated
in F if each of its directed edge is saturated in F. Obviously, given a chain of directed edges in a
face F from v to w, there exists a saturated chain of directed edges in F from v to w which contains
every vertex of the original chain.

is saturated in the face ACD, but not in the face CDE. Nevertheless, if a chain is saturated in a
face F and belongs to a face G Ă F, then it is also saturated in G.

3.1. Fiber polytopes and Chow quotients. In their seminal papers, Billera and Sturmfels [BS92]
and Kapranov, Sturmfels and Zelevinsky [KSZ91] developed a combinatorial framework for taking
quotients of toric varieties by (possibly low dimensional) tori. In our setting, the key point is that the

can be described as a fiber polytope. In case of action of one-dimensional torus C˚, fiber polytopes
are also called monotone path polytopes and described in detail in [BS92, Section 5].

monotone path polytope is defined as the Minkowski sum of explicitly described cuts of P given by

the dimension of the monotone path polytope CHℓpPq is always dim P ´ 1. When P is a point, we
define CHpPq to be a point as well.
