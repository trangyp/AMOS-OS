EBRU NAY˙IR AND MEL˙ISSA ¨OZSAHAKYAN
Abstract. We show that the VC-density in certain theories of oriented
abelian groups is at most the size of parameter variables, which yields
dp-minimality.
We further prove that the VC-density of formulas in
pairs of such models is bounded by twice the size of parameter variables.
This uniform upper bound is shown to be sharp, and as a consequence,
we show that such pairs have dp-rank 2.
1. Introduction
In this paper, we study oriented abelian groups and their pairs, with a
focus on understanding the combinatorial complexity of definable sets via
VC-density. An oriented abelian group A is a group equipped with a cyclic
ternary relation O, called orientation, that is preserved under group opera-
tion such that the set

In Section 3, we turn our focus to computing the VC-density of formulas
in the theories described above. The notion of VC-density is a refinement of
VC-dimension (Vapnik and Chervonenkis-dimension). Laskowski was the
first to give a characterization of NIP theories via VC-dimension, thereby
introducing this notion into model theory. Indeed, he proved that a complete
first-order theory has NIP if and only if VC-dimension of any formula is finite
(See [2]). For further background, we refer [1] to the reader and do not go
into any other details for the notions VC-dimension and VC-density.
A crucial concept for our purposes is that of uniform definability of types
over finite sets with parameters (UDTFS) (See [5]). In [1], Aschenbrenner
et al. introduce a stronger version of this notion, namely the VC d property,
that ensures a uniform bound on the VC-density of formulas in terms of
the size of parameter variables. In particular, VC 1 property implies dp-
minimality and yields a linear bound on VC-density.
We give necessary
tools in Section 3, following [1] as our main reference. Using the quantifier
elimination results of the theories of interest established in Section 2, we
actually prove the following results.
Theorem 1.2. The LO-theories of divisible oriented abelian groups have
the VC 1 property.
Theorem 1.3. The Lp
O-theories of pairs of divisible oriented abelian groups
described in Theorem 1.1 have the VC 2 property.
We also show that the upper bound derived from the second theorem is
optimal.
It is already known that divisible oriented abelian groups with
torsion, as well as pairs whose universe is divisible and torsion, have NIP, as
shown in [9]. As a by-product, we also deduce that all theories in Theorem
1.1 have NIP and dp-rank 2.

As mentioned in the Introduction, oriented minimality (O-minimality)
can be regarded as the counterpart of o-minimality in ordered groups. An
oriented abelian group A is called O-minimal if every definable subset of A
is a finite union of points and orientation intervals, where ‘definable’ refers
to sets definable in the structure (A, +, O), possibly with parameters. It is
well-known that a densely ordered abelian group (G, +, <) is o-minimal if
and only if it is divisible (See [4]); similarly, by [6] an oriented abelian group
A is O-minimal exactly when A |= T(⃗0, ⃗∞). Moreover, as in the ordered
case, it is shown in [9] that O-minimality implies NIP.
Having established that the theory T(⃗0, ⃗∞) has NIP, we note that, in
the following subsection, similar properties are also valid for pairs of such
models, which serves as a motivating role for our VC-density calculations.

3. VC-Density Calculations
Let L be a first order language and ψ(⃗x; ⃗y) be a partitioned L-formula in
object variables ⃗x = (x1, . . . , xm) and parameter variables ⃗y = (y1, . . . , yn).
The shatter function, VC-dimension, and VC-density associated to ψ are
elementary invariants and our original theories are complete, we will sim-
ply use the notations πψ, VC(ψ), and vc(ψ) to denote these combinatorial
parameters, respectively.
In [1], Aschenbrenner et al. introduce general methods yielding uniform
bounds on VC-density of formulas. One of them, which will be a key tool for
our main theorems, is stated below as Theorem 3.2. Since we are interested
in theories with quantifier elimination, instead of counting the diversity of
types over finite sets, we will benefit from the relation between breadth and
UDTFS. A collection S of sets is said to have breadth d if any nonempty
finite intersection of members of S is given by the intersection of d of them.
The following result shows that finite breadth gives uniform definability of
types over finite sets.

be a finite set of partitioned L-formulas in the single object variable x. If
