Kramers-Kronig Relations and Causality in Non-Markovian Open Quantum
Dynamics: Kernel, State, and Effective Kernel
Kejun Liu1, ∗
1State Key Laboratory of Bioinspired Interface Material Science,
Institute of Nano & Functional Materials, Soochow University, Suzhou 215123, China
(Dated: April 29, 2026)
Kramers-Kronig (KK) relations have not, to the best of our knowledge, been systematically for-
mulated for the central object of non-Markovian open quantum dynamics: the Nakajima-Zwanzig
(NZ) memory kernel. We organize the analysis around three distinct objects – the memory kernel
˜K(z), the reduced-state Laplace transform ˜σ(z), and the force-fit effective kernel ˜Keff(z) – each with
different sensitivity to initial system-bath correlations.
Under an explicit real-axis spectral-representation hypothesis for the projected generator, with a
coupling-weighted spectral density in L1∩Lp, ˜K(z) belongs to the vector-valued Hardy space Hp
+(B)
and obeys the corresponding KK or subtracted KK relations. Three consequences follow: a CPTP-
Hardy consistency criterion (uncancelled upper-half-plane kernel poles imply dynamics incompatible
with uniformly bounded CPTP reduction), a passivity-analyticity compatibility statement via the
Herglotz-Nevanlinna class for passive bosonic baths, and a finite-truncation Carleman diagnostic for
Pad´e reconstructions.
The reduced-state transform ˜σ(z) is analytic in the upper half-plane for any initial state, factorized
or correlated; microscopic unitarity forces ∥σ(t)∥op ≤1. In scalar channels, the force-fit effective
kernel ˜Keff(z) = ˜K(z) + ˜I(z)˜σ(z)−1 can develop upper-half-plane poles at simple zeros of ˜σ(z).
Passive bosonic baths occupy a robust regime where these zeros stay on the real axis, while near-
resonant systems can enter a fragile regime in which coherence-channel zeros migrate into the upper
half-plane – an intrinsic property of the channel symmetry, existing already for factorized initial
states, with initial correlations controlling only whether the inhomogeneous numerator fails to cancel
the pole.
We verify the bath-correlator KK at Born order (a proxy check), the robust-regime self-consistency
for the independent boson model, and – in the Jaynes-Cummings model, whose projected generator
has real spectrum – the full operator-valued KK relation for the extracted 4 × 4 NZ memory kernel.
The measured integrated relative L2 residual of 3.8% sits at or below the circular FFT-Hilbert
pipeline’s calibrated noise floor (≈5% at Nt=4000 on a known-analytic test function), so the data
are consistent with exact matrix-valued KK within the protocol’s accuracy and do not certify a
physical violation. In the Jaynes-Cummings model, a scalar force-fit extraction of the coherence-
channel effective kernel gives a slight increase of the KK residual under initial correlation (ratio
≈1.17 at Laplace shift ε = 0.3, rising where finite-truncation leakage dominates) – a qualitative
finite-truncation diagnostic rather than a quantitative prediction.
I.
INTRODUCTION
Apparent acausality in reduced open-quantum-system
dynamics does not, in general, signal a breakdown of
physical causality. When a modeller discards the inho-
mogeneous initial-correlation term and force-fits a homo-
geneous Volterra equation to correlated initial data, the
resulting effective memory kernel can develop upper-half-
plane poles. In scalar channels, the pole mechanism is
simple: an uncancelled simple zero of the reduced-state
Laplace transform ˜σ(z) becomes a pole of ˜Keff(z) through
the quotient term ˜I(z)˜σ(z)−1. The reduced-state trans-
form itself is never acausal, a consequence of microscopic
unitarity: ∥σ(t)∥op ≤1 forces ˜σ(z) to be analytic in H+
for any initial state, factorized or correlated. The exact
memory kernel is also well-behaved when the projected
dynamics admits a real-axis spectral representation with

JC kernel does not decay (Poincar´e recurrence in the

the KK check is no longer reliable for this finite model.

genuine kernel decay), the ε →0+ limit should be better
defined, but the finite-system scan presented here should
not be extrapolated without a continuous-bath calcula-
tion.

VIII.
DISCUSSION
Our results adapt Gavassino’s causality-from-coarse-
graining framework [1–3] to open quantum systems, in
the negative/diagnostic form described in the Introduc-
tion. Theorem 1 captures the “correct initialization pre-
serves causality” part of that picture: the factorized ini-
tial state is the quantum analogue of Gavassino’s causal
preparation. Theorem 7 captures the stability-causality
chain for passive bosonic baths in a conditional form: just
as thermodynamic stability constrains causal dispersion
in relativistic systems [1], dissipative passivity is compat-
ible with the Hardy-class analyticity and KK reconstruc-
tion supplied by the spectral hypothesis. The correlated-
state diagnostics in Sec. VII, together with the Jaynes-
Cummings coherence-kernel extraction in Sec. VI, illus-
trate the “stadium wave” mechanism [3]: correlations in
the joint state, combined with discarding the inhomoge-
neous term I(t), encode information that can masquer-
ade as acausal behaviour of the force-fit effective kernel.
The principal numerical result is the operator-valued KK
verification (Sec. VII): the measured 3.8% integrated rel-
ative L2 residual is at or below the ≈5% circular-Hilbert
noise floor calibrated on a known-analytic test function,
so the extracted 4×4 NZ memory kernel is consistent with
exact matrix-valued KK within the present protocol’s ac-
curacy. Establishing a quantitative sub-baseline residual
would require a calibrated non-circular Hilbert protocol,
which we identify as an immediate open problem. The
scalar effective-kernel diagnostic, by contrast, is a sugges-
tive but model-dependent signature whose quantitative
residual ratio depends on the Laplace regularization and
finite-truncation artifacts.

Experimental outlook.
The modified KK residual
∆(ω; {ζj}) in Eq. (19) is a concrete frequency-domain
signature of a force-fit reduced description; turning this
into a direct experimental test requires closing the input-
output gap between the force-fit kernel and the measured
susceptibility. Candidate THz platforms and the specific
steps needed are discussed in Sec. VIII A.
Extensions not covered here include: non-Hermitian
effective Hamiltonians, where Blaschke-Nevanlinna de-
composition provides residue-corrected KK [24]; ther-
mal/Matsubara structure, where the KMS condition
introduces additional imaginary-time constraints; and
exceptional-point singularities, where branch cuts on
multi-sheeted Riemann surfaces modify the dispersion
structure. The present paper identifies non-unitary mi-
crodynamics (PT -symmetric dimers, gain media, and re-
lated systems) as the boundary at which the framework
may break down, and leaves the rigorous analysis of that
regime open.

The THz correlation fingerprint
The modified KK relation (19) suggests a possible ex-
perimental signature of initial correlations in the fre-
quency domain. Consider a strong-coupling system, such
as a molecular crystal or a polaritonic cavity, interrogated
by terahertz (THz) spectroscopy. If the measured sus-
ceptibility can be modelled by the same force-fit reduced
kernel, then the absorption spectrum and refractive re-
sponse should inherit the Hilbert-pair structure of the
causal kernel.
In the fragile regime, Eq. (19) predicts a Lorentzian
residual ∆(ω) in the force-fit memory kernel.
Each
uncancelled upper-half-plane zero ζj of ˜σ0 contributes
a Lorentzian term centered at Re(ζj) with half-width
Im(ζj).
If this reduced-kernel description is the right
effective response variable, the correction could appear
as a “phantom resonance” — a feature in the reactive
response without the usual dissipative counterpart — or
as an anomalous line-shape asymmetry in 2D THz or
2D-IR echo spectroscopy. A plausible platform is a vi-
brational polariton or cavity-QED system near vacuum
Rabi splitting ωRabi ∼2g in the THz regime. Prepar-
ing a joint thermal state of system plus cavity, rather
than a factorized product, and tuning g toward stronger
coupling may move reduced-state zeros toward the up-
per half-plane. Turning this into a direct experimental
test requires deriving the measured susceptibility from
the same force-fit object and is left as future work.
IX.
OPEN PROBLEMS
The present framework suggests several directions.
Numerical extensions.
The Jaynes-Cummings veri-
fication in Sec. VII supports the matrix-valued KK re-
lation for a model whose projected generator has real
spectrum.
Extending this to the spin-boson model at
moderate-to-strong coupling – where QLQ develops com-
plex eigenvalues – requires either a modified Hilbert-pair
condition on the shifted spectral contour or a direct ex-
traction from HEOM/pseudomode data.
On the cali-
bration side, the operator-valued KK residual reported
in Sec. VII (integrated relative L2 of 3.8%) sits at or
below the noise floor of a circular FFT-Hilbert pipeline
(≈5% at Nt = 4000).
Quantifying genuine intrinsic
residuals requires a calibrated protocol that uses a non-
circular (aperiodic) Hilbert transform with endpoint ta-
pering, extrapolates to the Nt →∞and ωmax →∞
limits against a bank of analytically known test kernels,
and reports channel-by-channel absolute L2 errors with
error bars traceable to the tapering and extrapolation
steps.
Topological classification. Fig. 1 shows that the co-
herence channel of ˜σ0(z) already hosts UHP zeros in a
finite Jaynes-Cummings model. What remains is a quan-
titative classification: as a coupling or detuning parame-
ter is varied, how do the zeros move, and is the transition
from robust to fragile labelled by a winding number of
˜σ0(z) along a closed contour in H+? A perturbation the-
ory in the initial-correlation strength ϵ, combined with a
direct reconstruction of ˜Keff(z) from correlated JC trajec-
tories, would test whether a critical parameter γc exists
at which the number of UHP zeros changes discontinu-
ously, protected by a topological invariant – a “causal-
ity phase transition” connecting non-Hermitian physics,
topological phases, and open-quantum-system theory.
Experimental contact.
The modified KK residual
∆(ω) of Eq. (19) is a diagnostic, not yet a prediction.
Bridging this gap requires an input-output derivation
that relates the measured THz susceptibility to the force-
fit kernel, a specific phantom-resonance prediction for a
given correlated initial state, and a protocol that dis-
tinguishes the force-fit artifact from genuine drive/gain
physics. The author’s experimental background in THz
spectroscopy provides a natural pathway.
Mathematical refinements.
Several open problems
remain at the formal level. The operator-valued Titch-
marsh converse – if a memory kernel satisfies KK, must
it be causal and Hardy?
– has no known analogue of
the scalar Titchmarsh theorem and may fail if operator-
valued Hilbert transforms have nontrivial kernels. The
sharpness of the Carleman condition for the MKCT mo-
ment problem is unsettled: for unbounded Hamiltonians
(bosonic baths in the UV), the condition may become a
genuine constraint rather than an automatic diagnostic.
Multi-time KK relations, suggested by Breuer’s exten-
sion of the NZ formalism to multitime correlation func-
tions [41], would connect the present single-time frame-
work to nonlinear response theory and two-dimensional
spectroscopy.
Non-Hermitian regime.
Extension to structured
baths (multi-peak spectral densities, band-gap environ-
ments) tests the framework where Pad´e reconstructions
are most needed.
Non-equilibrium baths – driven,
squeezed, gain media – form the natural frontier: gain-
induced poles appear in H+, and the Blaschke prod-
uct B(z) = Q
k(z −¯zk)/(z −zk) factors them out,
yielding ˜K(z) = B(z) ˜Kpassive(z) where ˜Kpassive satis-
fies standard KK with explicit residue corrections. The
Blaschke winding number, counting UHP poles of the
gain-corrected kernel, naturally labels the robust/fragile
transition at the unitary/non-unitary boundary and con-
nects the present framework to active photonic environ-
ments, PT -symmetric dimers, and Floquet-engineered
baths.

Implications
These numerical tests support the conditional formula-
tion adopted in the main text. For the Jaynes–Cummings
model — the setting of Fig. 2 and Sec. VI — the real-
spectrum part of the spectral hypothesis is satisfied in
the tested finite truncations; strict Hardy membership
still requires the continuous-density assumption stated
in Theorem 2. For the spin-boson model at moderate-

does not apply without additional structural assump-
