The presence of degeneracies plays a crucial role in describing the behavior of non-Hermitian (NH)
systems. In these systems, there are two key types of degeneracies: 𝑛-bolical degeneracies, which
are analogous to Hermitian degeneracies, and various forms of exceptional points, each associated
with different orders that correspond to sizes of the Jordan blocks. These types of degeneracies
may coalesce at the same energy level, forming multi-block degeneracies.
To understand how a
multi-block degenerate NH system responds to perturbations, one should address how each types of
involved degeneracies disperse. In this work, we systematically characterize the asymptotic behavior
of all types of multi-block degeneracies in NH systems using a rigorous mathematical formulation.
Through a range of examples, we demonstrate that our algebraic approach can facilitate the analysis
of NH degeneracies in various settings relevant to experiments.
I.
INTRODUCTION
In recent years, we have witnessed a spur of inter-
est in non-Hermitian (NH) physics [1–4], which gov-
erns effective dissipation of information through par-
ticle/energy exchange between a subsystem and its
surrounding environment in a physical system. One
of the distinct properties of NH systems, without
counterparts in Hermitian physics, is the possibility
of hosting Exceptional Points (EPs), spectral sin-
gularities characterized by the simultaneous coales-
cence of both eigenvalues and eigenvectors. Explor-
ing the behavior of NH systems near EPs has yielded
interesting results from both theoretical and practi-
cal perspectives. The emergence of eigenvalue braid-
ing and their exotic topology [5–8], appearance of
EPs on phase boundaries of coupled/correlated sys-
tems [9–13], enhancement of sensors’ sensitivity [14–
22] and speeding up entanglement generation near
EPs [23] exemplify exciting results of investigating
EPs in NH systems.
Exploration of EPs in the literature primarily fo-
cuses on three main research themes. (i) Classifying
all possible degeneracies hosted by an NH system,
described by a matrix.
Examples of such matri-
ces are NH Hamiltonians [24–27] and the vectorized
form of the Liouvillian super-operators [9, 28–31].
(ii) Characterizing the asymptotic behavior of EPs
upon varying a tuning parameter [32–35]. Under-
standing the role of perturbations in either lifting

or maintaining NH degeneracies is a goal of such
studies, often motivated by experimental or practical
considerations. (iii) Providing a seemingly intuitive
and computationally stable formulation to theoreti-

retical methods using discriminant and resultant to
characterize certain types of EPs [41] is an outcome
of such studies. In this work and in our recent pa-

The coalescence of eigenvalues in non-Hermitian
models may occur when the geometric multiplicity
of the system is either smaller than or equal to its
algebraic multiplicity. In the former, the matrix is
defective [43], as the dimension of the eigenspace
is smaller than the rank of the matrix, and in the
latter, the matrix is nondefective.
For this rea-
son, EPs are referred to as defective degeneracies,
while Hermitian-like degeneracies are called nonde-
fective degeneracies, commonly known as 𝑚bolical
points, where 𝑚is the number of degenerate eigen-
values. Mathematically, all degeneracies in NH ma-
trices can be split into two different categories: i)
Non-derogatory EPs occur when the geometric mul-
tiplicity of the degenerate subspace of size 𝑛is one
while algebraic multiplicity equals 𝑛. ii) Derogatory
degeneracies emerge when the degenerate subspace
comprises multiple Jordan blocks, with sizes greater
than or equal to one; thus, 𝑚bolical points are in-
cluded in this category.
This type of degeneracy
has been referred to as fragmented EPs [44, 45] or
multiblock EPs [46]. The physical properties of the
derogatory EPs are mainly unexplored. Of our par-
ticular interest is the behavior of different degen-

Employing algebraic approaches, in particular,
amoeba theory has recently advanced the field of
NH skin effect [52–56], a phenomenon where a large
number of eigenvectors localizes at the boundaries of
the NH system. Mathematically, under certain lim-
its, amoebas [57] reduce to their spines, also known
as tropical diagrams or tropical curves [58]. Aside
from the amoeba theory, tropical diagrams are nicely
formulated within the modern field of tropical ge-
ometry.
By redefining summation and multiplica-
tion operators in this field, one can map a polyno-
mial into a tropical polynomial, which is a piecewise-
linear function.
Tropical diagrams are the visual-
ization of these linear functions. Ref. [50] presents
the possibility of employing tropical polynomials in
exploring NH degeneracies on a handful of exam-
ples. Another related description is Newton poly-
topes, which arise naturally when one tries to build a
systematic series expansion of the eigenvalues in the
perturbation parameter; a set of information that is
also accessible through the formulation of tropical
geometry. In this work, we present a self-contained
description of tropical polynomials, clarify their re-
lation to Newton polygons, and demonstrate how
these algebraic approaches allow us to characterize
all non-Hermitian degeneracies systematically.
The present work is arranged as follows. In Sec. II,
we discuss the eigenvalue perturbation problem and
introduce the tools necessary for its analysis: New-
ton polygons and tropical polynomials.
In Sec-
tions III, IV and V, we analyze the eigenvalue split-
ting for both generic and non-generic perturbations
for systems described by square matrices of size 2,
3 and 4, respectively. In Sec. VI, we apply the re-
sults from previous sections to the physically moti-
vated examples. Finally, we conclude this paper in
Sec. VII.

VII.
CONCLUSION
In this work, we have presented a systematic study
on characterizing eigenvalues under generic and non-
generic perturbations. We have explored all possi-
ble degeneracies hosted by non-Hermitian systems,
namely, 𝑛-bolical degeneracies and different types of
coexisting exceptional points. Our study goes be-
yond the main focus of the literature, which has ex-
plored exceptional points associated with geometric
multiplicity one.
By employing techniques from tropical geometry,


## Page 20


20
we have characterized the asymptotic dispersion of
NH degeneracies with any geometric multiplicity un-
der perturbation and identified the associated alge-
braic multiplicities of these degeneracies. By apply-
ing our algebraic techniques to 2 × 2, 3 × 3, and
4 × 4 matrices, we demonstrate how the tropical
geometric approach can naturally classify all possi-
ble degeneracies under both generic and non-generic
perturbations. To bring our theoretical formulation
into practical applications, we have further demon-
strated the applicability of our approach across vari-
ous settings, including sensing devices, nonreciprocal
systems, open quantum systems, and lattice models
relevant to condensed matter systems.
Having a formulation that tackles both generic
and non-generic perturbations is an asset for study-
ing NH degeneracies. This is because the behavior
of NH systems under generic perturbations is merely
determined by the type of NH degeneracies being
perturbed.
However, under non-generic perturba-
tion, the details of how the system is perturbed also
play a critical role. This opens a wide range of possi-
bilities for manipulating the degenerate structure of
NH systems. In particular, it is of interest to under-
stand how one type of EP can be transformed to an-
other types. Here, we either expect that perturbed
eigenvalues split under the action of certain pertur-
bations or the perturbation may change the type of
EP without altering its order [105]. The ability to
convert one type of EPs into another facilitates un-
derstanding the behavior of NH systems, allowing us
to engineer their properties more efficiently. In our
recent paper, we address this question employing a
rigorous mathematical formulation [42].
