Abstract
Fat minors are a coarse analogue of graph minors where the subgraphs modeling vertices and
edges of the embedded graph are required to be distant from each other, instead of just being
disjoint. In this paper, we give a coarse analogue of the classic theorem that an n-vertex graph
excluding a fixed minor admits a balanced separator of size O(pn). Specifically, we prove that for
every integer d, real ϵ > 0, and graph H, there exist constants c and r such that every n-vertex
graph G excluding H as a d-fat minor admits a set S ⊆V(G) that is a balanced separator of G and
can be covered by cn

the balance of the separator is measured with respect to any weight function on the vertices, and is
effective: we obtain a randomized polynomial-time algorithm to compute either such a balanced
separator, or a d-fat model of H in G.

Introduction
Coarse graph theory is a relatively young direction in structural graph theory, whose aim is to describe
the structure in graphs understood as metric spaces. As suggested in one of the foundational works
of Georgakopoulos and Papasoglu [10], the hope is that many classic notions and results of structural
graph theory, particularly the theory of graph minors, could be lifted to suitable coarse analogues.
The usual way of obtaining such a coarse analogue is to replace any constraints of disjointness or
intersection with the requirement that the relevant objects are far or close to each other, respectively. A
good example of such a lift is the coarse analogue of the notion of a minor, called a fat minor. Formally,
for graphs G and H, we say that a graph G contains H as a d-fat minor, for a parameter d ∈N, if there
are connected subgraphs {Bu: u ∈V(H)} and {Be : e ∈E(H)} of G such that
• whenever u is an endpoint of e, the subgraphs Bu and Be intersect; and
• except for the above, all the subgraphs in {Bf : f ∈V(H) ∪E(H)} are at distance at least d from
each other in G.

The collection of subgraphs {Bu: u ∈V(H)} and {Be : e ∈E(H)} as above is called a d-fat model of H
in G. Thus, the notion of a fat minor requires the constituent parts of the model to be far from each
other, rather than just disjoint as in the classic notion of a minor.
In the light of all the deep advances of the classic theory of graph minors, the following question
becomes natural: Supposing G excludes a fixed graph H as a d-fat minor, what can be said about the
metric structure of G? Georgakopoulos and Papasoglu [10] conjectured that such graphs are quasi-
isometric to graphs excluding H as a classic minor. Roughly speaking, this would mean that the metric
space of a graph excluding H as a d-fat minor can be mapped to a metric space of some H-minor-free
graph G′ so that the mapping preserves the distances up to a constant multiplicative and additive factor
(both depending on d and H). If this were true, then the wealth of known properties of H-minor-free
graphs could be pulled through the quasi-isometry to their coarse analogues that would apply to graphs
excluding H as a d-fat minor.
Unfortunately, as shown by Davies, Hickingbotham, Illingworth, and McCarty [7], the conjecture
of Georgakopoulos and Papasoglu turns out to be false; see also the newer works [2, 3] for simpler and
stronger counterexamples. However, many of its potential consequences — structural properties of
graphs excluding a fat minor — can still be true, at least to some extent. In this work we identify one
such property: the existence of balanced separators of sublinear size.
To measure the balance of a separator, it will be convenient to speak about vertex-weighted graphs.
A weighted graph is a graph G equipped with a vertex weight function wG : V(G) →R⩾0. (We often
omit the subscript if G is clear from the context.) A balanced separator of G is a set S ⊆V(G) such
that for every connected component C of G −S, we have w(C) ⩽w(G)/2. Here, if X is a subgraph or
a vertex subset of G, then w(X) denotes the total weight of the vertices featured in X.
It is well-known that H-minor-free graphs admit balanced separators of square root size.
Theorem 1 ([4]). For every graph H, every n-vertex weighted graph that excludes H as a minor has
a balanced separator of size OH(pn).1
Note that for planar graphs (which coincide with {K5, K3,3}-minor-free graphs), this is exactly the
Planar Separator Theorem of Lipton and Tarjan [15]. In general, the existence of square-root-sized
balanced separators is a fundamental reason behind countless combinatorial and algorithmic results
applicable to planar and H-minor-free graphs.
A natural coarse analogue of Theorem 1 would be the following statement, which we pose as
a conjecture. Here, for a graph G and a vertex subset S ⊆V(G), we say that S is (k, r)-coverable if S
can be covered by k balls of radius r in G; or in other words, there exists X ⊆V(G) with |X| ⩽k such
that every vertex of S is at distance at most r from some vertex of X.
Conjecture 1. For every graph H and d ∈N, there exist constants c, r ∈N such that every n-vertex
weighted graph G that excludes H as a d-fat minor has a (cpn, r)-coverable balanced separator.
Our results.
Our main result is a weaker form of Conjecture 1, where we lose a multiplicative factor
nϵ in the number of balls covering the separator, for any fixed ϵ > 0. In the formulation below we make
the dependence on H, d, and ϵ explicit, as it is actually not too terrible.
Theorem 2. For every graph H, integer d ∈N, and real ϵ > 0, the following holds: Every n-vertex weighted
graph that excludes H as a d-fat minor has a balanced separator that is (O(∥H∥2 · n1/2+ϵ),O(d/ϵ))-
coverable, where ∥H∥= |V(H)| + |E(H)|.
Furthermore, there is a randomized polynomial-time algorithm that, given a weighted graph G, either
returns such a balanced separator in G or finds a d-fat minor model of H in G.
We remark that while several previous works studied the structure of H-fat-minor-free graphs for
specific graphs H, to the best of our knowledge, Theorem 2 offers the first non-trivial structural property
of H-fat-minor-free graphs proved for an arbitrary H. The algorithm of Theorem 2 can actually report

Our techniques.
Our proof of Theorem 2 is inspired by the following result of Korhonen and
Lokshtanov [11] about the existence of balanced separators in induced-minor-free graphs.
Theorem 5 ([11, Theorem 1.1]). For every graph H, every m-edge weighted graph G that is H-induced-
minor-free has a balanced separator of size at most OH(pmlog m).
We note that in [11], this result is stated for unweighted graphs, but the proof can be easily lifted to
the weighted setting. (Alternatively, the weighted result can be obtained from the unweighted one using
a theorem of Dvořák and Norin [8, Theorem 1].) Also, the formulation in [11] postulates a stronger
bound of OH(pm), but the proof is partly based on a faulty lemma from an earlier work of Lee [13].
Without reliance on this lemma, the proof goes through with an extra log m factor.
The idea of Korhonen and Lokshtanov in the proof of Theorem 5 is to exploit the classic result of
Leighton and Rao [14] on the gap between product multicommodity flows and balanced separators.
Roughly speaking, from this result it follows that if a weighted graph G does not admit a balanced
separator of small size, then in G there is a multicommodity flow λ that pushes, for every pair of
vertices u, v, a significant amount of flow from u to v, and at the same time every vertex of G carries
only a relatively small amount of flow. Such a flow λ can be understood as a well-distributed system of
paths in G. Korhonen and Lokshtanov show that by sampling paths from this system, one can find,
with positive probability, an induced minor model of the supposedly excluded graph H, leading to a
contradiction.
In our proof of Theorem 2, we show that assuming the graph is suitably sparse, the same strategy can
be used to expose a fat minor model of H, not only an induced one. The precise notion of sparseness has
to be chosen carefully. We achieve it using the following clustering result of Filtser [9]: for every graph
G and ϵ > 0, one can partition the vertex set of G into subsets inducing subgraphs of diameter O(1/ϵ),
called clusters, so that every ball of radius 2 in G intersects only O(nϵ) clusters. The Leighton–Rao
argument is then applied to the graph G′ obtained from G by contracting every cluster to a single vertex.
Thus, a balanced separator of G′ of size OH(n1/2+ϵ) can be naturally lifted to a balanced separator of G
that can be covered by OH(n1/2+ϵ) balls of bounded radius.
The proof of Theorem 3 is much less involved. We first apply a simple clustering scheme that
vertex-partitions the graph into connected subgraphs, each dominated by a single vertex, so that
contracting the subgraphs yields a graph with OH(n4/3 log2/3 n) edges. Then we apply Theorem 5

Graphs.
All graphs considered in this paper are finite, simple (with no parallel edges or loops), and
undirected, unless explicitly stated. We use standard graph notation. In particular, for a graph G, we
write V(G) for the vertex set of G, E(G) for the edge set of G, distG(·,·) for the distance metric in G
(mapping every pair of vertices u, v to the length, i.e., number of edges, on a shortest path between u
and v), and diam(G) for the diameter of G, which is the largest distance between a pair of vertices of G.
When X, Y are subgraphs or subsets of vertices of G, we define distG(X, Y ) as the smallest distance in

Minors.
The notions of minors, fat minors, and induced minors were also already introduced in
Section 1. Similarly to the work of Korhonen and Lokshtanov [11] on induced minors, it will be useful
to work with the following relaxed notion of fat models. A crude d-fat model of a graph H in a graph G
is a pair of mappings (φ,π) such that φ maps vertices of H to vertices of G, π maps edges of H to
paths in G, and the following conditions hold:
• For every edge uv ∈E(H), π(uv) is a φ(u)–φ(v) path in G.
• For every pair of edges uv,u′v′ ∈E(H) with |{u, v,u′, v′}| = 4, we have distG(π(uv),π(u′v′)) ⩾d.
Note that we do not require the vertices φ(u), for u ∈V(H), to be far from each other, or even distinct.
The only distance constraints are between paths modeling pairs of edges that do not share endpoints.

Conjectures
Our work raises multiple questions about the existence of coverable balanced separators in fat-minor-
free and induced-minor-free graphs. The most prominent one is whether Conjecture 1 holds, or at least
improving the additional O(nϵ) factor to a polylogarithmic function of n. The only source of the O(nϵ)
factor is the clustering scheme of Lemma 1, hence we propose the following.
12


## Page 13


Conjecture 2. For every graph H there are constants p, d ∈N such that every graph G that excludes H
as a 3-fat minor admits a connected partition X of weak diameter at most d such that for every u ∈V(G),
BG(u,2) intersects at most p clusters of X.
We note that resolving Conjecture 2 would not automatically resolve Conjecture 1 because of
the additional log n factor stemming from the gap between multicommodity flows and balanced cuts
(Theorem 6). However, proving a weaker form of Conjecture 2 with p replaced by O(logα n), for some
α ∈R⩾0, would directly translate to the weaker form of Conjecture 1 with c replaced by O(logα+1 n).
We note that in Conjecture 2, it is not at all clear that the condition “every ball of radius 2 intersects
few parts of X” is the only correct one; other forms of asserting the sparsity of X may also be useful.
Noting that containing H as a 2-fat minor entails containing H as an induced minor, Theorem 2
implies that any n-vertex H-induced-minor-free graph has an (OH(n1/2+ϵ),O(1/ϵ))-coverable balanced
separator, for any ϵ > 0. As witnessed by Theorems 3 and 4, the induced-minor-free case may permit
a broader range of tools, hence we state the following weaker form of Conjecture 1.
Conjecture 3. For every graph H there exist constants c, r ∈N such that every n-vertex weighted graph G
that excludes H as an induced minor has a balanced separator that is (cpn, r)-coverable.
It would be particularly elegant if r = 1 sufficed in Conjecture 3, and Theorem 3 gives an indication
that this could indeed be the case. Towards this goal, we propose the following strengthening of
Lemma 5 as a first step.
Conjecture 4. For every graph H there exist constants c,q ∈N such that every n-vertex graph G that
excludes H as an induced minor admits a connected partition X such that every cluster of X is (q,1)-
coverable and the quotient graph G/X has at most c · n edges.
Again, a weaker form of this statement, with c or q (or both) replaced by a subpolynomial function
of n, would also be interesting.
