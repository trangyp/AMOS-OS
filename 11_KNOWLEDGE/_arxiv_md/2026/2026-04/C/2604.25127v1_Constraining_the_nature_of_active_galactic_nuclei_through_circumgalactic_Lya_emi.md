ABSTRACT
We present a comprehensive analysis of circumgalactic Lyα nebulae around 59 unobscured and 26
obscured quasars at z = 2 −3, observed with the Keck Cosmic Web Imager (KCWI), to constrain
the nature of active galactic nuclei (AGN) at cosmic noon. We find that Lyα nebulae around unob-
scured quasars are significantly less symmetric having a symmetry parameter of αw = 0.2 −0.6 and
more spatially extended having a scale length of rh = 10.7 ± 0.5 kpc than those around obscured
quasars (αw = 0.6 −0.8; rh ≈6.6 −7.7 kpc).
Unobscured quasars also exhibit steeply declining
velocity dispersion profiles with the slope of −4.3 ± 0.4 km s−1 kpc−1, indicative of large-scale out-
flows, whereas obscured quasars display flat profiles (−0.2 ± 0.7 and −0.6 ± 0.4 km s−1 kpc−1). The
degree of quasar obscuration appears to be intrinsically linked to nebular asymmetry and extent, a
relationship that could be in tension with the standard orientation-based AGN unified model, as it
expects unobscured-quasar nebulae to be more symmetric and compact. These results naturally fit
the evolutionary scenario, where AGN feedback drives a transition from an obscured to an unobscured
phase—progressively redistributing gas to larger radii, introducing anisotropy, and driving turbulence.
Taken together, our findings favor the evolutionary scenario over the purely orientation-based unified
model for quasars at cosmic noon.
Keywords: Quasar; Circumgalactic Medium; Lyα nebulae; high-redshift;
1. INTRODUCTION
The majority of baryons associated with galaxies re-
side not within their stellar bodies, but in the diffuse gas
that extends throughout their dark matter halos. This
reservoir, known as the circumgalactic medium (CGM),
plays a central role in galaxy evolution by mediating the
exchange of mass, energy, and metals between galaxies
and their surroundings. The CGM is inherently multi-
phase, spanning temperatures from 10−107 K (Li et al.
2021; Zhang et al. 2023a, 2024), and is shaped by a com-
plex interplay of large-scale gas inflows (Martin et al.
2015; Kereˇs et al. 2005; Decataldo et al. 2024), galactic
outflows (Fabian 2012; Harrison & Ramos Almeida 2024;
Vayner et al. 2024), and recycling processes (Zhang et al.
2023a,b; Angl´es-Alc´azar et al. 2017). These mechanisms
collectively regulate star formation and the growth of
supermassive black holes (SMBHs), making the CGM
a key component in understanding galaxy–SMBH co-
evolution (Tumlinson et al. 2017).
In recent years, rapid advances in deep imaging and
integral field spectroscopy (IFS) have opened a new win-
dow onto the CGM by enabling its direct mapping in
emission (Cantalupo et al. 2014; Arrigoni Battaia et al.
2019; Cai et al. 2019; Gonz´alez Lobos et al. 2026; Li et al.
2024). Since hydrogen constitutes the dominant bary-
onic component of the CGM, these observations have
primarily focused on extended Lyα emission—so-called
Lyα nebulae—powered by (i) photoionization and re-
combination due to strong incident ultraviolet radiation
(Farina et al. 2019), (ii) gravitational cooling radiation
(Daddi et al. 2021), (iii) shocks driven by energetic out-

Such direct imaging, particularly the spatially re-
solved spectra provided by IFS, has enabled a range
of fundamental measurements: imaging large-scale cos-
mic web filaments (Cantalupo et al. 2014; Daddi et al.
2021; Tornotti et al. 2025), characterizing large-scale gas
kinematics (Zhang et al. 2023a,b; Li et al. 2021), and
determining CGM metallicities (Guo et al. 2020). Be-
cause the CGM is illuminated by the central ionizing
source and part of its kinematics is driven by the galaxy
or SMBH itself, these nebulae encode information on
physical processes occurring on scales far smaller than
the nebulae themselves. Lyα nebulae can therefore serve
as powerful indirect probes of the central engine.
Beyond constraining the CGM, these large-scale neb-
ulae offer a unique window into the structure of high-
redshift active galactic nuclei (AGN) on scales of ≤100
pc that remain extremely difficult to resolve directly
(GRAVITY+ Collaboration et al. 2026). Although the
classical AGN unified model (Antonucci 1993; Urry &
Padovani 1995; Netzer 2015) has received support from
direct imaging of the dusty torus (Tristram et al. 2014;
Carilli et al. 2019) and from the comparable space den-
sities of obscured and unobscured AGN in the local Uni-
verse (Zakamska et al. 2003; Assef et al. 2013; Brandt
& Alexander 2015), mounting evidence suggests that
this purely orientation-based picture may break down
at z ≥2.
Among the most striking discoveries of the James
Webb Space Telescope (JWST) are the obscured AGN
known as little red dots (LRDs), found at 4 ≤z ≤9 to
be 3–4 orders of magnitude more abundant than their
unobscured counterparts at the same epochs (Matthee
et al. 2024; Kocevski et al. 2023; Harikane et al. 2023).
These objects display distinctive “V-shaped” spectral
energy distributions (SEDs) (Kocevski et al. 2025; Set-
ton et al. 2025; Hviding et al. 2025) and exhibit remark-
ably weak X-ray emission (Yue et al. 2024; Sacchi &
Bogd´an 2025)—properties that are difficult to recon-
cile with the predictions of the unified scheme (Richards
et al. 2006; Ross et al. 2013).
While some LRDs have been identified in the local
Universe (Lin et al. 2026), these objects are predomi-
nantly found at z = 4 −9 (Kocevski et al. 2023; Akins
et al. 2025) and decline rapidly in number density at
z ≤4 (Ma et al. 2026), suggesting that they may repre-
sent a brief but crucial stage of SMBH growth made pos-
sible only in the early Universe through super-Eddington
accretion (Inayoshi 2025). However, recent observations
have revealed that obscured quasars at z = 1.0 −3.5
share remarkably similar SEDs with LRDs, indicating
that these objects could be the luminous, low-redshift
analogues of the high-z LRD population (Wang et al.
2025c,a). Probing the small-scale physics of these ob-
scured quasars could therefore hold the key to under-
standing the nature of high-z LRDs.
Simulations of nebulae around unobscured quasars
have demonstrated that the CGM can be used to con-
strain the properties of the AGN engine, including the
SMBH mass and the Eddington ratio (Obreja et al.
2024). (Zhang et al. 2025) further showed that, under
the unified scheme, nebulae around obscured quasars
should exhibit more extended surface brightness (SB)
profiles and less symmetric morphologies than those
around unobscured quasars—providing a potential ob-
servational test of AGN orientation models. Yet, while
observations have predominantly targeted unobscured
quasars—with more than 300 Lyα nebulae now detected
(Cai et al. 2019; Arrigoni Battaia et al. 2019; Farina
et al. 2019)—only a handful of individual cases (Sander-
son et al. 2021; Zhang et al. 2023a,b) and small sam-
ples of ∼10 nebulae around obscured quasars have been
studied (Gillette et al. 2023; den Brok et al. 2020). Ex-
ploiting nebulae around different quasar types to probe
high-z AGN models thus remains largely unexplored.
In this work, we present a comprehensive analysis
of Lyα nebulae around a large sample of quasars at
z = 2 −3 observed with the Keck Cosmic Web Im-
ager (KCWI) (Morrissey et al. 2018).
By combining
archival data from multiple programs, we construct a
sample spanning a wide range of obscuration proper-
ties, including 59 unobscured and 26 obscured quasars.
This sample enables, for the first time, a controlled
comparison of CGM properties across different quasar
populations to test AGN models at cosmic noon. Af-
ter carefully comparing the nebular properties of unob-
scured and obscured quasars, we find that Lyα nebu-
lae around unobscured quasars exhibit more extended
SB profiles and less symmetric morphologies than those
around obscured quasars—a result that is opposite to
the prediction of the AGN unified scheme (den Brok
et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2025). However, this finding can
be naturally incorporated into an evolutionary scenario
(Sanders et al. 1988; Hopkins et al. 2008; Alexander &
Hickox 2012), in which the AGN transitions from an
obscured to an unobscured phase following a large-scale
feedback event.

quasar properties. Section 3 presents the measurements
of nebular morphology, surface brightness, and kinemat-
ics. In Section 4, we investigate the connection between

2. DATA
2.1. Sample selection
Datacubes of 96 quasars at z = 2 −3 obtained during
2017-2022 are collected from the KCWI public archive.
These data span 11 separate programs. Since four of the
programs target specific circumgalactic environments
and thus introduce strong selection biases, we restrict
our analysis to the remaining seven programs, yielding
a final sample of 85 quasars. The selection criteria of
each program are summarized below.

27 quasars.
By cross-matching with the Sloan Digi-
tal Sky Survey (SDSS) DR16 catalog, we identify six
quasars as obscured narrow-line (NL) quasars based on
the criterion FWHMCIV ≤2000 km s−1 (Alexandroff
et al. 2013; Law et al. 2018). One of these resides in the
MAMMOTH-1 overdensity field (Cai et al. 2017); the re-
maining five are drawn from an obscured NL candidate
catalog (Alexandroff et al. 2013), in which candidates
were selected from the Baryon Oscillation Spectroscopic
Survey (BOSS) of SDSS-III on the basis of weak opti-
cal continua together with strong Lyα and C iv emis-
sion. These quasars exhibit dust extinctions of AV ≈0.5
mag, indicating modest obscuration. The remaining 21
broad-line quasars were selected by restricting their red-
shifts to 2.1 ≤z ≤2.3 and their i-band magnitudes to
i ≤18.5 (Cai et al. 2019).
(ii) The FLASHES Survey - Witnessing Galaxy
Formation (PI: Christopher Martin).
This pro-
gram contributes 20 quasars drawn from the Fluorescent
Lyman-Alpha Structures in High-z Environments Sur-
vey (FLASHES) (O’Sullivan et al. 2020). The survey
targets quasars in the redshift range 2.3 ≤z ≤3.1. To
enable an investigation of the luminosity dependence of
nebular properties, the sample was designed to span a
wide range of absolute i-band magnitudes. Where avail-
able, radio-quiet (RQ) sources were preferentially se-
lected. Among the 20 quasars, four satisfy the obscured
quasar criterion W2 −W4 ≥4.8 (O’Sullivan et al. 2020);
two of these four are further classified as NL quasars.
(iii) Mapping the CGM of Forming Galaxies
(PI: Charles C. Steidel). This program includes 10
quasars, five of which are classified as obscured. Four of
the five obscured AGN were initially identified as star-
forming galaxies (SFGs) in the Keck Baryonic Structure
Survey (KBSS) fields but were subsequently reclassi-
fied as AGN based on their positions on the BPT di-
agram.
Spectroscopy reveals FWHMCIV ≤1300 km
s−1 for three of these sources (Law et al. 2018), con-
firming their NL nature, and SED fitting with obscured
AGN templates further supports their classification as
obscured objects (Law et al. 2018). The fourth source
shows FWHMHα ≤600 km s−1 (Steidel et al. 2014),
which is also consistent with an NL quasar; we there-
fore classify it as an obscured quasar on the basis of
its selection criteria and spectral features. The fifth ob-
scured quasar was identified through X-ray observations
that reveal a hydrogen column density of NH ≥1023
cm−2 (Geach et al. 2009). The remaining five quasars
are unobscured: three were selected by their redshifts
(2.25 ≤z ≤2.27) and brightness (i ≤18.5) (Langen
et al. 2023), while the other two lie in the KBSS field.

We cross-match these quasars with the SDSS DR16,
the Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer (WISE), and
the NASA/IPAC Extragalactic Database (NED) to ob-
tain their photometry. We compile photometry span-

a wavelength range that captures continuum emission
from the AGN accretion disc, the torus, and the host
galaxy.
We use cigale (Boquien et al. 2019) to fit the
SEDs, adopting the parameter settings from Zhang et al.
(2023c), who applied cigale to AGN SEDs at z ≈2.
We fix the dust torus opening angle at 50 deg, consistent
with typical constraints on AGN torus geometry (Fritz
et al. 2006). Because the Zhang et al. (2023c) sample
includes only unobscured quasars, a viewing angle of 90
deg between the equatorial axis and the line of sight
is assumed—that is, a configuration in which the torus
does not obscure the sightline. For the obscured quasars
in our sample, we instead set this angle to 0 deg, corre-
sponding to a sightline passing through the torus. We
further constrain the galaxy stellar mass to ≤1012 M⊙
to avoid the unphysical results.
Given that previous observations yield good fits with
a reduced chi-square of χ2

2.3. Data reduction
The standard KCWI pipeline 1 is employed to reduce
the data and produce the datacubes. We subtract the
bias, correct the pixel-to-pixel variation with the flat-
field images, and remove the cosmic rays. Then, the ge-
ometry transformation and the wavelength calibration
with the ThAr arc are performed. The twilight flat im-
ages are used to correct the slice-to-slice variance. The
spectrophotometric standard stars are adopted for cali-
brating the flux of the individual image in the datacube.
The sky subtraction is also done by scaling the flux as
a function of wavelength in the sky datacube to match
the data.
The cwitools (O’Sullivan & Chen 2020) is applied
for coadding the individual datacube.
The built-in
method, cwi coadd, calculates the footprint for each
pixel on the coadd frame and distributes its flux onto
this frame. The new world coordinate system (WCS)
for the coadd frame is constructed to achieve a 1:1 as-
pect ratio, resulting in a pixel scale of 0.3′′ × 0.3′′. The
point spread function (PSF) is subtracted for the un-
obscured BL quasars, as their bright PSFs contaminate
the faint nebular emission. This is carried out by the
cwi psf sub function from the cwitools. Our sample
comprises previously observed nebulae (Cai et al. 2019),
with consistent SB profiles and morphologies matching
prior results, validating the data reduction process. We
obtain the final stacked datacubes after reduction. The
median depth at 4250 ˚A over a width of 1 ˚A under an
aperture of 1′′ for the three types of quasar samples are

composite images and SB profiles. To ensure a fair com-

draw a subsample of unobscured quasars equal in num-

All images have
been corrected for the cosmic dimming effect to a ref-

As shown in Fig. 7, nebu-
lae around obscured quasars appear less extended than
those around unobscured quasars.
This difference is
driven by a steeper decline in nebular brightness around
obscured quasars, as evidenced by their SB profiles. We
quantify this behavior by fitting the profiles with an ex-
ponential function of the form SB(r) = Ce−r/rh, where
C is the normalization and rh is the scale length (i.e.,
the slope).
Unobscured quasars yield a larger scale length (rh =
10.7±0.5 kpc) than their obscured counterparts (rh,NL =
6.6 ± 0.4 kpc and rh,BL = 7.7 ± 0.5 kpc), with the dif-
ference exceeding the 1σ fitting uncertainties. Interest-
ingly, simulations based on the standard AGN unified
model predict the opposite trend: if the intrinsic quasar
properties are consistent, nebulae around unobscured
quasars should exhibit steeper profiles (rh = 10.5 ± 0.3
kpc) than those around obscured quasars (rh = 12.2±0.2
kpc) due to projection effects arising from the AGN ion-
ization cone (Zhang et al. 2025).
We return to this
tension between the observations and the unified-model
predictions in Sec. 4.

4. DISCUSSION
In this section, we explore the connections between
nebulae and AGN properties in Sec. 4.1. Then we dis-
cuss whether such connections can be reproduced by the
unified model in Sec. 4.2 and and evolutionary scenario
in Sec. 4.3. Finally, we discuss what we can gain from
the line ratio diagnostics in Sec. 4.4.
4.1. Connection between nebular and AGN properties
The results presented in Sections 3.1—3.3 reveal that
Lyα nebulae around unobscured and obscured quasars
differ markedly in morphology, surface brightness, and
kinematics.
A critical question is whether these dif-
ferences are intrinsically driven by the level of quasar
obscuration—which would constitute a departure from
the predictions of the unified scheme—or whether they
instead arise indirectly through correlations with sec-
ondary AGN properties such as the SMBH mass (MBH)
or radio loudness (RK).
We analyze the variation in nebulae properties in re-
lation to obscuration, MBH, and RK.
Obscuration is
quantified by r −W4, while MBH and RK are derived
as described in Sec. 2.2. We bin the sample by r −W4,
MBH, and RK, computing the median αw and fitting
the median-stacked SB profile to derive rh in each bin.
Connections between nebular and AGN properties are
summarized in Fig. 9.
Both the Spearman correlation coefficients (Rs) and
the linear fits yield consistent conclusions: nebular prop-
erties exhibit a strong dependence on r−W4 (|Rs| ≈0.7,
p ≤2.5%), but show no significant dependence on MBH
or RK (|Rs| ≤0.2, p ≥62%). These results indicate
that the extent and symmetry of Lyα nebulae are pri-
marily governed by the degree of quasar obscuration,
while SMBH mass and radio loudness play at most a
minor role. This finding is broadly consistent with ear-
lier studies of Lyα nebulae around unobscured quasars
at z ≈3, which likewise reported no significant cor-
relation between nebular SB profile slope or symmetry
and either quasar luminosity or radio loudness (Arrigoni
Battaia et al. 2019; Mackenzie et al. 2021).
To verify that these conclusions are robust against the
choice of binning, we further examine the stacked SB
profiles and αw values across different subdivisions of r−
W4, MBH, and RK (restricted to unobscured quasars for
the latter two). We also compare the nebular properties
of red quasars with and without [O iii] detections to
assess whether potential outflows introduce additional
heterogeneity into the obscured sample.
The stacked and normalized SB profiles are presented
in Fig. 10.
The upper-left panel reveals a clear and
continuous trend: as r −W4 increases—that is, as the
quasar becomes more obscured—the SB profile steep-
ens, corroborating the conclusion that more obscured
quasars are surrounded by more compact nebulae. Turn-
ing to MBH, we split the unobscured sample into two
bins (MBH > 109.5 M⊙and MBH < 109.0 M⊙). While
the resulting profiles share nearly identical scale lengths,
the higher-mass bin exhibits a systematically elevated
SB, implying that more massive—and hence more lumi-
nous—quasars illuminate brighter nebulae without sig-
nificantly altering their spatial extent.
Because MBH is derived from the SED-fitted bolomet-
ric luminosity and is therefore subject to considerable
uncertainty, we cross-check this result using the abso-
lute i-band magnitude Mi as an independent luminosity
proxy (squares in panel b of Fig. 10). Binning by Mi
reproduces the same pattern, consistent with previous
observations of Lyα nebulae at z ≈3 (Mackenzie et al.

4.2. Compare with the AGN unified model predictions
As demonstrated in Sec. 3.1 and Sec. 3.2, our observa-
tions reveal that Lyα nebulae around obscured quasars
are systematically more symmetric and more compact
than those around unobscured quasars—the opposite of
what is predicted by the unified-model simulations of
Zhang et al. (2025). Those simulations assume that ob-
scured and unobscured quasars share identical intrinsic
properties, specifically MBH and the dusty-torus open-

supports these assumptions, the SED-derived parame-

we relax the assumptions and perform a bootstrap re-
sampling of the mock observations, allowing both MBH
and the torus opening angle to vary within the ranges
spanned by the simulations.

bootstrap samples with replacement.

4.4. Line Ratio diagnostic
The evolutionary scenario should also predict that the
CGM surrounding unobscured quasars exhibit higher
metallicity than that around obscured quasars, due
to the enrichment from the large-scale AGN feedback
(Suresh et al. 2015). Indeed, the CGM of unobscured
quasars at z ≈3 has been found to exhibit metallicities
of ≈0.5 Z⊙(Guo et al. 2020), whereas the metallicity
of the CGM around obscured quasars remains poorly
constrained. Since a subset of our datacubes cover the
C iv emission line, we can use the C iv/Lyα ratio as a
metallicity proxy to test this prediction.
Among the datacubes with C iv coverage (18 unob-
scured and 11 obscured quasars), we detect C iv nebulae
around 15 unobscured and seven obscured quasars. For
these detections, we measure the Lyα and C iv lumi-
nosities within an annulus of 15 −30 kpc and compute
their ratio. The inner radius is chosen to minimize PSF
contamination, while the outer radius corresponds to
the maximum extent of the detected C iv nebulae. For
quasars without C iv detections, we derive 3-σ upper
limits on the C iv luminosity within the same aperture
and use these to place upper limits on the line ratio.
The resulting distributions, including upper limits, are
shown in Fig. 14. We measure median C iv/Lyα ratios
of [C iv/Lyα]unobs = 0.11 ± 0.10 and [C iv/Lyα]obs =
0.13±0.16 for the unobscured and obscured populations,
respectively. Since this line ratio serves as a proxy for
gas metallicity, the similarity between the two values
would indicate no significant metallicity difference in the
CGM surrounding the two populations. However, both
Lyα and C iv are resonant lines, making C iv/Lyα an
imperfect metallicity diagnostic.
We therefore repeat
the analysis using He ii—a non-resonant line—in place
of Lyα, deriving [C iv/He ii]unobs = 0.81 ± 4.9 and [C
iv/He ii]obs = 1.25±1.1. A Welch t-test yields a p-value
of 28%, confirming that the two populations are statis-
tically indistinguishable. A more accurate assessment
of the CGM metallicity requires extended non-resonant
emissions, such as Hα and C iii(Guo et al. 2020).
5. CONCLUSIONS
We have presented a comprehensive analysis of Lyα
nebulae around 59 unobscured and 26 obscured quasars
at z = 2 −3, observed with Keck/KCWI. By system-
atically comparing the morphology, surface brightness
profiles, and kinematics of nebulae between the two pop-
ulations, we have demonstrated how CGM nebulae can
be used to constrain the high-z AGN evolutionary pic-
ture. Our main conclusions are as follows:
• Lyα nebulae surrounding unobscured quasars
show notably lower symmetry (αw = 0.2 −0.6)
than those around obscured quasars (αw = 0.6 −
0.8).
This behavior runs counter to the unified
model expectation, which predicts that nebulae
viewed face-on through the ionization cone—as is
the case for unobscured quasars—should appear
more symmetric (Sec. 3.1).
• The Lyα nebulae of unobscured quasars are more
spatially extended, exhibiting rh = 10.7 ± 0.5 kpc
