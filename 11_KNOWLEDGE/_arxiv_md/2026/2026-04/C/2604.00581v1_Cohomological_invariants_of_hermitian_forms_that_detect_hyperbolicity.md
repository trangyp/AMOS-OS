Cohomological invariants of hermitian forms that
detect hyperbolicity
Yong Hu and Alexandre Lourdeaux
April 2, 2026
Abstract
By using unramified cohomology groups, we construct a full sequence of cohomological invariants
for hermitian forms of any type (orthogonal, symplectic or unitary) that can be used to detect
hyperbolicity. The base central simple algebras can have arbitrary degree and the base field can
have arbitrary characteristic. In the orthogonal case, we work with hermitian pairs, and we apply
our construction to show that over fields of separable dimension 3, hermitian pairs over quaternion
algebras with trivial classical invariants are hyperbolic. This last result extends a result of Berhuy
to arbitrary characteristic.

Introduction
In the development of the algebraic theory of quadratic forms, each time the construction of a new useful
cohomological invariant, such as the Clifford invariant, essentially due to Witt [Wit37], and the Arason
invariant [Ara75], marks a milestone. Since the proof of Milnor’s conjecture ([Voe03], [OVV07]), higher
degree cohomological invariants have also been well defined, so that a complete system of invariants has
been available to detect the hyperbolicity of quadratic forms, over any field of characteristic different
from 2. In fact, defining these invariants in characteristic 2 was made possible even earlier, thanks

to Kato’s work [Kat82].
Therefore, by Witt theory, at least theoretically one has a complete tool
kit to treat the classification problem for (nondegenerate) quadratic forms over any field (at least for
even-dimensional ones in characteristic 2).
The main purpose of this paper is to study the same problem for hermitian forms over central simple
involutorial algebras, for which Witt theory is also applicable.
To briefly recall some previous works in this direction, let us assume for convenience that our base
field has characteristic different from 2. Low degree cohomological invariants can be defined even for
involutions, which correspond to similarity classes of hermitian forms. By the time that the famous book
[KMRT98] was published, at least the following invariants have been defined and used in research: the
discriminant and the Clifford algebra for orthogonal involutions, which give cohomological invariants
in degrees 1 and 2 respectively; the discriminant algebra for unitary involutions, which has degree 2
as a cohomological invariant; and the Pfaffian for symplectic involutions, which serves essentially as a
degree 3 invariant, as is justified in [BMT03] and [GPT09]. On the other hand, in their pioneering work
[BFP98], Bayer-Fluckiger and Parimala use the Rost invariant of related semisimple simply connected
algebraic groups to define a degree 3 invariant, which they call Rost invariant, for hermitian forms in
the orthogonal and unitary cases. Their idea and method, as well as those explained in the nice survey
[Tig10], are further developed to study degree 3 invariants of involutions, by Qu´eguiner-Mathieu and
Tignol [QMT15], and by Barry, Masquelein and Qu´eguiner-Mathieu [BMQM22]. Among the above
mentioned invariants, some are defined first in a relative setting and then in the absolute case, in a
compatible way.
The classical invariants, for hermitian forms and involutions, all take values in cohomology groups
up to degree 3. As far as we know, higher degree cohomological invariants have been constructed only
in several special cases until the present work. An almost complete list, if the split case and the index
2 symplectic case are not counted, seems to be as follows:
• invariants in all degrees for Pfister involutions (of orthogonal type in characteristic ̸= 2) [BFPQM03];
• invariants in all degrees for quaternionic skew-hermitian forms, or equivalently orthogonal invo-
lutions, in characteristic ̸= 2 [Ber07];
• the relative e4-invariant for totally decomposable involutions on degree 8 algebras [QMT12];
• an absolute invariant of degree 4 for unitary involutions on degree 4 algebras (with an extra
assumption on its center) [RST06, Tig06].
The main result of this paper is the construction of a full sequence of cohomological invariants
(en)n≥1 for hermitian forms in all the three (orthogonal, symplectic and unitary) cases in arbitrary
characteristic. To be more precise, the orthogonal case refers to hermitian pairs (cf. § 2.2), which are
equivalent objects as generalized quadratic forms (or up to scalars, as quadratic pairs; see Remark 2.9),
are different than orthogonal involutions in characteristic 2. Our invariants are defined absolutely, and
they allow detecting the hyperbolicity of hermitian forms, at least when assuming the characteristic is
not 2 in the symplectic and unitary cases. Our main theorems are stated as Theorems 4.5, 4.15 and
5.5, showing the usefulness of our invariants.
One remark calls for special attention here. As is already known from [QM02] and [BFPQM03, § 3.4],
there is no hope to define functorially an absolute e3-invariant in the orthogonal case, if one insists on
using a quotient of the cohomology group of the base field; see also [Tig10, § 5.3] for the symplectic
case. The crucial observation that makes our construction valid, which is inspired by [BFPQM03] and
[Ber07], is that the use of the unramified cohomology (cf. § 3.1) can be a successful remedy. In fact, the
unramified cohomology group we utilize can be larger than any quotient of the cohomology of the base
field, but it is still canonically associated to basic input firmly tired to the problem, namely, the base
field, the Brauer class of the central simple algebra over which hermitian forms are considered, and the
type of hermitian forms (with orthogonal case corresponding to hermitian pairs, if one wants to be more
precise). That our invariants are indeed complete for the purpose of testing hyperbolicity relies on the
hyperbolicity theorems proved by Karpenko and Tignol [Kar10a, Kar12] in characteristic ̸= 2, as well as
a recent isotropy theorem from Medhi’s thesis [Med26] for the orthogonal case in characteristic 2. This
is the only place where a characteristic restriction might be needed in the symplectic and unitary cases;

result of Berhuy [Ber07, Prop. 15] about classification of quaternionic skew-hermitian forms over fields
of cohomological dimension 3 to arbitrary characteristic.
We work with hermitian forms more often than with involutions.
There two main reasons for
this.
The first one is that for hermitian forms the orthogonal sum operation is naturally defined,
so that our invariants induce functorial group homomorphisms. The second reason is, as is clear to
experts, that defining invariants for involutions may require more restrictive assumptions, since the
effect of similitudes may not be neglectable. Nevertheless, we do compare (in (4.9), (4.19) and § 5.2)
our invariants with those defined for involutions in the literature, at least in a few cases that are
important in our eyes.
In a forthcoming work we intend to apply our cohomological invariants to study Galois cohomology
of classical groups over some special fields with nice arithmetic properties.
Last but not least, it is perhaps worth mentioning that, compared to a number of earlier papers, we
allow non-division algebras as bases on which hermitian forms are defiend. This makes it more conve-
nient to deal with Morita invariance and base extension functoriality for our cohomological invariants.

(2.2) By an involutorial F-algebra we mean a pair (A, σ) consisting of a nonzero finite dimensional

To emphasize the relation between σ and K/F, we often say that σ is a K/F-involution (of whichever

(2.3) Let (A, σ) be a CSIA over F. A hermitian form over (A, σ) is a pair (V, h), where V is a finitely
generated right A-module of constant semisimple rank and h : V × V →A is a function such that the
following properties hold for all x, y, z ∈V and all λ ∈A:
h(x + y, z) = h(x, z) + h(y, z) , h(xλ, y) = σ(λ)h(x, y) , h(y, x) = σ(h(x, y)) .
We also say that h is a hermitian form on the A-module V with respect to the involution σ, or that h
is a σ-hermitian form on V . For simplicity, sometimes a hermitian form is simply denoted by h.
The (semisimple) rank rk(h) of a hermitian form (V, h) is defined as the semisimple rank of the
A-module V , i.e.,
rk(h) = ssrkA(V ).
The radical of a hermitian form (V, h) is defined to be the set
rad(h) := {x ∈V | h(x, y) = 0 for all y ∈V } .
We say that h is nondegenerate if rad(h) = 0. If there is a nonzero x ∈V such that h(x, x) = 0, then
we say that h is isotropic; otherwise we say that h is anisotropic. Clearly, any anisotropic hermitian
form is nondegenerate.
For an A-submodule U ⊆V , we define its orthogonal complement U ⊥in V (with respect to h) by
U ⊥:= {x ∈V | h(x, y) = 0 for all y ∈U} .
If U = U ⊥, then we say that U is a lagrangian of (V, h). If (V, h) is nondegenerate and has a lagrangian,
then we say that it is metabolic. Clearly, if h is metabolic with V ̸= 0, then h is isotropic.
If σ is unitary of inner type, one can show that nondegenerate hermitian forms over (A, σ) are all
metabolic. So, when studying anisotropic forms we are mostly interested in the case where K = Z(A)
is a field and A is a central simple K-algebra.
A hermitian form (V, h) over (A, σ) is called even if h(x, x) ∈Symd(A, σ) = {a + σ(a) | a ∈A} for
all x ∈V (cf. [Knu91, Chap. I, (3.1)]). If the base field F has characteristic char(F) ̸= 2 or if σ is
unitary, then all hermitian forms over (A, σ) are even.
An even metabolic hermitian form is called hyperbolic.
The isomorphism classes of nondegenerate hermitian forms over (A, σ) form a commutative monoid
with respect to the orthogonal sum operation. The quotient of the Grothendieck group of this monoid
by the subgroup generated by the classes of hyperbolic forms is called the Witt group of hermitian forms
over (A, σ) (cf. [Knu91, § I.10]). We denote this group by W(A, σ).
The class of any metabolic form is trivial in the Witt group, by [Knu91, Chap. I, (3.7.6)].

Quadratic and hermitian pairs
(2.6) Recall the following definition from [KMRT98, (5.4)]: Given a central simple F-algebra A, a
quadratic pair on A is a pair (σ, f), where σ is an involution of the first kind on A and f is an F-linear
map
f : Sym(A, σ) →F
such that
• dimF Sym(A, σ) = n(n+1)
2
, where n = deg(A);
• TrdA(Skew(A, σ)) = 0, where TrdA : A →F denotes the reduced trace map;
• f(x + σ(x)) = TrdA(x) for all x ∈A.
The map f is called a semi-trace for the pair (A, σ), and the triple (A, σ, f) is called a central simple
algebra with quadratic pair (CSAQP for short) over F.
If char(F) ̸= 2, then the involution σ must be orthogonal and the semi-trace f is uniquely determined
by f = 1
2TrdA.
If char(F) = 2, then the involution must be symplectic and hence deg(A) must be even.
The isotropy and hyperbolicity properties of a CSAQP are defined in [KMRT98, (6.5) and (6.12)].
Although not uniquely determined by the summands, orthogonal sums of two given central simple
algebras with quadratic pair can be defined as in [BFT07, Def. 1.4].
Let us define the degree of a central simple algebras with quadratic pair (A, σ, f) to be the degree of
the underlying algebra A. If (A, σ, f) is an orthogonal sum of (A1, σ1, f1) and (A2, σ2, f2), then A1, A2
and A are all Brauer equivalent, and deg(A) = deg(A1) + deg(A2) ([BFT07, Prop. 1.2]).

5.3
A hyperbolicity criterion in the quaternion case
For any prime number p, let sdp(F) denote the separable p-dimension of F as defined in [Gil00, p.62].
Proposition 5.12. Assume that ind(D) = 2 and sd2(F) ⩽3. Then a hermitian pair p over (D, θ) is
hyperbolic if and only if e1(p) = e2(p) = e3(p) = 0.
Proof. By Thm. 3.4, the assumption ind(D) = 2 guarantees that Hn
+(D) is a quotient of the 2-primary
torsion subgroup of Hn(F, Q/Z(n −1)) for every n ≥1. But if sd2(F) ⩽3, Hn(F, Q/Z(n −1)) has no
2-torsion for every n ≥4. So the result follows from Theorem 5.5.
Prop. 5.12 allows us to generalize [Ber07, Prop. 15] to arbitrary characteristic as follows.
Theorem 5.13. Assume that ind(D) ⩽2 and sd2(F) ⩽3. Then a hermitian pair of even rank over
(D, θ) is hyperbolic if and only if it has trivial discriminant, trivial Clifford invariant and trivial Rost
invariant.
Proof. The case ind(D) = 1 reduces to the well known case for quadratic forms. We may thus assume
ind(D) = 2. In this case, the invariants e1, e2 and e3 agree with the corresponding classical invariants
even over the base field, as we have seen in (5.7) and (5.10). So the theorem follows from Prop. 5.12.
