Abstract
The main aim of this paper is to study chains of model structures arising from co-
torsion pairs in extriangulated categories. Starting with a hereditary Hovey triple,
we construct further hereditary Hovey triples whose homotopy categories are equiv-
alent under suitable completeness assumptions, thereby refining results due to El
Maaouy and Shao-Wang-Zhang. As an application, we consider objects of finite
Gorenstein injective dimension with respect to a proper class of E-triangles. Under
mild set-theoretic assumptions, we obtain a chain of model structures whose homo-
topy categories are all triangulated equivalent to a common stable category. This
recovers known results for Gorenstein injective modules and yields new examples in
the derived category of a ring when the proper class is given by cohomological ghost
triangles.
Keywords:
extriangulated category; proper class; Gorenstein injective object;
model structure

Introduction
Cotorsion pairs and their relation with model structures have been studied extensively. By a
result of Hovey [9], suitable cotorsion pairs give rise to model structures. This correspondence
has been extended to exact and extriangulated categories via Hovey triples (see [7, 8, 18]).
Recall that extriangulated categories, introduced by Nakaoka and Palu [18], provide a common
generalization of exact and triangulated categories. In this setting, extensions, cotorsion pairs,
and homological dimensions can be developed in close analogy with the classical cases.
Given a cotorsion pair, one may consider the associated subcategories of objects of finite
homological dimension. These subcategories often induce new cotorsion pairs, and hence give
rise to further model structures. In this way, one obtains chains of model structures indexed

On the other hand, ξ-injective and ξ-Ginjective objects were introduced by Hu-Zhang-Zhou

The purpose of this paper is to investigate chains of model structures arising from cotorsion
pairs in extriangulated categories, and apply the resulting theory to objects of finite ξ-Ginjective
dimension. Our arguments rely essentially on the structural properties of extriangulated cate-
gories.
To state the main results, we recall some notation and definitions. Assume that (B, E, s) is
an extriangulated category. Here B is an additive category, E : Bop × B →Ab is an additive
bifunctor, and s associates to each δ ∈E(C, A) a collection of 3-term sequences whose end terms
are A and C, see [18, Definition 2.10]. In the absence of ambiguity, we denote the quadruple
simply by B := (B, E, s).
Let (X, Y) be a complete cotorsion pair in B. We call X ∩Y the core of (X, Y). For each
integer n ⩾0, we denote by Yn the full subcategory of B consisting of all objects of Y-coresolution
dimension at most n. For brevity, we write ⊥Yn in place of ⊥(Yn).
Recall that a triple (C, W, F) of B is called a Hovey triple if (C ∩W, F) and (C, W ∩F) are
complete cotorsion pairs, and W is thick in B, that is, W is closed under direct summands and
satisfies the two-out-of-three property for conflations. A Hovey triple is called hereditary if the
cotorsion pairs (C ∩W, F) and (C, W ∩F) are hereditary. The homotopy category associated
with a Hovey triple M = (C, W, F) is denoted by Ho(M).
For a full additive subcategory U of an additive category A, recall that the quotient category
A has the same objects as A, and
HomA(X, Y ) = HomA(X, Y )/HomA(X, U, Y ),
where HomA(X, U, Y ) denotes the subgroup of HomA(X, Y ) consisting of morphisms factoring
through an object of U. Then A is an additive category.
Our first result provides a method for constructing chains of model structures from a Hovey
triple, and shows that these model structures have equivalent homotopy categories under suitable
assumptions. For notation not defined in the theorem, we refer the reader to Section 2.
Theorem 1.1. Let (B, E, s) be a weakly idempotent complete extriangulated category, and M =
(C, W, F) a hereditary Hovey triple in B. Assume that (⊥(W ∩F)n, (W ∩F)n) is a complete
cotorsion pair for some non-negative integer n. Then
(1) The pair (⊥(W ∩F)n ∩W, Fn) is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair with core
⊥(W ∩F)n ∩(W ∩F)n.
(2) The triple Mn = (⊥(W ∩F)n, W, Fn) is a hereditary Hovey triple in B, and ⊥(W ∩F)n∩
Fn is a Frobenius extriangulated category with the class of projective-injective objects ⊥(W ∩F)n∩
(W ∩F)n. Furthermore, we obtain triangle equivalences
⊥(W ∩F)n ∩Fn ≃Ho(Mn) = Ho(M) ≃C ∩F.

Now, we compare Theorem 1.1 with some results in the literature.
• Theorem 1.1 improves the result of Shao-Wang-Zhang in [24, Theorem 3.9] by showing
that the condition “the exact category has enough projectives and enough injectives” is
unnecessary (see Corollary 3.4 and Remark 3.5(1)).
• Theorem 1.1 may be regarded as a refinement of the work of El Maaouy in [4, Theorem
A] (see Remark 3.5(2)). In that paper, the hereditary Hovey triple (⊥(W ∩F)n, W, Fn)
was obtained under the further hypotheses that B is an abelian category and (C, W, F)
is a hereditary Hovey triple on B for which:
(i) (W, W⊥) is a complete cotorsion pair;
(ii) (⊥(W ∩F)k, (W ∩F)k) is a complete cotorsion pair for every k ∈{0, 1, · · · , n}.
For an arbitrary ring R, it is a well-established fact that the subcategory GI(R) of Gorenstein
injective modules, together with the collection of short exact sequences whose three terms are
all in GI(R), is a Frobenius category. Let ModR denote the category of left R-modules and
I(R) the subcategory of injective modules. This result admits a natural extension to an infinite
sequence of subcategories:
GI(R) = ⊥I(R)0 ∩GI(R)0, ⊥I(R)1 ∩GI(R)1, · · · , ⊥I(R)k ∩GI(R)k, · · · .
Consequently, each associated stable category I(R)k ∩GI(R)k inherits a triangulated structure.
The question of how this stable category evolves as the Gorenstein injective dimension increases
does not yield an obvious answer when considered purely within the framework of stable cat-
egories. However, by analyzing these stable categories through the lens of model structures,
El Maaouy provided a definitive resolution in [4, Proposition 3.9], establishing the following
triangle equivalences:
GI(R) ≃⊥I(R)1 ∩GI(R)1 ≃· · · ≃⊥I(R)k ∩GI(R)k ≃· · · .
(1.1)
The essential ingredient underpinning this proof is that the complete cotorsion pair (ModR, I(R))
always induces the completeness of cotorsion pairs (⊥I(R)k, I(R)k) for all integers k > 0.
Let ξ be a proper class in an extriangulated category (B, E, s). By [12, Theorem 3.2], the
triple (B, Eξ, sξ) carries a natural extriangulated structure. Following [12], we denote by GI(ξ)
the full subcategory of ξ-Ginjective objects in B, and by I(ξ) the full subcategory of ξ-injective
objects in B.
It is known that GI(ξ) is a Frobenius extriangulated category, and hence its
stable category GI(ξ) is triangulated.
Analogously to the triangle equivalences in (1.1) for
module categories, one may ask how the stable category behaves as the ξ-Ginjective dimension
of objects in B increases. To address this question, one is naturally led to determine when the
complete cotorsion pair (B, I(ξ)) induces the completeness of the cotorsion pairs (⊥I(ξ)k, I(ξ)k)
for all integers k > 0. At present, we do not know how to achieve this in general. Nevertheless,
Proposition 4.9 shows that this is indeed the case under the following assumptions: the pair
(⊥I(ξ), I(ξ)) is cogenerated by a set S in the extriangulated category (B, Eξ, sξ), and B satisfies
(Ax1) Arbitrary transfinite compositions of ξ-inflations exist and remain ξ-inflations;
(Ax2) Every object of B is small relative to the class of all ξ-inflations.
These assumptions are inspired by the work of Saor´ın–ˇSˇtov´ıˇcek [22]. For notations not defined
in the proposition, we refer to Section 4.

As a consequence of Theorem 1.1, the following result provides sufficient conditions for con-
structing chains of model structures arising from objects of finite ξ-Ginjective dimension. More-
over, it shows that enlarging the ξ-Ginjective dimension does not alter the associated stable
category.
Theorem 1.2. Let n be a non-negative integer, and let (B, Eξ, sξ) be a weakly idempotent com-
plete extriangulated category satisfying (Ax1) and (Ax2).
Assume that (⊥GI(ξ), GI(ξ)) is a
complete cotorsion pair in (B, Eξ, sξ) and that the pair (⊥I(ξ), I(ξ)) is cogenerated by a set S in
(B, Eξ, sξ). Then
(1) The pair (⊥I(ξ)n ∩⊥GI(ξ), GI(ξ)n) is a complete hereditary cotorsion pair in (B, Eξ, sξ)
with core I(ξ)n ∩⊥I(ξ)n.
(2) The triple (⊥I(ξ)n, ⊥GI(ξ), GI(ξ)n) is a hereditary Hovey triple in (B, Eξ, sξ); ⊥I(ξ)n ∩
GI(ξ)n is a Frobenius extriangulated category such that ⊥I(ξ)n ∩I(ξ)n is its class of projective-
injective objects; and the homotopy category is the stable category ⊥I(ξ)n ∩GI(ξ)n, which is
triangle equivalent to GI(ξ).
It is worth noting that Theorem 1.2 not only extends El Maaouy’s result [4, Corollary 3.9]
on chains of model structures arising from modules of finite Gorenstein injective dimension, but
also yields a new result for the derived category of a ring when the proper class is given by the
cohomological ghost triangles introduced by Otake [19] (see Corollary 4.11).
While the work of ˇSaroch and ˇSˇtov´ıˇcek [23] shows that (⊥GI(R), GI(R)) is a complete cotor-
sion pair for any ring R, it remains unknown whether an analogous completeness result holds
for the cotorsion pair (⊥GI(ξ), GI(ξ)) in the extriangulated category (B, Eξ, sξ). Motivated by
the elegant construction of Gao–Lu–Zhang for chains of model structures on exact categories
arising from modules of Gorenstein projective dimension bounded by a non-negative integer (see
[6, Theorem 6.3]), we establish the following result.
Theorem 1.3. Let m and n non-negative integers with m ⩽n, and let (B, Eξ, sξ) be a weakly
idempotent complete extriangulated category satisfying (Ax1) and (Ax2). Assume that the pair
(⊥I(ξ), I(ξ)) is cogenerated by a set S in (B, Eξ, sξ).
(1) The pair (⊥I(ξ)m ∩I(ξ)n, GI(ξ)m) is a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair in the
extriangulated category (GI(ξ)n, EGI(ξ)n, sGI(ξ)n) with core ⊥I(ξ)m ∩I(ξ)m.
(2) The triple (⊥I(ξ)m ∩GI(ξ)n, I(ξ)n, GI(ξ)m) is a hereditary Hovey triple in the extri-
angulated category (GI(ξ)n, EGI(ξ)n, sGI(ξ)n), and the homotopy category is the stable category
⊥I(ξ)m ∩GI(ξ)m, which is triangle equivalent to GI(ξ).

Section 2 recalls the necessary preliminaries on extri-

2.4. Model structures. We refer to [21, 10, 8] for some basic concepts and results on model
structures.
Recall from [18, Definition 5.5] that an admissible model structure on a WIC extriangulated
category B is a model structure in the sense of [10, Definition 1.1.3] in which each of the following
holds.
(1) A map is a (trivial) cofibration if and only if it is an inflation with a (trivially) cofibrant
cone.
(2) A map is a (trivial) fibration if and only if it is a deflation with a (trivially) fibrant cocone.
The notion of an abelian model structure introduced in [9] and that of an exact model structure
introduced in [7] are both special cases of the concept of an admissible model structure.
A triple (C, W, F) of B is called a Hovey triple if (C ∩W, F) and (C, W ∩F) are complete
cotorsion pairs, and W is thick in B (i.e., W is closed under direct summands, and if two out
of three objects in a conflation are in W, then so is the third one). A Hover triple is called
hereditary if these two complete cotorsion pairs (C ∩W, F) and (C, W ∩F) are hereditary.
It was shown by Hovey [9] that there exists a translation between abelian model structures
and Hovey triples within an abelian category.
This result was subsequently generalized by
Gillespie [7] to the setting of WIC exact categories, and later by Nakaoka and Palu [18] to WIC
extriangulated categories. More precisely, given a WIC extriangulated category B, one obtains a
one-to-one correspondence between admissible model structures and Hovey twin cotorsion pairs.
Hence, we may safely regard Hovey triples and admissible model structures as synonymous
whenever no confusion is likely to arise. Further details can be found in [18, Section 5], [20,
Section 3.2], and [16, Section 2.5].
Recall from [18] that B is said to be Frobenius if B has enough projectives, enough injectives,
and the projectives coincide with the injectives. In this case, one has the quotient category B
of B by projectives, which is a triangulated category by [18, Corollary 7.4 and Remark 7.5]. We
refer to this category as the stable category of B. Denote by Ho(M) the homotopy category
associated with a Hovey triple M = (C, W, F) in B. Thus we have the following theorem.
Theorem 2.17. ([18, Sections 5-7]) Let M = (C, W, F) be a hereditary Hovey triple in B.
Then C ∩F is a Frobenius extriangulated category whose class of projective-injective objects is
C ∩W ∩F, and there is a triangle equivalence C ∩F ∼= Ho(M).
Proof. Since M = (C, W, F) is a hereditary Hovey triple in B, C∩F is a Frobenius extriangulated
category whose class of projective-injective objects is C ∩W ∩F, and therefore C ∩F is a

((C ∩W, F), (C, W ∩F)) forms a Hovey twin cotorsion pair as defined in [18, Definition 5.1]. It
