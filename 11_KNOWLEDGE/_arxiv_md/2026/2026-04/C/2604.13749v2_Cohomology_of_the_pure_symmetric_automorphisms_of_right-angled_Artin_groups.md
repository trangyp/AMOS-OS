COHOMOLOGY OF PURE SYMMETRIC AUTOMORPHISMS OF
RIGHT-ANGLED ARTIN GROUPS
PEIO ARDAIZ-GALE
Abstract. We compute the cohomology groups of the pure symmetric outer automorphism group
ΣPOut(AΓ) and the pure symmetric automorphism group ΣPAut(AΓ) of a right-angled Artin group
AΓ. Using the equivariant spectral sequence arising from the action of ΣPOut(AΓ) on the generalized
McCullough-Miller complex MMΓ, we show that Hq(ΣPOut(AΓ)) is free abelian and we compute its
rank in terms of the combinatorics of certain poset. Applying the Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral
sequence and the Leray-Hirsch theorem we do the same for Hq(ΣPAut(AΓ)). In both cases the cohomol-
ogy algebra is generated in degree 1. Finally, we introduce the Generalized Brownstein-Lee Conjecture,
proposing a presentation of H∗(ΣPAut(AΓ)), and prove that it holds in dimension 2.
1. Introduction
Right-angled Artin groups (RAAGs) and their automorphism groups are very active field of study in
geometric group theory. Given a RAAG AΓ, the automorphisms of AΓ that map each standard generator
to a conjugate of itself are called pure symmetric and form a subgroup denoted ΣPAut(AΓ) that contains
the subgroup of inner automorphism, so we can define the pure symmetric outer automorphism group
as ΣPOut(AΓ) ∼= ΣPAut(AΓ)/Inn(AΓ). In the case when AΓ is the free group Fn, ΣPAut(Fn) is the
fundamental group of the configuration space of n unknotted, unlinked circles in the sphere S3 [9], there
is also a version of this for symmetric automorphisms of other right-angled Artin groups [3].
In [15], McCullough and Miller constructed a contractible simplicial complex MMn that admits an
action of the group ΣPOut(Fn) with free abelian stabilizers. The construction is based on the Whitehead
poset Whn, whose elements can be expressed as labeled trees. In [9], Jensen, McCammond and Meier
use the McCullough-Miller complex to compute H∗(ΣPOut(Fn)) and H∗(ΣPAut(Fn)), the cohomology
groups of the (outer) pure symmetric automorphisms of the free group; even more, they prove the
Brownstein-Lee conjecture [5], which stablish a presentation of the cohomology ring H∗(ΣPAut(Fn)).
In [1], Ardaiz-Gale and Martínez-Pérez generalized both the Whitehead poset and the construction of
McCullough and Miller from the case of ΣPOut(Fn) to ΣPOut(AΓ). This new complex, MMΓ, admits
an action of the group ΣPOut(AΓ) with free abelian stabilizers and it is also contractible. The powerful
combinatorial machinery derived from the labeled trees in Whn is the key to prove the main results in
[9]. However, vertices in WhΓ cannot be expressed as labeled trees in general, only as families of based
partitions, so most arguments need to be adapted.
In this paper we will generalize some combinatorial results from Whn to WhΓ and use the equivariant
spectral sequence derived from the ΣPOut(AΓ) action over MMΓ to compute the cohomology groups
Hq(ΣPOut(AΓ)) and Hq(ΣPAut(AΓ)). We show (see Sections 2 and 4 for definitions):
Theorem A. Let Kq be the number of rank q essential vertex types in WhΓ, then
Hq(ΣPOut(AΓ)) = ZKq.

2. The McCullough-Miller complex
In this chapter we will explain some basic results about the McCullough-Miller complex for RAAGs.
A more detailed explanation can be found at [1].
Definition 2.1 (Graph). We will use Γ to denote a finite simplicial graph with vertex set V (Γ) and set
of edges E(Γ). Very often we will identify Γ or a subgraph of Γ with its vertex set. Let v ∈V (Γ), lk(v)
denotes the link of v, which is the subgraph induced by the vertices adjacent to v. We will define the
star of v, st(v), as the subgraph induced by lk(v) ∪{v}.
Associated to Γ we may define a group as follows.
Definition 2.2 (RAAG). The RAAG (right-angled Artin group) defined by a simplicial graph Γ is the
group with presentation
AΓ = ⟨v ∈V (Γ) | [v, w] = 1 whenever {v, w} ∈E(Γ)⟩
This is, AΓ is the group generated by the vertices of Γ, which are called standard generators so that
two standard generators commute if and only if they are adjacent in Γ. Two simple examples are the
extreme cases: if Γ is a graph with no edges, then AΓ is a free group, and if Γ a complete graph, then
AΓ is a free abelian group.

Definition 2.8 (Valid based partitions, petals and length). Let u ∈Γ be a vertex. A Γ-valid based
partition with operative factor u is a partition of the form τu = {{u}, P1, . . . , Pk} of the set Γ −lk(u) so

number of petals, k, is the length of the based partition, and we denote it l(τu).
From now on, every based partition we use will be a Γ-admissible based partition, for simplicity we
will simply say based partition if the graph Γ is clear.
Definition 2.9 (Crossings, disjoint partition and compatibility). We say that two based partitions τu
and τv cross if u /∈st(v) and there are petals P of τu and Q of τv such that Dv ̸⊂P, Du ̸⊂Q, and
P ∩Q ̸= ∅. It is easy to check that this is equivalent to asking that there is at least one shared component
C such that C ⊆P and C ⊆Q. We will say that two based partitions τu, τv are disjoint if u /∈st(v) and
they do not cross and we say that they are compatible if they are disjoint or u ∈st(v).
Definition 2.10 (Vertex type). We define a vertex type τ = {τv1, ..., τvn} as a collection of pairwise
compatible based partitions, such that there is one for each vertex in Γ.
We will say that two vertex types τ and τ ′ are compatible if their based partitions are pairwise
compatible. And we will say that a collection of vertex types is compatible if they are pairwise compatible.
Remark. Here we are using a slightly different notation from [1], where vertex types where denoted A,
and a based partition Au. The extensive notation in [1] is forced by the technical work done there. Here,
this simpler version will be enough.

Appendix A. Algorithm to compute the Kq:
The main theorems of this paper (Theorem A and B) give us a formula to compute the cohomology
groups Hq(ΣPOut(AΓ)) and Hq(ΣPOut(AΓ)). In the case when AΓ is the free group Fn, it is possible to
give a closed formula in terms of n only (Lemma 5.2 in [9]). However, that cannot be done for arbitrary
RAAGs; we can, nonetheless, implement an algorithm that can be used for explicit computations.
The idea is simple: for each vertex vi ∈Γ, we take all the connected components in Γ −st(vi) and
combine them in all possible ways to obtain Pi, the set of the based partitions associated to each vi.
Then, we consider the cartesian product of the Pi’s. We have to remove from the cartesian product the
elements that are not vertex types, so for each SIL-pair (v, w) we check which based partitions cross and
we remove all the elements which have those two based partitions at the same time.
Once we have all the vertex types, we select those of rank q and check if there is some based partition
which does not contain the minimal element and admits a split; if there is none, then the vertex type is
essential.

for each rank, the number of simplices in |WhΓ| and the E1 page for the ΣPOut(AΓ) action on MMΓ can
be found at https://github.com/peioardaiz-gale/Codes-for-the-generalized-Whitehead-poset/
