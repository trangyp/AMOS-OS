Abstract
Asymptotic properties of discrete, stochastic approximations to hybrid sys-
tems, modeled as hybrid inclusions, are studied. First, the internal chain
transitivity of omega-limits of solutions is concluded, along with other prop-
erties related to chain recurrence and transitivity. A concept of an asymptotic
solution is proposed to describe any mapping that, asymptotically, resembles
a solution, and for which the chain transitivity properties also turn out to
hold. The mentioned developments are carried out in an abstract setting of a
generalized hybrid system defined by a set of hybrid curves, each defined on
a hybrid time domain, and possibly consisting of all solutions to a given hy-
brid inclusion. Then, more specific kinds of perturbed solutions to a hybrid
inclusion are proposed and shown to include the solutions of a discretization
and of a stochastic approximation to the hybrid inclusion. Consequently, ap-
propriate discretizations and stochastic approximations of a hybrid inclusion
produce mappings whose omega limits are internally chain transitive for the
underlying hybrid inclusion.
Keywords:
omega-limit sets, internal chain transitivity, hybrid systems,
simulators, stochastic approximation.
1. Introduction
This paper aims to characterize the omega-limit set of each bounded,
complete sample path of a stochastic, discrete signal that is an asymptotic
simulation of a hybrid system. The findings are similar to those in the lit-
erature on stochastic approximation of differential equations and inclusions;

Compared to the results on stochastic

means and variances are relaxed.
The notions of chain recurrence and chain transitivity date back to [5],
where they were presented in the setting of flows.
Chain recurrence lays
the foundation for Conley’s decomposition, which has been called “the fun-
damental theorem of dynamical systems” [6].
The decomposition, among
other things, identifies the subset of an invariant set — its chain recurrent
part — to which all trajectories converge. The notion of chain recurrence
and the decomposition have been extended to multivalued dynamics in con-
tinuous time [7] and discrete time [8], to an abstract setting of multivalued
semiflows, with applications to infinite-dimensional dynamics, [9] to hybrid
dynamics [10], [11] and to other settings as well. Internal chain transitiv-
ity, considered early by [12], [13], further narrows down the sets to which a
trajectory of a dynamical system can converge. The key to this narrowing
is that the omega-limit of a trajectory of regular-enough dynamical system
is internally chain transitive. Converse results to this exist too; see [14] and
some references therein. An early and related result on internal chain recur-
rence is [15]. The concepts of internal chain recurrence and transitivity have
not been studied in the case of general hybrid dynamics, and this inspires
Section 2 below. There, the concepts are defined and studied in the setting
of an abstract hybrid system, defined as a set of “hybrid curves” and subject
to some structural properties. For an overview of abstract approaches to
continuous-time dynamics, multivalued or without uniqueness, see [16], [17,
Chapter I], or [18], and the references therein.
Chain transitivity properties are known to hold not just for trajectories
of a regular-enough dynamical system, but may hold for functions or more
general mappings that asymptotically resemble the said trajectories. This
applies, for example, to “asymptotic pseudotrajectories” in [19], [20] or solu-
tions to asymptotically autonomous dynamics as in [21]. The properties of
asymptotic pseudotrajectories were used in [1] to establish asymptotic prop-
erties of non-stochastic and stochastic simulations of a differential inclusion,

2.2. Chain and internal chain transitivity and recurrence
Given two points x, y ∈Rd and τ, ε > 0, a (τ, ε)-L-chain from x to
y consists of finite sequences of points x = x0, x1, . . . , xk∗= y in Rd, and
curves ϕ0, ϕ1, ..., ϕk∗−1 in L such that ϕk(0, 0) = xk, xk+1 ∈ϕk(tk, jk) + εB
for some (tk, jk) ∈dom ϕk with tk + jk ≥τ. A generalized (τ, ε)-L-chain
from x to y relaxes x0 = x to x0 ∈x + εB. When a nonempty and closed set
K ⊂Rd is under consideration, given two points x, y ∈K and τ, ε > 0, an
internal (τ, ε)-L-chain from x to y is a (τ, ε)-L-chain from x to y such that
ϕk(t, j) ∈K for all (t, j) ∈dom ϕi with 0 ≤t + j ≤tk + jk, for 0 ≤k < k∗.
A similar restriction defines a generalized internal (τ, ε)-L-chain from x to y.
The set K ⊂Rd is (internally) L-chain transitive if, for every x, y ∈K
and every τ, ε > 0, there exists an (internal) (τ, ε)-L-chain from x to y, and
generalized (internally) L-chain transitive if generalized (internal) (τ, ε)-L-
chains from x to y exist. Further, K is (internally) L-chain recurrent if, for
every x ∈K and every τ, ε > 0, there exists an (internal) (τ, ε)-L-chain from
x to x. Clearly, if K is (internally) L-chain transitive then it is (internally)
L-chain recurrent, by taking y = x.
Establishing the generalized version of (internal) chain recurrence or tran-
sitivity is often easier than establishing the not generalized version. However,
the two variations are often equivalent. For hybrid inclusions, the equivalence
of generalized chain recurrence to chain recurrence is shown in [11, Lemma
5]. A similar argument verifies the next result.

3. Asymptotic curves and their asymptotic properties
An asymptotic L-curve, defined formally in Subsection 3.1, is a poten-
tially set-valued mapping for which, roughly, the later its tail starts the
more it resembles an L-curve. Subsection 3.2 shows that the ω-limit of any
asymptotic L-curve is internally L-chain transitive, so that every asymptotic
L-curve converges to a set that can be identified through L. Subsection 3.3
concludes the study of the abstract hybrid dynamics represented by L, by
characterizing the smallest family L of hybrid curves for which a given hybrid
curve is an asymptotic L-curve.

7. An example: simulated annealing
7.1. Problem statement and assumptions
This section’s example is related to stochastic-approximation-based sim-
ulated annealing algorithms, which combine stochastic gradient descent with
random search for global minimization. See [33, Chapter 8] for a general
discussion of simulated annealing algorithms and their connections to the
stochastic approximation literature, which includes the detailed analysis of
stochastic-approximation-based simulated annealing given in [24]. The ap-
proach taken in this section is to consider an algorithm that can be viewed
as a stochastic approximation of a hybrid system.
The scalar-valued function to minimize is denoted Θ. It is defined on
an open set O ⊂Rn and is assumed to be nonpathological; see [34] or [35,
Definition 3]. In particular, Θ is locally Lipschitz and satisfies a convenient
chain rule when composed with an absolutely continuous function, e.g., a
solution to a differential inclusion. The set over which Θ is to be minimized
is denoted S ⊂O. The symbol Θ|S is used to denote the restriction of Θ to
55


## Page 56


the set S. The set S is assumed to be closed and prox-regular in the sense of
[36, Definition 1.1]; equivalently, according to [36, Theorem 1.3(a)⇔(k)], S is
closed and the closest point projection, i.e., the mapping that projects points
in Rn onto S, denoted PS, is single valued near S. An additional assumption
imposed on S and Θ pertains to the so-called regular values of Θ|S. A point
x ∈S is a critical point of Θ|S if 0 ∈∂Θ(x), where ∂Θ : O ⇒Rn is the Clarke
generalized gradient of Θ. Since Θ is assumed to be locally Lipschitz on O,
∂Θ is well defined with nonempty, convex values [37, Definition 10.3], locally
bounded [37, Proposition 10.5], and outer semicontinuous [37, Proposition
10.10]. Thus, it satisfies the conditions imposed on a flow map in the Hybrid
Basic Conditions. The set of critical points of Θ|S is denoted Cr(Θ|S). A
value r ∈Θ(S) is a regular value of Θ|S if r /∈Θ(Cr(Θ|S)). It is assumed that
the regular values of Θ|S are dense in Θ(S). By the Morse-Sard theorem,
this property holds when Θ is n times continuously differentiable [38], [39].
One situation covered by the assumptions is when S = Rm × Q, where
Q ⊂R is a finite set, and O = Rm × (Q + εB◦), where ε > 0 small enough
so that the projection of a point in Q + εB◦onto Q is unique. In this case,
Θ : O →R is assumed to have the form Θ(z, r) = ΘPQ(r) for all (z, r) ∈O,
where Θq : Rm →R is nonpathological for each q ∈Q with regular values
that are dense in Θq(Rm).
7.2. The underlying hybrid system
The stochastic minimization algorithm considered is constructed as a
stochastic approximation of a hybrid system. That hybrid system’s dynamics
is? the combination of a gradient descent differential inclusion and a hybrid
automaton that enforces an average dwell-time constraint on the number of
jumps experienced by a solution in a given amount of flow time [40]. In the
hybrid system considered, the jumps do not change the state that is execut-

can change the state that is executing (stochastic, approximate) gradient
descent, but to an extent that fades to zero with time almost surely. Such
jumps provide an opportunity for the algorithm to randomly search for a
