CUSPIDAL ℓ-MODULAR REPRESENTATIONS OF GLnpFq
DISTINGUISHED BY A GALOIS INVOLUTION, II
by
Robert Kurinczuk, Nadir Matringe & Vincent Sécherre
Abstract. — Let F{F0 be a quadratic extension of non-Archimedean locally compact fields with
residual characteristic p ‰ 2, and ℓbe a prime number different from p. We classify those ℓ-modular
cuspidal irreducible representations of GLnpFq which are GLnpF0q-distinguished, that is, which car-
ry a non-zero GLnpF0q-invariant linear form. In the case when ℓ‰ 2, an ℓ-modular cuspidal repre-
sentation of GLnpFq is GLnpF0q-distinguished if and only if it lifts to a GLnpF0q-distinguished cus-
pidal ℓ-adic representation, whereas when ℓ“ 2, it is GLnpF0q-distinguished if and only if it is con-
jugate-self-dual.

1. Introduction
1.1. Let F{F0 be a quadratic extension of non-Archimedean locally compact fields with residual
characteristic p ‰ 2. Fix a positive integer n and set G “ GLnpFq and H “ GLnpF0q. Let R be an
algebraically closed field of characteristic ℓą 0 different from p. In [24], we addressed the follow-

Problem 1.1. — Classify the cuspidal, irreducible, smooth R-representations of G which are dis-
tinguished by H, that is, which carry a non-zero H-invariant linear form.
In this article, we give a complete answer to Problem 1.1. The classification can be stated in ve-
ry simple terms. However, the case where ℓ‰ 2 and the case where ℓ“ 2 are completely different.

1.3. Let us now consider representations with coefficients in R. For simplicity, we will assume in
this introduction that R is an algebraic closure Fℓof a finite field of cardinality ℓ. First, as in the
complex case, any H-distinguished irreducible Fℓ-representation of G is σ-self-dual ([35] Theorem
4.1). Now let us focus on cuspidal representations.
Recall that over Fℓ, the two notions of cuspidal and supercuspidal representations do not coin-
cide ([37]), contrary to the case of complex representations: an irreducible representation of G is
cuspidal (respectively, supercuspidal) if it is not isomorphic to a subrepresentation (respectively, a
subquotient) of a representation induced from a proper parabolic subgroup of G. Any supercus-
pidal representation is cuspidal, but the converse does not hold in general.
Let us also fix an algebraic closure Qℓof the field Qℓof ℓ-adic numbers and identify the residue
field of its ring of integers Zℓwith Fℓ. In this situation, there is a notion of integral Qℓ-representa-
tion of G and such a representation can be reduced mod ℓ, which gives rise to a semi-simple Fℓ-re-
presentation of finite length of G (see §2.4 for precise definitions). One then defines a Qℓ-lift of an
irreducible Fℓ-representation π of G as an integral Qℓ-representation of G whose reduction mod ℓ
is isomorphic to π.
Cuspidal representations of the group G behave well with respect to reduction and lifting: the
reduction mod ℓof an integral cuspidal Qℓ-representation is irreducible and cuspidal, and any cus-
pidal Fℓ-representation has a Qℓ-lift (which is automatically cuspidal) ([37] III.1.1, III.5.10). Re-
duction mod ℓthus defines a surjective map from isomorphism classes of integral cuspidal Qℓ-re-
presentations of G to isomorphism classes of cuspidal Fℓ-representations of G.
Moreover, reduction mod ℓof integral cuspidal Qℓ-representations preserves H-distinction: the
reduction mod ℓof an H-distinguished integral cuspidal Qℓ-representation is H-distinguished ([23]
Theorem 3.4). (Equivalently, any cuspidal Fℓ-representation of G with an H-distinguished Qℓ-lift
is H-distinguished.) Conversely, any H-distinguished supercuspidal representation of G has an H-
distinguished Qℓ-lift ([35] Theorem 10.11, see also [9] Theorem 3.4 for a more general statement).
There is also a classification of the non-supercuspidal, cuspidal Fℓ-representations of G having an
H-distinguished Qℓ-lift ([24] Propositions 6.1, 6.2). We will come back to it in §1.6.
We show that, surprisingly, assuming that ℓ‰ 2, this Distinguished Lift Property of supercus-
pidal representations extends to all cuspidal representations. Our first main theorem is:
Theorem 1.2. — Assume that ℓ‰ 2. Then a cuspidal Fℓ-representation of G is H-distinguished
if and only if it has an H-distinguished Qℓ-lift.
Together with our classification of the cuspidal Fℓ-representations of G having an H-distingui-
shed Qℓ-lift, this thus gives a complete classification of all cuspidal Fℓ-representation of G distin-
guished by H in the case when ℓ‰ 2.

1.5. Let us now explain the mains ideas of our proof of our main theorems, starting with Theo-

1.9. Let us now explain the main ideas of our proof of Theorem 1.3. It is based on the following
observation: if π is a non-supercuspidal, cuspidal F2-representation of GLnpkq, then n is even and
there is a unique cuspidal F2-representation τ of GLn{2pkq such that π is the unique generic sub-
quotient of the induced representation τ ˆτ. By uniqueness, π is σ-self-dual if and only if τ is σ-
self-dual. Since Theorem 1.3 is known to hold for supercuspidal representations, it thus suffices to
prove that, if τ is distinguished, then π is distinguished.
For this, we examine the induced representation τ ˆ τ. It is indecomposable of length 3. As-
suming that τ is distinguished, we construct two independent invariant linear forms on the repre-
sentation τ ˆ τ vanishing on its socle. If π were not distinguished, one would thus get two inde-
pendent invariant F2-linear forms on the cosocle of τ ˆτ. This contradicts Proposition 7.7, which
says that the dimension of the space of invariant linear forms on this cosocle is at most 1. Theo-
rem 1.3 follows.
