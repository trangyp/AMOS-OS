Convex Hull Volumes in Hyperbolic 3-Space
Cameron MacMahon
April 2026
Abstract
In this paper we provide a geometric condition satisfied by certain closed subsets
of the Riemann sphere which implies that their hyperbolic convex hulls in H3 have
infinite volume. As a corollary, we characterize continua in the Riemann sphere whose
hyperbolic convex hulls have infinite volume, answering a question of Danny Calegari.
Furthermore, we give a geometric characterization of planar self-similar sets whose
hyperbolic convex hulls have infinite volume.
1
Introduction
It was claimed in [1] that the hyperbolic convex hull in H3 of a Jordan curve Γ in the
Riemann sphere ˆC has infinite volume except when Γ is a circle. More generally, Danny
Calegari asked whether or not it is possible to characterize those continua (compact,
connected sets) in ˆC whose hyperbolic convex hulls have infinite volume. In this work we
provide such a characterization:
Theorem 1.1. The volume of the hyperbolic convex hull of a continuum K in the Riemann
sphere is either zero or infinity, and is zero if and only if K lies on a circle.
This follows from Theorem 3.4 below, which gives a condition on a closed subset E ⊂ˆC
that, when satisfied, implies that the hyperbolic convex hull of E has infinite volume. This
condition will be satisfied if the one dimensional Hausdorff measure of E is positive and
E does not lie on a circle. Investigating whether or not this condition is necessary, we
discuss hyperbolic convex hull volumes of planar self-similar sets. We also characterize
planar self-similar sets whose hyperbolic convex hulls have infinite volume:
Theorem 1.2. The volume of the hyperbolic convex hull of a self-similar set K ⊂ˆC is
either zero or infinity, and is zero if and only if K lies on a circle.
It is the author’s belief that the methods in this paper can be tweaked to decide the
question of finite or infinite hyperbolic convex hull volume for other classes of sets in the
plane and/or particular sets. In the case of continua motivation for Theorem 1.1 comes
from hyperbolic geometry, in particular the discussion following Theorem 6.24 in [3]. The
proof of Theorem 6.24 (whose statement is beyond the scope of this work) would be

Convex Hull Volumes
We begin by recalling some facts about hyperbolic convex hulls. Let E ⊂ˆC be closed.
Definition 3.1. The hyperbolic convex hull of E, CH(E) ⊂B, is the hyperbolic convex
hull of the union of all hyperbolic geodesics in B with endpoints on E.
We begin by characterizing precisely which E the hyperbolic convex hull has nonempty
interior. The following argument is elementary, and can be found in standard references
on this topic such as Epstein and Marden [5]:
Theorem 3.2. For E ⊂ˆC closed, CH(E) has empty interior if and only if E is a subset
of a circle.
Proof. Suppose E is a subset of a circle C. Then by definition CH(E) ⊂CH(C). How-
ever, CH(C) is a dome on a sphere intersecting B orthogonally and thus has no interior.
Therefore, CH(E) has no interior.
The opposite direction follows from the fact that CH(E) is a closed convex subset
of B that is not contained in any totally geodesic hyperbolic plane. For if CH(E) were
contained in a totally geodesic hyperbolic plane, E would consist of only landing points
for geodesics within this plane and those landing points would belong to the circle given
by the intersection of the totally geodesic hyperbolic plane with the boundary.
Thus,
we may choose three non-colinear points in CH(E) and form the unique totally geodesic
hyperbolic plane containing the triangle they form. By the above, we may pick a fourth
point in CH(E) not lying on this plane. The hull of this fourth point and the triangular
patch on the plane forms a hyperbolic tetrahedron contained within CH(E) by convexity,
and so CH(E) has non-empty interior.

Necessity and Cantor Bridge sets
One may inquire as to whether the sufficient condition in Theorem 3.4 is also necessary
in order that the hyperbolic volume of CH(E) for a closed subset of ˆC is infinite. This
is not the case. In what follows, we construct a family of sufficiently thin Cantor sets for
which the sum in Theorem 3.4 converges for all λ ∈(0, 1) but whose hyperbolic convex
hull volumes are infinite.
Let Cβ, 0 < β < 1 be the standard 4-corners Cantor set in the plane, given by the
product of two Cantor sets in the interval given by removing the central interval of length
β. We call CH(Cβ) ⊂H3 a Cantor Bridge Set, as the geodesics with both landing points
on the totally disconnected set Cβ may be seen as “bridges.” We begin by showing that
all such sets have infinite volume.
