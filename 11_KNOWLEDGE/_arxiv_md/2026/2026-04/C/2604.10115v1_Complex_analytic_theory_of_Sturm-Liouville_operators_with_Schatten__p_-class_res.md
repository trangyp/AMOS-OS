COMPLEX ANALYTIC THEORY OF STURM–LIOUVILLE OPERATORS WITH
SCHATTEN p-CLASS RESOLVENTS
GUGLIELMO FUCCI, MATEUSZ PIORKOWSKI, AND JONATHAN STANFILL
Abstract. We use the theory of entire functions of finite order to prove a universal spectral depen-
dence of the blowup/decay rate of solutions of the Sturm–Liouville eigenvalue equation for problems
with Schatten p-class resolvents. The general form of the asymptotics turns out to depend exclusively
on the largest integer p such that the underlying resolvents fail to be in the Schatten p-class.
We then use the above result to construct a characteristic function of minimal order for Sturm–
Liouville problems with Schatten p-class resolvents. This immediately yields contour integral represen-
tations of spectral ζ-functions that were previously only known for quasi-regular problems (except for
a few examples). We also demonstrate how our methods lead to new results in connection to impor-
tant classic topics of Liouville–Green (or WKB) asymptotics and the approximation of the spectrum of
singular problems via underlying truncated regular problems. All our applications are accompanied by
illustrative examples, including the Airy differential equation, harmonic oscillator (and general power
potentials), and the Laguerre differential equation.
1. Introduction
The main motivation for the current work is spectral ζ-functions, in particular, the process of ana-
lytically continuing their sum definition to the rest of the complex plane. The properties revealed in
this process, such as the location and order of poles as well as trivial zeros and special values, are very
important in many areas of mathematics and physics [7, 8, 23]. The starting point for this analysis is
the contour integral representation for the spectral ζ-function recently generalized in [13]. A key part of
such formulas is the characteristic function, which is an entire function vanishing exactly at the eigen-
values (see Def. 1.2). While not unique, in the context of integral representations it is essential that the
characteristic function is of finite (ideally minimal) order as an entire function (see [29, Lect. 1]). This
is only possible if the eigenvalues do not grow too slowly; that is, they must satisfy at least λn ≳n1/κ
for some κ > 0. Equivalently, resolvents of self-adjoint realizations need to be in some Schatten p-class,
which we will assume throughout.
One of our main results is the construction of characteristic functions of minimal order, see Theorem
1.3 below. The key technical ingredient in our analysis is the establishment of precise formulas for the
asymptotics of principal and nonprincipal solutions at the singular endpoints found in Theorem 1.8.
These formulas are universal, in the sense that their general form depends only on the largest integer p
such that the underlying resolvents fail to be in the Schatten p-class. No additional smoothness of the
coefficients of the Sturm–Liouville expression, except for the standard L1
loc-assumptions (see Hypothesis
2.1), is required for this result to hold. In Section 4 we demonstrate how our asymptotic formulas for the
(non)principal solutions have immediate consequences to other topics of interest: Liouville–Green (or

As we have already mentioned earlier, the properties of the spectral ζ-function, for instance the
location and order of its poles, are very important and the integral representation (1.6) is a particularly
suitable tool for this analysis. In fact, it provides a convenient starting point for the process of analytic
continuation of the spectral ζ-function in the complex plane (a process described thoroughly in, for
example, [11, 12, 13]).
To fully benefit from the integral representation provided in (1.6) of Theorem 1.3, one would ideally
want an explicit characteristic function FA(z) of minimal order. In fact, if FA(z) is chosen to be of
minimal growth order ρ = κ, then the representation (1.6) becomes valid in the largest possible region
of the complex plane given by Re(s) > κ, providing us with the optimal starting point for computing
the analytic continuation to the region Re(s) ⩽κ as outlined in [12]. This observation is the main
motivation for the present work.

the construction of characteristic functions of minimal growth order in the case of operators without
trace-class resolvent (i.e., p ⩾1) proves to be much more difficult and interesting.

4.2. Approximation of singular problems by regular ones: convergence rate of eigenvalues.
Next, we revisit a classic problem in the spectral theory of Sturm–Liouville operators.
Consider a
generally singular Sturm–Liouville expression τ on (a, b), and a self-adjoint realization T (later we take
the Friedrichs realization). As we are interested in the singular case, we assume that τ is in the limit
point case at one (or both) of the endpoints. In particular, T might satisfy a boundary condition at
the limit circle endpoint, but will certainly not satisfy any coupled boundary conditions.
It is known that if we consider appropriate self-adjoint realizations Tn of the truncated differential
expression τ|(an,bn) for n ∈N and a < an < bn < b, then the spectrum σ(Tn) will approximate σ(T) in
the following sense. For any λ ∈σ(T) there will be an appropriate sequence jn such that λjn,n →λ.
Here λj,n denotes the jth eigenvalue of Tn (note that σ(Tn) = {λj,n}∞
j=1 is always discrete as τ|(an,bn) is
regular at both endpoints). Moreover, if T has exactly N ∈N∪{∞} discrete eigenvalues {λj}N
j=1 below
its continuous spectrum, then λj,n →λj as n →∞for j ⩽N, and λj,n →inf σess(T) as n →∞for
j > N. Further details, in particular the exact choice of boundary conditions satisfied by the ‘inherited
operator’ Tn can be found in [1, 9], [48, Ch. 10.8]. For our purposes, it is enough to state that the
