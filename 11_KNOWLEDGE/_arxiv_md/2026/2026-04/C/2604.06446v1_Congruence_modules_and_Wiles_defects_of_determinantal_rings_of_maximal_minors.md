CONGRUENCE MODULES AND WILES DEFECTS OF
DETERMINANTAL RINGS OF MAXIMAL MINORS
KASHIF KHAN AND ARYAMAN MAITHANI
ABSTRACT. Let O be a discrete valuation ring and A := O[Xm×n]/Im(X) the determinantal ring of maximal mi-
nors. We consider algebra maps λ : A −! O, which is tantamount to choosing rank-deficient matrices a ∈Om×n.
Following Iyengar–Khare–Manning, we compute the congruence module and the Wiles defect of A at λ, expressing
them in terms of the (m−1)-sized minors of a.
1. INTRODUCTION
Let O be a discrete valuation ring, and A an O-algebra equipped with a map λ : A −↠O of O-algebras. We set
p := kerλ, and let m denote the unique maximal ideal of A containing p. We say that A is regular at λ if Ap is
regular. Following [IKM], we let Ψλ(A) and δλ(A) denote the congruence module and the Wiles defect of Am
at λ, respectively; see §2 for the definitions. The purpose of this brief manuscript is to compute them for the
determinantal rings of maximal minors:
Theorem 1.1. Let O be a discrete valuation ring. Let 2 ⩽m ⩽n be integers and A := O[Xm×n]/Im(X) the
determinantal ring of maximal minors. Consider a point a ∈Om×n with Im(a) = 0; such a point defines a map
λa : A −↠O. The algebra A is regular at λa if and only if ranka = m−1, in which case, we have
Ψλa(A) ∼= O/Im−1(a)
and
δλa(A) = (n−m)wa,
where wa = lengthO(O/Im−1(a)).
We prove this theorem in §5 as Corollary 5.11, recovering [IKM, Proposition 4.2] for m = 2. The quantity
wa above may also be interpreted as the smallest valuation of an (m −1)-sized minor of a; see also (5.1) for
another reinterpretation. We expect the above result to aid in calculations in number theory; the case m = 2
already finds application in [IKM, §5] in computing congruence ideals for Steinberg deformation rings.
In §2, we recall the relevant definitions and results from [IKM] that are needed for this paper. In §3, we
introduce the auxiliary objects and notation needed for the proof of our result, proving facts about these objects
in §4 under the assumption that we are working over a field. Finally, in §5, we adapt these to the case where
the base ring is a discrete valuation ring, proving our main result.

We may extend the above definitions to not necessarily local O-algebras in the following manner: if A is an
O-algebra with a map λ : A −↠O, then there is a unique maximal ideal m containing kerλ, and λ extends
to a local map λm : Am −↠O. We then set Ψλ(A) := Ψλm(Am) and similarly for δλ(A) and Φλ(A); this is
unambiguous as m is uniquely determined by A and λ. Moreover, if (C,λC) −↠(A,λA) is a (not necessarily
local) surjection over O, then letting mC and mA denote the respective maximal ideals determined by these
maps, there is an induced local surjection CmC −↠AmA over O. This localisation will put us in a position to
apply Theorem 2.1; it happens to be the case that the rings C and A we consider are complete intersections and
Cohen–Macaulay not just locally, but globally as well.

4. COMPUTATIONS OVER FIELDS
Throughout this section, we continue with the notation of §3 under the additional assumption that O is a field.
We may then talk about the graded canonical module ωR of an N-graded noetherian ring R with [R]0 = O.
Recall that for such a ring R, the a-invariant aR is the negative of the minimal degree of a nonzero homogeneous
element of ωR. We first prove that C is a reduced complete intersection.
Lemma 4.1. The sequence f is a regular sequence in P. Thus, C is a complete intersection of dimension
mn−n+t. Moreover, C is reduced.
Proof. Consider the lexicographic monomial ordering < on P induced by the lexicographic ordering on the
