CHARACTERIZING FINITE SOLVABLE GROUPS THROUGH
THE NILPOTENCY PROBABILITY
ANDREA LUCCHINI
Abstract. Given a finite group G, we denote by ν(G) the probability that
two randomly chosen elements of G generate a nilpotent subgroup. We prove
that if ν(G) > 1/12, then G is solvable.
1. Introduction
Given a finite group G, let N(G) be the set of ordered pairs (g1, g2) ∈G2 with
the property that ⟨g1, g2⟩is a nilpotent subgroup of G. Then
ν(G) := |N(G)|
|G|2
expresses the probability that two randomly chosen elements of G generate a nilpo-
tent subgroup. Guralnick and Wilson [4] proved that if ν(G) > ν(Sym(3)) = 1/2,
then G is nilpotent. However, the following question, raised in [5], remains open:
does ν(G) > 1/12 imply that G is solvable? In this note, we provide an affirmative
answer.
Theorem 1. Let G be a finite group. If ν(G) >
1
12, then G is solvable.
Since ν(Alt(5)) = 1/12, the previous result is sharp.

Proof of Theorem 1. Assume that G is a nonsolvable finite group. Then G admits
a nonabelian chief factor X/Y . In particular G/CG(X/Y ) has a unique minimal
normal subgroup, and this minimal normal subgroup is isomorphic to X/Y. It can
be easily proved that ν(G) ≤ν(G/N) for every normal subgroup N of G (see for

3. Details on the computation of ˜ν(S)
To compute the exact value of ˜ν(S), with S ∈S, the following observation is
useful.
Lemma 6. Let N be a normal subgroup of a finite group G, and let Ωbe the set of
pairs (g1, g2) ∈G2 such that ⟨g1, g2⟩N = G. Then the value of νg1,g2(G, N) is the
same for every choice of (g1, g2) ∈Ω.
Proof. Denote by H the set of 2-generated subgroups H of G that are nilpotent
and such that G = HN. Assume that (g1, g2) ∈Ωand, for every H ∈H, let
∆(g1, g2, H) be the set of (n1, n2) such that H = ⟨n1g1, n2g2⟩. Then Ng1,g2(G, N)
is the disjoint union of the subsets ∆(g1, g2, H), with H ranging over H.
Fix H ∈H. Since HN = G, there exist n1, n2 ∈N such that g1 = n1h1 and
g2 = n2h2 and therefore (n−1
1 , n−1
2 ) ∈∆(g1, g2, H). Moreover
∆(g1, g2, H) = {(m1, m2) ∈(N ∩H)2 | ⟨(m1n1)(n−1
1 g1), (m2n2)(n−1
2 g2)⟩= H}.
It follows from the main result in [3] that |∆(g1, g2, H)|/|N ∩H|2 does not depend
on the choice of (g1, g2) and coincides with the conditional probability that two
randomly chosen elements of H generate H given that they generate H modulo
H ∩N. Consequently, the cardinality of the sets ∆(g1, g2, H), and hence also that
of their disjoint union, does not depend on the choice of (g1, g2).
□
Given S ∈S, for every almost simple group G with soc(G) = S, we use the
table of marks of G to determine the nilpotent 2-generated subgroups of G that
supplement soc(G), and for each such subgroup we compute the number of ordered
pairs that generate it. Let τ(G, S) be the sum of the numbers thus obtained, divided
by |S|2 and by the number of pairs that generate G/S.
The resulting number
coincides with νg1,g2(G, S), where ⟨g1, g2⟩S = G. Finally, ˜ν(S) is the maximum of
the values τ(G, S) as G ranges over the almost simple groups with socle S.
