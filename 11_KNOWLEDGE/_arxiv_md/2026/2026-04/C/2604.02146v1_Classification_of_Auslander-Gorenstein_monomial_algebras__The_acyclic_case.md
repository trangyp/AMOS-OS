VIKT´ORIA KL´ASZ⋆, MARKUS KLEINAU⋆, AND REN´E MARCZINZIK
Dedicated to Claus Michael Ringel on the occasion of his eightieth birthday
Abstract. We give a linear algebraic classification of Auslander regular acyclic monomial algebras
via the Bruhat factorisation of the Coxeter matrix. Namely, we show under mild assumptions that a
monomial acyclic quiver algebra is Auslander regular if and only if its Coxeter matrix C has a Bruhat
factorisation U1PU2 with U1 the identity matrix. In particular, this holds without restrictions for lin-
ear Nakayama algebras and we use the Bruhat decomposition to answer a question raised by Ringel
by showing that his homological permutation coincides with the permutation coming from the Bruhat
factorisation of the Coxeter matrix. We also use our methods to show that general Auslander regu-
lar acyclic quiver algebras are echelon-independent, proving a conjecture of Defant-Jiang-Marczinzik-
Segovia-Speyer-Thomas-Williams and we answer another question by Ringel on the delooping level of
simple modules over Nakayama algebras.

1. Introduction
Let R be a Noetherian (not necessarily commutative) ring. Then R is called n-Gorenstein if there
exists an injective coresolution of the R-module RR
0 →RR →I0 →I1 →I2 →. . .
such that the flat dimension of Ii is at most i for all n > i ≥0. We call R Auslander-Gorenstein if the
above resolution is finite and if R is n-Gorenstein for all n ≥0. We say that R is Auslander regular if it is
Auslander-Gorenstein of finite global dimension. By Bass’s classical result [B], a commutative local ring
R is Gorenstein (resp. regular) in the classical sense of commutative algebra if and only if it is Auslander-
Gorenstein (resp. Auslander regular). This was one of the motivations of Auslander to introduce the
notion of Auslander-Gorenstein rings. There are many important classes of non-commutative rings that
are Auslander-Gorenstein. Let us mention, for example, the enveloping algebras of finite dimensional Lie
algebras, Weyl algebras, and rings of C-linear differential operators on an irreducible smooth subvariety
of an affine space, see [VO, Chapter 3] for more details.

uniquely determined by M. Let A be a finite dimensional quiver algebra of finite global dimension with
a fixed numbering of the vertices of A from 1 to n. The permutation corresponding to the permutation
matrix in a Bruhat decomposition of the Coxeter matrix of A is called the Coxeter permutation of A with
respect to the given order of the vertices. In [KMT], the Coxeter permutation was introduced and it was
shown that it generalises the well-studied rowmotion bijection on distributive lattices. In [DJMSSTW],
the Coxeter permutation (or more precisely, its inverse) was studied under the name echelonmotion for
general incidence algebras of posets and it was shown that it recovers several well-studied bijections in
combinatorics. Note that, in general, the Coxeter permutation depends on the numbering of the vertices
of our quiver. For linear Nakayama algebras, we will always assume in the following that the vertices are
labelled by the canonical ordering from 1 to n, as shown in (1.1.1). More generally, for acyclic monomial
algebras, we will always assume that their vertices are naturally labelled, which means that if there is a
path from a vertex i to a vertex j with j ̸= i then we have i < j. Our first main result is the following:
Theorem 1.3. (Theorem 4.9 and Theorem 4.11) Let A be a linear Nakayama algebra or a 2-Gorenstein,
acyclic monomial algebra. Then A is Auslander regular if and only if there exists a Bruhat decomposition
CA = U1PU2 of the Coxeter matrix of A where U1 is the identity matrix.
We mention that this theorem is not only interesting from a theoretical point of view but also from
a computational aspect. When testing whether a given acyclic monomial algebra with around 50 simple
modules is Auslander regular using the homological definition, the GAP-package [QPA] took several hours,
while it only takes seconds when using the above theorem that reduces the problem to linear algebra.
We remark that it is an open question whether the same characterisation of Auslander regular algebras
via their Coxeter matrix holds for incidence algebras of finite bounded posets, see [KMT, Question 5.3]
where such a result was proven for the first time for the subclass of lattices. In [DJMSSTW, Conjecture
8.2] it was conjectured that if the incidence algebra KP of a poset P is Auslander regular, then it is
echelon-independent in the sense that the Coxeter permutation does not depend on the natural labelling,
see [DJMSSTW, Definition 1.1] for the precise definition. More generally, we define an acyclic quiver
algebra to be Coxeter-independent if the Coxeter permutation does not depend on the chosen natural
labelling of the algebra.
The next theorem proves one direction of Question 5.3 in [KMT].
Theorem 1.4. (Theorem 4.6) Let A be a naturally labelled acyclic Auslander regular algebra. Then there
exists a Bruhat decomposition of the Coxeter matrix CA = U1PU2 with U1 = id. In this case, the Coxeter
permutation coincides with the Auslander-Reiten permutation.
We refer to the preliminaries for the definition of the Auslander-Reiten permutation associated to
an Auslander-Gorenstein algebra. As a consequence of the previous theorem, we prove the conjecture
[DJMSSTW, Conjecture 8.2] in the following much more general version:
Theorem 1.5. (Theorem 4.7) Every acyclic Auslander regular algebra is Coxeter-independent.
We will study Coxeter-independent algebras more generally for acyclic algebras in forthcoming work.
In [R], Ringel studied the finitistic dimension of Nakayama algebras, and during his study, he discovered a
homological bijection h on the simple modules of Nakayama algebras that is defined as follows: For a simple
module S define e(S) := min{pdim S, pdim I(S)} and set h(S) = top Ωe(s)(N(S)), where N(S) = S if
e(S) is odd and N(S) = I(S) if e(S) is even.
Here I(S) denotes the injective envelope of S. Note
that e(S) was proven to be finite for every simple module S over a Nakayama algebra. Assume that our
Nakayama algebra has vertices {1, . . . , n}. Then we define the map ˆh : {1, . . . , n} →{1, . . . , n} as ˆh(i) = j
if h(Si) = Sj, where Si denotes the simple module corresponding to vertex i in the quiver. We call ˆh the
homological permutation of the Nakayama algebra. The importance of the invariant e(S) comes from the
fact that max{e(S) | S simple} is equal to the finitistic dimension of a Nakayama algebra and h can be
used to show that the left finitistic dimension coincides with the right finitistic dimension for Nakayama
algebras, as was shown by Ringel in [R]. We also refer to [R] for more information on the homological
bijection of Nakayama algebras and its properties.

2. Preliminaries
We always assume that our algebras are finite dimensional over a field K. Modules are finitely generated
right modules unless otherwise stated. The functor D = HomK(−, K) denotes the natural duality of a
finite dimensional algebra. If A is a finite dimensional algebra with n simple modules, then {e1, . . . , en}
denotes a complete set of primitive, pairwise orthogonal idempotents of A. When A is a quiver algebra,

2.1. Permutations on simple modules. In the following, we recall various permutations introduced on
the simple modules of finite dimensional algebras. For this, we recall the classical Bruhat decomposition
of an invertible matrix. For a proof and an algorithm, see, for example, [AB, Chapter 4] and [OOV].
Theorem 2.5. Let M be an invertible real n × n matrix. Then there exists a factorisation M = U1PU2,
where P is a permutation matrix, and U1 and U2 are upper triangular matrices. The permutation matrix
P is uniquely determined by M.
This decomposition is called a Bruhat decomposition of M. Since the permutation matrix P is uniquely
determined by M, it makes sense to denote it by P[M] in the following. In general, uniqueness for U1
and U2 is far from being true. However, by assuming some additional conditions on U1 and U2, one
can obtain a unique form of the Bruhat decomposition, we refer to [OOV] for details. Given an n × n
permutation matrix P, we define the row-permutation pr associated to P as the permutation acting on
{1, . . . , n} given by pr(i) = j if in the i-th row of P the unique non-zero entry appears in column j.
Dually, we define the column-permutation pc associated to P as the permutation acting on {1, . . . , n}
given by pc(i) = j if in the i-th column of P the unique non-zero entry is in row j. Note that pc is the
inverse permutation of pr.

Auslander-Gorenstein algebras by results of Iyama [I].
Remark 5.5. We would like to emphasise that our result does not recover Ringel’s bijection in its full
generality. While h defines a bijection for any Nakayama algebra, the grade bijection is only defined for
the ones which are Auslander-Gorenstein.
We have shown in Theorem 3.7 that in a general Nakayama algebra, e(S) = del(S) for every simple
module S and for an Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebra we have e(S) = grade(S), and thus,
del(S) = grade(S). The next proposition shows that the equality del(S) = grade(S) for simple modules
S holds for general Auslander-Gorenstein algebras:
Theorem 5.6. Let A be an Auslander-Gorenstein algebra and S a simple A-module. Then del(S) =
grade(S).
Proof. Since for Auslander-Gorenstein algebras grade(S) = pdim I(S) for every simple module S by
[KMT, Theorem 3.3], it is enough to show del(S) = pdim I(S).
The direction del(S) ≤pdim I(S) follows from [R, Proposition 2.3] as S is a submodule of I(S).
For the other direction, we use the statement from [AR, Proposition 5.4] which establishes the existence
of the Auslander-Reiten bijection. More precisely, it tells us that in an Auslander-Gorenstein algebra, for
every simple module S there exists an indecomposable projective module P so that I(S) is isomorphic to
the last term of the minimal injective coresolution of P. In other words, ψ−1(P) = Ω−idim P (P) ∼= I(S).
Moreover, idim P = pdim I(S). Then we have Extidim P (S, P) ̸= 0 by Lemma 2.3, so we can use Lemma
2.4 to obtain the inequality pdim I(S) = idim P ≤del(S).
□

CLASSIFICATION OF AUSLANDER-GORENSTEIN MONOMIAL ALGEBRAS: THE ACYCLIC CASE

In Proposition 5.2, we saw that every simple module S for which I(S) has odd projective dimension
is perfect. There is a stronger property for simple modules S called n-regularity, which is defined by
the condition that S satisfies n = pdim S = grade(S) and dim Extn
A(S, A) = 1. This property plays an
important role in commutative algebra, and especially for n = 2 in the classification of exact structures.
We refer, for example, to [En] for more information. For Nakayama algebras, 2-regular simple modules
were classified in [MRS]. We pose the following conjecture, which would imply even stronger homological
properties for simple modules with odd grade in Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebras:
Conjecture. Let A be an Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebra. Then dim Exti
A(S, A) ≤1 for S
simple and odd i.
Combined with Proposition 5.2, the truth of this conjecture would imply that every simple module S
with odd grade g is g-regular in an Auslander-Gorenstein Nakayama algebra. In forthcoming work, we
will study the classification of Auslander regular linear Nakayama algebras from a enumerative viewpoint
and establish several classification results for some special cases. We mention that the classification for
special subclasses such as dominant Auslander regular algebras and higher Auslander algebras is a recent
field of study, see for example [R2], [Sh2] and [STZ].
