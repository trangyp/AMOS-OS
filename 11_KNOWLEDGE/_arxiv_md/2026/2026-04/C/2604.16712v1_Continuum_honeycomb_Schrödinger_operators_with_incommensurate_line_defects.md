CONTINUUM HONEYCOMB SCHRÖDINGER OPERATORS WITH
INCOMMENSURATE LINE DEFECTS
P. AMENOAGBADJI AND M. I. WEINSTEIN
Abstract. We study wave propagation in 2D honeycomb structures with a non-commensu-
rate or “irrational” line defect or edge. Our model is a Schrödinger operator which interpo-
lates, across the edge, between two distinct bulk (asymptotic) Hamiltonians with a common
spectral gap about the “Dirac point” of an unperturbed honeycomb operator. We seek edge
states, eigenstates that are bounded and oscillatory parallel to the edge, and decaying in the
transverse direction. For non-commensurate edges, the rigorous definition of these states
is nontrivial due to the lack of translation invariance along the edge. To address this, we
exploit quasiperiodicity along the edge by expressing the Hamiltonian as the restriction of
a 3D (degenerate elliptic) Hamiltonian describing a 3D medium with a 2D interface within
which there is periodicity. Via multiscale analysis, we construct approximate edge states in
this 3D setting and obtain by restriction 2D edge states which are quasiperiodic along the
irrational edge. These edge states are seeded by eigenfunctions of an effective Dirac opera-
tor, which has an infinite block-diagonal structure due to the non-commensurate geometry.
A consequence is that infinitely many edge state eigenpairs arise, whose energies are dense
in the perturbed bulk spectral gap. In a forthcoming paper, we rigorously construct these
gap-filling edge states under a Diophantine condition. The main result here is a key tool in
this construction: a resolvent expansion for the 3D Hamiltonian, whose leading term is the
resolvent of the block-diagonal Dirac operator. The validity of this expansion requires an
omnidirectional non-resonance (no-fold) condition on the dispersion functions of the unper-
turbed honeycomb Hamiltonian. This condition is satisfied in the strong binding regime.
In contrast with earlier works on commensurate edges, the omnidirectional condition is
independent of the edge.

1. Introduction
1.1. Overview. The study of honeycomb materials in condensed matter physics was sparked
by the experimental isolation of graphene, a crystalline two-dimensional hexagonal arrange-
ment of carbon atoms extending to a macroscale [70, 122]. The remarkable properties of
quantum electronic waves in graphene have catalyzed investigations of wave propagation in
diverse engineered two-dimensional structures with hexagonal and other novel symmetries.
These so-called artificial graphene systems have been studied and realized experimentally,
for instance, in electronics [142], photonics [125, 101, 130, 131, 16, 151, 123, 82, 14], acoustics
[146, 100, 118, 152], and mechanics [119]; see also the survey [135] for further references.
Many of the interesting properties of graphene-like materials are related to the presence of
Dirac points in their Floquet-Bloch band structure (Section 2), which encodes the spectral
properties of an underlying periodic Hamiltonian, and hence wave propagation properties
of the system. A Dirac point is an energy/quasi-momentum pair, (K⋆, ED), at which two
consecutive dispersion surfaces touch conically. For Hamiltonians H0 governing graphene-
like structures, the existence of Dirac points is rooted in the inversion, complex conjugation,
and rotational symmetries of H0; see Section 2.4. Concerning their stability, roughly speak-
ing, Dirac points persist under small perturbations of H0 that break rotational invariance
(the dispersion cones merely distort), but breaking either inversion or complex conjugation
symmetry induces a local energy gap around the Dirac point.
In this paper, we study a class of continuum Schrödinger Hamiltonians introduced in [62],
building on ideas of [83, 129], for realizing quantum Hall-like edge states in photonic crystals.
Further mathematical investigations appear in [40, 111, 47], and related work on square and
deformed square lattices can be found in [31, 30]. Our Hamiltonian interpolates across a
line defect or edge, via a domain wall, between two distinct bulk (asymptotic) perturbed
honeycomb Hamiltonians, as illustrated in Figure 1. These asymptotic Hamiltonians are
designed to be insulating (“gapped”) about the Dirac energy, ED (Sections 3.1 and 6.2).
When the line defect is oriented in the direction of a period lattice vector, we say the
line defect is commensurate or “rational”. The Hamiltonian describing the medium with an
edge is translation-invariant along the edge, and this edge Hamiltonian can be studied using
Floquet-Bloch theory.
This edge Hamiltonian admits edge states: time-harmonic states
which are pseudo-periodic (propagating) in the direction parallel to the line defect, and
decaying in the transverse direction [62, 111, 40, 47]. The energies of such edge states lie
along curves in the bulk band gap, which are parameterized by a (scalar) parallel quasi-
momentum. An algebraic count of the number of gap-traversing curves (spectral flow) is
equal to the difference of bulk Chern numbers, integer-valued topological indices determined
by the asymptotic bulk band structures. In cases where complex conjugation symmetry is
broken, this difference may be non-zero, ensuring the existence of gap traversing edge state
curves. Spectral flow is also related to a notion of quantum conductivity. The corresponding
edge states are said to be topologically protected since a relatively compact perturbation of
the Hamiltonian (even large) does not change topological indices which depend only on the
bulk. This is a manifestation of the bulk-edge correspondence principle; see for example,
[86, 51, 52, 85, 78, 49, 48] for discrete models, and in [11, 47, 76, 41, 42, 136, 128, 12, 13] for
continuous Hamiltonians.

In this paper, we initiate the study of edge states in cases where the line defect lies in a
direction which is not a period lattice vector. In this case, the line defect is said to be non-
commensurate or “irrational”. The main difficulty is a complete lack of translation invariance
of the two-dimensional medium, which prevents the use of the Floquet-Bloch theory.
We propose a framework for the rigorous definition of edge states in systems which are non-
commensurate. Our approach relies on the observation that the Hamiltonian is quasiperiodic
along the edge (see Remark 3.2). More precisely, it is realized as the restriction, to a two-
dimensional plane, of a three-dimensional Hamiltonian, governing a material which inter-
polates between three-dimensional bulk periodic structures across a planar interface, within
which there is translation invariance. This observation has been used in the homogenization
theory of periodic elliptic boundary value problems in presence of a sharp termination [72,
73] or an interface [20]. It has also been exploited in [3] to numerically solve the Helmholtz
equation in a similar setting.
Using this observation, in this paper:
(a). We introduce a general notion of edge states for the original two-dimensional medium
by defining them as restrictions of three-dimensional (augmented) edge states. These
augmented edge states are nontrivial solutions of a spectral problem in R3 which
are pseudo-periodic along the directions of a two-dimensional hyperplane (although
determined by a single parallel quasi-momentum k∥∈[−π, π]) and decaying in the
direction transverse to this hyperplane; see further discussion in Section 1.3.1. In
this lifted or augmented setting, the underlying Hamiltonian is degenerate elliptic
on a three-dimensional cylinder. This approach recovers the known pseudo-periodic
edge states for commensurate media, but allows for edge states with more complex
(quasiperiodic) behavior along non-commensurate edges.
(b). In our augmented Hamiltonian, the length scale of the transition between asymptotic
bulk media across the line defect is large compared with the scale of periodicity. Via
multiple scale analysis, we exploit this scale separation, to construct approximate
edge states which are now quasiperiodic along the edge, and decaying transverse to
the edge. In the commensurate case, for a fixed k∥, edge states are seeded by one or

Our main result,
Theorem 7.1, is an asymptotic expansion for the centered and scaled resolvent of the
three-dimensional elliptically degenerate operator for energies near the Dirac energy;
see Section 1.3.3. For non-commensurate edges, this asymptotic expansion involves,
for each fixed parallel quasi-momentum k∥∈[−π, π], a countable set of effective
Dirac operators. In our proof, we extend the approach developed in [40, 44, 47].
The resolvent expansion (see (1.2) and (7.13)) shows that a “blowup” to order one of
a O(δ)–neighborhood of the bulk energy gap is characterized by resolvents of these
effective Dirac operators.
A crucial assumption for our analysis is an omnidirectional no-fold condition on the un-
perturbed honeycomb operator H0; we require that the dispersion surfaces associated with
the unperturbed honeycomb model attain the Dirac energy only at the vertices of the Bril-
louin zone. Previous works for commensurate edges [62, 111, 40, 44] only required a weaker
condition, which depends on the choice of commensurate edge. This more restrictive omni-
directional no-fold condition holds for instance for honeycomb Schrödinger operators in the
strong binding regime [61]. If H0 satisfies the omnidirectional no-fold condition, then our
edge Hamiltonian interpolates between two bulk Hamiltonians which are spectrally gapped
about the Dirac energy, ED.
The resolvent expansion of Theorem 7.1, for non-commensurate systems, plays a key role in
forthcoming work [4], where we complete the program discussed above to rigorously construct
genuine quasiperiodic edge states for irrational edges. Assuming a Diophantine condition
on the irrational parameter which sets the orientation of the edge, we show that near the
Dirac energy, the spectrum associated with the augmented three-dimensional problem in a
cylinder consists of a dense set of eigenvalues. Moreover, the associated eigenfunctions can be
restricted to the original two-dimensional hyperplane, hence giving rise to two-dimensional
edge states with quasiperiodic behavior along the edge.

tals are modeled as restrictions of higher dimensional (periodic) crystals.

In contrast, little seems to be known for irrational edges. Since Hδ
dw is not translation-
invariant along the edge, the notion of the plane wave-like behavior of edge states requires
a careful formulation. The results [88, 89, 77], for models of periodic media with sharp
terminations or junctions between two periodic media, suggest that all gaps in the L2(R2)–

in [22, 46] for a discrete variant of Hδ

tinuous spectrum, although there could be other spectral types. An important unanswered
question is the following:
Question. What are the detailed properties of the states associated with the spectrum that
fills the bulk spectral gaps, in particular their character along non-commensurate edges and
their decay behavior transverse to the edge?
In this paper and its follow-up [4], we address this question.

1.4. Open questions and future directions: From the eigenpairs of Dδ to genuine
augmented edge states. The spectrum of the block-diagonal effective Hamiltonian, Dδ,
decomposes into an absolutely continuous part R\(−θgap, θgap) and a pure point part. When
r is irrational, the point spectrum of Dδ is dense, with some of its eigenvalues that densely
fill the interval (−θgap, θgap), while its other eigenvalues are embedded in the absolutely
continuous spectrum; see Figure 7. Two questions arise from the present article:
• Do the eigenvalues of Dδ in (−θgap, θgap) seed genuine eigenvalues of Hδ
aug,K·v1?
• How do the eigenvalues of Dδ embedded in its absolutely continuous spectrum man-
ifest in the spectral properties of Hδ
aug,K·v1?

What makes these questions challenging is that the eigenvalues of Dδ, embedded or not, are
not “well-separated”1 from one another, and therefore general arguments (based on quasi-
modes) for gapped self-adjoint operators [40, 47, 44] do not apply. In fact, regarding the
first question, in perturbation theory of general linear operators, it is well-known that dense
point spectrum is unstable under general perturbations; see [39, 27, 140, 92]. Proving that
the dense eigenvalues of Dδ seed eigenvalues of Hδ
aug,K·v1 is therefore a delicate task.
Concerning the first question, we shall provide an affirmative answer in [4]. Specifically, we
prove that if the irrational edge parameter, r, satisfies a generic Diophantine condition, then
the operator Hδ
aug,K·v1 admits a dense set of eigenvalues near the Dirac energy ED. Further,
the associated eigenfunctions are continuous in s, and their restrictions to s = 0 define
genuine two-dimensional edge states. This result indicates that the energies populating the
bulk L2(R2)–gap of the two-dimensional edge operator Hδ
dw correspond to states which are
quasiperiodic along the edge, and decaying in the direction transverse to the edge.
As for the second question, while “well-separated” eigenvalues embedded in continuous
spectrum are known under certain conditions to perturb to scattering resonances [68, 91, 74,
143, 33] (see also [132, Section XII.6] and [90, Chapter 16]), it is not clear how an embedded
dense set of eigenvalues of Dδ behaves under perturbation.
