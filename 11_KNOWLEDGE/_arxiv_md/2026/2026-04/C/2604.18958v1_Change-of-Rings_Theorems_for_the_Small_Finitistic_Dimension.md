CHANGE-OF-RINGS THEOREMS FOR THE SMALL FINITISTIC
DIMENSION
TAO XIONG, YOUNES EL HADDAOUI, HWANKOO KIM†, AND QIANG ZHOU
Abstract. In this paper, we study the small finitistic dimension of a com-
mutative ring from the viewpoint of finitistic flat homological algebra.
Us-
ing the class ℱ𝒫ℛ(𝑅) of modules admitting finite projective resolutions, we
investigate the finitistic flat (FT-flat) dimension and establish several of its
basic properties. We prove change-of-rings results for the FT-flat dimension,
including quotient and polynomial extension results, as well as localization in-
equalities. As applications, we obtain characterizations of the small finitistic
dimension in terms of FT-flat dimension, derive quotient and polynomial ex-
tension theorems for the small finitistic dimension, and establish local upper
bounds in terms of the small finitistic dimensions of localizations.
1. Introduction
Throughout this paper, 𝑅denotes a commutative ring, unless otherwise specified.
For an 𝑅-module 𝑀, we write pd𝑅𝑀and fd𝑅𝑀for the projective dimension and
the flat dimension of 𝑀, respectively. We denote by 𝑇(𝑀) the torsion submodule
of 𝑀, namely
𝑇(𝑀) = { 𝑥∈𝑀| there exists a nonzerodivisor 𝑠∈𝑅such that 𝑠𝑥= 0 }.
We also write Max(𝑅) (resp. Spec(𝑅)) for the set of maximal (resp. prime) ideals
of 𝑅.
Let 𝑀be an 𝑅-module. We say that 𝑀admits a finite projective resolution,
and write 𝑀∈ℱ𝒫ℛ(𝑅), if there exists an exact sequence
0 −→𝑃𝑛−→𝑃𝑛−1 −→· · · −→𝑃1 −→𝑃0 −→𝑀−→0,
where each 𝑃𝑖is a finitely generated projective 𝑅-module. Thus, ℱ𝒫ℛ(𝑅) denotes
the class of all 𝑅-modules admitting finite projective resolutions by finitely gener-
ated projective modules. In particular, if 𝑀∈ℱ𝒫ℛ(𝑅), then pd𝑅𝑀= fd𝑅𝑀<
∞.
The invariant
fPD(𝑅) = sup{ pd𝑅𝑀| 𝑀is a finitely generated 𝑅-module and pd𝑅𝑀< ∞}
was introduced by Bass in [1] and is called the small finitistic dimension of 𝑅.
Since over an arbitrary ring the syzygies of finitely generated modules need not be
finitely generated, this definition is often inconvenient in practice. To remedy this,
Glaz redefined the small finitistic dimension in [2] by setting
fPD(𝑅) = sup{ pd𝑅𝑀| 𝑀∈ℱ𝒫ℛ(𝑅) }.

invariant fPD(𝑅) may also be interpreted in terms of finitistic flat dimension.
In classical homological algebra, both the global dimension and the weak global
dimension satisfy well-known change-of-rings theorems. For instance, for any com-
mutative ring 𝑅, one has
𝑤.𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅) = sup{ 𝑤.𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅m) | m ∈Max(𝑅) }.
Moreover, if 𝑅is Noetherian, then
𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅) = sup{ 𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅m) | m ∈Max(𝑅) },
which is the classical localization theorem. The quotient theorem asserts that if 𝑅=
𝑅/𝑎𝑅, where 𝑎∈𝑅is neither a zerodivisor nor a unit, then whenever 𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅) <
∞and 𝑤.𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅) < ∞, one has
𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅) ≥𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅) + 1
and
𝑤.𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅) ≥𝑤.𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅) + 1.
Likewise, the polynomial ring theorem states that for any ring 𝑅and indeterminates
𝑋1, . . . , 𝑋𝑛over 𝑅,
𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅[𝑋1, . . . , 𝑋𝑛]) = 𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅)+𝑛
and
𝑤.𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅[𝑋1, . . . , 𝑋𝑛]) = 𝑤.𝑔𝑙.dim(𝑅)+𝑛.
Bass also introduced in [1] the finitistic injective dimension
FID(𝑅) = sup{ id𝑅𝑀| 𝑀is an 𝑅-module and id𝑅𝑀< ∞}
and the finitistic flat dimension
FFD(𝑅) = sup{ fd𝑅𝑀| 𝑀is an 𝑅-module and fd𝑅𝑀< ∞}.
Change-of-rings theorems for these two homological dimensions were established in
[6, 7]. In contrast, as far as we know, no analogous change-of-rings theorem has yet
been established for the small finitistic dimension. Motivated by this, the aim of
this paper is twofold. First, we study structural properties of the class ℱ𝒫ℛ(𝑅).
Second, using these properties, we establish change-of-rings theorems for the small
finitistic dimension of commutative rings.
