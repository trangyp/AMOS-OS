The Onsager principle provides a variational route to the phenomenological equations of dissipa-
tive dynamics through the minimization of the Rayleighian. We develop a covariant formulation of
the Onsager principle for active systems, ensuring geometric consistency under coordinate transfor-
mations. To further incorporate thermal fluctuations, we formulate the Onsager-Machlup principle
for active systems by considering the Onsager-Machlup functional and the corresponding path prob-
ability for stochastic trajectories. Requiring that the path probability obeys the detailed fluctuation
theorem, we show that the extended Onsager-Machlup theory is consistent with stochastic ther-
modynamics. Moreover, we incorporate inertia into the variational framework and show that the
proper covariant equations follow when the covariant acceleration is held fixed during the variation.
I.
INTRODUCTION
In his seminal 1931 work, Onsager formulated the
theory of irreversible thermodynamics, based on phe-
nomenological relations in which fluxes are proportional
to thermodynamic forces [1, 2]. In this framework, he
showed that the phenomenological transport coefficient
matrix should be symmetric, a property known as On-
sager’s reciprocal relations [3]. Onsager’s theory was later
given a microscopic foundation by Kubo’s linear response
theory [4, 5], in which transport coefficients and sus-
ceptibilities are expressed in terms of equilibrium time-
correlation functions.
Onsager also formulated a variational principle from
which the phenomenological equations can be derived in
terms of energy dissipation and entropy production [1, 2].
By introducing a quantity called the Rayleighian, Doi
further developed this variational approach and referred
to it as the Onsager principle (OP) [3]. For isothermal
systems, the Rayleighian typically consists of a dissi-
pation function and the rate of change of the free en-
ergy.
The OP has been applied to various soft mat-
ter systems, such as colloidal suspensions [3, 6], poly-
mers [7, 8], binary mixtures [9], liquid crystals [6], bilayer
membranes and vesicles [10–12], and spreading of liquid
droplets [13, 14].
More recently, the OP has been ex-
tended to active systems [15–17], in which energy is con-
sumed for self-propulsion or other non-equilibrium dy-
namics.

In 1953, Onsager and Machlup generalized the OP to
incorporate thermal fluctuations in a path-integral for-
mulation [18, 19]. In what is now called the Onsager-
Machlup principle (OMP) [20], the Onsager-Machlup
functional (OMF) is minimized to determine the most
probable trajectory. The OMP has been used to study
long-time behavior in soft matter [20], non-equilibrium
statistical physics [21, 22], structural transitions in pro-
tein folding [23], chemical kinetic models [24], and tran-
sitions between laminar and turbulent flow [25]. Some of
the present authors have used the OMP to study Brow-
nian motion in a fluctuating fluid [26], non-reciprocal
stochastic systems [27, 28], active Brownian particles [29,
30], and informational active matter with observation
and feedback mechanisms [31].
Despite the remarkable success of the OP and OMP,
several limitations have been recognized. For example,
the close correspondence of these principles with analyti-
cal mechanics has remained unsatisfactory; in particular,
the covariance structure characteristic of the Lagrangian
formulation was not made clear. This is exemplified by
recent questioning of the invariance of the (restricted)
free energy under a change of variables [32, 33]. Further-
more, because the original OP and OMP were formu-
lated for purely dissipative systems, they do not provide
a variational formulation for the dynamics with inertia.
Although the time derivative of the kinetic energy has
conventionally been incorporated into the Rayleighian for
inertial systems [31, 34, 35], the covariance of the result-
ing phenomenological equations has yet to be rigorously
examined.
In order to address the above-mentioned open issues,
we first develop a covariant formulation of OP for ac-
tive systems, ensuring geometric consistency under co-

A.
Onsager principle with active forces
According to the second law of thermodynamics, meso-
state variables q evolve in such a way that the average en-
tropy production σ is non-negative. It is known that such
irreversible dynamics can be formulated by the OP [1–
3]. In the OP for active systems, we consider a scalar
Rayleighian defined as [16, 30]

VI.
SUMMARY AND DISCUSSION
In this paper, we have discussed the covariance of the
generalized Onsager principle (OP) and the Onsager-
Machlup principle (OMP), including both activity and
inertia. Dissipative dynamics are described by the OP,
where minimization of the Rayleighian gives the phe-
nomenological equation as in Eq. (10). Thermal fluctu-
ations are further incorporated through the path proba-
bility defined by the Onsager-Machlup functional (OMF)
in Eq. (16). By requiring that the path probability obeys
the detailed fluctuation theorem, we have confirmed that
the extended Onsager-Machlup theory is consistent with
stochastic thermodynamics.
For the phenomenological
equations with inertia, we also showed that the covari-
ant acceleration must be held fixed when minimizing the
Rayleighian, as shown in Eq. (28). The resulting phe-
nomenological equation is covariant under point trans-
formations.
Variational principles, including the OP and OMP,
should be covariant in the sense that their mathemat-
ical structure remains unchanged under point transfor-
mations.
This is a major advantage over approaches
that directly write down the dynamical equations. Pre-
viously, Uneyama pointed out that the free energy in the
Rayleighian is generally not a scalar under coordinate
transformations [32].
However, this issue was resolved
by Nakamura [33], who reconsidered the definition of the
canonical ensemble in terms of the scalar probability den-
sity ρ(q). Historically, this subtle issue was also noted by
Graham and others [44–46].
All covariance statements in this paper refer to smooth
one-to-one point transformations Q = Q(q) on a fixed
reduced-state manifold. Under such a reparametrization,
only the coordinate description changes, whereas the
reduced probability measure itself remains unchanged:
dP = ρ(q)
p
g(q)dq = ρ(Q)
p
G(Q)dQ.
Accordingly,
the scalar density ρ, and therefore the free energy F de-
fined from scalar local thermodynamic quantities, are in-
variant. In contrast, the coordinate-dependent probabil-
ity density P(q) = ρ(q)
p
g(q) is not invariant.
It is useful to distinguish this situation from other vari-
able changes that also arise in Onsager-type variational
formulations.
As discussed in Sec. II.B of Ref. [20], a
parametrization of the form q = q(α) defines collective
coordinates on a chosen trial manifold, where α denotes
the parameters. It should therefore be understood as an
approximation scheme, rather than as a coordinate trans-
formation of the entire reduced-state space.
Likewise,
introducing auxiliary velocities or fluxes in an extended
space subject to kinematic constraints, as in Sec. II.E
of Ref. [20], merely changes the representation of the
dissipation function and does not modify the underly-
ing reduced-state manifold itself.
By contrast, a non-
bijective mapping between reduced-state variables repre-
sents a genuine change in the coarse graining [51]. In that
case, because hidden variables have been integrated out,
the reduced entropy and the reduced free energy land-
scape are generally not connected simply by a Jacobian
factor.
In this sense, the present formulation complements Un-
eyama’s covariant formulation based on dissipation [32],
Nakamura’s invariant free energy landscape [33], and


## Page 7


7
Doi’s approaches based on trial coordinates and con-
strained fluxes [20]. Its main advantage lies in clearly dis-
tinguishing between coordinate covariance, constrained
representations, and genuine changes in the level of
coarse graining.
Since the Rayleighian framework is based on an op-
timization principle, various constraints can be incorpo-
rated naturally through Lagrange multipliers. For exam-
ple, in describing protein diffusion on a biomembrane,
where the protein is confined to a curved surface, the
noise term in the Langevin equation must be projected
onto the surface [52, 53]. Within the Rayleighian frame-
work, such a constraint can be imposed straightforwardly
by introducing a Lagrange multiplier, which will be ex-
plored in future work.
The present covariant variational formulation suggests
several directions for future work.
An important next
step is to extend the framework to field variables and
many-body systems, where geometric constraints, collec-
tive modes, and nontrivial dissipation may play essential
roles [54]. It would also be interesting to combine the
present formulation with information-driven or feedback-
controlled dynamics [31, 55], where activity, inertia, and
information flow may be treated within a unified covari-
ant framework. We expect that such developments will
further broaden the applicability of the OP and OMP
to active soft matter, stochastic thermodynamics, and
other non-equilibrium systems with complex geometrical
structure.
