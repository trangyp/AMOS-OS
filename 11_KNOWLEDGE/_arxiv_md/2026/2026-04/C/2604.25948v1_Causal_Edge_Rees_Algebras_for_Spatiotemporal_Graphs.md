Abstract
Understanding the evolution of connectivity in spatiotemporal systems requires mathematical frameworks capable of
encoding not only instantaneous interactions but also their cumulative causal structure. In this work, we introduce
the Causal Edge Rees Algebra (CERA), a new algebraic construction associated with causal spatiotemporal graphs.
Given a temporal filtration induced by causal constraints, we associate a sequence of edge ideals whose Rees algebra
encodes the full history of connectivity evolution in a single graded object. This construction establishes a bridge
between dynamic graph topology and commutative algebra. In particular, we show that successive quotients of the
filtration capture the emergence of new structural connections, allowing the identification of critical edges responsible
for the fusion of previously disconnected components. This leads to the definition of temporal bridge modules and to
a bridge detection theorem, which relates the dimension of these modules to the reduction in the number of connected
components over time. Unlike existing algebraic approaches in topological data analysis, which are primarily based on
geometric filtrations, the proposed framework is driven by intrinsic causal constraints. As a result, the CERA captures
not only topological features but also their temporal organization and mechanisms of coalescence. The theory provides
a new algebraic perspective on causal network dynamics, connecting edge ideals, Rees algebras, and temporal graphs.
Beyond its theoretical significance, the framework opens new directions for the analysis of spatiotemporal systems,
including epidemic networks, transport systems, and information propagation processes.
Keywords: Causal graphs; Rees algebras; Edge ideals; Commutative algebra; Temporal networks; Algebraic graph
theory; Spatiotemporal systems; Network dynamics

2. Spatiotemporal Causal Graphs and Temporal
Filtrations
In this section, we introduce the fundamental struc-
ture underlying the construction of the Causal Edge
Rees Algebra (CERA): spatiotemporal causal graphs
and the temporal filtration induced by their cumulative
evolution. Temporal networks and dynamic graphs have
been extensively studied in network science and com-
plex systems [1, 2, 3]. Here, we consider a formulation
driven by temporal causality, inspired by the framework
introduced in [6].
2.1. Spatiotemporal causal graphs
We consider graphs modeling systems in which re-
lations between events or entities emerge progressively
over time under causal constraints.
Definition 1 (Spatiotemporal causal graph). A spa-
tiotemporal causal graph is a triple
G = (V, E, τ),
where:
- V is a finite set of vertices;
- E ⊆V × V is a set of directed edges;
- τ : V →R is a temporal function assigning to each
vertex a time of occurrence.
The graph satisfies the causal condition:
(u, v) ∈E =⇒τ(u) < τ(v).
This definition is consistent with directed acyclic
graph (DAG) structures used to model temporal and
causal dependencies [15, 16].
The condition implies
that all edges respect temporal orientation, hence the
graph is acyclic.
Proposition 1. Every spatiotemporal causal graph is a
directed acyclic graph (DAG).
Proof. Assume there exists a directed cycle
v1 →v2 →· · · →vk →v1.
Then
τ(v1) < τ(v2) < · · · < τ(vk) < τ(v1),
a contradiction.
2.2. Spatiotemporal causal constraints
In many applications, particularly in epidemiological
and spatiotemporal propagation systems, the existence
of a causal edge depends on both temporal and spatial
constraints.

3. The Causal Edge Rees Algebra (CERA)
In this section, we introduce the central algebraic
construction of this work: the Causal Edge Rees Alge-
bra (CERA), associated with the temporal filtration of a
spatiotemporal causal graph. This structure encodes the
cumulative evolution of the graph into a graded object,
allowing the study of dynamical connectivity through
algebraic invariants.
The construction is inspired by
both the classical theory of Rees algebras [10, 11, 12]
and the theory of edge ideals associated with graphs
[7, 8, 13, 9].
3.1. Temporal edge ideals
Let
G = (V, E, τ)

E1 ⊆E2 ⊆· · · ⊆Ek.
Consider the commutative polynomial ring

Definition 4 (Edge ideal at time n). For each temporal

Since the filtration is increasing, we have
I1 ⊆I2 ⊆· · · ⊆Ik.
We extend this filtration to all n ≥0 by setting
I0 = (0),
In := Ik
for all n ≥k.
Thus, {In}n≥0 is an increasing filtration of monomial
ideals which stabilizes at level k.
Proposition 2. The sequence {In}n≥0 forms an increas-
ing filtration of monomial ideals.
Proof. For n < k, the inclusion follows from En ⊆En+1.
For n ≥k, we have In = Ik, so the sequence stabilizes.
Hence
In ⊆In+1
for all n.

9. Discussion and Conclusions
In this work, we introduced the Causal Edge Rees
Algebra (CERA), a graded algebraic structure associ-
ated with the temporal evolution of causal graphs. The
construction assigns to a filtered causal graph a graded
algebra that encodes the cumulative evolution of con-
nectivity within a single algebraic object.
The central idea of the theory is to reinterpret tem-
poral causality within the framework of Rees algebras,
which are classically used in commutative algebra and
algebraic geometry to study blow-ups, asymptotic be-
havior of ideals, and graded structures [10, 11, 12]. By
transporting these tools to the setting of causal graphs,
we obtain an algebraic formalism capable of encoding
dynamic phenomena such as the emergence of edges
and the merging of connected components.
A key structural result is the bridge detection theo-
rem, which establishes a correspondence between the
reduction in the number of connected components and
the dimension of the temporal bridge module Bn. This
provides an explicit link between a topological invariant
and an algebraic structure arising from the filtration.
9.1. Mathematical significance
The construction of CERA naturally connects three
levels of description:
1. Combinatorial: the filtered structure of the under-
lying graph;
2. Algebraic: the associated filtration of edge ideals;
3. Geometric: the projective scheme
Proj(CERA(G)).
This suggests that CERA may serve as a bridge be-
tween combinatorial commutative algebra and geomet-
ric methods associated with graded algebras [18].
9.2. Limitations
The theory developed here is preliminary, and several
structural questions remain open:
• characterization of normality;
• Cohen–Macaulay properties;
• description of singularities of Proj(CERA(G));
• asymptotic behavior of the ideals In;
• local structure via localization.

## Page 15


These questions are natural in the broader context of
Rees algebras and monomial ideals [10, 21, 11].
We also note that the present formulation is entirely
commutative. A noncommutative version, better suited
to directed causal structures, remains to be developed.
9.3. Future directions
Several directions for further investigation arise natu-
rally:
• noncommutative extensions of CERA;
• local and homological properties of CERA(G);
• extensions to hypergraphs;
• connections with persistent homology;
• interpretation in terms of quiver representations.
These directions connect the theory to areas such as
representation theory, combinatorial topology, and as-
sociative algebras [19, 20, 17].
From an applied perspective, the framework may be
relevant for the analysis of temporal networks, including
epidemic spreading, mobility systems, and information
propagation [1, 2, 3].
9.4. Concluding remarks
The results presented here indicate that temporal
structures in graphs can be studied within an algebraic
framework based on filtered Rees algebras.
In this
sense, CERA extends the classical theory of edge ideals
to a temporal setting, providing a mechanism to encode
both connectivity and its evolution.
The present work should be viewed as a first step to-
ward a broader theory of algebraic structures associated
with causal networks.
