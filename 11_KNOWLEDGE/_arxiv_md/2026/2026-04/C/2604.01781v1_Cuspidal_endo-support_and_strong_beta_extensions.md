CUSPIDAL ENDO-SUPPORT AND STRONG BETA EXTENSIONS
DAVID HELM, ROBERT KURINCZUK, DANIEL SKODLERACK, AND SHAUN STEVENS
Abstract. Let G be an inner form of a general linear group or classical group over a non-archimedean
local field of residual characteristic p, assumed odd in the classical case. We prove that every smooth
representation of G over an algebraically closed field R of characteristic ℓ‰ p contains a maximal
semisimple character, i.e., one for which the point in the building of the corresponding centralizer
is a vertex. Further, for every endo-parameter adapted to G, we define its support, which leads
also to the notion of cuspidal endo-support of an irreducible representation, and we relate this to its
cuspidal support. We also introduce beta extensions for strong facets in the building of a centralizer,
and show these are sufficient for the construction of types. These results are used in a subsequent
paper to decompose the category of smooth R-representations of G.

1. Introduction
Over the last thirty years, for inner forms of general linear and classical groups over a non-
archimedean local field, the theory of semisimple characters and the more conceptual theory of endo-
classes and endo-parameters has been developed: starting with simple characters of general linear
groups in [11] by Bushnell–Kutzko, extended to lattice sequences in [8] by Bushnell–Henniart, where
the authors introduce endo-classes of simple characters; semisimple characters for classical groups
with p odd were introduced in [34] by Stevens with their endo-classes and endo-parameters considered
by Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens in [17]; for inner forms of classical groups this theory developed
with p odd in [28] by Skodlerack; and for inner forms of general linear groups by Sécherre in [23],
Broussous–Sécherre–Stevens [5], and Skodlerack in [29]. Semisimple characters for the exceptional
group G2 over a non-archimedean local field of residual characteristic greater than 3 have also been
introduced by Blasco–Blondel [3].
Semisimple characters have an inductive arithmetic construction which makes it possible to compute
intertwining between them, a property crucial for their use in the construction of types for cuspidal
representations [11, 22, 35, 30] and Bernstein blocks [12, 25, 20, 32] in the cases of forms of general linear

1.1. m-realizations and exhaustion in endo-factors. We use semisimple characters to analyse
the category RepRpGq of smooth R-representations of G.
Given an endo-parameter t for G, i.e.,
an intertwining class of a semisimple R-valued character defined on a compact open subgroup of G,
we introduce the full subcategory RepRptq of RepRpGq consisting of all representations which are
generated by semisimple characters of class t, called endo-factor for t in RepRpGq.
We call such
representations of class t.
Note that RepRptq is not obviously a factor of RepRpGq (and this is not needed in this paper), but
we will see in an accompanying paper [15] in a more general setting that RepRptq is indeed a factor
of RepRpGq.
The main theorem of the current paper states that we can find a nice collection of semisimple
characters which exhausts RepRpGq; this is important for constructing finitely generated projective
generators of RepRptq. These characters are chosen more sophisticatedly: A semisimple character θ
is parametrized by a pair pΛ, βq consisting of a semisimple element β of the Lie algebra of G and a
lattice sequence Λ which corresponds to a point in the Bruhat-Tits building BpGq of G which lies
in the image of a canonical embedding jβ : BpGβq ãÑ BpGq of the building of the centralizer Gβ.
We show we can choose the characters θ such that its point in BpGβq defines a maximal parahoric
subgroup in Gβ. Such semisimple characters are called m-realizations of t.
Theorem 1.1 (see Theorem 5.7). Let t be an endo-parameter for G. Then, every representation
in RepRptq is generated by the sum of the isotypic components of all m-realizations of t.
For classical and general linear groups, this refines a Theorem of Dat [13], which says the category
is generated by the sum of the isotypic components of all semisimple characters of endo-parameter t.
The main advantage of this improvement to m-realizations, is that m-realizations satisfy an important
finiteness property. In general, there are infinitely many G-conjugacy classes of m-realizations of t,
but if we take for each m-realization an Iwahori decomposition into account then we can obtain a
coarser equivalence relation to obtain a finite number of equivalence classes: The idempotents of Frβs
define a Levi subgroup Mpβ, Gq of G. We call two m-realizations θ, θ1 of t essentially G-conjugate if
their restrictions θ|Mpβ,Gq and θ1|Mpβ1,Gq are conjugate in G. We obtain the following Theorem:
Theorem 1.2 (see Theorem 4.10). Let t be an endo-parameter for G. There are only finitely many
essential G-conjugacy classes of m-realizations of t.

1.4. Structure of the paper. We now explain the structure of the paper and we give remarks
on the proof of Theorem 1.1. In Section 2 we mainly introduce the description of G and its para-
horic subgroups. In Section 3 we recall the notion of semisimple characters and we recall the notion
of endo-parameter, i.e., the invariant for the intertwining class of a semisimple character. In Sec-
tion 4 we introduce m-realizations of an endo-parameter t and we introduce an equivalence relation
called essential-G-conjugacy on the set of m-realizations of t and we prove that the number of essential-
G-conjugacy classes of m-realizations of t is finite, see Theorem 4.10. This uses the numerical rigidity
of endo-parameters, related to the classification of pearl embeddings by Broussous–Grabitz [7]. In
Section 5 we define endo-factors and we prove the finiteness theorem (Theorem 1.1). The main idea
is that, starting with a semisimple character of class t in an irreducible representation π, we go along
a path in BpGβq and use compatibility to show that π contains a transfer of θ to a strong vertex.
After introducing beta extensions for the strong structure and recalling results on types for Bernstein
blocks in Section 1.3, we introduce the concept of cuspidal endo-support in Section 7.
