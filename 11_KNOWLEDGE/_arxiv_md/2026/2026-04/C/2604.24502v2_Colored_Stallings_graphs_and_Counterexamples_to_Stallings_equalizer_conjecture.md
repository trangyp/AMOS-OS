STALLINGS EQUALIZER CONJECTURE
JIALIN LEI AND TENG ZHANG
Dedicated to the memory of Professor J. R. Stallings
Abstract. The famous Stallings equalizer conjecture has remained open for more than 40
years, which asserts that for any free group Fn of rank n ≥2, any free group F, and any two
monomorphisms g, h : Fn →F, the equalizer Eq(g, h) = {w ∈Fn | g(w) = h(w)} satisfies
rk Eq(g, h) ≤n. The only known case is n = 2, due to A. D. Logan in 2022. By introducing
the notion of colored Stallings graphs, we show that for every integer n ≥2 there exist
monomorphisms g, h : Fn −→F2 such that rk Eq(g, h) ≥2n −2. This disproves the Stallings
equalizer conjecture for n ≥3.

Conjecture 1.1 (Stallings). Let n ≥2. Let Fn be a free group of rank n, and let F be a free
group. Let g, h : Fn →F be monomorphisms. Then
rk Eq(g, h) ≤n.
Goldstein and Turner [7] proved that under the hypotheses of Conjecture 1.1, Eq(g, h) is
finitely generated, that is, Eq(g, h) has finite rank. Later, they [8] proved that under the weaker
assumption that at least one of g and h is injective, Eq(g, h) has finite rank. Conjecture 1.1 is
also considered under such weaker assumption, see [1, Problem F31], [6, Problem 6] and [16,
Conjecture 8.3].
In the special case where Σ = ∆and h = id, the equalizer is precisely the fixed subgroup.
Indeed, for any endomorphism ϕ : F(Σ) →F(Σ),
Fix(ϕ) = {w ∈F(Σ) | ϕ(w) = w} = Eq(ϕ, id).
Thus the fixed subgroup problem is the one-map special case of the equalizer problem. By
using train-track maps, Bestvina and Handel [3] proved the well-known Scott conjecture for
automorphisms, that is, Conjecture 1.1 holds in the special case where Fn = F, g ∈Aut(Fn)
and h = id. Later, Imrich and Turner [9] extended Bestvina–Handel’s results to all endo-
morphisms. Dicks and Ventura [6] proved stronger inertness results for families of injective
endomorphisms, and Bergman [2] extended their results to families of endomorphisms. For
related recent developments, see [10, 12].
For general equalizers, the situation is harder. Ventura [16] noted that the injectivity hypoth-
esis in Conjecture 1.1 is essential: without it, equalizers may have infinite rank. Ciobanu and
Logan [5] proved algorithmic and rank results for immersions, relating the problem to the Post
Correspondence Problem. Logan [13] proved the case n = 2 in Conjecture 1.1 and obtained
further positive results under inertness or retract hypotheses on the images. Very recently,
Carvalho and Silva [4] investigated the fixed points and equalizers of injective endomorphisms
of the free group at infinity.
The purpose of this paper is to give a family of explicit counterexamples to Conjecture 1.1
for n ≥3. Our main result is the following.
Theorem 1.2. Let n ≥2 and Fn be a free group of rank n. Then there exist monomorphisms
g, h : Fn −→F2 such that
rk Eq(g, h) ≥2n −2.
Consequently, Conjecture 1.1 is false for every n ≥3.
Remark 1.3. In fact, we construct a subgroup family An of rank 2n −2 such that
An ≤ff Eq(g, h),
where ≤ff denotes a free factor inclusion. In particular,
rk Eq(g, h) ≥2n −2.
Moreover, the same family shows that the analogue of Conjecture 1.1 is false if one replaces
the assumption that both g and h are injective by the assumption that g is injective and h is
not, for every n ≥5; see Proposition 5.1.

2. Colored Stallings graphs and equalizers
Folded labeled graphs were introduced by Stallings [14] in the study of subgroups of free
groups. We first recall the standard combinatorial language of X-labeled graphs and Stallings
graphs; see Kapovich–Myasnikov [11, Section 2] for background. Readers already familiar with
this material may skip to Subsection 2.3.
2.1. X-labeled and folded graphs.

2.2. The Stallings graph of a subgroup. Let RX be the rose with one vertex and one
positively oriented edge labeled by x for each x ∈X. We identify
π1(RX) ∼= F(X).
Let H ≤F(X). The Schreier covering graph of H, denoted ΣX(H), has vertices the right
cosets
Hg,
g ∈F(X),
base vertex H, and, for every x ∈X, an oriented edge
Hg x−→Hgx.
The reverse edge has label x−1. Thus every reduced word w ∈F(X) determines a unique
reduced path in ΣX(H) starting at the base vertex H, and this path ends at the vertex Hw.
In particular, this path is closed at the base vertex if and only if w ∈H.
The based core subgraph of ΣX(H) is the union of all reduced closed paths in ΣX(H) based
at the vertex H. Equivalently, it is the subgraph consisting of all vertices and edges which
occur along the path labeled by the reduced representative of some element of H. We denote
this based subgraph by ΓX(H). If H = 1, then ΓX(H) is the one-vertex graph consisting only
of the base vertex.
Definition 2.3 (The Stallings graph of a subgroup). The above based graph ΓX(H) is called
the Stallings graph of H over X. When the underlying set and subgroup are clear, we simply
call it Stallings graph. By the former construction, ΓX(H) is connected and folded, and it
represents exactly H:
LX(ΓX(H), ∗H) = H,
where we denote the base vertex H by ∗H. Moreover, ΓX(H) is finite if and only if H is finitely
generated.
Now, we illustrate the correspondence between subgroups and graphs.
Remark 2.4. As shown above, every subgroup of F(X) has an associated based X-labeled
graph. Conversely, let (Γ, v0) be a connected folded based X-labeled graph. Suppose that
every topological edge of Γ lies in some reduced closed path based at v0. Then
(Γ, v0) ∼= ΓX(LX(Γ, v0))
as based X-labeled graphs. Thus subgroups H ≤F(X) are represented, up to based label-
preserving isomorphism, by connected folded based X-labeled graphs in which every edge lies
in a reduced closed path based at the base vertex.

2.3. Colored X-graphs and colored Stallings graphs.
Definition 2.7 (Colored X-graph). Let G be a free group, and let
g, h : F(X) →G

together with a map
C : V Γ →G,
called the color map, such that
C(v0) = 1

5. Stabilized non-injective applications
The same construction of Sections 3 and 4 also yields consequences when one allows non-
injective maps. The reason is that one may add dummy generators without changing the large
subgroup already lying in the equalizer.

Remark 5.3. Once both maps are allowed to be non-injective, the equalizer may even fail to

α(y) = 1,

Equivalently,

The Stallings graph of ker ϕ is the covering of the two-petal rose corresponding to this

there are two distinct unoriented edges: the x-edge from k to k + 1 and the y-edge from k + 1
to k. This infinite folded Stallings graph has one independent cycle between every consecutive
pair of vertices. Therefore its first Betti number is infinite. Hence Eq(α, β) is a free group of
countably infinite rank and is not finitely generated.
6. A possible sharp bound
The family above suggests the following candidate for the correct sharp upper bound in the
monomorphism case.
Conjecture 6.1. Let n ≥3, and let g, h : Fn →F be monomorphisms into a free group. Then
rk Eq(g, h) ≤2n −2.
Theorem 1.2 shows that, if this conjecture is true, then it is sharp.
