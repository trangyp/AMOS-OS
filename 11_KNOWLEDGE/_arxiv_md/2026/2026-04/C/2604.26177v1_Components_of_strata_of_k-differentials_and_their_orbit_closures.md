COMPONENTS OF STRATA OF k-DIFFERENTIALS AND THEIR
ORBIT CLOSURES
PAUL APISA AND JULIET AYGUN
Abstract. We obtain a complete classification of components of strata of holo-
morphic and meromorphic k-differentials. We show that, when genus is at least
two and outside of explicit exceptions when k ≤3, there is one primitive nonhyper-
elliptic component unless k is odd and all singularities have even order, in which
case there are two distinguished by their Arf invariant. The exceptions include new
sporadic components of strata of cubic differentials. Our work provides a new proof
of earlier results of Kontsevich-Zorich, Boissy, Lanneau, and Chen-Gendron when
k = 1, 2. The proofs are almost purely algebraic, relying on the multiscale compact-
ification of Bainbridge-Chen-Gendron-Grushevsky-M¨oller. This answers a question
of Chen-Yu.
We also show that for any component of a stratum of finite area k-differentials
of positive genus, the smallest GL(2, R)-orbit closure containing all of its holonomy
covers is as big as possible. This answers the positive genus analogue of a “long
term goal” of Mirzakhani-Wright. The result also holds in genus zero strata that
contain surfaces with Euclidean cylinders, thus addressing infinitely many cases of
the original question of Mirzakhani-Wright.

Perhaps surprisingly, components of strata have remained unclassified when k > 2.
The question of classifying them has been around for many years and was formally
posed by Chen-Yu [CY26, Problem 3.3].
For k = 1, the classification of strata
without poles was achieved by Kontsevich-Zorich [KZ03] and then by Boissy [Boi15]
in general. For k = 2, strata with at most simple poles were classified by Lanneau
[Lan08] and then by Chen-Gendron [CG22] in general.
A component of a stratum is primitive if one and hence every k-differential in it is
not a power of a lower order differential. A component is hyperelliptic if every element
(X, ω) has the property that X is hyperelliptic with the divisor of ω invariant under
the hyperelliptic involution. Hyperelliptic components of strata were classified by
Chen-Gendron [CG22]. In light of these observations, in order to classify components
of strata, it suffices to classify primitive nonhyperelliptic ones.
Given a k-differential (X, ω), let Σg,n be the underlying topological genus g surface
punctured at the n singularities. A k-differential has an associated winding number
w ∈H1(UTΣg,n, Z) where UTΣg,n is the unit tangent bundle. If k is odd and all
singularities of the k-differential are of even order, w mod 2 belongs to H1(UTΣg; Z/2)
and defines a Z/2-valued Arf invariant. See Section 2 for a description of this invariant
and its “relative” version. The Arf invariant was studied by Atiyah [Ati71], Mumford
[Mum71], and Johnson [Joh80].

provides a complete classification of components of strata.

Its proof is almost purely algebraic, relying most heavily on the multiscale
compactification of [BCG+19], which we recall in Section 3. Curiously, the global
k-residue condition makes almost no appearance in the sequel. Our proof provides a
new proof of results of Kontsevich-Zorich, Boissy, Lanneau, and Chen-Gendron when
k = 1, 2. The only geometric, non-algebraic inputs to the proof are: (1) the non-
compactness of strata that do not project to points in Mg,n, (2) horizontal nodes
in the multiscale compactification correspond to Euclidean cylinders, (3) nonseparat-
ing Euclidean cylinders are generically simple, and (4) collapsing a simple Euclidean
cylinder splits a zero or merges two singularities (see Figure 1). Removing this geo-
metric input would yield a purely algebraic proof and hence resolve another question
of Chen-Yu [CY26, Problem 3.1].
1.1. Geometric structures, orbit closures, and counting problems. A flat
cone metric on Σg,m is a set of n points P ⊆Σg,m, called cone points, and a metric
on Σg,m that is smooth and flat on Σg,n+m := Σg,m −P. This determines a (G, X)-
structure on Σg,n+m where X = C and G = S1 ⋉C is the group of rigid motions.
Each such surface admits a holonomy homomorphism from π1(Σg,n+m) to S1. The
cover associated to its kernel is the holonomy cover. It is characterized as the smallest
cover under which the metric pulls back to one induced from a meromorphic 1-form.
A k-differential is equivalently a flat cone metric with holonomy valued in the Z/k
subgroup of S1. It has finite area if all poles have (signed) order strictly greater than
−k. Any finite area flat cone metric can be described as a finite union of Euclidean
triangles in R2 with sides glued together by rigid motions.

The holonomy cover of a finite area k-differential is a finite area abelian differen-
tial. Strata of abelian differentials admit an action of GL(2, R) generated by scalar
multiplication and Teichm¨uller geodesic flow. By work of Eskin-Mirzakhani [EM18]
and Eskin-Mirzakhani-Mohammadi [EMM15], the orbit closure of any point in the
stratum is a submanifold and, by work of Filip [Fil16], a variety. Such orbit closures
are called invariant subvarieties.
Given a component C of a stratum of finite-area k-differentials, let MC be the
smallest invariant subvariety containing every holonomy cover of a k-differential in
the component.
When k is even, the subvariety MC is naturally contained in a
quadratic double, which is an invariant subvariety consisting of holonomy covers of
a component of a stratum of quadratic differentials. Similarly, if k is odd and C is
hyperelliptic, MC is contained in a codimension one hyperelliptic locus. Say that MC
is as big as possible if these containments are equalities or, when k is odd and C is
nonhyperelliptic, if MC coincides with the ambient stratum.
A deep body of work of Eskin-Masur [EM01], Eskin-Masur-Zorich [EMZ03],
Eskin-Kontsevich-Zorich [EKZ14], and others shows that the asymptotics (in L) of
geometric counting functions, like the number of saddle connections between two fixed
points or the number of cylinders of circumference at most L, for almost-every element
of C is governed by MC. Partially motivated by this, Mirzakhani-Wright [MW18]
wrote that their “long term goal is to compute” MC for components C of genus zero
strata. Another motivation is that MC can furnish exotic orbit closures. These in-
clude Teichm¨uller curves [Vee89, War98, Vor96, KS00] and the seven higher rank
examples discovered by Eskin-McMullen-Mukamel-Wright [MMW16, EMMW20].
We show that such unusual behavior is confined to genus zero.
Theorem 1.2. If C is a component of a stratum of surfaces of genus g ≥1, then
MC is as big as possible. The same holds if C = ΩkM0(κ) and κ contains a subset
summing to −k.
Previous known cases of Theorem 1.2 include Aygun [Ayg25], when k is prime
and g > 2, and Apisa [Api21, Theorems 1.9, 1.10] who proved a special case of the
genus zero part of the result. Given a multiset θ of n rational multiples of π summing
to (n −2)π, let P(θ) be the set of n-gons whose vertices have angles given by θ.
Corollary 1.3. If a subset S of θ sums to (|S| −1)π, then almost every element of
P(θ) has an unfolding with an orbit closure as big as possible. This assumption holds
for θ coming from cyclic n-gons when n is even.

3. Background
Fix integers g ≥0, k > 0, and a partition κ of k(2g −2) into n integers. The
main tool in the sequel is the multiscale compactification ΩkMg(κ) of a stratum of
k-differentials. This was introduced in [BCG+19] and further studied in [CMZ24].
The boundary points of this compactification, called multiscale k-differentials, are
nodal Riemann surfaces in Mg,n equipped with k-differentials whose singularities only
occur at the nodes and the n marked points and which must satisfy the following
conditions. First, the sum of the orders of vanishing of the k-differentials on both
sides of a node must be −2k.
The node is called vertical if one side, called the
higher level side, has strictly larger order of vanishing than the other. Second, the
notion of being at a higher level must define a partial order, called a level structure,
on irreducible components. Third, part of the data of a multiscale k-differential is
a combinatorial object associated to each vertical node called a (local equivalence
class of a) prong matching, which we will define in a moment. Finally, a multiscale
k-differential must satisfy the global k-residue condition and the matching k-residue
condition. The matching k-residue condition is vacuous unless there are horizontal

Proof of Corollary 1.4: Counting trajectories on translation surfaces using the Siegel-
Veech constant of its orbit closure is the work of Eskin-Masur [EM01] and Eskin-
Mirzakhani-Mohammadi [EMM15, Theorem 2.12]. The passage from counting on
the holonomy cover to counting on the underlying k-differential appears in various
references, e.g. [Api21, Lemma 8.6]. The holonomy cover of a generic surface in C has
orbit closure MC by [Ayg25, Lemma 3.1]. The large genus asymptotics of Siegel-
Veech constants for connected strata of abelian differentials is Aggarwal [Agg19],
whose estimates involve an additive O(1/h) term where h is the genus of the abelian
differential. The genus h of the holonomy cover of a genus g finite-area primitive
k-differential satisfies h ≥Ck(g + 1) where C is a constant independent of k and g.
In light of Theorem 1.2, the hypotheses placed on C are precisely those needed to
guarantee that MC is a connected stratum of abelian differentials. The asymptotics
in the statement of the corollary are, a priori, “weak asymptotics” in the sense of
Eskin-Masur [EM01], but, using recently announced work of Solan can be upgraded
to usual asymptotics.
□
