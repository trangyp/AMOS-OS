CLASSIFICATION OF IRREDUCIBLE REAL MODULES OF REAL LIE
SUPERALGEBRAS
SIDDHARTHA SAHI, HADI SALMASIAN, AND VERA SERGANOVA
Abstract. We classify irreducible finite-dimensional modules of a collection of real Lie su-
peralgebras that includes the simple ones, their classical variants, complex Lie superalgebras
after restriction of scalars, and all real Lie algebras. Our strategy is to reduce this classifica-
tion to determining the orbits of the parity and conjugation functors on irreducible modules
of the complexifications of the aforementioned algebras. Then we provide explicit results for
the computation of these orbits. For Lie superalgebras of basic type or of type Q(n), our
classification applies to any highest-weight parametrization of irreducible complex modules
with respect to an arbitrary Borel subalgebra.
As a consequence, in the special case of real simple Lie algebras we obtain a new perspec-
tive on the classification of real simple modules and establish a conceptual connection with
Kostant’s cascade of strongly orthogonal roots.
1. Introduction
While the study of finite dimensional modules of complex simple Lie (super)algebras has
been a mainstream research direction in algebra, much less attention has been paid to such
modules over real numbers. Still, the problem of characterizing modules of real simple Lie
algebras has a long history, going back to the works of ´E. Cartan, Iwahori, and Karpelevich [2,
6, 8]. The monograph by Onishchik [10] offers a simplified exposition of the classification
from the viewpoint of [8]. We refer the reader to the introduction of [10] for a meticulous
examination of the history of this problem. For the analogous problem over more general fields
(but in the framework of representations of reductive groups), we refer the reader to [13].
In the super setting, the only work on classification of representations over general fields
that we are aware of is [5]. The qualitative result of Hayashi’s paper (see [5, Thm 1.2]) is in
the setting of representations of affine supergroup schemes, and its more quantitative result
(see [5, Thm 1.7]) is stated for quasi-reductive algebraic supergroups. Despite working in
a general setting, Hayashi’s paper has three drawbacks. First, the characterization given
in [5, Thm 1.7] is only applicable when irreducible modules are parametrized by their highest
weights with respect to special choices of a Borel in the quasi-reductive supergroup G. When
the base field is R, the condition on the Borel B is that τ(B) and B are conjugate under
the Weyl group of the even part, where τ denotes the anti-holomorphic involution of G that
determines its real form. However, the majority of Borels B do not satisfy this property. For

We now define the collections of Lie superalgebras mentioned in the previous paragraph.
Definition 1.1. We say a Lie superalgebra g is of basic type if it is isomorphic to one of the
following Lie superalgebras:
(i) A simple Lie algebra.
(ii) One of the Lie superalgebras sl(m|n), gl(m|n), psl(m|n), or osp(m|2n) for m, n ≥1.
(iii) An exceptional Lie superalgebra G(2|1), F(3|1), or D(2|1, α).
We say g is of type Q(n) if it is isomorphic to one of q(n), pq(n), sq(n), or psq(n) for n ≥1.
We say g is of type P(n) if it is isomorphic to pe(n) or spe(n) for n ≥1. Finally, we say g is
of Cartan type if it is isomorphic to one of the Lie superalgebras W(n), H(n), S(n) or ˜S(2n)
for n ≥1.
Given a complex Lie superalgebra g, we denote the intersection of kernels of irreducible
finite dimensional g-modules by fg. For a real Lie superalgebra gR, we define fgR analogously.
We now give the definition of the collection M of Lie superalgebras whose irreducible real
modules are classified in this paper.
Definition 1.2. Let M be the smallest collection of finite dimensional real Lie superalgebras
that is closed under taking direct sums and satisfies the following properties.
(i) M contains real forms of the four types of Lie superalgebras of Definition 1.1, ev-
ery real Lie algebra, and every complex Lie superalgebra (considered as a real Lie
superalgebra via restriction of scalars).
(ii) If gR is a real Lie superalgebra such that either gR/fgR ∈M, or gR has a hyperbolic
type triangular decomposition gR = uR⊕lR⊕uR (see Subsection 4.2) such that lR ∈M,
then gR ∈M.
When g is either of basic type or of type Q(n), our method for the computation of ETg,τ(W)
from the highest weight of W (which is carried out in Theorems 6.10 and 6.12) is quite different
from the one used in [5]: rather than using tools from Galois cohomology in the spirit of [13],
we use the Harish-Chandra projection of products of root vectors of a sequence of isotropic
roots associated to the highest weight.
For Lie superalgebras of basic type or of type Q(n), in Section 7 we establish a slightly
different method for computing ETg,τ(W) that relies on the evaluation of the highest weight
λ of W on the sum of coroots associated to Kostant’s cascade of strongly orthogonal roots
in a Levi subalgebra of g0; see Theorem 7.11. Even in the special case of real reductive Lie
algebras, this viewpoint leads to a classification of irreducible modules that (in our opinion) is
substantially simpler than the one given in [10]; see Theorem 7.12. In [10], the characterization
of irreducible modules of real simple Lie algebras reduces to the calculation of an invariant of
complex modules known as the Karpelevich index. However, the arguments to arrive at this
result are rather intricate. Furthermore, the conceptual connection with Kostant’s cascade
has, to our best knowledge, remained unnoticed in [10].

2.2. The Brauer–Wall monoid. Isomorphism classes of finite dimensional central division
superalgebras over a field F form a group BWF with the binary operation uniquely determined
by the property that [D] · [D′] = [D′′] if and only if D ⊗F D′ is Morita equivalent to D′′. The
disjoint union BW := BWR ∪BWC naturally inherits a monoid structure that extends
the group structures of BWR and BWC: the product of [D] ∈BWR and [D′] ∈BWC is
defined to be [D ⊗C (C ⊗R D′)]. This monoid is called the Brauer–Wall monoid of the field
R (see [14] for a theory over general fields). It has 10 elements: 2 division superalgebras
with supercenter C that form a group isomorphic to Z2, and 8 division superalgebras with
supercenter R that form a group isomorphic to Z8. In what follows, we denote the former
2 division superalgebras by kC for 0 ≤k ≤1 and the latter 8 by kR for 0 ≤k ≤7. In this
notation, kF is Morita equivalent to the Clifford F-algebra on k square roots of unity.
