CONSTRUCTIBILITY ASPECTS OF THE CONE CONJECTURE
DANIIL SEREBRENNIKOV
Abstract. We establish two consequences of the Kawamata–Morrison–Totaro cone conjecture,
and prove them unconditionally in all dimensions. First, for a K-trivial variety, the natural action
of its automorphism group on the set of ample divisor classes of fixed volume has only finitely many
orbits. Second, the number of (isomorphism classes of) minimal models for a given K-trivial variety
is finite if these models admit a bounded polarization.

1. Introduction
In this paper, we study families of projective 0-pairs with klt singularities over the field of complex
numbers C. For the sake of clarity, we state our results for K-trivial varieties. We say that a smooth
projective variety X is K-trivial if the canonical line bundle ωX is torsion. The class of K-trivial
varieties includes classical examples such as the Fermat quartic surface in P3

birational geometry of K-trivial varieties is the Kawamata–Morrison–Totaro cone conjecture. The
latter conjecture is an unraveled problem in dimension ≥3. We provide an informal diagram that

The arithmetic cone conjecture predicts (cf. Proposition A.7) the finiteness of ample orbits, which
is our first result. To be precise, let NS(X) = Pic(X)/ ≡denote the group of line bundles on X
modulo the numerical equivalence. Then there is a natural (right) action of the automorphism group
Aut(X) of X on NS(X) given by [A] · φ = [φ∗A] for every φ ∈Aut(X), and every [A] ∈NS(X).
Theorem A (Finiteness of ample orbits). Fix N ∈Z>0. Let X be a projective smooth K-trivial
variety over C. Then the number of orbits for the natural action of Aut(X) on the set
{[A] ∈NS(X) : Adim X = N, A is ample}
is finite (whenever the defined set is non-empty).
Besides particular cases for which the cone conjecture is proven, Theorem A is known for abelian
varieties [NN81, Theorem 1.1], smooth K3 surfaces [Ste85, Proposition 2.6], smooth Calabi–Yau
threefolds [Sze99, Corollary 4.5], i.e. ωX ≈OX and H1(X, OX) = H2(X, OX) = 0, hyperk¨ahler
manifolds [Huy18, Theorem 0.1], and klt Calabi–Yau log surfaces [Tot10, Theorem 4.1] for which the
cone conjecture is proven in full generality. In addition, it is recently shown in [BFMT25, Lemma
2.22] that a pure Hodge structure admits only finitely many polarizations up to isomorphism that
is a generalization of [NN81, Theorem 1.1] in a slightly different direction.
