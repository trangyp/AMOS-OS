COHEN–MACAULAY AND GORENSTEIN PROPERTIES OF BI-AMALGAMATED
ALGEBRAS WITH APPLICATIONS TO ALGEBROID CURVES
EFE G¨UREL AND ABUZER G¨UND¨UZ
Abstract. Let A ▷◁f,g (J, J′) be the bi–amalgamation of a commutative ring A with (B, C) along the
ideals (J, J′) with respect to the ring homomorphisms (f, g). In this article, we study the basic homological
properties of the bi–amalgamated algebra construction. We first calculate the dimension and depth of the
bi–amalgamated algebra under fairly general circumstances and derive necessary and sufficient conditions
for Cohen–Macaulayness in terms of maximal and big Cohen–Macaulay modules of A.
Furthermore, we
characterize the Gorenstein property of the bi–amalgamated algebra through the canonical modules of f(A)+
J and g(A)+J′. We apply our results to the theory of curve singularities by constructing Gorenstein algebroid
curves through bi–amalgamated and amalgamated algebras.
We also give a brief remark concerning the
universally catenary property of A ▷◁f,g (J, J′).
1. Introduction
Let A and B be commutative rings, let J be an ideal of B, and let f : A →B be a ring homomorphism.
The set
A ▷◁f J = {(a, f(a) + j) | a ∈A, j ∈J}
is called the amalgamation of A and B along J with respect to f, and it is a subring of A × B.
This
construction extends the notion of amalgamated duplication introduced by D’Anna and Fontana in [10]. A
more general framework for amalgamations was later developed in [11] and [12], where amalgamated algebras
were constructed via pull–back. This perspective allowed the authors to establish several transfer results for
algebraic and homological properties from the rings A and f(A) + J to the amalgamated algebra A ▷◁f J.
Since then, amalgamations have attracted significant attention and have been successfully applied in various
contexts; see, for instance, [15] and [24].
Let A, B, and C be commutative rings, let J and J′ be ideals of B and C, respectively, and let f : A →B
and g: A →C be ring homomorphisms satisfying
f −1(J) = g−1(J′).
The set
A ▷◁f,g (J, J′) = {(f(a) + j, g(a) + j′) | a ∈A, (j, j′) ∈J × J′}
is called the bi–amalgamation of A with (B, C) along (J, J′) with respect to (f, g). It is a subring of B × C.
Bi–amalgamations, which naturally generalize amalgamations and duplications, were introduced in [20]. On
top of classical pull–back constructions, the rings f(A)+J, A+J, and furthermore the CPI–extension C(A, I)
can be viewed as bi–amalgamations.

Subsequent works have focused on the transfer of specific algebraic properties to bi–amalgamations. The
coherence property was studied in [26]. In [21], necessary and sufficient conditions were established for a
bi–amalgamation to be arithmetical, with applications to weak global dimension and the transfer of the
semihereditary property. The transfer of Gaussian and Pr¨ufer properties was investigated in [23], where the
authors recovered known results for amalgamations and constructed new examples of rings satisfying these
properties. For further background on bi–amalgamated algebras, we refer to [26], [20], [21], and [23].
The study of Cohen–Macaulay and Gorenstein properties in this context originates from the amalgamated
duplication A ▷◁I in the works of D’Anna [9]. It was shown that, under suitable annihilator conditions, A ▷◁I
is Gorenstein if and only if A admits a canonical ideal isomorphic to I, and later generalizations characterized
the quasi–Gorenstein and normal cases [3, 28]. Furthermore, an application to singularity theory has been
proposed, where amalgamated duplication was used as a tool to construct Gorenstein algebroid curves. Such
algebroid curves are characterized through the symmetry properties of their value semigroups. The value
semigroups of duplicated monomial algebroid branches are also constructed explicitly in [9].
Investigations of Cohen–Macaulay and Gorenstein properties were generalized to the framework of amal-
gamations in [29, 13]. Their main results include the calculation of the depth and dimension of amalgamation
rings explicitly under suitable conditions, and the characterization of the Cohen–Macaulay and Gorenstein
properties, along with a brief discussion of universally catenary rings. Our article will be primarily concerned
with these results and their extensions to the bi–amalgamated algebra. They further studied embedding
dimensions, generalized Cohen–Macaulay rings and modules, the Serre condition, and quasi– and general-
ized Gorenstein rings in relation to amalgamations. We have not deemed such investigations necessary for
this paper and have kept them as open questions for further research. There have been no investigations
regarding the construction of algebroid curves using amalgamated algebras in the literature to the best of
our knowledge.
In Section 2, we calculate the dimension and depth of the bi–amalgamated algebra by using tools from
local cohomology in Theorem 2.1. In particular, we prove in Theorem 2.2 that under suitable assumptions,
A ▷◁f,g (J, J′) is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if A is Cohen–Macaulay with maximal Cohen–Macaulay
modules J, J′.
In Section 3, we first prove a Hom-lemma useful in our investigations in Lemma 3.1. Then, we prove
a necessary condition for A ▷◁f,g (J, J′) to be Gorenstein in Proposition 3.1. Finally, we completely char-
acterize the Gorenstein property of A ▷◁f,g (J, J′) in Theorem 3.1 by using the pull–back description of
bi–amalgamations under suitable assumptions.
In Section 4, we apply our results to the theory of curve singularities. We show that bi–amalgamation
constructions applied to algebroid curves also yield algebroid curves in Theorem 4.1.
Furthermore, we
determine when the bi–amalgamated algebroid curve is Gorenstein in Theorem 4.2, utilizing our findings
from the previous section. This gives us a way of constructing algebroid Gorenstein curves, which are of
particular importance.
In Section 5, we give a brief remark on how the bi–amalgamated algebra is a homomorphic image of a
polynomial ring over A in Proposition 5.1. As a result, we characterize the universally catenary property of
A ▷◁f,g (J, J′) in Proposition 5.2.
As previously mentioned, the bi–amalgamated algebra generalizes the classical pull–back constructions in
the literature. We shall give a few examples of interest, which can be found in [20]. The amalgamated algebra
can be obtained by setting I = f−1(J) and ι = idA, yielding

2. Cohen–Macaulay Property of A ▷◁f,g (J, J′)
In this section, we characterize the Cohen–Macaulay property of the ring A ▷◁f,g (J, J′). To do so, we
first calculate the dimension and the depth of A ▷◁f,g (J, J′) under fairly general circumstances. It was first
shown in [9] that for a Cohen–Macaulay ring A, the ring A ▷◁I is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if I is a
maximal Cohen–Macaulay A–module. This easily follows from the A–module isomorphism A ▷◁I ∼= A ⊕I.
When J is a finitely generated A–module, it is shown in [13] that under the condition J ⊆Jac B, the ring
A ▷◁f J is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if J is a maximal Cohen–Macaulay A–module. This also follows
from the A–module isomorphism A ▷◁f J ∼= A ⊕J. It is also remarked that it is considerably more difficult
to extend these results to the case where J is not finitely generated. This case was thoroughly discussed in
[29]. The main difficulty in establishing analogous results for the bi–amalgamated algebra is the fact that an
analogous isomorphism of A–modules does not always hold. This problem first led us to investigate when
such an isomorphism exists and adopt methods similar to that of [29].

5. A remark on the universally catenary property
Bi–amalgamations naturally generalize some classic pull–back constructions of polynomial and power series
rings. In the previous section, we have also seen an application of the bi–amalgamated algebra construction
to curve singularities. In this section, we describe the bi–amalgamated algebra as a homomorphic image of
a polynomial ring. The results here generalize those of [29].

As a consequence of the above proposition, we reobtain a special case of the Noetherian characterization
of A ▷◁f,g (J, J′). Indeed, we get that whenever J and J′ are finitely generated A–modules, A ▷◁f,g (J, J′) is
Noetherian if and only if A is Noetherian. This agrees with the results already found in the literature (see
[20]) and gives an easier proof in the finitely generated case.
We say that a Noetherian ring A is catenary if all saturated chains of prime ideals between any two prime
ideals have the same length. If all finitely generated A–algebras are catenary, we call A universally catenary.
From the above description, we have the following characterization of the universally catenary property for
the bi–amalgamated algebra.
Proposition 5.2. Assume J and J′ are finitely generated as A–modules. Then A ▷◁f,g (J, J′) is universally
catenary if and only if A/I0 is universally catenary.
In particular, we get the following characterization for amalgamated algebras as given in [29].
Corollary 5.4. Assume J is a finitely generated A–module. Then A ▷◁f J is universally catenary if and
only if A is universally catenary.
Corollary 5.5. Assume M is a finitely generated A–module. Then A ⋉M is universally catenary if and
only if A is universally catenary.
Corollary 5.6. Assume I is an ideal of A. Then A ▷◁I is universally catenary if and only if A is universally
catenary.
