COHERENT FRAMES WITH ZERO BEURLING DENSITY
INGRID BELTIT¸ ˘A AND JORDY TIMO VAN VELTHOVEN
Abstract. We show the existence of a coherent frame in the orbit of a connected, simply
connected unimodular solvable Lie group of exponential growth for which the lower Beurling
density of its index set is zero.
1. Introduction
Let G be a second countable locally compact group and let (π, Hπ) be an irreducible,
square-integrable projective unitary representation of G. For a vector η ∈Hπ and a discrete
subset Γ ⊆G, a system of the form
π(Γ)η =

π(γ)η : γ ∈Γ
	
is often referred to as a coherent state subsystem or simply a coherent system.
A natural question on coherent systems that goes back to at least [24] is to derive quanti-
tative necessary conditions on the index set Γ ⊆G for the system π(Γ)η to have certain span-
ning/reproducing properties, such as completeness or orthogonality. For index sets forming
a lattice Γ, necessary conditions on the lattice covolume covol(Γ) for π(Γ)η being a complete
system or orthogonal system have been obtained in, e.g., [3,27]. Similar results for frames and
Riesz sequences associated to general index sets Γ ⊆G in a unimodular amenable group G
have been obtained in terms of the lower and upper Beurling densities D−(Γ) and D+(Γ) of
Γ, respectively, in the papers [6,8,9,13,21,23]. In particular, the following necessary density
condition for frames π(Γ)η satisfying a mild localisation assumption η ∈Bπ (see [17] and
Equation (2.2)) has been obtained via different methods in [6,8,9,23]. See Section 2 for the
precise definitions involved.
Theorem 1.1. Let G be a second countable unimodular amenable group. Let (π, Hπ) be an
irreducible, square-integrable projective unitary representation of G of formal degree dπ > 0.
If there exist Γ ⊆G and η ∈Bπ such that π(Γ)η is a frame for Hπ, then
D−(Γ) ≥dπ.
For abelian groups (more generally, IN groups), it is known that Theorem 1.1 also holds
without the localisation assumption η ∈Bπ, see, e.g., [8,26]. It is an open question whether
this assumption can be removed from Theorem 1.1 in general; see [30, Question 10].
The aim of this note is to show the existence of coherent frames for which the lower Beurling
density of their index set is zero. In particular, this shows that the localisation assumption
cannot be removed from Theorem 1.1. In addition, it shows that various results obtained in
[6, 8, 9, 23], such as the stability of frames under weak limits of translates [8] or the frame
measure formula [6], do not hold without the localisation assumption.
The following is the main result obtained in this note.

Theorem 1.2. Let (π, Hπ) be an irreducible, square-integrable projective representation of a
connected, simply connected unimodular solvable Lie group G of exponential growth. Then
there exists η ∈Hπ and a set Γ ⊆G with D−(Γ) = 0 such that π(Γ)η is a frame for Hπ.
We mention that the proof of Theorem 1.2 shows that the set Γ ⊆G can be chosen to be
contained in a subgroup of G of dimension at most 3.
Our proof of Theorem 1.2 is based on similar techniques as those used for the construction
of frames of translates for nonnilpotent exponential solvable Lie groups in [14]. Specifically,
as in [14], we use that any such group admits a nonunimodular closed subgroup for which
there exists a continuous frame of translates [11] and combine this with the fact that any such
continuous frame can be discretised to obtain a discrete frame [10]. We note, however, that
Theorem 1.2 cannot simply be obtained as a direct consequence (see, e.g., [14, Thm. 2.11])
of the main results in [14] as these are only known for genuine regular representations (i.e.,
not for general projective regular representations) and since unimodular connected, simply
connected solvable Lie groups do not admit irreducible (genuine) unitary representations that
are square-integrable in the strict sense, cf. [4, Cor. 4.2]. To circumvent these obstructions,
we carefully use the correspondence between projective representations of a given group and
representations of an associated central extension of the group and exploit the aforementioned
results on frames of translates for the latter group.
The note is organised as follows.
Section 2 provides the basic background on square-
integrable projective representations, Beurling densities and frames. The proof of Theorem 1.2
is carried out in Section 3. Lastly, we provide in Section 4 a class of solvable Lie groups to
which Theorem 1.2 applies.

Lemma 3.4. Let S be a connected solvable Lie group of exponential growth. Then there exists

p: eS →S the covering map, so that ker(p) is a discrete central subgroup of eS.
