ARNAUD BROTHIER, AIDAN SIMS AND DILSHAN WIJESENA
Abstract. We develop new techniques for the construction and classification of repre-
sentations of row-finite and locally convex higher-rank graph C∗-algebras O. This class
includes Cuntz–Krieger algebras associated to row-finite directed graphs. Our approach
relies on the representation theory of a certain non-self-adjoint algebra and a lifting pro-
cess of representations. We introduce a novel dimension vector for representations of O
yielding a countable partition of the spectrum. Given a Cuntz–Krieger algebra and a
finite dimension vector, we construct a smooth manifold parametrising the corresponding
spectral component. Our techniques are both explicit and functorial.
1. Introduction
Objects such as groups or algebras are often best understood through the study of their
actions. In the case of C∗-algebras, this viewpoint typically leads to the investigation of
their representations on Hilbert spaces, which are in general difficult to construct explic-
itly. Jones observed that, under suitable conditions, actions of relatively basic objects
can be lifted to actions of more complex ones [Jon17, Jon18]. This idea was successfully
applied to construct new unitary representations of the Richard Thompson groups F,
T, and V , as well as related groups, and to establish analytic and geometric properties
of these groups [BJ19a, Bro23]. Although these techniques were originally developed in
a group-theoretic setting, they were later adapted to the construction and classification
of explicit representations of C∗-algebras [BJ19b]. The first C∗-algebra to benefit from
this approach was the Cuntz(–Dixmier) algebra O2 [Cun77, Dix64]. The first and third
authors subsequently developed powerful techniques which, among other results, unravel
geometric structures on a large piece of the spectrum of O2 [BW25, BW26, BW24]. This
was a complete surprise—especially since O2 is a non-type I simple C∗-algebra and thus,
by general theorems, has, a priori, a highly pathological spectrum [Gli61].
The Cuntz(–Dixmier) algebra falls into the very general class of graph C∗-algebras
(sometimes called Cuntz–Krieger algebras of directed graphs) [CK80, KPRR97, KPR98],
see also [Rae05]. Specifically, the Cuntz algebra On is the C∗-algebra of the directed
graph with one vertex and n loops. Graph C∗-algebras form a rich class of C∗-algebras.
They include, up to stable isomorphism, all simple purely infinite nuclear C∗-algebras
with torsion-free K1-groups, and all AF algebras (see for instance [Rae05]). A further
generalisation, developed by Kumjian and Pask based on earlier work of Robertson and
Steger on groups acting on buildings [RS99], replaces directed graphs with a larger class
of combinatorial objects called higher-rank graphs [KP00] (see also [RSY03] for a fur-
ther extension and also [LV20, LV22]). Their aim was to realise larger classes of C∗-
algebras including purely infinite C∗-algebras with torsion in K1, and non-AF stably
finite C∗-algebras. These k-graph C∗-algebras include interesting examples beyond the

Purpose of this paper. We establish powerful new tools for constructing and studying
representations of (higher-rank) graph C∗-algebras, and apply them to key examples. We
also develop a deeper understanding of the techniques, which were first used on the Cuntz
algebra in a rather indirect manner, revealing a critical use of a non-self-adjoint algebra.
Strategy and main results. A higher-rank graph Λ or k-graph is roughly speaking
the path space of a countable directed graph whose edges are coloured by {1,⋯,k} that
we mod out by relations of the form µ ○ν = ν′ ○µ′ for bi-coloured paths (µ,µ′) and
(ν,ν′) of length two with colours in opposite orders (see Section 2.1 for details). The
1-graphs correspond to directed graphs (also called quivers).
A k-graph Λ defines a
universal C∗-algebra OΛ generated by isometries xλ indexed by vertices and edges of Λ.
A representation xλ ↦Xλ of OΛ on L decomposes over the vertices ⊕vLv and each edge
λ ∶v →w defines a partial isometry Xλ with domain Lv and codomain contained in Lw.
The generators are subject to four axioms in general but if k = 1 we may remove one of
them and recover the Cuntz–Krieger algebras of directed graphs.
In this article we define a new universal C∗-algebra PΛ, termed Pythagorean, that
is obtained by removing one type of relations (the one asking that the xλ are partial
isometries). Note that for convenience, we denote by aλ the generators of PΛ so that the
quotient map PΛ ↠OΛ is given by the formula aλ ↦s∗
λ (hence taking adjoints). Each
representation H of PΛ still decomposes into a direct sum of Hv over the vertices v of
Λ. For any Λ the C∗-algebra PΛ admits many finite dimensional representations while
generically OΛ has none. By adapting a construction pioneered by Jones we define a
directed system of Hilbert spaces constructed from H which yields a representation Π(H)
of OΛ. This process is functorial Π ∶Rep(PΛ) →Rep(OΛ) and is moreover essentially
surjective: any representation L of OΛ is in fact of the form Π(H), up to isomorphism.
The construction is explicit: we may interpret vectors of Π(H) as classes of trees whose
leaves are decorated by vectors of H. The generators of C∗-algebra OΛ act by growing
and reducing trees.
We now have a new and practical way to construct all representations of OΛ.
We
want to go further and perform some classification. We could naively take the dimension
vector of H and hope to obtain an invariant for L ≃Π(H). This does not work because
multiple H can give the same representation L of OΛ. But if there exists at least one finite
dimensional H satisfying Π(H) ≃L, then there is a smallest such H, which we denote
by Σ(L). The dimension vector of Σ(L) is then an invariant of L (which depends on the
choice of the k-graph Λ). Better still, L ↦Σ(L) is functorial and so Σ(L) itself is an
invariant of L. Finally, a copy of Σ(L) can be found inside L: it is the smallest subspace
of L closed under the x∗
λ that generates L as a representation of OΛ.
Now, we could naively try to classify representations of PΛ and hope to obtain a clas-
sification of representations of OΛ.

2. higher-rank graphs and higher-rank graph C∗-algebras
This is a preliminary section in which we define higher-rank graphs and their C∗-
algebras. We recommend [KP00, Sim10] for additional details on k-graphs and to the
book of Raeburn for the graph case [Rae05].
2.1. Higher-rank graphs. Fix a natural number k ≥1 and consider the free Abelian
monoid in k-generators Nk (hence we take the convention that N contains 0) with standard
basis (e1,⋯,ek). A k-graph is a pair (Λ,d) where Λ is a countable category, and d ∶Λ →Nk
is a functor called the degree map satisfying the factorisation property: for all λ ∈Λ and
m,n ∈Nk if d(λ) = m + n, then there exists a unique pair (µ,ν) in Λ satisfying λ = µ ○ν
and d(µ) = m,d(ν) = n.

and source maps; so for λ ∈Λ, we have r(λ),s(λ) ∈Λ0, and r(λ)λ = λ = λs(λ).

Remark 2.3.
(1) Row-finiteness ensures that the sums appearing in the presenta-
tions of the key C∗-algebras PΛ and OΛ of this paper (see Definitions 3.1 and
2.4) are finite. Local convexity ensures that the universal presentation of OΛ is
non-degenerate (see [RSY03, Theorem 3.15]). Local convexity also guarantees ex-
istence of common right-multiples for trees associated to Λ (see Lemma 4.3). This
property allows us to construct the direct limits of Hilbert spaces that we use to
describe the representations of OΛ.
(2) By [Far08, Theorem 2.1], given any row-finite locally convex k-graph Λ, there is
a row-finite k-graph Λ with no sources such that C∗(Λ) embeds as a full corner
of C∗(Λ). In particular, C∗(Λ) and C∗(Λ) have the same representation theory.
So we could in principle restrict attention to k-graphs with no sources and still
capture the representation theory of arbitrary row-finite locally convex k-graphs.
In practice the passage from Λ to Λ is complex, and it can be difficult to use it to
translate results, so we have chosen to deal directly with k-graphs that are locally
convex but admit sources.

Remark 4.13. Proposition 4.12(3) demonstrates the importance of introducing the non-
self-adjoint algebra AΛ. The point is that since OΛ is typically purely infinite, all of its
representations are necessarily on infinite-dimensional spaces, and so there are no finite-
dimensional reducing subspaces of L, or indeed of any representation of OΛ. However, the
subalgebra AΛ does not contain the adjoints of the generators of OΛ and so it is possible—
in fact, quite common—for an irreducible representation of OΛ to contain many proper
AΛ-submodules. In particular, the representation L of OΛ contains the AΛ-submodule
span{[v,ξ] ∶v ∈H,ξ ∈Hv}, which is equivalent to the initial AΛ-module H. This means
that whenever a representation K of OΛ has finite P-dimension (see Definition 5.6), it
contains a finite-dimensional AΛ-module. We exploit this in the proof of Proposition 5.7.
Moreover, Π is functorial not just from Rep(PΛ) to Rep(OΛ) but from all of Mod(AΛ)
to Rep(OΛ), so to obtain maximal information about arrows in Rep(OΛ), we must take
into account all the intertwiners of AΛ-modules, and not just intertwiners of PΛ-modules
(see Remark 3.6).
