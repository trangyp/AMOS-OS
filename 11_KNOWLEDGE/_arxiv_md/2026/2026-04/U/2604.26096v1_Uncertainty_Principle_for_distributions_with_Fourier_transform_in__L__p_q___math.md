Abstract
A version of the Uncertainty Principle says: There does not exist a non zero function in Lp(Rd)
if its Fourier transform is supported by a set of finite α-Hausdorff measure with α < 2d/p. This
UP does not hold at the endpoint α = 2d/p. We find the sharp form of the UP in the limit case.
We prove that there exists a non-zero function in the Lorentz space Lp,q(Rd) such that its Fourier
transform is supported by a set of zero ( 2d
p , β)-Netrusov–Hausdorff capacity if and only if β >

Introducion
The Uncertainty Principle (UP) in mathematical analysis is a family of facts that state: Both function
and its Fourier transform cannot be simultaneously small (see [16]). Denote the Hausdorff measure of
dimension α by Hα. The following theorem is yet another manifestation of the Uncertainty Principle.
Theorem 1.1. Let S ⊂Rd be a compact set such that Hα(S) < ∞. Let ζ be a tempered distribution
such that supp ζ ⊂S and ˆζ ∈Lp(Rd) for some p < 2d
α . Then ζ = 0.
In the article [9] it was proved that this UP does not hold at the endpoint.
Theorem 1.2. Let 2 < p < ∞. There exists a compact set S ⊂Rd and a probability measure µ such
that supp µ ⊂S, ˆµ ∈Lp, Hα(S) = 0 and p = 2d
α .
The present paper may be considered as an extension of [9].

case d −1 ⩽α (see [10]). In full generality, Theorem 1.1 follows from Kahane’s work in [17].
