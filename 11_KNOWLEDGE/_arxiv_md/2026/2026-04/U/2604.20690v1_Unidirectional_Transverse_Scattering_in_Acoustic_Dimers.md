Unidirectional Transverse Scattering in Acoustic Dimers
Mikhail Smagin,1 Iuliia Timankova,1 Pavel Pankin,1 Yong Li,2, 1 and Mihail Petrov1
1School of Physics and Engineering, ITMO University, 197101 St. Petersburg, Russia
2Institute of Acoustics, School of Physics Science and Engineering, Tongji University, Shanghai 200092, China
We study unidirectional transverse scattering in a two-dimensional acoustic dimer composed of
two isotropic subwavelength scatterers.
Using a coupled multipole model, we show that inter-
particle coupling enables effective monopole–dipole interference and supports a transverse Kerker
effect under plane wave excitation. In contrast to a single non-absorbing isotropic particle, where
Kerker-type cancellation is only approached in the weak-scattering limit, the dimer can combine
pronounced directionality with strong overall scattering.
This regime is promising for compact
acoustic beam steering and directional wave routing.
Directional scattering is a key concept behind wave
manipulation control [1], with applications such as beam
steering [2], frequency- [3] and polarization [4] selective
transport, and directional emission [5]. At the subwave-
length scale, directional scattering is achieved through
interference of a few resonant multipoles, which shape
the angular distribution of the scattered field. In optics,
the simplest case scenario was described by Kerker et al
in 1983 [6], showing that a combination of electric and
magnetic dipole can form a cardioid scattering pattern
when phase and amplitude conditions are satisfied. This
concept was later generalized to combinations of higher-
order multipoles [1].
The first and second Kerker conditions as originally
formulated correspond to cancellation of backward and
forward scattering, respectively. The idea of Kerker ef-
fect was then analytically extended [7, 8], and experi-
mentally demonstrated [9–11], to directional scattering
in the transverse direction. This transverse Kerker effect
corresponds to scattering suppression in both forward
and backward directions simultaneously [8, 9]. Such elec-
tromagnetic transverse Kerker effect can be either bidi-
rectional, corresponding to dumbbell radiation pattern
[8–10, 12], or unidirectional, corresponding to transverse
cardioid radiation pattern [7, 11, 13, 14].
Both types
of the effect have found to be uselful in a wide range
of appications, including lattice invisibility [12, 15, 16],
directional wave routing [17–19], antennas for directional
emission [20], precise displacement sensing [7, 21–23], and
lateral optical forces [11, 13].
In acoustics, classical forward and backward Kerker di-
rectionality was introduced only recently [24, 25]. In this
case directional scattering can similarly be understood
through multipole interference, which in the subwave-
length regime is dominated by monopole and dipole re-
sponses [26, 27]. The monopole-dipole interference has
been shown to induce directional acoustic forces [28–
30], and directional forward and backward scattering has
been experimentally demonstrated recently as well [31].
The directionality of acoustic scattering in transverse di-
rections, however, remains underreported.
Here, we show that a simple acoustic dimer provides
conditions for a unidirectional transverse acoustic Kerker
effect. We start off by postulating that in non-absorbing

For a non-absorbing particle, it is convenient to intro-
duce Mie angles θn ∈(−π/2, π/2) through acoustic Mie
coefficients an = −cos(θn)eiθn [26, 27, 32], which reduces
the description of each scattering channel to just one pa-
rameter θn [29, 33]. The resonance in such parametriza-
tion corresponds to θn = 0, and at θn = ±π/2 the
scattering amplitude goes to zero.
In the monopole-
dipole approximation the description of acoustic scatter-
ing on an isotropic particle reduces to just two param-
eters, namely monopolar Mie angle θm and dipolar Mie
angle θd. Using this parametrization, we show that for
a non-absorbing isotropic particle, the Kerker conditions
are satisfied only in the limit where (θm, θd) approaches
one of the four corner points (±π/2, ±π/2), which cor-
responds to the limit of zero scattering.
We illustrate
this for a two-dimensional case, by plotting a logarith-

an infinite 2D cylinder illuminated by a plane wave, see
Fig. 2(b). The region close to first and second Kerker

scattering, see normalized scattering cross section k σsc
in Fig. 2(c). Here and further in this letter, we assume
wavenumber k = 2π, such that all distances are expressed

density ρ0 = 1.23 kg/m3.

Finally, we also demonstrate that the regime of trans-
verse unidrectional scattering is accessible using realis-
tic structures with the dimer consisting of labyrinthine
acoustic meta-atoms [38], see Figure 4(a), and perform-
ing full-wave numerical simulations in COMSOL Multi-
physics. These meta-atoms emulate high-index Mie scat-
terers in airborne acoustics by elongating the internal
propagation path and enabling strong subwavelength res-
onances, allowing one to tune the monopole phase while
keeping dipole scattering small [31] by optimizing its ge-
ometric parameters.
At a separation d = 0.2λ, which
we choose to accommodate practical meta-atom sizes,
the target Mie-angles in a model of purely monopolar
particles are θm1 = −0.676, θm2 = 0.271, i.e close to
resonance for both particles. To optimize geometry for
the needed monopolar response, we calculate monopolar
scattering coefficient phase arg{−γ0}, and amplitude |γ0|
spectra, see Fig. 4 and SI for more details. For a meta-
atom with relatively small Willis’ [39] and higher order
[28] couplings, for an ˆx-oriented incident plane wave with
unit amplitude, it can be approximately treated as Mie
coefficient γ0 ≈a0 = −cos(θn)eiθn. As a result of ge-
ometry optimization, the top and bottom meta-atoms
of identical geometry but different sizes have the nec-
essary monopole response at frequency f = 332.7 Hz,
and the designed dimer exhibits the expected transverse
unidirectional scattering, see Fig. 4(a). The shape of nu-
merically calculated radiation pattern does not perfectly
correspond to the downward cardioid because each real-
ized labyrynthine particle retains a small residual dipolar
response, yet remains sufficiently close to the analytical
result expected from the model with purely monopolar
particles, Eq. (2).
In conclusion, we have shown that in a single non-
absorbing isotropic acoustic particle the directional scat-
tering conditions is reached only in the limiting of vanish-
ing total scattering. In contrast, we demonstrated that
a coupled acoustic dimer overcomes this fundamental re-
striction and enables pronounced directional response in
the strong-scattering regime. Most importantly, we have
used acoustic dimers to both theoretically and numeri-
cally demonstrate transverse Kerker scattering in acous-
tics for the first time, in particular unidirectional side
scattering. These results and developed analyical models
help establish coupled acoustic meta-atoms as a promis-
ing platform for controlling sound with subwavelength
structures and open the way toward new acoustic devices
and metasurfaces for beam steering, directional routing,
and advanced wavefront manipulation.
