UNIFORM SPECTRAL GAP OF SCL IN 2-ORBIFOLDS
LVZHOU CHEN AND NICOLAUS HEUER
Abstract. We show a uniform spectral gap of stable commutator length for all compact hyperbolic
2-orbifolds relative to the peripheral subgroups. Except for the case of a sphere with three cone
points, we have an explicit uniform gap 1/36. These estimates are needed in understanding stable
commutator length in 3-manifolds. Our methods use explicit quasimorphisms for the generic case,
and use hyperbolic geometry (pleated surfaces) for the exceptional case of a sphere with three cone
points.
1. Introduction
The current paper is revised and rewritten based on the appendix of [CH20a]. The older article
[CH20a] is shortened and revised into [CH20b] for publication purposes.
The stable commutator length (scl) in a group G is a characteristic function sclG : G →[0, +∞]
that captures geometric and dynamical information of elements in the group; see Section 2. It is
dual to quasimorphisms on the group by Bavard’s duality [Bav91].
A rather common feature of scl is that groups G with non-positive curvature typically have a
(scl-)spectral gap property: There is a constant C = C(G) > 0 such that for all g ∈G either
sclG(g) ≥C or sclG(g) = 0 (and such g has a special form). For instance, this holds for all word-
hyperbolic groups [CF10] and mapping class groups [BBF16], right-angled Artin or Coxeter groups
[Heu19, CH23]. In a previous work, the authors showed that this also holds for 3-manifold groups
[CH20b]. The spectral gap property has connections to homomorphism rigidity, and obtaining sharp
estimates of the gap C, especially for C = 1/2, has seen applications to the study of generalized
torsion elements [IMT19, CC25].
In this paper, we prove a uniform spectral gap for all orbifold fundamental groups π1(B) of
compact 2-dimensional orbifolds B with negative orbifold Euler characteristic χo(B). We write sclB
for sclπ1(B), and write scl(B,∂B) for stable commutator length relative to the peripheral subgroups
represented by the boundary components of B; see below for the definition.
Theorem A. There is a uniform constant C > 0, such that for any compact 2-dimensional orbifold
B with χo(B) < 0, we have either scl(B,∂B)(g) ≥C or scl(B,∂B)(g) = 0 for all g ∈π1(B). Moreover,
if B is not the 2-sphere with three cone points, we can take C to be 1/36.
Even if one is only interested in the closed case, it is essential to consider the case with boundary
and relative scl. This is because such orbifolds occur naturally as vertex pieces in natural splittings
of closed orbifold groups, for which we need to understand scl of vertex groups relative to edge
groups. In the case of nonempty boundary we obtain the stronger explicit estimate below.
Theorem B. Let B be a compact 2-dimensional orbifold with nonempty boundary and χo(B) < 0.
For any g ∈π1(B), we have either
scl(B,∂B)(g) ≥1/24
or scl(B,∂B)(g) = 0. Moreover, scl(B,∂B)(g) = 0 if and only if g has finite order, g is conjugate to
g−1, or g is peripheral.
This is helpful for proving spectral gap results for Seifert fibered 3-manifolds [CH20b]. Theo-
rem B makes certain scl estimates explicit and uniform for Seifert fibered 3-manifolds; See [CH20b,
Lemma 6.16 and Remark 6.17].

of generators and the hyperbolicity constant δ, and thus it is not uniform for an obvious reason
and it is not explicit. Their theorem shows that there is also a spectral gap for integral chains.

When the orbifold B has nonempty boundary, the orbifold group π1(B) is a free product of cyclic
groups. As a byproduct of our methods, we have the following explicit and uniform spectral gap for
integral chains for all such free products, where the equivalence of chains is explained in Section 2.
The gap 1/12 is sharp for this family of groups and agrees with the gap for elements; see [CFL16,
Theorem 6.9] and [CH20b, Lemma 2.6].
Theorem C. Let G be a free product of cyclic groups and let c = P cigi be an integral chain. Then
sclG(c) ≥1/12 unless c is equivalent to the zero chain, in which case sclG(c) = 0.
A powerful tool for proving lower bounds of scl is Bavard’s duality [Bav91]. However, it is very
difficult to use it to obtain good and explicit bounds due to our lack of understanding and limited
constructions. Our proof of Theorems B and C uses a type of counting quasimorphisms that we
define in free products of cyclic groups, which enjoy a separation property. The exceptional case
of a sphere with three cone points in Theorem A requires a different approach using hyperbolic
geometry. In particular, we provide the existence of pleated surfaces under soft assumptions in the
appendix (Theorem A.1), which seems hard to find in the literature.
Organization of the paper. In Section 2, we recall the necessary background on stable commu-
tator length, relative stable commutator length, quasimorphisms, Bavard’s duality, and 2-orbifolds.
In Section 3, we construct counting quasimorphisms in free products of cyclic groups and develop
their basic properties to prove Theorem C. Then we apply these quasimorphisms to prove the rel-
ative spectral gap for orbifolds with nonempty boundary (Theorem B) in Section 4. Finally we
deal with closed orbifolds and prove Theorem A in Section 5: the generic closed case is handled
using acylindrical Bass–Serre tree actions, while the case of a sphere with three cone points is
treated separately using hyperbolic geometry. The appendix establishes the existence of pleated
representatives for admissible surfaces, which is used in the exceptional closed case.

5.1. The generic case.
Theorem 5.1. Let B be a closed 2-dimensional orbifold B with χo(B) < 0. Suppose B is not
the 2-sphere with three cone points. Then for all g ∈π1(B) we have either sclB(g) ≥1/36 or

Our proof relies on a result of Clay–Forester–Louwsma [CFL16], so we start by recalling some
relevant definitions.
Definition 5.2. A group action on a tree (without inversion) simplicially is K-acylindrical for
some K ∈Z+ if the fixed point set of each nontrivial element has diameter at most K.
It is well known that any isometry of a tree is either elliptic (i.e. acting with fixed points) or
hyperbolic (i.e. acting by translation along a unique axis). In addition, the fixed point set of an
elliptic isometry is a subtree.
With the definition above, which differs from the one in [CFL16], the statement below is equiv-
alent to [CFL16, Theorem 6.11].
Theorem 5.3 (Clay–Forester–Louwsma [CFL16]). Let G be a group acting K-acylindrically and
let N be the least integer greater than or equal to (K + 3)/2. If g ∈G acts without fixed point (i.e.
by hyperbolic isometry), then either sclG(g) ≥1/(12N) or sclG(g) = 0. Moreover, the latter case
occurs exactly when g and g−1 are conjugate.
For our applications below, we consider the Bass–Serre tree actions for HNN extensions and
amalgamated free products [Ser80]. In such cases, an element acts elliptically if and only if it is
conjugate into a vertex group. For the HNN extension G associated to an isomorphism of two
subgroups C1, C2 ≤A, an element acting on the Bass–Serre tree with a fixed point set of diameter
≥2 must fix two adjacent edges and hence is conjugate into Ci ∩hCjh−1 for some i, j ∈{1, 2} and
h ∈A \ Cj. Thus the action is 1-acylindrical if all such subgroups are trivial. For an amalgamated
free product G = A⋆C B acting on the Bass–Serre tree, if the fixed point set of an element g ∈G has
diameter ≥3, then it contains a path of length 3. Up to conjugation, we may assume the middle
edge of such a path is the one fixed by C, in which case g lies in aCa−1 ∩C for some a ∈A \ C and
also in bCb−1 ∩C for some b ∈B \ C. In particular, the action is 2-acylindrical if aCa−1 ∩C = 1
for all a ∈A \ C or C ∩bCb−1 = 1 for all b ∈B \ C.
