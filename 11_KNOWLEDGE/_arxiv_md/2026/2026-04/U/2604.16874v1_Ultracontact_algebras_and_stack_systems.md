ULTRACONTACT ALGEBRAS AND STACK SYSTEMS
LUCA CARAI, IVO DÜNTSCH, RAFAŁ GRUSZCZYŃSKI, ANNA LAURA SUAREZ
Abstract. We study the class of structures that, in a way, generalize various
approaches to the contact relation on Boolean algebras.
Keywords: contact relation, ultracontact, Boolean algebras, Boolean contact
algebras, region-based topology, spatial logic
MSC: Primary 06E25, Secondary 03G05
1. Introduction
Boolean contact algebras (BCAs) are structures meant to study the phenomenon
of nearness of regions in an abstract setting [4, 5, 12]. As it is normally done, the
standard language of Boolean algebras is expanded with a binary predicate ‘C’ of
contact and we interpret x C y as the situation in which—in some sense—region x is
in contact with region y. In a point-based setting, we would say that x and y share
at least one point. The standard BCAs are subalgebras of the complete algebras
of regular closed subsets of topological spaces. In this context, x C y if x ∩y (the
set-theoretical intersection) is non-empty. If X is a topological space, RC(X) is its
complete Boolean algebra of regular closed sets, S is a subalgebra of RC(X), and
x, y ∈S, then ∩usually is not an operation of S, but the operation of the power set
algebra of RC(X). So the nearness of x and y is expressed by means of an operation
from beyond S. In the abstract setting, C is assumed as primitive, and the standard
axioms for it are listed in Section 6.
In this work, we aim to study an expansion of the notion of contact relations from
pairs of regions to arbitrary collections. That is, we can easily imagine a ternary
relation C3 that holds among x, y, and z if all three regions are in contact in the sense
that there is a location in space common to all three. Transferring the intuition to
the point-based environment again, it reduces to the existence of at least one point
that is in x ∩y ∩z. The ternary relation C3 allows one to distinguish between the
two configurations shown in Figure 1, which are indistinguishable from the point of
view of binary contact relations, since each pair of regions among x, y, z shares a
common point
This idea can be further generalized to arbitrary n-ary contact relations, as it was
done in [10]. However, we aim at something more far-reaching, and in a way we want
to study in one swoop all those possibilities, including infinite ones, by considering a
family K of subsets of a Boolean algebra B, such that every set M ∈K consists of
regions sharing a common location, or having «a mutual point of contact». Allowing
K to contain infinite subsets of B makes it possible, for example, to express that
the family {(0, 1
n] | n ∈ω} of subsets of the real interval (0, 1] lacks a common point
of contact; a property that cannot be captured by finitary contact relations. Thus,
we propose to study structures B := ⟨B, K ⟩where K is a family of subsets of B,
which we are going to call ultracontact algebras. In Section 9 we point to possible
ways to continue our work.

8. Stack systems
In this section, we introduce the notion of a stack system and a stack algebra that
are definitionally equivalent to ultracontact and ultracontact algebras, and offer
a different perspective on the general phenomenon we study.
Definition 8.1. We say that S ⊆Stack(B) is a stack system if the following
axioms hold for all U, V ∈Stack(B) and all x ∈B:
(SS0) ∅/∈S .
(SS1) B /∈S .
(SS2) If x ̸= 0, then ↑x ∈S .
(SS3) If ∅̸= U ⊆V and V ∈S , then U ∈S .
(SS4) If U ∩V ∈S , then U ∈S or V ∈S .
A stack algebra is a pair ⟨B, S ⟩such that B is a Boolean algebra and S is a stack
system on B.

9. Future work
The most natural continuation of the work presented above concerns finding
a proper topological representation of ultracontact algebras in the spirit of [7] and
