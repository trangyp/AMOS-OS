UNIFORM AMENABILITY AT INFINITY
NARUTAKA OZAWA
Abstract. We introduce the notion of uniform exactness, or uniform amenability at
infinity, for discrete groups and prove it for a wide class of groups containing free groups
and their limit groups. This shows a novel strong convergence phenomenon that any
convergent sequence of such groups in the space of marked groups converges strongly in
the operator algebraic sense. In particular, convergence of the spectral radius formula is
uniform over probability measures on such groups whose supports have a fixed cardinality.
1. Introduction
Amenability is one of the most important notions in analytic group theory. However,
many groups, in particular those groups that contain non-cyclic free groups, are not
amenable. It is a remarkable fact that many non-amenable groups still enjoy the weaker
yet very useful property, exactness or a.k.a. amenability at infinity ([A-D, KW]). A group
Γ is exact if the Γ-action on the Stone–ˇCech compactification βΓ, or equivalently on the
C∗-algebra ℓ∞Γ of bounded functions on Γ, is amenable. Here the action Γ ↷ℓ∞Γ is
given by (sf)(x) := f(s−1x) for s, x ∈Γ and f ∈ℓ∞Γ. For example, the class of exact
groups contains hyperbolic groups ([Ad]) and linear groups ([GHW]), but not all groups
are exact ([AD, Gr, Os]). Both amenability and exactness are inherited by subgroups.
See [AR, BO] for a comprehensive treatment on this topic.
A group Γ is said to be uniformly amenable ([Bo˙z, Ke, KS, Wy]) if its ultrapower
ΓU is amenable. Here and throughout the paper, we fix a free ultrafilter U on N. All
of the arguments do not depend on the choice of a free ultrafilter U. The ultrapower
ΓU is defined to be Q
N Γ/∼, where (sn)n ∼(tn)n if {n : sn = tn} ∈U. The element
in the ultrapower that arises from a sequence (xn)n etc will be written by [xn]n. For
example, solvable groups are uniformly amenable since being solvable of derived length
less than a fixed number is maintained by ultraproduct. On the other hand, the amenable
group S∞of finite permutations on N is not since it contains a non-cyclic free group in
the ultrapower. It is tempting to introduce the notion of uniform exactness similarly
by exactness of the ultrapower, but this appears too weak for meaningful applications.

(or more precisely, its marked isomorphism class) is in the one-to-one correspondence to

marked groups that are isomorphic to free groups. We say that a subgroup-closed class
C of groups is compact if the image of C in M (under all possible markings) is closed.
The class of limit groups is compact by definition. Every uniformly exact group has a
function k: N →N as a modulus of uniform exactness (see Section 9). The class UE
of uniformly exact groups is the union of uniformly exact groups UE(k) having k as a
modulus of uniform exactness.

(1) The class UE contains free groups and more generally torsion-free hyperbolic groups
having tame geometry at infinity (see Section 8).
(2) For every k ∈NN, the class UE(k) is closed under taking subgroups, directed
unions, and quotients by normal amenable subgroups.
(3) The class UE(k) is compact for every k ∈NN. In particular, limit groups are
uniformly exact with the same modulus of uniform exactness as free groups.

2. Main theorem for actions on Λ-trees
The following is the main technical result of this paper. The terminologies used in the
statement will be explained eventually.
Theorem 1. Let Γ be a countable group and X be a Λ-tree on which Γ acts isometrically
without inversions. Assume that the action Γ ↷X satisfies the following conditions.
(1) Every isometric embedding of an open segment (a, b) := {c ∈Λ : a < c < b} into
X extends to an isometric embedding of Λ.
(2) The set of amenable subgroups of Γ is countable.
(3) For every x, y ∈X, x ̸= y, the subgroup
{t ∈Γ : d(tx, x) + d(ty, y) ≪d(x, y)}
is amenable.
(4) The action Γ ↷X has finite Λ-rank rankΛ(Γ ↷X).

4. Real trees and the tree compactification
An R-tree (or a real tree) T is a geodesic metric space that is 0-hyperbolic. See [Bes,
Ch, Ev] for details. The Gromov product with respect to a point u ∈T is defined for
(x, y) ∈T × T by
⟨x, y⟩u := 1
2(d(x, u) + d(y, u) −d(x, y)).
An arc (resp. tripod) is a bounded complete subtree with exactly two (resp. three) end-
points. For every x, y ∈T, x ̸= y, there is a unique arc [x, y] connecting x and y. For
every x, y, u, the center w of tripod spanned by {x, y, u} satisfies d(w, u) = ⟨x, y⟩u. A
point in T is called a branching point if it is a center of tripod. A ray is the image in
T of an isometric embedding α of the interval [0, d) ⊂R, where d ∈R>0 ∪{∞}, such
that limt→d α(t) does not exist in T. The R-tree T is complete if and only if there are
no rays of finite length. The boundary ∂T is the space of the equivalence classes of rays,
where two rays are equivalent if their intersection is a ray. We write ¯T := T ∪∂T. The
Gromov product extends on ¯T × ¯T. For u ∈T and x ∈∂T, we write [u, x) for the unique
ray that represents x and has u as the endpoint, and [u, x] := [u, x) ∪{x}. We also write
[x, y) := [x, y] \ {y} and (x, y) := [x, y] \ {x, y}, which is called an open arc.
We will introduce the tree compactification ˆT of an R-tree, following the construction
for a simplicial tree (see [Bow, MS] and Section 5.2 in [BO]). It will be shown that ˆT
coincides with ¯T as a set of points. For every u, v ∈T, we define the function hu,v on T by
hu,v(x) := d(u, v)−1⟨x, v⟩u (or identically 0 if u = v). We consider the C∗-subalgebra C(T)
of ℓ∞T generated by {hu,v : u, v ∈T} and define the tree compactification ˆT of T to be
the Gelfand spectrum of the unital commutative C∗-algebra C(T). The point evaluation
at T gives rise to a dense embedding of T into ˆT. Since every hu,v is continuous w.r.t. the
original metric topology on T and has limit along every ray α, one has the following.
