Abstract—This paper develops a Doppler-aware sensing frame-
work for cell-free massive MIMO (CF-mMIMO) networks oper-
ating under OFDM-based integrated sensing and communication
(ISAC). The framework explicitly incorporates the 3D-bistatic
Doppler geometry across distributed access points (APs) into
a generalized likelihood ratio test (GLRT) detector. To address
the scalability, a user-target-centric AP association approach is
utilized. The 3D tangential components of the target’s veloc-
ity vector are estimated, and several search and optimization
strategies, including coarse grid search, gradient-based refine-
ment, and particle swarm optimization (PSO), are developed
and evaluated. The Doppler-aware GLRT statistic and receive
sensing signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) are derived. Simulation results
demonstrate that the proposed PSO-aided detector achieves the
most favorable accuracy–complexity trade-off, while Doppler
mismatch can cause substantial sensing-SNR degradation in
high-mobility scenarios. Additionally, leveraging more OFDM
subcarriers enhances frequency-domain diversity and yields fur-
ther sensing-SNR gains.
Index Terms—Integrated sensing and communications, cell-
free massive MIMO, 3D Doppler effect, PSO, OFDM.
I. INTRODUCTION
Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) is a unified
paradigm in 6G with growing relevance in applications such
as vehicular mobility [1] attracting strong interest across
both academia and industry. However, it demands careful
coordination between both functions. In terms of waveform de-
sign, the orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM)
waveform is widely used in ISAC thanks to its robustness
to frequency-selective fading, high-rate support, and favorable
range–Doppler coupling [2].
In terms of network deployment in ISAC, relying on a
single base station (BS) architectures inherently limits sensing
degrees of freedom (DoF), degrading spatial resolution and
constraining communication coverage and quality of service
(QoS). A promising solution that addresses these limitations
is cell-free massive MIMO (CF-mMIMO) architecture [3].
In a CF-mMIMO system, many distributed distributed access
points (APs), each with relatively simple hardware, coordi-
nated through central processing units (CPUs), jointly serve
each user equipment (UE). This architecture provides stable

SNR, improved interference control, and coherent-gain bene-
fits [4]. One of the most prominent deployment strategies for
CF-mMIMO is the user-centric (UC) architecture [5], which
enables scalable communication by allowing each user to be
served by a subset of APs. This concept naturally extends to
the sensing functionality of an ISAC system, where only a
selected subset of APs is assigned to serve a designated target
[6]. Moreover, a CF-mMIMO network can emulate a bistatic
or multistatic sensing configuration, thereby eliminating the
need for full-duplex operation at each AP [7].
Most ISAC studies focus on monostatic sensing configura-
tions [8]–[10] or single-BS architectures [10]–[12], which may
restrict the available DoF. Aforementioned works typically
estimate the radial velocity, which leaves the inherent line-
of-sight (LoS) ambiguity. Although [2] adopts a CF-based
framework, the final implementation relies on only a small
number of APs, contradicting the core principles of the CF
paradigm. In [13], the main emphasis is on target detection
probability, and Doppler/velocity estimation is not explicitly
treated. Hence, the current work adopts an ISAC-enabled
CF-mMIMO architecture based on an OFDM waveform to
investigate the role of accurate Doppler estimation in vehicular
environments and to quantify the impact of Doppler mismatch
on sensing performance. We examine this regime under a user-
target-centric approach formulation, explicitly accounting for
the 3D-bistatic Doppler and estimating the full 3D velocity
vector of the target, enabling accurate trajectory prediction.
The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. Sec-
tion II introduces the system model. Section III details the
sensing performance metrics. Section IV presents the numer-
ical results, and Section V summarizes the main conclusions.
II. SYSTEM MODEL

AP is equipped with Na antennas arranged either as a uni-
form linear/planar array (ULA/UPA), with the specific array

central processing unit (CPU) via high-capacity fronthaul,
which coordinates the distributed network. Fig. 1 illustrates the

A. Case Study 1
The distributed CF-mMIMO architecture largely resolves
velocity-vector ambiguity, enabling full 3D velocity estima-
tion instead of the radial-only measurements. We conduct an
experiment under 5 velocity-estimation strategies, including
PSO and gradient-based methods with random and coarse-
grid initializations (RI and CGI). As shown in Fig. 3,
the brute-force grid search serves as the worst-case com-
putational baseline, whereas PSO-RI offers the best accu-
racy–complexity trade-off. Fig. 4 reports the CDF of the
relative velocity-estimation error per component. In the major-
ity of the cases (90%), the PSO-based detector—under both
RI and CGI—achieves less than 5% error along the x and
y axis, and less than 20% along z axis, demonstrating its
strong robustness to initialization. The reduced accuracy in
the elevation component is expected, given the limited vertical
sensitivity of the ULA geometry.
B. Case Study 2
We examine the impact of Doppler compensation by com-
paring 3 scenarios: (i) a stationary target with zero Doppler;
(ii) a moving target whose 3D velocity vT is estimated using
PSO-RI; and (iii) the same moving target, but with the detector
forced to assume a zero-velocity vector. Fig. 5 reports the
resulting sensing-SNR CDFs. The results show that accurate
Doppler estimation is essential in high-mobility ISAC settings.
The proposed PSO-RI detector preserves sensing SNR across
all mobility levels, whereas neglecting Doppler—by impos-
ing a zero-velocity assumption—incurs an SNR degradation
exceeding 5 dB. The severity of this mismatch grows with

C. Case Study 3
To evaluate the influence of the OFDM structure on the
proposed PSO-RI detector, we analyze how the number of
subcarriers affects sensing performance. Unlike many existing
ISAC studies that restrict processing to a single coherence
block, we evaluate configurations with different number of
subcarriers, thereby exploiting time–frequency samples that
extend beyond the coherence bandwidth. The results indicate
that increasing Nc systematically improves the sensing SNR,
reflecting the additional frequency-domain diversity available
to the detector. In particular, Fig. 6 shows that increasing
from a single subcarrier to Nc = 24 yields more than a 10
dB improvement in sensing SNR.
V. CONCLUSIONS
This paper analyzed the impact of Doppler mismatch
in OFDM-based ISAC CF-mMIMO systems, showing that
inaccurate or ignored velocity estimation can significantly
degrade sensing performance. The proposed PSO-aided,
Doppler-aware GLRT demonstrated strong robustness across
diverse mobility conditions, reliably recovering 3D velocity
even under poor initialization. Results further showed that
leveraging richer OFDM resources improves sensing relia-
bility. Overall, the framework provides a scalable and robust
solution for high-mobility ISAC scenarios.
