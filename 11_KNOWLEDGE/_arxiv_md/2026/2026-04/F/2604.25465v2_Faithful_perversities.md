FAITHFUL PERVERSITIES
ALESSIO CIPRIANI AND JON WOOLF
Abstract. We show that the faithful highest weight hearts in an algebraic triangulated
category are the serially faithful glued hearts, equivalently the hearts containing a dual pair
of full exceptional collections in the sense of Bodzenta–Bondal [BB26]. We then characterise
faithful highest weight categories of perverse sheaves on topologically stratified spaces al-
gebraically, in terms of the exactness of certain functors, and topologically, in terms of the
vanishing of certain cohomology groups of pairwise links.
We prove that the global dimension of a faithful category of perverse sheaves on a topo-
logically stratified space X with finitely many strata is bounded by the dimension of X.
Finally, we show that in this setting the hypercohomology of a perverse sheaf can be com-
puted from a projective resolution of the constant sheaf, and conversely that the multiplicities
of the terms in a minimal projective resolution of the constant sheaf can be computed as
intersection cohomology groups.
1. Introduction
Categories of perverse sheaves on a topologically stratified space were introduced in [BBD82]
as hearts of bounded t-structures on the constructible derived category Dc(X) of a stratified
space X. Perverse sheaves play an important role in studying the topology of singular spaces
and have applications in stratified Morse and Hodge theory, geometric representation theory,
Kazhdan–Lusztig theory, and D-modules.
The category pPerv(X) of perverse sheaves on a topologically stratified space X depends
on a perversity p, that is on an assignment of an integer to each stratum of X. Among the
possible perversities one can consider, the ones which depend on the dimension and grow in a
controlled way, which we call GM-perversities — see Section 3.3 — are the most important.
For these, the category of perverse sheaves captures interesting topological information about
the space X: for instance, simple perverse sheaves are the intersection cohomology complexes
in the sense of [GM80].
When X has finitely many strata, each with finite fundamental group, the category pPerv(X)
is equivalent to the category pA(X)-mod of finite-dimensional modules over a finite dimen-
sional algebra pA(X). In this paper, we study the special situation in which pPerv(X) is a
faithful heart, i.e. in which the equivalence pPerv(X) ≃pA(X)-mod extends to a triangulated
equivalence Dc(X) ≃Db(pA(X)-mod). This is interesting because it is the case in which the
topological information in the constructible derived category is accessible from the algebra,
and vice versa. As a consequence, algebraic properties of the category pPerv(X) can be ex-
pressed in terms of topological properties of the space X, see for instance Theorem B below.
There are two main classes of examples of faithful perverse hearts coming from simplicial
topology and complex geometry respectively:

(1) The heart H ⊂D is faithful and highest weight;
(2) The sequence (∆0, . . . , ∆n) of standard objects is a full exceptional collection with

(3) The heart H contains a dual pair of full exceptional collections;
(4) The heart H is glued along a full exceptional collection and serially faithful for the

In Section 3.8 we make use of the faithfulness assumption on pPerv(X) to compute topolog-

X is a topologically stratified space with finitely many strata, each with finite fundamen-

pPerv(X) is faithful in Dc(X), the global dimension of the perverse heart is finite by Theo-
rem C. Therefore projective resolutions of objects in pPerv(X) exist and are finite. We show
that the hypercohomology of a perverse sheaf can be computed from a projective resolution of
the constant sheaf, and conversely that the multiplicities of the terms appearing in a minimal
projective resolution can be computed as intersection cohomology groups.
Theorem D (Corollary 3.24 and Corollary 3.25). Suppose X is a topologically stratified
space with finitely many strata, each with finite fundamental group. Let p be a perversity on
X such that pPerv(X) is a faithful heart in Dc(X). Then:
(1) The complex of morphisms from a projective resolution of the constant sheaf to a
perverse sheaf E computes the hypercohomology of E.
(2) When a shift of the constant sheaf is perverse the multiplicities of indecomposable
projectives in its minimal projective resolution are intersection Betti numbers.
There are dual results describing the hypercohomology in terms of an injective resolution of
the constant sheaf, and the multiplicity of each injective hull in a minimal injective resolution
when the constant sheaf is shifted perverse.
We apply the results of Section 3.8 when X is the geometric realisation of a finite simplicial
complex K. In Proposition 3.27 we show the constant sheaf on X admits a finite projective

[Gor21, §23.6]. Corollary 3.28 shows that Hom(P∗, E) gives the quiver representation of a

2. Categorical Preliminaries
This section surveys the necessary background material on triangulated categories and
hearts, introducing our notation and conventions. The material is almost all well-known,
with the exception of the notion of serially faithful heart.
2.1. Conventions and notation. We work over a field k. Let D be a Hom-finite k-linear
triangulated category with shift functor [1]. We set Extm
D (D, D′) := HomD(D, D′[m]).
By subcategory we mean strictly full subcategory, i.e. full and closed under isomorphisms.
A subcategory of D is triangulated if it is closed under shift and extensions, and thick if in
addition it is closed under taking direct summands. For a subcategory C ⊂D we denote
the smallest triangulated and thick subcategories of D containing C respectively by tr(C)
and th(C).
We let C⊥and ⊥C be the subcategories on objects D ∈D with respectively
HomD(C, D) = 0 and HomD(D, C) = 0 for all C ∈C.
For subcategories B, C ⊂D we let B∗C be the subcategory of all D ∈D sitting in a triangle
B →D →C →B[1] with B ∈B and C ∈C. A semi-orthogonal decomposition ⟨B, C⟩of D is a
pair of thick subcategories such that B = ⊥C and D = B ∗C.
2.2. Hearts and t-structures. A t-structure on the triangulated category D is a pair
(D≤0, D≥0) of additive subcategories such that the aisle D≤0 is closed under [1], the coaisle
D≥0 = (D<0)⊥, and D = D<0 ∗D≥0. Here we use the notation D≤n := D≤0[−n] and so forth.
The inclusion D≤0 ,→D has right adjoint τ ≤0, and D≥0 ,→D has left adjoint τ ≥0.
The heart of the t-structure is H := D≤0 ∩D≥0. It is an abelian subcategory of D. The
t-structure is bounded if D = S

t-structure. Henceforth by heart we will always mean heart of a bounded t-structure.

2.4. Highest weight categories. Highest weight categories were introduced in [CPS88] as
an abstraction of the representation theory of semisimple Lie algebras. We recall the definition
in the special case of a Deligne finite category; see [Kra17] for a more general notion with
coefficients in a division ring.
An abelian category A is Deligne finite if it is k-linear, Hom and Ext1-finite, length with
finitely many iso-classes of simple objects, and has enough projective objects. It follows that
A is equivalent to a module category over the endomorphism algebra of a projective generator,
and therefore that A also has enough injectives and an injective cogenerator.
For a subset A of objects of A let filt(A) be the extension closure of the objects in A.
Choose a total ordering S0, . . . , Sn of representatives of the iso-classes of simple objects in A.
The ordering determines a filtration 0 = A−1 ⊂A0 ⊂. . . ⊂An = A by the Serre subcategories
Ai = filt(S0, . . . , Si). Let Pi and Ii respectively be the projective cover and injective hull of
Si in A. The i-th standard object ∆i and costandard object ∇i are defined respectively as the
maximal quotient of Pi and the maximal subobject of Ii in Ai. These are respectively the
projective cover and injective hull of Si in Ai.
