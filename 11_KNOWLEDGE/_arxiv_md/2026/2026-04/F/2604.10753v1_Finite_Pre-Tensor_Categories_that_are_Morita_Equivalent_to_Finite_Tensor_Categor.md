FINITE PRE-TENSOR CATEGORIES THAT ARE MORITA EQUIVALENT
TO FINITE TENSOR CATEGORIES
THIBAULT DÉCOPPET AND MATEUSZ STROIŃSKI
Abstract. A finite pre-tensor category is a finite abelian category equipped with a right exact
tensor product for which every projective object has duals. Finite tensor categories, for which
every object has duals, are notable examples. More generally, the category of bimodules over an
algebra in a finite tensor category is a finite pre-tensor category. In particular, it is natural to extend
the notion of Morita equivalence between finite tensor categories to finite pre-tensor categories.
We characterize completely those finite pre-tensor categories that are Morita equivalent to finite
tensor categories. More precisely, we show that a finite pre-tensor category Cis Morita equivalent
to a finite tensor category if and only if the Drinfeld center of C is a finite tensor category. We
also discuss higher algebraic consequences of our characterization.

1. Introduction
Categorifying the notion of the center of an algebra, the Drinfeld center is a fundamental
invariant of a tensor category. As such, it is only natural to expect that the notion of Drinfeld
center behaves well with that of Morita equivalence. The first result in this direction is due to
Schauenburg [Sch], who proved, under faithful flatness assumptions, that the Drinfeld center
of the tensor category of bimodules over an algebra coincides with the Drinfeld center of the
original tensor category. The connection between Drinfeld centers and Morita equivalence is
especially strong in the theory of finite tensor categories as introduced by Etingof-Ostrik [EO].
For instance, over an algebraically closed field, two finite tensor categories are Morita equivalent
if and only if their Drinfeld centers are equivalent as finite braided tensor categories [OU].
Categorifying the notion of an algebra equipped with an involution, fiat categories were
introduced in [MM1]. Categories of Soergel bimodules considered in [MM1, Section 7.1] are
notable examples. A generalization, known as weakly (or quasi) fiat categories was presented
in [MM2]. Roughly speaking,1 a weakly fiat category is a rigid monoidal linear category with
finitely many equivalence classes of indecomposable objects and finite dimensional Hom-spaces.
Elementary examples are given by the categories of projective bimodules over a self-injective
finite dimensional algebra. Unlike finite tensor categories, weakly fiat categories are not required
to be abelian. We can nonetheless consider their abelianizations. While the abelianization of a
weakly fiat category is always a finite monoidal category, it may fail to be a tensor category — a
priori only projective objects have duals. The above construction thence provides examples of
finite pre-tensor categories, that is, finite monoidal categories for which every projective object
has duals.
In a different direction, finite pre-tensor categories also arise naturally in the context of the
cobordism hypothesis as formulated in [BD, L]. More precisely, there is a putative equivalence
between fully extended 𝑛-dimensional topological field theories valued in a target symmetric
monoidal 𝑛-category C and fully dualizable objects in C . The cobordism hypothesis therefore
produces interesting topological field theories from fully dualizable objects in symmetric monoidal
higher categories. Convenient targets are provided by higher Morita categories [H, JFS]. For
instance, there is a Morita 3-category Morten
1
of finite tensor categories and finite bimodule
categories [DS-PS2]. The fully dualizable objects therein are exactly the separable multifusion
categories, and the corresponding topological field theories are expected to be closely related to
those produced by the Turaev-Viro construction. Going up in dimension, it turns out that there is
simply no Morita 4-category of finite braided tensor categories [BJS]. Rather, there is a Morita
4-category Morpre
2
of finite braided pre-tensor categories. On the one hand, the study of fully
dualizable objects in Morpre
2
has received plenty of attention [BJS, BJSS, Déc]. On the other hand,
the dualizability properties of the Morita 3-category Morpre
1

finite bimodule categories have not yet been fully examined. In particular, there is a fully faithful
symmetric monoidal functor

fact not only relevant to the abstract analysis of the dualizability of higher Morita categories, but
can potentially also be used to extend the minimal non-degenerate extension theorem of [JFR]
beyond the semisimple case.

Recall that if C and D are Morita equivalent finite tensor categories, then Z(C) ≃Z(D) by
[EO, Corollary 3.35]. Via an analogous argument, we find that if C and D are Morita equivalent
finite pre-tensor categories, then Z(C) ≃Z(D). It follows immediately from the above discussion
that if C is a finite pre-tensor category that is Morita equivalent to a finite tensor category, then
Z(C) is a finite tensor category. Our main theorem is the converse of that statement.
Theorem 1.1. Let C be a finite pre-tensor category. Then, C is Morita equivalent to a finite tensor
category if and only if Z(C) is a finite tensor category.
Corollary 1.2. Let Cbe a finite pre-tensor category over an algebraically closed field. If the Drinfeld
center of C is trivial, i.e. Z(C) ≃Vec, then C is Morita equivalent to Vec. Specifically, there is an
equivalence of finite pre-tensor categories C ≃bimodk-𝐴for some finite dimensional k-algebra 𝐴.
Our proof has two main steps, and makes use of the following technical property: We say that a
finite pre-tensor category Cis indecomposable discrete if for every pair of projective objects 𝑃and
𝑄in C, there exists projective objects 𝑅and 𝑆along with an epimorphism 𝑅⊗𝑃⊗𝑆↠𝑄. Observe
that any finite tensor category is indecomposable discrete. It also follows from a straightforward
argument that any finite pre-tensor category that is Morita equivalent to a finite tensor category
is indecomposable discrete. The first step of our proof consists in establishing that a finite pre-
tensor category C is indecomposable discrete if its Drinfeld center Z(C) is rigid. This will allow
us to appeal to the machinery developed in [CSZ], or, more accurately, its generalization to
indecomposable discrete finite pre-tensor categories. Secondly, given a finite pre-tensor category
C whose Drinfeld center is rigid, we consider the finite left C-module category C/RadC(C) —
roughly speaking this is the quotient of the regular C-module category by its radical RadC(C).
By construction, the radical of C/RadC(C) vanishes so that Endrex
C (C/RadC(C)) is a finite tensor
category thanks to our variant of the main result of [CSZ]. Finally, we check, using the rigidity of
Z(C), that C/RadC(C) does exhibit a Morita equivalence between C and the monoidal opposite
of the finite tensor category Endrex
C (C/RadC(C)).

has trivial symmetric center, is also fully dualizable. Both of these results were subsequently
generalized in [Déc], where it was established that any finite braided tensor category whose
symmetric center is separable is fully dualizable. It is natural to expect that this last characterization
is sharp. With the aid of Theorem 1.1, we come close to answering this question.
Corollary 1.4. Let B be a finite braided pre-tensor category. If B is fully dualizable as an object of
Morpre
2 , then its symmetric center is separable.
As further motivation for the above abstract higher categorical statement, we note the above
theorem can be employed to investigate the existence of minimal non-degenerate extensions
for slightly degenerate finite braided tensor categories. Succinctly, given a finite braided tensor
category Bwhose symmetric center is the category of super vector spaces, does there always exist
a non-degenerate finite braided tensor category Athat contains Band such that the centralizer of
B in Ais the category of super vector spaces? Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
zero and under semisimplicity assumptions, this was answered positively in [JFR] using tools
from the theory of braided fusion 2-categories. Thanks to our main theorem, it is possible to
extract a braided fusion 2-category from any slightly degenerate finite braided tensor category.
This is a natural first step in the study of the existence of minimal non-degenerate extensions in
the non-semisimple setting, which we will tackle in future work.

2.2. Finite Pre-Tensor Categories. Fix a perfect field k. We assume familiarity with the content
and terminology of [EGNO]. We emphasize that we follow a different convention for duals.
Given an object 𝑉in a monoidal category C, a right dual to 𝑉is an object 𝑊of C together with
morphisms 𝜂∶1 →𝑊⊗𝑉and 𝜀∶𝑉⊗𝑊→1 satisfying the zigzag equations. If a right dual
to 𝑉exists, it is essentially unique. In this case, we denote the right dual to 𝑉by 𝑉∨, and we say
that 𝑉is a right rigid object. We say that the monoidal category Cis right rigid if all of its objects
are right rigid. Similar considerations apply to left duals and left rigidity. Provided it exists, we
denote the left dual to 𝑉by ∨𝑉. We say that a monoidal category is rigid if it is both left and right
rigid. Recall the following fundamental definition:
Definition 2.7. A finite multitensor category is a finite abelian rigid monoidal k-linear category.
A finite tensor category is a finite multitensor category whose monoidal unit 1 is a simple object.
We will be interested in the following more general notion which can be traced back to [BJS].
Definition 2.8. A finite pre-multitensor category is a finite abelian monoidal k-linear category
whose monoidal product is right exact in both variables, and whose projective objects are rigid. A
finite pre-tensor category is a finite pre-multitensor category whose monoidal unit 1 is indecom-
posable.
Example 2.9. Let 𝐴be a finite-dimensional k-algebra. The relative tensor product ⊗𝐴endows
the category bimodk-𝐴of 𝐴-𝐴-bimodules with a monoidal structure. It is a folklore result that
bimodk-𝐴is a finite pre-multitensor category (see e.g. [FSSW, Lemma 5.5] for a recent account).
In more detail, the left rigid objects of bimodk-𝐴are precisely the 𝐴-𝐴-bimodules that are
projective as left 𝐴-modules. Explicitly, if 𝑀is such an 𝐴-𝐴-bimodule, its left dual is given by
Hom𝐴-(𝑀, 𝐴). Similarly, the right rigid objects are exactly the 𝐴-𝐴-bimodules that are projective
as right 𝐴-modules. If 𝑁is such an 𝐴-𝐴-bimodule, its right dual is given by Hom-𝐴(𝑁, 𝐴). We
emphasize that these dual objects are not necessarily rigid themselves.
Lemma 2.10. Let C be a finite pre-multitensor category. For a projective object 𝑃and a right rigid
object 𝑉in C, the object 𝑉⊗𝑃is projective. Similarly, for a left rigid object 𝑊in C, the object
𝑃⊗𝑊is projective.
Proof. We consider the case when a right rigid object 𝑉∈C is given. The other is similar. We
have HomC(𝑉⊗𝑃, −) ≅HomC(𝑃, 𝑉∨⊗−). The former functor is clearly left exact, the latter
is right exact, being a composite of two right exact functors. This establishes the exactness of
HomC(𝑉⊗𝑃, −) and hence the projectivity of 𝑉⊗𝑃.
□

where ideals would correspond to closed subsets of the spectrum of a Z+-nearring, this would
