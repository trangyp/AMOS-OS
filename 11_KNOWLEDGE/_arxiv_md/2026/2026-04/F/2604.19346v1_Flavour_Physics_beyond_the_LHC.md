Abstract
The next 20 years will be the golden age of flavour physics, with the operation of the
LHCb and Belle II experiments. After that an e+e−collider could further improve
the precision with sizeable Z, W +W −and tt runs.

Introduction
1
“Flavour physics highlights the virtue of virtuality” wrote Eric Laenen in the ESPPU
2
Briefing Book [1]. While colliders access new physics by creating new particles, flavour
3
and electroweak physics provide tests that are sensitive to the ripples of new physics at
4
much lower energies.
5
For instance the absence of bs→Z couplings in the SM makes flavour-changing neutral
6
current processes sensitive to new physics that can hide in loops. The B →K∗µ+µ−
7
process is a benchmark process that gives access to multiple angular distributions. One of
8
those, dubbed P ′
5, is well known for deviating from the SM. Figure 1 (left) shows the most
9
precise measurements [2–7] versus the dilepton mass squared. Also, multiple branching
10
fractions are in tension with the Standard Model, as shown in Fig. 1 (right). It is too early
11
to say whether these effects are due to new physics of nonfactorisable QCD contributions,
12
but much larger samples will tell us.
13
Flavour physics also provides the only known source of CP violation in the standard
14
model1 via the KM mechanism [10]. The unitarity conditions of this matrix provide
15
precision tests of the standard model as shown in Fig. 3. There are also intriguing results
16
in semileptonic b→cτν processes, and a long-standing discrepancy in the determinations
17
of the CKM matrix elements Vub and Vcb by inclusive and exclusive methods [11].
18
2

22

23

Flavour physics at the Z pole
34
Owing to the lower cross-section, b-physics at the Z pole cannot compete with the LHC
35
in sheer numbers, see Fig. 4. However, the Z environment is cleaner, which permits a
36
much improved tagging efficiency [16]. It is however less clean than runs at the Υ(4S)
37
where only the two B mesons are produced (Fig. 4). A Z run is therefore somewhere in
38
between LHC and Belle II both in terms of signal yields and background levels.
39
A tera-Z run would exploit this environment, as well as very precise detectors, for few
40
selected processes as time-dependent B0
s →µ+µ−or B0
s →ϕµ+µ−, b→sνν, B →D(∗)τν,
41
as well as rare τ decays [1,16].
42
A benchmark process is B0 →K∗0τ +τ −, which is sensitive to the same physics as
43
B →D(∗)τν but at the loop level. Assuming the SM, a run with 6 × 1012 Z bosons would
44
just about allow for an evidence. The situation improves with a very thin beam pipe
45
restricting multiple scattering to less than 2 µm, see Fig 5, and assuming all detectors are
46
equally equipped for flavour physics.

Flavour physics beyond the Z pole
48
A close inspection of Fig. 3 shows minor improvements of the CKM unitarity triangle
49
from a Z run. The remaining dominating uncertainties affect the sides of the triangle
50
facing β, and the ϵK band. In both cases the uncertainty relates to |Vub| and |Vcb| which
51
are affected by the inclusive versus exclusive discrepancy. A measurement of |Vcb| from W
52
decays rather than from semileptonic b decays would likely solve the conundrum. Here
53
however, the dominating uncertainly is of systematic nature, related to the knowledge of
54
the jet flavour-tagging efficiency, see Fig. 5.
55
Similarly, the precision on the other triangle side is dominated by |Vts|, which is
56
obtained from B0
s oscillations. A direct measurement could be obtained from t→sW +
57
processes, provided a significant run beyond the tt threshold is recorded.2
58
5
Conclusion
59
The golden age of flavour is now. With the Belle II and LHCb Upgrade II experiments
60
unprecedented precision will be obtained. An e+e−collider could after that address some
61
remaining issues. For a full understanding large W +W −and tt runs are as important as a
62
tera-Z run. Additionally, all experiments must be equipped for flavour physics, including
63
sufficient PID capabilities and exquisite vertexing.
