Finite Kripke models and provability
interpretations in quantified modal logic
Haruka Kogure∗†and Taishi Kurahashi‡§
Abstract
In this paper, we investigate arithmetical completeness with respect
to finite Kripke models of quantified modal logic. We adapt the finite-
model embedding techniques of Artemov and Japaridze to two settings
involving finite Kripke models. First, for conversely well-founded finite
Kripke models of quantified modal logic, we construct a Σ2 Fefermanian
provability predicate together with an arithmetical interpretation that
embeds the model into arithmetic.
Second, for finite constant domain
Kripke models of quantified modal logic, we construct a Σ1 provability
predicate satisfying D2G and an arithmetical interpretation yielding such
an embedding.
1
Introduction
Solovay’s arithmetical completeness theorem [24], which states that the modal
propositional logic GL coincides exactly with the provability logic of PA, has
become a fundamental cornerstone in the study of provability predicates via
modal logic. In particular, the method of embedding transitive and conversely
well-founded finite Kripke frames into arithmetic by means of the so-called Solo-
vay function remains a central and indispensable technique to this day (see the
survey papers [13, 3] on provability logic for several applications of this method).
In recent years, the authors have established arithmetical completeness results
for K and for several non-normal modal logics. For instance, the arithmeti-
cal completeness theorem for the modal propositional logic K with respect to
Σ2 Fefermanian provability predicates was established in [16]. The key tech-
nique used in its proof is again an application of Solovay-style constructions.
In addition, the arithmetical completeness theorem for K with respect to Σ1
provability predicates was proved in [20].
An overview of recent studies on
arithmetical completeness for several logics is given in [14].

It is natural to attempt to extend this framework from propositional logic
to predicate logic. However, it has been shown that many of the positive results
obtained in the propositional setting do not extend to the predicate case. For
example, Montagna [23] proved that the predicate extension QGL of GL is nei-
ther Kripke complete nor arithmetically complete. Artemov [1] proved that the
quantified truth provability logic of PA is not arithmetical, and Vardanyan [25]
proved that the quantified provability logic of PA is Π0
2-complete (see also related
results by Berarducci [6], Boolos and McGee [7], and McGee [22]). Furthermore,
Artemov [2] showed that the quantified provability logic of PA depends on the
choice of formulas representing PA (see also [15, 19] for related results). These
results indicate that, unlike in the propositional case, it is nontrivial to control
quantified provability logic in a systematic way.
On the other hand, some positive results have been obtained. Among them,
in this paper, we focus on the work of Artemov and Dzhaparidze, currently
spelled Japaridze [4]. They showed that, for finite Kripke models, Solovay’s
method can be extended from propositional logic to predicate logic. More pre-
cisely, they proved that for every transitive and conversely well-founded Kripke
model of quantified modal logic with finitely many worlds and finite domains,
and for every formula A that is not valid in that model, there exists an arith-
metical interpretation f such that PA ⊬f(A). Moreover, Japaridze [8] further
refined this result by providing more explicit sufficient conditions under which
a Kripke model can be embedded into arithmetic.
The aim of the present paper is to combine the method of Artemov and
Japaridze [4] with the results on K obtained in [16, 20]. Our main results are
as follows.
1. For each conversely well-founded finite Kripke model of quantified modal
logic, there exist a Σ2 Fefermanian provability predicate of PA and a corre-
sponding arithmetical interpretation such that the model can be embedded
into arithmetic (Theorem 3.2).
2. For each finite constant domain Kripke model of quantified modal logic,
there exist a Σ1 provability predicate of PA satisfying the global version
D2G of the derivability condition D2 and a corresponding arithmetical
interpretation such that the model can be embedded into arithmetic (The-
orem 4.1).
The first result can be obtained by a straightforward combination of the ar-
guments of Artemov and Japaridze with those of [16]. Therefore, the technical
contribution of this paper lies in the second result. Unlike the first result, it can-
not be obtained merely by combining existing arguments. In order to connect
quantified modal logic with arithmetic, one must overcome substantial difficul-
ties arising from the treatment of quantifiers and substitutions of numerals. Our
proof introduces essentially new ideas to address these issues.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce preliminaries
on quantified modal logic, provability predicates, and arithmetical interpreta-
tions. In Section 3, we prove the first main theorem stating that conversely

Preliminaries
This section consists of three subsections. In Subsection 2.1, we recall some
basics of quantified modal logic. In Subsection 2.2, we introduce formal arith-
metic and provability predicates. In Subsection 2.3, we introduce arithmetical
interpretations, which connect quantified modal logic and formal arithmetic. In
particular, we explain why we prove the two kinds of theorems mentioned in
the introduction.
2.1
Quantified modal logic
The language of quantified modal logic consists of the following symbols:
• countably many variables x, y, z, . . .,
• for each n ∈ω, countably many n-ary predicate symbols P, Q, . . .,
• logical symbols ¬, ∧, ∨, →, ∀, ∃,
• logical constants ⊤, ⊥,
• unary modal operator □.
The modal operator ♢is defined by ♢:≡¬□¬.
Definition 2.1 (The logic QK). The quantified modal logic QK is defined as
follows.
• Axioms:
– All axioms of first-order classical logic,
– The universal closures of all instances of □(A →B) →(□A →□B).
• Rules:
– Modus Ponens: from A and A →B, infer B,
– Generalization: from A, infer ∀xA,
– Necessitation: from A, infer □A.

The quantified modal logic QGL is obtained from QK by adding the uni-
versal closures of all instances of □(□A →A) →□A as axioms.
Definition 2.2 (Kripke frames). A Kripke frame for quantified modal logic is
a triple F = (W, R, {Dw}w∈W ), where
• W is a nonempty set,
• R is a binary relation on W,
• for each w ∈W, Dw is a nonempty set,
• if xRy, then Dx ⊆Dy.
We say a Kripke frame F is finite if W and all Dw are finite sets. A Kripke
frame F is said to be constant domain if Dx = Dy for all x, y ∈W. When a
Kripke frame is constant domain, we write F = (W, R, D).
Definition 2.3 (Kripke models). A Kripke model is a tuple M = (W, R, {Dw}w∈W , ⊩
), where ⊩is a relation between elements of W and atomic formulas with pa-
rameters from the corresponding domains, extended inductively as follows: For
each w ∈W,
• w ⊮⊥and w ⊩⊤,
• w ⊩¬A ⇐⇒w ⊮A,
• w ⊩A ∧B ⇐⇒w ⊩A and w ⊩B,
• w ⊩A ∨B ⇐⇒w ⊩A or w ⊩B,
• w ⊩A →B ⇐⇒w ⊮A or w ⊩B,
• w ⊩∀xA ⇐⇒∀a ∈Dw (w ⊩A(a)),
• w ⊩∃xA ⇐⇒∃a ∈Dw (w ⊩A(a)),
• w ⊩□A ⇐⇒∀v ∈W (wRv ⇒v ⊩A).
We say that a formula A is valid in a model M if w ⊩A holds for all w ∈W,
and valid in a frame F if A is valid in all models based on F.
Definition 2.4 (Generated submodel). Let M = (W, R, {Dw}w∈W , ⊩) be a
Kripke model and let w ∈W. The generated submodel of M by w is the Kripke
model Mw = (Ww, R↾Ww, {Dv}v∈Ww, ⊩↾Ww), where Ww := {v ∈W | wR∗v}
and R∗is the reflexive transitive closure of R.
Lemma 2.5 (Generated submodel lemma). Let M = (W, R, {Dw}w∈W , ⊩) be
a Kripke model, let w ∈W, and let A be a modal sentence. Then w ⊩M A if
and only if w ⊩Mw A.
A Kripke model M = (W, R, {Dw}w∈W , ⊩) is said to be rooted if there exists
r ∈W such that W = {w | rR∗w}. Such an element r is called a root of the
model.

Theorem 2.6 (cf. Fitting and Mendelsohn [11]). The quantified modal logic
QK is Kripke complete. That is, for any modal sentence A, the following are
equivalent:
1. QK ⊢A.
2. A is valid in all Kripke frames.
Let QKbf be the logic obtained from QK by adding the universal closures
of all instances of Barcan formula ∀x□A(x) →□∀xA(x) as axioms.
Theorem 2.7 (cf. Fitting and Mendelsohn [11]). The quantified modal logic
QKbf is Kripke complete with respect to constant domain Kripke frames. That
is, for any modal sentence A, the following are equivalent:
1. QKbf ⊢A.
2. A is valid in all constant domain Kripke frames.
The following theorem is a consequence of one of Wolter and Zakharyaschev’s
results [26].
Theorem 2.8 (Wolter and Zakharyaschev [26, Corollary 5.7]). Let A be a modal
sentence in which only one variable appears. Then, the following are equivalent:
1. QKbf ⊢A.
2. A is valid in all finite constant domain Kripke frames.
A Kripke frame is said to be conversely well-founded if it has no infinite
R-increasing chains of elements. As in the propositional case, a Kripke frame is
transitive and conversely well-founded if and only if every modal sentence that
is a theorem of QGL is valid in that frame. On the other hand, the following
fact is known.
Theorem 2.9 (Montagna [23]). The quantified modal logic QGL is Kripke in-
complete. That is, there exists a modal sentence which is valid in every transitive
and conversely well-founded Kripke frame, but is not provable in QGL.

Let τ(v) be a formula weakly representing the theory T in PA, that is, for
any LA-sentence φ, φ ∈T if and only if PA ⊢τ(⌜φ⌝). τ(v) is not necessarily
the standard primitive recursive definition of T.
Using τ, we can uniformly
construct a formula Prτ(x) saying that x is provable in a theory defined by τ in
a natural way. The formula Prτ(x) is called a Fefermanian provability predicate.
This notion was originally introduced by Feferman [10]. It is known that if τ(v)
is Σn for n > 0, then Prτ(x) is also a Σn formula.
Theorem 2.16 (Feferman [10]). Every Fefermanian provability predicate sat-
isfies D1U, D2G, and Σ1CU.
2.3
Arithmetical interpretations
To connect quantified modal logic with arithmetic, we introduce arithmetical
interpretations.
Definition 2.17 (Arithmetical interpretations). Let PrT (x) be a provability
predicate of T. An arithmetical interpretation based on PrT (x) is a map f from
modal formulas to LA-formulas having the same free variables satisfying the
following conditions:
• if P is an n-ary predicate symbol and f(P(x0, . . . , xn−1)) ≡φ(x0, . . . , xn−1),
then f(P(y0, . . . , yn−1)) ≡φ(y0, . . . , yn−1),
• f(A ◦B) ≡f(A) ◦f(B) for ◦∈{∧, ∨, →},
• f(¬A) ≡¬f(A),
• f(QxA) ≡Qx f(A) for Q ∈{∀, ∃},
• f(□A(x0, . . . , xn−1)) ≡PrT (⌜f(A)( ˙x0, . . . , ˙xn−1)⌝).
Definition 2.18. Let PrT (x) be a provability predicate of T. The quantified
provability logic QPL(PrT ) of PrT (x) is the set of all modal sentences A such
that T ⊢f(A) for all arithmetical interpretations f based on PrT (x).
It is easily verified that if PrT (x) satisfies D1U and D2U, then QPL(PrT )
contains QK. Indeed, D1U yields the arithmetical soundness of necessitation,
and D2U yields the arithmetical soundness of the K-axiom in the framework
of quantified modal logic. In particular, if Prτ(x) is a Fefermanian provability

if PrT (x) satisfies D1U and D2U, then by Theorem 2.14, it also satisfies Σ1CU.
Therefore, if moreover PrT (x) is a Σ1 provability predicate, then it satisfies
D3U. Hence, by Theorem 2.15, PrT (x) satisfies the formalized L¨ob theorem
with parameters. In particular, QPL(PrT ) contains QGL.
Thus, for a Fefermanian provability predicate Prτ(x), if τ(x) is a Σ1 formula,
then QPL(Prτ) contains QGL. However, in this case, Vardanyan [25] proved
that the quantified provability logic of Prτ(x) is Π0
2-complete. In particular, it
has strictly higher arithmetical complexity than QGL and hence cannot coincide
with QGL.
On the other hand, Artemov and Japaridze proved the following significant
arithmetical completeness result with respect to finite Kripke frames.
Theorem 2.19 (Artemov and Japaridze [4]). Let τ(x) be a Σ1 formula weakly
representing T in PA. For any modal sentence A, if A ∈QPL(Prτ), then A is
valid in all transitive and conversely well-founded finite Kripke frames.
They proved this theorem by constructing an appropriate arithmetical inter-
pretation from each transitive conversely well-founded finite Kripke model by
extending Solovay’s method to the framework of quantified modal logic.
The aim of the present paper is to extend this connection between arithmeti-
cal interpretations and finite Kripke frames which are not necessarily transitive
nor conversely well-founded. For this purpose, one must find provability pred-
icates PrT (x) for which QPL(PrT ) does not necessarily contain QGL. There
are essentially two possible ways to achieve this. One is to drop the assumption
that PrT (x) is a Σ1 provability predicate. The other is to consider provability
predicates which do not satisfy at least one of D1U and D2U.
In Section 3, we establish a result based on Σ2 Fefermanian provability
predicates. In Section 4, we establish a result based on Σ1 provability predicates
satisfying D2G but not necessarily D1U.

In this section, we prove that for any finite constant domain Kripke model
of quantified modal logic, there exists a Σ1 provability predicate PrT (x) of T
satisfying D2G and an arithmetical interpretation based on PrT (x) such that the
model can be embedded into arithmetic. The main difference from the result
in Section 3 is that the provability predicate PrT (x) obtained in this section
is not Fefermanian. As discussed in Subsection 2.3, the provability predicate
constructed here does not necessarily satisfy D1U. Another important point is
that, unlike in Section 3, we assume that Kripke models have constant domains
rather than being conversely well-founded.
In [20], a method was developed for embedding arbitrary finite Kripke models
of propositional modal logic into arithmetic by using Σ1 provability predicates.
However, this method cannot be directly extended to quantified modal logic.
The reason is that, when considering arithmetical interpretations of quantified
modal logic, one must carefully deal with free variables and substitutions of

## Page 12


numerals.
To carry out the argument of [20] in this setting, the provability
predicate would be required to satisfy both D1U and D2U.
However, the
provability predicates we aim to construct cannot be guaranteed to satisfy both
of these conditions as we mentioned in Subsection 2.3. Therefore, it is necessary
to develop a different proof method.
Our strategy is as follows. We first construct a function h corresponding to
a Solovay function. The function h starts with h(0) = 0 and makes at most
one transition to a nonzero value. We then define λ(x) by ∃y (h(y) = x). In
contrast to the construction of Artemov and Japaridze [4], we can define the
formulas θk(x) in a simpler way by using the assumption that the given finite
Kripke frame is constant domain.
A crucial point is that, although the definition of h involves complicated
conditions related to quantifiers, substitutions, and tautological consequence,
the function h is in fact primitive recursive. This fact plays a key role in our
proof. Since the proof of this fact is somewhat lengthy, we postpone it to the
Appendix. As a consequence, λ(x) is a Σ1 formula. With these preparations in
hand, it remains to show that finite constant domain Kripke models can in fact
be embedded into arithmetic.
Before proving the theorem, we prepare several notions and notation. An

interpretation f based on Prg(x). By Lemma 4.10, we have PA ⊢λ(1) →¬f(A).

The constant domain assumption plays a substantial role in the present
proof. Since the provability predicate constructed in this section is not assumed
to satisfy D1U, the existing methods of Artemov and Japaridze [4] and of [20]
cannot be applied directly. Therefore, we introduced a different mechanism such
that the function h is allowed to change its value according to the activation
condition, which refers to the behavior of the function g.
This mechanism
enables us to prove Lemma 4.10 without relying on D1U. In the expanding
domain case, however, we do not know how to define the formulas θk(x) so as
to fit the present construction. For this reason, we restricted Theorem 4.1 to
constant domain Kripke models. We leave open whether this restriction can be
removed.
