FAILURE OF WEAK APPROXIMATION IN ADJOINT
GROUPS
CHAYANSUDHA BISWAS
Abstract. Platonov in 1991 conjectured that adjoint groups are ra-
tional as varieties over arbitrary infinite fields, and as a consequence
have weak approximation. The rationality part of the conjecture was
disproved by Merkurjev in 1996, but the question about weak approxi-
mation remained open. We settle this in the negative.
Introduction
A generalization of the Chinese Remainder Theorem is the property of
weak approximation of a field: if S is any finite set of inequivalent discrete
valuations on a field F, then the image of F is dense in Q
v∈S Fv under the
diagonal embedding, where Fv denotes the completion of F with respect to
a valuation v ∈S [PR94, Theorem 1.4]. The topology considered here is the
product adic topology. If X is a variety defined over the field F, then one
can ask if the image of X(F) is dense in Q
v∈S X(Fv) under the diagonal
embedding. If this is the case, then we say that X has weak approximation
with respect to S. We say that X has weak approximation if it has weak
approximation with respect to every finite set of discrete valuations of F.
All rational varieties have weak approximation, and hence failure of weak
approximation is an obstruction to rationality.
Weak approximation in the setting of algebraic groups is classical and
well understood in many cases.
For instance, it is well established that
all semisimple simply connected and semisimple adjoint groups over global
fields have weak approximation. The same statement is known to be false
(due to work by Platonov in [Pla76]) over arbitrary valued fields in the sim-
ply connected case, motivating a focus on adjoint groups instead. Platonov
conjectured all semisimple adjoint groups over arbitrary fields are rational
as varieties, and therefore have weak approximation [PR94, §7.3]. The ratio-
nality part of the conjecture was disproved by Merkurjev in [Mer96], where
examples were given of adjoint groups that fail rationality. More examples
of nonrational adjoint groups can be found in [Gil97], [BMT04] and [Bha14].
The obstruction to rationality in these examples is the failure of univer-
sal R-triviality: for a group G defined over a field F, we say two points

We say the group G is universally R-trivial if G(E)/R = 1 for all field ex-

It was still not known, however, if there exist adjoint groups failing weak
approximation. Over arbitrary fields, weak approximation has been shown
for many cases of adjoint groups by Th˘a´ng in [Th˘a96], but the question
remained open in general. To the author’s knowledge, no further progress
has been made towards the conjecture since.
In the present article, we will settle the problem by constructing adjoint
groups that fail weak approximation (see Theorem 11, and see Example 12
for an explicit example). For every global field F of characteristic different
from 2 in which −1 is a square, we will give a recipe to construct infinitely
many adjoint groups, defined over a suitable overfield of F, that fail weak
approximation.
To construct our counterexamples, we fix such a field F, and using a
criterion of Merkurjev we first construct an adjoint group G of type Dn
over F such that G(F)/R = 1 but G(E)/R ̸= 1 for some extension E
over F. The main trick is to slightly modify the situation above to obtain a
field K over F which admits a discrete valuation v such that G(K)/R = 1
and G(Kv)/R ̸= 1.
We then use a result of Raghunathan to show that
every Kv-rational point g of G has a v-adic neighborhood Ug contained in
the R-equivalence class of that point. In particular, we consider g ∈G(Kv)
not in the R-equivalence class of 1. Weak approximation in G would imply
that Ug has nonempty intersection with G(K). But G(K)/R = 1 implies
that points in this intersection are R-equivalent to the identity, which gives
us a contradiction.
1. Constructing adjoint groups failing universal R-triviality
The aim of this section is the construction of adjoint groups that fail
universal R-triviality. This is done in Proposition 2, using a criterion due to
Merkurjev. Proposition 2 will later be used in §2 to prove the main theorem.
The following proposition is the main technical ingredient:
Proposition 1. Let F be any global field with char F ̸= 2 in which −1
is a square.
Then there is a quaternion algebra Q over F and an ele-
ment d ∈F ×\F ×2 such that d ∈−Nrd(Q) and d /∈Nrd(Q+). Here Q+ de-
notes the subspace of Q consisting of the pure quaternions, and Nrd: Q →F
is the reduced norm.
For F and Q as in the proposition, let A = Mn(Q), for n ≥3 an odd
integer. Then the hypothesis of [Mer96, Proposition 11] is satisfied, and

Proposition 2. For any global field F with char F ̸= 2 in which −1 is a

Our next goal is to show that the base change of G to a suitable overfield
of F fails weak approximation. We begin with the following lemma:
Lemma 8. There is a field K containing F, and a valuation v on K with
corresponding completion Kv, such that G(K)/R = 1 and G(Kv)/R ̸= 1.
Proof. Choose a separating transcendence basis {e1, . . . , en} of E over F.
Then the field F ′ = F(e1, . . . , en) is a purely transcendental extension over
F while E is a separable algebraic extension of F ′. By the Primitive Element
Theorem, E = F ′(α) for some α ∈E.
Let fα ∈F ′[t] be the minimal polynomial of the element α. Then we
may endow the field K = F ′(t) with valuation v induced by the prime ideal
(fα) in F ′[t]. Observe that the corresponding residue field κv is isomorphic
to E.
Denote by Kv the completion of K with respect to v.
Observe
that G(Kv)/R ≃G(κv)/R ≃G(E)/R ̸= 1, where the first isomorphism

On the
other hand, G(K)/R ≃G(F ′)/R ≃G(F)/R = 1 by homotopy invariance
(see [Gil10, p.1]).
□
The next two lemmas prepare the key step in our argument: we show that
the R-equivalence class of any g ∈G(Kv) is v-adically open. This means, in
particular, that a Kv-rational point g lying outside the R-equivalence class
of 1 cannot be approximated by points R-equivalent to 1.

We are now ready to state and prove the main theorem:
Theorem 11. There exists an adjoint group G defined over a field K, with
a discrete valuation v and corresponding completion Kv, such that the em-
bedding G(K) →G(Kv) does not have v-adically dense image. In particular,
G does not have weak approximation.
Proof. Let F be any global field of characteristic different from 2 in which
−1 is a square. Let G be as in Proposition 2 and let K, v and Kv be as in
Lemma 8.
Let g ∈G(Kv) be a Kv-rational point that is not in the R-equivalence
class of 1. By Lemma 10, there is a v-adically open neighborhood U of g in
G(Kv) that is contained in the R-equivalence class of g.
If G had weak approximation, then there would be an element h ∈G(K)∩
U ⊂G(Kv). Hence, h is R-equivalent to g. Since G(K)/R = 1, the identity
element 1 would be in the R-equivalence class of the element h in G(K) and
therefore also in G(Kv). This is a contradiction since g was chosen to be
not R-equivalent to 1.
□
