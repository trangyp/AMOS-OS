Free Poisson Rota—Baxter algebra
V. Gubarev
Abstract
We construct a free Poisson algebra endowed with a Rota—Baxter operator.
The same construction works for a free Poisson algebra endowed with a Nijenhuis
operator.
Keywords: Rota—Baxter algebra, Poisson algebra, Nijenhuis operator.
1
Introduction
A linear operator R defined on an algebra A over a field F is called a Rota—Baxter
operator (RB-operator for short) of weight λ ∈F if the following relation holds:
R(x)R(y) = R(R(x)y + xR(y) + λxy),
x, y ∈A.
An algebra equipped with an RB-operator defined on it is called a Rota?Baxter algebra
(RB-algebra for short).
The study of commutative Rota—Baxter algebras originated in a 1960 paper by
G. Baxter [7], which addressed a specific problem in analysis. Subsequently, G.-C. Rota
and others [31, 11] explored the combinatorial properties of RB-operators and algebras.
A significant development occurred in the 1980s with the discovery of a close connection
between Lie RB-algebras and solutions to the classical Yang—Baxter equation [8, 33].
Today, RB-operators find extensive applications across mathematical physics, combina-
torics, number theory, and operad theory [4, 16, 22, 23].
In the works of G.-C. Rota, P. Cartier, and L. Guo [31, 11, 23], various constructions
were proposed to describe free commutative RB-algebras. In 2008, K. Ebrahimi-Fard
and L. Guo [17] provided a description of free associative RB-algebras using rooted trees.
Subsequently, in 2010, L.A. Bokut et al. [9] characterized these algebras through the
use of Gr¨obner—Shirshov bases. In 2016–17, different bases of free Lie RB-algebras were
constructed in the works of the author [19], the author and P. Kolesnikov [21], and J. Qiu
and Y. Chen [30].
In 2000, M. Aguiar [1] defined and studied Rota—Baxter operators of weight zero on
Poisson algebras. By the definition, an RB-operator on a Poisson algebra has to satisfy
Rota—Baxter identity with respect to both operations. Therein, he defined a prePois-
son algebra, a vector space endowed with two bilinear operations ◦and ∗such that ◦is
precommutative, ∗is preLie, and two analogues of the Leibniz identity hold. M. Aguair
showed that every RB-operator R of weight zero on a given Poisson algebra (A, ·, [, ]) de-
fines a prePoisson algebra structure on A as follows: x◦y = R(x)y, x∗y = [R(x), y]. Fur-
ther, different generalizations of prePoisson algebras have appeared [3, 12, 25]. In 2013,
the notion of postPoisson algebra and their connection with RB-operators of nonzero
weight on a Poisson algebra were established in [26]. In 2013 [20], it was proved, in
particular, that every pre- and postPoisson algebra injectively embeds into its univer-
sal enveloping Poisson RB-algebra. Koszul dual structures to prePoisson algebras were

In this paper, we construct a basis of the free Poisson RB-algebra. It is known that
we may construct a basis of the free Poisson algebra generated by a set X as follows.
By the Leibniz identity [x, y · z] = [x, y] · z + y · [x, z], we may assume that we ini-
tially compute all Lie products and only afterwards apply the commutative products.
Hence, one may conclude that Pois⟨X⟩∼= Com⟨Lie⟨X⟩⟩[32]. To obtain the free Rota—
Baxter Poisson algebra, we apply some similar strategy. Now, we apply the relation
[a, R(b) · R(c)] = [a, R(b)] · R(c) + [a, R(c)] · R(b) to avoid on the Lie algebra level the
leading part of all products of the form R(b) · R(c). Since we may apply operator R
any number of times, we have a countable numbers of steps of the form: RB Lie algebra
level, then commutative RB level. Roughly speaking, we construct RBPois⟨X⟩inside
. . . RBCom⟨RBLie⟨. . . RBCom⟨RBLie⟨X⟩⟩. . .⟩⟩. . . subject to certain prescribed restric-
tions. We propose a potential basis and then define on the linear span of it all operations
of Rota—Baxter Poisson algebra and finally prove that the resulting RB Poisson algebra
is free.
We apply the obtained basis for the free Poisson algebra to construct the free NS-
Poisson algebra, i. e. a free Poisson algebra endowed with a Nijenhuis operator. Recall
that a Nijenhuis operator defined on an algebra A satisfies the identity
N(x)N(y) = N(N(x)y + xN(y) −N(xy)),
x, y ∈A.
Such operators on Lie algebras appeared in 1979 [18] in the context of Hamiltonian
pairs. Since 2000, Nijenhuis operators have been studied on associative algebras as well,
see [10, 15]. By analogy with how one may obtain pre- and postalgebras of a variety Var
starting with an algebra from Var endowed with a Rota—Baxter operator, several au-
thors introduced NS-associative algebras [24], NS-Lie algebras [12] etc. defining them
via Nijnehuis operators. The general approach to defining NS-Var-algebras for a given
variety Var was proposed recently [28].
In [6], Nijenhuis operators on Poisson algebras as well as NS-Poisson algebras were
defined and studied. There it was shown that the semi-classical limit of an NS-algebra
deformation and a suitable filtration of an NS-algebra produce NS-Poisson algebras.
Free associative and Lie NS-algebras were constructed in [15] and in [19], respectively;
in both cases, they share the same basis as the free Rota—Baxter algebra of the corre-
sponding variety. Thus, it is not surprising that we are able to define a free NS-Poisson
algebra structure on the basis previously constructed for the free RB Poisson algebra.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 provides fundamental definitions along
with several examples of Poisson RB-algebras. Section 3 presents preliminaries on free
commutative RB-algebras. In Section 4, we construct free Rota—Baxter Poisson algebra.
In Section 5, we apply the obtained basis to construct the free NS-Poisson algebra.

Definition 1. A vector space V endowed with two bilinear products · and [, ] is
called a Poisson algebra, if (V, ·) is a commutative algebra, (V, [, ]) is a Lie algebra, and
the following relation holds:
[a, bc] = [a, b]c + b[a, c]
(1)
for all a, b, c ∈V .
The identity (1) is called a Leibniz identity, and the bracket [, ] is called the Poisson
bracket.
Definition 2. Let A be a Poisson algebra. A linear operator R is called a Rota—
Baxter operator (RB-operator, for short) of weight λ on A, if R satisfies the following
identities:
R(x)R(y) = R(R(x)y + xR(y) + λxy),
(2)
[R(x), R(y)] = R([R(x), y] + [x, R(y)] + λ[x, y]).
(3)
A Poisson algebra with a Rota—Baxter operator is called a Poisson Rota—Baxter alge-
bra.

Definition 3. A vector space A endowed with two bilinear products ◦and ∗is called
a prePoisson algebra, if (A, ◦) is a precommutative algebra, (A, ∗) is a preLie algebra,
and the following identities hold:

The number of appearances of the symbol R in the notation of an element w of the
standard basis of a free commutative RB-algebra is called R-degree of w denoted by
degR(w).
Suppose that a set X is well-ordered. We define the lexicographical order on the
standard basis of RBCom⟨X⟩as follows:
1) x < R(u) for every R-letter R(u) and each x ∈X,
2) R(u) < R(v) if and only if u < v.
We call an element w of the standard basis of RBCom⟨X⟩expressible, if, ignoring
the coefficient, w equals the leading word of the product R(a)∗R(b), where R(a) and R(b)
are some elements of the standard basis of RBCom⟨X⟩. It means that R(a) ∗R(b) = kw,
where k ∈N>0 is an appropriate number.
For example, the word w = R(R(x)x), where x ∈X, is expressible, since
R(x) ∗R(x) = 2R(R(x)x) + λR(x2).
So, R(x) ∗R(x) = 2R(R(x)x) = 2w.
