Fundamental Theorems on Controllability in
Wave-domain Processing for Holographic MIMO
Davide Dardari, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—Wave-domain processing is an emerging paradigm
where signal processing operations are partially shifted from the
digital to the electromagnetic (EM) domain. Leveraging recon-
figurable EM devices, this approach aims to reduce complexity,
energy consumption, and latency in next-generation wireless
systems employing holographic MIMO. This paper establishes
fundamental theorems on the controllability of generic reconfig-
urable EM devices, where wave processing is achieved through
the dynamic configuration of passive scatterers. Specifically, we
derive necessary and sufficient conditions for controllability as
a function of geometry and mutual coupling between elements.
Finally, we provide a detailed discussion and numerical results
characterizing the interplay between the number of elements,
physical size, degrees of freedom, and directivity.
Index Terms—Wave-domain processing, Controllability, Holo-
graphic MIMO, Dynamic Scattering Arrays, superdirectivity.
I. INTRODUCTION
It is broadly acknowledged that meeting the demanding
communication and sensing requirements of next-generation
wireless networks will necessitate deploying highly dense
and/or
large-scale
massive
multiple-input
multiple-output
(MIMO) antenna systems [1]–[4]. This evolution aims to fully
harness the potential of the wireless channel, for example, by
exploiting the additional degrees-of-freedom (DoF) available
in the near-field region of antenna arrays [5], [6]. Yet, massive
antenna arrays also bring substantial challenges related to
hardware complexity and energy consumption, raising con-
cerns about the sustainability of future wireless infrastructures.
In a fully digital transceiver design, every antenna element
is paired with its own radiofrequency (RF) chain, and all
signal processing takes place in the digital domain. This setup
inevitably constrains achievable data rates, energy efficiency,
and latency, primarily due to the limitations of digital clock
speeds and the sampling rates of ADCs and DACs. To address
these issues, hybrid analog–digital architectures have been
extensively explored in MIMO systems. By incorporating
networks of phase shifters and combiners between the digital
baseband, RF chains, and antenna elements, these architectures
significantly reduce the number of required RF chains and the
corresponding digital processing load. However, this reduction
comes at the expense of system flexibility and reconfigurability
[7].
Emerging technologies in reconfigurable antennas and
metamaterials introduce a novel research avenue: migrating
part of signal processing from the digital domain to the

and wave domains, forming what is termed the tri-hybrid

has seen renewed interest due to its energy-efficient and

equivalence between microwave network equations and those

Looking back, a key milestone in reconfigurable antennas
is reactively controlled arrays, proposed by Harrington [13].
These use passive scatterers reconfigured via programmable
reactive loads to alter radiation characteristics. This con-
cept evolved into electrically steerable passive array radiators
(ESPARs) antennas, extensively studied for programmable
radiation patterns featuring a single RF chain surrounded by
passive scatterers [14]–[16]. More recently, advances in mate-
rials and technologies have enabled novel antenna structures
tailored for next-generation wireless systems, offering higher
performance, flexibility, wider frequency coverage, and lower
costs [17]. This is often referred to as holographic MIMO
emerging as a key paradigm for EM-domain processing [17]–
[19].
Prominent examples include transmitarrays, fluid antennas,
and reconfigurable antennas (RAs) [20]–[24]. Another notable
case is the dynamic metasurface antenna (DMA), a traveling-
wave antenna with reconfigurable subwavelength apertures.
Requiring just one RF chain per row in planar arrays, DMAs
provide a flexible alternative with fewer RF chains [6], [25].
Among these technologies, stacked intelligent metasurfaces
(SIMs) have garnered particular attention [26]–[32]. The SIM
concept draws from advances in optics and microwave cir-
cuits that perform machine learning via layered EM surfaces
mimicking deep neural networks for image processing [26].
A typical SIM comprises a sealed vacuum chamber hous-
ing stacked metasurface layers. The first layer is an active
planar antenna array fed by multiple RF chains. The EM
wave it generates propagates through subsequent layers of
reconfigurable meta-atoms, with the final layer radiating the
processed EM field outward. By tuning the transmission prop-
erties of each meta-atom, the SIM shapes the propagating EM
wave into customized waveforms. Its layered design supports
backpropagation-like optimization. Optimization techniques
for SIMs enabling diverse EM wave processing tasks appear
in several works such as [27]–[29], [33]. Nonlinear extensions
are explored in [32].

While early studies employed simplified models for recon-
figurable EM devices, recent work uses rigorous multi-port
circuit models for their balance of physical accuracy and com-
putational tractability [9], [34]–[36]. Notably, most of these
reconfigurable EM technologies share a core principle: ac-
tive elements interacting with tunable passive scatterers. This
extends Harrington’s original idea across varied geometries,
element coupling, number of inputs, and implementations [13].
Starting from the consideration mentioned above, a general
framework for wave-domain processing, termed dynamic scat-
tering array (DSA), accounting for multiple inputs, arbitrary
geometries, and coupling effects has been recently proposed
[37], [38]. A DSA features a few active antenna elements,
each linked to an RF chain (input), surrounded by numerous
reconfigurable passive scatterers. These interact in the reactive
near-field, enabling joint EM processing and “over-the-air”
radiation. Devices like SIMs, RAs, and ESPARs represent
special cases. For example, a SIM is a particular DSA with
a block lower-bidiagonal impedance matrix. Unlike SIMs,
DSAs allow arbitrary scatterer distributions and couplings,
supporting simultaneous processing and radiation for greater
flexibility in compact designs [38]. Hereafter, we use DSA to
encompass these devices (e.g., SIMs, RAs, ESPARs) as special
cases.
The literature on DSAs and related particular cases has
focused on modeling and numerical optimization for specific
functionalities. Theoretical performance bounds for wave-
domain processing remain underexplored. In this direction,
the author of [39] proposes a framework to bound the fidelity
of realizing a target operator via reconfigurable EM systems.
This bound is computed numerically and applied to maximize
coupling in MIMO systems using reconfigurable intelligent
surfaces (RISs) and DMAs.
To the best of the author’s knowledge, no prior work
addresses this question: Under what conditions is a recon-
figurable EM device controllable, that is, for any desired
response, does there exist at least one configuration that
realizes it? Verifying controllability is challenging. In fact,
even though the EM device’s response is inherently linear, it
depends nonlinearly on a very large number of configuration
parameters.
A. Our Contribution
This paper addresses the aforementioned research question
by establishing fundamental theorems on the controllability of
generic reconfigurable EM devices.
The first major contribution is the derivation of a funda-
mental theorem, based on differential geometry arguments,
that provides a sufficient condition for the controllability
of a generic reconfigurable linear EM device, under mild
assumptions regarding its transfer matrix. The strength of this
theorem is that finding just one configuration that satisfies
a specific expression guarantees full system controllability.
Furthermore, this theorem extends well beyond the analysis
of DSAs; its applicability to generic transfer functions makes
it a broadly useful mathematical result.

VII. CONCLUSION
This paper derives fundamental theorems for the control-
lability of generic reconfigurable EM devices. A differential
geometry-based theorem provides a sufficient controllability
condition for linear EM devices under mild transfer matrix
assumptions that show that verifying the controllability for a
single configuration ensures the full controllability of the EM
device. Its applicability to generic transfer functions makes it
a broadly useful mathematical result.
For the case of single-input DSAs, we prove compliance
with the theorem and derive a closed-form criterion for dense
controllability depending on the dimension of the observation
space, the number of elements composing the DSA, and
mutual coupling. This facilitates a rapid assessment of con-
trollability for a given device characterized either analytically,
through simulations, or by measurements.
We further discuss the interplay between the number of el-
ements, physical size, DoF, and directivity through numerical
examples.
