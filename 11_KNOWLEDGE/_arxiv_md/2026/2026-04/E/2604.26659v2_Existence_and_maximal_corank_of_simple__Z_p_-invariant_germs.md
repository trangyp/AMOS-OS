EXISTENCE AND MAXIMAL CORANK OF SIMPLE
Zp-INVARIANT GERMS
IVAN PROSKURNIN
Abstract. In this paper we improve the previously achieved upper bound on
the corank of an equivariantly stable singularity for a group of prime order.
We also prove that the maximal corank of a simple Zp-invariant germ tends
to infinity as p increases and is asymptotically logarithmic, so the previously
obtained bound is valid up to order of magnitude.
1. Introduction
The present paper is a continuation of our former work. In a previous paper
([1]), a following theorem has been proven:
Theorem 1.1. Let τ be a linear action of Zp on (Cn, 0), rk(τ) the maximal rank
of a τ-invariant quadratic form, p – a prime number. Germs equivariantly simple
with respect to τ may only exist in one of the two cases:
1)det(τ) ̸= 1, n −rk(τ) ≤log2(p + 1);
2)det(τ) = 1, n −rk(τ) ≤log2(2p −1).
The statement of theorem 1.1. is not very encouraging, as one may hope that
there is a reasonably tame classification of all Zp-invariant simple singularities. For
such classification to exist, there should at least exist some constant upper bound
for the coranks of simple germs. Here we will prove that this is in fact not the case
and the estimate n −rk(τ) = O(log2(p)) is valid up to order of magnitude. We will
also narrow down the possible range of group actions for which simple singularities
may exist. Namely, we are going to prove the following theorems:
Theorem 1.2. Let τ be a linear action of Zp on (Cn, 0), rk(τ) the maximal rank
of a τ-invariant quadratic form, p – a prime number. Germs equivariantly simple
with respect to τ may only exist if det(τ) ̸= 1, n −rk(τ) ≤log2(p + 1) or if τ is a
real group action.
Theorem 1.3. For each N ∈N there is a prime number p and a Zp-invariant
simple (with respect to a nontrivial Zp-action) germ f : (Cn, 0) −→(C, 0) with
j2(f) = 0 and n > N. In fact, for every ε > 0 for allmost all prime p there is a
Zp-invariant simple germ f : (Cn, 0) −→(C, 0) with n > (1 −ε)(log(log(p))) and
j2(f) = 0.
Theorem 1.4. There are constants α, β and an infinite sequence of distinct prime
numbers p1, . . . pn such that there is a simple Zpi-invariant germ with corank at
least αlog2(pi −1) + β.
The last theorem means that the upper bound in the theorem 1.2. is in fact
valid up to an order of magnitude: the corank of a simple singularity does in fact
grow logarithmically as p increases.

3. Equivariantly simple and equivariantly stable germs
There are multiple papers on the classification of equivariantly simple singulari-
ties (see., e.g., [3], [4], [5]). Roughly speaking, an equivariantly stable singularity
is the simplest of the equivariantly simple singularities, i.e. the one that is only
adjacent to itself. If the case of real group action the equivariantly stable singu-
larity is the nondegenerate one, as the deformation of a Morse function is again a
Morse function. For the formal definitions of equivariant simplicity and stability
see below.
We will denote mG the maximal ideal in the ring of G-invariant analytic germs.
Here we only assume G to be finite, not nessesarily abelian or isomorphic to Zp.

Theorem 4.1. There is no equivariantly stable Zp-invariant function germ f with

If d1 . . . dn +(−1)n−1 is a prime number p such f gives us an example of a stable
(and therefore simple) Zp-invariant function of corank n. We will concentrate on
odd n, to be definite, so d1 . . . dn = p −1. Hence, to prove that the corank of a
stable f can be arbitrarily large it just needs to be shown that for each N there is
a prime number p such that p −1 has a decomposition p −1 = d1 . . . dn with at
least N + 1 nontrivial factors. This can be proven using a theorem of Erdős ([12]):
Theorem 5.2. As n tends to infinity for any ε > 0 and for allmost all prime p
not exceding n the number p −1 has more than (1 −ε)log(log(n)) distinct prime
divisors.
This in fact means that for “average” p Zp has an action with a simple singularity
with corank at least O(log(log(p))). We don’t need our divisors d1, . . . , dn to be
distinct or prime, however the function Ω(n) counting prime divisors with multi-
plicity has the same asymptotic behaviour as the number of distinct prime divisors
([13], sections 22.10 and 22.11), so it can be reasonably assumed that counting the
total number of not necessary distinct divisors d1, . . . , dn of p −1 will not lead to
a different estimate.
We will now prove that the corank of a stable invariant singularity in fact has
logarithmic growth.
Theorem 5.3. There are constants α, β and an infinite sequence of distinct prime
numbers p1, . . . pn such that pi −1 has at least αlog2(pi −1) + β prime divisors,
counted with multiplicity.
Proof. By Linnik’s theorem ([14]) there are absolute constants C, L such that every
arithmetical progression a+kd, k ∈Z≥0 with coprime a, d and 0 < a < d contains
at least one prime p < CdL. Consider a = 1, d = 2m and pick the first prime in the
arithmetic progression a + kd. p −1 is divisible by 2m, so Ω(p −1) ≥m. As p <
CdL = C2mL, log2(p −1) < log2(C) + mL. Therefore Ω(p −1) > log2(p−1)−log2(C)
L
.
By choosing each succesive 2m to be strictly greater than all the previously obtained
pi we can build an infinite sequence of such p.
□

to be at most 5 ([15]).
