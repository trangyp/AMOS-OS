Abstract. We study exceptional loci of F-blowups of normal toric varieties.
In the Q-factorial case, this study amounts to studying the exceptional loci
of G-Hilbert schemes. We give a formula for the dimension of the center of a
prime divisor on the F-blowup in terms of combinatorial data, together with
an algorithm for computing it. Moreover, we study the relation between F-
blowups and essential divisors for three-dimensional terminal singularities and
canonical singularities.
Finally, we give a simple condition ensuring that a
prime divisor over the given toric variety has a positive-dimensional center on
the F-blowup.
1. Introduction
The primary objective of this paper is to understand the exceptional set of the F-
blowup, associated to a Q-factorial affine toric variety. To a toric variety X, we can
associate another (possibly non-normal) toric variety FB(∞)(X) in a canonical way,
called the limit F-blowup [Yas12]. It is defined as the universal birational flattening
of a Frobenius-like endomorphism of X. If X is Q-factorial and affine, equivalently,
if X is associated to a simplicial cone, then X is a quotient variety by a finite abelian
group and FB(∞)(X) is isomorphic to the G-Hilbert scheme. In some special cases,
this birational transform provides a nice resolution of singularities of the given
toric variety such as minimal resolutions and crepant resolutions. However, it is
not smooth or even normal, in general.
Let us denote the normalization of f
FB(∞)(X) by e
X. Besides the problem of
smoothness, it is also interesting to study the geometry of the exceptional locus in
e
X. Recently, the first and third named authors together with Duarte proposed the
following problem in this direction:
Problem 1.1 ([CMDY25, Problem 1.1]). Do all essential divisors over X appear
on e
X as prime divisors ?
An essential divisor means a divisor over X whose center on every desingulariza-
tion of X is an irreducible component of the exceptional locus. This notion appears
in the famous Nash problem on arc families. We refer the reader to [PS15] for more
details on the Nash problem. Problem 1.1 itself is also regarded as an analog of the
Nash problem, since the F-blowup can be interpreted as a parameter space of some
sort of jets.
Remark 1.2. A similar problem for higher Nash blowups was addressed in [CM23].
There are also studies on toric resolutions that uses only essential divisors [BGS95,
Dai02, SS23, Sat25].

the G-Hilbert scheme, since e

5. Essential divisors of three-dimensional Q-factorial toric
singularities
In this section, we study the relation between F-blowups and essential divisors
for Q-factorial toric singularities.
5.1. Essential divisors and discrepancies. We briefly review the notion of es-
sential divisors.
Definition 5.1 ([IK03, PS15]). We say that a divisor over X is essential if, in any
resolution, whose center is an irreducible component of the exceptional locus.
Definition 5.2 ([BGS95]). We say that an exceptional divisor over X is BGS
essential if it appears on every resolution of X as a prime divisor.
Remark 5.3 ([CMDY25, Proposition A.8]). If X is a Q-factorial toric variety, then
a toric divisor E over X is essential if and only if it is BGS essential.
Proposition 5.4 ([BGS95, Theorem 1.10]). Let ρ ⊂σ be a ray and let Eρ be the
corresponding exceptional divisor over X. Then Eρ is BGS essential if and only if
ρ is spanned by an element of the Hilbert basis of B.
Definition 5.5. Let X be a normal Q-Gorenstein variety. The discrepancy a(E)
of a divisor E over X is the coefficient of E in the relative canonical divisor KY/X
associated to a proper birational morphism Y →X where E appears on Y as a
prime divisor. In our setting, each toric divisor E corresponds to a ray R≥0w in
the fan Σ corresponding to X. We denote the discrepancy a(E) by a(w).
Definition 5.6. Let X be a normal Q-Gorenstein variety. We say that X has
terminal (resp. canonical) singularities if there exists a proper birational morphism
f : Y →X with Y smooth, such that the discrepancy of every exceptional prime
divisor of f is positive (resp. non-negative).
