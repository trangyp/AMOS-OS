EXCEPTIONAL POLES OF ARCHIMEDEAN RANKIN–SELBERG L-FUNCTIONS
FOR PRINCIPAL SERIES REPRESENTATIONS OF GLn(R)
YEONGSEONG JO†, SANTOSH NADIMPALLI† AND AKASH YADAV†
ABSTRACT. We prove that for any pair of irreducible principal series representations (π1,π2)
of GLn(R) in general position, the notions of exceptional pole of type 1 and type 2 coincide.
Using this identification, we express the Rankin–Selberg L-function L(s,π1 × π2) in terms of
the exceptional L-factors attached to the irreducible constituents of the derivatives of π1 and π2.
1. INTRODUCTION
Let F be a local field of characteristic 0, and let π1 and π2 be irreducible generic repre-
sentations of GLn(F). When F is non-archimedean, Cogdell and Piatetski-Shapiro [CPS17]
developed a method for computing the local Rankin–Selberg L-functions L(s,π1 ×π2) in terms
of L-functions attached to supercuspidal representations. Their approach relies on the theory
of Bernstein–Zelevinsky derivatives [BZ76, BZ77, Zel80] together with a detailed analysis of
exceptional poles. More precisely, they express L(s,π1 × π2) in terms of exceptional L-factors
attached to derivatives of π1 and π2. In this setting, the Schwartz space S(F n) admits a one-
step filtration given by the subspace of functions vanishing at 0. Consequently, there is only
one notion of exceptional pole (see, e.g., [CPS17, Mat15]). In the archimedean framework, this
corresponds to exceptional poles with level 0 (see Subsection 2.1).
Exceptional poles have been extensively studied when F is non-archimedean (see, e.g.,
[CPS17, Mat15, Jo18]), whereas the archimedean case remains far less understood. A first
step in this direction was taken by Chang and Cogdell [CC99], who studied the η-homology of
representations, where η denotes the nilradical of a suitable parabolic subalgebra. They showed
that the η-homology of irreducible generic representations in general position is completely
reducible. By general position we mean that the representations have regular infinitesimal char-
acter (see Section 2.1).
Using this result together with [AGS15-1, Definition 3.0.2], Chai [Cha15] defined a notion
of archimedean derivative as a component of the η-homology and formulated a representation-
theoretic notion of exceptional poles of type 2 with level m for a nonnegative integer m. Excep-
tional poles of type 1 with level m, on the other hand, are defined via Rankin–Selberg integrals
[JPSS83]. Unlike the non-archimedean case, the level structure in the archimedean setting arises
from the infinite filtration of the Schwartz space. Chai [Cha15] conjectured that the two notions
of exceptional poles with level m coincide. This was proved at level 0 by the third-named au-
thor [Yad24], namely when π1̂⊗π2 is GLn(R)-distinguished. Here ̂⊗denotes the completed
projective tensor product of smooth Fr´echet representations.
The main result of this paper shows that the notions of type 1 and type 2 exceptional poles
agree at every level when π1 and π2 are irreducible principal series representations in general
position.

Theorem 1.1. Let π1 and π2 be irreducible principal series representations of GLn(R) in gen-
eral position. A complex number s = s0 is an exceptional pole of type 1 with level m for the pair
(π1,π2) if and only if it is an exceptional pole of type 2 with level m.
Extending Theorem 1.1 to arbitrary irreducible generic representations of GLn(R) is consid-
erably more difficult. When discrete series representations occur in the inducing data, tensoring
with finite-dimensional algebraic representations does not in general produce a finite GLn(R)-
stable filtration with irreducible generic subquotients. For this reason, we restrict our attention
to principal series representations in general position.
The proof of Theorem 1.1 is delicate, since exceptional poles may occur at multiple levels.
We briefly outline the strategy of the proof. The key step is Proposition 2.2.1. We show that if s0
is a pole of L(s,π1 × π2) but not an exceptional pole of type 1 at any level, then the Hom-space
HomPn(R)(π1ˆ⊗π2,∣det∣1−s0)
is nonzero, where Pn(R) denotes the mirabolic subgroup of GLn(R). This reduces the problem
to showing that the Hom-space vanishes in the presence of an exceptional pole of type 2. We
show this vanishing by applying the archimedean Bernstein–Zelevinsky filtration introduced by
Wu and Zhang [WZ25] to the restrictions of π1 and π2 to the mirabolic subgroup.
Apart from their role in the proof of our main result, these mirabolic Hom-spaces appear
naturally in the theory of local Rankin–Selberg periods and zeta integrals, both in the non-
archimedean setting [JPSS83, CPS17] and in its archimedean analogue (see Subsection 4.4).
Furthermore, these spaces are also of independent interest in branching problems (cf. [WZ25]).
As an application of Theorem 1.1, we show that the inverse of the archimedean Rankin–
Selberg L-function factors as the least common multiple of inverse exceptional L-factors at-
tached to the irreducible constituents of the derivatives of π1 and π2. Analogous results have
been established in the non-archimedean setting for the Rankin–Selberg L-function [CPS17,
Theorem 2.1], the exterior square L-function [Jo18, Theorems 2.7 and 2.13], and the Bump–
Friedberg L-function [Mat15, Proposition 4.5]. To the best of our knowledge, this provides the
first such description in the archimedean case. For definitions of l.c.m and Lex, we refer the
reader to Subsection 4.2.

We begin this section by proving that an irreducible generic representation remains indecom-

Proposition 3.1. Let π ∈Irrgen(Gn). Then π∣Pn is indecomposable.
Proof. Let λ ∈HomNn(π,ψ) be a nonzero Whittaker functional. Since π is irreducible generic,
