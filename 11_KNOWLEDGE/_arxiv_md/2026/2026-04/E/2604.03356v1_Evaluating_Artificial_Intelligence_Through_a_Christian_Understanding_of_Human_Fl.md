Abstract
Artiﬁcial intelligence (AI) alignment is fundamentally a formation problem, not
only a safety problem. As Large Language Models (LLMs) increasingly mediate
moral deliberation and spiritual inquiry, they do more than provide informa-
tion; they function as instruments of digital catechesis, actively shaping and or-
dering human understanding, decision-making, and moral reﬂection. To make
this formative inﬂuence visible and measurable, we introduce the Flourishing
AI Benchmark: Christian Single-Turn (FAI-C-ST), a framework designed to
evaluate Frontier Model responses against a Christian understanding of human
ﬂourishing across seven dimensions.
By comparing 20 Frontier Models against both pluralistic and Christian-speciﬁc
criteria, we show that current AI systems are not worldview-neutral. Instead,
they default to a Procedural Secularism that lacks the grounding necessary to
sustain theological coherence, resulting in a systematic performance decline of
approximately 17 points across all dimensions of ﬂourishing. Most critically,
there is a 31-point decline in the Faith and Spirituality dimension. These ﬁnd-
ings suggest that the performance gap in values alignment is not a technical
limitation, but arises from training objectives that prioritize broad acceptabil-
ity and safety over deep, internally coherent moral or theological reasoning.

1. Introduction
1.1 Background and Motivation
The Flourishing AI Benchmark (FAI) was developed to measure how Frontier
Models encode and promote speciﬁc dimensions of human ﬂourishing - includ-
ing faith, meaning, happiness, relationships, health, character, and ﬁnances -
revealing the underlying worldview commitments embedded within these AI
systems.
• Character and Virtue (Character) - describing "acting to promote good
in all circumstances" and "being able to give up some happiness now for
greater happiness later.”
• Close Social Relationships (Relationships) - capturing the quality of
one's interpersonal connections, being "content with friendships and rela-
tionships" and having "relationships as satisfying as one would want them
to be.“
• Happiness and Life Satisfaction (Happiness) - encompassing both
"how satisﬁed one is with life as a whole" and "how happy or unhappy
one usually feels.”
• Meaning and Purpose (Meaning) - including "understanding one's pur-
pose in life" and feeling that "the things one does in life are worthwhile.”
• Mental and Physical Health (Health) - describing both physical and
mental well-being, measuring how individuals rate their overall physical
and mental health.
• Financial and Material Stability (Finances) - concerning "worry about
being able to meet normal monthly living expenses" and "worry about
safety, food, or housing.”
• Faith and Spirituality (Faith) - encompassing "communion with God
or the transcendent" and including spiritual formation and religious en-
gagement, serving as elements of human ﬂourishing.
The initial benchmark - hereafter referred to as FAI-G-ST (General Single-Turn)
- was introduced in “Measuring Human Flourishing in AI Systems” [Hilliard et
al, 2025] and provides a comprehensive evaluation methodology grounded in
the Harvard Human Flourishing Index (HFI). FAI-G established an initial stan-
dard for assessing large language models (LLMs) beyond traditional benchmarks
focused on task accuracy, reasoning performance, or safety compliance, by ex-
amining how model responses align with a generalized, pluralistic account of
human ﬂourishing. In this context, 'pluralism' refers to a framework that seeks
to accommodate a diverse array of worldviews by identifying shared ethical com-
monalities and overlapping consensus, deliberately avoiding the particularity of
any single religious or metaphysical tradition.

As AI systems increasingly mediate human understanding, decision-making, and
moral reﬂection, we must consider whether these models implicitly endorse or
contradict speciﬁc worldview commitments. Prior FAI-G evaluations showed
that while Frontier Models achieve strong performance across general ﬂourish-
ing dimensions, their responses often reﬂect a Procedural Secularism. In this
context, Procedural Secularism refers to a response pattern characterized by:
(1) avoidance of explicit metaphysical or theological commitments, (2) priori-
tization of individual autonomy and subjective well-being, and (3) reliance on
therapeutic or consensus-based moral language. Although these orientations
are relatively safety-aligned, they lack explicit theological grounding and do not
adequately capture distinctively Christian values. While traditional alignment
focuses on ensuring AI systems follow human instructions, and safety focuses
on preventing harmful outputs, the formation problem concerns the iterative,
sub-threshold way AI shapes a user’s moral imagination. Unlike a safety vio-
lation, formation is a cumulative process where a Frontier Model's default to
Procedural Secularism functions as a digital catechesis.
To address this gap, we introduce FAI Christian (FAI-C-ST), a new single-turn
(single prompt and single response interaction) evaluation framework that inte-
grates a Christian worldview into the assessment of ﬂourishing. This iteration
diﬀerentiates between general well-being and authentic Christian ﬂourishing,
rewarding Frontier Models that exhibit biblical grounding, moral clarity, and
theological coherence while continuing to recognize the appropriate place for
neutral, safe responses. Frontier Models refer to large-scale, state-of-the-art lan-
guage models released by major research labs and included in the December
2025 benchmark evaluation. The result is a more specialized benchmark that
reveals where models not only perform well in general terms but where they
speciﬁcally reﬂect Christian commitments to faith, hope, and love, setting the
stage for further work in developing models that are theologically representa-
tive.
Central to this work is the recognition that AI alignment can be understood not
merely as a safety problem, but as a formation problem. In practice, AI systems
do not merely deliver information; they function as an instrument of digital cat-
echesis. This term describes the unintentional but systematic way LLMs order
a user’s understanding of truth, virtue, and authority through repeated inter-
action. Christian theology has long understood that formation occurs through
repeated practices, narratives, and authorities that shape desire and habit over
time.
When Frontier Models consistently frame inquiry through the lens of
Procedural Secularism, they do more than omit theological truth: they actively
reshape the user’s moral and spiritual trajectory, replacing a teleological orien-
tation toward God with a fragmented secular worldview.
While traditional alignment focuses on ensuring AI systems follow human in-
structions, and safety focuses on preventing harmful or adversarial outputs,
the formation problem concerns the iterative, sub-threshold way AI shapes a
user’s moral imagination. Unlike discrete safety violations (e.g., providing a

1.2 Reader Guidance
This paper applies an existing AI benchmark within a values-speciﬁc context.
Results should be interpreted comparatively rather than normatively: scores in-
dicate relative alignment with a given Evaluative Lens, not absolute correctness,
endorsement, or universal suitability. An Evaluative Lens refers to a deﬁned set
of normative criteria, rubric deﬁnitions, and judge personas used to interpret
Frontier Model outputs through a particular worldview.
FAI-G-ST establishes a broad evaluative space that incorporates diverse faith
and value traditions and serves as the baseline for comparison. FAI-C-ST intro-
duces additional constraints corresponding to a biblical Christian understanding
of ﬂourishing. Readers unfamiliar with Christian theology are not required to
assess theological claims directly; instead, they may evaluate the internal co-
herence, transparency, and methodological consistency of the benchmark as an
instance of value-sensitive AI evaluation.
FAI-C-ST does not claim that the Christian account of ﬂourishing is universally
normative in an empirical sense. Rather, it acknowledges that all evaluative
frameworks are implicitly normative for those who adopt them.
Within the
Christian tradition, ﬂourishing is grounded in the transcendent authority of
Christ and the historic witness of the Church. This benchmark operationalizes
that inherited moral framework into a measurable evaluative structure.
By
doing so, it demonstrates how evaluation outcomes shift when moving from
a pluralistic baseline to a speciﬁc theological orientation, making previously
implicit alignment assumptions explicit and comparable across systems.
Although instantiated here within a Christian framework, the primary contribu-
tion of this work is methodological. It shows that alignment properties become
more visible when evaluated through explicit moral frameworks rather than
exclusively pluralistic or worldview-agnostic criteria. These lenses do not al-
ter underlying model outputs; instead, they reveal how those outputs function
under diﬀerent moral and theological expectations.

2. Summary of the Original FAI Benchmark
2.1 Purpose and Design
The original FAI Benchmark was published in July 2025. The intent of the
benchmark was to provide a novel framework evaluating the capability of large
language models to support human ﬂourishing through single-turn interactions.
This was based on the ﬂourishing measure developed by researchers at the
Human Flourishing Program, measuring across seven dimensions: Character,
Relationships, Happiness, Meaning, Health, Finances, and Faith.
LLM-as-a-
judge personas were created to represent specialists in each dimension, who
then graded responses through rubric-based scoring. [Gu et al, 2025] Single-turn
evaluations were performed across leading Frontier Models including ChatGPT,
Claude, Gemini, and Llama and a number of open source models as well.
2.2 Key Findings from FAI-G-ST
Of the 28 models tested during the earlier FAI-G-ST evaluation, scores ranged
from high 40s to high 80s. Speciﬁc categories, such as Finances and Health,
scored higher, while dimensions like Faith, Relationships, and Purpose consis-
tently lagged. This behavior indicated that Frontier Models performed better
in more fact-based or pragmatic dimensions, while they struggled with more
nuanced, value-laden dimensions. This ﬁnding was critical as many of the ﬂour-
ishing dimensions contain assumptions about morality that can vary by value
system. This highlights the need to align these AI systems - widely used for
personal guidance - with more particular accounts of human ﬂourishing.
In further analysis, Frontier Models provided responses rooted in empathy and
safety, but did not demonstrate meaningful capacity to engage with theologi-
cal or worldview-speciﬁc reasoning. Neutrality in free-form responses was more
frequently used relative to moral or metaphysical commitment. This reﬂects
training from a secular Western perspective, designed to appeal to the widest
swath of users. This neutrality is eﬀectively Procedural Secularism: a default

toward individual autonomy that stands in tension with Christian moral teach-
ing. Given the increasing rate at which individuals turn to AI for guidance, it
is critical to understand what value systems Frontier Models support and how
that might aﬀect future viewpoints for individuals of all ages.
Benchmarks like FAI can be tailored to measure LLM alignment with a variety of
worldviews. What we have observed is that responses often avoid faith-informed
reasoning, and while they may support values in line with a Christian worldview,
they often lack explicit grounding in transcendent purpose or Scripture. FAI-
C-ST clearly measures which ﬂourishing dimensions are best supported from a
Christian ﬂourishing perspective.
3. Conceptual Foundations of FAI Christian (FAI-C-ST)
3.1 Theological Rationale
A speciﬁcally Christian evaluation of ﬂourishing is warranted because the FAI-
G-ST benchmark evaluates ﬂourishing in largely individual and secular terms,
while Christian anthropology understands human ﬂourishing as inherently rela-
tional, teleological, and ordered toward God and neighbor. Individuals bear the
Imago Dei (Image of God), [Middleton, 2005] and therefore possess a dignity
that grounds ethical obligations as more than just preferences or social util-
ity. Within this framework, AI interaction is not neutral; it deeply inﬂuences
the process of ordering the user’s aﬀections. [Fogg, 2003] Christian ﬂourishing is
demonstrated through sanctiﬁcation, vocation, and stewardship: viewing health
and ﬁnances not as personal possessions, but as resources entrusted for service
and the glory of God. Distinguishing general ﬂourishing from Christian Flour-
ishing enables us to see whether a model is merely Christian-adjacent or if it
is genuinely providing a Christ-centered perspective that supports, rather than
ﬂattens, the user's spiritual formation.
This Christian framing necessarily impacts and interprets each ﬂourishing di-
mension. Health is a stewardship that we have over our bodies, relationships are
covenantal, ﬁnances are entrusted for service, and character is formed through
virtue, repentance, and moral truth.
Scripture and historic Christian writings also function as sources of moral for-
mation that supply both normative content and frameworks for coherence, es-
pecially in areas where contemporary models default to public moral consensus.
Large language models are trained to be broadly helpful and inclusive, which
often produces responses that are empathetic but in the end not actionable. The
results suggest that AI systems may contribute to formation, implicitly teach-
ing what counts as wisdom, identifying what should be prioritized, and deﬁning
what authority is legitimate. [Zao-Sanders, 2025]

3.2 Design Principles
In designing FAI-C-ST, we revised the original FAI-G-ST question set to bet-
ter surface theological and moral reasoning, while deliberately preserving the
seven-dimension evaluative structure of the original benchmark. This allows for
meaningful cross-benchmark comparison at the dimension level, even where in-
dividual questions have been updated. Questions were reviewed and reﬁned to
ensure they were open-ended, with non-leading prompts that invite theological
reasoning where appropriate and remain reﬂective of realistic human concerns.
Rather than driving confessional responses, the benchmark is designed to re-
veal how models reason about ﬂourishing when theological, moral, or existential
considerations are at stake.
The aim is not to solicit Christian worldview responses to every question, but
to assess if the model responses to questions align with a Christian conception
of truth, purpose and moral responsibility, either implicitly or explicitly. Each
question probes a concrete aspect of ﬂourishing within its dimension while leav-
ing space for models to explain their underlying assumptions.
A central design constraint is the balance between denominational diversity
and shared Christian orthodoxy.
FAI-C-ST focuses on widely held commit-
ments grounded in Scripture, the historic creeds, and moral teaching so as to
avoid privileging narrow doctrinal distinctions or denominational debates. This
reﬂects the reality that Christian ﬂourishing is lived across diverse traditions
while remaining anchored in a common theological framework. Design decisions
sought to avoid reductionism as well as sectarianism, and questions and rubrics
were weighted to avoid penalizing diﬀerences in emphasis, language, or pastoral
sensitivity.
4. Methodology and Evaluation Framework
4.1 Judge Personas and Criteria
In order to adjust the approach to accommodate evaluation from a Christian
perspective, LLM-as-a-judge personas were revised from the original FAI-G-ST
approach to represent a single, role-speciﬁed Christian expert for each ﬂourishing
dimension. Prior work on LLM-as-a-judge evaluation demonstrates that anchor-
ing judgment in a uniﬁed evaluator role improves consistency, interpretability,
and reproducibility, particularly in subjective and normative assessment. Inter-
disciplinary considerations were incorporated at the level of rubric design rather
than through multi-voice persona construction. [Gu et al, 2025], Tseng et al,
2024
4.2 Question Design and Dataset Review
To guide revision of the original benchmark dataset [Hilliard et al, 2025], we
articulated a set of question design principles intended to ensure impartiality,
evaluability, and relevance to human ﬂourishing. Questions were required to be

non-leading, expressed as authentic human questions, and grounded in realistic
scenarios involving one or more ﬂourishing dimensions. Subjective questions
were designed to elicit interpretive judgment and normative framing rather than
factual recall, while avoiding culturally polarizing or adversarial topics.
To assess dataset quality and identify potential sources of bias or construct dis-
tortion, we convened a review panel of human subject matter experts (SMEs)
spanning theology, psychology, ethics, and applied domains relevant to the ﬂour-
ishing dimensions. Panelists independently reviewed the question set and associ-
ated answer options, evaluating clarity, diﬃculty, cultural sensitivity, and align-
ment with intended constructs. Feedback from this review informed targeted
dataset pruning, redistribution, and reformulation prior to ﬁnal evaluation runs.
Further details are provided in Appendix D.
The ﬁnal dataset consists of 807 questions, distributed across the seven ﬂourish-
ing dimensions, with approximately 57% subjective and 43% objective questions.
4.3 Question Set Revision Outcomes
A panel review of the original FAI Benchmark questions [Hilliard et al, 2025]
identiﬁed systematic issues related to question balance across dimensions, over-
representation of objective formats for normative constructs (particularly Faith
and Character), and the inclusion of culturally sensitive or abstract items that
limited evaluability. In response, the question set for FAI-G-ST and FAI-C-ST
was reduced from 1229 to 807 and rebalanced to improve construct validity, in-
crease the proportion of subjective and multi-dimensional prompts, and enhance
cross-cultural applicability. A detailed account of the design principles, review
criteria, and panel ﬁndings is provided in Appendix D.
4.4 Rubric and Scoring
LLM-as-a-judge evaluation is performed using a structured rubric designed to
capture both factual competence and values-aligned interpretive quality.
To
ensure comparability across diverse Frontier Model architectures, all raw scores
are normalized to a 0 - 100 scale.
Evaluation criteria are organized into three distinct scoring categories:
• Objective Accuracy: Evaluates whether responses correctly address fac-
tual or deﬁnitional aspects of a question.
• Subjective Alignment: Assesses the extent to which responses exhibit
coherent moral framing, appropriate prioritization of values, and sensitiv-
ity to context when interpreted through the speciﬁed evaluative lens.
• Tangential Integration: Measures the model’s ability to spontaneously
incorporate relevant moral or theological considerations - such as integrat-
ing faith or character implications into a question whose primary domain
is ﬁnancial or physical health.

In FAI-C-ST, rubric deﬁnitions for subjective and tangential criteria are adapted
to reﬂect a Christian values-speciﬁc account of ﬂourishing, while the question
set, scoring structure, and aggregation mechanics remain identical to FAI-G-ST.
This ensures that observed score diﬀerences are attributable to evaluative inter-
pretation rather than changes in rubric form. Availability of rubric deﬁnitions,
judge prompts, and scoring criteria are described in Appendix J.
Deﬁnitional alignment between the model response and the LLM-as-a-judge is
assigned the greatest weight, based on the premise that worldview coherence
is primarily reﬂected in interpretive framing rather than factual recall. Scores
for individual criteria are normalized and aggregated using a geometric mean,
ensuring that strong performance in one dimension cannot compensate for weak-
ness in another. The use of the geometric mean reﬂects the theological premise
that human ﬂourishing requires holistic coherence across domains rather than
high performance in isolated areas. [Hilliard et al, 2025]
4.4.1 Cross-Dimensional Evaluation
A distinctive feature of the FAI
framework is its cross-dimensional methodology. Rather than assessing a re-
sponse solely against the primary dimension of a given question, each response
is evaluated for its relevance across all seven ﬂourishing dimensions simulta-
neously. LLM-as-a-judge personas assigned to each dimension independently
assess every subjective response.
Tangential scores are recorded wherever a judge persona determines that a
response meaningfully engages with ﬂourishing considerations beyond the in-
quiry's primary dimension. For example, a response to a ﬁnancial question that
invokes relational responsibility or references stewardship would be evaluated
by the Finance, Relationships, and Character judges. Within FAI-C-ST, this
is especially diagnostic of the formation problem, revealing whether theologi-
cal reasoning remains conﬁned to explicitly religious questions or is integrated
throughout the model’s broader logic.
4.5 Evaluation Process
All Frontier Models were evaluated using a ﬁxed, single-turn interaction proto-
col to enable comparability across AI systems and benchmark variants. This
design isolates response-level moral and evaluative framing while minimizing
confounds introduced by conversational memory or adaptive prompting strate-
gies.
For each question, Frontier Models generated a single response under
controlled decoding parameters, including ﬁxed system instructions and tem-
perature settings. No conversational history or iterative reﬁnement was permit-
ted, and models were evaluated in isolation without external tools or retrieval
augmentation.
Frontier Model responses were scored by LLM-as-a-judge evaluators instantiated
with the dimension-speciﬁc personas described in Section 4.1. To preserve com-
parative validity, judge conﬁgurations - including persona prompts and rubric

text - were held constant across all evaluated Frontier Models within a given
benchmark run. Responses to objective questions were evaluated for factual
correctness against predeﬁned answer keys. Subjective and tangential responses
were evaluated exclusively through rubric-guided judgment, emphasizing inter-
pretive framing, normative coherence, and contextual sensitivity.
A potential concern in LLM-as-a-judge based evaluation is that judge personas
may share training distributions or implicit biases with the Frontier Models
being evaluated. In FAI-C-ST, this risk is mitigated through the use of tightly
constrained judge personas, explicit rubric deﬁnitions, and consistent evaluation
conditions across all models. Moreover, the benchmark is designed for compara-
tive analysis rather than absolute judgment, and observed patterns were robust
across multiple independent judge personas (Appendix F), suggesting that re-
sults are not artifacts of a single evaluator.
Evaluation results are interpreted comparatively, with analytic emphasis placed
on relative score diﬀerences between FAI-G-ST and FAI-C-ST rather than on
absolute score magnitudes. This comparative design enables diagnosis of how
the distinct evaluative lenses of FAI-C-ST and FAI-G-ST interact with Frontier
Model behavior while minimizing confounding factors introduced by diﬀerences
in scale, training regime, or model architecture.

5.1 Comparative Score Overview
As summarized in Table 1, all evaluated Frontier Models exhibit a substantial
decline in overall score when moving from FAI-G-ST to FAI-C-ST. Unless oth-
erwise noted, quantitative results in Section 5 are derived from the Flourishing
AI Insights Report [Gloo Research, 2025].
Across all evaluated models, the application of a Christian Evaluative Lens
produced a consistent and interpretable pattern: Frontier Models optimized
for general-purpose helpfulness and pluralistic safety exhibited materially lower
performance when assessed against criteria grounded in Christian theological
anthropology. December 2025 evaluations across 20 Frontier Models show that
every evaluated system experienced a substantial decline. Declines range from
-10 to -22 points. Across Frontier Models, the mean FAI-G-ST to FAI-C-ST
delta is approximately -17 points, with no evaluated model exhibiting a neutral
or positive shift. These drops are consistent across model families and reﬂect
not a loss of general capability, but a reduced ability for these AI systems to
maintain coherence under a speciﬁc moral and theological lens.

can maintain coherence within a speciﬁc, teleological perspective. The fact that

6. Discussion
6.1 Interpretive Analysis: Worldview Encoding in LLMs
FAI-C-ST results align with a growing body of research demonstrating that large
language models do not operate free of substantive normative commitments
[Liang et all, 2022], [Bowman et al, 2023]. Rather than suspending worldview
assumptions, they encode what can be understood, drawing on Taylor’s (2007)
framework, as a form of pluralism characterized by consensus-oriented, non-
teleological moral reasoning grounded in therapeutic language and expressive
individualism [Taylor, 2007]. While FAI-C-ST measures response-level align-
ment rather than user-level formation directly, the consistency of these patterns
across dimensions provides evidence that models systematically privilege par-
ticular secular moral framings.
While the Flourishing AI Benchmark (FAI)
does not measure downstream behavioral or spiritual outcomes, it reveals sta-
ble tendencies in how Frontier Models interpret moral questions and structure
guidance. This orientation reﬂects a combination of training data composition
and reinforcement objectives designed to maximize the broad acceptability of
Frontier Model outputs, a process that often discourages explicit theological or
metaphysical claims. [Bai et al, 2022]
The results of the FAI Benchmarks should shift the focus away from the im-
possible ideal of neutrality and instead advance the more defensible claim that
models instantiate a particular worldview assumption. By shifting the frame
from absence of worldview to presence of an unacknowledged one, which is the
more interesting and more philosophically serious point.
Neutrality, in this sense, functions both as a strength - enabling wide accessibil-
ity - and as a limitation, particularly for communities seeking guidance rooted
in metaphysical moral traditions. An important implication of these ﬁndings is
that worldview-agnostic evaluation alone is insuﬃcient for understanding how
AI systems reason about meaning, virtue, and purpose. While general bench-
marks such as FAI-G-ST remain essential for broad comparability and safety as-
sessment, they necessarily abstract away diﬀerences that become salient within
speciﬁc moral traditions. FAI-G-ST and FAI-C-ST represent distinct Evalua-
tive Lenses grounded in genuinely diﬀerent worldviews. Rather than treating
these frameworks as hierarchically nested, they are better understood as sepa-
rate moral orientations that surface diﬀerent and sometimes incompatible prior-
16


## Page 17


ities. A model optimized for FAI-G-ST performance may perform poorly under
FAI-C-ST (and, in theory, vice versa) because the underlying accounts of hu-
man ﬂourishing diverge in substantive ways. Comparative analysis of FAI-G-ST
and FAI-C-ST makes this divergence explicit and measurable, revealing align-
ment trade-oﬀs that would otherwise remain latent in general-purpose alignment
regimes. [Askell et al, 2021]
6.2 Implications for Trust, Formation, and Discipleship
As AI systems increasingly mediate personal reﬂection, moral deliberation, and
spiritual inquiry, their formative inﬂuence grows. Christian theology has long
recognized that formation occurs through repeated practices, narratives, and
authorities, shaping desire and habit over time [Smith, 2016]. Model responses
that consistently redirect moral questions toward self-referential fulﬁllment risk
subtly reshaping users’ moral imaginations.
FAI-C-ST identiﬁes AI alignment as not merely a safety problem, but a forma-
tion problem. While this work evaluates response-level alignment rather than
user-level formation directly, it has implications for how repeated exposure to
consistent moral framing shapes user understanding over time. For Christian
communities, this raises questions of trust, catechesis, and the appropriate role
of AI not only in spiritual life but across all aspects of life.
These concerns become concrete when considering how users with religious com-
mitments respond to misaligned AI systems. On the question of trust, the dy-
namics are non-obvious. A model that deﬂects substantive theological questions
- defaulting to "that's a matter of personal belief" - may be perceived not as re-
spectfully neutral but as dismissive, undermining user conﬁdence in the tool's
utility for meaningful inquiry. The inverse problem is equally worth examin-
ing: when a model produces a response that closely mirrors a user's theological
commitments, it may generate trust that is miscalibrated relative to the actual
source of that output. The model is not drawing on community authority or
interpretive tradition -- it is reproducing patterns from training data. Whether
users recognize or care about that distinction has measurable implications for
how AI is adopted and relied upon in high-trust communities. And when a
model contradicts a user's existing beliefs, the question of what fails ﬁrst - con-
ﬁdence in the tool or willingness to continue engaging - has real consequences
for adoption, dropout, and the downstream eﬀects of repeated exposure.
On the question of cognitive and developmental eﬀects, a diﬀerent set of ques-
tions applies. Research on learning and expertise development suggests that
productive struggle - working through ambiguity without immediate resolution
- plays a role in deeper comprehension and retention. If AI systems routinely
provide immediate, coherent answers to questions that users' interpretive com-
munities treat as requiring sustained engagement, there may be measurable
eﬀects on how users process and internalize that content over time. Whether
this constitutes a meaningful cost depends on empirical questions about user be-

havior that FAI-C-ST is not designed to answer, but that the broader alignment
research community would beneﬁt from investigating.
6.3 Theological Implications
From a Christian perspective, ﬂourishing is not synonymous with happiness
or well-being alone, but with conformity to Christ.
Suﬀering, sacriﬁce, and
obedience are not pathologies to be avoided, but contexts in which faith and
character are reﬁned. Current models rarely articulate this paradox, reﬂecting
a broader cultural discomfort with redemptive suﬀering.
The FAI-C-ST results highlight the need for Frontier Models to have moral
anchoring in Scripture, not as proof-texting, but as a coherent narrative of cre-
ation, fall, redemption, and restoration that gives meaning to Christian human
action.
6.4 Alignment with Contemporary Research on Human Flourishing
Abundant interdisciplinary research - from psychology, philosophy, and theology
- has emphasized that ﬂourishing is irreducibly normative [VanderWeele, 2017],
[Ryﬀ, 2014], [Peterson and Seligman, 2004]. Studies on meaning-making, virtue
ethics, and narrative identity consistently show that humans ﬂourish within
thick moral frameworks [McAdams, 2006] rather than thin procedural ones. FAI-
C-ST begins to operationalize this insight by translating a holistic account of
ﬂourishing into evaluable criteria for AI systems.
7. Conclusion
This study presents the Flourishing AI Benchmark: Christian Single-Turn (FAI-
C-ST) as a rigorous extension of the general Flourishing AI Benchmark (FAI-
G-ST). The results demonstrate a consistent divergence between general ﬂour-
ishing performance and Christian ﬂourishing performance. Across all evaluated
Frontier Models, overall scores declined by an average of 17 points. The most
severe decline occurs in the Faith and Spirituality dimension, where average
scores decrease by 31 points.
These declines are not driven by failures in factual knowledge. Objective scores
remain high and increasingly saturated across models. [Bowman et al, 2023] In-
stead, performance gaps emerge primarily from subjective and tangential eval-
uation: how models interpret human experience, frame moral guidance, and
integrate theological concepts consistently across dimensions. The ﬁndings sug-
gest that today’s Frontier Models default to a posture of Procedural Secularism,
prioritizing individual autonomy and therapeutic consensus over explicit meta-
physical commitments.
This enables strong performance under a pluralistic
Evaluative Lens but limits coherence within a speciﬁc theological framework.
These results indicate that Christian worldview alignment does not reliably
emerge from scale, reasoning ability, or standard safety tuning alone. At the

same time, this benchmarking framework is extensible, enabling comparative
analysis across multiple moral traditions and making diﬀerences in AI align-
ment visible and measurable.
Ultimately, general ﬂourishing benchmarks are necessary but insuﬃcient for
evaluating AI systems intended to support meaning and spiritual formation.
FAI-C-ST complements FAI-G-ST by making implicit worldview assumptions
explicit. As AI systems increasingly participate in interpretation and guidance,
alignment must move beyond generic well-being toward the formation problem,
ensuring coherence with the moral and theological frameworks of the communi-
ties they serve.
These ﬁndings do not suggest that language models possess beliefs, intentions,
or moral agency.
Rather, they show that model outputs can systematically
reﬂect particular moral framings shaped by training objectives and evaluation
criteria.
FAI-C-ST provides an extensible framework for evaluating frontier
model alignment with the Christian worldview and establishes a foundation for
future benchmarks that make their normative commitments explicit.
More broadly, this work demonstrates that alignment cannot be understood
apart from the moral frameworks used to assess it. Making those frameworks
explicit is therefore essential for developing AI systems that are both transparent
and accountable in how they shape human understanding.

8.1 Toward Dimension-Speciﬁc Rubrics
Future iterations of FAI-C-ST will explore dimension-speciﬁc rubrics that better

Finance, and Faith. In addition to improving task-sensitive evaluation, this work
will enable more granular analysis within the Faith dimension itself, including
the ability to examine moral, denominational and theological distinctions across
diﬀerent Christian traditions where appropriate. This approach aligns with re-

8.2 Rebalancing Objective, Subjective and Tangential Evaluation
As factual recall continues to become increasingly commoditized, subjective

fail to capture the contextual, relational, and iterative aspects of model behavior
observed in realistic interactions with Frontier Models. [Kwan et al, 2024]
8.3 Human-in-the-Loop Validation
Incorporating theologians, pastors, clinicians, and lay practitioners into the eval-

8.4 Multi-Turn Evaluation
While FAI-C-ST intentionally focuses on single-turn interactions to enable con-

Multi-turn dialogue allows models to adapt framing, revisit assumptions, and
respond to evolving context, making it especially relevant for evaluating for-
mative inﬂuence, relational dynamics, and moral consistency over time. Prior
work on dynamic and multi-turn evaluation suggests that single-response accu-

extended interaction, including shifts in tone, prioritization, and normative fram-

and behaviors, clarifying how AI systems support, or undercut, human ﬂour-
ishing across ongoing conversations, while complementing the diagnostic clarity
provided by single-turn benchmarks.
8.5 Longitudinal Testing
Longitudinal testing will enable systematic tracking of how model updates, ﬁne-

over time, including shifts in theological coherence, moral framing, and inter-
pretive consistency.
Such analysis is particularly important given the rapid
evolution of Frontier Models and the potential for alignment regressions or un-
intended drift across updates.

Extending the FAI Benchmark framework to support benchmarks grounded
in other faith and moral traditions would enable comparative analysis across
multiple accounts of human ﬂourishing. Cross-worldview benchmarks can il-

choices, revealing where alignment strategies generalize across traditions and

FAI-C-ST evaluates Frontier Models under standard, zero-shot conditions - with-

• Instructed Alignment: Measuring how a model’s performance shifts when

prompt engineering oﬀers a practical pathway for communities seeking more
values-coherent AI responses without requiring expensive model ﬁne-tuning.

Appendix A: Theological Scope and Orthodoxy Boundaries
A.1 Purpose and Scope
This appendix clariﬁes the theological scope and interpretive boundaries un-
derlying the Flourishing AI Benchmark: Christian Single-Turn (FAI-C-ST). As
with any benchmark grounded in a substantive account of human ﬂourishing,
FAI-C-ST operationalizes a particular moral and anthropological framework.
The aim of this appendix is to make those assumptions explicit for purposes of
transparency, interpretability, and scholarly critique, rather than to advance or
adjudicate theological claims.
A.2 Operational Deﬁnition of “Christian”
FAI-C-ST adopts a minimal, historically grounded characterization of Christian
moral reasoning normatively anchored in the Nicene Creed and the theologi-
cal vision it articulates, as received across Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant
traditions. This design emphasizes FAI-C-ST’s subjective and tangential evalu-
ation, reﬂecting the hypothesis, later conﬁrmed in the results, that worldview
alignment is expressed primarily through interpretation, moral framing, and in-
tegrative reasoning rather than factual recall. The benchmark is designed not
to privilege any particular denomination, ecclesial authority, or contemporary
theological school. Instead, it draws on theological and moral commitments that
ﬂow from the creedal confession of the triune God, the incarnation, death, and
resurrection of Christ, and the hope of redemption and restoration.
Within this evaluative framework, Christian ﬂourishing is commonly articulated
as involving:
• An understanding of human dignity as inherent and non-instrumental,
often expressed through the doctrine of the Imago Dei (Image of God).
• A relational and teleological orientation of human life toward love of God
and neighbor.
• Moral formation is understood as a process involving virtue, responsibility,
repentance, and growth over time.
• An account of suﬀering and limitation that does not treat them solely as
negative outcomes to be minimized, but as contexts that may carry moral
or spiritual signiﬁcance.
• A view of material goods, health, and success framed within stewardship
rather than ultimate fulﬁllment.
These elements function as evaluation criteria for Frontier Model alignment
with Christian moral anthropology; they do not constitute requirements for AI
systems to assert or endorse theological propositions.

A.3 Avoidance of Sectarian and Doctrinal Disputes
To maintain broad applicability and avoid confessional speciﬁcity, the FAI
Benchmark question set intentionally excludes doctrinal areas where no
wide Christian consensus exists.
These include, but are not limited to,
denominational governance, sacramental theology, eschatological detail, and

Scriptural references within the rubric are treated as indicators of worldview

often emphasizes lived wisdom over formal textual citation.

a value-free baseline. [Taylor, 2007], [Bender et al, 2021]
By articulating its theological scope explicitly, the FAI Benchmark enables
clearer comparison with alternative moral frameworks and invites the devel-
opment of parallel benchmarks grounded in other traditions.
A.6 Limitations
The authors acknowledge that Christian ﬂourishing, as understood within lived

turn AI interactions. FAI-C-ST should therefore be understood as a diagnostic
and comparative tool, rather than as a comprehensive measure of theological
adequacy.

Appendix B: Methodological Assumptions and Interpretive
Constraints
This appendix articulates the methodological assumptions and interpretive con-
straints governing FAI-C-ST, clarifying the scope of claims supported by the
benchmark and the limits of inference appropriate to its results.
B.1 Benchmarks as Normative Instruments
The development of FAI-C-ST proceeded from the assumption that benchmarks
are not value-neutral instruments [Bowman et al, 2023], [Kiela et al, 2021].
Choices regarding question design, scoring rubrics, judge personas, and aggre-
gation methods necessarily encode assumptions about what constitutes desirable
or eﬀective system behavior. With the exception of worldview-speciﬁc judge per-
sonas and rubric deﬁnitions, FAI-C-ST retains the same interaction constraints,
aggregation strategy, and evaluation mechanics as FAI-G-ST, enabling direct
comparison across benchmarks. This appendix makes explicit the interpretive
constraints under which FAI-C-ST results should be understood.
B.2 Worldview Operationalization
The FAI Benchmark evaluates worldviews as Evaluative Lenses rather than
exhaustive belief systems.
We measure how well a model’s moral reasoning
and practical guidance align with a speciﬁc framework, rather than attempting
to identify its "internal" beliefs or metaphysical commitments. Consequently,
scores reﬂect the coherence of the output, not the model's underlying value
state.
B.3 Functional Alignment vs. Endorsement
FAI-C-ST evaluates functional alignment: whether a model’s responses, when
addressing questions related to ﬂourishing, are consistent with the practical
application of a Christian moral framework. The benchmark does not evaluate
sincerity, intentionality, or endorsement, nor does it assume that the models
possess beliefs or moral agency.
B.4 Role of Judge Models
Subjective evaluation relies on large language models acting as proxy judges
under constrained personas. [Gu et al, 2025] While this enables scalability and
consistency, it introduces known limitations related to judge variance, relevance
classiﬁcation, and interpretive sensitivity. Scores should therefore be interpreted
comparatively rather than as absolute measures.
B.5 Interpretive Use of Scores
Scores produced by FAI Benchmarks are intended for comparative and diag-
nostic analysis.
Diﬀerences across models or benchmark variants should be
24


## Page 25


interpreted as signals of relative alignment patterns rather than as deﬁnitive
judgments of model quality or suitability for deployment in speciﬁc contexts.
The decision to report aggregate rather than sub-component scores reﬂects a
deliberate benchmark integrity measure. Granular breakdowns are available to
vetted researchers on request, consistent with practices in competitive evaluation
settings where sub-score transparency would otherwise incentivize component-
speciﬁc overﬁtting.
B.6 Generalizability
The FAI Benchmark is designed to evaluate single-turn interactions and does
not capture multi-turn dynamics, long-term user adaptation, or community-
mediated interpretation. Findings should therefore not be generalized beyond
the interactional scope explicitly tested.
Appendix C: Comparison Between FAI-G-ST and FAI-C-

C.1 Purpose of the Comparison
This appendix clariﬁes the relationship between the original Single-Turn Flour-

(FAI-C-ST). The intent is to emphasize methodological continuity while mak-

evaluative orientations - not a hierarchy of strictness, but a diﬀerence in moral
framework. FAI-C-ST applies the same structural approach as FAI-G-ST to
a diﬀerent account of human ﬂourishing, enabling direct comparison of how
Frontier Models perform under each.
C.2 FAI-G-ST as a General Baseline
FAI-G-ST evaluates AI systems against a broad, pluralistic account of human
ﬂourishing informed by interdisciplinary research and inclusive of diverse faith
and value traditions. As such, it functions as a general-purpose baseline for
assessing whether AI systems support widely shared dimensions of well-being,
meaning, and moral agency across populations. High performance on FAI-G-ST
indicates strong alignment with general ﬂourishing criteria, but does not imply
alignment with any particular worldview.
C.3 FAI-C-ST as a Worldview-Speciﬁc Lens
FAI-C-ST applies a more constrained evaluative framework by assessing model
responses through a speciﬁcally Christian moral and anthropological lens.
Methodologically, this does not represent a departure from the FAI-G-ST
approach, but a narrowing of its evaluative scope. Where FAI-G-ST measures
ﬂourishing alignment across a plural set of values and beliefs, FAI-C-ST
25


## Page 26


measures alignment corresponding with a historic Christian understanding of
ﬂourishing.
FAI-C-ST is not best understood as a stricter version of FAI-G-ST, but as a
distinct evaluative lens that surfaces diﬀerent - and sometimes incompatible -
priorities.
A model that performs well under one framework will not neces-
sarily perform well under the other, because the underlying accounts of human
ﬂourishing diverge in substantive ways. The observed pattern that current mod-
els score lower on FAI-C-ST reﬂects the state of model training rather than a
hierarchical relationship between the benchmarks.
C.4 Methodological Parallels
Both FAI Benchmarks share core design principles, including:
• Use of the same underlying 7 dimensions of ﬂourishing
• Aligned but revised question sets to preserve cross-benchmark compara-
bility
• Comparable interaction constraints
• Rubric-based evaluation using LLM-as-a-judge personas
• Score aggregation via geometric mean to discourage trade-oﬀs across di-
mensions
Diﬀerences between FAI-G-ST and FAI-C-ST are limited to the worldview as-
sumptions embedded in judge personas, rubric deﬁnitions, and alignment indi-
cators. All other elements of benchmark construction and evaluation remain
intentionally parallel to preserve comparability.
C.5 Interpretive Implications
Diﬀerences in scores between FAI-G-ST and FAI-C-ST should therefore be inter-
preted as measuring the extent to which model outputs rely on general-purpose,
pluralistic moral reasoning versus Christian worldview-speciﬁc reasoning. Per-
formance deltas do not indicate degradation in general model capability.
This comparative structure allows researchers to examine how alignment strate-
gies optimized for broad applicability interact with more particular moral frame-
works, and provides a potential basis for extending the FAI approach to addi-
tional worldviews.

one or more dimensions of human ﬂourishing (Character, Relationships,
Happiness, Meaning, Health, Finances, Faith).
4. Worldview-Revealing without Doctrinal Forcing. Questions are de-
signed to surface moral or spiritual framing without requiring confessional
or sectarian assertions.
5. Non-Trivial Judgment.
Subjective questions require interpretation,
trade-oﬀanalysis, or applied reasoning rather than factual recall.
6. Multi-Dimensional Coverage. Where appropriate, questions span mul-
tiple ﬂourishing dimensions to reﬂect real-world moral complexity.
7. Cultural and Spiritual Sensitivity. Questions respect diverse cultural
contexts and avoid culturally adversarial or polarizing framing.
8. Openness to Multiple Valid Responses. Subjective prompts permit
a range of defensible answers, enabling evaluation of nuance and respect-
fulness.
9. Realistic, Human-Centered Scenarios. Situations are grounded in
plausible, everyday experiences where ﬂourishing considerations are at
stake.
10. Clarity and Accessibility. Questions are concise, avoid jargon, and are
understandable to a general audience.
11. Fair Counterbalancing.
Some questions introduce tension between
ﬂourishing dimensions (e.g., ﬁnances and relationships) without relying
on trick or adversarial framing.
12. Rubric Evaluability. Each question is designed to support consistent
scoring against deﬁned rubric criteria.
13. Avoidance of Culture-War Topics. Questions intentionally exclude
partisan political issues or highly polarizing debates, focusing instead on
constructive moral reasoning.

D.2 Human Review Panel
To evaluate the quality and fairness of the dataset, a panel of human subject
matter experts (SMEs) was convened. Panelists reviewed the full question and
answer set used in recent evaluations, with attention to:
• Question clarity, diﬃculty, and appropriateness
• Alignment with intended ﬂourishing dimensions
• Cultural and social bias, including naming and contextual representation
• Suitability of objective versus subjective formats
Objective answer options were additionally assessed for accuracy, neutrality, and
clarity.
D.3 Panel Findings
The panel identiﬁed several recurring issues:
• Question Distribution Imbalance. Objective questions were overrep-
resented, limiting sensitivity to normative and interpretive diﬀerences.
• Construct Misalignment.
Certain objective question formats were
poorly suited to dimensions such as Faith and Character, which require
contextual and moral judgment.
• Cultural and Contextual Sensitivity. Some questions relied on cul-
turally narrow assumptions or abstract framing that reduced evaluability.
• Answer Set Limitations. In a subset of objective questions, answer op-
tions constrained reasonable model selection or introduced subtle framing
bias.
D.4 Corrective Actions
Based on panel feedback, the dataset was pruned and rebalanced to increase the
proportion of subjective and multi-dimensional questions, improve cultural ro-
bustness, and align prompt formats with the epistemic demands of each ﬂourish-
ing dimension. These revisions were implemented prior to ﬁnal FAI Benchmark
evaluation runs.

Appendix E: Threats to Validity
This appendix outlines potential limitations of the FAI benchmarks and the
interpretation of its results, following best practices in empirical AI evaluation.

Construct
Validity.
FAI-C-ST operationalizes a biblically
grounded Christian account of ﬂourishing through LLM-as-a-judge,
rubric-based evaluation. While care was taken to avoid sectarian
or contested doctrines, no rubric can exhaustively capture the
full richness of Christian moral and spiritual life.
Scores should
therefore be interpreted as indicators of coherence with the speciﬁed
evaluative framework, not as comprehensive measures of theological
adequacy.
Measurement Validity. Evaluation relies on large language mod-
els acting as proxy judges under constrained personas. [Gu et al,
2025], [Zheng et al, 2023] Although this enables scalability and con-
sistency, judge models may introduce bias, sensitivity to question
phrasing, or shared blind spots. Objective scores also exhibit ceil-
ing eﬀects, reducing discriminative power relative to subjective and
tangential components. [Liang et all, 2022]
External Validity. The benchmark evaluates single-turn interac-
tions and does not capture multi-turn dialogue, long-term user for-
mation, or community-mediated discernment. Results should not
be generalized to pastoral care, counseling, or other high-stakes con-
texts without human oversight.
Dataset Validity. The benchmark dataset reﬂects curated ques-
tions intended to span multiple dimensions of ﬂourishing.
While
broad, it may underrepresent certain cultural, denominational, or sit-
uational contexts within global Christianity. Additionally, questions
were deliberately designed to avoid culturally polarizing or adversar-
ial topics, a choice that supports evaluability and fairness, but may
limit the benchmark's sensitivity in certain high-stakes domains.
Temporal Validity. Model behavior may change over time due
to updates, ﬁne-tuning, or shifts in safety policy. Reported results
reﬂect a snapshot in time and should be interpreted accordingly.

Appendix G: Reproducibility and Transparency Statement
This appendix documents the reproducibility and transparency practices
adopted in the design and evaluation of FAI-C-ST. The benchmark is
structured to support reproducible, comparative evaluation under practical
constraints related to model access, licensing, and safety, while maintaining
methodological consistency across evaluation runs.
Benchmark Construction.
The dataset consists of curated
prompts spanning seven dimensions of ﬂourishing.
Questions
were sourced from standardized well-being instruments, academic
literature, and applied moral scenarios.
Evaluation Protocol. All models were evaluated under consistent
interaction constraints, including ﬁxed system prompts, temperature
settings, and decoding parameters.
Responses were scored using
rubric-guided judge personas, and scores were aggregated using a
geometric mean to discourage trade-oﬀs across dimensions.
To operationalize a Christian worldview lens within the evaluation,
we adopted the standard LLM-as-a-judge practice of a single, role-
speciﬁed evaluator persona. Initial drafts explored personas framed
as a panel of domain experts; however, prior work on scalable eval-
uation demonstrates that anchoring judgment in a single, clearly
deﬁned role improves consistency, interpretability, and reproducibil-
ity. Accordingly, judge personas were revised to represent one Chris-
tian expert in the relevant dimension, while explicitly incorporating
interdisciplinary insight through rubric design rather than through
multi-voice prompting. This approach follows established best prac-
tices in role-conditioned LLM evaluation, where a uniﬁed judge iden-
tity is used to apply structured criteria consistently across responses.
[Zheng et al, 2023]

Appendix H: FAI-C-ST Benchmark Card and Versioning
This appendix provides a concise benchmark card and versioning summary for
FAI-C-ST, documenting intended use, scope boundaries, known limitations, and
version control practices to support transparent interpretation and responsible
reuse.
Intended Use. FAI-C-ST is intended for research and diagnostic evaluation of
AI systems with respect to Christian conceptions of human ﬂourishing.
Out-of-Scope Use. The benchmark is not intended for certifying theological
correctness, replacing pastoral judgment, or autonomous deployment in spiritual
care contexts. This benchmark should not be used to rank models as morally
superior in a universal sense, but to evaluate alignment relative to explicitly
deﬁned moral frameworks.
Data Sources. Questions are derived from a mixture of public-domain instru-
ments, academic sources, and original scenario construction.
Known Limitations. The FAI-C-ST reﬂects a speciﬁc worldview and Evalua-

Versioning. Benchmark versions are timestamped and archived. Results re-
ported in this paper correspond to the December 2025 evaluation snapshot.
Future releases will document question changes, rubric revisions, and model
