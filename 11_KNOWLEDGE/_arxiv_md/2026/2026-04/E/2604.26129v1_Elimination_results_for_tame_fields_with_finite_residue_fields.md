ELIMINATION RESULTS FOR TAME FIELDS WITH FINITE RESIDUE FIELDS
SYLVY ANSCOMBE AND BLAISE BOISSONNEAU
Abstract. Building on work of Kuhlmann and Lisinski, we study the theory of the Hahn series field 𝔽𝑞((𝑡ℚ)), over a finite field
𝔽𝑞, equipped with the 𝑡-adic valuation, in a language of valued fields. We prove that every formula is equivalent to a formula
∃𝑦∶𝑓(𝑥1, … , 𝑥𝑛, 𝑦) = 0, for a polynomial 𝑓∈ℤ[𝑥1, … , 𝑥𝑛, 𝑦].
1. Introduction
We extend the known model theory of tame and separably tame valued fields, in the sense of Kuhlmann, specifically
those with a finite residue field. We obtain a relative quantifier elimination-style result, complementing completeness
and decidability results of Lisinski, which yields a simpler description of the definable sets in such structures. This
applies in particular to fields of Hahn series 𝑘((𝑡Γ)) for which the residue field 𝑘is finite and the value group 𝑝-divisible,
where 𝑝is the characteristic of 𝑘.
Kuhlmann’s theory of tame and separably tame valued fields was developed in [Kuh16, KP16, Ans25], in which it was
proved that separably tame valued fields satisfy a range of “Ax–Kochen/Ershov principles”. Extensions to this theory
can also be found in [KR23], who investigate “semi-tame” valued fields, and in [JK23], who study “roughly tame” valued
fields. As an example, is it proved in [Kuh16] that tame valued fields of equal characteristic form an AKE≡-class: that
is, two such valued fields (𝐾, 𝑣) and (𝐿, 𝑤) are elementarily equivalent in the language Lval of valued fields if and only
if their residue fields are elementarily equivalent in the language of rings Lring and their value groups are elementarily
equivalent in the language of ordered abelian groups Loag. On the other hand, it is clear—see [AK16]—that tame valued
fields of mixed characteristic are not an AKE≡-class. A deeper analysis of the mixed characteristic case can be found in
the recent paper [KD26].
Lisinksi’s work, including two papers1 [Lis23a, Lis23b] and his thesis [Lis21], contain the following theorem about
theories of certain tame valued fields of Hahn series in the language Lval(𝜛) of valued fields enriched by a constant
symbol 𝜛for the formal variable.
Theorem (Lisinski, [Lis23a, Theorem 1]). For every perfect field 𝑘and 𝑝-divisible pointed value group (Γ, 𝛾), the Lval(𝜛)-
theory Th(𝑘((𝑡Γ)), 𝑣𝑡, 𝑡) is axiomatized by the theory of equicharacteristic tame valued fields, with the theory Th(𝑘) imposed
on the residue field and the theory Th(Γ, 𝛾) imposed on the pointed value group (i.e. the value group with the value of the
interpretation of 𝜛distinguished) and together with
𝑆= {∃𝑦(𝑓(𝜛, 𝑦) = 0 ∧𝑣(𝑦) ≥0) ∶𝑓∈𝔽𝑝[𝑥, 𝑦] such that 𝑓(𝑡, 𝑦) is monic in 𝑦and has root in 𝑘((𝑡Γ))}.
Consequently, Th(𝑘((𝑡Γ)), 𝑣, 𝑡) is decidable if and only if Th(Γ, 𝛾), Th(𝑘), and 𝑆are decidable.
Combined with a result of Kedlaya from [Ked06], this yields the following.
Corollary (Lisinski, [Lis23a]). For every prime power 𝑞= 𝑝𝓁, both Th(𝔽𝑞((𝑡ℚ)), 𝑣𝑡, 𝑡) and Th(𝔽𝑞((𝑡𝑝−∞ℤ)), 𝑣𝑡, 𝑡) are decidable.

5. The additive fragment is NTP2
We now circle back to the question that has haunted us for 6 years:
Question 5.1. Is 𝔽𝑞((𝑡ℚ)) NTP2?
This question fits into the more general framework of classification/neo-stability, which explores links between
model-theoretic dividing lines and algebraic properties of pure fields, valued fields, or other structures such as ordered
fields or difference fields. In chronological order, [HHJ20, AJ24, Boi24b] have established classifications of strongly
dependent, NIP, and finally NIP𝑛henselian valued fields, down to their residue field.
Our friend 𝔽𝑞((𝑡ℚ)) has IP𝑛, as it has one Artin-Schreier extension, see [KSW11, Hem16]. Thus, it is out of the scope

dividing line to study those fields is NTP2, introduced by Shelah in [Sh80], which encompasses both Simple and NIP
theories, and is defined as follows:

𝑎𝑖𝑗∈𝑀|𝑦| such that {𝜑(𝑥, 𝑎𝑖𝑗)}𝑗<𝜔is 𝑘-inconsistent for every 𝑖< 𝜔and {𝜑(𝑥, 𝑎𝑖,𝑓(𝑖))}𝑖<𝜔is consistent for any 𝑓∶𝜔→𝜔.
Otherwise, we say that 𝜑(𝑥, 𝑦) is NTP2. We also say that a theory 𝑇is NTP2 if every formula is NTP2 in 𝑇, and that a
structure 𝑀is NTP2 if Th(𝑀) is NTP2.
We refer to [Che14] for details on NTP2.
A generic goal is to obtain an optimal NTP2 transfer theorem, that is, a classification of NTP2 henselian valued
field down to their residue field. The aforementioned work of Chernikov does so in equicharacteristic 0, see [Che14,
Theorem 7.6], but for other characteristics, we are far from a full classification. The strongest known results can be
found in [Boi22]. Let us focus on what happens in equicharacteristic 𝑝:
Proposition 5.3 ([Boi22, Propositions 2.5.17 and 2.5.19]). Let (𝐾, 𝑣) be a henselian valued field of positive characteristic.
If (𝐾, 𝑣) is NTP2, then it is semitame in the sense of [KR23], in particular, its value group is 𝑝-divisible and its residue field
is perfect. If (𝐾, 𝑣) is separably algebraic maximal Kaplansky and its residue field is NTP2, then (𝐾, 𝑣) is NTP2.
This led us to phrase the following:
Conjecture 5.4. Let (𝐾, 𝑣) be a henselian valued field of positive characteristic. Then (𝐾, 𝑣) is NTP2 if and only if it is tame
and its residue field is NTP2.
The Hahn series field 𝔽𝑞((𝑡ℚ)) is the simplest case we could possibly study, as it is tame with divisible value group
and finite residue field. Hence, a first step towards the conjecture is to determine whether 𝔽𝑞((𝑡ℚ)) is NTP2. Despite all
the efforts thrown at it, to this day, it still resits.
Definition 5.5. Let 𝑝be a prime number. The additive fragment 𝐹+ is the Lring-fragment generated by formulas of the
form ∃𝑤𝑃(𝑥, 𝑤) = 𝑦, where 𝑃∈𝔽𝑝[𝑥, 𝑤] is additive with respect to 𝑤. It is a sub-fragment of the Galois fragment
∃Pos
1 (Lring).
The work of Kaplan, Scanlon, and Wagner [KSW11] established a clear link between field extensions generated by
Artin-Schreier roots and dividing lines. Their work was then extended to NTP2 by Chernikov, Kaplan, and Simon. As
noted in [Boi22, Section 2.5.8], their methods can be extended to additive polynomials. More precisely:
Lemma 5.6. Let 𝐾be a field of characteristic 𝑝> 0 such that, for each 𝑛∈ℕ>0, 𝐾has finitely many Galois extensions of
degree 𝑝𝑛. Let 𝜓(𝑥; 𝑦, 𝑧) be a formula of the form ⋀𝑛
𝑖=1(∃𝑤𝑃𝑖(𝑦, 𝑤) = 𝑥−𝑧), where 𝑃𝑖(𝑦, 𝑤) ∈𝔽𝑝[𝑦, 𝑤] is additive with
respect to 𝑤. Then 𝜓is NTP2.
Proof. We follow the strategy appearing in the proof of [Boi24a, Corollary 4.8]. For each 𝑎∈𝐾|𝑦|, let 𝐻𝑎be ⋂𝑛
𝑖=1 𝑃𝑖(𝑎, 𝐾).
Since each 𝑃𝑖is additive, 𝐻𝑎is an additive subgroup of 𝐾. Algebraically speaking, the formula 𝜓(𝑥; 𝑎, 𝑧) is saying
“𝑥∈𝐻𝑎+ 𝑧”. By assumption on 𝐾, [𝐾∶𝐻𝑎] is bounded, and thus by [Boi24a, Porism 4.6] 𝜓is NTP2.
□
Since disjunctions of NTP2 formulas are NTP2, we obtain the following:
Corollary 5.7. Let 𝐾be a field of characteristic 𝑝> 0 such that for each 𝑛, 𝐾has finitely many Galois extension of degree
𝑝𝑛. Then any formula in the additive fragment of 𝐾is NTP2. In particular, this holds for 𝔽𝑞((𝑡ℚ)).
Recall that by Theorem 4.8, any Lval-formula is equivalent in the theory of 𝔽𝑝((𝑡ℚ)) to a formula of the form ∃𝑦𝑃(𝑥, 𝑦) =
0 for arbitrary polynomials 𝑃∈𝔽𝑝[𝑥, 𝑦]. Meanwhile, formulas in the additive fragment are of a similar form with the
extra requirement that the polynomials are additive. In order to prove NTP2, we would need to reduce from the arbitrary
case to the additive case, something that we cannot do as of now.
It is well-known that 𝔽𝑞((𝑡ℚ)) does not admit quantifier elimination, either in the language of valued fields, or in the
language Lac of ac-valued fields. In particular: the image of the Artin–Schreier function ℘𝑞∶𝔽𝑞((𝑡ℚ)) →𝔽𝑞((𝑡ℚ)) is not
quantifier-free Lac-definable.
