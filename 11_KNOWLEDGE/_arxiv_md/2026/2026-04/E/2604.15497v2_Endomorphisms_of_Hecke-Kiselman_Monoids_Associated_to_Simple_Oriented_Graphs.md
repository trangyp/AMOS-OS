Endomorphisms of Hecke–Kiselman Monoids
Associated to Simple Oriented Graphs
Luka Andrenšek
Abstract
Let HKΘ denote the Hecke–Kiselman monoid associated to a ﬁnite simple ori-
ented graph Θ. We present a Boolean matrix monoid that is isomorphic to the
endomorphism monoid End(HKΘ).
Keywords: Hecke–Kiselman monoid, endomorphism monoid, idempotent, Boolean
matrix monoid
1 Introduction and Main Results
Let Θ = (V, E) be a ﬁnite simple oriented graph, that is, a ﬁnite oriented graph with
no multiple arrows and no oriented cycles of length one or two. If (i, j) ∈E, we say
that there is an arrow from i to j, and we write i →j. If there are no arrows i →j
or j →i and i ̸= j, we say that i and j are not connected, or disconnected.
In [8], Ganyushkin and Mazorchuk deﬁne the Hecke–Kiselman monoid HKΘ
associated to Θ as the monoid generated by idempotents
{xi | i ∈V }
subject to the following deﬁning relations for all i, j ∈V :
• xixj = xjxi if i and j are not connected;
• xixjxi = xjxixj = xixj if i →j.
We denote by e the unit element of HKΘ.
Hecke–Kiselman monoids were originally introduced in the setting of simple mixed
graphs [8]. They have been studied by various authors [1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 11, 16]. Particular
interest has been devoted to the corresponding Hecke–Kiselman algebras [12–15].
From the proof of [8, Theorem 16], it follows that every monoid isomorphism
ϕ : HKΘ →HKΘ′ induces a graph isomorphism fϕ : Θ →Θ′ and every graph isomor-
phism f : Θ →Θ′ induces a monoid isomorphism ϕf : HKΘ →HKΘ′. Setting Θ′ = Θ,

A description of endomorphisms of the Kiselman monoid [6, 9, 10], which is

For a positive integer k, we denote [k] = {1, 2, . . . , k}. Throughout the paper, we
assume V = [n] for some positive integer n. In Section 2, we deﬁne AΘ to be the set
of all subsets X ⊆V such that the full subgraph of Θ with vertex set X does not
contain any oriented cycles. It is shown that for each X ∈AΘ, there exists a unique
idempotent eX in HKΘ with content X.
For X, Y ∈AΘ, we deﬁne the predicate p as follows. Condition p(X, Y ) holds if
and only if there does not exist a directed path in Θ which starts in some vertex in
Y \ X, never leaves the vertex set X ∪Y , and ends in some vertex in X \ Y .
We prove that for X, Y ∈AΘ, we have
p(X, Y ) ⇐⇒eXeY is an idempotent in HKΘ.
We deﬁne (Θ, p)-pure sequences of subsets of [n] to be sequences (X1, X2, . . . , Xn)
such that, for all i, j ∈[n], the following conditions are satisﬁed:
(i) Xi ∈AΘ;
(ii) p(Xi, Xj) and p(Xj, Xi) hold if i and j are not connected;
(iii) p(Xi, Xj) holds if i →j.
We denote the set of all such sequences by Sn(Θ, p). We further deﬁne a natural binary
operation ∗on Sn(Θ, p) and show that it becomes a monoid with this operation.
The main result of this paper is that
End(HKΘ) ∼= (Sn(Θ, p), ∗).
Using the characteristic-matrix encoding of such sequences, we then obtain a Boolean
matrix monoid isomorphic to End(HKΘ).
The paper proceeds as follows. In Section 2, we recall some results on Hecke–
Kiselman monoids which we use throughout the paper. In Section 3, we give a
necessary and suﬃcient condition on X, Y ∈AΘ such that eXeY is an idempotent in
HKΘ. In Section 4, we prove the main result.

2.3 Idempotents in HKΘ

We identify HKΘ[X] with its image in HKΘ. Then HKΘ[X] is a submonoid of HKΘ.
In [7, Theorem 3.6], the following theorem is proved for a general simple oriented
graph Θ.
Theorem 2.4. There is a bijection between idempotents in HKΘ and full subgraphs
of Θ which do not contain any oriented cycles.

is the content of the idempotent. Therefore, for each X ⊆V such that Θ[X] does not
contain any oriented cycles, there exists exactly one idempotent in HKΘ with content
X. We denote this idempotent by eX.
