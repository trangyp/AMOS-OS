ELECTRONIC FINAL STATES IN NUCLEAR β DECAY: A
SUDDEN-APPROXIMATION FRAMEWORK
G. V. D’YAKONOV
Abstract. Electronic final states generated by sudden changes of the Hamil-
tonian are studied here, with emphasis on nuclear charge variation in β decay.
A λ-parametrized family ˆH(λ) that continuously connects the initial and final
Hamiltonians, so that the electronic response can be represented as a contin-
uous deformation in Hilbert space, is introduced.
Within the sudden approximation, transition amplitudes are written as
overlaps between eigenstates of distinct Hamiltonians. To relate non-orthogonal
one-electron basis sets in a stable way, the paper uses a practical transport
scheme based on overlap metrics and truncated singular value decomposition
(SVD). This mapping is interpreted as a discrete counterpart of continuous
transport along the λ path.
The formalism is first developed for the one-
electron case, where analytic structure and selection rules are made explicit,
and then generalized to many-electron systems via nonorthogonal determinant
overlap expressions. The resulting formulation gives transition probabilities in
bound and continuum channels in a way that is both numerically stable and
easy to interpret.
1. Introduction
Nuclear β decay in an atomic or molecular system induces an abrupt change
in the electronic Hamiltonian through a change in nuclear charge. In β−decay
the transition is Z →Z + 1, whereas in β+ decay or electron capture it is Z →
Z −1. Consequently, the Coulomb part of the Hamiltonian changes. In the minimal
one-electron setting this primarily affects the electron–nucleus interaction. In the
general many-electron molecular case, electron–electron and internuclear (nucleus–
nucleus) contributions must also be included, so the full spectrum of stationary
electronic states at fixed nuclear geometry is rearranged.
A central systematic limitation of the present benchmark is its nonrelativistic
character. For the light channel 3H →3He+ this approximation is well controlled,
while for increasing Z the role of relativistic structure grows and must be treated
explicitly.
The relevant physical point is the separation of time scales: nuclear transmuta-
tion occurs much faster than electronic relaxation. Therefore, the sudden approx-
imation applies [13, 9], in which the electronic wave function is continuous at the
decay instant,

This problem is not only theoretical.
Electronic rearrangement after nuclear
transmutation enters the interpretation of precision β-decay spectra, ionization
yields in radioactive atoms and molecules, charge-state distributions relevant for
detector response, and the modeling of materials or molecular environments that
contain unstable nuclei. It is also relevant whenever one needs reliable electronic
final-state probabilities after a sudden change of nuclear charge, for example in
decay-assisted spectroscopy and related nuclear, atomic, and chemical applications.
A transparent theoretical framework is therefore needed for relating the initial
electronic state to the full set of final bound and continuum channels after such
a transition.

The aim is to give such a framework in a simple analytically controlled setting.
More specifically, exact one-electron benchmark results are derived, a mathemati-
cally consistent λ-path connecting the initial and final Hamiltonians is formulated,
and an operator-level many-electron projection formalism is set up for later quan-
titative work.
A historical reference in this direction is Ref. [8]. In that work, within the sud-
den approximation, the transition probability is formulated for the full N-electron
system; initial and final states are approximated by Slater determinants built from
Hartree–Fock orbitals; and matrix elements are evaluated through determinants
and radial overlap integrals. This line follows earlier shake-process foundations due
to Migdal’s sudden-perturbation treatment [13] and yielded a practically imple-
mentable scheme for calculating excitation and ionization of many-electron atoms.
At the same time, Ref. [8] emphasizes limitations of earlier approaches: the use
of purely hydrogen-like functions (effectively accurate mainly for the K shell) and
simplified “one-electron” transition formulae, which become internally inconsistent
for global changes of the nuclear field in a many-electron problem.
Here these earlier results serve mainly as context. The focus is on the λ-family
and on what can be obtained analytically from the exact one-electron solution. This
gives a one-electron benchmark derivation together with an operator-level extension
to nonorthogonal many-electron manifolds. Claims are limited to the sudden, fixed-
geometry, nonrelativistic model. Large-scale many-electron calculations are left for
later work.
In this formulation, λ is more than an interpolation parameter: it sets the path
between ˆHi and ˆHf and organizes the relation between electronic subspaces, am-
plitudes, and channels. In practical calculations, a finite λ grid supports branch
tracking by maximum overlap, so spectral continuity is checked rather than as-
sumed.
In mathematical terms, this construction is a λ-homotopy between endpoint
Hamiltonians. It does not replace the physical sudden approximation by adiabatic
time evolution: the decay event remains instantaneous at t = 0.
Instead, the
homotopy provides an auxiliary nonadiabatic continuation in Hilbert space, which
regularizes endpoint matching and makes the transition geometry between initial
and final manifolds explicit.
Endpoint matching by truncated SVD can be viewed as a discrete approximation
to this λ-transport: it keeps maximally overlapping subspaces and suppresses near-
null directions that destabilize numerics. In this paper, the λ ↔SVD relation links
the transport picture to implementable formulas.
Taken together, these results give a one-electron benchmark and a metric-consistent
many-electron projection formalism for later work.

defines a minimal route to many-electron calculations:
(1) choose molecular geometry and the transmuted center B, then construct
ˆHe(λ) on a λ grid;
(2) construct variational many-electron approximations to the relevant station-
ary states of ˆHe(λ) along the path, with orbitals and expansion coefficients
adapted to the instantaneous Hamiltonian;
(3) improve these approximations systematically within a controlled hierarchi-
cal sequence of configuration spaces or correlated subspaces;
(4) solve generalized eigenproblems in metric-consistent form and regularize
near-null metric modes;

5. Conclusions and Scope
A sudden-approximation framework for electronic final states after nuclear β
decay has been formulated. In the one-electron benchmark 3H(1s) →3He+, it yields
explicit branch relations for En(λ), closed-form bound and continuum amplitudes,
and the completeness closure Pbound ≈97.37%, Pcont ≈2.63%.
At the many-electron level, the projection structure needed for future extensions
has been written explicitly: nonorthogonal overlap objects, determinant contrac-
tions, and metric-consistent interpolation along λ.
The main systematic uncertainty of the present benchmark is the nonrelativistic
approximation. It is subleading for 3H(1s) →3He+, but it becomes important for
larger nuclear charges. The natural continuation is a Dirac–Coulomb version of the
same framework, with Breit and QED corrections added when high-Z accuracy is
needed.
