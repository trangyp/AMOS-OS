I.  INTRODUCTION 
The nuclear magnetic resonance free induction decays (FID`s) in      constitute 
almost ideal experimental data to validate quantum dynamical aspects in Hamiltonian 
systems. Since      , with 100% natural abundance, has spin I = ½ the interactions are 
purely dipole-dipole without any quadrupolar broadening effects. Furthermore the     
spins form a simple rigid cubic lattice with no motional effects at room temperature or 
below. Moreover, among the calcium isotopes      has spin I = 0 and     , the only 
isotope with non-zero spin, is only 0.13% abundant.  
Given that nuclear magnetic moments are exceedingly small, Zeeman energies in 
typical laboratory magnetic fields are much smaller than    even at liquid Helium 
temperatures. Thus, one is dealing, to an excellent approximation, with a simple cubic 
lattice of spin ½    nuclei interacting via the truncated (1) dipolar Hamiltonian in an 
infinite temperature regime. Given these unique characteristics, many theoretical 
investigations have referred to      NMR FID data as a reliable benchmark to 
ascertain the correctness of their results (2-8). 
Among reported FID data in      those of Ref. (9) span two orders of magnitude of 
signal amplitude. Furthermore, the positions of the first eight zeros of the FID are 
listed for a magnetic field along the       crystal direction. These data, although 
obtained many years ago, contain an experimental verification of a now-recognized 
universal hypothesis. 
Although more recent advances in instrumentation could extend this range by two 
orders of magnitude (10), some asymptotic features, like the exponential decay 
reported in Ref (9) and considered to be of a universal nature (6), were confirmed. For 
the present investigation, the range spanned by the data in Ref (9) was found to be 
adequate and we employed them throughout this paper.

From the data of table I one observes a striking inversion between the positions of the 
singularities for the orientations [110] and [111]. If one adopts the values of the root 
second moments as a measure of the strength of the interactions, the [110] orientation 
has a strength 48.5% higher than the orientation [111], but the position of the 
singularity A[110]=115      can be seen to occur at a larger time than 
A[111]=105.9     . The [100] orientation, with A[100] = 49.69       corresponds to 
the strongest interaction. 
The reason for this inversion may be related to the different degrees of spatial 
connectivity of the spin-spin interactions. Considering only interactions of a spin with 
its six nearest-neighbors, 81% of the total second moment for a magnetic field along 
[100] comes from interactions with its two nearest neighbors along a [100] chain. 
However, unlike the 3-d case of Eq.3, 1-d systems show sub-linear growth in the 
Lanczos coefficients (2) and the singularity may become unobservable. 
 Unlike this 1-d tendency for the [110] orientation, for a [111] orientation dipole-
dipole interactions with the six nearest neighbors vanish. Since the 12 next-nearest 
neighbors are distributed more isotropically the 3-d character is dominant, and the 
connectivity is large.

CONCLUSIONS 
We conclude that a particular function with branch-point singularities in its analytic 
continuation provides an excellent description of the FID in     . No entire function, 
of finite or even infinite degree (Eq.9) could be found that fits better the data. This 
strongly supports the hypothesis of Parker et al. (2) and permits to obtain numerical 
values for the growth parameter   of the theory. The experimental conditions for the 
observability of a such singularities are discussed and a method for revealing their 
presence is proposed.
