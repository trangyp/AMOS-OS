Energy Efficiency Maximization for Discrete Activation based
NOMA-assisted Pinching-Antenna Systems
Yishi Zhang, Student Member, IEEE, Aditya Powari, Kaidi Wang, Member, IEEE,
Yaru Fu, Member, IEEE and Daniel K. C. So, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—Pinching-antenna systems (PASS) have recently at-
tracted significant attention as a promising architecture for
flexible and reconfigurable wireless communications. Despite
notable advancements, research on energy efficiency (EE) max-
imization for PASS is limited as existing studies mainly focus
on transmit power minimization or utilizing a simple power
consumption model. This paper evaluates the impact of pinching
antenna (PA) activation power on EE maximization in a downlink
NOMA-assisted PASS by jointly optimizing PA activation and
user power allocation under quality-of-service and transmit
power constraints. To tackle the resulting mixed-integer non-
linear programming problem, we develop a two-layer iterative
algorithm, where the outer layer performs matching-based PA
selection and the inner layer computes a closed-form optimal
power allocation solution. Numerical results demonstrate that the
proposed solution achieves substantial EE gains over conventional
fixed antennas systems and the considered benchmark schemes,
approaches the exhaustive-search upper bound with significantly
reduced complexity, while exhibiting fast convergence. It also
demonstrates the significance of accounting for PA activation
power in EE maximization problem.
Index Terms—Energy efficiency, pinching antennas, non-
orthogonal multiple access, antenna selection, power allocation.
I. INTRODUCTION
Pinching-antenna systems (PASS) are an emerging flexible-
antenna paradigm where pinching antennas (PAs) are po-
sitioned along dielectric waveguides to form reconfigurable
radiating points [1]. PASS is a promising new technology
for future 6G “last-meter” connectivity. By mechanically
controlling PA locations and activations, PASS can create
strong and controllable line-of-sight-like links, reshape near-
field propagation, and enable scalable array reconfiguration
with low hardware complexity [2], [3].
The reconfigurability of PASS opens up new opportunities
for system-level optimization compared with conventional
antennas systems. Most existing optimization frameworks
idealize PA placement or continuously adjustable along the
waveguide. However, in practical implementations, PAs are
typically realized and installed at a finite set of preconfigured
candidate points due to mechanical mounting complexity, cal-
ibration, and deployment constraints. This motivates a discrete
placement model, which is more amenable to implementation
and thus underpins the system design in this paper.
A considerable amount of prior work has centered on
spectral efficiency (SE) maximization, leading to extensive
and well-established advances [4]–[6]. However, while energy
efficiency (EE) resource allocation is pivotal for sustainability,
existing studies on EE optimization for PASS are insufficient.
Initial efforts have addressed EE optimization in the downlink
time-division multiple access [7] and uplink transmission
scenarios [8]. Moreover, the EE-oriented optimization is fre-
quently simplified to a transmit power minimization problem.
For instance, a recursive closed-form solution is derived by
minimizing transmit power and guaranteed quality-of-service
(QoS) in [9]. This kind of energy efficient optimization scheme
has been extended to multiple waveguides scenarios [10]–
[13]. Additionally, since SE and EE objectives are not always
aligned, particularly in the moderate-to-high SNR regime, the
SE–EE trade-off has been systematically investigated in [14],
[15]. Notably, while limited recent works have also included
the fixed circuit term [7], [8] in the power consumption model,
the model remains incomplete since it either neglects the
additional power consumed by PA activation, such as the
pinching action, or simply treats this term as a predetermined
constant [14], [17] rather than evaluating its impact.
This paper therefore investigates the EE maximization prob-
lem for downlink NOMA-assisted PASS with discrete PA
activation, explicitly accounting for the associated activation
power. The contributions of this paper are listed as follows:
(i) A realistic power consumption model which includes the
static and PA activating power is firstly proposed. The
EE objective and constraints are then presented for the
subsequent optimization and algorithm design.
(ii) Next, a two-layer optimization algorithm is proposed to
solve the EE maximization problem. In the outer layer,
we determine the PA activating scheme based on matching
theory. For the inner layer, a closed-form optimal power
allocation solution for NOMA systems that utilizes Dinkel-
bach method is proposed.
(iii) The proposed algorithm is compared with several bench-
marks and analyzed under different parameter settings,
demonstrating its superior EE performance. The conver-
gence performance of the proposed algorithm is further
evaluated and the significance of PA activation power in
EE maximization is demonstrated.

III. EE-ORIENTED MATCHING FOR PA ACTIVATION
A. One-sided One-to-One Matching Model
To employ matching algorithm, the set of equally-spaced PA
positions is labelled as L = {1, · · · , l, · · · , L}, with Nm = L.
We then model the assignment between PAs index set N and
positions L as a one-sided one-to-one matching (N, L, ≻, Φ0),
where ≻denotes a list of strict preferences of the PAs over
the positions and Φ0 is the initial matching which is randomly
generated.
Definition 1 (One-sided one-to-one matching [5]). A matching
Φ is a mapping Φ : N →L ∪{0} such that
(1) Φ(n) ∈L ∪{0}, ∀n ∈N;
(2) |Φ(n)| ∈{0, 1}, ∀n ∈N; and
(3) Φ(n) = l ⇒Φ(n′) ̸= l, ∀n, n′ ∈N.
Conditions in Definition (1) ensure that each PA is assigned
to at most one position or remains unmatched, while each
position is occupied by at most one PA. Specifically, Φ(n) = l
indicates that the PA at position l is labeled as PA n and

V. SIMULATION RESULTS
This section presents the simulation results of the proposed
joint PA selection and power allocation algorithm (denoted
as “Prop”). We set D1 = 10m, D2 = 20m, the length
of waveguide is equal to D2, d = 3m, L = 40, the
total bandwidth of the system is B = 10 MHz, the carrier
frequency fc = 28 GHz, N0 = −174 dBm/Hz, neff = 1.4,
κ = 0.02 dB/m, Pstatic = 0.1 W and Pact = 3 dBm (5 dBm
in [14], [17] ) unless otherwise stated.
For comparison, several benchmark schemes employing
NOMA are considered: (1) Conventional antennas located

“Conventional”). (2) Minimum transmit power satisfying the
rate constraints (denoted as “Min power”). (3) Selecting the
nearest PAs for each users with optimal power allocation
(denoted as “Nearest”). (4) Exhaustively searching over all
feasible activated PAs sets with optimal power allocation
(denoted as “Exhaustive”).
Fig. 2 compares the EE performance of the proposed
scheme with the benchmarks against Pt when K equals 4. It is
observed that the EE monotonically increases when Pt ≤10
dBm, and then saturates, as further increasing the transmit
power achieves marginal rate gain comparing to the linear
increase in total energy consumption. The proposed algorithm
also outperforms conventional antennas even though the lat-
ter does not have the PA activation power. This highlights
the clear advantage of the proposed NOMA PASS over the
conventional setup. Additionally, the EE performance of the
considered system increases with the number of users. The

feasible activation options are limited to 1 to 3 with no smaller
configuration available; hence, although the activation ratio
does vary, the resulting average remains clustered around 2.
Fig. 4 shows the convergence behavior of the proposed algo-
rithm. In Fig. 4(a), EE increases monotonically and approaches
the exhaustive search result. A larger number of candidate PAs
results in slightly higher EE but slower convergence due to
the enlarged search space. In Fig. 4(b), the average number
of activated PAs gradually decreases and stabilizes near the
exhaustive search result, showing that redundant activated PAs
can be effectively removed while preserving near-optimal EE.
The results further indicate that a strict one-to-one mapping
between users and PAs is not required, since a smaller PA
set can optimally serve multiple users with lower power
consumption.

with PA activation power consumption for a NOMA-assisted
downlink PASS. A two-layer optimization algorithm is pro-
posed with a matching-based procedure in the outer layer
to determine the activated PAs, and the Dinkelbach-based
inner layer for optimal power allocation. Numerical results
demonstrate that the proposed algorithm converges rapidly
