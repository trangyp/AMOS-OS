DANIEL GALVIZ
Abstract. We prove a natural isomorphism between toral Chern–Simons theory with
gauge group T = t/Λ ∼= U(1)n and the Reshetikhin–Turaev theory associated with
the finite quadratic module determined by an even, integral, nondegenerate symmetric
bilinear form K : Λ × Λ →Z. More precisely, let GK = Λ∗/KΛ be the discriminant
group of K, equipped with its induced quadratic form qK, and let C(GK, qK) be the
corresponding pointed modular category. Using the geometric quantization formulation of
toral Chern–Simons theory, we show that the resulting TQFT is naturally isomorphic to
the Reshetikhin–Turaev TQFT determined by C(GK, qK). The equivalence is established
at the level of closed 3–manifold invariants, bordism operators for manifolds with boundary,
and the extended (2+1)-dimensional structure, yielding a natural isomorphism of extended
TQFTs.
Main Theorem. Let T = t/Λ ∼= U(1)n be a compact torus, let K : Λ × Λ →Z be an
even, integral, nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form, let (GK, qK) be the associated
finite quadratic module, and let C(GK, qK) denote the corresponding pointed modular
category. Then the Reshetikhin–Turaev theory associated with C(GK, qK) is naturally
isomorphic, as an extended (2 + 1)-dimensional TQFT, to the toral Chern–Simons theory
with gauge group T and level K. Equivalently, the two theories define naturally isomorphic
symmetric monoidal functors

1. Introduction
The equivalence between Abelian Chern–Simons theory [Wit89] and Reshetikhin–Turaev
theory [RT91] in the U(1) case was established in [Gal26a], where the U(1) Chern–Simons
TQFT at even level k was shown to be naturally isomorphic to the Reshetikhin–Turaev
theory associated with the finite quadratic module (Zk, qk). It is therefore natural to
ask whether the same relationship can be established for arbitrary compact tori. The
construction of the toral Chern–Simons extended TQFT in [Gal26c, Gal26b] provides the
geometric framework needed to formulate and prove such a higher-rank analogue.
Let T = t/Λ ∼= U(1)n be a compact torus, and let K : Λ × Λ →Z be an even, integral,
nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form. This datum has both a geometric and an algebraic
meaning. On the geometric side, K determines the toral Chern–Simons theory studied
in [Gal26c]: for each closed oriented surface Σ, one obtains a compact symplectic moduli
space of flat T-connections, whose geometric quantization in real polarization recovers
the finite group GK = Λ∗/KΛ through its Bohr–Sommerfeld data, and hence produces
finite-dimensional state spaces together with bordism operators and an extended functorial
structure.
On the algebraic side, K determines the discriminant finite quadratic module GK =
Λ∗/KΛ equipped with its induced quadratic form qK, and hence a pointed modular
category C(GK, qK). Applying the Reshetikhin–Turaev construction to this category yields
an extended (2 + 1)-dimensional TQFT [RT91, Tur94].
The main result of this paper is that these two theories are naturally isomorphic.
More precisely, we prove that the toral Chern–Simons extended TQFT associated with
(T, K) is naturally isomorphic to the Reshetikhin–Turaev theory associated with the
pointed modular category C(GK, qK). The comparison is established both for closed
3–manifold invariants and for bordisms with boundary. Thus the discriminant quadratic
module (GK, qK) provides the precise algebraic datum that matches the geometric toral
Chern–Simons theory.
On the Chern–Simons side, one must use the geometric quantization formalism
of [Gal26c], including the torsion decomposition of the moduli space, the Reidemeister-
torsion half-density, and the K-twisted Maslov correction in the extended theory. On the
Reshetikhin–Turaev side, the relevant algebraic object is no longer the cyclic quadratic
module (Zk, qk), but the general discriminant form (GK, qK).
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the toral Chern–Simons
extended TQFT of [Gal26c] and isolate the geometric ingredients needed later: the
boundary state spaces, the bordism vectors, the closed partition function, and the extended
functoriality. In Section 3, we describe the corresponding Reshetikhin–Turaev theory
associated with the finite quadratic module (GK, qK). In Section 4, we prove the closed
and boundary equivalence theorems and deduce the equivalence of the two extended
(2 + 1)-dimensional TQFTs.
