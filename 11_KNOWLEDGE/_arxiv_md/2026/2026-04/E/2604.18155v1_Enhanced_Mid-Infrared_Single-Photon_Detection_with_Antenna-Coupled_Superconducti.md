Abstract
Scaling the photon-detection area of superconducting nanowire single-photon de-
tectors (SNSPDs) has traditionally been achieved by nanowire meandering. However,
material inhomogeneities and fabrication-induced defects, such as line-edge roughness,
increase with nanowire length, leading to reduced internal photon-detection efficiency
and elevated dark-count rates.
This trade-off becomes increasingly pronounced as
nanowires are scaled to sub-100 nm widths and sub-5 nm thicknesses required for mid-
to far-infrared sensitivity. Here, we demonstrate an antenna-coupled SNSPD archi-
tecture that enhances the effective photon-detection area without increasing nanowire

Introduction
Mid-infrared imaging has facilitated a broad range of scientific and industrial applications in
recent decades by enabling the mapping of thermal signatures of objects and the identification
of biomolecular and chemical compounds.1 In addition, recent advancements in integrated
mid-infrared (mid-IR) photonics on silicon and III-V semiconductor platforms have signifi-
cantly expanded the applications of mid-IR photon detectors.2 As a result, high-performance
mid-IR detectors are increasingly in demand across multidisciplinary fields. Semiconductor-
based infrared detectors, such as HgCdTe avalanche photodiodes and blocked impurity band
detectors, are commonly used in many of these applications.1,3 However, these detectors often
fall short of providing key performance metrics, such as high detection efficiency, high count
rate, and low-noise performance, which are required for applications such as mid-IR tran-
sit spectroscopy of exoplanets,4,5 axion dark matter searches,6,7 remote light detection and
ranging (LIDAR),8 free-space quantum key distribution,9 and other mid-IR single-photon
counting and quantum sensing applications.1,3
Superconducting nanowire-based detectors have demonstrated superior performance in
sensitive, time-resolved single-photon detection in the near-infrared spectrum.8,10,11 However,
they have not yet been established as a mature platform for the mid-IR and far-IR spectral
ranges (> 5 µm wavelength).
The recent demonstration of spectral sensitivity in super-
conducting nanowire single-photon detectors (SNSPDs) at 29 µm wavelength has attracted
2


## Page 3


significant attention12,13 and positioned this technology as a promising path forward for
detecting single mid-infrared photons among competing superconductor-based detectors,1,3
although several performance metrics of the SNSPD, such as mid-IR photon-absorption ef-
ficiency, photon-detection area, and noise-equivalent power, still need to be improved for it
to become a prime choice for high-performance mid-IR single-photon detection.
The prevailing approach to extending SNSPD sensitivity to mid-IR photons has been to
decrease the critical temperature of the superconducting films by modifying the film sto-
ichiometry,12,14 which comes at the cost of operating the detector at lower temperatures,
typically below 1 K. In parallel, superconducting nanowires are fabricated to be narrower
(sub-100 nm) and thinner (sub-5 nm) to enhance mid-IR sensitivity.10,12,15 This dimen-
sional scaling, however, reduces the nanowire’s photon-absorption efficiency and increases
its susceptibility to nano-fabrication imperfections. As a result, large-area meanders often
fail to achieve saturated internal detection efficiency at mid-IR.10,12,15 Compared to short
nanowires, long nanowire meanders are more susceptible to material inhomogeneities and
nanofabrication-induced linewidth variations,16 often referred to as constrictions or weak
points of the detector, which can significantly reduce the bias margin and impede achieving
saturated detection efficiency.12,15 In addition, bends in meandered nanowires can introduce
current crowding and act as potential sources of constriction,17 and meandered nanowires
have been observed to exhibit higher dark-count rates than short nanowires biased at similar
fractions of their switching currents.12,15
In this work, we demonstrate that resonant metallic antennas on photon-sensitive super-
conducting nanowires offer a promising alternative to conventional meandered nanowires for
scaling the photon-detection area of SNSPDs at mid-IR wavelengths. In this antenna-coupled
configuration, the antennas capture and concentrate incoming free-space radiation within
their feed gaps, thereby significantly increasing the effective photon-detection area of the su-
perconducting nanowire beyond its geometric area. Thus, the antennas take over the role of
photon collection while the nanowires function solely as photon sensors, unlike conventional

## Page 4


meandered geometries where the nanowire serves both roles. Hence, the antenna-coupling
approach can significantly reduce the geometric footprint of photon-sensitive nanowires re-
quired to span a given detection area compared to the meandered configuration, thereby
reducing their susceptibility to constriction-induced dark-count events.
In addition, the conventional meandered geometry of SNSPDs exhibits intrinsic sensitiv-
ity to the polarization of the incident radiation, which can be engineered through antenna
coupling. Antennas can also be designed to function as spectral filters, making the sensitivity
of SNSPDs highly selective to photons within a specific wavelength range and incident an-
gle. By leveraging this capability, coupling an array of tailored antennas to photon-sensitive
nanowires can make them polarization-insensitive while achieving both spectral and angular
selectivity to the incoming radiation.
Enhancing the photon-absorption efficiency of SNSPDs through antenna coupling has
previously been demonstrated at 1550 nm wavelength, yielding 1.5× to 2.3× improvement
in photon-detection efficiency.18,19 Our work extends this concept to the mid-IR, showing
15.7× increase in detection efficiency at 7.4 µm through antenna coupling. Notably, con-
ventional cavity-based absorption enhancement schemes (e.g., distributed Bragg reflector)
become increasingly challenging at mid- and far-IR wavelengths due to absorption losses in
the constituent materials, increased cavity thickness, and limited refractive index contrast,
thereby providing strong motivation for adopting the antenna-coupling approach for mid-
and far-IR applications. In this work, we fabricated and characterized antenna-coupled WSi
nanowires and observed 15.7× higher photon-absorption efficiency than an otherwise iden-
tical WSi nanowire without an antenna at 7.4 µm wavelength, while maintaining similar
dark-count rates. We then investigated the scalability of this antenna-coupling approach to
larger detection areas using periodic antenna array configuration. Our analysis shows that
antenna-coupled nanowires in a periodic array configuration exhibit significantly improved
detection sensitivity, e.g., an approximately 900× reduction in the noise-equivalent power
(NEP) at 7 µm wavelength, compared to a conventional meandered nanowire with an identi-

more pronounced at longer wavelengths.
Results and Discussion
Figure 1a presents a schematic of an antenna-coupled superconducting nanowire, in which
the antenna functions as the photon collector and the nanowire serves as the single-photon
sensing element. A crossed bowtie-shaped antenna is placed on top of the superconducting
nanowire, with its narrow feed gap centered on the nanowire to maximize coupling between
the free-space electromagnetic radiation and the photon-sensitive nanowire. As the photon-
sensitive nanowire segment can be very short (i.e., sub-micron in length), the inclusion of
series inductors (Lind) may be necessary to increase the inductance of the detector, preventing
the photon-sensitive nanowire from latching into a resistive state after photon-detection
events. In addition, although a single antenna coupled to a short nanowire segment is shown
in Figure 1a, multiple such antenna-coupled nanowire structures can be connected via wider,
photon-insensitive superconducting connecting strips to form an antenna-coupled nanowire
meander geometry, as discussed later in the periodic antenna array configuration.
In this work, we employ a crossed bowtie-shaped antenna due to its simple geometry and
relatively broadband resonance, although alternative antenna designs can also be used.20
Figure 1b shows metallic (i.e., gold) bowtie antennas placed on a narrow, photon-sensitive
superconducting nanowire, where L denotes the antenna arm length, h the arm width, b the
apex width, and g the antenna feed gap. The photon-sensitive nanowire is positioned at the
center of the feed gap, which is typically chosen to be smaller than the nanowire width to
maximize overlap between the localized electromagnetic field and the nanowire.
A vertical cross-section of the antenna-coupled WSi superconducting nanowire is shown
in Figure 1c. Since metallic antennas are good electrical and thermal conductors, a thin in-
sulating layer is required between the antenna and the superconducting nanowire to provide
electrical and thermal isolation. Without this layer, the metallic antennas would act as a

To enhance the radiative characteristics of the antennas, a quarter-wavelength-thick di-
electric spacer (SiO2) beneath the antenna-coupled nanowire can be considered,20,21 as shown
in Figure 1c. Since bowtie antennas function as electric dipoles, the oxide spacer can im-
prove the local field enhancement by enabling constructive interference between the antenna-
scattered fields and the reflections from the silicon substrate. Further enhancement can be
achieved by introducing a gold (i.e., Au) reflector beneath the dielectric spacer, which en-
ables strong constructive interference of the antenna-scattered fields by inducing an image
dipole of opposite charge in the Au reflector. Although substrate choice is often constrained
by epitaxial growth requirements for crystalline superconductors, this constraint is relaxed
for amorphous materials such as WSi, making the inclusion of a dielectric spacer layer and
an Au reflector plane feasible while maintaining good superconducting film properties.
Figure 1d shows the photon-absorption area, or effective aperture, of a short super-
conducting nanowire for different device configurations. These results were obtained from
finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) simulations of the structures. In all configurations,
the superconducting nanowire remained the same, consisting of a WSi nanowire with 80
nm width, 200 nm length, and 3 nm thickness, encapsulated by 7 nm-thick a-Si layer. The
photon-absorption area was determined by calculating the ratio of the optical power absorbed
by the nanowire to the illumination intensity of the incident optical field. Three configura-
tions were examined in the simulations: (i) a resonant antenna-coupled WSi nanowire on
1.5 µm-thick SiO2 spacer layer on a silicon substrate (blue line in Figure 1d), (ii) a WSi

nanowire on the same SiO2/silicon substrate without antenna coupling (red line), and (iii) a
WSi nanowire without antenna coupling on a silicon substrate (violet line). The geometric
cross-section of the nanowire in the simulation is 80 nm × 200 nm, as indicated by the
dashed line in Figure 1d. A photon-absorption area equal to this value therefore corresponds
to 100% absorption of photons incident on the nanowire’s geometric area.
For the bare WSi nanowire on a silicon substrate, the effective photon-absorption area
decreases monotonically with wavelength, corresponding to the monotonic decrease in the
nanowire’s photon-absorption efficiency in the mid-IR. Introducing a 1.5 µm-thick SiO2
spacer without antenna coupling leads to enhanced photon absorption in WSi nanowire
at specific wavelengths due to the resonant cavity effects; however, even at resonance, the
absorption area does not exceed the nanowire’s geometric area, showing that the effective
photon-detection area of the nanowire remains constrained by its physical footprint.
In contrast, for the antenna-coupled nanowire configuration, the nanowire’s effective
photon-absorption area exceeds its geometric area for wavelengths greater than 4 µm and
reaches nearly 300× the nanowire’s geometric area at 7.7 µm wavelength. Unlike the pla-
nar dielectric cavities, which rely on standing-wave field enhancement and therefore cannot
achieve photon-absorption area exceeding the absorber footprint, antenna coupling can ex-
ceed this limit through near-field collection and concentration. The effective absorption area
enhancement can be further increased by incorporating a metallic reflector beneath the SiO2
spacer. Overall, these simulation results show that resonant metallic antennas can act as
effective photon collectors for photon-sensitive nanowires at mid-IR and enable substantial
scaling of the detection area while keeping the nanowire geometric footprint small.
To demonstrate the mid-IR photon-detection area scaling through antenna coupling,
we fabricated several antenna-coupled SNSPDs on a 3 nm-thick WSi thin film. The WSi
superconducting film was deposited on a 1.5 µm thermal oxide-coated silicon substrate. The
film stoichiometry was 50:50 W:Si and was sputtered at 130 W power and 5 mTorr pressure,
which has been shown to be optimal for achieving saturated internal detection efficiency

of SNSPDs at mid-IR wavelengths.12,14,15 A 3 nm a-Si layer was deposited in situ as a
passivation layer. The film had a sheet resistance of 850 Ω/□at room temperature, and its
critical temperature (Tc) was 2.6 K.
Figure 2a shows the sample stack and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of
three devices: an 80 nm-wide, 4.2 µm-long bare WSi nanowire, an identical nanowire with
cross-oriented bowtie antennas, and one with plus-oriented bowtie antennas. The connecting
strips were 300 nm wide, nearly four times the 80 nm nanowire width, so they remained
non-single-photon-sensitive at the operating bias current, ensuring that only the 80 nm
segment contributed to photon counts. All three devices were fabricated on a 1 mm × 1
mm chip and were close enough to experience similar illumination intensity. In addition, the
fourfold symmetric configuration of the crossed bowtie antenna is expected to be polarization-
insensitive and hence optimal for maximizing field enhancement for unpolarized incident
light. The antenna dimensions were optimized using finite-difference time-domain (FDTD)
simulations to achieve peak field enhancement at around 7 µm wavelength. The arm length
of each bowtie antenna is 1.55 µm, arm width 1 µm, apex width 20 nm, and feed gap 40 nm.
The antennas were fabricated using a metal lift-off process with a 45 nm-thick gold layer and
a 2 nm-thick titanium adhesion layer. Detailed fabrication steps are provided in Section V
of the Supplementary Information.

likely due to their equal nanowire lengths and similar linewidth roughness. Since the DCR

coupled and bare nanowire devices. Thus, resonant antenna coupling can significantly in-
crease photon-absorption efficiency without degrading nanowire homogeneity or affecting the
DCR, in contrast to conventional meander geometries.
Figure 2c compares the experimental photon-absorption enhancement with FDTD sim-
ulation results. At 7.4 µm, the simulated enhancement factors are about 33× for the cross-
oriented antenna and 25× for the plus-oriented antenna, which are higher than the measured
values of 15.7× and 12.8×, respectively. The higher enhancement of the cross-oriented an-
tenna likely arises from a stronger overlap between the localized near-field intensity and
the WSi nanowire than in the plus-oriented antenna. Notably, the experimentally measured
absorption-enhancement factors for both antenna devices are lower than the simulated values
by approximately the same factor, suggesting that the discrepancy between experiment and
simulation likely arises from effects common to both devices. Possible causes include fabri-
cation imperfections, such as antenna feed-gap misalignment with the nanowire center (as
shown in the zoomed-in view in Figure 2a), surface contamination at the antenna–nanowire
interface, or stress introduced in the gold during the lift-off process that may alter its op-
tical properties. In addition, the spectral response of the bandpass filter, characterized at

It is worth noting that the observed absorption enhancement depends strongly on the
length of the nanowire coupled to the antenna. For the fabricated 4.2 µm-long, 80 nm-wide
WSi nanowire coupled to a cross-oriented bowtie antenna, the simulated enhancement factor
at the resonant wavelength is approximately 33× relative to that of an identical nanowire
without an antenna (Figure 2c), whereas the much shorter 200 nm-long, 80 nm-wide nanowire
in Figure 1d shows an enhancement of ∼300× relative to its geometric area and ∼1200×
relative to an identical nanowire without an antenna. This comparison indicates that the
antenna-induced field enhancement is highly localized near the antenna feed gap, so only a
short segment of the longer nanowire interacts strongly with the antenna near-field. The re-
maining nanowire length therefore contributes absorption similar to that of a bare nanowire
and thereby reduces the overall enhancement when averaged over the full nanowire length.
Accordingly, the photon-sensitive nanowire length in antenna-coupled detectors should ide-
ally be comparable to this antenna-enhanced region, since extending it further provides little
additional absorption benefit and may increase the dark count rate. Further details on the
dependence of the observed absorption enhancement on the antenna-coupled nanowire length
are provided in Section III of the Supplementary Information.

As the normalized bias current approaches unity, the DCR transitions into a steep ex-
ponential regime, where dark-count events are primarily caused by intrinsic vortex-related
mechanisms such as thermally activated vortex entry at the most current-crowded or defect-
susceptible regions of the device.16,22,23 Hence, this intrinsic dark-count rate (Γintr) converges
to nearly the same exponential rate for both the meandered and straight nanowires, indicat-

set the dark-count behavior at high bias. A similar exponential slope is also observed for
the antenna-coupled and bare devices. Overall, Figure 3a shows that antenna coupling en-
ables scaling of the SNSPD photon-detection area without increasing the nanowire length
or introducing additional line-edge roughness. As a result, the PCR saturation plateau and
inflection point remain unchanged, and the background-induced dark-count rate does not
increase, unlike in area scaling achieved by meandering the nanowires.
We now examine how antenna coupling to photon-sensitive nanowires can be extended
to large detection areas and compare its detection sensitivity with a conventional nanowire
meander of identical geometric footprint.
As shown above, coupling a photon-sensitive
nanowire to a resonant antenna increases its effective photon-detection area; however, scal-
ing this configuration to large geometric footprints requires integrating multiple antenna-
coupled nanowire units in series. Figure 3b presents such an architecture, showing a periodic
antenna-coupled nanowire meander spanning 50 × 50 µm2 area, where each photon-sensitive
nanowire segment is coupled to a plus-oriented bowtie antenna, and adjacent antenna-
coupled nanowires are interconnected by wide, non-photon-sensitive tapers. The inset shows
a zoomed-in view of the array, highlighting two adjacent antenna-coupled nanowire segments
separated by the antenna array pitch (d). Although the schematic shows plus-oriented bowtie
antennas, cross-oriented antennas can also be used in this configuration.
In the periodic antenna-coupled nanowire configuration, the array pitch sets the spacing
between neighboring photon-sensitive nanowire segments. While closely spaced antennas
15


## Page 16


can strongly enhance the photon-absorption efficiency of the meander through collective
near-field interactions, such a design is not optimal for scaling, as increasing antenna density
increases the total length of the antenna-coupled photon-sensitive nanowires in the meander,
making the device more susceptible to background-induced DCR and line-edge roughness.
In contrast, increasing the array pitch reduces antenna density and improves scalability, but
eventually degrades photon-absorption efficiency as the antennas transition from a collec-
tive regime to effectively isolated scatterers. Therefore, an optimal array pitch should be
evaluated at each antenna resonant wavelength to balance photon-absorption efficiency with
antenna array sparsity.
To examine how antenna length and the optimal array pitch in a periodic antenna ar-

antenna-coupled nanowires with varying antenna length and array pitch. Details of the de-

maximizing antenna spacing. Here, the 45% threshold is chosen as a design criterion based
on the FDTD results, representing a regime of near-maximum nanowire photon-absorption
efficiency achievable across antenna resonant wavelengths from 4.5 µm to 7 µm for the device
geometry considered, while varying the antenna length. Further details of the optimal pitch
calculation are provided in Section IV of the Supplementary Information.

Conclusions
In conclusion, we demonstrated photon-absorption enhancement in a 3 nm-thick, 80 nm-wide
WSi SNSPD at 7.4 µm wavelength through resonant metallic antenna coupling. By concen-
trating the incident free-space radiation at the antenna feed gap, a resonant crossed bowtie
antenna enabled a short WSi nanowire segment to achieve an effective photon-absorption
19


## Page 20


area 15.7× larger than that of an otherwise identical nanowire without the antenna while
maintaining similar dark-count rates. Our results further show that the antenna-induced
field enhancement is strongly localized near the antenna feed gap, indicating that photon-
sensitive nanowires with lengths only a few times larger than the antenna feed gap are
preferable in this antenna-coupled SNSPD geometry to maximize the observed absorption
enhancement while avoiding unwanted dark-count contributions from nanowire sections out-
side the antenna-enhanced region. This localized coupling behavior supports the periodic
antenna-coupled nanowire array configuration as a route to large-area scaling. Our analysis
indicates that such arrays can provide substantially improved detection sensitivity, for ex-
ample, an approximately 900× reduction in NEP at 7 µm for a 50 × 50 µm2 area relative
to a conventional meandered nanowire of identical geometric footprint, with an even greater
reduction in NEP expected at longer wavelengths. These results highlight antenna-coupled
nanowire arrays as a promising approach for realizing large-area, low-NEP, high-efficiency
SNSPDs for single-photon detection in infrared astronomy, dark-matter detection, and bio-
logical sensing at mid- and far-infrared wavelengths.

III. Dependence of the Observed Photon Absorption Enhancement
on Antenna-Coupled Nanowire Length
The observed photon absorption enhancement in an antenna-coupled SNSPD depends on
both the antenna geometry and the length of the photon-sensitive nanowire coupled to the
antenna. As discussed in the main text, the electromagnetic field enhancement produced
by the antenna is highly localized near the antenna feed gap, so only a short segment of
the nanowire experiences strong field enhancement and contributes to increased photon ab-
sorption efficiency.
When the nanowire length extends beyond this localized region, the
remaining nanowire length absorbs photons similarly to an identical nanowire without an-
tenna coupling. As a result, the overall enhancement factor, defined relative to an identical
nanowire without the antenna, decreases with increasing nanowire length. This length de-
pendence explains why the simulated enhancement for the 200 nm-long nanowire in Figure
1d is much larger than that obtained for the fabricated 4.2 µm-long nanowire in Figure

antenna-coupled nanowire may still be insufficient for applications requiring large detection
areas. Hence, to scale the antenna-coupled nanowire configuration to large detection-area

To examine how the array pitch (d) and antenna arm length (L) affect the collective
antenna resonance in a periodic array configuration, we performed parametric FDTD simu-
lations by varying these two parameters. In the simulations, an Au reflector plane beneath
the dielectric spacer was considered, with the SiO2 spacer thickness fixed at 1 µm. We con-
sidered plus-oriented quadrupole bowtie antennas coupled to 80 nm-wide, 160 nm-long WSi
nanowires connected by 500 nm-wide superconducting stripes. The WSi thickness was 3 nm,
with 7 nm a-Si encapsulation layer to isolate it from the antenna. The antenna feed gap (g)
was fixed at 40 nm, the Au antenna thickness at 45 nm, and the antenna arm width (h) was
set to 0.642L. Each nanowire segment was kept at 160 nm, since we observed in the FDTD
simulations that the antenna-enhanced near-field is primarily confined to the vicinity of the
antenna feed gap. Hence, extending the nanowire length beyond a few feed-gap lengths (i.e.,
on the order of 2–5×) does not significantly increase the photon-absorption efficiency in the
nanowire, but instead increases its physical footprint and thereby the DCR.

length in a periodic antenna array enables tuning of the resonant wavelength. This tunability
can be exploited to design multispectral mid-IR SNSPD arrays on a single substrate, in which
different detector pixels on the same chip are optimized for different wavelengths. In this
configuration, each antenna array is designed to have a distinct resonance wavelength and is
coupled to photon-sensitive superconducting nanowires to detect photons at that resonance
wavelength with high photon-absorption efficiency. Overall, this wavelength tunability pro-
vides a versatile platform for designing complex mid-IR spectroscopic and imaging systems
using antenna-coupled SNSPDs.
In addition, Figure S4c shows the FDTD-simulated photon-absorption efficiency of peri-

peak value of ηabs,NW(λres) is approximately 60% at an antenna length of 1.4 µm and normal-

(∼40% – 60%) is observed over a range of array pitches at multiple resonant wavelengths
(i.e., 4.5–7.5 µm), followed by a sharp decrease as the array pitch increases further.
In the periodic array configuration, long-range dipolar interactions among the antennas
dominate the collective resonance behavior, causing the nanowire absorption efficiency to
remain nearly constant over a certain pitch range. As the array pitch increases further, long-
range dipolar interactions among the antennas weaken, causing the antenna array behavior

V. Device Fabrication
The device fabrication was carried out in three lithography steps at the MIT.nano cleanroom.
In the first step, alignment markers and contact pads were fabricated on a 1×1 cm2 WSi
sample using photolithography, followed by a Ti–Au liftoff process.
In the second lithography step, the WSi nanowires were patterned by electron-beam
lithography (EBL), followed by reactive ion etching. The sample was spin-coated with maN-
2401 negative-tone resist at 3k rpm and baked at 90°C for 1 minute. EBL was performed
using an Elionix HS-50 system with a base dose of 465 µC/cm2 with proximity-effect cor-
rection. The exposed sample was developed in AZ726 for 15 s, rinsed in DI water for 60 s,
and dried with nitrogen. WSi etching was carried out with CF4 plasma at 30 W bias power.
The resist was lifted off by immersing the sample in NMP at 60°C for a few hours. Following
this process, we were able to achieve 80 nm-wide WSi nanowires.
During early process development, we observed persistent maN-2401 residue on the pat-
terned nanowires after etching, which NMP alone could not fully remove. This was mitigated
by adopting a segmented slow-etch process to prevent resist overheating. A short oxygen
plasma clean could also remove the residue; however, we did not use ashing in this work.
The residue issue was more severe with maN-2401 than with ZEP-520 positive-tone resist
under identical etch conditions. Nonetheless, the negative-tone resist was essential in this
step to remove the WSi beneath the antenna region and avoid unwanted absorption loss.
