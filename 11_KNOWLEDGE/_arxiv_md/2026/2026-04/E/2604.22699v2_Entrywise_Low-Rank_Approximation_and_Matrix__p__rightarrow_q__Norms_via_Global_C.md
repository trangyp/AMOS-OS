Abstract
Given a matrix A, the goal of the entrywise low-rank approximation problem is to find
arg min ∥A −B∥p over all rank-k matrices B, where ∥· ∥p is the entrywise ℓp norm. When
p = 2 this well-studied problem is solved by the singular value decomposition, but for p ̸= 2
the problem becomes computationally challenging. For every even p > 2 and every fixed k,
we give the first polynomial-time approximation scheme for this problem, improving on
the (3 + ε) approximation of Ban, Bhattiprolu, Bringmann, Kolev, Lee, and Woodruff, the
bi-criteria approximation of Woodruff and Yasuda, and the additive approximation scheme
of Anderson, Bakshi, and Hopkins. Prior algorithmic approaches based on sketching and
column selection, which yielded a polynomial-time approximation scheme in the p < 2
setting, face concrete barriers when p > 2. Instead, we use the Sherali-Adams hierarchy of
convex programs, and in so doing establish a blueprint for how to use convex hierarchies to
design polynomial-time approximation schemes for continuous optimization problems.
We use the same algorithmic strategy to give a new family of additive approximation
algorithms for matrix p →q norms, which are intimately related to small-set expansion
and quantum information. In particular, we give the first nontrivial additive approximation
algorithms in the regime p < 2 < q.

Introduction
We design new approximation algorithms for two fundamental families of high-dimensional
continuous optimization problems: entrywise low-rank approximation of matrices and matrix
p →q norms. Our algorithms are obtained via a new analysis of the Sherali-Adams hierarchy
of convex relaxations for both problems.
Entrywise Low-Rank Approximation.
The goal of the low rank approximation problem (LRA) is
to find the closest low-rank matrix to a given A ∈Rn×d. Low-rank approximation is ubiquitous
in algorithms, machine learning, statistics, and beyond.
We study low rank approximation with entrywise error, where the approximation error is
measured via an entrywise ℓp norm

When p = 2, this is the well-studied Frobenius norm, which admits exact low-rank approxi-
mation via singular value decomposition (SVD). The cases p = 0 and p ∈[1, 2) have received
intensive study, generating numerous new algorithmic insights spanning sketching and sub-
space embeddings [SW11; Woo14; WZ13], randomized linear algebra [Sar06], robust statistics
[CLMW11], and fine-grained approximation algorithms [BBBKLW19], culminating in polyno-
mial time approximation schemes (PTASes) achieving (1 + ε)-approximation in time polynomial
in n for fixed k and p [BBBKLW19; MW20].
We focus on p > 2, which penalizes any poorly-approximated entry more aggressively
than p = 2 would, thus pushing the approximating matrix B to approximate A uniformly well
on all entries. When p →∞, entrywise ℓp low-rank approximation turns into the problem
of computing the approximate rank of A, an important quantity in communication complexity
and beyond, which is NP hard to compute even when k = 1 [ALSV13; LS09a; LS09b; CMS19;
ABGJKL17; GS19].
The p > 2 case remains algorithmically mysterious. The state of the art algorithms with
multiplicative approximation guarantees, draw on the tools that lead to a PTAS in the p < 2
case, are a (3 + ε) approximation by [BBBKLW19] and a bi-criteria approximation of [WY23],
which is allowed to output a matrix of rank larger than k. (See Table 1.) There are significant
barriers to extending these approaches to a PTAS for the p > 2 case.12 It has therefore been
unclear whether there is a PTAS for ℓp LRA when p > 2, which seems to require new techniques,
or whether this problem is fundamentally harder than in the p < 2 case.
Our first main result resolves this question by giving the first PTAS for p > 2.

A New Perspective on Pinning Lemmas: Taming Pairwise Correlations via Mirror Descent
Our third main contribution is a new perspective on pinning lemmas, which we use to prove
Theorems 1.2 and 1.1. Pinning lemmas are measure decomposition theorems in high-dimensional
probability which show that multivariate probability distributions can be decomposed as
mixtures of a small number of simple distributions. Here “simple” means having small pairwise
correlations. Each of the simple distributions in the mixture is just the original distribution
conditioned on the values of a few coordinates, or “pinned”.
Pinning lemmas arose separately in statistical physics [Mon08] and rounding algorithms for
semidefinite programs [BRS11; RT12], in particular the “global correlation rounding” paradigm,
and have since seen extensive use in both areas [YZ14; JKR18; CLN22; JST23; CKPZ18; CP19a;
CP19b], as well as high-dimensional probability [CH21], random matrix theory, and error-
correcting coding [CEGHR20]. More broadly, pinning lemmas are an important example of
the power of localization schemes [CE22], which have seen an explosion of applications in high-
dimensional probability and sampling [EG18a; EG18b; Eld20; CE22]. We introduce a new
connection between pinning lemmas and the mirror descent framework from online convex
optimization; this connection also unifies pre-existing pinning lemmas under the umbrella of a
single proof technique. Because of the broad applicability and importance of pinning lemmas,
we expect that our pinning lemmas, as well as the new bridge between pinning lemmas and
mirror descent, will have further applications in the future.
There are two known pinning lemmas [Mon08; BRS11; RT12], one of which applies to
discrete random variables and the other to real-valued random variables with bounded variance.
Because p →q norm and entrywise LRA are optimization problems over the reals, the version
which allows real-valued random variables is our starting point. (We will expand on the
relationship between pinning lemmas and rounding convex relaxations in Section 2.)
