EXTREMAL DISTRIBUTIONS OF PARTIALLY HYPERBOLIC SYSTEMS:
THE LIPSCHITZ THRESHOLD
MARTIN LEGUIL, DISHENG XU, AND JIESONG ZHANG
Abstract. We prove a sharp phase transition in the regularity of the extremal distribution
Es ⊕Eu for C∞volume-preserving partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms on closed 3-manifolds:
if Es ⊕Eu is Lipschitz, then it is automatically C∞. This extends the rigidity phenomenon
established by Foulon–Hasselblatt [32] for conservative Anosov flows in dimension 3 to the
partially hyperbolic setting.
This gain in regularity has several applications to rigidity problems. In particular, we study
the relationship between the ℓ-integrability condition introduced by Eskin–Potrie–Zhang [30]
and joint integrability in the conservative setting, yielding rigidity results for u-Gibbs measures.
We also obtain several C∞classification results for partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms on 3-
manifolds under various assumptions.

1. Introduction
1.1. A brief introduction to partially hyperbolic systems. Partially hyperbolic systems
arise naturally in smooth dynamics as a robust generalization of uniformly hyperbolic (Anosov)
systems, capturing many of their key features such as stability and rich ergodic behavior. Recall
that a diffeomorphism f : M →M is called partially hyperbolic if the tangent bundle admits a
continuous Df-invariant splitting
TM = Es ⊕Ec ⊕Eu,
where Es is uniformly contracting, Eu is uniformly expanding, and both dominate the center
bundle Ec. More precisely, there exists an integer k ≥1 such that for every x ∈M,
∥Dfk(x)|Es∥< 1 < ∥(Dfk(x)|Eu)−1∥−1,
and
∥Dfk(x)|Es∥< ∥(Dfk(x)|Ec)−1∥−1,
∥Dfk(x)|Ec∥< ∥(Dfk(x)|Eu)−1∥−1.
Typical examples include time-one maps of Anosov flows and skew products of the form
f(x, y) = (g(x), gx(y)), where g is an Anosov diffeomorphism and the contraction and expansion
of the fiber maps gx are dominated by the hyperbolicity of the base dynamics.
It is well-known that the invariant distributions Es and Eu are uniquely integrable into
H¨older continuous foliations Ws and Wu. The diffeomorphism f is called dynamically coherent
if, moreover, there exist f-invariant foliations Wcs and Wcu tangent to Ecs := Es ⊕Ec and
Ecu := Ec ⊕Eu respectively. We denote by Wc the center foliation (tangent to Ec) in that case.

In this paper, we study the regularity of the extremal distribution Es ⊕Eu. We show that
the Lipschitz condition is highly rigid, leading to strong regularity and dynamical consequences.
1.2. Extremal distributions and the Foulon–Hasselblatt cocycle. It is well known (see,
e.g., [44,58]) that the bundles Es and Eu (and hence Es ⊕Eu) are always H¨older continuous.
On the other hand, higher regularity such as Lipschitz or C1 is expected to be exceptional and
to impose strong restrictions on the dynamics. This phenomenon was established by Foulon–
Hasselblatt [32] for 3-dimensional conservative Cr Anosov flows. They showed that once Es⊕Eu
is Lipschitz, then Es ⊕Eu is automatically Cr−1, and the flow is either contact or (constant
roof) suspension. In higher dimensions, Ghys [37] showed that for a codimension one C2 Anosov
flow, C1 regularity of Es ⊕Eu forces the existence of a global cross section with constant return
time.
A key tool in [32] is the so-called “longitudinal KAM cocycle” (which we refer to as the Foulon–
Hasselblatt cocycle in the following), whose cohomology class vanishes if and only if Es ⊕Eu
has the aforementioned higher regularity. Roughly speaking, this cocycle represents the first
nontrivial derivative of the return times with respect to a suitable family of transversals to
the flow. This construction relies essentially on the flow structure and does not admit a direct
analogue for diffeomorphisms, where no notion of return time is available.
In [41], Gogolev–Leguil–Rodriguez Hertz developed a related cocycle-based approach in the
setting of 3-dimensional dissipative Anosov flows. They showed that the polynomial normal
forms introduced by Tsujii–Zhang [65] can be interpreted as defining a twisted cocycle (with
twist given by the Jacobian), which measures the obstruction to uniformly diagonalizing the
differential of the flow. In the conservative case, this cocycle reduces to the Foulon–Hasselblatt
cocycle, corresponding to the linear term of the polynomial normal form.
Motivated by this perspective, we define an analogue of the Foulon–Hasselblatt cocycle using
polynomial normal forms in the spirit of Tsujii–Zhang [65] and Eskin–Potrie–Zhang [30] (see
Section 4). This construction does not rely on return times and applies to general 3-dimensional
partially hyperbolic systems.
This allows us to extend the rigidity phenomenon of Foulon–
Hasselblatt [32] to the partially hyperbolic setting. Our main result is as follows:
Theorem A. Let f : M →M be a C∞volume-preserving partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism
on a closed 3-manifold M. If Es ⊕Eu is Lipschitz, then
(1) f is dynamically coherent;
(2) the distribution Es ⊕Eu is C∞, and the distributions Es, Eu are C1+α for some α > 0;
(3) the center cocycle x 7→Df(x)|Ec is cohomologous to a constant λc. Besides, λc ̸= ±1 if
and only if f is an Anosov diffeomorphism for which Es ⊕Eu is integrable.
Moreover, we have the following dichotomy:
• either Es ⊕Eu is integrable;
• or f is accessible and preserves a C∞contact form, and Ec is C∞. Up to a finite cover
and iteration, f is C∞-conjugate to one of the following:
– an isometric extension of a volume-preserving Anosov diffeomorphism on T2 with
total space M a Heisenberg nilmanifold;
– the time-one map of a contact Anosov flow.
Remark 1.1. Theorem A is sharp in the following sense:
• The Lipschitz assumption on Es ⊕Eu is essentially optimal; even if Es ⊕Eu is H¨older
continuous for every exponent α ∈(0, 1), the conclusion need not hold (see Example 1.5).
• The foliation Wc may fail to be absolutely continuous when Es ⊕Eu is integrable; see
Example 1.6.
• In the classification of the accessible case, one cannot in general expect further rigidity
in either of the two cases; indeed, such diffeomorphisms need not even be C0-conjugate
to algebraic models (see Section 7 and [33]). To obtain further rigidity, one must assume
in addition that Es and Eu are C1+Lip, in which case f is C∞-conjugate to an algebraic
model (see Corollary E).

1.3. Applications to the rigidity of u-Gibbs states. Recently, the lack of regularity of Es
and Eu has been linked to the study of u-Gibbs states for 3-dimensional partially hyperbolic
diffeomorphisms with expanding center. Roughly speaking, a u-Gibbs state is an invariant Borel
probability measure whose conditional measures along strong unstable manifolds are absolutely
continuous. We focus on the case where the center Lyapunov exponent is positive.
Building on ideas from homogeneous dynamics, Teichm¨uller dynamics, and random dynam-
ics [13,21,28,29], Katz [51] proved a rigidity result for such measures in the C∞setting1: if a
u-Gibbs state satisfies the quantitative non-integrability (QNI) condition, then it is in fact an
SRB measure, i.e., its conditional measures along the full unstable Pesin manifolds are abso-
lutely continuous.
A key difficulty in applying this criterion is to express the QNI condition in geometric terms.
This was achieved by Eskin–Potrie–Zhang [30], who showed that the failure of ℓ-integrability
(see Definition 6.2) implies the QNI condition. In particular, given a partially hyperbolic u-
Gibbs state µ such that Es⊕Eu is not C1 on its support, then the support of µ is not ℓ-integrable
for any ℓ≥2 (see Lemma 6.5) and hence µ satisfies QNI (see [30, Theorem 8.1]).
This naturally raises the question of when ℓ-integrability is equivalent to the integrability of
Es ⊕Eu. In a forthcoming work by Avila–Crovisier–Eskin–Potrie–Wilkinson–Zhang [5], it is
shown that for partially hyperbolic Anosov diffeomorphisms on T3, ℓ-integrability for sufficiently
large ℓimplies the integrability of Es ⊕Eu, which in particular leads to the uniqueness of the
u-Gibbs state when Es ⊕Eu is not integrable. The uniqueness of u-Gibbs states in the case
where Es ⊕Eu is integrable is also investigated in a joint work [2] by Alvarez–Crovisier–Leguil–
Obata–Santiago.

1.4. Applications to the classification of 3D partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms. In
2001, Pujals proposed a conjecture to address the classification problem for partially hyperbolic
diffeomorphisms on 3-manifolds up to leaf conjugacy which was formulated in [18] (see also [24]
for a modified version of this conjecture). Both conjectures turned out to be false (see [15–17]),
but it was later understood that the counterexamples still had some connection with Anosov
flows (see [10, Theorem A]). This allowed for a reformulation of the conjecture which involved
introducing the notion of collapsed Anosov flows, see for instance [10, Question 1], and was
recently proved by Fenley–Potrie [31].
In general, one cannot expect a classification of (partially) hyperbolic diffeomorphisms up to
C1 (or C∞) conjugacy, as the stable and unstable distributions may be merely H¨older continu-
ous. This lack of regularity is a fundamental obstruction to smooth rigidity. On the other hand,
when the invariant distributions of a hyperbolic diffeomorphism exhibit higher (Ck, k ≥2) regu-
larity, it often forces strong rigidity, sometimes implying that the system is C∞-conjugate to an
algebraic model. This phenomenon was first observed by A. Avez [4] for Anosov diffeomorphisms
on T2 with C∞distributions, and was further developed for general Anosov diffeomorphisms
and flows (see [11,12,36,38,45]).
It is therefore natural to ask whether a similar rigidity phenomenon persists for partially hy-
perbolic systems. However, in higher dimensions this question becomes substantially more sub-
tle, as the topological types of partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms can be very complicated [42],
and it is not even clear what the appropriate algebraic models should be. In dimension 3, mo-
tivated by the Pujals conjecture and the search for smooth rigidity, Carrasco–Pujals–Hertz [23]
posed the following question:
Question 1 (see [23, Question 2]). Can one obtain a C∞classification for partially hyperbolic
diffeomorphisms on 3-manifolds when Es, Ec, and Eu are C∞?
Under additional assumptions, several positive results have been obtained. When the in-
variant distributions are C∞and the pointwise Lyapunov exponents are constant, Carrasco–
Pujals–Hertz [23] obtain a C∞classification. It was later observed by Allout–Moghaddamfar [1]
that this classification remains valid in the C1 setting. Mion-Mouton [55] also obtains a C∞
classification when the invariant distributions are C∞and Es ⊕Eu carries a contact structure.
By combining Theorem A with results of Avila–Viana–Wilkinson [7,8], we obtain the follow-
ing result, showing that in the volume-preserving setting, even a surprisingly weak regularity
assumption is enough to force full smooth rigidity.
Corollary D. Let f : M →M be a C∞volume-preserving partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism
on a closed 3-manifold. If Es ⊕Eu is Lipschitz and Wc is absolutely continuous, then both
Es ⊕Eu and Ec are C∞. Up to a finite cover and an iterate, f is C∞-conjugate to one of the
following:
(1) an Anosov automorphism of T3;
(2) an isometric extension of a volume-preserving Anosov diffeomorphism on T2;
(3) the time-one map of a contact or suspension Anosov flow.
Remark 1.3. Note that, by Theorem A, the Lipschitz regularity of Es ⊕Eu already implies
dynamical coherence, so the additional assumption is only the absolute continuity of Wc. More-
over, the regularity assumptions above are essentially optimal; see Remark 1.1, Examples 1.5
and 1.6.

We also have a classification for contact partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms on 3-manifolds.
Recall that a C1 1-form α is called contact if α ∧dα is non-vanishing everywhere.
Corollary F. Let f : M →M be a C∞partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism on a closed 3-

the distributions Es⊕Eu and Ec are C∞. Up to a finite cover and an iterate, f is C∞-conjugate
to one of the following:
• an isometric extension of a volume-preserving Anosov diffeomorphism on T2 with total

• the time-one map of a contact Anosov flow.
1.5. Further remarks and examples. Point (3) in Theorem A is related to a result of Gan–
Shi [34, Theorem 1.1]. They show that for a C1+α (α > 0) volume-preserving partially hyper-
bolic diffeomorphism on T3 homotopic to an Anosov automorphism, if Es ⊕Eu is integrable
(and hence Lipschitz; see e.g. [49]), then the cocycle x 7→Df(x)|Ec is cohomologous to a con-
stant λc. Our result extends this phenomenon to a broader setting: assuming only that Es ⊕Eu
is Lipschitz, without any integrability or topological assumptions, we obtain the same cohomo-
logical rigidity. This suggests that the rigidity of the center dynamics can already be detected
at the level of the regularity of Es ⊕Eu.
There exist accessible volume-preserving partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms on 3-manifolds
with non-solvable fundamental group that are not homotopic to the identity (see [15, 16]). In
particular, Theorem A implies that the extremal distribution Es ⊕Eu of such diffeomorphisms
is never Lipschitz. Moreover, any volume-preserving partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism ho-
motopic to these examples also fails to admit a Lipschitz extremal distribution. This stands
in contrast with the Anosov setting, where every Anosov diffeomorphism on a 3-manifold is
homotopic to an affine automorphism of T3.
In a somewhat dual direction, several works study rigidity phenomena arising from the reg-
ularity of the center foliation.
Avila–Viana–Wilkinson [7] showed that for perturbations of
the time-one map of a geodesic flow on negatively curved surfaces, absolute continuity of the
center foliation forces the system to embed into a smooth flow, while otherwise the disintegra-
tion is atomic. Related rigidity results have since been obtained in various settings; see for
instance, [8,39,40]. Our results can be viewed as complementary to this line of work, replacing
the role of Ec by that of Es ⊕Eu. For instance, Theorem A implies that for any volume-
preserving partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism homotopic to the time-one map of a geodesic
flow on a negatively curved surface, either Es ⊕Eu fails to be Lipschitz, or the system embeds
as the time-one map of a contact flow.
In our setting, we also obtain smoothness of the center bundle Ec (when f is accessible in
Theorem A), but via a different mechanism: we exploit the smoothness of Es ⊕Eu together

We conclude with several examples highlighting the sharpness of our results, showing in
particular that both the Lipschitz regularity of Es ⊕Eu is indispensable for Theorem A, and
the absolute continuity of Wc are indispensable for Corollary D.
Example 1.5. Even if Es ⊕Eu is θ-H¨older for every θ ∈(0, 1), it need not be Lipschitz. It
is shown in [32] that for every volume-preserving Anosov flow φt on a closed 3-manifold, the
distribution Es ⊕Eu is θ-H¨older for all θ ∈(0, 1). However, Es ⊕Eu fails to be Lipschitz unless
φt is a contact or suspension Anosov flow.
We further remark that this threshold can be improved when the center bundle Ec is uniformly
expanding or contracting; in that case, a sufficiently high H¨older regularity of Es ⊕Eu already
forces smoothness. More precisely, Xu–Zhang [67] proved that for diffeomorphisms C1-close to
a volume-preserving partially hyperbolic system, if the H¨older exponent of Es ⊕Eu exceeds a
certain critical value θ0 ∈(0, 1), then Es ⊕Eu is automatically C∞.
Example 1.6. Even when Es ⊕Eu is integrable, the center foliation Wc need not be absolutely
continuous. Two typical constructions are as follows:
• Katok’s example: Let f = A × Id be the product of an Anosov automorphism A on
T2 and the identity on T1. One can perturb f along each su-torus so that the center
holonomy fails to be continuous (see [54] for details), which implies that Wc is not
absolutely continuous.
• Gogolev’s example: In [39], Gogolev showed that for volume-preserving Anosov diffeo-
morphisms on T3, the strong unstable Jacobian is a coboundary if and only if the center
foliation Wc is absolutely continuous. Therefore, by perturbing the Jacobian to destroy
the coboundary property, one obtains examples where Wc is not absolutely continuous.
Example 1.7. If only Es or Eu is C1, then Es ⊕Eu need not be Lipschitz. For instance, for a
volume-preserving partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism on T3 with uniformly expanding center
bundle Ec, the stable bundle Es is always C1. However, the unstable bundle Eu may fail to be
Lipschitz and can even exhibit fractal behavior [67] when the system is accessible.
1.6. Structure of the paper. This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we collect the
preliminary material used throughout the paper. In Section 3, we study the 1-form associated
with the Lipschitz distribution Es ⊕Eu and show that the cocycle Df|Ec is a coboundary on
every open accessibility class. In Section 4, we recall the notions of adapted charts and templates
and introduce the Foulon–Hasselblatt cocycle for general 3-dimensional partially hyperbolic
diffeomorphisms. This cocycle allows us to bootstrap the Lipschitz regularity to C1 regularity,
and, under ∞-bunching, to C∞regularity on every open accessibility class. In Section 5, we
establish a dichotomy between accessibility and integrability. In Section 6, we prove the main
theorem and its corollaries. Finally, in Section 7, we study a specific example on a Heisenberg
manifold.

5. A dichotomy: accessibility or integrability
Recall that by Proposition 2.7, for a C∞volume-preserving partially hyperbolic diffeomor-
phism f : M →M on a 3-manifold, if f is not accessible and Es ⊕Eu is not integrable, then,
up to a finite iterate, there exists an f-invariant open accessibility class U such that
• U is a C1 submanifold homeomorphic to T2 × (0, 1);
• f|U is ergodic;
• the boundary ∂U consists of two f-invariant 2-tori tangent to Es ⊕Eu.
We then prove by contradiction that, if Es ⊕Eu is Lipschitz, this case cannot happen.
Proposition 5.1. Let f : M →M be a C∞volume-preserving partially hyperbolic diffeomor-
phism with Es ⊕Eu being Lipschitz. Then either f is accessible or Es ⊕Eu is integrable.
The proof is divided into several parts. In what follows, we assume that Es ⊕Eu is Lipschitz
but not integrable and show that f is accessible. Otherwise, there exists a non-trivial f-invariant
open accessibility class U ⊊M, as described above. We begin by proving that Es ⊕Eu is C1
and constructing an f-invariant vector field X tangent to Ec|U, and study the flow ϕt generated
by X. This flow enables us to analyze the center dynamics both in the interior of U and on
its boundary ∂U.
On the boundary, we apply a Livshits-type inequality together with the
Ledrappier–Young formula to show that the SRB measure is a volume measure. This implies
that the center Lyapunov exponent vanishes, and as a consequence, f is ∞-bunched on the
closure ¯U.
We then apply the bootstrap argument (Proposition 4.12) to upgrade the regularity of Es⊕Eu
to C∞. As a result, we obtain the existence of a C1 contact form preserved by f|U, whose
Reeb vector field is tangent to Ec. This leads to a contradiction via Stokes’ theorem, thereby
completing the proof.
