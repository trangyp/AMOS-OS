Rate Loss Analysis for Multiple-Antenna NOMA with Limited
Feedback
Ruizhan Shen
and Hamid Jafarkhani , Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—In the limited feedback downlink multiple-input
single-output (MISO) non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA)
system, both the effective channel gain and the channel direction
need to be quantized. The quantization error affects the feasible
region of NOMA and the rate loss compared with the full channel
state information (CSI) case. In this letter, we analyze this effect
and obtain upper bound for the rate loss. The numerical results
show that the sum rate of the limited feedback MISO-NOMA
system approaches that of the full CSI as the number of feedback
bits increases.
Index Terms—NOMA, MISO, CSI, limited feedback, FDD,
quantization, rate loss, downlink.
I. INTRODUCTION
A
S a promising solution for the next-generation multiple
access, non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) has
been widely studied [1], [2]. A key advantage of NOMA is
the ability to support more devices. With the rapid growth
of devices in next generation wireless networks, for example
the needs of massive machine-type communications (mMTC),
the traditional orthogonal multiple access (OMA) faces the
challenges of spectrum deficiency and deficient use of re-
sources, as the devices may not exhaust the entire resource
block [3]. By allowing users to share the same time, frequency,
and code resources, NOMA can increase the overall efficiency
of wireless networks, especially user fairness and quality of
service (QoS) performance [4].
In this letter, we focus on downlink power-domain NOMA,
where the transmitter superposes messages from all users with
specific power allocation factors. At the receiver side, the
user equipments (UEs) perform successive interference can-
cellation (SIC). More specifically, we consider beamformer-
based MIMO-NOMA systems [5]. Similar to spatial-domain
multiple access (SDMA), beamformer-based MIMO-NOMA
uses precoding but in contrast, it applies SIC decoding.
The beamformer-based MIMO-NOMA solutions in the liter-
ature are generally based on full-CSI, which is not available in
frequency division duplex (FDD) systems [5]. Although lim-
ited feedback for SDMA has been well studied and has been
adopted by standards [6], research on limited feedback for
MIMO-NOMA is not as mature, especially for beamformer-
based systems. To use NOMA with limited feedback, Ding et
al. [7] proposed considering 1-bit feedback indicating whether
each UE’s effective gain is above a preset threshold or not.
Cui et al. [8] proposed a successive convex approximation

Yapici et al. [9] proposed a two-bit NOMA framework specif-
ically for mmWave environment, where the first bit reflects
if the user distance is above a threshold, and the other bit
represents if the channel angle is farther than a threshold from
the beamforming direction. Moreover, Morales-C´espedes et
al. [10] present a NOMA power allocation method in which
only large-scale effects are available to the transmitter.
Limited feedback and imperfect CSI constrain the choice
of beamforming directions and affect the SIC order. They
may lead to a scenario where NOMA is feasible with full
CSI but not with limited feedback. While the effects of
limited feedback in system performance have been studied for
different SISO-NOMA systems, including asynchronous and
reconfigurable intelligent surface-aided systems
[11], [12],
[13], such studies do not exist for MISO-NOMA.
To the best of our knowledge, the only similar analysis
for MISO-NOMA is in [14], in which the effects of the
feedback rate on outage probability are analyzed and the
optimal number of feedback bits to maximize net throughput is
derived. However, the work considers a different set-up, based
on a cluster-based NOMA framework, with the assumption
that the distances from UEs to the BS indicate the channel
quality indicator (CQI) and SIC order. In addition, in multiple
antenna NOMA, the SIC order depends on the beamforming
vectors as well, as we consider in this work.
The main contributions of this letter are as follows:
• We present a limited feedback multiple-antenna NOMA
framework which considers the interaction of beamform-
ing, power allocation, and SIC decoding order.
• We analyze the rate loss caused by limited feedback and
provide upper and lower bounds, which are independent
of the exact channel distributions.
• We analyze and present the feasible region where one
should use NOMA with limited feedback.
The letter is organized as follows: Section II includes the
system model and problem formulation. Section III introduces
the quantizer structure and Section IV presents the feasible
regions. Section V provides a rate loss analysis, where we
obtain the upper and lower bounds of the rate loss. Simulation
results are presented in Section VI. Section VII concludes the
letter.

minimum rate requirements, or (ii) SIC decoding. In such

depends on the quantization errors. In this section, we present
the sufficient channel conditions to use NOMA.

VII. CONCLUSION
This letter presented a limited feedback framework design
for 2-user MISO-NOMA, which is achieved by feeding back
the precoding vector’s index and the effective gain. We con-
sidered the effect of beamforming direction on the SIC order
and power allocation. We analyzed NOMA’s feasible region
and rate loss in limited feedback, providing a rate loss upper
bound, which converges to 0 at least exponentially. Simulation
results show that the rate loss decreases by increasing the
number of feedback bits B and B′. A similar rate loss analysis
for limited feedback MIMO-NOMA with more users is a
possible future research direction.
