Abstract
The interplay between real-space topology such as magnetic skyrmions and momentum-space
topology characterized by topological surface states (TSSs) is predicted to realize novel phenom-
ena and functionalities, yet materials hosting both topologies are scarce. Skyrmion-hosting heli-
magnet family EuGa2Al2 and EuAl4 has been a prime candidate for such a dual-topology system,
but conclusive evidence for its momentum-space topology has remained elusive. We provide this
evidence by directly observing TSSs that stem from bulk Dirac nodal lines using high-resolution
angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy. These TSSs are exceptionally robust against various
perturbations such as a 2×1 surface reconstruction, a chemical change in the termination of the
crystal surface, and the onset of helical antiferromagnetic order. Crucially, below the N´eel tem-
perature, we observe replica bands driven by the magnetic ordering. Moreover, we demonstrate
clear surface-termination dependence of this magneto-topological coupling. Our findings establish
Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4 as a dual-topology material and offer a rare platform to explore and control the
interaction between the two fundamental topological realms.
Nontrivial topology plays a pivotal role in modern condensed matter physics, stabilizing
exotic phases of matter and emergent phenomena. Magnetic skyrmions, particle-like spin
vortices, are a primary example of topological objects in real space [1–3]. Their nontriv-
ial spin texture gives rise to a large emergent electromagnetic field, leading to fascinating
phenomena such as the topological Hall and Nernst effects [4–6]. Their topological pro-
tection ensures remarkable robustness against perturbations, offering great potential for
low-power spintronic applications [7–9].
In parallel, topological phases characterized by
momentum-space topology have revolutionalized our understanding of quantum materials.
In topological insulators [10–12], for instance, nontrivial band topology originating from
band inversion leads to gapless topological surface states (TSSs) [13, 14], underpinning
unique quantum transport phenomena [15–17]. The concept of momentum-space topology
has since expanded to topological semimetals and superconductors [15–19], broadening the
landscape of topological quantum matter.
While these two notions of topology, real-space and momentum-space, have each spurred
extensive research, materials where they coexist and intertwine remain extremely rare. Es-
tablishing such “dual-topology” systems is of fundamental importance, as their interplay is

predicted to host fascinating phenomena such as charging of skyrmions by dressing them
with TSSs, emergent gauge coupling between spin and charge textures, and efficient and
novel topological spin control [20–24]. Yet a candidate material platform where both topo-
logical orders are unambiguously verified by experiments has not been firmly established.
In this context, the Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4 family [crystal structure and corresponding Brillouin
zone are shown in Figs.
1(a) and 1(b), respectively] has recently emerged as a promis-
ing candidate for realizing such dual topology [24–29]. Previous studies have revealed that
EuAl4 (x = 1.0) hosts multiple nanometer-scale skyrmion phases under applied magnetic
fields [26, 28], and the topological Hall effect observed across a wide composition range
(0.5 ≤x ≤1) attests to its nontrivial real-space topology [24, 28]. At the same time, the
Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4 series exhibits remarkable momentum-space features linked to its electronic
structure. The Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida interaction, originating from Fermi-surface
(FS) nesting, has been identified as the driving force of multi-Q helical magnetic orders
[30] that produce skyrmion lattices [26, 31, 32]. This implies a direct coupling between
fermiology and magnetic topology. Indeed, enormous unsaturated magnetoresistance up to
200, 000% has been discovered in EuGa4 (x = 0) and attributed to the formation of a topo-
logical Weyl nodal line near the Fermi level (EF) [27]. Given the same crystal symmetry,
Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4 at 0.5 ≤x ≤1.0, where skyrmion formation and topological Hall effect have
been reported, are expected to host a similar nodal line [i.e., Dirac nodal line (DNL) in the
absence of magnetic field]. However, the direct experimental evidence of momentum-space
topology, namely the corresponding TSSs, has been absent, despite intensive experimental
studies on the electronic structure [27, 30, 33–36]. This missing piece prevents the defini-
tive identification of Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4 as a true dual-topology system and the fundamental
interplay between its two topological realms has remained unexplored.
In this Letter, using high-resolution angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES),
we report the direct observation of the TSSs associated with the bulk Dirac nodal lines in
EuGa2Al2 (x = 0.5; the N´eel temperature TN = 19.0 K) and EuAl4 (x = 1.0; TN = 15.4 K).
We also uncover their exceptional robustness against surface and magnetic perturbations
and reveal a direct coupling between the helical magnetism and the electronic structure,
including the TSS, thereby completing the picture of Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4 as a dual-topology
magnet.
We begin with the investigation of the electronic states in the paramagnetic state of

EuGa2Al2 using surface-sensitive ARPES measurements with vacuum ultraviolet (VUV)
photons (see Section 1 of Supplemental Material for details on the experimental conditions
and Section 2 of Supplemental Material for a Fast-Fourier-transformation filtering method
applied to ARPES data to remove mesh grid artifacts [37]). Figure 1(c) shows the FS map-
ping at T = 30 K with hν = 113 eV on a surface terminated by the GaAl tetrahedral layer
with the Ga-Al-Ga ordered stacking arrangement [38] (surface termination will be demon-
strated later in Fig. 3). The high surface sensitivity is confirmed by three key characteristics.
First, FSs in the first and second Brillouin zones (BZs) appear similar [e.g., a large star-
shaped feature (labeled S1; yellow solid line) is commonly seen], despite a π-phase shift in
kz between the neighboring bulk BZs [see Fig. 1(b)]. This reflects strong kz broadening that
originates from the short mean free path of photoelectrons excited by VUV photons, i.e., the
emission of photoelectrons exclusively from the surface region [although some kz selectivity
remains, as seen later in Figs. 1(f) and 1(g)]. Second, the FSs exhibit C2 symmetry, rather
than the C4 symmetry of the bulk tetragonal lattice [see the calculated bulk C4-symmetric
FSs at kz = π in Fig. 1(d)], due to the appearance of replica FSs shifted by ±π/a along the
ky direction [e.g., see a replica of the S1 FS (S1′) traced by yellow dashed lines]. This replica
is associated with a 2 × 1 surface reconstruction, as supported by our scanning tunneling
microscopy (STM) results [Fig. 1(e)]. Third, and most importantly, we observe FSs that
cannot be ascribed to either the bulk bands or their 2 × 1 replica bands. For example, while
the bulk band calculations predict only two FS sheets whose vertices extend toward the ¯X
point of the surface BZ [Fig. 1(d); see Section 3 of Supplemental Material for details on
the calculations [37]], our experiment clearly resolves three sheets (S1, B1, and B3). This
additional feature serves as evidence for the SS formation.
To investigate the SSs in more detail, we present in Figs. 1(f) and 1(g) the ARPES-
intensity plots measured along the ¯Γ-¯X cut at hν = 113 eV and 90 eV that probe kz ∼0 and
π, respectively (see Section 4 of Supplemental Material for details on the normal-emission
measurements [37]). Many bands (S1, S1′, B1, B2, B3, B4, B5, and B6) are identified near
the Fermi level (EF). The S1′ band appears by folding the ¯X-centered hole-like dispersion
of the S1 band from ky = ±π/a (¯X- ¯M cut) onto the ¯Γ point, which contrasts with the
¯X-centered electron-like S1-band dispersion at ky = 0 (¯Γ-¯X cut). In addition, at a higher
binding energy (EB) of ∼0.5 eV, there is a relatively flat band (S2) irrespective of kz. A
comparison of the experimental band dispersions (lines and circles) with the calculated bulk

to the bulk states, those labeled S1 and S2 (circles) have no bulk counterparts, and extend
into the gapped region of the projection. Our slab-model calculations in Fig. 1(i) confirm
their SS origin. Notably, a near-EF electron-like SS bottomed at kx = π/a in the calculations

with their two-dimensional SS nature [compare Figs. 1(f) and 1(g); see also Section 6 in
Supplemental Material [37]]. The key question is the topological character of these SSs.
Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4 is a material family that hosts DNLs [27]. For example, the B2 and another
bulk band cross to form a nodal point at EB ∼0.65 eV and produce a DNL in kx-ky space,
as marked by a yellow arrow in Fig. 1(h) [see also a red dot Fig. 1(i)]. Crucially, the S2 band
is connected to this bulk DNL, and forms a drumhead-like dispersion. This connectivity is
an essential characteristic of TSSs in Dirac semimetals and strongly supports the nontrivial
topological origin (see Section 7 of Supplemental Material for the role of spin-orbit coupling
on the band dispersion [37]).

topological origin of the S2 band. Furthermore, we calculated the Zak phase θ, which is

This result is consistent with our experimental observation that the S2 band emerges at a

Our study also establishes the remarkable robustness of these TSSs. As shown in Figs.
1(c) and 1(f), the TSSs persist despite the presence of the 2×1 surface reconstruction. This
is a clear manifestation of the topological protection. We have also found that these SSs
persist robustly under more drastic changes to the surface structure, specifically, a change
in the surface termination layer, as described in the following.
EuGa2Al2 has a layered crystal structure composed of GaAl tetrahedral layers and Eu
layers [Fig. 3(a)], so that cleaving is expected to produce the surfaces terminated by either
layer. Correspondingly, by scanning a micro-focused photon beam (ϕ ∼10 µm) [47] across
the cleaved surface and imaging the spatial distribution of Ga core-level intensities, we
identified two distinct domains [red and blue areas in Fig. 3(b)]. Because the red region
exhibits weaker Ga-derived intensity (stronger Eu-derived intensity) than the blue region
(see Section 8 in Supplemental Material [37]), we identify the red area as Eu-rich (Eu-
terminated) domain and the blue area as Eu-poor (GaAl-terminated) domain. The FS in
the paramagnetic phase (T = 30 K) on the Eu-terminated surface [Fig. 3(f)] exhibits several
striking differences from that of the GaAl termination [Fig. 3(c), reproduced from Fig. 1(c)]:
it preserves the 1 × 1 structure due to the absence of surface reconstruction (Section 9 in
Supplemental Material [37]), and a one-dimensionally-elongated feature (S3) emerges near
the ¯X point. The band dispersion along the kx axis on the Eu-terminated surface [Fig. 3(g)]
reveals that, around the ¯X point, a nearly flat S3 band has appeared near EF (blue line).
Our slab calculations for the Eu termination [Fig. 3(h)] successfully reproduce the S3 band.
It is noted that intense signal of the B2 band (blue dashed line) in Fig. 3(g) likely signifies an
overlap with the S3 band that disperses to higher EB’s away from ¯X and connects to a bulk
DNL indicated by a red dot in Fig. 3(h). The similar dispersion relation and terminating (E,
k) point of the S3 band with those of the S2 band indicates its TSS nature. Our observation
of TSSs on two chemically and structurally different surfaces unambiguously demonstrates
that their formation is a robust and intrinsic property of the bulk topology. The SS origin
is further supported by bulk-sensitive soft x-ray ARPES data [Figs. 3(i)–3(k)], where all
SS-derived features are absent [30].

band dispersion along the kx axis measured on the Eu-terminated surface above and below

Finally, to demonstrate the generality of our findings, we investigated the Al-end member,
EuAl4. The FS and the band dispersion for an Al-terminated surface [Figs. 5(a) and 5(b)]
show a unidirectional band folding due to the 2×1 surface reconstruction and, importantly,
S1 and S2 bands analogous to those in EuGa2Al2 [see also slab calculations in Fig. 5(c)]. This
confirms that the nontrivial topology in momentum space is a fundamental characteristic of
the Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4 family for 0.5 ≤x ≤1.0.
Our findings provide the missing experimental evidence that completes the picture of
Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4 as a rare material system exhibiting dual topologies in both real and mo-
mentum spaces. The significance of this discovery is highlighted when compared to other
DNL semimetals. For instance, while materials such as the ZrSiS family are typical examples
of DNL semimetals [41, 42], the coexistence of their SSs with other quantum orders is rare,
and the SSs themselves may be unstable [43]. In stark contrast, the TSSs in Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4
not only coexist with magnetic order but also exhibit remarkable robustness. The distinction
extends to magnetic DNL systems hosting TSSs: unlike other systems such as ferromagnetic
Co2MnGa [44, 45] and antiferromagnetic YMn2Ge2 [46], where skyrmions are not an intrinsic
bulk property, both topologies are inherent to Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4, promoting an intrinsic cou-
pling without relying on complex artificial heterostructures. Indeed, our direct observation
of magnetic band folding provides evidence that the magnetism (hosting real-space topology)
and the electronic structure (hosting momentum-space topology) are strongly coupled. The
interplay between skyrmions and TSSs is predicted to enable conceptually new phenomena,
such as the formation of charged skyrmions due to the confinement of TSSs at the skyrmion
radius [20, 21]. This could realize a qualitative change from the conventional current-driven

manipulation of skyrmions to electric-field-driven dissipationless control [20, 21]. Moreover,
the present results demonstrate that the electronic and magnetic coupling is highly sensi-
tive to the surface termination, appearing pronouncedly only on the Eu-terminated surface.
This finding makes Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4 a platform for controlling the interplay between the two
topological facets by designing the surface environment.
In conclusion, we reported a surface-sensitive VUV-ARPES study of EuGa2Al2 and
EuAl4. We uncovered TSSs associated with bulk DNL and their exceptional robustness
against a variety of surface and magnetic perturbations.
We also found magnetic band
folding, which is pronounced on the Eu-terminated surface, providing evidence for a strong
and tunable coupling between the magnetism and the electronic structure. These findings
firmly establish the Eu(Ga1−xAlx)4 family as an intrinsically coupled dual-topology system,
offering a rare platform to investigate the interplay between two distinct topological realms
with high tunability, all in a single material.
