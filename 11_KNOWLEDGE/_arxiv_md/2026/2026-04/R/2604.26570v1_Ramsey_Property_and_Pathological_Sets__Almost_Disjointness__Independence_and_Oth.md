JIALIANG HE, JINTAO LUO∗, AND SHUGUO ZHANG
Abstract. We show that under ZF + CCR, if the Ramsey property holds for
all sets in a good pointclass Γ, then there is no MAD family in Γ, proving a
long-standing conjecture made by A.R.D. Mathias in 1977. This also holds
for I-MAD families with respect to analytic ideals I including ED, EDfin, and
Finα for all countable ordinals α. Under the same assumption, we show that
if any one of the Baire property, Lebesgue measurability or Ramsey property
holds for all sets in Γ, then there is no maximal independent family in Γ.
Under the stronger assumption ZF+DCR, we further prove that if the Ramsey
property holds for all sets in Γ, then Γ contains no Vitali sets and thus no
Hamel bases.
1. Introduction
1.1. Background. The study of regularity properties constitutes a central and
enduring theme in the foundations of mathematics. Since the emergence of mod-
ern real analysis, mathematicians have sought to delineate the boundary between
well-behaved objects, i.e. those that can be approximated by simple geometric or
combinatorial configurations, and pathological objects, whose existence challenges
our intuitive understanding of the mathematical universe. This dichotomy between
regularity and pathology remains a cornerstone of descriptive set theory and con-
tinues to drive the field’s development.
The classical regularity properties are well-established. A set of real numbers
is said to be Lebesgue measurable if it can be assigned a meaningful length or
volume. A set has the Baire property if it differs from an open set by a meager set,
a topological notion of regularity. A subset of [N]ω, the collection of infinite subsets
of N, is said to have the Ramsey property if it admits the existence of infinite
homogeneous subsets under partitions. These properties constitute the primary
framework for evaluating structural regularity, each capturing a different facet of
what it means for a set to be tame.
However, the Axiom of Choice AC, a foundational postulate of modern set the-
ory, guarantees the existence of sets that fail to satisfy certain regularity properties.
The Vitali set is the paradigmatic example of a non-measurable set whose existence
relies crucially on the Axiom of Choice. Hamel bases, i.e. bases of R viewed as a
vector space over Q, provide another example of pathological phenomena, as they

It turns out that the existence of MAD families is closely related to Ramsey

The systematic investigation of this topic was initiated by A. R. D.
Mathias. In his seminal paper [32], Mathias introduced the concept of a happy
family (now often referred to as a selective coideal) and developed the notion of
Mathias forcing. Within this framework, he proved that no analytic MAD family
exists [32, Corollary 4.7], and established the consistency of the non-existence of
MAD families from a Mahlo cardinal. As he had already shown that all sets are
Ramsey in Solovay’s model [30], a model collapsed from an inaccessible cardinal
[45], these results suggested the following questions:
Conjecture 1.1.
(1) In Solovay’s model where all sets are Lebesgue measurable, have Baire prop-
erty and Ramsey property, are there infinite MAD families?
(2) [32, Page 87] Is it a theorem of ZF + DCR that there are no (infinite) MAD
families assuming all sets are Ramsey?
Towards Conjecture 1.1(1), Asger Dag T¨ornquist proved that there are indeed no
infinite MAD families in Solovay’s model [48]. Subsequently, Itay Neeman and Zach
Norwood gave another proof of this result and additionally showed that under AD+
or within L(R) in the presence of large cardinals, MAD families are impossible [37].
Karen Bakke Haga, David Schrittesser and T¨ornquist generalized these results and
proved that under AD and V = L(R), or under AD+ and dependent choice, there
are no Finα-MAD families for all countable ordinals α [1]. Towards Conjecture
1.1(2), in their celebrated paper [41], Schrittesser–T¨ornquist showed that, under
the hypothesis that all sets have the Ramsey property augmented by the additional
principle R-Unif, there are indeed no MAD families.
Furthermore, Schrittesser-
T¨ornquist proved that, assuming R-Unif, all sets being Ramsey implies no MAD
families with respect to all transfinitely iterated Fr´echet ideals [42]. Recently, the
results on determinacy and MAD families have been generalized to uncountable
cardinals by William Chan, Stephen Jackson and Nam Trang [10].
The problem of the consistency strength of MAD families was eventually re-

Among these combinatorial pathologies, the structure and existence of maximal
independent families have also been extensively investigated. It is a classical result
that there are no analytic infinite MID families, proved by Arnold W. Miller [35]
via forcing methods.
Projective level MID families are investigated in [5], and
it is shown under ZFC that if all sets in Γ have the Baire property then there
are no infinite MID families in Γ, where Γ is a pointclass closed under existential
quantification over reals. Recent investigation has also focused on the spectrum of
MID families and its large cardinal version [14, 12].
Besides these maximal combinatorial objects, continuous pathologies have also
been widely studied. It is well known that Vitali sets are not Lebesgue measurable
and have no Baire property, so there are no analytic Vitali sets. While a Hamel
basis can be Lebesgue measure zero [44], it is also a classical result that there are
no analytic Hamel bases, from a measure-theoretic argument [22]. This was also
proved in [35] via Cohen forcing. And globally, classical regularities indeed destroy
Hamel bases classwise. It is folklore that the existence of a Hamel basis implies
the existence of a Vitali set [3], thus if all sets have Baire property or Lebesgue
measurability, there are no Hamel bases.
1.2. Main Results. Our first main result establishes that Ramsey regularity suf-
fices to eliminate a fundamental class of combinatorial pathology. Recall that a
boldface pointclass is a collection of subsets of Polish spaces closed under contin-
uous preimages and containing all clopen sets [25, p. 118]. For our purposes, we
call a pointclass good if it contains all clopen sets and is closed under countable
intersections, finite products, countable unions, continuous images, and continuous
preimages. With this terminology in place, we have the following theorem under
the Axiom of Countable Choice for Reals CCR.
Theorem 1.2 (ZF + CCR). Let Γ be a good pointclass such that every set in Γ has
the Ramsey property. Then there is no infinite MAD family in Γ.
This theorem provides a definitive answer to Mathias’ conjecture.
Our second main result extends this investigation from combinatorial pathology
to the classical non-combinatorial examples. Under the Axiom of Dependent Choice
for Reals DCR, we prove:
Theorem 1.3 (ZF + DCR). Let Γ be a good pointclass such that every set in Γ has
the Ramsey property. Then Γ contains no Vitali sets, and hence no Hamel bases.
This result is somewhat unexpected, as Vitali sets and Hamel bases are typically
connected to the failure of regularities in the sense of measure and category on the
one-dimensional real line, while the Ramsey property serves as a form of regularity
in the hypergraph [N]ω, which is a zero-dimensional space. It demonstrates that
the Ramsey property, despite its combinatorial nature, can also have implications
for the structure of the real line itself, beyond the zero-dimensional combinatorial
objects.
Our third main result concerns MID families. An independent family is a col-
lection of subsets of [N]ω such that any finite Boolean combination of distinct sets
from the family is infinite [35]. A MID family is a maximal such family. We prove
that:

Our fourth main result extends this investigation to generalized MAD notions

Theorem 1.5 (ZF + CCR). Let Γ be a good pointclass such that every set in Γ

variants [2].

hypothesis R-Unif, where the definition of I-MAD families for arbitrary ideals I on
N is also given. Our result improves upon [42] in two respects: we eliminate the
need for the extra assumption R-Unif, and our proof is considerably simpler.

7.4. Unifying Notions. Although we have had various examples of regularities
that destroy pathologies, there are also some exceptions. Maximal eventually dif-
ferent (MED) families, as an analog to MAD families in the Baire space, have Borel
and even compact instances [19, 38]; Maximal cofinitary groups, another kind of
combinatorial object related to MED families, turn out to have Borel and Fσ in-
stances [20, 34]. While all of these pathological sets can be realized by maximal
G-independent (discrete) sets for some hypergraph G [39, 33], they are in different
levels of definability.
We would like to pose a general question in a less formal way. Before that some
definitions need to be introduced.
Definition 7.11 ([26, Definition 2.2.1]). Let I be a σ-ideal on ωω, and PI be the
poset of Borel I-positive sets with the order given by inclusion. A subset S ⊆ωω
is called I-regular if and only if for any B ∈PI there exists A ∈PI, such that
A ≤B and either A ⊆S or A ∩S = ∅. Note that we do not assume the properness
of PI.
Definition 7.12 ([39]). A hypergraph G = (V, H) consists of a vertex set V and
a set of hyperedges H. It is called simple if it has no loops. It is called < ω-uniform
if every hyperedge is finite. We assume that a hypergraph is always simple and
< ω-uniform.
A set I ⊆V is called independent (discrete) if no points in I constitute a
hyperedge, and is called maximal if it is maximal under subsets.

The question is to find equivalent conditions on the σ-ideal I and hypergraph G of
reals such that the following postulate is true:

independent sets”.
