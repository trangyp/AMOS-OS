M.A. Grechkoseeva, A.M. Staroletov, and A.V. Vasil’ev
Abstract. For a finite group G, let ω(G) be the set of element orders of G and let h(G)
be the number of pairwise nonisomorphic finite groups H with ω(H) = ω(G). We say that
the recognition problem is solved for G if the number h(G) is known, and if it is finite,
then all finite groups H with ω(H) = ω(G) are described. We complete the solution of the
recognition problem for the finite simple linear and unitary groups.
Keywords: simple classical group, element order, recognition by spectrum.
1. Introduction
Like primes in number theory, simple groups lie in the core of finite group theory. Attempts
to characterize them in the class of all finite groups in one way or another have been made
many times and became especially persistent since the classification theorem was announced
and the complete list of simple groups became available.
In the middle of the 1980s Wujie Shi [27, 28] noticed that the smallest nonabelian simple
groups L2(5) and L2(7)1 can be recognized among all finite groups by the sets of their element
orders. Since then and to this day the question of recognizing simple groups by their element
orders attracts a lot of attention, see the recent survey [13].
To deal with the subject and simplify the notation we agree on the following terminology.
Given a finite group G, we write ω(G) for the set of element orders of G and refer to this
set as the spectrum of G. Groups are said to be isospectral if their spectra coincide. The
number of pairwise nonisomorphic finite groups isospectral to a group G is denoted by h(G).
We say that the recognition problem is solved for G if the number h(G) is known, and if it
is finite, then all the groups isospectral to G are described.
Mazurov and Shi [23] proved that h(G) = ∞if and only if G is isospectral to a group
with nontrivial normal abelian subgroup. In particular, h(G) is finite only if G has trivial
solvable radical. Observe that nonabelian simple groups are most basic among the groups
having such structure.
Returning to simple groups, it was discovered that the group L2(9) is not uniquely deter-
mined by spectrum, moreover, h(L2(9)) = ∞, see the proof in [2]. This motivated Praeger
and Shi to conjecture in [25] that h(L) ∈{1, ∞} for every finite simple group L, see also
Problem 12.84 in the Kourovka Notebook [20].
The conjecture turned out to be correct for the sporadic groups and alternating groups but
false for groups of Lie type. The first example of a group L with h(L) = 2, namely L = L3(5),
was found by Mazurov in [21]. He proved that L is isospectral to Aut L ≃L ⋊⟨τ⟩, where τ
is a graph automorphism of L, and there are no other finite groups isospectral to L. Later
in [40], Zavarnitsine showed that for every positive integer r there is a simple 3-dimensional

Mazurov’s conjecture was confirmed for sporadic groups: h(J2) = ∞and h(L) = 1 oth-

sense: every classical group of dimension greater than 60 is almost recognizable [33, 17].
Thus, to obtain the complete solution of the recognition problem for simple groups, one
needs to address the following two problems. The first one is to describe almost simple
groups isospectral to their socles. The second one is to decide which simple classical groups
of dimension at most 60 are almost recognizable. The solution of the first problem was
completed by Grechkoseeva in [8, 9, 10] with substantial use of the description of the spectra
of these groups given earlier by Buturlakin in [4, 5].
The main goal of this article is to solve the second problem for the linear and unitary
groups, thus completing for them the solution of the general recognition problem.
Theorem 1. Suppose that L is a finite nonabelian simple linear or unitary group. If L is
one of the groups L2(9), L3(3), U4(2), U5(2), U3(5), and U3(q), where q is a Mersenne prime
such that q2 −q + 1 is also a prime, then h(L) = ∞. Otherwise, h(L) < ∞, every finite
group G isospectral to L is an almost simple group with socle isomorphic to L and all such
G are known.
Remark 1.1. The only known Mersenne primes q such that q2 −q + 1 is also a prime are
3 and 7, and the conjecture is that there are no other such primes. In 2006, Zavarnitsine
verified this conjecture for the first 43 Mersenne primes (all known Mersenne primes at
that moment), see [42]. When we were preparing this article, he kindly checked that the
conjecture holds true for all the new 9 Mersenne primes numbers that have been discovered
since then.
Remark 1.2. If L is an almost recognizable simple group, then the precise value of h(L) and
the description of all groups G isospectral to L can be readily extracted from [13, Tables 1–
9], in particular, from Table 1 for linear groups and Table 2 for unitary groups. One should
proceed as follows: in the table corresponding to the type of L, find the section containing
the condition of the form “n ≥n0” and take h(L) and the description of isospectral groups
from this section. We decided not to repeat Tables 1 and 2 of [13] in the present article since
they need to be supplemented by explanations of how to use them from [13, Section 2.3] and
cumbersome notation for outer automorphisms from [13, Section 2.2].
In fact, Theorem 1 is a direct corollary of the bunch of known results and the following
theorem.
Theorem 2. If L is one of the simple groups L9(q), L10(q), U9(q), U10(q), where q is odd,
then every finite group isospectral to L is an almost simple group with socle isomorphic to L.
The formal proof of Theorem 1 will be done in Section 4.

In 2015, Vasil’ev proved that for every simple classical group L with t(L) ≥23, a group G
isospectral to L cannot have a composition factor isomorphic to a simple group of Lie type
whose defining characteristic distinct from that of L [33]. Modulo the previous thorough
analysis of a possible structure of a finite group isospectral to a simple classical group,
see details in [33, Section 2] or [13, Section 3], Vasil’ev’s result suffices to prove the almost
recognizability of simple linear and unitary groups of dimension at least 45 (equivalently, with
t(L) ≥23) and, with some additional efforts made by Vasil’ev and Grechkoseeva in [17], to
do the same for symplectic and orthogonal groups of dimension greater than 60 (this implies
t(L) ≥23).
Thus, the recognition problem was reduced to the case where L is a simple classical group
with t(L) ≤22. The analysis of the remaining groups turned out to be quite technically
complicated and took more than ten years.
The research was naturally divided in two
directions. On the one hand, in the series of papers [29, 30, 31], Staroletov was able to
significantly lower the upper bound on t(L), solving the recognition problem for the simple
linear and unitary groups with t(L) ≥6 and symplectic and orthogonal groups with t(L) ≥
14. On the other hand, Grechkoseeva and her colleagues completed the solution of recognition
problem for the simple classical groups with t(L) ≤4, see [18, 19, 11, 14, 24, 15]. As readily
seen from the above, to recognize by spectrum all the simple linear and unitary groups, it
suffices to handle the case t(L) = 5. The groups with this property are exactly the groups
L9(q), L10(q), U9(q), and U10(q) from Theorem 2.
It is quite natural to consider all simple classical groups with t(L) = 5 altogether. Thus,
we also prove the following theorem covering the case t(L) = 5 for symplectic and orthogonal

that is, the highest power of r dividing a. The number a/(a)r is called the r′-part of a and
denoted by (a)r′.

Let G be a finite group. The set ω(G) is uniquely determined by the subset µ(G) consisting
of element orders that are maximal under divisibility relations. The exponent exp(G) is the
least positive integer k such that gk = 1 for all g ∈G. In other words, it is the least common
multiple of elements of ω(G). Our standard references for the spectra of simple classical
groups are [4] and [5] (with corrections from [8, Lemma 2.3]).
Recall that π(G) is the set of all prime divisors of the order of G. The prime graph GK(G)
of G is the graph with vertex set π(G) in which vertices r and s are adjacent if and only
if r ̸= s and rs ∈ω(G). A set ρ ⊆π(G) is a coclique if the vertices of ρ are pairwise
nonadjacent. We refer to a coclique in GK(G) containing r ∈π(G) as an {r}-coclique. We
denote by t(G) the greatest size of a coclique in GK(G) and by t(r, G) the greatest size of
{r}-cocliques in GK(G). A prime r ∈π(G) is called large with respect to G if t(r, G) = t(G).
Our standard references for adjacency in the prime graphs of simple groups are [37] and [38].
