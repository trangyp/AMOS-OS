REPRESENTATION GROWTH OF
QUASI-SEMISIMPLE PROFINITE GROUPS
BENJAMIN KLOPSCH, MARGHERITA PICCOLO, AND BRITTA SPÄTH
Abstract. The representation zeta function of a profinite group G encodes the
distribution of continuous irreducible complex representations of G as a function of
the dimension. Its abscissa of convergence α(G) describes the polynomial degree of
representation growth of G.
Within the class of quasi-semisimple profinite groups, we characterise those of
polynomial representation growth (PRG) and we prove that whether such a group G
has PRG or not only depends on its semisimple part G/Z(G). Moreover, we show
that, for quasi-semisimple profinite groups G that have uniformly bounded Lie
ranks, the degree of growth satisfies α(G) = α(G/Z(G)). We provide a technique
to produce, for any prescribed positive real number ϱ, quasi-semisimple profinite
groups G with PRG of degree α(G) = ϱ. Our method allows for considerable flex-
ibility regarding the inclusion of finite simple groups of Lie type as composition
factors of G. Furthermore, we can arrange for the groups G of prescribed repre-
sentation growth to be profinite completions of suitable finitely generated discrete
groups Γ so that the group Γ has the same representation zeta function as G.

Theorem 1.2. Let G be an infinite, countably based, quasi-semisimple profinite group.
(1) If G is finitely generated then G is representation rigid.
(2) If G has PRG then G is finitely generated.
(3) The group G has PRG if and only if mn(G) is polynomially bounded in n.
Moreover, in (1) and (2) the converse does not generally hold.
In particular, whether a quasi-semisimple profinite group G has PRG, or not, de-
pends solely on the semisimple part of G. Empirical evidence suggests that we may
even expect a positive answer to the following question.
Invariant Degree Problem. Suppose that G is a quasi-semisimple profinite group
with PRG. Does it follow that G and its universal covering group eG have the same
degree of polynomial representation growth, i.e., does α(G) = α( eG) hold?
Using ideas and results from [2], we obtain a partial answer to this question. To
state our theorem, it is convenient to define the Lie rank rkLie(S) of a finite non-
abelian simple group S.
Apart from finitely many cases arising from exceptional
isomorphisms [31, Theorem 37], the rank of S is determined as follows. For finite
simple groups of Lie type, arising as described in Theorem 2.3 below, the Lie rank
is simply the rank of the corresponding simple algebraic group; the Suzuki and Ree
groups have rank 2; for alternating groups we set rkLie(Alt(n)) = n; the 26 sporadic
groups are pragmatically assigned the Lie rank 1. In the exceptional cases, one and the
same group S allows for more than one description and its Lie rank is the minimum of
the corresponding values. For instance, Alt(5) ∼= PSL(2, 4) ∼= PSL(2, 5) has Lie rank 1.
A quasi-semisimple profinite group G with semisimple part G/Z(G) ∼= Q
i∈I Si, as
above, is said to have uniformly bounded Lie ranks, if sup{rkLie(Si) | i ∈I} is finite,
and unbounded Lie ranks otherwise. If {i ∈I | rkLie(Si) ≤ℓ} is finite for each ℓ∈N,
we say that G has growing Lie ranks. We show that the Invariant Degree Problem has
a positive answer for quasi-semisimple groups that have uniformly bounded Lie ranks.
Theorem 1.3. Let G be a quasi-semisimple profinite group with PRG and let eG be its
universal covering group. If G has uniformly bounded Lie ranks, then α(G) = α( eG).
Kassabov and Nikolov [15] established that for every positive real number ϱ, there
exists a cartesian product G of finite alternating groups such that G has PRG and
α(G) = ϱ. Moreover, they proved that any such group G is a profinite completion,
in the sense that there exists a finitely generated discrete group Γ such that G is iso-
morphic to the profinite completion bΓ. We generalize their result and produce a more
flexible construction for quasi-semisimple profinite groups of prescribed representation
growth. This includes, in particular, semisimple profinite groups whose non-abelian
composition factors are finite simple groups of Lie type.
Our approach builds on pioneering work of García Rodríguez and Klopsch [10,
Chapter 6] in this direction; compare with Remark 5.5. Unlike García Rodríguez and
Klopsch, we can adjust the ranks of the finite simple groups involved to construct
finitely generated semisimple profinite groups which are profinite completions. For an
explanation of the terms ‘Lie type’ and ‘quasi-semisimple profinite group in charac-
teristic p and of Lie type λ’ that are used in the statement of the next result, we refer
to Definitions 2.5 and 5.1.

2. Finite groups of Lie type
For the background material collected in this section, we use [23] as a main reference;
other useful accounts, with a view toward Deligne–Lusztig theory, include [8, 11].
Let F be an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic p. For any power q of p,
let Fq ⊆F denote the finite subfield of q elements. Let G be a connected reductive
F-algebraic group, and let F : G →G be a Frobenius endomorphism providing an
Fq-structure on G. The fixed point group GF = {g ∈G | F(g) = g} is called a
finite group of Lie type, and we refer to the rank of the algebraic group G as the Lie
rank of GF . In some cases, there are multiple ways to realize a group as GF and
strictly speaking the Lie rank depends on the particular realization; compare with [31,
Theorem 37].
Remark 2.1. By restricting to Frobenius endomorphisms (instead of considering more
general Steinberg maps), we are excluding certain rank-2 groups known as Suzuki and
Ree groups. This is permissible as our main interest is in families of groups of growing
Lie rank. For the same reason, it is often enough to focus on classical Lie types.
Remark 2.2. We take the opportunity to correct a related statement in [2, Remark 3.6].
Partly due to a misprint in the first edition of [4], the list of degrees of polynomials
giving the unipotent character degrees of 2F4(q2) and F4(q) were not correctly re-
ported in [2, Remark 3.6]. In fact, the degree set for the twisted group 2F4(q2), viz.
{0, 11, 14, 20, 22, 23, 24}, is a subset of the degree set for the split group F4(q), viz.
{0, 11, 14, 15, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24}, in line with other groups.
A subgroup H ≤G is said to be F-stable if F(h) ∈H for all h ∈H. Let T be
an F-stable maximal torus in G. Let Φ and Φ∨denote the root and coroot system
with respect to T.
As T is F-stable, the Frobenius endomorphism acts naturally
on the character group X = X(T) and the cocharacter group Y = Y (T). Consider
the euclidean vector space XR = X ⊗Z R. By [23, Proposition 22.2], there exists an
automorphism ϕ ∈Aut(XR) of finite order δ and a p-power q = qF such that F acts
as qϕ on XR and F δ|X = qδ idX. In particular, F induces a graph automorphism,
also denoted by ϕ, on the Dynkin diagram ΓΦ associated to Φ. Non-trivial graph
automorphisms of connected Dynkin diagrams are known to occur in types Aℓfor
ℓ≥2, Dℓfor ℓ≥4 and E6; see [23, Table 11.1].
