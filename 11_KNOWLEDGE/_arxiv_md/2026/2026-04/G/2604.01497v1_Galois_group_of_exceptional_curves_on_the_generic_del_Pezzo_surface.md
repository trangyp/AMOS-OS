1. Introduction
Let k be any field and let ks be a separable closure. A del Pezzo surface over k is a smooth
projective surface over k with ample anticanonical divisor, which we denote by −KX. Its
degree is defined to be the self-intersection number d = (−KX)2. We have the following
classical result on the classification of del Pezzo surfaces (see, for example, [VA06, Theorem
2.1.1] or [Man86, Theorem 24.4]).
Theorem 1.1. Let X be a del Pezzo surface of degree d over k. Then Xks is isomorphic
to the blow-up of P2
ks at (9 −d) points in general position in P2(ks), or d = 8 and X is
isomorphic to P1
ks × P1
ks.
Given a smooth projective surface X over k, an exceptional curve on X is an irreducible
curve C ⊂Xk such that C2 = C · KX = −1, where k is an algebraic closure of k. In fact,
any such curve is necessarily defined over ks (see, for example, [Poo17, Theorem 9.3.1(b)(ii)]).
By adjunction, such a curve has arithmetic genus 0, so it is isomorphic to P1
ks. The Galois
action on Xks induces an action of Gal(ks/k) on the set of exceptional curves on Xks.

Since the Galois action on the exceptional curves on X must preserve their incidences,
the image of this action is a subgroup of Aut(Γd). We say that the action is maximal if the
image is equal to the whole group Aut(Γd). Let Gd denote the Galois image in Aut(Γd) for
the generic del Pezzo surface of degree d over k. Our main result is the following:
Theorem 1.2. For any field k and any 1 ≤d ≤7, Gd = Aut(Γd). In other words, the
generic del Pezzo surface has maximal Galois action on its exceptional curves.
Remark 1.3. The main new content in this theorem is the cases 1 ≤d ≤4 over an arbitrary
base field k. When d = 7, the situation is very simple. The cases 5 ≤d ≤6 follow immediately
from [Zai23, Theorems 1.2 and 1.7], but our proof provides an alternative approach. The case
d = 3 and k = C is very classical, proven as early as [Jor70, III.II.V]; see also [Tod35, §4] and
[Har79, §III.3]. This has been extended to d = 3 and char k ̸= 2, 3 in [Ach13, Proposition
4.8] using an arithmetic Torelli map which identifies cubic surfaces with marked lines with
certain principally polarized abelian fivefolds with level structure, and the existence of smooth
compactifications of Shimura varieties. See [KMSU25, §1.2] for a survey of some other partial
results (for example, over specific fields). In contrast to most of these partial results, our
proof is uniform over all fields k and in particular does not depend on the characteristic.
We deduce a consequence for the Brauer-Manin obstruction on cubic surfaces over Fq(u).
Corollary 1.4. 100% of cubic surfaces over Fq(u), when ordered by height of the coefficients
of their equations, have no Brauer-Manin obstruction.

2. The generic del Pezzo surface
In this section, for each 1 ≤d ≤7, we define a parameter space Hd for del Pezzo surfaces
X over k of degree d with their natural embeddings into the relevant (weighted) projective
space. Once we see that these parameter spaces are integral schemes, we define the generic
del Pezzo surface of degree d to be the generic fiber of the universal family over Hd.
To define Hd, it is convenient to separate into two cases based on the very ampleness of
the anticanonical bundle.

3. Monodromy formulation and irreducibility of the incidence scheme
Let K be the function field of Hd. Recall that our goal is to compute the image Gd of the
action of Gal(Ksep/K) on the exceptional curves on the generic del Pezzo surface of degree d
(as defined in §2). In this section, we reformulate this question in terms of the monodromy of
a certain finite étale cover Yd of Hd whose fibers parametrize the exceptional curves. We
show that this covering space is irreducible for 1 ≤d ≤6, which is equivalent to saying that
the monodromy action is transitive.

5. Consequence for Brauer-Manin obstruction on cubic surfaces
For a global field k, the action of Gal(ks/k) on the 27 lines on a cubic surface X over k is
related to the Brauer-Manin obstruction on X as follows.
Proposition 5.1 ([SD93]). Let k be a global field. Let X be a smooth cubic surface over k such
that the action of Gal(ks/k) on the 27 lines in Xks is the full W(E6). Then H1(k, Pic Xks) = 0.
In particular, there is no Brauer-Manin obstruction.
Proof. Let K be the smallest (finite) extension of k over which the 27 lines on Xks are defined.
Then the Galois group G = Gal(K/k) embeds into W(E6) via its action on the 27 lines by the
minimality of K. On the other hand, Pic Xks = Pic XK and H1(k, Pic Xks) = H1(G, Pic XK).
So H1(k, Pic Xks) is killed by |G|, which divides |W(E8)| = 27 · 34 · 5. By Lemma 1 of [SD93],
if the order of H1(k, Pic Xks) is divisible by 2, then there is a double-six S on Xks such that
G maps S into itself. By Lemma 4 of [SD93], if the order of H1(k, Pic Xks) is divisible by 3,
then there is a triple-nine on Xks such that G maps each nine into itself. Neither of these
can happen if G = W(E6). Moreover, Lemma 9 of [SD93] says that H1(k, Pic Xks) has order
not divisible by 5. This shows that H1(k, Pic Xks) must be trivial.
□
Remark 5.2. Swinnerton-Dyer worked over a number field k; however, his computation of
the Galois action on Pic Xks holds over any global field k, and hence also the conclusion on
H1(k, Pic Xks).
When k = Q, Poonen and Voloch ([PV04], Proposition 3.4) observed that the maximality
