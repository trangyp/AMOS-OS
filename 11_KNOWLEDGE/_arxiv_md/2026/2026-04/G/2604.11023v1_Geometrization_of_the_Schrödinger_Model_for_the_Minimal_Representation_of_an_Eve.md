GEOMETRIZATION OF THE SCHR¨ODINGER MODEL FOR THE MINIMAL
REPRESENTATION OF AN EVEN ORTHOGONAL GROUP: THE DE RHAM
SETTING
AARON SLIPPER
Abstract. We construct and compare three D-module models for the minimal representation of the confor-
mal group of an even-dimensional quadratic space. Let V be a quadratic space over a field κ of characteristic
0, let C be the isotropic cone in V ∗, and let G be the conformal group of V . We prove an equivalence be-
tween the category of modules over the Grothendieck differential operator algebra DC, a Kazhdan–Laumon
glued category attached to the smooth locus of the cone, and a category of “harmonic” twisted D-modules
on a flag variety G/P. Along the way, we construct a quadric Fourier transform on DC, provide a geometric
proof that the algebra DC is finitely generated despite the singularity of C, and explain the quasi-classical
analogue of this minimal representation.

1. Introduction
The object of this paper is to provide three different models for the categorification of the minimal
representation of the even orthogonal group via D-modules, and to prove their equivalence.1 In particular,
let V be a vector space of dimension n = 2k over a field κ of characteristic 0, with nondegenerate quadratic
form Q; let C be the isotropic locus of Q, and Co its smooth locus. We will show the equivalence of three
different categories:
i) the category of modules over the algebra DC of (global) Grothendieck differential operators on C;
ii) The Kazhdan-Laumon glued category of D-modules on the smooth locus of the cone Co (see Section
4); and
iii) The category of “harmonic” D-modules on the conformal compactification of V .
We will also discuss the quasi-classical analogue of the Schr¨odinger Model. We note that because C is
singular, it is something of a miracle that DC is well-behaved; as a byproduct of the proofs of the above
equivalences, this paper will also provide a new, geometric proof that DC is finitely generated.
Let G be the conformal group of V ; it is an even orthogonal group containing O(Q) as a Levi factor.
Unlike O(Q), the group G does not act geometrically on C. However, it manifestly acts on the conformal
compactification of V , and so on the category iii). As we will explain, this categorical action on the space
of harmonic D-modules can be viewed as a geometrization of the minimal representation of the minimal
representation of G. The corresponding G-action on DC-modules then provides a geomertization of the
“Schr¨odinger model” of the minimal representation of G.
In this introductory section, we will first provide some background on the Schr¨odinger model of the
minimal representation of the even orthogonal group, stressing the analogy between this representation and
the metaplectic Weil representation. We will then offer an overview of T. Kobayashi’s F-method, which is
the specific approach that we categorify in this paper. Finally, we provide a more detailed outline of the
present paper.

Consider the Hilbert space L2(C), taken with respect to a natural measure on C coming from the em-
bedding into Rn. Clearly O(p,q) acts on L2(C), as O(p,q) acts geometrically on C. It is somewhat more
surprising that this representation extends to an action of O(p + 1,q + 1) on L2(C), where O(p,q) lives in
O(p + 1,q + 1) as a Levi factor of a maximal parabolic. This action does not arise as an action on the
underlying geometry of C. It turns out that L2(C) is a model – the so-called Schr¨odinger Model – for the
minimal representation of O(p + 1,q + 1) [KM11]. That is to say, L2(C) is the representation with minimal
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension [GS05].
As pointed out by Kazhdan [GK23], the Schr¨odinger model of the minimal representation of O(p +
1,q + 1) has a striking resemblance to the metaplectic Weil representation. We now briefly recall the Weil
representation. Let V be a symplectic vector space, with L a Lagrangian subspace. GL(L) manifestly acts on
L2(L) (unitarized appropriately), since GL(L) acts on L. This “geometric” action extends to a representation
of Mp2n(R) on L2(L), where Mp2n(R) is a double-cover of Sp2n(R) – the Weil Representation. GLn lives
as a Levi factor of a Siegel parabolic of Mp2n.
It is standard to present the Weil representation’s action on L2(L) via generators of Mp2n. Under this
presentation, the Levi GLn ⊆Mp2n acts via the geometric action on L; the unipotent radical of the Siegel
parabolic acts via pointwise multiplication by certain Gaussians. (These are often called “modulations”

A parallel story exists for the minimal representation of O(p+1,q+1). One has O(p,q) ⊆P ⊆O(p+1,q+1),
where P is a maximal parabolic with Levi Gm × O(p,q). The unipotent radical of P acts on functions in
L2(C) via pointwise multiplication by unitary characters of Rn (“modulations”). And, finally, the analogous
Weyl element in O(p + 1,q + 1), which conjugates P to P op, corresponds to an involutive unitary operator
on L2(C), which is the natural analogue of the Fourier transform for the quadric cone C. Explicitly, one
may write this transform as a convolution by a kind of Bessel function [KM11], just as the classical Fourier
transform can be written as a convolution with an exponential.
The Schr¨odinger model of the minimal representation of the even orthogonal group has been extended to
arbitrary local fields by N. Gurevich and D. Kazhdan [GK23]. Moreover, the Bessel Fourier transform given
by the action of the Weyl element is perhaps the simplest example of a “nonlinear” Fourier transform of the
kind expected (e.g., in Braverman-Kazhdan theory and relative Langlands duality [BK00, Ngˆo20, BZSV24])
to exist in great generality. Remarkably, J. Getz has provided a full Poisson Summation formula on quadric
cones using this transform (with a complete geometric description of “boundary terms” and no restriction
on test functions) [Get22]. It is expected that this kind of Poisson Summation Formula should exist on
function spaces associated to a large class of algebraic varieties (e.g., reductive monoids, spherical varieties
[Sak12, Ngˆo20, Sak23]) and, in the mold of Tate’s thesis [Tat67], are expected to yield many instances
of Langlands functoriality. The analogue of the metaplectic/minimal representation is expected to arise
in this more general context too: geometric symmetries of the variety, along with modulations, and these
(conjectural) Fourier transforms give rise to so-called “modulation groups” [GTH+25].
The function space of a quadric cone is, after the case of vector spaces, one of the simplest and best-
understood examples of the above paradigm. It is worth noting, however, that the quadric cone’s Fourier
transform is already a great deal more subtle than the (very classical) Fourier transform on vector spaces:
the Bessel kernel is singular [KM11], and there are nontrivial boundary terms in the Poisson Summation
Formula [Get22].
In particular, these appear to be related to the (algebro-geometric) singularity of the
quadric cone at the origin.
Finally, we note that in the global setting (that is to say, on the moduli stack of principal SO2n-torsors over
a global curve, which corresponds to the geometrization of the Adelic minimal representation), V. Lafforgue
and S. Lysenko have constructed a (de-Rham) geometrization of the minimal representation [LL13] in the
spirit of the Geometric Langlands Program. (Lysenko constructed a global geometrization of the metaplectic
Weil representation in [Lys06].)
In this paper, we work in the more classical setting over a field κ of
characteristic 0; we do not consider stacky or derived phenomena.

1.3. Outline of This Paper. We now outline the structure and main results of this paper.
Section 2 discusses in detail the geometric structures which we will use throughout the paper. In particular,
Section 2.1 discusses in detail the two orthogonal groups G and H, where G is the conformal group (or
“modulation group” [GTH+25]) and H ⊂G is the orthogonal group preserving the quadric cone. It also
discusses the important parabolic, unipotent, and Levi subgroups of G which will be used throughout this
document. Section 2.2 discusses the geometry of the flag space G/P – the conformal compactification – in
detail. Physicists will recognize this as Penrose’s conformal compactification of spacetime [Pen63, Pen64,
Pen11].
Section 3 will consider the ring of differential operators on the quadric C and its quasi-classical analogue.
Section 3.1 shows how the embedding of the cone C into the vector space V satisfies what we call F-moment
descent, which we use to review that the affinization of the cotangent bundle of Co = C ∖{0} is the closure
of a minimal nilpotent orbit of the orthogonal group G (Proposition 3.4). This Proposition is known in the
folklore, though explicit references have been hard to find prior to [GTH+25].
The action of G on this orbit closure may be viewed as the quasi-classical analogue of the minimal
representation (which, conversely, may be viewed as the “geometric quantization” of the minimal nilpotent
orbit).
Section 3.2 discusses the ring of Grothendieck differential operators DC on the cone. In particular, we
unpack the F-method on the cone in a purely algebraic manner. Much of this work can be derived from
the relevant material in [Gon76, LSS89] and [KM11]; we intentionally provide very explicit calculations
throughout this section. Moreover, a very particular perspective informs these calculations: the action of

4. Kazhdan-Laumon Gluing of D-Modules on the Quadric Cone
In [KL88], Kazhdan and Laumon construct certain “glued” categories of perverse ℓ-adic sheaves on basic
affine space G/U, where the gluing data is indexed by the Weyl group, and simple reflections are realized
by Fourier–Deligne transforms. These glued categories have a rich structure and relate to, for example,
Braverman–Kazhdan theory and Deligne–Lusztig theory [Pol01, MF25]. In general, the Kazhdan–Laumon
gluing construction on basic affine space G/U (for G reductive and U a maximal unipotent) produces
categories which are not equivalent to the category of perverse sheaves on any space.16 By contrast, in
the D-module setting, [BBP02] show that the corresponding glued category is equivalent to the category of
modules over the Grothendieck ring of differential operators on the affinization G/U.17
As discussed above, there is a natural analogue of Fourier transform on both L2-spaces and on differential
operators on the quadric cone, namely the action of the Weyl element w0 ∈G ∶= O(Q+) (see (2.2.12) and
Proposition 3.18). Of course, this transformation does not act on the cone C itself. Rather, in the larger
orthogonal group G, it conjugates P to P op [KM11], while commuting with the smaller group H ∶= O(Q),
which does act on C.
In this section we will show, following [BBP02], that the category obtained by gluing D-modules on two
copies of Co along this non-linear Fourier transform is equivalent to the category of modules over the algebra
DC.
A note on terminology: throughout what follows, when we speak of D-modules on a variety, we will always
mean coherent D-modules, that is, D-modules which are locally finitely generated over D [HTT08].
