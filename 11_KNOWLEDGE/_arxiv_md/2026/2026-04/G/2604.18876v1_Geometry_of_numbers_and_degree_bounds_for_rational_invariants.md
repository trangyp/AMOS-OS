Geometry of numbers and degree bounds for rational invariants
Ben Blum-Smith, Sylvan Crane, Karla Guzman, Alexis Menenses,
and Maxine Song-Hurewitz
April 22, 2026
Abstract
We investigate degree bounds for fields of rational invariants of representations of finite groups. We
prove many cases of a bound for Z{pZ conjectured by Blum-Smith, Garcia, Hidalgo, and Rodriguez.
For arbitrary groups, we also prove a new bound on the minimum degree d such that the polynomials
of degree ď d span the field of rational functions as a vector space over the invariant field. This latter
quantity also bounds the degree d such that the polynomials of degree ď d contain a copy of the regular
representation of G, advancing an inquiry of Koll´ar and Tiep. The methods involve Euclidean lattices
and Minkowski’s geometry of numbers.

Introduction
Invariant rings of finite groups are finitely generated as algebras, and there is a line of research stretching
back over 100 years, starting with [Noe15], on bounding the degrees of the generators. Fields of rational
invariants of finite groups are an even older object of interest, going back at least to [Gal46]; and one can
recognize elements of the theory even as far back as [dL70], although it was not conceived of in that way at
the time. Nonetheless, the investigation of degree bounds in this latter context seems to be comparatively
young; see [FKW07, HL16, BSGHR24, BS25, EK26, EK25, RS25, BSD25]. New impetus was given to this
investigation by a recent development in signal processing: when estimating a signal that has been corrupted
not only by noise but by transformations randomly drawn from a group, degree bounds on generators for
the field of rational invariants yield information-theoretic bounds on the number of samples one needs to
view in order to accurately estimate (the orbit of) a generic signal [BBSK`23, Theorems 2.15 and 2.16 and
Corollary 3.31].
The present work is a contribution to this line of research. We make significant progress toward an upper
bound for the case G “ Z{pZ (p prime) that was conjectured in [BSGHR24]. We also give a bound on the
degrees of generators for the ambient rational function field as a vector space over the invariant field. In this
introduction, we identify the objects of study, state our main results, and discuss motivation, context, and
methods.

Motivation and context.
The quantities βfield and βr
field are field analogues to the classical Noether
number βpG, V q, the minimum d such that the invariant ring krV sG is generated (as a k-algebra) by the
invariant polynomials of degree ď d. Thus they are natural objects of study in view of the vast literature
on βpG, V q—a far-from-comprehensive list of highlights includes [Noe15, Sch91, DH00, Sez02, Fle00, Fog01,
FSSW06, Sym11, CD14a, CD14b, CDG16, Gan19, HMP19, CD19, FKMP21, KLO25]—and the classical
interest in fields of rational invariants [Gal46, Jor70, Bur11, Noe13].
They may also be viewed as analogues to βseppG, V q, the minimum d such that the polynomial invariants
of degree ď d separate the orbits of G on V , which is also the subject of a large and active literature [DK15,
Dom07, DKW08, Kem09, Sez09, Duf09, DEK09, KK10, DS11, Duf13, KS13, DJ15, Dom17, Rei18, Rei20,
LR21, Dom22, KLR22, DS24, Sch25, Dom26, DS25, JNM25]. If k is algebraically closed of characteristic zero,
then generators for the field of rational invariants separate generic orbits [Ros56, Theorem 2], so βfield, βr

are relaxations of βsep in this case (and β always).
The number βfield was defined (with slightly different notation) in [FKW07], where it was proven that
βfieldpG, V q ď |G| regardless of field characteristic.
It was further studied in [HL16, BSGHR24, EK26,
EK25, BSD25] (and see also [RS25]). In the case k “ R, it is the quantity of interest in the signal processing
application mentioned above. Various forms of this signal processing problem (with a finite or more generally
a compact G) are studied in [Sig16, APS17, Sin18, PWB`19, BNWR20, BBS20, FLS`21, BMS22, ABS22,
BELS22, BBSK`23, ES24, EK25, BE25]; see [BBSK`23, Sections 2 and 3] for the connection to βfield. A
much more detailed discussion of motivation and context for βfield is found in the introduction to [BSGHR24].
The alternative βr

makes sense to consider rational but not necessarily polynomial generators. Trivially, we have βr

are sharp) for both; compare [BSGHR24, Theorem 3.1] with [BS25, Theorem 4.1]. The state of current
knowledge is more preliminary with respect to upper bounds, but at least in the case G “ Z{pZ, the same
upper bounds are conjectured; see below.
The quantity Dspan is a field analogue to another quantity of longstanding interest in invariant theory:
the minimum degree required for not-necessarily-invariant polynomials to generate krV s as a module over
krV sG, denoted topdegpG, V q or βpkrV s, krV sGq, which was studied in [Sch91, KS14, CD19] (and see also
[CD13, CD14a, CD14b], which work with a common generalization of βpG, V q and topdegpG, V q). One has
immediately that
DspanpG, V q ď topdegpG, V q,

While this theorem is stated for any finite group, its main interest is if G is cyclic or dicyclic. As mentioned
above, DspanpG, V q is a field analogue to topdegpG, V q, the minimum degree of polynomials needed to
generate krV s as a krV sG-module. As mentioned above, we immediately have DspanpG, V q ď topdegpG, V q.
By combining [KS14, Theorem 1], with results of [CD14a, CD14b], one obtains topdegpG, V q ď |G|{2
when G is not cyclic or dicyclic (and char k ∤|G|); therefore DspanpG, V q ď |G|{2 as well—this inference
is carried out carefully in the proof of Theorem 4.1.

theorem tells us that Dspan is at most about half of topdeg whenever this holds but V is not essentially
scalar. Thus, the cyclic case is the main case.
Methods and structure.
The starting point for the proofs of all of our main results is a common strategy
in the invariant theory of finite abelian groups. Assuming k contains sufficiently many roots of unity, the
action of G on V can be diagonalized. Choosing a diagonal basis for V ˚, the action of G on krV s – SympV ˚q
is then a monomial action, and the invariant ring krV sG is the semigroup ring over the normal affine
semigroup of invariant monomials. Then properties of the invariant ring can be deduced from properties
of the underlying semigroup; see for example [Huf80, Sch91, BH98, Smi96, NS09, CDG16, Dom17, Gan19].

Similarly, the field kpV qG of rational invariants can be studied by analyzing the group of invariant Laurent
monomials [HL16, BSGHR24, BS25], which can be viewed as a Euclidean lattice.
In particular, it is shown in [BSGHR24], respectively [BS25], that βfield, respectively βr
field, can be de-
termined in this setting directly from the lattice of invariant Laurent monomials. In particular, βr
field is
precisely the smallest natural number d so that the group of invariant Laurent monomials, viewed as a
Euclidean lattice, is generated by its members of L1-norm ď d. In a similar way, it will be deduced below
from results in [BSGHR24] and [BSD25] that Dspan can be determined from the way the lattice of invariant
Laurent monomials sits inside the lattice of all Laurent monomials. Almost all of the results in the present
work are proven by analyzing these lattices. We quote the needed material from [BSGHR24, BS25, BSD25]
below in Section 2.
A key ingredient in the proof of Theorem 3.1, bounding βr
field in the case G “ Z{pZ, is Minkowski’s
geometry of numbers [Min10]. This body of theory concerns interactions between lattices and convex sets
in Euclidean space; a modern reference is [LG87]. It provides us with theorems that, given a lattice and a
vector space norm, will allow us to find a basis for that lattice such that all but one of the basis vectors are
short with respect to that norm. We work with these short vectors, and the equation that defines the lattice
of invariant Laurent monomials, to find an additional vector that completes the basis and is not too long.
Theorem 3.9 is deduced from Theorem 3.1.
Meanwhile, Theorem 4.1, which is our main result on Dspan, is proven with a case analysis depending
on whether G is cyclic, dicyclic, or neither. For groups that are neither cyclic nor dicyclic, we deduce it
with essentially no work from known results on βpG, V q and topdegpG, V q. For cyclic groups, we prove
it along lines reminiscent, in broad outline, of the proof of [BSGHR24, Theorem 3.11], which asserts that
βfieldpZ{pZ, V q ď pp ` 3q{2 if V contains at least three nontrivial isotypic components. Although the details
differ greatly, both proofs involve bootstrapping from detailed information on the m “ 2 case that is obtained
using convexity arguments. For dicyclic groups, we use a characterization of Dspan in terms of representation
theory, developed in [BSD25], to compute Dspan explicitly for a faithful irreducible representation, and then
bootstrap to an arbitrary representation. For both the cyclic and dicyclic cases, the bootstrapping technique
involves estimates obtained from a combinatorial analysis of the (order-theoretic) lattice of normal subgroups
of G.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 summarizes the reduction of the study of βfield, βr
field, and
Dspan to lattices, and collects various lemmas from the literature. Section 3 proves Theorem 3.1 and deduces
Theorem 3.9 from it. Section 4 proves Theorem 4.1, in two steps: Section 4.1 proves Theorem 4.4, a result
on lattices in the m “ 2 case that forms the basis of the cyclic case, and Section 4.2 uses this to complete
the cyclic case, and handles the rest of the cases.

Preliminaries
As mentioned above, the starting point for our proofs, except for the nonabelian part of Theorem 4.1, is
to reduce the invariant theory to a problem about sublattices of the integer lattice, and to study the latter.
In this section, we walk briefly through this reduction, and gather together the tools required to study the
resulting lattices, including a classical theorem from Minkowski’s geometry of numbers. We also describe a
characterization of Dspan in terms of representation theory, developed in [BSD25], that will be used in the
nonabelian part of Theorem 4.1.
