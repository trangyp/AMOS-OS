GRADED EQUIVALENCE FOR GRADED IDEMPOTENT RINGS
MIKHAILO DOKUCHAEVa AND JUAN JACOBO SIM´ONc
Abstract. In this paper, we extend the study of graded equivalences to the case of
general idempotent graded rings. We prove that the existence of a graded equivalence
between two categories of graded torsion-free unital modules may be characterized
by the existence of a Morita context with surjective trace maps. As an application
of our results we relate certain lattices of graded submodules and graded ideals
of graded equivalent garded rings and give some properties invariant under graded
equivalences.
1. Introduction
The classical Morita Theory has been addressed for the case of graded rings by
dealing with two kinds of graded-equivalences: one, the graded-equivalence, as defined
in [7] and [11], and a stronger one, the graded Morita equivalence, given in [3]. As it
has ocurred in the classical theory (see [1], [4]), the study of graded equivalences has
been carried out to the case of graded rings with local units in [8] (graded-equivalence)
and [9] (graded Morita equivalence). Besides rings with local units, the clasical Morita
Theory was extended to idempotent rings in [13] and [6]. In particular, it was proved
in [13] that, given an appropriate Morita context between idempotent torsion-free
rings, there exists an equivalence between the categories of unital torsion-free modules.
This was extended in [6] for general idempotent rings, proving also that a category
equivalence, as above, implies the existence of an appropriate Morita context.
In [5], besides the new concept of a strong equivalence for graded rings, the graded
equivalence from [7] and [11] was considered for general idempotent graded rings in
the language of Morita contexts. Thus, it is natural to study the relation between
Morita contexts and categories of modules for general idempotent graded rings, a task
acomplished in the present article.
After the preliminary Section 2, we prove in Section 3 that the graded functors,
which give a graded equivalence between the categories of unital torsion-free graded
modules, can be realized by means of appropriate HOM functors (see Proposition 3.9).
In the next Section 4, given two idempotent torsion-free graded rings A and B and a
graded equivalence between the categories of their unital torsion-free graded modules,
we produce a non-degenerate graded Morita context between A and B with surjective
trace maps (see Theorem 4.3). The main result of Section 5 is Theorem 5.9, which
states that if there exists a graded Morita context between idempotent torsion-free
graded rings A and B with torsion-free graded bimodules and surjective trace maps,

2. Background.
We take all standard definitions related to graded rings from [6] and [12]. All rings
considered in this paper are associative group graded rings, but we do not assume that
they have a unity element.
Let Γ be a multiplicative group, with identity element e ∈Γ, which will be fixed
in all what follows. Let, furthermore, A be a Γ-graded ring and let M be a Γ-graded
left A-module. We say that A is idempotent if A2 = A and M is said to be unital if
AM = M. As usual we define the left A-torsion part of M as tA(M) = {m ∈M |
Am = 0}. It is well-known that tA(M) is a graded unital left A-submodule of M. We
shall say that M is torsion-free if tA(M) = 0; that is, for any m ∈M, the equality
Am = 0 implies m = 0. There are analogous definitions for graded right A-modules.
For rings, we have to distinguish between left and right torsion. So we denote the left
torsion of A by l(A) and the right one by r(A).
Throughout this paper by a graded ring (respectively, module) we shall mean a ring
(respectively, module) graded by the group Γ. In all what follows A will denote an
idempotent graded ring, which occasionally will be assumed to be left and right torsion-
free. We denote by A−gr (resp. gr−A) the category of all unital and torsion-free left
(resp. right) graded A-modules. We shall say that a graded ring is torsion-free if it is
left and right torsion-free; in this case, A ∈A−gr and A ∈gr−A.
As we mentioned in the Introduction, we will study the graded equivalences between
categories of unital and torsion-free modules; however, these categories, as it is known,
are not closed, for example, under quotients or tensor products. Furthermore, some
Morita contexts that we will consider will not be formed by torsion-free modules,
although they will always be unital. Therefore, we will consider the category A−gr
(resp. gr−A) as a full subcategory of the category A−GR (resp. GR−A) of left (resp.
right) unital A-modules.
