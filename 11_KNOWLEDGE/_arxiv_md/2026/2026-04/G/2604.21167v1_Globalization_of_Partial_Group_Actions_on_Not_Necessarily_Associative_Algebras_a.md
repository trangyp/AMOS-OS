MIKHAILO DOKUCHAEV, EMMANUEL JEREZ, AND JOS´E L. VILCA-RODR´IGUEZ
Abstract. We extend the concept of a partial group action to non-associative algebras
in a variety V(I), solve the globalization problem within V(I) and examine its universal
property. It is achieved using what we call the “Λ-construction”, which we also apply to
deal with covariant representations in the associative and Lie algebra settings, considering
related categories and constructing an adjoint pair of functors between them. We also
show that the Λ-construction behaves well with semidirect products of Lie algebras.

Introduction
Partial group actions are a flexible and powerful generalization of classical (global) group
actions, they allow a group to act only on “pieces” of a mathematical object while retaining
much of the formalism and applications of global symmetry. They can be produced by
restricting global actions to not necessarily invariant subobjects. For example, the projective
linear group PGL(n, C) acts globally on the Riemann sphere but only partially on the
complex plane C (see [32]). This naturally suggests the question if a given partial action can
be seen as a restriction of a global one. Under the name of “maximum local transformation
groups” smooth partial actions of Lie groups on not necessarily Hausdorff manifolds were
studied from the globalization point of view by R. Palais [43, Theorem X, p. 72]. Later,

the globalization problem was independently studied by F. Abadie [1] for partial actions of
groups on topological spaces and on C∗-algebras and by J. Kellendonk and M. V. Lawson
for partial group actions on topological spaces and semilattices [32]. Since then constant
attention is payed to the globalization problem of partial actions and partial coactions:
[2–6,8–19,21,23–29,31,33–42,44–48]. The initial construction of the globalization was given
for partial group actions on smooth manifolds [43], topological spaces [1], [32], semilattices
[32] and 2-complexes [47]. The idea is essentially the same and, as it was pointed out by
J. Kellendonk and M. Lawson [32], it appeared in a wide variety of areas in the proofs of
remarkable results, which can be reformulated in terms of partial group actions. It is a
“partial” version of the tensor product of G-sets of the form G ⊗G X. The idea is natural
and efficient, and it was applied with eventual adaptations to produce globalizations in a
series of papers.
For unital associative algebras a construction was given in [20], which is an appropriate
subalgebra of the direct power AG, where the group G acts permuting the direct factors.
As in the case of the G ⊗G X-tool, this idea was further used in a number of articles with
appropriate adjustments and interesting developments.
In [31] a construction was given for the globalization of partial group actions on modules,
which is, loosly speeking, a linearization of the “partial” G ⊗G X, and produced by a
combination of tensor products of modules and that of G-sets (see Section 1.2). We refer
to it as the Λ-construction.
In this paper we further explore the Λ-construction not only for the globalization of par-
tial actions, but also to deal with covariant representations of partial representations and
their dilations. More specifically, we extend the theory of partial group actions to the setting
of non-associative algebras belonging to an arbitrary variety V(I) and develop the corre-
sponding notions of partial representations, globalizations, and covariant representations.
Our approach follows two complementary threads. First, given a partial representation π
of a group G by endomorphisms of an algebra A ∈V(I) (with images that are two-sided
ideals), we construct a canonical K-module Λ(A) that carries a natural algebra structure
and a global G-action induced from π. This construction makes Λ(A) into a non-associative
algebra in V(I), and moreover, it acts as a globalization of the partial action induced by π
on A. This result not only generalizes the classical globalization theorem for unital partial
group action on unital associative algebras proved in [20], but also provides a systematic
method to globalize partial actions in a wide variety of algebraic contexts.
Second, we
consider generalized partial actions of G on an algebra A ∈V(I) (where the images of
the partial action are subalgebras, not necessarily ideals) and show that the Λ-construction
yields a universal globalization Ω(A) for such actions. An importan example that illustrates
the deep connection between both threads is the case of partial actions on Lie algebras. In
Example 2.17 we show the interplay between the two globalization constructions in the case
of a partial action on a Lie algebra L and its induced partial representation on the universal
enveloping algebra U(L).
Next, we study partial covariant representations in the associative and Lie algebra set-
tings and the functorial behavior of the globalization process. For a partial representation
λ: G →End0(A), where A is an associative or Lie algebra and End0(A) denotes the algebra
endomorphisms of A, we define a category of partial covariant representations and show
how the Λ-construction lifts these to honest (global) covariant representations of Λ(λ). We
then isolate a natural subcategory of “Dilations” and prove an adjunction between the lifted

1. Preliminaries
Let K be an associative commutative unital ring. Throughout this work, K is fixed as
the ground ring. Unless stated otherwise, all modules considered are K-modules, and we
denote the tensor product over K of two K-modules X and Y simply as X ⊗Y . The
capital letter G will denote a group, and the identity element of an algebraic structure will
be denoted by 1, provided no ambiguity arises. As it is usual, by a non-associative algebra
we mean a not necessarily associative algebra.
This work focuses on partial actions and partial representations of groups. For simplicity,
the terms partial action and partial representation will refer specifically to partial group
actions and partial group representations, respectively.
We begin by recalling some foundational concepts related to varieties of algebras, partial
actions, and partial representations.
1.1. Varieties of algebras. Let X be a nonempty countable set. A non-associative word
of length n on X is a juxtaposition of n-elements of X with a specific way of placing
parentheses. For instance, if X = {a} then a, aa, a(aa), (aa)a are non-associative words
of length ≤3. We can multiply two non-associative words v, w by juxtaposition but we
must include parentheses around each factor. For example, the product of a(aa) and (aa)a
is the non-associative word (a(aa))((aa)a). Let us denote by F(X) the vector space with
a basis consisting of all non-associative words in X. That is, F(X) consists of all non-
associative polynomials in X with coefficients in K.
By extending the product of non-
associative words to F(X) via distributivity, we define a multiplication in F(X) that makes
it a non-associative algebra (without a unit element). The algebra F(X) is called the free
non-associtive algebra generated by X. There is a canonical inclusion j : X →F(X),
and F(X) verifies the following universal property: Given a non-associtive algebra A and
a map ϕ : X →A, there exists a unique algebra homomorphism bϕ : F(X) →A such that
bϕ ◦j = ϕ.
Let A be a non-associative algebra. A non-associative polynomial f(x1, . . . , xn) ∈F(X)
is called an identity of A if f(a1, . . . , an) = 0 for all a1, . . . , an ∈A. Let I be a subset of
F(X). The class V(I) of all non-associative algebras satisfying all identities in I is called
the variety defined by I, and it is clearly a category. One can construct a free algebra
in V(I) generated by a nonempty set Y as follows. Let T(Y, I) be the set of images of I in
F(Y ) under all homomorphisms from F(X) into F(Y ). Consider the ideal J(Y, I) in F(Y )
generated by T(Y, I). Then the algebra FI(Y ) = F(Y )/J(Y, I) belongs to the variety V(I).
If ν denotes the restriction to Y of the canonical homomorphism F(Y ) →FI(Y ), given by
b 7→b + J(Y, I), then one can verify that FI(Y ) satisfies the following universal property:
Given a non-associative algebra A ∈V(I) and a map ϕ : Y →A, there exists a unique

Let Y be a set and denote by K(Y ) the free K-module with basis Y. Then K(Y ) can be
seen as a K-submodule of the algebra F(Y ) and the map ν : Y →FI(Y ) extends to a
K-linear map i : K(Y ) →FI(Y ), which is the restriction of F(Y ) →FI(Y ) to K(Y ). Then
the above universal property of FI(Y ) implies that for any algebra A ∈V(I) and any linear
map ϕ : K(Y ) →A, there exists a unique algebra homomorphism bϕ : FI(Y ) →A such
that bϕ ◦i = ϕ. As above, the K-linear map i is injective if V(I) is non-trivial. For let A
be a non-zero algebra in V(I) and write B = ⊕y∈Y Ay, where Ay is a copy of A. Denote
by ay a non-zero element of Ay. Then the map Y →B such that y 7→ay extends to an
injective K-linear map ϕ : K(Y ) →B, and by the above property there exists an algebra
homomorphism bϕ : FI(Y ) →B such that bϕ ◦i = ϕ. Since ϕ is injective, so is i.
Let M be an arbitrary K-module and Y a generating set of M. Then the embedding
Y →M extends to an epimorphism K(Y ) →M. Let TM be the kernel of this epimorphism
and denote by JM the ideal of FI(Y ) generated by i(TM). Then the algebra
FI(M) := FI(Y )/JM
belongs to the variety V(I), and the composition of i : K(Y ) →FI(Y ) with the quotient
map FI(Y ) →FI(M) vanishes on TM, so that it defines a linear map j : M →FI(M). It
is readily seen that the following universal property is satisfied: Given an algebra A ∈V(I)
and a linear map ϕ : M →A, there exists a unique algebra homomorphism bϕ : FI(M) →A
such that bϕ ◦j = ϕ. It follows that up to isomorphism FI(M) is the unique algebra in V(I)
which satisfies this property and, in particular, FI(M) does not depend on the choice of the
generating set Y of M. We refer to FI(M) as the free algebra generated by M in V(I).
Remark 1.1. Suppose that V(I) is a variety which contains the algebras with zero multi-
plication. Then for any K-module M the above defined K-linear map j : M →FI(M)
is injective. Indeed, endowing M with the zero multiplication and applying the universal
property of FI(M) to the identity map M →M, we immediately obtain that j has to be
injective.
Note that Remark 1.1 is applicable to such classical varieties as associative algebras,
Lie algebras, alternative algebras and Jordan algebras.
More generally, any non-trivial
variety of algebras defined by a system I of homogeneous identities satisfies the condition of
Remark 1.1. This includes all non-trivial homogeneous varieties of algebras, in particular,
any non-trivial variety of algebras over an infinite field (see [49, p.8]).

(i) α is a partial action of G on the K-module A if each Ag is a submodule of A,

α = (G, A, {Ag}g∈G, {αg}g∈G)

A key example of a generalized partial action arises from the canonical restriction of a
(global) group action on an algebra. Specifically, let B ∈V(I) and let β : G →Aut(B) be
an action of G on B. If A is a subalgebra of B, then setting Ag = B ∩βg(B) and defining
αg as the restriction of βg to Ag−1, we obtain a generalized partial action of G on A. This
generalized partial action is referred to as the restriction of β to A. Furthermore, if A is
an ideal of B, the restriction of β to A is a partial action of G on A.
Definition 1.4. Let A and B be K-modules or algebras in V(I), and let α : G ↷A and
β : G ↷B be generalized partial actions. A G-equivariant map ϕ : A →B is a linear
map (or homomorphism) that satisfies the following conditions:
a) ϕ(Ag) ⊆Bg for all g ∈G,
b) ϕ(αg(x)) = βg(ϕ(x)) for all g ∈G and x ∈Ag−1.
Additionally, if ϕ is bijective, and ϕ−1 is also G-equivariant, then ϕ is called an isomor-
phism.
Clearly, the collection of all generalized partial actions of G forms a category, whose
morphisms are the G-equivariant maps.

3. Covariant representations of partial representations
This section is devoted to the covariant representation on algebras. For convenience, we
use the symbol · to denote the product on an algebra. Also, all algebras will be associative
(with or without unit) or Lie algebras over K unless otherwise stated.
We recall that if A is a Lie algebra, then a K-module M is an A-module if there exists
a K-bilinear map A × M →M, (x, m) 7→xm, satisfying the condition
(3.1)
(x · y)m = x(ym) −y(xm)
for all x, y ∈L, m ∈M.
Equivalently, M is an A-module if there exists a representation (Lie algebra homomorphism)
φ : A →gl(M),
where gl(M) denotes the Lie algebra of K-linear endomorphisms of M.
Given an algebra A and a (global) representation ρ : G →End0(A), by a covariant
representation of ρ we mean a pair (π, M), where M is an A-module and π : G →EndK(M)
is a (global) representation such that, for all g ∈G, x ∈A, and m ∈M, the following
condition holds:
πg(xm) = ρg(x)πg(m).
A morphism between two covariant representations is just a K-linear map that is simul-
taneously a morphism of A-modules and a morphism of representations. We denote by
CovRepρ(G, A) the category of covariant representations of ρ and their morphisms. If
there is no risk of confusion, we will use the notation CovRep(A) to denote the cate-
gory CovRepρ(G, A). The objects of CovRep(A) will be referred to simply as (global)
covariant representations of A.
Definition 3.1. Let λ : G →End0(A) be an ideal partial representation of G on A. A
covariant representation of λ is a pair (π, M), where M is an A-module and π : G →
EndK(M) is a partial representation of G such that, for all g ∈G, x ∈A, and m ∈M, the
following conditions hold:
(1) πg(x m) = λg(x) πg(m),
(2) εg(x m) = eg(x) m = x εg(m),
where εg = πgπg−1 and eg = λgλg−1.

and

For any m ∈M, we see that

4. Covariant representations and semidirect products of Lie algebras
Let L and M be Lie algebras. We say that L acts by derivations on M if there exists a
K-bilinear map L × M →M, (x, m) 7→xm, such that, for every x, y ∈L and m, n ∈M,
the following conditions are satisfied:
(x · y)m = x(ym) −y(xm),
x(m · n) = (xm) · n + m(x · n).
(4.1)
Equivalently, L acts by derivations on M if there exists a Lie algebra homomorphism
L →Der(M),
where Der(M) is the Lie algebra of derivations of M. Note that the first equality in (4.1)
says that M is a left L-module.
We recall that if L acts by derivations on M, then the semidirect product of L and M,
which is denoted by L ⋉M, is the Lie algebra whose underlying vector space is L × M, and
whose product is given by
(4.2)
(x, m) · (y, n) = (x · y, m · n + xn −ym).
Proposition 4.1. Let L and M be Lie algebras, with L acting by derivations on M. Suppose
that λ : G →End0(L) and π : G →End0(M) are ideal partial representations, such that
(π, M) is a covariant representation of λ. Then, the following hold:
(1) The mapping λ×π : G →End0(L⋉M), defined by (λ×π)g : (x, m) 7→(λg(x), πg(m)),
is an ideal partial representation on L ⋉M.
(2) The map Λ(L) × Λ(M) →Λ(M), defined by (⌊g, x⌋, ⌊h, m⌋) 7→⌊g, x⌋⌊h, m⌋=

quently, the mapping

is a (global) representation of G on Λ(L) ⋉Λ(M).
Proof.
(1) The only non-trivial condition required to prove for this item is that im(λ × π)g =
im λg×im πg is an ideal of L⋉M. Recall that an element (x, m) belongs to im(λ×π)g
if and only if

that εg(xn −ym) = xn −ym. Since (π, M) is a covariant representation of λ, we
obtain
εg(xm −yn) = εg(xn) −εg(ym) = eg(x)n −yεg(m) = xn −ym.

Theorem 4.2. Let L and M be Lie algebras, with L acting by derivations on M. Suppose
that λ : G →End0(L) and π : G →End0(M) are ideal partial representations such that
(π, M) is a covariant representation of λ. Then, the actions Λ(λ × π) and Λ(λ) × Λ(π) are
isomorphic. In particular:
Λ(L ⋉M) ∼= Λ(L) ⋉Λ(M).
Proof. We will verify that the mapping
ψ : Λ(L ⋉M) →Λ(L) ⋉Λ(M)
⌊g, (x, m)⌋7→(⌊g, x⌋, ⌊g, m⌋)
is an isomorphism between the actions Λ(λ × π) and Λ(λ) × Λ(π). It is easily seen that ψ

⌊g, (x, m)⌋, ⌊h, (y, n)⌋∈Λ(L ⋉M). First we calculate
⌊g, (x, m)⌋· ⌊h, (y, n)⌋= ⌊g, (x, m) · (λg−1h(y), πg−1h(n))⌋
= ⌊g, (x · λg−1h(y), m · πg−1h(n) + xπg−1h(n) −λg−1h(y)m)⌋.

+ ⌊g, xπg−1h(n)⌋−⌊g, λg−1h(y)m)⌋).

showing that ψ is a Lie algebra homomorphism.
