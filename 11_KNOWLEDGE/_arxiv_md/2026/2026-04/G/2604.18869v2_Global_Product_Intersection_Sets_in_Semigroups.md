WOUTER VAN DOORN, PIETRO MONTICONE, AND QUANYU TANG
Abstract. For a family (Aq)q∈Q of subsets of a semigroup, the product intersection set records
those exponents h ∈N for which the h-fold product set of the intersection, (T
q Aq)h, is equal to
T
q Ah
q , the intersection of the product sets. Nathanson recently asked which subsets of N can occur
as a product intersection set, both for arbitrary and for decreasing families (Aq)q∈Q. We solve
both problems by giving a complete classification. In particular, when |Q| ≥2, we show that in
either case any subset X ⊆N with 1 ∈X occurs as a product intersection set. Both classifications
were autonomously discovered and formally verified in Lean by Aristotle, a formal reasoning agent
developed by Harmonic.

4. The set HQ
This section will be devoted to determining HQ.
Proof of Theorem 1.2. Starting off with the easiest case, assume that |Q| = 1, say Q = {q}. Then
A = Aq, so that in particular Ah = Ah

Moving on to even fewer elements, let Q be empty and recall that for the empty family of subsets
of a semigroup S, the intersection is S itself. Hence the associated product intersection set is
{h ∈N : Sh = S}. We claim that this set is always either {1} or N.
To see this, if S2 = S, then by induction Sh = S for all h ∈N: once Sm = S, we have
Sm+1 = SmS = SS = S2 = S.
Conversely, if S2 ̸= S, then for every h ≥2 one has Sh ⊆S2 ̸= S, so Sh ̸= S. Thus the only
possibilities are {1} and N, and both possibilities do actually occur. Indeed, any monoid satisfies
Sh = S for all h, realizing N. On the other hand, let S = {0, 1} with multiplication xy = 0 for all
x, y ∈S. Then Sh = {0} for every h ≥2, so this semigroup realizes {1}.

Appendix: Formal Discovery and Verification with Aristotle
The proofs in this paper were autonomously discovered and formally verified in Lean by Aristotle,
a formal reasoning agent developed by Harmonic [1] and publicly available for free at aristotle.
harmonic.fun.
We asked Aristotle to characterise HQ and H∗
N separately, then to combine both characterisations
into a complete solution to Problems 10 and 11 in [2]. During the characterisation of H∗
N, our
prompt did not specify the meaning of “asymptotically strictly decreasing,” and Aristotle adopted a
definition stronger than the one in [2, Section 2]. A simple argument shows that the two definitions
yield the same set H∗
N, and the Lean code verifies this equivalence explicitly. We then asked Aristotle
to examine whether there were any other problems in [2] that could also be resolved, and it observed
that [2, Problems 4 and 7] are in fact consequences of the characterisations of HQ and H∗
N that it
had obtained at the outset.
The full Lean formalisation can be inspected interactively in the Lean 4 Web Editor. Aristotle
also informalised the Lean file into a first draft of the present paper, which we subsequently rewrote
and polished.
