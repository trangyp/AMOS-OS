G-BIRATIONALLY RIGID CUBIC THREEFOLDS
IVAN CHELTSOV, IGOR KRYLOV, SIONE MA’U
Abstract. We classify pairs (X, G) consisting of a (possibly singular) cubic threefold X ⊂P4 and
a finite subgroup G ⊂Aut(X) such that X is G-birationally rigid, i.e., X is a G-Mori fiber space
(over a point), and X is not G-birational to any G-Mori fibre space that is not G-biregular to X.

1. Introduction
The notion of birational rigidity originated in the work of Iskovskikh and Manin [IM72], where
they implicitly proved that every smooth hypersurface of degree 4 in P4 is birationally superrigid
and, in particular, non-rational. Recall that a Fano variety X is birationally rigid if X is a Mori
fiber space over a point (that is, X has terminal singularities and the class group Cl(X) has rank 1),
and X is the only Mori fiber space in its birational equivalence class. If, in addition,
Bir(X) = Aut(X),
then X is said to be birationally superrigid. Birational rigidity is an obstruction to rationality.
Problems concerning the irrationality of Fano varieties defined over non-algebraically closed fields
lead naturally to an equivariant generalization of the notion of birational rigidity. To formulate
this notion, we fix a Fano variety X that have at most terminal singularities, and we fix a finite
subgroup G ⊂Aut(X). Then the Fano variety X is said to be G-birationally rigid if the following
two conditions are satisfied:
(1) X is a G-Mori fiber space over a point. i.e., X has terminal singularities and rk Cl(X)G = 1,
(2) if Y is a G-Mori fiber space that is G-birational to X, then Y is G-biregular to X.
If, in addition, we have
BirG(X) = AutG(X),

2. The proof of Main Theorem
Let X be a smooth cubic threefold in P4, and let G be a subgroup in Aut(X). Then G is finite,
and its action on X lifts to P4, so we consider G as a subgroup in PGL5(C). To prove Theorem 3,
we must show that the following three conditions are equivalent:
(a) P4 does not contain G-invariant planes, G ̸≃C5 ⋊C4, C3 × (C5 ⋊C4), C11 ⋊C5;
(b) X is G-birationally superrigid;
(c) X is G-birationally rigid.
The implication (b) ⇒(c) follows from the definitions. If G ≃C5⋊C4 or G ≃C3×(C5⋊C4), then
the cubic threefold X is not G-birationally rigid by Theorem 11, which will be proved in Section 3.
Similarly, if G ≃C11 ⋊C5, then the cubic threefold X is not G-birationally rigid by Theorem 12,
which will be proved in Section 4. Therefore, the implication (c) ⇒(a) follows from
Lemma 7. Suppose that P4 contains a G-invariant plane. Then X is not G-birationally rigid.
Proof. Let P be the pencil in |OX(1)| consisting of all surfaces that are cut out by hyperplanes
that pass through Π.

Thus, to complete the proof of Theorem 3, it is enough to prove the implication (a) ⇒(b).
