Andriy Goychuk1, 2, 3, ∗

Analytical treatments of polymer dynamics have mostly been restricted to linear response the-
ory around some steady state obtained via perturbative field theory. Here, I derive an analytical
framework that yields unified access to the evolution of conformations, contact probabilities, and
fluctuations within a dynamical mean-field theory. Starting with the Langevin equation of a hy-
drodynamically coupled and self-avoiding heteropolymer, the key idea is to focus on the two-point
correlator as the lowest-order relevant observable. Truncating higher-order correlations via a Gaus-
sian closure leads to a self-consistent diffusion equation for the chain correlations.
The theory
is validated by contrasting coiled, globular, and self-avoiding polymers within a single dynamical
framework, and predicts hyper-compacted fractal states in hydrodynamically coupled active poly-
mers such as chromatin.
Introduction—The dynamics of polymers have at-
tracted attention for decades, from pioneering work based
on scaling concepts [1], to the theory of polymer solu-
tions [2] and melts [3], with important implications for
industrial applications and cell biology. In the cell nu-
cleus, for example, the spatiotemporal folding of chro-
mosomes into conformational ensembles with distinct fre-
quencies of contact among different regulatory elements
determines gene expression [4, 5].
Past studies have elucidated the statistics of single-
chain conformations in active and passive Rouse and
wormlike chains [6–13] and Gaussian network models [14–
19] via linear response theory.
In multi-chain systems
such as polymer melts, local polymer concentration pro-
files have been derived with self-consistent field the-
ory [3] and dynamical mean-field theory [20] based on
the Martin-Siggia-Rose formalism [21]. Exploration be-
yond the limits of analytical theory has been enabled
by Brownian dynamics simulations [22–24], perturbative
calculations, renormalization group theory, and scaling
arguments [25, 26].
The scaling behavior of self-avoiding and hydrodynam-
ically coupled homopolymers has been reproduced using
mean-field approaches reminiscent of Hartree-Fock the-
ory, similar to dynamics in mean-potential, operating
purely in Fourier space [27]. However, it is unclear how
such approaches can be used to study active heteropoly-
mers such as chromatin. Motivated by this gap in the
literature, this paper presents an analytically tractable
theory for nonlinear and nonlocal diffusion operating di-
rectly on the pairwise contact map in sequence space.

the sequence coordinate. The chain conformation is as-

Gaussian closure—A polymer such as chromatin lack-
ing a well-defined crystal structure, up to translation
and rotation, has a vanishing first moment ⟨ri(t)⟩= 0.
Such polymers only have structure in the statistical sense,
represented by correlators of second order and higher
even orders, while odd correlators vanish due to sym-
metry.
According to Marcinkiewicz’ theorem [33], the
hierarchy of correlators either truncates at the Gaussian
level, namely the two-point correlator ⟨ri(t) · rj(t′)⟩, or
is infinite. Current experimental measurements on chro-
matin yield pairwise contact probability maps, but lack
the information required to constrain higher-order cor-
relators.
Given this lack of data, I consider a Gaus-
sian approximation for the joint distribution of the chain
configurations. The Gaussian distribution can be inter-
preted as the least-biased distribution that maximizes
the information-theoretic entropy given available data,
and has been used successfully to reproduce chromatin

Spatial noise compacts Rouse polymers—Finally, moti-
vated by the literature on coherent chromatin flows [49–
51], consider the effects of spatially correlated excitations
on timescales where they are approximately Markovian.
Coherent flows in the cell nucleus occur on length scales
λ ∼1 µm [49], far exceeding the range of molecular con-
tacts, λ ≫a. Below the distance λ, coherent flows do not
agitate the separation of monomers via shear, thus recov-
ering the polymer behaviors discussed above. On larger
distances, the contact energy can be neglected, and, for
strong non-equilibrium driving, the active flows are ex-
pected to dominate over thermal noise ˆ
MinCnj ∼δij.
Finite-range correlated flows Cξ(r) = ¯δ(|r|, λ) scale as
∼∆s−3ν, whereas power-law correlated flows Cξ(r) ∼
1/|r|n scale as ∆s−nν, and both compete with the tension
term ∼−∆s2ν−2. Matching the exponents leads to an
asymptotic scaling ν = 2/5 for finite-range correlated
flows, which is more compact than a Rouse chain but less
compact than a globule [48, 52]. For power-law correlated
flows, one instead has ν = 2/(2 + n), which for n = 2
recovers Rouse statistics, for n = 3 is finite-range-like,
and for n ≥3 requires regularization to prevent a UV
divergence.
If chromatin undergoes spatially correlated nonequilib-
rium excitations as recently inferred from pairwise locus
tracking experiments and simulated with Rouse phan-
tom chains lacking hydrodynamic coupling [32], then
this structural signature should be visible in contact fre-
quency data. The underlying reason for the compaction
is that excitations correlated in real space map, via the
mean squared separation, to effective excitations corre-
lated along the sequence of the chain. As shown with lin-
ear response theory and simulations in our previous work,
correlated kicks lead to an effective attraction among
monomers independent of distance in sequence space [12].

Discussion—In summary, the main contribution of this
paper is a simple analytical framework [Eq. (2)], based on
a Gaussian closure, for the conformational dynamics of
active, hydrodynamically coupled, and self-avoiding or
self-attracting heteropolymers such as chromatin. The
unified theory geometrically interprets polymer folding
as a non-linear and non-local diffusion of the pairwise
contact probability in sequence space (Fig. 1).
It was
validated by reproducing the known scaling behavior of
fractal globules and self-avoiding chains, and predicts
hyper-compaction with fractal dimension 1/ν = 4 in ac-
tively driven Zimm chains. The trade-off is a slight devi-
ation of the scaling in self-avoiding chains from the exact
ν = 0.588, a surprising accuracy enabled by the same
cancellation of errors as Flory theory. In agreement with
our previous work [12], these results underscore that cor-
related hydrodynamic fluctuations can induce attraction
between different genomic loci. It will be interesting to
explore how this couples to other mechanisms that can
bring polymer loci together, such as correlated hydro-
dynamic fluctuations inducing capillary forces between
biomolecular condensates [55] and chemical reactions me-
diating non-reciprocal interactions [46].
In future work, this framework can be further tested by
studying the propagation of tension along chains that are
inextensible [56], incorporating hard confinement, and
considering tertiary interactions. For example, I expect
