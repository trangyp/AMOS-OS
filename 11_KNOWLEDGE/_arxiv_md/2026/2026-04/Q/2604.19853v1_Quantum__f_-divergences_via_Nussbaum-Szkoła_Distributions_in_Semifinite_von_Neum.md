QUANTUM f-DIVERGENCES VIA NUSSBAUM-SZKOŁA
DISTRIBUTIONS IN SEMIFINITE VON NEUMANN ALGEBRAS
THEODOROS ANASTASIADIS AND GEORGE ANDROULAKIS
Abstract. In this article, we prove that the quantum f-divergence between two normal
states on a semifinite von Neumann algebra is equal to the classical f-divergence between
two corresponding classical states, which are called Nussbaum-Szkoła distributions. This
result has been proved by the second named author and T.C. John for normal states on
the von Neumann algebra B(H ) of all bounded operators on a Hilbert space H . We
extend their result for normal states on any semifinite von Neumann algebra, not only
B(H ).
1
Introduction
The main result of the present article is to extend the concept of Nussbaum-Szkoła dis-
tributions to general semifinite von Neumann algebras and to prove that the f-divergence
between any two normal states on such algebras is equal to the classical f-divergence be-
tween the corresponding Nussbaum-Szkoła distributions. These distributions were origi-
nally introduced in [1] to study error exponents in quantum hypothesis testing problems.
In [1] the result was initially proved for the von Neumann algebra B(H ) of bounded
operators on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space H and for the Umegaki relative entropy.
Since then, these distributions have been used many times in the literature. A few in-
stances in which they have been useful are the following: multiple state discrimination
[2, 3, 4], Gaussian state discrimination [5], study of f-divergences between states on finite-
dimensional C∗-algebras [6, 7], obtain second order asymptotics for quantum hypothesis
testing [8, 9, 10], investigation of the large derivation regime [11], coherence distillation
[12] and derivation of quantum inequalities from their classical analogues [13, 14]. The
fact that the Nussbaum-Szkoła distributions can be used to compute divergences was
extended in [14] for any Hilbert space (finite or infinite-dimensional) and for the general
f-divergence. In this article, we extend the result for normal states on any semifinite
von Neumann algebra, not only B(H ).

the supervision of Androulakis.

Divergences are tools which are used to discriminate two classical or quantum states.
Examples of divergences include the Kullback-Leibler divergence [15], the Umegaki rela-
tive entropy [16], the Rényi [17] and Petz-Rényi divergence [18], the Hellinger divergence,
the χ2 divergence and the total variation (see [19, 20] for the last three divergences). The
notion of f-divergence for a convex function f generalizes all the above special cases of
divergences. A rich exposition of f-divergences between classical states can be viewed in
[19, 20]. The f-divergence between two quantum states was introduced by Petz in [18, 21].
An essential tool for defining f-divergences between quantum states is the relative mod-
ular operator, with respect to these states, introduced by Araki in [22] and [23].

This result is a far reaching generalization of [14, Theorem 3.8] where only states on

In the case of a general von Neumann algebra, there is no practical formula for the rel-
ative modular operator and this is an important obstacle when dealing with the quantum
f-divergence. However, Łuczak, Podsędkowska and Wieczorek in [24] discovered such a
formula for the relative modular operator in the case of semifinite von Neumann algebras.

We review the standard form of a von Neumann algebra. Recall that a cone in a Hilbert
space H is a convex set that is invariant under scalar multiplication by non-negative
scalars. Recall that a map J : H →H is called:
• conjugate-linear if it is additive and J(λξ) = λJ(ξ) for all ξ ∈H and λ ∈C,
• isometric if ∥J(ξ)∥= ∥ξ∥for all ξ ∈H ,
• involution if J2 = 1.
According to Haagerup [29, Theorem 1.6], every von Neumann algebra is isomorphic to a
von Neumann algebra M on a Hilbert space H , such that there exists a map J : H →H
which is a conjugate-linear isometric involution and there exists a closed cone P in H
which is self-dual (i.e. P = {ξ ∈H : ⟨ξ|η⟩≥0 for all η ∈P}) such that the following
four properties are satisfied:
(1) JM J = M ′,
(2) JxJ = x∗for all x ∈M ∩M ′,
(3) J(ξ) = ξ for all ξ ∈P and
(4) xJxJ(P) ⊆P for all x ∈M .
The quadruple (M , H , J, P) is called a standard form of the von Neumann algebra.
Throughout the article, we consider every von Neumann algebra in a standard form
(M , H , J, P). The standard form of a von Neumann algebra plays an important role in
defining the relative modular operator, which in turn is needed to define the quantum
f-divergence.

6
Closing Remarks
In this article, we prove that the quantum f-divergence between two normal states
on a semifinite von Neumann algebra is equal to the classical f-divergence between two
corresponding classical states. The divergences of states on these algebras have not been
explored in depth, but there is some physical motivation to study them. For example,
the Type II1 factor appears in super JT-gravity [35]. Furthermore, Type II algebras can
appear in some random matrix models [36], in quantum field theory [37] and in black hole
physics [38].
The main open problem that is related to our work is whether Theorem 1.3 still holds
if we drop the assumption that the von Neumann algebra is semifinite. Does Theorem 1.3
hold for normal states ϕ, ω on a general von Neumann algebra? It would not be surprising


## Page 28


28
if the answer to this question was affirmative. The main obstacle that has not allowed
us to prove the main theorem for any von Neumann algebra is the lack of a convenient
formula for the relative modular operator in the general case.
