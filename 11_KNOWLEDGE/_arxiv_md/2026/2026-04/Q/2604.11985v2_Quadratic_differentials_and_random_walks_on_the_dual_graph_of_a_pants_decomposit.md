GRAPH OF A PANTS DECOMPOSITION
CHARLES BORDENAVE, XINLONG DONG AND DRAGOMIR ˇSARI´C
Abstract. Let X be an infinite Riemann surface with an upper-bounded geodesic pants decom-
position. The vertices of the corresponding dual graph G are pairs of pants and edges are cuffs
with conductances equal to their lengths. We prove that the geodesic flow on X is ergodic if and
only if the random walk on G is recurrent. This yields explicit criteria for deciding, in terms of
cuff-length growth, whether the geodesic flow is ergodic. We provide concrete and new families of
Riemann surfaces with an explicit understanding of the phase transitions from recurrent to non-
recurrent geodesic flows. In addition, we show that rough isometry of surfaces does not preserve
the ergodicity of the geodesic flow while rough isometry of their dual graphs does.
The above equivalence result uses a characterization of the measured geodesic laminations on
X that arise as straightened horizontal foliations of finite-area holomorphic quadratic differentials.
The conditions on the measured laminations are translated into the conditions on the existence of
a square summable flow function on G.
1. Introduction
Let X = H/Γ be a Riemann surface with the Fuchsian group Γ not finitely generated. The
Riemann surface X supports a unique conformal hyperbolic metric, and X can be decomposed
into a union of countably many geodesic pairs of pants with possibly adding at most countably
many funnels and geodesic half-planes (see [4, 8]). The hyperbolic metric on X is determined by
the Fenchel-Nielsen parameters: the lengths of the boundary geodesics (called cuffs) and the twists
on them.
The type problem.
A Riemann surface X that does not support a Green’s function is closest
to compact Riemann surfaces (X ∈OG). In fact, X ∈OG is equivalent to many other natural
properties shared by compact surfaces such as: the Brownian motion is recurrent, the Poincar´e
series for Γ is divergent, the geodesic flow on the unit tangent bundle T 1X is ergodic for the
Liouville measure, the Dirichlet problem has a solution at infinity, etc (see [2, 33, 43, 42]). The
classical type problem is to determine whether an explicitly given Riemann surface X satisfies
X ∈OG (see [2]).

Our main result relates X ∈OG to the recurrence of the random walk on G weighted by the
cuff lengths. In this way, recurrence of Brownian motion on X and ergodicity of the geodesic flow
on T 1X are translated into an explicit combinatorial–geometric condition.
Theorem 1.1. Let X be an infinite Riemann surface with an upper-bounded geodesic pants de-
composition and associated dual graph G = (V, E, ℓX). The Brownian motion on X is recurrent if
and only if the random walk on G is recurrent.
For surfaces with positive injectivity radius, Theorem 1.1 is proved in [39]. We note that the
conclusion of Theorem 1.1 is false if we remove the assumption the pants decomposition has an
upper-bounded geodesic lengths.

Finite-area holomorphic quadratic differential.
The connection of X ∈OG to the random
walk on G in Theorem 1.1 is uncovered through the finite-area holomorphic quadratic differentials
on X.
Holomorphic quadratic differentials are fundamental objects in Teichm¨uller theory (for
example, see [17, 30, 35, 46]), hyperbolic geometry (see [2, 38]), and surface dynamics (see [13]).
On compact Riemann surfaces, the classical theorem of Hubbard and Masur [21] asserts that every
measured foliation (or, every measured lamination) is realized uniquely as the horizontal foliation
of a finite-area holomorphic quadratic differential. For infinite Riemann surfaces, however, this
correspondence fails in general: not every measured lamination arises from an integrable quadratic
differential (see [37]), and the obstruction is closely tied to the large-scale geometry of the surface.
A finite-area holomorphic quadratic differential on X induces a measured geodesic lamination
by straightening the leaves of its horizontal foliation. While this construction always yields an
injective map into the space of measured laminations (see Marden-Strebel [29] and [37]), the image
MLint(X) consists only of those laminations that satisfy additional quantitative constraints (see
[38, Theorem 1.6] and §3). Our objective is to characterize this image purely in terms of hyperbolic
geometry, expressed through intersection numbers with the cuffs of a pants decomposition.
Let X be an infinite Riemann surface and let {αn}n be an arbitrary ordering of the cuffs (i.e.,
boundary geodesics) of a geodesic pants decomposition of X. We denote by {βn}n the family
of simple closed geodesics that are pairwise disjoint, each βk intersects αk in a minimal number
of points and does not intersect αn for n ̸= k, and the length of βk is minimal among these
choices (see Figure 3). Given a measured lamination µ on X, we denote by i(µ, αn) and i(µ, βn)
its intersection numbers with αn and βn respectively, which are purely hyperbolic geometry data.
Our result gives a complete characterization of the image of the space of finite-area holomorphic
quadratic differentials in terms of such data.

5. Random walks and the type problem
In this section, we prove Theorem 1.1. The Hopf-Tsuji-Sullivan theorem states that the geodesic
flow on the unit tangent bundle T 1X of a Riemann surface X is ergodic if and only if the Brownian
motion on X is recurrent if and only if the Poincar´e series of the Fuchsian group Γ of X = H/Γ
is divergent if and only if X does not have a Green’s function. It is well-known that the above
conditions are satisfied when X is a compact (genus at least two) or a (hyperbolic) finite-area
Riemann surface, and the above properties are not satisfied when the Fuchsian group Γ is of the
second kind. When the Fuchsian group Γ is of the first kind but not finitely generated, then the
Riemann surface X = H/Γ can either have ergodic geodesic flow or it may not. The type problem

is deciding when an explicitly given Riemann surface has ergodic geodesic flow.
The classical
notation for this class is X ∈OG, which should be read as the Riemann surface X has no Green’s
function.
We are interested in determining when a Riemann surface given by the Fenchel-Nielsen param-
eters on a pants decomposition lies in OG. The standing assumption is that the cuff lengths are
bounded above. In this case, the deformations of the surface X by twists along the cuffs, which are
at most the full length of the cuffs, are quasiconformally equivalent to the original Riemann surface
structure. Since the class OG is invariant under the quasiconformal deformations, our conditions
will only contain the lengths of the cuffs.
