Quantization-Aware EE Optimization and SE-EE
Tradeoff for MiLAC-Aided MU-MISO Beamforming
Yuchen Zhang, Member, IEEE, Pinjun Zheng, Member, IEEE, and Tareq Y. Al-Naffouri, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—In large antenna arrays, hardware power consump-
tion becomes a dominant design constraint, making energy
efficiency (EE) a first-class objective alongside spectral efficiency
(SE). Microwave linear analog computer (MiLAC)-aided beam-
forming, whose front end is a passive reciprocal stream-to-
antenna network, emerges as a promising candidate to address
this tension by reducing the active radio-frequency chain count to
the stream number, at a moderate SE cost. Despite this promise,
no EE optimization framework has been established for MiLAC-
aided beamforming that accounts for digital-to-analog converter
quantization noise and post-quantized transmit power. We fill
this gap for downlink multiuser multiple-input single-output
systems by formulating quantization-aware EE maximization
over the MiLAC-feasible beamformer and characterizing the
resulting SE-EE tradeoff. Three contributions follow. First, we
prove a row-space optimality property of the effective MiLAC-
aided beamformer, yielding an equivalent reduced-dimension
reformulation whose complexity scales with the stream number
rather than the antenna number. Second, we develop a low-
complexity Dinkelbach-weighted minimum mean-square error
algorithm aided by projected gradient descent that is guaranteed
to converge to a stationary point. Third, we cast the SE-EE
tradeoff as a multi-objective optimization problem and trace the
Pareto boundary via the weighted-sum method. An alternative
reduced-dimension coordinate removes the bilinearity in the
original parameterization, and an auxiliary-variable lift combined
with successive convex approximation yields a convex per-iteration
subproblem with guaranteed convergence. Numerical results on
a DeepMIMO v4 deployment demonstrate that MiLAC-aided
beamforming substantially improves EE over digital and hybrid
benchmarks at a moderate SE cost. The tradeoff boundary anal-
ysis further shows that MiLAC-aided beamforming significantly
expands the achievable SE-EE operating region relative to digital
and hybrid architectures.
Index Terms—Beamforming, DAC quantization, energy effi-
ciency, MiLAC, MU-MISO, PGD, SE-EE tradeoff, WMMSE.

A microwave linear analog computer (MiLAC) is a tunable
multiport microwave network that processes signals linearly as
they propagate through it [9], [10]. For beamforming, K active
RF/DAC chains drive the stream-side ports while N antennas
connect to the remaining ports, so the stream-to-antenna map-
ping is executed at electromagnetic speed through a passive net-
work rather than in digital or active analog hardware [10]. Prac-
tically desirable considerations restrict admittances to purely
imaginary (lossless) and symmetric (reciprocal) values, since
nonzero real parts would either dissipate power or require
active components, and asymmetric admittances would require
non-reciprocal devices [11]. Under this restriction, the scatter-
ing matrix is unitary and symmetric, constraining the feasible
beamformer set [11]. This paper adopts lossless reciprocal
MiLAC throughout.
Two features distinguish MiLAC from the digital and hybrid
architectures above. First, its active front end is stream-scaled
(K chains) rather than antenna- or RF-chain-scaled, eliminating
the N-scaled RF/DAC cost of digital beamforming and the
NRF-scaled cost of hybrid beamforming. Second, its stream-
to-antenna network is passive and purely reactive: the N-
dependent power originates from per-element quiescent bias
currents of tunable admittances, not from per-element active
circuitry such as the PS networks in hybrid architectures [6],
[7]. This bias-driven dissipation mechanism is analogous to
the power model experimentally validated on reconfigurable
intelligent surface (RIS) prototypes [12]. Importantly, the pas-
sive bias term still grows with network size (quadratically
in N+K), so the architectural advantage is a reallocation of
N-dependent power from active to passive hardware, not a
complete decoupling from the antenna number. Whether (and
over what operating regime) this reallocation yields a EE gain

The MiLAC concept has developed rapidly along two
threads. The first treats MiLAC as a general-purpose analog
computer: the foundational theory in [9], [10] established
that, with unconstrained admittances, a tunable multiport net-
work can implement general linear operations with favor-
able computational complexity. The second applies MiLAC to
beamforming under lossless and reciprocal constraints. In the
point-to-point setting, lossless reciprocal MiLACs achieve full
MIMO capacity [13], and reduced-complexity stem-connected
architectures lower the admittance count from O((N+K)2)
to O(NK) while preserving capacity [14]. In the multiuser
multiple-input multiple-output (MU-MISO) setting, the feasible
beamformer set was characterized and beamforming designs
were proposed [11], while a performance-limit analysis showed
that lossless reciprocal MiLACs cannot replicate every fully
digital beamformer, although the SE gap shrinks as N/K
grows [15]. Complementary efforts address analog-domain
channel estimation [16], physics-compliant mutual-coupling-
aware modeling [17], and hardware realizations using hybrid
couplers and PSs [18].
Despite this progress, all existing MiLAC studies optimize
for SE. None addresses the power-consumption accounting
essential for EE analysis. Three elements are missing: (i) a
transmitter-side power model that captures DAC quantization
noise via the additive quantization noise model (AQNM)
and accounts for post-quantized transmit power, (ii) an EE-
oriented solution that exploits MiLAC-aided beamforming’s
unique stream-domain signal structure, and (iii) a systematic
characterization of the operating regime in which MiLAC-aided
beamforming’s hardware advantage translates into a tangible
SE-EE benefit relative to digital and hybrid benchmarks.
EE optimization for large-array architectures has been stud-
ied extensively. Foundational fractional-programming frame-
works appear in [19], [20], and scaling laws for very large
arrays are analyzed in [21]. For fully digital MIMO, jointly
optimal antenna/user/power EE design is studied in [22], joint
EE–SE analysis is treated in [5], coordinated multi-cell EE
precoding is developed in [23], and the interplay between DAC
resolution and EE is analyzed in [6], [7], [24]. For hybrid
beamforming, low-complexity designs that reduce front-end
power appear in [8], [25], and architecture-level EE compar-
isons accounting for PS power are reported in [7], [26]. For
RIS-aided systems, which share the passive multiport hardware
philosophy, EE and power-consumption frameworks appear
in [12], [27]. None of these works addresses MiLAC under the
combination of transmitter-side DAC-AQNM, post-quantized
transmit power accounting, and a passive-network static-power
model.
This paper aims to answer two coupled questions: how
should MiLAC-aided beamforming be optimized for EE under
quantization-aware modeling, and in which operating regimes
does that optimization expose a meaningful SE-EE tradeoff rel-
ative to digital and hybrid benchmarks? The main contributions
are as follows.
• Quantization-aware EE formulation for MiLAC: We
introduce the first EE maximization formulation for MU-
MISO lossless reciprocal MiLAC-aided beamforming,
coupling the transmitter-side DAC-AQNM signal model
with a post-quantized transmit power constraint and a
power consumption model that captures the power am-
plifier (PA) supply, stream-scaled RF/DAC circuit power,
and MiLAC circuit-incurred static power.1
• Row-space reduction and low-complexity EE solution:
We prove row-space optimality and derive an equivalent
reduced-dimension parameterization that preserves both
the achievable rate and the post-quantized power. This
reduction exploits a structural property unique to MiLAC:
stream-domain DAC placement yields a full-covariance
AQNM distortion compatible with row-space projec-
tion, a property that digital/hybrid beamforming lacks.
On the reduced-dimension problem, we develop a low-
complexity Dinkelbach-weighted minimum mean square
error (WMMSE) algorithm with closed-form weighted
power updates and a projected gradient descent (PGD)-
based F update, cutting the dominant per-iteration cost
from N- to K-scaling.
• SE-EE tradeoff characterization: We formulate joint SE
and EE maximization as a multi-objective optimization
(MOO) problem and adopt the weighted-sum method to
trace the frontier. Each weighted-sum subproblem is recast
in an equivalent K × K reduced coordinate that elimi-
nates the bilinearity, then lifted with auxiliary variables
and solved by successive convex approximation (SCA)
technique. The per-iteration subproblem is convex and the
iterates are proved to converge to a stationary point.
• Site-specific evaluation and operating-regime insights:
Extensive simulations on a DeepMIMO v4 deployment
show that MiLAC attains significantly higher EE than
digital and both FC and SC hybrid benchmarks across
a transmit-power sweep, at a moderate SE cost. The
tradeoff boundary comparison further reveals that MiLAC
achieves a substantially expanded SE-EE operating region
that digital and hybrid architectures cannot match.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section II
introduces the MiLAC system model and the quantization-
aware EE formulation. Section III formulates the optimiza-
tion problem and identifies the reduced-dimension property.
Section IV develops the low-complexity Dinkelbach-WMMSE
solution. Section V formulates the SE-EE tradeoff as a MOO
problem and develops the weighted-sum solver. Section VI
reports benchmark comparisons. Section VII concludes.

Remark 3 (Overall Convergence): Algorithm 2 features two
nested loops whose convergence properties are as follows.
Inner loop (Algorithm 1): The receiver and weight up-
dates (46)-(47) are globally optimal for fixed beamformers, and
the power update (52) solves its separable convex subproblem
in closed form. Both provide monotone descent on the WMMSE
objective (45). The Y-update (Proposition 4) solves its convex
subproblem over C to global optimality via PGD while joint
power-budget feasibility is maintained by the p-update at every
cycle. Therefore, the alternating minimization converges to a
stationary point of (45) [31].
Outer loop (Dinkelbach). The Dinkelbach parameter λ(n)
equals the EE achieved by the inner-loop solution at iteration
n. Because Algorithm 1 returns a point whose subtractive-
form value is nonnegative (the previous iterate is always
feasible), the sequence {λ(n)} is monotonically nondecreasing
and upper-bounded by the finite global EE optimum. Therefore,
{λ(n)} converges [29]. The limit point is guaranteed to be a
stationary point of the original fractional EE problem (35).

For fairness, all four solvers share a common outer struc-

into a subtractive-form inner problem, and the inner problem
is solved by WMMSE-based alternating optimization with
MMSE receiver, MSE weight, and beamformer updates. The
AQNM quantization parameters (αq, βq) enter all architectures
identically, and the Dinkelbach parameter λ is updated by
the same rule (40). What differs across architectures is the
beamformer update and the distortion structure it induces.
For digital beamforming, the beamformer W ∈CN×K is
updated in closed form via a regularized linear system with
an N × N inverse, and the AQNM distortion covariance is
diagonal in the antenna domain, depending on the per-antenna
row norms of W. For hybrid beamforming (FC and SC),
the precoder factorizes as W = FRFFBB, the baseband
precoder FBB ∈CNRF×K admits a closed-form update of the
same WMMSE-derived regularized form as the digital one,
operating in the effective channel G = HFRF, while the
analog precoder FRF is updated via projected gradient descent
onto the constant-modulus manifold (element-wise for hybrid-
FC, block-wise for hybrid-SC). The distortion covariance is
diagonal in the RF-chain domain, propagated to the antenna
domain through FRF.

VII. CONCLUSION
This paper established the first quantization-aware EE opti-
mization framework for MiLAC-aided MU-MISO beamform-
ing. Exploiting that MiLAC’s stream-domain DAC placement
induces a full-covariance AQNM distortion, we proved that the
EE-optimal beamformer lies in the row space of the channel
matrix, yielding a K×K reduced-dimension parameterization
that cuts the dominant per-iteration cost from N- to K-
scaling without approximation. On this reduced problem, a
Dinkelbach-WMMSE algorithm with closed-form weighted
power updates and a PGD-based mapping-matrix update con-
verges monotonically to a stationary point. For the SE-EE
tradeoff, an alternative K×K coordinate removes the bilin-
earity and an auxiliary-variable lift solved by SCA yields a
convex per-iteration subproblem that converges to a KKT point
at every weight, enabling the full boundary to be traced via the
weighted-sum method.
On a site-specific DeepMIMO deployment, MiLAC achieves
the highest EE across a 10-40 dBm power sweep at a moderate
SE cost, outperforming both FC and SC hybrid benchmarks and
remaining robust until the per-admittance static-power proxy
exceeds 100× (versus digital) or 60× (versus hybrid-SC) its
baseline. The tradeoff boundary analysis further shows that
MiLAC-aided beamforming expands the achievable operating
region along both the power and array-size axes, an advantage
digital and hybrid benchmarks cannot match.
Hardware-calibrated power measurements on MiLAC pro-
totypes are the most pressing next step. Further extensions
include using hybrid digital-MiLAC beamforming structure to
elevate SE while preserving EE advantage, exploiting reduced-
complexity architectures [14] to further lower the hardware
cost associated with admittance number, establishing insertion-
loss and mutual-coupling-aware modeling [17], and evaluating
multi-cell and multi-carrier deployment.
