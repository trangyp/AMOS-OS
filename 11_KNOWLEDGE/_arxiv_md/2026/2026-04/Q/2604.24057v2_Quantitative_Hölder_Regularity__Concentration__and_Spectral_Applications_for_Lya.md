QUANTITATIVE H¨OLDER REGULARITY, CONCENTRATION,
AND SPECTRAL APPLICATIONS FOR LYAPUNOV
EXPONENTS OF RANDOM GL(2, R) COCYCLES, WITH
EXTENSIONS TO GL(d, R)
ABDOULAYE THIAM
Abstract. This paper develops a quantitative regularity theory for the Lya-
punov exponents of random products of matrices in GL(2, R), with extensions
to GL(d, R) for all d ≥2.
At every compactly supported measure ν with
simple Lyapunov spectrum, we give an explicit closed-form H¨older exponent
β∗(ν, θ) and constant in the modulus of continuity of λ± in the Wasserstein-
plus-Hausdorff metric, depending only on the eccentricity of supp ν, the Lya-
punov gap, and the H¨older index θ.
At every ν ∈Mc(GL(2, R)) we iden-
tify the log-H¨older exponent of Tall and Viana as κ∗(ν, θ) = θ/(2 + θ) un-
der a natural mixing hypothesis, and θ/(8(1 + θ)) in the perpetuity regime.
The same spectral-gap method yields a large deviation principle with explicit
rate function, Hoeffding-Azuma concentration inequalities, an extension to
Markov-chain driven cocycles with closed-form exponent, and a quantitative
log-H¨older modulus of continuity for the integrated density of states of one-
dimensional random Schr¨odinger operators with absolutely continuous disor-
der. The H¨older theory extends to GL(d, R) for the top exponent under spec-
tral simplicity, and to the partial sums Λk = λ1 + · · · + λk under strong k-
irreducibility, yielding H¨older continuity of each individual sub-top exponent.
A method-optimality proposition shows that β∗is the best exponent obtain-
able from the linear balance of axioms (A1)-(A3) of the spectral-gap method;
strict improvement requires either modifying these axioms or adopting a dif-
ferent proof strategy. A lower-bound proposition adapted from Duarte, Klein,
and Santos rules out uniform H¨older continuity across Mc(GL(2, R)).

Three natural regularity questions present themselves:
(Q1) Continuity. When is ν 7→λ±(ν) continuous, and in what topology?
(Q2) Modulus of continuity. When continuity holds, what is the optimal H¨older
or log-H¨older modulus of continuity, and how do the constants depend on
the data?

The qualitative answer to (Q1) is essentially complete in GL(2, R). Furstenberg
and Kifer (1983) established continuity at strongly irreducible measures with simple
top spectrum, and Hennion (1984) extended the analysis to reducible products of in-
dependent random matrices; Le Page (1982), Guivarc’h and Raugi (1985), and Hen-
nion (1997) developed Kato perturbation theory for the projective Markov operator,
which gave continuity at all measures with simple Lyapunov spectrum; Bocker and
Viana (2017) extended continuity to every compactly supported measure, including
the degenerate locus λ+ = λ−, in the topology of weak-∗convergence plus Haus-
dorff convergence of supports. The qualitative extension to GL(d, R) for arbitrary
d was established by Avila, Eskin, and Viana (2023). Outside the i.i.d. random
matrix setting, continuity can fail at non-simple-spectrum measures, as shown for
area-preserving diffeomorphisms by Bochi (2002) and extended by Bochi and Viana
(2005).
For (Q2), the most refined available result is Tall and Viana (2020): H¨older con-
tinuity holds at ν in the non-degenerate regime λ+(ν) > λ−(ν), and a log-H¨older
modulus of continuity holds universally, in a neighborhood whose size and expo-
nents are not given in closed form (Proposition 1.1 below). A parallel weak-H¨older
continuity, by large-deviation methods and the avalanche principle, was obtained
by Duarte and Klein (2016, 2019); explicit Lipschitz and H¨older bounds in the
analytic-cocycle setting are due to Bourgain and Goldstein (2000); Goldstein and
Schlag (2001). Duarte, Klein, and Santos (2020) constructed Schr¨odinger cocy-
cles showing that uniform H¨older continuity at any positive exponent is impossible
across Mc(GL(2, R)), so a universal log-H¨older bound is the best one can hope for
at the level of generality of Tall and Viana (2020). Constructive lower bounds for
irreducible Bernoulli cocycles, with computable algorithms, are due to Baraviera
and Duarte (2019).

Despite this substantial progress, the existing literature leaves the closed-form
quantitative content of (Q1), (Q2), and (Q3) open: the H¨older exponent of Tall and
Viana (2020) is constructed implicitly and is not given as a function of the data; the
log-H¨older exponent is asserted to exist but is not identified; explicit variance prox-
ies and rate functions for (Q3) are absent; the qualitative Markov-chain continuity
of Malheiro and Viana (2015) has no quantitative form; quantitative continuity of
the IDS in the disorder distribution has not been recorded; quantitative continuity
of the top Lyapunov exponent in GL(d, R) for arbitrary d, and of the sub-top expo-
nents under strong irreducibility, has not been established. The aim of the present
paper is to address these gaps in a uniform way, by means of a single quantitative
spectral-gap argument for the Markov operator on H¨older functions on projective
space, augmented by a case-by-case treatment of the perpetuity regime in the spirit
of (Tall and Viana, 2020, §4.5.2) and a Grassmannian variant for the sub-top case.
We obtain seven main theorems, namely Theorem 1.2 through Theorem 1.9, each
with explicit closed-form constants.

1.1. What has not been done: gaps in the literature. Despite the substantial
progress just summarized, several questions remain open or addressed only at the
qualitative level. Our paper resolves the items below; we list each gap explicitly to
clarify the contribution that follows.
(G1) Closed-form H¨older exponent and constant in GL(2, R). Tall and Viana
(2020) established H¨older continuity of λ± in the non-degenerate regime,
but the H¨older exponent β in their Proposition 1.1(A) is obtained through
an implicit construction and is not expressed in closed form; the constant
C and the neighborhood U are not quantified. The proof proceeds through
a three-case analysis (strongly irreducible, one invariant line, two invariant
lines) that obscures the underlying geometric mechanism.
(G2) Identification of the log-H¨older exponent in Tall-Viana. The exponent κ in
Proposition 1.1(B) is asserted to exist but is not identified explicitly. The
dependence of κ on the H¨older index θ and on the structural type of ν
(within the Tall-Viana classification) is not given.
(G3) Explicit concentration with closed-form variance proxy. The exponential
concentration of n−1 log∥An
xv∥around λ+(ν) is implicit in Le Page (1982);
Guivarc’h and Raugi (1985), but a Hoeffding-Azuma-type bound with an
explicit variance proxy in closed form, suitable for direct numerical use, has
not appeared.
(G4) Explicit LDP rate function. The large deviation principle for the Lyapunov
averages is implicit in the spectral theory of the projective Markov operator,
but the rate function is not generally exhibited as the Legendre transform of
an explicit pressure functional with closed-form formulas for its derivatives.
(G5) Quantitative continuity for Markov-chain driven cocycles.
Malheiro and
Viana (2015) proved continuity of λ±(P, A) for Markov-chain driven cocy-
cles, but only qualitatively; an explicit H¨older modulus depending on the
chain spectral gap and the cocycle data has not been recorded.
(G6) Quantitative continuity of the IDS in the disorder distribution. The conti-
nuity of the IDS in the energy variable is well-studied, but the continuity
in the disorder distribution µ, with an explicit modulus in the Wasserstein
topology on µ, has not been previously established with the resolution we
obtain.
(G7) Quantitative continuity of λ1 in GL(d, R) for arbitrary d.
The qualita-
tive continuity in arbitrary dimension was established by Avila, Eskin, and
Viana (2023); a quantitative H¨older bound with closed-form constants in
arbitrary dimension has not been recorded.
(G8) Quantitative continuity of sub-top Lyapunov exponents. The sub-top Lya-
punov exponents λk for k ≥2 in GL(d, R) are subtle objects: they are con-
tinuous under strong k-irreducibility (a hypothesis going back to Fursten-
berg and Kifer (1983)), but a quantitative H¨older modulus has not been
derived.
(G9) Method-optimality of the H¨older exponent. A precise statement of which
exponents are achievable within the standard spectral-gap method (versus
what improvements would require different proof strategies) has not been
formalized.

We resolve (G8) by establishing quantitative H¨older continuity of the partial sums
Λk(ν) = λ1(ν) + · · · + λk(ν) under strong k-irreducibility (Theorem 1.9).

proof uses a Markov operator on the Grassmannian Gr(k, d) together with the
cocycle on ΛkRd; the resulting H¨older exponent depends on λk(ν)−λk+1(ν) and the
eccentricity. By subtraction, individual sub-top exponents λk(ν) inherit a H¨older
modulus (Corollary 11.10). This appears to be the first quantitative continuity result
for sub-top Lyapunov exponents in this generality.
Novelty 8: method-optimality and structural sharpness. We resolve (G9) by formal-
izing the spectral-gap method as a triple of axioms (Definition 11.15) and proving
that the H¨older exponent β∗(ν, θ) achieved by Theorem 1.2 is optimal within this
class of proofs (Proposition 11.16).
Strict improvement requires different proof
strategies (avalanche principle, harmonic analysis, weighted Banach spaces). Com-
bined with the lower bound from Duarte, Klein, and Santos (2020) that we record
as Proposition 11.12, this clarifies precisely what the spectral-gap method achieves
and what it does not.
Summary of the contribution. The paper contains seven main theorems (The-
orem 1.2 through Theorem 1.9) supplying explicit closed-form moduli of continu-
ity, concentration inequalities, large deviation principles, and a method-optimality
proposition for Lyapunov exponents of random matrix products. Every constant
is given in closed form, every result has a complete proof, and the nine gaps (G1)-
(G9) listed in Subsection 1.1 are addressed in turn (with (G3) and (G4) addressed
jointly under Novelty 3, so that nine gaps are resolved by eight novelties). The
unifying technical thread is a single quantitative spectral-gap argument for the
Markov operator on H¨older functions, refined to handle the degenerate locus, the
higher-dimensional case, and the sub-top case via the Grassmannian.

11. Extensions, sharpness, and optimality
In this section we extend the results of the previous sections in three direc-
tions. First, we establish that the quantitative H¨older continuity of Theorem 1.2
extends from GL(2, R) to GL(d, R) for all d ≥2, for the top Lyapunov exponent
λ1(ν) under the assumption of a simple top Lyapunov spectrum λ1(ν) > λ2(ν)
(Theorem 11.4 below). Second, we prove that the log-H¨older continuity of Theo-
rem 1.3 cannot be improved to uniform H¨older continuity across Mc(GL(2, R)), by
constructing explicit Schr¨odinger-cocycle families where H¨older continuity fails at
any positive exponent (Proposition 11.12). Third, we prove a method-optimality
proposition: the exponent β∗(ν, θ) of Theorem 1.2 is the best exponent derivable
from the spectral-gap interpolation scheme formalized in Section 4, so any strict
improvement requires a different proof strategy (Proposition 11.16).
These three extensions together articulate the precise scope of our results: the
GL(d) extension shows the regularity theory is structural and not specific to di-
mension 2; the lower bound shows that the log-H¨older bound is the best universal
modulus; and the method-optimality shows that improving β∗requires new tech-
niques beyond the spectral-gap method used throughout this paper.

Remark 11.17 (Scope of the optimality). Proposition 11.16 does not establish that
β∗(ν, θ) is the globally sharp H¨older exponent; it shows only that within the linear-
balance combination of (A1)-(A3), no rearrangement of the iteration count n as
a function of Wθ produces a better exponent. Strict improvement of β∗requires
either (i) sharper input bounds for (A1), (A2), or (A3) (e.g., a smaller effective C2
via weighted or anisotropic Banach spaces, or a larger τ −1 via finer mixing esti-
mates); (ii) a non-linear balance scheme (e.g., a multi-scale iteration that interleaves
contraction and perturbation steps differently); or (iii) a proof strategy outside
the spectral-gap class entirely. Examples of (iii) include the avalanche principle,
harmonic-analytic methods, and analytic perturbation theory; see Remark 11.18.
Remark 11.18 (Methods beyond the spectral-gap class). Several known techniques
are capable of producing sharper exponents than β∗in specific settings:
1. Analytic perturbation (for analytic families of measures): Ruelle (1979);
Peres (1991) obtain analytic dependence, which is much stronger than
H¨older.
2. Avalanche principle (for quasi-periodic and random cocycles): Goldstein
and Schlag (2001); Duarte and Klein (2016) obtain H¨older exponents un-
der specific structural hypotheses (Diophantine rotation numbers, large-
deviation estimates).
3. Harmonic analysis (for Bourgain-Goldstein-type models): Bourgain and
Goldstein (2000) use harmonic-analytic methods specific to the integer lat-
tice to obtain Lipschitz bounds.
Each of these methods goes beyond the spectral-gap setting of Definition 11.15, and
in their respective domains produces better exponents. The method-optimality of
β∗applies within our strictly axiomatic setting (A1)-(A3), and should be interpreted
as characterizing the limits of elementary spectral-gap reasoning.

The qualitative continuity of Lyapunov exponents in GL(2) at all

12. Concluding remarks and open problems
The results of this paper develop a quantitative regularity theory for Lyapunov
exponents of random products of matrices in GL(2, R), extending the qualitative

theorems of Tall and Viana (2020); Bocker and Viana (2017) through explicit ex-
ponents, concentration inequalities, Markov-chain extensions, and spectral applica-
tions. We close with several open questions and directions for future work.
12.1. Optimal exponents. The H¨older exponent β∗(ν, θ) of Theorem 1.2, pro-
duced by the spectral-gap method of Section 4, is far from optimal in numerical
examples (Section 10). Proposition 11.16 in Section 11 establishes that β∗is the
method-optimal exponent within the spectral-gap class of proofs; identifying the
globally sharp exponent, as a function of the data (ecc(ν), Λp, θ), remains an open
problem. Matching lower bounds via Schr¨odinger-cocycle constructions (Proposi-
tion 11.12, after Duarte, Klein, and Santos (2020)) show that H¨older continuity
fails uniformly, so any improvement to β∗would be measure-dependent. A promis-
ing direction is the avalanche principle of Goldstein and Schlag (2001), which has
been used successfully for quasi-periodic cocycles but remains untested in the i.i.d.
setting.
12.2. Sub-top Lyapunov exponents beyond strong irreducibility. Theo-
rem 11.9 of Subsection 11.2 establishes quantitative H¨older continuity for the par-
tial sums Λk(ν) = λ1(ν) + · · · + λk(ν) in GL(d, R) under strong k-irreducibility,
and yields H¨older continuity of the individual sub-top exponents λk(ν) via Corol-
lary 11.10.
The strong-irreducibility hypothesis is used in two essential places:
the uniqueness of the stationary measure on Gr(k, d) and the preservation of the
spectral gap under perturbation.
Removing this hypothesis, even qualitatively,
is an open problem in the Lyapunov-exponent continuity literature (Viana, 2014,
§6.4). Partial progress for reducible but diagonalizable cocycles would likely require
adapting the block-triangular arguments of Furstenberg and Kifer (1983), or the
renormalization-based constructions of Bonatti (2004), to the quantitative setting
of this paper.
12.3. Continuity in the Wasserstein-θ topology at non-simple spectrum.
Theorem 1.3 gives a universal log-H¨older bound, but the question of whether a
stronger bound (e.g., H¨older with small exponent) holds generically at λ+ = λ−is
open. The counterexamples of Duarte, Klein, and Santos (2020) rule out H¨older
continuity uniformly, but they leave open the possibility of a dense Baire-category
class at which the Lyapunov exponent is H¨older continuous, though generically not.
12.4. Analytic dependence. When the data (ν or A, P) is restricted to a real-
analytic family, one expects the Lyapunov exponent to depend analytically (under
simplicity assumptions). The analyticity result of Peres (1991) for i.i.d. random ma-
trices with fixed matrices and varying weights is a classical example; an extension
to the random-quasi-periodic setting is Bezerra, S´anchez, and Tall (2021). Com-
bining the quantitative spectral-gap estimates of this paper with the perturbation
theory of Kato would yield quantitative analyticity estimates, i.e., explicit domains
of analyticity. Such estimates would be useful in the spectral theory of random
Schr¨odinger operators, where the Lyapunov exponent as a function of the energy
E is a key ingredient.
12.5. Connection to random dynamical systems. The Lyapunov exponent
regularity theory developed here is a specific instance of the more general problem
