Quantum Property Testing for Bounded-Degree Directed Graphs
Pan Peng∗
Jingyu Wu†
Abstract
We study quantum property testing for directed graphs with maximum in-degree and out-
degree bounded by some universal constant d. For a proximity parameter ε, we show that any
property that can be tested with Oε,d(1) queries in the classical bidirectional model, where both
incoming and outgoing edges are accessible, can also be tested in the quantum unidirectional
model, where only outgoing edges are accessible, using n1/2−Ωε,d(1) queries. This yields an almost
quadratic quantum speedup over the best known classical algorithms in the unidirectional model.
Moreover, we prove that our transformation is almost tight by giving an explicit property Pε that
is ε-testable within Oε(1) classical queries in the bidirectional model, but requires eΩ(n1/2−f ′(ε))
quantum queries in the unidirectional model, where f′(ε) is a function that approaches 0 as ε
approaches 0.
As a byproduct, we show that in the unidirectional model, the number of occurrences of any
constant-size subgraph H can be approximated up to additive error δn using o(√n) quantum
queries.

Introduction
Graph property testing is a family of fundamental partial decision problems that seeks to
distinguish between graphs that satisfy a given property (e.g., being connected) and those that
are far from having that property (e.g., having many disconnected components), while examining
only a small portion of the input graph.
The notion of distance is quantified by a proximity
parameter ε, where a graph is said to be ε-far from satisfying a property if more than an ε-fraction
of its representation must be modified to make it satisfy the property. Given such a property,
a randomized algorithm that has query access to the graph and distinguishes between satisfying
and ε-far instances with probability at least 2/3 is called an ε-tester; the property is then said
to be ε-testable by the algorithm. This field provides a foundational framework for understanding
how much local information is sufficient to make reliable decisions about global graph properties.
Beyond its theoretical significance, graph property testing is motivated by practical needs: it
enables efficient analysis of massive networks without reading the entire input and can serve as
a lightweight preprocessing step before applying more computationally intensive methods such as
learning algorithms. Since the seminal works of Goldreich, Goldwasser, and Ron on dense graphs
[GGR98] and of Goldreich and Ron on bounded-degree graphs [GR97], the area has seen substantial
growth over the past nearly three decades. For comprehensive surveys, see [Gol17, BY22].
The rise of quantum computing has naturally led to the field of quantum property testing,
which lies at the intersection of quantum algorithms and property testing. It encompasses three
main directions: quantum testing of classical properties, classical testing of quantum properties,
and quantum testing of quantum properties. The most studied among these is quantum testing of
classical properties (see the survey [MdW16]). A central question is whether quantum algorithms
can outperform classical testers, and if so, by how much – ranging from exponential to polynomial
speedups, or none at all.
This is closely linked to understanding what kinds of symmetries a
property can exhibit while still enabling (super-polynomial) quantum speedups (see e.g. [AA14,
Cha19, BDCG+20]).
In the context of quantum testing of graph properties, Chakraborty, Fischer, Matsliah and de
Wolf [CFMdW10] studied the problem of testing graph isomorphism in the adjacency matrix model
for dense graphs. They demonstrated a polynomial quantum speedup in query complexity over
classical testers in the setting where one graph is given in advance, and the other can only be
accessed via adjacency matrix queries [FM08].
Subsequently, Ambainis, Childs and Liu [ACL11] introduced the first quantum algorithms for
testing bipartiteness and expansion in the adjacency list model for bounded-degree graphs.
By
combining classical and quantum techniques, they achieved1 eO(n1/3) query complexity for both
properties, demonstrating a polynomial quantum speedup over the classical lower bound of Ω(n1/2)
[GR97]. They also proved a quantum lower bound of Ω(n1/4) for expansion testing, ruling out expo-
nential speedup in this case. More recently, [BDCG+20] achieved a breakthrough by demonstrating
exponential quantum speedups for certain graph property testing problems in the adjacency list
model in bounded-degree graphs, showcasing the power of quantum algorithms in this domain. On
the other hand, Apers, Magniez, Sen and Szab´o [AMSS25] showed that testing 3-colorability in the
same model requires a linear number of quantum queries, indicating that not all graph properties
admit significant quantum speedups.
Furthermore, [AMSS25] initiated the study of quantum property testing for bounded-out-degree
directed graphs in the unidirectional model, focusing on the k-star-freeness property (and, more
generally, k-source-subgraph-freeness); see Definition 2.4 and the discussion below. They estab-

In this paper, we focus on quantum testers for bounded-degree directed graphs (digraphs), where
both in- and out-degree are bounded. In the classical setting, two natural models for accessing such
graphs were introduced by Bender and Ron [BR02]: the bidirectional model, which allows querying
both outgoing and incoming edges of a vertex, and the more restrictive unidirectional model, which
permits querying only the outgoing edges (see, e.g., [BR02, Gol10, HS12]).
The bidirectional
model closely resembles the standard adjacency list model for undirected graphs and is at least
as powerful as the unidirectional model. However, the unidirectional model is often more natural
in real-world applications – such as web crawling, social networks, or recommendation systems –
where querying incoming neighbors may not be feasible. Algorithmically, the unidirectional model
is also more challenging. For instance, strong connectivity can be tested using eO(1/ε) queries in
the bidirectional model, but requires Ω(√n) queries for two-sided error testing2 [BR02] and Ω(n)
queries for one-sided error testing [Gol10, HS12] in the unidirectional model.
Czumaj, Peng and Sohler [CPS16] advanced the study of property testing in bounded-degree
digraphs by providing a generic transformation between the bidirectional and unidirectional models.
They showed that any property Π that can be tested with a constant number of queries (i.e.,
Oε,d(1))3 in the bidirectional model can also be tested in the unidirectional model using a sublinear
number of queries, specifically n1−Ωε,d(1). This result was later shown to be essentially tight by
Peng and Wang [PW23], who constructed a property P that can be tested with Oε,d(1) queries in
the bidirectional model, but requires at least n1−f(ε,d) queries in the unidirectional model, where
f(ε, d) →0 as ε →0.
In this work, we investigate whether a similar transformation exists in the setting of quantum
property testing, and if so, whether the resulting quantum testers in the unidirectional model can
achieve an almost quadratic speedup over their classical counterparts. This question also remained
open by [AMSS25].
1.1
Our contributions
The upper bound Our main contribution is a generic transformation that converts testers with
constant query complexity in the classical bidirectional model into testers with o(n1/2) query com-
plexity in the quantum unidirectional model, where only queries to outgoing edges are allowed. A
digraph is d-bounded-degree if both its maximum in-degree and out-degree are at most a constant
d > 0. Formally, we establish the following result.
Theorem 1.1. If a graph property is ε-testable with two-sided error and classical query complexity
Oε,d(1) in the d-bounded-degree digraph bidirectional model, then it is also ε-testable with two-sided
error and quantum query complexity n1/2−Ωε,d(1) in the d-bounded-degree digraph unidirectional
model.
Thus, we answer affirmatively the open question proposed in [AMSS25], showing that our trans-
formation yields an almost quadratic quantum speedup over the best known classical algorithms
in the unidirectional model [CPS16]. We remark that many natural graph properties can be tested

Technical overview
1.2.1
The upper bound
The CPS approach Our transformation is built upon the framework of Czumaj, Peng and
Sohler [CPS16], referred to as the CPS approach, which shows that if a graph property Π can be
tested with two-sided error and classical query complexity q = q(ε, d) (independent of the graph
size) in the d-bounded-degree digraph bidirectional model, then testing Π reduces to approximating
the frequency vector F(G) = (cntΓ(G))Γ∈Dd,q, where
• Dd,q denotes the set of all possible isomorphism types Γ of d-bounded q-discs (that is, rooted
subgraphs induced by all vertices within distance at most q from a root vertex; Definition 2.2),
• and cntΓ(G) denotes the number of occurrences of any q-disc of isomorphism type Γ.
Then the vector F(G) records the number of occurrences of each such type in G. (Since G has
maximum degree at most d, the number of such isomorphism types is constant, and hence F(G) is
a constant-dimensional vector.) In particular, it suffices to approximate each cntΓ(G) to within an
additive error of δ(ε) · n for all Γ ∈D∗
d,q, where n is the number of vertices in G and
• D∗
d,q is Dd,q excluding the empty type (the type with a root vertex merely and no edges).
To estimate cntΓ(G) in the classical unidirectional model, the CPS approach samples n1−Ωq,d(1)
edges and counts the number of sampled subgraphs isomorphic to Γ (referred to as Γ-instances).
However, the fraction of sampled Γ-instances cannot be used simply as an estimator for cntΓ(G),
since this may lead to false positives. Specifically, a q-disc rooted at a vertex v in the sampled graph
might appear to be of type Γ when, in fact, its full neighborhood in the original graph corresponds
to a larger type Γ′ for some Γ′ ⪰Γ. The binary relation ⪰is defined over Dd,q such that Γ′ ⪰Γ if
and only if Γ′ contains Γ as a subgraph. This relation induces a partial order over all types in Dd,q.
To obtain an unbiased estimator for cntΓ(G), we must correct for this overcounting.
This
requires accounting for the contribution of all types Γ′ such that Γ′ ⪰Γ, i.e. those that can induce
a false appearance of Γ in the sampled view.
In the CPS approach, this correction is handled systematically by exploiting the fact that for any
pair of types Γ and Γ′, the conditional probability Pr[Γ | Γ′] – the probability that Γ appears in the
sampled graph given that the true q-disc is of type Γ′ – depends only on Γ and Γ′. Furthermore,
since the CPS algorithm is non-adaptive, each such probability is needed only once in the final
estimator for cntΓ(G). As a result, the contributions from all relevant Γ′ types that can cause false
positives can be corrected in a single final step of the estimation process.

Our approach We now describe our approach for proving the transformation result stated in
Theorem 1.1. As outlined above following the CPS framework, it suffices to approximate cntΓ(G)
with small additive error in the quantum unidirectional query model for each q-disc type Γ ∈D∗
d,q.
To this end, we give an adaptive algorithm that iteratively applies quantum counting and Grover
search, rather than relying on non-adaptive sampling and a one-shot correction of false positives.
It yields an almost quadratic speedup over the CPS algorithm.
The high-level idea Our algorithm iteratively collects rooted subgraphs with i edges, for i = 1
to md,q, where md,q is the maximum number of edges among the isomorphism types in Dd,q. In
each iteration, it samples edges to extend previously collected subgraphs. Specifically, it begins by
sampling a set of edges uniformly at random. Suppose that by the end of the i-th iteration, we
have a set Hi of subgraphs with i edges. At the (i + 1)-th iteration, we sample edges incident to
subgraphs in Hi to grow them into a set Hi+1 of subgraphs with i + 1 edges.
The key challenge is to balance query efficiency with accuracy. On one hand, we aim to minimize
the number of sampled edges to reduce query complexity. On the other hand, for each isomorphism
type Γ, we must ensure that if Γ appears frequently enough in the graph, then our sample includes a
sufficient number of its occurrences. This is essential for obtaining reliable estimates of cntΓ, using
relationships between different types to filter out false positives. This approach is inspired by the
adaptive algorithm for testing k-star-freeness in [AMSS25], which in turn builds on techniques from
[LZ19]. However, their algorithm only detects the presence of k-stars when they are sufficiently
abundant, and does not even attempt to estimate their count. Note that k-stars are special cases of
1-disc types, while we aim to count general q-disc types (for any constant q) – an inherently more
challenging task. As previously mentioned, our approach extends to estimating the frequency of
any constant-size subgraph.

The lower bound
We now outline the high-level idea behind our quantum lower bound for testing k-star-freeness,
i.e., the proof of Theorem 1.3.
We begin by reviewing the approach of [AMSS25], which builds on the framework of [BKT20] to
establish a lower bound for testing k-star-freeness in bounded-out-degree digraphs. Their idea is to
reduce the problem of testing k-subgraph-freeness in (N + R)-vertex bounded-out-degree digraphs
to the problem of testing k-collision-freeness, defined as follows: for some R = Θ(N), given query
access to a function f : [N] →[R], determine whether f is k-collision-free – that is, no value in
[R] is mapped by at least k distinct elements of [N] – or whether f is ε-far from satisfying this
property.
They show that this testing problem corresponds to the Boolean function Collisionk = GapOR ◦
THRk, where GapOR and THRk serve as key building blocks for applying the dual polynomial
10

method and the block-composition framework.
In particular, GapOR admits a dual witness ϕ
of the so-called pure high degree (see below) Ω(1), and THRk admits a dual witness ψ of pure
high degree Ω(N1/2−1/(2k)). Their dual block composition ϕ ⋆ψ then yields a dual witness for
Collisionk of pure high degree eΩ(N1/2−1/(2k)) through the zeroing-out trick of [BKT20], which in
turn implies the quantum lower bound of eΩ(n1/2−1/(2k)) queries for testing k-subgraph-freeness,
where n = N + R = Θ(N).
Our extension and new setting We extend the setting of [AMSS25], where only the maximum
out-degree is bounded, to the stricter regime in which both the maximum in-degree and out-degree
are bounded. This generalization necessitates several new structural insights and a refined reduction
framework.
In our approach, we study OCCUk, which corresponds to testing k-occurrence-freeness – that
is, for some R = Θ(N), given query access to a function f : [N] →[R] with the promise that no
value in [R] is mapped by more than k distinct elements of [N], the task is to decide whether f is
k-collision-free or ε-far from satisfying this property. We interpret OCCUk as a bounded variant of
Collisionk, where no value in [R] has more than k pre-images, and accordingly replace THRk with
BTHRk, which restricts the Hamming weight of the input to at most k.

dual witness. We provide a more detailed explanation below.
Reduction to k-occurrence freeness with double promises We reduce the problem of testing
k-star freeness in (N + R)-vertex bounded-degree digraphs to the problem of testing k-occurrence-

Other related work
The collision finding problem is a fundamental question in algorithm and cryptography theory.
There are several variants of this problem. The most general version is as follows: given a vector
s of length N, where each element takes values in the range [R], the goal is to find a tuple of
indices (i1, . . . , ik) such that si1 = · · · = sik, or assert that no such tuple exists. Without any addi-
tional promise on the input s, this problem is also known as the k-Element Distinctness problem.
Classically, even for the case k = 2, the problem requires Ω(N) queries. In the quantum setting,
although studied for over two decades, tight bounds remain elusive. The current best-known results
are an upper bound of O(N3/4−1/4(2k−1)) due to Belovs [Bel12], and a lower bound of Ω(N3/4−1/4k)
established by Mande, Thaler and Zhu [MTZ20]. Another version of the problem considers the
12


## Page 15


input is uniformly at random on a suitable alphabet size, making Θ(N) k-duplicates occur with
high probability. The classical query complexity in this case is Θ(N1−1/k) [HS12, PW23], while
the quantum query complexity is Θ(N1/2−1/2(2k−1)) [LZ19]. This version is more closely related
to our property testing problem, as the promise guarantees the existence of Ω(N) such tuples,
implying that the input is ε-far from being collision-free. Very recently, [AMSS25] studied an eas-
ier problem—testing k-collision freeness. The goal is to distinguish the inputs that have Θ(N)
k-collisions from those do not have any, instead of finding a specific one. They proved the same
upper bound O(N1/2−1/2(2k−1)) but a weaker lower bound ˜Ω(N1/2−1/2k).
Quantum counting, introduced by Brassard, Høyer, Mosca and Tapp [BHMT02], is a useful
technique for estimating the number of marked items defined by a Boolean function. Formally,
given a Boolean function F : [N] →{0, 1}, quantum counting can estimate M, the size of F −1(1),

Grover search, introduced by Grover [Gro96], is one of the most widely used quantum algo-

Czumaj, Fichtenberger, Peng, and Sohler [CFPS20] studied property testing of general graphs

at each step, if an explored vertex has degree exceeding q, only q randomly chosen neighbors are
used for the exploration. This characterization naturally extends to the bidirectional model for
digraphs as well.

the q-bounded q-discs (see Section 1.3). However, adapting these testers to the unidirectional
model, in both classical and quantum settings, appears to require fundamentally new ideas.
In particular, q-bounded q-discs are random subgraphs that may overlap significantly at high-
degree vertices, and it remains unclear how to efficiently estimate their frequencies or exploit
them for property testing in the unidirectional setting.
• The role of adaptivity: As discussed earlier, our framework crucially relies on adaptive
strategies, which distinguishes it from the CPS approach. For example, in the warm-up prob-

2.4
Testing k-star-freeness and k-occurrence-freeness
We study the problem of testing k-star-freeness to establish our lower bound, and give its formal
definition below.
Definition 2.4. (k-star-freeness) For a d-bounded-degree digraph G,
• G is said to be k-star-free if k-star does not appear as a subgraph in G.
• G is said to be ε-far from k-star-freeness, if one needs to modify at least εdn edges to make
it k-star-free.
The k-star-freeness testing problem is defined as follows: given parameters k ≥2, ε > 0, and
d, together with query access to a d-bounded-degree digraph G (in the unidirectional model), the
goal is to distinguish whether G is k-star-free or ε-far from being k-star-free. This problem can be
viewed as a special case of testing k-source-subgraph-freeness, where the k-star is replaced by an
arbitrary constant-size digraph H with at most k source components.
We will relate the problem of testing k-star-freeness in k-bounded-degree digraphs to that of
testing k-occurrence-freeness in functions. We formally define the latter below.
Definition 2.5. (k-occurrence-freeness) Let R = cN for some constant c, and let f : [N] →[R] be
a function promised to be k-bounded for k ≥2. We define:
• f is k-occurrence-free if |f−1(r)| < k for every r ∈[R].
• f is ε-far from k-occurrence-freeness at least εN function values must be modified to make it
k-occurrence-free; equivalently, there exist at least εN distinct r ∈[R] such that |f−1(r)| = k.
Note that the k-occurrence-freeness testing problem is a variant of the k-collision-freeness test-
ing, introduced in [AMSS25], obtained by restricting the function to be k-bounded.

D
A Simple Ω(n1/3) Lower Bound
The previously best-known lower bound on the query complexity for testing a property that is
constant-query testable in the bidirectional model, within the bounded-degree unidirectional model,
can be established via the quantum hardness of testing 2-star-freeness in the unidirectional model,
which in turn is derived from the support-size estimation problem studied in [LW18].
Specifically, [LW18] constructs two distributions that are indistinguishable with o(n1/3) quan-
tum queries. From these distributions, one can construct digraphs with bounded maximum in-
and out-degree such that the graph derived from one distribution contains no 2-stars, whereas the
graph derived from the other distribution contains Θ(εn) many 2-stars. Hence, any tester for 2-star
freeness in the quantum unidirectional model on bounded-degree digraphs can distinguish these two
distributions, which implies an Ω(n1/3) lower bound.
Theorem D.1. Given an n-vertex 2-bounded-degree digraph G, 2-star freeness testing requires at
least Ωd,ε(n1/3) quantum queries in the unidirectional model.
