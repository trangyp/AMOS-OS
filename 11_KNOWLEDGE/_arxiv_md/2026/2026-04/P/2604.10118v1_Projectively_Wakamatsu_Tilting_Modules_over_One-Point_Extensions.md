Introduction
Tilting theory plays a central role in the representation theory of Artin algebras. The notion
of a tilting module has been extensively generalized and developed in various directions. One
of the most prominent generalizations is the τ-tilting theory introduced by Adachi, Iyama, and
Reiten [AIR14], which completes the classical tilting mutation and connects closely to cluster
algebras. Another important generalization is given by Wakamatsu tilting modules, introduced
by Wakamatsu [Wa90], which provide a powerful framework in the study of derived equivalences
and Gorenstein homological algebra.
Recently, Enomoto [Eno23] introduced a subclass of Wakamatsu tilting modules called pro-
jectively Wakamatsu tilting (PWT) modules. A self-orthogonal module T is projectively Waka-
matsu tilting if it is exactly an Ext-progenerator for its right perpendicular category T ⊥>0.
Enomoto proved that for representation-finite algebras, PWT modules coincide with Waka-
matsu tilting modules as well as with maximal self-orthogonal modules. This result provides a
new perspective on the structure of self-orthogonal modules and motivates further investigation
of PWT modules.

One-point extensions of algebras form a classical and powerful inductive tool for constructing
new algebras from given ones. The behavior of tilting modules under one-point extensions was
extensively studied by Assem, Happel, and Trepode [AHT07]. More recently, Suarez [Sua18]
generalized this approach to support τ-tilting modules using restriction and extension functors.
Motivated by these developments, this paper aims to study the construction of PWT modules
over one-point extensions.
Let Λ be an algebra and M ∈mod Λ. Let Γ = Λ[M] be the one-point extension algebra of Λ
by M. The module categories mod Λ and mod Γ are connected by a pair of adjoint functors: the
exact restriction functor R and the fully faithful extension functor E. Let Pa and Sa denote the
new indecomposable projective and simple Γ-modules, respectively, associated with the extension
vertex a.
Our first main result provides a method to lift a PWT module from Λ to Γ.
Theorem A. (See Theorem 3.2)
Let Γ = Λ[M] be the one-point extension algebra of Λ by
M and U be a PWT module in mod Λ. If M ∈U ⊥Λ, then eU = EU ⊕Pa is a PWT module in
mod Γ.
We further show that mutation relations between PWT modules are preserved under suitable
homological conditions.
Theorem B. (See Theorem 3.5) Let Γ = Λ[M] be the one-point extension of Λ by M ∈mod Λ.
Suppose U is a PWT module over Λ and U ′ is a left mutation of U. If M ∈U ⊥Λ ∩(U ′)⊥Λ, then
eU ′ = EU ′ ⊕Pa is a left mutation of eU = EU ⊕Pa in mod Γ.
Finally, for source point extensions of representation-finite algebras, we obtain a complete
classification of PWT modules.
Theorem C. (See Theorem 3.9) Let Γ = Λ[Si] be a source point extension of a representation-
finite Artin algebra Λ. Then there exists a bijection:
PWT(Γ) ←→PWT(Λ)

Remark 2.2. The definition of PWT modules is deeply motivated by a foundational theorem
of Auslander and Reiten [AR91, Theorem 5.5]. They established that if T is a classical tilt-

PWT module T, the equality YT = T ⊥Λ holds. Since injective modules lie in T ⊥Λ, the injective

module (see [BS98, Proposition 2.2] or [Eno23, Proposition 2.8]). So,
{Tilting} ⊆{Projectively Wakamatsu tilting} ⊆{Wakamatsu tilting}.
