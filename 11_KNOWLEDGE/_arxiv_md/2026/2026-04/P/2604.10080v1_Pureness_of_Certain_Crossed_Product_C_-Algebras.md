PURENESS OF CERTAIN CROSSED PRODUCT C*-ALGEBRAS
DAWN ARCHEY, JULIAN BUCK, JAVAD MOHAMMADKARIMI, N. CHRISTOPHER
PHILLIPS, AND APURVA SETH
Abstract. We establish comparison and divisibility properties for crossed
product C*-algebras arising from automorphisms of algebras C(X, D) which
lie over minimal homeomorphisms, from actions of compact groups which have
finite Rokhlin dimension with commuting towers, and from actions of compact
groups which have the restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison.
We deduce that these crossed products we consider are pure, and conclude
they have stable rank one, and in certain cases have real rank zero. We give
examples in which these properties do not follow from previous results, in the
case of C(X, D) due to the lack of Z-stability of D, the underlying topological
spaces not being finite dimensional, or both.
1. Introduction
Significant progress has been made on the classification of crossed product C*-
algebras arising from minimal dynamical systems. The unpublished long preprint
[26] of Q. Lin and N. C. Phillips (see also the survey articles [24] and [25]) provides
a description of the transformation group C*-algebras arising from minimal dif-
feomorphisms of finite dimensional smooth compact manifolds in terms of a direct
limit decomposition. In [23] and [40], it is shown that crossed products arising from
minimal homeomorphisms of infinite compact metrizable spaces with finite cover-
ing dimension are classified by their ordered K-theory in the presence of sufficiently
many projections (for instance, when projections separate traces). In [40] it is fur-
ther proved that crossed products by such minimal homeomorphisms have finite
nuclear dimension, and hence absorb the Jiang-Su algebra Z tensorially (that is,
are Z-stable). Finally, G. A. Elliott and Z. Niu ([10]) have shown that crossed prod-
ucts by minimal homeomorphisms of compact metric spaces with mean dimension
zero (including all minimal homeomorphisms of finite dimensional compact metric
spaces) are Z-stable, from which it follows that they are classifiable in the sense of
the Elliott program by Corollary D of Theorem A of [11].
There is also substantial work on crossed products C∗(Z, A, α) when A is simple.

Much less is known when the C*-algebra A is neither commutative nor simple.
The easiest more general case to consider is presumably A = C(X, D), in which X
is a compact metric space and D is a simple unital C*-algebra. In this case, every
automorphism of A “lies over” a homeomorphism h of X. (See [6].) We want the
crossed product to be simple, and this happens if and only if h is minimal. Hua ([20])
has shown that such crossed products have tracial rank zero when moreover X is the
Cantor set, D has tracial rank zero, and some additional K-theoretic assumptions
are made. This case was more systematically studied in [6], in which several results
were given showing that if D has good properties (for example, has stable rank
one, has strict comparison of positive elements, or is tracially Z-stable), and other
conditions are satisfied, then C∗(Z, C(X, D), h) has good properties. In particular,
by [6, Theorem 4.5], if D is tracially Z-stable, then so is C∗(Z, C(X, D), h), even
if mdim(h) > 0.
If D is nuclear, it then follows from [21, Theorem 4.1] that
C∗(Z, C(X, D), h) is Z-stable. Therefore C∗(Z, C(X, D), h) is classifiable in the
sense of the Elliott program.
However, there are many interesting examples of
crossed products of this sort in which D is not nuclear and not even tracially Z-
stable. Many examples are given in [6, Section 6]. Many of the results there are
limited to the case dim(X) ≤1 or even dim(X) = 0, and are therefore somewhat
artificial. The reason is lack of sufficient information about algebras of the form
C(Y, D), or recursive subhomogenous algebras over D, and their direct limits, when
the dimensions of the spaces are greater than 1.
This is where pure C*-algebras come into play. Following Winter [43, Defini-
tion 3.6], a C*-algebra is said to be pure if it has strict comparison and is almost
divisible. More generally, weaker notions of comparison and divisibility, namely m-
comparison and n-almost divisibility, suffice: if A has m-comparison and is n-almost
divisible for some m, n ∈N, then A is pure by [4, Theorem 5.7]. See Section 2 for
the relevant definitions and further details. We warn that m-comparison as used
here is a much weaker condition than the property with the same name used to
define the radius of comparison, as for example in [39, Definition 6.1]. However,
0-comparison is the same for both definitions.
Pureness is closely connected to Z-stability. By [37], Z-stability implies pure-
ness, and for separable C*-algebras, the two notions coincide at the level of central
sequence algebras. (See [31].) In the nonnuclear setting, Z-stability is often un-
available, whereas pureness remains accessible and continues to play a useful role in
establishing strict comparison. It is therefore natural to view pureness as one plau-
sible extension of Z-stability to the case of nonnuclear simple C*-algebras. Two
other possible extensions are Z-stability itself and (in the unital case) tracial Z-
stability as in [21, Definition 2.1]. Certainly Z-stability implies tracial Z-stability.
It seems likely that tracial Z-stability implies pureness, although, as far as we know,
nobody has actually proved this. For simple separable nuclear unital C*-algebras,
tracial Z-stability implies Z-stability ([21, Theorem 4.1]). The Elliott classifica-
tion conjecture would imply that pureness implies Z-stability. Without nuclearity,
both reverse implications are false. It is shown in [30, Theorem 2.2] that tracial
Z-stability, even tracial rank zero, does not imply Z-stability. The reduced C*-
algebras of nonabelian free groups are pure but certainly not Z-stable. (See [2,
Theorem A] and [4, Proposition 5.13].)
Coming back to algebras of the form C(Y, D), it is well known that C(Y, D) need
not have strict comparison, even when D does. Positive results are available under

Related methods also give results for two other kinds of crossed products. The
first arises from actions of compact groups with finite Rokhlin dimension with
commuting towers, and the second from actions with the restricted tracial Rokhlin
property with comparison. Actions of the first type were introduced and studied
by Gardella ([13], [14]), generalizing the notion introduced by Hirshberg, Winter,
and Zacharias for finite group actions.
Crossed products by such actions enjoy
strong permanence properties: several important classes of C*-algebras, including
D-absorbing C*-algebras (where D is strongly self-absorbing), stable C*-algebras,
C*-algebras with finite nuclear dimension (or decomposition rank), and C*-algebras
with finite stable rank (or real rank), are preserved. (See [15, Theorem 4.17].) The
second class was introduced in [28] by the third and fourth named authors, where
similar preservation results are obtained. It is proved there that fixed point algebras
under such actions, and, in appropriate cases, the corresponding crossed products,
preserve properties such as simplicity, Property (SP), tracial rank zero, tracial rank
at most one, the Popa property, tracial Z-stability, Z-stability in the nuclear case,
infiniteness, and pure infiniteness.
In [29] the authors construct three types of
examples that have the restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison but do
not have finite Rokhlin dimension with commuting towers.
We show that pureness is likewise preserved under crossed products in these
settings. Let A be a simple separable stably finite unital C*-algebra, let G be a
second countable compact group, and let α: G →Aut(A) be an action of G on A.
Suppose that α has the restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison, in
the sense of [28, Definition 2.1], or that G is a Lie group and α has finite Rokhlin
dimension with commuting towers, in the sense of [14, Definition 3.2]. We prove
that if A is pure, then so is C∗(G, A, α).
The main step for pureness of C∗(Z, C(X, D), h) is showing that if B is a large
subalgebra of A, in the sense of [34, Definition 4.1], and if B is pure, then A is
pure. The reverse implication is also true. The proof depends on the fact that the
nonprojection parts of the Cuntz semigroups Cu(B) and Cu(A) are isomorphic.
Assuming instead the restricted tracial Rokhlin property with comparison, or finite
Rokhlin dimension with commuting towers, we compare the fixed point algebra Aα
with A. The isomorphism statement above is no longer true, but enough of it is
that the same underlying ideas can be made to work. In all three cases, under
suitable additional assumptions, we can say something about m-comparison and
n-almost divisibility separately, at the cost of allowing m and n to increase. Thus,

Definition 2.3 (Before Proposition 2.3 of [4]). For an ordered semigroup S and

Definition 2.5. Let A be a C*-algebra. We say A has n-comparison if Cu(A) has

Strict comparison as in Definition 2.5 agrees with the usual notion of strict
comparison of positive elements by quasitraces for a C*-algebra A, and this property

We caution that n-comparison in the sense of Definition 2.5 is different from
r-comparison (for r ∈[0, ∞)) as used in the definition of the radius of comparison,
in the sense of Definition 6.1 of [39].
Definition 2.6. Let S be an ordered semigroup, and let ξ, ξ′ ∈S. We write ξ′ ≪ξ
if for every increasing sequence (ξn) in S satisfying ξ ≤supn ξn, there is N ∈Z>0
such that ξ′ ≤ξN. We call ≪the way below relation (or compact containment

For this definition to be useful, we need to know that suprema of increasing
sequences in the ordered semigroup S actually exist. For the main semigroup of
interest here, this is in fact the case.
Definition 2.7. An ordered semigroup S is said to be a Cu-semigroup if the
following properties hold:
(1) If (ξn) is an increasing sequence in S, then supn ξn exists.
(2) For any ξ ∈S there exists a sequence (ξn) such that ξn ≪ξn+1 for all n
and ξ = supn ξn.

(4) If (ξn) and (ηn) are increasing sequences in S, then supn(ξn + ηn) =
