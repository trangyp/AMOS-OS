Abstract. Let F be a non-archimedean local field of residue characteristic p and G be one of the
groups GL2(F), SL2(F) or PGL2(F). Let HG denote the pro-p Iwahori-Hecke algebra of G over Fp.
We study the homotopy category Ho(HG) of Hovey’s Gorenstein projective model structure on the
category of HG-modules and relate it to the singularity category Sing(Xq,G) of an explicit scheme.
When G = GL2(F), this scheme was first introduced by Dotto-Emerton-Gee [8].
We obtain in that
case an equivalence Ho(HGL2) ≃Sing(Xq,GL2) and recover from this Große-Kl¨onne’s mod-p Langlands
correspondence for Hecke modules [16], building on work of P´epin-Schmidt [37]. We furthermore describe
Ho(HG) completely explicitly when G = SL2(F) or PGL2(F), and make additional computations in the
GL2 case.

1. Introduction
Let p be a prime and F be a non-archimedean local field with residue field Fq of characteristic p. Let
G be a split reductive group over the field F and let G = G(F). Let I be a pro-p Iwahori subgroup of
G and let HG = Fp[I/G/I] be the corresponding pro-p Iwahori-Hecke algebra of G, i.e. the convolution
algebra of I-biinvariant compactly supported functions on G. Write Mod(HG) for the category of left
HG-modules.
The Hecke algebra HG plays an important role in the mod-p Langlands program. For instance, by
work of Große-Kl¨onne [16] it is known when G = GLn and F is an extension of Qp that there is a
functorial bijection M ↦V (M) between the isomorphism classes of n-dimensional simple supersingular
HG-modules and the isomorphism classes of irreducible, continuous representations Gal(F/F) →GLn(Fp).
Furthermore, when G = GL2 there is a scheme with connected components given by chains of projective
lines, introduced by Dotto-Emerton-Gee [8] and P´epin-Schmidt [37], whose Fp-rational points are in
canonical bijection with the isomorphism classes of semisimple 2-dimensional Galois representations.
P´epin-Schmidt show that this scheme is a certain quotient of the centre Z(HGL2) of the Hecke algebra
and use this relationship to reconstruct geometrically Große-Kl¨onne’s bijection in that case. A more
general link between Z(HGLn) and Galois representations was also obtained in the recent preprint [38].
The algebra HG is in general known to be a Gorenstein ring [31] and it follows from work of Hovey
[17] that Mod(HG) has a so-called Gorenstein projective model structure. In earlier work [10] we in-
vestigated the associated homotopy category Ho(HG), which is the localisation of Mod(HG) obtained
after trivialising all modules of finite projective dimension. In particular, we showed that distinct simple
supersingular HG-modules remain generically non-isomorphic in Ho(HG). The goal of the present work
is to describe the category Ho(HG) in more details when G has semisimple rank one, and to relate it to
Galois representations via the aforementioned constructions.
From now on, we assume that G = GL2, SL2 or PGL2. The first part of our paper aims to explicitly
describe the category Ho(HG), which we recall is by construction triangulated. Our first result achieves
this when G is semisimple.

We also note that Ardakov-Schneider constructed a scheme ˜Xq,SL2 in [2], given by a disjoint union

At the end of our paper, we do some additional computations in the case G = GL2. Namely, we show
that Ho(HGL2) is equivalent to the derived category of an explicit DGA by singling out a generator, cf.

module. Finally, we show that the endomorphism rings of the simple supersingular modules in Ho(HGL2)

Using these notions, Hovey constructed the so-called Gorenstein projective and Goreinstein injective
model structures on Mod(R), cf. [17, Theorem 8.6]. We do not recall here the formalism of (abelian)
model categories, we refer the reader instead to our previous works [9, §1] and [10, §2.4] as well as the
references therein. In this paper we will exclusively work with the aforementioned model structures on
Mod(R), which are defined as follows. In the Gorenstein projective model structure, the fibrations are the
epimorphisms and the cofibrations are the monomorphisms with Gorenstein projective cokernel. Dually,
the Gorenstein injective model structure has fibrations the epimorphisms with Gorenstein injective kernel
and cofibrations the monomorphisms. In both cases, a fibration, resp. cofibration, is a weak equivalence
if and only if its kernel, resp. cokernel, is trivial. Furthermore, the factorisation axioms and [17, Lemma
5.8] imply that the weak equivalences in both of these model structures are the same, namely they are
given by the smallest class of morphisms closed under composition and containing all epimorphisms with
trivial kernels and all monomorphisms with trivial cokernel. Consequently, the two model structures have
the same homotopy category, which we will denote by Ho(R) (see also [10, Remark 2.12]).

preserves both Gorenstein projectives and modules of finite projective dimension. It follows that LX

we therefore have that LX is exact and preserves trivial objects. Furthermore, we also have that RX is
exact and that it preserves trivial objects since those are trivially fibrant and RX is right Quillen. By
applying [10, Lemma 2.8], we now immediately get that LX and RX preserve all weak equivalences and
that the derived adjunction is of the claimed form.

4. The singularity category of semisimple Galois representations
In this section, we give a Galois theoretic interpretation of the category Ho(H). More precisely, we
relate the homotopy categories Ho(H) in semisimple rank one to the singularity categories of certain
schemes parametrising the Galois representations occuring on the other side of the mod-p Langlands
program.
4.1. Singularity categories. We first recall some elementary notions related to singularity categories.
Following Krause [26], our definition of singularity categories is as follows.
Definition 4.1. Let X be a separated Noetherian scheme over k. The singularity category of X is defined
to be Sing(X) ∶= Kac(Inj(X)), the category of acyclic complexes of injective quasi-coherent sheaves on
X up to chain homotopy.
When X = Spec(S) is affine we will often also write Sing(S) for Sing(X).
Remark 4.2.
(i) The singularity category of a Gorenstein ring S was first investigated by Buchweitz [7], who
defined it to be the Verdier quotient Db(Coh(S))/Perf(S) of the bounded derived category of
finitely generated S-modules by the thick subcategory of perfect complexes.
This definition
was taken up and later studied systematically for a large family of schemes by Orlov [34]. In
general, one has that Sing(X) as we defined it is compactly generated and its compact objects
are equivalent to the idempotent completion of Db(Coh(X))/Perf(X) in a natural way, cf. [26,
Theorem 1.1]. In particular, one deduces that Sing(X) = 0 if and only if X is regular.
(ii) Singularity categories also feature in the geometric Langlands program, see for example [3, Ap-
pendix H]. In that context, these categories are defined as the quotient IndCoh(X)/QCoh(X)
of the ind-completion of the (derived infinity) category of coherent sheaves on X by the quasi-
coherent sheaves, and X itself is allowed to be a more general stack. The quotient category
IndCoh(X)/QCoh(X) is known to be compactly generated by Coh(X)/Perf(X) in relatively
high generality, cf. Remark H.1.5 of loc. cit., and hence agrees with our definition for sepa-
rated Noetherian schemes by the above. Finally, we note that there is a 6-functor formalism
on IndCoh(X), see [39, §8.7], and we expect that the pushforward/pullback functors between
singularity categories which we use later in this section can be recovered from that far more
general construction. In practice however, we will only work with affine schemes or chains of
projective lines in this paper, so that Definition 4.1 and the constructions we introduce later will
suffice for our purposes.
Let S be a commutative Noetherian ring. We recall that Becker showed that Sing(S) can be con-
structed as the homotopy category of the so-called singular injective model structure on on the category
Ch(S) of unbounded chain complexes of S-modules, cf. [6, Proposition 2.2.1]. When S is Gorenstein,
the functor ι0 ∶Mod(S) →Ch(S) sending an S-module to the corresponding chain complex concentrated
in degree 0 is a left Quillen equivalence for the Gorenstein injective model structure on Mod(S) and
the singular injective model structure on Ch(S), cf. the dual of Proposition 3.1.3 in loc. cit. There is
also a projective analogue of this singular model structure with Kac(Proj(S)) as its homotopy category,
and by loc. cit. the functor ι0 is then a right Quillen equivalence for the Gorenstein projective model
structure. Recalling that the Gorenstein projective and injective model structures have naturally the
same homotopy categories (cf. [10, Remark 2.12]), we therefore obtain the following.
Proposition 4.3. Let S be a commutative Gorenstein ring. Then Ho(S) is canonically equivalent to
Sing(S).
