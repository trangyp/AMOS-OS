POLISH SPACES FOR COUNTABLE AND SEPARABLE STRUCTURES
THROUGH QUOTIENT ENCODINGS
TOMASZ KANIA
Abstract. We develop a unified framework for locating natural properties of algebraic
and analytic structures within the Borel hierarchy. Objects are presented as quotients of
a universal generator and definability is read directly from the quotient data.
For separable Banach-type structures (Banach algebras, C∗-algebras, Banach lattices,
TROs) the kernel space is Polish under the Wijsman topology, and the quotient-norm
functional K 7→∥x+K∥is continuous, yielding a uniform definability scheme whose Borel
ranks are bounded by quantifier alternation depth. For countable algebraic structures
(groups, rings, lattices) we work on compact Polish spaces of congruences where atomic
predicates are clopen.
We obtain explicit Borel upper bounds: in the unital C∗-algebra coding based on
C∗
max(F∞), stable finiteness is closed, nuclearity is Borel, simplicity is Gδ, AF-ness lies
in Π0
3, nuclear dimension ⩽n lies in Π0
3, and for fixed exact D, D-absorption is analytic.
For countable groups, soficity is Gδ; for abelian groups, slenderness is Π0
3. We give an
internal Borel coding of the K0-assignment in the quotient/Wijsman framework; for each
fixed coordinate the corresponding section is Fσ, and suspension together with Bott pe-

are optimal (Σ0

1. Introduction
A central goal in modern mathematics is the classification of abstract structures up to
isomorphism. Descriptive set theory provides a powerful framework for quantifying the
complexity of such classification problems, primarily through the lens of Borel reducibil-
ity. This theory has been successfully applied to operator algebras. For example, Sabok
proved that the isomorphism relation for separable C∗-algebras is complete among orbit
equivalence relations induced by continuous actions of Polish groups [43]. Earlier work
of Farah–Toms–Törnquist established major non-classifiability results by turbulence and
analysed standard Borel codings and invariants for separable C∗-algebras [18, 19].

Very recently, Pi, Szachniewicz, and Tartarotti showed that KK-equivalence for unital
C∗-algebras is analytic and deduced that the class of separable C∗-algebras satisfying the
UCT is analytic [40].
A parallel line of work on the Banach-space side was recently developed by Cúth, Doležal,
Doucha, and Kurka. In [9] they introduced natural Polish spaces of separable Banach
spaces (as spaces of norms and pseudonorms on a countable rational vector space), com-
pared them with the admissible topologies of Godefroy–Saint-Raymond, and analysed their
generic structure. In the companion paper [10] they computed the descriptive complexity
of many natural isometry and isomorphism classes in those Polish spaces. Our approach is
complementary: rather than parameterising bare Banach spaces by norms, we work with
quotient presentations adapted to Banach-type categories carrying algebraic or analytic
operations.
However, this focus on the comparative difficulty of isomorphism problems leaves a
different, equally fundamental question unanswered: what is the intrinsic logical complexity
of the properties that define these structures?
While knowing that classifying all C∗-
algebras is intractable is crucial, it does not tell us how difficult it is to determine whether
a single, given C∗-algebra is simple, finite-dimensional, or admits a tracial state. Many
such properties are known to be Borel measurable in standard codings, but their precise
location within the Borel hierarchy (Σ0
α, Π0
α) — a measure of their descriptive complexity —
has remained largely unexplored.
The present paper develops a unified framework to address precisely this.
We con-
struct explicit Polish parameter spaces for two broad regimes of mathematical structures
— countable algebraic systems and separable Banach-type structures — and use their topo-
logical properties to compute the Borel complexity of key algebraic and analytic proper-
ties. Our approach is grounded in a single methodological principle: parameterise a class
of structures as quotients of a single, universal “quotient generator”.
Why quotients rather than embeddings? A natural first attempt at a unified coding of sepa-
rable Banach algebras would be to fix a single separable Banach algebra U and parameterise
all separable Banach algebras as (isomorphic copies of) subalgebras of U. However, this is
impossible: there is no universal separable Banach algebra for isomorphic embeddings; in-
deed, no separable Banach algebra contains an isomorphic copy of every separable Banach
algebra [29]. This makes quotient encodings conceptually natural: in many Banach-type
categories there are explicit separable quotient generators (e.g. ℓ1(S) for Banach algebras),
and the corresponding kernel/ideal hyperspaces carry canonical Polish topologies. Our
parameter spaces are therefore built from admissible kernels and quotient norms.

The Wijsman topology is one instance of a broader class of hyperspace topologies intro-
duced by Godefroy and Saint-Raymond [26].
Definition 2.3 (Admissible topology [26]). A Polish topology τ on F(X) is admissible if:
(i) for each open U ⊆X, E+(U) = {F ∈F(X) : F ∩U ̸= ∅} is τ-open;
(ii) τ has a subbase consisting of countable unions of sets of the form E+(U) \ E+(V )
with U, V open in X;
(iii) the membership relation {(x, F) : x ∈F} ⊆X × F(X) is τ-closed.
Condition (i) says that the topology detects hits: if a closed set meets an open region,
nearby sets (in the τ-sense) also do.
Condition (ii) is a regularity constraint ensuring
τ is generated by set-theoretically definable data.
Condition (iii) is the key geometric
requirement: it says precisely that the singleton embedding x 7→{x} is a homeomorphism
from X onto a closed subset of (F(X), τ) — in other words, points are “robustly detected”
by the topology, and any sequence of closed sets converging in τ to a point mass {x} must
eventually carry elements near x.

3. Main theorem for separable continuous structures
This section develops the analytic half of our general framework: we construct Polish pa-

abstractly, but concretely as quotients of a single universal object endowed with finitely or
countably many continuous multilinear operations.

10. D-absorption and approximate intertwining
Fix a separable unital C∗-algebra D. We say that a separable unital C∗-algebra B is

10.1. Elliott intertwining. A central theme in the classification programme for C∗-
algebras is the passage from approximate morphisms to genuine isomorphisms. The funda-
mental device for this is the Elliott intertwining argument, which first appeared in Elliott’s
classification of AF-algebras [15] and has since become one of the most widely used tools
in the field. The argument says, roughly, that if two separable C∗-algebras B and C can
be connected by a back-and-forth sequence of maps that approximately compose to the
identity on ever-larger finite subsets, then B ∼= C genuinely.
This theorem matters for us for two reasons. First, the reduction from an isomorphism to
finite-level witnesses is the mechanism that makes D-absorption (B ∼= B⊗minD) expressible
as an analytic condition (Theorem 10.3). Without the intertwining theorem, one would
need to quantify over an entire isomorphism—an object living in a non-separable function
space—and the resulting condition would a priori be Σ1
1 rather than Borel. Second, it
provides the Σ1
1 upper bound for isomorphism of separable C∗-algebras (Remark 10.2): the
approximate intertwining witnesses form a countable Borel family, so the existence of a
complete intertwining is an analytic condition.
The proof of the intertwining theorem is entirely constructive (a standard diagonal ar-
gument), and is by now well-established; see Rørdam–Larsen–Laustsen [41, Theorem 2.3.3]
and Elliott [15]. We do not reproduce it here, but state it for reference and proceed directly
to its descriptive set-theoretic consequences.

12.1.1. Universality and embeddability. A fundamental asymmetry between the countable-
group and C∗-algebraic settings is the failure of universality for embeddings. There is no
universal countable group with respect to embeddings: no single countable group con-
tains an isomorphic copy of every countable group. Indeed, there are 2ℵ0 pairwise non-
isomorphic finitely generated groups (already among 2-generated quotients of the free group
F2), whereas any fixed countable group has only countably many finitely generated sub-
groups (since each is generated by a finite subset). Thus no countable group can contain
copies of all finitely generated groups, and a fortiori none can contain copies of all count-
able groups; see, e.g., [36, Chapter IV] for related standard facts. In the quotient coding
this means that the parameter space NSub(F∞) cannot be replaced by the ideal space
of a single countable group, in contrast with the C∗-algebraic setting where C∗
max(F∞) is
universal for separable C∗-algebras. Nevertheless, F∞is universal for presentations: every
countable group is a quotient of F∞, and the normal subgroup lattice NSub(F∞) serves as
the standard Borel space for the classification problem.
Gromov [25] initiated a systematic study of the space of finitely generated groups (quo-
tients of Fk for fixed k, equipped with the Chabauty topology), viewing group-theoretic
properties as subsets of this space and asking for their topological and measure-theoretic
complexity. The present catalogue may be seen as an extension of this viewpoint to all
countable groups (using F∞in place of Fk) within the framework of the Borel hierarchy.

open for arbitrary groups, such as Gottschalk’s surjunctivity conjecture and Kaplansky’s
direct finiteness conjecture for group rings. Gromov [25] asked whether every group is
sofic; this remains one of the most prominent open problems in group theory.
