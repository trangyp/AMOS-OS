PULLING BACK CARTIER STRUCTURES ALONG REGULAR MAPS
JAVIER CARVAJAL-ROJAS AND AXEL ST¨ABLER
Abstract. We introduce a framework for pulling back Cartier modules and their associated
invariants along regular F-finite morphisms. To achieve this, we construct a relative Cartier
isomorphism and operator for an arbitrary regular F-finite map of locally noetherian schemes.
As an application, we obtain new results on the constancy regions of mixed test ideals, based
on the work of Felipe P´erez.
1. Introduction
Let f : X −→Y be a smooth morphism between locally noetherian schemes; that is, f
is a formally smooth morphism of finite type, or equivalently, a regular morphism of finite
type. In this situation, the relative cotangent sheaf Ωf is a locally free coherent sheaf on X,
and we can form the associated canonical sheaf ωf := det Ωf. This allows us to define the
twisted/exceptional inverse image functor on coherent sheaves by
f ! := ωf ⊗f ∗.
This functor is fundamental in the framework of Serre duality and, more broadly, in the
abstract theory of Grothendieck duality.
On the other hand, the theory of F-singularities in positive characteristic is substantially
based on the Grothendieck duality of the Frobenius morphisms. For example, this framework
is used to define the Cartier operator κ: F∗ωX −→ωX on a smooth (and then normal) scheme
X over a perfect field of characteristic p > 0; see [Kat70, Theorem 7.2] and [BS13].
It is therefore not surprising that the theory of Cartier modules (and crystals) is compatible
with f !. This was worked out by Blickle and the second named author in [BS19], where they
proved that f ! can be viewed as a functor between Cartier modules. More importantly, their
construction commutes with funtorial test modules; which we briefly recall next.
In judging the (F-)singularities of a Cartier module (F, ϕ), one considers the complexity
and size of its lattice of Cartier submodules. The funtorial test module τ(F, ϕ) ⊂F is the
smallest nontrivial one; where it is a bit technical and non-obvious to define non-triviality
in this generality (see Definition 4.1). Thus, the test module gives a lower bound on how
singular the Cartier module is. For example, (F, ϕ) is said to be F-regular if τ(F, ϕ) = F.
In the case of (ωX, κ) and X Cohen–Macaulay, this refers to the F-rationality of X, which is
the F-singularity analog to rational singularities.
All of this is very pleasing, but in many applications there is a problem: it depends on
f being of finite type. We will mention an explicit example of this being an issue in the
context of constancy regions of mixed test ideals. Our key observation here is that, in positive

2. On Regular Morphisms
In the local study of morphisms of locally noetherian schemes, the archetype of what
constitutes a good property requires a “continuous variation” on the fibers (e.g., flatness) and
“nonsingular” fibers in some sense. The quintessential example is regularity. A morphism
of locally noetherian schemes is said to be regular if it is flat and has geometrically regular
fibers. It turns out that this notion is encoded in the differential structure of the morphism
and, more precisely, in its cotangent complex. To make this precise, let us consider the affine
case given by a homomorphism θ: R −→S of noetherian rings with cotangent complex Lθ.
This reduction is justified by [Sta18, Tag 07R8].
Then θ is regular if and only if its first Andr´e–Quillen homology with coefficients in S
H1(θ, S) := H1(Lθ),
vanishes and its zeroth one (i.e., the module of K¨ahler differentials)
H0(θ, S) := H0(Lθ) = Ωθ
is flat as an S-module. See [MR10, Proposition 5.6.2].
