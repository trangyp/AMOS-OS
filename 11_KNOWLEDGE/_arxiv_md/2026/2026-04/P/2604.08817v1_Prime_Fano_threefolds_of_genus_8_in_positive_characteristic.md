CHARACTERISTIC
AKIHIRO KANEMITSU AND HIROMU TANAKA
Abstract. We prove that a prime Fano threefold of genus 8 over an algebraically
closed field of positive characteristic is isomorphic to a linear section of the Grass-
mannian variety Gr(2, 6). As applications, it is shown that a prime Fano threefold
of genus 8 is irrational and, if the characteristic is larger than two, then it is globally
F-regular.

1. Introduction
The classification of Fano varieties has been an interesting topic in algebraic ge-
ometry. The projective line P1 is the unique one-dimensional Fano variety. Two-
dimensional Fano varieties are called del Pezzo surfaces. It is classically known that a
del Pezzo surface is isomorphic to either P1×P1 or the blowup of P2 along at most gen-
eral eight points. Study of three-dimensional Fano varieties, called Fano threefolds,
was initiated by Fano himself. A classification of Fano threefolds in characteristic
zero was given by Mori–Mukai [MM81], [MM83], [MM03] based on earlier works by
Iskovskih and Shokurov [Isk77], [Isk78], [ˇSok79a], [ˇSok79b] (cf. [IP99], [Tak89]). After
Mori–Mukai’s classification, decisive descriptions for prime Fano threefolds had been
announced by Mukai [Muk89], which was later confirmed by Bayer–Kuznetsov–Macri
[BKM24], [BKM25].
Definition 1.1. Let k be an algebraically closed field. We say that X is a prime Fano
threefold over k if X is a smooth projective threefold over k such that −KX is ample
and Pic X is generated by ωX. The positive integer g defined by (−KX)3 = 2g −2 is
called the genus of X.
Recently, Mori–Mukai’s birational classification was extended to the case of positive
characteristic by Asai and the second author [Tan23a], [Tan23b], [AT23], [Tan23c].
Then it is natural to seek a positive-characteristic analogue of Mukai’s description
for prime Fano threefolds. The purpose of this paper is to settle the case of genus 8.
Specifically, the main result of this paper is as follows.
Theorem 1.2 (Theorem 5.11). Let X be a prime Fano threefold of genus 8 over an
algebraically closed field k of characteristic p > 0. Then there exists a 9-dimensional
linear subvariety L on P14
k such that X is isomorphic to the scheme-theoretic intersec-
tion Gr(6, 2)∩L, where Gr(6, 2) ⊂P14 is the Pl¨ucker embedding of the Grassmannian
variety Gr(6, 2).
In characteristic zero, the above result was established by Gushel and Mukai [Gus83],
[Gus92], [Muk89].
Remark 1.3. Let X ⊂Pg+1 be a prime Fano threefold of genus g such that | −KX|
is very ample. Then it is known that 3 ≤g ≤12 and g ̸= 11 [Tan23b, Theorem
1.1]. If g ≤5, then X is a complete intersection in Pg+1 [Tan23b, Proposition 2.8].
Mukai’s description for the case g = 6 is given in [KT25a, Theorem 5.4 and Theorem
5.5]. Theorem 1.2 settles the case when g = 8. Thus the remaining case is when
g ∈{7, 9, 10, 12}.

1.1. Applications.
1.1.1. Irrationality. It is known that a prime Fano threefold X of genus 8 in char-
acteristic zero is birational to a smooth cubic threefold, and hence is irrational. We
shall extend this result to the case of positive characteristic.
Theorem 1.4 (Theorem 6.1, Corollary 6.2). Let X be a prime Fano threefold of genus
8 over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p > 0. Then X is birational to a
smooth cubic threefold. Moreover, X is not rational.
We now overview the proof of Theorem 1.4, although it is quite similar to that
in characteristic zero ([Fan30], [Isk80], [Put82], [Kuz04]). Let X be a prime Fano

using Theorem 1.2, we obtain a suitable smooth cubic threefold Y , which is defined
as a hyperplane section of the Pfaffian cubic hypersurface C ⊂P14 (Definition 6.4,
Proposition 6.5). Then it is enough to show that X is birational to Y , because an
arbitrary smooth cubic threefold is known to be irrational [Ciu24, Theorem A] (cf.

1.1.2. F-splitting. Many classes of Fano (type) varieties in positive characteristic are
known to be F-split. For example, toric varieties and Schubert varieties are F-split
[BTLM97, 3.4 and the proof of Theorem 3], [MR85, Theorem 2 in page 38]. Then
it is natural to ask whether lower dimensional smooth Fano varieties are F-split. In
this direction, the one-dimensional Fano variety (i.e., P1) is F-split, whilst smooth
del Pezzo surfaces are F-split when p > 5 [Har98, Example 5.5]. Concerning smooth
Fano threefolds, there exists an integer p0 > 0 such that an arbitrary smooth Fano
threefold of characteristic p > p0 is F-split ([SS10, Theorem 1.2], [Tan23c, Theorem
1.1]). On the other hand, any explicit lower bound p0 is not known. Based on the
description as in Theorem 1.2, we give the optimal lower bound that works for prime
Fano threefolds of genus 8.
