1.3. Germs of groups and germs of actions. We wish to view Nmcc
X

fields on X. There are several weak points to this analogy, one of which is that the notion of a
conditionally convergent tensor is sensitive to the action G × X →X, and the data of this action is
not a local structure on X. We address this by noting that the action determines a structure on X,
a “germ action”, which is local, and that the class of conditionally convergent tensors is determined
by the germ action.
Two profinite groups G and G′ are said to have isomorphic germs if there is an open subgroup
of G that is isomorphic to an open subgroup of G′.
A category of germs can be defined as
a localization of the category of profinite groups: if P is the category of profinite groups and
continuous homomorphisms, and W is the class of morphisms that are open inclusions, then the
category of germs is P[W −1]. Results of Lazard [Laz65] show that P[W −1] has (for each prime p) a
full subcategory that’s equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional Lie algebras over Qp, and so
we denote germs in the same Fraktur font used to denote Lie algebras.
For any g ∈P[W −1], there is a sensible notion of a g-action g × X →X on a topological space X:
it means that some group G whose germ is g acts on X. If G acts on X with finitely many orbits,
then any open subgroup of G also acts on X with finitely many orbits, and in this case we say that
g acts on X with finitely many orbits. A g-action with finitely many orbits is a local structure in
the following sense: if X is partitioned into finitely many clopen subsets X1 ⨿· · · ⨿Xn, then a
g-action with finitely many orbits on X determines and is determined by a g-action with finitely
many orbits on each of the Xi.
In the body of the paper we do not work directly with P[W −1] but with equivalence classes of
subgroups of the group of self-homeomorphisms of X.
Germs of group actions also appear in the work of Harman and Snowden [HS24, Section 2.5] on
tensor categories.

1.7. An example. We have been motivated by some computations in Heegaard Floer homology.
Ozsváth and Szabó [OSz04b] introduced invariants of a closed, oriented 3-manifold Y , now called
the Heegaard Floer homology groups of Y . The Heegaard Floer homology of Y is meant to be a
model for the ground states of Seiberg–Witten theory on R × Y .
Ozsváth and Szabó presented several versions of Heegaard Floer homology, called “hat”, “minus”,
“plus”, and “infinity”. A version for manifolds with boundary, sutured Heegaard Floer homology,
was introduced by Juhász [Juh06]. We will describe our computations in the sutured framework.

of a tubular neighborhood of L. The boundary of M is a torus, which we equip with two “sutures”
Γ ⊂∂M, i.e. two disjoint meridional circles in this torus.

2. Tensor powers of vector spaces
Throughout this section, R denotes a commutative ring and V denotes a free R-module of finite
rank. In the later parts of this section (§§ 2.7–2.11), we assume that R = F2. We use X to stand for
either a finite set, or a profinite set with a “germ action” (§ 2.1). A germ action is an equivalence
class of group actions on X, we use G to stand for one of the groups of this equivalence class. These
groups are always profinite, and from § 2.7 on, they have an open subgroup of 2-power order.
2.1. Profinite sets with g-structures. Let X be a Hausdorff topological space. Let Homeo(X)
denote the group of self-homeomorphisms of X with its compact-open topology. Consider the set S
of all closed subgroups G ⊂Homeo(X) that have both of the properties
(1) G is profinite; and
(2) G acts on X with finitely many orbits.
If S is not empty, then X has the topology of a profinite set: it is the disjoint union of its finitely
many G-orbits, each of which is homeomorphic to G/H for a closed subgroup H ≤G.
Two groups G1, G2 ∈S are “commensurable” if G1 ∩G2 is an open subgroup of both G1 and of
G2—in that case G1 ∩G2 is another element of S. Commensurability is an equivalence relation on
S and we call an equivalence class for this equivalence relation a “germ action” on X.
If G is a group in S in the equivalence class g, we write G ⊃g and we sometimes say that g is
the germ of G. The inclusion relation makes the set of G whose germ is g into a filtered poset, in
the sense that any two elements of the poset have a common lower bound.
If one of the G ⊃g has pro-p-power order, or equivalently if every G ⊃g has an open subgroup
of pro-p-power order, we say that g has pro-p-power order. Later on (§ 2.7), we will assume that g
has pro-2-power order.
It is useful to think of g as an object that acts on X, as well as on other sets and spaces
derived from X. If Y is a topological space (often a discrete space, below), we will say that g acts
continuously on Y if there is a G ⊃g that acts continuously on Y .

3.1. Solenoidal structures. A multicyclic structure XS is a set X together with a bijection
S : X →X. The name comes from the observation that if X is a finite set, each orbit of S on X
has a cyclic order in an evident way: x precedes Sx in the order. We go on calling it a multicyclic
structure even when some orbits of S are infinite, and even when every orbit of S is infinite.
We say that a multicyclic structure is compatible with a topology on X if S is a homeomorphism.
If X is profinite and g is a germ action on X (§ 2.1), we say that S is compatible with g if there is
at least one G ⊃g such that S commutes with the action of every g ∈G:
gS(x) = S(gx) for all x ∈X and g ∈G.
In that case we call S a solenoidal structure, for the reason given in § 1.4.

the basis in (4.9.2). We claim that no edge can be labelled by an element of C. Assume the contrary,
