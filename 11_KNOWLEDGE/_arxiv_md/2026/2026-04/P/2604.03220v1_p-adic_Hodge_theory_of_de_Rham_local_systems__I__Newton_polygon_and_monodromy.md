p-ADIC HODGE THEORY OF DE RHAM LOCAL SYSTEMS, I:
NEWTON POLYGON AND MONODROMY
HENG DU
Abstract. We prove that the relative p-adic monodromy theorem holds over a
dense open subset. Moreover, we establish the equivalence of the following two
statements: the local constancy of the Newton polygon function associated with a
de Rham local system around rank-1 points, and the relative p-adic monodromy
theorem near rank-1 points. We demonstrate how to extend the relative p-adic mon-
odromy conjecture from the neighborhood of rank-1 points to the entire interiors
of Newton partitions.

1. Introduction
A guiding principle in algebraic geometry, originating with Grothendieck, is that
the analytic or arithmetic singularities of objects of geometric origin should be en-
coded by a finite amount of data. Specifically, a local system or differential equation
arising from geometry should not exhibit arbitrary wild behavior near a codimension-
one boundary; rather, after a finite base change, its singularity should be entirely
encoded by finite nilpotent data. In the arithmetic ℓ-adic setting, this manifests as
the local monodromy theorem, cf. [GRR72, Exp. I, Prop. 1.1].
The quest for a p-adic analog of this principle culminated in the p-adic local mon-
odromy theorem, proved independently by André [And02], Mebkhout [Meb02], and
Kedlaya [Ked04]. Subsequently, by demonstrating that the “de Rham” condition for
a p-adic representation translates to the regularity of its associated differential equa-
tion over Robba rings, Berger used the p-adic local monodromy theorem to prove
Fontaine’s conjecture that de Rham representations are potentially log-crystalline,
cf. [Ber02].
With the development of relative p-adic Hodge theory (cf. [Sch13, KL15]), as well
as [LZ17, DLMS24a, GR24, DLMS24b, GY24], it is now possible to study these
phenomena in p-adic geometric families. In this paper, we explore how Grothendieck’s
philosophy, or a relative analog of Berger’s theorem, governs the behavior of p-adic
local systems of geometric origin on rigid-analytic varieties over p-adic fields.
The main result of this paper is the following.
Theorem 1.1 (Corollary 6.13). Let X be a connected smooth rigid-analytic space
over a p-adic field K, and let L be a de Rham Qp-local system on X. There exists
a dense open subset U ⊂X such that the relative p-adic monodromy theorem holds
over U. That is, there exists an étale covering V →U of smooth rigid-analytic spaces
over K, such that L|V is log-crystalline at all classical points of V .
The key new ingredient for proving Theorem 1.1 is the following.
Theorem 1.2 (CNP =⇒RpMT, Theorem 6.12). Let X be a smooth rigid-analytic
space over a p-adic field K, and let L be a de Rham Qp-local system on X. Assume
that the Newton polygon function NP(L) associated with L is constant (CNP); then
the relative p-adic monodromy theorem (RpMT) holds for L. That is, there is an
étale covering Y →X of smooth rigid-analytic varieties over K, such that L|Y is
log-crystalline at all classical points of Y .
Here, the Newton polygon function NP(L) is defined via the Newton polygon
function of the admissible modification associated with the de Rham local system L.
We will review this construction later in the introduction; it is essentially motivated
by Pappas and Rapoport [PR24, §2.6], who used this translation to study integral
models of Shimura varieties. Furthermore, we prove that there is always a dense
open subset of |X| on which the Newton polygon function is locally constant (cf.
Proposition 6.11). We will show that Theorem 1.1 holds over this dense open subset.
This shows that the relative p-adic monodromy theorem completely follows from
a purely topological property: the local constancy of the Newton polygon function
associated with L. While this implication cannot always be reversed globally, we

Remark 1.10.
(1) Note that although we deduce a global theorem from local be-
havior around rank-1 points, we do not require X to be quasi-compact.
(2) The main results of this paper establish the following logical relations:
RpMT1 ⇐⇒CNP1 ⇐⇒RpMTw ⇐= RpMT.
Furthermore, because higher-rank points in adic spaces behave analogously to
higher-codimension boundaries, it is natural to expect purity-type results for de
Rham and log-crystalline local systems over smooth adic spaces. We therefore
conjecture that RpMTw =⇒RpMT, which would render all four statements
actually equivalent.
(3) In [LZ17, Rem. 1.4], Liu and Zhu conjecture that if one further assumes L is
a de Rham Zp-local system, the covering space Y can be chosen to be finite
étale over X. At present, we are able to establish this finiteness property only
locally (cf. Theorem 6.9).
Remark 1.11. Conjecture 1.9 is widely open. The only known case is when L is

Shtukas associated with de Rham local systems. Let X be a smooth rigid-
analytic space over a p-adic field K, and let L be a de Rham local system on X. An
important arithmetic setting in which these structures naturally emerge is the study of
Shimura varieties and their local analogs. For instance, over the rigid analytification
of a Shimura variety of Hodge type (base changed to a p-adic field), the relative p-adic
Tate module L of the universal abelian scheme defines a Zp-local system. This L is of
geometric origin, and it is in fact de Rham by [LZ17, Cor. 4.9]. Moreover, the p-adic
Hodge-theoretic properties of this universal local system are expected to govern the
geometry of the Shimura variety, including its reduction modulo p. Equivalently,
they can be used to demonstrate the existence of, or characterize, integral models.
These expectations lie at the heart of the original motivation for relative p-adic Hodge
theory; for an incomplete list, see [Fal89], [Kis10], [PR24], and [IKY24].
The geometric approach utilized by Pappas and Rapoport originates from Scholze’s
transformative vision in his Berkeley lectures [SW20a] and ICM lecture [Sch14].
Scholze defined local Shimura varieties as purely p-adic analogs of classical Shimura
varieties, over which the universal family is governed by a p-adic local shtuka. Building
on this foundation, Pappas and Rapoport demonstrated that these universal shtukas
provide the machinery to capture the arithmetic data necessary for the study of
canonical integral models.
One of Pappas and Rapoport’s observations is that the relevant universal shtuka
over the (rigid-analytified) Shimura variety can be constructed from the universal de
Rham local system. Moreover, their construction works for a general smooth base.
This differs from previous perfectoid methods, which require passing to the infinite-
level Shimura variety and then studying the Hodge–Tate period maps (cf. Scholze
[Sch15], Caraiani–Scholze [CS17]).

The idea of proving Theorem 1.2. Finally, let us briefly outline the strategy
for proving the relative p-adic monodromy theorem under the assumption that the
Newton polygon function NP(L) is constant.
We note that there are several established approaches to proving that a de Rham

Our strategy is heavily inspired by the approach of Fargues and Fontaine, which

sequence make sense, and suppose it is equipped with a symmetric relation called

(0) The objects of T ′ and T ′′ are of finite dimension.
(1) If T ∈T is of dimension 1, then T ∈T ′ implies T ∈T ′′.

a) if T1 and T2 are of finite dimension, then T1 ∈T ′′ ⇔T2 ∈T ′′,
b) T1 irreducible in T ′ ⇒T2 ∈T ′.

Ti+1 is a neighbor of Ti for all i, and Tr ∈T ′′.
