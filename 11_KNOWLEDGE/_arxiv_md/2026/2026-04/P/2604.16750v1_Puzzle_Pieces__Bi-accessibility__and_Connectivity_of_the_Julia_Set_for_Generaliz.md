Abstract
We study the dynamics of a parametric family of rational functions of odd degree,
where each function is a generalized Blaschke product that maps the unit circle onto
itself. The action of the Blaschke product restricted to the unit circle defines a circle map,
and the parameter space of the family exhibits Arnold tongues. As the parameter varies
over an Arnold tongue, the action of the circle map changes from a diffeomorphism to
a non-injective endomorphisms. Using a combinatorial study of puzzle pieces, we show
that for adjacent parameters inside the Arnold tongues, there exist bi-accessible repelling
cycles. This topological feature enables us to exclude the presence of multiply connected
Fatou components whenever Herman rings are absent. As a result, we obtain a complete
characterization of the connectivity of the Julia set for each member of the parametric
family.
Keywords: Blaschke product, Julia set, Arnold tongues, connectivity
MSC Classification: 37F10, 37F20, 37F46

Introduction
The study of the connectivity of Julia sets is a central theme in holomorphic dynamics and,
under certain conditions, it may be closely related to the behavior of critical orbits. For
example, consider the iteration of a complex polynomial of degree at least 2: if the orbits
of every finite critical point remains bounded, then its Julia set is connected, [12]. For the
case of a rational function f : bC →bC of degree d ≥2, its Julia set, J (f), is a compact set
of the Riemann sphere, thus, J (f) is connected if and only if all its Fatou components
are simply connected. Establishing the connectivity of every Fatou component is far more
intricate in the rational case, since either Herman rings may occur, [10], or an immediate
basin (either attracting or parabolic) may contain more than one critical point, which
could result in a component of infinite connectivity (see for example [6, Propositon 3.1]).
In this paper, we analyze the connectivity of the Julia set for a specific family of
rational maps known as generalized Blaschke products. While classical finite Blaschke
products are endomorphisms of the unit disk D that map the unit circle S1 to itself, we

of multiply connected basins. Combining our classification of Fatou components with the

Proof. If the rotation number of f is irrational, then f has no periodic points, and the

repelling. This is equivalent to assuming that for every fixed point z of g, the multiplier
satisfies g′(z) > 1.

such fixed point z0, our assumption implies that the derivative is positive:
ψ′(z0) = g′(z0) −1 > 0.
This means that at every zero, the function ψ is strictly increasing. However, a continuous
function on a circle cannot have only zeros where it is strictly increasing. If we view the
circle as the interval [0, 1] with identified endpoints and the function crosses the axis from
negative to positive, it must eventually cross back from positive to negative to ensure
ψ(0) = ψ(1). At such zero, zc, the derivative would have to be non-positive, ψ′(zc) ≤0,
which contradicts our assumption. Therefore, the initial assumption must be false. There
must exist at least one fixed point z0 of g where ψ′(z0) ≤0, which implies g′(z0) ≤1.
This point belongs to a non-repelling periodic orbit of f.

3.3
Fatou Components of the Blaschke family
This section classifies the Fatou components of the Blaschke family Ba. The existence
and topology of these components are strictly determined by the location of the critical
points and the parameter a.
We begin by ruling out the existence of Siegel disks for the entire family and charac-
terizing the existence of Herman rings.
Theorem 10 (Rotation Domains). Let Ba be the Blaschke product defined in (3).
1. The map Ba has no Siegel disks for any a ∈C.
2. If |a| ≤2d + 1, the map Ba has no Herman rings.
Proof. 1. Absence of Siegel Disks: We proceed by contradiction. Assume Ba possesses a
Siegel disk ∆of period p ≥1. Since S1 is totally invariant and contains super-attracting
points in its complement, ∆must be disjoint from S1. By the symmetry I(z) = 1/¯z,
there exists a disjoint mirror Siegel disk e∆= I(∆). These two disjoint cycles must attract
distinct critical orbits. Since the orbits of 0, ∞, a, 1/¯a are captured by the super-attracting
fixed points, only the free critical points c+ and c−remain. Thus, one attracts to ∆and
the other to e∆. This configuration yields four non-repelling cycles: {0}, {∞}, ∆, e∆. This
saturates the Shishikura inequality (4 ≤4). However, Ba|S1 is a circle diffeomorphism and
must possess a non-repelling cycle on the circle. This fifth cycle contradicts the inequality.
Thus, no Siegel disks exist.
2. Absence of Herman Rings: If a rational function has a Herman ring, two critical
orbits must accumulate on different boundary components [14]. For 1 ≤|a| ≤2d+1, both
free critical points c± lie on the invariant circle S1, preventing them from accumulating
on distinct boundaries. If |a| < 1, the Julia set is the circle itself, precluding the existence
of Fatou components other than the basins of 0 and ∞.
Remark 2. In contrast to Theorem 10(2), if |a| > 2d+1, Herman rings may exist. In this
range, the rotation number ρ(t) of Ba,t|S1 depends linearly on t (specifically ρ(t) = ρ0 + t
(mod 1)). By choosing t such that ρ(t) is a Brjuno number, one can generate Herman
rings as described in [8].
Next, we describe the topology of the immediate basins of the super-attracting fixed
points 0 and ∞.
Theorem 11 (Basins of 0 and ∞). For |a| > 1, the free critical points c± do not lie in
the immediate basins A∗(0) or A∗(∞). Consequently:
1. A∗(0) and A∗(∞) are simply connected.
2. The boundary ∂A∗(∞) contains exactly d fixed points (and by symmetry, so does
∂A∗(0)).
Proof. First, we locate the critical points. For 1 < |a| ≤2d + 1, c± ∈S1. Since S1 is
invariant, c+ /∈A∗(∞). For |a| > 2d + 1, assume for contradiction that c+ ∈A∗(∞).
Let U0 ⊂A∗(∞) be the maximal B¨ottcher domain. The invariant circle S1 cannot be
contained in U0 because the map is 1-to-1 on the circle but (d + 1)-to-1 in the basin.
However, the preimage of the image of U0 must contain the pole 1/¯a ∈D. This implies
A∗(∞) connects ∞to D, crossing S1. A basin intersecting the invariant circle must be
symmetric, implying it contains both 0 and ∞, a contradiction. Thus c+ /∈A∗(∞) and
by symmetry c−/∈A∗(0).

(Preimages are handled similarly via induction; components disjoint from S1 contain at

Proposition 13. Let B = Br,α be a Blaschke map of degree 2d + 1 with adjacent pa-
rameters. The restriction B|∂A∗(∞) is topologically conjugate to the multiplication map
md+1(t) = (d+1)t (mod Z). Consequently, B possesses exactly 2d+2 distinct fixed points
in bC, distributed as follows:
1. The super-attracting fixed points 0 and ∞;
2. d points on the boundary ∂A∗(∞);
3. d points on the boundary ∂A∗(0).
Proof. First, we establish the conjugacy. As A∗(∞) is simply connected and the map is
hyperbolic for adjacent parameters, the B¨ottcher coordinate ϕ: A∗(∞) →D extends to
a homeomorphism ϕ: A∗(∞) →D. This map conjugates B on the closure of the basin
to the monomial map w 7→wd+1 on D. Restricting this action to the boundary S1, the
dynamics correspond to the angle doubling map md+1(t).
Next, we classify the fixed points. A rational map of degree 2d + 1 has 2d + 2 fixed
points in bC (counted with multiplicity). We explicitly identify them:
• The points 0 and ∞are super-attracting fixed points by construction.
• The map md+1 on the circle has exactly d distinct fixed points given by angles
t = k/d for k = 0, . . . , d −1. via the conjugacy ϕ, these correspond to d distinct
fixed points on ∂A∗(∞).
• By the symmetry of the Blaschke product, the map is invariant under the involution
I(z) = 1/¯z. Therefore, the d fixed points on ∂A∗(∞) map to d distinct fixed points
on ∂A∗(0).
Summing these contributions yields 2 + d + d = 2d + 2 distinct fixed points. Thus, all
fixed points of B are simple and accounted for.
4.1
Admissible Words
To facilitate subsequent constructions within the three possible scenarios where B|S1 acts
as a diffeomorphism, homeomorphism, or endomorphism, we establish a criterion for form-
ing words that will serve as labels for certain sets (the drops). For a general degree d ≥1,
let Ad denote an alphabet consisting of 2d + 2 symbols: 0, 1, . . . , d, 0, 1, . . . , d. For k ≥1,
a word of length k given by i1 . . . ik, with ij ∈Ad, is said to be an admissible word if it
satisfies the following rules:
a) The last symbol is not underlined: ik ̸∈{0, 1, . . . , d}.
b) An underlined symbol must be followed by d: ij ∈{0, 1, . . . , d} ⇒ij+1 = d.
c) A non-underlined symbol cannot be followed by d: ij ∈{0, 1, . . . , d} ⇒ij+1 ̸= d.
We denote the set of finite admissible words with symbols in Ad as ΞAd.
Remark 3. It is important to note that for any d ≥1, there are cases where a word i1 . . . ik
is admissible but its prefix i1 . . . ik−1 is not. This happens if and only if ik−1 ∈{0, . . . , d}.
However, by the admissible rules, if i1 . . . ik is admissible, then the prefix i1 . . . ik−2 must
also be admissible since ik−2 cannot be an underlined symbol 0, 1, . . . , d.

Characterization of the Connectedness of Julia Sets
We have established the necessary tools to characterize the connectedness of the Julia
set for the Blaschke family. By combining the classification of Fatou components with
the dynamical properties proven in previous sections (the non-existence of Siegel disks in
Theorem 10 and the existence of bi-accessible cycles within the tongues in Theorem A)
we determine exactly when the Julia set fails to be connected.
Theorem B Let d ∈N and consider the Blaschke family Br,α. For any parameters r > 0
and α ∈[0, 1), the Julia set J (Br,α) is connected if, and only if, the Fatou set F (Br,α)
does not contain Herman rings.
Proof. If the Julia set is connected, then all Fatou components are simply connected.
Since Herman rings are doubly connected domains, they cannot exist.
Conversely, assume that Br,α has no Herman rings. By Theorem 10, we know that the
family does not possess Siegel disks. We demonstrate that all remaining Fatou components
are simply connected by analyzing the parameter space regions established before.
1. The unit disk (r ≤1): From Lemma 8, the Julia set is the unit circle S1. Since
the circle is a connected set, the result holds.
2. The homeomorphism/diffeomorphism region (r ≥2d+1): In this region, the
map is a diffeomorphism or homeomorphism on S1. If there are no Herman rings,
then every periodic Fatou component is simply connected. Hence, the Julia set is
connected.
3. The endomorphism region (1 < r < 2d + 1): In this endomorphism region, we
consider the possible types of Fatou components:
• The basins of attraction for 0 and ∞are simply connected by Proposition 11.
• If there is a Fatou component U that is not A(0) or A(∞), it must be an
attracting or parabolic basin associated with a cycle on S1. This implies that
the parameter belongs to a tongue Tp/q.
