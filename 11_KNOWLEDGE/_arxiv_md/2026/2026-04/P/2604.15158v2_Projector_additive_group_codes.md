Abstract
Let F = Fq and let K = Fqm be a finite extension. An additive left group code is
a left FG-submodule of the group algebra KG. In this paper, we introduce projector
additive left group codes and restricted projector additive left group codes as additive
counterparts of idempotent group codes in the classical theory of group codes. More
precisely, they are defined, respectively, as images of FG-linear projectors on KG and
as images of left FG-submodules under such projectors. This perspective is motivated
by the fact that idempotent elements of KG do not yield a sufficiently general and
natural algebraic framework for the study of additive left group codes.
Projector
additive left group codes are a particular class of projective left FG-submodules of KG.
Consequently, in the semisimple case every additive left group code arises in this way,
whereas in the non-semisimple case the projector construction captures precisely the
direct summands of KG as left FG-modules, and hence a natural subclass of projective
left FG-submodules. We further relate trace-Euclidean and trace-Hermitian duality
to adjoint projectors, establish criteria for the LCD and self-dual cases, study the
Murray–von Neumann equivalence of projectors, and interpret quotients by orthogonal
codes in terms of module duals.

Introduction
Let F = Fq and let K = Fqm be a finite extension of degree m. An additive code of
length n over K is an F-subspace of Kn. In particular, every K-linear code is additive,
but the converse does not hold in general. Thus, additive codes form a strictly larger
class than linear codes over K. Additive codes of length n over F4 are additive subgroups
of Fn
4 and were first introduced in [4] because of their connection with quantum codes.
More precisely, the authors in [4] transformed the problem of constructing quantum error-
correcting codes into the problem of finding additive codes over F4 that are self-orthogonal
with respect to a suitable trace inner product. Additive codes over F9 that are self-dual
with respect to the trace-Hermitian inner product were later classified in [7], where they
were related to ternary quantum error-correcting codes. More general classes of additive
codes have since been studied in [3, 9, 10].
Let G be a finite group of order n. Via the standard identification of KG with Kn,
the group algebra KG provides a natural algebraic setting for codes of length n. In this

additive left group codes and restricted projector additive left group codes within the
framework of additive group codes, as additive counterparts of idempotent codes in the

FG-linear projectors on KG and as images of left FG-submodules under such projectors.

direct summands of KG as left FG-modules, and hence a natural subclass of projective

and self-dual group codes, as well as for twisted and skew-twisted group codes.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 contains the necessary preliminaries on
σ-sesquilinear forms, additive codes in Kn and additive group codes in KG. In Section 3,
we develop the projector approach to additive group codes, including FG-projectors and
their adjoints, LCD and self-dual projector additive group codes, and the Murray–von

Basic facts on σ-sesquilinear forms
Let F = Fq and let K = Fqm be a finite extension of degree m, let σ ∈Aut(F), and let
V be a finite-dimensional K-vector space. A σ-sesquilinear form on V over F is a map
⟨·, ·⟩: V × V −→F such that, for all x, y, z ∈V and all α ∈F, one has ⟨x + y, z⟩=
⟨x, z⟩+ ⟨y, z⟩, ⟨x, y + z⟩= ⟨x, y⟩+ ⟨x, z⟩, ⟨αx, y⟩= α⟨x, y⟩, and ⟨x, αy⟩= σ(α)⟨x, y⟩.
When σ = idF , the form is called bilinear. If, in addition, it is symmetric, that is, if
⟨x, y⟩= ⟨y, x⟩for all x, y ∈V , then the form is called Euclidean.
If ord(σ) = 2 and σ(⟨x, y⟩) = ⟨y, x⟩for all x, y ∈V , then the form is called Hermitian.
A σ-sesquilinear form is called reflexive if ⟨x, y⟩= 0 if and only if ⟨y, x⟩= 0 for all
x, y ∈V . In particular, Hermitian and Euclidean forms are reflexive.
Finally, a σ-sesquilinear form is called non-degenerate if ⟨x, y⟩= 0 for all y ∈V implies
x = 0.
For any F-subspace W ≤V , the dual space of W with respect to a reflexive σ-
sesquilinear form ⋆= ⟨·, ·⟩, denoted by W ⊥⋆, is defined by
W ⊥⋆:= {v ∈V : ⟨v, w⟩= 0 for all w ∈W}.

dimF (V ) −dimF (W) and (W ⊥⋆)⊥⋆= W.

2.2
Additive codes in Kn
Let F = Fq and let K = Fqm be a finite extension of degree m. Then Kn is naturally
an F-vector space of dimension mn. A linear code over K is a K-subspace of Kn. An
F-additive code over K is an F-subspace of Kn. Therefore, every linear code over K is
an F-additive code, whereas an F-additive code need not be K-linear. Observe that any
additive subgroup of Kn is automatically linear over the prime subfield of K. In this work,
however, we restrict our attention to codes that are linear over the fixed subfield F = Fq.
For brevity, when no confusion is likely to arise, an F-additive code will simply be called
an additive code.
In particular, every F-additive code is an additive subgroup of (Kn, +). Unless oth-
erwise stated, the dimension of an F-additive code C ≤Kn will always mean dimF (C).
Hence, if dimF (C) = r, then |C| = qr. Equivalently, if |C| = (qm)k, then k = dimF (C)
m
is
called the dimension of C over K. In general, k need not be an integer.
For a vector x = (x1, . . . , xn) ∈Kn, the Hamming weight of x is defined by
wt(x) = |{i ∈{1, . . . , n} : xi ̸= 0}|,
and the Hamming distance between x, y ∈Kn is defined by d(x, y) = wt(x −y). The
minimum distance of a nonzero F-additive code C ≤Kn is defined by
d(C) = min{wt(x) : x ∈C, x ̸= 0}.
If C has cardinality qr and minimum distance d, we say that C has parameters (n, qr, d).

3
Projectors for additive group codes
An important class of group codes in KG is that of idempotent codes. These are the group
codes of the form C = KGe, where e ∈KG is an idempotent, that is, e2 = e. In this case,
C is the image of the map ρ : KG −→KG defined by ρ(x) = xe. Since ρ2 = ρ, the map ρ
is an idempotent projector, and C = Im(ρ) is a left ideal of KG. However, this classical
construction is too restrictive in the additive setting. Indeed, additive left group codes are
only required to be left FG-submodules of KG and need not be K-linear, so they do not
necessarily arise as left ideals of KG. This naturally leads to the more general framework
of FG-linear projectors on KG and their images.
