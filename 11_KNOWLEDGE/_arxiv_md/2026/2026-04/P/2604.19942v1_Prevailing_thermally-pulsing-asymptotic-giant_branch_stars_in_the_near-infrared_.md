ABSTRACT
We recently reported the discovery of prominent features from the thermally pulsing asymptotic giant
branch (TP-AGB) phase in the near-IR rest-frame of a massive quiescent galaxy (QG) at z ∼1 observed
with the JWST, which set strong constraints on population synthesis models. Here we compare those
results against similar measures from a much larger sample of JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectra for
27 QGs at z > 1 from programs GO-5019 and CEERS, with signal-to-noise ratios of ∼100 (15/27)
and ∼50 (12/27), respectively.
Each spectrum is modeled with three stellar population synthesis
models: the latest Maraston (M13) models with a sizable TP-AGB phase, the Bruzual & Charlot 2003
(BC03) and the Conroy & Gunn (2009, C09) models, both of which include TP-AGB contributions
of smaller magnitude. The M13 model generally provides the best fit quality. Compared to BC03
and C09, M13 yields systematically younger mass-weighted ages (by <500 Myr) hence lower stellar
masses (by <0.2 dex). All models favor super-solar (Z/Z⊙>1.5) metallicities. Signal-to-noise-weighted
stacked spectra reveal that TP-AGB-related features are strongest in galaxies with mass-weighted ages
of t =0.4–1.8 Gyr, consistent with the predicted peak TP-AGB contribution in M13 models. Further
sample subdivisions show that these features are most pronounced in high-mass (log M∗/M⊙≥10.445),
dusty (Av ≥0.6), and metal-rich (Z/Z⊙≥1.35) systems. These results confirm the prevalence of TP-
AGB stars in the NIR spectra of high-redshift, intermediate-age galaxies and pave the way towards
improved spectral population synthesis modeling and robust stellar ages and masses.

Other

contributions from the TP-AGB phase of smaller mag-
nitude than the former. Ample literature over the past
twenty years has demonstrated that the treatment of the
TP-AGB stellar phase influences stellar ages and hence
stellar masses that are derived when population synthe-
sis models are used to interpret observed galaxy spectra
and broad-band spectral energy distributions (SEDs,
e.g., A. van der Wel et al. 2005; C. Maraston et al. 2006;
D. Capozzi et al. 2016). In particular, models with a
sizable TP-AGB phase such as M05 (C. Maraston 2005)
and its latest revision with a lower energetic and a cali-
brated onset age (revised M05, hereafter M13; N. E. D.
No¨el et al. 2013) yield younger ages and lower stellar
masses (by ∼0.1–0.3 dex) than light TP-AGB models
such as BC03 (C. Maraston et al. 2006; D. Capozzi et al.
2016). These trends illustrate how the NIR luminosity
from TP-AGB stars allows the observed photometry to
be reproduced with a younger age over models which get
the same flux from older red-giant-branch (RGB) stars,
which then implies a lower stellar mass (C. Maraston
et al. 2006).
Although these systematics have plagued the robust
derivation of galaxy stellar ages and masses for two
decades (C. Maraston 2005; C. Maraston et al. 2006; C.
Conroy et al. 2009; C. Conroy & J. E. Gunn 2010; M.
Kriek et al. 2010; S. Zibetti et al. 2013; R. Riffel et al.
2015; D. Capozzi et al. 2016), a step forward towards
a solution of this conundrum came from the detection
of strong spectral features from TP-AGB stars in the
rest-frame NIR spectra of one massive quiescent galaxy
at z ∼1 observed with JWST, dubbed D36123 (S. Lu
et al. 2025)1. The spectrum of D36123 has very high
quality, with signal-to-noise ratio, SNR∼187, and com-
paring it to a wide range of population synthesis models
clearly points to a sizable TP-AGB contribution, such
as M13, as the best-fitting SPS models. The question
then arises as to whether galaxy D36123 is an excep-
tional case, or whether this is typical among the spectra
of high-redshift QGs2.
This Letter aims to address this question by analyz-
ing JWST/NIRSpec PRISM observations of 27 quies-
cent galaxies (QGs) from programs GO-5019 (PI: Shiy-
ing Lu) and the Cosmic Evolution Early Release Science
(CEERS) survey (DD-ERS-1345; PI: S. L. Finkelstein,
S. L. Finkelstein et al. 2025). We employ the same three
SPS models we used in S. Lu et al. (2025), namely M13,
BC03, and C09, to fit the galaxies’ spectra, evaluate the
goodness of fits and compare the physical parameters
derived in each case. Throughout this work, we adopt
a flat ΛCDM cosmology with H0 = 70 km s−1 Mpc−1,
Ωm = 0.3, and ΩΛ = 0.7. All magnitudes are reported
in the AB system (J. B. Oke & J. E. Gunn 1983), and
we assume the G. Chabrier (2003) initial mass function.
2. SAMPLE AND DATA REDUCTION
2.1. Quiescent Galaxy Sample from Program GO-5019
As part of JWST Cycle 3 observations, program GO-
5019 obtained NIRSpec multi-object spectroscopy us-
ing a single configuration of the Multi-Shutter Assembly
(MSA) with the PRISM/CLEAR disperser/filter com-
bination on 2025 April 19 (UTC), with a total exposure
time of ∼2.55 hours, employing three-shutter slitlets per
object and a three-point nodding pattern to facilitate
background subtraction. The primary goal of this pro-
gram is to substantially increase the sample of massive
QGs at 1 ≲z ≲3 with high-quality JWST/PRISM

low redshift limit ensures that JWST/NIRSpec cov-

frame 4000˚A (i.e., D4000 break).

wavelength coverage (0.5–5.3 µm) encompasses numer-
ous spectral features associated with AGB stars, which

AGB stars in SPS models. S. Lu et al. (2025) detected
strong NIR spectral features from TP-AGB stars in the
bright (Mag3.6µm =21.4) QG D36123. For this reason,

4. RESULTS
4.1. Physical Parameters from individual spectra
In Figure 2, we compare the physical properties de-
rived from the BC03 (C09) models shown as red circles
(blue squares) with those obtained from the M13 mod-
els. All three SPS models yield consistent spectroscopic
redshifts, as nearly all measurements fall well within
the 3σ deviation from the one-to-one relation (panel a),
demonstrating that for our sample, redshift estimates
are robust against the choice of SPS models.
Assessing the overall fitting performance, we find that
in 78% (89%) of the galaxies the M13 models yield best-
fit solutions with lower reduced Chi-square values, χ2
R,
than BC03 (C09) models (panel b), consistent with pre-
vious results (e.g., D. Capozzi et al. 2016; S. Lu et al.
2025). Systematic differences are apparent in the stellar
masses derived with TP-AGB-light models (BC03 and
C09, panel c in Figure 2), which yield values that are sys-
tematically higher by ∼0.2 dex for 93% (BC03) and 78%
(C09) of their samples, respectively. This offset stems
from the older galaxy ages obtained with TP-AGB-light
models (BC03 and C09, panel d in Figure 2), since the
NIR flux required to reproduce the data now is provided
by RGB stars whose contribution becomes dominant at
an age of ∼1 Gyr (L. Greggio & A. Renzini 2011). In
turn, older ages result in higher inferred stellar masses.
Age (mass-weighted, t, panel d), metallicity (panel e)
and dust attenuation (Av, panel f) are degenerate pa-
rameters in spectral fitting. Figure 2 reveals systematic
differences between the various SPS models. M13 mod-
els yields ages around ∼0.6 Gyr for about half of the
galaxies, whereas BC03 and C09 models spread their
ages up to ∼2.5 Gyr, well within the RGB-dominated
era of stellar populations. To compensate for the young
ages inferred by the M13 models, the fit resorts to red-
dening (to account for the blue side of the SED), which
is generally higher in this case than in the other mod-
els (Panel f). By the same token, having converged to
older, RGB-dominated ages, TP-AGB-light models re-
quire much lower reddening in order to match the blue
side of the spectra.
Notably, for over half of the sample, all SPS mod-
els yield super-solar metallicities, in many cases with
Z/Z⊙>1.5.
This compares to Z/Z⊙∼1.2 for the
stacked spectrum of a sample of QGs at z ∼1.6 (M.
Onodera et al. 2015).
4.2. Analysis of Stacked Spectra
4.2.1. The stacking procedure
The high quality of the JWST/PRISM spectra enables
us to probe relatively weak TP-AGB features in the NIR
via stacking. Before stacking, each spectrum was visu-
ally inspected to mask residual artifacts (e.g., skyline
contamination and bad pixels). To avoid blurring NIR
features in the stacked spectra, the individual spectra
are shifted to the rest frame adopting redshifts (zadopted
spec
in Table D2) that are robust, defined by requiring the
normalized redshift difference between models to satisfy
|zM13 −zmodel|/(1 + zM13) < 0.002 for both BC03 and
C09, or that are well matched to unambiguous NIR fea-
tures (e.g., CO through visual inspection). We apply
λrest = λobs/(1 + z) and scale the Fλ flux density by
(1 + z) to conserve the integrated flux. The associated
uncertainties were transformed accordingly. Each spec-
trum was resampled onto a common wavelength grid de-
fined by the highest SNR spectrum in the selected sam-
ple, preserving its native sampling. A flux-conserving
rebinning algorithm (“drizzing”) was implemented by
computing fractional overlaps between input and out-
put wavelength bins.
Fluxes were redistributed using
the overlap-weighted means, and the uncertainties were
propagated in quadrature with the same weights. Wave-
length regions not covered by a spectrum were assigned
zero flux and zero weight so that they do not affect the
stack.

ination by strong features in most sources. When nec-
essary, this continuum level was estimated via linear in-

5. SUMMARY
We present the analysis of JWST/NIRSpec PRISM
spectra of 27 high-redshift (1 < z ≲3) QGs to probe the
spectral signatures of the TP-AGB stellar phase in the
NIR region. This is a follow-up study of our detection
of strong AGB spectral features in the JWST spectra
of a massive quiescent galaxy at z ∼1 (S. Lu et al.
2025). By fitting individual as well as stacked spectra
with the three population synthesis models (M13, BC03,
and C09), we find that TP-AGB stars are ubiquitous
in the spectra of QG at Cosmic Noon, confirming our
previous results based on one single galaxy.
We compare the goodness of spectral fits obtained
from the three adopted models and quantify systematic
differences in galaxy properties such as age, metallicity,
dust attenuation, and stellar mass.
We find that the
M13 models systematically provide fits with the best
quality, especially in the NIR, and yield younger mass-
weighted ages (by 500 Myr) and lower stellar masses
(by ∼0.2 dex) compared to BC03 and C09 models. All
models consistently point to super-solar metallicities for
about half of the sample.
The SNR-weighted stacked spectra reveal enhanced
CN and H-bump features alongside many weaker TiO
absorptions in galaxies with mass-weighted ages of 0.4-
1.8 Gyr, consistent with the expected peak contribution
of TP-AGB stars from M13 models in this age range.
The strength of these features increases toward higher
stellar mass (log M∗/M⊙≥10.445), stronger dust at-
tenuation (Av ≥0.6), and higher metallicity (Z/Z⊙≥
1.35).
These results provide further direct empirical
constraints to TP-AGB prescriptions in SPS models and
underscore the importance of modelling evolved stellar
phases to interpret the NIR spectra of distant QGs.

A. DATA REDUCTION AND EXAMPLE SPECTRA
The primary QG sample from program GO-5019 was reduced following the same methodology of P. Arrabal Haro
et al. (2023b,a), with a detailed description in preparation (Arrabal Haro et al.). It includes three primary pipeline
stages. In brief, the calwebb detector1 module converts uncalibrated detector images into count-rate maps, which
serve as input for subsequent processing. The calwebb spec2 stage applies background subtraction, flat-field correction,
wavelength and flux calibration, and two-dimensional (2D) distortion correction. Finally, calwebb spec3 produces the
combined 2D and extracted one-dimensional (1D) spectra for sources MSA observed. In this work, the final 1D spectra
were extracted using optimized apertures, with uncertainties computed by the JWST pipeline based on instrumental
noise. We rescale these uncertainties by a factor of ∼1.4 to account for correlations introduced by the pipeline (P.
Arrabal Haro et al. 2023b; S. Lu et al. 2025).
