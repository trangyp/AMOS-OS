PRESHEAVES AND COCOMPLETIONS IN FORMAL CATEGORY THEORY
NATHANAEL ARKOR AND DYLAN MCDERMOTT
Abstract. We study the relationship between presheaf constructions and free cocompletions
in the context of formal category theory, elucidating the coincidence between the two concepts
in familiar settings. We show that, in a virtual equipment satisfying mild assumptions, free
cocompletions under classes of weights are exhibited by presheaf constructions. We furthermore
extend the theory of weighted colimits from enriched category theory to this setting, developing
the concepts of atomicity and rank, and providing recognition theorems for presheaf objects, free
cocompletions, and cocomplete objects. As an application of our methods, we construct free
cocompletions, under arbitrary classes of colimit-small weights, of (possibly large) categories
enriched in (not necessarily symmetric) monoidal categories and bicategories; this resolves a
longstanding omission in the literature on enriched category theory.

1. Introduction
The construction of the category of presheaves pA := SetAop on a locally small category A is
perhaps the most fundamental construction in category theory. When A is small, the presheaf
construction is characterised by two important universal properties.
(1) It classifies distributors (a.k.a. profunctors or bimodules), in the sense that functors
A →SetBop are in natural bijection with distributors A −7−→B.
(2) It is the free cocompletion of A, in that (a) pA admits all small colimits, and (b) functors
A →X, for X admitting small colimits, are in pseudo natural equivalence with functors
pA →X that preserve small colimits.
When A is large, the situation is more subtle. While pA still classifies distributors, it no longer
exhibits the free cocompletion of A. However, we may obtain the free cocompletion by restricting
pA to the full subcategory spanned by the small presheaves, i.e. the small colimits of representable
presheaves. More generally, for any class Φ of colimit-small weights (here meaning a class of
distributors satisfying an appropriate size condition), corresponding to a class of colimit shapes,
the free cocompletion of A under Φ-weighted colimits may be obtained as a full subcategory of pA
by restricting to the Φ-weighted colimits of representable presheaves.

For enriched categories, there is a similar story, though additional care is required. For one, given
an arbitrary monoidal category V and a V-enriched category A, it may not be possible to form
the V-category of presheaves pA, even assuming A is small. For another, even supposing we may
form pA, it is not necessarily constructed as a V-category of contravariant V-functors from A to V
(indeed, for V to itself form a V-category requires V to be closed, and to construct opposites of
V-categories requires some form of symmetry on V). However, as long as V is sufficiently nice1, it is
nevertheless again true that we may form free cocompletions (and, more generally, Φ-cocompletions)
of V-categories by restricting to subcategories of pA [Kel82].
This intimate relationship between presheaf constructions and free cocompletions is highly
useful in practice, as it gives a simple concrete construction of an a priori entirely abstract object.
Furthermore, the knowledge that free cocompletions are exhibited by categories of presheaves allows
one to deduce important properties of free cocompletions that are not evidently derivable merely
from their universal property. It is therefore desirable to understand how presheaf constructions
relate to free cocompletions in general, permitting these techniques to be extended to settings
beyond that of categories enriched in a nice monoidal category.
To explain what we mean by presheaf construction and free cocompletion in general, we recall
the philosophy of formal category theory [Gra74; SW78; Woo82]. Category theory provides an
expressive language with which to describe totalities of structures and their relationships. Formal
category theory arises when we apply the principles of category theory to category theory itself,
studying the totalities of categories – as well as variants such as enriched categories, internal
categories, and fibred categories – using (two-dimensional) category theoretic techniques. While
several frameworks for formal category theory have been proposed, in recent years the formalism
of virtual equipments has emerged as a particularly apposite setting in which to carry out formal
category theory [CS10; AM24; AM25a; AM25b; Kou24].
A virtual equipment is a two-dimensional structure that axiomatises the totality of (large,
enriched) categories, functors, distributors, and natural transformations. A virtual equipment has
two kinds of morphism: tight-cells (denoted →), which axiomatise functors; and loose-cells (denoted
−7−→), which axiomatise distributors. They thus extend the concept of a 2-category by regarding
distributors also as a primitive notion. It is possible to define notions of presheaf object and weighted
colimit in a virtual equipment [AM25b; AM24; Kou24], which recover the usual notions of enriched
category theory when instantiated in appropriate virtual equipments of enriched categories. We may
therefore ask to what extent the relationship between presheaf constructions and free cocompletions,
known classically to hold for categories enriched in nice monoidal categories, holds purely formally.
In this paper, we introduce the notions of P-presheaf object and Φ-cocompletion in a virtual
equipment, for P a class of loose-cells and Φ a class of weights, and study their relationship. In doing
so, we extend much of the classical theory of presheaves and cocompletions – such as atomicity,
rank, and recognition theorems – from enriched categories to formal category theory. Our central
result, Theorem 7.1, gives a construction of Φ-cocompletions via presheaf objects, establishing
that the classical relationship between presheaves and cocompletions is not specific to enriched
categories, but holds for entirely formal reasons.
To demonstrate the utility of our development, we apply our results in the setting of categories
enriched in a normal virtual bicategory (a structure simultaneously generalising a unital multicat-
egory and a bicategory). While the theory of categories enriched in a monoidal category is well
developed [Kel82], the theory of categories enriched in multicategories [Lam69] or in bicategor-
ies [Wal81; Str83] is much less mature, despite the abundance of motivating examples [Wal82;
BCSW83; Gar14; GP18]. For a normal virtual bicategory V satisfying some mild assumptions, we
show that it is possible to construct Φ-cocompletions of V-enriched categories via enriched presheaf
categories. In particular, when V is a bicategory, we have the following.
Theorem (9.45). Let V be a locally complete and locally cocomplete bicategory with right lifts and
let Φ be a class of colimit-small weights. For every V-category A (not assumed to be small), the free
cocompletion of A under Φ-weighted colimits exists, and is given by the largest sub-V-category of
the V-category of small presheaves on A that is closed under Φ-weighted colimits and representable
presheaves.

Despite the historical maturity of bicategory-enrichment, which dates back to the 1980s (originat-
ing in Walters’ observation that sheaves may be seen as categories enriched in a bicategory [Wal81;
Wal82]), the construction of arbitrary Φ-cocompletions appears to be entirely new; we give a survey
of free cocompletions of enriched categories in Section 10.1. In fact, many of the results throughout
this paper, which extend the classical theory of colimits and cocompletions for categories enriched
in symmetric monoidal categories [Kel82] to the context of formal category theory, appear to be
new even when instantiated in the setting of enrichment in a non-symmetric monoidal category.
Our paper may be thus be seen as a contribution to the theory of non-symmetric monoidal and
bicategorical enrichment, of independent interest to our formal development.
In the future, we intend to apply this framework to situations beyond enriched categories. There
are numerous examples in the literature of category-like structures that admit notions of presheaf
construction and free cocompletion, which satisfy the same kinds of theorems as in enriched category
theory, and yet have heretofore been studied on a case-by-case basis (necessarily, as no general theory
existed). For instance, in each of the following examples, a notion of cocompletion is constructed
for a category-like structure by considering an appropriate notion of presheaf construction.
• For internal categories, there are at least two notions of presheaf construction, depending on
one’s attitude to size. For instance, Street [Str80] and Weber [Web07] consider exponentials
of internal full subcategories (playing the role of an internal ‘category of sets’), whereas
Johnstone [Joh02, §B2.5] considers a presheaf indexed category on an internal category.
• Im and Kelly [IK86] show that, for a small monoidal category A, the convolution mon-
oidal structure on the category of presheaves SetAop exhibits it as the free mononoidal
cocompletion of A; this is generalised to pseudo algebras for certain pseudomonads by
Walker [Wal19].
• In [GL12], Garner and Lack develop the theory of lex colimits, which is the theory of
cocompletions for finitely complete enriched categories, and show that many notions of
exactness in category theory are thereby captured.
• Bunge [Bun13] and Shulman [Shu13] independently introduced the notion of indexed enriched
(a.k.a. enriched indexed) categories, providing a simultaneous generalisation of indexed
categories and enriched categories. In particular, Shulman exhibits free cocompletions of
indexed enriched categories via a presheaf construction.
• In [GL20], Garner and Lin develop the theory of presheaves and cocompletions for restriction
categories, which are axiomatisations of categories of partial functions [CL02].
Our expectation is that these arise, for appropriate choices of virtual equipment, as instances of the
formal theory of presheaves and cocompletions we develop herein, permitting the general theory to
be applied immediately in these settings and facilitating comparison between them.
1.1. Outline of the paper
In Section 2, we recall the definition of virtual equipment and develop some of the basic formal
category theory that will be used throughout the paper, focusing on the calculus of right lifts. This
may be viewed as an extension of the material in [AM24, §3] (though familiarity with that work
is not assumed here). In Section 3, we introduce the notion of P-presheaf object, for a class P of
loose-cells in a virtual equipment, and study its properties, showing it to satisfy the usual properties
satisfied by full subcategories of enriched presheaf categories. In Section 4, we introduce the notion
of atomicity, which generalises the concept of a finitely presentable object in a category, and show
how it can be used to characterise presheaf objects. In Section 5, we introduce the notion of rank,
which generalises the concept of a finitary functor. Rank is fundamental to Section 6, in which we
introduce the concept of a Φ-cocompletion in a virtual equipment and study its properties, showing
it to satisfy the usual properties satisfied by enriched cocompletions. Our formal development
culminates in Section 7, in which we establish sufficient conditions under which presheaf objects in
virtual equipments exhibit free cocompletions. (Naturally, while we have spoken so far only about
cocompletions under classes of colimits, the theory of completions under classes of limits is entirely
dual: for completeness, we discuss this in Section 8.)
To demonstrate the utility of our framework, we use our central result to construct in Section 9 free
(co)completions, under arbitrary classes of (co)limit-small weights, of categories enriched in normal

1.2. Referencing convention
Throughout, we frequently annotate our results with references to comparable results in standard
accounts (e.g. in Kelly [Kel82]). ‘Comparable’ here is intended loosely: it is typically the case that,
even when specialised to the setting of enriched categories, our results are more general than those
we reference, and the proofs we give simpler by virtue of the formal setting in which we work. The
references are intended rather to make it convenient for readers of those texts to find a formal
analogue (or generalisation) of a given result in the setting of ordinary or enriched categories.
1.3. Related work
The relationship between distributors, presheaf constructions, and free cocompletions is of
historical significance, as it provides the motivation for the three primary approaches to formal
category theory in the literature. Street and Walters’ formalism of Yoneda structures [SW78] is
an axiomatisation of the presheaf construction. Weber showed that, under strong assumptions
(holding, for instance, for internal categories, but not for enriched categories), if the presheaf objects
on small objects in a Yoneda structure are cocomplete, then they form free cocompletions [Web07,
Theorem 3.20; Kou24, Remark 7.7]. Crucially, cocompleteness is assumed rather than derived
(though specific examples are given where this assumption does hold [Web07, §7]).
Wood’s formalism of proarrow equipments [Woo82], as well as the later virtual equipments of
Cruttwell and Shulman [CS10] and the augmented virtual equipments of Koudenburg [Kou24],
are axiomatisations of the structure of distributors. Koudenburg [Kou24] introduced presheaf
constructions in the setting of augmented virtual equipments, established that this setting generalises
that of Yoneda structures [Kou24, §4.34], and proved that, under mild assumptions, every presheaf
object exhibits a free cocompletion with respect to a class of weighted diagrams. This result is
closely related to our central result. However, the universal property that we consider – which
is double categorical in nature, rather than merely 2-categorical – is significantly stronger than
that of Koudenburg, and is necessary to generalise important classical theorems about enriched
cocompletions to the formal setting. We give a more detailed comparison to Koudenburg’s work in
Remark 7.4.
Bunge and Funk demonstrated that lax-idempotent pseudomonads, conceived by Kock [Koc67]
and Zöberlein [Zöb76] to axiomatise free cocompletions, also provide a setting for formal category
theory. It was shown by Walker [Wal18] that every lax-idempotent pseudomonad induces a Yoneda
structure; in particular, the formal category theory of lax-idempotent pseudomonads is subsumed
by that of virtual equipments. Conversely, we show in Proposition 6.34 that every class of weights
in a virtual equipment induces a lax-idempotent relative pseudomonad.

3. Presheaves in a virtual equipment
A presheaf object in a virtual equipment axiomatises the structure of a category of
presheaves [AM25b, §7.1]. Its universal property expresses the bijection discussed in the Section 1,
between loose-cells A −7−→B and tight-cells A →PB. In particular, the identity 1PA : PA →PA
induces a loose-cell πA : PA −7−→A, which we require to be dense in the sense of Definition 2.29.
(This distinguishes presheaf objects from power objects [Lam22, §11], in which πA is not required to
be dense; see [AM25b, Remark 7.4] for a discussion of related concepts.) We may refine the notion
of presheaf object by parameterising it by a class P of loose-cells, requiring that the projection πA
witnesses a bijection between loose-cells A −7−→B in P and tight-cells from A into the P-presheaf
object on B.
Remark 3.1 (A comment on notation). Before we present the definition, it is necesssary to clarify
a distinction between classical enriched category theory and formal category theory, which was
mentioned in passing in Section 2.2. For the theory of categories enriched in a monoidal category,
it is typical to consider two notions of weight, depending on whether one is considering limits or
colimits [Kel82]: colimits are weighted by presheaves (equivalently distributors from the trivial
V-category), while limits are weighted by copresheaves (equivalently distributors to the trivial
V-category). This means that it is often reasonable to use the same notation for both colimit
notions and limit notions (e.g. free completions under a class of weights and free cocompletions
under a class of weights), since these notions will be disambiguated by whether one is considering a
class of weights for colimits, or a class of weights for limits. (Strictly speaking, when enriching in
a braided monoidal category, presheaves on A are in bijection with copresheaves on Aop, which
means that the two notions of weight do coincide. However, the two notions are distinguished
notationally in practice via the presence or absence of op.) In the setting of formal category theory,
the appropriate notion of weight (Definition 2.12) is entirely symmetric, and parameterises both
limits and colimits. We must therefore take care in our notation to be clear about whether we are
talking about limit notions or colimit notions. We decorate operations with ˆ and ˇ to denote colimit
notions and limit notions respectively, which aligns with the typical convention in enriched category
theory to denote the category of presheaves by pA and the category of copresheaves by qA.

5. Rank
Along with atomicity, a further important concept in the study of Φ-cocompletions is the notion
of rank with respect to a tight-cell, which extends the concept of the accessibility rank of a functor.
Classically, a functor is said to have rank κ, for a regular cardinal κ, if it preserves κ-filtered colimits
(i.e. if it is κ-accessible, a.k.a. finitary when κ = ℵ0). The rank of a functor intuitively captures the
‘size’ of a functor: a functor with rank may typically be recovered by specifying a functor out of a
smaller subcategory, and left-extending along the subcategory inclusion. The following definitions
are intended to capture this intuition.
Definition 5.1. Let j : A →E be a tight-cell.
(1) A tight-cell f : E →I has rank j if the left extension j · (j ; f) exists, and the canonical
2-cell j · (j ; f) ⇒f is invertible.
(2) A loose-cell p: I −7−→E has rank j if the right lift p(j, 1) ◀E(j, 1) exists, and the canonical
2-cell p ⇒p(j, 1) ◀E(j, 1) is invertible.
⌟
In particular, a tight-cell f : E →I has rank j precisely when its conjoint I(f, 1): I −7−→E does.

(1) Let j : A →Ind(A) denote the cocompletion of a small category A under filtered colimits.

A cocompletion in a virtual equipment axiomatises the universal property of a free cocompletion
of a category under a class of colimits. When considering free cocompletions in ordinary category
theory, one typically asks that every functor f : A →X into a category X admitting a certain
class Φ of colimits may be extended to a functor ˜f : pΦ(A) →X from the free cocompletion, in an
essentially unique way. While this universal property certainly plays a role in our treatment, it is
important for us to observe that free cocompletions also admit a second universal property, which
concerns distributors p: X −7−→A satisfying an exactness property with respect to Φ. The notion of
cocompletion we consider, therefore, is stronger than the 2-categorical universal property that has
been considered heretofore in the literature. As we will explain later, this stronger universal property
allows us to establish desirable properties of free cocompletions that do not follow from the mere
2-categorical universal property: for instance, the embedding of any object into a cocompletion in
our sense is fully faithful (Lemma 6.11), whereas there are pathological examples of cocompletions
in the 2-categorical sense for which this is not the case (Example 6.12).
We shall start by introducing the appropriate notion of cocompleteness in our setting (Section 6.1),
before introducing and studying cocompletions (Section 6.3).
6.1. Cocompleteness
The notion of completeness of an object and the cocontinuity of a tight-cell with respect to a class

an analogous role to cocontinuity for tight-cells.

6.2. Saturation
In general, there may be many classes of weights whose notions of cocompleteness coincide: for
instance, in the setting of ordinary categories, the existence of all small colimits may be reduced to
many different bases, such as:
• small coproducts and (reflexive) coequalisers;
• an initial object and wide pushouts;
• finite coproducts and sifted colimits;
• finite colimits and filtered colimits,
and so on. We introduce the following equivalence class on classes of weights to capture this
relationship.
Definition 6.2. Two classes of weights Φ and Φ′ are colimit equivalent, written Φ ˆ≈Φ′, if and
only if Φ ˆ≲Φ′ and Φ′ ˆ≲Φ, where Φ ˆ≲Φ′ holds if and only if
(1) every Φ′-cocomplete object is Φ-cocomplete;
(2) every Φ′-cocontinuous tight-cell with Φ′-cocomplete domain is Φ-cocontinuous;
(3) every Φ′-exact loose-cell with Φ′-cocomplete codomain is Φ-exact.
⌟

9. Cocompletions of enriched categories
To demonstrate the utility of the theory we have developed, we instantiate our main results in
the context of virtual equipments of enriched categories, providing constructions of completions
and cocompletions of enriched categories under classes of weights. Beyond merely justifying the
applicability of our results, this fills a conspicuous gap in the literature: while such constructions
are well known to exist in the setting of enrichment in particularly nice monoidal categories, it does
not appear that the existence of these constructions has been demonstrated even for categories
enriched in non-symmetric monoidal categories, let alone for categories enriched in bicategories,
despite the importance of these settings. (We give a survey of completion results in the literature
in Section 10.1.)
