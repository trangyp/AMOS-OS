Email: {lia15, liucy32, yue.wang}@chinatelecom.cn
Abstract—Radio map estimation from sparse measurements
is fundamental to wireless network planning, optimization, and
localized map updating. Most recent learning-based approaches
formulate the problem as dense map completion over a predefined
grid, whereas many practical deployments require estimating
transmitter-specific received signal strength only at queried
locations or refining an existing map after local changes. This
paper proposes a physics-aware query-conditioned hierarchical
graph attention network for transmitter-resolved point-wise radio
map estimation. For each queried target–transmitter pair, the
proposed encoder constructs a bounded local graph over sam-
pled reference observations and aggregates reference-to-query
evidence through transmitter-referenced geometric descriptors. A
global graph then exchanges representation-level context among
nearby target locations to improve neighborhood consistency
without revisiting a large number of reference measurements.
On top of this shared architecture, we instantiate three oper-
ating regimes: direct RSS estimation, prior-conditioned residual
correction, and post-hoc gated attenuation of the learned cor-
rection. The framework uses only measurement-side quantities
and does not rely on environment-side inputs. Simulations on
the DeepMIMO scenario show that, in the direct regime, the
proposed HGAT achieves the lowest RMSE and MAE among
the evaluated learning-based baselines on all reported sites. When
conventional prior estimate is available, the residual and gated
regimes further reduce the prior error.
Index Terms—Radio map estimation, graph attention net-
works, query-conditioned learning, kriging, sparse measure-
ments, wireless propagation.
I. INTRODUCTION
Radio maps (RMs) characterize radio quantities such as
received signal strength (RSS) or channel gain over a geo-
graphic region [1], [2], and they are fundamental to network
planning [3], resource management [4], localization [5], and
CSI feedback reduction [6]. The core problem of radio map
estimation (RME) is to infer unobserved map values from
sparse and irregularly distributed measurements. This task is
intrinsically difficult because measurement budgets are limited,
propagation conditions are spatially heterogeneous, and the
radio field can exhibit strongly non-uniform local statistics.
Recent theoretical analysis has further shown that the com-
plexity of RME is tightly related to the spatial variability of
the field and becomes more pronounced when the sources are
closer to the mapped region, which motivates estimators that
can adapt to non-uniform local structure rather than relying
on a single global smoothness assumption [7].

Existing RME methods can be broadly grouped into three
categories. The first category is model-based estimation, in-
cluding inverse-distance weighting [8], matrix completion [9]–
[11], kriging methods [12], [13], and kernel-based cartogra-
phy [14], [15], just to name a few. These methods embed
useful geometric or statistical priors, but their performance
may degrade when the assumed stationarity, smoothness, or
propagation model is mismatched to realistic blockage and
shadowing. The second category is data-driven estimation,
where deep neural networks learn to recover a radio map
from sparse observations [16]–[22]. Such methods can model
highly nonlinear propagation patterns, yet they are usually
more data-hungry and may generalize poorly across environ-
ments or sampling patterns. The third category is the hybrid
approach, where model-based estimates or physically derived
structures are incorporated into the learning pipeline [23]–[28].
Representative hybrid couplings intervene at different levels of
the estimation process. For example, neural network residual
kriging improves the path-loss before kriging is applied to
the remaining spatial component [27], whereas kriging convo-
lutional networks absorb kriging-like behavior into a query-
centered local graph predictor [28].
However, most existing formulations are designed for dense
map completion over a predefined spatial grid. This is natural
when the deployment objective is to reconstruct an entire radio
map, but it is not always the most relevant operating regime.
In many practical workflows, only a limited set of queried
locations is needed, or an existing map must be refreshed
after localized environmental changes. In such cases, a query-
conditioned point-wise estimator that balances predictive per-
formance and computational cost is more favorable. Recent
work has started to move in this direction. In particular, [29]
formulates RME as a gridless attention-based predictor that
estimates radio quantities only at queried locations from sparse
measurements and their coordinates.
Another distinction concerns the field being estimated.
The majority of prior studies target either an aggregate or
occupancy-style radio map whose value at each location
reflects the combined contribution of all active transmitters,
e.g., [25], [26], [29], [30]. By contrast, this paper considers a
transmitter-resolved setting and estimates the field associated
with a designated transmitter at queried locations. The two
formulations serve different deployment purposes: aggregate
maps are natural when transmitter identities or powers are
unavailable, whereas transmitter-resolved maps are prefer-

Among learning-based methods, graph-based approaches
are especially relevant because they can handle irregular sam-
pling without requiring all data to lie on a dense regular image
grid; related graph learning on irregular domains has also
been explored in kriging-like settings [31]. Early work viewed
the radio map as a graph signal and used graph construction
tailored to interpolation [32]. More recent GNN-based meth-
ods build graphs using building-aware LoS/NLoS connectivity
[22], or physics-informed structures derived from building
masks, urban maps, radio-depth maps, and superpixels [19],
[20], [25], [26]. Although it is clear that graph construction is
central to RME performance, two limitations remain important
for practical deployment. First, many recent methods ingest
environment-side inputs such as building maps and depth
features. Such information can improve accuracy, but it is not
universally available in measurement-driven RME pipelines
[33]. This view is also consistent with 3GPP-related docu-
ments; the minimization of drive test (MDT) measurement
collection specification and the 5GS architecture specification
are centered on UE- and network-side measurements and
standardized system functions, rather than assuming direct
access to rich environmental databases [34], [35]. Second,
existing graph estimators often exploit heterogeneous spatial
dependencies through a fixed graph construction, a single
graph scale, or a generic repeated hierarchy. Without claiming
that they exhaust the full relational structure of RME, we find
it useful to distinguish at least two interactions in point-wise
estimation: measurement-to-query relations, which determine
how observed RSS values should support a specific query, and
target-to-target relations, which provide neighborhood context
among nearby targets associated with the same transmitter. We
model these two interactions with distinct message-passing
procedures. Compared with hierarchical graph formulations
such as 5GNN [36], our design differs in the sense that both
graph construction and attention are conditioned on the current
target–transmitter query pair and its transmitter-referenced
geometry.
Motivated by these observations, we propose a physics-
aware query-conditioned hierarchical graph attention net-
work (HGAT) for point-wise RME. We first form a sam-
pled reference set from historical measurements using spa-
tially uniform sampling. For each target–transmitter pair,
a local pair-conditioned graph is instantiated from nearby
admissible references, and a local pair-conditioned graph-
attention block aggregates measurement-level evidence toward
the current target. The resulting local embedding is then
refined by a same-transmitter global graph-attention block that
exchanges representation-level context among nearby target
nodes. Given the resulting hierarchical embeddings, a regime-
specific MLP fusion head outputs the final estimate. Notably,
the node and edge attributes used by the proposed frame-
work are constructed only from quantities naturally available
in measurement-driven RME, such as observed RSS, entity
locations, derived distances, and relative bearings.
On top of the shared HGAT encoder, we study three
estimation regimes motivated by the complementarity between
classical prior-based and learning-based estimators. A classical
prior can provide a stable coarse trend and an interpretable
baseline, whereas a learned predictor can adapt to localized
environment-specific effects that are difficult to encode ana-
lytically. Rather than forcing one of these views to dominate,
we evaluate the same encoder in three settings. In the direct
regime, the graph network predicts RSS itself from the learned
hierarchical representation. In the prior-conditioned residual
regime, an external baseline such as kriging is kept explicit,
and the HGAT learns only the mismatch residual relative to
that baseline; the final estimate is then obtained by adding the
predicted residual to the prior estimate. In the post-hoc gated
regime, the learned residual correction is not assumed to be
uniformly beneficial at all queried points. After the residual
predictor is fixed, a lightweight gate predicts a bounded
attenuation factor from the explicit prior, the frozen residual
prediction, its magnitude, and local prior-variation descriptors,
and then calibrates the correction strength by minimizing the
final recomposition error. Together, these regimes span stand-
alone estimation, explicit prior-assisted refinement, and post-
hoc correction-strength calibration under a shared architecture.
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as
follows:
• We propose a physics-aware hierarchical graph atten-
tion encoder that decomposes point-wise RME into
pair-conditioned local evidence aggregation and same-
transmitter global contextual refinement. The local stage
constructs query-specific reference pages using target–
reference, reference–transmitter, and target–transmitter
geometric descriptors, while the global stage exchanges
representation-level context among nearby target loca-
tions.
• We instantiate prior-assisted refinement on top of the
same encoder by keeping a classical prior explicit,
learning the structured residual relative to that prior,
and calibrating the frozen residual correction through a
lightweight post-hoc attenuation gate.
• We evaluate the framework using DeepMIMO datasets
under a unified measurement-only protocol across seen
and held-out sites. The direct HGAT achieves the lowest
error among the evaluated learning-based baselines, while
explicit residual correction and the final gated regime
further reduce the ordinary-kriging prior error.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Sec-
tion II introduces the problem formulation, prior residual struc-
ture, and estimation regimes. Section III presents the query-
conditioned hierarchical graph attention encoder. The regime-
specific prediction heads, inference complexity, and training
protocol are then described in Section IV, followed by the
simulation results and qualitative comparisons in Section V.

C. Empirical Structure of Prior Residuals
Our hypothesis is that prior residual is spatially struc-
tured, rather than behaving as spatially white noise. Fig. 1
provides a representative diagnostic using aligned heatmaps.
Two patterns are especially relevant. First, narrow high-power
regions produced by reflection often induce localized signed
residual structures that are difficult for globally smooth priors
to capture. Second, abrupt attenuation changes near blockage
boundaries or other propagation transitions tend to appear as
coherent residual bands aligned with large prior gradients.
These observations suggest that the prior residual is not merely
unstructured noise, but a learnable mismatch field shaped by
local propagation conditions.

This view is consistent with prior studies on interpolation
over discontinuous spatial fields. In urban surface reconstruc-
tion, smoothed interpolators, blur sharp edges and yield their
largest errors near facades, roof ridges, and other disconti-
nuities [37]. More generally, for fields containing fronts or
region boundaries, kriging and related smoothing methods
tend to smear across the discontinuity unless boundaries or
region-specific statistics are modeled explicitly [38]. Existing
remedies mainly focuses on improving the interpolator itself,
whereas our goal is different: the conventional interpolator
only provides coarse prior estimates, and we omit variogram
design or discontinuity-aware geostatistical optimization. In-
stead, we keep the prior explicit and focus on learning
the structured residual left after interpolation. This motivates
studying explicit residual correction in addition to direct field
estimation.

V. SIMULATION SETUP AND PERFORMANCE EVALUATION
A. Scenario Configuration, Data Splits, and Training Protocol
a) Data preparation and discretization:
We utilize
datasets generated by the DeepMIMO platform for computer
simulations and performance evaluation [42]. For a particular
scenario, the UE-level measurements are aggregated into a
coordinate-indexed table by discretizing the covered area into
2 m × 2 m spatial bins. For each occupied bin, the received
signal strength is aggregated by averaging the linear-scale
powers of all UEs assigned to that bin and then converting
the result back to dBm. In the current experimental pipeline,
this discretization is used to standardize data export, sample
indexing, and evaluation. However, the proposed HGAT is
query-conditioned and coordinate-driven, and is therefore not
intrinsically tied to this specific spatial resolution. In the
reported setup, each site is modeled as a single-antenna trans-
mitter with isotropic radiation, so that each site contributes
one transmitter-specific measurement stream in the processed
dataset.
b) Scenario-wise site partition: Instead of training a
universal model across heterogeneous scenarios, for each sce-
nario, we train one shared model over multiple sites within the
same geographical region. This arrangement strikes a balance
between algorithmic generalization capability and practical
deployment cost: it exploits cross-site generalization within a
region defined by the scenario and avoids the data transfer and
coordination overhead required by inter-region model training.
In the reported experiments, site IDs follow the dataset-defined
indexing, and odd-numbered sites are assigned to the seen
set, whereas even-numbered sites are assigned to the held-out
set. The seen sites provide both sparse reference observations
and supervised training queries, whereas held-out sites provide
sparse reference observations only. Consequently, the seen-
site results quantify performance on sites participating in
supervision, whereas the held-out-site results evaluate intra-
scenario regional generalization to sites whose labels are never
exposed during training.
The roles of observations and supervised targets are explic-
itly decoupled. The former is used only as observed support
for graph construction and message passing, whereas the
latter is used only to define regression targets. We select
5% of the available samples as sparse observations using
a spatially uniform sampler for each site-specific dataset.
For seen sites, 15% of the remaining samples are further
allocated as supervised training queries, while the rest form
the evaluation pool. Unless otherwise stated, the same site
partition, the same observation and target allocation protocol
are used for all baselines as well as for all proposed regimes
during their performance evaluation. In the following, the
terms seen and held-out refer specifically to whether a site
contributes supervised training queries; held-out sites may still
provide sparse local reference observations during inference
and evaluation. Both the direct and residual regime are trained
in an end-to-end manner and the RMSE and MAE are reported
for performance evaluation.

The HGAT encoder realizes four learnable functions

two feature-specific branches, one for the relative position and
one for the observed RSS; their outputs are concatenated and

bearings are wrapped to [−π, π).
The local edge encoder acts on the composite descriptor

interpolated embeddings to preserve continuity across the

the local and global stages encode the same primitive object,
namely pairwise geometry, the global edge encoder follows
the same geometric encoding scheme and activates only the
target-to-target relation in the global stage. Both local and

VI. CONCLUSION
In
this
paper,
we
proposed
a
physics-aware
query-
conditioned HGAT framework for transmitter-resolved point-
wise radio map estimation from sparse measurements. The
proposed architecture combines pair-conditioned local evi-
dence aggregation over reference pages with same-transmitter
global contextual refinement among nearby target locations.
On top of this shared encoder, we studied three operating
regimes: direct RSS prediction, ordinary-kriging-conditioned
residual correction, and post-hoc gated attenuation of the
learned correction. This design keeps the estimator grid-
free, measurement-driven, and compatible with both stand-
alone prediction and prior-assisted map refinement. Simulation
results showed that in the direct regime, HGAT achieves the
strongest performance among the evaluated learning-based
baselines and better preserves sharp propagation transitions,
confirming the benefit of query-conditioned graph construction
and hierarchical aggregation. Meanwhile, the comparison with
kriging shows that classical priors remain highly competitive
under sparse observations. Second, in the residual regime,
keeping ordinary kriging explicit and learning only its struc-
tured mismatch further reduces the prior error on both seen and
held-out sites. The post-hoc gate further calibrates the frozen
correction and mitigates the RMSE–MAE imbalance observed
for ungated correction on the held-out site.
