Phase-Shifted Pilot Design for NOMA-Empowered
Uplink ISAC Systems
Ahmet Sacid S¨umer, Ebubekir Memis¸o˘glu, and H¨useyin Arslan, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—The deployment of multiple transmitters (TXs) in
integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) networks neces-
sitates efficient resource sharing to overcome the limitations
of orthogonal allocation. While conventional interleaved (CI)
pilots combined with non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA)
improve spectral efficiency (SE), they inherently compromise
sensing resolution due to spectral sparsity, rendering the CI
nulling (CIN) extension a strictly limited remedy. This paper
proposes a phase-shifted (PS) pilot design and its novel PS nulling
(PSN) variant to integrate a communication TX (CTX) over the
PS-ISAC framework. The PSN variant strategically punctures
sensing signals at CTX pilot locations to preserve initial channel
estimates, enabling a dense data overlay. To resolve the resulting
multi-TX interference, joint iterative interference cancellation
(IIC) is adapted for non-nulling configurations and sequential
IIC is adapted for nulling variants, optimizing for both detection
robustness and convergence speed. Simulation results across
varying STX densities and modulation orders demonstrate that
the phase-shifted frameworks maintain sensing integrity while
explicitly reducing receiver-side computational complexities by
18.8% and 21.0% against their respective interleaved baselines.
Index Terms—Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC),
Internet of things (IoT), iterative interference cancellation (IIC),
non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA), phase-shifted pilots,
spectral efficiency (SE), uplink (UL).

However, the ubiquity of wireless sensing introduces a

designs [18]–[20]. Consequently, within the framework of
next-generation multiple access [21], the research community
has increasingly pivoted toward non-orthogonal solutions to
fundamentally maximize resource utilization.
A. Prior Work and Motivation
To directly address the spectral scarcity bottleneck, non-
orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) has emerged as a crit-
ical enabler by permitting multiple TXs, encompassing both
communication TX (CTX) and sensing TXs (STXs), to si-
multaneously share identical time-frequency resources [22],
[23]. Various ISAC-NOMA frameworks have been devel-
oped to manage the mutual interference, explicitly incor-
porating power-domain successive interference cancellation
for full-duplex transceiver architectures [24], [25], the non-
orthogonal coexistence of orthogonal frequency-division mul-
tiplexing (OFDM) and frequency modulated continuous wave
(FMCW) signals [26], and advanced multi-domain orthogonal
time frequency space (OTFS) modulation schemes [7], [27].
Furthermore, to support massive connectivity, recent literature
has successfully expanded these non-orthogonal principles
into near-field extremely large-scale multiple-input multiple-
output frameworks, leveraging spatial degrees of freedom to
dynamically cluster IoT devices [28].

Advancing beyond these heterogeneous waveform strate-
gies, foundational CSI-based ISAC-NOMA architectures es-
tablished the concept of non-orthogonally superimposing uni-
fied OFDM signals for an isolated CTX and STX pair. By
utilizing an iterative interference cancellation (IIC) receiver
(RX), this architecture successfully disentangled the mutual in-
terference, achieving SE perfectly comparable to interference-
free benchmarks [29].
Expanding these architectures to support multiple STXs
is achieved via the conventional interleaved-ISAC (CI-ISAC)
pilot allocation strategy [30]. However, CI-ISAC inherently
degrades the maximum unambiguous range due to spectral
sparsity [31], [32]. Furthermore, interleaved allocations de-
mand a separate inverse fast Fourier transform (IFFT) for
every scheduled STX at the RX side, creating a substantial
computational burden as the network scales [33], [34]. Al-
though a spectral nulling technique has been proposed for
single-STX scenarios to protect the communication payload by
explicitly puncturing sensing pilots [35], extending this mech-
anism to support multiple STX nodes yields the CI nulling
(CIN) baseline. While this multi-STX extension is structurally
straightforward, it inherits the fundamental structural trade-
offs of interleaved designs.
To overcome these limitations, the phase-shifted-ISAC
(PS-ISAC) pilot design [36] and its adaptive extension,
adaptive PS-ISAC (APS-ISAC) [33], utilize full-bandwidth
pilot transmissions modulated by STX-specific cyclic phase
shifts. These structures ensure that multiple sensing channel
impulse responses (CIRs) remain perfectly separable in the
delay domain while significantly reducing computational com-
plexity. Specifically, the PS-ISAC architecture requires only a
single composite IFFT at the RX regardless of the number
of scheduled STXs, a saving that is multiplied across itera-
tions [33]. Despite maximizing range resolution and unam-
biguous range, phase-shifted designs have previously been re-
stricted to orthogonal contexts. This paper bridges this gap by
integrating the PS-ISAC structure into a NOMA-empowered
system, adapting joint and sequential IIC RXs to resolve
complex multi-TX interference within dense environments.
B. Contributions
To address the fundamental sensing-communication trade-
offs in multi-TX environments, the PS-ISAC-NOMA frame-
work and its spectral nulling variant, PS nulling (PSN)-ISAC-
NOMA, are proposed. The main contributions of this work
are summarized as follows:
• We introduce an uplink (UL) architecture that overlays
high-rate communication data onto full-bandwidth phase-
shifted sensing pilots. By assigning STX-specific cyclic
phase rotations based on the cyclic prefix (CP) duration,
the framework maps each sensing CIR into a distinct
and non-overlapping delay-domain window. We further
introduce the PSN variant, which utilizes spectral nulling
at communication pilot locations to protect the integrity
of initial channel estimates.
• We adapt and evaluate two distinct IIC architectures to
resolve the dense multi-TX interference. A joint IIC is
utilized as a robust solution for non-nulling configurations
where standard joint detection fails to resolve the multi-
STX interference. Additionally, we adapt a sequential IIC
specifically for the spectral nulling variants.
• We conduct an empirical comparison between the pro-
posed phase-shifted frameworks and established CI and
CIN baselines across multiple modulation orders and
STX densities. Performance is assessed in terms of
sensing normalized mean-square error (NMSE), com-
munication NMSE, bit error rate (BER), SE, and com-
putational complexity. By enabling multi-STX sensing
through time-domain phase shifts instead of frequency-
domain interleaving, the proposed approach substantially
reduces RX-side hardware complexity requirements.

II. SYSTEM MODEL
As illustrated in Fig. 1, an UL ISAC-NOMA scenario is
considered. The architecture comprises a single-antenna CTX
and U single-antenna STXs. All TXs simultaneously send
OFDM signals to the ISAC RX over shared time-frequency
resources. Consequently, the ISAC RX receives the non-
orthogonally superimposed signals to perform UL channel
estimation and data detection.
A. Transmitted Signal Model
The system operates over a frame of Msym OFDM symbols
utilizing N subcarriers. Let K = {0, . . . , N −1} denote
the set of subcarrier indices. To facilitate non-orthogonal
coexistence across the shared subcarrier grid, the transmission
architecture formally partitions the generated signals into a
dedicated communication payload and their respective sensing
waveforms.

IV. COMPUTATIONAL COMPLEXITY ANALYSIS
In this section, the computational complexity of the pro-
posed phase-shifted architectures is mathematically evaluated
and compared against the interleaved baselines. Complexity
is quantified by the total number of real additions and real
multiplications. A standard complex addition requires 2 real
additions, while a standard complex multiplication requires 4
real multiplications and 2 real additions, as established in [39].
For element-wise vector division, multiplication by a precom-
puted inverse is assumed, equating the hardware cost to a
standard complex multiplication [40]. In baseband processing,
the dominant burden arises from the FFT and IFFT operations.
The baseline complexity of an N-point unitary transform is
formally defined as CA = 3N log2 N −3N + 4 real additions
and CM = N log2 N −3N + 4 real multiplications [41].
Table I provides a comprehensive summary of the arithmetic
operations and asymptotic scaling required by the STX and
ISAC RX.

For the proposed pilot designs, an additional STX-specific

C. Receiver-Side Complexity: Sequential IIC Architecture
The sequential IIC architecture, explicitly adapted for the
PSN and CIN spectral nulling variants, significantly reduces
the initial hardware burden by systematically fixing the initial
communication channel.
• Phase 1, comprising the cancellation stage where q ≤Q1:

via (17) requires 4N multiplications and 4N additions.
Time-domain extraction mirrors the joint method re-
quiring U + 1 transforms. Communication isolation via
(11) requires 4UN multiplications and 4UN additions.

the algorithm entirely bypasses the DFT-based channel
refinement penalty. This phase requires only U + 1
transforms, alongside 4(U + 2)N multiplications and

Q1: The algorithm unfreezes the CTX channel. Applying
the DFT-based noise cancellation in (19) and (20) rein-
troduces 1 IFFT, 1 FFT, plus 4N multiplications and 2N
additions, aligning the transform count with the joint IIC
at U + 3 transforms per iteration.

from the IIC equations, the hardware efficiency of the phase-
shifted architectures is formally quantified.

extreme density of U = 16 scheduled STXs introduces signifi-
cant multi-STX interference to the CTX payload, significantly
degrading the overall performance. Consistent with the BER
degradation observed in Fig. 6(c), the spectral nulling variants,
explicitly PSN and CIN, provide superior SE retention under
this massive interference by structurally shielding the initial
CTX channel estimate.

D. Computational Complexity Evaluation
To empirically validate the mathematical derivations pre-
sented in Section IV, the exact hardware burden of the evalu-
ated IIC RXs is formally quantified. The evaluation considers
an OFDM framework utilizing N = 512 subcarriers and
serving U = 4 scheduled STXs. To ensure a fair and consistent
comparison, the iteration parameters Q1 = 3, Q2 = 4, and
Q = 7 are selected.
Table II details the aggregate number of real additions
and real multiplications required per OFDM symbol duration,
explicitly accounting for both the unitary transforms and the
element-wise linear overheads. At the STX, the proposed PS
and PSN frameworks incur a marginal linear overhead of
8, 192 real multiplications and 4, 096 real additions for the
four scheduled STXs. Because these phase-shift operations
scale at O(UN), they are mathematically negligible compared
to the dominant O(UN log2 N) transform costs, rendering
the design highly suitable for resource-constrained IoT edge
devices.
The architectural advantages become heavily pronounced
at the ISAC RX. The CI framework utilizing separate per-
STX IFFTs blocks across the seven iterations, culminating
in an exact arithmetic footprint of 344, 344 real multiplica-
tions. By substituting the separate per-STX IFFTs with a
single composite extraction array, the proposed PS architecture
drastically reduces the transform count to forty-nine. This
structural optimization yields an 18.8% reduction in the total
RX-side multiplication burden, dropping from 344, 344 to
279, 748 operations.
Furthermore, the integration of the sequential IIC architec-
ture delivers significant computational savings for the spectral
nulling variants. By fixing the initial CTX channel estimate
during the first three sequential cancellation iterations, the RX
entirely bypasses the complex overhead of iterative DFT-based
channel refinement during the initial phase. Consequently, the
proposed PSN architecture executes merely forty-three total
transform blocks over the entire IIC loop. When evaluated
directly against its interleaved counterpart, the proposed PSN
framework achieves a rigorous 21.0% reduction in exact RX-
side multiplications, scaling down from 307, 456 to 242, 860
operations. As a result, as explicitly validated in the preceding
performance evaluations, the phase-shifted architectures secure
these massive computational reductions without inflicting any
degradation upon the sensing NMSE, communication NMSE,
or BER metrics. These exact arithmetic results conclusively
prove that the proposed phase-shifted NOMA frameworks
provide a hardware-friendly, highly scalable solution perfectly
tailored for energy-constrained, high-density IoT deployments.
VI. CONCLUSION
This paper presented a comparative analysis of multi-STX
UL ISAC-NOMA networks utilizing phase-shifted sensing
pilots and non-orthogonal communication. Two distinct IIC
architectures were evaluated: a joint IIC for full-band con-
figurations and a sequential IIC for spectral nulling variants.
Simulation results demonstrate that the spectral nulling ar-
chitectures, PSN and CIN, provide superior communication
NMSE, BER, and SE as STX density increases. While the PS
framework facilitates convergence to interference-free lower
bounds, the PSN variant ensures robust SE retention under
high-order modulation. By shifting multi-STX separation to
the time domain, the proposed phase-shifted frameworks sig-
nificantly reduce hardware complexity compared to interleaved
baselines. Specifically, the PSN framework is established as
the optimal architecture for computational efficiency, followed
by the PS framework as the secondary most efficient solution.
These findings empirically validate the scalability of phase-
shifted NOMA for dense IoT deployments.
Future investigations will derive a mathematical interference
analysis to quantify performance boundaries as functions of
CTX modulation order and STX density. Additionally, adap-
tive power allocation and machine learning-based suppression
will be explored to enhance SE and sensing resolution in
dynamic IoT environments.
