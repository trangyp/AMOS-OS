POINTS OF LOW DEGREE ON CURVES OVER FUNCTION FIELDS
SIÈNA VAN SCHAICK
Abstract. We show that the geometric classification of smooth projective curves admitting infinitely
many points of degree d ⩽5 extends from number fields to function fields of characteristic 0. Over
number fields, this classification was established by Faltings for d = 1, Harris–Silverman for d = 2,
Abramovich–Harris for d = 3, 4 and Kadets–Vogt for d = 4, 5. Our approach uses a specialization
argument to reduce the problem over function fields to the number field case.
1. Introduction
In this paper we adapt results of [HS91], [AH91], and [KV25] on the minimal potential density degree
of curves over number fields, to curves over function fields of characteristic 0. In particular, we generalize
their results on curves over number fields to curves over fields which are finitely generated over Q.
We work with the following definitions. A function field over a field k is a finitely generated field
extension of k which has transcendence degree at least 1. With a curve X over a field k we mean a
geometrically integral, separated scheme of finite type over k of dimension 1. We say that the points of
degree d on X are potentially dense if there exists a finite field extension l/k such that the set of points
of degree d on Xl is Zariski dense in Xl. We define the potential density degree set ℘(X/k) to be the set
of positive integers d such that the set of points of degree d on X is potentially dense in X, and we define
the minimal potential density degree min(℘(X/k)) as the minimum of this set.
We first note two distinct cases in which a projective curve X over a number field or function field
of characteristic 0 has a (potentially) dense set of points of low degree. Firstly, if X admits a finite
morphism of degree d to P1 or an elliptic curve E of positive rank, the rational points of P1 or E can be
pulled back to a dense set of points of degree at most d on X. Secondly, if X is (the normalization of) a
DF curve of type d (see Theorem 2.3), then X has a potentially dense set of points of degree d. The
name “DF curve” is inspired by the work of Debarre and Fahlaoui, who in [DF93] constructed the first
examples of DF curves to show the existence of curves with a dense set of points of degree d not coming
from a morphism to P1 or an elliptic curve.
The central problem in this paper is to give a geometric characterization of smooth projective curves
over function fields of characteristic 0 with minimal potential density degree equal to a specific value of d.
Our starting point is the following classification over number fields. For more on the structure of the
potential density degree set of curves over number fields, see [VV24].
Theorem 1.1 (Classification over number fields [Fal83], [HS91], [AH91], [KV25]). Let K be a number
field, and let X/K be a smooth projective curve.
(1) If min(℘(X/K)) = d ⩽3, then XK is a cover of degree d of P1 or of an elliptic curve over K.
(2) If min(℘(X/K)) = d = 4, 5, then either XK is a cover of degree d of P1 or of an elliptic curve
over K, or XK is (the normalization of) a DF curve of type d.
The theorem states that for d ⩽5 the aforementioned possible reasons for having an infinite set of points
of degree d are the only way for a smooth projective curve over a number field to have d = min(℘(X/K)).

2.2. Constant varieties. Suppose K is a function field over an algebraically closed field k. Let X0 be a
variety over k, and consider the constant variety X := (X0)K. Then X has a dense set of rational points.
Indeed, X0(k) is dense in X0, and this set induces a dense set of K-points on X. Similarly, if XL is
constant for some finite extension L of K, then X has a potentially dense set of rational points.
More generally, any variety X over K such that XK is dominated by a cover of degree at most d of a
constant variety has a potentially dense set of points of degree at most d. Indeed, then there exists a
finite extension L/K such that XL is dominated by a cover of degree at most d of a constant variety,
say Y . Then the dense set of rational points of Y can be pulled back to a dense set of points of degree at
most d on XL.
In this case the origin of points of degree at most d on X is clear. Hence, in our results, we assume
that XK is not dominated by a degree at most d cover of a constant variety. If X is a curve, then by
the following lemma, it suffices to assume that XK is not dominated by a degree at most d cover of a
constant curve.

2.3. Debarre–Fahlaoui curves. We give the definition of Debarre–Fahlaoui curves, which were first
constructed by Debarre and Fahlaoui [DF93, Section 4] to provide counterexamples to the conjecture of
Abramovich and Harris [AH91] stating that all potentially dense sets of points of degree d on smooth
projective curves over number fields can by geometrically explained by a morphism of degree d to a curve
of genus 0 or 1. An introduction to Debarre–Fahlaoui curves is also given in Section 5 of [KV25].
Let E be an elliptic curve over a field k with neutral element 0 ∈E(k). Then the addition map
π: Sym2(E) →E gives Sym2(E) the structure of a ruled surface. Let H be the divisor in Sym2(E)
consisting of all effective divisors of degree 2 on E containing 0, and let F be the divisor corresponding
to the fiber of π over 0. Then H and F span all numerical equivalence classes of divisors, and we have
H2 = 1, H · F = 1 and F 2 = 0.

Definition 2.3. Let X be a projective curve over a field k, and let d be a positive integer. Then X is a
Debarre–Fahlaoui curve of type d (or DF curve of type d for short), if there exists an elliptic curve E
over k and a closed immersion X ,→Sym2(E) such that the numerical equivalence class of X considered

Remark 2.4. Debarre and Fahlaoui first considered DF curves [DF93], but only for the case m = 1. In
[KV25], Kadets and Vogt use the more general definition that we have given here.

on X parametrized by E, see the discussion below Definition 5.1 in [KV25]. Thus, if E has a potentially
dense set of rational points, which is true if k is a number field or function field, then X has a potentially
dense set of points of degree at most d.
Moreover, Debarre and Fahlaoui show that certain DF curves of type d do not admit morphisms
of degree d to curves of genus 0 or 1 [DF93, Propositions 5.7 and 5.14], disproving the conjecture of
Abramovich and Harris. Kadets and Vogt extend this [KV25, Proposition 5.2].
