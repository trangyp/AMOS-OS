Performance Analysis of HAPS-RIS-Assisted
MIMO Systems Under Phase-Dependent Amplitude
Response Using Saddle Point Approximation
Tayfun Yilmaz, Haci Ilhan, Ali Gorcin and Halim Yanikomeroglu
Abstract—This letter proposes a novel mathematical frame-
work for the statistical characterization of reconfigurable in-
telligent surface (RIS)-mounted high-altitude platform station
(HAPS)-assisted MIMO systems over cascaded Rician fading
channels. Due to the inherent coupling introduced by the RIS,
the resulting cascaded channel does not satisfy the independence
assumptions required for conventional Wishart-based modeling,
which motivates a tractable alternative approach. By adopting a
line-of-sight (LoS)-aligned precoding strategy, the received signal-
to-noise ratio (SNR) is represented as a non-central quadratic
form with a structured covariance matrix. Exploiting this struc-
ture, a saddle point approximation (SPA)-based framework is
developed to characterize the SNR distribution. Closed-form ex-
pressions for the probability density function (PDF), cumulative
distribution function (CDF), and outage probability are derived.
The proposed framework further incorporates practical RIS
hardware impairments, including discrete phase shifts and phase-
dependent amplitude responses. The accuracy of the proposed
analysis is validated through Monte Carlo simulations.
Index Terms—HAPS-RIS, MIMO, Outage Probability LoS
Beamforming, Saddle Point Approximation.
I. INTRODUCTION
The vision of sixth-generation (6G) wireless systems aims
to provide seamless connectivity for everyone, everything, and
everywhere. In this context, aerial platforms such as high-
altitude platform stations (HAPS) have emerged as promising
solutions to extend wireless coverage and enable connectivity
in hard-to-reach or underserved regions. Recently, reconfig-
urable intelligent surfaces (RIS) mounted on HAPS, a.k.a.
HAPS-RIS, have been proposed as an effective approach to
enhance coverage and improve link reliability by intelligently
shaping the wireless propagation environment [1], [2]. There-
fore, analyzing the performance of HAPS-RIS systems is
of critical importance, and several studies have investigated
different aspects of their performance in the literature [3]–[6].
The work in [3] proposes a HAPS-RIS assisted network
where the HAPS operates as a relay serving multiple users.
However, the RIS elements are assumed to have unit-amplitude
reflection with uniform phase shifts, and both the transmitter

the secrecy rate performance of a system with a HAPS-

is equipped with a single antenna. The outage probability

however, similar assumptions are adopted, including unit-
amplitude reflection, uniform phase responses, and the absence
of a MIMO configuration.
Since aerial-to-ground channels are commonly modeled by
Rician fading distributions, it is also important to examine
existing analytical studies on RIS-assisted MIMO systems
under cascaded Rician channels. In [7], the ergodic capacity
(EC) of a RIS-assisted MIMO system is studied under a
Rayleigh–Rician cascaded channel, assuming unit-amplitude
reflection and uniform RIS phase shifts, while the analysis is
mainly limited to relatively small RIS sizes. In [8], the capacity
maximization problem is investigated for RIS-assisted MIMO
systems under Rician fading; however, the users are assumed
to have single antennas and the RIS elements are modeled
with unit-amplitude reflection and uniform phase responses.
More recently, [9] developed tight approximations and bounds
for the EC of RIS-aided MIMO systems with amplitude-
phase coupling, but under a Rayleigh–Rician cascaded chan-
nel and through an eigenvalue-based capacity analysis rather
than a direct statistical characterization of the receive SNR.
Therefore, an SNR-statistics-based framework for HAPS-RIS-
assisted MIMO systems with Rician–Rician cascaded channels
and practical RIS responses is still unavailable. Furthermore,
in practical HAPS-RIS-assisted MIMO systems, the cascaded
channel does not generally satisfy the independence assump-
tions required by conventional Wishart-based formulations,
due to the statistical coupling introduced by the common RIS
response. This makes SNR-statistics-based performance anal-
ysis challenging, especially in the presence of practical RIS
impairments such as discrete phase shifts and phase-dependent
amplitude responses. Motivated by these limitations, this letter
develops a novel analytical framework for the performance
characterization of HAPS-RIS-assisted MIMO systems for
Rician–Rician cascaded fading channels.

RIS assisted MIMO system where a ground-based transmitter

arranged as a uniform rectangular array (URA), while the
RIS consists of NRIS passive reflecting elements also forming
a planar URA. The receiver is equipped with Nr antennas
arranged as a uniform linear array (ULA).

III. NUMERICAL RESULTS
In this section, the proposed framework is verified through
Monte Carlo simulations. Since the main objective is to
validate the analytical characterization rather than to perform
a detailed link-budget or path-loss study, the transmit power is
assumed to be adjusted such that the target average received
SNR is achieved. The numerical results are not intended to
quantify the effects of altitude or angular variations. Unless
otherwise stated, the carrier frequency is set to f = 3.6 GHz,
and the RIS hardware parameters are chosen as ζmin = 0.8,
c0 = 0.43π, and κ = 1.6. The angular parameters are
fixed as (ϕH, θH) = (π/6, π/4) for the transmitter-to-RIS
link and (ϕr, θr) = (π/3, π/5) for the RIS-to-receiver link.
Moreover, for consistency with the adopted LoS-aligned beam-
forming structure, the transmit steering direction is selected
as (ϕt, θt) = (ϕH, θH), while the receive steering direction
is aligned with the RIS-to-receiver LoS path. The outage
threshold is set to ρth = 15.

IV. CONCLUSIONS
In this letter, a tractable SPA-based framework is developed
for the SNR-statistics-based performance analysis of HAPS-
RIS-assisted MIMO systems over Rician–Rician cascaded
fading channels. Under LoS-aligned beamforming, closed-
form expressions for the PDF and CDF of the received
SNR are derived, and the outage probability is obtained
accordingly. The analytical results are validated by Monte
Carlo simulations. In addition, by optimizing the discrete RIS
phases to strengthen the LoS component, performance close to
that of the optimal eigenvector-based benchmark is achieved
when accurate geometric channel information is available. It
is also observed that increasing the RIS size and the phase
resolution provides notable SNR gains when the RIS phases
are optimized to strengthen the LoS component. Although the
proposed framework can be extended to different transmitter-
RIS-receiver geometries, the effects of distance and angular
variations are not investigated in this letter for brevity.
