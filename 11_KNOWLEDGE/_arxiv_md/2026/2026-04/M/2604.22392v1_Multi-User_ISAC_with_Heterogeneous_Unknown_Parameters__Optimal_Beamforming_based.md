Multi-User ISAC with Heterogeneous Unknown
Parameters: Optimal Beamforming based on
Distribution Information
Chan Xu* and Shuowen Zhang†
*South-Central Minzu University, Wuhan, China, xuchan@mail.scuec.edu.cn
†The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, Hong Kong SAR, China, shuowen.zhang@polyu.edu.hk
Abstract—This paper studies an integrated sensing and com-
munication (ISAC) system where a multi-antenna base station
(BS) communicates with multiple single-antenna users in the
downlink and senses the unknown and random angle information
of a target based on its prior distribution information and the
received echo signals. We focus on a challenging scenario with
heterogeneous unknown parameters where the target’s reflection
coefficient is also unknown with no prior information. We
consider a general transmit beamforming structure with both
communication beams and dedicated sensing beams, where the
communication users can cancel the interference caused by the
pre-determined sensing signals. By adopting the periodic posterior
Cram´er-Rao bound (PCRB) to quantify a lower bound of the
mean-cyclic error (MCE) for sensing the periodic angle param-
eter, we optimize the transmit beamforming to minimize the
periodic PCRB, subject to individual communication user rate
constraints, which is a non-convex problem. By leveraging the
semi-definite relaxation (SDR) technique and Lagrange duality
theory, we derive the optimal solution and prove that at most one
dedicated sensing beam is needed. Numerical results validate our
analysis and effectiveness of the proposed beamforming design.
I. INTRODUCTION
Sixth-generation (6G) wireless networks will incorporate
sensing as a new function, which enables various new applica-
tions such as autonomous driving, low-altitude UAV manage-
ment, and industrial digital twins [1]. Integrated sensing and
communication (ISAC) will support both functionalities via
a unified infrastructure, thereby enhancing spectral efficiency
and reducing deployment costs [2]. With ISAC, base stations
(BSs) can perform downlink communication and estimate
parameters of sensing targets via received echo signals, for
which transmit beamforming optimization is very critical.
Among the existing works on transmit beamforming op-
timization, Cram´er-Rao bound (CRB) has been commonly
adopted as the sensing performance metric [3]–[5], which
is a lower bound of the sensing mean-squared error (MSE)
[6]. However, CRB is a function of the exact values of the
parameters to be sensed, which are generally unknown prior to
beamforming and sensing. On the other hand, the probability
density function (PDF) of such parameters can be known a
priori based on historic data or statistical analysis [7]–[21].
Based on the available prior information, posterior Cram´er-
Rao bound (PCRB) can be derived to quantify a lower bound
of the MSE [22], which is only dependent on the PDF and
tight in moderate-to-high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) regimes.

Prior studies on PCRB-based beamforming mainly consid-
ered dual-function beams for both sensing and communication
[9]–[13]. Since sensing and communication have drastically
different objectives, and the locations of communication users
and sensing target are generally different, sending only dual-
functional beams may be strictly sub-optimal in terms of
the sensing-communication trade-off. Moreover, in a multi-
user system, the existence of multi-user interference further
limits the design flexibility of the communication beams. The
beamforming optimization in the general case with dedicated
sensing beams was recently studied in [15]–[17]. Specifically,
when all the unknown parameters in the system are all
random with known distribution, i.e., being homogeneous,
[16], [17] derived various bounds on the number of beams
needed. However, for the case with heterogeneous unknown
parameters where some parameters are random with known
distribution, while others are deterministic or with no known
prior information, the PCRB characterization will be more
complicated, for which only [15] studied a single-user case.
Note that this is a highly practical case and corresponds to
various scenarios where there is no information about the
target’s reflection coefficient, non line-of-sight (LoS) clutter,
etc., while the corresponding parameters still need to be jointly
estimated with the desired parameters (e.g., angle/range).
This paper aims to make the first attempt to study the
transmit beamforming optimization in a multi-user ISAC sys-
tem with heterogeneous unknown parameters, under a general
structure with possible dedicated sensing beams, as illustrated
in Fig. 1. The BS sends downlink signals to perform multi-
user communication and sense the unknown and random angle
of a point target based on its reflected echo signals and perfect
prior PDF of the angle; while the target reflection coefficient

results.
II. SYSTEM MODEL
Consider a multi-user ISAC system which consists of a BS
equipped with NT ≥1 transmit antennas and NR ≥1 co-
located receive antennas, K ≤NT single-antenna communica-
tion users, and a point ground target with unknown and random
location to be sensed, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Specifically, the
antennas at the BS are deployed under a uniform planar array
(UPA) configuration. We assume that the distance (range) r
and elevation angle ϕ of target with respect to the BS are
known, and the azimuth angle of target denoted by θ ∈[−π, π)
is the unknown and random location parameter to be sensed.
The PDF of θ denoted by pΘ(θ) is assumed to be known
a priori from historic data or target appearance pattern [7]–
[21]. The BS sends downlink signals to deliver information to
multiple users, and estimate the unknown parameter θ based
on the target-reflected echo signals and the prior distribution
information pΘ(θ).
We consider an LoS channel between the BS and the target.
Let a(ϕ, θ) ∈CNT×1 and b(ϕ, θ) ∈CNR×1 denote the
steering vectors of the transmit and receive antenna arrays,
respectively. Let α = αR+jαI ∈C denote the overall complex
reflection coefficient of the target, which consists of the round-
trip path loss and radar cross-section (RCS) coefficient. The
overall channel from the BS transmitter to the BS receiver via
target reflection is thus given by αb(ϕ, θ)aH(ϕ, θ).
Due to the unknown RCS of the target, the reflection
coefficient α is also unknown. Particularly, we focus on a
challenging case where no prior information on α is available
(e.g., α may be a deterministic parameter or a random param-
eter with no known information). On the other hand, different
from the desired parameter θ, α is a nuisance parameter
[24]. This thus corresponds to a complex yet practical sensing
scenario with heterogeneous parameters with known/unknown
prior information and desired/nuisance properties.
We consider linear transmit beamforming design under a
general structure where the transmit signal is the superposition
of K communication beams and one or multiple dedicated
sensing beams. We focus on L ≥1 symbol intervals, dur-
ing which both the target’s location and the communication
channel between the BS and the users remain unchanged. Let
wk ∈CNT×1 denote the communication beamforming vector
for the k-th single-antenna user. Let S = [s1, ..., sNT] ∈
CNT×NT denote the sensing beamforming matrix to be op-
timized, with si ∈CNT×1 denoting the beamforming vector
for the i-th sensing symbol. The minimum number of non-
zero columns in the optimized S represents the number of
dedicated sensing beams needed. In each l-th symbol interval,
let cl = [cl,1, ..., cl,K]T ∼CN(0, IK) denote the information

Remark 2: (Difference between numbers of sensing beams
needed with heterogeneous vs. homogeneous unknown pa-
rameters). It is worth noting that [16], [17] also derived
bounds on the number of sensing beams needed for multi-
user ISAC, under the assumption that θ and α are both
random parameters with known distributions. With α treated
as a nuisance parameter and sensing interference cancellation
capability ignored, the number of sensing beams needed was
shown to be 1 in [16], where the standard rank-reduction
method in [29] was adopted. With α treated as a desired
parameter, the number of sensing beams needed was shown to
be 2 in [17], where the rank-reduction method enforces all the
elements in F −1 to be kept the same in each step to take into
account the PFIM with respect to α. In contrast, our tailored
rank-reduction method only needs to ensure [F −1]1,1 is kept
the same, which may explain the tighter bound in this paper.

VI. CONCLUSIONS
This paper studied a multi-antenna ISAC system with
heterogeneous unknown parameters and sensing interference
cancellation capability at the communication users. With the
aim of minimizing the periodic PCRB for sensing the desired
unknown angle parameter under individual communication
user rate constraints, we formulated the transmit beamforming
optimization problem considering a general structure with both
communication beams and dedicated sensing beams, for which
the optimal solution was derived. Moreover, the number of
dedicated sensing beams needed was analytically proved to
be no larger than one. Numerical results verified our analysis
and effectiveness of our proposed design.
