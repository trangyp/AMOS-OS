Abstract. We prove a criterion for the mildness of a finitely presented
pro-p group G. It implies as a special case a cohomological mildness
criterion via Massey products, generalizing results due to Schmidt and
G¨artner. It subsumes Labute’s non-singular circuit criterion. We fur-
ther show connections with the triangle condition for the mildness of
pro-p right-angled Artin groups, due to Quadrelli, Snopce and Van-
nacci.

This leads to many attractive consequences for G, for example,
cd(G) = 2. Alternatively, G is mild if and only if the Golod–Shafarevich
inequality, in its graded-algebra version, becomes an equality for a certain
graded algebra naturally associated to a presentation of G. We refer to §6
for the precise definition, which is due to Labute and Anick. We mention
at this point that the associated graded algebra depends not only on the
presentation of G but also on fixing a filtration of F, typical examples being
the p-Zassenhaus filtration F(n), n ≥1, or the lower p-central filtration F (n),
n ≥1 (see §9).
Recent work has revealed a rich web of connections between mild groups
and major problems across algebra and arithmetic: In group theory (the
Gildenhuys–Serre conjecture on one-relator pro-p groups [Gil68], [G¨ar15,
§5]), in algebraic number theory (the structure of restricted ramification
Galois groups of number fields [Lab06], [Sch06], [Sch07], [Vog05], [Mai14]),
in Galois cohomology (higher Massey products and the Koszulity Conjec-
ture [MPQT22]), and in arithmetic geometry (the tame Fontaine–Mazur
Conjecture [Lab14], [Mai07]).
Verifying “by hand” that a given finitely presented pro-p group is mild is
generally difficult. Several sufficient conditions for mildness, to be discussed
below, were proved in works by Anick [Ani82], Labute [Lab06], Schmidt
[Sch10], [Sch07], G¨artner [G¨ar15], Labute–Min´aˇc [LM11], Quadrelli–Snopce–
Vannacci [QSV22] and others. See also works by Forr´e [For11] and Maire
[Mai14]. These criteria were developed in diverse contexts, including Lie
algebras, Galois cohomology, homological algebra, combinatorial group the-
ory and graph theory.

(b) If w1 ∈B and w2 ∈Xn satisfy αw1, αw2 ̸= 0 and w1 ≤w2, then
also w2 ∈B.

1.4. The structure of the paper. In §2 we study ordered structures on
monoids and present a lexicographic construction of such a structure. This
construction is developed further in §8, where it serves as a key step toward
the proof of Theorem B. In §3 and §4 we construct the Massey product
elements αw using pro-p Magnus theory and explain their connection to
relations via the transgression pairing. In §5 we give conditions equivalent
to condition (c) of Theorem A, formulated in terms of matrices of Mag-
nus coefficients. In §6 we recall the definition of mild groups, presenting
it in a uniform way that applies to both the p-Zassenhaus filtration and
the lower p-central filtration.
Building on these preparations, in §7 we
state and prove a slightly stronger version of Theorem A. Combining this
result with the lexicographic ordered structure constructed earlier on the
free monoid X∗, we obtain in §8 a word-combinatorial criterion for mild-
ness (Theorem 8.3). After recalling some basic facts about the standard
filtrations in §9, we translate Theorem 8.3 into the language of Massey
products in §10, yielding Theorem B. These results go beyond the earlier
work of Schmidt and G¨artner, providing a wealth of new cohomological
mildness criteria. Here, shuffle relations for Massey products (as found in
[Efr24] and [Vog04]) allow us to substantially weaken the assumptions on
the vanishing of Massey products required by these criteria. Finally, in
§11 and §12 we show that Labute’s linking diagram criterion, as well as
the triangle criterion of [QSV22] for bipartite graphs, are special cases of
Theorem A.
2. Ordered monoids
2.1. Generalities on monoids. Let M be a monoid with unit element
1M. We denote its multiplication map by (w, u) 7→wu. Given w, w′ ∈M,
we say that w is a left (resp., right, middle) factor of w′ if w′ = wv (resp.,
w′ = uw, w′ = uwv) for some u, v ∈M. The monoid M is cancellative if
for every w, w′, u ∈M, any of the equalities wu = w′u, uw = uw′ implies
that w = w′.
Definition 2.1. An ordered monoid (M, ≤) consists of a monoid M and
a total order ≤on the underlying set of M such that:
(i) 1M is minimal in M with respect to ≤;
(ii) The product map M × M →M is order-preserving, where we
partially order M × M by the product order.

G = F/R is the pro-p right-angled Artin group (abbreviated: RAAG)
associated with (X, E).
Quadrelli, Snopce and Vannacci [QSV22, Th. F], building on work by
Weigel [Wei15], proved that a pro-p RAAG G is mild if and only if the
underlying graph (X, E) is triangle-free, i.e., it does not contain any 3-
clique. We now show how Theorem 7.1 implies an important special case
of this result, namely the case of graphs with chromatic number 2, i.e., a
bipartite graph. Note that such graphs are necessarily triangle-free.
