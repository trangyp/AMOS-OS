Multi-reference GW approximation for strongly correlated molecules
Yuqi Wang,1, 2 Wei-Hai Fang,1, 2 and Zhendong Li1, 2, ∗
1Key Laboratory of Theoretical and Computational Photochemistry, Ministry of Education,
College of Chemistry, Beijing Normal University, Beijing, 100875, China
2Institute for Advanced Study, Beijing Normal University, Beijing, 100875, China
The GW approximation is a cornerstone of many-body perturbation theory for computing
single-particle excitations, yet it fundamentally breaks down in strongly correlated systems where
the single-reference picture fails.
To overcome this long-standing limitation, we introduce the
multi-reference GW (MR-GW) approximation, which incorporates strong correlation effects non-
perturbatively into an interacting multi-determinantal zeroth-order reference. While the standard
Dyson equation is inapplicable in this setting, we show that the GW approximation can be natu-
rally generalized by developing a rigorous diagrammatic framework with an interacting reference.
Specifically, we define the MR-GW self-energy using a diagrammatic expansion based on the gener-
alized Dyson equation, and utilize a multi-reference random phase approximation for the screened
interaction, which captures many-body processes absent in standard GW. Applications to challeng-
ing strongly correlated molecules demonstrate that MR-GW seamlessly captures both strong and
weak correlations, yielding more accurate ionization potentials and recovering complex many-body
satellites missed by standard GW. This work establishes a rigorous diagrammatic paradigm for
extending ab initio Green’s function methods into the strongly correlated regime.
Introduction—Green’s function is the central tool
to understand spectroscopic properties of electronic
systems.1 However, exact Green’s function of interact-
ing electrons is inaccessible in realistic systems. Prac-
tical methods to calculate interacting Green’s functions
are mainly developed under the framework of many-body
perturbation theory (MBPT).2,3 The standard paradigm
is to approximate the self-energy Σ, which by the Dyson
equation4 relates the interacting Green’s function G
to a non-interacting zeroth-order Green’s function G0
defined by a quadratic zeroth-order Hamiltonian
ˆH0.
Within this framework, the GW approximation stands
as a milestone,5–11 which has been efficiently imple-
mented and successfully applied in materials and molecu-
lar systems.12–21 Although GW is in principle formulated
non-perturbatively under Hedin’s equation5, in practice
it is routinely performed perturbatively, known as G0W0,
where the self-energy is constructed using G0 and the
lowest-order screened interaction W0. Without causing
ambiguity, GW in this Letter below refers to G0W0.
Despite its widespread success, the GW approximation
exhibits several well-documented theoretical and practi-
cal limitations.10,22–24 One of the most significant chal-
lenges of GW is the failure in the presence of strong cor-
relation, which is difficult to be captured perturbatively.
Among other manifestations of strong correlation25–28,
multi-configurational molecules represent a typical ex-
ample of strongly correlated systems, where the ground-
state wavefunction contains multiple configurations with
competing weights. They are encountered in a wide range
of important systems including conjugated molecules,
radicals, transition-metal complexes, point defects and
systems with stretched bonds.29 The performance of GW
in multi-configurational systems has recently been tested
systematically, revealing a series of qualitative errors.24
While previous attempts integrated GW into quantum
embedding frameworks30–32 for dealing with strongly cor-
related molecules, we propose a fundamentally differ-
ent strategy rooted in a quantum chemistry perspec-
tive to address this challenge. The lesson well learned
in quantum chemistry is that multi-configurational sys-
tems can be treated by multi-reference perturbation the-
ories (MRPT)33–35, which employ a multi-determinantal
reference state and an interacting ˆH0 to capture strong
correlation effects at the zeroth order.
The success of
MRPT for correlation energy motivates us to develop
multi-reference generalizations of Green’s function meth-
ods such as GW for spectroscopic properties.
How-
ever, this task is highly non-trivial36, because Wick’s
theorem,37 which relies on quadratic ˆH0 and determi-
nantal reference, breaks down for interacting ˆH0. Con-
sequently, a series of fundamental tools in MBPT, most
notably the Dyson equation and Hedin’s equations, be-
come unavailable.
In this Letter, we introduce a multi-reference general-
ization of the GW approximation for strongly correlated
molecules, referred to as multi-reference GW (MR-GW),
by developing a rigorous diagrammatic formalism start-
ing with an interacting reference.
This framework in-
cludes strong correlation at the zeroth-order nonpertur-
batively, and treats the residual weak interaction pertur-
batively by combining diagrammatic expansion38,39 with
the generalized Dyson equation40.
Despite the lack of
Hedin’s equation for interacting ˆH0, we demonstrate that
the MR-GW approximation can be naturally formulated
in this diagrammatic framework using the same diagrams
as for standard GW. Applications to challenging strongly
correlated molecules demonstrate that MR-GW can cap-
ture both strong and weak correlation simultaneously,
yielding more accurate spectral functions and recovering

Generalized Dyson equation—In standard MBPT, the
Dyson equation connects the exact Green’s function G to
the zeroth-order one G0 by the self-energy Σ. However,
when ˆH0 is interacting or the zeroth-order state is multi-
configurational, Wick’s theorem does not hold and the
standard Dyson equation is no longer valid.36 In such
case, the only known relationship between G and G0
was derived by Hall40 as a generalized Dyson equation

Conclusion—In summary, we present a multi-reference
generalization of the GW approximation by developing
a diagrammatic framework with an interacting reference,
which circumvents the absence of Hedin’s equations in
this context.
MR-GW naturally extends the standard
GW approach, reducing to it in the limiting cases where
the active space is absent or ˆV A = 0. As demonstrated
for prototypical strongly correlated molecules, MR-GW
yields more accurate spectral functions and captures
satellites missing in the standard GW method.
Since
the final equations share similar mathematical structures,
existing numerical techniques developed for efficient GW
implementations, such as the resolution-of-identity (RI)
approach12,17,20, can be readily adapted to extend MR-
