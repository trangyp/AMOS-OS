Moments at the hard edge and Rayleigh functions
Anna Maltsev∗
and
Nick Simm†
Abstract
Motivated by the analogy between spectral moments of random matrices and asso-
ciated zeta functions, we study inverse power trace moments of the Laguerre ensemble
of dimension N and inverse temperature parameter β > 0. We consider a large N
regime determined by the low-lying eigenvalues of the ensemble known as the hard
edge. In the classical cases β ∈{1, 2, 4}, we obtain explicit results for the inverse
moments and extend these to formulae for the corresponding Mellin transforms. In
the case of general β > 0, by a result of Fyodorov and Le Doussal [20], we obtain
a different formula for the moments given as a sum over partitions. We use this to
consider a low temperature limit where β →∞as N →∞. In this limit, we show
that the moments are given in terms of the Bessel zeta function.

Introduction and main results
The topic of spectral moments has a long history in random matrix theory, dating back
to Wigner’s work on the semi-circle law in 1955. Since then, several connections to other
areas have been found, such as the use of spectral moments for computing the Euler charac-
teristic of moduli spaces [23], as well as the connection to genus expansion and orthogonal
polynomials [22, 24]. There is continued interest in this topic from various perspectives.
For example, recent works treat non-Hermitian and elliptic ensembles, leading to explicit
formulae for mixed moments and Harer–Zagier type recursions for real-eigenvalue moments
[3, 5]. Another direction concerns deformed ensembles: q-deformed Gaussian and Laguerre
models exhibit large-N genus expansions and a q-deformation of the Marchenko–Pastur
law [7, 6]. Spectral moments also arise in combinatorics and applications through ribbon
graph descriptions of correction terms [36] and through Bures–Hall calculations related to
entanglement entropy [42].
This paper is inspired by the developments of [12] relating spectral moments of random
matrix ensembles to associated zeta functions. To introduce the problem we consider here,
recall that the Laguerre ensemble is the joint probability density function of eigenvalues
