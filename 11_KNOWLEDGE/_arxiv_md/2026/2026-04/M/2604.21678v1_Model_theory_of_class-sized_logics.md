Model theory of class-sized logics
Jonathan Osinski∗†and Trevor Wilson‡
April 24, 2026
Abstract. We study compactness and Löwenheim-Skolem properties of frag-
ments of the class-sized logic L∞∞and of class-sized versions of second-order
and sort logics. In these fragments, certain combinations of infinitary quanti-
fiers and boolean connectives are banned. While model-theoretic properties
fail for unrestricted class logics, this drastically changes in our more restricted
setting. We show that model-theoretic properties of class logics characterise a
wide array of large cardinals, and that some of them can even be obtained in
ZFC. In particular, we give a characterisation of Weak Vopěnka’s Principle
and Ord is Woodin by downwards Löwenheim-Skolem properties, and a char-
acterisation of Shelah cardinals by a compactness property of class-sized logics.
We further strengthen many known results about properties of set-sized logics
by studying how they transfer to class-sized extensions.
1 Introduction
In many discussions of model theory of strong logics L, i.e., of extensions of first-order
logic, it has become somewhat standard to reserve the term logic to set-sized languages,
i.e., to languages for which, given any set of non-logical symbols τ, the collection L[τ] of
L-sentences over τ is again a set (cf., e.g., [Mak85, BDGM24, Osi24, Lüc25] as examples
for this naming practice). As a consequence, some languages classically studied in abstract
model theory are not considered to be a logic in this sense, e.g., the infinitary language
L∞∞allowing for conjunctions and disjunctions over arbitrarily sized sets of sentences,
and quantification over arbitrarily sized sets of variables. Let us call L a class-sized logic
or class logic when L[τ], the collection of L-sentences over τ, is a proper class for some

As a motivation, let us mention some background about the relation of model theory of
strong logics and large cardinals. How large cardinals are characterized by model-theoretic
properties of strong logics is a classical realm of study since Tarski [Tar62] asked the

logics of the form Lκκ for an uncountable regular cardinal κ. The answer led to the

large cardinal axioms were found to be equivalent to statements about model-theoretic
properties of strong logics (cf., e.g., [Mag71, Ben78, Mak85]). Recently, this type of
research had a resurgence, and many more connections of large cardinals to compactness
properties of logics, generalizing the Compactness Theorem, as well as to (upward and
downward) Löwenheim-Skolem properties, generalizing the Löwenheim-Skolem Theorems,

The article is structured as follows. In Section 2 we recall some basic definitions
and background from abstract model theory, introduce the class-sized logics we will
work with, and collect definitions and background on large cardinal notions we will
consider. In Section 3 we study how switching from the set-sized versions of first-order
logic, infinitary logics, second-order logics, and sort logics, to certain class extensions
impacts their respective compactness and upward Löwenheim-Skolem properties. We
show that in some cases, this switch comes with a jump in large cardinal strength
of the considered property, while in others, the strength stays the same. In Section
4, we study downwards Löwenheim-Skolem properties of class extensions of sort logic.
In this vein, we obtain the first known characterization of Πn-strong cardinals, Weak
Vopěnka’s Principle, and Ord is Woodin by Löwenheim-Skolem properties. The connection
between Πn-strong cardinals, Weak Vopěnka’s Principle, and Ord is Woodin was recently
uncovered by work of Wilson [Wil22b] and Bagaria and Wilson [BW23]. That the usual
Vopěnka’s Principle has several model-theoretic characterizations is well-known. For
instance, it is equivalent to axiom schemas stating that all logics have certain compactness
properties and downwards Löwenheim-Skolem properties, respectively. Whether the
same is true for Weak Vopěnka’s Principle has been open. We give a positive answer by
showing that Weak Vopěnka’s Principle is equivalent to the simultaneous existence of
certain Löwenheim-Skolem numbers for a large collection of class logics. Independently,

2 Preliminaries
2.1 Logics
Let us start by making precise what we understand by a logic, as an abstract object of
study. The definition is the standard one taken from [BF85, Chapter II, Definition 1.1.1].
Note that we do not follow the naming practice described in the beginning, in which a
logic has to have set-many sentences over set-sized vocabularies. We will consider many-
sorted vocabularies, as one of the logics we will consider is sort logic, whose semantics
is naturally expressed in this context. As in standard definitions in first-order model
theory, a (many-sorted) vocabulary τ is a set consisting of finitely many sort symbols
and an arbitrary amount of finitary relation, function, and constant symbols. We denote
the set of sort symbols in τ by s(τ). We demand s(τ) ̸= ∅. A τ-structure is a tuple
consisting of non-empty sets As for every sort symbol s ∈τ and interpretations for the
relation, function, and constant symbols. We write A for S{As : s ∈s(τ)}. If τ and σ are
vocabularies, a bijection f : τ →σ is called a renaming if it respects arities and restricts
to bijections of the respective subsets of sort, relation, function, and constant symbols in
τ and σ. If A is a τ-structure and f : τ →σ a renaming, then f induces a σ-structure
we will denote by f(A) on A by letting Af(s) = As for s ∈s(τ) and f(r)f(A) = rA for
every r ∈τ \ s(τ). An isomorphism between τ-structures A and B is a bijection A →B
respecting the sorts, relations, functions, and constants in the obvious way. We write
A ∼= B if there is an isomorphism between A and B.
Definition 1. A logic L is a pair consisting of a definable class function that maps every
vocabulary τ to a class L[τ], called the class of L-sentences over τ, and a definable class
relation |=L, called the satisfaction relation of L, such that:
(i) If A |=L φ, then φ ∈L[τ] for some vocabulary τ and A is a τ-structure. In this
case we say that A is a model of φ. If the logic is clear from context, we also simply
write A |= φ.
(ii) If σ ⊆τ for vocabularies τ and σ, then L[σ] ⊆L[τ].
(iii) If A ∼= B for τ-structures A and B, then for every φ ∈L[τ]:
A |=L φ iff B |=L φ.

Let us now define the logics we will consider.
Intuitively, we study fragments of
class logics like L∞∞that drop some of the boolean connectives or quantifiers. For
instance, the logic L(∧∞, ∃∞, ∀∞) is allowed to form conjunctions over arbitrarily large

and quantifiers cannot occur inside of negations.

that S is a set.

2.2 Properties of logics
We mention the standard model-theoretic properties of logics considered in the literature
and studied below.
For a logic L and a cardinal κ, a set T ⊆L is called <κ-satisfiable iff every S ∈PκT
has a model. The compactness number comp(L) of L is the smallest cardinal κ such that
any <κ-satisfiable set T ⊆L has a model.
We also say that κ is a compactness number of L if every <κ-satisfiable set T ⊆L has
a model (while not necessarily requiring that κ is the smallest such cardinal). For the
notions introduced below we follow the analogous convention.
The weak compactness number of L is the smallest cardinal κ such that any <κ-
satisfiable set T ⊆L of size |T| = κ has a model.
The Löwenheim-Skolem number LS(L) of L is the smallest cardinal κ such that any
sentence of L has a model of size < κ.
The Löwenheim-Skolem-Tarski number LST(L) is the smallest cardinal κ such that if
τ is a vocabulary of size |τ| < κ and φ ∈L, then any model A |=L φ has a substructure
B ⊆A of size |B| < κ with B |=L φ.
The Hanf number of L is the smallest cardinal κ such any satisfiable sentence of L
with a model A of size |A| ≥κ has arbitrarily large models.
All the above are classically studied notions.
Recently, Galeotti, Khomskii, and
Väänänen [GKV25] introduced the upward Löwenheim-Skolem-Tarski number ULST(L),
as the smallest cardinal κ such that for all φ ∈L and A |=L φ of size |A| ≥κ, there are
arbitrarily large superstructures B ⊇A with B |=L φ.
This notion got strengthened by Gitman and Osinski [GO25] to the strong ULST
number SULST(L), as the smallest cardinal κ such that for all sets T ⊆L and A |=L T
of size |A| ≥κ, there are arbitrarily large superstructures B ⊇A with B |=L T.
It is folklore that for any set-sized logic L, it is a theorem of ZFC that the Löwenheim-
Skolem number and the Hanf number of L exist (cf., e.g., [BF85, Chapter II, Theorem
6.1.4] for a proof for the Hanf number case; the LS number can be treated similarly).
The other properties mentioned can have large cardinal strength though. For instance,
Magidor showed that comp(L2) is the smallest extendible cardinal, and that LST(L2)
is the smallest supercompact cardinal (cf. [Mag71]). Results by Fuchino and Sakai
[FS22] and by Lücke [Lüc25] show that weak compactness cardinals for L2 in L give
rise to so-called weakly extendible cardinals. Gitman and Osinski [GO25] and Boney and
Osinski [BO25] gave two independent proofs that both ULST(L2) and SULST(L2) are
the smallest extendible cardinal. The existence of (weak) compactness numbers, LST
numbers, and (S)ULST numbers of logics can therefore have large cardinal strength.
It is easy to see that for many of the classically studied class-sized logics, none of these
cardinals can consistently exist.

2.3 Large cardinal notions
In this section we will give an overview of the large cardinal notions we consider and some
of the background results we will refer to.
A cardinal κ is extendible if for every α > κ there is a some β with an elementary
embedding j : Vα →Vβ such that crit(j) = κ and j(κ) > α. Equivalently, we might drop
the condition that j(κ) > α (cf. [Kan03, Proposition 23.15]).
The cardinal κ is C(n)-extendible if for every α > κ with α ∈C(n) there is some β ∈C(n)
with an elementary embedding j : Vα →Vβ such that crit(j) = κ and j(κ) > α. Again,

we may equivalently drop j(κ) > α.1 With rising n, the existence of a C(n)-extendible
cardinal gains consistency strength (cf. [Bag12]).
Vopěnka’s Principle (VP) is the axiom schema stating for every class C of structures
in a shared vocabulary that there are distinct A, B ∈C with an elementary embedding
between them. VP is connected to C(n)-extendible cardinals, as well as to properties of
logics.
Fact 8. VP is equivalent to the following axiom schemas.
(i) (Bagaria [Bag12]) For every n, there is a C(n)-extendible cardinal.
(ii) (Makowsky [Mak85]) Every set-sized logic has a compactness number.
(iii) (Stavi [MV11]) Every set-sized logic has an LST number.
Inspired by equivalences of VP to statements from category theory, Adámek, Rosický,
and Trnková [ART88] introduced Weak Vopěnka’s Principle (WVP). While they use the
class theory GBC for its formulation, we adopt the ZFC perspective from [BW23]. WVP
is then the axiom schema positing that if (Gα : α ∈Ord) is a sequence of graphs, which
is definable possibly using set parameters, such that for every α ≤β there is exactly
one homomorphism Gβ →Gα, then there are ordinals α < β and a homomorphism
Gα →Gβ.2
Bagaria and Wilson [BW23] provided an analysis of WVP in terms of large cardinals.
For this, a cardinal κ is called Πn-strong if for every ordinal λ and every A, which is Πn
definable without parameters, there is a transitive class M and an elementary embedding
j : V →M such that crit(j) = κ, j(κ) > λ, Vλ ⊆M, and A ∩Vλ ⊆AM. With rising n,
the existence of a Πn-strong cardinal gains consistency strength (cf. [BW23, p. 164]).
When writing WVP(Πn) for the axiom that restricts the statement WVP to sequences
of graphs definable by a Πn formula, Bagaria and Wilson showed:
Fact 9 (Bagaria & Wilson [BW23, Theorem 5.11]). The following are equivalent for
n ≥1:
(1) WVP(Πn).
(2) There is a Πn-strong cardinal.
Πn-strong cardinals are a variation of the following classical notion. For a class A
(definable with possible set parameters), a cardinal κ is A-strong if for every ordinal λ,
there is an elementary embedding j : V →M such that crit(j) = κ, j(κ) > λ, Vλ ⊆M,
and j(A ∩Vκ) ∩Vλ = A ∩Vλ. The axiom schema stating that for every class A there is
an A-strong cardinal is known as Ord is Woodin.

4 Weak Vopěnka’s Principle and Löwenheim-Skolem properties
Both VP and WVP are characterized in terms of the existence of large cardinals, namely
of C(n)-extendible and of Πn-strong cardinals, respectively. Moreover, with increasing
n, the C(n)-extendible and Πn-strong cardinals form respective hierarchies of increasing
consistency strength below VP and WVP. There is thus a strong analogy of VP and
WVP in terms of their characterizations by large cardinals. VP is also equivalent to the
existence of compactness and of LST numbers for every set-sized logic. This begs the
question whether WVP also has a characterization in terms of model-theoretic properties
of logics. We will show that this is indeed the case. More concretely, we will show that
certain downwards Löwenheim-Skolem properties for the class version Ls,n(∨∞, ∀∞) of
sort logic are equivalent to the existence of Πn-strong cardinals. As WVP is equivalent
to the existence of Πn-strong cardinals for every n, this results in a characterization of
WVP.
Boney and Osinski [BO25] and Holy, Lücke, and Müller [HLM24] also give independent
model-theoretic characterizations of WVP. Both approaches use compactness properties
though. As VP is characterized by both compactness and downwards Löwenheim-Skolem
principles, our analysis pushes the model-theoretic analogy of VP and WVP further by
providing an equivalence to the latter type of properties. The analogy is also pushed in
a different sense, as, next to giving an equivalence to the global WVP, we characterize
the hierarchy of Πn-strong cardinals below WVP, similarly to how the C(n)-extendible
cardinals can be characterized model-theoretically (cf. Section 3.3).
