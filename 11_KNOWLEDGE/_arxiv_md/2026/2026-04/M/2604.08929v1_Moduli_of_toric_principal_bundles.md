MODULI OF TORIC PRINCIPAL BUNDLES
SHAOYU HUANG AND KIUMARS KAVEH
Abstract. Let G be a reductive algebraic group.
A toric principal G-bundle is a principal G-
bundle over a toric variety together with a torus action commuting with the G-action. In [KM22],
extending the Klyachko classification of toric vector bundles, toric principal bundles are classified by
piecewise linear maps to the (extended) Tits building of G. In this paper, we use the classification in
[KM22] to construct a moduli space of (framed) toric principal bundles with given total equivariant
characteristic class, as a locally closed subvariety of a product of partial flag varieties. This extends
the construction of moduli of toric vector bundles by Sam Payne [Pay06].

2. Background on Tits Buildings
2.1. Tits building of a linear algebraic group. In this section we review some background ma-
terial about the Tits building associated to a linear algebraic group following [KM22, HK24].
A building is a pair (∆, A) where ∆is a simplicial complex and A is a family of subcomplexes A
(called apartments) satisfying certain axioms.
Let G be a linear algebraic group over a field k. To G there corresponds a certain kind of simplicial
complex called the Tits building of G which we denote by ∆(G). A parabolic subgroup P of G is
parabolic if P contains a conjugate of B. The set of simplices in ∆(G) is the set of parabolic subgroups
of G ordered by reverse inclusion. The apartments in ∆(G) correspond to maximal tori in G. For a
maximal torus H ⊂G, the corresponding apartment consists of parabolic subgroups containing H.
Borel subgroups correspond to the maximal simplices, called chambers, in ∆(G). Since every parabolic
subgroup of G contains the solvable radical R(G), the simplicial complexes ∆(G) and ∆(G/R(G)) are
isomorphic.
Example 2.1. Consider G = GL(r). A parabolic subgroup P of GL(r) is the stabilizer of a flag
F• = ({0} ⊂F1 ⊂· · · ⊂Fk = Cr). This gives a one-to-one correspondence between the simplices in
the Tits building of GL(r) and flags in Cr. In particular, Borel subgroups are stabilizers of complete
flags.
In fact, there is a topological space B(G) together with a triangulation in which simplices in
the triangulation (which are subsets of B(G) homeomorphic to standard simplices) are in one-to-one
correspondence with the simplices in ∆(G) and intersect according to how simplices in ∆(G) intersect.

3.1. Classification of framed toric principal bundles. Throughout we fix a point x0 in the open

We start by recalling the notion of a principal bundle. Let G be an algebraic group, a principal
G-bundle over a variety X is a fiber bundle P over X with an action of G such that G preserves each
fiber and the action is free and transitive. Throughout, we take the action of G on P to be a right
action.
Let G, G′ be algebraic groups and P (respectively P′) be a principal G-bundle (respectively G′-
bundle) over X. A morphism of principal bundles with respect to a homomorphism of algebraic groups
α : G →G′ is a bundle map F : P →P′ such that
F(z · g) = F(z) · α(g), ∀z ∈P, ∀g ∈G.
We refer to a morphism between toric principal G-bundles, with respect to the identity homomorphism
G →G, simply as a morphism of toric principal G-bundles.
Definition 3.1 (Toric principal bundle). Let XΣ be the toric variety associated to a fan Σ and G an
algebraic group. A toric principal G-bundle over XΣ is a principal G-bundle P together with a torus
action lifting that of XΣ, such that the T-action and the G-action on P commute. More precisely,
∀t ∈T, ∀x ∈XΣ, ∀z ∈Px we have:
t : Px →Pt·x,
t · (z · g) = (t · z) · g.
