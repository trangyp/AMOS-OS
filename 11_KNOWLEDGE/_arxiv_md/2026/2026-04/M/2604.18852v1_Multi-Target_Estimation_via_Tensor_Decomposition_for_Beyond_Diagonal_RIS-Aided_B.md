Multi-Target Estimation via Tensor Decomposition
for Beyond Diagonal RIS-Aided Bistatic Sensing
Kenneth Ben´ıcio, Andr´e L. F. de Almeida, Fazal-E Asim, Bruno Sokal,
Gabor Fodor, Behrooz Makki, and A. Lee Swindlehurst
Abstract—We investigate the performance of beyond-diagonal
reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (BD-RIS) for bistatic MIMO
multi-target sensing using a two-stage tensor Doppler-delay-angle
estimation (TenDAE). The first stage solves a Kronecker sum
approximation (KSA) with a rank equal to the number of targets.
The second stage employs a nested tensor factorization estimation
(NTFE) that exploits the inherent multidimensional structure
via two tensor decompositions that are solved in parallel. The
first employs a PARAFAC decomposition to extract the targets’
angles, and the second uses a nested PARAFAC decomposition to
find the targets’ delay and Doppler parameters. This two-stage
approach decouples acquisition of the angles and delays/Dopplers
using either alternating least squares or a higher-order singular
value decomposition, followed by a high-resolution subspace
technique, such as ESPRIT. We further compare the performance
of a BD-RIS with a classical diagonal RIS. For the latter, we
solve a Khatri-Rao sum approximation problem rather than
the KSA due to the specific structure of the received signal.
Notably, our NTFE framework remains blind to the underlying
RIS architecture while simultaneously estimating all targets with
minimal sensing resources. Additionally, we show that employing
a nested-PARAFAC decomposition enables the decoupling of the
delay-Doppler and angle domains. We also derive the Cram´er-
Rao lower bound to further assess the performance of the Ten-
DAE framework. Finally, we numerically evaluate the solutions
presented in this paper and demonstrate their efficiency in terms
of RMSE compared with state-of-the-art approaches.
Index Terms—beyond diagonal reconfigurable surfaces, bistatic
sensing, multi-target estimation, tensor decomposition.
I. INTRODUCTION
O
VER the past decade, reconfigurable intelligent sur-
face (RIS) technology has emerged as a transforma-
tive approach for dynamically shaping wireless propagation
environments for enhanced coverage, spectral efficiency, and
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) [1], [2]. A typical passive RIS
consists of a planar array of reflecting elements whose phase
shifts can be individually tuned [3]. However, optimal tuning
requires estimation of the cascaded channel using pilot signals
reflected by the RIS under a prescribed training protocol.
Several works have addressed this problem [4]–[7]. More
recently, interest has shifted to leveraging passive RIS to en-
hance sensing and localization at the sensing transmitter (ST),
motivating integrated sensing and communications (ISAC)
architectures that improve both communication and sensing
performance [8], [9]. Recent work has also explored novel
“beyond diagonal” RIS (BD-RIS) architectures that introduce
controllable inter-element coupling, yielding a generally non-
diagonal phase-shift matrix [10]–[12]. The resulting additional
degrees of freedom can improve performance compared to
classical diagonal RIS designs [13], [14]. beyond-diagonal
RIS (BD-RIS) modeling and hardware architectures were
first investigated in [15]. Following [15], we adopt a fully-
connected BD-RIS model and focus on its use for multi-target
parameter estimation; related applications to estimation and
beamforming have also been considered in [16]–[19].
BD-RIS architectures for sensing/communication co-design
have recently been proposed. In [16], a BD-RIS-aided dual-
function radar-communication system is optimized to im-
prove both communication and sensing performance. A fully-
connected BD-RIS is considered in [17] for ISAC, demonstrat-
ing throughput gains while maintaining sensing gains relative
to a diagonal RIS. The work in [18] addresses parameter
estimation and channel prediction by iteratively estimating
decoupled BD-RIS channels using a Tucker decomposition,
yielding low-complexity estimates that initialize a prediction
stage. Finally, [19] proposes a block Tucker decomposition to
improve estimation relative to conventional least squares (LS)
methods and reduce training overhead.
Building on these advancements, our previous work has ad-
dressed parameter estimation in diagonal-RIS-assisted (single-
connected) multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) sensing by
exploiting the multidimensional structure of the received signal
at the ST [20]–[23]. In particular, we proposed a two-stage
nested tensor approach, followed by estimation of the signal
parameters via rotational invariance techniques (ESPRIT)-
based extraction of delay, Doppler, and angle parameters for
a single target in [22], [23].
In this paper, we study multi-target estimation in BD-
RIS-assisted networks. Our approach first estimates the rank-
K Kronecker sum approximation (KSA) of the received
signal, and then solves two third-order tensor models in
parallel to jointly recover each target’s delay, Doppler, and
angle parameters. The proposed tensor Doppler-delay and
angle estimation (TenDAE) framework consists of two stages.
In the first stage, a KSA is employed to obtain low-rank
subspace estimates of the effective channel. In the second,
an nested tensor factorization estimation (NTFE) procedure
jointly estimates the tensor factors that encapsulate the target
parameters. The parameters of interest are recovered through a
an extraction step using state-of-the-art estimation techniques.
We also discuss identifiability and uniqueness conditions, and
compare the fully connected BD-RIS with its single-connected
(diagonal) counterpart. We also compare the performance of
our proposed algorithms with state-of-the-art methods, e.g.,
[24]. Our results highlight the potential of fully-connected

enabling efficient tensor-based processing.
The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
First, we show that target estimation using the signal
reflected by a fully-connected BD-RIS can be recast as a rank-
K KSA that can be solved via a single singular value decom-
position (SVD) to estimate the overall channel subspace. We
then exploit (i) a third-order PARAFAC model to estimate
the angle parameters and (ii) a third-order nested PARAFAC
model to estimate the delay–Doppler parameters in parallel.
Second, we introduce the TenDAE framework, which em-
ploys a modular two-stage process that combines subspace
estimation with tensor fitting to achieve high accuracy and
reduce computational complexity. In TenDAE, tensor models
are solved via alternating least squares (ALS), with the final
parameters extracted using ESPRIT. We further extend the
higher-order singular value decomposition (HOSVD)-based
single-target solution from our previous work [22] to a general
multi-target solution integrated into the TenDAE framework.
Our approach estimates all targets simultaneously, demon-
strates robustness to noise, and requires minimal sensing
resources.
Third, we propose a nested-PARAFAC tensor model that
decouples the Doppler and angle domains with very low
computational cost. This decoupling enhances estimation per-
formance in both domains by enabling accurate acquisition of
high-speed target data without compromising angle estimation
due to Doppler-related imperfections.
Fourth, we compare fully-connected BD-RIS and diagonal
(single-connected) RIS architectures. In particular, we show
how the KSA model induced by BD-RIS reduces to a Khatri-
Rao sum approximation (KRSA) model under a diagonal RIS,
and we discuss the corresponding identifiability implications.
Fifth, we study identifiability and uniqueness conditions
for the proposed tensor models and derive system design
guidelines that guarantee parameter recovery. In addition, we
provide sufficient uniqueness conditions based on Kruskal’s
criteria for Nth-order PARAFAC models.
Sixth, we derive the Fisher information matrix (FIM) and
associated Cram´er-Rao lower bound (CRLB) for the proposed
scenario. The resulting bound characterizes the achievable es-
timation performance for the delay, Doppler, complex channel
coefficients, and angle parameters of all K targets.
Finally, we present numerical results demonstrating the
advantage of the proposed tensor-based estimators over recent
state-of-the-art methods [24], [25], highlighting performance
trade-offs and their proximity to the derived CRLB.
This work is organized as follows. Section II presents
notation and tensor decomposition preliminaries. Section III
presents the system model and formulates the KSA problem
for fully-connected BD-RIS and the KRSA problem for diago-
nal RIS. Section IV details the received signal model. Section
V develops the proposed parameter estimation method for
multi-target sensing based on subspace estimation. Section VI
discusses identifiability and uniqueness and their implications
for system design. Section VII derives the CRLB. Section IX
presents numerical results, and Section X concludes the paper.

A. Complexity Evaluation
In Fig. 6, we assess the computational complexity reported
in Table I as a function of key system parameters. This
analysis highlights how the proposed algorithms scale with
respect to the problem dimensions and clarifies the associated
performance–complexity trade-offs intrinsic to the proposed
scenario. To provide a conservative upper bound for the
complexity of the iterative approach, we consider a worst-case
scenario where convergence is achieved at a fixed threshold of
ALSiter = 10 iterations for both Fig. 6a and Fig. 6b. Typically,
our iterative solution requires 2 to 5 iterations per tensor fit.
Convergence requires more iterations at lower SNR and fewer
at higher SNR, as ALS convergence is strongly dependent on
the noise level. Fig. 6a shows the computational complexity
as a function of the number of targets K. As expected, the
overall complexity increases with K for all methods. NTFE
via ALS exhibits the highest complexity for large K, since it
jointly estimates the delay and Doppler parameters of all K
targets via two parallel tensor decompositions. This additional
cost reflects the increased estimation capability enabled by the
proposed framework, as it allows us to obtain information for
all K targets simultaneously without additional processing.
Furthermore, Fig. 6b illustrates the complexity as a function
of the number of BD-RIS reflecting elements N. This result
emphasizes the strong dependence of the first stage KSA
solution on N, despite its inability to determine the target
parameters on its own. This behavior is consistent with the
quadratic increase in complexity with N for KSA reported
in Table I. Both NTFE algorithms scale linearly with N,
although the HOSVD-based solution tends to have a higher
complexity due to the sequential SVDs that scales with N.
Overall, these results confirm that the proposed algorithms
offer a flexible trade-off between estimation quality and com-
putational complexity, enabling system designers to tailor the
sensing architecture to the available processing resources. For
example, one could use our modular approach to the proposed
scenario to combine the ALS and HOSVD solutions in a way
that reduces the overall complexity of the estimation process,
depending on the available sensing resources.

suffers a performance loss of approximately 5 dB in delay
and Doppler estimation compared to the BD-RIS.

velocities make it more difficult to obtain precise Doppler
parameter estimates. Conversely, our framework exhibits lower
sensitivity to range variations, yielding more reliable estimates
for the delay parameter.
X. CONCLUSION
We have proposed TenDAE, a two-stage framework for
multi-target parameter estimation that leverages the intrinsic
multidimensional structure of the sensing signal received from
a BD-RIS to solve tensor approximation problems involving
either closed-form HOSVD or iterative ALS-based solutions.

The proposed approach decomposes the sensing task into a
sequence of modular stages, namely KSA and NTFE, fol-
lowed by high-resolution parameter extraction, enabling joint
estimation of delay, Doppler, and angle parameters with low-
complexity processing. By exploiting the Kronecker structure
induced by fully connected BD-RIS architectures, the TenDAE
enables unique estimation of the arrival and departure angles
at the BD-RIS. Our results demonstrated that TenDAE consis-
tently outperforms state-of-the-art benchmarks in RMSE while
exhibiting favorable, controllable trade-offs among estimation
accuracy, computational complexity, and sensing resources. In
particular, the modular KSA-NTFE decomposition provides a
flexible design paradigm, allowing system designers to balance
performance and complexity according to the available sensing
resources. Also, numerical results show that our proposed solu-
tion outperforms current state-of-the-art methods [24], [25] in
terms of RMSE of the estimated parameters. Future work will
investigate alternative realizations of the TenDAE framework
that avoid explicit right-filtering of the BD-RIS phase-shift
matrix, thereby mitigating the computational burden associated
with the initial LS step.
