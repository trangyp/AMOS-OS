Abstract. We construct minimal resolutions of pushforwards of struc-
ture sheaves of toric substacks of smooth toric stacks by line bundles as
strong deformation retracts of cellular resolutions constructed by Han-
lon, Hicks and Lazarev. We also provide a canonical and combinatorial
description of the differentials of such minimal resolutions.
Two key
ingredients are the homological perturbation lemma and the Moore-
Penrose inverses.

1. Introduction
1.1. Motivation. Resolutions of diagonals play an important role in the
study of algebraic varieties. In the case of toric varieties and stacks, this
picture is particularly well understood. On the level of cohomology, Ful-
ton and Sturmfels [15] gave a combinatorial formula for the cohomology
classes of toric subvarieties in toric varieties in terms of the fan displace-
ment rule, which in turn has applications to toric mirror symmetry [7,16].
More recently, inspired by homological mirror symmetry for toric varieties
(see [6, 13]), Hanlon, Hicks and Lazarev [17] constructed canonical cellular
resolutions of pushforwards of structure sheaves of toric substacks of smooth
toric stacks by using a certain class of line bundles on the ambient toric stack,
namely the Bondal-Thomsen collection. Later, Borisov and the author [8]
provided an equivalent formulation and a simplified proof of their result; see
also [4] for a generalization in the context of commutative algebra.
In general, however, the Hanlon-Hicks-Lazarev resolution (HHL resolu-
tion for short) can be far from minimal. There are algorithmic methods

The goal of this paper is to address this problem. We give a canonical

result can be seen as an optimal refinement of the construction in [17]. It is
also worth mentioning that our proof does not rely on any form of mirror
symmetry. As a corollary, our construction yields a purely algebraic proof
of the Betti number formula of Favero-Sapronov.

1.4. Strategy of the proof. We briefly explain the strategy of the proof.
One of the main tools used in this paper is the homological perturbation
lemma. Roughly speaking, it addresses the following question.
Question 1.3. Let (H•, dH) and (C•, dC) be chain complexes. Suppose that
(H•, dH) is a strong deformation retract of (C•, dC), with homotopy map h
on (C•, dC). If we introduce a “small” perturbation δ of the differential dC,
so that the perturbed differential is dC,∞= dC + δ, can one modify the
differential dH accordingly so as to obtain a new complex (H•, dH,∞) which
is again a strong deformation retract of the perturbed complex (C•, dC,∞)?
The homological perturbation lemma gives an explicit formula for the
perturbed differential dH,∞in terms of the original data.
In our situation, (C•, dC) is the associated graded complex of the HHL
resolution with respect to the natural filtration induced by the poset struc-
ture on the Bondal-Thomsen collection, and (H•, dH) is its homology com-
plex with trivial differentials. We then consider the perturbation given by
the difference between the original HHL differential and the differential on
the associated graded complex. With this choice, the perturbed complex
(C•, dC,∞) is precisely the HHL resolution, and the resulting perturbed re-
tract (H•, dH,∞) is the desired minimal resolution.
There is, however, an important point in applying the homological pertur-
bation lemma: we have not yet specified the homotopy map h on (C•, dC).
In principle, the output of the perturbation lemma depends on this choice.
Thus a key point is to find a canonical homotopy contracting the associated
graded complex of the HHL resolution onto its homology complex.

Moore-Penrose inverse provides exactly such a choice.
The advantage of
using the Moore-Penrose inverse is that it does not require any auxiliary
choices. As a result, the construction in this paper is canonical in the sense
that the differentials are completely determined by the Bondal stratification
itself.
Moreover, the combinatorial nature of the Moore-Penrose inverse
allows us to give a purely combinatorial formula for these differentials.
The homotopy h used to construct the strong deformation retract is not
unique. If one drops the requirement of canonicity, it is sometimes possible to
choose homotopy maps of a simpler form, for example when a perfect Morse
matching on the Bondal stratification is available. See the discussion at the
beginning of Section 4 and Remark 5.8 for general discussions on alternative
choices of h, and see the end of Section 6 for an explicit example.
Our use of Moore-Penrose inverses is strongly inspired by the work of
Eagon-Miller-Ordog [12]. It was brought to the author’s attention by Ezra
Miller that the homological perturbation lemma also appears in the work of
Amelotte and Briggs [1].
1.5. Future directions. Minimal free resolutions have been studied ex-
tensively in commutative algebra in various settings; see, for example, [1,
2, 18, 19].
It seems plausible that the approach developed in this paper,
namely, combining the homological perturbation lemma with Moore-Penrose
inverses, or more generally with suitable splittings or homotopies, can be ap-
plied in broader settings. For instance, starting from any kinds of cellular
resolutions, one may hope to use the perturbation lemma together with
Moore-Penrose inverses to minimize them while preserving the underlying
combinatorics of the cellular resolutions. We leave a detailed investigation
of this direction to future work.
1.6. Organization of the paper. The paper is organized as follows. In
Section 2 we review the construction of the Bondal stratification and the
Hanlon-Hicks-Lazarev resolution.
In Section 3 we review the theory of
Moore-Penrose inverses and the homological perturbation lemma. In Sec-
tion 4 we apply the homological perturbation lemma to construct a strong
deformation retract of the HHL resolution, and show that it is indeed a
minimal resolution. In Section 5 we give a combinatorial description of the
differentials in this minimal resolution.
Finally, in Section 6 we give an
explicit example to illustrate how to compute the minimal resolutions in
practice.
