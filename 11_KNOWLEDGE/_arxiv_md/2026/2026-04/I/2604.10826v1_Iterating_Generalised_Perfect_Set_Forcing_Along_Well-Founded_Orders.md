MIRNA DŽAMONJA, BY-NAME LOGIQUE CONSULT, PARIS
Abstract. Vladimir Kanovei [20] developed the technique of geometric
iteration and used it to prove that the perfect set forcing can be iterated
with countable supports along any partial order, while preserving ℵ1. In
[8] we considered a generalised perfect set forcing with respect to a filter
on a cardinal κ satisfying κ<κ = κ, which we denoted P(F), and proved
that its iteration with supports of size ≤κ along any ordinal preserves
cardinals up and including κ+.
We show that there is a version of the geometric iteration technique
that applies to P(F), to yield that for κ satisfying κ<κ = κ, the forcing
P(F) can be iterated with supports of size ≤κ along any well-founded
partial order, while preserving cardinals up and including κ+.
1. Introduction and Motivation
Perfect set forcing is a forcing notion whose conditions are perfect subtrees
of 2<ω (or more generally, of ω<ω). A perfect tree is a nonempty tree with
no terminal nodes in which every node has two incompatible extensions;
forcing with such a tree adds a new real (an infinite branch through the
tree) that is “generic” in a strong topological sense.
Similarly to Cohen
forcing, which adds a very “free” generic real, or random forcing, which adds
a measure-theoretically generic real, perfect set forcing adds a special real:
one of a minimal Turing degree. This forcing notion is closely related to
Albert Muchnik’s solution of the Post problem in [23]. It was formulated as
a forcing notion by Gerald Sacks in [25].
For a set theorist, the importance of perfect set forcing lies in its ability
to control the structure of the continuum while preserving many regularity
properties. It keeps the cardinal invariants of the continuum low, while in-
creasing the value of the continuum itself. Perfect set forcing plays a central
role in minimality results for Turing degrees and degrees of constructibil-
ity. Iterations or products of perfect set forcing are often used to obtain
consistency results about the size of the continuum, the existence of certain
pathological sets, or the behaviour of definable sets of reals, all while keep-
ing the forcing “mild” enough not to destroy global features of the universe.
Over a model of CH, it can be iterated with countable support ω2 many
times while preserving both ℵ1 and ℵ2. Various combinatorial properties of
the resulting model are known, see [4] and [11].
One would think that the idea of perfect set forcing can be naturally gen-
eralised to higher cardinals, giving rise to the notion of perfect set forcing at
κ, or more generally κ-perfect forcing. This actually turns out to be quite dif-
ficult, with several wrong tries on the subject available in the literature. The
reason for this is the emerging evidence of the impossibility of generalising to

3. The difficulties of the generalisation to κ > ℵ0
A main difficulty of the generalisation is the lack of the convenient to-
pological structure. We may consider K in a topology given by the initial
segments : a basic open set is a set of the form {ρ : s ⊆ρ} for s ∈k. In
particular, this is a (< κ)-box topology, rather than the ordinary product
topology. In general K is neither metric nor compact in this topology, or
in any other topology that respects the order on κ to the extent to let us
identify perfect subsets of K as branches of k.
For (ζ, <) ∈κPO we may define the topological space Kζ as follows.
Its set of elements is the product (we may also work with the lexicographic
product, but it turns out to be irrelevant) of copies of K along ζ, so the set
Πi∈ζK ordered by the ordinary co-ordinatwise order. The set Kζ may be
given various topologies, for example the ordinary Tychonov product of the
topologies on K. As it turns out, unlike in the countable case for the perfect
set forcing, there are no useful topological properties of Kζ we may exploit.
We explain that now.
An important ingredient of the geometric iteration approach is that the
Cantor space D satisfies that its every countable power is homeomorphic to
D. The proof of this relies on the compactness of D. In particular, whatever
topology we give to K, we do not have the compactness and do not obtain
that K is homeomorphic to its powers of size ≤κ. We may try to ignore the
topological properties of Kζ and work only with the combinatorial properties.
As is known from descriptive set theory generalised to uncountable regular
cardinals, for example [10], not much of the ordinary descriptive set theory
carries through.
Notably because there is no metric available.
What is
happening here fits that pattern.
We may optimistically try to continue, saying that the proofs in [20] use
topology to be able to obtain applications about continuous functions and
other subjects coming from descriptive set theory, which is not our concern.
So we may try concentrate only on the properties relevant to the combin-
atorics of forcing. We immediately arrive to a new difficulty, which is that
what this is starting to look like is reducing the arbitrary iterations to the
product with ≤κ supports of P(F).
However, this product does not in
general preserve κ+, even in the case κ = ℵ0, see [8, Th 5.6].

have also shown [8, Th 8.1+Cor. 8.2] that the iterations with ≤κ-supports

9. Concluding Remarks
Throughout this paper we have considered κ an infinite cardinal satisfying
κ<κ = κ and the filter F∗of its co-bounded sets. We have shown that the
partial order P(F∗) can be iterated with supports of size ≤κ along any
well-founded order I while preserving cardinals up and including κ+.
We have shown that P(F∗) adds a generic function from κ to κ which
dominates modulo a club every such function from the ground model. In
consequence, the structure of κκ modulo domination on a club can be made
to look like an arbitrary well-founded order in an extension which preserves
cardinals up to and including κ+.
We have discussed the difficulties in preserving κ++, which already appear
in contexts much simpler than the one considered here.
