Abstract. Characterizing derived equivalences between algebras via combinatorial structures has recently
become a popular topic. In this paper, we study admissible fractional Brauer graph algebras, a new subclass
of self-injective special biserial algebras, and provide several easily checkable combinatorial invariants for
derived equivalences between them. In particular, we show that these algebras can be viewed as repetitive
algebras and r-fold trivial extensions of gentle algebras.
1. Introduction
Derived equivalence is a classical topic in the representation theory of algebras. Two derived equiva-
lent algebras share similar homological properties. By [28, 21], a fundamental method for determining
whether two algebras are derived equivalent is to find a suitable tilting complex and compute its endo-
morphism algebra. Although this approach always works in theory, in practice it often involves extensive
calculations. Therefore, we generally hope that within special classes of algebras, derived equivalence can
be characterized by certain complete invariants that are easy to verify.
In recent years, the study of certain classical finite-dimensional algebras using combinatorial structures
and geometric models has become increasingly popular. For these algebras, derived equivalence can often
be completely described by combinatorial or geometric data. For example, derived equivalence of gentle
algebras can be determined by their geometric models [8, 3, 20], and derived equivalence of Brauer graph
algebras (abbr. BGAs) can be determined by their ribbon graphs and multiplicity functions [4, 5, 27].
In order to study self-injective algebras via a similar combinatorial approach, [22] generalized BGAs to
fractional BGAs. In general, fractional BGAs are neither special biserial nor of tame representation type
[36]. Consequently, in [24], the authors considered a subclass, namely fractional BGAs of type MS on
which the Nakayama automorphism acts admissibly; these algebras are self-injective and special biserial.
Here, “MS” stands for “multiserial and self-injective” [17]. In this article, for simplicity, we refer to such
algebras as admissible fractional BGAs (abbr. AFBGAs). As in the case of BGAs, AFBGs are defined
from combinatorial data — a ribbon graph Γ equipped with a (fractional) multiplicity function m =

(see Definition 2.3 for details) — called an admissible fractional Brauer graph (abbr. AFBG). In fact, we
will prove in Proposition 2.7 that AFBGAs are uniquely determined by their AFBGs.
Similar to the case of gentle algebras [32] and BGAs [5], AFBGAs are expected to be closed under
derived equivalence (which is left as a conjecture in [37]). In particular, the representation-finite part [24]
and the tilting-discrete part [37] of such algebras are closed under derived equivalence.
Therefore, in this paper, we aim to provide some easily checkable invariants for derived equivalences
within this class of algebras with combinatorial structures. The proofs of these invariants differ from the
classical case of BGAs in [4, 5], because the existence of a non-trivial Nakayama automorphism makes

AFBGA A is associated with a BGA Ared (called the reduced form of A), our strategy is to use covering
techniques on derived categories [6], which were used in [7] to classify representation-finite self-injective

Notation
Throughout we assume that k is an algebraically closed field and all algebras considered are k-algebras.
Unless stated otherwise, all modules will be finitely generated left modules. Furthermore, we say that A
is a bound quiver algebra, if A  kQ/I, where Q is a finite quiver and I is an admissible ideal in the path

Recall that for an algebra A, the derived category D(A) is the localization of homotopy category K(A) by

2. Admissible fractional Brauer graph algebras
2.1. Ribbon graphs.
Ribbon graphs combinatorially encode the structure of oriented surfaces with boundary (see for example
in [27, Section 1.1]). A key feature of ribbon graphs is the cyclic ordering of (half-)edges at each vertex,
which captures the orientation data of the underlying surface. We begin this section by recalling their
formal definition.
Definition 2.1. A ribbon graph is a tuple Γ = (V, H, s, ι, ρ), where
(1) V (also denoted by V(Γ)) is a finite set whose elements are called vertices;
(2) H (also denoted by E(Γ)) is a set whose elements are called half-edges;
(3) s : H →V is a functions;
(4) ι : H →H is an involution without fixed points;
(5) ρ : H →H is a permutation whose cycles correspond to the sets Hv := s−1(v), v ∈V.

2.4. Determination by admissible fractional Brauer graphs.
In this section, we prove that, similarly to the case of BGA [5, Lemma 3.1], an AFBGA is uniquely
determined by its associated AFBG.
Proposition 2.7. Let A be an indecomposable self-injective special biserial k-algebra admitting a presen-
tation (Q, I) of the form given in the definition of AFBGAs, and let (Γ, d) be the associated AFBG. If (Γ, d)
is not one of the exceptional BGs described in [5, Lemma 3.1], then the underlying graph Γ is independent
of the choice of the presentation (Q, I).
Proof. While the structure of projective A-modules can be directly deduced from (Γ, d), we aim to show
that the AFBG (Γ, d) itself can be reconstructed from the module category of A, independently of the
specific presentation kQ/I. Without loss of generality, we may assume that A is not radical square zero.
Otherwise, A is a self-injective Nakayama algebra, and all its indecomposable projective modules are
uniserial. In this case, its AFBG (Γ, d) is uniquely determined by the orbits of simple modules under the
Nakayama automorphism.
Suppose that A admits two presentations kQ/I  kQ′/I′, both satisfying the definition of fms-BGAs.
This induces a Morita equivalence between kQ/I-mod and kQ′/I′-mod. Since the ideals I and I′ can be
chosen to be admissible, we may identify Q and Q′. Consequently, we obtain a bijection between:
• Primitive idempotents of the two presentations,
• Simple modules S i over kQ/I and kQ′/I′,
• Edges of the ribbon graph Γ and Γ′.

4. Invariants under derived equivalences
In the case of BGAs, [5] and [27] provide a complete derived invariant characterizing derived equiva-
lences between BGAs. In this section, we study invariants under derived equivalences for AFBGAs. The
situation is analogous to that of BGAs.
By [31, Corollary 2.13], two local algebras are derived equivalent if and only if they are Morita equiva-
lent. By Proposition 2.7, AFBGAs are completely determined by their associated AFBGs, except for two
exceptional local cases. Therefore, we restrict our discussion to the non-local case. We also remark that
the following theorem also holds for infinite-dimensional AFBGAs.

Theorem 4.1. Let A and B be non-local AFBGAs associated with AFBGs (Γ, d) and (Γ′, d′), respectively.
If A and B are derived equivalent, then the following statements hold:
(1) The associated BGAs Ared and Bred are derived equivalent;
(2) The following conditions are satisfied:
• Γ and Γ′ have the same number of vertices and edges;
• (Γ, d) and (Γ′, d′) have the same multisets of vertex multiplicities;
• either both Γ and Γ′ are bipartite, or neither is.
Proof. Denote by νA (resp. νB) the Nakayama automorphism of A (resp. B). By [15, 6], it is natural to
regard such locally bounded quiver algebras as spectroids. Since A and B are derived equivalent, it follows
from [28, Proposition 6.3] that there exists a tilting spectroid E for A such that E is equivalent to B as
categories. As both A and B are self-injective (Proposition 2.5), it follows from [2, Theorem 2.1] and
[1, Theorem A.4] that E is νA-stable. By the definition of AFBGAs, both ⟨νA⟩and ⟨νB⟩are finite cyclic
groups (or both isomorphic to Z). Moreover, since derived equivalent self-injective algebras have conjugate
Nakayama permutations ([35, Theorem 4.1]), we obtain ⟨νA⟩ ⟨νB⟩. Therefore, by [6, Theorem 3.5], the
reduced algebra Ared  A/⟨νA⟩is derived equivalent to Bred  B/⟨νB⟩.
For an AFBG (Γ, d), the ribbon graphs Γ and Γred have the same set of vertices. Moreover, by [5,
Proposition 4.5], the reduced ribbon graphs Γred and Γ′
red have the same number of vertices. It follows that
Γ and Γ′ have the same number of vertices. Since the number of edges of a ribbon graph corresponds to the
number of isomorphism classes of simple modules of the associated algebra, it follows that Γ and Γ′ have
the same number of edges. Assume that the (finite) cyclic group generated by the Nakayama automorphism
has order r, and that the multiset of vertex multiplicities of Γred is {m1, . . . , mn}. Then the multiset of vertex
multiplicities of Γ is given by {m1/r, . . . , mn/r}. Hence, by [5, Proposition 4.5], Γ and Γ′ have the same
multiset of vertex multiplicities. Finally, recall that a graph is bipartite if and only if it contains no odd
cycles ([10, Theorem 4.7]). If Γred is not bipartite, then since Γ →Γred is a covering of ribbon graphs
([19, 23]), any odd cycle in Γred lifts to an odd cycle in Γ. Hence Γ is not bipartite. By [4, 27], it follows
that either both or neither of Γ and Γ′ are bipartite.
□
We end this section with the following remarks.
Remark 4.2. Note that the invariants in the above theorem are not complete. For instance, in Example
3.5, the algebras T2(A) and T2(A′) satisfy all the invariants listed above. However, by [12, 14], T2(A) is 4-
domestic whereas T2(A′) is 2-domestic. Hence, T2(A) and T2(A′) are not stably equivalent, and therefore,
by [29], they are not derived equivalent.
Remark 4.3. We expect that a complete set of derived invariants for AFBGAs should extend Theorem 4.1
by incorporating information on the faces of the ribbon graph and their perimeters. Moreover, by [24,
Proposition 5.3], for derived equivalent AFBGAs, the ν−1(ρι)2-orbits on Γ and Γ′ also coincide. However,
this invariant still fails to distinguish the two non-derived equivalent algebras in Remark 4.2.
Therefore, unlike in the case of BGAs, the existence of a nontrivial Nakayama automorphism ν makes
the situation more subtle. Based on existing experience, a more refined analysis of the relationship between
the tubes in the stable Auslander–Reiten quiver of AFBGAs and the faces of the associated ribbon graph
will be required in future work.
