Anum Umer, Ivo M¨u¨ursepp, Member, IEEE, Muhammad Mahtab Alam, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—This paper investigates an uplink user equipment
(UE) location and orientation estimation problem in an indoor
rich-scattering environment (RSE) for a multiple-input-multiple-
output (MIMO) narrowband reconfigurable intelligent surfaces
(RIS)-assisted communication system. The localization problem
in RSE is challenging as the uplink pilot signal undergoes
multiple interactions with the RIS and dynamic scattering objects
(SOs). This paper proposes an approach where base station (BS)
adaptively senses the environment with the help of RIS. Based
on this sensing, it sequentially designs RIS configuration, BS
beamforming and UE beamforming vectors, using the sequence
of pilot transmissions from the UE to the BS, with an objective
of progressively focusing them onto the UE. Towards this end, we
train a bidirectional long-short term memory (biLSTM) network
based controller to capture the temporal dependencies between
measurements to first adaptively sense the RSE and then design
RIS, BS and UE beamforming vectors to localize the UE. We
evaluate the proposed approach under various RSE conditions
such as various distributed RIS installations, varying number
of randomly moving SOs and sensing RIS elements. Simulation
results illustrate that it effectively enables adaptive sensing to
achieve low localization error with robustness in various RSEs.
Index Terms—reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS), local-
ization, long-short term memory, sensing, channel estimation,
rich scattering, dynamic wireless environment.
I. INTRODUCTION
Achieving reliable localization in rich-scattering environ-
ments (RSEs) dominated by dense multipath and rapid changes
has become increasingly important as many modern appli-
cations depend on precise and resilient positioning [1], [2].
Conventional approaches often fail in such settings, especially
indoors, where infrastructure constraints and rich scattering
further complicate signal behavior. The need is amplified in
emerging 6G networks that span microwave through terahertz
and optical bands, where indoor environments exhibit strong
scattering [3]. The user equipment (UE) localization becomes
challenging in narrowband indoor environments with rich
scattering multipath propagation, where the UE pilot signal
is reflected by surrounding objects and reaches the base
station (BS) through multiple paths, after several reflections.

This paper explores the role of reconfigurable intelligent
surfaces (RIS) as an affordable substitute for large antenna
arrays for localizing the UE in RSEs. The RIS use numerous
passive low power elements to tune reflected wave phases and
regulate the propagation environment [4]–[6]. These charac-
teristics enable them to act as intelligent anchors or enhance
existing systems to mitigate interference, and directional issues
to support the localization requirements for autonomous plat-
forms, robotics, immersive applications, smart manufacturing,
and traffic management [1], [2], [7]–[9]. It is viewed as a
key enabler for 6G localization because it can maintain a
dependable reflected link when the direct path is weak or
obstructed and can introduce additional anchor points without
the need for extra Radio Frequency (RF) chains [2], [9]. Under
dynamic RSE conditions, repeated interactions with objects
and RIS elements create composite reflections with diverse
angle-of-arrival (AoA) and polarizations [10]–[12], leading to
nonlinear behavior far from free or quasi free space models [9].
In these settings, RIS supply controlled perturbations, whereas
moving objects or humans, referred to as scattering objects
(SOs), introduce uncontrolled, time varying effects, yielding
a nonlinear system shaped jointly by RIS configurations and
environmental evolution [13]. It has been proven in [14]
that with adapative sensing accurate UE localization can be
achieved using large antenna arrays at the UE and BS, with
the RIS acting as a virtual anchor in RSEs.
In this work we consider the uplink localization problem
in RSEs in which a UE repeatedly transmits the known pilot
signals and BS determines the location and orientation of the
UE via received pilots. Since the wireless channel depends
on the UE location, considered scenario can be viewed as
an inverse mapping problem, where the goal is to infer the
UE location from the channel realizations, which are obtained
by estimating the received pilot signals. The received signal
at the BS is reflected off multiple RIS and the scattering
environment. The main idea is to exploit the multiple paths
such that the BS uses the received measurements and senses
the environment via the dedicated sensing RIS elements and
adaptively reconfigures the RIS, BS and UE beamforming
vectors over multiple time slots, with an objective of localizing
the UE with maximum accuracy. The adaptive design approach
is investigated in diverse multipath RSE scenarios and the
robustness and applicability of the approach across varying
RSEs conditions is evaluated.

A. Related Work
Prior studies on UE localization assisted by RIS can be
broadly classified into model based and learning based ap-
proaches [9]. Model based methods optimize the RIS configu-
ration through analytical formulations, typically by minimizing
the Cram´er-Rao lower bound (CRLB) of the UE position
[15]–[19], providing useful theoretical performance bounds.
These approaches rely on explicit relationships between the
UE location and measurements such as time-of-arrival (ToA)
or AoA to construct the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM).
However, such formulations become impractical in multipath
dominated RSE, where reliable path separation and association
are difficult, particularly in narrowband indoor scenarios with
unknown SOs. Extensions including Bayesian FIM and robust
Bayesian Cram´er-Rao lower bound (BCRLB) incorporate prior
uncertainty in channel or geometric parameters [20], and
have enabled adaptive RIS beamforming schemes exploiting
location uncertainty [21]. Nevertheless, these methods often
neglect scattering effects, limiting performance in dynamic
environments. Recent works instead exploit multipath structure
for improved estimation, including Bayesian multi target tra-
jectory tracking [22], cooperative RIS assisted 3D positioning
with low complexity estimator [23], and tensor based inte-
grated sensing and communication framework supporting joint
multi user communication and near and far field localization
[24].
In contrast, model free localization techniques have gained
increasing attention with advances in machine learning. Fin-
gerprinting based method maps received signal measurements
under multiple RIS configurations to UE locations [7], while
deep reinforcement learning has been applied to optimize
RIS configurations for improved localization accuracy [25].
Despite their effectiveness, these approaches typically rely
on fixed or predefined configurations and therefore do not
fully exploit adaptive RIS reconfiguration. Adaptive sensing
has recently emerged as a powerful paradigm across wireless
applications, including initial beam alignment in millimeter
wave systems [26]–[28], precoder and combiner design for
reciprocal MIMO channels [29], RIS assisted beam tracking
[30], and localization in single path and multipath environ-
ments as well as with adaptive RIS codebooks [31]–[33].
These works commonly employ recurrent neural network
(RNN) based architectures that iteratively adapt beamforming
using previously observed pilot signals, progressively refining
sensing toward a desired objective. However, the resulting
models are generally not directly generalizable to complex
RSEs [14]. Recent studies address this limitation through
learning driven approaches designed for RSEs, including joint
fingerprinting and feature extraction networks for 3D localiza-
tion [34], self adaptive RIS optimization via learned surrogate
models [35], RNN based codebook driven joint RIS phase
and position estimation [36], and adaptive sensing strategies
that iteratively refine RIS configurations using pilot feedback
[14]. Building on [14], this paper investigates adaptive BS
and UE beamforming design in MIMO RIS-assisted RSEs
with randomly moving SOs, targeting joint UE location and
orientation estimation. The proposed approach is evaluated
under previously unexplored scenarios, including distributed
RIS deployments, varying numbers of mobile scatterers, and
variable sensing elements, and is analytically benchmarked
against conventional BCRLB based optimization methods.
B. Main Contributions
In this paper we develop an adaptive learning based local-
ization approach for distributed RIS assisted MIMO networks
operating in RSEs, where both the propagation medium and
network geometry are dynamic due to randomly moving SOs.
We propose a learning based approach to jointly design RIS
configurations as well as BS and UE beamforming vectors
with the objective of simultaneously estimating UE position
and orientation. Unlike conventional free-space or sparsely
scattered localization methods that rely on AoA extraction or
geometric ray tracing, dynamic RSEs exhibit dense and highly
coupled multipath propagation, where localization information
is embedded in complex spatio temporal scattering patterns.
To address this challenge, we exploit sequential learning to
capture long range dependencies in the received pilot measure-
ments and environmental sensing measurements, enabling the
network to learn the evolving relationship between scattering
dynamics, RIS configurations, and localization performance.
The proposed approach employs a bidirectional long-short
term memory (biLSTM) based architecture to model temporal
correlations induced by dynamic scattering, while adaptively
refining RIS configuration, BS and UE beamforming vectors
to progressively enhance localization accuracy. The main con-
tributions of this work are summarized as follows.
1) In a distributed RIS-assisted uplink MIMO network op-
erating in dynamic RSE, we study an adaptive sensing
based localization approach for jointly and adaptively
designing RIS configuration vectors, BS beamforming
vectors, and UE beamforming vectors based on past
measurements at BS and sensed environmental scattering.
The proposed approach enables simultaneous estimation
of UE position and orientation while accounting for
scattering from randomly moving SOs.
2) We provide analytical benchmarking of the proposed
learning based adaptive sensing approach using BCRLB
based modeling and optimization approach. This analysis
quantifies the performance gap between the proposed data
driven approach and conventional estimation theoretic
limits in RSEs.
3) We conduct a comprehensive performance evaluation of
the proposed approach under a variety of RSEs condi-
tions, including different deployment configurations of
distributed RIS arrays, varying numbers of randomly
moving SOs, and different numbers of sensing RIS
elements. This study provides insights into the impact
of environmental complexity and sensing capability on
scattering estimation, localization performance and the
robustness of proposed approach.
4) The proposed framework yields interpretable results,
demonstrating that the joint sequential design of RIS
configurations, and BS and UE beamforming vectors
progressively enhances the quality of sensed multipath

II. SYSTEM MODEL AND PROBLEM FORMULATION
A. System Model
Consider the problem of uplink UE localization in an indoor
RSE comprising distributed RIS installation, a multi-antenna
UE, a multi-antenna BS and M SOs. The BS, at known
location, aims to estimate the location u = [ux, uy]T ∈R2 and
orientation α ∈[0,2π) of UE with the assistance of a uniform
linear array of RIS distributed installation on the periphery of
the communication environment, as shown in Figure 1 (top
view). RSE is modeled using the physics based end-to-end
channel model [10], where the environment is treated as a
regularly shaped enclosure whose dimensions are large relative
to the wavelength scale λ.
The UE broadcasts pilot signals and BS can adapt the
received pilots by configuring the phase shifts of the RIS
elements via the RIS controller through the control signals. We
assume that M SOs are present in RSE and their movement in
the periphery is random, mimicking freely moving objects in
any indoor environment. The signals from UE experience mul-
tiple reflections and scatter off walls, multiple RIS elements,
and SOs in the propagation environment. This scattering and
the movement of SOs further alter the propagation path and
create a dynamic and complex fading wireless communication
environment [37].
We consider uplink transmission from a multi-antenna UE
with NUE antennas and a single RF chain to a BS with NBS

B. End-to-End Channel Modeling
We assume the channel is constant over a coherence in-
terval spanning multiple time frames, with small-scale RSE
perturbations, such as slight SOs movements or multipath
changes, modeled as discrete-time structured fast-fading to
capture temporal correlations and enhance scattering and UE
localization. We consider an indoor RSE with low UE mo-
tion with coherence interval exceeding the sensing interval.
Unlike the linear BS–RIS–UE cascade in free space, RSE
involves complex multipath with multiple RIS elements and
dynamic SOs, rendering the channel highly nonlinear and
analytically intractable [37]. We employ a physics-based end-
to-end channel for RIS-parameterized RSE [10], modeling
wireless entities as configurable dipole assemblies. This ap-
proach realistically captures scattering effects while adhering
to fundamental electromagnetic principles, including space-
time causality, frequency dispersion, RIS phase-amplitude
coupling, mutual coupling, and long-range correlations.

IV. ANALYTICAL MODELING OF UE LOCALIZATION WITH
ENVIRONMENT SENSING IN RSE
Analytical modeling of the UE localization problem with
scattering estimation in RSE is a valuable benchmark. The
proposed learning driven approach addresses UE location and
orientation estimation problem in (10) by estimating scattering
in RSE in (7) and jointly optimizing the RIS configuration, BS
and UE beamforming vectors mapping {F(t)(·)} in (8) over
t time frames, with the goal of reducing localization error.
Computational complexity of an analytical optimization of this
multistage design would grow as the number of time frames
increases. For this reason, analytical modeling of this formu-
lation would need stage by stage greedy design of F(t)(·). In
addition, evaluating the exact RMSE is analytically intractable,
thus, such modeling approach would rely on optimizing a
tractable lower bound on RMSE instead.
In this section, we outline an analytical modeling approach
for UE localization in RSE. The approach is devised on similar
lines as in [20], [31]. Rather than learning a direct mapping
from observations to RIS configuration, the mapping function
in F(t)(·) is decomposed into two consecutive mappings: first
from the accumulated observations to the BCRLB, and then
from that bound to the RIS configuration vector. At every
time step, the posterior distribution of the UE location is
refined using previously received pilots. This updated posterior
is then used to recompute the conditional BCRLB. The RIS
configuration vector is finally selected so as to minimize this
conditional BCRLB.

V. PERFORMANCE EVALUATION
A. Simulation Setup
The simulation parameters are summarized in Table I. As in
Figure 1, in the system setup considered, the location of BS
and the various distributed RIS installations are known and
fixed. The UE locations u are generated uniformly within the
rectangular periphery in the x-y plane with spacing λ/2. The
unknown orientation α of the UE is independently drawn from
a uniform distribution over the interval α ∈[0,2π). The pilot
signal x(t) is a deterministic scalar set to unity for all time
stages.
We consider M SOs present in the environment, each mov-
ing randomly within the rectangular region. In each simulation
realization, their positions are independently drawn from a
uniform distribution over the considered area. For simplicity,
the positions of all SOs are updated at the same time, while
their motion remains independent. The positions of the central
dipoles in each SOs cluster are recorded in each simulation
realization. The adjustable dipole parameters for transceivers,
the environment including the SOs, and the RIS such as
the resonance frequency (fres), the charge term (χ) and the
absorptive damping term (ΓL), are set as in Table I [10].
B. Proposed and Baseline Approaches
The proposed approach for UE location and orientation
estimation with environment sensing is implemented with the
parameters settings given in Table I. Bayesian Optimization
(BO) [41], [42] is used to tune the hyperparameters of the
biLSTM networks before training by selecting the number of
BiLSTM layers NL, the hidden dimension ds, the learning rate
η, and the batch size B to balance localization accuracy and
computational cost [14]. The network is trained with Adam
optimizer [43] on 2,136,000 samples over 200 epochs. The
training labels for scattering estimation biLSTM are of di-
mension 2M and for UE localization biLSTM are UE location
and orientation. After training, the model autonomously selects
a sequence of RIS configurations, BS and UE beamforming
vectors using sequential scattering estimates and received pilot
measurements at BS to accurately localize UEs within the
coverage area. The performance of the proposed approach is
evaluated against the following benchmarks.
Beamforming free biLSTM network [14]: The adaptive RIS
configuration is designed over T time frames while accounting
for scattering in the environment with the overall objective
of estimating location and orientation of the UE. The perfor-
mance of the approach is quantified with the RMSE loss in
location estimation as in (18) and scattering estimation as in
(19).
BCRLB based modeling and optimization: Here an analyt-
ical model is constructed using BCRLB as the optimization
metric at every time frame, as discussed in Section IV. At
each time instant t, the posterior distribution of the unknown
UE location and orientation and SOs is refined using the
pilot signals received previously, which in turn yields an
updated conditional BCRLB. Based on this bound, the RIS
configuration vector k is optimized to reduce the conditional
BCRLB. To determine posterior distributions, the 35λ ×55λ
service region in the x–y plane is discretized into 350 × 550
grid cells.
UE localization biLSTM only [31], [44]: This approach
relies on a UE localization biLSTM network only for both
UE location and orientation estimation and RIS configuration,
BS and UE beamforming vector design, while neglecting the
estimation of environmental scattering in RSE with scattering
estimation biLSTM network. The performance of the approach
is quantified with the RMSE loss in location estimation as in
(18).

tion design, the RIS density and spatial distribution are critical
in RSE, with geometry playing a greater role once sufficient
elements are deployed. Together, they play a significant role
in achieving the UE localization accuracy objective.
We investigate the influence of the number of sensing RIS
elements SRIS on scattering estimation performance using the
scattering estimation biLSTM, where the estimation accuracy
is quantified by the loss defined in (19), as illustrated in
Figure 7. The results show a consistent reduction in RMSE
as SRIS increases across all the time frame lengths considered
T. This behavior highlights the critical role of the density of
the sensing elements in capturing the spatial characteristics of
a RSE. A larger number of SRIS provides enhanced spatial
sampling of the impinging wavefronts, which improves mul-
tipath resolvability and allows the neural network to better
infer the underlying scattering. The decrease in RMSE with
increasing T for all values of SRIS, indicates that longer
temporal observation windows allow the scattering estimation
biLSTM network to better exploit temporal correlations in
the scattering process. However, for small SRIS, the reduction
in RMSE with increasing T is relatively gradual, suggesting
that limited spatial sampling constrains the effectiveness of
temporal averaging. In contrast, when SRIS is large, the com-
bined availability of rich spatial measurements and extended
temporal information results in a steeper RMSE decay and a
lower overall estimation error.
These improvements in scattering estimation accuracy trans-
late into more reliable RIS configuration design decisions,
as the learned scattering characteristics reflect the underlying
propagation environment more accurately. Consequently, en-
hanced scattering estimation enabled by larger SRIS leads to
improved RIS phase design and, ultimately, more precise UE
localization, since reliable characterization of the RSE allows
the adaptive RIS control and learning based proposed approach
to operate with lower ambiguity and greater robustness.

problem increases, resulting in a larger set of unknown pa-

conditions, multiple SOs generate densely overlapping mul-
tipath components with closely spaced delays and angles.
This strong multipath coupling reduces the separability of
individual scattering contributions and increases inter-path
interference, thereby elevating uncertainty in the learning-
based scattering estimation process in the proposed approach.
The resulting estimation errors propagate to subsequent UE
localization stages. This effect is especially pronounced at
low SNR, where noise further obscures the complex multipath
structure and amplifies the increase in RMSE with larger M.
Focusing on UE localization performance in Figure 8a, the
UE location RMSE is observed to increase significantly with
the number of SOs present. Although higher SNR improves
localization accuracy for all values of M, the relative per-
formance gap between different scattering densities remains
largely unchanged, indicating that increased SNR alone cannot
fully compensate for the loss of spatial resolution caused by
dense scattering. A similar trend is observed for UE orienta-
tion estimation in Figure 8b, where additional SOs introduce
greater angular ambiguity and a stronger correlation among
multipath components. The effect of M on SOs localization
is shown in Fig. 8c. As the number of scattering objects
increases, the RMSE of SOs location estimates also increases
due to mutual interference among the scattering paths and the
reduced identifiability of individual SOs contributions. While
increasing SNR mitigates this degradation to some extent,
performance improvements gradually saturate at moderate to
high SNR levels, revealing fundamental limitations imposed
by dense and dynamic RSE.
In general, this result demonstrates that although the pro-
posed approach remains robust in RSE, the number of SOs
plays a critical role in determining the achievable localization
accuracy. Moderate SOs densities provide a favorable bal-
ance between multipath diversity and estimation complexity,
whereas excessively dense and randomly varying scattering in-
troduces ambiguity that constrains further performance gains.
This highlights the importance of adaptive RIS configuration
and accurate scattering modeling for reliable UE localization
and SOs sensing in dynamic RSEs.
VI. CONCLUSION
This paper presents a learning-based UE location and ori-
entation estimation approach for RIS-assisted communication
network operating in RSE to sequentially design the RIS
configuration, the BS beamforming vector and the UE beam-
forming vector for accurate UE localization while estimating
scattering contributed by dynamic SOs in the environment.
Using RIS-assisted sensing and sequential measurements his-
tory in BS, our proposed approach models temporal features
and adaptively designs the RIS configuration, BS beamforming
vector, and UE beamforming vector to minimize UE local-
ization error. Numerical evaluation validates that the proposed
approach effectively performs beamforming and acheieves low
UE localization error across varying RIS sizes and RIS deploy-
ment scenarios, SNR conditions and varying RSE conditions.
