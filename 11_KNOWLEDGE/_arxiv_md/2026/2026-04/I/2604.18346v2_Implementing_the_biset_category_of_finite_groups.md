Abstract. We describe an implementation of the biset category of finite
groups as a tower of standard categorical constructions, all of which are
implemented in the software project Cap for algorithmic category theory.
In particular, we describe the composition of bisets as a composition in a
Kleisli category of some biadjunction monad. This composition relies on
the universal property of the coequalizer completion of a group viewed
as a groupoid on one object. Expressing this universal property offers an
elegant categorical interpretation of the Schreier–Sims orbit algorithm.
Indeed, the implementation relies on every aspect of the algorithm.
Keywords: biset categories · category of presheaves · coequalizer com-
pletion · coproduct completion · ur-algorithms · Schreier–Sims algorithm.

Bisets from a categorical point of view
3.1
Bisets for finite categories
Let FinCat denote the 2-category of finite categories, naturally enriched over
the elementary topos FinSet of finite sets. Let C, D, E ∈FinCat denote finite
categories.
Define the category FinBisetCat with the same objects as FinCat but with
functors C →D replaced by profunctors X : C −7−→D, i.e., functors X : Dop ×
C →FinSet. For the wide range of applications of bisets for categories see [9].
The composition of profunctors is usually described by a coend-construction,
which we do not know how to make algorithmically efficient. Alternatively, one
can use the 2-adjunction in Cat to rewrite X as a functor
X : C →PSh(D),
where PSh(D) := PSh(D, FinSet) := Func(Dop, FinSet) denotes the elemen-
tary topos of finite-set-valued presheaves on D. Now in order to compose two
functors X : C →PSh(D) and Y : D →PSh(E) to a functor XY : C →PSh(E)
we first need to lift Y to a (finitely cocontinuous) functor bY : PSh(D) →PSh(E).
We call this composition XY := X bY the Kleisli composition4. The first obvi-
ous advantage of this approach is that the 2-category of presheaf categories and
cocontinuous functors bY : PSh(D) →PSh(E) is a strictification of the weak
2-category of categories and profunctors Y : D −7−→E.

The universal property of PSh
A second advantage is the insight that the composition of two profunctors can be
reduced to the ur-algorithm5 expressing the universal property of the left biad-
joint 2-functor L = PSh: Given a finitely cocomplete category T and a functor
Y : D →T, determine a finitely cocontinuous lift bY : PSh(D) →T. Indeed,
the so-called co-Yoneda lemma states that each presheaf Ω∈PSh(D) can be
“resolved” as the colimit of a diagram of representable presheaves HomD(−, c),
while the fully faithful Yoneda functor よ: D ,→PSh(D) allows one to consider
this diagram as a diagram in D, but where its colimit is taken in PSh(D) and co-
incides with Ω. By applying Y : D →T to the resolving diagram of the presheaf
Ωin D we get a diagram in T, the colimit of which is the desired bY (Ω). The so
constructed finitely cocontinuous functor bY : PSh(D) →T is the extension of Y
along よ, expressing the universal property of PSh(D) as a model for the finite
colimit completion of D. In the rest of this subsection we will explain how one
can realize the finite colimit completion as the coequalizer completion Coeq(⊔D)
of the finite coproduct completion ⊔D establishing the equivalence
PSh(D) ≃Coeq(⊔D).

3.3
The category Γ -FinSet as a tower
Let Γ be a finite group, viewed as a groupoid on one object ∗. The presheaf
category PSh(Γ) of Γ with values in finite sets is just another encoding of the
category Γ-FinSet of finite left Γ-sets. Hence, by (6), we get the chain of equiv-
alences:
Γ-FinSet ≃PSh(Γ) ≃Coeq(⊔Γ).
An elegant categorical interpretation of the orbit theorem for (finite) groups is
that both completions in the categorical tower can be switched (which already
fails for the cyclic monoid ⟨e|e2 = e⟩of order 2), i.e.,
Γ-FinSet ≃Coeq(⊔Γ) ≃⊔Coeq(Γ),
where the coequalizer completion Coeq(Γ) is (equivalent to) the category of
transitive left Γ-sets. This flipped categorical tower will be the basis of the Cap
implementation of Γ-FinSet in Section 4.1.
3.4
The biset category of finite groups
From now on, we will denote the full subcategory of FinBisetCat on finite
groups (viewed as one-object groupoids) by FinBisetGrp or simply by FinBiset.
It is easy to see that this is equivalent to the definition from Section 1, i.e.,
FinBiset(G, H) ∼= {HXG | HXG an (H, G)-biset},
where we identify an (H, G)-biset HXG with a functor
X : G →H-FinSet ≃PSh(H) ≃Coeq(⊔H) ≃⊔Coeq(H).
(8)
Note that the Kleisli composition XY ∈FinBiset(G, K) of X ∈FinBiset(G, H)
and Y ∈FinBiset(H, K) now corresponds to the post-composition KYH ◦HXG.

The relation to the category of generalized morphism
in the Abelian group case
One can further restrict FinBiset to the full subcategory on finite Abelian
groups. Mackey’s formula (5) states that the composition of two transitive bisets

these multiplicities in the composition (e.g., by setting them to 1), the transitive
