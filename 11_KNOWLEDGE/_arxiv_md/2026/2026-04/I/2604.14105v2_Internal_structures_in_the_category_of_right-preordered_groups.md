Internal structures in the category of right-preordered groups
Ony Aubril
Abstract
We give explicit axioms for the algebraic theory of the quasivarieties of right-preordered groups and
preordered groups.
We then look at lattices of effective equivalence relations, which turn out to be
similar to the lattices of equivalence relations in the category of groups. Once this is established, we
study internal structures in the category of right-preordered groups. We start with some general results
and then prove the S-protomodularity of the category of right-preordered groups, when considering the
class S of Schreier split epimorphisms. Following this, we investigate further and prove that the category
of right-preordered groups turns out to be action representable when we restrict our attention to split
epimorphisms in S. Relatively to this class of split epimorphisms, we define the notion of S-precrossed
modules, and then of S-crossed modules; that correspond exactly to Schreier internal reflexive graphs
and Schreier internal categories, respectively. Lastly, we characterize groupoids among Schreier internal
categories and give some examples.
Introduction
A right-preordered is a group endowed with a preorder (i.e.
a transitive and reflexive relation) over its
underlying set such that the group operation is monotone on the right, i.e.
a group G together with a
preorder ≤such that for all g, h, k in G, g ≤h ⇒g + k ≤h + k. Right-preordered groups appear in the
literature as early as 1942 in [22], where F.W. Levi investigated quotients of totally preordered groups. Later,
in the context of Fourier analysis, W. Rudin introduced totally ordered abelian groups in [27], and proved,
most notably, that any archimedean, i.e. such that for all x ≥y ≥0, exists at least one natural integer n such
that ny ≥x, totally ordered abelian group is isomorphic (via order-preserving isomorphism) to a subgroup
of the field of real numbers. Later, the notion of left-ordered groups (which is equivalent to the notion of
right-ordered groups), became important since continuous order-preserving actions of a given group G on the
real line were able to be associated to left-preorders over G (see [10] for instance). Right-preordered groups
also appeared in [14], where some preorders are described as compatible only on one side for some groups.
More recently, in [13], M.M. Clementino and A. Montoli investigated the category RPreOGrp of right-
preordered groups and monotone group morphisms. This work follows a similar article they wrote about
preordered groups, which are groups endowed with a preorder compatible on both sides with the group law
([12]). They proved that the category of right-preordered groups (similarly to the category of preordered
groups), does not share most of the "good" algebraic properties with the category Grp of groups.
For
instance, the category is neither Bourn-protomodular nor Barr-exact. Consequently, they looked into under-
standing split extensions (for some fixed kernel and codomain), and found both examples of some pairs of
right-preordered groups (K, C) that could not induce a split extension with kernel K and codomain C, and
pairs such that there were an uncountable infinity of such split extensions (example 4.6 in [13]). Moreover,
even though the forgetful functor from RPreOGrp to the category Grp of groups is topological, the al-
gebraic behaviour of RPreOGrp is closer to the behaviour of the category Mon of monoids that it is to
the category of groups. In view of this observation, as well as the characterization of central extensions of
preordered groups made by M. Gran and A. Michel in [15], it becomes natural to turn our attention to a
special case of split epimorphisms. Indeed, in both [15] and [26], a similar class of split epimorphisms appear.
In [26], A. Patchkoria considers the class of so-called Schreier split epimorphisms in the category Mon, which
corresponds to the classical notion of internal monoid action via endomorphisms.

In [13], M.M. Clementino and A. Montoli proved that both categories RPreOGrp and PreOGrp, the
full subcategory of preordered groups, are quasivarieties by exhibiting a regular projective, regular generator
object in both categories (Proposition A.1. in [13]), and then with abstract arguments they showed that both
quasivarieties are finitary quasivarieties (proposition A.2 in [13]). However, it seems that no axiomatization
has been given for none of the categories RPreOGrp or PreOGrp.
The first section of this article is
dedicated to the description of both categories RPreOGrp and PreOGrp as quasivarieties of universal
algebras. In particular we find a axiomatization (Σ, H) (see section 1) that makes explicit the fact that the
category of monoid morphisms such that the codomain is a group is itself a variety of universal algebras, and
injectivity of said monoid morphism is the only quasi-identity in H. Considering the well-known description of
RPreOGrp as the category of groups together with a fixed submonoid (see [13]), the theory (Σ, H) induces
an equivalence of categories between the category of (Σ, H)-algebras and RPreOGrp. We finish the section
by showing that PreOGrp is not a subquasivariety of RPreOGrp, but can still be recovered as category
of algebras for a theory (Σ′, H′), with Σ ⊆Σ′ and H ⊆H′. Better still, Σ and Σ′ differ by only one element,
and H differs from H′ by only two identities. It results that axiom (Inj) in section 1, reflecting the injectivity
of previously mentioned morphism, is the only proper quasi-identity in both H and H′,
With this result is established, the framework for the study of right-preordered groups becomes clearer for
the second section. In particular, the description of kernel pairs becomes more intuitive (see 2.1.1), and we
are then able to investigate the links between lattices of normal subobjects in the category Grp and normal
subobjects in the category RPreOGrp. For a given right-preordered group (G, PG), its lattice of normal
subobjects in RPreOGrp turns out to be isomorphic to the lattice of normal subobjects of G in Grp.
This result is crucial. Indeed, the lattice of normal subobjects in Grp is a modular lattice, thus the lattice
of normal subobjects of (G, PG) is also a modular lattice, and because RPreOGrp is a normal category
in the sense of Z. Janelidze ([13]), the lattice of effective equivalence relations inherits the aforementioned
property as well. Considering the work of M. Gran and D. Bourn in [5], this result insures the uniqueness
of the composition map over any reflexive graph (note that it is however necessary to be careful and revisit
the proof given in [5], because we have modularity of the lattice of effective equivalence relations over any
right-preordered group, but it is not the case that the lattice of equivalence relations over an arbitrary right-
preordered group is a modular lattice). This consideration also gives a natural example of proper relatively
modular quasivariety in the sense of [21], fitting for some further investigations about commutator theory.
In the fourth section, we shortly give general statements about internal categories and internal groupoids
in RPreOGrp, giving a broad vision of what we could expect to find in terms of internal structures and
leading the way for the fifth section, which holds the more original results.
In the fifth section, we will present some results about internal structures in the category RPreOGrp.
We start by proving the protomodularity of RPreOGrp relatively to the class S. In doing so, we will actually
be proving a stronger result, and this is one of the main result of this article : relatively to S, the category
RPreOGrp is action representable (theorem 3.3.2). We describe the split extension classifier (still relatively
to S), and thus give a clear definition of what an internal action of right-preordered groups is. This result
is to be compared to some other recent developments that have been made in the domain, namely the split
extensions classifier that was discovered by M.M. Clementino and A. Montoli in [11] for all V -groups with
V a cartesian quantale (in particular, for preordered groups), relatively to some class of split epimorphisms.
The category RPreOGrp becomes another distinct example for the increasing list of categories that are
protomodular and action representable relatively to a specific case of split extensions.
Once we have further investigated internal actions, we take inspiration from the work of A. Patchkoria in
[26] and we work towards a definition of crossed module of right-preordered groups, still relative to the class
S. In order to do so, we first define S-precrossed modules of right-preordered groups, which correspond to
internal reflexive graphs in RPreOGrp whose "domain" morphism from a Schreier split epimorphism. We
then define S-crossed modules, which correspond to internal categories whose underlying reflexive graph has
"domain" morphism is a Schreier split epimorphism. Lastly, we characterize groupoids among these internal
categories. The proof heavily relies on the usual construction of (pre)crossed modules of groups. As such, it
will be clear that any of the above mentioned correspondence is an equivalence of categories. This will be
the object of section 3.4.

Preliminaries
0.1
The category of right-preordered groups
A right-preordered group is a (not necessarily abelian) group (G, 0, +), together with a preorder ≤over the
set |G| such that for any g, h, k in G, g ≤h implies g + k ≤h + k. For example, any preordered group, as
studied in [12] by M.M. Clementino and A. Montoli, is in particular a right-preordered group. As observed
in [13], this last category is isomorphic to the full subcategory of monomorphisms PG ↣G, in the category
Mon of monoids and monoid morphisms, such that G is a group. Under this equivalence of categories, the
category of preordered groups becomes isomorphic to the category of groups G, together with a submonoid
PG ⊆G (equivalently a monomorphism in Mon) that is stable under conjugation by any element of G. In
either one of these categories, we shall write objects as pairs (G, PG), and view PG as a submonoid of the
group G. We usually call PG the positive cone of G. A morphism in any of these two categories will be
written (f, f), where f is a group morphism and f is the restriction of f to the positive cones.
The category RPreOGrp is a quasivariety of universal algebras (Proposition A.1. in [13]), which makes
it a regular complete and cocomplete category. Moreover, the forgetful functor from RPreOGrp to Grp
sending a right-preordered group (G, PG) to G is topological ([13], proposition 2.5.), thus have both a left and
a right adjoint. The other forgetful functor, from RPreOGrp to the category of preordered sets, turns out
to be monadic ([13], proposition 2.5.). Considering both these facts, we have a good descriptions of limits,
the group structure of a limit is computed as in the category Grp of groups and group morphisms, and
it is given the limit preorder. We also recall that epimorphisms in RPreOGrp are morphisms (f, f) such
that f is surjective, and regular epimorphisms are exactly epimorphisms as previously such that f is also
surjective over the positive cone. The category RPreOGrp also admits a zero object, which is the trivial
group endowed with the trivial preorder. However, RPreOGrp fails to be a Barr-exact category (remark
2.6. in [13]), RPreOGrp is therefore a proper quasivariety and not a variety of universal algebras. Still, it
was proven that RPreOGrp is a normal category in the sense of [20] ([13], proposition 2.12.).

1.1
Algebraic theory of right-preordered groups
Without giving an explicit axiomatization, M.M. Clementino and A. Montoli proved that both categories
PreOGrp and RPreOGrp are finitary quasivarieties of universal algebra ([13], theorem A.2 and remark
A.3.). We give an example of explicit axiomatization.
First, we focus on right-preordered groups. To axiomatize this quasivariety, we consider a right-preordered
group as a set G × PG, together with a monoid structure ((0G, 0PG), +). To reflect the cartesian product
of the set, we ask the set of operations to contain two projections p0 and p1 to respectively G × {0PG} and
{0G}×PG. Both p0 and p1 are monoid morphisms, so we recover a monoid structure on both p0(G×PG) and
p1(G×PG). To get a group structure over p0(G×PG) we add an operation −that acts as the inverse operation
over p0(G × PG) and is "somehow orthogonal" to p1. Lastly, we ask for an injection i from p1(G × PG) to
p0(G × PG) that is also a monoid morphism. Asking i to be injective is actually the only quasi-identity that
we get in this axiomatization.

Algebraic theory for preordered groups
The category of preordered groups is not a subquasivariety of the quasivariety of right-preordered groups,
because the category of preordered groups is not closed under subobjects in the category of right-preordered
groups. To get an example, just consider the symmetric group S4 over four elements. It is a preordered
group if we consider the positive cone A4, the alternating group over four elements. A4 is a normal subgroup
thus a submonoid closed under S4-conjugation. With this group we have a subobject whose group is still
S4 but with positive cone P = {Id, (12)(34)}. It is a submonoid since it even is a subgroup of order 2 of S4,
but it is not closed under conjugation in S4 because (13)(12)(34)(13) = (32)(14) ̸∈P.
As a consequence, the axiomatization of preordered groups can not be done by adding identities and
quasi-identities to the theory of right-preordered groups. We can still describe its theory by adding a unary
operation expressed by ▷that is "right-orthogonal" to p0 and such that i▷is the conjugation of ip1 by p0.
