Abstract. We provide an approximate version of a rigidity result by Randrianantoan-
ina: for a large class of Orlicz sequence spaces, almost isometric embeddings almost
preserve disjointness. In specific cases, we can even prove that such embeddings almost
preserve basic vectors. As a consequence, we prove that some Orlicz sequences spaces
are guarded Fra¨ıss´e but not ω-categorical; moreover, they do not contain copies of ℓ2
and their age is not closed. This answers a question of C´uth–de Rancourt–Doucha.
1. Introduction
1.1. Fra¨ıss´e theory for Banach spaces. In this paper, all Banach spaces will be
separable. Say that a Banach space X is ultrahomogeneous if for every finite-dimensional
subspace E ⊆X, group Iso(X) of all (surjective linear) isometries of X acts transitively
on the set Emb(E, X) of all (linear) isometric embeddings E →X.
Hilbert spaces
are obviously ultrahomogeneous, and an important question, raised by Ferenczi–L´opez-
Abad–Mbombo–Todorcevic [5], is to know whether they are the only ones. This question
is related to the well-known rotation problem of Mazur, asking whether Iso(X) acts
transitively on the unit sphere SX of X.
Several approximate versions of ultrahomogeneity have been considered, the most im-
portant one being this of Fra¨ıss´e Banach spaces, introduced by Ferenczi–L´opez-Abad–
Mbombo–Todorcevic [5]. For C ⩾1, let EmbC(E, X) denote the set of all linear injec-
tions T : E →X with max(∥T∥, ∥T −1∥) ⩽C. Say that a group G acts ε-transitively on
a metric space M if for every x, y ∈M, we have d(y, Gx) ⩽ε.
Definition 1.1 (Ferenczi–L´opez-Abad–Mbombo–Todorcevic 2020). A Banach space X
is weak Fra¨ıss´e if for every finite-dimensional subspace E ⊆X and ε > 0, there exists
δ > 0 such that Iso(X) acts ε-transitively on Emb1+δ(E, X). It is Fra¨ıss´e if it is weak
Fra¨ıss´e with δ depending only on ε and dim(E).
Examples of infinite-dimensional Fra¨ıss´e Banach spaces include the Gurari˘i space [10]
and, as shown in [5], the spaces Lp([0, 1]) for 1 ⩽p < ∞with p /∈4, 6, 8, . . . (the spaces
L2n[0, 1] for integers n ⩾2 are known not to be Fra¨ıss´e, as a consequence of a result of
Randrianantoanina [20]). It is an important open question whether those examples are

The terminology Fra¨ıss´e comes from Fra¨ıss´e theory, a field that originated with the
proof of the Fra¨ıss´e correspondence [7], a model-theoretic result establishing a bijective
correspondence between countable, first-order ultrahomogeneous structures, and certain
classes of finitely generated structures called Fra¨ıss´e classes. (We refer to [12, Section
7.1] for more details on the basics of the theory.)
The definition of Fra¨ıss´e Banach
spaces has been tailored so that a similar correspondance holds in this setting (see
[5, Corollary 2.27]).
Beyond the Mazur rotation problem, the links between Fra¨ıss´e
theory and topological dynamics of automorphism groups (see [13]) also motivated the
introduction of this notion, and were exploited in [5] to provide a new proof of extreme
amenability of the groups Iso(Lp), 1 ⩽p ̸= 2 < ∞(a result by Giordano–Pestov [8]).
In a recent paper [3], C´uth–Doleˇzal–Doucha–Kurka introduced several Polish spaces
coding the class of all separable Banach spaces, and proved that the Gurari˘i space and
the spaces Lp([0, 1]), 1 ⩽p < ∞, all had Gδ isometry classes in those codings. They
asked about the existence of other examples. A natural first step was to characterize
Banach spaces having a Gδ isometry class; this was done by C´uth–de Rancourt–Doucha
[4]. The characterization they provided is a weakening of the weak Fra¨ıss´e property, and
Banach spaces satisfying it were called guarded Fra¨ıss´e.
Definition 1.2 (C´uth–de Rancourt–Doucha 2024+). A separable Banach space X is
guarded Fra¨ıss´e if for every finite-dimensional subspace E ⊆X and every ε > 0, there
exist δ > 0, and a finite dimensional subspace F ⊆X with E ⊆F, such that Iso(X)
acts ε-transitively on Emb1+δ(F, X) ↾E.
Here, Emb1+δ(F, X) ↾E denotes the set {T↾E | T ∈Emb1+δ(F, X)}. Note that guarded
Fra¨ıss´eness is a Banach space version of the notion of prehomogeneity from model theory;
see [15] for more information on this notion.
There is also a version of the Fra¨ıss´e
correspondence for guarded Fra¨ıss´e Banach spaces; see [4, Theorem 3.9].
As a consequence of [5, Proposition 3.7], all spaces Lp([0, 1]), 1 ⩽p < ∞, are guarded
Fra¨ıss´e. The proof of the above cited result importantly relies on Banach–Lamperti’s
isometric rigidity theorem for Lp-spaces [17]. Those spaces are actually even cofinally
Fra¨ıss´e, an intermediate notion between guarded Fra¨ıss´e and weak Fra¨ıss´e; see Definition
4.1 below.
New examples of non-weak Fra¨ıss´e, cofinally Fra¨ıss´e Banach spaces were
provided in [4], namely the Lebesgue–Bochner spaces Lp([0, 1], Lq([0, 1])), whenever 1 ⩽
p ̸= q < ∞and the pair (p, q) doesn’t satisfy q = 2 or p < q < 2. The proof, once
again, relies on an isometric rigidity result for those spaces due to Guerre–Raynaud [9].
In general, good candidates for cofinal Fra¨ıss´eness are spaces having the same degree of
isometric rigidity locally and globally.
1.2. Oligomorphy and related notions. One reason of the interest of model theorists
in the Fra¨ıss´e construction is its usefulness for constructing ω-categorical structures with
prescribed properties. A countable, first-order structure is said to be ω-categorical if it is
entirely determined, up to isomorphism, by its first-order theory. (We refer the reader to
[12, Section 7.3] for more details about this notion.) In continuous logic, a version of this
notion exists for separable first order metric structures, and in particular for separable

Definition 1.3. A separable Banach space X is ω-categorical, or oligomoprhic, if for

Oligomorphic Banach spaces have the following interesting property: if a Banach
space Y is finitely representable in an oligomorphic Banach space X, then Y can be
isometrically embedded in X (see [4, Theorem 9.9]). This has the two following conse-
quences.
• Every oligomorphic Banach space contains an isometric copy of ℓ2 (this is a
consequence of the Dvoretky theorem).
• Oligomorphic Banach spaces have closed age. The age of a Banach space X, de-
noted by Age(X), is the class of all of its finite-dimensional subspaces, considered
up to isometry. Age(X) is seen as a subset of the class of all finite-dimensional Ba-
nach spaces, endowed with the Banach-Mazur distance. So saying that Age(X) is
closed means that whenever a finite-dimensional space E admits almost isometric
copies in X, then it actually has an isometric copy in X.
It is a standard fact that all countable ultrahomogeneous first-order structures in a
finite relational language are ω-categorical.
Banach spaces behave quite similarly as
structures on a finite relational language, and as expected, all Fra¨ıss´e Banach spaces are
oligomorphic (see [6, Theorem 5.13]). In particular, they contain isometric copies of ℓ2
and have closed age; actually, a Banach space is Fra¨ıss´e iff it is weak Fra¨ıss´e and has
closed age, as shown in [5, Theorem 2.12]. Similarly, in [4], C´uth–de Rancourt–Doucha
asked whether guarded Fra¨ıss´e Banach spaces should necessarily be oligomorphic, have
closed age, and contain isometric copies of ℓ2. The main goal of the present paper is to
provide a negative answer to those questions.
Note that there are simple examples of discrete prehomogeneous structures that are
not ω-categorical. This is for instance the case of the infinite linear graph (with vertex
set Z, and where two integers are connected iff they are consecutive), see [15, Example
5.4]. A natural idea to find counterexamples to the above questions is to look at Banach
spaces presenting similar rigidity properties as this graph.
We will find such spaces
among Orlicz sequences spaces, a class of spaces generalizing ℓp spaces.

1.4. Results. Our main result is that some Orlicz sequence spaces are cofinally Fra¨ıss´e.
Set aside the fact that Theorem 1.5 only holds for function spaces over [0, 1], another
issue is that proving cofinal Fra¨ıss´eness usually requires an approximate rigidity result for
almost isometric embeddings (i.e. elements of Emb1+δ(E, X) for small δ). In the case of
spaces Lp([0, 1]) or Lp([0, 1], Lq([0, 1])), rigidity results for exact isometric embeddings
are enough, because oligomorphy allows one to pass from exact isometric to almost
isometric embeddings for free, see [4, Lemma 9.17]. We cannot hope for such a shortcut
in our case, given that our goal is precisely to find non-oligomorphic examples.

4. Cofinally Fra¨ıss´e Orlicz sequence spaces
This section is devoted to the proof of Corollary 1.10 and Theorem 1.11. We start
with introducing the definition of cofinally Fra¨ıss´e Banach spaces. In what follows, if E
and F are two finite-dimensional subspaces of the same Banach space, and ε > 0, we
write E ⊆ε F if every x ∈SE is at distance at most ε of SF (here, SE denotes the unit
sphere of E).
Definition 4.1. Let X be a separable Banach space.
• A class F of finite-dimensional subspaces of X is said to be cofinal in X if for
every finite-dimensional subspace E ⊆X and every ε > 0, there exists F ∈F
such that E ⊆ε F.
• The space X is said to be cofinally Fra¨ıss´e if for every ε > 0, there is a cofinal
class F of finite-dimensional subspaces of X satisfying the following property:
for every E ∈F, there exists δ > 0 such that Iso(X) acts ε-transitively on
Emb1+δ(E, X).
As observed in [4, Proposition 8.15], cofinally Fra¨ıss´e Banach spaces are guarded
Fra¨ıss´e. It is actually not known whether there exist guarded Fra¨ıss´e Banach spaces
that are not cofinally Fra¨ıss´e (although such examples exist in the setting of countable
first-order structures, see [14]).
Note that if the Orlicz function M satisfies the assumptions of Theorem 1.8, then by
Corollary 1.9, the elements of Iso(ℓM) are exactly given by signed permutations of the
canonical basis. In particular, there is some ε > 0 for which the action of Iso(ℓM) on
SℓM is not ε-transitive. If follows that ℓM is not weak Fra¨ıss´e. However, it is cofinally
Fra¨ıss´e, as we will now show.
