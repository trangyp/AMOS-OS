Abstract—Multi-band sensing has emerged as a key enabler
of integrated sensing and communication (ISAC), one of the
six primary usage scenarios defined for IMT-2030 (6G). The
introduction of frequency range 3 (FR3, 7-24 GHz), compris-
ing non-contiguous sub-bands across a wide frequency span,
further reinforces the importance of multi-band operation. In
such scenarios, frequency-dependent clutter, collectively referred
to as dense multipath components (DMC), must be carefully
considered. Building on prior literature and our experimental
observations, this paper analyzes the impact of DMC on multi-
band fusion ISAC systems by investigating Cram´er-Rao bound
(CRB)-based fundamental limits and the performance of our
proposed multi-band estimator. Numerical results show that
multi-band processing, especially in DMC-dominated scenarios,
can substantially reduce estimation error and boost system
resilience when channel statistics vary.
Index Terms—multi-band sensing, integrated sensing and com-
munication, localization, dense multipath components, ISAC,
FR3, 6G
I. INTRODUCTION
Future 6G systems are envisioned to reliably facilitate
novel applications that interact more deeply with the physical
world, such as the internet of things, autonomous driving,
extended reality, smart cities, digital twins, and radio maps for
environment-aware communication. Thus, to save resources
in bandwidth, hardware, processing power, and energy while
enabling the efficient operation of such a large number of
devices, integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) has
been proposed as one of the six key usage scenarios of IMT-
2030 (6G) [1]. One type of ISAC is communication-centric
ISAC, which investigates sensing via existing communication
protocols and infrastructure [2], including the allocated fre-
quency bands. With the addition of frequency range 3 (FR3,
7-24 GHz) [3], future 6G systems will likely operate in a wide
range of frequencies and even multiple sub-bands [4]. Hence,
performing multi-band sensing in FR3 using communication
signals is naturally a promising enabler of ISAC [5], [6], given
the potential of sensing capabilities for 6G [7].
Multi-band sensing is the technique of performing sensing
across possibly non-contiguous frequency bands. According to
the literature, some reasons for choosing multi-band sensing
include the following. First, although using a large, contiguous
bandwidth is a typical and intuitive approach to achieving
higher time resolution, it is associated with increased hardware
and algorithmic complexity [8]. Moreover, communication-
centric ISAC systems are typically unable to process ideal
radar-only wideband sensing waveforms, such as radar chirps
[9]. Additionally, the increased amount of sensing data implies
a lower error probability, such as when severe fading happens
at a particular sub-band. Finally, some spectrum, such as
FR3, is highly fragmented due to coexistence with incumbent
applications [9], such as satellites [10]. Multi-band sensing is
thus a reasonable choice for using the wide bandwidth in FR3.
Typical sensing processing in ISAC literature relies on
parameter estimation from specular components (SC)-only
channel models defined by target geometry [11]. However,
an important propagation effect, well studied in channel mod-
eling but largely overlooked in ISAC, is the dense multipath
components (DMC) [12], [13]. DMC refers to interference
due to clutter such as diffuse scattering and diffraction, which
can come from the background environment (walls, building
structure, etc.). Neglecting DMC leads to overly optimistic
predictions of sensing performance or to non-negligible esti-
mation bias and errors, yielding erroneous internal reliability
metrics.
This issue is exacerbated in multi-band settings, where
DMC exhibits frequency-dependent statistics, as observed in
our experiment-based works [14], [15]. This effect induces
nonlinear behavior that invalidates conventional intuitions and
necessitates DMC-aware modeling to effectively employ and
accurately assess multi-band ISAC systems. Despite its im-
portance, the role of DMC in multi-band sensing remains
poorly understood. For example, multi-band sensing works
such as [8], [9] all assumed an SC-only channel model with
white Gaussian noise, while DMC-related works such as [12],
[13] are focused on channel modeling (CM) and single-
band parameter estimation. Even our previous works [14],
[15] remain limited to single-band processing, despite the
DMC- and multi-band-sensing-related insights they provide.
The theoretical framework for multi-band fusion processing
with sub-band frequency-dependent DMC remains an open
problem.
In this paper, we bridge the gap between multi-band sensing
and DMC. First, we define the DMC-affected system model in
Section II. Next, we derive two important fundamental limits:
the Cram´er-Rao bound (CRB) and the estimation signal-
to-noise ratio (ESNR) in Section III. To operationalize our
insights, we then explain in Section IV how the fundamental
limits can be achieved through our proposed multi-band esti-
mation algorithm. Finally, we provide an in-depth analysis of
our theoretical and algorithmic numerical results in Section V.

II. SYSTEM MODEL
The scenario, as shown in Fig. 1, involves a dual-function
radar communication (DFRC) base station (BS) serving as
the transmitter (Tx) and a passive radio (PR) as the receiver
(Rx), whose Cartesian locations are assumed to be known.
The Tx DFRC BS has LT uniform linear array (ULA) antenna
elements, and the Rx PR has LR ULA antenna elements. The
PR receives the multi-band signal from the BS across M sub-
bands (indexed by m), each with N subcarriers (indexed by n),
and performs bistatic sensing using downlink preamble signals
from communications with user equipment in the environment.
In this paper, all sub-bands are assumed to have the same
number of subcarriers, even though this may not hold in
general. The channel consists of K paths (indexed by k, where
k = 1 is the line-of-sight (LoS) path) and the DMC. We
assume each path is specular, meaning the primary source of
DMC is the background environment.
In this paper, we study the typical ISAC system in which
sensing is performed on the multiple-input, multiple-output
(MIMO) frequency-domain channel state information (CSI).
In practice, the CSI is obtained via orthogonal frequency-
division multiplexing (OFDM) pilots that are designed to
ensure orthogonality across Tx antennas. Following the model
in [12], we express the multi-band MIMO channel transfer
function (TF) h ∈CNLTLRM×1 by h = s + d + w, where

A. System Model Analysis
Fig. 3 shows the PDP of an example two-path channel,
providing a visual illustration of how the DMC is impairing
the SC estimation. Since DMC is multipath interference, it
suffers from higher diffuse scattering, diffraction, and blockage
as sub-band frequency increases, just like the SC. Hence, it is
practical to assume higher decay rates ˜β for higher frequency
sub-bands, which is also experimentally verified by our other
works [14], [15]. We observe that the SC in certain sub-bands,
such as the scatterer path for the 8.75 GHz sub-band in the
figure, may be shadowed under the DMC profile. Also, due
to the randomness of the DMC profile, it may be hard to
distinguish SC from DMC in finite bandwidth PDPs. Finally, a
key tradeoff for multi-band systems in DMC-affected channels
is shown: although higher sub-bands typically suffer from
lower SC powers due to the propagation effects and more
directive antenna patterns [18], they are also blessed by the
faster DMC decay rates, which reduces interference.

VI. CONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE WORK
This paper, through both theoretical and algorithmic ap-
proaches, establishes the critical role of frequency-dependent
DMC-aware modeling and processing in multi-band ISAC for
future 6G applications. It reveals that conventional intuitions
about sensing drawn from SC-only models do not necessarily
hold, and that multi-band sensing performance gains in terms
of localization accuracy and system resilience manifest in
DMC-dominated scenarios. For future work, we plan to exper-
imentally measure the DMC with a multi-band ISAC focus.
We also plan to improve and use the proposed multi-band
estimation algorithm in upcoming measurement campaigns to
verify its capabilities experimentally.
