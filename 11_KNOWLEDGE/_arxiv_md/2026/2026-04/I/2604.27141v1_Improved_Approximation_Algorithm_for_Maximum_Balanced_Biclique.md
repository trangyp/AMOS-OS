Introduction
The Maximum Clique problem—where we are given an undirected graph and the goal is to find a max-
imum clique—is one of the most well studied problems in all of combinatorial optimization. One of the
original 21 NP-hard problems shown by Karp [Kar72], Maximum Clique also played a central role in the
foundation for connections between the PCP Theorem and hardness of approximation [FGL+96, ALM+98,
AS98, BGS98, H˚as96, Kho01, EH03, KP06, Zuc07]. Specifically, it is NP-hard to approximate Maximum
Clique to within a factor of n1−ϵ for any constant ϵ > 0 [H˚as96, Zuc07]. Under a stronger assumption
that NP ⊈BPTIME

2(log n)O(1)
, Khot and Ponnuswami [KP06] ruled out any polynomial-time algorithm
with approximation ratio n/2(log n)3/4+γ for any constant γ > 0. This remains the best known hardness
of approximation for Maximum Clique to date. Meanwhile, many approximation algorithms for Maximum
Clique have been devised (e.g. [BH90, AK98, Fei04]). In particular, Feige’s algorithm [Fei04] achieves the
best known approximation ratio of O

n · (log log n)2
(log n)3

in polynomial time.
In this work, we study a bipartite variant of the Maximum Clique problem, called the Maximum Balanced
Biclique (MBB) problem. Recall that a k-biclique, denoted by Kk,k, is the complete bipartite subgraph where
each side contains k vertices. In MBB, we are given a bipartite graph G = (U, V, E) where |U| = |V | = n,
and the goal is to find a maximum k-biclique in G.
The exact version of MBB was stated1 to be NP-hard in Garey and Johnson’s seminal book [GJ79,
page 196].
However, despite a considerable amount of effort over the years (e.g. [Fei02, FK04, Kho06,
Man17, BGH+17, Man18]), it is not even known to be NP-hard to approximate to within a factor of
1 + ϵ for any constant ϵ > 0.
Nevertheless, hardness of approximation results are known under other
assumptions [Fei02, FK04, Kho06, Man17, BGH+17, Man18]. Specifically, it is known to be NP-hard (under
randomized reductions) to approximate to within a factor of n1−ϵ for any ϵ > 0, assuming strengthenings of
the Unique Games Conjecture [BGH+17, Man18].

Conclusion and Open Questions
In this work, we give a polynomial-time

n
eΩ((log n)3)

-approximation algorithm for the Maximum Balanced
Biclique problem. Our algorithm is via SDP rounding in the case where the optimum is large, combined
with the known result of Chalermsook et al. [CJO20] in the case where the optimum is small. Apart from
trying to improve the approximation ratio further, it remains an interesting question whether the former
case can be handled directly via combinatorial algorithms. We remark that, in the Maximum Clique case,
subgraph removal techniques–which are completely combinatorial–suffice for this case [BH90].
Another intriguing question is whether one can achieve a similar approximation for the Maximum Edge
Biclique (MEB) problem. In MEB, we are looking for a (not necessarily balanced) biclique in G that contains
the maximum number of edges. A trivial algorithm yields n-approximation, and a simple “enumeration style”
algorithm can improve this to
n
˜Ω(log n). However, we are not aware of any further improvement to this. In
particular, it is unclear how to apply SDP rounding when the optimum is large.
