INDUCED AND NONLINEAR TOPOLOGICAL PRESSURE FOR RANDOM
DYNAMICAL SYSTEMS
CUNYI NAN
Abstract. In this paper, we investigate induced and nonlinear fiber topological pressure for
random dynamical systems. We define a non-averaged induced fiber pressure via spanning and
separated sets, characterize it as the pseudo-inverse of the classical fiber topological pressure
studied previously, and establish the corresponding variational principle.
We also define the
nonlinear fiber pressure and prove the associated variational principles for these types of pres-
sures. Finally, we extend the combined theory to the higher-dimensional setting.
1. Introduction
Entropy and pressure are among the most fundamental topological invariants in dynamical
systems. In the late 1950s, A. Kolmogorov [Kol58, Kol59] and Ya. G. Sina˘ı [Sin59] introduced
the measure-theoretic notion of entropy for measure-preserving transformations, now called the
Kolmogorov–Sina˘ı entropy. Motivated by this development, R. Adler, G. Konheim, and M. McAn-
drew [AKM65] introduced topological entropy in 1965 by means of open covers, and later R. Bowen
[Bow71a, Bow71b] and E. Dinaburg [Din70] gave equivalent formulations in terms of spanning and
separated sets.
Another central quantity in thermodynamic formalism is topological pressure, whose origins lie
in statistical physics. It was introduced by D. Ruelle [Rue78] for expansive maps and subsequently
extended by P. Walters [Wal75] to general continuous maps. The bridge between entropy and
pressure is provided by the variational principle, which identifies topological pressure with the
supremum of the sum of measure-theoretic entropy and the integral of the potential; see §2.1 for a
brief review and [Wal82] for a detailed account.
Induced versions of entropy and pressure arise naturally when time is reparametrized by a
positive observable. In 1998, S. V. Savchenko [Sav98] introduced a notion of entropy for special
flows over countable Markov shifts via Krengel’s formula and proved existence and uniqueness
results for measures of maximal entropy. In this setting, the flow is generated by a positive scaling
function, also called a height function. Building on this idea, J. Jaerisch, M. Kesseb¨ohmer, and S.
Lamei [JKL14] introduced induced topological pressure for H¨older potentials on countable Markov
shifts, and later Z. Xing and E. Chen [XC15] extended the theory to general topological dynamical
systems.
