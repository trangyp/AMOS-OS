Inertial 2-blocks with abelian defect groups
Kun Zhang and Yuanyang Zhou
Faculty of Mathematics and Statistics, Hubei University, Wuhan, China
School of Mathematics and Statistics, Central China Normal University, Wuhan, 430079, P.R. China
Abstract L. Puig defined inertial blocks. In this paper, we prove that 2-blocks with defect group
C2n1 × C2n2 × ... × C2nt are inertial, where ni ≥2 for all i.
Keywords Finite group; inertial block; abelian defect 2-group

Introduction
Let O be a complete discrete valuation ring with field of fractions K of characteristic 0
and with an algebraically closed residue field k of characteristic p. We assume that K is large
enough for all finite groups considered below. Let G be a finite group and let b be a p-block
(or a block) of G over O, a central primitive idempotent of the group algebra OG. Let P be
a defect group of b and b0 the Brauer correspondent of the block b in NG(P).
A Morita equivalence between block algebras is called basic if it is induced by an endop-
ermutation source bimodule (see [17]). Following [20], the block b is called inertial if the
block algebras OGb and ONG(P)b0 are basically Morita equivalent. By [9, Corollary 1.5(c)],
a block is inertial if and only if its block algebra is basically Morita equivalent to the block
algebra of a block with a normal defect group. For example, blocks of p-solvable groups with
abelian defect groups are inertial.
2-blocks of finite quasi-simple groups with abelian defect groups has been classified (see [5]
of Theorem 3.1 below). Since then, much work on 2-blocks with abelian defect groups has
been done (see [5, 6, 22]). For any positive integer n, let Cn be the cyclic group of order
n. According to the classification, 2-blocks of finite quasi-simple groups with defect group
isomorphic to C2n1 × C2n2 × ... × C2nt, where ni ≥2 for all i, are inertial. In this paper, we
generalize this observation to all finite groups.
Theorem 1.1. Let G a finite group and let b a 2-block of G.
If defect groups of b are
isomorphic to C2n1 × C2n2 × ... × C2nt, where ni ≥2 for all i, then b is inertial.

Reduced blocks with abelian defect groups
Let G be a finite group and b a block of G. The block b of G is called quasi-primitive if
every block of every normal subgroup of G covered by b is G-stable. In this case, b covers a
unique block of K for any normal subgroup K of G, denoted by bK. The block b is called
reduced (see [1, Proposition 6.1]) if b is quasi-primitive and for any N ⊴G such that b covers
a nilpotent block of N, N ≤Z(G)Op(G) and Op′(N) ≤Z(G) ∩[G, G]. Denoted by E(G)
the layer of G, by F(G) the Fitting subgroup of G, and by F∗(G) the generalized Fitting
subgroup of G.
Theorem 2.1. Let b be a block of G with defect group P. If b is reduced and P is abelian,
then one of the following holds.

Proof of Theorem 1.1
In this section, we always assume p = 2 and give a proof of Theorem 1.1. We firstly recall
the classification of blocks of quasi-simple groups with abelian defect groups.
Theorem 3.1 ([5, Theorem 6.1]). Let H be a finite quasi-simple group. If c is a block of H
with abelian defect group D, then one (or more) of the following holds.
(i) H/Z(H) is one of A1(2a), 2G2(q) (where q ≥27 is a power of 3 with odd exponent), or
J1, c is the principal block, and D is elementary abelian.
(ii) H is Co3, c is a non-principal block, and D ∼= C2 × C2 × C2 (there is one such block).
(iii) c is Morita equivalent to a block d of OL, where L = L0 × L1 ≤H such that L0 is
abelian and the block of L1 covered by d has Klein 4-defect groups.
(iv) c is nilpotent covered.
Lemma 3.2. Let H be a finite quasi-simple group and c a block of H with abelian defect
group. If a hyperfocal subgroup of the block b is isomorphic to C2m1 × C2m2 × · · · × C2mr,
where mi ≥2 for all i, then c is nilpotent covered.
Proof. Let (D, f) be a maximal c-Brauer pair and Q the hyperfocal subgroup of the block

where mi ≥2 for all i, by Theorem 3.1, c is nilpotent covered.
