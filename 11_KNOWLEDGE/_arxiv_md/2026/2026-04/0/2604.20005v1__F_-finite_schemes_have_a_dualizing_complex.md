1 Introduction
Among the most important rings in algebraic and arithmetic geometry in positive charac-
teristic are the F-finite rings, namely commutative rings of characteristic p > 0 for which
the Frobenius endomorphism is finite. Examples include quotients and localizations of poly-
nomial or power series rings over a perfect field. Such rings and schemes are known to
avoid various pathologies. For example, every Noetherian F-finite ring is excellent of finite
Krull dimension [Kun76]; moreover, every such ring is a quotient of an F-finite regular ring
[Gab04], and hence admits a dualizing complex (cf. [Lur18, Theorem 3.5.1] and [MP20] for
other expositions of Gabber’s result).
Our purpose here is to leverage Gabber’s construction further by showing that every
Noetherian F-finite scheme has a canonical dualizing complex compatible with all finite
type morphisms. More precisely, we show:

2.1 Preliminaries on p-bases and differential bases

If R is regular and F-finite, then the map Fp −→R is a regular morphism (i.e. flat with
geometrically regular fibers) and Popescu’s theorem [Sta22, Tag 07GC] asserts that R can be
written as a directed colimit of smooth Fp algebras Ri. Hence ΩR = colim ΩRi is a colimit of
flat modules and hence ΩR is a finitely generated flat R-module, i.e. a locally free R-module
of finite rank. The equivalence of the notions of differential basis and p-basis together with
Kunz’s theorem [Kun69] now imply:
Lemma 2.1.3. A Noetherian F-finite ring is regular if and only if it is reduced and locally
has a p-basis (equivalently a differential basis).
Without the F-finiteness assumption, the result is false, as the example of a power series
ring in one variable over a non-F-finite field shows [KN80, Example 3.8].
We point out that there are regular F-finite rings R where F∗R is not free and so in
particular R does not have a p-basis, as the following example shows.
Example 2.1.4. Consider R = F2[x, y]/(y2+xy +y +x3+x +1) an affine chart on a smooth
ordinary elliptic curve. R is then regular and F-finite and it is known that F∗R ∼= R ⊕Q
where Q is the prime ideal whose corresponding point has order p = 2 in the elliptic curve
group, see for instance [ST16] or [PST17, Exercise 2.19]. A direct computation shows that
Q = (x + 1, y + 1) is the point of order 2. It is easy to verify that Q is not principal. We
also observe that F∗R ∼= R ⊕Q is not free since its determinant is also equal to Q, which is
still not principal. In particular, the regular ring R does not have a p-basis. Note we used
Macaulay2 and Sage to verify some aspects of this particular example [GS, Sag23].
Similar examples can also be done with elliptic curves in other characteristics, indeed a
similar computation is explored in [DSPY19, Example 5.2].

3 Gabber’s construction

a p-basis mapping onto x.

to dualizing complexes for F-finite schemes in Section 5.

5.3 Canonical dualizing complex for F-finite rings – a sketch
