2-blocks with abelian defect groups and inertial
quotient of prime order
Qianhu Zhou and Kun Zhang
1.School of Mathematics and Statistics, Central China Normal University, Wuhan, 430079, China

Abstract In this paper, we classify all 2-blocks for which the defect groups are abelian and the
inertial quotient has prime order. As a consequence, we prove that Brou´e’s abelian defect group
conjecture holds for all blocks under consideration here.
Keywords blocks; inertial quotient; Brou´e’s abelian defect group conjecture
1
Introduction
Let p be a prime and (K, O, k) a complete p-modular system. We assume that the residue
field k is algebraically closed of characteristic p and the field of fractions K is of characteristic
0. Throughout this paper, we assume that K is large enough for the finite groups considered
below.
Let G be a finite group. A p-block b of G is a primitive central idempotent of the group
algebra OG.
Let P be a defect group of b and b0 the Brauer correspondent of b in the
normalizer NG(P). A key goal in block theory is to understand the connection between the
block algebras OGb and ONG(P)b0. The celebrated Brou´e’s abelian defect group conjecture
says that the block algebras OGb and ONG(P)b0 are derived equivalent when the defect
group P is abelian(see [3, 6.1]).
Following [20], a block is called inertial if OGb and ONG(P)b0 are basically Morita equiv-
alent [18], and it is well-known that Brou´e’s conjecture holds for such blocks. Numerous
examples of inertial blocks with abelian defect groups are known, such as blocks of p-solvable
groups, 2-blocks with defect groups isomorphic to C2n1 × C2n2 × · · · × C2nt where ni ≥2 for
all i [23], blocks with only one orbit of irreducible Brauer characters [12], and so on.
The classification of 2-blocks with abelian defect groups for finite quasi-simple groups has
been established (see [5, Theorem 6.1] or Theorem 3.1 below). This classification has been
used to determine the Morita equivalence classes of 2-blocks with certain abelian defect
groups [2,5–7,16,22]. This, in turn, allows for a proof of Brou´e’s conjecture in these cases.
Let (P, eP) be a maximal b-Brauer pair [14, Section 6.3]. We denote by NG(P, eP) the
stabilizer of (P, eP) in G under conjugation. The inertial quotient of b is then defined as
the quotient group E(b) = NG(P, eP)/PCG(P). In particular, when P is abelian, E(b) is
the quotient group NG(P, eP)/CG(P). Then it is a group of automorphisms of P, and the
hyperfocal subgroup (see [19, 1.7]) of b with respect to (P, eP) is equal to [P, E(b)].
We focus on 2-blocks with abelian defect groups whose inertial quotients have prime order,
and prove the following classification.
Theorem 1.1. Let b be a 2-block of a finite group G with an abelian defect group P and with
E(b) of prime order. Then one (or more) of the following holds:
(i) b is inertial;
(ii) the hyperfocal subgroup of b is a Klein four-group;

Reduced blocks
In this section, we consider reduced blocks with abelian defect groups. In general, we follow
the notation and terminology of [17]
We recall that a block b of G is called quasi-primitive if every block of a normal subgroup
of G covered by b is G-invariant. Further, b is said to be reduced (see [1, Proposition 6.1]) if
it is quasi-primitive and satisfies the following additional condition: for any normal subgroup
N ⊴G such that b covers a nilpotent block of N, we have
N ≤Z(G)Op(G)
and
Op′(N) ≤Z(G) ∩[G, G],
where Z(G) is the center of G, Op(G) is the maximal normal p-subgroup, Op′(N) is the
maximal normal p′-subgroup of N, and [G, G] is the commutator of G.
As usual, E(G) denotes the layer of G, F(G) the Fitting subgroup, and F∗(G) = F(G)E(G)
the generalized Fitting subgroup. Reduced blocks with abelian defect groups were charac-
terized in [23], leading to the following structural classification:
Theorem 2.1 ( [23, Theorem 2.1]). Let b be a block of G with defect group P. If b is reduced
and P is abelian, then one of the following holds:
(i) P is normal in G.
(ii) The layer E(G) is non-trivial, and there exists normal subgroups M and L of G such
that F∗(G) ≤M ≤L, such that G/L and M/F∗(G) are both p′-groups, such that
L = PM, and such that each component of G is normal in L.
A block b of G is nilpotent covered if there is a finite group ˜G with the normal subgroup G
and a nilpotent block ˜b of ˜G covering b (i.e. ˜b · b ̸= 0). A subnormal quasi-simple subgroup
of a finite group G is called a component of G. The layer E(G) is the central product of all
components of G.
Let P be a defect group of b. In the remainder of this section, we always assume that b
is reduced, and that P is abelian which is not normal in G. For any component X of G, let
bX be a block of X such that bX · b ̸= 0. Let bE(G) be the block of E(G) covered by b. It is
obvious that bX is the unique block of X covered by bE(G).
Proposition 2.2. Keep the notation and assumptions above. If the block bX is nilpotent
covered for every component X of G, then b is inertial.
Proof. By Theorem 2.1(ii), X is invariant under the conjugation action of P. So the block
bX can be viewed as a block of PX, and P serves as a defect group of it. Since bX is nilpotent
covered, the set IBr(bX), which denotes the set of all irreducible Brauer characters belonging
to bX, forms a single orbit under the action of Aut(X)bX, where Aut(X)bX is the stabilizer
of bX in the automorphism group Aut(X) under conjugation. Thus by [12, Theorem 1.1],
bX is inertial as a block of PX. Finally, by [23, Corollary 2.4] and the arbitrariness of X, it
follows that b is inertial.

Lemma 2.3. Keep the notation and assumptions above. Let N ⊴G such that CG(P) ≤N.
If the inertial quotient E(b) has prime order, then G = N.
Proof. Let c be a block of N covered by the block b. Since P is abelian and CG(P) ≤N, we
have P ≤N. Since b is reduced, c is G-invariant. Thus [17, Theorem 9.26] tells us that P is
also a defect group of c.
Fix a common maximal Brauer pair (P, eP) for b and c. Note that the inertial quotient
E(c) = NN(P, eP)/CN(P) = (NG(P, eP) ∩N)/CG(P). So E(c) is a subgroup of E(b). Since
E(b) has prime order, E(c) is either trivial or equal to E(b). Suppose that E(c) is trivial. Then
c is nilpotent by [14, Proposition 8.11.3]. Since b is reduced, we have N ≤Z(G)Op(G). Thus
P = Op(G), which contradicts our assumption that P is not normal in G. So E(c) is equal to
E(b), and then NG(P, eP) ≤N. Also, the Frattini argument tells us that G = NNG(P, eP),
so G = N.
Corollary 2.4. Keep the notation and assumptions above. If the inertial quotient E(b) has
prime order, then Z(G) = Op(G)Op′(G).
Proof. As b is reduced, Op′(G) ≤Z(G). So we may assume that Op(G) ̸= 1. Let N =
CG(Op(G)). Since any defect group of b contains Op(G), then N is normal in G and contains
CG(P).
By Lemma 2.3, we have G = N, so Op(G) ≤Z(G).
It follows that Z(G) =
Op(G) Op′(G).
Corollary 2.5. Keep the notation and assumptions above. If the inertial quotient E(b) has
prime order, then every component of G is normal in G.
Proof. Since E(G) is normal in G and the block b is reduced, the block of E(G) covered by b
is unique; denote it by bE(G). Set PE(G) = P ∩E(G). By [17, Theorem 9.26], PE(G) is a defect
group of bE(G). Let (PE(G), fPE(G)) be a maximal bE(G)-Brauer pair. The Frattini argument
then implies
G = E(G) NG(PE(G), fPE(G)).
Let K = E(G) CG(PE(G)). Then K is normal in G. Clearly, CG(P) ≤CG(PE(G)) ≤K.
Applying Lemma 2.3 to K yields G = K.
Let X be a component of G and let bX be the block of X covered by bE(G). By [17, Theorem
9.26], PX = PE(G) ∩X is a defect group of bX. Take an arbitrary element g ∈CG(PE(G)).
Then PX ≤X ∩Xg.
Suppose X ̸= Xg.
Then PX ≤Z(X), so bX has a central defect group.
Hence, the
block of every G-conjugate of X covered by bE(G) also has a central defect group. Let H
be the minimal normal subgroup of G containing X, which is a central product of all G-
conjugates of X. Then, by [15, Corollary 2.9], the block of H covered by b has a central
defect group. By Corollary 2.5, Op(G)Op′(G) = Z(G). Since b is reduced, it follows that
X ≤H ≤Op(G)Op′(G) = Z(G), which is a contradiction.
Therefore, Xg = X for all
g ∈CG(PE(G)), and since G = E(G)CG(PE(G)), X is normal in G.
Lemma 2.6. Keep the notation and assumptions above. Let X be a component of G and bX
the block of X covered by b. Let (P, eP) be a maximal b-Brauer pair. If bX is non-nilpotent
covered and the inertial quotient E(b) has prime order, then NG(P, eP) = NX(P, eP)CG(P).

Proof of the Main Results
This section is devoted to the proofs of Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2. Throughout this
section, we set p = 2. We begin by recalling the classification of blocks of quasi-simple groups
with abelian defect groups from [5].
Theorem 3.1 ( [5, Theorem 6.1]). Let X be a quasi-simple group. If c is a block of X with
abelian defect group D, then one (or more) of the following holds:
(i) X/Z(X) is one of A1(2a), 2G2(q) (where q ≥27 is a power of 3 with odd exponent), or
J1, c is the principal block and D is elementary abelian;
(ii) X is Co3, c is a non-principal block and D is an elementary abelian group of rank 3
(there is one such block);
(iii) c is Morita equivalent to a block d of a subgroup L = L0 × L1 of X as following: The
defect group of d is isomorphic to D, L0 is abelian and the block of L1 covered by d has
Klein four defect groups.
(iv) c is nilpotent covered.
Let b be a block of a finite group G with defect group P. Assume that b is reduced, that P
is abelian, that G has a component X, and that the inertial quotient E(b) has prime order.
By Corollary 2.5, X is normal in G. Denote by bX the unique block of X covered by b, and
assume further that bX is non-nilpotent covered.
Lemma 3.2. Keep the notation and assumptions above. If the block bX has a Klein four
hyperfocal subgroup, then the hyperfocal subgroup of b is also a Klein four group.
Proof. Let D = P ∩X. Then D is a defect group of the block bX. By Lemma 2.6, the
hyperfocal subgroup Q of b with respect to (P, eP) is contained in D. Suppose that D is a
Klein four group. Note that b is not nilpotent. So it follows from [21, Lemma 3.7] that the
hyperfocal subgroup of b is equal to D.

Thus we may assume that |D| > 4. Since bX is non-nilpotent covered and has a Klein four
hyperfocal subgroup, it follows from [16, Corollary 4.8] that the inertial quotient E(bX) has
order 3, and that the block bX occurs only in case (iii) of Theorem 3.1.
By the last paragraph of the proof of [5, Theorem 6.1] and [5, Proposition 5.3], it follows
that X/Z(X) is a simple group of type E7(q) or Dn(q) for q an odd prime power and n/2 odd.
Furthermore, by [8, Theorem 2.5.12], Out(X) has an abelian Hall 2′-subgroup. Therefore,
the lemma now follows by applying Lemma 2.7.
Lemma 3.3. Keep the notation and assumptions above. If X/Z(X) is isomorphic to A1(2a)
and bX is the principal block, then G is isomorphic to the direct product of A1(2a) and an
abelian 2-group. Furthermore, 2a −1 is prime.
Proof. By [16, Corollary 4.8] and the assumption that X/Z(X) ∼= A1(2a), it follows that X
is isomorphic to A1(2a) and that the inertial quotient E(bX) has order 2a −1.
Let L = CG(X). Since X is normal in G by Corollary 2.5, L is also normal in G. Since X
is simple, the product H = XL is a direct product. Obviously, H is a normal subgroup of
G, so the quotient group G/H embeds into Out(X).
Let D = P ∩X. Then D is a defect group of bX. Since bX is the principal block, D is
a Sylow 2-subgroup of X. By the Frattini argument, we have G = XNG(D). Furthermore,
the subgroup XCG(D) is normal in G. Obviously, CG(P) ≤CG(D). Applying Lemma 2.3,
we conclude that G = XCG(D). Now set ¯G = G/L, and let ¯D and ¯X be the images of D
and X in ¯G, respectively. Then ¯G = ¯XC ¯G( ¯D). We identify ¯G with a subgroup of Aut(X)
containing X as a normal subgroup. It follows form [16, Lemma 4.10] that ¯G = ¯X. Hence,
G = X × L.
Let bL be the block of L such that b = bX ⊗bL. Then the corresponding inertial quotient
satisfies E(b) ∼= E(bX) × E(bL). We know that |E(bX)| = 2a −1 and, by assumption, |E(b)| is
a prime number. Therefore, 2a −1 must be prime and E(bL) must be trivial. Consequently,
by [14, Proposition 8.11.3], the block bL is nilpotent.
Finally, since b is reduced and bL is nilpotent, it follows that L is an abelian 2-group. This
completes the proof of the lemma.
Lemma 3.4. Keep the notation and assumptions above. Then X/Z(X) cannot be isomorphic
to a simple group of type 2G2(q) (where q ≥27 is a power of 3 with an odd exponent), J1, or
Co3.
Proof. Suppose, for a contradiction, that X/Z(X) is isomorphic to one of the groups listed.
By [16, Corollary 4.8], the group X itself is isomorphic to a simple group of type 2G2(q) (with
q = 3m, m odd, m ≥3), J1, or Co3. Furthermore, the inertial quotient E(bX) is isomorphic
to a Frobenius group of the form C7 ⋊C3. Let L = CG(X), which is normal in G since X is.
As X is simple, the product XL is direct.
Suppose that X is isomorphic to J1 or Co3. By [8, Tables 5.3j and 5.3f], Out(X) is trivial.
Thus, G = X ×L. Let bL be a block of L such that b = bX ⊗bL. Then E(b) ∼= E(bX)×E(bL).
So E(b) cannot have prime order, contradicting our assumptions.
Suppose that X is isomorphic to 2G2(q). By [8, Theorem 2.5.12], Out(X) is a cyclic group
of odd order. Let ¯G = G/L, which we identify with a subgroup of Aut(X) containing X
as a normal subgroup. Let ¯bX be the block of ¯G covering bX. Since the trivial character
of X extends to ¯G and all automorphisms of the block algebra OXbX induced by ¯G are

3.5. Proof of Theorem 1.1.
Let G be a finite group and b a 2-block of G with abelian defect
group P. Assume that the inertial quotient E(b) of b has prime order. By [1, Proposition
6.1], we may assume that b is reduced.
If P is normal in G, then b is inertial, and we are in case (i) of Theorem 1.1.
Now suppose that P is not normal in G. Then the layer E(G) is non-trivial by Theorem
2.1. By Corollary 2.5, all components of G are normal. If the blocks of the components of G
covered by b are all nilpotent covered, then b is inertial by Proposition 2.2, and we are again
in case (i) of Theorem 1.1.
Finally, suppose that G has a component X such that the block bX covered by b is non-
nilpotent covered. Since the defect group of bX is abelian, the pair (X, bX) must satisfy one
of the cases (i), (ii), or (iii) of Theorem 3.1. In case (iii), the hyperfocal subgroup of bX is a
Klein four group. The conclusion of Theorem 1.1 therefore follows from the combined results
of Lemmas 3.2, 3.3, and 3.4.
3.6. Proof of Corollary 1.2.
The conclusion follows by combining Theorem 1.1 with [4,
Theorem 4.36] and [10, Theorem 1.2].
