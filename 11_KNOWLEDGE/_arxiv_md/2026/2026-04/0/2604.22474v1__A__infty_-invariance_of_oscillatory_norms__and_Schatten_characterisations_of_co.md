A∞-INVARIANCE OF OSCILLATORY NORMS, AND
SCHATTEN CHARACTERISATIONS OF COMMUTATORS
TUOMAS HYT¨ONEN
Abstract. Schatten class properties of commutators [b, T] of pointwise multi-
pliers b and singular integral operators T have been characterised in a variety
of settings.
An abstract framework, covering many of these results as spe-
cial cases, was proposed by the author [arXiv:2411.02613]. However, recent
results about commutators of the concrete Bessel–Riesz transforms by Fan–
Li–Sukochev–Zanin [arXiv:2411.14928] are beyond this abstract setting.
In this work, we present an extension of the framework of [arXiv:2411.02613],
introducing two measures µ and ν that are A∞-equivalent to each other. The
commutators act on a given space L2(µ), but the characterising function space
norms of the multiplier b are taken with respect to another measure ν. In this
way, assumptions like Ahlfors regularity and Poincar´e inequality on the origi-
nal measure µ may be relaxed, as long as there is an A∞-equivalent measure
ν that satisfies these assumptions. In the Bessel example, the original µ fails
to be Ahlfors regular, but ν is simply the Lebesgue measure.
Within this framework, the Schatten norm characterisations of commuta-
tors of the Bessel–Riesz transforms at the critical-index by Fan–Li–Sukochev–
Zanin [op cit.] are recovered by a completely different argument, replacing non-
commutative techniques by real-variable harmonic analysis and hardly using
any specifics of the Bessel setting. As a by-product, we also obtain a simpler
characterisation in the non-critical case, replacing an ad hoc Besov space of
Fan–Lacey–Li–Xiong [J. Funct. Anal. 2026] by a classical Besov space.

Over the past few years, classical characterisations [18, 27] of commutator Schat-
ten properties have been extended to various new settings like weighted Euclidean
spaces [13], Heisenberg groups [7, 9], more general Carnot groups [24], and the so-
called Bessel setting [8]. Besides its intrinsic interest, the last-mentioned case serves
as a useful model for a general theory, featuring a concrete example of a doubling
metric measure space with different upper and lower dimensions, thus failing to be
Ahlfors regular (see Definitions 2.2 and 2.4).
A unified framework covering many of these previous results as special cases
was provided in [15]; however, within three weeks (in terms of the original arXiv
dates in November 2024), it was challenged by [10] with a characterisation for the
concrete Bessel–Riesz transforms in the important critical-index case, which was
not covered by the abstract assumptions of [15]. The goal of this paper is to place
these bounds of [10] into a further general framework, a natural extension of [15].
This leads to a new proof of these bounds, quite transparent in our opinion, and
offers a new explanation of the underlying phenomenon in a broader context. At
the same time, the scope of the bounds of [10] is significantly expanded from the
concrete Bessel setting to a wide class of operators and spaces.
We should note that, besides the uniform bounds of [10, Theorem 1.2], the same
paper also proves related spectral asymptotics in [10, Theorem 1.3], and these latter
remain beyond the scope of our approach. However, to obtain these asymptotics
for all relevant functions rather than just test functions, the uniform bounds of
[10, Theorem 1.2] are used in the proof of the asymptotics in [10, Theorem 1.3].
Our present results, extending [10, Theorem 1.2], not only offer an alternative
(simpler, in our opinion) approach to this major component of the proof of [10,
Theorem 1.3], but they should similarly serve as a step towards the proof of spectral
asymptotics in new situations. In prospective new cases where spectral asymptotics
for commutators are hoped for, the specifics of the situation may still be needed for
the eventual asymptotic considerations, while the uniform non-asymptotic bounds
will be available from the general theory presented here.

6. Final comments
The motivation for this investigation came from [10], which showed that the
abstract framework of [15] was not broad enough to capture interesting and relevant
results in the concrete Bessel setting. By extending the said framework via the use
of two different measures, we have managed to view (1.9), originally obtained as
[10, Theorem 1.2], as an instance of general real-variable theory in abstract spaces.
Having completed the proof, let us briefly compare our approach with that of [10].
In [10], two proofs of the upper bound ≲in (1.9) are given. The first approach,
given in [10, Sections 3–5] is based on the following main ingredients:
(a) the corresponding result for the classical Riesz transforms Rk from [25];
(b) explicit formulas relating the kernels of Rλ,j and Rk; and
(c) recent deep results on Schur multipliers from [3] to deduce Schatten bounds for
the operators from the obtained pointwise relations of their kernels.
The second approach, in [10, Sections 6], only applies to n ≥2. It depends on:
(d) an explicit computation of certain S2 (Hilbert–Schmidt) norms by the Fourier–
Bessel transform and Fredholm’s trace formula;
(e) abstract Cwikel estimates (generalisations of the classical [6]) from [23] to lift
the p = 2 bounds to p ∈(2, ∞); and
(f) explicit computations with the Leibniz rule to pass over some derivatives from
the Bessel–Riesz transforms ∂j(−∆λ)−1
2 to the multiplier f.
In contrast to these, our approach was built on:
(g) a characterisation of Schatten bounds of [b, T] in terms of the Oscp,q(µ) norms
for general singular integrals T over abstract spaces (X, ρ, µ), from [15, 27];
(h) verification of the kernel bounds of the general theory for Rλ,j, from [8];
(i) identification of Oscd,∞(ν) with
˙M 1,d(ν) for “nice” ν, from [15, 17]; and
(j) coincidence of Oscp,q(µ) and Oscp,q(ν) for A∞-equivalent measures µ, ν.
In summary, both approaches of [10] use some deep tools of non-commutative
analysis (either the Schur multiplier theorem of [3] or the abstract Cwikel estimates
of [23]), and the specifics of the Bessel setting are somewhat present throughout the
argument. In contrast, our approach is based on real-variable harmonic analysis
and the use of the specifics of the Bessel setting is essentially restricted to showing
that this setting fits into the general theory in (h).
Of course, proving any results about the Schatten Sp,q spaces, also known as non-
commutative ℓp,q spaces, non-commutative analysis cannot be completely avoided,
and indeed some results about the Sp,q spaces like interpolation and duality are
hidden in (g). Nevertheless, these are arguably lighter (and certainly more classical)
aspects of the non-commutative theory than the tools used in [10]. Besides the new
results of this work, providing a common extension of [15, Theorem 1.1] and [10,
Theorem 1.2], we think that our approach makes the existing [10, Theorem 1.2]
more approachable to harmonic analysts without a non-commutative background.
