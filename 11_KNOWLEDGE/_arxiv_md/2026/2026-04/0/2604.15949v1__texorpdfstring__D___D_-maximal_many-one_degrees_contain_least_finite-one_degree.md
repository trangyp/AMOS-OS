Abstract. Richter, Stephan, and Zhang asked whether every nonrecursive many-one
degree contains a least finite-one degree. We prove this for every nonrecursive c.e.
many-one degree containing a D-maximal set. The proof handles the simple cases via
known results and develops a duplicate-cover method for the remaining D-maximal
types in the classification of Cholak, Gerdes, and Lange.
1. Introduction
Richter, Stephan, and Zhang explicitly asked whether every nonrecursive many-one
degree has a least finite-one degree inside it [10, p. 17]. In a companion paper [1], we
solved this question in the negative, already within the class of computably enumerable
(c.e.) many-one degrees.
The aim of the present paper is to show that, despite the negative answer in general,
the question has a positive answer for an important natural subclass of c.e. many-one
degrees: those containing a D-maximal set.
Theorem 1.1. Every nonrecursive c.e. many-one degree containing a D-maximal set
contains a least finite-one degree.
The proof combines two ingredients. For the simple cases in the Cholak–Gerdes–Lange
classification of D-maximal sets [2, Theorem 3.10 and Definition 3.11], we appeal to
Maslova’s theorem [4]; see also [10, p. 3].
For the remaining cases, we develop a duplicate-cover method which converts structural
information about the complement of a representative into the existence of a least finite-
one degree.
At a structural level, the argument splits into two patterns. Most types are treated by
reserve systems inside the complement, either in finite-union form or in finite-level form.
The final type is treated by a one-sided tail decomposition.
For the convenience of the reader, the ten D-maximal types are grouped in the proof
according to the mechanism involved.

The notion of a D-maximal set goes back to Herrmann and Kummer [3], and Maslova

its many-one degree [4]. Cholak, Gerdes, and Lange classified the possible generating-set

of one of ten standardized types [2, Theorem 3.10 and Definition 3.11].
Our contribution is to bring these strands together with the recent negative result:
although Open Question 1 has a negative answer in general, the c.e. many-one degrees
containing a D-maximal set still form a natural subclass in which the answer is positive.

3. D-maximal sets and the type classification
This section recalls the Cholak–Gerdes–Lange framework for D-maximal sets and
fixes the terminology for the ten-type classification of generating sets for D(A) [2]. The
classification theorem provides the structural basis for reducing the proof of Theorem 1.1
to a case analysis, while the final remark records how the ten types align with the criteria
proved in Section 2.
Definition 3.1 (Generating sets for D(A)). Let A be a c.e. set. We write D(A) for the
family of all c.e. sets disjoint from A.
A finite or countable family
G = {Gi : i ∈I}

Definition 3.2 (D-maximal). A noncomputable c.e. set A is D-maximal if for every c.e.
set W ⊇A, either W \ A is c.e., or there exists a computable set R such that
A ⊆R ⊆W.
Remark 3.3. The preceding definition is equivalent to the usual formulation of D-
maximality; see [2, Definition 2.4 and Lemma 2.5]. Namely, a noncomputable c.e. set A
is D-maximal if for every c.e. set W there exists a c.e. set E disjoint from A such that
W ⊆∗A ⊔E
or
W ∪(A ⊔E) =∗ω.
For the present paper, the superset formulation is the most convenient one.
The following is the classification theorem of Cholak, Gerdes, and Lange [2, Theo-
rem 3.10].
Theorem 3.4 (Cholak–Gerdes–Lange). For every c.e. set A, the family D(A) has a
generating set of one of the following ten standardized types.
Type 1: {∅}.
Type 2: {R}, where R is an infinite computable set.
Type 3: {W}, where W is an infinite noncomputable c.e. set.
Type 4: {R0, R1, . . . }, where the Ri are infinite pairwise disjoint computable sets.
Type 5: {D0, R0, R1, . . . }, where D0 is noncomputable, the Ri are computable, and all
sets are infinite and pairwise disjoint.
Type 6: {D0, D1, . . . }, where the Di are infinite pairwise disjoint noncomputable c.e.
sets.
Type 7: {D0, R0, R1, . . . }, where D0 is noncomputable, the Ri are infinite pairwise dis-
joint computable sets, and
D0 ∩Ri ̸= ∅
for infinitely many i.

7. The Type 10 case
We finally turn to Type 10, the only remaining case not covered by reserve systems of
finite-union or finite-level type. Here the argument is based on the one-sided tail criterion:
the Type 10 normal form yields a decomposition
A = K ⊔R,
where K is a c.e. reserve and R := A ∪K is atomless r-maximal. The key point is
that the r-cohesive tail R forces the duplicate set to be finite there, while the reserve K
supplies the required infinite preimage.
Before proceeding, we briefly recall the relevant definitions. An infinite set X ⊆ω is
called r-cohesive if for every recursive set S, either X ∩S is finite or X \ S is finite. A c.e.
set M is maximal (respectively, r-maximal) if its complement M is cohesive (respectively,
r-cohesive). Finally, an r-maximal set is said to be atomless if it is not contained in any
maximal set (see [2, Definition 2.6(iv),(v)]).
By [2, Theorem 3.10], if A is of Type 10, then there are c.e. sets
D0 ⊆D1 ⊆· · ·
all disjoint from A such that every c.e. set disjoint from A is contained modulo finite in
some Di, and Di+1 \ Di is not c.e. for all i.

is an atomless r-maximal set.

9. Concluding remarks
In a companion paper [1], we show that Open Question 1 of [10] has a negative answer
in general, already within the class of computably enumerable many-one degrees. The
present paper shows that this negative answer does not persist in the D-maximal setting:
every nonrecursive c.e. many-one degree containing a D-maximal set still contains a least
finite-one degree.
It would be interesting to determine for which further natural classes of many-one
degrees the existence of a least finite-one degree still holds.
