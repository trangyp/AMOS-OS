HYPER-OPERATIONS AND EXTENSION OF SCALARS FROM F1 TO Z
LUQIAO XU
Abstract. The additive structure of F1-modules (in the sense of Segal’s Γ-sets) differs fundamen-
tally from that of abelian groups: addition is encoded through a family of n-ary hyper-operations
that are multivalued and do not satisfy classical associativity.
We establish a law of general-
ized associativity showing that, despite this failure of strict associativity, all n-ary sums are con-
trolled by successive binary operations. This enables us to construct an extension of scalars functor
−⊗F1 Z : F1Mod →Ab that universally strictifies the hyper-additive structure of F1-modules into
classical abelian group addition. We prove this functor is left adjoint to the Eilenberg-MacLane
functor H : Ab →F1Mod.
Extending to the multiplicative setting, we obtain an adjunction
−⊗F1 Z : F1Alg ⇆CRing : H between commutative F1-algebras and commutative rings. This
recovers Deitmar’s monoid ring construction for spherical monoid algebras and provides a base
change mechanism needed for absolute algebraic geometry.

1. Introduction
1.1. Motivation: Hyperfields and the field with one element. The notion of a “field with one
element,” denoted F1, originates in Tits’ work on spherical buildings, where certain combinatorial
formulas suggested the existence of geometric objects “over F1”.
While F1 cannot exist as a
classical field, various frameworks have been proposed to give it mathematical meaning, including
Deitmar’s monoid schemes [Dei05], To¨en-Vaqui´e’s relative schemes [TV09], Lorscheid’s blueprints
[Lor12], Connes-Consani’s S-algebras [CC16, CC21], and Borger’s λ-rings [Bor09].
One particular challenge in developing F1-geometry is understanding its “additive” structure.
Deitmar’s approach, by considering monoids, effectively neglects the existence of addition alto-
gether, whereas Lorscheid’s blueprints involve partially defined addition, incorporating the monoid
case. In this paper, we propose the model of F1-modules as Γ-sets, following Connes-Consani’s
framework [CC16]. This allows for even more possibilities, namely multiply defined addition, or
hyper-operation.
In hyperstructure theory [Kra83, CL03], the notion of binary operation is generalized to a hyper-
operation: a map ⊕: H × H →P(H) assigning to each pair of elements not a unique sum, but
a set of possible sums. This accounts for the possibility of an empty sum, the classic one-element
sum, or a multiply defined sum, and is naturally linked to F1-geometry.
However, classical hyperfields have certain limitations: they capture only binary hyper-operation
and impose axioms (existence of additive inverses, distributivity, associativity) that don’t extend
naturally beyond fields. To overcome this deficiency, we study n-ary hyper-operations in the most
general sense, and deal with the problem of associativity in F1-modules.

Explicitly, we construct X ⊗F1 Z as a quotient of the free abelian group Z[X(1+)] by relations
encoding the binary hyper-additive structure. By Theorem A, imposing relations for binary sums
automatically ensures compatibility with all higher n-ary sums. The adjunction provides a universal

Extending to the multiplicative setting:
Theorem C (Extension of scalars for algebras). The functor −⊗F1 Z extends to commutative
F1-algebras, yielding an adjunction
−⊗F1 Z : F1Alg ⇆CRing : H.
This construction gives the following satisfying results:
• For a commutative ring R: (HR) ⊗F1 Z ∼= R
• For a pointed monoid M: (SM) ⊗F1 Z ∼= Z[M] (integral monoid ring)
In particular, we have F1 ⊗F1 Z ∼= Z.
1.3. Relation to existing work. Our construction relates to several existing approaches to F1-
geometry:
Deitmar’s monoid schemes [Dei05].
Deitmar defines F1-modules as (pointed) commutative
monoids and constructs the extension of scalars functor via the integral monoid ring construc-
tion M 7→Z[M].
Our functor generalizes this: for spherical monoid algebras SM, we recover
(SM) ⊗F1 Z ∼= Z[M], while for more general F1-modules this yields results utilizing the inherent
additive structure.
Borger’s λ-rings [Bor09]. Borger interprets F1-algebras as λ-rings, with the Witt vector functor
playing a central role, and the extension of scalars functor interpreted as the forgetful functor. We
discuss the relationship in detail in Section 5.6.
Connes-Consani’s absolute geometry [CC16, CC21]. Our work provides algebraic foundations
for their geometric program. They introduce Γ-sets as F1-modules but do not construct extension
of scalars explicitly. Our results provide the base change mechanism needed for their theory of
absolute affine schemes.
Hyperstructure theory [CL03, Lor12].
Classical hyperfields use binary hyper-operations with
axioms adapted from field theory.
Our framework allows n-ary hyper-operations for all n and
reveals that the relevant coherence condition is generalized associativity rather than axioms like
existence of additive inverses. This connects F1-geometry to the broader theory of hyperstructures.
Beardsley’s plasmas [BN24]. Beardsley and Nakamura propose plasmas (weakly unital commu-
tative hypermagmas) to encode the hypersum structure in F1-modules, establishing an adjunction
that captures information on the first two levels. Our approach could be seen as a further general-
ization: objects equipped with compatible families of n-ary hyper-operations satisfying generalized
associativity. This suggests a hierarchy of increasingly structured models for F1-geometry.
1.4. Outline. Section 2 reviews Γ-sets as F1-modules, including the Eilenberg-MacLane and spher-
ical monoid functors. Section 3 defines n-ary sums, presents examples showing failure of strict
associativity, and proves the law of generalized associativity (Theorem A). Section 4 constructs
extension of scalars for F1-modules and establishes the adjunction (Theorem B). Section 5 extends
to F1-algebras (Theorem C) and compares with existing frameworks. Section 6 discusses geometric
applications and future directions.

2. Preliminaries: Γ-sets and F1-modules
In this section, we review the theory of Segal’s Γ-sets following [DGM13] and [CC16].
We
establish the basic functors and monoidal structure that will be used throughout the paper. The
basic idea is that Γ-sets encode additive structure through their functoriality from the category Γop
of pointed finite sets, while additional multiplicative structure can be imposed to obtain F1-algebras.
2.1. The category Γop.
Definition 2.1. The category Γop has:
• Objects: Pointed finite sets n+ := {0, 1, . . . , n} for each integer n ≥0, where 0 is the
basepoint.
• Morphisms: Pointed maps (i.e., maps preserving the basepoint 0).
Equivalently, Γop is the skeleton of the category of finite pointed sets.
Definition 2.2. A Γ-set is a pointed functor F : Γop →Set∗from Γop to the category of pointed
sets. The pointed set F(n+) is called the n-th level of F. We denote by ΓSet∗the category of
Γ-sets, whose morphisms are natural transformations.
Remark 2.3. In the language of Segal [DGM13], Γ-sets are Γ-spaces with discrete topology, where
Γ-spaces provide a model for connective spectra. In the language of Connes-Consani [CC16], Γ-sets
are F1-modules (or denoted as S-modules), emphasizing their role as the fundamental objects in
F1-geometry. This connection to homotopy theory is one reason Γ-sets provide a natural framework
for absolute algebraic geometry.

3. Hyper-operations in F1-modules
In this section, we analyze the additive structure encoded in Γ-sets. Unlike classical abelian
groups, where addition is a binary operation + : M × M →M with a unique output, the additive
data of a Γ-set is encoded through a family of n-ary hyper-operations—operations whose outputs
are sets rather than single elements. We formalize this notion through the concept of n-ary sums,
establish that strict associativity fails in general, and prove the law of generalized associativity that
nonetheless controls all higher sums.
3.1. Hyper-operations: Definition and basic properties. Before defining n-ary sums in Γ-
sets, we establish the general framework of hyper-operations from hyperstructure theory.
This
provides the language for discussing multivalued operations systematically.
Definition 3.1. An n-ary hyper-operation on a nonempty set H is a map
f : Hn →P(H)
where P(H) denotes the power set of H. The number n is called the arity of f.
In particular, a binary hyper-operation is a map
⊕: H × H →P(H).
Remark 3.2. In some literature [CL03], hyper-operations are restricted to P∗(H), the non-empty
subsets of H, ensuring outputs are always “multivalued” in a strict sense. We allow the empty set
as an output to accommodate cases where certain operations are undefined, as it occurs naturally
in F1-modules (see Example 3.10 below). An algebraic structure satisfying similar axioms to a field,
but where the addition is a hyper-operation is called a hyperfield. [CL03]

3.6. Interpretation as higher hyperstructures. Having established the law of generalized as-
sociativity, we can now articulate the precise sense in which F1-modules generalize classical additive
structures. This provides conceptual context for the extension of scalars construction in the next
section.
Remark 3.20. An F1-module X can be viewed as consisting of:
(i) A pointed set X(1+) (the “underlying set”)
(ii) A family of n-ary hyper-operations L : X(1+)n →P(X(1+)) for each n ≥2
(iii) Satisfying the law of generalized associativity (Theorem 3.16)
In contrast, a Z-module (abelian group) M consists of:
(i) A pointed set M (the “underlying set”)
(ii) A family of n-ary operations + : Mn →M for each n ≥2 (all reducible to binary +)
(iii) Satisfying classical associativity: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
In the category of abelian groups, all n-ary operations reduce to iterated binary operations by
associativity, and the associativity law reduces to the 3-element equation (a + b) + c = a + (b + c).
The Γ-set framework makes explicit the “higher additive structures” that are implicit (and trivial)
in classical algebra. From this perspective:
• The Eilenberg-MacLane functor H : Ab →F1Mod can be seen as a forgetful functor,
forgeting that the hyper-operations are strict (have unique results).
• The natural question is: does there exist a left adjoint in the opposite direction? That
is, can we universally “strictify” a family of n-ary hyper-operations satisfying generalized
associativity into a single binary operation?
The answer is yes, and this left adjoint is precisely the extension of scalars functor −⊗F1 Z.

5. Extension of scalars for F1-algebras
In the previous section, we constructed the extension of scalars functor for F1-modules, which
strictifies the hyper-additive structure into classical abelian group addition. For applications to
algebraic geometry, we need more than just abelian groups—we need rings with both addition
and multiplication. This requires extending our construction to F1-algebras, which incorporate
multiplicative structure alongside the additive hyper-structure.
Since an F1-algebra A is a Γ-set with additional multiplicative structure making it a monoid
object in the symmetric monoidal category (ΓSet∗, ∧, F1), the underlying Γ-set determines an
abelian group A⊗F1Z by the construction of Section 4, and the multiplicative structure of A induces
a compatible ring structure on this abelian group. The result is a functor −⊗F1Z : F1Alg →CRing
left adjoint to the Eilenberg-MacLane embedding of rings into F1-algebras.

6. Geometric interpretation and future directions
Recall from classical algebraic geometry that for any commutative ring R, the base change functor

given by SpecA 7→Spec(A ⊗Z R).
Our construction provides an analogous functor for absolute algebraic geometry. In forthcoming
work [Xu26a], we develop a theory of affine schemes over F1 (or absolute affine schemes) by defining,

This construction provides the algebraic foundations for the Connes-Consani program of absolute
algebraic geometry [CC16, CC21]. While they introduce Γ-sets as F1-modules and develop the
geometric framework, an explicit extension of scalars functor is not provided. Our results fill this

of Γ-sets can be strictified despite lacking classical associativity, and the adjunction (−⊗F1 Z) ⊣H
(Theorems 4.7 and 5.9) provides a base change mechanism.
We hope that this work provides useful foundations for further developments in absolute algebraic
geometry and its applications.
