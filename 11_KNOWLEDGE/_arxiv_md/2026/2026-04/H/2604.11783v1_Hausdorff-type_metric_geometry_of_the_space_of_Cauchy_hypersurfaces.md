THE SPACE OF CAUCHY HYPERSURFACES
CHRISTIAN LANGE AND JONAS W. PETERANDERL
Abstract. We equip the space of Cauchy hypersurfaces in a globally hyperbolic spacetime
with a natural Hausdorff-type metric and study its properties, in particular completeness
and local compactness, for Lorentzian manifolds and in more general synthetic Lorentzian
settings. For this purpose, we also generalize results on completeness properties of spacetimes
due to Beem and Takahashi.
1. Introduction and main results
Cauchy hypersurfaces can be defined as subsets of a spacetime that are met exactly once by
every physical observer or, more precisely, by every inextendible timelike curve. Therefore,
they can be regarded as “instances in time”. As such, they are natural sets on which to
prescribe initial conditions for the Einstein equations. By a landmark result of Geroch [Ge70],
their existence in a given smooth Lorentzian manifold characterizes the strongest and most
important causality condition in general relativity: global hyperbolicity. This condition is
essential, for instance, in the singularity theorems of Hawking and Penrose [HP70, HE73] as
well as for Lorentzian splitting theorems as developed in [Es88, Ga89].
Motivated by questions from Lorentzian dynamics and by the lack of a canonical choice of
a Cauchy hypersurface in a given spacetime, Monclair [Mo23] recently initiated the study of
the space of Cauchy hypersurfaces by analyzing a weak Riemannian metric, defined in terms
of an L2-inner product, on an infinite-dimensional manifold of compact spacelike Cauchy
hypersurfaces in a smooth spatially compact, globally hyperbolic spacetime. In particular,
Monclair shows that the induced path distance of this weak Riemannian metric is not only a
pseudometric, but an actual metric [Mo23, Theorem 1.1].
In this work we endow the space of all Cauchy hypersurfaces in a given spacetime with a
natural Hausdorff-type metric, which can be regarded as an L∞-Finsler analogue of Monclair’s
metric. While our metric is not Riemannian anymore, it has the advantage that it does not
rely on smoothness assumptions so that it can also be defined in abstract synthetic settings;
see Section 2.
For simplicity, we first give a more precise description in the setting of a smooth globally
hyperbolic Lorentzian manifold (M, g). In this case, the induced Lorentzian distance d on
M is finite and continuous; see [BEE96, Lemma 4.5], for instance. In the special case of

The notion of a KS-Lorentzian pre-length space underlies another notion introduced by

x to y and for which further causality and localizability properties motivated by Lorentzian

space in the sense of Kunzinger–S¨amann as a KS-Lorentzian length space in the following.

Here we explain that the proof of [Mi25, Theorem 21], in particular, provides a strong

The discussion is based on the following definition in the setting of a Lorentzian pre-length
