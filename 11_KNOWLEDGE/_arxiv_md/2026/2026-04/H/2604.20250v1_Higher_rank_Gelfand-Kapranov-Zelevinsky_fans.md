HIGHER RANK GELFAND-KAPRANOV-ZELEVINSKY FANS
ROCCO CHIRIV`I, MARTINA COSTA CESARI, XIN FANG, AND PETER LITTELMANN
Abstract. We define and study the higher rank GKZ-fans of point configurations,
where the rank one cases coincide with the usual GKZ-fans. A point in a higher rank
GKZ-fan is then used to construct higher rank quasi-valuations to degenerate the toric
variety associated to the point configuration flatly to a reduced union of toric varieties.
Such a union encodes the polytopal subdivision arising from the point in the higher
rank GKZ-fan.
1. Introduction
In the paper we fix K to be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
1.1. GKZ-cones and GKZ-fans. The GKZ-cones and GKZ-fans, named after Gelfand,
Kapranov and Zelevinsky [11], originate in their work on A-hypergeometric systems.
Their main motivation was to give a modern treatment to the classical resultants and
discriminants using Newton polytopes and toric geometry.
Precisely, let A ⊆Zn be a finite set of points, called a point configuration. Their
convex hull P is a polytope in Rn with integral vertices. A point in a GKZ-cone is a
coherent assignment of a real number to each element in A, called a height function, i.e.,
a function A →R. Geometrically, one looks at the graph of this height function, takes
the convex hull of the graph, and projects down all “upper faces” of this convex hull to
the polytope P. The images of these projected faces form a polytopal subdivision of P
into polytopes. All polytopal subdivisions of P arising in this way from a height function
are called regular (in [11] they were called coherent). A GKZ-cone gathers all height
functions giving the same polytopal subdivision of P; in loc.cit. it has been shown that
all GKZ-cones can be glued together into a complete fan, called the secondary fan.
The algebraic geometry behind a point configuration is the toric variety XA, defined
as the closure of the (K∗)A-orbit though all characters in A. Such a toric variety carries
naturally an embedding into the projective space of the characters.
Let IA be the
defining ideal of XA in this embedding. Sturmfels [17] studied the Gr¨obner bases of the
ideal IA, and made use of these height functions to construct Gr¨obner degenerations of
the embedded toric variety XA into unions of toric schemes. More precisely, using the
height function, he constructed a flat family X →A1 whose generic fiber is isomorphic
to XA and the reduced structure of the special fiber encodes the combinatorics of the
polytopal subdivision.

The algebraic structure behind this assignment is the notion of a quasi-valuation on the
homogeneous coordinate ring of XP with images in Qn+1.
What good in this framework is the extra structure on the image Γ of the quasi-
valuation: in fact Γ is a finite union of finitely generated monoids, one for each maximal
chain in the face lattice of the polytope P. Geometrically, we proved that there exists
a flat family X →A1 whose generic fiber is isomorphic to XP and the special fiber is
Proj(K[Γ]), where K[Γ] is the fan algebra of Γ. This special fiber is a reduced union of
equidimensional toric varieties, one for each maximal chain in the face lattice of P. The
multiplication rule in this fan algebra encodes a barycentral-type polytopal subdivision
of P.
Compared to the height function used in the Gr¨obner degeneration, the degenerations
studied here are in the direction of a SAGBI (subalgebra analog to Gr¨obner bases of
ideals) degeneration, where the initial form of a homogeneous function is given by its
image under the quasi-valuation. The main difference between the Gr¨obner and SAGBI
approaches lies in the fact that the algebra generated by the initial form, called the
initial algebra, in the SAGBI setting is not always finitely generated. The approach
using Seshadri stratification overcomes this difficulty: the quasi-valuation admits a
finite Khovanskii basis [5] and the fan algebra K[Γ] is always finitely generated.
In this paper, we will construct a large family of higher rank quasi-valuations on the
homogeneous coordinate ring of an embedded toric variety having finitely generated
initial algebras (a.k.a. finite Khovanskii bases). These higher rank quasi-valuations
arise from the higher rank GKZ-fans.
1.3. Higher rank GKZ-fans. In [11], all possible such height functions which induce
regular subdivisions are studied: they form a complete polyhedral fan in RA, the vector
space of functions from A to R. Motivated by the higher rank quasi-valuations con-
structed in [3], we intend to associate to each point in A a vector in RN for some N ≥1
and not just a real number. This natural idea leads to the following questions:
(1) How to define analogues of the GKZ-cones in this setup? What is the discrete
geometric structure of the analogues of the GKZ-cones?
(2) With the structure in (1), can these analogues of GKZ-cones be glued together
into a complete fan?

(3) How to define a quasi-valuation from a point in the analogue of a GKZ-cone?

The goal of the paper is to give affirmative answers to all three questions.
Fix two integers n ≥2 and N ≥1. Indexing elements in A by χ1, . . . , χr ∈Rn−1, a
map from A to RN can be represented by a matrix in MN,r(R) where the j-th column
is the image of χj under the map. Instead of the pairing between Rr and itself via the
Euclidean inner product Rr × Rr →R in the polyhedral geometry, what we have in our
setup is only a bilinear map µ : MN,r(R) × Rr →RN given by matrix multiplication.
To speak about polyhedral objects in polyhedral geometry, one uses essentially the
standard order structure on R, the target space of the inner product. When the image
lies in a vector space RN but R, to define an analogue of a polyhedral object, a total
order has to be fixed on RN. In this paper we fix on RN the lexicographic order, and
define similarly the closed half spaces for Φ ∈MN,r(R) and w ∈Rr:

What
makes the difference between these two notions is the lexicographic order: in general,
the closed half spaces are no longer closed in the Euclidean topology on Rr and on
MN,r(R). To get rid of this issue, we work with the order topology on Rr and the
product order topology on MN,r(R). When N ≥2, these topologies are strictly finer
than the Euclidean topology and its products. With this topology, the real vector space
RN is no longer a topological vector space, and moreover, the famous Farkas lemma on
the existence of separating hyperplanes fails: studying geometry of µ-polyhedral cones
needs new tools.
Parallel to the usual polyhedral geometry, in Section 2 we develop the discrete ge-
ometry of µ-polyhedral cones such as polar duality (Theorem 2.18), faces and co-faces
(Lemma 2.4 and Proposition 2.22), fans and co-fans (Theorem 2.26) and their basic
properties and behaviors under polar duality. The new tool here is the notion of co-
faces of a convex cone, which is new up to our knowledge. It enables us to give short
proofs for properties of faces in the µ-polyhedral setup.
The higher rank GKZ-cones are defined and studied in Section 3.
Let P be the
convex hull of A and Q be a polytopal subdivision of P. The higher rank GKZ-cone
C(Q, N) is defined similar to the one in [11] by replacing functions to R by functions
to RN, and the total order on R by the lexicographic order on RN (Definition 3.18,
and Definition 3.13 for its closure). To show that the higher rank GKZ-cones are µ-

the GKZ-conditions-cones corresponds exactly to coarsening the polytopal subdivision
(Proposition 3.25). Moreover, using GKZ-condition-cones, we define the notion of reg-
ular subdivisions (Definition 3.28): it is shown that the definition coincides with the
usual one when N = 1. The higher rank GKZ-cones associated to regular subdivisions
can be glued together, they yield a complete µ-polyhedral fan in MN,r(R) (Theorem
3.21 and 3.31). The question (1) and (2) above have been fully answered so far.

1.4. GKZ-cones and quasi-valuations. In Section 4, starting from a point Ψ in
the higher rank GKZ-cone C(Q, N), we define two quasi-valuations VΨ and νΨ with
values in QN ∪{∞} on the homogeneous coordinate ring K[XA] of the toric variety XA
associated to the point configuration A, embedded to the projective space P(KA) using
points in A. The first one VΨ comes from the construction of the regular polytopal
subdivision associated to Ψ; and the second one νΨ is the push-forward of the natural
valuation defined on K[KA]. Their basic properties are studied in Section 4.
The comparison of these two quasi-valuations is the main theme of Section 5. We
first show that VΨ dominants νΨ, and they coincides on a large subalgebra of K[XA]
(Proposition 5.5). In fact, quite surprisingly, the set {VΨ(f)−νΨ(f) | f ∈K[XA], f ̸= 0}
is finite (Theorem 5.6).

4.2. Generalities on quasi-valuations and examples. We start with a slightly more
general setting. In the following let S = K[x1, . . . , xq] be a polynomial ring, and let
R = S/I be the K-algebra obtained as the quotient of S by a homogeneous radical ideal
I. The case we have in mind is q = r and I = IA (see Lemma 4.2 for the definition).
For N ≥1 consider the additive group (QN, +), endowed with the lexicographic
order. The order is compatible with the group structure of (QN, +) and multiplication
by positive rational numbers. To simplify the notation, we often tacitly add an element
∞to the ordered group QN which is assumed to be larger than any other element, and
any element, when added to ∞, equals ∞.
Definition 4.3. A quasi-valuation on R with values in the ordered group (QN, +) is a
map ν : R →QN ∪{∞} having the following properties for any x, y ∈R and λ ∈K∗:
(0) ν(x) = ∞if and only if x = 0;
(1) ν(x + y) ≥min{ν(x), ν(y)} (minimum property);
(2) ν(λx) = ν(x);
(3) ν(xy) ≥ν(x) + ν(y) (super-additivity).
Property (3) implies ν(xm) ≥mν(x) for m ∈N. The quasi-valuation ν is called
radical if ν(xm) = mν(x) for all m ∈N. If ν is always additive, i.e. ν(xy) = ν(x)+ν(y)
for all x, y ∈R, then it is called a valuation.
Throughout the following we assume the quasi-valuation to be discrete, i.e., the image,
without ∞, is a discrete subset of QN.
