HOMOLOGICAL ASPECTS OF SEPARABLE EXTENSIONS OF TRIANGULATED
CATEGORIES
MILTIADIS KARAKIKES AND PANAGIOTIS KOSTAS
Abstract. We investigate the homological behaviour of compactly generated triangulated categories
under separable extensions. We show that homological invariants (finiteness of global dimension, goren-
steinness and regularity) are preserved under such extensions.
We also establish a relation between
singularity categories in this setting, proving that the singularity category of a separable extension
is equivalent, up to retracts, to a separable extension of the singularity category.
Our results unify
and extend classical phenomena from commutative and equivariant algebra, and provide new examples
involving separable extensions of rings, quotient schemes, and skew group dg algebras.
1. Introduction
Separability is a recurrent theme in algebra. In commutative algebra, it is closely linked to ´etale exten-
sions, while in the noncommutative setting it naturally appears in skew group rings associated with finite
group actions of invertible order. Such constructions typically preserve homological invariants, including
finiteness of global dimension, gorensteinness, and regularity. This behaviour was first established in [14]
for ´etale morphisms of schemes and in [33] for skew group algebras.
Balmer [2,3] studied the category of modules over a separable exact monad on a triangulated category.
He showed that the latter, under mild assumptions, admits a unique canonical triangulated structure.
Both the derived category of an ´etale extension and the derived category of a skew group ring can, then,
be described as categories of modules over the derived category of their respective base rings.
The purpose of this paper is to formulate and exploit the homological behaviour of separable extensions
in a broader and more conceptual setting. Rather than working directly with rings and modules, we base
ourselves on the work of Balmer, and use instead triangulated categories and separable monads.
In
doing so, we provide a common framework that not only explains, but also extends, the homological
behaviour observed in classical commutative and equivariant settings. As for the homological part, we
use the intrinsic notions of finite global dimension, gorensteinness and regularity of compactly generated
triangulated categories, defined in [23].
Our first theorem showcases the interplay of homological invariants for separable constructions; see
Theorem 3.7 and Proposition 3.8. Below we present, for convenience, only its “equivariant” form, which
is proved in Corollary 4.2.
Theorem A. Let T be a compactly generated triangulated category and assume an action of a finite
group G on T such that |G| is invertible in T and further that TG is canonically triangulated. Consider
the following conditions that may be satisfied.
(i) T has finite global dimension.
(ii) T is Gorenstein.
(iii) T is regular (when T is k-linear).
Then T satisfies any of (i)-(iii) if and only if TG satisfies the same condition.
The above theorem, together with its more general form, subsume the homological behaviour of skew
group algebras observed in [33], cover the transfer of regularity for proper schemes along a group action
(Corollary 4.6) or an ´etale morphism (Corollary 4.14) and also provide completely new examples of smooth
and Gorenstein skew group dg algebras (see Corollary 4.11). The assumption that TG is canonically
triangulated is mild; as already mentioned, TG admits a canonical pre-triangulated structure and in
practice it is triangulated; see also Convention 1.

2. Preliminaries
We collect some preliminaries, that are used throughout the paper, regarding separable extensions of
triangulated categories [2,3] and intrinsic homological algebra for triangulated categories [23].
2.1. Separable extensions. Let A be an additive category. A monad on A is a triple (M, η, µ) consisting
of an additive endofunctor M: A →A, a natural transformation η: IdA →M called the unit and a natural
transformation µ: M2 →M called multiplication, subject to the following relations:
µ ◦Mµ = µ ◦µM
and
µ ◦Mη = µ ◦ηM.
Further, a monad as above is called separable if µ admits a section, i.e. there exists a natural transfor-
mation σ: M →M2 such that µ ◦σ = IdM and moreover
Mµ ◦σM = σµ = µM ◦Mσ.
When A is a pre-triangulated category, we further require that M, η, µ, σ commute with the suspension,
see [2] for details. Henceforth, we will be simply denoting a monad by M. We now recall the definition
of the Eilenberg-Moore category [12] associated to a monad, following the notation from [2].
Definition 2.1. Let M be a monad on an additive category A.
• An M-module is a pair (x, u) where x is an object in A and u is a morphism M(x) →x satisfying
u ◦Mu = u ◦µx and 1x = u ◦ηx.
• A morphism f : (x, u) →(y, v) of M-modules is a morphism f : x →y such that v ◦M(f) = f ◦u.
The Eilenberg-Moore category of M, henceforth denoted by M-ModA, is the category with objects the
M-modules and morphisms the morphisms of M-modules.
Lemma 2.2. ([12]) Let M be a monad on an additive category A. There exist functors, the forgetful
functor UM and the induction functor TM, defined as follows.
(i) UM : M-ModA →A is given by UM(x, u) = x on objects and by UM(f) = f on morphisms.
(ii) TM : A →M-ModA is given by TM(x) = (M(x), µx) on objects and TM(f) = M(f) on morphisms.
Observe that M = UMTM and, moreover, it holds that (TM, UM) is an adjoint pair.
Recall that a functor e: D →C between additive categories is separable if for all x, y ∈D, the maps
HomD(x, y) →HomC(e(x), e(y)) admit retractions, which are natural in x, y. In the setup of Lemma 2.2,
the functor UM is separable if and only if M is a separable monad; see for instance [9, Lemma 3.2].

2.1.2. Equivariant triangulated categories. For an in depth exposition of group actions on categories we
refer to [9,13,34]; instead let us quickly recall some facts.
Let A be an additive category and G a finite group. Denote by Aut(A) the group of isomorphism classes
of autoequivalences of A. A group action of G on A is a group homomorphism ρ: G →Aut(A), g 7→ρg,
such that the natural isomorphisms θg,h : ρgρh →ρgh satisfy θg,hk ◦ρgθh,k = θgh,k ◦θg,hρk (i.e. they are
associative). Given a group action on (any) category, one defines the equivariant category AG to be the
category with objects pairs (x, ϕ), where ϕ = {ϕg}g∈G is a family of isomorphisms ϕg : x →ρg(x) indexed
by G, called a linearization of x, satisfying θg,h ◦ρg(ϕh) ◦ϕg = ϕgh. The morphisms of the equivariant
category AG are the morphisms of A that respect the linearizations. Note that AG is additive (resp.
abelian) when A is additive (resp. abelian).
Under the above setup, there exist the following functors: the forgetful functor F: AG →A and the
induction functor Ind: A →AG, which form an adjoint triple (Ind, F, Ind).
If |G| is invertible in A,
then the monad M = F ◦Ind, which is given by M(x) = ⊕g∈Gρg(x), is separable; see for instance [34,
Lemma 2.14(2)]. Moreover, we know that the comparison functor AG →M-ModA is an equivalence; see
for instance [13, Proposition 3.11].
For a triangulated category T, an action of a finite group G on T is an action as above, where Aut(T)
denotes the group of isomorphism classes of exact autoequivalences of T. By the preceding discussion,
Theorem 2.3 and Lemma 2.4, it follows that TG admits a unique pre-triangulated structure such that
the forgetful functor is exact; see [34, Proposition 3.3] for details. In this case, the induced monad M is
exact and smashing, as a consequence of the ambidextrous adjunction.

6. Global dimension via t-structures
It is slightly unfortunate in our setup that we can only conclude, for instance for derived categories of
rings, that, under the assumptions of Corollary 4.3, gl. dim D(R) < ∞if and only if gl. dim D(R#G) < ∞,
while it is well-known that gl. dim R#G = gl. dim R (see [33]). We remedy this by considering a notion of
global dimension relative to a given t-structure defined in [22].

Definition 6.1. Let T be a triangulated category and (U, V) a t-structure in T with heart H := U∩V[1].
We say that the global dimension of T relative to H is equal to n ≥0, denoted by gl. dimH T = n, if
HomT(x, y[>n]) = 0, for all x, y ∈H
and n is the smallest integer with this property. If no such n exists, then the global dimension is infinite
and we write gl. dimH T = ∞.
Remark 6.2. When M is a compact silting generator of a compactly generated triangulated category
T (i.e. thick(M) = Tc and HomT(M, M[> 0]) = 0), then there is a t-structure (M ⊥>0, M ⊥≤0) with heart

We have the following result.
Proposition 6.3. Let T be a triangulated category and M a separable exact monad on T. Assume the
existence of t-structures in T and M-ModT =: D, say with hearts HT and HD respectively, such that the
functors TM and UM are t-exact. The following hold.
(i) gl. dimHD D ≤gl. dimHT T.
(ii) If x is a summand of M(x) for every x ∈T, then gl. dimHT T ≤gl. dimHD D.
Proof. (i) Assume that gl. dimHT T = n and let x, y be objects in HD. Then we have
HomD(TMUM(x), y[>n]) ∼= HomT(UM(x), UM(y)[>n]) ∼= 0,
since UM(x) and UM(y) both belong in HT, because UM is t-exact. By Lemma 2.5, we know that x is a
summand of TMUM(x), so it follows that HomD(x, y[>n]) ∼= 0. Therefore gl. dimHD D ≤n.
(ii) Assume that gl. dimHD D = n and let x, y be objects in HT. Then we have
HomT(x, UMTM(y)[>n]) ∼= HomD(TM(x), TM(y)[>n]) ∼= 0,
since TM(x), TM(y) belong in HD, as a consequence of t-exactness of TM. Since y is assumed to be a
summand of M(y) = UMTM(y), it follows that HomT(x, y[>n]) = 0, and therefore gl. dimHT T ≤n.
□
