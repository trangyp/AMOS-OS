Hybrid Six-Level Rydberg Atomic Quantum
Receiver for Multi-Band RF Communication
Lahiru Shyamal, Student Member, IEEE, Harini Hapuarachchi,
Saman Atapattu, Senior Member, IEEE, and Jared H. Cole
Abstract—Rydberg atomic receivers have recently emerged
as a promising platform for radio-frequency (RF) sensing
and reception due to their intrinsic broadband response and
calibration-free operation. Most existing receivers rely on four-
level ladder-type electromagnetically induced transparency
(EIT) schemes, which limit the number of simultaneously
accessible RF transitions within a given atomic manifold. In
this paper, a six-level hybrid Rydberg atomic quantum receiver
(H-RAQR) architecture is proposed that combines cascaded
and parallel RF coupling pathways to enable simultaneous
multi-band RF reception within a single vapor-cell platform.
A physically consistent system and electromagnetic coupling
model is developed, and a steady-state analytical expression
for the probe coherence is derived, establishing a direct re-
lationship between the incident RF fields and the optical
probe transmission. The analytical model is validated through
numerical simulations of the Lindblad master equation with re-
alistic relaxation and detuning parameters. Using the resulting
communication signal representation, the achievable ergodic
sum-rate performance of the receiver is evaluated. Numerical
results demonstrate that the proposed hybrid architecture
enables four simultaneous RF channels within the same six-
level system and achieves higher throughput than conventional
cascade Rydberg state (CRS) and parallel Rydberg state (PRS)
receivers. These results demonstrate the potential of hybrid
Rydberg receiver architectures for scalable multi-channel RF
sensing and communication systems.
Index Terms—Rydberg atomic receiver, RF sensing, electro-
magnetically induced transparency, multi-band RF reception,
atomic sensors.

A. Related Works
Early Rydberg RF sensing employed four-level ladder-
type atomic configurations [11]–[13], where probe and
coupling lasers establish an EIT window while a single
RF field couples two high-lying Rydberg states. Holloway
et al. [14] demonstrated coherent recovery of phase-
modulated signals (BPSK, QPSK, and QAM), establish-
ing RAQRs as phase-sensitive receivers. Subsequent het-
erodyne and superheterodyne architectures incorporating
RF local oscillators (LOs) improved sensitivity and en-
abled frequency down-conversion [12], and a variety of Ry-
dberg atomic receiver and RF sensing architectures have
since been demonstrated, including resonator-enhanced
sensitivity [15], [16], atomic receiver communication [12],
laser-array architectures [3], antenna-assisted and high-
frequency RF sensing [1], and broadband receivers with
array implementations [5], [6]. More recently, system-
level models bridging quantum sensing and communica-
tion theory were developed: Gong et al. [17] derived an
equivalent baseband signal model, while Cui et al. [5]
proposed a Rydberg-based MIMO architecture. Despite
these advances, most receivers operate on a single RF
transition. To extend spectral capability, two architectural
approaches have been explored: Parallel Rydberg States
(PRS) and Cascade Rydberg States (CRS).
PRS architectures exploit multiple RF-accessible Ryd-
berg transitions that share common intermediate states or
employ parallel excitation pathways. This enables concur-
rent detection of multiple frequency bands within a single
vapor cell. Meyer et al. [18] demonstrated simultaneous
demodulation of five RF tones spanning 1.7–116 GHz,

B. Research Gap, Motivation, and Contributions
Despite advances in PRS and CRS architectures, both
leave many dipole-allowed transitions unused: PRS ne-
glects intermediate couplings among upper states [18]–
[20], while CRS omits direct couplings between non-
adjacent states [21], [22], limiting simultaneously acces-
sible RF channels. From an antenna perspective, this
is analogous to a radiator that does not exploit all
available resonant modes. Therefore, receiver architectures
that exploit the full set of dipole-allowed transitions are
required for multi-channel RF reception and improved
spectral utilization.
Motivated by this, we propose a Hybrid Rydberg
Atomic Quantum Receiver (H-RAQR) that combines
PRS and CRS coupling pathways within a single atomic
system, exploiting the full dipole-allowed connectivity of
the Rydberg manifold to enable scalable multi-band RF
reception within a single vapor cell. The objective of this
work is to investigate receiver architecture and system-
level performance limits using physics-based modeling and
numerical emulation of experimentally realistic operating
conditions, prior to hardware implementation. The main
contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
• A hybrid six-level RAQR architecture is proposed
that combines parallel and cascaded RF coupling
pathways and exploits the dipole-allowed connectivity
of the Rydberg manifold to increase the number of
simultaneously accessible RF transitions.
• A physically consistent system model and analytical
steady-state solution for the six-level atomic system
are derived and validated using full density-matrix
simulations based on the Lindblad master equation.
• An equivalent communication signal model is de-
veloped and the achievable ergodic sum-rate perfor-
mance of the hybrid receiver is evaluated and com-
pared with conventional CRS and PRS architectures.
Since experimental realization of multi-level hybrid
atomic receiver architectures requires complex optical and
RF laboratory setups, this work adopts a physics-based
simulation framework for performance evaluation. The
receiver operation is modeled using the density-matrix
formalism and validated through numerical simulations

B. Proposed Hybrid Rydberg Atomic Architecture
Let the atomic manifold contain K levels {|1⟩, . . . , |K⟩},
where |1⟩, |2⟩, |3⟩denote the ground, intermediate, and
initial Rydberg states, and {|4⟩, . . . , |K⟩} form the RF-
driven manifold. RF transitions within this manifold are
governed by the electric-dipole selection rule, which re-
quires opposite-parity states [23], [24], constraining the set
of accessible coupling pathways as stated in the following
remark.
Remark 1 (Electric-dipole selection rule). A transition
|i⟩↔|j⟩is dipole-allowed only if ⟨i|⃗µ|j⟩̸= 0, where ⃗µ
is the atomic dipole operator. Since the dipole operator
is odd under spatial inversion, this requires the two
states to have opposite parity, i.e., πiπj = −1 [23], [24],
where πk ∈{+1, −1} denotes the parity of state |k⟩.
Consequently, the set of feasible RF coupling pathways
in a multi-level Rydberg manifold is constrained by this
parity condition.
Within the same K-level manifold, the three coupling
architectures differ in how they exploit the dipole-allowed
transitions. In a CRS architecture, RF fields couple
adjacent states sequentially (|3⟩↔|4⟩↔· · · ↔|K⟩),
supporting NCRS(K) = K −3 transitions but with limited
multi-band flexibility. In a PRS architecture, multiple RF
fields couple |3⟩directly to even-offset levels (|4⟩, |6⟩, . . . ),
yielding NPRS(K) = ⌊(K −2)/2⌋transitions but leaving
intermediate couplings unused.
To overcome these limitations, the proposed H-RAQR
combines CRS-type sequential links with additional PRS-
type non-adjacent branches from |3⟩, wherever permitted
by the dipole selection rule. The total number of accessible
RF transitions is Nhyb(K) = (K−3)+

IV. Analytical Model for the proposed HRAQR
Building on the coupling architecture introduced in
Section III, we develop the quantum dynamical model of
the six-level H-RAQR, derive the closed-form steady-state

of the resulting analytical model.
A. Open Quantum System Model
RAQRs operate in realistic vapor-cell environments,
where the atomic ensemble is continuously driven by
optical and RF fields while simultaneously interacting
with its surrounding environment. As a result, the system
cannot be treated as an isolated quantum system. Instead,
dissipative processes such as spontaneous emission play
a crucial role in determining the receiver dynamics and
detection performance. To accurately capture both coher-
ent atom–field interactions and irreversible environmental
effects, we model the six-level Rydberg atomic receiver as
an open quantum system.

C. Validity and Limitations of the Analytical Model
To assess the validity of the closed-form analytical
expression derived in Section IV-B, the steady-state probe
coherence term ρ2,1 is compared with numerical solutions
obtained from the Liouvillian superoperator in (18). The
analytical model assumes resonant excitation (∆n = 0,
n ∈{P, C, 1, 2, 3, 4}) and retains only the dominant
decay channel γ2,1, whereas the numerical simulations
incorporate experimentally realistic conditions including
finite detunings (∆n ̸= 0, n ∈{P, C, 1, 2, 3, 4}), and the
full set of decay channels {γi,j : (i, j) ∈D}. The upper-
state decay rates are taken to be several orders of mag-
nitude smaller than γ2,1, consistent with experimentally
reported parameters for Rydberg atomic receivers [8],
[20]. This comparison provides a systematic evaluation
of the robustness of the analytical model under realistic
experimental conditions.
The analysis reveals two parameter regimes where the
simplified analytical model exhibits noticeable deviations
from the full numerical solution.
1) Weak RF–Coupling Regime (Ω2, Ω3 →0). When the
RF coupling strengths Ω2 and Ω3 become very small,
the analytical model predicts ρ2,1 →0, whereas
the numerical solution of the full master equation
remains finite. This discrepancy originates from the
simplifying assumption that neglects decay processes
in the upper Rydberg manifold. In the analytical
model, the absence of upper-manifold decay channels
allows population to accumulate unphysically in the
upper Rydberg states when Ω2 and Ω3 are insuﬀi-
cient to couple them back to the lower levels, leaving
population trapped with no dissipative pathway out.
In contrast, the numerical model includes weak but
finite decay processes that redistribute population
back toward the lower levels, thereby stabilizing the
steady state and yielding a nonzero probe coherence.
2) Interference-Balanced Regime (Ω1Ω3 ≈Ω2Ω4). A
second regime arises when the condition Ω1Ω3 ≈
Ω2Ω4 is satisfied. This relation corresponds to de-
structive interference between the sequential path-
way |3⟩↔|4⟩↔|5⟩↔|6⟩and the direct transition
|3⟩↔|6⟩. Under this interference condition the
effective coupling between the Rydberg manifold
and the lower optical states vanishes, leading to
ρ2,1 →0. In this regime the analytical and numerical
predictions for ρ2,1 are in agreement. However, nu-
merical simulations reveal slow relaxation dynamics
within the Rydberg subspace {|3⟩, |4⟩, |5⟩, |6⟩} due

remain accurate only within a narrow neighborhood of
the LO operating point. The operating regime in which

D. Communication Performance Comparison
We evaluate the communication performance of the
proposed H-RAQR using the ergodic sum-rate metric
derived in Sec. VI-C. The performance is compared against
two reference architectures: the conventional CRS scheme
and the PRS scheme. All three receivers are simulated
using the same atomic system, vapor cell parameters, and
optical laser configuration to ensure a fair comparison.
Within the considered six-level manifold, the achiev-
able number of RF channels differs across the three
architectures. The PRS scheme supports only two RF
transitions (ω1, ω4) because any additional intermediate
coupling (e.g., |3⟩↔|5⟩) would introduce a dipole-
forbidden triangular loop as discussed in Remark 1. The
CRS architecture supports three sequential transitions but
does not activate the non-adjacent coupling |3⟩↔|6⟩. In
contrast, the proposed hybrid architecture exploits the
full dipole-allowed connectivity of the six-level system
and simultaneously activates four RF transitions. Con-
sequently, the hybrid receiver provides a larger number
of communication channels within the same atomic struc-
ture. The achievable ergodic sum-rate results are shown
in Figs. 7(a) and (b) under two representative Rabi-
frequency configurations in normalized units:
Set1 = {7, 1, 1, 1},

all three receiver architectures. This ensures that the
comparison reflects realistic operating conditions while
maintaining architectural fairness.
Fig. 7(a) shows the achievable rate as a function of
transmitted RF power with baseband bandwidth B =
100 kHz. The hybrid receiver consistently achieves higher
throughput across the entire power range due to its ability
to simultaneously utilize four RF channels. Fig. 7(b) shows

effect of bandwidth allocation on multichannel reception.
Across both scenarios and for both Rabi-frequency con-

type and PRS-type receivers. For example, under Set2

This corresponds to performance gains of roughly 14% and
48% over the CRS scheme (2.09 Mbps) and PRS scheme
(1.62 Mbps), respectively. These improvements arise from
the hybrid coupling strategy, which enables additional
dipole-allowed RF transitions that are not accessible in
conventional CRS or PRS configurations within the same
six-level atomic manifold.
VIII. Conclusion
This paper presented a six-level hybrid Rydberg atomic
quantum receiver for simultaneous multi-band RF re-
ception within a single atomic system. By combining
cascaded and parallel RF coupling pathways, the proposed
architecture exploits additional dipole-allowed transitions
beyond conventional CRS and PRS receivers. A physi-
cally consistent system model and analytical steady-state
solution were derived and validated using physics-based
numerical simulations of the Lindblad master equation
under experimentally realistic operating conditions. An

showed that the hybrid architecture enables four simul-

receiver architectures provide a scalable approach for
multi-channel RF sensing and reception using atomic
systems. Future work will extend the hybrid architecture
