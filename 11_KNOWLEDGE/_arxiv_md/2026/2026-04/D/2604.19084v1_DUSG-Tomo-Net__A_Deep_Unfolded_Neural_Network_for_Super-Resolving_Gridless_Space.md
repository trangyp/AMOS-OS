Abstract
Synthetic aperture radar tomography (TomoSAR) enables 3-D imaging by exploiting
multibaseline acquisitions and has become an important tool for urban mapping. To
achieve super-resolution inversion, sparse reconstruction methods based on compressive
sensing (CS) are widely adopted. However, most CS-based TomoSAR methods rely on
grid-based formulations and therefore suffer from off-grid bias. Gridless formulations pro-
vide a principled way to alleviate this limitation, whereas classical Toeplitz-Vandermonde
atomic norm minimization (ANM) is not directly applicable to spaceborne TomoSAR un-
der nonuniform baselines. Existing gridless methods for nonuniform-baseline TomoSAR
avoid the classical uniform linear array (ULA) assumption, but they are usually tightly
coupled to handcrafted iterative solvers and solver-specific parameter settings, while ro-
bust inversion under limited observations and low-SNR conditions remains challenging.
To address this gap, we propose DUSG-Tomo-Net, a deep unfolded gridless framework for
single-look spaceborne TomoSAR under nonuniform baselines. The proposed method re-
formulates the inversion in a Toeplitz-compatible lag domain via a structured single-look
approximation and recovers a Hermitian Toeplitz positive-semidefinite structured covari-
ance representation through layerwise learned regularization and projection-based struc-
tural enforcement. The actual acquisition geometry is embedded analytically into the
data-consistency step via a fixed, signal-independent operator, enabling operator-based
adaptation to varying baseline configurations. Scatterer elevations are then estimated
by a continuous-domain spectral estimator without elevation discretization. Simulation
results show that DUSG-Tomo-Net achieves better low-SNR spectral reconstruction than
the compared methods and delivers super-resolution performance competitive with the
strong grid-based baseline SL1MMER. Within the covered training baseline range and
under the same sensor mode, the trained model can be reused across previously unseen
numbers and distributions of baselines by updating only the geometry-dependent oper-
ator rather than retraining the full network. Experiments on real data further demon-

1. Introduction
Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) is an important tool for large-scale earth observation
because it provides all-weather, day-and-night imaging from spaceborne and airborne
platforms. However, conventional SAR projects the three-dimensional (3-D) scattering
structure of a scene onto a two-dimensional (2-D) azimuth-range image, so scatterers
located at different elevations may overlap within the same resolution cell, giving rise to
the layover effect. SAR tomography (TomoSAR) addresses this problem by exploiting
multibaseline acquisitions to synthesize an aperture along the elevation dimension (Reig-
ber and Moreira, 2000; Fornaro et al., 2005, 2014), thereby enabling elevation-resolved
reconstruction for a variety of earth observation applications, such as urban mapping
(Zhu and Bamler, 2010a; Yang et al., 2023), deformation monitoring (Fornaro et al.,
2009), forest profiling (Cloude, 2006; Lu et al., 2021), and cryosphere observation (Wu
et al., 2011). The layover-resolving capability is especially relevant to urban mapping,
where dense built-up structures such as facades, rooftops, and ground surfaces often gen-
erate severely superposed echoes within one resolution cell, making effective scatterer
separation essential for reliable 3-D reconstruction.
In repeat-pass spaceborne SAR systems, orbital constraints usually limit both the ef-
fective cross-track aperture and the number of coherent acquisitions, so the Rayleigh
resolution along the elevation dimension of conventional TomoSAR is often on the order
of tens of meters (Zhu and Bamler, 2010a, 2012). Such a coarse elevation resolution makes
conventional Fourier-type TomoSAR estimators, such as beamforming and Capon, insuf-
ficient for separating closely spaced scatterers within one azimuth-range cell, especially
in dense urban areas affected by severe layover. Therefore, developing super-resolution
TomoSAR algorithms is necessary for dense urban mapping. In urban TomoSAR scenes,
only a few dominant scatterers are typically present within one azimuth-range resolution
cell and account for most of the backscattered energy (Zhu and Bamler, 2010a, 2012).
Given the sparse scattering characteristic along the elevation dimension, compressive
sensing (CS)-based sparse reconstruction was naturally introduced into urban TomoSAR
imaging and has become a major paradigm for super-resolution inversion.
Representative CS-TomoSAR methods, including greedy pursuit methods and ℓ1-
regularized sparse reconstruction formulations, have demonstrated substantial improve-
ments in super-resolution capability (Zhu and Bamler, 2010a; Budillon et al., 2011; Reb-
meister et al., 2021). However, most existing CS-based TomoSAR methods remain grid-
based, in which the elevation axis is discretized into a predefined dictionary and the

and spurious responses (Chi et al., 2011). Although grid refinement can partially reduce

limitation of grid-based TomoSAR inversion rather than a numerical artifact that can be
eliminated by simply using a denser grid.
Gridless formulations provide a principled route to overcoming this limitation by es-
timating scatterer locations directly in the continuous domain. Among them, atomic
norm minimization (ANM) has emerged as a representative framework for off-grid spec-
tral estimation by replacing the finite-grid sparse prior with a continuous-domain atomic
norm (Tang et al., 2013; Bhaskar et al., 2013; Wang and Xu, 2022). However, the classi-
cal Toeplitz-Vandermonde ANM formulation is naturally derived for uniformly sampled,
uniform linear array (ULA)-like observations and is therefore not directly applicable to
repeat-pass spaceborne TomoSAR with nonuniform baselines. Existing remedies can be
broadly divided into two categories. The first maps nonuniform observations to a vir-
tual uniform array before gridless recovery, but such approaches introduce additional
approximation steps and may depend on sampling redundancy or platform-specific con-
ditions (Gao et al., 2024). The second develops genuinely nonuniform-baseline gridless
formulations, including TADCG (Shao et al., 2024), GDLS (Shi et al., 2023), and recent
ANM variants for nonuniform sampling (Liu et al., 2024).
These methods avoid the
classical ULA assumption, but they are built upon handcrafted iterative optimization
and solver-specific designs. As a result, their inference procedures remain tightly cou-
pled to particular optimizers and associated parameter settings, which usually require
substantial manual tuning of solver hyperparameters.
In this context, deep algorithm unrolling (Gregor and LeCun, 2010; Monga et al.,
2021) has emerged as a principled paradigm for inverse problems, as it converts itera-
tive model-based solvers into trainable networks with deterministic finite-depth inference
while preserving explicit data-consistency and structure-enforcing operations. This idea
has already motivated a series of learned reconstruction methods in TomoSAR (Qian
et al., 2022b,a; Wang et al., 2023a; Qian et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023b; Liu et al.,
2025).
These methods demonstrate the viability of model-driven deep networks for
super-resolution inversion and also offer significant improvements in computational ef-
ficiency. However, these TomoSAR unrolling methods are still predominantly built on
discretized elevation dictionaries and therefore continue to inherit the off-grid mismatch
induced by grid-based formulations. Meanwhile, unfolded gridless or ANM-inspired net-
works have also been explored for line spectral estimation and direction-of-arrival (DOA)
estimation. Nevertheless, these studies mostly assume ULA or uniformly sampled ob-
servations, where the classical Toeplitz-Vandermonde structure is directly available (Zhu
et al., 2022). Therefore, developing a deep unfolded gridless framework tailored to single-
look spaceborne TomoSAR under nonuniform baselines remains an open methodological
challenge.
These observations point to the need for a learned TomoSAR inversion framework that
avoids a predefined elevation dictionary, accommodates nonuniform baselines, and pre-
serves the model-based structure required for spaceborne TomoSAR. To address this gap,
we propose DUSG-Tomo-Net, a deep unfolded gridless framework for super-resolution

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
• We develop DUSG-Tomo-Net, a deep unfolded gridless framework for super-
resolution TomoSAR inversion under nonuniform baselines. By performing struc-
tured estimation in a Toeplitz-compatible lag domain derived from single-look pair-

• We propose a physics-consistent, signal-independent geometry operator that ana-

covered geometry range, to unseen numbers of baselines by updating the operator
rather than retraining the network.
• We design a covariance-domain unfolded solver that combines layerwise learned
lag-sequence regularization with projection-based PSD-Toeplitz enforcement and
continuous-domain spectral readout via Root-MUSIC. This unifies learned infer-
ence and gridless spectral estimation within a single model-based framework for
nonuniform-baseline TomoSAR inversion.

with conventional Fourier-type spectral estimators and has been the dominant super-

the dependence on the predefined elevation grid. When the true scatterer elevations do
not coincide with the grid points, the resulting basis mismatch causes energy leakage,
biased localization, and spurious peaks (Chi et al., 2011). Refining the grid can mitigate
this effect only partially, while at the same time increasing dictionary coherence and
computational cost. This intrinsic limitation motivates gridless TomoSAR formulations
in the continuous domain.

5.2. Estimation of Scatterer Number in Practical Applications
In the synthetic experiments, the number of scatterers is known from the simulator
and can therefore be passed directly to the final Root-MUSIC readout.
However, in
real-world scenes, the situation is fundamentally different, because the number of scat-
terers varies from pixel to pixel and is not available a priori. In the present study, we
restrict the model order to the practically relevant set K ∈{1, 2} and estimate it in a
conservative, physically constrained manner rather than assuming a globally fixed K.
This design is motivated by the fact that, in the urban-building scenes considered here,
coherent responses are predominantly consistent with single-scatterer or double-scatterer
interpretations, whereas cases requiring more than two dominant point-like components
are comparatively rare. Accordingly, the focus of this work is robust discrimination and
estimation for K ∈{1, 2} rather than unrestricted high-order model selection.
The first step is to reject pixels that are unlikely to admit a stable point-scatterer
interpretation. In the real-data pipeline, a local mean coherence map is computed from
a 3 × 3 spatial window, and only pixels with coherence above a prescribed threshold are
27


## Page 28


retained for inversion. Pixels below this threshold are labeled as K = 0 and excluded from
subsequent processing. This screening stage prevents the method from forcing a single- or
double-scatterer explanation onto decorrelated pixels whose responses are more consistent
with noise or distributed clutter than with a small number of coherent scatterers. For each
retained pixel, DUSG-Tomo-Net then determines whether the reconstructed Toeplitz-
PSD matrix supports one or two dominant scatterers. Let λ1 ≥λ2 ≥· · · ≥λNv denote
the eigenvalues of the final matrix eT(M). A tentative order is first obtained from the ratio
λ2/λ1: if this ratio exceeds a prescribed threshold, the pixel is provisionally classified as
K = 2; otherwise it is treated as K = 1. In the reported real-data experiments, this
threshold is chosen more conservatively than in the synthetic studies, which intentionally
biases the decision toward K = 1 unless the second mode is sufficiently pronounced.
The purpose is therefore not unrestricted model-order discovery, but stable separation
between clear single-scatterer responses and plausible double-scatterer responses. This
eigenspectrum test is followed by an explicit physical validation step. When the tentative
decision is K = 2, Root-MUSIC is executed with order two, and the result is accepted
only if both recovered elevations lie inside the admissible height range and are separated
by at least a prescribed minimum distance (set to 0.1 times the Rayleigh resolution in
our experiment). If either condition fails, the pixel is downgraded to K = 1 and only a
single dominant elevation is retained. This extra check is important in practice because
small secondary eigenvalues may also arise from residual model mismatch, imperfect
focusing, or root splitting near the noise floor. Imposing range and minimum-separation
constraints suppresses such physically implausible double-scatterer detections.
5.3. Choice of the Training Baseline Range
Another practical deployment question concerns how the training baseline range should
be selected. The key issue is whether the baseline span expected at test time is covered
by the virtual lag range used during training. DUSG-Tomo-Net is trained in the virtual
lag domain rather than on one specific physical baseline realization. The CNN regular-
izer learns a prior on lag-domain covariance sequences, whereas the actual acquisition
geometry is encoded analytically through Ψ and can be recomputed at test time with-
out modifying the network weights. Therefore, when a new physical baseline realization
still induces lag samples within the covered training range, no retraining is required and
geometry adaptation can be handled by updating Ψ alone.
The basic coverage condition is
(Nv −1)∆b ≥Bmax,
(45)
where Bmax denotes the largest baseline span expected at deployment. This condition
determines whether the virtual lag range is wide enough to cover the target baseline
range. If it is satisfied, then different nonuniform baseline realizations with spans up to
Bmax can in principle be embedded into the same virtual lag domain. If it is violated,
some required lags fall outside the supported range, the covariance-domain representa-
tion becomes truncated, and reliable transfer should no longer be expected.
In that
case, the model must be retrained with a larger virtual lag range. This baseline-range
perspective also explains why DUSG-Tomo-Net can be reused across different baseline
configurations without retraining, as long as those configurations remain within the cov-
ered range and correspond to the same sensor mode. The physical baselines may change
28


## Page 29


from one stack to another, but the learned regularization remains applicable because
it acts on a fixed lag-domain representation, while the geometry-dependent mapping is
updated analytically through Ψ. Accordingly, the main no-retraining claim of this work
should be interpreted as baseline range reuse within the same sensor mode, rather than
generalization to arbitrary acquisition geometries.
At the same time, the training baseline range should not simply be made as wide as
possible. Increasing Nv enlarges the admissible baseline span, but it also raises the cost of
the Toeplitz-PSD projection, whose dominant eigendecomposition scales as O(N3
v ). For
example, increasing Nv from 32 to 64 raises this cost by roughly a factor of eight. More-
over, an excessively wide training range mixes inverse problems with very different condi-
tioning, which may affect the training distribution and reduce accuracy near the nominal
operating regime. In practice, a better strategy is to choose a mission-oriented baseline
range, i.e., the virtual lag range should cover the largest expected baseline span with a
moderate safety margin, but should not be expanded far beyond the realistic deployment
requirement. This baseline range-based deployment also distinguishes DUSG-Tomo-Net
from representative grid-based deep learning TomoSAR methods such as γ-Net (Qian
et al., 2022b) and ATASI-Net (Wang et al., 2023a), whose models are typically tied more
directly to the specific baseline configuration used for training.
In summary, whether DUSG-Tomo-Net needs retraining is determined primarily by
whether the baseline span of the new stack remains inside the covered training range.
For a fixed sensor mode, if the deployment baselines are still covered by the chosen virtual
lag range, then adapting to the new stack only requires recomputing Ψ and its associated
matrices. If the new stack exceeds that range, retraining becomes necessary.
6. Conclusion
This paper presented DUSG-Tomo-Net, a deep unfolded gridless framework for single-
look TomoSAR inversion under nonuniform baselines. The proposed method performs
structured covariance estimation in a Toeplitz-compatible lag domain derived from single-
look pairwise products, injects acquisition geometry through the signal-independent op-
erator Ψ, and couples layerwise learned lag-domain regularization with projection-based
positive-semidefinite Toeplitz enforcement. In this way, DUSG-Tomo-Net avoids a pre-
defined elevation dictionary and enables continuous-domain elevation readout through
Root-MUSIC, while maintaining a fixed-depth inference pipeline.
Experimental results show that DUSG-Tomo-Net provides reliable low-SNR spectral
reconstruction, strong super-resolution capability, and clear suppression of discretization-
induced off-grid bias. The method also remains robust as baseline nonuniformity in-
creases and, within the covered training baseline range and under the same sensor mode,
remains transferable to previously unseen numbers and distributions of baselines by up-
dating Ψ rather than retraining the network weights. In real-scene urban experiments,
DUSG-Tomo-Net produces more spatially continuous facade reconstruction and clearer
double-scatterer separation than TADCG. In addition, the fixed-depth forward inference
achieves 3.65 ms/pixel in the reported implementation, corresponding to a 49× speedup
over ANM-ADMM.
These findings indicate that deep unfolding in a Toeplitz-compatible covariance lag
domain is a practical and scalable direction for gridless TomoSAR under non-ideal ac-
quisition geometries. Future work will extend the framework to more flexible acquisition
29

settings, broader practical model-order estimation beyond K ∈{1, 2}, and wider valida-
tion across additional sensors and larger real scenes.
