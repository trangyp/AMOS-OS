Abstract
We study the notion of debiasability for cost functions arising in optimal transport. We call a
symmetric cost function c : X ×X →R∪{+∞} debiasable if it satisfies c(x, y) ⩾1
2 c(x, x)+ 1
2 c(y, y)
for all x, y ∈X . Building on an equivalent characterization by an inf-representation c(x, y) =
infz∈Z ψ(x,z) + ψ(y,z) for some set Z and some function ψ : X × Z →R ∪{+∞}, interpreted as a
generalization of the midpoint identity for squared geodesic distances, we investigate the debiasability
of costs defined on spaces of probability measures. Our primary focus is the entropic regularization of
optimal transport across different regimes of the regularization parameter ϵ ∈[0,+∞], encompassing
classical optimal transport (ϵ = 0), entropic optimal transport (ϵ > 0), and the Maximum Mean
Discrepancy (ϵ = +∞). For ϵ ∈(0,+∞], we investigate sufficient conditions, such as negative
definiteness of the ground cost or continuity and positive definiteness of the induced kernel, handled
then via a convex-nonconcave minimax argument. All our results extend naturally to unbalanced
optimal transport settings and we generalize in this way the findings of [FSV+19] and [SFV+19]. As
a byproduct, we derive novel decomposition formulas for entropic optimal transport, which may be
of independent interest.

Introduction
Context.
For a long time, the main focus in optimal transport were the Wasserstein distances Wp. Being
distances, they are already debiased, in the sense nonnegative and with null self-transportation cost.

e.g. by defining the Sinkhorn divergence.

Structure of the article.
Section 2 develops the general theory of debiasable costs: we prove the
inf-representation characterization (Proposition 2.1), establish preservation under standard operations
(Lemma 2.4), and discuss strict debiasability (Corollary 2.2). Section 3 treats classical OT (ϵ = 0) and
introduces the lifting from ground costs to transport costs via a probabilistic reformulation. Section 4
addresses entropic OT (ϵ ∈(0,+∞)), progressing from costs admitting a log-sum-exp decomposition
(Section 4.1), to negative definite costs (Section 4.2), to continuous reproducing kernels (Section 4.3).
Section 5 handles the MMD limit ϵ = +∞, which requires a separate analysis. Useful auxiliary results
are collected in the Appendix.

Characterization of debiasable costs
We start with general results on (strictly) debiasable costs, outside of any optimal transport context.
2.1
Inf-representation of debiasable costs
Our main idea is to use the following result which characterizes any debiasable cost through an inf-
representation.
Proposition 2.1 (Characterization of debiasable costs). Let X be a set and take c : X × X →R ∪{+∞}.
The following properties are equivalent
i) c is symmetric and debiasable;
ii) there exists a set Z and a function ψ : X × Z →R ∪{+∞} such that
c(x, y) = inf
z∈Z ψ(x,z) + ψ(y,z).
(2.1)
A couple (Z,ψ) such that (2.1) holds is called an inf-representation of c.
If X is equipped with a (Hausdorff) topology, then Z can be chosen (Hausdorff) topological as well. If c
is separately lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) and X ∋x 7→c(x, x) is continuous with X a Hausdorff space,
then ψ(x,·) + ψ(y,·) can be chosen l.s.c. for all x, y ∈X . If c is furthermore jointly continuous, then we
equip X × Z also with the product topology and ψ can be chosen jointly l.s.c.
We postpone the proof to Appendix A. Eq.(2.1) first appeared in [AFG24, Proposition 3.3], we
here expand the result to later tackle measurability issues through lower semicontinuity. The proof
rests upon a constructive ψ for which Z = X × X and ψ is defined as: ψ(x,(x, y)) = c(x, x)/2 and
ψ(x,(y, x)) = c(x, y) −c(y, y)/2 for all x, y ∈X . All the other values of ψ(x,(u, v)), for x,u, v ∈X
such that x ̸∈{u, v}, are set to +∞. Hence ψ(x,z) is finite-valued only if z, seen as an assignment, has
x as an endpoint.
In (max,+)-terminology, a cost c is debiasable if and only if −c is tropically positive semidefinite, and
the inf-representation is the (max,+)-analogue of a factorization of c, seen as matrix, through rank-1
operators. Since we are more interested in inf than sup, we do not delve further into (max,+)-analysis
concepts and refer to [AFG24] for the latter.

One important application of the disintegration of measures is the so-called gluing of transport
plans. Namely, consider a third Polish space Z, a measure η ∈P (Z) and two couplings π1 ∈Π(µ,η),
π2 ∈Π(ν,η). Disintegrating these in the form π1 = (π1)z ⊗η and π2 = (π2)z ⊗η, we can construct a
coupling γ as γ = ((π1)z ⊗(π2)z) ⊗η, a probability measure on X × Y × Z. One can check in particular
that γ ∈Π(µ,ν,η), i.e. the marginals of γ are µ,ν and η respectively, and, by definition, its disintegration
with respect to η is precisely (π1)z ⊗(π2)z, the product measure between the respective disintegrations of
π1 and π2. This construction serves specifically to produce probability measures on larger product spaces
by gluing together measures defined on lower-dimensional spaces that share common marginals. Note in
particular that (p1,p3)#γ = π1 and (p2,p3)#γ = π2. Such a measure γ so constructed is not the only one
with this property, as one may define analogously γz ⊗η for any family of measures (γz)z∈Z ⊂Π(µ,ν).
See [AGS08, San15] for more details on the gluing of transport plans (therein referred as gluing lemma)
and its application in optimal transport.
