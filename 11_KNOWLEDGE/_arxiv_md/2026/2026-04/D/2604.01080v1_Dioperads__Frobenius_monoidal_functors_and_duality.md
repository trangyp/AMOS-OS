Dioperads, Frobenius monoidal functors and duality
Valerio Melani∗, Hugo Pourcelot†
April 2, 2026
Abstract
Motivated by duality phenomena for derived global sections on derived local systems on
compact oriented manifolds, we introduce the notion of a d-duality context between symmetric
monoidal enriched categories. In this setting, the right adjoint of a symmetric monoidal functor
carries compatible lax and colax structures twisted by an invertible object d.
For any enriched dioperad P, we define a d-twist P{d} and prove that, in a d-duality context,
the right adjoint sends P-algebras to P{−d}-algebras. To achieve this, the key conceptual result
is that Frobenius monoidal functors between symmetric monoidal categories are precisely those
functors inducing morphisms between the underlying dioperads. We also develop a dioperadic Day
convolution, yielding an alternative proof of the main theorem and suggesting an ∞-categorical
extension of the theory.

Introduction
Operads are mathematical objects designed to model algebraic structures whose operations have a finite
number of inputs and a single output. In addition to their theoretical importance, operads have also
proven to be a useful tool in more geometric contexts. Indeed, besides their first historical appearance
in algebraic topology in the recognition principle for iterated loop spaces via En-algebras, they play a
crucial role in string topology via operadic actions on free loop spaces, in the theory of deformation
quantization or in the study of configuration spaces of manifolds. When working in the context of
higher or homotopical geometry, it is often useful to employ operads to describe geometric structures
in terms of algebraic operations (see for example [AF15], [To¨e13], [MR18], [Mel16], [CPT+17]).
Dioperads are structures similar to operads:
they are mathematical tools designed to model
(bi)algebraic structures that involve operations with multiple inputs as well as multiple outputs. They
were originately defined by Szabo in [Sza75] under the name polycategories, and later studied in [CS97b]
for their connection to linear logic. Although they are of importance in theoretical computer science,
in this work we will adopt a more algebraic perspective, in which the typical examples of dioperads are
those governing Lie bialgebras and Frobenius algebras. As it is the case with operads, given a dioperad
P and a symmetric monoidal category C, one can consider the category of P-algebras in C, which can
be defined as the category of dioperadic morphisms bewteen P and UC, the underlying dioperad of C.
Dioperads form a 2-category in a natural way.
The present work is motivated by the idea that dioperads should provide an appropriate language
for certain duality phenomena in geometry, where algebraic and coalgebraic structures interact in a
controlled way (see for example [Joh14], [Joh16]). This general principle should not be too surprising:
indeed, in [BD13] Boyarchenko and Drinfeld explained that Verdier duality in algebraic geometry nat-
urally yields examples of ∗-autonomous categories (which they call Grothendieck–Verdier categories),
a categorical structure closely related to dioperads1. This paper is a step forward to study the possible
uses of dioperads in geometry.
Our starting point was the following elementary observation. Suppose M is a d-dimensional com-
pact oriented topological manifold, and let Dloc(M, k) be the subcategory of the derived category
of sheaves of k-modules on M for a commutative ring k, consisting of complexes whose cohomology
sheaves are local systems on M. Inside Dloc(M, k), let Dp
loc(M, k) be the full subcategory of objects
which are locally quasi-isomorphic to complexes of projective modules of finite type. Then the de-
rived global section functor Γ on Dp
loc(M, k) admits a lax symmetric monoidal structure (being a right
adjoint), while by Poincar´e duality its cohomological shift Γ[d] is identified with a left adjoint and
therefore carries a colax symmetric monoidal structure. As a result, Γ preserves algebraic structures,
while it preserves coalgebraic structures up to a cohomological shift. It is natural to investigate what
kind of bialgebraic structures are preserved by Γ.
An ∞-categorical way of modeling this type of situation is suggested by the discussion in [PTVV13,
Section 2.1], where the authors define notions of compactness and orientations for general derived
stacks. Although the ∞-categorical question is definitely interesting (and we will briefly discuss it
at the end of this introduction), this paper is devoted to answering the 1-categorical version, which
turned out to be interesting in its own right.
To start with, suppose that we are given two rigid symmetric monoidal categories C and D, both
enriched over a closed bicomplete symmetric monoidal category V. Let d ∈V be an invertible object.
We say that a d-duality context is a pair of adjoint functors F : D ⇌C: G, where the left adjoint F is
symmetric monoidal, together with a d-orientation on the functor G (see Definition 4.5 for details). In
the motivating example above, V is the category of complexes of k-modules, and by abuse of notation
d denotes the complex given by k[d].
Moreover, given a V-enriched dioperad P we define its d-twisted version P{d}, which is another
dioperad. Intuitively, P{d} is obtained from P by twisting every co-operadic operation (i.e., every
operation with several outputs) by tensoring with an appropriate power of d. We refer to Definition
3.6 for details. Our first main result is then the following.

Theorem A (Cor. 4.7). Let P be a dioperad. Given a d-duality context F : D ⇌C: G, the right
adjoint G sends P-algebras in C to P{−d}-algebras in D.
Our strategy for proving this statement is as follows. We start by identifying the functors that
preserve dioperadic structures. It turns out that the correct notion is that of Frobenius monoidal
functors, which was introduced and studied in [Szl01], [Szl05], [DP08]. We recall the precise definition
in Section 3.1, but roughly speaking a Frobenius monoidal functor between monoidal categories is
a functor that is both lax and colax in a suitably compatible way. In the same way lax symmetric
monoidal functors are exactly those preserving algebras over operads, we prove that Frobenius monoidal
functors are exactly those preserving algebras over dioperads. More precisely, our second result takes
the following form.
Theorem B (Thm. 3.4). Let C, D be symmetric monoidal V-enriched categories, and let UC, UD be
their underlying dioperads. Then there is an equivalence of categories
DiopV(UC, UD) ≃Frob⊗
V(C, D)
where the right hand side is the category of Frobenius monoidal functors from C to D.
We use Theorem B to prove Theorem A. In fact, in Section 4 we explicitly construct a d-twisted
Frobenius monoidal structure on the functor G coming from a d-duality context. The proof is mostly
carried out via diagrammatic/graphical calculus.
Finally, in Section 5 we propose a different approach to Theorem A. The idea is to use a dioperadic
Day convolution. To state our result, recall the notion of a ∗-autonomous category mentioned above,
which consists of a closed symmetric monoidal category together with a dualizing object (in the sense of
Definition 5.2). Given such a ∗-autonomous category C, there is a standard way (described in Section
5.1.2) to extract a dioperad from it, which we denote by UC. The following result was conjectured in
[Egg08].
Theorem C (Thm. 5.8). Let C be a ∗-autonomous category and D be a dioperad. Then there exists a
dioperad Fun(C, D) with the following universal property: for every dioperad O, there is an equivalence
of categories
Diop(O, Fun(C, D)) ≃Diop(O × UC, D).
We believe Theorem C may be interesting in its own right, but in Section 5.3 we sketch how
it can also give a different proof of Theorem A. The idea here is to use a straightforward enriched
generalization of Theorem C. In particular, one prove that if both C and D are enriched over V, then
there is an equivalence of categories
DiopV(O, Fun(C, D)) ≃DiopV(O ⊗UC, D).
where the tensor product on the right-hand side is the Hadamard tensor product of dioperads. In par-
ticular, if O is the dioperad Frob (which encodes Frobenius algebras, and is the unit for the Hadamard
tensor product), we get an equivalence
AlgFrob(Fun(C, D)) ≃DiopV(UC, D).
If moreover the dioperad D is of the form UE{d} for some rigid symmetric monoidal V-enriched category
E and some invertible object d ∈V, we obtain
AlgFrob (Fun(C, UE{d})) ≃DiopV(UC, UE{d}).
In particular, d-twisted Frobenius monoidal functors C →E correspond to Frobenius algebras in
Fun(C, UE{d}). Section 5.3 sketches how to produce such a Frobenius algebra starting from a d-duality
context in the sense of Definition 4.4, thus giving a different approach to Theorem A.

of Frobenius monoidal functors, as in [Yad24] and [FLP25]. In both papers, the authors construct

As we already mentioned in this Introduction, we think it would be interesting to have an ∞-

an underlying ∞-dioperad UC. Then a Frobenius monoidal ∞-functor between symmetric monoidal

the spirit of the definition of lax symmetric monoidal ∞-functors appearing in [Lur17, Section 2.1.3].
We plan to exploit this point of view in a forthcoming work.
As a possible application of the ∞-categorical version of our results, we think our approach could
lead to a different proof of the AKSZ theorem for shifted Poisson structures, which was recently proved
in [Tom26]. To this purpose, we hope to prove an ∞-categorical version of Theorem C, which may
also be of independent interest (see [Gla16], [Lur17, Section 2.2.6] for Day convolution results for ∞-
categories and ∞-operads). We then plan to follow the strategy outlined in Section 5.3 to show that,
in an ∞-categorical duality context (as in [PTVV13, Section 2.1]), the pushforward functor inherits a
natural d-twisted Frobenius monoidal structure.
Finally, we think that a dioperadic approach to the Poisson AKSZ construction would also work in
the relative setting, similarly to the relative versions of the symplectic AKSZ construction (see [Cal15]).
In the Poisson case, we expect that the AKSZ theorem will produce shifted coisotropic structures (in
the sense of [MS18a], [MS18b]). We refer to [Joh16] for a partial result in this direction.

Notations and conventions
On symmetric monoidal categories.
To keep the computations in symmetric monoidal categories
readable, we will systematically omit the unitors, associators and braidings in the notations, by virtue
of MacLane’s coherence theorem. Moreover, to shorten notations, we may sometimes remove symbols
⊗, leaving the tensor products implicit; for example, G(XY )GZ will stand for G(X ⊗Y ) ⊗G(Z).
On enriched categories.
Throughout this paper, we work with categories, dioperads and properads
enriched over a symmetric monoidal category (V, ⊗, 1), which we further assume to be bicomplete with
tensor product commuting with colimits in each variables. In particular, morphisms, functors, natural
transformations, symmetric monoidal categories, dualizable objects in them, etc. are to be interpreted
in the sense of enriched category theory.
Given a V-enriched category C, we will abuse notation by writing f : A →B for a morphism
1 →C(A, B) and say that f is a morphism from A to B in C. In the same spirit, we will typically treat
morphisms as in ordinary category theory, writing equations and composites of individual morphisms.
In every such situation, it is straightforward to reformulate the relevant equalities as commutative
diagrams in V.
The (large) 2-category of small symmetric monoidal V-categories, symmetric monoidal functors
and monoidal natural transformations between them will be denoted Cat⊗
V.

Recollections on properads and dioperads
In this section, we recall the basic definitions and properties of V-dioperads and V-properads, their
associated 2-categories DiopV and PrpdV and their relation with symmetric monoidal V-categories
through envelope functors. The results of this section are gathered from standard references (mainly
[YJ15, JY21]), if only slightly extended to the enriched setting, with the exception of Proposition 2.17,
whose statement we were not able to locate in the literature and of which we provide a proof (note
however that an ∞-categorical version of this proposition is established in [BS22]).
2.1
Graphs
We recall some definitions and conventions on graphs, following [YJ15].
Definition 2.1. A digraph is a directed graph with half-edges which has no directed cycles. Given
a digraph G, we let V (G), E(G), in(G), out(G) denote respectively the sets of vertices, edges, input
leaves and output leaves. Given a vertex v ∈V (G), we let inv and outv denote its sets of inputs and
outputs, which are either edges or leaves.
Fix a set S. We now introduce variants of digraphs whose edges are colored with elements in S.
Definition 2.2. An S-profile x = (x1, . . . , xn) is an (ordered) finite sequence of elements in S, possibly
empty. An S-biprofile is a pair of S-profiles, typically written as (x, y) or (x1, . . . , xn; y1, . . . , ym).
Whenever the context is clear, we will forget the set S in the notation.
Notation 2.3. Slightly abusing notation, we will sometimes write an S-profile x as (xi)i∈I, where
I = (Iu, <I) is a linearly ordered finite set; we say that Iu ∈Fin is the underlying set of I. If A ⊆Iu
is a subset, we can form the subprofile x|A := (xi)i∈A of x, where we implicitly endow A with the
restriction of the linear order <I.
Definition 2.4. An S-colored graph is a digraph G together with a coloring of edges, that is a function
E(G) →S, and orderings of the sets in(G), out(G), inv and outv for every vertex v. An S-colored
graph G therefore has an associated biprofile (in(G), out(G)), as well as biprofiles (inv, outv) for every
vertex v.
Example 2.5. Given a biprofile (x, y) = (x1, . . . , xn; y1, . . . , ym), the corolla cx,y is the colored graph
with one vertex, n input leaves colored by x and m output leaves colored by y, with the obvious
orderings.

the composition along a general S-colored connected graph G is determined by compositions along
graftings of two corollas, glued along k > 0 common colors.

morphism between such, to only specify these particular compositions. We refer to the book [HRY15,
Definition 3.5, Remark 3.9] for details on these alternative definitions of properads and dioperads.

This definition generalize in a straightforward

Envelopes
We will recall explicit descriptions of the symmetric monoidal and properadic envelope functors, re-

5
Day convolution for dioperads
The goal of this section is to define and study a notion of Day convolution for dioperads, by which we
mean the construction of exponential objects in the category of dioperads.
In the literature, the classical version of Day convolution is for monoidal categories and originates
in [Day70]. There is a variant for operads established in [Pis14]; from the point of view of the latter
result, Day’s original construction can be seen as the representable case. In the setting of dioperads,
a parallel story goes, the analog of Day convolution result being the main theorem of [Egg08], where
monoidal categories are replaced by linear distributive categories. Our Theorem 5.8 completes this
picture by establishing a non-representable counterpart of Egger’s result, which the author suggested
in [Egg08, Remark 4.4]4.
Remark 5.1 (Restriction to the unenriched setting). In this section, as opposed to the rest of the paper,
we work with unenriched dioperads. The results and proofs remain valid in the enriched setting mutatis
mutandis; the restriction to the unenriched situation hopefully makes the constructions and arguments
more readable. Note however that our main application in Section 5.3 consists in recovering Theorem
4.6 by means of the Day convolution for dioperads and therefore depends on an enriched version of
Theorem 5.8; in this sense, this second argument for Theorem 4.6 does not constitute a complete proof.
4Note however that Egger suggests that a slight generalization of Theorem 5.8 should hold true, in which the source
category C is allowed to be any bilinear linearly distributive category, of which ∗-autonomous are special cases.
