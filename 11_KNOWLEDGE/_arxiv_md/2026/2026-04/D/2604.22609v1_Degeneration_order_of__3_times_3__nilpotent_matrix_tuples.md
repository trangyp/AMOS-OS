DEGENERATION ORDER OF 3 × 3 NILPOTENT MATRIX
TUPLES
MÁTYÁS DOMOKOS AND BOTOND MIKLÓSI
Abstract. The degeneration order of simultaneous similarity classes of 3 × 3
nilpotent matrix tuples is determined, and is shown to be given by rank conditions.
1. Introduction
1.1. Basic definitions. For positive integers n ≥2, m, denote by (Kn×n)m the
space of m-tuples of n×n matrices over an algebraically closed field K. The general
linear group GLn(K) acts on (Kn×n)m by simultaneous conjugation: for g ∈GLn(K)
and A = (A1, . . . , Am) we have g · A = (gA1g−1, . . . , gAmg−1). We denote by O(A)
the GLn(K)-orbit of A.
We say that the matrix tuples A and B are similar if
O(A) = O(B). Write O(A) for the closure of O(A) in (Kn×n)m with respect to the
Zariski topology.
We shall consider two preorders on (Kn×n)m. We write A ≤deg B and say that A
degenerates to B if B ∈O(A) (or equivalently, O(B) ⊆O(A)). Clearly, ≤deg induces
a partial ordering on the set of GLn(K)-orbits in (Kn×n)m, called the degeneration
order.
Let us turn to the second preorder. Let R := K⟨x1, . . . , xm⟩be the free associative
K-algebra on m generators x1, . . . , xm (with 1 ∈R). Given an f ∈R and A ∈
(Kn×n)m it makes sense to substitute in f the non-commuting variables xi by Ai,
and we write f(A) for the resulting n × n matrix. Moreover, given an arbitrary
matrix φ = (φij)j=1,...,l
i=1,...,k ∈Rk×l, set φ(A) := (φij(A))j=1,...,l
i=1,...,k, which is a kn × ln
matrix over K, partitioned into k × l blocks of size n × n (and φij(A) is the n × n
block in the (i, j) position). Denote by rk(φ(A)) the rank of the kn × ln matrix
φ(A) over K. Now for A, B ∈(Kn×n)m we set A ≤hom B if rkφ(A) ≥rkφ(B) for
all possible matrices φ over R. By theorems of Auslander [2] and Riedtmann [29]
(see Section 2), A ≤hom B and B ≤hom A both hold if and only if O(A) = O(B).
So ≤hom also induces a partial ordering (denoted by ≤hom as well) on the set of
GLn(K)-orbits in (Kn×n)m. For reasons explained in Section 2, this is called the
hom-order. We shall use the notation A <hom B if A ≤hom B and O(A) ̸= O(B).
We have the following well known lemma:
Lemma 1.1. A ≤deg B implies A ≤hom B for A, B ∈(Kn×n)m.

GLn(K)-orbits in (Kn×n)m. On the other hand, for a fixed non-negative integer r,
the set {B ∈(Kn×n)m | rkφ(B) ≤r} is the common zero locus of the determinants

1.2.
Motivation. The study of orbit closures is motivated by several areas of
mathematics. For example, basic problems of Geometric Complexity Theory ask to
decide if an orbit under a linear action of a reductive group (say the general linear
group) is contained in the Zariski closure of another orbit, see for example [22]. So
it is a natural and challenging problem to characterize the preorder ≤deg. A possible
candidate for such a characterization is offered by ≤hom (see [9],[14]). However, the
converse of Lemma 1.1 does not hold in general, as an example attributed to Carlson
in [29] shows (see also [10], and Remark 1.3 below). Note also that the problem of
classifying the similarity classes in (Kn×n)2 for all n is wild in the sense of [11], but
some approaches to it are given in [7], [23], [3].
On the other hand, there are some notable partial converses for Lemma 1.1 in
the theory of finite dimensional modules over a finite dimensional associative alge-
bra. Note that for a fixed finite dimensional associative algebra Λ generated by m
elements, the space (Kn×n)m contains a GLn(K)-invariant affine subvariety denoted
by RepΛ(n), such that the GLn(K)-orbits in RepΛ(n) are in bijection with the iso-
morphism classes of n-dimensional Λ-modules. Under certain restrictions for Λ, it
is true that the restriction of ≤hom to RepΛ(n) implies the restriction of ≤deg to
RepΛ(n). This holds if Λ is a representation-finite algebra by [31], or when Λ is the
path algebra of an extended Dynkin quiver by [4].
An exact module theoretic characterization of when A ≤deg B for A, B ∈RepΛ(n)
was found in [29], [32]. The problem in a more geneal setup is considered in [12].
See [30] and [28] for a survey on the topic.
1.3. Content of the present paper. The nullcone Nn,m of the GLn(K)-variety
(Kn×n)m consists of the matrix tuples A such that the Zariski closure of O(A)
contains the zero tuple 0 (i.e. A ≤deg 0). It is also the common zero locus of all
homogeneous GLn(K)-invariant polynomial functions on (Kn×n)m, so it is an affine
GLn(K)-variety. Note that the general notion of the nullcone for a representation of
a reductive algebraic group plays a distinguished role in geometric invariant theory
[26].
A central result in our paper is the following partial converse to Lemma 1.1:
Theorem 1.2. For A, B ∈N3,m, we have that
A ≤deg B ⇐⇒A ≤hom B.
Remark 1.3. The analogues of Theorem 1.2 for Nn,m with n ≥4 does not hold.
Carlson’s example [29] mentioned above provides explicit pairs A, B ∈N4,2 with
A ≤hom B, such that B /∈O(A) (see also [10]). So Theorem 1.2 shows that this
example is in some sense minimal possible.

module theoretic description of the preorder ≤hom due to Riedtmann [29]; we present
a new proof fitting to our notation. Although A ≤hom B for A, B ∈(Kn×n)m does
not necessarily imply B ∈O(A) in general, we show the weaker statement that
A <hom B implies dim O(B) < dim O(A); this is the content of Lemma 2.2, proved
by combining Lemma 2.1 with a result of Bongartz [6]. Section 3 deals with the
case of 2 × 2 matrices, and we prove in Corollary 3.2 that ≤deg and ≤hom coincide

Although the converse of Lemma 1.1 does not hold in general (i.e. A ≤hom B does
not imply A ≤deg B), the following weaker statement holds, which is a consequence
of Lemma 2.1 and a result of Bongartz [6]:
Lemma 2.2. Let A, B ∈(Kn×n)m. If A <hom B, then dim O(A) > dim O(B).
Proof. The dimension dim O(A) of the locally closed algebraic variety O(A) equals
dim GLn(K) −dim StabGLn(K)(A).
The stabilizer StabGLn(K)(A) is the group of
units in the endomorphism algebra EndR(VA) of the R-module VA. Thus we have
dim O(A) = n2 −dimK HomR(VA, VA) and dim O(B) = n2 −dimK HomR(VB, VB).
Consequently, we have
(8)
dim O(A) −dim O(B) = dimK HomR(VB, VB) −dimK HomR(VA, VA).
By Lemma 2.1, A ≤hom B implies
(9)
dimK HomR(VA, VA) ≤dimK HomR(VA, VB) ≤dimK HomR(VB, VB).
Combining (8) and (9) we conclude that A ≤hom B implies dim O(A) ≥dim O(B).
Note finally that [6, Lemma 1.2] asserts that A ≤hom B and dimK HomR(VA, VA) =
dimK HomR(VB, VB) together imply VA ∼= VB as R-modules (equivalently, O(A) =
O(B)).
This finishes the proof of the implication A <hom B ⇒dim O(A) >
dim O(B).

Corollary 3.2. Let A, B ∈(Kn×n)m, and assume that the R-module VA or VB
has Jordan-Hölder composition length at most 2. Then A ≤deg B if and only if
A ≤hom B.
Proof. By Lemma 1.1 it is sufficient to show that that A ≤hom B implies A ≤deg B.
Suppose that A ≤hom B. Then dimK HomR(X, VA) ≤dimK HomR(X, VB) for all
finite dimensional R-modules X by Lemma 2.1. Therefore by Lemma 3.1 we get
that VB ∼= VA (and so O(A) = O(B), hence A ≤deg B), or VB ∼= S ⊕T, where S and
T are the composition factors of VA. Thus VB is a semi-simple R-module. By GIT
we know that the closure of O(A) contains a unique closed orbit (see for example
[26]), and by [1] it corresponds to the direct sum of the composition factors of VA.
Thus, O(B) is the unique closed orbit in the Zariski closure of O(A). In particular,
we have A ≤deg B.
□
