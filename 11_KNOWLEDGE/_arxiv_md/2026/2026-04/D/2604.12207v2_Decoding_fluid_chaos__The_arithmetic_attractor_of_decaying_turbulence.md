Abstract. This paper reviews a line of work on decaying turbulence that began with loop equations and
culminated in the Euler ensemble as a candidate statistical attractor. Most observable predictions discussed
here—including the decay law, velocity correlations, and anomalous exponents—were obtained in earlier papers.
The immediate motivation for the present review is recent 40963 direct numerical simulation, which found that
randomized initial data with two inequivalent infrared spectra, of Saffman (k2) and Loitsyansky (k4) type,
converge in the bulk toward the same Euler-ensemble behavior. This empirical universality calls for a concise
formulation of the underlying theory.
I therefore revisit the construction in a continuous algebraic form. Reformulating the Navier–Stokes equation in
the Lagrangian frame as a covariant-derivative flow, I show that advection cancels exactly and that the loop
dynamics reduce to a Yang–Mills-like operator evolution in Hilbert space. Feynman’s operational calculus maps
this noncommutative operator algebra to discontinuities on a one-dimensional momentum loop. The decaying
solutions are organized by rational turning angles β = 2πp/q, and in the continuum limit this structure condenses
into an arithmetically intermittent attractor governed by the Farey sequence of coprime pairs. The only genuinely
new ingredients of the present article are the continuous operator derivation and a heuristic dynamical-systems
interpretation in terms of mode locking onto rational data. Taken together with earlier analytical results and
recent DNS support, this framework suggests that the apparent chaos of decaying turbulence is organized by a
deterministic arithmetic skeleton.

Introduction
Decaying homogeneous isotropic turbulence has long been discussed in terms of phenomenological di-
mensional arguments, most notably in the historical contrast between Saffman (k2) and Loitsyansky (k4)
infrared behavior and in the broader Kolmogorov picture of continuous scaling. This Perspective reviews a
complementary line of work, initiated from loop equations in 1994 [1] and developed in a series of later
papers[2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7], in which the turbulent state is described in loop space and the late-time attractor
acquires a discrete arithmetic structure.
What is at stake in this line of work is broader than a particular closed-form attractor formula. Over
more than three decades, it has developed a loop-space language for nonlinear field evolution. That program
began in gauge theory, with the Makeenko–Migdal loop equation [8, 9] and subsequent continuum loop-space
formulations, and has now been extended to turbulence. In the present setting, the Navier–Stokes dynamics
are reformulated as diffusion in loop space and become exactly tractable in momentum-loop variables. In
that wider perspective, the arithmetic attractor is not merely a special formula for decaying turbulence, but
the turbulent realization of a broader loop-calculus framework for nonlinear dynamics [10].
The immediate reason to revisit this program now is empirical. Recent 40963 direct numerical simulations
by Sreenivasan and Rodhiya [11], starting from randomized initial data with two distinct infrared spectra, k2
and k4, were found to converge in the bulk toward the same Euler-ensemble predictions. This convergence

What is new here is mainly conceptual and structural. First, I present a continuous algebraic derivation
of the momentum-loop dynamics, bypassing the polygonal regularization used in earlier work [5, 12].
Reformulating the Navier–Stokes equation in the Lagrangian frame as a covariant-derivative flow, one
finds that advection cancels exactly, leaving a purely diffusive operator evolution. This evolution can
then be reduced to a one-dimensional momentum-loop dynamics on the ordering circle. For each finite
arithmetic cutoff N, meaning reduced turning angles with denominator q ≤N, the finite-N momentum-loop
representative may be realized as an SBV function [13, 14] on the ordering circle, with jumps at the N-th
roots of unity. The physical continuum description is then obtained only in the diffusive double-scaling
limit ν →0, N →∞, with ˜ν = νN2 fixed, in which the observables admit finite continuum limits. Second,
I propose a heuristic dynamical-systems interpretation of the rational stationary sector in terms of mode
locking onto reduced turning angles β/(2π) = p/q. In this form, intermittency appears not as a multifractal
continuum, but as an arithmetic intermittency encoded by the rational jump set itself.
This separation between old and new is important. The Euler ensemble should be viewed here as
an existing theoretical framework whose observable consequences were worked out previously and whose
empirical support has recently improved. The central message of this Perspective is that decaying turbulence
admits an exact decaying solution of the Navier–Stokes loop equations: not a phenomenological multifractal
fit, but an arithmetic trajectory whose rescaled profile is a fixed point with SBV momentum loops and
intermittency encoded by reduced rational turning angles. Its stability and role as the universal bulk
attractor are strongly supported by large-scale DNS, but not yet proved mathematically.

Operator Evolution in the Lagrangian Frame
Our viewpoint on turbulence is statistical from the outset. Rather than following a single deterministic
velocity field, we consider the evolution of an ensemble of Navier–Stokes solutions. At the initial time, this
ensemble may be sharply concentrated around a prescribed macroscopic flow, with thermal fluctuations
small in comparison with the imposed velocity scale. The onset of turbulence is then viewed as spontaneous
stochasticity: the dynamical spreading of this initially narrow distribution under the nonlinear Navier–Stokes
evolution, until it approaches a universal turbulent attractor, as envisioned long ago by Hopf.
The early development of turbulence may depend in a complicated and nonuniversal way on the details
of the initial state. The late-time attractor, however, should be determined by the nonlinear dynamics
themselves and is therefore expected to be universal. The problem addressed here is not the detailed
transient route to turbulence, but the exact description of that asymptotic statistical state.

Discrete target space and arithmetic intermittency
The discrete structure in the present formulation appears not in the spatial loop C, which remains continuous,
but in the conjugate momentum representation. Rational turning angles are therefore not artifacts of a
spatial lattice regularization; they arise from the noncommutative operator algebra itself. As we have
already mentioned, the commutators of original covariant derivatives are proportional to finite vorticity;
therefore, the angular jumps representing these commutators in momentum loop space must also stay
finite. The integer N acts only as an arithmetic cutoff, restricting reduced turning angles to denominators
q ≤N. As N →∞, the ordering circle is covered densely, but the target-space support does not approach a
smooth curve. Instead, it condenses onto the Farey hierarchy of reduced rational angles, leading to discrete
spectrum of jumps.

fixed point by the advection-annihilation condition. What remains dynamical is the arithmetic selection
within that planar fixed point: the autonomous flow may suppress incommensurate angular data and prefer
commensurate ones, with the strongest resonances at the reduced fractions p/q. Within that rational
sector, the equal-step walk is exactly the random walk on the regular star polygon {q/p}, namely the Euler
ensemble. In this sense, the Farey hierarchy is not responsible for the sphere-to-plane reduction; it quantizes
the planar fixed point.
A useful qualitative precedent for this interpretation is provided by the circle-map analysis of the wake
of an oscillating cylinder by Olinger and Sreenivasan [15]. In that work, a fluid-mechanical model was
shown to exhibit rational mode locking on the circle in the familiar Arnol’d-tongue pattern [16]. This is not
a theory of turbulence, and it does not derive the map from the exact Navier–Stokes loop equations. Its
relevance is narrower but directly pertinent to the present conjecture: it demonstrates that circle dynamics
arising in fluid mechanics can select reduced rational winding numbers as robust resonant states.
What is exact and what is heuristic
The loop-space diffusion equation and its momentum-loop representation are exact reformulations of the
statistical Navier–Stokes dynamics. In particular, (13) is not a closure or phenomenological model, but the
exact evolution equation for the one-dimensional momentum loop. The same is true of the decay rescaling
(28): it is an exact change of variables in momentum-loop space, so the autonomous flow (29) is again exact.
The finite arithmetic cutoff N enters only later as a regularization of the discontinuous momentum loop
on the ordering circle. It does not truncate the Navier–Stokes equation; it evaluates the loop on the N-th
roots of unity, where the finite-N representative is naturally an SBV function with a finite jump set. For
each fixed N, one then solves exactly the fixed-point equation and obtains the Euler ensemble, after which
observables are computed and only then the diffusive continuum limit is taken.
The first genuinely heuristic step is the conjecture that the exact autonomous flow dynamically selects
the rational Farey sector by a mode-locking mechanism analogous to Arnol’d tongues [16]. The planar form
of the fixed point is exact; the arithmetic selection of the rational sector is not yet a theorem.

Boundary corrections, lowest modes, and the statistical limit
The universality of the Euler ensemble should therefore be understood as a statement about the bulk
turbulent attractor in the infinite-volume, infinite-Reynolds-number limit. Finite DNS inevitably includes
corrections from the imposed initial spectrum, the box size, and the lowest lattice modes. This is why the
DNS effective index f(r/L(t)) agrees more cleanly with theory than the total energy decay: the effective
index probes the bulk of the spectrum, whereas the total energy is unusually sensitive to the infrared sector,
where BS and LKB initial conditions differ most strongly.
As the system size and Reynolds number increase, these corrections should become less important. In
the current DNS they already modify the fitted decay exponents by only a few percent. At higher Reynolds
number, one expects the measured global decay to move closer to the asymptotic law E(t) ∝t−5/4, but the
more important point is conceptual: the bulk attractor is already visible in observables that suppress the
boundary-dominated infrared tail.
The empirical success of the BS and LKB laws is therefore not accidental. It reflects dimensional counting
together with the valid observation that the global energy decay is influenced by macroscopic infrared
invariants, such as the Birkhoff–Saffman linear-impulse invariant or the Loitsyansky angular-momentum
invariant. Under such circumstances a dimensional power law is natural. What such a law does not
determine, however, is the bulk structure of turbulence, namely the attractor itself. In the total energy, the
bulk contribution is masked by boundary-sensitive infrared terms, because energy is not a statistically local
observable in the turbulent regime.

Conclusions: Arithmetic intermittency in turbulence
This Perspective has revisited a long line of work in which decaying turbulence is reformulated in loop
space and described by an exact decaying solution of the Navier–Stokes loop equations . In the continuous
operator derivation presented here, the Lagrangian-frame cancellation of advection reduces the dynamics to
a diffusive covariant-derivative flow, and Feynman’s operational calculus maps the resulting noncommutative
operator algebra to a one-dimensional momentum loop with ordering discontinuities . The corresponding
decaying solution is organized by reduced rational turning angles β = 2πp/q, and its continuum limit
condenses onto the Farey hierarchy rather than a smooth continuum .
The main conceptual point is that the singularity of the turbulent attractor is revealed as an arithmetic
intermittency carried by the rational jump structure of the momentum loop . In this representation, the
turbulent state remains continuous in its ordering parameter but singular in its target-space support: the
irregularity is encoded by reduced rational phases, changing multiplicities, and the corresponding walk
through regular star polygons . The arithmetic structure is therefore not an external decoration of the
solution; it is the form taken by the exact decaying solution itself . The multifractal phenomenology is not
adequate to describe this arithmetic intermittency, whose singular structure is organized by rational data
rather than by a random cascade of continuously varying exponents.
Most observable consequences of this framework were derived earlier, but their interpretation becomes
clearer in the present formulation . The Euler ensemble predicts the asymptotic decay law E(t) ∝t−5/4, a
Mellin representation for velocity correlations, and a discrete set of anomalous exponents governed in part
by the pole structure of the zeta function . Recent 40963 DNS supports the bulk predictions of this picture,
including the diffusive growth law L(t) ∝
√
t, the enstrophy decay, and the non-K41 form of the effective
second-order structure function . Particularly significant is the observed convergence, in the bulk, from
randomized k2 and k4 infrared initial conditions toward the same Euler-ensemble behavior.

The comparison with DNS also clarifies an important physical lesson. The strongest apparent mismatch
arises in the total energy, but this observable is unusually sensitive to the infrared boundary sector and to
the lowest Fourier modes of a finite box, whereas enstrophy and the effective index probe the universal
bulk much more directly . In that sense, the bulk attractor is better tested by observables with a cleaner
statistical limit than by the global energy alone . The resulting picture is not that the dimensional infrared
laws are meaningless, but that strong isotropic turbulence in the bulk is polluted by the boundary effects in
these phenomenological laws.
What remains open is equally clear. The exact decaying solution reviewed here is strongly supported
by large-scale DNS as the universal bulk attractor of decaying turbulence, but its stability and ergodic
selection have not yet been proved mathematically . The heuristic mode-locking scenario proposed here
is meant precisely in that spirit: not as a theorem, but as a plausible route by which the autonomous
momentum-loop flow could suppress the irrational sector and select the rational resonant one .
Seen in this wider perspective, these recent results are more than an exact decaying solution for
turbulence. They are also the mature turbulence realization of a loop-calculus program in which nonlinear
field evolution is reformulated in loop space, so that the Navier–Stokes dynamics become a diffusion in an
infinite-dimensional functional space and admit an exact momentum-loop representation. In that sense, the
arithmetic attractor should not be read merely as an analytic formula for the asymptotic state of decaying
turbulence. It is the expression, in turbulent variables, of a broader loop-space language for nonlinear
dynamics, developed over many years and aimed precisely at problems that had long resisted exact analysis
and were studied mainly through phenomenological closures.
This is also where the broader mathematical significance of the theory becomes visible. The natural
continuum language of the solution involves SBV [13, 14] momentum-loop representatives at finite arithmetic
cutoff, bounded-variation jump decompositions, nonconservative products, and singular limits that are
familiar in modern analysis but have not usually occupied the center of turbulence theory. At the same
time, the rational organization of the decaying solution points toward a direct interface with arithmetic and
dynamical systems. In 2005, Vladimir Arnol’d explicitly called for an “arithmetical project” linking fluid
dynamics with number-theoretic structure [19]. He spent his final years urging mathematicians to study
number theory through the phenomenological lens of fluid dynamics. A rigorous proof of our arithmetic
attractor would fulfill Arnol’d’s vision with an ironic twist, revealing the microscopic structure of turbulence
through the exact lens of number theory.
