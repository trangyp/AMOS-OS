Abstract. Let G be a linear semisimple Lie group without compact factors.
We show that uniform approximate lattices Λ arising as regular model sets in
G determine the ambient group G in a strong sense. Specifically, for every non-
compact Cartan subgroup C of G, there exists g ∈G such that the intersection
gCg−1∩Λ2 is non-empty and itself forms a uniform approximate lattice, extending
a classical result of Mostow for lattices. The proof relies on a Moore-type ergodicity
theorem for the hull of a strong approximate lattice, proved here as a key tool.
Moreover, we prove that such approximate lattices determine the R-rank of the
ambient group G, drawing on ideas from the work of Prasad and Raghunathan
on lattices.

1. Introduction
Approximate subgroups were introduced by Terence Tao [Tao08] to extend ideas
from additive combinatorics. A fundamental result of Breuillard, Green, and Tao
[BGT12] shows that finite approximate subgroups admit a strong algebraic structure.
In the infinite setting, however, the class of approximate subgroups is too large
to allow a comparable general structure theory.
This has led to the study of
more rigid subclasses, among which uniform approximate lattices, introduced by
Bj¨orklund and Hartnick [BH18], play a crucial role. Uniform approximate lattices

are approximate subgroups of locally compact groups that are both uniformly
discrete and relatively dense, and can be viewed as a non-commutative analogue of
mathematical quasicrystals. In Euclidean spaces, they coincide with the classical
Meyer sets, which arise from cut-and-project schemes. One of the central themes in
the subject is to determine for which classes of groups approximate lattices in fact
arise from such constructions. Some of the most significant breakthroughs in the
subject have emerged from the study of such questions. Simon Machado extended
Meyer’s original ideas to various classes of Lie groups, especially to nilpotent [Mac20],
solvable [Mac22], and S-adic Lie groups [Mac], while Hrushovski’s work [Hru22],
from a geometrical point of view, provides deep structural results in the semisimple
setting, providing an aperiodic analogue of Margulis’ arithmeticity theory, also
proved independently by Machado [Mac23] from an algebraic point of view.
In this article, our aim is to study the other way: how uniform approximate
lattices reflect structural properties of linear semisimple Lie groups (without compact
factors), motivated by the classical results of Mostow [Mos70] on uniform lattices
and of Prasad–Raghunathan [PR72] on lattices and Cartan subgroups in semisimple
Lie groups. In particular, our main goal is to understand how the presence of
a uniform approximate lattice determines the rank of such groups. To approach
this problem, we study the interaction between Cartan subgroups and uniform
approximate lattices that arise as regular model sets in linear semisimple Lie groups.
An approximate lattice that arises as a regular model set is automatically a strong
approximate lattice, a notion different from that of a uniform approximate lattice.
Strong approximate lattices are defined via the existence of invariant probability
measures on the hull under the natural group action. Using ergodic theory and
homogeneous dynamics, we study the interaction of regular elements and Cartan
subgroups with strong approximate lattices that arise as regular model sets; some
of these results may also be of independent interest.
1.1. Moore’s Ergodicity type theorem for hull of a strong approximate
lattice. Strong approximate lattices were introduced by Bj¨orklund and Hartnick
[BH18] as a dynamical strengthening of approximate lattices, defined via the
existence of a G-invariant probability measure on the hull of the set. A large and
important class of examples arises as model sets over cut-and-project schemes. More
precisely, let (G, H, Γ) be a cut-and-project scheme, where G and H are locally
compact groups and Γ is a lattice in G × H such that the projection of Γ to G is
injective and its projection to H is dense. Given a compact subset W ⊂H, called
the window, the associated model set is defined by
Λ = {πG(γ) : γ ∈Γ, πH(γ) ∈W},
where πG and πH denote the natural projections from G × H onto G and H,
respectively. Equivalently, one may write Λ = P0(Γ, W). When the window W
satisfies suitable regularity conditions, the resulting set is called a regular model set
(see §2.3 for details). Such sets are known to produce strong approximate lattices
and form a central class of examples in the theory.
Moore’s ergodicity theorem is a fundamental result in homogeneous dynamics.
It states that if Γ is an irreducible lattice in a semisimple Lie group G without
compact factors, then the action of any subgroup of G with non-compact closure on
the homogeneous space G/Γ is ergodic with respect to the G-invariant probability
measure. In the setting of strong approximate lattices, a natural analogue arises

1.2. Strong approximate lattice and regular elements. Let G be a connected

The R-rank of a semisimple Lie group G is defined as the dimension of a maximal
R-split torus in G. A theorem of Prasad and Raghunathan shows that if Γ is
a lattice in G, then Γ contains a free abelian subgroup of rank r = R-rank(G)
[PR72]. Thus the lattice necessarily contains a subgroup isomorphic to Zr, where r
is determined by the R-rank of the ambient group. It is natural to ask whether an
analogous structural property holds for approximate lattices. The following result
shows that this is indeed the case for uniform approximate lattices arising as regular
model sets.
Corollary 1.4. Let G be a linear semisimple Lie group without compact factors.
Let Λ be a uniform approximate lattice arising as a regular model set. Then ⟨Λ2⟩
contains a free abelian subgroup of rank r = R-rank(G).
For Λ and G as in Theorem 1.3, by using ergodicity, we show that Λ2 contains
R-regular elements in Proposition 5.2, in a similar spirit to Theorem 1.2.
1.4. Characterization of R-rank of linear semisimple group by Uniform
approximate Lattice. In the classical theory of lattices in semisimple Lie groups,
a central theme is that discrete subgroups reflect structural properties of the ambient
group. Let G be a semisimple Lie group and let Γ ⊂G be a lattice. The lattice
Γ is called uniform if G/Γ is compact. A fundamental result due to Prasad and
Raghunathan shows that a lattice determines the ambient group in a very strong
sense. More precisely, if two connected semisimple Lie groups contain isomorphic
lattices, then their R-ranks coincide [PR72]. In the uniform case, this result is due
to J. A. Wolf [Wol62].

2. Preliminaries
In this section, we review basic definitions and provide explicit examples and
results that will be used throughout the paper. Unless otherwise specified, all Lie
groups considered are connected.
2.1. Uniform approximate lattices. Let G be a locally compact, second-countable
group. Let A, B be subsets of G, then we denote by AB = {ab ∈G : a ∈A, b ∈B
and A−1 = {a−1 ∈G : a ∈A}, their product and inverse set, respectively.
Definition 2.1 (Approximate subgroup). An approximate subgroup of a group G
is a subset Λ ⊂G such that Λ is symmetric (i.e. Λ = Λ−1), contains the identity
element e ∈G, and there exists a finite subset F ⊂G satisfying
Λ2 := {λ1λ2 ∈G | λ1, λ2 ∈Λ} ⊂FΛ.
We recall that a subset P ⊂G is called relatively dense if there exists a compact
subset K ⊂G such that G = KP. Further, P is called uniformly discrete if the
identity e ∈G is not an accumulation point of P −1P, i.e. PP −1 ∩U = {e} for some
neighbourhood U of the identity.
Definition 2.2 (Uniform approximate lattice). An approximate subgroup Λ of
a group G is called a uniform approximate lattice if it is uniformly discrete and
relatively dense.
Definition 2.3 (Approximate lattice). A discrete approximate subgroup Λ of a
group G is called an approximate lattice if there exists a subset F ⊂G with finite
Haar-measure such that G = FP.

Chabauty–Fell topology on C(G) is defined by the subbasis of open sets
OU := {Q ∈C(G) | Q ∩U ̸= ∅}
and

We recall here that the map
G × C(G) −→C(G),

defines a continuous action of G on C(G), and that C(G) is a compact metrizable
space (see [Pau07, Propositions 1.7 and 1.8]). Convergence in the Chabauty–Fell
topology can be characterised as follows: a sequence (Qi)i≥0 in C(G) converges to
Q ∈C(G) if and only if the following two conditions hold:
(1) for every x ∈Q there exist xi ∈Qi such that xi →x as i →∞;
(2) if xi ∈Qi for all i ∈N, then every accumulation point of the sequence
(xi)i≥0 belongs to Q.
Given a closed subset Q ⊂G, its G-orbit in C(G) is the set G·Q := {gQ | g ∈G},
where gQ := { gq | q ∈Q}. The invariant hull ΩQ is defined as the closure of this
orbit in C(G) with respect to the Chabauty–Fell topology, that is,
ΩQ := { gQ | g ∈G } ⊂C(G).
Note that if H ≤G is a closed subgroup, then ΩH is G-equivariantly homeomor-
phic to the one-point compactification of G/H (see [BHS19, Lemma 6]).
Definition 2.4 (Strong approximate lattice). Let Λ be a discrete approximate
subgroup of a locally compact second countable group G. We say that Λ is a strong
approximate lattice if there exists a G-invariant Borel probability measure ν on ΩΛ
such that ν({∅}) = 0 (in which case we say that ν is proper).
2.3. Model set. Model sets were introduced in the seminal work of Meyer (1970).
The model set is used to describe special point patterns, especially in the study
of quasicrystals. A model set is a set of points obtained by projecting a higher-
dimensional lattice onto a lower-dimensional space and selecting points using a
window. These sets help explain non-periodic but highly ordered structures in
mathematics and physics. To define model sets formally, we use the cut-and-project
scheme. We recall definitions from Subsection 2.3 of [BH18].
Definition 2.5 (Cut-and-project scheme). A cut-and-project scheme is a triple
(G, H, Γ) where G and H are locally compact second countable (lcsc) groups and
Γ < G × H is a lattice which projects injectively to G and densely to H.
A
cut-and-project scheme is called uniform if Γ is a uniform lattice.
Given a cut-and-project scheme (G, H, Γ) we denote by πG, πH the coordinate
projections of G × H on G and H, respectively and set ΓG := πG(Γ), ΓH := πH(Γ).
We then define a map τ : ΓG →H by τ := πH ◦(πG|Γ)−1. Note that the image of τ
is precisely ΓH. If G and H are abelian, then τ is known as ∗-map.

Definition 2.6 (Regular model set). Let (G, H, Γ) be a cut-and-project scheme
with associated τ-map τ : ΓG →H. Given a compact subset W0 ⊂H, the pre-image
P0(Γ, W0) := τ −1(W0) ⊂G is called a weak model set, and W0 is called its window.
A weak model set is called a model set if its window has non-empty interior. It is
called regular if the window W0 is Jordan-measurable (i.e the boundary of W0, ∂W0,
has Haar measure zero) with dense interior, aperiodic (i.e. StabH(W0) = {e}), and
satisfies ∂W0 ∩ΓH = ∅.
Definition 2.7 (Uniform model set). A model set is called a uniform model set if
the underlying cut-and-project scheme is uniform.
Definition 2.8 (Meyer set). A relatively dense subset of a model set is called Meyer
Set.

Proposition 2.9. Let Λ = P0(Γ, W0) be a model set over (G, H, Γ).
(1) Λ, and in fact Λ−1Λ, is uniformly discrete.

2.4. Regular and R-regular elements. Let G be a connected semisimple alge-
braic group defined over R and contained in GL(n, C) for some n. We assume that if
g ∈G then conjugate transpose of g, ¯gt, also belongs to G, that is, G is self-adjoint.
Let GR := G ∩GL(n, R) be group of real points in G. The connected component of
the identity, G, of GR is a linear semisimple group. Moreover, any connected linear
semisimple group can be realized in this way (see [Mos55]). Let G be a connected
semisimple Lie group defined over R. Consider the adjoint representation Ad of G
on its Lie algebra g. Let σ be the Cartan involution of g. Then g = k + p, where
k = {X ∈g : σX = X} and p = {X ∈g : σX = −X} satisfying [k, k] ⊂k, [k, p] ⊂p
and [p, p] ⊂k. Any two Cartan decompositions are conjugate to each other under
the action of Ad(G), where Ad(G) denotes the adjoint group of g. Let K be the
analytic subgroup of G with Lie algebra k. We fix a maximal abelian subalgebra
(Cartan subspace) a ⊂p. It is well known that any two Cartan subspaces of p are
conjugate under K. Note that the Lie algebra of the subgroup of real points in
a maximal R-split torus in G can be conjugated to a Cartan-subspace of p. The
dimension of a is the R-rank of G.
For g ∈G, let n1(g) denote the multiplicity with which 1 occurs as an eigenvalue
of Ad(g) and m1(g) denote the number of eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity)
of modulus 1 of Ad(g).
Definition 2.13 (Regular and R-regular elements). An element g ∈G is regular
(resp. R-regular) if for all h ∈G we have n1(g) ≤n1(h) (resp. m1(g) ≤m1(h))
and −1 is not an eigen value of Ad(g).
It is known that any regular element in G is contained in a unique Cartan
subgroup. A subalgebra c is said to be a Cartan subalgebra of a Lie algebra g if c
is a nilpotent Lie algebra, and its normalizer Ng(c) = c. Lie algebra of a Cartan
subgroup is a Cartan subalgebra, and exp(c) = C0. Moreover, there is a one-to-one
correspondence between the Cartan subalgebras of Lie algebras and the Cartan
subgroups of Lie groups.

We conclude this section by raising the following natural question.
Remark and Question: Let G be a semisimple Lie group and Λ ⊂G an
approximate subgroup. By a work of Prasad and Raghunathan [PR72], the analogues
of Theorems 1.3 and 1.5 are known to hold when Λ is a lattice, and these results
extend to the setting of uniform approximate lattices arising as a regular model
set in this article (under some assumption on G). It is therefore natural to ask to
what extent these results remain valid, namely, under which conditions on G or for
which classes of approximate subgroups Λ ⊂G Theorems 1.3 and 1.5 continue to
hold; in particular, whether they hold for all strong approximate lattices, or more
generally for arbitrary approximate lattices.
