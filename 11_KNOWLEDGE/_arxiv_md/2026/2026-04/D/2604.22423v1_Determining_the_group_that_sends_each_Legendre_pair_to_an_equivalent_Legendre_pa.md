One way an exhaustive search algorithm for LPs can be expedited is by narrowing
its search space. However, the main risk of narrowing the search space is that no
LP of length ℓexists in the narrowed space while an LP of length ℓexists. However,
if this narrowing is designed based on the equivalence of LPs in such a way that
the narrowed search space contains at least a set of all non-equivalent LPs, then the
exhaustive search algorithm will find an LP of length ℓif it exists. This is true because
equation (1.1) is preserved among equivalent pairs of vectors in CZℓˆ CZℓ. Then,
restricting the search to finding a set of LPs that includes all non-equivalent LPs is a
viable way to accomplish computational savings. Hence, the concept of equivalence of
LPs plays a key role in designing faster LP finding methods by narrowing the search
space.
The concept of equivalence is based on decimations and cyclic shifts. So, we first
study the group D generated by all cyclic shifts and decimations. Each element of the
group D generated by all cyclic shifts and decimations acts on each vector v in CZℓby
permuting its entries. The orbits of this action constitute the decimation classes. In
Section 2, we will first show that D indeed acts on CZℓ, and determine the structure
of D. Then, we are going to construct the group GG that sends each pu, vq to another
pair pu1, v1q that is equivalent to it, determine the structure of GG, and show that
GG indeed acts on vectors in CZℓˆ CZℓ. The structure of GG has never been studied
before. However, the fact that that GG acts on LPs of length ℓhas been routinely
stated or used by researchers in the field Fletcher et al. (2001); Kotsireas et al. (2023);
