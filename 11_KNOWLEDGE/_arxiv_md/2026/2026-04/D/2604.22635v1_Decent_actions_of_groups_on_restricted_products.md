Decent actions of groups on restricted products
Chris Karpinski
April 27, 2026
Abstract
An action of a group G on a set X is called “decent” if every subgroup of G with a finite
orbit in X fixes a point in X and every finitely generated subgroup of G such that every element
of the subgroup fixes a point of X must itself have a global fixed point. In this article, we
study conditions on when actions of groups on restricted products are “decent”. We prove that
the action of the automorphism group of a restricted product with base space the projective
plane P2(k) over a field k is decent, generalizing a result of Lonjou–Przytycki–Urech.

Introduction
This paper examines actions of groups with various “fixed point” properties.
Definition 1.1. Let X0 be a set. An action of a group G on X0 is elliptic (respectively, purely
elliptic) if G has a global fixed point on X0 (respectively, each element of G fixes a point of X0).
An element of G (respectively, a subgroup of G) is called elliptic if it fixes a point of X0.
A recent question of interest has been to identify and study actions of groups on spaces having
the property that every subgroup of the group that locally fixes points must have a global fixed
point.
Definition 1.2. An action of a group G on a set X0 is decent if:
1. Every subgroup of G with a finite orbit on X0 fixes a point.
2. Every finitely generated subgroup of G acting purely elliptically on X0 fixes a point.
The terminology of “decent actions” was first introduced in [6, Definition 1.2], where they
appeared in the study of subgroups of the plane Cremona group C2(k), which is the group of all
birational transformations of the projective plane P2(k) over a field k. However, such actions have
been studied in the literature before their apparition in [6] (see, for instance, [2], [5], [7]).
Decent actions appear in many natural examples. For instance, any isometric action of a group
on a tree is decent (see, for instance, [1, Proposition 2.5]). For d = 2, 3, any isometric action of
a group on Rd equipped with the Euclidean metric is decent ([2, Proposition 9.2]). Also, by [3,
Chapter II.2, Corollary 2.8(1)], item 1 in Definition 1.2 holds for any isometric action of a group
on a complete CAT(0) space, however, item 2 in Definition 1.2 may fail even for actions on Hilbert
spaces (for every n ≥2, examples of finitely generated groups of isometries of R2n with the Euclidean
metric acting purely elliptically but not elliptically on R2n were constructed in [2, Example 9.1]).

1.1
Organization of the paper
This paper is organized into two main sections. In the first section, we cover the necessary back-
ground and preliminary notions. In particular, in Section 2.1 we define restricted products and their
automorphism groups and in Section 2.2, we discuss the concepts of biregularity and singularity.
Then in Section 2.3, we review the topology and metric structure of the projective space Pn(K) over
a normed field K, and define the notion of proximal and very proximal automorphisms of projective
space. We will need to use these notions in Section 3.2.
We prove Theorem 1.4 in Section 3. The proof of Theorem 1.4 is divided into two main cases
depending on whether the projection G of the acting group Γ onto PGL3(k) has a very proximal
element or not. The case of the existence of a very proximal element is handled in Section 3.2 and
in the case of no very proximal elements, we prove that G must have a finite index subgroup which
fixes a point of P2(K) for some extension K of a finitely generated subfield K of k. This latter case
is handled in Section 3.1, where we prove Lemma 1.10.

2.2
Biregularity
We review the notion of biregularity of maps as defined in [6, Section 4.1].
Let (X0, x0) be a based set and let X be the restricted product of (X0, x0) over a set P. Let
G0 ↷X0 and H ↷P, and G⊕the subgroup of G0 ≀P H preserving X.
Definition 2.3. Fix z ∈X. We say that f ∈G⊕is biregular over p ∈P with respect to z if
f(z)f(p) = zf(p). Equivalently, for f = (g, h) with g ∈Q
P G0 and h ∈H, we have gp(zh−1(p)) = zp.
Otherwise, we say that f is singular over p with respect to z.
An element f ∈G⊕has persistent fibre over p ∈P if there exists l ≥1 such that for all n ≥l,
we have that f n is singular over p with respect to z and f −n is biregular over p with respect to z.
Remark 2.4. ([6, Remark 4.5]) A map f ∈G⊕fixes a point z ∈X if and only if f is biregular
over each p ∈P with respect to z.
Remark 2.5. ([6, Remark 4.6]) If f ∈G⊕has persistent fibre over p ∈P, then it does not fix a
point of X.
