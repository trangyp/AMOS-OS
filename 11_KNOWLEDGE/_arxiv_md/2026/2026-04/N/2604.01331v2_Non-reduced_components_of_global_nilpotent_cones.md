NON-REDUCED COMPONENTS OF GLOBAL NILPOTENT
CONES
DAVID ZHIYUAN BAI AND DAVID FANG
Abstract. We determine the non-reduced components of global nilpotent
cones in various cases of interest. In particular, under the appropriate copri-
mality conditions, we show:
(1) the global nilpotent cone for an L-twisted GLr-Hitchin fibration asso-
ciated to a curve C of genus g ≥2 is nowhere reduced, where L is either the
canonical bundle or has degree greater than 2g −2;
(2) the global nilpotent cone for a moduli space of one-dimensional sheaves
on a K3, abelian, or del Pezzo surface is nowhere reduced;
(3) suppose ℓis a primitive, basepoint-free, big and nef class on a K3
surface, then a general fiber of a Beauville-Mukai system for the class rℓhas
primitive homology class if and only if r = 1.
Our methods include group scheme actions on Lagrangian fibrations, a
GIT-stratification of global nilpotent cones of Hitchin fibrations, and deforma-
tion to the normal cone.

If S is a K3 surface, then a smooth projective M is an example of a compact

To understand the geometry of h, it is natural to study its fibers. We are inter-

C is a smooth curve on S with g(C) ≥2 and β = r[C] for some r ≥2, then the

Definition 0.1. The fiber h−1(rC) is called the global nilpotent cone.
The most well-known example of this is the global nilpotent cone for Hitchin
fibrations. In the classical description, it parameterizes Higgs bundles (E, ϕ) with
nilpotent Higgs field ϕ.
The multiplicities of the components in global nilpotent cones are wide open.
The case r = 2 is quite well-studied: The multiplicities have been computed for
both the Hitchin fibration [16] and the Beauville-Mukai system of genus 2 [15]. For
the Hitchin fibration, the multiplicities of generically regular components are also
known [14].
The purpose of this article is to establish the nonexistence of reduced components
in the global nilpotent cone in almost all cases where h is known to be a fibration
between smooth varieties. Our results will show, in particular:
Theorem 0.1. Suppose gcd(r, χ) = 1, and we are in one of the following situations:
(CY1) S is a K3 or an abelian surface, and [C] is primitive;
(CY2) S = TotKC, and C embeds as the zero-section.
(F1) (S, H) is a polarized del Pezzo surface, and gcd([C] · H, χ) = 1;
(F2) S = TotL where L ∈Pic>2g−2(C), and C embeds as the zero-section.
Then h : M →|β| is a proper flat fibration between smooth varieties. If either
r > 2 or r = 2 and 2 | degC NC/S, then the global nilpotent cone h−1(rC) is
nowhere reduced.
Note that h is generally not flat when S is of general type, so we only consider
the Calabi-Yau and Fano cases.

0.6. Primitivity of the general fiber. For any fibration M →B between smooth
projective varieties with relative dimension d, one can ask whether a general fiber is
primitive, i.e. whether its class in H2d(M; Z) is indivisible. This contains significant
topological information: Indeed, it is a necessary condition for the existence of a
C∞-section.

Mukai system
MS(r[C], χ) →|r[C]|
with [C] primitive and gcd(r, χ) = 1 has primitive general fibers if and only if r = 1.
The statement that a general fiber is not primitive if r > 1 is true more gen-
erally, namely for situations (CY1) and (F1) in Theorem 0.1 under the respective
hypotheses.

1. Picard actions on moduli spaces of sheaves
1.1. Group schemes and fibrations. For our purposes, a fibration is a proper
flat morphism h : X →B with connected fibers between smooth varieties.
Definition 1.1. Let h : X →B be a fibration. Suppose X is equipped with a
holomorphic symplectic form σ, then the fibration h : X →B is called a Lagrangian
fibration if the generic fiber Xη is Lagrangian, i.e. σ|Xη = 0 and 2 dim Xη = dim X.
This definition requires a choice of a symplectic form, which we will keep implicit.
By [3], Lagrangian fibrations are generically abelian, i.e. the generic fiber is
an abelian variety. In particular, over a Zariski open B◦⊂B, the base-change
h◦: X◦→B◦is a torsor over the abelian scheme that is P ◦= Aut0
X◦/B◦→B◦.
Over this open B◦, the symplectic form gives an identification Lie(P ◦/B◦) ∼= ΩB◦
through the group action. Let us consider the extension of this to the entire base
B. This is related to the notion of weakly abelian fibrations studied by Ngô [22].
Definition 1.2. A Liouville structure for a Lagrangian fibration h : X →B is a
B-group space P →B acting on X →B such that:
(1) P is quasiprojective and smooth over B,
(2) P|B◦= P ◦, and
(3) there is an isomorphism Lie(P/B) ∼= ΩB extending Lie(P ◦/B◦) ∼= ΩB◦.
In particular, the group space P →B must be commutative.
Remark 1.1. If P is a subgroup space of AutX/B, then the last property is automatic
by [17, Theorem 4.8].
