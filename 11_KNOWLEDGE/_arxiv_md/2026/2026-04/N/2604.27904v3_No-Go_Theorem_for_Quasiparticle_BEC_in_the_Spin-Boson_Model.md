No-Go Theorem for Quasiparticle BEC in the Spin-Boson Model
Yoshitsugu Sekine
4429sekine@gmail.com
May 7, 2026
Abstract
We analyze the possibility of Bose-Einstein condensation (BEC) at finite temperature
in the spin-boson model within the frameworks of functional integral representations and
the resolvent algebra.
Because a sesquilinear form arising from the zero mode appears,
analogous to the case of the free Bose gas, a BEC-type component is also formally present
in the spin-boson model. However, according to [12], quasiparticles do not undergo BEC,
so we have to exclude this possibility. In particular, for moderate equilibrium states defined
by following the formulation of that paper, a no-go theorem for BEC is obtained.
Keywords: resolvent algebra, Bose-Einstein condensation, IR divergence

Introduction
The spin-boson model is one of the most fundamental models describing the linear interaction
between a two-level quantum system and a Bose field, and it has played a central role in the
study of non-relativistic quantum field theory, dissipative two-state systems, infrared problems,
and quantum phase transitions. In particular, when a massless Bose field is involved, infinitely
many low-energy bosons can contribute with finite energy, so that infrared divergence has an
essential effect on the structure of equilibrium states and ground states. This situation is similar
to that in the Nelson model and the Pauli-Fierz model; however, in the spin-boson model, the
internal degrees of freedom and the spin-flip symmetry participate in the cancellation of infrared
divergences and in phase-transition phenomena, and hence a richer structure appears than in a
simple free-field perturbation model.
For finite-temperature equilibrium states, Fannes, Nachtergaele, and Verbeure [6] studied
the model in the framework of C∗-algebras, proved the uniqueness of the equilibrium state for a
one-dimensional Bose field at all temperatures, and showed the absence of spontaneous breaking
of spin-reflection symmetry. This is a fundamental result showing that, at least in the sense of
the usual spin order parameter, equilibrium states of the spin-boson model do not exhibit phase
separation. On the other hand, in [11], Spohn defined the ground state as the zero-temperature
limit of finite-temperature KMS states and, using the correspondence with the one-dimensional
long-range Ising model in the Ohmic case, analyzed the critical coupling, the order parameter,
and the divergence of the boson number. This line of research subsequently developed into the
problem of existence and non-existence of ground states for infrared-singular spin-boson models,
and more recently led to the non-perturbative existence result of Hasler, Hinrichs, and Siebert
[7] and the analysis of ground-state non-existence at strong coupling and of the Ising-type phase
transition by Betz, Hinrichs, Kraft, and Polzer [2].
These studies have mainly focused on the ground state, infrared divergence, and the cor-
respondence between spin degrees of freedom and Ising-type correlations. In contrast, in this
paper we discuss how the zero mode of the Bose field, that is, a BEC-type component, is present
in equilibrium states in general higher dimensions and how it should be interpreted on the al-
gebra of physical observables. In the free Bose gas, a singular sesquilinear form corresponding
to the zero mode appears in the infinite-volume limit together with the limit of the chemical
potential µ ↑0, and it is related to the non-uniqueness of KMS states, the generation of the
center in the representation algebra, ergodic decomposition, and the breakdown of the cluster
property; this structure is described by operator algebras. In particular, the resolvent algebra
introduced by Buchholz and Grundling [4] has analytic properties suited to physical dynamics
and constrained systems as a C∗-algebraic formulation of the Bose field, and subsequent work
of Buchholz investigated its ideal structure in detail and clarified that the ideals carry physical
information.
The starting point of this paper is the functional integral representation of the spin-boson
model at finite temperature. The free spin part is represented by the finite-temperature spin-
loop measure, namely, as a two-state continuous-time Markov process with periodic boundary
conditions, while the free Bose field is represented as a β-periodic Gaussian field. Since the
interaction appears as a linear functional of the Bose field along the spin path, the quadratic
part of the characteristic functional arising from the Gaussian integration over the Bose field
is generated by the same covariance as in the free Bose field, while the spin degrees of freedom
remain as a phase factor and a weight. In this sense, the same zero-mode sesquilinear form qb,0,β
as in the free Bose gas also appears formally in the spin-boson model. In this paper we make
this structure explicit and show, as one of the main results, that for any physical test function
f, the characteristic functional of the equilibrium state decomposes into qb,0,β coming from
the free Bose gas and the non-condensed component qb,nz,β. In particular, qb,0,β induces the
appearance of the center of the representation algebra and the direct-integral decomposition of

However, this formal BEC component does not by itself mean condensation in a physical
equilibrium state. As shown in [12], quasiparticles generally do not undergo BEC. In the spin-
boson model there is no conservation of particle number, so the situation differs from the usual
control by the chemical potential in the free Bose gas. On the other hand, because of infrared
divergence, the admissible space of test functions becomes smaller. It is therefore necessary
to distinguish whether a component that appears to be a zero mode actually remains as a
regular direction on the algebra of observables, or whether it is pushed outside the algebra as
an infrared singularity or as a non-moderate long-range component. We formulate this point
from the viewpoint of the moderateness of equilibrium states, in particular through time and/or
spatial cluster properties. The conclusion is that if the moderateness of an equilibrium state
is identified with the validity of the time and spatial cluster properties, then the BEC direc-
tion on physical test functions disappears, and correspondingly the BEC ideal of the resolvent
algebra becomes trivial. Thus, although a BEC-like zero-mode component is formally present
in the spin-boson model, it does not remain as an independent condensation direction inside a
moderate equilibrium state.
This conclusion can also be understood naturally by comparison with the free Bose gas and
the van Hove type model. In the free Bose gas, the zero-mode component appears directly
through the decomposition of KMS states and the generation of the center, whereas in linearly
interacting models one must separate the change in the field average from the contribution of the
zero-mode covariance. In the van Hove model, when one considers equilibrium states absorbed
into quasiparticles dressed by the interaction effect, one obtains a no-go theorem stating that
BEC does not remain in moderate quasiparticle equilibrium states [10]. The present paper
applies the same viewpoint to the spin-boson model and studies how the factor arising from the
spin degrees of freedom carries the long-range correlation of the zero mode. In particular, for
the cluster property to hold, not only the zero-mode sesquilinear form but also the asymptotic
behavior of the spin factor must be compatible, and when this compatibility is imposed as
moderateness, the BEC direction disappears. This is also a marked difference from the van
Hove model, where there is no particle-Bose-field interaction.
Finally, we formulate this discussion in the language of the resolvent algebra. Since infrared-
divergent directions appear outside the space of physical test functions, they are removed by
passing to the quotient by the infrared ideal of the resolvent algebra. On this quotient algebra,
the equilibrium state is regular, and its GNS representation is faithful. While a BEC direction
that may remain inside the physical space is recorded as an auxiliary BEC ideal, this ideal also
becomes trivial under moderateness. Therefore, the main claim of this paper is a no-go theorem
for BEC in the sense that, although formal zero-mode and BEC components can be exhibited in
equilibrium states of the spin-boson model, they do not remain as an independent condensation
direction in the algebraic description of physical and moderate equilibrium states.

that is formally present in the spin-boson model is actually absorbed into a moderate equilibrium
state or should instead be expelled as a singular component outside the algebra.

Discussion via the Resolvent Algebra
Starting from the equilibrium state obtained by the functional integral, we organize the ideals
of the resolvent algebra and the algebra of all observables. The point is to distinguish which
directions are killed in the algebra from the outset because of infrared divergence and which
directions are excluded by the moderateness of the equilibrium state. Unlike the van Hove
model [10], which used a point source, the nature of the source ϱ has a strong effect here, so we
do not discuss the behavior with respect to the order s in the dispersion relation ωs(k) = |k|s.
In this section we clearly distinguish three spaces. First, Db,0,β is the minimal region on
which the zero-mode sesquilinear form qb,0,β makes sense; at this stage we do not assume
finiteness of the interaction-induced linear term ℓspin,β or of SSB,β,0. Next, only on Db,phys,β =
Db,0,β ∩dom m do the resolvent expectations of Propositions 4.26 and 4.27 become well-defined
finite quantities after removing the cutoff. Thus, Db,0,β \Db,phys,β consists of infrared directions
excluded not by the zero-mode sesquilinear form but by the divergence of the linear term giving
the mean. By contrast, BEC directions are present inside Db,phys,β as directions on which qb,0,β
remains positive. Below we separate how these two kinds of directions are reflected in the ideals
of the resolvent algebra.

Remark 5.8. Taking the equilibrium state as the starting point, the only essential ideal in the
resolvent algebra is JSB,irs, which kills the infrared-divergent directions. On the physical quo-
tient algebra ASB,phys,β, the equilibrium state is regular and its GNS representation is faithful,
so no additional state-dependent kernel appears in the representation space.
The issue of BEC is not the generation of another abstract quotient but whether there remain
directions in the physical space Db,phys,β satisfying qb,0,β (f) > 0. Proposition 5.6 shows that
even if such directions remain, what is visible is not the vanishing of a fixed generator but the
decay of the resolvent expectation in the large-amplitude limit. It is therefore natural to regard
Jb,BEC not as an essential quotient ideal like the infrared ideal Jb,irs, but as an auxiliary ideal
recording condensate directions remaining inside the physical space. Under the assumption of
moderateness, such directions are empty by Proposition 5.7, and as a result Jb,BEC = {0}.
