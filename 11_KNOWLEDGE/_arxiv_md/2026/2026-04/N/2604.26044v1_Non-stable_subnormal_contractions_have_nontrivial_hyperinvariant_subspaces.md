NON-STABLE SUBNORMAL CONTRACTIONS HAVE
NONTRIVIAL HYPERINVARIANT SUBSPACES
MARIA F. GAMAL’
Abstract. A contraction T on a (complex, separable) Hilbert space is
stable, or of class C0·, if T n →0 in the strong operator topology. It is
proved that for a non-stable pure subnormal contraction T there exists
a singular inner function θ such that the range of θ(T) is not dense.
Consequently, T has nontrivial hyperinvariant subspaces. The proof is
based on results by Esterle and K´erchy. Examples of stable subnormal
contractions are given for which the range of φ(T) is dense for every
φ ∈H∞(φ ̸≡0).
Keywords: Hyperinvariant subspace, subnormal operator, contraction.
MSC (2020): 47A15, 47A45, 47A60, 47B20.
1. Introduction
Let H be a (complex, separable) Hilbert space, and let L(H) be the
algebra of all (bounded linear) operators acting on H. The algebra of all
R ∈L(H) such that TR = RT is called the commutant of T and is denoted
by {T}′. A (closed) subspace M of H is called invariant for an operator T ∈
L(H), if TM ⊂M, and hyperinvariant for T if RM ⊂M for all R ∈{T}′.
The complete lattice of all invariant (resp., hyperinvariant) subspaces for T
is denoted by Lat T (resp., by Hlat T). The hyperinvariant subspace problem
is the question whether for every nontrivial operator T ∈L(H) there exists a
nontrivial hyperinvariant subspace. Here “nontrivial operator” means not a
scalar multiple of the identity operator IH, and “nontrivial subspace” means
different from {0} and H.
A (closed) subspace M is called reducing for T if M ∈Lat T ∩Lat T ∗. It
is known that if an operator T has a (non-zero) reducing subspace M such
that T|M has a nontrivial hyperinvariant subspace or T|M = λIM for some
λ ∈C, then T has a nontrivial hyperinvariant subspace (if T ̸= λIH), see [4].
In particular, this is fulfilled if T|M is a normal operator, because a normal
operator has nontrivial hyperinvariant subspaces or is a scalar multiple of
the identity operator.
Recall that an operator T ∈L(H) is called subnormal if there exists
a complex Hilbert space K and a normal operator N ∈L(K) such that
H ⊂K, H ∈Lat N and T = N|H. N is a minimal normal extension of T, if
∨∞
n=0N∗nH = K. Every subnormal operator T has a unique (up to unitary
equivalence) minimal normal extension, see Corollary II.2.7 in [3]. If T is
subnormal, M ∈Lat T and T|M is normal, then M is a reducing subspace
for T. A subnormal operator T is called pure if it has no (non-zero) reducing
subspaces M such that T|M is normal. Every subnormal operator T has

acts on a non-zero space.
Every unitary operator U has the form U = U(s) ⊕U(a), where U(a) is

measure of U(s) is singular with respect to m. (One of summands can act
on the zero space.) Thus,

For a Hilbert space H and a (closed) subspace M of H, the symbol
PM denotes the orthogonal projection onto M. The spectrum, the point
spectrum, and the spectral radius of an operator T are denoted by σ(T),
σp(T), and r(T), respectively.
Let H and K be Hilbert spaces, let T ∈L(H) and R ∈L(K), and let
X be a (linear, bounded) transformation acting from H to K such that
XT = RX.
If X is a unitary transformation, then T and R are called
unitarily equivalent, in notation: T ∼= R. If X is invertible, that is, X−1 is
bounded, then T and R are called similar, in notation: T ≈R.
2.1. Isometric and unitary asymptotes. To every contraction T ∈L(H)
it can be associated an isometric asymptote (X+, V ) of T, where V is an
isometry and X+ is a canonical intertwining mapping: X+T = V X+. The
unitary asymptote (X, U) of T is the minimal unitary extension of the iso-
metric asymptote of T. More precisely, a unitary operator U is the minimal
unitary extension of V , and X is a natural extension of X+. For exact defini-
tion of isometric and unitary asymptote see [11], or Sec. IX.1 in [15], or Sec.
2 in [13]. The properties (i)-(v) of isometric and unitary asymptotes below
will be used. The properties (i)-(iv) follow from the construction given in
[11] and in Sec. IX.1 from [15]. Property (v) is a particular case of Theorem
IX.3.5 from [15], see also [12].
(i) HT,0 = ker X+, where HT,0 is the stable subspace of T defined in (1.1).
(ii) The range of X+ is dense.
(iii) If M ∈Lat T, then (X+|M, V |clos X+M) is an isometric asymptote of
T|M.
(iv) If M ∈Lat T is such that T|M is similar to an isometry, then X+|M
is bounded below. Consequently, X+M is closed and T|M ≈V |X+M.
(v) If a scalar-valued spectral measure of U is m, then there exists M ∈
Lat T such that T|M ≈S.
Property (ii) of isometric asymptote has the following corollary: if the
isometry V from an isometric asymptote of a contraction T is not unitary,
then there exists a (linear, bounded) transfomation Y such that Y T = SY
and clos Y H = H2. This implies σp(T ∗) = D. Moreover, if this contraction
T does not have a singular unitary summand, then clos θ(T)H ̸= H for every
inner function θ, see Lemma 1.3 in [8].
