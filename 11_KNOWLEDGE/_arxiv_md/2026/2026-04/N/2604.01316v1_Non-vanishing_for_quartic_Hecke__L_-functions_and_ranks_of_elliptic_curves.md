1. Introduction
The non-vanishing problem for central values of L-functions is a fundamental question
of great arithmetic significance.
For example, the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD)
conjecture [BSD65] asserts that the rank of an elliptic curve is equal to the order of
vanishing of its L-function at the central point s “

n-th order Hecke characters over number fields containing Qpζnq, obtaining strong error

This was also proved in the aforementioned work of Blomer, Goldmakher, and Louvel
[BGL14], using the n-th order large sieve developed by them (based on work of Heath-

Over function fields, it is known for any n ě 2 that a positive proportion of L-functions
associated to n-th order characters with squarefree modulus do not vanish at the central
point [BF18,DFL21,DFL25]. We direct the interested reader to [DdFDS24] and [Sou23]
for more detailed expositions on moments of L-functions and non-vanishing.
1.1.2. Twists of elliptic curves. The non-vanishing of central L-values for twists of el-
liptic curves has been intensely studied. Goldfeld’s conjecture [Gol79] states that 50%
of the quadratic twists of an elliptic curve E{Q should have analytic rank 0 (respec-
tively 1). Recently, Smith [Smi25] showed that Goldfeld’s conjecture follows from BSD.
Moreover, much unconditional progress has been made due to his breakthrough work
[Smi26a, Smi26b] on Selmer groups. For instance, Burungale and Tian [BT26] proved
a p-converse theorem which combined with Smith’s results on 28-Selmer groups implies
Goldfeld’s conjecture for the congruent number family y2 “ x3 ´q2x, for q P Z squarefree.
Goldfeld’s minimalist philosophy applied to higher order twists over number fields pre-
dicts that their analytic ranks are typically as small as permitted by their root numbers.
For higher order twists over Q, Alp¨oge, Bhargava, and Shnidman [ABS24] proved a pos-
itive proportion of rank 0 (respectively rank 1) in the cubic family x3 ` y3 “ q and the
sextic family x3 ` y3 “ q2, for q P Z. Conjecturally, each of the ranks 0 and 1 occur 50%
of the time in this setting. See also [KL19,BES20,KS24] and references therein.
For twists over number fields, Burungale and Tian [BT26] showed that if 3 is not inert3
in the imaginary quadratic field K and the elliptic curve E{K has CM by an order of K,
then a positive proportion of quadratic twists of E{K associated to squarefree q P OK have
analytic rank 0. An important input is the work of Bhargava, Klagsbrun, Lemke Oliver,
and Shnidman [BKLOS19]. Conjecturally, 100% of quadratic twists over K have rank 0.
All of the results mentioned so far study Selmer ranks (using various techniques) and
