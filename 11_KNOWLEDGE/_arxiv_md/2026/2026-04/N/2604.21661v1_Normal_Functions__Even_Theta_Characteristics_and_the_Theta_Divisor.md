Introduction
Theta characteristics on compact Riemann surfaces are classical ob-
jects of study in algebraic geometry and they play a crucial role in
the theory of spin structures.
Recall that for a compact connected
Riemann surface C of genus g ≥2, a theta characteristic on C is a
holomorphic line bundle L on C such that
L⊗2 ≃ωC,
where ωC denotes the canonical line bundle of C (see Definition 1.1).
These objects are naturally identified with spin structures on the un-
derlying Riemann surface. More specifically, if Σ is a Riemann surface
equipped with a spin structure, the associated complexified spinor bun-
dle SC on Σ decomposes as S+ ⊕S−where S+ is a holomorphic line
bundle satisfying the condition that (S+)⊗2 is holomorphically isomor-
phic to the holomorphic cotangent bundle ωΣ of Σ. Thus, S+ is a theta
characteristic of Σ. Conversely, every theta characteristic of Σ uniquely
determines a spin structure on Σ [3].

Structure of the paper. The paper is organized as follows. In Sec-
tion 1, we recall the basic notation for projective curves, introducing
the Jacobian variety and the theta divisor. In Section 2, we provide the
necessary background on the relative Picard bundle and spin sections,
recalling the definition and the rank of normal functions. We also high-
light a key result concerning the local constancy of normal functions
which serves as a fundamental tool for our analysis (see Theorem 2.5).
Section 3 is devoted to the proof of Theorem 3.3, providing the L–even
criterion for the vanishing of the intersection locus GL = G ∩ΘL on a
general curve.
1. Preliminaries
Throughout this paper, the base field is assumed to be the field
of complex numbers C.
Let C be an irreducible smooth connected
projective curve. Denote by ωC the canonical line bundle of C, so
g := dimC H0(C, ωC)
coincides with the genus of C. Any divisor associated with ωC is called
a canonical divisor; such a divisor is denoted by KC, and the equality
deg(KC) = 2g −2 holds.
Let Div(C) denote the group of divisors on C and Divd(C) ⊂Div(C)
the subset of divisors of degree d. For any integer d, the Picard variety
of degree d is defined to be
Picd(C) := Divd(C)/ ∼,
where ∼denotes the linear equivalence. Equivalently, Picd(C) parametrizes
all the isomorphism classes of holomorphic line bundles of degree d on
C.
