NONLOCAL FOURIER LAWS FOR HEAT PROPAGATION VIA
FRACTIONAL POWERS OF VECTOR OPERATORS
FABRIZIO COLOMBO, FRANCESCO MANTOVANI, AND PETER SCHLOSSER
Abstract. The present work is devoted to the study of fractional powers of vector operators,
with particular emphasis on the gradient operator with non-constant coefficients. Within
the setting of Clifford algebra Rn, this operator turns out to have bisectorial properties. By
applying the spectral theory on the S-spectrum, we address a fundamental mathematical
challenge: unlike sectorial operators, bisectorial operators involve fractional powers that are
not analytic on the negative real line. To circumvent this, we introduce a novel definition
of the fractional power function in this setting. Building upon previous works on bisectorial
vector operators and weak solutions, we extend the definition of fractional powers to abstract
vector operators.
The core contribution of this work is the application of the functional
calculus for vector operators to the gradient operator, showing that these fractional powers
provide a rigorous mathematical foundation for nonlocal Fourier laws in heat propagation.
AMS Classification 47A10, 47A60.
Keywords: Nonlocal Fourier laws, Fractional powers, Vector operator, Generalized Gradient,
S-spectrum.

In order to give meaning to the fractional powers pα(T) and qα(T) of any bisectorial Clifford
operator T, we will use the so called H∞-functional calculus. The H∞-functional calculus,
see [26, 28, 29], extends the Riesz-Dunford functional calculus [18, 35] to sectorial and bisec-
torial operators. It is established via a three-step procedure: First, the calculus is defined for
functions that exhibit decay at zero and infinity, see Definition 2.3. In the second step it will
be extended to functions which are continuous at zero and at infinity, see Definition 2.4. In
the third step, via a regularization procedure, the calculus is extended to functions with poly-
nomial growth, see Definition 2.5 and Remark 2.6. This theory is of fundamental importance
for linear operators on Banach spaces, and it is used to study partial differential equations and
time-dependent evolution processes. In our previous works [34], there is already a study of
bisectorial operators in the Clifford setting and a detailed investigation of the H∞-functional
calculus is provided.
Our approach to fractional diffusion problems, based on the S-spectrum, extends beyond the
Fourier law with constant coefficients. It applies to the general variable-coefficient case (1.1)
and generates the associated non-local diffusion operator.

In this section, we define and analyse two versions of fractional powers of bisectorial operators
T with real exponent. More precisely, with the functions in (1.2), we investigate the operators
pα(T) and qα(T) for every α ∈R. Since the logarithm ln(s) in (1.4) and hence the fractional
power sα in (1.3) are intrinsic functions for Sc(s) > 0, both functions
pα(s) and qα(s) are intrinsic for Sc(s) ̸= 0.

need to regularize the function at s = 0, which makes it necessary to assume T injective, see
Definition 3.12.
