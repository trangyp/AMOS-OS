Abstract. We study families of abelian varieties over smooth proper curves with small l-adic local
system over characteristic p. We show that such abelian schemes have a non-nef Hodge bundle and

varieties over smooth proper curves in characteristic p, assuming W2(k)-liftability.

1.0.2. Isogeny leaves. In this paper, we aim to provide a moduli-wise description for the abelian
scheme X/C with small l-adic local system.
We write Ag for Ag,1,n, n ≥3 when no confusion arises. The scheme Ag,¯Fp has a rich geometric
structure admitting several stratifications. We mainly consider the Newton stratification, which
admits an almost product structure (also called a foliation, see [Oor07]). Namely, for each Newton
stratum Wξ, there are two kinds of subvarieties called central leaves C(x) and isogeny leaves I(x),
which are transversal to each other. Moreover, for any irreducible component W ⊆Wξ,there is a
finite dominant morphism
C(x) × I(x) ↠W.
A central leaf C(x) associated to a polarized abelian variety x = [A, λ] is the locus of abelian
varieties whose p-divisible groups are isomorphic to A and respecting the polarizations:
C(x) = {(B, µ) ∈Ag(k); (B[p∞], µ[p∞]) ∼= (A[p∞], λ[p∞])}.
The isogeny leaf is, roughly speaking, the maximal integral subvariety in which any family of abelian
varieties is isogenous to a constant family (see Section 2 for details).
Consider the l-adic local system given by the Tate modules. One can define Gl(Z) to be the
algebraic l-adic monodromy group of a variety Z with a morphism φ : Z →Ag, defined as the
Zariski closure of the image of the associated l-adic representation. One then considers the neutral
component Gl(Z)◦of Gl(Z), called the connected algebraic l-adic monodromy group of Z.
On the one hand, central leaves have full l-adic monodromy group GSp2g by the Hecke orbit
conjecture (now a theorem, see [Cha05]), while the isogeny leaves have small l-adic monodromy
groups by definition. These facts motivate the following slogans: the subvarieties contained in
the central leaves exhibit monodromy behavior similar to the characteristic zero case, whereas the
subvarieties contained in the isogeny leaves exhibit behavior specific to characteristic p.
The following fact is well known to experts. We include a proof in this paper for completeness.
Fact 1.4. Let Z be a smooth projective variety over k and let φ : Z →Ag,k be a nontrivial
morphism. Then G◦

Main theorem 1.2 then implies that isogeny leaves are purely characteristic p constructions, and
they do not even admit an infinitesimal deformation to W2(k).

2. Subvarieties of isogeny leaves and l-adic monodromy
2.1. Preliminaries on isogeny Leaves. Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p > 0.
Let Ag,d,n,k denote the (fine) moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties of
dimension g with degree-d polarization and a level-n structure over k, where n ≥3 and (n, p) = 1.
When no confusion arises, we write Ag for Ag,1,n,k. The isogeny leaves can be considered as mod p
reduction of the Rapoport-Zink spaces (see [RZ96]). Alternatively, Oort (see [Oor07]) defined the
isogeny leaves as the maximal integral Hα-scheme.
Definition 2.1 (Hα-scheme). Let S be a k-scheme and let (A, λ) →S be a polarized abelian
scheme over S. A reduced scheme I ⊆S is called an Hα-scheme if there exists a polarized abelian
variety (B, µ) over k, a scheme T of finite type over k, and a surjective morphism T ↠I together
with an isogeny
φ : (B, µ) × T →(A, λ) ×I T
such that every geometric fibre of φ is of local-local type. The pair (T →I, φ) is called a chart of
the Hα scheme.
Remark 2.2. Note that for an Hα-scheme S ⊂Ag,1,n with isogeny φ : (B, µ) × T →(A, λ) ×I T as
in the definition, we do not require µ to be principal. Indeed, (B, µ) ∈Ag,d,n(k), where d = deg(φ).
Here, we adopt the convention that a polarization λ = φL for some ample line bundle L on A has
degree d if χ(L) = d. So, deg(φL) = d2.
Lemma 2.3. A finite group scheme G over a perfect field k of characteristic p > 0 is of local-local
type if and only if G is a successive extension of αp over k.
Proof. Let pn be the rank of G. By Dieudonn´e theory, it suffices to show a finite-length Dieudonn´e
module (M, F, V ) over W(k) has nilpotent F, V if and only if M has a Dieudonn´e submodule
(k, 0, 0) ⊆(M, F, V ) and
(M, F, V )/(k, 0, 0)
also has nilpotent F, V .
One direction is clear. Conversely, one can consider the Dieudonn´e submodule (Ker(F), 0, V )
which is nontrivial as F is nilpotent. Similarly, (Ker(F) ∩Ker(V ), 0, 0) is a nontrivial Dieudonn´e
submodule of (M, F, V ), with Ker(F)∩Ker(V ) ∼= k⊕m for some m. This gives the desired submodule
(k, 0, 0) ⊆(M, F, V ). The quotient has nilpotent F, V obviously.
□
More generally, a finite flat group scheme G over a k-scheme S is of local-local type if and only if
G is locally isomorphic to a successive extension of αp over S. This explains the name of Hα-scheme.
The isogeny leaf will be considered as the irreducible component of maximal Hα-scheme.
Theorem 2.4 (Oort, [Oor07]). Let [(A, λ)] = x ∈Ag(k) be a point corresponding to a principally
polarized abelian variety over k. Then there exists a reduced, closed subscheme I(x) ⊂Ag which
is a Hα-scheme such that every irreducible component of I(x) contains x and I(x) is the union of
all irreducible Hα-schemes containing x.
Definition 2.5 (Isogeny leaf). Let x = [(A, λ)] ∈Ag(k) and let I(x) ⊂Ag as in the above theorem.
An irreducible component of I(x) with the induced reduced subscheme structure is called an isogeny
leaf through x and denoted by I(x).
