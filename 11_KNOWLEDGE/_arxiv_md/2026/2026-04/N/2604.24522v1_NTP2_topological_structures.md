NTP2 topological structures
Pablo And´ujar Guerrero
Abstract
A subset of a topological space is constructible if it is a finite Boolean combination
of closed sets. We prove that every NTP2 expansion of (R, <, +) by constructible sets
defines only constructible sets, and that definable functions are generically piecewise
continuous. The result also holds for all NTP2 expansions of (Qp, +, ·), and all NTP2
definably complete expansions of ordered groups. In the latter case, the structure is
generically locally o-minimal, has definable choice, and carries a well-behaved notion of
naive topological dimension. For NIP uniform topological structures, constructibility
of definable sets is preserved in the Shelah expansion. We classify strong expansions
of (R, <, +) by constructible sets, and obtain results on NTP2 d-minimal structures.
Mathematics Subject Classification 2020. 03C45, 03C64 (Primary); 54H05 (Secondary).
Key words. Neostability, NTP2, tame topology, constructible sets, open core.

Introduction
This paper advances two closely related programs at the interface of topology and model
theory.
On one hand, it forwards the exploration of first-order structures over the real
ordered group (R, <, +) known as Miller’s program [MS99, Mil05, DMS10], which aims to
identify meaningful dividing lines among these structures, governing the topological and
geometric behavior of definable sets and functions. On the other hand, it contributes to the
recent breakthroughs in the investigation of the connections between neostability —Shelah’s
model-theoretic framework of tame combinatorics— and topological tameness [DG17, HW18,
Wal22], with a focus on general topological structures whose topology arises from a definable
uniformity.
The main axiom of neostability that we will work with is the tree property of the second
kind (NTP2), a setting generalizing both simple and NIP theories. A subset of a topological
space is constructible if it is a finite Boolean combination of closed sets. Our first main result,
Theorem A, shows that every NTP2 expansion of (R, <, +) or (Qp, +, ·) by constructible
sets defines only constructible sets. This improves previous work of Walsberg in the NIP
setting [Wal22], and answers the NTP2 case of what is known as the main conjecture on
expansions of the real field. This conjecture postulates that an expansion of (R, +, ·) by
constructible sets defines a dense and codense subset of R iff it defines Z. Our result yields
that, if one such expansion is NTP2, then it cannot define a dense and codense subset of R.

We call a structure M = (M, . . .) a uniform (topological) structure if M is a (Hausdorff)
uniform space induced by a uniformity on M × M that admits a basis given by a definable
family of sets.
Our main examples of uniform structures are provided by expansions of
topological groups. Given the induced topology on M (and product topology on Mn), we
say that M is constructible if every definable set is constructible. The open core Mo of M
is defined as the reduct Mo, in the sense of definability, generated by all definable closed sets
(equivalently by all definable constructible sets). In particular, every continuous function
f : M n →M m definable in M is definable in Mo. In this framework, we prove the following.
See Section 2 for definitions.
Theorem A. Let M be an NTP2 definably σ-compact uniform structure. Then the open
core Mo is constructible. In particular, the following structures have constructible open core:
1. Any NTP2 definably complete expansion of an ordered group. For example, any NTP2
expansion of (R, <, +).
2. For any prime p, any NTP2 expansion of the field of p-adic numbers (Qp, +, ·).
We prove Theorem A by introducing a new notion of being “definably Fσ”, which we
denote F def
σ , and by showing that the NTP2 assumption implies that definable sets are F def
σ
iff they are constructible. Given this result, the rest of the proof generalizes ideas present
in [MS99, DMS10]. Specifically, our notion of definable σ-compactness ensures that F def

are closed under projections, and from this and the aforementioned equivalence it is easy to
see that definable constructible sets form a structure, namely the open core.
Our second main result further explores the assumptions in Theorem A, improving, in
this context, the conclusion of a theorem of Jayne and Rogers [JR82] on first level Borel
functions.
Specifically, it establishes that definable functions in the open core are both
generically and (finitely) piecewise continuous.
Theorem B. Let M be an NTP2 definably σ-compact uniform structure. Let f : X →Y
be a function definable in the open core. Let X0 = X and, for every n < ω, let Xn+1 be the
closure in Xn of the set of points of discontinuity of f|Xn. Then:
1. Xj has empty interior in Xi for every i < j (generic continuity).
2. There exists an m such that Xm = ∅(piecewise continuity).
Both Theorems A and B can be derived from a condition apparently weaker than NTP2,
which we call strongly hereditarily Baire (SHB). In the case of expansions R of (R, +, ·),
we are able to recast the previous two theorems into a dichotomy of descriptive set theoretic
flavour (Theorem 7.4). That is, either the open core Ro is constructible and every function
definable in it is generically piecewise continuous, or otherwise Ro defines a first level Borel
(in particular Baire class one) function that is not a finite union of continuous functions.
We investigate the smaller class of NIP, rather than NTP2, uniform structures. Many NIP
classes of topological structures (e.g. o-minimal, P-minimal) are known to be constructible.
Given a structure M, its Shelah expansion is the expansion of M generated by all externally
definable sets. Shelah expansions are central objects of study in NIP model theory.

Preliminaries
2.1
Model-theoretic conventions and terminology
Throughout we denote (first-order) structures by calligraphic letters M, R, . . ., and their
respective universes by uppercase roman letters M, R, . . .. We denote tuples of variables or
parameters by x, y, z, a, b, c, . . ., and non-negative integers by n, m, i, k, . . .. Given a structure
M, “definable” means “definable in M possibly with parameters” unless otherwise specified.
A type-definable set is an intersection of definable sets.
Given a (partitioned) formula φ(x, y) in the language of M, and parameters b ∈M |y|,
we let φ(M, b) = {a ∈M |x| : M |= φ(a, b)}. A family {Xb : b ∈B} of subsets of M n is
definable if B ⊆M m is a definable set and there exists a formula φ(x, y), with |x| = n and
|y| = m, such that Xb = φ(M, b) for every b ∈B. A collection of formulas is k-inconsistent if
every sub-collection of size k is inconsistent. We adopt the standard convention of identifying
formulas with the sets that they define in a given background structure and vice versa.
2.2
Neostability
We present the main notions from neostability that we will use. For other relevant definitions
such as NIP, strong dependence, and dp-rank, we direct the reader to [Sim15].
A structure M has the tree property of the second kind, denoted TP2, if there exists a for-
mula φ(x, y), some k and, for every n and m, a collection of parameters {bi,j : 1 ≤i ≤n, 1 ≤j ≤m}
in M |y|, such that

(1) {φ(x, bi,j) : j ≤m} is k-inconsistent for every i ≤n,
(2) {φ(x, bi,η(i)) : i ≤n} is consistent for every function η : {1, . . . , n} →{1, . . . , m}.
Equivalently, in the above definition one may always assume that k = 2. A structure is
NTP2 if it does not have TP2. A theory T is NTP2 if any (every) model is NTP2.
For any cardinal κ, an inp-pattern of depth κ in M is a family of pairs (φα(x, yα), kα), for
α < κ, where φα(x, yα) is a formula and kα a positive integer satisfying that, for every finite
subset {α1, . . . , αn} < κ and every m, there are parameters {bi,j : 1 ≤i ≤n, 1 ≤j ≤m},
with bi,j ∈M |yαi|, such that
(1) {φαi(x, bi,j) : j ≤m} is kαi-inconsistent for every i ≤n,
(2) {φαi(x, bi,η(i)) : i ≤n} is consistent for every function η : {1 . . . , n} →{1, . . . , m}.
We say that a set X ⊆M |x| admits an inp-pattern of depth κ if the conditions above are
satisfied and, in condition (2), each family {φαi(x, bi,η(i)) : i ≤n} is consistent with X. The
burden of M is the supremum of the depths of inp-patterns admitted by M. We say that
M is strong if every inp-pattern in M has finite depth. A theory T is strong if any (every)
model is strong. Every strong theory is NTP2.
Every strongly dependent theory is strong and NIP, and every NIP theory is NTP2.
2.3
Topological conventions and uniform structures
Let X be a subset of a topological space. We denote by cl(X), int(X), and ∂X = cl(X) \ X
the closure, interior and frontier of X respectively. For any n, we define ∂(n)X recursively
by letting ∂(0)X = X and ∂(n+1)X = ∂∂(n)X. Recall that a subset Y of a topological space
is nowhere dense if cl(Y ) has empty interior. Given two subsets X, Y of a topological space,
we say that Y is dense in X if cl(Y ∩X) ⊇X, and say Y is codense in X if cl(X \ Y ) ⊇X.
If X ⊆Y , we refer to the interior of X in Y always with respect to the subspace topology
on Y .
A (first-order) topological structure is a structure M together with a Hausdorff1 topology
on its universe M that has a basis given by a definable family of sets. A uniform structure2
is a structure M together with a uniformity on M that admits a basis B given by a definable
family of sets. More precisely, B is a definable collection of subsets of M 2 which satisfies:
(a) T B = {(x, x) : x ∈M},
(b) if U ∈B and (x, y) ∈U, then (y, x) ∈U (symmetry),
(c) for any U, V ∈B, there exists W ∈B such that W ⊆U ∩V (downward directedness),

adapted to the non-Hausdorff case.

may assume that basic entourages are open, which implies in particular that balls U[x] are
always open.
Uniform structures have been explored, largely in the context of dp-minimality, in [SW19,
DG22]. Two major classes of examples are given by Γ-valued metric spaces, for Γ any ordered
abelian group, and by topological groups (see [SW19]). We are particularly interested in
ordered groups and valued fields.
Example 2.1.
1. Let G = (G, 0, +, <, . . .) be an expansion of an ordered group. Then G
is a uniform structure, with basic entourages of the form Ub = {(x, y) ∈G2 : |x −y| <

2. An expansion K of a valued field with, say, the divisibility predicate v(x) ≤v(y), is

Given M a topological structure, the open core of M, denoted Mo, is the reduct of
M generated by all closed (open) definable sets, in the language with a symbol for every
nonempty definable open set. This notion was first introduced in [MS99] and further explored
in [DMS10], although the idea of analyzing topological structures whose basic predicates are
open or closed can be traced back to Robinson [Rob74]. Given a uniform structure M, we
assume throughout that Mo, endowed with the same topology, is also a uniform
structure. This assumption is made because it is not clear to the author whether a definable
basis for the uniformity can always be found in Mo. Nevertheless, whenever the uniformity is
given by a definable order < or valuation v, the assumption is redundant, since the relations
given respectively by x < y and v(x) ≤v(y) are both open in M 2.
Our assumption of definable σ-compactness (Definition 4.4) in Theorems A and B, made
in place of properties invoking classical topological compactness, is a meaningful one, since
it includes, for example, definably complete expansions of ordered groups (Lemma 7.2). Our
assumption of NTP2 in place of NIP is also relevant. Below we include some examples to
illustrate this point.

Strongly hereditarily Baire structures
We now introduce a definable hereditary form of the Baire property and prove versions of
Theorem A and Theorem B where we assume only this property in place of NTP2.
In
Section 5, we show that every NTP2 uniform structure has this property, yielding our main
theorems. The reason we isolate this condition is that it is upfront weaker than NTP2. Hence,
it would be interesting to know which uniform structures satisfy it (see Question 5.6). In
Remark 4.11, we isolate a slightly simpler more technical condition that is sufficient to derive
our theorems and that will be used in Section 7.
Definition 4.1. Let M be a topological structure. We say that M is Strongly Hereditarily
Baire (SHB) if, for every structure N ≡M, every set Z ⊆N m, and every upward directed
definable family of closed sets {Cb : b ∈B} in N m, if Cb ∩Z has empty interior in Z for
every b ∈B, then S
b∈B Cb is nowhere dense in Z.
We leave it to the reader to check that the definition of SHB is equivalent to asking that,
for every set Z ⊆N m and upward directed definable covering {Cb : b ∈B} of Z by closed
sets, there exists b ∈B such that Cb has nonempty interior in Z. This definition is strictly
stronger than satisfying the Strong Baire Category Theorem proved in [FHW17] for so-called
type A expansions of (R, <, +). The notion was motivated by the analysis of NTP2 uniform
structures laid out in Section 5, which resembles previous work of Walsberg [Wal22].

Definably complete structures
In this section we restrict our analysis of uniform structures to the setting of expansions of
linearly ordered groups. A linearly ordered structure (R, <, . . .) is definably complete if every
definable subset of R has a supremum in R ∪{−∞, +∞}. This definition clearly generalizes
the notion of being an expansion of (R, <). We first show that definable completeness implies
definable σ-compactness, completing the proof of Theorem A. We then recast Theorems A
and B, applied to expansions of (R, <, +), into a descriptive set theoretic dichotomy (Theo-
rem 7.4). Finally, we prove some facts about constructible expansions of definably complete
groups, including Theorem D.
Since notions such as constructibility and local o-minimality are trivial in discrete ordered
groups, we often focus on densely ordered groups, where we will implicitly rely on the
following fact.
Fact 7.1 ([Mil01], Proposition 2.2). Let R be a definably complete expansion of a densely
ordered group. Then R is divisible and abelian.
Through the next lemma and proposition we show that, in definably complete structures,
definable sets are definably compact iff they are closed and bounded, and so in particular
definably complete structures are definably σ-compact. We use an approach analogous to
Johnson’s [Joh18b].
It is worth noting that one could adapt the proof of Theorem 4.8
directly to the definably complete setting by using the analogue of Fact 4.5 (3) for closed
and bounded sets proved by Miller [Mil01]. Nevertheless, we consider that developing the
basic theory of definable compactness in definably complete structures is of independent
interest. Lemma 7.2 completes the proof of Theorem A.
Lemma 7.2. Let R be an expansion of a linear order. If R is definably complete, then
any closed and bounded interval I ⊆R is definably compact. In particular, R is definably
σ-compact.
Proof. Let C = {Cb : b ∈B} be a downward directed definable family of closed subsets of

that R is definably σ-compact.

D-minimal structures
A definably complete expansion R of a dense linear order (R, <) is d-minimal (short for
discrete-minimal) if, for every definable family C of subsets of R, there exists some n such
that every set in C is the union of an open set and at most n discrete sets6. This notion
originated from the work of Miller [Mil05]. Known examples of NIP d-minimal structures
include (R, <, +, Z) and (R, +, ·, 2Z).
It is an open question whether every NTP2 –or even just NIP– expansion of (R, <, +)
has d-minimal open core (Question 9.6). More generally, every constructible expansion of
(R, <, +) known to the author is d-minimal.
The role of d-minimality in characterizing
neostable structures over the reals is thus conspicuously open. In this section we approach
this subject by proving Theorem F (through Propositions 9.4 and 9.5).
