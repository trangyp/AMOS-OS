Wiener theorem for multipliers

Abstract
Let d ≥2, l ≥0 and suppose X is one of the function spaces W l,1(Td), W l,∞(Td) or
Cl(Td). We extend a result of Henkin (1967), showing that, for appropriate N × N matrix
operators A(D), the subspace of XN consisting of A(D)−free elements is noncomplemented.
In order to prove this we establish a new property of the Fourier multipliers that are bounded
on X: the kernel k of any such multiplier obeys a weaker version of Wiener’s theorem for
the singularities of measures.

Introduction
In [7](1967) G. M. Henkin obtained the following result:
Theorem 1. Suppose d ≥2 and l ≥0 are integers. Then, the space
G1(Cl(Td)) ∶= {∇f ∈(Cl(Td))d ∣f distribution on Td},
(1)
is not a complemented subspace of (Cl(Td))d.
The proof of Henkin in [7] (1967) is based on a clever use of the group of the symmetries of
the sphere. He considers the problem on the sphere instead of the torus. He shows (by averaging)
that, if Theorem 1 fails then there exists a unique corresponding projection that commutes with
the action of Od. However, there is an explicit formula for this projection and it turns out it can
not be Lipschitz. See also [1, Theorem 10.8] for an exposition of this proof.
In the case l = 0 there are other proofs of Theorem 1. For instance:
(i) The proof of S. V. Kislyakov in [11, Theorem 3] (1975) via the theory of absolutely summing
operators. He showed that C1(Td) is not an isomorphic copy of a quotient space of C(S).
Note that this result is much stronger than statement of Theorem 1 when l = 0.
(ii) The proof given in [5, Section 4.2] (2024) via complex interpolation. Here it is shown that the
existence of a bounded projection implies the existence of a projection that is simultaneously
bounded from (C(Td))d to G1(C(Td)) and from (L2(Td))d to G1(L2(Td)). We obtain that
the couple (C1(Td),H1(Td)) interpolates “in the same way” as (C(Td),L2(Td)). This is
shown to be false by using standard Littlewood-Paley and trace theory.

In this paper we study results of the same nature as Theorem 1. In particular, we give a
much simpler proof of Theorem 1 than the proofs listed above (see Remark 35). Instead of the
symmetries of the sphere, we use (in a rather standard way) the symmetries of the torus. By
this the problem gets reduced to a problem concerning the Fourier multipliers on Cl(Td). Then,
inspired by an idea that goes back to J.-P. Kahane ([13, p. 401 and Theorem 3.1]; see also [10,
Section 3] for a similar application), we conclude by using a variant of Wiener’s theorem on the
singularities of measures.
Similar arguments give us more. Our main result is a generalisation of Theorem 1 that does
not seem to be reachable by the methods (i), (ii) listed above neither by the method of Henkin.
