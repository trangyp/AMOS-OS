ANDRÉS RUBIANO AND ARMANDO REYES
Dedicated to Professor Oswaldo Lezama on the Occasion of His 70th Birthday
Abstract. We determine sufficient criteria for the differential smoothness of
ambiskew polynomial rings defined and studied by D. A. Jordan in several
papers [25, 31, 33, 35].
1. Introduction
Following Almulhem and Brzeziński [2, Section 1], “Determining which classes
of non-commutative algebras correspond to smooth non-commutative varieties or
manifolds is one of the outstanding problems of non-commutative geometry”. With
this aim, Brzeziński and Sitarz [16] argued that a method to construct a suitable
graded differential algebra - a differential structure - should determine smoothness
of a non-commutative variety: this kind of smoothness is referred to as differential.
The idea behind the differential smoothness of algebras is rooted in the observation
that a classical smooth orientable manifold, in addition to de Rham complex of
differential forms, admits also the complex of integral forms isomorphic to the de
Rham complex [41, Section 4.5].
Since its introduction, Brzeziński and several
authors (e.g. [11]–[16] and [36, 37, 44, 48, 49]) have characterized the differential
smoothness of different families of noncommutative algebras (see Section 2.1 for
more details).
On the other hand, in a series of papers started in [30], Jordan introduced a class
of certain iterated skew polynomial rings (in the Ore’s sense [43]) K[x; σ][y; σ−1, δ]
on two indeterminates x, y over a commutative ring K which include the universal
enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra sl2(C) and several quantum groups.
Two
years later, in [32] he extended this class of rings by considering an extra parameter
ρ ∈k \ {0} and includes the quantized Weyl algebra, the universal enveloping
algebra of the Dispin Lie superalgebra, and an algebra introduced by Woronowicz
[55]. Related with that, in his paper [33] he defined the ambiskew polynomial rings
which are closely related to the generalized Weyl algebras defined and studied by
Bavula [3, 5], and the down-up algebras defined by Benkart and Roby [7, 8].
Recently, in [47]–[53] the authors have considered the question on the differential
smoothness of different families of noncommutative algebras. Our purpose in this
paper is to investigate this smoothness in the setting of the ambiskew polynomial

3. Differential and integral calculus of ambiskew polynomial rings

We start with some preliminary facts.
Definition 3.1 ([1, Chapter 11]). Let K be a commutative ring. The Krull di-

prime ideals
p0 ⊊p1 ⊊· · · ⊊pn

5. Future work
Generalized down-up algebras (GDU algebras for short) were introduced by Cas-
sidy and Shelton [19] as a generalization of the down-up algebras considered in
Section 4.5.
Following [19, Section 2], let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
zero. Fix scalars r, s, γ ∈k and a polynomial f ∈k[x]. A generalized down-up
algebra L := L(f, r, s, γ) is a unital associative k-algebra generated by d, u and h,
subject to relations
dh −rhd + γd = 0,
(5.1)
hu −ruh + γu = 0,
and
(5.2)
du −sud + f(h) = 0.
(5.3)
When f has degree one, all down-up algebras in [7, 8] are retrieved. Cassidy
and Shelton [19] showed that L has Gelfand-Kirillov dimension three and it is
Noetherian if and only if it is a domain, or equivalently, rs ̸= 0
Almulhem and Brzeziński [2, Section 1] asserted on the GDU algebras that “It
is natural to ask whether they are differentially smooth. In this note we do not
attempt to answer this question, but rather provide the first paving stones for a
path that might lead to an answer by constructing a class of skew derivations.” We
have searched the literature and apparently no work has been done that studies
the differential smoothness of these algebras.
Due to the relationships between
generalized Weyl algebras and GDU algebras, and the study of the automorphisms
and the derivations of these two families of algebras carried out in [13, 17, 19, 38],
we consider that an immediate task is to address this investigation, so that we can
formulate sufficient conditions that guarantee the smoothness of GDU algebras,
and as a consequence, we can obtain some conclusions that were pending at the
end of Section 4.5 when we show the limitations of Theorem 3.10.
