Abstract
We study truncations of hierarchical equations of motion (HEOM) for finite-dimensional
open quantum systems. We prove that for finite-dimensional approximations constructed
with a Schur-complement-type terminator, the spectrum converges to that of the full
HEOM as the truncation depth increases. We also prove that this approximation is free
of spectral pollution: sufficiently deep truncations do not produce spurious unstable
modes, provided the exact HEOM is stable. We illustrate the results for the spin-boson
model.

Introduction
Hierarchical equations of motion (HEOM) [1,2] are a well-established and widely used method
for non-perturbative numerical analysis of open quantum system dynamics. The method has
a broad range of applications [2], including quantum chemistry [3], quantum transport [4],
correlated electronic systems [5,6], and other. Due to its conceptual simplicity and versatility,
HEOM has become a standard tool in computational quantum physics, several open-source
implementations are currently available online [7–10].
In the HEOM formalism, non-Markovian features of the environment are resolved by intro-
ducing an infinite hierarchy of auxiliary density operators (ADOs) [2,11,12] in addition to the
physical reduced density operator of the system. This raises a number of questions about the
mathematical structure of HEOM: whether the dynamics preserves complete positivity [13],
whether it is sensitive to the particular rational approximation of the bath correlation func-
tion [14,15], and which metric is natural in the ADO hierarchy space [16]. Since the hierarchy
is infinite, one also faces the practical problem of constructing a finite-dimensional truncation.
Naive truncations of HEOM are known to be unstable [17–21] because of the spectral pol-
lution. Several approaches have been proposed to address this issue, including projection
techniques [18] and continuous-variable representations of the hierarchy [16,19,20].
In Ref. [21], the authors conclude that “truncating the hierarchy to any finite size can be
problematic for strong coupling to a dissipative reservoir, in particular when combined with
an appreciable reservoir memory time”. In this work, we would like to follow up that study
and exlore stability of truncated HEOM rigorously. We prove that, given a proper construction
of truncated finite-dimensional Liouvillian, its spectrum converges to that of the exact HEOM
with the increase of the truncation size. As a consequence, if the exact HEOM is itself free
of the intrinsic instabilities, sufficiently deep truncations are also stable. We do not, however,
address the question of HEOM stability as such.
The paper is organized as follows. In Sec. 2, we introduce the HEOM formalism and fix the
notations used throughout the paper. In Sec. 3, we study spectral properties of the full HEOM
Liouvillian. We use the fact that the inter-hierarchy couplings grow slower than the diago-
nal dissipation terms and derive bounds on the Liouvillian resolvent inspired by Gershgorin’s
theorem [22,23]. We then use a Schur-complement reduction [24] with respect to the tail of
the hierarchy to analyze the resolvent inside the corresponding Gershgorin sets. In Sec. 4, we
present the main results of the paper: we introduce a finite-dimensional approximation to the
HEOM Liouvillian and prove two main theorems, on spectral convergence of the approxima-
tion and on its stability. Finally, in Sec. 5, we discuss the scope and limitations of the results
and present an illustrative example, followed by conclusions in Sec. 6.
To improve readability, most proofs of lemmas and theorems are presented in the appen-
dices. We keep, however, the proof of Theorem 2 in the main text, since it constitutes one of
the two central results of the paper.

5
Discussion and example
Although we prove spectral convergence for any exhausting truncation sequence, the prac-
tical use of the result is somewhat limited. In the proofs of Theorems 2 and 3, we rely on
bounds for the exact resolvent R(z;L), which is not directly accessible. In particular, we do
not provide any easily verifiable criterion that would determine whether a given truncation T
is sufficiently deep. If the truncated Liouvillian LT has unstable eigenvalues, our results do
not determine whether these are spurious truncation artifacts or whether the exact Liouvillian
L has unstable spectral values. Finally, the explicit bounds based on Gershgorin theorem are
rather pessimistic, in practice we observed eigenvalues to be quite far from the boundaries of
Gershgorin sets.

6
Conclusions
In conclusion, we have shown that for finite-dimensional open quantum systems, a suitable
truncation of HEOM does not lead to spectral pollution or spurious instabilities. This rigorous
analysis is made possible by the partial block-diagonal dominance of the HEOM Liouvillian,
which allows one to derive convenient Gershgorin-type bounds for its resolvent. Although the
practical implications of this result are limited, we believe it presents a valuable contribution to
the mathematical foundations of computational quantum physics. The present work suggests
several directions for further research, such as derivation of more practical bounds, analysis
of HEOM for driven systems, and the construction of suitable truncation schemes for infinite-
dimensional open quantum systems.
