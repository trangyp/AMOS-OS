Abstract. We prove that any 2-generated minimal Taylor algebra on a

to isomorphism and term-equivalence.
1. Introduction
The study of Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP) and its complexity
led to new directions in which Universal Algebra developed. In particular,
the regularity of structures in classical approach to Universal Algebra came
from having many term operations. The approach which seems most natu-
ral to CSP, however, is to squeeze regularity out of having the least amount
of term operations among those algebras which have at least one term op-
eration of some fixed type. This approach was formalized in [1], where
the authors defined minimal Taylor algebras, algebras which have a Taylor
term, but no proper subclone of their term clone contains a Taylor operation.
The increased understanding of minimal Taylor algebras directly translates
to better understanding of polynomial-time algorithms for solving CSP and
simpler proofs of the main result in the complexity of CSP, the Dichotomy
Theorem, a famously complicated result.
The colored edge theory invented by A. Bulatov in his proof of the Di-
chotomy Theorem defines edges on an algebra according to the types of
2-generated subalgebras. Problem 4.2.2 from [4] asks whether there exists
a 2-generated simple minimal Taylor algebra with at least three elements
which is not affine. If the answer is negative, then Bulatov’s theory would
be greatly simplified, since all large 2-generated algebras would be either
affine, or have a nontrivial congruence. The nontrivial congruence would
reduce the proofs of various theorems to the factor algebras and congruence
classes, all of which would be smaller algebras for which the theorem would
inductively hold. Indeed, many theorems in Bulatov’s theory have as the
hardest case the simple 2-generated algebras. If one could prove that all
of those are just the three 2-element minimal Taylor algebras, plus the sim-
ple affine ones, it would shorten and trivialize several proofs in Bulatov’s
theory.

case of the above problem. It was proved that there are no 3-element 2-
generated simple non-affine minimal Taylor algebras in [12], since all 24

Given an algebra A, by a clone of A, denoted Clo(A), we call the set of all
term operations of A, that is the set of operations generated by the set of
basic operations of A. We denote the collection of all operations of arity n
from Clo(A) by Clon(A). Two algebras, say A and B, with the same domain,
are term-equivalent if Clo(A) = Clo(B).
An algebra A is a Taylor algebra if it has an idempotent term operation f
which satisfies a system of identities of the form
f(. . . , ∗, x, ∗, . . . ) ≈f(. . . , ∗, y, ∗, . . . ),
for each coordinate, where ∗’s are substituted somehow with x’s and y’s.
Such an operation f is called a Taylor operation. A clone C on a finite domain
is called minimal Taylor clone if it contains a Taylor operation and every
proper subclone of C is not Taylor. A finite algebra A is a minimal Taylor
algebra if Clo(A) is a minimal Taylor clone. Equivalently, the algebra is a
minimal Taylor algebra if it is Taylor, and it has no proper reduct that is
also Taylor. The next proposition clarifies the existence of a minimal Taylor
clone.
Proposition 1 (Proposition 5.2 of [1], Proposition 4.2.2 of [4]). Every Taylor
clone on a finite domain contains a minimal Taylor clone.
It is not hard to show that the class of minimal Taylor algebras is closed
under the basic constructions.
Proposition 2 (Proposition 5.4 of [1], Theorem 4.2.4 of [4]). Any subalgebra,
finite power, or quotient of a minimal Taylor algebra is a minimal Taylor algebra.

2.2. On edges and absorption within minimal Taylor algebras. In this
part of the paper, we highlight some important notions and concepts from
[1], where three approaches to the CSP were unified through minimal Taylor
algebras.
Definition 5 (Definition 4.3.1 of [4]). Let A be an algebra and a, b ∈A.
• (weak semilattice edge) (a, b) is a weak semilattice edge if there is
a proper congruence θ on Sg{a, b} and a binary term t such that
t(a/θ, b/θ) = t(b/θ, a/θ) = b/θ.
• (weak majority edge) {a, b} is a weak majority edge if there is a proper
congruence θ on Sg{a, b} and a term m ∈Clo3(A) which acts as the
majority operation on {a/θ, b/θ}.
• (weak affine edge) {a, b} is a weak affine edge if there is a proper
congruence θ on Sg{a, b} and a term operation p ∈Clo3(A) such
that (Sg{a, b}/θ; p) is an affine Mal’cev algebra with respect to some
abelian group (Sg{a, b}/θ; +).

• (semilattice edge) (a, b) is a semilattice edge if there exists a binary

not. One can notice that in Definition 3.1 of [1], the more general notion
of so-called abelian edge is defined instead of the affine edge. However,
since we work with minimal Taylor algebras in this paper, where these two
notions coincide (see Proposition 3), we use the term “affine".

Proposition 6 (Proposition 5.3 of [1], Theorem 4.2.4 of [4]). Let A be a minimal
Taylor algebra and B ⊆A be closed under an operation f ∈Clo(A) such that B
together with the restriction of f to B forms a Taylor algebra. Then B is a subuniverse
of A.
Applying Proposition 6, together with Proposition 2, to weak edges and
edges, we obtain
Proposition 7. Let A be a minimal Taylor algebra and a, b ∈A. If (a, b) is a weak
semilattice edge, or {a, b} is a weak majority edge, with the witnessing congruence
θ, then a/θ ∪b/θ is a subuniverse of A. In particular, if (a, b) is a semilattice edge,
then {a, b} is a subuniverse of A.

the graph in which the orientation of weak semilattice edges is forgotten.
Theorem 8 (Theorem 5 of [6], Theorem 4.3.8 of [4]). A finite idempotent algebra
A has a Taylor term iff the graph of weak edges of every algebra B ≤A is weakly
connected.
