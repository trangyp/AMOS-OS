ON GROUPOIDS BEYOND PARTIAL ACTIONS, INNER
AMENABILITY, AND MODELS FOR KIRCHBERG ALGEBRAS
ALCIDES BUSS AND JULIAN KRANZ
Abstract. We construct the first explicit examples of locally compact Haus-
dorff étale groupoids that are not inner amenable and that do not arise as
transformation groupoids associated to partial actions of discrete groups. This
answers questions of Anantharaman–Delaroche and Exel. Our examples include
all Higson–Lafforgue–Skandalis groupoids associated to non-amenable residually
finite groups, as well as their principal variants constructed by Alekseev–Finn–
Sell. These can be chosen to be second countable, ample, and in the latter case
even principal.
We also show that large classes of Deaconu–Renault groupoids with con-
nected unit space do not arise from partial actions of discrete groups, including
cases whose C∗-algebras are Kirchberg algebras in the UCT class. We contrast
this with the totally disconnected case by giving ample transformation groupoid
models for all unital Kirchberg algebras in the UCT class as well as many higher
rank graph algebras. Finally, we characterize precisely when coarse groupoids
arise from partial actions of discrete groups in terms of coarse embeddings into
groups.

1. Introduction
Étale groupoids have become one of the central organizing structures in the
modern theory of C∗-algebras. Since Renault’s seminal work [Ren80], it has been
understood that a large and flexible class of C∗-algebras can be realized as C∗-
algebras of locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoids, and that the presence of a
Cartan subalgebra corresponds precisely to such a realization.
More precisely, Renault showed in [Ren08] that every (separable) Cartan pair
(A, B) arises from a twisted étale groupoid, in the sense that

Presenting abstract C∗-algebras via étale groupoids has led to tremendous progress
in understanding analytic approximation properties of C∗-algebras, most notably,
nuclearity and exactness. Nuclearity of étale groupoid C∗-algebras can be described
dynamically in terms of amenability of the underlying groupoid [ADR00, BM26] –
an approximation property whose consequences in operator algebras and noncommu-
tative geometry are hard to overestimate [CFW81, HK01, Tu99, GS24]. Similarly,
exactness of group C∗-algebras was characterized by Ozawa [Oza00] in terms of
amenability at infinity or Yu’s property A [Yu00] of the underlying groupoid. Ozawa’s
seminal result has similarly striking consequences including the resolution of the
Novikov conjecture for such groups [GK02]. In a recent preprint, Anantharaman–
Delaroche [AD26] (see also [AD23] for an accessible overview) generalized Ozawa’s
theorem to second countable Hausdorff étale groupoids under the additional as-
sumption of a technical condition called inner amenability. Inner amenability has
been established for many examples including all discrete groups, transformation
groupoids associated to group actions, and more generally transformation groupoids
associated to partial group actions with clopen domains (see [AD26, Corollary 5.18]).
Its validity in full generality was left as the main open problem in [AD26]:
Problem A (Anantharaman–Delaroche). Is every locally compact Hausdorff étale
groupoid inner amenable?
Transformation groupoids arising from partial actions of discrete groups on locally
compact Hausdorff spaces form a particularly rigid and well-understood subclass of
étale groupoids. Given a partial action of a discrete group Γ on a locally compact
Hausdorff space X by homeomorphisms between open subsets of X, the associated
transformation groupoid Γ ⋉X is Hausdorff étale and provides a crossed product

Problem B (Exel). Are all locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoids isomorphic

The purpose of this paper is to solve both problems by answering them negatively.
The counterexamples to the first problem are groupoids introduced by Higson–
Lafforgue–Skandalis [HLS02] and Alekseev–Finn-Sell [AFS18] as counterexamples
to the Baum–Connes conjecture and examples of non-amenable groupoids with the
weak containment property (see [Wil15]). We add to their list of exotic properties
by showing that they also fail to be inner amenable:
Theorem C (Theorem 6.8). Let Γ be a residually finite group with associated
Higson–Lafforgue–Skandalis groupoid GHLS and Alekseev–Finn-Sell groupoid GAFS.
Then the following are equivalent.
(i) Γ is amenable;
(ii) GHLS is inner amenable;
(iii) GAFS is inner amenable.
By taking Γ to be a non-amenable residually finite group such as the free group F2,
this yields the first explicit example of a second countable Hausdorff étale groupoid
which fails to be inner amenable in the sense of [AD26].
Although Problem A is intimately related to Problem B, our counterexamples
to the former are not automatically counterexamples to the latter, since the lack
of inner amenability of a given étale groupoid only obstructs its realization as a
partial transformation groupoid with clopen domains. Nonetheless, we prove that
the examples in Theorem C are, among many more, counterexamples to Problem B:
Theorem D. The following groupoids cannot be realized as transformation groupoids
associated to partial actions of discrete groups.
(i) Higson–Lafforgue–Skandalis and Alekseev–Finn-Sell groupoids associated to
residually finite groups which are not locally finite (Corollary 3.8);
(ii) Deaconu–Renault groupoids associated to nontrivial self-coverings of con-
nected spaces (Theorem 4.7);
(iii) The coarse groupoid associated to the maximal uniformly locally finite struc-
ture on an infinite set (Example 5.8).
For coarse groupoids, we in fact completely characterize when they arise as
transformation groupoids in terms of coarse injections and embeddings into groups;
this point of view was suggested to us in discussions with Exel [Exe]:
Theorem E (Theorem 5.5). Let G(X) be the coarse groupoid associated to a
uniformly locally finite coarse space. The following are equivalent:
(i) G(X) is isomorphic to a transformation groupoid by a partial group action;
(ii) X admits an injective coarse map into a discrete group.
Moreover, the partial group action can be chosen to have clopen domains if and only
if X coarsely embeds into a group.
While we do present an example of a coarse space that does not embed into a
group, we do not know whether every uniformly locally finite metric space coarsely
embeds (or even injects) into a group.
We complement our negative results with two positive results providing trans-
formation groupoid models for a large class of well-studied C∗-algebras. Our first
positive result is an adaptation of a theorem by Wu [Wu25] which presents every
stable UCT Kirchberg algebra as a crossed product by a (global) action of a discrete
group Γ on a locally compact space X.
Theorem F (Theorem 7.6). Every unital Kirchberg algebra in the UCT-class can be
realized as the crossed product C(Y )⋊r Γ associated to a partial action of a countable
discrete group Γ on the Cantor set Y .

Our approach is based on the following two key techniques.
(i) The first key ingredient from [dCK24] is a characterization of transformation
groupoids in terms of pure cocycles (see Definition 2.4) c: G →Γ of the
given groupoid G into the given group Γ. The properties of c (such as being
locally constant and non-trivial) are quite rigid and allow us to obstruct the
existence of transformation groupoid models for many Deaconu–Renault
groupoids. In the case of coarse groupoids, this approach moreover gives
a recipe to construct maps from the coarse space into a group out of a
transformation groupoid model and vice versa.
(ii) The second main technique is the abstraction of a structural property
inherent to transformation groupoids which we call property ∆(see Defi-
nition 2.5). Property ∆is inspired by a stronger condition appearing in
[AD26, Lemma 5.16] which aims to formalize the properties of the ‘diagonal’
subgroupoid Γ ⋉(X × X) ⊂(Γ ⋉X) × (Γ ⋉X) for a partial action Γ ↷X.
Our main observation is that property ∆can be obstructed whenever ‘infi-
nite pieces of a groupoid can be uniformly approximated by compact pieces’.
This core property of Higson–Lafforgue–Skandalis and Alekseev–Finn-Sell
groupoids is exploited in Theorem 3.7 in order to show that they do not
satisfy property ∆and therefore do not arise from partial actions.
Structure of the paper. In Section 2 we introduce property ∆and prove that
all transformation groupoids arising from partial actions satisfy it. In Section 3 we
establish failure of property ∆for HLS and AFS groupoids and deduce that they are
not transformation groupoids. In Section 4 we discuss Deaconu–Renault groupoids,
showing in particular that property ∆is not sufficient for being a transformation
groupoid. We obtain our positive results concerning higher rank graphs here. In
Section 5 we characterize when coarse groupoids arise from partial actions in terms
of coarse embeddings into groups. In Section 6 we study inner amenability and prove
that for HLS and AFS groupoids it is equivalent to amenability of the underlying
group. Finally, in Section 7 we realize all unital UCT Kirchberg algebras via partial
actions of discrete groups.

2. Transformation groupoids, pure cocycles and Property ∆
In this section we recall the definition of Hausdorff étale groupoids and develop
general tools that allow us to characterize the subclass of transformation groupoids
associated to partial actions among Hausdorff étale groupoids. Recall that a groupoid
G is a small category where all morphisms are invertible. Equivalently, a groupoid
consists of a set G of morphisms (called arrows), a subset G(0) ⊂G of objects2
(called units), maps r, s: G →G(0) (called range and source), a map
·: G(2) := {(g, h) ∈G × G: s(g) = r(h)} →G
(called multiplication) and a necessarily unique map (−)−1 : G →G (called inversion)
satisfying a list of natural axioms (c.f. [Ren80]). We emphasize that the composition
is written from right to left so that gh is defined whenever s(g) = r(h). Any group
Γ will be identified with the groupoid which has one unit and Γ as endomorphisms.
A topological groupoid is a groupoid G equipped with a topology such that all the
above maps are continuous. A locally compact Hausdorff (topological) groupoid G is
called étale if the range map r: G →G(0) is a local homeomorphism. A subset U ⊂G
is called a bisection if s|U : U →s(U) and r|U : U →r(U) are homeomorphisms.

3. Higson–Lafforgue–Skandalis and Alekseev–Finn-Sell groupoids
Our first class of Hausdorff étale groupoids which are not transformation groupoids
by partial actions goes back to Higson–Lafforgue–Skandalis [HLS02] and Alekseev–
Finn–Sell [AFS18]. Higson–Lafforgue–Skandalis groupoids (HLS groupoids) were
introduced in [HLS02] as counterexamples to the Baum–Connes conjecture. The
main feature of these groupoids for disproving the Baum–Connes conjecture are

a non-amenable étale groupoid whose full and reduced C∗-algebra agree. Later,

In this section, we add to the list of exotic properties of HLS and AFS groupoids
by proving that they lack property ∆(Definition 2.5) and therefore do not arise as
transformation groupoids from partial group actions. The argument relies on the
existence of large families of elements which can be approximated by compact pieces
and which remain uniformly close to the diagonal in G × G under translations by
the acting group. This phenomenon occurs near the fiber at infinity and reflects the
equicontinuity of the translation action of a group on its profinite completion. We
will moreover exhibit HLS and AFS groupoids as the first examples of non inner
amenable groupoids in Section 6.

5. Coarse groupoids
In this section, we investigate when the coarse groupoid associated to a uniformly
locally finite metric space (or more generally connected coarse space) is isomorphic
to a transformation groupoid associated to a partial action of a discrete group. We
prove that the coarse groupoid can be realized as a transformation groupoid by a
partial group action if and only if the space admits a coarse injection into the given
group and that the coarse injection can be chosen to be a coarse embedding if and
only if the partial action can be chosen to have clopen domains.
Coarse groupoids associated to uniformly locally finite metric spaces were origi-

Definition 7.3 ([Ma22, Definition 3.6]). A Hausdorff étale groupoid G with totally
disconnected unit space6 is said to have paradoxical comparison if for every nonempty
compact open subset O ⊂G(0), there are compact open bisections U1, U2 ⊂G such
that s(U1) = s(U2) = O and such that r(U1), r(U2) are disjoint subsets of O.

space and paradoxical comparison. Then the set
S := {[1O] | O ⊂G(0)compact open} ⊂K0(C∗(G))
is a subgroup.
Proof. We first prove that S is closed under addition. Let O1, O2 ⊂G(0) be compact
open subsets.
By paradoxical comparison, there are compact open bisections
U1, U2 ⊂G such that s(U1) = s(U2) = O1 ∪O2 and r(U1), r(U2) ⊂O1 ∪O2 are
disjoint. Using Lemma 7.1, we get
[1O1] + [1O2] = [1s(U1O1)] + [1s(U2O2)] = [1r(U1O1)] + [1r(U2O2)] = [1r(U1O1)∪r(U2O2)].
Next, we prove that S is closed under inversion. Let O ⊂G(0) be a compact open
subset. By paradoxical comparison, there are compact open bisections U1, U2 ⊂G
such that s(U1) = s(U2) = O and r(U1), r(U2) ⊂O are disjoint. Using Lemma 7.1,
we get
[1O] = [1r(U1)] + [1r(U2)] + [1O\(r(U1)∪r(U2))] = [1O] + [1O] + [1O\(r(U1)∪r(U2))],
and thus
−[1O] = [1O\(r(U1)∪r(U2))].
□
