ON THE NORMALIZED LOCAL VOLUME OF A NON-CLOSED
POINT
DONGHYEON KIM
Abstract. In this note, we show that the normalized local volume of a non-closed
point can be expressed in terms of the normalized local volumes of closed points.
1. Introduction
The notion of normalized local volume is introduced in [16] to investigate a canonical
metric structure of an affine cone. It has been revealed that this notion has connections
with Kähler-Einstein metric in [15]. Many conjectures are settled around the notion under
the name of stable degeneration conjecture (cf. [16, 17, 18]), and most of the conjectures
are now solved (cf. [1, 2, 24, 26, 27]).
Usually, the notion of normalized local volume is defined in a closed point of a variety.
One may wonder if we can extend the notion to a non-closed point (cf. [17, Conjecture
6.7] and [18, Remark 2.5]). For example, one can define the normalized local volume at a
non-closed point as a natural generalization of that in a closed point, as in Definition 2.1,
and one can also define it using the constructibility of normalized local volume (cf. [24,
Theorem 1.3]) for closed points (for example, [8, 28]). In particular, it was conjectured
that after the right scaling, the definitions are equivalent. In this note, we confirm the
intuition regarding the normalized local volume of a non-closed point and provide the
full affirmative answer on [17, Conjecture 6.7].
Theorem 1.1 (cf. [17, Conjecture 6.7]). Let (X, ∆) be a klt pair of a variety over an
uncountable field, and let x ∈X be a (not necessarily closed) point. Let x′ ∈{x} be a
general closed point, and let n := dim X and d := dim {x}. Then,
d
vol(x′, X, ∆) =
nn
(n −d)n−d · d
vol(x, X, ∆).
Let us provide a sketch of the proof. Intuitively, we want to mimic the argument in [20,
Proof of Theorem 2.16]. Using the henselian pair argument (cf. [11, Step 3 of Proof of
Theorem 1.1]), we can obtain an étale cover Y →X that admits a left inverse Y →Z
of Z →Y (which plays the role of π : X →A1 in [20, Proof of Theorem 2.16]); this is
sufficient to prove our main theorem using the arguments in [20, 21]. Note that for using
[24, Theorem 1.3] in our proof, we must treat the geometric generic fiber of Y →Z, and
that the quasi-monomiality and uniqueness of the minimizer of normalized local volume
is indispensable (cf. [24, 26]).
