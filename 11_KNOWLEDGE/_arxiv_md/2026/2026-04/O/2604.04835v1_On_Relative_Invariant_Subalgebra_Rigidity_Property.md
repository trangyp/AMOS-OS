groups have the relative C∗-ISR property. Furthermore, we establish an analogous
relative ISR-property for irreducible lattices in higher-rank semisimple Lie groups,
such as SLd(Z) (d ≥3), with trivial center.
1. Introduction
The structural theory of von Neumann algebras associated with discrete groups
has witnessed a remarkable resurgence through its deep interplay with geometric
group theory and Popa’s deformation/rigidity paradigm. A central theme in this
area is the rigidity of subalgebras A ⊆L(Γ) that remain invariant under conjugation
by Γ.
Motivated by the works of Chifan–Das [CD20] and Alekseev–Brugger [AB21]
on Γ-invariant subalgebras for negatively curved groups and higher-rank lattices,
Kalantar–Panagopoulos [KP23] proved that for irreducible lattices Γ in higher-rank
semisimple Lie groups, every Γ-invariant von Neumann subalgebra of L(Γ) arises as
the group von Neumann algebra of some normal subgroup of Γ.
This result strongly motivated the author and Jiang [AJ23] to initiate a system-
atic study for countable groups. In our work, we introduced the invariant subal-
gebra rigidity (ISR) property (a group Γ is said to have the ISR property if every
Γ-invariant von Neumann subalgebra of L(Γ) is of the form L(K) for some normal
subgroup K ⊴Γ). The ISR property has proven extremely fruitful and has been gen-
eralized and strengthened in many subsequent works, including those of Chifan–Das–
Sun [CDS23], Dudko–Jiang [DJ24], Jiang–Zhou [JZ24], Amrutam–Dudko–Jiang–
Skalski [ADJS25]. More recently, Jiang, along with Li and Liu [JL26b,JL26a], has

In recent years, the rigidity of invariant subalgebras in both the von Neumann
algebraic and the reduced C∗-algebraic settings has emerged as a powerful tool that
bridges operator algebras with geometric group theory. The present paper addresses
a natural yet subtler variant of this question: rigidity when invariance is required
only under a non-trivial normal subgroup N ⊴Γ rather than under the whole group.
Our goal is to develop a rigidity theory in two distinct but robust geometric settings:
the large and geometrically flexible class of torsion-free hyperbolic groups, and the
rigid realm of irreducible lattices in higher-rank semisimple Lie groups, such as
SLd(Z) (d ≥3). To motivate our results, we introduce the following relative version.
Definition 1.1. A group Γ is said to have the relative ISR-property if for every
non-trivial normal subgroup N ⊴Γ and every von Neumann subalgebra M ⊆L(Γ)
invariant under conjugation by N, one has M = L(K) for some subgroup K ≤Γ.
Moreover, we say that Γ has relative C∗-ISR-property if every N-invariant unital
C∗-subalgebra A ≤C∗

r(K) for some subgroup K ≤Γ.
Our first main result shows that this property holds for the class of acylindrically
hyperbolic groups.
Theorem 1.2. Let Γ be a torsion-free acylindrically hyperbolic group with trivial
amenable radical. Then Γ has the relative ISR-property.
Alongside the von Neumann algebraic setting, we also establish the same rigidity
for reduced group C∗-algebras.
Theorem 1.3. Let Γ be a torsion-free hyperbolic group. Then Γ has relative C∗-

2. Preliminaries
2.1. Acylindrical actions and acylindrically hyperbolic groups. We recall
the basic definitions following Osin [Osi16] and Dahmani–Guirardel–Osin [DGO17].
Definition 2.1 (Acylindrical action). An action of a group Γ on a metric space
(X, d) by isometries is acylindrical if for every ε > 0, there exist constants R, N > 0
such that for all x, y ∈X with d(x, y) ≥R,
|{g ∈Γ : d(x, gx) ≤ε and d(y, gy) ≤ε}| ≤N.
A group Γ is acylindrically hyperbolic if it admits a non-elementary acylindrical
action on a Gromov hyperbolic space.
A feature we exploit heavily is the rigid structure of maximal elementary sub-
groups of individual elements.
Definition 2.2 (Elementary closure and primitive elements). For a loxodromic el-
ement g in an acylindrically hyperbolic group Γ, the elementary closure is
EΓ(g) = {h ∈Γ : |⟨g⟩: ⟨g⟩∩h⟨g⟩h−1| < ∞}.
In a torsion-free group, EΓ(g) = CΓ(g), which is always infinite cyclic. An element
g ∈Γ is called primitive if EΓ(g) = ⟨g⟩, i.e., g has no proper roots in Γ.
Consider a group Γ acting on a hyperbolic space S. An element g ∈Γ of infinite
order is defined as loxodromic if it possesses exactly two fixed points, x+
g and x−
g , on
the Gromov boundary ∂S, with the property that gnx →x+
g for all x ∈∂S \ {x−
g }.
Bestvina and Fujiwara [BF02] introduced the condition of “weak proper disconti-
nuity” (WPD). An element g ∈Γ is called a WPD element if, for any x ∈S and
ϵ > 0, there exists an integer M ∈N such that only a finite number of elements
h ∈Γ satisfy both d(x, hx) < ϵ and d(gMx, hgMx) < ϵ. The connection between
these concepts and acylindrically hyperbolic groups was later formalized by Osin
[Osi16, Theorem 1.2]. He proved that Γ is acylindrically hyperbolic if and only if it
contains a loxodromic WPD element g ∈Γ. Furthermore, whenever such a g exists,
EΓ(g) = StabΓ({x+
g , x−
g }) (see, for example, [DGO17, Lemma 6.5]).

2.4. Furstenberg boundary and invariant amenable subalgebras. In this
subsection, we establish that invariant amenable subalgebras within the appropriate
C∗-simple geometric settings must be trivial. This automatically guarantees that
any N-invariant subalgebra possesses a trivial center. To achieve this, we make use
of the Furstenberg boundary.
The Furstenberg boundary, denoted by ∂FΓ, is the universal boundary associated
with the group Γ, in the sense that any other Γ-boundary Y can be obtained as a Γ-
equivariant continuous image of it. The existence of this space is typically established

The dynamics of the Γ-action on ∂FΓ encode deep structural information about
the group itself. For instance, Kalantar and Kennedy [KK17] utilized this boundary
action to provide a purely dynamical characterization of C∗-simplicity. Furthermore,
a classical result states that Γ is an amenable group if and only if its Furstenberg
boundary consists of a single point (see, e.g., [Gla76, Theorem 3.1, Chapter 3]).
Generalizing this, Furman [Fur03, Proposition 7] proved that the amenable radical,
Rad(Γ), coincides precisely with the kernel of the boundary action Γ ↷∂FΓ (also
see [BKKO17, Proposition 2.8]). This specific characterization serves as a primary
tool for our subsequent arguments.
Finally, we note that the induced affine action Γ ↷Prob(∂FΓ) is irreducible.
This means there are no non-trivial, weak*-closed, Γ-invariant convex subsets within
Prob(∂FΓ).

2.5. Higher-Rank Lattices and Characters. In Section 6, our focus shifts to
irreducible lattices in higher-rank semisimple Lie groups, for example, Γ = SLd(Z)
with d ≥3.
We will utilize the following fundamental theorem regarding their
normal subgroup structure.
Theorem 2.10 (Margulis Normal Subgroup Theorem). Let Γ be an irreducible
lattice in a higher-rank semisimple Lie group with trivial center and no compact
factors (such as SLd(Z) for d ≥3 odd). Then every normal subgroup of Γ is either
finite or of finite index in Γ. In particular, when Γ = SLd(Z) with d ≥3 odd, every
non-trivial normal subgroup has finite index, since Γ admits no non-trivial finite
normal subgroups.
Furthermore, we will rely on characters and their connections to operator alge-
bras in the higher-rank setup. A function ϕ : Γ →C is a character if it is normal-
ized (ϕ(e) = 1), positive-definite, and constant on conjugacy classes of Γ. Follow-

character decomposition property (CDP) if for any two characters ϕ, ψ: G →C –
that is, normalized (ϕ(e) = ψ(e) = 1), positive-definite functions constant on con-
jugacy classes of G – satisfying ϕ(s)ψ(s) = 0 for all e ̸= s ∈G, one has either
ϕ ≡δe or ψ ≡δe. Building on the operator-algebraic superrigidity of Bekka [Bek07],
the group SLd(Z) (d ≥3 odd) has the CDP [DJ24, Proposition 3.17].
More-

6. Relative ISR for Higher-Rank Lattices
In this section, we establish Theorem 1.4, proving the relative ISR-property for
irreducible lattices in higher-rank semisimple Lie groups. The setting completely
distances itself from acylindrical hyperbolicity and instead leverages unitary factor-
ization along with the Character Decomposition Property.
Our strategy is heavily influenced by the approach developed by Dudko and
Jiang [DJ24], where the Character Decomposition Property (CDP) – introduced
and systematically studied for the first time – plays a central role. The argument
proceeds in three steps.
First, a structural unitary factorization lemma (Lemma 6.1) decomposes unitaries
λ(g) with non-zero conditional expectation into M as a product of a unitary in M
and a unitary in its relative commutant M′ ∩L(Γ). Second, a direct computation
shows that the positive-definite functions ϕM and ϕM′ associated to M and its rel-
ative commutant satisfy ϕM(g)ϕM′(g) = 0 for all g ∈N \ {e}. Third, the CDP
inherited by N from Γ – via the Margulis Normal Subgroup Theorem and the in-
heritance result of [DJ24, Proposition 3.2(1)] – forces one of these functions to be
identically δe, from which the conclusion M = L(K) is extracted.
