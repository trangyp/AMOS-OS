ON TOPOLOGIES ON THE SPACE OF VALUATIONS
AND THE VALUATIVE TREE
V. MANFREDINI, J. NOVACOSKI AND C. H. SILVA DE SOUZA
Abstract. In this paper, we discuss topological aspects of the space of valua-
tions V and the valuative tree T (v, Λ) in the sense of [1]. We present a relation
between the weak tree topology and the Scott topology in T (v, Λ) and describe
the supremum of an increasing family of valuations in a special subtree. We
also view the valuative tree as a subset of the product (Λ∞)K[x] and prove
that it is closed if we consider the natural product topology.
1. Introduction
Topologies on spaces of valuations appear in many different contexts. The most
common is the Zariski topology. It was introduced by Zariski in the first half of
the twentieth century and it has been extensively studied since then. Initially, it
was defined as a topology on algebraic varieties, but in a modern language it is
defined as a topology on the spectrum of a ring R, i.e., the set of all prime ideals
of R. The space of Krull valuations on a ring (or valuations on a field) admits a
natural structure as inverse limit of spectra of rings with their respective Zariski
topologies. The corresponding topology is called again the Zariski topology on the
space of valuations.
The Zariski topology is constructed in the set of Krull valuations of a ring and
its definition cannot be extended directly to the set of all valuations on this ring.
To overcome this problem, Huber and Knebusch introduced the valuation spectrum
topology (see [4]).
The valuative tree structure also appears as an alternative in the study of sets of
valuations. It was developed by Favre and Jonsson in [3], where it is connected to
the theory of Berkovich spaces. In this original context, the valuative tree consisted
of all (normalized and centered) valuations in C[[x, y]], the ring of formal power
series in two variables over the field of complex numbers, and taking values in R.
Favre and Jonsson give a (rooted non-metric) tree structure to this space. This
tree structure induces different topologies on this set.

2. Preliminaries
Consider a commutative ring R with unity and a totally ordered abelian group
Γ. We denote by Γ∞the set Γ ∪{∞} where ∞is a symbol not belonging to Γ. We
extend the addition and the order to Γ∞by defining:
• γ + ∞= ∞+ γ = ∞for all γ ∈Γ∞.
• γ < ∞for all γ ∈Γ.
Definition 2.1. We say that a map v : R −→Γ∞is a valuation if it satisfies the
following conditions:
(V1): v(ab) = v(a) + v(b) for all a, b ∈R.
(V2): v(a + b) ≥min{v(a), v(b)} for all a, b ∈R.
(V3): v(1) = 0 and v(0) = ∞.
The subgroup of Γ generated by the set {v(a) | a ∈R and v(a) ̸= ∞} is called
the value group of v and is denoted by Γv. In the case where R is a field, we have
Γv = {v(a) | a ∈R and v(a) ̸= ∞}.

The set supp(v) := v−1(∞) is a prime ideal of R, called the support of v. If
supp(v) = (0), then we say that v is a Krull valuation (or that it has trivial
support). Otherwise, the valuation is said to be non-Krull (or that it has non-
trivial support).
If v is a Krull valuation, then R is a domain and we can extend v to K = Quot(R)
by additivity (axiom (V1)). In this case, define the valuation ring of v as
Ov := {a ∈K | v(a) ≥0}.
The ring Ov is a local ring with unique maximal ideal mv = {a ∈K | v(a) > 0}. We
define the residue field of v as the field Ov/mv and denote it by kv. The image
of a ∈Ov in kv is denoted by av.
Definition 2.2. We say that the valuations v and u on R are equivalent (denoted
v ∼u) if the following equivalent conditions are satisfied:
i) There exists an order-preserving isomorphism Φ : Γu −→Γv such that
v = Φ ◦u.
ii) For all a, b ∈R, v(a) > v(b) if and only if u(a) > u(b).
We denote the equivalence class of v by [v].
Let (K, v) be a valued field, i.e., K is a field and v is a fixed valuation on K.
Consider
V = V(v) = {ν : K[x] →(Γν)∞| ν is a valuation and ν|K ∼v},
the set of all valuations on K[x] whose restriction to K is equivalent to v. We have
an embedding Γv ,→Γν for every ν ∈V. For simplicity of notation, we will consider
this embedding to be an inclusion. Hence, V is the set of all valuations that extend
v to K[x].
Given µ ∈V with supp(µ) = (0), since Ov ⊆Oµ and mv = mµ ∩Ov, it follows
that kµ is a field extension of kv. We can classify µ as follows:
• Value-transcendental, if Γµ/Γv is not a torsion group.
• Residue-transcendental, if the extension kµ/kv is transcendental.
• Valuation-algebraic, if Γµ/Γv is a torsion group and kµ/kv is algebraic.
We say that µ ∈V is valuation-transcendental if it is either value-transcendental
or residue-transcendental.
Remark 2.3. By Abhyankar’s inequality (see [[12], p. 330]), a valuation cannot be
simultaneously value-transcendental and residue-transcendental. Therefore, Γµ/Γv
is a torsion group if µ is residue-transcendental.
Take the divisible hull ΓQ := Γv ⊗Z Q of Γv.

4.3. The Scott topology. Let (P, ≤) be a partially ordered set.
Definition 4.12. We say that a non-empty subset D of P is a directed set if

• Upper Set: For all x, y ∈P such that x ∈O and x ≤y, we have y ∈O
