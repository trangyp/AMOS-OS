ON SIMPLE COMPACT LIE SKEW BRACES
MARCO DAMELE AND ANDREA LOI
Abstract. We study simplicity for Lie skew braces from both the global and infinitesimal points
of view. After recalling the correspondence between connected Lie skew braces, simply transitive
affine actions, and post-Lie algebras, we investigate ideals and rigidity phenomena. Our main result
concerns compact connected Lie skew braces. We prove that a compact connected simple Lie skew
brace is either the trivial Lie skew brace on S1, or else both underlying Lie groups are simple and
the brace is trivial or almost trivial. In particular, outside the exceptional case of S1, simplicity of
a compact connected Lie skew brace is equivalent to simplicity of either underlying Lie group. We
also show that a connected compact solvable Lie skew brace is necessarily trivial. Finally, we exhibit
a noncompact example showing that this rigidity fails in general: there exists a connected simply
connected simple Lie skew brace whose additive and multiplicative Lie groups are both solvable.

1. Introduction
Skew braces, introduced by Guarnieri and Vendramin [15], form a natural algebraic framework for
the study of set-theoretic solutions of the Yang–Baxter equation. Over the last few years they have
also revealed deep connections with group theory, Hopf–Galois structures, and solvability phenomena;
see, for instance, [15, 20, 1, 7, 3]. In parallel, several Lie-theoretic structures related to Yang–Baxter
theory have proved to be equally fruitful, especially through their links with affine actions on Lie
groups and post-Lie algebra theory.
A smooth analogue of a skew brace is obtained by equipping a single smooth manifold with two
compatible Lie group structures. This leads to the notion of a Lie skew brace. As shown in [12],
connected Lie skew braces admit a geometric interpretation in terms of simply transitive affine actions
on Lie groups; see Theorem 2.4. On the infinitesimal side, they determine post-Lie algebra structures
in the sense of [2, 8]. Thus Lie skew braces provide a natural global framework in which one may study
post-Lie structures together with their integrability.

The present paper is concerned with simplicity for Lie skew braces.
More precisely, we study
connected Lie skew braces having no nontrivial proper connected closed ideals; see Definition 2.15. In
the finite theory, simplicity is known to exhibit striking and sometimes unexpected behaviour: simple
skew braces may have solvable, or even abelian, additive group, and need not arise from simple groups
[1, 5]. We return to this comparison in Remark 3.8. A guiding problem for this work is to determine
to what extent analogous phenomena persist in the Lie setting, and in particular how the answer
depends on compactness. Our results show that compactness is the decisive dividing line: the compact
connected case is highly rigid, whereas the noncompact case still admits unexpectedly flexible simple
examples; see Example 3.13.
Our first rigidity result deals with the case where the multiplicative Lie group is simple. Using a
theorem of Burde, Dekimpe, and Vercammen on post-Lie algebra structures [8], we prove in Theo-
rem 2.21 that if (G, ◦) is a simple Lie group, then every Lie skew brace (G, ·, ◦) is necessarily trivial
or almost trivial. Equivalently, the two Lie group laws either coincide or differ only by passage to the
opposite group law.
Our main result concerns the compact connected case; see Theorem 3.1.
Main Theorem. Let (G, ·, ◦) be a compact connected Lie skew brace, and assume that it is simple.
Then exactly one of the following holds:
(a) (G, ·) = (G, ◦) = S1;
(b) (G, ·) and (G, ◦) are simple Lie groups, and
◦= ·
or
◦= ·opp.
The proof of Theorem 3.1 is genuinely global and does not follow formally from the infinitesimal
theory alone. After passing to the simply connected case, we show that in the compact semisimple
setting the lambda-action is necessarily inner. This allows us to embed the multiplicative group into
a product group as a subgroup complementary to the diagonal. Passing to Lie algebras, we obtain a
decomposition to which a proposition of Ozeki [19] applies. This ultimately forces the additive group
to be simple. At that point, the rigidity result proved earlier implies that the brace is trivial or almost
trivial.
As an immediate consequence, outside the exceptional case of S1, simplicity of a compact connected
Lie skew brace is equivalent to simplicity of either of its underlying Lie groups; see Corollary 3.7. This
shows that the compact connected Lie setting is substantially more rigid than the finite one. We also
discuss solvability in this framework and prove that a connected compact solvable Lie skew brace is
necessarily trivial.
We conclude by showing that this rigidity is genuinely specific to the compact case. Indeed, in
Example 3.13 we construct an explicit connected simply connected simple Lie skew brace whose additive
and multiplicative Lie groups are both solvable. Thus, in sharp contrast with the compact connected
setting, simplicity of a Lie skew brace does not force simplicity of either underlying Lie group in the
noncompact case.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we develop the general theory of Lie skew braces,
affine actions, post-Lie algebras, ideals, and simplicity, and we prove the rigidity result for Lie skew
braces with simple multiplicative group. In Section 3 we establish the compact connected rigidity
theorem and its consequences, and we conclude with Example 3.13, which shows that such rigidity
fails outside the compact setting.

2.2. Affine actions, post-Lie algebras, and integrability. We now recall two complementary
points of view that will be used repeatedly throughout the paper. The first is the interpretation of Lie
skew braces in terms of simply transitive affine actions. The second is the infinitesimal description in
terms of post-Lie algebras and the corresponding integrability problem.
Affine actions.
Definition 2.2. Let (H, ◦) and (K, ·) be connected real Lie groups. An affine action of (H, ◦) on (K, ·)
is a Lie group homomorphism
ρ : (H, ◦) −→Aff(K, ·) = (K, ·) ⋊Aut(K, ·).

Conversely, every simply transitive affine action gives rise to a Lie skew brace structure on the
underlying manifold of the target Lie group. More precisely, the following correspondence between
connected Lie skew braces and simply transitive affine actions was proved in [12, Thm. 2.13].
Theorem 2.4. Let (K, ·) be a connected real Lie group. The following are equivalent:
(i) there exists a Lie group structure ◦on the manifold K such that (K, ·, ◦) is a Lie skew brace;
(ii) there exist a connected Lie group (H, ◦) and a simply transitive affine action
ρ : (H, ◦) −→Aff(K, ·).
Moreover, when these conditions hold, the Lie group (H, ◦) is isomorphic to the multiplicative group
of the corresponding Lie skew brace.
Remark 2.5. Theorem 2.4 shows that studying Lie skew brace structures on a fixed connected Lie
group (K, ·) is equivalent to studying connected Lie groups acting simply transitively on (K, ·) by affine
transformations. In particular, many algebraic questions on Lie skew braces may be reformulated as
geometric questions on affine actions.

2.4. The simple case: trivial and almost trivial Lie skew braces.
Definition 2.17. A connected Lie group G is called simple if it is nonabelian and has no nontrivial
proper connected closed normal Lie subgroups.
Remark 2.18. For connected Lie groups, this is equivalent to requiring that Lie(G) be a simple Lie
algebra. This notion should not be confused with simplicity in the sense of abstract group theory,
since a connected simple Lie group may still have nontrivial discrete normal subgroups.

skew brace structure is extremely rigid: the only possibilities are the trivial one and the opposite one.

proof is purely algebraic, it carries over verbatim to the present setting.
Proposition 3.11. If (G, ·, ◦) is a solvable Lie skew brace, then both underlying Lie groups (G, ·) and
(G, ◦) are solvable.
Corollary 3.12. Let (G, ·, ◦) be a connected compact Lie skew brace which is solvable. Then
(G, ·) ∼= (G, ◦) ∼= Tn
for some n ≥0. In particular, the Lie skew brace is trivial.
Proof. By Proposition 3.11, both underlying Lie groups (G, ·) and (G, ◦) are solvable. Since they are
also connected and compact, it is a standard fact that both are abelian. In particular, (G, ·) is abelian.
Therefore Lemma 3.4 applies. We conclude that (G, ·) ∼= Tn for some n ≥0, that the lambda-action
is trivial, and hence
a ◦b = a · b, for all a, b ∈G.
Thus the Lie skew brace is trivial. In particular, (G, ·) ∼= (G, ◦) ∼= Tn.
□
