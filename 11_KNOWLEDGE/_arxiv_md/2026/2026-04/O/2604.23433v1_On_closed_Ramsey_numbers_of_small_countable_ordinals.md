1. Introduction
As a natural extension of Ramsey’s theorem [Ram29] to Cantor’s transﬁnite
numbers, partition relations for ordinals and cardinals were introduced by Erd˝os
and Rado in [ER53] and [ER56], and have been thoroughly studied since then.
In [Bau86], Baumgartner extended this partition calculus to countable topological
spaces.
Following Baumgartner, Caicedo and Hilton initiated an investigation of topo-
logical and closed Ramsey numbers for pairs of countable ordinals in [CH17]. This
investigation was recently contributed by Mermelstein in [Mer19] and [Mer20], and
by Kaya and Sa˘glam in [KS21]. This paper is another contribution to this sys-
tematic investigation. In order to be able to state the existing results and explain
where the results of this paper ﬁt, let us recall some basic notation and deﬁnitions.
Let α, β be ordinals endowed with their respective order topologies and let X ⊆α
be a topological subspace. The subspace X is said to be order-homeomorphic to β
if there exists an order-isomorphism f : X →β which is also a homeomorphism.
In this case, we shall say that X is a closed copy of β.1
Given ordinals α, β, γ, we write γ →cl (α, β)2 in the case that for every function
c : [γ]2 →{0, 1} there exists a subspace X ⊆γ such that
• X is a closed copy of α and [X]2 ⊆c−1(0), or
• X is a closed copy of β and [X]2 ⊆c−1(1).
where [X]2 = {Y ⊆X : |Y | = 2} denotes the set of two element subsets of X.
For simplicity, we shall say that X is homogeneous of color red (respectively, blue)
whenever we have [X]2 ⊆c−1(0) (respectively, [X]2 ⊆c−1(1).) The closed Ramsey
number Rcl(α, β) is deﬁned to be the least ordinal γ such that γ →cl (α, β)2 if such
an ordinal exists at all.
In [CH17], among other interesting results, Caicedo and Hilton proved that
Rcl(ω · n + 1, k + 1) < ωω and Rcl(ω2, k) ≤ωω for all integers n, k ≥1.
In
[Mer19] and [Mer20], after introducing the notion of a canonical coloring, Mermel-
stein computed the closed Ramsey numbers Rcl(ω·2, 3) = ω3·2 and Rcl(ω2, 3) = ω6.
Using Mermelstein’s machinery on canonical colorings, in [KS21], Kaya and Sa˘glam
showed that ω2·n < Rcl(ω+n, 3) < ω2·(n+1), completely determining the leading
term of Rcl(ω +n, 3) in its Cantor normal form as ω2 ·n. The following summarizes
the existing state of the art for Rcl(α, 3) with ω < α ≤ω2.

Even though the proof of [CH17, Theorem 6.1] indirectly provides some upper
bounds for Rcl(ω · n + 1, 3), which require the computation of various Ramsey
numbers and closed pigeonhole numbers, these upper bounds seems to be already
beaten by the upper bound ω6. The main objective of this paper is to somewhat
ﬁll the gap between Rcl(ω · 2, 3) and Rcl(ω2, 3) and prove the following.
Theorem 1. ω4 < Rcl(ω ·2+1, 3) < ω5 and ω4 ·(n−2)+1 < Rcl(ω ·n+1, 3) < ω5
for every integer n ≥3.
Since we do not exactly pinpoint the leading term of Rcl(ω·n+1, 3) in its Cantor
normal form, there remains work to be done to determine the exact asymptotic
behavior of Rcl(α, 3) for ω ≤α ≤ω2.
Nevertheless, our result is still able to
illustrate the interesting phenomenon that Rcl(α, 3) jump from ω3 · 2 to above ω4
and jump from below ω5 to ω6 as α approaches ω2.
In regard to the closed Ramsey numbers Rcl(α, 3) where α ≥ω2, we do not carry
out an extensive analysis. On the other hand, as a byproduct of the approach that
we are using, we are able to prove the following negative closed partition relation
for powers of ω.
Theorem 2. Let 1 ≤α ≤θ < ω1. If θ < R(α, 3), then ωθ ↛cl (ωα, 3)2 where
R(·, ·) denotes the classical ordinal Ramsey number.
For ﬁnite powers of ω, Theorem 2 implies Rcl(ωn, 3) ≥ωR(n,3)−1 + 1 which
asymptotically strengthens the existing bound Rcl(ωn, 3) ≥ω5n−4 given by [Mer20]
because the ﬁnite Ramsey numbers R(n, 3) are known to have asymptotic order of
magnitude n2/ log(n) [Kim95].
We shall also provide a necessary condition for being a topological partition
ordinal. Recall that an ordinal α is said to be a partition ordinal (respectively,
a topological partition ordinal) in the case that α satisﬁes the partition relation
α →(α, 3)2 (respectively, α →top (α, 3)2.)2
In [CH17, Question 11.2], Caicedo and Hilton asked whether or not there exists
a countable topological partition ordinal greater than ω.
They noted that any
such ordinal has to be of the form ωωβ and that this question is equivalent to the
version of the question in which →top is replaced by →cl. An immediate corollary
of Theorem 2 is the following.
Corollary 1. Let 1 ≤α < ω1. If ωα is a topological partition ordinal, then α is a
partition ordinal.
Combining Corollary 1 with the necessary conditions for being a partition ordi-
nal given in [GL75] and [Sch10, Theorem 29.(3)], one obtains that any countable
topological partition ordinal greater than ω is of the form

2. Preliminaries
The main machinery that we shall use in our upper bound argument is the notion
of a canonical coloring introduced by [Mer19]. While we are not going to overview
the canonization process of an arbitrary coloring in this paper and simply take
the existence of canonical colorings as a blackbox, we still need to introduce some
preliminary notions and notation in order to be able to state the deﬁnition. In this
section, we shall brieﬂy recall these prerequisites and the main machinery, together
with a directed graph interpretation.

3. The upper bound
In this section, we shall prove that Rcl(ω · n + 1, 3) < ω5 for any integer n ≥2
by showing that the closed partition relation
γ = ω4 · M(n) + 1 →cl (ω · n + 1, 3)2
holds for a suitable choice of M(n). In order to be able to deﬁne M(n), we shall
