ON THE QUASI-MONOMIALITY OF THE α- AND δ-INVARIANTS
DONGHYEON KIM AND DAE-WON LEE
Abstract. In this paper, we show that for any projective klt pair (X, ∆) over an
algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 and any big Q-Cartier Q-divisor L on X,
the invariants α(X, ∆, L) and δ(X, ∆, L) are computed by quasi-monomial valuations,
without any uncountability assumption on the base field.
1. Introduction
The α-invariant was introduced by Tian (see [15]) in his study of the existence of
K¨ahler–Einstein metrics on Fano varieties. The δ-invariant, introduced by Fujita and
Odaka (see [9]), later became a central algebro-geometric invariant in the study of K-
stability. Together with the valuative criterion for K-stability [1, 13], these invariants
provide a useful bridge between birational geometry and existence problems for canonical
metrics.
For a projective klt pair (X, ∆) and an ample Q-Cartier Q-divisor L on X, one can
define the δ-invariant δ(X, ∆, L), which is related to Ding stability (cf. [4, Theorem 3.16]).
More generally, one can define the stability thresholds α(X, ∆, L) and δ(X, ∆, L) for a big
Q-Cartier Q-divisor L on X. These invariants admit valuative descriptions in terms of the
log discrepancy AX,∆(ν) and the asymptotic invariants S(ν, L) and T(ν, L) for ν ∈Val∗
X.
We recall these definitions in Subsection 2.3. It is therefore natural to ask whether the
corresponding valuative infima are attained.
The main result of this paper gives a positive answer over arbitrary algebraically closed
fields of characteristic 0.
Theorem 1.1. Let (X, ∆) be a projective klt pair over an algebraically closed field k of
characteristic 0, and let L be a big Q-Cartier Q-divisor on X. Then there exist quasi-
monomial valuations computing α(X, ∆, L) and δ(X, ∆, L).
We recall the previously known cases. When the base field is uncountable and L is ample,
the result follows from [1, Theorem E] together with [16, Theorem 1.1]. The corresponding
result for big divisors over an uncountable base field was obtained in [14]. Over countable
base fields, the result was known when L = −(KX + ∆) is ample under the additional

2. Preliminaries
In this paper, all schemes are assumed to be separated, and every field is assumed to have
characteristic 0. A variety means an integral scheme of finite type over an algebraically
closed field. We collect the notation used throughout the paper. For further details on the
notation, we refer the reader to [8, 11, 12].
• Let X be a normal scheme. For an effective Q-Weil divisor ∆on X, we say that
(X, ∆) is a couple. If KX + ∆is Q-Cartier, we say that (X, ∆) is a pair.
