On Fixed Points of Nonlinear Monotone and Strongly
Concave Operators Acting in Normal Cones
Khachatur A. Khachatryan
Abstract: We introduce and study a new class of nonlinear monotone
operators acting in normal cones of real Banach spaces and possessing the
property of strong concavity. We establish new constructive principles for
the existence of nonzero ﬁxed points for this class of operators. Further, we
prove that the corresponding iterative process converges to the ﬁxed point at
geometric rate. We also establish the uniqueness of the ﬁxed point in a suﬃ-
ciently wide conical segment. These results are applied to Hammerstein-type
and Urysohn-type nonlinear integral operators acting in non-reﬂexive Banach
spaces, as well as to the Cauchy problem for a nonlinear heat equation.
MSC: 47H10
Keywords: ﬁxed point, normal cone, monotonicity, strong concavity,
iterations.

Introduction
1.1
Basic notions and problem history
First, we present the necessary deﬁnitions and known facts from the theory
of nonlinear monotone operators acting in cones of real Banach spaces (see
[1]-[4]).
Let E be a real Banach space partially ordered by a cone K. The cone
K is called regular if every monotonically nondecreasing sequence that is
bounded from above converges in E.
The cone K is called minihedral if any pair of elements of E has a supre-
mum. We shall call the cone K strongly minihedral if, for every set M ⊂E
bounded from above, the least upper bound sup M exists.

The cone K is called normal if there exists a number δ > 0 such that
||e1 + e2|| ≥δ
for all e1, e2 ∈K with ||e1|| = ||e2|| = 1.
It is easy to verify that every regular cone is normal. Generally speaking,
the converse statement is not true. The cone of nonnegative functions in
the space of continuous functions C[a, b] is an example of a normal but not
regular cone.
It is well-known that cones of nonnegative functions in the spaces C and
Lp are normal.
However, the cone of nonnegative functions in the space
C1[a, b] of continuously diﬀerentiable functions on the interval [a, b] x(t),
endowed with the norm
∥x∥= max
t∈[a,b] |x(t)| + max

does not possess the property of normality.
We say that a norm in the space E with cone K is semi-monotone if there
exists a number N > 0 such that, for any elements x, y ∈K from x ≤y, we
have ∥x∥≤N∥y∥.
We say that the norm is monotone if from the inequalities θ ≤x ≤y (θ
is the zero element of the space E) it follows that ∥x∥≤∥y∥.
It is easy to verify that in spaces C and Lp the norm is monotone with
respect to the semi-ordering induced by the cone of nonnegative functions.
It is well-known that semi-monotonicity of the norm is equivalent to the
normality of the cone (see [1]). Let u0, v0 ∈E and u0 ≤v0. The set ⟨u0, v0⟩
of elements x ∈E satisfying inequalities u0 ≤x ≤v0 is called a conical
segment. We say that an operator A: E →E is monotone if from x ≤y it
follows that Ax ≤Ay.
We say that the operator A maps the conical segment ⟨u0, v0⟩into itself
(or leaves it invariant) if Au0 ≥u0 and Av0 ≤v0.
We say that the operator A is critical if Aθ = θ (see [2]).
The following results have been established (see [1], [3]) for operators that
preserve the invariance of the conical segment ⟨u0, v0⟩.
Theorem A (see [1]). Let A be a monotone and continuous operator
on the conical segment ⟨u0, v0⟩. If the operator A maps the conical segment
⟨u0, v0⟩into itself, and the cone K is regular, then the operator A has at least
one ﬁxed point x∗.

1.2
Summary of obtained results.
Nonlinear monotone operators possessing the property of criticality arise in
various areas of mathematical physics and mathematical biology (see [5]-[8]
and the references therein). In most cases, for such operators, it is necessary
to construct a second (nontrivial) ﬁxed point in the cone K. In various applied
problems it is also of interest to establish the uniqueness of this second ﬁxed
point in a certain conical segment.
We introduce the notion of strong concavity of monotone operators as
follows.
We say that a monotone operator A is strongly concave in the
subset K1 ⊆K of the cone K if there exists a continuous and monotonically
increasing concave mapping ϕ: [0, 1] →[0, 1] satisfying properties ϕ(0) = 0,
ϕ(1) = 1, ϕ′(+0) = +∞, such that
A(σu) ≥ϕ(σ) Au,
u ∈K1 ⊆K, σ ∈[0, 1].
(3)
We investigate the existence and uniqueness of nontrivial ﬁxed points
for strongly concave operators. Under certain natural conditions on strongly

concave operators, we prove constructive existence theorems for nonzero ﬁxed
points in normal cones. Moreover, we establish that the corresponding iter-

geometric progression. The geometric progression itself is determined by the
concave mapping ϕ.
Further, we prove the uniqueness of the constructed ﬁxed point in a suf-
ﬁciently wide conical segment.
We emphasize that the novelty of our assumptions and results are:
I) we require strong concavity of the operator under consideration;
II) in some cases, we do not assume that the operator is continuous;
III) we ﬁnd the solution of the nonlinear operator equation Ax = x in
normal cones, in a constructive way;
IV) we obtain the rate of convergence of the corresponding iterative process;
V) we obtain a uniqueness theorem for the constructed ﬁxed point in a
suﬃciently wide conical segment, without requiring the normality of
the cone or the continuity of the corresponding operator.
We conclude our paper as follows. Section 2 is devoted to existence the-
orems for ﬁxed points for strongly concave monotone operators. In section 3
we provide some consequences and generalizations of those theorems. Sec-
tion 4 we discuss uniqueness of ﬁxed points or its absence. Finally, the last
section 5 the obtained results we apply to Hammerstein-type and Urysohn-
type nonlinear integral equations, as well as to the Cauchy problem for the
nonlinear heat equation.
