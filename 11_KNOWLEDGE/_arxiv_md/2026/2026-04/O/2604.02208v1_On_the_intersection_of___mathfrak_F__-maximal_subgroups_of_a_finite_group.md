Abstract: We investigate the properties of the intersection IntF(G) of all
F-maximal subgroups of a finite group G for a hereditary formation F of finite
groups. We prove that IntF(G/IntF(G)) ≃1 holds for any finite group G if and
only if F contains every group G all of whose F-subgroups are F-subnormal. As
corollaries we obtain the results of A. N. Skiba (2011), J. C. Beidleman and
H. Heineken (2011) about IntF(G) for a hereditary saturated formation F.
Keywords: finite group, formation, F-subormal subgroup, weak F-
subnormalizer, intersection of F-maximal subgroups, F-hypercenter.
Introduction and the main results
All groups considered here are finite. The concept of the hypercenter as
the final term of the upper central series plays an important role in the modern
group theory. The development of this concept within the framework of the theory
of formations was carried out in the works of R. Baer [3] for the class of all
supersolvable groups, B. Huppert [14] for local formations, L. A. Shemetkov for
graduated formations [25] and appeared in its final form in the monograph of
L. A. Shemetkov and A. N. Skiba [27]. Let F be a formation. Recall (see [11, p. 6]
or [27]) that a chief factor H/K of a group G is called F-central provided that the
semidirect product of H/K with G/CG(H/K) belongs to F, where G/CG(H/K)
acts on H/K by conjugation. The largest normal subgroup of G such that all
chief factors of G below it are F-central in G is called the F-hypercenter (see [11]
or [27, p.142]) and is denoted by ZF(G).
Before the mentioned works R. Baer in 1966 [4] proposed a different
approach to define the e-hypercenter for a not necessary finite group and a
group-theoretical property e. The e-hypercenter heG of G is the set of elements
g of G such that ⟨g, E⟩is a subgroup of some e-subgroup of G for any e-
subgroup E of G [4, §4]. From [2] it follows that if e is nilpotency, then heG
is the hypercenter G. Note that if e is the property of a group to belong to a
class F, then heG is the intersection IntF(G) of all F-maximal subgroups of G.
Various properties of this subgroup were studied by A. N. Skiba [28,29], W. Guo

2 The intersection of weak F-subnormalizers
R. W. Carter [8] and C. J. Graddon [10] studied subnormalizers and F-
subnormalizers respectively. A subnormalizer or an F-subnormalizer may not exist
in a group for a given subgroup. A. Mann [17] proposed the concept of a weak
subnormalizer, which always exists, but may not be unique. In [23] the concept
of a weak K-F-subnormalizer was proposed. By analogy we propose the following
definition:
Definition 1. We shall call a subgroup T of G a weak F-subnormalizer of
H in G if H F-sn T and if H F-sn M ≤G and T ≤M, then T = M.
Let F be a hereditary formation and H ≤G. From the definitions of F-
subnormal subgroup and weak F-subnormalizer it follows that H F-sn G if and
only if G coincides with the weak F-subnormalizer of H in G.

If T is a weak F-subnormalizer of H in G, then T α is a weak F-subnormalizer of
Hα in G for any α ∈Aut(G).
