Abstract
We study equalizers and fixed points of monomorphisms of free groups at infinity. We show
that the action of the equalizer of two monomorphisms on the regular points of the equalizer at
infinity has finitely many orbits, showing that the equalizer at infinity is, in some sense, finitely
generated and generalizing a previous result of Cooper about fixed points. We additionally show
that it is decidable whether an automorphism of a free group has a nontrivial fixed point at
infinity. The same result is shown for monomorphisms satisfying the condition of being almost
length-increasing. We remark that almost length-increasing monomorphisms are generic among
endomorphisms of a free group. We also prove that being almost length-increasing is a decidable
condition. We end the paper with several open problems arising from this work.
1
Introduction
The study of fixed subgroups of endomorphisms of groups started with the (independent) work of
Gersten [16] and Cooper [10], proving that the subgroup of fixed points Fix(ϕ) = {x ∈Fn | xϕ = x}
of any automorphism ϕ of a free group Fn is finitely generated. Scott conjectured that the rank of
such a fixed subgroup could only be at most n and this was successfully proved by Bestvina and
Handel in 1992 [6]. Imrich and Turner extended these results for general endomorphisms of the free
group, by reducing it to the automorphism case in [19]. The problem of computing a basis for Fix(ϕ)
was settled by Bogopolski and Maslakova in 2016 in [7] for automorphisms and by Mutanguha [22]
for general endomorphisms of a free group. Hence, fixed subgroups of endomorphisms of free groups

are now (in some sense) well understood. We refer the reader to [30] for a survey on fixed subgroups
in free groups. Results about fixed subgroups of different classes of groups have since then been
obtained by many (see, for example [24, 26, 21]).
However, equalizers are not so well understood. In [29], Stallings conjectured that, given two
finitely generated free group homomorphisms, ϕ, ψ : Fn →Fm, with injective ϕ, the equalizer
Eq(ϕ, ψ) = {x ∈Fn | xϕ = xψ} would be finitely generated and its rank bounded by n (see also
[11, Problem 6], [30, Conjecture 8.3], [2, Problem F31]). It was proved in [18] that the equalizer was
always finitely generated, but, so far, the conjecture remains open as there is no bound on its rank.
For the free group of rank 2, the problem was solved by Logan in [20]. It is clear that equalizers
generalize fixed subgroups as Fix(ϕ) = Eq(ϕ, id) and that in case one of the homomorphisms, say
ψ, is bijective, the notions coincide because Eq(ϕ, ψ) = Fix(ϕψ−1).
Post Correspondence Problem (PCP) is a classical problem in computer science and it can
be stated as the problem of deciding, given as input two homomorphisms between free monoids,
ϕ, ψ : X∗→Y ∗, whether the equalizer Eq(ϕ, ψ) = {x ∈X∗| xϕ = xψ} is trivial or not. Its
undecidability was proved by Post in 1946 [25].
Naturally, one can think of the analogous question for groups. Let A, B be alphabets. The
Post Correspondence Problem for free groups is the problem of deciding, given homomorphisms
ϕ, ψ : FA →FB, whether Eq(ϕ, ψ) is trivial or not. While there are results for other classes of
groups [23, 8], the problem for free groups is an important open question (see [12, Problem 5.1.4]
and [23, Section 1.4]). We refer the reader to [9] for a survey on the PCP and similar questions for
free groups.
We will focus on the case of nonabelian free groups. Composing with an injective homomor-
phism θ : Fm →Fn on the right, we have that Eq(ϕ, ψ) is nontrivial if and only if Eq(ϕθ, ψθ) is
nontrivial. So, we will assume that both ranks are the same and talk about endomorphisms instead
of homomorphisms. We know that triviality (and even computability) of the fixed subgroup is
decidable [22] and it is easy to see that if ϕ and ψ are both noninjective, then Ker(ϕ) ∩Ker(ψ)
is always nontrivial (if u ∈Ker(ϕ) and v ∈Ker(ψ) are nontrivial, take a common power if they
commute and their commutator if they do not), so the PCP is trivially decidable in this case.
However, the case where one homomorphism is injective and the other one is not bijective remains
unknown.
For monoids, the infinite variation of the PCP asks whether there is a right-infinite word where
the two morphisms coincide and it is shown to be undecidable [13] and “not more complex than
the PCP” [15]. We will study equalizers and fixed points of endomorphisms of the free group at
infinity.

When considering the continuous extension bϕ of an injective endomorphism ϕ ∈End(Fn) to the
completion bFn, all elements in the topological closure Fix(ϕ)c of Fix(ϕ) are fixed by bϕ. But these
might not be the only ones. We will say that elements belonging to Fix(ϕ)c are singular (infinite)
fixed points and elements in Fix(bϕ) \ Fix(ϕ)c are regular (infinite) fixed points. In [10], Cooper
proved that the fixed subgroup of a free group automorphism ϕ is finitely generated and that, when
considering the extension bϕ of ϕ to the Gromov boundary, then the natural action of Fix(ϕ) on the
regular infinite fixed points of bϕ has finitely many orbits, showing that Fix(bϕ) is, in some sense,
finitely generated.
We show that Cooper’s techniques for fixed subgroups of automorphisms can be adapted to the
setting of equalizers of injective endomorphisms of the free group, by proving that the action of
Eq(ϕ, ψ) on the regular points of Eq(bϕ, bψ) has finitely many orbits, showing that, in some sense,
the equalizer at infinity is also finitely generated. We remark that, in general, equalizers are much
harder to understand than fixed subgroups.
Theorem 2.3. Let ϕ, ψ be injective endomorphisms of Fn. Then the action of Eq(ϕ, ψ) on the
regular points of Eq(bϕ, bψ) has finitely many orbits.
As a corollary, we have that the PCP answers yes on input (ϕ, ψ) if and only if Eq(bϕ, bψ) is finite
and we pose the infinite version of Stallings conjecture, which asks for a bound on the number of
orbits of the action.

We will then turn our attention to almost length-increasing monomorphisms, that is, monomor-

almost length-increasing endomorphisms must be injective (if not, there are infinitely many elements
in the kernel whose length reduces when applying the endomorphism), thus showing that length-
increasing (and so, almost length-increasing) monomorphisms are generic among endomorphisms
of the free group.
Under the assumption that our endomorphism is almost length-increasing, we not only show
that triviality of Fix(bφ) is decidable, but Fix(bφ) can, in some sense, be computed.
Theorem 3.12. Let φ be an almost length-increasing monomorphism of Fn with Fix(φ) = {1}.
Then Fix(bφ) is computable.
We also show that, given an endomorphism of Fn, it is decidable whether it is an almost length-
increasing monomorphism or not.
We end the paper with some future directions and possible
attacks on the case of general monomorphisms.

Equalizers at infinity
Let ϕ, ψ ∈Mon(Fn) be two injective endomorphisms of a nonabelian free group and bϕ, bψ be the
corresponding continuous extensions to the completion c
Fn. It is easy to see that Eq(ϕ, ψ) acts on
Eq(bϕ, bψ) by multiplication on the left. Elements that belong to the topological closure of Eq(ϕ, ψ)
are called singular, and those belonging to Eq(bϕ, bψ) \ (Eq(ϕ, ψ))c are called regular. Also, it is easy
to see that the orbit of a singular point contains only singular points (and so, the orbit of a regular
point contains only regular points). Notice that the action of Eq(ϕ, ψ) on the singular elements of
Eq(bϕ, bψ) can have infinitely many orbits: for example, if ϕ = ψ = id, then Eq(bϕ, bψ) is uncountable.
The main goal of this section is to show that the action of Eq(ϕ, ψ) on the regular points of
Eq(bϕ, bψ) has finitely many orbits, showing that, in some sense, the equalizer at infinity is finitely
generated. Notice that Eq(ϕ, ψ) is known to be finitely generated.

be the bounded reduction property. An endomorphism of the free group has the bounded reduction
property (BRP) if there is a constant M > 0 such that, whenever u, v ∈Fn are such that the
product uv is reduced, then |(uv)ϕ| ≥|uϕ| + |vϕ| −2M. In this case, M is said to be a bounded

3.2
Almost length-increasing monomorphisms
Let Rn denote the set of all reduced words on the alphabet eA. We now focus on proving the result
for almost length-increasing monomorphisms. An endomorphism φ of Fn is said to be:
• length-increasing if |uφ| ≥|u| for each u ∈Fn;
• strictly length-increasing if |uφ| > |u| for each u ∈Fn \ {1};
• almost length-increasing if |uφ| < |u| for only finitely many u ∈Fn;
• almost strictly length-increasing if |uφ| ≤|u| for only finitely many u ∈Fn.
It is clear that strictly length-increasing implies both length-increasing and almost strictly
length-increasing, and any of the latter two properties implies almost length-increasing. It is also
easy to see that almost length-increasing endomorphisms are injective. Indeed, if ϕ is noninjective,
the kernel must be infinite, and for every nontrivial element x in the kernel, we have that |xϕ| < |x|.
For showing that no other implication holds in general, it suffices to show that:
• there exists some length-increasing endomorphism of Fn which is not almost strictly length-
increasing;
• there exists some almost strictly length-increasing endomorphism of Fn which is not length-
increasing.
In fact, the identity homomorphism is length-increasing but not almost strictly length-increasing.
The following example serves as a counterexample for the second claim:
Example 3.5. Let φ be the endomorphism of F2 defined by aφ = a2 and bφ = a2b2a−2. Then φ
is an almost strictly length-increasing monomorphism of F2 which is not length-increasing.
Indeed, let ψ be the inner automorphism of F2 defined by uψ = a−2ua2 and let θ = φψ. Since
aθ = a2 and bθ = b2, then θ is a monomorphism and so must be φ. Moreover, |uθ| = 2|u| for every

Future work
Together with Conjecture 2.5, the following are the main questions arising from this work:
• The main open problem is the generalization of Theorem 3.4 to all injective endomorphisms.
