ON THE UNIVERSALITY OF MULTIEXCISIVE FUNCTORS
TOBIAS BARTHEL, KAIF HILMAN, AND NIKOLAY KONOVALOV
Abstract. We provide a multiplicative classification of polynomial endofunc-
tors on spectra in terms of their Mackey functors of cross–effects. More pre-
cisely, we prove that various categories of multivariable excisive functors from
spectra to spectra are symmetric monoidally equivalent to the corresponding
variants of spectral Mackey functors. The symmetric monoidal structures ap-
pearing here are the Day convolutions on both sides, and the Mackey functors
we consider involve variations on the category of finite sets and surjections.
The method is first to introduce certain multivariable functors we call sub-
diagonal functors. By considering them all at once using parametrised category
theory, we prove inductively that they all admit Mackey functor descriptions
as symmetric monoidal categories, endowing them with a universal property
along the way. In particular, specialising this to univariate functors gives a new
proof and strengthening of Glasman’s result about d–excisive endofunctors on
spectra. As application of our perspective, we prove a “Segal conjecture” in
the context of Goodwillie calculus when d is a prime number.

1. Introduction
The study of “polynomial” functors has a long and established history in alge-
bra and topology. In the derived and homotopical setting, polynomiality is often
phrased in terms of Goodwillie’s far-reaching and fundamental theory of calculus
via his notion of excisive functors, see [Goo90; Goo92; Goo03]. As such, the cate-
gory Excd of reduced d–excisive endofunctors on spectra is of central importance in
Goodwillie calculus. In a series of deep work [AC11; AC15; AC16], Arone–Ching
developed a presentation of this category using Goodwillie’s derivatives.
In a different direction, it has been observed that functor calculus on spectra
exhibits many features reminiscent of equivariant stable homotopy theory. For ex-
ample, the excisive approximation fracture squares of Kuhn looks like the standard
Tate fracture squares in the equivariant realm. Prompted by this, Arone–Ching
and McCarthy have shown that there is in fact an equivalence between the cat-
egory of reduced (d = 2)–excisive endofunctors on spectra with the category of
genuine C2–spectra, induced by taking cross–effects as opposed to derivatives.
This equivalence does not generalise to higher degrees d > 2, due to a bifurcation:
While G–spectra can be described as Mackey functors on the G–orbit category
([GM24; Bar17; Nar16; CMN+24]), Excd is controlled by the category Epid of
finite sets up to size d and surjections. For d = 2, we see that Epid is isomorphic to
the orbit category for C2, explaining the aforementioned result; in contrast, Epid is
not realised as the orbit category of a finite group for d > 2.
The main purpose of this paper is to systematically and completely develop a
multiplicative Mackey classification of excisive functors in terms of cross–effects:
Main Theorem. The category Excd can be canonically enhanced to an Epid–
parametrised symmetric monoidal stable category Exc⌟

It is worth mentioning that, unlike the key case of p–groups in the original
equivariant setting where the augmentation completion may be ignored after p–
completing both sides, here it plays a crucial role in conspiring for the correct
combinatorics to happen, and the result is false without it. For this reason, we think
that the role of the augmentation ideal in calculus deserves further investigation.
We prove this by reducing it to the equivariant Segal conjecture using a strat-

derivatives “commute” with the I(d)–completion under connectivity assumptions.
This uses a connectivity argument by transferring the standard t–structure on
Mack(Epid; Sp) to a “sensible” one on Excd afforded to us by Theorem C, which is
furthermore compatible with the various stratifications, as well as a “cellular model”
for the excisive approximations. It would be interesting to investigate other values
of d for which a semblance of the theorem above can be arranged to hold.
1.1. Organisation. This article is divided into two parts. We first develop all the
Goodwillie calculus that we need in Part 1 which we then package using the lan-
guage of parametrised categories in Part 2. We work out various properties of the
cross–effects in §2: we collect known facts about the cross–effects in §2.1. We then
introduce the subdiagonal functors and prove the key Theorem A in §2.2; we then
work out the interaction between the cross–effects and restrictions along the diago-
nal on the subdiagonal functors in §2.3. Next, we develop the categorical structures
and properties of these subdiagonal functors in §3. We show that subdiagonality
is a smashing local subcategory in §3.1, thus endowing multiplicative structures on
these objects. In §3.2, we enumerate the generators of the subdiagonal categories
and describe the kernels of excisive approximations in terms of these generators.
We switch gears by working out various aspects of parametrised category theory
in §4 over arbitrary base categories T , which might be of independent interest.
After recalling the setup in §4.1, we introduce the universal spaces and their as-
sociated categorified “geometric fixed point functors” in §4.2. We then axiomatise
the relevant stratification arguments in a recognition principle for spectra in §4.3.
Finally, we combine all the foregoing elements in §5: we assemble the subdiagonal
functor categories into a parametrised category and prove Theorem B in §5.1. Com-
bining this with our knowledge of the generators and the recognition principle, we
prove the main Theorem C in §5.2. We also summarise here the comparisons with
equivariant homotopy theory in a dictionary Figure 1. We then deduce Theorem D
in §5.3 and prove Theorem E in §5.4.
