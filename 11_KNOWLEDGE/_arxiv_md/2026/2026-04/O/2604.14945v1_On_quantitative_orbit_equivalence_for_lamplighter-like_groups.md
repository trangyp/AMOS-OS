On quantitative orbit equivalence for lamplighter-like groups
Corentin Correia and Vincent Dumoncel
April 17, 2026
Abstract
We focus on halo products, a class of groups introduced by Genevois and Tessera,
and whose geometry mimics lamplighters. Famous examples are lampshufflers. Motivated
by their work on the classifications up to quasi-isometry of these groups, we initiate a
more quantitative study of their geometry. Indeed, it follows from the work of Delabie,
Koivisto, Le Maître and Tessera that quantitative orbit equivalence between amenable
groups is closely related to their large scale geometry, such a connection being justified by
the use, in their main results, of a well-known quasi-isometry invariant: the isoperimetric
profile.
Inspired by their work on quantitative orbit equivalence between lamplighters, we prove
a stability result for orbit equivalence of permutational halo products, going beyond the
framework of standard halo products, using a new notion of orbit equivalence of pairs.
Combined with our asymptotics of isoperimetric profiles obtained in an earlier article,
we prove that most of these constructions are quantitatively optimal. For instance, we
show that ShufflerpZk`ℓq and ShufflerpZkq are Lp orbit equivalent if and only if p ă

thus quantifying how much the geometries of these non-quasi-isometric groups differ. We
finally build orbit equivalence couplings using the notion of Følner tiling sequences.
Keywords: Halo products, quantitative orbit equivalence, isoperimetric profile.

The motivation of [GT24a] to introduce such a general framework is that the semi-direct

Theorem 1.2 ([GT24a, Corollary 8.9]). Let H and K be finitely presented one-ended groups.
Then ShufflerpHq and ShufflerpKq are quasi-isometric if and only if H and K are biLipschitz
equivalent.
The main tool used in [GT24a] to establish such results is a new quasi-isometry invariant
referred to as the thick bigon property, also introduced in [GT24a]. Finitely presented one-
ended groups are particular examples of groups having this property [GT24a, Lemma 3.4].
There are also quasi-isometric classification results in some classes where the thick bigon
property is unavailable.

Quantitative orbit equivalence.
Measure equivalence has been introduced by Gro-
mov [Gro93] as a measured analogue of quasi-isometry, and in fact, it follows from the work
of Shalom [Sha04] that quasi-isometric amenable groups are L8 measure equivalent, namely
measure equivalent with some strong quantitative assumption on functions called the cocycles.
In the paper, we will not give the definitions of quantitative measure equivalence since we
focus on a particular instance, called orbit equivalence, and the historical results will also be
stated only in terms of this particular notion.

In contrast to Ornstein-Weiss theorem which states that any countable infinite amenable
groups are orbit equivalent, a natural question is the preservation of geometric properties
of groups under quantitative forms of orbit or measure equivalence. Historically, the most
natural quantitative forms of orbit equivalence (and even measure equivalence) are the L8
and L1 cases. Many rigidity results have been uncovered in this context, showing that L1 and
L8 orbit equivalence (as well as L1 and L8 measure equivalence) captures the geometry of
groups. Here is the famous rigidity result due to Shalom (see also [BFS13] and [Aus16] for
some results in the L1 case).
Theorem 1.7 ([Sha04]). Two amenable groups are bi-Lipschitz equivalent if and only if they
are L8 orbit equivalent.

result of Delabie, Koivisto, Le Maître and Tessera, providing explicit upper bounds on how
integrable the cocycles of an orbit equivalence coupling can be.

Constructions of orbit equivalence couplings.
Coming back to halo products, Dela-
bie, Koivisto, Le Maître and Tessera have already established quantitative orbit equivalence
couplings between wreath products [DKLMT22]. They used two techniques:
(A) the first one consists in finding stability results, for instance if H and K are pφ, ψq-
integrably orbit equivalent, then so are Γ ≀H and Γ ≀K for every group Γ;
(B) the second explicit construction of coupling use the notion of Følner tiling sequences,
that yields natural group actions generalizing Z-odometers.

The goal is to extend techniques (A) and (B) to other examples of halo products, in each
specific case: lampshufflers (more generally lampjugglers) and lampcloners. Moreover, regard-
ing technique (A), we go in fact even further and investigate the case of permutational halo
products. For instance, for permutational wreath products, the strategy is to derive an orbit
equivalence coupling between Γ ≀H{M H and Γ ≀K{N K, provided a coupling between H and K
that takes into account the subgroups M and N. This new notion, introduced in a common
work with Romain Tessera [CDT26], is called orbit equivalence of pairs.
Definition 1.9 ([CDT26]). Let H and K be countable groups, with subgroups M ď H,
N ď K. We say that the pairs pH, Mq and pK, Nq are orbit equivalent if there exist essentially
free pmp H- and K-actions on a standard and atomless probability space pX, µq, satisfying
H ¨ x “ K ¨ x and M ¨ x “ N ¨ x for almost every x P X. The space pX, µq is called an orbit
equivalence coupling between the pairs pH, Mq and pK, Nq.
An orbit equivalence coupling pX, µq between two pairs pH, Mq and pK, Nq is pφ, ψq-
integrable if it is pφ, ψq-integrable as a coupling between the ambient groups H and K. In
the paper, we will always be interested in normal pairs, namely the subgroups M and N are
normal in H and K respectively.
In a more quantitative manner, a pφ, ψq-orbit equivalence between Γ≀H{M H and Γ≀K{N K
will be given by a pφ, ψq-integrable coupling of pairs between the base groups H and K.

Comments and questions
In this final section, we record some questions that naturally arise from what has been done
in the article.
One of our main results is the construction of an explicit orbit equivalence coupling be-
tween the lampjugglers ShufflerrpHq and ShufflerrpKq, provided a coupling between H and K.
Additionally, our construction preserves quantification. Hence:
Question 5.1. Let r, s ě 1, r ‰ s. How can one construct an orbit equivalence coupling
between ShufflerspHq and ShufflerrpKq from a coupling between H and K?
As pointed out in Remark 1.12, there are reasons to think that a pφ, ψq-integrable orbit
equivalence between H and K does not provide the same for ShufflerspHq and ShufflerrpKq,
if r ‰ s. This kind of stability result could also gives stability of bi-Lipschitz equivalence,
which would contradicts the main results in [GT24a]. Replacing orbit equivalence by measure
equivalence would maybe provide a better framework when considering “similar”, but different,
halo products, for instance ShufflerspHq and ShufflerrpKq when r ‰ s, or ClonerfpHq and
ClonerlpKq when |f| ‰ |l|.
Beyond the quasi-isometric classification of lampshufflers established in [GT24a], an-
other important direction of the latter is to compare geometrically different classes of
halo products to rule out, or to establish, the existence of a quasi-isometry (resp.
quasi-
isometric/coarse/regular embedding) between, for instance, a lampshuffler and a lamplighter,
or a lampdesigner and a lamplighter. Analoguous questions are also relevant from the mea-
sured point of view. For example, note that the isoperimetric profiles of ShufflerpZq and Z{2Z≀Z
are asymptotically equivalent up to a logarithmic factor, so we may wonder:

Alternatively, another useful technique to produce orbit equivalences is the one of Følner
