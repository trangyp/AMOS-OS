Abstract
Oriented cohomology theories provide a general framework to perform intersection-theory-type
calculus. The Chow ring, algebraic K-theory, and Levine–Morel’s algebraic cobordism are all instances of
such theories satisfying A1-invariance. Topological Hochschild homology, topological cyclic homology,
and Hodge cohomology are important examples of theories without A1-invariance. In this paper, we
prove an additive blowup formula for oriented cohomology theories in the non-A1-invariant category of
motivic spectra, developed by Annala, Hoyois, and Iwasa. Then, we specialize to A1-invariant theories
and give presentations of oriented cohomology rings of the blowup of a smooth scheme along a smooth
center. We compute explicit examples of such presentations for the cases of del Pezzo surfaces, the blowup
of P3 along the twisted cubic, and the blowup of P5 along the Veronese surface, which can be identified
with the moduli space of complete conics. We demonstrate that one can recover solutions to classical
enumerative geometry problems, such as Steiner’s 3264 conics, using arbitrary oriented cohomology
theories. Finally, we give a presentation of oriented cohomology rings of M0,n, which generalizes Keel’s
presentation of the Chow ring.

INTRODUCTION
A central theme in algebraic geometry is to use algebraic invariants to distinguish schemes. Some examples
of such invariants include the Chow ring CH‚ and the Grothendieck ring of vector bundles K0. In [LM07],
Levine–Morel provided an axiomatic framework for oriented cohomology theory, which unifies both the above
examples of invariants. In particular, they constructed the geometric theory of algebraic cobordism Ω‚ and
showed that it is the universal oriented cohomology theory.
The axioms of oriented cohomology theory provide the “intersection theory package”, which includes
functorial pullback and pushforward, projection formula, projective bundle formula, extended homotopy
property, a theory of Chern classes governed by a formal group law, localization sequence, fundamental
classes, and the excess intersection formula. Hence, we can extend many familiar intersection theory
calculations (for example, the ones in Fulton [Ful98] and Eisenbud–Harris [EH16]) to a general oriented
cohomology theory.
An important class of schemes to which we want to extend these calculations is moduli spaces. A
moduli problem asks one to parametrize a family of objects with some defining properties. Under suitable
conditions, solutions to many such moduli problems are parametrized by schemes called fine/coarse moduli
spaces (see [New12], [Alp26]). Calculating intersection numbers on moduli spaces has been a central theme in
enumerative geometry [KM94; Kon95]. Subsequent research by e.g. Lee and Coates–Givental extended these
calculations to K-theory and cobordism-valued Gromov–Witten theory with rational coefficients [CG06;
Lee04]. Very recently, Ellis–Bloor extended ψ-class intersections on M0,n to algebraic cobordism with integral
coefficients [Ell26]. Intersection numbers in general oriented cohomology theories often contain refined
geometric information that is not witnessed by the Chow ring.
On the other hand, many of these moduli spaces, such as the moduli space of stable maps M0,0pP2, 2q
and the moduli space of stable rational n-pointed curves M0,n, have descriptions as blowups of smooth
schemes at smooth centers. In order to understand the oriented cohomology of such spaces with integer
coefficients, we develop a Blowup Presentation, which refers to a presentation of the oriented cohomology ring
of a blowup in terms of the cohomology rings of the base scheme and the blowup center. Such presentations
of the integral Chow ring are well-known, and Fulton–Lang developed the blowup formalism for the K0-ring
[FL85].
The goal of this paper is threefold. First, we provide a presentation in terms of generators and relations for
the oriented cohomology ring of blowups of smooth schemes at smooth centers and apply this presentation
to specific examples. Secondly, we demonstrate that we can recover classical enumerative geometry results,
using our Blowup Presentation for algebraic cobordism. Such examples include counting the number of
conics tangent to 5 general conics in P2 and counting the number of secant lines to the twisted cubic in P3
meeting two general lines. Thirdly, we use the iterated blowup description of M0,n in [Kee92], to give a
presentation of its oriented cohomology rings, which generalizes Keel’s presentation of the Chow ring. We

BACKGROUND ON ORIENTED COHOMOLOGY THEORIES
Cohomology theories with a complex orientation have long played an important role in homotopy theory.
Starting from the geometric constructions of Conner–Floyd, the subject was developed further by Quillen’s
identification of the formal group law of complex cobordism with the universal formal group law, by
Landweber’s exact functor theorem, and by the role of complex cobordism in the Adams–Novikov spectral
sequence. Together, these results established complex cobordism as a fundamental object of study [Ada74;
CF66; Lan76; Qui69; Qui71].
It is natural to ask for an algebro-geometric analogue of complex-oriented cohomology theories. Panin
[Pan03] and then Levine–Morel [LM07] developed the foundations for cohomology theories with an algebraic
orientation on the category of smooth schemes. At the same time, the A1-homotopy theory of Morel–
Voevodsky and the stable motivic homotopy category SHpkq provided a homotopical setting for studying
oriented cohomology theories through their representing objects called oriented motivic ring spectra.
Panin–Pimenov–Röndigs showed that Voevodsky’s spectrum MGL is again the universal object among
oriented motivic spectra, and further extensions such as motivic Landweber exact theories are due to
Naumann–Spitzweck–Østvær [MV99; NSØ09b; PPR08]. Levine–Morel [LM07] developed the corresponding
geometric cohomology and Borel–Moore homology theory called algebraic cobordism. Déglise’s work on
Gysin morphisms and orientations, together with the six-functor formalism of Ayoub and Cisinski–Déglise,
furnished SHpkq with functoriality and purity [Ayo07a; Ayo07b; CD19; Dég08; Dég12]. Déglise–Jin–Khan
[DJK21] developed intersection-theoretic tools on SHpkq, endowing oriented cohomology theories of schemes
with fundamental classes and refined Gysin pullbacks. Thus, oriented cohomology theories provide a
very general framework in which one may perform intersection theory-style calculus. In this section, we
summarize axioms and facts for oriented cohomology theory of schemes constituting what we refer to as the
“intersection theory package,” following [DJK21; LM07].

THE BLOWUP PRESENTATION FOR ORIENTED COHOMOLOGY THEORIES
In this section, we first prove a blowup formula for arbitrary (not necessarily A1-invariant) oriented
cohomology theories represented in the category MSS, which states that the Ep,q-cohomology of the blowup
of a smooth scheme along a smooth center decomposes into a direct sum of the cohomology of the base and
copies of the cohomology of the blowup center. Here, S can be taken to be an arbitrary scheme. This enables
such a decomposition to be applied to non-A1-invariant theories like topological Hochschild homology,
topological cyclic homology, and Hodge cohomology.
Secondly, we move back to the A1-invariant category SHpkq and give a general presentation of the
oriented cohomology ring (along the degree p2p, pq-line) of such a blowup, in terms of geometric generators
and four families of relations. Examples of theories here include the Chow ring, algebraic K-theory, and
algebraic cobordism. The switch back to SHpkq is necessitated by the absence of established “intersection
theory package” and “Gysin package” in MSS, to the best of our knowledge. Nevertheless, we remark that
intersection-theoretic calculations can still be largely performed. For example, we sketch an argument for
the self-intersection formula in MSS, which has been generously pointed out to us by Toni Annala.

We demonstrate how to recover the solution to Steiner’s enumerative problem.
: “Einen Kegelschnitt K zu finden, welche irgend fünf gegebenen Kegelschnitte berührt.”
—-Jakob Steiner, 1848 [Ste48].
: (Glossary) How many conics are simultaneously tangent to 5 general conics in P2?
Steiner then wrote in his article,
> “Daß fünf beliebige gegebene Kegelschnitte im Allgemeinen (und höchstens) von 7776 andern
Kegelschnitten K berührt werden.”
> That five arbitrary given conic sections are in general (and at most) touched by [tangent to]
7776 other conic sections K.

we outline a solution via cobordism-valued intersection theory. Consider the parameter space of plane
