Abstract. We investigate several fundamental properties of kinetic Langevin pro-
cesses in R2d, deﬁned as solutions to the following system:
dxt = vt dt,
dvt = B(xt, vt) dt + dLt
where (Lt, t ≥0) is a pure-jump L´evy process. Our analysis covers both the orig-
inal process and its killed counterpart, where killing occurs upon exiting domains
of the form D = O × Rd for an arbitrary open set O ⊂Rd. Operating within a
low-regularity framework - where the drift B is not assumed to be continuous - we
establish key structural and spectral properties for both the associated non-killed
and killed semigroups. These include: the strong Feller property, weak continuity
of trajectories with respect to initial conditions, topological irreducibility and the
existence of a spectral gap. Furthermore, we prove, in this low-regularity framework,
the existence and uniqueness of a weak solution when the driving noise is a rotation-
ally invariant α-stable process, when α ∈(1, 2). For this speciﬁc case, we show that
the aforementioned properties hold and further establish the existence of densities
within certain Lm spaces as well as the Feller C0(R2d)-semigroup property. Finally,
we address the existence and uniqueness of stationary and quasi-stationary distri-
butions, proving exponential ergodicity for the non-killed process and exponential
convergence to the quasi-stationary limit for the conditioned process. We show that
these results extend to every α ∈(0, 1] when the drift is smooth.

From a physical perspective, (Xt = (xt, vt), t ≥0) describes a particle subject to ex-
ternal forces, linear dissipation, and impulsive, discontinuous random forces generated
by the environment. Each jump of the L´evy process corresponds to a sudden velocity
change induced by violent collisions or interactions, providing a natural interpretation
as a Langevin equation with discontinuous forcing. Kinetic Langevin-type equations
play a central role in mathematical physics (e.g. in statistical physics and molecular
dynamics) and the natural sciences, as they describe the evolution of matter in motion.
In its classical formulation, the interaction with the heat bath is modeled by linear
friction and Gaussian noise. The motivation comes from the physical picture in which
the forcing results from adding up many independent random pulses with the same
law. Provided the variance is ﬁnite, the central limit theorem shows that this sum
becomes asymptotically Gaussian as the number of pulses increases without bound.
Brownian motion is, however, only a special case within the broader class of stochastic
processes that serve as natural models of random noise.
The central limit theorem also extends to i.i.d. random variables with inﬁnite vari-
ance.
In this setting, normalized sums may converge to a stable law in the sense
of L´evy–Khintchine. Such stable distributions are characterized by a parameter α ∈
(0, 2), called the L´evy stability index, which determines moment ﬁniteness: moments of
order p are ﬁnite only when p < α, while higher-order moments diverge. The Gaussian

1.1.3. Purpose of this work. In this work, in addition to drifts B which are C∞, we
consider drifts B belonging to two classes of locally bounded but not continuous vector
ﬁelds:
- Drifts B that are merely measurable and have at most linear growth, see As-

- Drifts of the form B(x) = −∇U(x) + Θ(x) (perturbed gradient ﬁeld), which
may exhibit superlinear growth in the position variable x (where x = (x, v) ∈
Rd × Rd), see Assumption [BP-Grad] below.
In this low-regularity setting, we study both non-killed and killed (outside of D) kinetic
Langevin processes (1.1) driven by pure jump L´evy noises, focusing on:
- The topological irreducibility of the non-killed and killed semigroups (Theo-
rem 2 and Theorem 9).
- The existence of a spectral gap for the associated killed semigroup (Theorem 1
and Proposition 6) as well as the compactness of this semigroup.
- When the noise is a rotationally invariant α-stable L´evy process with α ∈(1, 2):
- Weak existence and uniqueness of solutions to (1.1).
- Existence of a density in some Lm spaces, m > 1.
- The strong Feller property of the non-killed and killed semigroups.
- The weak continuity of the trajectories w.r.t. initial conditions.
- The Feller C0(R2d)-semigroup property of the non-killed process.
- The existence of a spectral gap for the non-killed semigroup.
