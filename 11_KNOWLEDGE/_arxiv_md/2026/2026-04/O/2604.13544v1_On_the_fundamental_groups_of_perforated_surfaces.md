Abstract. A perforated surface is the complement ˚Σ := Σ \ A of a countable
dense subset A in a connected paracompact surface Σ. It is known that the
topological type of Σ \ A is independent of the choice of A. Any perforated
surface is one-dimensional, connected, locally path connected, and is not semi-
locally simply connected at any of its points.
In this paper we obtain a classification theorem for perforated surfaces,
using the classification theorem for surfaces.
We show that any connected
covering of a perforated surface ˚Σ arises from a covering of a surface Σ′ such
that ˚Σ ∼
= ˚Σ′. We show that the fundamental group of perforated surfaces are
large. For example, the countable free σ-product ××
××N π1(S) of π1(S), where S
stands for the Sierpi´nski curve, and the free product ∗cπ1(˚Σ) embed in π1(˚Σ).
We also show that the fundamental groups of ˚Σ, the Sierpi´nski curve and
the Menger curve are not Hopfian.
1. Introduction
Let Σ be any connected paracompact surface without boundary. Let A be any
countable dense subset of Σ. We denote by ˚Σ the path connected space Σ\A. Ben-
nett [B] had shown that any connected metrizable manifold M is countable dense
homogeneous, that is, if A0, A1 are countable dense subspaces of M, there exists a
homeomorphism of M that maps A0 onto A1. In view of this, the homeomorphism
type of ˚Σ = Σ \ A is independent of the choice of the countable dense subset A.
We refer to ˚Σ as a perforated surface or a perforation of Σ. An important special
case is the space R2 \ Q2, which we shall denote by N. It is readily seen that there
are non-homeomorphic surfaces having homeomorphic perforations. For example,
˚S2 ∼= N.

point of a perforated surface has a neighbourhood which is semi-locally simply

2. Classification of perforated surfaces
We recall the notion of the end space of a surface and use it to obtain a classifica-
tion theorem for perforated surfaces. As a consequence we shall show that there is a
family of groups {π1(˚Σα)}α∈J of cardinality 2ℵ0 which are pairwise non-isomorphic.
2.1. Genus and the orientation class. Let Σ be a connected compact surface
with k boundary components, k ≥0. If Σ is orientable of genus g, then its Euler
characteristic equals χ(Σ) = 2 −2g −k and the surface is homeomorphic to the
2-sphere with g handles in which interiors of k embedded disks have been removed.
In the case Σ is non-orientable of genus g then χ(Σ) = 2−g−k and the surface Σ is
homeomorphic to the 2-sphere with g cross-caps in which interiors of k embedded
disks have been removed.
Let Σ be a non-compact connected separable surface. Then Σ is planar if it
can be embedded in R2. It is said to be of genus g ∈N if there exists a compact
(orientable or non-orientable) surface-with-boundary S ⊂Σ of genus g such that
every component of Σ \ S is planar.
The surface Σ is of infinite genus (resp. infinitely non-orientable) if, for any
compact surface-with-boundary S ⊂Σ, the surface Σ \ S is not planar (resp. non-
orientable). If Σ is non-orientable but Σ \ S is orientable for a compact surface-
with-boundary S, then Σ is finitely non-orientable.
Suppose that Σ is finitely non-orientable. Since P 2#P 2#P 2 ∼= P 2#T, it can
be seen that exactly one of the following alternative holds: Σ ∼= P 2#Σ0 or Σ ∼=
P 2#P 2#Σ0 for an orientable surface Σ0.
In the former case Σ is of odd type,
and in the latter, it is of even type. Thus, when the genus is infinite, there are
four orientability classes: one orientable class O, and three non-orientable classes
(collectively denoted NO), namely, infinitely non-orientable NO∞, (finitely) non-
orientable of odd and even types, denoted NOo and NOe respectively.
