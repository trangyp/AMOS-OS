ON ARC-TRANSITIVE INNER-AUTOMORPHIC CAYLEY GRAPHS
ON DIHEDRAL GROUPS
JUN-JIE HUANG, JIN-HUA XIE∗
Abstract. A Cayley graph Cay(G, S) is said to be inner-automorphic if S is a union
of conjugacy classes of a group G, and arc-transitive if its full automorphism group acts
transitively on the set of arcs. In this paper, we characterize four well-known families
of arc-transitive graphs that arise as connected inner-automorphic Cayley graphs on di-
hedral groups, and we provide a necessary condition for other connected arc-transitive
Cayley graphs on dihedral groups to be inner-automorphic. We further construct an
infinite family of examples satisfying this condition, thereby demonstrating the exis-
tence of such graphs. Finally, we complete the classification of all 2-distance-transitive
connected inner-automorphic Cayley graphs on dihedral groups.
keywords. arc-transitive, inner-automorphic Cayley graph, dihedral group, automorphism group
1. Introduction
In this paper, all graphs are finite, simple, connected and undirected. For a graph Γ, we
denote by V (Γ), E(Γ) and Aut(Γ) its vertex set, edge set, and full automorphism group,
respectively. Let G be a finite group. For every element g ∈G, we define the permuta-
tions R(g) : x 7→xg and L(g) : x 7→g−1x on the set G. The sets R(G) := {R(g) | g ∈G}
and L(G) := {L(g) | g ∈G} form regular subgroups of the symmetric group SG, known
respectively as the right regular representation and left regular representation of G. A
fundamental relation between them is given by L(G)R(G) = R(G)Inn(G), where Inn(G)
denotes the inner automorphism group of G.
Let G be a finite group and S ⊆G \ {1} an inverse-closed subset of G (that is,
S = S−1 := {s−1 | s ∈S}). The Cayley graph of G with respect to S, denoted by
Cay(G, S), has vertex set G, and two vertices x, y ∈G are adjacent if and only if
yx−1 ∈S. Clearly, Cay(G, S) is connected if and only if ⟨S⟩= G. Let Γ = Cay(G, S).
It is easy to verify that the right regular representation R(G) forms a regular subgroup
of Aut(Γ), and that Aut(G, S) := {α ∈Aut(G) | Sα = S} is a subgroup of Aut(Γ)1, the
stabilizer of the identity element 1 in Aut(Γ). In contrast, the left regular representation
L(G) is generally not contained in Aut(Γ). A fundamental result of Godsil [7] shows
that the normalizer of R(G) in Aut(Γ) is the semidirect product R(G)⋊Aut(G, S). The
Cayley graph was initially introduced by A. Cayley in 1878 as a graphical representation
of abstract groups. Research on Cayley graphs spans a variety of challenging topics,
including the graph isomorphism problem [5, 21, 36], graphical regular representations
(GRR) of groups [35], symmetry and normality of Cayley graphs [22, 24, 37], as well as
the construction and classification of specific families of Cayley graphs [4, 10, 11, 26].
When the set S is a union of conjugacy classes of G, the Cayley graph Cay(G, S) is
said to be inner-automorphic, a notion named by Cameron [2]. Clearly, Cay(G, S) is

The study of inner-automorphic Cayley graphs dates back to Imrich’s work [13] in
1979, which established that for n ≥5, an inner-automorphic Cayley graph Cay(Sn, S)
has greatest possible connectivity when the set S contains at least one odd permutation
in the symmetric group Sn of degree n. In subsequent years, this family of graphs has
continued to attract considerable interest. A number of papers have since explored a
wide range of problems related to these graphs, including Hamiltonicity (see [33]), the
isomorphism problem (see [28]), the property of being c-expanders [30, 31], spectral
properties and connectivity [13, 14, 38], the second largest eigenvalue [23], the ultimate
independence ratio and hom-idempotence [9, 19], as well as symmetry properties [12,
27, 40]. In this paper, we focus on the study of symmetry in inner-automorphic Cayley
graphs.
Now we introduce some notation. Let u and v be two distinct vertices of a graph
Γ. The distance between u and v in Γ is the smallest length of paths between u and
v, denoted by dΓ(u, v). For a positive integer i, denote by Γi(u) the set of vertices at
distance i with vertex u in Γ. In particular, the neighborhood Γ1(u) is abbreviated as
Γ(u). For a positive integer s, an s-arc of Γ is a sequence of s+1 vertices (u0, u1, . . . , us)
in Γ such that ui is adjacent to ui+1 for 0 ≤i ≤s −1 and uj−1 ̸= uj+1 for 1 ≤j ≤s −1.
A 1-arc is simply called an arc. Let X be a transitive subgroup of Aut(Γ). Then Γ
is called (X, s)-arc-transitive if X is transitive on the set of s-arcs of Γ, and moreover,
it is called (X, s)-distance-transitive, if for each vertex u ∈V (Γ), the stabilizer Xu is
transitive on Γi(u) for all i ≤s. When X = Aut(Γ), we simply say that Γ is s-arc-
transitive or s-distance-transitive, respectively. Furthermore, a 1-arc-transitive graph is
called an arc-transitive or symmetric graph.
The symmetry of inner-automorphic Cayley graphs has emerged as an important re-
search theme in recent algebraic graph theory. For example, Li [20] showed that every
connected (X, 2)-arc-transitive inner-automorphic Cayley graph Cay(G, S) with X =
R(G)L(G) is isomorphic to the complete bipartite graph K3,3. The classification of 2-
arc-transitive inner-automorphic Cayley graphs on nonabelian simple groups were given
in [27]. More recently, the first author and coauthors classified all 3-arc-transitive inner-
automorphic Cayley graphs, and investigated the properties of 2-distance-transitive
inner-automorphic Cayley graphs on nonabelian simple groups, refer to [12].
Building on existing results, we investigate the arc-transitivity of inner-automorphic
Cayley graphs on dihedral groups. Considerable progress has been made in classifying
dihedrants (a Cayley graph on a dihedral group) with specific symmetries. For exam-
ple, the distance-transitive and locally primitive cases were classified in [25] and [26],
respectively. The classification of 2-arc-transitive dihedrants was completed through a
sequence of papers [4, 15, 29]. More recently, Huang et al. [11] provided a full classi-
fication of connected 2-distance-transitive dihedrants, showing that each such graph is
either a complete multipartite graph or a known 2-arc-transitive example. Despite these
