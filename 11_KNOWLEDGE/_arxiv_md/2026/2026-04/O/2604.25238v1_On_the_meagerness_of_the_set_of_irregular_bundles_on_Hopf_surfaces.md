ON THE MEAGERNESS OF THE SET OF IRREGULAR
BUNDLES ON HOPF SURFACES
EDOARDO BALLICO AND ELIZABETH GASPARIM
Abstract. Let Mr,c denote the moduli space of stable bundles with rank r
and second Chern class c > 0 on a Hopf surface. We prove that the subset of
Mr,c formed by irregular bundles is meager.
Motivation and main result
Our interest in vector bundles on Hopf surfaces is motivated by open questions
about the moduli spaces of instantons on S3 ×S1 described by Edward Witten. As
a corollary of our result, we answer in the affirmative the question of connectedness
(posed in [W, Sec. 2.1.4]) of the moduli of SU(2) instantons.
Let π: X −→P1 be a classical Hopf surface. Hence X is elliptically fibered and
diffeomorphic to S3 ×S1. Let Mr,c denote the moduli space of stable bundles with
rank r and second Chern class c > 0 on X. Our main result here is:
Theorem. Let U be a connected component of Mr,c. Then the set of all E ∈U
which are not regular, i.e. either with jumps or irregular, is meager in U (and hence
the set of all E ∈U which are regular is dense in every open subset of U.
A caveat and some basic concepts
Before proving the main theorem, it is worth pointing out why it can not be
argued simply by analogy with the algebraic case. Using the concepts of filtrable
and unstable bundles, we give 2 illustrative examples of new phenomena appearing
in the non-K¨ahler setup.
Example 1. It is well know that bundles on projective surfaces are filtrable; e.g. if
E is a rank 2 bundle on a a smooth projective surface then then it may be written
as an extension
0 −→L −→E −→F −→0
of a rank 1 sheaf by a line bundle, see [Fr, Ch. 2, Prop. 5].
However, [BG2, Thm. 3.9] proves that filtrable bundles on a classical Hopf surface
X must have jumps. Therefore, the dense open set of Mr,c formed by stable bundles
without jumps consists solely of nonfiltrable bundles.
Example 2. On [BGRS, App. A] it is shown that the moduli stack of rank 2 vector
bundles on a projective variety of general type has entire components formed by
uniquely of unstable bundles, with some such components having dimension strictly
larger than that of the moduli space of stable bundles. With this in mind, a priori
one might have expected that there also existed large components of the moduli of
bundles on the Hopf surface which are formed only of irregular bundles. It is the
purpose of this note to prove this is not the case.

We now establish some notation and basic definitions. Fix a Gauduchon metric
on the classical Hopf X. Stability will always be with respect to this metric. For
each pair of integers r ≥2 and c > 0, let Mr,c denote the moduli space of all stable
rank r vector bundles E on X such that c2(E) = c and det(E) ∼= OX. It is known
that Mr,c is smooth and of dimension 2rc.
We now recall a couple of elementary concepts from topology.
Let M be a
topological space. The subspace A ⊂M is called nowhere dense in M if intM(A) =
∅, that is, if the closure of A contains no open subset of M. The set A is called
meager if it is a countable union A = ∪An of nowhere dense subsets An ⊂M.
Let π: X −→P1 be the elliptic fibration of the Hopf surface. For each x ∈P1
set Tx := π−1(x). Denote by T be the elliptic curve such that π is a principal
T-bundle, hence Tx ∼= T for all x ∈P1. Take E ∈Mr,c. We say that E has a jump
if there is x ∈P1 such that E|Tx is not semistable.
Let F be a rank r semistable vector bundle on T such that det(F) ∼= OT . Recall
from [FMW, Def. 1.12] that F is said to be regular if its indecomposable factors
are all different i.e. associated to pairwise distinct elements of Pic(T). Otherwise
F is said to be irregular on T. Regular bundles present good behaviour inside the
moduli spaces, because they have the smallest possible amount of automorphisms.
A bundles E on X is said to be irregular if there exists an x ∈P1 such that E|Tx
is irregular. Otherwise, E is said to be regular on X.
The meagerness of the irregular locus
Lemma 3. Let F be a rank r ≥2 vector bundle on an elliptic curve T such
that det(F) ∼= OT .
By Atiyah’s classification F is a direct sum of semistable
indecomposable ones, h0(T, End(F)) ≥r and equality holds if F is regular. If F is
not regular, then h0(T, End(F)) ≥r + 2.
Proof. For the first statement is proved in [FMW, Lem. 1.13]. Now assume that F
is not regular. In particular, F is decomposable. Write F = F1 ⊕· · ·⊕Fs with each
Fi indecomposable. Let ri be the rank of Fi. Set di := deg(Fi).
First, assume that F is not semistable.
Since det(F) has degree 0, there is
i ∈{1, . . . , s} such that di > 0.
Since d1 + · · · + ds = 0, there is an index j
such that dj < 0. Let e (resp. f) be the number of factors of F with positive
(resp. negative) degree. We have e > 0, f > 0 and e + f ≤s. Permuting the
factors of F we may assume da ≥db for all a ≥b. For all a, b ∈{1, . . . , s} the
vector bundle Hom(Fb, Fa) is semistable of rank rarb and of degree darb −dbra. Fix
a, b ∈{1, . . . , s} such that da > db. Since Hom(Fb, Fa) is semistable of rank rarb
and of degree darb −dbra > 0, we have h0(T, Hom(Fb, Fa)) = darb −dbra. Write
F = F[−1] ⊕F[0] ⊕F[1] with F[0] the direct sum of the degree 0 factors of F, if
any, and F[−1] the direct sum of the negative degree factors and F[1] the sum of
the positive degree factors of F. Set ai := rank(F[i]). We have a0 + a1 + a−1 = r,
a0 ≥0, a1 ≥1 and a2 ≥1. Since each factor of F[1] has degree ≥1 and each
factor of F[−1] has degree ≤−1, we have h0(T, Hom(F[−1], F[1]) ≥a1 + a−1.
We also have h0(T, Hom(F[−1], F[0]) ≥a0. Since H0(T, Hom(F, F)) contains also
H0(T, Hom(F[−1], F[−1]) and H0(T, Hom(F[1], F[1]) and these vector spaces are
non-zero (containing the multiplication by constants), we get h0(T, Hom(F, F)) ≥
a1 + a0 + a2 + 2 = r + 2.
Now assume that F is semistable. Hence (with the notation of [FMW, §1]) Fi ∼=
Iri(λi) for some λi ∈T ∗. By [FMW, Lemma 1.13] we have h0(T, End(Fi)) = ri.

Theorem 4. Let U be a connected component of Mr,c. Then the set of all E ∈U
which are not regular, i.e. either with jumps or irregular, is meager in U (and hence
the set of all E ∈U which are regular is dense in every open subset of U.
Proof. Let A be the set of all E ∈U which are not regular, i.e. such that there
is x ∈P1 for which E|Fx is not regular. For each x ∈P1, let Ax denote the set of
all E ∈U such that E|Tx is not regular. Since dim P1 = 1, it is sufficient to prove
that each Ax is locally, around each of its points, a closed analytic subset of U of
codimension at least 2. Fix x ∈P1 and E ∈Ax. Set F := E|Tx. Consider the exact
sequence
(1)
0 −→End(E)(−Tx) −→End(E) −→End(F) −→0.
Duality gives
h2(X, End(E)(−Tx)) = h0(X, End(E)(+Tx) ⊗ωX) = h0(X, End(E)(−Tx)).
Since E is simple, each element of H0(End(E)) is induced by the multiplication by
a constant. Therefore, h0(X, End(E)(−Tx)) = 0.
Thus, the linear map ρ: H1(X, End(E)) −→H1(Tx, End(F)) is surjective, and
an open neighborhood of 0 ∈H1(X, End(E)) is biholomorphic to an open neigh-
borhood of E in Mr,c. An open neighborhood of 0 in H1(Tx, End(F)) is a versal
deformation space for F. Since ρ is surjective, Lemma 3 gives that Ax has locally
at E at least codimension 2 in U.
□
The following corollary follows implicitly from [BH] and is discussed in further
detail in [Ga], where it is proved under the assumption that each component of
the moduli M2,c contains a stable bundle. Our Theorem 4 shows that such an
assumption is a truism.
Corollary 5. Take r = 2. The complex manifold M2,c is connected.
Proof. By Theorem 4 it is sufficient to prove that the set of all regular E ∈Mc,2 it
is connected. Recall that to each E ∈M2,c there corresponds a spectral curve CE,
which when E is regular is a smooth and connected curve of genus 2n −1 [BG1,
Thm. 1]. To each E ∈M2,c there also corresponds a graph GE, and each graph is
associated to a unique spectral curve.
The set of all graphs associated to at least one regular bundle is an irreducible
Zariski open subset of P2n+1. Furthermore, for each spectral curve CE associated
to a regular bundle, all bundles F having CE = CF are regular [BG1, Lem. 1].
Moreover, the set of such bundles (i.e.
the fibre of the graph map G−1(E)) is
parametrized, by a smooth and connected projective variety, the Jacobian of the
smooth genus 2n −1 curve CE.
□
