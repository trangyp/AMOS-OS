ON THE HEREDITARINESS OF THE REPRESENTATIONS OF THREAD QUIVERS
ENRICO MARIA DEL REGNO
ABSTRACT. We prove a conjecture of Paquette, Rock, and Yildirim by showing that, for
every thread quiver, the abelian category of pointwise finite dimensional representations
is hereditary. Since this category typically lacks enough projectives and injectives, stan-
dard homological methods do not apply directly. Our approach combines a Yoneda Ext
criterion for hereditariness, established in this paper, with structural reductions to the
subcategory of quasi noise free representations. We also indicate an alternative proof
using a Keller’s theorem on derived categories.
Introduction
The primary goal of this paper is to resolve a conjecture proposed in October 2024 by
Paquette, Rock, and Yildirim [1, Conjecture 7.5], proved in Theorem 3.1. It states that for
every thread quiver, the abelian category of pointwise finite dimensional representations
is hereditary, meaning that Ext2(−,−) = 0.
The concept of a thread quiver was originally introduced by Berg and Van Roosmalen
in [7], who considered only discrete linear orders as threads. However, we adopt the
recent generalization by Paquette, Rock, and Yildirim in [1]: a thread quiver is a standard
quiver where each arrow is equipped with an arbitrary linearly ordered set, called a
thread. The associated thread category C is built by inserting the elements of each thread
as intermediate objects between the source and the target of the corresponding arrow.
The morphisms in C are generated by the original arrows and the order relations along
the threads.
The study of thread quivers is motivated by the persistent homology pipeline in
Topological Data Analysis (TDA). While classical TDA typically computes homology over
discrete steps, modern applications increasingly require continuous, multiparameter
filtrations. Thread quivers are the algebraic framework needed for this shift: by replacing
discrete arrows with linearly ordered sets (threads), they model representations that
evolve continuously. For a comprehensive background on standard homology pipeline,
continuous and multiparameter persistence modules, see the survey paper by Botnan
and Lesnick [6].
Let K be a field. A representation of C over K is a covariant functor from C to the
category of K-vector spaces. In the classical setting of a finite quiver Q, the category
Rep(Q) of all representations is well known to be hereditary (see, e.g., [8]). However, as

1. Preliminaries: Quivers and thread categories
Definition 1.1 (Quiver). We let Q = (Q0,Q1,s,t) denote any quiver (or oriented graph),
possibly infinite. The set Q0 is the set of vertices, and Q1 is the set of arrows. The maps
s,t : Q1 →Q0 are the source and target maps, respectively.

P := {Pα}α∈Q1 determines a thread quiver, denoted by (Q,P).

Given a small category C, a representation of C over a field k is a covariant functor
M : C →Mod(k). We denote the category of all such representations by Rep(C). In this
work, we focus on its full subcategory reppwf(C) consisting of pointwise finite dimen-
sional (pwf) representations, namely those for which the vector space M(x) is finite
dimensional for every object x ∈C.
When C is a thread category, we can classify a representation M according to its
support supp(M), i.e., the set of objects x ∈C such that M(x) ̸= 0. A representation
M ∈reppwf(C) is called noise if its support does not intersect the set of vertices Q0, and
noise free if it does not contain non-zero noise direct summands.

us to reduce many homological arguments to the study of indecomposable summands.

A representation M ∈reppwf(C) is called quasi noise free (qnf) if, in its unique Krull–
Schmidt decomposition, only finitely many indecomposable noise summands occur on
any single thread Pα. We denote by repqnf(C) the full subcategory of reppwf(C) consisting
of all quasi noise free representations.
As shown in [1, Proposition 7.1], repqnf(C) is a Serre subcategory of reppwf(C); that is,
the class of quasi noise free representations is closed under taking subobjects, quotients,
and extensions. Being a Serre subcategory of an abelian category, repqnf(C) is itself
abelian.
The tool used to establish this homological property is the notion of a valid partition.
A partition P of the objects of the thread category C is called valid if each vertex x ∈Q0
forms a singleton cell and, for every arrow α ∈Q1, the thread Pα is partitioned into finitely
many interval cells. This concept, introduced in [1, Section 2.2], will also be crucial for
the proof of Theorem 3.2.
Given a representation M ∈reppwf(C), one can associate a canonical partition PM,
characterized by the property that x ≤y lie in the same cell if and only if they are in

2. A General Criterion for Hereditary Categories
In this section, we generalize an important criterion for Ext using the Yoneda defi-
nition of the Ext groups. We use this approach because the category reppwf(C) usually
lacks enough projectives or injectives, meaning we cannot use classical derived functor
techniques.
We briefly recall the structure of the Yoneda Ext and refer to [2, Chapter VII] for
a deeper explanation. For any abelian category C and objects A,C ∈C , the group
Extn(C, A) is the set of equivalence classes of n-extensions with end terms A and C.

E : 0 −→A −→B1 −→··· −→Bn −→C −→0.

3. Hereditariness of Pointwise Finite Dimensional
Representations
The primary objective of this section is to establish the central result of the paper: the
hereditariness of the category of pointwise finite dimensional representations of a thread
category.
Theorem 3.1. Let C be a thread category. Then reppwf(C) is a hereditary category. In
particular, for any objects X ,Y ∈reppwf(C) and any integer n ≥2, we have
Extn
reppwf(C)(X ,Y ) = 0.
The proof relies on the following preliminary results, which establish the strong
connection between the category of pointwise finite dimensional representation and the
category of quasi noise free representations.
