ON G-BIRATIONAL RIGIDITY OF PROJECTIVE SPACES
IVAN CHELTSOV, FR´ED´ERIC MANGOLTE, CONSTANTIN SHRAMOV
Abstract. In this paper, we study finite subgroups G ⊂Aut(Pn) such that Pn is G-birationally
rigid. For each n ⩾3, we prove that Aut(Pn) contains at most finitely many such subgroups up to
conjugation. For n = 4, we prove that P4 is G-birationally superrigid if G ≃PSp4(F3).

1. Introduction
1.1. Birational rigidity. The notion of birational rigidity originated in the work of Iskovskikh
and Manin on the irrationality of smooth quartic threefolds — implicitly they proved that every
smooth complex hypersurface of degree 4 in P4 is birationally superrigid and, in particular, it is
not rational. Recall that a Fano variety X is said to be birationally rigid if
(1) X is a Mori fiber space over a point (X has terminal singularities and Cl(X) is of rank 1),
(2) and X is the only Mori fiber space that is birational to X.
If X is birationally rigid and Bir(X) = Aut(X), we say that X is birationally superrigid. Being
birationally rigid is an obstruction to rationality: this notion is an extreme opposite of rationality.
At the moment, birational rigidity is proved for many complex Fano varieties. However, bira-
tionally rigid Fano varieties are rare among all Fanos. For instance, there are no birationally rigid
two-dimensional Fano varieties (del Pezzo surfaces), and only 3 out of 105 deformation families
of smooth three-dimensional Fano varieties (Fano threefolds) contain birationally rigid smooth
members. The latter are smooth sextic hypersurfaces in P(1, 1, 1, 1, 3) (all of them are birationally
superrigid), smooth complete intersections in P(1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 2) of a quadric and a quartic hypersur-
faces (all of them are birationally rigid), and complete intersections in P5 of a quadric and a cubic
hypersurfaces (a general member of this deformation family is known to be birationally rigid).
Smooth complex hypersurfaces in Pn of degree d ⩽n are birationally rigid only for d = n ⩾4.
All varieties are assumed to be projective, normal and defined over the field of complex numbers C unless stated
otherwise.

Irrationality problems for Fano varieties defined over non-algebraically closed fields and study of
finite subgroups of Cremona groups lead to an equivariant generalization of the notion of birational
rigidity. To state it, we fix a Fano variety X such that X has terminal singularities, and we fix
a finite subgroup G ⊂Aut(X).
Definition 1.1 ([18, Definition 3.1.1]). The Fano variety X is G-birationally rigid if the following
two conditions are satisfied:
(1) X is a G-Mori fiber space over a point (X has terminal singularities and rk Cl(X)G = 1),
(2) if Y is a G-Mori fiber space that is G-birational to X, then Y is G-equivariantly isomorphic
to X.
The Fano variety X is said to be G-birationally superrigid if X is G-birationally rigid and
BirG(X) = AutG(X),
where AutG(X) is the subgroup in Aut(X) consisting of all G-automorphisms (the normalizer of
the group G in Aut(X)), and BirG(X) is the subgroup in Bir(X) consisting of all G-birational
selfmaps (the normalizer of the group G in Bir(X)).
Remark 1.2. Definition 1.1 goes back to Manin–Segre theorem: if X is a smooth del Pezzo surface
such that (−KX)2 ⩽3 and rk Pic(X)G = 1, then X is G-birationally rigid. For the complete
classification of all G-birationally rigid del Pezzo surfaces, see [22, 52].
The simplest example of a Fano variety of dimension n is the projective space Pn. Moreover,
finite subgroups of the group Aut(Pn) ≃PGLn+1(C) have been extensively studied for n ⩽7, see
for instance [6, 7, 25, 36, 37, 38, 50, 51]. Thus, it is natural to pose the following question:
Question 1.3. For which finite subgroups G ⊂Aut(Pn), Pn is G-birationally rigid?
For P2 and P3, the complete answer to this question has been obtained in [16, 17, 20, 47], and
we know very simple geometric criteria for being G-birationally rigid in these two cases:
Theorem 1.4 ([47]). Let G be a finite subgroup in PGL3(C). Then P2 is G-birationally rigid if
and only if the following conditions are satisfied: G does not fix points in P2, G ̸≃A4, and G ̸≃S4.
Theorem 1.5 ([16, 17, 20]). Let G be a finite subgroup in PGL4(C). Then P3 is G-birationally
rigid if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:
(1) G does not fix points in P3,
(2) P3 does not contain G-invariant pair of skew lines,
(3) there exists no G-orbit in P3 of length 4,
(4) G ̸≃A5 and G ̸≃S5.

such that P2 is G-birationally rigid [25]. Indeed, if G is the subgroup in PGL3(C) generated by

Theorem A. If G is a finite subgroup in PGLn+1(C) for n ⩾3 such that Pn is G-birationally

The proof of Theorem A uses geometric and combinatorial results about toric symmetry of Pn,

primitive finite subgroups up to conjugation, see for instance [6, §§61,73,74].
Corollary B. Fix n ⩾3. Up to conjugation, the group PGLn+1(C) contains finitely many finite
subgroups G such that Pn is G-birationally rigid.
Remark 1.7. For higher-dimensional Fano varieties different from Pn, an analogue of Corollary B
does not always hold. For instance, if X ≃P1×P1×P1, then it follows from [10, Corollary 1.4] that
Aut(X) contains infinitely many finite subgroups G up to isomorphism such that rk Cl(X)G = 1
and X is G-birationally superrigid.
Using Theorem A, we can list all possibilities for G ⊂PGLn+1(C) such that Pn has a chance
to be G-birationally rigid in the case when n is small. For instance, in Proposition 5.2, we will
see that up to conjugation there are at most 8 finite subgroups G ⊂PGL5(C) such that P4 can
a priori be G-birationally rigid. One of them is the subgroup G ≃PSp4(F3) that preserves the
famous Burkhardt quartic hypersurface. In Section 5, we will prove the following result.
Theorem C. Let G be a finite primitive subgroup in PGL5(C) such that G ≃PSp4(F3). Then P4
is G-birationally superrigid.
It would be interesting to find out whether P4 is G-birationally rigid in each of the 8 cases listed
in Proposition 5.2. Unfortunately, our proof of Theorem C cannot be adapted to show that P4 is
G-birationally rigid in the remaining 7 cases. It should be pointed out that, except for Theorem A,
there are other obstructions for Pn to be G-birationally rigid, see e.g. Lemmas 2.4 and 2.5.
In dimensions n ⩾5, we do not know examples of finite groups G ⊂PGLn+1(C) such that Pn is
G-birationally rigid. See Sections 6 and 7 for a more detailed discussion of possible candidates in
dimensions n = 5 and n = 6. We find the next question particularly interesting.
Question 1.8 (cf. [15]). Let G ⊂PGL6(C) be the simple Hall–Janko group. Is it true that P5 is
G-birationally rigid?

In Definition 1.1, we defined equivariant birational rigidity for a Fano variety with terminal
singularities acted on by a finite group. The same definition applies for an algebraic group action,
so we can define G-birational rigidity for Pn exactly as in Definition 1.1. Our next goal is to prove
the following purely toric theorem.
Theorem 3.5. Suppose that n ⩾3. Then Pn is not G-birationally rigid.
To prove Theorem 3.5, we have to prove that
