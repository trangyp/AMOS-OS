ON THE COHOMOLOGY OF FINITE TENSOR CATEGORIES
PETTER ANDREAS BERGH
Abstract. It has been conjectured that finite tensor categories have finitely gener-
ated cohomology. We show that this is equivalent to finitely generated Hochschild
cohomology for the endomorphism algebras of the projective generators.
1. Introduction
Given a finite group G and a field k, the cohomology ring H˚pG, kq is a graded-
commutative k-algebra.
Moreover, for every left kG-module M, the tensor product
induces a graded ring homomorphism from H˚pG, kq to Ext˚
kGpM, Mq, turning the latter
into a module over the cohomology ring. By a classical result of Evens, Golod, and
Venkov, the cohomology ring is a finitely generated k-algebra, and Ext˚
kGpM, Mq is
a finitely generated H˚pG, kq-module for every finitely generated left kG-module M;
cf. [16, 17, 25]. One consequence of this is a powerful theory of support varieties for
modules over such group algebras, where the varieties encode homological information.
These support varieties are again a key ingredient in Benson, Carlson, and Rickard’s
classification in [2] of thick tensor ideals in the stable module category.
It is an open question whether finitely generated cohomology holds for all finite-
dimensional Hopf algebras, or even more generally, for all finite tensor categories. In
[15], Etingof and Ostrik conjectured that this is indeed true: for every finite tensor
category, the cohomology ring of the unit object is finitely generated, and every object
has finitely generated cohomology with respect to it. As for group algebras, finiteness
ensures a fruitful theory of support varieties, recently explored in [6, 7, 8, 9].
The concept of finitely generated cohomology also applies to finite-dimensional alge-
bras. For these, the Hochschild cohomology ring, which is always graded-commutative,
plays the role of the cohomology ring. Again, given a left module, the tensor product
induces a graded ring homomorphism into the cohomology of the module, turning this
into a module over the Hochschild cohomology ring. However, not all finite-dimensional
algebras have finitely generated cohomology. In fact, as shown in [28], there exist alge-
bras whose Hochschild cohomology rings are not finitely generated. Finding classes of
algebras for which finiteness holds is an ongoing program.
In this paper, we link the conjecture of Etingof and Ostrik to the Hochschild co-
homology version for algebras. More precisely, we show that a finite tensor category
has finitely generated cohomology if and only if finiteness with respect to Hochschild
cohomology holds for the endomorphism ring of some (equivalently, every) projective
generator in the category.

In our main result, we shall prove that Fgt holds for pC , b, 1q if and only if Fg holds
for certain algebras associated to C . However, before we start proving this, a couple of
remarks are in order.
Remark 2.1. (1) In the recent papers [5, 6, 7, 8, 9], the finiteness condition Fgt was
denoted by Fg. However, when we study both finiteness conditions defined above, then
we need to distinguish the two, and so we use Fg for the version involving algebras
and Hochschild cohomology.
This is historically correct, since this version was first
introduced in [12]. We use the letter t in Fgt to indicate that we are considering finite
tensor categories.
(2) The conjecture of Etingof and Ostrik states that Fgt holds for every finite tensor
category, and as mentioned above, we shall prove that this is equivalent to the statement
that Fg holds for certain finite-dimensional algebras. However, it is not the case that
Fg holds for all finite-dimensional algebras. In fact, this condition is quite strong, as
it implies, for example, that the algebra is Gorenstein; see [12, Theorem 2.5].
It is
not even true that the Hochschild cohomology ring is finitely generated in general; a
counterexample to this was first provided in [28].
In the proof of our main result, we shall make use of the dual category C _ of C .
This is the category with the same objects as C , but with the directions of all the
morphisms reversed. In category theory, this is usually called the opposite category, and
denoted by C op. However, for a monoidal category, like our C , the opposite category
C op usually refers to the monoidal category one obtains by switching the objects in the
tensor product. It is a finite tensor category whenever C is.

taking left duals, say, induces an equivalence C _ ÝÑ C op. Moreover, if Fgt holds for
pC , b, 1q, then it also holds for pC _, b, 1q.

Let us look at some consequences. The first one concerns finite generation of co-
homology over finite-dimensional Hopf algebras. For such an algebra A, the category
mod A is a finite tensor category, so Theorem 2.4 applies.
Corollary 2.5. Let A be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra. If Fg holds for A, then Fgt
holds for mod A, and the converse holds when the ground field is algebraically closed.
It should be noted that, in this case, the result is actually true without the assump-
tion that k is algebraically closed; see [4, Lemma 4.2], which is a strengthening of [21,
Proposition 3.4]. As an example, let k be a field, G a finite group, and consider the
group algebra kG. This is a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra, and Fgt holds for mod kG
by classical results of Evens, Golod, and Venkov; cf. [16, 17, 25]. Therefore Fg holds for
kG; see, for example, [26, Example 3.2].
Finally, recall that an abelian category is of finite representation type if it contains
only finitely many isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects. The following result
shows that Fgt holds for such finite tensor categories, over algebraically closed fields.
Corollary 2.6. If pC , b, 1q is a finite tensor category of finite representation type over
an algebraically closed field, then Fgt holds.
Proof. Let P be a projective generator of C , and consider the k-algebra A “ End C pPqop.

representation type. Moreover, since C is a quasi-Frobenius category, so is mod A, that
is, the algebra A is selfinjective. Therefore, as shown in [11], the algebra is periodic as a
