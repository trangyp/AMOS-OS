Abstract
Max-Cut is a classical graph-partitioning problem where given a graph G = (V, E), the
objective is to find a cut (S, Sc) which maximizes the number of edges crossing the cut. In
a seminal work, Goemans and Williamson gave an αGW ≈0.87856-factor approximation
algorithm for the problem, which was later shown to be tight by the work of Khot, Kindler,
Mossel, and O’Donnell. Since then, there has been a steady progress in understanding the
approximability at even finer levels, and a fundamental goal in this context is to understand
how the structure of the underlying graph affects the approximability of the Max-Cut problem.
In this work, we investigate this question by exploring how the chromatic structure of
a graph affects the Max-Cut problem. In particular, it is known that 2-colorable graphs are
perfect instances of Max-Cut and they are polynomial time solvable, and various works have
developed robust variants of this observation in the 99%-regime, i.e., when the graph is nearly
2-colorable. In this work, we study this question in the 1%-regime instead, where the graph
has significantly weaker structure. Specifically, we consider the settings where the graph is
3-colorable or contains a large independent set (in terms of its volume), and we ask if Max-Cut is
easier to approximate in such instances.
Our main contributions in this context are as follows:
• We show Max-Cut is αGW-hard to approximate for 3-colorable graphs.
• We identify a natural threshold α∗such that the following holds.
– For graphs which contain an independent set of size up to α∗, Max-Cut continues to
be αGW-factor hard to approximate.
– For any graph that contains an independent set of size > α∗, there exists an efficient
> αGW-approximation algorithm for Max-Cut.

Introduction
Max-Cut is a classical combinatorial optimization problem of longstanding interest in both theory
and practice. Formally, in Max-Cut, one is given a graph G = (V, E), and the objective is to
find a cut (S, Sc) which maximizes |E[S, Sc]|, i.e., the number of edges crossing the cut. It arises
as a natural computational task in various areas such as operations research [FIA11], statistical
physics [JKM18], machine learning [WJ08], among many others.
As a result, the complexity of the Max-Cut problem has been studied extensively from various
perspectives, as evidenced by the enormous literature on it. It was one of Karp’s original 21 NP-hard
problems [Kar75], which motivated the study of polynomial-time approximation algorithms for
Max-Cut. In a landmark breakthrough, Goemans and Williamson [GW94] gave an approximation
algorithm with ratio αGW ≈0.87856 based on semi-definite programming (SDP). This approxima-
tion guarantee was then shown to be tight by Khot, Kindler, Mossel, and O’Donnell [KKMO07],
who showed an (αGW + ε)-hardness for approximating Max-Cut [KKMO07] for any constant ε > 0,
assuming the Unique Games Conjecture (UGC) [Kho02]. In an even further refinement of these
results, O’Donnell and Wu [OW08] and Raghavendra [Rag08] independently gave UGC-based
optimal algorithms and hardness results for the entire approximation curve of Max-Cut.
Despite these tight approximation bounds for Max-Cut, our quest to understand the landscape of
approximation for Max-Cut (and other optimization problems in general) still remains far from
complete as we strive to obtain a more fine-grained view of its complexity. In this regard, a broad
ongoing effort revolves around understanding how the structure of the input graph affects the
approximability of Max-Cut. For instance, Max-Cut has been shown to admit improved approxima-
tion ratios for various classes structured instances, such as bounded-degree graphs [FKL02, HK23],
graphs with large spectral gap [AKK+08, Kol10, MM10] or low threshold rank [BRS11, GS11],
graphs with a linear number of edge-disjoint triangles [GLP25, ADKL25], dense graphs [AKK95],
interval graphs [ADKL25], and graphs with a low-rank SDP solution [AZ05]. These lines of work
serve to bridge the gap between existing worst-case approximation guarantees for the problem and
improved guarantees one can achieve when the instances have more structure.
One such natural source of graph structure for Max-Cut comes from the chromatic number. It is
well known that perfectly complete Max-Cut instances are bipartite (i.e., 2-colorable), and Max-Cut
is polynomial-time solvable on such graphs. Furthermore, if a graph is nearly bipartite (up to edge
or vertex deletions), then one can efficiently recover a near-optimal cut [CMM06, Tre09, ACMM05,
GL21]. This makes it natural to ask whether 3-colorability, the next simplest chromatic promise,
can help us do better than Goemans-Williamson algorithm.
▷Can we get better than αGW-approximation for Max-Cut on 3-colorable graphs?
Furthermore, bipartite graphs, as well as graphs with bounded degree, contain independent sets of
linear size. As mentioned earlier, both classes admit better than αGW-approximation for Max-Cut.
More generally, having large independent sets is a natural structural condition closely related to
the condition of having bounded chromatic number. This motivates us to ask whether graphs with
large independent sets are easier instances for Max-Cut. There is, however, one caveat: one can
always pad a graph with isolated vertices to increase the size of the maximum independent set. For
this reason, we instead measure the size of an independent set by the fraction of edges incident on
it, or equivalently by its volume, defined as the sum of (weighted) degrees of its vertices. This leads
us to ask:
▷Can we get better than αGW-approximation for Max-Cut on graphs that contain an independent set of

1.2
Discussion
Our first two results, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2, show that under the promise that the graph is
3-colorable, or it contains a somewhat large independent set, the Max-Cut problem is still hard to
approximate beyond the αGW-factor, even when the 3-coloring or the independent set is revealed
to the algorithm. In the case where the underlying independent set is hidden, we show that the
αGW-hardness continues to hold for graphs with even larger (hidden) independent sets (Theorem
1.3). Finally, as a sharp converse to Theorem 1.3, in Theorem 1.4 we show that if the underlying
graph contains an independent set that is slightly larger than the given threshold α∗≈0.81597, one
can get better than αGW-approximation.
We point out that Theorem 1.4 also serves as a weak converse to Theorem 1.1 in the following
sense: in Theorem 1.1, the underlying 3-coloring of the hard instances produced by the reduction is
perfectly balanced, that is, the three color classes all have equal size. On the other hand, if the input
graph admits a highly unbalanced 3-coloring where one of the color classes is incident on more
than an α∗-fraction of edges, then Theorem 1.4 yields an approximation ratio better than αGW.
Overall, our results explore the approximability of Max-Cut in regimes where the input graph

Related Works
We round off this section with a brief discussion on some additional related works. The related
problems of coloring a graph with small number of colors, and that of finding large independent sets,

the standard SDP relaxation for Max-Cut on G.

3.4
The Unique Games Conjecture
All our hardness results are based on reduction from the UNIQUE GAMES problem, which is defined
below.
Definition 3.6 (Unique Games) A UNIQUE GAMES instance G(U, V, [R], E, {πv,u}(u,v)∈E) is charac-
terized by a left-regular12 bipartite graph with left and right vertex sets U and V, and an edge set E ⊆U × V.
A labeling of the vertices in G is a map σ : U ∪V →[R] which assigns the vertices of G values from the
label set [R]. Each edge (u, v) ∈E is associated with a bijection constraint πv,u : [R] →[R].
A labeling σ : U ∪V →[R] is said to satisfy edge (u, v) if πv,u(σ(v)) = σ(u). The value of the
UNIQUE GAMES instance G, denoted by val(G), is simply the maximum fraction of constraints in G, that
can be satisfied by any labeling σ : U ∪V →[R].
For any c, s ∈(0, 1) such that c ≥s, the (c, s)-UNIQUE GAMES problem is a decision problem where given
a UNIQUE GAMES instance G, the objective is to distinguish between:
• Yes Case. val(G) ≥c.
• No Case. val(G) ≤s.
We now state Khot’s Unique Games Conjecture, which is the starting point for all our reduc-
tions.
Conjecture 3.7 (The Unique Games Conjecture [Kho02]) There exists constants ε0, δ0 ∈(0, 1) such
that the following holds. For every ε ∈(0, ε0], δ ∈(0, δ0] there exists R = R(ε, δ) such that the (1 −ε, δ)-
UNIQUE GAMES problem on instances with label set [R] is NP-hard.
