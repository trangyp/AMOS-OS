ON THE TAME ISOTROPY GROUP OF LOCALLY FINITE DERIVATIONS OF K[X, Y ]
LUIS CID AND MARCELO VELOSO
Abstract. Let K be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. We study the tame isotropy group
TameD(K[X, Y ]) of locally finite derivations of the polynomial ring K[X, Y ], using Van den Essen’s
classification up to conjugation. For each normal form, we explicitly determine the corresponding tame
isotropy group.
We then compare TameD(K[X, Y ]) with the tame isotropy group of the associated
exponential automorphism exp(D), and prove that these groups always coincide. This stands in contrast
to the behaviour of the full automorphism group, where such an equality may fail for derivations with a
nontrivial semisimple part.
1. Introduction
We denote by K[X1, . . . , Xm] the polynomial ring in m variables over an algebraically closed field K
of characteristic zero. In some cases, we simply write K[m]. Let B be an affine K-algebra. We denote by
End(B) the monoid of K-algebra endomorphisms of B, and by Aut(B) the group of K-automorphisms
of B.
An elementary automorphism of K[m] is an automorphism of the form
(X1, . . . , Xm) 7−→(X1, . . . , aXi + fi, . . . , Xm),
where i ∈{1, . . . , m}, a ∈K \ {0}, and fi ∈K[X1, . . . , Xm] is independent of Xi. We denote by
EL(K[m]) the set of elementary automorphisms of K[m]. Recall that an automorphism of K[m] is called
tame if it can be expressed as a composition of elementary automorphisms; otherwise, it is called wild.
A classical and highly nontrivial result (see [Nag72, vdK53, vdE00]) states that every automorphism of
K[X1, X2] is tame. In contrast, in the polynomial ring K[X1, X2, X3], wild automorphisms do exist (see
[SU04]).
A derivation on B is a K-linear map D: B →B satisfying the Leibniz rule
D(ab) = aD(b) + bD(a),
for all a, b ∈B.
We denote by Der(B) the set of all derivations of B.
In [AFV25], the authors introduce the notion of the tame isotropy group of a derivation. They compute
the tame isotropy group of triangular derivations over K[m] for m ≤3, and show that a Shamsuddin
derivation D is simple if and only if TameD(K[m]) is trivial.
Given D ∈Der(K[m]), the tame isotropy group of D is the subgroup generated by all elementary
automorphisms that commute with D, denoted by TameD(K[m]), that is,

In [CV26], the authors determine AutD(B) when D is a locally finite derivation, as well as the isotropy
group of the exponential automorphism associated with D.
The main purpose of this paper is to determine TameD(K[X, Y ]) for every locally finite derivation
D of K[X, Y ], using Van den Essen’s classification [vdE92]. We also study the relationship between
TameD(K[X, Y ]) and the tame isotropy group of the exponential automorphism exp(D). Recall that
exp(D) can be viewed as the time-one map of the polynomial vector field associated with D; this
connection, studied over R and C by Bass and Meisters [BM85] in the context of polynomial flows,
motivates the comparison between the isotropy of D and that of exp(D).
In [CV26], it is shown that the equality
AutD(B) = Autexp(D)(B)
may fail when D has a nontrivial semisimple part. One of our main results shows that this phenomenon
does not occur within the tame subgroup: for every locally finite derivation D of K[X, Y ], we have
TameD(K[X, Y ]) = Tameexp(D)(K[X, Y ]).
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls the necessary background on locally finite deriva-
tions, their Jordan decomposition, and the exponential map. Section 3 determines TameD(K[X, Y ]) for
each normal form. Section 4 compares the tame isotropy group of D with that of exp(D) and establishes
their equality in all cases.
2. Generalities
In this section, we recall the main concepts that will be addressed in the paper.
2.1. Locally finite derivations and their classification. An endomorphism E ∈End(B) is said to
be locally finite if, for every b ∈B, the K-vector space spanK{En(b) | n ≥0} is finite-dimensional.
Similarly, a derivation D ∈Der(B) is called locally finite if, for every b ∈B, the set {Dn(b) | n ≥0}
spans a finite-dimensional K-subspace of B. We denote by LFD(B) the set of all locally finite derivations
of B, and by LFA(B) the set of all locally finite automorphisms of B.
Two important classes of locally finite derivations are given by semisimple derivations those admitting
a basis of eigenvectors and locally nilpotent derivations, for which every element is annihilated by a
sufficiently high iterate. These classes represent, in a natural sense, opposite extremes within the class of
locally finite derivations.

4. Tame isotropy groups for exponential automorphisms
In this section we study Tameexp(D)(K[X, Y ]) for each normal form of Lemma 2.2 and compare it
with TameD(K[X, Y ]).
The conjugation identity (1) gives the inclusion TameD(K[X, Y ]) ⊆Tameexp(D)(K[X, Y ]). In the
full automorphism group, this inclusion can be strict: when D has eigenvalues in 2πiZ on some weight
space, exp(D) may equal the identity even though D ̸= 0, as shown by Example 4.5 and Remark 4.6 of
[CV26]. We show below that no such failure occurs within Tame(K[X, Y ]): in every case the two tame
isotropy groups coincide.
We begin with the locally nilpotent case, for which the argument is general.
Proposition 4.1. If D ∈LND(K[X, Y ]), then TameD(K[X, Y ]) = Tameexp(D)(K[X, Y ]).
Proof. Let φ ∈Tameexp(D)(K[X, Y ]) and set E = φDφ−1. Conjugation by a tame automorphism
preserves local nilpotency, so E ∈LND(K[X, Y ]). By (1), exp(E) = φ exp(D)φ−1 = exp(D). The
exponential map is injective on LND(K[X, Y ]) [CV26], so E = D and φ ∈TameD(K[X, Y ]).

4.5. Comparison with the full automorphism group. For every normal form of Lemma 2.2 we have
obtained
TameD(K[X, Y ]) = Tameexp(D)(K[X, Y ]).
As recalled above, the analogous equality for the full automorphism group can fail. The reason it holds
for the tame group is that the commutativity condition on an elementary automorphism reduces to a
polynomial functional equation such as f(eaX) = eamf(X) or g(ebY ) = g(Y ) that admits only trivial
solutions over K. This algebraic constraint is absent in the full group, where transcendental phenomena
(eigenvalues in 2πiZ) can cause exp(D) to be the identity while D ̸= 0.
