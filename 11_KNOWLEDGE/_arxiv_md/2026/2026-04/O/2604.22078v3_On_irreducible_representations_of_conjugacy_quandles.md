ON IRREDUCIBLE REPRESENTATIONS OF CONJUGACY QUANDLES
MOHAMAD MAASSARANI
Abstract. For G a finite group, one way to construct irreducible quandle representations
over C of the conjugacy quandle Conj(G) is by taking the product of an irreducible linear
group representation of G by what we call a quandle character of Conj(G) (a quandle
morphism into C× ). We show that these are all the irreducible quandle representations of
Conj(G) over C if and only if all the symmetric 2-cocyles over G (α(g, h) = α(h, g) for all
g, h) with values in C× are coboundaries. For instance, this is the case of groups with trivial
Bogomolov multiplier. We apply this to study the envelopping group of Conj(G). If G finite
satisfies the previous condition on symmetric 2-cocycles, we obtain that the enveloping group
of Conj(G) injects into G × ZcG where cG is the number of the conjugacy classes of G. If
moreover G is perfect the injection is an isomorphism.
Introduction and main results
A quandle is set equiped with binary operation satisfying some axioms. To a group G
one can associate a quandle Conj(G) called the conjugacy quandle of G. The underlin-
ing set of Conj(G) is G.
A quandle representation of Conj(G) is a quandle morphism
ρ : Conj(G) →Conj(GL(V )) (ρ(hgh−1) = ρ(h)ρ(g)ρ(h)−1) for some vector space V . A
quandle representation Conj(G) →Conj(GL(V )) is irreducible if the image of the mor-
phism stabilizes non subspace of V other than V and 0. In [MMa26], we proved than an
irreducible quandle representation over C (V is a complex vector space) of a finite quandle is
finite dimensional. In [MMb26], we prove, under some assumptions on a finite group G, that
the irreducible quandle representation of Conj(G) over C are the products of what we call
quandle characters of Conj(G) and irreducible linear representations of the group G. Here
we prove that all irreducible quandle representations of Conj(G) over C (G finite) are of this
form if and only if any symmetric 2-cocycle of G with values in C× is a coboundary. This
condition on symmetric 2-cocycle is satisfied for instance by groups with trivial Bogomolov
multiplier. We apply these results to study the envelopping groups of conjugacy quandles.
The notes are divided into 3 sections. In the first section, we recall the basic definitions.
In the second section, we provide proofs of the results concerning irreducible quandle rep-
resentations. The third section is devoted to the study of envelopping groups of conjugacy
quandles.
1. Reminders on quandles and projective representations of groups
1.1. Quandles.
Definition 1.1. A quandle is a set Q equipped with a binary operation ▷such that :
- x ▷x = x for x ∈Q.
- For all x, y ∈Q there exist a unique z ∈Q, such that x ▷z = y.
Key words and phrases. Groups, Quandles, envelopping groups, representations, Schur multipliers, Schur
covers.

One can constructe a quandle from a group G using the binary operation defined by
x ▷y = xyx−1 for x, y ∈G. The defined quandle is called the conjugacy quandle and is
usually denoted by Conj(G).
Definition 1.2. A quandle morphism is a map between two quandles (Q1, ▷1) and (Q2, ▷2)
such that f(z ▷1 w) = f(z) ▷2 f(w), for z, w ∈Q.
The composition of two quandle morphism is a quandle morphism. A group morhism
f : G →H is quandle morphism if we regard both sets as the conjugacy quandles. For Q
a quandle the left translation Lx by x ∈Q given by Lx(y) = x ▷y for y ∈Q is a bijective
quandle morphism
Definition 1.3. The inner automorphism group Inn(Q) of a quandle Q is the subgroup of
bijections of Q generated by the left translations.
Proposition 1.4. The map Q →Inn(Q), x 7→Lx is a quandle morphism if Inn(Q) is
regarded as the conjugacy quandle of the group Inn(Q).
For G a group, the group Inn(Conj(G)) is equal to Inn(G).
Definition 1.5. [ME18]Let Q be a quandle
1) A linear representation of Q is the data of a vector space V and a quandle morphism
ρ : Q →Conj(GL(V )), i.e. :
ρ(x ▷y) = ρ(x)ρ(y)ρ(x)−1,
for all x, y ∈Q.
2) A subrepresentation of ρ is a vector subspace W ⊂V , such that ρ(x)(W) ⊂W for
all x ∈Q.
3) An irreducible representation is a representation V that has no subrepresentations
other than 0 and V .
A linear group representation ρ : G →GL(V ) is a quandle representation of Conj(G).
Definition 1.6. [MMb26] A character of a quandle Q is a quandle morphism χ : Q →C×,
i.e. :
χ(x ▷y) = χ(y),
for x, y ∈Q.
Definition 1.7. The enveloping group G(Q) of a quandle Q is the group :
G(Q) = ⟨x ∈Q|xyx−1 = x ▷y, for x, y ∈Q⟩.
The enveloping group is also known as : the associated group, adjoint group or structure
group. The group G(Q) is infinite.
Proposition 1.8.
1) The map φQ : Q →G(Q) mapping x ∈Q to the corresponding generator is a quandle
morphism for the conjugcay quandle structure on G(Q).
2) For f : Q →Conj(G) a quandle morphism there is a unique group morphism ˜f : G(Q) →
G such f = ˜f ◦φQ. The map φQ : Q →G(Q) is universal with respect to morphism from Q
to conjugacy quandles of groups.

Definition 1.9.
1) A projective representation of a group G is a morphism G →PGL(V ).
2) A projective representation G →PGL(V ) is irreducible if P(V ) admit no proper projective
subspace stable by G.
Let ρ : G →PGL(V ) be a projective representation and ˜ρ be a lift of ρ to GL(V ) such
that ˜ρ(1) = Id. For g, h ∈G, we have:
˜ρ(gh) = α(g, h)˜ρ(g)˜ρ(h),
for some α(g, h) ∈C×. The mapping α : G × G →C×, (g, h) 7→α(g, h) is a 2-cocycle with
values in C×. If ˜ρ′ is another lift of ρ to GL(V ) such that ˜ρ′(1) = Id, then the cocycles α and

multiplier of a finite group is finite.
Proposition 1.10. A projcetive representation ρ : G →PGL(V ) lifts to a linear represen-
tation G →GL(V ) if and only if its class in H2(G, C×) is trivial.
A proof of the following can be found in [Ka87] (p. 40-42) :
Proposition 1.11. To each class α of the Schur multiplier H2(G, C×) there is at least one

Definition 2.6. A 2-cocycle α : G × G →C× is symmetric if :
α(g, h) = α(h, g),
for all g, h ∈G.
Lemma 2.7. A 2-cocycle α : G × G →C× satisfies the eqaution :
α(g, hg−1)α(h, g−1)α(1, 1)−1α(g, g−1)−1 = 1.
if and only if it is symmetric.
Proof. By replacing h by hg in the equation, we get the equivalent equation
α(g, h)α(hg, g−1)α(1, 1)−1α(g, g−1)−1 = 1.
By the cocycle condition :
α(hg, g−1)α(h, g)α(h, 1)−1α(g, g−1)−1 = 1.
Combining both equation we get the equivalent equation to the one in the lemma:
α(g, h)α(1, 1)−1α(h, g)−1α(h, 1) = 1.
But by the cocycle condition, α(h, 1)α(1, 1)α(h, 1)−1α(h, 1)−1 = 1 and hence α(h, 1) =
α(1, 1). This simplifies the last equation to :
α(g, h) = α(h, g).
We have proved the lemma.
□
Lemma 2.8. Let θ : G →PGL(V ) be an irreducible projective representation of G. There
is an irreducible quandle representation ρ : G →Conj(GL(V ) inducing θ (i.e. ¯ρ = θ) if and
only if the class of θ in H2(G, C×) admits a symmetric representative.
Proof. Let θ be a an irreducible projective represenation with class [θ] ∈H2(G, C×. Now
any lift (as a map) γ : G →GL(V ) of θ satisfies :
γ(gh) = α(g, h)γ(g)γ(h),
for α : G × G →C× a 2-cocycle representing [θ] and any 2-cocycle representing [θ] is
associated to some lift.
A lift γ is a quandle representation if and only if γ(ghg−1) =
γ(g)γ(h)γ(g)−1. Now in term of the cocycle :
γ(ghg−1) = α(g, hg−1)α(h, g−1)γ(g)γ(h)γ(g−1),
γ(1) = α(g, g−1)γ(g)γ(g−1),
γ(1) = α(1, 1)−1id.
Combining these equations we get :
γ(ghg−1) = α(g, hg−1)α(h, g−1)α(g, g−1)−1α(1, 1)−1γ(g)γ(h)γ(g)−1.
This proves that a lift γ is a quandle representation if and only if :
α(g, hg−1)α(h, g−1)α(g, g−1)−1α(1, 1)−1 = 1,
wich is equivalent to α symmetric by the previous lemma. What we have seen proves that θ
lifts to a quandle representation if and only if the class [θ] admits a symmetric representative.
It is not hard to check that the lift is irreducible.
□

Theorem 2.10. The irreducible quandle representations of G over C are the representations
of the form χ·ρ for χ a character of Conj(G) and ρ an irreducible linear group representation
of G, if and only if any symmetric 2-cocycle of G with values in C× is a coboundary.
Proof. For χ · ρ as in the theorem χ · ρ = ¯ρ. Hence, if the irreducible representations of G
are as in the theoreim, the class of the projective representation induced by an irreducible
quandle representation is zero. It follows from the previous lemma, that in this case, every
symetric 2-cocycle has a trivial class in H2(G, C×) and hence is a coboundary. Now assume
that every symmetric 2-cocyle is a coboundary. It follows from the previous lemma that the
only irreducible projective representation that lift to an irreducible quandle representation
in fact lift to an irreducible linear group representation of G. It follows from 2) of 2.5 that
any irreducible quandle representation of G is of the form χ · ρ as in the theorem.
□
