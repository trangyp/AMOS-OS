ON FORMALITY OF DIAGRAMS OF EILENBERG-MACLANE SPACES
GRIGORY SOLOMADIN AND ANTOINE TOUZ´E
Abstract. In this paper, we establish formality (over Q) for diagrams of Eilenberg-MacLane
spaces of any height n ⩾1. This implies spectral sequence (over Q) collapse at page 2 for any
diagram of EML spaces over any small category. We prove by functor calculus argument that
formality does not hold over any fixed commutative ring k not containing Q, where the category
of diagrams is over the category generated by finite direct sums of a cyclic group.
1. Introduction
A space X is called formal over k if its singular chain complex C∗(X; k) with coefficients in k is
connected by a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms to its singular homology H∗(X; k) (considered as a
complex with zero differential). There are many variants of formality: one can consider singular
chains or cochains, endow them with various additional structures (products, coproducts,. . . ) and
ask for a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms preserving the additional structures. More generally, one can
consider formality of diagrams. Namely, given a diagram of formal spaces, one can ask furthermore if
the zig-zags of quasi-isomorphisms connecting the singular (co)chains of each object of the diagram
to its (co)homology can be chosen in a way that is compatible with the maps of the diagram. If so,
then one says that the diagram is formal (see definition 2.5 for a more precise formulation).
While formal spaces are not rare, formal diagrams of spaces are much more exceptional. For
example, in rational homotopy theory, it is known that spheres, K¨ahler manifolds, Eilenberg-
MacLane spaces, and many others are formal [14]. On the contrary, every map can be interpreted as
a small diagram with one arrow and two vertices. By inspecting the Sullivan models, one can then
see that the Hopf fibration S3 →S2 is not formal. More generally, it is known that a homologically
trivial formal map must be nullhomotopic [18, Lemme 3.9].
In this article, we study the formality of diagrams of Eilenberg-MacLane spaces (EML spaces,
for short). Every Eilenberg-MacLane space can be given the structure of a strict abelian group,
and every diagram can be rigidified into a diagram in which the arrows preserve this abelian group
structure (see proposition 2.4). This abelian group structure on spaces induces a commutative
differential graded Hopf algebra structure on the level of singular chains, therefore we can ask
about formality in this context. In the present note, we establish formality for any diagram of
Eilenberg-MacLane spaces with fixed height n ⩾1 over any Q-algebra k (see theorem 3.1).
Theorem 1. For any n ⩾1 let D: C →sSet∗be any diagram of EML spaces of height n. Let k be
a Q-algebra. Then the diagram c 7→C∗(D(c); k) is formal in the category of augmented commutative
differential graded k-algebras if n = 1, and it is formal in the category of commutative differential
graded Hopf k-algebras if n ⩾2.
The proof of formality relies on simplicial models for EML spaces using iterated bar B and
W constructions [15]. If n = 1 or 2, we actually extend theorem 1 to more general rings k in
theorem 3.2, provided the homotopy groups of the EML-spaces of the diagram are “not too large
with respect to k”. For example, formality holds with coefficients k = Z[1/k!], if one restricts to
diagrams of Eilenberg-MacLane spaces with πn of k-dimension ⩽k, see example 3.3.
The formality results given in theorems 3.1 and 3.2 seem to be folklore albeit not published in
this generality to the knowledge of the authors (the case of tori was addressed in [13], and the
integral formality of certain diagrams of EML spaces was addressed in [24]).

2.2. Formality. Let k be a commutative ring, and let D denote one of the following categories:
(1) the category DGModk of complexes of k-modules,
(2) the category ACDGAlgk of augmented graded commutative differential graded k-algebras,
(3) the category CDGHopfk of graded commutative differential graded Hopf k-algebras.
A quasi-isomorphism in D is a morphism which induces an isomorphism in homology. If M is
an object of D, then we consider its homology H∗(M) as an object of D with trivial differential.
When doing this for Hopf algebras, we implicitly assume a flatness assumption on H∗(M) to ensure
that H∗(M ⊗2) is isomorphic to H∗(M)⊗2, hence to ensure that the coproduct on M induces a
coproduct on the homology.
Definition 2.5. A diagram D : C →D is formal if there is a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms in D,
natural with respect to c in C, between D(c) and H∗(D(c)).

The previous definition can be applied to diagrams of simplicial sets. To be more specific, for all
commutative rings k, we denote by k[X] the free simplicial k-module on X and by C∗(X; k) =
Cnormk[X] the associated normalized complex of k-modules, that is the complex of normalized
singular chains of X. The homology of this complex is the singular homology of X, and we
denote it by H∗(X; k). When X is a simplicial abelian group, the addition X × X →X and the
Eilenberg-Zilber shuffle map make C∗(X; k) into a graded commutative differential k-algebra with
unit induced by the inclusion of the constant simplicial abelian group 0 into X. Moreover, the
diagonal map X →X × X and the Alexander-Whitney map induce a coproduct on C∗(X, k) with
counit induced by the unique morphism of simplicial groups X →0. Together with the previous
product, it endows C∗(X; k) with the structure of a graded commutative differential graded Hopf
algebra over k [15, Corollary A.7].
Thus, given a diagram D : C →sSet∗, we can interpret the assignment c 7→C∗(D(c); k) as a
functor from C to DGModk, or to ACDGAlgk or to CDGHopfk if the functor D happens to factor
through the category of simplicial abelian groups.
Definition 2.6. A diagram D : C →sSet∗is formal in the category D if there exists a zig-zag of
quasi-isomorphisms of D, natural with respect to c in C, between C∗(D(c); k) and H∗(D(c); k).
Remark 2.7. In this article, we work with diagrams of pointed simplicial sets. One can alternatively
consider pointed topological spaces, and replace sSet∗by Top∗in the previous definition. But
then a diagram D: C →Top∗is formal if and only if the diagram Sing ◦D: C →sSet∗is formal.
Therefore all the results of the article apply to diagrams of pointed topological spaces as well.
