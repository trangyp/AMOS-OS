Abstract
To any Jordan curve one may associate a circle homeomorphism φ : S1 →S1
via conformal welding.
Through this correspondence, the Loewner energy IL,
also known as the universal Liouville action, is a Kähler potential for the unique
homogeneous Kähler metric on the universal Teichmüller space.
Despite this,
explicit expressions for IL in terms of φ alone do not seem to be available in the
literature.
In this paper, we obtain such formulas. For this, we introduce an operator Λφ
defined using the Fourier coefficients of the function

Introduction
1.1
Background
The Loewner energy IL is a nonnegative quantity associated with each Jordan curve γ
on the Riemann sphere ˆC = C∪{∞}. It was originally introduced in [23], in the context
of large deviations of Schramm–Loewner evolution curves. It is invariant under Möbius
transformations of ˆC, and is zero if and only if the curve is a circle. In a sense, IL(γ)
measures the “roundness” of the curve in a conformally invariant manner.
The Loewner energy is infinite for rough curves. The class of Jordan curves of finite
energy — the Weil–Petersson quasicircles — has attracted much attention in recent years.
It has a striking number of equivalent characterizations and connects a diverse range of
topics, including random conformal geometry [9, 34, 35], Kähler geometry on universal
Teichmüller space [33,36], Berezin quantization of the Bott–Virasoro group [2], minimal
surfaces in hyperbolic 3-space [7], renormalized volume of hyperbolic 3-manifolds [8], and
planar Coulomb gases [16,17]. Let us briefly recall one definition of the Loewner energy
and elaborate on the viewpoint from Teichmüller theory.
After applying a Möbius transformation, we can assume that γ separates 0 and ∞, and
we write D and D∗for the bounded and unbounded components of C ∖γ, respectively,
and D = {z ∈C: |z| < 1} and D∗= {z ∈C: |z| > 1}. Let f : D →D and g : D∗→D∗
be conformal maps (biholomorphic functions) such that f(0) = 0 and g(∞) = ∞. Let

The right-hand side of (1.1) is also known as the universal Liouville action, introduced
in [33] in the study of the Kähler geometry of universal Teichmüller space. To describe
this, note that Carathéodory’s theorem implies that the conformal maps f and g
extend to homeomorphisms of the closures of D and D∗, respectively.
The welding
homeomorphism associated with γ is the map φ : S1 →S1 defined by
φ = g−1 ◦f |S1,
(1.2)
where we write S1 for the unit circle. Here we started with γ and obtained φ. The
conformal welding problem is the inverse operation which starts with φ and tries to find
conformal maps f, g and a curve γ such that (1.2) holds. Under regularity assumptions
(such as requiring φ to be quasisymmetric), this can be done and gives a correspondence
between curves and homeomorphisms, see Section 2.
It is well known that γ is a quasicircle if and only if φ is quasisymmetric, and we denote
by QS = QS(S1) the group of quasisymmetric homeomorphisms of the circle. In [29] it
was shown that γ has finite Loewner energy if and only if φ ∈WP(S1) ⊂QS(S1), where
WP(S1) denotes the class of Weil–Petersson (quasisymmetric) homeomorphisms. This
class is defined by requiring that φ be absolutely continuous and that log |φ′| belongs to
the Sobolev space H1/2(S1). See Sections 2.1 and 2.2.
In this way, via conformal welding, these classes of Jordan curves can be identified
with the corresponding classes of circle homeomorphisms. The latter carry natural group
structures given by composition, and after factoring out Möbius invariance, we obtain
the homogeneous spaces:
T(1) = M¨ob(S1)\ QS(S1),
T0(1) = M¨ob(S1)\ WP(S1).
The former is the universal Teichmüller space, whereas the latter is the Weil–Petersson
Teichmüller space.
It was proved in [33] that T0(1) can be endowed with a unique
homogeneous Kähler metric, called the Weil–Petersson metric, and that IL, viewed as
a functional on circle homeomorphisms, defines a Kähler potential for this metric on
T0(1). However, the underlying reason for the appearance of IL (viewed as arising from
random geometry) in this context remains unclear. With a slight abuse of notation, we
will write IL(φ) for the Loewner energy of φ (identified with its welded curve).
Nevertheless, to our knowledge, all expressions for the Loewner energy of φ ∈WP(S1)
that appear in the literature1 require first solving the welding problem in order to then
express the energy in some way via the curve itself or the pair of conformal maps (f, g).
The goal of this paper is to obtain equivalent and intrinsic expressions for the Loewner
energy using only the circle homeomorphism φ. Although this question has been asked
in the area for some time, it was explicitly formulated in [37, Problem 4.1] and [20]; these
references also provide further motivation.
Finally, note that conformal welding can be viewed naturally as a correspondence
between Jordan curves on the conformal boundary of hyperbolic 3-space and positive

notions in geometric function theory.

1.3
Discussion
Several interesting classes of Jordan curves, including the Weil–Petersson curves, can be
described in terms of log |φ′|; in addition to the ones discussed in this paper, see also
Table 1 in [6]. However, (unlike g′ in D∗), φ′ is not always defined, even for general
φ ∈QS, and cannot be used to study the geometry of the welded curve. The approach
of the present paper is to replace log |φ′| by an operator encoding the log difference
ratio log(|φ(z) −φ(w)| / |z −w|), the latter which is defined a.e. in S1 × S1 for any
homeomorphism. The matrix Λφ is also well-defined under very mild conditions, e.g.,
assuming that φ, φ−1 are Hölder continuous.
The remarkable fact is that natural spectral properties of the operator Λφ are in exact
correspondence with natural classes of homeomorphisms and welded curves. Moreover,
in the case we are primarily interested in here, the correspondence is quantitative in the
sense that IL can be expressed cleanly as a determinant involving Λφ. This interesting
phenomenon can also be seen in the context of the classical Grunsky operator [15,18,33],
and the arc-Grunsky operator of [10].
By Theorem 1.5 and Theorem 1.6, we see that one possible way to generalize the
definition of Loewner energy to Jordan curves outside the Weil–Petersson class is to
consider the appropriate regularized Fredholm determinant −12 log detn(I−Λφ)−1. This
works for all p-Weil–Petersson curves when n ⩾p, since the corresponding Λφ is in the
p-Schatten class. Moreover, this gives a conformally invariant quantity, as can be seen
from the composition rule for Λφ and the invariance of detn by unitary conjugations.
(However, quasicircles with corners have non-compact Λφ, which hence lie in no Schatten
class.
See the discussion in Section 1.2 of [17] for more on attempting to define a
renormalized Loewner energy in the presence of corners motivated by Coulomb gas
asymptotics.)
It would be very interesting to study and characterize Λφ for the rough random
weldings appearing in Liouville quantum gravity and SLE theory.
