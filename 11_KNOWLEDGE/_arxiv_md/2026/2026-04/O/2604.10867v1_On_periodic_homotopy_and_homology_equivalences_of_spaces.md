On periodic homotopy and homology equivalences of spaces
Shaul Barkan, Gijs Heuts and Yuqing Shi
April 14, 2026
Abstract
There are at least two ways to approach the homotopy theory of spaces ‘at chromatic height
𝑛’: one may localize with respect to 𝑇(𝑛)-homology or with respect to 𝑣𝑛-periodic homotopy
groups. It was already observed by Bousfield that these two options yield rather different results.
We build on his work to prove precise comparison results between the two notions. A crucial
concept is a more robust notion of 𝑇(𝑛)-equivalence that we call ‘parametric 𝑇(𝑛)-equivalence’:
this is a map of spaces that induces an equivalence on ∞-categories of local systems valued in
𝑇(𝑛)-local spectra. Our results are sharpest in the case of infinite loop spaces, where amongst
other things we prove a 𝑇(𝑛)-local version of a result of Kuhn on the Morava 𝐾-theory of the
Whitehead tower. As a corollary of our results we also produce a formula for the L 𝑓
𝑛-localization
of an infinite loop space Ω∞𝐸of a spectrum satisfying L 𝑓
𝑛−1 𝐸≃0.

Introduction
The chromatic philosophy is the central organizing principle of stable homotopy theory. One studies
the ∞-category of spectra by decomposing it into ‘monochromatic’ pieces.
This can either mean
localizing with respect to the prime fields 𝐾(𝑛), the Morava 𝐾-theories, or localizing with respect
to the telescopes 𝑇(𝑛), recording the 𝑣𝑛-periodic homotopy groups of spectra. The two options are
closely related, but have recently been shown to differ when 𝑛≥2 [Bur+23].

In this paper we are concerned with unstable homotopy theory, i.e., the homotopy theory of spaces.
As in the stable case one can decompose the ∞-category of spaces into periodic or monochromatic
pieces, but there is potential ambiguity in what one means by this precisely. There are at least the
following two possibilities to consider:
(1) The localization of the ∞-category of pointed spaces S∗with respect to 𝑣𝑛-periodic homotopy
groups, denoted S𝑣𝑛. We will review the ∞-category S𝑣𝑛in Section 3. This localization can
be viewed as a generalization of rational homotopy theory to heights 𝑛> 0. The ∞-category
S𝑣𝑛admits a description in terms of Lie algebras analogous to Quillen’s results on rational
homotopy theory [Heu21].
(2) The localization of the ∞-category of spaces S with respect to 𝑇(𝑛)-homology isomorphisms
(or 𝐾(𝑛)-homology isomorphisms). This is an example of Bousfield localization with respect to
a homology theory [Bou75].
The first perspective emphasizes homotopy groups, the second focuses on homology.
For the ∞-
category of spectra, 𝑣𝑛-periodic homotopy and 𝑇(𝑛)-homology groups are the same thing, so the
distinction above does not play a role in stable homotopy theory.
Also, in the case 𝑛= 0 the
theory of Serre classes shows that for spaces rational homotopy equivalences and rational homology
isomorphisms agree, at least in the simply-connected case, so that (1) and (2) are almost identical.
However, in the cases 𝑛> 0, the localizations (1) and (2) behave very differently for the category of
spaces. Let us illustrate this by some examples:
(a) A 𝑣𝑛-periodic equivalence of pointed spaces 𝑓: 𝑋→𝑌need not be a 𝑇(𝑛)-homology isomor-
phism. For example, any truncated space 𝑋= 𝜏≤𝑘𝑋has vanishing 𝑣𝑛-periodic homotopy groups,
but need not have vanishing 𝑇(𝑛)-homology. Indeed, the 𝑇(𝑛)-homology of Eilenberg–MacLane
spaces 𝐾(F𝑝, 𝑘) is nontrivial for 𝑘≤𝑛by the calculations of Ravenel–Wilson [RW80].
(b) There exist more sophisticated examples of 𝑣𝑛-periodic equivalences that are not 𝑇(𝑛)-homology
isomorphisms and which cannot be avoided by imposing connectivity hypotheses. Indeed, con-
sider the space 𝑋= 𝐵𝑈. Then 𝑋has vanishing 𝑣2-periodic homotopy groups, but it does not
have vanishing 𝑇(2)-homology. One can show that any highly connected cover of 𝐵𝑈will have
the same features. (See Theorem 1.4 for more on this phenomenon.)
(c) In the converse direction, a 𝑇(𝑛)-homology isomorphism need not be a 𝑣𝑛-periodic equiva-
lence.
For example, Langsetmo–Stanley [LS01] construct modifications of the Adams map
𝛼: 𝑆𝑘+2( 𝑝−1)/𝑝→𝑆𝑘/𝑝that retain the property of it being a 𝑇(1)-homology isomorphism, but
fail to be 𝑣1-periodic equivalences.
In his extensive work on homotopical and homological localizations, Bousfield gives many results on
the relation between the notion of 𝑣𝑛-periodic equivalences and 𝑇(𝑛)-homology isomorphisms [Bou94;
Bou97; Bou01]. These play an important role in recent developments in unstable periodic homotopy
theory [Heu21] and in the study of telescopically localized algebraic 𝐾-theory [Lan+24]. In this paper
we further clarify the relation between localizations (1) and (2) above.
Main results
The starting point of our work is the following: rather than focusing on maps 𝑓inducing isomorphisms
on 𝑇(𝑛)-homology, it turns out to be more robust to consider maps giving 𝑇(𝑛)-isomorphisms ‘with
local coefficients’, in the following sense. We call a map 𝑓: 𝑋→𝑌of spaces a parametric 𝑇(𝑛)-
equivalence if it induces an equivalence of ∞-categories
𝑓∗: Fun(𝑌, Sp𝑇(𝑛)) →Fun(𝑋, Sp𝑇(𝑛)).
In other words, we want 𝑓to identify the ∞-categories of parametrized 𝑇(𝑛)-local spectra over 𝑋and
over 𝑌, hence the term. A parametric 𝑇(𝑛)-equivalence is in particular a 𝑇(𝑛)-homology isomorphism;
we will discuss the relation between the two notions in more detail in Section 2.
The main thread of this paper will be to compare the three notions of 𝑣𝑛-periodic equivalence, 𝑇(𝑛)-
equivalence, and parametric 𝑇(𝑛)-equivalence. Theorems 1.1, 1.2, and 1.3 are general comparison
results that are sharpenings of Bousfield’s existing work on the topic.

focuses on a single height 𝑛, while the second concerns all heights 𝑖≤𝑛. This is necessary. Indeed,
the statement of Theorem 1.3 cannot be improved to a statement of the following kind:
(*) An (𝑛+2)-connected map 𝑓: 𝑋→𝑌of spaces that is a 𝑣𝑛-periodic equivalence at every basepoint
is also a parametric 𝑇(𝑛)-equivalence.

particular, for such 𝑋the map 𝑋→∗is also not a parametric 𝑇(𝑛)-equivalence.

The role of periodic homology equivalences of spaces
The fact that Theorem 1.3 does not admit a version at a single height 𝑛(cf. Theorem 1.4) can be
seen as a consequence of the following result of Bousfield [Bou99, Theorem 1.1]:
Theorem 1.9 (Bousfield). Let 𝑓: 𝑋→𝑌be a 𝐾(𝑛)-equivalence of spaces. Then 𝑓is also a 𝐾(𝑖)-
equivalence for 0 < 𝑖< 𝑛.
This statement contrasts starkly with stable homotopy theory: for a map 𝑓of spectra, the statement
above is well-known in case 𝑋and 𝑌are assumed to be finite, but completely false in general. The fact
that it holds for arbitrary spaces suggests that localizing at 𝐾(𝑛)-homology (or at 𝑇(𝑛)-homology)
should not be thought of as making the homotopy theory of spaces ‘monochromatic’: indeed, it still
records information about all heights 𝑖< 𝑛(except 0) and is therefore perhaps best considered as a
‘transchromatic’ localization.
Remark 1.10. A statement like Theorem 1.9 does not hold for periodic homotopy groups, again
illustrating the difference between periodic homotopy and periodic homology. Indeed, if 𝑋is a pointed
space, then the nullification map 𝜂: 𝑋→P𝑉𝑛𝑋with respect to a type 𝑛suspension 𝑉𝑛(see Section 3.2)
kills the 𝑣𝑛-periodic homotopy groups but is an isomorphism on 𝑣𝑖-periodic homotopy groups for 𝑖< 𝑛.
Observe that the map 𝜂is also a 𝑇(𝑖)-homology isomorphism for 𝑖< 𝑛, simply because 𝑉𝑛is 𝑇(𝑖)-
acyclic. In particular, if one starts with a highly-connected space 𝑋with nonvanishing 𝐾(𝑖)-homology,
this yields an example of a highly connected space P𝑉𝑛𝑋with trivial 𝑣𝑛-periodic homotopy groups, but

Consequences for 𝑇(𝑛)-equivalences
We have discussed the limitations of the notion of 𝑇(𝑛)-equivalence and replaced it by the more
robust notion of parametric 𝑇(𝑛)-equivalence.

Theorem 1.13. Suppose that 𝑋and 𝑌are 𝐻-spaces whose homotopy groups are 𝑝∞-torsion in all

Theorem 1.14. If 𝑓: 𝑋→𝑌is an (𝑛+ 1)-connected map that is a 𝑣𝑖-periodic equivalence at every

Plan of this paper
In Section 2 we develop the notion of parametric 𝐸-equivalence, for a spectrum 𝐸, and compare it
to ordinary 𝐸-equivalences and Bousfield’s notion of virtual 𝐸-equivalences. We also record several
results on the 𝑇(𝑛)-homology of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces and truncated spaces that follow from the
work of Carmeli–Schlank–Yanovski [CSY22]. Section 3 is a survey of the notion of 𝑣𝑛-periodic homo-
topy groups of spaces, the Bousfield–Kuhn functor, and the theory of localization and nullification
of spaces developed by Bousfield [Bou94] and Farjoun [DroCS]. We include this material because we
need certain aspects of it that cannot be found in the literature in the precise form that we need them
here. In Section 4 we get to the proofs of our first set of main results, namely Theorems 1.1, 1.2, 1.3,
1.13, and 1.14. The final Section 5 is focused on the special case of infinite loop spaces and contains
the proofs of Theorem 1.6 and its corollaries. Some of the material on 𝑇(𝑛)-local commutative ring
spectra in that section may be of independent interest.

Several notions of homology equivalence
The aim of this section is to develop the notion of parametric 𝐸-equivalence of spaces, for a homology
theory 𝐸, as well as the corresponding localization of the ∞-category S of spaces. We will investigate
some basic properties, as well as the relation to ordinary 𝐸-equivalences, in Section 2.1. This includes
a proof of the existence of a parametric localization functor. In Section 2.2 we discuss features that
are particular to the case where 𝐸is a telescope 𝑇(𝑛). Finally, in Section 2.3 we establish the relation
between our parametric 𝐸-equivalences and Bousfield’s virtual 𝐸-equivalences.
2.1
Parametric homology equivalences
Let 𝐸be a spectrum. As usual, we call a map 𝑓: 𝑋→𝑌of spaces (resp. of spectra) an 𝐸-equivalence
if 𝐸⊗Σ∞
