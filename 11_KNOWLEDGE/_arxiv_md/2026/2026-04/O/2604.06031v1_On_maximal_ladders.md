ON MAXIMAL LADDERS
LORENZO NOTARO
Abstract. Given a positive integer n, an n-ladder is a lower finite lattice
whose elements have at most n lower covers. In 1984, Ditor proved that every
n-ladder has cardinality at most ℵn−1 and asked whether this bound is sharp,
i.e., whether for each n there is an n-ladder of cardinality ℵn−1. We isolate the
notion of maximal n-ladder and use it to study Ditor’s problem and related
questions. We show that Add(ω, ωω) forces every maximal n-ladder to have
cardinality ℵn−1, and hence forces a positive answer to Ditor’s question for
every n. In particular, it is consistent that there are no maximal 3-ladders of
cardinality ℵ1. However, we show that the existence of such a ladder follows
from d = ℵ1. Under ♣, we construct a maximal 3-ladder of breadth 2. Finally,
we prove that, consistently (under ♢), there exists a maximal 3-ladder that is
destructible by forcing with a Suslin tree.

have at most n lower covers. The notion of an n-ladder was introduced indepen-

results of Ditor we cite are from [Dit84].

general result, see Theorem 2.5). He then asked whether this cardinality bound
is sharp (see also [Gr¨a11, p. 291]):

Ditor’s Problem. For every n > 0, is there an n-ladder of cardinality ℵn−1?
The case n = 1 is trivial: ω with its usual ordering is a 1-ladder of cardinality ℵ0.
Ditor proved that the answer to his question is also positive when n = 2, i.e., there
exists a 2-ladder of cardinality ℵ1. Since then, 2-ladders have been used primarily,
but not exclusively, in representation problems in universal algebra for structures
of cardinality ≤ℵ1 (e.g. [Dob86; GLW00; Weh00; RTW07]).
Recent progress has been made on the remaining cases of Ditor’s Problem.
Wehrung [Weh10] proved that the existence of a 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ2 follows
from either of two independent set-theoretic assumptions: MAω1(ℵ1-precaliber),
that is, MAω1 restricted to forcings of precaliber ℵ1; and the existence of an (ω1, 1)-
morass.
Then, the author [Not26] proved that for every n > 2 the existence of an n-ladder
of cardinality ℵn−1 follows from □ω1 + □ω2 + . . . + □ωn−2, i.e., from Jensen’s □κ
holding at the first n−2 uncountable cardinals. In particular, □ω1 implies the exis-
tence of a 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ2. Moreover, since the axiom of constructibility
V = L implies □κ for every uncountable cardinal κ, we conclude from the author’s
result that Ditor’s problem has a positive solution for every n under V = L.
In this paper we introduce the notion of maximal n-ladder. An n-ladder is maxi-
mal if it is not isomorphic to a proper ideal of an n-ladder (Definition 2.6). This no-
tion stems naturally from the work of Ditor and Wehrung [Dit84; Weh10]. Although
the notion of maximal n-ladders has not previously been made explicit, several ex-
isting results can already be phrased in those terms. In particular, Ditor’s results
already imply that every n-ladder of cardinality ℵn−1 is maximal (Corollary 2.7)
and that every maximal n-ladder, with n > 1, is uncountable (Theorem 2.8).
Using the notion of maximality, we can also say something more precise about
Wehrung’s results.
In fact, Wehrung not only proved that MAω1(ℵ1-precaliber)
implies the existence of a 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ2, but, more generally, that every
maximal n-ladder, with n > 2, has cardinality at least ℵ2 (cf. Theorem 2.8).
After providing a characterization of maximality (Theorem 2.9), we prove our
main results.
The first builds on Wehrung’s forcing argument and yields, as a
corollary, a new proof of the consistency of a positive answer to Ditor’s Problem
for every n. Here Add(ω, ωn) is Cohen’s forcing for adding ℵn Cohen reals.
Theorem A. For every m > 1, Add(ω, ωm) forces that every maximal n-ladder
has cardinality at least ℵmin{n−1,m}.
Corollary A. Add(ω, ωω) forces that every maximal n-ladder has cardinality ℵn−1
for every n > 0.
In particular, since every n-ladder extends to a maximal n-ladder, adding ℵω
many Cohen reals forces Ditor’s Problem to have a positive answer for every n > 0.
Theorem A and the above-mentioned result of Wehrung show that, consistently,
every maximal 3-ladder has cardinality ℵ2, or, equivalently, there are no maximal
3-ladders of cardinality ℵ1. The natural question, asked implicitly by Wehrung
[Weh10, p. 7], is whether the existence of a maximal 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ1 is
consistent. Our second result answers this question in the positive.
Theorem B. If d = ℵ1, then there exists a maximal 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ1.
Thus, in particular, the existence of a maximal 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ1 follows
from CH. The proof of Theorem B isolates a natural class of 3-ladders that exhibits
a particularly strong relationship with the dominating number.

The question about the existence of maximal 3-ladders of cardinality ℵ1 has a
purely order-theoretic version, which we believe has independent interest: is there a
maximal 3-ladder of breadth 2? Indeed, a maximal 3-ladder of breadth 2 must have
cardinality ℵ1: every maximal 3-ladder is uncountable by Theorem 2.8 and every
lower finite join-semilattice of breadth 2 has cardinality at most ℵ1 by Theorem 2.5.
Our next theorem shows that, consistently, there are such maximal ladders. In
particular, we construct a maximal 3-ladder of breadth 2 from a guessing principle,
the club principle ♣, which is a weakening of Jensen’s diamond principle ♢.
Theorem C. If ♣holds, then there exists a maximal 3-ladder of breadth 2.
Finally, we study how forcing affects the maximality of n-ladders. For a forcing
notion P, a maximal n-ladder L is P-indestructible if P forces L to stay maximal
in every P-generic extension; otherwise, L is P-destructible. This notion lies at the
core of many open questions (see Questions 3 and 4) revolving around the main
one (Question 1): is the existence of a 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ2 a theorem of ZFC?
Our last result answers a question that naturally arises given Wehrung’s work and
Theorem A, and that is intimately connected to Ditor’s problem (see Question 3):
is it necessary to add new generic reals in order to destroy the maximality of an
n-ladder?
The proof of Theorem A, extending Wehrung’s forcing result, shows
that we can always destroy the maximality of an n-ladder of cardinality < ℵn−1
by adding enough Cohen reals, while Lemma 6.2 shows that for certain maximal
3-ladders adding new reals is indeed necessary. Our final theorem nevertheless gives
a negative answer: consistently, there exists a maximal 3-ladder whose maximality
is destroyed by a forcing that does not add new reals.
Theorem D. If ♢holds, then there is a maximal 3-ladder which is T-destructible
for some Suslin tree T.
In Section 2, we introduce the basic concepts and notation, discuss Ditor’s re-

Sections 3, 4 and 5 are devoted to the proofs of Theorems A, B and C, respectively.
In Section 6 we start by discussing the notion of destructibility with respect to the
results of the previous sections and then we prove Theorem D. Finally, Section 7
contains a selection of the many questions that remain open.
2. Preliminaries
2.1. Notation and basic concepts. The monographs [Jec03] and [Gr¨a11] are our
references for all classical definitions and notation in set theory and lattice theory,
respectively.
A tree (T, ≤) is a poset such that, for each x ∈T, the set {y ∈T | y < x} is
well-ordered by ≤. If x ∈T, the height of x in T, denoted by ht(x), is the order-
type of {y ∈T | y < x}. Moreover, for each ordinal α, the α-th level of T, denoted
by T(α) is the set of all the elements of T of height α.
A join-semilattice is a nonempty set equipped with a binary, associative, com-
mutative, and idempotent operation called join, denoted by ∨; it induces a partial
order via x ≤y ⇐⇒x ∨y = y. Equivalently, a join-semilattice is a partially or-
dered set in which every pair of elements x, y admits a least upper bound, denoted
by x∨y. The dual notion is the meet-semilattice, with the operation called meet. A
lattice is both a join- and a meet-semilattice. We treat (semi)lattices as algebraic

2.2. Ladders and Ditor’s results. An n-ladder is a lower finite lattice in which
every element has at most n lower covers. It is easy to prove that every n-ladder
has breadth at most n [Dit84, Proposition 4.1]. The converse fails: the breadth can
be strictly smaller than the lower cover bound; for example, the diamond lattice
M3 is a 3-ladder of breadth 2.
Figure 1. Hasse diagram of M3
In 1984, Ditor proved the following result, which gives a cardinal upper bound
on the domain of a join-semilattice given its (finite) breadth and the cardinality of
its principal ideals.
Theorem 2.5 (Ditor, [Dit84]). Given some n > 0 and an infinite cardinal κ, if
P is a join-semilattice of breadth at most n whose principal ideals have cardinality
< κ, then
(1) |P| ≤κ+(n−1), and
(2) |I| < κ+(n−1) for every proper ideal I of P.
As noted by Wehrung, Ditor’s Theorem 2.5(1) is in fact a fairly direct corollary
of Kuratowski’s Free Set Theorem [Kur51] (see also [Erd+84, Theorem 46.1]). Since
every n-ladder has breadth at most n, a direct consequence of Theorem 2.5(1) is
that every n-ladder has cardinality at most ℵn−1.
We now introduce the main notion of our paper: maximal n-ladders.
Definition 2.6. An n-ladder is maximal if it is not isomorphic to a proper ideal
of an n-ladder.
In this definition, it does not matter if we consider order-isomorphisms or lattice-
isomorphisms because these two notions coincide when the range of the isomorphism
is an ideal.

Note that no n-ladder is maximal as an (n + 1)-ladder. In other words, given an
n-ladder P, we can always find an (n + 1)-ladder Q such that P is isomorphic to
a proper ideal of Q. The construction is very simple: if P is an n-ladder, then the
product lattice P × {0, 1} equipped with the product ordering is an (n + 1)-ladder
and P is trivially isomorphic to P × {0}, which is a proper ideal of P × {0, 1}.
Even if maximal n-ladders have not yet been explicitly introduced, some results
of Ditor and Wehrung on n-ladders can be naturally recast using this notion. For
example, the following is a direct corollary of Theorem 2.5(2).
Corollary 2.7 (Ditor). Every n-ladder of cardinality ℵn−1 is maximal.
As mentioned in the introduction, Ditor proved that there is a 2-ladder of car-
dinality ℵ1—thus giving a positive answer to his problem for n = 2. He does so by
proving the following:
Theorem 2.8 (Ditor, [Dit84, §6.2]). Every maximal n-ladder, with n > 1, is
uncountable.
Therefore, not only is there a 2-ladder of cardinality ℵ1, but in fact every maximal
2-ladder has cardinality ℵ1—recall that, by Theorem 2.5(1), every 2-ladder has
cardinality at most ℵ1, and, more generally, every n-ladder has cardinality at most
ℵn−1.
Ditor’s proof of Theorem 2.8 relies crucially on the fact that every countable join-
semilattice has a cofinal chain: given a countable P, fix an enumeration ⟨xn : n ∈ω⟩
of P; then the set {W{xm : m ≤n} : n ∈ω} is easily seen to be a cofinal chain.

to characterizing the maximality of n-ladders.

Given f, g ∈ωω, we say that g dominates f, in symbols f <∗g, if for all but

ωω denoted by ≤∗.

element of D. The dominating number is the smallest cardinality of a dominating
family, i.e.,

5. A maximal 3-ladder of breadth 2
In the previous section, we constructed a maximal 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ1
under d = ℵ1. That 3-ladder has breadth 3. In this section we prove Theorem C,
showing that, consistently, there exists a maximal 3-ladder of breadth 2. Note that
such a ladder must have cardinality ℵ1. In fact, it follows from Theorem 2.5(1) that
a 3-ladder of breadth 2 (more generally, a lower finite join-semilattice of breadth
2) has cardinality at most ℵ1.
Furthermore, maximal 3-ladders are necessarily
uncountable by Ditor’s Theorem 2.8. Thus, a maximal 3-ladder of breadth 2 must
indeed have cardinality ℵ1.
We show that the existence of a maximal 3-ladder of breadth 2 follows from a
guessing principle, the club principle, denoted by ♣, introduced by Ostaszewski in
[Ost76]. In the literature this principle is also called Ostaszewski’s principle and
tiltan. Let us recall its definition.
Definition 5.1. The club principle ♣holds if there exists a sequence ⟨Aα : α <
ω1 and α is limit⟩such that:
(1) Aα is an unbounded subset of α, for every limit α < ω1,
(2) for every uncountable X ⊆ω1 the set {α < ω1 : Aα ⊆X} is stationary.

6. Maximality and destructibility
Definition 6.1. Let L be a maximal n-ladder, where n > 0, and let P be a forcing

P-destructible.
The notion of destructibility by a forcing stems naturally from the work of
Wehrung [Weh10] and from our proof of Theorem A. Our Proposition 3.3 together
with Theorem 2.9 directly imply that every maximal n-ladder of cardinality ℵk
with n > k + 1 is Add(ω, ωk)-destructible. In particular, every maximal 3-ladder
of cardinality ℵ1 is Add(ω, ω1)-destructible.
What about maximal 3-ladders of cardinality ℵ2? By the preceding paragraph,
these maximal 3-ladders are Coll(ω1, ω2)×Add(ω, ω1)-destructible. In other words,
we can destroy any maximal 3-ladder, regardless of the cardinality, by first collaps-

7. Open questions
7.1. Ditor’s problem. The main question that remains open is the following
[Gr¨a11; Weh12]:
Question 1. Is the existence of a 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ2 a theorem of ZFC?
We expect this question to have a negative answer, assuming the consistency of
large cardinals. The large cardinal assumption is necessary since the existence of a
3-ladder of cardinality ℵ2 follows from □ω1 [Not26].
We argued in the final paragraph of Section 3 that MAω1(Add(ω, ω1)) implies
the existence of a 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ2. In fact, it implies that every maximal
3-ladder must have cardinality ℵ2. It remains open whether cov(B) > ℵ1 suffices.
Notice that cov(B) > ℵ1 is equivalent to MAω1(Add(ω, 1)) [Bla10, Theorem 7.13].
Question 2. Does cov(B) > ℵ1 imply the existence of a 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ2?
The next two open questions are motivated by the following observation: if ev-
ery maximal 3-ladder is indestructible by a σ-closed forcing, then it follows from
standard arguments that collapsing a Mahlo cardinal κ via Col(ω1, <κ) would re-
sult in a model in which there are no 3-ladders of cardinality ℵ2, thus answering
Question 1 in the negative.
Similarly, if every maximal 3-ladder is indestructible by a countable support
iteration of Sacks forcing S [BL79], then the iteration of κ-many Sacks forcings,
where κ is Mahlo, would again result in a model of ZFC in which there are no
3-ladders of cardinality ℵ2.
Question 3. Is every maximal 3-ladder indestructible by σ-closed forcings?
Question 4. Is every maximal 3-ladder S-indestructible?
Another question related to Question 3 is the following:
Question 5. Does CH imply the existence of a 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ2?
Indeed, a positive answer to Question 3 would entail, by the argument sketched
above, that Question 5 has a negative answer (assuming the consistency of a Mahlo
cardinal).
7.2. Maximal 3-ladders of breadth 2. Theorem B implies, in particular, that
the existence of a maximal 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ1 follows from CH. But the
maximal 3-ladder constructed in the proof of Theorem B has breadth 3. On the
other hand, Theorem C shows that, assuming ♣, we can construct a maximal 3-
ladder of breadth 2. In other words, Theorem C provides a maximal 3-ladder of
cardinality ℵ1 which is “tamer” than the one provided by Theorem B.
The natural question is whether Theorem B can be improved by showing that
CH suffices to prove the existence of a maximal 3-ladder of breadth 2.
Question 6. Does CH imply the existence of a maximal 3-ladder of breadth 2?

These spectra encode a sizable portion of the set-theoretic behavior of n-ladders.
By Ditor’s Theorems 2.5 and 2.8, we know that for every n > 1,
lspn ⊆{ℵ1, ℵ2, . . . , ℵn−1}.
We can also recast Corollary A and Theorem B as follows:
• Add(ω, ωω) forces lspn = {ℵn−1} for every n (Corollary A).
• If d = ℵ1, then ℵ1 ∈lsp3 (Theorem B).
Moreover, once one observes that every n-ladder extends to a maximal n-ladder,
the existence of an n-ladder of cardinality ℵk is equivalent to max lspn ≥ℵk. As a
direct consequence, the sequence ⟨max lspn : n > 0⟩is non-decreasing.
It is natural to investigate the range of possible consistent behaviors of these
spectra. Are there structural—that is, provable in ZFC—constraints on their mutual
arrangements? For example:
Question 7. If there is a 4-ladder of cardinality ℵ2, does it follow that there is
also a 3-ladder of cardinality ℵ2?
Question 8. More generally, for a given m > 2, is it true that either ℵm−1 ∈lspm
or max lspn < ℵm−1 for every n ≥m?
Question 9. Is it always true that lspn ∩{ℵ1, . . . , ℵm−1} ⊆lspm holds for every
n > m > 1?
Question 10. Is it consistent that lspn = {ℵ1, ℵ2, . . . , ℵn−1} for every n > 1?
Question 11. Assuming the consistency of large cardinals, is it consistent that
lspn = {ℵ1} for every n > 1?
Note that a positive answer to Question 11 would entail a strong negative answer
to Ditor’s Problem: for every n > 2, there are no n-ladders of cardinality ℵ2, let
alone ℵn−1.
