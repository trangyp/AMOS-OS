ON GENERIC AND SUPERTIGHT AUTOMORPHISMS
PIOTR KOWALSKI♢AND PINAR U˘GURLU KOWALSKI♡
Abstract. We show that generic automorphisms of stable groups are super-
tight (a notion introduced in [17]) in a strong sense. In particular, we obtain the
existence of supertight automorphisms. We also answer a question from [17]
concerning the relationship between supertight automorphisms of PGL2pKq
and generic automorphisms of the underlying field K. Moreover, we provide
partial evidence—already suggested by Hrushovski in [14]—toward the princi-
ple that “fixed points are pseudofinite” in the setting of generic automorphisms
of simple groups of finite Morley rank.
1. Introduction
The Cherlin-Zilber conjecture (proposed independently by Cherlin [6] and Zilber
[26]) states that infinite simple groups satisfying some natural dimension conditions
(that is, they have finite Morley rank) must be algebraic. This note concerns a
possible approach, suggested by Hrushovski in [14], towards this conjecture. A more
general conjecture of Zilber—the Trichotomy Conjecture—asserting the algebraicity
of certain one-dimensional sets (without any assumed group structure, see [28]) was
refuted by Hrushovski in [13]. Thus, the Cherlin–Zilber conjecture perhaps remains
“closest in spirit” to Zilber’s Trichotomy Conjecture. The Cherlin-Zilber conjecture
is still open; however, the three-dimensional case was settled positively by Fr´econ
in [7].
It should be mentioned that the most promising strategy for addressing the
Cherlin-Zilber conjecture is the so-called Borovik program, which adapts techniques
from finite group theory to classify infinite simple groups of finite Morley rank.
This framework divides such groups into even, odd, mixed, and degenerate types.
Significant progress has been made: in particular, mixed types have been shown
not to exist, and even-type groups are isomorphic the groups of rational points of
algebraic groups over algebraically closed fields of characteristic 2 (see [1]).
Hrushovski’s suggestion in the paragraph titled ‘Connection to the Borovik pro-
gram?’ at the end of [14] can be summarized as follows.
(1) Take a generic automorphism σ of a simple group of finite Morley rank G.
(2) Show that the fixed-point subgroup Fixpσq is a pseudofinite group, i.e., a
model of the common theory of all finite groups.
(Note, however; that
Fixpσq need not be simple as an abstract group; see Remark 4.6(1).)
(3) Show that Fixpσq – HpFq, where H is a simple algebraic group defined
over a pseudofinite field F.

‚ The Algebraic identification step corresponding to Item (4) above.

In this paper, we establish the aforementioned “model-theoretic step”; specifi-
cally, we prove that generic automorphisms are supertight in a strong sense (see
Theorem 3.8). As a consequence, we also show that supertight automorphisms exist
on elementary extensions (see Corollary 3.9). Furthermore, we address a question
from [17] regarding the connection between supertight automorphisms of PGL2pKq
and generic automorphisms of the field K (see Section 4.2). We also show that the
fixed-point groups satisfy the Ax conditions for pseudofiniteness, assuming a very
weak version of elimination of imaginaries (see Section 4.3). Whether this is related
to actual pseudofiniteness for simple groups of finite Morley rank remains the main
challenge.
This paper is organised as follows. In Section 2, we discuss properties of generic
automorphisms on stable structures. In Section 3, we compare generic automor-
phisms on stable groups to supertight automorphisms and show the results men-
tioned above. In Section 4.1, we discuss an axiomatization of generic automor-
phisms using the results existing in the literature. In Section 4.2, we answer [17,
Question 6.1]. Finally, in Section 4.3, we provide a partial evidence (as suggested
by Hrushovski in [14]) toward the pseudofiniteness of the group of fixed points of a
generic automorphism.

3. Tight and supertight automorphisms
We assume in this section that M “ G is a stable group. The definition of tight-
ness from [17, Definition 4.1], originally introduced for groups of finite Morley rank,
generalizes naturally to the present context. Throughout this paper, “definable”
means “definable with parameters”; when we wish to specify a parameter set A,
we often say “A-definable”.
Definition 3.1. We say that σ P AutpGq is tight, if for any connected definable
subgroup H ď G such that σpHq “ H, we have that Fixpσ|Hq is not contained in
any proper definable subgroup of H.
Remark 3.2. We comment here on the definition of tightness.
(1) Since ω-stable groups satisfy the descending chain condition (dcc) on defin-
able subgroups, for any A Ď G there exists a smallest definable subgroup
containing A, which we denote by cldpAq. According to [17, Definition 4.1],
σ P AutpGq is said to be tight if, for any connected definable subgroup
H ď G such that σpHq “ H, we have:
cld ptg P H | σpgq “ guq “ H.
That is, Fixpσ|Hq must be “cld-dense” in H. Clearly, in the context of
ω-stable groups this is just another way of phrasing Definition 3.1.
(2) If G “ HpKq, where K is an algebraically closed field and H is an algebraic
group over K, then a subgroup of G is “cld-dense” in G if and only if it is
Zariski dense in G.
(3) It is worth noting that under Definition 3.1, the identity automorphism on
G is tight. This may be viewed as a weakness of this particular definition
of tightness.

(1) If σ is tight7, then it is also tight, as we may take f “ idH for any definable
subgroup H ď G such that σpHq “ H. Thus, the definition of tightness7
significantly relaxes the restrictions found in the original definition of tight-

it may be merely interpretable. Furthermore, the identity map on H can
be replaced by any interpretable epimorphism f : H Ñ σpHq.
(2) Under this new definition, the identity map on G is no longer tight7. More
generally, any definable automorphism of G fails to be tight7, except in the
case where G is a vector space over F2.
(3) In the context of ω-stable theories, Definition 3.3 translates as “the 7-points
are cld-dense” and it is motivated by the properties of the “7-points” of σ-
varieties (see [19] and [15]).

It is not clear for us whether simple groups of finite Morley rank code finite sets.
Given that vector spaces fail to code finite sets, the assumption of simplicity has to
be used indeed. Nevertheless, the following result suggests that coding finite sets
is a natural assumption when working toward the Cherlin–Zilber conjecture.
Proposition 4.11. Let GpKq is an infinite simple algebraic group over an alge-
braically closed field K. Then GpKq has elimination of imagineries in the language
of groups. In particular, GpKq codes finite sets.
Proof. Since the theory of algebraically closed fields have elimination of imagineries,
it is enough to observe that the bi-interpretation between the pure group GpKq and
the field K (discussed around Proposition 4.7) does not use any imaginary sorts.
Since K is algebraically closed, we can use Zilber’s description from [27, Lemma 2].
The group B appearing there is a Borel subgroup of GpKq, so it is definable in the
home sort of GpKq. The field K is defined on a minimal normal definable subgroup
of B, so it lives in the home sort again. Clearly, the algebraic group GpKq lives in
the home sort of K as well as a subgroup of the group of invertible matrices over
K of some fixed size.
□
Remark 4.12. One can identify model-theoretic properties satisfied by infinite
simple algebraic groups over algebraically closed fields and subsequently test the
Cherlin–Zilber conjecture by investigating whether simple groups of finite Morley
rank share these properties. We list several such properties below. Let G be an
infinite simple algebraic group over an algebraically closed field:
(1) The fixed-point subgroup of a generic automorphism of G is pseudofinite
(this property is crucial!).
(2) The group G eliminates imaginaries (see Proposition 4.11).
(3) The group G is model complete. (This was recently established in [11] for
any simple algebraic group over an arbitrary model complete field.)
After all of this, the most significant obstacle remains providing an “Ax-like”
characterization of pseudofinite groups.
It is not true in general that if σ is a
generic automorphism of M, then Fixpσq is a pseudofinite model of T “ ThpMq.
A counterexample is provided by any differentially closed field M.
Nevertheless, in [14, Proposition 11.1], Hrushovski establishes the existence of
a definable measure in the setting of Proposition 4.10. He suggests that certain
arguments from finite group theory may be applicable to definable sets in Fixpσq,
potentially providing a path toward proving the algebraicity of G by leveraging
Galois theory and numerical invariants beyond pure dimension theory.
