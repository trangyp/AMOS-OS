ON ESSENTIAL AND TOPOLOGICALLY ESSENTIAL
SUBMODULES OF HILBERT C∗-MODULES
K. KARTVELISHVILI
Abstract. We study two notions of largeness for closed submodules of Hilbert C∗-modules:
essentiality and topological essentiality. While the analogous properties are known to be equiv-
alent for closed two-sided ideals of C∗-algebras, the one-sided case is more subtle. We prove
that these two notions remain equivalent for closed right ideals of an arbitrary C∗-algebra.
Next, using the correspondence between submodules and right ideals of the algebra of compact
operators, we extend this result to closed submodules of Hilbert C∗-modules. In the commu-
tative case, where a Hilbert module can be realized as a continuous field of Hilbert spaces, we
give a geometric reformulation of essentiality and derive a fiberwise criterion.
1. Introduction
For an ideal of a C∗-algebra, there are several natural ways to say that it is large. Some of
these notions, together with their natural generalizations to the case of submodules of Hilbert
modules, were considered in [3].
It is well known that if one restricts attention to closed
two-sided ideals, then all these properties are equivalent. For one-sided ideals, however, this
equivalence fails, and that paper establishes some relations between different definitions of a
“large” closed one-sided ideal.
For general background on Hilbert C∗-modules, we refer the reader to [2].
We focus on
two notions for closed submodules of Hilbert C∗-modules, namely essentiality and topological
essentiality.
Definition 1.1. Let M be a Hilbert module over a C∗-algebra A. A closed submodule N ⊂M
is called
(E) essential if for every nonzero (not necessarily closed) submodule K ⊂M, the intersec-
tion N ∩K is nontrivial;
(TE) topologically essential if for every nonzero closed submodule K ⊂M, the intersection
N ∩K is nontrivial.
In the particular case where the algebra A is regarded as a right Hilbert C∗-module over itself,
this yields the definitions of topologically essential (TE) and essential (E) right ideals of a
C∗-algebra.
The property (E) may seem somewhat unnatural in the context of C∗-algebras and Hilbert
modules, since it involves nonclosed submodules or ideals. However, it admits a reformulation
that looks less algebraic and is more convenient in practice (see Lemma 3.3). Our main goal is
to prove that these definitions are equivalent.
In Section 2 we show that essentiality and topological essentiality are equivalent for closed
right ideals of an arbitrary C∗-algebra.
In Section 3 the result of Section 2 is generalized to submodules of Hilbert C∗-modules.
In Section 4 we consider the important special case of a Hilbert module over a commutative
C∗-algebra. In this setting, the module can be viewed as a continuous field of Hilbert spaces
over a locally compact Hausdorff space, and essentiality of a submodule can be reformulated in
geometric terms.

Clearly, (E) ⇒(TE). To prove the converse implication, we need several standard facts
from the operator algebra theory.
Let A be an arbitrary C∗-algebra. By means of the universal representation (see [1, Theorem
I.9.12]), we may identify it with a closed ∗-subalgebra of B(H), the algebra of bounded operators
on a Hilbert space. Denote by A′′ the bicommutant of A, that is, the smallest von Neumann
subalgebra of B(H) containing A. A projection p ∈A′′ is called open if there exists a net of
positive elements as ∈A+ increasing monotonically to p in the strong operator topology. It is
known (see Theorem 1.1 in [5]) that closed one-sided ideals of A admit a description in terms
of open projections. More precisely, for every open projection p ∈A′′, the set J = pA′′ ∩A is
a closed right ideal of A, and every closed right ideal is of that form.

the algebra of bounded Borel functions on the spectrum σ(a) to the algebra M, sending the

3. Generalization to Hilbert modules
Let M be a right Hilbert C∗-module over a C∗-algebra A. Denote by K(M) the C∗-algebra
of compact operators on M, that is, the closure of the linear span of the elementary operators
Θx,y(z) := x⟨y, z⟩over all x, y ∈M.
It is well known (see [4, Corollary 4.2]) that closed submodules of M are in one-to-one corre-
spondence with hereditary subalgebras, and hence with closed right ideals of K(M). Namely,
for a closed submodule N ⊂M define the ideal JN = {T ∈K(M) | Ran T ⊂N}. Conversely,
from a closed right ideal J ⊂K(M) one recovers the submodule N = JM.
Theorem 6.1 of [3] states that this correspondence preserves essentiality, that is, N is an (E)-
submodule if and only if JN is an (E)-ideal. We now show that the same is true for topological
essentiality.
Theorem 3.1. Let N be a closed submodule of a Hilbert C∗-module M. Then N is topologically
essential if and only if JN is topologically essential.

reduces to their equivalence for one-sided ideals of C∗-algebras, and we obtain the following
corollary.
Corollary 3.4. Let A be an arbitrary C∗-algebra. Then every topologically essential submodule
N of a Hilbert A-module M is essential.
4. Essential submodules of C0(X)-modules
We now give another, simpler proof of the equivalence of the properties (E) and (TE) for
submodules of Hilbert C∗-modules over commutative C∗-algebras.
Proposition 4.1. Let A be a commutative C∗-algebra. Then every topologically essential sub-
module N of a Hilbert A-module M is essential.
Proof. Let N ⊂M be a topologically essential submodule, and let m ∈M, m ̸= 0, be arbitrary.
We show that the submodule N ∩mA is nontrivial. By Lemma 3.3, one can find a sequence
ak ∈A and a nonzero n ∈N such that mak →n as k →∞. By continuity of the inner
product, we obtain
⟨n, m⟩ak = ⟨n, mak⟩→⟨n, n⟩.
Since the module action M × A →M is continuous, we also have
m⟨n, m⟩ak →m⟨n, n⟩.
On the other hand, since A is commutative,
m⟨n, m⟩ak = mak⟨n, m⟩→n⟨n, m⟩.
Hence m⟨n, n⟩= n⟨n, m⟩. It remains to note that n′ := n⟨n, m⟩is a nonzero element of the

It is well known (see [6]) that every Hilbert C∗-module M over a commutative C∗-algebra
A can be identified with the module C0(X, H) of continuous sections of a continuous field of
Hilbert spaces H = {Hx}x∈X over the locally compact Hausdorff space X = Sp(A). Moreover,
in the one-point compactification of X, the fiber over ∞is the zero space H∞= 0, that is,
sections vanish at infinity.
It is also known that every closed submodule N ⊂C0(X, H) is of the form
N = {m ∈M | ∀x ∈X m(x) ∈Lx} = C0(X, L),
where L = {Lx}x∈X is a continuous field of closed subspaces of H. Namely,
Lx = {n(x) | n ∈N}.
In these terms one obtains the following general criterion for essentiality of a submodule of
a C0(X)-module.
Proposition 4.2. Let H = {Hx}x∈X be a continuous field of Hilbert spaces over X, vanishing

submodule of M if and only if for every m ∈M the set
Ym = {x ∈X | m(x) /∈Lx}
is nowhere dense.

vanishes on Ym, so ma ∈N, while ma ̸= 0. Thus nowhere denseness of all sets Ym is sufficient
for essentiality of the submodule.
