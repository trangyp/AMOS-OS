1. Introduction
The theory of evolution equations in Banach spaces provides a fundamental framework
for studying time-dependent processes arising in partial differential equations, mathe-
matical physics, control theory, biological processes, and many other areas of application.
A central object in this theory is the abstract linear evolution equation
u′(t) = Au(t),
t ≥0,
(1.1)
where A is a (possibly unbounded) linear operator on a Banach space X. It is well known
that the well-posedness of evolution equation (1.1) is closely related to the generation of
a C0-semigroup on X. The theory of C0-semigroups provides a powerful framework for
studying such problems, see, for instance, the classical monographs [6, 7, 14].

A natural question concerns the stability of well-posedness under perturbations of the
generator. More precisely, one considers the perturbed evolution equation
u′(t) = (A + C)u(t),
t ≥0,
(1.2)
where the linear operator A generates a C0-semigroup and C is a linear operator on
X represents a certain perturbation. Classical results are well established in the case
of bounded or relatively bounded perturbations, relying on Variation-of-Constants For-
mulas and semigroup techniques. If C is bounded, the classical bounded perturbation
theorem ensures that A+C still generates a C0-semigroup, and hence the corresponding
evolution equation remains well-posed, see [6, 14]. However, when the perturbation terms
lack uniform boundedness or exhibit explicit time dependence, the standard framework
is no longer directly applicable, and additional analytical tools are required. We refer
the reader to [1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 13, 19], and their references for more information.
Recently, in [4] a new form for an unbounded perturbation problem was proposed. In
that paper, one investigated sufficient conditions ensuring that the linear operator A+C
generates a strongly continuous semigroup. The key idea was to apply the Hille–Yosida
Theorem to the perturbed linear operator, instead of using the Variation-of-Constants
Formula, in order to prove the generation of a C0-semigroup. In fact, one assumes that
the unperturbed linear evolution equation (1.1) is well-posed, that is, A generates a
C0-semigroup (T(t))t≥0, which satisfies the growth condition
∥T(t)∥≤Meω0t,
t ≥0.
(1.3)
Then, the main result in [4] shows that if the domain D(A) ⊂D(C) and the condition
sup
µ>ω0
∥(µ −ω0)CR(µ, A)∥< ∞,
(1.4)
where (1.3) is satisfied, then the perturbed evolution equation (1.2) is also well-posed,
that is, A + C generates a C0-semigroup. In other words, it is proved in [4] that if A
generates a C0-semigroup, and if C is an operator such that D(A) ⊂D(C), and
lim sup
µ→+∞∥CR(µ, A)∥< ∞,
then, A + C generates a C0-semigroup as well.
In various situations the perturbation may also depend explicitly on time. This leads
to evolution equations of the form
u′(t) = (A + B(t))u(t),
t ∈[a, b],
(1.5)
where {B(t)}t∈[a,b] is a family of (possibly unbounded) operators on X.
It is natural to ask under what conditions on the nonautonomous perturbation B(t)
the perturbed evolution equation (1.5) is well-posed.
The analysis of such nonau-
tonomous perturbations is considerably more delicate, since the classical semigroup
techniques are no longer directly applicable.
Understanding conditions under which
evolution equation (1.5) is well-posed therefore becomes an important and challenging
problem in the theory of evolution equations.
In the mathematical literature, there are relatively few works dealing with nonau-
tonomous perturbations of evolution equations, except for [15] and the references therein.

Although our approach is also based on the Euler polygon construction used in [14,
Theorem 3.1, Chapter 5], the assumptions in the present paper are essentially differ-
ent. In the classical framework, one typically requires stability-type conditions for the
operator family together with certain uniform continuity assumptions. In contrast, our
setting only assumes continuity with respect to the metric of GLA(X). The analysis is
then mainly based on estimates involving the Yosida distance and the distance induced
by GLA(X). This shows that these distances provide a natural and efficient framework
for treating perturbation problems in the theory of linear evolution equations.
The main result of this paper is stated in Theorem 3.6, where we establish the well-
posedness of evolution equations under unbounded nonautonomous perturbations. As
consequences of this result, we further obtain well-posedness on the whole real line
