Abstract
In this article, we consider a smooth, complex Coble surface X, with a
connected boundary curve C ∈| −2KX|. In [9], Pompilj defined a biregular
automorphism T : X →X, and he claimed that the restriction T|C : C →C
equals the identity 1C on every Coble surface X. Coble [2] and Dolgachev [4]
proved that the family of Coble surfaces where this happens is a proper,
locally closed subset of the moduli spaces of Coble surfaces.
Our goal is to show that this family has two components: one of them is
made up of nodal Coble surfaces, and moreover the stronger equality T = 1X
holds on every surface X of this component, while the other component has
codimension 3 in the moduli space of Coble surfaces.
Introduction
In what follows, a surface is a smooth, projective, irreducible variety X of di-
mension 2 over the complex field C. In this setting, we say that X is a Coble
surface if it is rational and its canonical divisor KX satisfies | −KX| = ∅and
| −2KX| = {C}. This curve C is known as the boundary of the surface X, and
one can prove (see [6]) that all its components are rational curves. The goal of this
article is to study a particular family of Coble surfaces which are special from the
point of view of their group of biregular automorphisms. The simplest example
of a Coble surface was given by Coble in [3], and it consists of the blow up of
P2 at 10 points p1, . . . , p10 which are nodes for an irreducible sextic plane curve
C ⊂P2. This construction also corresponds to the original definition given by
Coble. We will see in Remark 5 that such a blow up always satisfies the axioms
for Coble surface. In all this work, we will focus our attention on this model of
Coble surface.
In this case, the boundary curve C ⊂X is given just by the strict transform of the
sextic C. In particular, in the Picard lattice Pic(X) the class of C is C = −2KX,
and thus it is an invariant of the surface itself. As a consequence, every automor-
phism ϕ : X →X carries C to itself, that is, ϕ(C) = C. It is natural then to ask
if there are automorphisms ϕ of X which pointwise fix the entire curve C. This
question, known as Coble conjecture, is still an open problem. Coble expected
that, for the general Coble surface, defined via a general C as above, there are no
such automorphisms.
In [9], Pompilj claimed that the conjecture was false: he constructed a non iden-
tical automorphism T which is well defined on the general Coble surface, and he
claimed that the equality
T|C = 1C
(0.1)

was always true.
Coble [2], and more recently Dolgachev [4], showed that equality (0.1) is gener-
ically false on a Coble surface, and it becomes true only along a suitable family
defining a proper, locally closed subset in the moduli space of Coble surfaces.
The main result of this article is Theorem 19. It states that this family has two
components. Every member X of the first component is nodal, that is, there exists
an irreducible, smooth, rational curve R ⊂X such that R2 = −2. Moreover, if
equality (0.1) holds, then T = 1X.
The second component has codimension 3 in the moduli space of Coble surfaces.
For any Coble surface X in this component, we are able to give the explicit equa-
tion(see Lemma 18) of a quintic surface X ⊂P3 which is a birational model for
X.
The proof is structured in the following steps: In Section 1 we recall some standard
properties of the Del Pezzo surfaces of degree 1 or 2 and of the Coble surfaces,
which we will use later.
Section 2 is dedicated to a technical result: we take a Coble surface X with
irreducible boundary curve C ∈| −2KX|, together with three disjoint (−1)-
curves E1, E2, E3 ⊂X. Then we describe the linear system defined by the divisor
H := C + E1 + E2 + E3. By adjunction formula, the three line bundles OC(Ei)
have degree 2 on C ≃P1. Moreover the image of the restriction map
H0(OX(Ei)) →H0(OC(Ei)), i = 1, 2, 3,
is a 1-dimensional subspace, generated by a vector vi. The goal is to show that
if v1, v2, v3 are linearly dependent, then X admits (−2)-curves, whose classes are
explicitly defined in the Picard lattice Pic(X). As a corollary, we will see how the
linear system |H| behaves on unnodal Coble surfaces.
In Section 3 we prove our main result. As Coble and Dolgachev did, we first note
that the automorphism T decomposes as
T = (i3 ◦i2 ◦i1)2,
where the ik’s are three Bertini involutions. The proof of this fact is straight-
forward, based on the very definition of T given by Pompilj.
This means that the three restrictions σk := (ik)|C : C →C, k = 1, 2, 3, are
involutions on C ≃P1, such that
(σ3 ◦σ2 ◦σ1)2 = 1C.
At this point, we distinguish two cases: if σ3 ◦σ2 ◦σ1 ̸= 1C, Coble’s strategy is to
apply Pascal’s Theorem 7, to deduce a non - trivial condition of linear dependence
on the degree two effective divisors f1, f2, f3 which are respectively supported on

A projective model of a Coble surface
The goal of this section is to find a sufficient condition for a Coble surface X with
irreducible boundary curve C to contain (−2)-curves. We begin by considering a
set E1, E2, E3 ⊂X of disjoint (−1)-curves, since we are interested to describe the
linear system H := C + E1 + E2 + E3.

in view of our applications.
