Abstract. The number of connected components can be remembered
by the von Neumann algebra among Artin groups, the only possible ex-
ception being the case that corresponds to the free group factor problem.
In the case of Coxeter groups, this result is obtained in the absence of
relatively hyperbolicity. We also discuss a specific case of the analogous
problem in measure equivalence where each factor group is a product of
nonabelian free groups.
1. Introduction
In recent years, the study of classification and rigidity of von Neumann
algebras of graph products has generated some interesting mathematics. We
point the reader to [HI25] and the references therein for a detailed summary
of the story. Despite this, there is not much study at the moment into the
von Neumann algebras of the family of general Artin or Coxeter groups over
defining graphs with arbitrary edge relations (see [BGO25] for a recent result
on biexactness for general Coxeter groups).
A rather basic question is whether the number of connected components
of the defining graph can be recovered by the group von Neumann alge-
bra, among Artin/Coxeter groups in general. This is a notorious question
in full generality because it would solve the free group factor problem if
proved. Indeed, the connected components could be complete graphs and
all of the edge relations could be commutation, in which case the associated
von Neumann algebras are amenable, and thus yield free group factors of
the appropriate rank [Dyk93]. In this note, we show that this is the only
obstruction for this phenomenon in Artin groups.
Theorem 1.1. Suppose Γ1 and Γ2 are finite simple graphs, and AΓi are the
associated Artin groups with arbitrary choices of defining relations. Suppose
each connected component of Γ1 is not a complete graph with all edge rela-
tions equal to 2. If L(AΓ1) ∼= L(AΓ2), then Γ1 and Γ2 have the same number
of connected components.
The situation for Coxeter groups is quite a bit more subtle and one cannot
hope for the same result as above. This is because fundamental groups of
hyperbolic surfaces can appear as free factors in which case it is annoying
because it is another notorious open problem of Voiculescu whether such

2. Some Notation
Let Γ be a finite simple graph with vertex set V Γ and edge set EΓ, with
each of its edges labeled by an integer ≥2. A subgraph Γ′ of a graph Γ
is full, if whenever two vertices of Γ′ are adjacent in Γ, then any edge of Γ
between these two vertices are contained in Γ′.
Given a vertex v ∈V Γ, the link of v, denoted lk(v), is the set of vertices
in Γ that are adjacent to v. The star centered at v, denoted st(v) is defined
as st(v) = {v} ∪lk(v) and the closed star of v, denoted NΓ(v), is the full
subgraph of Γ spanned by v and all of the vertices that are adjacent to v. We
say that a vertex v ∈V Γ is essential if the closed star of v is not properly
contained in the closed star of w for a vertex w ̸= v. A star is maximal if it
is centered at an essential vertex. Every vertex v ∈V Γ is contained in some
maximal star.
Given two paths P1, P2 of Γ such that P1 ends at the same vertex that
P2 starts, we denote by P1; P2 the concatenation of P1 and P2. Given a set
of vertices V ⊂V Γ, we denote by ΓV the full subgraph of Γ spanned by V
and by HV the subgroup of AΓ generated by the vertex groups in V .
Given two letters a, b and positive integer m ≥2, let W(a, b, m) be the
alternating word of length m starting with a. The Artin group with defining
graph Γ, denoted AΓ, is given by the following presentation:
⟨si ∈V Γ | W(si, sj, mij) = W(sj, si, mij) for each si and sj spanning an
edge labeled by mij⟩.
The Coxeter group with defining graph Γ, denoted WΓ, is given by the fol-
lowing presentation:
⟨si ∈V Γ | s2
i = e, W(si, sj, mij) = W(sj, si, mij) for each si and sj
spanning an edge labeled by mij⟩
An Artin or Coxeter group is right angled if W(si, sj, mij) = 2 for all i, j.
Suppose W is a Coxeter group with generating set S = {s1, ..., sn}. The
pair (W, S) is called a Coxeter system. Given a subset A ⊂S, we denote
by WA the subgroup of W generated by A. The Coxeter system (WA, A)
irreducible if there is no partition of A into two nonempty, disjoint commut-
ing subsets, spherical if WA is finite, and minimal non-spherical if (WA, A)
is non-spherical but (WB, B) is spherical for every proper subset B ⊂A.
We say that a subset A ⊂S is irreducible (resp. spherical, minimal non-
spherical) if (WA, A) is irreducible (resp. spherical, minimal non-spherical).
Given s ∈S, we denote by s⊥the set of elements in S \ {s} commuting
with s, and given two disjoint, commuting subsets A, B ⊂S, we denote
by (WA, A) × (WB, B) the Coxeter system (WA∪B, A ∪B). Every Coxeter

3. αt-rigid groups
Here we will describe a family of countable groups which we call αt–
rigid. The motivation of this comes from Popa’s deformation/rigidity theory
[Pop07], in particular in the context of the malleable deformations associated
to a free product. The proof of the von Neumann algebraic side of the result
is in fact well known to experts. The result we aim for is known as “Kurosh-
type rigidity”, of which now there are plenty [Oza06, HU16, DKE24, Dri23],
etc. The main input in the argument is that the connected components are
αt–rigid, i.e rigid with respect to the standard mixing malleable deformation
arising from free products [IPP08]. Then the proof follows identically the
intertwining arguments in Corollary 8.1 in [Dri23].
Now we document some examples of groups whose von Neumann alge-
bras will be αt–rigid when they embed into free products endowed with the
standard mixing s-malleable deformation.
Proposition 3.1.
(i) If G is nonamenable and is the direct sum of two infinite groups,
then G is αt–rigid [ADP, Lemma 19.1.3].
(ii) If G is nonamenable and G has infinite center, then G is αt–rigid
[ADP, Lemma 19.1.3].
(iii) If H1, H2 < G are such that Hi are αt–rigid, H1, H2 generate G and
|H1 ∩H2| = ∞then G is αt–rigid [dSHHS21, Theorem 1.3].
(iv) If H < G is αt–rigid, then Nwq(H) is αt–rigid where Nwq(H) is the
group generated by the set of elements g ∈G such that gHg−1 ∩H
is infinite [dSHHS21, Theorem 1.6].
Owing to this, it will be enough to prove that each of the free components
of the groups in our theorems are αt–rigid in the above sense, to derive the
main results.

5. Coxeter Groups
We may obtain a similar decomposition for nonamenable, not relatively
hyperbolic Coxeter groups. For this, we require the class of Coxeter groups
T and T0 defined in [BHS17, Section A.1].
Definition 5.1. Define the class of Coxeter systems T inductively as fol-
lows:
(1) T contains T0, the class of all irreducible affine Coxeter systems
(W, S) with |S| ≥3 along with Coxeter systems of the form (W, S) =
(WS1, S1)×(WS2, S2) where (WSi, Si) is irreducible non-spherical for
i = 1, 2.

(3) If S = S1 ∪S2 is such that (WS1, S1), (WS2, S2) ∈T and S1 ∩S2 is
non-spherical, then (W, S) ∈T.
