CIRO CILIBERTO, ANDREAS LEOPOLD KNUTSEN, AND SARA TORELLI
Abstract. We give an alternative proof of the Hurwitz existence problem for branched
covers of P1 in the case where the number of ramification points equals the number of
branch points, that is, where all the ramification profiles are of the form [e, 1, . . . , 1]
with e ≥2.

genera g = g(C) and b = g(B) one may associate the combinatorial datum of g, b, d plus
the number n of branch points and the n partitions π1, . . . , πn of d given by the local
degrees of f at the preimages of the branch points. Precisely, for each branch point

An old question of Hurwitz [4] asks which combinatorial data satisfying (1) actually do
occur for a branched cover, and if so, how many covers with such data exist.
If b > 0 it is known that, for any fixed B, branched covers satisfying a given data
do indeed exist as soon as the Riemann-Hurwitz condition (1) is satisfied [3, Prop. 3.4]
(see also [7, Thm.1.11]). In the case b = 0, that is, when B = P1, a similar result is no
longer true. There are data satisfying the Riemann-Hurwitz condition (1) for which no
branched cover exists (see Example 1.1 below). Many partial answers to the Hurwitz
existence problem have been given, but a complete solution is still lacking. We refer to
[7] for an exposition of the problem and known results, with several examples, also in
the non-compact (and thus non-orientable) case.

1. Proof of the main theorem
1.1. The Riemann Existence Theorem. The proof is based on the Riemann Exis-
tence Theorem, which states that there is a one-to-one correspondence between degree
d-covers of P1 branched at n points y1, . . . , yn of P1 with ramification profile
πi := [ei,1, . . . , ei,ℓi]
over yi and conjugacy classes of n-tuples (σ1, . . . , σn) of permutations in the symmetric
group Sym(d) satisfying:
• σi has cycle structure πi,
• the subgroup generated by the σis is transitive,
• σ1 · · · σn = idd (the identity element in Sym(d)).
Before starting the proof, it can be instructive to look at the following example men-
tioned in the introduction:
Example 1.1. ([7, Prop. 1.7 or Prop. 1.10]) Take d = 4, an integer n ≥3, and the
partitions
π1 = · · · = πn−1 = [2, 2], πn = [3, 1].
Let g := n−3. Then the Riemann-Hurwitz formula (1) is satisfied. However, there is no
cover C →P1 from a genus g Riemann surface C with ramification profile [π1, . . . , πn]
over n points of P1. Indeed, if it existed, there would exist σ1, . . . , σn−1 ∈Sym(4) with
cyclic structure [2, 2] such that σ1 · · · σn−1 has cyclic structure [3, 1]. However, any two
permutations with cyclic structure [2, 2] are, up to conjugation, (1, 2)(3, 4) or (1, 3)(2, 4),
so their product is either the identity or (1, 4)(2, 3). Hence, the product of permutations
with cyclic structure [2, 2] can never be of cyclic structure [3, 1].
Next we will start the proof of the main result. We use the following conventions:
• The order of multiplication of two permutations σ, γ ∈Sym(d) is defined by
(σγ)(x) = σ(γ(x)).
• A cycle (of length or order l) in Sym(d) is a permutation with cyclic structure
[l, 1, . . . , 1], with l ≤d, that is, a permutation that can be written in the form
(a1 a2 · · · al), for l distinct integers a1, . . . , al ∈{1, . . . , d}. Occasionally, and by
some abuse of notation, we can consider such a cycle as an element in Sym(m)
for any m ≥l, acting as the identity on m −l elements, and cyclically on the
rest.
• The identity idd ∈Sym(d) is considered to be a cycle of length 1, as it may be
written as idd = (x) for any x ∈{1, . . . , d}.
