ON STATIONARY ACTIONS OF LOCALLY COMPACT GROUPS AND THEIR
RADON–NIKODYM COCYCLES
NACHI AVRAHAM-RE’EM AND MICHAEL BJ¨ORKLUND
Abstract. We study stationary actions of locally compact measured groups through the structure and
regularity of their Radon–Nikodym cocycles.
We start with two dynamical consequences of stationarity. Extending a theorem of Furstenberg–
Glasner from discrete groups to noncompact lcsc groups, we show that every stationary action is con-
servative. Thus stationary actions are never of type I. We then show that an ergodic stationary action
admitting an absolutely continuous invariant σ-finite measure is in fact probability preserving. Thus
stationary actions are never of type II8. Using a construction of Katznelson–Weiss and Vaes–Verjans,
we show that if a group admits a stationary action of type III1, then it admits stationary actions of
every type IIIλ, 0 ď λ ď 1.
The second part concerns the regularity of the Radon–Nikodym cocycle. We introduce the harmonic
majorant on normalized positive harmonic functions, which gives Harnack-type control of Radon–
Nikodym derivatives of stationary actions.
For compactly supported probability measures with an
Lp-density for some p ą 1, we prove that the harmonic majorant is finite and locally bounded. As a
consequence, such measured groups admit a universal compact Radon–Nikodym model: a single com-
pact G-space with a continuous cocycle into which stationary actions can be realized, so that the ambi-
ent cocycle gives a version of its Radon–Nikodym cocycle. This strengthens the Mackey–Varadarajan
compact model theorem by incorporating the Radon–Nikodym cocycle into the model.
By contrast, we construct a random walk on the real affine group whose Poisson boundary fails
Kaimanovich’s SAT˚ property: the Poisson kernel is unbounded arbitrarily close to the identity. There-
fore, Harnack’s inequality already fails for positive harmonic functions.
In particular, this Poisson
boundary admits no topological model with continuous Poisson kernel.

1. Introduction and main results
Let pG, θq be a measured group, that is, a locally compact second countable group equipped with an
admissible probability measure. A stationary pG, θq-space is a probability space pX, µq such that X is a
measurable G-space and µ satisfies
θ ˚ µ “ µ.
Stationary actions were introduced by Furstenberg in the study of random walks and Poisson bound-
aries, and they provide a natural nonsingular counterpart to measure preserving actions in settings where
invariant probability measures need not exist [8,18,19,40]. When G is discrete countable, a substantial
part of the theory is by now classical. In the locally compact setting, however, questions of recurrence,
absolutely continuous invariant measures, and regularity of the Radon–Nikodym cocycle become gen-
uinely analytic and topological in nature. The purpose of this paper is to study stationary actions of
locally compact groups through the structure and regularity of their Radon–Nikodym cocycles.

We begin with conservativity. Basic aspects of conservativity for nonsingular actions of locally compact
groups were clarified recently in [2], and one form of this property is Poincar´e recurrence, by which for
every positive measure set A in X, there are unboundedly many g P G satisfying µ pg.A X Aq ą 0.
Furstenberg–Glasner proved that stationary actions of countably infinite groups are Poincar´e recurrent
[20, §1]. Our first main result extends the recurrence of stationary actions to locally compact groups.
Theorem 1.1. Every stationary action of a noncompact measured group is conservative.
Viewed through the Krieger classification of nonsingular actions, this theorem says that ergodic sta-
tionary actions of noncompact measured groups are never of type I. The second structural result concerns
absolutely continuous invariant measures. For Poisson boundaries it is well-known that they are absent
(see [28, Theorem 3.2.1]); we show that this is in fact a general feature of stationary actions.
Theorem 1.2. An ergodic stationary action is either probability preserving or of type III, namely it
admits no absolutely continuous invariant σ-finite measure.
Thus ergodic stationary actions never exhibit type II8.
This gives a first rigidity statement for
stationary dynamics. Later we complement it by adapting a construction of Katznelson–Weiss [34, §5]
(for G “ Z) and Vaes–Verjans [43] (for general G) to show that, once a group admits a stationary action
of type III1, it also admits stationary actions of all types IIIλ, 0 ď λ ď 1.

4. Absolutely continuous invariant measure
Let G be an lcsc group and pX, µq a nonsingular G-space.
A Borel σ-finite measure m on X is
called a.c.i.m. of pX, µq if it is absolutely continuous with respect to µ and G-invariant. The question
whether a nonsingular G-space admits an a.c.i.m. is fundamental in ergodic theory and was addressed
by many authors. See e.g. [13] and the many references therein. In the common terminology, an ergodic
nonsingular G-space is called type II1 if it admits a finite a.c.i.m.; type II8 if it admits an infinite
a.c.i.m.; and type III if it admits no a.c.i.m.
For stationary pG, θq-spaces more is known. First, it was shown by Bader–Shalom [3, Prop. 2.6] that
every two ergodic θ-stationary probability measures on a Borel G-space X are either mutually singular or
equal. In particular, an ergodic stationary pG, θq-space of type II1 must be itself probability preserving.

following we give a general answer to this, generalizing Theorem 1.2:

probability preserving, or otherwise type III. More generally, every stationary pG, θq-space pX, µq admits

admits no a.c.i.m.

7. Compact Radon–Nikodym models
Topological models of measurable systems are a fundamental tool used widely across measurable
dynamics, and in particular in stationary dynamics; see e.g. [19, §8], [20, §3], [3, Thm. 2.1]. The goal is
to view a given measurable dynamical system as an invariant subset of a compact space with continuous
dynamics. It is of particular interest, especially in descriptive set theoretic aspects of Borel dynamics, to
find for a given group a universal compact metric space in which all topological models of its measurable
actions are realized; see e.g. [5,6,25]. The existence of such a universal model for actions of lcsc groups
is a classical result due to Mackey [38, §2] and Varadarajan [44, Thm. 5.7] (see [48, Thm. 2.1.19]).
Theorem 7.1 (Mackey–Varadarajan’s Compact model theorem). Every lcsc group G admits a compact
G-space Y “ Y pGq (i.e. a compact metric space with a continuous action of G), such that every Borel
G-space X admits a compact model in Y, that is, a G-equivariant measurable injective map κ : X Ñ Y.
In the presence of a quasi-invariant measure on X, it is natural to ask for a compact model in which
not only the action is continuous, but also the Radon–Nikodym cocycle can be taken continuous. In
Poisson boundaries finding such a model is a classical topic going back to the seminal work of Furstenberg
on semisimple Lie groups [17, 18], and its many extensions; see e.g. [29, §2.8], [28], [8, §4], [16, §3.10]).
Here we show that in many common situations this can be done for all stationary actions. Let us give a
precise definition of the object we seek for.
