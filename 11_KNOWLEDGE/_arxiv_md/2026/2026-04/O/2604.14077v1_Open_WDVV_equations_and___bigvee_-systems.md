OPEN WDVV EQUATIONS AND ⋁-SYSTEMS
ALESSANDRO PROSERPIO AND IAN A. B. STRACHAN
Abstract. The idea of a ⋁-system was introduced by Veselov in the study of rational solutions
of the WDVV equations of associativity. These are algebraic/geometric conditions on the set of
covectors that appear in rational solutions to the WDVV equations. Here, this idea is generalized
to open WDVV equations, which are an additional set of PDEs originating from open Gromow-
Witten Theory. We develop – for rank-one extensions – algebraic/geometric conditions on the
covectors that supplement the ⋁-system to give rational solutions to the open WDVV equations.
Examples, and the relation to superpotentials and to Dubrovin almost-duality, are given.

generalization have been introduced, for example:
- generalized Frobenius manifolds [LQZ25];

2.3. ⋁-systems
The notion of a ⋁-system was introduced by A. Veselov in [Ves99]. These are collections of
covectors on a fixed finite-dimensional real vector space V , satisfying some conditions, each produ-
cing a solution to the WDVV equations of dual type. The key observation under this construction
was that the dual-type solutions associated to the Saito Frobenius manifolds on orbit spaces of
Weyl groups [Dub98] could all be written in a closed form that depends on the corresponding
root system. Generalising this construction, then, one starts from a given set of covectors – which
determine a hyperplane arrangement in the corresponding vector space – and associates to it a
function of the same form as the ones coming from root systems. The ⋁-conditions are require-
ments on the hyperplane arrangement making sure that such a function is a solution to the WDVV
equations. In general, this solution will not be weighted-homogeneous, therefore it is of dual type.
Of course, the standard root systems are recovered, but ⋁-systems are a strictly larger class of
systems of covectors. The WDVV solutions associated to ⋁-systems in the original work by Veselov
are nowadays mostly known as rational solutions, as various generalisations have been introduced,
see e.g. [Fei06,FV07,FV08,Fei09,Str10,SV14,FV18,AF19,AF21,SS21,FKS24].

6.
Conclusion
The examples constructed in the last section were all based on Coxeter groups and hence all
have A = RW . In addition, as well as being derived from an orbit-space/Saito-construction, they
all can also be derived from Hurwitz-Frobenius manifolds and hence fall under the construction
given in Theorem 2.3. Other classes of examples may also be found by introducing multiplicities
and/or generalized root systems.
