Next, let us outline the proof of stable degeneration for klt singularities.
Existence.
The existence of a c
vol-minimizer is first proven in [Blu18].
To find a
critical point of a functional F, possibly enlarging the domain of definition, one first finds
a sequence such that the values of F approach the infimum. One then shows that the
approximation sequence admits a limit (compactness), which lies in the original space
(regularity) such that the infimum is actually achieved (continuity).
For the approximation step, a key observation of [Liu18] is that the infimum in (1.1) is
equal to the infimum of the normalized multiplicity of all graded sequences of m-primary
ideals or filtrations a•, that is,
c

The existence of a limit point of the approximating sequence is achieved by the generic
limit construction; see, for example, [dFM09, Kol08].
The idea is to parameterize all

one needs some uniform control for the convergence rate of the volume.

Compactness follows from a combination of the boundedness of complements and a
generic limit argument. Indeed, it is not hard to see that any Koll´ar component S is a
log canonical place of some Q-complement and hence of some N-complement for some N

one is still not done since the above statement does not yield a finite-dimensional, thus
compact, parameter space. The solution is to show, using a properness estimate, that all
information can be extracted up to the quotient by a fixed power of the maximal ideal.
Note that the argument yields another proof of the existence of the minimizer.
Finite generation. The finite generation problem turns out to be the most subtle
part of the stable degeneration conjecture. In the quasi-regular case, namely when the
minimizer v∗is divisorial, finite generation is known by [LX20, Blu18], which follows
essentially from [BCHM10]. In dimension 2, it is known by [Cut18] (see also [LLX20]),
essentially because a non-divisorial minimizer in this case is toric. The finite generation
for a general higher rank valuation is settled in [XZ25], which is a local analog of the
theory called higher rank finite generation established in [LXZ22]. The strategy is to
show that the minimizer of the functional is a special valuation (which is quasi-monomial
a priori), whose associated graded ring is then shown to be finitely generated.
Recently, [Che25] has provided an elegant and more algebraic approach to the finite
generation problem, which works in a slightly more general setting.
2. Applications
Next, we move on to a brief discussion of some applications of stable degeneration for
singularities.
2.1. Ingredients and corollaries. First, we discuss two byproducts of the proofs in the
last section, as well as an immediate corollary of the stable degeneration theorem.

3. Generalizations and discussions
3.1. Stable degeneration for Fano fibration germs. We next turn to a generalization
of the stable degeneration theory to the setting of Fano fibration germs, for which a
similar 2-step degeneration framework was conjectured in [SZ24]. A (log) Fano fibration
f : (X, ∆) →Z is a surjective projective morphism between normal varieties such that
(0) f is a fibration, i.e., f∗OX = OZ,
(1) (X, ∆) has klt singularities, and
(2) −(KX + ∆) is ample over Z.
A Fano fibration germ consists of a Fano fibration f : (X, ∆) →Z, where Z is affine,
together with a fixed closed point o ∈Z.
As one easily sees, a projective klt pair (X, ∆) is automatically a Fano fibration germ
over Spec C, and a klt singularity x ∈(X, ∆) (where we assume that X is affine by
convention) is a Fano fibration germ with respect to the identity morphism. Therefore,
Fano fibration germs can be viewed as a natural interpolation between klt Fano pairs and
klt singularities. Moreover, for any (KX + ∆)-negative contraction (X, ∆) →Z and any
closed point z ∈Z, we get a Fano fibration germ (X, ∆) →Z ∋z after replacing Z with
an affine neighborhood of z.

over Z. Let R := ⊕m∈rZ>0Rm, where Rm := H0(X, mL) is a finite R0-module. Write
n = dim X and m ⊂R0 for the maximal ideal of o ∈Z.
