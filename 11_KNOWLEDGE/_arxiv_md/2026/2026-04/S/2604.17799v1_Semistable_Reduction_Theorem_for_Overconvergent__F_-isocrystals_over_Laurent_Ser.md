We prove the semistable reduction theorem for E†
K-valued and K-valued overconvergent F-
isocrystals over k((t))-varieties which were introduced by Lazda and P´al. As an application, we
prove the finite dimensionality of E†

Introduction
Fix a field k of characteristic p > 0 and a complete discrete valuation field K of mixed character-
istic whose residue field is k. Suppose for simplicity that k is perfect. Let us recall the “classical”
semistable reduction theorem proved by Kedlaya in the series of papers [16][17][18][19]: for a closed
immersion Z ,→X of algebraic varieties over k with X−Z smooth and an F-isocrystal E over X−Z
overconvergent along Z, there is an alteration f : X′ →X such that for Z′ := f−1(Z)red, (X′, Z′) is
a smooth pair and f∗E extends to a locally free convergent log F-isocrystal over (X′, Z′). This the-
orem has many important applications, especially in rigid cohomology and arithmetic D-modules.
Shiho [26] has shown that it implies finite dimensionality of rigid cohomology with coefficients,
although it was proved in a more straighforward way in [14]. Caro and Tsuzuki [6] proved that
overconvergent F-isocrystals are overholonomic, which critically depends on the theorem.
Fur-
thermore Lazda [23] proved that there is a canonical equivalence between the derived category
of constructible isocrystals of Frobenius type and that of overholonomic complexes of arithmetic
D-modules of Frobenius type, which also uses the semistable reduction theorem in an essential way.
Let us move to the case of varieties over k((t)). In the “classical” setting, one has to find a
complete discrete valuation field whose residue field is k((t)), for which the Amice ring EK stands.
The main feature in this setting is that one can consider derivations by t and hence “absolute”
overconvergent isocrystals. For a k((t))-variety X and an absolute overconvergent isocrystal E on
X, Hi
rig(X/EK; E) has the structure of ∇-module over EK induced by Gauss-Manin connection.
Also, when E has a Frobenius structure, these rigid cohomology groups are (σ, ∇)-modules over
EK. However, from the viewpoint of p-adic representations, those objects are not really “geometric”
and they should be defined over the Robba ring RK instead of EK. Indeed, (σ, ∇)-modules over
RK are quasi-unipotent by p-adic local monodromy theorem.
Lazda and P´al [24] defined rigid cohomology and overconvergent isocrystals valued over the
bounded Robba ring E†
K = EK ∩RK to resolve this problem. The idea is to embed a k((t))-variety
into proper k[[t]]-schemes, not into proper k((t))-schemes. This slight modification seems subtle, but
technically it causes some problems in proving the same results as the “classical” case; for example,
it seems impossible to show finiteness of compactly supported E†
K-valued rigid cohomology with

The main idea of proving the semistable reduction theorem for E†

isocrystals is to reduce the problem by invoking “descent of unipotence” to the classical case proved
by Kedlaya. This fact is well-known in the context; it roughly states that for some sort of a smooth
weakly complete algebra A and for a ∇-module M over the Robba ring RA, M is unipotent if and
only if the base change M ⊗RA R b

type of statement appears in [14, Proposition 5.4.1] and [24, Proposition 3.42] for example. We
generalize this statement in a more geometric way in §3.4.
In this paper, following [16] we prove that for a strictly semistable pair (Y, Z) and an isocrystal
E on Y −Z overconvergent along Z, E extends to a log isocrystal along a component Zi of Z if and
only if E has unipotent monodromy along Zi. Locally choosing a lift Y of Y , ]Zi[Y is isomorphic to
the relative annulus ]Zi[Zi×A1[0, 1) and E induces some ∇-module Ei over ]Zi[Zi×A1(η, 1). If E has
unipotent monodromy along Zi, i.e., Ei is unipotent, then it extends uniquely to a log ∇-module
over ]Zi[Zi×A1[0, 1).
We now apply descent of unipotence here to conclude that an E†
K-valued
overconvergent isocrystal is log-extendable if and only if so is its completion bE, which is an EK-
valued overconvergent isocrystal. The latter is already known to hold after alteration by Kedlaya,
and the proof is almost done.
The K-valued case needs additional consideration on components of the closed fiber. As ex-
plained in [16, §7], it does not work to take an alteration so that after pullback the isocrystal has
unipotent monodromy by using p-adic local monodromy theorem because the alteration may pro-
duce exceptional divisors on the source which do not dominate any components of the boundary on
the target. In order to control the monodromy, we use some recent results of Kedlaya [20], in which
he gives a new simpler proof of the semistable reduction theorem.

p-adic cohomology and we will give a small application to the theory of rigid cohomology. Lazda-
