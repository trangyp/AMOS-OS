STABLE RANK ONE, TRACIAL LOCAL HOMOGENEITY
AND UNIFORM PROPERTY Γ
ANDREA VACCARO
Abstract. We prove that separable, simple, unital, non-elementary,
stably finite C∗-algebras that have stable rank one, and that have locally
finite nuclear dimension in a tracial sense, have uniform property Γ. In
particular, Villadsen algebras of the first type and crossed products of
free minimal actions of FC (in particular, abelian) groups on compact
metric spaces have uniform property Γ. This implies that all these C∗-
algebras satisfy the Toms–Winter conjecture, a fact already known for
C∗-algebras with stable rank one and locally finite nuclear dimension,
and here recovered via a different approach.
In their celebrated last work on rings of operators [MvN43], Murray
and von Neumann used the existence of non-trivial asymptotically central
sequences as an invariant to distinguish the hyperfinite II1 factor from the
free group factor, thereby providing the first examples of non-isomorphic II1
factors. The notion indicating the existence of such sequences was simply
labelled property Γ, being one among several properties considered in the
paper—the others expressing equivalent formulations of what now would be
called hyperfiniteness—and indexed by letters.
Property Γ would later come to play a key role in the subject, thanks to the
emergence of central sequence algebras as a fundamental tool in the theory,
beginning with the work of McDuff [McD70], and it has since remained an
important concept in von Neumann algebras. This notion eventually found
its way into the setting of stably finite C∗-algebras, with an adaptation,
uniform property Γ (Theorem 1.1), introduced in [CET+21] as an auxiliary
tool used to confirm one of the implications of the Toms–Winter conjecture.
Although its formulation is, mutatis mutandis, formally close to that of its
counterpart for II1 factors, uniform property Γ underlies a much more complex
and less apparent form of regularity. Broadly speaking, uniform property Γ
expresses a powerful form of approximately central tracial divisibility, and it
has been observed to have strong effects on the structure of the C∗-algebras
that satisfy it (see [CCE+]). Shortly after its introduction, its connection
to the Toms–Winter conjecture—which asserts that for separable, simple,
non-elementary, nuclear C∗-algebras the three key regularity conditions Z-
stability, finite nuclear dimension and strict comparison are equivalent (see
[STW25, Conjecture 5])—was further reinforced in [CETW22], where it was
discovered that the conjecture holds true under the assumption of uniform
property Γ, naturally prompting the following question.
Problem ([CETW22, Question C], [STW25, Problem XIX]). Do all sepa-
rable, simple, unital, non-elementary (i.e. non-isomorphic to the compact
operators) nuclear, stably finite C∗-algebras have uniform property Γ?

This problem remains open and, to the best of our knowledge, there are
essentially three, or rather four, routes to establishing uniform property Γ.
The first—and farthest from optimal—is assuming Z-stability (see
[CET+21, Proposition 2.3]). The second, possible if the given C∗-algebra
is nuclear, is requiring that the trace space is tractable in a topological
sense—more precisely, that the space of extremal traces is compact and
has finite covering dimension. This was proved in [KR14,TWW15,Sat12]
and more recently generalized under weaker assumptions even beyond the
nuclear setting in [ES25]. The third method—perhaps the least known among
non-experts—is highlighted in [CETW22, Theorem 5.5] and is due to Winter,
who proved in [Win12] that tracial almost divisibility (see Theorem 1.3)
implies uniform property Γ for simple C∗-algebras that have locally finite
nuclear dimension, that is C∗-algebras that can be locally approximated by
C∗-algebras with finite nuclear dimension (Theorem 1.5).
The fourth and last route only applies to C∗-algebras that are crossed
products of topological dynamical systems, and it is due to Kerr and Szabó.
They proved in [KS20] that free actions of countably infinite amenable groups
on compact metric spaces generate crossed products with uniform property
Γ, if they satisfy the small boundary property (see [KS20, Definition 5.1]), a
dynamical formulation of zero-dimensionality which was later shown to be
actually equivalent to a relative version of uniform property Γ for the Cartan
pairs generated by the crossed products [KLTV26].
The following question points to a remarkable blind spot in the state
of the art: are there examples of simple nuclear C∗-algebras with uniform
property Γ that are non-Z-stable and have intractable trace space? A natural
test case is the class of non-Z-stable Villadsen algebras of the first type
from [Vil98]—their trace space is the Poulsen simplex, by [ELN24, Theorem
4.5]—for which this problem has been open for some years and more recently
advertised in [STW25, Problem XX]. Other natural candidates to consider
are the non-Z-stable crossed products of free minimal Z-actions obtained in
[GK10], which cannot have tractable trace spaces by [EN24, Theorem 1.4].
In this paper we finally settle the problem of uniform property Γ for these
and other C∗-algebras, solving affirmatively [STW25, Problem XX].
Theorem A (Theorem 4.1, Theorem 4.6). Suppose that A is either
(1) A simple, unital, non-elementary AH-algebra with stable rank one,
for instance a non-elementary Villadsen algebra of the first type.
(2) A crossed product of a free minimal action by a countably infinite FC
(e.g. abelian) group on a compact metric space.
Then A has uniform property Γ.
Theorem A is a corollary of the following more general result, which permits
to establish uniform property Γ for a vast class of nuclear C∗-algebras, under
the assumption of stable rank one.
Theorem B (Theorem 2.4). Let A be a separable, simple, unital, non-
elementary, stably finite C∗-algebra with stable rank one and tracially locally
finite nuclear dimension (Theorem 1.5). Then A has uniform property Γ.
We remark that, by [CETW22, Theorem A], the C∗-algebras in Theorem B
verify the Toms–Winter conjecture. This was already proved in [Thi20] for

The argument for Theorem B is composed of three main steps. The first
is contained in the recent preprint [Fu25], where it is proved that separable,
simple, unital C∗-algebras with stable rank one have tracial approximate
oscillation zero, which is equivalent to say that their tracial ultrapowers
have real rank zero. This work refines the earlier [FL25], where the same
statement is deduced under the additional assumption of strict comparison.
The second ingredient—a tracial variant of a result due to Zhang from
[Zha91]—is Theorem 2.2, where it is shown that if A is a simple, unital,
non-elementary, stably finite C∗-algebra whose tracial ultrapower has real
rank zero, then A is tracially almost divisible.
Finally, the last ingredient, already mentioned in this introduction, is
Winter’s result from [Win12] stating that tracial almost divisibility, combined
with locally finite nuclear dimension, implies uniform property Γ. Winter’s
argument also applies to C∗-algebras that have tracially locally finite nuclear
dimension, namely those C∗-algebras for which every finite subset can be
approximated, in the uniform tracial norm, by some subalgebra with finite
nuclear dimension, bringing us back to the statement of Theorem B.
We remark that it is not known whether nuclear C∗-algebras that do
not have locally finite nuclear dimension exist, so the reduction to tracial
locally finite nuclear dimension could potentially be—at least in the nuclear
setting—inconsequential1. Nevertheless, tracially locally finite nuclear di-
mension comes in handy when deducing the second part of Theorem A from
Theorem B.
Indeed, while the first part of Theorem A immediately follows from The-
orem B—AH-algebra clearly have locally finite nuclear dimension, being
locally homogeneous, and Villadsen algebras of the first type are well-known
to have stable rank one by [Vil98, Proposition 10]—the author is not aware
of a straightforward proof showing that crossed products of dynamical sys-
tems have locally finite nuclear dimension. Nevertheless, a technical result
due to Niu from [Niu22] (which we will review in detail in Theorem 4.5)
shows that actions with the uniform Rokhlin property (abbreviated URP,
see Theorem 4.4) generate crossed products that enjoy a tracial form of ap-
proximate homogeneity, from which tracially locally finite nuclear dimension
immediately follows.
To finish the proof of the second part of Theorem A, one can finally rely
on the breakthrough paper [LN20] (see also [BGK25]), which establishes
stable rank one for crossed products of free minimal topological dynamical
system that have the URP and comparison in the sense of [Ker20, Definition
3.2]. The fact that the combination of these two conditions is automatic for
actions as in Theorem A is but one of the many striking results obtained in
[Nar24].

To better explain this, it is instructive to draw a parallel between Kerr
and Szabó’s proof and Winter’s argument in [Win12] that tracial almost
divisibility and locally finite nuclear dimension imply uniform property Γ. In
both cases, one starts with some non-central version of tracial divisibility—
tracial almost divisibility in Winter’s work, and the small boundary property
in Kerr and Szabó’s—and upgrades it to an approximately central form of
divisibility—almost finiteness in measure in [KS20]—via a strong variant of
amenability. In Winter’s case the strong variant of amenability is locally
finite nuclear dimension, as opposed to nuclearity, while in [KS20] this comes
from the amenability of the acting group, rather than merely that of the
action, which alone would not imply a Følner-type condition like almost
finiteness in measure.
In summary, Theorem 4.6 recovers [KS20, Theorem 9.4] through Winter’s
method, crucially leveraging the small boundary property—or just the weaker
URP—to obtain tracial almost divisibility and tracially locally finite nuclear
dimension, while completely bypassing almost finiteness in measure (a similar
approach, using comparison, can be found in [Niu21,LN20]).
Summary of the paper. Preliminaries are covered in §1. In §2 we show
that having a tracial ultrapower with real rank zero implies tracial almost
divisibility for simple C∗-algebras, and thus uniform property Γ if in addition
tracially locally finite nuclear dimension is assumed. We then use this to
prove Theorem B. In §3 we introduce tracially LTH-algebra with locally
flat dimension growth and we prove that they have, when simple, tracial
ultrapowers with real rank zero, from which we deduce uniform property Γ.
In §4 we put all pieces together and prove Theorem A.

The second part of Theorem A can be recovered via [Nar24, Corollary E],
which shows that all free minimal actions of FC groups—that is of groups
with finite conjugacy classes—have the URP. Moreover, Naryshkin’s theorem
yields comparison in the sense of [Ker20, Definition 3.2] for such actions. As
a consequence, uniform property Γ for the corresponding crossed products
could also be deduced by Theorem 2.4 using the main result of [LN20],
where Li and Niu show that the crossed products arising from free minimal
dynamical systems of amenable groups with the URP and comparison (or,
more precisely, the more operator algebraic Cuntz comparison on open sets
[LN20, Definition 2.21]) have stable rank one (see also [BGK25] for an
extension of those techniques beyond the nuclear setting).
It is worth noting, however, that this ‘stable rank one approach’ does
not, a priori, fully recover Theorem 4.6, as it requires comparison—we say a
priori since no example of topological dynamical system without comparison
is currently known.
