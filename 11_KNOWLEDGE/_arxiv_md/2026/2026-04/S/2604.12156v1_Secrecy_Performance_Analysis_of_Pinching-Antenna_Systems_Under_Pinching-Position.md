Secrecy Performance Analysis of Pinching-Antenna
Systems Under Pinching-Position Uncertainty
Saeid Pakravan, Imene Trigui, Wessam Ajib, Wei-Ping Zhu
Abstract—This paper investigates the secrecy performance
of pinching-antenna systems (PAS) under practical pinching-
position activation uncertainty. By dynamically selecting the
radiation point along a dielectric waveguide, PAS enables low-
cost spatial reconfigurability and enhanced secure transmission.
Unlike existing studies that assume ideal activation control, we
account for spatial inaccuracies caused by hardware limitations
and environmental perturbations, which induce statistical de-
pendence between the legitimate and eavesdropping channels.
To capture this effect, a copula-based framework is employed
to model the joint distribution of the corresponding signal-to-
noise ratios (SNRs), and approximate expressions for the secrecy
outage probability (SOP) are derived. Simulation results validate
the theoretical findings and demonstrate that PAS retains robust
secrecy performance compared with conventional fixed-antenna
systems, even in the presence of activation uncertainty.
Index Terms—Pinching-antenna systems, physical layer secu-
rity, secrecy outage probability, position uncertainty.
I. INTRODUCTION
The rapid expansion of intelligent mobile devices, mission-
critical services, and location-aware applications has height-
ened the demand for secure, reliable, and flexible communi-
cation in beyond-fifth-generation (B5G) and sixth-generation
(6G) wireless networks [1]. These networks are expected to
support dense connectivity, low-latency, and robust security
performance, particularly in challenging propagation condi-
tions and cost-constrained deployments. While technologies
such as massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO), re-
configurable intelligent surfaces (RIS), and adaptive antenna
architectures enhance spatial degrees of freedom (DoF) and
network controllability [2]–[4], their practical deployment is
often limited by hardware complexity, power consumption,
and physical design constraints. These limitations have mo-
tivated the exploration of alternative antenna architectures that
enable spatial reconfigurability with reduced radio-frequency
(RF) complexity.
In this context, pinching-antenna systems (PAS) have re-
cently emerged as an attractive architectural paradigm [5],
[6]. A PAS employs a dielectric waveguide along which a
small dielectric particle can be “pinched,” thereby creating a

reconfigure its effective antenna position without mechanically
rotating physical antenna elements or deploying additional
RF chains. This spatial adaptability reduces the propagation
distance to the intended receiver, alleviates blockage effects,
enhance line-of-sight (LoS) probability, and improve spatial
diversity, while incurring low hardware complexity and power
consumption [7]. These properties make PAS well suited
for lightweight, power-constrained, and cost-efficient wireless
platforms envisioned in next-generation networks.
Motivated by these advantages, recent studies have explored
PAS in a range of wireless communication scenarios. Initial
works focused on performance gains achievable through dy-
namic antenna repositioning; for instance, [8] showed that
optimizing the activation point in a downlink PAS can sig-
nificantly enhance user rate. Multi-user extensions in [9]
employed joint antenna positioning and resource allocation
to improve system energy efficiency. PAS has also been
investigated in emerging areas such as integrated sensing and
communication, where its LoS-enhancing capability supports
simultaneous sensing and data transmission [10]. In non-
orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) systems, their reconfig-
urability enables adaptive spatial separation and improved in-
terference management [11], [12]. Moreover, recent studies on
physical-layer security (PLS) [13]–[15] show that positioning
the radiating point near the legitimate receiver can provide
notable secrecy gains over conventional fixed-antenna designs.
Despite these advances, most existing studies focus on
antenna positioning or resource-allocation strategies, while
the theoretical aspects of PAS-enabled secure communication
remain largely unexplored. Prior studies often assume perfect
control over the antenna activation point, neglecting practical
spatial inaccuracies arising from estimation errors, hardware
constraints, or propagation uncertainties. Such imperfections
introduce statistical coupling between the legitimate and eaves-
dropping channels, which cannot be captured by traditional
independence-based secrecy models. To the best of our knowl-
edge, no existing work provides a rigorous analytical char-
acterization of the induced correlation or offers a principled
framework for quantifying the resulting secrecy degradation
under practical PAS operation. Motivated by this gap, we
present a secrecy analysis for PAS-assisted systems that incor-
porates pinching-position uncertainty, derives exact marginal
SNR distributions, models SNR dependence via copula theory,
and evaluates its impact on secrecy outage performance (SOP).

II. SYSTEM MODEL
We consider the downlink of a secure wireless system
consisting of a base station (BS), a legitimate user (Bob), and
a passive eavesdropper (Eve), as shown in Fig. 1. Bob and Eve
are equipped with single fixed-position antennas, while the BS
employs a dielectric waveguide with a reconfigurable pinching
radiator. By dynamically activating a radiating point along the
waveguide, the BS aims to enhance Bob’s link quality relative
to Eve’s, thereby improving overall secrecy performance.
The waveguide is mounted parallel to the x–axis at height h,
and the active pinching point has coordinates ψp = (xp, 0, h),
where xp denotes the horizontal activation position. Bob and
Eve are randomly distributed within a square service area of
side length D, with locations ψB = (x1, y1, 0) and ψE =
(x2, y2, 0), respectively, where xi, yi ∼Unif[−D/2, D/2],
i ∈{1, 2}. The waveguide feed point ψ0 = (−D/2, 0, h)

the active pinching point and each receiver. For analytical
simplicity, waveguide attenuation is neglected by setting the
absorption coefficient to α = 0, so that the dominant path
loss depends on the distance from the pinching point to each
user. Since Eve is passive, the BS is assumed to have channel
state information (CSI) only for Bob. Ideally, the pinching

xp = x1, which maximizes his received signal strength, but in

IV. SIMULATION RESULTS
This section presents numerical results to validate the de-
rived analytical expressions and assess the secrecy perfor-
mance of PAS under pinching-position uncertainty. Unless
otherwise stated, the service area is a square of side D = 20 m,
the waveguide height is h = 5 m, and the target secrecy rate
is Rth = 0.5 bit/s/Hz. The carrier frequency is fc = 28 GHz
with an effective refractive index of neff = 1.4, and the
receiver noise power is σ2 = −90 dBm. The Gaussian–
Chebyshev quadrature used in Theorem 1 employs N = 200
nodes to ensure numerical stability, and Monte Carlo (MC)
simulations are generated from 104 random realizations. For
comparison, a conventional fixed-antenna configuration, where
the BS radiates from a static location, is simulated as a
benchmark.
Fig. 2a depicts the SOP versus effective transmit SNR.
The results demonstrate that the PAS operating under ideal
pinching conditions consistently achieves a lower SOP than
the fixed-antenna benchmark across all SNRs, thereby high-
lighting the inherent secrecy advantage enabled by spatial
reconfigurability. Meanwhile, as the level of pinching-position
uncertainty increases, SOP gradually deteriorates, indicating
that positioning errors adversely affect secrecy performance by
reducing the effective channel gain of Bob and strengthening
the statistical coupling between the legitimate and eavesdrop-
ping channels.

ideal pinching conditions, while higher uncertainty result in

of spatial focusing.
V. CONCLUSION
This paper investigated the secrecy performance of PAS
under practical pinching-position uncertainty. By explicitly
modeling the induced statistical dependence between legiti-
mate and eavesdropping channels, closed-form expression for
the SOP was derived using a Gaussian-copula framework
and Gauss–Chebyshev quadrature approximation. Numerical
results validate the analysis and show that increasing position
uncertainty raises the SOP by weakening Bob’s link and
strengthening its dependence with Eve. Nevertheless, PAS
consistently achieves secrecy gains over fixed-antenna systems
across relevant operating regimes.
