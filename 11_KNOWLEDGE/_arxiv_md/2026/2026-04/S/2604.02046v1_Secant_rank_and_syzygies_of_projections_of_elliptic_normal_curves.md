SECANT RANK AND SYZYGIES OF PROJECTIONS OF ELLIPTIC
NORMAL CURVES
CHANGHO HAN AND EUISUNG PARK
Abstract. We study the syzygies of projections of elliptic normal curves. Let C ⊂
Pd−1 be an elliptic normal curve of degree d ≥5, and let Cq denote the projection of
C from a point q. We obtain sharp bounds for the Green–Lazarsfeld index of Cq in
terms of the secant rank of q. More precisely, if q ∈Cs \ C2, where Cs is the s-th
secant variety of C, then index(Cq) ≤s −3, and equality holds for a general point q
of Cs. In particular, index(Cq) = ⌈d
2⌉−3 for a general point q in Pd−1. The proof
realizes projected elliptic curves as hyperplane sections of elliptic ruled surface scrolls
and exploits the known syzygetic properties of these scrolls.
1. Introduction
Throughout this paper we work over an algebraically closed field K of characteristic
zero.
The modern study of syzygies of projective varieties began with the foundational work
of Mark Green [Gre84], who introduced Koszul cohomology as a tool for understanding
minimal free resolutions of homogeneous coordinate rings.
For a projective variety
X ⊂Pr, one says that X satisfies property N2,p if the homogeneous ideal of X is
generated by quadrics and the syzygies among them are generated by linear syzygies
up to the p-th step. The maximal such integer p is called the Green–Lazarsfeld index
of X and is denoted by index(X); by convention, index(X) = 0 if X fails to satisfy
property N2,1. It is well known that
index(X) < δ(X),
where δ(X) denotes the largest integer p such that X admits a (p + 2)-secant p-plane
(cf. [EGHP05, Theorem 1.1]).
Now let C ⊂Pd−g be a linearly normal smooth projective curve of genus g and
degree d ≥2g + 2. Green [Gre84] proved that C satisfies property N2,d−2g−1. Green
and Lazarsfeld [GL88] gave another proof and characterized the extremal case.
In
particular, index(C) ≥d −2g −1, and equality holds for all d ≥3g −2.
Recent
developments have further clarified the role of secant geometry in syzygies of projective
curves. The Green–Lazarsfeld secant conjecture predicts that the failure of property Np
for a curve embedded by a sufficiently positive line bundle is governed by the existence of
(p+2)-secant p-planes. This conjecture has been confirmed in several cases: Farkas and
Kemeny [FK16] proved the generic version of the Green–Lazarsfeld secant conjecture,
while Kemeny [Kem17] established the extremal case for curves of arbitrary gonality.

Another natural problem is to understand how the syzygies of a projective curve
change under linear projections. For q ∈Pd−g \ C2, let
Cq := πq(C) ⊂Pd−g−1
be the projection of C from q. When d ≥2g + 3, Cq is always 3-regular by [Nom02,
Theorem 1], so its homogeneous ideal is generated by quadratic and cubic polynomials.
It is therefore natural to ask: how does the Green–Lazarsfeld index of Cq depend on
the position of the projection center q?
To measure the position of q relative to C, we use secant varieties. Recall that the
s-th secant variety Cs of C is the Zariski closure of the union of linear spans of s general
points on C; these varieties form a strictly increasing filtration
C ⊂C2 ⊂C3 ⊂· · · ⊂C⌈d−g+1
2
⌉= Pd−g
with dim Cs = 2s −1. For every q ∈Pd−g, the secant rank of q with respect to C is
defined by
rkC(q) := min{s | q ∈Cs}.
Suppose that q is a general point of Cs for some s ≥3. Then rkC(q) = s, and q is
contained in an (s −1)-plane spanned by s points of C. The latter property implies
that the projected curve Cq admits an s-secant (s −2)-plane and hence
index(Cq) ≤s −3.
Combining this observation with upper semicontinuity of graded Betti numbers, we
obtain the following result.
Theorem 1.1. Let C ⊂Pd−g be a linearly normal smooth projective curve of genus g
and degree d ≥2g + 3, and let q ∈Pd−g \ C2 be a point. Then
index(Cq) ≤rkC(q) −3.
When C ⊂Pd is a rational normal curve, the second-named author showed in [Par07a]
that the inequality of Theorem 1.1 is an equality: for every q ∈Pd \ C2,
index(Cq) = rkC(q) −3.
It is then natural to ask whether such an equality extends to curves of positive genus.
Partial results were obtained by Lee and Park [LP13], who showed that for a fixed p
and sufficiently large d, a general projection Cq satisfies property N2,p.
Our main theorem shows that for elliptic normal curves, the equality holds for a
general projection center:
Theorem 1.2. Let C ⊂Pd−1 be an elliptic normal curve of degree d ≥5, and let
q ∈Pd−1 \ C2 be a point with s := rkC(q). Then
(1) index(Cq) ≤s −3.
(2) If q is a general point of Cs, then index(Cq) = s −3.
(3) If q is a general point of Pd−1, then index(Cq) = ⌈d
2⌉−3.
Thus the Green–Lazarsfeld index of a projected elliptic normal curve is governed by
the secant rank of the projection center, paralleling the behavior known for rational
normal curves.

4. Remarks on projection of higher dimensional varieties
Let X ⊂Pr be a linearly normal smooth projective variety of dimension n, and let q
be a point in Pr \ X2.
If X is a curve, then Theorem 1.1 in [Par07a], Theorem 1.1 in [LP13], and Theorem 1.2
in the present paper show that as the position of q relative to X becomes more general,
the Green–Lazarsfeld index of Xq increases. It is therefore natural to ask whether this
phenomenon also persists when n ≥2. However, the following example shows that this
cannot be expected to hold for all X.
Example 4.1. Let X = S(1, a2, . . . , an) ⊂Pr be an n-dimensional smooth rational
normal scroll, where 1 = a1 ≤a2 ≤· · · ≤an is a nondecreasing sequence of integers.
Also, let q be a point in Pr \ X2.
By Theorem 1.4 in [BS07] and Theorem 1.1 in [Par07b], there exists a smooth (n+1)-
fold rational normal scroll Y = S(b1, . . . , bn, bn+1) in Pr−1 containing Xq. Theorems 1.2
and 1.3 in [Par07b] show that b1 = 1 and the Green–Lazarsfeld index of Xq is 0.

This shows that even though index(X) = ∞and rkX(q) can be arbitrarily large, one
necessarily has index(Xq) = 0 for all q ∈Pr \ X2.
From this example, we see that the phenomenon observed for curves cannot, in
general, be expected for higher dimensional projective varieties. Note that this example
is special because X itself is a variety of minimal degree. On the other hand, there are
also positive results.
Example 4.2. Let S = ν3(P2) ⊂P9 be the Veronese cubic surface. Then S4 = P9 and
hence ord(S) = 4. In particular, if q is a general point of P9, then q is contained in a
3-plane spanned by 4 points of S. This implies that the projected surface Sq admits a
4-secant 2-plane, and hence
index(Sq) ≤1
(cf. [EGHP05, Theorem 1.1]). By Proposition 2.4, it follows that index(Sq) ≤1 for all
q ∈P9 \ S2. Also, Theorem 4.1 in [CC13] shows that index(Sq) = 0 for all q ∈S3 \ S2
and index(Sq) ≥1 for all q ∈P9 \ S3. Consequently,
index(Sq) = 1
for all q ∈P9 \ S3.
Therefore, for a given higher dimensional projective variety X, it is an interesting
problem to determine when index(Xq) behaves as in Example 4.1, and when it behaves
like the Veronese cubic surface in Example 4.2. In the latter case, it is also of interest
to find a lower bound for index(Xq).
