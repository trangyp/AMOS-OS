Van der Waals heterostructures made from atomically thin transition metal dichalcogenides
(TMD) and graphene have emerged as a building block for optoelectronic devices. Such systems
are also uniquely poised to investigate interfacial coupling as well as photoinduced charge and en-
ergy transfer in the 2D limit. Recent works have revealed efficient photoluminescence quenching
and picosecond transfer in TMD/graphene heterostructures. However, key questions regarding the
transfer mechanisms remain. Here, employing time-resolved photoluminescence spectroscopy with
1 ps resolution in MoSe2 monolayer directly coupled to a few-layer “staircase-like” graphene flake, we
consistently observe an exciton transfer time of ∼2.5 ps at cryogenic temperature that is marginally
affected by the number of graphene layers. Remarkably, exciton transfer vanishes in samples con-
sisting in an MoSe2 monolayer separated from graphene by a thin dielectric spacer of hexagonal
boron nitride, as soon as the spacer thickness reaches 1 nm.
These results suggest that charge
tunnelling processes govern exciton dynamics. Other mechanisms mediated the dipolar interactions
(F¨orster-type energy transfer) have no measurable impact on bright excitons (with near-zero center
of mass momentum) but may accelerate the relaxation of finite momentum “hot” excitons, leading
to larger photoluminescence quenching than anticipated based on the measurements of the photo-
luminescence decay rates. Our work provides important insights into charge and energy transfer in
van der Waals materials with direct implications for energy harvesting and funneling.
Introduction
A wealth of low-dimensional physical phenomena and
related models can be accurately tested using atomically-
thin layers of van der Waals materials.
These ma-
terials offer unique opportunities to investigate truly
two-dimensional physics, without being hindered by is-
sues associated with dangling bonds, buffer layers and
surface roughness [1].
In particular, it is now clear
that interfacial coupling may strongly alter the elec-
tronic, vibrational and optical response of individual two-
dimensional layers [2–6].
Interlayer (spatially-indirect)
excited states may form on ultrafast timescales upon
photoexcitation in heterobilayers of transition dichalco-
genides (TMD) [2, 3, 7].
Correlated electronic phases
emerge in twisted layers of graphene [8], as well as in
twisted TMD homo- or hetero-bilayers [9]. Coupled lay-
ers also offer a model system to investigate the role of
dielectric screening [5, 6, 10, 11] as well as charge and
energy transfer in a layered donor-acceptor system sep-
arated by a sub-nm van der Waals gap. Several experi-
mental observations of ultrafast interlayer charge and/or
energy transfer have been reported using a variety of

time-resolved (TR) experimental techniques such as tran-
sient absorption spectroscopy [12, 13], TR photocur-
rent spectroscopy [14], TR photoluminescence (TRPL)
spectroscopy [7, 15, 16], TR Raman spectroscopy [17]
TR angle-resolved photo-emission spectroscopy (TR-
ARPES) [18–20], key questions remain open regarding
the microscopic transfer mechanisms at play, specifically
the range of the interactions, the associated timescales
and the different behaviors between bright excitons (with
near-zero center of mass momentum), hot excitons (opti-
cally inactive, with finite center of mass momentum) and
charge carriers.
On the theoretical side, it becomes challenging and of-
ten speculative to address quantitatively the charge and
energy transfer rates in the limit of sub-nanometer sepa-
ration between coupled layers, where orbital overlap pre-
vail [20–22]. It is thus important to scrutinize the specific
case of tightly coupled TMD and graphene layers in or-
der to get an accurate estimation of the transfer time as
well as clear insights into the transfer mechanism.
In this work, we study the low-temperature excitonic
dynamics in two distinct types of MoSe2/graphene het-
erostructures. First, in a monolayer of MoSe2 directly
coupled to a graphene flake comprising several domains
with thicknesses ranging from N = 1 to N = 6 lay-
ers, we observe two effects: i) sizeable PL quenching and

new light on charge and energy transfer mechanisms at
ultrashort (sub-nm) distances and is a strong impetus
to further develop existing theoretical frameworks to ad-

regime.
Photoluminescence mapping of MoSe2/NLG
MoSe2 is one of the best documented two-dimensional
semiconductors.
Monolayer MoSe2 (thereafter simply
denoted as MoSe2) has a direct bandgap and displays
a lowest lying optically active (bright) excitonic state
(X0) [24, 25]. Thus, the excitonic emission characteris-
tics of MoSe2 are relatively simple as compared to other
materials such as WS2 and WSe2, which exhibit optically
inactive (dark) or weakly active (grey) states below the
bright excitonic state [26].
Figure 1a,b show an optical micrograph and a sketch of
sample S1, respectively. Our samples are van der Waals
heterostructures made using standard pick-up and trans-
fer methods [23]. Our sample consist of a “staircase-like”
graphene flake with several domains of different thick-
nesses (ranging from one to six layers, typically) covered
by an MoSe2 monolayer and a thin film of hexagonal
boron nitride (< 10nm−thick) as a capping layer. For
sample S1, the whole heterostructure lies onto an Si/SiO2
substrate.
Crucially, for our study, we have carefully chosen the
SiO2 epilayer thickness (500 nm) such that the MoSe2
layer is located at a node of the electromagnetic field (at a
photon energy corresponding to the main excitonic reso-
nance). In these conditions, the radiative lifetime of bare
MoSe2 is significantly increased, such that the impact of
non-radiative transfer to graphene can be observed more
prominently[23, 27].

are governed by interfacial coupling, the quality and ho-
mogeneity of the van der Waals heterointerface between
MoSe2 and graphene must be carefully examined. Thus,

Time-resolved photoluminescence
MoSe2/NLG – Figure 2 shows typical TRPL mea-
surements performed using a streak camera for the vari-
ous domains of sample S1 identified in Fig. 1. The high
resolution (≈1ps) of these measurements enable us to
unambiguously resolve exciton dynamics in MoSe2/NLG
and to quantitatively determine the X0 lifetime.
Let us first consider the SiO2/MoSe2/hBN reference.
The measured TRPL reveals sub-picosecond rise time,
i.e., faster than the instrument response function and a
decay time of (8.9 ± 0.5) ps. The decay time is assigned
to the recombination time of cold excitons in the light
cone (X0 in Fig. 2a), that is considerably longer than in
MoSe2 monolayers on a transparent dielectric substrate
(≈2 ps [16]), due the cavity effect discussed above [27].
The sub-picosecond rise time is similar to previous mea-
surements in SiO2 supported MoSe2 [29] assigned to the
lifetime of the hot exciton reservoir (X0) formed almost
instantaneously after non-resonant photoexcitation be-
low the free-carrier continuum [30] and from which X0
are formed. In the supplemental material, we discuss X0
dynamics in a fully hBN-encapsulated sample containing
MoSe2, MoSe2/1LG and MoSe2/2LG domains [23]
Non-radiative X0 relaxation to graphene layers leads
to shorter PL decays in MoSe2/NLG, slightly above
2 ps, while the PL rise time remains below our reso-
lution limit. Interestingly, the decay time measured in
MoSe2/NLG is nearly independent of N and we only ob-
serve a marginally longer decay time by less than 20%
in MoSe2/1LG as compared to MoSe2/NLG (N > 1), a
value that is slightly above our experimental error, con-
servatively estimated below 10 %. These results confirm
that interlayer coupling in a MoSe2/NLG heterointerface
is essentially determined by the first graphene layer.

1 nm).

FRET from TMD exci-
tons to graphene has recently been considered theoret-
ically and experimentally, following numerous studies
in 2D-2D hybrid systems, including organic/inorganic
semiconductor heterostructures [33] and semiconductor
nanoplatelet/graphene heterostructures[34]. In contrast
to the case of point-like systems or 0D-2D heterostruc-
tures [34–37], conservation of the exciton center of mass
momentum in a 2D-2D system has drastic consequences
on the FRET rate.
Indeed, since the momentum-
dependent FRET efficiency scales as q2
X exp (−2qXd) [22,
33], where d the TMD-graphene distance, no FRET is
expected for excitons at qX = 0 and more broadly for
X0 excitons in the light cone (such that qX ≲107m−1),
i.e.,that is qX ≪EX0/(ℏvF), with vF the Fermi velocity
of graphene).
Importantly, in the limit where the donor and acceptor
are separated by a sub-nanometer distance (arguably as
is the case for Sample 1), the FRET rate is marginally
dependent on the donor-acceptor distance and decays ex-
ponentially with the donor-acceptor distance d with a
characteristic length in the nanometer range at cryogenic
temperatures [22]. Beyond ∼10 nm, a well-known 1/d4
scaling is expected.
These theoretical predictions con-
trast with our observations that a 1 nm-thin hBN spacer
is sufficient to recover PL intensities and dynamics that
are very similar to bare MoSe2 (Fig. 3 and [23]). Further
studies at room temperature, where more efficient FRET

is expected, have revealed that the distance-dependence
of the transfer rate deviates strongly from theoretical pre-
dictions based solely on FRET, when the TMD-graphene
distance is within the tunnelling range (that is, with no
spacer between TMD and graphene or just one atomic
layer of hBN) [38].
We thus conclude that another microscopic mechanism
must be at play in order to explain the reduction of the
X0 lifetime that we consistently observe in MoSe2/NLG.
Considering that the X0 dynamics in MoSe2/Gr i) is ex-
tremely sensitive to the interlayer distance for a sub-nm
interlayer gap but then marginally depends on the dis-
tance outside the tunnelling range, and ii) depends very
weakly on N (also in contrast with a FRET-based sce-
nario [23] as further discussed below), it is sensible to
invoke charge tunnelling.
Dexter energy transfer (also
known as electron exchange)[39] may in principle con-
tribute but the Dexter energy transfer rate has been pre-
dicted is orders of magnitude smaller than the FRET
rate, chiefly due to limited overlap between TMD and
graphene electronic orbitals [20].
The only plausible
mechanism that may account for the shortening of X0
lifetime and its dependence on d and on N is direct charge
tunnelling of electrons and holes that compose X0. Since
our previous experiments did not reveal any fingerprint
of a long-lived charge separated state [18] on timescales
on the order of the measured exciton lifetime [17], we
suggest that sequential (balanced) tunnelling of electrons
and holes is responsible for the shortening of the exciton
lifetime.

We finally note that an alternate mechanism, involving
intraband transitions in graphene enabled by a transient
population of hot holes near the fermi level of graphene,
and referred to as Meitner Auger energy transfer has
been proposed to account for sub-ps exciton transfer
in WSe2/Gr under intense pulsed laser excitation. Im-
portantly, this mechanism involves excitons with large
qX ≈Eph/(ℏvF) ≈2.5 × 109m−1, with Eph the photon
energy and does not affect X0. Meitner Auger transfer
might affect hot excitons, however, our measurements are
performed at pulse fluences that are more than two or-
ders of magnitude lower than in Ref. [20], such that this
transfer process can safely be neglected in first approxi-
mation.
Conclusion and outlook
In conclusion, we demonstrate that the low tempera-
ture dynamics (typically T ≤16 K) of the lower lying,
bright excitons in MoSe2/N-layer graphene is largely in-
dependent on N and determined by sub-nm range pi-
cosecond non-radiative transfer processes to graphene.
In contrast, the photoluminescence quenching factor in-
creases appreciably in the small N limit before saturat-
ing for N ≳3. A slower exciton dynamics typical from
a bare MoSe2 monolayer is recovered for separations in
excess of 1 nm between MoSe2 and a graphene mono-
layer. Together, these results establish that F¨orster-type
resonant energy transfer is inefficient for bright excitons
at low temperatures, as the center of mass momentum of
these excitons is very close to zero, but may contribute
significantly to the transfer of hot, finite momentum exci-
tons. This hypothesis is supported by the increase of the
quenching factor with N and supported by an electrody-
