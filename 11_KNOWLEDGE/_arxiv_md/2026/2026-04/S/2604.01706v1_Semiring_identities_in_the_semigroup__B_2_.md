SEMIRING IDENTITIES IN THE SEMIGROUP B2
VYACHESLAV YU. SHAPRYNSKIˇI
Abstract. The 5-element Brandt semigroup B2 admits the struc-
ture of a naturally semilattice-ordered inverse semigroup, thus be-
coming an additively idempotent semiring with the operation of
taking greatest lower bounds as the semiring addition. For this
semiring we present a finite basis of identities and thus, by the
previous results, complete the solution of Finite Basis Problem for
combinatorial naturally semilattice-ordered inverse semigroups.
1. Introduction and summary
An inverse semigroup is a semigroup that satisfies the condition
∀x∃!y
xyx = x and yxy = y.
The element y is called inverse to x and denoted by x−1. The study of
inverse semigroups is a classical branch of semigroup theory. We will
use basic facts about inverse semigroups without direct reference, for
the details see monographs [4,6]. Every inverse semigroup admits the
natural partial order relation defined by
x ≤y ⇐⇒xx−1y = x.
An inverse semigroup is called naturally semilattice-ordered if it is a
lower semilattice under this order relation.
As an example, one can mention rook monoids. The rook monoid
Rn can be defined as the set of all injective partial transformations on
the set {1, . . . , n} under the operation of composition. This monoid
is an inverse semigroup with inverse partial transformations being in-
verse elements of the semigroup and the natural order relation being
the set-theoretical inclusion of partial transformations (as binary re-
lations). The semigroup Rn is a naturally semilattice-ordered inverse
semigroup: the set-theoretical intersection of partial transformations is
their greatest lower bound.

In every naturally semilattice-ordered inverse semigroup multiplica-
tion distributes over the operation of taking grestest lower bounds [8,
Proposition 1.22], so it can be considered an additively idempotent
semiring. Recall that an additively idempotent semiring (ai-semiring
for short) is an algebra with two binary operations (S, +, ·) such that
the additive reduct (S, +) is a semilattice, the multiplicative reduct
(S, ·) is a semigroup, and multiplication distributes over addition. In
particular, one can consider the Finite Basis Problem in the semiring
signature.
Recall that a system of identities is an identity basis of an algebra
A if it holds in A and implies all identities that hold in A. An al-
gebra is finitely based if it has a finite identity basis and nonfinitely
based otherwise. The Finite Basis Problem (FBP for short) consists in
determining which algebras are finitely based.

The aim of the present paper is to prove that the semiring B2 is
finitely based, thereby completing the solution of the Finite Basis Prob-
lem for naturally semilattice-ordered inverse semigroups in the combi-
natorial case. We call semigroup terms words and semiring terms poly-
nomials. For polynomials u and v, the inequality u ≤v denotes the
identity u + v ≈v which corresponds to the additive semilattice order
relation in ai-semirings1. The following theorem is the main result of
the present paper.
Theorem 1. The following identities constitute an identity basis for
the semiring B2 within the variety of all ai-semirings:
x2z2 ≤x1z1 + x1z2 + x2z1,
(4)

where x1, x2, z1, z2 can be empty.
In particular, Theorem 1 states (somewhat counter-intuitively) that
the identities (1), (2), and (3) follow from (4) and (5). Note that the
identities (4) and (5) have occurred in [9] as a part of the identity basis
of B0. The identity (4) has been known since the work [1] where it was
proved that this identity holds in all rook monoids.
The proof of Theorem 1 uses an explicit description of identities in
B2. This description seems to be of independent interest. It is related
to the “edge closure” technique used in [7] to describe identities in B2
in semigroup signature, however a different notation will be convenient
in the semiring case. By c(p) we denote the content of a polynomial
p, i.e. the set of all letters occurring in p. We consider the following
binary relations on the cartesian product c(p) × {1, 2}:
ρ1(p) = {((x, 1), (y, 1)) | x and y are first letters of two words in p};
ρ2(p) = {((x, 2), (y, 2)) | x and y are last letters of two words in p};
ρ3(p) = {((x, 2), (y, 1)) | xy is a subword of a word in p}.
We define ρ(p) as the equivalence relation on c(p)×{1, 2} generated by
ρ1(p)∪ρ2(p)∪ρ3(p). The initial [terminal] ρ(p)-class is the ρ(p)-class
which contains the pair (x, 1) [(x, 2)] where x is the first [last] letter of
a word in p. We denote this class by Init(p) [Term(p)].
