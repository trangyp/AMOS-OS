SYZYGIES OF THE TRANSFER IDEAL OF THE SYMMETRIC GROUP
HARM DERKSEN AND ALEXANDRA PEVZNER
Abstract. We consider the modular action of the symmetric group Sn on R = k[x1, . . . , xn]
when char(k) = p ≤n. We show that the image of the transfer map R →RSn is an elimination
ideal J ∩RSn, where J ⊂RSn[t] is generated by p polynomials with generic coefficients.
The
structure of this elimination ideal depends only on the quotient q when writing n = qp + r with
unique remainder 0 ≤r < p, implying that the image of the transfer also enjoys this stability. We
conjecture a determinantal presentation of the elimination ideal and prove it in the case that q = 2.
Furthermore, we exhibit a GL-equivariant, linear minimal free resolution of a certain initial ideal,
allowing us to extract the graded Betti numbers of the elimination ideal.
1. Introduction
Fix a field k and a finite group G. Let G act on the polynomial ring R = k[x1, . . . , xn] by graded
k-algebra automorphisms. When the action of G is modular, meaning that |G| is not invertible in
k, we cannot average elements of R over the group. Instead, the transfer map

Taking G to be the symmetric group acting on R by variable permutation, the invariant ring RG
is well known to be the polynomial algebra k[e1, . . . , en] generated by the elementary symmetric
polynomials e1, . . . , en. In previous work, the second author conjectured that when k = Fp, the
minimal free resolution of Im(TrSn) over RSn depends only on the quotient q ∈Z when writing n
uniquely as n = qp + r with 0 ≤r < p; see [9, Conj 6.1]. In this paper, we prove the conjecture
by realizing the image of the transfer as a module over a polynomial subalgebra of RSn which, for
fixed q, is the same for all 0 ≤r ≤p −1.
1.1. Outline of paper. In Section 2, we apply a theorem of Feshbach on the radical of Im(TrG) to
express Im(TrSn) as an elimination ideal ⟨f0, f1, . . . , fp−1⟩∩RSn, where the fi are certain polynomi-
als in RSn[t] with generic coefficients. We then note that the structure of the ideal ⟨f0, f1, . . . , fp−1⟩
only differs by a change of variables as n varies within {qp, qp + 1, . . . , qp + (p −1)}. Theorem 3.1
makes this idea precise and proves Conjecture 6.1 of [9].
We then consider the elimination ideal I = ⟨f0, f1, . . . , fp−1⟩∩RSn in its own right. Conjecture 4.2
posits that I is generated by the maximal minors of a matrix A, which is built out of concatenations
of Sylvester matrix blocks. We show in Proposition 4.4 that I is generated up to radical by sums
of maximal minors of A. In Section 5, we conjecture that the determinantal ideal has a certain
squarefree initial ideal with a combinatorial characterization. In Section 6, we use the initial ideal
to prove Conjecture 4.2 when q = 2. Finally, in Section 7, we build explicit, GLp−1-equivariant
minimal free resolutions of the associated graded ideal of I when q = 2. These resolutions are
linear, allowing us to deduce the projective dimension and graded Betti numbers of I.

Theorem 7.9. The mapping cone of φ, as defined just before Proposition 7.8, gives a minimal
free resolution of S/I′.
Moreover, there is an equality of projective dimensions pdim(S/I) =
pdim(S/I′) = 2p −3, and the total Betti numbers for S/I and S/I′ are equal.
Proof. Since 0 →K →S/n2 →S/I′ →0 is short exact, and F, G resolve K and S/n2, respectively,
the mapping cone Cone(φ) will be a free resolution of S/I′. Let S+ denote the ideal of all positive
degree elements of S. Because φ(Fi) ⊂S+Gi for all i, the mapping cone gives a minimal resolution
of S/I′. It was shown originally in [10] and also proven in [6, Thm 3.1/Cor 3.2] that the Betti
numbers for an associated graded ideal give an upper bound for the Betti numbers of the original
ideal, i.e. βS
i (S/I) ≤βS
i (S/I′) for all i.
From the construction of F, G, and the comparison maps, one can see that Cone(φ) gives a linear
free resolution of S/I′, meaning that all entries in the differential matrices are linear forms. By
[11, Theorem 3.1] (see also [8]), the total Betti numbers of S/I may be obtained from the graded
Betti numbers of S/I′ by a series of negative consecutive cancellations. This corresponds to the
presence of maps of the form S(−j) →S(−j′), with j′ > j, in the minimal free resolution of S/I′.
However, since the minimal free resolution of S/I′ is linear, a negative consecutive cancellation is
not possible. Hence, the total Betti numbers of S/I and S/I′ must agree. The projective dimension
of S/I′ can be computed directly from its resolution as the last nonzero degree in the resolution of
K.
