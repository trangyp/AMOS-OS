SUBINDICES AND SUBFACTORS OF INFINITE GROUPS AND NUMBERS
M.H. HOOSHMAND
ABSTRACT. The theory of subfactors of groups, together with the associated notions of subindices and index stability for
groups and their subsets, has recently been introduced and systematically developed. These concepts exhibit deep connections
with additive combinatorics and number theory, relating to important topics such as packing and covering numbers, syndetic
sets, group diameters, special integer sequences (e.g., primes and Fibonacci numbers), and classical rational sequences (e.g.,
Bernoulli numbers). Following the initial paper presented in 2020, two subsequent works further investigated these ideas
within the framework of finite groups. In the present paper, in addition to advancing several aspects of the topic, we focus
on infinite groups, with particular emphasis on groups of numbers. In this context, we introduce the RSFA (Right Subfactor
Algorithm) for infinite groups and resolve several previously open problems. One of the important results is that every infinite
group is index-unstable. We also correct several earlier inaccuracies and establish a weak version of a conjecture concerning
differences of prime numbers. Furthermore, we determine the exact subindices for several notable sequences of integers and
provide a general criterion for index stability and non-index stability of subsets in countable groups. Finally, we investigate
the index stability of infinite groups and present a collection of related projects, problems, questions, and conjectures.
1. INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARIES
The study of factorizations of groups offers rich mathematical landscapes intertwined with questions. In some
recent studies, a challenging open question concerning the factorization of finite groups by subsets, earlier in 2014 [14],
and then in the Kourovka Notebook (Problem 19.35, 20.37), is stated. The question has only been partially answered
in existing literature (e.g., see [28, 20]). Motivated by inquiries into the mentioned topic, Upper Periodic Subsets,
and functional equations on groups and semigroups, we were led to introduce subfactors and the generalization of
subgroup indices to arbitrary subsets of groups [19]. The theory of subfactors and subindices of groups has been
further developed in subsequent papers, such as [20, 22]. Also, in [26], a connection between the topic and an
interesting concept from graph theory has been introduced.
One of the central constructs we employ is that of the direct product of two subsets. For arbitrary subsets A, B ⊆G,
the product AB is said to be direct, denoted A · B, if each element of AB has a unique representation ab with a ∈A
and b ∈B. For nonempty subsets A, B, we have AB = A · B if and only if A−1A ∩BB−1 = {1}, and if and only if
aB ∩a′B = ∅for all distinct elements a, a′ ∈A (see [19, 20, 22] for more details). Note that G = A · B if and only
if G = AB and the product AB is direct (the additive notation is G = A ˙+B).
If G = A · B , then A is a left factor of G related to B, and call B a right factor complement of A. We call A a
left factor of G if and only if G = A · B for some B ⊆G. For example, every subgroup is a left (resp. right) factor
related to its right (resp. left) transversal, hence it is a two-sided factor of G. In reference [19], the author achieved a
generalization of factors that not only do not have the deficiency of factors but also lead to the important concept of
subindices for all subsets of groups.
Let A be a fixed subset of G. We call B a right subfactors of G related to A if B is an inclusion-maximal subset of G
with respect to the property AB = A · B. Also, we say B is a right subfactor of G if it is a right subfactor related to
some subset of G (left subfactors are defined analogously).

Definition 1.2 ([19]). Let G be an arbitrary group and A ⊆G. Then
a) A is called right (resp. left) index stable in G if |G : A|+ = |G : A|−(resp. |G : A|+ = |G : A|−), and we use the
notation |G : A|r (resp. |G : A|ℓ) for the common value and call it the right (resp. left) index of A in G.
b) A is called index stable (in G) if all of its four subindices are equal (equivalently |G : A|r = |G : A|ℓ), and the
common value is denoted by |G : A| and is called the index of A in G (a unique cardinal number corresponding to
A).
c) Also, G is called index stable (resp. right index stable) if all its subsets are index stable (resp. right index stable).
It is worth noting that if G is a group and H a subgroup, then H (as a subset) is always index stable in G, but as an
independent group, H may be not index stable (i.e., it contains a subset that is not index stable in H).

2.4. Index pervasive infinite groups. Motivated by a property of the subindices of subsets of finite groups we lead

Definition 2.22. We call an infinite group G right (resp. infinitely right) upper index pervasive if for every cardinal
number 1 ≤κ ≤|G| (resp. ℵ0 ≤κ ≤|G|) there exists a subset A such that |G : A|+ = κ (the concepts “left (resp.

right index) pervasive if for every 1 ≤κ ≤|G| (resp. ℵ0 ≤κ ≤|G|) there exists a right index stable subset with the
right index κ (the other cases are defined analogously).
The following theorem is a immediate result of the corollary of the main theorem of [4] and Lemma 1.15.
Theorem 2.23. Let G be an infinite abelian group. Then, for every cardinal number 2, 3 ̸= κ ≤|G| there is a subset
of G with the upper index κ. For the case κ = 2 (resp. κ = 3) also the property is held if and only if G is not
isomorphic to L Z3 (resp. G is not isomorphic to L Z2 or to Z4 ⊕(L Z2).
The theorem completely characterizes upper index pervasive infinite abelian groups as follows.
Corollary 2.24. An infinite abelian group is upper index pervasive if and only if it is not isomorphic to L Z2, L Z3,
and Z4 ⊕(L Z2).

Convention. Let {an} be a sequence of elements of a group of numbers. By |G : {an}|± we mean |G : A|± where
A is the image of the sequence an (i.e., A = {a1, a2, a3, · · · }). Similar conceptions for the sequences can be used
according other notations and definitions.
