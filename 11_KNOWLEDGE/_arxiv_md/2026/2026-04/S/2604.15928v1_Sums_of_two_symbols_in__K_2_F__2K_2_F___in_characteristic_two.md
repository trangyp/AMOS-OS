Abstract
In this paper, study sums A = {a, b}2 + {c, d}2 of two symbols in K2(F)/2K2(F) when char(F) = 2.
We first prove a chain lemma that connects A to B = {α, β}2 + {γ, δ}2 by a finite sequence of small
steps when A ≡B. We use this lemma to prove that {a, b, c, d}2 ∈K4(F)/2K4(F) is a well-defined
invariant of A, and that this invariant is trivial if and only if A is congruent to a single symbol in
K2(F)/4K2(F). We also bound the symbol length of C in K2(F)/2mK2(F) from above when C is
the sum of up to four symbols in K2(F)/2m+1K2(F).
Keywords: K-Theory; Symmetric Bilinear Forms; Fields of characteristic 2

1. Introduction
The mod 2 K-theory of fields F of characteristic 2 gained significance the moment Kato
showed in [9] that Kn(F)/2Kn(F) is isomrophic to InF/In+1F where IF is the fundamental ideal
of even-dimension forms in the Witt group WF of symmetric bilinear forms over F.
In this paper we focus on sums A = {a, b}2 + {c, d}2 of two symbols in K2(F)/2K2(F). We
first prove a chain lemma that connects A to B = {α, β}2 + {γ, δ}2 by a finite sequence of small
steps when A ≡B. We use this lemma to prove that {a, b, c, d}2 ∈K4(F)/2K4(F) is a well-defined
invariant of A, and that this invariant is trivial if and only if A is congruent to a single symbol in
K2(F)/4K2(F). We also bound the symbol length of C in K2(F)/2mK2(F) from above when C is
the sum of up to four symbols in K2(F)/2m+1K2(F).
All the results obtained in this paper have characteristic not 2 analogues, see [11], [12], [13]
and [3]. We refer to these analogous results, and when possible, we point out improvements in the
existing literature that can be made by mimicking the technique we present here.

3. Chain Lemma
Let τ ∈K2(F)/2K2(F) be a sum of two symbols, and let (V, E) be a labeled graph whose
vertices are all pairs (S 1, S 2) of symbols in K2(F)/2K2(F) satisfying S 1 + S 2 = τ.
Two vertices (S 1, S 2) and (T1, T2) are connected by an edge if there exist α, β, γ, δ ∈F× and
x ∈F such that: S 1 = {α, β}2, S 2 = {γ, δ}2, T1 = {α, (x2 + αγ)β}2 and T2 = {γ, (x2 + αγ)δ}2. If
x = 0 (resp. x , 0), then we say that (S 1, S 2) and (T1, T2) are connected by an edge of type I
(resp. of type II). Note that when x , 0, we can assume x = 1.
Lemma 3.1.
(1) If there exist α, β, γ, δ ∈F× and x, y ∈F not both zero such that S 1 = {α, β}2, S 2 = {γ, δ}2,
T1 = {α, (x2 + αγy2)β}2 and T2 = {γ, (x2 + αγy2)δ}2, then (S 1, S 2) and (T1, T2) are either

(2) If (S 1, S 2) is connected to (T1, T2) by an edge of type 2, then there exists a vertex (R1, R2)
connected to both (S 1, S 2) and (T1, T2) by edges of type I.
Proof.
(1) If y = 0, then (S 1, S 2) = (T1, T2). So suppose y , 0. If x = 0, then (S 1, S 2) is connected to
(T1, T2) by an edge of type I, and if x , 0, by an edge of type 2.
(2) Suppose that (S 1, S 2) is connected to (T1, T2) by an edge of type 2. Then, there exist

Remark 3.3. Note that what we call here an edge of type I is analogous what is called in [11]
“strongly simply-equivalent". In that paper, the author concluded that in characteristic not 2, every

to 40 symbols in K2F/2mK2F. The case of (ii) is similar.
□
Remark 5.2. Note that in the analogous statement to part (c) of Theorem 5.1 in [3], i.e., Theorem
3.5 of that paper, the bound can be reduced from 46 to 40 by the refinement of Sivatski’s chain
lemma as we presented here and can be easily adapted to the case of characteristic not 2 without
any significant change.
