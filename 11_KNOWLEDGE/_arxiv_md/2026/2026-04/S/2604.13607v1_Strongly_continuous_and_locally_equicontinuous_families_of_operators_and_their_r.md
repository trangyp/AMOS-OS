STRONGLY CONTINUOUS AND LOCALLY EQUICONTINUOUS FAMILIES OF
OPERATORS AND THEIR RELATION TO BI-CONTINUITY
KARSTEN KRUSE
AND CHRISTIAN SEIFERT
Abstract. We study strongly continuous and locally equicontinuous families of operators on sequen-
tially complete Hausdorff locally convex spaces. In case of Saks spaces, we relate the general notions
to bi-continuity as well as equitightness. In this way, we recover and also generalise known results for
special classes of operator families such as bi-continuous (C-)semigroups and (C-)cosine families by
well-known results for the corresponding families in Hausdorff locally convex spaces.
1. Introduction
The aim of this article is to investigate strongly continuous and (locally) equicontinuous families of
operators on Hausdorff locally convex spaces and to relate many different variants—particular classes
of operators on particular spaces—present in the literature to this situation. In this way, on the one
hand, we analyse and structure the various notions available. On the other hand, we are able to recover
existing, as well as to obtain new, results in special situations by means of the corresponding results for
operator families in Hausdorff locally convex spaces.
Strongly continuous and (locally) equicontinuous families of operators on Hausdorff locally convex
spaces are classical objects in functional analysis and operator theory. Such families appear, for example,
as fundamental solutions for abstract Cauchy problems (ACPs). Let us mention Stone’s theorem for
strongly continuous groups of unitary operators on Hilbert spaces [Sto30; Neu32] and the celebrated
Hille–Yosida theorem for strongly continuous semigroups on Banach spaces [Hil48; Yos48], for first order
ACPs. Furthermore, we also mention the corresponding generation theorems for strongly continuous
cosine families in Banach spaces [Sov66; Kis72] for second order ACPs, as well as the analogs for higher
order ACPs in Banach spaces [Neu86]. Standard monographs for semigroups and/or cosine families, also
including applications, are e.g. [HP57; Paz83; Gol85; EN00]. Since the late 1950s, various generalisations
have appeared in the literature. The theory for special classes of operator families on Banach or Hilbert
spaces has been generalised to operator families in Hausdorff locally convex spaces, see [Sch58; Kom64;
K¯om68; Yos68] for strongly continuous and (locally) equicontinuous semigroups on sequentially complete,
even locally complete [Tei02], Hausdorff locally convex spaces, [Fat69a; Fat69b; Kon72] for strongly
continuous and (locally) equicontinuous cosine families on sequentially complete Hausdorff locally convex
spaces. Another generalisation arises from so-called C-semigroups and C-cosine families, both on Banach
spaces [DP87; KS97a; KS97b] as well as on sequentially complete Hausdorff locally convex spaces [XL98].
Further, the adjoints of semigroups [Nee92] and cosine families [Sha86; Boc00; Boc02] on Banach spaces
have also been studied. Moreover, in the 1980s, integrated semigroups and integrated cosine families on
Banach spaces have been introduced and studied [Are84; Neu88; KH89] (to name a few). Starting from
the 2000s, two further variants have been studied, namely bi-continuous semigroups and cosine families,
where the operator families defined on Banach spaces are only strongly continuous with respect to a
weaker Hausdorff locally convex topology, see [Küh01; Küh03; Far03; AM04; Far11; BF19] for semigroups
and [DS06; CCS14; Bud24; Bud25] for cosine families. Moreover, so-called integrable semigroups on
norming dual pairs have been introduced by Kunze [Kun09].
Recently, also locally equicontinuous
semigroup on Hausdorff locally convex spaces which can also be equipped with an auxiliary norm (so the
concept is closely related to Saks spaces and bi-continuous semigroups) [Kra16] as well as sequentially
locally equicontinuous semigroups [FR20] have been considered.
From the point of view of the underlying spaces, the most general situation treated is the (sequentially
complete) Hausdorff locally convex case. This gives rise to the question of whether the above-mentioned
variants are special situations of this general setup. Our aim is to actually show that this is indeed the

2. Sequential equicontinuity
For a Hausdorff locally convex space (X, τ) we denote by P a system of seminorms generating the
topology τ. W.l.o.g. P is directed. If no confusion about the topology τ seems to be likely, we just write
X instead of (X, τ). On the other hand, if we want to emphasise the dependency of P on X or τ, we
write PX or Pτ instead of just P, respectively. We denote by L(X, τ) the space of continuous linear
maps from (X, τ) to (X, τ). If (X, ∥·∥) is a normed space, then τ∥·∥denotes the topology induced by ∥·∥
and we write L(X) := L(X, τ∥·∥) which we equip with operator norm ∥·∥L(X). Further, we denote by
(X, τ)′ the topological dual space of (X, τ).
Definition 2.1 ([FR20, pp. 1135, 1166]). Let X and Y be Hausdorff locally convex spaces and S be a
set of linear maps from X to Y . Then S is called
(a) equicontinuous if for all p ∈PY there exist q ∈PX and K ≥0 such that supT ∈S p(Tx) ≤Kq(x) for
all x ∈X.
(b) sequentially equicontinuous if for all p ∈PY and all sequences (xn) in X such that xn →0 we have
that supT ∈S p(Txn) →0.
We note that the definition of (sequential) equicontinuity does not depend on the choice of the systems
of seminorms PX and PY that generate the Hausdorff locally convex topologies on X and Y , respectively.
Clearly, if S is equicontinuous, then it is also sequentially equicontinuous. We note the following char-
acterisation of sequential equicontinuity whose proof we include for the sake of completeness. We recall
that a subset U of a topological space X is called sequentially open if every sequence (xn) in X converging
to a point x ∈U is eventually in U, see [Fra65, p. 108]. In particular, open sets are sequentially open
but the converse might not hold.
Proposition 2.2. Let X and Y be Hausdorff locally convex spaces and S a set of linear maps from X
to Y . Then the following assertions are equivalent.
(a) S is sequentially equicontinuous.
(b) For all absolutely convex open neighbourhoods V of zero in Y there is an absolutely convex sequen-
tially open neighbourhood U of zero in X such that T(U) ⊆V for all T ∈S.
(c) For all p ∈PY there are a sequentially continuous seminorm q on X and K ≥0 such that
supT ∈S p(T(x)) ≤Kq(x) for all x ∈X.
Proof. “(a)⇒(b)” Let V be an absolutely convex open neighbourhood of zero in Y . Then the Minkowski
functional pV : Y →R, pV (y) := inf{λ > 0 | y ∈λV }, is continuous and V = {y ∈Y | pV (y) < 1} by
[MV97, Lemma 22.3]. It follows that q(x) := supT ∈S pV (Tx), x ∈X, defines a seminorm on X which is
sequentially continuous since S is sequentially equicontinuous by (a). The set U := {x ∈X | q(x) < 1} is
