Some lower bounds for the maximal number of A-singularities
in algebraic surfaces. II
Juan Garc´ıa Escudero
Abstract. Algebraic surfaces in the complex projective space with a high number of A-type singu-
larities have been presented in a recent paper. We extend the construction in order to obtain lower
bounds for the maximal number of A singularities for certain additional cases.
Mathematics Subject Classification (2010). 14B05, 14J17, 14J70.
Keywords. singularities, algebraic surfaces.
1. Introduction
A classical problem in algebraic geometry consists of determining the possible number of singularities
of a given type for surfaces of degree d in the complex projective space P3(C). Upper bounds for the
maximal number of A-type singularities on degree d complex algebraic surfaces have been given in
[23, 20]. Lower bounds for degree d have been obtained in [7, 19, 9, 11, 14]. For a number of low degree
surfaces certain explicit constructions give better lower bounds [6, 17, 22, 2, 8, 21, 18, 5, 4]. The exact
value of the upper bounds is known only for a few cases [3, 16].
Complex algebraic surfaces of degrees d = 3q with a large number of type A singularities have
been constructed in a recent paper [14]. We extend the construction to obtain lower bounds for the
maximal number of singularities for a series of cases not considered in [14].
The affine equations of the surfaces studied in [14] consist of a sum of a bivariate polynomial
J with three critical values and a univariate polynomial B with two critical values. The surfaces
constructed in this paper have similar equations with new polynomials G replacing B. The polynomials
G have also two critical values and can be described in terms of planar trees which are obtained by a
series of transformations applied to the trees associated to B. In this way the results about cusps and
other Aν singularities obtained in [10, 11] can be seen as special cases of this constructions.
2. The families of polynomials with two critical values
A univariate polynomial with no more than two different critical values, also called a Belyi polynomial,
can be represented by a plane tree with a bicoloring for the vertices. The polynomial critical points
have the multiplicities given by the number of edges adjacent to the vertices minus one [1, 19]. The
degree of a vertex is the number of edges incident to it. A leaf vertex is a vertex with degree one.
Black and white vertices represent critical points with critical value ζ = −1 and ζ = 1 respectively,
and their multiplicities are given by the degrees of the vertices minus one. We denote by Nζ(P, ρ) the
number of critical points of a polynomial P with critical value ζ and multiplicity ρ.
