SATURATION AND ISOMORPHISM OF ABSTRACT
HARMONIC SPACES
HAOMING WANG
Abstract. This paper models the theory of abstract harmonic spaces in the
syntax of the continuous first-order logic of Banach lattices. It addresses a
topological question asking when a one-to-one harmonic map onto smooth
manifolds Mn is a diffeomorphism. We give Mn (n ≤2) a characterization
by U-rank and elementary saturation for large cardinals. Polar sets are char-
acterized by several equivalent conditions from the omitting type theorem.
Consequently, harmonic measures on the ideal boundary in Martin represen-
tation are bijectively mapped to Keisler measures supported on non-principal
types. Further problems concerning o-minimality and non-local potentials are
finally discussed.
1. Introduction
Axiomatic potential theory, initiated by M. Brelot [Bre60], provides a magnif-
icent method for studying harmonic functions on locally compact spaces. At its
heart lie the structural interplays between harmonic sheaves, excessive functions,
and the balayage (sweeping) operators. This theory culminates in the Martin rep-
resentation [Mar41], later generalized to a broader class of harmonic spaces by
Constantinescu and Cornea [CC63] and Bauer [Bau66], which recovers positive
harmonic functions h(x), x ∈U through integral representations via a minimal
boundary measure ωx(dy) on ∂U,

by Choquet [Cho54].

a comprehensive language for metric structures, developed by Chang and Keisler
[CK66], Ben Yaacov, Usvyatsov, Pedersen [BYU07, BYU10, BYP10], and others.

3. Martin Compactification Space
3.1. Types and φ-types. An atomic formula has the form P(x) ≤r or P(x) = 0,
where P is a predicate symbol. General formulas are built from atomic formulas
using the continuous connective ˙−(truncated subtraction) and the quantifiers sup,
inf. Every formula φ(x) takes a value in [0, 1] in any L-structure M. A sentence is
a formula without free variables.
Definition 3.1 (Type). A type of a continuous theory T is a set Σ of L-formulas
with a common finite tuple of free variables x such that there exists a model M |= T
and an element a ∈M with φM(a) = 0 for all φ ∈Σ.
Definition 3.2 (Realization and omission). Let Σ be a type of T.
• We say that a model M |= T realizes Σ if there exists a ∈M such that
φM(a) = 0 for every φ ∈Σ; in this case we also say that a realizes Σ in M.
• If a model M does not realize Σ, we say that M omits Σ.
Definition 3.3 (Partial and complete types). Let M be an L-structure and A ⊆M
a set of parameters.
• A partial type (over A) is a family of formulas Σ with parameters from
A that is consistent with T, that is, there exist an elementary extension
M′ ≻M and a ∈M′ such that φM′(a) = 0 for all φ ∈Σ.
• A complete type over A is a maximal consistent partial type over A. The
space of all complete 1-types over A is denoted by S1(A).
Definition 3.4 (φ-type). Given a formula φ(x; y), a φ-type (over a parameter set
A) is a complete type that is determined solely by instances of the form φ(x; b) = r
with b ∈A. The space of all such complete φ-types is denoted by Sφ(A); when
A = ∅we write Sφ(T).
Definition 3.5 (Principal type). A type Σ(x) of T is principal over T if there exist
an L-formula ϕ(y), terms t1(y), . . . , tn(y) (where n = |x|), and a rational number
r ∈Q ∩(0, 1) such that:
• T ∪{ϕ(y)} is satisfiable,
• T ∪{ϕ(y) ≤r} |= Σ(t1(y), . . . , tn(y)), i.e. in every model of T, whenever
ϕ(b) ≤r holds, the tuple (t1(b), . . . , tn(b)) realizes Σ.
In this case we say that ϕ and r witness the fact that Σ is principal over T.

5. Examples
In this section, we introduce an analogue of rank for types in the harmonic
setting. The guiding principle is that forking corresponds to a loss of harmonic
capacity, measured by the definable sweeping operator. In this framework, low-
rank types behave like rigid, locally determined objects, whereas higher-rank types
encode boundary pathologies and irregularity phenomena.
Let T be the Brelot theory and let M |= T be a κ-saturated model, where κ is an
uncountable regular cardinal. All parameter sets are assumed to have cardinality
strictly less than κ.
Definition 5.1 (Non-forking). Let A ⊆B be parameter sets and let q ∈S(B) be
a complete type extending p ∈S(A). The type q does not fork over A if for every
positive superharmonic function u definable over B with Eval(x, u) = 0 in q, the
sweeping of u onto the closure of A coincides with the sweeping onto the closure of
B:
Balcl(A)(u) = Balcl(B)(u).
Otherwise, q forks over A.
Definition 5.2 (U-rank). For a complete type p ∈S(A), the harmonic U-rank is
defined by transfinite recursion.
(1) U(p) ≥0.
(2) U(p) ≥α + 1 if there exists a parameter set B ⊇A and a forking extension
q ∈S(B) of p with U(q) ≥α.
(3) U(p) = α if α is the least ordinal such that U(p) ̸≥α + 1.
(4) If U(p) ≥α for every ordinal α, then U(p) = ∞.
A type p has U(p) = 0 if and only if it is algebraic, that is, there exists a positive
superharmonic function u definable over the parameters of p such that the formula
Eval(x, u) = 0 belongs to p and the zero set of Eval(·, u) is polar.

6. Further Remarks
The present axiom scheme is not expected to be complete a priori. Rather, it
should be viewed as a class of harmonic structures whose complete theories are
obtained by fixing a concrete model or a canonical representation. A more refined
first-order logic analysis may reveal whether the resulting theory is simple, or per-
haps merely stable on the harmonic fragment, with balayage and Harnack control
suggesting a weak form of independence. In low-dimensional settings, the rigidity
of harmonic extension also suggests an analogy with o-minimal tameness. Defin-
able sets may exhibit a strong geometric regularity, although a literal o-minimal
interpretation is unlikely in full generality.
