Spin-current model of electric polarization with the tensor gyromagnetic ratio
Mariya Iv.
Trukhanova1,2∗and Pavel A. Andreev1†
1 Faculty of Physics, Lomonosov Moscow State University, Leninskie Gory 1, 119991 Moscow, Russia
2 Theoretical Physics Laboratory, Nuclear Safety Institute,
Russian Academy of Sciences, B. Tulskaya 52, 115191 Moscow, Russia
The spin-current model of electric polarization of spin origin is developed for a magnetic structure
with anisotropic tensor gyromagnetic ratio (g-factor). Three mechanisms of the magnetoelectric
effect are proposed, caused by the symmetric Heisenberg exchange interaction, the Dzyaloshinsky-
Moriya interaction, and the spin-spin interaction related to the odd anisotropy of the symmetric
exchange interaction of magnetic ions via nonmagnetic ion. The dependence of electric polarization
on the spin density and tensor g-factor in the generalized spin-current model is derived.
New
solutions for macroscopic electric polarization, that arise in the cycloidal and helicoidal spin orders
and are caused by the non-diagonal components of the gyromagnetic ratio, are predicted.
The
extended of the spin-current model to including a tensor g-factor can be important for the magnetic
ferroelectrics with heavy ions which take part in the formation of magnetoelectric effect.
I.
INTRODUCTION
The physics of the magnetoelectric effect and multifer-
roic materials is greatly developed and extended in recent
years [1, 2]. At the same time, the focus of modern re-
search is still on understanding the microscopic origin of
the magnetoelectric effect of spin origin and constructing
its theoretical description [3, 4]. Multiferroics are divided
into two main groups [5] depending on the relation be-
tween their magnetic and ferroelectric properties. The
main example of I-type multiferroics is bismuth ferrite
BiFeO3, which has high critical magnetic and ferroelec-
tric temperatures and differ by an order of magnitude
[6, 7].
Thus, the magnetic and ferroelectric orders in
similar materials are rather weakly related [8]. In II-type
multiferroics, ferroelectricity is induced by a magnetic
ordering [9, 10], and often appears in conjunction with
a spiral magnetic phase, which can emerge in mangan-
ite oxide perovskites like RMnO3 (R =Tb, Dy, EuY) or
Ni3V2O6 [9].
The basic principle for understanding strongly corre-
lated magnetic dielectrics is the principle of symmetry
[11]. The necessary conditions for the existence of the
magnetoelectric effect in materials is the violation of the
time-reversal symmetry and the space-inversion symme-
try (T- and P-parity) separately, but the conservation of
the combined PT-parity [12]. But, for a clearer under-
standing of the physical principles of the magnetoelectric
effect, various models and new description principles are
proposed. In accordance with Ref. [13], two main mecha-
nisms of formation of the ferroelectricity of spin origin are
developed to understand the nature of the electric dipole
moment. The first mechanism appears from the symmet-
ric spin-exchange interaction in collinear magnetic struc-
tures without the necessary involvement of the spin-orbit
interaction [14, 15].
The second mechanism produces
polarization under the influence of spin-orbit coupling

[13]. Y. Tokura explains that the electric dipole moment
in this case appears in a cluster consisting of two mag-
netic ions and an oxygen ion between them. The descrip-
tion of the ferroelectricity in non-collinear spin orders in
such a cluster is proposed by Katsura, Nagaosa, and Bal-

In this model, the oxygen ion does not shift from the
middle point of the bond of two magnetic ions and the
microscopic electric dipole moment appears as a result
of electronic density deformation.
On the other hand,
attempt to describe magnetoelectric effect in spiral spin
structures is taken by I. Sergienko and E. Dagotto, with
the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction mechanism [17],
where the shift of the oxygen ion from the middle point
leads to the electric dipole moment formation.
A new theoretical model based on the method of many-
body quantum hydrodynamics is developed and applied
to substantiate the nature of electric polarization in var-
ious multiferroic spin structures [18]. The spin-current
model of electric polarization is justified and general-
ized for the ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic states
using this method and the mean field part of the spin-
orbit interaction [18–20], where the displacement of the
oxygen ion from the middle point of the bond of mag-
netic ions and the arbitrary spin structure of the mag-
netic sublattice are taken into consideration.
The di-
rect relation between electric polarization and effective
spin currents allows us to reconstruct the structure of
microscopic dipole moments caused by Heisenberg and
Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya exchange interactions. The anal-
ysis presented in [19] shows that considering the sym-
metric exchange interaction between ions in the form of
the Heisenberg Hamiltonian leads to the structure of the
electric dipole moment which is proportional to the vec-
tor product of spins, and the form of electric polarization
which corresponds the result of M. Mostovoy accurate
to the coefficient of proportionality [11]. On the other
hand, it is also shown that the antisymmetric anysotropic
Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya exchange interaction leads to the
structure of electric dipole moment which is proportional
to the scalar product of two neighboring spins.

B.
The anisotropic g-factor of heavy ions
It is well known that the magnetic moment is related to
the mechanical momet through the gyromagnetic ratio.
Let us discuss the introduction of the gyromagnetic ratio
tensor in more detail. In light atoms, the electrostatic
interaction of electrons in the atomic shell structure sig-
nificantly exceeds the spin-orbit interaction, and the so-
called LS-bond is formed. In this case, on specific time
scales, the effective magnetic moment and the total me-
chanical moment Ji of the i-th ion is approximately co-
directional µi = giµBJi when averaged over time. The
Zeeman energy in Hamiltonian is determined by the ex-
pression Hzeeman = −µi · Bi = −giµBJi · Bi, where gi
and µB are the Lande factor for ion and Bohr magneton,
respectively. When the nuclear charge number in atoms
exceeds 27, the energy of the residual electrostatic in-
teraction becomes less than the energy of the spin-orbit
interaction, and the LS-bond begins to break, leading to
a tensor nature of the gyromagnetic ratio. The breaking

especially important in describing the magnetoelectric ef-
fect. The symmetry of the Mn-O-Mn bonds is low and all
these bonds are noncentrosymmetric, which can lead to
additional contributions to electric polarization, besides
the predictions of the original spin-current model.
Thereby, when we describe a magnetoelectric phenom-
ena, we need to take into consideration that the LS-bond
does not work and the magnetic moments of transition
and rare-earth metal ions are not collinear to the me-
chanical moments. This feature of magnetoelectric ma-
terials should be taken into account in the development
of the spin-current model. We use the tensor nature of
the gyromagnetic ratio (g-factor) in the construction of
the many-particle interaction Hamiltonian (1).

VII.
CONCLUSION
In this paper a mechanism of the magnetoelectric ef-
fect, based on the effective spin currents, caused by dif-
ferent types of interactions, has been theoretically devel-
oped. We have described and justified the spin-current
model of Katsura, Nagaosa, and Balatsky (KNB) when
taking into account the tensor gyromagnetic ratio (or g-
factor), using the basic principles of the many-particle
quantum hydrodynamics method.
Our description is
based on the idea of a cluster model with two mag-
netic positively charged ions with the diamagnetic neg-
atively charged oxygen atom O2−between them, where
each cluster is considered as a point-like quasi-particle in
the many-particle description of quantum hydrodynam-
ics. However, in contrast to approach of KNB, the oxy-
gen ions can shift from the middle point of the magnetic
ions bond. Using the results of the investigated model,
we have considered main mechanisms of ferroelectricity
of spin origin. The first one, is the symmetric exchange
Heisenberg interaction. In the spin current, caused by
this type of interaction, the displacement of oxygen ions
is not explicitly taken into account for the occurrence
of polarization, although it should not be zero for the


## Page 9


9
antisymmetric Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction which
leads to the oxygen ion shift and the formation of the
canted spin structure. We demonstrate that the symmet-
ric exchange coupling leads to the effective spin current,
and this spin current produces the electric polarization
in cycloidal and screw (helicoidal) spin systems.
But,
unlike the isotropic case (16), where the cycloidal spin
order and the scalar g-factor give only one projection of
electric polarization (18), the non-diagonal component of
g-factor leads to the additional out-of-plane projection of
electric polarization (20), (21). For the screw spin order,
we have obtained non-zero components of electric polar-
ization (24), (25), also caused by non- diagonal elements
of gyromagnetic ratio, that is atypical for the isotropic
case.
In the second mechanism electric polarization caused
by the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction is determined
by the displacement vector of the oxygen ion from the
middle point of the bond and the square of the spin den-
sity. This means that this polarization can occur even
in systems with collinear spins, in contrast to the spin-
current model of KNB, where only a non-collinear struc-
ture is considered and electric polarization arises from the
deformation of electronic density. Solutions have been
also derived for the cycloidal and helicoidal spin orders
(30) - (32). We have demonstrated results of the model
for the ferromagnetic state, but its can be extended to
the antiferromagnetic orders.
In addition, we have considered the new mechanism of
electric polarization formation caused by the novel form
of the spin-spin interaction related to the odd anisotropy
of the symmetric exchange interaction. This is the indi-
rect exchange that is carried out through the oxygen ion,
that shifts from the middle point. We have derived the
electric polarization for the isotropic scalar g-factor (37)
and generalized the result on the case of tensor gyromag-
netic ratio (44). New solutions for electric polarization
which can be induced by this type of interactions have
been predicted (43), (45) and (46).
