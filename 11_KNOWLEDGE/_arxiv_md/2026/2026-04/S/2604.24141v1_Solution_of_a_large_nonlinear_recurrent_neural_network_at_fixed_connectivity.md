Introduction
Random recurrent neural networks are one of the only analytically tractable models of
strongly recurrent dynamics, and they provide a natural setting to study how synaptic
connectivity and external inputs shape neural dynamics [1–4]. In this model, a collec-
tion of N neurons communicate with one another through a set of random connections.
The preactivations of the units are denoted by xi(t) and the random connections by a
matrix Jij with 1 ≤i, j ≤N. The dynamics follow

Sketch of the proof
We now sketch the proof of Eqs. (1.4), (1.6), and (1.8). The strategy is to identify
the diagrams that survive at leading nontrivial order in 1/
√

involving higher order correlation functions systematically cancel each other, and resum
the remaining series. The result is a closed-form solution for moments and response
functions of intensive order.
The first step is to identify the leading order diagrams. Since J is held fixed, this
cannot be done by averaging the diagrams over J. Instead, for a typical realization of J,
we estimate the magnitude of a diagram D through its mean square ⟨|D|2⟩J (Sec. 4.2).
This turns the large N problem into a counting problem: each summed index brings
a factor of N, while each Jij gap brings a factor of N −1/2 to the average ⟨|D|2⟩J. The
leading diagrams are therefore those maximizing the number of summed indices at a
given order of the expansion. This argument shows that the leading order diagrams have
the form of the chains and rainbows shown in the examples above with no additional
connections drawn between the m/2 blocks. The latter condition corresponds to an
effective Wick factorization of the moments. The subleading diagrams are suppressed
by a factor of N −1/2 relative to the diagrams that are retained.
What types of sums do the remaining diagrams correspond to? The chain diagrams
are constrained products of J and J⊤in which each contracted index appears no more
than twice. As a result, every average ⟨·⟩0 appearing in a chain is a two-point function.
On the other hand, the rainbow diagrams involve products of J and J⊤with the
diagonal entries of JJ⊤. As such, they lead to a greater number of repeated indices
and generate four- and higher-point functions in the ⟨·⟩0 average. Diagrams involving
∆bubbles similarly generate higher correlation functions.

Conclusion
We have given a closed-form solution to arbitrary, intensive-order moments and re-
sponse functions in a nonlinear random recurrent neural network. Our results hold
for a fixed instance of the quenched synaptic variables Jij and are valid to leading
nontrivial order in 1/
√
N. In agreement with these results, Clark has developed an
alternative cavity derivation of the two-point functions, Eqs. (1.4) and (1.6); see Ref.
[13]. We note in addition that a recent work, Ref. [14], derived a related Wick formula
for intensive-order Fourier transformed moments at zero frequency ω = 0, after averag-
ing over the Jij. Our results complement these findings by providing a general formula
for intensive-order correlation and response functions at fixed Jij.
Our derivation involves perturbatively expanding around the mean-field solution
of the system, Eq. (1.1). Dynamical mean-field theory has been widely used to study
the dynamics of large disordered systems. Notable examples include the Lotka-Volterra
model of ecosystems [15], the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev model [16, 17], and various spin glasses
[18–20]. A particularly relevant class of models for neuroscience are random recurrent
neural networks with excitatory-inhibitory constraints on the synapses [3]. It is possible
that the arguments developed here can be used to analyze the behavior of these models
at fixed instances of the quenched disorder.
