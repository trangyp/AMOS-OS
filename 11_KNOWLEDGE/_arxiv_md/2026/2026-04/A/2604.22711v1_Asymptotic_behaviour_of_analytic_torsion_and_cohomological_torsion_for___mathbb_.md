Abstract. We extend the refined asymptotics of analytic torsion associated to con-
gruence subgroups of SL(n) in [Ber25], to congruence subgroups in a large family of
reductive groups. This is applied to give new asymptotics and bounds on the growth of
torsion in the cohomology of congruence subgroups of SL(2, OF ) for F a number field,
and of congruence subgroups in SO(n, 1) with n odd.

1. Introduction
For more than a decade, analytic torsion has been a valuable tool in studying growth
of torsion in the cohomology of arithmetic groups. In the seminal paper of Bergeron
and Venkatesh [BV13], they use analytic torsion to establish exponential growth for
an alternating sum of torsion cohomology with certain non-trivial coefficient systems
of families of cocompact arithmetic groups. Below we introduce their conjectures and
results before moving on to more recent generalizations.
1.1. Conjectures and the cocompact case. Let G be a connected semisimple al-
gebraic group defined over Q, and let Γ ⊆G(Q) be an arithmetic subgroup. A good
example to keep in mind is G = SL(n) and Γ = SL(n, Z). Let M be a Γ-module arising
from a rational finite-dimensional representation of G(R). Then the group cohomology
H∗(Γ, M) has long been understood to have number theoretic significance, such as its
link to automorphic forms by the work of Franke [Fra98]. If one now considers a lattice
L ⊆M stabilized by Γ, one can also consider the cohomology groups H∗(Γ, L). These
are finitely generated abelian groups, and these, too, carry number theoretic information.
In particular, it was conjectured by Ash [Ash92], and later partially proven by Scholze
[Sch15], that there is a correspondence between Hecke eigensystems in H∗(Γ, Fp) and
certain Galois representations ρ : Gal(Q/Q) →GL(n, Fp).
Investigating this cohomology, Bergeron–Venkatesh [BV13] gave the following heuris-
tic. Let K ⊆G(R) be a maximal compact subgroup, and define the deficiency of G
as δ(G) := rankC G(R) −rankC K. Then there is "a lot" of torsion in Hi(Γ, L) when
δ(G) = 1 and i is the middle dimension, and "little" torsion otherwise. To make this
precise, they make the following conjecture, generalized in [AGMY20].

Inspired by this question, this paper establishes subexponential upper bounds on the
size of torsion in the cohomology of certain families of subgroups. On the way we also
establish results on second order terms when δ(G) = 1. The results are obtained for
non-cocompact subgroups, and we introduce this generalization now.
1.2. Generalizations and results on analytic torsion. Since many important arith-
metic subgroups are not cocompact, a lot of consequent work has focused on removing
this assumption from (1.1). This was first successful for arithmetic groups associated to
hyperbolic manifolds of finite volume, see [Pfa14], [Rai19], [PR20], [MR21], and it was
recently extended to other Q-rank 1 arithmetic groups in [MR26].
All of the results quoted above are consequences of approximation theorems on analytic
torsion, combined with Cheeger-Müller theorems, identities relating analytic torsion to
Reidemeister torsion (see [Mül78],[Che79], also [Mül93],[BZ94]). Analytic torsion and
Reidemeister torsion are both invariants of Riemannian manifolds, the former built from
the spectra of p-form Laplacians (see Section 4), and the latter from topological data
of the manifold ([Rei35], also [Che79]). The consequence of Cheeger-Müller theorems is
that one may investigate cohomology by analytic methods.
At the time of the Bergeron–Venkatesh paper, there was no general definition of an-
alytic torsion outside of compact Riemannian manifolds. To remedy this, Matz–Müller
gave a definition and the necessary convergence results in [MM17], [MM20], [MM23] to
show that a non-cocompact generalization of analytic torsion also satisfies the desired
approximation theorem, namely ([MM23], Theorem 1.5).
Our method of getting improved bounds and second order terms on torsion cohomol-
ogy is sharpening the asymptotics of their approximation theorem. This is the main
technical result of this paper, and its preparation and proof will occupy most of the
