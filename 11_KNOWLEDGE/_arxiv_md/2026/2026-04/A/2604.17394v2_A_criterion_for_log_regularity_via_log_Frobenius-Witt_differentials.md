For a ring A and an A-module M, a map D: A →M is said to be an FW-derivation

In this paper, we introduce logarithmic analogues of FW-derivations and the
modules of FW-differentials for prelogarithmic rings.
For a prelogarithmic ring
(R, Q, α), we denote the module of logarithmic FW-differentials by FΩ(R,Q,α).
Moreover, we give a logarithmic regularity criterion of the following form:
Main Theorem (Theorem 4.5). Let (R, Q, α) be a local prelog ring such that R
is a Noetherian local ring with the residue field k of characteristic p > 0, that Q
is integral, and that Q is fine and saturated. Assume that k is F-finite and set
[k : kp] = pr. Consider the following conditions:
(1) FΩ(R,Q,α) ⊗R R/pR is a free R/pR-module of rank dim(R) + r.
(2) FΩ(R,Q,α) ⊗R k is a k-vector space of dimension dim(R) + r.
(3) (R, Q, α) is log regular.
Then we always have (1) ⇒(2) ⇔(3). Moreover, if R/pR is F-finite, then the
three conditions (1), (2), and (3) are equivalent.
Here, we give some comments on the proof of Main Theorem. First, we reduce
to the case where Q is sharp. Next, we prove that the conditions (2) and (3) are
equivalent by using [Sai22, Proposition 2.6] mainly. Finally, we prove (3) ⇒(1) by
passing to completions.
We expect that, as in the non-logarithmic case, the modules of logarithmic FW-
differentials have a relation with logarithmic cotangent complexes, which were es-
tablished by Olsson and Gabber in [Ols05].
The content of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we recall notions and basic
properties of monoids and (pre)logarithmic rings including logarithmic regularity.
In Section 3, we give the definition of logarithmic FW-derivations for prelogarith-
mic rings (Definition 3.2) and prove that every prelogarithmic ring has a universal
logarithmic FW-derivation (Theorem 3.4). Moreover, we study basic properties of
the modules of logarithmic FW-differentials.
In Section 4, we prove Main Theorem (Theorem 4.5) after some lemmas.

Definition 2.1 ([Ogu18, Chapter I, Section 1.3]). Let Q be a monoid.
(1) Q is called integral if the canonical homomorphism Q →Qgp is an injection.
(2) Q is called fine if Q is finitely generated and integral.
(3) Q is called saturated if Q is integral and Q has the following property: if

(4) Q is called sharp if Q× = 1.
