CORRESPONDENCE FOR CLOSED 1-FORMS
YOTA SHAMOTO
Abstract. Motivated by the work of Kontsevich–Soibelman on the comparison
of isomorphisms conjecture for closed algebraic 1-forms, we establish a Riemann–
Hilbert correspondence of Deligne–Malgrange type.
As an application, we prove a variant of the comparison of isomorphisms
theorem for a simple class of algebraic 1-forms on complex curves.
1. Introduction
In this paper, we reinterpret a conjecture of Kontsevich–Soibelman [8, Conjec-
ture 4.7.1], which is known as the comparison of isomorphisms conjecture, from the
perspective of the Deligne–Malgrange Riemann–Hilbert correspondence [3,10]. We
begin in §1.1 by recalling the part of [8] concerning the comparison of isomorphisms
conjecture. After the preparatory discussions in §1.2, §1.3, and §1.4, we formulate
our Riemann–Hilbert correspondence in §1.5. Finally, in §1.6, we describe our for-
mulation of a one-dimensional version of the comparison of isomorphisms theorem.

1.2. Background of the remark. If the 1-form α is exact, that is, if there exists
a meromorphic function f on X with poles in D such that α = df, then one obtains
a globally defined connection in the z-direction: ∇= d + d(f/z). It follows that
the global de Rham cohomology is naturally equipped with a connection in the
z-direction. Let (E, ∇) denote the associated germ of a meromorphic connection
over C({z}). One can apply the Deligne–Malgrange Riemann–Hilbert functor [3] to
(E, ∇) to obtain a Stokes-filtered local system on the circle. The underlying local
system can be identified with the global Betti cohomology.
When α is not exact, however, there is in general no globally defined connection in
the z-direction. Kontsevich–Soibelman [8] overcome this difficulty as follows. In [8,
Section 2], they reinterpret the Stokes structure of exponential integrals associated
with f, i.e., the Stokes-filtered local system associated with (E, ∇) in terms of wall-
crossing structure.
They generalize the wall-crossing structure to the non-exact
case in [8, Section 3]. This viewpoint appears to underlie their remark [8, Remark
4.7.1]. Their approach is ambitious, encompassing infinite-dimensional situations;
however, its relation to the classical Riemann–Hilbert correspondence becomes less
transparent.

1.7. Further directions. The pair (X, α), both in dimension one and in higher di-
mensions, naturally appears as a mirror Landau-Ginzburg model of a semi-projective
variety X∨equipped with a torus action [5]. It would be natural to formulate equi-
variant gamma conjecture III as an equivariant generalization of [16] using the
Riemann–Hilbert correspondence established in this paper (Theorem 3.17).
It is also worth mentioning that when α is exact, i.e., α = df, the Hodge theory
associated with f has been extensively developed in the framework of exponential,
irregular, and non-commutative Hodge structures. This theory can be viewed as
providing a substantial enrichment of the integrable twistor structure naturally
attached to f. See, for instance, [3, 4, 6, 12, 13, 15], without attempting to give a
complete list. When α is closed but not exact, one is naturally led to the theory
of non-integrable twistor structures [11]. Understanding the relationship between
our de Rham and Betti structures associated with α and the corresponding non-
integrable twistor structures might be an interesting direction for future research.
