A QUASI-PENTAGON EQUATION FOR A HEISENBERG DOUBLE OF A
QUASI-HOPF ALGEBRA
YOHEI OTA
Abstract. For a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra H, the canonical elements of the Heisenberg doubles
H(H∗) and H(H) satisfy the pentagon and Hopf equations, respectively. In this paper we construct quasi-
Hopf analogues of these structures.
For a finite-dimensional quasi-Hopf algebra H, we consider natural
quasi-Hopf analogues H1(H∗) and H1(H) of H(H∗) and H(H).
Although their canonical elements are
defined just as in the Hopf algebra case, they need not be invertible. We prove that there nevertheless exist
natural inverse-like elements. In H1(H∗), the canonical element satisfies a quasi-pentagon equation and its
inverse-like element satisfies a quasi-Hopf equation, while in H1(H) the roles are reversed.
1. Introduction
For a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra H, the Heisenberg double H(H∗) of the dual Hopf algebra H∗
carries a canonical element W satisfying the pentagon equation (see [Kas96]1):
W12W13W23 = W23W12.
Since W is invertible, its inverse ˜W satisfies
˜W23 ˜W13 ˜W12 = ˜W12 ˜W23.
Following Militaru’s terminology [Mil98], we call an identity of this form the Hopf equation.
By [Lu94,
Proposition 5.3], H(H) is isomorphic to the opposite algebra H(H∗)op. It follows that the canonical element
of H(H) satisfies the Hopf equation, while its inverse satisfies the pentagon equation.
These identities are useful for several reasons. First, Kashaev showed that the Drinfel’d double can be
realized as a subalgebra of H(H∗)⊗H(H), and that its universal R-matrix admits a factorized expression as
a product of four canonical elements in this Heisenberg-double setting, two coming from H(H∗) and H(H)
and two of mixed type. As a consequence, the Yang–Baxter equation can be derived from repeated use
of the pentagon and Hopf equations [Kas96]. Second, pentagon relations arising from Heisenberg doubles
also have topological applications. For instance, Mihalache, Suzuki, and Terashima constructed an invariant
of closed oriented 3-manifolds from the Heisenberg double of a finite-dimensional involutory Hopf algebra;
in their construction, the pentagon equation is the local algebraic relation underlying invariance under the
relevant moves [MST24,Suz18].
When H is a quasi-Hopf algebra, several inequivalent generalizations of the Heisenberg double are known;
see [PVO04]. In [BC03,PVO04], the first Heisenberg double H1(H) was introduced. In the present paper,
we also need a quasi-Hopf analogue of the Heisenberg double H(H∗) of the dual Hopf algebra H∗. Although
the definition of this object is naturally suggested by that of H1(H), we define it explicitly in Section 3
and denote it by H1(H∗). In the Hopf algebra case, once H(H) is defined, H(H∗) is obtained by the same
construction, since H∗is again a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra. For a finite-dimensional quasi-Hopf algebra
H, however, the linear dual H∗is in general not a quasi-Hopf algebra; instead, it carries the structure of
a dual quasi-Hopf algebra.
Accordingly, in the quasi-Hopf setting H1(H∗) and H1(H) must be defined
separately rather than by applying to H∗the same formal construction that defines H1(H). Here H1(H∗)
is an algebra object in the monoidal category MH of right H-modules, while H1(H) is an algebra object
in the monoidal category HM of left H-modules. (For comparison, the second Heisenberg double H2(H)
in [PVO04] is an algebra object in MH, while the third Heisenberg double H3(H) considered by Panaite
[Pan07] is an algebra object in the bimodule category D(H)MD(H)cop, where D(H) is the Drinfel’d double
of H.) Since H is a quasi-Hopf algebra, these multiplications are in general not associative.

A further motivation for the present paper comes from the class of quasi-Hopf algebras of the form kω(G),
where k is a field, G is a finite group, and ω is a normalized 3-cocycle on G with values in k×. We refer
to [DPR90] for the twisted quantum double Dω(G) associated with a finite group and a 3-cocycle, and
to [DW90] for the related finite-group and group-cohomological constructions in topological gauge theory.
In this setting, one is naturally led to consider an embedding of the twisted quantum double Dω(G) into
H1(kω(G)∗)⊗H1(kω(G)), extending Kashaev’s embedding [Kas96] to the twisted case. A related construction
for the twisted quantum double will appear elsewhere [Ota]. Under such an embedding, the two sides of
the quasi-Yang–Baxter equation should be related by repeated use of the quasi-pentagon and quasi-Hopf
equations. This indicates that, at least for this particular class of quasi-Hopf algebras, the quasi-pentagon
and quasi-Hopf equations play a role analogous to that played by the pentagon and Hopf equations in the
Hopf algebra case, where they are related to Yang–Baxter-type equations through Kashaev-type embeddings.
Whether similar constructions exist for more general quasi-Hopf algebras, or whether they lead to genuinely
new topological constructions such as 3-manifold invariants, remains an interesting open problem.

3. The first Heisenberg doubles
In this section, we introduce the first Heisenberg doubles H1(H∗) and H1(H) for a quasi-Hopf algebra H.
As explained in the Introduction, these two algebras must be defined separately. Here, H1(H∗) is an algebra
in the monoidal category MH of right H-modules, whereas H1(H) is an algebra in the monoidal category
HM of left H-modules. Unlike in the Hopf case, both are generally nonassociative as ordinary algebras.
We use the standard left and right actions of H on H∗: for h, a ∈H and ξ ∈H∗,
(h ⇀ξ)(a) := ξ(ah),
(ξ ↼h)(a) := ξ(ha).
