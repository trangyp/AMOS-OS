1. Introduction
Fisher [Fis75] and Baur [Bau75, Theorem 1] showed in the seventies that if T
is a complete first-order theory of R-modules then T is stable. A natural research
direction is to determine if the previous result can be extended beyond first-order
model theory. Recently, the focus has been on the following question for abstract
elementary classes:
Question 1.1 ([Maz21, Question 2.12]). Let R be an associative unital ring and
denote the pure submodule relation by ≤p. If (K, ≤p) is an abstract elementary
class such that K is a class of R-modules, is (K, ≤p) stable? Is this true if R = Z?
For many years the evidence pointed to a positive answer as for example it was
shown to be positive for the following classes of modules: torsion-free abelian groups
[BET07], R-modules [KuMa20], torsion abelian groups [Maz21], ℵ1-free abelian
groups [Maz21], R-flat modules [LRV23], R-absolutely pure modules [Maz23], and
R-flat modules of dimension ≤n [MaTr].2 Surprisingly, in December 2025 Paolini
and Shelah [PaSh] constructed a class of torsion-free abelian groups which shows
that the answer to Question 1.1 is negative even when R = Z.
The purpose of this paper is to study a variation of the example of Paolini and
Shelah [PaSh, Section 4]. This is pursued in order to better understand the core
mechanism of the Paolini-Shelah construction and to try to use this knowledge to
isolate general conditions that imply stability for AECs of modules.

The reason stability fails in our example is essentially the same reason as that
of [PaSh, Section 4], but the abstract elementary class we construct around the
abelian groups that witness unstability is described by fewer conditions which are
moreover easier to check than those of [PaSh, Section 4]. More importantly, our
abstract elementary class is likely better behaved than that of [PaSh]. To be precise,
it is unclear if the example of Paolini and Shelah satisfies Conditions (2) and (4) of

Of particular interest to us is that ˆK is (< ℵ0)-tame as one could have hoped
that the answer to Question 1.1 would be affirmative under this strong assumption.

are canonically built (see Proposition 3.7 and Theorem 3.9).
Beyond answering Question 1.1, we think that the existence of AECs of modules
that are not stable provides additional evidence that abstract elementary classes
are noticeably more complicated objects than their first-order counterparts.

2. Preliminaries
We briefly introduce the notions of abelian group theory and abstract elementary
classes that will be used in this paper. Further details on abelian group theory can
be consulted in [Fuc15] and on abstract elementary classes in [Bal09].
Abelian groups. All groups discussed in this paper are abelian groups. Given a
group G and n ∈Z, we let nG = {ng : g ∈G}.
An abelian group G is torsion-free if every 0 ̸= g ∈G has infinite order. We say
that H is a pure subgroup of G, denoted by H ≤p G, if H is a subgroup of G and
for every n ∈Z, nH = H ∩nG. If G, H are torsion-free groups, to check purity it
is enough to show that pH = H ∩pG for every prime number p.

Abstract elementary classes. Abstract elementary classes (or AECs) were in-
troduced by Shelah in the seventies [She87a]. An abstract elementary class is a pair
K = (K, ≤K) where K is a class of L-structures (for some fixed finitary language
L = L(K))3 and ≤K is a partial order on K extending the substructure relation4
such that K is closed under isomorphisms and increasing continuous ≤K-chains;
and satisfies coherence and a version of the downward L¨owenheim-Skolem theorem.
See [Bal09, Definition 4.1] for the full definition.
Given M ∈K, we will write |M| for the underlying set of the model and ∥M∥for
its cardinality. We say that f : M →N is a K-embedding if f : M ∼= f[M] ≤K N.
In this paper all K-embeddings will be pure embeddings.
We will investigate the following three properties in this paper.
Definition 2.2. Let K = (K, ≤K) be an AEC.
(1) K has no maximal models if for every M ∈K, there exists an N ∈K with
M ≤K N and |M| ⊊|N|.
(2) K has the joint embedding property if for every M1, M2 ∈K, there exist
N ∈K and K-embeddings f1 : M1 →N and f2 : M2 →N.
(3) K has the amalgamation property if every span of K-embeddings N1 ←
M →N2 can be completed to a commutative square of K-embeddings.
The main example of this paper has the additional property of admitting in-
tersections. This is a strong property that often fails even for elementary classes.
These AECs were introduced in [BaSh08] and further studied in [Vas17, Section 2].
