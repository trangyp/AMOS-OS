for Properly Colored Spanning Trees
Yuhang Baia

Abstract
In the Properly Colored Spanning Tree problem, we are given an edge-colored undirected graph and
the goal is to find a spanning tree in which any two adjacent edges have distinct colors. Since finding such
a tree is NP-hard in general, previous work often relied on minimum color degree conditions to guarantee
the existence of properly colored spanning trees. While it is known that every connected edge-colored
graph G contains a properly colored tree of order at least min{|V (G)|, 2δc(G)}, where δc(G) denotes the
minimum number of colors incident to a vertex, we study the algorithmic above-guarantee problem for
properly colored trees. We provide a polynomial-time algorithm that constructs a properly colored tree
of order at least min{|V (G)|, 2δc(G) + 1} in a connected edge-colored graph G, whenever such a tree
exists.
Keywords: Properly colored tree, Above-guarantee problem, Polynomial-time algorithm

Degree conditions for spanning structures are a central topic in extremal graph theory.

properly colored tree of order at least min{|V (G)|, 2δc(G) + 1}, and output one if it exists.
1.1
Related work and motivation
Finding properly colored spanning trees in graphs is closely related to constrained spanning tree problems,
or in a more general context, to the problem of finding a basis of a matroid subject to further matroid
constraints. In what follows, we give an overview of questions that motivated our investigations.
Properly colored trees
Properly colored spanning trees were first considered in [3] where their existence
was studied from both a graph-theoretic and an algorithmic perspective. They showed that the problem
of finding a properly colored spanning tree remains NP-complete even for complete graphs. Deciding the
existence of a properly colored spanning tree is hard in general, hence, a considerable amount of work has
focused on finding sufficient conditions [4, 14, 15]. Since a properly colored spanning tree may not exist, it
is natural to ask for the maximum size of a properly colored tree not necessarily spanning all the vertices,
called the Maximum-size Properly Colored Tree problem (Max-PT). Borozan et al. [3] proved that Max-PT
is hard to approximate within a factor of 55/56 + ε for any ε > 0, although they provided polynomial-time
algorithms for graphs not containing properly colored cycles. Recently, Bai, B´erczi, Cs´aji, and Schwarcz [1]
showed that Max-PT is NP-hard to approximate within a factor of 1/n1−ε for any ε > 0, even for instances
containing a properly colored spanning tree.
Furthermore, for any fixed constant ε > 0, they provided
a polynomial-time algorithm that achieves a 1/

(2 + ε)(n −1)-approximation for Max-PT on complete
multigraphs on n vertices. Hu et al. [13] proved that the maximum order of a properly colored tree in an
edge-colored connected graph G is at least min{|V (G)|, 2δc(G)}.
Above-guarantee problems
The study of parameterized problems above or below guarantees is a well-
established paradigm in parameterized complexity; see the survey by Gutin and Mnich [12]. Following this
line of research, Fomin et al. [7] gave an FPT algorithm, parameterized by k, to decide whether a 2-connected
graph G contains a cycle of length at least min{|V (G)|, 2δ(G) + k}. Later, Fomin, Golovach, Sagunov, and
Simonov [9] provided an FPT algorithm, parameterized by k, to decide whether a 2-connected graph G
contains a cycle of length at least mad(G) + k, where mad(G) denotes the maximum average degree of G.
In addition, the same authors [8] showed that given any polynomial-time algorithm finding a cycle of length
f(L) for a subadditive function f, one can construct a cycle of length at least 2δ(G) + Ω(f(L −2δ(G))) in a
2-connected graph G. For trees, they [10] gave a randomized FPT algorithm parameterized by k to decide
whether a graph contains a given tree of order at most δ(G) + k.
Motivated by the extremal bound of Hu et al. [13] and the above-guarantee paradigm, we study properly
colored trees beyond this bound. Since the bound is tight, finding a tree of order at least min{|V (G)|, 2δc(G)+
1} is the first nontrivial step above the guarantee. This task is challenging. Unlike the uncolored cycles
considered in Dirac-type problems, we seek a tree subject to local color constraints. Moreover, beyond the
strong inapproximability of Max-PT discussed earlier, even finding a maximum properly colored forest is
a nontrivial special case of matroid 3-parity; see [2]. While above-guarantee problems for uncolored cycles

1.2
Our results
Our contribution is twofold. First, we show that finding a maximum-size rainbow tree is NP-hard even in
connected star-colored graphs (Theorem 3.1). Second, we give a polynomial-time algorithm that constructs a
rainbow tree of order at least min{|V (G)|, 2δc(G)+1} in star-colored connected graphs, provided such a tree
exists (Theorem 4.1). We extend this result to general connected edge-colored graphs by giving a polynomial-
time algorithm that decides whether a properly colored tree of order at least min{|V (G)|, 2δc(G) + 1} exists
and outputs one if so (Theorem 4.11).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce basic definitions and
notation, and overview some results that we will use in our proofs. In Section 3, we discuss the complexity
of the maximum-size rainbow tree problem in star-colored graphs. Section 4 presents our main algorithms.

Basic notation.
We denote the set of positive integers by Z+. For a positive integer k, we use [k] :=
{1, . . . , k}. Given a ground set S, the difference of X, Y ⊆S is denoted by X \ Y . If Y consists of a single
element y, then X \ {y} and X ∪{y} are abbreviated as X −y and X + y, respectively. Similarly, the single
element set {y} is often denoted by y.
All graphs considered in this paper are finite and simple. For a graph G, we denote its vertex set and
edge set by V (G) and E(G), respectively. A graph is simple if it has no parallel edges and no loops. A
simple graph is complete if it contains exactly one edge between any pair of vertices. For a graph G, we write
|G| = |V (G)| for its order. Let G = (V, E) be a graph, and X ⊆V be a subset of vertices. The subgraph
of G induced by X is denoted by G[X]. The graph obtained by deleting X is denoted by G −X. Denote
∂(X) = {xy ∈E(G): x ∈X, y /∈X} for vertex set X ⊆V .
Let c: E →[k] be an edge-coloring of G using k colors. The function c is extended to subsets of edges
where, for a subset F ⊆E of edges, c(F) denotes the set of colors appearing on the edges in F. For an
edge-colored graph G = (V, E), we use Ei = {e ∈E | c(e) = i} to denote the set of edges of color i. An
edge-colored graph is called star-colored if Ei is a star for each color i ∈[k].

Matroids.
For basic definitions on matroids and on matroid optimization, we refer the reader to [11,16].
A matroid M = (E, I) is defined by its ground set E and its family of independent sets I ⊆2E that satisfies
the independence axioms: (I1) ∅∈I, (I2) X ⊆Y, Y ∈I ⇒X ∈I, and (I3) X, Y ∈I, |X| < |Y | ⇒∃e ∈
Y \ X s.t. X + e ∈I. Members of I are called independent, while sets not in I are called dependent.
Let M be a matroid on a ground set E with rank function r. For a subset X ⊆E, the restriction of M
to X, denoted by M|X, is the matroid on X whose independent sets are exactly the independent sets of M
that are contained in X. For an element e ∈E, the contraction of M by e, denoted by M/e, is the matroid
on the ground set E −e whose rank function rM/e is defined by
rM/e(Y ) = r(Y + e) −r(e)
for any subset Y ⊆E −e. Equivalently, for e that is not a loop, a subset I ⊆E −e is independent in
M/e if and only if I + e is independent in M. The intersection of two matroids M1 and M2 is denoted by
M1 ∩M2; note that this is not a matroid in general.
Let {E1, . . . , Et} be a partition of a finite set E, and let g1, . . . , gt be non-negative integers. A partition
matroid on E is a matroid whose independent sets are the subsets I ⊆E satisfying |I ∩Ei| ≤gi for all
i ∈[t]. For a graph G = (V, E), the graphic matroid M = (E, I) of G is a matroid whose independent sets
are the forests, that is, I = {F ⊆E : F is a forest}.

5
Conclusions
In this paper, we studied the above-guarantee problem for properly colored trees in connected edge-colored
graphs.
We also presented a polynomial-time algorithm that, given a connected graph G, constructs a
properly colored tree of order at least min{|V (G)|, 2δc(G) + 1} whenever such a tree exists.
We close the paper by mentioning some open problems:
1. The most straightforward question is whether there exists an FPT algorithm, parameterized by k, that
finds a properly colored tree of order at least min{|V (G)|, 2δc(G) + k} in a connected graph G.
2. Following the framework of Fomin et al. [8], a natural question is to study the above-guarantee ap-
proximability of the maximum properly colored tree problem.
