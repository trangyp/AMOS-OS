ASSOCIATED PRIMES OF POWERS OF CLOSED NEIGHBORHOOD
IDEALS AND DIAMETERS OF GRAPHS
HA THI THU HIEN AND THANH VU
Abstract. Let G be a simple connected graph and t ≥2 an integer. We prove that if the
maximal homogeneous ideal is an associated prime of the tth power of the closed neighborhood
ideal of G, then the diameter of G is at most 7t −8. We further show that this bound is sharp
for all t ≥2.
1. Introduction
Let G be a finite simple graph with vertex set V (G) = [n] = {1, . . . , n} and edge set E(G).
For a vertex i ∈V (G), the neighborhood of i is defined as
NG(i) = {j | {i, j} ∈E(G)},
and its closed neighborhood is NG[i] = NG(i)∪{i}. Let S = k[x1, . . . , xn] be the standard graded
polynomial ring over a field k. For any subset W ⊆[n], we denote the squarefree monomial
xW = Q
j∈W xj. The closed neighborhood ideal of G, denoted by NI(G), is defined by:
NI(G) = (xNG[i] | i = 1, . . . , n).
While the combinatorics of closed neighborhood complexes is a classical subject [B, L], the
algebraic study of closed neighborhood ideals is comparatively recent [CJRS, SM, HS, NBR, NQ,
NQBM]. In [HV], Hien and Vu investigated the associated primes of the second power of NI(G)
and proved that if the maximal homogeneous ideal m = (x1, . . . , xn) is an associated prime of
S/NI(G)2, then diam(G) ≤6, and this bound is sharp. This result establishes a compelling
link between the algebraic properties of neighborhood ideals and the diameter of the underlying
graph. In this work, we generalize this connection to arbitrary powers of the ideal. We recall
the definition of the diameter of a graph and state our main result.
Definition 1.1. Let G be a simple graph. For vertices u, v ∈V (G), the distance between u
and v, denoted by distG(u, v), is the length of the shortest path between them; if no such path
exists, then distG(u, v) = ∞. The diameter of G, denoted by diam(G), is the maximum distance
between any two vertices of G.
Theorem 1.2. Let G be a simple connected graph and t ≥2 be an integer. Assume that m is
an associated prime of S/NI(G)t. Then
diam(G) ≤7t −8,
and this bound is sharp.

We note that, unlike the case t = 2, a disconnected graph G may satisfy m ∈Ass(S/NI(G)t)
when t ≥3. Indeed, m is an associated prime of S/NI(G)t if and only if depth(S/NI(G)t) = 0.
Assume that G = G1 ∪· · · ∪Gs, where Gi are the connected components of G. It follows from
[NV, Theorem 1.1] that m is an associated prime of S/NI(G)t if and only if there exist positive
integers a1, . . . , as such that a1+· · ·+as = t+s−1 and mi is an associated prime of Si/NI(Gi)ai,
where Si and mi are the polynomial ring and the maximal homogeneous ideal on the variables
corresponding to the vertices of Gi, respectively. Hence, when t ≥3 and G is disconnected, our
theorem can be applied to bound the diameters of the connected components of G. The idea
of bounding the diameter of a graph under the condition that m is an associated prime of a
power of the closed neighborhood ideal is motivated by a result of Hien, Lam, and Trung [HLT,
Proposition 2.6] on the associated primes of powers of edge ideals of graphs.
The proof technique extends the framework established by Hien and Vu [HV]. In the following
section, we provide the necessary notation and prove our main result.
2. Associated primes of powers of closed neighborhood ideals
Throughout this paper, we denote by S = k[x1, . . . , xn] the polynomial ring over a field k, and
by m = (x1, . . . , xn) its maximal homogeneous ideal. For a nonzero monomial f ∈S and an
index i with 1 ≤i ≤n, we denote by degi(f) the largest exponent r such that xr
i divides f. The
support of f, denoted by supp(f), is the set of all variables of S that divide f. For an S-module
M, the notation Ass(M) denotes the set of associated primes of M. By [HV, Lemma 2.3], we
have the following.
Lemma 2.1. Let J be a squarefree monomial ideal. Then m is an associated prime of S/Jt if
and only if there exists a monomial f such that degi(f) < t for all i and J : f = m.

Proof of Theorem 1.2. By Lemma 2.2, it suffices to show that if m ∈Ass(S/NI(G)t), then
diam(G) ≤7t −8. We therefore assume that m ∈Ass(S/NI(G)t). By Lemma 2.1, there exists

W = {u ∈U | NG[u] ⊆U}.
For ease of reading, we divide the proof into several steps.
Step 1. W is nonempty and NG[W] = U.
Clearly, f ̸= 1, hence U ̸= ∅. Let u ∈U. Since xuf ∈NI(G)t, there exist vertices i1, . . . , it

j = 1, . . . , t. Hence, i1, . . . , it ∈W, and therefore W is nonempty.
Furthermore, since f /∈NI(G)t, there exists some j such that u ∈N[ij]. Thus, u ∈NG[ij] ⊆
NG[W], and hence NG[W] = U.

f, so there exists some j such that v ∈N[vj].

Remark 2.6. By a result of Brodmann [Br], there exists an integer t0 such that Ass(S/It) =
Ass(S/It0) for all t ≥t0. The smallest such integer is called the stability index of the associated
primes of powers of I, denoted by astab(I).
With the notation as in Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 1.2, we deduce that m ∈Ass(S/NI(Gt)t+1)
but m /∈Ass(S/NI(Gt)t). In particular, astab(NI(Gt)) ≥t. Hence, this provides a family of
graphs G for which astab(NI(G)) can be arbitrarily large.
