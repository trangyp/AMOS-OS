A GENERALIZATION OF THE INVERSE MAPPING THEOREM
IN INFINITE DIMENSIONS
SAJJAD LAKZIAN
Abstract. We present a generalization of the inverse mapping theorem, where variations of
a weaker non-expansiveness property (referred to as property A) replace the key C1 condition.
We also obtain inverse mapping theorems that can be applied to non-smooth maps. Also as a
by-product of the generalized inverse mapping theorem, we prove generalizations of the implicit
function theorem and existence and uniqueness theorem of abstract PDE systems as well.
1. Introduction and Main Results
Let (B, ∥· ∥) be a Banach space. The classical inverse mapping theorem (IMT) states that
a differentiable C1 map f : U ⊂B →B (where U is an open domain) with invertible (Frechét)
derivatives everywhere, is a local diffeomorphism. It is a folklore fact that the optimal regularity
requirements for the IMT to hold, must strictly lie somewhere between mere differentiability
and being C1.
The continuity of the Fréchet derivative is a key assumption that can be removed when B is
finite dimensional; this is attributed to Černavski˘ı [3] and is known as the everywhere differen-
tiable IMT. Alternative proofs were presented in [19, 25]. All the proofs in the aforementioned
literature heavily rely on the local compactness of (a finite dimensional) B which characteristi-
cally fails in infinite dimensions. Yet as we will see, still local weak compactness can be utilized
to get a generalized IMT.
There is an extensive literature concerning generalizations of IMT “beyond the C1 realm in in-
finite dimensions” and/or “beyond the everywhere differentiable with non-degenrate derivative
in finite dimensions”. Three major approaches are to either
• assume the non-degeneracy of stronger notions of derivatives: examples in this direction
are abundant. We will mention a few important ones. Clarke’s IMT in finite dimensions
that applies to Lipschitz maps and only requires the non-degeneracy of Clarke’s gen-
eralized derivatives [5]. In [12], an IMT is proven that works for continuous self maps
of reflexive Banach spaces yet it requires the non-degeneracy of a set-valued derivative.
Using strong derivatives (or strict derivative), there is a host of IMTs for set-valued
maps expressed in terms of strong and metric regularity including Bartle-Graves and
Lyusternik-Graves theorems; see [9].
• to consider weak derivatives but imposing stronger non-degeneracy conditions: This
approach works best in finite dimensions and usually involves integrability assumptions
on the distortion of maps. For example local inversion of Sobolev maps falls in this
category. See e.g. [13, 17] and the references therein.
• to impose appropriate contraction assumptions that are weaker than being C1: gen-
eralizations that utilize this approach, typically require imposing some sort of a “con-
traction” assumption that will allow the use of the Banach fixed point theorem. For

1.2. Main Results.
Convention: f is said to be differentiable at a general point a (not necessarily an interior point)
of the domain, when f admits a local extension at a that is differentiable. We say f has an
invertible derivative at a, when at least one of the said local extensions around a does so.
Definition 1.6 (the fixed point property (FPP)). A Banach space B is said to have the fixed
point property if any continuous non-expansive self mappings f : C →C, for bounded closed
convex subsets C, has at least one fixed point.
Theorem 1 (Generalized IMT). Let f : Dom(f) ⊂B1 →B2 be an everywhere differentiable
map with invertible derivative. Then f is a local diffeomorphism provided any of the following
items hold
(1) if f satisfies strong A everywhere on all scales;
(2) If B1 is locally weakly compact and f is weak-weak continuous and satisfies A everywhere
on all scales;
(3) If B1 is locally weakly compact with FPP and f satisfies A everywhere on all scales;
(4) B1 is strictly convex with FPP and f satisfies weak A everywhere on all scales.
Remark 1.7. A large class of Banach spaces B1 that the last theorem applies to, are k-
uniformly rotund spaces for k ∈N. These spaces enjoy FPP, see § 2.3. These are also strictly
convex and reflexive therefore, they are locally weakly compact, see § 2.1.
The following two corollaries are generalizations of Hadamard type global inverse mapping
theorems, see [15] for a survey on global inverse mapping theorems.

Corollary 3.
(1) If f has almost non-degenerate directional derivatives then, it is a local homeomorphism.
(2) If f is segmentally non-degenerate, then it is a local almost diffeomorphism.

1.3. Road map and Organization. In § 2, we provide some necessary background material
needed for better readability of these notes.
In § 3, we provide the detailed proofs of the main and auxiliary results. In particular, the
road map of the generalized IMT is as follows:
(1) Variants of property A along with suitable hypotheses on the domain Banach space in
each case, is utilized to prove the existence of fixed points of the maps ˜f; this in turn
results in local surjectivity;
(2) Differentiability along with an invertible derivative is argued to provide discreteness of
the map f i.e. the fact it has discrete fibers;
(3) Invoking the convexity hypotheses, we show local injectivity, thus establishing f is a
local homeomorphism;
(4) Revisiting differentiability with an invertible derivative, we show local diffeomorphism.
In order to demonstrate the significance of these results, we have included an application to
abstract ODE theory, of the generalized theorems we have established in § 4 where we show
existence and uniqueness for a certain type of ODE system without continuity or Lipschitz
condition on the RHS of the equation.

2. Preliminaries
2.1. Weak topology and local weak compactness. The weak topology on a Banach space
is one that topologizes weak convergence of sequences. In this paper, a weakly continuous map
f : Dom(f) ⊂B1 →B2 between Banach spaces, refers to a map that is weak-weak continuous.
A well-known consequence of Banach-Alaoglu’s theorem (known as Kakukati’s theorem) is
that reflexive Banach spaces are locally weakly compact; see [6, Theorem V.4.2]
By the Schauder-Tychonoff fixed point theorem, when B is locally weakly compact, any
weakly continuous self map f : C →C admits a fixed point when C is a bounded closed convex
set.

2.3. Fixed points of (quasi)-nonexpansive maps.
2.3.1. Fixed points of non-expansive maps. A nonexpansive map is simply one with Lipschitz
constant 1 i.e. it does not increase distances.
According to the Browder-Göhde-Kirk fixed point theorem [11], a non-expanding self map
on a bounded closed convex set C in a uniformly convex B, admits a fixed point. It is also
well-known that if B is merely strictly convex, then f has a convex set of fixed points (possibly
empty).
A simple argument combined with the convexity results obtained in [2] for nonexpansive
maps shows that, when C is a bounded closed convex subset of B and B satisfies FPP, then
the set of fixed points of a non-expansive self map f on C is a closed convex subset of C, see
Proposition 3.2.
It was proven in [20] that k-uniformly rotund spaces satisfy the FPP.
2.3.2. Fixed points of quasi-nonexpansive maps. A (not necessarily continuous) f : C →C (C
closed and convex) is called quasi-nonexpansive if it has at least one fixed point and distances
to the fixed point are not increased under it i.e. if for all such fixed points p ∈C, it satisfies
∥f(x) −f(p)∥≤∥x −p∥,
∀x ∈C.
This was introduced in [7].
In [10], it has been shown that if B is strictly convex, then the set of fixed points of a
quasi-nonexpansive map is a nonempty closed convex set on which f is continuous.
