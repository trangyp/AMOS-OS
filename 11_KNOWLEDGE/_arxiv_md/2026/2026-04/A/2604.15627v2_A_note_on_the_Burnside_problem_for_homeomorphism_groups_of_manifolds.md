A NOTE ON THE BURNSIDE PROBLEM FOR
HOMEOMORPHISM GROUPS OF MANIFOLDS
DONGGYUN SEO
Abstract. This note studies the Burnside problem for homeomorphism groups of
compact connected manifolds. For surfaces, we prove that the identity component
of the homeomorphism group is torsion-free precisely when the surface is not the
sphere, torus, projective plane, or Klein bottle. An extension argument based on the
Tits alternative for mapping class groups then implies that every finitely generated
periodic subgroup of the full homeomorphism group is finite for all surfaces outside
this exceptional list, recovering and extending a theorem of Guelman and Liousse
to non-orientable surfaces. For the circle, we prove that every finitely generated
periodic subgroup of its homeomorphism group is finite and cyclic. We close with
remarks on manifolds with boundary and open questions on the Burnside problem
for hyperbolic three-manifolds and doubled handlebodies.
Burnside problem for surface homeomorphisms
Let Σ be a real, compact, connected surface (orientable or non-orientable, possibly
with boundary). The main object of study in this note is the group Homeo(Σ) =
Homeo(Σ, ∂Σ) of homeomorphisms of Σ fixing the boundary pointwise. If Σ is ori-
entable, let Homeo+(Σ) denote the subgroup of orientation-preserving elements; if Σ
is non-orientable, set Homeo+(Σ) = Homeo(Σ). Let Homeo0(Σ) denote the identity
component of Homeo+(Σ). We write S2, T 2, RP 2, and K for the sphere, torus, real
projective plane, and Klein bottle, respectively.
Proposition 1. For a compact connected surface Σ, the following are equivalent:
(1) Σ /∈{S2, T 2, RP 2, K},
(2) Homeo0(Σ) is torsion-free, and
(3) Homeo0(Σ) is contractible.
The equivalence of conditions (1) and (3) was established by Kneser [Kne26], Ham-
strom [Ham65b, Ham65a, Ham66], and Luke–Mason [LM72]. Although the equiv-
alence of (1) and (2) is likely well known, the author could not discover a proof
in the literature. This gap motivated the present note. The implication (2)⇒(1)
can be derived by exhibiting a nontrivial torsion element in Homeo0(Σ) for each
Σ ∈{S2, RP 2, T 2, K}.

of surface braid groups and mapping class groups. Most of the material between
Proposition 1 and its proof is adapted from the survey paper of Guaschi–Juan-Pineda
[GJP15].

On the other hand, Hamstrom [Ham66] proved that π1(Homeo+(Σ), id) is trivial
whenever Σ /∈{S2, T 2, RP 2, K}. This yields the short exact sequence
1 →Bn(Σ, P) →Mod(Σ, P) →Mod(Σ) →1.
For n = 1, this coincides with the classical Birman exact sequence.
We summarize these facts in the following lemma.
Lemma 2. For all n ≥1, the following hold:
(1) Bn(Σ) is torsion-free if and only if Σ /∈{S2, RP 2}.
(2) If Σ /∈{S2, T 2, RP 2, K}, then the following exact sequence holds:
1 →Bn(Σ, P) →Mod(Σ, P) →Mod(Σ) →1.
Proof of Proposition 1. Suppose there exists a non-identity torsion element f ∈Homeo0(Σ).
Choose a point x ∈Σ in the support of f, and let P be the ⟨f⟩-orbit of x. Then P
contains finitely many points, with at least two elements. Since f cyclically permutes
the points of P, it represents a nontrivial element of Mod(Σ, P).
However, f represents the identity element in Mod(Σ). By Lemma 2(2), f therefore
corresponds to a finite-order element in Bn(Σ, P).
This contradicts Lemma 2(1),
which asserts that Bn(Σ, P) is torsion-free. Hence, no such f exists, and Homeo0(Σ)
is torsion-free.
□
An element of a group is called periodic if it has finite order, and a group is called
periodic if all of its elements are periodic. Burnside asked whether every finitely gen-
erated periodic group is finite. Many counterexamples have since been constructed
by Golod–Shafarevich [GŠ64], Adian–Novikov [NA68a, NA68b, NA68c], and others.
Nevertheless, one can often reduce Burnside-type questions to subgroups of specific
groups. For instance, Hurtado–Kocsard–Rodriguez-Hertz [HKRH20] proved that ev-
ery finitely generated periodic subgroup of smooth area-preserving homeomorphisms
of S2 is finite, and Guelman–Liousse [GL14] showed Burnside property holds for
measure-preserving homeomorphisms on T 2.
By Tits alternative for mapping class groups of orientable surfaces [McC85], every
periodic subgroup is virtually solvable. In the non-orientable case (excluding RP 2
and K), the orientable double cover induces an embedding of the mapping class group
into that of an orientable surface [BC72, Sze10, KK24], implying that Tits alternative
still holds. Since the mapping class groups of RP 2 and K are finite, it follows that
all mapping class groups of surfaces satisfy Tits alternative.
Lemma 3. Let G be a group satisfying the Tits alternative, i.e., every finitely gen-
erated subgroup of G is either virtually solvable or contains a free subgroup of rank 2.
Then every finitely generated periodic subgroup of G is finite.
Proof. Let K ≤G be a finitely generated periodic subgroup. By the Tits alternative,
K is either virtually solvable or contains a free subgroup F ∼= F2. The latter is im-
possible, since F2 contains elements of infinite order, contradicting K being periodic.
Hence K is virtually solvable, so there exists a solvable subgroup S ≤K of finite
index.
It remains to show that S is finite. Since [K : S] < ∞and K is finitely generated,
the Schreier transversal lemma implies that S is finitely generated. As a subgroup of
K, the group S is also periodic. We show by induction on the derived length d of S
that every finitely generated periodic solvable group is finite.

By the

Consequently, every finitely generated periodic subgroup of Mod(Σ) is finite. The
same conclusion holds for the extended mapping class group Mod±(Σ). Indeed, let
H ≤Mod±(Σ) be a finitely generated periodic subgroup. Since Mod(Σ) has index 2
in Mod±(Σ), the intersection H0 := H ∩Mod(Σ) has index at most 2 in H, hence is
finite index. Since H is finitely generated and [H : H0] < ∞, the Schreier transversal
lemma implies that H0 is finitely generated. As a subgroup of H, the group H0 is
also periodic. Since H0 ≤Mod(Σ), Lemma 3 implies that H0 is finite. Therefore
|H| = [H : H0] · |H0| < ∞,
so H is finite.

(that is, every finitely generated periodic subgroup is finite), then so does B.
Proof. Let H ≤B be a finitely generated periodic subgroup. Since π(H) is a finitely
generated periodic subgroup of C and C satisfies the Burnside property, π(H) is
finite. Set K := H ∩ker π = H ∩A, so that K is a normal subgroup of H with
H/K ∼= π(H). In particular, [H : K] = |π(H)| < ∞.
Since K is a subgroup of finite index in the finitely generated group H, it is itself
finitely generated by the Schreier transversal lemma. Moreover, every element of
K ≤ker π ∼= A has finite order since H is periodic. Thus K is a finitely generated
periodic subgroup of A, and since A satisfies the Burnside property, K is finite.
Since H is an extension of the finite group π(H) ∼= H/K by the finite group K,
we conclude that H is finite.
□
As a corollary of Lemma 4, the Burnside property is preserved under group exten-
sions with torsion-free kernel. Since Homeo0(Σ) is torsion-free for Σ /∈{S2, T 2, RP 2, K}
and Mod±(Σ) satisfies the Burnside property, the short exact sequence
1 →Homeo0(Σ) →Homeo(Σ) →Mod±(Σ) →1
immediately implies that Homeo(Σ) satisfies the Burnside property as well, yielding
the following theorem.
Theorem 5. If Σ /∈{S2, T 2, RP 2, K}, then every finitely generated periodic subgroup
of Homeo(Σ) is finite.
Note that Guelman–Liousse [GL17] established the Burnside property for groups
of homeomorphisms of compact orientable surfaces. More precisely, they proved that
every finitely generated periodic subgroup of the homeomorphism group of an ori-
entable compact surface, except for the sphere and the torus, is finite. Their approach

relies on a combination of dynamical and geometric arguments, including the analysis
of group actions with finite orbits and rigidity phenomena for periodic homeomor-
phisms. Our result recovers this conclusion through a different argument, based on
the torsion-freeness of the identity component and a general extension principle.
The four exceptional surfaces (the sphere, the torus, the projective plane, and the
Klein bottle) lie outside the scope of Theorem 5, and the Burnside problem for their
full homeomorphism groups remains incompletely resolved. For the sphere, Hurtado–
Kocsard–Rodríguez-Hertz [HKRH20] proved the Burnside property for the group of
area-preserving analytic diffeomorphisms of S2, but the purely topological case of
Homeo(S2) is open. Partial progress has been made: Conejeros [Con18] proved that
every finitely generated group of homeomorphisms of S2 whose elements all have finite
order that is a power of 2, with a uniform bound on the orders, is finite, and obtained a
parallel result for area-preserving homeomorphisms. For the torus, Guelman–Liousse
[GL14] proved that every finitely generated periodic group of homeomorphisms of T 2
preserving a probability measure is finite, but the measure-free case is open. For the
real projective plane and the Klein bottle, no analogue of these results appears in
the literature, and the Burnside problem for Homeo(RP 2) and Homeo(K) appears
to be entirely open. The difficulty in all four cases is precisely that Homeo0(Σ) is
not torsion-free, so the extension argument of Lemma 4 does not apply, and new
dynamical or algebraic methods seem to be required.
Conjecture 6. For each Σ ∈{S2, T 2, RP 2, K}, every finitely generated periodic
subgroup of Homeo(Σ) is finite.
Burnside problem for circle homeomorphisms
Let us focus on the circle S1 and the compact interval I = [0, 1].
The group
Homeo(I) = Homeo+(I) is torsion-free (see [CR16]), so every finitely generated peri-
odic subgroup is trivial. The group Homeo+(S1), by contrast, contains torsion: any
rotation of finite order provides an example. Nevertheless, finitely generated periodic
subgroups remain highly constrained.
Theorem 7. Every finitely generated periodic subgroup of Homeo(S1) is finite and
cyclic.
Proof. By Lemma 4, we need need only to check that every finitely generated periodic
subgroup of Homeo+(S1) is finite. Let G = ⟨f1, . . . , fk⟩≤Homeo+(S1) be finitely
generated and periodic, with f ni
i
= id for each i.
We first show that every non-identity element of G has no fixed point. Let g ∈G
with g ̸= id. Since G is periodic, gm = id for some m ≥1. Suppose for contradiction
that g(x0) = x0 for some x0 ∈S1. The restriction of g to the open arc S1 \ {x0} ∼= R
is then an orientation-preserving homeomorphism of R. Such a homeomorphism is
either the identity or fixed-point-free; in the latter case it satisfies h(x) > x for all
x ∈R or h(x) < x for all x ∈R (since h −id is continuous and nowhere zero, hence
of constant sign). In either sub-case, every orbit under h is strictly monotone, so
no orbit is finite, contradicting gm = id. Hence g must restrict to the identity on
S1 \ {x0}, giving g = id, a contradiction. Therefore g has no fixed point on S1.
Since every non-identity element of G has no fixed point, G acts freely on S1. By
[Nav11, Theorem 2.2.32], G is isomorphic to a subgroup of SO(2). Because SO(2) is
abelian and G is periodic, G is a finite abelian group.

It remains to show G is cyclic. By [BS18, Lemma 3.1], every finite subgroup of
Homeo+(S1) is cyclic. The proof is as follows. Since every non-identity element of
the finite group G has no fixed point, G acts freely on S1. Fix any x0 ∈S1; since the
stabilizer of x0 in G is trivial, the orbit O = G·x0 has exactly |G| points. Label them
x0, x1, . . . , x|G|−1 in their cyclic order inherited from S1. Every element of G sends O to
itself by an orientation-preserving bijection, hence acts on O as a cyclic permutation
xi 7→xi+φ(g) (mod |G|) for a unique φ(g) ∈Z/|G|Z. The map φ: G →Z/|G|Z is a
group homomorphism. It is injective: if φ(g) = 0 then g fixes every point of O, in
particular g(x0) = x0, so g = id by the free action. Since |G| = |Z/|G|Z|, φ is an
isomorphism, giving G ∼= Z/|G|Z.
□
Remarks and questions
By a theorem of Newman [New31] (see also Smith [Smi41], Dress [Dre69], and
Bredon [Bre72]), any periodic homeomorphism of a (finite-dimensional) compact
connected manifold that fixes an open set pointwise must be the identity.
Note
Homeo(M) is the group of pointwise boundary-fixing homeomorphisms of M. By
Newman’s theorem, we have the following.

So the Burnside problem for Homeo(M) is interesting when M is closed (i.e. with-
out boundary). The most general conjecture along the way is the following.
Conjecture 9. The homeomorphism group of a finite-dimensional closed connected
manifold satisfies the Burnside property, i.e., every finitely generated periodic sub-
group is finite.
Before closing this note, we raise two questions. The first concerns the Burnside
problem for closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds. If M is a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold,
then Mostow rigidity [Mos68] implies that Mod(M) is finite. By Lemma 4, it therefore
suffices to ask whether Homeo0(M) satisfies the Burnside property, as a positive
answer would imply that the full group Homeo(M) does as well.
The second concerns doubled handlebodies. A doubled handlebody is the closed 3-
manifold obtained by doubling a handlebody along its boundary. It is well known, by
work of Whitehead [Whi36], Laudenbach [Lau74], and Brendle–Broaddus–Putman
[BBP23], that its mapping class group is virtually isomorphic to Out(Fn); conse-
quently, the Tits alternative holds for this group by a theorem of Bestvina–Feighn–
Handel [BFH00, BFH05]. This motivates the following question: does the identity
component Homeo0(M) of the homeomorphism group of a doubled handlebody sat-
isfy the Burnside property?
