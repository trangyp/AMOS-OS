A note on computable ´etale spaces
Matthew de Brecht⋆
Graduate School of Human and Environmental Studies, Kyoto University, Japan
matthew@i.h.kyoto-u.ac.jp
Abstract. An ´etale space over a topological space Y is defined as a
local homeomorphism from a topological space X into Y . They often
come up in topos theory because of the equivalence between sheaves and
´etale spaces over a space. In this note, we define computable ´etale spaces
over a computable topological space Y within the TTE framework of
computable topology, and show they are naturally equivalent to com-
putable functions from Y to ODS, the effective quasi-Polish category of
overt-discrete quasi-Polish spaces. More generally, if C is a computable
category (or groupoid), then there is an equivalence between computable
functors from C to ODS, and computable ´etale spaces equipped with a
computable action by C.
1
Introduction
We recommend [26] for an intuitive explanation of the connections between
Grothendiek toposes and geometric logic. Briefly, frames (complete lattices sat-
isfying a certain infinitary distributive law) correspond to the Lindenbaum-
algebras of propositional geometric theories, and Grothendiek toposes in a sense
correspond to the “Lindenbaum algebras” of predicate geometric theories. A fa-
mous result by Joyal and Tierney [15] shows that every Grothendiek topos is
equivalent to the category of continuous actions of a localic groupoid.
An analogous connection between ω1-pretoposes and ω1-coherent theories
was established by R. Chen in [1, 2]. An ω1-pretopos is a generalization of a
Grothendiek topos where only countable coproducts are required to exist, rather
than all small coproducts. Similarly, ω1-coherent logic is the restriction of geo-
metric logic that requires infinitary disjunctions to be countable. R. Chen showed
that the syntactic ω1-pretopos (the “Lindenbaum algebra”) of a countable ω1-
coherent theory is equivalent to the category Act(C) of (countably based) con-
tinuous actions of a quasi-Polish groupoid C. This can be viewed as a general-
ization (from propositional logic to predicate logic) of R. Heckmann’s spatiality
result [12], which implies the Lindenbaum algebra of a countable ω1-coherent
propositional theory is isomorphic to the frame of opens of a quasi-Polish space.

This note is a contribution towards effectivizing R. Chen’s result and other
aspects of Grothendiek topos theory. We define computable ´etale spaces over a
computable topological space Y within the TTE framework of computable topol-
ogy [27], and show they are equivalent to computable functions from Y to ODS,
the effective quasi-Polish category of overt discrete quasi-Polish spaces. More
generally, we show that for any computable category C there is a computabil-
ity preserving equivalence between the category Act(C) of (countably based)
´etale spaces with continuous actions by C, and the functor category ODSC of
(topologically) continuous functors from C to ODS. It follows that the syntactic
ω1-pretopos of a countable ω1-coherent theory is equivalent to a functor cate-
gory ODSC for some quasi-Polish category C. These syntactic ω1-pretoposes are
analogous to classifying (Grothendiek) toposes of geometric theories, and the
categorically equivalent functor categories ODSC are suitable for computability
theory because they are internal categories of QCB0.
See [11, 19, 5, 10] for related work applying computability theory to category
theoretical structures. See also [16] for connections between Lawvere-Tierney
topologies and computability theory. We mainly work with computable topolog-
ical spaces1, but will need QCB0-spaces [24] for functor categories. One could
also generalize further to represented spaces [22], which appears necessary to in-
ternalize some categories of interest [5]. We will also emphasize the constructions
for quasi-Polish spaces, because they are exactly what is needed to establish the
connections with ω1-coherent logic. By encoding quasi-Polish spaces as spaces
of ideals and using the techniques of [20], most of our results can be formalized
within the subsytem ACA0 of second order arithmetic (or even weaker systems).

Preliminaries
2.1
Computable topological spaces
Let ≺be a transitive relation on N. A subset I ⊆N is an ideal if and only if:
1. I ̸= ∅,
(I is non-empty)
2. (∀a ∈I)(∀b ∈N) (b ≺a ⇒b ∈I),
(I is a lower set)
3. (∀a, b ∈I)(∃c ∈I) (a ≺c & b ≺c).
(I is directed)
The collection I(≺) of all ideals has the topology generated by basic open sets
of the form [a]≺= {I ∈I(≺) | a ∈I}.
A topological space is quasi-Polish if it is homeomorphic to a space of the
form I(≺) for some transitive relation ≺on N. A represented space is effectively
quasi-Polish if it is computably isomorphic to a space of the form I(≺) for some
computably enumerable (c.e.) transitive relation ≺on N. An effectively quasi-
Polish space is computably overt if furthermore the corresponding relation ≺
is such that E≺= {a ∈N | [a]⊀= ∅} is c.e. A computably overt effective
quasi-Polish space is called a computable quasi-Polish space.

2.2
Computable categories
A computable category is an internal category [14] in the category of computable
topological spaces and computable functions. More concretely, a computable
category is a tuple C = (CObj, CMor, σ, τ, ι, ◦) consisting of the following data:
– CObj (objects) and CMor (morphisms) are computable topological spaces.
– σ: CMor →CObj (source), τ : CMor →CObj (target), and ι: CObj →CMor (iden-
tity) are computable functions.
– ◦:⊆CMor ×CMor →CMor (composition) is a partial computable function with
domain
dom(◦) = {⟨g, f⟩∈CMor × CMor | σ(g) = τ(f)}
subject to the following:
– σ(g ◦f) = σ(f) and τ(g ◦f) = τ(g),
– σ(ι(c)) = c and τ(ι(c)) = c,
– (h ◦g) ◦f = h ◦(g ◦f) when the compositions h ◦g and g ◦f are defined,
– if σ(f) = c and τ(f) = d then f ◦ι(c) = f = ι(d) ◦f.
We write f : c →d to denote that f is a morphism with σ(f) = c and τ(f) = d.
We call C an effective quasi-Polish category if CObj and CMor are effective
quasi-Polish spaces. This implies C is an internal category in the category of

2.3
Computable functors
Let C = (CObj, CMor, σC, τC, ιC, ◦C) and D = (DObj, DMor, σD, τD, ιD, ◦D) be com-
putable categories. A computable functor from C to D is a pair F = (FObj, FMor)
of computable functions FObj : CObj →DObj and FMor : CMor →DMor satisfying
1. FObj ◦σC = σD ◦FMor,
2. FObj ◦τC = τD ◦FMor,
3. FMor ◦ιC = ιD ◦FObj, and
4. FMor(g ◦C f) = FMor(g) ◦D FMor(f) for all composable f, g ∈CMor.
A computable functor F from C to D will be denoted F : C →D. It is clear that
the identity functor 1C : C →C (which is the identity on objects and morphisms)
is computable, and that computable functors are closed under composition.
Given computable functors F, G: C →D, a computable natural transforma-
tion from F to G is given by a computable function η: CObj →DMor satisfying:
– σD ◦η = FObj and τD ◦η = GObj
(i.e., η(c): FObj(c) →GObj(c) for each c ∈CObj),
– η(τC(f)) ◦D FMor(f) = GMor(f) ◦D η(σC(f)) for each f ∈CMor.
Usually we will write η: F →G if it will not cause any confusion.
A continuous functor 3 is a functor that is computable with respect to an
oracle. Similarly, a continuous natural transformation is a natural transformation
that is computable with respect to an oracle. We will need these relativized
versions when defining functor categories in the next section.

(DC)Obj and (DC)Mor can be constructed as equalizers of continuous functions
between quasi-Polish spaces, and σDC, τDC, and ιDC are continuous.
2.5
Computable ´etale spaces
An ´etale space over a topological space Y is a local homeomorphism p: X →Y ,
which means that each x ∈X has an open neighborhood U such that the
restriction p|U is a homeomorphism with an open subset of Y . A morphism
between ´etale spaces p: X →Y and q: Z →Y is a continuous function f : X →
Z such that q ◦f = p. It is well-known that the category of ´etale spaces over Y
is equivalent to the category of sheaves of sets over Y (see [18]).
We only consider countably based ´etale spaces in this note. Having a count-
able basis forces the fibers to be countable, hence these can be viewed as sheaves
of countable sets over a space. Note that a countably based ´etale space over a
quasi-Polish space is automatically quasi-Polish (Lemma 2.2 of [1]).
Let X and Y be computable topological spaces. A computable ´etale space
consists of a computable function p: X →Y along with the following:
1. a computable enumeration of c.e. open subsets (Un)n∈N that cover X,
2. a computable enumeration of c.e. open subsets (Vn)n∈N of Y ,
3. a computable enumeration of computable functions (sections) sn : Vn →Un
(n ∈N) satisfying
– sn(p(x)) = x for each x ∈Un, and
– p(sn(y)) = y for each y ∈Vn.
(i.e., each sn is a computable inverse of the restriction p|Un : Un →Vn).
In this case, we call p: X →Y a computable local homeomorphism. We will
sometimes call X a computable ´etale space if omitting the computable local
homeomorphism p and the computable sections does not cause confusion.
We have the following easy fact that we write here as a lemma for convenience.
Lemma 1. If p: X →Y is a local homeomorphism with sections sn : Vn →Un
(n ∈N) and p(x) = y, then x = sn(y) if and only if x ∈Un.

2.6
Computable actions
The definitions in this section are a minor generalization of the continuous
groupoid actions in [1] to continuous actions by topological categories.
Let C be a computable category, and p: X →CObj be a computable function
from a computable topological space X to the space of objects of C. A computable
action by C on X is a partial computable function α :⊆CMor × X →X with
domain
dom(α) = {⟨f, x⟩∈CMor × X | σ(f) = p(x)}
that satisfies the following conditions (where we abbreviate α(f, x) by f · x):
1. τ(f) = p(f · x)
2. ι(p(x)) · x = x
3. (g ◦f) · x = g · (f · x).
Intuitively, α interprets (in a computable way) each morphism f : c →d in
C as a function α(f, −): p−1(c) →p−1(d) between the fibers of c and d, while
preserving identity morphisms and composition.
A computable equivariant function from p: X →CObj to q: Y →CObj (both
equipped with computable actions by C) is a computable function f : X →Y
such that p = q ◦f and f(g · x) = g · f(x).
If C is a computable category, then a computable ´etale space over CObj
equipped with a computable action by C is called a computable C-set.
These definitions relativize to countably based ´etale spaces, continuous ac-
tions, and continuous equivariant functions. We write Act(C) for the category
of countably based C-sets and continuous equivariant functions.

Main result
Our main result is the following:
Theorem 3. Let C be a computable category. The category Act(C) of count-
ably based C-sets and continuous equivariant functions and the category ODSC
of (topologically) continuous functors from C to ODS are equivalent. The equiv-
alence preserves the computability of both objects and morphisms.
⊓⊔
As a special case, if X is a computable topological space then there is an
equivalence between computable ´etale spaces over X and computable functions
from X to ODSObj. Thus, the functor category ODSCX is equivalent to the cate-
gory of countably based ´etale spaces over X.
We prove our main result in the next four sections by constructing functors
F : ODSC →Act(C) and G : Act(C) →ODSC that preserve computable objects
and morphisms, and then show that that G◦F and F◦G are naturally isomorphic
to the identity functors.
