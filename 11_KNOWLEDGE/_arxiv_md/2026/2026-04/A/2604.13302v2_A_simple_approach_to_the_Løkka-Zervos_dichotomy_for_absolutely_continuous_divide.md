A SIMPLE APPROACH TO THE LØKKA-ZERVOS DICHOTOMY FOR
ABSOLUTELY CONTINUOUS DIVIDEND STRATEGIES
TOMMY MASTROMONACO, NACER FENDRI, JEAN-FRANÇOIS RENAUD,
AND CLARENCE SIMARD
Abstract. We revisit the optimization problem solved in [10], i.e., the maximization of
dividends, in a Brownian risk model, with the possibility (not the obligation) of making
capital injections.
Following the approach introduced in [1, 15, 14], we consider instead
absolutely continuous (AC) dividend strategies with an affine bound on the payment rates,
while singular capital injections are still allowed. In addition, we incorporate a parameter
for the cost of ruin or, said differently, a penalty at ruin in the performance function. We
show that the solution is a so-called Løkka-Zervos dichotomy: the surplus is never ruined by
making bail-out payments, or no capital is injected and bankruptcy can occur; in either case,
dividends are paid at full rate when the surplus is above a threshold. Our framework allows
us to provide explicit conditions to express the dichotomy, either using the cost of capital
injections or the cost of ruin as a criterion, which also exposes the underlying structure of
the solution. In particular, for some values of the parameters, we show that it is optimal
to liquidate.
Moreover, we perform a numerical analysis highlighting the range of values
generated under this AC affine-bound structure.
1. Introduction
We revisit the optimization problem solved in [10], i.e., the maximization of dividends, in
a Brownian risk model, with the possibility (not the obligation) of making capital injections.
As the main focus is on the maximization of dividends, we follow the approach introduced in
[1, 15] and we consider absolutely continuous dividend strategies with an affine bound on the
payment rates, while singular capital injections are allowed. To further highlight the trade-
off between the maximization of dividend payments and the minimization of both the cost
of declaring ruin and/or making capital injections, we add a penalty-at-ruin component to
our performance function, in the spirit of [18]. It is well known that the solution to the joint
optimization of capital injections and dividend payments is somehow dictated by the solutions
of two auxiliary/sub-problems: the pure maximization of dividends, without the possibility of
injection capital, and the maximization of dividends with mandatory injections to avoid ruin.
The first optimization problem often bears the name of Bruno de Finetti [5], but there is
wealth of publications on the subject; see, e.g., [7, 2, 16, 4]. The second optimization problem
has been considered for example by [18] and [17] who have introduced forced capital injections
in the optimal dividends problem to avoid ruin.

However, our paper is more in the footsteps of Løkka & Zervos [10] who considered the
maximization of dividends (in a Brownian model) with the possibility, but not the obligation,
of injecting capital. Their solution takes the form of a dichotomy: either capital is always
injected, whenever the firm faces bankruptcy, or no capital is ever injected. Note that it does
not seem to be optimal to inject earlier than when facing bankruptcy nor is it optimal to inject
for some time and then give up and let ruin occur.
A recent trend in dividend optimization considers absolutely continuous dividend payments
with payment rates bounded by a function of the surplus. This has been partly motivated
by the desire of obtaining optimal strategies with the property of yielding a stable flow of
dividends; this motivation comes mainly from [3]. In [15], the bound is a linear function of
the surplus while in [9] a more general non-decreasing concave function is considered. In this
framework, along with the possibility of injecting capitals, maximizing dividends was solved
by [14] where they showed that the optimal solution is a dichotomy analogous to the one
in [10]. They obtained their solution by combining a probabilistic approach with viscosity
solutions theory.
In this paper, we adopt the mathematical framework established in [14], we fix the bound on
dividend rates to be an affine function of the surplus and incorporate a penalty for bankruptcy.
This specific bound provides the flexibility to derive semi-explicit expressions for the value
function and a allows for a simpler formulation of the dichotomy.
For instance, we will
find that, for some set of parameters, capital injections are sub-optimal regardless of the
cost of capital injections. Moreover, we use our semi-explicit expressions to gain a better
understanding of the dichotomous nature of the solution by performing numerical experiments.
Finally, adding a penalty at ruin in the optimization problem creates an intriguing duality
between the injection and penalty costs, as the two corresponding solutions of the dichotomy
have a unified representation that mirrors both cost parameters, indicating that they may in
fact represent the two sides of the same coin. This relationship yields interesting financial
interpretations and promising considerations for future problems.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows: in Section 2, we formulate the main stochastic
optimization problem studied in this article.
Its dichotomous solution emerges from two
related optimization sub-problems, for which we briefly present the solutions (see Section 3).
Then, in Section 4, we use the solutions of those sub-problems to construct a solution to the
main optimization problem. Finally, we perform the aforementioned numerical analyses in
Section 5.

• if the cost of injections is small, then an optimal dividend-injection strategy is to pay
dividends at the maximal rate when the surplus exceeds another threshold and to make
bail-out injections so that ruin never occurs.
Otherwise, the optimal dividend-injection strategy is to liquidate, i.e., to pay dividends con-
stantly at the maximum rate until the process is ruined (it reaches zero) and never inject any
capital.

Conclusion
Following the steps of [10] and [14], we have considered the problem faced by an insurance
company (or the managers of a cash fund) aiming at jointly optimizing capital injections and
dividend payments. While allowing for general capital injections, we have restricted dividend
payments to be absolutely continuous with payment rates bounded by an affine function of the
controlled process. This restriction is motivated by the desire of obtaining optimal strategies
with the property of yielding a stable flow of dividends. On the other hand, we have added
a cost for ruin in the performance function, in order to have a handle on the role played by
bankruptcy in the optimal trade-off between avoiding ruin and paying as much dividends as
possible.
Using elementary techniques, we have shown that the so-called Løkka–Zervos dichotomy is
valid: if the cost of capital injections is low (or if the penalty at ruin is high), it is optimal
to pay dividends at the maximum rate and inject capital as a last resort so that ruin never
occurs; conversely, if the cost of capital injections is high (or if the penalty at ruin is low), it
is optimal to pay dividends at the maximum rate and never inject capital so that ruin occurs
at the first passage below zero. Recall that, in [10] and [14], as there is no penalty at ruin, the
dichotomy is stated only in terms of the cost of capital injections. With this extra component,
we were able to provide an additional perspective on this optimal behaviour.
Finally, our numerical analyses are showing that an affine bound on the dividend rate is
sufficient to fill in the gap between absolutely continuous dividend payments and singular
dividend payments.
