ANY COUNTABLE TOPOLOGICAL Fp-VECTOR SPACE
HAS A CLOSED DISCRETE BASIS⋆
OL’GA SIPACHEVA
Abstract. It is proved that any countable topological vector space over
a finite field Fp or, equivalently, any countable Abelian topological group
of prime exponent has a closed discrete basis.
Let L be a Hausdorff topological vector space over the field Fp, where p is
an arbitrary prime, and let E be any basis of L. Then, algebraically, L is
the direct sum L
e∈E⟨e⟩, where ⟨e⟩denotes the linear span of e in L (which is
isomorphic to Fp), and any element v of L can be uniquely (up to the order
of terms) represented as a linear combination of elements of E with nonzero
coefficients in Fp. We refer to this linear combination as a decomposition of
v in E and to the number of terms in it as the length of v in E. Note that
any Abelian topological group of exponent p can be treated as a topological
Fp-vector space, because any such group is a direct sum of copies of the cyclic
group Z/pZ of order p (see, e.g., [4, Theorem 8.5]); clearly, the continuity of
the group operation implies that of the linear operations. For such a group,
linear combinations of elements and bases are defined in the same way as for
vector spaces.
The topology of L may induce an arbitrary Tychonoff topology on E. In-
deed, L can be treated as the free group FV (E) on E in the variety V of Abelian
groups of exponent p. It is well known that, given any Tychonoff topology τ
on E, there exists a group topology on FV (E) that induces the topology τ on
E (see, e.g., [6]). As mentioned, any group topology on FV (E) = L is a vector
space topology.
However, if L is countable, then, whatever the induced topology of E (and
whatever the Hausdorff vector space topology of L), there always exists a basis
E′′ in L which is closed and discrete in L. This is the main result of this paper.

tor space over the field Fp, where p is a prime; it is assumed to be fixed
throughout. In what follows, we treat L as an Abelian topological group of
exponent p.

A norm on a group G with neutral element e is a function ∥·∥: G →R with
the following properties:
(1) ∥g∥= 0 if and only if g = e;
(2) ∥g−1∥= ∥g∥;
(3) ∥gh∥≤∥g∥+ ∥h∥(the triangle inequality for norms).
We say that a topology of a topological group G is generated by a norm ∥·∥
if the sets {x ∈G : ∥x∥< ε}, ε > 0, form a neighborhood base at e for the
topology of G.
Note that when G is a vector space over a field F (e.g., G = L), this
definition of a norm differs from the conventional definition of a norm on a
vector space (the condition ∥λv∥= |λ| · ∥v∥is missing). In what follows, we
always understand norms in the sense defined above.
Any norm ∥·∥on any group G generates the metric d on G defined by
d(g, h) = ∥g−1h∥. This metric is left-invariant, i.e., d(xg, xh) = d(x, h) for any
x, g, h ∈G, and satisfies the condition d(g−1, h−1) = d(g, h) for all g, h ∈G.
We denote the completion of the metric space (G, d) by bG and call it the
completion of G with respect to the norm ∥·∥.
It is well known that the
multiplication and inversion group operations extend continuously to bG, so
that bG is a topological group containing G as a subgroup (see, e.g., [7, pp. 352–
353]).
Theorem. Any countable Hausdorff topological vector space over a finite field
or, equivalently, any countable Abelian topological group of prime exponent has
a closed discrete basis.
Proof. Let L be a countable topological Fp-vector space, and let E be any basis
of L. We will treat L as an Abelian topological group of exponent p and denote
its zero element by 0. The network weight of any countable topological space is
countable, any Hausdorff topological group of countable network weight admits
a coarser second-countable Hausdorff group topology [1, Theorem 2], and any
second-countable Hausdorff group topology is generated by a norm (see, e.g.,
[5]). Thus, there exists a continuous norm ∥·∥on L. In what follows, we
assume that the topology of L is generated by this norm and construct a basis
closed and discrete in the norm topology; clearly, it retains these properties in
the original finer topology of L.
