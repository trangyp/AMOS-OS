A Novel Reinforcement Learning Based Framework
for Scalable MIMO Interference Alignment
Samitha Gunarathne, Graduate Student Member, IEEE, Eslam Eldeeb, Member, IEEE, Nurul Huda Mahmood,
Member, IEEE and Italo Atzeni, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—Interference alignment (IA) is a widely recognized
approach for mitigating inter-cell interference in multi-user
multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) networks. Despite its
effectiveness, practical deployment remains constrained by two
major challenges, i.e., the need for global channel state informa-
tion (CSI) at each transmitter and the complexity of deriving
closed-form solutions for intricate MIMO systems. This work
aims to maximize network throughput by effectively mitigat-
ing interference using an IA-inspired learning algorithm that
addresses its aforementioned challenges. First, we propose a
predictive, transformer-based IA framework that estimates CSI
to reduce signaling overhead in small-scale MIMO systems. Next,
we formulate the IA problem as a multi-objective optimization
problem based on subspace coordination and develop two rein-
forcement learning-based algorithms to enhance the scalability of
IA in large-scale MIMO systems. Simulation results demonstrate
that the proposed methods signiﬁcantly outperform conventional
baselines with up to 30% average user throughput gains over the
best performing baseline.
Index Terms—Deep reinforcement learning, interference align-
ment, interference management, MIMO communication, trans-
formers.
I. INTRODUCTION
With the explosive growth of users, data, and applications,
sixth-generation (6G) wireless systems aim to transform com-
munications through artiﬁcial intelligence (AI)-driven automa-
tion capable of managing increasingly complex data trafﬁc.
The draft framework for 6G system requirements released
by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) outlines
six usage scenarios supported by stringent performance tar-
gets, such as extremely high data rates, dense deployment
scenarios, ultra high reliability and low latency [1]–[3]. Re-
cent research on 6G outlines different technology enablers
to support these stringent requirements [4]–[6]. Among these,
massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) technology is
a key enabler for achieving the required spectral efﬁciency
and data rates in 6G wireless networks. The large number
of antennas at the base stations in massive MIMO provides
seamless connectivity to the end users and highly directional
beamforming capabilities [7]. Incorporating massive MIMO
into ultra-dense networks enhances connectivity for highly

can increase system capacity nearly tenfold and the energy

However, the ultra-dense deployment of base stations and
user devices makes interference a major challenge in MIMO
systems. Therefore, interference management has become an
active research area in the ﬁeld of wireless communications.
Several approaches have been proposed to mitigate inter-
cell interference in wireless networks, including interference
alignment (IA), interference cancellation, interference coordi-
nation, beamforming and coordinated multipoint transmission.
This work particularly focuses on IA, which is a degrees-
of-freedom (DoF)-optimal interference management technique
that approaches the capacity of an interference network in the
regime of high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) [11]. IA schemes
ensure that all interference signals at unintended receivers are
aligned within the same subspace, thereby minimizing the
dimension of the interference subspace. The desired signal
is then recovered using a carefully designed post-processing
matrix that suppresses this interference.
Despite its theoretical effectiveness, the IA concept has sev-
eral practical limitations. First, it requires global channel state
information (CSI) at each node, which is used to compute the
appropriate precoding matrices. Second, it does not scale well
as the number of users in the network increases. To address the
global CSI requirement, several studies have proposed iterative
algorithms that exploit channel reciprocity to achieve IA using
only local CSI at each node, thereby reducing the dependence
on global CSI [12]. In addition, centralized feedback topolo-
gies have been introduced for MIMO interference channels
under IA constraints to reduce CSI overhead by supporting
sequential CSI exchange [13].
In this study, we consider both small-scale and large-scale
MIMO systems to evaluate the scalability of IA approaches.
As the number of users increases, IA becomes difﬁcult to
scale due to its rapidly growing complexity. This occurs
because each additional user increases the dimensionality of
the IA, thereby enlarging the precoding matrices and the
number of alignment constraints that must be satisﬁed. For
instance, if the users are more than three, it is nontrivial to
derive a closed-form solution using IA [14]–[16]. Therefore,
calculating optimal precoders and post-processing matrices
becomes a non-convex and NP-hard problem for complex
settings [14], [15]. This motivates the development of scalable
interference management schemes that efﬁciently project both
desired and interference signals onto their respective subspaces

To address the requirement for global CSI, we propose a
method that predicts the global CSI from the previous channel
data and proactively applies IA. This approach reduces the
CSI overhead, making IA a practically feasible interference
management solution for small-scale MIMO systems. The
CSI prediction challenge is formulated as a time-series fore-
casting problem, where we propose to adopt the transformer
architectures due to its superior performance over other time-
series models, such as recurrent neural network (RNN) and
long short-term memory (LSTM). Transformers effectively
resist the well-known vanishing gradient problem commonly
associated with RNNs [17]. Additionally, unlike LSTMs,
transformers can process data in parallel, making them more
efﬁcient in training large datasets. Given their capacity to
capture global contextual information, transformer models are
especially well-suited for CSI prediction.
The scalability challenge is addressed by adopting a deep
RL (DRL) framework for optimizing the IA precoding and
post-processing matrices. Reinforcement learning (RL) offers
a suitable framework, enabling agents to sense the environment
and make optimal decisions through interaction. As a model-
free method, decision making in RL occurs without prior
knowledge of the environment. DRL extends this capability
by integrating deep neural networks (DNNs) with RL, pro-
viding high efﬁciency in complex, high-dimensional settings.
Moreover, DRL is well suited for solving NP-hard problems.
Primary ﬁndings of this work was reported in [18], which
primarily focused on reducing the CSI feedback overhead
of IA through transformer-aided CSI prediction. Since the
work in [18] is limited to IA in small-scale MIMO systems,
the present study generalizes the system model, reformulates
the sum throughput maximization problem as a distance
minimization problem, and introduces an RL-based subspace
coordination method to enhance IA in large-scale MIMO
systems.
A. Motivation and Contributions
We propose two complementary approaches to address the
practical limitations of IA, i.e., the need for global CSI at the
transmitter and limited scalability with a large number of users.
To the best of our knowledge, no prior work has investigated
integrating CSI prediction into IA to reduce CSI overhead.
This study ﬁlls that gap by introducing a transformer-based
framework for accurate CSI forecasting, which is then used to
compute precoding matrices in a small-scale multi-user MIMO
system, effectively mitigating the CSI overhead problem.
Regarding scalability, as the number of transmitter-receiver
pairs increases, the complexity of IA grows exponentially [19],
[20]. The limited scalability of IA motivates the adoption
of learning-based interference management instead of closed-
form solutions. To overcome this, we propose a RL-based
scheme to enhance IA performance in large-scale MIMO
systems while maintaining efﬁciency. The main contributions
of this study are summarized as follows:
• We conduct a comprehensive analysis of the factors
inﬂuencing IA in a multi-user MIMO system and identify
key parameters that are optimized to enable an efﬁcient
interference management scheme.
• We propose a novel method, termed transformer-aided
IA (TF-IA), which employs a CSI prediction technique to
reduce signaling overhead in IA for small-scale MIMO
systems and demonstrate its superior performance com-
pared to two baseline methods.
• We formulate a multi-objective optimization problem
based on subspace coordination to enhance scalability
of IA. We introduce two RL-based models, i.e., blind
subspace coordination with RL (RL-Blind), which assigns
the subspaces arbitrarily, and maximum SNR subspace
coordination with RL (RL-MaxSNR), which assigns them
via eigendecomposition of the channel matrix.
• We evaluate the performance of the proposed methods in
small-scale and large-scale MIMO systems, where simu-
lation results demonstrate that our approaches outperform
conventional approaches in terms of sum rate and average
user throughput.

II. PRIOR WORKS
This section reviews prior work on IA, CSI prediction, and
RL-based wireless resource management.
A. Studies on IA
The IA algorithm for MIMO communication is initially
investigated in [21], where two signaling schemes are intro-
duced for a multiple-antenna system with two transmitters
and two receivers, considering both transmitter and receiver
perspectives. In these schemes, non-linear ﬁlters are employed
at both ends, enabling the decomposition of the system into

non-interfering broadcast or multiple-access channels. The
main goal of such implementation is to achieve the highest
multiplexing gain. The work in [22] investigates the feasibility
of IA in signal vector spaces for K-user MIMO interference
channels by analyzing the resolvability of multivariate poly-
nomial systems. In [23], IA is studied for MIMO multi-cell
downlink networks with square channel matrices, where a joint
user grouping and base station association is combined with
a design of the transmit and receive beamforming matrices
based on generalized eigendecomposition.
An IA scheme for multi-cell, multi-user MIMO systems
under Gaussian interference broadcast channels is proposed
in [24]. The transmit and receive beamforming vectors are
jointly designed using the grouping methods described in
prior studies and a multiple-access channel-broadcast channel
duality is introduced to perform IA while maximizing the
performance within each cell. The resulting dual problem is
convex and solved using the interior-point method. The work
in [25] presents a quantitative performance analysis of IA
under limited feedback and derived a closed-form expression
for the average transmission rate as a function of CSI accuracy
and the number of data streams. Furthermore, an adaptive
feedback allocation scheme is proposed, and the corresponding
theoretical claims are validated through simulation results.
B. Studies on CSI Prediction
Acquiring accurate CSI is crucial for efﬁcient resource man-
agement in wireless communication systems. Consequently,
CSI prediction has become an active research topic in recent
years. The work in [26] investigates machine learning (ML)-
based CSI prediction for massive MIMO, proposing a three-
dimensional complex convolutional neural network (CNN) that
has been tested with both simulated and ﬁeld data. The work
in [27] proposes an ML-based time-division duplex scheme
that obtains CSI by learning temporal correlations of the
channel. Speciﬁcally, the CSI data is represented as images
and processed using a CNN to extract spatial patterns, fol-
lowed by an autoregressive predictor for channel forecasting.
However, since autoregressive models enable only one-step
prediction, the approach is limited in its effectiveness for CSI
prediction. The work in [28] presents CSI prediction with RNN
incorporating LSTM and gated recurrent unit (GRU), mainly
focusing on rapid channel variation due to multi-path channel
fading. The work in [29] explores the potential of transformer-
based channel prediction, testing the proposed framework with
real-world data. Therein, CSI prediction was performed using
both the encoder and decoder modules of the transformer
architecture, which increases model complexity for time-series
forecasting tasks.
In [30], an online CSI prediction scheme that leverages
historical data is proposed. The framework is designed by
incorporating CNN and LSTM architectures, and is comple-
mented by an ofﬂine-online two-step training mechanism to
achieve stable results. The work in [31] proposes a technique
based on deep learning to predict the precoder design from
historical CSI data. To implement this design, transformers
and CNN architectures are utilized to extract spatial-temporal
features from previous CSI data. Both [31], [32] employ
encoder-decoder architectures for CSI prediction, although the
decoder component is not essential for time-series forecasting.
Most existing CSI prediction studies have focused on various
interference management approaches; however, the application
of CSI prediction for IA has not been adequately explored.
C. Studies on RL-Based Wireless Resource Management
In recent years, RL-based approaches have shown strong
potential in addressing the challenges of resource manage-
ment in wireless communication networks. For instance, [33]
proposes a distributed resource management framework with
interference mitigation for wireless networks based on multi-
agent reinforcement learning (MARL). Each transmitter acts
as a deep RL agent and jointly optimizes power control and
user selection in a decentralized manner. The work in [34]
investigates the role of RL in unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV)
trajectory planning to minimize power consumption while
maximizing information freshness. In [35], spectrum sharing
in vehicular networks incorporating deep MARL is investi-
gated, where each link is modeled as an autonomous agent
that interacts with the communication environment and learns
to optimize spectrum sharing and power allocation through
Q-networks. The work in [36] proposes an interference sup-
pression scheme for heterogeneous networks by applying co-
tier intra-cell IA and formulating power control problem as
a Markov decision process (MDP), which is solved using a
deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG)-based algorithm.
However, since the approach still relies on IA in conjunction
with RL, the requirement for global CSI remains a limitation.
The work in [37] addresses the joint optimization of beam-
forming, power control, and interference management in multi-
cell millimeter-wave networks, proposing both single- and
multi-agent RL approaches for centralized and distributed
settings, respectively. Although this framework eliminates the
need for explicit CSI, the multi-agent conﬁguration introduces
higher computational complexity. In [38], a RL-based power
control framework is proposed to suppress inter-cell inter-
ference and improve energy efﬁciency in ultra-dense small-
cell downlink deployments. Recent studies have demonstrated
that RL-based interference management achieves notable per-
formance gains in dynamic wireless environments. However,
these results depend on having global CSI, which limits their
use in real-world situations.

IV. TRANSFORMER-AIDED IA FOR SMALL-SCALE MIMO

A. Preliminaries on Transformers
The original vanilla transformer [48] is a sequence-to-
sequence model composed of an encoder and a decoder,
each implemented as a stack of identical blocks. Formally,
the encoder implements a mapping fenc : (x1, . . . , xn) 7→
(z1, . . . , zn), where (z1, . . . , zn) are continuous latent repre-
sentations of the input sequence. The decoder then applies a

output sequence in an autoregressive manner. The complete
transformer architecture is constructed by stacking attention
mechanisms and point-wise fully connected layers within each
encoder and decoder block.

B. Proposed TF-IA
As described in Section I, IA requires global CSI at
each transmitter to compute appropriate precoders and post-
processing matrices. The proposed TF-IA leverages the trans-
former’s capability for time series forecasting to enable accu-
rate CSI prediction. The prediction occurs at the transmitter
end and historical CSI data is used to learn the channel
behavior. We assume that the transmitters learn the historical
CSI data through transmission of dedicated pilot signals along
with the uplink feedback message, thereby avoiding the need
of a dedicated instantaneous CSI feedback loop. Speciﬁcally,
in this study, the instantaneous channel matrix represents the
CSI of interest. We approach the CSI prediction problem by
treating the channel matrix as a compact set of time-series
signals. Consequently, we model this problem as a multivariate
time series forecasting task and utilize only the encoder block
for the entire CSI prediction.

F. Advantages and Potential Limitations of the Proposed Al-
gorithms
Achieving DoF optimal interference management with IA
typically requires global CSI at the transmitter. Since TF-IA
and RL-Blind do not rely on channel estimation, they elimi-
nate feedback overhead, achieving 100% feedback bit savings
compared to conventional IA. In contrast, the desired sub-
space computation in RL-MaxSNR is channel dependent and
therefore requires receiver feedback to update the subspace
as the channel varies. In terms of pilot usage, TF-IA and
RL-MaxSNR reduce the pilot requirement by 50%, while
RL-Blind eliminates pilot signaling entirely, achieving 100%
pilot savings relative to conventional IA.
The TF-IA approach reduces pilot usage by 50% but incurs
higher computational complexity, as it relies on learning
from historical channel data. In contrast, RL-MaxSNR has
lower computational complexity but requires explicit chan-
nel information, resulting in increased signaling overhead.
Furthermore, RL-Blind achieves improved performance with
lower computational complexity compared to TF-IA in large-
scale MIMO systems. Therefore, the selection among these
approaches depends on system design priorities, particularly
the trade-off between computational complexity, signaling
overhead, and the scale of the MIMO system.
VII. CONCLUSION
In this study, we have investigated the practical challenges
of applying IA in downlink multi-user MIMO systems. We
have identiﬁed and analyzed key parameters that can be
optimized to enable efﬁcient IA. We have introduced TF-IA,
which employs CSI prediction to reduce signaling overhead
in small-scale MIMO systems, and it has demonstrated su-
perior performance by outperforming three baseline meth-
ods. Moreover, we have formulated a multi-objective opti-
mization problem and proposed two RL-based approaches,
RL-Blind and RL-MaxSNR, to address the scalability issue
in large-scale MIMO systems, achieving effective interfer-
ence management and improved system performance. The
performance of the proposed RL-MaxSNR and RL-Blind
schemes have been investigated under varying numbers of
users and interference conditions, benchmarking them against
conventional approaches. Simulation results indicate that at
25 dB, RL-MaxSNR and RL-Blind achieve average user
throughput gains of 29.13% and 25.92%, respectively, over
the best-performing baseline, highlighting the effectiveness
of the proposed approaches in managing interference and
enhancing system performance. Extending the proposed RL-
based framework to MARL for IA optimization is left for
future work.
