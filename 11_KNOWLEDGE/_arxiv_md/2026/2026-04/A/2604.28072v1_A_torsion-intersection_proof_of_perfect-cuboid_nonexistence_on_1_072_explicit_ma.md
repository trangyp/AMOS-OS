A torsion-intersection proof of perfect-cuboid
nonexistence on 1072 explicit master-tuple fibers
René Peschmann
May 1, 2026
Abstract. Building on the genus-3 reduction CA : w2 = λ8 + Aλ4 + 1 established in
[Pes26], we give an unconditional proof of the perfect-cuboid conjecture (“Conjecture B”)
on 1,072 explicit master-tuple fibers, excluding all rational (a, b)-specialisations on each
such fiber. Our three main contributions are: (i) a structural classification theorem show-
ing that every primitive Euler-brick arises from the standard (a, b, m, n)-parametrization
up to scaling; (ii) a torsion-intersection argument applied to the elliptic quotients E′
A
and E′′
A of [Pes26, §4.1]: whenever the rank-zero hypothesis and the appropriate tor-
sion condition hold for one of them, |Hm,n(Q)| = 8 is forced, with the eight points all
corresponding to degenerate bricks; (iii) two complementary techniques to verify the
rank-zero hypothesis algorithmically — PARI’s ellrank (2-descent), and where this is
ambiguous, Sage’s exact rational evaluation of L(E, 1)/ΩE via modular symbols, which
combined with the modularity theorem [BCDT01], Kolyvagin’s theorem [Kol88], and
Edixhoven’s bound on the Manin constant for semistable curves [Edi91] yields an uncon-
ditional rank-zero certificate — together with an explicit lift count refining the naive
torsion-intersection bound when the torsion is larger than the leading case. We exhibit
1,072 such fibers with max(m, n) ≤100 on which Conjecture B is thereby established
unconditionally.
1. Introduction
1.1. The perfect-cuboid problem
An Euler-brick is a rectangular parallelepiped with integer edges X, Y, Z ∈N and integer
face diagonals
√
X2 + Y 2,
√
X2 + Z2,
√
Y 2 + Z2 ∈N.
A perfect cuboid (or perfect Euler-brick) is an Euler-brick with additionally rational space
diagonal
√
X2 + Y 2 + Z2 ∈N.
Conjecture 1.1 (Cuboid Conjecture; “Conjecture B”). No perfect cuboid exists in N3.
The conjecture has been open since at least the work of Saunderson (1740) and remains
a long-standing problem in elementary number theory. Computational searches have ruled
out perfect cuboids with the smallest edge below 5 · 1011 and the odd edge below 2.5 · 1013
([Rat03], [Mat07]).
1.2. Master tuples
Following the standard parametrization, we associate to a 4-tuple (a, b, m, n) ∈N4 with
gcd(a, b) = gcd(m, n) = 1, a > b, m > n, and a −b, m −n odd, the quantities
U1 = a2 −b2,
V1 = 2ab,
W1 = a2 + b2,
U2 = m2 −n2,
V2 = 2mn,
W2 = m2 + n2.

4.3. Main theorem
Theorem 4.5 (Main result). Let (m, n) be a master-tuple fiber. Then |Hm,n(Q)| = 8 with
all rational points degenerate (and hence no perfect cuboid exists on the fiber) if either of
the following holds:
(a) (Naive case via E3.) rk(E3(Q)) = 0 and |E3(Q)tors| = 4.
(b) (Naive case via EuV .) rk(EuV (Q)) = 0 and |EuV (Q)tors| = 6.
(c) (Refined case via EuV .) rk(EuV (Q)) = 0, |EuV (Q)tors| = 8, and an explicit enumera-
tion of the eight rational torsion points on the EuV -quartic shows that exactly six of
them lift rationally to Hm,n (i.e. the four ramification points (u, V ) = (±2, ±4U 2
2),
written in the EuV -coordinate u = t + 1/t, together with the two points at infinity).
Proof. Case (a): Lemma 4.3 shows π3 has no rational ramification points, so Lemma 4.2
gives |Hm,n(Q)| ≤2 · 4 = 8. The eight trivial points of Lemma 4.1 provide the lower
bound |Hm,n(Q)| ≥8. Combining, |Hm,n(Q)| = 8.
Case (b): The map πuV : Hm,n →EuV has four rational ramification points (at t = ±1
with V = ±4U 2
2, all lying in EuV (Q)tors). For the bound, decompose EuV (Q) = R ∪N
where R is the set of four rational ramification points and N the remaining |tors| −4 = 2
rational torsion points. Each P ∈R has exactly one rational Hm,n-preimage; each P ∈N

has at most two. Hence |Hm,n(Q)| ≤4 · 1 + 2 · 2 = 8. With the lower bound from the

Case (c): Same decomposition with |R| = 4 ramification points and |N| = 4 non-
ramification rational torsion points (since |tors| = 8). The additional hypothesis says: of

4.4. Unconditional rank-zero certification

5. Discussion
5.1. What remains uncovered
The combined methodology covers 1,072 out of 2,040 fibers up to Mmax = 100. The
remaining 968 fibers fall into two categories:
(a) Persistently ambiguous rank. For at least one quotient Eq, PARI’s ellrank
returns [0, k] with k > 0 and the modular-symbol computation L(E, 1)/ΩE either
vanishes (forcing rk(Eq) ≥1) or fails to terminate within our compute budget
(large conductor > 1012, especially for EuV ). These fibers most likely either have
rk(Eq) ≥1 for every quotient, or are in principle treatable by allocating more
compute to the modular-symbol computation.
(b) Hard fibers. Fibers where all three elliptic factors have rank ≥1 (rigorously
bounded below). Then Lemma 4.2 does not apply to any factor. Standard (linear)
Chabauty–Coleman applies only when rk(Jac(Hm,n)(Q)) < g(Hm,n) = 3, that is
when the total rank from Corollary 3.2 is at most 2. Empirically, total rank ≥3
occurs in the majority of these fibers; quadratic Chabauty would be the natural
next tool.
The asymptotic regime max(m, n) > 100 is not addressed by the present scan; a uniform
statement (positive proportion of fibers with a rank-zero quotient, or a Brauer obstruction
independent of (m, n)) would be needed to settle this.
Example 5.1 (A hard-case fiber: (m, n) = (5, 2)). With U2 = 21, V2 = 20, W2 = 29, the
three elliptic factors have ranks
rk(EPQ(Q)) = 2,
rk(EuV (Q)) = 1,
rk(E3(Q)) = 1,
so rk(Jac(H5,2)(Q)) = 4 ≥g(H5,2) = 3. Neither Lemma 4.2 nor standard Chabauty
applies. PARI’s hyperellratpoints up to height B = 106 still finds only the eight trivial
points, but this provides only empirical evidence, not a proof.
5.2. Possible extensions
(i) Quadratic Chabauty. For genus-3 hyperelliptic curves with rk(Jac) = g, the methods
of Balakrishnan–Dogra–Müller–Tuitman [BDM+23] give effective enumeration of
rational points via p-adic heights. An adaptation to our family would close many of
the hard fibers in (b).
(ii) Mordell–Weil sieving.
Combining the rank-decomposition of Corollary 3.2 with
reductions modulo many small primes, one can in principle bound |Hm,n(Q)| tighter
than via Coleman alone. The decomposition Jac(Hm,n) ∼EPQ × EuV × E3 makes
this approach particularly attractive: each factor’s reduction is an explicit elliptic
curve over Fp.
(iii) Brauer–Manin obstruction. The cuboid surface S ⊂P7

conditions

out finding an obstruction so far. A uniform obstruction would settle Conjecture 1.1
entirely.
(iv) Modular and CM interpretations. The factor E3 in our decomposition has the form
V 2 = (V 2
2 w2 + 4U 2

a modular parametrization or CM structure that would yield uniform rank bounds.
(v) Statistical evidence. Goldfeld’s conjecture [Gol79] predicts that, in a one-parameter

with density 1

5.3. Connection to Conjecture B
Combining Theorems 2.4, 4.5 and Lemma 2.5, we have the following precise version of
our partial result:
Corollary 5.2 (Partial Conjecture B). Let S100 ⊂N2 denote the set of 1,072 pairs (m, n)
listed in Appendix A. Then no perfect cuboid (X, Y, Z) ∈N3 exists whose primitive form
arises from a master tuple (a, b, m, n) with (m, n) ∈S100.
The corollary covers an explicit family of 1,072 master-tuple fibers. For each fiber
(m, n) ∈S100, the result excludes a perfect cuboid arising from any master tuple (a, b, m, n)
with that fixed (m, n), regardless of the rank of Em,n (which controls how many master
tuples populate the fiber but does not enter the torsion-intersection argument). The
full Conjecture B would follow from extending S100 to all of {(m, n) ∈N2 : gcd(m, n) =
1, m −n odd}.

Appendix B. Computational verification
The verification scripts are written in SageMath 10.7 [Sag25] and use PARI/GP ver-
sion 2.17.3 [PAR25]. Three rigorous certificates are combined:
(1) Sharp ellrank. PARI’s ellrank (called with effort = 2) returns rank bounds
[rlow, rup] via 2-descent; we accept rank-zero only when rlow = rup = 0.
(2) Kolyvagin certification.
For ambiguous bounds [0, k] with k > 0, we call
Sage’s analytic_rank_upper_bound, which returns a rigorous upper bound on
ords=1L(E, s). When this is 0, rk(E(Q)) = 0 follows from Theorem 4.7.
(3) Lift refinement. For fibers with rk(EuV ) = 0 and |tors| = 8, the eight rational
torsion points on the EuV -quartic are enumerated via PARI’s hyperellratpoints
(with height 105); the lift to Hm,n is verified by the discriminant test of §4.5.
Torsion structure is computed via Sage’s E.torsion_subgroup() on the Weierstrass
model obtained from the quartic via PARI’s ellfromeqn. All scripts are reproducible
from the companion repository:
https://github.com/renpe/euler-brick-obstructions/tree/main/paper3
The main pipeline analysis/jh_torsion_full_dispatch.py (with subprocess
workers analysis/jh_torsion_full_worker.py) runs in roughly 5 minutes on a
30-core workstation, producing data/proven_fibers.csv and a full per-fiber log
data/scan_full_M100.jsonl.
The proof of Theorem 4.5 relies only on the three
certificates above; height searches via PARI’s hyperellratpoints up to B = 106 are an
independent sanity check and find no non-trivial rational points on any fiber.
