Abstract. We introduce (lim-)perfectoid splitting, which is a global variant of (lim-)perfectoid
purity. Our main result establishes a correspondence between the lim-perfectoid splitting
of projective schemes and the lim-perfectoid purity of their Gorenstein section rings. As
an application, we construct a new supply of examples of lim-perfectoid pure rings that go
beyond the previously known complete intersection or splinter-type cases.

1. Introduction
This paper studies a local–global correspondence for singularities in mixed-characteristic
algebraic geometry through perfectoid theory introduced by Scholze [Sch12]. In character-
istic p, the prototype is provided by Frobenius splitting and related F-singularities: global
Frobenius-theoretic properties of projective varieties are reflected in the F-singularities of
their section rings [Wat91, SS10]. In particular, this correspondence makes the F-splitting
computable in many examples, since one can apply local criteria such as Fedder’s criterion
[Fed83] to the section ring. Our goal is to develop a mixed-characteristic analogue of this
picture.
In mixed characteristic, the role of Frobenius is played by maps to perfectoid rings. Indeed,
a complete Noetherian local ring R of mixed characteristic is regular if and only if there exists
a faithfully flat map R →P to a perfectoid ring P [BIM19]. Motivated by this, Bhatt et al.
introduced (lim-)perfectoid pure1 singularities as mixed-characteristic analogues of F-pure
singularities [Bha+24].
From the perspective of lim-perfectoid pure singularities, it is natural to consider a global
analogue obtained by replacing the local splitting condition with its geometric counterpart.
In order to explain global theory, we prepare notations.
Notation 1.1. Let V be a p-torsion-free discrete valuation ring with residue characteristic p,
and let X be a flat projective scheme over V such that H0(X, OX) ∼= V . Write d := dim X.
Let c
X be the p-adic completion of X. Let OX(1) be an ample line bundle on X and set the
section ring R := L
m≥0 H0(X, OX(m)).
A natural global analogue of perfectoid purity is provided by the notion of perfectoid split.
Namely, we say that X is perfectoid split if there exists an affine morphism π: Y →X
such that the p-adic formal completion bY is a perfectoid formal scheme and the natural
map OX →π∗OY is ind-split, that is, a filtered colimit of split morphisms in QCoh(X)
(Definition 3.1).
In one direction, the relation with the local theory is relatively well behaved: if the section
ring R∧p is perfectoid pure, then X is perfectoid split (Proposition 5.3) by the theory of
graded perfectoid rings [IY25]. The converse direction is more delicate, since it is not clear
in general whether a perfectoid cover of X gives rise to a corresponding perfectoid cover of
R. For this reason, to establish a global-to-local correspondence, we instead adopt a different
approach based on absolute perfectoidization, which leads us to the notion of lim-perfectoid
splitting.

The comparison theorem Theorem A makes it possible to pass between the local theory
of the section ring R and the global geometry of X, and hence to take advantage of both
viewpoints. In particular, by combining Theorem A with the Fedder-type criteria for per-
fectoid purity established in [Yos25b,Yos25a], we obtain a practical method for constructing
examples. These examples further show that perfectoid splitting and lim-perfectoid splitting
are not merely mixed-characteristic analogues of F-splitting, but exhibit new phenomena
specific to mixed characteristic.
Example 1.3. Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p.
(1) Any W(k)-lift of an elliptic curve over k is perfectoid split (Example 5.19).
(2) The canonical W(k)-lift X of an ordinary abelian variety over k is perfectoid split
(Example 5.18).
(3) Let X be a hypersurface Calabi–Yau variety over k such that the Artin–Mazur height
of X is infinite and dim X ≥2. If p = 2, then there exist W(k)-lifts X1 and X2 of X
such that X1 is not lim-perfectoid split and X2 is perfectoid split (Example 5.20(2)).
(4) Let X ⊆P3
W(k) be a smooth hypersurface of degree 4 over W(k). If X is lim-perfectoid
split, then the p-adic completion of every section ring of X is lim-perfectoid pure
(Corollary 5.14). Furthermore, if p ̸= 2, then X is automatically lim-perfectoid split
by [Yos26, Thoerem A]. This yields a new supply of examples of mixed-characteristic
singularities that go beyond the previously known complete intersection or splinter-
type cases (cf. Example 5.21).
Structure of this paper. In Section 2, we introduce notation and terminology and review
our previous work. In Section 3, we introduce several notions of perfectoid purity for (formal)
schemes. In Section 4, we develop the basic theory of local cohomology for derived graded
modules and construct a fiber sequence (Theorem B), which is a key ingredient in the proof
of Theorem A. In Section 5, we prove Theorem A using the fiber sequence.
