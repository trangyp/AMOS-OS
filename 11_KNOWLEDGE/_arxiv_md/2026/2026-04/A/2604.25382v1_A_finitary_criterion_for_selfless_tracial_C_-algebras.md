A finitary criterion for selfless tracial C*-algebras
Ali Jabbari
Abstract. We study the class of selfless C*-probability spaces intro-
duced by Robert. It is known that a selfless tracial algebra has strict
comparison and a unique trace. We prove that for separable tracial C*-
algebras, selflessness is equivalent to approximate selflessness, a finitary
condition: for every finite set F, every N ≥1 and ε > 0 there exists a
unitary u with |τ(uk)| < ε (1 ≤|k| ≤N) and |τ(w)| < ε for all alter-
nating words w of length ≤N built from centered elements of F and
powers un (|n| ≤N). The equivalence is established using a diagonal-
isation argument in the tracial ultrapower. As an application we give
a concise proof that countable groups with a topologically-free extreme
boundary are C*-selfless. We also discuss the relation to nuclearity and
Z-stability.
1. Introduction
The notion of a selfless C*-probability space was introduced in [10]. A
unital C*-algebra A ̸= C equipped with a state ρ having faithful GNS repre-
sentation is called selfless if the first-factor embedding (A, ρ) ,→(A, ρ)∗(A, ρ)
is existential in the sense of continuous model theory. Intuitively, a selfless
algebra is one that, after passing to a suitable ultrapower, contains a free
Haar unitary independent from the diagonal copy of A; this property lies
strictly between being existentially closed and having a central sequence of
matrix units, blending aspects of model theory and free probability. Selfless
C*-probability spaces enjoy strong structural properties such as simplicity,
possessing a unique trace (if tracial), satisfying strict comparison, and pos-
sessing the uniform Dixmier property [10].

Authors in [1] have obtained deep structural results for reduced free
product C*-algebras. In particular, [1] proves that Voiculescu’s free semi-
circular algebras Sn (n ≥2) are selfless, and more generally establishes self-
lessness for a wide class of free products under mild rapid-decay hypotheses.
Those results do not follow from the approximate selflessness characterisa-
tion given here; rather they illustrate the power of constructive techniques
(filtrations, Khintchine inequalities, asymptotic orthogonality) that are com-
plementary to our model-theoretic approach. We therefore refer the reader
to [1] for a comprehensive treatment of selflessness in the context of free
products.
Recently, Hayes et al. [5] introduced the notion of selfless inclusions of
C*-probability spaces and used it to establish selflessness for new families
of C*-algebras, including the reduced free unitary compact quantum groups
Au(n) (n ≥2) and the half-liberated unitary quantum groups A∗
u(n). Their
methods rely on free complexifications and a Powers–Haagerup–Pisier prop-
erty, which are complementary to the finitary approach developed in the
present paper.
More recently, Gao et al. [4] have proved selflessness for reduced amal-
gamated free products and HNN extensions of C*-algebras, using a version
of Ozawa’s PHP property in the C*-algebraic setting. Their results further
expand the class of selfless algebras by addressing amalgamation and graph
products, and provide another route to selflessness that is independent of
the approximate selflessness characterisation used here.
It was proved in [9] (see also the comprehensive geometric study [8])
that all countable discrete groups admitting a minimal topologically-free
extremely proximal action are C*-selfless. The original proof uses a topolog-
ical tree-graded space construction. In the present paper we obtain a concise
proof of this result by applying our finitary criterion; see Theorem 3 and
its proof in Section 4. The geometric foundations of such boundary actions
(Myrberg points, visual boundaries of CAT(0) spaces, Roller boundaries,
etc.) are developed in depth in [8], and we freely rely on those results for the
construction of axial sequences; our contribution is a short, model-theoretic
verification of the approximate selflessness condition.
Existential embeddings are global statements that are often difficult to
verify directly. A natural goal is to find a finitary, quantitative reformulation
that can be tested on generators and relations. This paper provides such
a reformulation for separable algebras. We introduce the notion of approx-
imately selfless (Definition 2) which only requires, for every finite set and
every tolerance, the existence of a unitary satisfying explicit moment condi-
tions. This yields a finitary reformulation of an existential embedding prop-
erty. Our main result (Theorem 2) states that for separable C*-probability
spaces with a faithful trace, selflessness is equivalent to approximate self-
lessness.

Value of the finitary criterion. Approximate selflessness provides a fini-
tary, local test for an inherently infinitary (model-theoretic) property. In-
stead of constructing an existential embedding into an infinite free product,
one only needs to produce unitaries that satisfy explicit moment conditions
up to arbitrary precision. This makes selflessness accessible to concrete com-
putations and provides a bridge to free probability and asymptotic freeness.
The equivalence is obtained by a diagonalisation argument in a tracial
ultrapower. The approximate conditions allow us to construct, via a cofinal
sequence, a single unitary in an ultrapower that is exactly free from the
diagonal copy of A. This yields an existential embedding, and conversely
selflessness transfers back through the ultrapower to give the required ap-
proximate unitaries.
We also discuss the interaction with nuclearity and Z-stability. While
selfless nuclear algebras automatically have strict comparison and a unique
trace, it remains an open question whether they are necessarily Z-stable.
We emphasise that the known structural consequences (Theorem 1) are due
to Robert [10]; no claim of Z-stability is made here.
Conceptual position of selflessness. Selflessness is a global property: it
requires the first-factor embedding into the free product to be existential.
This is weaker than being existentially closed (which would require existen-
tial embeddings into any extension), and it is distinct from being strongly
self-absorbing (which involves tensor products and central sequences rather
than free products). In the tracial setting it is also unrelated to nuclearity:
C∗
r (F∞) is selfless and non-nuclear, while many nuclear algebras fail to be
selfless. It implies strict comparison and a unique trace, but it does not
obviously entail finite nuclear dimension or Z-stability. The property also
appears independent of exactness and of the completely bounded approx-
imation property, though a precise delineation remains a topic for future
study. The finitary criterion we provide — approximate selflessness — is
new and can be seen as a C*-algebraic analogue of mixing properties for
von Neumann algebras, but without requiring a distinguished subalgebra.
Unlike McDuff-type properties (which involve a central sequence of ma-
trix algebras), approximate selflessness involves only a single unitary that is
asymptotically free from the algebra. This is close in spirit to Voiculescu’s
asymptotic freeness and to central sequence techniques, but with the crucial
twist that the freeness is required only in an ultrapower, not in the algebra
itself.
In this paper we work exclusively with tracial C*-probability spaces,
i.e. the state is a faithful trace; we denote it by τ. The general selflessness
notion of [10] allows arbitrary states, but our results require a trace to make
the free product with C(T) well-behaved and to use the alternating word
conditions. The necessary background is collected in Section 2.

3. Approximately selfless algebras and the finitary criterion
We now give a quantitative characterisation of selflessness for separa-
ble algebras. All freeness statements below are understood with respect to
the trace and the reduced free product construction. The approximate con-
ditions are designed so that, via ultrapowers, they yield exact freeness in
the sense of free probability: a Haar unitary that is independent from the
original algebra with respect to the ultrapower trace.

4. Groups with topologically-free extreme boundaries are
C*-selfless
In this section we apply the approximate selflessness criterion to give a
concise proof of the main theorem of [9] (see also the comprehensive geomet-
ric treatment in [8]). The geometric construction of axial sequences relies
on the deep results of [8], but the verification of the approximate condition
is brief.
We recall the necessary definitions from [9] (and [8]). Let Γ be a count-
able discrete group and let Γ ↷X be an action on a compact Hausdorff
space X.
The action is called an extreme boundary if it is minimal and
extremely proximal: for every non-empty open sets U, V ⊂X there exists
g ∈Γ such that g(X \ U) ⊂V . The action is topologically free if the set
of points with trivial stabiliser is dense in X.
A sequence (zn)n in Γ is
called axial if there exists a topologically-free extreme boundary Γ ↷X
with distinct points z± ∈X such that:
(i) for every neighbourhood U± of z±, one has zn(X \ U−) ⊂U+ even-
tually, i.e., for all sufficiently large n,
(ii) the Γ-action on {z±} is free, i.e., g{z±} ∩{z±} ̸= ∅implies g = 1.
It is shown in [9] and [8] that a second-countable extreme boundary with
|X| > 2 admits an axial sequence if and only if it is topologically free.

5. Examples and non-examples
We now collect examples and non-examples to illustrate the concept.
Example 2. The reduced group C*-algebra C∗
r (F∞) is selfless; see [10,
Corollary 2.3]. The same argument shows that C∗
r (Fn) for n ≥2 is selfless.
As demonstrated in Example 1, approximate selflessness can be verified
directly using the free group generators, giving an alternative proof.
Example 3. Let A = Mk(C) with its unique tracial state. Then A is not
selfless. A selfless C*-algebra must contain a Haar unitary in its ultrapower
(Lemma 2), hence it is infinite-dimensional. Mk(C) is finite-dimensional, so
it cannot be selfless. (One may also note that the first-factor embedding
Mk ,→Mk ∗C(T) is not existential, but the dimension argument is simpler.)
Example 4. Let A = C(X) with a faithful tracial state τ, where X is
a compact metric space with a probability measure of full support. Then
A is not selfless because it is not simple (unless X is a singleton, but then
A ∼= C is excluded).
Example 5. It is not known whether the Jiang–Su algebra Z is selfless.
If it is, then Question 1 would be answered positively. Conversely, a coun-
terexample to Question 1 would be a nuclear selfless algebra not isomorphic
to Z ⊗A for any A; no such algebra is currently known.
6. Relation to nuclearity and Z-stability
We emphasise that we do not prove Z-stability here. The only known
consequences of selflessness for tracial algebras are those listed in Theo-
rem 1 (strict comparison, unique trace, uniform Dixmier property, stable
rank one). Whether nuclear selfless algebras are Z-stable is an open prob-
lem.
It is a common misconception that Z-stability implies nuclearity.
In
fact, if B is any non-nuclear C*-algebra, then A = B ⊗Z is Z-stable and
non-nuclear, since tensoring a non-nuclear algebra with a unital nuclear
algebra yields a non-nuclear algebra. Thus nuclearity is not necessary for
Z-stability. Consequently, the absence of nuclearity (as in the free group
C*-algebras) does not provide a counterexample.
The reduced group C*-algebra C∗
r (F∞) is selfless and non-nuclear. Whether
it is Z-stable is a deep open problem; it does not serve as a test case for
nuclear selfless algebras.
While selflessness implies strict comparison and uniqueness of trace,
these are also necessary conditions for Z-stability in the nuclear case. By
the Toms–Winter theorem, a nuclear, simple, separable, unital C*-algebra
with strict comparison and finite nuclear dimension is Z-stable [12, 15].
However, strict comparison alone is not sufficient; counterexamples exist
with infinite nuclear dimension (e.g., the Razak–Jacelon algebra [6]).
Selflessness provides additional structure: the existence, in every ul-
trapower, of a unitary that is free from the diagonal copy of A. This is

A positive answer would make selflessness a new sufficient condition
for Z-stability, independent of finite nuclear dimension. A negative answer
would require a nuclear, selfless algebra with strict comparison and unique
trace but infinite nuclear dimension – an object not known to exist.
7. Concluding remarks
(1) Theorem 2 provides a concrete, finitary criterion for selflessness for
separable algebras. The four alternating word patterns ensure that
the state on the free product vanishes on all mixed terms.
(2) Selfless tracial algebras automatically satisfy strict comparison and
have a unique trace. In the nuclear case, these are necessary con-
ditions for Z-stability, but they are not known to be sufficient.
Question 1 remains a natural open problem.
(3) The example C∗
r (F∞) shows that selflessness does not force nucle-
arity, but does not settle the Z-stability question.
Appendix A. Comparison with mixing and weak mixing notions
For completeness we briefly position selflessness relative to better-known
concepts in von Neumann algebra theory.
(i) Mixing MASA: A subalgebra B ⊂M (usually a MASA) is mix-
ing if for all a, b ∈M ⊖B one has τ(unau∗
nb) →0 along a net of
unitaries from B, see [11] for more details. This requires a distin-
guished subalgebra and is a property of an inclusion.
(ii) Weak mixing: Similar but with Ces`aro convergence, we refer to
[11].
(iii) Selflessness: A property of a single C*-algebra (with a faithful
trace) that does not refer to any subalgebra. It is equivalent to
the existence of a Haar unitary that is free from the algebra in the
ultrapower, i.e., asymptotic freeness. Selflessness is closer in spirit
to the notion of strongly self-absorbing but is distinct, see [14]. For
example Z is strongly self-absorbing but it is not known whether
it is selfless.
Thus selflessness is not directly comparable to mixing or weak mixing;
it is a different kind of regularity condition.
