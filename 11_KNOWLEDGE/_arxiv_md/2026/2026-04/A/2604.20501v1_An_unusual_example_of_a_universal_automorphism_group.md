AN UNUSUAL EXAMPLE OF A UNIVERSAL AUTOMORPHISM GROUP
ROB SULLIVAN AND JEROEN WINKEL
Abstract. Let M be a Fra¨ıss´e structure (a countably infinite ultrahomogeneous structure). We refer to the
class of structures embeddable in M as the ω-age of M. We consider the following two properties of M: we
say that M has a universal automorphism group if, for each A in the ω-age of M, there is an embedding
Aut(A) →Aut(M), and we say that M has group-extensible ω-age if, for each A in the ω-age of M, there is
an embedding A →M such that each automorphism of the image extends to an automorphism of M and the
extension map preserves group composition. It is immediate that if M has group-extensible ω-age, then M has
a universal automorphism group. We give an example of a Fra¨ıss´e structure with a universal automorphism
group whose ω-age is not group-extensible, showing that the above two properties are not equivalent.
1. Introduction
1.1. Universal automorphism groups. A first-order structure M is ultrahomogeneous if each isomorphism
between finitely generated substructures of M extends to an automorphism of M. We assume the reader is
familiar with ultrahomogeneous structures: see [Mac11], [Hod93] for background. In this paper, we are concerned
with the universality of automorphism groups of such structures. Given a countable ultrahomogeneous structure
M, we define the ω-age of M, written Ageω(M), to be the class of structures embeddable in M (including
infinite structures). We say that Aut(M) is universal if, for each A ∈Ageω(M), there is a group embedding
Aut(A) →Aut(M). Note that, a priori, the group embedding need not have any relation to the structure.
We call a countably infinite ultrahomogeneous structure M a Fra¨ıss´e structure. Many Fra¨ıss´e structures are
known to have universal automorphism groups. M¨uller proved in [M¨ul16] that Aut(M) is universal for any
Fra¨ıss´e structure M with a local stationary independence relation: examples of such structures are those with
free amalgamation, the generic poset and the rational Urysohn space. In addition, [KSW25, Theorem 4.4]
gives that any linearly ordered Fra¨ıss´e structure with a local stationary weak independence relation has a
universal automorphism group: examples include the generic ordered graph, the generic order expansion of
the rational Urysohn space and the generic n-linear order (the Fra¨ıss´e limit of the class of finite structures
A = (dom(A), <0, · · · , <n−1) where each <i is a linear order on dom(A)). Other examples of Fra¨ıss´e structures
with universal automorphism groups are the countable atomless Boolean algebra, the generic lattice, the generic
semilattice and the random tournament ([KM17]). See [Hen71], [Kat88], [Usp90], [MW92], [Bil12], [BM13] for
earlier results; there is a discussion of these in the introduction of [KSW25].
1.2. Group-extensive embeddings. In each example in the literature known to the authors, the method for
showing universality of Aut(M) is similar: we call it a tower construction. We first define some terminology:
Definition 1.1. Let M be a countable ultrahomogeneous structure. An embedding f : A →M is group-
extensive if there exists a group embedding η : Aut(f(A)) →Aut(M) with g ⊆η(g) for all g ∈Aut(f(A)). If
each A ∈Ageω(M) admits a group-extensive embedding A →M, we say that Ageω(M) is group-extensible.
For each A ∈Ageω(M), the tower construction builds a group-extensive embedding A →M. Universality of
Aut(M) then immediately results from the group-extensibility of Ageω(M). The most general formulation of
the tower construction is due to Kubi´s and Maˇsulovi´c, who provided a categorical version in [KM17] via what
they termed Katˇetov functors.
We provide a simple example to illustrate the idea of how group-extensive embeddings are built using the
tower construction, giving a sketch for the Rado graph, denoted by Γ. Given a countable graph A, inductively
construct A = M0 ⊆M1 ⊆· · · as follows: to construct Mk from Mk−1, for each F ⊆fin Mk−1, we add a new

1.3. Failure of universality. It was unknown for a long time whether there was in fact any Fra¨ıss´e structure
with a non-universal automorphism group. (Jaligot asked this in [Jal07].) The first such examples of non-
universality were given in [KS20] by Kubi´s and Shelah. They showed that universality can fail dramatically,
constructing for instance examples of Fra¨ıss´e structures M in a finite relational language where Aut(M) is
torsion-free and the class {Aut(A) | A ∈Age(M)} contains all finite symmetric groups. [KSW25, Theorem
5.1] gives more examples, showing that some well-known Fra¨ıss´e structures have non-universal automorphism
groups: for example, the semigeneric tournament and the generic n-anticlique-free oriented graph.
1.4. New results. In [KSW25], the authors and A. Kwiatkowska considered a range of Fra¨ıss´e structures M,
showing either the universality of Aut(M) via group-extensive embeddings (built with the tower construction,
using the framework of Katˇetov functors from [KM17]), or the non-universality of Aut(M) via ad hoc methods
particular to the structure in question.
In discussions around [KSW25], Kwiatkowska, Bartoˇs, Kubi´s and Vaccaro asked the following:
Question 1.2. Let M be a Fra¨ıss´e structure. If Aut(M) is universal, does this imply that Ageω(M) is group-
extensible?
This question resulted from the observation that, for all previous examples in the literature (those known
to the authors, at least), universality of Aut(M) was shown by proving the group-extensibility of Ageω(M).
Another way of viewing this question is that it asks whether the existence of an abstract group embedding
Aut(A) →Aut(M) must always be “structurally witnessed” by an embedding A →M.
The main result of this short paper gives a negative answer to Question 1.2.
Theorem 1.3. There exists a Fra¨ıss´e structure D in a finite relational language, where D is transitive and has
strong amalgamation, such that Aut(D) is universal and Ageω(D) is not group-extensible.
(In the above theorem we use the following standard terminology: a countable ultrahomogeneous structure
M is transitive if the permutation action Aut(M) ↷M is transitive, and M has strong amalgamation if, for
each pair of embeddings B ←A →C in Age(M), there is an amalgam B →D ←C in Age(M) where the
intersection of the images of B and C in D is exactly the image of A.)
We show universality of Aut(D) via a new method: for each A ∈Ageω(D), rather than using the tower
construction as in previous examples, we produce a group embedding Aut(A) →Aut(D) by constructing an
L-structure M ∼= D on which Aut(A) acts faithfully by automorphisms of M. We conjecture that this new
method should be applicable more generally, showing the universality of Aut(M) for other Fra¨ıss´e structures
M, though in this paper we only concern ourselves with the particular structure D.
Remark 1.4. [HS19, Example Two, pg. 13] can be adapted to give an example of a finite ultrahomogeneous
structure B such that Aut(B) is universal and Age(B) is not group-extensible, as follows. Let LR = {R} be a
language consisting of a binary relation symbol R, and let B be the LR-structure with domain {a, a′, b0, b1, b2, b3}
and
RB = {(a, b0), (a, b2), (a′, b1), (a′, b3), (a, a), (a′, a′)} ∪{(bi, bi+1) | i < 4},
where addition is (mod 4). Note that B is a digraph with loops. It is straightforward to check that B is
ultrahomogeneous and Aut(B) is a cyclic group of order 4 with generator f ∈Aut(B) specified by
f(a) = a′, f(a′) = a, f(bi) = bi+1 for i < 4,
and that Aut(B) is universal. Let A be the substructure induced by B on {a, a′}, and let g ∈Aut(A) be the
involution swapping a, a′. It is straightforward to see that idA, g are the only embeddings of A in B, and any
h ∈Aut(B) extending g has order 4. So Age(B) is not group-extensible. Note that B is not transitive and does
not have strong amalgamation. We thank W. Kubi´s for pointing this example out to us.
2. Notation and terminology
We write λ : G ↷V for an action of a group G on a set V . For brevity, we will often say that V is a G-set to
mean that V is equipped with an action λ : G ↷V . We write gv to mean g · v, and we extend this notation to
sets in the natural manner: for U ⊆V , we write gU = {gu | u ∈U}. For v ∈V we also write Gv = {gv | g ∈G}.
Let S be a structure with domain V . If the action λ : G ↷V is by automorphisms of S, we will say that S
is a G-structure. When we refer to a G-structure with domain V , where V is a G-set, we always assume the
actions on the G-structure and on the G-set are the same action. We also use this terminology with the specific

3. The structure
Definition 3.1. Let LR = {R} be a language consisting of a binary relation symbol R. We call an LR-structure
A an I3-free oriented graph if RA is an oriented graph relation (irreflexive and asymmetric) and the substructure
on any 3-set contains at least one oriented edge.
Let LS be a language consisting of a 4-ary relation symbol S. We say that an LS-structure A is a semifinal
structure if SA = S
B⊆A,|B|=4 SB and, for each B ⊆A, |B| = 4, there is an enumeration b0, b1, b2, b3 of the
vertices of B such that
SB = {(b0, b1, b2, b3), (b2, b3, b0, b1)}.
We refer to SB as the semifinal on B. (The name semifinal structure is intended to evoke the semifinals of a
football tournament where b0 wins against b1 and b2 wins against b3.)
Let L = LR ∪LS. We let D denote the class of finite L-structures A such that (dom(A), RA) is an I3-free
oriented graph and (dom(A), SA) is a semifinal structure. It is straightforward to see that the class D has
strong amalgamation: given B, C ∈D with B ∩C = A, define an amalgam of B, C over A by adding oriented
edges (b, c) for all b ∈B \ A, c ∈C \ A and by defining S arbitrarily on any set of four vertices which is not
contained in B or in C. We denote the Fra¨ıss´e limit of D by D, and we let D = Ageω(D).
We first give conditions for a G-set to admit a G-structure in D; we use these in Lemmas 4.4 and 4.6.
Lemma 3.2. Let G be a group, and V a G-set. The following are equivalent:
(i) for all U ∈[V ]3, there are distinct u, v ∈U such that there is no g ∈G swapping u, v;
(ii) there is an I3-free G-oriented graph A with domain V such that, for all distinct u, v ∈V , if there is no
edge between u, v, then there is g ∈G swapping u, v;
(iii) there is an I3-free G-oriented graph A with domain V .
Proof. (i) ⇒(ii): Define an oriented graph A = (V, RA) as follows: for each orbit O ⊆[V ]2 of the element-wise
action G ↷[V ]2 induced by G ↷V , take an orbit representative {u, v}, and if there is no g ∈G swapping
u, v, orient {u, v} as (u, v) and add G(u, v) to RA. As there is no g ∈G swapping u, v, this gives a well-defined
orientation on each pair of vertices in O. If there is g ∈G swapping u, v, do not add an edge to RA for any pair
of vertices in O. It is immediate that (V, RA) is a G-oriented graph such that, for all distinct u, v ∈V , if there
is no edge between u, v, then there is g ∈G swapping u, v, and (i) immediately gives that (V, RA) is I3-free.
(ii) ⇒(iii): trivial.
(iii) ⇒(i): As A is I3-free, for any U ⊆A, |U| = 3, there are u, v ∈U with R(u, v), and thus there is no g ∈G
swapping u, v, as G acts by automorphisms of A.
□
Lemma 3.3. Let G be a group, and V a G-set. The following are equivalent:
(i) for each U ∈[V ]4, there is an enumeration v0, v1, v2, v3 of U such that the action of each element of
G{U} \ G(U) on U is the double transposition (v0v2)(v1v3);
(ii) there is a G-semifinal structure A with domain V .
Proof. (i) ⇒(ii): For each orbit O of G ↷[V ]4, we enumerate an orbit representative of O as v0, v1, v2, v3 so that
condition (i) of Lemma 3.3 is satisfied, and add G(v0, v1, v2, v3) ∪G(v2, v3, v0, v1) to SA. It is straightforward
to verify that this gives a G-semifinal structure A = (V, SA).
(ii) ⇒(i): Let U ∈[V ]4, and let v0, v1, v2, v3 be an enumeration of U with (v0, v1, v2, v3) ∈SA; condition (i) for
U immediately follows from the fact that A is a G-semifinal structure, so any g ∈G{U} \G(U) preserves SA.
□
We use the (ii) ⇒(i) direction of the above Lemma 3.3 frequently throughout the paper – as we use it so often
and its proof is straightforward, we do not explicitly reference it, and simply state, for example, “this follows
from the semifinal on U”.
Note that Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3 imply that, given a countable G-set V , there is a G-structure A ∈D with domain
V if and only if condition (i) of Lemma 3.2 and condition (i) of Lemma 3.3 both hold.

Lemma 3.4. Let G be a countable group, and consider G as a G-set via the left-multiplicative action. Then
there is a G-structure A ∈D with domain G if and only if G does not have a subgroup isomorphic to C2 × C2
or C4.
Proof. ⇒: Let H ≤G, |H| = 4. By Lemma 3.3, the G-set G satisfies condition (i) of the lemma, and so as the
setwise-stabiliser of H contains H itself, we have H ̸∼= C2 × C2 and H ̸∼= C4.
⇐: As observed immediately before the statement of the current lemma, it suffices to show that condition (i) of
Lemma 3.2 and condition (i) of Lemma 3.3 hold. We first show condition (i) of Lemma 3.2. Let g, h ∈G \ {1},
g ̸= h. Consider the 3-set {1, g, h}: if there are elements of G swapping each pair of elements, then we have
g2 = 1, h2 = 1 and gh = hg, so {1, g, h, gh} ∼= C2 × C2, contradiction. So condition (i) of Lemma 3.2 holds for
{1, g, h}, and thus by translation for each 3-set. Let U ∈[G]4. For g ∈G, if g fixes a point of U then g = 1, so
each element of G{U} \ G(U) acts as a 4-cycle or a double transposition on U: as G does not contain a subgroup
isomorphic to C4 or C2 × C2, each element of G{U} \ G(U) must act on U as the same double transposition, so
we have condition (i) of Lemma 3.3.
□
4. The main result
4.1. Failure of group-extensibility.
Proposition 4.1. D is not group-extensible.
Proof. This is similar to the proof of [KSW25, Lemma 3.21]. Let A ∈D with domain {a, b, c} and oriented
edges (a, b), (a, c) (note that as |A| = 3, there is no semifinal on A). Let f : A →D be an embedding. By
the extension property, there is v ∈D with oriented edges (v, f(b)), (f(c), v) and no edge between v and f(a).
(There is also necessarily a semifinal on {v, f(a), f(b), f(c)}, but it will not play a role in the argument.) Suppose
for a contradiction that f is group-extensive. Then the involution of f(A) swapping f(b), f(c) and fixing f(a)
extends to an involution τ ∈Aut(D). As v is oriented oppositely to f(b) and f(c), we have τ(v) ̸= v. But then
{f(a), v, τ(v)} has no oriented edge, contradiction.
□
Remark 4.2. Let M be the generic I3-free oriented graph (the Fra¨ıss´e limit of the class of finite I3-free oriented
graphs). The same argument as in the preceding proof shows that Ageω(M) is not group-extensible. In fact
Aut(M) is not universal: in the proof of [KSW25, Proposition 3.23], it is shown that Aut(M) does not contain
a pair of commuting involutions, and as there is an element of Age(M) with automorphism group C2 × C2,
this gives the non-universality of Aut(M). In the case of the structure D of the present paper, the proof of
[KSW25, Proposition 3.23] also shows that Aut(D) does not contain a pair of commuting involutions. But it is
not difficult to see from Lemma 3.3 (which relies on the semifinal structure) that, for each A ∈D, the group
Aut(A) does not have a subgroup ∼= C2 × C2. We add the semifinal relation S specifically to prevent failure of
universality.
4.2. Universality of Aut(D).
4.2.1. Overview of the construction. Let A ∈D and let G = Aut(A). We produce a group embedding G →
Aut(D) by constructing a faithful G-structure M ∼= D.
We build M inductively as the union of a chain
M0 ⊆M1 ⊆· · · : given Mi−1 ∈D, a faithful G-structure Mi−1 and a one-point extension C →E, where
C ⊆fin Mi−1, E ∈D, we extend Mi−1 (and the action) to a faithful G-structure Mi ∈D containing a realisation
of C →E. Via a standard bookkeeping argument, we ensure for all i < ω that each one-point extension over a
finite substructure of Mi has a realisation in some Mj, j > i, which shows that M = S
i<ω Mi has the extension
property for D; thus M ∼= D.
The key idea of the construction is the observation that there are faithful actions G ↷V where, for each v ∈V ,
the point-stabiliser of v is equal to the pointwise-stabiliser of a finite subset of A of size ≥4 in the permutation
action ρA. We say that such actions are A-nice. For example, the coordinate-wise action G ↷(A)n induced
by ρA is A-nice for each n ≥4. We build the Mi using this: we define L-structures Mi on A-nice G-sets V ,
ensuring that Mi ∈D and that the action is via automorphisms. The fact that each point-stabiliser in V is
equal to the pointwise-stabiliser of some A′ ⊆fin A, |A′| = 4, gives us “enough information from the structure A”
to build the L-structure Mi on the G-set V (bearing in mind that the semifinal relation S is 4-ary): using the
fact that the oriented graph relation RA of A is I3-free and (dom(A), SA) is a semifinal structure, we construct
Mi such that (dom(Mi), RMi) is an I3-free oriented graph and (dom(Mi), SMi) is a semifinal structure.

Lemma 4.5. Let V be an A-nice G-set, and let U ∈[V ]4. Then there is an enumeration v0, v1, v2, v3 of U such
that the action of each element of G{U} \ G(U) on U is the double transposition (v0v2)(v1v3).
Proof. Let g ∈G{U} \ G(U). Take distinct t, u ∈U with gt = u.
First consider the case where there are distinct v, w ∈U \ {t, u} such that g acts on U as (tuv)(w). Take
a ∈bt with ga ̸= a. As g3t = t and V is A-nice, we have g3a = a. If g2a = a then ga = a, contradiction. So
a, ga, g2a are distinct, and g setwise-stabilises {a, ga, g2a}. As gw = w, for each b ∈bw we have gb = b, and so a
fortiori there is b ∈bw with b /∈{a, ga, g2a}. So g acts on the 4-set {a, ga, g2a, b} ⊆A as (a ga g2a)(b), giving a
contradiction as A has a semifinal structure.
Now consider the case where there are distinct v, w ∈U \{t, u} such that g acts on U as (tuvw). As t ̸= v, there
is a ∈bt with g2a ̸= a. Then a, ga, g2a are distinct. If g3a ∈{a, ga, g2a} we obtain a contradiction as g4a = a,

element of G{U} \ G(U) acts as a double transposition on U, and it remains to check that each g′ ∈G{U} \ G(U)

Let g′ ∈G{U} \ G(U). Suppose for a contradiction that g′ does not act on U as (tu)(vw). By relabelling v, w

as (b gb)(g′b gg′b), so g′gb = b, contradiction. So the only remaining case is where g moves each of b, g′b and
gb = g′b.
As g′gt = w and w ̸= t, there is c ∈bt with g′gc ̸= c. By an analogous argument to that in the previous

Proposition 4.10. The structure D has a universal automorphism group.
Proof. Let A ∈D. We show that Aut(A) embeds in Aut(D).
First consider the case where |A| ≥4. Let W be an A-nice G-set given by Lemma 4.7 (we may take any
A′ ⊆fin A, |A′| ≥4). Let M0 be the L-structure with domain W and with RM0, SM0 given by Lemma 4.4

union of the G-actions on each Mi. As each Mi is an A-nice G-structure, we have that G ↷M is faithful,
and so we obtain an embedding Aut(A) →Aut(M). As M ∼= D, there is an embedding Aut(A) →Aut(D) as
required.
It remains to consider the case |A| < 4. For A ∈D, |A| < 4, we have Aut(A) ∼= 1, C2 or C3 (an easy check), and
so it suffices to show that C6 embeds in Aut(D). Let G = C6. By Lemma 3.4, there is a faithful G-structure
A ∈D with domain G. As |A| = 6, by the prior paragraph there is an embedding Aut(A) →Aut(D), and as A is
a faithful G-structure, there is an embedding G →Aut(A); composition gives an embedding G →Aut(D).
