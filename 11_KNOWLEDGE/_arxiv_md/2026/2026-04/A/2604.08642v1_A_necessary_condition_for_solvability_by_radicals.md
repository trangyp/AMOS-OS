A necessary condition for solvability by
radicals
Askold Khovanskii
April 13, 2026
Abstract
This note was prepared as a handout for the MAT401 course “Poly-
nomial equations and fields”, taught at the University of Toronto in
Spring 2026. It presents a proof of a necessary condition for the solv-
ability of algebraic equations by radicals, based on Galois theory. We
begin with a brief overview of the relevant basic results from Galois
theory, as covered in MAT401, and use – without proof – several stan-
dard (and relatively simple) results from the course textbook [1].
The sufficient condition for solvability by radicals, which is based
on linear algebra, we will present in the next handout.
1
Some basic theorems of Galois theory
Our presentation of a part of the Galois theory in MAT401 is based on the
Degree formula (see [1], Lemma 49, page 53) and on the following definitions
and theorems:
Definition 1.1. A finite field extension K ⊂E is normal if E is a splitting
field over K of a separable polynomial P ∈K[x].
Definition 1.2. A group G(E, K) of all automorphisms of E, which fix
pointwise all elements of the field K, is called the Galois group of the normal
extension K ⊂E.
Theorem 1.3. The Galois group G of a normal field extension K ⊂E
contains #(G) = [E : K] elements.
Proof of Theorem 1.3 can be found in our textbook [1] (see Theorem 56,
page 60). Theorem 56 is a direct corollary of Theorem 51 (page 56).

Theorem 1.4. Let E be the splitting field over K of a separable polynomial
P ∈K[x], and let σ : K →K be an automorphism that fixes all coefficients
of the polynomial P. Then σ can be lifted to an automorphism ˆσ : E →E.
Theorem 1.4 also is a direct corollary of Theorem 51 (page 56) of our
textbook [1].
2
Immediate corollaries of basic theorems
Let us list immediate corollaries of the Degree formula and Theorems 1.3, 1.4.
Lemma 2.1. Let B be an intermediate field of a normal field extension
K ⊂E, i.e., the inclusions K ⊂B ⊂F hold. Then the field extension
B ⊂E is normal.
Proof. If E is the splitting field of a separable polynomial P(x) ∈K[x] over
K and B is an intermediate field, K ⊂B ⊂E, then E is the splitting field
of the same separable polynomial P(x) over B.
Corollary 2.2. The Galois group G(E, K) of a normal field extension K ⊂
E fixes only elements of the field K.
Proof. Let B ⊂E be a field of all pointwise fixed elements under the action
of the group G(E, K) on the field E. By Lemma 2.1 and by Theorem 1.3,
we have [E : B] = #G. By the same Theorem 1.3, we have [E : K] = #G.
By the Degree formula, the following identity holds [E : B][B : K] = [E : K]
which implies that [B : K] = 1. It implies that B = K.
Definition 2.3. Let B be an intermediate field of a normal field extension
K ⊂E. We denote by GB(E, K) a subgroup of the Galois group G(E, K)
consisting of all elements of the group G(E, K) which fixes pointwise all
elements of the field B.
Corollary 2.4. The group GB(E, K) is naturally isomorphic to the Galois
group G(E, B).
Proof. Indeed, each homomorphism g : E →E from the Galois group
G(E, K) is a lifting to the extension E of the identity map from K to K.
It belongs to the group GB(E, K) if and only if it fixes all elements of the
field B. Such element g is a lifting of the identity isomorphism of the field
B to the extension E.

Corollary 2.5. Let B be an intermediate field of a normal field extension
K ⊂E. Assume that the extension K ⊂B is normal, i.e., the field B is
the splitting field of some separable polynomial P ∈K[x]. Then the group
GB(E, K) is a normal subgroup of the Galois group G(E, K), and the fac-
tor group G(E, K)/GB(E, K) is naturally isomorphic to the Galois group
G(B, K).
Proof. Since B ⊂E is the splitting field of a polynomial over K, any
automorphism g ∈G(E, K) maps B to itself.
Restriction of homomor-
phisms g ∈G(E, K) to the intermediate field B provides a homomorphism
π : G(E, K) →G(B, K) of the Galois group G(E, K) to the Galois group
G(B, K). The group GB(E, K) is the kernel of the homomorphism π. Thus,
GB(E, K) is a normal subgroup of the Galois group G(E, K). The homomor-
phism π is surjective. Indeed, by Theorem 1.4, any isomorphism σ : B →B,
which belongs to the Galois group G(B, K), can be lifted to the isomorphism
of the field E over the field K, i.e., the isomorphism σ belongs to the image
of the group G(E, K) under the homomorphism π. Thus, the factor group
G(E, K)/G(E, B) is isomorphic to the Galois group G(B, K).

Another basic theorem of Galois theory
Let E be a splitting field of a separable polynomial over a field K. Since
the extension K ⊂E has finite degree, any element a ∈E is algebraic over
K. The Galois group G(E, K) provides an explicit formula for the minimal
polynomial of an element a ∈E over K.
Definition 3.1. An orbit O(a) of an element a ∈E under the action of the
Galois group G(E, K) is the set of elements ωi ∈E representable in the form
ωi = gi(a), where gi is an element of the group G(E, K). For an element
a ∈E we define a monic polynomial Qa(x) by the following formula:

4
Needed results of Galois theory. Summary
Let us summarize the above results of Galois theory which are needed for
proving the necessary condition of solvability of equations by radicals.
1. For every normal field extension K ⊂E its Galois group G(E, K) is
defined.
2. For every intermediate field K ⊂B ⊂E of the normal field ex-
tension K ⊂E the extension B ⊂E is normal.
Its Galois group
G(EB) is naturally isomorphic to the subgroup GB(E, K) of the Ga-
lois group G(E, K) which contains all isomorphisms g ∈G(E, K) such
that g(b) = b for every element b of intermediate field B.
3. Different intermediate fields B correspond to different subgroups GB(E, K):
the subgroup GB(E, K) fixes only the elements of the intermediate
field B.
4. If an intermediate field B is a splitting field of a separable polynomial
over K, then the group GB(E, B) (which is isomorphic to the Galois
group G(E, B)) is a normal subgroup in the Galois G(E, K).
The
factor group G(E, K)/GB(E, K) is isomorphic to the Galois group
G(B, K).
5. Moreover, Galois group G(E, K) allows to provide an explicit formula
for the minimal polynomial Qa(x) over K of any element a ∈E,
see (1).

Galois groups of radical field extensions
Lemma 8.1. Consider the ring Z/(n) of integers modulo n for some n > 1.
The group of all automorphisms of the additive group Zn of this ring is
isomorphic to its multiplicative group U(n).

Proof. An automorphism g of the group Zn sends the generator (1 mod n)
of the group to another its generator. Thus, g(1) ≡m mod n, where m is
relatively prime number with n, which could be considered as an element
of the group U(n). Since g is an automorphism of Zn, we have g(k) ≡mk
mod n. Thus, g acts on the group Zn by multiplication on an invertible
element m. On the other hand, the multiplication on any invertible element
m provides an automorphism of the group Zn.
Lemma 8.2. Let E be a splitting field over a ground field K of a polynomial
xn −1, where n > 1 is a natural number. Then the Galois group G(E, K)
is isomorphic to a subgroup of U(n). In particular, the group G(E, K) is
commutative.
Proof. Roots of the polynomial xn −1 in the splitting field E form a mul-
tiplicative group which is isomorphic to the additive cyclic group Zn. Each
element of the Galois group G(E, K) provides an automorphism of the group
Zn. Thus, we have a natural homomorphism π : G(E, K) →U(n). The ho-
momorphism π has no kernel since roots of the polynomial xn −1 generate
the field E over the field K. Thus, the group G(E, K) is isomorphic to a sub-
group of the group U(n)(which is equal to the image of the group G(E, K)
under the homomorphism π);
Lemma 8.3. Let E be a splitting field over a ground field K of a polynomial
xn −a, where n > 1 is a natural number and a ∈K.
Assume that K
contains all roots of unity of degree n. Then the Galois group G(E, K) is
isomorphic to a subgroup of the cyclic additive group Zn. In particular, the
group G(E, K) is commutative.
Proof. Let x0 be a root of the equation xn−a = 0. Each root of this equation
can be represented as x0ωk, where ω is a generator of the multiplicative
group of roots of unity of degree n, and the integer k is defined up to
addition of a number divisible by n. Thus, one can identify the set of roots
of the polynomial xn −a with the set of integral numbers modulo n.
If an element g ∈G(E, K) sends the root x0 to a root x0ωm, then it sends
a root x0ωk to the root x0ωkωm = x0ωm+k for every integer k. Indeed, by
the assumption, ω ∈K, so we have g(ω) = ω and g(ωk) = ωk. Thus, under
the identification of the set of roots of the polynomial xn −a with integers
modulo n, the action of the element g is reduced to the addition of the
element (m mod n) to elements k ∈Zn. We obtain a map from the Galois
group G(E, K) to the group Zn which sends an element g to an element m.
One can see that this map is a group homomorphism. This homomorphism

Necessary condition for solvability by radicals
Theorem 9.1. If a polynomial equation P(x) = 0 over a field K of char-
acteristic zero is solvable by radicals, and E is the splitting field of the poly-
nomial P, then the Galois group G(EK) is solvable.
Proof. If the equation P = 0 is solvable by radicals, then there is a chain of
radical extension K = R0 ⊂· · · ⊂Rn such that E ⊂Rn. By Theorem 7.2,
there exists a normalization K = E0 ⊂Ku = E1 ⊂E2 ⊂. . . ⊂En+1 of the
chain of radical extensions for which the inclusions En+1 ⊃Rn ⊃E hold.
By Lemma 8.2, the Galois group G(Ku, K) is commutative. By Lemma 8.4,
the Galois groups G(E2, E1), . . . , G(En+1En) are commutative. Thus, by
Corollary 5.4, the Galois group D = G(En+1, K) is solvable. The splitting
field E of the polynomial P(x) is a normal intermediate field the extension
