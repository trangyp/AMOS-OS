A NEUKIRCH–UCHIDA THEOREM FOR 3-MANIFOLDS
NADAV GROPPER, JUN UEKI, AND YI WANG
Abstract. The classical Neukirch–Uchida theorem states that the absolute Galois group deter-
mines a number field up to isomorphism. We prove an analogue of this theorem for 3-manifolds
in the framework of arithmetic topology. We study infinite links in 3-manifolds that behave like
the set of primes, satisfying a Chebotarev density property. Relative to such a stably Chebotarev
link L, we define the absolute Galois group of a 3-manifold as the inverse limit of profinite com-
pletions of finite sublink complements. Our main result shows that two branched covers of S3 over
L are homeomorphic if and only if their absolute Galois groups relative to L are isomorphic via a
characteristic-preserving isomorphism. The proof translates the key ideas from the number-theoretic
argument into topology, relying on Hilbert ramification theory for infinite covers and local-global
principles. In doing so, it also provides a systematic justification for viewing Chebotarev links as
the precise topological analogue of prime numbers in anabelian geometry. In addition, we discuss
further conditions for links to play the role of prime numbers.

1. Introduction
The main goal of this paper is to import tools from anabelian geometry to the study of 3-manifolds
in the spirit of arithmetic topology. In particular, we develop a topological analogue of the Neukirch–
Uchida theorem, which will give a rigidity result for a 3-manifold with respect to a profinite group.

1.1. Neukirch–Uchida and Mostow rigidity. In number theory, the Neukirch–Uchida theorem
is a foundational result in anabelian geometry, and is a sort of rigidity result for number fields with
respect to their ´etale fundamental groups. Let K denote an algebraic closure of a given number field
K. Recall that the absolute Galois group Gal(K|K) is a profinite group. Given two number fields
K1, K2 and some isomorphism α : K1 →K2 for which α(K1) = K2, we get an induced isomorphism
α∗: Gal(K1/K1) →Gal(K2/K2) defined by
(1.1)
α∗(g1)(x2) = α(g1(α−1(x2)))
g1 ∈Gal(K1/K1), x2 ∈K2.
The Neukirch–Uchida theorem asserts the converse:
Theorem 1.1 (The Neukirch–Uchida theorem [23, 24, 35]). Let K1, K2 be number fields, and let
σ : Gal(K1/K1) →Gal(K2/K2) be an isomorphism of profinite groups. Then there exists a unique
isomorphism α : (K1, K1) →(K2, K2) inducing σ, i.e., σ = α∗.
Arithmetic topology, which originated from observations of Mazur [14], later expanded on in [15],
pursues analogies between the behavior of knots in 3-manifolds and primes in number fields. For
a comprehensive overview on arithmetic topology, see Morishita [20].

The Neukirch–Uchida theorem is said to be an analogue of the following:
Theorem 1.2 (Mostow’s rigidity theorem [21]). Let M, N be two finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds
such that π1(M) ∼= π1(N). Then M and N are homeomorphic.
There are two main differences between the Mostow rigidity theorem and the Neukirch–Uchida theo-
rem, which we will address in this paper:
• For a finite set of primes S, Spec(ZS), where ZS = Z[1/p | p ∈S] is analogous to a hyperbolic
3-manifold with finitely many torus boundaries. To represent the spectrum of a number field,
one needs to invert the set of all primes S (i.e. an infinite set); for instance, Q can be viewed
as ZS where S is the set of all prime integers. In the Mostow rigidity theorem, the hyperbolic
manifolds have finitely many cusps, while a faithful analogue of the Neukirch–Uchida theorem
should deal with infinitely many knots.
• Galois groups are always profinite groups, as opposed to the discrete fundamental groups in
Mostow rigidity. A rigidity result for π´et
1 (Spec(ZS)) would be a rigidity result of 3-manifolds
with respect to a profinite group.

1.2. Main results. One major question of arithmetic topology is the question of which sets of knots
are analogous to the set of primes in number fields, especially with dealing with countable sets. Mazur
[16] further proposed an analogue of the Chebotarev density theorem for links with countably infinite
components, which was proven by McMullen [17]. Ueki [40] later defined stably Chebotarev links,
which are Chebotarev links for which any preimage link in a branched cover satisfies the Chebotarev
property; examples of stably Chebotarev links can be found in [17] and [40].
We fix throughout a stably Chebotarev link L in S3, together with a fixed ordering of L.

the induced ordering (see Section 2.3). We consider the category Cov(M, L) denote the set of all
finite branched covers with base points branched along finite sublinks of L. (See Section 2 for more
detailed information.) This forms a Galois category, and we define the absolute Galois group to be
the fundamental group of this category, which is pro-represented by a universal pro-cover S3
L →S3.

Theorem A (Neukirch–Uchida theorem for 3-manifolds). Let L ⊂S3 be a stably Chebotarev link.
Let hi : Mi →S3 ∈Cov(S3, L), i = 1, 2 be two coverings of S3 branched over finite sublinks Li ⊂L.

(1) Any isomorphism σ : Gal(M1, LM1) →Gal(M2, LM2) induces a bijection σ∗: LM1 →LM2.
(2) For any isomorphism which preserves characteristic, there exists a unique α ∈Gal(S3, L)
giving an isomorphism of covers α : M1 →M2, which induces σ.

the link ordering, we obtain the following:
Corollary B. Let L ⊂S3 be a stably Chebotarev link, and let hi : Mi →S3 ∈Cov(S3, L), i = 1, 2. If
α : M1 →M2 is a isomorphism of covers over S3, then the induced isomorphism α∗: Gal(M1, LM1) →
Gal(M2, LM2) preserves characteristic, and α is the unique element of Gal(S3, L) inducing α∗.

1.3. Relation to other works.
1.3.1. Arithmetic Topology. In [18], [40], versions of the idele class group have been studied (from
the cohomological and homological points of view, respectively) to establish a class field theory for
3-manifolds. In number-theoretic setups of local-global principles and Poitou–Tate duality, the idele
class group is a fundamental object. We expect the framework of these works to interact naturally
with the notions introduced in this paper.
Another direction is the analogue of Iwasawa theory for 3-manifolds [9] [38] [39]. In our paper, we
consider general pro-covers, while Iwasawa theory considers specific towers of Z/prZ-covers (a Zp
pro-cover) over certain links.

1.3.2. Profinite rigidity. We say that groups are profinitely rigid if they are distinguished by their
profinite completions among certain families of residually finite groups. Profinite rigidity, particularly
when restricting to the class of fundamental groups of low-dimensional orbifolds, is a central topic of
research in group theory and topology; see [31] for a survey on this topic. Since the Galois groups
developed in this paper can be expressed as an inverse limit of profinite completions of 3-manifolds
with toral boundary, the results in this paper have a natural relationship with profinite rigidity
of 3-manifold groups. In this paper, we provide a profinite group invariant which can distinguish 3-
manifolds as branched covers of S3. Although the invariant provided comes from profinite completions
of fundamental groups, the extra price paid is to allowing ramification at a countable collection of
knots.
1.3.3. Anabelian geometry. Anabelian geometry studies how much information can be recovered about
geometry and arithmetic from the Galois theory and the ´etale fundamental group. The field is based
on Grothendieck’s anabelian conjectures for curves [4].
The term anabelian comes from ”beyond
abelian” or “far from abelian”, since one needs the fundamental groups to be ”far enough from being
abelian” to be able to have enough information on the geometry. The Neukirch–Uchida theorem serves
as the foundational anabelian result dealing with number fields, i.e. arithmetic objects of dimension
0. We wish to highlight a few central theorems in anabelian geometry, and explain what the analogue
statement for manifold should be based on the current arithmetic topology dictionary. For information
on further themes and topics in anabelian geometry we refer to the surveys [30], [22].
We mention a few anabelian results for number fields that generalise and refine the Neukirch–Uchida
theorem. In [11] and [33], a refinement of the Neukirch–Uchida theorem was given, where instead of
working with the set of all primes, sets S with positive Dirichlet density (along with some other mild
conditions) are considered. These works were a big part of the inspiration of this work, and led us to
believe that stably Chebotarev links could satisfy a Neukirch–Uchida theorem. In particular, we wish
to draw attention to the characteristic preserving condition in [33] (which he refers to as “good”),
which led us to consider the characteristic preserving in our setup. Another work which led us to focus
on characteristic preserving isomorphisms is [10], which deals with whether homomorphisms between
the multiplicative group of fields K× arise from field isomorphisms, via a characteristic compatible
criteria.
We also wish to mention a recent preprint [12] which proves a tame Neukirch–Uchida theorem. In
this version, the decomposition groups considered are more analogous to those in our paper. Since it
is recent, the precise analogues of the ideas there are not fully explored in this paper, though they did
inspire some of the discussions in Section 9.

approach was tremendously improved in the work of Tamagawa [34], where an anabelian result for
affine curve X over finite fields was given. This was done by identifying the decomposition groups of
the points of X, but now from the much simpler group π´et

9. Further questions on links behaving like primes
9.1. Characteristic-preserving isomorphisms. We highlight several differences between links and
primes within the arithmetic topology dictionary which remain unaddressed. Further study of these
phenomena would deepen the connection between links and primes.
(1) Prime numbers are intrinsically different to each other in size; group-theoretically, one can
distinguish them via the maximal Galois group ramified outside p. Although this is similar
how prime knots are determined by their fundamental groups. In stably Chebotarev links, we
do not require the knot components are prime knots or distinct; in the planetary link of the
figure-eight knot, all topological link types exist [2].
(2) When distinguishing decomposition groups in number fields, an important tool is decomposing
decomposition groups into pro-p components.
This is clear in the wild inertia group, but
recently [12] it has worked when considering tame inertia. This comes from the fact that an
abelian profinite group has an intrinsic bZ-module structure, which is equipped with an action
of all distinct primes p. So Z and primes play two different roles. On the other hand, absolute
decomposition groups of knots do not possess this extra module structure; the extra role of Z
is not seen.
(3) Certain knots like the figure-eight knot can be universal, meaning that any closed orientable
3-manifold can be realized as a branched cover over that knot [8]. In addition, every closed
orientable 3-manifold can be a 3-fold branched covering space over a link in S3 [7].
The
analogous statements in number theory are false, indicating that branched coverings possess
less intrinsic rigidity than the number field extensions.
The above three observations are all reflections of the fact that within the arithmetic topology frame-
work used in this paper, the topology of knots alone lacks the rigidity of primes in number fields.
These phenomena indicate a need for some additional structure beyond pure topology to force rigid-
ity. The characteristic-preserving hypothesis of Theorem A is the precise additional structure needed
to supply the missing rigidity at the level of profinite group isomorphisms.
The extra structure required for full rigidity may also be provided by geometry, particularly hyperbolic
geometry, as speculated by Mazur [16]. Hyperbolic 3-manifold groups are conjectured to be profinitely
rigid [31]. Recently, Xu [44] prove that peripheral subgroups of hyperbolic 3-manifolds are detected by
their profinite completions, indicating that our absolute decomposition groups may be distinguished
when Chebotarev links are “sufficiently hyperbolic”.
