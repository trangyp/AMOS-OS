A BOCHNER-TYPE INTEGRATION THEORY
FOR RANDOM NORMED MODULES
ANDREA KUBIN AND ENRICO PASQUALETTO
Abstract. We develop a measure and integration theory for random normed modules.
Given a probability space (X, Σ, m), we introduce and study measures taking values into
the space L0(m) of m-measurable functions quotiented up to m-a.e. equality. Moreover, we
develop a Bochner-type integration theory with respect to an L0(m)-valued measure µ, for
maps whose target M is a complete random normed module with base (X, Σ, m), or equiva-
lently an L0(m)-Banach L0(m)-module. Inter alia, we prove versions of the Radon–Nikod´ym
theorem and of the Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem for L0(m)-valued mea-
sures. We also outline several applications of our integration theory: we introduce a notion
of martingale with values in a complete random normed module, we propose a definition of
random Radon–Nikod´ym property and we discuss random sets of finite perimeter.

It is worth pointing out that the theory of random normed modules is in fact an extension
of the Banach space theory: if X is a one-point space {o} and m is the Dirac delta δo, then
L0(δo) can be canonically identified with the real line R, and thus the complete random normed
modules with base ({o}, {∅, {o}}, δo) can be identified with the Banach spaces (exactly with
the same algebraic and metric structures). Let us also highlight that the structure of L0(m)
is typically more complicated than that of R, and this affects the kinds of results for random-
normed-module-valued measures and integrals that one might expect to hold, as well as their
proofs. For example, the fact that L0(m) – differently from R – is not totally ordered is
responsible for the lack of any Hahn-type decomposition for L0-valued measures, as we will
discuss in Section 3.1. Due to this and other reasons, it was not even clear how to formulate
some of the generalisations of classical results for Banach spaces to the random normed
module setting, e.g. which are the assumptions in the random Radon–Nikod´ym Theorem 4.20,
or what is the natural candidate for an isometric predual of the space of Radon L0-valued
measures over a compact Hausdorff space in the random Riesz–Markov–Kakutani Theorem
5.20. Moreover, some of the proofs will require new ideas and techniques if compared to their
Banach space counterparts, as we will see throughout the paper.
1.2. About random normed modules. Random normed modules, which lie at the core
of the so-called random functional analysis, were developed by Guo in [21, 22, 23] starting
from ideas that date back to the study of probabilistic metric spaces initiated by Menger,
Schweizer and Sklar (see [62]). A central object of study in the theory of random normed
modules is the random conjugate space, which was studied e.g. in [25, 39, 27, 33, 28, 40]. The

larger class of random locally convex modules was then introduced by Guo in [26] and studied
e.g. in [31, 41]. In recent years, one of the main topics of research about random normed and
locally convex modules is the random fixed point theory, which has been investigated e.g. in
the works [38, 42, 65, 66, 64, 37]. See also [35] for a survey on random fixed point theory.
Later and independently, Gigli developed in [19] (see also [18]) the theory of L0(m)-Banach
L0(m)-modules, taking inspiration from Weaver’s work [67], with the aim of building a non-
smooth differential structure for metric measure spaces. A couple of words on terminology:
in [19, 18] the term ‘L0(m)-normed L0(m)-module’ was used instead, but here (as well as in
some previous papers) we prefer to highlight that we assume completeness. L0(m)-Banach
L0(m)-modules provide a robust framework for defining different measurable tensor fields,
such as 1-forms and vector fields, in the nonsmooth setting.
Although complete random
normed modules and L0(m)-Banach L0(m)-modules are fully equivalent concepts, the latter
have a somewhat different interpretation, coming from differential geometry: their elements
are thought of as the measurable sections of some measurable bundle whose fibers are Banach
spaces.
The representation of L0(m)-Banach L0(m)-modules as section spaces of (various
kinds of) measurable Banach bundles was studied in [52, 13, 20], and the relation between
measurable Banach bundles and their section spaces was investigated further in [54, 9]. The
class of L0(m)-Banach L0(m)-modules was examined from a categorical perspective in [57].
Furthermore, other strictly related notions have been studied in the literature. For instance:
• The notion of randomly normed L0-module, which is consistent with Guo’s theory of
random normed modules, was developed by Haydon, Levy and Raynaud in [46] as a
tool for studying ultraproducts of Lebesgue–Bochner spaces.
• Filipovi´c, Kupper and Vogelpoth [16] introduced the locally L0-convex modules in [16],
as a framework for studying conditional convex risk measures. The locally L0-convex
topology on a random locally convex module was investigated in [68, 70], and its
relation with the topology usually considered on them – the so-called (ε, λ)-topology
– was then understood in [29]. These two topologies and their mutual connections
have been widely used in mathematical finance (see e.g. [30, 43, 44, 32]).
• In the works [19, 18] the focus was on Lp(m)-Banach L∞(m)-modules of exponent
p ∈[1, ∞], which are normed modules that satisfy an integrability condition. Similar
structures have been considered in the framework of Dirichlet forms (see e.g. [50, 48]).
• In [10] the second named author – together with Debin and Gigli – introduced and
studied the class of L0(Cap)-Banach L0(Cap)-modules, where Cap denotes the Sobolev
2-capacity (which is an outer measure, but not a σ-additive measure) over a given
metric measure space.
This specific variant of normed module turned out to be
of fundamental importance in understanding the fine differential structure of RCD
spaces, which are metric measure spaces with synthetic lower Ricci curvature and
upper dimension bounds; see e.g. [8, 6].
• Normed A-modules in the sense of [11, 12], where A is a suitable f-algebra, were
studied in connection with mathematical models in finance.

The random Riesz–Markov–Kakutani theorem. A considerable part of this paper – namely, the
whole Section 5 – is devoted to the study of the random Riesz–Markov–Kakutani theorem,
which consists of the identification of a random normed module whose random conjugate
space is isometrically isomorphic to the space M(Ω; L0(m)) of all Radon L0-valued measures
over a given compact Hausdorff space (Ω, τ). The classical Riesz–Markov–Kakutani theorem
states that the Banach space dual of C(Ω) is the Banach space of real-valued Radon measures
M(Ω). In the more general context of random normed modules, there are several different
random normed modules that can be regarded as generalisations of the space C(Ω) (see [56,
Section 3.4]), thus it was not a priori clear which could be a good candidate for a predual
of M(Ω; L0(m)). We have identified in the random normed module UCord(Ω; L0(m)) of all
uniformly-order-continuous maps f : Ω→L0(m) (see Definition 5.1) an isometric predual of
the space M(Ω; L0(m)). Namely, we will prove in Theorem 5.20 that
UCord(Ω; L0(m))∗and M(Ω; L0(m)) are isometrically isomorphic
for every compact Hausdorff space (Ω, τ). In Section 5.1 we study the space UCord(Ω; M),
previously introduced by the second named author in [56, Definition 3.13], in its own right.
Random normed modules are equipped with two distinguished uniform structures: the one
associated to the (ε, λ)-topology Tε,λ introduced by Guo [21] and considered also by Gigli [19],
and the locally L0-convex topology Tc introduced by Filipovi´c, Kupper and Vogelpoth [16]; see
Remark 5.2. Nevertheless, we show that – interestingly – the maps f : Ω→L0(m) belonging
to UCord(Ω; L0(m)) are (in general) neither those that are uniformly continuous with respect
to the (ε, λ)-uniformity (Example 5.4) nor those that are uniformly continuous with respect
to the locally L0-convex uniformity (Example 5.5). In fact, it is not even clear whether the
uniform order continuity is the uniform continuity with respect to some uniformity on L0(m).

In Section 5.2 we collect many auxiliary technical statements, while in Section 5.3 we
provide two different proofs of the random Riesz–Markov–Kakutani theorem. In the particular
case where (Ω, d) is a compact metric space, we first present an argument that relies on the
von Neumann theory of liftings, where we essentially reduce the proof to the classical Riesz–
Markov–Kakutani theorem for Banach spaces. The same arguments also allow us to show
that every Radon L0-valued measure on (Ω, d) can be foliated (Corollary 5.22), along with
an alternative, ‘pointwise’ characterisation of M(Ω; L0(m)) (Theorem 5.23). We then present
another proof for the general case where (Ω, τ) is a compact Hausdorff space. Shortly said, this
second argument is based on a reduction to the case where (Ω, τ) is extremally disconnected,
which can be more easily established. This technique is adapted from Hartig’s paper [45],
where the argument is attributed to Garling.
Applications. In Section 7 we set-up some potential applications of our measure and integra-
tion theory for random normed modules. More specifically:
• In Section 7.1 we introduce random-normed-module-valued martingales, see Definition
7.3. To show their well-posedness, we first need to prove the existence of conditional
expectations for maps with values in a random normed module; see Theorem 7.2.
• In Section 7.2 we propose a definition of random Radon–Nikod´ym property, see Defi-
nition 7.4. We also discuss some possible reformulations of this condition, related to
module-valued martingales and to the differentiability of random Lipschitz curves.
• In Section 7.3 we introduce the notion of random set of finite perimeter (Definition
7.6) and we discuss some of its basic properties.
1.4. Further research directions. In addition to the applications that we have outlined
above, we would like to discuss a few other potential research directions. First, it would be
interesting to compare our notions and results with others that are available in the literature,
for example with martingale-valued measures (see e.g. [3]), with the theory of local vector
measures developed by Brena and Gigli in [7], or with the integration theory for module-
valued maps developed by the second named author together with Caputo and Gigli in [10,
Section 3.3]. We expect that the last-mentioned concept of integration is consistent with the
one that we study in this paper; more precisely, the space L1([0, 1], H ) introduced in [10,
Definition 3.15(i)] – for H a separable Hilbertian random normed module H with base a
metric measure space – is likely to correspond to L1
µ([0, 1]; H ), where as µ we choose the
‘constant’ L0-valued measure induced by the Lebesgue measure (as in Remark 3.2).
Another possible research direction, which is one of the main motivations behind our newly-
developed integration theory, is to make further progress in L0-convex analysis. In the paper
[36], Guo, Wang and Tang proved a Krein–Milman theorem for random locally convex mod-
ules. A further step could be to obtain a version of the Choquet(–Bishop–de Leeuw) theorem
[58, Chapter 4] for random locally convex modules (or at least for random normed modules)
by leveraging our random Lebesgue–Bochner space machinery (or a suitable variant of it),
and to explore its consequences.

we believe that it would be very interesting to conduct an in-depth investigation of the

to understand whether the class of complete random normed modules having the random

or those that are countably generated and admits an isometric predual. Furthermore, it is
well known that the Radon–Nikod´ym property for Banach spaces can be characterised in
many different ways. To mention a few, for a Banach space B the following are equivalent:
i) B has the Radon–Nikod´ym property.

8. Open problems
In this conclusive section we collect several specific questions that are left open after this
paper. In order to develop further the theory that we have presented, it would be interesting
to prove or disprove the following statements:
1) Every L0-valued measure µ: A →L0(m) is of bounded variation.
Originally we were expecting that the above claim is true, by analogy with the
classical Banach space case: every vector measure whose target is a finite-dimensional
Banach space is of bounded variation, and L0(m) has dimension 1 as an L0(m)-module
(since it is generated by 1m
X). However, our attempts to prove the above claim failed,
due to the fact that L0-valued measures do not admit a Hahn-type decomposition.
Therefore, it could well be that the above claim is in fact false.
2) There exist L0-valued measures µ ∈M(Ω; L0(m)) that cannot be foliated.
We expect there there do exist, possibly even for µ Radon L0-valued measure over
some compact Hausdorff space (Ω, τ) that is not metrisable (since every Radon L0-
valued measure over a compact metric space can be foliated, cf. Corollary 5.22).
3) The elements of L1
