Abstract: This paper presents a high-order bound-preserving oscillation-eliminating
discontinuous Galerkin (BP-OEDG) scheme for simulating gas-gas and gas-liquid two-
phase flows governed by the Kapila five-equation model with the Tammann equation of
state (EOS). The primary computational bottleneck arises from the severe CFL restric-
tion imposed by the stiff κ-source term in the volume fraction equation. To circumvent
this, we propose a novel operator-splitting strategy that decouples the system into a
five-equation transport model and a stiff κ-source term. The former is discretized via
a quasi-conservative DG method [8], while the latter is resolved by an adaptive im-
plicit strategy hybridizing the backward Euler and second-order singly diagonally im-
plicit Runge-Kutta (SDIRK2) method. We rigorously prove that this implicit treatment
is unconditionally BP, effectively removing the stiffness-induced stability constraints in-
herent in traditional explicit schemes. To further enhance precision, a velocity divergence
reconstruction inspired by the Local Discontinuous Galerkin (LDG) method is integrated
into the implicit solver. Furthermore, an OE limiter is employed to suppress spurious os-
cillations without characteristic decomposition, complemented by a BP limiter to ensure
the BP property of partial densities, pressure, and volume fraction. Crucially, we prove
that the proposed BP-OEDG scheme, integrated with the splitting strategy, strictly
satisfies the Abgrall condition. Extensive numerical experiments, including challenging
water-air shock-bubble interactions, demonstrate the superior robustness and efficiency
of the method.

Introduction
Multiphase flow simulation is a challenging problem in fluid mechanics, with appli-
cations in various fields such as aerospace, energy, and environmental sciences. Many
models can be used to simulate two-phase flows, and most of them can be classified as
two-fluid models or as homogeneous models. Among the two-fluid models, the model of
Baer and Nunziato (BN) [3] is firstly proposed to simulate the deflagration-to-detonation
transition in reactive granular materials. The BN model includes mass, momentum, and
energy conservation laws for each phase and a volume fraction equation.
Then, the
Kapila five-equation model [15] has been derived as a reduced model under the assump-
tion that velocities and pressures across different phases are in equilibrium. Compared
to the five-equation transport model [2, 17], the Kapila five-equation model contains an
additional non-conservative κ-source term in the volume fraction equation. This addi-
tional term accounts for the different compressibility of two fluids. This term enables
the Kapila five-equation model to achieve higher fidelity than the five-equation trans-
port model in bubble dynamics simulation [21, 29]. However, its inherent stiffness poses
numerical instabilities when shock or rarefaction waves are hitting a material interface.
[20, 35, 4]. This presents a highly challenging problem for numerical schemes. To avoid
such numerical instabilities, Saurel et al. [20] proposed a six-equation model featuring
pressure relaxation. Nevertheless, the Kapila five-equation model is still widely used due
to its simplicity.
In recent years, the DG method has gained significant attention in computational fluid
dynamics due to its high-order accuracy, compact stencil, and excellent parallel scala-
bility. Consequently, its application to multiphase flows has been increasingly explored.
Zhang et al. [8] proposed a quasi-conservative DG method to solve the five-equation
transport model with the Tammann EOS. They subsequently extended this DG frame-
work to solve the Kapila five-equation model based on the ideal gas EOS [35], where the
discretization of the non-conservative source terms only achieves second-order accuracy.
To address this, Yan et al. [34] further developed a DG spatial discretization for the
Kapila model that successfully attains optimal high-order accuracy for the source terms.
Despite these advancements in spatial discretization, simulating gas-liquid two-phase
flows using the Kapila model remains a challenge because of the stiffness of the κ-source
term. For instance, at the instant a shock wave in water impacts an air bubble inter-
face, the velocity divergence at the gas-water interface reaches extremely high values. If
explicit time integration is applied to the volume fraction equation, the κ-source term
can cause the volume fraction to exceed its physical bounds under standard CFL condi-
tions. To prevent such numerical instability, the CFL condition traditionally demands
severe restrictions, often reducing the allowable time step by several orders of magnitude

[35, 32]. The DG method equipped with subcell finite volume discretization proposed by
White et al. [32], whose analysis of CFL condition for BP is similar to the theoretical
limitations analyzed by Zhang and Cheng [35]. The BP CFL condition in these methods
is heavily dominated by the stiff volume fraction equation. This inherent stiffness forces
the allowable time step to shrink by several orders of magnitude compared to the normal
CFL limitation. Simulating extreme gas-liquid cases, such as water-air shock-bubble
interaction, remains computationally prohibitive and conspicuously absent from current
DG literature. To overcome this stiffness, attempts have been made using alternative
approaches. Notable examples include the acoustic-convective splitting-based scheme by
Eikelder et al. [28] and the second-order GRP-FVM with a semi-implicit time discretiza-
tion for the κ-source term by Chen et al. [5]. However, addressing this stiffness through
high-order DG methods has yet to be explored. Therefore, developing a robust, BP DG
method for the gas-liquid Kapila five-equation model that can circumvent this extreme
time-step restriction and operate stably under a standard CFL condition represents a
highly non-trivial challenge, which forms the primary motivation of the present work.
In this paper, to circumvent the stiffness-induced instability of the κ-source term,
we propose an operator-splitting strategy in the DG framework that decomposes the
Kapila five-equation model into a five-equation transport model and a stiff κ term.
The five-equation transport model is discretized using a quasi-conservative DG method
which satisfies the Abgrall condition. Specifically, we solve the stiff κ-source term us-
ing a novel adaptive implicit strategy—a hybrid of backward Euler and second-order
SDIRK schemes that ensures the unconditional BP property. Furthermore, the velocity
divergence is reconstructed inspired by the LDG method [10], thereby restoring opti-
mal high-order precision. An OEDG framework is implemented to suppress numerical
oscillations. A key advantage of the OEDG approach is that it effectively eliminates os-
cillations without the need for characteristic decomposition, which is typically required
by traditional limiters such as WENO limiter [37] or TVD limiter [9]. This significantly
reduces the computational complexity and simplifies the implementation for complex
multiphase systems. To further ensure the BP property of critical physical variables
including partial densities, pressure, and volume fraction, a robust BP limiter is inte-
grated into the framework. We rigorously prove that this BP-OEDG scheme satisfies
the Abgrall condition (maintaining pressure and velocity equilibrium at interfaces) for
the Tammann EOS.
The paper is organized as follows. We introduce the governing equations of the Kapila
five-equation model in Section 2. Then, we present the operator-splitting DG method
in Section 3, where we describe the splitting strategy, a quasi-conservative DG method
for the five-equation transport model, and a specific implicit solver for κ-source term.
The implementation of the BP limiter and OEDG procedure are presented in Section 4,
where we prove the preservation of the Abgrall condition for the Tammann EOS. The
algorithmic summary is also presented in Section 4. In Section 5, we present 1D and 2D
benchmark cases to verify the performance of the BP-OEDG scheme combined with the
operator splitting method. In Section 6, we draw our conclusions.

Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a BP-OEDG scheme combined with operator splitting
method for solving the Kapila five-equation model to simulate compressible two-phase
flows. The operator splitting method is used to address the stiffness-induced instability
of the κ source term. An implicit solver is used in subsystem (3.23) to maintain the BP
property of the volume fraction. Additionally, a quasi-conservative DG method is used in
subsystem (3.21). The OEDG procedure and BP limiter are used to suppress numerical
oscillations and maintain BP property of the solution. The proposed framework preserves
conservation of the mass of each fluid, total momentum and total energy, meanwhile it
satisfies Abgrall condition.
While the proposed BP-OEDG scheme combined with operator splitting successfully
stabilizes two-phase flow cases in the paper and locks the splitting errors at the inter-
faces, we acknowledge that in certain extreme cases characterized by severe stiffness of
the κ source term, the operator splitting method may suffer from local order degradation
[11, 25] and fail to restore the correct physical solution. Nevertheless, for the extreme
compression dynamics targeted in this work (e.g., shock-bubble interactions), the pro-
posed method strictly preserves the boundedness of the volume fractions and maintains
robust computational stability. To ultimately overcome splitting-induced limitation, de-

veloping a fully coupled IMEX-OEDG framework remains a critical challenge and is the
focus of our ongoing research.
