AN EFFECTIVE VERSION OF THE STONE DUALITY
NIKOLAY A. BAZHENOV, ISKANDER SH. KALIMULLIN, AND MARINA V. SCHWIDEFSKY
Abstract. The paper studies computability-theoretic aspects of topological T0-spaces.
We introduce effective versions of the notions of a countable c-poset and a (second-
countable) topological space with base.
Based on this, we prove an effective version
of the known Stone-type duality between the category AS (whose objects are almost
semispectral spaces with base and whose morphisms are spectral mappings) and the
category DP (whose objects are distributive c-posets and whose morphisms are strict
mappings). Namely, we show that for an arbitrary set Z ⊆ω, this duality is preserved
when one restricts to objects which have Z-computably enumerable presentations only.
Following this approach, we establish several results in computable topology. We prove
that every degree spectrum of a countable algebraic structure can be realized as the
degree spectrum of a topological space with base. We show that for any non-zero natural
number N, there is a computable topological space with base that has precisely N-many
computable copies, up to effective spectral homeomorphisms.
Introduction
In 1937, M. H. Stone [1] established the dual equivalence of the category of distributive
lattices with 0 and 1 (distributive (0, 1)-lattices) with lattice homomorphisms preserving
the two constants, 0 and 1, ((0, 1)-lattice homomorphisms) as morphisms and the category
of spectral spaces with spectral maps as morphisms. This result extended an earlier result
of his [2] on the dual equivalence of the category of Boolean algebras with homomorphisms
and the category of Stone spaces (that is, compact totally disconnected Hausdorff spaces)
with continuous maps. Later in 1970, H. A. Priestley [3, 4] established another type of
duality (nowadays, generally referred to as the Priestley duality).
Since that time, there was a fair amount of different successful attempts to extend the
Stone duality to a wider class of partially ordered sets than distributive bounded lattices.
It is very hard to present here a complete account on the papers dedicated to this topic.
We mention here just two of them—the paper [5] by L. J. Gonz´alez and R. Jansana and
the paper [6] by S. A. Celani and L. J. Gonz´alez. In the first one, a Stone-type duality was
established for the category of all posets, while in the second one, Stone-type dualities
were found for distributive meet-semilattices and lattices (not necessarily distributive).
In [7, 8] a representation theorem for the so-called distributive c-posets was proved. In
[9, 10], this representation theorem was extended to a Stone-type duality which, in its
turn, is an extension of the Stone duality for distributive (0, 1)-lattices was made to the
category of the distributive c-posets, see Definition 1 below. It is a curious fact that the

1.2. Topological spaces with base.
For all the topological notions which we do not define here, we refer to the monograph
of Yu. L. Ershov [26] as well as to the one of G. Gierz et al. [27].
A topological T0-space X is [almost] sober if, for each closed [proper] nonempty set
F ⊆X, there is x ∈X such that F = ↓x. We note that, whenever we speak of a partial
order in a topological T0-space, we always mean the specialization order.
Definition 7. [9, 10] A triple X = ⟨X, T, B⟩is a topological space with base or just a space
with base, if the following conditions are satisfied:
(i) ⟨X, T⟩is a topological T0-space and B forms a basis of the topology T;
(ii) ∅∈B if and only if X is sober and the poset ⟨B; ⊆⟩is down-directed;
(iii) X ∈B if and only if X is compact and the poset ⟨B; ⊆⟩is up-directed.
We write T(X) for T and B(X) for B in this case.
We say that X is a [topological] space with up-directed base [down-directed base] if the
poset ⟨B; ⊆⟩is up-directed [down-directed]; X is a space with 1-base [0-base] if ⟨B; ⊆⟩has
a greatest element [a least element]. Moreover, X is a space with multiplicative base if B
is closed under finite nonempty intersections; X is a space with additive base if B is closed
under finite nonempty unions.
Definition 8. A topological space with base X is called an almost semispectral space with
base, if ⟨X, T(X)⟩is an almost sober space, and B(X) consists of open compact sets.
Definition 9. Let AS be the category whose objects are almost semispectral spaces with
base and whose morphisms are spectral mappings; that is, mappings which satisfy the
following condition:
if f : X →Y, where X, Y are almost semispectral spaces with base, then f−1(U) ∈
B(X) for each U ∈B(Y).
Lemma 10. [10, Lemma 3] Let X be an almost sober topological space with base.
(i) X is a space with 0-base if and only if ∅∈B(X). In particular, X is a space with
0-base if and only if X is a sober space with down-directed base.
(ii) X is a space with 1-base if and only if X ∈B(X). In particular, X is a space with

3.2. On degree spectra of structures.
In this subsection,
we consider only computable signatures σ.
A σ-formula

If A is a σ-structure with dom(A) ⊆ω, then by D(A) we denote the atomic diagram of
the structure A.
Let S be a countably infinite σ-structure. The degree spectrum of the structure S is the
following set of Turing degrees:
DgSp(S) = {degT (D(A)) | A ∼= S, dom(A) = ω}.
A structure S is automorphically trivial if there exists a finite set F ⊆dom(S) such
that every permutation of dom(S) that keeps the set F fixed is an automorphism of the
structure S.
Knight [31] proved that, for every automorphically nontrivial, countable
structure S, its degree spectrum DgSp(S) is closed upwards in Turing degrees. Hence, for
such a structure S we have
DgSp(S) = {d | d is a Turing degree such that
S has a d-computable isomorphic copy}.
(3.2.1)
The following result is well-known.
Proposition 23 (see, e.g., Theorem 3.2 of [32]). For every automorphically non-trivial,
countable σ-structure S, there exists a countable poset PS = (ω; ≤S) such that DgSp(PS) =
DgSp(S).

4. Computable versions of dualities II: Subcategories and morphisms
In this section, following [9, 10], we focus on some familiar subcategories of the category
DP. Subsection 4.1 discusses semilattices and lattices. In computable structure theory,
there is a well-established notion of, say, a computable join-semilattice or a computable

Similarly to Section 3, we

In Subsection 4.2, we discuss the complexity of morphisms. We introduce the notion of

spectrum equal to the set of all non-computable degrees.
