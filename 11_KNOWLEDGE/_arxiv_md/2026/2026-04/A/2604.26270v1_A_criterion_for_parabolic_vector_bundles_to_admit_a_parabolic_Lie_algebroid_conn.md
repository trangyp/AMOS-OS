A CRITERION FOR PARABOLIC VECTOR BUNDLES TO ADMIT A
PARABOLIC LIE ALGEBROID CONNECTION
DAVID ALFAYA, ASHIMA BANSAL, INDRANIL BISWAS, AND ANOOP SINGH
Abstract. Given a holomorphic Lie algebroid (V, ϕ) on a compact connected Riemann
surface X, we give a necessary and sufficient condition for a parabolic vector bundle on
X, with parabolic structure over a nonzero reduced effective divisor, to admit a parabolic
Lie algebroid connection for the Lie algebroid (V, ϕ).
1. Introduction
Let X be a compact connected Riemann surface.
A holomorphic Lie algebroid on
X is a pair of the form (V, ϕ), where V is a holomorphic vector bundle over X whose
sheaf of holomorphic sections is equipped with a structure of a complex Lie algebra and
ϕ : V −→TX is a holomorphic homomorphism of vector bundles such that
[s, f · t] = f · [s, t] + ϕ(s)(f) · t
for all locally defined holomorphic sections s, t of V and all locally defined holomorphic
functions f on X.
Fix a holomorphic Lie algebroid (V, ϕ) on X, and take any holomorphic vector bundle
E on X.
A holomorphic Lie algebroid connection on E is a holomorphic differential
operator
D : E −→E ⊗V ∗
satisfying the Leibniz identity which says that D(fs) = fD(s)+s⊗ϕ∗(df) for all locally
defined holomorphic sections s of E and all locally defined holomorphic functions f on X,
where ϕ∗is the dual of the above homomorphism ϕ (See [AO], [Al], [To1], [To2], [CM],
[ELW], [LM], [PW] for Lie algebroids and Lie algebroid connections).
Fix a nonzero reduced effective divisor S on X. Let E∗be a parabolic vector bundle on
X with parabolic structure over the divisor S. A quasi-parabolic Lie algebroid connection
on E∗for (V, ϕ) is a holomorphic Lie algebroid connection D on the holomorphic vec-
tor bundle E underlying E∗for (V, ϕ) such that D induces a holomorphic Lie algebroid
connection (for (V, ϕ)) on each of the subsheaves of E given by the quasi-parabolic filtra-
tions of E∗. A parabolic Lie algebroid connection on E∗is a quasi-parabolic Lie algebroid
connection D on E∗such that the second fundamental forms of D, for the subsheaves
of E given by the quasi-parabolic filtrations of E∗, is induced by multiplication by the
parabolic weights.

connection for (V, ϕ) was obtained: If E∗admits a parabolic Lie algebroid connection for
the (V, ϕ), then the homomorphism ϕ vanishes on every parabolic point of E∗, namely
the points of the divisor S.
Assume that ϕ vanishes on every point of the parabolic divisor S. Let
ϕ0 : V

σ : TX ⊗OX(−S) −→V
such that ϕ0 ◦σ = IdTX⊗OX(−S). Notice that σ is not required to preserve the Lie bracket

The holomorphic Lie algebroid (V, ϕ) is called logarithmically non-split if it is not
logarithmically split.
We prove the following criterion for the existence of a parabolic Lie algebroid connection
(see Corollary 6.3):
Theorem 1.1. Let (V, ϕ) be a holomorphic Lie algebroid on X satisfying the condition
that ϕ vanishes over every parabolic point x ∈S. A parabolic vector bundle E∗on X
admits a parabolic Lie algebroid connection for (V, ϕ) if and only if at least one of the
following two conditions holds:
(1) The holomorphic Lie algebroid (V, ϕ) is logarithmically non-split.
(2) The parabolic degree of every indecomposable component of E∗is zero.
In particular, we prove the following (see Corollary 5.3):
Theorem 1.2. Let (V, ϕ) be a holomorphic Lie algebroid on X satisfying the condition
that ϕ vanishes over every parabolic point x ∈S, and (V, ϕ) is logarithmically non-split.
Then any parabolic vector bundle E∗on X with parabolic divisor S admits a parabolic Lie
algebroid connection for (V, ϕ).
In [ABKS], Theorem 1.1 was proved under the assumption that the vector bundle V
stable.
Since a stable vector bundle is indecomposable, if (V, ϕ) is a logarithmically
split holomorphic Lie algebroid, and V is stable, then ϕ is an isomorphism of V with
TX ⊗OX(−S).

Corollary 6.6).

that ϕ vanishes over every parabolic point x ∈S, and let E∗be a parabolic vector bundle.
Then the following two statements hold:

The above homomorphism ϕ is called the anchor map of the Lie algebroid. As a conse-
quence of (3), this ϕ is a Lie algebroid map (see [ABKS, Remark 2.1]).
