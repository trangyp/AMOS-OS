A note on Bondal’s conjecture
Darío Martín Aza
April 9, 2026
Abstract
We prove that the connection vector fields associated to ample Poisson line bundles are
not locally hamiltonian unless the Poisson structure is zero. We use this result to provide
further evidence on Bondal’s conjecture regarding the dimensions of the degeneracy loci of
a holomorphic Fano Poisson manifold.
1
Introduction
Let (X, π) be a holomorphic Poisson manifold, which means that X is a holomorphic manifold
and π is a holomorphic bivector field such that [π, π] = 0 for the Schouten bracket [ , ] of
multivector fields. The study of holomorphic or algebraic Poisson geometry amounts to the
study of Poisson brackets on the rings of holomorphic or algebraic functions on a holomorphic
manifold or sometimes more singular spaces such as schemes or complex analytic varieties [Pol97]
[Pym18]. To such a Poisson manifold we can assign a collection of Poisson subvarieties called
degeneracy loci given by
D2k(π) = {x ∈X | πk+1(x) = 0}.
These subspaces provide X with a stratification
D0(π) ⊆D2(π) ⊆D4(π) ⊆. . . D2r−2(π) ⊆X
where r is the rank of the Poisson structure.
In the year 1993, Bondal made the following
conjecture involving these subspaces
Conjecture 1.1. ([Bon93]) If (X, π) is a Fano Poisson complex manifold of rank r then D2k
has an irreducible component of dimension at least 2k + 1 for all k ∈{0, 1, . . . , r −1}.
Bondal’s conjecture includes the following conjectures:
• If X is Fano then π vanishes on a curve.
• If X is Fano and π of rank 2k then D2k−2 has a component of dimension at least 2k −1.
This result was proved in [Pol97] .
In low dimensions the conjecture takes the following form
• In the case of dimension 3, where the rank of π must be 2, the conjecture is reduced to
proving the statement that D0 contains a curve. This case was settled in [Pol97].

• In the case of dimension 4 the conjecture is the pair of statements D0 contains a curve
and D2 contains a subspace of dimension 3. This was proved in [GP12].
• In the case of dimension 5 the conjecture is the same pair of statements as before: D0
contains a curve and D2 contains a subspace of dimension 3. This is still open.
In the year 1997, Polischuk published the seminal paper on algebraic Poisson structures
[Pol97], in which he tackled Bondal’s conjecture, proving it in the case of dimension 3.
In
the work [GP12], the authors approached Bondal’s conjecture via the study Poisson modules.
An invertible Poisson module is a line bundle L together with a flat Poisson connection ∇. To
any invertible Poisson module there is associated a connection vector field and the connection
vector field associated with the canonical module is the modular vector field which was initially
studied in [BZ99] and [Wei97]. The connection vector field is not a globally defined vector field
but it is actually a global section of the quotient sheaf XPoiss/XHam between the sheaf of Poisson
vector fields and that of hamiltonian vector fields. To each invertible Poisson module the authors
of [GP12] assigned multiderivations supported on each degeneracy locus which could provide an
explanation for the dimensions ocurring in Bondal’s conjecture. These multiderivations were
called residues. The k-th modular residue of a Poisson manifold (X, π) is the globally defined
section associated to the canonical module via this method. It would be interesting to relate
the ampleness of an invertible Poisson module with nonvanishing properties of these residues in
order to approach Bondal’s conjecture. In this note we first prove the following
Theorem 1.2. Let (L, ∇) be an ample Poisson line bundle on the complex Poisson analytic
space (X, π) and suppose that π ̸= 0. Then the connection vector field of (L, ∇) defines a non
trivial class in H0(X, XPoiss/XHam).
This means that the connection vector field assigned to an ample Poisson line bundle is not
locally hamiltonian. Since Gualtieri-Pym’s residues only depend on said class defined by the
connection vector field of the line bundle, this is a necessary condition for the nonvanishing of
Gualtieri-Pym’s residues. In particular, if (X, π) is a Fano Poisson manifold its modular vector
field defines a nonvanishing section of the quotient sheaf XPoiss/XHam.
It is important that
we prove that this result is valid on Poisson analytic spaces which are possibly singular, since
we want to apply it to the degeneracy locus themselves of a Poisson manifold. In this way,
we obtain our main result which gives some evidence in favour of Bondal’s conjecture, more
precisely proving that the dimensions appearing in Bondal’s conjecture are satisfied by at least
half of the degeneracy loci involved.
Theorem 1.3. Given (X, π) a complex Fano Poisson manifold such that D2k−2(π) ⊊D2k(π),
then either D2k−2(π) contains an irreducible component of dimension at least 2k −1 or D2k(π)
contains an irreducible component of dimension at least 2k + 1.
The paper is organized as follows: in section 2 we provide definitions involving complex Poisson
manifolds and analytic spaces. In section 3 we review some facts concerning Poisson modules
and Gualtieri-Pym’s definition of the residues assigned to an invertible Poisson module. Our
original results are discussed in section 4.

We now turn our attention to Poisson subvarieties. If (X, π) is a Poisson analytic space, a
Poisson subspace of X is an analytic subspace Y ⊆X together with a Poisson structure σ,
such that the inclusion i : Y →X satisfies π(df, dg) = σ(i∗(df), i∗(dg)). In case Y ⊆X has
the structure of a Poisson subspace, this structure is unique and therefore we can say without
ambiguity that Y ⊆X is a Poisson subspace.
Proposition 2.4. Let (X, π) be a Poisson analytic space and let Y ⊆X be an analytic subspace
with ideal sheaf I ⊆OX. The following statements are equivalent:
• Y admits the structure of a Poisson subspace.
• I is a sheaf of Poisson ideals, meaning that {I, OX} ⊆I.
• Every local hamiltonian vector field is tangent to Y.
Proof. A reference for the above result is [GP12].
Given (X, π) a Poisson analytic space we call a subspace Y ⊆X a strong Poisson subspace
if every local Poisson vector field is tangent to Y. A strong Poisson subspace is always a Poisson
subspace since hamiltonian vector fields are Poisson. We now define some important strong
Poisson subspaces that can be considered inside any Poisson analytic space. Given a Poisson
analytic space (X, π) we define the 2k −th degeneracy locus of π as
D2k = Zeros(πk+1).

Poisson modules
On a Poisson manifold or analytic space there are distinguished vector bundles which serve as
vector bundles with flat connections in Poisson geometry. Given a Poisson analytic space (X, π)
we will call a vector bundle E over X together with a flat Poisson connection ∇, a Poisson
module. This means a pair (E, ∇) such that ∇: E →TX ⊗E is a C−linear morphism satisfying
∇(fs) = −Xf ⊗s + f∇(s) and such that the composition ∇◦∇vanishes. This last condition is
referred to as the flatness of the Poisson connection. Poisson modules induce an action of Ω1

It would be interesting to know if the connection vector field of an ample Poisson line bundle
can vanish along D0(π). If this was not possible the modular vector field of a Fano Poisson
manifold would always be a non zero vector field tangent to D0(π) proving that D0(π) contains a
curve. There are known examples where the degeneracy locus D0(π) has a connected component

submanifolds it becomes aparent that the connection vector field of an ample Poisson line bundle

degree 2k + 1. Since Z is not locally hamiltonian there is at least one point where R is non zero
and therefore dim(X) ≥2k + 1.
Since the argument in theorem 1.2 is local, a similar result is valid even when considering a
quasi-projective non complete variety.
Corollary 4.8. Let U be a quasi projective complex analytic space with a Poisson structure π
of constant rank k ̸= 0. If (L, ∇) is a Poisson line bundle on U such that L is the pullback of
an ample line bundle on a projective space and c1(L) ̸= 0, then dim(U) ≥2k + 1.
We are now in position to prove our main theorem.
Theorem 1.3. Given (X, π) a complex Fano Poisson manifold such that D2k−2(π) ⊊D2k(π),
then either D2k−2(π) contains an irreducible component of dimension at least 2k −1 or D2k(π)
contains an irreducible component of dimension at least 2k + 1.
Proof. Since the inclusion is strict, there exists a point p ∈D2k(π)−D2k−2(π). We have then that
πk(p) ̸= 0 and therefore D2k(π) has an irreducible component passing through p of dimension at
least 2k. We call that component V. Since V is a strong Poisson subspace of X the anticanonical
bundle (ωX)−1 of X restricts to V producing an ample Poisson line bundle over V which we call
L. Let F = D2k−2(π) ∩V and U = V −F. We know that c1(L) ∈H2(V, C) is not zero and we
have the following exact sequence of relative cohomology
. . . →H2(V, U, C) →H2(V, C) →H2(U, C) →. . .
but then either the class of c1(L|U) ̸= 0 or there exists a nontrivial class on H2(V, U, C). There
is a map H2(V, U, C) →H2n−2(F, C), where n is the complex dimension of V which is an iso-
morphism if V is smooth, given by the cap product −∩[V ]. Suppose that ξ ∈H2(V, U, C) is
such that i∗(ξ) = c1(L) and consider the class ξ ∩[V ] ∈H2n−2(F, C). We have that i∗(ξ ∩[V ]) =
c1(L) ∩[V ] ̸= 0 and therefore H2n−2(F, C) ̸= 0, which implies that dim(F) ≥n −1 ≥2k −1.
Now suppose that c1(L|U) ̸= 0. Since π has rank k in U we have that the Poisson structure
on U does not vanish and then we can apply corollary 4.8 to U and therefore dim(U) ≥2k + 1,
showing that dim(V ) ≥2k + 1.
