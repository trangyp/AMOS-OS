Abstract. Let A1, . . . , Ar be Hermitian n×n matrices and S = P Ai⊗
si the associated matrix semicircle, where s1, . . . , sr are free semicircu-
lar variables. We prove that the following are equivalent: (i) the ma-
trix pencil A = P Aixi is LR-semisimple (decomposes, up to left–right
equivalence, as a direct sum of unsplittable pencils); (ii) S is non-singular
at t = 0 (the matrix-valued Cauchy transform has a continuous bound-
ary limit near the origin); (iii) the covariance map η : X 7→P AiXAi
is symmetrically DS-scalable (there exists C ≻0 with η(C) = C−1).
When these hold, the spectral density satisfies f(0) = 1
π tr(C), where C
is the unique trace minimizer of the solution set {W ≻0 : η(W) W = I}.
The proof combines algebraic and analytic ingredients. On the alge-
braic side, we establish the equivalence (i) ⇔(iii) using Gurvits’ capacity
theory for indecomposable maps and a geodesic reflection theorem in the
Riemannian manifold of positive definite matrices, which upgrades DS-
scalability to symmetric DS-scalability for self-adjoint completely posi-
tive maps. On the analytic side, we prove (iii) ⇒(ii) via a Lyapunov–
Schmidt reduction of Speicher’s equation at a trace-minimizing solution,
showing that the Jacobian of the bifurcation equations is positive def-
inite. This removes a stability hypothesis that was required in earlier
approaches.

§1
Introduction
Let A1, . . . , Ar ∈Mn(C) be Hermitian matrices and let s1, . . . , sr be
free standard semicircular variables in a non-commutative probability space
(A, φ). The random variable S = Pr
i=1 Ai ⊗si is called a matrix semi-
circle. Its spectral distribution encodes the interplay between the algebraic
structure of the matrix pencil A = P Aixi and the free probabilistic behav-
ior of the semicircular variables. A natural question is: when is the spectral
distribution of S non-singular?
In Mai et al. (2023), it was shown that the distribution of S has no atom
at 0 if and only if the matrix pencil A has full inner rank (equal to n).
In this paper, we address the finer question of when the distribution has
a bounded continuous density at 0. The answer turns out to be governed
by an algebraic property of the pencil that we call LR-semisimplicity: A
is LR-semisimple if it decomposes, up to left–right equivalence, as a direct
sum of unsplittable pencils.

Together, these results give a complete picture. LR-semisimplicity is the
precise algebraic condition governing non-singularity of the matrix semicircle
at t = 0 and ensuring that its covariance map is scalable to the doubly-
stochastic map. If the pencil is in addition unsplittable then the scaling is
unique up to unitary transformations. If the pencil is not LR-semisimple
but still full, then we lose the bounded density but the spectral distribution
does not have an atom at x = 0 (see the cusp singularity in Example 6.2).
The formal versions of these theorems are proved as follows: Theorem A
as Theorem 5.3 in § 5.2 and Theorem B as Corollary 5.4 in § 5.3.
Strategy of proof. The proof consists of three components.
Algebraic (§3): (i) ⇔(iii).
LR-semisimple ⇐⇒symmetrically DS-scalable.
⇒:
The pencil–map dictionary (§2.4) reduces an LR-semisimple pencil to
indecomposable blocks. Gurvits’ capacity theory gives DS-scalability of each
block. Since ηA is self-adjoint, DS-scalability produces a 2-cycle (C, D) of
the map F(X) = η(X)−1. We show that F acts as a geodesic reflection in
the Riemannian manifold of positive definite matrices, so that the geometric
mean C # D is a fixed point of F. This yields symmetric DS-scalability.
⇐:
A splitting lemma for doubly stochastic maps shows that every splitting
pair automatically completes to a full block-diagonal decomposition. The
result then follows by induction on the matrix size.
Forward analytic (§4): (iii) ⇒(ii).
sym DS-scalable =⇒non-singular.
We study the HFS form of Speicher’s equation, η(W)W + uW = I, for
the modified Cauchy transform W(u) = iGS(iu) with Re W(u) ≻0 for
Re u > 0. We apply the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction at a trace-minimizing
solution of η(W)W = I. The key step is establishing that the Jacobian of
the reduced bifurcation equations is positive definite, which follows from a
multiplicative domain identity. The implicit function theorem then produces
a real-analytic extension of W(u) through u = 0, yielding non-singularity
and the exact density formula f(0) = 1
π tr(C).
Converse analytic (§5): (ii) ⇒(iii).
non-singular =⇒sym DS-scalable.

on the real axis. Non-singularity provides a boundary limit W0 = W(0+),

Organization. Section 2 collects the definitions and structural results used
throughout: matrix semicircles and their Cauchy transforms, the hierarchy
of matrix pencil properties (unsplittable, LR-semisimple, full), completely
positive maps and their scalings, and the pencil–map dictionary relating the
two sides.
Section 3 establishes the algebraic equivalence LR-semisimple
⇔symmetrically DS-scalable. Section 4 develops the Lyapunov–Schmidt
analysis that gives the forward analytic implication sym DS-scalable ⇒non-
singular. Section 5 proves the main theorem by combining these with the
converse analytic implication. Section 6 discusses open questions, includ-
ing the nature of singularities for non-LR-semisimple pencils, biased matrix
semicircles, and the non-self-adjoint case.

Remark 2.8. If the pencil A = P Aixi is full (equivalently, ηA is rank non-
decreasing; see Theorem 2.35(i)), then µS has no atom at zero; more pre-

Remark 6.1. Geometric invariant theory perspective The algebraic
results of §3 admit a natural interpretation in the framework of geometric
invariant theory (GIT), as developed by Garg et al. (2020); see also B¨urgisser
et al. (2018, 2019) for a comprehensive treatment.
The group G = GLn(C)×GLn(C) acts on r-tuples of matrices (A1, . . . , Ar) ∈
Mn(C)r by
(g, h) · (A1, . . . , Ar) = (g A1 h−1, . . . , g Ar h−1).
Under this action, LR-equivalence classes of pencils correspond to G-orbits.
The key entries of the GIT dictionary are:
• Capacity as a Kempf–Ness functional. The log-capacity log Cap(ηA)
plays the role of the Kempf–Ness function, which in GIT measures the
distance of an orbit from being closed. Its critical points are the DS-
scalings of ηA.
• DS-scalability as polystability. A pencil A is DS-scalable (equiva-
lently, Cap(ηA) > 0 and the capacity infimum is attained) if and only
if the G-orbit of (A1, . . . , Ar) is closed in the semistable locus — the
GIT notion of polystability. In this language, Proposition 3.10 says that
LR-semisimplicity is equivalent to polystability.
• Operator Sinkhorn iteration as gradient descent.
The alter-
nating row/column normalization of Definition 2.26 can be interpreted
as Riemannian gradient descent on the Kempf–Ness function over the
symmetric space GLn/Un. The GGOW algorithm Garg et al. (2020)
provides polynomial-time convergence guarantees for this iteration.
We have not used this perspective in the proofs, but it provides useful
geometric intuition for the algebraic results of §3.
The main theorem characterizes non-singularity at x = 0 for unbiased
self-adjoint matrix semicircles. Several natural extensions remain open.
