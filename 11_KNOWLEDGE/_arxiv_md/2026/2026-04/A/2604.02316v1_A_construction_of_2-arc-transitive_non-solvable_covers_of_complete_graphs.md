A CONSTRUCTION OF 2-ARC-TRANSITIVE NON-SOLVABLE
COVERS OF COMPLETE GRAPHS
Jiyong Chen, Cai Heng Li, Ci Xuan Wu, and Yan Zhou Zhu
Abstract. We construct connected 2-arc-transitive covers of complete graphs with non-
abelian characteristically simple transformation groups. This solves the existence prob-
lem for non-solvable 2-arc-transitive covers of complete graphs.
Key words: complete graph, non-solvable cover
2000 Mathematics subject classification: 05C38, 20B25
1. Introduction
Throughout this paper, a group is assumed to be finite, and a graph is assumed to be
finite, simple and undirected. Given a graph Γ = (V, E), an s-arc of Γ is a sequence of
s + 1 vertices, where any two consecutive vertices are adjacent and any three consecutive
vertices are distinct. Let Γ be a connected regular graph. For a group G of automorphisms
of Γ, the graph Γ is called (G, s)-arc-transitive (or simply s-arc-transitive) if G acts
transitively on the set of all s-arcs of Γ. Finite s-arc-transitive graphs form an important
class of graphs and have received considerable attention. In particular, Weiss [17] proved,
using local action analysis, that there exists no 8-arc-transitive graph except for cycles.
For a (G, s)-arc-transitive graph Γ = (V, E), the orbits B of a normal subgroup N on V
form a G-invariant partition of V . The normal quotient ΓN of Γ is defined as the graph
with vertex set B, where two vertices B1 and B2 are adjacent if and only if there exist
β1 ∈B1 and β2 ∈B2 that are adjacent in Γ. It was shown by Praeger [13, Theorem 4.1]
that if Γ is a connected (G, s)-arc-transitive graph with s ⩾2 and N has at least 3 orbits
on V , then ΓN is (G/N, s)-arc-transitive, and Γ and ΓN have the same valency. In this
case, Γ is called a cover of ΓN. To emphasize the normal subgroup N, the graph Γ is called
an N-cover of ΓN, and N is called the transformation group of this cover. In other words,
the class of s-arc-transitive graphs is closed under taking normal quotients. The minimal
objects in the class are called basic s-arc-transitive graphs, which are (G, s)-arc-transitive
graphs (V, E) with no non-trivial normal quotient, so that either G is quasiprimitive
on V (each minimal normal subgroup is transitive on V ), or G is bi-quasiprimitive on
V (each minimal normal subgroup of G has exactly two orbits on V ). Thus studying
s-arc-transitive graphs can be divided into two steps as follows:
(A) Determine basic s-arc-transitive graphs.
(B) Construct and characterize normal covers of the basic graphs in Step (A).
Praeger gave systematic descriptions of basic 2-arc-transitive graphs, see [13] for non-
bipartite graphs and [14] for bipartite graphs. This initiated a program for the study
of s-arc-transitive graphs, and the program has been successfully applied to characterize
some important classes of s-arc-transitive graphs, see [7,11,12,19] and references therein.
For Step (B) of the program, a remarkable result due to J.H. Conway shows that each
connected s-arc-transitive graph has a non-trivial connected s-arc-transitive cover with
an elementary abelian 2-group as its transformation group, see [1, Chapter 19]. However,

(1) Γ is connected if and only if ⟨H, g⟩= G;
(2) Γ is undirected if and only if HgH = Hg−1H;
(3) Γ is (G, 2)-arc-transitive if and only if H is 2-transitive on [H : H ∩Hg].

G ⩽Aut(Kn) is 2-arc-transitive if and only if G is 3-transitive on the vertices. Thus, we
