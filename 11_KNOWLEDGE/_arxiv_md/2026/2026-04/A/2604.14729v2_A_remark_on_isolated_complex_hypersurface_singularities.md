A REMARK ON ISOLATED COMPLEX HYPERSURFACE
SINGULARITIES
FABRIZIO CATANESE, CIRO CILIBERTO, AND CONCETTINA GALATI
Abstract. This is now an expository note about the following classical prob-
lem.
Let (X, 0) be the germ of a hypersurface in (Cn, 0) with an ordinary
singularity of multiplicity m at the origin 0.
A natural question to ask is
whether X and its tangent cone at the origin are analytically isomorphic. The
answer is negative in general, in view of a theorem of Kioji Saito.
However there is an integer D(n, m) > m such that, given a regular homo-
geneous polynomial f(x1, . . . , xn) of degree m (this means that {f = 0} is a
smooth hypersurface in Pn−1) then, for all d ⩾D(n, m), any convergent power
series of the form g = f + o(d) (here, as usual, o(d) stays for a power series of
order at least d), defines a germ {g = 0} which is analytically equivalent to the
germ {f = 0}. In this note we compute D(n, m) explicitly as n(m −2) + 1.
We also give an extension to the case in which f is a quasihomogeneous poly-
nomial. It was pointed out that the value of D(n, m) was already known by
[4, Exercise 7.31].

1. Introduction
Let (X, p) be the germ of an isolated hypersurface singularity in Cn, such that
the tangent cone CX,p of X at p is the cone over a smooth hypersurface of degree
m ⩾2 in Pn−1, where n ⩾2. Then we shall say that p is an ordinary singularity
of multiplicity m or an ordinary m–tuple point of X. A natural question to ask is
whether (X, p) and (CX,p, p) are analytically isomorphic. It is well known that this
is always the case if m = 2. The answer is also affirmative if m = n = 3 [3, Prop.
3.3], and if 3 ⩽m ⩽4 and n = 2 (see [5]).
Despite this, the answer is, as we will see, negative in general. However, by the
finite determinacy theorem (see [6, Thm. 1.2.23, 2nd ed.] and also [4, Ch. 6]), the
following holds.

d, then the germ at the origin defined by g = 0 is analytically equivalent to the

2.2. Saito’s theorem. Conversely, an important theorem of Kioji Saito [7, p. 123]
proved that the condition Mf ∼= Tf is also sufficient for f to be quasi-homogeneous.
Since Tf is a quotient of Mf, this condition is equivalent to the equality of Milnor
number µf and Tyurina number τf, i.e.,
µf := dimC(Mf) = dimC(Tf) =: τf
Saito’s theorem can be reformulated as follows, using the forthcoming Lemma
2.2, the Euler relation for quasihomogeneus polynomials, and the chain rule.
Theorem 2.1. Let f ∈m ⊂On such that f = 0 defines a germ of hypersurface
with an isolated singularity at the origin. Then this germ is analytically isomorphic
to the germ defined by a quasihomogeneous polynomial if and only if f ∈J(f),
equivalently, if and only if Mf ∼= Tf.
2.3. Right equivalence. We now define an equivalence relation ∼in On (called
right equivalence in [6, Def. 1.2.9, 2nd ed.]). Namely, given f, g ∈On, we will define
f ∼g if and only if there exists an automorphism ϕ of On such that
ϕ(f) := f ◦ϕ = g.
In general, the right equivalence between f and g implies that the germs of hyper-
surfaces defined by f = 0 and g = 0 at the origin are analytically equivalent (in
that case one says that f and g are contact equivalent [6, Rmk. 1.2.9.1 and Def.
1.2.9, 2nd ed.]).
Lemma 2.2 (Lemma 1.2.13 and Definition 1.2.9 of 2nd ed. [6]). If f, g ∈On and
f is a quasihomogeneous polynomial, then f ∼g if and only if the germs defined by
f = 0 and g = 0 at the origin are analytically equivalent.

f (k) of f to be

Theorem 3.1. Let f ∈On be a regular homogeneous polynomial of degree m ⩾3,
and exclude the case n = 2, m = 3 in view of Corollary 2.6.

Remark 3.3. The expert reader will now be easily convinced that the same coun-
terexample holds for every regular homogeneous polynomial f.
In fact, by the cited result of Scheja and Storch, the Milnor algebra Mf is a
graded Gorenstein algebra whose socle, i.e., its graded piece of maximal degree
δf = n(m−2), has dimension 1 and is generated by a the polynomial φ := Hess(f).
If we take gt := f + tφ, then for general t we have that gt is not analytically
equivalent to f.
4. Generalization to the quasi-homogeneous case
