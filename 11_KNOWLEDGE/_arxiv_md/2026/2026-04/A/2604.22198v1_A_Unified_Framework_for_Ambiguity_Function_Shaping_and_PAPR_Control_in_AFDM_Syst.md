A Unified Framework for Ambiguity Function
Shaping and PAPR Control in AFDM Systems
Lingsheng Meng, Yong Liang Guan, Senior Member, IEEE, Zilong Liu, Senior Member, IEEE,
Yirui Luo, Graduate Student Member, IEEE, and Pingzhi Fan, Life Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—Affine frequency division multiplexing (AFDM) has
emerged as a promising integrated sensing and communication
(ISAC) waveform due to its intrinsic chirp signalling nature.
Nevertheless, practical AFDM-based ISAC still faces two key ob-
stacles, namely, high ambiguity function (AF) sidelobes and high
peak-to-average power ratio (PAPR). By leveraging the reserved
chirp-subcarrier (RCS) symbols, we develop a unified AFDM
waveform design framework for AF shaping and/or PAPR con-
trol. The proposed framework supports three modes: AF shaping
via weighted integrated sidelobe level (ISL) minimization, PAPR
minimization, and joint AF shaping and PAPR control under a
prescribed PAPR constraint. To solve the formulated nonconvex
problem and to accommodate the discrete-phase constraints on
the optionally optimized pre-chirp parameters, a joint ISL-PAPR-
discrete-phase majorization-minimization (JIPD-MM) algorithm
is developed. Simulation results verify the effectiveness of the
proposed framework under all three design modes. The joint
mode further demonstrates that the prescribed PAPR constraint
can be effectively satisfied while still achieving meaningful ISL
reduction. These gains are also reflected in improved weak-target
detectability under multitarget scenarios and lower bit error rate
(BER) under power-amplifier (PA) nonlinearity.

I. INTRODUCTION
Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC), as a major
use case for the upcoming sixth-generation (6G) systems
[1]–[3], has attracted tremendous attention from academia,
industry, the International Telecommunication Union (ITU)
and the 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) [4], [5].
By allowing both the sensing and communication modules to
share the same hardware and spectrum resources as well as a
majority of signal processing tasks, ISAC not only supports
flexible performance trade-offs and efficient resource utiliza-
tion but can also mutually enhance the two functionalities [6].
To support emerging ISAC applications in a diverse set of
use cases, such as vehicle-to-everything (V2X), low-altitude
economy, and satellite networks, a central question is how
to design highly flexible and adaptable waveforms that are
resilient to doubly selective channels while permitting accurate
sensing. The legacy orthogonal frequency division multiplex-
ing (OFDM) has been extensively studied for ISAC, but it

AFDM modulates its data symbols through a set of orthog-
onal chirp subcarriers that are induced by the discrete affine
Fourier transform (DAFT). By properly tuning the chirp rate
specified by the post-chirp parameter c1 according to the
maximum Doppler frequency, AFDM is able to attain full
channel diversity and hence significantly enhanced error rate
performance. Moreover, AFDM subsumes both OFDM and
orthogonal chirp division multiplexing (OCDM) [12] and can
be efficiently generated through minimum modification of an
OFDM transmitter. As such, AFDM is backward-compatible
and allows seamless integration into the existing infrastructure
that is dominated by OFDM [8], [13]. Additionally, one can
adjust the other pre-chirp parameter c2 to enhance physical-
layer security [14].
From the ISAC perspective, AFDM has attracted increasing
attention. Since sensing is often realized through matched fil-
tering (MF) in the time or DAFT domain, the ambiguity func-
tion (AF) of the transmitted waveform plays a central role [15],
[16]. Existing AFDM-ISAC designs mainly focus on pilot-
aided sensing structures. For example, a single dedicated pilot
framework was proposed in [17], where pilot–data separability
is ensured by allocating surrounding null-guard subcarriers,
while superimposed-pilot strategies were considered in [18],
[19] to improve spectral efficiency. Complementary analyses
have further characterized the AF properties of AFDM chirp
subcarriers and typical pilot structures [20], [21]. Meanwhile,
some studies also explored sensing using the data-bearing
AFDM signal itself [17], [22]. In this case, however, the
sensing performance may be compromised due to the random
data.
Beyond the ISAC perspective, AFDM in practical systems
also faces a critical transmitter-side challenge, namely high
peak-to-average power ratio (PAPR). Similar to OFDM, such
an issue arises from the multicarrier nature of AFDM and a
high PAPR could drive the power-amplifier (PA) to operate in
nonlinear region. In practice, certain amount of input back-off
(IBO) may be necessary to ensure linear power amplification,
but this could lead to reduced communication/sensing range
[23]. For OFDM, a number of PAPR reduction techniques
have been developed, including selective mapping, precoding,
and reserved-tone based schemes [24]–[26]. In contrast, PAPR
reduction for AFDM remains much less explored. A DAFT-
spread-based affine frequency division multiple access scheme

In summary, existing AFDM-ISAC studies mainly focus on
pilot-aided structures and related analyses, with little consider-

PAPR reduction methods for AFDM either rely on a prescribed
spreading structure and thus cannot flexibly optimize a given
AFDM waveform instance, or depend on pre-chirp parameter
selection at the cost of additional side-information overhead.
A unified and adaptable waveform design framework that
accommodates AF shaping and PAPR control across diverse
system requirements is missing.
Given the above research gaps, we present a novel AFDM
waveform design framework that jointly considers AF side-
lobes and PAPR. Inspired by the tone-reservation for OFDM-
PAPR reduction [26], we uphold the key idea of reserving
and optimizing certain AFDM chirp subcarriers in the DAFT
domain. This reserved chirp-subcarrier (RCS) approach en-
ables AF sidelobe suppression and PAPR control without
modifying the data-bearing subcarriers. In contrast to OFDM,
AFDM also allows flexible configurations to the per-subcarrier
pre-chirp parameters, i.e., c2,m to vary across subcarriers
rather than using a single c2. This provides additional design
flexibility and can be leveraged to further optimize the AFDM
waveforms.

B. Design Modes and Performance Objectives
Depending on whether only the RCS symbols or both the
RCS symbols and the per-subcarrier pre-chirp parameters are

optimized, the proposed framework supports the following
three design modes that reflect different priorities between
sensing performance and hardware efficiency.
1) AF shaping mode: The first mode focuses on sensing
performance by minimizing the weighted ISL metric JISL in
(4). This mode can be implemented either by optimizing only
the RCS symbols or by jointly optimizing the RCS symbols
and the per-subcarrier pre-chirp parameters. The goal is to
suppress AF sidelobes within certain LAZ, thereby improving
target detectability.
2) PAPR minimization mode: The second mode aims to
minimize the PAPR metric PAPRx in (5). Likewise, this mode
can be realized either with RCS-symbol optimization only
or with additional per-subcarrier pre-chirp design. This mode
focuses on reducing the dynamic range at the PA input, which
mitigates nonlinear distortion.
3) Joint AF shaping and PAPR control mode: The third
mode jointly accounts for sensing and hardware constraints
by minimizing the ISL subject to an explicit PAPR constraint,
i.e., by enforcing PAPR ≤Γ for a prescribed threshold Γ.
This mode balances AF-sidelobe suppression in the LAZ and
the PAPR requirement. In this work, this mode is instantiated
through the joint optimization of the RCS symbols and the
per-subcarrier pre-chirp parameters. This mode will be the
primary focus of our subsequent development, while the first
two modes can be considered as special cases of the unified
formulation based on this mode.

IV. PROPOSED ALGORITHM
We develop a joint ISL-PAPR-discrete-phase MM (JIPD-
MM) algorithm for solving the unified AFDM waveform
optimization problem in (18). The RCS-only designs can be
obtained by fixing the pre-chirp parameters of DCSs as shown
in (21).
The unified optimization problem in (18) is challenging for
three reasons: i) both the ISL objective JISL(u) in (19) and the
PAPR metric PAPRu in (20) are nonconvex functions of u; ii)
the PAPR constraint involves a fractional max-type structure,
which is difficult to handle directly in an iterative algorithm;
iii) the per-DCS sets Um impose discrete-phase constraints. To
cope with these difficulties, the proposed JIPD-MM algorithm
proceeds in iterations indexed by r. At r-th iteration, the non-
convex ISL objective and the PAPR constraint are replaced
by smooth surrogate functions constructed around the current
iterate u(r). The construction of the ISL surrogate is presented
in Section IV-A. In Section IV-B, the fractional max-type
PAPR constraint is converted into a tractable smooth surrogate
and incorporated via a penalty formulation. In Section IV-C,
the discrete-phase constraints u[m] ∈Um are addressed by
relaxing each Um to its convex hull and adding a negative
square penalty term, which promotes solutions lying on the
extreme points of the convex hull. The overall proposed JIPD-
MM algorithm is presented in Section IV-D.

VI. CONCLUSION
In this work, we investigated AFDM-ISAC waveform de-
sign for AF-sidelobe suppression and PAPR control. We
developed a unified optimization framework in which the
RCS symbols serve as the basic waveform-design variables,


## Page 13


13
while the per-subcarrier pre-chirp parameters can be further
exploited through the same formulation to introduce addi-
tional design flexibility. To handle the formulated nonconvex
problem that couples AF-related and PAPR-related metrics
with discrete-phase constraints, the ISL objective, the PAPR
constraint, and the discrete-phase constraint are replaced with
smooth surrogate objectives or penalty terms, and the resulting
subproblems are solved iteratively. Three operating modes
were considered to support different design priorities. In the
AF shaping mode, the proposed framework suppresses AF
sidelobes over the prescribed LAZ. In the PAPR minimiza-
tion mode, it reduces the waveform PAPR. In the joint AF
shaping and PAPR control mode, the design based on both
RCS symbols and pre-chirp parameters satisfies the prescribed
PAPR constraint while achieving substantial ISL reduction.
Compared with conventional AFDM, the proposed designs
improve weak-target detectability and reduce BER under PA
nonlinearity.
Possible directions for future research include extending the
framework to more general scenarios, such as multi-antenna
systems and adaptive designs under practical hardware and sig-
naling constraints, as well as developing lower-complexity im-
plementations and statistical-metric-based design approaches.
