Almost Free Non-Archimedean Banach Spaces
and Relation to Large Cardinals
Tomoki Mihara
Abstract
Let k be a complete valuation field. We formulate a free Banach k-vector space
as a Banach k-vector space with an orthonormal Schauder basis, and an almost free
Banach k-vector space as a non-Archimedean analogue of an almost free Abelian
group. As non-Archimedean analogues of the classical facts that an almost free
Abelian group is free under the assumption of the ℵ1-strong compactness or the
weak compactness of the cardinality, we show that an almost free Banach k-vector
space is free under similar assumptions.

Introduction
An Abelian group is said to be κ-free for a cardinal κ if every its subgroup of rank < κ is
free. Every free Abelian group is κ-free for any cardinal κ (cf. [Fuc15] Theorem 3/1.6).
For any cardinals κ and λ with λ < κ, every κ-free Abelian group is λ-free by definition.

Therefore, the notion of κ-freeness defines a linear hierarchy of Abelian groups toward
free Abelian groups.
Although there have been various studies on the hierarchy (cf. [Fuc15] §3.8), we con-
centrate on one simple topic among them: “Let κ be an uncountable cardinal. When the
κ-freeness implies the freeness?” The question is much deeper than it looks.
P. Hills verified in [Hil70] Theorem 3 that if cf(κ) = ω, then every κ-free Abelian group
of cardinality κ is free. Shelah’s singular compactness theorem, which is named after
the study of Whitehead problem by S. Shelah in [She74], generalises the result for an
axiomatically generalised notion of freeness (cf. [Ekl06] §0.1 and §1.4). As a speciali-
sation, it implies that if κ is singular, then every κ-free Abelian group of cardinality κ is
free (cf. [Fuc15] Theorem 3/9.2).
The question for the case where κ is regular is not completed, and is closely related
to studies of large cardinals. For example, J. Gregory verified in [Gre73] Corollary of
Theorem 1 that under the axiom V = L of constructibility, there exists a non-free κ-free
Abelian group of cardinality κ if (and only if) κ is not weakly compact.
The same question arises when we consider notions alternative to freeness, e.g. Σ-cyclicness.
As a preceding study, F. Calderoni and A. Ostrem focused on the following two classical
results on the freeness, and verified analogues on the Σ-cyclicness (cf. [CO25] Theorem
1.1 and Theorem 1.2):
(1) If κ is ℵ1-strongly compact, then every κ-free Abelian group of cardinality κ is free.
(2) If κ is weakly compact, then every κ-free Abelian group of cardinality κ is free.
The literature [CO25] compactly deals with the topics so that the reader not familiar
with large cardinal axioms can learn the relation of almost free Abelian group theory
and compactness in large cardinal axioms. Therefore, it is valuable for an author of an
analogous study to follow the structure of [CO25].
The aim of this paper is to imitate the structure of [CO25] in the study of non-Archimedean
analysis, i.e. analysis of Banach vector spaces over a complete valuation field k. Precisely
speaking, we formulate a non-Archimedean analogue of freeness as the existence of an
orthonormal Schauder basis, and verify corresponding results in a way completely par-
allel to the proofs of [CO25] Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2, except for conventional
differences. See Proposition 3.2 and Proposition 3.3 for characterisation of the freeness
for the case where k is a trivial valuation field or a local field, and Example 3.4 for exam-
ples of non-free Banach k-vector spaces for the case where the valuation of k has dense
image under the assumption of the non-existence of a measurable cardinal.
We briefly explain contents of this paper. In §1, we introduce convention for this paper.
In §2, we recall basic properties of Banach k-vector spaces. In §3, we introduce the

Free Filtration
In this section, we introduce the notion of a free filtration as a non-Archimedean analogue
of a filtration of free subgroups or Σ-cyclic subgroups (cf. [Fuc15] Lemma 3/8.6 and
[CO25] Proposition 4.6), and study its properties. For this purpose, we first introduce the
notion of a free Banach k-vector space.
Definition 3.1. A V ∈Ban(k) is said to be free if V admits an orthonormal Schauder
basis, is said to be κ-free for a κ ∈Card if every V′ ∈L<κ(V) is free, and is said to be
almost free if V is rank(V)-free.
The freeness of a Banach k-vector space of countable type is characterised in terms
of a strictly k-Cartesian normed k-vector space (cf. [BGR84] Proposition 2.7.5/2). By
definition, a Banach k-vector space is free if and only if it is isomorphic to C0(I, k) for
some I ∈Set.
We note that an isomorphism in this paper refers to an isometric k-linear isomorphism.
If we formulated the freeness in terms of an admissible isomorphism, i.e. a bounded
k-linear isomorphism whose inverse is also bounded, then under the assumption of the
non-triviality of the valuation of k, every Banach k-vector space of countable type would
be free (cf. [BGR84] Theorem 2.8.2/2 and [Sch99] Theorem 6) and hence every Banach
k-vector space would be ℵ1-free. Therefore, the situation would be less interesting.
We give characterisations of the freeness for simple cases.
Proposition 3.2. If the valuation of k is trivial, then for any V ∈Ban(k), the following
are equivalent:
(1) The Banach k-vector space V is free.
(2) The Banach k-vector space V is almost free, and is isomorphic to k if dimk = 1.
(3) The inclusion ∥V∥⊂|k| holds.
Proof. The equivalence between (1) and (3) follows from the definition of an orthonor-
mal Schauder basis. Since (3) is equivalent to the condition that for any v ∈V, ∥v∥∈|k|
holds, i.e. (3) restricted to each closed k-linear subspace of V of dimension ≤1, (1) is
equivalent to (1) restricted to each closed k-linear subspace of dimension ≤1. Therefore,
(1) is equivalent to (2) if dimk V , 1. If dimk V = 1, then (3) is equivalent to the condition
that V is isomorphic to C0({∗}, k)  k, and every zero-dimensional Banach k-vector space
is free.
□

(1) The Banach k-vector space V is free.
(2) The Banach k-vector space V is almost free, and is isomorphic to k if dimk = 1.
